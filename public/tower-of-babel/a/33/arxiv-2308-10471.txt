. Let 𝕜 \Bbbk be an algebraically closed field of characteristic p > 0 p>0 . 1.
Introduction

Hopf algebras are generalizations of groups and Lie algebras. In the last few decades, they have played an essential role in invariants of knots, links, and 3-manifolds via their representations. In particular, the representation categories of finite-dimensional Hopf algebras provide fundamental examples of finite tensor categories (see, e.g.,
[
3
,
9
]
).

Let
\Bbbk
be an algebraically closed field and
p
a prime number. It has long been a program classifying finite-dimensional Hopf algebras over
\Bbbk
of characteristic zero. Kaplansky first conjectured that the only
p
-dimensional Hopf algebra in characteristic zero is the group algebra, later proved by Zhu
[
43
]
. For
p^{2}
-dimensional Hopf algebras, Masuoka proved that the semisimple ones are group algebras
[
19
]
. At that time, the only known nonsemisimple Hopf algebras of dimension
p^{2}
were the Taft algebras, which were introduced by Taft in
[
38
]
and are described as follows.

Definition 1.1
.

[
38
]

Let
\Bbbk
be a field, and
\omega\in\Bbbk
a primitive
n
th root of unity for some positive integer
n
. The
Taft algebra

T_{n}(\omega)
is the free algebra
\Bbbk\langle x,g\rangle
subject to the relations

g^{n}=1,\quad x^{n}=0,\quad xg=\omega gx.

The Hopf algebra structure on the Taft algebra
T_{p}(\omega)
is given by

\displaystyle\Delta(g)

\displaystyle=g\otimes g,

\displaystyle\epsilon(g)

\displaystyle=1,

\displaystyle S(g)

\displaystyle=g^{-1},

\displaystyle\Delta(x)

\displaystyle=x\otimes 1+g\otimes x,

\displaystyle\epsilon(x)

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle S(x)

\displaystyle=-g^{-1}x.

Montgomery, in several international conferences, asked (cf. Section 5 of
[
28
]
):

Question 1.2
.

Suppose the base field
\Bbbk
is algebraically closed of characteristic zero, and
p
is a prime number. Are the Taft algebras
T_{p}(\omega)
the only nonsemisimple Hopf algebras in dimension
p^{2}
?

The first author of this paper gave an affirmative answer to Montgomery’s question in
[
24
]
.

Meanwhile, the classification of finite-dimensional Hopf algebras over an algebraically closed field
\Bbbk
of positive characteristic
p
remains wide open. For any semisimple and cosemisimple Hopf algebra in positive characteristic, Etingof and Gelaki showed that it could be lifted to a semisimple Hopf algebra in characteristic zero
[
6
]
. Hence it yields the same classification result. Recently, the classification of the
p
-dimensional Hopf algebras over
\Bbbk
in characteristic
p
has been accomplished by the authors of this paper in
[
26
]
. Etingof later generalizes this result to finite tensor categories in
[
5
]
.

Turning to the
p^{2}
-dimensional Hopf algebras, the pointed ones are classified in
[
39
]
. Based on this classification, there is only one
pointed
noncommutative and noncocommutative
p^{2}
-dimensional Hopf algebra. This Hopf algebra was discovered by Radford in
[
31
]
and is described as follows.

Definition 1.3
.

[
31
]

Let
\Bbbk
be an algebraically closed field of characteristic
p>0
. The
Radford algebra

R(p)
is the free algebra
\Bbbk\langle x,g\rangle
subject to the relations

g^{p}=1,\quad x^{p}=x,\quad[g,x]=g(g-1).

The Hopf algebra structure on
R(p)
is given by

\displaystyle\Delta(g)

\displaystyle=g\otimes g,

\displaystyle\epsilon(g)

\displaystyle=1,

\displaystyle S(g)

\displaystyle=g^{-1},

\displaystyle\Delta(x)

\displaystyle=x\otimes 1+g\otimes x,

\displaystyle\epsilon(x)

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle S(x)

\displaystyle=-g^{-1}x.

It is clear that the Radford algebra could be over any field of characteristic
p
. However, we generally assume
\Bbbk
is algebraically closed and the notation
R(p)
is defined under this assumption.

The first author of this paper, on many occasions, asked the following question:

Question A
.

Let
\Bbbk
be an algebraically closed field of characteristic
p>0
. Is the Radford algebra
R(p)
the unique noncommutative and noncocommutative
p^{2}
-dimensional Hopf algebra over
\Bbbk
up to isomorphism?

So far, there is no example of
p^{2}
-dimensional Hopf algebra
H
over
\Bbbk
such that both
H
and
H^{*}
are not pointed. An affirmative answer to the following question will lead to a complete classification of
p^{2}
-dimensional Hopf algebras over
\Bbbk
, and hence an affirmative answer to Question
A
.

Question B
.

Let
\Bbbk
be an algebraically closed field of characteristic
p>0
. Must every
p^{2}
-dimensional Hopf algebra over
\Bbbk
be pointed?

In this paper, most of our results are stated over an algebraically closed base field
\Bbbk
of positive characteristic
p
. In the first part of our paper, we study the general structures of the
p^{n}
-dimensional Hopf algebras over
\Bbbk
with
n\geq 2
. We first show that any noncosemisimple
p^{n}
-dimensional Hopf algebra over
\Bbbk
with
p^{n-1}
group-like elements must be pointed. Along this line of thoughts, one would like to understand those
p^{n}
-dimensional Hopf algebras
H
which admit a
p^{n-1}
-dimensional Hopf subalgebra
\Bbbk[x]
generated by a primitive element

. In this case, assuming

is semisimple and

is nonsemisimple, we show that

has exactly

indecomposable projectives whose dimensions are equal to

. Moreover,

is involutive if it is unimodular. When

is
p^{2}
-dimensional, we are able to show if it contains a primitive element, then its coradical

cannot have dimension greater than

. As an application, we answer Questions
A
and

affirmatively for

, which completes the classification of all
p^{2}
-dimensional Hopf algebras over
\Bbbk
for

.

For the second part of our paper, we introduce the notion of twist equivalence for cleft extensions to classify the

-dimensional Hopf algebras, which are extensions of

-dimensional ones. We show the second Sweedler cohomology

is trivial for any elementary abelian

which acts as automorphisms on a finite-dimensional commutative algebra

over
\Bbbk
. Consequently, we describe the corresponding cleft extensions in terms of twist equivalence. Independent of the classification result of pointed Hopf algebras of dimension
p^{2}

[

]
, we provide a list of characterizations of

. In particular,

is the unique nontrivial Hopf algebra extension of

\Bbbk[C_{p}]^{*}
by
\Bbbk[C_{p}]
, where

denotes the (multiplicative) cyclic group of order

. Finally, we use the classification result
[

]
to identify all the

-dimensional Hopf algebras with a normal Hopf subalgebra of dimension
p
.

2.
Preliminaries and background

Let
\Bbbk
be a ground field of arbitrary characteristic. We use
H
to denote a finite-dimensional Hopf algebra over
\Bbbk
with counit
\epsilon:H\rightarrow\Bbbk
, comultiplication
\Delta:H\rightarrow H\otimes H
and antipode
S:H\rightarrow H
. The dual vector space
H^{*}
of
H
is also a Hopf algebra with the structure induced from that of
H
. The
augmentation ideal
of
H
is defined to be
H^{+}:={\rm ker}\,\epsilon
. We use Sweedler’s notation to write
\Delta(h)=\sum_{(h)}h_{1}\otimes h_{2}
for all
h\in H
. When the context is clear, we may suppress
\sum_{(h)}
. The readers are referred to
[
27
]
and
[
37
]
for more details on Hopf algebras and some fundamental results.

By an
H
-module, we always mean a left
H
-module of finite dimension over
\Bbbk
. For any two
H
-modules
V
and
W
, their tensor product
V\otimes W
over the base field
\Bbbk
admits a natural action of
H
via
\Delta
. The
left dual

V^{*}
of an
H
-module
V
is the
\Bbbk
-linear space
V^{*}=\operatorname{Hom}_{\Bbbk}(V,\Bbbk)
equipped with the
H
-module structure given by

(hf)(v)=f(S(h)v),\quad\text{for all $h\in H$, $f\in V^{*}$, $v\in V$}.

Given an algebra automorphism
\sigma
of
H
, one can twist the
H
-action of any
H
-module
V
to obtain another
H
-module
\!{}_{\sigma}V
. More precisely,
\!{}_{\sigma}V=V
as vector spaces with the
\sigma
-twisted action given by

h\cdot_{\sigma}v=\sigma(h)v,\quad\text{for all $h\in H$, $v\in V$.}

It is immediate to see that
\!{}_{\sigma}(\cdot)
defines a
\Bbbk
-linear equivalence on the category
H
-mod of left
H
-modules. Moreover, the natural isomorphism between
V
and
V^{**}
of finite-dimensional vector spaces defines an isomorphism
\!{}_{S^{2}}V\cong V^{**}
of
\Bbbk
-linear monoidal functors.

We denote by
\operatorname{Irr}(H)
a complete set of nonisomorphic simple
H
-modules. For any
V\in\operatorname{Irr}(H)
, the
projective cover
of
V
is denoted by
P(V)
. Since
H
is a Frobenius algebra
[
15
]
, we have (see
[
4
, 61.13]
)

(2.1)

\dim H=\sum_{V\in\operatorname{Irr}(H)}\dim V\cdot\dim P(V).

For any
H
-module
M
and
V\in\text{Irr}(H)
, we define
[M:V]
to be the multiplicity of
V
appearing as a composition factor of
M
. Let
V,W\in\text{Irr}(H)
. Since
V^{*}\otimes P(W)
is projective, it is a direct sum of indecomposable projective
H
-modules. More precisely,

\displaystyle V^{*}\otimes P(W)=

\displaystyle\bigoplus_{U\in\mathrm{Irr}(H)}\dim\left(\operatorname{Hom}_{H}(V^{*}\otimes P(W),U)\right)\cdot P(U)

(2.2)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bigoplus_{U\in\mathrm{Irr}(H)}[V\otimes U:W]\cdot P(U).

Recall that a
group-like element
in a Hopf algebra
H
is a nonzero element
g
such that
\Delta(g)=g\otimes g
. The group-like elements in
H
form a subgroup of the group of units in
H
, denoted by
G(H)
. Note that the set of 1-dimensional characters of
H
coincides with the set of group-like elements of the dual Hopf algebra
H^{*}
, which means
G(H^{*})=\operatorname{Hom}_{\rm Alg}(H,\Bbbk)
. For any
\beta\in G(H^{*})
, we denote by
\Bbbk_{\beta}
the corresponding
H
-module with the underlying
\Bbbk
-linear space equal to
\Bbbk
. In particular, the trivial
H
-module determined by the counit map
\epsilon:H\rightarrow\Bbbk
will simply be denoted by
\Bbbk
. Moreover, we define an injective group homomorphism
R:G(H^{*})\rightarrow{\rm Aut}_{\rm Alg}(H)
such that

(2.3)

R(\beta)(h):=h\leftharpoonup\beta=\sum_{(h)}\beta(h_{1})\,h_{2},\text{ for all $\beta\in G(H^{*})$ and $h\in H$.}

For any
H
-module
V
, one can check directly that

(2.4)

\Bbbk_{\beta}\otimes V\cong\!_{R(\beta)}V\quad\text{as $H$-modules}.

An element
x\in H
is called
skew primitive
if
\Delta(x)=x\otimes g+h\otimes x
for some group-like elements
g
and
h
in
H
. In this case,
x
is also called a
(g,h)
-primitive element
. An
(1,1)
-primitive element is simply called a
primitive element
. For any group-like elements
g,h
of
H
, we use
P_{g,h}(H)
to denote the subspace of all
(g,h)
-primitive elements in
H
. In particular, the subspace
P(H)=P_{1,1}(H)
of primitive elements of
H
is closed under the commutator bracket, and hence a Lie subalgebra of
H
. Moreover, if the base field
\Bbbk
has characteristic
p
, then
P(H)
is a
p
-restricted Lie algebra
, where the restricted map
[p]:P(H)\rightarrow P(H)
is given by the
p
th power map in
H
(see
[
14
]
).

The sum of simple subcoalgebras of a coalgebra
C
is called the
coradical
of
C
and denoted by
C_{0}
. A Hopf algebra is called
pointed
if
H_{0}=\Bbbk[G(H)]
, and called
connected
if
H_{0}=\Bbbk 1_{H}
. A finite-dimensional Hopf algebra
H
is often called
basic
if
H^{*}
is pointed. In particular,
H
is
local
, which means
H^{+}
is the Jacobson radical of
H
, if and only if
H^{*}
is connected. The endomorphism ring
\operatorname{End}_{H}(V)
of any indecomposable
H
-module
V
is a local ring (see
[
1
,
29
]
).
The following trivial lemma is a reminder of these terminologies.

Lemma 2.1
.

[
40
, Lem. 2.2]

The following statements concerning a finite-dimensional Hopf algebra
H
with counit
\epsilon
are equivalent.

(i)

H
is local;

(ii)

{\rm ker}\,\epsilon
is a nilpotent ideal;

(iii)

{\rm ker}\,\epsilon
is the Jacobson radical of
H
;

(iv)

H^{*}
is connected.

Moreover,
H
is basic if, and only if,
H^{*}
is pointed.

Finally, we recall some basic notations on extensions of finite-dimensional Hopf algebras. In the sense of
[
18
, Definition 1.3]
, a short exact sequence of finite-dimensional Hopf algebras
K
,
A
and
H
, denoted by

(2.5)

1\rightarrow K\xrightarrow{\iota}A\xrightarrow{\pi}H\rightarrow 1,

consists of Hopf algebra homomorphisms
\iota
and
\pi
such that
\iota
is injective,
\pi
is surjective,
\iota(K)
is a normal Hopf subalgebra of
A
and
{\rm ker}\,\pi=\iota(K^{+})A
(see
[
18
, Lem.-Definition 1.1]
). In this case, we say that
A
is an
extension
of
H
by
K
.

Lemma 2.2
.

For a short exact sequence of finite-dimensional Hopf algebras described in (
2.5
), the following hold true.

(i)

Suppose
K
is local. Then
\operatorname{Irr}(H)=\operatorname{Irr}(A)
, and so
H
is basic if and only if
A
is basic.

(ii)

Suppose
H
is connected. Then,
\operatorname{Irr}(K^{*})=\operatorname{Irr}(A^{*})
, and so
K
is pointed if and only if
A
is pointed.

In particular, we have

(iii)

A
is local if and only if both
K
and
H
are local.

(iv)

A
is connected if and only if both
K
and
H
are connected.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we may assume
\iota
is just the inclusion map and so
K
is
a normal Hopf subalgebra of
A
.

(i) Since
K
is local,
K^{+}
is nilpotent. The normality of
K
in
A
implies that
I:={\rm ker}\,\pi=K^{+}A=AK^{+}
is also a nilpotent ideal of
A
. Therefore, simple
A
-modules are simple modules over
A/I\cong H
. Hence the result follows by definition.

(iii) If
H
and
K
are local, then
A
is local by the proof of (i). Now suppose
A
is local. Then
A^{+}
is nilpotent, and so is
K^{+}
. Since
H^{+}=\pi(A^{+})
,
H^{+}
is also nilpotent. So
K
and
H
are local by Lemma
2.1
.

(ii) and (iv) follow directly from (i) and (iii) by duality.
∎

3.
Hopf algebras of dimension
p^{n}
with
p^{n-1}
group-like elements

In this section, we discuss the pointedness of finite-dimensional Hopf algebras with large group-like elements. Though some of our results may apply to arbitrary characteristics, we mainly focus on the positive characteristic case.

For any module
V
over a finite-dimensional Hopf algebra
H
, we define

\operatorname{stab}(V)~=~\{\beta\in G(H^{*})\mid\Bbbk_{\beta}\otimes V\cong V\text{ as $H$-modules}\}.

Obviously,
\operatorname{stab}(V)
is a subgroup of
G(H^{*})
.
The following result was established over a base field
\Bbbk
of characteristic zero in
[
7
, Prop. 2.5]
and
[
25
, Lem. 1.4]
. We extend its proof to positive characteristics by applying group cohomology for subgroups of
\operatorname{stab}(V)
for any simple
H
-module
V
. We first observe the following cohomology vanishing lemma.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\Bbbk
be an algebraically closed field of characteristic
p>0
, and
G
a
p
-group. For any finite-dimensional commutative semisimple algebra
A
over
\Bbbk
on which
G
acts as algebra automorphisms,
H^{2}(G,A^{\times})
is trivial.

Proof.

For any
\sigma\in Z^{2}(G,A^{\times})
,
\sigma^{|G|}
is a coboundary by
[
42
, Thm. 6.5.8]
, and so
\sigma^{|G|}=\partial f
for some function
f:G\rightarrow A^{\times}
. Since
A
is commutative semisimple,
A=\oplus_{i=1}^{n}\Bbbk\,e_{i}
where
\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{n}\}
is the complete set of primitive orthogonal idempotents for
A
. Note that for any
a\in A
, the polynomial equation
X^{|G|}-a=0
has a unique solution in
A
since
\Bbbk
has characteristic
p
and
G
is a
p
-group. Therefore, there exists
\overline{f}:G\rightarrow A^{\times}
such that
\overline{f}^{|G|}=f
. Thus,
\left(\sigma/\partial\overline{f}\right)^{|G|}=1
or
\sigma=\partial\overline{f}
. Hence,
H^{2}(G,A^{\times})
is trivial.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
H
be a finite-dimensional Hopf algebra over an algebraically closed field
\Bbbk
, and
V
a simple
H
-module. Suppose there is a subgroup
G\leq\operatorname{stab}(V)
such that
H^{2}(G,\Bbbk^{\times})
is trivial. Then
V
admits a Hopf module structure in
{{}_{\Bbbk[G]}}\!\mathcal{M}^{H^{*}}
, and hence is a free
\Bbbk[G]
-module. In particular, if
(i)

{\rm char}(\Bbbk)=0
and
G
is a cyclic group , or
(ii)

{\rm char}(\Bbbk)=p
and
G
is a
p
-group, then
|G|\mid\dim(V)
.

Proof.

In view of (
2.4
), we have
H
-module isomorphisms

{{}_{R(\beta)}}\!V\cong\Bbbk_{\beta}\otimes V\cong V\quad\text{for }\beta\in G\,.

Let
F_{\beta}:{{}_{R(\beta)}}\!V\rightarrow V
be such an isomorphism of
H
-modules for each
\beta\in G
, which are not canonical. Since
R(\epsilon)=\operatorname{id}_{H}
, we choose
F_{\epsilon}=\operatorname{id}_{V}
. Note that
F_{\beta}\in\operatorname{GL}(V)
and
{{}_{R(\beta)}}\!({{}_{R(\beta^{\prime})}}\!V)={{}_{R(\beta\beta^{\prime})}}\!V
(or
\Bbbk_{\beta}\otimes\Bbbk_{\beta^{\prime}}\cong\Bbbk_{\beta\beta^{\prime}}
) for any
\beta,\beta^{\prime}\in G
. We find
F_{\beta}\circ F_{\beta^{\prime}}
and
F_{\beta\beta^{\prime}}:{{}_{R(\beta\beta^{\prime})}}\!V\rightarrow V
are
H
-module isomorphisms. By Schur’s lemma,

F_{\beta}\circ F_{\beta^{\prime}}=\gamma(\beta,\beta^{\prime})F_{\beta\beta^{\prime}}

for some
\gamma(\beta,\beta^{\prime})\in\Bbbk^{\times}
.
The associativity of composition

(F_{\beta}\circ F_{\beta^{\prime}})\circ F_{\beta^{\prime\prime}}=F_{\beta}\circ(F_{\beta^{\prime}}\circ F_{\beta^{\prime\prime}}),

implies
\gamma(\beta^{\prime},\beta^{\prime\prime})\gamma(\beta,\beta^{\prime}\beta^{\prime\prime})=\gamma(\beta\beta^{\prime},\beta^{\prime\prime})\gamma(\beta,\beta^{\prime})
for any
\beta,\beta^{\prime},\beta^{\prime\prime}\in G
. Since
F_{\epsilon}=\operatorname{id}_{V}
,
\gamma(\beta,\epsilon)=\gamma(\epsilon,\beta)=1
. Therefore,
\gamma
defines a normalized 2-cocycle in
Z^{2}(G,\Bbbk^{\times})
. Since
H^{2}(G,\Bbbk^{\times})
is trivial,
\gamma
is a coboundary, namely, there exists a map
f:G\rightarrow\Bbbk^{\times}
such that
\gamma(\beta,\beta^{\prime})=f(\beta)f(\beta^{\prime})/f(\beta\beta^{\prime})
with
f(\epsilon)=1
for all
\beta,\beta^{\prime}\in G
. Now we define the
\Bbbk
-linear map
\eta:\Bbbk[G]\rightarrow\operatorname{End}_{\Bbbk}(V)
by

\displaystyle\eta(\beta)=F_{\beta}/f(\beta)\quad\text{for any}\ \beta\in G.

By the definitions of
\gamma
and
f
, it is straightforward to check that
\eta
is indeed an
\Bbbk
-algebra homomorphism. Therefore,
V
is a left
\Bbbk[G]
-module via
\eta
, i.e.,
\beta v=\eta(\beta)(v)
for any
v\in V
and
\beta\in G
.

Next, the left
H
-action on
V
defines a right
H^{*}
-coaction
\rho
on
V
, namely
hv=\sum_{(v)}v_{1}(h)v_{0}
for
h\in H
and
v\in V
, where
\rho(v)=\sum_{(v)}v_{0}\otimes v_{1}\in V\otimes H^{*}
. This makes
V
simultaneously a left
\Bbbk[G]
-module and a right
H^{*}
-comodule. We claim that
V
is a Hopf module in
{{}_{\Bbbk[G]}}\!\mathcal{M}^{H^{*}}
, and hence by the Nichols-Zoeller Theorem (cf.
[
23
]
),
V
is a free
\Bbbk[G]
-module. It suffices to show that

\sum_{(\beta v)}(\beta v)_{0}\otimes(\beta v)_{1}=\sum_{(v)}\beta v_{0}\otimes\beta v_{1}\quad\text{or}\quad\sum_{(\beta v)}(\beta v)_{1}(h)(\beta v)_{0}=\sum_{(v)}(\beta v_{1})(h)\beta v_{0}

for all
\beta\in G
,
v\in V
and
h\in H
. Note that
\eta(\beta)
is an
H
-module isomorphism from
\!{}_{R(\beta)}V
to
V
. So our claim follows from this calculation:

\displaystyle\sum_{(\beta v)}(\beta v)_{1}(h)(\beta v)_{0}

\displaystyle=h(\beta v)=\beta\left(R(\beta)(h)v\right)=\beta\left(\sum_{(v)}v_{1}(R(\beta)(h))v_{0}\right)

\displaystyle=\beta\left(\sum_{(v)}v_{1}\left(\sum_{(h)}\beta(h_{1})h_{2}\right)v_{0}\right)=\sum_{(v)}\left(\sum_{(h)}\beta(h_{1})v_{1}(h_{2})\right)\beta v_{0}

\displaystyle=\sum_{(v)}(\beta v_{1})(h)(\beta v_{0}).

If
G
is a cyclic group and
{\rm char}(\Bbbk)=0
,
H^{2}(G,\Bbbk^{\times})
is well-known to be trivial (cf.
[
42
, Thm. 6.2.2]
). If
{\rm char}(\Bbbk)=p
and
G
is a
p
-group, then
H^{2}(G,\Bbbk^{\times})
is trivial by Lemma
3.1
. Now, the final assertion follows directly from the first statement.
∎

Our next lemma is derived from the proof of
[
26
, Lem. 1.3]
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
\Bbbk
be an algebraically closed field of characteristic
p>0
,
H
a nonsemisimple Hopf algebra of dimension

over
\Bbbk
for some integer

, and

an indecomposable projective
H
-module. Suppose
P\cong\Bbbk_{\beta}\otimes P^{**}
for some
\beta\in G(H^{*})
. Then,

(i)

\dim P
is a positive even integer if
p=2
;

(ii)

\dim P\geq p
if
\dim P
and
p
are odd.

Proof.

Note that
\Bbbk_{\beta}\otimes P^{**}\cong\!_{\sigma}P
, where
\sigma=R(\beta)\circ S^{2}=S^{2}\circ R(\beta)
. So there exists an
H
-module isomorphism
\phi:P\rightarrow\!_{\sigma}P
. By the celebrated formula of Radford
[
30
]
,
S^{4p^{n}}=\operatorname{id}_{H}
. Hence
\sigma^{2p^{n}}=R(\beta)^{2p^{n}}\circ S^{4p^{n}}=R(\beta^{2p^{n}})\circ S^{4p^{n}}=\operatorname{id}_{H}
. Thus,
\phi^{2p^{n}}
is an
H
-module automorphism of
P
. Since
P
is indecomposable,
\operatorname{End}_{H}(P)
is a local
\Bbbk
-algebra and so
\phi^{2p^{n}}-c\operatorname{id}_{P}
is nilpotent for some
c\in\Bbbk^{\times}
. By rescaling, we can assume that both
\phi^{2p^{n}}
and
\phi^{2}
are unipotent. Thus, the eigenvalues of
\phi
are
\pm 1
if
{\rm char}(\Bbbk)=p>2
, and are
1
if
p=2
.

We claim
\operatorname{tr}(\phi)=0
. If
\beta
is trivial, it follows from
[
26
, Lem. 1.1]
. If
\beta
is nontrivial, it follows from
[
25
, Lem. 1.2]
. Thus, if
p=2
,
\operatorname{tr}(\phi)=0
implies
\dim P
is a positive multiple of 2.

Now, we assume
p>2
and
\dim P
is odd. Let
d_{\pm}
be the multiplicities of the eigenvalues
\pm 1
for
\phi
, respectively. Since

0=\operatorname{tr}(\phi)=d_{+}-d_{-},

|d_{+}-d_{-}|=mp
for some nonnegative integer
m
. If
m=0
, then
\dim(P)=2d_{+}
, which is even. Since
\dim(P)
is odd,
m\geq 1
and hence
\dim(P)=d_{+}+d_{-}\geq p
.
∎

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
\Bbbk
be an algebraically closed field of characteristic
p>0
and
H
a nonsemisimple Hopf algebra of dimension
p^{n}
over
\Bbbk
for some integer
n\geq 1
. If
|G(H^{*})|=p^{n-1}
, then
H^{*}
is pointed.

Proof.

We first consider
p=2
. Suppose
H^{*}
is not pointed. Then the sum of all the simple subcoalgebras of
H^{*}
with dimensions
>1
is a nontrivial subcoalgebra
C
of
H^{*}
. Note that
C
is a free
\Bbbk[G(H^{*})]
-module since it is a
{}_{\Bbbk[G(H^{*})]}\mathcal{M}^{H^{*}}
Hopf module. Thus,

\dim(H_{0}^{*})=|G(H^{*})|+\dim C\geq 2|G(H^{*})|=2^{n}=\dim H^{*}\,.

Hence
H^{*}
is cosemisimple, or
H
is semisimple. This proves the statement for
p=2
.

Now, we assume
p>2
. By Lemma
2.1
, it suffices to show that
\operatorname{Irr}(H)=\{\Bbbk_{\beta}\,|\,\beta\in G(H^{*})\}
. It is easy to see that
P(\Bbbk_{\beta}\otimes V)\cong\Bbbk_{\beta}\otimes P(V)
and
P(V^{**})\cong P(V)^{**}
for any
V\in\operatorname{Irr}(H)
. In particular,
\dim P(\Bbbk)=\dim P(\Bbbk_{\beta})
for all
\beta\in G(H^{*})
.

If
\dim P(\Bbbk)
is odd, then
\dim P(\Bbbk)\geq p
by Lemma
3.3
. So (
2.1
) implies that

p^{n}=\dim H\geq\sum_{\beta\in G(H^{*})}\dim\Bbbk_{\beta}\cdot\dim P(\Bbbk_{\beta})\geq p^{n-1}p\,.

Hence,
\dim P(\Bbbk)=p
and
\operatorname{Irr}(H)=\{\Bbbk_{\beta}\,|\,\beta\in G(H^{*})\}
.

To complete the proof of the theorem, it suffices to show that
\dim P(\Bbbk)
must be odd. Let
K=\langle S^{2}\rangle\subset{\rm Aut}_{\Bbbk}(H)
. Since
S^{4p^{n}}=\operatorname{id}_{H}
, we have
|K|
divides
2p^{n}
. For
\beta\in G(H^{*})
,
R(\beta)
is an algebra automorphism of
H
via (
2.3
). Thus, both
G(H^{*})
and
K
act on
\operatorname{Irr}(H)
by twisting the
H
-actions on irreducible modules through their associated algebra automorphisms of
H
.

Since
R(\beta)\circ S^{2}=S^{2}\circ R(\beta)
for any
\beta\in G(H^{*})
, the above two actions commute with each other. Thus, the set of
K
-orbits in
\operatorname{Irr}(H)
admits a
G(H^{*})
-action induced by its action of
\operatorname{Irr}(H)
. Let
\mathcal{O}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{O}_{r}
be the
K
-orbits, and
V_{1},\ldots,V_{r}
be representative irreducible
H
-modules in these
K
-orbits, respectively. Then (
2.1
) implies that

(3.1)

p^{n}=\dim H=\sum_{i=1}^{r}|\mathcal{O}_{i}|\,\dim(V_{i})\,\dim P(V_{i}).

Since
\dim H
is odd, there exists some index
i_{0}
such that
|\mathcal{O}_{i_{0}}|
,
\dim V_{i_{0}}
and
\dim P(V_{i_{0}})
are all odd. Without loss of generality, we may assume
i_{0}=1
.

If we could show that
\dim V_{1}=1
, then
V_{1}\cong\Bbbk_{\beta}
for some
\beta\in G(H^{*})
, and hence
\dim P(\Bbbk)=\dim P(\Bbbk_{\beta})
is odd. Therefore, it suffices to show that
\dim V_{1}=1
.

Since
|\mathcal{O}_{1}|
is odd and
|\mathcal{O}_{1}|\mid|K|\mid 2p^{n}
,
|\mathcal{O}_{1}|=p^{k}
for some nonnegative integer
k\leq n
. Let

G=\{\beta\in G(H^{*})\mid\Bbbk_{\beta}\otimes V_{1}\in\mathcal{O}_{1}\}

and
G_{1}
be the stabilizer of
V_{1}
. Obviously,
G_{1}\subseteq G
, and
|G_{1}|\mid\dim(V_{1})
by Lemma
3.2
.
Since the
K
-action and the
G(H^{*})
-action on
\operatorname{Irr}(H)
commute,
\mathcal{O}_{1}
is closed under the action of
G
and
K
acts transitively on the set of
G
-obits in
\mathcal{O}_{1}
. Consequently, all the
G
-orbits in
\mathcal{O}_{1}
have the same size
\frac{|G|}{|G_{1}|}
and we have

(3.2)

\ell\cdot\frac{|G|}{|G_{1}|}=|\mathcal{O}_{1}|=p^{k},

where
\ell
is the number of
G
-orbits in
\mathcal{O}_{1}
. In particular,
\ell
is a power of
p
.

Now suppose
\dim V_{1}>1
. Let
\{\beta\mathcal{O}_{1}\mid\beta\in G(H^{*})\}=\{\mathcal{O}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{O}_{t}\}
for some nonnegative integer
t\leq r
by relabelling the indices of the
K
-orbits. Then (
3.1
) and (
3.2
) imply that

\displaystyle p^{n}=\dim(H)

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{\beta\in G(H^{*})}\dim(\Bbbk_{\beta})\,\dim P(\Bbbk_{\beta})+\sum_{1\leq i\leq t}|\mathcal{O}_{i}|\,\dim(V_{i})\,\dim P(V_{i})

\displaystyle=|G(H^{*})|\dim P(\Bbbk)+\frac{|G(H^{*})|}{|G|}|\mathcal{O}_{1}|\,\dim(V_{1})\dim P(V_{1})

\displaystyle=p^{n-1}\left(\dim P(\Bbbk)+\ell\,\dim(P(V_{1}))\,\frac{\dim(V_{1})}{|G_{1}|}\right).

Therefore,

(3.3)

p=\dim P(\Bbbk)+\ell\,\dim(P(V_{1}))\,\frac{\dim(V_{1})}{|G_{1}|}>\ell\dim P(V_{1})

and hence
\ell<p
. It follows from
(
3.2
) that
\ell=1
, or
G
acts transitively on
\mathcal{O}_{1}
. Since both
V_{1}
and
V_{1}^{**}
are in the
K
-orbit
\mathcal{O}_{1}
, there exists
\beta\in G
such that
V_{1}^{**}\cong\Bbbk_{\beta}\otimes V_{1}
. Hence
\Bbbk_{\beta^{-1}}\otimes P(V_{1})^{**}\cong P(V_{1})
. It follows from Lemma
3.3
that
\dim P(V_{1})\geq p
, which contradicts (
3.3
). Therefore,
\dim V_{1}=1
.
∎

The dual statement of the preceding theorem is stated as follows.

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
\Bbbk
be an algebraically closed field of characteristic
p>0
and
H
a non-cosemisimple Hopf algebra of dimension
p^{n}
over
\Bbbk
for some integer
n\geq 1
. If
|G(H)|=p^{n-1}
, then
H
is pointed.

4.
Finite-dimensional Hopf algebras with primitive elements

In this section, we turn our attention to the existence of primitive elements in finite-dimensional Hopf algebras over an algebraically closed field
\Bbbk
. It is well-known that finite-dimensional Hopf algebras in characteristic zero do not possess nonzero primitive elements
[
34
, Prop. 1(b)]
. One may assume, a priori, all results about primitive elements are asserted over positive characteristics.

We first observe the following lemma regarding extensions of simple
H
-modules, and we simply denote
H^{+}
by
\mathfrak{m}
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
H
be a finite-dimensional Hopf algebra over an algebraically closed field
\Bbbk
, and
L
any simple
H
-module. Denote by
\mathfrak{n}=Ann(L)
the annihilator ideal of
L
in
H
. Then we have

\dim L\cdot\dim\operatorname{Ext}_{H}^{1}(L,\Bbbk)~=~\dim\left(\mathfrak{m}\cap\mathfrak{n}/\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{n}\right).

Proof.

Applying
\operatorname{Ext}_{H}^{1}(-,\Bbbk)
to the short exact sequence
0\rightarrow\mathfrak{n}\rightarrow H\rightarrow H/\mathfrak{n}\rightarrow 0
, we obtain the exact sequence

0\rightarrow\operatorname{Hom}_{H}(H/\mathfrak{n},\Bbbk)\rightarrow\operatorname{Hom}_{H}(H,\Bbbk)\rightarrow\operatorname{Hom}_{H}(\mathfrak{n},\Bbbk)\rightarrow\operatorname{Ext}_{H}^{1}(H/\mathfrak{n},\Bbbk)\rightarrow 0.

Note that
H/\mathfrak{n}=H/Ann(L)\cong\bigoplus_{\dim L}L
as
H
-modules. Also we have

\operatorname{Hom}_{H}(V,\Bbbk)~\cong~\operatorname{Hom}_{\Bbbk}(V/\mathfrak{m}V,\Bbbk)~\cong~(V/\mathfrak{m}V)^{*}

as vector spaces for any
H
-module
V
. Thus,

\displaystyle\dim L\cdot\dim\operatorname{Ext}_{H}^{1}(L,\Bbbk)\,=\,\dim\operatorname{Ext}_{H}^{1}(H/\mathfrak{n},\Bbbk)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\dim\operatorname{Hom}_{H}(H/\mathfrak{n},\Bbbk)+\dim\operatorname{Hom}_{H}(\mathfrak{n},\Bbbk)-\dim\operatorname{Hom}_{H}(H,\Bbbk)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\dim(H/(\mathfrak{m}+\mathfrak{n}))+\dim(\mathfrak{n}/\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{n})-\dim(H/\mathfrak{m})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\dim(\mathfrak{n}/\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{n})-\dim((\mathfrak{m}+\mathfrak{n})/\mathfrak{m})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\dim(\mathfrak{n}/\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{n})-\dim(\mathfrak{n}/\mathfrak{m}\cap\mathfrak{n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\dim(\mathfrak{m}\cap\mathfrak{n}/\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{n}).\qed

Note that if
L=\Bbbk
, the condition
\Bbbk
being algebraically closed in the preceding lemma can be dropped, which applies to the following two generally known lemmas (e.g., see

[
11
]
). We give their proofs for the sake of completeness.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
H
be a finite-dimensional Hopf algebra over any field
\Bbbk
. Then

\dim\operatorname{Ext}_{H}^{1}(\Bbbk,\Bbbk)~=~\dim(\mathfrak{m}/\mathfrak{m}^{2})~=~\dim P(H^{*}).

Proof.

The first equality in the statement follows from Lemma
4.1
. Next, consider the subcoalgebra
C=\pi^{*}(H/\mathfrak{m}^{2})^{*}
of
H^{*}
, where
\pi:H\rightarrow H/\mathfrak{m}^{2}
is the natural algebra surjection. For
f\in C
,
\Delta_{H^{*}}(f)(\mathfrak{m}\otimes\mathfrak{m})=f(\mathfrak{m}^{2})=0
.
This implies that
\Delta_{H^{*}}(f)\in\epsilon\otimes H^{*}+H^{*}\otimes\epsilon
.

Let
I=1^{\perp}\cap C
. Then
\dim I=\dim H-\dim\mathfrak{m}^{2}-1=\dim(\mathfrak{m}/\mathfrak{m}^{2})
. We will complete the proof by showing
I=P(H^{*})
. For
f\in I
,
\Delta_{H^{*}}(f)=\epsilon\otimes u+v\otimes\epsilon+c\epsilon\otimes\epsilon
for some
c\in\Bbbk
and
u,v\in I
. The counit property of
\Delta_{H^{*}}
implies
f=v+c\epsilon=u+c\epsilon
. Since
f,u,v\in 1^{\perp}
,
c=0
and
f=u=v
. Thus,
f\in P(H^{*})
and hence
I\subseteq P(H^{*})
. Conversely, if
f\in P(H^{*})\setminus\{0\}
, then
f(1)=0
and
\Delta_{H^{*}}(f)=\epsilon\otimes f+f\otimes\epsilon
. For any
x,y\in\mathfrak{m}
,
f(xy)=\Delta_{H^{*}}(f)(x\otimes y)=0
. Thus,
f\in I
and hence
P(H^{*})\subseteq I
.
∎

We recall the associated graded algebra

(4.1)

{\rm gr}_{\mathfrak{m}}H~:=~\bigoplus_{i=0}^{\infty}\mathfrak{m}^{i}/\mathfrak{m}^{i+1}

with respect to the
\mathfrak{m}
-adic filtration in
H
.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
H
be a finite-dimensional Hopf algebra over
\Bbbk
of characteristic
p>0
. Then
\bigcap_{i\geq 0}\mathfrak{m}^{i}
is a Hopf ideal of
H
with the quotient Hopf algebra
H/\bigcap_{i\geq 0}\mathfrak{m}^{i}
of dimension
p^{n}
for some
n\geq\dim P(H^{*})
. In particular, if
{\rm gr}_{\mathfrak{m}}H
is commutative, then so is
H/\bigcap_{i\geq 0}\mathfrak{m}^{i}
.

Proof.

The assertion that
\bigcap_{i\geq 0}\mathfrak{m}^{i}
is a Hopf ideal of
H
and that
{\rm gr}_{\mathfrak{m}}H
is a graded Hopf algebra follows directly from
[
11
, Lem. 3.3]
. The graded algebra
{\rm gr}_{\mathfrak{m}}H
is connected, cocommutative, and generated by degree one elements, which are primitive. Thus,
{\rm gr}_{\mathfrak{m}}H
is isomorphic to the restricted universal enveloping algebra
u(\mathfrak{g})
(cf.
[
37
, 13.2.3]
,
[
21
, Thm. 1]
), where
\mathfrak{g}=P({\rm gr}_{\mathfrak{m}}H)\supseteq\mathfrak{m}/\mathfrak{m}^{2}
. Hence we have

\dim H\big/\bigcap_{i\geq 0}\mathfrak{m}^{i}=\dim{\rm gr}_{\mathfrak{m}}H=p^{\dim\mathfrak{g}}\geq p^{\dim(\mathfrak{m}/\mathfrak{m}^{2})}=p^{\dim P(H^{*})}

by Lemma
4.2
. The last conclusion is
[
11
, Lem. 3.5]
.
∎

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
H
be a finite-dimensional Hopf algebra over an algebraically closed field
\Bbbk
of characteristic
p>0
, which contains a nonzero primitive element
x
. Suppose
C=\sum_{i}C_{i}
is a sum of simple subcoalgebras of
H
.

(i)

If
xC\subset C
, then any simple right
C
-comodule
M
admits a left
\Bbbk[x]
-action such that
M
is a Hopf module in
{}_{\Bbbk[x]}\!\mathcal{M}^{H}
. In particular,
\dim(\Bbbk[x])\mid\dim(M)
and hence
\dim(\Bbbk[x])^{2}\mid\dim(C_{i})
for all
i
.

(ii)

If
C
is simple and
p\nmid\dim(C)
, then
\Bbbk[x]C
is a free left
\Bbbk[x]
-module of rank
\dim(C)
.

Proof.

(i) Since
xC\subset C
, we can let
x
act on the dual algebra
C^{*}
via usual dual module action, namely
(xg)(c)=g(S(x)c)=-g(xc)
for any
c\in C,g\in C^{*}
. Furthermore, one can check that
x
acts as a
\Bbbk
-derivation on
C^{*}
for
x
is primitive. Since
C^{*}
is separable,
HH^{1}(C^{*},C^{*})
is trivial. Therefore,
x
acts as an inner derivation on
C^{*}
, which means there exists
f_{x}\in C^{*}
such that
xg=[f_{x},g]=\operatorname{ad}(f_{x})(g)
for all
g\in C^{*}
. Note that
f_{x}\in C^{*}
is uniquely determined by
x
up to the center of
C^{*}
. Since
C^{*}=\bigoplus_{i}C^{*}_{i}
as algebra,
f_{x}=\sum_{i}f_{i}
where
f_{i}\in C_{i}^{*}
, where
f_{i}
is uniquely determined up to a scalar multiple of
1_{C_{i}^{*}}
. By definition we obtain that
xc=\sum_{(c)}f_{x}(c_{2})c_{1}-f_{x}(c_{1})c_{2}=\sum_{(c)}f_{i}(c_{2})c_{1}-f_{i}(c_{1})c_{2}
for
c\in C_{i}
. In particular,
xC_{i}\subset C_{i}
for each
i
.

Suppose
M
is a simple right comodule of
C
. Then
M
is also a simple right comodule of
C_{i}
for some
i
, and hence a
C_{i}^{*}
-module given by

g\cdot m~=~\sum_{(m)}m_{0}\,g(m_{1})\,

for all
m\in M
and
g\in C_{i}^{*}
. We claim that one can choose a suitable
f_{i}\in C_{i}^{*}
to define an
\Bbbk[x]
-module action on
M
given by
x\cdot m:=f_{i}\cdot m
for
m\in M
. Since
\Bbbk[x]
is primitively generated, so
\Bbbk[x]\cong u(\mathfrak{g})
with
\mathfrak{g}={\rm span}_{\Bbbk}(x,x^{p},x^{p^{2}},\ldots)
. Thus
x
satisfies the following relation in
\Bbbk[x]

(4.2)

x^{p^{n}}+a_{n-1}x^{p^{n-1}}+\cdots+a_{1}x^{p}+a_{0}x=0

for some coefficients
a_{0},\ldots,a_{n-1}\in\Bbbk
. Then

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\operatorname{ad}(f_{x})^{p^{n}}+a_{n-1}\operatorname{ad}(f_{x})^{p^{n-1}}+\cdots+a_{1}(\operatorname{ad}f_{x})^{p}+a_{0}\operatorname{ad}(f_{x})

\displaystyle=\,\operatorname{ad}\left(f_{x}^{p^{n}}+a_{n-1}f_{x}^{p^{n-1}}+\cdots+a_{1}f_{x}^{p}+a_{0}f_{x}\,\right),

which is equivalent to

0=\operatorname{ad}\left(f_{j}^{p^{n}}+a_{n-1}f_{j}^{p^{n-1}}+\cdots+a_{1}f_{j}^{p}+a_{0}f_{j}\,\right)\quad\text{ for all }j.

This implies that
f_{j}^{p^{n}}+a_{n-1}f_{j}^{p^{n-1}}+\cdots+a_{1}f_{j}^{p}+a_{0}f_{j}=\lambda_{j}\,1_{C^{*}}
for some scalar
\lambda_{j}\in\Bbbk
. For each
j
, replacing
f_{j}
by
f_{j}+\eta_{j}\,1_{C_{j}^{*}}
with
\eta_{j}\in\Bbbk
satisfying
\eta_{j}^{p^{n}}+a_{n-1}\eta_{j}^{p^{n-1}}+\cdots+a_{1}\eta_{j}^{p}+a_{0}\eta_{j}=-\lambda_{j}
, we have
f_{x}
satisfy the same relation as
x
in (
4.2
). This makes the
\Bbbk[x]
-action:

x\cdot m=\sum_{m}m_{0}f_{i}(m_{1})=\sum_{m}m_{0}f_{x}(m_{1}),\quad\text{ for }m\in M,

well-defined on
M
.

With this
\Bbbk[x]
-module action on
M
, one can check directly that
M
is a Hopf module in
\!{}_{\Bbbk[x]}\mathcal{M}^{H}
. This amounts to show that

\sum_{(u\cdot m)}(u\cdot m)_{0}\otimes(u\cdot m)_{1}~=~\sum_{(u),\,(m)}u_{1}\cdot m_{0}\otimes u_{2}m_{1}

for any
u\in\Bbbk[x]
and
m\in M
. Since
\Bbbk[x]
is generated by
x
, it suffices to check the above equation for
u=x
. Indeed, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{(x),(m)}x_{1}\cdot m_{0}\otimes x_{2}m_{1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{(m)}x\cdot m_{0}\otimes m_{1}+m_{0}\otimes xm_{1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{(m)}m_{0}\otimes f_{x}(m_{1})m_{2}+m_{0}\otimes(f_{x}(m_{2})m_{1}-f_{x}(m_{1})m_{2})

\displaystyle=\sum_{(m)}m_{0}\otimes m_{1}f_{x}(m_{2})

\displaystyle=\sum_{(x\cdot m)}(x\cdot m)_{1}\otimes(x\cdot m)_{2}.

Therefore,
M
is a free
\Bbbk[x]
-module by the Nichols-Zoeller Theorem. So
\dim(\Bbbk[x])\mid\dim(M)
, and hence
\dim(\Bbbk[x])^{2}\mid\dim(M)^{2}=\dim(C_{i})
.

(ii) It follows from (i) that
xC\not\subset C
. Then, the comultiplication
\Delta
defines a natural nontrivial
C
-
C
-bicomodule structure on
(xC+C)/C
. Since every simple
C
-
C
-bicomodule has dimension
\dim(C)
and
\dim(xC+C)/C\leq\dim(C)
, we find
\dim(xC+C)/C=\dim(C)
. Hence
xC+C=xC\oplus C
. By repeating the argument, one can inductively show that

\Bbbk[x]\,C~=~\bigoplus\limits_{i=0}^{n}x^{i}\,C\quad\text{and}\quad\dim(x^{i}\,C)~=~\dim(C)

where
x^{i}C\not\subseteq C\oplus xC\oplus\cdots\oplus x^{i-1}C
for all
i\leq n
. Thus,
\dim(C)\mid\dim(\Bbbk[x]C)
. It is clear that
\Bbbk[x]C
is a Hopf module in
\!{}_{\Bbbk[x]}\mathcal{M}^{H}
, and so
\Bbbk[x]C
is a free
\Bbbk[x]
-module. In particular

\dim(\Bbbk[x])\mid\dim(\Bbbk[x]C)
. Therefore,
\dim(\Bbbk[x])\dim(C)\mid\dim(\Bbbk[x]C)\leq\dim(\Bbbk[x])\dim(C)
. Hence,
\dim(\Bbbk[x])\dim(C)=\dim(\Bbbk[x]C)
. Therefore, the
\Bbbk[x]C
is a free
\Bbbk[x]
-module of rank
\dim(C)
.
∎

Corollary 4.5
.

If
H
is a
p^{2}
-dimensional Hopf algebra over an algebraically closed field
\Bbbk
of characteristic
p>0
, which admits a nonzero primitive element
x
, then
\dim(H_{0})\leq p
.

Proof.

Let
K=\Bbbk[x]
. If
K=H
, then
H_{0}=\Bbbk
, and the statement holds vacuously. Suppose
K\neq H
. Then
\dim(K)=p
by Nichols-Zoeller Theorem since
K
is a Hopf subalgebra of
H
. Let
C
be any simple subcoalgebra of
H
. It is clear that
p\nmid\dim C
for
\dim H=p^{2}
. By Proposition
4.4
(ii),
\dim(KC)=p\dim(C)
. Note that

C\subseteq C+xC\subseteq C+xC+x^{2}C\subseteq\cdots\subseteq KC

is a coalgebra filtration on
KC
. So
[
27
, Lem. 5.3.4]
implies that
KC
is an irreducible subcoalgebra of
H
with
(KC)_{0}=C
(see
[
27
, Definition 5.6.1]
). Thus,

\sum_{C}KC~=~\bigoplus_{C}KC,

where the sum is over the set of all simple subcoalgebra
C
of
H
.
Hence

p^{2}=\dim(H)\geq\dim\left(\bigoplus_{C}KC\right)=p\left(\sum_{C}\dim C\right)=p\dim H_{0},

which yields that
p\geq\dim(H_{0})
.
∎

Proposition 4.6
.

Let
H
be a nonsemisimple
p^{n}
-dimensional Hopf algebra over an algebraically closed field
\Bbbk
of characteristic
p>2
. If
H

for all

\displaystyle p^{2}

In

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
\Bbbk
be an algebraically closed field of characteristic

, and

a nonsemisimple Hopf algebra of dimension

over
\Bbbk
such that

and

are not pointed. Then

and

are unimodular, and hence

-invariant 4-dimensional simple subcoalgebras.

Proof.

Since

is not semisimple and

is not pointed,

is trivial by Theorem
3.4
, and hence

is unimodular. We claim that

is also unimodular. It is true if

is semisimple. On the other hand, if

Now suppose

be the subalgebra of

generated by

, which is a noncocommutative Hopf subalgebra of

[
26
]

Note that the argument of
[

S(e_{21})&S(e_{22})\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}e_{22}&-\mathbf{i}e_{12}\\
\mathbf{i}e_{21}&e_{11}\end{pmatrix},

(5.6)

(5.7)

As a consequence,

over
\Bbbk

since

is not involutive. So

or

. We have two cases to consider according to the classification of

by Proposition

and so

for

∎

Lemma 5.3

The statement is obvious when

and

are not pointed. Then

is trivial by Theorem

In this section, we introduce the notion of twist equivalence for cleft extensions and crossed products. As an application, we classify certain cleft extensions, up to twist equivalence, over group algebras of elementary abelian
p
-groups and their duals over an algebraically closed field
\Bbbk
of characteristic

.
The readers are referred to
[

for definitions and results on cleft extensions.

Let
B

-comodule algebra and

-cleft extension

\gamma
is called a

. By
[

is a surjection of Hopf algebras, then

is an
H
-cleft extension. In particular, for the short exact sequence (

),

is an
H
-cleft extension of
\iota(K)
.

According to
[
27
, Thm. 7.2.2]
,
H
-cleft extensions can be characterized by crossed products, which we now recall. Let
H
be a Hopf algebra that
measures
an algebra
A

\Bbbk

, given by
h\otimes a\mapsto h\cdot a
such that
h\cdot 1=\epsilon(h)1
and
h\cdot(ab)=\sum_{(h)}(h_{1}\cdot a)(h_{2}\cdot b)
, for all
h\in H
and
a,b\in A
. In this paper, we simply call the triple
(H,A,\cdot)
a
measurement
. It is worth noting that
A
may not be an
H
-module algebra as the
H
-action on
A
may not be associative.

Given a measurement
(H,A,\cdot)
and a convolution invertible map
\sigma\in\operatorname{Hom}_{\Bbbk}(H\otimes H,A)
, one can construct the
crossed product

A\#_{\sigma}H
of
A
with
H
, which is the vector space
A\otimes H
equipped with multiplication given by

(6.1)

\displaystyle(a\#h)(b\#k)~=~\sum_{(h),(k)}a(h_{1}\cdot b)\sigma(h_{2},k_{1})\#h_{3}k_{2}

for all
h,k\in H
and
a,b\in A
. In this case, the associativity of (
6.1
) with identity element
1_{A}\#1_{H}
is equivalent to the fact that
A
is a
twisted

H
-module and
\sigma
is a 2-cocycle (see
[
27
, Lem. 7.1.2]
and the definitions therein) of the measurement
(H,A,\cdot)
. Note that the crossed product
A\#_{\sigma}H
canonically admits a left
A
-module and a right
H
-comodule structure. In particular,
A\#_{\sigma}H
is an
H
-cleft extension of
A
with the cleft map
\gamma:H\rightarrow A\#_{\sigma}H,\,h\mapsto 1_{A}\#h
, under the identification
A\equiv A\#1_{H}
(cf.
[
27
, Prop. 7.2.7]
).

Recall that one can twist a left
A
-module
M
by an algebra automorphism
\varphi
of
A
to
{}_{\varphi}\!M
. By duality, if
\phi
is a coalgebra automorphism of a coalgebra
C
, and
V
is a right
C
-comodule with the
C
-coaction
\rho:V\rightarrow V\otimes C
,
V^{\phi}
denotes the right
C
-comodule with the
C
-coaction given by
(\operatorname{id}_{V}\otimes\phi)\circ\rho
.

Definition 6.1
.

Let
H
be a Hopf algebra and
A
an algebra.

(i)

We say two
H
-cleft extensions
B
,
B^{\prime}
of
A
are
twist equivalent
, if there exist an algebra automorphism
\varphi
of
A
, a Hopf algebra automorphism
\phi
of
H
and
an algebra isomorphism
f:B\rightarrow\!_{\varphi}(B^{\prime})^{\phi}
that is a left
A
-module and a right
H
-comodule map. In this case,
f
is called a (
\phi,\varphi
)-
twist isomorphism
or simply a
twist isomorphism
of the
H
-cleft extensions.

(ii)

Two crossed products
A\#_{\sigma}H
and
A\#^{\prime}_{\sigma^{\prime}}H
are said to be
twist equivalent
if they are twist equivalent as
H
-cleft extensions of
A
.

(iii)

In particular, if
\varphi=\operatorname{id}_{A}
and
\phi=\operatorname{id}_{H}
, we will say an
equivalent
(resp.
isomorphism
) instead of twist equivalent (resp.
twist isomorphism
) of
H
-cleft extensions or crossed products as in (i) and (ii).

One can easily see that twist equivalence is an equivalence relation on the set of all
H
-cleft extensions of
A
.

The following result is a natural extension of
[
27
, Thm. 7.3.4]
to the context of twist equivalence. We include its proof for the sake of completeness.

Proposition 6.2
.

Let
(H,A,\cdot)
and
(H,A,\bullet)
be measurements,
\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}
be 2-cocycles of the measurements respectively, and
A\#_{\sigma}H
,
A\#^{\prime}_{\sigma^{\prime}}H
their corresponding crossed products. Suppose

\Phi:~A\#_{\sigma}H~\rightarrow~_{\varphi}(A\#^{\prime}_{\sigma^{\prime}}H)^{\phi}

is a
(\phi,\varphi)
-twist isomorphism for some algebra automorphism
\varphi
of
A
and some Hopf automorphism
\phi
of
H
. Then there exists a convolution invertible map
u\in\operatorname{Hom}_{\Bbbk}(H,A)
satisfying
u(1_{H})=1_{A}
with the convolution inverse
\overline{u}
such that for all
a\in A
,
h,k\in H
,

(i)

\Phi(a\#h)=\sum_{(h)}\varphi(au(h_{1}))\#^{\prime}\phi^{-1}(h_{2})
,

(ii)

h\bullet a=\varphi\left(\sum_{(h)}\overline{u}(\phi(h_{1}))\,\left(\phi(h_{2})\cdot\varphi^{-1}(a)\right)\,u(\phi(h_{3}))\right)
,

(iii)

\sigma^{\prime}(h,k)=m\left(\overline{u}(\phi(h_{1}))\#\phi(h_{2}))(\overline{u}(\phi(k_{1}))\#\phi(k_{2}))\right)
where the
\Bbbk
-linear map
m:A\#_{\sigma}H\rightarrow A
is given by
m(a\#h)=\varphi(au(h))
.

Conversely given a convolution invertible map
u\in\operatorname{Hom}_{\Bbbk}(H,A)
with
u(1_{H})=1_{A}
such that
(ii)
and
(iii)
hold, then the map
\Phi
in
(i)
is a
(\varphi,\phi)
-twist isomorphism.

Proof.

We define
u\in\operatorname{Hom}_{\Bbbk}(H,A)
by
u(h)=(\varphi^{-1}\otimes\epsilon)\Phi(1_{A}\#h)
for
h\in H
. Then
\varphi(u(h))=(\operatorname{id}\otimes\epsilon)\Phi(1_{A}\#h)
and
\Phi(a\#h)=\varphi(a)\Phi(1_{A}\#h)
as
\Phi
is a left
A
-module map. Since
\Phi
is a right
H
-comodule map, we have

(\operatorname{id}\otimes\Delta)\circ\Phi=(\Phi\otimes\phi^{-1})\circ(\operatorname{id}\otimes\Delta)\,.

By applying
(\operatorname{id}\otimes m_{H})\circ(\operatorname{id}\otimes\epsilon 1_{H}\otimes\operatorname{id})
to both sides of this equation, we get

\displaystyle\Phi(1\#h)

\displaystyle=\,(\operatorname{id}\otimes m_{H})\circ(\operatorname{id}\otimes\epsilon 1_{H}\otimes\operatorname{id})\left(\sum\Phi(1_{A}\#h_{1})\otimes\phi^{-1}(h_{2})\right)

\displaystyle=\,\sum(\operatorname{id}\otimes m_{H})(\varphi(u(h_{1}))\#^{\prime}1_{H}\otimes\phi^{-1}(h_{2}))

\displaystyle=\,\sum\varphi(u(h_{1}))\#^{\prime}\phi^{-1}(h_{2}),

which proves the formula in (i). Note that
\Phi(1_{A}\#1_{H})=1_{A}\#1_{H}
implies that
u(1_{H})=1_{A}
.

It is immediate to see that
\Phi^{-1}:A\#^{\prime}_{\sigma^{\prime}}H\rightarrow{}_{\varphi^{-1}}(A\#_{\sigma}H)^{\phi^{-1}}
is a
(\varphi^{-1},\phi^{-1})
-twist isomorphism. By (i), there exists
v\in\operatorname{Hom}_{\Bbbk}(H,A)
given by
v(h)=(\varphi\otimes\epsilon)\Phi^{-1}(1\#^{\prime}h)
such that
\Phi^{-1}(a\#^{\prime}h)=\sum\varphi^{-1}(av(h_{1}))\#\phi(h_{2})
. We claim that
\overline{u}=\varphi^{-1}\circ v\circ\phi
is the convolution inverse of
u
. This is because

\displaystyle 1_{A}\#h=\Phi^{-1}\Phi(1_{A}\#h)

\displaystyle=\Phi^{-1}\left(\sum\varphi(u(h_{1}))\#^{\prime}\phi^{-1}(h_{2})\right)

\displaystyle=\sum u(h_{1})\varphi^{-1}(v(\phi^{-1}(h_{2})))\#h_{3}.

By applying
\operatorname{id}_{A}\otimes\epsilon
to this equation, we see that
u*(\varphi^{-1}\circ v\circ\phi^{-1})=\epsilon 1_{A}
in
\operatorname{Hom}_{\Bbbk}(H,A)
. Similarly, we see
v*(\varphi\circ u\circ\phi)=\epsilon 1_{A}
, which is equivalent to
(\varphi^{-1}\circ v\circ\phi^{-1})*u=\epsilon 1_{A}
. Thus,
\overline{u}=\varphi^{-1}\circ v\circ\phi^{-1}
is the convolution inverse of
u
.

Now we consider the equation
\Phi^{-1}((1_{A}\#^{\prime}h)(1_{A}\#^{\prime}k))=\Phi^{-1}(1_{A}\#^{\prime}h)\Phi^{-1}(1_{A}\#^{\prime}k)
. We apply (
6.1
) to obtain

\displaystyle\sum\varphi^{-1}\left(\sigma^{\prime}(h_{1},k_{1})v(h_{2}k_{2})\right)\#\phi(h_{3}k_{3})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\varphi^{-1}(v(h_{1}))\left(\phi(h_{2})\cdot\varphi^{-1}(v(k_{1}))\right)\sigma(\phi(h_{3}),\phi(k_{2}))\#\phi(h_{4}k_{3}).

This proves (iii) after applying
m
to the equation.

Applying
\operatorname{id}\otimes\epsilon
to
\Phi^{-1}((1_{A}\#^{\prime}h)(b\#^{\prime}1_{H}))=\Phi^{-1}(1_{A}\#^{\prime}h)\Phi^{-1}(b\#1_{H})
or

\sum\varphi^{-1}\left((h_{1}\bullet b)v(h_{2})\right)\#\phi(h_{3})=\sum\varphi^{-1}(v(h_{1}))\left(\phi(h_{2})\cdot\varphi^{-1}(b)\right)\#\phi(h_{3}),

we find

\displaystyle\sum\varphi^{-1}(h_{1}\bullet b)\varphi^{-1}(v(h_{2}))=\varphi^{-1}(v(h_{1}))\left(\phi(h_{2})\cdot\varphi^{-1}(b)\right).

Note that
\overline{u}\circ\phi=\varphi^{-1}\circ v
and
\varphi^{-1}\circ v
is the convolution inverse of
u\circ\phi
. Inverting
\varphi^{-1}\circ v
gives (ii).

Finally, the converse follows by direct computation.
∎

Throughout the remainder of this section, we will assume that
H
is a cocommutative Hopf algebra and
A
is a commutative algebra. In this setting, a crossed product
A\#_{\sigma}H
is defined as an
H
-module algebra
A
equipped with a 2-cocycle
\sigma:H\otimes H\rightarrow A
, which can be placed within the context of Sweedler’s cohomology
H^{\bullet}_{sw}(H,A)
, as described in
[
36
]
. In particular, the measurement
(H,A,\cdot)
simply means an
H
-module algebra.

For any integer
n\geq 0
, denote by
{\rm Reg}^{n}(H,A)\subseteq\operatorname{Hom}_{\Bbbk}(H^{\otimes n},A)
the set of all convolution invertible linear maps, which forms an abelian subgroup under the convolution product. The coface operator
d^{i}:\operatorname{Reg}^{n}(H,A)\rightarrow\operatorname{Reg}^{n}(H,A)
is defined as follows:

d^{i}(f)(h_{0}\otimes\cdots\otimes h_{n})=\begin{cases}h_{0}f(h_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes h_{n})&\text{for}\ i=0,\\
f(h_{0}\otimes\cdots\otimes h_{i-1}h_{i}\otimes\cdots\otimes h_{n})&\text{for}\ 1\leq i\leq n,\\
f(h_{0}\otimes\cdots\otimes h_{n-1})\epsilon(h_{n})&\text{for}\ i=n+1\end{cases}

for any
f\in{\rm Reg}^{n}(H,A)
and
h_{0},\dots,h_{n}\in H
. This gives rise to a cochain complex
({\rm Reg}^{\bullet}(H,A),\partial)
whose differential
\partial
is defined by

\partial(f)=d^{0}(f)*d^{1}(f^{-1})*\cdots*d^{n+1}(f^{\pm 1})

for any
f\in{\rm Reg}^{n}(H,A)
where
*
denotes the convolution product and
f^{-1}
means the convolution inverse of
f
. The
Sweedler cohomology
H_{sw}^{\bullet}(H,A)
of
H
with coefficients in
A
is defined to be the cohomology of the above cochain complex. In particular,

H_{sw}^{n}(H,A):={\rm Ker}\,\partial^{n}/{\rm Im}\,\partial^{n-1},

for any
n\geq 0
under the convention
\partial^{-1}
is trivial.

As noted in
[
36
]
, one can use the normalized subcomplex
({\rm Reg}_{+}^{\bullet}(H,A),\partial)
of
({\rm Reg}^{\bullet}(H,A),\partial)
to compute
H_{sw}^{\bullet}(H,A)
, where

{\rm Reg}_{+}^{n}(H,A)
consists of those convolution invertible maps
f\in\operatorname{Hom}_{\Bbbk}(H^{\otimes n},A)
satisfying
f(x_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes x_{n})=\epsilon(x_{1}\cdots x_{n})1_{A}
whenever
1_{H}\in\{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\}
.
In particular,
\sigma\in{\rm Reg}_{+}^{2}(H,A)
is a 2-cocycle if and only if
d^{0}(\sigma)*d^{1}(\sigma^{-1})*d^{2}(\sigma)*d^{3}(\sigma^{-1})=\epsilon\otimes\epsilon\otimes\epsilon\,1_{A}
. Since
{\rm Reg}_{+}^{2}(H,A)
is an abelian group,
\sigma\in{\rm Reg}_{+}^{2}(H,A)
is a 2-cocycle if and only if

(6.2)

\displaystyle\sum_{(h),\,(k),\,(m)}h_{1}\cdot\sigma(k_{1},m_{1})\,\sigma(h_{2},k_{2}m_{2})=\sum_{(h),\,(k)}\sigma(h_{1},k_{1})\,\sigma(h_{2}k_{2},m)

for any
h,k,m\in H
.

The first assertion of our following result is well-known since Sweedler’s second cohomology classifies all cleft extensions up to equivalence (see
[
36
, Thm. 8.6]
).

Proposition 6.3
.

Let
H
be a cocommutative Hopf algebra and
A
a commutative
H
-mdoule algebra.

(i)

If
H_{sw}^{2}(H,A)
is trivial, then the crossed product
A\#_{\sigma}H
for any Sweedler 2-cocycle
\sigma
is equivalent to the smash product
A\#H
.

(ii)

Suppose
H_{sw}^{2}(H,A)
is trivial for any
H
-module algebra structure on
A
. Then every right
H
-cleft extension of
A
is equivalent to a smash product
A\#H
, where the
H
-module action on
A
is determined by a cleft map.

(iii)

Suppose
H_{sw}^{2}(H,A)
is trivial for any
H
-module algebra structure on
A
. For any two
H
-actions
\cdot,\bullet:H\otimes A\rightarrow A
, the corresponding smash products
A\#H
and
A\#^{\prime}H
are twist equivalent if and only if there is some algebra automorphism
\varphi
of
A
and some Hopf automorphism
\phi
of
H
such that

(6.3)

h\bullet a~=~\varphi\left(\phi(h)\cdot\varphi^{-1}(a)\right)\,

for all
h\in H
and
a\in A
.

Proof.

(i) Since
H^{2}_{sw}(H,A)
is trivial, the Sweedler 2-cocycle
\sigma:H\otimes H\rightarrow A
is a 2-coboundary, namely there is some
u\in\operatorname{Reg}^{1}_{+}(H,A)
such that
u(1_{H})=1_{A}
and

\displaystyle\sigma(h,k)~=~\sum\left(h_{1}\cdot u(k_{1})\right)\,\overline{u}(h_{2}k_{2})\,u(h_{3}),

where
\overline{u}
is the convolution inverse of
u
. Then by applying Proposition
6.2
in case of
\varphi,\phi=\operatorname{id}
, one can check that
f:A\#_{\sigma}H\rightarrow A\#H
defined by
f(a\#h)=\sum_{(h)}au(h_{1})\#h_{2}
with inverse
f^{-1}(a\#h)=\sum_{(h)}a\overline{u}(h_{1})\#h_{2}
for any
a\in A
and
h\in H
is an isomorphism of
H
-cleft extensions.

(ii) By
[
27
, Thm. 7.2.2]
, every
H
-cleft extension of
A
is equivalent to
A\#_{\sigma}H
as
H
-cleft extensions of
A
for some Sweedler’s 2-cocycle
\sigma\in\operatorname{Reg}^{2}_{+}(H,A)
of the
H
-module algebra
A
with its
H
-action determined by a cleft map. Since
H^{2}_{sw}(H,A)
is trivial, the statement follows from (i).

(iii) Suppose
\Phi:A\#H\rightarrow\!_{\varphi}(A\#^{\prime}H)^{\phi}
is a twist isomorphism of smash products for some algebra automorphism
\varphi
of
A
and some Hopf algebra automorphism
\phi
of
H
. By Theorem
6.2
, there is some
u\in\operatorname{Reg}^{1}_{+}(H,A)
with
u(1_{H})=1_{A}
and convolution inverse
\overline{u}
such that

h\bullet a=\varphi\left(\sum_{(h)}\overline{u}(\phi(h_{1}))\,\left(\phi(h_{2})\cdot\varphi^{-1}(a)\right)\,u(\phi(h_{3}))\right)=\varphi\left(\phi(h)\cdot\varphi^{-1}(a)\right),

where the last equality follows from the cocommutativity of
H
and the commutativity of
A
. Moreover, by letting
u=\epsilon 1_{A}
and
\sigma=\sigma^{\prime}=\epsilon 1_{A}\otimes\epsilon 1_{A}
, one can check (iii) in Theorem
6.2
holds. This implies our result.
∎

It was observed in
[
12
, Thm. 5.10]
and
[
8
, Prop. 5.7]
that
H^{2}_{sw}(\Bbbk[C_{p}^{n}]^{*},\Bbbk)
is trivial for any positive integer
n
. Our next result extends this vanishing property to nontrivial coefficients.

Theorem 6.4
.

Let
\Bbbk
be an algebraically closed field of characteristic
p>0
, and
K=\Bbbk[C_{p}^{n}]^{*}
for some positive integer
n
. For any finite-dimensional commutative
K
-module algebra
A
over
\Bbbk
,
H^{2}_{sw}(K,A)
is trivial.

Proof.

Note that
\Bbbk[C_{p}^{n}]^{*}
is a connected semisimple Hopf algebra. By the Hochschild theorem
[
13
]
, we have
\Bbbk[C_{p}^{n}]^{*}\cong\Bbbk[x]/(x^{p^{n}}-x)
as Hopf algebras, where
x
is primitive. Now, we simply assume
K=\Bbbk[x]/(x^{p^{n}}-x)
with
x
primitive. Suppose
\sigma\in{\rm Reg}_{+}^{2}(K,A)
. Then
\sigma(1\otimes 1)=1
and
\sigma(x^{i}\otimes 1)=\sigma(1\otimes x^{i})=0
for
i\geq 1
. We claim that there exists
f\in{\rm Reg}_{+}^{1}(K,A)
such that

(6.4)

\displaystyle(\sigma*\partial f^{-1})(x^{i}\otimes x)=0,

for
0\leq i\leq p^{n}-1
. We proceed with the proof of the above claim in several steps.

(i) We first find
f\in{\rm Reg}_{+}^{1}(K,A)
, where (
6.4
) holds for
0\leq i\leq p^{n}-2
. Note that (
6.4
) holds trivially for
i=0
and one can rewrite (
6.4
) as
(\sigma*d^{1}f)(x^{i}\otimes x)=(d^{0}f*d^{2}f)(x^{i}\otimes x)
or

(6.5)

f(x^{i+1})=-\sum_{j=1}^{i}{i\choose j}\,\sigma(x^{j}\otimes x)f(x^{i-j})+\sum_{j=0}^{i}\,{i\choose j}\,(x^{j}\cdot f(x))f(x^{i-j})

for
1\leq i\leq p^{n}-2
. For any
a\in A
, we can define
f(1)=1
,
f(x)=a
, and set
f(x^{i+1})
inductively by (
6.5
) for
1\leq i\leq p^{n}-2
. Then (
6.4
) holds for
0\leq i\leq p^{n}-2
.

(ii) By (i), we may assume
\sigma(x^{i}\otimes x)=0
for all
0\leq i\leq p^{n}-2
. Then we set
h=x,k=x^{p^{n}-1},m=x
in (
6.2
) to get

\displaystyle\sum_{i,l=0}^{1}\sum_{j=0}^{p^{n}-1}\binom{p^{n}-1}{j}(x^{i}\cdot\sigma(x^{j},x^{l}))\sigma(x^{1-i},x^{p^{n}-j-l})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{1}\sum_{j=0}^{p^{n}-1}\binom{p^{n}-1}{j}\sigma(x^{i},x^{j})\sigma(x^{p^{n}-i-j},x)\,,

which is equivalent to

x\cdot\sigma(x^{p^{n}-1},x)+\sigma(x,x)=\sigma(x,x)\,.

Therefore,
x\cdot\sigma(x^{p^{n}-1},x)=0
, namely,
\sigma(x^{p^{n}-1},x)\in A^{K}
.

(iii) Now for any
f\in{\rm Reg}_{+}^{1}(K,A^{K})
that satisfies (
6.4
) for
0\leq i\leq p^{n}-2
, (
6.5
) yields
f(x^{i+1})=f(x)^{i+1}
for
0\leq i\leq p^{n}-2
and when
i=p^{n}-1
it is equal to

f(x)=f(x^{p^{n}})=-\sigma(x^{p^{n}-1}\otimes x)+f(x)f(x^{p^{n}-1})=-\sigma(x^{p^{n}-1}\otimes x)+f(x)^{p^{n}}.

(iv) We show that the polynomial equation
F(X)=0
, where
F(X)=X^{p^{n}}-X-\sigma(x^{p^{n}-1},x)\in A^{K}[X]
has a solution in
A^{K}
. Since
A^{K}
is finite-dimensional commutative over an algebraically closed field
\Bbbk
, the equation
F(X)
has solution in
A^{K}/J(A^{K})=\Bbbk\times\cdots\times\Bbbk
, where
J(A^{K})
denotes the Jacobson radical of
A^{K}
. Note
A^{K}
is complete with respect to its
J(A^{K})
-adic filtration. Since
F^{\prime}(X)=-1\neq 0
in
A^{K}
, we conclude that
F(X)=0
has a solution in
A^{K}
by Hensel’s lifting lemma. We choose
\alpha\in A^{K}
as a solution that
F(\alpha)=0
.

(v) We construct
f\in{\rm Reg}_{+}^{1}(K,A^{K})
by setting
f(1)=1
,
f(x)=\alpha
and
f(x^{i+1})=f(x)^{i+1}
for
1\leq i\leq p^{n}-2
. By the previous discussion, it is straightforward to check that
(\sigma*\partial f^{-1})(x^{i},x)=0
for all
0\leq i\leq p^{n}-1
. This proves our claim.

Now up to a coboundary, we can assume that
\sigma(x^{t},x)=0
for all nonnegative integers
t
. By setting
h=x,k=x^{t},m=x
in (
6.2
) again, we find

\displaystyle\sum_{i,l=0}^{1}\sum_{j=0}^{t}\binom{t}{j}(x^{i}\cdot\sigma(x^{j},x^{l}))\sigma(x^{1-i},x^{t-j-l+1})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{1}\sum_{j=0}^{t}\binom{t}{j}\sigma(x^{i},x^{j})\sigma(x^{t-i-j+1},x)\,,

which implies
that
\sigma(x,x^{t+1})=0
. Therefore,
\sigma(x,m)=0
for all
m\in K
. Inductively, suppose
t
is a positive integer such that
\sigma(x^{j},m)=0
for all
m\in K
whenever
1\leq j\leq t
. By setting
h=x,k=x^{t}
in (
6.2
), we find

x\cdot\sigma(x^{t},m)=\sigma(x^{t+1},m)

for all
m\in K
. Therefore,
\sigma(x^{t+1},m)=0
for all
m\in K
. Thus,
\sigma
is trivial. This completes our proof.
∎

Remark 6.5
.

The assumption that
\Bbbk
is algebraically closed is essential for Theorem
6.4
. For instance, if
\Bbbk
is of characteristic
p=2
(not necessarily algebraically closed), then
[
12
, Thm. 1.1]
states that
H^{2}_{sw}(\Bbbk[C_{p}^{n}]^{*},\Bbbk)=(\Bbbk/\{x+x^{2}\,|\,x\in\Bbbk\})^{\oplus n}
.

An immediate application of Theorem
6.4
is the classification of certain cleft extensions up to twist equivalence over
\Bbbk[C_{p}^{n}]
and their duals.

Proposition 6.6
.

Let
\Bbbk
be an algebraically closed field of characteristic
p>0
, and
H=\Bbbk[C_{p}^{n}]^{*}
for some positive integer
n
. Then any
H
-cleft extension of a finite-dimensional commutative local algebra
A
over
\Bbbk
is twist equivalent to some smash product
A\#H
. Moreover, there are exactly 3 twist inequivalent smash products
A\#H
,
A\#^{\prime}H
, and
A\#^{\prime\prime}H
, and their
H
-actions on
A
are respectively given by

x\cdot t~=~0,\ t,\ t+1,

where
x
is a primitive generator of
H
satisfying
x^{p^{n}}=x
, and
t
is a nilpotent generator of
A
. The last action
x\cdot t=t+1
only occurs when
p\mid\dim A
.

Proof.

By Theorem
6.4
,
H_{sw}^{2}(H,A)
is trivial for any
H
-action on
A
. So it suffices to classify all possible
H
-actions on
A
, which yields twist inequivalent smash products
A\#H
by Proposition
6.3
. Again, by Hochschild’s theorem, we may assume
H=\Bbbk[x]
with some primitive generator
x
satisfying
x^{p^{n}}=x
. Thus any
H
-action on
A
is determined by the action of
x
on
A
as a
\Bbbk
-derivation satisfying
x^{p^{n}}=x
. Denote by
\mathbb{F}_{q}
the finite field of
q=p^{n}
elements. Since the operator
x
is semisimple, there is a decomposition
A=\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}A_{i}
where
A_{i}
is the eigenspace of
x
for the eigenvalue
i\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
. It is immediate to see
A_{i}A_{j}\subseteq A_{i+j}
for
i,j\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
.

Since
A
is a finite-dimensional local commutative algebra over
\Bbbk
,
A=\Bbbk[t]/(t^{m})
with
m=\dim A
and
t
a nilpotent generator of
A
. Then
t=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}t_{i}
where
t_{i}\in A_{i}
, and there exists
s\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
such that
t_{s}=\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}a_{i}t^{i}
with
a_{i}\in\Bbbk
and
a_{1}\neq 0
. Thus,
t_{s}=a_{0}+tu
for some invertible element
u\in A
, and hence
t_{s}^{i}\in t^{i}u^{i}+F_{i-1}
where
F_{\ell}
is the
\Bbbk
-linear span of
\{t^{0},\dots,t^{\ell}\}
. This implies

\{t_{s}^{0},\ldots,t_{s}^{m-1}\}
is a
\Bbbk
-basis for
A
and
1,t_{s}
generate
A
as
\Bbbk
-algebra.

If
s=0
, then
A=A_{0}
and so
x\cdot t=0
. Now, we assume
s\neq 0
. Recall that
t_{s}=a_{0}+tu
for some invertible element
u\in A
and
x\cdot t_{s}=st_{s}
. We first consider the case for
a_{0}\neq 0
and set
t^{\prime}=tu/a_{0}
. Then
x\cdot t^{\prime}=s(t^{\prime}+1)
. Consider the algebra automorphism
\varphi
of
A
defined by
\varphi(t)=t^{\prime}
and the Hopf algebra automorphism
\phi
of
H
defined by
\phi(x)=x/s
. Since
\varphi^{-1}(\phi(x)\cdot\varphi(t))=t+1
, the smash product in this is twist equivalent to
A\#^{\prime\prime}H
, with the
H
-action given by
x\cdot t=t+1
by (
6.3
). Since
x\cdot(t^{m})=mt^{m-1}(x\cdot t)
, it is easy to see that the action
x\cdot t=t+1
is not well-defined if
p\nmid m
.

Now we deal with the case for
a_{0}=0
. By considering the algebra automorphism
\varphi
of
A
defined by
\varphi(t)=tu
and the Hopf algebra automorphism
\phi
of
H
defined by
\phi(x)=x/s
, we can assume that
x\cdot t=t
up to twist equivalence of smash products by (
6.3
) again.

The smash product
A\#H
given by the trivial
H
-action is commutative, and
A\#^{\prime}H\cong\Bbbk\langle x,t\rangle/(x^{p^{n}}-x,t^{m},[x,t]=t)
and
A\#^{\prime\prime}H\cong\Bbbk\langle x,t\rangle/(x^{p^{n}}-x,t^{m},[x,t]=t+1)
as algebras. But the former has
p^{n}
1-dimensional characters while the latter has none. Therefore, they are twist inequivalent.
∎

Regarding the second
H
-action described in the preceding proposition, when

and

\Bbbk[C_{p}]
, as shown in the following example.

Example 6.7
.

[

, Ex. 4.1.10]

Let

be any finite-dimensional Hopf algebra. There is a left

-action
\rightharpoonup
on the dual Hopf algebra

given by

h\rightharpoonup f~=~\sum_{(h)}f_{2}(h)f_{1}

for all

. Under this

is a left

-module algebra. The corresponding smash product

is the

of

, denoted by

. Note that

is isomorphic to

) and it is an

.

Corollary 6.8
.

Let
\Bbbk
be an algebraically closed field
\Bbbk
of characteristic

and

. Then any

(1)

The tensor product algebra:
\Bbbk[C_{p}]\otimes\Bbbk[C_{p}]^{*}
.

(2)

The Heisenberg double:
\mathcal{H}(\Bbbk[C_{p}])

(3)

The smash product:
\Bbbk[C_{p}]\#\Bbbk[C_{p}]^{*}
, where the
\Bbbk[C_{p}]^{*}
-action on

x\cdot g=g-1
with

and

Proof.

Note that

as Hopf algebras, where

Our result follows from Theorem

since

.
∎

Proposition 6.9
.

Let
\Bbbk
be an algebraically closed field of characteristic
p>0
, and
H=\Bbbk[G]
for some elementary abelian
p
-group
G
. Then any
H
-cleft extension of the semisimple algebra
A=\Bbbk[x]/(x^{p}-x)
is twist equivalent to either
A\otimes H
or
\mathcal{H}(\Bbbk[C]^{*})\otimes\Bbbk[G^{\prime}]
for some subgroups
C,G^{\prime}
of
G
such that
C\cong C_{p}
and
C\oplus G^{\prime}=G
, where
\Bbbk[G]
is naturally identified with
\Bbbk[C]\otimes\Bbbk[G^{\prime}]
and
A
is identified with
\Bbbk[C]^{*}
. In particular, if
G=C_{p}
, then any
H
-cleft extension of
A=H^{*}
is twist equivalent to one of the smash products below:

(1)

The tensor product algebra:
\Bbbk[C_{p}]^{*}\otimes\Bbbk[C_{p}]
.

(2)

The Heisenberg double:
\mathcal{H}(\Bbbk[C_{p}]^{*})
.

Proof.

We write
A=\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\Bbbk\,e_{i}
where
\{e_{0},\ldots,e_{p-1}\}
is a complete set of primitive orthogonal idempotents of
A
and
x=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}}ie_{i}
. By
[
36
, Thm. 3.1]
, we have

H_{sw}^{2}(\Bbbk[G],A)=H^{2}(G,A^{\times}).

It follows from Lemma
3.1
that
H_{sw}^{2}(\Bbbk[G],A)
is trivial for any
G
-action on
A
.

Now, it suffices to classify all possible
\Bbbk[G]
-actions on
A
up to automorphisms of
A
and
H
by Proposition
6.3
. It is clear that
{\rm Aut}_{\Bbbk}(A)
is in one-to-one correspondence with the permutation group
S_{p}
of
\{e_{0},\ldots,e_{p-1}\}
. One can identify
\varphi\in{\rm Aut}_{\Bbbk}(A)
with the permutation in
S_{p}
, again denoted by
\varphi
, via
\varphi(e_{i})=e_{\varphi(i)}
. Hence any
\Bbbk[G]
-action on

is determined by a group homomorphism

. If
f
is trivial, then the
\Bbbk[G]
-action on
A
is trivial, so the smash product is
A\otimes H
. If
f
is nontrivial, then
{\rm Im}(f)
is a nontrivial elementary
p
-subgroup of
S_{p}
. Since

, every nontrivial
p
-subgroup of

is a Sylow
p
-subgroup isomorphic to
C_{p}
. In particular,
{\rm Im}(f)\cong C_{p}
. Hence
G^{\prime}=\ker(f)
is an index
p
subgroup of
G
, and there exists a cyclic subgroup
C\cong C_{p}
of
G
such that
G=C\oplus G^{\prime}
and

is injective.
Since
{\rm Im}(f)
is a Sylow
p
-subgroup of
S_{p}
, there is some automorphism
\varphi
of
A
(or
S_{p}
) such that
\varphi\circ f(g)\circ\varphi^{-1}
sends every

to
e_{i-1}
, where

is a generator of

. By (

), we can assume that
g\cdot e_{i}=e_{i-1}
for all

. Hence

\displaystyle g\cdot x=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}}i(g\cdot e_{i})=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}}(i-1)e_{i-1}+\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}}e_{i}=x+1.

Note the above action of

on

coincides with the natural action
\rightharpoonup
of
\Bbbk[C_{p}]
on

. Therefore, it gives the corresponding smash product

A\#H\cong A\#(\Bbbk[C]\otimes\Bbbk[G^{\prime}])\cong(A\#\Bbbk[C])\otimes\Bbbk[G^{\prime}]\cong\mathcal{H}(\Bbbk[C]^{*})\otimes\Bbbk[G^{\prime}].

Finally, it is clear that these smash products are not twist equivalent.
∎

7.
Short exact sequences of
p
-dimensional Hopf algebras

In the last section, we will provide a list of the Radford algebra
R(p)
characterizations and identify all
p^{2}
-dimensional Hopf algebra extensions of
p
-dimensional ones. In particular, these extensions are all pointed.

We first recall the classification of
p
-dimensional Hopf algebras over an algebraically closed field
\Bbbk
of characteristic
p
. There are only two nonisomorphic
p
-restricted Lie algebras of dimension 1 generated by
x
, and they are given by (i)
\mathfrak{a}_{1}
:
x^{p}=x
; (ii)
\mathfrak{a}_{2}
:
x^{p}=0
. Note that the restricted universal enveloping algebra
u(\mathfrak{a}_{1})=\Bbbk[x]/(x^{p}-x)
is isomorphic to the dual group algebra
\Bbbk[C_{p}]^{*}
.

Theorem 7.1
.

[
26
, Thm. 2.1]

Let
\Bbbk
be an algebraically closed field of characteristic
p>0
and
H
a
p
-dimensional Hopf algebra over
\Bbbk
. Then
H\cong\Bbbk[C_{p}]
,
u(\mathfrak{a}_{1})
or
u(\mathfrak{a}_{2})
.

Independent of the classification of
p^{2}
-dimensional pointed Hopf algebras, we provide some characterizations of the Radford algebra
R(p)
as the unique
p^{2}
-dimensional Hopf algebra over
\Bbbk
which satisfies any of the equivalent conditions in the following theorem.

Theorem 7.2
.

Let
\Bbbk
be an algebraically closed field of characteristic
p>0
, and
H
a
p^{2}
-dimensional Hopf algebra over
\Bbbk
. The following statements are equivalent:

(i)

H
is isomorphic to the Radford algebra
R(p)
;

(ii)

H
is noncommutative, pointed and
G(H)
is not trivial;

(iii)

H is noncommutative, and it admits a normal Hopf subalgebra isomorphic to
\Bbbk[C_{p}]
;

(iv)

H
is a nontrivial extension, i.e., not isomorphic to the tensor Hopf algebra
\Bbbk[C_{p}]\otimes\Bbbk[C_{p}]^{*}
, that fits into the exact sequence

(7.1)

1\rightarrow\Bbbk[C_{p}]\xrightarrow{\iota}H\xrightarrow{\pi}\Bbbk[C_{p}]^{*}\rightarrow 1.

In particular,
R(p)\cong R(p)^{*}
.

Proof.

It is clear that (i) implies (ii), (iii), and (iv). It remains to show the converse implications.

(ii)
\Rightarrow
(i) Suppose
H
is pointed and noncommutative with nontrivial
G(H)
. Then
G(H)\cong C_{p}
since
H
is noncommutative and
\dim H=p^{2}
. Therefore,
\Bbbk[G(H)]=H_{0}\subsetneq H_{1}
or
H
admits a nontrivial
(1,a)
-primitive element
x
for some
a\in G(H)
.

Let
g
be a generator of
G(H)
. Then
g,x
generate a Hopf subalgebra of
H
of dimension
>p
. Thus,
g,x
generate
H
as an algebra by the Nichols-Zoeller theorem. Since
\dim H=p^{2}
,
P_{1,a}(H)
is spanned by
x,a-1
and is a
G(H)
-module by conjugation. Let
N=\Bbbk(a-1)
, which is a trivial
G(H)
-submodule of
P_{1,a}(H)
. Note that
N
could be zero if
a=1
but
P_{1,g}(H)/N
is a nonzero trivial
G(H)
-module. Thus
gxg^{-1}=x+\alpha(a-1)
for some
\alpha\in\Bbbk
. If
\alpha(a-1)=0
, then
H
generated by
g,x
would be commutative. Therefore,
\alpha(a-1)\neq 0
which is equivalent to
\alpha\in\Bbbk^{\times}
and
G(H)=\langle a\rangle
. Without loss of generality, one can assume
a=g
and replace the
(1,g)
-primitive element
x
by
x/\alpha
to obtain the relation
[g,x]=g(g-1)
or
[x,g]=g-g^{2}
. In particular,
H_{0}
is normal in
H
. Since
[x,g^{j}]=jg^{j-1}[x,g]=j(g^{j}-g^{j+1})
for
0\leq j\leq p-1
, the set of eigenvalues of
\operatorname{ad}(x)|_{H_{0}}
is
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
and so the characteristic polynomial of
\operatorname{ad}(x)|_{H_{0}}
is
X^{p}-X
. Thus,
\operatorname{ad}(x^{p})|_{H_{0}}=\operatorname{ad}(x)^{p}|_{H_{0}}=\operatorname{ad}(x)|_{H_{0}}
. Thus,
x^{p}-x
lies in the center of
H
.

Let
L=H/(H^{+}_{0}H)
and
\pi:H\rightarrow L
be the natural surjection. We claim that
L\cong u(\mathfrak{a}_{1})
. It is clear that
L
is generated by the nonzero primitive element
\pi(x)
, and so
L\cong u(\mathfrak{a}_{i})
for
i=1
or
2
. If
L\cong u(\mathfrak{a}_{2})
, then
H
is local by Lemma
2.2
(iii). This implies that
x^{p^{2}}\in(H^{+})^{p^{2}}=0
, which contradicts to the fact that
\operatorname{ad}(x^{p^{2}})=\operatorname{ad}(x)^{p^{2}}=\operatorname{ad}(x)\neq 0
on
H_{0}
. Therefore,
L\cong u(\mathfrak{a}_{1})
.

Let
\overline{x}
be a primitive generator for
L
such that
\overline{x}^{p}=\overline{x}
. Since
\pi(x)
is a nonzero primitive element of
L
,
\beta\pi(x)=\overline{x}
for some
\beta\in\Bbbk^{\times}
and
\{x^{j}\mid j=0,\dots,p-1\}
is a
\Bbbk
-linearly independent subset in
H
. Define the
\Bbbk
-linear map
\gamma:L\rightarrow H
by
\gamma(\overline{x}^{i})=(\beta x)^{i}
for
0\leq i\leq p-1
. On can check directly that
\gamma
is a left
L
-comodule map with the convolution inverse
\overline{\gamma}:L\rightarrow H
given by
\overline{\gamma}(\overline{x}^{i})=(-\beta x)^{i}
for
0\leq i\leq p-1
. Thus
H
is spanned by
\{x^{i}g^{j}\mid i,j=0,\ldots,p-1\}
as
H
is a left
\Bbbk[C_{p}]^{*}
-cleft extension of
\Bbbk[C_{p}]
, and hence it forms a basis for
H
as
\dim H=p^{2}
.

Now, we can write
x^{p}-x=\sum_{0\leq j\leq p-1}f_{j}(x)g^{j}
for some unique polynomial
f_{j}(x)
in
x
of degree
\leq p-1
. We find

\displaystyle 0=[x,x^{p}-x]

\displaystyle=\sum_{0\leq j\leq p-1}f_{j}(x)[x,g]=\sum_{0\leq j\leq p-1}jf_{j}(x)(g^{j}-g^{j+1})

\displaystyle=f_{1}(x)g+\left(\sum_{j=1}^{p-2}((j+1)f_{j+1}(x)-jf_{j}(x))g^{j+1}\right)+f_{p-1}(x).

This implies
f_{j}(x)=0
for all
j=1,\dots,p-1
. Thus
x^{p}-x=f_{0}(x)
. Applying
\pi
to this equality, we find

(\beta^{-p}-\beta^{-1})\overline{x}=\beta^{-p}\overline{x}^{p}-\beta^{-1}\overline{x}=\pi(x^{p}-x)=\pi(f_{0}(x))=f_{0}(\beta^{-1}\overline{x})\,,

or equivalently,
h(\overline{x})=0
where
h(X)=f_{0}(\beta^{-1}X)-(\beta^{-p}-\beta^{-1})X\in\Bbbk[X]
has degree
\leq p-1
. Thus,
h(X)=0
or
f_{0}(X)=(\beta^{(1-p)}-1)X
. Now, we find

0=[x^{p}-x,g]=[(\beta^{(1-p)}-1)x,g]=(\beta^{(1-p)}-1)g(1-g)

which forces
\beta^{(1-p)}-1=0
. Hence
f_{0}(x)=0
or
x^{p}-x=0
in
H
. Therefore,
H
is isomorphic to the Radford algebra
R(p)
.

(iii)
\Rightarrow
(ii) Suppose
H
is noncommutative and admits a normal Hopf subalgebra
K\cong\Bbbk[C_{p}]
. In view of Theorem
3.4
, it suffices to show that
H^{*}
is nonsemisimple. We have an exact sequence of Hopf algebras:

1\rightarrow L^{*}\rightarrow H^{*}\rightarrow K^{*}\rightarrow 1,\quad\text{where }L=H/K^{+}H\,.

Suppose
H^{*}
is semisimple. Then so is
L^{*}
. It follows from Theorem
7.1
that
L^{*}\cong u(\mathfrak{a}_{1})
. Since
K^{*}
and
L^{*}
are connected, so is
H^{*}
by Lemma
2.2
(ii). However, this implies
H\cong\Bbbk[C_{p}\times C_{p}]
or
\Bbbk[C_{p^{2}}]
by
[
20
, Thm. 0.1]
. This contradicts the noncommutativity of
H
.

(iv)
\Rightarrow
(iii) Let
H
be a commutative Hopf algebra which fits into the exact sequence (
7.1
). It suffices to show that
H
is a trivial extension. Note that
H^{*}
satisfies the same exact sequence. Therefore, both
H
and
H^{*}
are nonsemisimple, so
H
and
H^{*}
are pointed by Theorem
3.4
. Then
H^{*}
is also commutative; otherwise,
H^{*}\cong R(p)
as Hopf algebras by (ii), which would imply
H
is noncommutative. Since
H_{0}\subsetneq H_{1}
, there exists a nontrivial primitive element
x\in H
and
\dim P(H)=1
. It is clear that
\Bbbk[x]
maps surjectively onto
\Bbbk[C_{p}]^{*}
and hence
\Bbbk[x]\cong\Bbbk[C_{p}]^{*}
as Hopf algebras. Since
H
is generated by
G(H)
and
x
as an algebra,

H\cong\Bbbk[G(H)]\otimes\Bbbk[x]\cong\Bbbk[C_{p}]\otimes\Bbbk[C_{p}]^{*}
as Hopf algebras.

The last assertion follows directly from the equivalence of (i) and (iv).
∎

Recently, Gelaki considered in
[
10
, Ex. 6.14]
the Drinfeld twist

(7.2)

J=\sum_{i=0}^{p-1}\frac{x(x-1)\cdots(x-i+1)\otimes y^{i}}{i!}

of the restricted universal enveloping algebra
u(\mathfrak{g}_{5})
of the 2-dimensional nonabelian
p
-restricted Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}_{5}
spanned by
x,y
such that
[x,y]=y
,
x^{p}=x
and
y^{p}=0
. It is pointed out that the corresponding twisted Hopf algebra
u(\mathfrak{g}_{5})^{J}
is noncommutative and noncocommutative of dimension
p^{2}
in characteristic

. Now, we show that this noncommutative and noncocommutative Hopf algebra is isomorphic to the Radford algebra
R(p)
due to the preceding theorem. The readers are referred to
[
17
]
for the notions of a Drinfeld twist
J
of a Hopf algebra
H
, the corresponding twisted Hopf algebra
H^{J}
, and gauge equivalence of Hopf algebras.

Corollary 7.3
.

Let
\Bbbk
be an algebraically closed field of characteristic
p>0
and
\mathfrak{g}_{5}
the 2-dimensional nonabelian
p
-restricted Lie algebra over
\Bbbk
described above. For any Drinfeld twist
J
of
u(\mathfrak{g}_{5})
such that
u(\mathfrak{g}_{5})^{J}
is noncocommutative,
u(\mathfrak{g}_{5})^{J}\cong R(p)
as Hopf algebras. In particular,
u(\mathfrak{g}_{5})
and
R(p)
are gauge equivalent.

Proof.

Note that
u(\mathfrak{g}_{5})=\Bbbk\langle x,y\rangle/(x^{p}-x,y^{p},[x,y]-y)
as Hopf algebras where
x,y
are primitive. Thus
(y)=u(\mathfrak{g}_{5})y
is a nilpotent ideal of
u(\mathfrak{g}_{5})
and
u(\mathfrak{g}_{5})/(y)\cong\Bbbk[x]/(x^{p}-x)
is semisimple and commutative. Therefore,
(y)
is the Jacobson radical of
u(\mathfrak{g}_{5})
and
u(\mathfrak{g}_{5})^{*}
is pointed with
|G(u(\mathfrak{g}_{5})^{*})|=p
. Let
J
be a Drinfeld twist of
u(\mathfrak{g}_{5})
such that its associated twisted Hopf algebra
H=u(\mathfrak{g}_{5})^{J}
is noncocommutative or
H^{*}
is noncommutative. Since
H\cong u(\mathfrak{g}_{5})
as algebras,
H^{*}=u(\mathfrak{g}_{5})^{*}
as coalgebras. Therefore,
H^{*}
is pointed with
|G(H^{*})|=p
. Now, it follows from Theorem
7.2
(ii) that
H^{*}\cong R(p)
, and so
H\cong R(p)
as
R(p)
is self-dual. The last assertion follows from the existence of such a Drinfeld twist

given by (
7.2
) according to
[
10
, Ex. 6.14]
.
∎

Remark 7.4
.

The Radford algebra first appeared in the work of Radford as an example of pointed Hopf algebra of dimension
p^{2}
with antipode of order
2p
(see
[
31
]
). Taft pointed out that it is the smash product of the commutative and cocommutative Hopf algebras
\Bbbk[C_{p}]
and
\Bbbk[C_{p}]^{*}
, and its antipode is not semisimple. Theorem
7.2
provides several characterizations of the Radford algebra. In particular, it is the unique nontrivial extension of
\Bbbk[C_{p}]^{*}
by
\Bbbk[C_{p}]
.

Finally, we would like to classify all the extensions of
p
-dimensional Hopf algebras using the classification theorem of
p^{2}
-dimensional Hopf algebras. Recall that there are five nonisomorphic 2-dimensional
p
-restricted Lie algebras
\mathfrak{g}_{i}
with basis elements
x,y
(cf.
[
41
, Appendix A]
). Four of them are abelian, and they are given by

\mathfrak{g}_{1}
:
x^{p}=x
,
y^{p}=y
;
\mathfrak{g}_{2}
:
x^{p}=x
,
y^{p}=0
;

Abstract
This paper considers energy-efficient connectivity for Internet of Things (IoT) devices in a coexistence scenario between two distinctive communication models: pull- and push-based. In pull-based, the base station (BS) decides when to retrieve a specific type of data from the IoT devices, while in push-based, the IoT device decides when and which data to transmit. To this end, this paper advocates introducing the content-based wake-up (CoWu), which enables the BS to remotely activate only a subset of pull-based nodes equipped with wake-up receivers, observing the relevant data. In this setup, a BS pulls data with CoWu at a specific time instance to fulfill its tasks while collecting data from the nodes operating with a push-based communication model. The resource allocation plays an important role: longer data collection duration for pull-based nodes can lead to high retrieval accuracy while decreasing the probability of data transmission success for push-based nodes, and vice versa. Numerical results show that CoWu can manage communication requirements for both pull-based and push-based nodes while realizing the high energy efficiency (up to 38 % \% ) of IoT devices, compared to the baseline scheduling method.



I
Introduction

In the future generation of wireless communication, energy and spectrum efficiencies are considered among the fundamental
Key Performance Indicators (KPIs)
for
Internet of Things (IoT)
networks
[
1
]
. It is then imperative to design communication protocols that support
sustainable connectivity
between different
IoT
devices, serving different communication classes, service requirements, and tasks.
To this aim, recent research brings focus on
goal-oriented communications
as a promising approach, in which communication is designed as a means to achieve certain goals of the communicating parties
[
2
,
3
]
. In
IoT
networks, this principle can be translated into transmission policy that focuses on information relevant for receiver’s current task, which reduces unnecessary data transmission and thereby increases the overall energy efficiency. Accordingly, the data receiver, e.g., the
Base Station (BS)
, can strategically decide when and which devices to query for relevant data. This communication regime, named
pull-based
communication, has recently gained attention
[
4
,
5
]
. It differs from the classical
push-based
communication, in which the devices generating data decide autonomously when and what to transmit.

The technology enabler for low-energy pull-based
IoT
communication is the
wake-up radio
, an ultra low-power wake-up receiver installed into the
IoT
devices that remains active waiting for specific
wake-up signals (WuSs)
while the higher energy-demanding main radio is turned off
[
6
]
. Therefore, this allows devices to operate in a demand-driven manner, i.e., upon a received
WuS
from the
BS
, the device activates its main radio and transmits its observations, reducing the energy consumption during idle periods
[
7
]
.
The design of the
WuSs
is currently studied in the
3rd Generation Partnership Project (3GPP)
standardization body
[
8
,
9
,
10
]
, which considers the
Identity-based Wake-up (IDWu)
as a viable solution. In
IDWu
, the
BS
transmits a wake-up signal embedding the unique
IDentity (ID)
of a device
[
6
]
. However, employing
IDWu
cannot avoid the wasteful wake-up of nodes which might transmit data irrelevant to the current task, deteriorating the energy efficiency of the
IoT
devices.
To address this problem, we have proposed
Content-based Wake-up (CoWu)

[
11
]
. In
CoWu
, the information about the data needed by the
BS
is embedded into the
WuS
, letting only the nodes having suitable readings turn on their main radio and transmit
[
11
]
. However, in practical
IoT
scenarios, nodes operating with pull-based and push-based communication models might coexist
[
12
]
. For example, in a digital twin setting, the
BS
might constantly collect status updates from the push-based nodes to maintain the accuracy of the digital twin while it directly requests critical data in a pull-based manner to control the elements in the physical twin, such as
Automated Guided Vehicle (AGV)
or mobile actuator. In these cases, designing the
Medium Access Control (MAC)
layer frame in which push-based and pull-based communications coexist is also fundamental. In this regard, the authors
[
13
]
described an analytical formulation for choosing the two communication modes from the
BS
perspective. Similarly, in a recent work
[
12
]
,
MAC
layer access problem for
IoT
data collection in the coexistence of
IDWu
pull-based and push-based communication was addressed.

Related works thereof indicate a general problem within the coexistence of pull-push communication. Activating pull-based nodes to transmit often for goal-oriented objectives at the
BS
eventually deteriorates the performance of push-based nodes due to fewer transmission opportunities within the shared communication resources. This motivates the objective of this work:
how to design an efficient
MAC
protocol for pull/push coexistence in
IoT
data collection scenarios to realize sustainable connectivity
. To the best of our knowledge, no previous work has considered applying
CoWu
for the pull/push communication coexistence scenario.

This paper investigates the performance of
CoWu
in particular, proposing a mechanism to allow the coexistence of the two traffics. In this regard, our contributions are two-fold: first, we characterize the basic trade-off between the accuracy of the retrieved data of pull-based communication and the probability of successful access for push-based communication through theoretical analysis; second, we evaluate the gain of applying
CoWu
against a scheduling method, showing its robustness against the traffic of push nodes.

. Remark that for

Regardless of the types of agents, we assume a collision channel for
UL
communication and an error-free channel for the
DL
direction. To simplify the analysis, we assume that all devices employ a
p
-persistent
Carrier Sense Multiple Access (CSMA)
: when a device attempts to access the channel, it first conducts carrier sensing at the beginning of a slot. If the channel is idle, the node attempts transmission of its packet with transmission probability
p

ACKnowledgment (ACK)
is received from the
BS
immediately after each successful transmission, as in
[
12
]
. The nodes detecting a packet loss by the absence of the

p
-persistent
CSMA
. Here, we assume that all nodes, including the
BS
, are within each other’s communication, wake-up, and carrier-sensing range. Accordingly, two types of collisions may happen: intra-class and inter-class collisions. The former happens among the data transmission operating in the same communication mode, e.g., pull-based with pull-based communication, whereas the latter happens among the nodes operating in different modes. While intra-class collisions are independent of the frame structure, if the
BS
reduces
\tau_{w}
(see Fig.
1(b)
), the data transmission from the push-based nodes would succeed with high probability while there is a high risk that the data transmission from the pull-based nodes will fail due to inter-class collisions. On the other hand, if the
BS
increases
\tau_{w}
(see Fig.
1(c)
), the transmission of the active pull-based nodes is highly likely to succeed, but the performance of push-based nodes deteriorates due to the fewer transmission opportunities.

II-A

Model of pull-based communication

Each
SA-Pull
,
n\in\{1,\dots,N_{w}\}
, is equipped with a wake-up receiver, and monitors an independent and identically distributed physical random process
V_{n}[m]\in[V_{\mathrm{min}},V_{\mathrm{max}}]

probability density function (PDF)

BS
receives an external query via cloud – for example, from a mobile actuator – it retrieves data from the
SAs-Pull

m
-th frame, assuming the

received a query request in the previous
(m-1)
-th frame.

In general, the query request could be for a

, where

. Without loss of generality, in this paper, we focus on the case with

and assume the query asks the data from observations within the range
V_{L}\leq V_{n}[m]\leq V_{U}
,
\forall n\in\{1,\dots,N_{w}\}
. In other words, the
BS
has to conduct the so-called range-query employing a
CoWu
signal which embeds the information of target range threshold

1

1

This can be realized, e.g., by encoding the lower and higher values of a range of interests into the length of wake-up signal
[
5
,
11
]
. In this work, without loss of generality, we ignore the impact of the variable length of the
CoWu
and restrict our analysis to a given number

UL
available slots.

\mathbf{V}_{\mathrm{th}}=[V_{L},V_{U}]
. When a wake-up receiver receives the
CoWu
signals, the
SA-Pull
samples its observed physical process and checks whether its observation is within the range threshold
\mathbf{V}_{\mathrm{th}}

[
5
,
11
]
. If so, it activates the main radio and attempts to access the channel transmitting a single packet. Consequently, if a
SA-Pull
receives an

signaling successful access, it turns off its main radio interface. Therein, the energy consumption of the

is evaluated according to the following assumptions. When no communication is requested, each

\xi_{T}

.

In this setting, we can define the

, namely
\gamma_{w}
. Denoting by

the subset of

\mathcal{S}

\gamma_{w}=\mathrm{Pr}(\mathcal{T}=\mathcal{S}).

(1)

The accuracy becomes 1 if the

collects data from all

II-B

Model of push-based communication

SAs-Push
are intermittently active, taking measurements of the environment according to a
Poisson Point Process (PPP)
and generating packets to transmit toward the
BS
with an average arrival rate
\lambda
[packet/slot]. Arguably, one can consider the
BS
queries “all contents” of the
SAs-Push
, while only specific content of
SAs-Pull
through the
CoWu
signal; thus, reaching a unified understanding of how the
BS
can collect data. At the beginning of the frame, the
BS
notifies the time periods
[t_{c}(\alpha),L]
, during which
SAs-Push
can transmit its packet inserting this information into the control signal for push nodes. For simplicity of analysis, we assume that in each frame,
SAs-Push
store only the latest packet in the queue and transmit it in the next frame, if any. Thus, the probability that a
SA-Push
attempts data transmission in the frame
m
is
p_{\lambda}=1-e^{-\lambda L}
, which is the probability that a node generates at least one packet during the
m-1
-th frame.

Here, as in the scope of this paper, we look at the access mechanism for reducing the energy consumption for
SA-Pull
while satisfying the requirements for both
SAs-Pull
and
SAs-Push
. Thus, we only focus on the
probability of successful access
for
SAs-Push
- or just
push success probability
, -
\gamma_{u}
, as the main metric for the push-based communication thereof, ignoring the energy consumption for
SA-Push
and keep it for future works.
Therein,
\gamma_{u}
is defined as the probability that data transmission of
SA-Push
is succeeded in the
m
-th frame.

III-A

Analysis of the distribution of successful transmissions

To characterize the system level performance, it is required to obtain the distribution of the number of successful data transmissions at the end of the portion of the frame reserved for pull, where only intra-class collision can happen, and the end of the portion of shared resources, where both intra- and inter-class collisions can happen. In other words, we are interested in finding the number of successful transmission attempts happened from slot 1 to slot
\lfloor\alpha L\rfloor

t_{c}(\alpha)

L

To analyze the aforementioned distribution in a general manner, we construct a Markov chain in which each state

p_{s,s-1}=s\,p\,(1-p)^{s-1}.

(2)

On the contrary, the probability of remaining in the same state can be described as

\forall s\in\{1,\dots,J\}

where

\mathbf{\Phi}(0)=[1,0,0,\ldots,0]^{\mathsf{T}}

is the transition matrix containing the transition probabilities. Using eq. (

V
Conclusions

We have investigated a pull/push coexistence scenario, where
IoT
devices equipped with wake-up receivers respond to
CoWu
signal sent by the
BS
for a high data retrieval accuracy. We established theoretical models and derived optimal operation points for an energy-efficient transmission strategy between
SA-Pull
and
SA-Push
that ensures a high transmission success rate for
SA-Push
. Our numerical results have shown the basic trade-off between the accuracy of push success probability and pull retrieved data accuracy, and energy consumption.

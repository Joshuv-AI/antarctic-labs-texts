Abstract
. The paper studies the asymptotic analysis of a model coupling elastoplasticity and damage depending on three parameters – governing viscosity, plastic hardening, and convergence rate of plastic strain and displacement to equilibrium – as they vanish in different orders. The notion of limit evolution obtained is proven to coincide in any case with a notion introduced by Crismale and Rossi in [ CR19 ] ; moreover, such solutions are closely related to those obtained in the vanishing-viscosity limit by Crismale and Lazzaroni in [ CL16 ] , for the analogous model where only the viscosity parameter was present.

35A15, 35Q74, 74C05.

Key words and phrases:
rate-independent systems, variational models, vanishing viscosity and hardening, Balanced Viscosity solutions, damage, elasto-plasticity.

1.
Introduction

Rate-independent processes model evolutionary phenomena where the external loading is much slower than the internal oscillations of materials, while viscosities may be neglected. Despite a wide literature on the subject (see
[
MR15
]
and references therein), a further understanding is needed of the relations between the different notions of solution that have been proposed. In particular, a problem of interest in applications is to determine which kind of solution captures the limiting behavior of dynamical systems for small viscosity or inertia. For this reason, in this paper we compare different notions of solution, obtained with different approximation methods as viscosities tend to zero at different rates (but with no inertia).

We focus on a rate-independent system modeling damage in an elasto-plastic body occupying a bounded Lipschitz domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
n\geq 2
. The model was advanced and first studied in
[
AMV14
,
AMV15
]
,
while the existence of
globally minimizing quasistatic evolutions
(or, equivalently,
Energetic solutions
) was first proved in

[
Cri16
]
. In
[
CL16
,
CR19
]
, the
vanishing-viscosity
approach was instead adopted to find the so-called

Balanced Viscosity
solutions, obtaining the rate-independent system as the limit of a viscously perturbed system.
We refer to the pioneering
[
EM06
]
, and the subsequent

[
MRS12a
,
MRS16a
]
, for the definition and properties of such solutions in the context of an ‘abstract’ rate-independent system. The vanishing-viscosity technique has also been adopted in various concrete applications,
ranging from plasticity (cf., e.g.,
[
DMDMM08
,
DMDS11
,
BFM12
,
FS13
,
Sol14
]
),
to damage, fracture, and fatigue (see for instance
[
KMZ08
,
LT11
,
KRZ13
,
Alm17
,
CL17
,
ACO19
]
).

In this paper we aim to gain further insight into the different ways of constructing
Balanced Viscosity
solutions to the model for damage and plasticity from
[
AMV15
]
, which were
explored in
[
CL16
]
and
[
CR19
]
.
We show that these notions of solutions essentially coincide if the hardening vanishes together with viscosities, while they retain different features if the hardening parameter is positive. In particular, it turns out that perfect plasticity coupled with damage may equivalently be approximated by means of processes where viscosity is confined to the flow rule for damage, or with viscosity also in the momentum equation and in the plastic flow rule.

The model

The rate-independent process we are going to address
describes the evolution, in the time interval
(0,T)
, of the
displacement

u:(0,T)\times\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
, of the plastic strain
p:(0,T)\times\Omega\to{\mathbb{M}}^{n{\times}n}_{\mathrm{D}}
, and of the damage variable
z:(0,T)\times\Omega\to[0,1]
that describes the soundness of the material:
for
z(t,x)=1
(respectively,
z(t,x)=0
) the material is in the undamaged (fully damaged, resp.) state, at the time
t\in(0,T)
and ‘locally’ around the point
x\in\Omega
.
In fact, the related PDE system
consists of

-

the momentum balance

(1.1a)

\displaystyle-\mathrm{div}\,\sigma=f\quad\text{ in }\Omega\times(0,T)\,,\qquad\quad\sigma{\mathrm{n}}=g\text{ on }\Gamma_{\mathrm{Neu}}\times(0,T),

with
f,\,g
some external forces,
{\mathrm{n}}
the outer unit normal vector to
\Omega
,
\sigma
the
stress
tensor

(1.1b)

\sigma=\mathbb{C}(z)e\quad\text{ in }\Omega\times(0,T),

\mathbb{C}
the elastic stress tensor, and
e:(0,T)\times\Omega\to{\mathbb{M}^{n\times n}_{\mathrm{sym}}}
the elastic strain; together with the plastic strain
p
, the elastic strain
e
concurs to
the kinematic admissibility condition for the
strain

{\mathrm{E}}(u)=\frac{\nabla u+\nabla u^{T}}{2}
, i.e.

(1.1c)

{\mathrm{E}}(u)=e+p\quad\text{ in }\Omega\times(0,T);

-

the flow rule for the damage variable
z

(1.1d)

\displaystyle\partial\mathrm{R}(\dot{z})+A_{\mathrm{m}}(z)+W^{\prime}(z)\ni-\tfrac{1}{2}\mathbb{C}^{\prime}(z)e:e\quad\text{ in }\Omega\times(0,T),

where
\partial\mathrm{R}:\mathbb{R}\rightrightarrows\mathbb{R}
denotes the convex analysis subdifferential of the density of dissipation potential

\mathrm{R}:\mathbb{R}\to[0,+\infty]\ \text{ defined by }\ \mathrm{R}(\eta):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\kappa|\eta|&\text{ if }\eta\leq 0,\\
+\infty&\text{ otherwise},\end{array}\right.

encompassing the unidirectionality in the evolution of damage,
A_{\mathrm{m}}
is
the
\mathrm{m}
-Laplacian operator, with
\mathrm{m}>\tfrac{n}{2}
, and
W
is a suitable nonlinear, possibly nonsmooth, function;

-

the flow rule for the plastic tensor

(1.1e)

\displaystyle\partial_{\dot{p}}\mathrm{H}(z,\dot{p})\ni\sigma_{\mathrm{D}}\quad\text{ in }\Omega\times(0,T),

with
\sigma_{\mathrm{D}}
the deviatoric part of the stress tensor
\sigma
and
\mathrm{H}(z,\cdot)
the density of the plastic dissipation potential.

System (
1.1a
)–(
1.1e
) is complemented by
the boundary conditions

(1.1f)

u=w\text{ on }\Gamma_{\mathrm{Dir}}\times(0,T),\qquad\sigma{\mathrm{n}}=g\text{ on }\Gamma_{\mathrm{Neu}}\times(0,T),\qquad\partial_{{\mathrm{n}}}z=0\text{ on }\partial\Omega\times(0,T),

where
\Gamma_{\mathrm{Dir}}
is the Dirichlet part of the boundary
\partial\Omega
and
w
a time-dependent Dirichlet loading, while

\Gamma_{\mathrm{Neu}}
is the Neumann part of
\partial\Omega
and
g
an assigned traction.

Alternative models for damage and plasticity have been analyzed in, e.g.,
[
RV16
,
RV17
,
DRS19
]
, albeit from a different perspective. In fact, those papers address
the rate-independent evolution of the damage and plastic processes coupled with a
rate-dependent
momentum balance, featuring viscosity and even inertial terms. Therefore, the resulting system has a
mixed
rate-dependent/independent character and is formulated in terms of a weak, energetic-type notion of solution. Instead, both in
[
CL16
]
and
[
CR19
]
,
(two distinct)
viscous regularization procedures, described below, were advanced to construct Balanced Viscosity solutions to
the fully rate-independent
system (
1.1
).

Balanced Viscosity solutions

In
[
CL16
]

the vanishing-viscosity approximation of
system (
1.1
) was carried out by perturbing the
damage flow rule by a viscous term,
which led to the
viscously regularized
system

(1.2a)

\displaystyle-\mathrm{div}\,\sigma=f\quad\text{with $\sigma=\mathbb{C}(z)e$}

\displaystyle\text{ in }\Omega\times(0,T),

(1.2b)

\displaystyle\partial\mathrm{R}(\dot{z})+\varepsilon\dot{z}+A_{\mathrm{m}}(z)+W^{\prime}(z)\ni-\tfrac{1}{2}\mathbb{C}^{\prime}(z)e:e

\displaystyle\text{ in }\Omega\times(0,T),

(1.2c)

\displaystyle\partial_{\dot{p}}\mathrm{H}(z,\dot{p})\ni\sigma_{\mathrm{D}}

\displaystyle\text{ in }\Omega\times(0,T),

supplemented by the boundary conditions

(1.2d)

u=w\text{ on }\Gamma_{\mathrm{Dir}}\times(0,T),\quad\sigma{\mathrm{n}}=g\text{ on }\Gamma_{\mathrm{Neu}}\times(0,T),\quad\partial_{\mathrm{n}}z=0\text{ on }\partial\Omega\times(0,T).

Passing to the limit in a reparameterized version of (
1.2
)
led to a first construction of
\mathrm{BV}
solutions to system (
1.1
).
We shall illustrate the notion of
parameterized
Balanced Viscosity solution thus obtained in the forthcoming Section

3.2
. In what follows, for quicker reference we will call the
\mathrm{BV}
solutions from
[
CL16
]

\mathrm{BV}_{0}
solutions to system (
1.1
)
,
where the subscript 0 indicates that the solutions are obtained in the limit as
\varepsilon\downarrow 0
.

In
[
CR19
]
, a different construction of
\mathrm{BV}
solutions
to system (
1.1
)
was proposed, based on a viscous regularization of the momentum balance and of the plastic flow rule,
in addition
to that of the damage flow rule. This alternative approach was first proposed in

[
MRS16b
]
in a finite-dimensional context, and extended to infinite-dimensional systems in the recent
[
MR21
]
. Both papers address the vanishing-viscosity analysis of an abstract evolutionary system, that can be thought of as prototypical of rate-dependent systems in solid mechanics,
governing the evolution of an elastic variable
\mathsf{u}
and of an internal variable
\mathsf{z}
. In those papers,

\mathsf{z}
has relaxation time
\varepsilon
, while

\mathsf{u}
has a viscous damping with relaxation time
\varepsilon^{\alpha}
,
\alpha>0
.
To emphasize the occurrence of these three time scales (the time scale
\varepsilon^{0}=1
of the external loading, the relaxation time
\varepsilon
of
\mathsf{z}
, and the - possibly different - relaxation time
\varepsilon^{\alpha}
of
\mathsf{u}
),
the term ‘multi-rate system’ was used in
[
MRS16b
]
. Therein, as well as in

[
MR21
]
, it was shown that, in the
three cases
\alpha\in(0,1)
,
\alpha=1
and
\alpha>1
,
the vanishing-viscosity analysis leads to different notions of
Balanced-Viscosity solutions, including in particular different descriptions of the
jump behavior developing in the limit
\varepsilon\downarrow 0
.

Thus, along the lines of
[
MRS16b
]
, in
[
CR19
]
the authors addressed the following, alternative, viscous regularization of system (
1.1
):

(1.3a)

\displaystyle-\mathrm{div}(\varepsilon\nu\mathbb{D}{\mathrm{E}}{(\dot{u})}+\sigma)=f

\displaystyle\text{ in }\Omega\times(0,T),

(1.3b)

\displaystyle\partial\mathrm{R}(\dot{z})+\varepsilon\dot{z}+A_{\mathrm{m}}(z)+W^{\prime}(z)\ni-\tfrac{1}{2}\mathbb{C}^{\prime}(z)e:e

\displaystyle\text{ in }\Omega\times(0,T),

(1.3c)

\displaystyle\partial_{\dot{p}}\mathrm{H}(z,\dot{p})+\varepsilon\nu\dot{p}+{\mu}p\ni\sigma_{\mathrm{D}}

\displaystyle\text{ in }\Omega\times(0,T),

supplemented by the boundary conditions

(1.3d)

u=w\text{ on }\Gamma_{\mathrm{Dir}}\times(0,T),\quad(\varepsilon\nu\mathbb{D}{\mathrm{E}}{(\dot{u})}+\sigma){\mathrm{n}}=g\text{ on }\Gamma_{\mathrm{Neu}}\times(0,T),\quad\partial_{\mathrm{n}}z=0\text{ on }\partial\Omega\times(0,T),

where
\mathbb{D}
is a positive-definite fourth-order tensor.
System (
1.3
) features a viscous regularization
both
in
the damage flow rule
and
in
the displacement equation and the plastic flow rule.
Let us now illustrate the role of the various parameters appearing therein, namely
the

-

(vanishing-)viscosity parameter
\varepsilon>0
,

-

(vanishing-)hardening parameter
\mu>0
,

-

additional parameter
\nu>0
, which was required to fulfill
\nu\leq\mu
in order to get suitable a priori estimates. We have referred to
\nu
as a
rate
parameter, since it sets
the mutual rate at which, on the one hand, the displacement and the plastic strain converge to equilibrium and rate-independent
evolution, and, on the other hand, the damage parameter
converges to rate-independent evolution. More precisely,
if
\nu>0
stays fixed
then

u
and
p
converge at the same rate as
z
, while their convergence occurs at a faster rate if
\nu\downarrow 0
. This is clear if one chooses e.g.
\nu=\mu=\varepsilon
, so that the viscous terms
{\mathrm{E}}{(\dot{u})}
and
\dot{p}
in (
1.3a
) and
(
1.3c
) are modulated by
the
coefficient
\varepsilon^{2}
, as opposed to the coefficient
\varepsilon
in the damage flow rule.
Observe that, upon taking the vanishing-hardening limit
\mu\downarrow 0
, the constraint
\nu\leq\mu
forces the
joint
vanishing-viscosity and vanishing-hardening limit to occur at a faster rate for
u
and
p
than for
z
.

We will refer to the vanishing-viscosity analyses in system (
1.3
) as
full
, as opposed to the
partial
vanishing-viscosity approximation provided by system (
1.2
), where only the damage flow rule is regularized.

Indeed, in
[
CR19
]
three
full
vanishing-viscosity analyses
have been carried out for system (
1.3
),
leading to three different notions of solution for system (
1.1
), possibly regularized by a hardening term. Let us briefly illustrate them.

(1)
\mathrm{BV}_{0}^{\mu,\nu}
solutions:

The limit passage in a (reparameterized) version of (
1.3
) as
\varepsilon\downarrow 0
, while the positive parameters
\mu
and
\nu
stayed fixed, has led to
\mathrm{BV}
solutions for a variant of the system (
1.1
), where the plastic flow rule was regularized by the hardening term
\mu p
,
i.e. for
the rate-independent system with hardening

(1.4a)

\displaystyle-\mathrm{div}(\mathbb{C}(z)e)=f

\displaystyle\text{ in }\Omega\times(0,T),

(1.4b)

\displaystyle\partial\mathrm{R}(\dot{z})+A_{\mathrm{m}}(z)+W^{\prime}(z)\ni-\tfrac{1}{2}\mathbb{C}^{\prime}(z)e:e

\displaystyle\text{ in }\Omega\times(0,T),

(1.4c)

\displaystyle\partial_{\dot{p}}\mathrm{H}(z,\dot{p})+{\mu}p\ni\sigma_{\mathrm{D}}

\displaystyle\text{ in }\Omega\times(0,T),

coupled with the boundary conditions

(1.4d)

u=w\text{ on }\Gamma_{\mathrm{Dir}}\times(0,T),\quad\sigma{\mathrm{n}}=g\text{ on }\Gamma_{\mathrm{Neu}}\times(0,T)\quad\qquad\partial_{\mathrm{n}}z=0\text{ on }\partial\Omega\times(0,T).

The
\mathrm{BV}
solutions to system (
1.4
) thus constructed reflect their origin from a system viscously regularized in all of the three variables
u
,
z
,
p
. In fact, in the jump regime the system may switch to viscous behavior in the three variables
u
,
z
, and
p
. Since the convergence of
u

p

u
,
z
, and
p

\mathrm{BV}_{0}^{\mu,\nu}

solutions to system
(

\varepsilon\downarrow 0

\mu

\nu
appears in the notation,

it still features in the limiting evolution as a coefficient

(2)
\mathrm{BV}_{0}^{\mu,0}
solutions:

The limit passage in a (reparameterized) version of (
1.3
) as
\varepsilon\downarrow 0
simultaneously with
\nu\downarrow 0
, while
\mu>0
stayed fixed, has again led to
\mathrm{BV}
solutions for the rate-independent elasto-plastic damage system with hardening (
1.4
).
These solutions still have the feature that,
in the jump regime, the system may switch to viscous behavior in
u
,
z
, and
p
. However, the
\mathrm{BV}
solutions thus obtained reflect the fact that the convergence of
u
and
p
to
elastic equilibrium and rate-independent evolution
has occurred at a
faster rate
(as
\nu\downarrow 0
) than that for
z
. To emphasize this, such solutions were termed
\mathrm{BV}
solutions to the multi-rate system for damage with hardening
. We will refer to them as
\mathrm{BV}_{0}^{\mu,0}
solutions to system (
1.1
)
. In this notation, the double occurrence of
0

relates to the fact that such solutions were obtained in the limit

, as opposed to the

(3)
\mathrm{BV}_{0}^{0,0}

The limit passage in a (reparameterized) version of (

) as
\varepsilon,\,\mu,\,\nu\downarrow 0

led to
\mathrm{BV}
solutions to the multi-rate system for damage and perfect plasticity

and

Here, the triple occurrence of

\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
1\leq p<+\infty

\|\cdot\|_{H^{\mathrm{m}}}
for the norm of the Sobolev-Slobodeskij space

, for

. The symbol

stands for the space of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
-valued bounded Radon measures in

.

The space of symmetric

-matrices is denoted by

where

of

The symbol
\mathrm{Sym}({\mathbb{M}}^{n{\times}n}_{\mathrm{D}};{\mathbb{M}}^{n{\times}n}_{\mathrm{D}})
stands for the set of symmetric endomorphisms on

Given a function

.
A different notation will be employed when considering

with values in a Lebesgue or Sobolev space

The symbols

We will sometimes employ the symbols

Function of bounded deformation

The state space for the displacement variable for the systems with hardening will be

H_{\mathrm{Dir}}^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n}):=\{u\in H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})\,:\ u=0\text{ on }\Gamma_{\mathrm{Dir}}\}

(recall that
\Gamma_{\mathrm{Dir}}
is the Dirichlet part of
\partial\Omega
, cf. (
2.

Ω

) ahead).

For the perfectly plastic damage system, displacements will belong to the space of
functions of bounded deformations
, defined by

\mathrm{BD}(\Omega):=\{u\in L^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})\,:\ \mathrm{E}(u)\in\mathrm{M}_{\mathrm{b}}(\Omega;{\mathbb{M}^{n\times n}_{\mathrm{sym}}})\},

with
\mathrm{M}_{\mathrm{b}}(\Omega;{\mathbb{M}^{n\times n}_{\mathrm{sym}}})
the space of
{\mathbb{M}^{n\times n}_{\mathrm{sym}}}
-valued bounded Radon measures on
\Omega
.
We recall that

\mathrm{M}_{\mathrm{b}}(\Omega;{\mathbb{M}^{n\times n}_{\mathrm{sym}}})
can be identified with the dual of the space
\mathrm{C}^{0}_{0}(\Omega;{\mathbb{M}^{n\times n}_{\mathrm{sym}}})
of continuous
{\mathbb{M}^{n\times n}_{\mathrm{sym}}}
-valued functions vanishing at the boundary of
\Omega
.
The space
\mathrm{BD}(\Omega)
has a Banach structure if equipped with the norm

\|u\|_{\mathrm{BD}(\Omega)}:=\|u\|_{L^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\|\mathrm{E}(u)\|_{\mathrm{M}_{\mathrm{b}}(\Omega;{\mathbb{M}^{n\times n}_{\mathrm{sym}}})}.

Indeed,
\mathrm{BD}(\Omega)
is the dual of a normed space, cf.
[
TS80
]
, and
such duality provides a weak
∗
convergence on
\mathrm{BD}(\Omega)
:
a sequence
(u_{k})_{k}
converges to
u
weakly
∗
in
\mathrm{BD}(\Omega)
if
u_{k}\rightharpoonup u
in
L^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
\mathrm{E}(u_{k})\overset{*}{\rightharpoonup}\mathrm{E}(u)
in
\mathrm{M}_{\mathrm{b}}(\Omega;{\mathbb{M}^{n\times n}_{\mathrm{sym}}})
.
It holds
\mathrm{BD}(\Omega)\subset L^{n/(n{-}1)}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})
.
Up to subsequences, every bounded sequence in
\mathrm{BD}(\Omega)
converges weakly
∗
, weakly in
L^{n/(n{-}1)}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})
, and strongly in
L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})
for any
1\leq p<\frac{n}{n-1}
.
Finally, we recall that the trace
u|_{\partial\Omega}
of a function
u\in\mathrm{BD}(\Omega)
is well defined and is an element in
L^{1}(\partial\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

A divergence operator

First of all, we observe that
any
\sigma\in{L^{2}(\Omega;{\mathbb{M}^{n\times n}_{\mathrm{sym}}})}
such that
\mathrm{div}\,\sigma\in L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})
induces
the distribution
[\sigma{\mathrm{n}}]
defined by

(2.1)

\langle[\sigma{\mathrm{n}}],\psi\rangle_{\partial\Omega}:=\langle\mathrm{div}\,\sigma,\psi\rangle_{L^{2}}+\langle\sigma,{\mathrm{E}}(\psi)\rangle_{L^{2}}\qquad\text{for every }\psi\in H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n}).

By
[
KT83
, Theorem 1.2]
and
[
DMDM06
, (2.24)]
we have that
[\sigma{\mathrm{n}}]\in H^{-1/2}(\partial\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})
;
moreover, if
\sigma\in\mathrm{C}^{0}(\overline{\Omega};{\mathbb{M}^{n\times n}_{\mathrm{sym}}})
, then the distribution
[\sigma{\mathrm{n}}]
fulfills
[\sigma{\mathrm{n}}]=\sigma{\mathrm{n}}
,
where the right-hand side is
the standard pointwise product of the matrix
\sigma
and the normal vector
{\mathrm{n}}
in
\partial\Omega
.

For the treatment of the perfectly plastic system for damage it will be crucial to work with the space

(2.2)

\Sigma(\Omega):=\{\sigma\in{L^{2}(\Omega;{\mathbb{M}^{n\times n}_{\mathrm{sym}}})}\colon\mathrm{div}\,\sigma\in L^{n}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n}),\ \sigma_{\mathrm{D}}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega;{\mathbb{M}}^{n{\times}n}_{\mathrm{D}})\}.

Furthermore,
our choice of external loadings
(see (
2.8a
)) will ensure that
the stress fields
\sigma
that we consider, at equilibrium, have the additional property that
[\sigma{\mathrm{n}}]\in L^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
\sigma\in\Sigma(\Omega)
(cf. Lemma
3.1
). Therefore, any of such fields
induces a functional
-\mathrm{Div}\,\sigma\in\mathrm{BD}(\Omega)^{*}
via

(2.3)

\langle-\mathrm{Div}\,\sigma,v\rangle_{\mathrm{BD}(\Omega)}:=\langle-\mathrm{div}\,\sigma,v\rangle_{L^{\frac{n}{n-1}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\langle[\sigma{\mathrm{n}}],v\rangle_{L^{1}(\Gamma_{\mathrm{Neu}};\mathbb{R}^{n})}\qquad\text{for all }v\in\mathrm{BD}(\Omega).

With slight abuse of notation, we shall denote by
-\mathrm{Div}\,\sigma
also the restriction of the above functional to
H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})^{*}
.

The
A_{\mathrm{m}}
-Laplacian

The damage flow rule features
a gradient regularizing contribution
in terms of the
A_{\mathrm{m}}
-Laplacian operator, that is defined from the bilinear form

\displaystyle a_{\mathrm{m}}:H^{\mathrm{m}}(\Omega)\times H^{\mathrm{m}}(\Omega)\to\mathbb{R},\quad a_{\mathrm{m}}(z_{1},z_{2}):=\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}\frac{\big(\nabla z_{1}(x)-\nabla z_{1}(y)\big)\cdot\big(\nabla z_{2}(x)-\nabla z_{2}(y)\big)}{|x-y|^{n+2(\mathrm{m}-1)}}\;\!\mathrm{d}x\;\!\mathrm{d}y\text{ with }\mathrm{m}>\frac{n}{2}.

Then,

A_{\mathrm{m}}:H^{\mathrm{m}}(\Omega)\to H^{\mathrm{m}}(\Omega)^{*}\text{ is defined by }\langle A_{\mathrm{m}}(z),w\rangle_{H^{\mathrm{m}}(\Omega)}:=a_{\mathrm{m}}(z,w)\quad\text{for
every $z,\,w\in H^{\mathrm{m}}(\Omega)$.}

The inner product
\sideset{}{{}_{H^{\mathrm{m}}(\Omega)}}{\mathop{\langle z_{1},z_{2}\rangle}}:=\int_{\Omega}z_{1}z_{2}\;\!\mathrm{d}x+a_{\mathrm{m}}(z_{1},z_{2})
makes
H^{\mathrm{m}}(\Omega)
a Hilbert space.
Throughout the paper we shall assume
\mathrm{m}>\tfrac{n}{2}
and rely on the compact embedding
H^{\mathrm{m}}(\Omega)\Subset\mathrm{C}^{0}(\overline{\Omega})
.

2.1.
Assumptions and preliminary results

This section and Sec.
2.2
collect all our assumptions on the constitutive functions of the model and on the problem data. We will omit to invoke them explicitly in the statement of the various results.

The reference configuration

In what follows we will assume that
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
n\in\{2,3\}
, is a bounded Lipschitz domain
satisfying
the so-called
Kohn-Temam condition

(2.
\Omega
)

\begin{gathered}\partial\Omega=\Gamma_{\mathrm{Dir}}\cup\Gamma_{\mathrm{Neu}}\cup\Sigma\quad\text{ with $\Gamma_{\mathrm{Dir}},\,\Gamma_{\mathrm{Neu}},\,\Sigma$ pairwise disjoint,}\\
\text{
$\Gamma_{\mathrm{Dir}}$ and $\Gamma_{\mathrm{Neu}}$ relatively open in $\partial\Omega$, and $\partial\Gamma_{\mathrm{Dir}}=\partial\Gamma_{\mathrm{Neu}}=\Sigma$ their relative boundary in $\partial\Omega$,}\\
\text{ with $\Sigma$ of class $\mathrm{C}^{2}$ and ${\mathcal{H}}^{n-1}(\Sigma)=0$, and with $\partial\Omega$ Lipschitz and of class $\mathrm{C}^{2}$ in a neighborhood of $\Sigma$.}\end{gathered}

The elasticity and viscosity tensors

We assume that the elastic tensor
\mathbb{C}:[0,+\infty)\to\mathrm{Lin}({\mathbb{M}^{n\times n}_{\mathrm{sym}}};{\mathbb{M}^{n\times n}_{\mathrm{sym}}})

fulfills the following conditions

(
2.\mathbb{C}_{1}
)

\displaystyle\mathbb{C}\in\mathrm{C}^{1,1}([0,+\infty);\mathrm{Lin}({\mathbb{M}^{n\times n}_{\mathrm{sym}}};{\mathbb{M}^{n\times n}_{\mathrm{sym}}})),

(
2.\mathbb{C}_{2}
)

\displaystyle z\mapsto\mathbb{C}(z)\xi:\xi\ \text{is nondecreasing for every}\ \xi\in{\mathbb{M}^{n\times n}_{\mathrm{sym}}},

(
2.\mathbb{C}_{3}
)

\displaystyle\exists\,\gamma_{1},\,\gamma_{2}>0\ \ \forall\,z\in[0,+\infty)\ \forall\,\xi\in{\mathbb{M}^{n\times n}_{\mathrm{sym}}}\,:\quad\gamma_{1}|\xi|^{2}\leq\mathbb{C}(z)\xi:\xi\leq\gamma_{2}|\xi|^{2}.

For the viscosity tensor
\mathbb{D}
we require that

(
2.\mathbb{D}_{1}
)

\displaystyle\mathbb{D}\in\mathrm{C}^{0}(\overline{\Omega};\mathrm{Sym}({\mathbb{M}}^{n{\times}n}_{\mathrm{D}};{\mathbb{M}}^{n{\times}n}_{\mathrm{D}})),\text{ and }

(
2.\mathbb{D}_{2}
)

\displaystyle\ \exists\,\delta_{1},\,\delta_{2}>0\ \forall x\in\Omega\ \ \forall A\in\mathbb{M}_{\mathrm{sym}}^{d\times d}\,:\quad\ \delta_{1}|A|^{2}\leq\mathbb{D}(x)A:A\leq\delta_{2}|A|^{2}.

Thus,
\mathbb{D}
induces an equivalent (by a Korn-Poincaré-type inequality)
Hilbert norm on
H_{\mathrm{Dir}}^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})
, i.e.

(2.4)

\|u\|_{H^{1},\mathbb{D}}:=\left(\int_{\Omega}\mathbb{D}\mathrm{E}(u):\mathrm{E}(u)\;\!\mathrm{d}x\right)^{1/2}\quad\text{ and }\quad\exists\,K_{\mathbb{D}}>0\ \forall\,u\in H_{\mathrm{Dir}}^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n}):\ \|u\|_{H^{1},\mathbb{D}}\leq K_{\mathbb{D}}\|\mathrm{E}(u)\|_{L^{2}}\,.

The related
‘dual norm’ is

(2.5)

\|\eta\|_{(H^{1},\mathbb{D})^{*}}:=\left(\int_{\Omega}\mathbb{D}^{-1}\xi:\xi\right)^{1/2}\quad\text{for all }\eta\in H_{\mathrm{Dir}}^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})^{*}\text{ with }\eta=\mathrm{Div}\,\xi\text{ for some }\xi\in\widetilde{\Sigma}(\Omega).

The potential energy for the damage variable

In addition to the regularizing, nonlocal gradient contribution featuring the bilinear form
a_{\mathrm{m}}
, the
z
-dependent part of the mechanical energy functional shall feature a further term with density
W
satisfying

(
2.W_{1}
)

\displaystyle W\in{\mathrm{C}}^{2}((0,+\infty);\mathbb{R}^{+})\cap{\mathrm{C}}^{0}([0,+\infty);\mathbb{R}^{+}{\cup}\{+\infty\}),

(
2.W_{2}
)

\displaystyle s^{2n}W(s)\rightarrow+\infty\text{ as }s\rightarrow 0^{+},

where
W\in{\mathrm{C}}^{0}([0,+\infty);\mathbb{R}^{+}\cup\{+\infty\})
means that
W(0)=\infty
and

W(z)\to+\infty
if
z\downarrow 0
, in accordance with
(

2
.

W
2

).

Indeed, the energy contribution involving
W
forces

to be strictly positive; consequently, the material never reaches the most damaged state at any point.

The plastic and the damage dissipation densities

The plastic dissipation potential shall reflect the requirement that the admissible stresses belong to given constraint sets which, in turn,
depend on the damage variable
z
.
More precisely,
as in

[
CL16
]
we ask that the constraint sets
(K(z))_{z\in[0,+\infty)}
fulfill

(
2.K_{1}
)

\displaystyle K(z)\subset{\mathbb{M}}^{n{\times}n}_{\mathrm{D}}\text{ is closed and convex for all }z\in[0,+\infty),

(
2.K_{2}
)

\displaystyle\exists\,0<\bar{r}<\bar{R}\quad\forall\,0\leq z_{1}\leq z_{2}\,:\qquad B_{\bar{r}}(0)\subset K(z_{1})\subset K(z_{2})\subset B_{\bar{R}}(0),

(
2.K_{3}
)

\displaystyle\exists\,C_{K}>0\quad\forall\,z_{1},\,z_{2}\in[0,+\infty)\,:\qquad d_{\mathscr{H}}(K(z_{1}),K(z_{2}))\leq C_{K}|z_{1}{-}z_{2}|,

with

d_{\mathscr{H}}
the Hausdorff distance between two subsets of
{\mathbb{M}}^{n{\times}n}_{\mathrm{D}}
, defined by

d_{\mathscr{H}}(K_{1},K_{2}):=\max\left(\sup_{x\in K_{1}}\mathrm{dist}(x,K_{2}),\,\sup_{x\in K_{2}}\mathrm{dist}(x,K_{1})\right).

The associated support function
H:[0,+\infty)\times{\mathbb{M}}^{n{\times}n}_{\mathrm{D}}\to[0,+\infty)
, defined by

(2.6)

H(z,\pi):=\sup_{\sigma\in K(z)}\sigma:\pi\qquad\text{for all }(z,\pi)\in[0,+\infty)\times{\mathbb{M}}^{n{\times}n}_{\mathrm{D}},

will act as density function for the plastic dissipation potential, cf. (
2.14
) later on.

We choose as damage dissipation density the function
\mathrm{R}:\mathbb{R}\to[0,+\infty]
given by

(2.7)

\mathrm{R}(\zeta):=\begin{cases}-\kappa\zeta&\text{if }\zeta\leq 0,\\
+\infty&\text{otherwise,}\end{cases}

with
\kappa>0
a constant related to the toughness of the material.

Body and surface forces, Dirichlet loading, and initial data

We assume that the volume force
f
and the assigned traction

fulfill

(2.8a)

f\in H^{1}(0,T;L^{n}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})),\quad g\in H^{1}(0,T;L^{\infty}(\Gamma_{\mathrm{Neu}};\mathbb{R}^{n})).

The induced total load is the function

F\colon[0,T]\to\mathrm{BD}(\Omega)^{*},\qquad\langle F(t),v\rangle_{\mathrm{BD}(\Omega)}:=\langle f(t),v\rangle_{L^{n/(n{-}1)}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\langle g(t),v\rangle_{L^{1}(\Gamma_{\mathrm{Neu}};\mathbb{R}^{n})}\quad\text{for all }v\in\mathrm{BD}(\Omega).

Furthermore, as customary for perfect plasticity,
we shall impose a
uniform safe load condition
, namely that there exists

(2.8b)

\rho\in H^{1}(0,T;{L^{2}(\Omega;{\mathbb{M}^{n\times n}_{\mathrm{sym}}})})\quad\text{ with }\quad\rho_{\mathrm{D}}\in H^{1}(0,T;{L^{\infty}(\Omega;{\mathbb{M}}^{n{\times}n}_{\mathrm{D}})})

and there exists

such that for every

(recall (

-\mathrm{div}\,\varrho(t)=f(t)\text{ a.e.\ on }\Omega,\quad\qquad[\varrho(t){\mathrm{n}}]=g(t)\text{ on }\Gamma_{\mathrm{Neu}},

(2.8d)

\rho_{\mathrm{D}}(t,x)+\xi\in K\qquad\text{for a.a.}\ x\in\Omega\ \text{and for every}\ \xi\in{\mathbb{M}^{n\times n}_{\mathrm{sym}}}\ \text{s.t.}\ |\xi|\leq\alpha.

Observe that, combining (
2.8a
) with (

)–(

) yields

for all
t\in[0,T]
.

As for the time-dependent Dirichlet boundary condition
w
, we assume that

(2.8e)

w\in H^{1}(0,T;H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathbb{R}^{n})).

Finally, we shall consider initial data
q_{0}=(u_{0},z_{0},p_{0})
with

(2.8f)

u_{0}\in H_{\mathrm{Dir}}^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n}),\qquad z_{0}\in H^{\mathrm{m}}(\Omega)\text{ with }W(z_{0})\in L^{1}(\Omega)\text{ and }z_{0}\leq 1\text{ in }\overline{\Omega},\qquad p_{0}\in L^{2}(\Omega;{\mathbb{M}}^{n{\times}n}_{\mathrm{D}}).

The stress-strain duality

For the treatment of the perfectly plastic damage system it is essential to resort to
a suitable notion of stress-strain duality that we borrow from
[
KT83
,
DMDM06
]
, also relying on the more recent extension to Lipschitz boundaries from
[
FG12
]
,
to which we refer for the properties mentioned below.
Following
[
DMDM06
]
we introduce
the class

of

admissible displacements and strains

associated with a function

,
that is

\begin{split}A(w):=\{(u,e,p)\in\,&\mathrm{BD}(\Omega)\times L^{2}(\Omega;{\mathbb{M}^{n\times n}_{\mathrm{sym}}})\times{\mathrm{M}_{\mathrm{b}}(\Omega\cup\Gamma_{\mathrm{Dir}};{\mathbb{M}}^{n{\times}n}_{\mathrm{D}})}\colon\\
&{\mathrm{E}}(u)=e+p\text{ in }\Omega,\,p=(w-u){\,\odot\,}{\mathrm{n}}\,\mathscr{H}^{n-1}\text{ on }\Gamma_{\mathrm{Dir}}\},\end{split}

where
\mathrm{n}
denotes the normal vector to
\partial\Omega
and

the symmetrized tensorial product.
The

is

\begin{split}\Pi(\Omega):=\{p\in{\mathrm{M}_{\mathrm{b}}(\Omega\cup\Gamma_{\mathrm{Dir}};{\mathbb{M}}^{n{\times}n}_{\mathrm{D}})}\colon\exists\,(u,w,e)\in\mathrm{BD}(\Omega)\times H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathbb{R}^{n})\times L^{2}(\Omega;{\mathbb{M}^{n\times n}_{\mathrm{sym}}})\,\text{ s.t.}\,(u,e,p)\in A(w)\}.\end{split}

Given

(cf. (

, and

we define

\langle[\sigma_{\mathrm{D}}:p],\varphi\rangle:=-\int_{\Omega}\varphi\sigma\cdot(e{-}{\mathrm{E}}(w))\,\mathrm{d}x-\int_{\Omega}\sigma\cdot[(u{-}w)\odot\nabla\varphi]\,\mathrm{d}x-\int_{\Omega}\varphi\,(\mathrm{div}\,\sigma)\cdot(u{-}w)\,\mathrm{d}x

for every

;
in fact, this definition is independent of
u
and

.
Under these assumptions,

\sigma\in L^{r}(\Omega;{\mathbb{M}^{n\times n}_{\mathrm{sym}}})
for every

,
and

is a bounded Radon measure with

\|[\sigma_{\mathrm{D}}:p]\|_{1}\leq\|\sigma_{\mathrm{D}}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|p\|_{1}
in

.
Restricting such measure to

, we set

\langle\sigma_{\mathrm{D}}\,|\,p\rangle:=[\sigma_{\mathrm{D}}:p](\Omega\cup\Gamma_{\mathrm{Dir}}).

By (

), since

we get the following integration by parts formula, valid if the distribution

defined in (

:

\langle\sigma_{\mathrm{D}}\,|\,p\rangle=-\langle\sigma,e-{\mathrm{E}}(w)\rangle_{{L^{2}(\Omega;{\mathbb{M}^{n\times n}_{\mathrm{sym}}})}}+\langle-\mathrm{div}\,\sigma,u-w\rangle_{L^{\frac{n}{n-1}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\langle[\sigma{\mathrm{n}}],u-w\rangle_{L^{1}(\Gamma_{\mathrm{Neu}};\mathbb{R}^{n})}

2.2.
Energetics

A key ingredient for the construction of
\mathrm{BV}
solutions to the rate-independent systems (
1.1
)
(damage with perfect plasticity) and (
1.4
) (damage and plasticity with hardening) is the observation that
their
rate-dependent regularizations (
1.2
) and (
1.3
)
have a
gradient-system structure
.
Namely, they can be reformulated in terms of the generalized gradient flow

\partial\Psi(q^{\prime}(t))+{\mathrm{D}}{\mathcal{E}}(t,q(t))\ni 0\qquad\text{in }{\mathbf{Q}}^{*},\ \text{a.e.\ in }\,(0,T),

for suitable choices of

-

the state space
{\mathbf{Q}}
for the triple
q=(u,z,p)
;

-

the driving energy functional
{\mathcal{E}}:(0,T)\times{\mathbf{Q}}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
;

-

the dissipation potential
\Psi:{\mathbf{Q}}\to[0,+\infty]
, with convex analysis subdifferential
\partial\Psi:{\mathbf{Q}}\rightrightarrows{\mathbf{Q}}^{*}
,

as
rigorously proved in
[
CR19
]
.
Observe that also the rate-independent systems (
1.1
)
and (
1.4
) have a gradient structure that is, however, only formal
due to the fact that the functions
u
,
z
, and
p
may have jumps as functions of time.
Nonetheless,
for our analysis
it is crucial to
detail the energetics underlying both the rate-dependent and the rate-independent systems.

The state spaces

The state space for the rate-dependent/independent damage systems with hardening is

{\mathbf{Q}}_{\scriptstyle\tiny\mathrm{H}}:=H^{1}_{\mathrm{Dir}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})\times H^{\mathrm{m}}(\Omega)\times L^{2}(\Omega;{\mathbb{M}}^{n{\times}n}_{\mathrm{D}}).

For the rate-independent damage system with perfect plasticity,
the displacements are just functions of bounded deformation and the plastic strains are only bounded Radon measures on
\Omega\cup\Gamma_{\mathrm{Dir}}
, so that
the associated state space is

(2.11)

\displaystyle{\mathbf{Q}}_{\scriptstyle\tiny\mathrm{PP}}:=\{q=(u,z,p)\in

\displaystyle\mathrm{BD}(\Omega)\times H^{\mathrm{m}}(\Omega)\times{\mathrm{M}_{\mathrm{b}}(\Omega\cup\Gamma_{\mathrm{Dir}};{\mathbb{M}}^{n{\times}n}_{\mathrm{D}})}\,:

\displaystyle e:=\mathrm{E}(u)-p\in{L^{2}(\Omega;{\mathbb{M}^{n\times n}_{\mathrm{sym}}})},\ u\odot\mathrm{n}\,\mathscr{H}^{n-1}+p=0\text{ on }\Gamma_{\mathrm{Dir}}\}.

Observe that in the definition of
{\mathbf{Q}}_{\scriptstyle\tiny\mathrm{PP}}
it is in fact required that
(u,e,p)\in A(0)
: indeed,
the condition
u\odot\mathrm{n}\,\mathscr{H}^{n-1}+p=0
is a relaxation of the homogeneous Dirichlet condition

u=0
on
\Gamma_{\mathrm{Dir}}
.

The energy functionals

The energy functional governing the rate-dependent and rate-independent systems
with hardening

(
1.3
) and (
1.4
), respectively, consists of

(1)

a contribution featuring the elastic energy

{\mathcal{Q}}(z,e)=\int_{\Omega}\tfrac{1}{2}\mathbb{C}(z)e:e\;\!\mathrm{d}x;

(2)

the potential energy for the damage variable and for the hardening term;

(3)

the time-dependent volume and surface forces.

Namely, for
\mu>0
given,

{\mathcal{E}}_{\mu}\colon[0,T]\times{\mathbf{Q}}_{\scriptstyle\tiny\mathrm{H}}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
is defined by

\displaystyle{\mathcal{E}}_{\mu}(t,u,z,p):={\mathcal{Q}}(z,\mathrm{E}(u{+}w(t)){-}p)+\int_{\Omega}\left(W(z){+}\frac{\mu}{2}|p|^{2}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x+\frac{1}{2}a_{\mathrm{m}}(z,z)-\langle F(t),u+w(t)\rangle_{H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

In what follows, we will use the short-hand notation

e(t):=\mathrm{E}(u{+}w(t)){-}p

for the elastic part of the strain tensor.

The energy functional for the rate-independent perfectly plastic damage system (
1.1
) is

The dissipation potentials

The dissipation density
\mathrm{R}
from (

) clearly induces an integral functional

{\mathcal{R}}:L^{1}(\Omega)\to[0,+\infty]
. However,
since the damage flow rule will be posed in

(cf. (

) ahead),
we will restrict

to the space

denoting the restriction by the same symbol. Hence, we will work work with the functional

{\mathcal{R}}:H^{\mathrm{m}}(\Omega)\to[0,+\infty],\quad{\mathcal{R}}(\zeta)=\int_{\Omega}\mathrm{R}(\zeta(x))\;\!\mathrm{d}x

and with its convex analysis subdifferential

.
We will often use the following characterization of
\partial{\mathcal{R}}
, due to the
1
-homogeneity of the potential
{\mathcal{R}}
:

(2.15)

\chi\in\partial{\mathcal{R}}(\zeta)\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad\begin{cases}{\mathcal{R}}(\tilde{\zeta})\geq\sideset{}{{}_{H^{\mathrm{m}}}}{\mathop{\langle\chi,\tilde{\zeta}\rangle}}\text{ for all }\tilde{\zeta}\in H^{\mathrm{m}}(\Omega)\\
{\mathcal{R}}(\zeta)\leq\sideset{}{{}_{H^{\mathrm{m}}}}{\mathop{\langle\chi,\zeta\rangle}}\end{cases}\,.

For the systems with hardening (i.e. (
1.3
) and (
1.4
)), the

plastic dissipation potential

\mathcal{H}\colon{\mathrm{C}}^{0}(\overline{\Omega};[0,+\infty))\times L^{1}(\Omega;{\mathbb{M}}^{n{\times}n}_{\mathrm{D}})\to\mathbb{R}
is defined by

(2.16)

\mathcal{H}(z,\pi):=\int_{\Omega}H(z(x),\pi(x))\;\!\mathrm{d}x,

where
H
is given by (
2.6
)
and
\pi
is a place-holder for the plastic rate
\dot{p}
.
Its convex analysis subdifferential

\partial_{\pi}{\mathcal{H}}\colon{\mathrm{C}}^{0}(\overline{\Omega};[0,+\infty))\times L^{1}(\Omega;{\mathbb{M}}^{n{\times}n}_{\mathrm{D}})\rightrightarrows L^{\infty}(\Omega;{\mathbb{M}}^{n{\times}n}_{\mathrm{D}})
, w.r.t. the second variable
\pi
, given by

\omega\in\partial_{\pi}{\mathcal{H}}(z,\pi)\quad\text{ if and only if }\quad{\mathcal{H}}(z,\varrho)-{\mathcal{H}}(z,\pi)\geq\int_{\Omega}\omega(\varrho-\pi)\;\!\mathrm{d}x\quad\text{for all }\varrho\in L^{1}(\Omega;{\mathbb{M}}^{n{\times}n}_{\mathrm{D}}),

fulfills

(2.17)

\omega\in\partial_{\pi}{\mathcal{H}}(z,\pi)\quad\text{ if and only if }\quad\omega(x)\in\partial_{\pi}H(z(x),\pi(x))\quad\text{for a.a.}\,x\in\Omega.

A characterization analogous to (
2.17
) holds for the subdifferential

\partial_{\pi}{\mathcal{H}}(z,\cdot)\colon L^{1}(\Omega;{\mathbb{M}}^{n{\times}n}_{\mathrm{D}})\rightrightarrows L^{\infty}(\Omega;{\mathbb{M}}^{n{\times}n}_{\mathrm{D}})
.

In order to handle the perfectly plastic system for damage,
the plastic dissipation potential

has to be extended to the space

{\mathrm{M}_{\mathrm{b}}(\Omega\cup\Gamma_{\mathrm{Dir}};{\mathbb{M}}^{n{\times}n}_{\mathrm{D}})}
. We define

\mathcal{H}_{\scriptstyle\tiny\mathrm{PP}}\colon{\mathrm{C}}^{0}(\overline{\Omega};[0,1])\times{\mathrm{M}_{\mathrm{b}}(\Omega\cup\Gamma_{\mathrm{Dir}};{\mathbb{M}}^{n{\times}n}_{\mathrm{D}})}\to\mathbb{R}

\mathcal{H}_{\scriptstyle\tiny\mathrm{PP}}(z,\pi):=\int_{\Omega\cup\Gamma_{\mathrm{Dir}}}H\biggl(z(x),\frac{\mathrm{d}\pi}{\mathrm{d}\mu}(x)\biggr)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x),

where
\mu\in{\mathrm{M}_{\mathrm{b}}(\Omega\cup\Gamma_{\mathrm{Dir}};{\mathbb{M}}^{n{\times}n}_{\mathrm{D}})}
is a positive measure such that
\pi\ll\mu
and
\frac{\mathrm{d}\pi}{\mathrm{d}\mu}
is the Radon-Nikodým derivative of
p
with respect to
\mu
; by one-homogeneity of

, the definition of
\mathcal{H}_{\scriptstyle\tiny\mathrm{PP}}
does not depend of
\mu
.
For the theory of convex functions of measures we refer to
[
GS64
]
.
By
[
AFP05
, Proposition 2.37]
, for every

the functional

We recall that by Reshetnyak’s lower semicontinuity theorem, if
(z_{k})_{k}\subset{\mathrm{C}}^{0}(\overline{\Omega};[0,1])
and
(\pi_{k})_{k}\subset{\mathrm{M}_{\mathrm{b}}(\Omega\cup\Gamma_{\mathrm{Dir}};{\mathbb{M}}^{n{\times}n}_{\mathrm{D}})}
are such that

in
{\mathrm{C}}^{0}(\overline{\Omega})
and
\pi_{k}\rightharpoonup\pi
weakly
∗
in
{\mathrm{M}_{\mathrm{b}}(\Omega\cup\Gamma_{\mathrm{Dir}};{\mathbb{M}}^{n{\times}n}_{\mathrm{D}})}
, then

\mathcal{H}_{\scriptstyle\tiny\mathrm{PP}}(z,\pi)\leq\liminf_{k\rightarrow+\infty}\mathcal{H}_{\scriptstyle\tiny\mathrm{PP}}(z_{k},\pi_{k}).

Finally,
from
[
FG12
, Proposition 3.9]
it follows that for every
\sigma\in\mathcal{K}_{z}(\Omega)

(2.18)

H\biggl(z,\frac{\mathrm{d}p}{\mathrm{d}|p|}\biggr)|p|\geq[\sigma_{\mathrm{D}}:p]\quad\text{as measures on }\Omega\cup\Gamma_{\mathrm{Dir}}.

In particular, we have

(2.19)

\mathcal{H}_{\scriptstyle\tiny\mathrm{PP}}(z,p)\geq\sup_{\sigma\in\mathcal{K}_{z}(\Omega)}\langle\sigma_{\mathrm{D}}\,|\,p\rangle\,\qquad\text{for every $p\in\Pi(\Omega)$.}

The gradient structure for system (

It was proved in

The energy-dissipation balance for the viscous system

As observed in

, Proposition 3.4]

\int_{0}^{t}\left(\Psi_{\varepsilon,\nu}(q(r),q^{\prime}(r))+\Psi_{\varepsilon,\nu}^{*}(q(r),{-}{\mathrm{D}}_{q}{\mathcal{E}}_{\mu}(r,q(r)))\right)\;\!\mathrm{d}r+{\mathcal{E}}_{\mu}(t,q(t))={\mathcal{E}}_{\mu}(0,q(0))+\int_{0}^{t}\partial_{t}{\mathcal{E}}_{\mu}(r,q(r))\;\!\mathrm{d}r

for every
t\in[0,T]
.
Let us now consider a family
(q_{\varepsilon,\nu}^{\mu})
of solutions to (
2.20
). In
[
CR19
]
suitable
a priori estimates, uniform w.r.t. the parameters

, for the length (measured in an appropriate norm) of the curves

were derived. Based on such estimates it is possible to reparameterize such curves, obtaining
parameterized curves
defined on an ‘artificial’ time interval
[0,S]

(\mathsf{t}_{\varepsilon,\nu}^{\mu},\mathsf{q}_{\varepsilon,\nu}^{\mu}):[0,S]\to[0,T]\times{\mathbf{Q}}_{\scriptstyle\tiny\mathrm{H}}\quad\text{with }\mathsf{t}_{\varepsilon,\nu}^{\mu}:=(\mathsf{s}_{\varepsilon,\nu}^{\mu})^{-1},\quad\mathsf{q}_{\varepsilon,\nu}^{\mu}:=q_{\varepsilon,\nu}^{\mu}\circ\mathsf{t}_{\varepsilon,\nu}^{\mu}

(suitable) arclength functions associated with the curves
(q_{\varepsilon,\nu}^{\mu})
.
Now, in terms of the parameterized curves
(\mathsf{t}_{\varepsilon,\nu}^{\mu},\mathsf{q}_{\varepsilon,\nu}^{\mu})
, (
3.1
) translates into the

energy-dissipation balance

(3.2)

\displaystyle{\displaystyle\mathcal{E}}(\mathsf{t}_{\varepsilon,\nu}^{\mu}(s),\mathsf{q}_{\varepsilon,\nu}^{\mu}(s))+\int_{0}^{s}{\mathcal{M}}_{\varepsilon}^{\mu,\nu}(\mathsf{t}_{\varepsilon,\nu}^{\mu}(r),\mathsf{q}_{\varepsilon,\nu}^{\mu}(r),(\mathsf{t}_{\varepsilon,\nu}^{\mu})^{\prime}(r),(\mathsf{q}_{\varepsilon,\nu}^{\mu})^{\prime}(r))\;\!\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle={\mathcal{E}}(\mathsf{t}_{\varepsilon,\nu}^{\mu}(0),\mathsf{q}_{\varepsilon,\nu}^{\mu}(0))+\int_{0}^{s}\partial_{t}{\mathcal{E}}(\mathsf{t}_{\varepsilon,\nu}^{\mu}(r),\mathsf{q}_{\varepsilon,\nu}^{\mu}(r))\,(\mathsf{t}_{\varepsilon,\nu}^{\mu})^{\prime}(r)\;\!\mathrm{d}r

featuring the functional
{\mathcal{M}}_{\varepsilon}^{\mu,\nu}:[0,T]\times{\mathbf{Q}}_{\scriptstyle\tiny\mathrm{H}}\times(0,+\infty)\times{\mathbf{Q}}_{\scriptstyle\tiny\mathrm{H}}\to[0,+\infty]

(3.3)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{M}}_{\varepsilon}^{\mu,\nu}(t,q,t^{\prime},q^{\prime}):={\mathcal{R}}(z^{\prime})+{\mathcal{H}}(z,p^{\prime})+{\mathcal{M}}_{\varepsilon,\mathrm{red}}^{\mu,\nu}(t,q,t^{\prime},q^{\prime})

with the reduced functional

\displaystyle{\mathcal{M}}_{\varepsilon,\mathrm{red}}^{\mu,\nu}\text{ defined by }

\displaystyle{\mathcal{M}}_{\varepsilon,\mathrm{red}}^{\mu,\nu}(t,q,t^{\prime},q^{\prime}):=\ \frac{\varepsilon}{2t^{\prime}}\mathcal{D}_{\nu}(q^{\prime})^{2}+\frac{t^{\prime}}{2\varepsilon}(\mathcal{D}_{\nu}^{*,\mu}(t,q))^{2},

and

(3.4)

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{\nu}({q}^{\prime}):=\sqrt{\nu\|{u}^{\prime}(t)\|^{2}_{H^{1},\mathbb{D}}{+}\|{z}^{\prime}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}{+}\nu\|{p}^{\prime}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}},

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{\nu}^{*,\mu}({t},{q}):=\sqrt{\frac{1}{\nu}\,\|{-}\mathrm{D}_{u}{\mathcal{E}}_{\mu}({t},{q})\|^{2}_{(H^{1},\mathbb{D})^{*}}+\widetilde{d}_{L^{2}}({-}\mathrm{D}_{z}{\mathcal{E}}_{\mu}({t},{q}),\partial{\mathcal{R}}(0))^{2}+\frac{1}{\nu}\,d_{L^{2}}({-}\mathrm{D}_{p}{\mathcal{E}}_{\mu}({t},{q}),\partial_{\pi}{\mathcal{H}}({z},0))^{2}}\,.

In (
3.4
),
\|\cdot\|_{H^{1},\mathbb{D}}
and
\|\cdot\|_{(H^{1},\mathbb{D})^{*}}
are the norms introduced in (
2.4
) and (
2.5

is defined by

\widetilde{d}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}({\chi},\partial{\mathcal{R}}(0))^{2}:=\min_{\gamma\in\partial{\mathcal{R}}(0)}\mathfrak{f}_{2}({\chi}{-}\gamma)\quad\text{with }\mathfrak{f}_{2}(\beta):=\begin{cases}\|\beta\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}&\text{if }\beta\in L^{2}(\Omega),\\

From (

. Hence,

in
(s_{1},s_{2})

property
(
4.3a
),
joint with the previously found

(4.3b)

\displaystyle\mathsf{t}(s)\equiv\mathsf{t}(s_{1}),\ \ \mathsf{z}(s)\equiv\mathsf{z}(s_{1})

\displaystyle\text{ for all }s\in[s_{1},s_{2}],

(4.3c)

\displaystyle-\mathrm{div}\,\mathbb{D}\mathrm{E}(\mathsf{u}^{\prime}(s))-\widehat{\lambda}(s)\mathrm{Div}\,{\sigma}(s)=\widehat{\lambda}(s)F(\mathsf{t}(s_{1}))

\displaystyle\text{for a.a.}\,s\in(s_{1},s_{2}),

\displaystyle\widehat{\lambda}(s)\zeta(s)+\mathsf{p}^{\prime}(s)\ni\widehat{\lambda}(s){\sigma}_{\mathrm{D}}(s)

\displaystyle\text{for a.a.}\,s\in(s_{1},s_{2})\,,

\displaystyle\text{with }\widehat{\lambda}:=\tfrac{1{-}\lambda_{\mathsf{u},\mathsf{p}}}{\lambda_{\mathsf{u},\mathsf{p}}}=\frac{\sqrt{\|\mathsf{u}^{\prime}\|_{H^{1},\mathbb{D}}^{2}{+}\|\mathsf{p}^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega;{\mathbb{M}}^{n{\times}n}_{\mathrm{D}})}^{2}}}{\mathcal{D}^{*}(\mathsf{t},\mathsf{q})}

(observe that
\widehat{\lambda}=\tilde{\lambda}^{-1}
with
\tilde{\lambda}
from, (

In turn, it can be easily checked that, for a
given
function

4.3b
)—(

) does
admit a unique solution.

Step
2
:

Since the function
[0,S]\ni s\mapsto\mathcal{D}^{*}(\mathsf{t}(s),\mathsf{q}(s))
is lower semicontinuous by

[
CR19
, Lemma 7.8]
,
the set
\mathfrak{S}
is closed, hence its complement
[0,S]\setminus\mathfrak{S}
is relatively open, and thus it is the finite or countable union of disjoint intervals. Its connected components are of the form
(s_{1},S]
,

(s_{2},s_{3})
,
[0,s_{4})
or
[0,S]
(if
\mathfrak{S}=\mathchar 31\relax
). By the lower semicontinuity of

, it is immediate to check that

for all
j\in\{1,\ldots,4\}
.

Now, we aim to show that connected components of the type
(s_{1},S]
and

(s_{2},s_{3})

cannot occur. To this end,
let us study the properties of the
\mathrm{BV}_{0}^{0,0}
solution

(\mathsf{t},\mathsf{q})
on an interval of the type
(s_{1},S]
or
(s_{2},s_{3})
, with
s_{1},\,s_{2}\in\mathfrak{S}
. Since

\mathcal{D}^{*}(\mathsf{t}(s_{j}),\mathsf{q}(s_{j}))=0
, we have
that

{\mathcal{S}}_{u}{\mathcal{E}}_{0}(\mathsf{t}(s_{j}),\mathsf{q}(s_{j}))=\mathcal{W}_{p}{\mathcal{E}}_{0}(\mathsf{t}(s_{j}),\mathsf{q}(s_{j}))=0

As shown in Step
1
, the evolution on the intervals

and
(s_{2},s_{3})
is characterized by
(
4.3
). Recall that system (

) admits a unique solution. Now,

{\mathcal{S}}_{u}{\mathcal{E}}_{0}(\mathsf{t}(s_{j}),\mathsf{q}(s_{j}))=\mathcal{W}_{p}{\mathcal{E}}_{0}(\mathsf{t}(s_{j}),\mathsf{q}(s_{j}))=0

and

Therefore,

does not possess connected components of the form

or

, or

Suppose that

5.1.

As recalled in the Introduction,

and

5.2
ahead.

In order to recall the definition of

\mu>0
, we need to introduce the related vanishing-viscosity contact potential

\displaystyle{\displaystyle\mathcal{M}}_{0}^{\mu,0}:[0,T]\times{\mathbf{Q}}_{\scriptstyle\tiny\mathrm{H}}\times[0,+\infty)\times{\mathbf{Q}}_{\scriptstyle\tiny\mathrm{H}}\to[0,+\infty],

\displaystyle{\displaystyle\mathcal{M}}_{0}^{\mu,0}(t,q,t^{\prime},q^{\prime}):={\mathcal{R}}(z^{\prime})+{\mathcal{H}}(z,p^{\prime})+{\mathcal{M}}_{0,\mathrm{red}}^{\mu,0}(t,u,z,p,t^{\prime},u^{\prime},z^{\prime},p^{\prime})

where

(5.1a)

\displaystyle\text{if }t^{\prime}>0,\quad

\displaystyle{\mathcal{M}}_{0,\mathrm{red}}^{\mu,0}(t,q,t^{\prime},q^{\prime}):=\begin{cases}0&\text{if }\begin{cases}-\mathrm{D}_{u}{\mathcal{E}}_{\mu}(t,q)=0,\\
-\mathrm{D}_{z}{\mathcal{E}}_{\mu}(t,q)\in\partial{\mathcal{R}}(0),\ \text{and}\\
-\mathrm{D}_{p}{\mathcal{E}}_{\mu}(t,q)\in\partial_{\pi}{\mathcal{H}}(z,0),\end{cases}\\
+\infty&\text{otherwise,}\end{cases}

(5.1b)

\displaystyle\text{if }t^{\prime}=0,\quad

\displaystyle{\mathcal{M}}_{0,\mathrm{red}}^{\mu,0}(t,q,t^{\prime},q^{\prime}):=\begin{cases}\mathcal{D}(u^{\prime},p^{\prime})\,\mathcal{D}^{*,\mu}(t,q)&\text{if }z^{\prime}=0,\\
\|z^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}}\,\widetilde{d}_{L^{2}}({-}\mathrm{D}_{z}{\mathcal{E}}_{\mu}(t,q),\partial{\mathcal{R}}(0))&\text{if }\mathcal{D}^{*,\mu}(t,q)=0\\

In (

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}(u^{\prime},p^{\prime}):=\sqrt{\|u^{\prime}\|_{H^{1},\mathbb{D}}^{2}{+}\|p^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega;{\mathbb{M}}^{n{\times}n}_{\mathrm{D}})}^{2}},

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}^{*,\mu}(t,q):=\sqrt{\|{-}\mathrm{D}_{u}{\mathcal{E}}_{\mu}(t,q)\|^{2}_{(H^{1},\mathbb{D})^{*}}{+}d_{L^{2}}({-}\mathrm{D}_{p}{\mathcal{E}}_{\mu}(t,q),\partial_{\pi}{\mathcal{H}}(z,0))^{2}}.

We are now in a position to recall the notion of solution to system (
1.4
) from

[
CR19
, Def. 6.10]
. Observe that it involves (reparameterized) curves that are absolutely continuous on the
whole
interval
[0,S]
, with values in
{\mathbf{Q}}_{\scriptstyle\tiny\mathrm{H}}
.

Definition 5.1
.

We call a parameterized curve

(parameterized) Balanced Viscosity

Remark 5.2
.

In
[
CR19
, Prop. 6.11]
a characterization was provided for

solutions in terms of a subdifferential system that features two positive parameters

Accordingly, repeating the very same arguments as in the proof of Theorem

concept).

We mention in advance that the analogue of Theorem
4.1
does not hold, instead, for
\mathrm{BV}_{0}^{\mu,\nu}
solutions.

5.2.
Vanishing-hardening analysis for
single-rate
solutions

\mathrm{BV}
solutions to the
single-rate
system with hardening have been obtained in
[
CR19
, Section 6.1]
by
performing the asymptotic analysis of the reparameterized energy-dissipation balance
(
3.2
) as the viscosity parameter
\varepsilon
tends to
0
, while keeping the hardening and the rate parameters
\mu
and
\nu
fixed. That is why we refer to them as
\mathrm{BV}_{0}^{\mu,\nu}
solutions to the system with hardening.
Their definition involves the
corresponding
vanishing-viscosity contact potential
{\mathcal{M}}_{0}^{\mu,\nu}:[0,T]\times{\mathbf{Q}}_{\scriptstyle\tiny\mathrm{H}}\times[0,+\infty)\times{\mathbf{Q}}_{\scriptstyle\tiny\mathrm{H}}\to[0,+\infty]
given by

\displaystyle{\mathcal{M}}_{0}^{\mu,\nu}(t,q,t^{\prime},q^{\prime}):={\mathcal{R}}(z^{\prime})+{\mathcal{H}}(z,p^{\prime})+{\mathcal{M}}_{0,\mathrm{red}}^{\mu,\nu}(t,q,t^{\prime},q^{\prime}),\quad\text{where}

(5.13a)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\text{if }t^{\prime}>0,\quad{\mathcal{M}}_{0,\mathrm{red}}^{\mu,\nu}(t,q,t^{\prime},q^{\prime}):=\begin{cases}0&\text{if }\begin{cases}-\mathrm{D}_{u}{\mathcal{E}}_{\mu}(t,q)=0,\\
-\mathrm{D}_{z}{\mathcal{E}}_{\mu}(t,q)\in\partial{\mathcal{R}}(0),\ \text{and}\\
-\mathrm{D}_{p}{\mathcal{E}}_{\mu}(t,q)\in\partial_{\pi}{\mathcal{H}}(z,0),\end{cases}\\
+\infty&\text{otherwise,}\end{cases}\end{aligned}

(5.13b)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\text{if }t^{\prime}=0,\quad{\mathcal{M}}_{0,\mathrm{red}}^{\mu,\nu}(t,q,0,q^{\prime}):=\mathcal{D}_{\nu}(q^{\prime})\,\mathcal{D}_{\nu}^{*,\mu}(t,q).\end{aligned}

For better readability, we also recall that

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{\nu}({q}^{\prime}):=\sqrt{\nu\|{u}^{\prime}(t)\|^{2}_{H^{1},\mathbb{D}}{+}\|{z}^{\prime}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}{+}\nu\|{p}^{\prime}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}},

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{\nu}^{*,\mu}({t},{q}):=\sqrt{\frac{1}{\nu}\,\|{-}\mathrm{D}_{u}{\mathcal{E}}_{\mu}({t},{q})\|^{2}_{(H^{1},\mathbb{D})^{*}}+\widetilde{d}_{L^{2}}({-}\mathrm{D}_{z}{\mathcal{E}}_{\mu}({t},{q}),\partial{\mathcal{R}}(0))^{2}+\frac{1}{\nu}\,d_{L^{2}}({-}\mathrm{D}_{p}{\mathcal{E}}_{\mu}({t},{q}),\partial_{\pi}{\mathcal{H}}({z},0))^{2}}.

It is worthwhile to remark that the
reduced
functional
{\mathcal{M}}_{0,\mathrm{red}}^{\mu,\nu}
, at
t^{\prime}=0
,
simultaneously
encompasses viscosity for the three variables
u
,
z
, and
p
. Instead,
its counterpart

{\mathcal{M}}_{0,\mathrm{red}}^{\mu,0}
for
\mathrm{BV}_{0}^{\mu,0}
solutions features, in the jump regime
t^{\prime}=0
, a viscous contribution in the variables
(u,p)
when
z^{\prime}=0
, and viscosity in
z
when
\mathcal{D}^{*,\mu}(t,q)=0
, i.e. when
u
is at elastic equilibrium and
p
is locally stable.

We are now in a position to recall
the notion of
\mathrm{BV}_{0}^{\mu,\nu}
solution, cf.
[
CR19
, Definition 6.2]
.

Definition 5.5
.

We call a parameterized curve
(\mathsf{t},\mathsf{q})=(\mathsf{t},\mathsf{u},\mathsf{z},\mathsf{p})\in\mathrm{AC}([0,S];[0,T]{\times}{\mathbf{Q}}_{\scriptstyle\tiny\mathrm{H}})

a
(parameterized) Balanced Viscosity
solution to the

system with hardening
(
1.4
)
(a
\mathrm{BV}_{0}^{\mu,\nu}

\mathsf{t}\colon[0,S]\to[0,T]

(\mathsf{t},\mathsf{q})
fulfills for all

(5.14)

{\mathcal{E}}_{\mu}(\mathsf{t}(s),\mathsf{q}(s))+\int_{0}^{s}{\mathcal{M}}_{0}^{\mu,\nu}(\mathsf{t}(\tau),\mathsf{q}(\tau),\mathsf{t}^{\prime}(\tau),\mathsf{q}^{\prime}(\tau))\;\!\mathrm{d}\tau={\mathcal{E}}_{\mu}(\mathsf{t}(0),\mathsf{q}(0))+\int_{0}^{s}\partial_{t}{\mathcal{E}}_{\mu}(\mathsf{t}(\tau),\mathsf{q}(\tau))\,\mathsf{t}^{\prime}(\tau)\;\!\mathrm{d}\tau.

We say that
(\mathsf{t},\mathsf{q})
is
non-degenerate
if it fulfills (
4.1
).

Let us now address the asymptotic analysis of the above solutions for
a vanishing sequence
(\mu_{k})_{k}

As mentioned in the Introduction, in the construction of

\displaystyle\exists\,C>0\ \ \forall\,k\in\mathbb{N}\,\ \text{for a.a.}\,s\in(0,S)\,:

Lemma A.1
.

Let
\mathsf{f},\mathsf{f}_{k}\,:\ [0,S]\to[0,T]
be nondecreasing functions such that
\mathsf{f}_{k}\to\mathsf{f}
uniformly.
Let
A:=\{s\in[0,S]\,:\ \mathsf{f}^{\prime}(s)>0\}
.
Then for a.a.
s\in A
there is a subsequence
k_{j}

and, for every
j
, there is
s_{k_{j}}\in(0,S)
such that

|s_{k_{j}}{-}s|<\frac{1}{j}
and
\mathsf{f}^{\prime}_{k_{j}}(s_{k_{j}})>0
.

Proof.

Let

\Xi:=\{s\in A\,:\ \exists\,k(s)\ \forall\,k>k(s)\ \exists\,\delta_{k}\ \mathsf{f}^{\prime}_{k}(\sigma)=0\ \text{for a.a.}\ \sigma\in(s-\delta_{k},s+\delta_{k})\}.

We shall prove that
\Xi
is at most countable, which implies the statement of the lemma.
Let
s^{1},s^{2}\in\Xi
.
Then one has
\mathsf{f}(s^{1})\neq\mathsf{f}(s^{2})
; indeed,
since
\mathsf{f}
is nondecreasing,
\mathsf{f}(s^{1})=\mathsf{f}(s^{2})

\mathsf{f}
is constant in

which is in contrast with the assumption

(and thus

Let now

for
i=1,2
.
Since
y^{i}_{k}\to\mathsf{f}(s^{i})
for
i=1,2
, for
k
sufficiently large one has
|y^{1}_{k}-y^{2}_{k}|>\frac{1}{2}|\mathsf{f}(s^{1})-\mathsf{f}(s^{2})|>0
.
Let
\phi_{k}\in\mathrm{BV}(0,T)
denote the inverse function of
\mathsf{f}_{k}
.
It turns out that
y^{1}_{k},y^{2}_{k}
are both jump points of
\phi_{k}
for every

.
Since the jump points of a
\mathrm{BV}
function are countable, it follows that
\Xi
is countable, too.
∎

Piecewise smoothness for linear elliptic systems with piecewise smooth coefficients
Youchan Kim

Abstract
. Li and Vogelius, and Li and Nirenberg obtained piecewise C 1 , γ \displaystyle C^{1,\gamma} -regularity for linear elliptic problems with piecewise C γ \displaystyle C^{\gamma} -coefficients which come from composite materials. In this paper, we obtain piecewise C m + 1 , γ \displaystyle C^{m+1,\gamma} -regularity for linear elliptic systems with piecewise C m , γ \displaystyle C^{m,\gamma} -coefficients which also come from composite materials. This answers the open problem suggested by Li and Vogelius, and Li and Nirenberg.

Introduction and main result

1.1.
Introduction

We consider linear elliptic systems arising from composite materials such as fiber reinforced materials. Assume that
\displaystyle\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
is an open bounded
\displaystyle C^{m,\gamma}
-domain, and
\displaystyle\{\Omega^{k}\}_{k=1}^{k_{0}}
are the mutually disjoint
\displaystyle C^{m,\gamma}
-subdomains in
\displaystyle\Omega
which are allowed to touch each other. We denote
\displaystyle\Omega^{0}=\Omega\backslash\cup_{k=1}^{k_{0}}\Omega^{k}
. Let
\displaystyle u
be a weak solution of an linear elliptic equation

D_{\alpha}\left[A^{\alpha\beta}_{ij}D_{\beta}u^{j}\right]=D_{\alpha}F_{\alpha}^{i}\quad\text{in}\quad\Omega,

where
\displaystyle A^{\alpha\beta}_{ij}\in C^{m,\gamma}(\Omega^{k})
and
\displaystyle F_{\alpha}^{i}\in C^{m,\gamma}(\Omega^{k})
for any
\displaystyle 1\leq\alpha,\beta\leq n
,
\displaystyle 1\leq i,j\leq N
and
\displaystyle k\in[0,k_{0}]
. With our assumption,
\displaystyle A^{\alpha\beta}_{ij}
and
\displaystyle F_{\alpha}^{i}
are allowed to be discontinuous across the boundary of the subdomains
\displaystyle\{\partial\Omega^{k}\}_{k=1}^{k_{0}}
. In this paper, we prove that the weak solution
\displaystyle u
is locally piecewise
\displaystyle C^{m+1,\gamma}
, meaning that
\displaystyle u
is
\displaystyle C^{m+1,\gamma}(\tilde{\Omega}\cap\Omega^{k})
for any compact subset
\displaystyle\tilde{\Omega}\subset\subset\Omega
and
\displaystyle k\in[0,k_{0}]
.

An application of our result is the following. Suppose that the fibers and resin of a fiber reinforced plastic(FRP) is assumed to be linearly elastic, and described by a linear elliptic equation. Then due to the discontinuity of the coefficients or elasticity, the gradient of the weak solution might be discontinuous on the boundary of each materials. Nevertheless, our result shows that the weak solution on each materials(fiber, resin) can be smooth, even if the fibers are allowed to touch each other. To prove our result, we generalize the difference quotient method to curves instead of traditional fixed directions.

One of long standing problem for the regularity theory of composite material is establishing a new regularity theory when the subdomains
\displaystyle\{\Omega^{k}\}_{k=1}^{k_{0}}
are allowed to touch each other. Under this assumption, the flattening argument could not be applied at the points where two subdomains touch each other, and the difficulty arises. With this geometric assumption, local Lipschitz regularity can be derived as in
[
3
,
8
,
9
]
. But better regularity than Lipschitz regularity is not feasible, because discontinuity of the gradient might occur on the boundary of subdomains. However, from the observations made in the previous literatures, one can consider so called piecewise regularity results. For example, if the distance between the boundary of subdomains are bigger than some positive constant, then one may use the flattening argument to prove local piecewise
\displaystyle C^{m,\gamma}
-regularity. But this estimate depends on the distance between the boundary of subdomains and could not be applied for composite materials. When two subdomains are allowed to touch each other, which might happen for composite materials, the best result known in the previous literatures is piecewise
\displaystyle C^{1,\gamma}
-regularity by Vogelius and Li
[
9
]
, and Li and Nirenberg
[
8
]
. In that papers, they used observation that in small scales, the boundaries of two touching subdomains are almost flat near the touching point, and such geometric structures could be viewed as linear laminates. Indeed,
[
9
]
and
[
8
]
proved that the solution is
\displaystyle C^{1,\gamma}(\tilde{\Omega}\cap\Omega^{k})
for any compact subset
\displaystyle\tilde{\Omega}\subset\Omega
and
\displaystyle k\in[0,k_{0}]
by using the regularity theory of linear laminates such as Chipot, Kinderlehrer and Vergara-Caffarelli
[
4
]
, and in fact their estimates does not depend on the distance between the boundary of subdomains. However, the method in
[
9
]
and
[
8
]
could not be extended to higher derivatives
\displaystyle C^{m,\gamma}
with
\displaystyle m\geq 2
, and in
[
8
]
they suggest an open question that piecewise
\displaystyle C^{m,\gamma}
-regularity even holds for
\displaystyle m\geq 2
. On the other-hand, regularity results for higher derivatives have been obtained for special geometric structures with dimension
\displaystyle 2
, when the domain
\displaystyle\Omega
is a disk, and two subdomains are also disks. In that case,
[
9
]
proved
\displaystyle C^{m,\gamma}
for
\displaystyle m\geq 2
by using conformal mapping theory, but unfortunately their method could not be extended to higher dimensions. We also refer to
[
10
]
for a similar result by using Green functions. With the observation in this paragraph, it has been expected that the conjecture about piecewise
\displaystyle C^{m,\gamma}
-regularity for
\displaystyle m\geq 2
seems true, and in this paper we prove that this conjecture indeed holds.

To prove our main result, we generalize the difference quotient method. As far as we are concerned, the difference quotient method have been applied for a fixed direction, but we generalize this method by applying to curves. In fact, we use so called a ‘flow’ to obtain higher regularity, because the computation of difference quotient method is complicated for the higher regularity and we will use an approximation argument for obtaining higher regularity.

If a curve does not across the boundary of subdomains, then the coefficients remain regular along that curve, even if the coefficients are discontinuous between different subdomains. So we choose a suitable flow so that if we restrict the domain of the flow to some subdomain
\displaystyle\Omega^{k}
, then the range of that flow still remains in the same subdomain
\displaystyle\Omega^{k}
. To explain our new method, let’s consider our model case.
Let
\displaystyle\Omega=[-2,2]\times[-4,4]\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
,
\displaystyle\varphi_{+}(x_{2})=\left(x^{2}\right)^{2}
and
\displaystyle\varphi_{-}(x^{2})=-\left(x^{2}\right)^{2}
where
\displaystyle x=\left(x^{1},x^{2}\right)
. Then
\displaystyle\varphi_{+}
and
\displaystyle\varphi_{-}
touches each other at the origin
\displaystyle(0,0)
. We choose a function
\displaystyle\sigma:[-1,1]\times[-1,1]\times[-1,1]\rightarrow[-4,4]
such that

\sigma(x,t)=\frac{[\varphi_{+}(x^{2}+t)-\varphi_{+}(x^{2})][x_{1}-\varphi_{-}(x^{2})]}{\varphi_{+}(x^{2})-\varphi_{-}(x^{2})}+\frac{[\varphi_{-}(x^{2}+t)-\varphi_{-}(x^{2})][\varphi_{+}(x^{2})-x_{1}]}{\varphi_{+}(x^{2})-\varphi_{-}(x^{2})}.

Then
\displaystyle t\mapsto x+(\sigma(x,t),t)
can be viewed as a flow in
\displaystyle Q_{1}^{0}:=\{(x_{1},x^{2})\in[-1,1]\times[-1,1]:\varphi_{-}(x^{2})\leq x_{1}\leq\varphi_{+}(x^{2})\}
, and we find that
\displaystyle\partial_{t}[f(x+(\sigma,t))]_{t=0}
is the first difference quotient or the first derivative along the curve
\displaystyle t\mapsto\sigma(x,t)
.

However, this approach only works for piecewise
\displaystyle C^{2,\gamma}
-regularity. The reason is that if two subdomains touches each other, then the first derivative of two boundaries coincide at that touching point, but the higher derivatives might not coincide at that touching point. To explain, we calculate
\displaystyle 2
-nd derivative of
\displaystyle h\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
along the curves
\displaystyle\{\left(\varphi_{+}(x^{2}),x^{2}\right):x^{2}\in[-1,1]\}
and
\displaystyle\{\left(\varphi_{-}(x^{2}),x^{2}\right):x^{2}\in[-1,1]\}
at the origin by using the following calculation:

\displaystyle\partial_{t}^{2}[h(\varphi(t),t)]

\displaystyle=\varphi^{\prime}(x^{2}+t)^{2}D_{11}h(\varphi(t),t)+2\varphi^{\prime}(x^{2}+t)^{2}D_{12}h(\varphi(t),t)

\displaystyle+D_{22}h(\varphi(t),t)+\varphi^{\prime\prime}(x^{2}+t)D_{1}h(\varphi(t),t).

Since
\displaystyle\varphi^{\prime\prime}_{+}(0)=2\not=\varphi^{\prime\prime}_{-}(0)=-2
and
\displaystyle\varphi^{\prime}_{+}(0)=\varphi^{\prime}_{-}(0)=0
,
\displaystyle D_{1}h(0,0)\not=0
implies
\displaystyle\partial_{t}^{2}[h(\varphi_{+}(t),t)]\big|_{t=0}\not=\partial_{t}^{2}[h(\varphi_{-}(t),t)]\big|_{t=0}
. On the other-hand, it is possible to obtain
\displaystyle C^{2,\gamma}
by just using the approach in the previous paragraph because
\displaystyle\partial_{t}[h(\varphi_{+}(t),t)]\big|_{t=0}=\partial_{t}[h(\varphi_{-}(t),t)]\big|_{t=0}
.

By our model case, we see that there are jumps of the higher order derivatives on the boundary of subdomains. In fact, this jumps appears even if
\displaystyle h
is smooth which means that the jumps of the higher order derivatives are mainly due to the geometry of the composite domains. Because of this reason, we obtained geometric property of composite domains in
[
7
,
2
]
. In
[
7
]
, the author and Jang prove that the boundary of the subdomains are almost parallel if they are sufficiently close, even if the subdomains are fractal, say Reifenberg flat domains. Also
[
2
]
answers an question for the typical examples of the composite domains. If
\displaystyle C^{1,\gamma}
-domains satisfy certain inclusion conditions, they form a composite domains.

To overcome the difficulty for handling the jumps of the higher order derivatives on the boundary of subdomains, we extend functions defined in a subdomain. We briefly explain this idea by an example. Let
\displaystyle u
be a function which might have jumps on the boundary of subdomains. Let
\displaystyle u^{k}
be an extension of
\displaystyle u
from
\displaystyle\Omega^{k}
to
\displaystyle\Omega
such that
\displaystyle u^{k}
is regular in
\displaystyle\Omega\setminus\Omega^{k}
. Then we see that
\displaystyle\sum_{k}u^{k}
has no jumps on the boundary of the subdomains. So by considering
\displaystyle\sum_{k}u^{k}
instead of
\displaystyle u
, jumps of the function is removed and the function became easier for obtaining piecewise regularity. The value of
\displaystyle u
and
\displaystyle\sum_{k}u^{k}
might not be the same in
\displaystyle\Omega
, but
\displaystyle u-\sum_{k}u^{k}
is piecewise regular because
\displaystyle u^{k}
is an extension of
\displaystyle u
in
\displaystyle\Omega^{k}
and defined to be regular in
\displaystyle\Omega\setminus\Omega^{k}
. If a suitable perturbations could be done to
\displaystyle\sum_{k}u^{k}
in each subdomains, then one can obtain piecewise regularity of
\displaystyle u
. For partial differential equations, the functions can be differentiated. But the ideas in the previous paragraphs still work for our problem.

The results and the key idea of this paper was announced at ‘International Conference on
Partial Differential Equations Related to Material Science’, May 6-9, 2021.

Abstract
. This paper analyzes the cohomological dimension of the generalized binomial edge ideal 𝒥 K m , G \mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G} for a complete r r -partite graph G G . Additionally, the Krull dimension, the depth, the Castelnuovo–Mumford regularity, the Hilbert series, and the multiplicity of its quotient ring are explicitly determined.



1.
Introduction

When
n
is a positive integer,
[n]
represents the set
\{1,2,\dots,n-1,n\}
according to convention. Suppose that
m
and
n
are positive integers.
Let
S={\mathbb{K}}[\bm{X}]\coloneqq{\mathbb{K}}[x_{ij}:(i,j)\in[m]\times[n]]
be the polynomial ring in
mn
variables over a field
{\mathbb{K}}
.
In their paper
[
7
]
, Ene et al. introduced the binomial edge ideal of a pair of graphs. Specifically, let
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
be two simple graphs on the vertex sets
[m]
and
[n]
with the edge sets
E(G_{1})
and
E(G_{2})
, respectively. Given
e=\{i,j\}\in E(G_{1})
and
f=\{k,l\}\in E(G_{2})
, where
i<j
and
k<l
, we can assign a
2
-minor
p_{(e,f)}=[i,j\,|\,k,l]\coloneqq x_{ik}x_{jl}-x_{il}x_{jk}
to the pair
(e,f)
. The
binomial edge ideal of the pair
(G_{1},G_{2})
is defined as the ideal

\mathcal{J}_{G_{1},G_{2}}\coloneqq(p_{(e,f)}:e\in E(G_{1}),f\in E(G_{2}))

in
S
.

Let
G
be a finite simple graph with the vertex set
[n]
. The
generalized binomial edge ideal
of
G
is the binomial edge ideal of the pair
(K_{m},G)
, where
K_{m}
is a complete graph with
m
vertices.
Generalized binomial edge ideals have numerous applications in algebraic statistics for studying conditional independence, as noted in
[
19
]
. These ideals are a generalization of the classical binomial edge ideals studied in
[
8
,
16
]
. It is not surprising that certain properties of the classical binomial edge ideal
J_{G}
can be naturally extended to
\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G}
. For instance,
\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G}
is a radical ideal, and the subsets of
G
with the cut-point property determine the minimal primes of
\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G}
, as proven in
[
19
]
.

However, for
m\geq 3
, the generalized binomial edge ideal
\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G}
cannot be generated by maximal minors of the matrix
\bm{X}
. This gives rise to new phenomena arise. For example, Chaudhry and Irfan in
[
4
]
proved that, for a block graph
G
, the ideal
\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G}
is Cohen–Macaulay if and only if
\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G}
is unmixed, if and only if
G
is a complete graph. Kumar
[
15
]
demonstrated
that the upper bound of the regularity
\operatorname{reg}(S/\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G})
is
n-1
, which is achieved if
m\geq n
.

Building on their work, we explored the algebraic properties of generalized binomial edge ideals for specific graphs. In
[
22
]
, we provided precise formulas for the Krull dimension, depth and regularity of
S/\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G}
, where
G
is either a bipartite graph
F_{p}
or a
k
-fan graph
F_{k}^{W}(K_{n})
. These graphs serve as the foundation for constructing Cohen–Macaulay binomial edge ideals of bipartite graphs. In a recent study
[
23
]
, we analyzed the regularity and depth of the powers of the generalized binomial edge ideal
\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},P_{n}}
of a path graph
P_{n}
using the Sagbi basis theory.
Furthermore, we showed that the symbolic powers and ordinary powers of
\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},P_{n}}
are identical.

This paper focuses on the generalized binomial edge ideals of complete
r
-partite graphs.
In their work
[
21
]
, Schenzel and Zafar computed the Krull dimension, depth, regularity, Hilbert function and multiplicity of the binomial edge ideal of a complete bipartite graph. Wang and Tang also studied the regularity and depth of the powers of the binomial edge ideal of a complete bipartite graph in their papers
[
25
,
26
]
. Meanwhile, Ohtani
[
17
]
demonstrated that the symbolic powers and ordinary powers of
J_{G}
coincide when
G
is a complete
r
-partite graph.
However, there is limited understanding of the generalized binomial edge ideal of a complete
r
-partite graph.

The cohomological dimension of binomial edge ideals is also an intriguing topic. In their work
[
3
]
, Bruns and Schwänzl computed the cohomological dimension of
\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},K_{n}}
. Katsabekis
[
13
]
provided a lower bound on the cohomological dimension of
\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G}
for any non-complete graph
G
, based on its vertex connectivity. Particularly, he obtained interesting results for path graphs and star graphs. What can be said about the cohomological dimension of the generalized binomial edge ideal of complete
r
-partite graphs?

The article is organized as follows. Section
2
presents essential definitions and terminology that will be necessary later.
In Section
3
,
we begin by analyzing the primary decomposition of
\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G}
, where
G
is a complete
r
-partite graph. This enables us to quickly obtain the Krull dimension and the depth of
S/\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G}
. As a corollary, we generalize a recent result of Katsabekis on the cohomological dimension.
We then compute the Castelnuovo–Mumford regularity, Hilbert series, and multiplicity of
S/\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G}
. It is important to note that Schenzel and Zafar computed the regularity of binomial edge ideals of complete bipartite graphs as a by-product of a long and meticulous analysis of the modules of deficiencies. In contrast, we present here an exact formula for the regularity of the generalized binomial edge ideal of a complete
r
-partite graph, using the comparison-by-induced-subgraph strategy, which significantly shortens the proof.
Finally, we conclude this paper by demonstrating that the binomial edge ideal of a complete
r
-partite graph is of König type, following the definition of Herzog et al. in
[
9
]
. This generalizes a recent result of Williams in
[
27
]
for complete bipartite graphs.

[

,
2
,

.

2.1.
Notions of simple graphs

To begin, let us review some fundamental concepts regarding graphs. Let
G
be a simple graph with the vertex set
V(G)
and the edge set
E(G)
. For any subset
A
of
V(G)
, the
induced subgraph
of
G
on the vertex set
A
, denoted by
G|_{A}
, satisfies that
V(G|_{A})=A
and for any
i,j\in A
,
\{i,j\}\in E(G|_{A})
if and only if
\{i,j\}\in E(G)
. At the same time, the induced subgraph of
G
on the set
V(G)\setminus A
will be denoted by
G\setminus A
. In particular, if
A=\{v\}
then we will write
G\setminus v
instead of
G\setminus\{v\}
for simplicity.

Given positive integers
n_{1}
and
n_{2}
, where
n_{1}\leq n_{2}
, the set
[n_{1},n_{2}]
is defined as
\{n_{1},n_{1}+1,\dots,n_{2}-1,n_{2}\}
. If
n_{1}=1
, we will use the simplified notation
[n_{2}]
.

Let
r
be an integer greater than or equal to
2
. A simple graph
G
is
r
-partite
if its vertex set
V(G)
can be partitioned into
r
pairwise disjoint subsets, such that no two vertices in the same subset are adjacent in
G
. A
complete
r
-partite
graph with
\sum_{i=1}^{r}n_{i}
vertices, denoted by
K_{n_{1},n_{2},\ldots,n_{r}}
, is a graph with the vertex set
V(K_{n_{1},n_{2},\ldots,n_{r}})=[n_{1}]\sqcup[n_{1}+1,n_{1}+n_{2}]\sqcup\cdots\sqcup[1+\sum_{i=1}^{r-1}n_{i},\sum_{i=1}^{r}n_{i}]
and the edge set
E(K_{n_{1},n_{2},\ldots,n_{r}})=\{\{u,v\}:u\in V_{i},v\in V_{j}\text{\ for different\ }i,j\}
. A graph with
r=2
is called a
complete bipartite
graph. In contrast, a graph with
n_{1}=n_{2}=\cdots=n_{r}=1
is known as a
complete graph
, conventionally denoted by
K_{r}
.

A
walk

W
of length
n-1
in a graph
G
is a sequence of vertices
w_{1}
through
w_{n}
, where each consecutive pair of vertices
\{w_{i},w_{i+1}\}
is connected by an edge in
G
.
A
path
is a walk where all vertices are distinct. To simplify notation, a path of length
n-1
is denoted by
P_{n}
.

2.2.
Primary decompositions

Rauh
[
19
]
has already studied the primary decomposition of generalized binomial edge ideals.
Specifically, let
m,n\geq 2
be two positive integers and
G
be a simple graph with the vertex set
[n]
. For each
T\subseteq[n]
, let
\overline{T}=V(G)\setminus T
, and
G_{1},\ldots,G_{c(T)}
be the connected components of
G|_{\overline{T}}
. In addition, let
\widetilde{G_{i}}
be the complete graph on the set
V(G_{i})
for
i=1,\dots,c(T)
. Then, we have the ideal

P_{T}(K_{m},G)\coloneqq(x_{ij}:(i,j)\in[m]\times T)+\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},\widetilde{G}_{1}}+\cdots+\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},\widetilde{G}_{c(T)}}

in the polynomial ring
S={\mathbb{K}}[x_{ij}:(i,j)\in[m]\times[n]]
. Note that
P_{T}(K_{m},G)
is a prime ideal containing
\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G}
.

To describe the minimal primes of
\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G}
,
we need to impose restrictions on
T
. We define a

cut point
of
G
as a vertex
v\in V(G)

such that
c(G)<c(G\setminus v)
, where
c(G)

denotes the number of connected components of
G
.
Let
T
be a subset of
V(G)
, and let
c(T)

denote the number of connected components of

G\setminus T
. If
v
is a cut point of the
induced subgraph
G\setminus(T\setminus\{v\})

for any
v\in T
, then we say that
T
has the

cut point property
. We define
\mathcal{C}(G)
as
the set of all subsets of
V(G)
that have the cut
point property,
and we let
\overline{\mathcal{C}}(G)\coloneqq\mathcal{C}(G)\setminus\{\emptyset\}
.

Now, we can describe the minimal prime ideals of
\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G}
.

Lemma 2.1

(
[
19
, Theorem 7]
)
.

Let
H
be a finite simple graph. Then, the generalized binomial edge ideal
\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},H}
is reduced and

\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},H}=\bigcap_{T\in\mathcal{C}(H)}P_{T}(K_{m},H)

is a minimal primary decomposition of this ideal.

2.3.
Depth and regularity

In the sequel, let
\mathfrak{m}
be the unique graded maximal ideal of
S
with respect to the standard grading.
The local cohomology modules of a finitely generated graded
S
-module
M
with respect to
\mathfrak{m}
are denoted by
H_{\mathfrak{m}}^{i}(M)
for
i\in{\mathbb{Z}}
.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
M
be a finitely generated graded
S
-module.

(1)

For each
i
from
0
to
\operatorname{dim}(M)
, the
a_{i}
-invariant
of
M
is defined as

a_{i}(M)\coloneqq\max\{t:(H_{\mathfrak{m}}^{i}(M))_{t}\neq 0\},

with the convention that
\max\emptyset=-\infty
.

(2)

The
Castelnuovo–Mumford regularity
of
M
is defined as

\operatorname{reg}(M)\coloneqq\max\{a_{i}(M)+i:0\leq i\leq\operatorname{dim}(M)\}.

Remark 2.3
.

For a finitely generated graded
S
-module
M
,
with
M\neq 0
, the local cohomology module

H_{\mathfrak{m}}^{i}(M)=0
when
i<\operatorname{depth}(M)
or

i>\operatorname{dim}(M)
. However,

H_{\mathfrak{m}}^{\operatorname{depth}(M)}(M)\neq 0
and

H_{\mathfrak{m}}^{\operatorname{dim}(M)}(M)\neq 0

(
[
2
, Theorem
3.5.7]
). In particular, if

M
is Cohen–Macaulay, then
\operatorname{reg}(M)=a_{d}(M)+d

for
d=\operatorname{dim}(M)
.

The lemma below is frequently utilized to calculate a module’s depth and regularity.

Lemma 2.4

(
[
11
, Lemmas 2.1, 3.1]
)
.

Let
0\rightarrow M\rightarrow N\rightarrow P\rightarrow 0
be a short exact sequence of finitely generated graded
S
-modules.

(1)

One has
\operatorname{depth}(M)\geq\min\{\operatorname{depth}(N),\operatorname{depth}(P)+1\}
. The equality holds if
\operatorname{depth}(N)\neq\operatorname{depth}(P)
.

(2)

One has
\operatorname{reg}(M)\leq\max\{\operatorname{reg}(N),\operatorname{reg}(P)+1\}
. The equality holds if
\operatorname{reg}(N)\neq\operatorname{reg}(P)
.

The following two lemmas are highly valuable for analyzing the regularity of generalized binomial ideals.

Lemma 2.5

(
[
20
, Proposition 8]
)
.

For a simple graph
G
and its induced subgraph
H
,
\operatorname{reg}(\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},H})
is less than or equal to
\operatorname{reg}(\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G})
.

Lemma 2.6

(
[
15
, Theorems 3.6 and 3.7]
)
.

For a connected graph
G
on the vertex set
[n]
and
m\geq 2
,
\operatorname{reg}(S/\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G})
is less than or equal to
n-1
. If
m\geq n\geq 2
, then
\operatorname{reg}(S/\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G})
equals
n-1
.

3.
Generalized binomial edge ideals of a complete
r
-partite graph

This section analyzes the generalized binomial edge ideal
\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G}
associated with a complete
r
-partite graph
G
where
r\geq 2
. The primary decomposition of
\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G}
is examined to determine the Krull dimension and the depth of
S/\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G}
. A recent result of Katsabekis on the cohomological dimension is generalized as a corollary. Additionally, the Castelnuovo–Mumford regularity, the Hilbert series, and the multiplicity of the quotient ring
S/\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G}
are computed.

Assuming
G
is a complete
r
-partite graph, its vertices can be rearranged to meet the following conditions:

Setting 3.1
.

Let
m,r,n_{1},n_{2},\dots,n_{r}
be positive integers such that
m\geq 2
,
r\geq 2
,
n_{r}\geq 2
, and
1=n_{1}=n_{2}=\cdots=n_{s-1}<n_{s}\leq\cdots\leq n_{r}
for some
s\in[r]
. Suppose that
G
is a complete
r
-partite graph with a partition
V_{1}\sqcup\cdots\sqcup V_{r}
, where
V_{k}\coloneqq\left[1+\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k-1}n_{j},\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k}n_{j}\right]
for each
k\in[r]
. At the same time, let
\widetilde{G}
be the complete graph on
[n]
with
n\coloneqq\sum_{j=1}^{r}n_{j}
.
As usual, let
\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G}
and
\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},\widetilde{G}}
be the generalized binomial edge ideals of the graphs
G
and
\widetilde{G}
, respectively, in the polynomial ring
S={\mathbb{K}}[x_{ij}:i\in[m],j\in[n]]
. Furthermore, let

A_{i}\coloneqq\begin{cases}S,&\text{if $n_{i}=1$,}\\
(x_{ij}:i\in[m],j\in T_{i}),&\text{if $n_{i}\geq 2$,}\end{cases}

where
T_{i}\coloneqq\bigsqcup_{j\neq i}V_{j}
.

Remark 3.2
.

If
n_{1}=\cdots=n_{r}=1
, then
G
is a complete graph
K_{r}
. In this scenario,
S/\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G}
is Cohen–Macaulay with
\operatorname{dim}(S/\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G})=m+r-1
and
\operatorname{reg}(S/\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G})=\min\{m-1,r-1\}
, as proven by
3.3
and
3.4
below.

Lemma 3.3

(
[
12
, Corollary 4]
)
.

Let
\bm{X}=(x_{ij})_{1\leq i\leq m,1\leq j\leq n}
be a generic matrix over
{\mathbb{K}}
. Then the ideal
I_{t}(\bm{X})
in
S
, generated by all
t\times t
minors of
\bm{X}
, is perfect of height
(m-t+1)(n-t+1)
. In particular,
\operatorname{depth}({\mathbb{K}}[\bm{X}]/I_{t}(\bm{X}))=(m+n-1)(t-1)
.

Lemma 3.4

(
[
15
, Proposition 3.3]
or
[
18
, Theorem 5.1]
)
.

For the complete graph
K_{n}
with
n
vertices, we have
\operatorname{reg}(S/\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},K_{n}})=\min\{m-1,n-1\}
.

Now, we will discuss the primary decomposition and the dimension of
\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G}
.

Proposition 3.5
.

Under
3.1
, the minimal primary decomposition is

\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G}=\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},\widetilde{G}}\cap A_{s}\cap A_{s+1}\cap\cdots\cap A_{r},

and the dimension is

\operatorname{dim}(S/\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G})=\max\{m+n-1,mn_{r}\}.

Proof.

Notice that
\operatorname{dim}(S/\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},\widetilde{G}})=m+n-1
by
3.3
and
\operatorname{dim}(S/A_{i})=mn_{i}
for each
i\in[r]
. Thus, by
2.1
, it suffices to show that

(1)

\mathcal{C}(G)=\Set{\emptyset,T_{s},T_{s+1},\dots,T_{r}}.

Let
G_{i}
be the empty graph on the vertex set
V_{i}
for
i\in[r]
. Then,
G
is the join graph
G_{1}*\cdots*G_{r}
in the sense of
[
14
, Section 4]
.
Therefore, the equality of (
1
) follows from
[
14
, Propositions 4.1, 4.5 and 4.14]
. ∎

Using the primary decomposition of
\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G}
, we can calculate the depth of
S/\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G}
.

Theorem 3.6
.

Under
3.1
, we have
\operatorname{depth}(S/\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},{G}})=m+n_{s}
.

Proof.

To utilize the primary decomposition
\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G}=\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},\widetilde{G}}\cap A_{s}\cap\cdots\cap A_{r}
from
3.5
, we introduce
\mathfrak{a}_{s-1}\coloneqq\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},\widetilde{G}}
and
\mathfrak{a}_{k}\coloneqq\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},\widetilde{G}}\cap A_{s}\cap\cdots\cap A_{k}
for
s\leq k\leq r
.
With these preparations,
for each
k\in[s,r]
, we have

0\to S/(\mathfrak{a}_{s-1}\cap A_{k})\to S/\mathfrak{a}_{s-1}\oplus S/A_{k}\to S/(\mathfrak{a}_{s-1}+A_{k})\to 0.

Note that
\operatorname{depth}(S/\mathfrak{a}_{s-1})=m+n-1
,
\operatorname{depth}(S/A_{k})=mn_{k}
and
\operatorname{depth}(S/(\mathfrak{a}_{s-1}+A_{k}))=m+n_{k}-1
. Since
m\geq 2
,
r\geq 2
and
n_{k}\geq 2
, we have
\min\{m+n-1,mn_{k}\}>m+n_{k}-1
. So
\operatorname{depth}(S/(\mathfrak{a}_{s-1}\cap A_{k}))=m+n_{k}
by
2.4
1
.

Next, we prove by induction on
k\in[s,r]
that
\operatorname{depth}(S/\mathfrak{a}_{k})=m+n_{s}
. The base case when
k=s
is clear from the above calculation, since
\mathfrak{a}_{s}=\mathfrak{a}_{s-1}\cap A_{s}
. Now, suppose that
k\geq s+1
and we consider the short exact sequence

0\to S/\mathfrak{a}_{k}\to S/\mathfrak{a}_{k-1}\oplus S/(\mathfrak{a}_{s-1}\cap A_{k})\to S/(\mathfrak{a}_{k-1}+\mathfrak{a}_{s-1}\cap A_{k})\to 0.

By induction, we have
\operatorname{depth}(S/\mathfrak{a}_{k-1})=m+n_{s}
. By the calculation in the previous part, we have
\operatorname{depth}(S/(\mathfrak{a}_{s-1}\cap A_{k}))=m+n_{k}
. Let
A_{k}^{\complement}\coloneqq(x_{ij}:i\in[m],j\in V_{k})\subset S
. Then, we have

\mathfrak{a}_{s-1}\supseteq\mathfrak{a}_{k-1}+\mathfrak{a}_{s-1}\cap A_{k}\supseteq\mathfrak{a}_{s-1}\cap A_{k}^{\complement}+\mathfrak{a}_{s-1}\cap A_{k}\supseteq\mathfrak{a}_{s-1}.

So
\mathfrak{a}_{s-1}=\mathfrak{a}_{k-1}+\mathfrak{a}_{s-1}\cap A_{k}
and
\operatorname{depth}(S/(\mathfrak{a}_{k-1}+\mathfrak{a}_{s-1}\cap A_{k}))=\operatorname{depth}(S/\mathfrak{a}_{s-1})=m+n-1
. Since
k\geq s+1
, we have

\min\{m+n_{s},m+n_{k}\}=m+n_{s}<m+n_{s}+n_{s+1}-1\leq m+n-1.

So we obtain
\operatorname{depth}(S/\mathfrak{a}_{k})=m+n_{s}
by
2.4
1
.

In particular, we can take
k=r
and obtain that

\operatorname{depth}(S/\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G})=\operatorname{depth}(S/\mathfrak{a}_{r})=m+n_{s}.\qed

As a corollary to
3.6
, we will consider the cohomological dimension of
\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G}
. Recall that in a Noetherian ring
R
, the
cohomological dimension

\operatorname{cd}_{R}(I)
of an ideal
I
is defined as follows:

\operatorname{cd}_{R}(I)\coloneqq\max\{i\in{\mathbb{Z}}:H_{I}^{i}(R)\neq 0\}.

Here,
H_{I}^{i}(R)
represents the
i
-th local
cohomology module of
R
with support in
I
. It is
worth noting that the star graph is a special
complete bipartite graph, and the following result
is a generalization of
[
13
, Proposition
2.12]
.

Corollary 3.7
.

Under
3.1
, we have the following results:

(1)

If the characteristic of the field
{\mathbb{K}}
is positive, then

\operatorname{cd}(\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G})=mn-m-n_{s}.

(2)

If the characteristic of the field
{\mathbb{K}}
is zero, then

mn-m-n_{s}\leq\operatorname{cd}(\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G})\leq mn-3.

Proof.

Based on
[
19
, Theorem 2]
,

\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G}
has a squarefree initial ideal
with respect to some monomial order on
S
.
Therefore, by applying

3.6
and

[
6
, Proposition 3.6]
, we can infer
that
\operatorname{cd}(\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G})\geq mn-m-n_{s}
, with
equality when the characteristic of
{\mathbb{K}}
is
positive. Additionally, since

\operatorname{depth}(R/\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G})=m+n_{s}\geq 3
, as per

3.6
, we can conclude
that
\operatorname{cd}(\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G})\leq mn-3
, according to

[
24
, Theorem 3.5]
.
∎

Next, we will compute the regularity of
S/\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G}
under
3.1
. Before proceeding, let us examine a special case.

Lemma 3.8
.

For
m\geq 2
, we have
\operatorname{reg}(S/\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},K_{1,m}})=m
.

Proof.

We will treat
K_{1,m}
here as the complete
r
-partite graph
G
in
3.1
, by taking
r=2
,
n_{1}=1
, and
n_{2}=m
. When
m=2
, the result can be derived from
[
21
, Theorem 4.1]
. In the following, we will assume that

Theorem 3.9
.

Under
3.1
, we have

\operatorname{reg}(S/\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},{G}})=\begin{cases}n-1,&\text{if $m\geq n$,}\\
m-1,&\text{if $n>m>n_{r}$,}\\
m,&\text{if $n_{r}\geq m\geq 2$.}\end{cases}

Proof.

By
3.5
, we have

\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G}=A_{s}\cap\cdots\cap A_{r}\cap\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},\widetilde{G}}.

Let
\mathfrak{b}_{k}\coloneqq A_{k}\cap\cdots\cap A_{r}\cap\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},\widetilde{G}}
for any
k\in[s,r]
and
\mathfrak{b}_{r+1}\coloneqq\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},\widetilde{G}}
. Then,
\mathfrak{b}_{k}=\mathfrak{b}_{k+1}\cap A_{k}
and
\mathfrak{b}_{k+1}+A_{k}=\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},\widetilde{G}|_{V_{k}}}+A_{k}
for all
k\in[s,r]
.

We have three cases.

(1)

If
m\geq n
, then
\operatorname{reg}(S/\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},{G}})=n-1
, by
2.6
.

(2)

Suppose that
n>m>n_{r}
. In this case, we can see that for any
k\in[s,r]
,

\displaystyle\operatorname{reg}(S/\mathfrak{b}_{r+1})=\operatorname{reg}(S/\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},\widetilde{G}})=\min\{m-1,n-1\}=m-1,

\displaystyle\operatorname{reg}(S/(\mathfrak{b}_{k+1}+A_{k}))=\operatorname{reg}(S/(\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},\widetilde{G}|_{V_{k}}}+A_{k}))=\min\{m-1,n_{k}-1\}=n_{k}-1,

and

\displaystyle\operatorname{reg}(S/A_{k})=0,

as per
3.4
. Consider the following short exact sequence

(5)

0\to S/\mathfrak{b}_{k}\to S/\mathfrak{b}_{k+1}\oplus S/A_{k}\to S/(\mathfrak{b}_{k+1}+A_{k})\to 0

for such
k
. If we argue by induction on
k
from
r+1
down to
s
, it follows from
2.4
2
that
\operatorname{reg}(S/\mathfrak{b}_{k})=m-1
. In particular,
\operatorname{reg}(S/\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},{G}})=\operatorname{reg}(S/\mathfrak{b}_{s})=m-1
.

(3)

Suppose that
n_{r}\geq m\geq 2
. Likewise, we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{reg}(S/\mathfrak{b}_{r+1})=\operatorname{reg}(S/\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},\widetilde{G}})=\min\{m-1,n-1\}=m-1,

\displaystyle\operatorname{reg}(S/(\mathfrak{b}_{k+1}+A_{k}))=\operatorname{reg}(S/(\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},\widetilde{G}|_{V_{k}}}+A_{k}))=\min\{m-1,n_{k}-1\}\leq m-1,

and

\displaystyle\operatorname{reg}(S/A_{k})=0

for each
k\in[s,r]

\operatorname{reg}(S/\mathfrak{b}_{k})\leq m

\operatorname{reg}(S/\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},{G}})=\operatorname{reg}(S/\mathfrak{b}_{s})\leq m

On the other hand, the complete
r
-partite graph

contains an induced subgraph

\operatorname{reg}(S/\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},{G}})\geq\operatorname{reg}(S/\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},K_{1,m}})=m
by
2.5
and
3.8
. Thus,
\operatorname{reg}(S/\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},{G}})=m
, which completes the proof. ∎

Proposition 3.10
.

Under
3.1
, the Hilbert series of
S/\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},{G}}
is given by

\displaystyle\operatorname{Hilb}(S/\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G})=\frac{\sum_{i\geq 0}{m-1\choose i}{n-1\choose i}t^{i}}{(1-t)^{m+n-1}}+\sum_{k=s}^{r}\left(\frac{1}{(1-t)^{mn_{k}}}-\frac{\sum_{i\geq 0}{m-1\choose i}{n_{k}-1\choose i}t^{i}}{(1-t)^{m+n_{k}-1}}\right).

Proof.

We adopt the notation from the proof of
3.9
. It follows from the short exact sequence in (
5
) that

\operatorname{Hilb}(S/\mathfrak{b}_{k},t)=\operatorname{Hilb}(S/\mathfrak{b}_{k+1},t)+\operatorname{Hilb}(S/A_{k},t)-\operatorname{Hilb}(S/(\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},\widetilde{G}|_{V_{k}}}+A_{k}),t).

Since
\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G}=\mathfrak{b}_{s}
and
\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},\widetilde{G}}=\mathfrak{b}_{r+1}
, we obtain that

\displaystyle\operatorname{Hilb}(S/\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G})=\operatorname{Hilb}(S/\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},\widetilde{G}})+\sum_{k=s}^{r}\left(\operatorname{Hilb}(S/A_{k},t)-\operatorname{Hilb}(S/(\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},\widetilde{G}|_{V_{k}}}+A_{k}),t)\right).

It follows from
[

that

\displaystyle\operatorname{Hilb}(S/\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},\widetilde{G}},t)

\displaystyle\operatorname{Hilb}(S/(\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},\widetilde{G}|_{V_{k}}}+A_{k}),t)

Meanwhile, the Hilbert series of the polynomial rings

\operatorname{in}_{<}(I)

Proposition 3.13
.

If
G
is a complete
r
-partite graph, the binomial edge ideal
J_{G}
is of König type.

Proof.

If
G
is a complete graph, it is
traceable
, meaning it has a spanning path as a subgraph. As a result,
J_{G}
is of König type according to
[
9
, Proposition 3.6]
.

In the following, we may assume that
G
is a graph in
3.1
. Let
h
be the height of
J_{G}
.
3.5
states that
h=2n-\max\{n+1,2n_{r}\}
. To show that
J_{G}
is of König type, it suffices to find a path of length
h
in
G
by
[
9
, Theorem 3.5]
. Let
n^{\prime}\coloneqq n_{1}+n_{2}+\cdots+n_{r-1}
. There are two cases to consider:

(1)

Suppose that
n_{r}>n^{\prime}
. Notice that
V_{1}\sqcup\cdots\sqcup V_{r-1}=[n^{\prime}]
. The path we choose is:

n^{\prime}+1,1,n^{\prime}+2,2,n^{\prime}+3,3,\dots,n^{\prime}+n^{\prime},n^{\prime},n^{\prime}+(n^{\prime}+1).

(2)

Suppose that
n_{r}\leq n^{\prime}
. We can show that
G
is traceable.
For notational simplicity, we rewrite the vertices of
V_{i}
as
V_{i}=\{v_{i,1},v_{i,2},\dots,v_{i,n_{i}}\}
for
i\in[r]
. Thus, we obtain a total order
\succ
on
V_{1}\sqcup\cdots\sqcup V_{r-1}
by setting
v_{i,j}\succ v_{i^{\prime},j^{\prime}}
if and only if
j<j^{\prime}
, or
j=j^{\prime}
and
i<i^{\prime}
. With respect to the order
\succ
, we can rewrite
V_{1}\sqcup\cdots\sqcup V_{r-1}
as
\{u_{1}\succ u_{2}\succ\cdots\succ u_{n^{\prime}}\}
. Furthermore, set
\delta\coloneqq n^{\prime}-n_{r}+2
. The path in

we take will be:

n^{\prime}+1,u_{1},u_{2},\dots,u_{\delta},n^{\prime}+2,u_{\delta+1},n^{\prime}+3,u_{\delta+2},\dots,n^{\prime}+n_{r}-1,u_{\delta+n_{r}-2}=u_{n^{\prime}},n^{\prime}+n_{r}=n.

∎

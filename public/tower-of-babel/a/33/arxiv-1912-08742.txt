Computation of Kontsevich Weights of Connection and Curvature Graphs for Symplectic Poisson Structures Thanks: This research was (partly) supported by the NCCR SwissMAP, funded by the Swiss National Science Foundation, and by the SNF grant No. 200020_172498/1.
Nima Moshayedi, Fabio Musio

Abstract
. We give a detailed explicit computation of weights of Kontsevich graphs which arise from connection and curvature terms within the globalization picture as in [ 12 ] for the special case of symplectic manifolds. We will show how the weights for the curvature graphs can be explicitly expressed in terms of the hypergeometric function as well as by a much simpler formula combining it with the explicit expression for the weights of its underlined connection graphs. Moreover, we consider the case of a cotangent bundle, which will simplify the curvature expression significantly.

1.2.
Notions of Formal Geometry

Let
M
be a smooth manifold and let
\phi\colon U\rightarrow M
be a map where
U\subset TM
is an open neighbourhood of the zero section. For
x\in M,y\in T_{x}M\cap U
we write
\phi_{x}(y):=\phi(x,y)
. We say that
\phi
is a
generalized exponential map
if for all
x\in M
we have that
\phi_{x}(0)=x
, and
{\mathrm{d}}\phi_{x}(0)=\mathrm{id}_{T_{x}M}
. In local coordinates we can write

(6)

\phi_{x}^{i}(y)=x^{i}+y^{i}+\frac{1}{2}\phi_{x,jk}^{i}y^{j}y^{k}+\frac{1}{3!}\phi^{i}_{x,jk\ell}y^{j}y^{k}y^{\ell}+\dotsm

where the
x^{i}
are coordinates on the base and the
y^{i}
are coordinates on the fibers.
We identify two generalized exponential maps if their jets agree to all orders. A
formal exponential map
is an equivalence class of generalized exponential maps. It is completely specified by the sequence of functions
\left(\phi^{i}_{x,i_{1}\ldots i_{k}}\right)_{k=0}^{\infty}
. By abuse of notation, we will denote equivalence classes and their representatives by
\phi
. From a formal exponential map
\phi
and a function
f\in C^{\infty}(M)
, we can produce a section
\sigma\in\Gamma(\widehat{\textnormal{Sym}}(T^{*}M))
by defining
\sigma_{x}=\textsf{T}\phi_{x}^{*}f
, where
T
denotes the Taylor expansion in the fiber coordinates around
y=0
and we use any representative of
\phi
to define the pullback. We denote this section by
\textsf{T}\phi^{*}f
, it is independent of the choice of representative, since it only depends on the jets of the representative.

As it was shown
[
5
,
3
,
11
]
, one can define a
flat
connection
D_{G}
on
\widehat{\textnormal{Sym}}(T^{*}M)
with the property that
D_{G}\sigma=0
if and only if
\sigma=\textsf{T}\phi^{*}f
for some
f\in C^{\infty}(M)
.
As already mentioned before, this connection is called the
classical Grothendieck connection
.
In fact,
D_{G}={\mathrm{d}}_{x}+L_{R}
where
R\in\Omega^{1}(M,{\mathrm{Der}}(\widehat{\textnormal{Sym}}(T^{*}M)))
is a 1-form with values in derivations of
\widehat{\textnormal{Sym}}(T^{*}M)
, which we identify with
\Gamma(TM\otimes\widehat{\textnormal{Sym}}(T^{*}M))
. We have denoted by
{\mathrm{d}}_{x}
the de Rham differential on
M
and by
L
the Lie derivative.
In coordinates we have

(7)

R(\sigma)_{\ell}=-\frac{\partial\sigma}{\partial y^{j}}\left(\left(\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial y}\right)^{-1}\right)_{k}^{j}\frac{\partial\phi^{k}}{\partial x^{\ell}}.

Define
R(x,y):=R_{\ell}(x,y){\mathrm{d}}x^{\ell}
,
R_{\ell}(x,y):=R_{\ell}^{j}(x,y)\frac{\partial}{\partial y^{j}}
,
R^{j}(x,y):=R^{j}_{\ell}(x,y){\mathrm{d}}x^{\ell}
, and

(8)

R_{\ell}^{j}=-\left(\left(\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial y}\right)^{-1}\right)_{k}^{j}\frac{\partial\phi^{k}}{\partial x^{\ell}}=-\delta_{\ell}^{j}+O(y).

For
\sigma\in\Gamma(\widehat{\textnormal{Sym}}(T^{*}M))
,
L_{R}\sigma
is given by the Taylor expansion (in the
y
coordinates) of

-{\mathrm{d}}_{y}\sigma\circ({\mathrm{d}}_{y}\phi)^{-1}\circ{\mathrm{d}}_{x}\phi\colon\Gamma(TM)\rightarrow\Gamma(\widehat{\textnormal{Sym}}(T^{*}M)),

where we denote by
{\mathrm{d}}_{y}
the de Rham differential on the fiber.
This shows that
R
does not depend on the choice of coordinates. One can generalize this also for any fixed vector
\xi=\xi^{i}(x)\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}}\in T_{x}M
, instead of just considering the de Rham differential
{\mathrm{d}}_{x}
, by

(9)

D_{G}^{\xi}=\xi+\widehat{\xi},

where

(10)

\widehat{\xi}(x,y)=\iota_{\xi}R(x,y)=\xi^{i}(x)R_{\ell}^{j}(x,y)\frac{\partial}{\partial y^{j}}.

Here
\xi^{i}(x)
would replace the 1-form part
{\mathrm{d}}x^{i}
.

\displaystyle w_{\Gamma}=\int\limits_{\bar{C}_{n,m}}\omega_{\Gamma}.

Let us briefly recall the construction of the needed configuration spaces. We define the open configuration space

\mathrm{Conf}_{n,m}=\{(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n},q_{1},\ldots,q_{m})\in\mathbb{H}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\mid x_{i}\not=x_{j},\,\forall i\not=j,\,q_{1}<\ldots<q_{m}\}.

The 2-dimensional real Lie group of orientation preserving affine transformations of the real line

G^{(1)}=\{z\mapstochar\rightarrow az+b\mid a,b\in\mathbb{R},\,a>0\}

acts freely on

The differential form

\displaystyle\omega_{\Gamma}=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{2n+m-2}}\bigwedge\limits_{\text{edges $e$}}{\mathrm{d}}\varphi_{e},

where the wedge product is over all

Let

\mathrm{Conf}_{n}=\{(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\in\mathbb{C}^{n}\mid x_{i}\not=x_{j},\,\forall i\not=j\}.

We have an action on

G^{(2)}=\{z\mapstochar\rightarrow az+b\mid a\in\mathbb{R},\,b\in\mathbb{C},\,a>0\}.

We know that the dimension of the configuration space

Actually, we will see below that all the integrals over the non-compactified configuration spaces

(30)

\displaystyle\int\limits_{y\in\mathbb{H}\backslash\{x\}}{\mathrm{d}}\varphi(x,y)^{m}{\mathrm{d}}\varphi(y,x)^{n}=\left(2^{m}-\sum\limits_{k=0}^{m}{m\choose k}\frac{n}{m+n-k}-\sum\limits_{l=0}^{n-1}{n-1\choose l}\frac{n}{m+n-l}\right)\pi^{m+n}.

It is not hard to see that for
n=1
the above formula simplifies to

(31)

\displaystyle\int\limits_{y\in\mathbb{H}\backslash\{x\}}{\mathrm{d}}\varphi(x,y)^{m}{\mathrm{d}}\varphi(y,x)=2^{m}\left(1-\frac{2}{m+1}\right)\pi^{m+1},

which agrees with the result in
[

, Section 4]
.

2.1.3.
Step 3

In a third step we want to compute an integral similar to (
25
), but with an additional factor
[x;y]
as defined in (

). So we want to compute the integral

(32)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{n+m}}\int\limits_{y\in\mathbb{H}\backslash\{x\}}[x;y]{\mathrm{d}}\varphi(x,y)^{m}{\mathrm{d}}\varphi(y,x)^{n}

As usual, we use Stokes’ theorem

(33)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\int\limits_{y\in\mathbb{H}\backslash\{x\}}[x;y]{\mathrm{d}}\varphi(x,y)^{m}{\mathrm{d}}\varphi(y,x)^{n}&=\int\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}y\in\mathbb{H}\backslash\{x\}\\
\mathrm{Re}(y)<\mathrm{Re}(x)\end{subarray}}{\mathrm{d}}\varphi(x,y)^{m}{\mathrm{d}}\varphi(y,x)^{n}-\int\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}y\in\mathbb{H}\backslash\{x\}\\
\mathrm{Re}(y)>\mathrm{Re}(x)\end{subarray}}{\mathrm{d}}\varphi(x,y)^{m}{\mathrm{d}}\varphi(y,x)^{n}\\
&=\int\limits_{\partial_{+}}\varphi(x,y)^{m}{\mathrm{d}}\varphi(y,x)^{n}-\int\limits_{\partial_{-}}\varphi(x,y)^{m}{\mathrm{d}}\varphi(y,x)^{n}\end{split}

with boundaries
\partial_{+}
and
\partial_{-}
of the integration domain depicted in Figure
6
below.

As before, we discuss the different boundary components:

•

With this we compute the integral

(34)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\int\limits_{y\in\mathbb{H}\backslash\{x\}}[x;y]{\mathrm{d}}\varphi(x,y)^{m}{\mathrm{d}}\varphi(y,x)^{n}=\int\limits_{\partial_{+}}\varphi(x,y)^{m}{\mathrm{d}}\varphi(y,x)^{n}-\int\limits_{\partial_{-}}\varphi(x,y)^{m}{\mathrm{d}}\varphi(y,x)^{n}\\
&=-n\int\limits_{\frac{\pi}{2}}^{\frac{3\pi}{2}}\left(\frac{3\pi}{2}-\theta\right)^{m}\left(\frac{5\pi}{2}-\theta\right)^{n-1}d\theta-(2\pi)^{m}\pi^{n}+n\int\limits_{-\frac{\pi}{2}}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}\left(\frac{3\pi}{2}-\theta\right)^{m}\left(\frac{\pi}{2}-\theta\right)^{n-1}d\theta\\
&=\left(-2^{m}+\sum\limits_{k=0}^{m}{m\choose k}\frac{n}{m+n-k}-\sum\limits_{l=0}^{n-1}{n-1\choose l}\frac{n}{m+n-l}\right)\pi^{m+n},\end{split}

where we used (
28
) and (

) in the last step.

2.1.4.
Putting everything together

Finally, we are able to compute the Kontsevich weight (
19
) of the graphs
\Gamma_{n}
described at the beginning of Section
2.1
. Integrating over
z_{i}
for
i=1,\ldots,n
, and applying the result for (
22
) obtained in the first step we get

(35)

\displaystyle\begin{split}w_{\Gamma_{n}}&=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{2n+2}}\int\limits_{C_{n+2,0}}{\mathrm{d}}\varphi(x,y){\mathrm{d}}\varphi(y,x){\mathrm{d}}\varphi(z_{1},x){\mathrm{d}}\varphi(z_{1},y)\cdots{\mathrm{d}}\varphi(z_{n},x){\mathrm{d}}\varphi(z_{n},y)\\
&=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{n+2}}\int\limits_{y\in\mathbb{H}\backslash\{x\}}\big(\varphi(x,y)-\varphi(y,x)+[x;y]\pi\big)^{n}{\mathrm{d}}\varphi(x,y){\mathrm{d}}\varphi(y,x)\\
&=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{n+2}}\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n}\sum\limits_{l=0}^{n-k}{n\choose k}{n-k\choose l}(-1)^{l}\int\limits_{y\in\mathbb{H}\backslash\{x\}}\varphi(x,y)^{n-k-l}\varphi(y,x)^{l}([x;y]\pi)^{k}{\mathrm{d}}\varphi(x,y){\mathrm{d}}\varphi(y,x)\\
&=\frac{1}{2^{n+2}}\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n}\sum\limits_{l=0}^{n-k}{n\choose k}{n-k\choose l}\frac{(-1)^{l}}{\pi^{n-k+2}(n-k-l+1)(l+1)}\int\limits_{y\in\mathbb{H}\backslash\{x\}}[x;y]^{k}{\mathrm{d}}\varphi(x,y)^{n-k-l+1}{\mathrm{d}}\varphi(y,x)^{l+1}.\end{split}

Note that for even
k
we have

(36)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\int\limits_{y\in\mathbb{H}\backslash\{x\}}[x;y]^{k}{\mathrm{d}}\varphi(x,y)^{n-k-l+1}{\mathrm{d}}\varphi(y,x)^{l+1}=\int\limits_{y\in\mathbb{H}\backslash\{x\}}{\mathrm{d}}\varphi(x,y)^{n-k-l+1}{\mathrm{d}}\varphi(y,x)^{l+1}\\
&=\left(2^{n-k-l+1}-\sum\limits_{r=0}^{n-k-l+1}{n-k-l+1\choose r}\frac{l+1}{n-k-r+2}-\sum\limits_{s=0}^{l}{l\choose s}\frac{l+1}{n-k-s+2}\right)\pi^{n-k+2},\end{split}

where we have used (
30
). Similarly, for odd
k
we get

(37)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\int\limits_{y\in\mathbb{H}\backslash\{x\}}[x;y]^{k}{\mathrm{d}}\varphi(x,y)^{n-k-l+1}{\mathrm{d}}\varphi(y,x)^{l+1}=\int\limits_{y\in\mathbb{H}\backslash\{x\}}[x;y]{\mathrm{d}}\varphi(x,y)^{n-k-l+1}{\mathrm{d}}\varphi(y,x)^{l+1}\\
&=\left(-2^{n-k-l+1}+\sum\limits_{r=0}^{n-k-l+1}{n-k-l+1\choose r}\frac{l+1}{n-k-r+2}-\sum\limits_{s=0}^{l}{l\choose s}\frac{l+1}{n-k-s+2}\right)\pi^{n-k+2},\end{split}

where we have used (
34
).

We will now simplify the expressions we got. We start by observing a few things:

First of all, we clearly have that

(38)

\displaystyle{\mathrm{d}}\varphi(x,y)^{n-k-l+1}{\mathrm{d}}\varphi(y,x)^{l+1}=-{\mathrm{d}}\varphi(y,x)^{l+1}{\mathrm{d}}\varphi(x,y)^{n-k-l+1}.

Similarly, we also have that

(39)

\displaystyle[x;y]=-[y;x].

Furthermore, we can obviously swap
x
and
y
in the integral and get the same result, i.e.

(40)

\displaystyle\int\limits_{y\in\mathbb{H}\backslash\{x\}}[x;y]^{m}{\mathrm{d}}\varphi(x,y)^{n-k-l+1}{\mathrm{d}}\varphi(y,x)^{l+1}=\int\limits_{x\in\mathbb{H}\backslash\{y\}}[y;x]^{m}{\mathrm{d}}\varphi(y,x)^{n-k-l+1}{\mathrm{d}}\varphi(x,y)^{l+1}.

Now assume that
n
is even. Applying (
38
), (
39
) and (
40
) to the last line of (
35
), it follows that

(41)

\displaystyle w_{\Gamma_{n}}=\frac{1}{2^{n+2}}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=0\\
k\text{ even}\end{subarray}}^{n}{n\choose k}{n-k\choose\frac{n-k}{2}}\frac{(-1)^{\frac{n-k}{2}}}{\pi^{n-k+2}\left(\frac{n-k}{2}+1\right)^{2}}\int\limits_{y\in\mathbb{H}\backslash\{x\}}{\mathrm{d}}\varphi(x,y)^{\frac{n-k}{2}+1}{\mathrm{d}}\varphi(y,x)^{\frac{n-k}{2}+1}.

So most of the terms cancel for
n
even.

Now using (
30
) we observe that

(42)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\frac{1}{\pi^{2m}}\int\limits_{y\in\mathbb{H}\backslash\{x\}}{\mathrm{d}}\varphi(x,y)^{m}{\mathrm{d}}\varphi(y,x)^{m}=2^{m}-\sum\limits_{k=0}^{m}{m\choose k}\frac{m}{2m-k}-\sum\limits_{l=0}^{m-1}{m-1\choose l}\frac{m}{2m-l}\\
&=2^{m}-\sum\limits_{k=0}^{m-1}\left({m\choose k}+{m-1\choose k}\right)\frac{m}{2m-k}-1=2^{m}-\sum\limits_{k=0}^{m-1}{m\choose k}-1=2^{m}-\sum\limits_{k=0}^{m}{m\choose k}=0.\end{split}

Plugging this result into (
41
) with
m=\frac{n-k}{2}
we finally get that

(43)

\displaystyle w_{\Gamma_{n}}=0\qquad\text{for even }n\geq 0.

For
n
odd the different terms in the last line of (
35
) do not cancel anymore. Instead, we will try to write (
30
) and (
34
) more compactly. To do this, let us introduce the so-called
hypergeometric function

{}_{2}F_{1}(a,b;c;z)
. It is defined by the series

(44)

{}_{2}F_{1}(a,b;z;c):=\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(a)_{k}(b)_{k}}{(c)_{k}}\frac{z^{k}}{k!}

for
z\in\mathbb{C}
with
|z|<1
, where
(a)_{k}
is the
Pochhammer symbol
given by

(45)

\displaystyle(a)_{k}=\begin{cases}1,&\text{if }k=0\\
a(a+1)\cdots(a+k-1),&\text{if }k>0\end{cases}

It is not hard to see that the series terminates if either
a
or
b
is a non-positive integer. In that case the hypergeometric function reduces to a polynomial and can therefore also be defined for
|z|\geq 1
.

We can now use the hypergeometric function to write

(46)

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{k=0}^{m}{m\choose k}\frac{1}{m+n-k}=\frac{{}_{2}F_{1}(-m,-m-n;1-m-n;-1)}{m+n},

and

(47)

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n-1}{n-1\choose k}\frac{1}{m+n-k}=\frac{{}_{2}F_{1}(1-n,-m-n;1-m-n;-1)}{m+n}.

This allows us to write (
30
) as

(48)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\int\limits_{y\in\mathbb{H}\backslash\{x\}}{\mathrm{d}}\varphi(x,y)^{m}{\mathrm{d}}\varphi(y,x)^{n}\\
&=\Big(2^{m}-\frac{n}{m+n}\big({{}_{2}F_{1}}(-m,-m-n;1-m-n;-1)+{{}_{2}F_{1}}(1-n,-m-n;1-m-n;-1)\big)\Big)\pi^{m+n},\end{split}

and (
34
) as

(49)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\int\limits_{y\in\mathbb{H}\backslash\{x\}}[x;y]{\mathrm{d}}\varphi(x,y)^{m}{\mathrm{d}}\varphi(y,x)^{n}\\
&=\left(-2^{m}+\frac{n}{m+n}\big({{}_{2}F_{1}}(-m,-m-n;1-m-n;-1)-{{}_{2}F_{1}}(1-n,-m-n;1-m-n;-1)\big)\right)\pi^{m+n}.\end{split}

Plugging those results into (
35
) we finally get for all
n\geq 0

(50)

\displaystyle\begin{split}w_{\Gamma_{n}}&=\frac{1}{2^{n+2}}\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n}\sum\limits_{l=0}^{n-k}{n\choose k}{n-k\choose l}\frac{(-1)^{l}}{(n-k-l+1)(l+1)}\Big((-1)^{k}2^{n-k-l+1}\\
&-\frac{l+1}{n-k+2}\big({{}_{2}F_{1}}(-l,-n+k-2;-n+k-1;-1)\\
&+(-1)^{k}{{}_{2}F_{1}}(-n+k+l-1,-n+k-2;-n+k-1;-1)\big)\Big).\end{split}

2.2.1.
Step 1

For a wedge as in Figure

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{2}}\int\limits_{z\in\mathbb{H}\backslash\{x\}}{\mathrm{d}}\varphi(z,x){\mathrm{d}}\varphi(z,q),

i.e. we want to integrate out
z
(with

fixed). The computation is almost the same as the one we have already done in Section

above: Again we make a branch cut such that
\varphi(z,x)\in(0,2\pi)
and use Stokes’ theorem

(53)

\displaystyle\int\limits_{z\in\mathbb{H}\backslash\{x\}}{\mathrm{d}}\varphi(z,x){\mathrm{d}}\varphi(z,q)=\int\limits_{\partial}\varphi(z,x){\mathrm{d}}\varphi(z,q),

where
\partial
is the boundary of the integration domain depicted in Figure
9
below.

Figure 9.
Boundary
\partial
of the integration domain:
C
is the half-circle at infinity,
B_{+}
and
B_{-}
are infinitesimally close together, the (half-)circles
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
have infinitesimal radius and
H_{+,1}\cup H_{+,2}\cup H_{-}
is the real line.

Let us have a look at the different boundary components:

•

On
H_{+,1}\cup H_{+,2}\cup H_{-}
:
z\in\mathbb{R}
and hence
{\mathrm{d}}\varphi(z,q)=0

•

On
B_{+}
:
\varphi(z,x)=2\pi

•

On
B_{-}
:
\varphi(z,x)=0

•

On
C_{1}
:
z=x+\varepsilon\mathrm{e}^{-i\theta}
for
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0

\implies

{\mathrm{d}}\varphi(z,q)={\mathrm{d}}\text{arg}\left(\frac{q-x}{q-\bar{x}}\right)=0

•

On
C_{2}
:
z=q+\varepsilon\mathrm{e}^{-i\theta}
for
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
and
\theta\in[-\pi,0]

\implies

\varphi(z,x)\rightarrow\varphi(q,x)
,
\varphi(z,q)=-2\theta

•

On

for

\displaystyle\begin{split}\int\limits_{z\in\mathbb{H}\backslash\{x,q\}}{\mathrm{d}}\varphi(z,x){\mathrm{d}}\varphi(z,q)&=\int\limits_{\partial}\varphi(z,x){\mathrm{d}}\varphi(z,q)=2\pi\int\limits_{B_{+}}{\mathrm{d}}\varphi(z,q)+\int\limits_{0}^{\pi}4\theta{\mathrm{d}}\theta-2\varphi(q,x)\int\limits_{-\pi}^{0}{\mathrm{d}}\theta\\

\displaystyle[x;q]=\begin{cases}+1,&\text{if }\mathrm{Re}(x)>q\\

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\big(\varphi(x,q)-\varphi(q,x)\big)\pm\frac{1}{2},

.

Finally, observe that one obtains (

) by simply taking the limit

in (

Step 2

In a second step we want to compute the integral

(57)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{m+n}}\int\limits_{C_{1,1}}\varphi(q,x)^{m}{\mathrm{d}}\varphi(x,q)^{n}

for

and
m\geq 0
.

First note that

, shown in Figure

below, is a smooth manifold of dimension

which is homeomorphic to an open interval and

is homeomorphic to a closed interval.

.

, which is induced by the standard orientation on the plane

.

\partial

(58)

\displaystyle\partial=\{(q,s),(q,t),(q,y_{+}),(q,y_{-})\},

where

, i.e. with Re
(y)=q
, and

and

are the limits

on the half circle from the left (i.e. from the region Re
(x)<q

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\int\limits_{C_{1,1}}\varphi(q,x)^{m}{\mathrm{d}}\varphi(x,q)^{n}=\int\limits_{\partial}\varphi(q,x)^{m}\varphi(x,q)^{n}\\

Step 3

Let us start with writing (

) differently as

\displaystyle 2\pi\big(\varphi(x,q)-\varphi(q,x)+\pi[x;q]\big)=2\pi\big(\varphi(x,q)-\varphi(q,x)-\pi+2\pi(x;q)\big)

\displaystyle(x;q)=\begin{cases}+1,&\text{if }\mathrm{Re}(x)>q\\

In this step we then want to compute an integral similar to (

), but with an additional factor

as defined above. So we want to compute

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{m+n}}\int\limits_{C_{1,1}}(x;q)\varphi(q,x)^{m}{\mathrm{d}}\varphi(x,q)^{n}

for

and

.

As before we use Stokes’ theorem and find that

(63)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\int\limits_{C_{1,1}}(x;q)\varphi(q,x)^{m}{\mathrm{d}}\varphi(x,q)^{n}=\int\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}C_{1,1}\\
\mathrm{Re}(x)>q\end{subarray}}\varphi(q,x)^{m}{\mathrm{d}}\varphi(x,q)^{n}\\

and all

.

2.2.4.
Putting everything together

Now we can use the results from steps
1
-
3
to compute the Kontsevich weight (

) of the graphs
\Upsilon_{n}
,
n\geq 0
, described at the beginning of Section

. Integrating over

for

, and applying (

), we get

(64)

\displaystyle\begin{split}w_{\Upsilon_{n}}&=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{2n+1}}\int\limits_{C_{n+1,1}}{\mathrm{d}}\varphi(x,q){\mathrm{d}}\varphi(z_{1},x){\mathrm{d}}\varphi(z_{1},q)\cdots{\mathrm{d}}\varphi(z_{n},x){\mathrm{d}}\varphi(z_{n},q)\\
&=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{n+1}}\int\limits_{C_{1,1}}\big(\varphi(x,q)-\varphi(q,x)-\pi+2\pi(x;q)\big)^{n}{\mathrm{d}}\varphi(x,q)\\
&=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{n+1}}\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n}\sum\limits_{l=0}^{n-k}\sum\limits_{s=0}^{n-k-l}{n\choose k}{n-k\choose l}{n-k-l\choose s}(-1)^{l+s}\\
&\times\int\limits_{C_{1,1}}\varphi(x,q)^{n-k-l-s}\varphi(q,x)^{s}\pi^{l}(2\pi(x;q))^{k}{\mathrm{d}}\varphi(x,q)\\
&=\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n}\sum\limits_{l=0}^{n-k}\sum\limits_{s=0}^{n-k-l}{n\choose k}{n-k\choose l}{n-k-l\choose s}\frac{(-1)^{l+s}}{2^{n-k+1}\pi^{n-k-l+1}(n-k-l-s+1)}\\
&\times\int\limits_{C_{1,1}}(x;q)^{k}\varphi(q,x)^{s}{\mathrm{d}}\varphi(x,q)^{n-k-l-s+1}.\end{split}

We note that for
k=0
we have

(65)

\displaystyle\int\limits_{C_{1,1}}\varphi(q,x)^{s}{\mathrm{d}}\varphi(x,q)^{n-l-s+1}=\begin{cases}(2\pi)^{n-l+1},&\text{if }s=0\\
2^{s}\pi^{n-l+1},&\text{if }s>0\end{cases}

where we have used (
59
). Similarly, for
k\geq 1
we have

(66)

\displaystyle\int\limits_{C_{1,1}}(x;q)^{k}\varphi(q,x)^{s}{\mathrm{d}}\varphi(x,q)^{n-k-l-s+1}=\int\limits_{C_{1,1}}(x;q)\varphi(q,x)^{s}{\mathrm{d}}\varphi(x,q)^{n-k-l-s+1}=2^{s}\pi^{n-k-l+1},

where we have used (
63
).

Plugging the above two results into the last line of (
64
) we get

(67)

\displaystyle\begin{split}w_{\Upsilon_{n}}&=\underbrace{\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n}\sum\limits_{l=0}^{n-k}\sum\limits_{s=0}^{n-k-l}{n\choose k}{n-k\choose l}{n-k-l\choose s}\frac{(-1)^{l+s}}{2^{n-k-s+1}(n-k-l-s+1)}}_{=:A(n)}\\
&-\underbrace{\sum\limits_{l=0}^{n}{n\choose l}\frac{(-1)^{l}}{2^{n+1}(n-l+1)}}_{=:B(n)}+\underbrace{\sum\limits_{l=0}^{n}{n\choose l}\frac{(-1)^{l}}{2^{l}(n-l+1)}}_{=:C(n)}.\end{split}

As shown in Appendix
A
, we have that

(68)

\displaystyle\begin{split}A(n)&=\frac{(-1)^{n}}{2^{n+1}(n+1)},\\
B(n)&=\frac{(-1)^{n}}{2^{n+1}(n+1)},\\
C(n)&=\frac{1+(-1)^{n}}{2^{n+1}(n+1)}.\end{split}

Hence, we finally have

(69)

\displaystyle w_{\Upsilon_{n}}=\frac{1+(-1)^{n}}{2^{n+1}(n+1)},\qquad n\geq 0.

In particular, we see that

(70)

\displaystyle w_{\Upsilon_{n}}=0\qquad\text{for odd }n\geq 1.\;

2.4.
Another Approach for the Explicit Computation of
w_{\Gamma_{n}}
and
w_{\Upsilon_{n}}

We want to give a more fast and explicit approach for the computation of the weights
w_{\Gamma_{n}}
and
w_{\Upsilon_{n}}
. The following approach has the advantage that it doesn’t require the use of the hypergeometric function for
w_{\Gamma_{n}}
but rather gives an explicit expression in terms of
w_{\Upsilon_{n}}
.
First note that

(79)

{\mathrm{d}}\varphi(z,x)=\frac{1}{4\pi i}{\mathrm{d}}\log\left(\frac{(z-x)(z-\bar{x})}{(\bar{z}-x)(\bar{z}-\bar{x})}\right),\qquad\forall z,x\in\mathbb{H}\cup\mathbb{R}.

Moreover, recall from
[
2
, Lemma 5.3]
the formula

(80)

\int_{z\in\mathbb{H}}{\mathrm{d}}\varphi(z,x)\land{\mathrm{d}}\varphi(z,y)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\log\left(\frac{x-\bar{y}}{y-\bar{x}}\right),\qquad\forall x,y\in\mathbb{H}\cup\mathbb{R}.

Integrating out the
z_{i}
variables in
w_{\Upsilon_{n}}
using (
80
), we get

(81)

w_{\Upsilon_{n}}=\int_{C_{1,1}}\left(\frac{1}{2\pi i}\log\left(\frac{x-p}{p-\bar{x}}\right)\right)^{n}\frac{1}{2\pi i}{\mathrm{d}}\log\left(\frac{x-p}{\bar{x}-p}\right)=\frac{1}{n+1}\int_{C_{1,1}}{\mathrm{d}}\left(\frac{1}{2\pi i}{\mathrm{d}}\log\left(\frac{x-p}{p-\bar{x}}\right)\right)^{n+1}

because
{\mathrm{d}}\log\left(\bar{x}-p\right)={\mathrm{d}}\log\left(p-\bar{x}\right)
. Now recall that
C_{1,1}=(\mathbb{H}\times\mathbb{R})/(\mathbb{R}^{>0}\ltimes\mathbb{R})
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{R}
; for instance every point in the quotient can be represented uniquely by a pair
(i,p)
with
i
the imaginary unit and
p\in\mathbb{R}
. Hence we get

(82)

\displaystyle\begin{split}w_{\Upsilon_{n}}&=\frac{1}{n+1}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}}{{\mathrm{d}}p}\left(\frac{1}{2\pi i}\log\left(\frac{i-p}{p+i}\right)\right)^{n+1}{\mathrm{d}}p\\
&=\left(\frac{1}{2\pi i}\log\left(\frac{i-p}{p+i}\right)\right)^{n+1}\Bigg|_{p={-\infty}}^{p=\infty}\\
&=\frac{1+(-1)^{n}}{2^{n+1}(n+1)},\end{split}

where we have used the boundary values

(83)

\lim_{p\rightarrow\pm\infty}\log\left(\frac{i-p}{p+i}\right)=\lim_{p\rightarrow\pm\infty}\log\left(-1+\frac{2i}{p}+O(1/p^{2})\right)=\pm i\pi.

Now, using (
80
) and integrating out the
z_{i}
variables of
w_{\Gamma_{n}}
, we get

(84)

w_{\Gamma_{n}}=\int_{C_{2,0}}\left(\frac{1}{2\pi i}\log\left(\frac{x-\bar{y}}{z-\bar{x}}\right)\right)^{n}{\mathrm{d}}\varphi(x,y)\land{\mathrm{d}}\varphi(y,x).

Note that
{\mathrm{d}}\varphi(x,y)={\mathrm{d}}\varphi(y,x)+\frac{1}{2\pi i}\log\left(\frac{x-\bar{y}}{y-\bar{x}}\right)
, and thus

(85)

\displaystyle\begin{split}w_{\Gamma_{n}}&=\int_{C_{2,0}}\left(\frac{1}{2\pi i}\log\left(\frac{x-\bar{y}}{y-\bar{x}}\right)\right)^{n}\frac{1}{2\pi i}{\mathrm{d}}\log\left(\frac{x-\bar{y}}{y-\bar{x}}\right)\land{\mathrm{d}}\varphi(y,x)\\
&=\frac{1}{n+1}\int_{C_{2,0}}{\mathrm{d}}\left[\left(\frac{1}{2\pi i}\log\left(\frac{x-\bar{y}}{y-\bar{x}}\right)\right)^{n+1}{\mathrm{d}}\varphi(y,x)\right].\end{split}

By Stokes’ theorem, the integral is reduced to the boundary of Kontsevich’s eye
\bar{C}_{2,0}

[
17
, Section 5.2.]
. This boundary has three components:

•

The iris of the eye which is isomorphic to
S^{1}
, corresponding to the collision
x\rightarrow y
. This gives zero contribution, since
\log\left(\frac{x-\bar{y}}{y-\bar{x}}\right)=0
for
x=y
.

•

The upper eyelid, corresponding to the limit
|x|\rightarrow\infty
. This gives no contribution, since
{\mathrm{d}}\varphi(y,x)=0
in the limit.

•

The lower eyelid, corresponding to the collision
x\rightarrow\bar{x}
onto the real line.

The lower eyelid is isomorphic to
C_{1,1}
and by comparison with the integrand of
w_{\Upsilon_{n+1}}
in (
81
) with
p:=x=\bar{x}
, we get

(86)

w_{\Gamma_{n}}=\frac{1}{n+1}w_{\Upsilon_{n+1}}=\frac{1-(-1)^{n}}{2^{n+2}(n+1)(n+2)}.

One can easily check that this formula produces the same values as in Table
1
.

where we sum over all the indices

. Moreover, we use the notation, where the indices on the right of the comma denote derivatives with respect to the corresponding variable, e.g.

(88)

R_{i,i_{1}\dotsm i_{k}}:=\partial_{i_{1}}\dotsm\partial_{i_{k}}R_{i}.

3.2.
The Connection 1-form
A(R,\mathsf{T}\phi^{*}\pi)

In Section

we have obtained the Kontsevich weights

\displaystyle w_{\Upsilon_{n}}=\frac{1+(-1)^{n}}{2^{n+1}(n+1)},\qquad n\geq 0

of the family of graphs

. Let

be a section and fix

. For

as in Section

we set

and
(R_{x})^{k}_{i}(y):=R^{k}_{i}(x,y)

(90)

\displaystyle\begin{split}A(R_{x},\textsf{T}\phi^{*}_{x}\pi)(\sigma_{x})&={\mathrm{d}}x^{i}A\left((R_{x})^{k}_{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial y^{k}},\textsf{T}\phi^{*}_{x}\pi\right)(\sigma_{x})\\

\displaystyle\begin{split}(\mathcal{D}_{G})_{x}&={\mathrm{d}}_{x}+A(R_{x},\textsf{T}\phi^{*}_{x}\pi)=\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}}+A\big((R_{x})_{i},\textsf{T}\phi^{*}_{x}\pi\big)\right){\mathrm{d}}x^{i}\\
&=\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}}+\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{\hbar^{n}}{2^{n}n!}\frac{1+(-1)^{n}}{2^{n+1}(n+1)}(\textsf{T}\phi^{*}_{x}\pi)^{i_{1}j_{1}}\cdots(\textsf{T}\phi^{*}_{x}\pi)^{i_{n}j_{n}}(R_{x})^{k}_{i,i_{1}\cdots i_{n}}\frac{\partial^{n+1}}{\partial y^{j_{n}}\cdots\partial y^{j_{1}}\partial y^{k}}\right){\mathrm{d}}x^{i}\end{split}

F(R,R,\mathsf{T}\phi^{*}\pi)

In Section

we have obtained the Kontsevich weights in terms of the hypergeometric function and gave a more explicit formula in Section

w_{\Gamma_{n}}=\frac{1-(-1)^{n}}{2^{n+2}(n+1)(n+2)},\qquad n\geq 0

for the family of graphs

3.4.
A Fedosov-type Equation for Poisson Manifolds

We can now write down the
modified deformed Grothendieck connection
as defined in
[
5
,
12
]
, namely

(94)

\displaystyle\overline{\mathcal{D}}_{G}=\mathcal{D}_{G}+[\gamma,\kern 5.0pt]_{\star},

where the deformed Grothendieck connection
\mathcal{D}_{G}
is explicitly given by (
91
), the star product is explicitly given by (
87
),
[\kern 5.0pt,\kern 5.0pt]_{\star}
denotes the star commutator and
\gamma\in\Omega^{1}(M,\widehat{\textnormal{Sym}}(T^{*}M)[[\hbar]])
is such that the following Fedosov-type equation holds:

(95)

\displaystyle F^{M}+\mathcal{D}_{G}\gamma+\gamma\star\gamma=0,

with Weyl curvature
F^{M}=F(R,R,\textsf{T}\phi^{*}\pi)
explicitly given by (
93
). This equation appears in the globalization construction for deformation quantization of Poisson manifolds.
The existence of such a
\gamma
was given in
[
5
,
6
]
. Let us emphasize a bit more on this existence result.
Since
\gamma
takes values in
\widehat{\textnormal{Sym}}(T^{*}M)[[\hbar]]
we may write

(96)

\displaystyle\gamma=\gamma_{0}+\hbar\gamma_{1}+\hbar^{2}\gamma_{2}+\ldots

Similarly, for the deformed Grothendieck connection we may write

(97)

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{G}=D_{G}+\hbar^{2}\mathcal{D}_{2}+\hbar^{4}\mathcal{D}_{4}+\ldots

where
D_{G}={\mathrm{d}}+L_{R}
is the classical Grothendieck connection and where we have used that the Kontsevich weights (
89
) satisfy
w_{\Upsilon_{0}}=1
and
w_{\Upsilon_{n}}=0
for all odd
n\geq 1
.

Finally, for the curvature we can write

(98)

\displaystyle F^{M}=\hbar F_{1}+\hbar^{3}F_{3}+\hbar^{5}F_{5}+\ldots

where we have used that that the Kontsevich weights (
92
) satisfy
w_{\Gamma_{n}}=0
for all even
n\geq 0
.

This now allows us to decompose Equation (
95
) into a system of equations depending on the order of
\hbar
.

In order
\hbar^{0}
we get the equation

(99)

\displaystyle D_{G}\gamma_{0}=0,

which, according to Section
1.2
, can be solved by
\gamma_{0}=\textsf{T}\phi^{*}f
for some smooth function
f\in C^{\infty}(M)
, since the cohomology of the classical Grothendieck connection
H^{\bullet}_{D_{G}}(\Gamma(\widehat{\textnormal{Sym}}(T^{*}M)))
is concentrated in degree zero (by the Poincaré Lemma) and

(100)

H^{0}_{D_{G}}(\Gamma(\widehat{\textnormal{Sym}}(T^{*}M)))\cong\textsf{T}\phi^{*}C^{\infty}(M)\cong C^{\infty}(M).

In order
\hbar^{1}
we get the equation

(101)

\displaystyle F_{1}+D_{G}\gamma_{1}+(\gamma_{0}\star\gamma_{0})_{1}=0.

Using the Bianchi identity we see that
D_{G}F_{1}=0
and by Equation (
99
) it also immidiately follows that
D_{G}(\gamma_{0}\star\gamma_{0})_{1}=0
. So we get that
D_{G}\gamma_{1}
is equal to a
D_{G}
-closed form, but the corresponding cohomology group is trivial. Hence it follows that
D_{G}\gamma_{1}
is equal to a
D_{G}
-exact form, and thus it is possible to find a
\gamma_{1}
that solves Equation (
101
).

By induction, one can show that in each order
\hbar^{k}
for
k\geq 1
,
D_{G}\gamma_{k}
is equal to a
D_{G}
-closed and hence
D_{G}
-exact form depending on the lower order coefficients of
F^{M}
and
\gamma
. In particular it follows that there exists a
\gamma_{k}
solving the equation for the corresponding order.

Remark 3.1
.

Note that in order to globalize Kontsevich’s star product one may be tempted to define a bullet product

(102)

(f\bullet g)(x):=\big(P(\textsf{T}\phi^{*}\pi)(\textsf{T}\phi^{*}f\otimes\textsf{T}\phi^{*}g)\big)(x;0).

This is indeed a well-defined global product on
C^{\infty}(M)[[\hbar]]
, but it is in general not associative. To make this product associative one has to introduce a quantization map (see e.g.
[
5
]
)

(103)

\rho\colon H^{0}_{D_{G}}(\Gamma(\widehat{\textnormal{Sym}}(T^{*}M)))\rightarrow H^{0}_{\overline{\mathcal{D}}_{G}}(\Gamma(\widehat{\textnormal{Sym}}(T^{*}M)[[\hbar]]))

which then again leads to the global star product

(104)

f\star_{{}_{M}}g:=\left(\rho^{-1}\big(\rho(\textsf{T}\phi^{*}f)\star\rho(\textsf{T}\phi^{*}g)\big)\right)\Big|_{y=0}.

Here
\star
denotes Kontsevich’s star product and
\star_{M}
its global version on
M
.
Using the weights, we can also get an explicit expression for the bullet product (
102
) by

(105)

\big(P(\mathsf{T}\phi^{*}\pi)(\mathsf{T}\phi^{*}f\otimes\mathsf{T}\phi^{*}g)\big)(x;0)\\
=\left(\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{\hbar^{n}}{2^{2n}n!}(\mathsf{T}\phi^{*}_{x}\pi)^{i_{1}j_{1}}\cdots(\mathsf{T}\phi^{*}_{x}\pi)^{i_{n}j_{n}}(\mathsf{T}\phi^{*}_{x}f)_{\,,i_{1}\cdots i_{n}}(\mathsf{T}\phi^{*}_{x}g)_{\,,j_{1}\cdots j_{n}}\right)(0).

We will consider the lifted vector fields
\overline{R}
to the cotangent case, which induce lifted interaction vertices within the Feynman graphs which appear in the computation of the connection
1
-form and its curvature

Appendix A
Binomial Sums

Here we will treat the binomial sums appearing in the expression (
67
) and show that we indeed get the results stated in (
68
).

A.1.
B(n)

Let us start with

(108)

\displaystyle B(n)=\sum\limits_{l=0}^{n}{n\choose l}\frac{(-1)^{l}}{2^{n+1}(n-l+1)}.

Using the well known identity

(109)

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{l=0}^{n}(-1)^{l}{n+1\choose l}=\sum\limits_{l=0}^{n}(-1)^{l}{n\choose l}\frac{n+1}{n-l+1}=(-1)^{n}

it immidiately follows that

(110)

\displaystyle B(n)=\frac{(-1)^{n}}{2^{n+1}(n+1)}.

A.2.
C(n)

Let us continue with

(111)

\displaystyle C(n)=\sum\limits_{l=0}^{n}{n\choose l}\frac{(-1)^{l}}{2^{l}(n-l+1)}.

Using the identity (
109
), we can write

(112)

\displaystyle(n+1)C(n)=\sum\limits_{l=0}^{n}{n+1\choose l}\left(-\frac{1}{2}\right)^{l}.

Using the Binomial theorem we then find

(113)

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{l=0}^{n}{n+1\choose l}\left(-\frac{1}{2}\right)^{l}=\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{n+1}-\left(-\frac{1}{2}\right)^{n+1},

and hence

(114)

\displaystyle C(n)=\frac{1+(-1)^{n}}{2^{n+1}(n+1)}.

A.3.
A(n)

Finally, let us treat the case

(115)

\displaystyle A(n)=\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n}\sum\limits_{l=0}^{n-k}\sum\limits_{s=0}^{n-k-l}{n\choose k}{n-k\choose l}{n-k-l\choose s}\frac{(-1)^{l+s}}{2^{n-k-s+1}(n-k-l-s+1)}.

Write

(116)

\displaystyle A(n)=\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n}\sum\limits_{l=0}^{n-k}{n\choose k}{n-k\choose l}\frac{(-1)^{l}}{2^{n-k+1}}\sum\limits_{s=0}^{n-k-l}{n-k-l\choose s}\frac{(-2)^{s}}{(n-k-l-s+1)}.

We first treat the innermost sum: Set
m=n-k-l
. Then

(117)

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{s=0}^{n-k-l}{n-k-l\choose s}\frac{(-2)^{s}}{(n-k-l-s+1)}=\sum\limits_{s=0}^{m}{m\choose s}\frac{(-2)^{s}}{(m-s+1)}.

Using
{m+1\choose s}=\frac{m+1}{m-s+1}{m\choose s}
, we find that

(118)

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{s=0}^{m}{m\choose s}\frac{(-2)^{s}}{(m-s+1)}=\frac{1}{m+1}\sum\limits_{s=0}^{m}{m+1\choose s}(-2)^{s}.

Applying the Binomial theorem we get that

(119)

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{s=0}^{m}{m+1\choose s}(-2)^{s}=(-1)^{m+1}\big(1-2^{m+1}\big).

Plugging all of this into (
116
), we find

(120)

\displaystyle\begin{split}A(n)&=\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n}\sum\limits_{l=0}^{n-k}{n\choose k}{n-k\choose l}\frac{(-1)^{l}}{2^{n-k+1}}\frac{(-1)^{n-k-l+1}}{n-k-l+1}\big(1-2^{n-k-l+1}\big)\\
&=\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n}{n\choose k}\frac{(-1)^{n-k+1}}{2^{n-k+1}}\sum\limits_{l=0}^{n-k}{n-k\choose l}\frac{1}{n-k-l+1}\big(1-2^{n-k-l+1}\big).\end{split}

Moreover, we have

(121)

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{l=0}^{n-k}{n-k\choose l}\frac{1}{n-k-l+1}=\frac{1}{n-k+1}\sum\limits_{l=0}^{n-k}{n-k+1\choose l}=\frac{1}{n-k+1}\big(2^{n-k+1}-1\big),

and

(122)

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{l=0}^{n-k}{n-k\choose l}\frac{2^{n-k-l+1}}{n-k-l+1}=\frac{1}{n-k+1}\sum\limits_{l=0}^{n-k}{n-k+1\choose l}2^{n-k-l+1}=\frac{1}{n-k+1}\big(3^{n-k+1}-1\big).

Hence

(123)

\displaystyle\begin{split}A(n)&=\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n}{n\choose k}\frac{(-1)^{n-k+1}}{2^{n-k+1}(n-k+1)}\big(2^{n-k+1}-3^{n-k+1}\big)\\
&=(-1)^{n+1}\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n}{n\choose k}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{n-k+1}\left(1-\left(\frac{3}{2}\right)^{n-k+1}\right).\end{split}

Now

(124)

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n}{n\choose k}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{n-k+1}=\frac{1}{n+1}\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n}{n+1\choose k}(-1)^{k}=\frac{(-1)^{n}}{n+1},

and

(125)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n}{n\choose k}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{n-k+1}\Big(\frac{3}{2}\Big)^{n-k+1}&=\frac{(-1)^{n+1}}{n+1}\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n}{n+1\choose k}\left(-\frac{3}{2}\right)^{n-k+1}=\frac{(-1)^{n}}{n+1}\\
&=\frac{1}{2^{n+1}(n+1)}+\frac{(-1)^{n}}{n+1}.\end{split}

Finally, we get

(126)

\displaystyle A(n)=(-1)^{n+1}\left(\frac{(-1)^{n}}{n+1}-\frac{1}{2^{n+1}(n+1)}-\frac{(-1)^{n}}{n+1}\right)=\frac{(-1)^{n}}{2^{n+1}(n+1)}.

Automorphisms of a Chevalley group of type
𝐆

Abstract




Abstract.

In this paper we prove that every automorphism of a Chevalley group with the root system
\mathbf{G}_{2}
over a commutative ring
R
with
1/3
, generated by all its invertible elements and the ideal
2R
is a composition of ring and inner automorphisms.

Introduction

Study of automorphisms of classical groups was started by the work of Schreier and van der Warden
[
34
]
in 1928. They described all automorphisms of the group
{\,\mathrm{PSL}\,}_{n}
(
n\geqslant 3
) over an arbitrary field.

Diedonne
[
22
]
(1951) and Rickart
[
33
]
(1950) introduced the involution method, and with the help of this method described automorphisms of the group
{\,\mathrm{GL}\,}_{n}
(
n\geqslant 3
) over a skewfield.

The first step in construction of the automorphism theory over rings, namely, for the group
{\,\mathrm{GL}\,}_{n}
(
n\geqslant 3
) over the ring of integer numbers, was made by Hua Logen and Reiner
[
25
]
(1951), later some papers over commutative integral domains appeared.
The methods of the papers mentioned above were based mostly on studying involutions in the corresponding linear groups.

O’Meara
[
29
]
in 1976 invented very different (geometrical) method, which did not use involutions. By its aid, O”Meara described automorphisms of the group
\,\mathrm{GL}\,_{n}
(
n\geqslant 3
) over domains.

In 1982 Petechuk
[
30
]
described automorphisms of the groups
{\,\mathrm{GL}\,}_{n},{\,\mathrm{SL}\,}_{n}(n\geqslant 4)
over arbitrary commutative rings. If
n=3
, automorphisms of given linear groups are not always standard. They are standard either if in a ring
2
is invertible, or if a ring is a domain, or it is a semisimple ring.

Isomorphisms of the groups
{GL}_{n}(R)
and
{GL}_{m}(S)
over arbitrary associative rings with
1/2
for
n,m\geqslant 3
were described in 1981 by I.Z. Golubchik and A.V. Mikhalev
[
23
]
and independently by E.I. Zelmanov
[
40
]
. In 1997 I.Z  Golubchik
[
24
]
described isomorphisms between these groups for
n,m\geqslant 4
, but over arbitrary associative rings with
1
.

In 50-th years of the previous century Chevalley
[
20
]
, Steinberg
[
35
]
and others introduced the concept of Chevalley groups
over commutative rings, which includes classical linear groups (special linear
{\,\mathrm{SL}\,}
, special orthogonal
{\,\mathrm{SO}\,}
, symplectic
{\,\mathrm{Sp}\,}
, spinor
{\,\mathrm{Spin}\,}
, and also projective groups connected with them) over commutative rings.

Clear that isomorphisms and automorphisms of Chevalley groups were also studied intensively.
The description of isomorphisms of Chevalley groups over fields was obtained by Steinberg for the finite case
[
36
]
and by Humphreys for the infinite one
[
26
]
. Many papers were devoted to description of automorphisms of Chevalley groups over different commutative rings, we can mention here the papers of Borel–Tits
[
4
]
, Carter–Chen Yu
[
18
]
, Chen Yu
[
19
]
, E. Abe
[
1
]
, A. Klyachko
[
28
]
.

In the paper
[
6
]
Bunina proved that automorphisms of adjoint elementary Chevalley groups with root systems
\mathbf{A}_{l},\mathbf{D}_{l},\mathbf{E}_{l},

l\geqslant 2
, over local rings with invertible
2
can be represented as the composition of a ring automorphism and an automorphism-conjugation by some matrix from the normalizer of this group in
{\,\mathrm{GL}\,}(V)
. In the paper
[
8
]
according to the results of
[
6
]
it was shown that every automorphism of an elementary Chevalley group of the described type is standard, i. e., is represented by the composition of ring, inner, central and graph automorphisms. In the same paper the theorem describing the normalizer of Chevalley groups in their adjoint representation, which also holds for local rings without
1/2
, was obtained.

In the papers
[
7
]
,
[
9
]
,
[
12
]
, by the same methods it was shown that all automorphisms of Chevalley groups with the root systems
\mathbf{F}_{4},\mathbf{G}_{2},\mathbf{B}_{l}
,
l\geqslant 2
, over local rings with
1/2
(in the case
\mathbf{G}_{2}
also with
1/3
) are standard. In the paper
[

]
it was proved that all automorphisms of Chevalley groups of types
\mathbf{A}_{l},\mathbf{D}_{l},\mathbf{E}_{l},l\geqslant 3
, over local rings without
1/2
, are standard.

In the paper
[
14
]
with the help of all previous results and localization method the automorphisms of adjoint Chevalley groups over arbitrary commutative rings were described, where a root system has rank
>1
, and for
\mathbf{A}_{2},\mathbf{F}_{4},\mathbf{B}_{l},\mathbf{C}_{l}
the ring contains
1/2
, for
\mathbf{G}_{2}
the ring contains
1/2
and
1/3
. In the recent paper
[
15
]
this result was extended to all Chevalley groups (not only adjoint) with the same restrictions for rings.

In the papers
[
10
]
,
[

E.I. Bunina and P.A. Weryovkin proved that every automorphism of a Chevalley group of the type
\mathbf{G}_{2}
over a local ring with

Over fields of characteristic
2
and the root systems
\mathbf{F}_{4}
there exists a non-standard automorphism of the corresponding Chevalley group (see
[

]
). Over fields of characteristic
2
and the root systems
\mathbf{B}_{l}
and
\mathbf{C}_{l}
here exists a non-standard isomorphism between the corresponding Chevalley groups (also see

\mathbf{A}_{2}

So it is a natural goal to continue the result of standardity of automorphisms of the type

1
Definitions and main theorem.

We fix the root system of type
\mathbf{G}_{2}
with the system of simple roots
\Delta(\mathbf{G}_{2})=\{\alpha_{1}=e_{1}-e_{2},\alpha_{2}=-2e_{1}+e_{2}+e_{3}\}
.

The system of positive roots :

\mathbf{G}_{2}^{+}=\{\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}=e_{1},2\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2},3\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2},3\alpha_{1}+2\alpha_{2}\}
.

More details about root systems and their properties can be found in
[
5
]
and
[
27
]
.

Suppose now that we have some semisimple complex Lie algebra
{{\mathcal{L}}}
of type
\mathbf{G}_{2}
with Cartan subalgebra
{{\mathcal{H}}}
(detailed information about semisimple Lie algebras can be found in the book
[
27
]
).
Then we can choose a basis
\{h_{1},...,h_{l}\}\in{{\mathcal{H}}}
and for every
\alpha\in\mathbf{G}_{2}
elements
x_{\alpha}\in{{\mathcal{L}}}_{\alpha}
so that
\{h_{i};x_{\alpha}\}
form a basis in
{{\mathcal{L}}}
and for every two elements of this basis their commutator is an integral linear combination of the elements of the same basis.

Let us introduce elementary Chevalley groups (see, for example,
[
35
]
).
Let
{{\mathcal{L}}}
be a semisimple Lie algebra (over
{{\mathcal{L}}}
) with a root system
\mathbf{G}_{2},\,\pi:{{\mathcal{L}}}\rightarrow gl_{n}(V)
be its finitely dimensional faithful representation (of dimension
n
). If
{{\mathcal{H}}}
is a Cartan subalgebra of
{{\mathcal{L}}}
, then a functional
\lambda\in{{\mathcal{H}}}^{*}
is called a
weight
of a given representation, if there exists a nonzero vector
v\in V
(called a
weight vector
) such that for any
h\in{{\mathcal{H}}}\,\pi(h)v=\lambda(h)v

in the space
V
there exists a basis of weight vectors such that all operators
t^{k}\pi(x_{\alpha})^{k}/k!\quad k\in\mathbb{N}
are written as integral (nilpotent) matrices. This basis is called a
Chevalley basis
. An integral matrix also can be considered as a matrix over an arbitrary commutative ring with
1
. Let
R
be such a ring. Consider matrices
n\times n
over
R
, matrices
t^{k}\pi(x_{\alpha})^{k}/k!
for
\alpha\in\mathbf{G}_{2},k\in\mathbb{N}
are included in
M_{n}(R)
.

Now consider automorphisms of the free module
\,\mathrm{Aut}\,(R^{n})
of the form

x_{\alpha}(t):=\exp(tx_{\alpha})=1+t\pi(x_{\alpha})+\dots+t^{k}\pi(x_{\alpha})^{k}/k!+\dots

Since all matrices
x_{\alpha}
are nilpotent, this series is finite. Automorphisms
x_{\alpha}(t)
are called
elementary root elements
. The subgroup
\,\mathrm{Aut}\,(R^{n})
, generated by
x_{\alpha}(t),\alpha\in\mathbf{G}_{2}
, is called an
elementary adjoint Chevalley group
.

The action of
x_{\alpha}(t)
on the Chevalley basis is described in
[
39
]
.

All weights of a given representation generate (by addition) a lattice (a free Abelian group, where every
\mathbb{Z}
-basis is also a
\mathbb{C}
-basis in
{{\mathcal{H}}}^{*}
), which is called the
weight lattice

\Lambda_{\pi}
.

Elementary Chevalley groups are defined not even by a representation of the Chevalley groups,
but just by its
weight lattice
. More precisely, up to an abstract
isomorphism an elementary Chevalley group is completely defined by a
root system
\Phi
, a commutative ring
R
with
1
and a weight
lattice
\Lambda_{\pi}
.

Among all lattices we mark the lattice corresponding to the
adjoint representation, it is generated by all roots (the
root
lattice

\Lambda_{ad}
).
The corresponding (elementary) Chevalley group is called
adjoint
.

Note that for the root system
\mathbf{G}_{2}
there exists only one weight lattice, which is simultaneously simply connected and adjoint, therefore for every ring
R
there exists only one Chevalley group of the type
\mathbf{G}_{2}
:
G(R)=G_{\,\mathrm{ad}\,}(\mathbf{G}_{2},R)
.

In elementary Chevalley group it is possible to consider the following important elements:

—
w_{\alpha}(t)=x_{\alpha}(t)x_{-\alpha}(-t^{-1})x_{\alpha}(t),\;t\in R^{\star}
;

—
h_{\alpha}(t)=w_{\alpha}(t)w_{\alpha}(1)^{-1}
.

Every elementary Chevalley group satisfies the following relations:

(R1)
\forall\alpha\in\mathbf{G}_{2},\forall t,u\in R,\;x_{\alpha}(t)x_{\alpha}(u)=x_{\alpha}(t+u);

(R2) If
\{\alpha,\beta\}
are simple roots of the system
\mathbf{G}_{2}
,
\alpha
is long,
\beta
is short,
t,u\in R
, then

\displaystyle\left[x_{\alpha}(t),x_{\beta}(u)\right]

\displaystyle=x_{\alpha+\beta}(tu)x_{\alpha+3\beta}(-tu^{3})x_{\alpha+2\beta}(-tu^{2})x_{2\alpha+3\beta}(t^{2}u^{3}),

\displaystyle\left[x_{\alpha+\beta}(t),x_{\beta}(u)\right]

\displaystyle=x_{\alpha+2\beta}(2tu)x_{\alpha+3\beta}(-3tu^{2})x_{2\alpha+3\beta}(3t^{2}u^{3});

\displaystyle\left[x_{\alpha}(t),x_{\alpha+3\beta}(u)\right]

\displaystyle=x_{2\alpha+3\beta}(tu),

\displaystyle\left[x_{\alpha+2\beta}(t),x_{\beta}(u)\right]

\displaystyle=x_{\alpha+3\beta}(-3tu),

\displaystyle\left[x_{\alpha+\beta}(t),x_{\alpha+2\beta}(u)\right]

\displaystyle=x_{2\alpha+3\beta}(3tu);

(R3)
\forall\alpha\in\mathbf{G}_{2}\,\,\,w_{\alpha}:=w_{\alpha}(1)
;

(R4)
\forall\alpha,\beta\in\mathbf{G}_{2},\forall t\in R^{\star}\;w_{\alpha}h_{\beta}(t)w_{\alpha}^{-1}=h_{w_{\alpha}(\beta)}(t)
;

(R5)
\forall\alpha,\beta\in\mathbf{G}_{2}\,\,\,\forall t\in R\;w_{\alpha}x_{\beta}(t)w_{\alpha}^{-1}=x_{w_{\alpha}(\beta)}(ct);
, where
c=c(\alpha,\beta)=\pm 1
;

(R6)
\forall\alpha,\beta\in\mathbf{G}_{2}\,\,\,\forall t\in R^{\star}\;\forall u\in R\;h_{\alpha}(t)x_{\beta}(u)h_{\alpha}(t)^{-1}=x_{\beta}(t^{\langle\beta,\alpha\rangle}u)
.

Consider semisimple linear algebraic groups over algebraically
closed fields. These are precisely elementary Chevalley groups

E_{\pi}(\Phi,K)
.

All these groups are defined in
\,\mathrm{SL}\,_{n}(K)
as common set of zeros of
polynomials of matrix entries
a_{ij}
with integer coefficients
It is clear now that multiplication and
taking inverse element are defined by polynomials with integer
coefficients. Therefore, these polynomials can be considered as
polynomials over an arbitrary commutative ring with a unit. Let some
elementary Chevalley group
E
over
\mathbb{C}
be defined in

\,\mathrm{SL}\,_{n}(\mathbb{C})
by polynomials
p_{1}(a_{ij}),\dots,p_{m}(a_{ij})
.
For a commutative ring
R
with a unit let us consider the group

G(R)=\{(a_{ij})\in\,\mathrm{SL}\,_{n}(R)\mid\widetilde{p}_{1}(a_{ij})=0,\dots,\widetilde{p}_{m}(a_{ij})=0\},

where
\widetilde{p}_{1}(\dots),\dots\widetilde{p}_{m}(\dots)
are
polynomials having the same coefficients as

p_{1}(\dots),\dots,p_{m}(\dots)
, but considered over
R
.

This group is called
the Chevalley group

G_{\,\mathrm{ad}\,}(\mathbf{G}_{2},R)
of the type
\mathbf{G}_{2}
over the ring
R
, and it coincides with the elementary Chevalley group for every algebraically
closed field
K
.

Define two types of automorphisms of a Chevalley group
G_{\,\mathrm{ad}\,}(\mathbf{G}_{2},R)
, we call them
standard
.

Ring automorphisms.
Let
\rho:R\to R
be an automorphism of
the ring
R
. The mapping
(a_{i,j})\mapsto(\rho(a_{i,j}))
from
G_{\,\mathrm{ad}\,}(\mathbf{G}_{2},R)

onto itself is an automorphism of the group
G_{\,\mathrm{ad}\,}(\mathbf{G}_{2},R)
,
denoted by the same letter
\rho
. It is called a
ring
automorphism
of the group
G_{\,\mathrm{ad}\,}(\mathbf{G}_{2},R)
. Note that for all

\alpha\in\mathbf{G}_{2}
and
t\in R
an element
x_{\alpha}(t)
is mapped to

x_{\alpha}(\rho(t))
.

Inner automorphisms.
Let
S
be some ring containing
R
,
g

be an element of
G_{\,\mathrm{ad}\,}(\mathbf{G}_{2},S)
, that normalizes the subgroup
G_{\,\mathrm{ad}\,}(\mathbf{G}_{2},R)
. Then
the mapping
x\mapsto gxg^{-1}
is an automorphism
of the group
G_{\,\mathrm{ad}\,}(\mathbf{G}_{2},R)
, denoted by
i_{g}
. It is called an

inner automorphism
,
induced by the element

g\in G_{\,\mathrm{ad}\,}(\mathbf{G}_{2},S)
. If
g\in G_{\,\mathrm{ad}\,}(\mathbf{G}_{2},R)
, then we call
i_{g}
a
strictly inner

automorphism.

These two automorphisms are called
standard
. Also central and graph automorphisms are called standard,
but in the case under consideration nontrivial central and graph automorphisms do not exist, therefore we will call an automorphism of a group
G(R)

standard
, if it is a composition of two introduced types of automorphisms.

Our goal is to prove the following theorem:

Theorem 1
.

Let
G=G_{\,\mathrm{ad}\,}(\mathbf{G}_{2},R)
or
G=E_{\,\mathrm{ad}\,}(\mathbf{G}_{2},R)

be a Chevalley group of the type
\mathbf{G}_{2}
or its elementary subgroup,
R
be a commutative ring with
1/3
, generated by its invertible elements and the ideal
2R
. Then any automorphism of the group
G

is standard, i. e., is a composition of a ring and strictly inner automorphisms.

Notions and theorems, necessary for the proof.

2.1
Localization of rings and modules; injection of a ring into the product of its localizations.

Definition 1.

Let
R
be a commutative ring. A subset
Y\subset R
is called
multiplicatively closed
in
R
, if
1\in Y
and
Y
is closed under multiplication.

Introduce an equivalence relation
\sim
on the set of pairs
R\times Y
as follows:

\frac{a}{s}\sim\frac{b}{t}\Longleftrightarrow\exists u\in Y:\ (at-bs)u=0.

By
\frac{a}{s}
we denote the whole equivalence class of the pair
(a,s)
, by
Y^{-1}R
we denote the set of all equivalence classes. On the set
S^{-1}R
we can introduce the ring structure by

\frac{a}{s}+\frac{b}{t}=\frac{at+bs}{st},\quad\frac{a}{s}\cdot\frac{b}{t}=\frac{ab}{st}.

Definition 2.

The ring
Y^{-1}R
is called the
ring of fractions of
R
with respect to
Y
.

Let
\mathfrak{p}
be a prime ideal of
R
. Then the set
Y=R\setminus{\mathfrak{p}}
is multiplicatively closed (it is equivalent to the definition of the prime ideal). We will denote the ring of fractions
Y^{-1}R
in this case by
R_{\mathfrak{p}}
. The elements
\frac{a}{s}
,
a\in\mathfrak{p}
, form an ideal
\mathfrak{M}
in
R_{\mathfrak{p}}
. If
\frac{b}{t}\notin\mathfrak{M}
, then

, therefore
\frac{b}{t}
is invertible in

. Consequently the ideal
\mathfrak{M}
consists of all non-invertible elements of the ring

, i. e.,

is the greatest ideal of this ring, so

is a local ring.

The process of passing from
R
to

is called

{\mathfrak{p}}
.

The construction
S^{-1}A
can be easily carried trough with an

.
Let

denote the equivalence class of the pair

, the set

of all such fractions is made as a module

with obvious operations of addition and scalar multiplication. As above we will write

for

, where

is a prime ideal of

.

Proposition 1
.

Every commutative ring
R
with

can be naturally embedded in the cartesian product of all its localizations by maximal ideals

S=\prod\limits_{{\mathfrak{m}}\text{ is a maximal ideal of }A}A_{\mathfrak{m}}

r_{J}\varphi(x_{\alpha}(1))=i_{g_{J}}r_{J}(x_{\alpha}(1)),

where
g_{J}\in G_{\,\mathrm{ad}\,}(\mathbf{G}_{2},\overline{R_{J}})
(an extesion of
R_{J}
).

Second step.

We prove, that an inner automorphism of the group
G_{\,\mathrm{ad}\,}(\mathbf{G}_{2},S)
, generated by
g=\prod g_{J}
, induces an automorphism of the group
G_{\,\mathrm{ad}\,}(\mathbf{G}_{2},R)
and in fact is strictly inner.

Further, we show, that if we take a composition of the initial automorphism and the inner automorphism
i_{g^{-1}}
, the obtained automorphism is a ring one.

4
Proof of the first step.

Consider an arbitrary element
x_{\alpha}(1)\in E_{\,\mathrm{ad}\,}(\mathbf{G}_{2},R)
,
\alpha\in\mathbf{G}_{2}
. Its image under the mapping
r_{I}
is
x_{\alpha}(1)=x_{\alpha}(1/1)\in E_{\,\mathrm{ad}\,}(\mathbf{G}_{2},R_{I})
. In the field
R/I
its image has the same form. The element
x_{\alpha}^{\prime}=\varphi(x_{\alpha}(1))\in E_{\,\mathrm{ad}\,}(\mathbf{G}_{2},R)
under the factorization by the ideal
J
gives
\overline{\varphi}(x_{\alpha}(1))=i_{\overline{g}}(x_{\alpha}(1))
, where
\overline{g}\in G_{\,\mathrm{ad}\,}(\mathbf{G}_{2},R/J)
.

Now choose
g\in G_{\,\mathrm{ad}\,}(\mathbf{G}_{2},R_{J})
such that under the factorization of ring
R_{J}
by its radical
g
is mapped to
\overline{g}
.

Then consider the following mapping
\psi:E_{\,\mathrm{ad}\,}(\mathbf{G}_{2},R)\to E_{\,\mathrm{ad}\,}(\mathbf{G}_{2},R_{J})
:

\psi=i_{g^{-1}}\circ r_{J}\circ\varphi.

Under the mapping
\psi
all
x_{\alpha}(1)
,
\alpha\in\mathbf{G}_{2}
are mapped to
x_{\alpha}^{\prime}
such that
x_{\alpha}(1)-x_{\alpha}^{\prime}\in M_{N}(\,\mathrm{Rad}\,R_{J})
.

Therefore, we now have a set of elements
\{x_{\alpha}^{\prime}\mid\alpha\in\mathbf{G}_{2}\}\subset E_{\,\mathrm{ad}\,}(\mathbf{G}_{2},R_{J})
, satisfying the same relations as
\{x_{\alpha}(1)\mid\alpha\in\mathbf{G}_{2}\}
, and equivalent to the corresponding
x_{\alpha}(1)
modulo radical
R_{J}
.

It is precisely the situation of the paper
[
10
]
, in which for a local ring
R
and the root system
\mathbf{G}_{2}
if
3\in R^{*}
without any additional conditions for the ring it is proved, that if in the group
E_{\,\mathrm{ad}\,}(\mathbf{G}_{2},R)
some elements
x_{\alpha}^{\prime}
are the images of the corresponding
x_{\alpha}(1)
,
\alpha\in\mathbf{G}_{2}
, and also
x_{\alpha}(1)-x_{\alpha}^{\prime}\in M_{N}(\,\mathrm{Rad}\,R)
, then there exists
g^{\prime}\in G_{\,\mathrm{ad}\,}(\mathbf{G}_{2},R)
,
g^{\prime}-E\in M_{N}(\,\mathrm{Rad}\,R)
such that for any
\alpha\in\mathbf{G}_{2}

x_{\alpha}(1)=i_{g^{\prime}}(x_{\alpha}^{\prime}).

Thus, the first step of the theorem completely follows from the statements of the previous paragraph.
\square

Now embedding the initial ring
R
into the ring
S=\prod\limits_{J}R_{J}
, we see that

\varphi(x_{\alpha}(1))=g(x_{\alpha}(1))g^{-1},

where
g=\prod\limits_{J}g_{J}
.

5
Proof of the second step of the theorem.

Conjugation by an element
g\in G_{\,\mathrm{ad}\,}(\mathbf{G}_{2},S)
can be extended up to the automorphism of the whole matrix ring
M_{N}(S)
.

Let us prove the following lemma:

Lemma 1
.

Any matrix
x_{\alpha}(t),\alpha\in\mathbf{G}_{2}
,
t\in R^{*}
or
t\in 2R
, under conjugation by
g
is mapped into a matrix from
\,\mathrm{GL}\,_{N}(R)
.

Proof.

For
x_{\alpha}(1)
, where
\alpha\in\mathbf{G}_{2}
, this statement is true (see the first step).

Let
\alpha
— be a long root,
\alpha\in\mathbf{G}_{2}
. For the long roots let us consider

x_{\alpha}(1)=E+X_{\alpha}+\frac{X_{\alpha}^{2}}{2},\quad

where

\frac{X_{\alpha}^{2}}{2}=E_{(\alpha,-\alpha)}.

Lets us take two long roots
\gamma
,
\beta\in\mathbf{G}_{2}
such that
\gamma+\beta=\alpha
.
Note that

(x_{\gamma}(1)x_{\beta}(1)-x_{\gamma}(1)-x_{\beta}(1)+E)^{2}=\pm E_{(\alpha,-\alpha)},

it easily gives
X_{\alpha}
.

Then any matrix
x_{\alpha}(t)
under the conjugation by
g
is mapped into a matrix from
M_{N}(R)
.

Now consider some invertible element from the ring
R
.
For any invertible element
t\in R^{*}

h_{\alpha}(t)=x_{\alpha}(t)x_{-\alpha}(-t^{-1})x_{\alpha}(t)x_{\alpha}(1)^{-1}x_{-\alpha}(-1)^{-1}x_{\alpha}(1)^{-1}.

Therefore, conjugation
g
maps the element
h_{\alpha}(t)
to a matrix from
M_{N}(R)
.

Now let us consider an arbitrary short root
\beta
and a long root
\alpha
, such that

\langle\beta,\alpha\rangle=1.

Then

x_{\beta}(t^{\langle\beta,\alpha\rangle})=h_{\alpha}(t)x_{\beta}(1)h_{\alpha}(t)^{-1},

i. e.,
x_{\beta}(t)
for any invertible element
t\in R^{*}
is mapped under conjugation by
g
into a matrix with coefficients from
R
.

Now let again
\beta
be some short root. Then

x_{\beta}(\pm 1)=E\pm X_{\beta}+\frac{X_{\beta}^{2}}{2}\pm\frac{X_{\beta}^{3}}{6},

hence

x_{\beta}(-1)+x_{\beta}(1)=2E+X_{\beta}^{2}\Longrightarrow X_{\beta}^{2}=x_{\beta}(-1)+x_{\beta}(1)-2E.

[formula]

Similarly

x_{\beta}(1)-x_{\beta}(-1)=2X_{\beta}+\frac{X_{\beta}^{3}}{3}\Longrightarrow\frac{X_{\beta}^{3}}{3}=x_{\beta}(1)-x_{\beta}(-1)-2X_{\beta}.

[formula]

Besides,

x_{\beta}(2)=E+2X_{\beta}+2X_{\beta}^{2}+\frac{4X_{\beta}^{3}}{3}=\\
=E+2X_{\beta}+2x_{\beta}(1)+2x_{\beta}(-1)-4E+4x_{\beta}(1)-4x_{\beta}(-1)-8X_{\beta}=\\
=-3E-6X_{\beta}+6x_{\beta}(1)-2x_{\beta}(-1),

therefore, using the invertibility of
3
, we obtain

2X_{\beta}=\frac{-x_{\beta}(2)-2x_{\beta}(-1)}{3}-E+2x_{\beta}(1).

[formula]

From (2) and (3) it now follows

\frac{X_{\beta}^{3}}{3}=-x_{\beta}(1)-\frac{x_{\beta}(-1)}{3}+\frac{x_{\beta}(2)}{3}+E.

[formula]

Then any
x_{\beta}(t)
, where
t\in 2R
, can be expressed through
x_{\beta}(1)
and
t
, therefore, it also mapped into a matrix with coefficients from
R
under the conjugation by
g
.

Therefore all
x_{\beta}(t)
, where
t
is either invertible, or is divided by
2
, are mapped into matrices with coefficients from
R
under the conjugation by
g
.
∎

If (as in our case)
R
is generated by invertible elements and the ideal
2R
, then

gG_{\,\mathrm{ad}\,}(\mathbf{G}_{2},R)g^{-1}\subseteq M_{N}(R)\cap G_{\,\mathrm{ad}\,}(\mathbf{G}_{2},S)=G_{\,\mathrm{ad}\,}(\mathbf{G}_{2},R),

Therefore

gG_{\,\mathrm{ad}\,}(\mathbf{G}_{2},R)g^{-1}=G_{\,\mathrm{ad}\,}(\mathbf{G}_{2},R)\;\text{and}\;gE_{\,\mathrm{ad}\,}(\mathbf{G}_{2},R)g^{-1}=E_{\,\mathrm{ad}\,}(\mathbf{G}_{2},R).

Consequently, if we take composition of the initial automorphism
\varphi\in\,\mathrm{Aut}\,(G_{\,\mathrm{ad}\,}(\mathbf{G}_{2},R))
and conjugation
i_{g^{-1}}
by the element
g^{-1}
, we get some automorphism
\rho\in\,\mathrm{Aut}\,(G_{\,\mathrm{ad}\,}(\mathbf{G}_{2},R))
, for which
\rho(x_{\alpha}(1))=x_{\alpha}(1)
for any
\alpha\in\mathbf{G}_{2}
.

Lemma 2
.

Any automorphism
\rho\in\,\mathrm{Aut}\,(G_{\,\mathrm{ad}\,}(\mathbf{G}_{2},R))
(or
\rho\in\,\mathrm{Aut}\,(E_{\,\mathrm{ad}\,}(\mathbf{G}_{2},R))
), for which for all
\alpha\in\mathbf{G}_{2}

\rho(x_{\alpha}(1))=x_{\alpha}(1)
, is a ring automorphism of the Chevalley group (respectively, its elementary subgroup).

Proof.

For a given
\alpha\in\mathbf{G}_{2}
by
\Gamma_{\alpha}
let us denote the set of all
x_{\beta}(1)
such that
[x_{\alpha}(1),x_{\beta}(1)]=e
. In the paper
[
16
]
it is proved, that the centralizer of
\Gamma_{\alpha}
in the Chevalley group (or in its elementary subgroup) coincides with
CX_{\alpha}
, where
C
is the center of the Chevalley group, and
X_{\alpha}=\{x_{\alpha}(t)\mid t\in R\}
. In our root system
\mathbf{G}_{2}
center is trivial (since the group is adjoint), so we get
X_{\alpha}
.

If under the action of
\rho
the elements
x_{\beta}(1)
are mapped into itself, so the centralizer of any its set also is mapped into itself, so for any
t\in R
there exists
s\in R
such that
\rho(x_{\alpha}(t))=x_{\alpha}(s)
.

Let us show, that the obtained mapping
t\mapsto s
does not depend of choosing a root
\alpha\in\mathbf{G}_{2}
. Actually, it must coincide on roots of the same length, since if
\alpha_{1}
and
\alpha_{2}
have the same length, there exists an element from the Weil group
w
(generated only by
x_{\beta}(1)
) such that

\forall t\in R\quad wx_{\alpha_{1}}(t)w^{-1}=x_{\alpha_{2}}(t).

Let us denote the obtained mapping on long roots by
\rho_{1}
, and on short roots by
\rho_{2}
.

Apply the automorphism
\rho
to the expression
[x_{\alpha}(t),x_{\beta}(1)]
, where
\alpha,\beta
are short roots of the system
\mathbf{G}_{2}
,
\alpha
is long,
\beta
is short:

\rho([x_{\alpha}(t),x_{\beta}(1)])=[\rho(x_{\alpha}(t)),\rho(x_{\beta}(1))]\Longleftrightarrow\\
\Longleftrightarrow x_{\alpha+\beta}(\rho_{2}(t))x_{\alpha+3\beta}(\rho_{1}(-t))x_{\alpha+2\beta}(\rho_{2}(-t))x_{2\alpha+3\beta}(\rho_{1}(t^{2}))=\\
=x_{\alpha+\beta}(\rho_{1}(t))x_{\alpha+3\beta}(-\rho_{1}(t))x_{\alpha+2\beta}(-\rho_{1}(t))x_{2\alpha+3\beta}(\rho_{1}(t^{2})),

so
\rho_{1}
and
\rho_{2}
coincide.

Denote the obtained mapping also by
\rho:R\to R
, we just need to prove that this mapping is an automorphism of
R
.

Actually, its bijectivity follows from the fact that the initial automorphism
\rho
is bijective.

Its additivity follows from the formula

x_{\alpha}(\rho(t_{1})+\rho(t_{2}))=x_{\alpha}(\rho(t_{1}))x_{\alpha}(\rho(t_{2}))=\rho(x_{\alpha}(t_{1}))\cdot\rho(x_{\alpha}(t_{2}))=\\
=\rho(x_{\alpha}(t_{1})x_{\alpha}(t_{2}))=\rho(x_{\alpha}(t_{1}+t_{2})),

and multiplicativity follows from formula
(R2)

x_{2\alpha+3\beta}(\rho(t_{1})\rho(t_{2}))=[x_{\alpha}(\rho(t_{1})),x_{\alpha+3\beta}(\rho(t_{2}))]=\rho([x_{\alpha}(t_{1}),x_{\alpha+3\beta}(t_{2})])=\\
=\rho(x_{2\alpha+3\beta}(t_{1}t_{2}))=x_{2\alpha+3\beta}(\rho(t_{1}t_{2}))

where
\alpha,\beta
are simple roots of
\mathbf{G}_{2}
.

Thus,
\rho
is a ring automorphism on all
x_{\alpha}(t)
,
\alpha\in\mathbf{G}_{2}
,
t\in R
, therefore it is a ring automorphism of the elementary Chevalley group
E_{\,\mathrm{ad}\,}(\mathbf{G}_{2},R)
.
If an initial automorphism was considered only on the elementary subgroup, lemma is proved.

Then consider the general Chevalley group
G_{\,\mathrm{ad}\,}(\mathbf{G}_{2},R)
. If an automorphism
\rho
coincides with ring automorphism on an elementary subgroup, so if we take the composition of it and the inverse mapping of the ring automorphism, we obtain the automorphism
\rho^{\prime}
of the Chevalley group, which is identical on its elementary subgroup. In this case for any
g\in G_{\,\mathrm{ad}\,}(\mathbf{G}_{2},R)
and all
x\in E_{\,\mathrm{ad}\,}(\mathbf{G}_{2},R)
we have

gxg^{-1}=x^{\prime}\Longrightarrow\rho^{\prime}(g)x\rho^{\prime}(g)^{-1}=x^{\prime}\Longrightarrow\rho^{\prime}(g)x\rho^{\prime}(g)^{-1}=gxg^{-1}\Longrightarrow(g^{-1}\rho^{\prime}(g))x=x(g^{-1}\rho^{\prime}(g)).

As this equality is true for
x\in E_{\,\mathrm{ad}\,}(\mathbf{G}_{2},R)
, and the centralizer of the elementary subroup of an adjoint Chevalley group is trivial, then
g^{-1}\rho^{\prime}(g)=e
and
\rho^{\prime}
is a trivial automorphism.

Therefore,
\rho
is a ring automorphism of the Chevalley group, and the initial automorphism
\varphi
is a composition of a ring and inner automorphisms, what was required.

∎

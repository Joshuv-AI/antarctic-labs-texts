Superconductivity in nickelate and cuprate superconductors with strong bilayer coupling
Zhen Fan, Jian-Feng Zhang, Bo Zhan, Dingshun Lv, Xing-Yu Jiang, Bruce Normand, Tao Xiang

Abstract
The discovery of superconductivity at 80 K under high pressure in La 3 ​ Ni 2 ​ O 7 \text{La}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}\text{Ni}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{7}}} presents the groundbreaking confirmation that high- T c T_{c} superconductivity is a property of strongly correlated materials beyond cuprates. We use density functional theory (DFT) calculations of the band structure of La 3 ​ Ni 2 ​ O 7 \text{La}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}\text{Ni}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{7}}} under pressure to verify that the low-energy bands are composed almost exclusively of Ni 3 d x 2 − y 2 d_{x^{2}-y^{2}} and O 2 p p orbitals. We deduce that the Ni 3 d z 2 d_{z^{2}} orbitals are essentially decoupled by the geometry of the high-pressure structure and by the effect of the Ni Hund coupling being strongly suppressed, which results from the enhanced interlayer antiferromagnetic interaction between 3 d z 2 d_{z^{2}} orbitals and the strong intralayer hybridization of the 3 d x 2 − y 2 d_{x^{2}-y^{2}} orbitals with O 2 p p . By introducing a tight-binding model for the Fermi surfaces and low-energy dispersions, we arrive at a bilayer t t - t ⟂ t_{\perp} - J J model with strong interlayer hopping, which we show is a framework unifying La 3 ​ Ni 2 ​ O 7 \text{La}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}\text{Ni}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{7}}} with cuprate materials possessing similar band structures, particularly the compounds La 2 ​ CaCu 2 ​ O 6 \text{La}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{CaCu}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{6}}} , Pb 2 ​ Sr 2 ​ YCu 3 ​ O 8 \text{Pb}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{Sr}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{YCu}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{8}}} , and EuSr 2 ​ Cu 2 ​ NbO 8 \text{EuSr}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{Cu}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{NbO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{8}}} . We use a renormalized mean-field theory to show that these systems should have ( d d + i ​ s is )-wave superconductivity, with a dominant d d -wave component and the high T c T_{c} driven by the near-optimally doped β \beta band, while the α \alpha band adds an s s -wave component that should lead to clear experimental signatures.

Superconducting phase diagram

An analytical treatment of the

In a one-orbital model on a square lattice with only a dilute concentration,

\displaystyle t_{\mathrm{eff}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2\delta}{1+\delta}t,

(5)

\displaystyle J_{\mathrm{eff}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{4}{(1+\delta)^{2}}J,

(6)

(and similarly for the other hopping integrals). These expressions contain a strong reduction of the effective bandwidth of the mobile holes, arising from the difficulty of propagating in an AF background, and a strong enhancement of the spin correlations arising from the increased weight of the

-

IV
Summary

We have deduced a bilayer
t
-
t_{\perp}
-
J

\text{La}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}\text{Ni}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{7}}}
at high pressure. Our model is based on two key observations. The first is that the
\alpha
and
\beta

d_{x^{2}-y^{2}}
and in-plane O 2
p_{x}
and 2
p_{y}

∘

d_{x^{2}-y^{2}}
and 3
d_{z^{2}}
orbitals, which arises from the strong interactions within their spatially orthogonal subsystems (respectively the planar NiO
2
3

-2

c

3d_{z^{2}}
-2

-3
d_{z^{2}}
units), and hence to a near-complete decoupling. An essential consequence of the 3
d_{x^{2}-y^{2}}
symmetry of the single active low-energy orbital is the
\mathbf{k}
-dependence of the interlayer tunneling, and the resulting degeneracy of the
\alpha
and
\beta
bands along the nodal directions in the Brillouin zone is an important fingerprint of this physics. Other orbitals (such as Ni 4
s

\alpha
and
\beta

\text{La}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}\text{Ni}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{7}}}
are very similar to several cuprate materials with strong bilayer coupling, specifically
\text{La}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{CaCu}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{6}}}
,
\text{Pb}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{Sr}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{YCu}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{8}}}

\text{EuSr}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{Cu}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{NbO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{8}}}

-

By solving the superconducting gap equations of the bilayer

-

Concluding with the broader experimental context, bilayer nickelate superconductivity remains a challenging field of research due to the high pressures required to drive the structural transition to the
Fmmm
phase. Early results showed a very low superconducting volume fraction, which appears to have been a consequence of sample inhomogeneity, and recent experiments suggest that ensuring a robust component of the bilayer (2222) phase with good O stoichiometry does solve the problem of superconducting fragility encountered in first-generation experiments. We reiterate that our minimal
t
-
t_{\perp}
-
J

The new generation of samples with high structural quality, the increasing development of high-pressure experimental techniques, and further efforts to mimic high physical pressure by the use of substrates or chemical pressure offer a promising near-term future for nickelate superconductivity.

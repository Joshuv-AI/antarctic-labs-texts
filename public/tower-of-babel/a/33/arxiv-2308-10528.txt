Birational classification of toric orbifolds
Johannes Schmitt

Abstract
We give a complete classification of the torus-equivariant birational equivalence classes of smooth proper toric Deligne–Mumford stacks with trivial generic stabilizer in terms of their associated stacky fans.



1
Introduction

An
algebraic orbifold
is a smooth separated irreducible Deligne–Mumford stack of finite type over a field
k
(assumed to be algebraically closed of characteristic zero) with trivial generic stabilizer.
Following
[
KT21
]
we say that such orbifolds
\mathcal{X},\mathcal{X}^{\prime}
are birationally equivalent if there is a third algebraic orbifold
\widehat{\mathcal{X}}
admitting proper, birational and representable morphisms

\mathcal{X}\xleftarrow{f}\widehat{\mathcal{X}}\xrightarrow{g}\mathcal{X}^{\prime}\,.

(1)

In our paper, we consider the torus-equivariant birational classification problem for
smooth proper toric Deligne–Mumford (DM) stacks
with generically trivial stabilizer (see below for the precise definition). This problem was previously studied by Levchenko in
[
Lev22
]
, who considered the case of dimension
2
and obtained some birational invariants. In our paper, we give a full classification in arbitrary dimension.

As a consequence of the above result, we can give a complete classification of the birational equivalence classes of proper toric orbifolds. To state it, we introduce the following notion (which we did not find in this precise shape in the literature).

Definition 3
.

A
conical polyhedral partition
of
N_{\mathbb{R}}
is a finite collection
(C_{i})_{i\in I}
of non-empty subsets
\emptyset\neq C_{i}\subseteq N_{\mathbb{R}}
such that

•

each
C_{i}
is a finite union of full-dimensional rational polyhedral cones,

•

the union of the
C_{i}
is all of
N_{\mathbb{R}}
,

•

the interiors of the
C_{i}
are pairwise disjoint.

We claim that for such a conical polyhedral partition, one can in fact find a fan
\Sigma_{0}
with support
N_{\mathbb{R}}
such that each of the
C_{i}
is a union of cones in
\Sigma_{0}(d)
. Indeed, one way to obtain
\Sigma_{0}
is to subdivide
N_{\mathbb{R}}
at each defining hyperplane of each of the full-dimensional rational polyhedral cones used to cover the sets
C_{i}
. Conversely, given a complete fan
\Sigma_{0}
and a partition
I
of
\Sigma(d)
into non-empty subsets, the sets
C_{i}=\bigcup_{\sigma\in I}\sigma
form a conical polyhedral partition.

Definition 4

A

C_{N^{\prime}}\cap C_{N^{\prime\prime}}\cap N^{\prime}=C_{N^{\prime}}\cap C_{N^{\prime\prime}}\cap N^{\prime\prime}

Given a stacky fan
\mathbf{\Sigma}
with an underlying fan

which is complete, we define

\mathcal{N}=\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{\Sigma})=\{N_{\sigma}:\sigma\in\Sigma(d)\}

as the set of lattices associated to maximal cones of

we denote by

N_{\sigma}=N^{\prime}\end{subarray}}\sigma

the associated union of maximal cones
\sigma

. We claim that

Corollary 5
.

The
T
-equivariant birational equivalence classes of proper toric orbifolds
\mathcal{X}_{\mathbf{\Sigma}}
with torus
T=\mathrm{Spec}(k[N^{\vee}])
are in bijection to sublattice colorings of
N
by sending the class
[\mathcal{X}_{\mathbf{\Sigma}}]
to the sublattice coloring
(C_{N^{\prime}})_{N^{\prime}\in\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{\Sigma})}
above.

Proof.

Theorem
2
implies that two proper toric orbifolds are birationally equivalent if and only if they have the same associated sublattice coloring. This shows that their birational equivalence classes inject into the set of sublattice colorings.

Conversely, given such a sublattice coloring
(C_{N^{\prime}})_{N^{\prime}\in\mathcal{N}}
choose a fan
\Sigma_{0}
such that each
C_{N^{\prime}}
is a union of maximal cones of
\Sigma_{0}
. By performing a barycentric subdivision, we can assume without loss of generality that
\Sigma_{0}
is simplicial. For each cone

such that

containing
\sigma

We would like to construct a stacky fan

whose underlying fan
\Sigma_{0}^{\prime}

\Sigma_{0}

\Sigma_{0}^{\prime}

To obtain

\Sigma_{0}

Remark 6
.

The proofs of Theorem
2
and Corollary
5
featured many combinatorial and convex geometric operations (like the multiplicity reduction above). A more conceptual and geometric approach is possible via the theory of possibly singular toric DM-stacks and their associated
KM fans
in the sense of
[
GM15
]
. Here a (lattice) KM fan is a triple
F=(N,\Sigma,(N_{\sigma})_{\sigma\in\Sigma})
of a rational polyhedral fan
\Sigma
in the lattice
N
together with choices of sublattices
N_{\sigma}\subseteq N
associated to its cones which are finite index in
\mathrm{Span}(\sigma)\cap N
and compatible under face inclusions.
Associated to
F
, the paper
[
GM15
]
defines a toric, but possibly singular DM-stack
\mathcal{X}(F)
. Any stacky fan
\mathbf{\Sigma}
defines a KM fan
F
by taking
N_{\sigma}
to be the lattice spanned by the chosen ray generators
\rho_{\tau}
of
\sigma
as before. The constructed toric DM-stacks
\mathcal{X}(\mathbf{\Sigma})
and
\mathcal{X}(F)
agree and for a KM fan
F
, we have that
\mathcal{X}(F)
is smooth if and only if
F
comes from a stacky fan.

With this formalism in mind, we can sketch a geometric interpretation of the proofs presented above. Indeed, given complete stacky fans
\mathbf{\Sigma},\mathbf{\Sigma}^{\prime}
with compatible lattices on their maximal cones as in Theorem
2
, choose any common refinement
\Sigma^{\prime\prime}
of their underlying fans. Then the cones
\sigma^{\prime\prime}
of
\Sigma^{\prime\prime}
carry natural sublattices

of

induced from the associated lattices of their coarsenings in
\Sigma,\Sigma^{\prime}
(which are compatible by assumption). Denote by
F^{\prime\prime}=(N,\Sigma^{\prime\prime},(N_{\sigma^{\prime\prime}})_{\sigma^{\prime\prime}})
the associated KM fan.
By functoriality, the associated toric DM-stack
\mathcal{X}(F^{\prime\prime})
admits toric maps to
\mathcal{X}_{\mathbf{\Sigma}},\mathcal{X}_{\mathbf{\Sigma}^{\prime}}
which are representable by
[
GM15
, Theorem 3.11.2]
.
Composing these with a toric resolution of singularities
\widehat{\mathcal{X}}\to\mathcal{X}(F^{\prime\prime})
gives the diagram (
2
) showing that
\mathcal{X}_{\mathbf{\Sigma}},\mathcal{X}_{\mathbf{\Sigma}^{\prime}}
are
T
-equivariantly birationally equivalent.

Similarly, in Corollary
5
given a sublattice coloring
(C_{N^{\prime}})_{N^{\prime}}
choose a fan
\Sigma_{0}
such that each

is a union of maximal cones of

Given a cone

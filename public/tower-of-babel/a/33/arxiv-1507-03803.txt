α \alpha -Parameterized Differential Transform Method
K. Aydemir a,, O. Sh. Mukhtarov a,b

Abstract
In this paper we propose a new version of differential transform method (we shall call this method as α \alpha -parameterized differential transform method), which differs from the traditional differential transform method in calculating coefficients of Taylor polynomials. Numerical examples are presented to illustrate the efficiency and reliability of own method. The result reveal that α \alpha -Parameterized differential transform method is a simple and effective numerical algorithm.



1
Introduction

Many problems
in mathematical physics, theoretical physics and chemical physics
are modelled by the so-called initial value and boundary value
problems in the second-order ordinary differential equations. In
most cases, these problems may be too complicated to solve
analytically. Alternatively, the numerical methods can provide
approximate solutions rather than the analytic solutions of
problems. There are various approximation methods for solving a
system of differential equations, e.g. Adomian decomposition method
(ADM), Galerkin method, rationalized Haar functions method, homotopy
perturbation method (HPM), variational iteration method (VIM) and
the differential transform method (DTM).

The DTM is one of the numerical methods which enables to find
approximate solution in case of linear and non-linear systems of
differential equations. The main advantage of this method is that it
can be applied directly to nonlinear ODEs without requiring
linearization. The well known advantage of DTM is its simplicity and
accuracy in calculations and also wide range of applications.
Another important advantage is that this method is capable of
greatly reducing the size of computational work while still
accurately providing the series solution with fast convergence rate.
With this method, it is possible to obtain highly accurate results
or exact solutions for differential equations. The concept of the
differential transform method was first proposed by
[
19
]
, who
solved linear and nonlinear initial value problems in electric
circuit analysis. Afterwards, Chiou and Tzeng
[
18
]
applied the
Taylor transform to solve nonlinear vibration problems, Chen and Ho

[

developed this method to various linear and nonlinear
problems such as two point boundary value problems and Ayaz

[
3
]
applied it to the system of differential equations.
Abbasov et al.
[
1
]
used the method of differential transform
to obtain approximate solutions of the linear and non-linear
equations related to engineering problems and observed that the
numerical results are in good agreement with the analytical
solutions. In recent years many authors has been used this method
for solving various types of equations. For example, this method has
been used for differential-algebraic equations
[

]
, partial
differential equations
[

,

, fractional

In

2
The classical DTM

In this section, we describe the definition and some basic
properties of
the classical differential transform method. An arbitrary analytic function f(x) can
be expanded in Taylor series about a point
x=x_{0}
as

\displaystyle f(x)=\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(x-x_{0})^{k}}{k!}\left[\frac{d^{k}f(x)}{dx^{k}}\right]_{x=x_{0}}

(1)

The classical differential transformation of f(x) is defined as

\displaystyle F(k)=\frac{1}{k!}\left[\frac{d^{k}f(x)}{dx^{k}}\right]_{x=x_{0}}

(2)

Then the inverse differential transform is

\displaystyle f(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(x-x_{0})^{k}F(k).

(3)

The fundamental mathematical operations performed by differential
transform method are listed in following

i) If
f(x)=g(x)\pm h(x)
then
F(k)=G(k)\pm H(k)

ii) If
f(x)=\alpha g(x)
,
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
, then

F(k)=\alpha G(k)

iii) If
f(x)=\frac{d^{m}g}{dx^{m}}
then

F(k)=(k+1)(k+2)...(k+m)G(k+m)

iv) If
f(x)=x^{m}
then
F(k)=\delta(k-m)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{c}1\ \ for\ k=m\\
0\ \ for\ k\neq m\end{array}\right.

v) If
f(x)=g(x)h(x)
then
F(k)=\sum\limits_{m=0}^{k}H(m)G(k-m)

3
\alpha
-Parameterized differential
transform method (
\alpha
-p DTM)

In this section we suggest a new
version of classical differential transform method
by
following.

Let
I=[a,b]\subset\mathbb{R}
be an arbitrary real
interval,
f:I\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is an infinitely differentiable
function (in real applications it is enough to required that
f(x)

is sufficiently large order differentiable function) ,
\alpha\in[0,1]
any real parameter and
N any integer (large enough). Let us introduce the following
notations

\displaystyle D_{a}(f;k):=\frac{f^{(k)}(a)}{k!},\ \ \ D_{b}(f;k):=\frac{f^{(k)}(b)}{k!}

(4)

\displaystyle D(f,\alpha;k):=\alpha D_{a}(f;k)+(1-\alpha)D_{b}(f;k),\ \ \ \ \alpha\in[0,1],\ \ k\in\mathbb{N}

(5)

Definition 1

The sequence

(D_{\alpha}(f)):=(D(f,\alpha;1),D(f,\alpha;2),...)

is called the
\alpha
-P transformation of the original function
f(x). The so-called ,,differential inverse" transform of

D_{\alpha}(f)
we define as

\displaystyle E_{\alpha}(D_{\alpha}(f)):=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}D(f,\alpha;k)(x-x_{\alpha})^{k}

(6)

if the series is convergent, where
x_{\alpha}=\alpha a+(1-\alpha)b
.

The
function
\widetilde{f_{\alpha}}(x)
defined by equality

\widetilde{f_{\alpha}}(x):=E_{\alpha}(D_{\alpha}(f))

we called the
\alpha
-parameterized approximation of the function
f(x).

Remark 1

In the cases of
\alpha=1\ \textrm{and}\ \alpha=0
the
\alpha
-p differential
transform (
5
) reduces to the classical differential
transform (
2
) at the points
x=a\ \textrm{and}\ x=b

respectively. Namely for
\alpha=0\ \textrm{and}\ \alpha=1
the
equality
\widetilde{f_{\alpha}}(x)=f(x)
is hold.

Remark 2

For practical application, instead of
\widetilde{f_{\alpha}}(x)

it is convenient to introduced
N
-term
\alpha
-parameterized
approximation of the function
\widetilde{f_{\alpha}}(x)
which we
shall define as

\displaystyle\widetilde{f}_{\alpha,N}(x):=E_{\alpha,N}(D_{\alpha}(f)):=\sum_{k=0}^{N}D(f,\alpha;k)(x-x_{\alpha})^{k}

(7)

Theorem 1

If f(x) is constant function then

\widetilde{f_{\alpha}}(x)=f(x)
and

\widetilde{f}_{\alpha,N}(x)=f(x)
for each
N
.

Proof 1

The proof is immediate from Definition
1
and
Remark
2
.

Theorem 2

If
f(x)=cg(x)
,
c\in\mathbb{R}
, then

D_{\alpha}(f)=cD_{\alpha}(g)\ \textrm{and}\ \widetilde{f_{\alpha}}(x)=c\widetilde{g_{\alpha}}(x).

Proof 2

By applying the well-known properties of classical DTM we get

\displaystyle D(f,\alpha;k)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\alpha D_{a}(f;k)+(1-\alpha)D_{b}(f;k)

(8)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\alpha cD_{a}(g;k)+(1-\alpha)cD_{b}(g;k)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle c(\alpha D_{a}(g;k)+(1-\alpha)D_{b}(g;k))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle cD(g,\alpha;k)

Consequently
D_{\alpha}(f)=cD_{\alpha}(g)
, from which immediately
follows that
\widetilde{f_{\alpha}}(x)=c\widetilde{g_{\alpha}}(x)
.

Theorem 3

If
f(x)=g(x)\pm h(x)
then
D_{\alpha}(f)=D_{\alpha}(g)\pm D_{\alpha}(h)\ \textrm{and}\ \widetilde{f_{\alpha}}(x)=\widetilde{g_{\alpha}}(x)\pm\widetilde{h}_{\alpha}(x).

Proof 3

By using the definition of transform (
5
)

\displaystyle D(f,\alpha;k)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\alpha D_{a}(f;k)\pm(1-\alpha)D_{b}(f;k)

(9)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\alpha D_{a}(g+h;k)\pm(1-\alpha)D_{b}(g+h;k)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle D(g,\alpha;k)\pm D(h,\alpha;k)

Consequently
D_{\alpha}(f)=D_{\alpha}(g)\pm D_{\alpha}(h)
, from
which immediately follows that
\widetilde{f_{\alpha}}(x)=\widetilde{g}_{\alpha}(x)\pm\widetilde{h}_{\alpha}(x)
.

Theorem 4

Let
f(x)=\frac{d^{m}g}{dx^{m}}
and
m\in\mathbb{N}
. Then

D(f^{(m)},\alpha;k)=\frac{(k+m)!}{k!}D(f,\alpha;k+m)

and

\widetilde{f}_{\alpha}^{(m)}(x)=\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(k+m)!}{k!}D(f,\alpha;k+m)(x-x_{\alpha})^{k}

where
x_{\alpha}=\alpha a+(1-\alpha)b
.

Proof 4

We have from definition (
5
)

\displaystyle D(f^{(m)},\alpha;k)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\alpha D_{a}(f^{(m)};k)+(1-\alpha)D_{b}(f^{(m)};k)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\alpha(k+1)(k+2)...(k+m)D_{a}(f;k+m)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle(1-\alpha)(k+1)(k+2)...(k+m)D_{b}(f;k+m)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(k+1)(k+2)...(k+m)(\alpha D_{a}(f;k)+(1-\alpha)D_{b}(f;k+m))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(k+m)!}{k!}D(f,\alpha;k+m)

Thus we get
D(f^{(m)},\alpha;k)=\frac{(k+m)!}{k!}D(f,\alpha;k+m)
.
Using this we find

\widetilde{f}_{\alpha}^{(m)}(x)=\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(k+m)!}{k!}D(f,\alpha;k+m)(x-x_{\alpha})^{k}
.

Theorem 5

Let
f(x)=x^{m}
,
m\in\mathbb{N}
. Then

D(f,\alpha;k)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{c}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}m\\
k\\
\end{array}\right)(\alpha a^{m-k}+(1-\alpha)b^{m-k})\ \ for\ k<m\\
1\ \ \ \ for\ \ \ \ k=m\\
0\ \ \ \ for\ \ k>m\end{array}\right.

Proof 5

Let
k<m
. By using the definition of the transform
(
5
) we have

\displaystyle D(f,\alpha;k)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\alpha D_{a}(f;k)+(1-\alpha)D_{b}(f;k)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\alpha D_{a}(x^{(m)};k)+(1-\alpha)D_{b}(x^{(m)};k)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{c}m\\
k\\
\end{array}\right)(\alpha a^{(m-k)}+(1-\alpha)b^{(m-k)})

The equalities
\ D(x^{m},\alpha;m)=1
and
\ D(x^{m},\alpha;m+s)=0
for
s\geq 1
is obvious.

Theorem 6

If
f(x)=g(x)h(x)
then
D(f,\alpha;k)=\sum\limits_{m=0}^{k}[\alpha D_{a}(g;m)D_{a}(h;k-m)+(1-\alpha)D_{b}(g;m)D_{b}(h;k-m)]

Proof 6

By using the definition of transform given in Eq.
(
5
) we have

\displaystyle D(f,\alpha;k)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\alpha D_{a}(f;k)+m(1-\alpha)D_{b}(f;k)

(18)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\alpha D_{a}(gh;k)+(1-\alpha)D_{b}(gh;k)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha}{k!}\sum\limits_{m=0}^{k}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}k\\
m\\
\end{array}\right)g^{(m)}(a)h^{(k-m)}(a)+\frac{(1-\alpha)}{k!}\sum\limits_{m=0}^{k}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}k\\
m\\
\end{array}\right)g^{(m)}(b)h^{(k-m)}(b)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{m=0}^{k}[\alpha D_{a}(g;m)D_{a}(h;k-m)+(1-\alpha)D_{b}(g;m)D_{b}(h;k-m)].

5
Analysis of the method

In this study we introduce a new version of classical DTM that will
extend the application of the method to spectral analysis of
boundary-value problems involving eigenvalue parameter, which arise
from problems of mathematical physics. Numerical results reveal that
the
\alpha
-p DTM is a powerful tool for solving many initial value
and boundary value problems. It is concluded that comparing with the
standard DTM, the
\alpha
-p DTM reduces computational cost in
obtaining approximated solutions. This method unlike most numerical
techniques provides a closed-form solution. It may be concluded that

\alpha
-p DTM is very powerful and efficient in finding approximate
for wide classes of boundary value problems. The main advantage of
the method is the fact that it provides its user with an analytical
approximation, in many cases an exact solution, in a rapidly
convergent sequence with elegantly computed terms.

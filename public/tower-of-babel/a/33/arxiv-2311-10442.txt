Convex body domination for a class of multi-scale operators
Aapo Laukkarinen

Abstract
. The technique of sparse domination, i.e., dominating operators with sums of averages taken over sparsely distributed cubes, has seen rapid development recently within the realms of harmonic analysis. A useful extension of sparse domination called convex body domination allows one to estimate operators in matrix-weighted spaces. In this paper, we extend recent sparse domination results for a class of multi-scale operators due to Beltran, Roos and Seeger to the convex body setting and prove that this implies quantitative matrix-weighted norm bounds for these operators and their commutators.

Introduction

A recent technique in harmonic analysis called sparse domination allows one to dominate many complicated operators by sums of averages taken over cubes that have major disjoint subsets. This type of domination, initiated by Lerner
[
16
,
17
]
, is primarily motivated by the sharp quantitative scalar-valued weighted norm inequalities that arise from it. In the vector case sparse domination walks into the problem that ordinary averages lose information about direction, which is often essential when considering matrix-valued weights. This problem can be addressed by replacing the averages with convex sets that naturally enjoy the property of containing information about behaviour in different directions.

This technique of dominating via convex sets is called convex body domination and it was conjured by Nazarov et al. in
[
20
]
in order to extend the sharp weighted
A_{2}
bound for Calderón-Zygmund operators (CZO) by Hytönen
[
11
]
to the vector valued setting, where the weight functions are matrix-valued. While the conjectured linear bound on the dependence on the weight was not achieved, the method in
[
20
]
yielded a result that, at the time of writing, holds the record for most optimal. Consequent convex body domination results include two weight matrix
A_{p}
bounds for CZOs by Cruz-Uribe et al.
[
5
]
, matrix weighted bounds for variational CZOs by Duong et al.
[
8
]
, for commutators of CZOs by Isralowitz et al.
[
13
,
14
]
and for rough singular integral operators by Di Plinio et al.
[
6
]
and Muller and Rivera-Ríos
[
19
]
.

In
[
12
]
Hytönen generalized the notion of convex bodies to an arbitrary normed space
X
. As an application of this general setting one is able to perform convex body domination on the Bochner space
X=L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n},E)
, where
E
is an arbitrary Banach space. In this paper we will use this general framework to extend the sparse domination results of Beltran et al.
[
1
]
to the convex body setting. In particular, we consider a sum of operators

T=\sum_{j=N_{1}}^{N_{2}}T_{j},

where certain
2^{j}
-scalings of the operators
T_{j}
enjoy uniform
L^{p}\to L^{q}
boundedness and they are supported in a
1
-neighbourhood of the input function. The individual rescaled operators and their adjoints are also assumed to satisfy a certain regularity condition. Furthermore, the full sum enjoys uniform
L^{p}\to L^{p,\infty}
and
L^{q,1}\to L^{q}
bounds. Our main result is that a bilinear form related to
T
permits convex body domination. For the exact statement see Theorem
3.3
and the preceding discussion in Section 3. We will also explore the properties of general operators that satisfy convex body domination. In particular, we prove new quantitative matrix-weighted bounds for these operators and their commutators.

The outline of the paper is as follows. In Section 2, we study the general framework for convex bodies from
[
12
]
and equip ourselves with results that provide the foundation of our argument. In Section 3, we introduce the setting for multi-scale operators from
[
1
]
that is also the setting for our main result. This includes the dense subspace that we will work with in the following two sections. In Section 4, we begin the induction proof of the main result by proving the base case for the induction. In other words, we restrict ourselves to the single-scale setting and prove that the single-scale operators satisfy convex body domination. The induction step is provided in Section 5. In Section 6, we study matrix-weighted norm inequalities that arise from convex body domination. In particular, we will show that if
T
satisfies convex body domination, then for
1\leq p<r<q\leq\infty
,
t=\frac{r}{p}
and
s=\left(\frac{q}{r}\right)^{\prime}
we have

\|T\vec{f}\|_{L^{r}_{B_{2}^{n}}(W)}\leq C[W]_{A_{t}}^{\frac{t^{\prime}}{r}+\frac{s}{r^{\prime}}}[W]_{RH_{t,s}}^{\frac{1}{r}+\frac{s}{r^{\prime}}}\|\vec{f}\|_{L^{r}_{B_{1}^{n}}(W)},

where
[W]_{A_{t}}
is the usual matrix-
A_{t}
constant of
W
and
[W]_{RH_{t,s}}
is a natural extension of the reverse Hölder constant for matrix weights (see Definition
6.3
).
Another application is explored in Section 7, where we study the commutators of operators that satisfy convex body domination. More explicitly, we will show that if
T
satisfies convex body domination, then for
1\leq p<r<q\leq\infty
,
t=\frac{r}{p}
and
s=\left(\frac{q}{r}\right)^{\prime}
we have

\displaystyle\|[B,T]\vec{f}

\displaystyle\|_{L^{r}_{B_{2}^{n}}(W)}\leq

\displaystyle C\|B\|_{BMO}\left([W]_{A_{t}}^{s}[W]_{RH_{t,s}}^{s}+[W]_{A_{t}}^{\frac{1}{t-1}}\right)[W]_{A_{t}}^{\frac{t^{\prime}}{r}+\frac{s}{r^{\prime}}}[W]_{RH_{t,s}}^{\frac{1}{r}+\frac{s}{r^{\prime}}}\|\vec{f}\|_{L^{r}_{B_{1}^{n}}(W)}.

In Section 8, we prove that our results in the dense subspace extend to the global setting. Finally, in Section 9, following
[
1
]
, we give some examples of Fourier multiplier operators that enjoy a multi-scale structure and therefore satisfy convex body domination.

2.
General convex bodies

We follow the framework from
[
12
]
. Let
\bar{B}_{X}
be the closed unit ball of a space
X
.

Definition 2.1
.

For a real normed space
X
and
\vec{x}\coloneqq(x_{i})_{i=1}^{n}\in X^{n}
, the convex body
\llangle\vec{x}\rrangle_{X}
of
\vec{x}
is

\llangle\vec{x}\rrangle_{X}\coloneqq\{\langle\vec{x},x^{*}\rangle\,\colon\,x^{*}\in\bar{B}_{X^{*}}\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n},

where
\langle\vec{x},x^{*}\rangle=(\langle x_{i},x^{*}\rangle)_{i=1}^{n}
.

As the name suggests, the set
\llangle x\rrangle_{X}
is convex, symmetric and compact (see Lemma 2.3 from
[
12
]
).
The Minkovski dot product of two sets
A,B\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is defined by

A\cdot B\coloneqq\{a\cdot b\,\colon\,a\in A,b\in B\}.

In particular,

\displaystyle\llangle\vec{x}\rrangle_{X}\cdot\llangle\vec{y}\rrangle_{Y}

\displaystyle=\{\langle\vec{x},x^{*}\rangle\cdot\langle\vec{y},y^{*}\rangle\,\colon\,x^{*}\in\bar{B}_{X^{*}},y^{*}\in\bar{B}_{Y^{*}}\}

\displaystyle=\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\langle x_{i},x^{*}\rangle\langle y_{i},y^{*}\rangle\,\colon\,x^{*}\in\bar{B}_{X^{*}},y^{*}\in\bar{B}_{Y^{*}}\right\}.

Since the sets
\llangle\vec{x}\rrangle_{X}
and
\llangle\vec{y}\rrangle_{Y}
are convex bodies, the above set is a closed interval
[-c,c]
and we can identify it with its right end point
c\in\mathbb{R}
. In particular, for
r,s\in\mathbb{R}
the inequalities
r\leq\llangle\vec{x}\rrangle_{X}\cdot\llangle\vec{y}\rrangle_{Y}\leq s
mean
r\leq c\leq s
.

We extend the bilinear form
t\,\colon\,X\times Y\to\mathbb{R}
to
X^{n}\times Y^{n}
by

t(\vec{x},\vec{y})=\sum_{i=1}^{n}t(\vec{x}\cdot\vec{e}_{i},\vec{y}\cdot\vec{e}_{i}),

where
(\vec{e}_{i})_{i=1}^{n}
is a fixed orthonormal basis of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. In
[
12
]
this definition is shown to be independent of the chosen orthonormal basis and for
\vec{x}\in X^{n}
and
\vec{y}\in Y^{n}
it holds that

(2)

\displaystyle t(A\vec{x},\vec{y})=t(\vec{x},A^{\top}\vec{y}).

We will restate two results from
[
12
]
that will be used later in this text.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
X,Y
be normed spaces and
\vec{f}\in X^{n},\vec{g}\in Y^{n}
. Let
\mathcal{E}_{f}
be the John ellipsoid of
\llangle\vec{f}\rrangle_{X}
such that

\mathcal{E}_{f}\subset\llangle\vec{f}\rrangle_{X}\subset\sqrt{n}\mathcal{E}_{f},

and suppose that
\mathcal{E}_{f}
is non-degenerate (i.e. of full dimension). Let
R_{f}
be the linear transformation such that
R_{f}\mathcal{E}_{f}=\bar{B}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
and let
(\vec{e}_{i})_{i=1}^{n}
be an orthonormal basis of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. If

f_{i}\coloneqq R_{f}\vec{f}\cdot\vec{e}_{i},\quad g_{i}\coloneqq R_{f}^{-\top}\vec{g}\cdot\vec{e}_{i},\quad i=1,\dots,n,

then

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|f_{i}\|_{X}\|g_{i}\|_{Y}\leq n^{\frac{3}{2}}\llangle\vec{f}\rrangle_{X}\cdot\llangle\vec{g}\rrangle_{Y}.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
X,Y
be normed spaces with subspaces
F\subset X
and
G\subset Y
, and let
t\,\colon X\times Y\to\mathbb{R}
be a bilinear form. Consider the following conditions

(1)

For all
(f,g)\in F\times G
, we have

|t(f,g)|\leq C\|f\|_{X}\|g\|_{Y}.

(2)

For all
(f,g)\in F\times G
, we have

|t(\vec{f},\vec{g})|\leq C_{n}\llangle f\rrangle_{X}\cdot\llangle g\rrangle_{Y}.

For each
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
, condition
(1)
implies condition
(2)
with
C_{n}=Cn^{\frac{3}{2}}
.

Dealing with the whole dual space will sometimes turn out to be problematic when we want to calculate with the convex bodies. Fortunately, we can estimate the convex bodies by considering a norming subspace of the dual. To see this we need the so called Helly’s condition.

Proposition 2.4

(Helly’s condition)
.

Let
E
be a normed space. Let
e^{*}_{i}\in E^{*}
,
c_{i}\in\mathbb{K}
,
i=1,\dots,n
, and
M>0
be given. The
following assertions are equivalent.

(1)

For every
\varepsilon>0
there exists
e\in E
such that
\|e\|_{E}\leq M+\varepsilon
and

\langle e,e^{*}_{i}\rangle=c_{i},\quad\forall i=1,\dots,n.

(2)

We have

\Big|\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}c_{i}\Big|\leq M\Big\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}e_{i}^{*}\Big\|_{E^{*}},\quad\forall a_{i}\in\mathbb{K},\,\,i=1,\dots,n.

See, e.g.,
[
10
, Proposition 4.3.1]
for a proof of Helly’s condition.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
Z\subset X^{*}
be norming for
X
. For
\vec{x}\in X^{n}
we have

\llangle\vec{x}\rrangle_{X}=\bigcap_{\varepsilon>0}(1+\varepsilon)\{\langle\vec{x},z\rangle\,\colon\,z\in\bar{B}_{Z}\}.

Proof.

Let
J\colon X\to Z^{*}
be the mapping defined by
\langle z,Jx\rangle=\langle x,z\rangle
for
x\in X
and
z\in Z
. Since
Z
is norming for
X
, the mapping
J
is an isometry. Indeed,

\|Jx\|_{Z^{*}}=\sup_{\|z\|_{Z}\leq 1}\langle z,Jx\rangle=\sup_{\|z\|_{Z}\leq 1}\langle x,z\rangle=\|x\|_{X}.

We will now check that condition
(\mathit{2})
of Proposition
2.4

holds with
E=Z
,
c_{i}=\langle x_{i},x^{*}\rangle
,
e_{i}^{*}=Jx_{i}
and
M=\|x^{*}\|_{X^{*}}
. Since
J
is an isometry, we have

\displaystyle\Big|\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}\langle x_{i},x^{*}\rangle\Big|=\Big|\langle\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}x_{i},x^{*}\rangle\Big|

\displaystyle\leq\|x^{*}\|_{X^{*}}\Big\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}x_{i}\Big\|_{X}

\displaystyle=\|x^{*}\|_{X^{*}}\Big\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}Jx_{i}\Big\|_{Z^{*}}.

Now Helly’s condition gives us that for every
\varepsilon>0
there exists
z_{\varepsilon}\in Z
such that
\|z_{\varepsilon}\|_{Z}\leq\|x^{*}\|_{X^{*}}+\varepsilon
and

\langle x_{i},x^{*}\rangle=\langle z_{\varepsilon},Jx_{i}\rangle=\langle x_{i},z_{\varepsilon}\rangle,\qquad\forall i=1,\dots,n.

Thus we have

\displaystyle\llangle\vec{x}\rrangle_{X}

\displaystyle=\{\langle\vec{x},x^{*}\rangle\,\colon\,\|x^{*}\|_{X^{*}}\leq 1\}

\displaystyle\subset\bigcap_{\varepsilon>0}\{\langle\vec{x},z_{\varepsilon}\rangle\,\colon\,\|z_{\varepsilon}\|_{Z}\leq 1+\varepsilon\}

\displaystyle=\bigcap_{\varepsilon>0}\{\langle\vec{x},z_{\varepsilon}\rangle\,\colon\,\Big\|\frac{z_{\varepsilon}}{1+\varepsilon}\Big\|_{Z}\leq 1\}

\displaystyle=\bigcap_{\varepsilon>0}\{\langle\vec{x},(1+\varepsilon)z_{\varepsilon}\rangle\,\colon\,\|z_{\varepsilon}\|_{Z}\leq 1\}

\displaystyle=\bigcap_{\varepsilon>0}(1+\varepsilon)\{\langle\vec{x},z_{\varepsilon}\rangle\,\colon\,\|z_{\varepsilon}\|_{Z}\leq 1\}.

Conversely, we have

\displaystyle\bigcap_{\varepsilon>0}(1+\varepsilon)\{\langle\vec{x},z_{\varepsilon}\rangle\,\colon\,\|z_{\varepsilon}\|_{Z}\leq 1\}

\displaystyle\subset\bigcap_{\varepsilon>0}(1+\varepsilon)\{\langle\vec{x},x^{*}\rangle\,\colon\,\|x^{*}\|_{X^{*}}\leq 1\}

\displaystyle=\bigcap_{\varepsilon>0}(1+\varepsilon)\llangle\vec{x}\rrangle_{X},

and by the properties of convex bodies we get

\bigcap_{\varepsilon>0}(1+\varepsilon)\llangle\vec{x}\rrangle_{X}=\llangle\vec{x}\rrangle_{X}.

Indeed, if
a\in\bigcap_{\varepsilon>0}(1+\varepsilon)\llangle\vec{x}\rrangle_{X}
, then for any
\varepsilon>0
we have

a_{\varepsilon}\coloneqq\frac{a}{1+\varepsilon}\in\llangle\vec{x}\rrangle_{X}.

The sequence
a_{\varepsilon}
clearly converges to
a
as
\varepsilon\to 0
. On the other hand, by compactness we can extract a subsequence
a_{\varepsilon_{j}}
that converges to some
\tilde{a}\in\llangle\vec{x}\rrangle_{X}
and it follows that
a=\tilde{a}\in\llangle\vec{x}\rrangle_{X}
. The reverse inclusion
\llangle\vec{x}\rrangle_{X}\subset\bigcap_{\varepsilon>0}(1+\varepsilon)\llangle\vec{x}\rrangle_{X}
is clear, since
\llangle\vec{x}\rrangle_{X}
is convex and symmetric.
∎

3.
Setting and the main result

We follow the notation and set-up from
[
1
]
. We denote the collection of dyadic subcubes of a cube
Q
by
\mathscr{D}(Q)
. We will work with a dyadic lattice
\mathscr{D}
that satisfies the following four properties:

(1)

The cubes in
\mathscr{D}
have side length of the form
2^{k}
with
k\in\mathbb{Z}
.

(2)

If
Q\in\mathscr{D}
and
Q^{\prime}\in\mathscr{D}(Q)
, then
Q^{\prime}\in\mathscr{D}
.

(3)

If
Q^{\prime},Q^{\prime\prime}\in\mathscr{D}
, then there exists
Q\in\mathscr{D}
such that
Q^{\prime},Q^{\prime\prime}\in\mathscr{D}(Q)
.

(4)

Every compact set in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
is contained in some cube from
\mathscr{D}
.

We say that a collection of cubes
\mathscr{Q}\subset\mathscr{D}
is
\gamma
-sparse if for every cube
Q\in\mathscr{Q}
there exists a subset
E_{Q}
such that
|E_{Q}|\geq\gamma|Q|
and the subsets
E_{Q}
are pairwise disjoint.

For a Banach space
B
and
p,r\in[1,\infty]
we define the Lorentz space
L^{p,r}_{B}

as the space of strongly measurable
functions
f\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\to B
so that the function
x\mapsto|f(x)|_{B}
is in the scalar
Lorentz space
L^{p,r}
. The Bochner space
L^{p}_{B}
is defined analogously.

We also define
S_{B}
to be the space of all functions of the form

f=\sum_{i=1}^{N}a_{i}\mathbbm{1}_{Q_{i}},

where
a_{i}\in B
and
Q_{i}
are dyadic cubes of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
that are contained in a compact set.
For Banach spaces
B_{1},B_{2}
we consider the
space
\operatorname{Op}_{B_{1},B_{2}}

of linear operators
T
mapping functions in
S_{B_{1}}

to weakly
measurable
B_{2}
-valued functions with the property that
x\mapsto\langle Tf(x),\lambda\rangle
is locally
integrable for any
\lambda\in B_{2}^{*}
.

For a function
f
we define
\operatorname{Dil}_{t}f(x)=f(tx)
and for an operator
T
we define the
dilated operator
\operatorname{Dil}_{t}T
by

\operatorname{Dil}_{t}T=\operatorname{Dil}_{t}\circ\hskip 2.84544ptT\circ\operatorname{Dil}_{t^{-1}}.

There will be six conditions that we assume the multi-scale operator to satisfy.

Definition 3.1
.

We say that the sum
T=\sum_{j}T_{j}
is a BRS operator (named after the authors of
[
1
]
, where this class of operators is studied) if it satisfies the following conditions.

The single scale operators
T_{j}
are in
\operatorname{Op}_{B_{1},B_{2}}
such that the support condition

\operatorname{supp}(\operatorname{Dil}_{2^{j}}T_{j})f\subset\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\,\colon\,\operatorname{dist}(x,\operatorname{supp}f)\leq 1\}

holds for all
f\in S_{B_{1}}
, and they satisfy the bound

\underset{j\in\mathbb{Z}}{\sup}\,||\operatorname{Dil}_{2^{j}}T_{j}||_{L^{p}_{B_{1}}\to L^{q}_{B_{2}}}\leq A_{\circ}(p,q).

Furthermore, the single scale operators and their adjoints satisfy the following regularity conditions

(T3)

\displaystyle\sup_{|h|\leq 1}|h|^{-\kappa}\sup_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\|(\operatorname{Dil}_{2^{j}}T_{j})\circ\Delta_{h}\|_{L^{p}_{B_{1}}\to L^{q}_{B_{2}}}\leq B

and

(T4)

\displaystyle\sup_{|h|\leq 1}|h|^{-\kappa}\sup_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\|(\operatorname{Dil}_{2^{j}}T^{*}_{j})\circ\Delta_{h}\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}_{B_{2}^{*}}\to L^{p^{\prime}}_{B_{1}^{*}}}\leq B,

where
\Delta_{h}f(x)=f(x+h)-f(x)
and
\kappa>0
is a fixed parameter.

Let
N_{1},N_{2}
be integers such that
N_{1}\leq N_{2}
. We consider the sum
\sum_{j=N_{1}}^{N_{2}}T_{j}
and stipulate that it satisfies

(T5)

\displaystyle\sup_{N_{1}\leq N_{2}}\left\|\sum_{j=N_{1}}^{N_{2}}T_{j}\right\|_{L^{p}_{B_{1}}\to L^{p,\infty}_{B_{2}}}\leq A(p)

and

(T6)

\displaystyle\sup_{N_{1}\leq N_{2}}\left\|\sum_{j=N_{1}}^{N_{2}}T_{j}\right\|_{L^{q,1}_{B_{1}}\to L^{q}_{B_{2}}}\leq A(q).

To facilitate the discussion for convex body domination we use the following general definition.

Definition 3.2
.

Suppose that pairs of normed spaces
(X(Q),Y(Q))
are associated to every dyadic cube
Q\in\mathscr{D}
. We say that a bilinear form
t\colon F\times G\to\mathbb{R}
satisfies
(X,Y)
convex body domination, if
F\subset X(Q)
and
G\subset Y(Q)
for every
Q\in\mathscr{D}
, and if there exists a constants
C>0
and
\gamma>0
such that for every
(\vec{f},\vec{g})\in F^{n}\times G^{n}
, there exists a
\gamma
-sparse collection
\mathscr{Q}\subset\mathscr{D}
that satisfies

|t(\vec{f},\vec{g})|\leq C\sum_{Q\in\mathscr{Q}}|Q|\llangle\vec{f}\rrangle_{X(Q)}\cdot\llangle\vec{g}\rrangle_{Y(Q)}.

In addition, we say that a linear operator
T
satisfies
(X,Y)
convex body domination, if the associated bilinear form
t(\vec{f},\vec{g})\coloneqq\langle T\vec{f},\vec{g}\,\rangle
satisfies
(X,Y)
convex body domination.

Our results are in the case
(X,Y)=(\textit{\L}^{p}_{B_{1}},\textit{\L}^{q^{\prime}}_{B_{2}^{*}})
, where

\|f\|_{\textit{\L}^{p}(Q,B_{1})}\coloneqq\left(\fint_{Q}|f(x)|_{B_{1}}^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

and
\|\cdot\|_{\textit{\L}^{q^{\prime}}(Q,B_{2}^{*})}
is defined analogously. We fix an orthonormal basis
(\vec{e}_{i})_{i=1}^{n}
of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and denote
f_{i}\coloneqq\vec{f}\cdot\vec{e}_{i}
. Then the bilinear form is defined by

\langle T\vec{f},\vec{g}\,\rangle\coloneqq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\langle Tf_{i},g_{i}\rangle\coloneqq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\langle Tf_{i}(x),g_{i}(x)\rangle_{(B_{2},B_{2}^{*})}\,\mathrm{d}x.

The main result of this article is the following theorem, which asserts that the multi-scale operator
\sum_{j}T_{j}
satisfies
(\textit{\L}^{p}_{B_{1}},\textit{\L}^{q^{\prime}}_{B_{2}^{*}})
convex body domination.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
T=\sum_{j=N_{1}}^{N_{2}}T_{j}
be a BRS operator and let
N_{1},N_{2}
be integers such that
N_{1}\leq N_{2}
. Let
1<p\leq q<\infty
and
0<\gamma<1
.
For all
\vec{f}\in S_{B_{1}}^{n}
and
\vec{g}\in S_{B_{2}^{*}}^{n}
there exists a
\gamma
-sparse collection of dyadic cubes
\mathscr{Q}
such that

\displaystyle|\langle T\vec{f},\vec{g}\,\rangle|\lesssim_{d,p,q,\gamma,\kappa}\mathcal{C}n^{\frac{3}{2}+\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}\sum_{Q\in\mathscr{Q}}|Q|\,\llangle\vec{f}\rrangle_{\textit{\L}^{p}(Q,B_{1})}\cdot\llangle\vec{g}\rrangle_{\textit{\L}^{q^{\prime}}(Q,B_{2}^{*})},

where
\mathcal{C}=A(p)+A(q)+A_{\circ}(p,q)\log(2+\frac{B}{A_{\circ}(p,q)})
.

The proof will be done by adapting the induction argument of
[
1
]
on the difference
N_{2}-N_{1}
. The base case is covered in Section 4 and the rest of the proof in Section 5.

4.
Single scale estimate

We use the notation
f_{i}\coloneqq\vec{f}\cdot\vec{e}_{i}
throughout this section, where, where
(\vec{e}_{i})_{i=1}^{n}
is a fixed orthonormal basis of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. For this section we will only need conditions
3.1
and
3.1
from Definition
3.1
.
Note that by rescaling
3.1
we get

||T_{j}||_{L^{p}_{B_{1}}\to L^{q}_{B_{2}}}\leq 2^{-jd(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q})}A_{\circ}(p,q).

Indeed, by defining
\tilde{f}\coloneqq\operatorname{Dil}_{2^{j}}f
we get

\|T_{j}f\|_{L^{q}_{B_{2}}}=\|T_{j}(\operatorname{Dil}_{2^{-j}}\tilde{f})\|_{L^{q}_{B_{2}}}

and via change of variables
x=2^{j}y
, we get

\displaystyle\left(\int|T_{j}(\operatorname{Dil}_{2^{-j}}\tilde{f})(x)|_{B_{2}}^{q}\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle=2^{\frac{jd}{q}}\left(\int|(\operatorname{Dil}_{2^{j}}T_{j})\tilde{f}(y)|_{B_{2}}^{q}\,\mathrm{d}y\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{\frac{jd}{q}}A_{\circ}(p,q)\left(\int|\tilde{f}(y)|_{B_{1}}^{p}\,\mathrm{d}y\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle=2^{\frac{jd}{q}}A_{\circ}(p,q)\left(\int|f(2^{j}y)|_{B_{1}}^{p}\,\mathrm{d}y\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Changing variables back leads to (
4
).

The following proposition will serve as the base case in the induction proof of the
(\textit{\L}^{p}_{B_{1}},\textit{\L}^{q^{\prime}}_{B_{2}^{*}})
convex body domination for the sum
\sum_{j}T_{j}
. The conclusion is that the single scale operators
T_{j}
satisfy
(\textit{\L}^{p}_{B_{1}},\textit{\L}^{q^{\prime}}_{B_{2}^{*}})
convex body domination.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
T_{j}
satisfy
3.1
and
3.1
from Definition
3.1
. Then for all
\vec{f}\in S_{B_{1}^{n}}
and
\vec{g}\in S_{(B_{2}^{*})^{n}}
there exists a disjoint family
\mathscr{Q}
of dyadic cubes such that

|\langle T_{j}\vec{f},\vec{g}\,\rangle|\leq 3^{d(\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}-\frac{1}{p^{\prime}})}A_{\circ}(p,q)\,n^{\frac{3}{2}}\sum_{Q\in\mathscr{Q}}|3Q|\,\llangle\vec{f}\rrangle_{\textit{\L}^{p}(3Q,B_{1})}\cdot\llangle\vec{g}\rrangle_{\textit{\L}^{q^{\prime}}(3Q,B_{2}^{*})}.

Proof.

Consider

\displaystyle a\coloneqq\langle T_{j}\vec{f},\vec{g}\,\rangle,\qquad a_{Q}\coloneqq t_{Q}(\vec{f},\vec{g})\coloneqq\Big\langle T_{j}\big[\mathbbm{1}_{Q}\vec{f}\,\,\big],\mathbbm{1}_{3Q}\vec{g}\,\Big\rangle

and

c_{Q}=C_{n}\,|3Q|\,\llangle\vec{f}\rrangle_{\textit{\L}^{p}(3Q,B_{1})}\cdot\llangle\vec{g}\rrangle_{\textit{\L}^{q^{\prime}}(3Q,B_{2}^{*})},

where
Q
is a cube and
C_{n}=3^{d(\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}-\frac{1}{p^{\prime}})}A_{\circ}(p,q)\,n^{\frac{3}{2}}
.
We we will check that there exists a disjoint family of cubes
\mathscr{Q}
such that

(4)

\displaystyle a=\sum_{Q\in\mathscr{Q}}a_{Q}

and for every
Q\in\mathscr{Q}
, we have

(5)

\displaystyle|a_{Q}|\leq c_{Q}.

Indeed, we can choose
\mathscr{Q}
to be a partition of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
consisting of cubes that have side length equal to
2^{j}
.

(
4
)

Note that for
Q\in\mathscr{Q}
the support condition gives that
T_{j}[\mathbbm{1}_{Q}f_{i}]
is supported in
3Q
. Now we can calculate

\displaystyle a

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\langle T_{j}f_{i},g_{i}\rangle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left\langle T_{j}\Big[\sum_{Q\in\mathscr{Q}}\mathbbm{1}_{Q}f_{i}\Big],g_{i}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{Q\in\mathscr{Q}}\langle T_{j}[\mathbbm{1}_{Q}f_{i}],g_{i}\rangle=\sum_{Q\in\mathscr{Q}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\langle T_{j}[\mathbbm{1}_{Q}f_{i}],\mathbbm{1}_{3Q}g_{i}\rangle=\sum_{Q\in\mathscr{Q}}a_{Q}.

Since
f_{i}
is compactly supported, there are no issues of convergence in the above calculation.

(
5
)

Note that the scalar Hölder inequality applied to the function

x\mapsto|T_{j}[f\mathbbm{1}_{Q}](x)|_{B_{2}}|g\mathbbm{1}_{3Q}(x)|_{B_{2}^{*}}

implies that

\displaystyle\langle T_{j}[f\mathbbm{1}_{Q}],g\mathbbm{1}_{3Q}\rangle

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\langle T_{j}[f\mathbbm{1}_{Q}](x),g\mathbbm{1}_{3Q}(x)\rangle_{(B_{2},B_{2}^{*})}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|T_{j}[f\mathbbm{1}_{Q}](x)|_{B_{2}}|g\mathbbm{1}_{3Q}(x)|_{B_{2}^{*}}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq\|T_{j}[f\mathbbm{1}_{Q}]\|_{L^{p}_{B_{2}}}\|g\mathbbm{1}_{3Q}\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}_{B_{2}^{*}}}.

Applying this and (
4
) we get

\displaystyle|t_{Q}(f,g)|

\displaystyle=|\langle T_{j}[f\mathbbm{1}_{Q}],g\mathbbm{1}_{3Q}\rangle|

\displaystyle\leq\|T_{j}[f\mathbbm{1}_{Q}]\|_{L^{q}_{B_{2}}}\|g\mathbbm{1}_{3Q}\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}_{B_{2}^{*}}}

\displaystyle\leq A_{\circ}(p,q)2^{-jd(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q})}\|f\mathbbm{1}_{Q}\|_{L^{p}_{B_{1}}}\|g\mathbbm{1}_{3Q}\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}_{B_{2}^{*}}}

\displaystyle=3^{\frac{d}{q^{\prime}}}A_{\circ}(p,q)\,|Q|\,\|f\|_{\textit{\L}^{p}(Q,B_{1})}\|g\|_{\textit{\L}^{q^{\prime}}(3Q,B_{2}^{*})}

\displaystyle\leq 3^{d\left(\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}-\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}\right)}A_{\circ}(p,q)\,|3Q|\,\|f\|_{\textit{\L}^{p}(3Q,B_{1})}\|g\|_{\textit{\L}^{q^{\prime}}(3Q,B_{2}^{*})},

for all
f\in S_{B_{1}}
and
g\in S_{B_{2}^{*}}
.
This is the first condition from Proposition
2.3
with

t=t_{Q},\quad X=\textit{\L}^{p}(3Q,B_{1}),\quad Y=\textit{\L}^{q^{\prime}}(3Q,B_{2}^{*})

and

C=3^{d\left(\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}-\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}\right)}A_{\circ}(p,q)\,|3Q|.

By Proposition
2.3
this implies that

|a_{Q}|=|t_{Q}(\vec{f},\vec{g})|\leq C_{n}\,|3Q|\,\llangle\vec{f}\rrangle_{\textit{\L}^{p}(3Q,B_{1})}\cdot\llangle\vec{g}\rrangle_{\textit{\L}^{q^{\prime}}(3Q,B_{2}^{*})}=c_{Q}.

Now we can conclude

\displaystyle|\langle T_{j}\vec{f},\vec{g}\,\rangle|=|a|

\displaystyle=|\sum_{Q\in\mathscr{Q}}a_{Q}|\leq\sum_{Q\in\mathscr{Q}}|a_{Q}|\leq\sum_{Q\in\mathscr{Q}}c_{Q}

\displaystyle=C_{n}\sum_{Q\in\mathscr{Q}}|3Q|\,\llangle\vec{f}\rrangle_{\textit{\L}^{p}(3Q,B_{1})}\cdot\llangle\vec{g}\rrangle_{\textit{\L}^{q^{\prime}}(3Q,B_{2}^{*})}

\displaystyle=3^{d(\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}-\frac{1}{p^{\prime}})}A_{\circ}(p,q)\,n^{\frac{3}{2}}\sum_{Q\in\mathscr{Q}}|3Q|\,\llangle\vec{f}\rrangle_{\textit{\L}^{p}(3Q,B_{1})}\cdot\llangle\vec{g}\rrangle_{\textit{\L}^{q^{\prime}}(3Q,B_{2}^{*})}.

∎

5.
Estimating the full sum

In this section we will prove Theorem
3.3
. To facilitate the proof we will need the following definitions.

Definition 5.1
.

Given a dyadic cube
Q_{0}
let

\mathfrak{G}_{Q_{0}}(\vec{f},\vec{g})=\sup\sum_{Q\in\mathfrak{S}}|3Q|\,\llangle\vec{f}\rrangle_{\textit{\L}^{p}(3Q,B_{1})}\cdot\llangle\vec{g}\rrangle_{\textit{\L}^{q^{\prime}}(3Q,B_{2}^{*})},

where the supremum is taken over all
\gamma
-sparse collections
\mathfrak{S}
consisting of cubes in
\mathscr{D}(Q_{0})
.

Definition 5.2
.

For
N\in\mathbb{N}
, let
U(N)
be the smallest constant so that for all families of operators
\{T_{j}\}
satisfying the assumptions
3.1
–
T6
of Definition
3.1
for all pairs
(N_{1},N_{2})
with
N_{2}-N_{1}\leq N
and for all dyadic cubes
Q_{0}
of side length
2^{N_{2}}
, we have

|\langle T\vec{f},\vec{g}\,\rangle|\leq U(N)\mathfrak{G}_{Q_{0}}(\vec{f},\vec{g})

whenever
\vec{f}\in S_{B_{1}^{n}}
with
\operatorname{supp}(\vec{f})\subset Q_{0}^{n}
and
\vec{g}\in S_{(B_{2}^{n})^{*}}
.

Note that, as a consequence of the single scale support condition
3.1
, in the above definition we can assume that
\operatorname{supp}(\vec{g})\subset 3Q_{0}^{n}
.

Remark 5.3
.

By similar arguments as in
[
12
, Proof of Proposition 4.2]
, one can show that when pursuing inequalities that are of the form (
5.2
),
we may assume that the John ellipsoid
\mathcal{E}_{f}
of
\llangle f\rrangle_{\textit{\L}^{p}(3Q_{0},B_{1})}
is non-degenerate.

Indeed, let
\mathcal{O}
denote the orthogonal projection of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
onto the linear span of
\llangle f\rrangle_{\textit{\L}^{p}(3Q_{0},B_{1})}
that we denote by
H
. Now since
(\vec{f},\vec{g})\mapsto\langle T\vec{f},\vec{g}\,\rangle
defines a bilinear form, on the left-hand side we have

\langle T\vec{f},\vec{g}\rangle=\langle T\left[\mathcal{O}^{\top}\mathcal{O}\vec{f}\right],\vec{g}\rangle=\langle T\left[\mathcal{O}\vec{f}\right],\mathcal{O}\vec{g}\rangle.

For the right-hand side we fix a
\gamma
-sparse collection
\mathfrak{S}\subset\mathscr{D}(Q_{0})
and calculate for
Q\in\mathfrak{S}
that

\displaystyle\llangle\vec{f}\rrangle_{\textit{\L}^{p}(3Q,B_{1})}\cdot\llangle\vec{g}\rrangle_{\textit{\L}^{q^{\prime}}(3Q,B_{2}^{*})}

\displaystyle=\llangle\mathcal{O}^{\top}\mathcal{O}\vec{f}\rrangle_{\textit{\L}^{p}(3Q,B_{1})}\cdot\llangle\vec{g}\rrangle_{\textit{\L}^{q^{\prime}}(3Q,B_{2}^{*})}

\displaystyle=\llangle\mathcal{O}\vec{f}\rrangle_{\textit{\L}^{p}(3Q,B_{1})}\cdot\llangle\mathcal{O}\vec{g}\rrangle_{\textit{\L}^{q^{\prime}}(3Q,B_{2}^{*})}.

By summing over
Q\in\mathfrak{S}
and taking supremums over
\gamma
-sparse collections in
\mathscr{D}(Q_{0})
we get

\mathfrak{G}_{Q_{0}}(\vec{f},\vec{g})=\mathfrak{G}_{Q_{0}}(\mathcal{O}\vec{f},\mathcal{O}\vec{g}).

Thus we can assume that the functions
\vec{f},\vec{g}
are in
H
and
\mathcal{E}_{f}
is non-degenerate as a subset of
H
.

Our next goal is to show that

\displaystyle U(N)\lesssim_{d,p,q,\gamma,\kappa}\mathcal{C}n^{\frac{3}{2}+\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}

uniformly in
N\in\mathbb{N}
. The case
n=1
is due to
[
1
]
and we adapt their argument in our setting. The proof will be done by induction on
N
and Proposition
4.1
gives us the base case

U(0)\leq 3^{d(\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}-\frac{1}{p^{\prime}})}A_{\circ}(p,q)\,n^{\frac{3}{2}}.

The inductive step is included in the following lemma. Note that after the proof of the following lemma we are done since the rest of the proof of theorem
3.3
is similar to Section 4.2 from
[
1
]
.

Lemma 5.4
.

There is a constant
c=c_{d,p,q,\gamma,\kappa}
such that for all
N>0
,

U(N)\leq\max\{U(N-1),\,c\,\mathcal{C}n^{\frac{3}{2}+\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}\}.

Proof.

Let
Q_{0}
be a dyadic cube of side length
2^{N_{2}}
and
\mathcal{M}_{p}f=(\mathcal{M}|f|^{p})^{1/p}
, where
\mathcal{M}
is the Hardy-Littlewood maximal operator.
By Remark
5.3
, we may assume that the John ellipsoid
\mathcal{E}_{f}
of the convex body
\llangle\vec{f}\rrangle_{\textit{\L}^{p}(Q_{0},B_{1})}
is non-degenerate and consider the linear transformation
R_{f}
that maps
\mathcal{E}_{f}
to the closed unit ball of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. For the rest of the proof we denote
f_{i}\coloneqq R_{f}\vec{f}\cdot\vec{e}_{i}
and
g_{i}\coloneqq R_{f}^{-\top}\vec{g}\cdot\vec{e}_{i}
. Note that we have

\langle T\vec{f},\vec{g}\,\rangle=\left\langle T\Big[R_{f}^{-1}R_{f}\vec{f}\Big],\vec{g}\right\rangle=\left\langle T\Big[R_{f}\vec{f}\Big],R_{f}^{-\top}\vec{g}\right\rangle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\langle Tf_{i},g_{i}\rangle.

Then we define
\Omega_{i}\coloneqq\Omega_{i}^{(f)}\cup\Omega_{i}^{(g)}
, where

\Omega_{i}^{(f)}\coloneqq\left\{x\in 3Q_{0}\,\colon\,\mathcal{M}_{p}f_{i}(x)>\Big(\frac{100^{d}n}{1-\gamma}\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}}\|f_{i}\|_{\textit{\L}^{p}(Q_{0},B_{1})}\right\}

and

\Omega_{i}^{(g)}\coloneqq\left\{x\in 3Q_{0}\,\colon\,\mathcal{M}_{q^{\prime}}g_{i}(x)>\Big(\frac{100^{d}n}{1-\gamma}\Big)^{\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}\|g_{i}\|_{\textit{\L}^{q^{\prime}}(3Q_{0},B_{2}^{*})}\right\}.

We write
\Omega\coloneqq\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}\Omega_{i}
and note that by the
L^{1}\to L^{1,\infty}
boundedness of
\mathcal{M}
we have

\displaystyle|\Omega|\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}|\Omega_{i}^{(f)}|+|\Omega_{i}^{(g)}|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}5^{d}\frac{1-\gamma}{100^{d}n}(|Q_{0}|+3^{d}|Q_{0}|)

\displaystyle=\frac{3^{d}+1}{20^{d}n}(1-\gamma)|Q_{0}|<(1-\gamma)|Q_{0}|

and by setting
E_{Q_{0}}\coloneqq Q_{0}\setminus\Omega
we have
|E_{Q_{0}}|\geq\gamma|Q_{0}|
.

We perform a Whitney decomposition
\mathcal{W}
of
\Omega
consisting of disjoint dyadic cubes
P
with side length
2^{L(P)}\in 2^{\mathbb{Z}}
, so that

5\operatorname{diam}(P)\leq\operatorname{dist}(P,\Omega^{\complement})\leq 12\operatorname{diam}(P),\quad\text{for all }P\in\mathcal{W}.

We set for
i=1,\dots,n,

\displaystyle b_{i,P}=(f_{i}-\fint_{P}f_{i})\mathbbm{1}_{P},\qquad b_{i}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}P\in\mathcal{W}\\
P\subset Q_{0}\end{subarray}}b_{i,P},

and

\displaystyle h_{i}\coloneqq f_{i}\mathbbm{1}_{\Omega^{\complement}}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}P\in\mathcal{W}\\
P\subset Q_{0}\end{subarray}}\mathbbm{1}_{P}\fint_{P}f_{i}

We were able to take cubes
P
that satisfy
P\subset Q_{0}
, since
\operatorname{supp}(f_{i})\subset Q_{0}
. Indeed, if
P\cap Q_{0}\neq\emptyset
, then
P
is a true subset of
Q_{0}
. Otherwise, we would have
Q_{0}\subset P
as
Q_{0}
and
P
are dyadic, but this leads to

|Q_{0}|\leq|P|\leq|\Omega|<(1-\gamma)|Q_{0}|,

which is a contradiction.
Now we have the Calderon-Zygmund decompositions
f_{i}=h_{i}+b_{i}
and it follows that

\langle T\vec{f},\vec{g}\,\rangle=\langle T\vec{h},\vec{g}\,\rangle+\langle T\vec{b},\vec{g}\,\rangle.

For a dyadic cube
Q
with side length
2^{L(Q)}\in 2^{\mathbb{Z}}
, we define

S_{Q}f\coloneqq\sum_{N_{1}\leq j\leq L(Q)}T_{j}[f\mathbbm{1}_{Q}].

Note that for small enough
P
the function
f_{i}
will be a constant on
P
, which implies that
b_{i,P}=0
.
Thus without issues of convergence we can write

\displaystyle\langle T\vec{b},\vec{g}\,\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\langle Tb_{i},g_{i}\rangle=\sum_{P\in\mathcal{W}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\langle Tb_{i,P},g_{i}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{P\in\mathcal{W}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\bigg(\langle S_{P}f_{i},g_{i}\rangle-\langle S_{P}\Big[\mathbbm{1}_{P}\fint_{P}f_{i}\Big],g_{i}\rangle

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+\langle(T-S_{P})b_{i,P},g_{i}\rangle\bigg)

\displaystyle\eqqcolon I+II+III.

We handle the first term
I
with the lower scale quantity
U(N-1)
. Indeed, since each
P
that contributes a non-zero summand in the first term is a proper subcube of
Q_{0}
, we have
L(P)-N_{1}\leq N-1
and hence by definition

|\langle S_{P}\vec{f},\vec{g}\,\rangle|\leq U(N-1)\mathfrak{G}_{P}(\vec{f}\mathbbm{1}_{P},\vec{g}).

Meaning that, given any
\varepsilon>0
, there exists a
\gamma
-sparse collection
\mathfrak{S}_{P,\varepsilon}\subset\mathscr{D}(P)
, such that

|\langle S_{P}\vec{f},\vec{g}\,\rangle|\leq(U(N-1)+\varepsilon)\sum_{Q\in\mathfrak{S}_{P,\varepsilon}}|3Q|\llangle\vec{f}\rrangle_{\textit{\L}^{p}(3Q,B_{1})}\cdot\llangle\vec{g}\rrangle_{\textit{\L}^{q^{\prime}}(3Q,B_{2}^{*})}.

We combine these collections

\mathfrak{S}^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}\coloneqq\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}P\in\mathcal{W}\\
P\subset Q_{0}\end{subarray}}\mathfrak{S}_{P,\varepsilon},

and get

(7)

\displaystyle|I|

\displaystyle\leq(U(N-1)+\varepsilon)\sum_{Q\in\mathfrak{S}^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}}|3Q|\llangle\vec{f}\rrangle_{\textit{\L}^{p}(3Q,B_{1})}\cdot\llangle\vec{g}\rrangle_{\textit{\L}^{q^{\prime}}(3Q,B_{2}^{*})}.

Note that

\displaystyle|\langle T\vec{h},\vec{g}\,\rangle+II+III|=|\langle T\vec{f},\vec{g}\,\rangle-I|

\displaystyle=|\langle T\vec{f},\vec{g}\,\rangle-\sum_{P\in\mathcal{W}}\langle S_{P}\vec{f},\vec{g}\,\rangle|.

Claim:
For all
i=1,\dots,n
we have

\displaystyle|\langle Tf_{i},g_{i}\rangle-\sum_{P\in\mathcal{W}}

\displaystyle\langle S_{P}f_{i},g_{i}\rangle|

(8)

\displaystyle\lesssim_{d,p,q,\gamma,\kappa}\mathcal{C}n^{\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}\,|3Q_{0}|\,\|f_{i}\|_{\textit{\L}^{p}(3Q_{0},B_{1})}\|g_{i}\|_{\textit{\L}^{q^{\prime}}(3Q_{0},B_{2}^{*})}.

Taking the above claim for granted and applying Lemma
2.2
, we obtain

\displaystyle|\langle T\vec{f},\vec{g}\,\rangle-\sum_{P\in\mathcal{W}}\langle S_{P}\vec{f},

\displaystyle\vec{g}\,\rangle|\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}|\langle Tf_{i},g_{i}\rangle-\sum_{P\in\mathcal{W}}\langle S_{P}f_{i},g_{i}\rangle|

\displaystyle\lesssim_{d,p,q,\gamma,\kappa}\mathcal{C}n^{\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}\,|3Q_{0}|\,\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|f_{i}\|_{\textit{\L}^{p}(3Q_{0},B_{1})}\|g_{i}\|_{\textit{\L}^{q^{\prime}}(3Q_{0},B_{2}^{*})}

(9)

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{C}n^{\frac{3}{2}+\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}\,|3Q_{0}|\,\llangle\vec{f}\rrangle_{\textit{\L}^{p}(3Q_{0},B_{1})}\cdot\llangle\vec{g}\rrangle_{\textit{\L}^{q^{\prime}}(3Q_{0},B_{2}^{*})}.

Applying (
7
) and (
9
) we see that with the collection

\mathfrak{S}_{\varepsilon}\coloneqq\{Q_{0}\}\cup\mathfrak{S}^{\prime}_{\varepsilon},

which is a
\gamma
-sparse collection of cubes in
\mathscr{D}(Q_{0})
, we may estimate

\displaystyle|\langle T\vec{f},\vec{g}\rangle|

\displaystyle\leq|I|+|\langle T\vec{f},\vec{g}\rangle-I|

\displaystyle\leq\max\{U(N-1)+\varepsilon,\mathcal{C}n^{\frac{3}{2}+\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}\}\sum_{Q\in\mathfrak{S}_{\varepsilon}}|3Q|\llangle\vec{f}\rrangle_{\textit{\L}^{p}(3Q,B_{1})}\cdot\llangle\vec{g}\rrangle_{\textit{\L}^{q^{\prime}}(3Q,B_{2}^{*})}

\displaystyle\leq\max\{U(N-1)+\varepsilon,\mathcal{C}n^{\frac{3}{2}+\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}\}\,\mathfrak{G}_{Q_{0}}(\vec{f},\vec{g})

and letting

concludes the proof.
∎

6.
Matrix
A_{p}
weights and norm inequalities

A matrix weight is a locally integrable function
W\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
that is almost everywhere
positive definite -valued. For a Banach space
B
the space

L^{r}_{B^{n}}(W)
consists of all measurable
\vec{f}\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\to B^{n}
such that
W^{\frac{1}{r}}\vec{f}\in L^{r}_{B^{n}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
and
\|\vec{f}\|_{L^{r}_{B^{n}}(W)}\coloneqq\|W^{\frac{1}{r}}\vec{f}\|_{L^{r}_{B^{n}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}
. For a matrix weight
W\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
and
1<r<\infty
, we
use the definition introduced in
[
22
]
, i.e.,

[W]_{A_{r}}\coloneqq\sup\limits_{Q}\fint_{Q}\left(\fint_{Q}\Big|W^{\frac{1}{r}}(x)W^{-\frac{1}{r}}(y)\Big|^{r^{\prime}}_{op}\,\mathrm{d}y\right)^{\frac{r}{r^{\prime}}}\,\mathrm{d}x

and say that
W\in A_{r}
if
[W]_{A_{r}}<\infty
. It is known that with
\Sigma=W^{-\frac{r^{\prime}}{r}}
we have

[W]_{A_{r}}^{\frac{1}{r}}\eqsim_{n,r}[\Sigma]_{A_{r^{\prime}}}^{\frac{1}{r^{\prime}}}.

See
[
7
, proof of Lemma 3.2]
for more details on the above fact.
For
y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
we also define the associated scalar weights

W^{Sc}_{r,y}(x)\coloneqq\left|W^{\frac{1}{r}}(x)y\right|^{r}.

In
[
9
, Corollary 2.2]
it was shown that for any
y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
the scalar weight
W^{Sc}_{r,y}
is in
A_{r}
and the weight constant satisfies

[W]^{Sc}_{A_{r}}\coloneqq\sup_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}[W^{Sc}_{r,y}]_{A_{r}}\lesssim_{n,r}[W]_{A_{r}}.

As an application of the John ellipsoid theorem, one can reduce a norm
\rho
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
to an ellipsoid. More explicitly, there exists a self-adjoint matrix
A
such that
\rho(x)\eqsim_{n}|Ax|
for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Such a matrix
A
is called the reducing matrix (or operator) of
\rho
. We will make great use of the reducing matrices
A_{Q,r}
and
B_{Q,r}
that give

|A_{Q,r}x|\eqsim_{n}\left(\fint_{Q}\left|W^{\frac{1}{r}}(y)x\right|^{r}\,\mathrm{d}y\right)^{\frac{1}{r}}

and

|B_{Q,r}x|\eqsim_{n}\left(\fint_{Q}\left|W^{-\frac{1}{r}}(y)x\right|^{r^{\prime}}\,\mathrm{d}y\right)^{\frac{1}{r^{\prime}}},

for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. In
[
22
, proof of Lemma 1.3]
it is shown that

|A_{Q,r}B_{Q,r}|_{op}\eqsim_{n,r}[W]_{A_{r}}^{\frac{1}{r}}.

The following lemma is essentially Proposition 2.4 from
[
9
]
.

Lemma 6.1
.

Assume that
W\in A_{r}
and let
\Sigma=W^{-\frac{r^{\prime}}{r}}
. Let
A_{Q}\coloneqq A_{Q,r}

and
B_{Q}\coloneqq B_{Q,r}
.
Then with
\delta=\frac{1}{2^{d+1}[W]^{Sc}_{A_{r}}-1}
and
\sigma=\frac{1}{2^{d+1}[\Sigma]^{Sc}_{A_{r^{\prime}}}-1}
we have

\sup_{Q}\left(\fint_{Q}\left|A_{Q}^{-1}W^{\frac{1}{r}}(x)\right|_{op}^{r(1+\delta)}\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{r(1+\delta)}}\lesssim_{n}1

and

\sup_{Q}\left(\fint_{Q}\left|B_{Q}^{-1}W^{-\frac{1}{r}}(x)\right|_{op}^{r^{\prime}(1+\sigma)}\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{r^{\prime}(1+\sigma)}}\lesssim_{n}1.

The following classical inequality on operator norm of powers of matrices will turn out to be helpful.

Lemma 6.2

(Cordes inequality,
[
4
]
)
.

Let
A
and
B
be self-adjoint positive definite matrices and
0<\alpha<1
. Then

\left|A^{\alpha}B^{\alpha}\right|_{op}\leq|AB|_{op}^{\alpha}.

We will also need the following reverse Hölder class.

Definition 6.3
.

A matrix weight
W
is a
RH_{t,s}
weight, if

[W]_{RH_{t,s}}\coloneqq\sup_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\sup_{Q}\left(\fint_{Q}\left|W^{\frac{1}{t}}(x)y\right|^{ts}\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{s}}\left(\fint_{Q}\left|W^{\frac{1}{t}}(x)y\right|^{t}\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{-1}<\infty.

Another way of formulating the above definition is to say that
W\in RH_{t,s}
if
W^{Sc}_{t,y}
is in the scalar reverse Hölder class
RH_{s}
uniformly in
y
. This motivates the following lemma.

Lemma 6.4
.

Let
w
be a scalar weight and
s>1
. Then

(10)

\displaystyle w\in RH_{s}\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad w^{s}\in A_{\infty}.

Furthermore,

(11)

\displaystyle\frac{[w^{s}]_{A_{\infty}}^{\frac{1}{s}}}{[w]_{A_{\infty}}}\leq[w]_{RH_{s}}\leq[w^{s}]_{A_{\infty}}^{\frac{1}{s}}.

See
[
23
, Corollary 6.2]
for the proof of (
10
) and
[
15
, Lemma 3.1]
for the proof of (
11
).

We also highlight an elementary inequality for positive numbers, which will make some computations more presentable.

Lemma 6.5
.

Let
\alpha,\beta\in\left[1,\infty\right[
,
\eta\in\left]0,1\right[
and
\lambda=\alpha(\beta^{\prime}(1+\eta))^{\prime}
. Then

\left(\frac{1}{\eta}\right)^{\frac{1}{\lambda}}\lesssim\left(\frac{1}{\eta}\right)^{\frac{1}{\alpha\beta}}.

Proof.

We simply write

\lambda=\frac{\alpha\beta(1+\eta)}{1+\beta\eta}

and thus

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{\eta}\right)^{\frac{1}{\lambda}}=\left(\left[\frac{1}{\eta}\right]^{\frac{1}{\alpha\beta}}\right)^{\frac{1+\beta\eta}{1+\eta}}

\displaystyle=\left(\left[\frac{1}{\eta}\right]^{\frac{1}{\alpha\beta}}\right)^{\frac{1}{1+\eta}}\left(\left[\frac{1}{\eta}\right]^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}\right)^{\frac{\eta}{1+\eta}}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{1}{\eta}\right)^{\frac{1}{\alpha\beta}}\left(\frac{1}{\eta}\right)^{\eta}

\displaystyle\leq e^{\frac{1}{e}}\left(\frac{1}{\eta}\right)^{\frac{1}{\alpha\beta}}.

∎

The goal now is to make use of convex body domination to get a matrix weighted norm inequality. For the sake of maximal applicability we simply assume that
T
satisfies the convex body domination inequality

|\langle T\vec{f},\vec{g}\rangle|\leq C_{1}\sum_{Q\in\mathscr{Q}}|Q|\,\llangle\vec{f}\rrangle_{\textit{\L}^{p}(Q,B_{1})}\cdot\llangle\vec{g}\rrangle_{\textit{\L}^{q^{\prime}}(Q,B_{2}^{*})},

where
C_{1}
is a constant independent of
\vec{f}
and
\vec{g}
and
\mathscr{Q}
is a
\gamma
-sparse collection of cubes possibly depending on
\vec{f}
and
\vec{g}
. Then
we want to find a new constant
C=C(C_{1},W)
such that

\|T\vec{f}\|_{L_{B_{2}^{n}}^{r}(W)}\leq C\|\vec{f}\|_{L_{B_{1}^{n}}^{r}(W)}.

Note that for a Banach space
B
and a measure space
S
the Bochner space
L^{p^{\prime}}_{B^{*}}(S)\subset(L^{p}_{B}(S))^{*}
is norming for
L^{p}_{B}(S)
(see
[
10
]
Proposition 1.3.1). Thus the above inequality is equivalent to

\langle W^{\frac{1}{r}}T[W^{-\frac{1}{r}}\vec{f}\,],\vec{g}\,\rangle\leq C\|\vec{f}\|_{L^{r}_{B_{1}^{n}}}\|\vec{g}\|_{L^{r^{\prime}}_{(B^{*}_{2})^{n}}}.

Directly from the assumed convex body domination, we get

\displaystyle\langle W^{\frac{1}{r}}T[W^{-\frac{1}{r}}\vec{f}\,],\vec{g}\,\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle T[W^{-\frac{1}{r}}\vec{f}\,],W^{\frac{1}{r}}\vec{g}\,\rangle

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}\sum_{Q\in\mathscr{Q}}|Q|\llangle W^{-\frac{1}{r}}\vec{f}\rrangle_{\textit{\L}^{p}(Q,B_{1})}\cdot\llangle W^{\frac{1}{r}}\vec{g}\rrangle_{\textit{\L}^{q^{\prime}}(Q,B_{2}^{*})}.

We estimate the convex bodies in the following lemma.

Lemma 6.6
.

Let
W
be a matrix weight and
p,r,q^{\prime}\in\left[1,\infty\right[
. For
\vec{f}\in\textit{\L}^{p}_{B_{1}^{n}}
and
\vec{g}\in\textit{\L}^{q^{\prime}}_{(B_{2}^{*})^{n}}
we have

\displaystyle\llangle W^{-\frac{1}{r}}\vec{f}\rrangle_{\textit{\L}^{p}(Q,B_{1})}

\displaystyle\cdot\llangle W^{\frac{1}{r}}\vec{g}\rrangle_{\textit{\L}^{q^{\prime}}(Q,B_{2}^{*})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\bigg(\fint_{Q}|\vec{g}(y)|^{q^{\prime}}_{(B_{2}^{*})^{n}}\Big[\fint_{Q}\left|W^{-\frac{1}{r}}(x)W^{\frac{1}{r}}(y)\right|_{op}^{p}|\vec{f}(x)|^{p}_{B_{1}^{n}}\,\mathrm{d}x\Big]^{\frac{q^{\prime}}{p}}\,\mathrm{d}y\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}.

Proof.

In the light of Lemma
2.5
, for every
\varepsilon>0
it holds that

\displaystyle(1+\varepsilon

\displaystyle)^{-2}\,\llangle W^{-\frac{1}{r}}\vec{f}\rrangle_{\textit{\L}^{p}(Q,B_{1})}\cdot\llangle W^{\frac{1}{r}}\vec{g}\rrangle_{\textit{\L}^{q^{\prime}}(Q,B_{2}^{*})}\leq

\displaystyle\quad\Big|\fint_{Q}W^{-\frac{1}{r}}(x)\langle\vec{f}(x),\phi(x)\rangle_{(B_{1},B_{1}^{*})}\,\mathrm{d}x\cdot\fint_{Q}W^{\frac{1}{r}}(y)\langle\vec{g}(y),\psi(y)\rangle_{(B_{2}^{*},B_{2}^{**})}\,\mathrm{d}y\Big|,

for some
\phi\in\bar{B}_{\textit{\L}^{p^{\prime}}_{B_{1}^{*}}(Q)}
and
\psi\in\bar{B}_{\textit{\L}^{q}_{B_{2}^{**}(Q)}}
. Furthermore,

\displaystyle\left|\fint_{Q}W^{-\frac{1}{r}}(x)\langle\vec{f}(x),\phi(x)\rangle_{(B_{1},B_{1}^{*})}\,\mathrm{d}x\cdot\fint_{Q}W^{\frac{1}{r}}(y)\langle\vec{g}(y),\psi(y)\rangle_{(B_{2}^{*},B_{2}^{**})}\,\mathrm{d}y\right|

\displaystyle\quad=\left|\fint_{Q}\fint_{Q}W^{\frac{1}{r}}(y)W^{-\frac{1}{r}}(x)\langle\vec{f}(x),\phi(x)\rangle_{(B_{1},B_{1}^{*})}\cdot\langle\vec{g}(y),\psi(y)\rangle_{(B_{2}^{*},B_{2}^{**})}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y\right|

\displaystyle\quad\leq\fint_{Q}\fint_{Q}\left|W^{-\frac{1}{r}}(x)W^{\frac{1}{r}}(y)\right|_{op}|\vec{f}(x)|_{B_{1}^{n}}|\phi(x)|_{B_{1}^{*}}|\vec{g}(y)|_{(B_{2}^{*})^{n}}|\psi(y)|_{B_{2}^{**}}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y.

Applying Hölder’s inequality twice we see that the last quantity is dominated by

\displaystyle\bigg(\fint_{Q}|\vec{g}(y)|^{q^{\prime}}_{(B_{2}^{*})^{n}}\Big[\fint_{Q}\left|W^{-\frac{1}{r}}(x)W^{\frac{1}{r}}(y)\right|_{op}^{p}|\vec{f}(x)|^{p}_{B_{1}^{n}}\,\mathrm{d}x\Big]^{\frac{q^{\prime}}{p}}\,\mathrm{d}y\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}.

Combining the estimates and letting
\varepsilon\to 0
concludes the proof.
∎

As a warm up we cover the endpoint case first.

Case
\bm{p=1}
,
\bm{q=\infty}
:
We choose
p=1
,
q=\infty
and
r=2
. In this case Lemma
6.6
yields

\displaystyle\sum_{Q\in\mathscr{Q}}|Q|\llangle W^{-\frac{1}{2}}\vec{f}\rrangle_{\textit{\L}^{1}(Q,B_{1})}\cdot\llangle W^{\frac{1}{2}}\vec{g}\rrangle_{\textit{\L}^{1}(Q,B_{2}^{*})}\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\tilde{L}(|\vec{f}|_{B^{n}_{1}})(y)|\vec{g}(y)|_{(B_{2}^{*})^{n}}\,\mathrm{d}y,

where

\tilde{L}f(y)\coloneqq\sum_{Q\in\mathscr{Q}}\mathbbm{1}_{Q}(y)\fint_{Q}\left|W^{-\frac{1}{2}}(x)W^{\frac{1}{2}}(y)\right|_{op}|f(x)|\,\mathrm{d}x

for a scalar valued function
f
and
\tilde{L}(|\vec{f}|_{B^{n}_{1}})(y)\coloneqq\tilde{L}(x\mapsto|\vec{f}(x)|_{B^{n}_{1}})(y)
.
In
[
20
, Lemma 5.6]
it was shown that
\tilde{L}
is bounded in
L^{2}
with

\|\tilde{L}\|_{L^{2}\to L^{2}}\lesssim_{d,n,\gamma}[W]_{A_{2}}^{\frac{3}{2}}.

Thus we get a matrix weighted
A_{2}
norm inequality

\displaystyle\|T\vec{f}\|_{L^{2}_{B_{2}^{n}}(W)}\lesssim_{d,n,\gamma}C_{1}\,[W]_{A_{2}}^{\frac{3}{2}}\|\vec{f}\|_{L^{2}_{B_{1}^{n}}(W)}.

The extrapolation result of
[
3
]
now gives that for all
1<r<\infty
, we have

\|T\vec{f}\|_{L^{r}_{B_{2}^{n}}(W)}\lesssim_{d,n,r,\gamma}C_{1}\,[W]_{A_{r}}^{\frac{3}{2}\max\{1,\frac{r^{\prime}}{r}\}}\|\vec{f}\|_{L^{r}_{B_{1}^{n}}(W)}.

If we want to study the whole range
1<r<\infty
without extrapolation arguments, then the weighted norm inequality is reduced to studying the
L^{r}
-boundedness of the operator
\tilde{L}_{r}
defined by

\tilde{L}_{r}f(y)\coloneqq\sum_{Q\in\mathscr{Q}}\mathbbm{1}_{Q}(y)\fint_{Q}\left|W^{-\frac{1}{r}}(x)W^{\frac{1}{r}}(y)\right|_{op}|f(x)|\,\mathrm{d}x.

We study the boundedness of the above operator in the following proposition.

Proposition 6.7
.

Let
1<r<\infty
. Then the operator
\tilde{L}_{r}
is bounded on
L^{r}
with

\|\tilde{L}_{r}\|_{L^{r}\to L^{r}}\lesssim_{d,n,r,\gamma}[W]_{A_{r}}^{1+\frac{1}{r-1}-\frac{1}{r}}

Remark 6.8
.

The exponent
1+\frac{1}{r-1}-\frac{1}{r}
can also be found in
[
5
, Corollary 1.16]
and
[
8
, Theorem 1.5]
. Our proof has the same idea, but it is more elementary in the sense that we do not use any Orlicz space theory.

Proof.

Let
A_{Q}
and
B_{Q}
be as in Lemma
6.1
.
Since
|A_{Q}B_{Q}|_{op}\eqsim_{n,r}[W]_{A_{r}}^{\frac{1}{r}}
we get

\displaystyle\left|W^{-\frac{1}{r}}(x)W^{\frac{1}{r}}(y)\right|_{op}\lesssim_{n,r}[W]_{A_{r}}^{\frac{1}{r}}\left|A_{Q}^{-1}W^{\frac{1}{r}}(y)\right|_{op}\left|B_{Q}^{-1}W^{-\frac{1}{r}}(x)\right|_{op}

and hence

\displaystyle\langle L_{r}f,g\rangle_{(L^{r},L^{r^{\prime}})}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{Q\in\mathscr{Q}}|Q|\fint_{Q}\fint_{Q}\left|W^{-\frac{1}{r}}(x)W^{\frac{1}{r}}(y)\right|_{op}|f(x)|\,\mathrm{d}x\,|g(y)|\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\lesssim_{n,r}[W]_{A_{r}}^{\frac{1}{r}}\sum_{Q\in\mathscr{Q}}|Q|\fint_{Q}\left|B_{Q}^{-1}W^{-\frac{1}{r}}(x)\right|_{op}|f(x)|\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\fint_{Q}\left|A_{Q}^{-1}W^{\frac{1}{r}}(y)\right|_{op}|g(y)|\,\mathrm{d}y.

By Hölder’s inequality we have

\sum_{Q\in\mathscr{Q}}|Q|\fint_{Q}\left|B_{Q}^{-1}W^{-\frac{1}{r}}(x)\right|_{op}|f(x)|\,\mathrm{d}x\,\fint_{Q}\left|A_{Q}^{-1}W^{\frac{1}{r}}(y)\right|_{op}|g(y)|\,\mathrm{d}y\leq S_{f}\,S_{g},

where we denoted

S_{f}\coloneqq\left(\sum_{Q\in\mathscr{Q}}|Q|\left[\fint_{Q}\left|B^{-1}_{Q}W^{-\frac{1}{r}}(x)\right|_{op}|f(x)|\,\mathrm{d}x\right]^{r}\right)^{\frac{1}{r}}

and

S_{g}\coloneqq\left(\sum_{Q\in\mathscr{Q}}|Q|\left[\fint_{Q}\left|A_{Q}^{-1}W^{\frac{1}{r}}(x)\right|_{op}|g(x)|\,\mathrm{d}x\right]^{r^{\prime}}\right)^{\frac{1}{r^{\prime}}}

To estimate
S_{f}
, we use Hölder’s inequality to get

\displaystyle\fint_{Q}\left|B_{Q}^{-1}W^{-\frac{1}{r}}(x)\right|_{op}

\displaystyle|f(x)|\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\fint_{Q}\left|B_{Q}^{-1}W^{-\frac{1}{r}}(x)\right|_{op}^{r^{\prime}(1+\sigma)}\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{r^{\prime}(1+\sigma)}}\left(\fint_{Q}|f(x)|^{a}\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{a}},

where
\sigma\coloneqq\frac{1}{2^{d+1}[\Sigma]^{Sc}_{A_{r}^{\prime}}-1}
and
a\coloneqq(r^{\prime}(1+\sigma))^{\prime}<r
.
By Lemma
6.1
we have that

\left(\fint_{Q}\left|B_{Q}^{-1}W^{\frac{1}{r}}(x)\right|_{op}^{r^{\prime}(1+\sigma)}\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{r^{\prime}(1+\sigma)}}\lesssim_{n}1

Thus applying the sparseness of the cubes we get

\displaystyle S_{f}\lesssim_{n}\left(\sum_{Q\in\mathscr{Q}}|Q|\left[\fint_{Q}|f(x)|^{a}\,\mathrm{d}x\right]^{\frac{r}{a}}\right)^{\frac{1}{r}}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sum_{Q\in\mathscr{Q}}\gamma^{-1}\int_{E_{Q}}\left[\mathcal{M}_{a}^{\mathscr{D}}f(y)\right]^{r}\,\mathrm{d}y\right)^{\frac{1}{r}}

\displaystyle\leq\gamma^{-\frac{1}{r}}\|\mathcal{M}^{\mathscr{D}}_{a}f\|_{L^{r}},

where
\mathcal{M}^{\mathscr{D}}_{a}f=(\mathcal{M}^{\mathscr{D}}|f|^{a})^{\frac{1}{a}}
with
\mathcal{M}^{\mathscr{D}}
being the dyadic Hardy-Littlewood maximal operator.
Similarly, with
\delta\coloneqq\frac{1}{2^{d+1}[W]^{Sc}_{A_{r}}-1}
we have

S_{g}\lesssim_{n,\gamma}\|\mathcal{M}^{\mathscr{D}}_{b}g\|_{L^{r^{\prime}}},

where
b\coloneqq(r(1+\delta))^{\prime}<r^{\prime}
.

Combining everything together we get

\displaystyle\langle L_{r}f,g\rangle_{(L^{r},L^{r^{\prime}})}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{n,r,\gamma}[W]_{A_{r}}^{\frac{1}{r}}\|\mathcal{M}^{\mathscr{D}}_{a}f\|_{L^{r}}\|\mathcal{M}^{\mathscr{D}}_{b}g\|_{L^{r^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\leq[W]_{A_{r}}^{\frac{1}{r}}\left(\frac{r}{r-a}\right)^{\frac{1}{a}}\left(\frac{r^{\prime}}{r^{\prime}-b}\right)^{\frac{1}{b}}\|f\|_{L^{r}}\|g\|_{L^{r^{\prime}}}.

For
y
, and
x=y(1+\delta)
we have

y^{\prime}-x^{\prime}=\frac{y\delta}{(y-1)(y\delta+y-1)}.

Applying this with
y=r
yields that
r^{\prime}-b\gtrsim_{r}\delta
and similarly
r-a\gtrsim_{r}\sigma
. We may then apply Lemma
6.5
twice, first with
\alpha=1
,
\beta=r
and
\eta=\sigma
and then with
\alpha=1

\beta=r^{\prime}
and
\eta=\delta
, to get

\left(\frac{r}{r-a}\right)^{\frac{1}{a}}\left(\frac{r^{\prime}}{r^{\prime}-b}\right)^{\frac{1}{b}}\lesssim_{r}\left(\frac{1}{\sigma}\right)^{\frac{1}{a}}\left(\frac{1}{\delta}\right)^{\frac{1}{b}}\lesssim\left(\frac{1}{\sigma}\right)^{\frac{1}{r}}\left(\frac{1}{\delta}\right)^{\frac{1}{r^{\prime}}}.

Then we are done once we note that

\left(\frac{1}{\sigma}\right)^{\frac{1}{r}}\left(\frac{1}{\delta}\right)^{\frac{1}{r^{\prime}}}\lesssim_{d,n,r}[\Sigma]_{A_{r^{\prime}}}^{\frac{1}{r}}[W]_{A_{r}}^{\frac{1}{r^{\prime}}}\eqsim_{n,r}[W]_{A_{r}}^{1+\frac{1}{r-1}-\frac{2}{r}}.

∎

Now it follows that if
1<r<\infty
and
W\in A_{r}
, then we have

\|T\vec{f}\|_{L^{r}_{B_{2}^{n}}(W)}\lesssim_{d,n,r,\gamma}C_{1}\,[W]_{A_{r}}^{1+\frac{1}{r-1}-\frac{1}{r}}\|\vec{f}\|_{L^{r}_{B_{1}^{n}}(W)}.

We note that the exponent of
[W]_{A_{r}}
in the above estimate is better than the one in (
6
) that arose from the extrapolation argument. As we have already discussed, similar results have been achieved in
[
5
,
8
]
. The novelty in the above result comes from the fact that we consider Banach space -valued functions and we are dominating a bilinear form. In the scalar case it is known, see
[
21
, proof of Theorem 1]
, that one has the sharp weight exponent
\max\{1,\frac{1}{r-1}\}
.

Case
\bm{p>1}
,
\bm{q<\infty}
:

Recall that in this case Lemma
6.6
gives

\displaystyle\langle W^{\frac{1}{r}}T[W^{-\frac{1}{r}}\vec{f}\,],\vec{g}\,\rangle

\displaystyle\qquad\leq C_{1}\sum_{Q\in\mathscr{Q}}|Q|\bigg(\fint_{Q}|\vec{g}(y)|^{q^{\prime}}_{(B_{2}^{*})^{n}}\Big[\fint_{Q}\left|W^{-\frac{1}{r}}(x)W^{\frac{1}{r}}(y)\right|_{op}^{p}|\vec{f}(x)|^{p}_{B_{1}^{n}}\,\mathrm{d}x\Big]^{\frac{q^{\prime}}{p}}\,\mathrm{d}y\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}.

Let
t\coloneqq\frac{r}{p}
. We assume that
p<r
and
W\in A_{t}
.
The term inside the sum can be dominated with

\displaystyle|Q|\left|A_{Q,t}^{\frac{1}{p}}B_{Q,t}^{\frac{1}{p}}\right|_{op}

\displaystyle\left(\fint_{Q}|\vec{f}(x)|^{p}_{B_{1}^{n}}\left|B^{-\frac{1}{p}}_{Q,t}W^{-\frac{1}{r}}(x)\right|_{op}^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\left(\fint_{Q}|\vec{g}(y)|^{q^{\prime}}_{(B_{2}^{*})^{n}}\left|A^{-\frac{1}{p}}_{Q,t}W^{\frac{1}{r}}(y)\right|_{op}^{q^{\prime}}\,\mathrm{d}y\right)^{\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}.

By the Cordes inequality (Lemma
6.2
) we have

\left|A_{Q,t}^{\frac{1}{p}}B_{Q,t}^{\frac{1}{p}}\right|_{op}\leq\left|A_{Q,t}B_{Q,t}\right|_{op}^{\frac{1}{p}}\eqsim_{n,p,r}[W]_{A_{t}}^{\frac{1}{r}},

\left|B^{-\frac{1}{p}}_{Q,t}W^{-\frac{1}{r}}(x)\right|_{op}^{p}\leq\left|B^{-1}_{Q,t}W^{-\frac{1}{t}}(x)\right|_{op}

and

\left|A^{-\frac{1}{p}}_{Q,t}W^{\frac{1}{r}}(y)\right|_{op}^{q^{\prime}}\leq\left|A^{-1}_{Q,t}W^{\frac{1}{t}}(y)\right|_{op}^{\frac{q^{\prime}}{p}}.

We combine the estimates so far and use Hölder’s inequality to get

\displaystyle\langle W^{\frac{1}{r}}T[W^{-\frac{1}{r}}\vec{f}],\vec{g}\,\rangle\lesssim_{n,p,r}[W]_{A_{t}}^{\frac{1}{r}}S_{\vec{f}}\,\tilde{S}_{\vec{g}},

where

S_{\vec{f}}\coloneqq\left(\sum_{Q\in\mathscr{Q}}|Q|\left[\fint_{Q}|\vec{f}(x)|^{p}_{B_{1}^{n}}\left|B^{-1}_{Q,t}W^{-\frac{1}{t}}(x)\right|_{op}\,\mathrm{d}x\right]^{\frac{r}{p}}\right)^{\frac{1}{r}}

and

\tilde{S}_{\vec{g}}\coloneqq\left(\sum_{Q\in\mathscr{Q}}|Q|\left[\fint_{Q}|\vec{g}(y)|^{q^{\prime}}_{(B_{2}^{*})^{n}}\left|A^{-1}_{Q,t}W^{\frac{1}{t}}(y)\right|_{op}^{\frac{q^{\prime}}{p}}\,\mathrm{d}y\right]^{\frac{r^{\prime}}{q^{\prime}}}\right)^{\frac{1}{r^{\prime}}}.

We will first deal with
S_{\vec{f}}
. An application of Hölder’s inequality yields

\displaystyle\left(\fint_{Q}|\vec{f}(x)|^{p}_{B_{1}^{n}}\left|B^{-1}_{Q,t}W^{-\frac{1}{t}}(x)\right|_{op}\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\leq\left(\fint_{Q}|\vec{f}(x)|^{a}_{B_{1}^{n}}\right)^{\frac{1}{a}}\left(\fint_{Q}\left|B^{-1}_{Q,t}W^{-\frac{1}{t}}(x)\right|_{op}^{t^{\prime}(1+\sigma)}\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{pt^{\prime}(1+\sigma)}},

where
a\coloneqq p(t^{\prime}(1+\sigma))^{\prime}
and
\sigma\coloneqq\frac{1}{2^{d+1}[\Sigma]^{Sc}_{A_{t^{\prime}}}-1}
. Lemma
6.1
gives that

\left(\fint_{Q}\left|B^{-1}_{Q,t}W^{-\frac{1}{t}}(x)\right|_{op}^{t^{\prime}(1+\sigma)}\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{t^{\prime}(1+\sigma)}}\lesssim_{n}1.

Thus

\displaystyle S_{\vec{f}}

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{Q\in\mathscr{Q}}|Q|\left[\fint_{Q}|\vec{f}(x)|^{p}_{B_{1}^{n}}\left|B^{-1}_{Q,t}W^{-\frac{1}{t}}(x)\right|_{op}\,\mathrm{d}x\right]^{\frac{r}{p}}\right)^{\frac{1}{r}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{n}\left(\sum_{Q\in\mathscr{Q}}|Q|\left[\fint_{Q}|\vec{f}(x)|^{a}_{B_{1}^{n}}\,\mathrm{d}x\right]^{\frac{r}{a}}\right)^{\frac{1}{r}}.

Note that
a=p(t^{\prime}(1+\sigma))^{\prime}<pt=r
. Thus similar calculations as in Proposition
6.7
yield

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{Q\in\mathscr{Q}}|Q|\left[\fint_{Q}|\vec{f}(x)|^{a}_{B_{1}^{n}}\,\mathrm{d}x\right]^{\frac{r}{a}}\right)^{\frac{1}{r}}\lesssim_{\gamma}\left(\frac{r}{r-a}\right)^{\frac{1}{a}}\|\vec{f}\|_{L^{r}_{B^{n}_{1}}}

and hence

\displaystyle S_{\vec{f}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{n,\gamma}\left(\frac{r}{r-a}\right)^{\frac{1}{a}}\|\vec{f}\|_{L^{r}_{B^{n}_{1}}}.

Furthermore, we have
r-a\gtrsim_{r,p}\sigma
and hence

\left(\frac{r}{r-a}\right)^{\frac{1}{a}}\lesssim_{p,r}\sigma^{-\frac{1}{a}}\lesssim\sigma^{-\frac{1}{r}}\lesssim_{d,n,p,r}[W]_{A_{t}}^{\frac{1}{r(t-1)}},

where the second inequality is due to Lemma
6.5
with
\alpha=p
,
\beta=t
and
\eta=\sigma
.

In order to estimate
\tilde{S}_{\vec{g}}

we assume
q>r
, fix
s\coloneqq\left(\frac{q}{r}\right)^{\prime}
and consider weights
W\in RH_{t,s}
.
We then note that

b\coloneqq q^{\prime}\left(\frac{pts(1+\vartheta)}{q^{\prime}}\right)^{\prime}=q^{\prime}\left(\frac{rs(1+\vartheta)}{q^{\prime}}\right)^{\prime}<q^{\prime}\left(\frac{rs}{q^{\prime}}\right)^{\prime}=r^{\prime}

and apply Hölder’s inequality to get

\displaystyle\left(\fint_{Q}|\vec{g}(y)|^{q^{\prime}}_{(B_{2}^{*})^{n}}\left|A^{-1}_{Q,t}W^{\frac{1}{t}}(y)\right|_{op}^{\frac{q^{\prime}}{p}}\,\mathrm{d}y\right)^{\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}\leq\left(\fint_{Q}\left|A^{-1}_{Q,t}W^{\frac{1}{t}}(y)\right|_{op}^{ts(1+\vartheta)}\,\mathrm{d}y\right)^{\frac{1}{ts(1+\vartheta)}\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\left(\fint_{Q}|\vec{g}(y)|^{b}_{(B_{2}^{*})^{n}}\,\mathrm{d}y\right)^{\frac{1}{b}}

,

where

\vartheta\coloneqq\frac{1}{2^{d+1}[(AW^{Sc}_{Q,t})^{s}]_{A_{\infty}}-1}\quad\text{ with }\quad AW^{Sc}_{Q,t}(y)\coloneqq\left|A^{-1}_{Q,t}W^{\frac{1}{t}}(y)\right|_{op}^{t}.

The above is well defined since by Lemma
6.4
the scalar weight
(AW^{Sc}_{Q,t})^{s}
is in
A_{\infty}
, and we can apply the reverse Hölder inequality and the definition of
RH_{t,s}
to get

\displaystyle\left(\fint_{Q}\left|A^{-1}_{Q,t}W^{\frac{1}{t}}(y)\right|_{op}^{ts(1+\vartheta)}\,\mathrm{d}y\right)^{\frac{1}{ts(1+\vartheta)}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\fint_{Q}\left|A^{-1}_{Q,t}W^{\frac{1}{t}}(y)\right|_{op}^{ts}\,\mathrm{d}y\right)^{\frac{1}{ts}}

\displaystyle\leq[W]^{\frac{1}{t}}_{RH_{t,s}}\left(\fint_{Q}\left|A^{-1}_{Q,t}W^{\frac{1}{t}}(y)\right|_{op}^{t}\,\mathrm{d}y\right)^{\frac{1}{t}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{n}[W]^{\frac{1}{t}}_{RH_{t,s}}.

We arrive at

\displaystyle\left(\fint_{Q}|\vec{g}(y)|^{q^{\prime}}_{(B_{2}^{*})^{n}}\left|A^{-\frac{1}{p}}_{Q,t}W^{\frac{1}{r}}(y)\right|_{op}^{q^{\prime}}\,\mathrm{d}y\right)^{\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{n}[W]^{\frac{1}{r}}_{RH_{t,s}}\left(\fint_{Q}|\vec{g}(y)|^{b}_{(B_{2}^{*})^{n}}\right)^{\frac{1}{b}}

and hence

\displaystyle\tilde{S}_{\vec{g}}

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{Q\in\mathscr{Q}}|Q|\left[\fint_{Q}|\vec{g}(y)|^{q^{\prime}}_{(B_{2}^{*})^{n}}\left|A^{-\frac{1}{p}}_{Q,t}W^{\frac{1}{r}}(x)\right|_{op}^{q^{\prime}}\,\mathrm{d}x\right]^{\frac{r^{\prime}}{q^{\prime}}}\right)^{\frac{1}{r^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{n,\gamma}[W]^{\frac{1}{r}}_{RH_{t,s}}\left(\frac{r^{\prime}}{r^{\prime}-b}\right)^{\frac{1}{b}}\|\vec{g}\|_{L^{r^{\prime}}_{(B_{2}^{*})^{n}}}\lesssim_{q,r}[W]^{\frac{1}{r}}_{RH_{t,s}}\vartheta^{-\frac{1}{b}}\|\vec{g}\|_{L^{r^{\prime}}_{(B_{2}^{*})^{n}}}.

We again have

\vartheta^{-\frac{1}{b}}\lesssim\vartheta^{-\frac{1}{r^{\prime}}},

which can be seen by recalling that

b=q^{\prime}\left(\frac{rs}{q^{\prime}}(1+\vartheta)\right)^{\prime}

and applying Lemma
6.5
with
\alpha=q^{\prime}
,
\beta=\left(\frac{rs}{q^{\prime}}\right)^{\prime}=\frac{r^{\prime}}{q^{\prime}}
and
\eta=\vartheta
.
By Lemma
6.4
we have

[(AW^{Sc}_{Q,t})^{s}]_{A_{\infty}}\leq[AW^{Sc}_{Q,t}]_{RH_{s}}^{s}[AW^{Sc}_{Q,t}]_{A_{\infty}}^{s},

and the terms on the right-hand side satisfy

[AW^{Sc}_{Q,t}]_{RH_{s}}\leq[W]_{RH_{t,s}}

and

[AW^{Sc}_{Q,t}]_{A_{\infty}}\leq[W]^{Sc}_{A_{t}}\lesssim_{n,r}[W]_{A_{t}}.

Thus we get

\vartheta^{-\frac{1}{r^{\prime}}}\lesssim_{d}\left([AW^{Sc}_{Q,t}]_{RH_{s}}[AW^{Sc}_{Q,t}]_{A_{\infty}}\right)^{\frac{s}{r^{\prime}}}\lesssim_{n,q,r}\left([W]_{RH_{t,s}}[W]_{A_{t}}\right)^{\frac{s}{r^{\prime}}}.

Combining everything together we get that, assuming
1<p<r<q<\infty
and
W\in A_{t}\cap RH_{t,s}
where
t\coloneqq\frac{r}{p}
and
s\coloneqq\left(\frac{q}{r}\right)^{\prime}
, we have

\displaystyle\|T\vec{f}\|_{L^{r}_{B_{2}^{n}}(W)}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{d,n,p,q,r,\gamma}C_{1}\,[W]_{A_{t}}^{\frac{t^{\prime}}{r}+\frac{s}{r^{\prime}}}[W]_{RH_{t,s}}^{\frac{1}{r}+\frac{s}{r^{\prime}}}\|\vec{f}\|_{L^{r}_{B_{1}^{n}}(W)}.

Note that if we set
p=1
and
q=\infty
in the above inequality, then we get (
6
). Furthermore, the same proof with obvious changes gives the same inequality with
p=1,q<\infty
and
p>1,q=\infty
.
The results of the above discussion are summarized in the following theorem.

Theorem 6.9
.

Let
1\leq p<r<q\leq\infty
,
t=\frac{r}{p}
,
s=\left(\frac{q}{r}\right)^{\prime}
and
W\in A_{t}\cap RH_{t,s}
. Let
T
be an operator that satisfies the convex body domination estimate

|\langle T\vec{f},\vec{g}\,\rangle|\leq C_{1}\sum_{Q\in\mathscr{Q}}|Q|\llangle\vec{f}\rrangle_{\textit{\L}^{p}(Q,B_{1})}\cdot\llangle\vec{g}\rrangle_{\textit{\L}^{q^{\prime}}(Q,B_{2}^{*})},

where
\mathscr{Q}
is a
\gamma
-sparse family of cubes and
C_{1}
is a constant. Then
T
satisfies a matrix weighted norm inequality

\|T\vec{f}\|_{L^{r}_{B_{2}^{n}}(W)}\leq C[W]_{A_{t}}^{\frac{t^{\prime}}{r}+\frac{s}{r^{\prime}}}[W]_{RH_{t,s}}^{\frac{1}{r}+\frac{s}{r^{\prime}}}\|\vec{f}\|_{L^{r}_{B_{1}^{n}}(W)},

where
C=C_{1}C_{2}
and
C_{2}
depends only on
d,n,p,q,r
and
\gamma
.

To the author’s best knowledge the above is the first matrix-weighted norm inequality that has arisen from
(\textit{\L}^{p},\textit{\L}^{q^{\prime}})
convex body domination at this level of generality with general exponents
1\leq p<q\leq\infty
. See Section 6 in
[
6
]
for results in the special case, where one can take
p=q^{\prime}=1+\varepsilon
for small enough
\varepsilon>0
.

Note that
\frac{1}{r}+\frac{s}{r^{\prime}}=\frac{q-1}{q-r}
and
\frac{t^{\prime}}{r}+\frac{s}{r^{\prime}}=\frac{p}{(r-p)r}+\frac{q-1}{q-r}
. This modifies the conclusion of the above theorem to a form that is somewhat close to the scalar-valued version from
[
2
, Proposition 6.4]
. The scalar-valued conclusion is that the needed weight constant is

([w]_{A_{t}}[w]_{RH_{s}})^{\max\{\frac{1}{r-p},\frac{q-1}{q-r}\}}

and the exponent is sharp. Unfortunately, in most cases our result cannot reach the same exponent.

Theorem
3.3
combined with Theorem
6.9
yields the following corollary.

Corollary 6.10
.

Let
p,r,q,t
and
s
be as in Theorem
6.9
. If
T=\sum_{j=N_{1}}^{N_{2}}T_{j}
is a BRS operator, i.e.,
T
satisfies conditions
3.1
–
T6
from Definition
3.1
, then we have

\|T\vec{f}\|_{L^{r}_{B_{2}^{n}}(W)}\lesssim_{d,n,p,q,r,\gamma,\kappa}\mathcal{C}\,[W]_{A_{t}}^{\frac{t^{\prime}}{r}+\frac{s}{r^{\prime}}}[W]_{RH_{t,s}}^{\frac{1}{r}+\frac{s}{r^{\prime}}}\|\vec{f}\|_{L^{r}_{B_{1}^{n}}(W)}.

7.
Commutators

In this section we note how convex body domination for an operator
T
implies sparse domination for the commutator of
T
. It is known that pointwise convex body domination of the operator
T
implies certain pointwise convex body domination bounds for the commutator of
T
. See
[
13
, Theorem 4]
and
[
14
, Theorem 1.8]
for more details. We will not pursue such results in our setting, but instead we will prove that our definition of convex body domination for
T
implies matrix-weighted bounds also for the commutator of
T
.

We assume that
B
is a locally integrable matrix-valued function. Then the commutator
[B,T]
is defined by

[B,T]\vec{f}\coloneqq BT\vec{f}-T[B\vec{f}].

We will denote the average of
B
over
Q
by
\langle B\rangle_{Q}
and define

\mathcal{A}_{\mathscr{Q}}(\vec{f},\vec{g})\coloneqq\sum_{Q\in\mathscr{Q}}|Q|\left(\fint_{Q}|B(x)-\langle B\rangle_{Q}|_{op}^{p}|\vec{f}(x)|_{B_{1}^{n}}^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\left(\fint_{Q}|\vec{g}(y)|_{B_{2}^{*}}^{q^{\prime}}\,\mathrm{d}y\right)^{\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}

and

\mathcal{A}^{*}_{\mathscr{Q}}(\vec{f},\vec{g})\coloneqq\sum_{Q\in\mathscr{Q}}|Q|\left(\fint_{Q}|B(x)-\langle B\rangle_{Q}|_{op}^{q^{\prime}}|\vec{g}(y)|_{(B_{2}^{*})^{n}}^{q^{\prime}}\,\mathrm{d}y\right)^{\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}\left(\fint_{Q}|\vec{f}(x)|_{B_{2}^{*}}^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

The above notations and the following theorem are inspired by
[
18
, Theorem 1.1]
.

Proposition 7.1
.

Assume that
T
satisfies
(\textit{\L}^{p}_{B_{1}},\textit{\L}^{q^{\prime}}_{B_{2}^{*}})
convex body domination, then for some sparse collection of dyadic cubes
\mathscr{Q}
we have

\langle[B,T]\vec{f},\vec{g}\rangle\lesssim\mathcal{A}_{\mathscr{Q}}(\vec{f},\vec{g})+\mathcal{A}^{*}_{\mathscr{Q}}(\vec{f},\vec{g}).

Proof.

We denote
\vec{F}\coloneqq\begin{bmatrix}\vec{f}\\
B\vec{f}\end{bmatrix}
and
\vec{G}\coloneqq\begin{bmatrix}B^{\top}\vec{g}\\
-\vec{g}\end{bmatrix}
.
Recalling (
2
) and (
2
), we have

\langle[B,T]\vec{f},\vec{g}\rangle=\langle T\vec{f},B^{\top}\vec{g}\rangle-\langle T[B\vec{f}],\vec{g}\rangle=\langle T\vec{F},\vec{G}\rangle.

Thus from the
(\textit{\L}^{p}_{B_{1}},\textit{\L}^{q^{\prime}}_{B_{2}^{*}})
convex body domination we get that

\displaystyle\langle[B,T]\vec{f},\vec{g}\rangle

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{Q\in\mathscr{Q}}|Q|\llangle\vec{F}\rrangle_{\textit{\L}^{p}(Q,B_{1})}\cdot\llangle\vec{G}\rrangle_{\textit{\L}^{q^{\prime}}(Q,B_{2}^{*})}.

We estimate the convex bodies via Lemma
2.5
and get

\displaystyle(1+\varepsilon)^{-2}\llangle\vec{F}\rrangle_{\textit{\L}^{p}(Q,B_{1})}\cdot\llangle\vec{G}

\displaystyle\rrangle_{\textit{\L}^{q^{\prime}}(Q,B_{2}^{*})}\leq

\displaystyle\left|\fint_{Q}\fint_{Q}\langle\vec{F}(x),\phi(x)\rangle_{(B_{1},B_{1}^{*})}\cdot\langle\vec{G}(y),\psi(y)\rangle_{(B_{2}^{*},B_{2}^{**})}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y\right|,

for some
\phi\in\bar{B}_{\textit{\L}^{p^{\prime}}_{B_{1}^{*}}(Q)}
and
\psi\in\bar{B}_{\textit{\L}^{q}_{B_{2}^{**}(Q)}}
.
A simple calculation shows that the integrand is equal to

\displaystyle\Big(B(y)-B(x)\Big)\langle\vec{f}(x),\phi(x)\rangle_{(B_{1},B_{1}^{*})}\cdot\langle\vec{g}(y),\psi(y)\rangle_{(B_{2}^{*},B_{2}^{**})}.

Thus we have

\displaystyle(1+\varepsilon)^{-2}

\displaystyle\llangle\vec{F}\rrangle_{\textit{\L}^{p}(Q,B_{1})}\cdot\,\llangle\vec{G}\rrangle_{\textit{\L}^{q^{\prime}}(Q,B_{2}^{*})}

\displaystyle\leq\fint_{Q}\fint_{Q}|B(y)-B(x)|_{op}|\langle\vec{f}(x),\phi(x)\rangle_{(B_{1},B_{1}^{*})}\cdot\langle\vec{g}(y),\psi(y)\rangle_{(B_{2}^{*},B_{2}^{**})}|\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\leq\fint_{Q}\fint_{Q}|B(y)-B(x)|_{op}|\vec{f}(x)|_{B_{1}}|\phi(x)|_{B_{1}^{*}}|\vec{g}(y)|_{B_{2}^{*}}|\psi(y)|_{B_{2}^{**}}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y.

Then we apply triangle inequality and Hölder’s inequality to see that the last expression is bounded by

\displaystyle\left(\fint_{Q}|\vec{f}(x)|_{B_{1}}^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\left(\fint_{Q}|B(y)-\langle B\rangle_{Q}|_{op}^{q^{\prime}}|\vec{g}(y)|_{B_{2}^{*}}^{q^{\prime}}\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\left(\fint_{Q}|\vec{g}(y)|_{B_{2}^{*}}^{q^{\prime}}\,\mathrm{d}y\right)^{\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}\left(\fint_{Q}|B(x)-\langle B\rangle_{Q}|_{op}|^{p}\vec{f}(x)|_{B_{1}}^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Then letting
\varepsilon\to 0
gives us the result.
∎

We will then assume that the component functions
B_{ij}
of
B
are in
\operatorname{BMO}
. We find the
\operatorname{BMO}
class useful due to the following well known result that follows by the John-Nirenberg inequality and Stirling approximation.

Lemma 7.2
.

Let
1<a<\infty
and
b\in\operatorname{BMO}
. Then we have

\left(\fint_{Q}|b(x)-\langle b\rangle_{Q}|^{a}\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{a}}\lesssim_{d}a\,\|b\|_{BMO}.

Now we are ready to show the sparse bound for the commutator
[B,T]
.
By the properties of the standard orthonormal basis of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
we have

|B(x)-\langle B\rangle_{Q}|_{op}\eqsim_{n}\max_{i,j}|B_{ij}(x)-\langle B_{ij}\rangle_{Q}|.

Then applying the Hölder inequality and Lemma
7.2
we get that for any
u>1
it holds that

\displaystyle\left(\fint_{Q}|B(x)-\langle B\rangle_{Q}|_{op}^{p}|\vec{f}(x)|_{B_{1}^{n}}^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\left(\fint_{Q}|B(x)-\langle B\rangle_{Q}|_{op}^{pu^{\prime}}\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{pu^{\prime}}}\left(\fint_{Q}|\vec{f}(x)|_{B_{1}^{n}}^{pu}\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{pu}}

\displaystyle\qquad\eqsim_{n}\max_{i,j}\left(\fint_{Q}|B_{ij}(x)-\langle B_{ij}\rangle_{Q}|^{pu^{\prime}}\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{pu^{\prime}}}\left(\fint_{Q}|\vec{f}(x)|_{B_{1}^{n}}^{pu}\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{pu}}

\displaystyle\qquad\lesssim_{d,p}u^{\prime}\max_{i,j}\|B_{ij}\|_{BMO}\left(\fint_{Q}|\vec{f}(x)|_{B_{1}^{n}}^{pu}\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{pu}}.

Similarly for the other term we have

\displaystyle\left(\fint_{Q}|B(y)-\langle B\rangle_{Q}|_{op}^{q^{\prime}}|\vec{g}(y)|_{(B_{2}^{*})^{n}}^{q^{\prime}}\,\mathrm{d}y\right)^{\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\lesssim_{d,n,q}u^{\prime}\max_{i,j}\|B_{ij}\|_{BMO}\left(\fint_{Q}|\vec{g}(y)|_{(B_{2}^{*})^{n}}^{q^{\prime}u}\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{q^{\prime}u}}

and thus by Proposition
7.1
we get a sparse domination inequality

\langle[B,T]\vec{f},\vec{g}\rangle\lesssim_{d,n,p,q,u}\max_{i,j}\|B_{ij}\|_{BMO}\sum_{Q\in\mathscr{Q}}|Q|\|\vec{f}\|_{\textit{\L}^{pu}(Q,B_{1}^{n})}\|\vec{g}\|_{\textit{\L}^{q^{\prime}u}(Q,(B_{2}^{*})^{n})},

where the dependence on
u
is
u^{\prime}
. We note that for the multi-scale operator that satisfies
3.1
–
T6
, the sparse domination of commutators is new even in the scalar case. It is well documented, see
[
2
, Proposition 6.4]
, that with
pu<r<(q^{\prime}u)^{\prime}
and
w\in A_{\frac{r}{pu}}\cap RH_{(\frac{(q^{\prime}u)^{\prime}}{r})^{\prime}}
the sparse bound (
7
) implies a norm inequality in
L^{r}(w)
for the commutator of
T
. If
u
is close enough to one, then by the self improvement properties of the Muckenhoupt and the reverse Hölder classes, it even suffices to take
w\in A_{\frac{r}{p}}\cap RH_{(\frac{q}{r})^{\prime}}
. However, the dependence on
u
will increase the exponent of the weight constant. We will see that these ideas can be extended to the matrix-weighted case.

Our next goal is to adapt the proofs of Theorem
6.9
and Proposition
7.1
to get a matrix-weighted norm inequality for the commutator
[B,T]
. To do this we will need the following elementary result.

Lemma 7.3
.

Let
1<r<q<\infty
,
0<\vartheta<\frac{1}{2}
,
s=\left(\frac{q}{r}\right)^{\prime}=\frac{q}{q-r}
and
u=1+C_{q,r}\vartheta
with
C_{q,r}=\frac{q-r}{r(q-1)+q(r-1)}
. Then

r^{\prime}-q^{\prime}u\left(\frac{rs(1+\vartheta)}{q^{\prime}u}\right)^{\prime}\gtrsim_{q,r}\vartheta.

Proof.

We start by calculating that

q^{\prime}u\left(\frac{rs(1+\vartheta)}{q^{\prime}u}\right)^{\prime}=r^{\prime}\frac{(r-1)q(1+\vartheta)u}{r(q-1)(1+\vartheta)-u(q-r)}.

Thus, we get

\displaystyle r^{\prime}-q^{\prime}u\left(\frac{rs(1+\vartheta)}{q^{\prime}u}\right)^{\prime}

\displaystyle=r^{\prime}\left(1-\frac{(r-1)q(1+\vartheta)u}{r(q-1)(1+\vartheta)-u(q-r)}\right)

\displaystyle\eqsim_{r}1-\frac{(r-1)q(1+\vartheta)(1+C_{q,r}\vartheta)}{r(q-1)(1+\vartheta)-(1+C_{q,r}\vartheta)(q-r)}

\displaystyle\eqqcolon\frac{N}{D}

The denominator
D
is clearly less than
2r(q-1)
and for the numerator
N
we have

\displaystyle N

\displaystyle=r(q-1)(1+\vartheta)-(1+C_{q,r}\vartheta)(q-r)-(r-1)q(1+\vartheta)(1+C_{q,r}\vartheta)

\displaystyle=\vartheta C_{q,r}q(r-1)(1-\vartheta)

\displaystyle\gtrsim_{q,r}\vartheta.

The second equality above is due to the definition of
C_{q,r}
.
∎

Now we are ready to show that convex body domination for the operator
T
implies matrix-weighted bounds also for the commutator
[B,T]
. This result was inspired by similar results from
[
13
]
and
[
14
]
.

Theorem 7.4
.

Let
1\leq p<r<q\leq\infty
,
t=\frac{r}{p}
,
s=\left(\frac{q}{r}\right)^{\prime}
and
W\in A_{t}\cap RH_{t,s}
. If
T
satisfies
(\textit{\L}^{p}_{B_{1}},\textit{\L}^{q^{\prime}}_{B_{2}^{*}})
convex body domination and the components of the matrix
B
are in
\operatorname{BMO}
, then

\displaystyle\|[B,T]

\displaystyle\|_{L_{B_{1}^{n}}^{r}(W)\to L_{B_{2}^{n}}^{r}(W)}\leq

\displaystyle\qquad C\max_{i,j}\|B_{i,j}\|_{BMO}\left([W]_{A_{t}}^{s}[W]_{RH_{t,s}}^{s}+[W]_{A_{t}}^{\frac{1}{t-1}}\right)[W]_{A_{t}}^{\frac{t^{\prime}}{r}+\frac{s}{r^{\prime}}}[W]_{RH_{t,s}}^{\frac{1}{r}+\frac{s}{r^{\prime}}},

where
C=C_{n,d,p,q,r,\gamma}
is a constant.

Proof.

We denote
\vec{f}_{W}^{\,-}\coloneqq W^{-\frac{1}{r}}\vec{f}
,
\vec{g}_{W}^{\,+}\coloneqq W^{\frac{1}{r}}\vec{g}
and

\mathscr{I}^{\phi,\psi}_{B,f,g}(x,y)\coloneqq|B(y)-B(x)|_{op}|\langle\vec{f}_{W}^{\,-}(x),\phi(x)\rangle_{(B_{1},B_{1}^{*})}\cdot\langle\vec{g}_{W}^{\,+}(y),\psi(y)\rangle_{(B_{2}^{*},B_{2}^{**})}|,

where
\phi\in\bar{B}_{\textit{\L}^{p^{\prime}}_{B_{1}^{*}}(Q)}
and
\psi\in\bar{B}_{\textit{\L}^{q}_{B_{2}^{**}(Q)}}
.
Similarly as in the proof of Proposition
7.1
, for any
\varepsilon>0
we have

\displaystyle\langle W^{\frac{1}{r}}[B,T](W^{-\frac{1}{r}}\vec{f}),\vec{g}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle[B,T]\vec{f}_{W}^{\,-},\vec{g}_{W}^{\,+}\rangle

\displaystyle\lesssim(1+\varepsilon)^{2}\sum_{Q\in\mathscr{Q}}|Q|\fint_{Q}\fint_{Q}\mathscr{I}^{\phi,\psi}_{B,f,g}(x,y)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y.

We note that
\mathscr{I}^{\phi,\psi}_{B,f,g}(x,y)
is bounded from above by

|B(y)-B(x)|_{op}\left|W^{-\frac{1}{r}}(x)W^{\frac{1}{r}}(y)\right|_{op}|\vec{f}(x)|_{B_{1}^{n}}|\phi(x)|_{B_{1}^{*}}|\vec{g}(y)|_{(B_{2}^{*})^{n}}|\psi(y)|_{B_{2}^{**}}.

Thus applying triangle inequality
|B(x)-B(y)|\leq|B(x)-\langle B\rangle_{Q}|+|B(y)-\langle B\rangle_{Q}|
and Hölder’s inequality yields

\sum_{Q\in\mathscr{Q}}|Q|\fint_{Q}\fint_{Q}\mathscr{I}_{B,f,g}(x,y)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y\leq\mathcal{A}_{\mathscr{Q},W}(\vec{f},\vec{g})+\mathcal{A}_{\mathscr{Q},W}^{*}(\vec{f},\vec{g}),

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{\mathscr{Q},W}(\vec{f},\vec{g})\coloneqq\sum_{Q\in\mathscr{Q}}|Q|\Bigg(\fint_{Q}

\displaystyle|B(y)-\langle B\rangle_{Q}|_{op}^{q^{\prime}}|\vec{g}(y)|_{(B_{2}^{*})^{n}}^{q^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\left[\fint_{Q}\left|W^{-\frac{1}{r}}(x)W^{\frac{1}{r}}(y)\right|_{op}^{p}|\vec{f}(x)|^{p}_{B_{1}^{n}}\,\mathrm{d}x\right]^{\frac{q^{\prime}}{p}}\,\mathrm{d}y\Bigg)^{\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}

and

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{\mathscr{Q},W}^{*}(\vec{f},\vec{g})\coloneqq\sum_{Q\in\mathscr{Q}}|Q|\Bigg(\fint_{Q}

\displaystyle|B(x)-\langle B\rangle_{Q}|_{op}^{p}|\vec{f}(x)|_{B_{1}^{n}}^{p}

\displaystyle\left[\fint_{Q}\left|W^{-\frac{1}{r}}(x)W^{\frac{1}{r}}(y)\right|_{op}^{q^{\prime}}|\vec{g}(y)|^{q^{\prime}}_{(B_{2}^{*})^{n}}\,\mathrm{d}y\right]^{\frac{p}{q^{\prime}}}\,\mathrm{d}x\Bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Letting
\varepsilon\to 0
gives

(15)

\langle W^{\frac{1}{r}}[B,T](W^{-\frac{1}{r}}\vec{f}),\vec{g}\rangle\lesssim\mathcal{A}_{\mathscr{Q},W}(\vec{f},\vec{g})+\mathcal{A}_{\mathscr{Q},W}^{*}(\vec{f},\vec{g})

and we are left with bounding
\mathcal{A}_{\mathscr{Q},W}
and
\mathcal{A}^{*}_{\mathscr{Q},W}
.

We will consider
\mathcal{A}_{\mathscr{Q},W}
first. The term inside the sum is dominated by

\displaystyle|Q|\left|A_{Q,t}^{\frac{1}{p}}B_{Q,t}^{\frac{1}{p}}\right|_{op}

\displaystyle\left(\fint_{Q}|\vec{f}(x)|^{p}_{B_{1}^{n}}\left|B^{-\frac{1}{p}}_{Q,t}W^{-\frac{1}{r}}(x)\right|_{op}^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\left(\fint_{Q}|B(y)-\langle B\rangle_{Q}|^{q^{\prime}}_{op}|\vec{g}(y)|^{q^{\prime}}_{(B_{2}^{*})^{n}}\left|A^{-\frac{1}{p}}_{Q,t}W^{\frac{1}{r}}(y)\right|_{op}^{q^{\prime}}\,\mathrm{d}y\right)^{\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}},

where
A_{Q,t}
and
B_{Q,t}
are the reducing matrices that we defined in the beginning of Section 6. Similarly as in the proof of Theorem
6.9
we apply the Cordes inequality (Lemma
6.2
) and the Hölder inequality to get

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{\mathscr{Q},W}(\vec{f},\vec{g})\lesssim[W]_{A_{t}}^{\frac{1}{r}}S_{\vec{f}}\,\tilde{S}_{\vec{g},B},

where

S_{\vec{f}}\coloneqq\left(\sum_{Q\in\mathscr{Q}}|Q|\left[\fint_{Q}|\vec{f}(x)|^{p}_{B_{1}^{n}}\left|B^{-1}_{Q,t}W^{-\frac{1}{t}}(x)\right|_{op}\,\mathrm{d}x\right]^{\frac{r}{p}}\right)^{\frac{1}{r}}

and

\tilde{S}_{\vec{g},B}\coloneqq\left(\sum_{Q\in\mathscr{Q}}|Q|\left[\fint_{Q}|B(y)-\langle B\rangle_{Q}|^{q^{\prime}}_{op}|\vec{g}(y)|^{q^{\prime}}_{(B_{2}^{*})^{n}}\left|A^{-1}_{Q,t}W^{\frac{1}{t}}(x)\right|_{op}^{\frac{q^{\prime}}{p}}\,\mathrm{d}x\right]^{\frac{r^{\prime}}{q^{\prime}}}\right)^{\frac{1}{r^{\prime}}}.

In the proof of Theorem
6.9
we showed that

S_{\vec{f}}\lesssim_{d,n,p,r,\gamma}[W]_{A_{t}}^{\frac{1}{r(t-1)}}\|\vec{f}\|_{L^{r}_{B_{1}^{n}}}.

In order to bound
\tilde{S}_{\vec{g},B}
we let

\vartheta\coloneqq\frac{1}{2^{d+1}[(AW^{Sc}_{Q,t})^{s}]_{A_{\infty}}-1}\quad\text{ with }\quad AW^{Sc}_{Q,t}(y)\coloneqq\left|A^{-1}_{Q,t}W^{\frac{1}{t}}(y)\right|_{op}^{t}

and
u\coloneqq 1+C_{q,r}\vartheta
with
C_{q,r}\coloneqq\frac{q-r}{r(q-1)+q(r-1)}
.
Applying the Hölder inequality and Lemma
7.2
to the term inside the sum in
\tilde{S}_{\vec{g},B}
we get

\displaystyle\left[\fint_{Q}|B(y)-\langle B\rangle_{Q}|^{q^{\prime}}_{op}|\vec{g}(y)|^{q^{\prime}}_{(B_{2}^{*})^{n}}\left|A^{-1}_{Q,t}W^{\frac{1}{t}}(y)\right|_{op}^{\frac{q^{\prime}}{p}}\,\mathrm{d}y\right]^{\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\qquad\quad\lesssim_{d,n,q}u^{\prime}\max_{i,j}\|B_{i,j}\|_{BMO}\left[\fint_{Q}|\vec{g}(y)|^{q^{\prime}u}_{(B_{2}^{*})^{n}}\left|A^{-\frac{1}{p}}_{Q,t}W^{\frac{1}{r}}(y)\right|_{op}^{\frac{q^{\prime}u}{p}}\,\mathrm{d}y\right]^{\frac{1}{q^{\prime}u}}.

Furthermore, Lemma
6.4
implies that

u^{\prime}\lesssim_{q,r}\vartheta^{-1}\lesssim_{d,n,p,q,r}([W]_{RH_{t,s}}[W]_{A_{t}})^{s}.

Then we apply Hölder’s inequality to get

\displaystyle\left[\fint_{Q}|\vec{g}(y)|^{q^{\prime}u}_{(B_{2}^{*})^{n}}\left|A^{-\frac{1}{p}}_{Q,t}W^{\frac{1}{r}}(y)\right|_{op}^{\frac{q^{\prime}u}{p}}\,\mathrm{d}y\right]^{\frac{1}{q^{\prime}u}}\leq\left(\fint_{Q}\left|A^{-1}_{Q,t}W^{\frac{1}{t}}(y)\right|_{op}^{ts(1+\vartheta)}\,\mathrm{d}y\right)^{\frac{1}{ts(1+\vartheta)}\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\left(\fint_{Q}|\vec{g}(y)|^{b}_{(B_{2}^{*})^{n}}\,\mathrm{d}y\right)^{\frac{1}{b}},

where

b\coloneqq q^{\prime}u\left(\frac{pts(1+\vartheta)}{q^{\prime}u}\right)^{\prime}=q^{\prime}u\left(\frac{rs(1+\vartheta)}{q^{\prime}u}\right)^{\prime}.

We already showed in the proof of Theorem
6.9
that

\left(\fint_{Q}\left|A^{-1}_{Q,t}W^{\frac{1}{t}}(y)\right|_{op}^{ts(1+\vartheta)}\,\mathrm{d}y\right)^{\frac{1}{ts(1+\vartheta)}\frac{1}{p}}\lesssim_{n}[W]^{\frac{1}{t}}_{RH_{t,s}}

and by Lemma
7.3
we have

\sum_{Q\in\mathscr{Q}}\left(\fint_{Q}|\vec{g}(y)|^{b}_{(B_{2}^{*})^{n}}\,\mathrm{d}y\right)^{\frac{1}{b}}\lesssim_{\gamma}\left(\frac{r^{\prime}}{r^{\prime}-b}\right)^{\frac{1}{b}}\|\vec{g}\|_{L^{r^{\prime}}_{(B_{2}^{*})^{n}}}\lesssim_{q,r}\vartheta^{-\frac{1}{b}}\|\vec{g}\|_{L^{r^{\prime}}_{(B_{2}^{*})^{n}}}.

Furthermore, applying Lemmas
6.5
and
6.4
yields

\vartheta^{-\frac{1}{b}}\leq\vartheta^{-\frac{1}{q^{\prime}u\left(\frac{rs}{q^{\prime}u}\right)^{\prime}}}\leq\vartheta^{-\frac{1}{q^{\prime}\left(\frac{rs}{q^{\prime}}\right)^{\prime}}}=\vartheta^{-\frac{1}{r^{\prime}}}\lesssim_{d,n,p,q,r}\left([W]_{RH_{t,s}}[W]_{A_{t}}\right)^{\frac{s}{r^{\prime}}}.

We have now shown that

\mathcal{A}_{\mathscr{Q},W}(\vec{f},\vec{g})\lesssim_{d,n,p,q,r,\gamma}\max_{i,j}\|B_{i,j}\|_{BMO}[W]_{A_{t}}^{s+\frac{t^{\prime}}{r}+\frac{s}{r^{\prime}}}[W]_{RH_{t,s}}^{s+\frac{1}{r}+\frac{s}{r^{\prime}}}\|\vec{g}\|_{L^{r^{\prime}}_{(B_{2}^{*})^{n}}}\|\vec{f}\|_{L^{r}_{B_{1}^{n}}}.

Similar calculations shows that for the term
\mathcal{A}_{\mathscr{Q},W}^{*}
we have

\mathcal{A}^{*}_{\mathscr{Q},W}(\vec{f},\vec{g})\lesssim_{d,n,p,q,r,\gamma}\max_{i,j}\|B_{i,j}\|_{BMO}[W]_{A_{t}}^{\frac{1}{t-1}+\frac{t^{\prime}}{r}+\frac{s}{r^{\prime}}}[W]_{RH_{t,s}}^{\frac{1}{r}+\frac{s}{r^{\prime}}}\|\vec{g}\|_{L^{r^{\prime}}_{(B_{2}^{*})^{n}}}\|\vec{f}\|_{L^{r}_{B_{1}^{n}}}.

Combining the above bounds with (
15
) we get

\displaystyle\langle

\displaystyle W^{\frac{1}{r}}[B,T](W^{-\frac{1}{r}}\vec{f}),\vec{g}\rangle\lesssim_{d,n,p,q,r,\gamma}

\displaystyle\max_{i,j}\|B_{i,j}\|_{BMO}\|\vec{f}\|_{L^{r}_{B_{1}^{n}}}\|\vec{g}\|_{L^{r^{\prime}}_{(B_{2}^{*})^{n}}}\left([W]_{A_{t}}^{s}[W]_{RH_{t,s}}^{s}+[W]_{A_{t}}^{\frac{1}{t-1}}\right)[W]_{A_{t}}^{\frac{t^{\prime}}{r}+\frac{s}{r^{\prime}}}[W]_{RH_{t,s}}^{\frac{1}{r}+\frac{s}{r^{\prime}}}

and duality completes the proof.
∎

and

–

\displaystyle\|_{L^{r}_{B_{1}^{n}}(W)\to L^{r}_{B_{2}^{n}}(W)}\lesssim_{d,n,p,q,r,\gamma,\kappa}

8.
From dense subspaces to global bounds

In this section we show that in order to prove convex body domination bounds in the Bochner spaces
L^{r}_{B_{1}}
and
L^{r^{\prime}}_{B_{2}^{*}}
, it suffices to prove the same bounds in some dense subspaces. The proof is once again an adaptation of the argument from the scalar valued case, see
[
1
, Lemma A.1.]
.

Proposition 8.1
.

Suppose
1\leq p<r<q\leq\infty
, and let
V_{1}\subset L^{r}_{B_{1}}
and
V_{2}\subset L^{r^{\prime}}_{B_{2}^{*}}
be dense subspaces. Then the
(\textit{\L}^{p}_{B_{1}},\textit{\L}^{q^{\prime}}_{B_{2}^{*}})
convex body domination bound extends from
V_{1}\times V_{2}
to
L^{r}_{B_{1}}\times L^{r^{\prime}}_{B_{2}^{*}}
.

Proof.

Let
\vec{f}\in L^{r}_{B_{1}^{n}}
and
\vec{g}\in L^{r^{\prime}}_{(B_{2}^{*})^{n}}
. To simplify notation we will denote the Bochner
L^{p}
norm simply with
\|\cdot\|_{p}
. We will also denote

\Lambda_{p,q}^{\mathscr{Q}}(\vec{f},\vec{g})\coloneqq\sum_{Q\in\mathscr{Q}}|Q|\llangle\vec{f}\rrangle_{\textit{\L}^{p}(Q,B_{1})}\cdot\llangle\vec{g}\rrangle_{\textit{\L}^{q^{\prime}}(Q,B_{2}^{*})},

where
\mathscr{Q}
is some
\gamma
-sparse collection.
From the weighted bounds with
W=Id_{n}
we see that

(16)

\displaystyle\Lambda_{p,q}^{\mathscr{Q}}(\vec{f},\vec{g})\lesssim_{p,q,r,\gamma}\|\vec{f}\|_{r}\|\vec{g}\|_{r^{\prime}}.

Thus the assumed convex body domination implies that the bilinear form associated to
T
is bounded on
V_{1}^{n}\times V_{2}^{n}
and hence there exists a unique extension that is bounded on
L^{r}_{B_{1}^{n}}\times L^{r^{\prime}}_{(B^{*}_{2})^{n}}
.
For any
\varepsilon>0
, we choose
\vec{f}_{*}\in V_{1}^{n}
and
\vec{g}_{*}\in V_{2}^{n}
such that
\|\vec{f}-\vec{f}_{*}\|_{r}\leq\varepsilon
and
\|\vec{g}-\vec{g}_{*}\|_{r^{\prime}}\leq\varepsilon
. The choice can be made so that we also have
\|\vec{f}_{*}\|_{r}\leq 2\|\vec{f}\|_{r}
and
\|\vec{g}_{*}\|_{r^{\prime}}\leq 2\|\vec{g}\|_{r^{\prime}}
.
We need to show that

|\langle T\vec{f}_{*},\vec{g}_{*}\rangle|\lesssim\Lambda_{p,q}^{\mathscr{Q}}(\vec{f}_{*},\vec{g}_{*})\quad\Rightarrow\quad|\langle T\vec{f},\vec{g}\,\rangle|\lesssim\Lambda_{p,q}^{\mathscr{Q}}(\vec{f},\vec{g}).

We start by estimating

\displaystyle|\langle T\vec{f},\vec{g}\,\rangle|

\displaystyle\leq|\langle T[\vec{f}-\vec{f}_{*}],\vec{g}\,\rangle|+|\langle T\vec{f}_{*},\vec{g}-\vec{g}_{*}\rangle|+|\langle T\vec{f}_{*},\vec{g}_{*}\rangle|

\displaystyle\leq\|T\|_{r\to r}\left(\|\vec{f}-\vec{f}_{*}\|_{r}\|\vec{g}\|_{r^{\prime}}+\|\vec{f}_{*}\|_{r}\|\vec{g}-\vec{g}_{*}\|_{r^{\prime}}\right)+|\langle T\vec{f}_{*},\vec{g}_{*}\rangle|

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon\,\|T\|_{r\to r}\left(\|\vec{g}\|_{r^{\prime}}+2\|\vec{f}\|_{r}\right)+|\langle T\vec{f}_{*},\vec{g}_{*}\rangle|.

By assumption we have

|\langle T\vec{f}_{*},\vec{g}_{*}\rangle|\lesssim\Lambda_{p,q}^{\mathscr{Q}}(\vec{f}_{*},\vec{g}_{*}).

Then we have the identity

\displaystyle\llangle\vec{f}_{*}\rrangle_{\textit{\L}^{p}(Q,B_{1})}\cdot\llangle\vec{g}_{*}\rrangle_{\textit{\L}^{q^{\prime}}(Q,B_{2}^{*})}=

\displaystyle\,\,\llangle\vec{f}_{*}-\vec{f}\rrangle_{\textit{\L}^{p}(Q,B_{1})}\cdot\llangle\vec{g}_{*}\rrangle_{\textit{\L}^{q^{\prime}}(Q,B_{2}^{*})}

\displaystyle+\llangle\vec{f}\rrangle_{\textit{\L}^{p}(Q,B_{1})}\cdot\llangle\vec{g}_{*}-\vec{g}\rrangle_{\textit{\L}^{q^{\prime}}(Q,B_{2}^{*})}

\displaystyle+\llangle\vec{f}\rrangle_{\textit{\L}^{p}(Q,B_{1})}\cdot\llangle\vec{g}\rrangle_{\textit{\L}^{q^{\prime}}(Q,B_{2}^{*})}

and this implies that

\Lambda_{p,q}^{\mathscr{Q}}(\vec{f}_{*},\vec{g}_{*})=\Lambda_{p,q}^{\mathscr{Q}}(\vec{f}_{*}-\vec{f},\vec{g}_{*})+\Lambda_{p,q}^{\mathscr{Q}}(\vec{f},\vec{g}_{*}-\vec{g})+\Lambda_{p,q}^{\mathscr{Q}}(\vec{f},\vec{g}).

By (
16
) we have

\displaystyle\Lambda_{p,q}^{\mathscr{Q}}(\vec{f}_{*}-\vec{f},\vec{g}_{*})+\Lambda_{p,q}^{\mathscr{Q}}(\vec{f},\vec{g}_{*}-\vec{g})

\displaystyle\lesssim_{p,q,r,\gamma}\|\vec{f}_{*}-\vec{f}\|_{r}\|\vec{g}_{*}\|_{r^{\prime}}+\|\vec{f}\|_{r}\|\|\vec{g}_{*}-\vec{g}\|_{r^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon\left(2\|\vec{g}\|_{r^{\prime}}+\|\vec{f}\|_{r}\right).

We have now shown that

\displaystyle|\langle T\vec{f},\vec{g}\,\rangle|

\displaystyle\lesssim\varepsilon\,\|T\|_{r\to r}\left(\|\vec{g}\|_{r^{\prime}}+\|\vec{f}\|_{r}\right)+\Lambda_{p,q}^{\mathscr{Q}}(\vec{f}_{*},\vec{g}_{*})

\displaystyle\lesssim_{p,q,r,\gamma}\varepsilon\,\big(\|T\|_{r\to r}+1\big)\big(\|\vec{g}\|_{r^{\prime}}+\|\vec{f}\|_{r}\big)+\Lambda_{p,q}^{\mathscr{Q}}(\vec{f},\vec{g}).

Letting
\varepsilon\to 0
in the above inequality gives us

|\langle T\vec{f},\vec{g}\,\rangle|\lesssim_{p,q,r,\gamma}\Lambda_{p,q}^{\mathscr{Q}}(\vec{f},\vec{g})

and we are done.
∎

The above result lets us extend the results that we have proven in sections 4 and 5 for
V_{1}=S_{B_{1}}
,
V_{2}=S_{B_{2}^{*}}
to
L^{r}_{B_{1}}\times L^{r}_{B_{2}^{*}}
.

9.
Fourier multipliers

In this section we will note that certain Fourier multiplier operators satisfy the conditions
3.1
–
T6
and hence the convex body estimate of Theorem
3.3
is true for these operators. The proof is almost identical to the one in
[
1
]
Section 6.3 so we only give an outline of the proof. For more details see the proof in
[
1
]
.

Let
\mathcal{H}_{1}
and
\mathcal{H}_{2}
be two separable Hilbert spaces and denote the space of
(\mathcal{H}_{1},\mathcal{H}_{2})
-bounded linear operators by
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}_{1},\mathcal{H}_{2})
. We consider the multiplier operator
T=T_{m}
that maps
\mathcal{H}_{1}
-valued functions to
\mathcal{H}_{2}
-valued functions and is defined on the frequency side by

\widehat{Tf}(\xi)=m(\xi)\widehat{f}(\xi),

where
m
is an operator-valued function such that
m(\xi)\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}_{1},\mathcal{H}_{2})
for almost every
\xi
. For
1\leq p\leq\infty
we define the operator-valued
L^{p}
space

L^{p}_{\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}_{1},\mathcal{H}_{2})}\coloneqq\{m\,\colon\,mx\in L^{p}_{\mathcal{H}_{2}},\forall x\in\mathcal{H}_{1}\}

that has the norm

\|m\|_{L^{p}_{\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}_{1},\mathcal{H}_{2})}}\coloneqq\sup_{|x|_{\mathcal{H}_{1}}\leq 1}\|mx\|_{L^{p}_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}}.

Also for
1\leq p\leq q\leq\infty
we define
M^{p,q}_{\mathcal{H}_{1},\mathcal{H}_{2}}
to be the space of multipliers
m
that satisfy

\|Tf\|_{L^{q}_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{p}_{\mathcal{H}_{1}}}.

Furthermore, the optimal constant defines a norm in
M^{p,q}_{\mathcal{H}_{1},\mathcal{H}_{2}}
.

To see the multi-scale structure of
T
, we consider functions of the form

\Psi_{\ell}(x)\coloneqq\Psi_{0}(2^{-\ell}x)-\Psi_{0}(2^{-\ell+1}x),

where
\ell>0
and
\Psi_{0}\in C^{\infty}_{c}
with
\operatorname{supp}\Psi_{0}\subset\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\colon|x|<\frac{1}{2}\}
and
\Psi_{0}(x)=1
for
|x|<\frac{1}{4}
. Let
\theta\in C^{\infty}_{c}
such that
\operatorname{supp}\theta\subset\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\colon|x|<\frac{1}{2}\}
and
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\pi(x)\theta(x)\,\mathrm{d}x=0
for all polynomials
\pi
of degree at most
10d
. We also take a radial
C^{\infty}
function
\phi
that is supported in
\{\xi\in\widehat{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\,\colon\,\frac{1}{2}<|\xi|<2\}
and

\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\phi(2^{-k}\xi)\widehat{\theta}(2^{-k}\xi)=1.

For now we will assume that
m
is compactly supported in
\widehat{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\setminus\{0\}
. Then with
n_{1},n_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}
we can write

m(\xi)=\sum_{k=n_{1}}^{n_{2}}\phi(2^{-k}\xi)\widehat{\theta}(2^{-k}\xi)m(\xi)

and the multiplier operator can be decomposed into a multi-scale sum

Tf=\sum_{\ell\geq 0}\sum_{j=\ell+1-n_{2}}^{\ell+1-n_{1}}T_{j}^{\ell}f,

where
T^{\ell}_{j}
is a convolution operator with kernel
K_{\ell+1-j}^{\ell}
defined by

K_{k}^{\ell}(x)\coloneqq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathcal{F}^{-1}[\phi(2^{-k}\cdot)m](x-y)\Psi_{\ell}(2^{k}(x-y))2^{jd}\theta(2^{k}y)\,\mathrm{d}y,\quad k\in\mathbb{Z}.

We denote

\mathfrak{a}_{\circ,\ell}[m]\coloneqq\sup_{t>0}\|[\phi m(t\cdot)]*\widehat{\Psi}_{\ell}\|_{L^{\infty}_{\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}_{1},\mathcal{H}_{2})}},\quad B_{\circ}[m]\coloneqq\sum_{\ell\geq 0}\mathfrak{a}_{\circ,\ell}[m].

and

\mathfrak{a}_{\ell}[m]\coloneqq\sup_{t>0}\|[\phi m(t\cdot)]*\widehat{\Psi}_{\ell}\|_{M^{p,q}_{\mathcal{H}_{1},\mathcal{H}_{2}}}2^{\ell d\big(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}\big)},\quad\mathcal{B}[m]\coloneqq\sum_{\ell\geq 0}\mathfrak{a}_{\ell}[m](1+\ell).

It was shown in
[
1
]
Section 6.3 that for
\ell\geq 0
the operator

T^{\ell}\coloneqq\sum_{j=\ell+1-n_{2}}^{\ell+1-n_{1}}T_{j}^{\ell}

satisfies conditions
3.1
–
T6
with

A(p)=A(q)\eqsim_{d,p,q}\mathfrak{a}_{\circ,\ell}[m]+\mathfrak{a}_{\ell}[m](1+\ell),\quad A_{\circ}(p,q)\eqsim_{d,p,q}\mathfrak{a}_{\ell}[m]

and

B\eqsim_{d,p,q}2^{\kappa\ell}\mathfrak{a}_{\ell}[m].

Thus applying Theorem
3.3
and summing over
\ell\geq 0
we get that if
m
is compactly supported,
1<p\leq q<\infty
and

\mathcal{C}\coloneqq\mathcal{B}_{\circ}[m]+\mathcal{B}[m]<\infty,

then

\langle T\vec{f},\vec{g}\,\rangle\lesssim_{d,n,p,q,\gamma}\mathcal{C}\sum_{Q\in\mathscr{Q}}|Q|\,\llangle\vec{f}\rrangle_{\textit{\L}^{p}(Q,B_{1})}\cdot\llangle\vec{g}\rrangle_{\textit{\L}^{q^{\prime}}(Q,B_{2}^{*})}.

In
[
1
, p. 63-64]
it was shown how to remove the assumption that
m
is compactly supported in the scalar case and the same argument with minor obvious changes works also in the vector case. Thus if
1<p\leq q<\infty
and

B_{\circ}[m]+\mathcal{B}[m]<\infty,

then we have the
(\textit{\L}^{p}_{B_{1}},\textit{\L}^{q^{\prime}}_{B_{2}^{*}})
convex body domination bound (
9
) with the general multiplier
m
.

We refer to
[
1
, Chapters 6 and 7]
for many concrete examples of Fourier multiplier operators that satisfy (
9
). Their list includes Miyachi classes with subdyadic Hörmander conditions, prototypical versions of singular Radon transforms, multi-scale variants of oscillatory multipliers and certain radial multipliers. By (
9
), Corollary
6.10
and Corollary
7.5
all these operators and their commutators with BMO functions satisfy matrix-weighted norm inequalities.

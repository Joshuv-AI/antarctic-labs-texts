Poincaré Duality, Degeneracy, and Real Lefschetz Property for T-Hypersurfaces ————–
Jules Chenal

Abstract
In Bounding the Betti Numbers of Real Hypersurfaces near the Tropical Limit , [ RS23 ] , A. Renaudineau and K. Shaw presented a filtration of the cellular complex of a real, primitively patchworked, hypersurface in a non-singular toric variety. This filtration, inspired by the work of Kalinin, allows, by computation of the first page of its induced spectral sequence, for individual bounds on the Betti numbers of such a hypersurface. We present two structural results about this spectral sequence. The first is a Poincaré duality satisfied by all its pages of positive index. The second is a vanishing criterion. It reformulates the vanishing of the boundary operators of the spectral sequence in terms of homological properties of the inclusion of the hypersurface in its surrounding toric variety. It implies further that the Renaudineau-Shaw spectral sequence of a T-hypersurface degenerates at the second page if and only if the T-hypersurface satisfies a real version of the Lefschetz hyperplane section theorem. As a consequence the spectral sequence of an odd degree T-hypersurface in a projective space always degenerates at the second page.

Lemma 1.5

Definition 2.14

(Cup product)
.

Let
F
be a cubical sheaf of algebras over
\mathbb{F}_{2}
on a simplicial complex
K

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\cup\colon\Omega^{k}(K;F)\otimes_{\mathbb{F}_{2}}\Omega^{l}(K;F)&\longrightarrow&\Omega^{k+l}(K;F)\\

This description is a special case of D. Quillen’s description of graded rings associated with the augmentation filtration of a group algebras

. The proof of the special case of interest here is recalled in

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
V
be a finite dimensional vector space over
\mathbb{F}_{2}
. For all
v\in V
, the graded endomorphism of
\bigwedge V\cong\textnormal{gr}\,\mathbb{F}_{2}[V]
induced by the multiplication by
\textnormal{x}^{v}
is the identity.

Proof.

Let
v
be a vector of
V
and
v_{1},...,v_{k}\in V
be a linearly independent family of
V
, we have:

\prod_{i=1}^{k}(1+\textnormal{x}^{v_{i}})=\textnormal{x}^{v}\prod_{i=1}^{k}(1+\textnormal{x}^{v_{i}})+(1+\textnormal{x}^{v})\prod_{i=1}^{k}(1+\textnormal{x}^{v_{i}}).

Hence
\prod_{i=1}^{k}(1+\textnormal{x}^{v_{i}})
and
\textnormal{x}^{v}\prod_{i=1}^{k}(1+\textnormal{x}^{v_{i}})
correspond to the same element in
{\raisebox{1.00006pt}{$\mathfrak{m}_{V}^{k}$}\left/\raisebox{-1.00006pt}{$\mathfrak{m}_{V}^{k+1}$}\right.}
.
∎

Proposition 4.5
.

Let
V
be a finite dimensional vector space over
\mathbb{F}_{2}
. For all linear sub-spaces
W\subset V
and all
k\in\mathbb{N}
, the intersection of the sub-algebra
\mathbb{F}_{2}[W]
of
\mathbb{F}_{2}[V]
with
\mathfrak{m}_{V}^{k}
is the ideal
\mathfrak{m}_{W}^{k}
.

Proof.

This is a consequence of the functoriality of
\mathbb{F}_{2}[-]
and the natural transformation
\eta
described in Proposition
4.3
. However, we can also give a direct proof here. Using lemma 4.1 p.21 of
[
RS23
]
we know that a generating set of
\mathfrak{m}_{V}^{k}
is given by the elements
\sum_{u\in U}\textnormal{x}^{u}
where
U
is a
k
-linear sub-space of
V
. Now let
H
be a linear hyperplane of
V
and
P=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{u\in U_{i}}\textnormal{x}^{u}\in\mathbb{F}_{2}[H]\cap\mathfrak{m}_{V}^{k}
. We want to write this element as a sum
\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{u\in U^{\prime}_{i}}\textnormal{x}^{u}
where
U_{i}^{\prime}
is a linear sub-space of
H
. Since
P
belongs to
\mathbb{F}_{2}[H]
we have:

\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{u\in U_{i}\setminus H}\textnormal{x}^{u}=0.

Let
v
be the generator of a supplementary line of
H
. We define
U_{i}^{\prime}
as follows:

1.

If
U_{i}\subset H
, we set
U_{i}^{\prime}\coloneqq U_{i}
;

2.

If
U_{i}\not\subset H
and
(U_{i}\cap H)\neq\big(v+(U_{i}\setminus H)\big)
, we set
U_{i}^{\prime}\coloneqq(U_{i}\cap H)\cup\big(v+(U_{i}\setminus H)\big)
;

3.

Otherwise we set
U_{i}^{\prime}\coloneqq\varnothing
.

Note that in each case
U_{i}^{\prime}\subset H
and is ether a
k
-dimensional vector space or the empty set. Now we have:

\begin{split}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{u\in U_{i}}\textnormal{x}^{u}&=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{u\in U_{i}\cap H}\textnormal{x}^{u}+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{u\in U_{i}\setminus H}\textnormal{x}^{u}\\
&=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{u\in U_{i}\cap H}\textnormal{x}^{u}+\textnormal{x}^{v}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{u\in U_{i}\setminus H}\textnormal{x}^{u}\\
&=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left(\,\sum_{u\in U_{i}\cap H}\textnormal{x}^{u}+\sum_{u+v\in U_{i}\setminus H}\textnormal{x}^{u}\,\right)\\
&=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{u\in U_{i}^{\prime}}\textnormal{x}^{u}.\end{split}

Thus
\mathbb{F}_{2}[H]\cap\mathfrak{m}_{V}^{k}=\mathfrak{m}_{H}^{k}
. Let
W
be a sub-space of codimension
l\geq 1
and
H_{1},...,H_{l}
be hyperplanes that intersect along
W
. We have:

\begin{split}\mathbb{F}_{2}[W]\cap\mathfrak{m}_{V}^{k}&=\mathbb{F}_{2}[\cap_{i=1}^{l}H_{i}]\cap\mathbb{F}_{2}[\cap_{i=2}^{l}H_{i}]\cap...\cap\mathbb{F}_{2}[H_{l}]\cap\mathfrak{m}_{V}^{k}\\
&=\mathbb{F}_{2}[\cap_{i=1}^{l}H_{i}]\cap\mathbb{F}_{2}[\cap_{i=2}^{l}H_{i}]\cap...\cap\mathbb{F}_{2}[H_{l-1}\cap H_{l}]\cap\mathfrak{m}_{H_{l}}^{k}\\
&\hskip 5.0pt\vdots\\
&=\mathbb{F}_{2}[\cap_{i=1}^{l}H_{i}]\cap\mathfrak{m}^{k}_{\cap_{i=2}^{l}H_{i}}\\
&=\mathfrak{m}^{k}_{W},\end{split}

since
\cap_{i=k}^{l}H_{i}
is a hyperplane of
\cap_{i=k+1}^{l}H_{i}
for all
1\leq k\leq l
.
∎

In the category of finite dimensional Hopf algebras there is a duality contravariant functor. We denote it by
{(-)^{*}:\big(\mathbf{Hopf}^{\mathbf{f}}_{\mathbb{F}_{2}}\big)^{\textnormal{op}}\rightarrow\mathbf{Hopf}^{\mathbf{f}}_{\mathbb{F}_{2}}}
. If we only remember the vector space structure, it corresponds to the classical functor
(-)^{*}=\textnormal{Hom}_{\mathbb{F}_{2}}(-;\mathbb{F}_{2})
. If
A
is a finite dimensional Hopf algebra, the product of
A^{*}
is the adjoint of the coproduct of
A
, the unit of
A^{*}
is the adjoint of the counit of
A
and vice-versa.

Definition 4.6
.

We denote by
\mathcal{O}
the functor
{\big(\mathbb{F}_{2}[-]\big)^{*}:\big(\mathbf{Vect.}^{\mathbf{f}}_{\mathbb{F}_{2}}\big)^{\textnormal{op}}\rightarrow\mathbf{Hopf}^{\mathbf{f}}_{\mathbb{F}_{2}}}
. The functor
\mathcal{O}
is naturally isomorphic to the functor of
\mathbb{F}_{2}
-valued function algebras
{V\mapsto\{f:V\rightarrow\mathbb{F}_{2}\}}
with pointwise addition and multiplication. We will not describe the coalgebra structure as we will not need it further. The natural isomorphism is realised by means of a duality pairing. For all finite dimensional vector spaces
V
, all functions
f\in\mathcal{O}(V)
and all elements
P=\sum_{v\in V}p_{v}\textnormal{x}^{v}\in\mathbb{F}_{2}[V]
, we define such pairing by:

\langle\,f\,;P\,\rangle\coloneqq\sum_{v\in V}f(v)p_{v}.

Remark 4.7
.

We justify the notation
\mathcal{O}(V)
by the known fact that, for all finite dimensional vector spaces
V
over a finite field
\mathbb{F}
, every function
f:V\rightarrow\mathbb{F}
is polynomial. It is only a matter of expressing the indicatrix functions as polynomials in a set of coordinates of
V
.

Definition 4.8
.

The functor
\mathcal{O}^{(k)}:\big(\mathbf{Vect.}^{\mathbf{f}}_{\mathbb{F}_{2}}\big)^{\textnormal{op}}\rightarrow\mathbf{Vect.}^{\mathbf{f}}_{\mathbb{F}_{2}}
is given, for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
and all finite dimensional vector spaces
V
over
\mathbb{F}_{2}
, by the formula
32
32

32

c.f. Definition
1.3
or
[
Del71
]
(1.1.6) p.7.
:

\mathcal{O}^{(k)}(V)\coloneqq\textnormal{im}\bigg(\left({\raisebox{1.99997pt}{$\mathbb{F}_{2}[V]$}\left/\raisebox{-1.99997pt}{$\mathfrak{m}^{k+1}$}\right.}\right)^{*}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{O}(V)\bigg).

Furthermore, they are naturally included in one another
\mathcal{O}^{(k)}\rightarrow\mathcal{O}^{(l)}
, for all
l\geq k
. We denote by
\textnormal{gr}\,\mathcal{O}:\big(\mathbf{Vect.}^{\mathbf{f}}_{\mathbb{F}_{2}}\big)^{\textnormal{op}}\rightarrow\mathbf{gr.Vect.}^{\mathbf{f}}_{\mathbb{F}_{2}}
the functor
\bigoplus_{k\geq 0}{\raisebox{1.00006pt}{$\mathcal{O}^{(k)}$}\left/\raisebox{-1.00006pt}{$\mathcal{O}^{(k-1)}$}\right.}
.

By construction and Proposition
4.3
we have:

Proposition 4.9
.

The functor
\textnormal{gr}\,\mathcal{O}
is naturally isomorphic to
\bigwedge(-)^{*}
through
\eta^{*}
.

Since we did not define a product on
\textnormal{gr}\,\mathcal{O}
, we should emphasise that in Proposition
4.9
the two functors
\textnormal{gr}\,\mathcal{O}
and
\bigwedge(-)^{*}
are naturally isomorphic as functors to the category of graded vector spaces. However, Proposition

will ensure that we can endow
\textnormal{gr}\,\mathcal{O}
with a product compatible with
\eta^{*}
.

Lemma 4.10
.

For all functions
u:\{1,...,l\}\rightarrow\{1,...,k\}
, the number:

\sum_{\beta\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{k}}\prod_{j=1}^{l}\beta_{u(j)}\in\mathbb{F}_{2}\,,

equals
1
if and only if
u
is surjective.

Proof.

If
u
is onto the only non-zero term in the sum is the one for which
\beta_{i}=1
for all
i
. If on the contrary
u
is not surjective it misses an index
i_{0}
and we have:

\sum_{\beta\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{k}}\prod_{j=1}^{l}\beta_{u(j)}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\beta\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{k}\\
\beta_{i_{0}}=0\end{subarray}}\prod_{j=1}^{l}\beta_{u(j)}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\beta\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{k}\\
\beta_{i_{0}}=1\end{subarray}}\prod_{j=1}^{l}\beta_{u(j)}=0\,,

since the two terms are equals.
∎

Proposition 4.11
.

The composition
\mathcal{O}^{(k)}\otimes\mathcal{O}^{(l)}\rightarrow\mathcal{O}\otimes\mathcal{O}\rightarrow\mathcal{O}
where the first natural transformation is given by the tensor product of the inclusions and the second is given by the product of
\mathcal{O}
takes values in
\mathcal{O}^{(k+l)}
. In other words
\mathcal{O}^{(k)}\mathcal{O}^{(l)}\subset\mathcal{O}^{(k+l)}
.

Proof.

Let
V
be a finite dimensional vector space over
\mathbb{F}_{2}
. We need to show that for all
k,l\in\mathbb{N}
, all
f\in\mathcal{O}^{(k)}(V)
, and all
g\in\mathcal{O}^{(l)}(V)
, the product
fg
belongs to
\mathcal{O}^{(k+l)}(V)
. To do so we will show that a function is an element of
\mathcal{O}^{(k)}(V)
if and only if it can be represented by a polynomial of degree at most
k
(c.f. Remark
4.7
). The functions of
\mathcal{O}^{(k)}(V)
are those which vanish against elements of
\mathfrak{m}_{V}^{k+1}
. Following lemma 4.1 p.21 of
[
RS23
]
, the vector space
\mathfrak{m}_{V}^{k+1}
is generated by the elements
\sum_{v\in W}\textnormal{x}^{v}
for all linear sub-spaces
W
of
V
of dimension
(k+1)
. Such an element can be written as
\prod_{i=1}^{k+1}(1+\textnormal{x}^{w_{i}})
where
\{w_{1},...,w_{k+1}\}
is a basis of
W
. Let us fix a set of linear coordinates
x_{1},...,x_{n}
of
V
, i.e. a basis of
V^{*}
. Because of Fermat’s little theorem
33
33

33

a^{2}=a
for all
a\in\mathbb{F}_{2}
, a function
f\in\mathcal{O}(V)
can be uniquely written as follows:

f=\sum_{\alpha\in\{0,1\}^{n}}f_{\alpha}x^{\alpha},

in the usual multi-index notations:
x^{\alpha}\coloneqq\prod_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}^{\alpha_{i}}
and
|\alpha|\coloneqq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}
. For all integers
k\geq 1
, let us denote by
[k]
the integer interval
\{1,...,k\}
. Let
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
with
|\alpha|=l
and
W=\langle w_{1},...,w_{k}\rangle
be a
k
-dimensional sub-space of
V
. We have:

\begin{split}\left\langle x^{\alpha}\;;\prod_{i=1}^{k}(1+\textnormal{x}^{w_{i}})\right\rangle&=\sum_{w\in W}\langle x^{\alpha}\;;\;\textnormal{x}^{w}\rangle\\
&=\sum_{w\in W}\prod_{j=1}^{n}x_{j}(w)^{\alpha_{j}}\\
&=\sum_{\beta\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{k}}\prod_{j=1}^{n}\Bigg(x_{j}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}\beta_{i}w_{i}\right)\Bigg)^{\alpha_{j}}\\
&=\sum_{\beta\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{k}}\sum_{u:[n]\rightarrow[k]}\left(\,\prod_{j=1}^{n}\beta_{u(j)}^{\alpha_{j}}\,\right)\left(\,\prod_{j=1}^{n}\big(x_{j}(w_{u(j)})\big)^{\alpha_{j}}\,\right).\end{split}

Up to reordering we can assume that
\alpha_{i}=1
if and only if
1\leq i\leq l
, thus:

\begin{split}\sum_{\beta\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{k}}\sum_{u:[n]\rightarrow[k]}\left(\,\prod_{j=1}^{n}\beta_{u(j)}^{\alpha_{j}}\right)\left(\,\prod_{j=1}^{n}\big(x_{j}(w_{u(j)})\big)^{\alpha_{j}}\right)&=\sum_{\beta\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{k}}\sum_{u:[l]\rightarrow[k]}\left(\,\prod_{j=1}^{l}\beta_{u(j)}\right)\left(\,\prod_{j=1}^{l}x_{j}(w_{u(j)})\right)\\
&=\sum_{u:[l]\rightarrow[k]}\left(\,\sum_{\beta\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{k}}\prod_{j=1}^{l}\beta_{u(j)}\right)\left(\,\prod_{j=1}^{l}x_{j}(w_{u(j)})\right).\end{split}

By Lemma
4.10
, the number
\sum_{\beta\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{k}}\prod_{j=1}^{l}\beta_{u(j)}
equals
1
if and only if
u
is onto. Therefore, the number:

\left\langle x^{\alpha}\;;\prod_{i=1}^{k}(1+\textnormal{x}^{w_{i}})\right\rangle,

vanishes whenever
k
is bigger than
l
. Thus the polynomial functions of degree at most
k
belong to
\mathcal{O}^{(k)}(V)
. If
k
equals
l
, and
\mathfrak{S}_{k}
denotes the
k
-th symmetric group, the number:

\left\langle x^{\alpha}\;;\prod_{i=1}^{k}(1+\textnormal{x}^{w_{i}})\right\rangle=\sum_{u\in\mathfrak{S}_{k}}\prod_{j=1}^{l}x_{j}(w_{u(j)})\,,

is the minor of the matrix of the coordinates of
w_{1},...,w_{k}
in the dual basis of
x_{1},...,x_{n}
with the row set selected by the multi-index
\alpha
. As a consequence we can find, for all polynomials of degree
k
, a sub-space
W
of dimension
k
such that the evaluation of
f
, as a linear form, against the generator of
\mathfrak{m}^{k}_{V}
associated with
W
is
1
. It implies that
\{f\colon\deg f=k\}
is included in
\mathcal{O}^{(k)}(V)\setminus\mathcal{O}^{(k-1)}(V)
and that the two partitions of
\mathcal{O}(V)
, by the degree on one hand, and by the sets
\mathcal{O}^{(k)}(V)\setminus\mathcal{O}^{(k-1)}(V)
on the other hand, coincide.
∎

As a direct consequence of Proposition
4.11
we have:

Proposition 4.12
.

The functor
\mathcal{O}
together with the filtration
(\mathcal{O}^{(k)})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
takes values in the category of filtered finite dimensional algebras over
\mathbb{F}_{2}
, and therefore
\textnormal{gr}\,\mathcal{O}
takes values in the category of graded algebras.

Proposition 4.13
.

The natural isomorphism
\eta^{*}:\textnormal{gr}\,\mathcal{O}\rightarrow\bigwedge(-)^{*}
is compatible with the products.

Proof.

Let
V
be a finite dimensional vector space over
\mathbb{F}_{2}
and let
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Let us consider
f\in\mathcal{O}^{(k)}(V)
and express it in a system of linear coordinates
x_{1},...,x_{n}
:

f=\sum_{|\alpha|\leq k}f_{\alpha}x^{\alpha}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\subset\{1,..,n\}\\
|I|\leq k\end{subarray}}f_{I}\prod_{i\in I}x_{i}\,.

Using Lemma
4.10
, we find that, for all
v_{1}\wedge...\wedge v_{k}\in\bigwedge^{k}V
:

\begin{split}\Big\langle f\,\big(\textnormal{mod}\,\mathcal{O}^{(k-1)}(V)\big)\;;\;\eta_{V}^{k}(v_{1}\wedge...\wedge v_{k})\big\rangle&=\sum_{|I|=k}f_{I}\left\langle\prod_{i\in I}x_{i}\;;\prod_{j=1}^{k}(1+\textnormal{x}^{v_{j}})\right\rangle\\
&=\sum_{|I|=k}f_{I}\sum_{u\in\mathfrak{S}_{k}}\prod_{i=1}x_{I(i)}(v_{u(i)})\\
&=\sum_{|I|=k}f_{I}\left(\,\bigwedge_{i\in I}x_{i}\,\right)\left(\,\bigwedge_{i=1}^{n}v_{i}\,\right),\end{split}

where
I(i)
is the
i
-th element of

. The inverse of
(\eta_{V}^{k})^{*}
sends a wedge product of linear forms
\alpha_{1}\wedge...\wedge\alpha_{k}
to the polynomial function
\alpha_{1}...\alpha_{k}
modulo
\mathcal{O}^{(k-1)}(V)
. It respects the product structures.
∎

Proposition 4.15

Let

\mathbb{F}_{2}

∎

Corollary 4.28
.

If
{\mathbb{R}X_{\varepsilon}}
is even dimensional, i.e.
P
has odd dimension, then the total Betti number of
\mathbb{R}X_{\varepsilon}
satisfies:

\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\dim H_{i}({\mathbb{R}X_{\varepsilon}};\mathbb{F}_{2})=\sum_{p,q=0}^{n-1}\dim H_{p,q}(X;\mathbb{F}_{2})\;\;(\textnormal{mod}\,4)\,.

Proof.

By the Universal Coefficient Theorem
36
36

36

c.f.
[
CE56
]
Theorem 3.3a p.114.
and duality the statement of the corollary is equivalent to:

\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\dim H^{i}({\mathbb{R}X_{\varepsilon}};\mathbb{F}_{2})=\sum_{p,q=0}^{n-1}\dim H^{p,q}(X;\mathbb{F}_{2})\;\;(\textnormal{mod}\,4)\,.

Using the convergence of the spectral sequence
(E^{p,q}_{r}({\mathbb{R}X_{\varepsilon}}))_{p,q,r\geq 0}
and Theorem
4.26
we find that:

\begin{split}\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\dim H^{i}({\mathbb{R}X_{\varepsilon}};\mathbb{F}_{2})&=\sum_{p,q=0}^{n-1}\dim E_{1}^{p,q}({\mathbb{R}X_{\varepsilon}})-\sum_{r\geq 1}\sum_{p,q=0}^{n-1}2\,\textnormal{rk }\textnormal{d}_{r}^{p,q}\\

From Theorem

\textnormal{d}_{r}^{\frac{n+r}{2},\frac{n-r}{2}-1}

\textnormal{d}_{r}^{\frac{n+r}{2}-1,\frac{n+r}{2}-1}

Generalization of the basis theorem for the B B -type Coxeter groups
Sawsan Khaskeia, Robert Shwartz

Abstract
The O ​ G ​ S OGS for non-abelian groups is an interesting generalization of the basis of finite abelian groups. The definition of O ​ G ​ S OGS states that every element of a group has a unique presentation as a product of some powers of specific generators of the group, in a specific given order. In case of the symmetric groups S n S_{n} there is a paper of R. Shwartz, which demonstrates a strong connection between the O ​ G ​ S OGS and the standard Coxeter presentation of the symmetric group, which is called the standard O ​ G ​ S OGS of S n S_{n} . In this paper we generalize the standard O ​ G ​ S OGS of S n S_{n} to the finite classical Coxeter group B n B_{n} . We describe the exchange laws for the generalized standard O ​ G ​ S OGS of B n B_{n} , and we connect it to the Coxeter length and the descent set of B n B_{n} .

The fundamental theorem of finitely generated abelian groups states the following:
Let

a_{1},a_{2},\ldots a_{n}

i_{k}\in\mathbb{Z}
, where

has infinite order in

Where, the meaning of the theorem is that every abelian group

(where

In contrast to finitely generated abelian groups, the existence of an

norm(\pi)=s_{0}\cdot(s_{1}\cdot s_{0}\cdot s_{1})\cdot(s_{3}\cdot s_{2}\cdot s_{1}\cdot s_{0})\cdot(s_{4}\cdot s_{2}\cdot s_{2}\cdot s_{1}\cdot s_{0}\cdot s_{1}\cdot s_{2})\cdot(s_{5}\cdot s_{4}\cdot s_{3})

2.1
The generalized standard OGS of
B_{n}

In this subsection we generalize the definition of the standard OGS (defined in
[
9
]
) for the group
B_{n}
, and we show the arising exchange laws.

Now, similar to the definition of
t_{k}
in
S_{n}

[
9
]
, we define
\tau_{k}
in
B_{n}
for
k=1,2,\ldots,n
as follow:

Definition 2.1.1
.

For
k=1,2,\ldots,n
let
\tau_{k}
be :

\tau_{k}=\prod_{j=0}^{k-1}s_{j}

Remark 2.1.2
.

For every
1\leqslant k\leqslant n
,
\tau_{k}
satisfies the following properties:

\begin{array}[]{l}\tau_{k}(1)=-k\\
\tau_{k}(j)=j-1,\text{ for }2\leq j\leq k\\
\tau_{k}(j)=j,\text{ for }k+1\leq j\leq n\end{array}

Remark 2.1.3
.

\tau_{k}=s_{0}\cdot t_{k}.

Remark 2.1.4
.

For
1\leq k\leq n
, let
\tau_{k}
be an element of
B_{n}
as it is defined in Definition
2.1.1
, then the following holds:

•

For
i_{k}=-k

\tau_{k}^{i_{k}}(j)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-j&\text{ for }1\leq j\leq k\\
\\
j&\text{ for }k+1\leq j\leq n\end{array}\right.

•

For
0<i_{k}<k

\tau_{k}^{i_{k}}(j)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-(j-i_{k}+k)&\text{ for }1\leq j\leq i_{k}\\
\\
j-i_{k}&\text{ for }i_{k}+1\leq j\leq k\\
\\
j&\text{ for }k+1\leq j\leq n\end{array}\right.

•

For
-k<i_{k}<0

\tau_{k}^{i_{k}}(j)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}j-i_{k}&\text{ for }1\leq j\leq i_{k}+k\\
\\
-(j-i_{k}-k)&\text{ for }i_{k}+k+1\leq j\leq k\\
\\
j&\text{ for }k+1\leq j\leq n\end{array}\right.

Now, we define the generalized standard
OGS
for the group
B_{n}
as follow

Theorem 2.1.5
.

For
k=1,2,\ldots,n
let
\tau_{k}
be the elements of
B_{n}
as defined in Definition
2.1.1
then the following holds:

Every element
g\in B_{n}
has a unique presentation in the following form:

\tau_{1}^{i_{1}}\cdot\tau_{2}^{i_{2}}\cdots\tau_{n}^{i_{n}}

such that,

-k\leq i_{k}<k
.

Proof.

The proof is by induction on
n
. for
n=1
, it is easy to see that
B_{1}
is generated by
\tau_{1}
since
B_{1}
is a cyclic group of order
2
. Now, assume by induction that theorem holds for every
k
such that
k\leq n-1
. Denote by
\dot{B}_{n-1}
the parabolic subgroup of
B_{n}
generated by
s_{0},s_{1},\ldots s_{n-2}
. Easy to see that
\dot{B}_{n-1}
is isomorphic to
B_{n-1}
(which satisfy the theorem by the induction hypothesis). Notice, that in the permutation presentation of
B_{n}
, every element
x\in\dot{B}_{n-1}
satisfies
x(n)=n
. Now, consider the right cosets of
\dot{B}_{n-1}
in
B_{n}
. There are
2n
different right cosets, where every two elements
x
and
y
in the same right coset of
\dot{B}_{n-1}
in
B_{n}
satisfy
x(n)=y(n)
.
Now, notice that the powers of
\tau_{n}
satisfy the following properties:

•

For
0\leq i_{n}<n
,
\tau_{n}^{i_{n}}(n)=n-i_{n}
;

•

For
-n\leq i_{n}<0
,
\tau_{n}^{i_{n}}(n)=i_{n}
.

Hence, for
-n\leq i_{n}\leq n-1
, the elements
\tau_{n}^{i_{n}}
gives the
2n
different images of
n
in the permutation presentation. Hence, for
-n\leq i_{n}\leq n-1
, we have the following
2n
different right cosets of
\dot{B}_{n-1}
in
B_{n}
:

\dot{B}_{n-1}\tau_{n}^{i_{n}}.

Then the result of the theorem holds for
k=n
.
∎

Example 2.1.6
.

Consider the element
\pi=[-2;~-1;~-4;~-3]
of

First, notice that

i_{3}

Continuing by the same process, consider

i_{2}

Remark 2.1.7

Proposition 2.1.8

In order to transform the element

•

The case

\tau_{q}^{r_{q}}\cdot\tau_{p}^{r_{p}}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\tau_{r_{q}}^{-r_{q}}\cdot\tau_{r_{q}+r_{p}}^{r_{q}}\cdot\tau_{p+r_{q}}^{r_{p}}\cdot\tau_{q}^{r_{q}}&q-r_{q}\geq p\\
\\
\tau_{r_{q}}^{p-q}\cdot\tau_{r_{q}+r_{p}}^{q-p}\cdot\tau_{q}^{r_{q}+r_{p}}&r_{p}\leq q-r_{q}\leq p\\
\\
\tau_{p+r_{q}-q}^{r_{q}+r_{p}-q}\cdot\tau_{r_{q}}^{p-r_{p}-r_{q}}\cdot\tau_{q}^{r_{q}+r_{p}-p-q}&q-r_{q}\leq r_{p}\end{array}\right.

•

The case

\tau_{q}^{r_{q}}\cdot\tau_{p}^{r_{p}}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\tau_{r_{q}}^{-r_{q}}\cdot\tau_{p+r_{q}}^{-p-r_{q}}\cdot\tau_{q}^{r_{q}}&q-r_{q}\geq p\\
\\
\tau_{p+r_{q}-q}^{-p-r_{q}+q}\cdot\tau_{r_{q}}^{-r_{q}}\cdot\tau_{q}^{r_{q}-q}&q-r_{q}<p\end{array}\right.

•

The case
-p<r_{p}<0

\tau_{q}^{r_{q}}\cdot\tau_{p}^{r_{p}}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\tau_{r_{q}+r_{p}+p}^{r_{q}}\cdot\tau_{p+r_{q}}^{r_{p}-r_{q}}\cdot\tau_{q}^{r_{q}}&q-r_{q}\geq p\\
\\
\tau_{p+r_{q}-q}^{-p-r_{q}+q}\cdot\tau_{r_{q}}^{p+r_{q}-q}\cdot\tau_{r_{q}+r_{p}+p}^{q-p}\cdot\tau_{q}^{p+r_{p}-q+r_{q}}&r_{p}+p\leq q-r_{q}\leq p\\
\\
\tau_{p+r_{q}-q}^{r_{p}}\cdot\tau_{r_{q}}^{-r_{p}}\cdot\tau_{q}^{r_{q}+r_{p}}&q-r_{q}\leq r_{p}+p\end{array}\right.

Proof.

By Remark
2.1.3.

Then,

\tau_{q}^{r_{q}}\cdot\tau_{p}^{r_{p}}=(s_{0}\cdot t_{q})^{r_{q}}\cdot(s_{0}\cdot t_{p})^{r_{p}}

Similar to the exchange laws for the standard

1.0.4

1.

The case

r_{q}+1\rightarrow 1\rightarrow-(p-r_{p}+1).

r_{q}+r_{p}\rightarrow r_{p}\rightarrow-(p).

r_{q}+r_{p}\rightarrow r_{p}\rightarrow-(p+r_{q})\rightarrow-p.

r_{q}+r_{p}+1\rightarrow r_{q}+r_{p}+1\rightarrow r_{q}+1\rightarrow 1.

q\rightarrow q-r_{q}\rightarrow q-r_{q}.

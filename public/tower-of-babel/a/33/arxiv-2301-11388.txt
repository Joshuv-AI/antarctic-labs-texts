Perturbation Determinant and levinson’s formula for Schrödinger operators with generalized Point interaction
M. Fazeel Anwar, Muhammad Usman and Muhammad Danish Zia

Abstract
. We consider the one dimensional Schrödinger operator with properly connecting generalized point interaction at the origin. We derive a trace formula for trace of difference of resolvents of perturbed and unperturbed Schrödinger operators in terms of a Wronskian which results into an explicit expression for perturbation determinant. Using the estimate for large time real argument on the trace norm of the resolvent difference of the perturbed and unperturbed Schrödinger operators we express the spectral shift function in terms of perturbation determinant. Under certain integrability condition on the potential function, we calculate low energy asymptotics for the perturbation determinant and prove an analog of Levinson’s formula.

and

Using Krĕin’s resolvent formula (see

This trace formula allows us to derive an explicit expression for the perturbation determinant. The perturbation determinant is an interesting object in the spectral theory of Schrödinger operators, as the zeros of perturbation determinant coincide with the eigenvalues of the Schrödinger operator and the multiplicity of each eigenvalue is equal to the order of the corresponding zero of the perturbation determinant

in terms of its phase shift function. This formula, also called a zero order trace formula gives a relationship between the number of negative eigenvalues and the scattering data via phase shift function.

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{2\zeta L(\zeta)}\left(\left(a+b\dfrac{\theta_{2}^{\prime}(0)}{\theta_{2}(0)}\right)\dfrac{d}{d\zeta}\dfrac{\theta^{\prime}_{1}(0,\zeta)}{\theta_{1}(0,\zeta)}+\left(b\dfrac{\theta_{1}^{\prime}(0)}{\theta_{1}(0)}-d\right)\dfrac{d}{d\zeta}\dfrac{\theta^{\prime}_{2}(0,\zeta)}{\theta_{2}(0,\zeta)}\right)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{\dot{L}(\zeta)}{2\zeta L(\zeta)}.

(2.11)

By a density argument the above result can be extended to all potentials satisfying the condition
\int_{0}^{\infty}|V_{j}(x)|<\infty
.

Now it only remains to compute the last integral on the right hand side of (
2.8
), which is

\displaystyle\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\left(\dfrac{(a-d+2i\zeta b)}{(a-d)i\zeta-b\zeta^{2}+c}e^{2ix\zeta}\right)dx=\dfrac{(a-d+2i\zeta b)}{2\zeta((a-d)\zeta+(b\zeta^{2}+c)i)}.

(2.12)

Substituting values from (
2.9
), (
2.11
) and (
2.12
) in (
2.8
), we get

\displaystyle\Tr(R^{\mathcal{A}}_{V}(z)-R^{\mathcal{A}}_{0}(z))

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2\zeta}\left(\sum\limits_{j=1}^{2}\frac{\dot{w}_{j}(\zeta)}{w_{j}(\zeta)}+\dfrac{\dot{L}(\zeta)}{L(\zeta)}-\dfrac{(a-d+2i\zeta b)}{(a-d)\zeta+(b\zeta^{2}+c)i}\right)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2\zeta}\left(\sum\limits_{j=1}^{2}\frac{d}{d\zeta}\ln({w_{j}(\zeta)})+\frac{d}{d\zeta}\ln({L(\zeta)})-\frac{d}{d\zeta}\ln({(a-d)\zeta+(b\zeta^{2}+c)i})\right)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2\zeta}\left(\dfrac{d}{d\zeta}\ln\left(\dfrac{L(\zeta)}{(a-d)\zeta+(b\zeta^{2}+c)i}\prod\limits_{j=1}^{2}w_{j}(\zeta)\right)\right).

The second term on the right hand side of (
2.1
) with values of
\lambda_{j,l}
given by lemma (
2.1
) is a finite rank operator and if the potential function
V
satisfy condition (
1.2
) then the operator
\sqrt{|V|}(H^{\mathcal{A}}_{0})^{-1/2}
is Hilbert-Schmidt and therefore,
\sqrt{V}R_{0}^{\mathcal{A}}\sqrt{|V|}
is trace class. This implies that the perturbation determinant
D(z)
is well defined. The perturbation determinant and trace of the resolvent difference
R^{\mathcal{A}}_{V}(z)-R_{0}^{\mathcal{A}}(z)
are related by the following expression

\Tr(R^{\mathcal{A}}_{V}(z)-R_{0}^{\mathcal{A}}(z))=\frac{\frac{d}{dz}D(z)}{D(z)},\quad z\in\rho(H_{V}^{\mathcal{A}})\cap\rho(H_{0}^{\mathcal{A}}).

In order to find an explicit expression for the perturbation determinant we choose
\zeta=\sqrt{z}
such that Im
(z)>0
. It follows from Theorem
1.1
that

\displaystyle\dfrac{\dfrac{d}{dz}(D(z))}{D(z)}=\frac{\dfrac{d}{dz}\left(\dfrac{L(\sqrt{z})}{(a-d)\sqrt{z}+(bz+c)i}\prod\limits_{j=1}^{2}w_{j}(\sqrt{z})\right)}{\dfrac{L(\sqrt{z})}{(a-d)\sqrt{z}+(bz+c)i}\prod\limits_{j=1}^{2}w_{j}(\sqrt{z})}.

From here we deduce that

\displaystyle D(z)=A\dfrac{L(\sqrt{z})}{(a-d)\sqrt{z}+(bz+c)i}\prod\limits_{j=1}^{2}w_{j}(\sqrt{z}),\hskip 14.22636ptA\in\mathbb{C}.

To find the value of the complex coefficient

, we use the following asymptotic of perturbation determinant

\displaystyle\lim_{|\text{Im}(z)|\rightarrow\infty}D(z)=1.

This asymptotic holds if
\sqrt{|V|R^{\mathcal{A}}_{0}(z)^{-1}}
is Hilbert-Schmidt operator (see
[
23
]
). For the asymptotic behaviour of
w_{j}(\sqrt{z})
as
|\sqrt{z}|\rightarrow\infty
, we refer to
[
6
]
, which provides

\displaystyle w_{j}(\sqrt{z})=\theta_{j}(0,\sqrt{z})=1+O(|\sqrt{z}|^{-1}).

Further, it is easy to find that

\displaystyle L(\sqrt{z})=(a-d)\sqrt{z}i-(bz+c)+O(1),\quad|\sqrt{z}|\rightarrow\infty.

(2.13)

This implies,
A=\dfrac{1}{i}
.

3.
Levinson’s formula

In this section we derive a zero order trace formula, commonly known as Levinson’s formula, which describes the number of negative eigenvalues of an operator in terms of phase shift function which is denoted by
\eta
and it is defined, for real
k
, as
\eta(k):=\arg D(k)
. The Levinson’s formula derived in this section is closely related to the Levinson’s formulas obtained in Theorem 9.3 of
[
3
]
and Theorem 9.3 of
[
21
]
. Levinson’s formula obtained in
[
3
]
relates the number of negative eigenvalues with the determinant of the scattering matrix and certain parameters that depend on the boundary conditions. Formula obtained in
[
21
]
relates the number of negative eigenvalues with the spectral shift function. The vertex conditions of this paper can be expressed as a particular case of more general conditions considered in references
[
3
]
and
[
21
]
and therefore the Levinson’s formulas obtained there also hold for the operator
H_{V}^{\mathcal{A}}
. For higher order trace formulas of integer and half-integer order we refer to
[
23
,
22
,
19
]
.

We will need the following low-energy asymptotics of the perturbation determinant

Lemma 3.1

(Low energy asymptotics)
.

Let the potential
V
satisfies the condition

\displaystyle\int\limits_{e_{j}}(1+x)|V_{j}(x)|dx<\infty,\hskip 8.5359pt1\leq j\leq n.

(3.1)

Then as
\zeta\rightarrow 0
the perturbation determinant satisfies

D(\zeta)=\begin{cases}\zeta^{(Q-P)}(1+o(1))&w_{j}(0)\neq 0$, $j=1,2,\\
\zeta^{(R-P)}(1+o(1))&w_{1}(0)=0,w_{2}(0)\neq 0,\\
\zeta^{(S-P)}(1+o(1))&w_{1}(0)\neq 0,w_{2}(0)=0,\\
\zeta^{(T-P)}(1+o(1))&w_{j}(0)=0$, $j=1,2.\end{cases}

Where the functions
P,Q,R,S
and
T
are defined in Theorem (
1.2
).

Proof.

Expression for perturbation determinant is

\displaystyle D(\zeta)=\dfrac{L(\zeta)\prod\limits_{j=1}^{2}w_{j}(\zeta)}{(a-d)i\zeta-(b\zeta^{2}+c)}.

We consider the following five different cases

(1)

w_{j}(0)\neq 0
,
j=1,2
and
L(0)\neq 0
.

(2)

w_{j}(0)\neq 0
,
j=1,2
and
L(0)=0
.

(3)

w_{1}(0)=0
and
w_{2}(0)\neq 0
.

(4)

w_{1}(0)\neq 0
and
w_{2}(0)=0
.

(5)

w_{j}(0)=0
,
j=1,2
.

In the first case
D(\zeta)
has no zeros and we can let

\displaystyle L(0)\prod\limits_{j=1}^{2}w_{j}(0)=\tilde{c}\neq 0

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\lim\limits_{\zeta\rightarrow 0}D(\zeta)=\lim\limits_{\zeta\rightarrow 0}\dfrac{\tilde{c}}{(a-d)i\zeta-(b\zeta^{2}+c)}

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\lim\limits_{\zeta\rightarrow 0}D(\zeta)((a-d)i\zeta-(b\zeta^{2}+c))=\tilde{c}

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\lim\limits_{\zeta\rightarrow 0}\dfrac{D(\zeta)((a-d)i\zeta-(b\zeta^{2}+c))-\tilde{c}}{\tilde{c}}=0

\displaystyle\Rightarrow D(\zeta)=\frac{\tilde{c}}{(a-d)i\zeta-(b\zeta^{2}+c)}(1+o(1)).

In the second case, we make use of the following small energy asymptotics (c.f. Theorem 2.3 (iii)of
[
2
]
)

\displaystyle\dfrac{\theta_{j}^{\prime}(0,\zeta)}{\theta_{j}(0,\zeta)}=\dfrac{\theta_{j}^{\prime}(0,0)}{\theta_{j}(0,0)}-\dfrac{i\zeta}{\theta_{j}^{2}(0,0)}+o(\zeta)

which implies

\displaystyle L(\zeta)=

\displaystyle a\left(\frac{\theta_{1}^{\prime}(0,0)}{\theta_{1}(0,0)}-\frac{i\zeta}{\theta_{1}^{2}(0,0)}+o(\zeta)\right)-d\left(\frac{\theta_{2}^{\prime}(0,0)}{\theta_{2}(0,0)}-\frac{i\zeta}{\theta_{2}^{2}(0,0)}+o(\zeta)\right)

\displaystyle+b\left(\frac{\theta_{1}^{\prime}(0,0)}{\theta_{1}(0,0)}-\frac{i\zeta}{\theta_{1}^{2}(0,0)}+o(\zeta)\right)\left(\frac{\theta_{2}^{\prime}(0,0)}{\theta_{2}(0,0)}-\frac{i\zeta}{\theta_{2}^{2}(0,0)}+o(\zeta)\right)+c

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a\frac{\theta_{1}^{\prime}(0,0)}{\theta_{1}(0,0)}-d\frac{\theta_{2}^{\prime}(0,0)}{\theta_{2}(0,0)}+b\frac{\theta_{1}^{\prime}(0,0)}{\theta_{1}(0,0)}\frac{\theta_{2}^{\prime}(0,0)}{\theta_{2}(0,0)}-c+a\left(\frac{-i\zeta}{\theta_{1}^{2}(0,0)}+o(\zeta)\right)

\displaystyle-d\left(\frac{-i\zeta}{\theta_{2}^{2}(0,0)}+o(\zeta)\right)+b\left[\left(\frac{\theta_{1}^{\prime}(0,0)}{\theta_{1}(0,0)\theta_{2}^{2}(0,0)}+\frac{\theta_{2}^{\prime}(0,0)}{\theta_{1}^{2}(0,0)\theta_{2}(0,0)}\right)i\zeta\right]

\displaystyle+b\left(o(\zeta)+o(\zeta^{2})-\frac{\zeta^{2}}{\theta_{1}(0,0)\theta_{2}(0,0)}\right)

This yields

\displaystyle L(\zeta)=

\displaystyle L(0)+a\left(\frac{-i\zeta}{\theta_{1}^{2}(0,0)}+o(\zeta)\right)-d\left(\frac{-i\zeta}{\theta_{2}^{2}(0,0)}+o(\zeta)\right)

\displaystyle+b\left[\left(\frac{\theta_{1}^{\prime}(0,0)}{\theta_{1}(0,0)\theta_{2}^{2}(0,0)}+\frac{\theta_{2}^{\prime}(0,0)}{\theta_{1}^{2}(0,0)\theta_{2}(0,0)}\right)i\zeta+o(\zeta)+o(\zeta^{2})-\frac{\zeta^{2}}{\theta_{1}(0,0)\theta_{2}(0,0)}\right].

Therefore,

\displaystyle D(\zeta)=\dfrac{\alpha_{1}\zeta+o(\zeta)+b(\zeta^{2}+(o(\zeta^{2})))}{(a-d)i\zeta-(b\zeta^{2}+c)},

where,
\alpha_{1}=\left(b\left(\dfrac{\theta_{1}^{\prime}(0,0)}{\theta_{2}(0,0)}+\dfrac{\theta_{2}^{\prime}(0,0)}{\theta_{1}(0,0)}\right)-a\dfrac{\theta_{2}(0,0)}{\theta_{1}(0,0)}+d\dfrac{\theta_{1}(0,0)}{\theta_{2}(0,0)}\right)i
.

The above calculations show that the order of the numerator of
D(\zeta)
depends on the value of the parameter
b
. If
b=0
, then it will be of order
\zeta
otherwise it will be of order
\zeta^{2}
.

The study of first two cases can be summarized as follows

•

If
w_{j}(0)\neq 0
,
j=1,2
and
L(0)\neq 0
then the numerator of
D(\zeta)
is a non zero constant and it will have no zeros.

•

If
w_{j}(0)\neq 0
,
j=1,2
and
L(0)=0
then the numerator of
D(\zeta)
for small
\zeta
will have one zero in case
\alpha_{1}\neq 0
and it will have two zeros in case
\alpha_{1}=0
,
b\neq 0
.

This gives us the following low energy asymptotics in the first two cases

\displaystyle D(\zeta)=\zeta^{(Q-P)}(1+o(1)),

where

Q=Q(b,\alpha_{1},L(0))=\begin{cases}0&L(0)\neq 0,\\
1&L(0)=0,\alpha_{1}\neq 0\\
2&L(0)=0,\alpha_{1}=0,b\neq 0\end{cases}

and

P=P(a,b,c,d)=\begin{cases}0&c\neq 0,\\
1&c=0,a-d\neq 0\\
2&c=0,a-d=0,b\neq 0.\end{cases}

The function
P
gives the number of poles of
D(\zeta)
in the given cases.

Let us now consider the third case, i.e., when
w_{1}(0)=0
and
w_{2}(0)\neq 0
. We rewrite
D(\zeta)
as

\displaystyle D(\zeta)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{\left(a\dfrac{\theta_{1}^{\prime}(0,\zeta)}{\theta_{1}(0,\zeta)}-d\dfrac{\theta_{2}^{\prime}(0,\zeta)}{\theta_{2}(0,\zeta)}+b\prod\limits_{k=1}^{2}\dfrac{\theta_{k}^{\prime}(0,\zeta)}{\theta_{k}(0,\zeta)}-c\right)\prod\limits_{j=1}^{2}\theta_{j}(0,\zeta)}{(a-d)i\zeta-(b\zeta^{2}+c)}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{\left(a\theta_{1}^{\prime}(0,\zeta)\theta_{2}(0,\zeta)+b\theta_{1}^{\prime}(0,\zeta)\theta_{2}^{\prime}(0,\zeta)-d\theta_{1}(0,\zeta)\theta_{2}^{\prime}(0,\zeta)-c\prod\limits_{j=1}^{2}\theta_{j}(0,\zeta)\right)}{(a-d)i\zeta-(b\zeta^{2}+c)}

(3.2)

and use the following asymptotic for
\theta_{1}(x,\zeta)
(c.f.
[
4
]
)

\displaystyle\theta_{1}(0,\zeta)=\zeta\dot{\theta}_{1}(0,0)+o(\zeta).

This implies

\displaystyle D(\zeta)=\dfrac{\left(\alpha_{2}-c_{3}\zeta+o(\zeta)\right)}{(a-d)i\zeta-(b\zeta^{2}+c)}

Where
\alpha_{2}=a\theta_{1}^{\prime}(0,0)\theta_{2}(0,0)+b\theta_{1}^{\prime}(0,0)\theta_{2}^{\prime}(0,0)
and
c_{3}=(c\theta_{2}(0,0)+d\theta_{2}^{\prime}(0,0))\dot{\theta}_{1}(0,0)
.
The numerator of
D(\zeta)
is equal to
\alpha_{2}-c_{3}\zeta+o(\zeta)
as
\zeta\rightarrow 0
.
If
\alpha_{2}\neq 0
then the numerator of
D(\zeta)
has no zeros for small
\zeta
and if
\alpha_{2}=0
, then the numerator of
D(\zeta)
has one zero for small
\zeta
. Hence,

\displaystyle D(\zeta)=\zeta^{(R-P)}(1+o(1)),\quad\zeta\rightarrow 0,

where,

R=R(\alpha_{2})=\begin{cases}0&\alpha_{2}\neq 0,\\
1&\alpha_{2}=0.\end{cases}

For the fourth case we substitute

\displaystyle\theta_{2}(0,\zeta)=\zeta\dot{\theta}_{2}(0,0)+o(\zeta),\zeta\rightarrow 0

into (
3.2
) and obtain

\displaystyle D(\zeta)=\dfrac{\left(\alpha_{3}+c_{5}\zeta+o(\zeta)\right)}{(a-d)i\zeta-(b\zeta^{2}+c)}.

Here,
\alpha_{3}=b\theta_{1}^{\prime}(0,0)\theta_{2}^{\prime}(0,0)-d\theta_{1}(0,0)\theta_{2}^{\prime}(0,0)
and
c_{5}=(a\theta_{1}^{\prime}(0,0)-c\theta_{1}(0,0))\dot{\theta}_{2}(0,0)
. The numerator of
D(\zeta)
for small
\zeta
has no zeros if
\alpha_{3}\neq 0
and it has one zero if
\alpha_{3}=0
.
Therefore,

\displaystyle D(\zeta)=\zeta^{(S-P)}(1+o(1)),\quad\zeta\rightarrow 0,

where,

S=S(\alpha_{3})=\begin{cases}0&\alpha_{3}\neq 0,\\
1&\alpha_{3}=0.\end{cases}

Finally, for the fifth case we substitute the asymptotics

\displaystyle\theta_{1}(0,\zeta)=\zeta\dot{\theta}_{1}(0,0)+o(\zeta)\text{ and }\theta_{2}(0,\zeta)=\zeta\dot{\theta}_{2}(0,0)+o(\zeta),\quad\zeta\rightarrow 0

into (
3.2
) and obtain

\displaystyle D(\zeta)=\dfrac{c\left(c_{6}\zeta^{2}+o(\zeta^{2})\right)+\alpha_{4}\zeta+bc_{8}+o(\zeta)}{(a-d)i\zeta-(b\zeta^{2}+c)},\quad\zeta\rightarrow 0.

Here,
c_{6}=\dot{\theta}_{1}(0,0)\dot{\theta}_{2}(0,0)\neq 0
,
c_{8}=\theta_{1}^{\prime}(0,0)\theta_{2}^{\prime}(0,0)\neq 0
(because
\theta(0,0)=0\Rightarrow\dot{\theta}(0,0)\theta^{\prime}(0,0)=-i

, hence
\dot{\theta}(0,0)\neq 0
and
\theta^{\prime}(0,0)\neq 0
)
\alpha_{4}=a\theta_{1}^{\prime}(0,0)\dot{\theta}_{2}(0,0)-d\dot{\theta}_{1}(0,0)\theta_{2}^{\prime}(0,0)
.
This implies the numerator of
D(\zeta)
for small
\zeta
has no zeros if
b\neq 0
, it has one zero if
b=0,\alpha_{4}\neq 0
and it has two zeros if
b=0
,
\alpha_{4}=0
,

.
This yields

\displaystyle D(\zeta)=\zeta^{(T-P)}(1+o(1)),\quad\zeta\rightarrow 0,

where

T=T(b,c,\alpha_{4})=\begin{cases}0&b\neq 0,\\
1&b=0,\alpha_{4}\neq 0\\

∎

Using the low energy asymptotics of
D(\zeta)
we now prove the analogue of Levinson’s formula for the negative eigenvalues of
H_{V}^{\mathcal{A}}
, stated in Theorem (
1.2
).

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

The function
D(\zeta)
has a zero in
\zeta
of order
r
if and only if
\zeta^{2}
is an eigenvalue of multiplicity
r
of the operator
H_{V}^{\mathcal{A}}

[
3
]
. As
H_{V}^{\mathcal{A}}
is a self-adjoint operator, so the zeros of
D(\zeta)
lie on the positive imaginary axis and it may have only real eigenvalues.

Let
\Gamma_{R,\varepsilon}
denotes the contour (counterclockwise) consisting of semicircles
C^{+}_{R}=\{|\zeta|=R,\quad\mbox{Im }\zeta\geq 0\}
and
C^{+}_{\varepsilon}=\{|\zeta|=\varepsilon,\quad\mbox{Im }\zeta\geq 0\}
and the intervals
(\varepsilon,R)
and
(-R,-\varepsilon)
.

R
and
\varepsilon
are chosen such that all
N
negative eigenvalues lie inside the contour
\Gamma_{R,\varepsilon}
.

The function
w_{j}(\zeta)
is analytic in the upper halfp-plane, therefore,
D(\zeta)
is analytic inside and on the contour
\Gamma_{R,\varepsilon}
. Hence,

\displaystyle\int\limits_{\Gamma_{R,\varepsilon}}\dfrac{\frac{d}{d\zeta}D(\zeta)}{D(\zeta)}d\zeta=2\pi iN.

(3.3)

Note that,

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{\mbox{Im }\zeta\rightarrow\infty}D(\zeta)=\lim\limits_{\mbox{Im }\zeta\rightarrow\infty}\dfrac{L(\zeta)\prod\limits_{j=1}^{2}w_{j}(\zeta)}{(a-d)i\zeta-(b\zeta^{2}+c)}=1+O(|\zeta|^{-1}).

(3.4)

The branch of the function
\ln D(\zeta)
can be fixed by the condition
\ln D(\zeta)\rightarrow 0
as
|\zeta|\rightarrow\infty
.
For
k\in\mathbb{R}
, we set
D(k)=a(k)e^{i\eta(k)}
, where
a(k)=|D(k)|
and
\eta(k)=\arg D(k)
.
Now

\displaystyle\mbox{var}_{\Gamma_{R,\varepsilon}}\arg D(\zeta)=\eta(R)-\eta(\varepsilon)+\mbox{var}_{C_{R}^{+}}\arg D(\zeta)+\{\eta(-\varepsilon)-\eta(-R)\}+\mbox{var}_{C_{\varepsilon}^{+}}\arg D(\zeta).

It follows from the representation of
D(k)
that
\eta(-k)=-\eta(k)
. Hence,

\displaystyle\mbox{var}_{\Gamma_{R,\varepsilon}}\arg D(\zeta)=2(\eta(R)-\eta(\varepsilon))+\mbox{var}_{C_{R}^{+}}\arg D(\zeta)+\mbox{var}_{C_{\varepsilon}^{+}}\arg D(\zeta)

Equation (
3.3
) implies that

\displaystyle\mbox{var}_{\Gamma_{R,\varepsilon}}\arg D(\zeta)=2\pi N.

This implies

\displaystyle 2\pi N=2(\eta(R)-\eta(\varepsilon))+\mbox{var}_{C_{R}^{+}}\arg D(\zeta)+\mbox{var}_{C_{\varepsilon}^{+}}\arg D(\zeta).

Using (
3.4
), one can see that
\lim\limits_{R\rightarrow\infty}\mbox{var}_{C_{R}^{+}}\arg D(\zeta)=0
. We are left with

\displaystyle\eta(\infty)-\eta(\varepsilon)=\pi N-\dfrac{1}{2}\mbox{var}_{C_{\varepsilon}^{+}}\arg D(\zeta).

Now letting
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
and using Lemma (
3.1
), we arrive at

\lim\limits_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\mbox{var}_{C_{\varepsilon}^{+}}\arg D(\zeta)=\begin{cases}-(Q-P)\pi&w_{j}(0)\neq 0$, $j=1,2,\\
-(R-P)\pi&w_{1}(0)=0,w_{2}(0)\neq 0,\\
-(S-P)\pi&w_{1}(0)\neq 0,w_{2}(0)=0,\\
-(T-P)\pi&w_{j}(0)=0$, $j=1,2.\end{cases}

Therefore,

\eta(\infty)-\eta(0)=\begin{cases}\pi(N+\dfrac{Q-P}{2})&w_{j}(0)\neq 0$, $j=1,2,\\
\pi(N+\dfrac{R-P}{2})&w_{1}(0)=0,w_{2}(0)\neq 0,\\
\pi(N+\dfrac{S-P}{2})&w_{1}(0)\neq 0,w_{2}(0)=0,\\
\pi(N+\dfrac{T-P}{2})&w_{j}(0)=0$, $j=1,2.\end{cases}

∎

5.
conclusions

The Schrödinger operators on the real-line with generalized point interaction at zero can be imagined as a
2\times 2
matrix-valued Schrödinger operator on semi-axis with general self-adjoint conditions at zero.
Scattering theory of matrix-valued Schrödinger operators
on semi-axis with general self-adjoint conditions at zero has been studied in much detail
in the last few years. On the other hand, explicit derivation of perturbation determinants and its connections with spectral shift function and Levinson’s formulas has received relatively less attention. The present article is devoted to explicit
derivation of perturbation determinant via trace formula for Schrödinger operators and expressing Levinson’s formula and spectral shift function in terms of perturbation determinant. Our results complement the previous studies in this regard. We used operator theoretic approach in our derivations similar to Demirel’s work which deals
with star graphs with Kirchhoff conditions at the vertex. The
expressions for perturbation determinants are further used to derive Levinson’s formulas connecting
the number of negative eigenvalues with phase shift. Phase shift is defined in terms of perturbation
determinant. Looking forward, it would be interesting to extend and generalize the results on perturbation determinant to
matrix-valued Schrödinger operators with most general self-adjoint conditions at zero.

Appendix A
Estimate on trace norm of resolvent difference

Here we’ll provide and explicit derivation of the estimate

\displaystyle||R_{V}^{\mathcal{A}}(-t)-R_{0}^{\mathcal{A}}(-t)||_{1}\leq C_{\varepsilon}t^{-\frac{3}{2}+\varepsilon}

for all
\varepsilon>0
and large
t
. We will need the following lemma.

Lemma A.1

(
[
5
]
, Lemma 3.4 )
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a Hilbert space and
f,g\in\mathcal{H}
. Assume that
\mathcal{R}=(\cdot,\,f)f-(\cdot,\,g)g

is an operator of rank two on
\mathcal{H}
. Then, the trace norm of
\mathcal{R}
is given by

\displaystyle||\mathcal{R}||_{1}=\sqrt{(||f||^{2}+||g||^{2})^{2}-4|(f,g)|^{2}}.

(A.1)

Moreover, if we let
g=f+h
then,

\displaystyle(||f||^{2}+||g||^{2})^{2}-4|(f,g)|^{2}\leq 6||h||^{2}||f||^{2}+3||h||^{4}.

(A.2)

Lemma A.2
.

Assume that the potential
V
satisfies condition (
1.2
). Then the resolvents
R_{V}^{\mathcal{A}}
of
H_{V}^{\mathcal{A}}
and
R_{0}^{\mathcal{A}}
of
H_{0}^{\mathcal{A}}
satisfy, for all
\varepsilon>0
and for large
t,

\displaystyle||R_{V}^{\mathcal{A}}(-t)-R_{0}^{\mathcal{A}}(-t)||_{1}\leq C_{\varepsilon}t^{-\frac{3}{2}+\varepsilon}.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{R}_{1}=R^{\mathcal{A}}_{V}(-t)-R^{D}_{V}(-t)+R^{D}_{0}(-t)-R^{\mathcal{A}}_{0}(-t)
and
\mathcal{R}_{2}=R^{D}_{V}(-t)-R^{D}_{0}(-t)
then,

\displaystyle R^{\mathcal{A}}_{V}(-t)-R^{\mathcal{A}}_{0}(-t)=\mathcal{R}_{1}+\mathcal{R}_{2}.

Hence

\displaystyle||R^{\mathcal{A}}_{V}(-t)-R^{\mathcal{A}}_{0}(-t)||_{1}\leq||\mathcal{R}_{1}||_{1}+||\mathcal{R}_{2}||_{1}.

(A.3)

Trace norm on
\mathcal{R}_{2}
can be estimated as
||\mathcal{R}_{2}||_{1}\leq ct^{-\frac{3}{2}+\varepsilon}
(c.f. Lemma 4.5.6.
[
23
]
). We only need to find estimate on the trace norm of rank two operator
\mathcal{R}_{1}
. By adding and subtracting the quantity

\displaystyle\dfrac{\theta_{j}^{2}(x_{j},i\sqrt{t})}{L(i\sqrt{t})\theta_{j}^{2}(0,i\sqrt{t})}+\dfrac{e^{-2x_{j}\sqrt{t}}}{(d-a)\sqrt{t}+bt-c}\hskip 28.45274ptj=1,2

in the diagonal of
\mathcal{R}_{1}
we can re-write it as

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{1}=\mathcal{S}_{1}+\mathcal{S}_{2},

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{1}=\begin{bmatrix}\frac{(a-\sqrt{t}b+1)e^{-2x_{1}\sqrt{t}}}{(d-a)\sqrt{t}+bt-c}-\frac{\left(a+b\frac{\theta_{2}^{\prime}(0,i\sqrt{t})}{\theta_{2}(0,i\sqrt{t})}+1\right)\theta_{1}^{2}(x_{1},i\sqrt{t})}{L(i\sqrt{t})\theta_{1}^{2}(0,i\sqrt{t})}&0\\
0&\frac{(1-\sqrt{t}b-d)e^{-2x_{2}\sqrt{t}}}{(d-a)\sqrt{t}+bt-c}-\frac{\left(b\frac{\theta_{1}^{\prime}(0,i\sqrt{t})}{\theta_{1}(0,i\sqrt{t})}-d+1\right)\theta_{2}^{2}(x_{2},i\sqrt{t})}{L(i\sqrt{t})\theta_{2}^{2}(0,i\sqrt{t})}\end{bmatrix}

and

\displaystyle(\mathcal{S}_{2})_{j,k}=\begin{bmatrix}\frac{\theta_{j}(x_{j},i\sqrt{t})\theta_{k}(x_{k},i\sqrt{t})}{L(i\sqrt{t})\theta_{j}(0,i\sqrt{t})\theta_{k}(0,i\sqrt{t})}-\frac{e^{-(x_{j}+x_{k})\sqrt{t}}}{(d-a)\sqrt{t}+bt-c}\end{bmatrix}\hskip 28.45274ptj,k=1,2.

Now, as
\mathcal{S}_{1}
is a diagonal matrix and in order to apply definition of trace norm, we can let
\mathcal{S}_{1}=I\mathcal{S}_{1}I
, where

is second order identity matrix. This implies,

\displaystyle||\mathcal{S}_{1}||_{1}=|\sigma_{1}|+|\sigma_{2}|

where

\displaystyle|\sigma_{1}|=\int_{0}^{\infty}\left|\frac{(a-\sqrt{t}b+1)e^{-2x_{1}\sqrt{t}}}{(d-a)\sqrt{t}+bt-c}-\frac{\left(a+b\frac{\theta_{2}^{\prime}(0,i\sqrt{t})}{\theta_{2}(0,i\sqrt{t})}+1\right)\theta_{1}^{2}(x_{1},i\sqrt{t})}{L(i\sqrt{t})\theta_{1}^{2}(0,i\sqrt{t})}\right|dx_{1}

\displaystyle|\sigma_{2}|=\int_{0}^{\infty}\left|\frac{(1-\sqrt{t}b-d)e^{-2x_{2}\sqrt{t}}}{(d-a)\sqrt{t}+bt-c}-\frac{\left(b\frac{\theta_{1}^{\prime}(0,i\sqrt{t})}{\theta_{1}(0,i\sqrt{t})}-d+1\right)\theta_{2}^{2}(x_{2},i\sqrt{t})}{L(i\sqrt{t})\theta_{2}^{2}(0,i\sqrt{t})}\right|dx_{2}.

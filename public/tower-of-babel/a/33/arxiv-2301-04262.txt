Rational singularities and q q -birational morphisms
Donghyeon Kim

Abstract
. In this paper, we generalize the notion of rational singularities for any reflexive sheaf of rank 1 1 , link our notion of rational singularities with the notion of rational singularities in [ Kov11 ] , and prove generalizations of standard facts about rational singularities. Moreover, by using a definition of non-rational locus, we introduce the notion of ( B q + 1 ) (B_{q+1}) as a dual notion of well-known Serre’s notion of ( S q + 1 ) (S_{q+1}) , and prove a theorem about q q -birational morphisms.

Introduction

In this paper,
q\geq 2
is an integer, and
k
is any algebraically closed field of arbitrary characteristic. Any variety is quasi-projective, separated, finite type and integral scheme over
k
. Any divisor is assumed to be Cartier unless otherwise stated.

Throughout the paper, we will assume that for any normal variety
X
, there is a proper birational morphism
f:X^{\prime}\to X
where
X^{\prime}
is smooth and
f
is an isomorphism outside of the singular locus of
X
. We call such a map a
resolution
of
X
. If
k
is of characteristic
0
, our assumption follows from Hironaka’s resolution of singularities, and if
k
is of positive characteristic, only the threefold case is known to be affirmative (see
[
Hir64
]
and
[
Cut09
]
). Moreover, for a pair
(X,\Delta)
, a
log resolution

f:X^{\prime}\to X
of
(X,\Delta)
is a resolution
f:X^{\prime}\to X
of
X
such that
\operatorname{Supp}f^{-1}_{*}\Delta\cup\mathrm{Exc}(f)
is simple normal crossing.

A variety
X
is said to have
rational singularities
if for any resolution
f:X^{\prime}\to X
,
f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X^{\prime}}=\mathcal{O}_{X},R^{i}f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X^{\prime}}=0
and
R^{i}f_{*}\omega_{X^{\prime}}=0
for

(see
[
Kol13
]
, Definition 2.76, and introduction of
[
Kov17
]
). The notion of rational singularities plays an important role in algebraic geometry. For instance, if
X
is a variety with rational singularities and
f:X^{\prime}\to X
is a resolution of
X
, we have

H^{i}(X^{\prime},f^{*}\mathcal{E})\cong H^{i}(X,\mathcal{E})

for any
i\geq 0
and any vector bundle
\mathcal{E}
on
X
. In this paper, we generalize the notion to any reflexive sheaf of rank
1
.

The fundamental property of the notion of rational singularities is that if
X
has rational singularities, then
X
is CM. In this paper, from this perspective, we will define another analogous notion of rational singularities for a reflexive sheaf
\mathcal{F}
of rank
1
on a normal variety

.

(See
[

Let

Note that for a normal variety

Definition-Theorem 1.2
.

Let

\mathcal{F}
any reflexive sheaf of rank

Let

(See Definition 2.5, and Theorem 2.9 in

Here, we highlight the distinctions between our definition and those in

]

•

We used double dual to define the notion of rational singularities instead of strict transform. The advantage of using double dual is that the notion of normal pair in
[

]

In particular, if a pair

•

It is obvious that some Cartier divisor on

•

We defined the notion of rational singularities for any reflexive sheaf of rank

One of the fundamental properties of klt and strongly

]

Theorem 1.5
.

Let

-Weil divisor such that

is dlt and

-Cartier Weil divisor on

Theorem 1.6
.

Let

be any strongly

-regular variety over a positive characteristic field, and

any Weil divisor on

Note that ?THM?

One of the advantages of our notion is that our notion of rational singularities is well-behaved under finite morphisms under mild conditions, and it provides a partial answer to a question posed by Kollár (see Remark 2.81 (3) in

]

3.
Reflexive sheaves

In this section, we collect important facts about reflexive sheaves. For a variety
X
, the generic point
\eta\in X
and a coherent sheaf
\mathcal{F}
on
X
,
\mathrm{rank}\,\mathcal{F}
denotes the dimension of
\mathcal{F}_{\eta}
over the function field of
X
.

Remark 3.1
.

For any normal Gorenstein variety
X
and any reflexive sheaf
\mathcal{F}
of rank
1
on
X
, we have
\mathcal{F}^{D}=\mathcal{F}^{\vee}\otimes\omega_{X}
.

Moreover, we can construct a natural map
\mathcal{F}\to\mathcal{F}^{DD}
. Indeed, for any local section
a\in\mathcal{F}
, consider a map

\varphi:\mathcal{F}\to\mathcal{F}^{DD},a\mapsto(f\mapsto f(a))\,\text{ for }f\in\mathcal{F}^{D}:=\mathcal{H}om_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(\mathcal{F},\omega_{X}).

Note that if
X
is normal, the map
\mathcal{F}^{D}\to\mathcal{F}^{DDD}
is an isomorphism, because
\mathcal{F}^{D}
is a reflexive sheaf. See Lemma 0AY4 in
[
Stacks
]
, Lemma 0AWE in
[
Stacks
]
, and ?THM?
below.

Now, let us prove the following two lemmas.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
X
be any normal variety and
\mathcal{F}
any coherent sheaf on
X
. Then the following are equivalent:

(a)

\mathcal{F}
is torsion-free.

(b)

The map
\mathcal{F}\to\mathcal{F}^{DD}
is injective.

Proof.

It is worth noting that for any variety
X
,
\omega_{X}
is
(S_{2})
. See Lemma 0AWE in
[
Stacks
]
. Hence, if
X
is normal, then
\omega_{X}
is reflexive.

If
\mathcal{F}
is torsion-free, then the kernel
\mathcal{K}
of the map is torsion-free because any subsheaf of a torsion-free sheaf is also torsion-free. Furthermore, since
\omega_{X}
is reflexive,
\mathcal{F}^{DD}
is torsion-free by Lemma 0AY4 in
[
Stacks
]
. Consider the fact that for every point
x\in X
of codimension
1
,
\mathcal{F}_{x}\to\mathcal{F}^{DD}_{x}
is an isomorphism (which follows from Lemma 0CC4 in
[
Stacks
]
, and the fact that
\mathcal{O}_{X,x}
is a discrete valuation ring). Then
\mathcal{K}=0
because
\mathrm{rank}\,\mathcal{K}=0
.

For the converse,
\mathcal{F}^{DD}
is torsion-free. By our assumption,
\mathcal{F}
is torsion-free because any subsheaf of a torsion-free sheaf is also torsion-free.
∎

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
X
be any normal variety and
\mathcal{F}
any coherent sheaf on
X
. Then the following are equivalent:

(a)

\mathcal{F}
is reflexive.

(b)

The natural map
\mathcal{F}\to\mathcal{F}^{DD}
is an isomorphism.

Proof.

Let us assume that
\mathcal{F}
is reflexive. By Lemma 0AY4 in
[
Stacks
]
, we know that
\mathcal{F}^{DD}
is also reflexive and
\mathcal{F}\to\mathcal{F}^{DD}
is an isomorphism outside a codimension
\geq 2
closed subscheme of
X
. Hence the map is an isomorphism by
[
Har94
]
, Proposition 1.11.

For the converse, let
\mathcal{E}_{1}\to\mathcal{E}_{0}\to\mathcal{F}^{D}\to 0
be any resolution of
\mathcal{F}
in which
\mathcal{E}_{0},\mathcal{E}_{1}
are vector bundles. By taking
\mathcal{H}om_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(-,\omega_{X})
, we have the following exact sequence

0\to\mathcal{F}\cong\mathcal{F}^{DD}\to\mathcal{E}^{D}_{0}\to\mathcal{E}^{D}_{1},

and
\mathcal{E}^{D}_{i}
are reflexive for
i=0,1
. Hence,
\mathcal{F}
is reflexive (see Lemma 0EBG in
[
Stacks
]
).
∎

Remark 3.4
.

If
X
is not normal, then such an equivalence is false. See Remark 0AY1 in
[
Stacks
]
.

Let us note an important fact about reflexive sheaves. For any normal variety
X
and any reflexive sheaf
\mathcal{F}
on
X
,
\mathcal{F}
is a vector bundle outside codimension
\geq 2
closed subscheme of
X
. See Lemma 0AY6 in
[
Stacks
]
.

We will use the following three lemmas without any mention. We believe that they are well-known to experts but we cannot find any reference about them. Hence, we include their proof.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
X
be any variety and
\varphi:\mathcal{F}\to\mathcal{G}
any map between torsion-free sheaves on
X
. Suppose that
\varphi
is generically injective. Then
\varphi
is an injection.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{K}
be the kernel of
\varphi
. Note that
\mathcal{K}
is a subsheaf of a torsion-free sheaf
\mathcal{F}
. By the condition on
\varphi
,
\mathrm{rank}\,\mathcal{K}=0
and hence
\mathcal{K}=0
, because any subsheaf of torsion-free sheaf is also torsion-free.
∎

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
X,X^{\prime}
be any normal varieties and
f:X^{\prime}\to X
any proper birational morphism. Suppose that
\mathcal{F}
is any reflexive sheaf on
X
. Then
\mathcal{F}\cong f_{*}(f^{*}\mathcal{F})^{DD}

Proof.

Let us consider the adjunction property

\mathcal{H}om_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(\mathcal{F},f_{*}(f^{*}\mathcal{F})^{DD})\cong f_{*}\mathcal{H}om_{\mathcal{O}_{X^{\prime}}}(f^{*}\mathcal{F},(f^{*}\mathcal{F})^{DD})

and the map
f^{*}\mathcal{F}\to(f^{*}\mathcal{F})^{DD}
. Then we can construct the following map
\mathcal{F}\to f_{*}(f^{*}\mathcal{F})^{DD}
. Note that the map is an injection because it is an isomorphism on the regular locus of

and
\mathcal{F},f_{*}(f^{*}\mathcal{F})^{DD}
are torsion-free sheaves.

Let
\mathcal{Q}
be the cokernel. Then we have the following exact sequence

0\to\mathcal{F}\to f_{*}(f^{*}\mathcal{F})^{DD}\to\mathcal{Q}\to 0.

Note that the support of
\mathcal{Q}
has codimension
\geq 2
. Let
x\in X
be any point of codimension
\geq 2
. Then the local cohomology exact sequence tells us that
H^{0}_{x}(X,\mathcal{Q})=0
. Hence

is not an associated point of
\mathcal{Q}
(cf. Lemma 0EEZ in
[
Stacks
]
). Thus
\mathcal{Q}=0
by Lemma 05AG in
[
Stacks
]
.
∎

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
X,Y
be any normal varieties and
p:X^{\prime}\to X
any proper flat morphism. Suppose that
\mathcal{F}
is any coherent sheaf on
X
. Then
(p^{*}\mathcal{F})^{DD}=p^{*}(\mathcal{F}^{DD}).

Proof.

Note that the pullback of a reflexive sheaf along a flat morphism is also reflexive. See Proposition 1.8 in
[
Har80
]
.

Taking the natural map
\mathcal{F}\to\mathcal{F}^{DD}
, we have a map
\varphi:(p^{*}\mathcal{F})^{DD}\to p^{*}(\mathcal{F}^{DD})
. We know that both
(p^{*}\mathcal{F})^{DD},p^{*}(\mathcal{F}^{DD})
on

are reflexive and
\varphi
is an isomorphism outside codimension
\geq 2

(see ?THM?
). Hence by
[

]
, Proposition 1.11, we have the assertion.
∎

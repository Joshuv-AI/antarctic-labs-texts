On type II degenerations of hyperkähler manifolds
D. Huybrechts & M. Mauri

Abstract
. We give a simple argument to prove Nagai’s conjecture for type II degenerations of compact hyperkähler manifolds and cohomology classes of middle degree. Under an additional assumption, the techniques yield the conjecture in arbitrary degree. This would complete the proof of Nagai’s conjecture in general, as it was proved already for type I degenerations by Kollár, Laza, Saccà, and Voisin [ 10 ] and independently by Soldatenkov [ 18 ] , while it is immediate for type III degenerations. Our arguments are close in spirit to a recent paper by Harder [ 8 ] proving similar results for the restrictive class of good degenerations.

2.1.

We begin by recalling some well-known properties of the logarithmic
monodromy operator
N=\bigoplus N_{k}
.

Lemma 2.1
.

For classes
\alpha\in H^{k}(X,\mathbb{C})
and
\alpha^{\prime}\in H^{\ell}(X,\mathbb{C})
one has

(2.1)

N_{k+\ell}(\alpha\wedge\alpha^{\prime})=N_{k}(\alpha)\wedge\alpha^{\prime}+\alpha\wedge N_{\ell}(\alpha^{\prime}).

Furthermore, for a degeneration of compact hyperkähler manifolds, the operator
N_{2}
is compatible with the Beauville–Bogomolov form:

(2.2)

q(N_{2}(\alpha),\alpha^{\prime})+q(\alpha,N_{2}(\alpha^{\prime}))=0.

Proof.

The monodromy operator
T
on
H^{\ast}(X,\mathbb{C})
is an algebra isomorphism
and, hence,

(2.3)

T^{m}(\alpha\wedge\alpha^{\prime})=T^{m}(\alpha)\wedge T^{m}(\alpha^{\prime})

for all
m\geq 0
.
Now use
T^{m}=\exp(mN)
and expand (
2.3
) in
m
. Comparing linear terms, yields the first assertion. The proof of the second assertion is similar, relying on the fact that the monodromy operator
T_{2}
is orthogonal, i.e.
q(T_{2}(\alpha),T_{2}(\alpha^{\prime}))=q(\alpha,\alpha^{\prime})
.
∎

Let us now consider a degeneration
{\cal X}\lx@xy@svg{\hbox{\raise 2.5pt\hbox{\kern 1.0pt\hbox{\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\hbox{\vtop{\halign{\entry@#!@&&\entry@@#!@\cr&\crcr}}}\ignorespaces{\hbox{\kern-1.0pt\raise 0.0pt\hbox{\hbox{\kern 0.0pt\raise 0.0pt\hbox{\hbox{\kern 1.0pt\raise-2.5pt\hbox{$\textstyle{\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}$}}}}}}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces{}{\hbox{\lx@xy@droprule}}\ignorespaces{\hbox{\kern 14.0pt\raise 0.0pt\hbox{\hbox{\kern 0.0pt\raise 0.0pt\hbox{\lx@xy@tip{1}\lx@xy@tip{-1}}}}}}{\hbox{\lx@xy@droprule}}{\hbox{\lx@xy@droprule}}{\hbox{\kern 14.0pt\raise 0.0pt\hbox{\hbox{\kern 0.0pt\raise 0.0pt\hbox{\hbox{\kern 1.0pt\raise-2.5pt\hbox{$\textstyle{}$}}}}}}}\ignorespaces}}}}\ignorespaces\Delta

of order two
or, equivalently, such that

For the convenience of the reader we recall the following observation due to Schreieder and Soldatenkov
[
16

Lemma 2.2
.

For a type II degeneration of compact hyperkähler manifolds the image of the logarithmic monodromy operator
N_{2}\colon H^{2}(X,\mathbb{C})\lx@xy@svg{\hbox{\raise 2.55554pt\hbox{\kern 1.0pt\hbox{\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\hbox{\vtop{\halign{\entry@#!@&&\entry@@#!@\cr&\crcr}}}\ignorespaces{\hbox{\kern-1.0pt\raise 0.0pt\hbox{\hbox{\kern 0.0pt\raise 0.0pt\hbox{\hbox{\kern 1.0pt\raise-2.55554pt\hbox{$\textstyle{\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}$}}}}}}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces{}{\hbox{\lx@xy@droprule}}\ignorespaces{\hbox{\kern 14.0pt\raise 0.0pt\hbox{\hbox{\kern 0.0pt\raise 0.0pt\hbox{\lx@xy@tip{1}\lx@xy@tip{-1}}}}}}{\hbox{\lx@xy@droprule}}{\hbox{\lx@xy@droprule}}{\hbox{\kern 14.0pt\raise 0.0pt\hbox{\hbox{\kern 0.0pt\raise 0.0pt\hbox{\hbox{\kern 1.0pt\raise-2.55554pt\hbox{$\textstyle{}$}}}}}}}\ignorespaces}}}}\ignorespaces H^{2}(X,\mathbb{C})

is an isotropic plane.

Furthermore, for any basis
w_{1},w_{2}\in{\rm Im}(N_{2})

one has

(2.4)

N_{2}(\alpha)=q(w_{2},\alpha)\,w_{1}-q(w_{1},\alpha)\,w_{2}

up to scaling
N_{2}
by a factor.

Proof.

Due to (
2.2
), one has
q(N_{2}(\alpha),N_{2}(\alpha^{\prime}))=-q(\alpha,N_{2}^{2}(\alpha^{\prime}))=0
,
which shows that the image is indeed isotropic. A computation with Hodge numbers, see

Thus, one can write

for certain linear forms
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}
on

.
Then, since by (

q(N_{2}(\alpha),\alpha)=0
for all
\alpha\in H^{2}(X,\mathbb{C})
, one finds

\lambda_{1}(\alpha)\,q(w_{1},\alpha)+\lambda_{2}(\alpha)\,q(w_{2},\alpha)=0

which proves
[\lambda_{1}(~):\lambda_{2}(~)]=[q(w_{2},~):-q(w_{1},~)]
.
∎

\displaystyle M^{n+1}(\alpha)=M^{n+1}(\sigma^{p-n}\wedge\gamma)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i+j=n+1}{n+1\choose i}\,M^{i}(\sigma^{p-n})\wedge M^{j}(\gamma)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i+j=n+1\\
j\leq 2n-p\end{subarray}}{n+1\choose i}\,M^{i}(\sigma^{p-n})\wedge M^{j}(\gamma),

where we use that for bidegree reasons

On the other hand, since

This yields the assertion.

2.4.

The arguments in the previous proof can be used to deduce

on

assuming the following holds for all

Corollary 2.5
.

Assuming

)

Proof.

It suffices to prove that

,

Let

(and

is maximal

Since

As the image of

Remark 2.6

The condition (

Remark 2.7

(i) Note that the argument in the above proof reducing

(ii) Also, one can combine the arguments in the proofs of Proposition

(

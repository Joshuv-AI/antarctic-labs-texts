Abstract
This paper considers LEO satellite- and cache-assisted UAV communications for content delivery in terrestrial networks, which shows great potential for next-generation systems to provide ubiquitous connectivity and high capacity. Specifically, caching is provided by the UAV to reduce backhaul congestion, and the LEO satellite supports the UAV’s backhaul link. In this context, we aim to maximize the minimum achievable throughput per ground user (GU) by jointly optimizing cache placement, the UAV’s resource allocation, and trajectory while cache capacity and flight time are limited. The formulated problem is challenging to solve directly due to its non-convexity and combinatorial nature. To find a solution, the problem is decomposed into three sub-problems: (1) cache placement optimization with fixed UAV resources and trajectory, followed by (2) the UAV resources optimization with fixed cache placement vector and trajectory, and finally, (3) we optimize the UAV trajectory with fixed cache placement and UAV resources. Based on the solutions of sub-problems, an efficient alternating algorithm is proposed utilizing the block coordinate descent (BCD) and successive convex approximation (SCA) methods. Simulation results show that the max-min throughput and total achievable throughput enhancement can be achieved by applying our proposed algorithm instead of other benchmark schemes.

Motivated by these observations, our work proposes a novel system model in satellite- and cache-assisted UAV wireless networks that further explores the impact of data transmission latency for backhaul link from satellite-to-UAV due to large distance and limited resource allocation, which makes the problem design even more challenging to solve and has not been investigated before.
To the best of our knowledge, this is the first work that maximizes the GUs’ minimum throughput via jointly optimizing UAV’s bandwidth, UAV’s transmit power, trajectory design, and cache placement in LEO satellite- and cache-assisted UAV wireless networks
. In summary, our contributions are as follows:

•

We propose a novel satellite- and cache-assisted UAV communication system. Specifically, caching techniques can reduce the burden on the backhaul link during peak times and prolong the UAV’s lifetime. In addition, the LEO satellite helps to deliver requested content that is not cached by the UAV.

•

We formulate an optimization problem to maximize the minimum throughput at GUs, subject to the UAV’s limited operation time, the UAV’s maximum speed, the UAV’s trajectory, limited cache capacity of the UAV, bandwidth allocation for the access link from the UAV to GUs, transmit power allocation at the UAV to transmit data of GU
k
. The formulation belongs to a mixed-integer nonlinear programming (MINLP) problem, which is challenging to solve. Especially, the binary nature of caching decision-making variables makes it more troublesome.

•

We solve the problem by decomposing it into three sub-problems: (1) cache placement optimization with given UAV resources (i.e., bandwidth and transmit power) and trajectory, followed by (2) the UAV bandwidth and transmit power optimization with given cache placement vector and trajectory, and finally, (3) we optimize the UAV trajectory with given cache placement vector and UAV resources. Based on the sub-problems’ obtained solutions, we propose an iterative algorithm to solve the non-convex optimization problem by adopting the block coordinate descent (BCD) method and successive convex approximation (SCA) techniques.

•

The proposed algorithm’s effectiveness is shown via simulation results. In particular, our approach yields an improvement up to 26.64
\%
, 79.79
\%
, and 87.96
\%
in max-min throughput compared to the benchmark scheme one (BS1), benchmark scheme two (BS2), and benchmark scheme three (BS3), respectively. More specifically, BS1, BS2, and BS3 are designed similarly to the proposed algorithm but with fixed trajectory, no caching capability, and fixed resource allocation, respectively.

z\triangleq\frac{p_{2k}^{n}{|{\tilde{h}}_{2k}^{n}|^{2}\beta_{0}}}{\left({H^{2}}+{{\left\|{{\bf q}_{n}-{\bf w}_{n}}\right\|}^{2}}\right)^{\alpha/2}\sigma^{2}}

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{Z}[\ln z]

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{+\infty}\lambda_{Z}e^{-z\lambda_{Z}}\ln{z}dz=-\big(\ln(\lambda_{Z})+E\big),

\displaystyle=\ln\frac{p_{2k}^{n}\beta_{0}}{\zeta_{1k}}-E,

(16)

where
E
is the Euler–Mascheroni constant, i.e.,
E=0.5772156649
as in
[

, Eq. 8.367.1]
.

By substituting (
II-B
) into (

) and combining with (

). Thus, the Lemma

In this work, the bandwidth allocation on the AL is applied to efficiently utilize the resource. Besides, the spectrum allocation should satisfy:

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{k\in{\cal K}}b_{2k}^{n}\leq 1,\forall n,

(17)

\displaystyle 0\leq b_{2k}^{n}\leq 1,\forall k,n.

(18)

From (
14
), the achievable throughput of user
k
(in bits) during flight time
T
can be given as

\displaystyle\Upsilon_{2k}=\delta_{t}P_{k,f}\Bigg[n_{f}\sum\limits_{n_{1k}}^{N}\bar{r}_{2k}^{n}+(1-n_{f})\sum\limits_{n_{2k}+1}^{N}\bar{r}_{2k}^{n}\Bigg],

(19)

where the first component represents the case when the UAV pre-stores the file
f
in its cache, i.e.,
n_{f}=1
, and thus it can transmit directly to GU

; While the second component means that the UAV does not cache the file
f
, i.e.,

, it thus starts transmitting data to GU
k
when it finishes receiving the requested file from the satellite;

denotes the first time slot during the flight time

;

II-C

Problem Formulation

In this section, we aim to maximize the minimum achievable throughput among GUs through jointly optimizing the cache placement, resources allocation (i.e., bandwidth and transmit power of the UAV to convey the GU
k
’s data at time slot
n
), and the UAV trajectory, with the assumption that the GUs’ locations, the UAV altitude, and the satellite height are known a priori.

Let
{\bf q}\triangleq\{{\bf q}_{n},\forall n\}
,
{\mathbb{b}}\triangleq\{b_{2k}^{n},n\in{\cal N},k\in{\cal K}\}
,
{\mathbb{p}}\triangleq\{p_{2k}^{n},n\in{\cal N},k\in{\cal K}\}
,
\boldsymbol{\eta}\triangleq\{\eta_{f},\forall f\in{\cal F}\}
. Based on the above discussions, the optimization problem is formulated as follows

\displaystyle{\cal P}_{1}:

\displaystyle{\max_{{\bf q},{\bf b},{\bf p},{\boldsymbol{\eta}}}~~\min_{\forall k\in{\cal K}}\Upsilon_{2k}}

(20a)

\displaystyle\mathtt{s.t.}~~\vskip 0.28436pt

\displaystyle\delta_{t}\sum\limits_{n=n_{1k}}^{n_{2k}}B_{1k}\log_{2}\Bigg(1+\frac{p_{1k}^{n}\beta_{0}}{(d_{1k}^{n})^{\alpha}\sigma^{2}}\Bigg)\geq(1-\eta_{f})Q,

(21a)

\displaystyle\forall n,k,

\displaystyle\eta_{f}\in\{0,1\},\forall f\in{\cal F},

(22a)

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{f\in{\cal F}}\eta_{f}\leq S,\forall f,

(23a)

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{k\in{\cal K}}b_{2k}^{n}\leq 1,\forall n,k,

(24a)

\displaystyle 0\leq b_{2k}^{n}\leq 1,\forall n,

(25a)

\displaystyle\left\|{{\bf q}_{n}-{\bf q}_{n-1}}\right\|\leq\delta_{d},n=1,\dots,N,

(26a)

\displaystyle{\bf q}_{0}={\bf q}_{I},{\bf q}_{N}={\bf q}_{F},

(27a)

\displaystyle 0\leq\sum\limits_{k\in{\cal K}}p_{2k}^{n}\leq P_{u}^{\rm max},\forall n,\vskip 5.69046pt

(28a)

where constraint (
21a
) guarantees that the non-cached file
f
is transmitted from satellite to the UAV; (
22a
) be the binary variable for the UAV to cache file
f
or not; (
23a
) means that the total number of contents cached at the UAV should be less than or equal to the storage capacity; (
24a
) and (
25a
) signify the bandwidth allocation constraints; (
26a
) shows the maximum velocity constraint of the UAV; constraint (
27a
) explains for the start and end points of the UAV; constraint (
28a
) implies that the transmit power of the UAV is restrained by its maximum power budget.

It is troublesome to obtain the direct solution of
{\cal P}_{1}
since this is a mixed-integer non-linear program (MINLP), which is NP-hard. Specifically, the objective function is a max-min and non-convex function. Besides, the binary nature of constraint (
22a
) and the non-convexity of (
26a
), which introduces intractability. In the next section, an efficient method is proposed to solve it.

III
Proposed Solution for Solving
{\cal P}_{1}

III-A

Tractable formulation for
{\cal P}_{1}

It can be seen that the
n_{2k}
value in problem
{\cal P}_{1}
is not determined, thus we cannot solve
{\cal P}_{1}
if
n_{2k}
is a variable. Consequently, we intend to find a specified value for
n_{2k}
. In the case that file
f
is stored at the UAV, the data transmission time from
s\to u
equals zero, i.e.,
t_{1k}=0
. Therefore, from (
11
), the total transmission time to transmit GU
k
’s data from
s\to u
can be specified as

\displaystyle t_{1k}\triangleq\frac{(1-\eta_{f})Q}{\bar{r}_{1k}}.

(29)

where
\bar{r}_{1k}\triangleq\frac{\sum\limits_{\forall n\in{\cal N}}r_{1k}^{n}}{N}
is the average data transmission rate from
s\to u
during flying time
T
.

Based on (
29
), we have

\displaystyle n_{2k}\triangleq n_{1k}+\big\lceil\frac{t_{1k}}{\delta_{t}}\big\rceil,

(30)

Without loss of generality, we assume that
n_{2k}\leq N
.

Let us introduce an auxiliary variable
\chi({\bf q},{\mathbb{b}},{\mathbb{p}},\boldsymbol{\eta})\triangleq\min\limits_{\forall k\in{\cal K}}\Upsilon_{2k}
to transform the max-min problem
{\cal P}_{1}
into maximization problem, we then have

\displaystyle{\cal P}_{1.1}:

\displaystyle\max_{{\bf q},{\bf b},{\bf p},{\boldsymbol{\eta}},\chi}~~\chi({\bf q},{\mathbb{b}},{\mathbb{p}},\boldsymbol{\eta})

(31a)

\displaystyle\mathtt{s.t.}~~

\displaystyle{\Upsilon_{2k}\geq\chi,\forall k,}

(32a)

\displaystyle\eqref{eq:P1:b}-\eqref{eq:P1:i},

(33a)

To overcome the non-convexity of the problem
{\cal P}_{1}
, we first decompose
{\cal P}_{1}
into three sub-problems, in which we first optimize the cache placement for a given trajectory and resource allocation, followed
by the optimization of resource allocation (i.e., bandwidth and transmit power allocation) for a given trajectory and cache placement, and finally, we perform the UAV trajectory optimization for a given cache placement and resource allocation. Based on the block coordinate descent (BCD) method
[
40
,
41
]
and SCA technique
[
42
]
, an efficient iterative algorithm is proposed, while three sub-problems are alternately optimized until convergence.

III-B

Subproblem 1: Cache Placement Optimization

For any given
\mathbb{b}^{j}
,
\mathbb{p}^{j}
, and
{\bf q}^{j}
, the cache placement
\boldsymbol{\eta}
can be obtain by solving the following optimization problem:

\displaystyle{\cal P}_{1}^{\boldsymbol{\eta}}:

\displaystyle\max_{{\boldsymbol{\eta}},\chi}~~\chi({\bf q}^{j},\mathbb{b}^{j},\mathbb{p}^{j},\boldsymbol{\eta})

(34a)

\displaystyle\mathtt{s.t.}~~\vskip 1.42271pt

\displaystyle\delta_{t}\sum\limits_{n=n_{1k}}^{n_{2k}}\bar{r}_{1k}\geq(1-\eta_{f})Q,\forall n,k,

(35a)

\displaystyle{\Upsilon_{2k}\geq\chi,\forall k,}

(36a)

\displaystyle\eta_{f}\in\{0,1\},\forall f\in{\cal F},

(37a)

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{f\in{\cal F}}\eta_{f}\leq S,\forall f.

(38a)

It can be seen that the problem
{\cal P}_{1}^{\boldsymbol{\eta}}
is non-convex due to the binary nature of the (
37a
) constraint. Thus, we first relax the binary cache placement variable into a continuous one. As a result,
{\cal P}_{1}^{\boldsymbol{\eta}}
can be re-written as

\displaystyle{\cal P}_{1.1}^{\boldsymbol{\eta}}:

\displaystyle\max_{{\boldsymbol{\eta}},\chi}~~\chi({\bf q}^{j},\mathbb{b}^{j},\mathbb{p}^{j},\boldsymbol{\eta})

(39a)

\displaystyle\mathtt{s.t.}~~\vskip 1.42271pt

\displaystyle 0\leq\eta_{f}\leq 1,\forall f,

(40a)

\displaystyle\eqref{eq:P1.2:b0},\eqref{eq:P1.2:b},\eqref{eq:P1.2:d}.

(41a)

It is observed that the problem
{\cal P}_{1.1}^{\boldsymbol{\eta}}
is convex since the objective function and constraints are convex, i.e., linear. Consequently, the problem
{\cal P}_{1.1}^{\boldsymbol{\eta}}
can be solved by using standard methods
[
43
]
. Nevertheless, since the cache placement variable is relaxed into continuous values between 0 and 1, and thus it does not ensure that the
\eta_{f}
can converge to 0 or 1. Thus, we introduce a penalty function
\mathbb{P}(\eta_{f})\triangleq\eta_{f}(\eta_{f}-1)
, which is a convex function
[
29
,
43
]
.
Hence, the sub-problem
{\cal P}_{1.1}^{\boldsymbol{\eta}}
is reformulated as,

\displaystyle{\cal P}_{1.2}^{\boldsymbol{\eta}}:

\displaystyle\max_{{\boldsymbol{\eta}},\chi}~~\big(\chi({\bf q}^{j},\mathbb{b}^{j},\mathbb{p}^{j},\boldsymbol{\eta})+\kappa\mathbb{P}(\eta_{f})\big)

(42a)

\displaystyle\mathtt{s.t.}~~

\displaystyle\eqref{eq:P1.3:b},\eqref{eq:P1.3:c}.

(43a)

where
\kappa>0
is a penalty factor. Especially, the objective in
{\cal P}_{1.2}^{\boldsymbol{\eta}}
is a difference of concave function, i.e.,
f(\eta_{f},\chi)\triangleq\big[\chi({\bf q}^{j},\mathbb{b}^{j},\mathbb{p}^{j},\boldsymbol{\eta})-\big(-\kappa\mathbb{P}(\eta_{f})\big)\big]
with convex constraints. As a result, the problem
{\cal P}_{1.2}^{\boldsymbol{\eta}}
becomes a DC Programming Problem (DCP). To transform
{\cal P}_{1.2}^{\boldsymbol{\eta}}
become a convex problem, we substitute
\mathbb{P}(\eta_{f})
in the objective by its first-order Taylor expansion at
(j+1)
-th iteration:

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathbb{P}}(\eta_{f})\triangleq\kappa\Big(\mathbb{P}(\eta_{f}^{(j)})+\triangledown\mathbb{P}(\eta_{f}^{(j)})\big(\eta_{f}-\eta_{f}^{(j)}\big)\Big),

(44)

where

\displaystyle\triangledown\mathbb{P}(\eta_{f}^{(j)})=2\eta_{f}^{(j)}-1.

(45)

Thus, we have

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathbb{P}}(\eta_{f})\triangleq\kappa\Big(\eta_{f}(2\eta_{f}^{(j)}-1)-(\eta_{f}^{(j)})^{2}\Big),

(46)

Consequently, sub-problem
{\cal P}_{1.2}^{\boldsymbol{\eta}}
is approximately converted to the following linear programming:

\displaystyle{\cal P}_{1.3}^{\boldsymbol{\eta}}:

\displaystyle\max_{{\boldsymbol{\eta}},\chi}~~\big(\chi({\bf q}^{j},\mathbb{b}^{j},\mathbb{p}^{j},\boldsymbol{\eta})+\widehat{\mathbb{P}}(\eta_{f})\big)

(47a)

\displaystyle\mathtt{s.t.}~~

\displaystyle\eqref{eq:P1.3:b},\eqref{eq:P1.3:c}.

(48a)

Remark
1

For cache placement optimization in Algorithm
1
, we only solve the relaxed problem (
47a
) instead of the original problem (
34a
),
where the binary cache placement variable
\boldsymbol{\eta}
in the original problem (
34a
) is relaxed to continuous values between 0 and 1. Notably, if the cache placement variable
\boldsymbol{\eta}
obtained by Algorithm 1 can converge to binary values, then the relaxation is tight and the solution in (
47a
) is also a feasible solution of the problem (
34a
). Especially, with an appropriate, sufficiently large, and positive value of the penalty parameter
\kappa
, the relaxed problem (
47a
) is equivalent to the original problem (
34a
), as clearly mentioned in
[
44
, Theorem 2.1]
or in the introduction of
[
45
]
.

Remark
2

The solutions of sub-problem
{\cal P}_{1.3}^{\boldsymbol{\eta}}
can be obtained by applying standard optimization solver, i.e., CVX
[
43
]
. Besides, we observe that constraint (
40a
) is equivalent to (
37a
) when we achieve the optimal solutions of
{\cal P}_{1.3}^{\boldsymbol{\eta}}
. By applying the lower bound result in (
44
), it can be seen that the feasible solutions for
{\cal P}_{1.3}^{\boldsymbol{\eta}}
are also satisfy
{\cal P}_{1}^{\boldsymbol{\eta}}
, which means that we can obtain at least a locally optimal solution.

III-C

Subproblem 2: UAV Bandwidth and Transmit Power Optimization

For any given cache placement
\boldsymbol{\eta}^{j}
and UAV trajectory
{\bf q}^{j}
,
\mathbb{b}
and
\mathbb{p}
can be obtained by solving the following optimization problem:

\displaystyle{\cal P}_{1}^{\mathbb{b},\mathbb{p}}:

\displaystyle\max_{\bf b,\bf p,\chi}~~\chi({\bf q}^{j},\mathbb{b},\mathbb{p},\boldsymbol{\eta^{j}})

(49a)

\displaystyle\mathtt{s.t.}~~

\displaystyle\zeta_{\bf b,\bf p}^{n}\geq\chi,\forall n,k,

(50a)

\displaystyle\delta_{t}\sum\limits_{n=n_{1k}}^{n_{2k}}\bar{r}_{1k}\geq(1-\eta_{f})Q,\forall n,k,

(51a)

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{f\in{\cal F}}\eta_{f}\leq S,\forall f,

(52a)

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{k\in{\cal K}}b_{2k}^{n}\leq 1,\forall n,k,

(53a)

\displaystyle 0\leq b_{2k}^{n}\leq 1,\forall n,k,

(54a)

\displaystyle 0\leq\sum\limits_{k\in{\cal K}}p_{2k}^{n}\leq P_{u}^{\rm max},\forall n,\vskip 5.69046pt

(55a)

where

\displaystyle\zeta_{\bf b,\bf p}^{n}

\displaystyle\triangleq\delta_{t}P_{k,f}b_{2k}^{n}B_{2k}\Bigg[n_{f}\sum\limits_{n_{1k}}^{N}\Phi_{\bf p}^{n}+(1-n_{f})\sum\limits_{n_{2k}+1}^{N}\Phi_{\bf p}^{n}\Bigg],

(56)

\displaystyle\Phi_{\bf p}^{n}

\displaystyle\triangleq\log_{2}\Bigg(1+\frac{e^{-E}p_{2k}^{n}\beta_{0}}{\left({H_{u}^{2}}+{{\left\|{{\bf q}_{n}-{\bf w}_{k}}\right\|}^{2}}\right)^{\alpha/2}\sigma^{2}}\Bigg).

(57)

Due to the non-convexity of constraints (
50a
), sub-problem
{\cal P}_{1}^{\mathbb{b},\mathbb{p}}
is also non-convex. To make
{\cal P}_{1}^{\mathbb{b},\mathbb{p}}
more tractable, we first introduce slack variable
\bar{\Phi}_{\bf p}^{n}
such that

\displaystyle\Phi_{\bf p}^{n}\triangleq\log_{2}\Bigg(1+\frac{e^{-E}p_{2k}^{n}\beta_{0}}{\left({H_{u}^{2}}+{{\left\|{{\bf q}_{n}-{\bf w}_{k}}\right\|}^{2}}\right)^{\alpha/2}\sigma^{2}}\Bigg)\geq\bar{\Phi}_{\bf p}^{n}.

(58)

Consequently,
\zeta_{\bf b,\bf p}^{n}
is converted to the following constraint

\displaystyle\zeta_{\bf b,\bf p}^{n}

\displaystyle\geq\delta_{t}P_{k,f}B_{2k}

\displaystyle\times\Bigg[n_{f}\sum\limits_{n_{1k}}^{N}b_{2k}^{n}\bar{\Phi}_{\bf p}^{n}+(1-n_{f})\sum\limits_{n_{2k}+1}^{N}b_{2k}^{n}\bar{\Phi}_{\bf p}^{n}\Bigg]\triangleq\bar{\zeta}_{\bf b,\bf p}^{n}.

(59)

Lemma
2

For any given
b_{2k}^{n,j}
and
\bar{\Phi}_{\bf p}^{n,j}
at
(j+1)
-th iteration, the lower bound of
\bar{\zeta}_{\bf b,\bf p}^{n}
is expressed as

\displaystyle\bar{\zeta}_{\bf b,\bf p}^{n}

\displaystyle\geq\delta_{t}P_{k,f}B_{2k}

\displaystyle\times\Bigg[n_{f}\sum\limits_{n_{1k}}^{N}\Theta_{\bf b,\bf p}^{n}+(1-n_{f})\sum\limits_{n_{2k}+1}^{N}\Theta_{\bf b,\bf p}^{n}\Bigg]\triangleq\widehat{\zeta}_{\bf b,\bf p}^{n},

(60)

where

\displaystyle\Theta_{\bf b,\bf p}^{n}

\displaystyle\triangleq\frac{\big(b_{2k}^{n,j}+\bar{\Phi}_{\bf p}^{n,j}\big)^{2}}{4}+\frac{\big(b_{2k}^{n,j}+\bar{\Phi}_{\bf p}^{n,j}\big)}{2}\times\big(b_{2k}^{n}-b_{2k}^{n,j}

\displaystyle+\bar{\Phi}_{\bf p}^{n}-\bar{\Phi}_{\bf p}^{n,j}\big)-\frac{\big(b_{2k}^{n}-\bar{\Phi}_{\bf p}^{n}\big)^{2}}{4}.

(61)

Proof:

It is observed that
b_{2k}^{n}\widehat{\Phi}_{2k}^{n}
is a non-convex function. To make it tractable, we replace
b_{2k}^{n}\bar{\Phi}_{2k}^{n}
by an equivalent different of convex function
0.25\Big[\big(b_{2k}^{n}+\bar{\Phi}_{2k}^{n}\big)^{2}-\big(b_{2k}^{n}-\bar{\Phi}_{2k}^{n}\big)^{2}\Big]
. Then, we adopt first-order Taylor expansion to approximate function
0.25\big(b_{2k}^{n}+\bar{\Phi}_{2k}^{n}\big)^{2}
as follow

\displaystyle 0.25\big(b_{2k}^{n}+\bar{\Phi}_{2k}^{n}\big)^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\big(b_{2k}^{n,j}+\bar{\Phi}_{2k}^{n,j}\big)^{2}}{4}+\frac{\big(b_{2k}^{n,j}+\bar{\Phi}_{2k}^{n,j}\big)}{2}\times

\displaystyle\big(b_{2k}^{n}-b_{2k}^{n,j}+\widehat{\Phi}_{2k}^{n}-\bar{\Phi}_{2k}^{n,j}\big).

(62)

Consequently, by substituting the lower bound of
0.25\big(b_{2k}^{n}+\bar{\Phi}_{2k}^{n}\big)^{2}
as in (
2
) into
0.25\Big[\big(b_{2k}^{n}+\bar{\Phi}_{2k}^{n}\big)^{2}-\big(b_{2k}^{n}-\bar{\Phi}_{2k}^{n}\big)^{2}\Big]
, the lower bound of
b_{2k}^{n}\bar{\Phi}_{2k}^{n}
can be obtained as (
2
), which finishes the proof.
∎

Bearing all the above developments in mind, the problem
{\cal P}_{1}^{\mathbb{b},\mathbb{p}}
is re-formulated as

\displaystyle{\cal P}_{1.1}^{\mathbb{b},\mathbb{p}}:

\displaystyle\max_{{\bf b},{\bf p},\chi}~~\chi({\bf q}^{j},\mathbb{b},\mathbb{p},\boldsymbol{\eta^{j}})

(63a)

\displaystyle\mathtt{s.t.}~~

\displaystyle\widehat{\zeta}_{\bf b,\bf p}^{n}\geq\chi,\forall n,k,

(64a)

\displaystyle{\log_{2}\Bigg(1+\frac{e^{-E}p_{2k}^{n}\beta_{0}}{\left({H_{u}^{2}}+{{\left\|{{\bf q}_{n}-{\bf w}_{k}}\right\|}^{2}}\right)^{\alpha/2}\sigma^{2}}\Bigg)\geq\bar{\Phi}_{\bf p}^{n},}

\displaystyle{\forall n,k,}

(65a)

\displaystyle\eqref{eq:P1.6:b0}-\eqref{eq:P1.6:f}.

(66a)

Remark
3

It is noted that the problem
{\cal P}_{1.1}^{\mathbb{b},\mathbb{p}}
is a convex problem because the objective function and all constraints are convex. Therefore, it can be numerically solved via standard optimization solvers such as CVX
[
43
]
. By applying the lower bound as in Lemma
2
, the set of feasible solutions for
{\cal P}_{1.1}^{\mathbb{b},\mathbb{p}}
is also a subset feasible solutions for

. Thus, it guarantees that at least a locally optimal solution can be achieved.

III-D

Subproblem 3: UAV Trajectory Optimization

For any given bandwidth allocation
\mathbb{b}^{j}
, UAV transmit power
\mathbb{p}^{j}
, and cache placement
\boldsymbol{\eta}^{j}
, the UAV trajectory
{\bf q}
can be obtained by solving following optimization problem

\displaystyle{\cal P}_{1}^{{\bf q}}:

\displaystyle\max_{{\bf q},\chi}~~\chi({\bf q},\mathbb{b}^{j},\mathbb{p}^{j},\boldsymbol{\eta}^{j})

(67a)

\displaystyle\mathtt{s.t.}~~\vskip 1.42271pt

\displaystyle\zeta_{{\bf q}}^{n}\geq\chi,\forall n,k,

(68a)

\displaystyle\delta_{t}\sum\limits_{n=n_{1k}}^{n_{2k}}\bar{r}_{1k}\geq(1-\eta_{f})Q,\forall n,k,

(69a)

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{f\in{\cal F}}\eta_{f}\leq S,\forall f,

(70a)

\displaystyle\left\|{{\bf q}_{n}-{\bf q}_{n-1}}\right\|\leq\delta_{d}=V_{max}\delta_{t},n=1,\dots,N,

\displaystyle{\bf q}_{0}={\bf q}_{I},{\bf q}_{N}={\bf q}_{F},

(72a)

where

\displaystyle\zeta_{{\bf q}}^{n}

\displaystyle\triangleq\delta_{t}P_{k,f}b_{2k}^{n}B_{2k}\Bigg[n_{f}\sum\limits_{n_{1k}}^{N}\Phi_{{\bf q}}^{n}+(1-n_{f})\sum\limits_{n_{2k}+1}^{N}\Phi_{{\bf q}}^{n}\Bigg],

(73)

\displaystyle\Phi_{{\bf q}}^{n}

\displaystyle\triangleq\log_{2}\Bigg(1+\frac{\psi}{\left({H_{u}^{2}}+{{\left\|{{\bf q}_{n}-{\bf w}_{k}}\right\|}^{2}}\right)^{\alpha/2}}\Bigg),

(74)

\displaystyle\psi

\displaystyle\triangleq\frac{e^{-E}p_{2k}^{n}\beta_{0}}{\sigma^{2}}.

(75)

The problem
{\cal P}_{1}^{{\bf q}}
is a non-convex optimization problem due
to the non-convexity of constraints (
68a
) and the combinatorial of designing UAV trajectory.
To convexify (
68a
), we introduce slack variable such that
\left({H_{u}^{2}}+{{\left\|{{\bf q}_{n}-{\bf w}_{k}}\right\|}^{2}}\right)\leq(\nu_{2k}^{n})^{2/\alpha}
. Let us denote
{\boldsymbol{\nu}}\triangleq\{\nu_{2k}^{n},\forall n,k\}
, then the sub-problem
{\cal P}_{1}^{{\bf q}}
can be re-written as

\displaystyle{\cal P}_{1.1}^{{\bf q}}:

\displaystyle\max_{{\bf q},{\boldsymbol{\nu},\chi}}~~\chi({\bf q},\mathbb{b}^{j},\mathbb{p}^{j},\boldsymbol{\eta}^{j})

(76a)

\displaystyle\mathtt{s.t.}~~

\displaystyle\left({H_{u}^{2}}+{{\left\|{{\bf q}_{n}-{\bf w}_{k}}\right\|}^{2}}\right)\leq(\nu_{2k}^{n})^{2/\alpha},\forall n,k,

(77a)

\displaystyle\bar{\zeta}_{{\bf q}}^{n}\geq\chi,\forall n,k,

(78a)

\displaystyle\eqref{eq:P1.8:b0}-\eqref{eq:P1.8:e},

(79a)

where

\displaystyle\bar{\zeta}_{{\bf q}}^{n}

\displaystyle\triangleq\delta_{t}P_{k,f}b_{2k}^{n}B_{2k}\Bigg[n_{f}\sum\limits_{n_{1k}}^{N}\bar{\Phi}_{{\bf q}}^{n}+(1-n_{f})\sum\limits_{n_{2k}+1}^{N}\bar{\Phi}_{{\bf q}}^{n}\Bigg],

(80)

\displaystyle\bar{\Phi}_{{\bf q}}^{n}

\displaystyle\triangleq\log_{2}\Bigg(1+\frac{\psi}{\nu_{2k}^{n}}\Bigg).

(81)

It is noted that the problem
{\cal P}_{1.1}^{{\bf q}}
is still non-convex. To solve this problem, we transform
{\cal P}_{1.1}^{{\bf q}}
into a convex form by giving following lemmas:

Lemma
3

For any given
\nu_{2k}^{n,j}
at
(j+1)
-th iteration,
\bar{\Phi}_{{\bf q}}^{n}\triangleq\log_{2}\Bigg(1+\frac{\psi}{\nu_{2k}^{n}}\Bigg)
is lower bounded by

\displaystyle\bar{\Phi}_{{\bf q}}^{n}\geq\log_{2}\big(1+\frac{\psi}{\nu_{2k}^{n,j}}\big)-\frac{\psi(\nu_{2k}^{n}-\nu_{2k}^{n,j})}{\nu_{2k}^{n,j}(\nu_{2k}^{n,j}+\psi)\ln 2}\triangleq\hat{\Phi}_{{\bf q}}^{n}.

(82)

Proof:

Due to the convexity of the function
f(x)=\log_{2}(1+1/x)
with
x>0
. By applying the first-order Taylor expansion to achieve the lower bound of
f(x)
at given feasible point
x^{j}
as

\displaystyle\log_{2}\big(1+\frac{\Omega_{1}}{x}\big)\geq\log_{2}\big(1+\frac{\Omega_{1}}{x^{j}}\big)-\frac{\Omega_{1}(x-x^{j})}{x^{j}(x^{j}+\Omega_{1})\ln 2}.

(83)

By adopting
\Omega_{1}=\psi
and
x=\nu_{2k}^{n}
, the Lemma
3
is then proved.
∎

Consequently, we have

\displaystyle\bar{\zeta}_{{\bf q}}^{n}\geq\delta_{t}P_{k,f}B_{2k}\Bigg[n_{f}\sum\limits_{n_{1k}}^{N}\hat{\Phi}_{{\bf q}}^{n}+(1-n_{f})\sum\limits_{n_{2k}+1}^{N}\hat{\Phi}_{{\bf q}}^{n}\Bigg]\triangleq\hat{\zeta}_{{\bf q}}^{n}.

(84)

Bearing all the above discussions in mind, the problem
{\cal P}_{1.1}^{{\bf q}}

V
Conclusion and Future Directions

This paper studied LEO satellite- and cache-assisted UAV communications. Especially, we proposed a novel system model that jointly considers UAV, caching, and satellite communications in content delivery networks. In this context, we maximized the minimum achievable throughput among GUs via joint optimization of the cache placement, resource allocation, and UAV trajectory. Because the formulated problem was in the form of MINLP, it is difficult to solve directly. Thus, we transformed the original problem into a solvable form using an alternative algorithm based on BCD method and SCA techniques. Extensive simulation results showed that our proposed algorithm improves up to 26.64
\%
, 79.79
\%
, and 87.96
\%
in the max-min throughput compared to BS1, BS2, and BS3, respectively. Notably, in the cases such as the UAV transmit power or the total traveling time size is large enough, the UAV should operate in fixed trajectory mode for a simple implementation.

The outcome of this work will motivate future works in satellite- and cache-assisted UAV wireless systems. One possible problem is to extend this work to a multi-UAV system, which imposes higher complexity but might further improve the network performance.

The probability of spanning a classical space by two non-degenerate subspaces of complementary dimensions Journal: Finite Fields and their Applications
S.P. Glasby, Alice C. Niemeyer, Cheryl E. Praeger

Abstract
Let n , n ′ n,n^{\prime} be positive integers and let V V be an ( n + n ′ ) (n+n^{\prime}) -dimensional vector space over a finite field 𝔽 {\mathbb{F}} equipped with a non-degenerate alternating, hermitian or quadratic form. We estimate the proportion of pairs ( U , U ′ ) (U,U^{\prime}) , where U U is a non-degenerate n n -subspace and U ′ U^{\prime} is a non-degenerate n ′ n^{\prime} -subspace of V V , such that U + U ′ = V U+U^{\prime}=V (usually such spaces U U and U ′ U^{\prime} are not perpendicular). The proportion is shown to be at least 1 − c / | 𝔽 | 1-c/|{\mathbb{F}}| for some constant c < 2 c<2 in the symplectic or unitary cases, and c < 3 c<3 in the orthogonal case.

Introduction

Let
n,n^{\prime}
be positive integers and let
V
be an

(n+n^{\prime})
-dimensional vector space over a finite field
{\mathbb{F}}
. The
proportion of pairs of subspaces
U,U^{\prime}
of dimensions
n
and
n^{\prime}
,
respectively, that intersect trivially, and hence satisfy
U+U^{\prime}=V
, is
close to 1 if
|{\mathbb{F}}|
is large and in all cases is greater than

1-3/(2|{\mathbb{F}}|)
, see Lemma
2.1
. Our aim is to estimate this
proportion in the case where
V
is a symplectic, hermitian or
orthogonal space and both
U
and
U^{\prime}
are non-degenerate. Such an
analogue of Lemma
2.1
seems not to have been considered before.

Configurations involving complementary subspaces in vector spaces
arise in a number of different ways; for example, they have been
studied graph theoretically
Cla1992
;
JJ2016
;
Songpon
,
geometrically
IM1995
, and algorithmically
PSY2015
. For
different applications the types of subspaces considered are
restricted. In representation theory they are usually submodules as
in
Yar2013
. Often the finite vector space
V
is endowed with
a
classical form
, that is, a non-degenerate sesqui-linear or
quadratic form, and in finite geometry the subspaces are usually
totally isotropic while in computational group theory they are
frequently non-degenerate.

The problem that we address in this paper arises from algorithmic
considerations connected with computations in finite classical
groups
random
;
stingray
. In order to show that two isometries,
each leaving invariant a non-degenerate
proper subspace, generate a classical group with high probability (see
for example
DLLO13
;
PSY2015
), a fundamental problem arises.
Show that, with high probability, for a vector space
V

endowed with a classical form, two random non-degenerate subspaces
whose dimensions sum to
\dim(V)
, are complements of each other, that
is, they intersect trivially and span
V
. Our aim is to solve this
problem, and in fact to prove that the probability approaches
1
as

|{\mathbb{F}}|
approaches
\infty
. We emphasize that, for algorithmic applications,
an explicit lower bound for this probability, rather than an
asymptotic expression, is required.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
n,n^{\prime}
be positive integers, let
V={\mathbb{F}}^{n+n^{\prime}}
be equipped with a
non-degenerate alternating, hermitian or quadratic linear form, and
let
c
be a constant, with the type of form,
|{\mathbb{F}}|
and
c
as in
Table
1
. Then the proportion of pairs
(U,U^{\prime})
of
non-degenerate subspaces of dimensions
n,n^{\prime}
respectively (of given
type as in Table
1
in the orthogonal case) that span

V
is positive and at least
1-c/|{\mathbb{F}}|
.

Instead of proving a lower bound for the pairs of subspaces we seek,
our strategy is to determine an upper bound for the pairs of subspaces that intersect non-trivially.
In fact, many authors have considered problems concerning families of
subspaces which pairwise intersect non-trivially, for example,
generalisations of the Erdős-Ko-Rado theorem for sets, or the
Hilton-Milnor Theorem which gives upper bounds for the sizes of such families
of subspaces (see
HMtheorem
;
GLW
;
Hsieh
or related references).
Unfortunately we cannot exploit these results to prove our theorem: usually
our families of subspaces do not have the pairwise intersecting
property, and our families are orbits under a finite classical group. Moreover,
it is essential that the subspace dimensions sum to
\dim(V).

A result
DeBoeck
*Lemma 4
most closely related in spirit to ours determines the proportion
of pairs of subspaces
(U,U^{\prime})
of a hermitian space
V
, such that
U
is
totally isotropic of dimension
\dim(V)/2
,
U^{\prime}
is non-degenerate
of given dimension at most
\dim(V)/2
, and
U\cap U^{\prime}=\{0\}.

Other authors

have considered orbits of subspaces of finite
classical groups but study different aspects. In our proof we exploit the
symmetry of classical geometry and employ the combinatorial technique
of “double counting”, see Section
3

2
Notation and strategy for proving Theorem
1.1

In this section we establish the notation and hypotheses
used throughout this paper. First, we prove the analogue of
Theorem
1.1
mentioned in Section
1
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
q
be a power of a prime and let
n,n^{\prime}
be positive integers.
Let
V=({\mathbb{F}}_{q})^{n+n^{\prime}}
be a vector space and let
\binom{V}{n}
denote the
set of
n
-subspaces of
V
. Then the proportion
\rho

of pairs
(U,U^{\prime})
in

\binom{V}{n}\times\binom{V}{n^{\prime}}
with
U\cap U^{\prime}=0
satisfies

\rho=\prod_{i=1}^{n^{\prime}}\frac{1-q^{-i}}{1-q^{-n-i}}>1-3/(2q)
.

Proof.

As
{\mathrm{GL}}(V)
is transitive on
\binom{V}{n}
, we fix some

U\in\binom{V}{n}
, and then
\rho
is the proportion of

U^{\prime}\in\binom{V}{n^{\prime}}
satisfying
U\cap U^{\prime}=0
. There are
q^{nn^{\prime}}
complements

U^{\prime}\in\binom{V}{n^{\prime}}
to
U
and

\left|\binom{V}{n^{\prime}}\right|=\frac{|{\mathrm{GL}}_{n+n^{\prime}}(q)|}{|{\mathrm{GL}}_{n}(q)||{\mathrm{GL}}_{n^{\prime}}(q)|q^{nn^{\prime}}}=q^{nn^{\prime}}\prod_{i=1}^{n^{\prime}}\frac{1-q^{-n-i}}{1-q^{-i}}.

Hence

\rho=q^{nn^{\prime}}/\left|\binom{V}{n^{\prime}}\right|=\prod_{i=1}^{n^{\prime}}\frac{1-q^{-i}}{1-q^{-n-i}}
.
Therefore
\rho\geqslant\prod_{i=1}^{n^{\prime}}(1-q^{-i})>\prod_{i=1}^{\infty}(1-q^{-i})
, and so

\rho>1-q^{-1}-q^{-2}\geqslant 1-3/(2q)
by
NP
*Lemma 3.5.
∎

Let
n,n^{\prime}
be positive integers and
let
q
be a power of a prime
p
.
Let
V
be an
(n+n^{\prime})
-dimensional classical space over a field
\mathbb{F}
,
that is, a non-degenerate symplectic or orthogonal
space over
\mathbb{F}={\mathbb{F}}_{q}
, or a non-degenerate hermitian space over

\mathbb{F}={\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}}
.

The isometry type of a subspace of
V
depends only on its dimension in the
symplectic and unitary cases, but is more complicated to
describe in the orthogonal case, see Remark
6.1
.
In the symplectic and unitary cases, let
{\mathcal{U}}={\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{V}{n}}

(resp.
{\mathcal{U}}^{\prime}={\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{V}{n'}}
) denote the set
of non-degenerate
n
-subspaces (resp.
n^{\prime}
-subspaces) of
V
.
In the case of an orthogonal space of type
\varepsilon
, let

{\mathcal{U}}={\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{V}{n}}^{\varepsilon}_{\sigma}
and

{\mathcal{U}}^{\prime}={\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{V}{n'}}^{\varepsilon}_{\sigma^{\prime}}

denote the set of non-degenerate
n
- and
n^{\prime}
-subspaces of
V
with
subspace type
\sigma
and
\sigma^{\prime}
,
respectively, as in Remark
6.1
.

We will show that the
proportion of pairs

(U,U^{\prime})\in{\mathcal{U}}\times{\mathcal{U}}^{\prime}
satisfying
U\cap U^{\prime}=0
is bounded away from zero, and is close to
1
if
q
is large.
The isometry group

G(V)
leaves invariant the set of the pairs
(U,U^{\prime})\in{\mathcal{U}}\times{\mathcal{U}}^{\prime}
satisfying
U\cap U^{\prime}=0
,
and by Witt’s Theorem,
G(V)
is transitive on
{\mathcal{U}}
. We henceforth
fix a subspace
U\in{\mathcal{U}}
and note that

\frac{|\{(U,U^{\prime})\in{\mathcal{U}}\times{\mathcal{U}}^{\prime}\colon U\cap U^{\prime}=0\}|}{|{\mathcal{U}}\times{\mathcal{U}}^{\prime}|}=\frac{|\{U^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{U}}^{\prime}\colon U\cap U^{\prime}=0\}|}{|{\mathcal{U}}^{\prime}|}.

Rather than showing that the above proportion is bounded away from zero,
we find an upper bound, less than
1
, for the quantity

(1)

\varphi\vcentcolon=\frac{|\{U^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{U}}^{\prime}\colon U\cap U^{\prime}\neq 0\}|}{|{\mathcal{U}}^{\prime}|}.

We use the following strategy to bound
\varphi
. If

U\cap U^{\prime}
is not trivial, it must contain a
1
-subspace. Thus

\{U^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{U}}^{\prime}\colon U\cap U^{\prime}\neq 0\}=\bigcup_{W}\;\{U^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{U}}^{\prime}\colon W\leqslant U\cap U^{\prime}\},

where
W
ranges over the
1
-subspaces of
U
.
A
1
-subspace
W
either lies in the set
{\mathcal{N}}_{1}(U)
of
non-degenerate
1
-subspaces of
U
, or the
set
{\mathcal{P}}_{1}(U)
of totally isotropic/singular
1
-subspaces of
U
.
(We say that
W
is
totally isotropic
in the symplectic and unitary cases if
W\leqslant W^{\perp}
and
totally singular
in the orthogonal case if
Q(W)=0
, which implies
W\leqslant W^{\perp}
. Since
\dim(W)=1
we sometimes omit the adjective “totally”.)
For
W\leqslant U
, let

{\mathcal{U}}^{\prime}(W)=\{U^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{U}}^{\prime}\colon W\leqslant U^{\prime}\}.

Thus we have

\{U^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{U}}^{\prime}\colon U\cap U^{\prime}\neq 0\}=\left(\bigcup_{W\in{\mathcal{P}}_{1}(U)}{\mathcal{U}}^{\prime}(W)\right)\cup\left(\bigcup_{W\in{\mathcal{N}}_{1}(U)}{\mathcal{U}}^{\prime}(W)\right).

Hence we have the upper bound

(2)

\varphi=\frac{|\{U^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{U}}^{\prime}\colon U\cap U^{\prime}\neq 0\}|}{|{\mathcal{U}}^{\prime}|}{\leqslant}\,\frac{|\bigcup_{W\in{\mathcal{P}}_{1}(U)}{\mathcal{U}}^{\prime}(W)|}{|{\mathcal{U}}^{\prime}|}{+}\frac{|\bigcup_{W\in{\mathcal{N}}_{1}(U)}{\mathcal{U}}^{\prime}(W)|}{|{\mathcal{U}}^{\prime}|}.

In the symplectic case the set
{\mathcal{N}}_{1}(U)
is empty.
Given an arbitrary element
U^{\prime}
of
{\mathcal{U}}
, we prove in
Lemma
5.2
that

(3)

\varphi=\frac{\left|\bigcup_{W\in{\mathcal{P}}_{1}(U)}{\mathcal{U}}^{\prime}(W)\right|}{|{\mathcal{U}}^{\prime}|}\leqslant\frac{|{\mathcal{P}}_{1}(U)||{\mathcal{P}}_{1}(U^{\prime})|}{|{\mathcal{P}}_{1}(V)|}

and moreover that
\varphi<1.5/q
if
q\geqslant 3.
The exceptional case when
q=2
is handled in the remainder of Section
5
, completing the proof
of Theorem
5.1
that
\varphi<5/(3q)
for
n,n^{\prime},q\geqslant 2
.

We deal with the unitary and orthogonal cases in Sections
4
and
6
respectively. In both cases the isometry group
G(V)
is transitive on the isotropic/singular
1
-subspaces of
V
and we show, in the proof of Theorem

(see Equation (
10
)) and Lemma

respectively, that

(4)

\frac{\left|\bigcup_{W\in{\mathcal{P}}_{1}(U)}{\mathcal{U}}^{\prime}(W)\right|}{|{\mathcal{U}}^{\prime}|}\leqslant\frac{|{\mathcal{P}}_{1}(U)||{\mathcal{P}}_{1}(U^{\prime})|}{|{\mathcal{P}}_{1}(V)|}=\vcentcolon\frac{c_{2}}{q|\mathbb{F}|}

obtaining an explicit estimate for

.
In the unitary case
G(V)
is also transitive on the non-degenerate

1
-subspaces of
V
and we show in the proof of Theorem

(see Equation (
9
)), that

(5)

\frac{\left|\bigcup_{W\in{\mathcal{N}}_{1}(U)}{\mathcal{U}}^{\prime}(W)\right|}{|{\mathcal{U}}^{\prime}|}\leqslant\frac{|{\mathcal{N}}_{1}(U)||{\mathcal{N}}_{1}(U^{\prime})|}{|{\mathcal{N}}_{1}(V)|}=\vcentcolon\frac{c_{1,\bf U}}{|\mathbb{F}|}

and find an explicit estimate for
c_{1,\bf U}
.

The orthogonal case requires a more delicate analysis because the

has at most two orbits on non-degenerate
1

of

. For this reason, the definition (

(6)

\frac{\left|\bigcup_{W\in{\mathcal{N}}_{1}(U)}{\mathcal{U}}^{\prime}(W)\right|}{|{\mathcal{U}}^{\prime}|}\leqslant\frac{\sum_{W\in{\mathcal{N}}_{1}(U)}|{\mathcal{U}}^{\prime}(W)|}{|{\mathcal{U}}^{\prime}|}=\vcentcolon\frac{c_{1,\bf O}}{|\mathbb{F}|}.

\varphi\leqslant\frac{c_{1,\bf U}}{|{\mathbb{F}}|}+\frac{c_{2}}{q|{\mathbb{F}}|}\qquad\textup{and}\qquad\varphi\leqslant\frac{c_{1,\bf O}}{|{\mathbb{F}}|}+\frac{c_{2}}{q|{\mathbb{F}}|}.

and

.
In the orthogonal case, our proof needs

.

and

3
Double counting lemma

The following simple result is surprisingly effective in our numerous
different contexts when solving estimation problems.
Let
{\mathcal{W}}
and
{\mathcal{U}}^{\prime}
be sets of subspaces of
V
. Define

\displaystyle{\mathcal{U}}^{\prime}(W)

\displaystyle=\{U^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{U}}^{\prime}\mid W\leqslant U^{\prime}\}\quad\,\textup{for fixed $W\in{\mathcal{W}}$, and}

\displaystyle{\mathcal{W}}(U^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\{W\in{\mathcal{W}}\mid W\leqslant U^{\prime}\}\quad\textup{for fixed $U^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{U}}^{\prime}$}.

Lemma 3.1

(Double counting lemma)
.

Let
G\leqslant{\mathrm{GL}}(V)
and let
{\mathcal{W}}
and
{\mathcal{U}}^{\prime}
be
G
-orbits on the set of all
subspaces of
V
.
For fixed
W\in{\mathcal{W}}
,
U^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{U}}^{\prime}
and
{\mathcal{U}}^{\prime}(W),{\mathcal{W}}(U^{\prime})
as above, we have

|{\mathcal{W}}|\cdot|{\mathcal{U}}^{\prime}(W)|=|{\mathcal{W}}(U^{\prime})|\cdot|{\mathcal{U}}^{\prime}|.

Proof.

Let
\Omega=\{(W,U^{\prime})\in{\mathcal{W}}\times{\mathcal{U}}^{\prime}\mid W\leqslant U^{\prime}\}
. Observe that

\{(W,U^{\prime})\mid W\in{\mathcal{W}},U^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{U}}^{\prime}(W)\}=\Omega=\{(W,U^{\prime})\mid U^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{U}}^{\prime},W\in{\mathcal{W}}(U^{\prime})\}.

Since
{\mathcal{W}}
and
{\mathcal{U}}^{\prime}
are
G
-invariant, it is easy to show
for all
g\in G
and fixed
(W,U^{\prime})\in{\mathcal{W}}\times{\mathcal{U}}^{\prime}
that
{\mathcal{U}}^{\prime}(W)^{g}={\mathcal{U}}^{\prime}(W^{g})

and
{\mathcal{W}}(U^{\prime})^{g}={\mathcal{W}}((U^{\prime})^{g})
. Thus we have

|{\mathcal{U}}^{\prime}(W^{g})|=|{\mathcal{U}}^{\prime}(W)|
and
|{\mathcal{W}}((U^{\prime})^{g})|=|{\mathcal{W}}(U^{\prime})|
for all
g\in G
.
It follows from the above display that

|{\mathcal{W}}|\cdot|{\mathcal{U}}^{\prime}(W)|=|\Omega|=|{\mathcal{W}}(U^{\prime})|\cdot|{\mathcal{U}}^{\prime}|

for any chosen
(W,U^{\prime})\in{\mathcal{W}}\times{\mathcal{U}}^{\prime}
, as claimed.
∎

, and let

.

Theorem 4.1
.

Suppose that
n,n^{\prime}\geqslant 1
and
V=({\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}})^{n+n^{\prime}}
is a non-degenerate hermitian
space. Let
{\mathcal{U}}={\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{V}{n}}
and
{\mathcal{U}}^{\prime}={\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{V}{n'}}
,
and fix
U\in{\mathcal{U}}
. Then

\varphi=\frac{|\{U^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{U}}^{\prime}\mid U\cap U^{\prime}\neq 0\}|}{|{\mathcal{U}}^{\prime}|}\leqslant\frac{|{\mathcal{N}}_{1}(U)||{\mathcal{N}}_{1}(U^{\prime})|}{|{\mathcal{N}}_{1}(V)|}+\frac{|{\mathcal{P}}_{1}(U)||{\mathcal{P}}_{1}(U^{\prime})|}{|{\mathcal{P}}_{1}(V)|}\leqslant\frac{9}{5q^{2}}

holds if
(n,n^{\prime},q)\neq(1,1,2)
, and
\varphi\leqslant\frac{2}{q^{2}}
always holds.

We need the following technical lemma which estimates
certain polynomials in
-q^{-1}
.

Lemma 4.2
.

Suppose
\zeta_{n}\vcentcolon=\zeta_{n}(q)=(1-(-q)^{-n+1})(1-(-q)^{-n})
and
q\geqslant 2
. Then

(a)

0=\zeta_{1}<\zeta_{3}<\zeta_{5}<\cdots<1<\cdots<\zeta_{6}<\zeta_{4}<\zeta_{2}=(1+q^{-1})(1-q^{-2})
,

(b)

\zeta_{2i+2}\zeta_{2j}<\zeta_{2i}\zeta_{2j+2}
for
1\leqslant i<j
,

(c)

\zeta_{n}\zeta_{n^{\prime}}\zeta_{4}\leqslant\zeta_{2}^{2}\zeta_{n+n^{\prime}}
for
n,n^{\prime}\geqslant 1
.

Proof.

(a) Suppose that
i\geqslant 1
. By definition, the inequality

\zeta_{2i-1}<\zeta_{2i+1}
is

(1-q^{-2i+2})(1+q^{-2i+1})<(1-q^{-2i})(1+q^{-2i-1}).

After subtracting
1
from both sides, multiplying by
q^{4i+1}
,
and rearranging, this is equivalent to

q^{2i+2}+q^{2i+1}+1<q^{2i+3}+q^{2i}+q^{4}.

The stronger inequality
q^{2i+2}+q^{2i+1}+1<q^{2i+3}
is true for

q\geqslant 2
. Therefore
\zeta_{2i-1}<\zeta_{2i+1}
holds, and
since
\lim_{i\to\infty}\zeta_{2i+1}=1
we see that

0=\zeta_{1}<\zeta_{3}<\zeta_{5}<\cdots<1
holds.

Similarly, by definition, the inequality
\zeta_{2i+2}<\zeta_{2i}
is

(1+q^{-2i-1})(1-q^{-2i-2})<(1+q^{-2i+1})(1-q^{-2i}),

and after subtracting
1
from both sides, multiplying by
q^{4i+3}
,
and rearranging, this is equivalent to

q^{2i+3}+q^{2i+2}+q^{4}<q^{2i+4}+q^{2i+1}+1.

Since,
i\geqslant 1
and
q\geqslant 2
, we have

q^{2i+3}+q^{2i+2}+q^{4}\leqslant q^{2i}(q^{3}+q^{2}+q^{2})\leqslant q^{2i+4}<q^{2i+4}+q^{2i+1}+1.

Hence
\zeta_{2i+2}<\zeta_{2i}
holds, and since

\lim_{i\to\infty}\zeta_{2i}=1
we see that

1<\cdots<\zeta_{6}<\zeta_{4}<\zeta_{2}=(1+q^{-1})(1-q^{-2})
holds, proving part (a).

(b) Observe first that

\zeta_{2i}=q^{-4i+1}(q^{2i-1}+1)(q^{2i}-1)
. Replacing
i
with
i+1
gives

\zeta_{2i+2}=q^{-4i-3}(q^{2i+1}+1)(q^{2i+2}-1)
. Hence
\zeta_{2i}\zeta_{2j+2}-\zeta_{2i+2}\zeta_{2j}
equals

q^{-4i-4j-2}x
where
x
is the following difference

(q^{2i-1}{+}1)(q^{2i}{-}1)(q^{2j+1}{+}1)(q^{2j+2}{-}1)-(q^{2i+1}{+}1)(q^{2i+2}{-}1)(q^{2j-1}{+}1)(q^{2j}{-}1).

Mathematica

Wolfram
shows that

x=q^{-1}(q^{2}-1)(q^{2j}-q^{2i})y
where

y=q^{2i+2j+2}-q^{2i+2j+1}-q^{2j+2}-q^{2j}-q^{2i+2}-q^{2i}-q+1.

Suppose that
1\leqslant i<j
. Proving

\zeta_{2i}\zeta_{2j+2}-\zeta_{2i+2}\zeta_{2j}>0
is equivalent to proving
x>0
,
which is equivalent to proving
y>0
. If the exponents of
q
are
distinct, that is if
i+1<j
, then this is clear. If
i+1=j
, then

y=q^{2i+2j+2}-q^{2i+2j+1}-q^{2j+2}-2q^{2j}-q^{2i}-q+1
and
2q^{2j}\leqslant q^{2j+1}

and it is also clear that
y>0
.
Consequently,
\zeta_{2i+2}\zeta_{2j}<\zeta_{2i}\zeta_{2j+2}
as claimed.

(c) If
n
is even and
n^{\prime}
is odd, then part (a) shows that

\zeta_{n}\leqslant\zeta_{2}
,
\zeta_{n^{\prime}}<\zeta_{n+n^{\prime}}
,
\zeta_{4}<\zeta_{2}
and
hence
\zeta_{n}\zeta_{n^{\prime}}\zeta_{4}\leqslant\zeta_{2}^{2}\zeta_{n+n^{\prime}}
.
The case when
n
is odd and
n^{\prime}
is even is proved similarly.
If both
n
and
n^{\prime}
are odd, then

\zeta_{n^{\prime}}<\zeta_{2}
,
\zeta_{n}<\zeta_{n+n^{\prime}}
and
\zeta_{4}<\zeta_{2}

by part (a),
and hence
\zeta_{n}\zeta_{n^{\prime}}\zeta_{4}\leqslant\zeta_{2}^{2}\zeta_{n+n^{\prime}}
.
Finally, suppose that both
n
and
n^{\prime}
are even.
By symmetry, we may assume that
n\leqslant n^{\prime}
. The inequality holds trivially
if
n=n^{\prime}=2
, so we may assume in addition that
n^{\prime}\geqslant 4
.
Then
\zeta_{4}\zeta_{n^{\prime}}\leqslant\zeta_{2}\zeta_{n^{\prime}+2}
by part (b)
(with

Hence

.
Then
\zeta_{n}\zeta_{n^{\prime}+2}<\zeta_{n-2}\zeta_{n^{\prime}+4}<\cdots<\zeta_{2}\zeta_{n+n^{\prime}}

Proof of Theorem
4.1
.

Let

,
{\mathcal{U}}={\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{V}{n}}
,
{\mathcal{U}}^{\prime}={\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{V}{n'}}
and
fix

.
The isometry group
G=G(V)
has two orbits on the 1-subspaces of

,
namely the set of isotropic 1-subspaces
{\mathcal{P}}_{1}(V)
and the set of non-degenerate 1-subspaces

{\mathcal{N}}_{1}(V)
. Similarly,
G_{U}
has two orbits on the 1-subspaces of

,
namely
{\mathcal{P}}_{1}(U)
and
{\mathcal{N}}_{1}(U)

and

W_{\mathcal{P}}\in{\mathcal{P}}_{1}(U)
. Then

\displaystyle\left|\cup_{W\in{\mathcal{N}}_{1}(U)\cup{\mathcal{P}}_{1}(U)}\,{\mathcal{U}}^{\prime}(W)\right|

\displaystyle\leqslant\left|\cup_{W\in{\mathcal{N}}_{1}(U)}{\mathcal{U}}^{\prime}(W)\right|+\left|\cup_{W\in{\mathcal{P}}_{1}(U)}{\mathcal{U}}^{\prime}(W)\right|

\displaystyle\leqslant|{\mathcal{N}}_{1}(U)|\cdot|{\mathcal{U}}^{\prime}(W_{\mathcal{N}})|+|{\mathcal{P}}_{1}(U)|\cdot|{\mathcal{U}}^{\prime}(W_{\mathcal{P}})|.

We apply the double counting lemma (Lemma
3.1
) with
G=G(V)

and a fixed
U^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{U}}^{\prime}={\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{V}{n'}}
,
first with

to obtain

|{\mathcal{N}}_{1}(V)|\cdot|{\mathcal{U}}^{\prime}(W_{\mathcal{N}})|=|{\mathcal{N}}_{1}(U^{\prime})|\cdot|{\mathcal{U}}^{\prime}|
, and then
with
{\mathcal{W}}={\mathcal{P}}_{1}(V)
to obtain

|{\mathcal{P}}_{1}(V)|\cdot|{\mathcal{U}}^{\prime}(W_{\mathcal{P}})|=|{\mathcal{P}}_{1}(U^{\prime})|\cdot|{\mathcal{U}}^{\prime}|
. Hence, substituting
into the above display yields

\varphi=\frac{\left|\bigcup_{W\in{\mathcal{N}}_{1}(U)\cup{\mathcal{P}}_{1}(U)}{\mathcal{U}}^{\prime}(W)\right|}{|{\mathcal{U}}^{\prime}|}\leqslant\frac{|{\mathcal{N}}_{1}(U)||{\mathcal{N}}_{1}(U^{\prime})|}{|{\mathcal{N}}_{1}(V)|}+\frac{|{\mathcal{P}}_{1}(U)||{\mathcal{P}}_{1}(U^{\prime})|}{|{\mathcal{P}}_{1}(V)|}.

A formula for
|{\mathcal{P}}_{1}(U)|
is given in
Taylor
*10.4, p. 117
and since
|{\mathcal{N}}_{1}(U)|+|{\mathcal{P}}_{1}(U)|
equals the number
(q^{2n}-1)/(q^{2}-1)

of 1-subspaces of

, we have

\displaystyle|{\mathcal{N}}_{1}(U)|

\displaystyle=\frac{q^{2n-2}(1-(-q)^{-n})}{1+q^{-1}}\quad\textup{and}

\displaystyle|{\mathcal{P}}_{1}(U)|

\displaystyle=\frac{q^{2n-3}(1-(-q)^{-n+1})(1-(-q)^{-n})}{1-q^{-2}}.

Using the convenient notation
\vartheta_{n}=1-(-q)^{-n}
and

\zeta_{n}=\vartheta_{n-1}\vartheta_{n}
, these expressions become

(8)

|{\mathcal{N}}_{1}(U)|=\frac{q^{2n-2}\vartheta_{n}}{1+q^{-1}},\quad\textup{and}\quad|{\mathcal{P}}_{1}(U)|=\frac{q^{2n-3}\zeta_{n}}{1-q^{-2}}.

Thus

(9)

\displaystyle\frac{\left|\cup_{W\in{\mathcal{N}}_{1}(U)}{\mathcal{U}}^{\prime}(W)\right|}{|{\mathcal{U}}^{\prime}|}

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{|{\mathcal{N}}_{1}(U)|\cdot|{\mathcal{N}}_{1}(U^{\prime})|}{|{\mathcal{N}}_{1}(V)|}=\vcentcolon\frac{c_{1}}{q^{2}}\quad\textup{where by~\eqref{E:N1P1},}

\displaystyle c_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{\vartheta_{n}\vartheta_{n^{\prime}}}{(1+q^{-1})\vartheta_{n+n^{\prime}}},

and

(10)

\displaystyle\frac{\left|\cup_{W\in{\mathcal{P}}_{1}(U)}{\mathcal{U}}^{\prime}(W)\right|}{|{\mathcal{U}}^{\prime}|}

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{|{\mathcal{P}}_{1}(U)|\cdot|{\mathcal{P}}_{1}(U^{\prime})|}{|{\mathcal{P}}_{1}(V)|}=\vcentcolon\frac{c_{2}}{q^{3}}\quad\textup{where by~\eqref{E:N1P1},}

\displaystyle c_{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{\zeta_{n}\zeta_{n^{\prime}}}{(1-q^{-2})\zeta_{n+n^{\prime}}}.

Thus
\varphi\leqslant(c_{1}+c_{2}/q)q^{-2}
. We now bound
c_{1}+c_{2}/q
.

First suppose that
n=1
. Then
\vartheta_{1}=1+q^{-1}
and
\zeta_{1}=0

implies that
c_{1}=\vartheta_{n^{\prime}}/\vartheta_{n^{\prime}+1}
by (
9
) and
c_{2}=0

by (
10
). Therefore, when
n=1
,

c_{1}+\frac{c_{2}}{q}=c_{1}=\frac{\vartheta_{n^{\prime}}}{\vartheta_{n^{\prime}+1}}.

If
n^{\prime}
is even then
\theta_{n^{\prime}}<1<\theta_{n^{\prime}+1}
so
c_{1}<1<\frac{9}{5}
.
On the other hand if
n^{\prime}
is odd, then

c_{1}=\frac{1+q^{-n^{\prime}}}{1-q^{-n^{\prime}-1}}\leqslant\frac{1+q^{-1}}{1-q^{-2}}=\frac{1}{1-q^{-1}}<\frac{9}{5}
if
q\geqslant 3
.
If
(n,n^{\prime},q)=(1,1,2)
, we have
c_{1}+\frac{c_{2}}{q}=\frac{1+2^{-1}}{1-2^{-2}}=2
.
Hence, for
n=1
,
c_{1}+\frac{c_{2}}{q}\leqslant\frac{9}{5}
when

(n,n^{\prime},q)\neq(1,1,2)
, and
c_{1}+\frac{c_{2}}{q}
is always at most
2
, so
Theorem
4.1
is proved in this case.
As the expressions for
c_{1}
and
c_{2}
are symmetric in
n,n^{\prime}
,
it follows that Theorem
4.1
is also proved when
n^{\prime}=1
.

Thus we may (and will) assume henceforth that
n,n^{\prime}\geqslant 2
.
We first prove that
c_{1}<1
using the expression in (
9
).
Since
n,n^{\prime}\geqslant 2
, we have

\frac{3}{4}\leqslant 1-q^{-2}=\vartheta_{2}<\vartheta_{4}<\vartheta_{6}<\cdots<1<\cdots<\vartheta_{5}<\vartheta_{3}=1+q^{-3}\leqslant\frac{9}{8},

and hence
1-q^{-2}\leqslant\vartheta_{n},\vartheta_{n^{\prime}}\leqslant 1+q^{-3}
.
If
n,n^{\prime}
have different parities, then

c_{1}<\frac{1\cdot\vartheta_{3}}{(1+q^{-1})\cdot 1}=\frac{1+q^{-3}}{1+q^{-1}}=1-q^{-1}+q^{-2}<1.

If

are both even, then

c_{1}<\frac{1^{2}}{(1+q^{-1})\cdot\vartheta_{4}}=\frac{1}{(1+q^{-1})(1-q^{-4})}<1.

If
n,n^{\prime}
are both odd, then both

, and so

c_{1}\leqslant\frac{\vartheta_{3}^{2}}{(1+q^{-1})\cdot\vartheta_{6}}=\frac{(1+q^{-3})^{2}}{(1+q^{-1})\cdot(1-q^{-6})}=\frac{1-q^{-1}+q^{-2}}{1-q^{-3}}<1.

In summary, for

, we have

as claimed.

We now show that
c_{2}\leqslant\frac{8}{5}
when

.
By (

) and Lemma
4.2
(c),

\displaystyle c_{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{\zeta_{n}\zeta_{n^{\prime}}}{(1-q^{-2})\zeta_{n+n^{\prime}}}\leqslant\frac{\zeta_{2}^{2}}{(1-q^{-2})\zeta_{4}}=\frac{(1+q^{-1})\zeta_{2}}{\zeta_{4}}

\displaystyle=\frac{(1+q^{-1})^{2}(1-q^{-2})}{(1+q^{-3})(1-q^{-4})}=\frac{1+q^{-1}}{(1-q^{-1}+q^{-2})(1+q^{-2})}.

The last expression on the right above is a decreasing function of

. Setting

shows

. Thus
c_{1}+c_{2}/q<1+4/5=9/5
when

.

Thus we have proved, for all

,

and

, that

\varphi\leqslant\frac{|{\mathcal{N}}_{1}(U)|\cdot|{\mathcal{N}}_{1}(U^{\prime})|}{|{\mathcal{N}}_{1}(V)|}+\frac{|{\mathcal{P}}_{1}(U)|\cdot|{\mathcal{P}}_{1}(U^{\prime})|}{|{\mathcal{P}}_{1}(V)|}=\frac{c_{1}}{q^{2}}+\frac{c_{2}}{q^{3}}=\frac{c_{1}+c_{2}/q}{q^{2}}\leqslant\frac{9}{5q^{2}},

and when

, then
\varphi\leqslant\frac{2}{q^{2}}
.
This completes the proof.

5
The symplectic case

Let
V=({\mathbb{F}}_{q})^{n+n^{\prime}}
be a non-degenerate symplectic space where
n,n^{\prime}
are even, and
let
{\mathcal{U}}={\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{V}{n}}
and
{\mathcal{U}}^{\prime}={\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{V}{n'}}
, as in Section
2
.
In this section we prove Theorem
1.1
in the symplectic case by establishing the following result.

Theorem 5.1
.

Suppose that
n,n^{\prime}
are even and
V=({\mathbb{F}}_{q})^{n+n^{\prime}}
is a non-degenerate symplectic
space. Let
{\mathcal{U}}={\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{V}{n}}
and
{\mathcal{U}}^{\prime}={\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{V}{n'}}
, and fix
U\in{\mathcal{U}}
. Then

\varphi\vcentcolon=\frac{|\{U^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{U}}^{\prime}\colon U\cap U^{\prime}\neq 0\}|}{|{\mathcal{U}}^{\prime}|}<\frac{5}{3q}\qquad\textup{for all $n,n^{\prime},q$.}

Furthermore if either
q\geqslant 3
or

\min\{n,n^{\prime}\}=2
, then
\varphi<\frac{3}{2q}
.

As every 1-subspace of
U
is totally isotropic,
{\mathcal{N}}_{1}(U)
is empty.
Lemma
5.2
below proves Equation (
3
), and so justifies
the last sentence of Theorem
5.1
. In particular it proves
Theorem
5.1
for
q\geqslant 3
.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
V,n,n^{\prime},{\mathcal{U}},{\mathcal{U}}^{\prime}
and
\varphi
be as in Theorem
5.1
. Then

\varphi\leqslant\frac{|{\mathcal{P}}_{1}(U)|\cdot|{\mathcal{P}}_{1}(U^{\prime})|}{|{\mathcal{P}}_{1}(V)|}<\frac{1-q^{-\min\{n,n^{\prime}\}}}{q-1},

for all
U\in{\mathcal{U}}
and
U^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{U}}^{\prime}
.
Moreover, if
q\geqslant 3
, or if
\min\{n,n^{\prime}\}=2
, then
\varphi<\frac{3}{2q}<\frac{5}{3q}
.

Proof.

As all the 1-subspaces of
V
are isotropic,

|{\mathcal{P}}_{1}(V)|=(q^{n+n^{\prime}}-1)/(q-1)
and

\varphi=|\bigcup_{W^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{P}}_{1}(U)}{\mathcal{U}}^{\prime}(W^{\prime})|/|{\mathcal{U}}^{\prime}|
.
Since the restriction of
G_{U}
to
U
is
G(U)
, and
G(U)
is
transitive on
{\mathcal{P}}_{1}(U)
, we have, for a chosen
W\in{\mathcal{P}}_{1}(U)
,

\varphi\cdot|{\mathcal{U}}^{\prime}|=\left|\bigcup_{W^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{P}}_{1}(U)}{\mathcal{U}}^{\prime}(W^{\prime})\right|\leqslant|{\mathcal{P}}_{1}(U)|\cdot|{\mathcal{U}}^{\prime}(W)|.

Applying Lemma
3.1
with
G
the isometry group
{\mathrm{Sp}}(V)
of
V
,

{\mathcal{U}}^{\prime}={\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{V}{n'}}
,
{\mathcal{W}}={\mathcal{P}}_{1}(V)
and fixed
U^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{U}}^{\prime}
,
W\in{\mathcal{W}}
,
we obtain
|{\mathcal{P}}_{1}(V)|\cdot|{\mathcal{U}}^{\prime}(W)|=|{\mathcal{P}}_{1}(U^{\prime})|\cdot|{\mathcal{U}}^{\prime}|
. Thus

\displaystyle\varphi

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{|{\mathcal{P}}_{1}(U)|\cdot|{\mathcal{U}}^{\prime}(W)|}{|{\mathcal{U}}^{\prime}|}=\frac{|{\mathcal{P}}_{1}(U)|\cdot|{\mathcal{P}}_{1}(U^{\prime})|}{|{\mathcal{P}}_{1}(V)|}=\frac{(q^{n}-1)(q^{n^{\prime}}-1)}{(q-1)(q^{n+n^{\prime}}-1)}

\displaystyle=\frac{(1-q^{-n})(1-q^{-n^{\prime}})}{(q-1)(1-q^{-n-n^{\prime}})}<\frac{1-q^{-\min\{n,n^{\prime}\}}}{q-1}.

If
q\geqslant 3
, then
\varphi<\frac{1}{q-1}\leqslant\frac{3}{2q}<\frac{5}{3q}
.
Finally, if
\min\{n,n^{\prime}\}=2
, then

\varphi<\frac{1-q^{-2}}{q-1}=\frac{1-q^{-2}}{q(1-q^{-1})}=\frac{1+q^{-1}}{q}\leqslant\frac{3}{2q}<\frac{5}{3q}.\qed

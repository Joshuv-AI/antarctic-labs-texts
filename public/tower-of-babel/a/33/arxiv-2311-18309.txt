Abstract
. In this paper, we present a method to construct the Leech lattice from other Niemeier lattices.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
11H06

1.
Introduction

The Leech lattice
is an important mathematical object.
Various constructions of this beautiful lattice are known.
In this paper, we give a method to construct
the Leech lattice as modifications of other Niemeier lattices.

Let
N
be a Niemeier lattice with the intersection form
\langle\phantom{a},\phantom{a}\rangle_{N}
. Suppose that
N
is not isomorphic to the Leech lattice.
Let
R
be the set of vectors in
N
of square-norm
2
,
and
\langle{R}\rangle\subset N
the sublattice generated by
R
.
Then
N/\langle{R}\rangle
is a finite abelian group, which we call the
code
of
N
.
(In
[
6
]
, it is called the
glue code
.)
For each codeword of this code,
we give a method to construct the Leech lattice.
The main result is given in Theorem
7.8
.

Considering the case where this codeword is
0
,
we obtain the following.
Let
N
and
R
be as above.
We choose a simple root system
\Theta
of
R
,
that is,
\Theta
is a set of vectors in
R
that form a basis of
\langle{R}\rangle

with the dual graph being a Dynkin diagram of an ordinary
\mathrm{ADE}
-type.
Then we have a vector
\rho\in N\otimes\mathord{\mathbb{Q}}
such that
\langle r,\rho\rangle_{N}=-1
for all
r\in\Theta
.
In fact, we have
\rho\in N
.
Let
h
be the Coxeter number of
N
.

Corollary 1.1
.

The
\mathord{\mathbb{Z}}
-module

\{\,{u\in N}\mid{\langle u,\rho\rangle_{N}\equiv 0\bmod 2h+1}\,\}

with the quadratic form

u\mapsto\langle u,u\rangle_{N}-\frac{2}{(2h+1)^{2}}\,(\langle u,\rho\rangle_{N})^{2}

is isomorphic to the Leech lattice.

\Box

Our construction is similar to the twenty-three holy constructions
of Conway and Sloane
[
7
]
.
See also
[
5
]
.
In the proof of our construction,
the classification of deep holes of the Leech lattice
[
4
]

and
the determination of the fundamental domain of the Weyl group
of the even unimodular Lorentzian lattice
\mathord{I\hskip-0.7ptI}_{25,1}

[
3
]

play important roles.
Some ideas
in our construction have already appeared in Borcherds
[
1
]
.

A novelty of our approach is that we use the geometry of
K3
surfaces
as a heuristic guide.
Let
\mathbb{X}
be a
K3
surface whose Néron-Severi lattice
S_{\mathbb{X}}

is an even unimodular lattice
\mathord{I\hskip-0.7ptI}_{1,25}
of signature
(1,25)
.
It is needless to say that such a
K3
surface
\mathbb{X}
does
not
exist.
Applying the lattice theoretic tools for the study of
K3
surfaces to this
virtual

K3
surface
\mathbb{X}
,
however,
we can rephrase the above mentioned results of Conway et al.
[
3
,
4
]

in terms of geometry of
K3
surfaces.
Then, using an algorithm
that we have developed in
[
14
]
for the study of
K3
surfaces,
we obtain our construction.

Note that, in the proof of our main result (Theorem
7.8
), we do not use
\mathbb{X}
,
and hence
the result is rigorously correct.
In fact, we can verify our result by direct computations.
See Remark
7.10
.
Computational data relevant to this paper is presented in
[
15
]

in the format of
GAP

[
10
]
.

Notation.

A lattice is a free
\mathord{\mathbb{Z}}
-module of finite rank
with a non-degenerate symmetric bilinear form that takes values in
\mathord{\mathbb{Z}}
.
This bilinear form is called the
intersection form
of the lattice.
For a lattice
M
with the intersection form
\langle\phantom{i},\phantom{i}\rangle_{M}
,
let
M^{-}
denote the lattice whose underlying
\mathord{\mathbb{Z}}
-module is
M

and whose intersection form is equal to
\langle\phantom{i},\phantom{i}\rangle^{-}_{M}:=-\langle\phantom{i},\phantom{i}\rangle_{M}
.

Let
\Lambda
be the Leech lattice.
In this paper, we construct
the
negative-definite
Leech lattice
\Lambda^{-}
from

negative-definite
Niemeier lattices
N^{-}
using
the result on the lattice
\mathord{I\hskip-0.7ptI}_{25,1}^{-}=\mathord{I\hskip-0.7ptI}_{1,25}
.
We make this change of sign
because we want to use ideas coming from the geometry of algebraic surfaces.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Roots and reflections

A lattice
M
is
even
if
\langle v,v\rangle_{M}\in 2\mathord{\mathbb{Z}}
holds for all
v\in M
.
Let
M
be an even lattice.
A vector
r
of
M
is said to be a
root
if
|\langle r,r\rangle_{M}|=2
.
A lattice is said to be a
root lattice
if it is generated by roots.
A root
r
of
M
with
\langle r,r\rangle_{M}=\pm 2
defines the
reflection

s_{r}\colon x\mapsto x\mp\langle x,r\rangle_{M}\cdot r,

which is an element of the automorphism group
\mathord{\mathrm{O}}(M)
of the lattice
M
.
Let
M^{\vee}
denote the
dual lattice

\{\,{x\in M\otimes\mathord{\mathbb{Q}}}\mid{\langle x,v\rangle_{M}\in\mathord{\mathbb{Z}}\;\;\textrm{for all}\;\;v\in M}\,\}.

The
discriminant group
of
M
is
the finite abelian group
M^{\vee}/M
.
The group
\mathord{\mathrm{O}}(M)
acts on
M^{\vee}
, and hence on
M^{\vee}/M
.
Since
s_{r}(x)-x\in M
holds for any
x\in M^{\vee}
,
the reflections
s_{r}\in\mathord{\mathrm{O}}(M)
act on
M^{\vee}/M
trivially.

2.2.
\mathrm{ADE}
-configurations

A
(-2)
-vector
of an even lattice
M
is a vector
r\in M
such that
\langle r,r\rangle_{M}=-2
.
Let
\{r_{1},\dots,r_{m}\}
be a set of
(-2)
-vectors of an even lattice
M

such that
\langle r_{i},r_{j}\rangle_{M}\in\{0,1,2\}
holds for any
i,j
with
i\neq j
.
The
dual graph
of
\{r_{1},\dots,r_{m}\}
is the graph whose set of nodes is
\{r_{1},\dots,r_{m}\}

and whose set of simple edges (resp. of double edges) is the set of pairs
\{r_{i},r_{j}\}
such that
\langle r_{i},r_{j}\rangle_{M}=1
(resp.
\langle r_{i},r_{j}\rangle_{M}=2
).
(A double edge appears only in extended
\mathrm{ADE}
-configuration whose
\mathrm{ADE}
-type contains
A_{1}
.)
We say that
\{r_{1},\dots,r_{m}\}
forms an
ordinary
\mathrm{ADE}
-configuration
(resp. an
extended
\mathrm{ADE}
-configuration
)
if the dual graph is the ordinary Dynkin diagram (resp. the extended Dynkin diagram) of type
\mathrm{ADE}
,
and in this case, we define
the type of the configuration
to be the type of the dual graph.

Let
M
be a negative-definite root lattice, and

R
the set of
(-2)
-vectors in
M
.
We have
M=\langle{R}\rangle
.
A
simple root system
of
M
is a subset of
R
that is a basis of
M
and
that forms an ordinary
\mathrm{ADE}
-configuration.
A negative-definite root lattice always has a simple root system.
The set of simple root systems of
M
is described as follows.
We denote by
W(M)

the subgroup of
\mathord{\mathrm{O}}(M)

generated by all the reflections

s_{r}
with respect to
r\in R
,
and call it the
Weyl group
of
M
.
We consider the unit sphere

S:=\{\,{x\in M\otimes\mathord{\mathbb{R}}}\mid{\langle x,x\rangle_{M}=-1}\,\}.

For
r\in R
, we put

(r)^{\perp}:=\{\,{x\in S}\mid{\langle x,r\rangle_{M}=0}\,\},\quad H^{+}(r):=\{\,{x\in S}\mid{\langle x,r\rangle_{M}>0}\,\},

and set

S^{\mathord{\circ}}:=S\;\setminus\;\bigcup_{r\in R}\;(r)^{\perp}.

Then
W(M)
acts on the set of connected components of
S^{\mathord{\circ}}

simple-transitively.
A subset
\Theta
of
R
is a simple root system of
M

if and only if the space

\Delta(\Theta):=\bigcap_{r\in\Theta}\;H^{+}(r)

is a connected component of
S^{\mathord{\circ}}
and,
for each
r\in\Theta
,
the intersection

(r)^{\perp}\cap\overline{\Delta(\Theta)}
contains
a non-empty open subset of
(r)^{\perp}
, where
\overline{\Delta(\Theta)}

is the closure of
\Delta(\Theta)
in
S
.
Therefore

W(M)
acts on the set of simple root systems of
M

simple-transitively.

2.3.
The coefficients of the highest root

Let
\widetilde{\Sigma}
be a set of
(-2)
-vectors
that form an extended
\mathrm{ADE}
-configuration of type
\tau

with the dual graph being
connected
,
that is, we have
\tau=A_{l}
or
\tau=D_{m}
or
\tau=E_{n}
.
We choose a vector
\theta\in\Sigma
such that
\Sigma:=\widetilde{\Sigma}\setminus\{\theta\}

forms an ordinary
\mathrm{ADE}
-configuration of type
\tau
.
Then
\Sigma
is a simple root system
of the negative-definite root lattice
\langle{\Sigma}\rangle
.
Let
\mu\in\langle{\Sigma}\rangle
be the highest root with respect to
\Sigma
(see
[
9
, Section 1.5]
for the definition).
We define a function
m\colon\widetilde{\Sigma}\to\mathord{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}
by

m(r):=\begin{cases}1&\textrm{if $r=\theta$},\\
\textrm{the coefficient of $r$ in $\mu$}&\textrm{if $r\in\Sigma$}.\end{cases}

In fact,
the function
m

does not depend on the choice of
\theta\in\widetilde{\Sigma}
.
We have
m(r)=1
if and only if
\widetilde{\Sigma}\setminus\{r\}
forms an ordinary
\mathrm{ADE}
-configuration of type
\tau
.
The values of the function
m
for each connected
\mathrm{ADE}
-type
\tau

can be found in
[
8
, Figure 23.1]
or in
[
9
, Figure 1.8]
.

Let
\mathord{\mathbb{Z}}^{\widetilde{\Sigma}}
be the
\mathord{\mathbb{Z}}
-module of functions
\widetilde{\Sigma}\to\mathord{\mathbb{Z}}
.
We can also define
m
as
the function
\widetilde{\Sigma}\to\mathord{\mathbb{Z}}
that takes values in
\mathord{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}
and that is
the generator of the following
\mathord{\mathbb{Z}}
-submodule of of
\mathord{\mathbb{Z}}^{\widetilde{\Sigma}}
:

\{\,{(\;x(r)\;)\in\mathord{\mathbb{Z}}^{\widetilde{\Sigma}}}\mid{\textstyle{\sum_{r\in\widetilde{\Sigma}}}\;x(r)\langle r,r^{\prime}\rangle=0\;\;\textrm{for all $r^{\prime}\in\widetilde{\Sigma}$}}\,\}.

2.4.
Hyperbolic lattices

We say that
a lattice
M
of rank
n>1
is
hyperbolic
if its signature is
(1,n-1)
.

Example 2.1
.

If
n
is a positive integer satisfying
n\equiv 2\bmod 8
,
then there exists an even unimodular hyperbolic lattice
L_{n}
of rank
n
, and
L_{n}
is unique up to isomorphism.
The lattice
L_{26}\cong\mathord{I\hskip-0.7ptI}_{1,25}
plays a central role in this paper.

Example 2.2
.

The
hyperbolic plane

U
is
the even unimodular hyperbolic lattice
L_{2}
of rank
2
.
We fix a basis
u_{0},u_{1}
of
U
such that the Gram matrix of
U
with respect to
u_{0},u_{1}
is

\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}0&1\\
1&0\end{array}\right].

Let
M
be an even hyperbolic lattice.
A
positive cone
of
M
is
one of the two connected components of
the space
\{\,{x\in M\otimes\mathord{\mathbb{R}}}\mid{\langle x,x\rangle_{M}>0}\,\}
.
Let
\mathord{\mathcal{P}}_{M}
be a positive cone of
M
.
We denote by
\overline{\mathord{\mathcal{P}}}_{M}
the closure of
\mathord{\mathcal{P}}_{M}
in
M\otimes\mathord{\mathbb{R}}
,
and by
\partial\,\overline{\mathord{\mathcal{P}}}_{M}
the boundary
\overline{\mathord{\mathcal{P}}}_{M}\setminus\mathord{\mathcal{P}}_{M}
.
We put

\mathord{\mathcal{R}}_{M}:=\{\,{r\in M}\mid{\langle r,r\rangle_{M}=-2}\,\},

and, for
r\in\mathord{\mathcal{R}}_{M}
, let
(r)^{\perp}
be the hyperplane

\{\,{x\in\mathord{\mathcal{P}}_{M}}\mid{\langle x,r\rangle_{M}=0}\,\}
in
\mathord{\mathcal{P}}_{M}
.
The
Weyl group

W(M)

is a subgroup of
\mathord{\mathrm{O}}(M)
generated by all the reflections

s_{r}

associated with
r\in\mathord{\mathcal{R}}_{M}
.
Then
W(M)
acts on
\mathord{\mathcal{P}}_{M}
.

Definition 2.3
.

A
standard fundamental domain of
W(M)
is the closure in
\mathord{\mathcal{P}}_{M}
of a connected component
of

\mathord{\mathcal{P}}_{M}\;\setminus\;\bigcup_{r\in\mathord{\mathcal{R}}_{M}}(r)^{\perp}.

Let
D
be a standard fundamental domain of
W(M)
.
We say that
r\in\mathord{\mathcal{R}}_{M}

defines a wall of
D

if
D\cap(r)^{\perp}
contains a non-empty open subset of

(r)^{\perp}
and
\langle x,r\rangle_{M}\geq 0
holds for all
x\in D
.

Note that, contrary to the case where
M
is a negative-definite root lattice,
a standard fundamental domain of
W(M)
in the positive cone of hyperbolic lattice may have infinitely many walls.

3.
Niemeier lattices

A
Niemeier lattice
is an even positive-definite unimodular lattice of rank
24
.
Niemeier
[
11
]
classified
Niemeier lattices.
It was shown in
[
11
]
that, up to isomorphism,
there exist exactly
24
Niemeier lattices,
that one of them contains no roots,
whereas each of the other
23
lattices contains a sublattice of finite index generated by roots.
See also
[
6
]
.
The Niemeier lattice containing no roots is called the
Leech lattice
,
and is denoted by
\Lambda
.

Let
N
be a Niemeier lattice with roots.
Let
R
be the set of
(-2)
-vectors of
N^{-}
,
and
\langle{R}\rangle
the sublattice of
N^{-}
generated by
R
.
Then the
code

N^{-}/\langle{R}\rangle
of
N^{-}
is a subgroup of the discriminant group
\langle{R}\rangle^{\vee}/\langle{R}\rangle
.
Let
\Theta\subset R
be a simple root system of
\langle{R}\rangle
,
and
let
\tau(N)
denote the
\mathrm{ADE}
-type of
\Theta
.
We call
\tau(N)
the
\mathrm{ADE}
-type
of
N^{-}
.
Note that
\mathord{\mathrm{O}}(N^{-})
is a subgroup of
\mathord{\mathrm{O}}(\langle{R}\rangle)
.
More precisely, we have

\mathord{\mathrm{O}}(N^{-})=\{\,{g\in\mathord{\mathrm{O}}(\langle{R}\rangle)}\mid{\textrm{the action of $g$ on $\langle{R}\rangle^{\vee}/\langle{R}\rangle$ preserves $N^{-}/\langle{R}\rangle$}}\,\}.

Since the subgroup
W(\langle{R}\rangle)
of
\mathord{\mathrm{O}}(\langle{R}\rangle)

acts on
\langle{R}\rangle^{\vee}/\langle{R}\rangle
trivially,
we see that
W(\langle{R}\rangle)
is contained in
\mathord{\mathrm{O}}(N^{-})
.
Since
W(\langle{R}\rangle)
acts on the set of simple root systems of
\langle{R}\rangle

transitively, we have the following:

Proposition 3.1
.

The group
\mathord{\mathrm{O}}(N^{-})
acts on the set of simple root systems of
\langle{R}\rangle
transitively.

\Box

We write

(3.1)

\tau(N)=\tau(N)_{1}+\cdots+\tau(N)_{K},

where
\tau(N)_{1},\dots,\tau(N)_{K}
are the
\mathrm{ADE}
-types of the connected components of the dual graph of
\Theta
.
Accordingly, we obtain the decompositions

(3.2)

\Theta=\Theta_{1}\sqcup\dots\sqcup\Theta_{K},\quad R=R_{1}\sqcup\dots\sqcup R_{K},

in such a way that

\Theta_{i}\subset R_{i}
is a simple root system
of the root lattice
\langle{R_{i}}\rangle
of type
\tau(N)_{i}
.
We put

n_{i}:=\mathrm{Card}(\Theta_{i})=\operatorname{\mathrm{rank}}\nolimits{\langle{R_{i}}\rangle}.

Then we have
24=n_{1}+\dots+n_{K}
.

Definition 3.2
.

The
Coxeter number

h_{i}
of
R_{i}

is defined by any of the following:

(a)

\mathrm{Card}(R_{i})=n_{i}h_{i}
.

(b)

Let
\rho_{i}\in\langle{R_{i}}\rangle\otimes\mathord{\mathbb{Q}}
be the vector satisfying
\langle\rho_{i},r\rangle_{N}^{-}=1
for all
r\in\Theta_{i}
.
Then we have
\langle\rho_{i},\rho_{i}\rangle_{N}^{-}=-n_{i}h_{i}(h_{i}+1)/12
.

(c)

Let
\mu_{i}\in R_{i}
denote the highest root with respect to
\Theta_{i}
.
(See Section 1.5 of
[
9
]
).
Then we have
h_{i}=\langle\mu_{i},\rho_{i}\rangle_{N}^{-}+1
, that is,

h_{i}-1
is the sum of the coefficients
m(r)
of
\mu_{i}
expressed as a linear combination of vectors
r\in\Theta_{i}
,
where
m
is the function defined in Section
2.3
.

(d)

The product of all the reflections
s_{r}
with respect to
r\in\Theta_{i}
(where the product is taken in arbitrary order)
is of order
h_{i}
in
\mathord{\mathrm{O}}(\langle{R_{i}}\rangle)
.

A remarkable fact about the
\mathrm{ADE}
-type
\tau(N)
is that

h_{i}
does not depend on
i
.
We put

h:=h_{1}=\dots=h_{K},

and call it the
Coxeter number
of
N^{-}
.
(See Table
3.1
.)
We also put

\rho:=\rho_{1}+\dots+\rho_{K},

which is called a
Weyl vector
of
N^{-}
.
By property (b) of
h
above, we have

(3.3)

\langle\rho,\rho\rangle_{N}^{-}=-2h(h+1).

4.
Deep holes

In this section,
we review the classification of deep holes of the Leech lattice
\Lambda

due to Conway, Parker, Sloane
[
4
]
,
and the determination of the fundamental domain of the Weyl group
W(L_{26})

of
L_{26}\cong\mathord{I\hskip-0.7ptI}_{1,25}

due to Conway
[
3
]
.

For
x,y\in\Lambda\otimes\mathord{\mathbb{R}}
, we put

d(x,y):=\sqrt{\langle x-y,x-y\rangle_{\Lambda}}\;,\quad\textrm{and}\quad d(x,\Lambda):=\min{}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\,d(x,\lambda).

The
covering radius
of
\Lambda
is defined to be
the maximum of
d(x,\Lambda)
,
where
x
runs through

First, we give a brief review of lattice theoretic aspects of the theory of (non-virtual)

[

For simplicity, we work over an algebraically closed field of characteristic

Let

We denote by

Let
\langle\phantom{i},\phantom{i}\rangle_{S}
denote
the intersection form of

For a curve

on

[C]\in S_{X}
be the class of

.
Suppose that the Picard number

is

Then

is an even hyperbolic lattice.
Let
\mathord{\mathcal{P}}_{X}
be the positive cone of

that contains an ample class.
The

of

\mathord{\mathcal{N}}_{X}:=\{\,{x\in\mathord{\mathcal{P}}_{X}}\mid{\langle x,[C]\rangle_{S}\geq 0\;\;\textrm{for all curves $C$ on $X$}}\,\}.

\overline{\mathord{\mathcal{N}}}_{X}
the closure of
\mathord{\mathcal{N}}_{X}
in
\overline{\mathord{\mathcal{P}}}_{X}
.
We put

\mathord{\mathrm{Rats}}(X):=\{\,{r\in S_{X}}\mid{\textrm{$r$ is the class of a smooth rational curve on $X$}}\,\}.

Then we have the following:

Proposition 6.1
.

(1)

The nef-and-big cone
\mathord{\mathcal{N}}_{X}
is
the standard fundamental domain of the Weyl group

containing an ample class.

(2)

A
(-2)
-vector

of
S_{X}
belongs to
\mathord{\mathrm{Rats}}(X)

if and only if

defines a wall of the standard fundamental domain

\Box

f
and

be vectors of

Then we have an elliptic fibration
\phi\colon X\to\mathord{\mathbb{P}}^{1}

is the class of a fiber of

.

, and

.

Let

-vectors in

,

\langle{R(U_{f,z}^{\perp})}\rangle

\mathrm{ADE}
-configuration.

) of
(\phi_{\Lambda},\zeta)
is isomorphic to the Leech lattice
\Lambda
.

Let
c\in\Lambda^{-}\otimes\mathord{\mathbb{Q}}
be a deep hole.
We have defined in (
5.1
)
a primitive vector
f(c)\in L_{\Lambda}

generating a half-line in
\overline{\mathord{\mathcal{C}}}(w_{\Lambda})\cap\partial\,\overline{\mathord{\mathcal{P}}}_{L}
.
Then the vector
f(c)\in S_{\mathbb{X}}\cap\overline{\mathord{\mathcal{N}}}_{\mathbb{X}}

is the class of a fiber of an elliptic fibration

\phi(c)\colon\mathbb{X}\to\mathord{\mathbb{P}}^{1}.

The set
\,\Xi_{0}(c)
is the set of classes of smooth rational curves on
\mathbb{X}
that are contained in fibers of
\phi(c)
.
Let
\phi(c)^{*}(p_{i})
(
i=1,\dots,K
) be the reducible fibers of
\phi(c)
.
Renumbering the points
p_{1},\dots,p_{K}\in\mathord{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
if necessary, we have that,
for each
i=1,\dots,K
, the set
\,\Xi_{0}(c)_{i}
is the set of classes

[C]
of irreducible components
C
of
\phi(c)^{*}(p_{i})

with
m([C])\in\mathord{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}
being the multiplicity of
C
in the fiber
\phi(c)^{*}(p_{i})
.
The set
\,\Xi_{1}(c)
is the set of classes of sections of
\phi(c)
.
Hence Proposition
5.4
follows from (
6.1
),
Proposition
5.5
follows from the fact that a section and a fiber intersect only at one point and with intersection multiplicity
1
.
We choose
z\in\,\Xi_{1}(c)
as in (
5.4
).
Then we have a section
\zeta\colon\mathord{\mathbb{P}}^{1}\to\mathbb{X}
whose class is
z
.
We consider
\zeta
as a zero section of
\phi(c)
.
Then
\theta(i,z)
is the class of the smooth rational curve in
\phi(c)^{*}(p_{i})
that intersects
\zeta
,
and hence
\Theta(c,z)
is the set of classes of irreducible components of reducible fibers of
\phi(c)

that are disjoint from
\zeta
.
Hence
the Mordell-Weil group of the Jacobian fibration
(\phi(c),\zeta)
is isomorphic to
\varGamma(c,z)=N^{-}(c,z)/\langle{\Theta(c,z)}\rangle

by Proposition
6.2
, and
Proposition
5.6
also follows from Proposition
6.2
.

7.
Constructions of the Leech lattice

Let
N
be a Niemeier lattice with roots.
Let

\Theta=\Theta_{1}\sqcup\dots\sqcup\Theta_{K}\;\;\subset\;\;R\;\;\subset\;\;\langle{R}\rangle\;\;\subset\;\;N^{-}

and
\tau(N)=\tau(N)_{1}+\cdots+\tau(N)_{K}
be defined as in Section
3
.
We give a construction of the Leech lattice
\Lambda^{-}
for each codeword
\gamma
of the finite abelian group
N^{-}/\langle{R}\rangle
.

Remark 7.1
.

By Proposition
3.1
,
this construction does not depend on the choice of the simple root system
\Theta
of
\langle{\Theta}\rangle=\langle{R}\rangle

up to the action of
\mathord{\mathrm{O}}(N^{-})
.

First we present a lemma about the discriminant group of
a negative-definite root lattice. Let
\Sigma=\{r_{1},\dots,r_{k}\}
be a set of
(-2)
-vectors that form a
connected
ordinary

\mathrm{ADE}
-configuration, and let
\langle{\Sigma}\rangle
denote the lattice generated by
\Sigma
.
Let
m\colon\Sigma\to\mathord{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}
be the function defined in Section
2.3
.
We put

J(\Sigma):=\{\,{j}\mid{m(r_{j})=1}\,\}\;\;\subset\;\;\{1,\dots,k\}.

Let
r_{1}^{\vee},\dots,r_{k}^{\vee}
be the basis of
\langle{\Sigma}\rangle^{\vee}
dual to
the basis
r_{1},\dots,r_{k}
of
\langle{\Sigma}\rangle
.
Then the following can be checked easily for each connected
\mathrm{ADE}
-configuration
\Sigma
.

Lemma 7.2
.

The mapping
j\mapsto r_{j}^{\vee}\bmod\langle{\Sigma}\rangle
gives rise to a bijection from

J(\Sigma)
to
the set of non-zero elements of the discriminant group
\langle{\Sigma}\rangle^{\vee}/\langle{\Sigma}\rangle
.

\Box

Definition 7.3
.

For a codeword
\alpha\in\langle{\Sigma}\rangle^{\vee}/\langle{\Sigma}\rangle
,
we define its
canonical representative

\tilde{\alpha}\in\langle{\Sigma}\rangle^{\vee}

by the following:
if
\alpha=0
, then
\tilde{\alpha}=0
,
and
if
\alpha\neq 0
, then
\tilde{\alpha}=r_{j}^{\vee}
,
where
j\in J(\Sigma)
corresponds to
\alpha
by the bijection in
Lemma
7.2
.

We put

A_{i}:=\langle{\Theta_{i}}\rangle^{\vee}/\langle{\Theta_{i}}\rangle.

By the natural embedding

(7.1)

N^{-}\hookrightarrow\langle{R}\rangle^{\vee}=\langle{\Theta_{1}}\rangle^{\vee}\oplus\cdots\oplus\langle{\Theta_{K}}\rangle^{\vee},

we have an embedding

(7.2)

N^{-}/\langle{R}\rangle\;\;\hookrightarrow\;\;A_{1}\times\cdots\times A_{K}.

Let
\gamma\in N^{-}/\langle{R}\rangle
be a codeword.
For
i=1,\dots,K
,
let
\gamma_{i}\in A_{i}
be the
i
th component of
\gamma
by the embedding (
7.2
).
Let
\tilde{\gamma}_{i}\in\langle{\Theta_{i}}\rangle^{\vee}
be the canonical representative of
\gamma_{i}
, and
we put

v_{\gamma}:=\tilde{\gamma}_{1}+\cdots+\tilde{\gamma}_{K}\;\;\in\;\;\langle{\Theta_{1}}\rangle^{\vee}\oplus\cdots\oplus\langle{\Theta_{K}}\rangle^{\vee}=\langle{R}\rangle^{\vee}.

Then we have the following:

Proposition 7.4
.

We have
v_{\gamma}\in N^{-}
.

Proof.

Let
U_{N}
be a copy of the hyperbolic plane
U
, and we put

L_{N}:=U_{N}\oplus N^{-}.

The intersection form on
L_{N}
is denoted by
\langle\phantom{a},\phantom{a}\rangle_{L}
.
Let
u_{0},u_{1}
be the basis of
U_{N}
given in Example
2.2
.
A vector of
L_{N}
is written as

(7.3)

(a,b,v)_{N}=au_{0}+bu_{1}+v,\quad\textrm{where $a,b\in\mathord{\mathbb{Z}}$ and $v\in N^{-}$.}

By
v\mapsto(0,0,v)_{N}
,
we regard
N^{-}
as a sublattice of
L_{N}
.
In particular, we have
R\subset L_{N}
and
\Theta\subset L_{N}
.
We then put

f_{N}:=(1,0,0)_{N}\in L_{N},\quad z_{N}:=(-1,1,0)_{N}\in L_{N}.

Note that
L_{N}
is isomorphic to
L_{\Lambda}
.
We construct an isometry
L_{\Lambda}\cong L_{N}
explicitly by means of deep holes.
Let
c\in\Lambda^{-}\otimes\mathord{\mathbb{Q}}
be a deep hole such that
\tau(c)=\tau(N)
.
Recall from Section
5
that
we have defined subsets
\,\Xi_{0}(c)
and
\,\Xi_{1}(c)
of the set
\mathord{\mathcal{L}}(w_{\Lambda})
of the Leech roots of
w_{\Lambda}
,
and for a fixed element
z\in\,\Xi_{1}(c)
, we constructed an orthogonal direct-sum decomposition

L_{\Lambda}=U(c,z)\oplus N^{-}(c,z).

Note that
N^{-}(c,z)
is isomorphic to
N^{-}
.
By Proposition
3.1
, after renumbering the connected components
\Theta_{1},\dots,\Theta_{K}
of
the simple root system
\Theta
of
N^{-}
if necessary,
we have an isometry
N^{-}(c,z)\cong N^{-}
that maps
\Theta(c,z)_{i}
to
\Theta_{i}
for
i=1,\dots,K
.
Then we obtain an isometry

(7.4)

L_{\Lambda}\cong L_{N}\;\;\textrm{satisfying}\;\;f(c)\mapsto f_{N},\;\;z\mapsto z_{N},\;\;\Theta(c,z)_{i}\xrightarrow{\sim}\Theta_{i}\;(i=1,\dots,K).

For
i=1,\dots,K
,
let
\mu_{i}\in\langle{\Theta_{i}}\rangle
be the highest root with respect to
\Theta_{i}
.
We put

\theta_{i}:=(1,0,-\mu_{i})_{N}.

The
(-2)
-vector
\theta_{i}\in L_{N}
satisfies the following:

(a)

\langle f_{N},\theta_{i}\rangle_{L}=0
,
\langle z_{N},\theta_{i}\rangle_{L}=1
,

(b)

if
j\neq i
, then
\langle r,\theta_{i}\rangle_{L}=0
for all
r\in\Theta_{j}
,
and

(c)

\Theta_{i}\cup\{\theta_{i}\}
form an extended
\mathrm{ADE}
-configuration of type
\tau_{i}
.

By Remark
5.7
,
we see that
\theta_{i}\in L_{N}
corresponds to
\theta(i,z)\in\,\Xi_{0}(c)_{i}\subset L_{\Lambda}
via the isometry (
7.4
)
given above.
We put

\widetilde{\Theta}_{i}:=\Theta_{i}\cup\{\theta_{i}\}.

Then the isometry (
7.4
) induces a bijection from
\,\Xi_{0}(c)_{i}
to
\widetilde{\Theta}_{i}
.

The isometry (
7.4
) induces an isomorphism

(7.5)

\varGamma(c,z)=N^{-}(c,z)/\langle{\Theta(c,z)}\rangle\cong N^{-}/\langle{R}\rangle.

Let
s_{\gamma}^{\prime}\in\,\Xi_{1}(c)
denote the
(-2)
-vector of
L_{\Lambda}
that corresponds to
the codeword
\gamma\in N^{-}/\langle{R}\rangle
via the isomorphism (
7.5
) and
the bijection
\,\Xi_{1}(c)\cong\varGamma(c,z)
in Proposition
5.6
.
Let
s_{\gamma}
be the
(-2)
-vector of
L_{N}
that corresponds to
the
(-2)
-vector
s_{\gamma}^{\prime}\in L_{\Lambda}
via the isomorphism (
7.4
).
Since
\langle f_{N},s_{\gamma}\rangle_{L}=\langle f(c),s_{\gamma}^{\prime}\rangle_{L}=1
and
\langle s_{\gamma},s_{\gamma}\rangle_{L}=-2
,
there exists a vector
u_{\gamma}\in N^{-}

such that

s_{\gamma}=(a,1,u_{\gamma})_{N},\quad\textrm{where $a=-1-\langle u_{\gamma},u_{\gamma}\rangle_{N}^{-}/2$}.

By Proposition
5.5
transplanted to
s_{\gamma}\in L_{N}
from
s_{\gamma}^{\prime}\in L_{\Lambda}
,
we see that the
i
th component of
u_{\gamma}

by the embedding (
7.1
)
is equal to the canonical representative
\tilde{\gamma}_{i}
.
Indeed, Proposition
5.5
implies that
there exists a unique element
r_{\gamma}\in\widetilde{\Theta}_{i}

such that
m(r_{\gamma})=1
and that, for any
r\in\widetilde{\Theta}_{i}
, we have

\langle s_{\gamma},r\rangle_{L}=\begin{cases}1&\textrm{if $r=r_{\gamma}$},\\
0&\textrm{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Hence
s_{\gamma}
intersects the elements of
\Theta_{i}

with the same intersection numbers as the canonical representative
\tilde{\gamma}_{i}\in\langle{\Theta_{i}}\rangle^{\vee}
does.
(If
r_{\gamma}=\theta_{i}
, then we have
\tilde{\gamma}_{i}=0
.)
Therefore we obtain
v_{\gamma}=u_{\gamma}\in N^{-}
.

\Box

Definition 7.5
.

We call
v_{\gamma}\in N^{-}
the
canonical representative
of
\gamma\in N^{-}/\langle{R}\rangle
.

Recall from Section
3
that
h
is the Coxeter number of
N^{-}

and that
\rho\in N^{-}\otimes\mathord{\mathbb{Q}}
is the Weyl vector of
N^{-}

with respect to
\Theta
.

Proposition 7.6
.

The vector

w_{N}:=(h+1,h,\rho)_{N}

corresponds to the Weyl vector
w_{\Lambda}\in L_{\Lambda}
via the isometry (
7.4
).
In particular, the lattice
(\mathord{\mathbb{Z}}w_{N})^{\perp}/\mathord{\mathbb{Z}}w_{N}
is isomorphic to
\Lambda^{-}
.

Remark 7.7
.

The vector
w_{N}
appeared in Borcherds
[
1
]
.
Proposition
7.6
gives a proof that
\rho\in N^{-}\otimes\mathord{\mathbb{Q}}
is in fact
\rho\in N^{-}
.

Proof.

Note that equality (
3.3
) implies
\langle w_{N},w_{N}\rangle_{L}=0
.
From defining property (b) of
\rho_{i}
and property (c) of
h=h_{i}
in Definition
3.2
, we see that
the vector
w_{N}\in L_{N}\otimes\mathord{\mathbb{Q}}

satisfies

\langle w_{N},z_{N}\rangle_{L}=1
and

\quad\langle w_{N},r\rangle_{L}=1\quad\textrm{for all $r\in\Theta$},\qquad\langle w_{N},\theta_{i}\rangle_{L}=1\quad\textrm{for $i=1,\dots,K$}.

Since
z_{N}
,
r
(
r\in\Theta
) and
\theta_{i}
(
i=1,\dots,K
) span
L_{N}\otimes\mathord{\mathbb{Q}}
and correspond,
via the isometry (

),
to Leech roots of

,
we see that
w_{N}
corresponds to
w_{\Lambda}

via the isometry (
7.4
).

\Box

Theorem 7.8
.

Let
\gamma
be a codeword of the code
N^{-}/\langle{R}\rangle
,
and let
v_{\gamma}\in N^{-}
be the canonical representative of
\gamma
.
We put

n_{\gamma}:=\langle v_{\gamma},v_{\gamma}\rangle_{N}^{-},\quad a_{\gamma}:=2h+1+h\,n_{\gamma}/2.

We define linear forms
\alpha_{0}\colon N^{-}\to\mathord{\mathbb{Q}}
and
\alpha_{1}\colon N^{-}\to\mathord{\mathbb{Q}}
by

\displaystyle\alpha_{0}(u)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\langle hv_{\gamma}-\rho,u\rangle_{N}^{-}/a_{\gamma},

\displaystyle\alpha_{1}(u)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\left(1+n_{\gamma}/2\right)\alpha_{0}(u)-\langle v_{\gamma},u\rangle_{N}^{-},

and put

\varLambda^{-}(\gamma):=\{\,{u\in N^{-}}\mid{\alpha_{0}(u)\in\mathord{\mathbb{Z}}}\,\}.

Then the
\mathord{\mathbb{Z}}
-module
\varLambda^{-}(\gamma)
with the intersection form

(7.6)

\langle u,u^{\prime}\rangle:=\langle u,u^{\prime}\rangle_{N}^{-}+\alpha_{0}(u)\alpha_{1}(u^{\prime})+\alpha_{1}(u)\alpha_{0}(u^{\prime})

is isomorphic to the negative-definite Leech lattice
\Lambda^{-}
.

Proof.

Recall that
s_{\gamma}=(-1-n_{\gamma}/2,1,v_{\gamma})_{N}\in L_{N}
is the vector corresponding, via the isometry (
7.4
),
to the Leech root

s_{\gamma}^{\prime}\in\,\Xi_{1}(c)
of
w_{\Lambda}
.
Let
U(w_{N},s_{\gamma})
be the hyperbolic plane in
L_{N}
generated by
w_{N}
and
s_{\gamma}
.
Since
w_{N}
is a Weyl vector of
L_{N}
,
the orthogonal complement
U(w_{N},s_{\gamma})^{\perp}\cong(\mathord{\mathbb{Z}}w_{N})^{\perp}/\mathord{\mathbb{Z}}w_{N}
is isomorphic to the negative-definite Leech lattice
\Lambda^{-}
.
A vector
(x,y,u)_{N}
of
L_{N}\otimes\mathord{\mathbb{Q}}
is orthogonal to both of
w_{N}
and
s_{\gamma}

if and only if

x=\alpha_{1}(u),\quad y=\alpha_{0}(u).

Hence the image of the orthogonal projection

U(w_{N},s_{\gamma})^{\perp}\hookrightarrow L_{N}=U_{N}\oplus N^{-}\to N^{-}

is equal to the
\mathord{\mathbb{Z}}
-submodule
\varLambda^{-}(\gamma)
of
N^{-}
,
and the restriction of
\langle\phantom{a},\phantom{a}\rangle_{L}
to
U(w_{N},s_{\gamma})^{\perp}

gives rise to the intersection form (
7.6
) on
\varLambda^{-}(\gamma)
.

\Box

Remark 7.9
.

In terms of
\mathbb{X}
,
the construction above is described as follows.
The sublattice
U_{N}\subset L_{N}
yields a Jacobian fibration of
\mathbb{X}

by the isometry
L_{N}\cong L_{\Lambda}=S_{\mathbb{X}}
given in (
7.4
), and
the
(-2)
-vector
s_{\gamma}
is the class of the image of the element
of the Mordell-Weil group corresponding to

.

Considering the case where
\gamma=0
in Theorem
7.8
,
and changing signs of intersection forms of lattices,
we obtain Corollary
1.1
in Introduction.

Remark 7.10
.

Since the Leech lattice
\Lambda^{-}
is characterized, up to isomorphism, as the unique even unimodular negative-definite lattice of rank
24

with no roots (see
[
2
]
), we can confirm Theorem
7.8
by direct computation,

v_{\gamma}
of codewords

The set of
(-2)
-vectors
s_{\gamma}\in L_{N}

surfaces

On traceable iterated line graph and hamiltonian path index
Zhaohong Niu, Liming Xiong, Weihua Yang

Abstract
Xiong and Liu [L. Xiong and Z. Liu, Hamiltonian iterated line graphs, Discrete Math. 256 (2002) 407-422] gave a characterization of the graphs G G for which the n n -th iterated line graph L n ​ ( G ) L^{n}(G) is hamiltonian, for n ≥ 2 n\geq 2 . In this paper, we study the existence of a hamiltonian path in L n ​ ( G ) L^{n}(G) , and give a characterization of G G for which L n ​ ( G ) L^{n}(G) has a hamiltonian path. As applications, we use this characterization to give several upper bounds on the hamiltonian path index of a graph. Keywords: iterated line graph; traceable; hamiltonian index; hamiltonian path index

Introduction

The graphs considered in this paper are finite, undirected and loopless. Undefined notation and terminology will follow

[

The
line graph

L(G)
of a graph
G
has
E(G)
as its vertex set and two vertices are adjacent in

if and only if they are adjacent as edges in

Theorem 1

(Harary and Nash-Williams,
[
7
]
) Let

is hamiltonian if and only if

has a dominating closed trail.

Theorem

characterized those graphs

(expecially, non-hamiltonian graphs) for which

is hamiltonian. It is natural to believe that for most graphs, after applying the line graph operation iteratively a finite number of times, the resulting will be hamiltonian. Chartrand
[
4
]
showed this is indeed the case. He considered the
n
-iterated line graph

of

, and introduced the
hamiltonian index
of a graph, denoted by

, i.e., the minimum number

is hamiltonian. Here the
n
-
iterated line graph
of a graph

is defined to be

, where

denotes the line graph
L(G)
of
G
, and

is assumed to have a nonempty edge set. Chartrand showed that for any graph
G
other than a path, the hamiltonian index of

exists. Since then, many results of determing the exact values or upper bounds of

for special graphs are proved, such as Chartrand and Wall
[
5
]
for trees (other than paths) and connected graphs
G
with
\delta(G)\geq 3
, Kapoor and Stewart
[
10
]
for

, Xiong and Liu
[
16
]
for connected graphs using the split blocks and Catlin’s reduction method, Han et al.
[
6
]
for 2-connected graph with
\kappa(G)\geq\alpha(G)-t
(where

[
13
]
showed that the problem to decide whether the hamiltonian index of a
given graph is less than or equal to a given constant is NP-complete.

Let
G
be a graph and

and

denote the sets of vertices and edges of

. A

and with internal vertices, if any, of degree 2. We denote by

. The distance between two subgraphs

and

of

\min\{d_{G}(v_{1},v_{2}):v_{1}\in V(H_{1})\text{ and }v_{2}\in V(H_{2})\}

For a positive integer

[

]
used

to denote the set of subgraphs

that satisfy the following conditions, and then investigated the existence of a characterization of those graphs

.

(I)

d_{H}(x)\equiv 0\,(\!\!\!\!\mod 2)
for every
x\in V(H)
;

(II)

V_{0}(H)\subseteq\bigcup_{i=3}^{\Delta(G)}V_{i}(G)\subseteq V(H)

(III)

of

(IV)

(V)

(Xiong and Liu,
[

) Let

Proof of Theorem
4

In this section, we will prove Theorem

symmetric difference
of two non-empty sets

and

, is the set

.

Theorem 13

Let
G
be a connected graph and
k\geq 1
be an integer. Then
EUP_{k}(L(G))\neq\emptyset
if and only if
EUP_{k+1}(G)\neq\emptyset
.

Proof.

Sufficiency
. Supposing that
EUP_{k+1}(G)\neq\emptyset
. Note that if
EU_{k+1}(G)\neq\emptyset
, then by Theorem
11
,
EU_{k}(L(G))\neq\emptyset
, and hence,
EUP_{k}(L(G))\neq\emptyset
by the fact that
EU_{k}(G)\subseteq EUP_{k}(G)
. So we may assume that
EU_{k+1}(G)=\emptyset
, which implies that each subgraph of
G
in
EUP_{k+1}(G)
contains exactly two odd vertices.

Now let
H\in EUP_{k+1}(G)
with a minimum number of components denoted by
C_{1},C_{2},\dots,C_{t}
. If
H
is connected, then
H\cong C_{1}
. Without loss of generality, we let
|O(C_{1})|=2
, and then
C_{i}
is a circuit for
2\leq i\leq t
.

Since
|O(C_{1})|=2
and

is connected, we have
|\bar{E}(C_{1})|\geq 2
: for otherwise,
|V(G)|=|V(C_{1})|=2
, Theorem
13
holds obviously. Hence, by Lemma
10
(2),

has a nontrivial path
P
with
V(P)=\bar{E}(C_{1})
. Now we claim that
|\bar{E}(C_{i})|\geq 3
for
2\leq i\leq t
: if
C_{i}
is nontrivial, then we are done; if
C_{i}
is an isolated vertex, then by the definition of
EUP_{n}(G)
,

, our claim holds. By Lemma
5
(2), we can find a cycle
C_{i}^{\prime}
in
L(G)
with
V(C_{i}^{\prime})=\bar{E}(C_{i})\,(2\leq i\leq t)
. Let

H^{\prime}=P\cup\big(\bigcup_{i=2}^{t}C_{i}^{\prime}\big).

We will prove that
H^{\prime}\in EUP_{k}(L(G))
.

By Lemma
8
and the minimality of
t
,
E(P)\cap E(C_{i}^{\prime})=\emptyset
and
E(C_{i}^{\prime})\cap E(C_{j}^{\prime})=\emptyset
for

with

, which implies that
|O(H^{\prime})|=2
. (I)
′
holds.

Since

is nontrivial, and
V(C_{i}^{\prime})=\bar{E}(C_{i})\geq 3\,(2\leq i\leq t)
,

contains no isolated vertex. Note that
\bigcup_{i=3}^{\Delta(G)}V_{i}(G)\subseteq V(H)
and

. We have

\bigcup_{i=3}^{\Delta(L(G))}V_{i}(L(G))\subseteq V(H^{\prime}).

Hence,

satisfies (II).

The details of

′
are almost the same as the proof of Theorem

in
[
16
]
, so we omit them here.

It follows that

.

Necessity
. Supposing that
EUP_{k}(L(G))\neq\emptyset
. Note that if
EU_{k}(L(G))\neq\emptyset
, then by Theorem
11
,
EU_{k+1}(G)\neq\emptyset
, and hence,
EUP_{k+1}(G)\neq\emptyset
. So we may assume that

, which implies that each subgraph of
L(G)
in

contains exactly two odd vertices.

Let
H
be a subgraph of
L(G)
in
EUP_{k}(L(G))
with a minimum number of isolated vertices. Then
H
contains no isolated vertices. For otherwise, suppose
C_{1}=\{e_{0}\}
is an isolated vertex of

, then by (II),

. By Lemma
6
, there exist

such that

. Now we construct a subgraph

of

as follows.

Obviously

, and without loss of generality, let

. Since

has no isolated vertices,

. Then

Theorem 14

Proof.

′

G-H
has exactly
k

|E(b)|\leq k+1

satisfies (IV) and (V)

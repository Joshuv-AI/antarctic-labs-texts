A note on the effects of magnetic field on holographic fermions with dipole-like coupling
Sayan Chakrabarti, Debaprasad Maity, Wadbor Wahlang

Abstract
In this note, we study the effects of non-zero magnetic field on the fermion spectral function coupled with a non-minimal coupling in the background of a dyonic A ​ d ​ S 4 AdS_{4} black hole. The system can be reduced to a non magnetic one with the momentum being quantized into Landau levels n n . By tuning the coupling parameter, we study its effect on the Fermi level and the quasiparticle width. We found an interesting scaling relation between the decay width and the coupling parameter, with a universal scaling exponent which is independent of the levels n n .



Despite many years of active research, strongly coupled fermionic systems still have a lot of exciting puzzles with various unconventional phenomena uncovered through condensed matter experiments like ARPES. Especially in the realm of low-temperature physics where experiments showed that though a Fermi surface still dictates the underlying features, several phenomena disagree with the conventional Landau’s Fermi
liquid theory. The so-called non-Fermi liquids
Anderson:1990
;
Varma:1989
are an example, where a well-defined quasi-particle is absent.

Further, the problems posed by these strongly correlated systems has caught the attention of both condensed matter theorist and holographers. In recent years, the holographic method, which results from the AdS/CFT correspondence
Maldacena:1997re
or gauge/gravity duality, has become an important framework to tackle these strongly coupled systems. This method is widely used to model strongly coupled dynamics in various condensed matter systems, including strange metals and high
T_{c}
superconductors.
Liu:2009dm
;
Lee:2008xf
;
Cubrovic:2009ye
;
Hartnoll:2008vx
;
Hartnoll:2009sz
;
Herzog:2009xv
;
Chakrabarti:2019gow
. The holographic study of the fermionic systems at a finite charge density has shown to exhibit the interesting behaviour of non-Fermi liquid using simple probe fermions in an extremal AdS black hole geometry
Liu:2009dm
;
Faulkner:2011tm
;
Faulkner:2009wj
. Recently, more complicated models were also proposed, with extra symmetries included in the background gravity in order to mimic the real condensed matter system at the boundary where the observables, like the spectral function, can be extracted
Andrade:2020hpu
;
Cremonini:2019fzz
;
Balm:2019dxk
;
Ling:2013aya
;
Ling:2013nxa
;
Andrade:2017ghg
;
Cremonini:2018xgj
. But some have introduce a coupling directly to the probe fermions fields in the bulk. One such coupling is the dipole coupling which has been used to study the dynamical gap in Mott physics
PhysRevLett.106.091602
;
Vanacore:2014hka
.

In this paper, we are going to extend such studies where a magnetic field is present. Only few works
Gubankova:2010rc
;
Basu:2009qz
;
Albash:2009wz
have been done towards the study of holographic fermionic system in a non-zero magnetic field. In our study, we will explore just a tiny portion from the vastness of possibilities in this topic. We consider a non-minimal dipole-like coupling taking the dyonic AdS black hole as our background geometry. The motivation for choosing a dyonic black hole is to have both magnetic and electric charged, which correspond to a
(2+1)
dimensional boundary CFT with a non-zero chemical potential and a magnetic field. For this, we introduce a probe fermion with a coupling in this geometry to study the properties of the spectral functions which is the most relevant quantity directly related to condensed matter experiments.

We organise the paper as follows, in Section
I
we discuss the set up of background geometry which is the dyonic black hole solution and further parametrize the solution parameters to arrive at the conditions for zero and non-zero temperature. In Section
II
we discuss the fermionic action and the non-minimal coupling in the background geometry followed by solution to the Dirac equations and the spectral function. In Section
III
, we discuss and comment on our findings of the results and finally in Section
IV
we summarize the paper with some discussion of possible future directions and final thoughts.

I
Review of the Background geometry

In order to study the affect of magnetic field on the probe fermions field, we will consider a dyonic black hole that has both electric and magnetic charges in
AdS_{4}
spacetime. The most general Maxwell-Einstein action with a negative cosmological constant in 4-dimensions can be written as:

S=\frac{1}{\kappa^{2}}\int d^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\left[{\mathcal{R}}-\frac{6}{R^{2}}-\frac{R^{2}}{g_{F}^{2}}F_{MN}F^{MN}\right]

(1)

Here
{\mathcal{R}}
is the Ricci scalar and
R
is the radius of the AdS spacetime. From the given action, the solution to the Einstein equations in a metric form is given by

\displaystyle\frac{ds^{2}}{R^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle r^{2}\Big[-f(r)dt^{2}+dx^{2}+dy^{2}\Big]+\frac{dr^{2}}{r^{2}f(r)}

(2)

\displaystyle f(r)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1+\frac{h^{2}+\mu^{2}}{r^{4}}-\frac{\left(1+(h^{2}+\mu^{2})\right)}{r^{3}}\;.

(3)

with components of the gauge fields given by

\displaystyle A_{y}=A_{r}=0,\;A_{t}(r)=\mu(1-1/r),\;A_{x}(y)=-h\,y.

(4)

where
\mu
is the chemical potential of the system and
h
is the magnetic field strength, and the temperature of the system is

T=\frac{1}{4\pi}\Big(3-(\mu^{2}+h^{2})\Big).

The condition for zero temperature is given by

h^{2}+\mu^{2}=3.

(5)

The relevant dimensionless parameter related to the strength of the magnetic field is
H=h/\mu=\frac{h}{\sqrt{3-h^{2}}}
which goes from zero to infinity.
To study a system at finite temperature we set
h^{2}+\mu^{2}=3\,\eta
, where
\eta
ranges from
0
to
1
. The metric coefficient
f(r)
can be written as

f(r)=1+\frac{3\,\eta}{r^{4}}-\frac{(1+3\,\eta)}{r^{3}}.

(6)

For
\eta=1
, we have the extremal black hole with temperature
T=0
as seen above, and for
\eta=0
we have a system at finite temperature .

II
Fermion action

We will consider a bulk Dirac fermion field
\psi
coupled to a non-minimal coupling as a probe to the system. The action for
\psi
is given by

\displaystyle S_{\psi}=\frac{1}{\kappa^{2}}\int d^{4}x\sqrt{-g}i\,\bar{\psi}\left(\,/\penalty\!\!\!\!D\,\psi-m\,\,\psi-i\wp\,\left(/\penalty\!\!\!\!F\,+\,\tilde{/\penalty\!\!\!\!F}\right)\,\psi\right).

(7)

where
/\penalty\!\!\!\!D
,
/\penalty\!\!\!\!F
and
\tilde{/\penalty\!\!\!\!F}
stands for

\displaystyle/\penalty\!\!\!\!D=e_{\penalty\ a}^{M}\Gamma^{a}\Big[\partial_{M}+\frac{1}{8}\omega_{M}^{ab}[\Gamma_{a},\Gamma_{b}]-iqA_{M}\Big],

\displaystyle\penalty\ /\penalty\!\!\!\!F=\frac{1}{2}\Gamma^{ab}e^{M}_{a}\,e^{N}_{b}\,F_{MN},\penalty\ \tilde{/\penalty\!\!\!\!F}=\frac{1}{4}\Gamma^{cd}\epsilon_{cdab}e^{a}_{M}\,e^{b}_{N}\,F^{MN}.

(8)

Here
e_{\penalty\ a}^{M}
is the inverse vielbein,
\omega^{ab}_{\penalty\ M}
is the spin-connection and
q
is the fermion charge. The fermionic operator
\hat{\mathcal{O}}
at the boundary is dual to the field
\psi
in the bulk, and its conformal dimension
\Delta
is mapped to spinors
\psi
with mass

as

\Delta=m+\frac{3}{2}.

(9)

The Dirac equation from the above action is given by:

(\;/\penalty\!\!\!\!D-m-i\wp\,/\penalty\!\!\!\!F-i\wp\,\tilde{/\penalty\!\!\!\!F}\;)\,\psi=0

(10)

which can be written in the form

\displaystyle\Big(Z(r)+Y(y)\Big)\,\psi=0,

(11)

where,

\displaystyle Z(r)=\sqrt{\frac{g_{ii}}{g_{rr}}}\;\Gamma^{\underline{r}}\,(\partial_{r}+S_{p})-m\,\sqrt{g_{ii}}+\Gamma^{\underline{t}}\sqrt{\frac{g_{ii}}{-g_{tt}}}\left(\partial_{t}-iqA_{t}\right)

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;-i\wp\,\Gamma^{\underline{r}\underline{t}}\left(\partial_{r}A_{t}\sqrt{\frac{g_{ii}}{-g_{tt}g_{rr}}}+\frac{h}{\sqrt{g_{ii}}}\right)

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;-i\wp\Gamma^{\underline{x}\underline{y}}\left(\frac{h}{\sqrt{g_{ii}}}+\partial_{r}A_{t}\sqrt{\frac{g_{ii}}{-g_{tt}g_{rr}}}\right).

\displaystyle Y(y)=\Gamma^{\underline{x}}(\partial_{x}-iqA_{x})+\Gamma^{\underline{y}}\partial_{y}.

(12)

The term
S_{p}
in
Z(r)
is the contribution from the spin connection. If we now perform a Fourier transform along
(t,x)
, then
\partial_{t}\to-i\omega,\penalty\ \partial_{x}\to ik_{x}
and also we can remove the
S_{p}
by rescaling the spinors
\psi=(-gg^{rr})^{-\frac{1}{4}}\Psi
. Then we have
Z(r),\;Y(y)
as

\displaystyle Z(r)=

\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{g_{ii}}{g_{rr}}}\;\Gamma^{\underline{r}}\,\partial_{r}-m\sqrt{g_{ii}}+\Gamma^{t}\sqrt{\frac{g_{ii}}{-g_{tt}}}\left(-i\omega-iqA_{t}\right)

\displaystyle-i\wp\,\Gamma^{\underline{r}\underline{t}}\,P(r)-i\wp\Gamma^{\underline{x}\underline{y}}\,P(z)\;,

\displaystyle Y(y)=

\displaystyle\Gamma^{\underline{x}}\cdot(i\,k_{x}-iqA_{x})+\Gamma^{\underline{y}}\cdot\partial_{y}.

(13)

where,
P(r)=\,\frac{(h+\mu)}{r}
.
For convenience, we will choose the following Gamma matrices:

\displaystyle\Gamma^{\underline{r}}=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}1\!\!1&0\\
0&-1\!\!1\end{array}\right),\;\Gamma^{\underline{t}}=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}0&i\sigma_{3}\\
i\sigma_{3}&0\end{array}\right),

\displaystyle\Gamma^{\underline{x}}=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}0&\sigma_{1}\\
\sigma_{1}&0\end{array}\right),\;\Gamma^{\underline{y}}=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}0&\sigma_{2}\\
\sigma_{2}&0\end{array}\right)\;.

Then we have

\displaystyle Z\,(r)=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}{\mathcal{D}}^{-}_{r}&i\sigma_{3}\,V_{+}\\
i\sigma_{3}\,V_{-}&-{\mathcal{D}}^{+}_{r}\end{array}\right),

\displaystyle Y\,(y)=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}0&\sigma_{1}{\mathcal{C}}_{y}+\sigma_{2}\partial_{y}\\
\sigma_{1}{\mathcal{C}}_{y}+\sigma_{2}\partial_{y}&0\end{array}\right).

where

\displaystyle{\mathcal{D}}^{\pm}_{r}=\,\sqrt{\frac{g_{ii}}{g_{rr}}}\,\partial_{r}\,\pm m\,\sqrt{g_{ii}}\;+\wp\,P(r)\,\sigma_{3}\,,\;\;{\mathcal{C}}_{y}=i(k_{x}+qhy)\,\;.

\displaystyle V_{\pm}=-i\;\Bigg(\;\sqrt{\frac{g_{ii}}{-g_{tt}}}\left(\omega+qA_{t}\right)\;\pm\;\wp\,P(r)\;\Bigg)\;\;

(14)

Clearly, the matrices
Z
and
Y
do not commute. However, it is possible to find a constant matrix
{\mathcal{M}}
such that
[{\mathcal{M}}Z,{\mathcal{M}}Y]=0
and then look
for common eigenvectors of
{\mathcal{M}}Z
and
{\mathcal{M}}Y
as they are
commuting Hermitian operators.
The transformation matrix statisfies the following relations:

\displaystyle\bigl\{{\mathcal{M}},\Gamma^{\underline{r}}\bigr\}\,=0\;,\;\;\;\bigl\{{\mathcal{M}},\Gamma^{\underline{t}}\bigr\}\,=0\;,\;\;\big[{\mathcal{M}},\Gamma^{\underline{x}}\big]\,=0\;,

\displaystyle\big[{\mathcal{M}},\Gamma^{\underline{y}}\big]\,=0\;,\;\big[{\mathcal{M}},\Gamma^{\underline{x}\,\underline{y}}\big]\,=0\;,\;\;\big[{\mathcal{M}},\Gamma^{\underline{r}\,\underline{t}}\big]\,=0\;.

It turns out that a convenience choice for
{\mathcal{M}}
is
\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}0&\sigma_{3}\\
-\sigma_{3}&0\end{array}\right)
.
In order to separate the variables, one can multiply (
11
) by
{\mathcal{M}}
from the left to have

\displaystyle{\mathcal{M}}\;Z=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}i\,V_{-}&-\sigma_{3}{\mathcal{D}}^{+}_{r}\\
-\sigma_{3}D^{-}_{r}&-i\,V_{+}\end{array}\right),

\displaystyle{\mathcal{M}}\;Y=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}\sigma_{3}(\sigma_{1}{\mathcal{C}}_{y}+\sigma_{2}\partial_{y})&0\\
0&-\sigma_{3}(\sigma_{1}{\mathcal{C}}_{y}+\sigma_{2}\partial_{y})\end{array}\right).

Now we can look for solutions of the eigenvalue equation of the form

\displaystyle{\mathcal{M}}Z\Psi=-{\mathcal{M}}Y\Psi=\lambda\Psi.

(15)

with real
\lambda
. Writing
\Psi\,=\,(\Psi_{+}\;,\Psi_{-})^{T}
.
First we solve the
y
dependent part of the above equation, which is identical to that of a massless free fermion in (
2+1
) dimensions

{\mathcal{M}}Y\Psi=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}\sigma_{3}(\sigma_{1}{\mathcal{C}}_{y}+\sigma_{2}\partial_{y})\Psi_{+}\\
-\sigma_{3}(\sigma_{1}{\mathcal{C}}_{y}+\sigma_{2}\partial_{y})\Psi_{-}\end{array}\right)=-\lambda\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}\Psi_{+}\\
\Psi_{-}\end{array}\right).

or,

U_{1}\Psi_{+}\,=\,-\lambda\,\Psi_{+}\;\text{and}\;U_{2}\Psi_{-}\,=\,-\lambda\,\Psi_{-}

where
U_{1,2}\,=\,\pm\sigma_{3}(\sigma_{1}{\mathcal{C}}_{y}+\sigma_{2}\partial_{y})
.
For the
\Psi_{+}
part, let us left multiply
U_{1}
, then

U_{1}\,U_{1}\Psi_{+}\,=-\,U_{1}\,\lambda\,\Psi_{+}\;=\,-\lambda\,U_{1}\Psi_{+}\,=\,-\lambda(-\lambda\Psi_{+})

\displaystyle\left[\sigma_{3}(\sigma_{1}{\mathcal{C}}_{y}+\sigma_{2}\partial_{y})\right]\left[\sigma_{3}(\sigma_{1}{\mathcal{C}}_{y}+\sigma_{2}\partial_{y})\right]\Psi_{+}=\lambda^{2}\Psi_{+}.

(16)

On further simplification (16) gives

\displaystyle\partial_{y}^{2}\;\Psi_{+}+(\lambda^{2}+C_{y}^{2}-i\sigma_{3}C^{\prime}_{y})\Psi_{+}=0.

(17)

Now separating the variables
r
and
y
as
\Psi_{+}=\Big(Z_{1}(r)Y_{1}(y),Z_{2}(r)Y_{2}(y)\Big)^{T}
, and noting equation (
17
) does not depend on

, one can set
Z_{1}=Z_{2}
, leading to

\displaystyle-\partial_{y}^{2}\;Y_{1,2}+T_{\pm}(y)\;Y_{1,2}=0,

(18)

where
T_{\pm}(y)=-(\lambda^{2}+C_{y}^{2}\mp iC_{y}^{\prime})=(k_{x}+qh\,y)^{2}-\lambda^{2}\mp qh
,
which is similar to a simple harmonic oscillator differential equation. To see this more explicitly, let us define

\tilde{x}=\sqrt{qh}\;(y+\frac{k_{x}}{qh})
. Then we have

\displaystyle-\partial_{\tilde{x}}^{2}\;Y_{1,2}+\tilde{x}^{2}Y_{1,2}=\left(\frac{\lambda^{2}}{qh}\pm 1\right)Y_{1,2}.

(19)

These equations do not have the form of the standard Hermite differential equation that arises in the case of harmonic oscillator problem. But on rescaling
Y_{1,2}=e^{-\frac{\tilde{x}^{2}}{2}}\;Y_{1,2}(\tilde{x})
, we have

\displaystyle\partial_{\tilde{x}}^{2}\;Y_{1,2}-\tilde{x}\,\partial_{\tilde{x}}\;Y_{1,2}+(E^{\pm}-\,1)\;.Y_{1,2}=0

Therefore by setting
E^{\pm}-1\,=\,2n
,
where

denotes the Landau levels, the eigenvalues and eigenvectors are given by

\displaystyle E_{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\lambda^{2}}{qh}\pm 1\right)=n+\frac{1}{2},\penalty\ \penalty\ n=0,1,2,\dots

\displaystyle Y_{1,2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle N_{n}e^{-\tilde{x}^{2}/2}H_{n}(\tilde{x})\equiv I_{n}(\tilde{x}),

(20)

where
N_{n}
is a normalization constant. By substituting this solution back into the first order equations for
\Psi_{+}
assuming the same eigenvalues we get

\Psi_{+}=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}I_{n}(\tilde{x})\penalty\ Z_{1}\\
-iI_{n-1}(\tilde{x})\penalty\ Z_{1}\end{array}\right),

whose eigenvalues are
\lambda_{n}=\sqrt{2nqh},\penalty\ n=0,1,2,\cdots
. For completeness, we define
I_{-1}(\tilde{x})=0
.
Together with the expression for
\Psi_{-}
, which can be obtained in a similar fashion, we have

\displaystyle\Psi=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}I_{n}(\tilde{x})\penalty\ Z_{1}\\
-iI_{n-1}(\tilde{x})\penalty\ Z_{1}\\
I_{n}(\tilde{x})\penalty\ Z_{2}\\
iI_{n-1}(\tilde{x})\penalty\ Z_{2}\end{array}\right).

Another independent solution comes from the values corresponding to
-\lambda_{n}
, i.e.

\displaystyle{\mathcal{M}}\,Z\,\tilde{\Psi}=-{\mathcal{M}}\,Y\,\tilde{\Psi}=-\lambda_{n}\tilde{\Psi}\;.

(26)

Proceeding in a similar way, we get

\displaystyle\tilde{\Psi}=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}-i\penalty\ I_{n}\penalty\ \tilde{Z}_{1}\\
I_{n-1}\penalty\ \tilde{Z}_{1}\\
-i\penalty\ I_{n}\penalty\ \tilde{Z}_{2}\\
-I_{n-1}\penalty\ \tilde{Z}_{2}\end{array}\right).

The equations for
Z_{i}
and

are respectively,

\displaystyle\Bigg(\sqrt{\frac{g_{ii}}{g_{rr}}}\partial_{r}\,-m\,\sqrt{g_{ii}}\pm\wp\,P(r)\Bigg)Z_{1}=-(i\,V_{+}\,+\lambda_{n})Z_{2}\;,

\displaystyle\Bigg(\sqrt{\frac{g_{ii}}{g_{rr}}}\partial_{r}\,+m\,\sqrt{g_{ii}}\pm\wp\,P(r)\Bigg)Z_{2}=\;(i\,V_{-}\,-\lambda_{n})Z_{1}\;,

\displaystyle\Bigg(\sqrt{\frac{g_{ii}}{g_{rr}}}\partial_{r}\,-m\,\sqrt{g_{ii}}\pm\wp\,P(r)\Bigg)\tilde{Z}_{1}=-(i\,V_{+}\,-\lambda_{n})\tilde{Z}_{2}\;,

\displaystyle\Bigg(\sqrt{\frac{g_{ii}}{g_{rr}}}\partial_{r}\,+m\,\sqrt{g_{ii}}\pm\wp\,P(r)\Bigg)\tilde{Z}_{2}=\;(i\,V_{-}\,+\lambda_{n})\tilde{Z}_{1}\;.

(31)

To derive the flow equation and the Green’s function, we follow the construction from
Basu:2009qz
;
Gubankova:2010rc
, by writing the general solution as linear combination of both (
II
) for
\lambda_{n}
and (

) for (

) as follows

\displaystyle\Psi

\displaystyle=\sum_{n}\Psi^{(n)},\;\;\Psi^{(n)}=\Psi^{(\lambda_{n})}+\Psi^{\prime(-\lambda_{n})}

\displaystyle=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}iZ_{1}^{(n)}e_{1}^{(n)}\\
Z_{2}^{(n)}e_{2}^{(n)}\end{array}\right)+\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}-i\bar{Z}_{1}^{(n)}e_{2}^{(n)}\\
-\bar{Z}_{2}^{(n)}e_{1}^{(n)}\end{array}\right),

where,

and

are the basis spinors. In terms of the above Hermite polynomials, the basis spinors are given by

\displaystyle G_{R}^{(n)}=-iS^{(n)}\gamma^{t}=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}-\frac{Z_{2}^{(n)}}{Z_{1}^{(n)}}&0\\

\displaystyle A^{(n)}\sim{\mathrm{Im}}\left(-\frac{Z^{(n)}_{2}}{Z^{(n)}_{1}}+\frac{\bar{Z}^{(n)}_{2}}{\bar{Z}^{(n)}_{1}}\right).

(38)

\displaystyle\hskip 85.35826pt+(i\,V_{+}+\lambda_{n})(G_{11}^{(n)})^{2}\;,

(40)

IV
Conclusions

In this paper we have studied the holographic fermionic spectral function, taking into account the effects of an external magnetic field along with a non-minimal coupling. We also find the Landau’s levels arising from the quantization of electron orbits. We observed that the results are different from the case of zero magnetic field. From our results, we have observed some interesting phenomena like the disappearance of the quasi particles for large magnetic field, the splitting of spectral function for large coupling constant, which we have not seen in a non magnetic systems. In addition, we also see a spectral weight transfer from upper band to a lower band by inducing a gap at the Fermi level for different Landau levels
n
. Further, we studied the decay width of the quasiparticles, which shown to have a unique scaling exponent for all
n
levels. Finally, we studied the variation of the zero temperature gap parameter
\Delta
versus coupling parameter
\wp
for the levels
n=1\,2
and
3
respectively.

In this paper, we have left out some important aspects, such as transport properties. One can further extend our study with this type of coupling to examine other interesting properties such as the Quantum Hall Effect
Davis:2008nv
;
KeskiVakkuri:2008eb
, computation of resistivity and conductivity in this system. It would be also interesting to study other intriguing phenomena induced by magnetic field, such as the de-Haas-van Alphen effect, but for now we leave these analyzes for future exploration.

3d Mirror Symmetry and the β ​ γ \beta\gamma VOA
Andrew Ballin, Wenjun Niu

Abstract
We study the simplest example of mirror symmetry for 3d 𝒩 = 4 \mathcal{N}=4 SUSY gauge theories: the A-twist of a free hypermultiplet and the B-twist of SQED. We particularly focus on the category of line operators in each theory. Using the work of Costello-Gaiotto, we define these categories as appropriate categories of modules for the boundary vertex operator algebras present in each theory. For the A-twist of a free hyper, this will be a certain category of modules for the β ​ γ \beta\gamma VOA, properly containing the category previously studied by Allen-Wood. Applying the work of Creutzig-Kanade-McRae and Creutzig-McRae-Yang, we show that the category of line operators on the A side possesses the structure of a braided tensor category, extending the result of Allen-Wood. In addition, we prove that there is a braided tensor equivalence between the categories of line operators on the A side and B side, completing a non-trivial check of the 3d mirror symmetry conjecture. We derive explicit fusion rules as a consequence of this equivalence and obtain interesting relations with associated quantum group representations.

To appropriately define and study the structure of these categories, we put these theories on a manifold with boundary. This is a direct extension of Witten’s approach of braiding in 3d Chern-Simons theory to the setting of supersymmetric gauge theories

.

As a side note, the atypicals can equivalently be defined by the Loewy diagrams

\mathcal{W}_{0}^{+}=\Big(\mathcal{V}\longrightarrow\sigma^{-1}\mathcal{V}\Big)\qquad\mathcal{W}_{0}^{-}=\Big(\sigma^{-1}\mathcal{V}\longrightarrow\mathcal{V}\Big)

(2.14)

In this paper, a Loewy diagram
X\longrightarrow Y
represents a module that is an extension of
Y
by
X
. One may have instead drawn such a module as
Y\longrightarrow X
, but we feel our convention better matches the visual appearance of the corresponding short exact sequence describing the extension.

With these examples in hand, we can now define the category that we study in our paper, which we believe properly matches our physical systems of interest.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
{\mathcal{C}_{\beta\gamma}}
be the abelian subcategory of smooth, finite-length,
{\beta\gamma}
VOA modules generated by
\mathcal{V}
,
\mathcal{W}_{\lambda}
, and their spectral flows, such that
{\mathcal{C}_{\beta\gamma}}
is closed under taking extension.

While the element
L_{0}
(
2.7
) provides the
\mathbb{C}
-grading included in the definition of a VOA module, the element
J_{0}
(
2.6
) provides an
additional
grading on the modules; see
[
HLZ10
, Definition 2.25]
for details. A main feature the reader should keep in mind from Definition
2.1
is that we do not exclude modules on which
L_{0}
and
J_{0}
act non-semisimply. Additionally, modules in
{\mathcal{C}_{\beta\gamma}}
can be decomposed into a direct sum of finite-dimensional simultaneous generalized eigenspaces for
L_{0}
and
J_{0}
. This should be contrasted with the representation category studied in, e.g.,
[
AW20
]
wherein
J_{0}
was required to act semisimply; the category that we study will consequentially be strictly larger. While
{\mathcal{C}_{\beta\gamma}}
is strictly smaller than the category studied in
[
HR21
]
, the authors do not provide a classification of their category, and more structure exists on
{\mathcal{C}_{\beta\gamma}}
that we study that does not exist on their category. To the best of our knowledge, our present study of
{\mathcal{C}_{\beta\gamma}}
is a new addition to the existing literature on
{\beta\gamma}
representation categories.

2.3
Extension structure and classification results

To characterize the objects in
{\mathcal{C}_{\beta\gamma}}
, we must understand the new modules that are present when we demand closure under extension. For modules induced by polynomial representations of a 1D Weyl subalgebra of
{\beta\gamma}
, one can explicitly construct self-extensions by adjoining powers of a formal variable
\log\beta_{0}
or
\log\gamma_{0}
before inducing. For example, the first self-extension of
\mathcal{W}_{0}^{-}
, which we denote by
\mathcal{W}_{0}^{-,2}
, can be constructed by

\mathcal{W}_{0}^{-,2}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=\text{Ind}_{{\beta\gamma}_{\geq 0}}(\mathbb{C}[\beta_{0},\beta_{0}^{-1}]\oplus\mathbb{C}[\beta_{0},\beta_{0}^{-1}]\log\beta_{0})

(2.15)

where
\gamma_{0}\cdot\log\beta_{0}=\beta_{0}^{-1}
. This module is also is described by the Loewy diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sigma^{-1}\mathcal{V}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{V}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sigma^{-1}\mathcal{V}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{V}}

(2.16)

This is an object of
{\mathcal{C}_{\beta\gamma}}
that does
not
carry a semi-simple action of
J_{0}
, demonstrating that
{\mathcal{C}_{\beta\gamma}}
is an enlargement of the representation category studied in
[
AW20
]
. We denote the
(n-1)^{\text{th}}
iterated extension of
\mathcal{W}_{0}^{-}
by itself as
\mathcal{W}_{0}^{-,n}
. A similar line of reasoning gives the definition of
\mathcal{W}_{0}^{+,n}
. For example,
\mathcal{W}_{0}^{+,2}
looks like

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{V}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sigma^{-1}\mathcal{V}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{V}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sigma^{-1}\mathcal{V}}

(2.17)

We call these modules “chains”, and by taking submodule/quotient, we can form chains of odd length. We say a chain is
positive
(
negative
) if it is a quotient of some
\sigma^{n}\mathcal{W}_{0}^{+,k}
(
\sigma^{n}\mathcal{W}_{0}^{-,k}
). To the best of our knowledge, these modules have not been studied in the literature.

Since every module can be expressed as a direct sum of indecomposable modules, it suffices to restrict our focus to non-trivial extensions. From the commutation relations in equation (
2.9
), we see that
{\mathcal{C}_{\beta\gamma}}
admits a block decomposition

{\mathcal{C}_{\beta\gamma}}=\bigoplus_{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{Z}}\mathcal{C}_{{\beta\gamma},\lambda}

(2.18)

where
\mathcal{C}_{{\beta\gamma},\lambda}
is the full abelian subcategory of
{\mathcal{C}_{\beta\gamma}}
that contains all
{\beta\gamma}
modules such that the generalized eigenvalues of the representation of
J_{0}
lie in
\lambda
. The morphisms and extensions between a module from
\mathcal{C}_{{\beta\gamma},\lambda}
and another from
\mathcal{C}_{{\beta\gamma},\lambda^{\prime}}
are trivial for
\lambda\neq\lambda^{\prime}
because morphisms in
{\mathcal{C}_{\beta\gamma}}
respect the generalized eigenvalues of
L_{0}
and
J_{0}
, and equation (
2.9
) tells us that the
{\beta\gamma}
modes can only shift them by an integer. Therefore we only need to study the extension structure
within
each
\mathcal{C}_{{\beta\gamma},\lambda}
. Modules in
\mathcal{C}_{{\beta\gamma},\lambda}
for
[\lambda]\neq[0]
are called typical modules while modules in
\mathcal{C}_{{\beta\gamma},[0]}
are called atypical modules.

This task is rather involved, essentially because there are not enough injectives and projectives in
\mathcal{C}_{\beta\gamma}
, so we outline our approach before getting into the technical details. It will follow from the definition of
{\mathcal{C}_{\beta\gamma}}
and Lemma
2.2
that every module in
\mathcal{C}_{{\beta\gamma},\lambda}
can be constructed as an induced module of a representation for a finite-dimensional Weyl subalgebra of
{\beta\gamma}
. Consequentially, we will see that the extension structure in
\mathcal{C}_{{\beta\gamma},\lambda}
can be understood in terms of the extension structure between modules of finite-dimensional Weyl algebras. Therefore we begin by computing the latter, then we state a lemma that explains how to utilize finite-dimensional results to understand the extension structure in
\mathcal{C}_{{\beta\gamma},\lambda}
, and finally we classify the indecomposable objects within each
\mathcal{C}_{{\beta\gamma},\lambda}
. Along the way, we describe a useful way to visualize
{\beta\gamma}
modules in terms their corresponding finite-dimensional Weyl algebra modules. Concrete examples will also be provided.

As a warm up, we start by computing extensions between some basic modules for the 1D Weyl algebra
H=\mathbb{C}[x,\partial]
in the category of all
H
-modules. This category corresponds to a
{\beta\gamma}
VOA module category strictly larger than
{\mathcal{C}_{\beta\gamma}}
, but the computations will nonetheless provide us with important information about
{\mathcal{C}_{\beta\gamma}}
. In particular, with a bit of extra work, we find that the computations of
\text{Ext}^{1}
in this
H
-module category are the same as those in
{\mathcal{C}_{\beta\gamma}}
. Let us now begin.

The simple modules
\mathbb{C}[x],\mathbb{C}[\partial],
and
x^{\lambda}\mathbb{C}[x,x^{-1}]
(
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{Z}
) each have a 2-step free, hence projective, resolution by

. For example,

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cdots}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{C}[x]}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f_{1}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f_{0}}

(2.19)

is exact, where
f_{1}(1)=\partial
and
f_{0}(1)=1
. Using these resolutions to compute
\text{Ext}^{\bullet}
, we arrive at the following results:

•

\text{Ext}^{k}(\mathbb{C}[x],\mathbb{C}[x])=\text{Ext}^{k}(\mathbb{C}[\partial],\mathbb{C}[\partial])=\mathbb{C}\,\delta_{k,0}
. In particular, neither
\mathbb{C}[x]
nor
\mathbb{C}[\partial]
have non-trivial self-extensions.

•

\text{Ext}^{k}(\mathbb{C}[\partial],\mathbb{C}[x])=\mathbb{C}\,\delta_{k,1}
, where the unique non-trivial extension is
\mathbb{C}[x,x^{-1}]
.

•

\text{Ext}^{k}(\mathbb{C}[x],\mathbb{C}[\partial])=\mathbb{C}\,\delta_{k,1}
, where the unique non-trivial extension is
\mathbb{C}[\partial,\partial^{-1}]
.

•

\text{Ext}^{k}(x^{\lambda}\mathbb{C}[x,x^{-1}],x^{\lambda}\mathbb{C}[x,x^{-1}])=\mathbb{C}\,\delta_{k,0}\oplus\mathbb{C}\,\delta_{k,1}
where the unique self-extension is given by
\overline{\mathcal{W}}_{\lambda}^{2}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=x^{\lambda}\mathbb{C}[x,x^{-1}]\oplus x^{\lambda}\mathbb{C}[x,x^{-1}]\log x
. Furthermore the unique iterated self-extensions of

are

\overline{\mathcal{W}}_{\lambda}^{k}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=x^{\lambda}\mathbb{C}[x,x^{-1}]\oplus\cdots\oplus x^{\lambda}\mathbb{C}[x,x^{-1}]\log^{k-1}x

(2.20)

•

The extension algebra between
\overline{\mathcal{W}}_{\lambda_{1}}^{k_{1}}
and
\overline{\mathcal{W}}_{\lambda_{2}}^{k_{2}}
is zero for
\lambda_{1}\neq\lambda_{2}

•

The extension algebra between
\overline{\mathcal{W}}_{\lambda}^{k}
and
\mathbb{C}[x]
or
\mathbb{C}[\partial]
are both zero.

The following lemma explains why it suffices to compute the extensions between modules by restricting focus to their structure under a finite-dimensional Weyl subalgebra of
{\beta\gamma}
. For this, call

A_{N}=\mathbb{C}[\beta_{k},\gamma_{-k}]_{-N\leq k\leq N}.

(2.21)

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
U,V
, and
W
be in
\mathcal{C}_{\beta\gamma}
, and assume that they fit in the short exact sequence
0\to U\to V\to W\to 0
. Suppose also that both
U
and
W
come from induction over
A_{N}
, namely:

U=\mathrm{Ind}_{A_{N}[\beta_{j},\gamma_{j}]_{j>N}}^{\beta\gamma}U_{N},~~W=\mathrm{Ind}_{A_{N}[\beta_{j},\gamma_{j}]_{j>N}}^{\beta\gamma}W_{N}.

(2.22)

Then
V
also comes from induction of an
A_{N}
-module
V_{N}
, and the above short exact sequence comes from the induction of the short exact sequence:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces U_{N}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces V_{N}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces W_{N}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0.}

(2.23)

Proof.

Given a module
M
of
\beta\gamma
, denote by
K_{N}(M)
the kernel of all the
\beta_{k}
and
\gamma_{k}
for
k>N
. Such
K_{N}(M)
is easily seen to be a module of
A_{N}
. When
M
comes from induction from some
A_{N}
-module
M_{N}
, we have
K_{N}(M)=M_{N}
. Applying this to the short exact sequence
0\to U\to V\to W\to 0
, using the fact that taking kernel is left exact, we get:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces U_{N}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces K_{N}(V)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces W_{N}.}

(2.24)

We claim that
K_{N}(V)\to W_{N}
is surjective. Given any
w\in W_{N}
, since
V\to W
is onto, we may choose
v\in V
such that it’s image in
W
is
w
. Since
V
is a generalized VOA module, there exists
K
such that
\beta_{k}v=\gamma_{k}v=0
for
k>K
. We will adjust
v
in a way that it’s image in
W
is still
w
, but it will be annihilated by
\beta_{j}
and
\gamma_{j}
for
N<j\leq K
. For any such mode, say
\beta_{j}
, if
\beta_{j}v\neq 0
, then since it’s image in
W
is zero, it must be in the kernel of
V\to W
, which is
U
. Thus
\beta_{j}v\in U
. By the fact that
U
comes from induction, there exists
n
such that
\beta_{j}^{n+1}v=0
. Now using
[\beta_{j}^{n},\gamma_{-j}]=-n\beta_{j}^{n-1}
, one gets:

\beta_{j}^{n}\gamma_{-j}\beta_{j}v=-n\beta_{j}^{n}v.

(2.25)

In other words,
\beta_{j}^{n}(v+\frac{1}{n}\gamma_{-j}\beta_{j}v)=0
. The element
v+\frac{1}{n}\gamma_{-j}\beta_{j}v
has the same image as
v
in
W
, since
\gamma_{-j}\beta_{j}v\in U
. Inductively, one can find
u\in U
such that
v-u
is in fact annihilated by
\beta_{j}
, and
u
is of the form
f(\beta_{j},\gamma_{-j})\beta_{j}v
for some polynomial
f
of two variables, where we always choose the normal ordering in the polynomial, namely
\gamma_{-j}
appears before
\beta_{j}
. Notice that if
v
is annihilated by
\beta_{k}
for
k\neq j
, then so is
v-f(\beta_{j},\gamma_{-j})\beta_{j}v
. Applying this to all the
\beta_{j}
and
\gamma_{j}
for
N<j<K
, one gets
v
that is annihilated by all
\beta_{j}
and
\gamma_{j}
for
j>N
, namely
v\in K_{N}(V)
whose image is
w
.

In conclusion, we have a short exact sequence:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces U_{N}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces K_{N}(V)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces W_{N}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}

(2.26)

Induction gives us a short exact sequence that fits in the diagram:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces U}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Ind}_{A_{N}[\beta_{j},\gamma_{j}]_{j>N}}^{\beta\gamma}(K_{N}(V))}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces W}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces U}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces V}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces W}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}

(2.27)

The left and right down-arrows are isomorphisms, so by the snake lemma, the middle down-arrow is an isomorphism as well. This completes the proof.

Similarly,

By Lemma

,

(2.30)

It must be that

would split. From our computational results just below (

Before proceeding to characterize the objects in
{\mathcal{C}_{\beta\gamma}}
, we need to investigate how to compute extensions between induced objects a bit further. Suppose we have a module
M\in{\mathcal{C}_{\beta\gamma}}
that comes from the induction of an

-module

. Then since

{\beta\gamma}\simeq\bigotimes_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}{\beta\gamma}_{k}=A_{N}\otimes\left(\bigotimes_{|k|>N}{\beta\gamma}_{k}\right),

(2.33)

we have the decomposition

(2.34)

where each
M_{k}
is a
{\beta\gamma}_{k}
-module. In fact, each
M_{k}
is
\mathbb{C}[\partial_{k}]
for
k>0
and
\mathbb{C}[x_{-k}]
for
k<0
. We visualize this data in terms of the following “column picture” for
M
:

\begin{array}[]{c @{\qquad} @{\cdots\quad\otimes\quad} c @{\quad\otimes\quad} c @{\quad\otimes\quad} c @{\quad\otimes\quad} c @{\quad\otimes\quad} c @{\quad\otimes\quad\cdots}}{\beta\gamma}&{\beta\gamma}_{-N-2}&{\beta\gamma}_{-N-1}&A_{N}&{\beta\gamma}_{N+1}&{\beta\gamma}_{N+2}\\
M&M_{-N-2}&M_{-N-1}&M^{\prime}&M_{N+1}&M_{N+2}\end{array}

(2.35)

\mathcal{V}
. Recalling that
\mathcal{V}=\text{Ind}_{{\beta\gamma}\geq 0}^{\beta\gamma}\mathbb{C}[\gamma_{0}]
(i.e.
\mathcal{V}
comes from the induction of the

-module

), its column picture is

\begin{array}[]{c @{\qquad} @{\cdots\quad\otimes\quad} c @{\quad\otimes\quad} c @{\quad\otimes\quad} c @{\quad\otimes\quad} c @{\quad\otimes\quad} c @{\quad\otimes\quad\cdots}}{\beta\gamma}&{\beta\gamma}_{-2}&{\beta\gamma}_{-1}&{\beta\gamma}_{0}&{\beta\gamma}_{1}&{\beta\gamma}_{2}\\

(2.36)

Note that spectral flow simply shifts the column picture of a module horizontally. For example, the column picture for

can be obtained by shifting the column picture of

by one unit to the right. We typically relabel the indices on

Let us demonstrate how the column picture can be used to compute the extensions between modules with an example. To compute the extensions of
\mathcal{V}
by
\sigma\mathcal{V}
, we first stack the column pictures for
\mathcal{V}
and

\begin{array}[]{c @{\qquad} @{\cdots\quad\otimes\quad} c @{\quad\otimes\quad} c @{\quad\otimes\quad} c @{\quad\otimes\quad} c @{\quad\otimes\quad} c @{\quad\otimes\quad\cdots}}{\beta\gamma}&{\beta\gamma}_{-2}&{\beta\gamma}_{-1}&{\beta\gamma}_{0}&{\beta\gamma}_{1}&{\beta\gamma}_{2}\\

(2.37)

Notice that

\sigma\mathcal{V}=\text{Ind}_{A_{1}[\beta_{j},\gamma_{j}]_{j>1}}^{\beta\gamma}(\mathbb{C}[\partial_{-1}]\otimes\mathbb{C}[\partial_{0}]\otimes\mathbb{C}[\partial_{1}])

where

act as 0 for

. According to Lemma

(2.39)

Applying a Künneth formula, the only non-zero contribution to

(2.40)

which is precisely

.

\mathcal{C}_{{\beta\gamma},\lambda}
for

and

.

Let
M
be an indecomposable object in
\mathcal{C}_{{\beta\gamma},\lambda}
and pick any
\mu\in\lambda
. We induct on the length of
M
. If

\sigma^{n}\mathcal{W}_{\lambda}
for some

.

Now suppose
M
has length

and assume its length

. Then

is a simple module in
\mathcal{C}_{{\beta\gamma},\lambda}
, hence is isomorphic to
\sigma^{m}\mathcal{W}_{\lambda}
for some

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sigma^{n}\mathcal{W}_{\lambda}^{k-1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sigma^{m}\mathcal{W}_{\lambda}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}

(2.41)

We now characterize all possible extensions of this type. In the column picture of

, there is a

’s and

\sigma^{m}\mathcal{W}_{\lambda}
has a

column, and the other columns are similarly

’s and
\mathbb{C}[\partial]

\mathcal{C}
. Given an object
X\in\text{Ob}(\mathcal{C})
, its parity conjugate
\Pi X
is also an object in
\mathcal{C}
. There is no corresponding notion of the parity-shifted version of a module in
{\mathcal{C}_{\beta\gamma}}
, therefore one might be concerned about the correctness of the equivalence. It turns out that
X

are isomorphic in

, albeit via an

3.1.3
Tensor structure

As we have mentioned before, we still need to pass through a few more categories and constructions before connecting with
{\mathcal{C}_{\beta\gamma}}
. However, much of the tensor structure on
{\mathcal{C}_{\beta\gamma}}
can ultimately be obtained from the structure on
\mathcal{C}
, where computations are much easier. In this section, we give the tensor product decompositions that we computed that will be relevant for the remainder of this paper.

The action of
{\mathfrak{gl}(1|1)}
on a tensor product of super modules is

x\cdot(v\otimes w)=(x\cdot v)\otimes w+(-1)^{|x||v|}v\otimes(x\cdot w)

(3.14)

for homogeneous
x
and
v
. Also the map
\tau\colon V\otimes W\to W\otimes V
given by
\tau(x\otimes y)=(-1)^{|x||y|}y\otimes x
is an isomorphism. Since we will eventually restrict to a subcategory labeled by a particular value of
x\in\mathbb{CP}^{1}
, we only compute tensor products between modules with the same
x
label and will ignore the
x=\infty
case. By tedious but not conceptually challenging computations, one sees

\displaystyle V^{s}_{n,e,x}\otimes V^{t}_{m,f,x}

\displaystyle\cong\begin{cases}\bigoplus_{k=0}^{\min(s,t)-1}\left(V^{s+t-1-2k}_{n+m+\frac{1}{2},e+f,x}\oplus V^{s+t-1-2k}_{n+m-\frac{1}{2},e+f,x}\right)&e+f\neq 0\\
\bigoplus_{k=0}^{\min(s,t)-1}P^{s+t-1-2k}_{n+m,x}&e+f=0\end{cases}

(3.15)

\displaystyle V^{s}_{n,e,x}\otimes V^{t}_{m,0,\pm,x}

\displaystyle\cong\bigoplus_{k=0}^{\min(s,t)-1}\left(V^{s+t-1-2k}_{n+m+\frac{1}{2},e,x}\oplus V^{s+t-1-2k}_{n+m-\frac{1}{2},e,x}\right)

(3.16)

\displaystyle V^{s}_{n,0,\epsilon_{1},x}\otimes V^{t}_{m,0,\epsilon_{2},x}

\displaystyle\cong\begin{cases}V^{t}_{n+m+\frac{1}{2},0,\epsilon_{1},x}\oplus V^{t}_{n+m-\frac{1}{2},0,\epsilon_{2},x}&\epsilon_{1}=\epsilon_{2},\,s=1\\
\bigoplus_{k=0}^{\min(s,t)-1}P^{s+t-1-2k}_{n+m,x}&\epsilon_{1}=-\epsilon_{2}\end{cases}

(3.17)

The tensor products where
\epsilon_{1}=\epsilon_{2}
with

can generate new indecomposables that we will not write down here. Furthermore:

\displaystyle P^{s}_{n,x}\otimes P^{t}_{m,x}

\displaystyle\cong\bigoplus_{j=0}^{\min(s,t)-1}\left(P^{s+t-1-2j}_{m+n+1,x}\oplus 2\,P^{s+t-1-2j}_{m+n,x}\oplus P^{s+t-1-2j}_{m+n-1,x}\right)

(3.18)

\displaystyle P^{s}_{n,x}\otimes V^{t}_{m,e,x}

\displaystyle\cong\bigoplus_{j=0}^{\min(s,t)-1}\left(V^{s+t-1-2j}_{m+n+1,e,x}\oplus 2\,V^{s+t-1-2j}_{m+n,e,x}\oplus V^{s+t-1-2j}_{m+n-1,e,x}\right)

(3.19)

3.2
The vertex superalgebra
\widehat{\mathfrak{gl}(1|1)}

Now we turn to the definition of the affine Lie superalgebra
\widehat{\mathfrak{gl}(1|1)}
associated to the bilinear form
\kappa
. It is defined as the super vector space
{\mathfrak{gl}(1|1)}\otimes\mathbb{C}[t,t^{-1}]\oplus\mathbb{C}\mathbf{k}
where
\mathbb{C}[t,t^{-1}]
and
\mathbf{k}
are even, together with the following non-trivial Lie brackets:

[N_{r},E_{s}]=r\mathbf{k}\delta_{r+s,0},~~[N_{r},\psi^{\pm}_{s}]=\pm\psi^{\pm}_{r+s},~~\{\psi_{r}^{+},\psi_{s}^{-}\}=E_{r+s}+r\mathbf{k}\delta_{r+s,0}

(3.20)

where
a_{r}
denotes
a\otimes t^{r}
.

Given a module
M
of
{\mathfrak{gl}(1|1)}
, one may obtain a module of
\widehat{\mathfrak{gl}(1|1)}
as follows: one first view
M
as a module of
{\mathfrak{gl}(1|1)}\otimes\mathbb{C}[t]\oplus\mathbb{C}\mathbf{k}
such that
{\mathfrak{gl}(1|1)}\otimes t\mathbb{C}[t]
acts trivially and
\mathbf{k}
acts as a number
k\in\mathbb{C}
; one may then define the induced module:

\widehat{M}^{k}=\mathcal{U}(\widehat{\mathfrak{gl}(1|1)})\otimes_{\mathcal{U}({\mathfrak{gl}(1|1)}\otimes\mathbb{C}[t]\oplus\mathbb{C}\mathbf{k})}M

(3.21)

as a representation of
\widehat{\mathfrak{gl}(1|1)}
. When
M
is the trivial module,
\widehat{M}^{k}
has the structure of a vertex operator superalgebra (VOSA), which we denote by
V_{k}(\widehat{\mathfrak{gl}(1|1)})
. For general
M
,
\widehat{M}^{k}
becomes a module of
V_{k}(\widehat{\mathfrak{gl}(1|1)})
. The assignment
M\to\widehat{M}^{k}
defines a functor
{\mathcal{I}}nd
, which we will call the induction functor.

Remark.
It turns out, as explained in
[
CMY20
]
, that for different choice of
k\neq 0
, the vertex algebras
V_{k}(\widehat{\mathfrak{gl}(1|1)})
are isomorphic to each other. Thus we will once and for all fix
k=1
, and drop
k
from all notations.

The VOSA
V(\widehat{\mathfrak{gl}(1|1)})
has the following conformal element:

\omega=\frac{1}{2}(N_{-1}E_{-1}+E_{-1}N_{-1}-\psi_{-1}^{+}\psi_{-1}^{-}+\psi_{-1}^{-}\psi_{-1}^{+})+\frac{1}{2}E_{-1}^{2}

(3.22)

with the associated Virasoro zero mode:

\displaystyle L_{0}=

\displaystyle\sum_{r>0}\left(N_{-r}E_{r}+E_{-r}N_{r}-\psi_{-r}^{+}\psi_{r}^{-}+\psi_{-r}^{-}\psi_{-r}^{+}\right)+\sum_{r>0}E_{-r}E_{r}

(3.23)

\displaystyle+(N_{0}+E_{0}/2)E_{0}-\frac{1}{2}(\psi_{0}^{+}\psi_{0}^{-}-\psi_{0}^{-}\psi_{0}^{+})

It also enjoys spectral flow symmetries
\sigma^{l}
:

\sigma^{l}(N_{r})=N_{r},~~\sigma^{l}(E_{r})=E_{r}-l\delta_{r,0},~~\sigma(\psi_{r}^{\pm})=\psi^{\pm}_{r\mp l}

(3.24)

as well as the conjugation
w
:

w(N_{r})=-N_{r},~~w(E_{r})=-E_{r},~~w(\psi_{r}^{+})=\psi_{r}^{-},~~w(\psi_{r}^{-})=-\psi_{r}^{+}.

(3.25)

These can be used to twist representations to obtain new representations.

3.3
The Kazhdan-Lusztig category

In this section, we recall the Kazhdan-Lusztig category

V(\widehat{\mathfrak{gl}(1|1)})

Definition 3.1
.

The Kazhdan-Lusztig category

Any simple module of this category is generated by its lowest conformal weight space, which is a finite-dimensional representation of
{\mathfrak{gl}(1|1)}

\widehat{V}_{n,e}

\widehat{A}_{n}

{\mathcal{I}}nd

The following is shown in

•

•

When

(3.26)

When

Remark.
To unify the notation, we will write
\widehat{A}_{n,0}
for

\widehat{A}_{n,e}

are called simple currents.

Since
E_{0}
is a central element, any representation of
V(\widehat{\mathfrak{gl}(1|1)})
can be decomposed into direct sums according to the generalized eigenvalues of
E_{0}
, which is possible by finite-length property. We may thus write

KL=\bigoplus_{e\in\mathbb{C}}KL_{e}

(3.28)

where
KL_{e}
is the subcategory such that the generalized eigenvalue of
E_{0}
is
e
. From the description of the above simple modules, it is clear that
KL_{e}
for
e\notin\mathbb{Z}
is generated by
\widehat{V}_{n,e}
and for
m\in\mathbb{Z}
,
KL_{m}
is generated by
\widehat{A}_{n,m}
. There is a similar decomposition of
\mathcal{C}
, the category of finite-dimensional representations of
{\mathfrak{gl}(1|1)}
, so we write
{\mathcal{C}}_{e}
to be the subcategory of
\mathcal{C}
where the action of
E
has generalized weight
e
. Clearly, induction is a functor from
{\mathcal{C}}_{e}
to

. In fact, more is true about this induction:

Proposition 3.2
.

When
e\notin\mathbb{Z}
or
e=0
, induction functor
{\mathcal{I}}nd
gives an equivalence of abelian supercategories:

\mathcal{C}_{e}\cong KL_{e}

(3.29)

Proof.

We only consider the case
e\notin\mathbb{Z}
, since the proof for
e=0
is almost identical. By definition, any simple module in
KL_{e}
comes from induction. We now show that induction is essentially surjective. For this, choose
M\in KL_{e}
, we will use induction on the length of
M
. Choose a maximal sub-module
N\subseteq M
. Then by assumption
N
is induced from
\mathcal{C}_{e}
and
M/N\cong\widehat{V}_{n,e}
for some
n
. We claim that we may choose generators of
M
that are annihilated by all the positive modes of
\widehat{\mathfrak{gl}(1|1)}
. We may choose such generators for
N
and
M/N

and
\overline{m}
, that are generates the lowest-weight spaces for the respective module. Choosing a pre-image
m
of
\overline{m}
, we will adjust
m
step by step so that it is annihilated by all positive modes.

First, suppose there exists
t>0
such that
E_{t}m\neq 0
. By grading restriction,
E_{t}^{l}m=0
for some

. Using
[E_{t}^{l},N_{-t}]=(tl)E_{t}^{l-1}
, we see that
E_{t}^{l}N_{-t}m=(tl)E_{t}^{l-1}m
. Let
m^{\prime}=m-\frac{1}{tl}E_{t}N_{-t}m
, then
E_{t}^{l-1}m^{\prime}=0
, and
\overline{m^{\prime}}=\overline{m}-\frac{1}{l}\overline{m}\neq 0
since

. This is again a generator. Using this procedure, we may adjust

such that

for all

.

Suppose
\psi_{t}^{+}m\neq 0
for some

(\psi_{t}^{+})^{l}m=0
for some

. Since
\{(\psi_{t}^{+})^{l},\psi_{-t}^{-}\}=l(E_{0}+t)(\psi_{t}^{+})^{l-1}
, we have
(\psi_{t}^{+})^{l}\psi_{-t}^{-}m=l(\psi_{t}^{+})^{l-1}(E_{0}+t)m
. Let
m^{\prime}=l(E_{0}+t)m-\psi_{t}^{+}\psi_{-t}^{-}m
, then
(\psi_{t}^{+})^{l-1}m^{\prime}=0
, and
\overline{m^{\prime}}=(l-1)(e+t)\overline{m}
which is still a generator since

and

. If the starting

satisfies
\psi_{j}^{\pm}m=E_{k}m=0
for

and

, then after the adjustment,
\psi_{j}^{\pm}m^{\prime}=E_{k}m^{\prime}=0
for
j\geq t
and
k>0
. We may then adjust
m
downward from the largest

such that
\psi_{t}^{\pm}m\neq 0
, and obtain

that is annihilated by all
\psi_{t}^{\pm},E_{t}
for

.

Finally we perform a similar procedure for

to obtain the final
m
. We may also assume that
N_{0}\overline{m}=(n-1/2)\overline{m}
for some

, which means that
(N_{0}-n+1/2)m\in N
. This is an element annihilated by all positive modes of
\widehat{\mathfrak{gl}(1|1)}
, which then must be in the lowest conformal weight space of
N
.

Now consider the
{\mathfrak{gl}(1|1)}
module
V
generated by
m
and the

. By the above consideration, this module is finite dimensional. The map
V\to M
then induces a surjection
\widehat{V}\to M
as
V(\widehat{\mathfrak{gl}(1|1)})
modules. We claim that the kernel

must be of the form
\widehat{W}
for some

. To prove the claim, we use an inductive argument on the number of composition factors of
\widehat{V}
. Choose a minimal sub-module

of

and let

be the quotient
V/V_{n^{\prime},e}
. We have a short exact sequence:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widehat{V}_{n^{\prime},e}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widehat{V}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widehat{U}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}

(3.30)

If

, then we are done by inductive hypothesis. Otherwise,

fits in the exact sequence:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widehat{V}_{n^{\prime},e}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widehat{V}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widehat{U}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}

Because of this, the difficulty in the study of

3.4
Fusion structure

By the work of
[
HLZ10
]
-
[
HLZ11a
]
,
P(z)
-intertwining operators define a monoidal structure on
KL
. In this section, we will compute the fusion products of modules by relating the fusion product to the tensor product of
{\mathfrak{gl}(1|1)}
modules.

To start, we have the following statement, which is proved in
[
CMY20
]
:

Theorem 3.3
.

KL
is a rigid braided tensor supercategory; moreover, it is a ribbon category with even natural twist
\theta=e^{2\pi iL_{0}}
.

The fusion product (if it exists) of two generalized modules
W_{1}
and
W_{2}
of a VOSA
V
will be denoted by
W_{1}\times_{V}W_{2}
, or simply
W_{1}\times W_{2}
if it is clear what the VOSA is. It turns out that one can understand the fusion structure of
KL
using the tensor structure of
{\mathfrak{gl}(1|1)}
modules. Let
M_{1},M_{2}
and
M_{3}
be finite-dimensional
{\mathfrak{gl}(1|1)}
modules. Given an intertwiner operator
\mathcal{Y}
of type
\binom{\widehat{M}_{3}}{\widehat{M}_{1}\widehat{M}_{2}}
, consider the assignment
\pi(\mathcal{Y})\colon M_{1}\otimes M_{2}\to M_{3}
given by:

\pi(\mathcal{Y})(m_{1}\otimes m_{2})=\pi_{0}(\mathcal{Y}(m_{1},1)m_{2})

(3.32)

where
\pi_{0}
denotes projection onto the lowest conformal weight space. This
\pi(\mathcal{Y})
is in fact a homomorphism of
{\mathfrak{gl}(1|1)}
modules. The inverse of this is established in
[
CMY20
]
. To state it, let’s recall the definition of taking contragredient dual. For a full definition, see
[
CKM17
]
. Let
M
be a finite-dimensional module of
{\mathfrak{gl}(1|1)}
, then the linear dual
M^{*}=\mathrm{Hom}(M,\mathbb{C})
has the structure of a
{\mathfrak{gl}(1|1)}
module, induced by the action of
{\mathfrak{gl}(1|1)}
on
M
. There is a similar operation on modules of
\widehat{\mathfrak{gl}(1|1)}
. Given a grading-restricted generalized
\widehat{\mathfrak{gl}(1|1)}
module
W
. Write the decomposition of
W
into generalized conformal weight spaces as:

W=\bigoplus_{h\in\mathbb{C}}W_{[h]}.

(3.33)

Define
W^{\prime}
to be the super vector space:

W^{\prime}=\bigoplus_{h\in\mathbb{C}}W_{[h]}^{*},

(3.34)

together with the action of
V(\widehat{\mathfrak{gl}(1|1)})
by:

\langle Y_{W^{\prime}}(v,z)w^{\prime},w\rangle=\langle w^{\prime},Y_{W}(e^{zL_{1}}(-z^{2})^{L_{0}}v,z^{-1})w\rangle.

(3.35)

Here
\langle-,-\rangle
is the natural pairing between
W
and
W^{\prime}
, and the above is well-defined by the grading restriction condition. The module
W^{\prime}
is called the contragredient dual of
W
. The following is proved in
[
CMY20
]
:

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
M_{1}
,
M_{2}
and
M_{3}
be finite-dimensional
{\mathfrak{gl}(1|1)}
modules, and let
f\colon M_{1}\otimes M_{2}\to M_{3}^{*}
be a homomorphism of
{\mathfrak{gl}(1|1)}
modules. Then there exists a unique intertwiner operator
\mathcal{Y}
of type
\binom{\widehat{M}_{3}^{\prime}}{\widehat{M}_{1}\widehat{M}_{2}}
such that
\pi(\mathcal{Y})=f
.

This Proposition, together with rigidity and Proposition
3.2
, can help us compute fusion product explicitly for any pair modules in
KL
. Let
S_{b}:=\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}
and
S_{g}:=\mathbb{C}\setminus S_{b}
.

Lemma 3.5
.

\widehat{M_{e}^{*}}\cong\widehat{M_{e}^{\prime}}
for any
e\in S_{g}
.

Proof.

By definition, the lowest conformal weight space of
\widehat{M_{e}^{\prime}}
is
M_{e}^{*}
, thus the identity map
M_{e}^{*}\to M_{e}^{*}
induces a map of VOSA modules
\widehat{M_{e}^{*}}\to\widehat{M_{e}^{\prime}}
. We claim that this map is injective. Suppose otherwise, then its kernel would contain a minimal module
\widehat{V}_{n-1/2,e}
for some
n
in the case
e\neq 0
or
\widehat{A}_{n,0}
for the case
e=0
. In any case we denote such minimal module by
\widehat{V}
. Since
\mathrm{Hom}(V,M_{e}^{*})\cong\mathrm{Hom}(\widehat{V},\widehat{M_{e}^{*}})
, the embedding
\widehat{V}\to\widehat{M_{e}^{*}}
comes from the induces map of a injection
V\to M_{e}^{*}
, and the map
\widehat{V}\to\widehat{M_{e}^{\prime}}
then comes from the map
\widehat{V}\to\widehat{V^{*}}^{\prime}\to\widehat{M_{e}^{\prime}}
. The first map is an isomorphism by
[
CMY20
]
, and the second is nonzero and thus is an embedding, a contradiction. Since this map is injective, by counting the number of composition factors, this is also surjective, so it is an isomorphism.

∎

As a corollary, we obtain the following:

Corollary 3.6
.

In the case when
e,e^{\prime}\in S_{g}
and
e+e^{\prime}\in S_{g}
,
\widehat{M_{e}\otimes M_{e^{\prime}}}\cong\widehat{M}_{e}\times\widehat{M}_{e^{\prime}}
.

Proof.

By Proposition
3.4
and Lemma
3.5
, one has a map
\widehat{M}_{e}\times\widehat{M}_{e^{\prime}}\to\widehat{M_{e}\otimes M_{e^{\prime}}}
. This is surjective since
\widehat{M_{e}\otimes M_{e^{\prime}}}
is generated by
M_{e}\otimes M_{e^{\prime}}
. Indeed, by Proposition
3.2
, if the image of this intertwiner was contained in some sub-module, it would be a sub-module of the form
\widehat{W}
for some
W\subseteq M_{e}\otimes M_{e^{\prime}}
. This is contradicting the fact that
\pi(\mathcal{Y})
is an isomorphism onto
M_{e}\otimes M_{e^{\prime}}
. To conclude the proof, we only need to compute the number of composition factors on both sides. Rigidity of
KL
implies that fusion is exact, and so the number of composition series can be computed using fusion rules of irreducible modules as in
[
CMY20
, Theorem 3.2.4]
. Comparing this with the composition series of
M_{e}\otimes M_{e^{\prime}}
, we conclude that the map is an isomorphism.

∎

We can use Corollary
3.6
to also deal with fusion rules of
M_{e}
for
e\in S_{b}
. Indeed, since fusion product with simple currents preserves socle series
[
CR13a
]
, the functor:

\widehat{A}_{n,l}\times-\colon KL_{e}\to KL_{e+l}

(3.36)

is an equivalence, with inverse given by
\widehat{A}_{-n,-l}
. In particular, for any
V\in KL_{e}
where
e\in S_{b}
, we can “shift” V to be in
KL_{0}
using simple currents

V=\widehat{A}_{n,e}\times(\widehat{A}_{-n,-e}\times V).

(3.37)

Suppose we want to compute
V\times W
for
V\in KL_{e}
and
W\in KL_{f}
. If either
e,f
or
e+f\in S_{b}
, then we can use simple currents to pull the computation into
KL_{0}
, so that we can apply Corollary
3.6
. The question now becomes identifying
\widehat{A}_{n,e}\times V
for various
V
and
e\in S_{b}
. It suffices to take
V
to be indecomposable. We introduce a collection of indecomposable modules that will be relevant for connecting to
\mathcal{C}_{\beta\gamma}
(they either come from induction, or a spectral flow of a module from induction). Note that this is not a complete list of indecomposables in

.

•

\widehat{A}_{n,l}
for

. When

,
\widehat{A}_{n,l}\cong\sigma^{l}(\widehat{A}_{n+l+\frac{1}{2},0})
. When

,
\widehat{A}_{n,l}\cong\sigma^{l}\circ w(\widehat{A}_{-n-l+\frac{1}{2},0})\cong\sigma^{l}(\widehat{A}_{n+l-\frac{1}{2},0})
.

•

\widehat{V}_{n,0,\pm,x}^{t}
for

and

as well as
\widehat{V}_{n,e,x}^{t}
for

. These are the induced module from

and

respectively. For

.

•

\widehat{P}_{n,x}^{t}
, which is induced from

. Define
\widehat{P}_{n-l-\frac{1}{2},l,x}^{t}
for

and for

We will also introduce a function

on

\epsilon(l)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}-\frac{1}{2}&\text{if }l<0,\\

\frac{1}{2}&\text{if }l>0.\end{array}\right.

\widehat{V}_{(2b-a-1)/2,a,x}^{t}\cong V_{\mu,x,Z}^{t}
.

Proof.

For the case when
a\notin\mathbb{Z}
, we proceed in the same way as the proof in
[
CGN21
]
: note that there is a map of
{\mathfrak{gl}(1|1)}
modules
V_{b-(a-1)/2,a,x}^{t}\to V_{\mu,x,Z}^{t}[0]
, where
V_{\mu,x,Z}^{t}[0]
is the lowest conformal weight space. This morphism induces a morphism of
V(\widehat{\mathfrak{gl}(1|1)})
modules:
\widehat{V}_{b-(a-1)/2,a,x,-}^{t}\to V_{\mu,x,Z}^{t}
. This is an isomorphism on the unique minimal submodule of
\widehat{V}_{b-(a-1)/2,a,x}^{t}
, thus is an embedding. It is thus an isomorphism by counting the number of irreducible factors. Similar situation holds for
a=0
.

When
a\in S_{b}
, we have the free field logarithmic intertwiner
V_{\mu,Z}\times_{V_{Z}}V_{0,x,Z}^{t}\cong V_{\mu,x,Z}^{t}
. Restricting this to the submodule
\widehat{A}_{\frac{2b-a}{2}+\epsilon(a),a}\subseteq V_{\mu,Z}
one gets an intertwiner of
V(\widehat{\mathfrak{gl}(1|1)})
modules:

\widehat{A}_{\frac{2b-a}{2}+\epsilon(a),a}\times V_{0,x,Z}^{t}\to V_{\mu,x,Z}^{t}.

(4.20)

It is injective because it is non-zero when restricted to the unique irreducible sub-module
\widehat{A}_{\frac{2b-a}{2}+\epsilon(a),a}\times\widehat{A}_{0,0}
. It is then an isomorphism by counting the number of composition factors. Thus comparing this with Lemma
3.8
we obtain the desired result.

∎

In conclusion, many modules of
V(\widehat{\mathfrak{gl}(1|1)})
can be identified with restrictions of modules of
V_{Z}
. We want to remark here that although only
\widehat{V}_{n,e,-,x}^{t}
show up in the above consideration, we can also find
\widehat{V}_{n,e,+,x}^{t}
by pre-composing the embedding
V(\widehat{\mathfrak{gl}(1|1)})\to V_{Z}
with the automorphism
w
introduced in Section
3.2
.

4.2.2
A free-field realization of
{\mathcal{V}}_{ext}
.

Let us return to the VOSA
{\mathcal{V}}_{ext}
. Our goal is to introduce a free-field realization of
{\mathcal{V}}_{ext}
that is compatible with the free-field realization
V(\widehat{\mathfrak{gl}(1|1)})\to V_{Z}
described in the last section. Recall that
{\mathcal{V}}_{ext}\cong V_{\beta\gamma}\otimes V_{bc}
. It is well known (see for example
[
AW20
]
) that
V_{\beta\gamma}
has a free-field realization by a lattice VOA generated by
\partial\psi,\partial\theta
and
e^{\pm\psi+\theta}
, where
(\psi,\psi)=-(\theta,\theta)=1
. There is a screening operator
S=:\mathrel{\mspace{2mu}e^{\psi}\mspace{2mu}}:
such that
V_{\beta\gamma}
is the kernel of
S
. By Bose-Fermi correspondence,
V_{bc}
is isomorphic to a lattice VOSA of a single free boson. To conform with the free-field realization of
V(\widehat{\mathfrak{gl}(1|1)})
, we set
\psi=Z-Y
and
\theta=Y-Z-X
, which means that
\psi+\theta=-X
. We again treat the modes of
:\mathrel{\mspace{2mu}e^{\pm Z}\mspace{2mu}}:
as odd. With this redefinition,
Z+X
becomes an independent variable (it has zero pairing with
\psi,\theta
), whose associated lattice VOSA is
V_{bc}
. Thus we can extend
V_{Z}
by the operator
:\mathrel{\mspace{2mu}e^{\pm X}\mspace{2mu}}:
, or in other words, the
V_{Z}
module
\mathcal{V}=\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}V_{nX,Z}
has the structure of a VOSA, and is the lattice VOSA associated to the lattice generated by
X,Z
. The embedding
{\mathcal{V}}_{ext}\to\mathcal{V}
is given by:

b\mapsto:\mathrel{\mspace{2mu}e^{Z+X}\mspace{2mu}}:,~~~c\mapsto:\mathrel{\mspace{2mu}e^{-Z-X}\mspace{2mu}}:,~~~\beta\mapsto:\mathrel{\mspace{2mu}e^{-X}\mspace{2mu}}:,~~~\gamma\mapsto:\mathrel{\mspace{2mu}(\partial Z-\partial Y)e^{X}\mspace{2mu}}:

(4.21)

and its image is the kernel of the screening operator
S=\oint_{z}:\mathrel{\mspace{2mu}e^{\psi}\mspace{2mu}}:=\oint_{z}:\mathrel{\mspace{2mu}e^{Z-Y}\mspace{2mu}}:
. We thus have:

Proposition 4.5
.

There are free-field realizations compatible with the embedding
V(\widehat{\mathfrak{gl}(1|1)})\to{\mathcal{V}}_{ext}
given by the following diagram:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces V(\widehat{\mathfrak{gl}(1|1)})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces V_{Z}\cong\bigoplus_{n}\mathbb{F}_{nZ}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{V}}_{ext}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{V}}\cong\bigoplus_{m,n}\mathbb{F}_{mX+nZ}}

(4.22)

We now come back to the question of lifting modules via the functor
{\mathcal{F}}
. This can be done by lifting modules from
V_{Z}
to
{\mathcal{V}}
, and then restricting them to
{\mathcal{V}}_{ext}
modules. The modules of
V_{Z}
are given by
V_{mX+nY,Z,x}^{t}
, and they are generated by the vacuum module
(mX+nY)^{t-1}|\mu\rangle
. However, not all of them can be lifted to
{\mathcal{V}}
. In order to lift to
{\mathcal{V}}
,
n=0
otherwise the action of
:\mathrel{\mspace{2mu}e^{X}\mspace{2mu}}:
is non-local. Since
n-m(x+\frac{1}{2})=0
, this requires that
x=-\frac{1}{2}
. Similarly, we cannot use any linear combination
\mu=aX+bY
, since otherwise the conformal weight of
:\mathrel{\mspace{2mu}e^{-X}\mspace{2mu}}:
is not an integer. To have integer conformal weight, we need that
b\in\mathbb{Z}
. Thus we require
b\in\mathbb{Z}
and
x=-\frac{1}{2}
. We denote by
{\mathcal{V}}_{\mu}^{t}
the resulting module of
{\mathcal{V}}
. On the other hand, given a module of
\mathcal{W}
of
V_{\beta\gamma}
, we can view the Deligne product
\mathcal{W}\boxtimes V_{bc}
as a module of
\mathcal{V}_{ext}
using the isomorphism
{\mathcal{V}}_{ext}\cong V_{\beta\gamma}\otimes V_{bc}
. Recall the
V_{\beta\gamma}
modules
{\mathcal{W}}_{[a]}^{t}
and
({\mathcal{W}}_{0}^{\pm})^{t}
. When
t=1
, the following is shown in
[
AW20
, Proposition 2.12]
:

Proposition 4.6
.

Let
\mu=aX+bY
with
b\in\mathbb{Z}
. When
a\notin\mathbb{Z}
, there are isomorphisms of
{\mathcal{V}}_{ext}
modules:

{\mathcal{V}}_{\mu}\cong\sigma^{-b+1}{\mathcal{W}}_{[-a]}\boxtimes V_{bc}.

(4.23)

When
a\in\mathbb{Z}
, there are isomorphism of
{\mathcal{V}}_{ext}
modules:

{\mathcal{V}}_{\mu}\cong\sigma^{-b+1}({\mathcal{W}}_{[0]}^{-})\boxtimes V_{bc}.

(4.24)

We can extend it to the following:

Proposition 4.7
.

Let
\mu=aX+bY
with
b\in\mathbb{Z}
. When
a\notin\mathbb{Z}
, there are isomorphisms of
{\mathcal{V}}_{ext}
modules:

{\mathcal{V}}_{\mu}^{t}\cong\sigma^{-b+1}{\mathcal{W}}_{[-a]}^{t}\boxtimes V_{bc}.

(4.25)

When
a\in\mathbb{Z}
, there are isomorphism of
{\mathcal{V}}_{ext}
modules:

{\mathcal{V}}_{\mu}^{t}\cong\sigma^{-b+1}({\mathcal{W}}_{[0]}^{-})^{t}\boxtimes V_{bc}.

(4.26)

Proof.

When
t=1
, this is simply
[
AW20
, Proposition 2.12]
. When
t>1
, we only need to show that the module
{\mathcal{V}}_{\mu}^{t}
is indecomposable, and the result will follow from Theorem
2.3
and Theorem
2.4
: the only indecomposable modules having
\sigma^{-b+1}{\mathcal{W}}_{[-a]}
or
\sigma^{-b+1}{\mathcal{W}}_{[0]}^{-}
in its composition series are the long chains. To show that it is indecomposable, we compute the action of
:\mathrel{\mspace{2mu}\gamma\beta\mspace{2mu}}:
on the generator
X^{t-1}|\mu\rangle
. It is easy to see that there is a Jordan block of size
t
, and thus it is indecomposable.

∎

With this, we derive:

Proposition 4.8
.

Let
b\in\mathbb{Z}
. If
a\notin\mathbb{Z}
, one has the following isomorphism of
{\mathcal{V}}_{ext}
modules:

{\mathcal{F}}\left(\widehat{V}_{(2b-a-1)/2,a,x=-1/2}^{t}\right)\cong\sigma^{-b+1}\mathcal{W}_{[-a]}^{t}\boxtimes V_{bc}.

(4.27)

For
a\in\mathbb{Z}
, one has the following isomorphism of
{\mathcal{V}}_{ext}
modules:

\displaystyle{\displaystyle\mathcal{F}}\left(\widehat{V}_{(2b-a-1)/2,a,-,x=-1/2}^{t}\right)\cong\sigma^{-b+1}(\mathcal{W}_{0}^{-})^{t}\boxtimes V_{bc},

(4.28)

\displaystyle{\displaystyle\mathcal{F}}\left(\widehat{V}_{(2b-a-1)/2,a,+,x=-1/2}^{t}\right)\cong\sigma^{-b}(\mathcal{W}_{0}^{+})^{t}\boxtimes V_{bc}.

Proof.

We have seen that
{\mathcal{V}}\times_{V_{Z}}V_{\mu,x=-1/2,Z}^{t}\cong{\mathcal{V}}_{\mu}^{t}
. The inclusion
{\mathcal{V}}_{ext}\to{\mathcal{V}}
induces an isomorphism:

{\mathcal{F}}\left({\mathcal{V}}_{\mu}^{t}\right)\cong{\mathcal{V}}\times_{V_{Z}}V_{\mu,x=-1/2,Z}^{t}

(4.29)

as modules of the mode algebra of
{\mathcal{V}}_{ext}
. Comparing this with Proposition
4.4
and Proposition
4.7
, we get the desired result for
\widehat{V}_{b-(a-1)/2,a,x=-1/2}^{t}
when

and
\widehat{V}_{b-\frac{a-1}{2},a,-,x=-1/2}^{t}
when

. Twisting both free-field realizations of
V(\widehat{\mathfrak{gl}(1|1)})
and
V_{\beta\gamma}
by conjugation

gives the result for
\widehat{V}_{b-\frac{a-1}{2},a,+,x=-1/2}^{t}
.

∎

Remark.
The requirement that
x=-1/2
implies that
N_{0}+E_{0}/2
acts semi-simply, and requiring that
b\in\mathbb{Z}
implies that it has integer eigenvalues. The above Proposition implies that a sufficient condition for a
V(\widehat{\mathfrak{gl}(1|1)})
module to be lifted to
{\mathcal{V}}_{ext}
is that
N_{0}+E_{0}/2
acts semi-simply with integer eigenvalues. We denote by
KL^{N+E/2}
the subcategory of
KL
where
N_{0}+E_{0}/2
acts semi-simply with integer eigenvalues.

As a corollary, we prove the first part of Theorem
1.1
:

Corollary 4.9
.

The category
{\mathcal{C}_{\beta\gamma}}
has the structure of a braided tensor category defined by
P(z)
-intertwining operators.

Proof.

As discussed in Section
4.1
, the supercategory
\mathrm{Rep}^{0}(\mathcal{V}_{ext})
has the structure of a braided tensor supercategory defined by
P(z)
-intertwining operators. By Theorem
2.3
, Theorem
2.4
as well as Proposition
4.8
, we conclude that
{\mathcal{C}_{\beta\gamma}}\boxtimes\mathrm{SVect}
is a full subcategory of
\mathrm{Rep}^{0}(\mathcal{V}_{ext})
, so we only need to show that this subcategory is a closed under fusion product. By
[
CMY20
]
, we conclude that fusion product on
KL
, and consequently on
\mathrm{Rep}^{0}(\mathcal{V}_{ext})
, is exact. Thus by the definition of
{\mathcal{C}_{\beta\gamma}}
, we only need to show that given two modules in
{\mathcal{C}_{\beta\gamma}}\boxtimes\mathrm{SVect}
, fusion product of their composition factors are still in
{\mathcal{C}_{\beta\gamma}}\boxtimes\mathrm{SVect}
. This is computed in
[
AW20
]
, and we conclude that
{\mathcal{C}_{\beta\gamma}}\boxtimes\mathrm{SVect}
is closed under fusion, and is thus a braided tensor subcategory. Thus
P(z)
-intertwining operators define a braided tensor category structure on
{\mathcal{C}_{\beta\gamma}}
.
∎

4.3
The equivalence

In this section, we complete the proof of Theorem
1.1
. Since the work of
[
CKM17
,
CMY20a
]
already implies that the functor
\mathcal{F}
is a braided tensor functor, we only need to show that the image of
KL^{0}
under
\mathcal{F}
coincides with
{\mathcal{C}_{\beta\gamma}}\boxtimes\mathrm{SVect}
as abelian supercategories. The procedure for the proof is the following:

1.

Recall the category
KL^{N+E/2}
. We first conclude from Proposition
4.8
that
KL^{N+E/2}
is a subcategory of
KL^{0}
.

2.

We then use Theorem
2.3
and
2.4
to conclude that the lifting functor
\mathcal{F}
restricted to
KL^{N+E/2}
is essentially surjective onto
{\mathcal{C}_{\beta\gamma}}\boxtimes\mathrm{SVect}
.

3.

Finally, we conclude from the above two points that the functor
{\mathcal{F}}
identifies
KL^{0}/\mathbb{Z}
with
{\mathcal{C}_{\beta\gamma}}\boxtimes\mathrm{SVect}
, and that
KL^{0}
coincides with with
KL^{N+E/2}
.

Let us start with the following:

Lemma 4.10
.

KL^{N+E/2}
is a subcategory of
KL^{0}
.

Proof.

We need to show that objects in
KL^{N+E/2}
can be lifted to
\mathrm{Rep}^{0}({\mathcal{V}}_{ext})
. Consider first
e\notin\mathbb{Z}
with
\widehat{M}\in KL_{e}
for some
{\mathfrak{gl}(1|1)}
module
M
, and suppose
\widehat{M}\in KL^{N+E/2}
. To show that
\widehat{M}
can be lifted to a local module of
{\mathcal{V}}_{ext}
, we only need to show that it is a quotient of a module that can be lifted, since if the monodromy is trivial on a module, it is trivial on all quotients. This is guaranteed if
M
is a quotient of a direct sum of
V_{n,e,x=-1/2}^{t}
for various
n\in(1-e)/2+\mathbb{Z}
and
t
. Without loss of generality one may assume that
M
is generated by a single
m
such that
(E-e)^{t}m=0
, and
(N+E/2)m=km
for some
k\in\mathbb{Z}
. We may choose
k
to be the largest such value so that
\psi^{+}m=0
. It is clear then that the module
M
is spanned by the vectors coming from applying
{\mathfrak{gl}(1|1)}
to
m
:

m,\psi^{-}m,(E-e)m,(E-e)\psi^{-}m,\ldots,(E-e)^{t-1}m,(E-e)^{t-1}\psi^{-}m.

(4.30)

From the analysis of Section
3.1
, there is a surjection:

V_{k-(e+1)/2,e,x=-1/2}^{t}\to M

(4.31)

by mapping the highest weight vector of the generator to
m
. This is a well-defined map since the structure of
V_{k-(e+1)/2,e,x=-1/2}^{t}
is determined by an element
v
with
(N+E/2)v=kv
,
\psi^{+}v=0
as well as
(E-e)
has order
t
. This shows that
M
is a quotient of
V_{k-(e+1)/2,e,x=-1/2}^{t}
, and so
\widehat{V}_{k-(e+1)/2,e,x=-1/2}^{t}
maps onto
\widehat{M}
. Since
\widehat{V}_{k-(e+1)/2,e,x=-1/2}^{t}
can be lifted to a local module, so can
\widehat{M}
.

A similar argument can be applied when
e=0
since any
M
such that
N
acts semi-simply with integer eigenvalues and
E
acts nilpotently is a quotient of
P_{n,0,x=-1/2}^{t}
.

Finally, let us consider when
e\in S_{b}
. Let
W\in KL_{e}
, then
W=\widehat{A}_{-e/2+\epsilon(e),e}\times\widehat{M}
for some
M
. This means that
W
can be extended if and only if
\widehat{M}
can be. We must show that
N+E/2
acts semi-simply on
M
with integer eigenvalues. If this were not the case, two things can go wrong: first,
N+E/2
does not have integer eigenvalues, or it does not act semi-simply. Suppose it was the first case, which means that
M
has a sub-quotient
A_{n,0}
such that
n\notin\mathbb{Z}
, but this means that
W
has a sub-quotient
\widehat{A}_{-e/2+\epsilon(e),e}\times\widehat{A}_{n,0}=\widehat{A}_{n-e/2+\epsilon(e),e}
, a sub-module of
\widehat{V}_{n-e/2-\epsilon(e),e}
on which
N_{0}+E_{0}/2
acts semi-simply with eigenvalues in
n+\mathbb{Z}\neq\mathbb{Z}
, a contradiction to our assumption on
W
. If
N+E/2
does not act semi-simply, then since
\widehat{A}_{-e/2+\epsilon(e),e}=\sigma^{e}(\widehat{A}_{e,0})
, one has

\widehat{A}_{-e/2+\epsilon(e),e}\times\widehat{M}\cong\sigma^{e}\left(\widehat{A_{e}\otimes M}\right).

(4.32)

By the definition of spectral flow, the action of
N_{0}+E_{0}/2
on
\sigma^{e}\left(\widehat{A_{e}\otimes M}\right)
is the same as the action of
N_{0}+E_{0}/2+e_{0}/2
on
\widehat{A_{e}\otimes M}
. If
N+E/2
does not act semi-simply on
M
, then
N_{0}+E_{0}/2+e_{0}/2
can not act semi-simply since they only differ by a scalar, giving a contradiction to our assumption on
W
.

This means that we may apply the previous argument to
M
, so that
\widehat{M}
can be lifted, and thus so can
W
. This completes the proof.

∎

Next, we prove:

Proposition 4.11
.

The functor
\mathcal{F}
restricted to
KL^{N+E/2}
is essentially surjective onto
{\mathcal{C}_{\beta\gamma}}\boxtimes\mathrm{SVect}
.

Proof.

To show that it is essentially surjective, by Theorem
2.3
and
2.4
, we need only show that the image of
\mathcal{F}
contains
\sigma^{l}{\mathcal{W}}_{[a]}^{t}
for
a\notin\mathbb{Z}
and
\sigma^{l}({\mathcal{W}}_{[0]}^{\pm})^{t}
. These then follow from Proposition
4.8
.

∎

Now we can finish the proof of Theorem
1.1
:

Theorem 4.12
.

The functor
\mathcal{F}
provides an equivalence of braided tensor supercategories:

{\mathcal{C}_{\beta\gamma}}\boxtimes\mathrm{SVect}\cong KL^{0}/\mathbb{Z}.

(4.33)

Proof.

Let us show that the image of
KL^{0}
under
\mathcal{F}
is
{\mathcal{C}_{\beta\gamma}}\boxtimes\mathrm{SVect}
. For this, it is enough to show that the simple modules of
KL^{0}
coincides with those of
KL^{N+E/2}
. First of all, consider
e\notin\mathbb{Z}
, in which case the simple modules are
\widehat{V}_{n,e}
. If monomdromy acts trivially on
{\mathcal{V}}_{ext}\times_{V(\widehat{\mathfrak{gl}(1|1)})}\widehat{V}_{n,e}
, then the monodromy of
{\mathcal{V}}\times_{V_{Z}}\widehat{V}_{n,e}
would also be trivial. We have seen that this only happens if
n+e/2\pm\frac{1}{2}\in\mathbb{Z}
, or in other words, when
\widehat{V}_{n,e}\in KL^{N+E/2}
. When
e\in\mathbb{Z}
, the simple modules are
\widehat{A}_{n,e}
, and we embed them into
\widehat{V}_{n,e,-}
. Since monodromy can be computed by using the twist element
e^{2\pi iL_{0}}
, and
L_{0}
acts semi-simply on
\widehat{V}_{n,e,-},{\mathcal{V}}_{ext}
as well as the fusion
{\mathcal{V}}_{ext}\times_{V(\widehat{\mathfrak{gl}(1|1)})}\widehat{V}_{n,e,-}
, we know that the monodromy map is semi-simple. More-over,
\widehat{V}_{n,e,-}
is indecomposable as a
V(\widehat{\mathfrak{gl}(1|1)})
module, and so the monodromy must be scalar on each direct summand of
{\mathcal{V}}_{ext}\times_{V(\widehat{\mathfrak{gl}(1|1)})}\widehat{V}_{n,e,-}
. This means that if monodromy action on
{\mathcal{V}}_{ext}\times_{V(\widehat{\mathfrak{gl}(1|1)})}\widehat{V}_{n,e,-}
is non-trivial, then it is nontrivial on
{\mathcal{V}}_{ext}\times_{V(\widehat{\mathfrak{gl}(1|1)})}\widehat{A}_{n,e}
by naturality of commutativity constraints and exactness of fusion. Using similar argument as in the case of
e\notin\mathbb{Z}
, this means that
\widehat{V}_{n,e,-}
is an object in
KL^{N+E/2}
, and so
\widehat{A}_{n,e}
must also be in
KL^{N+E/2}
.

By the discussions of Section
4.1
, the functor
\mathcal{F}
identifies the image of
KL^{0}
with the de-equivariantization. Thus we have an equivalence:

KL^{0}/\mathbb{Z}\cong\mathcal{C}_{\beta\gamma}\boxtimes\mathrm{SVect}

(4.34)

as desired.

∎

Corollary 4.13
.

KL^{N+E/2}
coincides with
KL^{0}
. Moreover, for each
e\in\mathbb{C}
,
\mathcal{F}
gives an equivalence between abelian supercategories:

\mathcal{F}:KL^{N+E/2}_{e}=KL^{0}_{e}\cong\mathcal{C}_{\beta\gamma,[e]}\boxtimes\mathrm{SVect}

(4.35)

Proof.

We first show that
KL^{N+E/2}
coincides with
KL^{0}
. Let
W\in KL^{0}_{e}
for some
e
, then
{\mathcal{F}}(W)
is an element in
\mathcal{C}_{\beta\gamma,[e]}
. By Corollary
4.11
, there is an element
V\in KL_{e}^{N+E/2}
such that:

\mathcal{F}(V)\cong\mathcal{F}(W)

(4.36)

This is an equivalence as modules of
{\mathcal{V}}_{ext}
, so must be an equivalence as modules between
V(\widehat{\mathfrak{gl}(1|1)})
. Taking the generalized
E_{0}
weight
e
part, we must have
W\cong V
. Thus
KL^{N+E/2}
coincides with
KL^{0}
.

We now show that
\mathcal{F}
restricts to an equivalence of abelian supercategories:

\mathcal{F}:KL^{N+E/2}_{e}=KL^{0}_{e}\cong\mathcal{C}_{\beta\gamma,[e]}\boxtimes\mathrm{SVect}

(4.37)

It is essentially surjective from Corollary
4.11
, and we only need to show that it is fully-faithful. For any
W_{1},W_{2}\in KL_{e}^{N+E/2}
, if
f\colon W_{1}\to W_{2}
is nonzero, because
{\mathcal{V}}_{ext}
is a direct sum of simple currents, the associated map:

{\mathcal{F}}(W_{1})\to\mathcal{F}(W_{2})

(4.38)

is non-zero. Thus the functor
{\mathcal{F}}
is faithful. To show that it is full, take any
W_{1},W_{2}\in KL_{e}^{N+E/2}
and
f\in\mathrm{Hom}(\mathcal{F}(W_{1}),\mathcal{F}(W_{2}))
. It is a morphism as
{\mathcal{V}}_{ext}
modules, so must be a morphism in
\mathrm{Ind}(KL)
. By restricting this morphism to the generalized
E_{0}
weight
e
part, one obtains a morphism between
W_{1}
and
W_{2}
. This provides natural bijections, and so the functor
\mathcal{F}
is fully faithful when restricted to
KL_{e}^{N+E/2}
.

∎

4.4
The fusion structure of
{\mathcal{C}_{\beta\gamma}}

Using the equivalence as well as the fusion structure of
V(\widehat{\mathfrak{gl}(1|1)})
obtained in Section
3.4
, we have the following fusion structure for
\beta\gamma
VOA:

Corollary 4.14
.

For any
\mathcal{M}\in{\mathcal{C}_{\beta\gamma}}
, denote by
{\mathcal{M}}_{n}
the spectral flow
\sigma^{n}(\mathcal{M})
. We have the following fusion rules for
\beta\gamma
modules:

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{n}^{t}\times\mathcal{P}_{m}^{s}\cong\bigoplus_{l=0}^{\min\{t,s\}-1}P_{n+m-1}^{s+t-1-2l}\oplus 2P_{n+m}^{s+t-1-2l}\oplus P_{n+m+1}^{s+t-1-2l},

(4.39)

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}_{[\lambda],n}^{t}\times\mathcal{W}_{[\mu],m}^{s}\cong\bigoplus_{l=0}^{\min\{t,s\}-1}\mathcal{W}_{[\lambda+\mu],n+m}^{t+s-1-2l}\oplus\mathcal{W}_{[\lambda+\mu],n+m-1}^{t+s-1-2l},\text{if }\lambda+\mu\notin\mathbb{Z},

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}_{[\lambda],n}^{t}\times\mathcal{W}_{[-\lambda],m}^{s}\cong\mathcal{W}_{+,n}^{t}\times\mathcal{W}_{-,m}^{s}\cong\bigoplus_{l=0}^{\min\{t,s\}-1}\mathcal{P}_{m+n-1}^{t+s-1-2l}

5
Quiver algebra and quantum group

In this section, we will focus on the subcategory of atypical modules
{\mathcal{C}}_{\beta\gamma,[0]}
. In Section
5.1
, we will compute the endomorphism of the identity line operator, and compare the result with the computation in the smaller category studied in
[
AW20
]
. In Section
5.2
, we show that
{\mathcal{C}}_{\beta\gamma,[0]}
can be described as the category of modules of a quiver algebra, and show that this quiver algebra can be related to the quantum group
\overline{U}_{q}^{H}(\mathfrak{sl}(2))
.

5.1
The category of atypical modules

Recall the following decomposition in equation (
2.18
):

{\mathcal{C}_{\beta\gamma}}=\bigoplus_{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{Z}}\mathcal{C}_{{\beta\gamma},\lambda}.

(5.1)

When
\lambda=[0]
, the full subcategory
{\mathcal{C}}_{\beta\gamma,[0]}
is called the category of atypical modules. This is a tensor subcategory of
{\mathcal{C}_{\beta\gamma}}
, and the tensor identity of
{\mathcal{C}_{\beta\gamma}}
, which is
\mathbbm{1}={\mathcal{V}}=V_{\beta\gamma}
, lies in
{\mathcal{C}}_{\beta\gamma,[0]}
.

On the other hand, by Theorem
1.1
, there are equivalences of abelian categories:

{\mathcal{C}}_{\beta\gamma,[0]}\cong KL_{0}^{N+E/2}/\mathbb{Z}_{2}\cong{\mathcal{C}}_{0}^{N+E/2}/\mathbb{Z}_{2},

(5.2)

where recall that
{\mathcal{C}}_{0}^{N+E/2}
is the category of finite-dimensional modules of
\mathfrak{gl}(1|1)
such that
N+E/2
acts semi-simply with integer eigenvalues and
E
acts nilpotently. Now consider the category
{\mathcal{C}}_{0}^{N+E/2}
. In the following, we will view the action of
N+E/2
as a
\mathbb{C}^{*}
grading on the objects of
{\mathcal{C}}_{0}^{N+E/2}
. Denote by
A
the algebra over
\mathbb{C}
generated by
\psi^{\pm}
. The only relations the two generators satisfy are
(\psi^{+})^{2}=(\psi^{-})^{2}=0
. The algebra
A
can be viewed as a super algebra if we view
\psi^{\pm}
as odd elements. The adjoint action of
N+E/2
gives a
\mathbb{C}^{*}
grading under which
\psi^{\pm}
have weight
\pm 1
. Denote by
A-\mathrm{Mod}^{\mathbb{C}^{*}}
the category of finite-dimensional
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-equivariant modules of the (non-super) algebra
A
, then it is clear by definition, that we have the following equivalence of abelian categories:

{\mathcal{C}}^{N+E/2}/\mathbb{Z}_{2}\cong A-\mathrm{Mod}^{\mathbb{C}^{*}}.

(5.3)

Indeed, the supercategory
{\mathcal{C}}^{N+E/2}
is equivalent to the supercategory of
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-equivariant, finite-dimensional modules of the super algebra
A
. Since the superalgebra structure can be induced by the action of
\mathbb{C}^{*}
, the de-equivariantization simply forgets the superalgebra structure. In other words, a
\mathbb{C}^{*}
equivariant module automatically has a compatible
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
grading. From the above two equations we conclude:

{\mathcal{C}}_{\beta\gamma,[0]}\cong A-\mathrm{Mod}^{\mathbb{C}^{*}}_{nil}.

(5.4)

Here
A-\mathrm{Mod}^{\mathbb{C}^{*}}_{nil}
is the subcategory of
A-\mathrm{Mod}^{\mathbb{C}^{*}}
where
E
acts nilpotently. Under this isomorphism, the identity object
\mathbbm{1}
corresponds to the trivial representation
\mathbb{C}
of
A
, on which
\psi^{\pm}
acts trivially. We are now ready to compute:

Proposition 5.1
.

The derived endomorphism algebra of the identity line in
{\mathcal{C}_{\beta\gamma}}
is trivial:

\mathrm{End}_{D^{b}{\mathcal{C}_{\beta\gamma}}}(\mathbbm{1})\cong\mathbb{C}.

(5.5)

Here
D^{b}{\mathcal{C}_{\beta\gamma}}
is the bounded derived category of the abelian category
{\mathcal{C}_{\beta\gamma}}
.

Proof.

From the above, we only need to show that:

\mathrm{End}_{D^{b}A-\mathrm{Mod}^{\mathbb{C}^{*}}}(\mathbb{C})\cong\mathbb{C}.

(5.6)

The algebra
A
is well-known to be a Koszul algebra, and its Koszul dual is
A^{!}=\mathbb{C}[x,y]/(xy)
, a commutative algebra of two variables
x,y
satisfying
xy=0
. Thus, the derived endomorphism of
\mathbb{C}
as an ungraded
A
module is:

\mathrm{End}_{D^{b}A-\mathrm{Mod}}(\mathbb{C})\cong A^{!}=\mathbb{C}[x,y]/(xy).

(5.7)

Here we need to use a projective resolution of
\mathbb{C}
, which technically do not belong to
D^{b}A-\mathrm{Mod}
. This is not an issue for us since
D^{b}A-\mathrm{Mod}
is a full subcategory of the derived category of finitely-generated
A
modules. The
\mathbb{C}^{*}
action gives
x
and
y
weight
1
and
-1
respectively. Taking
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-invariants, we find:

\mathrm{End}_{D^{b}A-\mathrm{Mod}^{\mathbb{C}^{*}}}(\mathbb{C})\cong(A^{!})^{\mathbb{C}^{*}}=\mathbb{C}.

(5.8)

This completes the proof.
∎

We have finally proved the claim that
{\mathcal{C}_{\beta\gamma}}
produces the correct Coulomb branch! In contrast, the category of modules considered in
[
AW20
]
will not produce the correct answer for our setting. Indeed, the category considered in
[
AW20
]
can be obtained from
{\mathcal{C}_{\beta\gamma}}
by requiring
J_{0}
to act semi-simply. On the algebra
A
, this corresponds to a further restriction
E=0
. We are thus restricted to the Grassmann algebra of two variables
B=\mathbb{C}[\epsilon_{1},\epsilon_{2}]
. The Koszul dual is
B^{!}=\mathbb{C}[x,y]
, and we have:

\mathrm{End}_{D^{b}B-\mathrm{Mod}^{\mathbb{C}^{*}}}(\mathbb{C})\cong(B^{!})^{\mathbb{C}^{*}}=\mathbb{C}[xy].

(5.9)

The computation above produces a commutative algebra generated by a single variable
xy
, instead of the trivial algebra (
\mathbb{C}
) which we expect. The
xy
in equation (
5.9
) corresponds to a degree
2
extension of
V_{\beta\gamma}
by itself, in the form of the following exact sequence:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{V}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{W}}_{0}^{+}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{W}}_{0}^{-}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{V}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0.}

(5.10)

This is trivialized in our category
{\mathcal{C}_{\beta\gamma}}
due to the existence of the indecomposable module with the following Loewy diagram:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{V}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sigma^{-1}{\mathcal{V}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{V}}.}

(5.11)

This is similar to the discussion in
[
CCG19
]
, where the authors showed that the choice of the category changes the resulting endomorphism algebra.

5.2
A quiver description

5.2.1
Constructing the quiver

From the mirror symmetry statement, we have an equivalence

\mathcal{C}_{\beta\gamma,[0]}\boxtimes\mathrm{SVect}\cong KL^{N+E/2}_{0}\cong\mathcal{C}^{N+E/2}_{0}.

(5.12)

Let us analyze the category
\mathcal{C}^{N+E/2}_{0}
. Since the action of
N+E/2
is semi-simple, let
e_{n}
be the operator of projection onto the eigenspace with eigenvalue
n\in\mathbb{Z}
. Let
\sigma_{n}=\psi^{+}\circ e_{n}
and
\tau_{n}=\psi^{-}\circ e_{n}
, it is clear then
\psi^{2}=0
now becomes
\sigma_{n}\sigma_{n-1}=\tau_{n}\tau_{n+1}=0
. These
e_{n},\sigma_{n}
and
\tau_{n}
is the path algebra of the following quiver:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cdots}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces e_{n-1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces e_{n}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces e_{n+1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cdots}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sigma_{n-1}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\tau_{n}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sigma_{n}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\tau_{n+1}}

(5.13)

quotient by the quadratic relation
\sigma_{n}\sigma_{n-1}=\tau_{n}\tau_{n+1}=0
. Let this algebra be denoted by
\Lambda
. It is graded once we give
\sigma_{n}
and
\tau_{n}
degree
1
. From the above description, the supercategory
\mathcal{C}^{N+E/2}_{0}
is equivalent to the supercategory of graded finite-dimensional unitary modules of
\Lambda
. To get an ordinary category instead of a supercategory, we only need to choose a grading for each module. We say that a vector is even if it’s in the image of
e_{n}
for
n
even. Once we do this, we find an equivalence between
\mathcal{C}_{\beta\gamma,[0]}
with the category of finite-dimensional unitary modules of
\Lambda
(as an ungraded algebra) on which
\sigma_{n}\tau_{n+1}
and
\tau_{n}\sigma_{n-1}
act nilpotently for all
n
. The last condition comes from that
E
acts nilpotently for objects in
\mathcal{C}_{0}
. This category we denote by
\Lambda-\mathrm{mod}_{nil}\cong\mathcal{C}_{\beta\gamma,[0]}
. The interesting thing about this category is that it has three in principle distinct braided tensor category structure. One of them comes from the identification with objects in
\mathcal{C}^{N+E/2}_{0}
, another from the identification with objects in
{\mathcal{C}}_{\beta\gamma,[0]}
. The third one is rather surprising, as it comes from a morphism from
\overline{U}_{q}^{H}(\mathfrak{sl}(2))
.

5.2.2
Relation to quantum group

Consider the unrolled restricted quantum group
\overline{U}_{q}^{H}(\mathfrak{sl}(2))
at the fourth root of unity
q=i
. By this, we mean the algebra generated by
E,F,H,K^{\pm}
with the relation:

\displaystyle KK^{-1}=K^{-1}K=1,~~KE=-EK,~~KF=-FK,

(5.14)

\displaystyle[H,E]=2E,~~[H,F]=-2F,~~[E,F]=\frac{K-K^{-1}}{2i},~~E^{2}=F^{2}=0.

When considering modules, we also consider modules that satisfies relation
K=q^{H}=e^{\pi iH/2}
, although this is not a well-defined relation in the algebra itself.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
\sigma=\sum_{n}\sigma_{n}
,
\tau=\sum_{n}\tau_{n}
and
L=\sigma\tau+\tau\sigma
. Let
f(x)
be the Taylor series of the function
\frac{1-e^{-\pi ix}}{x}
. The assignment:

H\mapsto\left(\sum_{n}2ne_{n}\right)+L,~~K\mapsto e^{\frac{\pi i}{2}H},~~E\mapsto\sigma,~~F\mapsto\frac{\tau}{2i}f(L)K

(5.15)

gives a well-defined action of
\overline{U}_{q}^{H}(\mathfrak{sl}(2))
on finite-dimensional unitary modules of
\Lambda
, and satisfies
K=q^{H}
.

Proof.

These give well-defined operators on any finite-dimensional unitary module of
\Lambda
, so we only need to show that this gives the right commutation relation, which is a simple algebraic check. We only show the relation
[E,F]=\frac{K-K^{-1}}{2i}
. We have that:

[\sigma,\frac{\tau}{2i}f(L)K]=\frac{1}{2i}\{\sigma,\tau\}f(L)K=\frac{1}{2i}Lf(L)K.

(5.16)

By definition,
Lf(L)=1-e^{-\pi iL}=1-e^{-\pi iH}=1-K^{-2}
, and so
Lf(L)K=K-K^{-1}
, thus the relation.

∎

Remark.
It can be shown that when restricted to the category of atypical modules of
\overline{U}_{q}^{H}(\mathfrak{sl}(2))
, this gives an equivalence to
\Lambda-\mathrm{mod}_{nil}
, with a well-defined inverse assignment. This assignment should be, in some sense, similar to the induction functor.

It turns out, that one can transfer the structure of the braided tensor category from
\overline{U}_{q}^{H}(\mathfrak{sl}(2))
to
\Lambda-\mathrm{mod}
. For example, the co-product
\Delta_{t}
(t for twisted) is given by:

\displaystyle\Delta_{t}(e_{n})=\sum_{p+q=n}e_{p}\otimes e_{q},~~\Delta_{t}(\sigma)=\sigma\otimes 1+e^{\frac{\pi i}{2}H}\otimes\sigma,~~\Delta_{t}(L)=L\otimes 1+1\otimes L

(5.17)

\displaystyle\Delta_{t}(\tau)=2i(1\otimes F+F\otimes e^{-\frac{\pi i}{2}H})(e^{-\frac{\pi i}{2}H}\otimes e^{-\frac{\pi i}{2}H})\frac{1}{f(L)}(L\otimes 1+1\otimes L)

where
\frac{1}{f(x)}
should be understood as the Taylor series of the quotient. This is very asymmetric compared to the coproduct
\Delta
from
{\mathfrak{gl}(1|1)}
:

\Delta(e_{n})=\sum_{p+q=n}e_{p}\otimes e_{q},~~\Delta(\sigma)=\sigma\otimes 1+e^{\pi i\sum_{n}ne_{n}}\otimes\sigma,~~\Delta(\tau)=\tau\otimes 1+e^{\pi i\sum_{n}ne_{n}}\otimes\tau.

(5.18)

Under the co-product
\Delta
, the braiding is trivial, but the braiding under
\Delta_{t}
is non-trivial:

R=e^{\pi iH\otimes H/4}(1+2iE\otimes F),

(5.19)

and so there is a non-trivial twist:

\theta=K(e^{-\pi iH^{2}/4}-2iKFe^{-\pi iH^{2}/4}E),

(5.20)

where
H,E,F
should be expressed using elements in
\Lambda
as in equation (
5.15
). These formulas can be found in
[
Oht02
]
. We expect that for any
M,N\in\Lambda-\mathrm{mod}
, the two co-product
M\otimes N
and
M\otimes_{t}N
are isomorphic as
\Lambda
modules. However, we do not expect the two coproducts to give equivalent braided tensor structures. It remains a question whether the tensor structure
\Delta_{t}
induced from
\overline{U}_{q}^{H}(\mathfrak{sl}(2))
is equivalent to that coming from
{\mathcal{C}_{\beta\gamma}}
.

There is no complete numerical invariant for smooth conjugacy of circle diffeomorphisms
Philipp Kunde

Abstract
Classical results by Poincaré and Denjoy show that two orientation-preserving C 2 C^{2} diffeomorphisms of the circle are topologically conjugate if and only if they have the same rotation number. We show that there is no possibility of getting such a complete numerical Borel invariant for the conjugacy relation of orientation-preserving circle diffeomorphisms by homeomorphisms with higher degree of regularity. For instance, we consider conjugacy by Hölder homeomorphisms or by C k C^{k} -diffeomorphisms with k ∈ ℤ + ∪ { ∞ } k\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}\cup\{\infty\} . The proof combines techniques from Descriptive Set Theory and a quantitative version of the Approximation by Conjugation method for circle diffeomorphisms.

Introduction

A fundamental theme in dynamics is the classification of systems up to appropriate equivalence relations. For instance, the equivalence relation of
topological conjugacy
preserves the qualitative behavior of topological dynamical systems. Here, two continuous maps
T:X\to X
and
S:Y\to Y
of compact metric spaces
X,Y
are called topological conjugate (or topologically equivalent) if there is a homeomorphism
h:X\to Y
such that
S=h\circ T\circ h^{-1}
. Smale’s celebrated program proposes to classify topological or smooth dynamical systems up to topological conjugacy.

For the purpose of classification, one seeks
dynamical invariants
that are easy to compute and help determine whether two systems can be equivalent to each other. In the best case scenario, these invariants can be expressed as a single number. While one often can find invariants that are preserved under equivalence, even within specific classes of dynamical systems it turns out to be very hard to find
complete invariants
, that is, invariants that agree for two systems if and only if the systems are equivalent to each other. Indeed, having complete numerical invariants is one of the key notions of classifiability.

One well-studied numerical invariant is the
rotation number

\tau(f)
for an orientation-preserving circle homeomorphism
f
(see Section
2.1
for its definition). We denote the collection of orientation-preserving homeomorphisms of the circle by
\mathcal{H}
. Then the rotation number is an invariant for the equivalence relation on
\mathcal{H}
of conjugacy by an orientation-preserving homeomorphism. In the converse direction, Poincaré showed in 1885 that any
f\in\mathcal{H}
with an irrational rotation number
\tau(f)
and a dense orbit is topologically conjugate to
R_{\tau(f)}
(see
[
HK95
, Theorem 11.2.7]
). Thus, homeomorphisms of the circle with a dense orbit present a success story of a classification by a numerical invariant. Furthermore, Denjoy showed that
C^{1}
circle diffeomorphisms with irrational rotation number and derivative of bounded variation have a dense orbit and, hence, are completely classified by their rotation number (see
[
De32
]
or
[
HK95
, Theorem 12.1.1]
).

In general, the interplay between descriptive set theory and dynamical systems has been fruitful to establish several
anti-classification results
in dynamics which demonstrated in a rigorous way that classification is not possible. A pioneering work
[
Fe74
]
by J. Feldman showed that there cannot exist a complete numerical invariant for measure-theoretic isomorphism of ergodic transformations or even of
K

[
FRW11
]
, M. Foreman, D. Rudolph and B. Weiss showed that the measure-isomorphism relation for ergodic transformations is not a Borel set when viewed as a subset of
\mathcal{E}\times\mathcal{E}
, where
\mathcal{E}
denotes the collection of ergodic transformations. Informally speaking, this result says that determining isomorphism between ergodic transformations is inaccessible to countable methods that use a countable amount of information. Recently, Foreman and Weiss
[
FW22
]
generalized the aforementioned anti-classification result from
[
FRW11
]
to the
C^{\infty}
category by proving that the measure-isomorphism relation among pairs of volume-preserving ergodic
C^{\infty}
-diffeomorphisms of on compact surfaces admitting a non-trivial circle action is not a Borel set with respect to the
C^{\infty}
-topology. In joint work with S. Banerjee we are even able to show this anti-classification result for real-analytic diffeomorphisms of the
2
-torus
[
BKpp
]
. Hence, von Neumann’s classification problem for the isomorphism relation in ergodic theory is impossible even when restricting to real-analytic diffeomorphisms of
\mathbb{T}^{2}
. M. Gerber and the author obtained analogous results for the Kakutani equivalence relation
[
GKpp
]
. Recently, Foreman and A. Gorodetski proved anti-classification results in the context of topological equivalence. For any compact manifold
M
of dimension at least
2
, Foreman and Gorodetski showed that there is no Borel function from the
C^{\infty}
diffeomorphisms to the reals
that is a complete invariant for topological equivalence
[
FGpp
, Theorem 2]
. Moreover, if dim
(M)\geq 5
, they proved that the set of topologically equivalent pairs of diffeomorphisms is not Borel
[
FGpp
, Theorem 1]
. We refer to the survey article
[
Fopp
]
by Foreman for an overview of complexity results of structure and classification of dynamical systems. Problem 1 in
[
Fopp
]
asks if there are complete numerical invariants for orientation-preserving diffeomorphisms of the circle up to conjugation by orientation-preserving diffeomorphisms. Our Main Theorem
A
answers this question in the negative.

The conjugacy in Theorem
A
could be either a singular or an absolutely continuous map. In Section
5.1
we show that both cases can be realized in the setting of our Main Theorem
A
. In Section
6
we obtain another generalization of our Main Theorem
A
to the setting of higher rank actions: we show that there is no complete numerical Borel invariant for
\mathcal{D}
-conjugacy of free
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
actions by orientation-preserving
C^{\infty}
diffeomorphisms of the circle.

Our Main Theorem
A
does not rule out a classification along the lines of the Halmos–von Neumann
classification of ergodic transformations with discrete spectrum by countable subgroups of the unit circle. To exclude such a classification, it suffices to show that the respective conjugacy relation is not reducible to an
S_{\infty}
-action. Hjorth devised the concept of
turbulence
as a mechanism for showing that an equivalence relation is not reducible to an
S_{\infty}
-action (see
[
Fopp
, section 5.5]
for details).

Question
.

Is the smooth conjugacy relation of smooth circle diffeomorphisms turbulent?

As mentioned before, the Borel/non-Borel distinction is an important benchmark for classification problems. Hence, it is a natural question if the smooth conjugacy relation of smooth circle diffeomorphisms is Borel or not.

Question
.

Is the smooth conjugacy relation of smooth circle diffeomorphisms complete analytic?

Preliminaries

2.1
Homeomorphisms of the circle

Let
\mathbb{S}^{1}=\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}
be the unit circle with rotations
R_{\alpha}:\mathbb{S}^{1}\to\mathbb{S}^{1}
,
R_{\alpha}(x)=x+\alpha\mod 1
. We denote the collection of orientation-preserving homeomorphisms of
\mathbb{S}^{1}
by
\mathcal{H}
. For
x\in\mathbb{R}
we write
[x]=\pi(x)\in\mathbb{S}^{1}
, where
\pi:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{S}^{1}
is the quotient map. Hereby, we can define a
lift
of
f\in\mathcal{H}
as an increasing continuous function
F:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
with
f\circ\pi=\pi\circ F
, that is,
[F(x)]=f([x])
. Using this terminology we can introduce the rotation number.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
f\in\mathcal{H}
and
F
be a lift of
f
. Define

\tau(F)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{F^{n}(x)-x}{n}.

Then
\tau(f):=[\tau(F)]
is called the
rotation number
of
f
.

The definition is justified since
\tau(F)
exists for all
x\in\mathbb{R}
, is independent of
x
, and we have
[\tau(F_{1})]=[\tau(F_{2})]
for all lifts
F_{1},F_{2}
of
f

[
HK95
, Proposition 11.1.1]
. Furthermore, one can show that the rotation number is an invariant for conjugacy by orientation-preserving homeomorphisms of the circle, that is,
\tau(f)=\tau(hfh^{-1})
for all
h\in\mathcal{H}

[
HK95
, Proposition 11.1.3]
. We also note that
\tau(R_{\alpha})=\alpha
. Hence, the question of the rotation number being a complete numerical invariant can be restated as asking whether
\tau(f)=\alpha
implies that
f
is conjugate to
R_{\alpha}
. If
f\in\mathcal{H}
is topologically conjugate to an irrational rotation, then the conjugacy satisfies the following uniqueness property.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
f\in\mathcal{H}
be topologically conjugate to an irrational rotation. Then the conjugating homeomorphism
h\in\mathcal{H}
is unique up to a rotation, that is, if
h_{i}\circ f=R_{\tau(f)}\circ h_{i}
for
i=1,2
, then
h_{1}\circ h^{-1}_{2}
is a rotation.

In particular, there is a unique conjugating homeomorphism
h\in\mathcal{H}
satisfying
h(0)=0
.

This lemma also shows that the regularity of the conjugacy to the irrational rotation is uniquely determined.

Proof.

For the reader’s convenience we provide a proof of this folklore result (see also
[
He79
, Proposition 3.3.3]
). We start with the following observation.

Claim:
If a circle homeomorphism
h
commutes with an irrational rotation
R_{\alpha}
, then
h
is a rotation.

Proof:
From
h\circ R_{\alpha}=R_{\alpha}\circ h
we also get
h\circ R^{n}_{\alpha}=R^{n}_{\alpha}\circ h
for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, that is,

h(x+n\alpha)=h(x)+n\alpha\ \text{ for all $x\in\mathbb{S}^{1}$ and $n\in\mathbb{Z}$}.

Since
\alpha
is irrational, the orbit
\left\{n\alpha\mathrel{}\middle|\mathrel{}n\in\mathbb{Z}\right\}
lies dense in
\mathbb{S}^{1}
. Hence, we obtain

h(x+y)=h(x)+y\ \text{ for all $x,y\in\mathbb{S}^{1}$}

using continuity of
h
. In particular, taking
x=0
yields

h(y)=y+h(0)\ \text{ for all $y\in\mathbb{S}^{1}$},

that is,
h
is a rotation by
h(0)
. ∎

In the next step, we suppose that

h^{-1}_{1}\circ R_{\tau(f)}\circ h_{1}=f=h^{-1}_{2}\circ R_{\tau(f)}\circ h_{2}.

Hence,
h_{1}\circ h^{-1}_{2}
commutes with
R_{\tau(f)}
. Since
\tau(f)
is irrational by assumption of the lemma, we can apply the aforementioned Claim and conclude that
h_{1}\circ h^{-1}_{2}
is a rotation.
∎

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
S,T\in\mathcal{H}
be topologically conjugate to an irrational rotation
R_{\alpha}
. Then there is a unique

with

and

.

Proof.

We consider any

satisfying
g_{1}(0)=0=g_{2}(0)
and

g_{1}\circ S\circ g^{-1}_{1}=T=g_{2}\circ S\circ g^{-1}_{2}.

Furthermore, let

be a conjugacy between

and
R_{\alpha}
. Then we obtain

h\circ g_{1}\circ S\circ(h\circ g_{1})^{-1}=h\circ T\circ h^{-1}=R_{\alpha}=h\circ g_{2}\circ S\circ(h\circ g_{2})^{-1}.

By Lemma
2.2
we have

h\circ g_{1}\circ(h\circ g_{2})^{-1}=R_{\beta}

for some
\beta\in\mathbb{S}^{1}
. Hence,

g_{1}\circ g^{-1}_{2}=h^{-1}\circ R_{\beta}\circ h.

(2.4)

Then the condition
g_{1}(0)=0=g_{2}(0)
implies
\beta=0
. Using
\beta=0
in equation (
2.4
) gives
g_{1}=g_{2}
.
∎

2.3
Some tools from Descriptive Set Theory

The main tool is the idea of a
reduction for equivalence relations. See
[
Fopp
]
and
[
Ga09
]
for further information.

Definition 2.7
.

Let
X
and
Y
be Polish spaces and
E\subseteq X\times X
,
F\subseteq Y\times Y
be equivalence relations.

A function
f:X\to Y

reduces

E
to
F

if and only if

for all
x_{1},x_{2}\in X
:
x_{1}Ex_{2}
if and only if
f(x_{1})Ff(x_{2})
.

Such a function
f
is called a Borel (respectively, continuous) reduction
if
f
is a Borel (respectively, continuous) function.

We use the notation
E\precsim_{\mathcal{B}}F
(respectively,
E\precsim_{C}F
).

E
being reducible to
F
can be interpreted as saying that
F

is at least as complicated as
E
.

In this work, we want to exclude the existence of complete numerical invariants. Thus, we consider the
equality equivalence relation

=_{Y}\subseteq Y\times Y
on a Polish space
Y
. Since for every Polish space
Y
there is a Borel injection
g:Y\to\mathbb{R}\setminus\mathbb{Q}
, we can extend a Borel reduction
f
of any equivalence relation
E\subseteq X\times X
to
(Y,=_{Y})
to a Borel reduction
g\circ f
of
(X,E)
to
(\mathbb{R},=_{R})
. Thus, we can assume that Borel reductions to any
=_{Y}
can be changed to Borel reductions to equality on the real numbers.

In the next step, we define the equivalence relation
E_{0}
which is an important tool to exclude the existence of complete numerical invariants.

Definition 2.8
.

Let
E_{0}
be the equivalence relation on
\{0,1\}^{{\mathbb{N}}}
defined by setting

(a_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}E_{0}(b_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}\text{ if and only if there is $N\in{\mathbb{N}}$ such that $a_{m}=b_{m}$ for all $m>N$.}

The significance of equivalence relation
E_{0}
for excluding complete numerical invariants comes from the so-called
Harrington-Kechris-Louveau dichotomy
(sometimes also refered to as Glimm-Effros dichotomy). It states that for any Borel equivalence relation
E
on a Polish space
X
exactly one of the following alternatives holds: either
E\precsim_{\mathcal{B}}=_{\mathbb{R}}
or
E_{0}\precsim_{\mathcal{B}}E
.

To exclude the existence of complete numerical invariants, we use the following direction from the Harrington-Kechris-Louveau dichotomy (see
[
Fopp
, Theorem 36]
or
[
Ga09
, Proposition 6.1.7]
).

Theorem 2.9
.

Suppose that
E
is an equivalence relation on an uncountable Polish space
X
and
E_{0}\precsim_{\mathcal{B}}E
. Then
E\not\precsim_{\mathcal{B}}=_{\mathbb{R}}
.

Proof.

For the reader’s convenience we include Feldman’s argument from
[
Fe74
]
.

Since
E_{0}\precsim_{\mathcal{B}}E
, there is a Borel map
\psi:\{0,1\}^{\mathbb{N}}\to X

such that for
(a_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}},(b_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\in\{0,1\}^{\mathbb{N}},

\psi\left((a_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\right)E\psi\left((b_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\right)\text{ if and only if }a_{n}=b_{n}\text{ for all but at most finitely many }n.

(2.10)

We want to show that there does not exist a complete numerical Borel invariant for
E
, that
is, there does not exist a Borel function
\varphi:X\to\mathbb{R}

such that
xEy
if and only if
\varphi(x)=\varphi(y)
.

Suppose such a Borel function
\varphi

exists. Then for
(a_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}},(b_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\in\{0,1\}^{\mathbb{N}}
,

\varphi\circ\psi\left((a_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\right)=\varphi\circ\psi\left((b_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\right)

if and only if
a_{n}=b_{n}
for all but at most finitely many
n
.
Thus, for any Borel set
B\subset\mathbb{R}
,
(\varphi\circ\psi)^{-1}(B)

is invariant under maps that consist of a permutation of finitely
many terms in the elements of
(\varphi\circ\psi)^{-1}(B)
. If we
endow
\{0,1\}^{\mathbb{N}}
with the
(\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2})
-Bernoulli measure, then by the Hewitt-Savage zero-one law, any such
set
(\varphi\circ\psi)^{-1}(B)
has measure
0
or
1
. Therefore

\varphi\circ\psi
is almost everywhere constant on
\{0,1\}^{\mathbb{N}}
.
Hence, there are uncountably many elements of
\{0,1\}^{\mathbb{N}}

that are mapped to the same element of
\mathbb{R}.
This contradicts
(
2.10
).
∎

a_{n}\neq b_{n}
, then the
C^{\infty}
-diffeomorphisms
\Psi(\mathbf{a})
and
\Psi(\mathbf{b})
are not Hölder-conjugate.

Using the notions from descriptive set theory in Section
2.3
, Proposition
3.1
implies the following statement.

Corollary 3.2
.

Let
\alpha\in\mathbb{S}^{1}
be a Liouville number and
\mathcal{D}
be the collection of orientation-preserving circle homeomorphisms with regularity
(D)
, where
(D)
could be any degree of regularity from Hölder to
C^{\infty}
. Then there is a continuous reduction from
E_{0}
to the
\mathcal{D}
-conjugacy relation of diffeomorphisms in
\mathcal{H}^{\infty}_{\alpha}
.

Proof.

If
\mathbf{a}E_{0}\mathbf{b}
, then the
C^{\infty}
-diffeomorphisms
\Psi(\mathbf{a})
and
\Psi(\mathbf{b})
are
C^{\infty}
-conjugated by part (1) of Proposition
3.1
. Hence, they are
\mathcal{D}
-conjugate.

If
\mathbf{a}
and
\mathbf{b}
are not equivalent in
E_{0}
, then there are infinitely many
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
with
a_{n}\neq b_{n}
. Thus, the
C^{\infty}
-diffeomorphisms
\Psi(\mathbf{a})
and
\Psi(\mathbf{b})
are not Hölder-conjugate by part (2) of Proposition
3.1
and, hence, they are not
\mathcal{D}
-conjugate.

Altogether, the
C^{\infty}
-diffeomorphisms
\Psi(\mathbf{a})
and
\Psi(\mathbf{b})
are
\mathcal{D}
-conjugate if and only if
\mathbf{a}E_{0}\mathbf{b}
. This shows that the continuous map
\Psi:\{0,1\}^{{\mathbb{N}}}\to\mathcal{H}^{\infty}_{\alpha}
is a reduction from
E_{0}
to the
\mathcal{D}
-conjugacy relation of orientation-preserving
C^{\infty}
-diffeomorphisms of the circle.
∎

Then we apply Theorem
2.9
to conclude the following result which in particular implies our Main Theorem
A
.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
\alpha\in\mathbb{S}^{1}
be a Liouville number and
\mathcal{D}
be the collection of orientation-preserving circle homeomorphisms with regularity
(D)
, where
(D)
could be any degree of regularity from Hölder to
C^{\infty}
. Then there is no complete numerical Borel invariant for the
\mathcal{D}
-conjugacy relation of diffeomorphisms in
\mathcal{H}^{\infty}_{\alpha}
.

4
Construction of the reduction

Let
\alpha\in\mathbb{S}^{1}
be a Liouville number. For every sequence
\mathbf{a}=(a_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
we construct a diffeomorphism
T_{\mathbf{a}}\in\mathcal{H}^{\infty}_{\alpha}
by an inductive construction process as the limit of a sequence
(T_{\mathbf{a},n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}
of
C^{\infty}
-diffeomorphisms of the circle defined by

T_{\mathbf{a},n}=H_{\mathbf{a},n}\circ R_{\alpha_{n+1}}\circ H^{-1}_{\mathbf{a},n}\text{ for }n\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}.

(4.1)

Here, we take
H_{\mathbf{a},0}=\operatorname{id}
and for
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
we successively construct rational numbers
\alpha_{n}=\frac{p_{n}}{q_{n}}
as well as conjugation maps
H_{\mathbf{a},n}=H_{\mathbf{a},n-1}\circ h_{\mathbf{a},n}
with a
C^{\infty}
-diffeomorphism
h_{\mathbf{a},n}
satisfying

h_{\mathbf{a},n}\circ R_{1/q_{n}}=R_{1/q_{n}}\circ h_{\mathbf{a},n}.

(4.2)

In Subsection
4.1
we present the construction of our conjugation map
h_{\mathbf{a},n}
which depends on entry
a_{n}
in the sequence
\mathbf{a}=(a_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
. This map will allow explicit norm estimates that we use in Subsection
4.2
to prove convergence of the sequence
(T_{\mathbf{a},n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
in the space
\mathcal{H}^{\infty}_{\alpha}
of orientation-preserving
C^{\infty}
-diffeomorphisms with the prescribed rotation number
\alpha
.

4.1
Construction of conjugation maps

We use a
C^{\infty}
-function
\psi:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow[0,1]
satisfying
\psi\left((-\infty,0]\right)={0}
,
\psi\left([1,\infty)\right)={1}
and
\psi
is strictly monotone increasing on
[0,1]
. For any
t\in(0,1/4)
we define a strictly increasing
C^{\infty}
diffeomorphism
\hat{h}_{t}:[0,1]\to[0,1]
as follows:
\hat{h}_{t}\left(x\right)=

\begin{cases}x&\textit{ if }x\in\left[0,t\right],\\
\left(1-\psi(t^{-1}(x-t))\right)\cdot x+\psi(t^{-1}(x-t))\cdot\left(t\cdot\left(x-\frac{1}{4}\right)+3t\right)&\textit{ if }x\in\left[t,2t\right],\\
t\cdot\left(x-\frac{1}{4}\right)+3t&\textit{ if }x\in\left[2t,\frac{1-t+8t^{2}}{2}\right],\\
\left(1-\psi(t^{-2}(x-\frac{1-t+8t^{2}}{2}))\right)\cdot\left(t\left(x-\frac{1}{4}\right)+3t\right)&\\
+\psi(t^{-2}(x-\frac{1-t+8t^{2}}{2}))\cdot\left(t^{-1}\left(x-\frac{1}{2}\right)+\frac{1}{2}\right)&\textit{ if }x\in\left[\frac{1-t+8t^{2}}{2},\frac{1-t+10t^{2}}{2}\right],\\
t^{-1}\cdot\left(x-\frac{1}{2}\right)+\frac{1}{2}&\textit{ if }x\in\left[\frac{1-t+10t^{2}}{2},\frac{1+t-10t^{2}}{2}\right],\\
\left(1-\psi(t^{-2}(x-\frac{1+t-10t^{2}}{2}))\right)\cdot\left(t^{-1}\left(x-\frac{1}{2}\right)+\frac{1}{2}\right)&\\
+\psi(t^{-2}(x-\frac{1+t-10t^{2}}{2}))\cdot\left(t\left(x-\frac{3}{4}\right)+1-3t\right)&\textit{ if }x\in\left[\frac{1+t-10t^{2}}{2},\frac{1+t-8t^{2}}{2}\right],\\
t\cdot\left(x-\frac{3}{4}\right)+1-3t&\textit{ if }x\in\left[\frac{1+t-8t^{2}}{2},1-2t\right],\\
\left(1-\psi(t^{-1}(x-1+2t))\right)\cdot\left(t\left(x-\frac{3}{4}\right)+1-3t\right)+\psi(t^{-1}(x-1+2t))x&\textit{ if }x\in\left[1-2t,1-t\right],\\
x&\textit{ if }x\in\left[1-t,1\right].\end{cases}

In particular,
\hat{h}_{t}
coincides with the identity in the neighborhood of the boundary. See Figure
1
for a visualisation of such a map.

Figure 1:
Qualitative shape of the function
\hat{h}_{t}

We use this explicit form of the map
\hat{h}_{t}
to obtain norm estimates as a power of
q_{n}
in (
4.6
). We also note that
\hat{h}^{2}_{t}
has slope
t^{-2}
on

\hat{I}_{t}\coloneqq\left[\frac{1}{2}-\frac{t^{2}}{4},\frac{1}{2}+\frac{t^{2}}{4}\right].

(4.3)

Since
\hat{h}_{t}
is a strictly increasing
C^{\infty}
function, it has a strictly increasing smooth inverse function satisfying

\hat{h}^{-1}_{t}\left(x\right)=\begin{cases}x&\textit{ if }x\in\left[0,t\right],\\
t^{-1}\cdot\left(x-3t\right)+\frac{1}{4}&\textit{ if }x\in\left[3t-\frac{t-8t^{2}}{4},3t+\frac{t-2t^{2}}{4}\right],\\
t\cdot\left(x-\frac{1}{2}\right)+\frac{1}{2}&\textit{ if }x\in\left[5t,1-5t\right],\\
t^{-1}\cdot\left(x-1+3t\right)+\frac{3}{4}&\textit{ if }x\in\left[1-3t-\frac{t-2t^{2}}{4},1-3t+\frac{t-8t^{2}}{4}\right],\\
x&\textit{ if }x\in\left[1-t,1\right].\end{cases}

Hence,
\hat{h}^{-2}_{t}
has slope
t^{-2}
on

\hat{J}_{t}\coloneqq\left[3t-\frac{t}{4}+3t^{2}-\frac{t^{2}}{8},3t-\frac{t}{4}+3t^{2}+\frac{t^{2}}{8}\right]\cup\left[1-3t+\frac{t}{4}-3t^{2}-\frac{t^{2}}{8},1-3t+\frac{t}{4}-3t^{2}+\frac{t^{2}}{8}\right].

(4.4)

For any
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
we take

t_{n}=q^{1-2n}_{n}

(4.5)

and define
\tilde{h}_{n}=\hat{h}_{t_{n}}
. Let
h_{q_{n}}
be the lift of
\tilde{h}_{n}
by the cyclic
q_{n}
-fold covering map
\pi_{q_{n}}
such that
\operatorname{Fix}(h_{q_{n}})\neq\emptyset
. Finally, we define

h_{\mathbf{a},n}=\begin{cases}h_{q_{n}}&\text{ if }a_{n}=0,\\
h^{-1}_{q_{n}}&\text{ if }a_{n}=1.\end{cases}

Clearly, the commutativity condition (
4.2
) is satisfied. We also observe for any
r\in{\mathbb{N}}
that

{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|h_{\mathbf{a},n}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{r}\leq C_{n,r}\cdot q^{N(n,r)}_{n}

(4.6)

with some positive integer
N(n,r)
and some constant
C_{n,r}
that are independent of
q_{n}
and
\mathbf{a}\in\{0,1\}^{{\mathbb{N}}}
.
With the aid of this estimate and a generalized chain rule we are able to prove an estimate on the norms of
H_{\mathbf{a},n}=H_{\mathbf{a},n-1}\circ h_{\mathbf{a},n}
.

Lemma 4.7
.

For every
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
we have

{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|H_{\mathbf{a},n}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{k}\leq C(n,H_{\mathbf{a},n-1},k)\cdot q^{k\cdot N(n,k)}_{n},

where
C(n,H_{\mathbf{a},n-1},k)
is a constant depending solely on
n
,
H_{\mathbf{a},n-1}
, and
k
. Since
H_{\mathbf{a},n-1}
is independent of

q_{n}
in particular, the same is true for
C(n,H_{\mathbf{a},n-1},k)
.

Proof.

By the Faà di Bruno’s formula we get

\left\lVert D^{k}\left(H_{\mathbf{a},n-1}\circ h_{\mathbf{a},n}\right)\right\rVert\leq C(k)\cdot\left\lVert H_{\mathbf{a},n-1}\right\rVert_{k}\cdot\left\lVert h_{\mathbf{a},n}\right\rVert^{k}_{k}

and

\left\lVert D^{k}\left(h^{-1}_{\mathbf{a},n}\circ H^{-1}_{\mathbf{a},n-1}\right)\right\rVert\leq C(k)\cdot\left\lVert h^{-1}_{\mathbf{a},n}\right\rVert_{k}\cdot\left\lVert H^{-1}_{\mathbf{a},n-1}\right\rVert^{k}_{k}

with a constant
C(k)
solely depending on
k
. Using (
4.6
) these estimates imply

{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|H_{\mathbf{a},n}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{k}\leq C(k)\cdot{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|H_{\mathbf{a},n-1}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{k}_{k}\cdot{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|h_{\mathbf{a},n}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{k}_{k}\leq C(n,H_{\mathbf{a},n-1},k)\cdot q^{kN(n,k)}_{n},

where
C(n,H_{\mathbf{a},n-1},k)
is a constant depending solely on
n
,
H_{\mathbf{a},n-1}
, and
k
.
∎

4.2
Convergence of the sequence
(T_{\mathbf{a},n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}

We start our proof of convergence of the sequence
(T_{\mathbf{a},n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
in
\mathcal{H}^{\infty}_{\alpha}
by stating the following lemma based upon the mean value theorem and the chain rule.

Lemma 4.8

(
[
FS05
]
, Lemma 5.6)
.

Let
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
h
be a
C^{\infty}
-diffeomorphism. Then we get for every
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{R}
:

d_{k}\left(h\circ R_{\alpha}\circ h^{-1},h\circ R_{\beta}\circ h^{-1}\right)\leq C_{k}\cdot{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|h\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{k+1}_{k+1}\cdot\left|\alpha-\beta\right|,

where the constant
C_{k}
depends solely on
k
. In particular
C_{0}=1
.

Since
\alpha
is a Liouville number, the proof of convergence of
(T_{\mathbf{a},n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
in
\mathcal{H}^{\infty}_{\alpha}
is similar to the proof of the general criterion in
[
FS05
, Lemma 5.7]
. We additionally ensure that the same sequence
(\alpha_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
yields convergence for all
\mathbf{a}=(a_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}\in\{0,1\}^{{\mathbb{N}}}
. This is used in the third part of the lemma which will guarantee continuity of our reduction
\Psi
.

Lemma 4.9
.

There is a sequence
(\alpha_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
of rational numbers
\alpha_{n}=\frac{p_{n}}{q_{n}}
with

q_{n+1}\geq q^{2n}_{n}\ \text{ for every }n\in{\mathbb{N}}

(4.10)

such that the following properties hold:

1.

For every sequence
\mathbf{a}\in\{0,1\}^{{\mathbb{N}}}
the sequence of diffeomorphisms
(T_{\mathbf{a},n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
defined by (
4.1
) converges in the
\text{Diff}^{\infty}
-topology to a smooth diffeomorphism
T_{\mathbf{a}}
.

2.

Also the sequence of diffeomorphisms
\hat{T}_{\mathbf{a},n}=H_{\mathbf{a},n}\circ R_{\alpha}\circ H^{-1}_{\mathbf{a},n}\in\mathcal{H}^{\infty}_{\alpha}
converges to
T_{\mathbf{a}}
in the
\text{Diff}^{\infty}
-topology. Hence,
T_{\mathbf{a}}\in\mathcal{H}^{\infty}_{\alpha}
.

3.

For every
\varepsilon>0
there is
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that for all sequences
\mathbf{a}=(a_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}},\mathbf{b}=(b_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}\in\{0,1\}^{{\mathbb{N}}}
with
a_{n}=b_{n}
for all
n\leq N
we have

d_{\infty}(T_{\mathbf{a}},T_{\mathbf{b}})<\varepsilon.

Proof.

1.

According to condition (
4.2
) of our construction we have
h_{\mathbf{a},n}\circ R_{\alpha_{n}}=R_{\alpha_{n}}\circ h_{\mathbf{a},n}
and, hence,

\displaystyle T_{\mathbf{a},n-1}

\displaystyle=H_{\mathbf{a},n-1}\circ R_{\alpha_{n}}\circ H^{-1}_{\mathbf{a},n-1}=H_{\mathbf{a},n-1}\circ R_{\alpha_{n}}\circ h_{\mathbf{a},n}\circ h^{-1}_{\mathbf{a},n}\circ H^{-1}_{\mathbf{a},n-1}

\displaystyle=H_{\mathbf{a},n-1}\circ h_{\mathbf{a},n}\circ R_{\alpha_{n}}\circ h^{-1}_{\mathbf{a},n}\circ H^{-1}_{\mathbf{a},n-1}=H_{\mathbf{a},n}\circ R_{\alpha_{n}}\circ H^{-1}_{\mathbf{a},n}

for every sequence
\mathbf{a}\in\{0,1\}^{{\mathbb{N}}}
. Applying Lemma
4.8
we obtain for every
k,n\in\mathbb{N}
:

\begin{split}d_{k}\left(T_{\mathbf{a},n},T_{\mathbf{a},n-1}\right)&=d_{k}\left(H_{\mathbf{a},n}\circ R_{\alpha_{n+1}}\circ T^{-1}_{\mathbf{a},n},H_{\mathbf{a},n}\circ R_{\alpha_{n}}\circ H^{-1}_{\mathbf{a},n}\right)\\
&\leq C_{k}\cdot{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|H_{\mathbf{a},n}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{k+1}_{k+1}\cdot\left|\alpha_{n+1}-\alpha_{n}\right|.\end{split}

(4.11)

We assume
\left|\alpha-\alpha_{n}\right|\stackrel{{\scriptstyle n\rightarrow\infty}}{{\longrightarrow}}0
monotonically. Using the triangle inequality we obtain
\left|\alpha_{n+1}-\alpha_{n}\right|\leq\left|\alpha_{n+1}-\alpha\right|+\left|\alpha-\alpha_{n}\right|\leq 2\cdot\left|\alpha-\alpha_{n}\right|
and therefore equation (
4.11
) becomes:

d_{k}\left(T_{\mathbf{a},n},T_{\mathbf{a},n-1}\right)\leq C_{k}\cdot{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|H_{\mathbf{a},n}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{k+1}_{k+1}\cdot 2\cdot\left|\alpha-\alpha_{n}\right|.

To estimate the norm of
H_{\mathbf{a},n}
we use Lemma
4.7
. Here, we note that
H_{\mathbf{a},n-1}
depends on the sequence entries
a_{1},\dots,a_{n-1}
only. Hence,

\hat{C}_{n}\coloneqq\max_{\mathbf{a}\in\{0,1\}^{{\mathbb{N}}}}C\left(n,H_{\mathbf{a},n-1},n+1\right)

is well-defined, where
C(n,H_{\mathbf{a},n-1},k)
are the constants from Lemma
4.7
. Then Lemma
4.7
yields

{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|H_{\mathbf{a},n}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{n+1}\leq\hat{C}_{n}\cdot q^{(n+1)\cdot N(n,n+1)}_{n}

for every sequence
\mathbf{a}\in\{0,1\}^{{\mathbb{N}}}
.

Since
\alpha
is a Liouville number, there are
q_{n}>q^{2\cdot(n-1)}_{n-1}
and
p_{n}\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that

\left\lvert\alpha-\frac{p_{n}}{q_{n}}\right\rvert<\min\left(\left\lvert\alpha-\alpha_{n-1}\right\rvert,\,\frac{1}{2\cdot n^{2}\cdot C_{n}\cdot\left(\hat{C}_{n}\cdot q^{(n+1)\cdot N(n,n+1)}_{n}\right)^{n+1}}\right).

In particular, this implies condition (
4.10
) and

\left\lvert\alpha-\frac{p_{n}}{q_{n}}\right\rvert<\frac{1}{2\cdot n^{2}\cdot C_{n}\cdot{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|H_{\mathbf{a},n}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{n+1}_{n+1}}

(4.12)

for every sequence
\mathbf{a}\in\{0,1\}^{{\mathbb{N}}}
. It follows for every sequence
\mathbf{a}\in\{0,1\}^{{\mathbb{N}}}
and every
k\leq n
that

\begin{split}d_{k}\left(T_{\mathbf{a},n},T_{\mathbf{a},n-1}\right)&\leq d_{n}\left(T_{\mathbf{a},n},T_{\mathbf{a},n-1}\right)\leq C_{n}\cdot{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|H_{\mathbf{a},n}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{n+1}_{n+1}\cdot 2\cdot\left|\alpha-\alpha_{n}\right|\\
&\leq C_{n}\cdot{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|H_{\mathbf{a},n}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{n+1}_{n+1}\cdot 2\cdot\frac{1}{2\cdot n^{2}\cdot C_{n}\cdot{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|H_{\mathbf{a},n}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{n+1}_{n+1}}\leq\frac{1}{n^{2}}.\end{split}

(4.13)

In the next step we show, that for every sequence
\mathbf{a}\in\{0,1\}^{{\mathbb{N}}}
and for arbitrary
k\in\mathbb{N}
our sequence
\left(T_{\mathbf{a},n}\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a Cauchy sequence in
\text{Diff}^{k}(\mathbb{S}^{1})
, that is,
\lim_{n,m\rightarrow\infty}d_{k}\left(T_{\mathbf{a},n},T_{\mathbf{a},m}\right)=0
. For this purpose, we calculate

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}d_{k}\left(T_{\mathbf{a},n},T_{\mathbf{a},m}\right)\leq\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sum^{n}_{i=m+1}d_{k}\left(T_{\mathbf{a},i},T_{\mathbf{a},i-1}\right)=\sum^{\infty}_{i=m+1}d_{k}\left(T_{\mathbf{a},i},T_{\mathbf{a},i-1}\right).

(4.14)

We consider the limit process
m\rightarrow\infty
, i.e. we can assume
k\leq m
, and obtain from equations (
4.13
) and (
4.14
) that

\lim_{n,m\rightarrow\infty}d_{k}\left(T_{\mathbf{a},n},T_{\mathbf{a},m}\right)\leq\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}\sum^{\infty}_{i=m+1}\frac{1}{i^{2}}=0.

Since
\text{Diff}^{k}(\mathbb{S}^{1})
is complete, the sequence
\left(T_{\mathbf{a},n}\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
converges consequently in
\text{Diff}^{k}(\mathbb{S}^{1})
for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
and for every sequence
\mathbf{a}\in\{0,1\}^{{\mathbb{N}}}
. Thus, the sequence converges in
\text{Diff}^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{1})
by definition.

2.

To show
\hat{T}_{\mathbf{a},n}\rightarrow T_{\mathbf{a}}
in
\text{Diff}^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{1})
we compute with the aid of Lemma
4.8
and equation (
4.12
) that for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
k\leq n
we have

\displaystyle d_{k}\left(T_{\mathbf{a},n},\hat{T}_{\mathbf{a},n}\right)

\displaystyle\leq d_{n}\left(H_{\mathbf{a},n}\circ R_{\alpha_{n+1}}\circ H^{-1}_{\mathbf{a},n},H_{\mathbf{a},n}\circ R_{\alpha}\circ H^{-1}_{\mathbf{a},n}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C_{n}\cdot{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|H_{\mathbf{a},n}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{n+1}_{n+1}\cdot\left|\alpha_{n+1}-\alpha\right|\leq C_{n}\cdot{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|H_{\mathbf{a},n}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{n+1}_{n+1}\cdot\left|\alpha_{n}-\alpha\right|

\displaystyle\leq C_{n}\cdot{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|H_{\mathbf{a},n}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{n+1}_{n+1}\cdot\frac{1}{2\cdot n^{2}\cdot C_{n}\cdot{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|H_{\mathbf{a},n}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{n+1}_{n+1}}\leq\frac{1}{n^{2}}.

This yields
\hat{T}_{\mathbf{a},n}\rightarrow T_{\mathbf{a}}
in
\text{Diff}^{k}(\mathbb{S}^{1})
for any
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}d_{\infty}\left(\hat{T}_{\mathbf{a},n},T_{\mathbf{a}}\right)=0
as asserted.

Furthermore, we recall that the rotation number
\tau:\mathcal{H}\to\mathbb{S}^{1}
is a continuous map in the
C^{0}
-topology
[
HK95
, Proposition 11.1.6]
. Since
\tau(\hat{T}_{\mathbf{a},n})=\alpha
for all
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, we also obtain
\tau(T_{\mathbf{a}})=\alpha
.

3.

Since
T_{\mathbf{a},n}
converges to
T_{\mathbf{a}}
in
\text{Diff}^{\infty}
by the first part of the lemma, there is
N_{1}\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that
d_{\infty}(T_{\mathbf{a},n},T_{\mathbf{a}})<\frac{\varepsilon}{2}
for all
n\geq N_{1}
. Analogously, there is
N_{2}\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that
d_{\infty}(T_{\mathbf{b},n},T_{\mathbf{b}})<\frac{\varepsilon}{2}
for all
n\geq N_{2}
. Let
N\coloneqq\max(N_{1},N_{2})
and suppose that
a_{n}=b_{n}
for all
n\leq N
. In particular, this implies
T_{\mathbf{a},n}=T_{\mathbf{b},n}
for all
n\leq N
. Altogether we have

\displaystyle d_{\infty}(T_{\mathbf{a}},T_{\mathbf{b}})

5.1
Absolute continuous and singular invariant measures

It is a well-known fact that every circle homeomorphism
T
with irrational rotation number is uniquely ergodic (see
[
HK95
, Theorem 11.2.9]
). We denote the unique
T
-invariant probability measure by
\mu_{T}
. If
T=H\circ R_{\alpha}\circ H^{-1}
with unique
H\in\mathcal{H}
satisfying
H(0)=0
, then
\mu_{T}
is given by
\mu_{T}=H_{\ast}m
, where
m
is the Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{S}^{1}
.

Moreover,
[
He79
, Theorem VII.1.4]
shows that
T\in\mathcal{H}^{2}
with
\tau(T)\notin\mathbb{Q}
is ergodic with respect to Lebesgue measure
m
. Hence, any
T
-invariant
Borel subset is either
m
-null or
m
-conull. Together with the uniqueness of
\mu_{T}
, this implies that either
\mu_{T}
is equivalent to
m
or singular to
m
.

If
\mu_{T}
is equivalent to
m
, then the unique conjugating homeomorphism
H
with
H(0)=0
maps any Lebesgue null set to a null set. In this case, the conjugacy is called
absolutely continuous
. If
\mu_{T}
is singular to
m
, then conjugacy
H
maps some Lebesgue null set to a conull set. In this case, the conjugacy is called
singular
.

In this section, we show that both cases can be realized in the setting of our Main Theorem
A
. We start by showing that the conjugation maps as defined in Section
4.1
result in a singular conjugacy
H_{\mathbf{a}}
. Here, we follow an approach similar to
[
Ma12
, section 3]
and
[
Ku18
, Lemma 1.1]
.

Lemma 5.2
.

H_{\mathbf{a}}
is singular.

Proof.

Let
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
and suppose that
a_{n}=0
. Then we let
\ell_{n,1},\ell_{n,3}\in{\mathbb{N}}
be the smallest positive integers and
\ell_{n,2},\ell_{n,4}\in{\mathbb{N}}
be the largest positive integers such that

\left[\frac{\ell_{n,1}}{q_{n+1}},\frac{\ell_{n,2}}{q_{n+1}}\right]\subseteq\left[\frac{2t_{n}}{q_{n}},\frac{1-t_{n}+8t^{2}_{n}}{2q_{n}}\right]\ \text{ and }\ \left[\frac{\ell_{n,3}}{q_{n+1}},\frac{\ell_{n,4}}{q_{n+1}}\right]\subseteq\left[\frac{1+t_{n}-8t^{2}_{n}}{2q_{n}},\frac{1-2t_{n}}{q_{n}}\right].

Lemma 5.3

.

Let
(D)
be some degree of regularity from Hölder to
C^{\infty}
and
\mathcal{D}
be the collection of orientation-preserving circle homeomorphisms with regularity
(D)
. Then there is no complete numerical Borel invariant for
\mathcal{D}
-conjugacy of free
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
actions by orientation-preserving
C^{\infty}
diffeomorphisms of the circle.

By our strategy of proof from Section
3
, this theorem follows from the subsequent criterion.

Proposition 6.1
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be the space of free
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
actions by orientation-preserving
C^{\infty}
diffeomorphisms of the circle. There is a continuous one-to-one map

\Phi:\{0,1\}^{{\mathbb{N}}}\to\mathcal{A}

such that for any two sequences
\mathbf{a}=(a_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
and
\mathbf{b}=(b_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
the following properties hold:

(R1)

If there is
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that
a_{n}=b_{n}
for every
n\geq N
, then the
C^{\infty}
-actions
\Phi(\mathbf{a})
and
\Phi(\mathbf{b})
are
C^{\infty}
-conjugate.

(R2)

If there are infinitely many
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
with
a_{n}\neq b_{n}
, then the
C^{\infty}
-actions
\Phi(\mathbf{a})
and
\Phi(\mathbf{b})
are not Hölder-conjugate.

In order to build the smooth
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
actions, we construct generating diffeomorphisms by a slight modification to the constructions in Section
4
. In particular, we have to arrange for commutativity of the generators and freeness of the action. For the latter one, the following number-theoretical lemma from
[
Ha99
]
will prove useful.

Lemma 6.2

(
[
Ha99
]
, Theorem 2.1)
.

Let
d\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
,
\epsilon>0
and
(a_{i,n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
for
i=1,\dots,d
be sequences of positive integers such that

1.

a_{1,n}
divides
a_{1,n+1}
and
\frac{a_{1,n+1}}{a_{1,n}}\geq 2^{(d+1)^{n-1}}

2.

b_{i,n}<2^{(d+1)^{n-(\sqrt{2}+\epsilon)\sqrt{n}}}
for
i=1,\dots,d

3.

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{a_{i,n}b_{j,n}}{b_{i,n}a_{j,n}}=0
for all
i,j\in\{1,\dots,d\}
,
i>j

4.

a_{i,n}2^{-(d+1)^{n-(\sqrt{2}+\epsilon)\sqrt{n}}}<a_{1,n}<a_{i,n}2^{(d+1)^{n-(\sqrt{2}+\epsilon)\sqrt{n}}}
for
i=1,\dots,d

hold for every sufficiently large
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
. Then the numbers
\sum^{\infty}_{n=1}\frac{b_{1,n}}{a_{1,n}},\dots,\sum^{\infty}_{n=1}\frac{b_{d,n}}{a_{d,n}}
and
1
are linearly independent over the integers.

Proof of Proposition
6.1
.

We construct the
d
geneators
T^{(i)}_{\mathbf{a}}
,
i=1,\dots,d
, of the
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
action
\Phi(\mathbf{a})
as limits of AbC diffeomorphisms
T^{(i)}_{\mathbf{a},n}=H_{\mathbf{a},n}\circ R_{\alpha^{(i)}_{n+1}}\circ H^{-1}_{\mathbf{a},n}
, where the conjugation maps
H_{\mathbf{a},n}=H_{\mathbf{a},n-1}\circ h_{\mathbf{a},n}
are constructed as in Section
4.1
and
(\alpha^{(i)}_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
are sequences of rational numbers
\alpha^{(i)}_{n}=\frac{p^{(i)}_{n}}{q_{n}}
with
p^{(i)}_{n}
and
q_{n}
relatively prime.

For a start, we let
H_{\mathbf{a},0}=\operatorname{id}
,
q_{1}
be a power of
2
and for each
i=1,\dots,d
we let
p^{(i)}_{1}
be an odd integer. In the induction step from
n-1
to
n
we construct the conjugation map
h_{\mathbf{a},n}
as in Section
4.1
using the number
q_{n}
. Then we choose
l_{n}
as a sufficiently large power of
2
such that

l_{n}>4^{dn}\cdot C_{n}\cdot{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|H_{\mathbf{a},n}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{n+1}_{n+1}

(6.3)

for all sequences
\mathbf{a}\in\{0,1\}^{{\mathbb{N}}}
, where
C_{n}
is the constant from Lemma
4.8
. We proceed by defining the rational numbers

\alpha^{(i)}_{n+1}\coloneqq\alpha^{(i)}_{n}+\frac{3^{(i-1)\cdot n}}{l_{n}q^{2}_{n}}=\frac{p^{(i)}_{n}l_{n}q^{2}_{n}+3^{(i-1)\cdot n}}{l_{n}q^{2}_{n}}

(6.4)

for each
i=1,\dots,d
. Since
l_{n}
and
q_{n}
are powers of
2
, we note that
q_{n+1}=l_{n}q^{2}_{n}
is a power of
2
and
p^{(i)}_{n+1}
is relatively prime to
q_{n+1}
. Furthermore, we apply Lemma
4.8
to get the following estimate for each
i=1,\dots,d
and every
\mathbf{a}\in\{0,1\}^{{\mathbb{N}}}
:

\displaystyle d_{n}\left(T^{(i)}_{\mathbf{a},n},T^{(i)}_{\mathbf{a},n-1}\right)

\displaystyle=d_{n}\left(H_{\mathbf{a},n}\circ R_{\alpha^{(i)}_{n+1}}\circ H^{-1}_{\mathbf{a},n},H_{\mathbf{a},n-1}\circ R_{\alpha^{(i)}_{n}}\circ H^{-1}_{\mathbf{a},n-1}\right)

\displaystyle=d_{n}\left(H_{\mathbf{a},n}\circ R_{\alpha^{(i)}_{n+1}}\circ H^{-1}_{\mathbf{a},n},H_{\mathbf{a},n-1}\circ R_{\frac{p^{(i)}_{n}}{q_{n}}}\circ h_{\mathbf{a},n}\circ h^{-1}_{\mathbf{a},n}\circ H^{-1}_{\mathbf{a},n-1}\right)

\displaystyle=d_{n}\left(H_{\mathbf{a},n}\circ R_{\alpha^{(i)}_{n+1}}\circ H^{-1}_{\mathbf{a},n},H_{\mathbf{a},n-1}\circ h_{\mathbf{a},n}\circ R_{\frac{p^{(i)}_{n}}{q_{n}}}\circ h^{-1}_{\mathbf{a},n}\circ H^{-1}_{\mathbf{a},n-1}\right)

\displaystyle=d_{n}\left(H_{\mathbf{a},n}\circ R_{\alpha^{(i)}_{n+1}}\circ H^{-1}_{\mathbf{a},n},H_{\mathbf{a},n}\circ R_{\alpha^{(i)}_{n}}\circ H^{-1}_{\mathbf{a},n}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C_{n}\cdot{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|H_{\mathbf{a},n}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{n+1}_{n+1}\cdot\left\lvert\alpha^{(i)}_{n+1}-\alpha^{(i)}_{n}\right\rvert

\displaystyle<\frac{3^{(d-1)n}}{4^{dn}\cdot q^{2}_{n}},

where we used equations (
6.3
) and (
6.4
) in the last step. As in the proof of Lemma
4.9
, this estimate allows us to conclude convergence of the sequence
(T^{(i)}_{\mathbf{a},n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
to a
C^{\infty}
-diffeomorphism
T^{(i)}_{\mathbf{a}}
. By Lemma
5.1
we again obtain convergence of
(H_{\mathbf{a},n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
to a homeomorphism
H_{\mathbf{a}}
. Hence, for each
i=1,\dots,d
we get
T^{(i)}_{\mathbf{a}}=H_{\mathbf{a}}\circ R_{\alpha^{(i)}}\circ H^{-1}_{\mathbf{a}}
, where
\alpha^{(i)}
is the limit of the sequence
(\alpha^{(i)}_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
. In particular, we have
T^{(i)}_{\mathbf{a}}\circ T^{(j)}_{\mathbf{a}}=T^{(j)}_{\mathbf{a}}\circ T^{(i)}_{\mathbf{a}}
for all
i,j\in\{1,\dots,d\}
. Thus, the
C^{\infty}
-diffeomormphisms
T^{(1)}_{\mathbf{a}},\dots,T^{(d)}_{\mathbf{a}}
generate a smooth
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
action. The properties (R1) and (R2) follow from the corresponding properties in Proposition
3.1
.

Finally, we apply Lemma
6.2
with
a_{i,n}=q_{n+1}
and
b_{i,n}=3^{(i-1)n}
to verify that the numbers
\alpha^{(1)},\dots,\alpha^{(d)}
and
1
are linearly independent over the integers. This yields that the action
\Phi(\mathbf{a})
generated by
T^{(1)}_{\mathbf{a}},\dots,T^{(d)}_{\mathbf{a}}
is a free action.
∎

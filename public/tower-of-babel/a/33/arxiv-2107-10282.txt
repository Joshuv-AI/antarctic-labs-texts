p p -Form Surface Charges on AdS: Renormalization and Conservation Preprint: IPM/P-2021/nnn
Erfan Esmaeili a,b, Vahid Hosseinzadeh a

Abstract
Surface charges of a p p -form theory on the boundary of an AdS d+1 spacetime are computed. Counter-terms on the boundary produce divergent corner-terms which holographically renormalize the symplectic form. Different choices of boundary conditions lead to various expressions for the charges and the associated fluxes. With the usual standard AdS boundary conditions, there are conserved zero-mode charges. Moreover, we explore two leaky boundary conditions which admit an infinite number of charges forming an Abelian algebra and non-vanishing flux. Finally, we discuss magnetic p p -form charges and electric/magnetic duality.

Introduction

The relation between asymptotic symmetries, memory effects and soft theorems has provoked considerable progress in understanding the IR properties of field theories in the asymptotically flat spacetimes
Strominger:2014pwa
;
Strominger:2013lka
;
He:2014laa
;
Campiglia:2015qka
. See also

and the references therein. Similar to global symmetries, large gauge transformations/diffeomorphisms lead to Ward identities in the quantum theory, as can be seen in the path integral formulation

. Physical boundary conditions for the gauge field or the metric can be assigned such that the equivalence of Ward identities with soft theorems hold. For instance, subleading soft graviton theorems in four dimensional asymptotically flat space, suggest an extension of the standard BMS group to include superrotations
Kapec:2014opa
;
Barnich:2009se
;
Campiglia:2014yka
. Identifying the correct set of boundary conditions that describe localized systems is a key step in understanding holography in asymptotically flat spacetimes, as it specifies the symmetries of the putative CFT in the semi-classical regime
Barnich:2010eb
;
Cheung:2016iub
;
Ball:2019atb
;
Banerjee:2020kaa
.

An insightful approach to holography in asymptotically flat spacetimes is to embed these spaces in a larger Anti-de Sitter space (AdS)
Polchinski:1999ry
;
Susskind:1998vk
;
Barnich:2012aw
. In particular, the relation between boundary CFT Ward identities and bulk soft theorems in the flat (i.e. large AdS radius) limit is explored in
Hijano:2019qmi
;
Hijano:2020szl
. A natural expectation is to find appropriate boundary conditions for AdS theories which reduce to the standard asymptotically flat boundary conditions in large AdS radius limit. The standard treatment of AdS
d+1
Einstein gravity is to fix a conformal class of metrics on the boundary, for which the asymptotic symmetry group is isomorphic to the
d
-dimensional conformal group. Otherwise, by fixing Neumann or mixed boundary conditions, the boundary metric is allowed to fluctuate which translates into energy flux on the boundary
Compere:2008us
;
Krishnan:2016dgy
. In AdS
4
Compère
et al
found that by choosing a foliation and a measure on the boundary manifold, the asymptotic symmetry group is the so-called
\Lambda
-BMS group
Compere:2019bua
;
Compere:2020lrt
which reduces to the centerless BMS
4
in the large AdS radius limit. See also
Fiorucci:2020xto
for extension to higher dimensions.

In a previous study
Esmaeili:2019mbw
, we considered Maxwell theory in Anti-de Sitter space and constructed the infinite-dimensional asymptotic symmetry algebra. To analyze this problem we adopted hyperbolic slicing of the AdS space. The phase space consisted of charged particles moving freely and scattering off at the origin of the hyperbolic coordinate system. In this special system, we can treat the boundary like the spatial infinity of Minkowski space as pointed out in
Hijano:2020szl
and henceforth we expect charge conservation. In this paper, we generalize the boundary conditions to include generic solutions to Maxwell’s field equations including radiation that reach the boundary, as well as generalization to
p
-form theories.

Higher form gauge fields are natural objects of study from a supergravity and string theory perspectives
Horowitz:1991cd
;
Freedman:2012zz
.
p
-brane solutions couple to and source the
p+1
-form gauge fields.
p
-branes and likewise
p+1
-forms, may be residing on various background geometries, such as flat or AdS backgrounds.
The main objective of this paper is a near-boundary analysis of
p
-form gauge fields and constructing the surface charges associated with residual gauge transformations.

A clear and thorough treatment of field equations and Cauchy development for scalar theory, Maxwell theory and linearized gravity in
d+1
dimensional Anti-de Sitter space is performed by Ishibashi and Wald
Ishibashi:2004wx
. For Maxwell theory, that involves a decomposition of gauge field components into scalar and vector modes with respect to rotation group. Here we first generalize their analysis to a
(p+1)
-form gauge field, for which the components can be expanded in terms of
p
-form and
p+1
-form spherical harmonics on the sphere
S^{d-1}
.
These two sets differ in their asymptotic behavior. We discuss normalizablity of these modes in different dimensions.

In Lorentzian AdS, non-normalizable modes are fixed as background fields upon which the normalizable modes fluctuate
Balasubramanian:1998sn
;
Marolf:2004fy
. For some
p,d
values both bulk modes become normalizable
Breitenlohner:1982jf
, as we specify for different components of
p
-form fields. In our case, non-normalizable modes are those which make the symplectic form divergent. However, the symplectic form is modified when counter-terms are added on the boundary. Subtracting the divergences in the on-shell action is provided by local Lagrangians defined on the AdS boundary
Balasubramanian:1999re
;
Skenderis:2002wp
. Variation of the counter-terms produce total divergence terms, which are known as corner terms. These divergent contributions modify the symplectic form of the theory and make it finite
Papadimitriou:2005ii
;
Compere:2008us
;
Freidel:2019ohg
. We will exhibit the counter-term for
p
-form theory and construct the renormalized symplectic form, which guarantees finiteness of surface charges.

The residual gauge transformations after fixing the radial gauge have finite surface charges that follow from the renormalized symplectic structure. We derive the charge expression without imposing boundary conditions, and in particular allow finite gauge transformations on the boundary. For a Maxwell field this amounts to defining the Dirichlet condition as

\delta F^{(0)}|_{\mathscr{I}}=0

(1.1)

(instead of the usual condition
\delta A^{(0)}|_{\mathscr{I}}=0
). The generalized boundary condition that leads to this kind of constructions is in general leaky: one can not demand conservation and also infinite asymptotic symmetry at the same time. Thus, the flux on AdS boundary is non-vanishing except for the
U(1)
electric charge. Addition of boundary terms to the action leads to different charge expressions and different fluxes with no preferred criterion for choosing one. We examine two cases of boundary conditions with flux and derive the charge expressions for the boundary gauge mode (sketched in table
2
).

As it turns out, conservation of the charges is closely related to the existence of an energy functional on the solution space. The reason is that addition of the boundary gauge transformations deforms the expression for the energy by a surface term and spoils its integrability. We discuss specific configurations with conserved charges and an integrable energy functional.

3
The renormalized action and symplectic form

Before we delve into renormalization, we shortly review the Harlow-Wu method for defining the symplectic form in the presence of corner terms
Harlow:2019yfa
. Then we introduce the counter-terms that make the on-shell action finite. In appendix
A
, we work out the divergences in the action and the symplectic form explicitly in a few examples.

3.1
Symplectic form in presence of corner terms

Considering a boundary action
S_{b}=\int_{\mathscr{I}}L_{b}
, the boundary term from variation of the total action becomes

\displaystyle\delta(S+S_{b})\approx\int_{\mathscr{I}}\left(\theta^{r}+\delta L_{b}\right).

(3.36)

Assume that for a particular boundary condition,

\displaystyle(\theta^{r}+\delta L_{b})|_{\mathscr{I}}=\partial_{\mu}\mathcal{C}^{\mu}

(3.37)

where
\mathcal{C}^{\mu}
is a phase space one-form leading to a two-form
\omega^{\mu}_{\mathcal{C}}=\delta\mathcal{C}^{\mu}
. According to the Harlow-Wu prescription, the symplectic form of the theory is

\displaystyle\Omega=\int_{\Sigma}\omega^{t}+\oint_{\partial\Sigma}\omega^{t}_{\mathcal{C}}

(3.38)

This expression is manifestly conserved, as the symplectic flux is a total divergence by the boundary condition (
3.37
), and it is subtracted as two corner terms by the Stokes theorem. Note also that with the boundary condition (
3.37
), the action principle is satisfied by fixing
C^{\mu}
at initial and final surfaces. In addition, this definition is free from
Y
ambiguity.

Having said that, there are situations that we have the following boundary condition

\displaystyle(\theta^{r}+\delta L_{b})|_{\mathscr{I}}=\partial_{\mu}\mathcal{C}^{\mu}+\mathbbmss{F}

(3.39)

where
\mathbbmss{F}
is a given flux expression, prescribed on physical grounds or some other criterion. The symplectic form (
3.38
) is not conserved any more and the symplectic flux at the boundary is given by
\delta\mathbbmss{F}
. Furthermore, with the flux present, the action principle can not be satisfied,

\displaystyle\delta(S+S_{b})\approx\int_{\mathscr{I}}\mathbbmss{F}.

(3.40)

On the boundary of AdS spacetime, one can make the flux term
\mathbbmss{F}
vanishes by imposing boundary conditions. However, as we show in the next section, in presence of large gauge transformations, the flux is generically non-vanishing unless further dynamical constraints are imposed.

3.2
Renormalization

In this section, we consider renormalization of the action of a
p
-form theory.
This is done by introducing an appropriate counter-term which renders both the on-shell action and the variation of the action finite. The key point which is pointed out by Compére and Marolf in
Compere:2008us
is that variation of the counterterm produces divergent corner terms which also renormalize the symplectic form of the theory. This follows from the Harlow-Wu construction of the symplectic form (
3.38
) for some divergent
C^{\mu}
determined from the counter-terms, and leads to a finite symplectic form.

The appropriate counter-term action would be,

S_{ct}[R]=\int_{r=R}L_{ct}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\frac{1}{2(p+1)!}\int_{r=R}\sqrt{g}\mathcal{A}^{[\alpha]}_{\underline{\mu}^{p+1}}\mathcal{F}_{[\alpha]}^{r\underline{\mu}^{p+1}}\qquad\qquad n_{p}\geq 2\\
\\
\frac{1}{2(p+1)!}\int_{r=R}\sqrt{g}\mathcal{A}^{[\beta]}_{\underline{\mu}^{p+1}}\mathcal{F}_{[\beta]}^{r\underline{\mu}^{p+1}}\qquad\qquad n_{p}<2\end{array}\right.

(3.41)

This in turn leads to
S_{\text{ren}}=\lim_{R\rightarrow\infty}(S_{reg}[R]+S_{ct}[R])
and
S_{reg}[R]
is the bulk action regularized at the cutoff
R
.
S_{\text{ren}}
is then the renormalized action.
To fix the renormalization scheme, we assume that the counter term (
3.41
) has no finite term.

The variation of the renormalized action leads to,

\displaystyle\delta S_{\text{ren}}=\int_{\mathscr{I}}\big(\partial_{\mu}\theta_{ct}^{\mu}+\Theta\big)

(3.42)

where
\Theta
is finite at
R\to\infty
, containing both
A^{[\alpha]}
and
A^{[\beta]}
and their variations and it is originated from the bulk action
S
. We dismiss the finite term for the moment and come back to it in the next section. The corner term
\theta^{\mu}_{ct}
on the other hand is divergent and is defined by the relation,

\displaystyle\delta L_{ct}=\frac{\delta L_{ct}}{\delta\mathcal{A}_{\underline{\mu}^{p+1}}}\delta\mathcal{A}_{\underline{\mu}^{p+1}}+\partial_{\mu}\theta_{ct}^{\mu}.

(3.43)

In practice the “equation of motion” part of
L_{ct}
cancel the divergent terms in
\theta^{r}
. The other divergent term
\partial_{\mu}\theta_{ct}^{\mu}
does not concern the action principle because it is a corner term and is fixed by the initial and final data. However, it leads to a renormalized finite symplectic structure,

\displaystyle\Omega_{\text{ren}}=\int_{\Sigma_{t}}\omega^{t}_{\text{ren}}=\int_{\Sigma_{t}}\omega^{t}+\oint_{\partial\Sigma_{t}}\omega^{t}_{ct}.

(3.44)

Explicit calculations for some of the cases are gathered in the appendix
A
.

4.1
The Conservative approach
\mathbbmss{F}=0

Among the choices we have, there is a special case where we take the system to be completely conservative. This can be done by taking the decomposition (

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{{}_{\circ}}^{\mu}

\displaystyle=\frac{c_{p}\sqrt{h}}{p!}A^{\mu\underline{\nu}^{p}}_{[\beta]}\delta\varphi_{\underline{\nu}^{p}}

(4.51)

\displaystyle\mathbbmss{F}_{{}_{\circ}}

\displaystyle=0

(4.52)

where we have used the leading term of constraint equation (

Then, the symplectic form will be deformed as,

(4.53)

This choice is interesting as guarantees conservation by setting the flux to zero. As it turns out, the surface charges are vanishing

\displaystyle\Omega_{{}_{\circ}}(\cdot,\delta_{\lambda})=\delta Q_{\lambda}=0.

(4.54)

The reason is that, the corner-term exactly cancels a similar term coming from the bulk symplectic form
\omega^{t}_{\text{ren}}
. To show this, using the decomposition (

), dependence of the bulk symplectic form on

\displaystyle\int\omega^{t}_{\text{ren}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(p+1)!}\int\sqrt{g}\delta\mathcal{A}_{\underline{i}^{p+1}}\delta\mathcal{F}^{t\underline{i}^{p+1}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{p!}\int\sqrt{g}\partial_{j}\delta\varphi_{\underline{i}^{p}}\delta\mathcal{F}^{tj\underline{i}^{p}}+\int_{\Sigma_{t}}\omega^{t}_{\text{ren,gi}}

\displaystyle\approx\frac{1}{p!}\oint\sqrt{g}\delta\varphi_{\underline{i}^{p}}\delta\mathcal{F}^{tr\underline{i}^{p}}+\int_{\Sigma_{t}}\omega^{t}_{\text{ren,gi}}

\displaystyle=\frac{c_{p}}{p!}\oint\sqrt{q}\delta\varphi_{\underline{i}^{p}}\delta A^{t\underline{\nu}^{p}}_{[\beta]}+\int_{\Sigma_{t}}\omega^{t}_{\text{ren,gi}}

(4.55)

where

is the renormalized symplectic form free of the gauge variable
\varphi
. In consequence, the total symplectic form is gauge invariant (i.e. annihilated by all gauge transformations)

\displaystyle\Omega_{{}_{\circ}}=\int_{\Sigma_{t}}\omega^{t}_{\text{ren,gi}}.

(4.56)

The action principle is satisfied with this choice.

Exact parameters.

The genuine feature of higher form theories with respect to Maxwell theory is the possibility of gauge parameters which are exact;
\lambda_{\underline{i}^{p}}=(d\epsilon)_{\underline{i}^{p}}

Harmonic parameters.

The flux term can be written equivalently as,

\displaystyle=\frac{-c_{p}\sqrt{h}}{p!}\Phi^{\underline{i}^{p}}\delta\Delta\varphi_{\underline{i}^{p}}

. The only non-vanishing charge belongs to the harmonic parameter and is proportional to

There are more interesting possibilities for the harmonic parameter if

p
-form gauge parameters that are closed but not exact around some non-trivial cycle, the value of the charge gives the electric flux through that cycle.

Magnetic charges

The electric charges are associated with gauge transformations that are non-zero on the boundary. In contrary, it is not possible to obtain magnetic charges from such symmetry principles. However, we can define magnetic charges of a
p+1
-form theory in

\displaystyle Q^{M}_{\lambda}=\oint\lambda\wedge F^{[\alpha]}=\frac{1}{\tilde{p}!}\frac{1}{(p+2)!}\oint\sqrt{q}\epsilon^{\underline{i}^{\tilde{p}}\underline{j}^{p+2}}\lambda_{\underline{i}^{\tilde{p}}}F^{[\alpha]}_{\underline{j}^{p+2}}.

Magnetic charge as the electric charge of the dual theory

Integrability

The electric surface charges in
p+1
-form theory are integrable as long as the gauge parameter is not field-dependent. This is not the case for the charges of AdS isometries. For instnace, including boundary modes modifies the energy of the system by surface terms. As it turns out, the general form of the energy computed as
\Omega(\cdot,\delta_{t})
will not be integrable in the presence of the gauge mode
f
(or
\varphi
) by a non-integrable boundary term. The integrable bulk term is universal and is given by the standard energy-momentum tensor as expected,

\displaystyle E_{\text{bulk}}=\frac{1}{2}\int\frac{dr}{(r^{2}+1)}\Bigl[{\text{k}_{p}^{2}}\Big(\dot{\boldsymbol{\phi}}^{2}+\boldsymbol{\phi}A_{0}\boldsymbol{\phi}\Big)+\Big(\dot{\boldsymbol{\psi}}^{2}+\boldsymbol{\psi}A_{1}\boldsymbol{\psi}\Big)\Bigr]

(6.108)

where
A_{0}
and
A_{1}
are differential operators in the equations of motion (
2.26
) and (
2.16
) as
\ddot{\boldsymbol{\phi}}=-A_{0}\boldsymbol{\phi}
and
\ddot{\boldsymbol{\psi}}=-A_{1}\boldsymbol{\psi}
. Sum over the different spherical modes
(k)
is assumed here. The extra non-integrable part denoted as
\not{\delta}E
is different using different symplectic forms. We have,

\displaystyle\not{\delta}E_{\circ}

\displaystyle=0

(6.109a)

\displaystyle\not{\delta}E_{\Delta}

\displaystyle=-\sum\text{k}_{p}^{2}\dot{\phi}^{(k,0)}\delta f_{(k)}=-\mathbbmss{F}_{\Delta}

(6.109b)

\displaystyle\not{\delta}E_{\Box}

\displaystyle=-2c_{p}\sum\dot{\phi}^{(k,2)}\delta f_{(k)}=-\mathbbmss{F}_{\Box}.

(6.109c)

Clearly, the energy is integrable when the associated fluxes are vanishing and the surface charges are conserved.
In the third case, there is also an extra integrable contribution from the boundary modes to the energy,

\displaystyle E_{\Box}=\sum\ddot{\phi}^{(k,0)}\dot{f}_{(k)}+\text{k}_{p}^{2}\dot{\phi}^{(k,0)}f_{(k)}\,.

(6.110)

Concluding Remarks

In this paper we computed the surface charges for a
(p+1)
-form theory on AdS
d+1
associated with the gauge mode on the boundary of spacetime assuming the usual reflective boundary condition modulo residual gauge transformations. This results in infinite dimensional Abelian symmetry algebra. Different expressions for the charge are possible depending on the flux expression, leading to different boundary terms of the symplectic form. We studied three cases of interest where the results summarized in table

. The first one was a conservative boundary condition which resulted in an empty set of charges. Similarly to the recent gravity studies
Compere:2019bua

By proposing a generic counter-term, we showed how the divergences in the on-shell action are subtracted in various cases. In addition, we explicitly showed how variation of the counter term on the boundary leads to divergent corner terms which render the symplectic form finite. This procedure has also been applied in Maxwell theory in flat space
Freidel:2019ohg
. The charges are then finite by construction as they are built out of the renormalized symplectic form.

Appendix A
Holographic renormalization of action and the symplectic form

We show that adding the counter term (
3.41
) makes the on-shell action, variation of the action, and the symplectic form finite.

On-shell action and variation of the action

S_{\text{reg}}[R]\approx-\frac{1}{2(p+1)!}\int_{r=R}d^{d}x\sqrt{g}\mathcal{A}_{\underline{\mu}^{p+1}}\mathcal{F}^{r\underline{\mu}^{p+1}}

(A.111)

According to fall-offs (
2
), the divergent terms come from
A_{[\alpha]}
modes in
n_{p}\geq 2
and from
A_{[\beta]}
modes in
n_{p}<2
. Thus, the counterterm (
3.41
) cancels the divergences trivially. This leads to,

\displaystyle S^{\text{on-shell}}_{\text{ren}}=\frac{c_{p}}{2(p+1)!}\int_{\mathscr{J}}d^{d}x\sqrt{h}A_{\underline{\nu}^{p+1}}^{[\beta]}A^{\underline{\nu}^{p+1}}_{[\alpha]}

(A.112)

On the other hand, the variation of the renormalized action is turned out to be,

\displaystyle\delta S_{\text{ren}}=\frac{c_{p}}{(p+1)!}\int_{\mathscr{J}}d^{d}x\sqrt{h}A_{\underline{\nu}^{p+1}}^{[\beta]}\delta A^{\underline{\nu}^{p+1}}_{[\alpha]}

(A.113)

Symplectic form

In the radial gauge, the symplectic form is,

\displaystyle\Omega=\frac{1}{(p+1)!}\int\sqrt{g}d^{n}x^{i}dr\delta\mathcal{A}_{\underline{i}^{p+1}}\delta\mathcal{F}^{t\underline{i}^{p+1}}=\frac{1}{(p+1)!}\int\sqrt{q}d^{n}x^{i}dr\frac{r^{n_{p}-2}}{r^{2}+1}\delta\mathcal{F}_{t\underline{i}^{p+1}}\delta\mathcal{A}_{\underline{i}^{p+1}}

(A.114)

where the term
\delta\mathcal{A}_{r\underline{i}^{p}}\delta\mathcal{F}^{tr\underline{i}^{p}}
drops out of the symplectic form. This has crucial consequences. First note that in this gauge fixed approach, the
p
-form and
(p+1)
-form components have the same contribution in the bulk symplectic form. One can see the symplectic form as a
p
-form sector plus a
(p+1)
-from sector,
\delta\mathcal{A}^{(p)}_{\underline{i}^{p+1}}\delta\mathcal{F}_{(p)}^{t\underline{i}^{p+1}}+\delta\mathcal{A}^{(p+1)}_{\underline{i}^{p+1}}\delta\mathcal{F}_{(p+1)}^{t\underline{i}^{p+1}}
where the term
\delta\mathcal{A}^{(p)}_{r\underline{i}^{p}}\delta\mathcal{F}_{(p)}^{tr\underline{i}^{p}}
is absent due to radial gauge. We will see that for example in
n_{p}=0,1
this fact leads to the conclusion that we have two normalizable modes for both
p
and
(p+1)
-form sector, in contrast to the gauge invariant approach where we were working with gauge invariant quantities
\psi
and
\phi
in the section
2.2
. Specifically in the table
1
, we had
2
normalizable modes for
p+1
components and
1
normalizable mode for
p
-form components sector in that gauge invariant approach.

Now we review how the holographic renormalization of the action eliminates some divergences of the symplectic form and leads to finite one in some cases.

\boldsymbol{n_{p}=0}

While the action is divergent in this case and need to be renormalized, the symplectic form is finite as we explained above. One can see this by writing the symplectic form expansion,

\displaystyle\Omega=\int\sqrt{q}d^{n}x^{i}drr^{-4}(1-r^{-2}+\cdots)\delta(F^{[\beta]}_{t\underline{i}^{p+1}}r+\cdots)\delta(A_{[\beta]}^{\underline{i}^{p+1}}r+\cdots)\sim\text{finite}

(A.115)

where the leading term in the symplectic density is
\mathcal{O}(r^{-2})
and so normalizable. On the other hand, the variation of the action has the following divergent term,

\displaystyle\delta S_{div}\approx\frac{-1}{(p+1)!}\int_{\mathscr{I}}\sqrt{h}\left(rA^{[\beta]}_{\underline{\nu}^{p+1}}\delta A_{[\beta]}^{\underline{\nu}^{p+1}}\right)

(A.116)

which is cured by the renormalization.

\boldsymbol{n_{p}=1}

This case is also like the previous one. One can see that the symplectic form is convergent thanks to the radial gauge,

\displaystyle\Omega=\int\sqrt{q}d^{n}x^{i}drr^{-3}(1-r^{-2}+\cdots)\delta(F^{[\beta]}_{t\underline{i}^{p+1}}\ln{r}+\cdots)\delta(A_{[\beta]}^{\underline{i}^{p+1}}\ln{r}+\cdots)\sim\text{finite}

(A.117)

where the leading term in the symplectic density is
\mathcal{O}(r^{-3}\ln^{2}{r})
. The divergent term of the variation of the action is,

\displaystyle\delta S_{div}\approx\frac{-1}{(p+1)!}\int_{\mathscr{I}}\sqrt{h}\left(\ln{r}A^{[\beta]}_{\underline{\nu}^{p+1}}\delta A_{[\beta]}^{\underline{\nu}^{p+1}}\right)

(A.118)

which is again cured by the renormalization.

\boldsymbol{n_{p}=2}

The case
n_{p}=2
is a special case. This case has had already two independent normalizable modes in terms of
\psi
and
\phi
and the action is also finite and does not need renormalization. The symplectic form is also finite,

\displaystyle\Omega=\int\sqrt{q}d^{n}x^{i}drr^{-2}(1-r^{-2}+\cdots)\delta(F^{[\alpha]}_{t\underline{i}^{p+1}}+\cdots)\delta(A_{[\alpha]}^{\underline{i}^{p+1}}+\cdots)\sim\text{finite}

(A.119)

where the leading term in the symplectic density is
\mathcal{O}(r^{-2})
.

\boldsymbol{n_{p}=3}

In this case the symplectic form and also the action diverge and it needs a renormalization.
We explain in detail the renormalization of this case in detail which almost contains all the possible situations in a simple way. Considering the expansion of the gauge field as,

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}=(A^{[\alpha]}+A^{[\alpha,2]}r^{-2}\ln{r}+\cdots)+(A^{[\beta]}r^{-2}+\cdots)

(A.120)

where we suppressed the indices. Then from the equations of motion (

\displaystyle\left[\frac{n_{p}}{r}+\partial_{r}\right]\partial_{r}\mathcal{A}_{\underline{\nu}^{p+1}}+r^{-4}\bar{D}^{\mu}\mathcal{F}_{\mu\underline{\nu}^{p+1}}=0

(A.121)

\displaystyle A^{[\alpha,2]}_{\underline{\mu}^{p+1}}=\frac{1}{2}D_{\mu}F^{\mu\underline{\mu}^{p+1}}_{[\alpha]}\qquad\qquad F_{\mu\underline{\mu}^{p+1}}^{[\alpha]}\equiv(p+2)D_{[\mu}A^{[\alpha]}_{\underline{\mu}^{p+1}]}.

Now the variation of the action reads,

\displaystyle\delta S\approx\frac{-1}{(p+1)!}\int_{\mathscr{I}}\sqrt{h}\left((1-2\ln{r})A^{[\alpha,2]}_{\underline{\nu}^{p+1}}\delta A_{[\alpha]}^{\underline{\nu}^{p+1}}+2A_{[\beta]}^{\underline{\nu}^{p+1}}\delta A^{[\alpha]}_{\underline{\nu}^{p+1}}\right)

\displaystyle S_{ct}=\frac{1}{2(p+1)!}\int d^{d}x^{\mu}\sqrt{g}\mathcal{A}^{[\alpha]}_{\underline{\mu}^{p+1}}\mathcal{F}_{[\alpha]}^{r\underline{\mu}^{p+1}}

(A.124)

which leads to,

\displaystyle\delta S_{\text{ren}}\approx\frac{1}{(p+1)!}\int_{\mathscr{I}}\sqrt{h}\left\{\frac{(2\ln{r}-1)}{2}\left(A^{[\alpha,2]}_{\underline{\nu}^{p+1}}\delta A_{[\alpha]}^{\underline{\nu}^{p+1}}-A^{[\alpha]}_{\underline{\nu}^{p+1}}\delta A_{[\alpha,2]}^{\underline{\nu}^{p+1}}\right)+2A_{[\beta]}^{\underline{\nu}^{p+1}}\delta A^{[\alpha]}_{\underline{\nu}^{p+1}}\right\}

Our goal here is to use the equation of motion (

\displaystyle\delta S_{\text{ren}}\approx\int_{\mathscr{I}}\partial_{\mu}\theta_{ct}^{\mu}+\Theta

(A.126)

where

which is a corner term.
It is straightforward that using the equation of motion,

\displaystyle\delta S_{\text{ren}}\approx\frac{1}{(p+1)!}\int_{\mathscr{I}}\sqrt{h}\left\{\frac{(2\ln{r}-1)}{4}D_{\mu}\left(F^{\mu\underline{\nu}^{p+1}}_{[\alpha]}\delta A^{[\alpha]}_{\underline{\nu}^{p+1}}-A^{[\alpha]}_{\underline{\nu}^{p+1}}\delta F^{\mu\underline{\nu}^{p+1}}_{[\alpha]}\right)+2A_{[\beta]}^{\underline{\nu}^{p+1}}\delta A^{[\alpha]}_{\underline{\nu}^{p+1}}\right\}

(A.127)

Where we can identify,

\displaystyle\theta^{\mu}_{ct}=\frac{1}{(p+1)!}\sqrt{h}\frac{(2\ln{r}-1)}{4}\left(F^{\mu\underline{\nu}^{p+1}}_{[\alpha]}\delta A^{[\alpha]}_{\underline{\nu}^{p+1}}-A^{[\alpha]}_{\underline{\nu}^{p+1}}\delta F^{\mu\underline{\nu}^{p+1}}_{[\alpha]}\right)

(A.128)

\displaystyle\Theta=\frac{1}{(p+1)!}\sqrt{h}2A_{[\beta]}^{\underline{\nu}^{p+1}}\delta A^{[\alpha]}_{\underline{\nu}^{p+1}}

(A.129)

Now the symplectic form has the following expansion,

\displaystyle\Omega=\int\sqrt{q}d^{n}x^{i}drr^{-1}\delta F^{[\alpha]}_{t\underline{i}^{p+1}}\delta A_{[\alpha]}^{\underline{i}^{p+1}}+\text{finite}

(A.130)

where the leading term has logarithmic divergence.
Then, it is then easily seen that the counter term symplectic potential renormalizes the symplectic form by the relation,

\displaystyle\Omega_{\text{ren}}=\int_{\Sigma_{t}}\omega^{t}+\oint_{\partial\Sigma_{t}}\omega^{t}_{ct}

(A.131)

where

\displaystyle\omega^{\mu}_{ct}=\frac{1}{(p+1)!}\sqrt{h}\ln{r}\delta F^{\mu\underline{\nu}^{p+1}}_{[\alpha]}\delta A^{[\alpha]}_{\underline{\nu}^{p+1}}

(A.132)

\boldsymbol{n_{p}=4}

This case is almost similar to the previous

\displaystyle\Omega=\int\sqrt{q}d^{n}x^{i}dr\delta F^{[\alpha]}_{t\underline{i}^{p+1}}\delta A_{[\alpha]}^{\underline{i}^{p+1}}+\text{finite}

\displaystyle\theta^{\mu}_{ct}=\frac{1}{(p+1)!}\sqrt{h}\frac{r}{2}\left(F^{\mu\underline{\nu}^{p+1}}_{[\alpha]}\delta A^{[\alpha]}_{\underline{\nu}^{p+1}}-A^{[\alpha]}_{\underline{\nu}^{p+1}}\delta F^{\mu\underline{\nu}^{p+1}}_{[\alpha]}\right)

The gauge independent part of

Here we examine the flux term
\hat{\Theta}
. This leads to the symplectic flux for the gauge invariant sector
\hat{\omega}\sim\delta A^{\underline{\nu}^{p+1}}_{[\beta]}\delta\hat{A}_{\underline{\nu}^{p+1}}^{[\alpha]}
. The fact is that although we have two independent normalizable modes, we must choose one out of these two to have vanishing symplectic flux at least in the absence of

The easy ones are,
\delta A^{[\beta]}=0

\displaystyle A_{\underline{\nu}^{p+1}}^{[\beta]}=J_{\underline{\nu}^{p+1}}^{[\beta]}(x,\hat{A}_{\underline{\nu}^{p+1}}^{[\alpha]})\qquad\text{or}\qquad\hat{A}_{\underline{\nu}^{p+1}}^{[\alpha]}=J_{\underline{\nu}^{p+1}}^{[\alpha]}(x,A_{\underline{\nu}^{p+1}}^{[\beta]})

where,

are ultra-local functions of
\hat{A}^{[\alpha]}
and

Vanishing the symplectic flux then indicates the existence of the following potentials in each case,

\displaystyle J^{\underline{\nu}^{p+1}}_{[\beta]}=\frac{(p+1)!}{c_{p}\sqrt{h}}\frac{\delta W_{[\beta]}}{\delta\hat{A}_{\underline{\nu}^{p+1}}^{[\alpha]}},\qquad\qquad J^{\underline{\nu}^{p+1}}_{[\alpha]}=\frac{(p+1)!}{c_{p}\sqrt{h}}\frac{\delta W_{[\alpha]}}{\delta{A}_{\underline{\nu}^{p+1}}^{[\beta]}}

Spherical Harmonics

-forms under Hodge duality

Let us start by some basic definitions. Consider the inner product of
p
-forms on a compact
n
-manifold

\langle\alpha,\beta\rangle=\int_{\mathcal{M}}\alpha\wedge\star\beta\,.

(D.149)

The adjoint of the exterior derivative
d
with respect to this inner product is called the co-differential operator
\text{d}^{\dagger}
, related to the exterior derivative by

\text{d}^{\dagger}=(-1)^{n(p+1)+1}\star\text{d}\star\,.

(D.150)

The Laplace-Beltrami operator
\Delta
is a second-order differential operator, mapping
p
-forms to
p
-forms

\Delta=\text{d}\text{d}^{\dagger}+\text{d}^{\dagger}\text{d}\,.

(D.151)

A differential form
\alpha
is called harmonic if
\Delta\alpha=\text{d}\alpha=\text{d}^{\dagger}\alpha=0\,.
According to the Hodge decomposition theorem, any differential
p
-form on a closed Riemannian manifold can be decomposed into exact, co-exact and harmonic forms:

\omega_{p}=\text{d}\alpha_{p-1}+\extdm\!^{\dagger}\beta_{p+1}+\gamma_{p}\,.

(D.152)

The number of harmonic
p
-forms on
\mathcal{M}
is equal to the dimension of the
p
-th de Rham cohomology group
\mathbf{H}_{\text{dR}}^{p}(\mathcal{M})
.

Here we obtain the Hodge dual of
\mathbb{T}_{\underline{i}^{p}}

-forms in terms themselves which is useful to obtain the relations (
5.96
). This can be easily seen in form notation. First note that we have

. So

is an eigen-form of

Since we assumed that

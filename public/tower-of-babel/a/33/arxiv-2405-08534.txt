Status of Dark Photons
James M. Cline

Abstract
I review recent developments in the field of dark photons—here taken to be U(1) gauge bosons with mass less than the Z Z —including both kinetically mixed vectors and those that couple to anomaly-free U(1)’s. Distinctions between Higgs and Stueckelberg masses are highlighted, with discussion of swampland constraints, UV completions, and new experimental search strategies.



1. Introduction.
As we have often been reminded, the possible
range of viable dark matter (DM) masses spans many orders of magnitude, and
this is also true for DM in the form of U(1) vector gauge bosons. The
heavier version is usually called a
Z^{\prime}
in the literature, whereas
lighter ones are called dark photons. I take the the
Z^{0}
mass to be
the upper boundary in this review of dark photons, while below the
lower limit
\sim 10^{-22}
eV, such particles would have too long a
de Broglie wavelength to function as dark matter in galaxies.
[
1
]

Below this limit, dark photons could still play an
interesting role in a larger dark sector, such as binding dark
atoms and giving rise to millicharged dark matter (see Ref.
[
2
]
for a review). Here I will not discuss massless
dark photons except as an approximation to massive ones in the case
where the masses are negligible relative to relevant energy scales.

Hence, the dark photon could play the role of dark matter, dark force
mediator, or possibly both. There are two ways in which it could
interact with the standard model: through kinetic mixing, which is
generically expected to be present, or by coupling to an anomaly-free
U(1) such
B-L
or a difference between two lepton flavors.

We first recall how kinetic mixing works.
[
3
]

The Lagrangian for the SM U(1) gauge boson (either hypercharge, above
the electroweak symmetry breaking scale, or electromagnetism below it)
plus the dark one is

{\cal L}=-{\textstyle\frac{1}{4}}F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu}-{\textstyle\frac{1}{4}}F^{\prime}_{\mu\nu}F^{\prime\mu\nu}-{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}\epsilon F_{\mu\nu}F^{\prime\mu\nu}-{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}m_{A^{\prime}}^{2}A^{\prime}_{\mu}A^{\prime\mu}\,.

(1)

If
m_{A^{\prime}}
is nonzero, the kinetic term is diagonalized (up to

O(\epsilon^{2})
corrections) by
A_{\mu}\to A_{\mu}-\epsilon A^{\prime}_{\mu}
.
Then the dark photon will couple to particles of charge
qe
in the SM with
strength
\epsilon qe
.
1
1

1

If
m_{A^{\prime}}=0
, one can instead
diagonalize the kinetic term using
A^{\prime}_{\mu}\to A^{\prime}_{\mu}+\epsilon A_{\mu}
. In this case, dark sector
particles coupling to
A^{\prime}
become millicharged. In the limit of
small
m_{A^{\prime}}
, where
A^{\prime}
can be approximated as massless, for example
in a stellar environment, the dark Higgs looks
millicharged, which leads to strong constraints.

It is interesting to notice that citations of Holdom’s seminal paper
started to rise steeply in 2008, after the appearance of Refs.
[
4
,
5
]
, that highlighted the importance of kinetic mixing in extended
dark sectors.

3. Higgs or Stueckelberg mass?

In presenting the previous bounds, it was implicitly assumed that the

A^{\prime}
gets its mass by the Stueckelberg “mechanism,” which
essentially means putting in a bare mass term as in Eq. (
1
)
with no other degrees of freedom. One can restore gauge invariance by
introducing a fictitious field
\theta
and rewriting the mass term
as

{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}m_{A^{\prime}}^{2}(A^{\prime\mu}-\partial^{\mu}\theta)^{2}\,,

(2)

but this by
itself has no additional physical significance. On the other hand,
if
A^{\prime}
gets its mass from a dark scalar
h^{\prime}
by the Higgs mechanism, one
might expect
m_{h^{\prime}}\sim m_{A^{\prime}}
. If this is the case, then
h^{\prime}

behaves like a light millicharged particle, if
m_{A^{\prime}}
is small
compared to the energy scale of interest. There is a vertex
g^{\prime}m_{A^{\prime}}h^{\prime}A^{\prime 2}
which leads to
2\epsilon g^{\prime}m_{A^{\prime}}A^{\prime\mu}A_{\mu}
in
conjunction with kinetic mixing. This yields much stronger
constraints on
\epsilon
, ruling out large regions that were allowed
for the Stueckelberg case, as shown in Fig.
3
. For example,
stellar cooling is accelerated by Brehmsstrahlung of
h^{\prime}
from
photons.
[
16
]
Further limits arise from production
of
h^{\prime}
in DM direct detection experiments through
ionization of atoms in the detector by
A^{\prime}
produced in the sun.
[
17
]

These limits however are
model-dependent, because of the assumption
m_{h^{\prime}}\sim m_{A^{\prime}}
and

g^{\prime}\sim e
.

Typically, phenomenological studies assume that there is no analog of
the Higgs field corresponding to the Stueckelberg mechanism, but in
consistent UV completions, namely string theory, the field
\theta
in
(
2
) can be thought of as an axion-like degree of freedom,
that has an accompanying radial excitation
\phi
. Unlike the Higgs
mechanism, the
A^{\prime}
mass arises without
\phi
getting a VEV, and the
limit
\phi\to 0
in field space is singular because of a kinetic term
of the form
(\partial\ln\phi)^{2}
.
[
18
]
In contrast
to Higgs masses, the limit
m_{A^{\prime}}\to 0
is thus inconsistent in the
Stueckelberg case.

Light Stueckelberg masses can naturally arise in large volume
string compactifications, but there is a lower limit on the mass,
which is related to the dimensionless volume
{\cal V}
of the compactification
and thereby the string scale
m_{s}
and the Planck mass, through
m_{P}^{2}=(4\pi/g_{s}^{2}){\cal V}m_{s}^{2}
. The
A^{\prime}
mass is of order
[
19
]

m_{A^{\prime}}\sim g_{s}^{3/2}(m_{P}/{\cal V})\gtrsim g_{s}^{3/2}{\rm\,eV}\gtrsim 1{\,\rm eV}
,
using
m_{s}\gtrsim 1\,
TeV (from collider limits), hence
{\cal V}\lesssim 10^{27}
.

4. Origin of kinetic mixing.
The kinetic mixing operator

F_{\mu\nu}F^{\prime\mu\nu}
has dimension 4, so its coefficient
\epsilon

can be put in “by hand.” But usually one imagines that it gets
generated at some high scale by integrating out a heavy particle that
carries both U(1)
′
and SM U(1) charges. In that case, its natural
value is of order
eg^{\prime}/(16\pi^{2})
. To reconcile this with the
XENON1T limit would require
g^{\prime}<10^{-11}
, precluding gauge
unification. Otherwise, one could forbid matter charged under both
gauge groups, but this violates a strong version of the weak gravity
conjecture
[
21
]
(WGC, see below), which is supported by known string
compactifications.

If one ignores WGC and assumes
\epsilon=0
the Planck scale, then
pure gravity plus Higgs mediation can generate
\epsilon
through a
6-loop diagram, involving three graviton exchanges and one Higgs from
each (SM and dark) sector.
[
20
]
It is also
possible to evade the WCG constraints by having exchanges of several
heavy states in the loops whose contributions cancel each other.
This can happen naturally in string theory compactifications,
[
22
,
23
]

where the interaction that
generates the kinetic mixing is a closed string exchange between two

D
-branes, which host the respective
U(1)
matter fields (Fig.
4
). Small kinetic mixing can arise from large-volume
compactifications with
{\cal V}=(2\pi R)^{6}
(in string length units

\ell_{s}=2\pi\sqrt{\alpha^{\prime}}
), which are associated with a light
volume modulus of mass
[
24
]

m_{\cal V}\sim m_{P}{\cal V}^{-3/2}
.
The kinetic mixing is related to
m{\cal V}

by
[
23
,
19
]

\epsilon\gtrsim 10^{-16}\left(m_{\cal V}/2m_{h}\right)^{8/9}
,
where
m_{h}
is the SM Higgs mass. To avoid overclosure of the
Universe by such moduli, they must be heavy enough to decay into two
Higgs bosons, hence this gives a lower bound on
\epsilon
that is
close to the XENON1T constraint.

5. DM Relic density from dark photons.
In constrast to axions
or other light spin-0 DM candidates, the misalignment mechanism
(where the field is stuck at a nontrivial value while the Hubble rate

H
exceeds its mass, and starts oscillating in its potential
subsequently) does
not easily account for a significant dark photon relic density.
This is because vector fields in a nontrivial gravitational background
couple to the Ricci scalar in such a way as to be diluted by the
expansion of the Universe. One needs to introduce a nonminimal
coupling
RA^{\prime}_{\mu}A^{\prime\mu}
to counteract this effect,
[
9
,
25
]

but doing so leads to the
longitudinal polarization becoming ghost-like for some range of
momenta.
[
26
]
This can be overcome by modifying
the kinetic term by coupling it to a scalar field (the inflaton)
via
f(\phi)F^{\prime}_{\mu\nu}F^{\prime\mu\nu}
,
[
27
]
but this involves significantly
more model building than for a spin-0
particle, which only needs a quadratic potential for misalignment to
work.

Alternatively, a generic mechanism is through inflationary fluctuations of

A^{\prime}
, which like for any approximately massless field are of order

H_{I}
, the Hubble rate during inflation. Ref.
[
28
]
showed that
the desired relic density is achieved if

m_{A^{\prime}}\cong 6\times 10^{-6}{\rm\,eV}\left(10^{14}\,\rm GeV/H_{I}\right)^{4}
.
Another relatively simple mechanism is to couple dark photons to an
axion via
-(\beta/f_{a})aF^{\prime\mu\nu}\widetilde{F}^{\prime\mu\nu}
.
[
29
,
30
]

The axion can be produced by
misalignment; when it starts to oscillate, a tachyonic instability
quickly transfers energy from
a
to
A^{\prime}_{\mu}
over a wide range of
axion masses and
A^{\prime}
masses, spanning
m_{A^{\prime}}\sim(10^{-7}-10^{7})
eV.
A similar mechanism works for dark Higgs oscillations producing

A^{\prime}
pairs by parametric resonance.
[
31
]

Dark photons can also serve as the mediator that gives rise to thermal
relic dark matter through the classic processes
\chi\bar{\chi}\to A^{\prime}A^{\prime}

or
\chi\bar{\chi}\to A^{\prime*}\to f\bar{f}
. However there are many other
possible production pathways that were explored in Ref.
[
32
]
. For
example, kinetic mixing can be enhanced through resonant
A\to A^{\prime}

conversion at finite temperature, when
m_{A^{\prime}}
coincides with the
photon plasmon mass.

6. Swampland constraints.
The weak gravity conjecture has
strong evidence, and its variants are well motivated by string
theory/quantum gravity arguments. The magnetic WCG imples a UV cutoff

\Lambda>\sqrt{m_{P}m_{A^{\prime}}/g^{\prime}}
for Stueckelberg masses.
[
18
]

Since
\Lambda>H_{I}
during inflation for a
consistent description, the inflationary production mechanism for
A^{\prime}

becomes limited to masses
m_{A^{\prime}}\gtrsim 0.3\,
eV, a very significant
restriction. The WCG also demands that
\Lambda<g^{\prime}m_{P}
, which
combined with the generic loop estimate for
\epsilon
implies that

\Lambda\lesssim(16\pi^{2}/e)\epsilon m_{P}\sim 100\,
TeV (using the
XENON constraint on
\epsilon
),
[
33
]
suggesting new states would be accessible at the
Future Circular Collider. Similarly, combining the magnetic WGC with
the LHC bound
\Lambda\gtrsim 10\,
TeV and the loop estimate for

\epsilon
disfavors a large region of parameter space,
[
34
]

including interesting values
m_{A^{\prime}}\sim 10^{-11}
,
\epsilon\sim 10^{-7}
where axion decays
a\to A^{\prime}A^{\prime}

followed by
A^{\prime}\to A
resonant oscillations can boost the spectrum of
low-frequency CMB photons, suppressing the 21 cm signal as seen by the
EDGES collaboration.
[
35
,
36
]

7. Nonabelian kinetic mixing and Composite dark photons.
It was
realized early on
[
4
]
that the photon could kinetically mix with a
nonabelian gauge boson through the diagram

where
\Phi
gets a VEV and breaks the gauge symmetry. Then kinetic
mixing comes from a dimension-5 operator, giving
another possibility for suppressing
\epsilon
, without needing small

g^{\prime}
. It was pointed out
[
37
]
that this can also work in a confining
phase, where
\langle\Phi\rangle=0
; for example
\Phi
binds with the
dark gluon to form a massive composite dark photon
\tilde{A}
, with

\epsilon\sim\sqrt{\alpha\alpha^{\prime}}\,y_{X}\Lambda/(4\pi m_{X})
, where

\Lambda
is the dark confinement scale. An interesting feature is
that the kinetic mixing
\epsilon
can be correlated with
m_{\tilde{A}}\sim\Lambda
by their dependence on
\Lambda
(note that

\alpha^{\prime}\sim 1/\ln\Lambda
is not independent). Dark baryons provide
a natural DM candidate in such models, and can have direct detection
signals by mediation of
\tilde{A}
.

8. Gauging SM global symmetries.
In addition to kinetic mixing,

A^{\prime}
could couple directly to SM particles by gauging one of its
global symmetries. The simplest possibilities involve anomaly-free
combinations,
B-L
,
L_{e}-L_{\mu}
,
L_{e}-L_{\tau}
and
L_{\mu}-L_{\tau}

since then no further matter content is required (except possibly
right-handed neutrinos). If
\epsilon=0
at
a high scale, then it is calculable at lower scales.
[
38
]
For example

\epsilon\sim eg^{\prime}\ln(m_{\tau}/m_{\mu})/(4\pi^{2})\sim 0.036\,g^{\prime}
for gauged
L_{\mu}-L_{\tau}

at vanishing momentum transfer
q^{2}=0
; hence it makes a subdominant
contribution to observable processes.

Constraints on
g^{\prime}
versus
m_{A^{\prime}}
for the gauged models are shown in
Fig.
5
. They are most severe when
A^{\prime}
couples to
electrons, making
L_{\mu}-L_{\tau}
a special case. In fact, it is the
only one that can explain the muon
g-2
anomaly (through one-loop
dressing of the muon vertex by
A^{\prime}
exchange), illustrated by the
narrow pink band. For the same reason, kinetically mixed models are
also ruled out for
(g-2)_{\mu}
(see Ref.
[
39
]
for a loophole).

From a model-building
standpoint, it might seem arbitrary to gauge only
L_{\mu}-L_{\tau}
as
opposed to other flavor combinations, but Ref.
[
40
]
showed that such a vector boson could be
just the lightest of eight such, coming from the gauging of SU(3)
lepton flavor, broken at the
(5-10)
TeV scale, except for one
scalar in the 6 representation with VEV
(20-200)
GeV to explain the
lightest vector mass. The model predicts heavy neutral leptons at the
GeV scale, constrained by BBN, and that the lightest neutrino has mass

m_{\nu_{1}}\gtrsim 10^{-4}
eV. It can also address the
Cabbibo anomaly.

The
L_{\mu}-L_{\tau}
dark photon can have interesting implication for
neutrino physics if it is the dark matter. One can treat it as an
oscillating condensate which gives a time-dependent effective mass to
\nu_{\mu}
and

\nu_{\tau}
, with potential impact on neutrino oscillations. This can
re-open parameter space for sterile neutrino dark matter, by making

\nu_{s}
-
\nu_{\mu}
oscillations resonant and thereby making
\nu_{s}

production efficient at smaller mixing angles than ordinarily
required;
[
41
]
this is illustrated in Fig.
6
(left).
Similarly, this effect can impact oscillations between active neutrino
flavors,
[
42
,
43
,
44
]
giving
rise to leading constraints on the coupling, as shown in Fig.
6
(right). It was recently shown that
B-L
dark photons can be
probed through dark matter-neutrino scattering if the DM carries

B-L
, using IceCube observations of neutrinos from the active galaxy
NGC 1068.
[
45
]
Dark matter self-interactions mediated
by dark photons might also play a role in accelerating the mergers of supermassive
black holes,
[
46
]
to overcome the
long-standing “final parsec” problem.

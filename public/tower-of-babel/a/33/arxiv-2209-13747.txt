Abstract
. Rigid particles suspended on a micropolar fluid provide microstructure that coexists and interacts with the local rotation of the fluid given by the vorticity. In this work we prove that the particles’ angular velocity and the vorticity strongly align for large times. We provide average and supnorm estimates for the decay rate of the difference between these two vectors, which measures the alignment.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Equations for micropolar fluids

Micropolar fluids are an important type of non-Newtonian fluids with velocity
u
and pressure
p
which have “micro-structure”, given by microscopic particles suspended or diluted in the fluid. These rigid particles can rotate about their center of mass and have angular velocity
w
. Since its introduction by Eringen
[
13
]
, this fluid model has been extensely studied from a physical and mathematical point of view due to its importance in many applications.

We describe now the equations that model
3D
micropolar fluids. Given an initial incompressible velocity
\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{0}\in L^{2}_{\sigma}
and an initial micro-rotation
\mbox{\boldmath$w$}_{0}\in L^{2}
, we consider a solution
(\mbox{\boldmath$u$},\mbox{\boldmath$w$})
, in the Leray-Hopf sense, of the micropolar fluid equations

(1.1)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}+\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\cdot\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$u$}+\nabla p

\displaystyle=(\mu+\chi)\Delta\mbox{\boldmath$u$}+2\chi\nabla\wedge\mbox{\boldmath$w$},

(1.2)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\mbox{\boldmath$w$}+\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\cdot\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$w$}

\displaystyle=\nu\Delta\mbox{\boldmath$w$}+\kappa\nabla(\nabla\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$w$})-4\chi\mbox{\boldmath$w$}+2\chi\nabla\wedge\mbox{\boldmath$u$},

(1.3)

div

u

\displaystyle=0,

see Eringen
[
13
]
, Galdi and Rionero
[
14
]
, Lukaszewicz
[
23
]
, Yuan
[
32
]
. The coefficients
\mu
(kinematic viscosity),
\nu
(angular viscosity) and
\chi
(vortex or micro-rotation viscosity) are positive, and
\kappa
(gyroviscosity) is nonnegative, all of them assumed to be constant. Leray-Hopf (or simply Leray) solutions are global mappings

\mbox{\boldmath$z$}=(\mbox{\boldmath$u$},\mbox{\boldmath$w$})\in C_{\sf w}\left([0,\infty),L^{2}_{\sigma}\times L^{2}\right)\cap L^{2}\left((0,\infty),\dot{H}^{1}\times\dot{H}^{1}\right)

with
\mbox{\boldmath$z$}_{0}(x)=\mbox{\boldmath$z$}(x,0)=(\mbox{\boldmath$u$},\mbox{\boldmath$w$})(x,0)=(\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{0}(x),\mbox{\boldmath$w$}_{0}(x))
that satisfy the equations in weak sense for
t>0
and also the energy estimate

(1.4)

\|\mbox{\boldmath$z$}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\int_{s}^{t}\left(\mu\|D\mbox{\boldmath$u$}(\tau)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\nu\|D\mbox{\boldmath$w$}(\tau)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)\,d\tau\leq\|\mbox{\boldmath$z$}(s)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

for all
t>s
and a.e.
s>0
(see Section
2.1
for notation). The existence of Leray solutions for the equations (
1.1
) - (
1.3
) and other similar dissipative systems is well known,
but their uniqueness and exact regularity properties are still open except for small initial data in suitable spaces, see Boldrini and Rojas-Medar
[

[

]

[

]

[

[

\|(\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{0},\mbox{\boldmath$w$}_{0})\|^{\frac{1}{2}}_{L^{2}}\|(D\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{0},D\mbox{\boldmath$w$}_{0})\|^{\frac{1}{2}}_{L^{2}}\leq 3.182\cdot\min\,\{\mu,\nu\},

see (

1.2.
Physical motivation

We present now the ideas that motivate our study. From (
1.2
), it is clear that the fluid advects its microstructure through the term
\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\cdot\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$w$}
. Note that this is the only term in (
1.1
) and (
1.2
) in which
u
and
w
interact directly. As any vector field
u
can be infinitesimally decomposed as a translation, a deformation through shear and a rotation around an axis given by the vorticity
\frac{1}{2}\nabla\wedge\mbox{\boldmath$u$}
(see pages 19 - 21, Chorin and Marsden
[
5
]
), we would like to understand how the vorticity and the micro-rotation compare to each other at a common “micro” scale. This makes sense from the dimensional point of view, as both vectors are proportional to the vortex viscosity
\chi>0
in (
1.2
). From Braz e Silva, Cruz, Freitas and P. Zingano
[
3
]
, Cruz and Novaes
[
7
]
, Niche and Perusato
[
24
]
, Cruz, Perusato, Rojas-Medar and P. Zingano
[
6
]
, we know that

\|\mbox{\boldmath$w$}(t)\|_{L^{2}},\|\nabla\wedge\mbox{\boldmath$u$}(t)\|_{L^{2}}=O\left(t^{-\left(\alpha+\frac{1}{2}\right)}\right),

for the same
\alpha>0
, which depends on the regularity of the initial data. Moreover, for a certain range of values of
\alpha
, we also have

\|\mbox{\boldmath$w$}(t)\|_{L^{2}},\|\nabla\wedge\mbox{\boldmath$u$}(t)\|_{L^{2}}\geq C\,t^{-\left(\alpha+\frac{1}{2}\right)}

see Niche and Perusato
[
24
]
. Then, in order to be able to provide a more precise meaning to the qualitative assertion
\mbox{\boldmath$w$}\approx\frac{1}{2}\nabla\wedge\mbox{\boldmath$u$}
, we need to find a good estimate for the difference

(1.9)

\mbox{\boldmath$\varepsilon$}(x,t)=\mbox{\boldmath$w$}(x,t)-\frac{1}{2}\nabla\wedge\mbox{\boldmath$u$}(x,t).

1.3.
Main results

We now describe our main results. We are concerned throughout this paper with Leray solutions
\mbox{\boldmath$z$}(x,t)=\left(\mbox{\boldmath$u$}(x,t),\mbox{\boldmath$w$}(x,t)\right)
such that the velocity
u
satisfies

(1.10)

\|\mbox{\boldmath$u$}(t)\|_{L^{2}}\leq C_{0}\,t^{-\alpha},\qquad\forall\,t\geq T_{0},

for some constants
\alpha,T_{0},C_{0}=C_{0}\left(\|\mbox{\boldmath$z$}_{0}\|_{L^{2}},\alpha,T_{0}\right)\geq 0
. For the existence of solutions satisfying (
1.10
) with
\alpha>0
, see Braz e Silva, Cruz, Freitas and P. Zingano
[
3
]
, Braz e Silva, Guterres, Perusato and P. Zingano
[
10
]
, Dong and Chen
[
8
]
, Niche and M.E. Schonbek
[
25
]
.

We first state our results concerning upper bounds for
\mbox{\boldmath$u$},\mbox{\boldmath$w$},\mbox{\boldmath$\varepsilon$}
and its derivatives.

Theorem 1.1
.

If (
1.10
) is valid for some
\alpha\geq 0
, then we have that for
m\geq 1

(1.11)

\|D^{m}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}(t)\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\,\mu^{-\frac{m}{2}}\,t^{-\left(\alpha+\frac{m}{2}\right)},\qquad\forall\,t\geq T_{m},

where
C=C(C_{0},m,\alpha)
and
T_{m}>t_{\ast\ast}
sufficiently large, see Remark
1.4
for the dependence of
T_{m}
on the constants appearing in the problem. Moreover, we have that for every
m\geq 0

(1.12)

\|D^{m}\mbox{\boldmath$w$}(t)\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\,\mu^{-\frac{m+1}{2}}\,t^{-\left(\alpha+\frac{m+1}{2}\right)},\qquad\forall\,t\geq T^{\prime}_{m},

where
C=C(C_{0},m+1,\alpha)
and
T^{\prime}_{m}>t_{\ast\ast}
sufficiently large, see Remark
1.4
.

Theorem 1.2
.

If (
1.10
) is valid for some
\alpha\geq 0
, then we have that for
m\geq 0

(1.13)

\|D^{m}\mbox{\boldmath$\varepsilon$}(t)\|_{L^{2}}\leq C_{1}\,|\mu-\nu|\,t^{-\left(\alpha+\frac{m+3}{2}\right)}+C_{2}\,t^{-\left(2\alpha+\frac{m+3}{2}+\frac{1}{4}\right)},\qquad\forall\,t\geq T^{\prime\prime}_{m},

where
C_{i}
are
C_{i}=C_{i}(C_{0},m,\alpha,\mu,\chi),i=1,2
and
T^{\prime\prime}_{m}>t_{\ast\ast}

1.4
.

We emphasize the fact that the constants in (

\mu

\chi
and
\nu
, see also Section

Remark 1.4

In Theorems
1.1

1.2

and

in (
1.10

Remark 1.5

From (

From (

1.4.
Sketch of the proof

We describe now the main ideas behind the proof of our estimates. As solutions are eventually regular, i.e. they are
C^{\infty}
smooth for large enough times, all of our estimates are rigorous. One of the main tools used in our proofs is the monotonicity property for
\|D\mbox{\boldmath$z$}(t)\|_{L^{2}}
in Theorem
2.1
, which can be extended to
\|D^{m}\mbox{\boldmath$z$}(t)\|_{L^{2}}
, see Theorem
2.4
. From these, the decay estimate

\|D^{m}\mbox{\boldmath$z$}(t)\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\,t^{-\frac{m}{2}},m\geq 1,

can be proved, see Theorems
2.2
and
2.6
. This, in turn, leads to

\|D^{m}\mbox{\boldmath$w$}(t)\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\,t^{-\frac{m+1}{2}},m\geq 0,

as seen in Theorem
2.7
. Note that the constants above depende on
m,C_{0},\mu,\nu,\chi
.

The proof of these preliminary estimates leads us to notice that faster decay rates may be obtained if we have further information concerning
\|\mbox{\boldmath$z$}(t)\|_{L^{2}}
. This is due to the finer estimate

(1.17)

\|D^{m}\mbox{\boldmath$z$}(t)\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\,\|\mbox{\boldmath$z$}(t/2)\|_{L^{2}}\,t^{-\frac{m}{2}},\quad\|D^{m}\mbox{\boldmath$w$}(t)\|_{L^{2}}\leq\,C\|\mbox{\boldmath$z$}(t/2)\|_{L^{2}}\,t^{-\frac{(m+1)}{2}},

see Remark
2.8
. From assumption (
1.10
), i.e.
\|\mbox{\boldmath$u$}(t)\|_{L^{2}}\leq C_{0}\,t^{-\alpha},\alpha>0
, we deduce that
\|\mbox{\boldmath$w$}(t)\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\,t^{-\alpha}
, which leads to Theorem
3.1
, that is, for
m\geq 0

\|D^{m}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}(t)\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\,t^{-\left(\alpha+\frac{m}{2}\right)},\quad\|D^{m}\mbox{\boldmath$w$}(t)\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\,t^{-\left(\alpha+\frac{m+1}{2}\right)}.

This is a first version of Theorem
1.1
, but now the constant
C
depends on
\gamma=\min\{\mu,\nu\}
, instead of just
\mu
.

A second consequence of Remark
2.8
and (
1.17
) is Theorem
3.2
, which provides a preliminary estimate for the decay of
\varepsilon
,

\|D^{m}\mbox{\boldmath$\varepsilon$}(t)\|_{L^{2}}\leq Ct^{-\left(\alpha+\frac{m+3}{2}\right)}.

This result, together with a new monotonicity estimate (
3.11
) in Theorem
3.3
, leads to the proof of Theorem
1.1
. In turn, the estimates in this last result and in Theorem
3.2
, lead to the proof of Theorem
1.2
. Finally, (
3.11
) in Theorem
3.3
, Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
3.2
, imply Theorem
1.7
.

2.
Mathematical preliminaries

2.1.
Summary of notations

Throughout this text
\langle\mbox{\boldmath$v$},\mbox{\boldmath$w$}\rangle
indicates the standard inner product of two vectors
v
and
w
in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, and
|\cdot|
is used to denote the absolute value of a scalar or the Euclidean norm of a vector. The usual Lebesgue spaces are denoted by
L^{p},1\leq p\leq\infty
;
\mbox{\boldmath$v$}=(v_{1},v_{2},v_{3})\in L^{p}
means that
v_{i}\in L^{p}
for every
1\leq i\leq 3
. By
\|\cdot\|_{L^{p}}
we mean the usual
L^{p}
norm; for vector functions,

\|\mbox{\boldmath$v$}\|^{p}_{L^{p}}=\|(v_{1},\cdots,v_{k})\|^{p}_{L^{p}}=\sum_{i=1}^{3}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|v_{i}(x)|^{p}\,dx

if
1\leq p<\infty
, and

\|\mbox{\boldmath$v$}\|_{L^{\infty}}=\esssup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}}\{|\left(v_{1}(x),\cdots,v_{3}(x)\right|\},

if
p=\infty
. Similarly, if
1\leq p<\infty
and
v=(v_{1},v_{2},v_{3})
, we have

\|D\mbox{\boldmath$v$}\|_{L^{p}}^{p}=\sum_{i\,j\,=\,1}^{3}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|D_{j}v_{i}(x)|^{p}\,dx,

and, for general
m\geq 1

\|D^{m}\mbox{\boldmath$v$}\|_{L^{p}}^{p}=\sum_{i\,=\,1}^{3}\sum_{j_{1}\,=\,1}^{3}\sum_{j_{2}\,=\,1}^{3}\cdots\sum_{j_{m}\,=\,1}^{3}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|D_{j_{1}}D_{j_{2}}\cdots D_{j_{m}}v_{i}(x)|^{p}\,dx,

where
D_{j}=\partial/\partial x_{j},D_{j}D_{\ell}=\partial^{2}/\partial x_{j}\partial x_{\ell}
, and so forth, while

\|D\mbox{\boldmath$v$}\|_{L^{\infty}}=\max\{\esssup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}}|D_{j}v_{i}(x)|:1\leq j\leq 3\},

and, more generally

\|D^{m}\mbox{\boldmath$v$}\|_{L^{\infty}}=\max\{\esssup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}}|D_{j_{1}}\cdots D_{j_{m}}v_{i}(x)|:1\leq j_{1},\cdots,j_{m}\leq 3\},

for
m\geq 1
, if
p=\infty
. In the text, we will only use
p=2
or
p=\infty
. We will also be using
the Sobolev space
H^{m}
, i.e., the space of all those functions in
L^{2}
whose derivatives of order
m
are again in
L^{2}
. If
v=(v_{1},v_{2},v_{3})
, we write
\mbox{\boldmath$v$}\in H^{m}
when
\mbox{\boldmath$v$}_{i}\in H^{m}
for all
1\leq i\leq 3
. By
L^{2}_{\sigma}
we denote the space of vector functions
\mbox{\boldmath$v$}=(v_{1},v_{2},v_{3})\in L^{2}
with
\nabla\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$v$}=0
in distributional sense, that is,
\nabla\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$v$}=0
in
{\mathcal{D}}^{\prime}
and similarly
H^{m}_{\sigma}=\{\mbox{\boldmath$v$}\in L^{2}_{\sigma}:\mbox{\boldmath$v$}\in H^{m}\}
. Here,
\nabla\cdot
denotes the divergence operator; the curl and gradient are denoted by
\wedge
and
\nabla
respectively. Finally, we will also occasionally mention the homogeneous Sobolev space
\dot{H}^{m},m\geq 1
, that is, the space of tempered distributions with locally integrable Fourier transforms and whose distributional derivatives of order
m
are all in
L^{2}
.

where

Uniqueness and exact regularity properties of solutions are not known for arbitrary data, but the following result is available.

Theorem 2.1
.

Given
\mbox{\boldmath$z$}_{0}\in L^{2}_{\sigma}\times L^{2}
and Leray solutions
z
to (
1.1
) - (
1.3
), there exists
t_{\ast\ast}\geq 0
satisfying

t_{\ast\ast}\leq 0.005\cdot\gamma^{-5}\,\|\mbox{\boldmath$z$}_{0}\|^{4}_{L^{2}}

such that
\mbox{\boldmath$z$}\in C^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}^{3}\times(t_{\ast\ast},\infty)\right)
,
\mbox{\boldmath$z$}\in C^{\infty}\left((t_{\ast\ast},\infty),H^{m}\times H^{m}\right)
, for every
m\geq 0
and
\|D\mbox{\boldmath$z$}(t)\|_{L^{2}}
is monotonically decreasing in
(t_{\ast\ast},\infty)
.

Proof.
If
\mbox{\boldmath$z$}\in C\ \left([t_{0},T],H^{1}\right)
for some
0\leq t_{0}<T
, the solution is smooth in
\left(t_{0},T\right)
and differentiating (
1.1
) - (
1.2
) we obtain the energy estimate

\displaystyle\|D\mbox{\boldmath$z$}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle+2\gamma\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\|D^{2}\mbox{\boldmath$z$}(\tau)\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\,d\tau\leq\|D\mbox{\boldmath$z$}(t_{0})\|^{2}_{L^{2}}

(2.3)

\displaystyle+2\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\|D^{2}\mbox{\boldmath$z$}(\tau)\|_{L^{2}}\|\mbox{\boldmath$z$}(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}\|D\mbox{\boldmath$z$}(\tau)\|_{L^{2}}\,d\tau

for all
t\in\left(t_{0},T\right)
, where
\gamma
is as in (
2.1
). Note that

\displaystyle\|\mbox{\boldmath$z$}(\tau)\|_{L^{\infty}}\|D\mbox{\boldmath$z$}(\tau)\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq K\,\|\mbox{\boldmath$z$}(\tau)\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{4}}\,\|D\mbox{\boldmath$z$}(\tau)\|_{L^{2}}\,\|D^{2}\mbox{\boldmath$z$}(\tau)\|)_{L^{2}}^{\frac{3}{4}}

(2.4)

\displaystyle\leq K\,\|\mbox{\boldmath$z$}(\tau)\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\,\|D\mbox{\boldmath$z$}(\tau)\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\,\|D^{2}\mbox{\boldmath$z$}(\tau)\|_{L^{2}},

where
K=\frac{\sqrt[8]{12}}{\sqrt{6\pi}}
, see Theorem 2.2 in Schutz, Ziebell, J. Zingano and P. Zingano
[
29
]
. It then follows from (
2.2
) that
\|D\mbox{\boldmath$z$}(t)\|_{L^{2}}
is monotonically decreasing for
t\geq t_{0}
if we have

(2.5)

K\,\|\mbox{\boldmath$z$}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\,\|D\mbox{\boldmath$z$}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq\gamma.

In this case, we have
\mbox{\boldmath$z$}\in C^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}^{3}\times(t_{0},\infty)\right)
and
\mbox{\boldmath$z$}\in C^{\infty}\left((t_{0}\infty),H^{m}\times H^{m}\right)
for all
m\geq 0
, see Dong and Chen
[
9
]
, Eringen
[
13
]
, Galdi and Rionero
[
14
]
. Taking
\hat{t}>\frac{1}{2}\,K^{4}\,\gamma^{-5}\,\|\mbox{\boldmath$z$}_{0}\|^{4}_{L^{2}}
, from (
1.4
) we obtain

2\gamma\int_{0}^{\hat{t}}\|D\mbox{\boldmath$z$}(\tau)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\,d\tau\leq\|\mbox{\boldmath$z$}_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{2}},

so there exists a set
E\subset(0,\hat{t})
with positive Lebesgue measure such that for all
t^{\prime}\in E

\|D\mbox{\boldmath$z$}(t^{\prime})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\frac{1}{2\gamma}\,\hat{t}^{-1}\,\|\mbox{\boldmath$z$}_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}.

Therefore, for each
t^{\prime}\in E

\|\mbox{\boldmath$z$}(t^{\prime})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\,\|D\mbox{\boldmath$z$}(t^{\prime})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\|\mbox{\boldmath$z$}(t^{\prime})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\,\frac{1}{2\gamma}\,\hat{t}^{-1}\,\|\mbox{\boldmath$z$}_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\leq\frac{1}{2\gamma}\,\hat{t}^{-1}\,\|\mbox{\boldmath$z$}_{0}\|^{4}_{L^{2}}\leq K^{-4}\gamma^{4},

where the last estimate follows from the choice of
\hat{t}
. The result follows from (
2.5
).
\Box

The monotonicity of

Theorem 2.2

For any Leray solution

to (

) - (

\|D\mbox{\boldmath$z$}(t)\|_{L^{2}}\leq\gamma^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|\mbox{\boldmath$z$}_{0}\|_{L^{2}}\,t^{-\frac{1}{2}},\qquad t>2\,t_{\ast\ast},

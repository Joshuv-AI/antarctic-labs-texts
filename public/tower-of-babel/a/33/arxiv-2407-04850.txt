On the modified Zakharov–Kuznetsov equation on the cylinder: A trilinear Bourgain-space estimate and its application to local well-posedness
Ali Mezher

1.
Introduction

The modified Zakharov–Kuznetsov equation is a multidimensional dispersive
model of Korteweg–de Vries type in which the quadratic nonlinearity of the
Zakharov–Kuznetsov equation is replaced by a cubic one. In this work, we
consider the initial-value problem on the semi-periodic domain

{\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{T}}
,

(1.1)

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u+\partial_{x}\Delta u+\partial_{x}(u^{3})=0,\vskip 6.00006pt\\
u(0,x,y)=u_{0}(x,y),\end{cases}\qquad(x,y)\in{\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{T}}.

The semi-periodic geometry combines a continuous longitudinal frequency with
a discrete transverse frequency and consequently produces a resonance
structure that differs substantially from that of the purely Euclidean
problem. At the Fourier level, the linear evolution associated with
(
1.1
) is governed by the dispersion relation

(1.2)

\omega(\xi,q)=\xi^{3}+\xi q^{2},\qquad(\xi,q)\in{\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{Z}}.

Thus, the analysis of the derivative cubic nonlinearity requires simultaneous
control of spatial frequencies, modulation variables, and Sobolev weights,
while retaining the interaction between the continuous frequency
\xi
and
the discrete transverse mode
q
. The purpose of the present paper is to
establish and apply a Bourgain-space estimate on the full timeline, with input
modulation exponent
\frac{1}{2}+\delta
and output modulation exponent

-\frac{1}{2}+3\delta
. Direct trilinear Bourgain-space estimates for the same
cubic derivative term already appears in

[
8
,
24
]
; the precise relation between
those estimates and the estimate proved here are described below.

The Zakharov–Kuznetsov equation was introduced by Zakharov and Kuznetsov as
a multidimensional model for the propagation of nonlinear ion-acoustic waves
in magnetized plasmas
[
29
]
; related derivations and
physical aspects can be found in

[
16
,
14
]
. A rigorous derivation from the
Euler–Poisson system in the presence of a magnetic field was subsequently
obtained by Lannes, Linares, and Saut
[
17
]
. Modified
versions of the equation arise when the leading nonlinear interaction is
cubic rather than quadratic. In particular, Munro and Parkes derived a
modified Zakharov–Kuznetsov equation and studied the stability of its
solutions
[
22
]
; further dynamical aspects of the
modified equation were considered in
[
27
]
. From the
analytical point of view, this change in the nonlinear structure leads
naturally from the bilinear estimates associated with the quadratic ZK
nonlinearity to multilinear estimates capable of controlling a derivative
cubic interaction.

For sufficiently smooth solutions of (
1.1
), the mass
and Hamiltonian energy are conserved

(1.3)

\displaystyle M(u(t))

\displaystyle:=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{T}}}|u(t,x,y)|^{2}\,dx\,dy,

\displaystyle E(u(t))

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{2}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{T}}}|\nabla u(t,x,y)|^{2}\,dx\,dy-\frac{1}{4}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{T}}}|u(t,x,y)|^{4}\,dx\,dy.

Consequently, the energy space
H^{1}({\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{T}})
plays a distinguished role in the dynamics. On
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, the modified equation possesses the scaling

u_{\lambda}(t,x)=\lambda u(\lambda^{3}t,\lambda x),

under which

\left\lVert u_{\lambda}(0)\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{s}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}=\lambda^{s+1-\frac{d}{2}}\left\lVert u_{0}\right\rVert_{\dot{H}^{s}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}.

Hence the Euclidean scaling-critical Sobolev index is

s_{c}=\frac{d}{2}-1.

The isotropic Euclidean scaling, however, does not preserve

{\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{T}}
, since it changes the period in the transverse variable. Therefore, on the cylinder, scaling alone does not determine the expected
regularity threshold. The mixed continuous-discrete resonance geometry
becomes an essential part of the low-regularity analysis.

The well-posedness theory for the modified equation on Euclidean spaces has
developed through a sequence of increasingly refined dispersive and
multilinear estimates. Biagioni and Linares established local well-posedness
for the two-dimensional mZK equation in
H^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})

[
3
]
. Linares and Pastor subsequently proved local
well-posedness in
H^{s}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
for
s>\frac{3}{4}
and showed that well-posedness
cannot hold in the scaling-critical space
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})

[
18
]
. They also obtained global results for the
two-dimensional generalized ZK equation; in the cubic case, under the
appropriate mass restriction in the focusing regime, they proved global
well-posedness for
s>\frac{53}{63}

[
19
]
. Ribaud and Vento lowered the local threshold for
the two-dimensional cubic generalized ZK equation to

s>\frac{1}{4}

[
26
]
, while related local, global, and
scattering results for generalized ZK equations were developed in

[
9
]
. Kinoshita subsequently reached the endpoint

s=\frac{1}{4}
for the two-dimensional modified equation and established the
sharpness of this regularity in terms of the smoothness of the flow map

[
15
]
. In three dimensions, Grünrock proved local
well-posedness for
s>\frac{1}{2}

[
12
]
. Global
low-regularity theory in two dimensions was
further developed by Bhattacharya, Farah, and Roudenko using the
I
-method

[
2
]
. More recently, Correia and Kinoshita introduced
anisotropic Sobolev-type spaces adapted to the dispersive geometry of the
two-dimensional modified equation and established sharp local and small-data
global scattering results in that framework

[
6
]
.

The semi-periodic problem has a different history. For the original
Zakharov–Kuznetsov equation on
{\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{T}}
, Linares, Pastor, and Saut
initiated the well-posedness theory on the cylinder and obtained local
well-posedness in
H^{s}({\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{T}})
for
s>\frac{3}{2}

[
20
]
. Molinet and Pilod subsequently developed bilinear
Strichartz estimates adapted to the ZK dispersion relation and obtained,
among other results, global well-posedness in the energy space

H^{1}({\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{T}})

[
21
]
. Osawa extended the local
theory below the energy level, proving local well-posedness for

\frac{9}{10}<s<1

[
25
]
. More recently, Cao-Labora refined
the analysis of the resonant set and proved local well-posedness for arbitrary

H^{s}({\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{T}})
data when
s>\frac{3}{4}
and, under an additional
low-frequency condition on the initial data, when
s>\frac{1}{2}

[
5
]
. These developments illustrate a characteristic feature of
semi-periodic dispersive equations: the mixture of a continuous and a
discrete Fourier variable creates resonant configurations that require
estimates specifically adapted to the geometry of the cylinder.

For higher-order nonlinearities on
{\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{T}}
, Farah and Molinet developed
a local and global theory for the generalized Zakharov–Kuznetsov equation

[
8
]
. In the modified case, corresponding to the cubic
nonlinearity, their Bourgain-space argument yields local well-posedness for

s>\frac{5}{6}
, together with global results in the energy space under
appropriate smallness assumptions on the initial data. More recently,
Nowicki-Koth established an almost optimal linear
L^{4}
Strichartz estimate
and a family of bilinear refinements adapted to the mixed
continuous-discrete Fourier geometry. As an application, the local
well-posedness threshold for the semi-periodic modified
Zakharov–Kuznetsov equation was lowered to
s>{11}/{24}
; see
[
24
]
.

In subsequent work, these estimates were combined with the
I
-method to obtain global well-posedness for initial data satisfying

(1.4)

\left\lVert u_{0}\right\rVert_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{T}})}<\left\lVert Q_{2}\right\rVert_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})},

where
Q_{2}
denotes
the two-dimensional ground state, whenever
s>{36}/{49}
; see
[
23
]
.

Thus, the currently available
low-regularity theory on the cylinder exploits a delicate combination of
linear Strichartz estimates, bilinear smoothing, resonance analysis, and the
arithmetic structure of the transverse frequencies.

Direct trilinear Bourgain-space estimates for the cubic derivative term are
already available in the literature. In the modified case
k=2
,

[
8
, Proposition 3.3]
implies, after placing all three inputs
at the same spatial regularity, that for every
s>\frac{5}{6}
and sufficiently
small
\varepsilon>0
,

(1.5)

\left\|\partial_{x}(u_{1}u_{2}u_{3})\right\|_{X^{s,-\frac{1}{2}+2\varepsilon}}\lesssim\prod_{j=1}^{3}\left\lVert u_{j}\right\rVert_{X^{s,\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}}.

More recently,
[
24
, Proposition 4.3]
established
the same symmetric modulation balance for
s>\frac{11}{24}
under a
time-localization assumption on the inputs.

The estimate proved in the present paper has a different modulation balance
and is stated on the full timeline. More precisely, for every
s>1
, there
exists
\delta=\delta(s)>0
such that

(1.6)

\left\|\partial_{x}(u_{1}u_{2}u_{3})\right\|_{X^{s,-\frac{1}{2}+3\delta}}\lesssim\prod_{j=1}^{3}\left\lVert u_{j}\right\rVert_{X^{s,\frac{1}{2}+\delta}}.

For the same positive parameter, the target space in
(
1.6
) has a higher modulation exponent than the
target space in (
1.5
). This exact balance
does not follow from the preceding estimates merely by monotonicity of
Bourgain norms: applying an estimate with parameter
\varepsilon
to inputs
known only to belong to
X^{s,\frac{1}{2}+\delta}
requires

\varepsilon\leq\delta
, whereas control of the target norm in
(
1.6
) would require

2\varepsilon\geq 3\delta
. On the other hand, the condition
s>1
is more
restrictive than the spatial regularity ranges in the cited works.

The proof of (
1.6
) uses a dyadic decomposition
of both spatial frequencies and modulations. After ordering the input
frequencies, we consider the six regimes low–low–low
\to
low,
high–low–low
\to
high, high–high–low
\to
low,
high–high–low
\to
high, high–high–high
\to
low, and
high–high–high
\to
high. Frequency-separated interactions are controlled
by first-derivative bounds for the resonance function. In the
high–high–high
\to
high regime, we retain a dyadic pair-frequency
parameter
K
and use a quadratic-phase sublevel-set estimate. The underlying

K^{-\frac{1}{4}}
bilinear gain is the pair-frequency-localized form of the
high-output-frequency estimate appearing in the proof of

[
8
, Proposition 3.1]
. In the present argument, that

K
-dependence is retained, and the bilinear estimate is applied to two paired
convolutions in the dual formulation of the trilinear bound.

The condition
s>1
is the threshold at which the dyadic summations in this
particular proof close; no claim of optimality is made. Combined with the
inhomogeneous Bourgain-space estimate, the output exponent

-\frac{1}{2}+3\delta
produces the factor
T^{2\delta}
used in the contraction
argument below.

As an application of (
1.6
), together with the
standard homogeneous and inhomogeneous linear estimates in Bourgain spaces,
we obtain local well-posedness of (
1.1
) in

H^{s}({\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{T}})
for every
s>1
. The solution is constructed in the
restricted Bourgain space
X_{T}^{s,\frac{1}{2}+\delta}
, which embeds continuously
into
C([0,T];H^{s}({\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{T}}))
. The same framework yields uniqueness in the
full restricted Bourgain class. Finally, the analytic structure of the
nonlinear mapping, together with the implicit function theorem in Banach
spaces, gives real-analytic dependence of the solution on the initial data.
We now state the main result.

Theorem 1.1
.

Assume that
s>1
. Then there exists
\delta=\delta(s)>0
such that, for
every
u_{0}\in H^{s}({\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{T}})
, there exists

0<T=T\left(\left\lVert u_{0}\right\rVert_{H^{s}({\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{T}})}\right)

for which the initial-value problem
(
1.1
) admits a unique solution

u\in C\left([0,T];H^{s}({\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{T}})\right)\cap X_{T}^{s,\frac{1}{2}+\delta}.

Moreover, for every
T^{\prime}\in(0,T)
, there exists
r>0
such that, with

B_{r}(u_{0}):=\left\{v_{0}\in H^{s}({\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{T}}):\left\lVert v_{0}-u_{0}\right\rVert_{H^{s}({\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{T}})}<r\right\},

the data-to-solution map

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{r}:B_{r}(u_{0})

\displaystyle\longrightarrow C\left([0,T^{\prime}];H^{s}({\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{T}})\right)\cap X_{T^{\prime}}^{s,\frac{1}{2}+\delta},

\displaystyle v_{0}

\displaystyle\longmapsto v

is real-analytic.

The restricted Bourgain space
X_{T}^{s,b}
is defined in
Section
2
.

3.
The trilinear estimate on the cylinder

We use the following elementary one-dimensional sublevel-set estimate; see
also
[
21
, Lemma 3.8]
.

Lemma 3.1

(One-dimensional sublevel-set estimate)
.

Let
I,J\subset{\mathbb{R}}
be intervals and let
f\in C^{1}(J;{\mathbb{R}})
. If

\lambda:=\inf_{x\in J}\left|f^{\prime}(x)\right|>0,

then

(3.1)

\left|\left\{x\in J:f(x)\in I\right\}\right|\leq\frac{\left|I\right|}{\lambda}.

Proof.

Since
f^{\prime}
is continuous and does not vanish on the interval
J
, it has a fixed sign there. Thus
f
is one-to-one on
J
, and the estimate follows
from the change-of-variables formula.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
N_{i},L_{i}
,
i=1,2,3
, be dyadic numbers in

\left\{2^{k}:k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{+}\cup\left\{0\right\}\right\}
, and assume that

u_{i}(t,x,y)\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{T}})
. We denote by
N_{\min}
and
N_{\mathrm{med}}
the
minimum and median, respectively, of
N_{1},N_{2},N_{3}
, and define

L_{\min}
,
L_{\mathrm{med}}
, and
L_{\max}
analogously. Then

(3.2)

\left\lVert\prod_{j=1}^{3}{P_{N_{j}}Q_{L_{j}}u_{j}}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\lesssim L_{\min}^{\frac{1}{2}}L_{\mathrm{med}}^{\frac{1}{2}}N_{\min}N_{\mathrm{med}}\prod_{j=1}^{3}\left\lVert P_{N_{j}}Q_{L_{j}}u_{j}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}.

Moreover,

(I)

If
N_{1}\geq 4N_{3}
or
N_{3}\geq 4N_{1}
, then

(3.3)

\left\lVert\prod_{j=1}^{3}{P_{N_{j}}Q_{L_{j}}u_{j}}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\lesssim(N_{\min}N_{\mathrm{med}}L_{1}L_{2}L_{3})^{\frac{1}{2}}\frac{N_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}}{N_{1}\lor N_{3}}\prod_{j=1}^{3}\left\lVert P_{N_{j}}Q_{L_{j}}u_{j}\right\rVert_{L^{2}},

(II)

If
N_{2}\geq 4N_{3}
or
N_{3}\geq 4N_{2}
, then

(3.4)

\left\lVert\prod_{j=1}^{3}{P_{N_{j}}Q_{L_{j}}u_{j}}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\lesssim(N_{\min}N_{\mathrm{med}}L_{1}L_{2}L_{3})^{\frac{1}{2}}\frac{N_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}}{N_{2}\lor N_{3}}\prod_{j=1}^{3}\left\lVert P_{N_{j}}Q_{L_{j}}u_{j}\right\rVert_{L^{2}},

(III)

If
N_{1}\geq 4N_{2}
or
N_{2}\geq 4N_{1}
, then

(3.5)

\left\lVert\prod_{j=1}^{3}{P_{N_{j}}Q_{L_{j}}u_{j}}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\lesssim(N_{\min}N_{\mathrm{med}}L_{1}L_{2}L_{3})^{\frac{1}{2}}\frac{N_{3}^{\frac{1}{2}}}{N_{1}\lor N_{2}}\prod_{j=1}^{3}\left\lVert P_{N_{j}}Q_{L_{j}}u_{j}\right\rVert_{L^{2}},

Proof.

By Plancherel’s identity, we have

\displaystyle\left\lVert\prod_{j=1}^{3}P_{N_{j}}Q_{L_{j}}u_{j}\right\rVert_{L^{2}_{txy}}

\displaystyle=\left\lVert\mathcal{F}\left(\prod_{j=1}^{3}P_{N_{j}}Q_{L_{j}}u_{j}\right)\right\rVert_{L^{2}_{\tau\xi q}}

\displaystyle=\left\|\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{4}}\sum_{(q_{1},q_{2})\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}}\prod_{j=1}^{3}\widehat{P_{N_{j}}Q_{L_{j}}u_{j}}(\tau_{j},\xi_{j},q_{j})\,d\nu\right\|_{L^{2}_{\tau,\xi,q}},

where
\circledast_{j=1}^{n}u_{j}:=u_{1}\ast\cdots\ast u_{n}
,
\ast
denotes
convolution,
d\nu:=d\tau_{1}\,d\tau_{2}\,d\xi_{1}\,d\xi_{2}
,

\tau=\tau_{1}+\tau_{2}+\tau_{3}
,
\xi=\xi_{1}+\xi_{2}+\xi_{3}
, and

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\times\left\lVert\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{4}}\displaystyle\sum_{(q_{1},q_{2})\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}}\left|\prod_{j=1}^{3}\widehat{P_{N_{j}}Q_{L_{j}}u_{j}}(\tau_{j},\xi_{j},q_{j})\right|^{2}d\nu\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right\rVert_{L^{2}_{\tau\xi q}}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\times\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\sum_{q}\left|\widehat{P_{N_{3}}Q_{L_{3}}u_{3}}\right|^{2}d\tau d\xi\right)d\nu\Bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=\sup_{(\tau,\xi,q)}\left|A_{(\tau,\xi,q)}\right|^{\frac{1}{2}}\left\lVert\widehat{P_{N_{3}}Q_{L_{3}}u_{3}}\right\rVert_{L^{2}_{\tau\xi q}}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\times\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{4}}\sum_{(q_{1},q_{2})\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}}\left|\prod_{j=1}^{2}\widehat{P_{N_{j}}Q_{L_{j}}u_{j}}(\tau_{j},\xi_{j},q_{j})\right|^{2}d\nu\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=\sup_{(\tau,\xi,q)}\left|A_{(\tau,\xi,q)}\right|^{\frac{1}{2}}\prod_{j=1}^{3}\left\lVert P_{N_{j}}Q_{L_{j}}u_{j}\right\rVert_{L^{2}_{txy}},

where

(3.6)

\displaystyle A_{(\tau,\xi,q)}=

\displaystyle\{((\tau_{1},\xi_{1},q_{1}),(\tau_{2},\xi_{2},q_{2}))\in{\mathbb{R}}^{4}\times{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}:\left|(\xi_{1},q_{1})\right|\in I_{N_{1}},

\displaystyle\left|(\xi_{2},q_{2})\right|\in I_{N_{2}},\left|(\xi-\xi_{1}-\xi_{2},q-q_{1}-q_{2})\right|\in I_{N_{3}},

\displaystyle\left|\tau_{1}-\omega(\xi_{1},q_{1})\right|\in I_{L_{1}},\left|\tau_{2}-\omega(\xi_{2},q_{2})\right|\in I_{L_{2}},

\displaystyle\left|\tau-\tau_{1}-\tau_{2}-\omega(\xi-\xi_{1}-\xi_{2},q-q_{1}-q_{2})\right|\in I_{L_{3}}\}.

Therefore, we have

(3.7)

\left\lVert\prod_{j=1}^{3}P_{N_{j}}Q_{L_{j}}u_{j}\right\rVert_{L^{2}_{txy}}\lesssim\sup_{(\tau,\xi,q)}\left|A_{(\tau,\xi,q)}\right|^{\frac{1}{2}}\prod_{j=1}^{3}\left\lVert P_{N_{j}}Q_{L_{j}}u_{j}\right\rVert_{L^{2}_{txy}}.

For the trivial estimate, choose the two input spatial frequencies
corresponding to
N_{\min}
and
N_{\mathrm{med}}
; the third is determined by the
fixed output. A block

\left\{(\xi_{j},q_{j}):\left|(\xi_{j},q_{j})\right|\in I_{N_{j}}\right\}
has product measure

O(N_{j}^{2})
. Combining this observation with the modulation-fiber argument
used below gives

(3.8)

\left|A_{(\tau,\xi,q)}\right|\lesssim L_{\min}L_{\mathrm{med}}N_{\min}^{2}N_{\mathrm{med}}^{2},

for all
(\tau,\xi,q)\in{\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{Z}}
. To prove
(
3.3
)–(
3.5
),
it is enough by symmetry to prove the first estimate. Set

(3.9)

\xi_{3}:=\xi-\xi_{1}-\xi_{2},\qquad q_{3}:=q-q_{1}-q_{2},\qquad\tau_{3}:=\tau-\tau_{1}-\tau_{2}.

By the definition of the resonance function,

\displaystyle\tau-\omega(\xi,q)+\mathcal{H}(\xi_{1},q_{1},\xi_{2},q_{2},\xi_{3},q_{3})

(3.10)

\displaystyle\qquad=\left(\tau_{1}-\omega(\xi_{1},q_{1})\right)+\left(\tau_{2}-\omega(\xi_{2},q_{2})\right)+\left(\tau_{3}-\omega(\xi_{3},q_{3})\right).

Set
C_{0}:=24/5
. By the support properties of the three modulations
projections,

(3.11)

\left|\tau-\omega(\xi,q)+\mathcal{H}(\xi_{1},q_{1},\xi_{2},q_{2},\xi_{3},q_{3})\right|\leq\frac{8}{5}(L_{1}+L_{2}+L_{3})\leq C_{0}L_{\max}.

For fixed spatial frequencies, introduce

\lambda_{j}:=\tau_{j}-\omega(\xi_{j},q_{j})
,
j=1,2,3
. Their sum is fixed by
(
3.10
). The map from

(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})
to any two of the variables
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\lambda_{3}

has a Jacobian of absolute value one. Choosing the two variables corresponding
to
L_{\min}
and
L_{\mathrm{med}}
therefore bounds the modulation fiber by

O(L_{\min}L_{\mathrm{med}})
. Consequently,

(3.12)

\left|A_{(\tau,\xi,q)}\right|\lesssim L_{\min}L_{\mathrm{med}}\left|B_{(\tau,\xi,q)}\right|,

where

\displaystyle B_{(\tau,\xi,q)}:=

\displaystyle\Big\{\big((\xi_{1},q_{1}),(\xi_{2},q_{2})\big)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\times{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}:

\displaystyle\left|(\xi_{1},q_{1})\right|\in I_{N_{1}},\quad\left|(\xi_{2},q_{2})\right|\in I_{N_{2}},

\displaystyle\left|(\xi-\xi_{1}-\xi_{2},q-q_{1}-q_{2})\right|\in I_{N_{3}},

(3.13)

\displaystyle\left|\tau-\omega(\xi,q)+\mathcal{H}(\xi_{1},q_{1},\xi_{2},q_{2},\xi-\xi_{1}-\xi_{2},q-q_{1}-q_{2})\right|\leq C_{0}L_{\max}\Big\}.

Recall that

(3.14)

\mathcal{H}(\xi_{1},q_{1},\xi_{2},q_{2},\xi_{3},q_{3})=\omega(\xi,q)-\sum_{j=1}^{3}\omega(\xi_{j},q_{j}),

where

(3.15)

\xi_{3}=\xi-\xi_{1}-\xi_{2},\qquad q_{3}=q-q_{1}-q_{2}.

Since the output
(\xi,q)
is fixed, differentiation with respect to

\xi_{1}
gives

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial\xi_{1}}\mathcal{H}(\xi_{1},q_{1},\xi_{2},q_{2},\xi_{3},q_{3})

\displaystyle=-\partial_{\xi}\omega(\xi_{1},q_{1})+\partial_{\xi}\omega(\xi_{3},q_{3})

(3.16)

\displaystyle=\left|(\xi_{3},q_{3})\right|^{2}-\left|(\xi_{1},q_{1})\right|^{2}.

Assume that

(3.17)

N_{1}\geq 4N_{3}\qquad\text{or}\qquad N_{3}\geq 4N_{1}.

If
N_{1}\geq 4N_{3}
, then the support properties of the spatial
Littlewood–Paley multipliers give

\left|(\xi_{1},q_{1})\right|\geq\frac{5}{8}N_{1},\qquad\left|(\xi_{3},q_{3})\right|\leq\frac{8}{5}N_{3}\leq\frac{2}{5}N_{1}.

Consequently,

\displaystyle\left|(\xi_{1},q_{1})\right|^{2}-\left|(\xi_{3},q_{3})\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\left(\frac{25}{64}-\frac{4}{25}\right)N_{1}^{2}

(3.18)

\displaystyle=\frac{369}{1600}N_{1}^{2}.

The same argument with the indices
1
and
3
interchanged applies when

N_{3}\geq 4N_{1}
. Therefore, throughout the spatial-frequency support under
consideration,

(3.19)

\left|\frac{\partial}{\partial\xi_{1}}\mathcal{H}(\xi_{1},q_{1},\xi_{2},q_{2},\xi_{3},q_{3})\right|\geq\frac{369}{1600}(N_{1}\vee N_{3})^{2}.

This calculation includes the borderline cases
N_{1}=4N_{3}
and

N_{3}=4N_{1}
.
For fixed
(q_{1},q_{2})\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}
, define the continuous fiber

(3.20)

\displaystyle B_{(\tau,\xi,q)}(q_{1},q_{2}):=\Big\{(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}:\big((\xi_{1},q_{1}),(\xi_{2},q_{2})\big)\in B_{(\tau,\xi,q)}\Big\}.

Set
q_{3}:=q-q_{1}-q_{2}
. For each fixed
\xi_{2}\in{\mathbb{R}}
, let

(3.21)

\displaystyle E_{\xi_{2}}(q_{1},q_{2}):=\Big\{\xi_{1}\in{\mathbb{R}}:

\displaystyle\left|(\xi_{1},q_{1})\right|\in I_{N_{1}},

\displaystyle\left|(\xi-\xi_{1}-\xi_{2},q_{3})\right|\in I_{N_{3}}\Big\}.

For a fixed integer
m
and a dyadic number
M
, the set

\left\{r\in{\mathbb{R}}:\left|(r,m)\right|\in I_{M}\right\}

is the union of at most two intervals. The same remains true after a
translation or reflection of

. Hence
E_{\xi_{2}}(q_{1},q_{2})
is the union
of at most four intervals.

For fixed
\xi_{2}
, define

(3.22)

\displaystyle f_{\xi_{2}}(\xi_{1}):=\tau-\omega(\xi,q)+\mathcal{H}(\xi_{1},q_{1},\xi_{2},q_{2},\xi-\xi_{1}-\xi_{2},q_{3}).

On every interval component

3.19

Applying

on each component with the interval

and summing over the at most four components,
we obtain

\left|\left\{\xi_{1}\in E_{\xi_{2}}(q_{1},q_{2}):\left|f_{\xi_{2}}(\xi_{1})\right|\leq C_{0}L_{\max}\right\}\right|\lesssim\frac{L_{\max}}{(N_{1}\vee N_{3})^{2}}.

Moreover, for fixed
q_{2}
,

\left|\left\{\xi_{2}\in{\mathbb{R}}:\left|(\xi_{2},q_{2})\right|\in I_{N_{2}}\right\}\right|\lesssim N_{2}.

Fubini’s theorem and (
3.23
) therefore yield

\left|B_{(\tau,\xi,q)}(q_{1},q_{2})\right|\lesssim L_{\max}\frac{N_{2}}{(N_{1}\vee N_{3})^{2}}.

The projection of
B_{(\tau,\xi,q)}
onto the
(q_{1},q_{2})
-lattice is
contained in

|\mathcal{Q}_{q}|:=\left\{(q_{1},q_{2})\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}:\left|q_{1}\right|\leq\frac{8}{5}N_{1},\ \left|q_{2}\right|\leq\frac{8}{5}N_{2},\ \left|q-q_{1}-q_{2}\right|\leq\frac{8}{5}N_{3}\right\}.

Choose the two lattice variables corresponding to the two smallest members

\left\{N_{1},N_{2},N_{3}\right\}

O(N_{\min})
and
O(N_{\mathrm{med}})

. Therefore,

|\mathcal{Q}_{q}|\lesssim N_{\min}N_{\mathrm{med}}.

Summing (
3.24
) over

(3.25)

\left|B_{(\tau,\xi,q)}\right|\lesssim L_{\max}N_{\min}N_{\mathrm{med}}\frac{N_{2}}{(N_{1}\vee N_{3})^{2}}.

Combining (
3.25
) with (

(3.26)

\left|A_{(\tau,\xi,q)}\right|\lesssim N_{\min}N_{\mathrm{med}}L_{\max}L_{\min}L_{\mathrm{med}}\frac{N_{2}}{(N_{1}\vee N_{3})^{2}}.

Since
L_{\max}L_{\min}L_{\mathrm{med}}=L_{1}L_{2}L_{3}

(
3.26

3.7

(
3.3
). The other two estimates follow

Lemma 3.3

(Trilinear estimate in Bourgain spaces)
.

Let
s>1
. Then there exists
\delta>0
sufficiently small such that

(3.27)

\left\lVert\partial_{x}\left(\prod_{j=1}^{3}u_{j}\right)\right\rVert_{X^{{s},{-\frac{1}{2}+3\delta}}}\lesssim\prod_{j=1}^{3}\left\lVert u_{j}\right\rVert_{X^{{s},{\frac{1}{2}+\delta}}},

for all
u_{j}\in X^{s,\frac{1}{2}+\delta}
,
j=1,2,3
.
The implicit constant depends only on
s
and
\delta
.

Remark 3.4

(Comparison with previous trilinear estimates)
.

For
k=2
,
[
8
, Proposition 3.3]
yields the corresponding
estimate on the full timeline with output exponent

-\frac{1}{2}+2\varepsilon
for
s>\frac{5}{6}
. Its stated right-hand side places
one input at regularity
s
and the other two at regularity
\frac{5}{6}+
;
choosing
\frac{5}{6}<\theta<s

(
1.5
). The time-localized estimate of

[

has the same

. Estimate (
3.27
) therefore has a

balance is not obtained from either cited estimate merely by

Proof.

By density, it suffices to prove the estimate for Schwartz functions.
We fix throughout

(3.28)

By duality, (
3.27
) follows once we prove that

(3.29)

\displaystyle\left|\int_{{\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{T}}}u_{1}u_{2}u_{3}\,\partial_{x}\overline{g}\,dt\,dx\,dy\right|\lesssim\left(\prod_{j=1}^{3}\left\lVert u_{j}\right\rVert_{X^{{s},{\frac{1}{2}+\delta}}}\right)\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{X^{{-s},{\frac{1}{2}-3\delta}}}

for every
g\in X^{-s,\frac{1}{2}-3\delta}
.

Set

(3.30)

f_{j}(\tau_{j},\xi_{j},q_{j}):=\left\langle\sigma_{j}\right\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}+\delta}\left\langle\left|(\xi_{j},q_{j})\right|\right\rangle^{s}\left|\widehat{u_{j}}(\tau_{j},\xi_{j},q_{j})\right|,\qquad j=1,2,3,

and

(3.31)

Here

(3.32)

\sigma_{j}:=\tau_{j}-\omega(\xi_{j},q_{j}),\qquad\sigma:=\tau-\omega(\xi,q),

with

(3.33)

\omega(\xi,q)=\xi^{3}+\xi q^{2}.

By Plancherel’s identity,

(3.34)

\left\lVert f_{j}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}=\left\lVert u_{j}\right\rVert_{X^{{s},{\frac{1}{2}+\delta}}},\qquad j=1,2,3,

and

(3.35)

\left\lVert f_{0}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}=\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{X^{{-s},{\frac{1}{2}-3\delta}}}.

Let

(3.36)

\tau_{3}=\tau-\tau_{1}-\tau_{2},\qquad\xi_{3}=\xi-\xi_{1}-\xi_{2},\qquad q_{3}=q-q_{1}-q_{2},

and write

(3.37)

d\nu:=d\tau\,d\tau_{1}\,d\tau_{2}\,d\xi\,d\xi_{1}\,d\xi_{2}.

The left-hand side of (
3.29
) is bounded by

(3.38)

I:=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{6}}\sum_{q,q_{1},q_{2}\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\Gamma\prod_{j=0}^{3}f_{j}(\tau_{j},\xi_{j},q_{j})\,d\nu,

where

Thus, it remains to prove

(3.40)

I\lesssim\prod_{j=0}^{3}\left\lVert f_{j}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}.

For notational convenience, in the remainder of the proof, we denote the inverse Fourier transforms of the nonnegative profiles
f_{j}

again by
u_{j}
. Thus

(3.41)

\left\lVert u_{j}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}=\left\lVert f_{j}\right\rVert_{L^{2}},\qquad j=0,1,2,3.

We use the spatial Littlewood–Paley projections

and the

when

\sum_{{L,L_{1},L_{2},L_{3}}}J_{K}\lesssim K^{\frac{1}{2}}N^{1-2s}\prod_{j=0}^{3}\left\lVert P_{N_{j}}u_{j}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}.

Using
K\lesssim N
,

(3.152)

\sum_{K\lesssim N}K^{\frac{1}{2}}\lesssim N^{\frac{1}{2}},

and hence

(3.153)

\sum_{K\lesssim N}\sum_{{L,L_{1},L_{2},L_{3}}}J_{K}\lesssim N^{\frac{3}{2}-2s}\prod_{j=0}^{3}\left\lVert P_{N_{j}}u_{j}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}.

Since

,

(3.154)

\frac{3}{2}-2s<-\frac{1}{2},

so

(3.155)

N^{\frac{3}{2}-2s}\lesssim 1.

Finally, by bounded dyadic multiplicity, discrete Hölder, and

\ell^{2}\subset\ell^{4}
,

\displaystyle\sum_{N_{1}\sim N_{2}\sim N_{3}\sim N}\prod_{j=0}^{3}\left\lVert P_{N_{j}}u_{j}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\prod_{j=0}^{3}\left(\sum_{N_{j}}\left\lVert P_{N_{j}}u_{j}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{4}\right)^{\frac{1}{4}}

(3.156)

\displaystyle\lesssim\prod_{j=0}^{3}\left\lVert u_{j}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}.

The regions in which
\left|\eta_{13}\right|
or
\left|\eta_{23}\right|
is
maximal are identical after permuting the three input indices.
Thus

(3.157)

I_{{H}{H}{H}\to{H}}\lesssim\prod_{j=0}^{3}\left\lVert u_{j}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}.

Combining Cases
1
–
6
proves

(3.158)

I\lesssim\prod_{j=0}^{3}\left\lVert u_{j}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}.

Returning to the weighted profiles and using
(
3.34
)–(
3.35
), we obtain

(3.159)

\displaystyle\left|\int_{{\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{T}}}u_{1}u_{2}u_{3}\,\partial_{x}\overline{g}\,dt\,dx\,dy\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\prod_{j=1}^{3}\left\lVert u_{j}\right\rVert_{X^{{s},{\frac{1}{2}+\delta}}}\right)\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{X^{{-s},{\frac{1}{2}-3\delta}}}.

By duality, this is precisely
(
3.27
), and the proof is complete.
∎

Let
u_{0}\in\overline{B}_{r}^{H}

The definition of the restricted norm, therefore, gives

\displaystyle\left\lVert\Phi(u_{0},u)\right\rVert_{X^{{s},{\frac{1}{2}+\delta}}_{T}}

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert\widetilde{\Phi}(u_{0},w)\right\rVert_{X^{{s},{\frac{1}{2}+\delta}}}

\displaystyle\leq c_{1}\left\lVert u_{0}\right\rVert_{H^{s}}+c_{2}c_{3}T^{2\delta}\left(\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{X^{{s},{\frac{1}{2}+\delta}}_{T}}+\varepsilon\right)^{3}.

\displaystyle\left\lVert\Phi(u_{0},u)\right\rVert_{X^{{s},{\frac{1}{2}+\delta}}_{T}}

\displaystyle\leq c_{1}\left\lVert u_{0}\right\rVert_{H^{s}}+c_{2}c_{3}T^{2\delta}\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{X^{{s},{\frac{1}{2}+\delta}}_{T}}^{3}

Then

cr+cT^{2\delta}R^{3}<\frac{R}{2}+\frac{R}{6}<R,

so

\Phi(u_{0},\cdot)
maps
\overline{B}_{R}^{X}
into itself, uniformly for

.

To prove contraction, let
f_{1},g_{1}\in\overline{B}_{R}^{X}

\displaystyle\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{X^{{s},{\frac{1}{2}+\delta}}}

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert f_{1}\right\rVert_{X^{{s},{\frac{1}{2}+\delta}}_{T}}+\varepsilon,

\displaystyle\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{X^{{s},{\frac{1}{2}+\delta}}}

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert g_{1}\right\rVert_{X^{{s},{\frac{1}{2}+\delta}}_{T}}+\varepsilon,

\displaystyle\left\lVert h\right\rVert_{X^{{s},{\frac{1}{2}+\delta}}}

On

,

\displaystyle\left\lVert\Phi(u_{0},f_{1})-\Phi(u_{0},g_{1})\right\rVert_{X^{{s},{\frac{1}{2}+\delta}}_{T}}

\displaystyle\left\lVert\Phi(u_{0},f_{1})-\Phi(u_{0},g_{1})\right\rVert_{X^{{s},{\frac{1}{2}+\delta}}_{T}}

\displaystyle\qquad<\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert f_{1}-g_{1}\right\rVert_{X^{{s},{\frac{1}{2}+\delta}}_{T}}.

\Phi(u_{0},\cdot)
is a strict contraction on

For an arbitrary initial datum, take

For

\displaystyle u(t)-v(t)

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{t}e^{-(t-t^{\prime})\partial_{x}\Delta}\partial_{x}\left(u^{3}(t^{\prime})-v^{3}(t^{\prime})\right)\,dt^{\prime}.

Since

\displaystyle u(t)-v(t)

\displaystyle=-\eta\left(\frac{t}{T_{0}}\right)\int_{0}^{t}e^{-(t-t^{\prime})\partial_{x}\Delta}

\displaystyle}{\displaystyle\mathbin{\times}\partial_{x}\left[\begin{aligned} &\left(\eta(t^{\prime}/T_{0})u(t^{\prime})\right)^{3}\\
&\quad-\left(\eta(t^{\prime}/T_{0})v(t^{\prime})\right)^{3}\end{aligned}\right]\,dt^{\prime}.

For every
\varepsilon>0

\displaystyle\left\lVert U\right\rVert_{X^{{s},{\frac{1}{2}+\delta}}}

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{X_{T_{0}}^{s,\frac{1}{2}+\delta}}+\varepsilon,

\displaystyle\left\lVert V\right\rVert_{X^{{s},{\frac{1}{2}+\delta}}}

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{X_{T_{0}}^{s,\frac{1}{2}+\delta}}+\varepsilon,

\displaystyle\left\lVert u-v\right\rVert_{X_{T_{0}}^{s,\frac{1}{2}+\delta}}

\displaystyle\lesssim T_{0}^{2\delta}\Big(\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{X_{T_{0}}^{s,\frac{1}{2}+\delta}}^{2}+\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{X_{T_{0}}^{s,\frac{1}{2}+\delta}}\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{X_{T_{0}}^{s,\frac{1}{2}+\delta}}

.

Thus

is continuous, affine in

, and a

\mathcal{F}
is real-analytic. Fix

and let

be the solution obtained above. Then

(4.29)

\mathcal{F}(u_{0},u)=0.

The Fréchet derivative of

with respect to the second
variable is

(4.30)

\displaystyle D_{u}\mathcal{F}(u_{0},u)[v]

\displaystyle=v-D_{u}\Phi(u_{0},u)[v],

where

(4.31)

\displaystyle D_{u}\Phi(u_{0},u)[v]

\displaystyle=-3\eta\left(\frac{t}{T}\right)\int_{0}^{t}e^{-(t-t^{\prime})\partial_{x}\Delta}\partial_{x}\left(u^{2}(t^{\prime})v(t^{\prime})\right)\,dt^{\prime}.

By
Lemma
A.3
and
Lemma
3.3
,
we obtain

\displaystyle\left\lVert D_{u}\Phi(u_{0},u)[v]\right\rVert_{X_{T}^{s,\frac{1}{2}+\delta}}

\displaystyle\leq 3cT^{2\delta}\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{X_{T}^{s,\frac{1}{2}+\delta}}^{2}\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{X_{T}^{s,\frac{1}{2}+\delta}}

Hence

(4.34)

\left\lVert D_{u}\Phi(u_{0},u)\right\rVert_{\operatorname{op}}<1.

Therefore,

(4.35)

Theorem
A.9

A.10

\mathcal{F}
at

agrees with the data-to-solution map wherever both

. Since

A.1.
Linear estimates in Bourgain’s spaces

In this section, we recall some standard results regarding Bourgain spaces; see
[
10
]
. Let
\eta
be the cutoff fixed in the proof of Theorem
1.1
. The estimates below hold for any dispersion function
\omega
and any spatial domain. The following lemma shows that the
X^{s,b}
norm is stable under time localization.

Lemma A.1
.

Let
s,b\in{\mathbb{R}}
and
u\in X^{s,b}
. Then

\left\lVert\eta(t)u\right\rVert_{X^{{s},{b}}}\lesssim_{b}\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{X^{{s},{b}}}.

Proof.

Using Fourier inversion formula on
\eta
and Minkowski’s inequality,

\displaystyle\left\lVert\eta(t)u\right\rVert_{X^{{s},{b}}}

\displaystyle=\left\lVert\left(\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\widehat{\eta}(\tau_{0})e^{it\tau_{0}}d\tau_{0}\right)u\right\rVert_{X^{{s},{b}}}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left\lVert\widehat{\eta}(\tau_{0})e^{it\tau_{0}}u\right\rVert_{X^{{s},{b}}}d\tau_{0}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left|\widehat{\eta}(\tau_{0})\right|\left\lVert e^{it\tau_{0}}u\right\rVert_{X^{{s},{b}}}d\tau_{0}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left|\widehat{\eta}(\tau_{0})\right|\left\lVert\left\langle|(\xi,q)|\right\rangle^{s}\left\langle\tau-\omega(\xi,q)\right\rangle^{b}\widehat{u}(\tau-\tau_{0},\xi,q)\right\rVert_{L^{2}_{\xi q}L^{2}_{\tau}}d\tau_{0}

Applying the change of variables
\tau^{\prime}=\tau-\tau_{0}
and
\left\langle A+B\right\rangle^{b}\lesssim_{b}\left\langle A\right\rangle^{|b|}\left\langle B\right\rangle^{b}
gives

\displaystyle\left\lVert\eta(t)u\right\rVert_{X^{{s},{b}}}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left|\widehat{\eta}(\tau_{0})\right|\left\lVert\left\langle|(\xi,q)|\right\rangle^{s}\left\langle\tau^{\prime}+\tau_{0}-\omega(\xi,q)\right\rangle^{b}\widehat{u}(\tau^{\prime},\xi,q)\right\rVert_{L^{2}_{\xi q}L^{2}_{\tau^{\prime}}}d\tau_{0}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left|\widehat{\eta}(\tau_{0})\right|\left\langle\tau_{0}\right\rangle^{|b|}\left\lVert\left\langle|(\xi,q)|\right\rangle^{s}\left\langle\tau^{\prime}-\omega(\xi,q)\right\rangle^{b}\widehat{u}(\tau^{\prime},\xi,q)\right\rVert_{L^{2}_{\xi q}L_{\tau^{\prime}}^{2}}d\tau_{0}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{b}\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{X^{{s},{b}}}.

∎

The following is Lemma 3.1 in
[
10
]
with
T_{0}=1
fixed. This choice is advantageous because, when using the contraction mapping principle in
X^{s,b}
with
b>1/2
and
T
small, taking
T_{0}=1
rather than
T_{0}=T
allows us to work on a ball of fixed size independent of
T
.

Lemma A.2

(Homogeneous linear estimate)
.

Let
s,b\in{\mathbb{R}}
and
u_{0}\in H^{s}({\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{T}})
.

(A.1)

\left\lVert{\eta(t)e^{-t\partial_{x}\Delta}u_{0}}\right\rVert_{X^{{s},{b}}}\lesssim_{b}\left\lVert u_{0}\right\rVert_{H^{s}_{xy}}.

Proof.

We start by calculating the space-time Fourier transform of
\eta\,e^{-t\partial_{x}\Delta}u_{0}
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}{\left({\eta(t)e^{-t\partial_{x}\Delta}u_{0}}\right)}(\tau,\xi,q)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{3}}\int e^{-i(t\tau+(x,y)\cdot(\xi,q))}\eta(t)e^{it\omega(\xi,q)}u_{0}(x,y)dxdydt

\displaystyle=\left\{\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-it(\tau-\omega(\xi,q))}\eta(t)dt\right\}

\displaystyle\quad\times\left\{\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{2}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{T}}}e^{-i(x,y)\cdot(\xi,q)}u_{0}(x,y)dxdy\right\}

\displaystyle=\widehat{\eta}(\tau-\omega(\xi,q))\widehat{u_{0}}(\xi,q).

Now we can calculate the norm,

\displaystyle\left\lVert\eta(t)e^{-t\partial_{x}\Delta}{u_{0}}\right\rVert_{X^{{s},{b}}}

\displaystyle=\left\lVert\left\langle|(\xi,q)|\right\rangle^{s}\left\langle\tau-\omega(\xi,q)\right\rangle^{b}\widehat{\eta}(\tau-\omega(\xi,q))\widehat{u_{0}}(\xi,q)\right\rVert_{L_{\xi q}^{2}L^{2}_{\tau}}

\displaystyle=\left\lVert\left\lVert\left\langle\tau-\omega(\xi,q)\right\rangle^{b}\widehat{\eta}(\tau-\omega(\xi,q))\right\rVert_{L^{2}_{\tau}}\left\langle|(\xi,q)|\right\rangle^{s}\widehat{u_{0}}(\xi,q)\right\rVert_{L^{2}_{\xi q}}

\displaystyle=\left\lVert\eta\right\rVert_{H^{b}_{t}}\left\lVert\left\langle|(\xi,q)|\right\rangle^{s}\widehat{u_{0}}\right\rVert_{L^{2}_{\xi q}}

\displaystyle=\left\lVert\eta\right\rVert_{H^{b}_{t}}\left\lVert u_{0}\right\rVert_{H^{s}_{xy}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{b}\left\lVert u_{0}\right\rVert_{H^{s}_{xy}}.

∎

Lemma A.3

(Inhomogeneous linear estimates)
.

Let
s\in{\mathbb{R}}
,

-\frac{1}{2}<b^{\prime}\leq 0\leq b\leq b^{\prime}+1
,

0<T\leq 1
, and
F\in X^{s,b^{\prime}}
. Then,

(A.2)

\left\lVert\eta(t/T)\int_{0}^{t}e^{-(t-t^{\prime})\partial_{x}\Delta}F(t^{\prime})dt^{\prime}\right\rVert_{X^{s,b}}\leq c\,T^{1-b+b^{\prime}}\left\lVert F\right\rVert_{X^{s,b^{\prime}}},

where
c=c(\eta,b,b^{\prime})>0
is independent of
T
and
F
.

Proof.

See Lemma 2.1 (ii) in
[
11
]
and Lemma 3.2 in
[
10
]
.
∎

Lemma A.4

(Time Localization Estimate)
.

Let
0<T<1
,
s\in{\mathbb{R}}
,
-\frac{1}{2}<b^{\prime}\leq b<\frac{1}{2}
, and

u\in X^{s,b}
. Then

(A.3)

\left\lVert\eta(t/T)u\right\rVert_{X^{s,b^{\prime}}}\lesssim_{b,b^{\prime}}T^{b-b^{\prime}}\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{X^{s,b}}.

Proof.

Set

(A.4)

U(t):=e^{t\partial_{x}\Delta}u(t).

By the definition of the Bourgain norm,

(A.5)

\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{X^{{s},{b}}}=\left\lVert U\right\rVert_{H_{t}^{b}H_{xy}^{s}},

and, since multiplication by
\eta(t/T)
commutes with the spatial
group,

(A.6)

\left\lVert\eta(t/T)u\right\rVert_{X^{{s},{b^{\prime}}}}=\left\lVert\eta(t/T)U\right\rVert_{H_{t}^{b^{\prime}}H_{xy}^{s}}.

The standard one-dimensional time-localization estimate

(A.7)

\left\lVert\eta(t/T)F\right\rVert_{H_{t}^{b^{\prime}}({\mathbb{R}};H)}\lesssim_{b,b^{\prime}}T^{b-b^{\prime}}\left\lVert F\right\rVert_{H_{t}^{b}({\mathbb{R}};H)},

valid for every Hilbert space
H
and

(A.8)

-\frac{1}{2}<b^{\prime}\leq b<\frac{1}{2},\qquad 0<T<1,

therefore gives, with
H=H^{s}({\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{T}})
,

(A.9)

\left\lVert\eta(t/T)u\right\rVert_{X^{{s},{b^{\prime}}}}\lesssim_{b,b^{\prime}}T^{b-b^{\prime}}\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{X^{{s},{b}}}.

The one-dimensional estimate is standard; see
Lemma 2.11 in
[
28
]
and
Lemma 3.11 in
[
7
]
.
∎

A.2.
Analytic maps and the implicit function theorem

In this section, we present the mathematical framework for analytic maps and the implicit function theorem in Banach spaces. The following definitions and theorems are standard; see
[
13
]
for details.

Definition A.5
.

Let
X,Y
be Banach spaces over
{\mathbb{R}}
or
{\mathbb{C}}
, and let
U\subset X

be open. We denote by
C^{1}(U,Y)
the set of all Fréchet
differentiable maps
F:U\to Y
such that

(A.10)

F^{\prime}:U\to L(X,Y)

is continuous. Inductively, we define
C^{k}(U,Y)
for

k=2,\ldots,\infty
.

Definition A.6
.

Let
X,Y
be Banach spaces over
{\mathbb{R}}
, let
U\subset X
be open, and
let
F:U\to Y
. We say that
F
is real-analytic if, for every

u\in U
, there exists
r>0
such that

(A.11)

B_{r}(u)\subset U,

and there exist continuous
k
-linear maps

(A.12)

L_{k}:X^{k}\to Y,\qquad k\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0},

such that, writing

(A.13)

L^{(k)}(x):=L_{k}(x,\ldots,x),

one has

(A.14)

F(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}L^{(k)}(x-u),\qquad x\in B_{r}(u),

with convergence in
Y
.

Theorem A.7

(Proposition 1.4.1 in
[
13
]
)
.

Let
X,Y
be Banach spaces over
{\mathbb{R}}
or
{\mathbb{C}}
, let
U\subset X
be open,
and let
F:U\to Y
be differentiable. Assume that for every
u\in U
there
exists
\epsilon>0
such that
B_{\epsilon}(u)\subset U
and

F^{\prime}:B_{\epsilon}(u)\to L(X,Y)
is bounded. Then

F\big|_{B_{\epsilon}(u)}
is Lipschitz continuous. In particular, if

F\in C^{1}(U,Y)
, then
F
is locally Lipschitz continuous.

Theorem A.8

(Proposition 1.4.5 in
[
13
]
)
.

Let
X,Y,Z
be Banach spaces over
{\mathbb{R}}
, let
U\subset X
and

V\subset Y
be open, and let

(A.15)

F:U\to V,\qquad G:V\to Z

be real-analytic maps. Then

(A.16)

G\circ F:U\to Z

is real-analytic.

Theorem A.9

(Neumann-series criterion)
.

Let
X
be a Banach space and let
T\in L(X,X)
satisfy

(A.17)

\left\lVert T\right\rVert_{L(X,X)}<1.

Then
I-T
is boundedly invertible on
X
, and

(A.18)

(I-T)^{-1}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}T^{n}

with convergence in
L(X,X)
.

Theorem A.10

(
Implicit Function Theorem
)
.

Let
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
, let
X,Y,Z
be Banach spaces over
{\mathbb{R}}
, and let

U\subset X
and
V\subset Y
be open neighborhoods of
x_{0}
and

y_{0}
, respectively. Let

(A.19)

F:U\times V\to Z

be of class
C^{k}
(respectively, real-analytic), and assume that

(A.20)

F(x_{0},y_{0})=0.

Assume moreover that

(A.21)

D_{y}F(x_{0},y_{0}):Y\to Z

is a bounded linear isomorphism, with

(A.22)

\big(D_{y}F(x_{0},y_{0})\big)^{-1}\in L(Z,Y).

Then there exist neighborhoods
B_{r}(x_{0})\subset U
and

B_{R}(y_{0})\subset V
and a unique map of class
C^{k}
(respectively,
real-analytic)

(A.23)

G:B_{r}(x_{0})\to B_{R}(y_{0})

such that

(A.24)

G(x_{0})=y_{0}

and

(A.25)

F(x,G(x))=0

for every
x\in B_{r}(x_{0})
.

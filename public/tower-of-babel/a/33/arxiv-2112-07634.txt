1.
Introduction

For those with an interest in nonlinear PDEs, the two-dimensional (2D) Kuramoto-Sivashinsky equation (KSE) is full of tantalizing possibilities.
There is such strong diffusion in the equation that the nonlinear term seems incapable of overpowering it, and yet, it is precisely the structure of the nonlinear term that has so far thwarted all efforts at a general proof of global well-posedness. It is sometimes thought that this is due to the backward diffusion term in the equations, but this term serves mainly as the source of energy and instability, fueling a kind of self-perpetuating chaos. Instead, the major difficulty is due to the fact that the nonlinear term does not vanish in any known energy estimates, similar to the vorticity stretching term in the Navier-Stokes equations (NSE), where an analogous barrier arises. This paper aims to quantify these difficulties by proving several integrability-type component based regularity criteria for well-posedness, and also to shed light on well-posedness issues via numerical simulations.

For those with a more practical eye, the KSE enjoys a wealth of applications. Originally proposed in the late 1970’s by Kuramoto, Sivashinsky, and Tsuzuki in investigations of crystal growth

We note that the proofs of the results on individual components are non-standard, and do not follow as in, e.g., the Navier-Stokes case; a major difference being the loss of the divergence-free condition, which is what makes the multi-dimensional KSE difficult in the first place.

), and some of such results have been extended to other systems of PDEs that involve divergence-free velocity field (e.g.,
[
9
,
75
]
on MHD system;
[
74
]
on SQG equations). In short, the proof of (
2.12a
) relies on the fact that upon
\lVert(\partial_{1}u,\partial_{2}u)\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}
-estimate of the 3D NSE (
1.3
), one can separate
u_{3}
within the nonlinear terms as follows:

\displaystyle\int(u\cdot\nabla)u\cdot(\partial_{1}^{2}+\partial_{2}^{2})u=

\displaystyle-\sum_{i,j=1}^{3}\sum_{k=1}^{2}\int\partial_{k}u_{i}\partial_{i}u_{j}\partial_{k}u_{j}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\int\lvert u_{3}\rvert\lvert\nabla u\rvert\lvert\nabla(\partial_{1},\partial_{2})u\rvert.

E.g., we can estimate by summing the terms when
(i,j,k)=(1,1,2)
and
(i,j,k)=(2,1,2)
to bound

\displaystyle\int\partial_{2}u_{1}\partial_{1}u_{1}\partial_{2}u_{1}+\partial_{2}u_{2}\partial_{2}u_{1}\partial_{2}u_{1}=

\displaystyle-\int(\partial_{2}u_{1})^{2}\partial_{3}u_{3}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\int\lvert u_{3}\rvert\lvert\nabla u\rvert\lvert\nabla(\partial_{1},\partial_{2})u\rvert

where the divergence-free property played an important role. Despite the large number of papers devoted to this direction of research, we are not aware of any example of a PDE that admitted such component reduction of regularity criteria without a divergence-free property. Remarkably, we have been able to prove such results for the KSE (see Theorems
3.6
,
3.10
,
3.16
, and
3.20
).

Let us continue the

(3.1)

\displaystyle\int(u\cdot\nabla)u\cdot\Delta u\leq\lVert u\rVert_{L^{\frac{Np}{N-mp}}}\lVert\nabla u\rVert_{L^{\frac{2Np}{Np+mp-N}}}\lVert\Delta u\rVert_{L^{\frac{2Np}{Np+mp-N}}}

\displaystyle\text{ for any }N\in\{2,3\},p\in\begin{cases}[1,\frac{N}{m}]&\text{ if }m\in(0,1),\\

(3.2b)

\displaystyle\lVert\nabla f\rVert_{L^{\frac{2Np}{Np+mp-N}}}\lesssim

\displaystyle\lVert f\rVert_{L^{2}}^{\frac{(2+m)p-N}{4p}}\lVert f\rVert_{H^{2}}^{\frac{(2-m)p+N}{4p}}

\displaystyle\begin{cases}p\in[1,\infty]&\text{ if }N=2,m\in(0,1),\\

Now if

assuming

as part of hypothesis in case

and see that

\displaystyle\int(u\cdot\nabla)u\cdot\Delta u\lesssim

\displaystyle\lVert u\rVert_{W^{m,p}}\lVert\nabla u\rVert_{L^{2}}^{\frac{(m+3)p-N}{2p}}\lVert\nabla u\rVert_{H^{2}}^{\frac{(1-m)p+N}{2p}}

(3.3)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{8}\lVert\Delta\nabla u\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+C(\lVert u\rVert_{W^{m,p}}^{\frac{4p}{(3+m)p-N}}+1)\lVert\nabla u\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}

by Young’s inequality. As mentioned, we also need an

estimate; thus, taking

inner-products on (

) with

(3.4)

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\lVert u\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+\lVert\Delta u\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}=\lambda\lVert\nabla u\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}-\int(u\cdot\nabla)u\cdot u

where we estimate

\displaystyle\int(u\cdot\nabla)u\cdot u

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\lVert u\rVert_{L^{\frac{Np}{N-mp}}}\lVert u\rVert_{L^{\frac{2Np}{Np+mp-N}}}\lVert\nabla u\rVert_{L^{\frac{2Np}{Np+mp-N}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\lVert u\rVert_{W^{m,p}}\lVert u\rVert_{L^{2}}^{\frac{(m+3)p-N}{2p}}\lVert u\rVert_{H^{2}}^{\frac{(1-m)p+N}{2p}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\lVert u\rVert_{W^{m,p}}\Big(\lVert u\rVert_{L^{2}}^{\frac{(m+3)p-N}{2p}}\lVert\nabla u\rVert_{L^{2}}^{\frac{(1-m)p+N}{4p}}\lVert\Delta\nabla u\rVert_{L^{2}}^{\frac{(1-m)p+N}{4p}}+\lVert u\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\Big)

(3.5)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{8}\lVert\Delta\nabla u\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\Big(1+\lVert u\rVert_{W^{m,p}}+\lVert u\rVert_{W^{m,p}}^{\frac{4p}{(3+m)p-N}}\Big)\lVert u\rVert_{H^{1}}^{2}

by H
\ddot{\mathrm{o}}
lder’s inequality, Sobolev embedding
W^{m,p}(\mathbb{T}^{N})\hookrightarrow L^{\frac{Np}{N-mp}}(\mathbb{T}^{N})
assuming
p<\frac{N}{m}
in case
m\in(0,1)
as part of hypothesis, (
3.2a
)-(
3.2b
), and Young’s inequality. Applying (
2.10
) and (
3.3
) to (

), and (
3.5
) to (

Theorem 3.1

Suppose

where

(3.6)

\begin{cases}p\in[1,\frac{N}{m}),r\in(\frac{4}{3},\frac{4}{1+m}]&\text{ if }N=2,m\in(0,1),\\

In comparison to Remark

\displaystyle-\int(u\cdot\nabla)u_{1}u_{1}=

,

\displaystyle C\int\lvert\nabla u\rvert^{3}\lesssim

\displaystyle\lVert\nabla u\rVert_{L^{p}}\lVert\nabla u\rVert_{L^{2}}\lVert\nabla u\rVert_{L^{\frac{2p}{p-2}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\lVert\nabla u\rVert_{L^{p}}\lVert\nabla u\rVert_{L^{2}}\lVert\nabla u\rVert_{L^{2}}^{\frac{2p-N}{2p}}\lVert\nabla u\rVert_{H^{2}}^{\frac{N}{2p}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\lVert\Delta\nabla u\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+C(\lVert\nabla u\rVert_{L^{p}}^{\frac{4p}{4p-N}}+\lVert\nabla u\rVert_{L^{p}})\lVert\nabla u\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}

by H
\ddot{\mathrm{o}}
lder’s, Gagliardo-Nirenberg, and Young’s inequalities. This only leads to a criterion of
\nabla u\in L_{T}^{r}L_{x}^{p}
where
\frac{N}{p}+\frac{2}{r}=2+\frac{N}{2p}
,
p\in[2,\infty],r\in[1,\frac{8}{8-N}]
. In fact, we approach this estimate differently from the NSE as follows in both cases
N\in\{2,3\}
:

\displaystyle C\int\lvert\nabla u\rvert^{3}\lesssim

\displaystyle\lVert\nabla u\rVert_{L^{p}}\lVert\nabla u\rVert_{L^{\frac{2p}{p-1}}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\lVert\nabla u\rVert_{L^{p}}\lVert\nabla u\rVert_{L^{2}}^{\frac{4p-N}{2p}}\lVert\nabla u\rVert_{H^{2}}^{\frac{N}{2p}}

(3.19)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{4}\lVert\Delta\nabla u\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+C(\lVert\nabla u\rVert_{L^{p}}^{\frac{4p}{4p-N}}+\lVert\nabla u\rVert_{L^{p}})\lVert\nabla u\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}

by H
\ddot{\mathrm{o}}
lder’s inequality, (
3.2a
) and Young’s inequality. We also continue from (
3.4
)
and estimate the nonlinear term as

\displaystyle-\int(u\cdot\nabla)u\cdot u\leq

\displaystyle\lVert\nabla u\rVert_{L^{p}}\lVert u\rVert_{L^{\frac{2p}{p-1}}}^{2}\lesssim\lVert\nabla u\rVert_{L^{p}}\lVert u\rVert_{L^{2}}^{\frac{4p-N}{2p}}\lVert u\rVert_{H^{2}}^{\frac{N}{2p}}

(3.20)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{8}\lVert\Delta u\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+C(\lVert\nabla u\rVert_{L^{p}}^{\frac{4p}{4p-N}}+\lVert\nabla u\rVert_{L^{p}})\lVert u\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}

by H
\ddot{\mathrm{o}}
lder’s inequality (
3.2a
) and Young’s inequality. Considering (
3.18
), (
3.19
) and (
3.20
) in (
2.4
) and (
3.4
) gives us the following result.

Theorem 3.12
.

Suppose
\nabla u\in L_{T}^{r}L_{x}^{p}
where
p\in[1,\infty],r\in[1,\frac{4}{4-N}]
satisfy

\frac{N}{p}+\frac{2}{r}=2+\frac{N}{2p}.

Then the
N
D KSE is globally well-posed in
H^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{N})
.

Taking
p=1
turns
2+\frac{N}{2p}
to
2+\frac{N}{2}
. We emphasize again that
2+\frac{N}{2}
is remarkably larger than 2 in (
2.3b
).

3.4.
Results on
\nabla\cdot u
, and
\partial_{2}u_{2}

Next, we aim to obtain a result on divergence
\nabla\cdot u
, which is an improvement of
\nabla u
, and in fact, even
\partial_{2}u_{2}
at the optimal level.

If we decide to work on the
H^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{N})
-estimate, then we can look at (
2.4
) again

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\lVert\nabla u\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+\lVert\Delta\nabla u\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}=\int(u\cdot\nabla)u\cdot\Delta u+\lambda\lVert\Delta u\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Now if we integrate by parts, we can get

\int(u\cdot\nabla)u\cdot\Delta u=-\sum_{i,j,k=1}^{N}\int\left((\partial_{i}u_{i})u_{j}\partial_{k}^{2}u_{j}+u_{i}u_{j}\partial_{i}\partial_{k}^{2}u_{j}\right)

or

\int(u\cdot\nabla)u\cdot\Delta u=-\sum_{i,j,k=1}^{N}\int\left(\partial_{k}u_{i}\partial_{i}u_{j}\partial_{k}u_{j}-\frac{1}{2}\partial_{i}u_{i}\lvert\partial_{k}u_{j}\rvert^{2}\right).

Although we have separated the divergence
\nabla\cdot u
in
(\partial_{i}u_{i})u_{j}\partial_{k}^{2}u_{j}
or
\frac{1}{2}\partial_{i}u_{i}\lvert\partial_{k}u_{j}\rvert^{2}
, this will require regularity criteria of an additional term besides the divergence due to
u_{i}u_{j}\partial_{i}\partial_{k}^{2}u_{j}
or
\partial_{k}u_{i}\partial_{i}u_{j}\partial_{k}u_{j}
.

In fact, thanks to Theorem
3.1
we can work in
L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{N})
-norm and avoid this issue. Indeed, we can restart from (
3.4
) where we can estimate
\lambda\lVert\nabla u\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\frac{1}{4}\lVert\Delta u\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\lVert u\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}
identically to (
3.10
), while

-\int(u\cdot\nabla)u\cdot u=-\int(u\cdot\nabla)\frac{1}{2}\lvert u\rvert^{2}=\frac{1}{2}\int(\nabla\cdot u)\lvert u\rvert^{2}

(note that this term vanishes for the NSE but not for the
N
D KSE when
N>1
). Now if we estimate

\int(\nabla\cdot u)\lvert u\rvert^{2}\leq\lVert\nabla\cdot u\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\lVert u\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2},

we can get a criterion in terms of
\int_{0}^{T}\lVert\nabla\cdot u\rVert_{L^{\infty}}d\tau
analogously to the 3D Euler equations (
[
3
]
). But to make full use of the fourth-order diffusion, we compute similarly to (
3.19
)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int(\nabla\cdot u)\lvert u\rvert^{2}\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\lVert\nabla\cdot u\rVert_{L^{p}}\lVert u\rVert_{L^{\frac{2p}{p-1}}}^{2}\lesssim\lVert\nabla\cdot u\rVert_{L^{p}}\lVert u\rVert_{L^{2}}^{\frac{4p-N}{2p}}\lVert u\rVert_{H^{2}}^{\frac{N}{2p}}

(3.21)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\lVert\Delta u\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+C(\lVert\nabla\cdot u\rVert_{L^{p}}^{\frac{4p}{4p-N}}+\lVert\nabla\cdot u\rVert_{L^{p}})\lVert u\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}

by H
\ddot{\mathrm{o}}
lder’s inequality, (
3.2a
), and Young’s inequality. Because Theorem
3.1
implies that
L_{T}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}
-bound leads immediately to the global well-posedness of the
N
D KSE, we obtain the following result.

Theorem 3.13
.

Suppose
\nabla\cdot u\in L_{T}^{r}L_{x}^{p}
where
p\in[1,\infty],r\in[1,\frac{4}{4-N}]
satisfy

\frac{N}{p}+\frac{2}{r}=2+\frac{N}{2p}.

Then the
N
D KSE is globally well-posed in
H^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{N})
.

By a similar trick to the proof of Theorem
3.6
, we can also obtain the following result in the 2D case:

Proposition 3.14
.

Suppose
\partial_{2}u_{2}\in L_{T}^{r}L_{x}^{p}
where
p\in[1,\infty],r\in[1,2]
satisfy

\frac{2}{p}+\frac{2}{r}=2+\frac{1}{p}.

Then
u_{1}\in L_{T}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}\cap L_{T}^{2}H_{x}^{2}
.

Proof.

This is almost identical to the previous computation for the criteria of
\nabla\cdot u
. We restart from (
3.12
) where
\lambda\lVert\nabla u_{1}\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}
may be handled again identically to (
3.10
) while similarly to (
3.21
)

\displaystyle-\int(u\cdot\nabla)u_{1}u_{1}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int\partial_{2}u_{2}(u_{1})^{2}\leq\frac{1}{2}\lVert\partial_{2}u_{2}\rVert_{L^{p}}\lVert u_{1}\rVert_{L^{\frac{2p}{p-1}}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\lVert\partial_{2}u_{2}\rVert_{L^{p}}\lVert u_{1}\rVert_{L^{2}}^{\frac{2p-1}{p}}\lVert u_{1}\rVert_{H^{2}}^{\frac{1}{p}}

(3.22)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\lVert\Delta u_{1}\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+C(\lVert\partial_{2}u_{2}\rVert_{L^{p}}^{\frac{2p}{2p-1}}+\lVert\partial_{2}u_{2}\rVert_{L^{p}})\lVert u_{1}\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Now applying (
3.22
) to (
3.12
) completes the proof of Proposition
3.14
.
∎

We can raise this regularity immediately as follows:

Proposition 3.15
.

Suppose
\partial_{2}u_{2}\in L_{T}^{r}L_{x}^{p}
where
p\in[1,\infty],r\in[1,2]
satisfy

\frac{2}{p}+\frac{2}{r}=2+\frac{1}{p}.

Then
u_{2}\in L_{T}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}\cap L_{T}^{2}H_{x}^{2}
.

Proof.

We restart from (
3.9
) again where
\lambda\lVert\nabla u_{2}\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}
may be handled identically to (
3.10
) while

\displaystyle-\int(u\cdot\nabla)u_{2}u_{2}=

\displaystyle-\int u_{1}\partial_{1}u_{2}u_{2}\leq\lVert u_{1}\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\lVert\partial_{1}u_{2}\rVert_{L^{2}}\lVert u_{2}\rVert_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle(\lVert\nabla u_{2}\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+\lVert u_{1}\rVert_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\lVert u_{2}\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2})

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle(\lVert u_{2}\rVert_{L^{2}}\lVert\Delta u_{2}\rVert_{L^{2}}+\lVert u_{1}\rVert_{H^{2}}^{2}\lVert u_{2}\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2})

(3.23)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\lVert\Delta u_{2}\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+C(1+\lVert u_{1}\rVert_{H^{2}}^{2})\lVert u_{2}\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}

by the Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality as in (
3.10
), the Sobolev embedding
H^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2})\hookrightarrow L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
, and Young’s inequality. Therefore, applying (
3.23
) to (
3.9
) leads to

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\lVert u_{2}\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+\lVert\Delta u_{2}\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\frac{1}{2}\lVert\Delta u_{2}\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+C(1+\lVert u_{1}\rVert_{H^{2}}^{2})\lVert u_{2}\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Now applying Proposition
3.14
completes the proof of Proposition
3.15
.
∎

By Propositions
3.14
-
3.15
, we deduce that
u\in L_{T}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}
and thus by Theorem
3.1
we see that the 2D KSE is globally well-posed in
H^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
as follows:

Theorem 3.16
.

Suppose
\partial_{2}u_{2}\in L_{T}^{r}L_{x}^{p}
where
p\in[1,\infty],r\in[1,2]
satisfy

\frac{2}{p}+\frac{2}{r}=2+\frac{1}{p}.

Then 2D KSE (
1.1
) is globally well-posed in
H^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
.

Remark 3.17
.

We emphasize again that Theorem
3.16
is surprising due to the KSE lacking divergence-free property and that
2+\frac{1}{p}
in Theorem
3.16
is same as the condition in Theorem
3.12
; in comparison, “
\frac{3(p+2)}{4p}
for
p>2
” in (
2.12b
) is much smaller than “2” in (
2.3b
).

Lastly, we aim for
\partial_{2}u_{1}
. It is well-known that in general, such a criterion is expected to be more difficult than
\partial_{2}u_{2}
where the components of
\nabla
and
u
match; indeed,
\frac{p+3}{2p}<\frac{3(p+2)}{4p}
in (
2.12b
)-(
2.12c
). Now if we return again to (
3.4
) where we can rely on the same estimates in (
3.10
) to handle the term
\lambda\lVert\nabla u\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}
, then we are only faced with a nonlinear term of

\displaystyle\int(u\cdot\nabla)u\cdot u=-\int\left((u\cdot\nabla)u_{1}u_{1}+(u\cdot\nabla)u_{2}u_{2}\right).

The first term allows us to separate
\partial_{2}u_{1}
as follows:

(3.24)

\displaystyle-\int(u\cdot\nabla)u_{1}u_{1}=-\int\left(u_{1}\partial_{1}u_{1}u_{1}+u_{2}\partial_{2}u_{1}u_{1}\right)=-\int u_{2}\partial_{2}u_{1}u_{1};

however, for the second term we are faced with

\displaystyle\int(u\cdot\nabla)u_{2}u_{2}=-\int\left(u_{1}\partial_{1}u_{2}u_{2}+u_{2}\partial_{2}u_{2}u_{2}\right)=-\int u_{1}\partial_{1}u_{2}u_{2}.

We seem to face a significant difficulty here. We can integrate by parts to deduce
\frac{1}{2}\int\partial_{1}u_{1}u_{2}u_{2}
, but
\partial_{2}u_{1}
is nowhere to be found. One idea would be to rely on some analogue of anisotropic Sobolev inequality that has proven to be useful in the theory of fluid PDEs such as the NSE and the MHD system (e.g.,
[
10
, Lemma 1.1]
). However, those results are typically all on the whole space such as
\mathbb{R}^{2}
because they rely on the decay at infinity; i.e., while

f(x)=\int_{-\infty}^{x}\partial_{z}f(z)dz,

an analogous attempt in
\mathbb{T}^{2}
gives

f(x)=\int_{-\pi}^{x}\partial_{z}f(z)dz+f(-\pi).

Remarkably, there is a trick that seems to be special to the KSE here. Let us make our argument precise by working on the equation of
\phi
rather than
u
and attain a criterion in terms of
\partial_{12}\phi
, informally
\partial_{1}u_{2}
as desired (see Theorem
3.20
).

3.5.
Results on
\phi
and
\partial_{12}\phi

We emphasize that the following two results focus on (
1.2
), independently of (
1.1
). Let us start with a criterion in terms of
\phi
.

Theorem 3.18
.

Suppose
\phi\in L_{T}^{r}W_{x}^{m,p}
where
m\in[0,1)
, and

\displaystyle\begin{cases}p\in[1,\frac{N}{m}),r\in(2,\frac{4}{m}]&\text{ if }N=2,m\in(0,1),\\
p\in(\frac{N}{m+2},\frac{N}{m}),r\in(2,\infty)&\text{ if }N=3,m\in(0,1),\\
p\in(\frac{N}{2},\infty],r\in[2,\infty)&\text{ if }N\in\{2,3\},m=0.\end{cases}

satisfy

\displaystyle\frac{N}{p}+\frac{2}{r}=\frac{2+m}{2}+\frac{N}{2p}.

Then the
N
D KSE (
1.2
) is globally well-posed in
L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{N})
.

Remark 3.19
.

Very recently, Feng and Mazzucato
[
26
]
showed a blowup criterion of
\phi\in L_{T}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}
for 2D KSE. If
N=2
, we only require
\frac{2}{p}+\frac{2}{r}\leq 1+\frac{1}{p}
, allowing cases such as
L_{T}^{\infty}L_{x}^{p}
for any
p>1
and
L_{T}^{4}L_{x}^{2}
.

Proof.

We take
L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{N})
-inner products on (
1.2
) with
\phi
to deduce

(3.25)

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\lVert\phi\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+\lVert\Delta\phi\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}=-\lambda\int\Delta\phi\phi-\frac{1}{2}\int\lvert\nabla\phi\rvert^{2}\phi.

Now

(3.26)

-\int\Delta\phi\phi\leq\lVert\Delta\phi\rVert_{L^{2}}\lVert\phi\rVert_{L^{2}}\leq\frac{1}{4}\lVert\Delta\phi\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\lVert\phi\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}

by Young’s inequality, while

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\int\lvert\nabla\phi\rvert^{2}\phi\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\lVert\phi\rVert_{L^{\frac{Np}{N-mp}}}\lVert\nabla\phi\rVert_{L^{\frac{2Np}{Np+mp-N}}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\lVert\phi\rVert_{W^{m,p}}\lVert\phi\rVert_{L^{2}}^{\frac{(m+2)p-N}{2p}}\lVert\phi\rVert_{H^{2}}^{\frac{(2-m)p+N}{2p}}

(3.27)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\lVert\Delta\phi\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\Big(\lVert\phi\rVert_{W^{m,p}}^{\frac{4p}{(2+m)p-N}}+\lVert\phi\rVert_{W^{m,p}}\Big)\lVert\phi\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}

by H
\ddot{\mathrm{o}}
lder’s inequality, Sobolev embedding
W^{m,p}(\mathbb{T}^{N})\hookrightarrow L^{\frac{Np}{N-mp}}(\mathbb{T}^{N})
in case
m\in(0,1)
so that
p<\frac{N}{m}
by hypothesis, (
3.2b
), and Young’s inequality. By applying (
3.26
)-(
3.27
) to (
3.25
), we deduce that
\phi\in L_{T}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}\cap L_{T}^{2}H_{x}^{2}
and thus the desired result.
∎

In the following theorem, we assume
N=2
and aim for a result analogous to a criterion of (
1.1
) in terms of
\partial_{1}u_{2}
as promised.

Theorem 3.20
.

Suppose
\partial_{12}\phi\in L_{T}^{r}L_{x}^{p}
where
p\in[1,\infty],r\in[1,2]
satisfy

\frac{2}{p}+\frac{2}{r}=2+\frac{1}{p}.

Then 2D KSE (
1.2
) is globally well-posed in
L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
.

Proof.

Let us consider the equation (
1.2
), take
L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
-inner products with
-\Delta\phi
so that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\lVert\nabla\phi\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+\lVert\Delta\nabla\phi\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda\lVert\Delta\phi\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\int\partial_{1}((\partial_{1}\phi)^{2}+(\partial_{2}\phi)^{2})\partial_{1}\phi-\frac{1}{2}\int\partial_{2}((\partial_{1}\phi)^{2}+(\partial_{2}\phi)^{2})\partial_{2}\phi

(3.28)

\displaystyle\triangleq\lambda\lVert\Delta\phi\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+I+II.

It is straight-forward to estimate

(3.29)

\lambda\lVert\Delta\phi\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\frac{1}{8}\lVert\Delta\nabla\phi\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\lVert\nabla\phi\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}

by Young’s inequality. Next, we estimate

(3.30)

\displaystyle I=

\displaystyle-\int\frac{1}{3}\partial_{1}(\partial_{1}\phi)^{3}+\partial_{2}\phi\partial_{12}\phi\partial_{1}\phi

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\lVert\partial_{2}\phi\rVert_{L^{\frac{2p}{p-1}}}\lVert\partial_{12}\phi\rVert_{L^{p}}\lVert\partial_{1}\phi\rVert_{L^{\frac{2p}{p-1}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\lVert\partial_{2}\phi\rVert_{L^{2}}^{\frac{2p-1}{2p}}\lVert\partial_{2}\phi\rVert_{H^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2p}}\lVert\partial_{12}\phi\rVert_{L^{p}}\lVert\partial_{1}\phi\rVert_{L^{2}}^{\frac{2p-1}{2p}}\lVert\partial_{1}\phi\rVert_{H^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2p}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{8}(\lVert\partial_{1}\Delta\phi\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+\lVert\partial_{2}\Delta\phi\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2})

\displaystyle+C(\lVert\partial_{12}\phi\rVert_{L^{p}}+\lVert\partial_{12}\phi\rVert_{L^{p}}^{\frac{4p}{4p-1}}+\lVert\partial_{12}\phi\rVert_{L^{p}}^{\frac{2p}{2p-1}})\lVert\nabla\phi\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}

by H
\ddot{\mathrm{o}}
lder’s inequality, (
3.2a
) and Young’s inequality. Next, we get a break here as it turns out that we can apply identical estimates in
I
to
II
because

\displaystyle II=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\int\partial_{2}((\partial_{1}\phi)^{2}+(\partial_{2}\phi)^{2})\partial_{2}\phi

(3.31)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int[(\partial_{1}\phi)(\partial_{12}\phi)+(\partial_{2}\phi)(\partial_{22}\phi)]\partial_{2}\phi=-\int\partial_{1}\phi\partial_{12}\phi\partial_{2}\phi.

By applying (
3.29
)-(
3.31
) to (
3.28
), we obtain
\phi\in L_{T}^{\infty}\dot{H}_{x}^{1}\cap L_{T}^{2}\dot{H}_{x}^{3}
. It is not difficult to ensure the lower regularity at
L_{T}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}
from such high regularity by going back to (
3.25
). Indeed, we can compute from (
3.25
)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\lVert\phi\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle+\lVert\Delta\phi\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\lambda\lVert\nabla\phi\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\lVert\phi\rVert_{L^{2}}\lVert\nabla\phi\rVert_{L^{2}}\lVert\nabla\phi\rVert_{L^{\infty}}

(3.32)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\lVert\phi\rVert_{L^{2}}\lVert\Delta\phi\rVert_{L^{2}}+\lVert\phi\rVert_{L^{2}}\lVert\nabla\phi\rVert_{L^{2}}(\lVert\nabla\phi\rVert_{L^{2}}+\lVert\Delta\nabla\phi\rVert_{L^{2}})

where we used the same estimate in (
3.10
) and the Sobolev embedding
H^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2})\hookrightarrow L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
. This estimate (
3.32
) immediately implies

(3.33)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\lVert\phi\rVert_{L^{2}}\lesssim\lVert\nabla\phi\rVert_{L^{2}}+\lVert\Delta\nabla\phi\rVert_{L^{2}}+\lVert\nabla\phi\rVert_{L^{2}}(\lVert\nabla\phi\rVert_{L^{2}}+\lVert\Delta\nabla\phi\rVert_{L^{2}})

due to Young’s inequality. Integrating (
3.33
) over
[0,t]
now and relying on the fact that
\phi\in L_{T}^{\infty}\dot{H}_{x}^{1}\cap L_{T}^{2}\dot{H}_{x}^{3}
complete the proof of Theorem
3.20
.
∎

4.
Computational Results

In this section, we present some computational results. In particular, we simulate solutions to the 2D KSE. All simulations are done for the scalar form of the system (
1.2
),
(except for the simulation used to generate Figure
1.1
, which directly simulated (
1.1
)),
with the slight modification of subtracting the spatial average at each time step, as is common in simulations of the KSE (see, e.g.,
[
39
]
). Note that this modification formally preserves the differential form of the KSE, system (
1.1
). In this section, we assume that
u=\nabla\phi
;
that is, here,
u
is essentially a notation for
\nabla\phi
.

The existing literature on computational studies of the 2D KSE is fairly limited, but we refer to
[
39
]
where an exhaustive computational study of the KSE was carried out in various rectangular domains. Various modifications to the 2D KSE have been investigated computationally in several works; see, e.g.,
[
72
,
50
]
.

Our simulations were computed via MATLAB (R2020a) on the periodic square domain
[-\pi,\pi)^{2}
, using a uniform rectangular grid
256\times 256
. Derivatives were computed using spectral methods (also called pseudo-spectral methods), specifically using Matlab’s
fftn
and
ifftn
implementations of the
N
D discrete Fourier transform, making sure to respect the 2/3’s dealiasing law for quadratic nonlinearities, as well as the vanishing of the Nyquist frequency for only odd-order derivatives. Time stepping was carried out using a 4th-order ETD-RK4 method (see, e.g.,
[
40
,
41
]
) to handle the linear terms implicitly, while the nonlinear term was computed explicitly and in physical space. In particular, a complex contour with 64 nodes (technically 32 nodes plus a symmetry condition) was used to handle the removable singularities in the ETD-RK4 coefficients, as proposed in
[
40
]
. We used a uniform time-step of
\Delta t=2.1227\times 10^{-5}
, well below the advective CFL given by
\Delta t<\Delta x/(\tfrac{1}{2}\|\nabla\phi\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{2}))})\approx 4.4\times 10^{-5}
. All simulations presented here were well-resolved at each time step, in the sense that the magnitude of the energy spectrum of
\varphi
was below machine precision (
\epsilon=2.2204\times 10^{-16}
in MATLAB) for all wave numbers above the
2/3
’s dealising cutoff (i.e., for all
\vec{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
with
|\vec{k}|\geq 256/3
). This can be seen in the plots of the spectrum at each time step in Figure

Here, we leave details concerning the local well-posedness of the KSE (
1.1
) in
H^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{N})
for
N\in\{2,3\}
, formally stated in Theorem
2.2
. The proof follows the argument in
[
50
]
, which in turn follows
[
51
]
; we only sketch the main steps. We consider a Galerkin approximation with
P_{n}
being the projection onto the Fourier modes of order up to
n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
:

P_{n}u(x)\triangleq\sum_{\lvert k\rvert\leq n}\hat{u}(k)e^{ix\cdot k}.

We consider the following Galerkin approximation system

(5.1a)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u^{n}+P_{n}((u^{n}\cdot\nabla)u^{n})=-\lambda\Delta u^{n}-\Delta^{2}u^{n},

(5.1b)

\displaystyle u^{n}(\cdot,0)\triangleq P_{n}u^{in}(\cdot).

Relying on
[
51
, Theorem 3.1]
we can deduce the following statement:

Proposition 5.1
.

Given initial data
u^{in}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{N})
, there exists
T=T(\lVert u^{in}\rVert_{H^{1}})>0
such that (
5.1a
)-(
5.1b
) has a solution

(5.2)

u^{n}\in L^{\infty}([0,T];H^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{N}))\cap L^{2}([0,T];H^{3}(\mathbb{T}^{N}));

additionally,
\partial_{t}u^{n}\in L^{2}([0,T];H^{-1}(\mathbb{T}^{N}))
. Moreover, these bounds are independent of
n
. Finally, if
T^{\ast}
is the maximal existence time and
T^{\ast}<\infty
, then
\limsup_{t\to T^{\ast}}\lVert u^{n}(t)\rVert_{H^{1}}=+\infty
.

Proof.

By
[
51
, Theorem 3.1]
, it can be immediately shown that given
u^{in}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{N})
, there exists a unique solution
u^{n}\in C^{1}([0,T_{n}),H^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{N})\cap O^{M})
for some
T_{n}>0
and
O^{M}\triangleq\{f\in H^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{N}):\lVert f\rVert_{H^{1}}\leq M\}
. Now we can take
L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{N})
-inner products on (
5.1a
) with
u
and then with
-\Delta u^{n}
, sum the resulting equations and prove that there exists a constant
c\geq 0
such that

\frac{d}{dt}\lVert u^{n}\rVert_{H^{1}}\leq c(1+\lVert u^{n}\rVert_{H^{1}})^{3}

We can fix such a constant
c>0
and deduce that
H^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{N})
-norm does not blow up for all
t<T^{\ast}\triangleq\frac{1}{2c(1+\lVert u^{in}\rVert_{H^{1}})^{2}}
. Hence,
T_{n}>T\triangleq\frac{T^{\ast}}{2}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
u^{n}
has the regularity of (
5.2
). This regularity leads to to
\int_{0}^{T}\lVert\partial_{t}u^{n}\rVert_{H^{-1}}^{2}d\tau\lesssim 1
by (
1.1
) and a standard argument (see
[
51
, Cor. 3.2]
) completes the proof of Proposition
5.1
.
∎

5.1
, we can deduce the following convergence results by standard compactness lemma (e.g.,
[
63

,
[

): weak
∗
in
L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{N}))
; weak in

; strong in
L^{2}(0,T;H^{s}(\mathbb{T}^{N}))
for

C([0,T];H^{s}(\mathbb{T}^{N}))

]

On fillings of contact links of quotient singularities
Zhengyi Zhou

Abstract
. We study several aspects of fillings for links of general isolated quotient singularities using Floer theory, including co-fillings, Weinstein fillings, strong fillings, exact fillings and exact orbifold fillings, focusing on the non-existence of exact fillings of contact links of isolated terminal quotient singularities. We provide an extensive list of isolated terminal quotient singularities whose contact links are not exactly fillable, including ℂ n / ( ℤ / 2 ) {\mathbb{C}}^{n}/({\mathbb{Z}}/2) for n ≥ 3 n\geq 3 , which settles a conjecture of Eliashberg, quotient singularities from general cyclic group actions and finite subgroups of S ​ U ​ ( 2 ) SU(2) , and all terminal quotient singularities in complex dimension 3 3 . We also obtain uniqueness of the orbifold diffeomorphism type of exact orbifold fillings of contact links of some isolated terminal quotient singularities.



1.
Introduction

Contact structures arise naturally from symplectic manifolds with a convex boundary. The study of contact manifolds as convex boundaries of symplectic manifolds and their symplectic fillings was pioneered by Gromov
[
40
]
and Eliashberg
[
29
]
. In particular, they showed that any contact 3-fold with a (weak) symplectic filling is necessarily tight. This result was generalized to all dimensions by Massot, Niederkrüger and Wendl,
[

]
, see also
[

. As a consequence, the different types of fillability give a coarse understanding of the landscape of contact structures beyond the fundamental dichotomy between overtwistedness and tightness
[

,
28
]
. Namely, we have the following inclusions among classes of contact structures:

\{\text{Weinstein fillable}\}\subset\{\text{exactly fillable}\}\subset\{\text{strongly fillable}\}\subset\{\text{weakly fillable}\}\subset\{\text{tight}\}.

All of the inclusions are proper: in dimension three, they were shown by Bowden
[
13
]
, Ghiggini
[
36
]
, Eliashberg
[
32
]
, Etnyre and Honda
[
34
]
, and in higher dimensions by Bowden, Crowley, and Stipsicz
[
14
]
, the author
[
72
]
, Bowden, Gironella, and Moreno
[
15
]
, Massot, Niederkrüger, and Wendl
[
45
]
.

The guiding question in understanding the second inclusion is the following conjecture by Eliashberg:

Conjecture A

(
[
25
, §1.9]
)
.

(\mathbb{R}\mathbb{P}^{2n-1},\xi_{\rm std})
is not exact fillable if
n\geq 3
.

Fortunately, the real projective space is natural and simple enough so that one can understand both pseudo-holomorphic curves as well as the topology sufficiently well. We showed in
[

]
that Conjecture

holds if

. The odd

[
37
]

Theorem B
.

For
n\geq 3
,
(\mathbb{R}\mathbb{P}^{2n-1},\xi_{\rm std})
is not exactly fillable.

When
n=2^{k}
, the total Chern class of
\xi_{\rm std}
becomes trivial, which sabotages the topological obstructions in
[
72
]
. Hence to prove

[
38
]
and study the secondary coproduct on positive symplectic cohomology, which was proposed by Seidel
[
63
]
, defined by Ekholm and Oancea
[

]
, and recently studied extensively by Cieliebak, Hingston and Oancea
[
20
,
21
,
23

. Then by a result of Eliashberg, Ganatra, and Lazarev
[

, we can get information on the intersection form of the filling. On the topological side, the contradiction is a non-integer Chern number derived from the Hirzebruch signature theorem. The argument essentially boils down to studying the
2

1.1.
Fillings of Contact links of isolated quotient singularities

Real projective spaces with the standard contact structure fall in a much larger class of contact manifolds: contact links of isolated singularities. Links of isolated singularities are natural and interesting examples of contact manifolds, which are always strongly fillable by Hironaka’s resolution of singularities. In the case of smoothable singularities, the smoothing provides natural exact/Weinstein fillings to the contact link. In complex dimension
2
, contact properties of links, especially filling properties, in particular Weinstein fillings, were extensively studied, see
[
55
]
for an introduction and references therein for details. In such a low dimension, tools from low dimensional topology and intersection theory of pseudo-holomorphic curves allow us to obtain classification results. For example, the diffeomorphism type of symplectic fillings of lens spaces is classified by Lisca
[
44
]
. Moreover, such classification can be strengthened to a classification up to symplectic deformation by a powerful result of Wendl
[
69
]
. Moreover, there are surprising connections between the contact properties of the link and algebro-geometric properties of the singularity, e.g. the beautiful theorem of McLean
[
48
]
relating the terminality of the singularity with the SFT degrees.

In this paper, we study, by means of Floer theories, symplectic fillings of contact links of isolated quotient singularities in any dimension, where
(\mathbb{R}\mathbb{P}^{2n-1},\xi_{\rm std})
should be understood as the contact link of the simplest quotient singularity
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}/({\mathbb{Z}}/2)
. More generally, let
G
be a finite subgroup of
U(n)
, such that every nontrivial element in
G
does not have one as an eigenvalue. Since
G
preserves the standard contact structure on the unit sphere
S^{2n-1}\subset{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
, we get a natural contact manifold
(S^{2n-1}/G,\xi_{\rm std})
, which is the contact link of the isolated quotient singularity
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}/G
. Our discussion will cover co-fillings (a.k.a. semi-fillings), Weinstein fillings, and strong fillings, but with an emphasis on exact fillings and exact orbifold fillings, recalled in the following.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
(Y,\xi)
be a co-oriented contact manifold, then a symplectic manifold

(1)

(2)

(3)

(4)

(5)

The general definition of a weak filling was given in

1.2.
Weinstein fillings, strong fillings, and co-fillings.

Unlike the situation in complex dimension

Let

(1)

If

(2)

If

of the contact link, then

(3)

If

1.3.
Non-existence of exact fillings

The first example of a strongly but not exactly fillable contact manifold was found in dimension
3
by Ghiggini
[
36
]
, and many more examples in dimension
3
were found by Min
[
51
]
. In higher dimensions, such examples were predicted by Conjecture
A
. It is worth noting that the threshold
n\geq 3
in Conjecture
A
is precisely the threshold for the singularity
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}/({\mathbb{Z}}/2)
to be terminal. Therefore
B
leads to the following conjecture in
[
72
]
.

Conjecture E
.

Let
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}/G
be an isolated terminal singularity, then the contact link
(S^{2n-1}/G,\xi_{\rm std})
is not exactly fillable.

In view of the conjecture above and Hironaka’s resolution of singularities, isolated terminal quotient singularities shall provide tons of natural contact manifolds with strong fillings that are not exactly fillable. In this paper, we will prove this conjecture for a list of cases.

For general group actions, we have the following result with a simple assumption. Let
\Conj(G)
be the set of conjugacy classes of
G
.

Theorem F
.

Assume
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}/G
is an isolated terminal singularity with
n
odd and
|\Conj(G)|
even, then the contact link
(S^{2n-1}/G,\xi_{\rm std})
has no exact filling.

In the smallest non-trivial dimension, namely complex dimension
3
, terminal quotient singularities are completely classified in
[
52
]
, where the group can be any finite cyclic group. Conjecture
E
indeed holds in those cases.

Theorem G
.

The contact link of any isolated terminal quotient singularity in complex dimension
3
has no exact filling.

We also consider the case of
{\mathbb{Z}}/3,{\mathbb{Z}}/4
actions, more general cyclic group actions as well as finite subgroups of
SU(2)
acting on
{\mathbb{C}}^{2n}
diagonally. The proof of the above results is based on the study of symplectic cohomology of a hypothetical exact filling. The starting point is that the rational first Chern class
c_{1}^{{\mathbb{Q}}}(S^{2n-1}/G,\xi_{\rm std})
vanishes, and all Reeb orbits are of torsion homotopy classes, therefore we have a well-defined rational Conley-Zehnder index assigned to each Reeb orbit. It is important to note that, as a hypothetical filling may not have vanishing first Chern class, those “boundary” Conley-Zehnder indices may not agree with actual Conley-Zehnder indices used in the construction of symplectic cohomology. However, when the singularity is terminal, those boundary Conley-Zehnder indices induce a filtration on the cochain complex of the positive symplectic cohomology, which is compatible with the differential. Moreover, certain pages of the spectral sequence are independent of the filling/augmentation. Therefore, we can compare it with the computation we did in
[
38
, Theorem B]
for the standard exact
orbifold
filling
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}/G
and conclude that the spectral sequence degenerates at some point due to parity reasons. Moreover, we can show that the total dimension
\dim H^{*}(W;{\mathbb{Q}})
is
|\Conj(G)|
, the size of the set of conjugacy classes, for a hypothetical exact filling
W
whenever
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}/G
is terminal. Many topological properties (e.g. intersection form, fundamental groups, cup product length) of the hypothetical filling
W
can be derived this way through the Floer theory, which in many cases lead to contradictions to the existence of
W
.

1.4.

The celebrated Eliashberg-Floer-McDuff theorem

Conjecture H
.

Let
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}/G
be an isolated terminal singularity, then exact

(S^{2n-1}/G,\xi_{\rm std})
have a unique diffeomorphism type
1
1

1

More aggressively, we can conjecture that exact orbifold fillings are unique.
.

By

In this paper, we prove this conjecture in some simple cases.

Theorem I

Let

2.1.
Topology of

Since

Proposition 2.1

The inclusion

Proof.

On the unit sphere

2.3.1.

To describe the product structure on

[(g_{1})]\cup[(g_{2})]=\sum_{(h_{1},h_{2})\in I_{g_{1},g_{2}}}\frac{|C(h_{1}h_{2})|}{|C(h_{1})\cap C(h_{2})|}[(h_{1}h_{2})],

The formula (

2

and one negative puncture asymptotic to

. In view of the pull-push definition of the structural constants in
[

{|C(h_{1}h_{2})|}/{|C(h_{1})\cap C(h_{2})|}
.

5.

In this section, we discuss fundamental groups for

holds for Calabi-Yau fillings by the argument in

Appendix A
Bernoulli numbers

Bernoulli numbers
B_{1},B_{2},\ldots
can be defined as the coefficients which occur in the power series expansion

\frac{x}{\tanh x}=1+\frac{B_{1}}{2!}(2x)^{2}-\frac{B_{2}}{4!}(2x)^{4}+\frac{B_{3}}{6!}(2x)^{6}-\ldots.

Hence they appear as coefficients in
L
-genus naturally. An alternative formula due to Worpitzky
[
71
]
in 1883 for the Bernoulli numbers is given by

(A.1)

B_{n}=(-1)^{n-1}\sum_{r=0}^{2n}\frac{1}{r+1}\sum_{s=0}^{r}(-1)^{s}\binom{r}{s}s^{2n}

One fundamental property of the Bernoulli numbers is the following theorem due to Clausen and Staudt back in 1840.

Theorem A.1

(c.f.
[
50
, Theorem B.3]
)
.

(-1)^{n}B_{n}
is congruent modulo
1
to
\sum\frac{1}{p}
, where the sum is over all primes
p
such that
(p-1)|2n
.

An instant corollary of the theorem is that
B_{n}
, when written in the lowest fraction, is

\frac{N_{n}}{2D_{n}},\text{ where }D_{n}=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}\text{odd prime }p,\\
(p-1)|2n\end{subarray}}p.

The property of Bernoulli numbers we need is the following, whose proof is motivated from Worpitzky’s proof of the Clausen-Staudt Theorem explained in
[
16
]
.

Lemma A.5

Lemma A.6
.

Let
p
be a prime number and
m=\sum_{i=0}^{N}a_{i}p^{i}
, the number of factor
p
in
m!
is given by

\sum_{i=1}^{N}a_{i}\cdot\frac{p^{i}-1}{p-1}

Proof.

Note that there are precisely
\sum_{i\geq j}^{N}a_{i}p^{i-j}
many integers no larger than
m
which are divisible by
p^{j}
. It is clear that number of factor
p
in
m!
is given by

\sum_{j\leq 1}^{N}\sum_{i\geq j}^{N}a_{i}p^{i-j}=\sum_{i\geq 1}^{N}a_{i}\sum_{j=0}^{i-1}{p^{j}}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}a_{i}\cdot\frac{p^{i}-1}{p-1}.

∎

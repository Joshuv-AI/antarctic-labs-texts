Abstract
We study properties of nonnegative functions satisfying (E) − Δ ​ u + u p − M ​ | ∇ u | q = 0 \;-\Delta u+u^{p}-M|\nabla u|^{q}=0 in a domain of ℝ N \mathbb{R}^{N} when p > 1 p>1 , M > 0 M>0 and 1 < q < p 1<q<p . We concentrate our analysis on the solutions of (E) with an isolated singularity, or in an exterior domain, or in the whole space. The existence of such solutions and their behaviours depend strongly on the values of the exponents p p and q q and in particular according to the sign of q − 2 ​ p p + 1 q-\frac{2p}{p+1} , and when q = 2 ​ p p + 1 q=\frac{2p}{p+1} , also on the value of the parameter M M which becomes a key element. The description of the different behaviours is made possible by a sharp analysis of the radial solutions of (E).

Introduction

The aim of this article is to study existence and properties of nonnegative singular solutions of the following equation

\begin{array}[]{lll}{\mathcal{L}}^{M}_{p,q}u:=-\Delta u+u^{p}-M|\nabla u|^{q}=0\end{array}

(1.1
)

in a domain
\Omega
of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
or in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
(
N\geq 2
), where
M
is a real number and
p>q>1
.

In the case
M<0
many results dealing with isolated singularities have been obtained in
[
17
]
. Therefore we will mainly concentrate on the case
M>0
where the two nonlinear terms act in a opposite direction: one is an absorption and
the other is a source. Furthermore they are not of the same type, one involves the function and the other its gradient.

First we consider the case
q=\frac{2p}{p+1}
. Then
(\ref{Z1})
becomes

\begin{array}[]{lll}{\mathcal{L}}^{M}_{p,\frac{2p}{p+1}}u:=-\Delta u+u^{p}-M|\nabla u|^{\frac{2p}{p+1}}=0,\end{array}

(1.2
)

and this equation is invariant under the scaling transformation
T_{\ell}
,
\ell>0
, defined by

T_{\ell}[u](x)=\ell^{\alpha}u(\ell x).

(1.3
)

In that case there may exist self-similar solutions, necessarily under the form
u(x)=u(r,s)=r^{-\alpha}\omega(s)
, where
(r,s)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times S^{N-1}
are the spherical coordinates in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
. The function
\omega
is a solution of the following equation on
S^{N-1}

-\Delta^{\prime}\omega+\ell_{N,p}\omega+\omega^{p}-M\left(\alpha^{2}\omega^{2}+|\nabla^{\prime}\omega|^{2}\right)^{\frac{p}{p+1}}=0,

(1.4
)

where
\Delta^{\prime}
and
\nabla^{\prime}
denote respectively the Laplace-Beltrami operator and the tangential gradient on
S^{N-1}
, identified with the covariant gradient on
S^{N-1}
for the metric induced by the standard one in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, and where

\alpha=\frac{2}{p-1}\,\text{ and }\,\ell_{N,p}=\alpha K\,

(1.5
)

with

K=N-2-\alpha={\displaystyle\frac{(N-2)p-N}{N-2}}.

(1.6
)

The nonzero constant solutions of
(\ref{Z3})
are the positive zeros of the function

{\mathcal{P}}_{\!{}_{M}}(x)=x^{p-1}-M\alpha^{\frac{2p}{p+1}}x^{\frac{p-1}{p+1}}+\ell_{N,p}.

(1.7
)

The following value of the parameter
M
, which exists only if
N\geq 3
and
p\geq\frac{N}{N-2}

(\ref{Z3})
:

m^{*}:=(p+1)\left({\displaystyle\frac{(N-2)p-N}{2p}}\right)^{\frac{p}{p+1}}.

(1.8
)

The separable solutions obtained in the next theorem are at the core of the process of describing the behaviour of positive solutions of
(\ref{Z1})
near an isolated singularity or in an exterior domain of

2.1
The equation on the sphere

The existence of particular solutions of

, and eventually their uniqueness, plays a key role in the description of the behaviour of all the solutions. Due to the invariance of the equation under the transformations

these natural particular solutions are the ones which are self-similar, i.e. invariant by these transformations. In spherical coordinates

Lemma 2.3

1- If

and

, or

2- If

3- If

and

is not hyperbolic. One eigenvalue is

Proof.
Set

. In view of
(\ref{Z5})
,

satisfies

\begin{array}[]{lll}\alpha^{-p}y^{p-1}_{{}_{M}}-My^{\frac{p-1}{p+1}}_{{}_{M}}+K=0.\end{array}

(2.18
)

Setting
x=x_{{}_{M}}+\overline{x}
,
y=y_{{}_{M}}+\overline{y}
, the linearized equation at
(x_{{}_{M}},y_{{}_{M}})
is

\begin{array}[]{lll}\overline{x}_{t}=\alpha\overline{x}-\overline{y}\\
\overline{y}_{t}=-px^{p-1}_{{}_{M}}\overline{x}+\left(\frac{2Mp}{p+1}y_{{}_{M}}^{\frac{p-1}{p+1}}-K\right)\overline{y}.\end{array}

(2.19
)

The characteristic polynomial of the corresponding matrix is

\begin{array}[]{lll}T_{{y_{{}_{M}}}}(X)=X^{2}-\left({\displaystyle\frac{2Mp}{p+1}}y_{{}_{M}}^{\frac{p-1}{p+1}}-L\right)X+2K-{\displaystyle\frac{2Mp}{p+1}}y_{{}_{M}}^{\frac{p-1}{p+1}}.\end{array}

(2.20
)

1- If
p\leq\frac{N}{N-2}
and
M>0
, or
p<\frac{N}{N-2}
and
M=0
,
P_{{}_{M}}
is unique. Since either
K\leq 0
and
M>0
or
K<0
and
M=0
, the product of the roots is negative. Hence
P_{{}_{M}}
is a saddle point.

2-3- Next we assume
N\geq 3
and
M\geq m^{*}
.
The sum of the roots of
T_{{y_{{}_{M}}}}(X)
is equal to

\begin{array}[]{lll}{\displaystyle\frac{2Mp}{p+1}}y_{{}_{M}}^{\frac{p-1}{p+1}}-L={\displaystyle\frac{2p}{p+1}}\left(\alpha^{-p}y^{p-1}_{{}_{M}}+K\right)-L>{\displaystyle\frac{p-1}{p+1}}K+\alpha>0.\end{array}

Concerning the product
\Pi(y_{{}_{M}})
of the roots, we deduce from
(\ref{Y6})
that
T_{y_{m^{*}}}(0)=0
hence
\Pi(y_{m^{*}})=0
. Since by
(\ref{W7})

\Pi(y_{{}_{M}})=2K-{\displaystyle\frac{2Mp}{p+1}}y_{{}_{M}}^{\frac{p-1}{p+1}}={\displaystyle\frac{2K}{p+1}}-{\displaystyle\frac{2p}{p+1}}\alpha^{-p}y_{{}_{M}}^{p-1},

we infer that for

,

\begin{array}[]{lll}\Pi(y_{{}_{2,M}})<\Pi(y_{m^{*}})=0<\Pi(y_{{}_{1,M}}).\end{array}

(2.21
)

Hence
P_{{}_{2,M}}
is a saddle point and
P_{{}_{1,M}}
is a source. In order to characterize the nature of this source we denote by
D(T_{{y_{{}_{M}}}})
the discriminant of
T_{{y_{{}_{M}}}}
. Then

\begin{array}[]{lll}D(T_{{y_{{}_{1,M}}}})=\left({\displaystyle\frac{2Mp}{p+1}}y_{{}_{1,M}}^{\frac{p-1}{p+1}}-L\right)^{2}+4\left({\displaystyle\frac{2Mp}{p+1}}y_{{}_{1,M}}^{\frac{p-1}{p+1}}-2K\right)\\[11.38109pt]
\phantom{D(T_{{y_{{}_{1,M}}}})}=\left({\displaystyle\frac{2Mp}{p+1}}y_{{}_{1,M}}^{\frac{p-1}{p+1}}-L+2+2\sqrt{N-1}\right)\left({\displaystyle\frac{2Mp}{p+1}}y_{{}_{1,M}}^{\frac{p-1}{p+1}}-L+2-2\sqrt{N-1}\right).\end{array}

By
(\ref{W7})
,
My_{{}_{1,M}}^{\frac{p-1}{p+1}}>K
, hence

\begin{array}[]{lll}{\displaystyle\frac{2Mp}{p+1}}y_{{}_{1,M}}^{\frac{p-1}{p+1}}-L+2-2\sqrt{N-1}>{\displaystyle\frac{p-1}{p+1}}K+{\displaystyle\frac{2}{p-1}}+2-2\sqrt{N-1}\\[11.38109pt]
\phantom{{\displaystyle\frac{2Mp}{p+1}}y_{{}_{1,M}}^{\frac{p-1}{p+1}}-L+2-2\sqrt{N-1}}={\displaystyle\frac{N-2\sqrt{N-1}}{p^{2}-1}}\left(p-{\displaystyle\frac{N-2}{N-2\sqrt{N-1}}}\right)^{2}.\end{array}

Hence
D(T_{{y_{{}_{1,M}}}})>0
which implies that the roots are real and

is a node.

If
M=m^{*}
the product of the roots is
0
, hence one root is
0
. Since their sum is equal to
\frac{2p}{p+1}m^{*}y_{{}_{m^{*}}}^{\frac{p-1}{p+1}}-L=N-2
, the nonzero root is equal to

.

\square

\begin{array}[]{lll}\displaystyle E(X,Y,t)={\displaystyle\frac{X^{p+1}}{p+1}}-M\gamma^{q}{\displaystyle\frac{X^{q+1}}{q+1}}-e^{\frac{\sigma t}{p-q}}\left({\displaystyle\frac{(\gamma X-Y)^{2}}{2}}+{\displaystyle\frac{\gamma\theta X^{2}}{2}}\right).\end{array}

)

\begin{array}[]{lll}\displaystyle{\mathcal{E}}_{t}(t)=-M\left(\gamma X-Y\right)\left(\gamma^{q}X^{q}-|Y|^{q}\right)\\[11.38109pt]
\phantom{----------}-e^{\frac{\sigma t}{p-q}}\left(\left({\displaystyle\frac{\alpha}{2}}+\gamma+\theta\right)\left(\gamma X-Y\right)^{2}+{\displaystyle\frac{\sigma\gamma\theta}{2(p-q)}}X^{2}\right),\end{array}

(3.19

where
(X,Y)=(X(t),Y(t))

Proof.
There holds
Y=\gamma X-X_{t}
and

X_{tt}=-\theta\gamma X-(N-2-2\gamma)X_{t}-e^{-\frac{\sigma t}{p-q}}(M|Y|^{q}-X^{p}).

Multiplying by
e^{\frac{\sigma t}{p-q}}X_{t}
we get

e^{\frac{\sigma t}{p-q}}{\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}}\left({\displaystyle\frac{X_{t}^{2}}{2}}+\theta\gamma{\displaystyle\frac{X^{2}}{2}}\right)-{\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}}\left({\displaystyle\frac{X^{p+1}}{p+1}}.\right)=(\gamma+\theta)e^{\frac{\sigma t}{p-q}}X_{t}^{2}-M|Y|^{q}X_{t}.

Putting

{\mathcal{F}}(t)={\displaystyle\frac{X^{p+1}}{p+1}}-e^{\frac{\sigma t}{p-q}}\left({\displaystyle\frac{X_{t}^{2}}{2}}+\theta\gamma{\displaystyle\frac{X^{2}}{2}}\right),

we obtain

{\mathcal{F}}_{t}(t)=-e^{\frac{\sigma t}{p-q}}\left({\displaystyle\frac{\sigma}{p-q}}\left({\displaystyle\frac{X_{t}^{2}}{2}}+\theta\gamma{\displaystyle\frac{X^{2}}{2}}\right)+\left(\gamma+\theta\right)X_{t}^{2}\right)+M|Y|^{q}X_{t}.

Since

{\mathcal{E}}(t)={\mathcal{F}}(t)-M\gamma^{q}{\displaystyle\frac{X^{q+1}}{q+1}},

and

, we obtain

{\mathcal{E}}_{t}(t)=-M(\gamma X-Y)(\gamma^{q}X^{q}-|Y|^{q})-e^{\frac{\sigma t}{p-q}}\left(\left({\displaystyle\frac{\sigma}{2(p-q)}}+\gamma+\theta\right)X_{t}^{2}+{\displaystyle\frac{\sigma\gamma\theta}{2(p-q)}}X^{2}\right).

and
(\ref{U17})
follows.
\square

The slope of a trajectory has shown its importance in the previous section when studying solutions of

near an equilibrium. We introduce it as a Lyapounov type function the variations of which will be of particular interest for studying solutions of eikonal type.

Definition 3.2

The slope of a solution

\begin{array}[]{lll}\displaystyle S(t)=-{\displaystyle\frac{ru^{\prime}(r)}{u(r)}}={\displaystyle\frac{y(t)}{x(t)}}={\displaystyle\frac{\eta(t)}{\xi(t)}}={\displaystyle\frac{Y(t)}{X(t)}}.\end{array}

(3.20
)

Since
\frac{S_{t}}{S}=\frac{\eta_{t}}{\eta}-\frac{\xi_{t}}{\xi}
, there holds

\begin{array}[]{lll}\displaystyle S_{t}=S(S+2-N)+\xi^{q-1}M|S|^{q}-x^{p-1}\\
\phantom{S_{t}}=S(S+2-N)+\xi^{q-1}(M|S|^{q}-X^{p-q})\quad\text{if }q\neq p.\end{array}

(3.21
)

Note that

if

.

large enough.
Furthermore

X(t)=X_{{}_{M}}+{\displaystyle\frac{\theta\gamma^{1-q}X^{2-q}_{{}_{M}}}{p(q-1)M}}e^{\frac{\sigma t}{p-q}}+O\left(e^{\frac{2\sigma t}{p-q}}\right)\text{ as }t\to-\infty\;\;(\text{resp. }t\to\infty).

(3.68
)

Equivalently, with

,

u(r)=X_{{}_{M}}r^{-\frac{q}{p-q}}+{\displaystyle\frac{\theta\gamma^{1-q}X^{2-q}_{{}_{M}}}{p(q-1)M}}r^{-\frac{p(2-q)}{p-q}}+O\left(r^{-\frac{(2p+1)q-2p}{p-q}}\right)\text{ as }r\to 0\;\;(\text{resp. }r\to\infty).

(3.69
)

Proof.
(i)
Expansion of
MY^{q}-X^{p}
. Set

\Psi(t)=Y_{t}-\theta Y=e^{-\frac{\sigma t}{p-q}}(MY^{q}-X^{p}).

Then

\Psi_{t}=e^{-\frac{\sigma t}{p-q}}\left(-{\displaystyle\frac{\sigma}{p-q}}(MY^{q}-X^{p})+MqY^{q-1}(\theta Y+\Psi)-pX^{p-1}(\gamma X-Y)\right).

If
\Psi
is not monotone, one has at the local extremum
t_{n}
of
\Psi
, denoting
\Psi_{n}=\Psi(t_{n})
,
X_{n}=X(t_{n})
and
Y_{n}=Y(t_{n})
,

MqY_{n}^{q-1}\Psi_{n}=-Mq\theta Y_{n}^{q}+pX_{n}^{p-1}(\gamma X_{n}-Y_{n})+{\displaystyle\frac{\sigma}{p-q}}(MY_{n}^{q}-X_{n}^{p}).

But
\displaystyle\lim_{t\to-\infty}(X(t),Y(t))=(X_{{}_{M}},Y_{{}_{M}})
, then
pX_{n}^{p-1}(\gamma X_{n}-Y_{n})+{\displaystyle\frac{\sigma}{p-q}}(MY_{n}^{q}-X_{n}^{p})\to 0
when
t_{n}\to-\infty
. Therefore
\Psi_{n}\to-\theta\gamma X_{{}_{M}}
. Since the limit is valid for local minima or maxima it follows that
\displaystyle\lim_{t\to-\infty}\Psi(t)=-\theta\gamma X_{{}_{M}}
.

If
\Psi
is monotone, then
Y_{t}(t)-\theta Y(t)
admits a limit when
t\to-\infty
. Since
Y(t)\to Y_{{}_{M}}
, it follows that
Y_{t}(t)
has a also a limit at
-\infty
and the only possible one is
0
. Hence
\Psi(t)\to-\theta\gamma X_{{}_{M}}
. In both case it yields, since
\theta\neq 0
,

MY(t)^{q}-X(t)^{p}=-\theta\gamma X_{{}_{M}}e^{\frac{\sigma t}{p-q}}(1+o(1))\quad\text{ as }t\to-\infty.

(3.70
)

(ii)
We claim that
X-X_{{}_{M}}
has a constant sign
. If
X
is nondecreasing (resp. nonincreasing) then
X(t)\geq X_{{}_{M}}
(resp.
X(t)\leq X_{{}_{M}}
) for
t\leq 0
. Actually the inequality is strict, otherwise, if there is some

such that

, we would have

for

and
X(t)=X_{{}_{M}}
for
t\leq t_{0}
. If
\theta\neq 0
this contradicts the fact that

. If
\theta>0
we deduce from
(\ref{U10})
that if

is a local minimum we have

e^{-\frac{\sigma t_{n}}{p-q}}\left(M\gamma^{q}X^{q}(t_{n})-X^{p}(t_{n})\right)=-\theta X(t_{n})-X_{tt}(t_{n})<0\Longrightarrow X(t_{n})>X_{{}_{M}}.

This implies that
X(t)>X_{{}_{M}}
. Similarly, if
\theta<0
we get
X(t)<X_{{}_{M}}
.

(iii)
Asymptotic expansion
. We write
X=X_{{}_{M}}(1+w)
and

. Then

\begin{array}[]{lll}w_{t}=\gamma(w-z)\\
\,z_{t}=\theta(1+z)+M\gamma^{q-1}X^{q-1}_{{}_{M}}e^{-\frac{\sigma t}{p-q}}\left((1+z)^{q}-(1+w)^{p}\right).\end{array}

(3.71
)

There holds

(1+z)^{q}-(1+w)^{p}=qz-pw+\phi(z)-\psi(w),

where

and

, therefore

and

for

,

qz+\phi(z)=pw+\psi(w)+a_{{}_{M}}e^{\frac{\sigma t}{p-q}}(z_{t}-\theta(1+z)),

where

, and

and

when
t\to-\infty
. Therefore the previous identity becomes

qz+\phi(z)=pw+\psi(w)-\theta a_{{}_{M}}e^{\frac{\sigma t}{p-q}}(1+h(t)),

(3.72
)

where

when

. Next

\begin{array}[]{lll}\,qz\leq qz+\phi(z)\leq\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}qz(1+\epsilon)&\text{ if }z>0\\

that

\begin{array}[]{lll}qz-pw(1+\epsilon_{w})+\theta a_{{}_{M}}e^{\frac{\sigma t}{p-q}}(1+h(t))\leq 0\\
\;qz(1+\epsilon_{z})-pw+\theta a_{{}_{M}}e^{\frac{\sigma t}{p-q}}(1+h(t))\geq 0.\end{array}

)

\begin{array}[]{lll}w_{t}=\gamma(w-z)={\displaystyle\frac{1}{p-q}}(qw-qz)\geq{\displaystyle\frac{1}{p-q}}\left(qw-pw(1+\epsilon_{w})+\theta a_{{}_{M}}e^{\frac{\sigma t}{p-q}}(1+h)\right)\\[11.38109pt]

and

\begin{array}[]{lll}w_{t}\leq{\displaystyle\frac{q}{p-q}}\left(w+{\displaystyle\frac{1}{q(1+\epsilon_{z})}}(-pw+\theta a_{{}_{M}}e^{\frac{\sigma t}{p-q}}(1+h)\right)\\[11.38109pt]

and we know from (i) that

and tends to

\theta>0
,

is increasing for another

and tends to

\begin{array}[]{lll}(1-\epsilon)\left(\theta^{-1}w-{\displaystyle\frac{a_{{}_{M}}}{p-q}}e^{\frac{\sigma t}{p-q}}\right)\leq-(\theta^{-1}w)_{t}\leq(1+\epsilon)\left(\theta^{-1}w-{\displaystyle\frac{a_{{}_{M}}}{p-q}}e^{\frac{\sigma t}{p-q}}\right).\end{array}

(3.74

as

is decreasing, tends to

hence it is negative. Therefore we infer that

\begin{array}[]{lll}w(t)={\displaystyle\frac{\theta a_{{}_{M}}}{p(q-1)}}e^{\frac{\sigma t}{p-q}}(1+o(1)).\end{array}

(3.75
)

This implies
\psi(w)=O\left(e^{\frac{2\sigma t}{p-q}}\right)
. From
(\ref{O6})
,

, then

and

, we deduce

. Notice also that from

there holds

hence

\begin{array}[]{lll}w_{t}=\gamma(w(t)-z(t))={\displaystyle\frac{\sigma\theta a_{{}_{M}}}{p(p-q)(q-1)}}e^{\frac{\sigma t}{p-q}}(1+o(1)).\end{array}

(3.76
)

In particular

has the sign of

, and therefore

is monotone.
\square

3.6
Local or global existence results

3.6.1
The systems of order 3

Since
q\neq\frac{2p}{p+1}
, we can perform the transformation
T_{\ell}
and assume that
M=1
. For proving the existence of solutions to
(\ref{Z1})
there are essentially three methods: the methods of sub and super solutions which has already been developed in Section 2.3, the method of fixed points, and the use of a specific autonomous system of order 3. This last method appears to be entirely new and we explain it below. This system uses the variables
(X,\xi,S)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}
,

\begin{array}[]{lll}X_{t}=X(\gamma-S)\\
\;\xi_{t}=\xi(\beta-S)\\
\;S_{t}=S(S+2-N)+\xi^{q-1}(MS^{q}-X^{p-q}).\end{array}

(3.78
)

Lemma 3.10

Let
1<q<p
with
q\neq\frac{2p}{p+1}
and
M>0
. If
u
is a decreasing positive solution of
(\ref{W1})
, then

\begin{array}[]{lll}(X(t),\xi(t),S(t))=\left(r^{\gamma}u(r),r^{\beta}u(r),r{\displaystyle\frac{|u^{\prime}(r)|}{u(r)}}\right)\quad\text{with }t=\ln r,\end{array}

(3.79
)

satisfies
(\ref{N1})
. Conversely, to each trajectory of
(\ref{N1})
in
\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}
corresponds a unique solution of
(\ref{W1})
.

Proof.
Let
u
be a decreasing solution of
(\ref{W1})
. We recall that
(X,Y)
are solutions of
(\ref{U9})
,
S=\frac{Y}{X}
and
\xi(t)=r^{\beta}u(r)
with
t=\ln r
. Then
(X,S)
satisfies the following system which is equivalent to
(\ref{U9})
,

\begin{array}[]{lll}\!X_{t}=X(\gamma-S)\\
S_{t}=S(S+2-N)+e^{-\frac{\sigma t}{p-q}}X^{q-1}(MS^{q}-X^{p-q}).\end{array}

(3.80
)

Using
(\ref{U13})
we have that
\xi^{q-1}=e^{-\frac{\sigma t}{p-q}}X^{q-1}
. Since by computation
\xi_{t}=\xi(\beta-S)
, we deduce that
(X,\xi,S)
satisfies
(\ref{N1})
.

Conversely, let
(X,\xi,S)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}
be a solution of
(\ref{N1})
, then

{\displaystyle\frac{\xi_{t}}{\xi}}-{\displaystyle\frac{X_{t}}{X}}=\beta-\gamma=-\frac{\sigma}{(p-q)(q-1)}.

Hence
\xi(t)=be^{-\frac{\sigma t}{(p-q)(q-1)}}X(t)
for some
b>0
. If we set
a=-\frac{(p-q)(q-1)}{\sigma}\ln b
, we see that

\xi(t)=e^{-\frac{\sigma(t+a)}{(p-q)(q-1)}}X(t).

Hence

\begin{array}[]{lll}\!X_{t}=X(\gamma-S)\\
S_{t}=S(S+2-N)+e^{-\frac{\sigma(t+a)}{p-q}}X^{q-1}(MS^{q}-X^{p-q}).\end{array}

(3.81
)

Setting
\tau=t+a
,
X^{(a)}(\tau),S^{(a)}(\tau)=(X(t),S(t))=(X(\tau-a),S(\tau-a))
, then

\begin{array}[]{lll}\!X^{(a)}_{\tau}=X^{(a)}(\gamma-S^{(a)})\\
S^{(a)}_{\tau}=S^{(a)}(S^{(a)}+2-N)+e^{-\frac{\sigma\tau}{p-q}}(X^{(a)})^{q-1}(M(S^{(a)})^{q}-(X^{(a)})^{p-q}).\end{array}

(3.82
)

Then the function
\rho\mapsto u^{(a)}(\rho)=\rho^{-\gamma}X^{(a)}(\ln\rho)
satisfies
(\ref{W1})
. Let
(X,\xi,S)
and
(\tilde{X},\tilde{\xi},\tilde{S})
be two solutions of

(\ref{N1})
. Then there exist
a,\tilde{a}
such that

\xi(t)=e^{-\frac{\sigma(t+a)}{(p-q)(q-1)}}X(t)\quad\text{and }\,\tilde{\xi}(t)=e^{-\frac{\sigma(t+\tilde{a})}{(p-q)(q-1)}}\tilde{X}(t),

and

\begin{array}[]{lll}u^{(a)}(\rho)=\rho^{-\gamma}X^{(a)}(\ln\rho)=\rho^{-\gamma}X(\ln\rho-a)\\
u^{(\tilde{a})}(\rho)=\rho^{-\gamma}X(\ln\rho-\tilde{a}).\end{array}

If
(X,\xi,S)
and
(\tilde{X},\tilde{\xi},\tilde{S})
correspond to the same trajectory, there exists
h\in\mathbb{R}
such that

(\tilde{X},\tilde{\xi},\tilde{S})(t)=(X,\xi,S)(t+h)
for all
t
, thus

\xi(t+h)=e^{-\frac{\sigma(t+\tilde{a})}{(p-q)(q-1)}}X(t+h)=e^{-\frac{\sigma(t+a+h)}{(p-q)(q-1)}}X(t+h).

Therefore
\tilde{a}=a+h
. Hence

u^{(\tilde{a})}(\rho)=\rho^{-\gamma}\tilde{X}(\ln\rho-\tilde{a})=\rho^{-\gamma}\tilde{X}(\ln\rho-a-h)=\rho^{-\gamma}X(\ln\rho-a)=u^{(a)}(\rho).

In conclusion, there is a one to one correspondence between the trajectories of
(\ref{N1})
and the solutions of
(\ref{W1})
.
\square

1.
Introduction

Let
\left(M,g\right)
be a smooth, closed (i.e. compact and without boundary) Riemannian manifold of dimension
n\geq 3
. We consider the stationary Schrödinger equation

\Delta_{g}u+h_{0}u=\left|u\right|^{2^{*}-2}u\quad\text{in }M,

(1.1)

where
\Delta_{g}:=-\text{div}_{g}\nabla
is the Laplace–Beltrami operator on functions,
h_{0}\in C^{1}\left(M\right)
and
2^{*}=\frac{2n}{n-2}
is the critical exponent for the embeddings of the Sobolev space
H^{1}\left(M\right)
into Lebesgue’s spaces. In this article, we are interested in a question of stability of sign-changing solutions to (
1.1
) under perturbations of the potential function
h_{0}
. More precisely, we are interested in the question of existence of families of functions
\left(h_{\varepsilon}\right)_{0<\varepsilon\ll 1}
in
C^{1}\left(M\right)
and
\left(u_{\varepsilon}\right)_{0<\varepsilon\ll 1}
in
C^{2}\left(M\right)
such that
h_{\varepsilon}\to h_{0}
in
C^{1}\left(M\right)
as
\varepsilon\to 0
,
\left(u_{\varepsilon}\right)_{\varepsilon}
is bounded in
H^{1}\left(M\right)
and blows up at least at some point
\xi_{0}\in M
as
\varepsilon\to 0
(i.e. there exists a family of points
\left(\xi_{\varepsilon}\right)_{0<\varepsilon\ll 1}
such that
\xi_{\varepsilon}\to\xi_{0}
and
\left|u_{\varepsilon}\left(\xi_{\varepsilon}\right)\right|\to\infty
as
\varepsilon\to 0
) and for each
\varepsilon>0
,
u_{\varepsilon}
is a sign-changing solution to the equation

\Delta_{g}u_{\varepsilon}+h_{\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon}=\left|u_{\varepsilon}\right|^{2^{*}-2}u_{\varepsilon}\quad\text{in }M.

(1.2)

By “sign-changing solutions” we mean solutions that are allowed to change sign. Stability questions of this type have been extensively studied in recent years, and most of the focus has been on positive solutions. 1.1.
A stability result for locally conformally flat manifolds

We obtain the following stability result:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
(M,g)
be a smooth, closed, locally conformally flat Riemannian manifold of dimension
n\geq 7
and
\left(h_{\varepsilon}\right)_{0\leq\varepsilon\ll 1}
be a family of functions in
C^{1}\left(M\right)
such that
h_{\varepsilon}\to h_{0}
in
C^{1}\left(M\right)
and

h_{0}\left(x\right)\neq c_{n}\Scal_{g}\left(x\right)\quad\forall x\in M.

(1.3)

Then every family of solutions
\left(u_{\varepsilon}\right)_{0<\varepsilon\ll 1}
to (
1.2
) that is bounded in
H^{1}\left(M\right)
converges up to a subsequence in
C^{2}\left(M\right)
.

This result extends previous results obtained by Druet
[
Dr1
]
in the case where
u_{\varepsilon}>0
in
M
for all
\varepsilon
and Vétois
[
V
]
in the case where
h_{0}<c_{n}\Scal_{g}
in
M
. A simple corollary is that when the assumptions of Theorem
1.1
are satisfied, for a fixed
C^{1}
function
h_{0}
satisfying (
1.3
), every bounded set in
H^{1}\left(M\right)
of (possibly sign-changing) solutions to (
1.1
) is precompact in
C^{2}\left(M\right)
.

Remark that if we remove the
H^{1}
bound on
\left(u_{\varepsilon}\right)_{\varepsilon}
while assuming (
1.3
), then this result does not remain true. Indeed, existence results of families of solutions with unbounded
H^{1}
norm have been obtained by Vétois
[
V
]
in the case where
h_{0}<c_{n}\Scal_{g}
in
M
and
\left(M,g\right)
is locally conformally flat and by Chen, Wei and Yan
[
CWY
]
when
n\geq 5
,
h_{0}
is constant and
h_{0}>n\left(n-2\right)/4
(the solutions obtained in
[
CWY
]
are positive; see also the article by Vétois and Wang
[
VW
]
for the
n=4
counterpart and the article by Premoselli and Vétois
[
PrV1
]
for a construction of sign-changing blowing-up solutions in this case). In the case where
h_{0}\equiv c_{n}\Scal_{g}
, at least on the standard sphere of dimension
n\geq 3
, it follows from Ding’s result
[
Di
]
that an analogue of Theorem
1.1
cannot hold true.

The statement of Theorem
1.1
is very similar to the analogous result obtained by Druet
[
Dr1
]
for positive solutions. However, in the sign-changing case, very general sign-changing bubbles can a priori arise in the asymptotic behavior of energy-bounded families of solutions, hence it is actually quite surprising that the condition (
1.3
) remains sufficient to ensure the pre-compactness of such families. This is crucially related to our conformal flatness assumption as is made clear in the proof. In the case of a non-locally conformally flat manifold, it is likely that local necessary conditions for the blow-up of families of solutions to (
1.2
) would involve the potential function
h_{0}
together with an interplay between the local geometry of the manifold and the arising sign-changing bubbles. It is therefore still unclear what would be a natural generalization of Theorem
1.1
to the non-locally conformally flat setting.

The assumptions
n\geq 7
and (
1.3
) in Theorem
1.1
are sharp. We prove this in Theorems
1.3
and
1.4
below. To better understand these counterexamples it is instructive to restrict the focus of Theorem
1.1
to specific blow-up configurations. In this case, more precise information on the blow-up can be obtained. In the next result, we consider solutions to (
1.2
) of the form

u_{\varepsilon}=u_{0}-\left(\frac{\sqrt{n\left(n-2\right)}\mu_{\varepsilon}}{\mu_{\varepsilon}^{2}+d_{g}\left(\cdot,\xi_{\varepsilon}\right)^{2}}\right)^{\frac{n-2}{2}}+\smallo\left(1\right)\text{ in }H^{1}\left(M\right)\text{ as }\varepsilon\to 0,

(1.4)

where
u_{0}
is a solution to (
1.1
),
d_{g}
is the geodesic distance with respect to the metric
g
and
\left(\mu_{\varepsilon}\right)_{0<\varepsilon\ll 1}
and
\left(\xi_{\varepsilon}\right)_{0<\varepsilon\ll 1}
are two families of positive numbers and points in
M
, respectively, such that
\mu_{\varepsilon}\to 0
and
\xi_{\varepsilon}\to\xi_{0}
as
\varepsilon\to 0
for some point
\xi_{0}\in M
. In this case, the location of the blow-up point
\xi_{0}
is constrained. More precisely, we obtain the following:

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\left(M,g\right)
be a smooth, closed Riemannian manifold of dimension
n\geq 3
,
h_{0}\in C^{0,\vartheta}\left(M\right)
,
\vartheta\in\left(0,1\right)
, and
u_{0}
be a solution to (
1.1
). Assume that
\varphi_{0}\neq 0
for all points in M, where

\varphi_{0}:=\left\{\begin{aligned} &u_{0}&&\text{if }n\in\left\{3,4,5\right\}\\
&h_{0}+2u_{0}-c_{n}\Scal_{g}&&\text{if }n=6\\
&h_{0}-c_{n}\Scal_{g}&&\text{if }n\geq 7.\end{aligned}\right.

(1.5)

Then there does not exist any families of functions
\left(h_{\varepsilon}\right)_{0<\varepsilon\ll 1}
in
C^{0,\vartheta}\left(M\right)
and
\left(u_{\varepsilon}\right)_{0<\varepsilon\ll 1}
in
C^{2}\left(M\right)
, positive numbers
\left(\mu_{\varepsilon}\right)_{0<\varepsilon\ll 1}
and points
\left(\xi_{\varepsilon}\right)_{0<\varepsilon\ll 1}
in
M
satisfying (
1.2
) and (
1.4
) and such that
\mu_{\varepsilon}\to 0
,
\xi_{\varepsilon}\to\xi_{0}
and
h_{\varepsilon}\to h_{0}
in
C^{0,\vartheta}\left(M\right)
as
\varepsilon\to 0
.

In the case of positive solutions (and possibly with multiple bubbles), this result is due to Druet
[
Dr1
]
. In particular, it follows from Theorem
1.2
that if a family
\left(u_{\varepsilon}\right)_{0<\varepsilon\ll 1}
of solutions to (
1.2
) blows up as in (
1.4
), then
\varphi_{0}\left(\xi_{0}\right)=0
, where
\varphi_{0}
is as in (
1.5
). In dimensions
3,4
and
5
, for configurations of type (
1.4
), the weak limit
u_{0}
thus vanishes at the blow-up point
\xi_{0}
. Since
u_{0}
is now a sign-changing solution to (
1.1
), this does not imply that
u_{0}
vanishes on the whole manifold, unlike in the case of positive solutions. Note also that the assumption (
1.4
) allows us, in Theorem
1.2
, to lift the local conformal flatness assumption from Theorem
1.1
since there cannot be any cancellation phenomena between several sign-changing bubbles in this case.

Our proofs of Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
rely on the pointwise estimates for sign-changing solutions recently obtained by Premoselli
[
P
]
. The proof of Theorem
1.1
that we give here is conceptually different from the proof in case
h_{0}<c_{n}\Scal_{g}
in the article by Vétois
[
V
]
, which was based on subtle comparison arguments inspired from those in the Euclidean case by Devillanova and Solimini
[
DS
]
.

In what follows, we provide counterexamples showing the optimality of the conditions (
1.3
) and
n\geq 7
in Theorem
1.1
. These counterexamples are obtained by using the Lyapunov–Schmidt reduction method. We obtain two different types of counterexamples, the first in dimensions
n\geq 6
and the second in dimensions
n\in\left\{3,4,5\right\}
, which are inspired from Theorem
1.2
.

1.2.
Counterexamples: the case of dimensions
n\geq 6

In the case where
n\geq 6
, we construct solutions of (
1.2
) of the form

u_{\varepsilon}=u_{0}-\left(\frac{\sqrt{n\left(n-2\right)}\mu_{\varepsilon}}{\mu_{\varepsilon}^{2}+d_{g}\left(\cdot,\xi_{\varepsilon}\right)^{2}}\right)^{\frac{n-2}{2}}+\smallo\left(1\right)\text{ in }H^{1}\left(M\right)\text{ as }\varepsilon\to 0,

where
u_{0}
is a solution to (
1.1
),
d_{g}
is the geodesic distance with respect to the metric
g
and
\left(\mu_{\varepsilon}\right)_{0<\varepsilon\ll 1}
and
\left(\xi_{\varepsilon}\right)_{0<\varepsilon\ll 1}
are two families of positive numbers and points in
M
, respectively, such that
\mu_{\varepsilon}\to 0
and
\xi_{\varepsilon}\to\xi_{0}
as
\varepsilon\to 0
for some point
\xi_{0}\in M
. As Theorem
1.2
shows, a necessary condition for such solutions to exist is that
\varphi_{0}\left(\xi_{0}\right)=0
, where
\varphi_{0}
is as in (
1.5
).

We say that
u_{0}
is nondegenerate if there does not exist any functions
\psi\in C^{2}\left(M\right)\backslash\left\{0\right\}
such that

\Delta_{g}\psi+h_{0}\psi=\left(2^{*}-1\right)\left|u_{0}\right|^{2^{*}-2}\psi\quad\text{in }M.

We obtain the following result, which, except in the case where
n=6
and
u_{0}\left(\xi_{0}\right)>0
, is a straightforward adaptation of a result by Robert and Vétois
[
RV4
]
:

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
(M,g)
be a smooth, closed Riemannian manifold of dimension
n\geq 6
,
h_{0},h\in C^{2}\left(M\right)
,
u_{0}
be a nondegenerate solution to (
1.1
) and
\varphi_{0}
be the function defined in (
1.5
). Assume that there exists a point
\xi_{0}\in M
such that the following two conditions hold true:

(i)

\varphi_{0}\left(\xi_{0}\right)=\left|\nabla\varphi_{0}\left(\xi_{0}\right)\right|=0
and
\left\{\begin{aligned} &D^{2}\varphi_{0}\left(\xi_{0}\right)<0\text{ if }n=6\text{ and }u_{0}\left(\xi_{0}\right)>0\\
&D^{2}\varphi_{0}\left(\xi_{0}\right)\text{ is nondegenerate otherwise,}\end{aligned}\right.

(ii)

K_{0}\left(\xi_{0}\right)\times\left\{\begin{aligned} &\left[h-2\left(\Delta_{g}+h_{0}-2\left|u_{0}\right|\right)^{-1}\left(hu_{0}\right)\right]\left(\xi_{0}\right)&&\text{if }n=6\\
&h\left(\xi_{0}\right)&&\text{if }n\geq 7\end{aligned}\right\}>0
, where

K_{0}:=\left\{\begin{aligned} &\Delta_{g}\varphi_{0}+\frac{c_{6}}{6}\left|\Weyl_{g}\right|_{g}^{2}&&\hbox{if }n=6\text{ and }u_{0}\left(\xi_{0}\right)<0\\
&u_{0}&&\hbox{if }n=6\text{ and }u_{0}\left(\xi_{0}\right)\geq 0\\
&-u_{0}&&\hbox{if }7\leq n\leq 9\\
&\Delta_{g}\varphi_{0}+\frac{c_{10}}{6}\left|\Weyl_{g}\right|_{g}^{2}-672u_{0}&&\hbox{if }n=10\\
&\Delta_{g}\varphi_{0}+\frac{c_{n}}{6}\left|\Weyl_{g}\right|_{g}^{2}&&\hbox{if }n\geq 11.\end{aligned}\right.

Then there exists a family of solutions
\left(u_{\varepsilon}\right)_{0<\varepsilon\ll 1}
to (
1.2
) with
h_{\varepsilon}:=h_{0}+\varepsilon h
, which satisfies (
1.4
) with
\xi_{\varepsilon}\to\xi_{0}
as
\varepsilon\to 0
.

Theorem
1.3
shows in particular that the assumption (
1.3
) in Theorem
1.1
is sharp when
n\geq 7
. Note that we do not assume here that
\left(M,g\right)
is locally conformally flat. We refer to Section
2
for extensions of Theorem
1.3
and further results in the case where
n\geq 6
. Our results in this case are very close to those obtained in the case of positive solutions in the article by Robert and Vétois
[
RV4
]
. This is not the case for our results in dimensions
n\in\left\{3,4,5\right\}
, which we discuss in what follows.

1.3.
Counterexamples: the case of dimensions
n\in\left\{3,4,5\right\}

We now consider the case where
n\in\left\{3,4,5\right\}
. Our counterexamples in this case are again of the form (
1.4
), but this time the solution
u_{0}
changes sign and the point
\xi_{0}
is such that
u_{0}\left(\xi_{0}\right)=0
. Here again, the condition
u_{0}\left(\xi_{0}\right)=0
is necessary for the existence of solutions of type (
1.4
) to (
1.1
) when
n\in\left\{3,4,5\right\}
in view of Theorem
1.2
. Additional difficulties arise in this low-dimensional case. They are related on the one hand to the degeneracy issues in the Lyapunov–Schmidt procedure and the construction of suitable initial functions
h_{0}
and
u_{0}
and on the other hand to the higher precision required in the expansions due to the condition
u_{0}\left(\xi_{0}\right)=0
.

We obtain our result when
n\in\left\{3,4,5\right\}
under some additional symmetry assumptions. We recall the following definition, which was used for a different construction in the case of positive solutions by Morabito, Pistoia and Vaira
[
MPV
]
: we say that
\left(M,g\right)
is symmetric with respect to a point
\xi_{0}\in M
if there exists an isometry
\sigma:M\to M
such that
\sigma\left(\xi_{0}\right)=\xi_{0}
and
d\sigma_{\xi_{0}}\left(v\right)=-v
for all vectors
v\in T_{\xi_{0}}M
. Moreover, if
\left(M,g\right)
is symmetric with respect to
\xi_{0}
, then we say that a function
u:M\to M
is symmetric with respect to
\xi_{0}
if
u\circ\sigma=u
in
M
.

Let us also recall (see for example the article by Li and Zhu
[
LZhu
]
) that in dimension
n=3
, for every smooth metric
g
on
M
and every function
h\in C^{0}\left(M\right)
, if the operator
\Delta_{g}+h
has empty kernel, then there exists a function
m_{g,h}\in C^{0}\left(M\right)
, usually called the mass of the operator
\Delta_{g}+h
, such that the Green’s function
G_{g,h}
of
\Delta_{g}+h
satisfies

G_{g,h}\left(x,\xi\right)=\left(4\pi d_{g}\left(x,\xi\right)\right)^{-1}+m_{g,h}\left(\xi\right)+\smallo\left(1\right)

(1.6)

as
x\to\xi
. A similar result holds true in dimensions
n\in\left\{4,5\right\}
when the potential function touches the function
c_{n}\Scal_{g}
at order 1 at some point on the manifold. More precisely, a straightforward extension of a result by Robert and Vétois
[
RV4
]
*Proposition 8.1 gives that for every smooth metric
g
on
M
and every function
h\in C^{1,\theta}\left(M\right)
,
\vartheta\in\left(0,1\right)
, if the operator
\Delta_{g}+h
has empty kernel and

\left(h-c_{n}\Scal_{g}\right)\left(\xi\right)=\left|\nabla\left(h-c_{n}\Scal_{g}\right)\left(\xi\right)\right|=0

for some point
\xi\in M
, then there exists a function
m_{g,h}\in C^{0}\left(M\right)
such that

G_{g,h}\left(x,\xi\right)=\left(\left(n-2\right)\omega_{n-1}\right)^{-1}\Lambda_{\xi}\left(x\right)d_{g_{\xi}}\left(x,\xi\right)^{2-n}+m_{g,h}\left(\xi\right)+\smallo\left(1\right)

(1.7)

as
x\to\xi
, where
\omega_{n-1}
is the volume of the standard unit sphere of dimension
n-1
and
g_{\xi}:=\Lambda_{\xi}^{2^{*}-2}g
is a conformal metric to
g
yielding conformal normal coordinates at the point
\xi
as in the article by Lee and Parker
[
LP
]
(see (
3.14
)).

With these definitions, we obtain the following:

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
\left(M,g\right)

,
h_{0}\in C^{1,\theta}\left(M\right)
,
\vartheta\in\left(0,1\right)
, and

). Assume that there exists a point
\xi_{0}\in M
such that
\left(M,g\right)
,
h_{0}
and
u_{0}
are symmetric with respect to
\xi_{0}
and
u_{0}\left(\xi_{0}\right)=0

•

if

m_{g,\tilde{h}_{0}}\left(\xi_{0}\right)\neq 0
,

•

if

[h_{0}\left(\xi_{0}\right)=c_{n}\Scal_{g}\left(\xi_{0}\right)

,
\vartheta^{\prime}\in\left(0,1\right)

\xi_{0}
and such that

\big[\big(\Delta_{g}+\tilde{h}_{0}\big)^{-1}\left(hu_{0}\right)\big]\left(\xi_{0}\right)\times\\
\left\{\begin{aligned} &-m_{g,\tilde{h}_{0}}\left(\xi_{0}\right)&&\text{if }n=3\text{ or }[n=4,5\text{ and }h_{0}\left(\xi_{0}\right)=c_{n}\Scal_{g}\left(\xi_{0}\right)]\\
&h_{0}\left(\xi_{0}\right)-c_{n}\Scal_{g}\left(\xi_{0}\right)&&\text{if }n\in\left\{4,5\right\}\text{ and }h_{0}\left(\xi_{0}\right)\neq c_{n}\Scal_{g}\left(\xi_{0}\right)\end{aligned}\right\}>0,

(1.8)

there exists a family of solutions
\left(u_{\varepsilon}\right)_{0<\varepsilon\ll 1}
to (

) with

as

.

Note that
\tilde{h}_{0}\in C^{1,\theta}\left(M\right)
for some
\vartheta\in\left(0,1\right)
when

and the symmetry assumptions on

,

and

, so the mass

. Theorem

is in stark contrast with the results by Druet

]
for positive solutions. It shows that, in the sign-changing case, blowing-up solutions can exist in dimensions

) holds true or the weak limit

is not identically zero. Note also that in the case where

], the weak limit surprisingly appears in a global constraint via the mass

. Like in Theorem
1.3
, we do not assume in Theorem

We refer to Section

2.2.
The case of dimensions

To obtain examples of situations where we can apply Theorem
1.4
, we now introduce some additional symmetry assumptions. We say that
\left(M,g\right)
is
2
-symmetric with respect to a point
\xi_{0}\in M
if
M
is connected and there exist
3
isometries
\sigma_{0},\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}:M\to M
and a domain
\Omega\subset M
such that

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\text{$\sigma_{0}\circ\sigma_{0}=\sigma_{1}\circ\sigma_{1}=\sigma_{2}\circ\sigma_{2}=id_{M}$ in $M$,}\\
&\text{$\sigma_{0}\circ\sigma_{1}=\sigma_{1}\circ\sigma_{0}$, $\sigma_{0}\circ\sigma_{2}=\sigma_{2}\circ\sigma_{0}$ and $\sigma_{1}\circ\sigma_{2}=\sigma_{2}\circ\sigma_{1}$ in $M$,}\\
&\text{$\xi_{0}\in\Gamma_{\sigma_{0}}\cap\Gamma_{\sigma_{1}}\cap\Gamma_{\sigma_{2}}$ and $d\left(\sigma_{0}\circ\sigma_{1}\circ\sigma_{2}\right)_{\xi_{0}}\left(v\right)=-v$ for all vectors $v\in T_{\xi_{0}}M$,}\\
&\text{$\sigma_{0}\left(\Omega\right)=\Omega$ and $M=\Omega\sqcup\sigma_{1}\left(\Omega\right)\sqcup\sigma_{2}\left(\Omega\right)\sqcup\sigma_{1}\circ\sigma_{2}\left(\Omega\right)\sqcup\left(\Gamma_{\sigma_{1}}\cup\Gamma_{\sigma_{2}}\right)$}\\
\end{aligned}\right.

where
\sqcup
is the disjoint union and
\Gamma_{\sigma_{i}}:=\left\{x\in M:\,\sigma_{i}\left(x\right)=x\right\}
for

. Remark that it easily follows from these properties that
\xi_{0}\in\partial\Omega
. Moreover, if
\left(M,g\right)
is
2
-symmetric with respect to
\xi_{0}
, then we say that a function

is
2
-symmetric with respect to
\xi_{0}
if
h\circ\sigma_{0}=h\circ\sigma_{1}=h\circ\sigma_{2}=h
in
M
. Clearly, if
\left(M,g\right)
and
h
are
2
-symmetric with respect to
\xi_{0}
, then by taking
\sigma:=\sigma_{0}\circ\sigma_{1}\circ\sigma_{2}
, we obtain that
\left(M,g\right)
and
h
are symmetric with respect to
\xi_{0}
.

A first example of a
2
-symmetric manifold is given by the standard unit sphere
\left(\mathbb{S}^{n},g_{0}\right)
with
n\geq 2
and

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\xi_{0}:=\left(0,\dotsc,0,1\right),\,\Omega:=\left\{x\in\mathbb{S}^{n}:\,x_{1}>0\text{ and }x_{2}>0\right\},\\
&\sigma_{0}\left(x\right):=\left(x_{1},x_{2},-x_{3},\dotsc,-x_{n},x_{n+1}\right),\,\sigma_{1}\left(x\right):=\left(-x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},\dotsc,x_{n},x_{n+1}\right)\\
&\text{and }\sigma_{2}\left(x\right):=\left(x_{1},-x_{2},x_{3},\dotsc,x_{n},x_{n+1}\right)\end{aligned}\right.

for all points
x:=\left(x_{1},\dotsc,x_{n+1}\right)\in\mathbb{S}^{n}
. Another example is given by the product of spheres

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\xi_{0}:=\left(\left(0,\dotsc,0,1\right),\left(0,\dotsc,0,1\right)\right),\\

.

\left(u_{\varepsilon}\right)_{0\leq\varepsilon\ll 1}

and such that no subsequences of
\left(u_{\varepsilon}\right)_{\varepsilon}

\left(u_{\varepsilon}\right)_{\varepsilon}
blows up as
\varepsilon\to 0
. We can then apply the main results in the articles by Premoselli
[
P
]
and Struwe
[
St
]
, which give that there exist a subsequence
\left(u_{\alpha}\right)_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}}
of
\left(u_{\varepsilon}\right)_{\varepsilon}
, a solution
u_{0}\in C^{2}\left(M\right)
to (
1.1
), a number
k\in\mathbb{N}
and for every
i\in\left\{1,\dotsc,k\right\}
, a sequence of positive numbers
\left(\mu_{i,\alpha}\right)_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}}
, a sequences of points
\left(\xi_{i,\alpha}\right)_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}}
in
M
and a nontrivial solution
V_{i}\in D^{1,2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
to the equation

\Delta_{\delta_{0}}V_{i}=\left|V_{i}\right|^{2^{*}-2}V_{i}\quad\text{in }\mathbb{R}^{n},

where
\delta_{0}
is the Euclidean metric, such that

u_{\alpha}=u_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{k}V_{i,\alpha}+\smallo\left(1\right)\quad\text{in }H^{1}\left(M\right)

and

\left|u_{\alpha}-u_{0}-\sum_{i=1}^{k}V_{i,\alpha}\right|=\smallo\left(1+\sum_{i=1}^{k}B_{i,\alpha}\right)\quad\text{ uniformly in }M

(3.1)

as
\alpha\to\infty
, where

B_{i,\alpha}\left(x\right):=\left(\frac{\sqrt{n\left(n-2\right)}\mu_{i,\alpha}}{\mu_{i,\alpha}^{2}+d_{g}\left(x,\xi_{i,\alpha}\right)^{2}}\right)^{\frac{n-2}{2}}\quad\forall x\in M

and the bubbling profiles
V_{i,\alpha}
are defined as follows:

•

In the case where
u_{0}\not\equiv 0
or
\ker\left(\Delta_{g}+h_{0}\right)\neq\left\{0\right\}

V_{i,\alpha}\left(x\right):=\chi\left(d_{g}\left(\xi_{i,\alpha},x\right)\right)\mu_{i,\alpha}^{-\frac{n-2}{2}}V_{i}\left(\frac{1}{\mu_{i,\alpha}}\exp_{\xi_{i,\alpha}}^{-1}\left(x\right)\right)\quad\forall x\in M,

where
\exp_{\xi_{i,\alpha}}
is the exponential map at the point
\xi_{i,\alpha}
with respect to the metric
g
, we identify

with
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\chi
is a smooth, nonnegative cutoff function in
\left[0,\infty\right)
such that
\chi=1
in
\left[0,r_{0}/2\right)
and
\chi=0
in
\left[r_{0},\infty\right)
for some number

smaller than the injectivity radius of the metric
g
.

•

In the case where
u_{0}\equiv 0
and
\ker(\Delta_{g}+h_{0})=\left\{0\right\}
, we define

F\left(\xi_{i,\alpha},x\right):=\left(n-2\right)\omega_{n-1}d_{g}\left(\xi_{i,\alpha},x\right)^{n-2}G_{g,h_{0}}\left(\xi_{i,\alpha},x\right)\quad\forall x\in M,

where
\omega_{n-1}
is the volume of the standard unit sphere of dimension
n-1
and
G_{g,h_{0}}
is the Green’s function of the operator
\Delta_{g}+h_{0}
. We then let

V_{i,\alpha}\left(x\right):=\chi\left(d_{g}\left(\xi_{i,\alpha},x\right)\right)F\left(\xi_{i,\alpha},x\right)\mu_{i,\alpha}^{-\frac{n-2}{2}}V_{i}\left(\frac{1}{\mu_{i,\alpha}}\exp_{\xi_{i,\alpha}}^{-1}\left(x\right)\right)\\
+\left(1-\chi\left(d_{g}\left(\xi_{i,\alpha},x\right)\right)\right)\left(n-2\right)\omega_{n-1}\lambda_{i}\mu_{i,\alpha}^{\frac{n-2}{2}}G_{g,h_{0}}\left(\xi_{i,\alpha},x\right)\quad\forall x\in M,

where
\exp_{\xi_{i,\alpha}}
and
\chi
are as in the first case and
\lambda_{i}\in\mathbb{R}
is such that

V_{i}\left(y\right)=\lambda_{i}\left|y\right|^{2-n}+\bigO\left(\left|y\right|^{1-n}\right)\textrm{ as }|y|\to+\infty,

where
y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\left|y\right|:=\left|y\right|_{\delta_{0}}
.

Moreover, the sequences
\left(\mu_{i,\alpha}\right)_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}}
and
\left(\xi_{i,\alpha}\right)_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}}
satisfy

\frac{d_{g}\left(\xi_{i,\alpha},\xi_{j,\alpha}\right)^{2}}{\mu_{i,\alpha}\mu_{j,\alpha}}+\frac{\mu_{i,\alpha}}{\mu_{j,\alpha}}+\frac{\mu_{j,\alpha}}{\mu_{i,\alpha}}\longrightarrow\infty

(3.2)

as
\alpha\to\infty
, for all
i,j\in\left\{1,\dotsc,k\right\}
such that

. By renumbering and passing to a subsequence if necessary, we may assume that

for all

and
\xi_{1,\alpha}\to\xi_{1,0}
as
\alpha\to\infty
for some point

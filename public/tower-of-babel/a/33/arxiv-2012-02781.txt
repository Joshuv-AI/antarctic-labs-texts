One-shot dynamical resource theory
Xiao Yuan, Pei Zeng, Minbo Gao, Qi Zhao

Abstract
A fundamental problem in resource theory is to study the manipulation of the resource. Focusing on a general dynamical resource theory of quantum channels, here we consider tasks of one-shot resource distillation and dilution with a single copy of the resource. For any target of unitary channel or pure state preparation channel, we establish a universal strategy to determine upper and lower bounds on rates that convert between any given resource and the target. We show that the rates are related to resource measures based on the channel robustness and the channel hypothesis testing entropy, with regularization factors of the target resource measures. The strategy becomes optimal with converged bounds when the channel robustness is finite and measures of the target resource collapse to the same value. The single-shot result also applies to asymptotic parallel manipulation of channels to obtain asymptotic resource conversion rates. We give several examples of dynamical resources, including the purity, classical capacity, quantum capacity, non-uniformity, coherence, and entanglement of quantum channels. Our results are applicable to general dynamical resource theories with potential applications in quantum communication, fault-tolerant quantum computing, and quantum thermodynamics.

II
Framework

II.1
Dynamical resource theories

We first consider a general framework for resource
T
, which consists of the set of free resource objects
\mathbb{F}_{T}
and the set of free operations
\mathbb{O}_{T}
. The two sets are related by the
golden rule
, which requires that for any operation in
\mathbb{O}_{T}
, it can only map a free resource object to a free one.
For static resource theories, the objects are quantum states and the operations are quantum channels.
For example, considering the resource theory of coherence
Aberg 2006
;
Baumgratz et al. 2014
;
Streltsov et al. 2017
, the set of free states
\mathbb{F}_{Coh}
, also known as incoherent states, are
\sigma=\sum_{i}p_{i}\ket{i}\bra{i}
, which are diagonal in the computational basis
\{\ket{i}\}
. For the set of free operations
\mathbb{O}_{T}
, there are several different definitions, such as the maximally incoherent operation (MIO)
Aberg 2006
, dephasing-covariant incoherent operation
Chitambar and Gour 2016
;
Marvian and Spekkens 2016
, the incoherent operation
Baumgratz et al. 2014
, etc, which could lead to different resource manipulation capabilities
Winter and Yang 2016
;
Zhao et al. 2018a
;
Zhao et al. 2018b
;
Regula et al. 2018
. The largest set of free operations allowed by the golden rule is called the resource non-generating operations (RNG), as
\{\Phi(\sigma)\in\mathbb{F}_{T},\,\forall\sigma\in\mathbb{F}_{T}\}
, e.g., the MIO for coherence. Any other definition of free operations is a subset of RNG operations.

For a dynamical resource, the objects are quantum channels and the operations are superchannels
Chiribella et al. 2008
;
Gour 2019
. Specifically, for a system
A
, we denote its Hilbert space as
\mathbb{H}(A)
and linear operators as
\mathbb{L}(A)
. A quantum channel
\mathcal{N}^{A\rightarrow B}
from system
A
to system
B
is a completely positive (CP) and trace preserving (TP) linear map from
\mathbb{L}(A)
to
\mathbb{L}(B)
. Denote the set of linear maps as
\mathbb{L}^{A\rightarrow B}
. Regarding a channel as a dynamical object, a quantum superchannel
\mathbf{\Lambda}^{AB\rightarrow CD}
from
\mathcal{N}^{A\rightarrow B}
to
\mathcal{M}^{C\rightarrow D}
is a supermap from
\mathbb{L}^{A\rightarrow B}
to
\mathbb{L}^{C\rightarrow D}
. A supermap is a superchannel when it is completely CP preserving and TP preserving
Gour 2019
. We refer to Appendixfor the exact definition of superchannels and their properties. Here we only need to notice that a superchannel can always be written as

\mathbf{\Lambda}(\mathcal{N}^{A\to B})=\mathcal{U}^{BE\to D}\circ\left(\mathcal{N}^{A\to B}\otimes\mathcal{I}^{E}\right)\circ\mathcal{V}^{C\to AE},

(1)

where
E
is an ancillary system and
\mathcal{U}
and
\mathcal{V}
are quantum channels.

An example dynamical resource is quantum memory or channel capacity
Rosset et al. 2018
;
Yuan et al. 2019
, which measures the capability of the channel in preserving quantum information or quantum entanglement. The set of free resources
\mathbb{F}_{QC}
are measure and prepare channels
\mathcal{M}(\rho)=\sum_{i}\mathrm{Tr}[\rho O_{i}]\sigma_{i}
with positive observable value measure
\{O_{i}\}
and quantum states
\{\sigma_{i}\}
. Free operations could be RNG superchannels that map free channels to free channels
Yuan et al. 2019
or physically realizable ones as introduced in Ref.
Rosset et al. 2018
. Unitary channels are regarded as the optimal resource since they fully preserve the input information.

Meanwhile, for any state resource theory, one can define a corresponding channel resource theory to characterize its capability in manipulating the state resource
Liu and Yuan 2020
;
Liu and Winter 2019
;
Theurer et al. 2019
. Focusing on the capability of creating the resource, one can regard the set of RNG channels (or its subset) as a free dynamical resource, which cannot create a static resource from a non-resourceful state. Free operations on the dynamical resource could be defined as the set of RNG superchannels or its subset such as
\mathbf{\Lambda}(\mathcal{N}^{A\to B})=\mathcal{M}^{BE\to D}\circ\left(\mathcal{N}^{A\to B}\otimes\mathcal{I}^{E}\right)\circ\mathcal{N}^{C\to AE}
with RNG channels
\mathcal{M}
and
\mathcal{N}
. One can regard a unitary that rotates a free state to the maximal resource as the target channel. Considering the interplay between state and channel resource theories, one can also choose the deterministic maximal resource state generation channel as the target.

II.2
Resource monotones

Based on the framework, now we introduce monotones to characterize the resource. We focus on quantum channels and consider a function
\mu
that maps a channel object to a real number. It is called a resource monotone if it cannot be increased under free operations, i.e.,
\mu(\mathcal{N})\geq\mu(\mathbf{\Lambda}(\mathcal{N})),\,\forall\mathbf{\Lambda}\in\mathbb{O}
. We first introduce the monotone based on how robust the object is against the noise of free resource,

\displaystyle R_{T}(\mathcal{N})

\displaystyle=\min_{\mathcal{M}\in\mathbb{F}_{T},}\left\{\lambda\geq 0:\frac{1}{1+\lambda}\mathcal{N}+\frac{\lambda}{1+\lambda}\mathcal{M}\in\mathbb{F}_{T}\right\},

(2)

which is monotonically non-increasing under any RNG operations. It is called robustness or free robustness since the optimization is over free resources. The measure
R_{T}(\mathcal{N})
could be infinite when the set of free resource does not span the whole space. For example, when the robustness of state resources is infinite, the state-induced channel resource also have infinite robustness. In this case, we consider the general robustness

\displaystyle R_{G,T}(\mathcal{N})

\displaystyle=\min_{\mathcal{M}}\left\{\lambda\geq 0:\frac{1}{1+\lambda}\mathcal{N}+\frac{\lambda}{1+\lambda}\mathcal{M}\in\mathbb{F}_{T}\right\},

(3)

where the channel
\mathcal{M}
could be an arbitrary CPTP map. We note that by defining the Choi state
\Phi^{+}_{\mathcal{N}}=\mathcal{I}\otimes\mathcal{N}(\Phi^{+})
with the maximally entangled state
\Phi^{+}
, the above optimization over channel can be re-expressed as an optimization over Choi states or any bipartite state with full Schmidt rank.

In the following, we will show that the robustness or the generalized robustness will play a key role in characterizing one-shot resource dilution. To have a even clearer connection, we consider the log versions of the two measures as

\displaystyle LR_{T}(\mathcal{N})

\displaystyle=\log(1+R_{T}(\mathcal{N})),

(4)

\displaystyle D_{\max,T}(\mathcal{N})

\displaystyle=\log(1+R_{G,T}(\mathcal{N})).

Here we denote the logarithm version of the generalized robustness to be
D_{\max,T}
since it coincides with the definition based on the max-relative entropy of channels.
Furthermore, for any resource monotone, we can smooth it by allowing a nonzero error parameter
\epsilon
. For example, the smoothed logarithmic robustness and max-entropy measures are

\displaystyle LR_{T}^{\epsilon}(\mathcal{N})

\displaystyle=\min_{\frac{1}{2}\left\|\mathcal{N}^{\prime}-\mathcal{N}\right\|_{\diamond}\leq\epsilon}LR_{T}(\mathcal{N}^{\prime}),

(5)

\displaystyle D^{\epsilon}_{\max,T}(\mathcal{N})

\displaystyle=\min_{\frac{1}{2}\left\|\mathcal{N}^{\prime}-\mathcal{N}\right\|_{\diamond}\leq\epsilon}D_{\max,T}(\mathcal{N}^{\prime}),

where
\frac{1}{2}\left\|\mathcal{N}^{\prime}-\mathcal{N}\right\|_{\diamond}=\frac{1}{2}\max_{\psi}\|(\mathcal{N^{\prime}}\otimes\mathcal{I})(\psi)-(\mathcal{N}\otimes\mathcal{I})(\psi)\|_{1}
is the diamond norm of channels with optimization over all states from the channel input and any ancillary input.

To characterize the one-shot distillation process, we consider another type of resource monotone based on the channel hypothesis testing entropy,

\displaystyle D_{H}^{\epsilon}(\mathcal{N}||\mathcal{M})

\displaystyle=\max_{\psi}D_{H}^{\epsilon}(\mathcal{N}\otimes\mathcal{I}(\psi)||\mathcal{M}\otimes\mathcal{I}(\psi)),

(6)

with the state hypothesis testing entropy

D_{H}^{\epsilon}(\rho\|\sigma)=\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq A\leq I\\
\mathrm{Tr}[A\rho]\geq 1-\epsilon\end{subarray}}-\log\mathrm{Tr}[A\sigma].

(7)

We note that the smooth is called operator smoothing, which is different from Eq. (
5
).
The induced resource monotone is defined by minimizing
D_{H}^{\epsilon}(\mathcal{N}||\mathcal{M})
over all free resources as

D_{H,T}^{\epsilon}(\mathcal{N})=\min_{\mathcal{M}\in\mathbb{F}_{T}}D_{H}^{\epsilon}(\mathcal{N}||\mathcal{M}).

(8)

Operationally, the measure
D_{H,T}^{\epsilon}(\mathcal{N})
characterizes asymmetric discrimination between
\mathcal{N}
and any free resource.

We prove in Appendix
that all the above measures satisfy the monotonicity requirement and study their other properties such as convexity.

Lemma 1
.

The quantifiers
D^{\epsilon}_{\max,T}(\mathcal{N})
,
LR^{\epsilon}_{T}(\mathcal{N})
, and
D_{H,T}^{\epsilon}(\mathcal{N})
are resource monotones.

The channel hypothesis testing entropy
D_{H}^{\epsilon}(\mathcal{N}||\mathcal{M})
in Eq. (
6
) involves an optimization over all input states that are possibly entangled with any ancillary system. Considering the maximally entangled state or states without entanglement as the input, we define other entropy measures

\displaystyle D_{\tilde{H}}^{\epsilon}(\mathcal{N}||\mathcal{M})

\displaystyle=D_{H}^{\epsilon}(\Phi^{+}_{\mathcal{N}}||\Phi^{+}_{\mathcal{M}}),

(9)

\displaystyle D_{\hat{H}}^{\epsilon}(\mathcal{N}||\mathcal{M})

\displaystyle=\max_{\psi}D_{H}^{\epsilon}(\mathcal{N}(\psi)||\mathcal{M}(\psi)).

We note that

D_{H}^{\epsilon}(\mathcal{N}||\mathcal{M})\geq\max\{D_{\tilde{H}}^{\epsilon}(\mathcal{N}||\mathcal{M}),D_{\hat{H}}^{\epsilon}(\mathcal{N}||\mathcal{M})\},

(10)

yet the order between
D_{\tilde{H}}^{\epsilon}(\mathcal{N}||\mathcal{M})
and
D_{\hat{H}}^{\epsilon}(\mathcal{N}||\mathcal{M})
is case dependent
Yuan 2019
. We also define the corresponding resource quantifiers as

\displaystyle D_{\tilde{H},T}^{\epsilon}(\mathcal{N})

\displaystyle=\min_{\mathcal{M}\in\mathbb{F}_{T}}D_{\tilde{H}}^{\epsilon}(\mathcal{N}||\mathcal{M}),

(11)

\displaystyle D_{\hat{H},T}^{\epsilon}(\mathcal{N})

\displaystyle=\min_{\mathcal{M}\in\mathbb{F}_{T}}D_{\hat{H}}^{\epsilon}(\mathcal{N}||\mathcal{M}),

which will be useful for bounding the achievability part of one-shot resource distillation.

II.3
Collapse of resource quantifiers

The five resource quantifiers are not independent and they are ordered as

\displaystyle LR_{T}^{\epsilon}(\mathcal{N})

\displaystyle\geq D_{\max,T}^{\epsilon}(\mathcal{N})\geq D_{{H},T}^{\epsilon}(\mathcal{N})\geq...

(12)

\displaystyle...

\displaystyle\geq\{D_{\tilde{H},T}^{\epsilon}(\mathcal{N}),D_{\hat{H},T}^{\epsilon}(\mathcal{N})\},

and whether the equal signs could be achieved is now under investigation.
We first consider a state-induced channel resource with the target being a pure state preparation channel
\mathcal{G}(\rho)=\psi
with pure state
\psi
.
We show that the four resource monotones of
\mathcal{G}
with
\epsilon=0
are bounded by monotones of the state resource.

Lemma 2
.

Consider a state induced channel resource with a state preparation channel,
\mathcal{G}(\rho)=\psi
,
\forall\rho
, with pure state
\psi
.
Suppose that free channels are
RNG
channels of the state resource theory and the maximally mixed state is a free resource state, we have

\displaystyle D^{0}_{\max,T}(\psi)

\displaystyle\geq D^{0}_{\max,T}(\mathcal{G})\geq D_{H,T}^{0}(\mathcal{G})\geq...

(13)

\displaystyle...

\displaystyle\geq\{D_{\tilde{H},T}^{0}(\mathcal{G}),D_{\hat{H},T}^{0}(\mathcal{G})\}\geq D_{H,T}^{0}(\psi),

where
D^{0}_{\max,T}(\psi)
and
D_{H,T}^{0}(\psi)
are state resource monotones with respect to the set of free state resource.

It has been shown that the monotones of the maximal resource, i.e., the golden state, collapse to the same value when the set of free states is a convex hull of pure free states
Liu et al. 2019
. Therefore the resource monotones of the state preparation channel also collapse.

Lemma 3
.

For a state resource theory, suppose the set of free states is a convex hull of pure states and includes all maximally mixed states. Suppose
\psi
is the golden state resource that maximize
D_{H,T}^{0}(\psi)
.
Consider the corresponding channel resource with
RNG
free channels. The resource monotones of the state preparation channel,
\mathcal{G}(\rho)=\psi
,
\forall\rho
, collapse

\displaystyle D^{0}_{\max,T}(\mathcal{G})=D_{H,T}^{0}(\mathcal{G})=D_{\tilde{H},G}^{0}(\mathcal{G})=D_{\hat{H},G}^{0}(\mathcal{G})=D_{H,T}^{0}(\psi).

(14)

Note that even though the logarithmic robustness is not included in the theorem, it also coincides with the measures (when it is finite) for resources such as dynamical entanglement as we show in Sec.
IV
.

When
\mathcal{N}
is a unitary channel
\mathcal{U}
, the hypothesis testing entropy monotones for
\epsilon=0
equal

\displaystyle D_{H,T}^{0}(\mathcal{U})

\displaystyle=-\log F_{T}(\mathcal{U}),

(15)

\displaystyle D_{\tilde{H},T}^{0}(\mathcal{U})

\displaystyle=-\log\tilde{F}_{T}(\mathcal{U}),

which are closely related to channel fidelity measures

\displaystyle F_{T}(\mathcal{N})

\displaystyle=\max_{\mathcal{M}\in\mathbb{F}_{T}}\min_{\psi}F(\mathcal{N}(\psi)\otimes\mathcal{I},\mathcal{M}(\psi)\otimes\mathcal{I}),

(16)

\displaystyle\tilde{F}_{T}(\mathcal{N})

\displaystyle=\max_{\mathcal{M}\in\mathbb{F}_{T}}F(\mathcal{N}(\Phi^{+})\otimes\mathcal{I},\mathcal{M}(\Phi^{+})\otimes\mathcal{I}),

and state fidelity
F(\rho,\sigma)=(\mathrm{Tr}[\sqrt{\sqrt{\rho}\sigma\sqrt{\rho}}])^{2}
. It is an open problem when the fidelity measures will be the same as the robustness measures. Nevertheless, for each resource theory, we can explicitly lower bound
D_{\tilde{H},T}^{0}(\mathcal{U})
via the Choi state of
\mathcal{U}
and a specific free resource, and upper bound
LR(\mathcal{U})
or
D_{\max}(\mathcal{U})
via an explicit decomposition. We can then prove the collapse of the measures by showing the convergence of the bounds, as we discuss in Sec.
IV
.

We also consider regularized measures of
n
copies of resources with zero smooth as

\displaystyle{{m}_{LR,T}(\mathcal{N}^{\otimes n})}

\displaystyle=LR^{0}_{T}(\mathcal{N}^{\otimes n})/n,

(17)

\displaystyle{{m}_{\max,T}(\mathcal{N}^{\otimes n})}

\displaystyle=D^{0}_{\max,T}(\mathcal{N}^{\otimes n})/n,

\displaystyle m_{H,T}(\mathcal{N}^{\otimes n})

\displaystyle=D_{H,T}^{0}(\mathcal{N}^{\otimes n})/n,

\displaystyle m_{\tilde{H},T}(\mathcal{N}^{\otimes n})

\displaystyle=D_{\tilde{H},T}^{0}(\mathcal{N}^{\otimes n})/n,

\displaystyle m_{\hat{H},T}(\mathcal{N}^{\otimes n})

\displaystyle=D_{\hat{H},T}^{0}(\mathcal{N}^{\otimes n})/n.

We will show that different regularized measures of the target resource (being either
\mathcal{G}
or
\mathcal{U}
) will be different normalizers to the upper and lower bounds of the distillation and dilution rates, as we shortly define.
The collapse of these measures will be crucial for exact distillation and dilution protocol.

III
One-shot distillation and dilution

Now we consider general one-shot distillation and dilution between a given channel
\mathcal{N}
and a target channel. We consider two types of targets with unitary channels and state preparation channels.

III.1
Target — unitary channels

We first regard a unitary channel
\mathcal{U}
as the target resource, and consider one-shot conversion between single use of a given resource
\mathcal{N}
and multiple copies of
\mathcal{U}
. Specifically, we consider the maximal number of
\mathcal{U}
that can be distilled from
\mathcal{N}
and the minimal number of
\mathcal{U}
that is required to synthesize
\mathcal{N}
, i.e., the tasks of resource distillation and dilution. By allowing a smoothing parameter
\epsilon\in[0,1]
, we define the distillation and dilution rates as

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}^{1,\epsilon}_{d,T}(\mathcal{N})

\displaystyle=\max_{\mathbf{\Lambda}\in\mathcal{O}}\left\{n:\frac{1}{2}\left\|\mathbf{\Lambda(\mathcal{N})-\mathcal{U}^{\otimes n}}\right\|_{\diamond}\leq\epsilon\right\},

(18)

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}^{1,\epsilon}_{c,T}(\mathcal{N})

\displaystyle=\min_{\mathbf{\Lambda}\in\mathcal{O}}\left\{n:\frac{1}{2}\left\|\mathbf{\Lambda(\mathcal{U}^{\otimes n})-\mathcal{N}}\right\|_{\diamond}\leq\epsilon\right\}.

Here the optimization is over all possible free operations and the parameter
\epsilon
allows a certain error in the process. Determining the distillation and dilution rates is a central task in resource theories and it is in general challenging and resource-dependent for an arbitrary set of
\mathcal{O}
. However, when focusing on the maximal set of free operations, i.e., RNG superchannels, we can provide a unified approach to bound the distillation and dilution rates separately. Depending on whether the robustness measure is finite, we have two general schemes that are based on the optimization of the robustness and the generalized robustness measures.

III.1.1
Finite robustness

We first show the one-shot distillation and dilution results given finite robustness measure of the channel
\mathcal{N}
.

Theorem 1
.

When the free robustness measure
R(\mathcal{N})
is finite for all the channels. For any given channel
\mathcal{N}
, the one-shot distillation rate
\mathcal{R}^{1,\epsilon}_{d,T}
of the target resource
\mathcal{U}
is given by

n_{l}\leq\mathcal{R}^{1,\epsilon}_{d,T}(\mathcal{N})\leq D^{\epsilon}_{H,T}(\mathcal{N})/m_{H,T}(\mathcal{U}^{\otimes n_{u}})

(19)

with
n_{l}=\max\{n:n\leq D_{H,T}^{\epsilon}(\mathcal{N})/{{m}_{LR,T}(\mathcal{U}^{\otimes n})}\}
and
n_{u}=\max\{n:n\leq D^{\epsilon}_{H,T}(\mathcal{N})/m_{H,T}(\mathcal{U}^{\otimes n})\}
.

Theorem 2
.

When the free robustness measure
R(\mathcal{N})
is finite for all the channels. For any given channel
\mathcal{N}
, the one-shot dilution rate
\mathcal{R}^{1,\epsilon}_{c,T}
of the target resource
\mathcal{U}
is given by

LR^{\epsilon}(\mathcal{N})/{{m}_{LR,T}(\mathcal{U}^{\otimes n_{l}})}\leq\mathcal{R}^{1,\epsilon}_{c,T}(\mathcal{N})\leq n_{u},

(20)

with
n_{l}=\min\{n\in\mathbb{D}:n\geq LR^{\epsilon}_{T}(\mathcal{N})/{{m}_{LR,T}(\mathcal{U}^{\otimes n})}\}
and
n_{u}=\min\{n\in\mathbb{D}:n\geq LR^{\epsilon}_{T}(\mathcal{N})/m_{H,T}(\mathcal{U}^{\otimes n})\}
.

The above results become clearer when the regularized measures for
\mathcal{U}
collapse. In this case, we can use resource monotones to exactly characterize the one-shot distillation and dilution rates.

Corollary 1
.

When the robustness measure is finite and
{{m}_{LR,T}(\mathcal{G}^{\otimes n})}=m_{H,T}(\mathcal{G}^{\otimes n})=c
for all
n
, we have

\lfloor D^{\epsilon}_{H,T}(\mathcal{N})/c\rfloor\leq\mathcal{R}^{1,\epsilon}_{d,T}(\mathcal{N})\leq D^{\epsilon}_{H,T}(\mathcal{N})/c.

(21)

Corollary 2
.

When the robustness measure is finite and
{{m}_{LR,T}(\mathcal{U}^{\otimes n})}=m_{H,T}(\mathcal{U}^{\otimes n})=c
for all
n
, we have

LR^{\epsilon}_{T}(\mathcal{N})/c\leq\mathcal{R}^{1,\epsilon}_{c,T}(\mathcal{N})\leq\lceil LR^{\epsilon}_{T}(\mathcal{N})/c\rceil.

(22)

We will show that the collapse of measures for unitary channels indeed happens in the quantum capacity and dynamical entanglement resource theories.

III.1.2
Infinite robustness

When a channel
\mathcal{N}
has infinite free robustness, we can instead construct one-shot distillation and dilution processes based on the generalized robustness measure.

Theorem 3
.

When there exist quantum channels with infinite free robustness, for a given channel
\mathcal{N}
, the one-shot distillation rate is alternatively upper bounded by

\mathcal{R}^{1,\epsilon}_{d,T}(\mathcal{N})\leq D^{\epsilon}_{H,T}(\mathcal{N})/m_{H,T}(\mathcal{U}^{\otimes n_{u}})

(23)

where
n_{u}=\max\{n:n\leq D^{\epsilon}_{H,T}(\mathcal{N})/m_{H,T}(\mathcal{U}^{\otimes n})\}
.

Theorem 4
.

When there exist quantum channels with infinite free robustness, for a given channel
\mathcal{N}
, the one-shot dilution rate is lower bounded by

\mathcal{R}^{1,\epsilon}_{c,T}(\mathcal{N})\geq D_{\max,T}^{\epsilon}(\mathcal{N})/{{m}_{\max,T}(\mathcal{U}^{\otimes n_{l}})},

(24)

Moreover, if the target channel satisfies the constant trace condition, i.e,
\mathrm{Tr}[\Phi^{CD}_{\mathcal{C}}\Phi^{CD}_{\mathcal{U}^{\otimes n}}]
is a constant for all free channels
\mathcal{C}
and a fixed
n
,
we have

\mathcal{R}^{1,\epsilon}_{c,T}(\mathcal{N})\leq n_{u}.

(25)

with
n_{l}=\min\{n:n\geq D_{\max,T}^{\epsilon}(\mathcal{N})/{m}_{\max,T}(\mathcal{U}^{\otimes n})\}
and
n_{u}=\min\{n\in\mathbb{D}:n\geq D_{\max,T}^{\epsilon}(\mathcal{N})/m_{\tilde{H},T}(\mathcal{U}^{\otimes n})\}
.

We note that the major difference to the results with finite robustness is that the achievability part is replaced with
D_{\tilde{H},T}^{\epsilon}
or
m_{\tilde{H}}(\mathcal{U}^{\otimes n})
. This is because here the distillation and dilution superchannels are constructed based on inputting the maximally entangled state, whereas the superchannels used in the previous part does not have this assumption.
When the regularized measures collapse, we have bounds as follows.

Corollary 3
.

When the robustness measure is infinite and
{{m}_{\max,T}(\mathcal{U}^{\otimes n})}=m_{\tilde{H},T}(\mathcal{U}^{\otimes n})=c
, we have

D_{\max,T}^{\epsilon}(\mathcal{N})/c\leq\mathcal{R}^{1,\epsilon}_{c,T}(\mathcal{N})\leq\lceil D_{\max,T}^{\epsilon}(\mathcal{N})/c\rceil.

(26)

III.2
Target — state preparation channels

Now consider that the target channel is a pure state preparation or resource generation channel
\mathcal{G}
, which maps any input state to a pure output state. We can similarly construct the distillation and dilution channels. The result also depends on whether the robustness is finite and the constructions assume the input states are maximally entangled states. We summarize the result with both finite and infinite robustness as follows.

III.2.1
Finite robustness

Theorem 5
.

When the free robustness
R(\mathcal{N})
is finite for all the channels, for a given
\mathcal{N}
, the one-shot distillation rate of the optimal state preparation channel
\mathcal{G}
is bounded by

n_{l}\leq\mathcal{R}^{1,\epsilon}_{d,T}(\mathcal{N})\leq D^{\epsilon}_{H,T}(\mathcal{N})/m_{\hat{H},T}(\mathcal{G}^{\otimes n_{u}}),

(27)

with
n_{l}=\max\{n:n\leq D_{\tilde{H},T}^{\epsilon}(\mathcal{N})/{{m}_{LR,T}(\mathcal{G}^{\otimes n})}\}
and
n_{u}=\max\{n:n\leq D^{\epsilon}_{H,T}(\mathcal{N})/m_{\hat{H},T}(\mathcal{G}^{\otimes n})\}
.

Theorem 6
.

When the free robustness
R_{T}(\mathcal{N})
is finite for all the channels, for a given channel
\mathcal{N}
, the one-shot dilution rate
\mathcal{R}^{1,\epsilon}_{c,T}(\mathcal{N})
of the optimal state preparation channel
\mathcal{G}
under the RNG operations is bounded by

LR^{\epsilon}_{T}(\mathcal{N})/{{m}_{LR,T}(\mathcal{G}^{\otimes n_{l}})}\leq\mathcal{R}^{1,\epsilon}_{c,T}(\mathcal{N})\leq n_{u},

(28)

with
n_{l}=\min\{n\in\mathbb{D}:n\geq LR_{T}^{\epsilon}(\mathcal{N})/{m}_{LR,T}(\mathcal{G}^{\otimes n})\}
and
n_{u}=\min\{n\in\mathbb{D}:n\geq LR_{T}^{\epsilon}(\mathcal{N})/m_{\tilde{H},T}(\mathcal{G}^{\otimes n})\}
.

When the regularized measures collapse, we have bounds as follows.

Corollary 4
.

When the robustness measure is finite with
{{m}_{LR,T}(\mathcal{G}^{\otimes n})}=m_{\hat{H},T}(\mathcal{G}^{\otimes n})=c
, we have

\lfloor D^{\epsilon}_{\tilde{H},T}(\mathcal{N})/c\rfloor\leq\mathcal{R}^{1,\epsilon}_{d,T}(\mathcal{N})\leq D^{\epsilon}_{H,T}(\mathcal{N})/c.

(29)

Corollary 5
.

When the robustness measure is finite and
{{m}_{LR,T}(\mathcal{G}^{\otimes n})}=m_{\tilde{H},T}(\mathcal{G}^{\otimes n})=c
, we have

LR^{\epsilon}_{T}(\mathcal{N})/c\leq\mathcal{R}^{1,\epsilon}_{c,T}(\mathcal{N})\leq\lceil LR^{\epsilon}_{T}(\mathcal{N})/c\rceil.

(30)

III.2.2
Infinite robustness

Similarly, when the free robustness of a channel
\mathcal{N}
is infinite, we can evaluate the one-shot distillation and dilution processes as follows.

Theorem 7
.

When there exist quantum channels with infinite free robustness, for a given channel
\mathcal{N}
, the one-shot distillation rate
\mathcal{R}^{1,\epsilon}_{d,T}(\mathcal{N})
of the target state preparation channel is bounded by

\mathcal{R}^{1,\epsilon}_{d,T}(\mathcal{N})\leq D^{\epsilon}_{H,T}(\mathcal{N})/m_{\hat{H},T}(\mathcal{G}^{\otimes n_{u}})

(31)

where
n_{u}=\max\{n:n\leq D^{\epsilon}_{H,T}(\mathcal{N})/m_{\hat{H},T}(\mathcal{G}^{\otimes n})\}
.

Theorem 8
.

When there exist quantum channels with infinite free robustness, for a given channel
\mathcal{N}
, the one-shot dilution rate
\mathcal{R}^{1,\epsilon}_{c,T}(\mathcal{N})
is bounded by

\mathcal{R}^{1,\epsilon}_{c,T}(\mathcal{N})\geq D_{\max}^{\epsilon}(\mathcal{N})/{{m}_{\max}(\mathcal{G}^{\otimes n_{l}})},

(32)

Moreover, if the target channel satisfies the constant trace condition, i.e,
\mathrm{Tr}[\Phi^{CD}_{\mathcal{C}}\Phi^{CD}_{\mathcal{G}^{\otimes n}}]
is a constant for all free channels
\mathcal{C}
and fixed
n
. Then we have:

\mathcal{R}^{1,\epsilon}_{c,T}(\mathcal{N})\leq n_{u}.

(33)

with
n_{l}=\min\{n:n\cdot{{m}_{\max}(\mathcal{}^{\otimes n})}\geq D_{\max}^{\epsilon}(\mathcal{N})\}
and
n_{u}=\min\{n\in\mathbb{D}:n\geq D_{\max}^{\epsilon}(\mathcal{N})/m_{\tilde{H}}(\mathcal{G}^{\otimes n})\}
.

When the regularized measures collapse, we have bounds as follows.

Corollary 6
.

When the robustness measure is infinite and
{{m}_{\max,T}(\mathcal{G}^{\otimes n})}=m_{\tilde{H}}(\mathcal{G}^{\otimes n})=c
, we have

D_{\max}^{\epsilon}(\mathcal{N})/c\leq\mathcal{R}^{1,\epsilon}_{c,T}(\mathcal{N})\leq\lceil D_{\max}^{\epsilon}(\mathcal{N})/c\rceil.

(34)

IV
Examples

Here we consider several typical channel resource theories, describing intrinsic channel properties and state-induced manipulation capabilities. We show how the general results in the previous section could be applied to specific resource theories.

IV.1
Intrinsic channel resources

We first discuss three intrinsic channel resource properties in preserving quantum information without relying on state resource theories.

IV.1.1
Purity

Almost all existing quantum properties relies on pure quantum states. Therefore, we define the
purity
of a channel by its ability in producing pure states. Since the least pure state is the maximally mixed state
\pi_{d}=I_{d}/d
with dimension
d
, the least pure channel is the maximally depolarizing channels
\mathcal{D}(\rho)=\pi_{d}
. To quantify the purity of channels, we define the corresponding free resources to be the set of maximally depolarizing channels

\mathbb{F}_{P}=\{\mathcal{D}|\mathcal{D}(\rho)=\pi_{d}\}.

(35)

Since an arbitrary channels could not be decomposed as linear combination of maximally depolarizing channels,
the robustness is infinite.

For the target channel, we consider it to be the identical qubit channel
\mathcal{I}_{2}
. We note that applying a unitary after a channel, i.e.,
\bf\Lambda(\mathcal{N})=\mathcal{U}\circ\mathcal{N}
is a free superchannel, therefore it is equivalent to consider an arbitrary unitary as the target.
Consider the qubit channel
\mathcal{I}_{2}
, we have

\displaystyle m_{\max,P}(\mathcal{I}_{2}^{\otimes n})=m_{H,P}(\mathcal{I}_{2}^{\otimes n})=m_{\tilde{H},P}(\mathcal{I}_{2}^{\otimes n})=2

(36)

for any integer
n
. Therefore we can apply Theorem
3
and Corollary
3
to have the one-shot distillation and dilution rates. We further prove refined bounds for the distillation part and we conclude our results as follows.

Result 1
.

The one-shot distillation and dilution rates of channel purity under RNG superchannels are

\displaystyle\lfloor D^{\epsilon}_{H,P}(\mathcal{N})/2\rfloor\leq\mathcal{R}^{1,\epsilon}_{d,P}(\mathcal{N})\leq D^{\epsilon}_{H,P}(\mathcal{N})/2,

(37)

\displaystyle D_{\max,P}^{\epsilon}(\mathcal{N})/2\leq\mathcal{R}^{1,\epsilon}_{c,P}(\mathcal{N})\leq\lceil D_{\max,P}^{\epsilon}(\mathcal{N})/2\rceil.

We can apply the one-shot result to obtain the asymptotic resource conversion limit. Defining the asymptotic parallel distillation and dilution rates as

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}^{\infty}_{d,T}(\mathcal{N})

\displaystyle=\lim_{\epsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}}\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\mathcal{R}^{1,\epsilon}_{d,T}(\mathcal{N}^{\otimes n}),

(38)

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}^{\infty}_{c,T}(\mathcal{N})

\displaystyle=\lim_{\epsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}}\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\mathcal{R}^{1,\epsilon}_{c,T}(\mathcal{N}^{\otimes n}).

Consider the channel relative entropy

S(\mathcal{N}\|\mathcal{M})=\max_{\psi}S(\mathcal{N}(\psi)\|\mathcal{M}(\psi)),

(39)

and the channel entropy

S(\mathcal{N})=S(\mathcal{N}\|\mathcal{D}).

(40)

With the asymptotic equal partition of channel entropy
Gour and Wilde 2018
, we can show that the asymptotic parallel distillation and dilution rates converges to
S(\mathcal{N})
.

Result 2
.

The asymptotic parallel distillation and dilution rates of channel purity under RNG superchannels are

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}^{\infty}_{d,P}(\mathcal{N})=\mathcal{R}^{\infty}_{c,P}(\mathcal{N})=S(\mathcal{N})/2.

(41)

Here we only consider parallel strategies and generalizing the results to incorporate adaptive strategies is an interesting future work.

IV.1.2
Classical capacity

In addition to producing pure states, a higher level resource of a channel is its capability in preserving the input state information. We note that a replacement channel
\mathcal{R}(\rho)=\sigma
outputs a state that is independent of the input. Therefore, we define the set of free resources to be

\mathbb{F}_{CC}=\{\mathcal{R}|\mathcal{R}(\rho)=\sigma\}

(42)

We call it the
classical capacity
or
reversibility
of channels and we also note that it corresponds to the
classical capacity
of quantum Shannon theory in quantum communication
Takagi et al. 2020
.

Consider the qubit channel
\mathcal{I}_{2}
as the target resource, which maximally preserves the input information. The robustness measure is infinite since linear combinations of replacement channels are still replacement maps. We thus focus on the generalized robustness measure and show that

\displaystyle m_{\max,CC}(\mathcal{I}_{2}^{\otimes n})=m_{H,CC}(\mathcal{I}_{2}^{\otimes n})=m_{\tilde{H},CC}(\mathcal{I}_{2}^{\otimes n})=2.

(43)

Applying Theorem
3
and Corollary
3
, we have the one-shot distillation and dilution rates.

Result 3
.

The one-shot distillation and dilution rates of channel classical capacity under RNG superchannels are

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}^{1,\epsilon}_{d,CC}(\mathcal{N})\leq D^{\epsilon}_{H,CC}(\mathcal{N})/2,

(44)

\displaystyle D_{\max,CC}^{\epsilon}

\displaystyle(\mathcal{N})/2\leq\mathcal{R}^{1,\epsilon}_{c,T}(\mathcal{N})\leq\lceil D_{\max,CC}^{\epsilon}(\mathcal{N})/2\rceil.

We also get the asymptotic conversions rates by applying the results to the asymptotic limit. Define measures

\displaystyle S^{\infty}_{T}(\mathcal{N})

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\min_{\mathcal{M}\in\mathbb{F}_{T}}\frac{1}{n}S(\mathcal{N}^{\otimes n}\|\mathcal{M}),

(45)

\displaystyle\tilde{S}^{\infty}_{T}(\mathcal{N})

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\min_{\mathcal{M}\in\mathbb{F}_{T}}\frac{1}{n}S(\Phi_{\mathcal{N}^{+}}^{\otimes n}\|\Phi_{\mathcal{M}^{+}}),

where
\tilde{S}^{\infty}_{T}(\mathcal{N})
only consider inputs with the maximally entangled state.
We have the asymptotic distillation and dilution rates with the help of tools from Ref.
Takagi et al. 2020
;
Fang et al. 2020
;
Wang and Wilde 2019
.

Result 4
.

The asymptotic parallel distillation and dilution rates of channel classical capacity under RNG superchannels are

\displaystyle R^{\infty}_{d,CC}(\mathcal{N})\leq S^{\infty}_{CC}(\mathcal{N})/2=R^{\infty}_{c,CC}(\mathcal{N}).

(46)

IV.1.3
Quantum capacity

The classical capacity is defined with respect to preserving any input state information. We can also focus on the
quantum capacity
or
quantum memory
in preserving quantum information
Rosset et al. 2018
;
Kuo et al. 2018
;
Yuan et al. 2019
. We consider a measure-and-prepare channel
\mathcal{M}(\rho)=\sum_{i}\mathrm{Tr}[\rho M_{i}]\sigma_{i}
with POVM
\{M_{i}\}
with POVM
\{M_{i}\}
and quantum states
\{\sigma_{i}\}
, which destroys the input state by the measurement and only forwards the classical measurement outcome. We define the set of free resources to be

\mathbb{F}_{QC}=\{\mathcal{M}|\mathcal{M}(\rho)=\sum_{i}\mathrm{Tr}[\rho M_{i}]\sigma_{i}\}.

(47)

Since the entanglement between the input state and any other systems are destroyed by a measure-and-prepare channel, the quantum capacity also characterizes the ability in preserving quantum correlation.

Consider the qubit channel
\mathcal{I}_{2}
as the target resource, which preserves any quantum correlation. The robustness measure is finite and we have

\displaystyle m_{LR,QC}(\mathcal{I}_{2}^{\otimes n})=m_{H,QC}(\mathcal{I}_{2}^{\otimes n})=m_{\tilde{H},QC}(\mathcal{I}_{2}^{\otimes n})=1.

(48)

Applying Corollary
1
and
2
, we have the one-shot distillation and dilution rates.

Result 5
.

The one-shot distillation and dilution rates of channel quantum capacity under RNG superchannels are

\displaystyle\lfloor D^{\epsilon}_{H,QC}(\mathcal{N})\rfloor\leq\mathcal{R}^{1,\epsilon}_{d,QC}(\mathcal{N})\leq D^{\epsilon}_{H,QC}(\mathcal{N}),

(49)

\displaystyle LR_{QC}^{\epsilon}(\mathcal{N})\leq\mathcal{R}^{1,\epsilon}_{c,QC}(\mathcal{N})\leq\lceil LR_{QC}^{\epsilon}(\mathcal{N})\rceil.

We have the asymptotic distillation rate with the help of tools from Ref.
Wang and Wilde 2019
.

Result 6
.

The asymptotic parallel distillation rate of channel quantum capacity under RNG superchannels is

\displaystyle R^{\infty}_{QC,P}(\mathcal{N})=S^{\infty}_{QC}(\mathcal{N}).

(50)

Whether the dilution rate converges to
S^{\infty}_{QC}(\mathcal{N})
is an open question.

IV.2
State induced channel resources

Meanwhile, for any state resource theory, it also induces channel resources. For example, focusing on the capability of generating state resources, the set of resource non-generating operations for states (or their subsets) could be regarded as the set of free channel resource. We consider several examples related to state resource theories of non-uniformity, coherence, and entanglement.

non-uniformity

Considering the state resource theory of non-uniformity

We regard the pure qubit state generation channel

V
Summary

In this work, we study one-shot distillation and dilution of general dynamical resource theories. For any unitary channel or any pure state preparation channel as the target resource, we give universal upper and lower bounds to the distillation and dilution rates for conversions between any channel and the target resource. The schemes become optimal for several example resources with specific targets. We expect our results to have wide applications in quantum information science from quantum communication to quantum computing.

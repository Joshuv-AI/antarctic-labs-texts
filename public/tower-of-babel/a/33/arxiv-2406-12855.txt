Moving frame and spin field representations of submanifolds in flat space
Shou-Jyun Zou

Abstract
We introduce a spin field approach, that is compatible with the Cartan moving frame method, to describe the submanifold in a flat space. In fact, we consider a kind of spin field ψ \psi , that satisfies a Killing spin field equation (analogous to a Killing spinor equation) written in terms of the Clifford algebra, and we use the spin field to locally rotate the orthonormal basis { e ^ 𝙸 } \{\hat{e}_{\mathtt{I}}\} . Then, the deformed orthonormal frame { ψ ~ ​ e ^ 𝙸 ​ ψ } \{\tilde{\psi}\hat{e}_{\mathtt{I}}\psi\} can be seen as the moving frame of a submanifold. We find some solutions to the Killing spin field equation and demonstrate an explicit example. Using the product of the spin fields, one can easily generate a new immersion submanifold, and this technique should be useful for studies in geometry and physics. Through the spin field, we find a linear relation between the connection and the extrinsic curvature of the submanifold. We propose a conjecture that any solution of the Killing spin field equation can be locally written as the product of the solutions we find.

2.1
Moving frame approach

The Cartan moving frame approach
[
4
]
describes the submanifold
\mathcal{M}
and its tangent space by using the system
({\boldsymbol{q}}(x),\{{\mathbf{e}}_{\boldsymbol{I}}(x)\})
, where
{\boldsymbol{q}}(x)
is the position vector
\vec{oq}
of the point
q\in\mathcal{M}\subset\mathbb{R}^{1,9}
(where
o\in\mathbb{R}^{1,9}
is the origin point of the ambient space), and
\{{\mathbf{e}}_{\boldsymbol{I}}(x)\}
is the orthonormal moving frame on the position
q
and

is the local coordinate on the submanifold.
These vectors can be written in terms of the fixed frame, e.g.
{\boldsymbol{q}}(x)=q^{{\mathtt{0}}}(x)\hat{e}_{{\mathtt{0}}}+q^{{\mathtt{1}}}(x)\hat{e}_{{\mathtt{1}}}+\dots+q^{{\mathtt{9}}}(x)\hat{e}_{\mathtt{9}}
which describes the local embedding map of
{\boldsymbol{q}}:\mathcal{M}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{1,9}
.
The following differential equation system describes the behaviour of the moving frame:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccc}d{\boldsymbol{q}}(x)&=&\vartheta^{\boldsymbol{I}}(x){\mathbf{e}}_{\boldsymbol{I}}(x)\\
d{\mathbf{e}}_{\boldsymbol{I}}(x)&=&\omega^{\;\;{\boldsymbol{J}}}_{{\boldsymbol{I}}}(x){\mathbf{e}}_{{\boldsymbol{J}}}(x)\end{array}\right.

(1)

where
\vartheta^{\boldsymbol{I}}(x)=\vartheta_{{\underline{\mu}}}^{\;\;{\boldsymbol{I}}}(x)dx^{{\underline{\mu}}}
is the vielbein 1-form field, and
\omega_{\boldsymbol{I}}^{\;\;{\boldsymbol{J}}}(x)=\omega_{{\underline{\mu}}{\boldsymbol{I}}}^{\;\;\;\;{\boldsymbol{J}}}(x)dx^{{\underline{\mu}}}
is the connection 1-form on the submanifold
\mathcal{M}^{4}
, and here we use the Einstein summation convention for the repeated indices.
In fact, the index
{\boldsymbol{I}}
of the vielbein should only has the range
0\sim 3
, because the tangent space is spanned by
{\mathbf{e}}_{{\boldsymbol{0}}},\dots,{\mathbf{e}}_{{\boldsymbol{3}}}
.

If
g_{{\underline{\mu}}{\underline{\nu}}}
is the metric of the local coordinate of submanifold, and
{\boldsymbol{q}}
satisfies

From the first order differential equation (
1

{\boldsymbol{q}}(x)
is deteremated by
\vartheta^{\boldsymbol{I}}(x)

\omega^{\;\;{\boldsymbol{J}}}_{{\boldsymbol{I}}}(x)

And, the exact condition

(d\vartheta^{{\boldsymbol{I}}}){\mathbf{e}}_{\boldsymbol{I}}-\vartheta^{{\boldsymbol{K}}}\omega_{{\boldsymbol{K}}}^{\;\;{\boldsymbol{J}}}{\mathbf{e}}_{\boldsymbol{J}}=0

\omega^{\;\;{\boldsymbol{J}}}_{{\boldsymbol{I}}}(x)

Because the moving frame basis
{\mathbf{e}}_{\boldsymbol{I}}
are orthonormal
{\mathbf{e}}_{\boldsymbol{I}}\cdot{\mathbf{e}}_{\boldsymbol{J}}=\eta_{{\boldsymbol{I}}{\boldsymbol{J}}}
, so the connection 1-forms
\omega_{{\boldsymbol{I}}{\boldsymbol{J}}}
are antisymmetry:

\displaystyle 0\;=\;d\left({\mathbf{e}}_{\boldsymbol{I}}\cdot{\mathbf{e}}_{\boldsymbol{J}}\right)\;=\;

\displaystyle d{\mathbf{e}}_{\boldsymbol{I}}\cdot{\mathbf{e}}_{\boldsymbol{J}}+{\mathbf{e}}_{\boldsymbol{I}}\cdot d{\mathbf{e}}_{\boldsymbol{J}}

\displaystyle\;=\;

\displaystyle\omega^{{\boldsymbol{K}}}_{\;\;{\boldsymbol{I}}}{\mathbf{e}}_{\boldsymbol{K}}\cdot{\mathbf{e}}_{\boldsymbol{J}}+{\mathbf{e}}_{\boldsymbol{I}}\cdot\omega^{{\boldsymbol{K}}}_{\;\;{\boldsymbol{J}}}{\mathbf{e}}_{\boldsymbol{K}}

\displaystyle\;=\;

\displaystyle\omega^{{\boldsymbol{K}}}_{\;\;{\boldsymbol{I}}}\eta_{{\boldsymbol{K}}{\boldsymbol{J}}}+\omega^{{\boldsymbol{K}}}_{\;\;{\boldsymbol{J}}}\eta_{{\boldsymbol{I}}{\boldsymbol{K}}}

i.e.

\omega_{{\boldsymbol{I}}{\boldsymbol{J}}}\;=\;-\omega_{{\boldsymbol{J}}{\boldsymbol{I}}}

(2)

According to the equation

D{\mathbf{e}}_{\boldsymbol{I}}\;:=\;d{\mathbf{e}}_{\boldsymbol{I}}-\omega_{{\boldsymbol{I}}}^{\;\;{\boldsymbol{J}}}{\mathbf{e}}_{\boldsymbol{J}}\;=\;0

(3)

So,

We can define the curvature 2-form associated to this covariant derivative

We can seperate the indices into the tangent and normal subspace of the submanifold

Here, we assume that a spin field

4
Discussion

This paper introduces a kind of spin field
\psi
, that satisfies a Killing spin field equation.
And we check that the local rotated basis
\tilde{\psi}{\hat{e}}_{\mathtt{I}}\psi
can be seen as the moving frame of a submanifold.
With the Janet-Cartan theorem, in 10-dimensional flat space this approach cam locally describe all 4-dimensional (pseudo-)Riemannian manifolds.
Through the spin field, we can see a linear relation between the connection and the extrinsic curvature of the submanifold.

At the beginning, our aim is to develop an approach using the Clifford algebra or spin field to calculate the non-linear problems in general relativity.
According to this paper, we indeed can use the spin fields to describe the geometry of space-time, but now we lack the associated physical rules to pick the appropriate spin fields.
We hope this aim can be achieved in future studies.

On the other hand, some people use the Clifford algebra to construct the unified field models
[
9
,
10
,
11
]
.
3
3

3

In fact, our approach is different to theirs.
They use the Clifford algebra to represent the algebraic structure of all bosons and fermions.
But, we only use the even grade Clifford algebras to describe the fermions, and the (boson) gauge fields are not the directly represented in our Clifford algebra.
However, the spin fiedl satisfies the Killing spin field equation
\partial\psi=\dots+A\psi+\dots
that implies the gauge field in fact related with the Clifford algebra.
We would like to point out that geometrizing the elementary particle with their results is not straightforward, but it should be worth a try.

The reasons are :
(1) the spinor can always be presented as the minimal left ideal of the spin group (which belongs to the even grade Clifford algebra);
(2) the Lie algebra in the Standard Particle Model can be embedded in certain Clifford algebras;
(3) the Clifford algebras are the more natural geometric objects than the unknown algebraic combinations.
And this paper provides another intuitive geometric interpretation that if a spinor of the elementary particle could be presented as the even grade Clifford algebras, then it should have a corresponding space-time geometry, i.e. the elementary particles could be interpreted as the nontrivial 4-dim manifolds.
We think the purely geometrical formulation of unified field theory is much closer to the idea of Einstein’s unified field theory.

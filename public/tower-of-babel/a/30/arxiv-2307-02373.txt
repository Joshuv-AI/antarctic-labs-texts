Maker-Breaker Strong Resolving Game
Cong X. Kang, Aleksander Kelenc, Eunjeong Yi, Texas A&M University at Galveston, Galveston, TX 77553, USA, University of Maribor, FEECS Koroška cesta 46, 2000 Maribor, Slovenia, kangc@tamug.edu ; aleksander.kelenc@um.si ; yie@tamug.edu

Abstract
Let G G be a graph with vertex set V V . A set S ⊆ V S\subseteq V is a strong resolving set of G G if, for distinct x , y ∈ V x,y\in V , there exists z ∈ S z\in S such that either x x lies on a y − z y-z geodesic or y y lies on an x − z x-z geodesic in G G . In this paper, we study maker-breaker strong resolving game (MBSRG) played on a graph by two players, Maker and Breaker, where the two players alternately select a vertex of G G not yet chosen. Maker wins if he is able to choose vertices that form a strong resolving set of G G and Breaker wins if she is able to prevent Maker from winning in the course of MBSRG. We denote by O SR ​ ( G ) O_{\rm SR}(G) the outcome of MBSRG played on G G . We obtain some general results on MBSRG and examine the relation between O SR ​ ( G ) O_{\rm SR}(G) and O R ​ ( G ) O_{\rm R}(G) , where O R ​ ( G ) O_{\rm R}(G) denotes the outcome of the maker-breaker resolving game of G G . We determine the outcome of MBSRG played on some graph classes, including corona product graphs, Cartesian product graphs, and modular product graphs.



Keywords:
resolving set, strong resolving set, Maker-Breaker strong resolving game, mutually maximally distant vertices, vertex cover, corona product, Cartesian product, modular product

We recall the notion of a (strong) resolving set and the (strong) metric dimension of a graph. Let

Now, following
[
12
]
, MBRG is played on a graph

by two players, Maker and Breaker. Maker and Breaker alternately select (without missing their turn) a vertex of

In a network

if Breaker has a winning strategy for both the M-game and the B-game; (iii)

if the first player has a winning strategy. In
[
12
,
14
]
, the authors studied the minimum number of moves/steps/time for Maker (Breaker, respectively) to win the game provided Maker (Breaker, respectively) has a winning strategy in MBRG or MB
k

In this section, we obtain some general results on MBSRG and examine the relationship between

and
O_{\rm R}(G)

Theorem 2.1
.

[
19
]

For any connected graph

\textnormal{sdim}(G)=\tau(G_{\rm SR})
.

Theorem 2.2
.

[
24
]

Let
G
be a connected graph of order

. Then

, and we have the following:

(a)

\textnormal{sdim}(G)=1
if and only if
G=P_{n}
,

(b)

\textnormal{sdim}(G)=n-1
if and only if
G=K_{n}
,

(c)

for

\textnormal{sdim}(G)=n-2
if and only if
\textnormal{diam}(G)=2
and
\overline{\omega}(G)=2
.

It is known that metric dimension is not a monotone parameter on subgraph inclusion (see
[
5
,
7
]
). We show that strong metric dimension is not a monotone parameter with respect to subgraph inclusion, but it is monotone with respect to strong resolving graphs. For analogous results on the fractional strong metric dimension of graphs, we refer to
[

]
.

Proposition 2.3
.

(a)

The ratio
\frac{\textnormal{sdim}(H)}{\textnormal{sdim}(G)}
is arbitrarily large for suitable choice of graphs
H\subset G
.

(b)

If
H_{\rm SR}\subseteq G_{\rm SR}
, then
\textnormal{sdim}(H)\leq\textnormal{sdim}(G)
.

Proof.

(a) We denote by

the Cartesian product of two graphs
X
and
Y
. Let

and
G=P_{m}\square K_{2}
, where

. Then

,

and

. So,

\textnormal{sdim}(G)=2

\frac{\textnormal{sdim}(H)}{\textnormal{sdim}(G)}=\frac{m}{2}\rightarrow\infty

(b) Let

. Since an edge in

Now, we obtain some general results on the outcome of MBSRG, which will be used in determining

in Section

denote the set of positive integers. Let

Theorem 2.6
.

Let
G
be a connected graph.

(a)

Suppose
G
admits a pairing strong resolving set (equivalently, if
G_{\rm SR}
admits a pairing vertex cover), then
O_{\rm SR}(G)=\mathcal{M}
.

(b)

Suppose
G
admits a quasi-pairing strong resolving set (equivalently, if
G_{\rm SR}
admits a quasi-pairing vertex cover), then

.

(c)

Suppose

, then
O_{\rm SR}(G)\in\{\mathcal{N},\mathcal{B}\}
.

(d)

Suppose
\Delta(G_{\rm SR}[u^{c}])\geq 2
for every

; then
O_{\rm SR}(G)=\mathcal{B}
.

Proof.

Parts (a) and (b) follow immediately from the definitions of a pairing strong resolving set and a quasi-pairing strong resolving set, respectively. For part (c), suppose

contains a vertex

of degree at least two. In the
B

. The following is a proof of part (d). Breaker, playing second, picks as her first move a vertex

is the first move of Maker. Breaker can pick as her second move a neighbor of
v
in
G_{\rm SR}
regardless of the two moves made by Maker, thus denying Maker a vertex cover of
G_{\rm SR}
. ∎

be an
n
-path,

be a tree with

and
\sigma(G_{2})=3
, and

(G_{2})_{\rm SR}=K_{3}

. ∎

Next, we examine the relationship between
O_{\rm SR}(G)
and
O_{\rm R}(G)
. We begin with the following useful observation.

Observation 2.9
.

[
21
]

For any connected graph
G
, a strong resolving set of
G
is a resolving set of
G
.

To facilitate statements, let us identify
\mathcal{B},\mathcal{N},\mathcal{M}
with
-1,0,1
respectively. Observation

, along with the rules of MBRG and MBSRG, implies
O_{\rm SR}(G)\leq O_{\rm R}(G)
. We thus have the following result.

Corollary 2.10
.

The set
\{(\mathcal{B},\mathcal{B}),(\mathcal{B},\mathcal{N}),(\mathcal{B},\mathcal{M}),(\mathcal{N},\mathcal{N}),(\mathcal{N},\mathcal{M}),(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{M})\}
is a codomain of the ordered pair
(O_{\rm SR}(G),O_{\rm R}(G))
, viewed as a function on the space of graphs.

We next show that the codomain identified in Corollary
2.10
is in fact the range of
(O_{\rm SR}(G),O_{\rm R}(G))
.

Proposition 2.11
.

(a)

There exist graphs
G
satisfying
O_{\rm SR}(G)=\mathcal{M}=O_{\rm R}(G)
.

(b)

There exist graphs
G
satisfying
O_{\rm SR}(G)=\mathcal{N}=O_{\rm R}(G)
.

(c)

There exist graphs
G
satisfying
O_{\rm SR}(G)=\mathcal{B}=O_{\rm R}(G)
.

(d)

There exist graphs
G
satisfying
O_{\rm SR}(G)=\mathcal{N}
and
O_{\rm R}(G)=\mathcal{M}
.

(e)

There exist graphs
G
satisfying
O_{\rm SR}(G)=\mathcal{B}
and
O_{\rm R}(G)=\mathcal{M}
.

(f)

There exist graphs
G
satisfying
O_{\rm SR}(G)=\mathcal{B}
and
O_{\rm R}(G)=\mathcal{N}
.

Proof.

(a) Let
G
be an
n
-path given by
u_{1},u_{2},\ldots,u_{n}
, where
n\geq 2
. Then
G_{\rm SR}=K_{2}
and
\{\{u_{1},u_{n}\}\}
is a pairing (strong) resolving set of
G
. Thus,
O_{\rm SR}(G)=\mathcal{M}=O_{\rm R}(G)
by Corollary
2.7
(a) and Proposition
2.5
(a).

(b) Let
G\in\{K_{3},K_{1,3}\}
. Then
G_{\rm SR}=C_{3}
and
G
has exactly one twin equivalence class of cardinality
3
with
\dim(G)=2
. So,
O_{\rm SR}(G)=\mathcal{N}=O_{\rm R}(G)
by Corollary
2.7
(b) and Proposition
2.5
(c).

(c) Let
G
be the star
K_{1,x}
on (
x+1
) vertices, where
x\geq 4
. Then
G_{\rm SR}=K_{x}
and the leaves of
G
form a twin equivalence class of cardinality
x
, where
x\geq 4
. So,
O_{\rm SR}(G)=\mathcal{B}=O_{\rm R}(G)
by Corollary
2.7
(c) and Proposition
2.5
(d).

(d) Let
G
be a tree with
ex(G)=1
and
\sigma(G)=3
such that
\ell_{1},\ell_{2},\ell_{3}
are the terminal vertices of the exterior major vertex
v
in
G
with
d(v,\ell_{1})\geq 2
,
d(v,\ell_{2})\geq 2
and
d(v,\ell_{3})\geq 1
. For each
i\in[2]
, let
s_{i}
be the support vertex lying on the
v-\ell_{i}
path. Then
G_{\rm SR}=C_{3}
and
\{\{s_{1},\ell_{1}\},\{s_{2},\ell_{2}\}\}
is a pairing resolving set of
G
. So,
O_{\rm SR}(G)=\mathcal{N}
by Corollary
2.7
(b) and
O_{\rm R}(G)=\mathcal{M}
by Proposition
2.5
(a).

(e) Let
G
be a tree with
ex(G)=1
and
\sigma(G)=x\geq 4
. Let
v
be the exterior major vertex and
\ell_{1},\ell_{2},\ldots,\ell_{x}
be the terminal vertices of
v
in
G

and
d(v,\ell_{i})\geq 2
for each

. For each

, let
s_{i}
be the support vertex lying on the
v-\ell_{i}
path. Since
G_{\rm SR}=K_{x}\supseteq K_{4}

O_{\rm SR}(G)=\mathcal{B}
by Corollary
2.7
(c). Since
\cup_{i=1}^{x-1}\{\{s_{i},\ell_{i}\}\}
is a pairing resolving set of
G

O_{\rm R}(G)=\mathcal{M}
by Proposition
2.5
(a).

(f) Let
G
be a tree with
ex(G)=1
and
\sigma(G)=x\geq 4
. Let
v
be the exterior major vertex and
\ell_{1},\ell_{2},\ldots,\ell_{x}
be the terminal vertices of
v
in
G
such that
d(v,\ell_{x-2})=d(v,\ell_{x-1})=d(v,\ell_{x})=1
and
d(v,\ell_{i})\geq 2
for each
i\in[x-3]
. For each
i\in[x-3]

s_{i}
be the support vertex lying on the

O_{\rm SR}(G)=\mathcal{B}
by Corollary
2.7
(c). Next, we show that
O_{\rm R}(G)=\mathcal{N}
. Note that, for any resolving set
W
of

|W\cap\{\ell_{x-2},\ell_{x-1},\ell_{x}\}|\geq 2
by Observation
2.4
(a). Let
X=(\cup_{i=1}^{x-3}\{\{s_{i},\ell_{i}\}\})\cup\{\{\ell_{x-2},\ell_{x-1}\}\}

with

. So,

by Proposition

(b). In the B-game, Breaker can select two vertices of
\{\ell_{x-2},\ell_{x-1},\ell_{x}\}
, and thus preventing Maker from occupying vertices that form a resolving set of

. Thus,
O_{\rm R}(G)=\mathcal{N}
. ∎

3
Some graph classes

In this section, we examine the outcome of MBSRG played on some graph classes such as trees, cycles, the Petersen graph, complete multipartite graphs, corona product graphs, Cartesian product graphs, and modular product graphs.

3.1
Trees, Cycles, Petersen Graph, Complete Multipartite Graphs

We begin with the following observation for MBSRG that is analogous to the result obtained in
[
12
]
for MBRG. We provide its proof to be self-contained.

Observation 3.1
.

Let
G
be a connected graph of order
n\geq 2
.

(a)

If
O_{\rm SR}(G)=\mathcal{M}
, then
\textnormal{sdim}(G)\leq\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor
.

(b)

If
\textnormal{sdim}(G)\geq\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil+1
, then
O_{\rm SR}(G)=\mathcal{B}
.

Proof.

Let
G
be a connected graph of order
n\geq 2
.

(a) Suppose
O_{\rm SR}(G)=\mathcal{M}
. In the B-game, Maker can select at most
\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor
vertices in the course of MBSRG. Since Maker must occupy at least
\textnormal{sdim}(G)
vertices, it follows that
\textnormal{sdim}(G)\leq\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor
.

(b) Suppose
\textnormal{sdim}(G)\geq\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil+1
. Since Maker can occupy at most
\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil
vertices in the course of MBSRG, Maker fails to occupy vertices that form a strong resolving set of
G
. Thus,
O_{\rm SR}(G)=\mathcal{B}
. ∎

Next, we recall the strong metric dimension of trees, cycles, the Petersen graph and complete multi-partite graphs.

Proposition 3.2
.

(a)

[
21
]
For any tree
T
of order at least two,
\textnormal{sdim}(T)=\sigma(T)-1
.

(b)

[
21
]
For
n\geq 3
,
\textnormal{sdim}(C_{n})=\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil
.

(c)

[
25
]
For the Petersen graph
\mathcal{P}
,
\textnormal{sdim}(\mathcal{P})=8
.

(d)

[
25
]
For
k\geq 2
, let
G=K_{a_{1},\ldots,a_{k}}
be a complete
k
-partite graph of order

. Let
s
be the number of partite sets of

consisting of exactly one element. Then

\textnormal{sdim}(G)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}n-k&\mbox{ if }s=0,\\
n-k+s-1&\mbox{ if }s\neq 0.\end{array}\right.

Next, we determine

when

is a tree, a cycle, the Petersen graph and a complete multipartite graph, respectively.

Proposition 3.3
.

(a)

For any tree

\mathcal{N}&\mbox{ if $\sigma(T)=3$},\\

(d)

, let

(a) Let

2.7

(b) Let

(c).

(c) For the Petersen graph

, we have

(d) Let

Second, suppose

(b). If

Third, suppose

3.2
Corona Product Graphs

Let
G
and
H
be two graphs of order
n
and
m
, respectively, and let
V(G)=\{u_{1},u_{2},\dots,u_{n}\}
. The
corona product

G\odot H
is obtained from
G
and
n
copies of
H
, say
H_{1},H_{2},\ldots,H_{n}
, by adding an edge from
u_{i}
to every vertex of
H_{i}
for each
i\in[n]
. The
join
of two graphs
G
and
H
, denoted by
G+H
, is the graph obtained from the disjoint union of
G
and
H
by joining an edge between each vertex of
G
and each vertex of
H
. We note that
K_{1}\odot H=K_{1}+H
. For the strong metric dimension of corona product graphs, see
[
17
]
; for the fractional strong metric dimension of corona product graphs, see
[
13
]
. We first consider
O_{\rm SR}(G\odot H)
when
G
is a connected graph of order at least two.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
G
be a connected graph of order
n\geq 2
, and let
H
be a graph of order
m\geq 1
.

(a)

If
n\geq 4
, then
O_{\rm SR}(G\odot H)=\mathcal{B}
.

(b)

If
n=3
, then
O_{\rm SR}(G\odot H)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\mathcal{N}&\mbox{ if }m=1,\\
\mathcal{B}&\mbox{ if }m\geq 2.\end{array}\right.

(c)

If
n=2
, then
O_{\rm SR}(G\odot H)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\mathcal{M}&\mbox{ if }m=1,\\
\mathcal{B}&\mbox{ if }m\geq 2.\end{array}\right.

Proof.

Let
G
be a connected graph of order
n\geq 2
and
H
be a graph of order
m\geq 1
. Let
V(G)=\{u_{1},u_{2},\ldots,u_{n}\}
and let
H_{1},\ldots,H_{n}
be copies of
H
. For each
i\in[n]
, let
V(H_{i})=\{w_{i,1},\ldots,w_{i,m}\}
, where the vertex
w_{s,x}
in
H_{s}
corresponds to the vertex
w_{t,x}
in
H_{t}
for distinct
s,t\in[n]
and
x\in[m]
. Since
n\geq 2
, we have
V(G)\cap V((G\odot H)_{\rm SR})=\emptyset
.

(a) Let
n\geq 4
. For distinct
i,j\in[n]
, we have
w_{i,1}
MMD
w_{j,1}
in
G\odot H
. So,
(G\odot H)_{\rm SR}\supseteq K_{4}
and
O_{\rm SR}(G\odot H)=\mathcal{B}
by Corollary
2.7
(c).

(b) Let
n=3
. If
m=1
, then
(G\odot H)_{\rm SR}=C_{3}
and

by Corollary
2.7
(b). If

, we have
w_{i,1}
MMD

and
w_{i,2}
MMD
w_{j,2}
in
G\odot H
for any distinct
i,j\in[3]
; thus,
(G\odot H)_{\rm SR}\supseteq 2C_{3}
and
O_{\rm SR}(G\odot H)=\mathcal{B}
by Corollary
2.7
(c).

(c) Let
n=2
. If
m=1
, then
(G\odot H)_{\rm SR}=K_{2}
and
O_{\rm SR}(G\odot H)=\mathcal{M}
by Corollary
2.7
(a). So, suppose

. Since
\textnormal{diam}(G\odot H)=3=d_{G\odot H}(w_{i,x},w_{j,z})
for

and

, we have

. By Corollary
2.7
(c),
O_{\rm SR}(G\odot H)=\mathcal{B}
. ∎

Next, we consider

.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let

and
b\geq 0
, or
a=0

.

Let
G_{\rm SR}=K_{1}+(aK_{2}\cup bK_{1})
, where

and

, or
a=0
and

. If
G_{\rm SR}=K_{1}+K_{2}=C_{3}
(when

and
b=0
) or
G_{\rm SR}=K_{1}+2K_{1}=P_{3}
(when

and

), then

by Corollary
2.7
(b). So, suppose
G_{\rm SR}\not\in\{C_{3},P_{3}\}
. Since

,
O_{\rm SR}(G)\in\{\mathcal{N},\mathcal{B}\}

2.6
(c). To prove

, we need to show that Maker wins the M-game of MBSRG. Let

be the vertex in the

with

. If

, let

such that

.

(c).

(c) Let

for each

; thus,

3.3
Cartesian Product Graphs

The
Cartesian product
of two graphs
G
and
H
, denoted by
G\square H
, is the graph with the vertex set
V(G)\times V(H)
such that
(u,w)
is adjacent to
(u^{\prime},w^{\prime})
if and only if either
u=u^{\prime}
and
ww^{\prime}\in E(H)
, or
w=w^{\prime}
and
uu^{\prime}\in E(G)
. The
direct product
(also known as the
tensor product
) of two graphs
G
and
H
, denoted by
G\times H
, is the graph with the vertex set
V(G)\times V(H)
such that
(u,w)
is adjacent to
(u^{\prime},w^{\prime})
if and only if
uu^{\prime}\in E(G)
and
ww^{\prime}\in E(H)
. For the strong metric dimension of Cartesian product graphs, see
[
20
]
; for the fractional strong metric dimension of Cartesian product graphs, see
[
13
]
. We first recall the following result connecting Cartesian product and direct product.

Theorem 3.10
.

[
20
]

If
G
and
H
are two connected graphs of order at least two, then
(G\square H)_{\rm SR}=G_{\rm SR}\times H_{\rm SR}
.

Next, for connected graphs
G
and
H
of order at least two, we show that
O_{\rm SR}(G\square H)\in\{\mathcal{M},\mathcal{B}\}
and we characterize Cartesian product graphs satisfying
O_{\rm SR}(G\square H)=\mathcal{M}
. We begin with the following lemma that is used in proving our main result on the outcome of MBSRG played on Cartesian product graphs.

Lemma 3.11
.

Let
G
and
H
be two connected graphs of order at least two. If
\Delta(G_{\rm SR})\geq 2
or
\Delta(H_{\rm SR})\geq 2
, then
O_{\rm SR}(G\square H)=\mathcal{B}
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, suppose
\Delta(G_{\rm SR})\geq 2
. By Theorem
3.10
,
(G\square H)_{\rm SR}=G_{\rm SR}\times H_{\rm SR}\supseteq P_{3}\times K_{2}\supseteq 2P_{3}
. Thus,
O_{\rm SR}(G\square H)=\mathcal{B}
by Corollary
2.7
(c). ∎

Theorem 3.12
.

Let
G
and
H
be two connected graphs of order at least two. Then
O_{\rm SR}(G\square H)\in\{\mathcal{M},\mathcal{B}\}
. Moreover,

O_{\rm SR}(G\square H)=\mathcal{M}
if and only if
G_{\rm SR}=aK_{2}
and
H_{\rm SR}=bK_{2}
, where
a\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
and
b\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
.

Proof.

First, suppose
\Delta(G_{\rm SR})\geq 2
or
\Delta(H_{\rm SR})\geq 2
. Then
O_{\rm SR}(G\square H)=\mathcal{B}
by Lemma
3.11
.

Second, suppose
\Delta(G_{\rm SR})=1=\Delta(H_{\rm SR})
; then
G_{\rm SR}=aK_{2}
and
H_{\rm SR}=bK_{2}
, where
a,b\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
. In this case,
(G\square H)_{\rm SR}=G_{\rm SR}\times H_{\rm SR}=aK_{2}\times bK_{2}=(2ab)K_{2}
, and thus
O_{\rm SR}(G\square H)=\mathcal{M}
by Corollary
2.7
(a). ∎

We note that
(K_{n})_{\rm SR}=K_{n}
for
n\geq 2
,
(C_{2m-1})_{\rm SR}=C_{2m-1}
and
(C_{2m})_{\rm SR}=mK_{2}
for
m\geq 2
, and
(T_{\rm SR})=K_{\sigma}
for any tree
T
with
\sigma
leaves. So, Lemma
3.11
and Theorem
3.12
imply the following.

Corollary 3.13
.

Let
G
be a connected graph of order at least two.

(a)

For
n\geq 3
,
O_{\rm SR}(G\square K_{n})=\mathcal{B}
.

(b)

For
n\geq 3
,
O_{\rm SR}(G\square C_{n})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\mathcal{M}&\mbox{ if $n$ is even and $\Delta(G_{\rm SR})=1$},\\
\mathcal{B}&\mbox{ if $n$ is odd or $\Delta(G_{\rm SR})\geq 2$}.\end{array}\right.

(c)

For any tree
T
that is not a path,
O_{\rm SR}(G\square T)=\mathcal{B}
.

3.4
Modular Product Graphs

The
modular product
of two graphs
G
and
H
, denoted by
G\diamond H
, is the graph with vertex set
V(G\diamond H)=V(G)\times V(H)
and edge set
E(G\diamond H)=E(G\Box H)\cup E(G\times H)\cup E(\overline{G}\times\overline{H})
, where
\overline{X}
denotes the complement of a graph
X
. The
lexicographic product
of two graphs
G
and
H
, denoted by
G\circ H
, is the graph with the vertex set
V(G)\times V(H)
such that
(u,w)
is adjacent to
(u^{\prime},w^{\prime})
if and only if either
uu^{\prime}\in E(G)
, or
u=u^{\prime}
and
ww^{\prime}\in E(H)
. It was shown in
[
11
]
that, if (i) neither
G
nor
H
is a complete graph and (ii)
G
and
H
are not both disjoint unions of two cliques, then
\textnormal{diam}(G\diamond H)\leq 3
.

First, we consider
O_{\rm SR}(G\diamond K_{t})
for
t\geq 2
. It was observed in
[
11
]
that
G\diamond K_{t}=G\circ K_{t}
.

Proposition 3.14
.

If
G
is a connected graph of order at least two and
t\geq 2
, then
O_{\rm SR}(G\diamond K_{t})=\mathcal{B}
.

Proof.

Suppose
d_{G}(u,w)=\textnormal{diam}(G)
. Then
u\ {\rm MMD}\ w
in
G
. Let
{}^{u}K_{t}
and
{}^{w}K_{t}
be the copies of
K_{t}
corresponding to the vertices
u
and
w
in
G\circ K_{t}
, respectively. Then, for
x\in V(^{u}K_{t})
and
y\in V(^{w}K_{t})
, we have
x\ {\rm MMD}\ y
in
G\circ K_{t}=G\diamond K_{t}
. So,
(G\diamond K_{t})_{\rm SR}\supseteq K_{t,t}\supseteq C_{4}
since
t\geq 2
. Thus,
O_{\rm SR}(G\diamond K_{t})=\mathcal{B}
by Corollary
2.7
(c). ∎

Second, we consider
O_{\rm SR}(G\diamond H)
when
\textnormal{diam}(G\diamond H)\leq 2
. Let
{\rm TW}(G)
denote the set of all twin edges of
G
, where a twin edge is an edge between adjacent twins. We recall the following result on modular product graphs with diameter
2
.

Theorem 3.15
.

[
11
]

If
\textnormal{diam}(G\diamond H)=2
, then

E((G\diamond H)_{\rm SR})={\rm TW}(G\diamond H)\cup E(\overline{G}\Box\overline{H})\cup E(G\times\overline{H})\cup E(\overline{G}\times H).

.

If
G
and
H
are graphs of order at least two with

O_{\rm SR}(G\diamond H)=\mathcal{B}
.

Proof.

Suppose
G
and

are graphs of order
n
and

. Since
\textnormal{diam}(G\diamond H)=1
,

. So,

. By Corollary
2.7
(c),

. ∎

Proposition 3.17
.

Let
G
and
H
be connected graphs of order at least two such that neither
G
nor
H
is a complete graph. If
\textnormal{diam}(G\diamond H)=2
, then
O_{\rm SR}(G\diamond H)=\mathcal{B}
.

Since neither
G
nor

Next, we consider
O_{\rm SR}(G\diamond H)
when

of

\gamma_{G}
-pair graph
of

\mathbb{GP}(G)

V(\mathbb{GP}(G))=V(G)
and

if and only if
\{u,v\}
is a
\gamma_{G}
-pair. A vertex
u\in V(G)
is called a
boundary vertex
of
G
if there is a vertex
v\in V(G)
such that
d_{G}(u,v)=\textnormal{diam}(G)
. We recall the following important results in modular product graphs.

.

[
11
]

G
nor

(2)

.

11

.

[

Next, we consider MBSRG played on modular product graphs with

G
and
H

nor
H

\textnormal{diam}(G\diamond H)=3
.

(a)

If
\{g,g^{\prime}\}
is a
\gamma_{G}
-pair and
\{h,h^{\prime}\}
is a
\gamma_{H}
-pair, then
O_{\rm SR}(G\diamond H)=\mathcal{B}
.

(b)

If
G
has at least two distinct universal vertices and
\textnormal{diam}(H)\geq 3
, then
O_{\rm SR}(G\diamond H)=\mathcal{B}
.

(c)

Suppose there’s no
\gamma_{G}
-pair and no universal vertex in
G
, but
G
has a pair of distinct adjacent twins and
H
has a
\gamma_{H}
-pair. Then
O_{\rm SR}(G\diamond H)=\mathcal{B}
.

(d)

Suppose there’s no
\gamma_{G}
-pair and no universal vertex in
G
, and
H
has a
\gamma_{H}
-pair and at least two distinct vertices in
H
belong to no
\gamma_{H}
-pair. Then
O_{\rm SR}(G\diamond H)=\mathcal{B}
.

(e)

Suppose there’s no
\gamma_{G}
-pair and no universal vertex in
G
, and
H
has distinct adjacent twins
h_{1}
and
h_{2}
such that both
\{h_{1},h\}
and
\{h_{2},h\}
are
\gamma_{H}
-pairs for some
h\in V(H)-\{h_{1},h_{2}\}
. Then
O_{\rm SR}(G\diamond H)=\mathcal{B}
.

Proof.

Let
G
and
H
be graphs described in the statement with
\textnormal{diam}(G\diamond H)=3
.

(a) Let
\{g,g^{\prime}\}
be a
\gamma_{G}
-pair and
\{h,h^{\prime}\}
be a
\gamma_{H}
-pair. By Theorem
3.19
(b),
(g,h)
and
(g^{\prime},h^{\prime})
are distinct adjacent twins in
G\diamond H
, and
(g,h^{\prime})
and
(g^{\prime},h)
are distinct adjacent twins in
G\diamond H
; thus,
(g,h)(g^{\prime},h^{\prime})\in E((G\diamond H)_{\rm SR})
and
(g,h^{\prime})(g^{\prime},h)\in E((G\diamond H)_{\rm SR})
by Theorem
3.20
. Also, by (
1
) of Theorem
3.18
, we have
d_{G\diamond H}((g,h),(g,h^{\prime}))=3
and
d_{G\diamond H}((g^{\prime},h),(g^{\prime},h^{\prime}))=3
; thus,
(g,h)(g,h^{\prime})\in E((G\diamond H)_{\rm SR})
and
(g^{\prime},h)(g^{\prime},h^{\prime})\in E((G\diamond H)_{\rm SR})
by Theorem
3.20
. Since
(G\diamond H)_{\rm SR}\supseteq C_{4}
,
O_{\rm SR}(G\diamond H)=\mathcal{B}
by Corollary
2.7
(c).

(b) Let
g
and
g^{\prime}
be distinct universal vertices in
G
and
d_{H}(h,h^{\prime})\geq 3
. From (
1
) of Theorem
3.18
, we have
d_{G\diamond H}((g,h),(g,h^{\prime}))=3
,
d_{G\diamond H}((g^{\prime},h),(g^{\prime},h^{\prime}))=3
,
d_{G\diamond H}((g,h),(g^{\prime},h^{\prime}))=3
and
d_{G\diamond H}((g^{\prime},h),(g,h^{\prime}))=3
. So,
(g,h)(g,h^{\prime})\in E((G\diamond H)_{\rm SR})
,
(g^{\prime},h)(g^{\prime},h^{\prime})\in E((G\diamond H)_{\rm SR})
,
(g,h)(g^{\prime},h^{\prime})\in E((G\diamond H)_{\rm SR})
and
(g^{\prime},h)(g,h^{\prime})\in E((G\diamond H)_{\rm SR})
by Theorem
3.20
. Since
(G\diamond H)_{\rm SR}\supseteq C_{4}
,
O_{\rm SR}(G\diamond H)=\mathcal{B}
by Corollary
2.7
(c).

(c) Let
g_{1}
and
g_{2}
be distinct adjacent twins in
G
; then
N_{G}[g_{1}]=N_{G}[g_{2}]
. Suppose
\{h_{1},h_{2}\}
is a
\gamma_{H}
-pair. From (
1
) of Theorem
3.18
, we have
d_{G\diamond H}((g_{1},h_{1}),(g_{1},h_{2}))=3
,
d_{G\diamond H}((g_{2},h_{1}),(g_{2},h_{2}))=3
,
d_{G\diamond H}((g_{1},h_{1}),(g_{2},h_{2}))=3
and
d_{G\diamond H}((g_{2},h_{1}),(g_{1},h_{2}))=3
. By Theorem
3.20
,
(g_{1},h_{1})(g_{1},h_{2})\in E((G\diamond H)_{\rm SR})
,
(g_{2},h_{1})(g_{2},h_{2})\in E((G\diamond H)_{\rm SR})
,
(g_{1},h_{1})(g_{2},h_{2})\in E((G\diamond H)_{\rm SR})
and
(g_{2},h_{1})(g_{1},h_{2})\in E((G\diamond H)_{\rm SR})
. Since
(G\diamond H)_{\rm SR}\supseteq C_{4}
, we have
O_{\rm SR}(G\diamond H)=\mathcal{B}
by Corollary
2.7
(c).

(d) Since
H
has a
\gamma_{H}
-pair, (1) of Theorem
3.18
implies
\textnormal{diam}(G\diamond H)=3
.

Let
g_{1}
and
g_{2}
be distinct vertices in
G
with
g_{1}g_{2}\notin E(G)
, and
h_{1}
and
h_{2}
be distinct vertices in
H
with
h_{1}\not\in\mathbb{P}(H)
and
h_{2}\not\in\mathbb{P}(H)
. Let
X=\{(g_{1},h_{1}),(g_{1},h_{2}),(g_{2},h_{1}),(g_{2},h_{2})\}
; we show that no vertex in
X
is a boundary vertex in
G\diamond H
. Since
G
has no universal vertex,
N_{G}[g]\neq V(G)
for each
g\in V(G)
. If
N_{H}[h]=V(H)
for some
h\in V(H)
, then
\textnormal{diam}(H)=2
since
H
is not a complete graph, but
\textnormal{diam}(H)=2
contradicts the hypothesis that
H
has a
\gamma_{H}
-pair. So,
H
has no universal vertex. Since neither
G
nor
H
has a universal vertex,
G
has no
\gamma_{G}
-pair,
h_{1}\not\in\mathbb{P}(H)
and
h_{2}\not\in\mathbb{P}(H)
, Theorem
3.18
implies that, for each
(g_{i},h_{j})\in X
and for each
(g^{\prime},h^{\prime})\in V(G\diamond H)
,
d_{G\diamond H}((g_{i},h_{j}),(g^{\prime},h^{\prime}))\neq 3
, where
i,j\in[2]
. So, no vertex in
X
is a boundary vertex.

Next, we consider pairs of vertices in
X
that are at distance two apart in
G\diamond H
. Since
g_{1}g_{2}\notin E(G)
,
d_{G\diamond H}((g_{1},h_{1}),(g_{2},h_{1}))=2
and
d_{G\diamond H}((g_{1},h_{2}),(g_{2},h_{2}))=2
. By Theorem
3.20
,
(g_{1},h_{1})(g_{2},h_{1})\in E((G\diamond H)_{\rm SR})
and
(g_{1},h_{2})(g_{2},h_{2})\in E((G\diamond H)_{\rm SR})
.

Now, we consider two cases depending on the adjacency relation between
h_{1}
and
h_{2}
in
H
. If
h_{1}h_{2}\in E(H)
, then
d_{G\diamond H}((g_{1},h_{1}),(g_{2},h_{2}))=2
and
d_{G\diamond H}((g_{1},h_{2}),(g_{2},h_{1}))=2
, since
g_{1}g_{2}\notin E(G)
; thus,
(g_{1},h_{1})(g_{2},h_{2})\in E((G\diamond H)_{\rm SR})
and
(g_{1},h_{2})(g_{2},h_{1})\in E((G\diamond H)_{\rm SR})
by Theorem
3.20
. If
h_{1}h_{2}\not\in E(H)
, then
d_{G\diamond H}((g_{1},h_{1}),(g_{1},h_{2}))=2
and
d_{G\diamond H}((g_{2},h_{1}),(g_{2},h_{2}))=2
; thus,
(g_{1},h_{1})(g_{1},h_{2})\in E((G\diamond H)_{\rm SR})
and
(g_{2},h_{1})(g_{2},h_{2})\in E((G\diamond H)_{\rm SR})
by Theorem
3.20
.

In each case,
(G\diamond H)_{\rm SR}\supseteq C_{4}
. Thus,
O_{\rm SR}(G\diamond H)=\mathcal{B}
by Corollary
2.7
(c).

(e) We show that
(G\diamond H)_{\rm SR}\supseteq 2C_{3}
. Let
h_{1}
and
h_{2}
be distinct adjacent twins in
H
, and both
\{h_{1},h\}
and
\{h_{2},h\}
be
\gamma_{H}
-pairs for some
h\in V(H)-\{h_{1},h_{2}\}
. Suppose
g_{1},g_{2}\in V(G)
with
g_{1}\neq g_{2}
. From (
1
) of Theorem
3.18
, we have
d_{G\diamond H}((g_{1},h),(g_{1},h_{1}))=3
,
d_{G\diamond H}((g_{1},h),(g_{1},h_{2}))=3
,
d_{G\diamond H}((g_{2},h),(g_{2},h_{1}))=3
and
d_{G\diamond H}((g_{2},h),(g_{2},h_{2}))=3
; thus, for each
i\in[2]
,
(g_{i},h)(g_{i},h_{1})\in E((G\diamond H)_{\rm SR})
and
(g_{i},h)(g_{i},h_{2})\in E((G\diamond H)_{\rm SR})
by Theorem
3.20
. Since
h_{1}
and
h_{2}
are distinct adjacent twins in
H
, for each
i\in[2]
,
(g_{i},h_{1})
and
(g_{i},h_{2})
are distinct adjacent twins in
G\diamond H
by Theorem
3.19
(b). By Theorem
3.20
,
(g_{i},h_{1})(g_{i},h_{2})\in E((G\diamond H)_{\rm SR})
for each
i\in[2]
. Since
(G\diamond H)_{\rm SR}\supseteq 2C_{3}
,
O_{\rm SR}(G\diamond H)=\mathcal{B}
by Corollary
2.7
(c). ∎

Next, we consider MBSRG played on modular product graphs with
O_{\rm SR}(G\diamond H)\neq\mathcal{B}
. Let
G^{-}=G-\mathbb{P}(G)
and
\overline{G}^{-}=\overline{G}-\mathbb{P}(G)
. We recall the following result on modular product of graphs where one factor has a
\gamma
-pair and the other factor has no universal vertex.

Theorem 3.22
.

[
11
]

For non-complete graphs
G
and
H
such that at least one of the two is not the disjoint union of two cliques, suppose
G\diamond H
has a
\gamma
-pair in one factor and no universal vertex in the other factor. Then

E((G\diamond H)_{\rm SR})={\rm TW}(G\diamond H)\cup E(\overline{G}^{-}\square\overline{H}^{-})\cup E(G^{-}\times\overline{H}^{-})\cup E(\overline{G}^{-}\times H^{-})\cup E(\mathbb{GP}(G)\Box\mathbb{GP}(H))

\cup\ E(G[V({\rm TW}(G))]\times\mathbb{GP}(H))\cup E(\mathbb{GP}(G)\times H[V({\rm TW}(H))]).

Theorem 3.23
.

Let
G
and
H
be connected graphs of order at least two such that neither
G
nor
H
is a complete graph. Suppose
\textnormal{diam}(G\diamond H)=3
, and neither
G
nor
H
has a pair of distinct adjacent twins. Further, suppose
G
has neither a universal vertex nor a
\gamma_{G}
-pair, and
H
has a
\gamma_{H}
-pair.

(a)

If
V(H)-\mathbb{P}(H)=\{h^{*}\}
, then
(G\diamond H)_{\rm SR}=\overline{G}\cup\frac{|V(G)|(|V(H)|-1)}{2}K_{2}
.

(b)

If
V(H)=\mathbb{P}(H)
, then
(G\diamond H)_{\rm SR}=\frac{|V(G)||V(H)|}{2}K_{2}
.

Proof.

Let
G
and
H
be graphs as described in the statement of the present theorem. Since neither
G
nor
H
has a pair of distinct adjacent twins,
E(G[V({\rm TW}(G))]\times\mathbb{GP}(H))=\emptyset
and
E(\mathbb{GP}(G)\times H[V({\rm TW}(H))])=\emptyset
. These, together with the assumption that
G
has no
\gamma_{G}
-pair, imply that there are no distinct adjacent twins in
G\diamond H
by Theorem
3.19
(b). So,
{\rm TW}(G\diamond H)=\emptyset
. Since there’s no
\gamma_{G}
-pair,
\mathbb{P}(G)=\emptyset
; thus,
G^{-}=G
,
\overline{G}^{-}=\overline{G}
, and
\mathbb{GP}(G)
is an edgeless graph with vertex set
V(G)
.

(a) Since
V(H)-\mathbb{P}(H)=\{h^{*}\}
and
V(\overline{H})-\mathbb{P}(H)=\{h^{*}\}
, we have
H^{-}=H-\mathbb{P}(H)=\{h^{*}\}
and
\overline{H}^{-}=\overline{H}-\mathbb{P}(H)=\{h^{*}\}
. So,
E(G^{-}\times\overline{H}^{-})=\emptyset
,
E(\overline{G}^{-}\times H^{-})=\emptyset
, and
E(\overline{G}^{-}\Box\overline{H}^{-})=E(\overline{G}^{-})=E(\overline{G})
. By Theorem
3.22
,
E((G\diamond H)_{\rm SR})=E(\overline{G}^{-}\square\overline{H}^{-})\cup E(\mathbb{GP}(G)\square\mathbb{GP}(H))
. For an arbitrary
\gamma_{H}
-pair
\{h,h^{\prime}\}
, if
\{h,h^{\prime\prime}\}
is also a
\gamma_{H}
-pair for some
h^{\prime\prime}\in V(H)-\{h^{\prime}\}
, then
N_{H}[h^{\prime}]=N_{H}[h^{\prime\prime}]
, which contradicts the hypothesis that
H
has no distinct adjacent twins. So,
\mathbb{GP}(H)=(\frac{|V(H)|-1}{2})K_{2}\cup K_{1}
, where
h^{*}
is the vertex in the
K_{1}
. Since
\mathbb{GP}(G)
is an edgeless graph with vertex set
V(G)
, we have
(G\diamond H)_{\rm SR}=\overline{G}\cup\frac{|V(G)|(|V(H)|-1)}{2}K_{2}
.

(b) Since
V(\overline{H})=V(H)=\mathbb{P}(H)
, we have
H^{-}=H-\mathbb{P}(H)=\emptyset
and
\overline{H}^{-}=\overline{H}-\mathbb{P}(H)=\emptyset
. So,
E(G^{-}\times\overline{H}^{-})=E(\overline{G}^{-}\times H^{-})=\emptyset
and
E(\overline{G}^{-}\Box\overline{H}^{-})=\emptyset
. By Theorem
3.22
,
E((G\diamond H)_{\rm SR})=E(\mathbb{GP}(G)\square\mathbb{GP}(H))
. By the same argument used in the proof for (a) of the present theorem, we have
\mathbb{GP}(H)=\frac{|V(H)|}{2}K_{2}
. Since
\mathbb{GP}(G)
is an edgeless graph with vertex set
V(G)
, we have
(G\diamond H)_{\rm SR}=\frac{|V(G)||V(H)|}{2}K_{2}
. ∎

Corollary 3.24
.

Let
G
and
H
be connected graphs of order at least two such that neither
G
nor
H
is a complete graph. If
\textnormal{diam}(G\diamond H)=3
, then
O_{\rm SR}(G\diamond H)\in\{\mathcal{M},\mathcal{N},\mathcal{B}\}
.

Proof.

Let
G
and
H
be graphs described in the statement satisfying
\textnormal{diam}(G\diamond H)=3
. We provide modular product graphs
G\diamond H
such that
O_{\rm SR}(G\diamond H)=\mathcal{M}
,
O_{\rm SR}(G\diamond H)=\mathcal{N}
and
O_{\rm SR}(G\diamond H)=\mathcal{B}
, respectively.

First, let
G=C_{4}
and let
H=C_{6}
be given by
w_{1},w_{2},w_{3},w_{4},w_{5},w_{6},w_{1}
. Note the following: (i)
G
has no universal vertex, no distinct adjacent twins, and no
\gamma_{G}
-pair; (ii)
\{w_{1},w_{4}\}
,
\{w_{2},w_{5}\}
, and
\{w_{3},w_{6}\}
are
\gamma_{H}
-pairs, and
H
has no distinct adjacent twins. Note that
V(H)=\mathbb{P}(H)
. By Theorem
3.23
(b),
(G\diamond H)_{\rm SR}=12K_{2}
. By Corollary
2.7
(a),
O_{\rm SR}(C_{4}\diamond C_{6})=\mathcal{M}
.

Second, let
G=\overline{P_{5}}
and let
H=P_{5}
be given by
w_{1},w_{2},w_{3},w_{4},w_{5}
. Note the following: (i)
G
has no universal vertex, no distinct adjacent twins, and no
\gamma_{G}
-pair; (ii)
\{w_{1},w_{4}\}
and
\{w_{2},w_{5}\}
are
\gamma_{H}
-pairs,
w_{3}
belongs to no
\gamma_{H}
-pair, and
H
has no distinct adjacent twins. Note that
V(H)-\mathbb{P}(H)=\{w_{3}\}
. By Theorem
3.23
(a),
(\overline{P_{5}}\diamond P_{5})_{\rm SR}=P_{5}\cup 10K_{2}
. Thus,
O_{\rm SR}(\overline{P_{5}}\diamond P_{5})=\mathcal{N}
by Corollary
2.7
(b).

Third, let
G=P_{4}
be given by
u_{1},u_{2},u_{3},u_{4}
and
H=P_{4}
be given by
w_{1},w_{2},w_{3},w_{4}
. Note that
\{u_{1},u_{4}\}
is a
\gamma_{G}
-pair and
\{w_{1},w_{4}\}
is a
\gamma_{H}
-pair. By Theorem
3.21
(a),
O_{\rm SR}(P_{4}\diamond P_{4})=\mathcal{B}
. ∎

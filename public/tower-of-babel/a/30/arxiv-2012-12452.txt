Yang–Mills Monopoles in Extremal Reissner–Nordström Black Hole Metric Email address for correspondence: yisongyang@nyu.edu
Xiangqin Zhang

Abstract
We show that the closed-form monotone solution linking two different vacuum states, for the exterior Yang–Mills wave equation over the extremal Reissner–Nordström spacetime found in a recent work of Bizoń and Kahl, is the unique solution to an associated domain-wall type energy minimization problem. We also derive the accompanying interior Yang–Mills wave equation within the same formalism. We obtain a few more closed-form solutions, regular and singular, among other solutions of oscillatory behavior, and discuss several interesting features of the solutions based on some energy consideration. Keywords : Reissner–Nordström black hole metric, Yang–Mills fields, monopoles, domain-wall solitons, minimization problem, existence and uniqueness of solution. PACS numbers : 02.30.Hq, 11.15.q, 11.27.+d, 12.39.Ba MSC numbers : 34B40, 35J50, 81T13, 83C57

The concept of magnetic monopoles was first conceived by P. Curie

]

based on electromagnetic duality of the Maxwell theory. A remarkable feature of this concept is that its existence could lead to an
explanation why electric charges are multiples of a unit charge
[
10
,
13
,
25
,
27
,
39
]
. Such a charge quantization phenomenon was
then extended by Schwinger
[
28
]
to dyons, namely, particles of both electric and magnetic charges. In modern development,
thought experiments with monopoles immersed in a type-II superconductor led
Mandelstam
[
19
,
20
]
, Nambu
[
23
]
, and t’ Hooft
[
33
,
33
]

to come up with a linear confinement mechanism for
monopoles and their colored, non-Abelian or the Yang–Mills, field theory versions, which has propelled enormous activities and progress towards
an understanding
[
29
,
30
]
of the unsettled puzzle of quark confinement
[
14
]
.
Like that in the classical Maxwell theory, a monopole in the pure Yang–Mills theory, for example, the celebrated Wu–Yang monopole

[
1
,
13
]
, is of infinite energy
[
16
]
. There are several well-recognized paths which may be taken to overcome this infinity or divergence problem. These
include adopting the model of the Born–Infeld nonlinear electrodynamics
[
4
,
5
]
, coupling the Yang–Mills fields with matter
fields
[
1
,
13
,
17
,
24
,
25
,
32
,
35
,
38
,
40
]
, namely,
considering the Yang–Mills–Higgs theory, and hosting the Yang–Mills fields over curved spacetime backgrounds due to gravity. In the context of this last approach, if a gravitational black hole is present
and fields are considered outside the event horizon so that possible singularities leading to energy divergence are concealed inside the horizon, as in the scenario
of the cosmic censorship hypothesis
[
22
,
37
]
, the total energy of the fields outside the horizon could remain finite, as a result.
The present study belongs to this category of investigation. Specifically our work is motivated by the recent paper of Bizoń and Kahl
[
3
]

who considered spherically symmetric
SU(2)
Yang–Mills fields in the exterior of the event horizon of the extremal
Reissner–Nordström black hole and obtained a countable family of static regular solutions to the Yang–Mills equations,
given in terms of a scalar field sequence
\{W_{n}\}
, say, among which the first two,
W_{1}
and
W_{2}
, are
in closed forms. These solutions resemble the Bartnick–McKinnon solutions
[
2
,
31
]
featured as soliton-like
configurations connecting vacuum states at infinity and
oscillating about the unit-charge monopole state in local domains.
The main result of our present work is to show that, viewed as a connecting orbit between different vacuum states which passes
through the unit-charge monopole state midway,
W_{1}
is the unique global energy minimizer of the problem. The solution
W_{2}
,
however, enjoys a different signature – it exhibits itself as a local energy maximizer in a well-formulated sense.
Besides, we shall also obtain a new closed-form solution which has two singular points (in fact, two singular shells), near which
energy blow-up takes place. Furthermore, we adapt the formalism of
[
3
]
to consider interior monopoles (inside the event
horizon) and derive a governing wave equation which may be viewed as a companion of the wave equation of Bizoń–Kahl
[
3
]

for exterior monopoles. For this interior monopole equation, we obtain three closed-form solutions, similar to
W_{1}
,
W_{2}
, and
the third singular solution to the exterior monopole equation described above, among which two are regular everywhere away from
the curvature singularity of the Reissner–Nordström spacetime and the third one is singular at one point (in fact, one shell).
It is noted that this interior monopole equation possesses a family of oscillatory solutions as the exterior equation, as well.

The content of the rest of the paper is outlined as follows. In Section 2, we formulate the problems to be studied with
a review of the Yang–Mills fields over an extremal Reissner–Nordström spacetime and the exterior monopole equation derived
in
[
3
]
, followed with an extension of such a formulation to get the interior, ‘accompanying’, monopole equation. Subsequently,
we focus on static equations.
In Section 3, we review the two closed-form regular solutions,
W_{1}
and
W_{2}
, obtained in
[
3
]
, and present a third, singular, one, for
the exterior equation. We obtain the exact values of the energies of these two regular solutions to be further explored in later sections. We then present two closed-form regular solutions and a third, singular, one, for the interior equation, similar to those
for the exterior equation. In Section 4, we study several issues of the exterior solutions based on some energy consideration.
In particular, we formulate a domain-wall type minimization problem that associates a distinguishing significance to
W_{1}
as
the unique global minimizer of the problem. We also present a virial identity which displays an energy partition relation
for finite-energy solutions. In Section 5, we first prove the existence of a solution to the domain-wall minimization problem
formulated in Section 4. Note that, due to the behavior of the weight function in the energy functional, it is not a trivial matter to
preserve the asymptotic limit of the weak limit of a minimizing sequence. Fortunately, such a difficulty may be overcome by a local
convergence method leading to realizing the weak limit as a monotone solution to the exterior equation and then by an energy
comparison. We next prove the uniqueness of a monotone solution to the exterior equation. Since the energy functional is not
convex, such a uniqueness result is usually not ensured
[
11
]
and seems surprising in the present context. Consequently, we recognize that
W_{1}

is actually this energy minimizer whose energy calculated in Section 3 is thus the energy minimum of the formulated problem.
We also illustrate that
W_{2}
is a local energy maximizer among a class of testing configurations, which seems to
suggest that, energetically, the closed-form solution
W_{2}
of the exterior monopole equation has
a certain ‘significance’ associated as well. In Section 6, we briefly comment on how to obtain oscillatory solutions for both exterior
and interior monopole equations using their polar-variable representations.

Yang–Mills monopole equations in Reissner–Nordström metric

Consider the Reissner–Nordström metric
[

,
37

\mathrm{d}s^{2}=-\left(1-\frac{2M}{r}+\frac{Q^{2}}{r^{2}}\right)\,\mathrm{d}t^{2}+\left(1-\frac{2M}{r}+\frac{Q^{2}}{r^{2}}\right)^{-1}\,\mathrm{d}r^{2}+r^{2}(\mathrm{d}\theta^{2}+\sin^{2}\theta\mathrm{d}\phi^{2}),

(2.1)

describing a charged black hole of mass
M
and charge
Q
in the Schwarzschild spherical coordinates. At the critical situation

M=Q,

(2.2)

the metric (
2.1
) assumes the form

\mathrm{d}s^{2}=-\frac{(r-M)^{2}}{r^{2}}\mathrm{d}t^{2}+\frac{r^{2}}{(r-M)^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}r^{2}+r^{2}(\mathrm{d}\theta^{2}+\sin^{2}\theta\mathrm{d}\phi^{2}).

known as the extremal Reissner–Nordström black hole metric with its event horizon at

r>M

associated Kretschmann invariant given by

Exterior region

We first consider the space region exterior to the event horizon,

[
3
]

\tau=\frac{t}{4M},\quad x=\ln\left(\frac{r}{M}-1\right),

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}s^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Omega(x)\left(-\mathrm{d}\tau^{2}+C^{4}(x)(\mathrm{d}x^{2}+\mathrm{d}\theta^{2}+\sin^{2}\theta\,\mathrm{d}\phi^{2})\right),

\displaystyle\Omega(x)

\displaystyle\frac{16M^{2}}{(1+\mathrm{e}^{-x})^{2}}\quad C(x)=\cosh\left(\frac{x}{2}\right).

Thus the exterior region in terms of the radial variable,

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}A+A\wedge A

\displaystyle t_{1}(W_{\tau}\,\mathrm{d}\tau+W_{x}\,\mathrm{d}x)\wedge\mathrm{d}\theta+t_{2}\sin\theta(W_{\tau}\,\mathrm{d}\tau+W_{x}\,\mathrm{d}x)\wedge\mathrm{d}\phi+t_{3}\sin\theta(W^{2}-1)\,\mathrm{d}\theta\wedge\mathrm{d}\phi.\quad

4
Energy consideration of exterior monopoles

We now investigate the problem whether there is a nontrivial energetically stable exterior monopole solution linking two vacuum states,

W=\pm 1
, characterized by
the boundary condition
W(-\infty)=\pm 1
and
W(\infty)=\pm 1
. It is clear that
the problem of linking the same vacuum state with
W(-\infty)=W(\infty)=\pm 1
is not well posed without some further elaboration
since the vacuum states themselves minimize the energy and may be approached arbitrarily by nontrivial field configurations.
Thus it remains to consider the problem of linking two different vacuum states, say,

\eta=\inf\{E(W)\,|\,W(-\infty)=-1,W(\infty)=1\},

(4.1)

and ask whether this minimization problem has a solution, where
E(W)
is as given in (
3.12
). This is typically a domain wall
problem
[
6
,
7
,
8
,
26
,
36
]
, interpolating two
ground state domains,
W=\pm 1
. However, it is not hard to see that this problem is not well posed either.

In fact, let
u(x)
be a smooth function satisfying

u(x)=-1,\quad x\leq-1;\quad u(x)=1,\quad x\geq 1.

(4.2)

Then, using
u_{(a)}=u(x-a)
as a testing function, we have

\displaystyle E(u_{(a)})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\pi\int_{a-1}^{a+1}\frac{1}{C^{2}(x)}\left((u^{\prime}_{(a)})^{2}+\frac{1}{2}(u^{2}_{(a)}-1)^{2}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x

(4.3)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle K_{0}\int_{a-1}^{a+1}\frac{\mathrm{d}x}{C^{2}(x)}\to 0\quad\mbox{as }|a|\to\infty.

Here
K_{0}>0
is a constant depending on the properties of
u
over
[-1,1]
. As a consequence, this shows
the quantity
\eta
in (
4.1
) is zero which
is therefore not attainable.

On the other hand, since the boundary condition in (
4.1
) dictates that
the unit magnetic charge monopole configuration,
W=0
, is to occur somewhere as an intermediate state, we may conveniently impose the following additional ‘intermediate state condition’

W(0)=0.

(4.4)

It is interesting that this condition implies that the potential part of the energy density

{\cal H}_{W}=\frac{1}{2C^{2}(x)}W_{x}^{2}+\frac{1}{4C^{2}(x)}(1-W^{2})^{2}

(4.5)

is maximized at the spot where the intermediate state occurs,
x=0
. In Figure
1
, we plot the energy density (
4.5
) for
the solutions
W_{1}
and
W_{2}
given in (
3.1
) as an illustration. It is interesting to notice that the former peaks sharply at
x=0

where the solution passes the intermediate state and the latter also peaks at the same spot which is not where the solution passes
the intermediate state but is the midpoint between the two spots,

x^{\pm}_{0}=\ln\left(1+\frac{\sqrt{6}}{2}\pm\frac{\sqrt{6+4\sqrt{6}}}{2}\right),

(4.6)

where the solution passes the intermediate state. Such
a property of energy concentration is typical for solitons in field theories.

Thus, we are led to modifying (
4.1

\eta_{0}\equiv\inf\{E(W)\,|\,W(-\infty)=-1,W(\infty)=1,W(0)=0\}.

(4.7)

We shall show that

Let

be a finite-energy critical point of the energy functional (
3.12
). Set
W_{\lambda}(x)=W(\lambda x)
. Then,
from
\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\lambda}E(W_{\lambda})|_{\lambda=1}=0
, we get the virial identity

\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{1}{C^{2}(x)}\left(\left[1+x\tanh\left(\frac{x}{2}\right)\right]W_{x}^{2}+\frac{x}{2}\,\tanh\left(\frac{x}{2}\right)(W^{2}-1)^{2}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{(W^{2}-1)^{2}}{2C^{2}(x)}\,\mathrm{d}x,

(4.9)

which is an energy partition relation. This identity demonstrates that the ‘potential energy’ is much greater than the ‘elastic energy’
for an exterior monopole. For example, for the exact solution
W=W_{1}
in (
3.1
), we have

\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{(W^{2}-1)^{2}}{4C^{2}(x)}\,\mathrm{d}x=\frac{8}{15},\quad\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{W_{x}^{2}}{2C^{2}(x)}\,\mathrm{d}x=\frac{4}{15},

(4.10)

and, for
W=W_{2}
given in (
3.1
), these quantities are approximately
0.8765154575
and
0.08988507022
, respectively.

5
Existence and uniqueness of exterior energy minimizer

The symmetry of the functional (
3.12
) indicates that the problem (
4.7
) amounts to considering the minimization problem

I_{0}\equiv\inf\left\{I(W)\equiv\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{C^{2}(x)}\left((W^{\prime})^{2}+\frac{1}{2}(W^{2}-1)^{2}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\bigg|\,W(0)=0,W(\infty)=1\right\},

(5.1)

over the set of admissible functions which are absolutely continuous on all compact subintervals of the half line
[0,\infty)
.

Let
\{{\cal W}_{n}\}
be a minimizing sequence of (
5.1
) satisfying

\lim_{n\to\infty}I({\cal W}_{n})=I_{0};\quad I({\cal W}_{n})\leq I_{0}+\frac{1}{n},\quad n=1,2,\dots.

(5.2)

By modifying the sequence
\{{\cal W}_{n}\}
if necessary, we may assume that
\{{\cal W}_{n}\}
enjoys the property that each function
{\cal W}_{n}
satisfies
0\leq{\cal W}_{n}(x)\leq 1
.
Furthermore, for each
{\cal W}_{n}
, we may modify it such that it minimizes the
partial functional

I_{n}(W)\equiv\int_{0}^{n}\frac{1}{C^{2}(x)}\left((W^{\prime})^{2}+\frac{1}{2}(W^{2}-1)^{2}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x,

(5.3)

among functions fulfilling the boundary condition
W(0)=0,W(n)={\cal W}_{n}(n)
since the functional is differentiable and
weakly lower semicontinuous over the Sobolev space
W^{1,2}(0,n)
, say (cf. Theorem 2 on page 448 in Evans
[
11
]

specifically as well as the studies in
[
12
,
35
,
40
]
in other contexts).
Therefore
{\cal W}_{n}
when restricted to the interval
[0,n]

is a critical point of
I_{n}
. As a consequence, we have

\int_{0}^{n}\frac{1}{C^{2}(x)}\left({\cal W}^{\prime}_{n}w^{\prime}+({\cal W}_{n}^{2}-1){\cal W}_{n}w\right)\,\mathrm{d}x=0,\quad\forall w\in C_{0}^{1}(0,n).

(5.4)

Extracting a suitable subsequence if necessary, for example, using a diagonal subsequence argument, we may assume that
\{{\cal W}_{n}\}
is weakly convergent
in the Sobolev space
W^{1,2}(0,a)
, with the weighted measure
C^{-2}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x
, for
any

a>0
. Let
{{\cal W}}
denote
such a weak limit which is well defined over
[0,\infty)
.
Fix
a>0
.
Neglecting the first few terms of the sequence if necessary, we may also assume
n>a
. Thus (
5.4
) gives us

\int_{0}^{a}\frac{1}{C^{2}(x)}\left({\cal W}^{\prime}_{n}w^{\prime}+({\cal W}_{n}^{2}-1){\cal W}_{n}w\right)\,\mathrm{d}x=0,\quad\forall w\in C_{0}^{1}(0,a).

(5.5)

Using the compact embedding from the Sobolev space into the space
C[0,a]
and letting
n\to\infty
in (
5.5
), we arrive at

\int_{0}^{a}\frac{1}{C^{2}(x)}\left({{\cal W}}^{\prime}w^{\prime}+({{\cal W}}^{2}-1){{\cal W}}w\right)\,\mathrm{d}x=0,\quad\forall w\in C_{0}^{1}(0,a),

(5.6)

which leads us to conclude by the standard elliptic regularity theory
[
12
,
18
]
that
{{\cal W}}
is a classical solution to the Euler–Lagrange equation of the functional
I
. Hence

{\cal W}
satisfies

\left(\frac{{\cal W}^{\prime}}{C^{2}(x)}\right)^{\prime}+\frac{1}{C^{2}(x)}(1-{\cal W}^{2}){\cal W}=0,\quad{\cal W}(0)=0.

(5.7)

Since
\{{\cal W}_{n}\}
is a sequence with values confined in
[0,1]
, so is its limit
{\cal W}
. However, since
{\cal W}
satisfies
(
5.7
), we see that
{\cal W}\equiv 0
or
0<{\cal W}(x)<1
for
x>0
because
{\cal W}\equiv 0
and
{\cal W}\equiv 1
are two equilibria
of the differential equation in (
5.7
).
We now show that the former does not occur.

In fact, by using weak convergence, we have

I_{a}({\cal W})\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}I_{a}({\cal W}_{n})\leq\lim_{n\to\infty}I({\cal W}_{n})=I_{0},

(5.8)

for any
a>0
. Thus, letting
a\to\infty
in (
5.8
), we get

I({\cal W})\leq I_{0}.

(5.9)

Recall the result

I_{0}\leq I\left(\tanh\,\frac{x}{2}\right)=\frac{4}{5}.

(5.10)

Besides, we also have
I(0)=1
. Hence
{\cal W}\not\equiv 0
. Consequently,
{\cal W}
satisfies
0<{\cal W}(x)<1
for
x>0
.

Since
{\cal W}
is bounded, we deduce that there is a sequence
\{x_{n}\}
,
x_{n}\to\infty
as
n\to\infty
, such that

{\cal W}^{\prime}(x_{n})\to 0
as
n\to\infty
. Using this result as the boundary condition and integrating the differential equation in (
5.7
), we have

\frac{{\cal W}^{\prime}(x)}{C^{2}(x)}=\int^{\infty}_{x}\frac{1}{C^{2}(y)}(1-{\cal W}^{2}(y)){\cal W}(y)\,\mathrm{d}y.

(5.11)

Hence
{\cal W}^{\prime}(x)>0
for
x>0
.
In particular,

\lim_{x\to\infty}{\cal W}(x)=L,

(5.12)

for some
L\in(0,1]
.
Thus, if
L<1
in (
5.12
), we infer from (
5.11
) that

\lim_{x\to\infty}{\cal W}^{\prime}(x)=(1-L^{2})L>0,

(5.13)

which is false. Therefore
L=1

in (
5.12
). This establishes that
{\cal W}
lies in the admissible space of the problem (
5.1
). Thus
I({\cal W})\geq I_{0}
.
Combining this result with (
5.9
), we arrive at

I({\cal W})=I_{0}.

(5.14)

In other words, the existence of a solution to the minimization problem (
5.1
) follows.

We next prove that, actually,

{\cal W}(x)=\tanh\left(\frac{x}{2}\right).

(5.15)

Thus, in particular, equality in (
5.10
) holds. We achieve this goal by showing that a positive solution to the initial value
problem (
5.7
), with a limiting value at infinity, is unique.

In fact, let
{\cal W}_{1}
and
{\cal W}_{2}
be two such solutions. Then
w={\cal W}_{1}-{\cal W}_{2}
satisfies

\left(\frac{w^{\prime}}{C^{2}(x)}\right)^{\prime}+\frac{w}{C^{2}(x)}(1-{\cal W}_{1}^{2}-{\cal W}_{2}^{2}-{\cal W}_{1}{\cal W}_{2})=0.

(5.16)

On the other hand, for any differentiable function
h
, we have

\displaystyle C^{2}(x)\left(\frac{{\cal W}_{1}{\cal W}^{\prime}_{1}h^{2}}{C^{2}(x)}\right)^{\prime}+{{\cal W}_{1}^{2}(h^{\prime})^{2}}+{({\cal W}_{1}{\cal W}_{2}+{\cal W}_{2}^{2}){\cal W}_{1}^{2}h^{2}}

\displaystyle=([{\cal W}_{1}h]^{\prime})^{2}-(1-{\cal W}_{1}^{2}-{\cal W}_{2}^{2}-{\cal W}_{1}{\cal W}_{2})({\cal W}_{1}h)^{2}.

(5.17)

Now substituting
h=\frac{w}{{\cal W}_{1}}
into (
5
), we have

\displaystyle\frac{(w^{\prime})^{2}}{C^{2}}-\frac{1}{C^{2}}(1-{\cal W}_{1}^{2}-{\cal W}_{2}^{2}-{\cal W}_{1}{\cal W}_{2})w^{2}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{{\cal W}_{1}^{\prime}w^{2}}{C^{2}{\cal W}_{1}}\right)^{\prime}+\frac{(w^{\prime}{\cal W}_{1}-w{\cal W}_{1}^{\prime})^{2}}{C^{2}}+\frac{({\cal W}_{1}{\cal W}_{2}+{\cal W}_{2}^{2})w^{2}}{C^{2}}.

(5.18)

Moreover, using the Cauchy–Kovalevskaya theorem, we know that the solution of (
5.7
) has the asymptotic form

{\cal W}(x)=bx+cx^{3}+\mbox{O}(x^{5}),\quad x\sim 0.

(5.19)

As a consequence of (
5.19
), we may integrate (
5
) and drop the resulting boundary terms to arrive at

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{(w^{\prime})^{2}}{C^{2}}-\frac{1}{C^{2}}(1-{\cal W}_{1}^{2}-{\cal W}_{2}^{2}-{\cal W}_{1}{\cal W}_{2})w^{2}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{C^{2}(x)}\left({(w^{\prime}{\cal W}_{1}-w{\cal W}_{1}^{\prime})^{2}}+{({\cal W}_{1}{\cal W}_{2}+{\cal W}_{2}^{2})w^{2}}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x.

(5.20)

However, by virtue of (
5.16
), we may rewrite the left-hand side of (
5
) as

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{(w^{\prime})^{2}}{C^{2}}-\frac{1}{C^{2}}(1-{\cal W}_{1}^{2}-{\cal W}_{2}^{2}-{\cal W}_{1}{\cal W}_{2})w^{2}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{ww^{\prime}}{C^{2}}\right)_{0}^{\infty}-\int_{0}^{\infty}w\left(\left[\frac{w^{\prime}}{C^{2}}\right]^{\prime}+\frac{w}{C^{2}}(1-{\cal W}_{1}^{2}-{\cal W}_{2}^{2}-{\cal W}_{1}{\cal W}_{2})\right)\,\mathrm{d}x=0.

(5.21)

Combining (
5
) and (
5
), we conclude with
w\equiv 0
.

Hence the existence and uniqueness of an exterior energy-minimizing monopole solution linking different vacuum states at

x\pm\infty
and
passes through the unit-charge monopole state at
x=0
is established which is the exact solution
W_{1}
stated in (
3.1
)
and found in
[
3
]
which determines the minimum energy,
E_{\min}=\frac{8\pi}{5}
, as stated in (
3.13
).

It is interesting to note that a rich family of even and odd solutions of (
2.17
) linking the same and different vacuum
states is obtained in
[
3
]
which are characterized
by the number
n
of zeros they possess. When
n=1
, their calculated energy coincides with our exact value; when
n\geq 2
, their calculated
energies are all significantly greater than that with
n=1
.

Motivated by the construction in this section and the even solutions with multiple zeros linking to the same vacuum state,
W(-\infty)=W(\infty)=\pm 1
, obtained in
[

]
, we may impose the following minimization problem

\sigma_{0}\equiv\inf\{E(W)\,|\,W(-\infty)=W(\infty)=1,W(0)\leq 0\},

(5.22)

over the admissible set of functions which are absolutely continuous on all compact subintervals of the real line. Thus, with
the function (
5.15
) as a testing function, i.e., choosing
W(x)=|\tanh(\frac{x}{2})|
for all

, we have the estimate

\sigma_{0}\leq\frac{8\pi}{5}.

(5.23)

With the problem (

) in mind, we consider the exact solution
W_{2}
given in (

) whose value at

W_{2}(0)=-h_{0},\quad h_{0}=\frac{1}{3+\sqrt{6}},

(5.24)

W_{h}(x)=\frac{2\cosh x-d}{2\cosh x+4+3\sqrt{6}},\quad d\equiv 2+3(2+\sqrt{6})h,\quad 0<h<1,

6
Remarks on oscillatory solutions

In
[
3
]
, a family of solutions of (
2.17
) with prescribed numbers of zeros are obtained. In this section, we revisit such
a construction of oscillatory solutions from the viewpoint of recasting the equation into a form of its ‘polar variable’ representation
such that the appearance of multiple zeros of the solutions becomes somewhat more transparent as we now show.

For our purpose, we use the following polar variable anzatz

W=\rho\cos{\omega},\quad W^{\prime}=\rho\sin{\omega},

(6.1)

to represent a solution of (
2.17
). Thus, we see that the pair
(\rho,{\omega})
satisfies the coupled system of equations

\displaystyle\rho^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tanh\left(\frac{x}{2}\right)\,\rho\sin^{2}{\omega}+\rho^{3}\cos^{3}{\omega}\sin{\omega},

(6.2)

\displaystyle{\omega}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-1+\tanh\left(\frac{x}{2}\right)\,\sin{\omega}\cos{\omega}+\rho^{2}\cos^{4}{\omega}.

(6.3)

To justify this representation, we observe in view of the uniqueness theorem for solutions of the initial value problems of ordinary
differential equations that if
\rho
satisfying (
6.2
) is positive at a point then it will stay positive where it exists. So, we may
impose the initial condition

\rho(0)=a>0,\quad{\omega}(0)=b.

(6.4)

Thus, in particular, to produce an even solution of (
2.17
), we take
b=0
, and an odd solution, we impose
b=\frac{\pi}{2}
.
In the subsequent discussion, we fix
b
and adjust
a
to achieve multiple zeros for the field
W
. Accordingly, in view of
(
6.1
), such a goal may be achieved by making the angular variable
{\omega}
circulate through the full circle multiple times,
in a sense to be made precise below.

Indeed, we fix
b,K_{0}>0
. Then the theorem of continuous dependence of solutions on the initial data allows us to get
some number
a_{0}>0
such that the solution to (
6.2
)–(
6.4
) satisfies

0<\rho(x)\leq\frac{1}{2},\quad 0\leq x\leq K_{0},

(6.5)

when
a\in(0,a_{0}]
.
Applying (
6.5
) to (
6.3
) and using
\sin{\omega}\cos{\omega}\leq\frac{1}{2}
, we get

{\omega}^{\prime}(x)\leq-\frac{1}{4},\quad 0\leq x\leq K_{0}.

(6.6)

Therefore, for any integer
n\geq 1
, we may set
K_{0}=4n\pi
(say). Then (
6.6
) indicates that there are
n
points

x_{1},\dots,x_{n}
in
(0,K_{0}]
such that

{\omega}(x_{i})=\mbox{an odd multiple of }\,\frac{\pi}{2},\quad i=1,\dots,n.

(6.7)

As a consequence of this observation and (
6.1
), we see that if
b
is not an odd multiple of
\frac{\pi}{2}
, then
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}

are the zeros of
W
, and if
b
is an odd multiple of
\frac{\pi}{2}
, then
b,x_{1},\dots,x_{n}
are the zeros of
W
.
Hence a construction of a local solution with a prescribed number of zeros is obtained. Note that such a solution may be even, odd, or
neither.

For the interior monopole equation (
2.23
), if we use the polar representatiion (
6.1
) with the flippled variable
x=-y
,
then we get the transformed governing system

\displaystyle\rho^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\coth\left(\frac{y}{2}\right)\,\rho\sin^{2}{\omega}-\rho^{3}\cos^{3}{\omega}\sin{\omega},

(6.8)

\displaystyle{\omega}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1+\coth\left(\frac{y}{2}\right)\,\sin{\omega}\cos{\omega}-\rho^{2}\cos^{4}{\omega},

(6.9)

where
0<y<\infty
and
\rho^{\prime}=\frac{\mathrm{d}\rho}{\mathrm{d}y}
, etc. Although
\coth(\frac{y}{2})
is unbounded as
y\to 0
,
corresponding to the curvature singularity, we may again restrict our attention to a region away from the singularity in order to
get solutions with multiple zeros in
W
. For example, it suffices to request
\coth(\frac{y}{2})<2
for our method here to work. Such
a condition translates into

y>\ln 3.

(6.10)

In view of (
2.18
), this restriction gives us the interior region

\frac{2M}{3}<r<M,

(6.11)

to accommodate oscillatory monopole solutions similar to those in the exterior region.

In order to obtain a global solution with a prescribed number of zeros with desired asymptotic limits at infinity,
W(\pm\infty)=\pm 1
,
additional work is needed for the choice of the initial data. Here we omit the details since much of the idea
along the line is presented in
[
3
]
mathematically and numerically. See also
[
15
,
21
,
31
]
.

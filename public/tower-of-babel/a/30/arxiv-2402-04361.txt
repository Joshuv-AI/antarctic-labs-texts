Resonance Cascades and Number Theory Preprint: AIP/123-QED
Oleksandr V. Marchukov, Maxim Olshanii

Abstract
In this article, we are interested in situations where the existence of a contiguous cascade of quantum resonant transitions is predicated on the validity of a particular statement in number theory. The setting is a tailored one-atom one-dimensional potential with a prescribed spectrum, under a weak periodic perturbation. The former is, by now, an experimental reality [D. Cassettari, G. Mussardo and A. Trombettoni, PNAS Nexus 2 , pgac279 (2022)]. As a case study, we look at the following trivial statement: “Any power of 3 3 is an integer.” Consequently, we “test” this statement in a numerical experiment where we demonstrate an unimpeded upward mobility along an equidistant, ln ⁡ ( 3 ) \ln(3) -spaced subsequence of the energy levels of a potential with a log-natural spectrum, under a frequency ln ⁡ ( 3 ) \ln(3) time-periodic perturbation. We further show that when we “remove” 9 9 from the set of integers—by excluding the corresponding energy level from the spectrum—the cascade halts abruptly.



I
Introduction

The links between number theory and quantum physics can not yet be labeled as “numerous.” The majority of the text-book quantum
problems constitute the solvable ones. The latter possesses a regular spectrum that appears to be disparate from the quasi-random nature of the number-theoretical objects. The notable exception is the quantum factorization algorithm
Shor 1994
; it does not relate on properties of the prime numbers, and, hence, it does not contain any quasi-random elements. Quantum-chaotic systems are, by their nature, closer to the number theory entities. The most prominent example of such a connection is the suspected interpretation of the Riemann zeros as eigenenergies of an
xp
Hamiltonian with yet unknown boundary conditions
Berry and Keating 1999
.

The situation changed when it became apparent that some artificial quantum objects can be, instead, experimentally tailored to acquire number-theoretical properties
Cassettari, Mussardo, and Trombettoni 2023
. (See also the proposal
Latorre and Sierra
.)
In this article, we show how to create a quantum system, similar to the one in
Cassettari, Mussardo, and Trombettoni 2023
, where the existence of a
contiguous cascade of quantum resonant transitions, in a one-body one-dimensional system is predicated on the validity
of a particular statement in number theory. The empirical relevance of our exploits is backed by the recent experiments
Cassettari, Mussardo, and Trombettoni 2023
on creating quantum atomic potentials with a tailored spectrum. Our work derives its inspiration from a host of previous proposals for analogue quantum number-theoretical machines; there, the focus is mainly on the problem of prime factorization (see
Gleisberg et al. 2018
and references therein and
Mussardo and Trombettoni
).

Our article is a part of a broader cycle of works set to explore the number-theory-inspired effects in quantum systems and how best to make them prominent. In particular it can be regarded as an improvement over as similar scheme suggested

Cassettari et al.
; the latter suffered from the localization of the atomic population, requiring some ad hoc improvements. In the current article, this problem is resolved by means of choosing a more optimal spatial profile of the perturbation, (
15
).
Also, the same article studies fidelity of the individual two-state transitions under a periodic perturbation, in a class of number-theory potentials. An experimental proof of the Goldbach conjecture
Dudley 1969
(p. 147) belongs to a group of more distant goals of this program (see, also,
Prudencio and Silva
). A scheme enhanced by a quantum speed-up was proposed in
Marchukov et al.
. An alternative scheme involving two atoms can be found in
Cassettari et al.
. The article
Carmona-Pírez et al.

establishes a relationship between Mersenne numbers
Dudley 1969
(p. 60) and ground states in certain spin lattices. Finally
Ramesh and Olshanii
utilizes a Brahmagupta identity
Stillwell 2002
and an identity due to Hélène Perrin
Jackson et al. 2024
to explain systematic degeneracies in a particular rectangular quantum billiard.

II
The model and the strategy

We will focus on a potential whose spectrum is logarithms of natural numbers. Powers of a given integer for an equidistant ladder, with a possibility of igniting a resonance
cascade of transitions under an external periodic drive. Existence of a resonance cascade will serve as a quantum-analogue “proof” that positive integer powers of an integer belong to a set of natural numbers. We will also attempt to simulate a universe where one of the powers is excluded from the natural set: in that case, our analogue machine will indeed halt.

II.1
The Hamiltonian

Consider a one-body potential
U^{\text{L}}(x)
whose lowest
N
bound state energies are logarithms of natural numbers:

\displaystyle\begin{split}&E_{n}=U_{0}\ln n\\
&n=1,\,2,\,3,\,\ldots,\,N_{\text{b}}\end{split}\,\,.

(1)

Methods of designing tailored potentials are described in
Cassettari, Mussardo, and Trombettoni 2023
.
The approximate classical counterpart of the
\ln(n)
-potential is a logarithmic potential:

\displaystyle U_{\text{cl.}}(x)=U_{0}\ln(\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\frac{x}{a})\,\,;

(2)

here

a\,\equiv\hbar/\sqrt{mU_{0}}\,\,.

Indeed, let us treat the neighboring energy level differences as classical frequencies,

E_{n+1}-E_{n}\approx\hbar\omega(E\approx E_{n}\approx E_{n+1})
, arriving at

\omega(E)=\frac{U_{0}}{\hbar}e^{-\frac{E}{U_{0}}}\,\,.

Assume that the classical potential we are looking for,
U_{\text{cl.}}(x)
is even,
U_{\text{cl.}}(-x)=U_{\text{cl.}}(+x)
. Also, assume that the potential is infinitely deep, so that
U_{\text{cl.}}(x)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle x\to 0}}{{\longrightarrow}}-\infty
. Using a known result

Landau and Lifshitz 1976
(§12) that
allows one to relate the dependence of the classical frequency on energy,
\omega(E)
to the potential
U_{\text{cl.}}(x)
that governs this motion, one gets the following expression for the inverse of
U_{\text{cl.}}(x)
for
x>0
:

x(U_{\text{cl.}})=\int_{-\infty}^{U_{\text{cl.}}}\frac{dE^{\prime}}{\omega(E^{\prime})\sqrt{2m(U_{\text{cl.}}-E^{\prime})}}=\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2}}\frac{\hbar}{\sqrt{mU_{0}}}e^{\frac{U_{\text{cl.}}}{U_{0}}}\,\,.

Inverting this expression with respect to
U_{\text{cl.}}
leads us to (
2
).

II.2
The tight-binding model

Consider a monochromatic time-dependent
perturbation

\displaystyle V(x)\cos(\Omega t)\,\,,

(3)

with

\displaystyle\Omega=\frac{1}{\hbar}U_{0}\ln(\tilde{n})\,\,,

(4)

for some
\tilde{n}
that optimizes the speed of a particular time-dependent process, for some profile
V(x)
. Namely, we want to ensure that the level population can propagate freely through any subset of the unperturbed energy levels (
1
) that is simply connected via
resonant (with respect to the perturbation (
4
)) transitions. Naively, one may choose a parametric
excitation (where the coupling constant
U_{0}
acquires a small time-dependent correction) as the most experimentally relevant.
However an investigation in
Cassettari et al.
showed that the rapid decay of the matrix elements of the perturbation as a function of energy results in a localization. Below, we undergo a more systematic search for the optimal spatial profile
V(x)
.

Similarly to
Cassettari et al.
, we will use a resonant approximation to predict and control the population dynamics.
Our Schrödinger equation reads:

\displaystyle\begin{split}&i\hbar\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\Psi=\hat{H}_{0}\Psi+V(x)\cos(\Omega t)\Psi\\
&\Psi=\Psi(x,\,t)\\
&\Psi(x,\,0)=\Psi_{0}(x)\\
&\hat{H}_{0}=-\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2m}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x^{2}}+U^{\text{L}}(x)\end{split}\,\,.

(5)

Let us formally decompose
\Psi(x,\,t)
onto a Floquet series with the nature of the coefficients
\chi_{l}(x,\,t)
, to
be discussed later in the text:

\Psi(x,\,t)=\sum_{l=-\infty}^{+\infty}\chi_{l}(x,\,t)\exp[+il\Omega t]\,\,.

Let us now substitute the above series to the Schrödinger equation (
5
). We get:

\displaystyle\sum_{l=-\infty}^{+\infty}i\hbar\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\chi_{l}(x,\,t)\exp[+il\Omega t]

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=-\infty}^{+\infty}(\hat{H}_{0}+\hbar l\Omega)\chi_{l}(x,\,t)\exp[+il\Omega t]+

\displaystyle\quad\quad\sum_{l=-\infty}^{+\infty}\frac{1}{2}V(x)\chi_{l}(x,\,t)\exp[+i(l+1)\Omega t]+

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\sum_{l=-\infty}^{+\infty}\frac{1}{2}V(x)\chi_{l}(x,\,t)\exp[+i(l-1)\Omega t]

\displaystyle\sum_{l=-\infty}^{+\infty}\chi_{l}(x,\,0)=\Psi_{0}(x)\,\,.

This system of equations for
\chi_{l}(x,\,t)
is greatly overdetermined. However, if one (a)
requires that in the resulting Schrödinger equation prefactors in front of each
oscillating exponent
\exp[+il^{\prime}\Omega t]
be balanced individually
and (b) reserves the
l=0
harmonic to encode the initial state, the resulting system is well determined:

\displaystyle i\hbar\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\chi_{l}(x,\,t)=(\hat{H}_{0}+\hbar l\Omega)\chi_{l}(x,\,t)+

\displaystyle\quad\frac{1}{2}V(x)\left(\chi_{l-1}(x,\,t)+\chi_{l+1}(x,\,t)\right)

\displaystyle\chi_{l}(x,\,0)=\delta_{l,0}\Psi_{0}(x)\,\,.

As the next step, we decompose the above equation onto a basis of the eigenstates of
\hat{H}_{0}
:

\displaystyle\chi_{l}(x,\,t)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\chi_{l,n}(t)\psi_{n}(x)

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{0}\psi_{n}=E_{n}\psi_{n}\,\,.

For the coefficients
\chi_{l,n}(t)
,
we get

\displaystyle i\hbar\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\chi_{l,n}(t)=(E_{n}+\hbar l\Omega)\chi_{l,n}(t)+

\displaystyle\quad\frac{1}{2}\sum_{n^{\prime}=1}^{\infty}\langle n|V|n^{\prime}\rangle(\chi_{l-1,n^{\prime}}(t)+\chi_{l+1,n^{\prime}})(t)

\displaystyle\chi_{l,n}(0)=\delta_{l,0}\langle n|\Psi_{0}\rangle\,\,.

Notice that the unperturbed manifold that contains the initial state is massively degenerate. Indeed, since
\hbar\Omega=U_{0}\ln(\tilde{n})
and
E_{n}=U_{0}\ln(n)
, all the
Floquet quasi-energies with indices
n=\tilde{n}^{m},\,l=-m
have zero Floquet energy

\mathcal{E}_{l,n}\equiv E_{n}+\hbar l\Omega
:

\mathcal{E}_{-m,\,\tilde{n}^{m}}=E_{\tilde{n}^{m}}+\hbar(-m)\Omega=0\,\,.

If the perturbation is sufficiently weak, one can restrict oneself to the zero order of the degenerate perturbation theory where the matrix elements
\langle n|V|n^{\prime}\rangle
of the perturbation leading to the states outside of the
\mathcal{E}=0
degenerate manifold
(where
n=\tilde{n}^{-l}
)
are neglected. There one arrives at a tight-binding model:

\displaystyle i\hbar\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\phi_{m}=-J_{m+1,m}=\phi_{m+1}-J_{m,\,m-1}\phi_{m-1}\,\,,

(6)

where

\displaystyle\phi_{m}(t)\equiv\chi_{\tilde{n}^{m},-m}

\displaystyle J_{m,\,m-1}=J_{m-1,\,m}=\langle\tilde{n}^{m}|V|\tilde{n}^{m-1}\rangle\,\,.

(7)

In a
constant-hopping
tight-binding model (
J_{m}=J=\text{const}
), population density propagates ballistically. For an initial state
\phi_{m}(0)=\delta_{m,0}
, the r.m.s. and absolute values of the position
m
will grow as

\displaystyle\sqrt{\langle m^{2}\rangle}=\sqrt{2}Jt

\displaystyle\langle|m|\rangle\approx 1.27Jt\,\,.

Recall also that the maximal speed of sound in this model is
c=2J
.

A semi-infinite tight-binding model,

\displaystyle m\geq 0

\displaystyle\phi_{-1}=0\,\,,

is closer to the problem at hand. There one finds

\displaystyle\sqrt{\langle m^{2}\rangle}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle t\gg\hbar/J}}{{\approx}}-0.67+1.71Jt

(8)

\displaystyle\langle m\rangle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle t\gg\hbar/J}}{{\approx}}-0.76+1.68Jt\,\,.

(9)

Hence, if one finds a way to ensure a relative uniformity of the hopping coefficients (
7
), one may expect to find a linear in time growth of energy. Recall that for the cascade states, their energies are

E_{n=\tilde{n}^{m}}=U_{0}\ln(\tilde{n})m\,\,.

Our goal
is to find a perturbation
V(x)
that produces a set of relatively homogeneous set of the hopping constants
J_{m}
(
7
).

III
Finding the optimal perturbation profile,
V(x)

In this Section, our goal is to design a perturbation that generates a tight-binding model with relatively homogeneous hopping coefficients (
7
). The technical difficulty is that while the semiclassical expressions for the eigenstate wavefunctions are valid in the area of parameters we are interested in, the relevant changes in quantum numbers are too great to support the standard estimate for the transition matrix elements as classical Fourier transforms

Landau and Lifshitz 1965a
. This case was worked out in
Landau and Lifshitz 1965b
.

For a single
\delta
-peak,

\displaystyle V^{(\delta)}(x|\,g,\,x_{0})=g\delta(x-x_{0})\,\,,

(10)

the WKB prediction
Landau and Lifshitz 1965b
for the matrix elements leads to the following estimate

\displaystyle\begin{split}&V^{(\delta)}_{n_{1},n_{2}}\sim\frac{g}{a}\,\frac{1}{\sqrt{n_{1}n_{2}}}\times\\
&\quad\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}1&\text{for}&n_{1}>n_{\rm min}(x_{0})\text{ and }n_{2}>n_{\rm min}(x_{0})\\
0&\text{otherwise}&\end{array}\right\}\end{split}\,\,,

(11)

where

\displaystyle n_{\rm min}(x_{0})=\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\,\frac{x_{0}}{a}

is the lowest quantum number such that the corresponding eigenstate has
x_{0}
inside the classically allowed region. In (
11
),
slow, logarithmic with
n
contributions are neglected.

The result (
11
) can be understood as follows. For
n_{1}\sim n_{2}\sim n
, the matrix element of the

\delta
-perturbation (
10
) can be estimated as
g
times the classical probability density (see e.g.
Olshanii 2024
, Eq. 5.32)

\rho_{\text{class.}}(x)=\frac{\omega(E)}{\pi v(x\,|\,\,E)}

at
x_{0}
, where
E\sim E_{n_{1}}\sim E_{n_{2}}\sim U_{0}\ln(n)
,
\omega(E)
is the classical frequency, and

v(x\,|\,\,E)=\sqrt{2(E-V(x))/m}
is the magnitude of the classical velocity. Notice that beyond the classical aphelion, where

V(x)=E
or
x=\sqrt{\pi/2}\,a\exp(E/U_{0})
, the density vanishes. Furthermore, since the spatial variation of the density is
logarithmically slow, in an estimate, it can be replaced by a constant. All in all, we get

\displaystyle\begin{split}&V^{(\delta)}_{n_{1},n_{2}}\sim\frac{g}{a}\,\frac{1}{n}\times\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}1&\text{for}&n>n_{\rm min}(x_{0})\\
0&\text{otherwise}&\end{array}\right\}\end{split}

with

\displaystyle n\sim n_{1}\sim n_{2}\,\,;

this estimate is consistent with a more accurate expression (
11
).

The (
11
) scaling inspires the following form for the perturbation:

\displaystyle V^{(\delta,\,\text{array})}(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}V^{(\delta)}(x|\,g,\,(x_{0})_{i})\,\,,

(12)

with

(x_{0})_{i}=\Delta x\times i\,\,.

For the potential (
12
), we get, for the hopping constants (
7
)

J_{m}\sim\frac{g}{\sqrt{\tilde{n}}\Delta x}=\text{const}(m)\,\,,

the result we were hoping for.

In our numerical calculations, we replace the
\delta
-function by a finite width Gaussian:

\displaystyle V_{\sigma}(x|\,g,\,x_{0})=gD_{\sigma}(x-x_{0})\,\,,

(13)

with

\displaystyle D_{\sigma}(x)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}\sigma}e^{-\frac{x^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}}\,\,.

(14)

Accordingly, the infinite array of
\delta
-potentials is replaced by a finite array of Gaussian perturbation:

\displaystyle V^{\text{(array})}(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{i_{\rm max}}V_{\sigma}(x|\,g,\,(x_{0})_{i})\,\,,

(15)

with

(x_{0})_{i}=\Delta x\times i\,\,,

and
with

\sigma_{i}=\Delta\sigma\times i\,\,.

IV
Numerical Results

Our numerical experiment consists of two parts. First, in order to construct the logarithmic potential we use the QM-SUSY approach described in
Cassettari, Mussardo, and Trombettoni 2023
. We solve the associated Riccatti equations numerically, using standard SciPy library methods
Virtanen et al. 2020
. Second, we solve the one-dimensional Schrödinger equation with the obtained potential (and perturbation given in the text) using fast Fourier transform-based spectral method for spatial discretization and a fourth-order symplectic integrator
scheme for the time evolution. We use 16384 points for both spatial and temporal grids, to ensure that the relative error between the lowest numerical eigenvalues of the potential and the correct eigenvalues is not larger that
10^{-6}
.

We used the perturbation (
15
) with
g=0.01
,
\Delta x=2.82
,
\Delta\sigma=0.25
, and
i_{\rm max}=10
. The resulting numerical resonance cascade hopping constants read:

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\{J_{1,0},\,J_{2,1},\,J_{3,2},\,J_{4,3}\}=\\
&\quad\{0.00027,0.00049,0.00024,0.00011\}\times U_{0}\end{split}\,\,.

In the first experiment, all natural numbers
1\leq n\leq 120
were present
in the
\ln(n)
spectrum. The atomic population was able to travel freely among all five cascade levels, all the way to the highest available

We also designed a new potential where the energy level
n=9
was excluded. This created bottleneck in the middle of the
resonance cascade. In this case, the atomic population does not propagate further than
n=3
, undergoing Rabi oscillations between
n=1
and
n=3
.

V
Conclusion, Context, and Outlook

In this article, we attempted to create an atomic system where

(a)

the movement of the population between the energy levels under a periodic perturbation
occurs exclusively via exact resonances while at the same time the atomic population does explore
all
phase space reachable via the resonant transitions.

(b)

a possibility for an unlimited growth of energy is predicated by the truthfulness of a number-theoretical statement; such growth can be disrupted by a single violation of the statement.

In the example we looked at, the unlimited growth of energy required the closeness of the set of natural numbers (
including
zero) under multiplication. This was achieved using an atom in a logarithmic potential perturbed by a comb of narrow Gaussian peaks (in space) with periodically oscillating magnitudes (in time).

In the future, we would like to apply the scheme of the current article to a set of sums of two squares of natural numbers (including zero). This set is also known to be closed under multiplication, thanks to
the Diophantus-Brahmagupta-Fibonacci identity

Dudley 1969
(Lemma 1, p. 142).

Dynamical zeta functions for differentiable parabolic maps of the interval
Claudio Bonanno, Roberto Castorrini

Abstract
. This paper explores the domain of meromorphic extension for the dynamical zeta function associated to a class of one-dimensional differentiable parabolic maps featuring an indifferent fixed point. We establish the connection between this domain and the spectrum of the weighted transfer operators of the induced map. Furthermore, we discuss scenarios where meromorphic extensions occur beyond the confines of the natural disc of convergence of the dynamical zeta function.



1.
Introduction

In the last few years, the
dynamical zeta function
introduced by D. Ruelle in the late 1970s has proved to play a crucial role in the study of the statistical properties of hyperbolic dynamical systems, providing a powerful tool for understanding the underlying structure and behavior of such systems. Its definition is based on the distribution of the periodic orbits of the system, therefore the dynamical zeta functions can be considered a bridge between the microscopic and macroscopic aspects of the system.

One of the most interesting properties of the dynamical zeta function is that its poles are related with the eigenvalues of the associated transfer operator, another important tool in the thermodynamic formalism approach to dynamical systems.
The eigenvalues of the transfer operator, in turn, shed light on the stability and mixing properties of the system, offering a deeper understanding of its overall dynamics. The basic idea is that the smaller is the essential spectrum of the transfer operator, the broader is the domain over which the dynamical zeta function can be meromorphically extended. We refer the reader to
[
3
]
for a detailed discussion on the subject and for an exhaustive list of the literature on the first steps of the development of this theory.

It is clear from the basic idea sketched above that it is important to find a good space of functions on which the essential spectral radius of the transfer operator becomes smaller. This is typically obtained by looking at functions of increasing regularity

However, in not uniformly hyperbolic systems, capturing information on the dynamical zeta function becomes more challenging due to a lack of information in the spectrum of the associated transfer operator, for example the strategy of looking at the space of functions with high regularity does not work in this situation. An important case for which the classic approach fails is that of parabolic maps, i.e. maps with parabolic fixed points. The properties of the dynamical zeta function have been studied in a symbolic dynamic context equivalent to this case in
[
15

, but the direct approach to differentiable parabolic maps is to our knowledge still lacking.

A notable exception is the case of parabolic maps which have an analytic extension to a complex domain. This case has been studied in
[
24

for maps of the interval and in
[
21
]
for higher dimensional maps. In the analytic case the transfer operator of the map, or of its induction, has strong properties which allow for a complete understanding of the problem in the one-dimensional case. This technique has been used also in
[

for the Farey map and its relations with the Selberg zeta function on the modular surface. In the multidimensional case the results in
[
21
]
give only a partial meromorphic extension of the dynamical zeta function by using the powerful property of nuclearity for the transfer operator of the induced map.

In this paper we deal with the case of differentiable parabolic maps of the unit interval, under the simplifying assumption that the maps are piecewise monotone with full branches (see Section
2
for all the assumptions). By using induction and the thermodynamic formalism for uniformly hyperbolic systems, we first show that results similar to those in
[
15
]
and
[
21
]
hold in this case. Then we introduce a method to further extend the dynamical zeta function under an additional assumption on the potential. Our conjecture is that this method can be improved to obtain a complete understanding of the extension of the dynamical zeta function also in the differentiable context.

The structure of the paper unfolds as follows: Section

T

Remark 2.4
.

By definition,
\phi_{n}(x)\in A_{n}
for all

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\,\min_{x\in A_{i}}\,v(x)\leq w(\phi_{n}(x))\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\,\max_{x\in A_{i}}\,v(x).

Therefore, it follows

In the following we need to consider iterates of

(2.7)

\displaystyle\phi_{\beta}:=\phi_{\beta_{1}}\circ\phi_{\beta_{2}}\circ\dots\circ\phi_{\beta_{m}},

\displaystyle W_{\beta}(x):=\exp\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{m}\,w(\phi_{{}_{\beta_{j}\dots\beta_{m}}}(x))\Big).

The main results use the following quantities:

(2.8)

\Lambda_{m}(z):=\sup_{x\in(0,1)}\,\sum_{\beta\in\mathbb{N}^{m}}\,|z|^{|\beta|}\,W_{\beta}(x),

and the limit

3.
The spectrum of the transfer operators

In this section we study the spectral properties of the transfer operators of
T
and of the corresponding induced map
G
on the space of differentiable functions. The results for
G
are expected by the previous literature on the subject. We give some proofs for completeness.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let (H1)-(H7) hold. Then for all
z\in D:=\{|z|\leq 1\}
, the power series
\mathcal{Q}_{w}(z)
converges to a bounded operator from
C^{k}([0,1])
to
C^{k}([0,1])
. In addition, recalling (
2.8
) and (
2.9
), we have for all
z\in D
:

(i)

The limit

\Lambda(z)=\lim_{m\to\infty}\,[\Lambda_{m}(z)]^{\frac{1}{m}}

exists and
\Lambda(z)\leq c_{k}
;

(ii)

The essential spectral radius of
\mathcal{Q}_{w}(z)
on
C^{k}([0,1])
satisfies
r_{ess}(\mathcal{Q}_{w}(z)|_{C^{k}})\leq\rho^{-k}\Lambda(z)
;

(iii)

If
z\in(0,1]
then
\Lambda(z)
is an eigenvalue of
\mathcal{Q}_{w}(z)
on
C^{k}([0,1])
.

Proof.

The proof of the first assertion goes as follows.
Let us write
\mathcal{Q}_{w}(z)
as

(\mathcal{Q}_{w}(z)f)(x)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\,z^{n}\,(Q_{n,w}f)(x),

where

(3.1)

Q_{n,w}f:=(e^{w}\cdot f)\circ\phi_{n},\quad n\geq 1.

Then we estimate
\|Q_{n,w}f\|_{k}
for all
n\geq 1
. We use two formulas to compute the derivatives of the product and composition of functions. One is the well known Leibniz’s formula

(f\cdot g)^{(j)}=\sum_{i=0}^{j}\,{j\choose i}\,f^{(i)}\,g^{(j-i)}.

The other is the Faà di Bruno’s formula (see e.g.
[
12
]
). Given a multi-index
a=(a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{j})\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{j}
the formula writes

(f\circ\phi)^{(j)}=\sum_{s=1}^{j}\,(f^{(s)}\circ\phi)\,\sum_{a\in B_{j,s}}\,\frac{j!}{a_{1}!\dots a_{j}!}\,\prod_{\ell=1}^{j}\,\Big(\frac{1}{\ell!}\,\phi^{(\ell)}\Big)^{a_{\ell}},

where
B_{j,s}=\{a\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{j}\,:\,a_{1}+\dots+a_{j}=s,\,a_{1}+2a_{2}+\dots+ja_{j}=j\}
. Thus for all
j=1,\dots,k
,

\|(Q_{n,w}f)^{(j)}\|_{\infty}\leq\sum_{i=0}^{j}\,{j\choose i}\,\|(e^{w\circ\phi_{n}})^{(j-i)}\|_{\infty}\,\|(f\circ\phi_{n})^{(i)}\|_{\infty}

and

\displaystyle\|(f\circ\phi_{n})^{(j)}\|_{\infty}\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{s=1}^{j}\,\|f\|_{s}\,\sum_{a\in B_{j,s}}\,const(j,s)\,\prod_{\ell=1}^{j}\,\|\phi_{n}^{(\ell)}\|_{\infty}^{a_{\ell}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle const(j)\,\sum_{s=1}^{j}\,\|f\|_{s}\,\|\phi_{n}\|_{j}^{j}\leq\,const(j)\,\|f\|_{j}\,\|\phi_{n}\|_{j}^{j}.

Therefore for all
n\geq 1

\|Q_{n,w}f\|_{k}\leq const(k)\,\|e^{w\circ\phi_{n}}\|_{k}\,\|\phi_{n}\|_{k}^{k}\,\|f\|_{k}.

It follows by (H7) that for all
z\in D

\|\mathcal{Q}_{w}(z)f\|_{k}\leq const(k)\,\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\,|z|^{n}\,\|e^{w\circ\phi_{n}}\|_{k}\,\|\phi_{n}\|_{k}^{k}\,\|f\|_{k}\leq const(k)\,\|f\|_{k}.

Hence
\mathcal{Q}_{w}(z)
converges absolutely on
D
to a bounded operator from
C^{k}([0,1])
to
C^{k}([0,1])
.

(i) By definition, for all
z\in D
and
m\geq 1
,

\displaystyle\Lambda_{m}(z)\leq

\displaystyle\Big\|\sum_{\beta_{1},..,\beta_{m-1}=1}^{\infty}\,|z|^{\beta_{1}+\beta_{2}+..+\beta_{m-1}}\,\exp\Big(\sum_{\ell=1}^{m-1}\,w\circ\phi_{\ell}\Big)\Big\|_{\infty}\,\sum_{\beta_{m}=1}^{\infty}\,|z|^{\beta_{m}}\,\|e^{w\circ\beta_{m}}\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c_{k}\,\Big\|\sum_{\beta_{1},..,\beta_{m-1}=1}^{\infty}\,|z|^{\beta_{1}+..+\beta_{m-1}}\,\exp\Big(\sum_{\ell=1}^{m-1}\,w\circ\phi_{\ell}\Big)\Big\|_{\infty}=c_{k}\,\Lambda_{m-1}(z),

hence
\Lambda_{m}(z)\leq c_{k}^{m}
. In addition, it is immediate to show that
\Lambda_{m+m^{\prime}}(z)\leq\Lambda_{m}(z)\cdot\Lambda_{m^{\prime}}(z)
, hence
\Lambda(z)
exists and satisfies
\Lambda(z)\leq c_{k}
.

(ii) follows as in
[
16
, Thm. 3.1]
(see also
[
9
, Thm. 6]
and
[
2
, Thm. 2.5]
). The main idea is to notice that for all
j=1,\dots,k
one can write as above

(\mathcal{Q}_{w}(z)f)^{(j)}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\,z^{n}\,e^{w\circ\phi_{n}}\,(f^{(j)}\circ\phi_{n})\,(\phi_{n}^{\prime})^{j}+\mathcal{R}_{z,j,w}f\,,

with

\rho^{-\min\{p,q\}}\,\Lambda(z)

Let’s begin with the case
m=1
. For
p=0
we have

\begin{split}&\|\mathcal{M}_{w}(z)f\|_{0,q}\\
&=\sup_{x\in(0,1)}\,\sup_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\xi\in C^{q}_{0}(0,1)}}{{\|\xi\|_{q}\leq 1}}}\,\sum_{n\geq 1}\,z^{n}\,e^{w(\phi_{n}(x))}\,\int_{0}^{1}\,z^{1+\tau(y)}\,e^{w(y)}\,|G^{\prime}(y)|\,f(\phi_{n}(x),G(y))\,\xi(y)\,dy\end{split}

and since

\begin{split}&\int_{0}^{1}\,z^{1+\tau(y)}\,e^{w(y)}\,|G^{\prime}(y)|\,f(\phi_{n}(x),G(y))\,\xi(y)\,dy=\\

&=\int_{0}^{1}\,f(\phi_{n}(x),s)\,(\mathcal{Q}_{w}(z)\xi)(s)\,ds,\end{split}

and by (

)

\sum_{n\geq 1}\,z^{n}\,e^{w(\phi_{n}(x))}=\Lambda_{1}(z),

we use that
\|\mathcal{Q}_{w}(z)\xi\|_{q}\leq const(q)\,\|\xi\|_{q}
to obtain (

) for
m=1
and
p=0
.

For

we need to control the term involving
\partial_{x}\mathcal{M}_{w}(z)f
. Since

\displaystyle\partial_{x}(\mathcal{M}_{w}(z)f)(x,y)=\sum_{n\geq 1}

\displaystyle z^{n+1+\tau(y)}\,e^{w(\phi_{n}(x))+w(y)}\,|G^{\prime}(y)|\,\phi^{\prime}_{n}(x)\,\cdot

\displaystyle\cdot\,\Big(w^{\prime}(\phi_{n}(x))\,f(\phi_{n}(x),G(y))+\partial_{x}f(\phi_{n}(x),G(y))\Big),

we have

(4.8)

\Big|\int_{0}^{1}\,\partial_{x}(\mathcal{M}_{w}(z)f)(x,y)\,\xi(y)\,dy\Big|\leq const(w)\,\|M_{w}f\|_{0,q}+\rho^{-1}\,\|M_{w}(z)(\partial_{x}f)\|_{0,q}.

Then we use (

) and the definition of
\|\cdot\|_{1,q}
to write

\|M_{w}(z)(\partial_{x}f)\|_{0,q}\leq const\cdot\,\Lambda_{1}(z)\,\|f\|_{1,q},

and we show that

\|M_{w}f\|_{0,q}\leq const\cdot\Lambda_{1}(z)\,\Big(\rho^{-q}\,\|f\|_{1,q}+\rho^{q}\,\|f\|_{0,q+1}\Big),

to obtain in (

)

\Big|\int_{0}^{1}\,\partial_{x}(\mathcal{M}_{w}(z)f)(x,y)\,\xi(y)\,dy\Big|\leq const\cdot\Lambda_{1}(z)\,\Big(\rho^{-1}\,\|f\|_{1,q}+\rho^{-q}\,\|f\|_{1,q}+\rho^{q}\,\|f\|_{0,q+1}\Big)

which gives (
4.7
) for
m=1
and
p=1
. It remains to prove (
4.9
). Consider a fixed
\gamma:(0,1)\to[0,\infty)
of class
C_{0}^{q+1}
with
\int_{0}^{1}\,\gamma(s)\,ds=1
. For
\varepsilon>0
small let

for which we have

where the constants do not depend on

\begin{split}&\int_{0}^{1}\,(\mathcal{M}_{w}(z)f)(x,y)\,\xi(y)\,dy=\\

5.
The meromorphic extension outside the unit disc

Applying Theorem
2.5
, the meromorphic extension of
\zeta_{{}_{T,v}}(z)
strongly depends on the set of
z
for which
\mathcal{Q}_{w}(z)
is defined. For the moment we can define
\mathcal{Q}_{w}(z)
on
D=\{|z|\leq 1\}
and all the results are therefore restricted to this domain. We now show a strategy to extend the definition of
\mathcal{Q}_{w}(z)
to larger domains.

We recall the following result by LeRoy and Lindelöf as stated in
[
1
]
. Let us set

\displaystyle\Delta_{c}:=\left\{z\in\mathbb{C}\,:\,|\arg z|<\frac{c}{2}\right\},\quad\text{for $c\in[0,2\pi)$;}

\displaystyle\Pi:=\left\{z\in\mathbb{C}\,:\,\text{Re($z$)}\geq 0\right\}.

Let us denote by
H(\Pi)
the set of holomorphic functions in some neighborhood of
\Pi
. Then we say that
h\in H(\Pi)
is
of exponential type
e_{{}_{\Pi}}\in[0,+\infty)
on
\Pi
if

e_{{}_{\Pi}}=\limsup_{z\to\infty,\,z\in\Pi}\,|z|^{-1}\,\log^{+}|h(z)|.

Then the following result holds for the analytic continuation of power series.

Theorem 5.1
.

For a function
h\in H(\Pi)
, assume that the power series

q(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\,h(n)\,z^{n}

converges in the unit disc
D
. If
h
is of exponential type
e_{{}_{\Pi}}<\pi
on
\Pi
then
q(z)
admits an analytic continuation to the sector
\mathbb{C}\setminus\Delta_{2e_{{}_{\Pi}}}
.

In the case
e_{{}_{\Pi}}=0
the theorem yields a sufficient condition for the analytic continuation of
q(z)
to the cut complex plane
\mathbb{C}\setminus(1,\infty)
.

Theorem
5.1
has been improved in
[
1
]
by stating a necessary and sufficient condition on a function
h\in H(\Pi)
for the analytic continuation of the power series
q(z)
to a sector
\mathbb{C}\setminus\Delta_{c}
. However, for our aims it is enough to consider the sufficient condition considered by LeRoy and Lindelöf. It is shown in
[
1
]
that the proof of Theorem
5.1
follows by choosing for each compact set
K\subset\mathbb{C}\setminus\Delta_{2e_{{}_{\Pi}}}
an unbounded contour
\Gamma
on which the integral

\int_{\Gamma}\,\frac{h(s)\,z^{s}}{e^{2\pi is}-1}\,ds

converges uniformly to
q(z)
for all
z\in K
.

Let
f\in C^{k}([0,1])
and consider the series

(\mathcal{Q}_{w}(z)f)(x)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\,z^{n}\,e^{w(\phi_{n}(x))}\,f(\phi_{n}(x))

which, by assumption (H7), converges for
z\in D
uniformly in
x
. Fixed
x\in[0,1]
, we study the existence of a function
h\in H(\Pi)
such that
h(n)=e^{w(\phi_{n}(x))}\,f(\phi_{n}(x))
for all
n\geq 1
.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
a_{n}=e^{w(\phi_{n}(x))}\,f(\phi_{n}(x))
and assume that
v(0)<0
. Then for all
\varepsilon>0
, the function

h(s):=\frac{\sin(\pi s)}{\pi}\,\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\,(-1)^{i}\,\frac{a_{i}}{s-i}\,e^{(1-\varepsilon)\,(s-i)\,v(0)}

converges in
\mathbb{C}
and satisfies
h(n)=a_{n}
for all
n\geq 1
. Moreover it is of exponential type
e_{{}_{\Pi}}=\max\{0,\pi+(1-\varepsilon)v(0)\}<\pi
.

Proof.

Given the sequence
\{a_{n}\}
, an application of the Pringsheim interpolation formula gives the function
h
we are looking for. In details, the function
g(s)=\sin(\pi s)
has zeroes of first order at all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, therefore the formal power series

\tilde{h}(s):=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\,\frac{g(s)}{g^{\prime}(i)\,(s-i)}

satisfies
\tilde{h}(n)=1
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Since
g^{\prime}(i)=(-1)^{i}\,\pi
and
a_{n}\sim e^{n\,v(0)}\,f(0)
as showed in Remark
2.4
, the series defining
h(s)
is convergent in
\mathbb{C}
and satisfies
h(n)=a_{n}
for all
n\geq 1
.

In addition, for all
s\in\Pi
we have

|h(s)|=|\sin(\pi s)|\,e^{(1-\varepsilon)\,v(0)\,\Re(s)}\,\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\,\frac{|a_{i}|}{\pi\,|s-i|}\,e^{-(1-\varepsilon)\,v(0)\,i}.

Since the series converges if
\varepsilon>0
and
|\sin(\pi s)|\leq 2\,e^{\pi\,|s|}
, we obtain that
e_{{}_{\Pi}}=\max\{0,\pi+(1-\varepsilon)v(0)\}
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
2.6
. Put together Theorem
2.5
and Theorem
5.1
for all
\varepsilon>0
. ∎

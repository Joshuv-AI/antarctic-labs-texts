The Intercalation Chemistry of the Disordered RockSalt Li 3 ​ V 2 ​ O 5 \text{Li}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}\text{V}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{5}}} Anode from Cluster Expansions and Machine Learning Interatomic Potentials
Xingyu Guo, Chi Chen, Shyue Ping Ong

Abstract
Disordered rocksalt (DRX) Li 3 ​ V 2 ​ O 5 \text{Li}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}\text{V}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{5}}} is a promising candidate for anode in rechargeable lithium-ion batteries because of its ideal low voltage, high rate capability, and superior cycling stability. Herein, we presents a comprehensive study of intercalation chemistry of the DRX- Li 3 ​ V 2 ​ O 5 \text{Li}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}\text{V}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{5}}} anode using density functional theory calculations combined with machine learning cluster expansions and interatomic potentials. The predicted voltage profile of the disordered Li 3 ​ V 2 ​ O 5 \text{Li}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}\text{V}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{5}}} anode at room temperature based on Monte Carlo simulations with a fitted cluster expansion model is in excellent agreement with experiments. In contrast to previous DFT results, we find that Li ions predominately intercalate into tetrahedral sites during charging, while the majority of Li and V ions at octahedral sites remain stable. In addition, MD simulations with a fitted moment tensor potential attribute the fast-charging capability of DRX- Li 3 ​ V 2 ​ O 5 \text{Li}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}\text{V}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{5}}} to the facile diffusivity of Li + \text{Li}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{+}} via tetrahedral - octahedral - tetrahedral pathway. We further suggest tuning the Li:V ratio as a means to trade off increased lithiation capacity and decreased anode voltage in this system. This work provides in-depth insights into the high-performance DRX- Li 3 ​ V 2 ​ O 5 \text{Li}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}\text{V}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{5}}} anode, and paves the way to the discovery of other disordered anode materials.

Introduction

Rocksalt oxides have been extensively studied as electrodes for rechargeable lithium-ion batteries (LIBs).
Clément et al. 2020
As the name implies, the
\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{\hskip 0.90417pt--\hskip 0.90417pt}}
anions in rocksalt oxides are arranged in a close-packed face-centered-cubic (fcc) framework, with the cations occupying the tetrahedral and octahedral interstitial sites, as shown in Figure
1
(a). For instance, the common layered transition metal (M) oxide
\text{LiMO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
cathode used in LIBs is formed by an ordered arrangement of Li and M in this framework. In the past decade or so, lithium-rich disordered rocksalt (DRX) oxides have emerged as a promising class of alternative electrode materials with extraordinarily high specific capacities and high rate capabilities.
Lee et al. 2014
;
Urban et al. 2016
;
Clément et al. 2020
;
Kitchaev et al. 2018

While DRX materials have been extensively studied as cathodes, relatively few have been explored as anodes.
Liu et al. 2020
;
Barnes et al. 2022
;
Xiong et al. 2011
Among the most promising is the DRX-
\text{Li}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}\text{V}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{5}}}
recently reported by
Liu et al. 2020
. The DRX-
\text{Li}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}\text{V}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{5}}}
anode operates at a near-optimal average voltage of
\sim 0.6
V vs
\text{Li}\text{/}\text{Li}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{+}}
- high enough to alleviate the safety concerns attributed to Li plating that occurs during fast charge/discharge of the commercial graphite anode used in LIBs. At the same time, it is substantially lower than the 1.5 V operating voltage of lithium titanate, thereby yielding a much higher energy density.

Despite its great promise, there remains major ambiguity on the intercalation chemistry of DRX-
\text{Li}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}\text{V}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{5}}}
. Previously, Zheng et al.
Zhang et al. 2017
proposed that the related DRX-
\text{Li}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{0.78+x\/}}}\text{V}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{0.75}}}\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
anode undergoes a conversion-type reaction, in which the single phase material converts to
\text{VO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
and
\text{Li}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{O}
as it is discharged to 0.55 V vs
\text{Li}\text{/}\text{Li}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{+}}
. However, previous density functional theory (DFT) calculations by the present authors attributed the low voltage and high rate capability of DRX-
\text{Li}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}\text{V}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{5}}}
to a redistributive lithium intercalation mechanism with low energy barriers.
Liu et al. 2020
These initial conclusions were reached based on 0K DFT calculations with small model cells, which did not fully explore the configurational space of the DRX anode at finite temperatures.

In this work, we revisit the intercalation chemistry of the DRX-
\text{Li}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3+x\/}}}\text{V}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{5}}}
(
0\leq x\leq 2)
) at finite temperatures using large-scale simulations with machine-learned energy models. Monte Carlo simulations using a fitted cluster expansion model predict that Li primarily inserts into the tetrahedral sites of DRX-
\text{Li}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}\text{V}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{5}}}
while Li occupancy in octahedral sites remains largely unchanged, in contrast with earlier DFT results. Molecular dynamics (MD) simulations using a machine learning interatomic potential reveal that
\text{Li}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{+}}
diffusivity reaches the maximum at intermediate states of charge, but sharply decreases at the start of charge/discharge. The exceptionally high rate capability and superior cycling stability of DRX-
\text{Li}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}\text{V}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{5}}}
anode are a result of facile diffusion of Li ions through tetrahedral - octahedral - tetrahedral pathway, consistent with previous nudged elastic band calculations.

3.2
Monte Carlo simulations

Monte Carlo (MC) simulations were performed using the fitted cluster expansion model. A 5

\times

into DRX structures is strongly affected by the distribution of the tetrahedral sites with different local environments and connectivity. The Li in tetrahedral sites share faces with four neighboring octahedral sites. As shown in previous works,
Lee et al. 2014
;
Urban et al. 2016
;
Liu et al. 2020
;
Clément et al. 2020
the Li/transition metal occupancy of these neighboring octahedral sites have a strong influence on the local site occupancies and
\text{Li}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{+}}
migration barriers. The local environment of a tetrahedral site can be denoted by
n
-V (
n
= 0
\sim
4), where
n
represents the number of face-shared V atoms. Figure
5
(a) shows the distribution of the calculated

\text{Li}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{tet}}}

\text{Li}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{tet}}}

\text{Li}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{tet}}}
and the neighboring
\text{Li}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{oct}}

Conclusions

A major conclusion from this work is that the lithium insertion mechanism in
\text{Li}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}\text{V}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{5}}}
depends greatly on the initial Li/V disorder. For highly ordered Li/V arrangements with lower energies, Li insertion occurs via a redistribution mechanism with the formation of highly distorted structures and accompanied by large volume changes. This is consistent with previous DFT results, but inconsistent with experimental observations for the DRX-
\text{Li}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}\text{V}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{5}}}
electrode.
Liu et al. 2020
In contrast, Li/V disorder leads to Li inserting primarily into the tetrahedral sites, while retaining a cubic-like structure with small volume changes. The MC-predicted voltage profile based on this latter mechanism is also in much better agreement with the experimental voltage profile.

The
\text{Li}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{+}}
insertion mechanism and transport depends on the local environment around each tetrahedral site and the distribution of sites. Our results show that
\text{Li}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{+}}
in 0-V sites are the most energetically favorable and have the lowest migration barrier and
\geq

The above analysis suggest that one potential avenue to tune the electrochemical properties of the DRX anode is by varying the Li:V ratio in
\text{[}\text{Li}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{1\hskip 0.90417pt--\hskip 0.90417ptx\/}}}\text{V}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{x\/}}}\text{]}\text{}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{oct}}\text{O}
. Increasing the Li:V ratio would increase the fraction of 0-V/1-V tetrahedral sites, but at the price of increasing the V oxidation state and hence, the anode voltage. Conversely, lowering the Li:V ratio would decrease lithiation capacity but also decrease the anode voltage. The optimal ratio would depend on the specific application, for example, whether a high rate or a high energy density anode is prioritized.

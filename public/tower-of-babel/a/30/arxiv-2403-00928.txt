The limit points of the bass notes of arithmetic hyperbolic surfaces
Michael Magee

Abstract
We prove that the limit points of the bass notes of arithmetic hyperbolic surfaces are the interval [ 0 , 1 4 ] \left[0,\frac{1}{4}\right] .

Related works

Graphs.
As we mentioned before, the analog of Theorem
1.1

for all
d
-regular graphs was proved by Alon and Wei
[
AW23
]
.

Our approach certainly has the same overall strategy as Alon–Wei
(a type of discrete continuity between two known end points) but differs
in several ways:

•

We use two–covers rather than flipping edges in graphs. While it
is possible flipping edges could have worked in our setting, using
two-covers provides an alternative method that possibly extends more
easily to more general manifolds e.g. hyperbolic three-manifolds.
We plan to return to this topic.

•

We produce (doubly) exponentially stronger bounds on the type of continuity
one can obtain. This is at least partly because we relax girth conditions
to tangle-free conditions, but are able to use them in a similar way.

•

Obviously, we have to deal with all complications arising from the
geometric setting, with the especial complication that our surfaces
are not compact (this complication was chosen in favor of having non-free
fundamental groups, which is a separate issue in the compact case).

In the setting of

to use two-covers as in this paper instead of flips.

Lattice point count

Consider that

In summary,

(5.2)

Let

with respect to the generators

from

cuts a side of the tiling
of
\mathbb{H}

\Gamma

Fix
\gamma

Now fix

(6.1)

7
Bumping off the cusps

We need the following localization formula due to Ismagilov–Morgan–Simon–Sigal

[
CFKS87
, Thm. 3.2]
.

Theorem 7.1

(IMS localization formula)
.

Suppose
(M,g)
is a Riemannian manifold and
\{J_{i}\}_{i\in\mathcal{I}}

are a family of smooth functions
J_{i}:M\to[0,1]
such that

1.

\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}}J_{i}^{2}\equiv 1

2.

on any compact set

, only finitely many
J_{i}
are
not zero

3.

\sup_{x\in M}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}}|\nabla J_{i}(x)|^{2}<\infty
.

Then

\Delta=\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}}J_{i}\Delta J_{i}-\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}}|\nabla J_{i}|^{2}.

Above,
\Delta
and
\nabla
are the (non-negative) Laplace-Beltrami
and gradient operators defined w.r.t.
g
.

Suppose that we are given
X_{\phi}
satisfying
Connected

and
GTF(
c\log n
).
Given

and

,

and

\displaystyle\equiv 1\quad r\geq 3,

\displaystyle\in C^{\infty}.

Let

\displaystyle=\langle\mathfrak{J}f,f\rangle+\langle\Delta f,f\rangle-\langle\Delta J_{1}f,J_{1}f\rangle-\langle\Delta J_{2}f,J_{2}f\rangle

\displaystyle=|\nabla J_{0}|^{2}+|\nabla J_{1}|^{2}=y^{2}\left(\frac{\partial J_{0}}{\partial y}\right)^{2}+y^{2}\left(\frac{\partial J_{1}}{\partial y}\right)^{2}.

Since derivatives of

Exactly the same bound holds in

and hence for

,

Using this in (

now dividing by

and using (

gives

\frac{\langle\Delta f^{\prime},f^{\prime}\rangle}{\|f^{\prime}\|^{2}}-\lambda\ll_{c^{\prime},\lambda_{0}}n^{-c^{\prime}\sqrt{\frac{1}{4}-\lambda}}.

(7.8)

Recall that

Appendix A
Common fixed points of subgroups under random homomorphisms to permutation
groups

Let
\Gamma
denote a finitely generated free group with a fixed
basis
\mathcal{B}
and
H
a finitely generated (necessarily free) subgroup.
For
\phi\in\mathrm{Hom}(\Gamma,S_{n})
, let
\mathrm{fix}_{H}
denote the
number of points in
[n]
fixed by every element of
\phi(H)\leq S_{n}
.
For a function
F
on
\mathrm{Hom}(\Gamma,S_{n})
we write
\mathbb{E}_{n}[F]

for the expected value of
F
with respect to the uniform probability
measure on the finite set
\mathrm{Hom}(\Gamma,S_{n})
. Let
\ell_{\mathcal{B}}(H)

denote the minimum, over all bases
\mathcal{B}^{\prime}
of
H
, of the sum of word
lengths of
\mathcal{B}^{\prime}
w.r.t.
\mathcal{B}
.

Proposition A.1
.

If
\mathrm{rank}(H)\geq 2
and
n\geq\ell_{\mathcal{B}}(H)^{3}
,
then

\mathbb{E}_{n}(\mathrm{fix}_{H})\ll\frac{\ell_{\mathcal{B}}(H)^{6}}{n}.

Proof.

Let
m=\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}\ell_{\mathcal{B}}(H)
. In
[
PP15
, Lemma 6.4]
, Puder and
Parzanchevski show that there is a finite set
\mathcal{Q}_{H}
of rational
functions, depending on
H
, such that:

•

\mathbb{E}_{n}[\mathrm{fix}_{H}]=\sum_{q\in\mathcal{Q}_{H}}q(n)
for
n\geq\ell_{\mathcal{B}}(H)

•

each
q\in\mathcal{Q}_{H}
is of the form

q(n)=\frac{(n)_{V(q)}}{\prod_{b\in\mathcal{B}}(n)_{E_{b}(q)}}

where
V(q),E_{b}(q)\leq\ell_{B}(H)
and
(n)_{a}
is the falling
Pochhammer symbol. Hence since (e.g.
[
MNP22
, Lemma 5.23]
)
if
a\leq\frac{1}{2}n

n^{a}\left(1-\frac{a^{2}}{n}\right)\leq(n)_{a}\leq n^{a}

(A.1)

we get for
n\geq 2\ell_{\mathcal{B}}(H)^{2}
that

q(n)\asymp n^{V(q)-\sum_{b\in\mathcal{B}}E_{b}(q)}

in the regime of
n\to\infty
. Let
\chi(q)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}V(q)-\sum_{b\in\mathcal{B}}E_{b}(q)
.

•

For
q\in\mathcal{Q}_{H},

\chi(q)\leq 1-\mathrm{rank}(H)
.

In fact, Puder and Parzanchevski give a much more detailed description
of the set
\mathcal{Q}_{H}
above, but this will not be needed here. It follows
immediately that for fixed
H
,
\mathbb{E}_{n}[\mathrm{fix}_{H}]=O(n^{\chi(H)})

but we need a version of this statement that is uniform over (mildly)
varying
H
. It is possible to do this using the detailed description
of
H
as was done in Puder
[
Pud15
, §5]
but we do not need
such fine estimates so can bypass this by analytic methods. A variant
form of the following trick appeared in
[
MNP22
]
for
a similar purpose.

Suppose
n\geq m^{3}
and note from (
A.1
) that

m^{3}\geq\mathbb{E}_{m^{3}}[\mathrm{fix}_{H}]\gg\sum_{q\in\mathcal{Q}_{H}}(m^{3})^{\chi(q)}.

Then supposing
\mathrm{rank}(H)\geq 2

\mathbb{E}_{n}[\mathrm{fix}_{H}]\ll\sum_{q\in\mathcal{Q}_{H}}n^{\chi(q)}\leq\sum_{\chi=-1}^{-\infty}m^{3(1-\chi)}n^{\chi}\ll\frac{m^{6}}{n}

as required.
∎

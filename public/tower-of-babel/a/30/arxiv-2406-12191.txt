Quantum K K -invariants via Quot schemes I
SHUBHAM SINHA, Ming Zhang

Abstract
. We study the virtual Euler characteristics of sheaves over Quot schemes of curves, establishing that these invariants fit into a topological quantum field theory (TQFT) valued in ℤ ⁡ [ [ q ] ] \mathbb{Z}[[q]] . Utilizing Quot scheme compactifications alongside the TQFT framework, we derive presentations of the small quantum K K -ring of the Grassmannian. Our approach offers a new method for finding explicit formulas for quantum K K -invariants.



1.
Introduction

1.1.
Overview

Quantum
K
-theory was introduced by Givental
[
22
]
and Lee

[
32
]
as a
K
-theoretic generalization of Gromov–Witten theory.
Let
X
be a projective manifold.
Quantum
K
-invariants (or sometimes referred to as the
K
-theoretic Gromov–Witten invariants)
are defined as Euler characteristics of
K
-theory classes on the Kontsevich’s moduli spaces of stable maps to
X
.
Using 2-pointed and 3-pointed, genus zero quantum
K
-invariants of
X
, one can define an associative product, called the quantum product,
on the
K
-group of
X
.
The resulting algebra is called the (small) quantum
K
-ring.

Let
\mathrm{Gr}(r,N)
be the Grassmann variety of
r
-planes in
\mathbb{C}^{N}
.
The small quantum
K
-ring of Grassmannians was first studied by Buch and Mihalcea in
[
13
]
.
Buch–Mihalcea’s results rely on the analysis of the geometry of the Kontsevich moduli space
\overline{M}_{0,3}(\mathrm{Gr}(r,N),d)
, which compactifies the moduli space
\mathrm{Mor}_{d}(\mathbb{P}^{1},\mathrm{Gr}(r,N))
of degree-
d
morphisms from
\mathbb{P}^{1}
to
\mathrm{Gr}(r,N)
.
Note that the moduli space
\mathrm{Mor}_{d}(\mathbb{P}^{1},\mathrm{Gr}(r,N))
has a simpler compactification, the Grothendieck’s Quot
scheme
[
26
]
.
In the early study of
quantum cohomology
of Grassmannians, many results have been obtained by using the Quot scheme compactification
[
10
,
11
,
8
]
.

In this paper, we show that virtual Euler characteristics over Quot schemes of any genus
g
curve form a
\mathbb{Z}[[q]]
-valued
(1+1)
-Topological Quantum Field Theory (TQFT). Additionally, we establish that the quantum
K
-ring

\mathrm{QK}(\mathrm{Gr}(r,N))
can be fully reconstructed using the Quot schemes of
\mathbb{P}^{1}
.
In particular, our approach provides a new way to derive a ring presentation for
\mathrm{QK}(\mathrm{Gr}(r,N))
. We observe that fundamental properties of
\mathrm{QK}(\mathrm{Gr}(r,N))
, such as finiteness and
S_{3}
-symmetry
of the structure constants, are geometrically elucidated using this approach.

In part II
[
52
]
,
we establish explicit formulas for the Euler characteristics over Quot schemes of curves,
similar to the Vafa–Intriligator formula for cohomological invariants.
For the genus zero case, we employ torus localization for calculation, inspired by
[
44
]
in
K
-theory (see also
[
39
]
for cohomology calculations).
For higher-genus cases, we utilize the TQFT structure developed here.
In this paper, some results critically depend on the vanishing results in Theorem
1.11
from
[
52
, Theorem 1.6]
.

1.2.
Quantum
K
-ring of the Grassmannian

Throughout this paper, we set
X=\mathrm{Gr}(r,N)
and
k=N-r
. Let

\mathrm{P}_{r,k}
be the set of partitions contained in the
r\times k
rectangular partition. The complement partition for
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{r})\in\mathrm{P}_{r,k}
is given by
\lambda^{*}=(k-\lambda_{r},\dots,k-\lambda_{1})
.

Let
K(X)
be the Grothendieck group of coherent sheaves (or, equivalently,
vector bundles since
X
is smooth).
For any partition
\lambda\in\mathrm{P}_{r,k}
, let
X_{\lambda}
denote the Schubert cycle for a fixed complete flag of
\mathbb{C}^{N}

(see Section
2
for precise definitions).
The
K
-theory classes of the structure sheaves of these Schubert cycles

\cO_{\lambda}:=[\cO_{X_{\lambda}}]
form a
\mathbb{Z}
-linear basis for
K(X)
. The structure constants
c_{\lambda,\mu}^{\nu}
for the product

\cO_{\lambda}\cdot\cO_{\mu}=\sum_{\nu\in\mathrm{P}_{r,k}}c_{\lambda,\mu}^{\nu}\cO_{\nu}

in
K(X)
are given by the
K
-theoretic Littlewood-Richardson rule in
[
15
]
,
which involves counting set-valued tableaux.

The
K
-group of Grassmannian admits another natural basis consisting of Schur bundles. Let
S\subset\mathcal{O}_{X}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{N}
be the tautological subbundle over
X
.
For any partition
\lambda
, we have a corresponding vector bundle
\mathbb{S}^{\lambda}(S)
, where
\mathbb{S}^{\lambda}
is the Schur functor associated to
\lambda
. For example,

\mathbb{S}^{(m)}(S)=\Sym^{m}S
and
\mathbb{S}^{(1)^{m}}(S)=\wedge^{m}S
where
(1)^{m}=(1,\dots,1)
.
Let
\rm{A}=({\rm{A}}_{\lambda}^{\nu})_{\lambda,\nu\in\mathrm{P}_{r,k}}
be the transition matrix between the Schur
basis
\{\mathbb{S}^{\lambda}(S):\lambda\in\mathrm{P}_{r,k}\}
and the Schubert basis
\{\mathcal{O}_{\nu}:\nu\in\mathrm{P}_{r,k}\}
:

(1)

\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}=\sum_{\nu\in\mathrm{P}_{r,k}}{\rm{A}}_{\lambda}^{\nu}\ \mathbb{S}^{\nu}(S)

for any
\lambda\in\mathrm{P}_{r,k}
.

Next we recall the definition of Givental–Lee’s quantum
K
-invariants over
X
. Let
\overline{M}_{0,n}(X,d)
be the moduli stack of degree-
d
, genus zero,
n
-pointed stable maps into
X
. Let
\mathrm{ev}_{i}:\overline{M}_{0,n}(X,d)\rightarrow X
denote the evaluation map at the
i
-th marking for
1\leq i\leq n
.
Then the quantum
K
-invariants are defined by

(2)

\langle\phi_{1},\dots,\phi_{n}\rangle_{0,n,d}:=\chi\bigg(\overline{M}_{0,n}(X,d),\prod_{i=1}^{n}\mathrm{ev}_{i}^{*}(\phi_{i})\bigg),

where
\phi_{1},\dots,\phi_{n}
are
K
-theory classes on
X
.
For partitions
\alpha,\beta,\gamma\in\mathrm{P}_{r,k}
, we define

(3)

F_{\alpha,\beta}:=\sum_{d\geq 0}q^{d}\langle\cO_{\alpha},\cO_{\beta}\rangle_{0,2,d}\quad\text{and}\quad F_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}:=\sum_{d\geq 0}q^{d}\langle\cO_{\alpha},\cO_{\beta},\cO_{\gamma}\rangle_{0,3,d},

where
q
is the Novikov variable.
Here,
(F_{\alpha,\beta})_{\alpha,\beta\in\mathrm{P}_{r,k}}
is referred to as the
quantized pairing
matrix.
Let
F^{\alpha,\beta}
denote the entries of its inverse.
The
quantum
K
-ring
of
X
, denoted by
\mathrm{QK}(X)
, is the
\mathbb{Z}[[q]]
-module
K(X)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Z}[[q]]
equipped with the
quantum
K
-product

defined by

\cO_{\lambda}\bullet\cO_{\mu}=\sum_{\nu\in\mathrm{P}_{r,k}}N_{\lambda,\mu}^{\nu}\cO_{\nu},

where the structure constants
N_{\lambda,\mu}^{\nu}
are given by

(4)

N_{\lambda,\mu}^{\nu}:=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathrm{P}_{r,k}}F^{\nu,\alpha}F_{\alpha,\lambda,\mu}.

According to the general results in
[
22
,
32
]
, the quantum
K
-product is commutative, associative, and a deformation of the tensor product.
Furthermore, the structure constants
N_{\lambda,\mu}^{\nu}
are polynomials in
q
(see
[
13
]
),
a property commonly known as the finiteness of the quantum
K
-ring.

1.3.
Quot schemes

Fix a smooth projective curve
C
of genus
g
. Let
\quot_{d}(C,N,r)
be the Quot scheme
parametrizing
short exact sequences

0\rightarrow S\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{C}^{\oplus N}\rightarrow Q\rightarrow 0

of coherent sheaves on
C
, where
S
has rank
r
and degree
-d
.
When
C=\mathbb{P}^{1}
, the Quot scheme
\quot_{d}(\mathbb{P}^{1},N,r)
, is a smooth projective scheme and isomorphic to
X
when
d=0
.
Strømme
[
51
]
studied geometric aspects of
\quot_{d}(\mathbb{P}^{1},N,r)
using torus localization.
In higher genus,
\quot_{d}(C,N,r)
is used to compactify the space
Mor_{d}(C,X)
of degree-
d
morphisms from
C
to
X
and to calculate intersection numbers (see
[
8
,
11
,
10
]
)).
In
[
39
]
, Marian and Oprea defined virtual fundamental classes for Quot schemes on curves of arbitrary genus and calculated virtual intersection numbers.

Let
\pi_{C}
and
\pi
be the projections from
\quot_{d}(C,N,r)\times C
to
C
and
\quot_{d}(C,N,r)
, respectively.
There exists a universal short exact sequence

0\rightarrow\mathcal{S}\rightarrow\pi_{C}^{*}\mathcal{O}_{C}^{\oplus N}\rightarrow\mathcal{Q}\rightarrow 0.

of coherent sheaves over
\quot_{d}(C,N,r)\times C
. For any
p\in C
, we denote by
\mathcal{S}_{p}
the restriction of the universal subbundle
\mathcal{S}
to
\quot_{d}(C,N,r)\times\{p\}
.

Definition 1.1
.

For any partition
\lambda\in\mathrm{P}_{r,k}
, we define the
K
-theory class

\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}_{\lambda}=\sum_{\nu\in\mathrm{P}_{r,k}}{\rm{A}}_{\lambda}^{\nu}\ \mathbb{S}^{\nu}(\mathcal{S}_{p})

on
\quot_{d}(C,N,r)
, where
\rm{A}_{\lambda}^{\nu}
are the coefficients of the transition matrix in (
1
) and
\mathbb{S}^{\nu}(\mathcal{S}_{p})
are the Schur functors applied to the vector bundle
\mathcal{S}_{p}
. Note that the point
p
is suppressed from the notation of
\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}_{\lambda}
. We define

\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}_{\nu}^{*}:=\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}_{\nu^{*}}\cdot\det(\mathcal{S}_{p}),

where
\nu^{*}
is the complement partition in the
r\times k
rectangle.

Theorem 1.2
.

For any partitions
\lambda,\mu,\nu\in\mathrm{P}_{r,k}
, the structure constants for the product in the quantum
K
-ring
\mathrm{QK}(\mathrm{Gr}(r,N))
satisfy:

N_{\lambda,\mu}^{\nu}=\sum_{d\geq 0}q^{d}\chi(\quot_{d}(\mathbb{P}^{1},N,r),\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}_{\lambda}\cdot\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}_{\mu}\cdot\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}_{\nu}^{*}).

The above theorem provides a more insightful description of the structure constants. For example, Theorem
1.2
immediately shows the
S_{3}
-symmetry
N_{\lambda,\mu}^{\nu}=N_{\lambda,\nu^{*}}^{\mu^{*}}
.
This symmetry was established for
q=0
(ordinary
K
-theory) in
[
54
]
and generally in
[
13
, Theorem 5.12]
, which is not evident from the original definition (
4
).
Furthermore, the vanishing results of the Euler characteristics of Schur bundles
\mathbb{S}^{\lambda}(\mathcal{S}_{p})
over high-degree Quot schemes
imply that
N_{\lambda\mu}^{\nu}
is a polynomial in
q
of degree at most
\min\{r,N-r\}
(see Corollary
3.7
).

1.4.
Topological Quantum Field Theory

The Quot scheme
\quot_{d}(C,N,r)
may not be smooth when
C\neq\mathbb{P}^{1}
.
A two-term perfect obstruction theory for
\quot_{d}(C,N,r)
was constructed
in
[
39
]
, yielding a virtual fundamental class via
[
36
,
12
]

and a virtual structure sheaf through
[
32
]
.
When
d\gg 0
, the Quot scheme is an irreducible scheme of
the expected dimension (see
[
8
,
45
]
),
and the virtual fundamental class and the virtual structure sheaf, denoted
\mathcal{O}_{\quot}^{\text{vir}}
,
equal the ordinary fundamental class and structure sheaf
\mathcal{O}_{\quot}
, respectively.

For any
K
-theory class
\phi
on
\quot_{d}(C,N,r)
, we define the virtual Euler characteristics by

\chi^{\mathrm{vir}}\big(\quot_{d}(C,N,r),\phi\big):=\chi\big(\quot_{d}(C,N,r),\mathcal{O}_{\quot}^{\text{vir}}\cdot\phi\big).

Definition 1.3
.

For any tuples of partitions
\underline{\lambda}=(\lambda^{1},\dots,\lambda^{s})
and
\underline{\nu}=(\nu^{1},\dots,\nu^{t})
in
\mathrm{P}_{r,k}
, we define the power series

N(g)_{\underline{\lambda}}^{\underline{\nu}}:=\sum_{d\geq 0}q^{d}\chi^{\mathrm{vir}}(\quot_{d}(C,N,r),\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}_{\lambda^{1}}\cdots\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}_{\lambda^{s}}\cdot\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}_{\nu^{1}}^{*}\cdots\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}_{\nu^{t}}^{*}),

where
\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}_{\lambda}
and
\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}_{\lambda}^{*}
are defined in Definition
1.1
. Note that
N_{\lambda,\mu}^{\nu}=N(0)_{\lambda,\mu}^{\nu}
by Theorem
1.2
.

Next, we define a Topological Quantum Field Theory (TQFT) valued in the power series ring

\mathbf{R}=\mathbb{C}[[q]]
, as considered in
[
14
]
.
Let
\mathbf{2Cob}
be the catergory where objects are disjoint union of circles
and morphisms are diffeomorphism classes of cobordisms.
The composition of morphisms is given by the concatenation of cobordisms.

Definition 1.4
.

A (1+1)-TQFT with coefficients in
\mathbf{R}
is a symmetric monoidal functor

F:\mathbf{2Cob}\to\mathbf{Mod_{R}},

where
\mathbf{Mod_{R}}
is the category of
\mathbf{R}
-modules, which is equivalent to:

(i)

Let
M
be an
\mathbf{R}
-module associated to a circle, then
F
associates
M^{\otimes s}
to the disjoint union of
s
circles.

(ii)

The trivial cobordism from a circle to a circle is associated to the identity map on
M
.

(iii)

The concatenation of two cobordisms correspond to the composition in
\mathbf{Mod_{R}}
.

Consider the free
\mathbb{C}[[q]]
-module
M=K(X)\otimes\mathbb{C}[[q]]
, with the basis
\{\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}:\lambda\in\mathrm{P}_{r,k}\}
.
For a genus
g
cobordism from
s
circles to
t
circles,
we define the morphism
M^{\otimes s}\to M^{\otimes t}
using the matrix given by:

\cO_{\lambda^{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\cO_{\lambda^{s}}\to\sum_{\underline{\nu}}N(g)_{\underline{\lambda}}^{\underline{\nu}}\cdot\cO_{\nu^{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\cO_{\nu^{t}}.

Theorem 1.5
.

The invariants
N(g)_{\underline{\lambda}}^{\underline{\nu}}
define a (1+1)-TQFT with values in the power series ring
\mathbb{C}[[q]]
. In particular, we prove the orthogonality relation

N(0)_{\lambda}^{\nu}=\sum_{d\geq 0}q^{d}\chi(\quot_{d}(\mathbb{P}^{1},N,r),\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}_{\lambda}\cdot\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}_{\nu}^{*})=\delta_{\lambda,\nu},

and the gluing formula

\sum_{\underline{\mu}}N(g_{1})_{\underline{\lambda}}^{\underline{\mu}}\cdot N(g_{2})_{\underline{\mu}}^{\underline{\nu}}=N(g+t-1)_{\underline{\lambda}}^{\underline{\nu}},

where
g=g_{1}+g_{2}
and the sum runs over all
t
-tuples,
\underline{\mu}=(\mu^{1},\cdots,\mu^{t})
, of partitions in
\mathrm{P}_{r,k}
.

Remark 1.6
.

The cohomological Quot scheme invariants also exhibit a TQFT structure with
\mathbb{C}
-coefficients, called Grassmann/
GL
-Verlinde TQFT in
[
40
]
, which differs from the standard TQFT coming from the quantum cohomology of the Grassmannian. Their proof relies on the fact that the cohomological invariants are enumerative in the large degree regime, which is not applicable in the
K
-theoretic setting.

1.5.
Evaluation map

To present the next result, we will introduce notation that is basis-independent for defining invariants over the Quot scheme.

The Grassmannian
X=\mathrm{Gr}(r,N)
has a GIT presentation

X=[M_{r\times N}^{ss}/\text{GL}_{r}(\mathbb{C})],

where
M_{r\times N}
is the affine space of
r
by
N
complex matrices and
M_{r\times N}^{ss}\subset M_{r\times N}
is the open subset of matrices of full rank.
Let

\mathfrak{X}:=[M_{r\times N}/\text{GL}_{r}(\mathbb{C})]

be the quotient stack. Note that the
K
-theory of the stack
K(\mathfrak{X})
is isomorphic to the ring
R(\mathrm{GL}_{r}(\mathbb{C}))
of rational
\mathrm{GL}_{r}(\mathbb{C})
-representations.
Given a
\mathrm{GL}_{r}(\mathbb{C})
-representation
\mathrm{V}
, we define its associated
K
-theory classes
on
\mathfrak{X}
as
M_{r\times N}\times_{\mathrm{GL}_{r}(\mathbb{C})}\mathrm{V}\in K(\mathfrak{X})
, also denoted by
\mathrm{V}
using the isomorphism
R(\mathrm{GL}_{r}(\mathbb{C}))\cong K(\mathfrak{X})
.
An element
\mathrm{V}\in K(\mathfrak{X})
restricts to an element
V\in K(X)

Let

2.1.
Algebraic representations of
\mathrm{GL}_{r}(\mathbb{C})

Recall that a
partition
(with
r
nonnegative parts) is a weakly decreasing sequence of nonnegative integers

\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{r})
, where
\lambda_{1}\geq\lambda_{2}\geq\dots\geq\lambda_{r}\geq 0
. Let

\mathrm{P}_{r,k}=\{\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{r})\mid\lambda_{1}\leq k\}

be the set of partitions contained in the rectangle
(k)^{r}=(k,k,\dots,k)
with
r
rows and
k
columns.

Let
\mathbb{S}^{\lambda}
be the
Schur functor
associated to
\lambda
(see
[
21
, §6]
) and let
\mathbb{C}^{r}
be the standard representation of
\mathrm{GL}_{r}(\mathbb{C})
.
Then
\mathbb{S}^{\lambda}(\mathbb{C}^{r})
is the unique irreducible (polynomial) representation of
\mathrm{GL}_{r}(\mathbb{C})
of highest weight
\lambda
.
For example, if
\lambda=(a)
, then
\mathbb{S}^{(a)}(\mathbb{C}^{r})=\Sym^{a}(\mathbb{C}^{r})
; if
\lambda=(1)^{b}:=(1,\dots,1)
with 1 repeated
b
times, then
\mathbb{S}^{(1)^{b}}(\mathbb{C}^{r})=\wedge^{b}(\mathbb{C}^{r})
. Let
R^{0}(\mathrm{GL}_{r}(\mathbb{C}))
denote the ring of polynomial representations of
\mathrm{GL}_{r}(\mathbb{C})
. Note that, for any
g\in\mathrm{GL}_{r}(\mathbb{C})
, the trace of
g
on
\mathbb{S}^{\lambda}(\mathbb{C}^{r})
is given by

\chi_{\mathbb{S}^{\lambda}(\mathbb{C}^{r})}(g)=s_{\lambda}(x_{1},\dots,x_{r}),

where
x_{1},\dots,x_{k}
are the eigenvalues of
g
and
s_{\lambda}
is the
Schur polynomial
.
This gives an isomorphism
\chi
between
R^{0}(\mathrm{GL}_{r}(\mathbb{C}))
and the ring of symmetric polynomials in
r
variables.

Let
R(\mathrm{GL}_{r}(\mathbb{C}))
denote the ring of algebraic representations of
\mathrm{GL}_{r}(\mathbb{C})
. As a ring,
R(\mathrm{GL}_{r}(\mathbb{C}))
is obtained from
R^{0}(\mathrm{GL}_{r}(\mathbb{C}))
by inverting the determinant representation
\det(\mathbb{C}^{r})
. The trace map
\chi
extends to the isomorphism between
R(\mathrm{GL}_{r}(\mathbb{C}))
and the ring of symmetric Laurent polynomials in the
r
variables
x_{1},\dots,x_{r}
.

Let
\mathrm{S}:=\big(\mathbb{C}^{r}\big)^{\vee}
denote the dual of the standard representation. Taking the dual of representations gives an automorphism of the representation ring
R(\mathrm{GL}_{r}(\mathbb{C}))
. At the level of Laurent polynomials, it is given by mapping
x_{i}\to x_{i}^{-1}
. In particular, the representation ring
R(\mathrm{GL}_{r}(\mathbb{C}))
is also generated by
\{1,\mathrm{S},\wedge^{2}\mathrm{S},\dots,\det(\mathrm{S}),\det(\mathrm{S})^{\vee}\}
over
\mathbb{C}
. Let
\mathcal{R}^{r}
denote subring
R(\mathrm{GL}_{r}(\mathbb{C}))
generated by
\{1,\mathrm{S},\wedge^{2}\mathrm{S},\dots,\det(\mathrm{S})\}
, that is,
\mathcal{R}^{r}
corresponds to the subring consisting of symmetric polynomials in
x_{1}^{-1},\dots,x_{r}^{-1}
inside the ring of symmetric Laurent polynomials.

Definition 2.1
.

Given a positive integer
\ell
, let
\mathcal{R}^{r,\ell}
be the linear subspace of
\mathcal{R}^{r}
spanned by polynomials in

\{\mathrm{S},\wedge^{2}\mathrm{S},\dots,\det(\mathrm{S})\}

of degree at most
\ell
. Note that each element
\wedge^{i}\mathrm{S}
has degree 1. The Jacobi-Trudi formula (of second type) implies that
\mathcal{R}^{r,\ell}
is spanned by

\{\mathbb{S}^{\lambda}(\mathrm{S})\mid\lambda\in\mathrm{P}_{r,\ell}\}

.

\displaystyle R(\mathrm{GL}_{r}(\mathbb{C}))\rightarrow K_{T}^{0}(\mathfrak{X}),\quad\mathrm{V}\mapsto M_{r\times N}\times_{\mathrm{GL}_{r}(\mathbb{C})}\mathrm{V}.

\lambda\cdot[m,v]=[m\lambda,v]

\lambda\in T
,
m\in M_{r\times N}
,
and
v\in\mathrm{V}
.
Since
K_{T}^{0}(\mathfrak{X})
is a
\Gamma
-module,
we can extend (
9
) to a surjective homomorphism

R(\mathrm{GL}_{r}(\mathbb{C}))\otimes\Gamma\rightarrow K_{T}^{0}(\mathfrak{X})
.
We denote by
V\in K_{T}^{0}(X)
the restriction of
\mathrm{V}
to
X
, i.e.,

V=M^{ss}_{r\times N}\times_{\mathrm{GL}_{r}(\mathbb{C})}\mathrm{V}.

We abuse notation by referring to
\mathrm{V}
as the
K
-theory class in
K_{T}^{0}(\mathfrak{X})
associated with the
\mathrm{GL}_{r}(\mathbb{C})
-module
\mathrm{V}
,
using Roman font to distinguish classes over the quotient stack
\mathfrak{X}
from those over the Grassmannian
X
.

Let
\mathbb{C}^{r}
denote the standard representation of
\mathrm{GL}_{r}(\mathbb{C})
, and let
\mathrm{S}=(\mathbb{C}^{r})^{\vee}
be
its dual.
Let

(10)

\mathrm{S}:=M_{r\times N}\times_{\mathrm{GL}_{r}(\mathbb{C})}(\mathbb{C}^{r})^{\vee}

be the associated vector bundle to the representation
\mathrm{S}
.
Note that the restriction of
\mathrm{S}
to
X
is the tautological subbundle
S
.

The
K
-theoretic Kirwan map
is the composition of the homomorphism (
9
) with the restriction map
K_{T}^{0}(\mathfrak{X})\rightarrow K_{T}^{0}(X)
. It induces the following linear isomorphism

\mathcal{R}^{r,k}\xrightarrow{\sim}K(X),

sending the representation
\mathbb{S}^{\lambda}(\mathrm{S})

to the vector bundle
\mathbb{S}^{\lambda}(S)
.
The collection

\{\mathbb{S}^{\lambda}(S)\}_{\lambda\in\mathrm{P}_{r,k}}

is a
\mathbb{Z}
-basis of the
K
-group
K(X)
and
a basis of the equivariant
K
-group
K_{T}^{0}(X)
over
\Gamma=K_{T}^{0}(\mathrm{pt})
.

There is another
\mathbb{Z}
-basis of
K(X)
given by the
Schubert structure sheaves
.
Recall that the
Schubert variety
for
\lambda
relative to the Borel subgroup of
\mathrm{GL}_{r}(\mathbb{C})
stabilizing a complete flag

0=F_{0}\subset F_{1}\subset\dots\subset F_{N}=\mathbb{C}^{N}
is defined by

X_{\lambda}=\{V\in X\mid\dim(V\cap F_{k+i-\lambda_{i}})\geq i,\quad\forall\,1\leq i\leq r\}.

The codimension of

equals the weight

of

.
The

-theory class
\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}:=[\mathcal{O}_{X_{\lambda}}]

is referred to as the Schubert structure sheaf.
The set
\{\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\mathrm{P}_{r,k}}

is a
\mathbb{Z}
-basis of
K(X)
.

Recall that Schubert structure sheaves can be represented in terms Schur bundles in
K(X)
using a linear transformation given by

\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}=\sum_{\nu\in\mathrm{P}_{r,k}}{\rm{A}}_{\lambda}^{\nu}\ \mathbb{S}^{\nu}(S)

for any
\lambda\in\mathrm{P}_{r,k}
. Below we use
stable Grothendieck polynomials
to describe the the coefficient
\mathrm{A}_{\lambda}^{\nu}
. Furthermore we define the “stacky” structure sheaves
\mathrm{O}_{\lambda}\in K(\mathfrak{X})
such that the Kirwan map (see
[
15
, §8]
) takes
\mathrm{O}_{\lambda}
to the Schubert structure sheaf

\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}=\mathbb{G}^{\lambda}(S^{\vee})\in K(X)

for
\lambda\in\mathrm{P}_{r,k}
.

2.4.
Grothendieck functor

Let
G_{\lambda}
be the stable Grothendieck polynomial for the partition
\lambda
(see
[
15
]
).
It has the following Weyl-type bialternant formula:

G_{\lambda}(x_{1},\dots,x_{r})=\frac{\det(x_{j}^{\lambda_{i}+r-i}(1-x_{j})^{i-1})_{1\leq i,j\leq r}}{\det(x_{j}^{r-i})_{1\leq i,j\leq r}}.

By
[
33
, Theorem 2.2]
, we have the following expansion

(11)

G_{\lambda}(x_{1},\dots,x_{r})=\sum_{\lambda\subseteq\mu\subseteq\hat{\lambda}}(-1)^{|\mu|-|\lambda|}g_{\lambda\mu}s_{\mu}(x_{1},\dots,x_{r}),

where
\hat{\lambda}
is the unique maximal partition with
r
rows obtained from
\lambda
by adding at most
i-1

boxes to its
i
th row for
2\leq i\leq r
,
and the coefficients
g_{\lambda,\mu}
are nonnegative and count certain increasing skew tableaux.

According to
[
33
, §8]
,
there is a well-defined virtual
\mathrm{GL}_{r}(\mathbb{C})
-representation
\mathbb{G}^{\lambda}(\mathbb{C}^{r})
whose character satisfies

\chi_{\mathbb{G}^{\lambda}(V)}(g)=G_{\lambda}(1-x_{1}^{-1},\dots,1-x_{r}^{-1}),

where
x_{1},\dots,x_{r}
are the eigenvalues of
g
on
V
.
Let
\lambda
be a partition with at most
r
parts, then

\mathbb{G}^{\lambda}(\mathbb{C}^{r})=\sum_{\nu\in\mathrm{P}_{r,\lambda_{1}}}\mathrm{A}_{\lambda}^{\nu}\mathbb{S}^{\nu}(\mathrm{S}),

where
\mathrm{S}=(\mathbb{C}^{r})^{\vee}
is the dual representation.
The coefficients
\mathrm{A}_{\lambda}^{\nu}
can be calculated using (
11
) and expressing
s_{\mu}(1-x_{1}^{-1},\dots,1-x_{r}^{-1})
as a linear combination of
s_{\nu}(x_{1}^{-1},\dots,x_{r}^{-1})
.

The description of
g_{\lambda,\mu}
in (
11
)
and the fact that
s_{\lambda}(1-x_{1}^{-1},\dots,1-x_{r}^{-1})
can be written as a linear combination of
s_{\mu}(x_{1}^{-1},\dots,x_{r}^{-1})
with
\mu\subseteq\lambda
(see, e.g.,
[
38
, §3, Example 10]
) implies that
\mathcal{R}^{r,k}
in Definition
2.1
is spanned by
\{\mathbb{G}^{\lambda}(\mathbb{C}^{r}):\lambda\in P_{r,k}\}
. This eludes to the fact that the Schubert structure sheaves
\{\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}:\lambda\in\mathrm{P}_{r,k}\}
give a linear basis for the
K
-theory of
\mathrm{Gr}(r,N)
.

Definition 2.2
.

For any partition
\lambda
(with
r
nonnegative parts), we define
the
stacky
Schubert structure sheaf by

\mathrm{O}_{\lambda}:=\mathbb{G}^{\lambda}(\mathrm{S}^{\vee})\in K(\mathfrak{X}),

where
\mathrm{S}
corresponds to the
\mathrm{GL}_{r}(\mathbb{C})
representation
(\mathbb{C}^{r})^{\vee}
(see (
10
)).

Note that
\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}
equals zero in
K(X)
if
\lambda
is not contained in
(k)^{r}
, while
\mathrm{O}_{\lambda}
is a non-zero element in
K(\mathfrak{X})
for any partition
\lambda
with at most
r
parts.

Remark 2.3
.

Note that the quantized
K
-theoretic dual
\mathrm{O}_{\lambda}^{*}=\mathrm{O}_{\lambda}^{*}\cdot\det(\mathrm{S})

restricts to the classical dual
\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}^{*}
for
\lambda\in\mathrm{P}_{r,k}
.
However, if we expand
\mathrm{O}_{\lambda}^{*}

in terms of the stacky Schubert structure sheaves,
the expansion may involve
\mathrm{O}_{\nu}
with
\nu\notin\mathrm{P}_{r,k}
which restricts to zero in
K(X)
.
For example, when
r=2
and
N=4
, we have

\mathrm{O}_{(1)}^{*}=\mathrm{O}_{(2,1)}\cdot\det{\mathrm{S}}=\mathrm{O}_{(2,1)}-\mathrm{O}_{(2,2)}-\mathrm{O}_{(3,1)}+\mathrm{O}_{(3,2)},

while

\mathcal{O}_{(1)}^{*}=\mathrm{O}_{(1)}^{*}|_{X}=\mathcal{O}_{(2,1)}-\mathcal{O}_{(2,2)}.

This shows that in general, the quantized
K
-theoretic duals
may not live in
K(X)
and they contain more information than
their classical counterparts.

2.5.
Quantum
K
-theory

There are different variants of quantum
K
-invariants (see, for example,
[

,
47

In this subsection, we recall the definition of genus-zero quantum
K
-invariants of Grassmannians in the sense of Givental–Lee
[
32
,
22
,
13
]
.

Let
X=\mathrm{Gr}(r,N)
be a Grassmannian.
For
d\in H_{2}(X)\cong\mathbb{Z}
, let
\overline{M}_{0,n}(X,d)
be the moduli stack of degree-
d
, genus zero,
n
-pointed
stable maps
into

.
This is a smooth proper DM stack equipped with

\mathrm{ev}_{i}:\overline{M}_{0,n}(X,d)\rightarrow X

for

Definition 2.4
.

Let
E_{1},\dots,E_{n}\in K_{T}^{0}(X)
.
We define the genus zero,
n
-pointed,
T
-equivariant quantum
K
-invariant with the insertions
E_{i}
’s by

\langle E_{1},\dots,E_{n}\rangle_{0,n,d}^{T}:=\chi^{T}\bigg(\overline{M}_{0,n}(X,d),\prod_{i=1}^{n}\mathrm{ev}_{i}^{*}(E_{i})\bigg).

The corresponding correlation function is defined by

\langle\!\langle E_{1},\dots,E_{n}\rangle\!\rangle^{T}_{0,n}:=\sum_{d\geq 0}q^{d}\langle E_{1},\dots,E_{n}\rangle_{0,n,d}^{T},

where
q
is a formal variable called the Novikov variable.
The non-equivariant quantum
K
-invariant and its correlation function are denoted by

\langle E_{1},\dots,E_{n}\rangle_{0,n}

and
\langle\!\langle E_{1},\dots,E_{n}\rangle\!\rangle_{0,n}
,
respectively.

We assume
k=N-r>0
. Let
\{\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\mathrm{P}_{r,k}}
be a basis for
K(X)
. We recall the definition of quantum
K
-ring
[
22
,
32
,
13
]
. In general, we can choose any basis
\{\phi_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\mathrm{P}_{r,k}}
of
K_{T}^{0}(X)
and define
T
-equivariant quantum K-ring.

Recall the following generating series of the 3-pointed Givental–Lee’s invariants

F_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}:=\langle\!\langle\mathcal{O}_{\alpha},\mathcal{O}_{\beta},\mathcal{O}_{\gamma}\rangle\!\rangle_{0,3}=\sum_{d\geq 0}q^{d}\langle\mathcal{O}_{\alpha},\mathcal{O}_{\beta},\mathcal{O}_{\gamma}\rangle_{0,3,d}

for partitions
\alpha,\beta,\gamma\in\mathrm{P}_{r,k}

F_{\alpha,\beta}=\langle\!\langle\mathcal{O}_{\alpha},\mathcal{O}_{\beta}\rangle\!\rangle_{0,2}
.
Let
(F^{\alpha,\beta})
be the matrix inverse to the quantized pairing matrix

(F_{\alpha,\beta})
.

Given three partitions

by

N_{\lambda,\mu}^{\nu}:=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathrm{P}_{r,k}}F^{\nu,\alpha}F_{\alpha,\lambda,\mu}.

The WDVV equation in quantum
K
-theory (see
[

]

K
-product

defined by

\Phi_{\lambda}\bullet\Phi_{\mu}=\sum_{\nu}N_{\lambda,\mu}^{\nu}\Phi_{\nu}

The

-ring
of

, denoted by
\mathrm{QK}(X)
, is the
\mathbb{C}[[q]]
-algebra

K(X)\otimes\mathbb{C}[[q]]
equipped with the quantum
K
-product.
If we take the non-equivariant limit, we obtain the quantum
K
-ring of
X
, denoted by
\mathrm{QK}(X)
.

Givental–Lee showed that the quantum
K
-ring
\mathrm{QK}(X)
equipped with the
quantized pairing

is a (formal) commutative

with unit 1;
see
[

, Corollary 1]

and
[

, Proposition 12 (1)]

2.6.
Perfect obstruction theories and virtual structure sheaves

Let
\quot_{d}(C,N,r)

Let
\mathcal{S}

In general, consider a projective morphism
\mathcal{C}\rightarrow B

where the fibers are prestable curves. Let

Consider the universal sequence

\pi:\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{Q}^{\prime}}:=\mathrm{Q}^{\prime}\times_{B}\mathcal{C}\rightarrow B

According to the proof of
[

(12)

\left(R\shom_{\pi}(\mathcal{S},\mathcal{Q})\right)^{\vee}=\left(R\pi_{*}\shom(\mathcal{S},\mathcal{Q})\right)^{\vee}

If we consider the

and the trivial

\nu_{T}:[\mathrm{Q}^{\prime}/T]\rightarrow[B/T]

Note that the injection
\mathcal{S}\rightarrow\mathcal{O}^{\oplus N}_{\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{Q}^{\prime}}}

induces a map

\mathcal{C}\xrightarrow{f}\mathfrak{X},

Since the tangent complex

3.
quantum-
K
TQFT

In this section, we prove Theorem
1.2
and Theorem
1.5
,
assuming the wall-crossing result from Corollary
1.13
and the degeneration formulas
from Theorem
1.15
.
We make critical use of a vanishing theorem for the Quot scheme from an adjoining paper
[
52
]
.
Notably, this vanishing result, proven using Atiyah–Bott localization on the Quot scheme over
\mathbb{P}^{1}
, is logically independent of the findings in this paper.

3.1.
Orthogonality

Recall that
\mathcal{R}^{r,\ell}
in Definition
2.1
is the subspace of the representation ring
R(\mathrm{GL}_{r}(\mathbb{C}))
spanned by

\{\mathbb{S}^{\lambda}(\mathrm{S})\mid\lambda\in\mathrm{P}_{r,\ell}\}
. The classical Littlewood-Richardson rule implies the following very useful property: For any
\mathrm{V}\in\mathcal{R}^{r,\ell}
and
\mathrm{W}\in\mathcal{R}^{r,m}
, the product

(14)

\mathrm{V}\cdot\mathrm{W}\in\mathcal{R}^{r,\ell+m}.

We use the the vanishing result in Theorem
1.11
to prove the following results.

Corollary 3.1
.

For any
\mathrm{V},\mathrm{W}\in\mathcal{R}^{r,k}\cong K(X)
and
d\geq m>0
, we have

\displaystyle\langle\det(\mathrm{S})^{m},\mathrm{V},\mathrm{W}\rangle^{\quot}_{0,d}=0.

Proof.

Note that
\det(\mathrm{S})^{m}=\mathbb{S}^{(m)^{r}}(\mathrm{S})
, where
(m)^{r}=(m,\dots,m)
.
Therefore,

\det(\mathrm{S})^{m}\cdot\mathrm{V}\cdot\mathrm{W}\in\mathcal{R}^{r,m+2k}.

Since
\mathrm{S}
has rank
r
, the Littlewood-Richardson rule/Pieri rule implies that
\det(\mathrm{S})\cdot\mathbb{S}^{\lambda}(\mathrm{S})=\mathbb{S}^{\nu}(\mathrm{S})
where
\nu=(\nu_{1},\dots,\nu_{r})=(\lambda_{1}+1,\dots\lambda_{r}+1)
. Each term
\mathbb{S}^{\nu}(\mathrm{S})
in the Schur expansion of
\det(\mathrm{S})^{m}\cdot\mathrm{V}\cdot\mathrm{W}
satisfies
\nu\in\mathcal{R}^{r,m+2k}
and
\nu_{r}\geq m>0
. For all such
\nu
, part(ii) of Theorem
1.11
implies

\langle\mathbb{S}^{\nu}(\mathrm{S})\rangle^{\quot}_{0,d}=0

whenever
d\geq m
.
∎

By setting
m=1
in the above corollary and using Theorem
1.12
,
we obtain an alternate proof of a conjecture of
Buch–Mihalcea
[
23
, Conjecture 4.3]
in the case of
Grassmannians.
This result may be thought of as a
divisor equation
for the
K
-theoretic Gromov-Witten invariants: For
V,W\in K_{T}^{0}(X)
and
d>0
, the quantum
K
-invariants satisfy

\langle\mathcal{O}_{(1)},V,W\rangle_{0,3,d}=\langle V,W\rangle_{0,2,d}.

For more general homogeneous spaces, the
K
-theoretic ‘divisor equation’ conjectuerd by Buch–Mihalcea
plays crucial roles in studying quantum
K
-product.
For example, it is used in the proof of the Chevalley formula for the equivariant quantum
K
-theory of cominuscule varieties
[
7
, Theorem 3.9]
.
Gu–Mihalcea–Sharpe–Xu–Zhang–Zou
[
23
]

also used it to (partially) verify the conjectured complete set of relations for the equivariant quantum
K
-ring of the partial flag manifolds.

Proposition 3.2

(Orthogonality)
.

We have

N(0)^{\nu}_{\lambda}=\langle\!\langle\mathrm{O}_{\lambda},\mathrm{O}_{\nu}^{*}\rangle\!\rangle^{\quot}_{0}=\delta_{\lambda,\nu}

for
\nu,\lambda\in\mathrm{P}_{r,k}
.

Proof.

The restriction of
\mathrm{O}_{\nu}^{*}
to
\mathrm{Gr}(r,N)
is the classical

K
-theoretic dual
\mathcal{O}_{\nu}^{*}=(1-\mathcal{O}_{(1)})\cdot\mathcal{O}_{\nu^{*}}
defined in
[
13
, §5.1]

satisfying

(15)

\chi(X,\mathcal{O}_{\nu}\cdot\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}^{*})=\delta_{\nu,\lambda}.

The degree zero part of the identity follows from (
15
) and the identity
(1-\mathcal{O}_{(1)})=\det(S)
.
For
d>0
, we have

\langle\mathrm{O}_{\lambda},\mathrm{O}_{\nu}^{*}\rangle^{\quot}_{0,d}=\langle\mathrm{O}_{\lambda},\mathrm{O}_{\nu^{*}},\det(\mathrm{S})\rangle^{\quot}_{0,d}=0,

where the last equality follows from Corollary
3.1
.
∎

Remark 3.3
.

Note that
\mathrm{O}_{\lambda}^{*}
may
not correspond to a representation in
\mathcal{R}^{r,k}
.
In fact,
\operatorname{Span}\{\mathrm{O}_{\lambda}^{*}\mid\lambda\in\mathrm{P}_{r,k}\}\subset K(\mathfrak{X})

correspond to
\operatorname{Span}\{\mathbb{S}^{\lambda}(\mathrm{S})\mid\lambda\in\mathrm{P}_{r,k+1}\text{ and }\lambda_{r}>0\}\subset R(\mathrm{GL}_{r}(\mathbb{C})).

Nevertheless, the above proposition shows that
\{\mathrm{O}_{\lambda}^{*}\}
are dual to
\{\mathrm{O}_{\lambda}\}
with respect to the pairing

\langle\!\langle-,-\rangle\!\rangle^{\quot}_{0}
.

3.2.
Structure constant

Corollary
1.13
states that

F_{\lambda,\mu,\nu}=N(0)_{\lambda,\mu,\nu}
and
F_{\lambda,\mu}=N(0)_{\lambda,\mu}
for any partitions
\lambda,\mu,\nu\in\mathrm{P}_{r,k}
. Below we give a description of the inverse of the quantized pairing matrix
(F^{\alpha,\beta})
.

Corollary 3.4
.

We have

(i)

F^{\lambda,\nu}=N(0)^{\lambda,\nu}
.

(ii)

F^{\lambda,\nu}
is a linear function in
q
.

Proof.

It follows from the degeneration formula in Theorem
1.15
that

\displaystyle N(0)^{\lambda,\nu}=\langle\!\langle\mathrm{O}_{\lambda}^{*},\mathrm{O}_{\nu}^{*}\rangle\!\rangle^{\quot}_{0}=\sum_{\alpha,\beta\in\mathrm{P}_{r,k}}\langle\!\langle\mathrm{O}_{\lambda}^{*},\mathrm{O}_{\alpha}\rangle\!\rangle^{\quot}_{0}F^{\alpha,\beta}\langle\!\langle\mathrm{O}_{\beta},\mathrm{O}_{\nu}^{*}\rangle\!\rangle^{\quot}_{0}=F^{\lambda,\nu}.

Here we have used orthogonality property of Proposition
3.2
in the last equality.
(ii) follows from the fact

\langle\mathrm{O}_{\nu}^{*},\mathrm{O}_{\lambda}^{*}\rangle^{\quot}_{0,d}=\langle\mathrm{O}_{\nu^{*}},\mathrm{O}_{\lambda^{*}},\det(\mathrm{S})^{2}\rangle^{\quot}_{0,d}=0,

for any
d\geq 2
by Corollary
3.1
.
∎

Remark 3.5
.

For a finite-dimensional Frobenius algebra, the formula in Corollary
3.4
(i)
follows trivially from the orthogonality in Proposition
3.2
.
Here, in the infinite-dimensional setting, the proof relies on the degeneration formula.
The above corollary also implies that
N(0)^{\lambda,\mu}
is the inverse of the pairing matrix
N(0)_{\lambda,\mu}
.

As further applications of the quantized
K
-theoretic duals, we finish the proof of Theorem
1.2
, i.e. show that the structure constant of the quantum
K
-product
N_{\lambda,\mu}^{\nu}
equals
N(0)_{\lambda,\mu}^{\nu}
.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Recall that the structure constant is given by the sum

N_{\lambda,\mu}^{\nu}=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathrm{P}_{r,k}}F_{\lambda,\mu,\alpha}F^{\alpha,\nu}.

Using Corollary
1.13
, we obtain

\displaystyle N_{\lambda,\mu}^{\nu}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathrm{P}_{r,k}}\langle\!\langle\mathrm{O}_{\lambda},\mathrm{O}_{\mu},\mathrm{O}_{\alpha}\rangle\!\rangle^{\quot}_{0}F^{\alpha,\nu}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha,\beta\in\mathrm{P}_{r,k}}\langle\!\langle\mathrm{O}_{\lambda},\mathrm{O}_{\mu},\mathrm{O}_{\alpha}\rangle\!\rangle^{\quot}_{0}F^{\alpha,\beta}\langle\!\langle\mathrm{O}_{\beta},\mathrm{O}^{*}_{\nu}\rangle\!\rangle^{\quot}_{0}

\displaystyle=\langle\!\langle\mathrm{O}_{\lambda},\mathrm{O}_{\mu},\mathrm{O}^{*}_{\nu}\rangle\!\rangle^{\quot}_{0}=N(0)_{\lambda,\mu}^{\nu}.

Here we used the degeneration formula from Theorem
1.15
in the second-to-last equality.

∎

The formula of the structure constant in the above corollary
resembles that in a Frobenius algebra. It is not only more transparent and compact but also allows
us to give intuitive proofs of some results of Buch–Mihalcea
[
13
]
.

Corollary 3.6

(
[
13
, Theorem 5.13]
)
.

The structure constants satisfy the
S_{3}
-symmetry, i.e.,
N_{\lambda,\mu}^{\nu}=N_{\lambda,\nu^{*}}^{\mu^{*}}
.

Proof.

It follows from Theorem
1.2
and Definition
1.3
that

\langle\!\langle\mathrm{O}_{\lambda},\mathrm{O}_{\mu},\mathrm{O}^{*}_{\nu}\rangle\!\rangle^{\quot}_{0}=\langle\!\langle\mathrm{O}_{\lambda}\cdot\mathrm{O}_{\mu}\cdot\mathrm{O}_{\nu^{*}}\cdot\det(\mathrm{S})\rangle\!\rangle^{\quot}_{0}.

This easily implies the
S_{3}
-symmetry.
∎

Part (i) of Theorem
1.11
implies that the structure constants

N_{\lambda,\mu}^{\nu}
are polynomials in the Novikov variable
q
of degree at most

4.
Quantum reduction map

In this section, we introduce the quatum reduction map

4.1.
Ring homomorphism property

Recall that the

-theoretic Kirwan map is the following isomorphism

R(\mathrm{GL}_{r}(\mathbb{C}))\rightarrow K(\mathfrak{X}),\quad\mathrm{V}\mapsto M_{r\times N}\times_{\mathrm{GL}_{r}(\mathbb{C})}\mathrm{V},

-theory class on

We define the following deformation of the

-theoretic Kirwan map:

Definition 4.1

The quantum reduction map

\mathbb{Z}[[q]]
-linear extension of the formula

\begin{aligned} \kappa(\mathrm{V})=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathrm{P}_{r,k}}\langle\!\langle\mathrm{V},\mathrm{O}^{*}_{\alpha}\rangle\!\rangle^{\quot}_{0}\mathcal{O}_{\alpha}\end{aligned},

where
\mathrm{V}
, by abuse of notation, also denotes the

-theory class on

associated to
\mathrm{V}\in R(\mathrm{GL}_{r}(\mathbb{C}))

Proposition 4.2
.

For any

, we have
\kappa(\mathrm{V})=V
where

V=M^{ss}_{r\times N}\times_{\mathrm{GL}_{r}(\mathbb{C})}\mathrm{V}\in K(X).

Proof.

Recall that
\mathcal{R}^{r,k}
is spanned by
\{\mathrm{O}_{\lambda}:\lambda\in\mathrm{P}_{r,k}\}
. The statement follows from Proposition
3.2
and the fact that
\mathrm{O}_{\lambda}
restricts to the class of structure sheaf
\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}
over

.
∎

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
\mathrm{V}\in R(\mathrm{GL}_{r}(\mathbb{C}))\cong K(\mathfrak{X})
be the associated
K
-theory class.
We have

\langle\!\langle\kappa(\mathrm{V}),\mathrm{W}\rangle\!\rangle^{\quot}_{0}=\langle\!\langle\mathrm{V},\mathrm{W}\rangle\!\rangle^{\quot}_{0}

for any
\mathrm{W}\in K(\mathfrak{X})
.
Here we extend
\kappa(V)
to a class in
\mathbb{Z}[[q]]\otimes K(\mathfrak{X})
by replacing

by
\mathrm{O}_{\nu}
in (

Proof.

It follows from the definition of
\kappa
(see (

\displaystyle\langle\!\langle\kappa(\mathrm{V}),\mathrm{W}\rangle\!\rangle^{\quot}_{0}=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathrm{P}_{r,k}}\langle\!\langle\mathrm{V},\mathrm{O}^{*}_{\alpha}\rangle\!\rangle^{\quot}_{0}\langle\!\langle\mathrm{O}_{\alpha},\mathrm{W}\rangle\!\rangle^{\quot}_{0}=\langle\!\langle\mathrm{V},\mathrm{W}\rangle\!\rangle^{\quot}_{0}.

∎

Theorem 4.4
.

The quantum reduction map
\kappa
is a surjective ring homomorphism.

Proof.

It is clear from the definition that
\kappa
is surjective and additive.
Let
\mathrm{V},\mathrm{W}\in R(\mathrm{GL}_{r}(\mathbb{C}))\cong K(\mathfrak{X})
,
then by the definition of the quantum product, Theorem
1.2
, and repeatedly using the identity in (

\displaystyle\kappa(\mathrm{V})\bullet\kappa(\mathrm{W})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha,\beta\in\mathrm{P}_{r,k}}\langle\!\langle\mathrm{V},\mathrm{O}^{*}_{\alpha}\rangle\!\rangle^{\quot}_{0}\cdot\langle\!\langle\mathrm{W},\mathrm{O}^{*}_{\beta}\rangle\!\rangle^{\quot}_{0}\mathcal{O}_{\alpha}\bullet\mathcal{O}_{\beta}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha,\nu\in\mathrm{P}_{r,k}}\langle\!\langle\mathrm{V},\mathrm{O}^{*}_{\alpha}\rangle\!\rangle^{\quot}_{0}\cdot\langle\!\langle\mathrm{W},\mathrm{O}^{*}_{\beta}\rangle\!\rangle^{\quot}_{0}\langle\!\langle\mathrm{O}_{\alpha},\mathrm{O}_{\beta},\mathrm{O}^{*}_{\nu}\rangle\!\rangle^{\quot}_{0}\mathcal{O}_{\nu}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha,\beta,\nu\in\mathrm{P}_{r,k}}\langle\!\langle\mathrm{V},\mathrm{O}^{*}_{\alpha}\rangle\!\rangle^{\quot}_{0}\cdot\langle\!\langle\mathrm{W},\mathrm{O}_{\alpha},\mathrm{O}^{*}_{\nu}\rangle\!\rangle^{\quot}_{0}\mathcal{O}_{\nu}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\nu\in\mathrm{P}_{r,k}}\langle\!\langle\mathrm{V},\mathrm{W},\mathrm{O}^{*}_{\nu}\rangle\!\rangle^{\quot}_{0}\mathcal{O}_{\nu}

\displaystyle=\kappa(\mathrm{V}\cdot\mathrm{W}).

∎

4.2.

The product structure in quantum

in

We now use our technique to give an alternate proof of the quantum

-ring relations in
[

Let

\det(Q)=\sum_{\lambda\in\mathrm{P}_{r,k}}\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}\in K(X).

\kappa(\det(\mathrm{S}^{\vee}))=\frac{\det(Q)}{1-q}.

Proof.

For each

Recall that
\mathcal{R}^{r,\ell}
denotes the subspace of the representation ring

spanned by

\{\mathbb{S}^{\lambda}(\mathrm{S})\mid\lambda\in\mathrm{P}_{r,\ell}\}

) of the Littlewood-Richardson rule several times below.

Corollary 4.8
.

For
0<i\leq r

-product

\wedge^{i}S\bullet\det Q=(1-q)\wedge^{i}S\cdot\det Q.

Proof.

Using Proposition

\displaystyle\langle\wedge^{i}\mathrm{S},\det(\mathrm{S}^{\vee}),\mathrm{O}^{*}_{\nu}\rangle^{\quot}_{0,d}=\langle\wedge^{i}\mathrm{S},\mathrm{O}_{\nu^{*}}\rangle^{\quot}_{0,d}=0.

We can express

by extracting the coefficient of

of degree at most

Proposition

and (

(
[

In

For

(

In

5.1.

Definition 5.1

at

For any
\epsilon\in\mathbb{Q}_{>0}

.
It is empty unless

Remark 7.6
.

The degeneration formula (

) in Theorem

(ii) can be proven similarly by analyzing a simple degeneration

\mathcal{C}^{\prime}\rightarrow B
over a smooth pointed curve
(B,0)
.
Here, the fibers outside 0 are smooth curves of genus
g
,
while the fiber at 0,
C^{\prime}_{\deg}
, is an irreducible curve with one node.
The associated stack of expanded degenerations,

\mathfrak{C}^{\prime}\rightarrow\mathfrak{B}
, has central fibers formed by
inserting a chain of
\mathbb{P}^{1}

C^{\prime}_{\deg}
(see
[
37
, §2.2]
).
The good degeneration of Quot schemes,
\mathfrak{Quot}_{d}(\mathfrak{C}^{\prime}/\mathfrak{B},N,r)\rightarrow B

stable quotients of rank

and degree
d
over the universal family

\mathfrak{C}^{\prime}\rightarrow\mathfrak{B}
.

Let
\tilde{C}
be the normalization of
C^{\prime}_{\deg}
, with
x_{-}
and
x_{+}
in
\tilde{C}
representing the preimages of the unique node in
C^{\prime}_{\deg}
.
The central fiber of this degeneration of Quot schemes is ‘virtually’ a complete intersection
substack, and its ‘normalization’ is

(38)

\coprod\limits_{n,\underline{d}\in\tilde{\Lambda}_{d}^{n}}\tilde{\mathcal{M}}_{0}^{d_{0}}\times_{X}\mathcal{M}^{d_{1}}_{\sim}\times_{X}\dots\mathcal{M}^{d_{n}}_{\sim}\times_{X}\big(\tilde{\mathcal{M}}_{0}^{d_{0}}\big),

,

\tilde{\mathcal{M}}_{0}^{d_{0}}\cong Q^{0+,0+}_{\tilde{C},1|2}(X,d_{0}),

and we put the further right
\tilde{\mathcal{M}}_{0}^{d_{0}}
in parenthesis
to indicate is is the same as the one on the left.
Here, a source curve in
Q^{0+,0+}_{\tilde{C},2|2}(X,d_{0})
is formed
by attaching rational tails to
\tilde{C}
at
x_{\pm}
and
placing heavy markings on the outermost components of these rational tails.
The inclusion of one light marking on the parametrized component
\tilde{C}

does not alter the moduli space but allows us to define invariants with one insertion in
K_{T}^{0}(\mathfrak{X})
.
Similar to the proof of Theorem
1.15
(i),
we apply the deformation invariance of virtual Euler characteristics to relate
\langle\!\langle\mathrm{V}\rangle\!\rangle^{\quot,T}_{g}

to the alternating sum of virtual Euler characteristics over the components in (

).

)

6.6
and Proposition
5.3
.

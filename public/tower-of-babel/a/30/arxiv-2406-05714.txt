A conversion theorem and minimax optimality for continuum contextual bandits
Arya Akhavanarya.akhavan@stats.ox.ac.uk, Karim Lounici karim.lounici@polytechnique.edu, Massimiliano Pontil massimiliano.pontil@iit.it, Alexandre B. Tsybakov alexandre.tsybakov@ensae.fr

Abstract
We study the contextual continuum bandits problem, where the learner sequentially receives a side information vector and has to choose an action in a convex set, minimizing a function associated to the context. The goal is to minimize all the underlying functions for the received contexts, leading to the contextual notion of regret, which is stronger than the standard static regret. Assuming that the objective functions are γ \gamma -Hölder with respect to the contexts, 0 < γ ≤ 1 0<\gamma\leq 1 , we demonstrate that any algorithm achieving a sub-linear static regret can be extended to achieve a sub-linear contextual regret. We prove a static-to-contextual regret conversion theorem that provides an upper bound for the contextual regret of the output algorithm as a function of the static regret of the input algorithm. We further study implications of this general result for three fundamental cases of dependency of the objective function on the action variable: (a) Lipschitz bandits, (b) convex bandits, (c) strongly convex and smooth bandits. For Lipschitz bandits and γ = 1 \gamma=1 , combining our results with the lower bound of Slivkins 2014 , we prove that the minimax optimal contextual regret for the noise-free adversarial setting scales as T ( p + d + 1 ) / ( p + d + 2 ) T^{(p+d+1)/(p+d+2)} up to logarithmic factors, where p p and d d are the dimensions of the context and of the action spaces, respectively, and T T is the number of queries. Then we prove that, when the evaluations are noisy, the rate of contextual regret in T T for convex bandits is the same as for strongly convex and smooth bandits and scales as T ( p + γ ) / ( p + 2 ​ γ ) T^{(p+\gamma)/(p+2\gamma)} up to logarithmic factors. Lastly, we present a minimax lower bound, implying two key facts. First, obtaining a sub-linear contextual regret may be impossible over functions that are not continuous with respect to the context. Second, for convex bandits and strongly convex and smooth bandits, the algorithms that we propose achieve, up to a logarithmic factor, the minimax optimal rate of contextual regret as a function of the number of queries.

Introduction

The continuum bandit problem presents a sequential decision-making challenge where, at each round, an action is taken as a continuous variable followed by suffering a corresponding loss. Various variants of this problem have been explored in the literature. The most extensively studied framework involves the learner receiving a function
f_{t}:\Theta\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
(random or non-random), at each round
t
, where
\Theta\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a convex body (compact and convex set with a non-empty interior). Based on
\{(y_{k},\boldsymbol{z}_{k})\}_{k=1}^{t-1}
, the learner selects
\boldsymbol{z}_{t}\in\Theta
, suffers loss
f_{t}(\boldsymbol{z}_{t})
, and receives the noisy feedback

y_{t}=f_{t}(\boldsymbol{z}_{t})+\xi_{t},

where
\xi_{t}
denotes the noise variable. The standard goal of the learner is to minimize the static regret

\displaystyle R_{T}:=\mathbf{E}\Big[\sum_{t=1}^{T}f_{t}(\boldsymbol{z}_{t})-\min_{\boldsymbol{z}\in\Theta}\sum_{t=1}^{T}f_{t}(\boldsymbol{z})\Big].

(1)

In the present paper, we assume that the learner has access to side information about the loss function, namely a context at each round. Leveraging this additional information, the learner is expected to minimize another type of regret referred to as the contextual regret. We consider the problem of contextual continuum bandits stated as follows.

Contextual continuum bandits.
Let
\Theta\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a convex body. Let
f:\mathbb{R}^{d}\times[0,1]^{p}\to\mathbb{R}
be an unknown function. At each round
t
:

•

A context
\boldsymbol{c}_{t}\in[0,1]^{p}
is revealed by the adversary.

•

Based on the past values
\{(y_{k},\boldsymbol{z}_{k},\boldsymbol{c}_{k})\}_{k=1}^{t-1}
, the learner chooses a query point
\boldsymbol{z}_{t}\in\Theta
and gets a noisy evaluation of
f(\boldsymbol{z}_{t},\boldsymbol{c}_{t})
:

\displaystyle y_{t}=f(\boldsymbol{z}_{t},\boldsymbol{c}_{t})+\xi_{t},

(2)

where
\xi_{t}
is a scalar noise variable.

The learner’s objective in this framework is to minimize the
contextual regret
, defined as:

\displaystyle R_{T}^{\mathrm{cntx}}:=\mathbf{E}\Big[\sum_{t=1}^{T}f(\boldsymbol{z}_{t},\boldsymbol{c}_{t})-\sum_{t=1}^{T}\min_{\boldsymbol{z}\in\Theta}f(\boldsymbol{z},\boldsymbol{c}_{t})\Bigg].

(3)

Some remarks about this setting are in order. As a general framework for the problem, we assume that the contexts are drawn sequentially from the hypercube
[0,1]^{p}
. This presentation is chosen to simplify the notation. However, all the upper bounds on the regret throughout the paper hold if the contexts are drawn from a non-empty compact subset of
[0,1]^{p}
. Moreover, at first sight, by introducing the notation
f_{t}(\boldsymbol{z})=f(\boldsymbol{z},\boldsymbol{c}_{t})
, the contextual continuum bandit problem as stated above might appear as a special case of the
non-contextual
continuum bandit problem with dynamic regret (CBDR). In the CBDR setting, the learner selects the query point
\boldsymbol{z}_{t}
at time
t
based on past observations
\{y_{k},\boldsymbol{z}_{k}\}_{k=0}^{t-1}
to minimize the dynamic regret:

\displaystyle{R_{T}^{\mathrm{dyn}}:=}\mathbf{E}\Big[\sum_{t=1}^{T}f_{t}(\boldsymbol{z}_{t})-\sum_{t=1}^{T}\min_{\boldsymbol{z}\in\Theta}f_{t}(\boldsymbol{z})\Big].

(4)

With this notation, we have
{R_{T}^{\mathrm{cntx}}=R_{T}^{\mathrm{dyn}}}
. However, the two settings are not equivalent. Indeed, the contextual setting that we consider is “statistically easier" than CBDR since partial information about
f_{t}
in the form of the context
\boldsymbol{c}_{t}
is revealed to the learner before the action is chosen. This suggests that a better behavior of the regret is potentially achievable.

Another performance measure for continuum contextual bandits called the
L
-regret was introduced by
Hazan and Megiddo 2007
:

\displaystyle R_{T}^{L}:=\mathbf{E}\left[\sum_{t=1}^{T}f_{t}(\boldsymbol{z}_{t})-\min_{\boldsymbol{z}(\cdot)\in X_{L}}\sum_{t=1}^{T}f_{t}(\boldsymbol{z}(\boldsymbol{c}_{t}))\right].

(5)

Here, the minimum is taken over the class
X_{L}
of all actions satisfying the Lipschitz condition with respect to the context, with given Lipschitz constant
L
. For
L=0
the
L
-regret is equal to the static regret
R_{T}
.

Considering
f_{t}(\boldsymbol{z})=f(\boldsymbol{z},\boldsymbol{c}_{t})
we have that
R_{T}^{\mathrm{cntx}}=R_{T}^{\mathrm{dyn}}\geq R_{T}^{L}\geq R_{T}
. The setting with
L
-regret, where
L>0
, is more challenging than the static regret setting as constant actions trivially satisfy the Lipschitz condition. In turn, controlling the contextual regret
R_{T}^{\mathrm{cntx}}
is more challenging than the
L
-regret since it evaluates the risk against all possible choices of actions and not only against the Lipschitz actions.
By definition, small static regret indicates that the policy is at least as effective as a single constant action performing well on the average of all observed functions during learning. In contrast, small dynamic regret signifies that the policy performs well for each function individually. For non-contextual bandits, the CBDR problem has been recently investigated by
Yang et al. 2016
;
Chen and Giannakis 2018
;
Zhao et al. 2021
;
Wei and Luo 2021
;
Wang 2023
under restrictions on various characteristics of the evolution of
f_{t}
’s over time, which are not interpretable in terms of the smoothness assumptions on
f(\cdot,\cdot)
used in continuum contextual settings.

In Section
5
, we demonstrate that, regardless of the behavior of the function
f(\cdot,\boldsymbol{c})
, if the function
f(\boldsymbol{x},\cdot)
lacks continuity, obtaining a sub-linear contextual regret may be impossible. Hence, we focus on the setting where
f(\boldsymbol{x},\cdot)
is a continuous function for any
\boldsymbol{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Specifically, in the sequel we adopt the following slightly stronger assumption.

Assumption 1
.

For
(L,\gamma)\in[0,\infty)\times(0,1]
we assume that
f\in\mathcal{F_{\gamma}}(L)
, where
\mathcal{F_{\gamma}}(L)
denotes the class of all functions
f:\mathbb{R}^{d}\times[0,1]^{p}\to\mathbb{R}
such that

\displaystyle|f(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{c})-f(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{c}^{\prime})|\leq L\left\lVert\boldsymbol{c}-\boldsymbol{c}^{\prime}\right\rVert^{\gamma},\quad\text{for all}\quad\boldsymbol{x}\in\Theta,\,\,\boldsymbol{c},\boldsymbol{c}^{\prime}\in[0,1]^{p},

(6)

where
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert
is the Euclidean norm.

In this paper, we develop a meta-algorithm approach to continuum contextual bandit problem. In short it can be described as follows. Let
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
be a class of real-valued functions on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Assume that we are given an input algorithm
\pi

achieving a sub-linear

static regret
in adversarial bandit setting over
T
runs for the sequence of functions
\{f_{t}(\cdot)\}_{t=1}^{T}
, where each
f_{t}\in\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
. Then, we associate to it an output contextual bandit algorithm with sub-linear

contextual regret
for any loss
f(\cdot,\cdot)
belonging to the class

\mathcal{G}_{\gamma}(\mathcal{F}^{\prime}):=\{f\in\mathcal{F_{\gamma}}(L):f(\cdot,\boldsymbol{c})\in\mathcal{F}^{\prime}\ \text{for all}\,\boldsymbol{c}\in[0,1]^{p}\}
. We prove a static-to-contextual regret conversion theorem that provides an upper bound for
the contextual regret of the output algorithm as a function of the static regret of the input algorithm.
Our construction of the output algorithm consists in discretizing the space of contexts into cells and running an algorithm
\pi
separately within each cell. Similar constructions can be traced back to
Hazan and Megiddo 2007
, where the proposed method was not a meta-algorithm valid for any
\pi
and
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
but a single contextual bandit algorithm with a specific input algorithm
\pi
(projected gradient) and a specific class
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
(convex and Lipschitz functions). The analysis given in
Hazan and Megiddo 2007
focuses on the
L
-regret and does not provide a control of the contextual regret. The subsequent work
Cesa-Bianchi et al. 2017
adopts a similar discretization strategy, with similar limitations. It derives a bound on the
L
-regret when the input algorithm is chosen as Exp3 and the class
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
is the set of all 1-Lipschitz functions.

Another approach to static-to-contextual regret conversion is developed in
Slivkins 2014
. It relies
on zooming with dyadic splits and is valid, again, for a specific input class
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
, namely, a subset of Lipschitz functions, and a specific class of input algorithms
\pi
, namely, those achieving the optimal rate on this particular class. The class of functions
f(\cdot,\cdot)
considered in
Slivkins 2014
is a subclass of
\mathcal{G}_{1}(\mathcal{F}^{\prime})
satisfying, along with the Lipschitz condition, the additional restrictive condition that for each
\boldsymbol{c}
, there exists
\boldsymbol{z}^{*}(\boldsymbol{c})=\arg\min_{\boldsymbol{z}\in\Theta}f(\boldsymbol{z},\boldsymbol{c})
such that
\boldsymbol{c}\mapsto f(\boldsymbol{z}^{*}(\boldsymbol{c}),\boldsymbol{c})
is Lipschitz. The algorithm of
Slivkins 2014
requires exact knowledge of the Lipschitz constant, and the resulting bound on the contextual regret is exponential in the dimension of the context.

The contributions of the present paper can be summarized as follows.

(i)

In Section
3
, we propose a meta-algorithm that converts any input non-contextual bandit algorithm into an output contextual bandit algorithm. We prove a general conversion theorem that derives the contextual regret of the output algorithm from the static regret of the input algorithm (Theorem
2
).

(ii)

We apply our static-to-contextual conversion to handle the following two fundamental settings.

(ii-a)

First, we consider the setting, where
f(\cdot,\cdot)
is Lipschitz in both arguments, that is,
\gamma=1
and
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
is the class of Lipschitz functions. In this setting, when the noises
\xi_{i}
are zero, we show that our conversion approach leads to an output algorithm with a rate optimal contextual regret improving upon
Slivkins 2014
in several respects. Unlike
Slivkins 2014
, we
do not require the above restrictive additional condition on
f
nor the knowledge of Lipschitz constant, and our bound scales polynomially in the dimension of the context.

(ii-b)

Second, we consider the setting where
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
is the class of all convex functions and the noise in observations (
2
) is sub-Gaussian. In this case, by applying our conversion theorem together with the static regret bound from
Fokkema et al. 2024
we prove that the contextual regret of the proposed output algorithm achieves the minimax optimal rate in
T
up to a logarithmic factor.

(iii)

In Section
4
, we apply our conversion theorem in the setting where

\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
is the class of strongly convex and smooth functions, and the noise is sub-Gaussian. We first construct a bandit algorithm, see Algorithm
2
, which is an extension of the Bandit Convex Optimization (BCO) algorithm from
Hazan and Levy 2014
to the problem with noisy queries. We prove a bound on its static regret. Then, combining it with the conversion theorem of Section
3
, we derive a bound on the contextual regret of the proposed output algorithm.

(iv)

In Section
5
, we establish a minimax lower bound leading to several useful corollaries. First, it implies that the contextual regret bounds from items (ii-b) and (iii) are minimax optimal in
T
up to logarithmic factors. Second, it shows that if

f(\boldsymbol{x},\cdot)
lacks continuity, obtaining a sub-linear contextual regret may be impossible.

Related work

Contextual bandit problem has been introduced by
Woodroofe 1979
in the framework of multi-armed bandits, that is, when the space of actions is finite. The literature on the contextual bandit problem with finite number of arms is now very rich, see, e.g.,
Goldenshluger and Zeevi 2009
;
Wang et al. 2005
;
Bastani and Bayati 2020
;
Perchet and Rigollet 2013
;
Rigollet and Zeevi 2010
;
Pandey et al. 2007
;
Langford and Zhang 2007
;
Auer et al. 2002
;
Kakade et al. 2008
;
Yang and Zhu 2002
;
Foster and Rakhlin 2023
;
Cella et al. 2023
;
Cella et al. 2022
and the references therein. In this literature, it is not uncommon to assume that
the contexts are i.i.d. rather than adversarial.

There was also a considerable work on the problem of contextual bandits with continuous action space:
Hazan and Megiddo 2007
;
Slivkins 2014
;
Kleinberg et al. 2008
;
Cesa-Bianchi et al. 2017
;
Chen and Gallego 2018
;
Li et al. 2019
.
These papers deal with adversarial contexts and, with the exception of
Li et al. 2019
, consider the noiseless setting (
\xi_{i}\equiv 0
). The analysis in
Slivkins 2014
addresses the case where the context and the action vary in metric spaces with covering dimensions
p
and
d
, respectively, under the assumption that
f(\cdot,\cdot)
is Lipschitz in both arguments and satisfies an additional strong restriction (see the Introduction). The results in
Slivkins 2014
provide a lower bound of the order
O(T^{\frac{p+d+1}{p+d+2}})
on the contextual regret and an algorithm based on zooming techniques that attains this rate up to a logarithmic factor. Bounds for the
L
-regret for continuum contextual bandit algorithms are developed in
Hazan and Megiddo 2007
;
Cesa-Bianchi et al. 2017
. The paper
Chen and Gallego 2018
deals with the setting where
f(\cdot,\cdot)
is Lipschitz in both arguments, the actions belong to
[0,1]
, the function
f(\cdot,\boldsymbol{c})
has a unique minimizer for any context
\boldsymbol{c}
and exhibits approximately quadratic behavior around the minimizer. Under these assumptions,
Chen and Gallego 2018
propose an algorithm with
O(T^{\frac{p+2}{p+4}})
contextual regret.
In
Li et al. 2019
, the authors addressed the problem of contextual bandits when the functions are strongly convex and smooth and derived a bound for the contextual regret that scales as
O(T^{\frac{p+1}{p+2}})
up to logarithmic factors. To get this result,
Li et al. 2019
additionally assumed that, for each
\boldsymbol{c}\in[0,1]^{p}
, the global minimizer
\argmin_{\boldsymbol{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{c})
is an interior point of the constraint set
\Theta
.
While this assumption might be hard to check in practice, it does simplify the analysis considerably. In the present paper, we drop this assumption and obtain in Section
4
an upper bound that improves upon the result of

Li et al. 2019
. Furthermore, in Section
5
we present a matching lower bound demonstrating that the algorithm that we propose is minimax optimal to within a logarithmic factor.

In all the papers on continuum contextual bandits cited above, the contexts are adversarial, so the contextual regret is a variant of the dynamic regret as discussed after (
3
). The dynamic regret was also in the focus of studies in various settings of online convex optimization. For zero-order optimization (aka gradient-free optimization), the dynamic regret has been studied in
Zinkevich 2003
;
Yang et al. 2016
;
Zhang et al. 2018
;
Chen and Giannakis 2018
;
Zhao et al. 2021
. Among these papers,
Yang et al. 2016
;
Chen and Giannakis 2018
;
Zhao et al. 2021
have addressed the bandit framework, in which the learner is not allowed to query function evaluations outside of the constraint set
\Theta
.

In the framework of adversarial multi-armed contextual bandits with finite number of arms, without imposing any regularity assumptions on the context such as our Assumption
1
,
Chen et al. 2019
;
Luo et al. 2018
;
Auer et al. 2019
achieved sub-linear regret by assuming that the functions

f_{t}
either change infrequently or vary negligibly over time.

A general conversion theorem

In this section, we propose a meta-algorithm that to any input algorithm achieving a sub-linear static regret in
adversarial bandit setting associates a contextual bandit algorithm with sub-linear contextual regret.
We establish a conversion theorem (Theorem
2
below) that provides an upper bound for the contextual
regret of the output algorithm as a function of the static regret of the input algorithm.

Here and in the sequel, unless other assumption is explicitly stated, we assume that the context
\boldsymbol{c}_{t}
is drawn by an adversary who may be aware of all previous actions of the learner and past information received by the learner. We also assume throughout that for any
\boldsymbol{c}\in[0,1]^{p}
the minimum
\min_{\boldsymbol{z}\in\Theta}f(\boldsymbol{z},\boldsymbol{c})
is attained by some
\boldsymbol{z}^{*}(\boldsymbol{c})\in\Theta
.

We consider strategies for choosing the query points such that
\boldsymbol{z}_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a random variable, and
\boldsymbol{z}_{t}=\pi_{t}(\{\boldsymbol{c}_{k},\boldsymbol{z}_{k},y_{k}\}_{k=1}^{t-1},\boldsymbol{c}_{t},\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{t})
for
t\geq 1
, where
\pi_{t}
is a measurable function with values in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and
(\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{t})_{t\geq 1}
is a sequence of random variables with values in a measurable space
(\mathcal{Z},\mathcal{U})
such that
\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{t}
is independent of
\{\boldsymbol{c}_{k},\boldsymbol{z}_{k},y_{k}\}_{k=1}^{t-1},\boldsymbol{c}_{t}
. We call the process
\pi=(\pi_{t})_{t\geq 1}
a randomized procedure and we denote the set of all randomized procedures by
\Pi
.

Let
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
be a class of real-valued functions on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. We define
\mathcal{F}=\{f:f(\cdot,\boldsymbol{c})\in\mathcal{F}^{\prime}\text{ for all }\boldsymbol{c}\in[0,1]^{p}\}
. Assume that there exists a randomized procedure
\pi

T
runs for the sequence of functions
\{f(\cdot,\boldsymbol{c}_{t})\}_{t=1}^{T}
where
f\in\mathcal{F}
. Namely, if
\{\boldsymbol{z}_{t}\}_{t=1}^{T}
are outputs of
\pi

F:\mathbb{R}^{+}\cup\{0\}\to\mathbb{R}^{+}\cup\{0\}

F_{1}:\mathbb{R}^{+}\cup\{0\}\to\mathbb{R}^{+}\cup\{0\}
such that
F
is a concave function,
F_{1}
is a non-decreasing function, and

\displaystyle{\sup_{f\in\mathcal{F}}\,}\mathbf{E}\left[\sum_{t=1}^{T}f(\boldsymbol{z}_{t},\boldsymbol{c}_{t})-\min_{\boldsymbol{z}\in\Theta}\sum_{t=1}^{T}f(\boldsymbol{z},\boldsymbol{c}_{t})\right]\leq F(T){F_{1}(T)}.

(7)

In particular, we will be interested in the case where there exists
(\tau_{1},\tau_{2},\tau_{3},\mathrm{T}_{0})\in[0,\infty)\times[0,1)\times[0,\infty)\times[1,\infty)
such that, for

,

\displaystyle F(T)\leq Cd^{\tau_{1}}T^{\tau_{2}}\quad\text{and}\quad F_{1}(T)\leq\log^{\tau_{3}}(T+1),

(8)

where
C>0
is a constant that does not depend on
T
and
d
.

.

We call a randomized procedure that satisfies (
7
) and (

-consistent randomized procedure and we denote the set of all such procedures by
\Pi(\mathcal{F},\tau_{1},\tau_{2},\tau_{3},\mathrm{T}_{0})
.

Algorithm 1

Algorithm
1

\{B_{i}\}_{i=1}^{K^{p}}
of the hypercube
[0,1]^{p}
, where all the cells

are hypercubes of equal volume with edges of length
1/K
. Fix a consistent randomized procedure
\pi
for the class of functions
\mathcal{F}
. At each round

\pi

\displaystyle K=K(\tau_{1},\tau_{2},\tau_{3},\gamma)\triangleq\max\left(1,\left\lfloor\left(Lp^{\frac{\gamma}{2}}d^{-\tau_{1}}{T^{(1-\tau_{2})}\log^{-\tau_{3}}(T+1)}\right)^{\frac{1}{p(1-\tau_{2})+\gamma}}\right\rfloor\right),

(9)

then Algorithm

Theorem 2
.

Let
(L,\gamma)\in[0,\infty)\times(0,1]
. Let
\pi

) holds with a concave function
F
, a non-decreasing function

, and let
\boldsymbol{z}_{t}

(a)

It is a known fact (see Appendix

In this section, along with the classes

Definition 10

For

\displaystyle|f(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{c})-f(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{c}^{\prime})|\leq L,\quad\text{for all}\quad\boldsymbol{x}\in\Theta,\,\,\boldsymbol{c},\boldsymbol{c}^{\prime}\in[0,1]^{p}.

Next, we introduce the distribution of the contexts used in deriving the lower bound. Similar to previous sections, for any

, consider the partition of
[0,1]^{p}
, denoted by
\{B_{i}\}_{i=1}^{K^{p}}

Theorem 11
.

For
(\alpha,\beta,M,\gamma,L)\in(0,\infty)\times[3\alpha,\infty)\times[\alpha+1,\infty)\times[0,1]\times[0,\infty)
consider the class of functions
\bar{\mathcal{F}}=\mathcal{F}_{\alpha,\beta}(M)\cap\mathcal{F}_{\gamma}(L)
. Let
\Theta=B^{d}
,
and let
\{\boldsymbol{c}_{t}\}_{t=1}^{T}
be independently distributed according to
\mathbb{P}_{K}
, where
K=\max\left(1,\left\lfloor\left(\min\left(1,L^{2}\right)T\right)^{\frac{1}{p+2\gamma}}\right\rfloor\right)
. Let
\pi\in\Pi
be any randomized procedure and assume that
\{\boldsymbol{z}_{t}\}_{t=1}^{T}
are outputs of
\pi
. Then,

\displaystyle\sup_{f\in\bar{\mathcal{F}}}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbf{E}\left[f(\boldsymbol{z}_{t},\boldsymbol{c}_{t})-\min_{\boldsymbol{z}\in\Theta}f(\boldsymbol{z},\boldsymbol{c}_{t})\right]\geq\texttt{A}\left(\min\left(1,L^{\frac{2(p+\gamma)}{p+2\gamma}}\right)T^{\frac{p+\gamma}{p+2\gamma}}+\min\left(T,d\sqrt{T}\right)\right),

(17)

where
\texttt{A}>0
is a constant that does not depend on
d
,
p
, and
T
.

Applying (
17
) with
L>0
and

\gamma=0
shows that no randomized procedure can achieve sub-linear dynamic regret on the corresponding class

\bar{\mathcal{F}}
. Thus, controlling the increments of
f
with any
L>0
, in the absence of continuity with respect to
\boldsymbol{c}
, is not sufficient to get a sub-linear dynamic regret.

For

\gamma\in(0,1]
, the bound (
17
) combined with Corollary
9
implies that Algorithm
3
with
K
chosen as in Corollary
9
achieves the optimal rate of regret in
T
up to logarithmic factors. An additional (mild) gap between the upper and lower bounds is due to the discrepancy in the factors depending on
d,p,
and
L
.

If
L=0
the bound in (
17
) scales as
\min(T,d\sqrt{T})
. In this case, the objective functions are constant with respect to the context variable,
f(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{c}_{1})=f(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{c}_{t})
, for
\boldsymbol{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
t\in[T]
, so that the contextual regret in (
17
) is equal to the static regret. Moreover, by convexity, both regrets are bounded from below by the optimization error corresponding to
f(\cdot,\boldsymbol{c}_{1})
multiplied by
T
. Using this remark we notice that, for
L=0
, the bound (
17
) agrees with the lower bound on the optimization error for strongly convex and smooth functions, known to be of the order
d/\sqrt{T}
, see
Shamir 2013
;
Akhavan et al. 2020
;
Akhavan et al. 2024
.

Furthermore, note that since the class of strongly convex functions is included in the class of convex functions, (
17
) is a valid lower bound for convex contextual bandits. Together with Corollary
4
, this implies that the minimax optimal rate as a function of
T
for convex contextual bandits is of the order
T^{\frac{p+\gamma}{p+2\gamma}}
up to logarithmic factors.

Theorem
11
can be stated in a more general manner. In the definition of
\Pi
, the randomized procedures are restricted to query only within the constraint set
\Theta
. However, in the proof of the lower bound, we did not impose such a restriction, allowing
\pi
to query anywhere within
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Thus, Theorem
11
remains valid for this broader set of algorithms.
Furthermore, the proof of Theorem
11
can be extended to hold for any convex body
\Theta
and not only for
\Theta=B^{d}
. Indeed, by shifting and scaling the unit Euclidean ball, we can embed it in
\Theta
. Subsequently, we can apply the same shifting and scaling to the action argument of the set of functions
f_{\boldsymbol{\omega},\boldsymbol{\tau}}
in the proof of Theorem
11
.

Lemma 14

.

, and set

\alpha

\boldsymbol{x}_{t}

\displaystyle=\argmin_{\boldsymbol{u}\in\Theta}\sum_{k=1}^{t}\langle\boldsymbol{g}_{k},\boldsymbol{u}\rangle+\underbrace{\frac{\alpha}{2}\sum_{k=1}^{t}\left\lVert\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{x}_{k-1}\right\rVert^{2}+\eta_{t}^{-1}\mathcal{R}(\boldsymbol{u})}_{=\vcentcolon R_{t}(\boldsymbol{u})}

\left\lVert\boldsymbol{c}\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq\varepsilon/2
we have

d^{2}(\boldsymbol{c},\partial B_{j})=\left\lVert\boldsymbol{c}-\boldsymbol{u}^{*}\right\rVert^{2}\geq|c_{i^{*}}-u_{i^{*}}^{*}|^{2}\geq\min(|c_{i^{*}}-1/2|^{2},|c_{i^{*}}+1/2|^{2})\geq\Big(\frac{1-\varepsilon}{2}\Big)^{2}.

This proves (
47
).
∎

Appendix D
General comments on self-concordant barriers

In
(
Nesterov and Nemirovskii 1994
, Proposition 2.3.6)
, the authors demonstrated the existence of a convex body
\Theta
such that any
\mu
-self-concordant barrier over
\Theta
should have
\mu\geq d
. The proposition is stated as follows:

Statement of
(
Nesterov and Nemirovskii 1994
, Proposition 2.3.6)
: Let
\Theta\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a convex polytope, such that certain boundary points of
\Theta
belong to
m
of
(d-1)
-dimensional facets of
\Theta
, where
m\in[d]
. Moreover, assume that the normal vectors to these facets are linearly independent. Then the value of
\mu
for any
\mu
-self-concordant barrier on
\Theta
cannot be less than
m
. In particular, the
d
-dimensional non-negative orthant, simplex, and hyper-cube do not admit barriers with the parameter less than
\mu
.

For the case when
\Theta
is a polytope, one can construct the following self-concordant barrier
(
Nemirovski 2004
, Corollary 3.1.1)

•

\mathcal{R}(\boldsymbol{u})=-\sum_{j=1}^{m}\log(\langle\boldsymbol{a}_{j},\boldsymbol{u}\rangle-b_{j})
is an
m
-self-concordant barrier for the polytope

\Theta=\{\boldsymbol{u}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\langle\boldsymbol{a}_{j},\boldsymbol{u}\rangle\geq b_{j},\quad\text{for }j\in[m]\quad\text{and}\quad\boldsymbol{a}_{j}\in\mathbb{R}^{d},b_{j}\in\mathbb{R}\}.

Note that in the above, if
\Theta
is the
d
-dimensional hyper-cube then
m=2d
, and in this case
(
Nesterov and Nemirovskii 1994
, Proposition 2.3.6)
indicates that the above
\mathcal{R}
has an optimal
\mu
as a function of
d
. Moreover, if
\Theta
is the
d
-dimensional simplex then
m=d+1
, which again by
(
Nesterov and Nemirovskii 1994
, Proposition 2.3.6)
we can see that the above example of
\mathcal{R}
has an optimal
\mu
as a function of
d
.

For the case when
\Theta
is scaled version of the unit Euclidean ball with ratio
r>0
, one can consider the following self-concordant barrier
(
Nemirovski 2004
, Example 9.3.2)

•

\mathcal{R}(\boldsymbol{u})=-\log(r^{2}-\left\lVert\boldsymbol{u}\right\rVert^{2})
is a
2
-self-concordant barrier for
\Theta
.

Considering the result in
(
Nesterov and Nemirovskii 1994
, Corollary 2.3.3)
, we have always that
\mu\geq 1
which shows the optimality of the above
\mathcal{R}
for the unit Euclidean ball.

We refer the reader to
(
Nemirovski 2004
, Chapter 9)
for further examples of self-concordant barrier functions.

In the general case of a convex body
\Theta
,
Bubeck and Eldan 2014
;
Hildebrand 2014
;
Fox 2015
, proposed
\mu
-self-concordant barriers with
\mu\leq\texttt{A}d
, where
\texttt{A}>0
is a non-increasing function of
d
. Particularly,
(
Bubeck and Eldan 2014
, Theorem 1)
shows that
\texttt{A}\leq 1+100\sqrt{\log(d)/d}
, for all
d\geq 80
. All of these constructions by
Bubeck and Eldan 2014
;
Hildebrand 2014
;
Fox 2015
are computationally expensive (see
(
Bubeck and Eldan 2014
, Section 2.2)
for further details on the computational aspects of these methods). Now, let us consider the special case where
\Theta
is the unit
\ell_{q}
-ball for
q\in(2,\infty]
, i.e.,

\Theta=\{\boldsymbol{u}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\sum_{j=1}^{d}|\boldsymbol{u}_{j}|^{q}\leq 1\},

for
q\in(2,\infty)
, and

\Theta=\{\boldsymbol{u}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\max_{j\in[d]}|\boldsymbol{u}_{j}|\leq 1\},

for
q=\infty
. In
(
Bubeck et al. 2018
, Lemma 11 and Theorem 5)
, the authors showed that any
\mu
-self-concordant barrier on
\Theta
should satisfy

\mu\geq\texttt{A}\max\left(d^{\frac{1}{q}}\log(d)^{-\frac{q}{q-2}},d^{1-\frac{2}{q}}\right),

where
\texttt{A}>0
only depends on
q
. The lower bound indicates that for the case of
q=\infty
, the
(d+1)
-self-concordant barrier proposed by
Bubeck and Eldan 2014
;
Hildebrand 2014
;
Fox 2015
is optimal with respect to
\mu
as a function of
d
. However, constructing an optimal
\mu
-self-concordant barrier for any
\ell_{q}
-ball for
q\in(2,\infty)
remains an open problem.

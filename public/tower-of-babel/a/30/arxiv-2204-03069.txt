Generalized level measure based on a family of conditional aggregation operators
Michał Boczek, Ondrej Hutník, Marek Kaluszka, Miriam Kleinová

Abstract
Extending the concept of level measure μ ( { f ⩾ a } ) \displaystyle\mu(\{f\geqslant a\}) we introduce a generalized level measure based on a family of conditional aggregations operators. We investigate in detail several basic properties, including connections with the family of level measures, the generalized survival function and the transformation of monotone measures to hyperset. Applications in scientometrics and information science are described.

2.2
Overview of conditional aggregation operators

A crucial concept used in this paper is a conditional aggregation operator introduced in
[

]

4

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\displaystyle E\in\Sigma^{0}.
A map
\displaystyle\mathsf{A}(\cdot|E)\colon\mathbf{F}\to[0,\infty]

is said to be a
conditional aggregation operator w.r.t.
\displaystyle E
(CAO, for short) if it satisfies the following conditions:

\displaystyle(C1)

\displaystyle\mathsf{A}(f|E)\leqslant\mathsf{A}(g|E)
for any
\displaystyle f,g\in\mathbf{F}
such that
\displaystyle f(x)\leqslant g(x)
for all
\displaystyle x\in E
;

\displaystyle(C2)

\displaystyle\mathsf{A}(\mathbbm{1}_{E^{c}}|E)=0,
where
\displaystyle E^{c}=X\setminus E.

The set
\displaystyle E
will be called a
conditional set
.

The value
\displaystyle\mathsf{A}(f|E)
can be interpreted as “an aggregated value of
\displaystyle f
on
\displaystyle E.
” In other words, the CAO only depends on the value of the considered function defined on the conditional set. Some basic properties of CAOs are summarized in the following proposition.

Proposition 2.2
.

(cf.
[
1

)
If
\displaystyle\mathsf{A}(\cdot|E)
is a CAO w.r.t.
\displaystyle E\in\Sigma_{0},
then
\displaystyle\mathsf{A}(0_{X}|E)=0
and
\displaystyle\mathsf{A}(f|E)=\mathsf{A}(f\mathbbm{1}_{E}|E)
for any
\displaystyle f\in\mathbf{F}.

Several classes of CAOs used in this paper are introduced in the following definition.

Definition 2.3
.

\displaystyle\theta\in(0,\infty).
We say that a CAO w.r.t.
\displaystyle E

•

(super)homogeneous of degree

\displaystyle\theta

\displaystyle\mathsf{A}(\lambda f|E)(\geqslant)=\lambda^{\theta}\mathsf{A}(f|E)

\displaystyle\lambda\in(0,\infty)

\displaystyle f\in\mathbf{F}

•

idempotent

\displaystyle\mathsf{A}(b\mathbbm{1}_{X}|E)=b

\displaystyle b\in(0,\infty).

A

\displaystyle\displaystyle\mathscr{A}=\{\mathsf{A}(\cdot|E)\colon E\in{\mathcal{E}}\},

(1)

2.3
Motivations

In
[
1
]
it is introduced a generalization of the survival function. Starting from the formula

\displaystyle\displaystyle\mu(\{f>a\})

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\mu\big(X\setminus\{x\in X\colon f(x)\leqslant a\}\big)

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\inf\big\{\mu(E^{c})\colon\sup_{x\in E}f(x)\leqslant a,\,E\in\Sigma\big\},\quad a\in[0,\infty),\,f\in\mathbf{F},

where
\displaystyle\{f>a\}=\{x\in X\colon f(x)>a\}
,
and replacing
\displaystyle{\textstyle{}\sup_{x\in E}f(x)}
and
\displaystyle\Sigma
with a CAO
\displaystyle\mathsf{A}(f|E)
and
\displaystyle{\mathcal{E}},
respectively,
the generalized survival function in the form

\displaystyle\displaystyle\mu_{\mathscr{A}}^{S}(f,a)=\inf\big\{\mu(E^{c})\colon\mathsf{A}(f|E)\leqslant a,\,E\in{\mathcal{E}}\big\}

(2)

is obtained (see
[
1
, Def. 4.1]
). It turns out that this modification can be used in several applications related to decision-making processes (see
[
1
, Sec. 2 and 6]
).

In this paper we propose an extension of the level measure. Observe that the level measure can be rewritten as follows

\displaystyle\displaystyle\mu(\{f\geqslant a\})

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\sup\{\mu(E)\colon E\subseteq\{f\geqslant a\},\,E\in\Sigma\}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\sup\{\mu(E)\colon\inf_{x\in E}f(x)\geqslant a,\,E\in\Sigma\},\quad a\in[0,\infty),\,f\in\mathbf{F}.

Putting
\displaystyle\mathsf{A}(f|E)
instead of
\displaystyle{\textstyle{}\inf_{x\in E}f(x)}
and
\displaystyle{\mathcal{E}}
instead of
\displaystyle\Sigma,
respectively, we get the following generalization of the level measure

\displaystyle\displaystyle\mu_{\mathscr{A}}(f,a)=\sup\big\{\mu(E)\colon\mathsf{A}(f|E)\geqslant a,\,E\in{\mathcal{E}}\big\}

(3)

(see Section
3
for more details).

Formulas (
2
) and (
3
) can be used for evaluation of both the survival function and the level measure.
Indeed, observe that if
\displaystyle\mathscr{A}=\{\mathsf{A}(\cdot|E)\colon E\in\Sigma\}
and
\displaystyle\mathsf{A}^{\inf}(\cdot|E)\leqslant\mathsf{A}(\cdot|E)\leqslant\mathsf{A}^{\sup}(\cdot|E)
for all
\displaystyle E\in\Sigma^{0}
, then for all
\displaystyle a\geqslant 0
and any
\displaystyle(f,\mu)\in\mathbf{F}\times\mathbf{M}
we get the following bounds

\displaystyle\displaystyle\mu_{\mathscr{A}}^{S}(f,a)\leqslant\mu(\{f>a\})\leqslant\mu(\{f\geqslant a\})\leqslant\mu_{\mathscr{A}}(f,a).

(4)

There exist situations in which the generalized survival function (
2
) cannot be used, but the generalized level measure (
3
) can help to fix them. As a demonstration we consider the following motivation from scientometry.

Example 2.5
.

Let
\displaystyle X={\mathbb{N}}
,
\displaystyle\Sigma=2^{\mathbb{N}}
and
\displaystyle\mu
be the counting measure.
A scholar with some publications is described by a function
\displaystyle f\colon\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
called a
scientific record
such that
\displaystyle f(1)\geqslant f(2)\geqslant\ldots.

The positive value of
\displaystyle f(i)
gives the number of citations of scholar’s
\displaystyle i
th publication, whereas the value
\displaystyle f(i)=0
means either a paper with zero citations or a nonexisting paper.
Then
\displaystyle\mu(\{f\geqslant k\})
with
\displaystyle k\in{\mathbb{N}}
returns the number of scholar’s papers having at least
\displaystyle k
citations each. The modified formula (
3
) with
\displaystyle\mathsf{A}(f|E)={\textstyle{}\inf_{x\in E}f(x)},
i.e.,

\displaystyle\displaystyle\mu(f,k)=\sup\big\{\mu(E)\colon\inf_{x\in E}f(x)\geqslant k,\,E\in{\mathcal{E}}\big\}

(5)

admits various interpretations. For instance, if
\displaystyle{\mathcal{E}}=\{\emptyset,E\},
where
\displaystyle E
consists of scholar’s papers published just in the last five years, then
\displaystyle\mu(f,k)
defined in (
5
) gives a number of papers having at least
\displaystyle k
citations among the papers from this period. This approach may provide a fairer comparison of researchers with different professional internship (e.g., a young scientist beginning of his/her carrier versus a well-enabled scientist with many years in science).
Obviously one can replace
\displaystyle\textstyle{\inf_{x\in E}f(x)}
in (
5
) with a CAO
\displaystyle\mathsf{A}(f|E)
to change the criterion. More examples are provided in Section
4
.

Remark 2.6
.

Formula (
3
) with
\displaystyle\mathscr{A}=\{\mathsf{A}^{\inf}(\cdot|E)\colon E\in{\mathcal{E}}\}
uncovers a connection between the level measure and inner set function. Recall that the inner set function of
\displaystyle\mu\in\mathbf{M}
(see
[
10
, p. 21]
) is defined as follows

\displaystyle\mu_{\star}(B)=\sup\{\mu(E)\colon E\subseteq B,\,E\in{\mathcal{E}}\}

for any
\displaystyle B\in 2^{X}.
If
\displaystyle f\in\mathbf{F},
then for
\displaystyle a\geqslant 0
we can write

\displaystyle\displaystyle\mu_{\star}(\{f\geqslant a\})

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\sup\{\mu(E)\colon E\subseteq\{f\geqslant a\},\,E\in{\mathcal{E}}\}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\sup\big\{\mu(E)\colon\inf_{x\in E}f(x)\geqslant a,\,E\in{\mathcal{E}}\big\}=\mu_{\mathscr{A}}(f,a).

The level measure and the survival function appear interchangeably in commonly known integrals of nonnegative functions, such as the Choquet and the Sugeno integral. However, for some extensions of the above operators, interchanging the level measure and the survival function can change their value.
The following example illustrates such situation.

Example 2.7
.

Let
\displaystyle\mathrm{S}
be a semicopula

2

A binary function

\displaystyle\mathrm{S}(x,y)=x\wedge y

\displaystyle\mu(C)=0.5

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sup_{a\in[0,1]}\mathrm{S}\big(a,\mu(\{f\geqslant a\})\big)

3
Generalized level measure

Inspired by stochastic processes, we consider families of concepts from Section

Note that a family
\displaystyle\bm{\mu}=(\mu_{t})_{t\in[0,1]}

\displaystyle\mu_{t}\in\mathbf{M}

\displaystyle t,

\displaystyle\mu\in\mathbf{M}

\displaystyle\mu_{s}\leqslant\mu_{t}

\displaystyle\mu_{s}\geqslant\mu_{t}

\displaystyle s,t

\displaystyle s<t.

\displaystyle\displaystyle\mathscr{A}_{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}|\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}}=\{\mathsf{A}_{t}(\cdot|E)\colon E\in{\mathcal{E}}_{t},\,t\geqslant 0\},

\displaystyle{\mathcal{E}}

\displaystyle\bm{\mu}_{\mathscr{A}_{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}|}}(f,t)>0.
Hence there exists
\displaystyle E\in\Sigma^{0}
such that
\displaystyle\mathsf{A}_{t}(f|E)\geqslant t
and
\displaystyle\mu_{t}(E)>0.
Then
\displaystyle M\mathbbm{1}_{X}\geqslant f
and
\displaystyle 0.5M=0.5\,\mathsf{A}^{\inf}(M\mathbbm{1}_{X}|E)\geqslant 0.5\,\mathsf{A}^{\inf}(f|E)=\mathsf{A}_{t}(f|E)\geqslant t>M,
a contradiction.
To sum up, the equality (

) is true for any
\displaystyle t\in(0,\infty)
and any
\displaystyle(f,\bm{\mu})\in\mathbf{F}_{\ast,M}\times\mathbf{M}_{\mathrm{f}},

but
\displaystyle\mathscr{A}_{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}|}\neq\mathscr{A}^{\inf}.

Theorem
3.18
results that in order to get the equality (

for families of CAOs

can be used.
This highlights the role of the level measure in the developed theory. However, for certain families of monotone measures one can show that the generalized level measure still may end up in the level measure (

For instance, consider
\displaystyle\mathscr{A}_{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}|}^{\operatorname*{ess\,inf}_{\bm{\mu}}}=\{\mathsf{A}^{\operatorname*{ess\,inf}_{\mu_{t}}}(\cdot|E)\colon E\in\Sigma,\,\mu_{t}\in\mathbf{M},\,t\geqslant 0\}

\displaystyle f\in\mathbf{F}
on
\displaystyle E
w.r.t.
\displaystyle\mu_{t}.

Recall that
\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{\mu}
is the set of all

w.r.t.
\displaystyle\mu\in\mathbf{M}

\displaystyle N\in\mathcal{N}_{\mu}

[

Proposition 3.20
.

Let
\displaystyle\bm{\mu}\in\mathbf{M}_{\mathrm{f}}
be such that
\displaystyle\mu_{t}
is continuous from below for any
\displaystyle t>0.

Then for any
\displaystyle t>0

we have

\bm{\mu}_{\mathscr{A}_{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}|}^{\operatorname*{ess\,inf}_{\bm{\mu}}}}(f,t)=\mu_{t}(\{f\geqslant t\}).

Proof.
Clearly,
\displaystyle\mathsf{A}^{\inf}(\cdot|E)\leqslant\mathsf{A}^{\operatorname*{ess\,inf}_{\mu_{t}}}(\cdot|E)
for any
\displaystyle t
and any
\displaystyle E\in\Sigma,
as
\displaystyle{\textstyle{}\inf_{x\in E}f(x)}=\sup\{a\in[0,\infty)\colon\{f\mathbbm{1}_{E}<a\}=\emptyset\}.
Then by Proposition

we get

\displaystyle\displaystyle\mu_{t}(\{f\geqslant t\})\leqslant\bm{\mu}_{\mathscr{A}_{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}|}^{\operatorname*{ess\,inf}_{\bm{\mu}}}}(f,t)

(19)

for any
\displaystyle t>0.

Now we shall show the validity of the reverse inequality. For a fixed
\displaystyle t>0

\displaystyle E\in\Sigma

\displaystyle\mathsf{A}^{\operatorname*{ess\,inf}_{\mu_{t}}}(f|E)\geqslant t

Put

\displaystyle n\in\mathbb{N}

Obviously,

By continuity from below of

and

By

\displaystyle\displaystyle\mu_{t}(E)=\mu_{t}(E\setminus N)\leqslant\mu_{t}(\{f\geqslant t\}\setminus N)=\mu_{t}(\{f\geqslant t\}).

(21)

Combining inequalities (

21

we get the result.

3.3
Connection with the generalized survival function

In this section we describe the relationship between the generalized level measure (
7
) and the generalized survival function introduced in
[
1
, formula (12)]
. We first recall the latter concept.

Definition 3.22
.

Let
\displaystyle\widehat{\mathscr{A}_{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}|\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}}}=\{\widehat{\mathsf{A}}_{t}(\cdot|E)\colon E\in{\mathcal{E}}_{t},\,t\geqslant 0\}
be a pFCA such that
\displaystyle\widehat{\mathsf{A}}_{t}(\cdot|\emptyset)=0
for any
\displaystyle t.
The
generalized survival function of
\displaystyle f\in\mathbf{F}
w.r.t.
\displaystyle\widehat{\mathscr{A}_{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}|\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}}}

is defined as

\displaystyle\displaystyle\bm{\mu}_{\widehat{\mathscr{A}_{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}|\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}}}}^{S}(f,t)=\inf\big\{\mu_{t}(E^{c})\colon\widehat{\mathsf{A}}_{t}(f|E)\leqslant t,\,E\in{\mathcal{E}}_{t}\big\},\quad t\geqslant 0.

(22)

It is worth mentioning that the generalized survival function (
22
) is well defined as
\displaystyle E^{c}\in\Sigma
for any
\displaystyle E\in\mathcal{E}_{t}
and
\displaystyle t
, and the set

\displaystyle\{E\in{\mathcal{E}}_{t}\colon\widehat{\mathsf{A}}_{t}(f|E)\leqslant t\}
is nonempty for all
\displaystyle t,
since
\displaystyle\widehat{\mathsf{A}}_{t}(\cdot|\emptyset)=0
and
\displaystyle\emptyset\in{\mathcal{E}}_{t}.

The generalized survival function extends the concept of survival function
\displaystyle\mu(\{f>t\}),
where
\displaystyle\mu\in\mathbf{M}.

Indeed, setting a constant
\displaystyle\bm{\mu}
and
\displaystyle\widehat{\mathscr{A}_{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}|\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}}}=\mathscr{A}^{\sup}
with
\displaystyle\mathsf{A}^{\sup}(\cdot|\emptyset)=0

in (

) we get the survival function (for more details, see
[
1
]
).

For the benefit of the reader we summarize the main differences of the generalized level measure and generalized survival function:

•

both notions are based on the pFCA
\displaystyle\mathscr{A}_{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}|\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}}=\{\mathsf{A}_{t}(\cdot|E)\colon E\in{\mathcal{E}}_{t},\,t\geqslant 0\}
, but with different conventions on the empty set;

•

in the generalized level measure we measure the sets
\displaystyle E\in{\mathcal{E}}_{t}
for which
\displaystyle\mathsf{A}_{t}(f|E)\geqslant t
, whereas in the generalized survival function we measure complements of sets
\displaystyle E\in{\mathcal{E}}_{t}
for which
\displaystyle\mathsf{A}_{t}(f|E)\leqslant t
;

•

finally, in the generalized level measure we take supremum of all such values of
\displaystyle\mu_{t}(E)
, whereas in the generalized survival function we take infimum of all
\displaystyle\mu_{t}(E^{c})
.

These observations suggest to describe their “complementary” or “dual” behaviour.
Assume that

\displaystyle b\in(0,\infty).

Given a family
\displaystyle\bm{\mu}=(\mu_{t})_{t\geqslant 0}
of monotone measures such that
\displaystyle\mu_{t}(X)=\mu_{0}(X)<\infty
for all
\displaystyle t
,
we define the
dual family
\displaystyle\widehat{\bm{\mu}}=(\widehat{\mu}_{t})_{t\geqslant 0}

to
\displaystyle\bm{\mu}
by

\displaystyle\displaystyle\widehat{\mu}_{t}(E)=\mu_{0}(X)-\mu_{(b-t)_{+}}(E^{c}),\quad t\geqslant 0,\,E\in\Sigma,

(23)

where
\displaystyle a_{+}=\max\{a,0\}.

It is clear that
\displaystyle\widehat{\bm{\mu}}\in\mathbf{M}_{\mathrm{f}}
with
\displaystyle\widehat{\mu}_{t}(X)=\mu_{0}(X)
for any
\displaystyle t.
Let
\displaystyle\mathscr{A}_{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}|}=\{\mathsf{A}_{t}(\cdot|E)\colon E\in{\mathcal{E}},\,t\geqslant 0\}
be a pFCA such that
\displaystyle\mathsf{A}_{t}(b\mathbbm{1}_{X}|E)=b
for any
\displaystyle t
and any
\displaystyle E\in{\mathcal{E}}^{0}.
Define the
dual pFCA to

\displaystyle\mathscr{A}_{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}|}
by

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathscr{A}_{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}|}}=\{\widehat{\mathsf{A}}_{t}(\cdot|E)\colon E\in{\mathcal{E}},\,t\geqslant 0\}
with

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathsf{A}}_{t}(f|E)=b-\mathsf{A}_{(b-t)_{+}}\big((b\mathbbm{1}_{X}-f)_{+}|E\big)

and
\displaystyle\widehat{\mathsf{A}}_{t}(\cdot|\emptyset)=0.

Clearly,
\displaystyle\widehat{\mathsf{A}}_{t}(b\mathbbm{1}_{X}|E)=\mathsf{A}_{t}(b\mathbbm{1}_{X}|E)=b
for any
\displaystyle t.

Put
\displaystyle f\in\mathbf{F}_{b}=\{f\in\mathbf{F}\colon\textstyle{\sup_{x\in X}f(x)}\leqslant b\}.

Then, for each
\displaystyle(f,\bm{\mu})\in\mathbf{F}_{b}\times\mathbf{M}_{\mathrm{f}}
and
\displaystyle t\in[0,b]
we get

\displaystyle\displaystyle\bm{\mu}_{\mathscr{A}_{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}|}}(f,t)

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\sup\{\widehat{\mu}_{0}(X)-\widehat{\mu}_{b-t}(E^{c})\colon\mathsf{A}_{t}(f|E)\geqslant t,\,E\in{\mathcal{E}}\}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\widehat{\mu}_{0}(X)-\inf\{\widehat{\mu}_{b-t}(E^{c})\colon\mathsf{A}_{t}(f|E)\geqslant t,\,E\in{\mathcal{E}}\}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\widehat{\mu}_{0}(X)-\inf\{\widehat{\mu}_{b-t}(E^{c})\colon\widehat{\mathsf{A}}_{b-t}(b\mathbbm{1}_{X}-f|E)\leqslant b-t,\,E\in{\mathcal{E}}\}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\mu_{0}(X)-\widehat{\bm{\mu}}^{S}_{\widehat{\mathscr{A}_{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}|}}}(b\mathbbm{1}_{X}-f,b-t).

To sum up, we get the following relationship between the generalized level measure and the generalized survival function.

\displaystyle b\in(0,\infty)

Example 4.2

Consider a system with components
\displaystyle b
and
\displaystyle c,
which can be selected from the sets
\displaystyle\{b_{i}\colon i\in[k]\}
and
\displaystyle\{c_{j}\colon j\in[m]\},

Let
\displaystyle p(b_{i})
(resp.
\displaystyle p(c_{j})

\displaystyle b_{i}
(resp.
\displaystyle c_{j}

If
\displaystyle b
and
\displaystyle c
are connected in parallel and operate independently of each other, then the probability of survival of the system is equal to
\displaystyle 1-(1-p(b))(1-p(c))=p(b)+p(c)-p(b)p(c).

Denote by
\displaystyle\pi(B)
the price of the system
\displaystyle B=\{b,c\}
and let
\displaystyle K

We say that
\displaystyle B
is
the best system
if it has the lowest

\displaystyle p
, where
\displaystyle p
is fixed.

Set
\displaystyle X=\{b_{1},\ldots,b_{k},c_{1},\ldots,c_{m}\}
,
\displaystyle\widehat{B}=\{\{b_{i},c_{j}\}\colon i\in[k],\,j\in[m]\},

\displaystyle\mu(B)=(1-\pi(B)/K)_{+}

\displaystyle\mathsf{A}(f|B)=p(b)+p(c)-p(b)p(c)

\displaystyle f(x)=p(x)

\displaystyle x\in X.

\displaystyle\widehat{\mu}_{6}(\widehat{B})
given by (

).
An extension of this example to multicomponent systems is possible.

5
Conclusion

In this paper, we have proposed a generalization of the level measure with its examples. We have presented some properties and applications related to scientometric indices. It turns out that the generalization of the survival function introduced in
[
1
]
cannot be used in this application, hence the need to introduce the generalized level measure. When using Proposition
3.17
, the level measure is, for some parametric families of CAOs, smaller than the generalized level measure.
From (
4
) it follows that we may give both the lower and upper bound for the Choquet integral by means of new Choquet-like functionals of the form

\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\mu_{\mathscr{A}}^{S}(f,a)\,{\mathrm{d}}a\quad\text{and}\quad\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\mu_{\mathscr{A}}(f,a)\,{\mathrm{d}}a,

and introduce novel classes of integrable functions
\displaystyle L_{\mu}^{S}
, cf.
[
1
]
, and
\displaystyle L_{\mu},
respectively. Clearly,
\displaystyle L_{\mu}\subseteq L_{\mu}^{C}\subseteq L_{\mu}^{S}
, where

\displaystyle L^{C}_{\mu}
is the set of Choquet integrable functions. A detailed study and practical consequences are left for the future work.

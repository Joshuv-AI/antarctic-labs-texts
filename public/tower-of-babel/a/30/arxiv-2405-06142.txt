Codeword Stabilized Codes from m m -Uniform Graph States
Sowrabh Sudevan, Sourin Das, Thamadathil Aswanth, Nupur Patanker, Navin Kashyap

Abstract
An m m -uniform quantum state on n n qubits is an entangled state in which every m m -qubit subsystem is maximally mixed. Starting with an m m -uniform state realized as the graph state associated with an m m -regular graph, and a classical [ n , k , d ≥ m + 1 ] [n,k,d\geq m+1] binary linear code with certain additional properties, we show that pure [ [ n , k , m + 1 ] ] 2 [[n,k,m+1]]_{2} quantum error-correcting codes (QECCs) can be constructed within the codeword stabilized (CWS) code framework. As illustrations, we construct pure [ [ 2 2 ​ r − 1 , 2 2 ​ r − 2 ​ r − 3 , 3 ] ] 2 [[2^{2r}-1,2^{2r}-2r-3,3]]_{2} and [ [ ( 2 4 ​ r − 1 ) 2 , ( 2 4 ​ r − 1 ) 2 − 32 ​ r − 7 , 5 ] ] 2 [[(2^{4r}-1)^{2},(2^{4r}-1)^{2}-32r-7,5]]_{2} QECCs. We also give measurement-based protocols for encoding into code states and for recovery of logical qubits from code states.

[[n,k,m+1]]
QECC if and only if the minimum symplectic weight of a non-zero vector in
\mathcal{W}
is at least
m+1
.

Proof.

Suppose that there is no non-zero vector of symplectic weight at most
m
in
\mathcal{W}
. Then, for any non-identity operator
E\in\mathcal{P}_{n}
of weight at most
m
, we must have
Z_{\mathbf{c}}E\notin\mathcal{S}
for all
\mathbf{c}\in\mathcal{C}
. This implies

\bra{\mathrm{G}}Z_{\mathbf{c}}E\ket{\mathrm{G}}=0\hskip 14.22636pt\forall\mathbf{c}\in\mathcal{C},

(1)

so that, for any
\mathbf{c}_{1},\mathbf{c}_{2}\in\mathcal{C}
, we have

\bra{\mathrm{G}}Z_{\mathbf{c}_{1}}Z_{\mathbf{c}_{2}}E\ket{\mathrm{G}}=0.

Since
Z_{\mathbf{c}_{2}}E=\pm EZ_{\mathbf{c}_{2}}
and
Z_{\mathbf{c}_{1}}^{\dagger}=Z_{\mathbf{c}_{1}}
, we in fact have

\bra{\mathrm{G}}Z_{\mathbf{c}_{1}}^{\dagger}EZ_{\mathbf{c}_{2}}\ket{\mathrm{G}}=0\hskip 14.22636pt\forall\mathbf{c}_{1},\mathbf{c}_{2}\in\mathcal{C}.

(2)

Therefore,
\mathcal{Q}:=\text{span}\{Z_{\mathbf{c}}\ket{\mathrm{G}}:\mathbf{c}\in\mathcal{C}\}
is a pure
[[n,k,m+1]]
QECC.

Conversely, if
\mathcal{Q}
is a pure
[[n,k,m+1]]
QECC, then for any non-identity operator
E\in\mathcal{P}_{n}
of weight at most
m
, Eq. (
2
) must hold. This implies that Eq. (
1
) also holds, from which it follows that the minimum symplectic weight of a non-zero vector in
\mathcal{W}
is at least
m+1
.
∎

Theorem
1
does not explicitly impose an
m
-uniformity condition on the graph state
\ket{\mathrm{G}}
. However, to obtain a pure
[[n,k,m+1]]
QECC via the theorem,
\ket{\mathrm{G}}
must necessarily be
m
-uniform. This is because the minimum symplectic weight of a non-zero vector in
\mathcal{W}
can be at least
m+1
only if
\ket{\mathrm{G}}
is
m
-uniform. Indeed,
\mathcal{W}
contains all the non-zero vectors in
\mathcal{V}_{\mathcal{S}}
, which correspond to the non-identity stabilizers of
\ket{\mathrm{G}}
. Therefore, the condition on the minimum symplectic weight of a non-zero vector in
\mathcal{W}
implies that all the non-identity stabilizers of
\ket{\mathrm{G}}
have weight at least
m+1
, so that, by Proposition
1
,
\ket{\mathrm{G}}
must be
m
-uniform. Thus, in all the constructions that follow of
[[n,k,m+1]]
QECCs based on Theorem
1
, we will always start with an
m
-uniform graph state
\ket{\mathrm{G}}
.

Given
n
and an
m
-uniform graph state
\ket{\mathrm{G}}
, one can ask what the largest value of
k
is for which an
[[n,k,m+1]]
QECC can be constructed using Theorem
1
. However, this is not easy to determine. Consider the set,
\mathcal{B}_{m}(\mathcal{V}_{\mathcal{S}})\subseteq\{0,1\}^{2n}
, consisting of all vectors of the form
\mathbf{v}+\mathbf{x}
with
\mathbf{v}\in\mathcal{V}_{\mathcal{S}}
and
\mathbf{x}\in\{0,1\}^{2n}
,
\mathrm{wt}_{\mathrm{s}}(\mathbf{x})\leq m
. (Here, again, the vector addition is coordinatewise, modulo
2
.) Let
\mathcal{V}_{X}
denote the binary vectors corresponding to the Pauli operators containing at least one
X
. In order to get the tent peg code that maximizes the dimension of the resulting QECC, we need to find the largest subspace of
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{2n}
that has no intersection with
\mathcal{B}_{m}(\mathcal{V}_{\mathcal{S}})\cup\mathcal{V}_{X}
except
\mathbf{0}
. This problem reduces to the 4-colorability problem, which is known to be NP-hard
[
42
]
. So, instead of trying to find the QECC of largest dimension that can be constructed in this way, we aim to identify some simple conditions on the linear code
\mathcal{C}
that will result in QECCs of reasonably large dimension.

As we saw in Proposition
1
,
m
-uniformity of
\ket{\mathrm{G}}
implies that the degree of every vertex in
G
is at least
m
. If we further insist that
\ket{\mathrm{G}}
arises from a graph
G
in which every vertex has degree equal to
m
(i.e., an
m
-regular graph), then by choosing a tent peg code
\mathcal{C}
of sufficiently large minimum distance, we obtain a construction of a pure
[[n,\dim\mathcal{C},m+1]]
QECC via Theorem
1
.

Theorem 2
.

Let
\ket{\mathrm{G}}
be an
m
-uniform graph state on
n
qubits constructed from an
m
-regular graph with
n
vertices. Then, for any
[n,k,d]
binary linear code
\mathcal{C}
with
d>m(m+1)
, the quantum code
\mathcal{Q}:=\text{span}\bigl(\{Z_{\mathbf{c}}\ket{\mathrm{G}}:\mathbf{c}\in\mathcal{C}\}\bigr)
is a pure
[[n,k,m+1]]
QECC.

Proof.

The idea is to show that in the vector space
\mathcal{W}
defined in the statement of Theorem
1
, there are no non-zero vectors of symplectic weight at most
m
, so that we can draw the desired conclusion from Theorem
1
. We will show the equivalent statement that for any non-identity operator
E\in\mathcal{P}_{n}
of weight at most
m
, the operator
Z_{\mathbf{c}}E
is not a stabilizer of
\ket{\mathrm{G}}
, for all
\mathbf{c}\in\mathcal{C}
. To this end, consider any non-identity operator
E\in\mathcal{P}_{n}
of weight at most
m
. Note first that
E
itself cannot be a stabilizer of
\ket{\mathrm{G}}
, since by the characterization of
m
-uniformity given after Definition
2
, we have
\braket{\mathrm{G}|E|\mathrm{G}}=0
.

Now, consider
E^{\prime}=Z_{\mathbf{c}}E
, for some
\mathbf{c}\in\mathcal{C}
,
\mathbf{c}\neq\mathbf{0}
. Since
\mathbf{c}
has Hamming weight strictly greater than
m(m+1)
and the weight of
E
is at most
m
, the number of
Z
s in
E^{\prime}
must be strictly greater than
m^{2}
. On the other hand, the number of
X
s in
E^{\prime}
is at most
m
, since all of these have to be contributed by
E
. Suppose that
E^{\prime}
were a stabilizer of
\ket{\mathrm{G}}
. Then, it is a product of stabilizer generators of the form
S_{i}=X_{i}Z_{N(i)}
. Since the number of
X
s in
E^{\prime}
is at most
m
,
E^{\prime}
is a product of at most
m
generators
S_{i}
. Moreover, each generator
S_{i}
contributes at most
|N(i)|=m

Z
s to
E^{\prime}
. So, the total number of
Z
s in
E^{\prime}
cannot exceed
m\times m=m^{2}
, which contradicts the lower bound derived earlier for the number of
Z
s in
E^{\prime}
. Hence,
Z_{\mathbf{c}}E
cannot be a stabilizer of
\ket{\mathrm{G}}
, which proves the theorem.
∎

Since Proposition
2
gives us a concrete construction of a graph state that satisfies the requirement of the above theorem, we have the following corollary.

Corollary 1
.

Let
\ket{\mathrm{G}}
be the cluster state associated with the
D
-dimensional rectangular lattice
\Lambda_{n_{1},n_{2},\ldots,n_{D}}
, with

for all

. Let

. Then, for any
[n,k,d]
binary linear code
\mathcal{C}
with
{d>2D(2D+1)}
, the quantum code
\mathcal{Q}:=\text{span}\bigl(\{Z_{\mathbf{c}}\ket{\mathrm{G}}:\mathbf{c}\in\mathcal{C}\}\bigr)
is a pure
[[n,k,2D+1]]
QECC.

From the Gilbert-Varshamov bound (see e.g.,
[
43
, Section 4.3]
), we know that for any fixed

, there exist
[n,k,d]
binary linear codes with

and

as

. More precisely, we have the following additional corollary to Theorem
2
.

Corollary 2
.

For any fixed
D\geq 1
, we can obtain, via the CWS code construction, pure
[[n^{D},k,2D+1]
QECCs with
k=n^{D}-\lceil 2D^{2}(2D+1)\log_{2}n\rceil
for all
n\geq 8
.

Proof.

Let
N=n^{D}
,
n\geq 8
. By a straightforward application of the Gilbert-Varshamov bound for binary linear codes, there exists a binary linear code,
\mathcal{C}
, of blocklength
N
, dimension

and minimum distance

. The claimed pure QECC is obtained by applying the construction in Corollary
1
to this code
\mathcal{C}
and the cluster state
\ket{\mathrm{G}}
associated with the
D
-dimensional rectangular lattice
\Lambda_{n,n,\ldots,n}
.
∎

The lower bound of
m(m+1)
on the minimum distance of the linear code
\mathcal{C}
in Theorem
2
is a sufficient, but not necessary, condition for the conclusion of the theorem to hold. Indeed, Theorem
1
shows that only a weaker lower bound of
m+1
is necessary (note that the minimum distance of
\mathcal{C}
bounds from above the minimum symplectic weight of a non-zero vector in

). It would thus be useful to try to find linear codes

with minimum distance that is of the order of

rather than of the order of

, from which we can still obtain the same conclusion as in Theorem
2
. Linear codes with a less stringent requirement on minimum distance will have a larger dimension

, which directly translates to a larger dimension for the resulting QECCs.

Given an
m
-uniform graph state
\ket{\mathrm{G}}
, to weaken the minimum distance requirement on the tent peg code

, we need to incorporate the condition on
\mathcal{W}
in Theorem

into the design of the tent peg code. While this seems difficult to do for arbitrary

-uniform graph states, we show how this strategy can be successfully implemented for the cluster states obtained from

- and

-dimensional lattices.

\displaystyle\text{\rule[2.5pt]{28.45274pt}{0.5pt}}\,1\ast\ast\,1\,\text{\rule[2.5pt]{28.45274pt}{0.5pt}}

(3)

\displaystyle\text{\rule[2.5pt]{28.45274pt}{0.5pt}}\,1\ast 0\ast 1\,\text{\rule[2.5pt]{28.45274pt}{0.5pt}}

(4)

\displaystyle\text{\rule[2.5pt]{28.45274pt}{0.5pt}}\,1\ast 1\,\text{\rule[2.5pt]{28.45274pt}{0.5pt}}\,1\ast 1\,\text{\rule[2.5pt]{28.45274pt}{0.5pt}}

(5)

\displaystyle\text{\rule[2.5pt]{28.45274pt}{0.5pt}}\,1\ast 1\,\text{\rule[2.5pt]{28.45274pt}{0.5pt}}\,\ast\,\text{\rule[2.5pt]{28.45274pt}{0.5pt}}

Let

Consider the binary code

[1\ \ \ \alpha^{b}\ \ \ \alpha^{2b}\ \ \ 1\ \ \ \alpha^{b}\ \ \ \alpha^{2b}\ \ \ \cdots\ \ \ 1\ \ \ \alpha^{b}\ \ \ \alpha^{2b}]

Remark 1
.

Note that

, since

. So, if we assume that

\begin{split}H\mathbf{c}^{T}&=[1+\alpha^{b}+\alpha^{2b},\ \ 1+({\alpha^{b}})^{b}+({\alpha^{b}})^{2b}]^{T}\\

Indeed, the fact that

.

Let

We propose a similar measurement-based approach to recover the
k

\mathcal{Q}
. Note that any correctable errors affecting the physical qubits will first need to be corrected using the standard error-correcting techniques for a stabilizer code before we apply the recovery protocol.

Consider a CWS encoding
\ket{\mathrm{e}}
of
k

11

n

\ket{\mathrm{G}}
and an
[n,k]

. Start by preparing
k

\ket{+}^{\otimes k}

|\psi\rangle=\frac{1}{2^{k/2}}\sum_{\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}\in\{0,1\}^{k}}a_{\mathbf{x}}\ket{\mathbf{y}}_{\textsc{r}}Z_{\mathbf{x}\mathbf{A}}\ket{\mathrm{G}},

\ket{\psi^{\prime}}=\frac{1}{2^{k/2}}\sum_{\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}\in\{0,1\}^{k}}a_{\mathbf{x}}\ket{\mathbf{y}}_{\textsc{r}}Z_{(\mathbf{x}+\mathbf{y})\mathbf{A}}\ket{\mathrm{G}}.

Observe that, if we now apply the projector
\ket{\mathrm{G}}\bra{\mathrm{G}}

\sum_{\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}\in\{0,1\}^{k}}a_{\mathbf{x}}\bra{\mathrm{G}}Z_{(\mathbf{x}+\mathbf{y})\mathbf{A}}\ket{\mathrm{G}}\ket{\mathbf{y}}_{\textsc{r}}\ket{\mathrm{G}}.

Arguing as in the proof of Proposition
3

is equal to the centralizer and contains no non-identity operators consisting only of Pauli-

\bra{\mathrm{G}}Z_{(\mathbf{x}+\mathbf{y})\mathbf{A}}\ket{\mathrm{G}}

\sum_{\mathbf{x}\in\{0,1\}^{k}}a_{\mathbf{x}}\ket{\mathbf{x}}_{\textsc{r}}\ket{\mathrm{G}}.

(18)

Note that this is the same as the state
\ket{\psi}
in (

One way to effect the projection onto
\ket{\mathrm{G}}
required to obtain (

\ket{\phi^{\prime}}
in (
16

. If, for a particular

in the sequence, the measurement outcome is ‘
+1
’, then we retain the post-measurement state and measure

, but commutes with all other

\frac{1}{2^{n}}\prod_{i=1}^{n}(I+S_{i})=\ket{\mathrm{G}}\bra{\mathrm{G}}

As in Section

\ket{\mathrm{e}}=\sum_{\mathbf{x}^{\prime}\in\{0,1\}^{k-1}}\bigg(a_{\mathbf{x}^{\prime}}b_{0}\,Z_{\mathbf{x}^{\prime}\mathbf{A}^{\prime}}\ket{\mathrm{G}}+a_{\mathbf{x}^{\prime}}b_{1}\,Z_{\mathbf{a}_{k}}Z_{\mathbf{x}^{\prime}\mathbf{A}^{\prime}}\ket{\mathrm{G}}\bigg).

We prepare a recovery qubit in the

\ket{\psi^{\prime}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(\ket{0}_{\mathrm{a}}\ket{\mathrm{e}}+\ket{1}_{\mathrm{a}}Z_{\mathbf{a}_{k}}\ket{\mathrm{e}}\right).

We then measure the

. If the measurement outcome is ‘

’, then the post-measurement state,
\ket{\zeta_{+}}
, is the appropriately normalized projection
\frac{1}{2}\Big(I+S_{k}\Big)\ket{\psi^{\prime}}
. Note that, since the generator matrix

was chosen to be in systematic form,

and commutes with all other

\displaystyle\ket{\zeta_{+}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mathbf{x}^{\prime}\in\{0,1\}^{k-1}}\Big(a_{\mathbf{x}^{\prime}}b_{0}\ket{0}_{\mathrm{a}}Z_{\mathbf{x}^{\prime}\mathbf{A}^{\prime}}\ket{\mathrm{G}}+a_{\mathbf{x}^{\prime}}b_{1}\ket{1}_{\mathrm{a}}Z_{\mathbf{x}^{\prime}\mathbf{A}^{\prime}}\ket{\mathrm{G}}\Big)

\displaystyle=\ \ket{\mathrm{f}}\otimes\left(b_{0}\ket{0}_{\mathrm{a}}+b_{1}\ket{1}_{\mathrm{a}}\right).

On the other hand, if the measurement outcome is ‘

’, then the post-measurement state

is
\frac{1}{2}(I-S_{k})\ket{\psi^{\prime}}
, appropriately normalized. This can be shown to be related to

\ket{\zeta_{+}}=\left(X\otimes Z_{\mathbf{a}_{k}}\right)\ket{\zeta_{-}}.

Thus, the state

IV-E

Connection between recovery procedure and code distance

In the decoding subsection, we used
CZ
gates between recovery qubits and the physical qubits in the encoded state. We see that the number of
CZ
s used in encoding and recovery are identical. In fact, we apply the exact same tent pegs, and the difference between encoding and recovery only lies in whether we measure the input qubits or the graph state qubits. In this section, we show how recovery can involve a smaller number of interactions than what is needed for the encoding if one allows controlled-
X,Y,Z
gates controlled by the recovery qubits.

Since any stabilizer for the graph state contains at least one
X
, strings of
Z
s can never be in the graph stabilizer and, hence, are chosen for constructing CWS codes. This restricts us to using only
CZ
gates in the encoding procedure. However, this is not the case for the recovery procedure. We can use a mix of controlled gates to retrieve information without any loss.

For instance, consider recovering the single logical qubit
\alpha\ket{0}+\beta\ket{1}
from its encoding
\ket{\mathrm{e}}=\alpha\ket{\mathrm{G}}+\beta Z_{A}\ket{\mathrm{G}}
. Similar to the recovery procedure discussed in Subsection
IV-C
, we append the physical qubits in state
\ket{\mathrm{e}}
with a qubit in state
\ket{+}_{a}
. Let
U\in\mathcal{P}_{n}
be a Pauli string acting on the
n
physical qubits. Let
CU
denote the controlled-
U
gate controlled by the recovery qubit, with the
n
physical qubits as the target. The action of
CU
on the joint system results in the state

\ket{\psi_{1}}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\ket{0}_{a}\ket{\mathrm{e}}+\ket{1}_{a}U\ket{\mathrm{e}}\right).

Measuring all the stabilizer generators of
\ket{\mathrm{G}}
on the physical qubits as done in (
17
) gives the state

\ket{\psi_{2}}=\Big[\alpha\ket{0}_{a}+\Big(\alpha\bra{\mathrm{G}}U\ket{\mathrm{G}}+\beta\bra{\mathrm{G}}UZ_{A}\ket{\mathrm{G}}\Big)\ket{1}_{a}\Big]\ket{\mathrm{G}}.

(21)

This expression reduces to the required product state
\left(\alpha\ket{0}_{a}+\beta\ket{1}_{a}\right)\ket{\mathrm{G}}
when the following two conditions are satisfied.

\bra{\mathrm{G}}UZ_{A}\ket{\mathrm{G}}=1\ \ \ (\text{i.e., }UZ_{A}\in\mathcal{S})

(22)

\bra{\mathrm{G}}U\ket{\mathrm{G}}=0\ \ \ (\text{i.e., }U\notin\mathcal{S})

(23)

Conditions
22
and
23
are not independent, and in fact, we prove the following proposition in Appendix
E
.

Proposition 6
.

If
\mathcal{S}
is the stabilizer group of a graph state, then for any Pauli operator
U
,

UZ_{A}\in\mathcal{S}\implies U\notin\mathcal{S}.

Therefore, in Eq. (
21
), the logical state is recovered on the recovery qubit when
UZ_{A}\in\mathcal{S}
. Comparing conditions in Eq. (
22
) and (
23
) to Theorem
1
for one logical qubit, we can see that the support of
U
or the number of qubits that the recovery qubit needs to interact with can be as small as the distance of the code. This can be smaller than the number of
CZ
s used in encoding.

V
Concluding Remarks

In this paper, we revisit the CWS code construction proposed in
[
26
]
. Using highly entangled graph states and carefully chosen classical binary linear codes, we show that
[[n,k,d]]
CWS codes can be constructed with
d=O(1)
and
k/n\to 1
as
n\to\infty
. We further propose a measurement-based encoding algorithm that supports sequential encoding of logical qubits. A recovery algorithm is also given for partial recovery of logical qubits.

Several interesting directions of future research arise from this work. One is the problem of finding higher-dimensional analogues of the code construction encapsulated within Theorem
3
. This will require constructing codes whose coordinates are identified with the sites of a
D
-dimensional lattice with periodic boundary conditions (i.e., a
D
-dimensional torus) with certain constraints imposed on the positions of
1
s in codewords.

The stabilizer group of a CWS code obtained from a graph state and a classical binary linear code is described in Lemma
4
. It would be of interest to see whether we can impose conditions on the binary linear code so as to obtain stabilizer generators of bounded weight and, hence, quantum low-density parity-check (QLDPC) codes.

Based on practical considerations, it would be useful to explore the possibility of scalably constructing CWS code families with properties such as high error thresholds and fault-tolerant logical operations. It would also be desirable to have fault-tolerant implementations of the measurement-based encoding and recovery procedures we have proposed.

We would now like to show that adding a binary vector of symplectic weight less than
5

\mathcal{C}^{\prime}

On Quantum Obstruction Spaces and Higher Codimension Gauge Theories
Nima Moshayedi

Abstract
Using the quantum construction of the BV-BFV method for perturbative gauge theories, we show that the obstruction for quantizing a codimension 1 theory is given by the second cohomology group with respect to the boundary BRST charge. Moreover, we give an idea for the algebraic construction of codimension k k quantizations in terms of 𝔼 k \mathbb{E}_{k} -algebras and higher shifted Poisson structures by formulating a higher version of the quantum master equation. Keywords Quantum Field Theory, Gauge Theory, BV-BFV Formalism, Deformation Quantization, Extended Field Theory, Shifted Poisson Structures, Higher Categories



I
Introduction

The Batalin–Vilkovisky formalism
BV1
;
BV2
;
BV3
is a powerful method to deal with perturbative quantizations of local gauge theories. The extension of this formalism to manifolds with boundary combines the Lagrangian approach of the Batalin–Vilkovisky (BV) formalism in the bulk with the Hamiltonian approach of the Batalin–Fradkin–Vilkovisky (BFV) formalism
FV1
;
BF1
on the boundary of the underlying source (spacetime) manifold. This construction is known as the BV-BFV formalism
CMR1
;
CMR2
;
CattMosh1
. In particular, it describes a codimension 1 quantum gauge formalism. Within a classical gauge theory one is interested in describing the obstructions for it to be quantizable. The cohomological symplectic formulation suggests an operator quantization for the boundary action. To get a well-defined and consistent cohomology theory, one has to require that this induced operator squares to zero. This will lead to obstruction spaces for boundary theories by considering a deformation quantization of the boundary action in order to formulate a boundary version of the quantum master equation as the gauge-independence condition.
We will show that the obstruction for the quantization of manifolds with boundary is controlled by the second cohomology group with respect to the cohomological vector field on the boundary fields.
Moreover, we formulate a classical extension of higher codimension
k

II
Obstruction spaces for quantization on manifolds with boundary

II.1
Classical BV theories

We start with the BV approach for the bulk theory.
A BV manifold is a triple

(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{S},\omega)

such that
\mathcal{F}
is a
\mathbf{Z}
-graded supermanifold,
\mathcal{S}\in\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{F})
is an even function of degree 0, and
\omega\in\Omega^{2}(\mathcal{F})
an odd symplectic form of degree
-1
.
The
\mathbf{Z}
-grading corresponds to the
ghost number
which we will denote by “
\mathrm{gh}
”. The BV space of fields
\mathcal{F}
is usually given as the
(-1)
-shifted cotangent bundle of the BRST space of fields, i.e.
\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{BV}}:=T^{*}[-1]\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{BRST}}
. In many cases,
\mathcal{F}
is an infinite-dimensional Fréchet manifold.
Denote by
Q
the Hamiltonian vector field of
\mathcal{S}
of degree
+1
, i.e.
\iota_{Q}\omega=\delta\mathcal{S}
, where
\delta
denotes the de Rham differential on
\mathcal{F}
.
If we denote by
(\kern 5.0pt,\kern 5.0pt)
the odd Poisson bracket induced by the odd symplectic form
\omega
(also called the
anti bracket
, or
BV bracket
), we get

Q=(\mathcal{S},\kern 5.0pt).

Note that, by definition,
Q
is
cohomological
, i.e.
Q^{2}=0
.
Moreover,
Q
is a
symplectic
vector field, i.e.
L_{Q}\omega=0
, where
L
denotes the Lie derivative.
For a BV theory we require the
classical master equation (CME)

Q(\mathcal{S})=(\mathcal{S},\mathcal{S})=0

(II.1)

to hold.
The assignment
\Sigma\mapstochar\rightarrow(\mathcal{F}_{\Sigma},\mathcal{S}_{\Sigma},\omega_{\Sigma})
of a (usually, closed compact oriented) manifold
\Sigma
to a BV manifold is called a
BV theory
. By the physical property of
locality
, given a BV theory, we usually want to work over
local functions
on
\mathcal{F}_{\Sigma}
, which we denote by
\mathcal{O}_{loc}(\mathcal{F}_{\Sigma})\subset\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{F}_{\Sigma})
. These are defined by functions on
\mathcal{F}_{\Sigma}
of the form

\Phi\mapstochar\rightarrow\int_{x\in\Sigma}\mathscr{L}\big(x,\Phi(x),\partial\Phi(x),\partial^{2}\Phi(x),\ldots,\partial^{N}\Phi(x)\big),

where
\Phi\in\mathcal{F}_{\Sigma}
denotes some field configuration and
\mathscr{L}
denotes the
Lagrangian density
of the given theory which depends on
\Phi
and higher derivatives for
N\in\mathbf{Z}_{>0}
.

Examples of classical BV theories

We want to give some examples of non-reduced classical BV theories. For the reduced case see

II.2.1
Electrodynamics

We want to consider the (minimal) BV extension of classical Euclidean electrodynamics for a trivial
U(1)
-bundle. Let
\Sigma
be a smooth oriented
n
-dimensional Riemannian manifold. Denote by
*\colon\Omega^{j}(\Sigma)\rightarrow\Omega^{n-j}(\Sigma)
the
Hodge star
induced by the metric on
\Sigma
. The BV space of fields is then given by the shifted cotangent bundle
T^{*}[-1]E_{\Sigma}
, where

E_{\Sigma}:=\Omega^{1}(\Sigma)\oplus\Omega^{n-2}(\Sigma)\oplus\Omega^{0}(\Sigma)[1].

The first term of
E_{\Sigma}
denotes the space of connections
A
of a trivial
U(1)
-bundle over
\Sigma
, the second term denotes the the space of the Hamiltonian counterpart of those connections (i.e. the momentum), which we denote by
B
, and the third term denotes the space of ghost fields
c
. Hence, we have

\mathcal{F}_{\Sigma}:=T^{*}[-1]E_{\Sigma}=\Omega^{1}(\Sigma)\oplus\Omega^{n-2}(\Sigma)\oplus\\
\oplus\Omega^{0}(\Sigma)[1]\oplus\Omega^{n-1}(\Sigma)[-1]\oplus\Omega^{2}(\Sigma)[-1]\oplus\Omega^{n}(\Sigma)[-2]

We denote a field in
\mathcal{F}_{\Sigma}
by
(A,B,c,A^{+},B^{+},c^{+})
. Then the BV symplectic form is given by

\omega_{\Sigma}=\int_{\Sigma}\left(\delta A\land\delta A^{+}+\delta B\land\delta B^{+}+\delta c\land\delta c^{+}\right).

The BV action is given by

\mathcal{S}_{\Sigma}=\int_{\Sigma}\left(B\land F_{A}+\frac{1}{2}B\land*B+A^{+}\land{\mathrm{d}}c\right),

where
F_{A}:={\mathrm{d}}A
, denotes the curvature of the connection
A
. The cohomological vector field is given by

Q_{\Sigma}=\int_{\Sigma}\bigg({\mathrm{d}}c\land\frac{\delta}{\delta A}+{\mathrm{d}}B\land\frac{\delta}{\delta A^{+}}+\\
+(*B+{\mathrm{d}}A)\land\frac{\delta}{\delta B^{+}}+{\mathrm{d}}A^{+}\land\frac{\delta}{\delta c^{+}}\bigg).

In particular, we have the following symmetries:

\displaystyle Q_{\Sigma}(A)

\displaystyle={\mathrm{d}}c,

\displaystyle Q_{\Sigma}(A^{+})

\displaystyle={\mathrm{d}}B,

\displaystyle Q_{\Sigma}(B^{+})

\displaystyle=*B+{\mathrm{d}}A,

\displaystyle Q_{\Sigma}(c^{+})

\displaystyle={\mathrm{d}}A^{+},

and
Q_{\Sigma}(B)=Q_{\Sigma}(c)=0
. It is easy to show that
Q^{2}=0
and that the CME is indeed satisfied, i.e.
Q_{\Sigma}(\mathcal{S}_{\Sigma})=0
.

II.2.2
Yang–Mills theory

Consider an
n
-dimensional closed oriented compact smooth Riemannian manifold
\Sigma
. Let
\mathfrak{g}
be the Lie algebra of a finite-dimensional simply connected Lie group
G
endowed with a
\mathfrak{g}
-invariant inner product given by
\langle g,h\rangle:=\textnormal{Tr}(gh)
. Moreover, let
P
be principal
G
-bundle over
\Sigma
and assume for simplicity that
P
is trivial. The BV space of fields is given by

\mathcal{F}_{\Sigma}:=\Omega^{1}(\Sigma)\otimes\mathfrak{g}\oplus\Omega^{n-2}(\Sigma)\otimes\mathfrak{g}\oplus\\
\oplus\Omega^{0}(\Sigma)\mathfrak{g}[1]\oplus\Omega^{n-1}(\Sigma)\otimes\mathfrak{g}[-1]\oplus\\
\oplus\Omega^{2}(\Sigma)\otimes\mathfrak{g}[-1]\oplus\Omega^{n}(\Sigma)\otimes\mathfrak{g}[-2]

We denote a field in
\mathcal{F}_{\Sigma}
by components
(A,B,c,A^{+},B^{+},c^{+})
. The BV symplectic form is given by

\omega_{\Sigma}=\int_{\Sigma}\textnormal{Tr}\big(\delta A\land\delta A^{+}+\delta B\land\delta B^{+}+\delta c\land\delta c^{+}\big)

and the BV action by

\mathcal{S}_{\Sigma}=\int_{\Sigma}\tr\bigg(B\land F_{A}+\frac{1}{2}B\land*B+\\
+A^{+}\land{\mathrm{d}}_{A}c+B^{+}\land[B,c]+\frac{1}{2}c^{+}\land[c,c]\bigg),

where
F_{A}:={\mathrm{d}}A+\frac{1}{2}[A,A]
denotes the curvature of the connection
A
and
{\mathrm{d}}_{A}
is the covariant derivative for
A
. The cohomological vector field is given by

Q_{\Sigma}=\int_{\Sigma}\bigg({\mathrm{d}}_{A}c\land\frac{\delta}{\delta A}+[B,c]\land\frac{\delta}{\delta B}+\frac{1}{2}[c,c]\land\frac{\delta}{\delta c}+\\
+({\mathrm{d}}_{A}B+[A^{+},c])\land\frac{\delta}{\delta A^{+}}+(F_{A}+*B+[B^{+},c])\land\frac{\delta}{\delta B^{+}}+\\
+({\mathrm{d}}_{A}A^{+}+[B,B^{+}]+[c,c^{+}])\land\frac{\delta}{\delta c^{+}}\bigg).

In particular, we have the following symmetries:

\displaystyle Q_{\Sigma}(A)

\displaystyle={\mathrm{d}}_{A}c,

\displaystyle Q_{\Sigma}(B)

\displaystyle=[B,c],

\displaystyle Q_{\Sigma}(c)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}[c,c],

\displaystyle Q_{\Sigma}(A^{+})

\displaystyle={\mathrm{d}}_{A}B+[A^{+},c],

\displaystyle Q_{\Sigma}(B^{+})

\displaystyle=F_{A}+*B+[B^{+},c],

\displaystyle Q_{\Sigma}(c^{+})

\displaystyle={\mathrm{d}}_{A}A^{+}+[B,B^{+}]+[c,c^{+}].

\displaystyle Q_{\Sigma}(A)

\displaystyle Q_{\Sigma}(c)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}[c,c],

\displaystyle Q_{\Sigma}(A^{+})

\displaystyle=F_{A}+[c,A^{+}],

\displaystyle Q_{\Sigma}(c^{+})

\displaystyle={\mathrm{d}}_{A}A^{+}+[c,c^{+}].

The BV action is given by

\mathcal{S}_{\Sigma}=\int_{\Sigma}\textnormal{Tr}\bigg(\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{A}\land{\mathrm{d}}\mathbf{A}+\frac{1}{6}\mathbf{A}\land[\mathbf{A},\mathbf{A}]\bigg)=\\

+\frac{1}{2}c\land{\mathrm{d}}_{A}A^{+}+\frac{1}{2}c^{+}\land[c,c]\bigg)

II.3
Obstruction space in the bulk

It is well known that the obstruction space for quantization in the BV formalism is given by the first cohomology group with respect to
Q
. See e.g.
BarnichDelMonte2018
and references therein.

Theorem II.2
.

The obstruction space for a BV theory to be quantizable is given by

\mathrm{H}^{1}_{Q}(\mathcal{O}_{loc}(\mathcal{F})).

(II.2)

Proof.

Consider a deformation of the BV action
\mathcal{S}
, denoted by
\mathcal{S}_{\hbar}
, depending on
\hbar
and consider its expansion as a formal power series

\mathcal{S}_{\hbar}:=\mathcal{S}_{0}+\hbar\mathcal{S}_{1}+\hbar^{2}\mathcal{S}_{2}+O(\hbar^{3})\\
=\sum_{k\geq 0}\hbar^{k}\mathcal{S}_{k}\in\mathcal{O}_{loc}(\mathcal{F})[\![\hbar]\!],

(II.3)

where each
\mathcal{S}_{k}\in\mathcal{O}_{loc}(\mathcal{F})
for all
k\geq 0
and
\lim_{\hbar\rightarrow 0}\mathcal{S}_{\hbar}=\mathcal{S}
, i.e.
\mathcal{S}_{0}:=\mathcal{S}
. Note that
\gh\mathcal{S}_{k}=0
for all
k\geq 0
since
\gh\mathcal{S}=0
.
For the quantum BV picture (see e.g.
S
;
R
) one should note that there is a canonical second order differential operator
\Delta
on
\mathcal{O}_{loc}(\mathcal{F})
such that
\Delta^{2}=0
. It is called BV Laplacian (see
Khudaverdian2004
;
Severa2006
for a mathematical exposure). In particular, if
\Phi^{i}
and
\Phi^{+}_{i}
denote field and anti-field respectively, one can define
\Delta
as

\Delta f=\sum_{i}(-1)^{\gh\Phi^{i}+1}f\left\langle\frac{\overleftarrow{\delta}}{\delta\Phi^{i}},\frac{\overleftarrow{\delta}}{\delta\Phi^{+}_{i}}\right\rangle,\quad f\in\mathcal{O}_{loc}(\mathcal{F}).

We have denoted by
\frac{\overleftarrow{\delta}}{\delta\Phi^{i}}
and
\frac{\overrightarrow{\delta}}{\delta\Phi^{i}}
the left and right derivatives with respect to
\Phi^{i}
. An analogue version also holds for the anti-fields
\Phi^{+}_{i}
. In fact, we have

\displaystyle\frac{\overrightarrow{\delta}}{\delta\Phi^{i}}f

\displaystyle=(-1)^{\gh\Phi^{i}(\gh f+1)}f\frac{\overleftarrow{\delta}}{\delta\Phi^{i}},

(II.4)

\displaystyle\frac{\overrightarrow{\delta}}{\delta\Phi^{+}_{i}}f

\displaystyle=(-1)^{(\gh\Phi^{i}+1)(\gh f+1)}f\frac{\overleftarrow{\delta}}{\delta\Phi^{+}_{i}}.

(II.5)

Remark II.3
.

To be precise, for our constructions we want to consider a (global) BV Laplacian on
half-densities
on
\mathcal{F}
. It can be shown that for any odd symplectic supermanifold
\mathcal{F}
there exists a supermanifold
\mathcal{M}
such that
\mathcal{F}\cong T^{*}[1]\mathcal{M}
. Then
\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{F})\cong\mathcal{O}(T^{*}[1]\mathcal{M})=\Gamma(\bigwedge^{\bullet}T\mathcal{M})
. The
Berezinian bundle
on
\mathcal{F}
is given by

\mathrm{Ber}(\mathcal{F})\cong\bigwedge^{\mathrm{top}}T^{*}\mathcal{M}\otimes\bigwedge^{\mathrm{top}}T^{*}\mathcal{M}.

The half-densities on
\mathcal{F}
are defined by

\mathrm{Dens}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathcal{F}):=\Gamma\left(\mathrm{Ber}(\mathcal{F})^{\frac{1}{2}}\right).

One can then show that there is a canonical operator
\Delta^{\frac{1}{2}}_{\mathcal{F}}
on
\mathrm{Dens}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathcal{F})
such that it squares to zero
Khudaverdian2004
. At this point one should mention that this is only canonical in the finite-dimensional setting. For the infinite-dimensional case this only holds after a suitable
renormalization
.
We can define a Laplacian by

\Delta_{\sigma}f:=\frac{1}{\sigma}\Delta^{\frac{1}{2}}_{\mathcal{F}}(f\sigma),\quad f\in\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{F}),

where
\sigma
is a non-vanishing reference half-density on
\mathcal{F}
which is
\Delta^{\frac{1}{2}}_{\mathcal{F}}
-closed. Note that
(\Delta_{\sigma})^{2}=0
. We usually just write
\Delta\equiv\Delta_{\sigma}
without mentioning
\sigma
.

To observe gauge-independence in the BV formalism, one requires the
quantum master equation (QME)

\Delta\exp\left(\mathcal{S}_{\hbar}/\hbar\right)=0\Longleftrightarrow(\mathcal{S}_{\hbar},\mathcal{S}_{\hbar})+2\hbar\Delta\mathcal{S}_{\hbar}=0

(II.6)

to hold. Here we denote by
\Delta
the BV Laplacian.
Solving (
II.6
) for each order in
\hbar
, we get the system of equations

\displaystyle(\mathcal{S}_{0},\mathcal{S}_{0})

\displaystyle=0,

(II.7)

\displaystyle\Delta\mathcal{S}_{0}

\displaystyle=(\mathcal{S}_{0},\mathcal{S}_{1}),

(II.8)

\displaystyle\Delta\mathcal{S}_{1}

\displaystyle=(\mathcal{S}_{0},\mathcal{S}_{2})+\frac{1}{2}(\mathcal{S}_{1},\mathcal{S}_{1})

(II.9)

\displaystyle\quad\vdots

(II.10)

Note that Equation (
II.7
) is the CME which we assume to hold. Then, using the CME and the formula

\Delta(f,g)=(f,\Delta g)-(-1)^{\gh g}(\Delta f,g),\quad\forall f,g\in\mathcal{O}_{loc}(\mathcal{F}),

we get

0=\Delta(\mathcal{S}_{0},\mathcal{S}_{0})=(\mathcal{S}_{0},\Delta\mathcal{S}_{0})=Q(\Delta\mathcal{S}_{0}).

Hence
\Delta\mathcal{S}_{0}
is closed with respect to the coboundary operator
Q=(\mathcal{S}_{0},\kern 5.0pt)
. Moreover, if we assume that it is also
Q
-exact, we get that there is some
\mathcal{S}_{1}\in\mathcal{O}_{loc}(\mathcal{F})
such that
\Delta\mathcal{S}_{0}=Q(\mathcal{S}_{1})=(\mathcal{S}_{0},\mathcal{S}_{1})
, which is exactly the statement of Equation (
II.8
). This will automatically imply that all the higher order equations hold. Indeed, if
\Delta\mathcal{S}_{1}=(\mathcal{S}_{0},\mathcal{S}_{1})
for some
\mathcal{S}_{1}\in\mathcal{O}_{loc}(\mathcal{F})
, we get

0=\Delta\underbrace{(\mathcal{S}_{0},\mathcal{S}_{1})}_{\Delta\mathcal{S}_{0}}=(\Delta\mathcal{S}_{0},\mathcal{S}_{1})-(-1)^{\gh\mathcal{S}_{0}}(\mathcal{S}_{0},\Delta\mathcal{S}_{1})\\
=((\mathcal{S}_{0},\mathcal{S}_{1}),\mathcal{S}_{1})-(\mathcal{S}_{0},\Delta\mathcal{S}_{1}),

(II.11)

where we used
\Delta^{2}=0
. Using the graded Jacobi formula for the BV bracket, we get

((\mathcal{S}_{0},\mathcal{S}_{1}),\mathcal{S}_{1})=(\mathcal{S}_{0},(\mathcal{S}_{1},\mathcal{S}_{1}))-\\
-(-1)^{(\gh\mathcal{S}_{0}-1)(\gh\mathcal{S}_{1}-1)}(\mathcal{S}_{1},(\mathcal{S}_{0},\mathcal{S}_{1})).

(II.12)

Furthermore, by graded commutativity of the BV bracket we have

((\mathcal{S}_{0},\mathcal{S}_{1}),\mathcal{S}_{1})=\\
=-(-1)^{(\gh(\mathcal{S}_{0},\mathcal{S}_{1})-1)(\gh\mathcal{S}_{1}-1)}(\mathcal{S}_{1},(\mathcal{S}_{0},\mathcal{S}_{1})).

(II.13)

Now since

\gh(\mathcal{S}_{0},\mathcal{S}_{1})=\gh\mathcal{S}_{0}+\gh\mathcal{S}_{1}+\gh(\kern 5.0pt,\kern 5.0pt)

we get

2((\mathcal{S}_{0},\mathcal{S}_{1}),\mathcal{S}_{1})=(\mathcal{S}_{0},(\mathcal{S}_{1},\mathcal{S}_{1})).

Hence, using Equation (
II.11
), we get

(\mathcal{S}_{0},\Delta\mathcal{S}_{1})=\left(\mathcal{S}_{0},\frac{1}{2}(\mathcal{S}_{1},\mathcal{S}_{1})\right).

This will give us

\Delta\mathcal{S}_{1}=\frac{1}{2}(\mathcal{S}_{1},\mathcal{S}_{1})+\text{$Q$-exact term},

so we can find some
\mathcal{S}_{2}\in\mathcal{O}_{loc}(\mathcal{F})
such that the
Q
-exact term is given by
Q(\mathcal{S}_{2})=(\mathcal{S}_{0},\mathcal{S}_{2})
. This implies that Equation (
II.9
) holds. The higher order equations hold in a similar iterative computation.
∎

II.4
Classical BV-BFV theories

Let us describe the BFV approach for the space of boundary fields.
A BFV manifold is a triple

\left(\mathcal{F}^{\partial},\omega^{\partial},Q^{\partial}\right),

where
\mathcal{F}^{\partial}
is a
\mathbf{Z}
-graded supermanifold,
\omega^{\partial}\in\Omega^{2}(\mathcal{F}^{\partial})
an even symplectic form of ghost number
0
and
Q^{\partial}
cohomological and symplectic vector field of degree
+1
with odd Hamiltonian function
\mathcal{S}^{\partial}\in\mathcal{O}_{loc}(\mathcal{F}^{\partial})
of ghost number
+1
, i.e.
\iota_{Q^{\partial}}\omega^{\partial}=\delta\mathcal{S}^{\partial}
, where
\delta
denotes the de Rham differential on
\mathcal{F}^{\partial}
. Moreover, we want

Q^{\partial}(\mathcal{S}^{\partial})=\{\mathcal{S}^{\partial},\mathcal{S}^{\partial}\}=0.

We say that a BFV manifold is
exact
, if there exists a primitive
1
-form
\alpha^{\partial}
, such that
\omega^{\partial}=\delta\alpha^{\partial}
. A BV-BFV manifold over an exact BFV manifold
(\mathcal{F}^{\partial},\omega^{\partial}=\delta\alpha^{\partial},Q^{\partial})
is a quintuple

(\mathcal{F},\omega,\mathcal{S},Q,\pi),

where
\pi\colon\mathcal{F}\rightarrow\mathcal{F}^{\partial}
is a surjective submersion such that

•

\delta\pi Q=Q^{\partial}
,

•

\iota_{Q}\omega=\delta\mathcal{S}+\pi^{*}\alpha^{\partial}
.

A consequence of this definition is

Q(\mathcal{S})=\pi^{*}\left(2\mathcal{S}^{\partial}-\iota_{Q^{\partial}}\alpha^{\partial}\right)

(II.14)

which is called the
modified classical master equation (mCME)
. The assignment
\Sigma\mapstochar\rightarrow(\mathcal{F}_{\Sigma},\mathcal{S}_{\Sigma},Q_{\Sigma},\pi_{\Sigma}\colon\mathcal{F}_{\Sigma}\rightarrow\mathcal{F}^{\partial}_{\partial\Sigma})
of a manifold
\Sigma
with boundary
\partial\Sigma
to a BV-BFV manifold is called a
BV-BFV theory
.

II.6
Obstruction space on the boundary

Similarly as for BV theories one can ask about the quantization obstruction for a BV-BFV theory, i.e. for a codimension 1 theory. In fact, we get the following theorem.

Theorem II.4
.

Let
(\mathcal{F},\omega,\mathcal{S},Q,\pi\colon\mathcal{F}\rightarrow\mathcal{F}^{\partial})
be a BV-BFV manifold over an exact BFV manifold
(\mathcal{F}^{\partial},\omega^{\partial}=\delta\alpha^{\partial},Q^{\partial})
. The obstruction space for quantization on the underlying boundary BFV manifold
\mathcal{F}^{\partial}
is given by

\mathrm{H}^{2}_{Q^{\partial}}(\mathcal{O}_{loc}(\mathcal{F}^{\partial})),

(II.15)

where

Q^{\partial}=\{\mathcal{S}^{\partial},\kern 5.0pt\}

with
\{\kern 5.0pt,\kern 5.0pt\}
the Poisson bracket induced by the symplectic form
\omega^{\partial}
.

Proof.

Consider a deformation of the BFV action
\mathcal{S}^{\partial}
, denoted by
\mathcal{S}^{\partial}_{\hbar}
, depending on
\hbar
and consider its expansion as a formal power series

\mathcal{S}^{\partial}_{\hbar}:=\mathcal{S}^{\partial}_{0}+\hbar\mathcal{S}^{\partial}_{1}+\hbar^{2}\mathcal{S}^{\partial}_{2}+O(\hbar^{3})\\
=\sum_{k\geq 0}\hbar^{k}\mathcal{S}^{\partial}_{k}\in\mathcal{O}_{loc}(\mathcal{F}^{\partial})[\![\hbar]\!],

(II.16)

where
\mathcal{S}^{\partial}_{k}\in\mathcal{O}_{loc}(\mathcal{F}^{\partial})
for all
k\geq 0
such that
\mathcal{S}^{\partial}_{0}:=\mathcal{S}^{\partial}
. Note that
\gh\mathcal{S}^{\partial}_{k}=+1
since
\gh\mathcal{S}^{\partial}=+1
and the corresponding symplectic form
\omega^{\partial}
is even of ghost number
0
.
In the BV-BFV construction one assumes a symplectic splitting of the BV space of fields

\mathcal{F}=\mathcal{B}\times\mathcal{Y}

(II.17)

where the BV symplectic form
\omega
is constant on
\mathcal{B}
. One should think of
\mathcal{B}
as the boundary part and
\mathcal{Y}
as the bulk part of the fields. In fact, the space
\mathcal{B}
is constructed as the leaf space for a chosen polarization on the space of boundary fields
\mathcal{F}^{\partial}
(i.e. a Lagrangian subbundle of
T\mathcal{F}^{\partial}
closed under the Lie bracket) and
\mathcal{Y}
is just a symplectic complement.
Using this splitting, we can write the mCME

\displaystyle\delta_{\mathcal{Y}}\mathcal{S}

\displaystyle=\iota_{Q_{\mathcal{Y}}}\omega,

(II.18)

\displaystyle\delta_{\mathcal{B}}\mathcal{S}

\displaystyle=-\alpha^{\partial},

(II.19)

where
Q_{\mathcal{Y}}
denotes the part of the cohomological vector field
Q
on
\mathcal{Y}
,
\delta_{\mathcal{Y}}
and
\delta_{\mathcal{B}}
denote the corresponding parts of the de Rham differential
\delta
on the BV space of fields
\mathcal{F}
according to the splitting (
II.17
).
Note that we have dropped the pullback
\pi^{*}
. These two equations together with (
II.14
) imply

\frac{1}{2}(\mathcal{S},\mathcal{S})_{\mathcal{Y}}=\frac{1}{2}\iota_{Q_{\mathcal{Y}}}\iota_{Q_{\mathcal{Y}}}\omega=\mathcal{S}^{\partial}.

(II.20)

Choose Darboux coordinates
(b^{i},p_{i})
on
\mathcal{F}^{\partial}
such that
b^{i}
denotes the coordinates on the base
\mathcal{B}
and
p_{i}
on the leaves.
In the case of an infinite-dimensional Banach manifold, locally one has Darboux’s theorem by using Moser’s trick, whenever the tangent spaces are split, i.e. there are two Lagrangian subspaces
\mathcal{L}_{1},\mathcal{L}_{2}\subset T_{\beta}\mathcal{F}^{\partial}
such that
T_{\beta}\mathcal{F}^{\partial}=\mathcal{L}_{1}\oplus\mathcal{L}_{2}
for some
\beta\in\mathcal{F}^{\partial}
. This is in general not true for Fréchet manifolds, even if the tangent spaces are split. Note that such a splitting is guaranteed if each fiber is a Hilbert space (see
CattaneoContreras2018
for similar discussions).
However, for the quantization we want to perturb around each critical point, thus we only have to use the linear structure. Additionally, we have to assume that the tangent spaces are split and that there is Darboux’s theorem if we work in the Fréchet setting (see
Kaveh2019
for discussions about Darboux’s theorem on infinite-dimensional Fréchet manifolds).
This allows us to write

\alpha^{\partial}=-\sum_{i}p_{i}\delta b^{i}.

Using Equation (
II.19
), we get

\frac{\overrightarrow{\delta}}{\delta b^{i}}\mathcal{S}=p_{i},\quad\forall i.

Denote by
\Delta_{\mathcal{Y}}
the BV Laplacian restricted to
\mathcal{Y}
. We will assume that
\Delta_{\mathcal{Y}}\mathcal{S}=0
. For the closed case this means that we assume that
\mathcal{S}
solves both, the CME and the QME. For the case with boundary, the BV Laplacian anyway only makes sense on
\mathcal{Y}
, so
\Delta=\Delta_{\mathcal{Y}}
. Next, we can obtain

\Delta_{\mathcal{Y}}\exp\left(\mathrm{i}\mathcal{S}/\hbar\right)=\left(\frac{\mathrm{i}}{\hbar}\right)^{2}\frac{1}{2}(\mathcal{S},\mathcal{S})_{\mathcal{Y}}\exp\left(\mathrm{i}\mathcal{S}/\hbar\right)

(II.21)

Now consider the standard quantization
\widehat{p}_{i}:=-\mathrm{i}\hbar\frac{\overrightarrow{\delta}}{\delta b^{i}}
. If
\widehat{p}_{i}
acts on a function
\mathcal{S}
on
\mathcal{B}
parametrized by
\mathcal{Y}

.

\mathcal{S}_{\hbar}^{\partial}\star\mathcal{S}_{\hbar}^{\partial}=(\mathcal{S}^{\partial}_{0}+\hbar\mathcal{S}^{\partial}_{1}+\hbar^{2}\mathcal{S}^{\partial}_{2}+O(\hbar^{3}))\times\\

III.2.2
Geometric Quantization Picture

The phase space of an
n
-dimensional classical field theory associated to a closed
d
-dimensional submanifold is endowed with a
(n-d-1)
-shifted symplectic structure
PantevToenVaquieVezzosi2013
. This also makes sense for the case when
d=n
, i.e. for the case of a
(-1)
-shifted symplectic structure which corresponds to the case of the BV formalism. Let us briefly consider the example of 3-dimensional classical Chern–Simons theory as in
II.2.3

Safronov2020
. For a compact Lie group
G
we associate the phase space which is given by the moduli space of flat
G
-connections. If
\Sigma
is a closed oriented
3
-manifold, the phase space is given by the critical locus of the Chern–Simons functional, so it carries the induced BV symplectic structure. If
\Sigma
is a closed oriented 2-manifold, the phase space is endowed with the Atiyah–Bott symplectic structure
AtiyahBott1983
. If
\Sigma=S^{1}
(1-dimensional closed compact oriented manifold), the phase space is the stack of conjugacy classes
[G/G]
and the corresponding 1-shifted symplectic structure is given in terms of the canonical 3-form on
G
. If
\Sigma=\mathrm{pt}
(0-dimensional case), the phase space is given by the classifying stack
\mathrm{B}G=[\mathrm{pt}/G]
and the corresponding 2-shifted symplectic structure is given in terms of the invariant symmetric bilinear form on the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}:=\mathrm{Lie}(G)
used in the definition of Chern–Simons theory (Killing form).

The analog of geometric quantization for
k
-shifted symplectic structures uses the notion of
higher categories

Lurie2017
and
derived algebraic geometry

Toen2014
;
PantevToenVaquieVezzosi2013
. It was recently shown that it corresponds to the notion of an
(\infty,k)
-category
Safronov2020
. Let us give a bit more insights on this construction. Recall that the data for geometric quantization of a symplectic manifold
(M,\omega)
, is given by a
prequantization
, i.e. a line bundle (usually called
prequantum line bundle
)
(\mathscr{L},\nabla)
together with a connection on
M
with curvature
\omega
, and a
polarization
, i.e. a Lagrangian foliation
\mathscr{F}\subset TM
which is a subbundle closed with respect to the Lie bracket of vector fields which is Lagrangian with respect to
\omega

Kir85
;
Wood97
;
Moshayedi2020_geomquant
. The constructed vector space is then given by the space of
\nabla
-flat sections
\Gamma_{\mathrm{flat}}(M,\mathscr{L})\subset\Gamma(M,\mathscr{L})
of
\mathscr{L}
along the foliation
\mathscr{F}
. In the
k
-shifted case, one defines the analog of a prequantum line bundle, called a
prequantum
k
-shifted Lagrangian fibration
, to be given by a
k
-gerbe
\mathscr{G}
on the base manifold together with an extension of the natural relative flat connection on the pullback of
\mathscr{G}
to the fiber to a connective structure
\nabla
. The polarization is encoded in a
k
-shifted Lagrangian foliation (see
ToenVezzosi2020
;
BorisovSheshmaniYau2019
). For a 1-shifted symplectic structure we get the
\infty
-category
\mathrm{QCoh}^{\mathscr{G}}
of twisted quasi-coherent sheaves on the base and for the 2-shifted case the
\infty
-category of quasi-coherent sheaves on a certain category and so on, such that the output for
k
-shifted structures gives an
(\infty,k)
-category (see
Safronov2020
for a detailed construction).

Note that this indeed is compatible with the quantum BV-BFV construction which assigns a chain complex
(\mathcal{H},\Omega)
to the 0-shifted case on the boundary (rather than a vector space). It is also compatible with the BV construction in the bulk, i.e. for a
(-1)
-shifted symplectic structure. Namely, the constructions as in
S
give rise to a “geometric quantization”. The expectation value, assigning to an observable a value in the ground field through a path integral of a half-density (usually
\exp(\mathrm{i}\mathcal{S}/\hbar)\sigma
where
\sigma
is some
\Delta
-closed reference half-density) over a chosen Lagrangian submanifold (gauge-fixing), is considered as some analog for the polarization. This can be seen by an extension of the result in
Severa2006
. The original form states that for an odd symplectic supermanifold
(\mathcal{F},\omega)
there is a quasi-isomorphism between the complex
(\Omega^{\bullet}(\mathcal{F}),\omega\land)
, consisting of differential forms on
\mathcal{F}
endowed with the differential given by wedging with
\omega
, and the complex
(\mathrm{Dens}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathcal{F}),\Delta)
, consisting of half-densities on
\mathcal{F}
endowed with the BV Laplacian (This is obviously related to the BV theorem as in
S
).
Moreover, it states that the de Rham differential vanishes on the cohomology of
(\Omega^{\bullet}(\mathcal{F}),\omega\land)
and that the BV Laplacian is given by
\Delta=({\mathrm{d}}\circ(\omega\land)^{-1}\circ{\mathrm{d}})
.
In fact, it is the third differential in the spectral sequence of the bicomplex
(\Omega^{\bullet}(\mathcal{F}),\omega\land,{\mathrm{d}})
and all higher differentials are zero. The shifted analog states a similar quasi-isomorphism
(
Safronov2020
, Proposition 2.34)
.

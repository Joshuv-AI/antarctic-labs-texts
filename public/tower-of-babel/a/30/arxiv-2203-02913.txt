Delorme’s intertwining conditions for sections of homogeneous vector bundles on two and three dimensional hyperbolic spaces
Martin Olbrich, Guendalina Palmirotta

Abstract
The description of the Paley-Wiener space for compactly supported smooth functions C c ∞ ​ ( G ) C^{\infty}_{c}(G) on a semi-simple Lie group G G involves certain intertwining conditions that are difficult to handle. In the present paper, we make them completely explicit for G = 𝐒𝐋 ​ ( 2 , ℝ ) d G=\mathbf{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})^{d} ( d ∈ ℕ d\in\mathbb{N} ) and G = 𝐒𝐋 ⁡ ( 2 , ℂ ) G=\mathbf{SL}(2,\mathbb{C}) . Our results are based on a defining criterion for the Paley-Wiener space, valid for general groups of real rank one, that we derive from Delorme’s proof of the Paley-Wiener theorem. In a forthcoming paper, we will show how these results can be used to study solvability of invariant differential operators between sections of homogeneous vector bundles over the corresponding symmetric spaces.

Introduction

Consider a Riemannian symmetric space of non-compact type
X=G/K
, where
G
is a real connected semi-simple Lie group with finite center of non-compact type and
K\subset G
is its maximal compact subgroup.

Arthur
[

]
as well as Delorme
[
Del05
, Thm. 2]
established a Paley-Wiener theorem for (
K

Later van den Ban and Souaifi
[
vdBS14
]
proved, without using the proof nor validity of any associated Paley-Wiener theorem, that the two compatibility conditions are equivalent.

In
[
OlPa22a
]
, we proved a topological Paley-Wiener(-Schwartz) theorem for sections of homogeneous vector bundles by adapting Delorme’s intertwining conditions for our purposes. We considered the intertwining conditions in three levels, namely (
Level 1
) refered to Delorme’s condition in the setting of van den Ban and Souaifi
[
vdBS14
]
, (
Level 2
) corresponded to the conditions for sections of homogeneous vector bundles and (
Level 3
) stood for spherical functions.

However, these intertwining conditions are very difficult to check in practice, even for special
K
-types.

The most important source of such conditions are the Knapp-Stein
[
KSt71
]
,
[
KSt80
]
and
Zelobenko
[
Zel76
]
intertwining operators, as well as the embedding of discrete series into principal series.

Therefore, in this article, we rewrite them in a more accessible way involving such intertwining operators and the Harish-Chandra
\mathbf{c}
-functions, which we introduce in the first Section
2
.
Moreover, in Section
3
, we show that only a part of them is already sufficient for
G
of real rank one (Thm
3
).
This is already implicitly contained in Delorme’s proof of the Paley-Wiener theorem
[
Del05
, Thm. 2]
.
For our proof, we essentially use an intermediate result of Delorme
[
Del05
, Prop. 1]
and his induction procedure
[
Del05
, Prop. 2]
on the length of minimal
K
-types of a generalized principal series representation.

To apply Thm.
3

\mathbf{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})
and
\mathbf{SL}(2,\mathbb{C})
, which are in the focus of the present paper.

In fact, for
G=\mathbf{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})
, in Section
4
, by drawing its principal series representations

by ’

’ (Fig.

Afterwards, we can deduce the corresponding results also for the other levels (Thms.

(Thm.

As a last example, in Section

The description of its intertwining conditions (Thms.

We remark that

In fact, based on Thm.

We are grateful to P. Delorme for mentioning to us that the results for odd

(5.13)

Moreover, if
L=\max(n,m)
, then
\prescript{}{n}{\emph{\text{Pol}}}_{m}
(resp.
\prescript{}{n}{\mathcal{A}}_{m}
) is a
\prescript{}{L}{\emph{\text{Pol}}}_{L}
(resp.
\prescript{}{L}{\mathcal{A}}_{L}
)-module generated by
q_{n,m}
.

Proof.

Consider the case
m<n
, it suffices to prove that

(a)

there exists a unique
h\in\prescript{}{m}{\mathcal{A}}_{m}
such that
\varphi=h\cdot q_{n,m}\in\prescript{}{n}{\mathcal{A}}_{m}
,.

(b)

there exists a
\tilde{h}\in\prescript{}{n}{\mathcal{A}}_{n}
such that
\varphi=q_{n,m}\cdot\tilde{h}\in\prescript{}{n}{\mathcal{A}}_{m}
.

The polynomial case as well as
m>n
can be proved in a similar way.
Consider

q_{m,n}\prescript{}{n}{\mathcal{A}}_{m}\subset\{g\in\prescript{}{n}{\mathcal{A}}_{n}\;|\;g_{k}=0,\;\forall|k|>m\}\subset\prescript{}{n}{\mathcal{A}}_{n}.

We also have zeros between the lines
-m
and
m
, this means
g_{l}(k)=0,k=\pm(m+2),\pm(m+4),\dots,\pm n
.
Let
\varphi\in\prescript{}{n}{\mathcal{A}}_{m}
.
Every component of
g=q_{m,n}\cdot\varphi
has zeros as described above and
q_{m,n}
has zeros only at negative
\lambda
. It follows that every component
\varphi
has zeros at
m+2,\dots,n
.
Hence, there exists a unique
h\in\text{Hol}(\mathfrak{a}^{*}_{\mathbb{C}},\text{End}(E_{m}))
so that

\varphi=h\cdot q_{n,m}.

Since
g
satisfies the intertwining conditions
g_{k}(l)=g_{l}(k),g_{k}(\lambda)=g_{-k}(-\lambda)
, and

\displaystyle q_{m,n}(k,\lambda)q_{n,m}(k,\lambda)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle q_{m,n}(-k,-\lambda)q_{n,m}(-k,-\lambda)

\displaystyle q_{m,n}(k,l)q_{n,m}(k,l)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle q_{m,n}(l,k)q_{n,m}(l,k)

we see that
h_{l}(k)=h_{k}(l)
and
h_{k}(\lambda)=h_{-k}(-\lambda)

for
k\equiv l\equiv m
(mod 2),
|k|\leq m
and
\lambda\in\mathfrak{a}^{*}_{\mathbb{C}}
.
This proves (a).

Concerning (b), we know from (a) that
\varphi=h\cdot q_{n,m}
.
Thus we need to find

\tilde{h}=\text{diag}(\tilde{h}_{n},\dots,\tilde{h}_{m+2},\tilde{h}_{m},\dots,\tilde{h}_{-m},\tilde{h}_{-(m-2)},\dots,\tilde{h}_{-n})\in\prescript{}{n}{\mathcal{A}}_{n}

with
\tilde{h}_{k}=h_{k}
for
|k|\leq m
.
Note that the crucial condition to be satisfied is
\tilde{h}_{k}(l)=\tilde{h}_{l}(k).

Then
\varphi=\prescript{}{n}{q}_{m}\cdot\tilde{h}
.
By using interpolation polynomials, we define recursively

\displaystyle\tilde{h}_{m+2}(\lambda)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=-m\\
i\equiv m\text{ (mod 2)}\end{subarray}}^{m}h_{i}(m+2)\prod^{m}_{\begin{subarray}{c}l=-m\\
l\neq i\end{subarray}}\Big(\frac{\lambda-l}{i-l}\Big)

\displaystyle\tilde{h}_{m+4}(\lambda)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=-(m+2)\\
i\equiv m+2\text{ (mod 2)}\end{subarray}}^{m+2}\tilde{h}_{i}(m+4)\prod^{m+2}_{\begin{subarray}{c}l=-(m+2)\\
l\neq i\end{subarray}}\Big(\frac{\lambda-l}{i-l}\Big)

\displaystyle\tilde{h}_{n-2}(\lambda)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=-(n-4)\\
i\equiv n-4\text{ (mod 2)}\end{subarray}}^{n-4}\tilde{h}_{i}(n-2)\prod^{n-4}_{\begin{subarray}{c}l=-(n-4)\\
l\neq i\end{subarray}}\Big(\frac{\lambda-l}{i-l}\Big)

\displaystyle\tilde{h}_{n}(\lambda)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=-(n-2)\\
i\equiv n-2\text{ (mod 2)}\end{subarray}}^{n-2}\tilde{h}_{i}(n)\prod^{n-2}_{\begin{subarray}{c}l=-(n-2)\\
l\neq i\end{subarray}}\Big(\frac{\lambda-l}{i-l}\Big),

and
\tilde{h}_{-k}(\lambda):=\tilde{h}_{k}(-\lambda)
.
Then
h\in\prescript{}{n}{\mathcal{A}}_{n}
.
∎

We observe that an anologue of
Thm.
13
, in particular relation (
5.7
), is also true in general for distinct
K
-type
n
and
m
.

Corollary 1
.

With the notations above,
\prescript{}{n}{\mathcal{A}}_{m}\cong\emph{\text{Hol}}(\mathbb{C})\otimes_{\emph{\text{Pol}}(\mathbb{C})}\prescript{}{n}{\emph{\text{Pol}}}_{m}.

Proof.

As a consequence of Thm.
14
, the

\text{Hol}(\mathbb{C})
-module
\prescript{}{n}{\mathcal{A}}_{m}
is isomorphic to
\prescript{}{l}{\mathcal{A}}_{l}
, and
\prescript{}{n}{\text{Pol}}_{m}
is isomorphic to
\prescript{}{l}{\text{Pol}}_{l}
as
\text{Pol}(\mathbb{C})
-module. Then, we can apply Thm.
13
and obtain the desired result.
∎

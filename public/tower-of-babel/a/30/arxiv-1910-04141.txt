Detecting isomorphisms in the homotopy category Thanks: 1 né Carlson
Kevin Arlin 1, J. Daniel Christensen

Abstract
. We show that no generalization of Whitehead’s theorem holds for unpointed spaces. More precisely, we show that the homotopy category of unpointed spaces admits no set of objects jointly reflecting isomorphisms. We give an explicit counterexample involving infinite symmetric groups. In contrast, we prove that the spheres do jointly reflect equivalences in the homotopy 2-category of spaces. We also show that homotopy colimits of transfinite sequential diagrams of spaces are not generally weak colimits in the homotopy category, and furthermore exhibit such a diagram with the property that none of its weak colimits is privileged , which means, roughly, that it sees the spheres as compact objects. The non-existence of a set jointly reflecting isomorphisms in the homotopy category was originally claimed by Heller, but our results on weak colimits show that his argument had an inescapable gap, leading to the need for the new proof given here.

Introduction

Let
\mathbf{Hot}
denote the homotopy category of spaces, and let
\mathbf{Hot}_{*,c}
denote
the homotopy category of pointed, connected spaces.
Whitehead’s theorem says that in
\mathbf{Hot}_{*,c}
, the set of spheres jointly
reflects isomorphisms.
One is naturally led to wonder whether there is a set of spaces in
\mathbf{Hot}

which jointly reflects isomorphisms.

In
[
1
]
, Brown proved that a functor
\mathbf{Hot}_{*,c}^{\mathrm{op}}\to\mathbf{Set}
is
representable if and only if it is half-exact, in the sense that it sends
coproducts and weak pushouts in
\mathbf{Hot}_{*,c}
to products and weak pullbacks in
\mathbf{Set}
.
In
[
4
]
, Heller proved an abstract representability theorem: if
\mathbf{C}
is a
category with coproducts and weak pushouts and
\mathbf{C}
contains a “bounded” set
{\mathcal{G}}

of objects that jointly reflects isomorphisms (see Definition
1.1
below), then a functor

\mathbf{C}^{\mathrm{op}}\to\mathbf{Set}
is representable if and only if it is half-exact.
In the same paper, Heller gave an example of a half-exact functor
\mathbf{Hot}^{\mathrm{op}}\to\mathbf{Set}

which is not representable.
He then claimed without proof
[
4
, Prop. 1.2]
that every set of spaces
in
\mathbf{Hot}
is bounded,
and concluded
[
4

that no set of spaces jointly reflects isomorphisms
in
\mathbf{Hot}
.

We show that it is not true that every set of spaces is bounded,
reopening the question of whether there is a set of spaces that jointly reflects
isomorphisms in
\mathbf{Hot}
.
We thus also give an independent proof that no set of spaces jointly reflects isomorphisms.

We use the word “set” to mean what is sometimes called a “small set,” i.e.,

As mentioned above,

In the homotopy category of pointed, connected spaces, the set of spheres

If this is true, it means that Heller’s abstract representability theorem,

Next we state the result that shows that the statement of

Theorem

.

The category
\mathbf{Hot}
contains no set
{\mathcal{G}}
of spaces that jointly reflects isomorphisms.

{\mathcal{G}}
of spaces such that, if

2.
\mathbf{Hot}
admits no set that jointly reflects isomorphisms

We make the following definitions.
For an ordinal
\alpha
, write
\Sigma_{\alpha}
for the group of all
bijections of the set
\alpha
, ignoring order.
When
\beta<\alpha
, there is a natural inclusion

\Sigma_{\beta}\hookrightarrow\Sigma_{\alpha}
,
and we define
\Sigma_{\alpha}^{\mathrm{c}}
to be the union of the images of
\Sigma_{\beta}

for all
\beta<\alpha
.
We typically consider
\Sigma_{\alpha}^{\mathrm{c}}
when
\alpha
is a cardinal,
considered as the smallest ordinal with that cardinality,
and we call the elements of
\Sigma_{\alpha}^{\mathrm{c}}

essentially constant
permutations.

Theorem 2.1
.

The category
\mathbf{Hot}
contains no set
{\mathcal{G}}
of spaces that jointly reflects isomorphisms.
(See Definition
1.1
.)

Proof.

Let
{\mathcal{G}}
be a set of spaces and let
\alpha
be a regular cardinal
larger than the cardinality of
\pi_{1}(S,s_{0})
for each
S\in{\mathcal{G}}

and each
s_{0}\in S
.
We must construct a map
f:X\to Y
which is not a homotopy equivalence
but which induces bijections on homotopy classes of maps from spaces in

{\mathcal{G}}
.

Our example will be
Bs:B\Sigma_{\alpha}^{\mathrm{c}}\to B\Sigma_{\alpha}^{\mathrm{c}}
,
where
s:\Sigma_{\alpha}^{\mathrm{c}}\to\Sigma_{\alpha}^{\mathrm{c}}
is the shift homomorphism given by

(s\sigma)(\gamma)=\begin{cases}\sigma(\gamma^{\prime})+1,&\gamma=\gamma^{\prime}+1\\
\gamma,&\gamma\text{ a limit ordinal,}\end{cases}

for
\sigma\in\Sigma_{\alpha}^{\mathrm{c}}
.
(Here and in what follows, if
\gamma
is a successor ordinal, we write
\gamma^{\prime}
for its predecessor.)
We must check that
s\sigma\in\Sigma_{\alpha}^{\mathrm{c}}
. First, it is essentially
constant: if
\beta<\alpha
and
\sigma
fixes each
\gamma\geq\beta
, then
for
\gamma>\beta
we have
(s\sigma)(\gamma)=\gamma
, if

\gamma
is a limit ordinal, and
(s\sigma)(\gamma)=\sigma(\gamma^{\prime})+1=\gamma^{\prime}+1=\gamma
,
if
\gamma
is a successor.
Next, we see that
s
is a homomorphism:

s(\sigma\tau)
and
(s\sigma)(s\tau)
both fix all limit ordinals,
while for successors we have

(s\sigma)((s\tau)(\gamma))=\sigma([\tau(\gamma^{\prime})+1]^{\prime})+1=\sigma\tau(\gamma^{\prime})+1=s(\sigma\tau)(\gamma),

as desired. Note that setting
\tau=\sigma^{-1}
, respectively

\sigma=\tau^{-1}
, we confirm that
s\sigma
is indeed a
bijection.

Let
H
be a group with classifying space
BH
and let
X
be a connected space. If
\mathbf{Gp}
denotes the category of groups, recall that
\mathbf{Hot}(X,BH)
is isomorphic to
\mathbf{Gp}(\pi_{1}(X),H)
modulo conjugation by elements of
H.

(See, for example,
[
8
, Corollary V.4.4]
.)
In particular, we have a natural isomorphism
\mathbf{Hot}(X,BH)\cong\mathbf{Hot}(B\pi_{1}(X),BH)
.
It also follows that for groups
G
and
H
,

\mathbf{Hot}(BG,BH)
is isomorphic to
\mathbf{Gp}(G,H)
modulo conjugation by elements of
H
,
and that an element of
\mathbf{Hot}(BG,BH)
is a homotopy equivalence
if and only if it is represented by an isomorphism.

Note that
s
is not surjective, since
s\sigma
always preserves limit ordinals.
Therefore,
Bs:B\Sigma_{\alpha}^{\mathrm{c}}\to B\Sigma_{\alpha}^{\mathrm{c}}
is not a homotopy equivalence.
However, we will show that it induces an isomorphism on
{\mathcal{G}}
.
First observe that it suffices to prove this for connected components of spaces in
{\mathcal{G}}
.
It follows that it is enough
to prove this for spaces of the form
BG
, where
G
is a group of cardinality less than
\alpha
.

Any map
BG\to B\Sigma_{\alpha}^{\mathrm{c}}
arises from a homomorphism
\varphi:G\to\Sigma_{\alpha}^{\mathrm{c}}
, well-defined up to conjugation.
Since
\alpha
is regular, there is a limit ordinal

\beta<\alpha
so that
\varphi(g)\in\Sigma_{\beta}
for every
g\in G
. We claim that

s\circ\varphi
is conjugate to
\varphi
by an element
\tau\in\Sigma_{\alpha}^{\mathrm{c}}
defined as follows:

\tau(\gamma)=\begin{cases}\gamma^{\prime},&\gamma<\beta\text{ a successor ordinal}\\
\beta+\gamma,&\gamma<\beta\text{ a limit ordinal}\\
\gamma+1,&\beta\leq\gamma<\beta+\beta\\
\gamma,&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

It is straightforward to check that
\tau
is a permutation,
and it clearly
fixes ordinals greater than or equal to
\beta+\beta
, which is less
than
\alpha
.
For
g\in G
, let
\sigma=\varphi(g)
. Then, noting that

\tau^{-1}(\gamma)=\gamma+1
for any
\gamma<\beta
,
we have

\displaystyle(\tau^{-1}\sigma\tau)(\gamma)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\tau^{-1}(\sigma(\gamma^{\prime})),&\gamma<\beta\text{ a successor ordinal}\\
\tau^{-1}(\sigma(\beta+\gamma)),&\gamma<\beta\text{ a limit ordinal}\\
\tau^{-1}(\sigma(\gamma+1)),&\beta\leq\gamma<\beta+\beta\\
\tau^{-1}(\sigma(\gamma)),&\text{otherwise}\\
\end{cases}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\tau^{-1}(\sigma(\gamma^{\prime})),&\gamma<\beta\text{ a successor ordinal}\\
\tau^{-1}(\beta+\gamma),&\gamma<\beta\text{ a limit ordinal}\\
\tau^{-1}(\gamma+1),&\beta\leq\gamma<\beta+\beta\\
\tau^{-1}(\gamma),&\text{otherwise}\\
\end{cases}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\sigma(\gamma^{\prime})+1,&\gamma<\beta\text{ a successor ordinal}\\
\gamma,&\gamma<\beta\text{ a limit ordinal}\\
\gamma,&\beta\leq\gamma<\beta+\beta\\
\gamma,&\text{otherwise}\\
\end{cases}

\displaystyle=s(\sigma)(\gamma).

We have used that if
\gamma\geq\beta
, then
\sigma(\gamma)=\gamma
,
and the consequence that if
\gamma<\beta
, then
\sigma(\gamma)<\beta
.

In summary, we have shown that
Bs
induces the identity on
\mathbf{Hot}(S,B\Sigma_{\alpha}^{\mathrm{c}})
for every
S\in{\mathcal{G}}
,
proving the claim.
∎

Remark 2.2
.

Since the map
Bs:B\Sigma_{\alpha}^{\mathrm{c}}\to B\Sigma_{\alpha}^{\mathrm{c}}
used in the proof has connected domain and
codomain, it follows that there is no set of connected spaces that jointly
reflects isomorphisms in the homotopy category of connected spaces.

We explain the origin of the maps

\tau

Morally,

The map

implements this by “making room” for the relevant limit ordinals in a range

In fact, if we denote the map
\tau
above by
\tau_{\beta}
, then

itself is conjugation by

\tau_{\alpha}
in
\Sigma_{\gamma}^{\mathrm{c}}
for a regular cardinal
\gamma>\alpha
.

3.
The lack of privileged weak colimits

In this section, we give an example showing that Heller’s privileged weak colimits
do not generally exist.

Theorem 3.1
.

The set
{\mathcal{G}}=\{S^{n}\mid n\geq 0\}
of spheres in
\mathbf{Hot}
is not
\kappa
-bounded
for any ordinal
\kappa
of uncountable cofinality, e.g., for any uncountable regular cardinal.
That is, for each such
\kappa
, there exists a diagram
D:\kappa\to\mathbf{Hot}
that admits no

{\mathcal{G}}
-privileged weak colimit.

In particular,
D
admits no
{\mathcal{G}}
-privileged weak colimit for any set
{\mathcal{G}}
containing
the spheres.
Note that the set of spheres is
\aleph_{0}
-bounded, so we learn that boundedness
for one ordinal does not imply it for ordinals with larger cofinality.

Corollary 3.2
.

Let
T
denote a countably infinite, discrete space.
Then the set
\{S^{n}\mid n\geq 0\}\cup\{T\}
is not
\kappa
-bounded in
\mathbf{Hot}

for any limit ordinal
\kappa
.

Proof.

Since
\kappa
is a limit ordinal, it has infinite cofinality.
If
\kappa
has uncountable cofinality, then Theorem
3.1
applies.
If
\kappa
has countable cofinality, then
\{T\}
is not
\kappa
-bounded.
∎

3.1.
Reducing from spaces to groupoids

To prove Theorem
3.1
we will
work primarily in the homotopy category
\mathbf{HoGpd}
of groupoids, that is,
the category of groupoids and isomorphism classes of functors. It is well known
that the geometric realization of groupoids induces a reflective embedding

B:\mathbf{HoGpd}\to\mathbf{Hot}
whose left adjoint is the fundamental groupoid functor

\Pi_{1}
and whose image consists of the
1
-types, i.e., the spaces
X

with
\pi_{n}(X,x)=0
for all

and

All this follows from the adjunction between
\pi_{1}
and the classifying

that was used in the proof of Theorem

Lemma 3.3
.

Suppose given a diagram
D:J\to\mathbf{HoGpd}
, a set
{\mathcal{G}}^{\prime}
of groupoids,
and a set
{\mathcal{G}}
of spaces containing
B{\mathcal{G}}^{\prime}
as well as

for all

.
If
D
admits no
{\mathcal{G}}^{\prime}
-privileged weak colimit in
\mathbf{HoGpd}
, then

admits no
{\mathcal{G}}
-privileged weak colimit in

\mathbf{Hot}
.

Proof.

We prove the contrapositive.
Let
\lambda:B\circ D\to X
be a
{\mathcal{G}}
-privileged weak colimit, with

.
Then, since left adjoints preserve weak colimits,

is
a weak colimit. We will show that it is
{\mathcal{G}}^{\prime}
-privileged.

First, since

is

-privileged, every map

. We conclude

.

Since

is fully faithful, we see that

\displaystyle\mathbf{HoGpd}(G,\Pi_{1}X)

\displaystyle\cong\mathbf{Hot}(BG,B\left(\Pi_{1}X\right))\cong\mathbf{Hot}(BG,X)

\displaystyle\cong\colim_{j}\mathbf{Hot}(BG,BD(j))\cong\colim_{j}\mathbf{HoGpd}(G,D(j)).

.
∎

Note that, for any groupoid

4.
The spheres reflect equivalences in the 2-category of spaces

We saw in Theorem
2.1
that in the homotopy category of spaces
there is no set of objects that jointly reflects isomorphisms.
In this section, we show that in the homotopy
2-category
of spaces,
the spheres do jointly reflect equivalences.
We first define the terms we are using.

Definition 4.1
.

By
\underline{\mathbf{Hot}}
, we mean the 2-category
whose objects are spaces of the homotopy type of a CW-complex
and whose hom-categories are the fundamental
groupoids of mapping spaces, that is
\underline{\mathbf{Hot}}(X,Y)=\Pi_{1}(Y^{X})
.

Definition 4.2
.

A set
\mathcal{G}
of objects in a 2-category
\mathcal{K}

jointly reflects equivalences
if, whenever
f:X\to Y

is a morphism in
\mathcal{K}
such that, for every
S\in\mathcal{G}
, the induced
functor
\mathcal{K}(S,f):\mathcal{K}(S,X)\to\mathcal{K}(S,Y)
is an equivalence of categories,
then

itself must be an equivalence in
\mathcal{K}
.

We shall show in Theorem
4.3
that the 2-category

\underline{\mathbf{Hot}}
admits a set
\mathcal{G}
of objects that jointly reflects
equivalences, namely
\mathcal{G}=\{S^{n}\mid n\geq 0\}
.
Note that a map
f
in
\underline{\mathbf{Hot}}
is an equivalence if and only if it
is a homotopy equivalence. This theorem is a corollary of Theorem 1 in

[
6
]
, which shows that for a map
f:X\to Y
of (arcwise connected) spaces
which is surjective on all fundamental groups, bijectivity of

on
higher homotopy groups is equivalent to that on free homotopy classes of
maps from spheres.

With this, we are prepared to show that the spheres satisfy the analogue
of Whitehead’s theorem for
\underline{\mathbf{Hot}}

Theorem 4.3
.

The set

Since this has a retraction, the functor

That is,
f

\Pi_{1}(X)\to\Pi_{1}(Y)

Thus

\pi_{0}

\pi_{1}

\underline{\mathbf{Hot}}(S^{n},X)
. Since
f
induces an equivalence
\underline{\mathbf{Hot}}(S^{n},X)\to\underline{\mathbf{Hot}}(S^{n},Y)
,
a fortiori
it induces an isomorphism on
connected components, and the theorem is proven.

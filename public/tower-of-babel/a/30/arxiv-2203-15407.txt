Abstract
The ℤ p s \mathbb{Z}_{p^{s}} -additive codes of length n n are subgroups of ℤ p s n \mathbb{Z}_{p^{s}}^{n} , and can be seen as a generalization of linear codes over ℤ 2 \mathbb{Z}_{2} , ℤ 4 \mathbb{Z}_{4} , or ℤ 2 s \mathbb{Z}_{2^{s}} in general. A ℤ p s \mathbb{Z}_{p^{s}} -linear generalized Hadamard (GH) code is a GH code over ℤ p \mathbb{Z}_{p} which is the image of a ℤ p s \mathbb{Z}_{p^{s}} -additive code by a generalized Gray map. A partial classification of these codes by using the dimension of the kernel is known. In this paper, we establish that some ℤ p s \mathbb{Z}_{p^{s}} -linear GH codes of length p t p^{t} are equivalent, once t t is fixed. This allows us to improve the known upper bounds for the number of such nonequivalent codes. Moreover, up to t = 10 t=10 , this new upper bound coincides with a known lower bound (based on the rank and dimension of the kernel).

Introduction

Let
\mathbb{Z}_{p^{s}}
be the ring of integers modulo
p^{s}
with
p
prime and
s\geq 1
. The set of

n
-tuples over
\mathbb{Z}_{p^{s}}
is denoted by
\mathbb{Z}_{p^{s}}^{n}
. In this paper,
the elements of
\mathbb{Z}^{n}_{p^{s}}
will also be called vectors.
The order of a vector
\mathbf{u}
over
\mathbb{Z}_{p^{s}}
, denoted by

, is the smallest positive integer

such that
m\mathbf{u}={\mathbf{0}}
.

A code over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
of length

is a nonempty subset of
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{n}
,
and it is linear if it is a subspace of
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{n}
. Similarly, a nonempty
subset of
\mathbb{Z}_{p^{s}}^{n}
is a
\mathbb{Z}_{p^{s}}
-additive if it is a subgroup of
\mathbb{Z}_{p^{s}}^{n}
.
Note that, when
p=2
and
s=1
, a
\mathbb{Z}_{p^{s}}
-additive code is a binary linear code and, when
p=2
and
s=2
,
it is a quaternary linear code or a linear code over
\mathbb{Z}_{4}
.

In
[
15
]
, a Gray map from
\mathbb{Z}_{4}
to
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{2}
is defined as

\phi(0)=(0,0)
,
\phi(1)=(0,1)
,
\phi(2)=(1,1)
and
\phi(3)=(1,0)
. There exist different generalizations of this Gray map, which go from
\mathbb{Z}_{2^{s}}
to

\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{2^{s-1}}

[
7
,
5
,
8
,
21
,
17
]
.
The one given in
[
21
]
can be defined in terms of the elements of a Hadamard code
[
17
]
, and Carlet’s Gray map
[
7
]
is a particular case of the one given in
[
17
]

satisfying
\sum\lambda_{i}\phi(2^{i})=\phi(\sum\lambda_{i}2^{i})

[
10
]
.
In this paper, we focus on a generalization of Carlet’s Gray map, from
\mathbb{Z}_{p^{s}}
to
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{p^{s-1}}
, which is also a particular case of the one given in
[
27
]
. Specifically,

\phi_{s}(u)=(u_{s-1},\dots,u_{s-1})+(u_{0},\dots,u_{s-2})Y_{s-1},

(1)

where

,
[u_{0},u_{1},\ldots,u_{s-1}]_{p}
is the p-ary expansion of

Let
{\cal C}
be a
\mathbb{Z}_{p^{s}}
-additive code of length
n
. We say that its image

is a
\mathbb{Z}_{p^{s}}
-linear code of length
p^{s-1}n
.
Since
{\cal C}
is a subgroup of

\mathbb{Z}_{p^{s}}^{n}
, it is isomorphic to an abelian structure

\mathbb{Z}_{p^{s}}^{t_{1}}\times\mathbb{Z}_{p^{s-1}}^{t_{2}}\times\dots\times\mathbb{Z}_{p^{2}}^{t_{s-1}}\times\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{t_{s}}
, and we say that
{\cal C}
, or equivalently

, is of type
(n;t_{1},\dots,t_{s})
.
Note that
|{\cal C}|=p^{st_{1}}p^{(s-1)t_{2}}\cdots p^{t_{s}}
.
Unlike linear codes over finite fields,
linear codes over rings do not have a basis, but there
exists a generator matrix for these codes having minimum number of rows, that is,
t_{1}+\cdots+t_{s}
rows.
A
p
-linear combination of the elements of
\mathcal{B}=\{\mathbf{b}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{b}_{r}\}\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_{p^{s}}^{n}
is
\sum_{i=1}^{r}\lambda_{i}\mathbf{b}_{i}
, for
\lambda_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}
. We say that
\mathcal{B}
is a
p
-basis of
{\cal C}
if the elements in
\mathcal{B}
are
p
-linearly independent and any
\mathbf{c}\in{\cal C}
is a
p
-linear combination of the elements of
\cal{B}
.

Let
{\cal S}_{n}
be the symmetric group of permutations on the set
\{1,\dots,n\}
.
Two codes
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
, are said to be equivalent if there is a vector
\textbf{a}\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{n}
and a
permutation of coordinates
\pi\in{\cal S}_{n}
such that
C_{2}=\{\textbf{a}+\pi(\textbf{c}):\textbf{c}\in C_{1}\}
.
Two
\mathbb{Z}_{p^{s}}
-additive codes,
{\cal C}_{1}
and
{\cal C}_{2}
, are said to be permutation equivalent if they differ
only by a permutation of coordinates, that is, if there is a permutation of coordinates
\pi\in{\cal S}_{n}

such that
{\cal C}_{2}=\{\pi(\textbf{c}):\textbf{c}\in{\cal C}_{1}\}
.

Two structural properties of codes over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
are the rank and
dimension of the kernel. The rank of a code
C
over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
is simply the
dimension of the linear span,
\langle C\rangle
, of
C
.
The kernel of a code
C
over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
is defined as

\mathrm{K}(C)=\{\textbf{x}\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{n}:\textbf{x}+C=C\}

[
2
,
23
]
. If the all-zero vector belongs to
C
,
then
\mathrm{K}(C)
is a linear subcode of
C
.
Note also that if
C
is linear, then
K(C)=C=\langle C\rangle
.
We denote the rank of
C
as
\text{rank}(C)
and the dimension of the kernel as
\text{ker}(C)
.
These parameters can be used to distinguish between non-equivalent codes, since equivalent ones have the same rank and dimension of the kernel.

A generalized Hadamard
(GH)
matrix
H(p,\lambda)=(h_{ij})
of order
n=p\lambda
over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
is a
p\lambda\times p\lambda
matrix with entries from
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
with the property that for every
i,j
,
1\leq i<j\leq p\lambda,
each of the multisets
\{h_{is}-h_{js}:1\leq s\leq p\lambda\}
contains every element of
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
exactly
\lambda
times
[
16
]
.
An ordinary Hadamard matrix of order
4\mu
corresponds to
GH
matrix
H(2,\lambda)
over
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
, where
\lambda=2\mu

[
1
]
.
Two
GH
matrices
H_{1}
and
H_{2}
of order
n
are said to be equivalent if one can be obtained from the other by a permutation of the rows and columns and adding the same element of
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
to all the coordinates in a row or in a column. We can always change the first row and column of a
GH
matrix into zeros and we obtain an equivalent
GH
matrix which is called normalized. From a normalized Hadamard matrix
H
, we denote by
F_{H}
the code over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
consisting of the rows of
H
, and
C_{H}
the one defined as
C_{H}=\bigcup_{\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}}(F_{H}+\alpha\textbf{1})
,
where
F_{H}+\alpha\textbf{1}=\{\textbf{h}+\alpha\textbf{1}:\textbf{h}\in F_{H}\}
and
1
denotes the
all-one vector. The code
C_{H}
over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
is called generalized
Hadamard
(GH)
code
[
9
]
. Note that
C_{H}
is generally a nonlinear code over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
.

Let
{\cal C}
be a
\mathbb{Z}_{p^{s}}
-additive code such that
\Phi({\cal C})
is a GH code. Then, we say that
{\cal C}
is a
\mathbb{Z}_{p^{s}}
-additive GH code and
\Phi({\cal C})
is a
\mathbb{Z}_{p^{s}}
-linear GH code.
Note that a GH code over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
of length
N
has
pN
codewords and minimum distance
\frac{N(p-1)}{p}
.

The
\mathbb{Z}_{4}
-linear Hadamard codes of length
2^{t}
can be classified by using either the rank or the dimension of the kernel

18
,
22
]
. There are exactly

such codes for all

.
Later, in
[

]
, an iterative construction for
\mathbb{Z}_{2^{s}}
-linear Hadamard codes was described, and the linearity and kernel of these codes were established. It was proved that the dimension of the kernel only provides a complete classification for some values of

and

. A partial classification by using the kernel was obtained, and the exact amount of non-equivalent such codes was given up to

for any
s\geq 2
. Authors also give a lower bound based on the rank and dimension of the kernel. Later, in paper
[
12
]
, for

, it was established that some

-linear Hadamard codes of length

are equivalent, once

is fixed, and this fact improved the known upper bounds for the number of such nonequivalent codes. Moreover, authors show that, up to

, this new upper bound coincides with the known lower bound (based on the rank and dimension of the kernel). Finally, for

, they establish the full classification of the

-linear Hadamard codes of length

by giving the exact number of such codes.
For

, and

prime, the dimension of the kernel for

-linear GH codes of length

is established in
[

]
, and it is proved that this invariant only provides a complete classification for certain values of

. Lower and upper bounds are also established for the number of nonequivalent

-linear GH codes of length

, when both

is fixed; denoted by

and

, respectively.
From
[
6
]
, we can check that there are nonlinear codes having the same rank and dimension of the kernel for different values of

is fixed, for all

In this paper, we show that there are generalized Hadamard codes of length

-linear codes for different values of

The paper is organized as follows. In Section

Lemma 3.4

Let
s\geq 2
. Then,

(i)

\tau_{s}(1)=(0,p^{s-2},\dots,(p-1)p^{s-2})
,

(ii)

\tau_{s}(p^{i}u)=p^{i-1}(u,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle p}}{{\dots}},u)
for all
i\in\{1,\dots,s-1\}
and
u\in\{0,1,\dots,p^{s-1}-1\}
.

Proof.
First, let
v=(0,1,\dots,p-1)\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{p}
. Then,

\begin{split}\tau_{s}(1)&=\Phi_{s-1}^{-1}(\gamma_{s}^{-1}(\phi_{s}(1)))\\
&=\Phi_{s-1}^{-1}(\gamma_{s}^{-1}(v,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle p^{s-2}}}{{\dots}},v))\\
&=\Phi_{s-1}^{-1}(\mathbf{0},\mathbf{1},\dots,\mathbf{p-1}),\textrm{by Lemma \ref{lem:gamma}}.\end{split}

Therefore by Lemma
3.3
,
\tau_{s}(1)=(0,p^{s-2},\dots,(p-1)p^{s-2})
, and
(i)
holds.

In order to prove
(ii)
, let
u\in\mathbb{Z}_{p^{s}}
and
[u_{0},\dots,u_{s-1}]_{p}
be its p-ary expansion. The p-ary expansion of
p^{i}u
is
[0,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle i}}{{\dots}},0,u_{0},\dots,u_{s-i-1}]_{p}
and we have that
\phi_{s}(p^{i}u)=(u_{s-i-1},\dots,u_{s-i-1})+(0,\dots,0,u_{0},\dots,u_{s-i-2})Y_{s-1}
.
Recall that the matrix
Y_{s-1}
given in (
1
), related to the definition of
\phi_{s}
, is a matrix of size
(s-1)\times p^{s-1}
whose columns are the elements of
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{s-1}
. Without loss of generality, we consider that
Y_{s}
is the matrix obtained recursively from
Y_{1}=(0\penalty\ 1\penalty\ \cdots\penalty\ p-1)
and

Y_{s}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}Y_{s-1}&Y_{s-1}&\cdots&Y_{s-1}\\
{\mathbf{0}}&{\mathbf{1}}&\cdots&\mathbf{p-1}\\
\end{array}\right).

(11)

By Lemma
3.2
, we can write

\gamma_{s}^{-1}(Y_{s-1})=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}{\mathbf{0}}&{\mathbf{1}}&\cdots&\mathbf{p-1}\\
Y_{s-2}&Y_{s-2}&\cdots&Y_{s-2}\\
\end{array}\right).

(12)

Then, we have that

\begin{split}&\gamma_{s}^{-1}(\phi_{s}(p^{i}u))\\
&=(u_{s-i-1},\stackrel{{\scriptstyle p^{s-1}}}{{\dots}},u_{s-i-1})+(0,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle i}}{{\dots}},0,u_{0},\dots,u_{s-i-2})\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}{\mathbf{0}}&{\mathbf{1}}&\cdots&\mathbf{p-1}\\
Y_{s-2}&Y_{s-2}&\cdots&Y_{s-2}\\
\end{array}\right)\\
&=(u_{s-i-1},\stackrel{{\scriptstyle p^{s-1}}}{{\dots}},u_{s-i-1})+(0,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle i-1}}{{\dots}},0,u_{0},\dots,u_{s-i-2})\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}Y_{s-2}&Y_{s-2}&\cdots&Y_{s-2}\\
\end{array}\right)\\
&=(\phi_{s-1}(p^{i-1}u),\phi_{s-1}(p^{i-1}u),\dots,\phi_{s-1}(p^{i-1}u))=\Phi_{s-1}(p^{i-1}(u,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle p}}{{\dots}},u)).\end{split}

Therefore,
\tau_{s}(p^{i}u)=\Phi_{s-1}^{-1}(\gamma_{s}^{-1}(\phi_{s}(p^{i}u)))=p^{i-1}(u,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle p}}{{\dots}},u)
, and
(ii)
holds.

\square

Proposition 3.1

Let
s\geq 2
and
\lambda_{i}\in\{0,1,\dots,p-1\}
,
i\in\{0,\dots,s-1\}
. Then,

\phi_{s}(\sum_{i=0}^{s-1}\lambda_{i}p^{i})=\gamma_{s}(\Phi_{s-1}(\sum_{i=0}^{s-1}\tau_{s}(\lambda_{i}p^{i}))).

(13)

Proof.
By Lemma
3.4
, we know that for all
i\in\{1,\dots,s-1\}
,
\tau_{s}(p^{i})=(p^{i-1},p^{i-1},\dots,p^{i-1})
and
\tau_{s}(1)=(0,p^{s-2},\dots,(p-1)p^{s-2})
.
Then, by Lemma
3.1
, we have that

\gamma_{s}(\Phi_{s-1}(\sum_{i=0}^{s-1}\tau_{s}(\lambda_{i}p^{i})))=\gamma_{s}(\sum_{i=0}^{s-1}\Phi_{s-1}(\tau_{s}(\lambda_{i}p^{i}))).

Moreover,
\gamma_{s}
commutes with the addition. Therefore, by applying the definition of the map
\tau_{s}
given in (
9
), we obtain that

\gamma_{s}(\Phi_{s-1}(\sum_{i=0}^{s-1}\tau_{s}(\lambda_{i}p^{i})))=\sum_{i=0}^{s-1}\gamma_{s}(\Phi_{s-1}(\tau_{s}(\lambda_{i}p^{i})))=\sum_{i=0}^{s-1}\phi_{s}(\lambda_{i}p^{i}),

which is equal to
\phi_{s}(\sum_{i=0}^{s-1}\lambda_{i}p^{i})
by Lemma
3.1
.

\square

Corollary 3.1

(14)

Proof.
By Proposition
3.1
, we have that

\phi_{s}(\sum_{i=0}^{s-1}\lambda_{i}p^{i})=\gamma_{s}(\Phi_{s-1}(\sum_{i=0}^{s-1}\tau_{s}(\lambda_{i}p^{i}))),

and, therefore,

\Phi_{s-1}^{-1}(\gamma_{s}^{-1}(\phi_{s}(\sum_{i=0}^{s-1}\lambda_{i}p^{i})))=\sum_{i=0}^{s-1}\tau_{s}(\lambda_{i}p^{i}),\\

that is,
\tau_{s}(\sum_{i=0}^{s-1}\lambda_{i}2^{i})=\sum_{i=0}^{s-1}\tau_{s}(\lambda_{i}p^{i})
.

\square

Now, we extend the permutation

C_{H}(t_{1},\dots,t_{s})
with
\sigma^{\prime}=1
, that is, the only one with
t^{\prime}_{1}\geq 2
.

(ii)

The
\mathbb{Z}_{p^{s^{\prime}}}
-linear Hadamard code

is the only one in
C_{H}(t_{1},\dots,t_{s})
with
t^{\prime}_{s^{\prime}}=0
.

Now, given any
\mathbb{Z}_{p^{s}}
-linear Hadamard code
H^{t_{1},\dots,t_{s}}
, we determine the chain of equivalences containing this code, as well as its position in the sequence. Therefore, note that indeed we prove that any code
H^{t_{1},\dots,t_{s}}
(with
t_{1}\geq 1
) belongs to a unique chain of equivalences

C_{H}(t^{\prime}_{1},\dots,t^{\prime}_{s^{\prime}})
with
t^{\prime}_{1}\geq 2
.

Proposition 3.4

Let
t_{1},t_{2},\dots,t_{s}
be nonnegative integers with
t_{1}\geq 1
. The
\mathbb{Z}_{p^{s}}
-linear Hadamard code
H^{t_{1},\dots,t_{s}}
belongs to
an unique chain of equivalences.
If
t_{1}\geq 2
, then
\sigma=1
and
H^{t_{1},\dots,t_{s}}=C_{H}(t_{1},\dots,t_{s})[1]
. Otherwise, if
t_{1}=1
, then
\sigma>1
and
H^{t_{1},\dots,t_{s}}=C_{H}(t^{\prime}_{1},\dots,t^{\prime}_{s^{\prime}})[\sigma]
, where
(t^{\prime}_{1},\dots,t^{\prime}_{s^{\prime}})=(t_{\sigma}+1,t_{\sigma+1},\dots,t_{s-1},t_{s}+\sigma-1)
and
s^{\prime}=s-\sigma+1
.

Proof.
If
t_{1}\geq 2
, it is clear by the definition of chain of equivalences and Corollary
3.3
.
If
t_{1}=1
, then
\sigma>1
. By Proposition
3.3
, since
H^{t_{1},\dots,t_{s}}=C_{H}(t^{\prime}_{1},\dots,t^{\prime}_{s^{\prime}})[\sigma]
, we have that
(t_{1},\dots,t_{s})=(1,{\mathbf{0}}^{\sigma-2},t^{\prime}_{1}-1,t^{\prime}_{2},\ldots,t^{\prime}_{s^{\prime}-1},t^{\prime}_{s^{\prime}}-\sigma+1)
. Therefore,
t_{\sigma}=t^{\prime}_{1}-1
,
t_{\sigma+1}=t^{\prime}_{2}
,
\ldots
,
t_{s-1}=t^{\prime}_{s^{\prime}-1}
,
t_{s}=t^{\prime}_{s^{\prime}}-\sigma+1
, and the result follows.

\square

Corollary 3.4

Let
H^{t_{1},\dots,t_{s}}
be a
\mathbb{Z}_{p^{s}}
-linear Hadamard code and
C_{H}(t^{\prime}_{1},\dots,t^{\prime}_{s^{\prime}})
the chain of equivalences such that

. Then,

|C_{H}(t^{\prime}_{1},\dots,t^{\prime}_{s^{\prime}})|=t_{s}+\sigma.

Proof.
If
t_{1}\geq 2

3.4

3.2
,
|C_{H}(t_{1},\dots,t_{s})|=t_{s}+1=t_{s}+\sigma

t_{1}=1

H^{t_{1},\dots,t_{s}}=C_{H}(t_{\sigma}+1,t_{\sigma+1},\dots,

t_{s-1},t_{s}+\sigma-1)[\sigma]

3.4

|C_{H}(t_{\sigma}+1,t_{\sigma+1},\dots,

t_{s-1},t_{s}+\sigma-1)|=t_{s}+\sigma-1+1=t_{s}+\sigma
by Corollary
3.2
.

\square

Corollary 4.1

Let
H^{t_{1},\dots,t_{s}}
be a
\mathbb{Z}_{p^{s}}
-linear GH code. Then,
H^{t_{1},\dots,t_{s}}
is equivalent to
t_{s}+\sigma

\mathbb{Z}_{p^{s^{\prime}}}
-linear GH codes for
s^{\prime}\in\{s+1-\sigma,\dots,s+t_{s}\}
. Among them, there is exactly one
H^{t^{\prime}_{1},\dots,t^{\prime}_{s^{\prime}}}
with
t^{\prime}_{1}\geq 2
, and there is exactly one
H^{t^{\prime}_{1},\dots,t^{\prime}_{s^{\prime}}}
with
t^{\prime}_{s^{\prime}}=0
.

Proof.
The code
H^{t_{1},\dots,t_{s}}
belongs to a chain of equivalences
C
, which can be determined by Proposition
3.4
. We have that
H^{t_{1},\dots,t_{s}}
is equivalent to any code in
C
by Theorem
3.1
, and the number of codes in
C
is
t_{s}+\sigma
by Corollary
3.4
. By Proposition
3.4
, the first code in
C
has
s^{\prime}=s-\sigma+1
. By Proposition
3.3
, the
i
th code

H^{t^{\prime}_{1},\dots,t^{\prime}_{s^{\prime}}}=C[i]
has
s^{\prime}=s-\sigma+i
for
i\in\{1,\dots,t_{s}+\sigma\}
.
Therefore,
s^{\prime}\in\{s-\sigma+1,\dots,s+t_{s}\}
.
Finally, by Corollary
3.3
,
C[1]
is the only code in
C
with
t^{\prime}_{1}\geq 2
and
C[t_{s}+\sigma]
is the only code in
C
with
t^{\prime}_{s^{\prime}}=0
.
\square

Analytical structure of the binary collision integral and the ultrarelativistic limit of transport coefficients of an ideal gas
David Wagner, Victor E. Ambru s ,, Etele Molnár

Abstract
In this paper we discuss the analytical properties of the binary collision integral for a gas of ultrarelativistic particles interacting via a constant cross-section. Starting from a near-equilibrium expansion over a complete basis of irreducible tensors in momentum space we compute the linearized collision matrices analytically. Using these results we then numerically compute all transport-coefficients of relativistic fluid dynamics with various power-counting schemes that are second-order in Knudsen and/or inverse Reynolds numbers. Furthermore, we also exactly compute the leading-order contribution with respect to the particle mass to the coefficient of bulk viscosity, the relaxation time, and other second-order transport coefficients of the bulk viscous pressure.



I
Introduction

The kinetic theory of rarefied gases contains a collision term which describes the interaction among constituents through collisions. The well known collision term defined by Boltzmann’s
Stoßzahlansatz
, or the assumption of
molecular chaos
, defines the number of binary collisions through a product of two single-particle distribution functions.
The resulting integro-differential equation, the Boltzmann transport equation, describes the space-time
evolution of the single-particle distribution function
de Groot et al. 1980
;
Cercignani and Kremer 2002
;
Denicol and Rischke 2021

k^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}f_{\mathbf{k}}=C\left[f\right]\;,

(1)

where
C\left[f\right]
is the collision term. In the case of binary
elastic collisions, the collision term reads,

\displaystyle C\left[f\right]

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{1}{2}\int\mathrm{d}K^{\prime}\mathrm{d}P\mathrm{d}P^{\prime}\,\left(W_{\mathbf{pp}^{\prime}\rightarrow\mathbf{kk}^{\prime}}f_{\mathbf{p}}f_{\mathbf{p}^{\prime}}\tilde{f}_{\mathbf{k}}\tilde{f}_{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}\right.

\displaystyle-\left.W_{\mathbf{kk}^{\prime}\rightarrow\mathbf{pp}^{\prime}}f_{\mathbf{k}}f_{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}\tilde{f}_{\mathbf{p}}\tilde{f}_{\mathbf{p}^{\prime}}\right)\;,

(2)

where
f_{\mathbf{k}}\equiv f_{\mathbf{k}}(x^{\mu},k^{\mu})
denotes the Lorentz-invariant single-particle distribution function, while
\tilde{f}_{\mathbf{k}}\equiv 1-af_{\mathbf{k}}
, with
a=\pm 1
for
fermions/bosons and
a=0
for classical particles.
The Lorentz-invariant differential element is

\mathrm{d}K\equiv g\,\mathrm{d}^{3}\mathbf{k/}\left[(2\pi)^{3}k^{0}\right]
, while
g
denotes the number of internal degrees of freedom. The
1/2
factor removes the double counting from the integrations with respect to
\mathrm{d}P
and
\mathrm{d}P^{\prime}
.
The four-momentum of particles
k^{\mu}=(k^{0},\mathbf{k})
is normalized to their rest mass
squared,
k^{\mu}k_{\mu}=m_{0}^{2}
, where
k^{0}=\sqrt{\mathbf{k}^{2}+m_{0}^{2}}
is the
on-shell energy. In this paper we use natural units
\hbar=c=k_{B}=1
.

The binary transition rate is defined as

W_{\mathbf{kk}^{\prime}\rightarrow\mathbf{pp}^{\prime}}\equiv\frac{s}{g^{2}}(2\pi)^{6}\frac{\mathrm{d}\sigma(\sqrt{s},\Omega)}{\mathrm{d}\Omega}\delta(k^{\mu}+k^{\prime\mu}-p^{\mu}-p^{\prime\mu})\;,

(3)

where the factor
(2\pi)^{6}/g^{2}
appears due to our convention for the momentum-space integration measure. For simplicity, in the remainder of this paper we set
g=1
.
The delta function ensures the conservation of the energy and momentum in binary collision.
The transition rate depends on the total center-of-momentum (CM) energy squared

s\equiv(k^{\mu}+k^{\prime\mu})^{2}=(p^{\mu}+p^{\prime\mu})^{2}
, while the total cross section integrated over the solid angle
\Omega
is defined as
Fotakis et al. 2022

\sigma_{T}(\sqrt{s})=\frac{1}{2}\int\mathrm{d}\Omega\frac{\mathrm{d}\sigma(\sqrt{s},\Omega)}{\mathrm{d}\Omega}\;.

(4)

In this paper we employ the so-called
hard-sphere

approximation which assumes that the transport cross section is isotropic and independent of the total CM energy,

\sigma_{T}\equiv 2\pi\frac{\mathrm{d}\sigma(\sqrt{s},\Omega)}{\mathrm{d}\Omega}=\frac{1}{n_{0}\lambda_{\rm mfp}}\;,

(5)

where
n_{0}
is the particle density and

\lambda_{\textrm{mfp}}
is the mean free path between collisions.

The relativistic Boltzmann equation provides a framework for studying various properties of
matter in and out of equilibrium, as well as for deriving the macroscopic conservation laws,
i.e., fluid dynamics, based on the microscopic properties of the system.

A vanishing collision term,
C\left[f_{0\mathbf{k}}\right]=0
, due to detailed balance,
defines the local equilibrium distribution, the Jüttner distribution function
Jüttner 1911
;
Cercignani and Kremer 2002
;
Denicol and Rischke 2021
,

f_{0\mathbf{k}}=\left[\exp\left(\beta k^{\mu}u_{\mu}-\alpha\right)+a\right]^{-1}\;,

(6)

where
u^{\mu}=\gamma(1,\mathbf{v})
is the timelike fluid-flow four-velocity normalized to
u^{\mu}u_{\mu}=1
, while
\gamma=(1-\mathbf{v}^{2})^{-1/2}
.
Furthermore,
\beta=1/T
is the inverse temperature and
\alpha=\beta\mu
, with
\mu
the chemical potential.
Out of equilibrium, the distribution function is separated as

f_{\mathbf{k}}\equiv f_{0\mathbf{k}}+\delta f_{\mathbf{k}}\;.

(7)

In this paper we apply a relativistic version of Grad’s method of moments
Grad 1949
, as formulated
by Denicol, Niemi, Molnár and Rischke, (referred to as DNMR)
Denicol et al. 2012
to obtain the transport coefficients for a classical gas of massless particles interacting via an isotropic constant cross-section.
Therein the irreducible moments of tensor-rank
\ell
of
\delta f_{\mathbf{k}}
are defined as

\rho_{r}^{\mu_{1}\cdots\mu_{\ell}}\equiv\int\mathrm{d}KE_{\mathbf{k}}^{r}k^{\langle\mu_{1}}\cdots k^{\mu_{\ell}\rangle}\delta f_{\mathbf{k}}\;.

(8)

Here,
r
denotes the power of energy
E_{\mathbf{k}}\equiv k^{\mu}u_{\mu}
,
while
k^{\left\langle\mu_{1}\right.}\cdots k^{\left.\mu_{\ell}\right\rangle}=\Delta_{\nu_{1}\cdots\nu_{\ell}}^{\mu_{1}\cdots\mu_{\ell}}k^{\nu_{1}}\cdots k^{\nu_{\ell}}

are the irreducible tensors forming a complete orthogonal basis
Denicol et al. 2012
;
de Groot et al. 1980
.
The four-momentum is decomposed as
k^{\mu}=E_{\mathbf{k}}u^{\mu}+k^{\langle\mu\rangle}
, where

k^{\left\langle\mu\right\rangle}\equiv\Delta_{\nu}^{\mu}k^{\nu}
is defined using the
elementary projection operator
\Delta^{\mu\nu}\equiv g^{\mu\nu}-u^{\mu}u^{\nu}
, with
g^{\mu\nu}=\text{diag}(+,-,-,-)
being the metric tensor.
The symmetric, traceless, and orthogonal
projection tensors of rank
2\ell
,

\Delta_{\nu_{1}\cdots\nu_{\ell}}^{\mu_{1}\cdots\mu_{\ell}}
, are constructed
using the
\Delta^{\mu\nu}
projectors.

Expressing the comoving derivative of irreducible moments,

\dot{\rho}_{r}^{\left\langle\mu_{1}\cdots\mu_{\ell}\right\rangle}\equiv\Delta_{\nu_{1}\cdots\nu_{\ell}}^{\mu_{1}\cdots\mu_{\ell}}u^{\alpha}\partial_{\alpha}\rho_{r}^{\nu_{1}\cdots\nu_{\ell}}
,
the equations of motion for these moments follow from the Boltzmann equation (
1
).
For the sake of concision, we do not list the complete equations of motion, since they
can be found in Eqs. (35)–(46) of Ref.
Denicol et al. 2012
, and instead quote just the following terms:

\displaystyle\dot{\rho}_{r}-C_{r-1}

\displaystyle=\alpha_{r}^{\left(0\right)}\theta+(\textrm{higher-order terms})\;,

(9)

\displaystyle\dot{\rho}_{r}^{\left\langle\mu\right\rangle}-C_{r-1}^{\left\langle\mu\right\rangle}

\displaystyle=\alpha_{r}^{\left(1\right)}\nabla^{\mu}\alpha+(\textrm{higher-order terms})\;,

(10)

\displaystyle\dot{\rho}_{r}^{\left\langle\mu\nu\right\rangle}-C_{r-1}^{\left\langle\mu\nu\right\rangle}

\displaystyle=2\alpha_{r}^{\left(2\right)}\sigma^{\mu\nu}+(\textrm{higher-order terms})\;,

(11)

where the irreducible moments of the collision term (
2
) are defined similarly
to Eq. (
8
) as

C_{r}^{\langle\mu_{1}\cdots\mu_{\ell}\rangle}\equiv\int\mathrm{d}K\,E_{\mathbf{k}}^{r}k^{\langle\mu_{1}}\cdots k^{\mu_{\ell}\rangle}C[f]\;.

(12)

The coefficients
\alpha^{(\ell)}_{r}\equiv\alpha^{(\ell)}_{r}(\alpha,\beta)
are thermodynamic functions.
Furthermore,
\nabla^{\mu}=\Delta^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\nu}
is the gradient operator,
\theta\equiv\nabla_{\mu}u^{\mu}
is the expansion scalar and

\sigma^{\mu\nu}\equiv\frac{1}{2}\left(\nabla^{\mu}u^{\nu}+\nabla^{\nu}u^{\mu}\right)-\frac{1}{3}\theta\Delta^{\mu\nu}
is the shear tensor.

The conservation of particle number as well as of energy and momentum in binary collisions require that the corresponding moments of the collision term vanish equivalently,
i.e.,
C_{0}=0
,
C_{1}=0
, and
C_{1}^{\langle\mu\rangle}=0
.
The resulting equations of motion are the conservation laws of fluid dynamics,

\partial_{\mu}N^{\mu}=0\;,\quad\partial_{\mu}T^{\mu\nu}=0\;,

(13)

where the particle four-current and energy-momentum tensor are given by

\displaystyle N^{\mu}

\displaystyle=n_{0}u^{\mu}+V^{\mu}\;,

(14)

\displaystyle T^{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=e_{0}u^{\mu}u^{\nu}-\left(P_{0}+\Pi\right)\Delta^{\mu\nu}+\pi^{\mu\nu}\;.

(15)

Here,
n_{0}
,
e_{0}
, and
P_{0}
, are the particle density, energy density, and the isotropic pressure, in equilibrium.
The bulk viscous pressure, the particle diffusion four-current and the shear-stress tensor are defined by

\displaystyle\Pi

\displaystyle\equiv-\frac{1}{3}T^{\mu\nu}\Delta_{\mu\nu}-P_{0}=-\frac{m^{2}_{0}}{3}\rho_{0}\;,

(16)

\displaystyle V^{\mu}

\displaystyle\equiv\Delta^{\mu}_{\alpha}N^{\alpha}=\rho^{\mu}_{0}\;,

(17)

\displaystyle\pi^{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle\equiv\Delta^{\mu\nu}_{\alpha\beta}T^{\alpha\beta}=\rho^{\mu\nu}_{0}\;.

(18)

In the above decompositions the fluid-flow four-velocity is
the timelike eigenvector of the energy-momentum tensor,
u^{\mu}=T^{\mu\nu}u_{\nu}/e_{0}
, as per Landau’s definition
Landau and Lifšic 1991
, hence

\rho^{\mu}_{1}\equiv\Delta^{\mu}_{\alpha}T^{\alpha\beta}u_{\beta}=0\;.

(19)

The chemical potential and the temperature are determined through the
Landau matching conditions,

\rho_{1}\equiv N^{\mu}u_{\mu}-n_{0}=0\;,\quad\rho_{2}\equiv T^{\mu\nu}u_{\mu}u_{\nu}-e_{0}=0\;.

(20)

The equations of motion for the primary dissipative quantities

\rho_{0}=-3\Pi/m^{2}_{0}
,
\rho_{0}^{\mu}=V^{\mu}
, and
\rho_{0}^{\mu\nu}=\pi^{\mu\nu}

follow from Eqs. (
9
)–(
11
).
The five conservation equations (
13
) couple to these
nine transport equations which contain various transport coefficients that
explicitly depend on the underlying approximations and the influence of all non-dynamical moments
included in this truncation.
These equations are truncated according to a power-counting scheme in Knudsen and inverse Reynolds numbers.
The Knudsen number,
\textrm{Kn}\equiv\lambda_{\textrm{mfp}}/L
, is the ratio of the particle mean free
path
\lambda_{\text{mfp}}
and a characteristic macroscopic scale
L
, while the inverse Reynolds number
\mathrm{Re}^{-1}
is the ratio of an out-of-equilibrium and a local equilibrium macroscopic field.
The resulting equations of fluid dynamics are of second-order in Knudsen and/or inverse Reynolds numbers, and are closed in terms of 14 dynamical moments contained in
N^{\mu}
and
T^{\mu\nu}
.

Focusing on this second-order theory of relativistic fluid dynamics,
we compute the moments of the linearized collision term for a gas of ultrarelativistic hard spheres with constant cross-section.
Introducing a novel anisotropic decomposition of the collision integral in the center-of-momentum frame,
the calculation of the linearized collision matrices is done analytically in the ultrarelativistic limit.
Using these exact results in the 14-dynamical moment approximation we collect and compute all transport coefficients, with five significant digits of precision, for truncation orders
N_{0}-2=N_{1}-1=N_{2}=1000
, corresponding to
N_{0}+3N_{1}+5N_{2}+9=9014
moments included in the basis.

We also compare the effect of three slightly different power-counting schemes for
the non-dynamic moments introduced in Refs.
Denicol et al. 2012
,
Wagner et al. 2022
, and
Ambrus et al. 2022
, on all transport coefficients.
Specifically, we will consider and compare

1.

the DNMR approach with additional corrections to the
O({\rm Kn}^{2})
transport coefficients of Refs.
Denicol et al. 2012
;
Molnár et al. 2014
,

2.

the Inverse Reynolds Dominance (IReD) approach of Ref.
Wagner et al. 2022
, where all
O({\rm Kn}^{2})
terms are rewritten and absorbed into the
O({\rm Re}^{-1}{\rm Kn})
terms,

3.

the corrected DNMR (cDNMR) approach of Ref.
Ambrus et al. 2022
, where the
O({\rm Kn}^{2})
transport coefficients receive contributions only from the asymptotic matching of the moments
\rho^{\mu_{1}\cdots\mu_{\ell}}_{r>0}
.

All three schemes considered here fully account for all second-order terms with respect to
{\rm Kn}
and
{\rm Re}^{-1}
and are therefore equivalent (up to terms of third order) within this truncation scheme.
At asymptotically long times, when the magnitudes of the Knudsen and
the inverse Reynolds numbers are of the same order, i.e.,
\textrm{Kn}\sim\textrm{Re}^{-1}
,
also known as the order of magnitude approximation
Molnár et al. 2014
;
Fotakis et al. 2022
;
Struchtrup 2004
,
there is freedom to re-arrange the transport coefficients.
The IReD approximation of Ref.
Wagner et al. 2022
expresses the thermodynamic forces in terms
of the primary dissipative quantities to replace
\textrm{Kn}^{2}\rightarrow\textrm{Kn}\,\textrm{Re}^{-1}

and hence removes terms that are of second order in the Knudsen number from the fluid-dynamical equations
of motion.
In this paper, we focus solely on the linearized binary collision integral, which does not provide
{\rm Re}^{-2}
terms. However, one may include nonlinear contributions to the collision integral as described in Ref.
Molnár et al. 2014
.

The main results of this paper are the closed-form computation of the collision matrices for the scalar,
vector and tensor moments in the case of massless ultrarelativistic particles interacting through a constant isotropic cross-section.
This interaction model reduces in the non-relativistic limit to the well-studied hard-sphere interaction model, for which the first-order transport coefficients, i.e., the shear viscosity and heat conductivity, can be obtained in terms of the so-called Chapman-Cowling collision integrals
Chapman and Cowling 1991
;
Liboff 2003
via a successive iterative refinement procedure.
This method can be extended into the relativistic regime
Stewart 1971
;
Cercignani and Kremer 2002
, where the exact expression requires a resummation over the entire hierarchy of moments
Denicol et al. 2012
.
We will demonstrate the truncation-order dependence with an analytical result only for the leading-order contribution with respect to the particle mass
m_{0}
to the bulk viscosity coefficient
\zeta
and to the relaxation time
\tau_{\Pi}
for the bulk viscous pressure. For all other transport coefficients, we rely on numerical methods to obtain their values in the limit of infinite truncation order.

Another collision model for which the transport coefficients are obtained with similar accuracy as for the hard-sphere model is that of the so-called Maxwell molecules
Cercignani 1988
;
Liboff 2003
, interacting via a potential
V(r)\sim r^{-5}
, with
r
being the distance between two interacting particles.
Two relativistic generalizations of this model correspond to the Israel particles model
Israel 1963

and the Polak model
Polak et al. 1973
.
More recently, the collision operator corresponding to the
\lambda\phi^{4}
scalar field theory was studied in Refs.
Mullins et al. 2022
;
Denicol and Noronha 2022
. These results were used to compute transport coefficients in several fluid dynamical theories in Ref.
Rocha et al. 2023
.
The present work complements these studies considering the analogous problem for hard spheres.

This paper is structured as follows. In Sect.
II
, we introduce the expansion of the distribution function and the linearized collision integral in terms of irreducible moments.
Then we discuss the various power-counting methods and the transport coefficients of second-order fluid dynamics.
Section
III
clarifies the analytical structure of the collision integrals appearing in the moment equations up to tensor-rank two. These expressions are the main results of this work.
In Sect.
IV
all first- and second-order transport coefficients are computed in the ultrarelativistic limit.
The exact results for the coefficient of bulk viscosity and the relaxation time of the bulk viscous pressure are computed in Sect.
V
.
Finally, Sect.
VI
concludes this work. For reasons of brevity and clarity, various computations
are relegated to the Appendices.

II
Second-order fluid dynamics with 14 dynamical moments

For reasons of completeness, we first summarize the derivation of second-order relativistic
fluid dynamics from the Boltzmann equation based on Ref.
Denicol et al. 2012
.
The near-equilibrium expansion is summarized in Sect.
II.1
.
In Sect.
II.2
, we discuss the linearized collision integral and the various
power-counting schemes in a unitary fashion.
Finally, Sect.
II.3
provides the relaxation equations of second-order fluid
dynamics with 14 dynamical moments.
The particle rest mass
m_{0}
is considered arbitrary (non-vanishing) throughout this section.

II.1
Near-equilibrium expansion over a complete basis of irreducible tensors

The expansion of
\delta f_{\mathbf{k}}
is given by,

\displaystyle\delta f_{\mathbf{k}}

\displaystyle\equiv f_{0\mathbf{k}}\tilde{f}_{0\mathbf{k}}\phi_{\mathbf{k}}

\displaystyle=f_{0{\mathbf{k}}}\tilde{f}_{0\mathbf{k}}\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\sum_{n=0}^{N_{\ell}}\rho_{n}^{\mu_{1}\cdots\mu_{\ell}}k_{\left\langle\mu_{1}\right.}\cdots k_{\left.\mu_{\ell}\right\rangle}\mathcal{H}_{\mathbf{k}n}^{(\ell)}\;,

(21)

where the coefficient
\mathcal{H}_{{\mathbf{k}}n}^{(\ell)}
is a polynomial in
energy of order
N_{\ell}\rightarrow\infty
defined through another polynomial

P_{\mathbf{k}m}^{(\ell)}
as

\mathcal{H}_{{\mathbf{k}}n}^{(\ell)}\equiv\frac{W^{(\ell)}}{\ell!}\sum_{m=n}^{N_{\ell}}a_{mn}^{(\ell)}P_{\mathbf{k}m}^{(\ell)}\;,\quad P_{\mathbf{k}m}^{(\ell)}\equiv\sum_{r=0}^{m}a_{mr}^{(\ell)}E_{\mathbf{k}}^{r}\;.

(22)

The negative-order moments
\rho_{r<0}^{\mu_{1}\cdots\mu_{\ell}}
are not included in the expansion (
21
)
but they are expressed by a linear combination of positive-order moments through

\rho_{-r}^{\mu_{1}\cdots\mu_{\ell}}=\sum_{n=0}^{N_{\ell}}\mathcal{F}_{rn}^{(\ell)}\rho_{n}^{\mu_{1}\cdots\mu_{\ell}}\;,

(23)

where we defined

\mathcal{F}_{rn}^{(\ell)}\equiv\frac{\ell!}{(2\ell+1)!!}\int\mathrm{d}KE_{\mathbf{k}}^{-r}\left(\Delta^{\alpha\beta}k_{\alpha}k_{\beta}\right)^{\ell}\mathcal{H}_{\mathbf{k}n}^{(\ell)}f_{0\mathbf{k}}\tilde{f}_{0\mathbf{k}}\;,

(24)

such that
\mathcal{F}_{-r,n}^{(\ell)}=\delta_{rn}
by construction.

The coefficients
a_{mn}^{(\ell)}
are obtained via the Gram-Schmidt orthogonalization procedure
by imposing the following condition,

\int\mathrm{d}K\,\omega^{(\ell)}P_{\mathbf{k}m}^{(\ell)}P_{\mathbf{k}n}^{(\ell)}=\delta_{mn}\;,

(25)

where the weight
\omega^{(\ell)}
is defined as

\omega^{(\ell)}\equiv\frac{W^{(\ell)}}{(2\ell+1)!!}\left(\Delta^{\alpha\beta}k_{\alpha}k_{\beta}\right)^{\ell}f_{0\mathbf{k}}\tilde{f}_{0\mathbf{k}}\;,

(26)

while the normalization parameter
W^{(\ell)}
is fixed according to

P_{\mathbf{k}0}^{(\ell)}\equiv a_{00}^{(\ell)}=1
, leading to

W^{(\ell)}=\frac{(-1)^{\ell}}{J_{2\ell,\ell}}\;.

(27)

Here the thermodynamic integrals are defined as

\displaystyle I_{nq}(\alpha,\beta)

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{(-1)^{q}}{(2q+1)!!}\int\mathrm{d}KE^{n-2q}_{\mathbf{k}}\left(\Delta^{\alpha\beta}k_{\alpha}k_{\beta}\right)^{q}f_{0\mathbf{k}}\;,

(28)

\displaystyle J_{nq}(\alpha,\beta)

\displaystyle\equiv\left(\frac{\partial I_{nq}(\alpha,\beta)}{\partial\alpha}\right)_{\beta}\;,

(29)

where
(2q+1)!!=(2q+1)!/(2^{q}q!)
is the double factorial of odd integers.
For classical particles,
a=0
and
J_{nq}(\alpha,\beta)=I_{nq}(\alpha,\beta)
.
Using these integrals, the particle number density, the energy density, and the isotropic pressure are
n_{0}=I_{10}
,
e_{0}=I_{20}
, and
P_{0}=I_{21}
.

The coefficients
\alpha^{(\ell)}_{r}(\alpha,\beta)
in Eqs. (
9
–
11
) are

\displaystyle\alpha_{r}^{(0)}

\displaystyle\equiv-\beta J_{r+1,1}-\frac{n_{0}}{D_{20}}\left(h_{0}G_{2r}-G_{3r}\right)\;,

(30)

\displaystyle\alpha_{r}^{(1)}

\displaystyle\equiv J_{r+1,1}-\frac{J_{r+2,1}}{h_{0}}\;,

(31)

\displaystyle\alpha_{r}^{(2)}

\displaystyle\equiv\beta J_{r+3,2}\;,

(32)

where
h_{0}\equiv\left(e_{0}+P_{0}\right)/n_{0}
is the enthalpy per particle and

\displaystyle G_{nm}

\displaystyle\equiv J_{n0}J_{m0}-J_{n-1,0}J_{m+1,0}\;,

(33)

\displaystyle D_{nq}

\displaystyle\equiv J_{n+1,q}J_{n-1,q}-J_{nq}^{2}\;.

(34)

Note that the thermodynamic integrals introduced in this subsection are computed explicitly for the case of ultrarelativistic particles in Appendix
A
.

II.2
The linearized collision integral and power counting methods

Substituting the near-equilibrium distribution function from Eq. (
7
) into the binary collision term (
2
) and
using the identity
f_{0\mathbf{p}}f_{0\mathbf{p}^{\prime}}\tilde{f}_{0\mathbf{k}}\tilde{f}_{0\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}=f_{0\mathbf{k}}f_{0\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}\tilde{f}_{0\mathbf{p}}\tilde{f}_{0\mathbf{p}^{\prime}}
while neglecting quadratic terms in
\delta f_{\mathbf{k}}
, the irreducible moments of the linearized collision integral become

\displaystyle\hskip-7.0ptC_{r}^{\left\langle\mu_{1}\cdots\mu_{\ell}\right\rangle}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int\mathrm{d}K\mathrm{d}K^{\prime}\mathrm{d}P\mathrm{d}P^{\prime}\,W_{\mathbf{kk}^{\prime}\rightarrow\mathbf{pp}^{\prime}}\,f_{0\mathbf{k}}f_{0\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}\tilde{f}_{0\mathbf{p}}\tilde{f}_{0\mathbf{p}^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\times E_{\mathbf{k}}^{r}k^{\left\langle\mu_{1}\right.}\cdots k^{\left.\mu_{\ell}\right\rangle}\left(\phi_{\mathbf{p}}+\phi_{\mathbf{p}^{\prime}}-\phi_{\mathbf{k}}-\phi_{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}\right)\;.

(35)

Now, inserting the expansion from Eq. (
21
) into the above formula,
the moments of the collision integral can be expressed in terms of a linear
combination of irreducible moments from Eq. (
8
) as

C_{r-1}^{\langle\mu_{1}\cdots\mu_{\ell}\rangle}=-\sum_{n=0}^{N_{\ell}}\mathcal{A}_{rn}^{\left(\ell\right)}\rho_{n}^{\mu_{1}\cdots\mu_{\ell}}\;.

(36)

The matrix
\mathcal{A}_{rn}^{\left(\ell\right)}
is defined as
Denicol et al. 2012

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}^{(\ell)}_{rn}

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{1}{2(2\ell+1)}\int\mathrm{d}K\mathrm{d}K^{\prime}\mathrm{d}P\mathrm{d}P^{\prime}\,W_{\mathbf{kk}^{\prime}\rightarrow\mathbf{pp}^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\times f_{0\mathbf{k}}f_{0\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}\tilde{f}_{0\mathbf{p}}\tilde{f}_{0\mathbf{p}^{\prime}}E_{\mathbf{k}}^{r-1}k^{\left\langle\mu_{1}\right.}\cdots k^{\left.\mu_{\ell}\right\rangle}

\displaystyle\times\left(\mathcal{H}_{\mathbf{k}n}^{(\ell)}k_{\left\langle\mu_{1}\right.}\cdots k_{\left.\mu_{\ell}\right\rangle}+\mathcal{H}_{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}n}^{(\ell)}k^{\prime}_{\left\langle\mu_{1}\right.}\cdots k^{\prime}_{\left.\mu_{\ell}\right\rangle}\right.

\displaystyle\quad\left.-\mathcal{H}_{\mathbf{p}n}^{(\ell)}p_{\left\langle\mu_{1}\right.}\cdots p_{\left.\mu_{\ell}\right\rangle}-\mathcal{H}_{\mathbf{p}^{\prime}n}^{(\ell)}p^{\prime}_{\left\langle\mu_{1}\right.}\cdots p^{\prime}_{\left.\mu_{\ell}\right\rangle}\right)\;,

(37)

and it is separated in loss and gain parts as

\mathcal{A}_{rn}^{\left(\ell\right)}=\mathcal{A}_{rn}^{\left(\ell\right),{\rm l}}-\mathcal{A}_{rn}^{\left(\ell\right),{\rm g}}\;.

(38)

Now, using Eq. (
22
) to express
\mathcal{H}^{(\ell)}_{{\mathbf{k}}n}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{rn}^{(\ell),{\rm l}}

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{W^{(\ell)}}{\ell!}\sum_{m=n}^{N_{\ell}}\sum_{q=0}^{m}a^{(\ell)}_{mn}a^{(\ell)}_{mq}\mathcal{L}^{(\ell)}_{r-1,q}\;,

(39)

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{rn}^{(\ell),{\rm g}}

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{W^{(\ell)}}{\ell!}\sum_{m=n}^{N_{\ell}}\sum_{q=0}^{m}a^{(\ell)}_{mn}a^{(\ell)}_{mq}\mathcal{G}^{(\ell)}_{r-1,q}\;,

(40)

where the corresponding summands
\mathcal{L}_{rn}^{(\ell)}
and
\mathcal{G}_{rn}^{(\ell)}
are given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{rn}^{(\ell)}

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{1}{2(2\ell+1)}\int\mathrm{d}P\mathrm{d}P^{\prime}\mathrm{d}K\mathrm{d}K^{\prime}\,W_{\mathbf{kk}^{\prime}\rightarrow\mathbf{pp}^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\times f_{0\mathbf{k}}f_{0\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}\tilde{f}_{0\mathbf{p}}\tilde{f}_{0\mathbf{p}^{\prime}}E_{\mathbf{k}}^{r}k^{\langle\mu_{1}}\cdots k^{\mu_{\ell}\rangle}

\displaystyle\times\left(E_{\mathbf{k}}^{n}k_{\langle\mu_{1}}\cdots k_{\mu_{\ell}\rangle}+E_{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}^{n}k^{\prime}_{\langle\mu_{1}}\cdots k^{\prime}_{\mu_{\ell}\rangle}\right),

(41)

and

\displaystyle\mathcal{\mathcal{G}}_{rn}^{(\ell)}

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{1}{(2\ell+1)}\int\mathrm{d}P\mathrm{d}P^{\prime}\mathrm{d}K\mathrm{d}K^{\prime}\,W_{\mathbf{kk}^{\prime}\rightarrow\mathbf{pp}^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\times f_{0\mathbf{k}}f_{0\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}\tilde{f}_{0\mathbf{p}}\tilde{f}_{0\mathbf{p}^{\prime}}E_{\mathbf{k}}^{r}E_{\mathbf{p}}^{n}k^{\langle\mu_{1}}\cdots k^{\mu_{\ell}\rangle}p_{\langle\mu_{1}}\cdots p_{\mu_{\ell}\rangle}\;,

(42)

respectively.
The computation of these summands and of the collision matrix in the ultrarelativistic limit

m_{0}\beta\rightarrow 0
is one of the main purposes of this paper and will be discussed in Sect.
III
.

The inverse of the collision matrix, the relaxation-time matrix, contains the microscopic
time scales proportional to the mean free time between collisions
\tau_{\rm mfp}=\lambda_{\rm mfp}/c
,

\tau^{(\ell)}_{rn}\equiv\left(\mathcal{A}^{(\ell)}\right)^{-1}_{rn}=\sum_{m=0}^{N_{\ell}}\Omega^{(\ell)}_{rm}\frac{1}{\chi^{(\ell)}_{m}}\left(\Omega^{(\ell)}\right)^{-1}_{mn}\;.

(43)

Here the matrix
\Omega^{(\ell)}
diagonalizes
\mathcal{A}^{(\ell)}
,
leading to eigenvalues that are arranged in
increasing order,
\chi^{(\ell)}_{r}\leq\chi^{(\ell)}_{r+1}
,

\left(\Omega^{(\ell)}\right)^{-1}\mathcal{A}^{(\ell)}\Omega^{(\ell)}={\rm diag}\left(\chi_{0}^{(\ell)},\chi_{1}^{(\ell)},\cdots\right),

(44)

where without loss of generality we set
\Omega^{(\ell)}_{00}=1
.

The diagonalization of the collision term identifies the slowest microscopic
time scale that dominates the evolution of the linearized Boltzmann equation
Denicol et al. 2012
.
However, as discussed in Ref.
Wagner et al. 2022
, the diagonalization procedure is not required for the computation of the inverse collision matrix
\tau^{(\ell)}
, since it can be obtained by directly inverting
\mathcal{A}^{(\ell)}
as apparent in Eq. (
43
).

Using the relaxation-time matrices (
43
) and the
\alpha^{(\ell)}_{r}
coefficients from Eqs. (
30
)–(
32
), the first-order transport coefficients

\zeta_{r}
,
\kappa_{r}
, and
\eta_{r}
, are defined as

\displaystyle\zeta_{r}\equiv\frac{m_{0}^{2}}{3}\sum_{n=0,\neq 1,2}^{N_{0}}\!\tau_{rn}^{(0)}\alpha^{(0)}_{n},

\displaystyle\kappa_{r}\equiv\sum_{n=0,\neq 1}^{N_{1}}\!\tau^{(1)}_{rn}\alpha^{(1)}_{n},\quad\eta_{r}\equiv\sum_{n=0}^{N_{2}}\tau^{(2)}_{rn}\alpha^{(2)}_{n},

(45)

where the exclusions of
n\neq 1,2
and
n\neq 1
from the first and second summations are imposed by the conservation laws (
13
).

The equations of motion for the primary dissipative quantities,
\Pi=-m_{0}^{2}\rho_{0}/3
,

V^{\mu}=\rho^{\mu}_{0}
, and
\pi^{\mu\nu}=\rho^{\mu\nu}_{0}
,
are obtained by performing the matrix multiplication of Eqs. (
9
)–(
11
) with
\tau^{(\ell)}_{nr}
, followed by setting
n=0
.
In these equations, the terms of second order in Knudsen and/or inverse Reynolds numbers also contain irreducible moments
\rho_{r}^{\mu_{1}\cdots\mu_{\ell}}
with
r\neq 0
, which need to be specified,
while moments with tensor rank
\ell>2
are omitted in what follows.

Following the DNMR method
Denicol et al. 2012
, the irreducible moments for
0<r\leq N_{\ell}
are approximated by their asymptotic solutions as

\displaystyle\rho_{r>0}

\displaystyle\simeq-\frac{3}{m_{0}^{2}}\Omega^{(0)}_{r0}\Pi+\frac{3}{m_{0}^{2}}(\zeta_{r}-\Omega^{(0)}_{r0}\zeta_{0})\theta\;,

(46a)

\displaystyle\rho^{\mu}_{r>0}

\displaystyle\simeq\Omega^{(1)}_{r0}V^{\mu}+(\kappa_{r}-\Omega^{(1)}_{r0}\kappa_{0})\nabla^{\mu}\alpha\;,

(46b)

\displaystyle\rho^{\mu\nu}_{r>0}

\displaystyle\simeq\Omega^{(2)}_{r0}\pi^{\mu\nu}+2(\eta_{r}-\Omega^{(2)}_{r0}\eta_{0})\sigma^{\mu\nu}\;.

(46c)

The remaining moments of negative order
\rho^{\mu_{1}\cdots\mu_{\ell}}_{-r}
are obtained
by substituting only the first terms,
and hence neglecting terms of order
O(\textrm{Kn})
, from the right-hand sides of Eqs. (
46a
)–(
46c
)
into Eq. (
23
), leading to

\displaystyle\rho_{-r}

\displaystyle\simeq-\frac{3}{m_{0}^{2}}\gamma^{(0)}_{r0}\Pi+\frac{3}{m_{0}^{2}}(\Gamma^{(0)}_{r0}-\gamma^{(0)}_{r0})\zeta_{0}\theta\;,

(47a)

\displaystyle\rho_{-r}^{\mu}

\displaystyle\simeq\gamma^{(1)}_{r0}V^{\mu}+(\Gamma^{(1)}_{r0}-\gamma^{(1)}_{r0})\kappa_{0}\nabla^{\mu}\alpha\;,

(47b)

\displaystyle\rho_{-r}^{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle\simeq\gamma^{(2)}_{r0}\pi^{\mu\nu}+2(\Gamma^{(2)}_{r0}-\gamma^{(2)}_{r0})\eta_{0}\sigma^{\mu\nu}\;.,

(47c)

where we displayed explicitly both the
O({\rm Re}^{-1})
and the
O({\rm Kn})
contributions.
The DNMR coefficients,
\gamma_{r0}^{(\ell)}
, are

\displaystyle\gamma_{r0}^{(0)}\equiv\sum_{n=0,\neq 1,2}^{N_{0}}\!\mathcal{F}^{(0)}_{rn}\Omega^{(0)}_{n0}\;,

\displaystyle\gamma_{r0}^{(1)}\equiv\sum_{n=0,\neq 1}^{N_{1}}\!\mathcal{F}^{(1)}_{rn}\Omega^{(1)}_{n0}\;,\quad\gamma_{r0}^{(2)}\equiv\sum_{n=0}^{N_{2}}\mathcal{F}^{(2)}_{rn}\Omega^{(2)}_{n0}\;.

(48)

In addition, we introduced the so-called corrected DNMR coefficients,
\Gamma^{(\ell)}_{r0}
, defined as
Wagner et al. 2022
;
Ambrus et al. 2022

\displaystyle\Gamma^{(0)}_{r0}\equiv\sum_{n=0,\neq 1,2}^{N_{0}}\mathcal{F}^{(0)}_{rn}\frac{\zeta_{n}}{\zeta_{0}}\;,

\displaystyle\Gamma^{(1)}_{r0}\equiv\sum_{n=0,\neq 1}^{N_{1}}\mathcal{F}^{(1)}_{rn}\frac{\kappa_{n}}{\kappa_{0}}\;,\quad\Gamma^{(2)}_{r0}\equiv\sum_{n=0}^{N_{2}}\mathcal{F}^{(2)}_{rn}\frac{\eta_{n}}{\eta_{0}}\;.

(49)

On the other hand, in the cDNMR approach, the thermodynamic forces can be replaced by the Navier-Stokes relations,

\theta=-\Pi/\zeta_{0}
,
\nabla^{\mu}\alpha=V^{\mu}/\kappa_{0}
,
and
\sigma^{\mu\nu}=\pi^{\mu\nu}/(2\eta_{0})
. Therefore substituting the right-hand sides of Eqs. (
47
) eliminates the
O({\rm Kn})
contributions to the negative-order moments
\rho^{\mu_{1}\cdots\mu_{\ell}}_{r<0}
and yields
Wagner et al. 2022
;
Ambrus et al. 2022

\rho_{-r}\simeq-\frac{3}{m_{0}^{2}}\Gamma^{(0)}_{r0}\Pi\;,\ \rho_{-r}^{\mu}\simeq\Gamma^{(1)}_{r0}V^{\mu}\;,\ \rho_{-r}^{\mu\nu}\simeq\Gamma^{(2)}_{r0}\pi^{\mu\nu}\;.

(50)

Finally, the so-called Inverse-Reynolds Dominance (IReD) approximation of Ref.
Wagner et al. 2022

defines a power counting scheme without the diagonalization procedure, such that the irreducible moments are of order
O({\rm Re}^{-1})
.
The non-dynamical positive-order moments are given by

\rho_{r>0}\simeq-\frac{3}{m_{0}^{2}}\mathcal{C}^{(0)}_{r0}\Pi\;,\ \rho^{\mu}_{r>0}\simeq\mathcal{C}^{(1)}_{r0}V^{\mu}\;,\ \rho^{\mu\nu}_{r>0}\simeq\mathcal{C}^{(2)}_{r0}\pi^{\mu\nu}\;,

(51)

where the corresponding IReD coefficients,
\mathcal{C}^{(\ell)}_{r0}
, are

\mathcal{C}^{(0)}_{r0}\equiv\frac{\zeta_{r}}{\zeta_{0}}\;,\quad\mathcal{C}^{(1)}_{r0}\equiv\frac{\kappa_{r}}{\kappa_{0}}\;,\quad\mathcal{C}^{(2)}_{r0}\equiv\frac{\eta_{r}}{\eta_{0}}\;,

(52)

while the negative-order moments are given by Eqs. (
50
).

To simplify our notation we will introduce a common variable,
\xi^{(\ell)}_{r}
, for the transport coefficients (
45
) in what follows,

\xi^{(0)}_{r}=\zeta_{r}\;,\quad\xi^{(1)}_{r}=\kappa_{r}\;,\quad\xi^{(2)}_{r}=\eta_{r}\;.

(53)

To study the different power-counting schemes, we introduce the following notation for the non-dynamical moments encompassing the DNMR, the cDNMR, and the IReD approximations,

\displaystyle\rho_{r}

\displaystyle=-\frac{3}{m^{2}_{0}}\mathcal{X}^{(0)}_{r0}\Pi+\frac{3}{m^{2}_{0}}\mathcal{Y}^{(0)}_{r0}\theta\;,

(54)

\displaystyle\rho_{r}^{\mu}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{X}^{(1)}_{r0}V^{\mu}+\mathcal{Y}^{(1)}_{r0}\nabla^{\mu}\alpha\;,

(55)

\displaystyle\rho_{r}^{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{X}^{(2)}_{r0}\pi^{\mu\nu}+2\mathcal{Y}^{(2)}_{r0}\sigma^{\mu\nu}\;.

(56)

Here, for
r=0
, in all cases, by definition,

\mathcal{X}^{(\ell)}_{00}=\Omega^{(\ell)}_{00}=\mathcal{C}^{(\ell)}_{00}=1\;,\;\;\;\;\mathcal{Y}^{(\ell)}_{00}=0\;.

(57)

For
r\neq 0
, the DNMR coefficients are

\displaystyle\mathcal{X}^{(\ell)}_{r0}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\Omega^{(\ell)}_{r0}\;,\;\;&r>0\;,\\
\gamma^{(\ell)}_{-r,0}\;,\;\;&r<0\;,\end{cases}

(58)

and

\displaystyle\mathcal{Y}^{(\ell)}_{r0}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\xi^{(\ell)}_{r}-\Omega^{(\ell)}_{r0}\xi^{(\ell)}_{0}\;,&r>0\;,\\
\left(\Gamma^{(\ell)}_{-r,0}-\gamma^{(\ell)}_{-r,0}\right)\xi^{(\ell)}_{0}\;,&r<0\;,\end{cases}

(59)

as follows from Eqs. (
46
) and (
47
).

Similarly, the cDNMR coefficients are

\displaystyle\mathcal{X}^{(\ell)}_{r0}=\begin{cases}\Omega^{(\ell)}_{r0}\;,\;\;&r>0\;,\\
\Gamma^{(\ell)}_{-r,0}\;,\;\;&r<0\;,\end{cases}

(60)

and

\displaystyle\mathcal{Y}^{(\ell)}_{r0}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\xi^{(\ell)}_{r}-\Omega^{(\ell)}_{r0}\xi^{(\ell)}_{0}\;,&r>0\;,\\
0\;,&r<0\;,\end{cases}

(61)

as it is apparent from Eqs. (
46
) and (
50
).

Finally, the IReD coefficients can be identified from Eqs. (
50
) and (
51
):

\displaystyle\mathcal{X}^{(\ell)}_{r0}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\mathcal{C}^{(\ell)}_{r0}\;,\;\;&r>0\;,\\
\Gamma^{(\ell)}_{-r,0}\;,\;\;&r<0\;,\end{cases}

(62)

while, by definition,

\mathcal{Y}^{(\ell)}_{r0}=0\;,\;\;\;\;r\neq 0\;.

(63)

Note that the following relation holds for all of the approaches:

\displaystyle\xi^{(\ell)}_{0}\mathcal{X}^{(\ell)}_{r0}+\mathcal{Y}^{(\ell)}_{r0}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\xi^{(\ell)}_{r}\;,\;\;&r\geq 0\;,\\
\xi^{(\ell)}_{0}\Gamma^{(\ell)}_{-r,0}\;,\;\;&r<0\;.\end{cases}

(64)

II.3
Second-order fluid dynamical equations

The relaxation equations for the irreducible moments
are obtained by multiplying Eqs. (
9
)–(
11
) by
\tau^{(\ell)}_{nr}
and then
summing over
r
.
Employing the expression

\sum^{N_{\ell}}_{r=0}\tau^{(\ell)}_{nr}C_{r-1}^{\langle\mu_{1}\cdots\mu_{\ell}\rangle}=-\rho_{n}^{\langle\mu_{1}\cdots\mu_{\ell}\rangle}\;,

(65)

valid for the linearized collision model (
35
)–(
36
) and derived using the property

\sum_{r=0}^{N_{\ell}}\tau^{(\ell)}_{nr}\mathcal{A}^{(\ell)}_{rm}=\delta_{nm}\;,

(66)

the second-order transport equations with a linearized collision integral for

\Pi
,
V^{\mu}
, and
\pi^{\mu\nu}
from Ref.
Denicol et al. 2012
read

\displaystyle\tau_{\Pi}\dot{\Pi}+\Pi

\displaystyle=-\zeta\theta+\mathcal{J}+\mathcal{K}\;,

(67)

\displaystyle\tau_{V}\dot{V}^{\langle\mu\rangle}+V^{\mu}

\displaystyle=\kappa\nabla^{\mu}\alpha+\mathcal{J}^{\mu}+\mathcal{K}^{\mu}\;,

(68)

\displaystyle\tau_{\pi}\dot{\pi}^{\langle\mu\nu\rangle}+\pi^{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=2\eta\sigma^{\mu\nu}+\mathcal{J}^{\mu\nu}+\mathcal{K}^{\mu\nu}\;.

(69)

Here,
\tau_{\Pi}
,
\tau_{V}
, and
\tau_{\pi}
are the relaxation
times, while
\zeta=\zeta_{0}
,
\kappa=\kappa_{0}
, and
\eta=\eta_{0}

are the first-order transport coefficients,

\displaystyle\zeta

\displaystyle=\frac{m_{0}^{2}}{3}\!\sum_{r=0,\neq 1,2}^{N_{0}}\!\tau_{0r}^{(0)}\alpha^{(0)}_{r}\;,\quad

\displaystyle\tau_{\Pi}=\sum_{r=0,\neq 1,2}^{N_{0}}\!\tau_{0r}^{(0)}\mathcal{X}_{r0}^{(0)}\;,

(70)

\displaystyle\kappa

\displaystyle=\sum_{r=0,\neq 1}^{N_{1}}\!\tau_{0r}^{(1)}\alpha^{(1)}_{r}\;,\quad

\displaystyle\tau_{V}=\sum_{r=0,\neq 1}^{N_{1}}\!\tau_{0r}^{(1)}\mathcal{X}_{r0}^{(1)}\;,

(71)

\displaystyle\eta

\displaystyle=\sum_{r=0}^{N_{2}}\tau_{0r}^{(2)}\alpha^{(2)}_{r}\;,\quad

\displaystyle\tau_{\pi}=\sum_{r=0}^{N_{2}}\tau_{0r}^{(2)}\mathcal{X}_{r0}^{(2)}\;.

(72)

Furthermore,
\mathcal{J},\mathcal{J}^{\mu}
, and
\mathcal{J}^{\mu\nu}

collect the terms of order
O({\rm Re}^{-1}\rm{Kn})
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}

\displaystyle=-\ell_{\Pi V}\nabla_{\mu}V^{\mu}-\tau_{\Pi V}V_{\mu}\dot{u}^{\mu}-\delta_{\Pi\Pi}\Pi\theta

\displaystyle-\lambda_{\Pi V}V_{\mu}\nabla^{\mu}\alpha+\lambda_{\Pi\pi}\pi^{\mu\nu}\sigma_{\mu\nu}\;,

(73)

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}^{\mu}

\displaystyle=-\tau_{V}V_{\nu}\omega^{\nu\mu}-\delta_{VV}V^{\mu}\theta-\ell_{V\Pi}\nabla^{\mu}\Pi

\displaystyle+\ell_{V\pi}\Delta^{\mu\nu}\nabla_{\lambda}\pi^{\lambda}{}_{\nu}+\tau_{V\Pi}\Pi\dot{u}^{\mu}-\tau_{V\pi}\pi^{\mu\nu}\dot{u}_{\nu}

\displaystyle-\lambda_{VV}V_{\nu}\sigma^{\mu\nu}+\lambda_{V\Pi}\Pi\nabla^{\mu}\alpha-\lambda_{V\pi}\pi^{\mu\nu}\nabla_{\nu}\alpha\;,

(74)

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}^{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=2\tau_{\pi}\pi^{\langle\mu}_{\lambda}\omega^{\nu\rangle\lambda}-\delta_{\pi\pi}\pi^{\mu\nu}\theta-\tau_{\pi\pi}\pi^{\lambda\langle\mu}\sigma^{\nu\rangle}_{\lambda}+\lambda_{\pi\Pi}\Pi\sigma^{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle-\tau_{\pi V}V^{\langle\mu}\dot{u}^{\nu\rangle}+\ell_{\pi V}\nabla^{\langle\mu}V^{\nu\rangle}+\lambda_{\pi V}V^{\langle\mu}\nabla^{\nu\rangle}\alpha\;.

(75)

Finally, the tensors
\mathcal{K}
,
\mathcal{K}^{\mu}
, and

\mathcal{K}^{\mu\nu}
contain all contributions of order
O({\rm Kn}^{2})
, given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}

\displaystyle=\widetilde{\zeta}_{1}\omega_{\mu\nu}\omega^{\mu\nu}+\widetilde{\zeta}_{2}\sigma_{\mu\nu}\sigma^{\mu\nu}+\widetilde{\zeta}_{3}\theta^{2}+\widetilde{\zeta}_{4}I^{\mu}I_{\mu}

\displaystyle+\widetilde{\zeta}_{5}\dot{u}^{\mu}\dot{u}_{\mu}+\widetilde{\zeta}_{6}I^{\mu}\dot{u}_{\mu}+\widetilde{\zeta}_{7}\nabla^{\mu}I_{\mu}+\widetilde{\zeta}_{8}\nabla^{\mu}\dot{u}_{\mu}\;,

(76)

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}^{\mu}

\displaystyle=\widetilde{\kappa}_{1}\sigma^{\mu\nu}I_{\nu}+\widetilde{\kappa}_{2}\sigma^{\mu\nu}\dot{u}_{\nu}+\widetilde{\kappa}_{3}I^{\mu}\theta+\widetilde{\kappa}_{4}\dot{u}^{\mu}\theta

\displaystyle+\widetilde{\kappa}_{5}\omega^{\mu\nu}I_{\nu}+\widetilde{\kappa}_{6}\Delta^{\mu}_{\lambda}\nabla_{\nu}\sigma^{\lambda\nu}+\widetilde{\kappa}_{7}\nabla^{\mu}\theta\;,

(77)

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}^{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=\widetilde{\eta}_{1}\omega^{\lambda\langle\mu}\omega^{\nu\rangle}{}_{\lambda}+\widetilde{\eta}_{2}\theta\sigma^{\mu\nu}+\widetilde{\eta}_{3}\sigma^{\lambda\langle\mu}\sigma^{\nu\rangle}_{\lambda}+\widetilde{\eta}_{4}\sigma^{\langle\mu}_{\lambda}\omega^{\nu\rangle\lambda}

\displaystyle+\widetilde{\eta}_{5}I^{\langle\mu}I^{\nu\rangle}+\widetilde{\eta}_{6}\dot{u}^{\langle\mu}\dot{u}^{\nu\rangle}+\widetilde{\eta}_{7}I^{\langle\mu}\dot{u}^{\nu\rangle}+\widetilde{\eta}_{8}\nabla^{\langle\mu}I^{\nu\rangle}

\displaystyle+\widetilde{\eta}_{9}\nabla^{\langle\mu}\dot{u}^{\nu\rangle}\;,

(78)

where
I^{\mu}=\nabla^{\mu}\alpha
was introduced.

Note that all coefficients appearing in Eqs. (
73
)–(
75
) and Eqs. (
76
)–(
78
) are calculated using the
\mathcal{X}^{(\ell)}_{r0}
and
\mathcal{Y}^{(\ell)}_{r0}
notation in Appendix
B
.

\displaystyle\times k^{\langle\mu_{1}}\cdots k^{\mu_{\ell}\rangle}\left(E_{\mathbf{k}}^{n}k_{\langle\mu_{1}}\cdots k_{\mu_{\ell}\rangle}+E_{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}^{n}k^{\prime}_{\langle\mu_{1}}\cdots k^{\prime}_{\mu_{\ell}\rangle}\right)\;,

and

\displaystyle\times\delta\left(k^{\mu}+k^{\prime\mu}-p^{\mu}-p^{\prime\mu}\right)k^{\langle\mu_{1}}\cdots k^{\mu_{\ell}\rangle}p_{\langle\mu_{1}}\cdots p_{\mu_{\ell}\rangle}\;.

III.3
The collision matrices

Collecting the results from the previous subsections, we can write down closed-form expressions for the elements
\mathcal{A}^{(\ell)}_{rn}
of the collision matrices.
In the case when
\ell=0
, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}^{(0)}_{00}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\lambda_{\text{mfp}}}\frac{N_{0}-1}{N_{0}+1}\;,

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}^{(0)}_{0,n>0}

\displaystyle=\frac{2(-\beta)^{n}}{\lambda_{\rm mfp}(n+1)!}\left[S^{(0)}_{n}(N_{0})-\frac{\delta_{n1}}{2}\right]\;,

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}^{(0)}_{r>0,n\leq r}

\displaystyle=\frac{\beta^{n-r}(r+1)!}{\lambda_{\rm mfp}(n+1)!}\left(\delta_{nr}+\frac{2}{r}\delta_{n0}+\delta_{n1}-\frac{2}{r}\right)\;,

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{r>0,n>r}^{(0)}

(88)

Note that
\mathcal{A}_{1n}^{(0)}=\mathcal{A}_{2n}^{(0)}=0
, since the particle number and energy are conserved, while
\mathcal{A}^{(0)}_{r>0,0}=0

Similarly, when
\ell=1

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}^{(1)}_{0n}

\displaystyle=\frac{16(-\beta)^{n}}{\lambda_{\rm mfp}(n+3)!}\left[S^{(1)}_{n}(N_{1})-\frac{\delta_{n0}}{2}\right],

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{r>0,n\leq r}^{(1)}

\displaystyle=\frac{\beta^{n-r}(r+2)!}{\lambda_{\rm mfp}(n+3)!r}\left(4n+nr-r\right)

\displaystyle\times\left(\delta_{nr}+\delta_{n0}-\frac{2}{r+1}\right),

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{r>0,n>r}^{(1)}

\displaystyle=0,

(89)

where
\mathcal{A}_{1n}^{(1)}=0
reflects the momentum conservation in binary collisions.

Summarizing the results for
\ell=2
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}^{(2)}_{0n}

\displaystyle=\frac{432(-\beta)^{n}}{\lambda_{\rm mfp}(n+5)!}S^{(2)}_{n}(N_{2})\;,

\rho_{0}^{\mu}

\rho_{0}^{\mu\nu}
, couple to all moments of the same tensor-rank, which was also a conclusion found in

Denicol and Noronha 2022
in the case of the
\lambda\varphi^{4}
-theory.

IV
Second-order transport coefficients

In this section, we compute all second-order transport coefficients from
Eqs. (
73
)–(
78
) in the ultrarelativistic limit.
The general expressions of these coefficients for arbitrary particle mass and various power-counting schemes are listed in Appendix
B
.
All second-order transport coefficients are related to the inverse of the collision matrices
\tau^{(\ell)}_{rn}
, for which we obtained analytical expression only in the scalar case when
\ell=0
.
For
\ell=1
and
2
, we employed numerical computations to find the inverse of the collision matrices
given in Eqs. (
89
) and (
90
).

The numerical values were obtained through an extrapolation with respect to
1/N_{2}
by computing the best fit parameters
a
,
b
, and
{\mathfrak{t}}_{\infty}
of the power law
{\mathfrak{t}}(N_{2})={\mathfrak{t}}_{\infty}+aN_{2}^{-b}
, where
{\mathfrak{t}}
denotes a generic transport coefficient with convergence value
{\mathfrak{t}}_{\infty}
. The fits are done on data points up to

N_{2}=1000
through
gnuplot
scripts that are included in the supplementary material to this paper.
All transport coefficients are listed to five significant digits, which is justified by the asymptotic standard deviation of the fit being of order
O(10^{-6})
or lower.
We remark that the coefficients do not converge at the same speed. Specifically, we can estimate the values of
N_{2}
for fixed relative differences between all transport coefficients and their respective convergence values as
N_{2}[O(10^{-4})]\simeq 16491
,
N_{2}[O(10^{-5})]\simeq 168329
, and
N_{2}[O(10^{-6})]\simeq 1718186
, respectively. These large numbers can be attributed mainly to the slow convergence of the coefficient
\zeta_{4}
in the cDNMR approach. For contrast, IReD leads to
N_{2}[O(10^{-4})]\simeq 171
,
N_{2}[O(10^{-5})]\simeq 554
, and
N_{2}[O(10^{-6})]\simeq 1803
.

For the validation of our numerical computations against analytically solvable models, we verified that our computations reproduce the results of Ref.
Ambrus et al. 2022
, where all transport coefficients were computed in the well-known relaxation-time approximation of Anderson and Witting
Anderson and Witting 1974
.

In the following, all transport coefficients are computed involving the general power-counting scheme, in terms of
\mathcal{X}^{(\ell)}_{r0}
and
\mathcal{Y}^{(\ell)}_{r0}
.
Henceforth as in Sect.
II.2
, we will report results for three power-counting schemes: DNMR, the corrected DNMR, and IReD.
Differences between the DNMR and cDNMR methods appear only for the transport coefficients involving the functions
\mathcal{X}^{(\ell)}_{r0}
with
r<0
, or the functions
\mathcal{Y}^{(\ell)}_{r0}
.
Conversely, the cDNMR and the IReD methods show discrepancies only for the coefficients involving
\mathcal{X}^{(\ell)}_{r0}
and
\mathcal{Y}^{(\ell)}_{r0}
with
r>0
.

Furthermore, in order to assess the magnitude of the higher-order corrections originating from the irreducible moments
\rho_{r}^{\mu_{1}\cdots\mu_{\ell}}
with
\ell\leq 2
and
r\neq 0
, we also list the values for the transport coefficients appearing in the
\mathcal{J}^{\mu_{1}\cdots\mu_{\ell}}
-terms for the lowest-possible truncation order of 14 dynamical moments (14M) contained in
N^{\mu}
and
T^{\mu\nu}
,
i.e.,
N_{0}=2
,
N_{1}=1
, and
N_{2}=0
.

The computation of the transport coefficients is done via a
Mathematica
notebook, which can be found in the supplementary material to this article.

Since the bulk viscous pressure
\Pi
vanishes in the ultrarelativistic limit, only the coefficients which are unrelated to it are computed in Sect.
IV.2
.
The remaining second-order coefficients involving the bulk viscosity are expanded up to leading order with respect to the particle mass
m_{0}
in Sect.
IV.3
.

Finally, Sect.
IV.4
ends with a discussion about the possible combinations
of transport coefficients that remain invariant under the reshuffling between the
{\rm Kn}^{2}

and
{\rm Re}^{-1}{\rm Kn}
terms, as also considered in Ref.
Wagner et al. 2022
.

IV.1
Thermodynamic functions in the massless limit

In this section we present the various thermodynamic functions necessary for the computation of
the transport coefficients. Since
n_{0}\sim\beta^{-3}
and
P_{0}\sim\beta^{-4}
, it follows that

\mathcal{C}^{(\ell)}_{r0}\sim\beta^{-r}\;,\quad\Omega^{(\ell)}_{r0}\sim\beta^{-r}\;,\quad\mathcal{X}^{(\ell)}_{r0}\sim\beta^{-r}\;,

(91)

while the mean free path
\lambda_{\rm mfp}=1/\sigma_{T}n_{0}\sim\beta^{3}
, and thus,

\tau^{(\ell)}_{nr}\sim\lambda_{\rm mfp}\beta^{n-r}\sim\beta^{3+n-r}\;.

(92)

The thermodynamic functions
\mathcal{H}
and
\bar{\mathcal{H}}
are given to leading order with respect to
m_{0}
by

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}(\alpha,\beta)

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{n_{0}}{D_{20}}\left(h_{0}J_{20}-J_{30}\right)=m^{2}_{0}\frac{\beta^{2}}{3}\;,

\displaystyle\bar{\mathcal{H}}(\alpha,\beta)

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{n_{0}}{D_{20}}\left(h_{0}J_{10}-J_{20}\right)=\frac{\beta}{3}\;.

(93)

Furthermore,

\displaystyle\frac{G_{2r}}{D_{20}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\beta^{2-r}}{6}(1-r)(r+1)!\;,

(94a)

\displaystyle\frac{G_{3r}}{D_{20}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\beta^{1-r}}{2}(2-r)(r+1)!\;,

(94b)

\displaystyle\frac{\beta J_{r+2,1}}{e_{0}+P_{0}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\beta^{1-r}}{24}(r+3)!\;.

(94c)

These relations can be used to show that
\alpha^{(0)}_{r}
vanishes in the massless limit.
To leading order with respect to
m_{0}
, the
\alpha^{(\ell)}_{r}
coefficients evaluate to

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha^{(0)}_{r}}{m_{0}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\beta^{4-r}P}{36}r!(r-1)(r-2)\;,

(95a)

\displaystyle\alpha^{(1)}_{r}

\displaystyle=\frac{\beta^{1-r}P}{24}(r+2)!(1-r)\;,

(95b)

\displaystyle\alpha^{(2)}_{r}

\displaystyle=\frac{\beta^{-r}P}{30}(r+4)!\;.

(95c)

Note that
\alpha^{(0)}_{1}=\alpha^{(0)}_{2}=\alpha^{(1)}_{1}=0
for arbitrary mass.
We can thus derive the following relations:

\displaystyle\frac{\beta}{m_{0}^{4}}\frac{\partial\zeta_{r}}{\partial\beta}=(3-r)\frac{\zeta_{r}}{m_{0}^{4}}\;,

\displaystyle\beta\frac{\partial\kappa_{r}}{\partial\beta}=-r\kappa_{r}\;,\quad\beta\frac{\partial\eta_{r}}{\partial\beta}=-(r+1)\eta_{r}\;.

(96)

This gives an identical behaviour for
\mathcal{Y}^{(\ell)}_{r0}
:

\displaystyle\frac{\beta}{m_{0}^{4}}\frac{\partial\mathcal{Y}^{(0)}_{r0}}{\partial\beta}=(3-r)\frac{\mathcal{Y}^{(0)}_{r0}}{m_{0}^{4}}\;,

\displaystyle\beta\frac{\partial\mathcal{Y}^{(1)}_{r0}}{\partial\beta}=-r\mathcal{Y}^{(1)}_{r0}\;,\quad\beta\frac{\partial\mathcal{Y}^{(2)}_{r0}}{\partial\beta}=-(r+1)\mathcal{Y}^{(2)}_{r0}\;.

(97)

IV.4
Invariant combinations of transport coefficients

It was already noticed in Ref.
Wagner et al. 2022
that there are various combinations of second-order transport coefficients that stay invariant regardless of the power-counting scheme. To keep the discussion in this section as general as possible, we consider the functions
\mathcal{X}^{(\ell)}_{r0}
to be arbitrary and enforce Eq. (
64
) to determine

\mathcal{Y}^{(\ell)}_{r0}=\xi^{(\ell)}_{0}(\mathcal{C}^{(\ell)}_{r0}-\mathcal{X}^{(\ell)}_{r0})\;,

(107)

where
\mathcal{C}^{(\ell)}_{r>0,0}=\xi^{(\ell)}_{r}/\xi^{(\ell)}_{0}
was introduced in Eq. (
52
) and since
\mathcal{F}_{-r,n}^{(\ell)}=\delta_{rn}
, therefore

\mathcal{C}^{(\ell)}_{r<0,0}=\Gamma^{(\ell)}_{-r,0}
.

The combinations of transport coefficients that are invariant with respect to the power-counting method
are those which have no explicit dependence on the essentially arbitrary functions
\mathcal{X}^{(\ell)}_{r0}
.
In the more general case of massive particles, these combinations are listed in Table II of Ref.
Wagner et al. 2022
. We now identify similar combinations in the case of massless particles.
In order to do so, we compare the expressions for the leading-order contributions of the second-order transport coefficients belonging to terms of order
O(\mathrm{Kn}^{2})
,
i.e., Eqs. (
99
), (
101
), (
103
), and (
105
), to the ones belonging to terms of order
O(\mathrm{Re}^{-1}\mathrm{Kn})
, i.e., Eqs. (
98
), (
100
), (
102
), (
104
), and (
106
) and make use of Eq. (
107
) to eliminate
\mathcal{Y}^{(\ell)}_{r0}
in favor of
\mathcal{X}^{(\ell)}_{r0}
.

For the relaxation times, the invariant combinations are

\tau_{\Pi}+\frac{\widetilde{\zeta}_{1}}{\zeta}\;,\quad\tau_{V}+\frac{\widetilde{\kappa}_{5}}{2\kappa}\;,\quad\tau_{\pi}+\frac{\widetilde{\eta}_{1}}{2\eta}\;,

(108)

while for the second-order coefficients appearing in the equation of motion for
\Pi
, they read

\ell_{\Pi V}-\frac{\widetilde{\zeta}_{7}}{\kappa}\;,\quad\tau_{\Pi V}-\frac{\widetilde{\zeta}_{6}}{\kappa}\;,\quad\lambda_{\Pi V}-\frac{\widetilde{\zeta}_{4}}{\kappa}\;,\quad\frac{\lambda_{\Pi\pi}}{m_{0}^{2}}+\frac{\widetilde{\zeta}_{2}}{2m_{0}^{2}\eta}\;.

(109)

The invariant combinations for the coefficients in the equation for
V^{\mu}
are given by

\displaystyle\frac{\ell_{V\Pi}}{m_{0}^{2}}+\frac{\widetilde{\kappa}_{7}}{m_{0}^{2}\zeta}+\frac{\beta^{2}\widetilde{\kappa}_{5}}{6\zeta}\;,\quad\ell_{V\pi}+\frac{\widetilde{\kappa}_{6}}{2\eta}\;,

\displaystyle m_{0}^{2}\tau_{V\Pi}-m_{0}^{2}\frac{\widetilde{\kappa}_{4}+3\widetilde{\kappa}_{7}}{\zeta}\;,\quad\tau_{V\pi}+\frac{\widetilde{\kappa}_{6}}{2\eta}\;,

\displaystyle\lambda_{VV}+\frac{2\eta}{\kappa}\lambda_{V\pi}-\frac{4\widetilde{\kappa}_{1}-2\widetilde{\kappa}_{5}+\widetilde{\kappa}_{6}}{4\kappa}\;,

(110)

while those contained in the equation for
\pi^{\mu\nu}
read

\tau_{\pi\pi}+\frac{\widetilde{\eta}_{1}-\widetilde{\eta}_{3}}{2\eta}\;,\quad\tau_{\pi V}-\frac{\widetilde{\eta}_{7}}{\kappa}\;,\quad\ell_{\pi V}+\frac{\widetilde{\eta}_{8}}{\kappa}\;,\quad\lambda_{\pi V}+\frac{\widetilde{\eta}_{5}}{\kappa}\;.

(111)

The above relations are in full agreement to the massless limit of the relations in Table II of Ref.
Wagner et al. 2022
, which are valid for arbitrary mass and statistics. Note that, compared to that table, we do not list any relations for
\lambda_{V\Pi}
and
\lambda_{\pi\Pi}
. Establishing such relations within the present framework requires the next-to leading order contributions in
m_{0}^{2}
for the coefficients
\widetilde{\kappa}_{3}
,
\widetilde{\kappa}_{5}
,
\widetilde{\eta}_{1}
and
\widetilde{\eta}_{3}
, which were not considered in this work.

V
Exact results for the scalar sector

In this section we discuss several analytical results derived from the collision matrix
\mathcal{A}^{(0)}_{rn}
for the irreducible scalar moments.
We derive exact results for the inverse matrix
\tau^{(0)}_{rn}
in Sect.
V.1
,
while the first-order bulk viscosity coefficients
\zeta_{r}
are computed in Sect.
V.2
.
The relaxation times of bulk viscosity
\tau_{\Pi;r}
are computed in Sect.
V.3
. Finally, the scalar contribution to the deviation
\delta f_{\mathbf{k}}
from local equilibrium is discussed in Sect.
V.4
.
Note that, since the bulk viscous pressure vanishes in the ultrarelativistic limit,
one has to take appropriate care to derive the leading-order terms in an expansion in
m_{0}\beta
.
The respective calculations are detailed in Appendix
I
.

V.1
The inverse collision matrix

The inverse collision matrix is given by

\displaystyle\tau^{(0)}_{00}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\mathcal{A}^{(0)}_{00}}=\lambda_{\text{mfp}}\frac{N_{0}+1}{N_{0}-1}\;,

\displaystyle\tau^{(0)}_{m>2,2<n\leq m}

\displaystyle=\lambda_{\rm mfp}\beta^{n-m}\frac{(m+1)!(m-1)(m-2)}{(n+1)!(n-1)(n-2)}

\displaystyle\times\left(\delta_{mn}+\frac{2}{m-2}\right)\;,

\displaystyle\tau_{0,n>0}^{(0)}

\displaystyle=-\frac{2\lambda_{\text{mfp}}(-\beta)^{n}}{(n-1)(n-2)(n+1)!}

\displaystyle\times\binom{1+N_{0}}{n}\frac{(1+N_{0}-n)[N_{0}(n-2)+n]}{(N_{0}-1)N_{0}(N_{0}+1)}\;,

\displaystyle\tau^{(0)}_{m>0,0}

\displaystyle=\tau^{(0)}_{m>2,n>m}=0\;,

(112)

which then allows for the computation of the coefficient of bulk viscosity
\zeta_{r}
and
the relaxation times
\tau_{\Pi;r}
.

V.2
Bulk viscosity coefficients

Considering Eqs. (
45
), we can write the coefficients of bulk viscosity as

\frac{3}{m_{0}^{4}}\zeta_{r}=\frac{1}{m_{0}^{2}}\sum_{n=0,\neq 1,2}^{N_{0}}\tau^{(0)}_{rn}\alpha_{n}^{(0)}\;,

(113)

where we divided by
m_{0}^{4}
to obtain the leading-order contribution in the massless limit.
Inserting the results for the inverse matrices
\tau^{(0)}_{rn}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\zeta_{r\geq 3}}{m_{0}^{4}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\lambda_{\rm mfp}P_{0}\beta^{4-r}}{108}(r-1)(r+1)!\left(2H_{r}-\frac{1}{1+r}-\frac{8}{3}\right)\;,

(114)

while
\frac{1}{m_{0}^{4}}\zeta_{1}=\frac{1}{m_{0}^{4}}\zeta_{2}=0
.
In the above,
H_{r}\equiv\sum_{n=1}^{r}n^{-1}
is the
r
-th harmonic number.
A calculation detailed in Appendix
I.2
yields the bulk viscosity as an exact expression in terms of
N_{0}
, namely

\frac{1}{m_{0}^{4}}\zeta=P_{0}\beta^{4}\lambda_{\text{mfp}}\frac{6+7N_{0}+11N_{0}^{3}}{324N_{0}(N_{0}^{2}-1)}\;,

(115)

interpolating between the 14-moment approximation corresponding to
N_{0}=2
and its convergence value when
N_{0}\rightarrow\infty
:

\left.\frac{\zeta}{m_{0}^{4}}\right|_{N_{0}=2}=\frac{P_{0}\beta^{4}\lambda_{\rm mfp}}{18}\;,\;\;\lim_{N_{0}\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\zeta}{m_{0}^{4}}=\frac{11P_{0}\beta^{4}\lambda_{\rm mfp}}{324}\;.

(116)

The second equation above gives the exact value of the leading-order contribution to the bulk viscosity. Their ratio,
\zeta(N_{0}\rightarrow\infty)/\zeta(N_{0}=2)=11/18
shows that including higher-order moments can lead to a decrease of the bulk viscosity of almost
40\%
.

V.3
The relaxation time of the bulk viscous pressure

Depending on which power-counting method is considered, the relaxation times of the bulk viscous pressure take on different values

\displaystyle\mathrm{DNMR\,\&\,cDNMR:}\quad\tau_{\Pi}

\displaystyle\equiv\tau^{(0)}_{00}=\left[\chi^{(0)}_{0}\right]^{-1}\;,

(117)

\displaystyle\mathrm{IReD:}\quad\tau_{\Pi;r}

\displaystyle\equiv\sum_{n=0,\neq 1,2}^{N_{0}}\tau^{(0)}_{rn}\frac{\zeta_{n}}{\zeta_{r}}\;,

(118)

where the eigenvalues
\chi_{r}^{(0)}
are defined in Eq. (
44
). Note that the IReD relaxation time of
the bulk viscous pressure is denoted as
\tau_{\Pi}\equiv\tau_{\Pi;0}
.

Due to the fact that
\mathcal{A}^{(0)}_{r0}=0
for
r>0
, the eigenvalues of the matrix
\mathcal{A}^{(0)}
are given by its diagonal entries.
The eigenvalues are solutions of the equation

0=\det\left[\tau^{(0)}-\chi\mathbb{I}\right]=\prod_{r=0,\neq 1,2}^{N_{0}}\left[\tau^{(0)}_{rr}-\chi\right]\;,

(119)

where the diagonal entries
\tau^{(0)}_{rr}
of the inverse collision matrix are given by

\tau^{(0)}_{00}=\lambda_{\text{mfp}}\frac{N_{0}+1}{N_{0}-1}\;,\quad\tau^{(0)}_{rr}=\lambda_{\text{mfp}}\frac{r}{r-2}\;,

(120)

where we considered
r\geq 3
. In the case when
N_{0}=2
, there is a single eigenvalue equal to
\chi^{(0)}_{0}(N_{0}=2)\equiv 3\lambda_{\rm mfp}
. For
N_{0}>2
, the largest eigenvalue corresponds to
\tau^{(0)}_{rr}
with
r=3
, being equal to
3\lambda_{\rm mfp}
, while

\tau^{(0)}_{00}>\lambda_{\rm mfp}
becomes the lowest eigenvalue.
Rearranging the above expressions in decreasing order gives the set of eigenvalues
[\chi^{(0)}_{r}]^{-1}
as

\left[\chi^{(0)}_{0}\right]^{-1}=3\lambda_{\rm mfp}\;,\quad\left[\chi^{(0)}_{r\geq 3}\right]^{-1}=\lambda_{\rm mfp}\frac{r+1}{r-1}\;.

(121)

Thus, the relaxation time of the bulk viscous pressure in the DNMR and cDNMR approaches becomes independent of
N_{0}\geq 2
and is given by

\tau_{\Pi}=3\lambda_{\text{mfp}}\;.

(122)

Note that the inverse eigenvalues are bounded

\lambda_{\text{mfp}}<\left[\chi_{r}^{(0)}\right]^{-1}\leq 3\lambda_{\text{mfp}}\;,

(123)

while from
\left[\chi_{0}^{(0)}\right]^{-1}=3\lambda_{\text{mfp}}
and
\left[\chi_{3}^{(0)}\right]^{-1}=2\lambda_{\text{mfp}}

we see that there is a clear separation of scales.

A calculation provided in Appendix
I.3
yields the exact result for the relaxation time of the bulk viscous pressure in the IReD approach as a function of
N_{0}
,

\tau_{\Pi}=\lambda_{\rm mfp}\Bigg\{\frac{11+6\pi^{2}}{33}-\frac{12}{11}\psi^{(1)}(N_{0})+\frac{2}{N_{0}-1}\\
+\frac{2}{6+7N_{0}+11N_{0}^{3}}\Big[(N_{0}-2)(3+5N_{0})+\frac{6\pi^{2}}{11}(1+3N_{0})\\
-\frac{32}{11}(1+3N_{0})\psi^{(1)}(N_{0})\Big]\Bigg\}\;.

(124)

When
N_{0}\rightarrow\infty
, we arrive at

\tau_{\Pi}=\lambda_{\rm mfp}\left(\frac{1}{3}+\frac{2\pi^{2}}{11}\right)\simeq 2.13\lambda_{\rm mfp}\;.

(125)

The moments of higher orders on the other hand relax with

\tau_{\Pi;r}=\frac{\lambda_{\rm mfp}}{2H_{r}-\frac{1}{r+1}-\frac{8}{3}}\Bigg[\frac{28r^{2}+33r+11}{9(r^{2}-1)}\\
-\frac{2(r+2)(5r-3)}{3r(r-1)}H_{r}+2H_{r}^{2}+2H_{r,2}\Bigg]\;,

(126)

where
H_{r,m}=\sum_{n=1}^{r}n^{-m}
is the generalized Harmonic number, with
H_{r}\equiv H_{r,1}
. At large
r
, the harmonic numbers
H_{r}
and
H_{r,2}
are given asymptotically as

H_{r}=\ln r+\gamma+O(r^{-1}),\quad H_{r,2}=\frac{\pi^{2}}{6}+O(r^{-1}),

(127)

with
\gamma\simeq 0.577
being the Euler-Mascheroni constant, such that that the highest-order moments relax without bounds for
r\to\infty
as:

\tau_{\Pi;r}=\frac{\lambda_{\rm mfp}(18L^{2}-30L+28)}{6(3L-4)}+O(r^{-1}).

(128)

where we introduced
L=\gamma+\ln r
. It can be seen that
\lim_{r\rightarrow\infty}\tau_{\Pi;r}\simeq\lambda_{\rm mfp}\ln r
, such that higher-order moments relax slower.

VI
Conclusion

In this work, we have analytically computed the linearized collision matrices for an ultrarelativistic gas of hard spheres, and determined the correlation structure of the moment equations.
It was found that the collision matrices feature a nearly lower-triangular structure, coupling the moments of a given order
r>0
to all lower-order ones. On the contrary, the irreducible moments of energy-rank zero, the primary dissipative quantities

\rho_{0}=-3\Pi/m^{2}_{0}
,
\rho_{0}^{\mu}=V^{\mu}
, and
\rho_{0}^{\mu\nu}=\pi^{\mu\nu}
, couple to all higher-order ones included in the basis.

Expressions for all first- and second-order transport coefficients that appear in different formulations of second-order fluid dynamics, i.e., DNMR, cDNMR, and IReD, have been obtained.
The coefficients appearing in the terms of second-order in the Knudsen number are nonvanishing in both the DNMR and cDNMR approaches and we have computed them here for the first time.
Even though they vanish in the strict 14-moment approximation, their convergence values are non-negligible. This is also evidenced by the fact that some of the second-order transport coefficients appearing in the terms of order
O(\mathrm{Kn}\,\mathrm{Re}^{-1})
differ between the various power-counting methods in second-order fluid dynamics.

Furthermore, we obtained a closed-form expressions for the bulk viscosity and the relaxation time for the bulk viscous pressure. Compared to their values in the strict 14-moment approximation, there is a decrease of
39\%
and
29\%
, respectively, as shown in Table
5
.
Also, our results in Table
1
show that the diffusion coefficient
\kappa
increases its value from
1/12\simeq 0.083
in the 14-moment approximation to
0.16
, by almost
100\%
. Even though the shear viscosity
\eta
exhibits only a
5\%
decrease from
4/3
to
1.27
in Table
2
, our results show that the inclusion of higher-order moments leads to sizeable changes for the second-order transport coefficients.
The results reported in our study may serve as a reference for modeling high-energy ultrarelatvistic fluids with binary hard sphere interactions
Karpenko et al. 2014
;
Schenke et al. 2010
;
Schenke et al. 2011
;
Paquet et al. 2016
;
Bouras et al. 2009a
;
Bouras et al. 2009b
;
Bouras et al. 2010
;
Song and Heinz 2008
;
Shen et al. 2016
.
Furthermore, the computation of the collision integrals and corresponding transport coefficients presented here could easily be extended to the relativistic third-order theory of dissipative fluid dynamics introduced recently in
Ref.
de Brito and Denicol 2023
.

Appendix C
Reference frames and projection operators

In order to calculate the collision matrix, it is beneficial to define the total momentum
involved in binary collisions

P^{\mu}_{T}\equiv k^{\mu}+k^{\prime\mu}=p^{\mu}+p^{\prime\mu}\;.

(152)

Its squared norm corresponds to the Mandelstam variable
s\equiv P^{\mu}_{T}P_{T,\mu}
.
The projection operator orthogonal to the total momentum,
i.e.,
\Delta^{\mu\nu}_{T}P_{T,\nu}=0
, is

\Delta^{\mu\nu}_{T}\equiv g^{\mu\nu}-\frac{P^{\mu}_{T}P^{\nu}_{T}}{s}\;.

(153)

Using these definitions the particle momentum can be decomposed with respect to the total
momentum and the corresponding projection operator as

p^{\mu}=P^{\mu}_{T}\frac{(P^{\nu}_{T}p_{\nu})}{s}+\Delta_{T}^{\mu\nu}p_{\nu}\;.

(154)

Furthermore it is useful to define the center-of-momentum (CM) frame where the total momentum is

P^{\mu}_{T}\overset{\mathrm{CM}}{=}\left(\sqrt{s},\mathbf{0}\right)
, such that

\displaystyle P^{0}_{T}

\displaystyle\overset{\mathrm{CM}}{=}k^{0}+k^{\prime 0}=p^{0}+p^{\prime 0}=\sqrt{s}\;,

(155)

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}_{T}

\displaystyle\overset{\mathrm{CM}}{=}\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{k}^{\prime}=\mathbf{p}+\mathbf{p}^{\prime}=\mathbf{0}\;.

(156)

However, in the CM-frame the fluid four-flow vector is

u^{\mu}\overset{{\mathrm{CM}}}{=}(u^{0},\mathbf{u})
, hence it follows that

P_{T}^{\mu}u_{\mu}\overset{{\mathrm{CM}}}{=}\sqrt{s}u^{0}\;,

(157)

while the normalization condition
u^{\mu}u_{\mu}=1
yields

\sqrt{\left(P_{T}^{\mu}u_{\mu}\right)^{2}-s}\overset{{\mathrm{CM}}}{=}\sqrt{s}u\;,

(158)

where we denoted
u\equiv|\mathbf{u}|
.

In the local rest (LR) frame, where
u^{\mu}\overset{{\mathrm{LR}}}{=}(1,\mathbf{0})
, we have
the following representation of the invariant scalars

P_{T}^{\mu}u_{\mu}\overset{{\mathrm{LR}}}{=}k^{0}+k^{\prime 0}=p^{0}+p^{\prime 0}\;,

(159)

and

\sqrt{\left(P_{T}^{\mu}u_{\mu}\right)^{2}-s}\overset{{\mathrm{LR}}}{=}|\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{k}^{\prime}|=|\mathbf{p}+\mathbf{p}^{\prime}|\;.

(160)

In the ultrarelativistic limit,
k^{\mu}k_{\mu}=m^{2}_{0}\rightarrow 0
, and hence

k^{0}=|\mathbf{k}|\equiv k
, while

s\equiv 2k^{\mu}k^{\prime}_{\mu}\overset{\mathrm{LR}}{=}2kk^{\prime}\left(1-\cos\theta_{kk^{\prime}}\right)\;,

(161)

where
\theta_{kk^{\prime}}
is the center-of-mass angle between the colliding particles with momenta
\mathbf{k}
and
\mathbf{k}^{\prime}
in the LR-frame.

Similarly to the projection operator in Eq. (
153
), we introduce another four-vector,
z_{T}^{\mu}
, in the CM-frame that is orthogonal to
P^{\mu}_{T}
, i.e.,
z_{T}^{\mu}P_{T,\mu}=0
,

z_{T}^{\mu}\equiv u^{\mu}-P_{T}^{\mu}\frac{(P^{\nu}_{T}u_{\nu})}{s}\overset{\mathrm{CM}}{=}\left(0,\mathbf{u}\right)\;,

(162)

normalized as
z_{T}^{\mu}z_{T,\mu}\equiv 1-(P_{T}^{\mu}u_{\mu})^{2}/s\overset{\mathrm{CM}}{=}-\mathbf{u}^{2}
.
Hence, using Eq. (
162
) we also obtain that

E_{\mathbf{p}}\equiv u^{\mu}p_{\mu}=z_{T}^{\mu}p_{\mu}+P^{\mu}_{T}u_{\mu}\frac{(P^{\nu}_{T}p_{\nu})}{s}\;.

(163)

The underlying space-like unit vector,
l^{\mu}_{T}\overset{\mathrm{CM}}{=}(0,\mathbf{u}/u)
constructed from
z^{\mu}_{T}
, is also orthogonal to the total momentum,
P^{\mu}_{T}l_{T,\mu}=0
, and it is defined in a covariant fashion as

l^{\mu}_{T}\equiv\frac{z^{\mu}_{T}}{u}=\frac{u^{\mu}}{u}-P_{T}^{\mu}\frac{(P^{\nu}_{T}u_{\nu})}{su}\;.

(164)

With the help of this new space-like four-vector a new symmetric and traceless projection operator,
similarly as in anisotropic fluid dynamics, see for example Ref.
Molnar et al. 2016
, can be constructed. Here besides the usual space-like projection operator a new projection onto the two-dimensional subspace that is orthogonal to both
P^{\mu}_{T}
and
l^{\mu}_{T}
is defined as,

\Xi^{\mu\nu}_{T}\equiv g^{\mu\nu}-\frac{P^{\mu}_{T}P^{\nu}_{T}}{s}+l^{\mu}_{T}l^{\nu}_{T}=\Delta^{\mu\nu}_{T}+l^{\mu}_{T}l^{\nu}_{T}\;,

(165)

where
\Xi^{\mu\nu}_{T}P_{T,\nu}=\Xi^{\mu\nu}_{T}l_{T,\nu}=0
,
while
\Xi^{\mu\nu}_{T}g_{\mu\nu}=2
.
Using these projectors, the particle momentum can be decomposed with respect to
P^{\mu}_{T}
,
l^{\mu}_{T}

and
\Xi^{\mu\nu}_{T}
as

p^{\mu}=P^{\mu}_{T}\frac{(P^{\nu}_{T}p_{\nu})}{s}-l^{\mu}_{T}(l^{\nu}_{T}p_{\nu})+\Xi^{\mu\nu}_{T}p_{\nu}\;.

(166)

Appendix D
The
P
and
P^{\prime}
integrals

In order to evaluate the collision matrix, i.e., Eqs. (
41
) and (
42
), we have to compute the following type of momentum integrals,

\mathcal{P}^{\mu_{1}\cdots\mu_{n}}_{i}\equiv\frac{1}{2}\int\mathrm{d}P\mathrm{d}P^{\prime}W_{\mathbf{kk}\prime\rightarrow\mathbf{pp}\prime}E_{\mathbf{p}}^{i}p^{\mu_{1}}\cdots p^{\mu_{n}}\;.

(167)

It is beneficial to first introduce the following auxiliary integral from Refs.
Denicol et al. 2012
;
Molnár et al. 2014
,

\displaystyle\Theta^{\mu_{1}\cdots\mu_{n}}

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{1}{2}\int\mathrm{d}P\mathrm{d}P^{\prime}W_{\mathbf{kk}\prime\rightarrow\mathbf{pp}\prime}p^{\mu_{1}}\cdots p^{\mu_{n}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{q=0}^{\left[n/2\right]}\left(-1\right)^{q}b_{nq}\mathcal{B}_{nq}

\displaystyle\times\Delta_{T}^{\left(\mu_{1}\mu_{2}\right.}\cdots\Delta_{T}^{\mu_{2q-1}\mu_{2q}}\,P_{T}^{\mu_{2q+1}}\cdots P_{T}^{\left.\mu_{n}\right)}\;,

(168)

where we used Eq. (
154
) repeatedly to replace
p^{\mu_{1}}\cdots p^{\mu_{n}}
.
Here,
n
,
q
are natural numbers while the sum runs up to
[n/2]

denoting the largest integer which is less than or equal to
n/2
.
The symmetrized tensors

\Delta^{\left(\right.}_{T}\cdots P_{T}^{\left.\right)}
are counted by

b_{nq}\equiv\frac{n!}{2^{q}q!\left(n-2q\right)!}
, while the
\mathcal{B}_{nq}
coefficients are

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{nq}

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{(-1)^{q}}{\left(2q+1\right)!!}\frac{1}{2}\int\mathrm{d}P\mathrm{d}P^{\prime}W_{\mathbf{kk}\prime\rightarrow\mathbf{pp}\prime}

\displaystyle\times\left(\frac{P_{T}^{\mu}p_{\mu}}{\sqrt{s}}\right)^{n-2q}\left(\Delta_{P}^{\alpha\beta}p_{\alpha}p_{\beta}\right)^{q}\;.

(169)

In evaluating
\mathcal{B}_{nq}
, we changed
p^{\mu}
and
p^{\prime\mu}
to the CM frame defined by
P^{\mu}_{T}=k^{\mu}+k^{\prime\mu}
, such that

P^{\mu}_{T}p_{\mu}=s/2
and
\Delta_{P}^{\alpha\beta}p_{\alpha}p_{\beta}=-s/4
.
The integrals in Eq. (
167
) are then obtained via

\mathcal{P}_{i}^{\mu_{1}\cdots\mu_{\ell}}=u_{\nu_{1}}\cdots u_{\nu_{i}}\Theta^{\nu_{1}\cdots\nu_{i}\mu_{1}\cdots\mu_{\ell}}\;.

(170)

Even though the integral
\mathcal{P}^{\mu_{1}\cdots\mu_{n}}_{i}
could in principle be evaluated via
Eq. (
170
), doing so is rather complicated.
Instead, it is more sensible to use the decomposition from Eq. (
163
) to write

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}^{\mu_{1}\cdots\mu_{n}}_{i}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int\mathrm{d}P\mathrm{d}P^{\prime}W_{\mathbf{kk}\prime\rightarrow\mathbf{pp}\prime}p^{\mu_{1}}\cdots p^{\mu_{n}}

\displaystyle\times\left(P^{\nu}_{T}u_{\nu}\frac{(P^{\mu}_{T}p_{\mu})}{s}+z^{\mu}_{T}p_{\mu}\right)^{i}\;,

(171)

from where it is clear that the tensor structure of
\mathcal{P}^{\mu_{1}\cdots\mu_{n}}_{i}
can only consist of the tensors
l_{T}^{\mu}
,
P_{T}^{\mu}
and
\Xi_{T}^{\mu\nu}
.

Now, in the CM-frame we express
z^{\mu}_{T}p_{\mu}=u(l^{\mu}_{T}p_{\mu})=(\sqrt{s}u/2)\cos\theta_{pu}
, where
\theta_{pu}
is the angle between
\mathbf{p}
and
\mathbf{u}
. Furthermore, we have

P^{\mu}_{T}u_{\mu}=E_{\mathbf{p}}+E_{\mathbf{p}^{\prime}}=\sqrt{s}u^{0}
, and using the binomial formula
we obtain

\displaystyle E_{\mathbf{p}}^{i}=\sum_{j=0}^{i}\binom{i}{j}\frac{(u^{0})^{i-j}}{u^{-j}}\left(\frac{P^{\mu}_{T}p_{\mu}}{\sqrt{s}}\right)^{i-j}\left(l^{\mu}_{T}p_{\mu}\right)^{j}\;.

(172)

Subsequently, we expand the integral
\mathcal{P}^{\mu_{1}\cdots\mu_{n}}_{i}

l_{T}^{\mu}

P_{T}^{\mu}

\Xi_{T}^{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}^{\mu_{1}\cdots\mu_{n}}_{i}\equiv\sum_{q=0}^{\left[n/2\right]}\sum_{m=0}^{n-2q}\left(-1\right)^{q}b_{nmq}\mathcal{D}^{(i)}_{nmq}

\displaystyle\times\Xi_{T}^{\left(\mu_{1}\mu_{2}\right.}\cdots\Xi_{T}^{\mu_{2q-1}\mu_{2q}}\,l_{T}^{\mu_{2q+1}}\cdots l_{T}^{\mu_{2q+m}}P_{T}^{\mu_{2q+m+1}}\cdots P_{T}^{\left.\mu_{n}\right)}\;,

(173)

where
b_{nmq}\equiv n!/[2^{q}q!m!(n-2q-m)!]

\mathcal{D}^{(i)}_{nmq}

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}^{(i)}_{nmq}\equiv\frac{(-1)^{q+m}}{\left(2q\right)!!}\frac{1}{2}\int\mathrm{d}P\mathrm{d}P^{\prime}W_{\mathbf{kk}\prime\rightarrow\mathbf{pp}\prime}\left(\Xi_{T}^{\alpha\beta}p_{\alpha}p_{\beta}\right)^{q}

\displaystyle\times\sum_{j=0}^{i}\binom{i}{j}\frac{(u^{0})^{i-j}}{u^{-j}}\left(\frac{P_{T}^{\mu}p_{\mu}}{\sqrt{s}}\right)^{n+i-m-j-2q}\left(l_{T}^{\mu}p_{\mu}\right)^{m+j}\;,

(174)

where the double factorial for even numbers is
(2q)!!\equiv 2^{q}q!

To evaluate these coefficients, we note that

l_{T}^{\mu}p_{\mu}=(E_{\mathbf{p}}-E_{\mathbf{p}^{\prime}})/(2u)

s=2p^{\mu}p^{\prime}_{\mu}=4p^{2}

\Xi^{\alpha\beta}_{T}p_{\alpha}p_{\beta}=-s/4+(l_{T}^{\mu}p_{\mu})^{2}=-p^{2}\sin^{2}\theta_{pu}
.

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}^{(i)}_{nmq}=\frac{(-1)^{m}}{4\left(2q\right)!!}\frac{\sigma_{T}}{2^{n+1+i}}s^{q+1+\frac{m}{2}}

\displaystyle\times\sum_{j=0}^{i}\binom{i}{j}\left[1+(-1)^{m+j}\right]\left(\sqrt{s}u^{0}\right)^{i-j}\left(\sqrt{s}u\right)^{j}

(175)

where
B(i,j)\equiv\Gamma(i)\Gamma(j)/\Gamma(i+j)
denotes the Euler Beta function. Note that
\mathcal{D}^{(0)}_{n0q}=\mathcal{B}_{nq}
, as expected.

(177)

Note that the latter scalar integrals can be evaluated in other ways as in Refs.

;
Bazow et al. 2016b

Appendix E
Computation of the loss terms

In this section we compute the loss terms
\mathcal{L}_{rn}^{(\ell)}
defined in
Eq. (
41
) for
\ell=0,1,2
.
These integrals are Lorentz scalars and thus can be evaluated in any frame. Here
we choose the LR-frame of the fluid, where
E_{\mathbf{k}}\equiv k^{\mu}u_{\mu}\overset{\mathrm{LR}}{=}k^{0}=\sqrt{k^{2}+m^{2}_{0}}
. In the following, we will omit the notation “LR” for brevity.

In spherical coordinates,
\mathrm{d}K=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{k^{2}}{k^{0}}\sin\theta\mathrm{d}k\mathrm{d}\theta\mathrm{d}\varphi
,
where
k\in\left[0,\infty\right)
,
\theta\in\left[0,\pi\right]
,
and
\varphi\in\left[0,2\pi\right)
.
Furthermore, by choosing the orientation of
\mathbf{k}^{\prime}
parallel to the
z
-axis,
the angle between the colliding particles
\theta_{kk^{\prime}}

is equivalent to the elevation angle
\theta=\arccos(k^{z}/k)
.
Substituting now
k^{0}=k
,
k^{\prime 0}=k^{\prime}
, and

s/2=kk^{\prime}\left(1-x\right)
, with
x\equiv\cos\theta_{kk^{\prime}}
,
the loss term for
\ell=0
yields

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{rn}^{(0)}

\displaystyle\equiv\sigma_{T}\int\mathrm{d}K\mathrm{d}K^{\prime}f_{0\mathbf{k}}f_{0\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}\frac{s}{2}E_{\mathbf{k}}^{r}\left(E_{\mathbf{k}}^{n}+E_{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}^{n}\right)

\displaystyle=\sigma_{T}\frac{g^{2}e^{2\alpha}}{8\pi^{4}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}kk^{r+2}\int_{-1}^{1}\mathrm{d}x\left(1-x\right)

\displaystyle\times\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}k^{\prime}k^{\prime 2}e^{-\beta(k+k^{\prime})}\left(k^{n}+k^{\prime n}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\sigma_{T}P_{0}^{2}\beta^{2-r-n}}{4}

\displaystyle\times\big[2\Gamma(r+n+3)+\Gamma(r+3)\Gamma(n+3)\big]\;,

(186)

where we used Eqs. (
134
–
135
), as well as the definition of the
Gamma function to compute the integrals

\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\mathrm{d}y\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\mathrm{d}y^{\prime}e^{-y-y^{\prime}}y^{r+a}y^{\prime b}=\Gamma(r+a+1)\Gamma(b+1)\;.

(187)

For computing the result for
\ell=1
that can be found in the same way, we note that in the LR frame we have

\displaystyle k^{\langle\mu\rangle}k_{\mu}

\displaystyle\equiv k^{\langle\mu\rangle}k_{\langle\mu\rangle}=-k^{2}\;,

(188)

\displaystyle k^{\langle\mu\rangle}k^{\prime}_{\mu}

\displaystyle\equiv k^{\langle\mu\rangle}k^{\prime}_{\langle\mu\rangle}=-kk^{\prime}x\;.

(189)

Using these results, Eq. (
41
) for
\ell=1
yields

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{rn}^{(1)}

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{\sigma_{T}}{3}\int\mathrm{d}K\mathrm{d}K^{\prime}f_{0\mathbf{k}}f_{0\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}\frac{s}{2}E_{\mathbf{k}}^{r}k^{\langle\mu\rangle}\left(E_{\mathbf{k}}^{n}k_{\mu}+E_{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}^{n}k^{\prime}_{\mu}\right)

\displaystyle=-\frac{\sigma_{T}}{3}\frac{g^{2}e^{2\alpha}}{8\pi^{4}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}kk^{r+3}\int_{-1}^{1}\mathrm{d}x(1-x)

\displaystyle\times\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}k^{\prime}k^{\prime 2}e^{-\beta(k+k^{\prime})}\left(k^{n+1}+k^{\prime n+1}x\right)

\displaystyle=-\frac{\sigma_{T}P_{0}^{2}\beta^{-r-n}}{36}

\displaystyle\times\big[6\Gamma(r+n+5)-\Gamma(r+4)\Gamma(n+4)\big]\;.

(190)

In the case when
\ell=2
, we make use of the following identities

\displaystyle k^{\langle\mu}k^{\nu\rangle}k_{\mu}k_{\nu}

\displaystyle\equiv k^{\langle\mu}k^{\nu\rangle}k_{\langle\mu}k_{\nu\rangle}=\frac{2}{3}k^{4}\;,

(191)

\displaystyle k^{\langle\mu}k^{\nu\rangle}k^{\prime}_{\mu}k^{\prime}_{\nu}

\displaystyle\equiv k^{\langle\mu}k^{\nu\rangle}k^{\prime}_{\langle\mu}k^{\prime}_{\nu\rangle}=k^{2}k^{\prime 2}\left(x^{2}-\frac{1}{3}\right)\;,

(192)

where
k^{\langle\mu}k^{\nu\rangle}\equiv\Delta^{\mu\nu}_{\alpha\beta}k^{\alpha}k^{\beta}=k^{\langle\mu\rangle}k^{\langle\nu\rangle}-k^{\langle\alpha\rangle}k_{\langle\alpha\rangle}\Delta^{\mu\nu}/3

and
\Delta^{\mu\nu}_{\alpha\beta}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\Delta^{\mu}_{\alpha}\Delta^{\nu}_{\beta}+\Delta^{\mu}_{\beta}\Delta^{\nu}_{\alpha}\right)-\frac{1}{3}\Delta^{\mu\nu}\Delta_{\alpha\beta}
.
The corresponding loss term now reads,

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{rn}^{(2)}

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{\sigma_{T}}{5}\int\mathrm{d}K\mathrm{d}K^{\prime}f_{0\mathbf{k}}f_{0\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}\frac{s}{2}E_{\mathbf{k}}^{r}k^{\langle\mu}k^{\nu\rangle}

\displaystyle\times\left(E_{\mathbf{k}}^{n}k_{\langle\mu}k_{\nu\rangle}+E_{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}^{n}k^{\prime}_{\langle\mu}k^{\prime}_{\nu\rangle}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\sigma_{T}}{5}\frac{g^{2}e^{2\alpha}}{24\pi^{4}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}kk^{r+4}\int_{-1}^{1}\mathrm{d}x(1-x)

\displaystyle\times\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}k^{\prime}k^{\prime 2}e^{-\beta(k+k^{\prime})}\left[2k^{n+2}+k^{\prime n+2}(3x^{2}-1)\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{\sigma_{T}P_{0}^{2}\beta^{-2-r-n}}{15}\Gamma(r+n+7)\;.

(193)

These results for
\ell=0,1,2
, corresponding to Eqs. (
83

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{rn}^{(\ell)}

\displaystyle=\frac{\sigma_{T}P_{0}^{2}\beta^{2-2\ell-r-n}}{2}\left[\frac{(-1)^{\ell}\ell!}{(2\ell+1)!!}\Gamma(r+n+2\ell+3)\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\mathcal{B}^{(\ell)}\Gamma(r+\ell+3)\Gamma(n+\ell+3)\right]\;.

(194)

where we introduced the coefficient
\mathcal{B}^{(\ell)}=\left\{1/2,1/18,0\right\}

Appendix G
Computation of the loss matrices

Having computed the terms
\mathcal{L}_{rn}^{(\ell)}
in Appendix
E
, we now have to perform the sums to obtain the loss part of the collision matrix
\mathcal{A}_{rn}^{(\ell),{\rm l}}
defined in
Eq. (
39
).
We start by substituting the explicit expression (
194
) into Eq. (
39
) together with the expressions from Eqs. (
140
) and (
141
). After some straightforward algebra we obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{rn}^{(\ell),{\rm l}}

\displaystyle=\sigma_{T}P_{0}\beta^{1-r+n}\frac{(-1)^{\ell+n}(2\ell+1)!!}{\ell!(n+2\ell+1)!}

\displaystyle\times\sum_{m=n}^{N_{\ell}}\frac{(m+2\ell+1)!}{n!(m-n)!}\sum_{q=0}^{m}(-1)^{q}\binom{m}{q}\left[\frac{(-1)^{\ell}\ell!}{(2\ell+1)!!}\frac{(r+q+2\ell+1)!}{(q+2\ell+1)!}+\mathcal{B}^{(\ell)}\frac{(r+\ell+1)!(q+\ell+2)!}{(q+2\ell+1)!}\right]\;.

(218)

The sum over
q
can be evaluated with the help of the identity

\displaystyle\sum_{q=0}^{m}(-1)^{q}\binom{m}{q}\frac{(q+a+b)!}{(q+a)!}

\displaystyle=\frac{(a+b)!}{a!}{}_{2}F_{1}(a+b+1,-m;a+1;1)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{m}\frac{b!(a+b)!}{(b-m)!(m+a)!}\;,

(219)

where we used Eq. (15.8.7) of Ref.
Olver et al. 2010
to evaluate the Gauss hypergeometric function:

{}_{2}F_{1}(a+b+1,-m;a+1;z)=\frac{(-b)_{m}}{(a+1)_{m}}{}_{2}F_{1}(-m,a+b+1;b-m+1;1-z)\;,

(220)

with the Pochhammer symbols evaluating to
(a+1)_{m}=(a+1)\cdots(a+m)=(a+m)!/a!
and
(-b)_{m}=(-b)(-b+1)\cdots(-b+m-1)=(-1)^{m}b!/(b-m)!
. Noting that
{}_{2}F_{1}(a,b;c;0)=1
, we arrive at

{}_{2}F_{1}(a+b+1,-m;a+1;1)=\frac{(-1)^{m}a!b!}{(a+m)!(b-m)!},

(221)

in agreement with Eq. (
219
).
Using Eq. (
219
), we arrive at

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{rn}^{(\ell),{\rm l}}

\displaystyle=\sigma_{T}P_{0}\beta^{1-r+n}\frac{(-1)^{\ell+n}(2\ell+1)!!}{\ell!(n+2\ell+1)!}(r+2\ell+1)!

\displaystyle\times\sum_{m=n}^{N_{\ell}}(-1)^{m}\binom{m}{n}\left[\frac{(-1)^{\ell}\ell!}{(2\ell+1)!!}\binom{r}{m}+\mathcal{B}^{(\ell)}\frac{(1-\ell)!(\ell+2)!(r+\ell+1)!}{m!(1-\ell-m)!(r+2\ell+1)!}\right]\;.

(222)

The binomial coefficient
\binom{r}{m}
vanishes when

m>r
, such that the first term in the sum over
m
gives a nonvanishing contribution only when
n\leq r
. In this case, the sum over
m
runs between
n
and
r
, yielding a Kronecker delta:

\sum_{m=n}^{r}(-1)^{m}\binom{m}{n}\binom{r}{m}=(-1)^{n}\delta_{rn}\;.

(223)

The term involving
\mathcal{B}^{(\ell)}
vanishes for
\ell=2
, since by definition
\mathcal{B}^{(2)}=0
. For
\ell=0
and
\ell=1
, the sum over
m
terminates at
m=1-\ell
. Performing this sum separately for
\ell=0
and
\ell=1
, we find

\displaystyle\ell

\displaystyle=0:\qquad\quad\sum_{m=n}^{1}\binom{m}{n}\frac{(-1)^{m}}{m!(1-m)!}

\displaystyle=-\delta_{n1}\;,

(224a)

\displaystyle\ell

\displaystyle=1:\qquad\quad\sum_{m=n}^{0}\binom{m}{n}\frac{(-1)^{m}}{m!(-m)!}

\displaystyle=\delta_{n0}\;.

(224b)

With all these results, the contributions of the loss terms to the collision matrix are

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}^{(0),{\rm l}}_{rn}

\displaystyle=\sigma_{T}P_{0}\beta\left[\delta_{nr}+\frac{(r+1)!}{2}\delta_{n1}\beta^{1-r}\right]\;,

(225a)

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}^{(1),{\rm l}}_{rn}

\displaystyle=\sigma_{T}P_{0}\beta\left[\delta_{nr}-\frac{(r+2)!}{6}\delta_{n0}\beta^{-r}\right]\;,

(225b)

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}^{(2),{\rm l}}_{rn}

\displaystyle=\sigma_{T}P_{0}\beta\,\delta_{nr}\;,

(225c)

in agreement with Eqs. (
84
).

Appendix H

40

Appendix I
Calculations for the bulk viscous pressure

In this Appendix, we provide some intermediate calculations needed to arrive at the exact results for the inverse collision matrix
\tau^{(0)}
, the bulk viscosity
\zeta
and the relaxation time of the bulk viscous pressure
\tau_{\Pi}
. Furthermore, we show how to compute the correction to the local-equilibrium distribution function proportional to the bulk viscous pressure.

I.1
Inverse matrix

The matrix in the scalar case has the following structure:

\mathcal{A}^{(0)}_{rn}=\begin{pmatrix}\mathcal{A}^{(0)}_{00}&\mathcal{A}^{(0)}_{0,n>2}\\
0&\mathcal{A}^{(0)}_{r>2,n>2}\end{pmatrix},

(267)

where
\mathcal{A}^{(0)}_{r>2,n>r}=0
, i.e. the matrix appearing in the bottom-right corner of the above expression is lower-triangular. The inverse matrix
\tau^{(0)}_{rn}
inherits the same form,

\tau^{(0)}_{rn}=\begin{pmatrix}\tau^{(0)}_{00}&\tau^{(0)}_{0,n>2}\\
0&\tau^{(0)}_{r>2,n>2}\end{pmatrix},

(268)

where
\tau^{(0)}_{r>2,n>2}
is also lower-triangular. It is easy to see that

\tau^{(0)}_{00}=\frac{1}{\mathcal{A}^{(0)}_{00}}=\lambda_{\rm mfp}\frac{N_{0}+1}{N_{0}-1}\;.

(269)

For future convenience, we parametrize
\tau^{(0)}_{rn}
for
3\leq n\leq r\leq N_{0}
as

\tau^{(0)}_{rn}=\frac{\lambda_{\rm mfp}(r+1)!}{\beta^{r-n}(n+1)!}\left(\delta_{rn}+\tilde{\tau}^{(0)}_{rn}\right)\;.

(270)

Imposing
\sum_{m=0,\neq 1,2}^{N_{0}}\tau^{(0)}_{rm}\mathcal{A}^{(0)}_{mn}=\delta_{rn}

gives for
r,n>2
:

\tilde{\tau}^{(0)}_{rm}-\sum_{n=m}^{r}\frac{2}{n}\tilde{\tau}^{(0)}_{rn}=\frac{2}{r}\;.

(271)

The above relation can be arranged into a simple recurrsion,

\tilde{\tau}^{(0)}_{rm}=\frac{m}{m-2}\tilde{\tau}^{(0)}_{r,m+1}\;.

(272)

Noting that
\tau^{(0)}_{rr}=1/\mathcal{A}^{(0)}_{rr}
, we have
\tilde{\tau}^{(0)}_{rr}=2/(r-2)
, such that

\tilde{\tau}^{(0)}_{rm}=\frac{2(r-1)}{(m-1)(m-2)},

(273)

leading to

\tau^{(0)}_{r>2,2<n\leq r}=\frac{\lambda_{\rm mfp}(r+1)!}{\beta^{r-n}(n+1)!}\left[\delta_{rn}+\frac{2(r-1)}{(m-1)(m-2)}\right]\;.

(274)

The elements on the zeroth line can be found by imposing
\sum_{r=0}^{N_{0}}\mathcal{A}^{(0)}_{0r}\tau^{(0)}_{rn}=0
for
n>2
:

\mathcal{A}^{(0)}_{00}\tau^{(0)}_{0,n>2}=-\sum_{r=n}^{N_{0}}\mathcal{A}^{(0)}_{0r}\tau^{(0)}_{rn}\;.

(275)

Using Eqs. (
88
), (
269
), and (
274
), we get

\frac{\tau_{0,n>0}^{(0)}}{\tau^{(0)}_{00}}=-\frac{2\beta^{n}}{(n-1)(n-2)(n+1)!}\\
\times\sum_{r=n}^{N_{0}}(-1)^{r}(r-1)S_{r}^{(0)}(N_{0})[2+(r-2)\delta_{rn}]\;.

(276)

Using the explicit expression (
87
) for
S^{(0)}_{n}
, the summation over
r
can be performed, leading to:

\displaystyle\frac{\tau_{0,n>0}^{(0)}}{\tau^{(0)}_{00}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{2(-\beta)^{n}}{(n-1)(n-2)(n+1)!}

\displaystyle\times\sum_{m=n}^{N_{0}}\binom{m}{n}\frac{2n+m(n-2)(nm-m+n+1)}{m^{2}(m^{2}-1)}

\displaystyle=-\frac{2(-\beta)^{n}}{(n-1)(n-2)(n+1)!}

\displaystyle\times\binom{1+N_{0}}{n}\frac{(1+N_{0}-n)[N_{0}(n-2)+n]}{N_{0}(N_{0}+1)^{2}}\;.

(277)

Collecting the above results, we find Eqs. (
112
).

I.2
Bulk viscosity

We compute
\zeta/m^{4}\equiv\zeta_{0}/m^{4}
by substituting Eqs. (
269
) and (
277
) in Eq. (
113
):

\frac{1}{m_{0}^{4}}\zeta=\frac{P_{0}\beta^{4}\lambda_{\rm mfp}(N_{0}+1)}{54(N_{0}-1)}\Bigg[1-\sum_{n=3}^{N_{0}}\frac{(-1)^{n}}{n+1}\\
\times\sum_{m=n}^{N_{0}}\binom{m}{n}\frac{n+m(n-2)(nm-m+1)}{m^{2}(m^{2}-1)}\Bigg]\;.

(278)

Swapping the summation with respect to
n
with that with respect to
m
and using the properties

\displaystyle\sum_{n=3}^{m}\binom{m}{n}\frac{(-1)^{n}}{n+1}

\displaystyle=-\frac{m(m-1)(m-2)}{6(m+1)}\;,

(279a)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=3}^{m}\binom{m}{n}(-1)^{n}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}(m-1)(m-2)\;,

(279b)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=3}^{m}\binom{m}{n}(-1)^{n}n

\displaystyle=-m(m-2)\;.

(279c)

Eq. (
278
) can be reduced to

\frac{1}{m_{0}^{4}}\zeta=\frac{P_{0}\beta^{4}\lambda_{\rm mfp}(N_{0}+1)}{54(N_{0}-1)}\\
\times\left[1+\sum_{m=3}^{N_{0}}\frac{(m-2)(11m^{2}+4m-3)}{3m^{2}(m-1)(m+1)^{2}}\right]\;.

(280)

The sum over
m
appearing above represents a correction to the 14-moment approximation, represented by the prefactor of the square brackets. After preforming this sum, we arrive at Eq. (
115
).

I.3
IReD relaxation time

We begin with
\tau_{\Pi}\equiv\tau_{\Pi;0}
and use Eqs. (
114
) and (
115
),

\displaystyle\tau_{\Pi}

\displaystyle=\tau^{(0)}_{00}+\sum_{r=3}^{N_{0}}\tau^{(0)}_{0r}\frac{\zeta_{r}}{\zeta}

\displaystyle=\tau^{(0)}_{00}\Bigg\{1-\frac{6N_{0}(N_{0}^{2}-1)}{6+7N_{0}+11N_{0}^{3}}\sum_{m=3}^{N_{0}}\frac{1}{m(m+1)}

\displaystyle\times\sum_{r=3}^{m}\binom{m}{r}(-1)^{r}\left(2H_{r}-\frac{1}{r+1}-\frac{8}{3}\right)

\displaystyle\times\left[r-1+\frac{2r}{m-1}+\frac{2r}{m(m-1)(r-2)}\right]\Bigg\}\;.

(281)

In order to evaluate the sums not containing harmonic numbers, we need the identities (
279

\sum_{r=3}^{m}\binom{m}{r}\frac{(-1)^{r}}{r-2}=\frac{1}{4}m(m-1)(3-2H_{m})\;.

(282)

In order to perform the summation over
r
for the terms involving
H_{r}
, we employ its integral representation,

H_{r}=\int_{0}^{1}\mathrm{d}t\,\frac{1-t^{r}}{1-t}\;,

(283)

together with the relations

\displaystyle\sum_{r=3}^{m}\binom{m}{r}(-t)^{r}

\displaystyle=-1+(1-t)^{m}+\frac{mt}{2}(2+t-mt)\;,

\displaystyle\sum_{r=3}^{m}\binom{m}{r}(-1)^{r}r

\displaystyle=mt[1-(1-t)^{m-1}-t(m-1)]\;,

\displaystyle\sum_{r=3}^{m}\binom{m}{r}\frac{(-1)^{r}}{r-2}

\displaystyle=-\frac{mt^{3}}{6}(m-2)(m-1)

\displaystyle\times{}_{3}F_{2}(1,1,3-m;2,4;t)\;.

(284)

Interchanging the summation with respect to
r
with the integration with respect to
t
, we arrive at

\displaystyle\sum_{r=3}^{m}\binom{m}{r}H_{r}

\displaystyle=-\frac{3m^{3}-7m^{2}+4}{4m}\;,

(285a)

\displaystyle\sum_{r=3}^{m}\binom{m}{r}(-1)^{r}rH_{r}

\displaystyle=\frac{m}{2}(5-3m)+\frac{1}{m-1}\;,

(285b)

\displaystyle\sum_{r=3}^{m}\binom{m}{r}\frac{(-1)^{r}H_{r}}{r-2}

\displaystyle=\frac{m}{4}(m-1)(7-3H_{m}-2H_{m,2})\;,

(285c)

where
H_{m,n}=\sum_{r=1}^{m}r^{-n}
is the generalized Harmonic number, with
H_{m}\equiv H_{m,1}
. Adding everything up, we find

\tau_{\Pi}=\tau^{(0)}_{00}\Bigg\{1+\frac{4N_{0}(N_{0}^{2}-1)}{6+7N_{0}+11N_{0}^{3}}\sum_{m=3}^{N_{0}}\frac{1}{m(m+1)}\\
\times\left[\frac{11}{6}-\frac{2m}{m^{2}-1}-\frac{6+m}{m^{2}}-\frac{6}{(m-1)^{2}}+6H_{m,2}\right]\Bigg\}\;.

(286)

The summation over
m
can be performed, yielding Eq. (
124
).
For the relaxation times of the higher-order moments we have

\tau_{\Pi;r}=\sum_{n=0,\neq 1,2}^{N_{0}}\tau^{(0)}_{rn}\frac{\zeta_{n}}{\zeta_{r}}\\
=\frac{m^{4}}{\zeta_{r}}\frac{\lambda_{\rm mfp}P\beta^{4-r}}{108r}(r-2)(r-1)(r+1)!\Bigg[2H_{r}-\frac{1}{r+1}\\
-\frac{8}{3}+2\sum_{n=3}^{r}\frac{1}{n-2}\left(2H_{n}-\frac{1}{n+1}-\frac{8}{3}\right)\Bigg]\;.

(287)

Performing the summation then gives Eq. (
126
).

I.4
Correction to the distribution function

We start from Eq. (
132
) and use
Eqs. (
114
) and (
115
) for
\zeta_{n}
and
\zeta_{0}
, as well as Eq. (
142

\mathcal{H}^{(0)}_{{\mathbf{k}}n}

\frac{\delta f^{(0)}_{\mathbf{k}}}{f_{0{\mathbf{k}}}}=-\frac{6\Pi}{m_{0}^{2}\beta^{2}P}\left[\sum_{m=0}^{N_{0}}L^{(1)}_{m}(\beta E_{\mathbf{k}})+\frac{3N_{0}(N_{0}^{2}-1)}{6+7N_{0}+11N_{0}^{3}}\right.\\

\left.\times\sum_{m=n}^{N_{0}}\frac{m!}{n!(m-n)!}L^{(1)}_{m}(\beta E_{\mathbf{k}})\right]\;.

(288)

In the second term, the sums over
n
and

n

(289)

Plugging the above into Eq. (

\frac{\delta f^{(0)}_{\mathbf{k}}}{f_{0{\mathbf{k}}}}=-\frac{6\Pi}{m_{0}^{2}\beta^{2}P}\left[\sum_{m=0}^{N_{0}}L^{(1)}_{m}(\beta E_{\mathbf{k}})+\frac{3N_{0}(N_{0}^{2}-1)}{6+7N_{0}+11N_{0}^{3}}\right.\\

(290)

We now consider the limit

\frac{\delta f^{(0)}_{\mathbf{k}}}{f_{0{\mathbf{k}}}}=-\frac{6\Pi}{m_{0}^{2}\beta^{2}P}\Bigg[1+L^{(1)}_{1}(\beta E_{\mathbf{k}})+L^{(1)}_{2}(\beta E_{\mathbf{k}})\\

(291)

\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}t^{m}L^{(\alpha)}_{m}(x)=\frac{1}{(1-t)^{\alpha+1}}e^{-tx/(1-t)}\;.

In our case, we must evaluate

\sum_{m=3}^{\infty}L^{(1)}_{m}(x)\left(\frac{1}{m-1}+\frac{1}{m}+\frac{1}{m+1}\right)\\

\sum_{m=3}^{\infty}L^{(1)}_{m}(x)\left(\frac{1}{m-1}+\frac{1}{m}+\frac{1}{m+1}\right)=\frac{3}{x}\\

(294)

Plugging the above into Eq. (

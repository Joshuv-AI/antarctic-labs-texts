Abstract
We present the first theoretical calculation of nonfactorizable charm-quark loop contributions to the B s → γ ​ l + ​ l − B_{s}\to\gamma l^{+}l^{-} amplitude. We calculate the relevant form factors, H A , V NF ​ ( k ′ 2 , k 2 ) H_{A,V}^{\rm NF}(k^{\prime 2},k^{2}) , and provide convenient parametrizations of our results in the form of fit functions of two variables, k ′ 2 k^{\prime 2} and k 2 k^{2} , applicable in the region below hadron resonances, k ′ 2 < M J / ψ 2 k^{\prime 2}<M_{J/\psi}^{2} and k 2 < M ϕ 2 k^{2}<M_{\phi}^{2} . We report that factorizable and nonfactorizable charm contributions to the B s → γ ​ l + ​ l − B_{s}\to\gamma l^{+}l^{-} amplitude have opposite signs. To compare the charm and the top contributions, it is convenient to express nonfactorizable charming loop contribution as a non-universal (i.e., dependent on the reaction) q 2 q^{2} -dependent correction Δ NF ​ C 7 ​ ( q 2 ) \Delta^{\rm NF}C_{7}(q^{2}) to the Wilson coefficient C 7 C_{7} . V
Discussion and conclusions

This paper extended the theoretical approach to NF charming loops in FCNC
B_{s}
decays recently
formulated in
[
1
]
and for the first time reports the results for NF charm in
B_{s}\to\gamma ll
decays:

(i) We derived analytical expressions for the form factors
H_{i}^{\rm NF}(k^{\prime 2},k^{2})
,
i=A,V
,
describing NF contribution of charming loops to the amplitude of the
B_{s}
meson transition
into two virtual photons (the first argument,
k^{\prime 2}

corresponds to the momentum emitted from the charming loop, whereas the second argument,
k^{2}
,
corresponds to the momentum emitted by the valence
s
-quark of the
B_{s}
-meson).
These expressions may be written in the form

\displaystyle H_{i}^{\rm NF}(k^{\prime 2},k^{2})=2C_{2}Q_{s}Q_{c}\left[\lambda_{E}^{2}R_{iE}(k^{\prime 2},k^{2})+\lambda_{H}^{2}R_{iH}(k^{\prime 2},k^{2})\right]\qquad i=A,V,3.

(5.67)

Since an approximate relation
\lambda_{H}^{2}\simeq 2\lambda_{E}^{2}
is expected, the linear combination

\displaystyle R_{i}(k^{\prime 2},k^{2})=2R_{iH}(k^{\prime 2},k^{2})+R_{iE}(k^{\prime 2},k^{2})

(5.68)

is approprate for the description of NF charming loops in
B_{s}
decays such that

\displaystyle H_{i}^{\rm NF}(k^{\prime 2},k^{2})=2C_{2}Q_{s}Q_{c}\lambda_{E}^{2}R_{i}(k^{\prime 2},k^{2}).

(5.69)

We emphasize that according to our analysis,
R_{i}(k^{\prime 2},k^{2})>0
in the region
k^{\prime 2}<M_{J/\psi}^{2}
and
k^{2}<M_{\phi}^{2}

and thus
H_{i}^{\rm NF}(k^{\prime 2},k^{2})
is
positive
in this region. Recall that
the factorizable contribution
H_{i}^{\rm F}(k^{\prime 2},k^{2})
is
negative
.

(ii) The analytic expressions allow one to calculate the form factors
R_{i}(k^{\prime 2},k^{2})
in a broad range

k^{\prime 2}<4m_{c}^{2}
and
k^{2}<0
.
However, calculations based on finite-order QCD diagrams are not expected to provide good description
of the physical hadron amplitudes near quark thresholds
(for instance, the calculated form factors exhibit steep rise at
k^{2}\to 0
which is unphysical,
as the nearest meson pole lies at
k^{2}=M_{\phi}^{2}
and the two-meson threshold lies at
k^{2}=4M_{K}^{2}
).
So we pursue the following strategy:
We make use of the results of our calculation in the rectangular region

0<k^{\prime 2}({\rm GeV}^{2})<4
and
-5<k^{2}({\rm GeV}^{2})<-0.6
(i.e., sufficiently far from quark thresholds)
and
interpolate
them by a simple analytic formula depending on
k^{\prime 2}
and
k^{2}
, which
takes into account the presence of the poles at
k^{\prime 2}=M_{J/\psi}^{2}
and
k^{2}=M_{\phi}^{2}
.
Numerical parameters in this formula are obtained by the fit to the results of our calculations
and interpolate them with a 2% accuracy in the rectangular region mentioned above.
The corresponding easy-to-use fit formulas for
R_{i}(k^{\prime 2},k^{2})
are presented in Appendix
C
.

(iii) Since the interpolating formulas exhibit the correct location of the lowest hadron singularities,
i.e., poles at
k^{\prime 2}=M_{J/\psi}^{2}
and at
k^{2}=M_{\phi}^{2}
, our fit formulas are expected to provide reliable theoretical
predictions for the form factors in a broader range
0<k^{\prime 2}<M_{J/\psi}^{2}
and
-5\,{\rm GeV}^{2}<k^{\prime 2}<M_{\phi}^{2}
.
Fig.
3
shows
R_{i}(0,q^{2})
and
R_{i}(q^{2},0)
related to

decays.

(iv) The contribution of factorizable charm in

decay may be treated as the

-dependent

C_{9}
, such that
\Delta^{\rm F}C_{9}(q^{2})/C_{9}>0

At the same time, the contribution of nonfactorizable charm in

Appendix A
Constraints on the transition form factors

We present here a discussion of the constraints imposed by the electromagnetic gauge invariance on the

\langle\gamma^{*}|\bar{q}O_{i}b|\bar{B}_{q}(p)\rangle
transition amplitudes induced by the vector, axial-vector, tensor, and pseudotensor weak currents.
This discussion extends the discussion of
[
41
]
and includes also the case when the real photon is emitted from the
FCNC
b\to q
vertex. The corresponding form factors are functions of two variables,
k^{\prime 2}
and
k^{2}
,
where
k^{\prime}
is the momentum of the weak
b\to q
current,
and
k
is the momentum of the electromagnetic current,
p=k+k^{\prime}
.
Gauge invariance provides constraints on some of the form factors describing the transition
of
B_{q}
to the real photon emitted directly from the quark line, i.e. for the form factors at
k^{2}=0
.

These form factors fully determine the amplitudes of the FCNC
B
-decays into leptons in the final state.
For instance, the four-lepton decay of the
B
meson requires the form factors
f_{i}(k^{\prime 2},k^{\prime 2})
for
0<k^{2},k^{\prime 2}<M_{B}^{2}
.
For the case of the
B\to\gamma l^{+}l^{-}
transition one needs the form factors
f_{i}(k^{\prime 2}=q^{2},k^{\prime 2}=0)
and
f_{i}(k^{\prime 2}=0,k^{\prime 2}=q^{2})
, where

q
is the momentum of the
l^{+}l^{-}
pair.

A.1
Form factors of the vector weak current

In case of the vector FCNC current, the gauge-invariant amplitude contains one form factor
g(k^{\prime 2},k^{2})
:

\displaystyle T_{\alpha,\mu}=i\int dxe^{ikx}\langle 0|T\left\{j^{\rm e.m.}_{\alpha}(x),\bar{q}\gamma_{\mu}b(0)\right\}|\bar{B}_{q}(p)\rangle=e\,\epsilon_{\mu\alpha k^{\prime}k}2g(k^{\prime 2},k^{2}).

(A.70)

The amplitude is automatically transverse and is free of the kinematic singularities so no constraints on
g(k^{\prime 2},k^{2})
emerge.

A.2
Form factors of the axial-vector weak current

For the axial-vector current, the corresponding amplitude has three independent gauge-invariant structures and three form factors, and
in addition has the contact term which is fully determined by the conservation of the electromagnetic current,
\partial^{\mu}j_{\mu}^{e.m.}=0
:

\displaystyle T^{5}_{\alpha,\mu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle i\int dxe^{ikx}\langle 0|T\left\{j^{\rm e.m.}_{\alpha}(x),\bar{q}\gamma_{\mu}\gamma_{5}b(0)\right\}|\bar{B}_{q}(p)\rangle

(A.71)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle ie\,\left(g_{\mu\alpha}-\frac{k_{\alpha}k_{\mu}}{k^{2}}\right)f(k^{\prime 2},k^{2})+ie\,\left(k^{\prime}_{\alpha}-\frac{kk^{\prime}}{k^{2}}k_{\alpha}\right)\bigg[p_{\mu}a_{1}(k^{\prime 2},k^{2})+k_{\mu}a_{2}(k^{\prime 2},k^{2})\bigg]+iQ_{B_{q}}e\,f_{B_{q}}\frac{k_{\alpha}p_{\mu}}{k^{2}}.

Here
Q_{\bar{B}_{q}}=Q_{b}-Q_{q}
is the electric charge of the
\bar{B}_{q}
meson and
f_{\bar{B}_{q}}>0
is defined according to

\displaystyle\langle 0|\bar{q}\gamma_{\mu}\gamma_{5}b|\bar{B}_{q}(p)\rangle=if_{\bar{B}_{q}}p_{\mu}.

(A.72)

The kinematical singularity in the projectors at
k^{2}=0
should not be the singularity of the amplitude, and therefore gauge invariance
yields the following relation between the form factors at
k^{2}=0
:

\displaystyle\left[f+(k^{\prime}k)a_{2}\right]_{k^{2}=0}=0,\qquad a_{1}(k^{\prime 2},k^{2}=0)=Q_{\bar{B}_{q}}f_{\bar{B}_{q}}.

(A.73)

For the neutral
\bar{B}_{d,s}
mesons, the contact term is absent and therefore the form factor
a_{1}
should vanish at

k^{2}=0
,
a_{1}(k^{\prime 2},k^{2}=0)=0
. This relation is fulfilled automatically, as the two contributions, corresponding to the
the photon emission from the valence
b
-quark and from the valence
s,d
-quark cancel each other at
k^{2}=0
.

The amplitude of the transition to the real photon is described by a single form factor

\displaystyle\langle\gamma(k)|\bar{q}\gamma_{\mu}\gamma_{5}b|\bar{B}_{q}(p)\rangle=-ie\varepsilon^{\alpha}(k)\left(g_{\mu\alpha}k^{\prime}k-k^{\prime}_{\alpha}k_{\mu}\right)a_{2}(k^{\prime 2},k^{2}=0).

(A.74)

A.3
Form factors of the tensor weak current

The transition amplitudes induced by the tensor weak current can be decomposed in the Lorentz structures transverse
with respect to
k_{\alpha}
:

\displaystyle T_{\alpha,\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle i\int dxe^{ikx}\langle 0|\left\{Tj_{\alpha}^{e.m.}(x),\bar{q}\sigma_{\mu\nu}b(0)\right\}|\bar{B}_{q}(p)\rangle

(A.75)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle ie\,\left(\epsilon_{\mu\nu\alpha p}-\frac{k_{\alpha}}{k^{2}}\epsilon_{\mu\nu kp}\right)g_{1}(k^{\prime 2},k^{2})+ie\,\epsilon_{\mu\nu\alpha k}g_{2}(k^{\prime 2},k^{2})+ie\,\left(p_{\alpha}-\frac{pk}{k^{2}}k_{\alpha}\right)\epsilon_{\mu\nu k^{\prime}k}g_{0}(k^{\prime 2},k^{2}),

The contact terms are absent in this amplitude as well as in the amplitude of the pseudotensor current.
The kinematic singularity of the projectors at
k^{2}=0
should not be the singularity of the amplitude, therefore

\displaystyle\left[g_{1}-(kp)g_{0}\right]_{k^{2}=0}=0.

(A.76)

Multiplying (
A.75
) by
k^{\prime}_{\nu}
, we obtain the penguin transition amplitude

\displaystyle i\int dxe^{ikx}\langle 0|\left\{Tj_{\alpha}^{e.m.}(x),\bar{q}\sigma_{\mu\nu}k^{\prime\nu}b(0)\right\}|\bar{B}_{q}(p)\rangle=ie\,\epsilon_{\mu\alpha kp}(g_{1}+g_{2}).

(A.77)

Notice that the penguin amplitude contains only one combination of the form factors. Nevertheless,
the requirement of the regularity of the amplitude (
A.75
) yields the constraint (
A.76
).

A.4
Form factors of the pseudotensor weak current

The transition amplitude of the pseudotensor weak current is given in terms of the same form factors as the amplitude (
A.75
),
and, similar to (
A.75
), contains no contact terms:

\displaystyle T^{5}_{\alpha,\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle i\int dxe^{ikx}\langle 0|\left\{Tj_{\alpha}^{e.m.}(x),\bar{q}\sigma_{\mu\nu}\gamma_{5}b(0)\right\}|\bar{B}_{q}(p)\rangle

(A.78)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bigg[\bigg(g_{\alpha\nu}-\frac{k_{\alpha}k_{\nu}}{k^{2}}\bigg)p_{\mu}-\bigg(g_{\alpha\mu}-\frac{k_{\alpha}k_{\mu}}{k^{2}}\bigg)p_{\nu}\bigg]e\,g_{1}+(g_{\alpha\nu}k_{\mu}-g_{\alpha\mu}k_{\nu})e\,g_{2}+\bigg(p_{\alpha}-\frac{k\cdot p}{k^{2}}k_{\alpha}\bigg)(k_{\mu}p_{\nu}-p_{\nu}k_{\mu})e\,g_{0}.

The kinematical singularity in the projectors at
k^{2}=0
should cancel in the amplitude, again leading to the constraint
Eq. (
A.76
).

For the penguin pseudotensor amplitude we then obtain

\displaystyle i\int dxe^{ikx}\langle 0|\left\{Tj_{\alpha}^{e.m.}(x),\bar{q}\sigma_{\mu\nu}\gamma_{5}k^{\prime\nu}b(0)\right\}|\bar{B}_{q}(p)\rangle

\displaystyle=e\,\left(k^{\prime}_{\alpha}k_{\mu}-g_{\alpha\mu}kk^{\prime}\right)\left\{g_{1}+g_{2}+\frac{k^{\prime 2}}{kk^{\prime}}g_{1}\right\}+e\,\left(k^{\prime}_{\alpha}-\frac{kk^{\prime}}{k^{2}}k_{\alpha}\right)\left(k_{\mu}-\frac{kk^{\prime}}{k^{\prime 2}}k^{\prime}_{\mu}\right)\frac{k^{\prime 2}}{kk^{\prime}}\left\{kk^{\prime}g_{0}-g_{1}\right\}.

(A.79)

Notice that the contribution of the second Lorentz structure in (
A.79
) vanishes both for

k^{2}=0
(because of the constraint Eq. (
A.76
): at
k^{\prime 2}=0
,
kp=kk^{\prime}
) and for
k^{\prime 2}=0
.
However, it does not vanish for both
k^{2},k^{\prime 2}\neq 0
; therefore, the second Lorentz structure contributes to the
amplitude of the four-lepton decays.

We can now build the bridge to the form factors which describe the real photon emission by the valence quarks
defined in Eq. (
2.6
): denoting the momentum of the
l^{+}l^{-}
pair as
q
, i.e., setting
k^{2}=0

and replacing
k^{\prime 2}\to q^{2}
, we obtain the form factors in Eq. (
2.6
) through the form factors

g,a_{2},g_{2},g_{1}(k^{\prime 2}=q^{2},k^{2}=0)
:

\displaystyle F_{V}(q^{2},0)=2M_{B}g(q^{2},0),\qquad F_{A}(q^{2},0)=-M_{B}a_{2}(q^{2},0),

(A.80)

\displaystyle F_{TV}(q^{2},0)=-\left[g_{2}(q^{2},0)+g_{1}(q^{2},0)\right],\qquad F_{TA}(q^{2},0)=-\left[g_{2}(q^{2},0)+\frac{M_{B}^{2}+q^{2}}{M_{B}^{2}-q^{2}}g_{1}(q^{2},0)\right].

(A.81)

The form factors describing the real photon emission from the penguin, are obtained by setting
k^{\prime 2}=0
and replacing

k^{2}\to q^{2}
in the form factors
g_{1,2}(k^{\prime 2},k^{2})
:

\displaystyle F_{TV}(0,q^{2})=F_{TA}(0,q^{2})=-[g_{2}(0,q^{2})+g_{1}(0,q^{2})].

(A.82)

Appendix B
Derivation of

Here we provide the derivation of Eq. (

Our starting point is the matrix element

\displaystyle H_{\mu\alpha}(k^{\prime},k)=i\int dze^{ik^{\prime}z}\langle 0|T\{eQ_{c}\bar{c}\gamma_{\mu}c(z),eQ_{s}\bar{s}\gamma_{\alpha}s(0)\}|B_{s}(p)\rangle,\qquad p=k^{\prime}+k,

where quark operators are Heisenberg operators in the SM, i.e. the corresponding

(B.84)

at

by shifting coordinates

{\cal O}(x)=e^{i\hat{P}y}{\cal O}(x-y)e^{-iy\hat{P}}
.
Using the relations

\langle 0|e^{i(\hat{P}y)}=\langle 0|

,

\displaystyle H^{\rm NF}_{\mu\alpha}(k^{\prime},k)=i^{3}\,e^{2}\,Q_{c}Q_{s}\int dxdydz\>e^{ik^{\prime}z+iky}\langle 0|T\{\bar{c}(z)\gamma_{\mu}c(z),L^{b\to s\bar{c}c[8\times 8]}_{\text{weak}}(0),L_{Gcc}(x),\bar{s}(y)\gamma_{\eta}s(y)\}|\bar{B}_{s}(p)\rangle.

(B.85)

\displaystyle\hskip 113.81102pt\times\langle 0|T\left\{\bar{s}(y)\gamma_{\alpha}s(y),\bar{s}(0)\gamma_{\beta}(1-\gamma_{5})t^{a}b(0),B^{b}_{\nu}(x)\right\}|\bar{B}_{s}(p)\rangle

(B.87)

This allows us to isolate the contribution of the charm-quark loop
\Gamma^{\beta\nu\mu(ab)}_{cc}(\kappa,q)

\displaystyle H^{\rm NF}_{\mu\alpha}(k^{\prime},k)

\displaystyle=

Appendix C
Numerical results for the form factors
R_{A,V}(k^{\prime 2},k^{2})

We have calculated the form factors
R_{i}(k^{\prime 2},k^{2})
in the region
-5<k^{2}({\rm GeV}^{2})<0
and
0<k^{\prime 2}({\rm GeV}^{2})<4m_{c}^{2}
.
However, calculations based on finite-order QCD diagrams cannot be trusted near quark thresholds
(for instance, the calculated form factors exhibit steep rise at
k^{2}\to 0
which is unphysical,
as the nearest meson pole lies at
k^{2}=M_{\phi}^{2}
and the two-meson threshold lies at
k^{2}=4M_{K}^{2}
).
So we pursue the following strategy:
We make use of the results of our calculation in the restricted rectangular region
-5<k^{2}(GeV^{2})<-0.6
and
0<k^{\prime 2}(GeV^{2})<4

(i.e., relatively far from quark thresholds) and interpolate them by a simple analytic formula which
takes into account the presence of the poles at
k^{\prime 2}=M_{J/\psi}^{2}
and
k^{2}=M_{\phi}^{2}
. Numerical parameters in this formula
are obtained by the fit in the mentioned restricted area.

For the form factors
H_{V}
and
H_{A}
we use the following fitting function:

\displaystyle R_{A,V}(y_{1},y_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{R_{00}}{(1-y_{1})(1-y_{2})\big(1-g_{11}y_{1}-g_{12}y_{1}^{2}\big)\bigg(1-(a_{20}+a_{21}y_{1}+a_{22}y_{1}^{2})y_{2}+(b_{20}+b_{21}y_{1}+b_{22}y_{1}^{2})y_{2}^{2}\bigg)},

(C.91)

\displaystyle y_{1}\equiv k^{\prime 2}/M^{2}_{J/\psi},\;y_{2}\equiv k^{2}/M^{2}_{\phi}.

This formula takes into account the correct location of meson poles at
k^{\prime 2}=M^{2}_{J/\psi}
and
k^{2}=M^{2}_{\phi}
.
The coefficients in this formula are obtained by
interpolation
in the region where our results may be trusted.

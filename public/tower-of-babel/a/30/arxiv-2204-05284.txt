Higgs physics confronts the
M

Abstract




1
Introduction

Electroweak precision observables
have played a crucial role to firmly establish the Standard Model (SM) as a quantum field theory
and to constrain possible New Physics (NP) extensions.
Remarkably, electroweak precision observables were instrumental to indirectly infer
the masses of the top quark and the Higgs boson well before their direct detection at Tevatron and LHC, respectively.
Nowadays, the SM electroweak fit is performed using as input parameters the fine structure constant
\alpha
, the muon decay constant
G_{\mu}
, the
Z
boson mass
M_{Z}
, the strong coupling
\alpha_{s}(M_{Z})
, the top quark mass
m_{t}
, the Higgs mass
M_{h}
, and the hadronic contribution to the running of
\alpha
, i.e.
\Delta\alpha^{5}_{\rm had}(M_{Z})
. In terms of these parameters, all other observables can be predicted.
In particular, the resulting value of the
W^{\pm}
boson mass from the electroweak fit is
M_{W}=80354.5\pm 5.7\,\text{MeV}

[
1
]
.

The CDF collaboration has recently published a high-precision measurement of
M_{W}=80433.5\pm 9.4\,\text{MeV}

[
2
]
, whose precision exceeds that of the current PDG world average,
M_{W}=80379\pm 12\,\text{MeV}

[
3
]
, obtained from the combination of all previous measurements from LEP, D0, CDF, and ATLAS. The new CDF value turns out to be considerably larger than the current
PDG world average as well as the value previously inferred from the SM electroweak fit
[
1
]
.

Taking the new CDF result at face value,
a few collaborations have already assessed its impact in the global electroweak fit, in the attempt of highlighting the favoured NP scenario to solve this anomaly
(see e.g.
[
4
,
5
,
6
,
7
]
). In particular, it turned out that universal NP models,
which are fully described by the famous
\hat{S},\hat{T},U,W,\text{and}\,Y

parameters
[
8
,
9
,
10
]
,
provide an overall good quality of the fit.
The viable solutions prefer either
\hat{T}\approx 10^{-3}
and
\hat{S}=U=W=Y=0
or highly-correlated positive
\hat{S}
and
\hat{T}
parameters
of comparable size
\hat{T}\sim\hat{S}\sim 10^{-3}
and
U=W=Y=0
. If
\hat{S}
and
\hat{T}
are loop-induced, they are of order
(g^{4}_{\rm NP}/16\pi^{2})\times M^{2}_{W}/M^{2}_{\rm{NP}}
and therefore, weakly-interacting theories require
M_{\rm NP}
to lie at the electroweak scale to accommodate the
M_{W}
anomaly.
Such a solution can be hardly reconciled with the direct-search bounds on new particles. Instead, tree-level NP effects –which are equivalent
to the effects stemming from a strongly-coupled sector with
g_{\rm{NP}}\sim 4\pi
– can provide the desired values of

\hat{S}
and
\hat{T}
even for
M_{\rm{NP}}\sim 10
TeV.

The primary goal of this work is to establish a
connection between the NP effects entering
M_{W}
and Higgs physics observables.
Indeed, since
within the SM Effective Field Theory (SMEFT)

\hat{S}
and
\hat{T}

receive contributions respectively from
the
d=6
operators
(H^{\dagger}\tau^{a}H)W^{a}_{\mu\nu}B^{\mu\nu}
and

(H^{\dagger}D_{\mu}H)((D_{\mu}H)^{\dagger}H)
,
it seems rather natural that NP effects in
M_{W}
need
to be accompanied by modifications of the SM predictions for Higgs decay processes like
h\to\gamma\gamma,Z\gamma
and
h\to ZZ,WW
.
In Sect.
2

we quantitatively assess this connection
in the context of the SMEFT.
Another goal of the present analysis is to systematically classify explicit NP extensions of the SM which can give a sizeable contribution to
\hat{T}
at the tree level.
We provide this classification in Sect.
3

and, for those simplified models predicting a positive shift in
\hat{T}
, we
discuss accordingly the correlated signals in Higgs physics.
We conclude in Sect.
4
with a summary of our findings.

2
SMEFT approach to the
M_{W}
anomaly and Higgs physics

Parametrizing the SMEFT Lagrangian as

\mathcal{L}_{\rm SMEFT}=\mathcal{L}_{\rm SM}+\sum_{i}c_{i}\mathcal{O}_{i}\,,

(2.1)

where we adopt the Warsaw basis
[
11
]

and focus in particular on the following subset of operators,
which are relevant for
electroweak and Higgs physics:

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}_{HW}

\displaystyle=(H^{\dagger}H)W^{a}_{\mu\nu}W^{a\mu\nu}\,,

(2.2)

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}_{HB}

\displaystyle=(H^{\dagger}H)B_{\mu\nu}B^{\mu\nu}\,,

(2.3)

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}_{HWB}

\displaystyle=(H^{\dagger}\tau^{a}H)W^{a}_{\mu\nu}B^{\mu\nu}\,,

(2.4)

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}_{HD}

\displaystyle=(H^{\dagger}D_{\mu}H)((D_{\mu}H)^{\dagger}H)\,,

(2.5)

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}_{H}

\displaystyle=(H^{\dagger}H)^{3}\,,

(2.6)

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}_{H\Box}

\displaystyle=(H^{\dagger}H)\Box(H^{\dagger}H)\,,

(2.7)

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}_{eH}

\displaystyle=(H^{\dagger}H)\overline{\ell}_{L}e_{R}H\,,

(2.8)

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}_{uH}

\displaystyle=(H^{\dagger}H)\overline{q}_{L}u_{R}\tilde{H}\,,

(2.9)

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}_{dH}

\displaystyle=(H^{\dagger}H)\overline{q}_{L}d_{R}H\,,

(2.10)

with the covariant derivative defined as

D_{\mu}=\partial_{\mu}+ig_{2}W^{a}_{\mu}\tau^{a}+ig_{1}B_{\mu}Y
.
Employing the notation of Refs.
[
10
,
6
]
,
the leading electroweak oblique corrections

\displaystyle\hat{S}

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{c_{W}}{s_{W}}\Pi^{\prime}(0)_{W_{3}B}=\frac{c_{W}}{s_{W}}v^{2}c_{HWB}\,,

(2.11)

\displaystyle\hat{T}

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{1}{M^{2}_{W}}(\Pi_{W_{3}W_{3}}(0)-\Pi_{W^{+}W^{-}}(0))=-\frac{v^{2}}{2}c_{HD}\,,

(2.12)

with
v=246

s_{W}\equiv\sin\theta_{W}

(

).

so-called “universal” bosonic operators.

Concretely, in terms of the Warsaw basis these are four-fermion operators as well as operators of the type
(H\overleftrightarrow{D}_{\mu}H)(\overline{\psi}\gamma^{\mu}\psi)
. These operators can also lead to contributions to electroweak precision observables beyond the oblique parameters
(with the exception of top-quark operators

by electroweak

[

.
)
and hence are neglected in the present analysis.

The
M_{W}
anomaly could be due to a universal new physics correction
to
\hat{T}

[
6
]

\hat{T}\simeq(0.84\pm 0.14)\times 10^{-3}\,,

(2.13)

(
c_{HD}=-(0.17\pm 0.07/\text{TeV})^{2}
)
as well as a correlated contribution to

\hat{S}\sim 10^{-3}

(

of the same size of
\hat{T}
, but
compatible with zero
[
6

7

.
The inclusion of higher-order corrections

(
Y
and

[

, while
a non-vanishing
\hat{U}
parameter
can also explain by itself
the
M_{W}
anomaly
[
7
]
.
However, under the assumption of heavy NP,
which is captured by the SMEFT
description,
the
\hat{U}

parameter is usually neglected
since it arises from
d=8
operators.

Since the
\hat{S}
and
\hat{T}
parameters are obtained
by condensing the Higgs fields in
\mathcal{O}_{HWB}

and
\mathcal{O}_{HD}

clearly a

as highlighted schematically in Fig.

.

Writing the SMEFT Lagrangian in the
electroweak broken phase as

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{\rm int}_{\rm SMEFT}

\displaystyle\ni g_{hWW}^{(1)}hW^{+}_{\mu}W^{-\mu}+g_{hWW}^{(2)}hW^{+}_{\mu\nu}W^{-\mu\nu}+g_{hZZ}^{(1)}hZ_{\mu}Z^{\mu}+g_{hZZ}^{(2)}hZ_{\mu\nu}Z^{\mu\nu}+g_{h\gamma\gamma}hF_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle+g_{h\gamma Z}hF_{\mu\nu}Z^{\mu\nu}+g_{hhh}h^{3}+(g_{he}h\overline{e}_{L}e_{R}+g_{hu}h\overline{u}_{L}u_{R}+g_{hd}h\overline{d}_{L}d_{R}+\text{h.c.})+\ldots\,,

(2.14)

one finds at tree level (see e.g.
[
17
]
)

\displaystyle g_{hWW}^{(1)}

\displaystyle=\frac{2M_{W}^{2}}{v}\left(1-\frac{v^{2}}{4}(c_{HD}-4c_{H\Box})\right)\,,

(2.15)

\displaystyle g_{hWW}^{(2)}

\displaystyle=2vc_{HW}\,,

(2.16)

\displaystyle g_{hZZ}^{(1)}

\displaystyle=\frac{M^{2}_{Z}}{v}\left(1+\frac{v^{2}}{4}(c_{HD}+4c_{H\Box})\right)\,,

(2.17)

\displaystyle g_{hZZ}^{(2)}

\displaystyle=v\left[\frac{M_{W}^{2}}{M_{Z}^{2}}c_{HW}+\frac{M_{Z}^{2}-M_{W}^{2}}{M_{Z}^{2}}c_{HB}+\frac{g_{1}g_{2}}{g_{1}^{2}+g_{2}^{2}}c_{HWB}\right]\,,

(2.18)

\displaystyle g_{h\gamma\gamma}

\displaystyle=v\left[\frac{M_{Z}^{2}-M_{W}^{2}}{M_{Z}^{2}}c_{HW}+\frac{M_{W}^{2}}{M_{Z}^{2}}c_{HB}-\frac{g_{1}g_{2}}{g_{1}^{2}+g_{2}^{2}}c_{HWB}\right]\,,

(2.19)

\displaystyle g_{h\gamma Z}

\displaystyle=2v\left[\frac{g_{1}g_{2}}{g_{1}^{2}+g_{2}^{2}}c_{HW}-\frac{g_{1}g_{2}}{g_{1}^{2}+g_{2}^{2}}c_{HB}+\frac{1}{2}\frac{g_{1}^{2}-g_{2}^{2}}{g_{1}^{2}+g_{2}^{2}}c_{HWB}\right]\,,

(2.20)

\displaystyle g_{hhh}

\displaystyle=-\frac{M^{2}_{h}}{2v}\left[1-\frac{3v^{2}}{4}(c_{HD}-4c_{H\Box})-\frac{2v^{4}}{M_{h}^{2}}c_{H}\right]\,,

(2.21)

\displaystyle g_{h\psi}

\displaystyle=-\frac{m_{\psi}}{v}\left(1-\frac{v^{2}}{4}(c_{HD}-4c_{H\Box})\right)+\frac{c_{\psi H}v^{2}}{\sqrt{2}}\,,

(2.22)

with
\psi=e,u,d

In order to canonically normalize the Higgs kinetic term we adopted the field redefinition

h\to h\left(1+v^{2}(c_{H\Box}-\frac{1}{4}c_{HD})\left(1+\frac{h}{v}+\frac{h^{2}}{3v^{2}}\right)\right)\,,

(2.23)

which removes the momentum-dependence in the Higgs self-couplings.

The most relevant Higgs observables, which are affected by the presence of non-zero
\hat{S},\hat{T}\sim 10^{-3}

are the Higgs boson decays into vector bosons.
Defining the Higgs signal strengths as

(2.25)

\displaystyle\mu_{Z\gamma}

3.1
\Delta\sim(1,3,0)_{\mathcal{S}}

From the interaction Lagrangian

\mathcal{L}_{\Delta}^{\rm int}\ni-\kappa_{\Delta}H^{\dagger}\Delta^{a}\sigma^{a}H-\frac{\lambda_{H\Delta}}{2}(H^{\dagger}H)\Delta^{a}\Delta^{a}\,,

(3.3)

(3.4)

and hence

\hat{T}=\frac{\kappa^{2}_{\Delta}v^{2}}{M_{\Delta}^{4}}=0.84\times 10^{-3}\left(\frac{|\kappa_{\Delta}|}{M_{\Delta}}\right)^{2}\left(\frac{8.5\,\text{TeV}}{M_{\Delta}}\right)^{2}\,,

M_{W}
anomaly.

When the
\Delta

the tree-level contribution to
\hat{T}

can be alternatively understood to arise from
the generation of a
tree-level vacuum expectation value (VEV) for
\Delta

that is
\left\langle\Delta\right\rangle\equiv v_{\Delta}=\kappa_{\Delta}v^{2}/(2M^{2}_{\Delta})
.
In general, the VEV of a scalar representation
\mathcal{S}\sim(1,2j+1,y)
yields
[

,
27

\hat{T}\simeq 4\left(\eta[j(j+1)-y^{2}]-2y^{2}\right)\frac{\left\langle\mathcal{S}\right\rangle^{2}}{v^{2}}\,,

(3.6)

with
\left\langle\mathcal{S}\right\rangle=\alpha v_{\mathcal{S}}

\alpha=1
(
\alpha=1/\sqrt{2}
)
for a real (complex) representation and

is the VEV of the canonically normalized real scalar component of
\mathcal{S}
.
In the case
\mathcal{S}=\Delta

\hat{T}\simeq\kappa^{2}_{\Delta}v^{2}/M_{\Delta}^{4}

3.5

The connection between this scalar triplet and electroweak
precision measurements was previously considered e.g. in Refs.
[

29

30

Note that the perturbativity range of the massive
\kappa_{\Delta}

\Delta H^{\dagger}H

the tree-level value
[
31
]
.
This yields
|\kappa_{\Delta}|/M_{\Delta}\lesssim 4\pi

[
32

.
Hence, a scalar triplet well above the TeV scale and with perturbative couplings
can explain the value of
\hat{T}
while
easily evading all direct collider searches.
In particular, saturating the perturbativity bound, it turns out that

\displaystyle c_{H}

\displaystyle=-4\frac{\kappa_{\Delta}^{2}}{M_{\Delta}^{4}}\left(\frac{\lambda_{H\Delta}}{8}-\lambda\right)=-4\frac{\hat{T}}{v^{2}}\left(\frac{\lambda_{H\Delta}}{8}-\lambda\right)\,,

\displaystyle=\frac{\kappa_{\Delta}^{2}}{2M_{\Delta}^{4}}=\frac{\hat{T}}{2v^{2}}\,,

\displaystyle c_{eH,\,uH,\,dH}

can be correlated to various Higgs signals

2

The strongest dependence on
\hat{T}

\Delta_{1}\sim(1,3,1)_{\mathcal{S}}

3.3
\Theta_{1}\sim(1,4,1/2)_{\mathcal{S}}

Electroweak quadruplets contribute to
\hat{T}
via the

(H^{\dagger}H)\mathcal{O}_{HD}
.
5
5

5

tree-level contributions to the
\hat{S}
and
\hat{U}
parameters at
d=8

[
34

.

It is hence more practical to
directly

\hat{T}
via the VEV contribution.
We consider the interaction Lagrangian

\mathcal{L}^{\rm int}_{\Theta_{1}}\ni M^{2}_{\Theta_{1}}(\Theta_{1})_{ijk}(\Theta^{*}_{1})^{ijk}-\lambda_{H3\Theta_{1}}H^{*i}(\Theta_{1})_{ijk}H^{*j}\epsilon^{kl}H_{l}+\text{h.c.}\,,

(3.13)

in a phase convention where
\lambda_{H3\Theta_{1}}
is real and

latin indices in
{\rm SU}(2)_{L}
space and

\epsilon=i\sigma^{2}
.
The embedding of the
canonically normalized
charge
eigenstates reads

(\Theta_{1})_{111}=\Theta^{++}_{1}
,

(\Theta_{1})_{112}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}\Theta^{+}_{1}
,

(\Theta_{1})_{122}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}\Theta^{0}_{1}
,

(\Theta_{1})_{222}=\Theta^{-}_{1}
.
In particular, for the VEV of the neutral component one obtains

\hat{T}\simeq 12\frac{\left\langle\Theta^{0}_{1}\right\rangle^{2}}{v^{2}}\simeq\frac{\lambda_{H3\Theta_{1}}^{2}v^{4}}{2M^{4}_{\Theta_{1}}}=0.84\times 10^{-3}\,\lambda_{H3\Theta_{1}}^{2}\left(\frac{1.2\,\text{TeV}}{M_{\Theta_{1}}}\right)^{4}\,,

(3.15)

which has the correct sign to explain the
M_{W}
anomaly.
Note that due to the different scaling of the
\hat{T}
parameter (compared e.g. to the triplet case in

\mathcal{O}(1)

At the leading order in the SMEFT, one also generates the

c_{H}=\frac{\lambda_{H3\Theta_{1}}^{2}}{M^{2}_{\Theta_{1}}}=\frac{2M^{2}_{\Theta_{1}}}{v^{4}}\hat{T}=\frac{0.040}{v^{2}}\left(\frac{M_{\Theta_{1}}}{1.2\,\text{TeV}}\right)^{2}\left(\frac{\hat{T}}{0.84\times 10^{-3}}\right)\,,

(3.16)

which is directly correlated with

\hat{T}
.
Substituting the above value of
c_{H}
into Eq. (
2.21
) we then obtain

\frac{g_{hhh}}{g^{\rm SM}_{hhh}}-1=-31\%\left(\frac{M_{\Theta_{1}}}{1.2\,\text{TeV}}\right)^{2}\left(\frac{\hat{T}}{0.84\times 10^{-3}}\right)\,,

(3.17)

which implies up to an
\mathcal{O}(1)
variation in the trilinear Higgs self-coupling,
depending on the value of
M_{\Theta_{1}}
, which receives an upper bound from perturbativity
(see e.g.
[
23
]
).
All in all, the reason for such a large effect in the trilinear Higgs self-coupling
can be understood from the fact that in the quadruplet case
\hat{T}
is
generated by a
d=8
operator, while the contribution to
c_{H}
arises at
d=6
.

3.4
\Theta_{3}\sim(1,4,3/2)_{\mathcal{S}}

We consider the interaction Lagrangian

\mathcal{L}^{\rm int}_{\Theta_{3}}\ni M^{2}_{\Theta_{3}}(\Theta_{3})_{ijk}(\Theta^{*}_{3})^{ijk}-\lambda_{H3\Theta_{1}}H^{*i}H^{*j}H^{*k}(\Theta_{3})_{ijk}+\text{h.c.}\,,

(3.18)

in a phase convention where
\lambda_{H3\Theta_{3}}
is real.
The
embedding of the
canonically normalized
charge
eigenstates reads

(\Theta_{3})_{111}=\Theta^{+++}_{3}
,

(\Theta_{3})_{112}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}\Theta^{++}_{3}
,

(\Theta_{3})_{122}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}\Theta^{+}_{3}
,

(\Theta_{3})_{222}=\Theta^{0}_{3}
.
For the VEV of the neutral component one obtains

\left\langle\Theta^{0}_{3}\right\rangle\equiv\frac{v_{\Theta_{3}}}{\sqrt{2}}\simeq\frac{\lambda_{H3\Theta_{3}}v^{3}}{2\sqrt{2}M^{2}_{\Theta_{3}}}\,,

(3.19)

and hence, using the VEV formula in Eq. (
3.6
)

\hat{T}\simeq-12\frac{\left\langle\Theta^{0}_{3}\right\rangle^{2}}{v^{2}}\simeq-\frac{3\lambda_{H3\Theta_{3}}^{2}v^{4}}{2M^{4}_{\Theta_{3}}}\,,

(3.20)

which predicts the wrong sign to solve the
M_{W}
anomaly.

3.5
{\cal B}\sim(1,1,0)_{\cal V}

From the interaction Lagrangian

\mathcal{L}^{\rm int}_{{\cal B}}\ni-g_{\cal B}^{H}{\cal B}^{\mu}H^{\dagger}iD_{\mu}H+\text{h.c.}\,,

(3.21)

one obtains

c_{HD}=-2\frac{(\text{Re}\,(g_{\cal B}^{H}))^{2}}{M^{2}_{{\cal B}}}\,,

(3.22)

and hence

\hat{T}=\frac{(\text{Re}\,(g_{\cal B}^{H}))^{2}v^{2}}{M^{2}_{{\cal B}}}=0.84\times 10^{-3}\,(\text{Re}\,(g_{\cal B}^{H}))^{2}\left(\frac{8.5\,\text{TeV}}{M_{{\cal B}}}\right)^{2}\,,

(3.23)

which has the correct sign to explain the

M_{W}
anomaly.
The possibility of raising the
M_{W}
mass via a
Z^{\prime}

boson was previously considered e.g. in

[
35
,
6
]
.
Note that the
Z^{\prime}
phenomenology
highly depends on its
coupling to SM fermions, which have not been specified here.

3.6
{\cal B}_{1}\sim(1,1,1)_{\cal V}

From the interaction Lagrangian

\mathcal{L}^{\rm int}_{{\cal B}_{1}}\ni-g_{{\cal B}_{1}}^{H}{\cal B}_{1}^{\mu{\dagger}}iD_{\mu}H^{T}i\sigma^{2}H+\text{h.c.}\,,

(3.24)

one obtains

c_{HD}=\frac{|g_{{\cal B}_{1}}^{H}|^{2}}{M^{2}_{{\cal B}_{1}}}\,,

(3.25)

and hence

\hat{T}=-\frac{|g_{{\cal B}_{1}}^{H}|^{2}v^{2}}{2M^{2}_{{\cal B}_{1}}}\,,

(3.26)

which predicts the wrong sign to accommodate the
M_{W}
anomaly.

3.7
{\cal W}\sim(1,3,0)_{\cal V}

From the interaction Lagrangian

\mathcal{L}^{\rm int}_{{\cal W}}\ni-\frac{1}{2}g_{\cal W}^{H}{\cal W}^{\mu a}H^{\dagger}\sigma^{a}iD_{\mu}H+\text{h.c.}\,,

(3.27)

one obtains

c_{HD}=\frac{(\text{Im}\,(g_{\cal W}^{H}))^{2}}{2M^{2}_{{\cal W}}}\,,

(3.28)

and hence

\hat{T}=-\frac{(\text{Im}\,(g_{\cal W}^{H}))^{2}v^{2}}{4M^{2}_{{\cal W}}}\,,

(3.29)

which predicts the wrong sign to accommodate the
M_{W}
anomaly.

3.8
{\cal W}_{1}\sim(1,3,1)_{\cal V}

From the interaction Lagrangian

\mathcal{L}^{\rm int}_{{\cal W}_{1}}\ni-\frac{1}{2}g_{{\cal W}_{1}}^{H}{\cal W}_{1}^{\mu a{\dagger}}iD_{\mu}H^{T}i\sigma^{2}\sigma^{a}H+\text{h.c.}\,,

(3.30)

one obtains

c_{HD}=-\frac{|g_{{\cal W}_{1}}^{H}|^{2}}{4M^{2}_{{\cal W}_{1}}}\,,

(3.31)

and hence

\hat{T}=\frac{|g_{{\cal W}_{1}}^{H}|^{2}v^{2}}{8M^{2}_{{\cal W}_{1}}}=0.84\times 10^{-3}\,|g_{{\cal W}_{1}}^{H}|^{2}\left(\frac{3.0\,\text{TeV}}{M_{{\cal W}_{1}}}\right)^{2}\,,

(3.32)

which has the correct sign to explain the
M_{W}

anomaly.
Note that
the interaction in Eq. (
3.30
)
does not necessarily arise
from a renormalizable theory
and here we have included only
d=4

interactions with the SM fields.

3.9
{\cal L}\sim(1,2,1/2)_{\cal V}

From the interaction Lagrangian

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{\rm int}_{{\cal L}}

\displaystyle\ni-(\gamma_{\cal L}{\cal L}^{\dagger}D^{\mu}H+\text{h.c.})-ig^{B}_{\cal L}{\cal L}^{\dagger}_{\mu}{\cal L}_{\nu}B^{\mu\nu}-ig^{W}_{\cal L}{\cal L}^{\dagger}_{\mu}\sigma^{a}{\cal L}_{\nu}W^{a\mu\nu}-h^{(2)}_{\cal L}{\cal L}^{\dagger}HH^{\dagger}{\cal L}

\displaystyle-(h^{(3)}_{\cal L}({\cal L}^{\dagger}H)^{2}+\text{h.c.})\,,

(3.33)

after an Higgs field redefinition in order to
have canonical kinetic terms (see
[
25
]
),
one obtains

c_{HD}=\frac{g_{1}g^{B}_{\cal L}|\gamma_{\cal L}|^{2}}{M_{\cal L}^{4}}-\frac{h^{(2)}_{\cal L}|\gamma_{\cal L}|^{2}}{M_{\cal L}^{4}}+\frac{2\text{Re}\,(h^{(3)}_{\cal L}\gamma^{*2}_{\cal L})}{M_{\cal L}^{4}}\,,

(3.34)

and hence

\hat{T}=-\frac{g_{1}g^{B}_{\cal L}|\gamma_{\cal L}|^{2}v^{2}}{2M_{\cal L}^{4}}+\frac{h^{(2)}_{\cal L}|\gamma_{\cal L}|^{2}v^{2}}{2M_{\cal L}^{4}}-\frac{\text{Re}\,(h^{(3)}_{\cal L}\gamma^{*2}_{\cal L})v^{2}}{M_{\cal L}^{4}}\,,

(3.35)

which can have both signs.
Moreover, after integrating out
{\cal L}
,
also the Wilson
coefficient

c_{WB}=-\frac{g_{1}g_{2}|\gamma_{\cal L}|^{2}}{4M_{\cal L}^{4}}-\frac{g_{2}g^{B}_{{\cal L}}|\gamma_{\cal L}|^{2}}{4M_{\cal L}^{4}}-\frac{g_{1}g^{W}_{{\cal L}}|\gamma_{\cal L}|^{2}}{4M_{\cal L}^{4}}

(3.36)

is generated,
which implies

\hat{S}=\frac{c_{W}}{s_{W}}\left[-\frac{g_{1}g_{2}|\gamma_{\cal L}|^{2}v^{2}}{4M_{\cal L}^{4}}-\frac{g_{2}g^{B}_{{\cal L}}|\gamma_{\cal L}|^{2}v^{2}}{4M_{\cal L}^{4}}-\frac{g_{1}g^{W}_{{\cal L}}|\gamma_{\cal L}|^{2}v^{2}}{4M_{\cal L}^{4}}\right]\,,

(3.37)

which is of the same order as the contribution to the
\hat{T}
parameter. Hence, this model could yield a large effect
in the
h\to\gamma\gamma
rate (as discussed in Sect.
2
).

4
Conclusions

In this work, we have investigated the connection
between the
M_{W}
anomaly
(stemming from the recent
M_{W}
measurement by
the CDF collaboration
[
2
]
)
and Higgs physics.
This connection is quite natural from the
point of view of the SMEFT,
since the

\hat{S}
and
\hat{T}
parameters arise from

d=6
operators containing multiple insertions of
the Higgs doublet set on its VEV.
Hence, by promoting one of those VEVs
to a dynamical field, one automatically predicts
modified Higgs signals (cf. Fig.
1
).
The largest effect turns out to be
in
h\to\gamma\gamma
, which is modified up to

+20\%
for values of
\hat{S}\sim 10^{-3}

which are generically suggested by the electroweak fit.
Modifications of the
h\to Z\gamma
rate as
well as the ratio
h\to ZZ/WW
(which might be used to
probe the
\hat{T}
parameter) are instead too small
to be presently detected.

Since a non-zero and positive
\hat{T}\sim 10^{-3}

is suggested by global electroweak fits,
in Sect.
3

we classified SM extensions which predict a
positive tree-level shift of
\hat{T}
.
Remarkably, there are only few solutions
with NP states that couple directly to the Higgs
via
d\leq 4
interactions:
a scalar triplet
\Delta\sim(1,3,0)_{\mathcal{S}}
,
a scalar quadruplet
\Theta_{1}\sim(1,4,1/2)_{\mathcal{S}}
,
a
Z^{\prime}
boson
{\cal B}\sim(1,1,0)_{\cal V}
,
a vector triplet
{\cal W}_{1}\sim(1,3,1)_{\cal V}

and a vector boson
{\cal L}\sim(1,2,1/2)_{\cal V}
.
In all these cases the value of
\hat{T}\sim 10^{-3}

can be easily explained via a NP state
with mass around 10 TeV
and
\mathcal{O}(1)
couplings to the Higgs
(barring the quadruplet case which needs to be at the TeV scale
and implies as well a large deviation in the trilinear Higgs self-coupling – see Eq. (
3.17
)).

Although the NP states implied by such scenarios can escape direct detection at particle colliders,
they might still leave their imprints via modifications of Higgs signals,
which become
especially
correlated in explicit models.

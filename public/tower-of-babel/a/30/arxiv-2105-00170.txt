Prescribed Webster scalar curvature flow on CR manifold
Pak Tung Ho, Quô ´ c Anh Ngô, Hong Zhang

Abstract
. Using the prescribed Webster scalar curvature flow, we prove some existence results on the 3-dimensional compact CR manifold with nonnegative CR Yamabe constant.

1.
introduction

Let
(M,\theta_{0})
be a compact strictly pseudoconvex CR manifold of real dimension
2n+1
with a background contact form
\theta_{0}
.
Similar to the Yamabe problem, the CR Yamabe problem is to find a contact form
\theta

conformal to
\theta_{0}
such that its Webster scalar curvature
is constant. This was first introduced by Jerison and Lee
in
[
10
]
, and was solved
in
[
4
,
5
,
11
,
12
,
9
,
20
]
.

The CR Yamabe flow is the geometric flow
introduced to solve the CR Yamabe problem. It is
defined as the evolution of
\theta(t)
:

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\theta(t)=-(R_{\theta(t)}-\overline{R}_{\theta(t)})\theta(t),

(1.1)

where
R_{\theta(t)}
is the Webster scalar curvature of the contact form
\theta(t)
, and
\overline{R}_{\theta(t)}
is the average of

R_{\theta(t)}
:

\overline{R}_{\theta(t)}=\frac{\int_{M}R_{\theta(t)}\dv}{\int_{M}\dv}.

We refer the readers to
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
13
,
16
,
21
,
22
,
29
]

for results related to the CR Yamabe flow.

Let
f
be a smooth function on
M
.
As a generalization of the CR Yamabe problem,
the prescribed Webster scalar curvature problem is to find a contact form
\theta
conformal to
\theta_{0}
such that the its Webster scalar curvature
R_{\theta}
is the given function
f
. If we write
\theta=u^{2/n}\theta_{0}
, then the prescribed Webster scalar curvature problem is equivalent to solving the semi-linear equation

-(2+2/n)\Delta_{\theta_{0}}u+\Ro u=fu^{1+2/n},

(1.2)

where
\Delta_{\theta_{0}}
and
\Ro
are, respectively, the sub-Laplacian and the Webster scalar curvature of the contact form
\theta_{0}
.
See
[
6
,
8
,
19
,
24
,
26
,
27
,
28
]
and references therein for results related to the prescribed Webster scalar curvature problem.

Similar to the CR Yamabe flow,
the prescribed Webster scalar curvature flow is introduced
to study the prescribed Webster scalar curvature problem,
and is defined as

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\theta(t)=-(R_{\theta(t)}-\lambda(t)f)\theta(t).

Note that when
f\equiv 1
, the prescribed Webster scalar curvature flow reduces to the CR Yamabe flow.
In
[
14
,
17
,
18
]
, the first author studied the
prescribed Webster scalar curvature flow on the sphere
S^{2n+1}
, when the prescribed function
f
is positive.
On the other hand, in
[
15
]
,
the first author studied the
prescribed Webster scalar curvature flow on the
3
-dimensional CR manifold
with negative CR Yamabe constant, when
f
is assumed to be negative.

In this paper, we study the prescribing Webster scalar problem
on
3
-dimensional CR manifold with nonnegative CR Yamabe constant.
By using the prescribed Webster scalar flow,
we prove some existence results. Unlike the results
mentioned above, the prescribed function
f
here in our existence results
is allowed to change sign. More precisely, our main results read as

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
(M,\theta_{0})
be a compact strictly pseudoconvex CR manifold of real dimension
3
with positive CR Yamabe constant. Suppose that
M
is not conformally equivalent to the CR sphere
\mathbb{S}^{3}
and the CR Paneitz operator of
M
is nonnegative. Also, let
f
be a smooth function on
(M,\theta_{0})
with
\sup_{M}f>0
. Then there exists a positive constant
c_{*}
depending only on
(M,\theta_{0})

such that if

\bigg\{x\in M:f(x)=\sup_{M}f\bigg\}\subseteq\bigg\{x\in M:f(x)>0~~\mbox{and}~~\Delta_{\theta_{0}}f(x)/f(x)>-c_{*}\bigg\},

(1.3)

then there exist a positive solution to the prescribed Webster scalar curvature equation (
1.2
).

Comparing Theorem
1.1
with
the result of Escobar and Schoen in
[
7
]

where
f
is only assumed to be positive somewhere in the dimension
3
, one can see that we need an extra condition (
1.3
), which shows the difference between the Riemannian case and the CR case.
The reason why we need this extra condition (
1.3
)
is as follows: In view of Lemma
5.15
below,
the dominant term is
O(\varepsilon^{2n})
, and the expansion of the prescribed function
f
around a blow-up point
a
is something like
f(a)+A\varepsilon+B\varepsilon^{2}+O(\varepsilon^{3})
, where
A
and
B
involve the gradient and Laplacian of
f
at
a
. When
n=1
, we can see that the dominant term is
O(\varepsilon^{2})
, but
\varepsilon^{2}
also appears in the expansion of
f
. Since
B
is generally not small, we would lose control if we do not impose any extra condition on
f
. While in the Riemannian case, the dominant term is
O(\varepsilon^{n-2})
, and the expansion of the prescribed function
f
is the same as above. When
n=3
, the the dominant term becomes
\varepsilon
. Although
\varepsilon
appears in the expansion of
f
, the coefficient
A
is
o(1)
as time goes to infinity, since
A
involves only gradient of
f
at
a
. Therefore, in the Riemannian case, one has full control without any extra condition.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
(M,\theta_{0})
be a compact strictly pseudoconvex CR manifold of real dimension
3
with vanishing CR Yamabe constant. Also, let
f
be a smooth function on
(M,\theta_{0})
satisfying

\sup_{M}f>0~~\mbox{and}~~\int_{M}f~\dv<0.

(1.4)

Then there exists a positive solution to the prescribed Webster scalar curvature equation (
1.2
).

We remark that Theorem
1.2
is sharp in the following sense:
if (
1.2
) is solvable on

(M,\theta_{0})
with vanishing CR Yamabe constant,
then either
f\equiv 0
or
f
must satisfies (
1.4
).
To see this, suppose that
u

is a positive solution to (
1.2
)
with
f\not\equiv 0
.
Since
(M,\theta_{0})
has vanishing CR Yamabe constant,
we may assume, by a conformal change if necessary,
that
\Ro\equiv 0
. Then (
1.2
) reduces to

-(2+2/n)\Delta_{\theta_{0}}u=fu^{1+2/n}.

(1.5)

Integrating (
1.5
) over
M
yields

\displaystyle\int_{M}fu^{1+2/n}\dv=0.

Since
u>0
and
f\not\equiv 0
, this implies that
f
changes sign,
and in particular,
\displaystyle\sup_{M}f>0
. On the other hand,
multiplying
u^{-1-2/n}
to (
1.5
) and integrating it over
M
,
we obtain

\int_{M}f\dv=-(2+2/n)(1+2/n)\int_{M}u^{-2-2/n}|\nabla_{\theta_{0}}u|_{\theta_{0}}\dv<0.

We also remark that most of the arguments in the proof
of Theorems
1.1
and
1.2

are still valid in dimension
2n+1
.
In fact, we wonder if Theorems
1.1
and
1.2

are still true when the dimension is
2n+1
.
We hope to come back to this in future.
As a result, we will try to write down the proof
in the general case when the dimension is
2n+1
,
and point out exactly the part when the assumption
n=1
is needed.

The method in this paper is inspired by Mayer
[
25
]
,
in which he considered the prescribed scalar curvature problem
on a closed
n
-dimensional Riemannian manifold of positive Yamabe invariant, where
3\leq n\leq 5
.

2.
Prescribed Webster scalar curvature flow

Let
(M,\theta_{0})
be a compact strictly pseudoconvex CR manifold of real dimension
2n+1
with a contact form
\theta_{0}
,
f
a smooth function on
M
.
As in Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
,
the CR Yamabe constant of
(M,\theta_{0})
is assumed to be nonnegative.
Therefore, by a conformal change if necessary, we may assume that the Webster scalar curvature of
\theta_{0}
is
positive (is zero respectively) when the CR Yamabe constant is positive (is zero respectively).

We consider the prescribed Webster scalar curvature flow, which is
the evolution equation of

\theta(t)
satisfying

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\theta(t)=-(R_{\theta(t)}-\lambda(t)f)\theta(t),\\[5.0pt]
\theta(0)=\theta^{0},\end{array}\right.

(2.1)

where
R_{\theta(t)}
is the Webster scalar curvature of the contact form
\theta(t)
,
\lambda(t)
is to be determined and
\theta^{0}
is an initial conformal contact form.
If we write
\theta(t)=u(t)^{2/n}\theta_{0}
and
\theta^{0}=u_{0}^{2/n}\theta_{0}
for some smooth functions
u(t)
and
u_{0}
, then we can obtain an evolution equation for
u(t)
from (
2.1
).

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\dfrac{\partial}{\partial t}u(t)=-\dfrac{n}{2}\big(R_{\theta(t)}-\lambda(t)f\big)u(t),\\
u(0)=u_{0}.\end{array}\right.

(2.2)

Here, in terms of
u
,
R_{\theta(t)}
can be written as

R_{\theta(t)}=u^{-1-\frac{2}{n}}\bigg(-\big(2+2/n\big)\Delta_{\theta_{0}}u(t)+R_{\theta_{0}}u(t)\bigg).

(2.3)

To deal with the case where
f
changes sign, it is convenient to consider the energy functional associated with the prescribed Webster scalar curvature problem

\E(u)=\int_{M}\Big(\big(2+2/n\big)|\nabla_{\theta_{0}}u|_{\theta_{0}}^{2}+R_{\theta_{0}}u^{2}\Big)\dv

(2.4)

with the constraint condition

X_{f}:=\Big\{u\in\mathbf{S}_{1}^{2}(M):u\geqslant 0,\int_{M}fu^{2+2/n}\dv=1\Big\}.

(2.5)

The constraint condition (
2.5
) above reminds us that we should keep the quantity
\int_{M}fu(t)^{2+2/n}\dv
unchanged during the evolution. That is

\displaystyle 0=\frac{d}{dt}\int_{M}fu(t)^{2+2/n}\dv=\big(2+2/n\big)\int_{M}fu_{t}u(t)^{1+2/n}\dv

\displaystyle\quad=-(n+1)\int_{M}\Big(fR_{\theta(t)}-\lambda(t)f^{2}\Big)\dvg.

(2.6)

Solving (
2
), we find that

\lambda(t)=\frac{\int_{M}fR_{\theta(t)}\dvg}{\int_{M}f^{2}\dvg}.

(2.7)

If we let
u_{0}\in X
, from (
2
) we then have

\int_{M}fu(t)^{2+2/n}\dv\equiv 1.

(2.8)

To end this section, we collect some well-known results for later reference.

(i)

Given a closed CR-manifold
(M,\theta)
, the CR-Yamabe constant, denoted by
\mu(M)
, is defined by (see
[
10
]
for example)

\mu(M)=\inf_{\big\{u\geqslant 0,~0\not\equiv u\in\mathbf{S}_{1}^{2}(M)\big\}}\frac{\int_{M}(2+2/n)|\nabla_{\theta}u|^{2}+R_{\theta}u^{2}\dvg}{\Big(\int_{M}u^{2+2/n}\dvg\Big)^{\frac{n}{n+1}}}.

(2.9)

Here,
\mathbf{S}_{1}^{2}(M)
is the Folland-Stein space of
(M,\theta)
.

(ii)

Given any
\varepsilon>0
, there exist
B>0
such that for
u\in\mathbf{S}_{1}^{2}(M)

\displaystyle\bigg(\int_{M}u^{2+2/n}\dvg\bigg)^{\frac{n}{n+1}}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\big(K_{n}+\varepsilon\big)\int_{M}|\nabla_{\theta}u|^{2}\dvg

(2.10)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad+B\int_{M}u^{2}\dvg,

where
K_{n}=1/(2\pi n^{2})
(see Theorem A.1 in
[
20
]
). In particular, there exists a positive constant
\mathscr{C}
such that

\bigg(\int_{M}u^{2+2/n}\dvg\bigg)^{\frac{n}{n+1}}\leqslant\mathscr{C}\bigg(\int_{M}(|\nabla_{\theta}u|^{2}+u^{2})\dvg\bigg).

(2.11)

3.
Global existence of the flow

Lemma 3.1
.

For a smooth solution
u(t)
of the flow (
2.2
) there holds

\frac{d}{dt}\E(u(t))=-n\int_{M}\Big(\R-\lambda(t)f\Big)^{2}\dvg.

In particular, the energy function
\E
is non-increasing along the flow.

Proof
.
By (
2.2
) and (
2.3
), we can compute

\begin{split}\frac{d}{dt}\E(u(t))&=2\int_{M}\big(2+2/n\big)\langle\nabla_{\theta_{0}}u,\nabla_{\theta_{0}}u_{t}\rangle+\Ro uu_{t}\dv\\
&=2\int_{M}\bigg(-\big(2+2/n\big)\Delta_{\theta_{0}}u+\Ro\bigg)u_{t}\dv\\
&=-n\int_{M}\R\Big(\R-\lambda(t)f\Big)\dvg\\
&=-n\int_{M}\Big(\R-\lambda(t)f\Big)^{2}\dvg,\end{split}

where we have used (
2
) in the last equality.

Lemma
3.1
implies a uniform priori bound

\E(u(t))+n\int_{0}^{t}\int_{M}(\R-\lambda(t)f)^{2}\dvg\mathrm{d}t\leqslant\E(u_{0}),\quad\mbox{ for all }~t>0.

(3.1)

Now, we derive the evolution equation for the Webster scalar curvature
\R
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Assume that
u(t)
is a smooth solution of the flow (
2.2
). Then one has

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}(\lambda(t)f-\R)=(n+1)\Delta_{\theta(t)}(\lambda(t)f-\R)+(\lambda(t)f-\R)\R+\lambda^{\prime}(t)f,

where
\lambda^{\prime}(t)
is given by

\begin{split}\lambda^{\prime}(t)&=-\bigg(\int_{M}f^{2}\dvg\bigg)^{-1}\bigg[(n+1)\int_{M}f\Delta_{\theta(t)}(\lambda(t)f-\R)\dvg\\
&\quad+n\int_{M}f(\lambda(t)f-\R)^{2}\dvg+\lambda(t)\int_{M}f^{2}(\lambda(t)f-\R)\dvg\bigg].\end{split}

Proof
.
Using (
2.3
) and (
2.2
) we have

\begin{split}\partial_{t}\R=&\Big(-1-\frac{2}{n}\Big)u^{-2-\frac{2}{n}}u_{t}\bigg(-\big(2+2/n\big)\Delta_{\theta_{0}}u+\Ro u\bigg)\\
&\quad+u^{-1-\frac{2}{n}}\bigg(-\big(2+2/n\big)\Delta_{\theta_{0}}u_{t}+\Ro u_{t}\bigg)\\
=&\Big(-\frac{n}{2}-1\Big)(\lambda(t)f-\R)\R-(n+1)u^{-1-\frac{2}{n}}\Delta_{\theta_{0}}\Big((\lambda(t)f-\R)u\Big)\\
&\quad+\frac{n}{2}u^{-\frac{2}{n}}\Ro(\lambda(t)f-\R)\\
=&\Big(-\frac{n}{2}-1\Big)(\lambda(t)f-\R)\R-(n+1)u^{-1-\frac{2}{n}}\Big[u\Delta_{\theta_{0}}(\lambda(t)f-\R)\\
&\quad+2\langle\nabla_{\theta_{0}}(\lambda(t)f-\R),\nabla_{\theta_{0}}u\rangle\Big]+\Big[-(n+1)u^{-1-\frac{2}{n}}(\lambda(t)f-\R)\Delta_{\theta_{0}}u\\
&\quad+\frac{n}{2}u^{-\frac{2}{n}}\Ro(\lambda(t)f-\R)\Big]\\
=&\Big(-\frac{n}{2}-1\Big)(\lambda(t)f-\R)\R-(n+1)\Delta_{\theta}(\lambda(t)f-\R)\\
&\quad+\frac{n}{2}(\lambda(t)f-\R)\R\\
=&-(n+1)\Delta_{\theta}(\lambda(t)f-\R)-(\lambda(t)f-\R)\R.\end{split}

Notice that in the second last equality above we have used the identity

\Delta_{\theta}\varphi=u^{-1-\frac{2}{n}}\Big(u\Delta_{\theta_{0}}\varphi+2\langle\nabla_{\theta_{0}}\varphi,\nabla_{\theta_{0}}u\rangle\Big)~~\mbox{ for any }\varphi.

As for
\lambda^{\prime}(t)
, we use (
2.7
), the evolution equation for
\R
above and (
2.2
) to calculate it as follows.

\begin{split}\lambda^{\prime}(t)&=\bigg(\int_{M}f^{2}\dvg\bigg)^{-1}\bigg[\int_{M}f\partial_{t}\R\dvg+\int_{M}f\R\partial_{t}(u^{2+2/n})\dv\bigg]\\
&\quad-\bigg(\int_{M}f^{2}\dvg\bigg)^{-2}\int_{M}f\R\dvg\int_{M}f^{2}\partial_{t}(u^{2+2/n})\dv\\
&=\bigg(\int_{M}f^{2}\dvg\bigg)^{-1}\bigg[-(n+1)\int_{M}f\Delta_{\theta(t)}(\lambda(t)f-\R)\dvg\\
&\quad+n\int_{M}f\R(\lambda(t)f-\R)\dvg-(n+1)\lambda(t)\int_{M}f^{2}(\lambda(t)f-\R)\dvg\bigg]\\
&=-\bigg(\int_{M}f^{2}\dvg\bigg)^{-1}\bigg[(n+1)\int_{M}f\Delta_{\theta(t)}(\lambda(t)f-\R)\dvg\\
&\quad+n\int_{M}f(\lambda(t)f-\R)^{2}\dvg+\lambda(t)\int_{M}f^{2}(\lambda(t)f-\R)\dvg\bigg].\end{split}

Lemma 3.3
.

There exist two positive constant
c_{1}
and
c_{2}
such that

c_{1}\leqslant{\mathop{\rm vol}}(M,\theta(t))\leqslant c_{2},

where
c_{1}
depends only on
f,M
,
and
c_{2}
depends only on
n,f,M,u_{0}
.

Proof
.
First, we show the lower bound. To see this, from (
2.8
) it follows that

1=\int_{M}f\dvg\leqslant\left(\sup_{M}f\right){\mathop{\rm vol}}(M,\theta(t)).

This implies that

{\mathop{\rm vol}}(M,\theta(t))\geqslant\frac{1}{\sup_{M}f}=:c_{1}.

Next, we prove the upper bound. We split the argument into two cases.

Case 1
.
\Ro>0
, i.e., positive case. In this case, it follows from Folland-Stein embedding (
2.11
) and the energy bound (
3.1
) that

{\mathop{\rm vol}}(M,\theta(t))=\|u\|_{L^{2+2/n}}^{2+2/n}\leqslant c_{0}\E(u)^{1+1/n}\leqslant c_{0}\E(u_{0})^{1+1/n},

for some positive constant
c_{0}
.

Case 2
.
\Ro\equiv 0
, i.e., null case. In this case, we first use Poincaré type inequality (see
[
23
]
for example) and (
3.1
) to obtain

\|u-\bar{u}\|_{L^{2+2/n}}\leqslant c_{0}\|\nabla_{\theta_{0}}u\|_{L^{2}}=c_{0}\sqrt{\frac{n\E(u)}{2n+2}}\leqslant c_{0}\sqrt{\frac{n\E(u_{0})}{2n+2}}.

(3.2)

It remains to bound
\bar{u}
. Noting that
\int_{M}f\dv<0
, we have by (
2.8
) that

\begin{split}\bar{u}^{2+2/n}\int_{M}-f\dv&=-1+\int_{M}f\Big(u^{2+2/n}-\bar{u}^{2+2/n}\Big)\dv\\
&\leqslant\sup_{M}|f|\int_{M}\Big|u^{2+2/n}-\bar{u}^{2+2/n}\Big|\dv\end{split}

(3.3)

Recall that for any
a,b>0
, we have the elementary inequality

\begin{split}\Big|a^{2+2/n}-b^{2+2/n}\Big|&\leqslant\Big(2+2/n\Big)(a+b)^{1+2/n}|a-b|\\
&\leqslant\Big(2+2/n\Big)2^{2/n}\Big(|a-b|^{1+2/n}+(2b)^{1+2/n}\Big)|a-b|\\
&\leqslant c(n)\Big(|a-b|^{2+2/n}+b^{1+2/n}|a-b|\Big),\end{split}

where
c(n)
is a positive constant depending only on
n
. Applying this inequality with
a=u
and
b=\bar{u}
we get

\Big|u^{2+2/n}-\bar{u}^{2+2/n}\Big|\leqslant c(n)\Big(|u-\bar{u}|^{2+2/n}+\bar{u}^{1+2/n}|u-\bar{u}|\Big).

(3.4)

Substituting (
3.4
) into (
3.3
) and using Hölder’s inequality and Young’s inequality, we obtain

\begin{split}\bar{u}^{2+2/n}&\leqslant\frac{c(n)\sup_{M}f}{\int_{M}-f\dv}\Big(\|u-\bar{u}\|_{L^{2+2/n}}^{2+2/n}+\bar{u}^{1+2/n}{\mathop{\rm vol}}(M,\theta_{0})^{\frac{n+2}{2(n+1)}}\|u-\bar{u}\|_{L^{2+2/n}}\Big)\\
&\leqslant\frac{1}{2}\bar{u}^{2+2/n}+c(n,f,M)\|u-\bar{u}\|_{L^{2+2/n}}^{2+2/n},\end{split}

for some positive constant
c(n,f,M)
depending only on
n,f
and
M
. This together with (
3.2
) yields

\bar{u}\leqslant c(n,f,M,u_{0}),

for some positive constant
c(n,f,M,u_{0})
depending only on
n,f,M
and
u_{0}
. Then from Minkowski’s inequality and (
3.2
), it follows that there exists a positive constant
c_{2}
depending only on
n,f,M
and
u_{0}
such that

{\mathop{\rm vol}}(M,\theta(t))\leqslant c_{2},

which completes the proof.

The proof of the lemma above also implies the
S_{1}^{2}
-bound.

Corollary 3.4
.

There exists a positive constant
c_{3}
depending only on
n,M,f,u_{0}
such that

\|u(t)\|_{\mathbf{S}_{1}^{2}(M)}\leqslant c_{3}.

Next lemma shows that the normalized factor
\lambda
keeps bounded
as long as the flow exists.

Lemma 3.5
.

There exists a positive constant
\Lambda_{0}
depending only on
n,f,M,u_{0}
such that

|\lambda(t)|\leqslant\Lambda_{0}.

Proof
.
On one hand, by (
2.3
) and integration by parts we can write

\begin{split}\int_{M}f&\R\dvg=\int_{M}fu\Big(-\big(2+2/n\big)\Delta_{\theta_{0}}u+\Ro u\Big)\dv\\
&\quad=\int_{M}(2+2/n)\langle\nabla_{\theta_{0}}(fu),\nabla_{\theta_{0}}u\rangle+fR_{\theta_{0}}u^{2}\dv\\
&\quad=\int_{M}f\Big[(2+2/n)|\nabla_{\theta_{0}}u|^{2}+R_{\theta_{0}}u^{2}\Big]\dv\\
&\qquad\qquad\qquad+\int_{M}(2+2/n)u\langle\nabla_{\theta_{0}}f,\nabla_{\theta_{0}}u\rangle\dv.\end{split}

Hence, from Corollary
3.4
it follows that

\begin{split}\bigg|\int_{M}f\R\dvg\bigg|&\leqslant\sup_{M}\Big(|f|\Big(2+2/n+R_{\theta_{0}}\Big)\Big)\|u\|_{\mathbf{S}_{1}^{2}(M)}^{2}+(2+2/n)\sup_{M}|\nabla_{\theta_{0}}f|\|u\|_{\mathbf{S}_{1}^{2}(M)}^{2}\\
&\leqslant c_{3}\Big[\sup_{M}\Big(|f|\Big(2+2/n+R_{\theta_{0}}\Big)\Big)+(2+2/n)\sup_{M}|\nabla_{\theta_{0}}f|\Big].\end{split}

On the other hand, using Hölder’s inequality, (
2.8
) and Lemma
3.3
we can obtain

1=\bigg(\int_{M}f\dvg\bigg)^{2}\leqslant{\mathop{\rm vol}}(M,\theta(t))\int_{M}f^{2}\dvg\leqslant c_{2}\int_{M}f^{2}\dvg,

that is

\int_{M}f^{2}\dvg\geqslant c_{2}^{-1}.

(3.5)

Substituting the estimates above into (
2.7
) we thus find a positive constant
\Lambda_{0}
depending only on
n,f,M,u_{0}
such that
|\lambda(t)|\leqslant\Lambda_{0}.

To obtain the global existence of the flow (
2.2
), we need to bound the derivative of
\lambda(t)
. To this end, we let

F_{p}(\theta(t))=\int_{M}|\R-\lambda(t)|^{p}\dvg,\quad p>0

and try to show that
F_{2}(\theta(t))
is bounded. We calculate the derivative of
F_{p}(\theta(t))
. By Lemma
3.2
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}F_{p}(\theta(t))=\frac{d}{dt}\int_{M}|\lambda(t)f-\R|^{p}\dvg

(3.6)

\displaystyle=p\int_{M}(\lambda(t)f-\R)_{t}(\lambda(t)f-\R)|\lambda(t)f-\R|^{p-2}\dvg

\displaystyle+(n+1)\int_{M}|\lambda(t)f-\R|^{p}(\lambda(t)f-\R)\dvg

\displaystyle=-p(n+1)\int_{M}\Delta_{\theta(t)}(\lambda(t)f-\R)(\lambda(t)f-\R)

\displaystyle|\lambda(t)-\R|^{p-2}\dvg+p\int_{M}\R|\lambda(t)f-\R|^{p}\dvg

\displaystyle+\lambda^{\prime}(t)p\int_{M}f(\lambda(t)f-\R)|\lambda(t)f-\R|^{p-2}\dvg

\displaystyle+(n+1)\int_{M}|\lambda(t)f-\R|^{p}(\lambda(t)f-\R)\dvg

\displaystyle+(n+1-p)\int_{M}|\lambda(t)f-\R|^{p}(\lambda(t)f-\R)\dvg.

Proof

Lemma 3.7

\displaystyle+c_{2}^{3/2}\Lambda_{0}\left(\sup_{M}f^{2}\right)\sqrt{F_{2}(\theta(t))}.

In this section, we devote ourselves to proving the

Lemma 4.1
.

There exists a positive constant

Proof

Recalling (

,

,

\begin{split}\frac{d}{dt}F_{p}(\theta(t))&\leqslant p\Lambda_{0}\Big(\sup_{M}f\Big)F_{p}(\theta(t))+p\Big(\sup_{M}f\Big)C\sqrt{F_{2}(\theta(t))}F_{p-1}(\theta(t))\\

Lemma 4.2

.

Case 2.

\Ro\equiv 0

5.
Blow-up analysis

In this section, we perform the so-called blow-up analysis. To do so, let us start with the CR version of concentration-compactness theorem.

5.1.
Concentration-Compactness

Consider the semi-linear equation

-\Delta_{\theta}u+hu=fu^{1+2/n}

(5.1)

and the associated functional

I_{f}[u]=\frac{1}{2}\int_{M}\left(|\nabla u|_{\theta}^{2}+hu^{2}\right)\dvg-\frac{n}{2(n+1)}\int_{M}fu^{2+2/n}\dvg.

It is known (c.f.
[
9
]
) that any nonnegative and nontrivial solution of the equation on Heisenberg group

-\Delta_{\theta_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}}u=u^{1+2/n}

must be of the form

\U_{\zeta}^{\mu}=\mu^{n}\Big|(s-s_{0})+i\Big(|z-z_{0}|^{2}+(\mu/n)^{2}\Big)\Big|^{-n},

(5.3)

where
\zeta=(z_{0},s_{0})
with
z_{0}\in\mathbb{C}^{n},s_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
and
\mu>0
. Furthermore, the functions of the form (
5.3
) are, up to left translations and dilations
\T_{\delta}:

(z,s)\mapsto(\delta z,\delta^{2}s)
, the only extremals of the the sharp Sobolev inequality on Heisenberg group

\bigg(\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}|u|^{2+2/n}\dvg\bigg)^{\frac{n}{n+1}}\leqslant K_{n}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}|\nabla u|_{\theta}^{2}\dvg,

(5.4)

where

is the same constant as in (
2.10
). The energy on Heisenberg group is denoted by

E(u)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}|\nabla u|_{\theta_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}}^{2}\dvh-\frac{n}{2(n+1)}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}|u|^{2+2/n}\dvh.

If we choose
\mu>0
such that

\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}|\U_{\zeta}^{\mu}|^{2+2/n}\dvh=K_{n}^{-(n+1)},

(5.5)

then by (
5.2
) and (
5.4
) we get

E\Big(\U_{\zeta}^{\mu}\Big)=\frac{K_{n}^{-(n+1)}}{2(n+1)}.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
(u_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
be an
\mathbf{S}_{1}^{2}(M)
-bounded Palais-Smale sequence of nonnegative functions for
I_{f}
, where

and a nonnegative weak solution

(i)
there exist

as

(ii)
there exist
\varepsilon_{k}^{j}>0
such that

and

\frac{\varepsilon^{i}_{k}}{\varepsilon^{j}_{k}}+\frac{\varepsilon^{j}_{k}}{\varepsilon_{k}^{i}}+\frac{\mathrm{d}(x_{k}^{i},x_{k}^{j})^{2}}{\varepsilon^{i}_{k}\varepsilon^{j}_{k}}\rightarrow+\infty,~\mbox{for all}~i\neq j,~\mbox{as}~k\rightarrow+\infty,

(5.6)

where

with respect to the contact form
\theta_{0}
;

(iii)
with

\U_{k}^{j}(x)=\big(\varepsilon_{k}^{j}\big)^{-n}f(x^{j})^{-n/2}\U^{j}\bigg(\T_{(\varepsilon_{k}^{j})^{-1}}\exp_{x_{k}^{j}}^{-1}(x)\bigg)

\Big\|u_{k}-u_{\infty}-\sum_{j=1}^{m}\eta_{k}^{j}\U_{k}^{j}\Big\|_{\mathbf{S}_{1}^{2}(M)}\rightarrow 0,~~\mbox{as}~k\rightarrow+\infty,

(5.7)

where
\U^{j}=\U_{\zeta_{j}}^{\mu_{j}}
satisfies (

),

are the local charts centered at

,

\eta_{k}^{j}\equiv 1
in
\exp_{x_{k}^{j}}(B_{r_{0}}(0))
and
\eta_{k}^{j}\equiv 0
in
M\backslash\exp_{x_{k}^{j}}(B_{2r_{0}})
. Moreover,

I_{f}[u_{k}]=I_{f}[u_{\infty}]+\frac{K_{n}^{-(n+1)}}{2(n+1)}\sum_{j=1}^{m}f(x^{j})^{-n}+o(1),

(5.8)

where
o(1)\rightarrow 0
as
k\rightarrow+\infty
.

Proof
.
To prove Theorem
5.1
, one can just follow the proof of Proposition 5.1 in
[
21
]
which is contained in Appendix B of
[
21
]
;
the only difference between Theorem
5.1
here and Proposition 5.1 in
[
21
]
is that we are considering (

) in which

is a function, while in Proposition 5.1 in
[

]
they are considering
(
5.1
) in which

is a positive constant.
Note that the assertion

in Theorem

(i)
follows from the fact that if

must be positive.

5.2.
Weak solutions

A simple characterization of the asymptotic behavior of energy
\E(u)
is given as below

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
u(\cdot,t)
be a smooth solution of the flow (
2.2
). Then there hold

(i)

\E(u(t))=\lambda(t)+o(1)
, where
o(1)\rightarrow 0
as
t\rightarrow\infty
;

(ii)

there exists a constant
\lambda_{\infty}
such that
\lambda(t)\rightarrow\lambda_{\infty}
as
t\rightarrow\infty
.

Proof
.
(i) From (
2.3
), (
2.8
) and Lemma
4.2
it follows that

\E(u(t))=\int_{M}\R\dvg=\int_{M}\Big(\R-\lambda(t)f\Big)\dvg+\lambda(t)=\lambda(t)+o(1),

where
o(1)\rightarrow 0
, as
t\rightarrow\infty
.

(ii) By Lemma
3.1
, we know that
\E(u(t))
is monotone non-increasing. In addition, (
2.10
) implies that
\E(u(t))
has a lower bound. Hence
\E(u(t))
converges as
t\rightarrow\infty
. Then the assertion in (i) concludes that
\lambda(t)\rightarrow\lambda_{\infty}
for some constant
\lambda_{\infty}
as
t\rightarrow\infty
.

For a nonnegative function
w\in\mathbf{S}_{1}^{2}(M)
, we define the energy functional

J_{f}[w]=\frac{1}{2}\int_{M}\left((2+2/n)|\nabla w|_{\theta_{0}}^{2}+\Ro w^{2}-\frac{n}{n+1}\lambda_{\infty}fw^{2+2/n}\right)\dv.

Then, it is easy to compute

\mathrm{d}J_{f}[w](\phi)=\int_{M}(2+2/n)\langle\nabla w,\nabla\phi\rangle_{\theta_{0}}+\Ro w\phi-\lambda_{\infty}fw^{1+2/n}\phi\dv.

(5.9)

Moreover, we have

Lemma 5.3
.

For any smooth solution
u(\cdot,t)
of the flow (
2.2
), there hold

J_{f}[u(t)]=\frac{1}{2}\E(u(t))-\frac{n}{2(n+1)}\lambda_{\infty}\quad\text{and}\quad J_{f}[u(t)]=\frac{1}{2(n+1)}\lambda_{\infty}+o(1),

where
o(1)\rightarrow 0
as
t\rightarrow+\infty
.

Proof
.
From (
2.8
) and definition of
J_{f}
, it follows that

\displaystyle J_{f}[u(t)]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{M}(1+1/n)|\nabla u|_{\theta_{0}}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\Ro u^{2}-\frac{n}{2(n+1)}\lambda_{\infty}fu^{2+2/n}\dv

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\E(u(t))-\frac{n}{2(n+1)}\lambda_{\infty}.

Rewriting the above equality as

J_{f}[u(t)]=\frac{1}{2}(\E(u(t))-\lambda_{\infty})+\frac{1}{2(n+1)}\lambda_{\infty}.

Thanks to Lemma
5.2
(i) that
\E(u(t))\to\lambda_{\infty}
as
t\rightarrow+\infty
, we thus finished the proof.

Let
\{t_{k}:k\in\mathbb{N}\}
be a time sequence such that
t_{k}\rightarrow+\infty
as
k\rightarrow+\infty
. Also, we set
u_{k}:=u(t_{k}),\theta_{k}:=\theta(t_{k}),\lambda_{k}:=\lambda(t_{k})
and
R_{k}:=R_{\theta_{k}}
. Then we will show the following:

Lemma 5.4
.

(u_{k})_{k}
is a Palais-Smale sequence for the energy functional
J_{f}
.

Proof
.
We need to show that

\bullet
The sequence
(J_{f}[u_{k}])_{k}
is bounded;

\bullet

dJ_{f}[u_{k}]\rightarrow 0
strongly in
\mathbf{S}_{1}^{2}(M)^{-1}
as
k\rightarrow+\infty
.

The boundedness of
(J_{f}[u_{k}])_{k}
is straightforward by Lemma
5.3
and (
3.1
). Hence, we are left to check the strong convergence
dJ_{f}[u_{k}]\rightarrow 0
in
\mathbf{S}_{1}^{2}(M)^{-1}
as
k\rightarrow+\infty
. To see this, we fix any
\phi\in\mathbf{S}_{1}^{2}(M)
. Then (
2.3
), (
2.11
)
and Hölder’s inequality yield

\displaystyle dJ_{f}[u_{k}](\phi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{M}\Big[\big(-(2+2/n)\Delta_{\theta_{0}}u_{k}+\Ro u_{k}-\lambda_{\infty}fu_{k}^{1+2/n}\Big]\phi\dv

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{M}(R_{k}-\lambda_{\infty}f)u_{k}^{1+2/n}\phi\dv

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle||R_{k}-\lambda_{k}f+(\lambda_{k}-\lambda_{\infty})f||_{L^{\frac{2(n+1)}{n+2}}(M,\theta_{k})}||\phi||_{L^{\frac{2(n+1)}{n}}(M,\theta_{0})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\Big(F_{\frac{2(n+1)}{n+2}}(\theta_{k}))^{\frac{n+2}{2(n+1)}}+|\lambda_{k}-\lambda_{\infty}|\sup_{M}fc_{2}^{\frac{n+2}{2(n+1)}}\Big)~||\phi||_{\mathbf{S}_{1}^{2}(M)}.

Notice that
2(n+1)/(n+2)<2
, it follows from Lemma
4.2
that
F_{\frac{2n}{n+1}}(g_{k})=o(1)
as
k\rightarrow+\infty
. Moreover, by Lemma
5.2
, we have
\lambda_{k}-\lambda_{\infty}=o(1)
, as
k\rightarrow+\infty
. Hence, the assertion follows.

Lemma 5.5
.

There exists
u_{\infty}\in\mathbf{S}_{1}^{2}(M)
such that
u_{k}
converges to
u_{\infty}
weakly in
\mathbf{S}_{1}^{2}(M)
as
k\rightarrow+\infty
. Moreover, if
u_{\infty}\not\equiv 0
, then (
1.2
) possess a positive solution.

Proof
. Since
(u_{k})_{k}
is bounded in
\mathbf{S}_{1}^{2}(M)
by Corollary
3.4
, there exists
u_{\infty}\in S_{1}^{2}(M)
such that, up to a subsequence,
u_{k}\rightharpoonup u_{\infty}
weakly in
\mathbf{S}_{1}^{2}(M)
as
k\rightarrow+\infty
. By Lemma
5.4
, we have
dJ_{f}[u_{k}]=o(1)
in
\mathbf{S}_{1}^{2}(M)^{-1}
. Then, it follows from (
5.9
) that

\int_{M}(2+2/n)\langle\nabla u_{k},\nabla\phi\rangle_{\theta_{0}}+\Ro u_{k}\phi~\dv=\int_{M}\lambda_{\infty}fu_{k}^{1+2/n}\phi~\dv+o(1).

Letting
k\rightarrow+\infty
, we obtain that
u_{\infty}
solves the Eq.(
1.2
) in the weak sense

\int_{M}(2+2/n)\langle\nabla u_{\infty},\nabla\phi\rangle_{\theta_{0}}+\Ro u_{\infty}\phi~\dv=\int_{M}\lambda_{\infty}fu_{\infty}^{1+2/n}\phi~\dv.

Moreover, standard regularity theory (c.f. Theorem 5.15 in
[
10
]
) implies that
u_{\infty}
is smooth if
f
is smooth. Hence,
u_{\infty}
satisfies

-(2+2/n)\Delta_{\theta_{0}}u_{\infty}+\Ro u_{\infty}=\lambda_{\infty}fu_{\infty}^{1+2/n}.

(5.10)

Now, if
u_{\infty}\not\equiv 0
, we can apply Proposition A.1 in
[
16
]

to conclude that
u_{\infty}>0
.

Next, we claim that
\lambda_{\infty}>0
. If
\Ro>0
, it follows from
(
2.8
), (
2.10
) and Lemma
3.3
that

\E(u(t))\geqslant\mu(M){\mathop{\rm vol}}(M,\theta(t))^{\frac{n}{n+1}}\geqslant\mu(M)\Big(\sup_{M}f\Big)^{-\frac{n}{n+1}}.

Then by Lemma
5.2
we get that

\lambda_{\infty}=\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\E(u(t))\geqslant\mu(M)\Big(\sup_{M}f\Big)^{-\frac{n}{n+1}}>0.

If
\Ro\equiv 0
, we first note that there exists a subsequence, still denoted by
\{t_{k}\}
, such that
u_{k}\rightharpoonup u_{\infty}
weakly in
\mathbf{S}_{1}^{2}(M)
and strongly in
L^{2}(M)
. Now, our argument is split into two cases.

Case 1
.
u_{\infty}\equiv 0
. In this case, using (
2.8
), (
2.11
), Lemma
3.3
and the fact that
\|u_{k}\|_{L^{2}(M)}\rightarrow 0
as
k\rightarrow\infty
, we obtain

\begin{split}\int_{M}|\nabla_{\theta_{0}}u_{k}|^{2}\dv&\geqslant{\mathscr{C}}^{-1}{\mathop{\rm vol}}(M,\theta_{k})^{\frac{n}{n+1}}-\int_{M}u_{k}^{2}\dv\\
&\geqslant{\mathscr{C}}^{-1}\Big(\sup_{M}f\Big)^{-\frac{n}{n+1}}+o(1).\end{split}

This implies that

\begin{split}\lambda_{\infty}&=\lim_{\nu\rightarrow\infty}\E(u_{k})=\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\int_{M}\big(2+2/n\big)|\nabla_{\theta_{0}}u_{k}|^{2}\dv\\
&\geqslant\big(2+2/n\big){\mathscr{C}}^{-1}\Big(\sup_{M}f\Big)^{-\frac{n}{n+1}}>0.\end{split}

Case 2
.
u_{\infty}\not\equiv 0
. In this case, if follows from (
5.10
) that
u_{\infty}>0
solves

-(2+2/n)\Delta_{\theta_{0}}u_{\infty}=\lambda_{\infty}fu_{\infty}^{1+2/n}.

To prove that
\lambda_{\infty}>0
, we suppose on contrary
\lambda_{\infty}=0
,
which gives
\Delta_{\theta_{0}}u_{\infty}=0
. Hence,
u_{\infty}
must be a positive constant, say
c>0
. Since
\lambda_{\infty}=0
,
it follows from Lemma
5.2
(i) that

\int_{M}|\nabla_{\theta_{0}}\big(u_{k}-u_{\infty}\big)|^{2}\dv=\int_{M}|\nabla_{\theta_{0}}u_{k}|^{2}\dv=\frac{n\E(u_{k})}{2n+2}\rightarrow 0

as
k\rightarrow\infty
.
This together with (
2.11
) implies that

\begin{split}&\bigg(\int_{M}|u_{k}-u_{\infty}|^{2+2/n}\dv\bigg)^{\frac{n}{n+1}}\\
&\leqslant{\mathscr{C}}^{-1}\bigg(\int_{M}|\nabla_{\theta_{0}}\big(u_{k}-u_{\infty}\big)|^{2}\dv\quad+\int_{M}|u_{k}-u_{\infty}|^{2}\dv\bigg)\rightarrow 0\end{split}

as
k\to\infty
.
This together with (
2.8
) implies that
\int_{M}fu_{\infty}^{2+2/n}\dv=1
. Since
u_{\infty}\equiv c>0
, we have
\int_{M}f\dv=c^{-2-2/n}>0
, contradicting the assumption
\int_{M}f\dv<0
. This proves the claim, i.e.
\lambda_{\infty}>0
.

Therefore, if
u_{\infty}\not\equiv 0
, then
\lambda_{\infty}^{n/2}u_{\infty}
produces a positive solution to (
1.2
).

Lemma
5.5
shows that Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
will immediately follow provided that we can show that there exists a time sequence
(t_{k})_{k}
such that
u_{k}\rightharpoonup u_{\infty}\not\equiv 0
weakly in
\mathbf{S}_{1}^{2}(M)
. However, this is generally hard to be proved via direct estimates. Hence, we shall adopt a contradiction argument. To do so, we assume, from now on, that
u(t)\rightharpoonup 0
weakly in
\mathbf{S}_{1}^{2}(M)
as
t\rightarrow+\infty
. Under this assumption, it is easy to see from (
2.11
) and Lemma
3.3
that
u_{k}
cannot converge to
0
strongly in
\mathbf{S}_{1}^{2}(M)
, and the concentration phenomenon will occur.

5.3.
Single concentration point

Set

\lambda_{*}=\frac{2(n+1)}{n}\Big(\sup_{M}f\Big)^{-\frac{n}{n+1}}K_{n}^{-1},

where
K_{n}=1/(2\pi n^{2})
is the optimal constant appeared in (
2.10
).
Then we have the following:

Lemma 5.6
.

\lambda_{\infty}\geqslant\lambda_{*}
.

Proof
.
Since
u\rightharpoonup 0
weakly in
\mathbf{S}_{1}^{2}(M)
,
u\rightarrow 0
strongly in
L^{2}(M)
as
t\rightarrow 0
. This fact together with Lemma
5.2
implies that

\displaystyle\lambda_{\infty}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\E(u(t))=\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\int_{M}(2+2/n)|\nabla u|^{2}_{\theta_{0}}+\Ro u^{2}\dv

(5.11)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\int_{M}(2+2/n)|\nabla u|^{2}_{\theta_{0}}\dv

On the other hand, given
\varepsilon>0
, by (
2.8
), (
2.10
), Lemma
3.3
and the fact that
\|u\|_{L^{2}}=o(1)
we get

\displaystyle\int_{M}(2+2/n)|\nabla u|^{2}_{\theta_{0}}\dv

\displaystyle\geqslant

\displaystyle\frac{2(n+1)}{n(K_{n}+\varepsilon)}\Big(\mathrm{vol}(M,\theta(t))\Big)^{\frac{n}{n+1}}+o(1)

(5.12)

\displaystyle\geqslant

\displaystyle\frac{2(n+1)}{n(K_{n}+\varepsilon)}\big(\sup_{M}f\big)^{-\frac{n}{n+1}}+o(1)

Combining (
5.11
) and (
5.12
) yields

\lambda_{\infty}\geqslant\frac{2(n+1)}{n(K_{n}+\varepsilon)}\big(\sup_{M}f\big)^{-\frac{n}{n+1}}.

Letting
\varepsilon
to zero, we thus complete the proof.

With a suitable choice of initial data, we can guarantee the single concentration point of the flow.

Lemma 5.7
.

If the initial data
u_{0}
satisfies
\E(u_{0})<2^{\frac{1}{n+1}}\lambda_{*}
, then
m=1
, where
m
is the positive integer in Theorem
5.1
.

Proof
.
First, note that

J_{f}[w]=(2+2/n)I_{\frac{n}{2(n+1)}\lambda_{\infty}f}[w].

Thus, we can apply Theorem
5.1
to the functional
J_{f}[u(t)]
to obtain

J_{f}[u(t)]=\frac{K_{n}^{-(n+1)}}{2(n+1)}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\bigg(\frac{2(n+1)}{n}\bigg)^{n+1}\Big(\lambda_{\infty}f(x_{j})\Big)^{-n}+o(1).

This together with Lemma
5.3
implies that

\displaystyle\frac{\lambda_{\infty}}{2(n+1)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}J_{f}[u(t)]=\frac{K_{n}^{-(n+1)}}{2(n+1)}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\bigg(\frac{2(n+1)}{n}\bigg)^{n+1}\Big(\lambda_{\infty}f(x_{j})\Big)^{-n}

(5.13)

\displaystyle\geqslant

\displaystyle\frac{mK_{n}^{-(n+1)}}{2(n+1)}\bigg(\frac{2(n+1)}{n}\bigg)^{n+1}\lambda_{\infty}^{-n}\Big(\sup_{M}f\Big)^{-n}.

Hence

\lambda_{\infty}^{\frac{1}{n+1}}\geqslant m^{\frac{1}{n+1}}\frac{2(n+1)}{n}\Big(\sup_{M}f\Big)^{-\frac{n}{n+1}}K_{n}^{-1}=m^{\frac{1}{n+1}}\lambda_{*}.

On the other hand, it follows from Lemma
5.2
and (
3.1
) that

\lambda_{\infty}=\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\E(u(t))\leqslant\E(u_{0})<2^{\frac{1}{n+1}}\lambda_{*}.

Combining the estimates above yields

m<2
, i.e.
m=1
.

We have the following convention:
For the rest of the paper, our initial data is chosen to satisfy

\int_{M}fu_{0}^{1+2/n}\dv=1~~\mathrm{and}~~\E(u_{0})<2^{\frac{1}{n+1}}\lambda_{*}.

(5.14)

With such a choice of initial data, we know by Lemma
5.7
that there would be only one concentration point denoted by
a_{\infty}
. Then (
5.13
) can be reduced as

\frac{\lambda_{\infty}}{2(n+1)}=\frac{K_{n}^{-(n+1)}}{2(n+1)}\bigg(\frac{2(n+1)}{n}\bigg)^{n+1}\Big(\lambda_{\infty}f(a_{\infty})\Big)^{-n}.

Solving this gives

\lambda_{\infty}=\frac{2(n+1)}{n}K_{n}^{-1}f(a_{\infty})^{-\frac{n}{n+1}}.

(5.15)

Moreover, we may choose suitable cut-off function
\eta(t)
,
\varepsilon^{*}(t)>0
with
\varepsilon^{*}(t)\rightarrow 0
and
a^{*}(t)\in M
with
a^{*}(t)\rightarrow a_{\infty}
such that as

\Big\|u(t)-\varphi^{*}(t)\Big\|_{\mathbf{S}_{1}^{2}(M)}\rightarrow 0,

(5.16)

where

\varphi^{*}(t,x)=\bigg(\frac{n\lambda_{\infty}f(a_{\infty})}{2(n+1)}\bigg)^{-n/2}\eta(t)(\varepsilon^{*}(t))^{-n}\U(\T_{{\varepsilon^{*}(t)}^{-1}}\exp_{a^{*}(t)}^{-1}(x))

and

\U=\big|s+i\big(|z|^{2}+(1/n)^{2}\big)\big|^{-n}.

Bubbles

We have the following:

.

(5.40)

Substituting this into (

) gives

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\partial\E_{f}(\alpha\varphi)h

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f_{\alpha\varphi}^{-\frac{n}{n+1}}\bigg[\int_{M}\SL_{\theta_{0}}(\alpha\varphi)h\dv-\lambda\int_{M}f(\alpha\varphi)^{1+2/n}h\dv\bigg]

(5.41)

\displaystyle\quad+O\Big(\delta^{6-2n}\varepsilon^{2n}+\varepsilon^{6}+|\nabla f(a)|^{2}_{\theta_{0}}\varepsilon^{2}+F_{2}(\theta(t))^{1/2}+\|v\|^{2}\Big).

By Lemma
5.9
and following the proof of Lemma

around

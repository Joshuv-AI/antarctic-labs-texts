On illposedness of the Hall and electron magnetohydrodynamic equations without resistivity on the whole space
In-Jee Jeong, Sung-Jin Oh

Abstract
. It has been shown in our previous work [ 19 ] that the incompressible and irresistive Hall- and electron-magnetohydrodynamic (MHD) equations are illposed on flat domains M = ℝ k × 𝕋 3 − k M=\mathbb{R}^{k}\times\mathbb{T}^{3-k} for 0 ≤ k ≤ 2 0\leq k\leq 2 . The data and solutions therein were assumed to be independent of one coordinate, which not only significantly simplifies the systems but also allows for a large class of steady states. In this work, we remove the assumption of independence and conclude strong illposedness for compactly supported data in ℝ 3 \mathbb{R}^{3} . This is achieved by constructing degenerating wave packets for linearized systems around time-dependent axisymmetric magnetic fields. A few main additional ingredients are: a more systematic application of the generalized energy estimate, use of the Bogovskiǐ operator, and a priori estimates for axisymmetric solutions to the Hall- and electron-MHD systems.



1.
Introduction

1.1.
The systems

In this paper, we are concerned with the Cauchy problem for the incompressible
Hall-magnetohydrodynamics
(Hall-MHD for short) equations, which describe the motion of a plasma whose electron speed is significantly faster than the ion speed. Still, the equations give a single-fluid description of the plasma with
{\bf u}(t):M\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{3}
as the so-called bulk plasma velocity. Together with the magnetic field
{\bf B}(t):M\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{3}
, the equations are given by

(Hall-MHD)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}{\bf u}+{\bf u}\cdot\nabla{\bf u}+\nabla{\bf p}-\nu\Delta{\bf u}=(\nabla\times{\bf B})\times{\bf B},\\
&\partial_{t}{\bf B}-\nabla\times({\bf u}\times{\bf B})+\nabla\times((\nabla\times{\bf B})\times{\bf B})=0,\\
&\nabla\cdot{\bf u}=\nabla\cdot{\bf B}=0.\end{aligned}\right.

Here,
{\bf p}(t):M\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is the plasma pressure and
\nu\geq 0
is the plasma viscosity. We assume that the domain is given by a flat manifold
M=\mathbb{T}^{k}\times\mathbb{R}^{3-k}
(
0\leq k\leq 3
). Note that there is no dissipation term for the magnetic field (i.e. no magnetic resistivity), which will be assumed throughout the paper. The special case
\nu=0
is called the
ideal Hall-MHD equation
. This is just the usual ideal MHD equation with an extra term
\nabla\times((\nabla\times{\bf B})\times{\bf B})
on the left hand side, which is referred to as the Hall current term. This modification over the usual MHD system was suggested by Lighthill
[
24
]
to take into account the disparity between the electron speed and the ion speed, and is known to be directly responsible for various physical phenomena including collisionless magnetic reconnection, planetary magnetospheres, and magnetic field dynamics in neutron stars (
[
3
,
28
,
17
,
11
,
32
,

,
27

10

25
,
15

22

16

30
]
). We shall also consider the simpler variant obtained by formally taking
{\bf u}=0
, called the electron-MHD (E-MHD for short) equations:

(E-MHD)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}{\bf B}+\nabla\times((\nabla\times{\bf B})\times{\bf B})=0,\\
&\nabla\cdot{\bf B}=0.\end{aligned}\right.

It turns out that, as long as the issue of local wellposedness is concerned, the reduced system (
E-MHD
) serves as a good approximation for the more realistic (
Hall-MHD
). Due to the potential loss of derivatives present in the Hall current term, the basic question of local wellposedness had been open in the irresistive case. In
[
19
]
we have established that the Cauchy problem for the equations (
Hall-MHD

z

The goal of the current paper is to remove the assumption of

1.2.
Energy identities, stationary solutions and linearization

A fundamental property satisfied by (
Hall-MHD
) and (
E-MHD
) is the energy identity. In the following proposition, it will be assumed that
M=\mathbb{T}^{k}\times\mathbb{R}^{3-k}
and the solutions have sufficient smoothness and decay at spatial infinity.

Proposition 1.2
.

For a solution
({\bf u},{\bf B})
to (
Hall-MHD
), we have

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\left(\frac{1}{2}\int_{M}(|{{\bf u}}|^{2}+|{{\bf B}}|^{2})(t)\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}z\right)=-\nu\int_{M}|{\nabla{\bf u}}|^{2}(t)\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}z.

Similarly, for a solution
{\bf B}
to (
E-MHD
), we have

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\left(\frac{1}{2}\int_{M}|{{\bf B}}|^{2}(t)\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}z\right)=0.

The proofs of the above identities are straightforward; one simply multiply the equations for
{\bf u}
and
{\bf B}
by
{\bf u}
and
{\bf B}
respectively, integrate over the domain
M
, and use integration by parts. Instead of supplying a proof, we just note that the Hall current term drops out by the symmetric nature of
\nabla\times

\int_{M}{\bf B}\cdot(\nabla\times((\nabla\times{\bf B})\times{\bf B})\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}z=\int_{M}(\nabla\times{\bf B})\cdot((\nabla\times{\bf B})\times{\bf B})\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}z=0.

This symmetry in general allows us to gain one derivative back from the Hall current term; if one attempts to control
\|{\partial^{(N)}{\bf B}(t)}\|_{L^{2}}
for a solution
{\bf B}
to (
E-MHD
) (
\partial^{(N)}
refers to an
N
-th order spatial derivative), we have from the Hall term a contribution of the form

(1.1)

\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\int_{M}|{\partial^{(N)}{\bf B}}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}z=-\int_{M}(\nabla\times\partial^{(N)}{\bf B})\cdot((\nabla\times{\bf B})\times\partial^{(N)}{\bf B})\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}z+\cdots

where the other terms only involve up to
N
derivatives of
{\bf B}
. It is not clear how to handle this integral on the right hand side, and the main results of this paper and
[
19
]
confirms that there are situations where this expression is an actual loss of a derivative. (In contrast to equations for which the Cauchy–Kovalevskaya theorem is applicable, the situation is not better at all in analytic or even in Gevrey regularity classes; see
[
19
, Section 6]
.)

While the systems (
Hall-MHD
) and (
E-MHD
) possess a large set of stationary solutions, a special class of them played a crucial role in
[
19
]
, namely
planar stationary magnetic fields with an additional symmetry
. We first recall the definition:

•

(Stationary magnetic field) The solution is of the form
{\bf B}=\mathring{{\bf B}}
for (

({\bf u},{\bf B})=(0,\mathring{{\bf B}})
for (

), where
\mathring{{\bf B}}
is a

\mathbb{R}^{3}
such that
\nabla\cdot\mathring{{\bf B}}=0
(divergence-free) and

•

(Planarity)
\mathring{{\bf B}}

\mathring{{\bf B}}^{z}

•

(

1.2.1.
Linearized systems around stationary solutions

Given a stationary solution to (
Hall-MHD
) of the form
(0,\mathring{{\bf B}})
, let us consider perturbations of the form
({\bf u},{\bf B})=(u,\mathring{{\bf B}}+b)
. The linearized equation satisfied by
(u,b)
(i.e., the linearization of (
Hall-MHD
) around
\mathring{{\bf B}}
) is:

(1.2)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}u-\nu\Delta u=\mathbb{P}((\nabla\times\mathring{{\bf B}})\times b+(\nabla\times b)\times\mathring{{\bf B}})\\
&\partial_{t}b+\nabla\times(u\times\mathring{{\bf B}})+\nabla\times((\nabla\times b)\times\mathring{{\bf B}})+\nabla\times((\nabla\times\mathring{{\bf B}})\times b)=0,\\
&\nabla\cdot u=\nabla\cdot b=0,\end{aligned}\right.

where
\mathbb{P}
is the Leray projection operator onto divergence-free vector fields. In the case of (
E-MHD
), the linearization is simply given by

(1.3)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}b+\nabla\times((\nabla\times b)\times\mathring{{\bf B}})+\nabla\times((\nabla\times\mathring{{\bf B}})\times b)=0,\\
&\nabla\cdot b=0.\end{aligned}\right.

The
L^{2}
-norm of the perturbation is (formally) under control, for the linearized systems.

Proposition 1.4
.

For a sufficiently regular and decaying solution
(u,b)
to (
1.2
), we have

(1.4)

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\left(\frac{1}{2}\int_{M}|{u}|^{2}(t)+|{b}|^{2}(t)\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}z\right)+\nu\int_{M}|{\nabla u}|^{2}(t)\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle=\int_{M}((b\cdot\nabla)\mathring{{\bf B}}_{j})u^{j}-((u\cdot\nabla)\mathring{{\bf B}}_{j})b^{j}\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}z+\int_{M}((b\cdot\nabla)(\nabla\times\mathring{{\bf B}})_{j})b^{j}\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}z.

Similarly, for a sufficiently regular and decaying solution
b
to (
1.3
), we have

(1.5)

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\left(\frac{1}{2}\int_{M}|{b}|^{2}(t)\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}z\right)=\int_{M}((b\cdot\nabla)(\nabla\times\mathring{{\bf B}})_{j})b^{j}\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}z.

1.4.
Time-dependent axisymmetric magnetic fields and linearized equations

In this work, we focus on axisymmetric background magnetic fields (rather than translationally symmetric ones), as they are directly responsible for illposedness in
\mathbb{R}^{3}

To this end, take an axisymmetric vector field of the form
{\bf B}=\Pi(r,z)\partial_{\theta}=r\Pi(r,z)e_{\theta}

\nabla\times(\Pi\partial_{\theta})=-r\partial_{z}\Pi\partial_{r}+r^{-1}\partial_{r}(r^{2}\Pi)\partial_{z}

\begin{split}({\bf B}\cdot\nabla)(\nabla\times{\bf B})&=-r\Pi\partial_{z}\Pi e_{\theta}=-\Pi\partial_{z}\Pi\partial_{\theta}\end{split}

and

\begin{split}((\nabla\times{\bf B})\cdot\nabla){\bf B}&=(-\partial_{z}\Pi\partial_{r}(r\Pi)+r^{-1}\partial_{r}(r^{2}\Pi)\partial_{z}\Pi)\partial_{\theta}=\Pi\partial_{z}\Pi\partial_{\theta},\end{split}

so that
{\bf B}(t)=\Pi(t,r,z)\partial_{\theta}
satisfies (
E-MHD
) if
\Pi
solves the inviscid Burgers’ equation:

(1.7)

\begin{split}\partial_{t}\Pi-2\Pi\partial_{z}\Pi=0.\end{split}

Let us move on to the Hall-MHD case. Unlike the translationally symmetric case, now that
(\nabla\times{\bf B})\times{\bf B}
is not a gradient, we cannot keep
{\bf u}=0
. However, it is still possible to propagate that velocity is axisymmetric and swirl-free, i.e.
{\bf u}\cdot e^{\theta}=0
. In this case, it is convenient to introduce
\boldsymbol{\omega}=\nabla\times{\bf u}=:\Omega(r,z)\partial_{\theta}
; we obtain that
(\Omega,\Pi)
is a solution to Hall-MHD system if

(1.8)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}\Omega+(V^{r}\partial_{r}+V^{z}\partial_{z})\Omega+2\Pi\partial_{z}\Pi=\nu(\partial_{r}^{2}+\frac{3}{r}\partial_{r}+\partial_{z}^{2})\Omega\\
&\partial_{t}\Pi+(V^{r}\partial_{r}+V^{z}\partial_{z})\Pi-2\Pi\partial_{z}\Pi=0.\end{aligned}\right.

This system (as well as (
1.7
)) seems to have appeared for the first time in
[
7
]
, from where we have taken the notation
(\Omega,\Pi)
. It is to be supplemented with the following relation between
\Omega
and
V^{r},V^{z}
:

(1.9)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{z}V^{r}-\partial_{r}V^{z}=r\Omega,\\
&\partial_{r}V^{r}+\frac{1}{r}V^{r}+\partial_{z}V^{z}=0.\end{aligned}\right.

As in
[
7
,
23
]
, it will be convenient to introduce a stream function
\Phi
, such that

\begin{split}-(\partial_{r}^{2}+\frac{3}{r}\partial_{r}+\partial_{z}^{2})\Phi=\Omega,\quad V^{r}=-r\partial_{z}\Phi,\quad V^{z}=\frac{1}{r}\partial_{r}(r^{2}\Phi).\end{split}

Existence, uniqueness, and regularity of
\Phi
satisfying the above can be seen by observing that the second-order operator on the left hand side is simply
-\Delta
in
\mathbb{R}^{5}
(see
[
7
]
). With some abuse of notation, from now on we shall refer to a solution of (
1.7
) by
\mathbf{\Pi}=\Pi\partial_{\theta}
. Similarly, given a solution
(\Omega,\Pi)
of (
1.8
), the corresponding axisymmetric solution of (
Hall-MHD
) will be written as
({\bf V},\mathbf{\Pi})
, with
{\bf V}=V^{r}\partial_{r}+V^{z}\partial_{z}.

Remark 1.5
.

In the translationally symmetric case, a parallel magnetic field
{\bf B}=F(y,z)\partial_{x}
defines a stationary solution to (
E-MHD
). Similarly,
({\bf u},{\bf B})=(0,F\partial_{x})
defines a stationary solution to (
Hall-MHD
). While one may repeat the analysis in the current paper to the case of

, we omit this case entirely for the sake of brevity.

1.4.1.

Given a stationary solution of the form

(

&\nabla\cdot b=0.\end{aligned}\right.

and

(1.13)

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\left(\frac{1}{2}\int_{M}|{b}|^{2}(t)\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}z\right)=\int_{M}((b\cdot\nabla)(\nabla\times{\mathbf{\Pi}})_{j})b^{j}\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}z.

where we ignore the condition for

1.5.1.
Linear illposedness

Let
b_{0}=b_{0}^{(\lambda)}
and
h_{0}=h_{0}^{(\lambda)}
be sequences of functions parameterized by
\lambda

\begin{split}&b_{0}=h_{0}(1+O(\lambda^{-1}))\end{split}

to denote that the support of
b_{0}
is contained in that of
h_{0}
, and
\|{b_{0}-h_{0}}\|_{W^{s,p}}\lesssim_{s,p}\lambda^{-1}\|{h_{0}}\|_{W^{s,p}}

Theorem A*

(Norm growth for the linearized (E-MHD) and (Hall-MHD))
.

Take
M=\mathbb{R}^{3}
and let
s\geq 0,p\geq 2
satisfy
s+1/p>1/2
. Fix some smooth cutoff function
\chi(z)
satisfying
\chi(z)=1
for
|z|\leq 1
.

(1)

Case of (E-MHD): Take a time interval
I\ni 0
and a time-dependent axisymmetric solution
{\mathbf{\Pi}}
to (
E-MHD
) defined on
I
, such that the initial data takes the form
{\mathbf{\Pi}}_{0}(r,z)=f(r)\partial_{\theta}
on
|z|\leq 1
where

satisfies the assumptions from
1.3.1
. We consider the linearized (
E-MHD
) (
1.12
) on around
{\mathbf{\Pi}}
.

Then, there exist
\delta>0
and a compactly supported smooth function

depending only on
{\mathbf{\Pi}}_{0}
such that with
\mathfrak{G}(r)
and
\mathfrak{b}(r)
as in Theorem
A
, for

, there exists an initial data set of the form

(1.16)

\quad b_{0}^{(\lambda)}=\Re(e^{i(\lambda\theta+\lambda\mathfrak{G}(r))})\mathfrak{b}(r)\chi(z)(1+O(\lambda^{-1}))

such that any corresponding

-solution

\|{b^{(\lambda)}(t)}\|_{W^{s,p}(M)}\geq c_{s,p,{\mathbf{\Pi}}_{0}}e^{c_{0}({\mathbf{\Pi}}_{0})\cdot(s+\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p})\lambda t}\|{b_{0}^{(\lambda)}}\|_{W^{s,p}(M)}\quad\mbox{for all}\quad t\in I\cap[0,\delta].

(2)

of (
Hall-MHD

, such that the initial data is of the form

, any

-solution

corresponding to the initial data

as in Case (1)) satisfies

\begin{split}&\|{b^{(\lambda)}(t)}\|_{W^{s,p}(M)}\geq c_{s,p,{\mathbf{\Pi}}_{0}}e^{c_{0}(\mathring{{\bf B}})\cdot(s+\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p})\lambda t}\|{b_{0}^{(\lambda)}}\|_{W^{s,p}(M)}\quad\mbox{for all}\quad t\in I\cap[0,\delta_{1}],\end{split}

Remark 1.6
.

Unfortunately, in the Hall-MHD case, the growth of

1.5.2.
Nonlinear illposedness

We now present our nonlinear illposedness results on
\mathbb{R}^{3}

Theorem B*

(Unboundedness of the solution map)
.

Let
M=\mathbb{R}^{3}
and the axisymmetric solution
({\bf V},{\mathbf{\Pi}})
(resp.
{\mathbf{\Pi}}
) is given as in the case (2) (resp. case (1)) of Theorem
A*
. Assume that for some

, the solution map for (

\displaystyle\left(\hbox{resp. }\mathcal{B}_{\epsilon}({\mathbf{\Pi}}_{0};H^{s}_{comp})\to L^{\infty}_{t}([0,\delta];H^{s_{0}})\right).

In the (

Remark 1.7

(Norm inflation)

One may rephrase the above statement as follows: in the (

Note that

(Nonexistence)

Let

Some remarks about these theorems are in order.

Remark 1.8

(Degenerate dispersion)
.

As in
[
19
]
, the main illposedness mechanism exploited in this paper is

, i.e., the rapid growth of frequencies of solutions that travel towards a degeneracy of the principal term. This mechanism may be found in other classes of quasilinear dispersive equations as well; see
[
5
,
20
]

Remark 1.9

(Wellposedness in the nondegenerate case)
.

In
[
21
]
, the following local well(!)posedness theorem for (
E-MHD
) on
\mathbb{R}^{3}
was proved:

Let
s>\frac{7}{2}
and consider a vector field
{\bf B}_{0}:\mathbb{R}^{3}\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
satisfying
\nabla\cdot{\bf B}_{0}=0
. Assume furthermore that
{\bf B}_{0}
satisfies the following properties:

(1)

Nondegeneracy.

{\bf B}_{0}(x)\neq 0
at every point
x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
,

(2)

Asymptotic uniformity.

\|{{\bf B}_{0}-{\bf e}_{3}}\|_{\ell^{1}_{\mathcal{I}}H^{s}}<+\infty
,

(3)

Nontrapping.
Every nonconstant solution
(X,\Xi)(t)
to the Hamiltonian system associated with

escapes to

or

Then the Cauchy problem for (

We remark that the techniques in
[
21
]
can be used to prove a similar theorem for (

[

Observe that the above theorem from
[

requires

to be asymptotic to the uniform magnetic field

The nonlinear illposedness results can be similarly stated and proved in the scale of

1.6.
Ideas of the proof

In this section, we discuss key ideas of the proof, mainly emphasizing the additional difficulties arising from having compact
z
-support. Main ideas in the
z
-independent case are given in
[
19
, Sections 1.6, 1.7]
.

1.6.1.
Analysis at the level of bicharacteristics.

Here we give a heuristic explanation as to why the same illposedness results are expected in the
z
-dependent case as well. This is most easily seen under the (formal) framework of bicharacteristics. As argued in
[
19
]
, our basic viewpoint towards (
Hall-MHD
) is to regard it as a quasilinear dispersive system, and the basic mechanism for the illposedness is given by the existence bicharacteristics quickly converging to the
degeneracy
of the background magnetic field. Let us recall the notion of bicharacteristics: Linearizing around a stationary magnetic field
\mathring{{\bf B}}
and neglecting the equation for the velocity, we may write the resulting equation in the form

\begin{split}\partial_{t}b+(\mathring{{\bf B}}\cdot\nabla)\nabla\times b=l.o.t.,\end{split}

where the right hand side contains either first or zeroth order terms in
b
. We may then diagonalize the matrix-valued symbol
{\bf p}=-(\mathring{{\bf B}}\cdot\xi)\xi\times
on the subspace
\{\zeta\in\mathbb{R}^{3}:\xi\cdot\zeta=0\}
with eigenvalues
\pm p=\pm i\mathring{{\bf B}}\cdot\xi|\xi|
. Then neglecting the lower order terms, the above vector equation splits into two scalar dispersive PDE of the form

\begin{split}\partial_{t}b_{\pm}\pm ip(i^{-1}\nabla)b_{\pm}=0.\end{split}

Then the associated ODE system with the Hamiltonian vector field
(\nabla_{\xi}p,-\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}p)
on
T^{*}M

\left\{\begin{aligned} \dot{X}&=\nabla_{\xi}p(X,\Xi),\\
\dot{\Xi}&=-\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}p(X,\Xi)\end{aligned}\right.

is referred to as the Hamiltonian ODE, and its solution
(X,\Xi)(t)
a bicharacteristic. This is a natural generalization of the notion of the group velocity from constant coefficient dispersive PDEs. Given a Gaussian-like initial data with physical and frequency centers
(X_{0},\Xi_{0})
, the associated bicharacteristics
(X,\Xi)(t)
describes the evolution of the (approximate) centers, at least for some period of time.

We now take the concrete choice
\mathring{{\bf B}}=y\chi(z)\partial_{x}
where
\chi(\cdot)\geq 0
is a smooth cutoff of
O(1)
length scale. The associated Hamiltonian is
y(X)\chi(z(X))\Xi_{x}|\Xi|
and since
\Xi_{x}
is conserved along bicharacteristics (from
x
-invariance of
\mathring{{\bf B}}
), we conclude that
y(X)\chi(z(X))|\Xi|
is conserved as well. Hence as in the case
\chi\equiv 1
,
y\rightarrow 0
will imply
|\Xi|\rightarrow+\infty
.

Towards this goal we first note that, as long as
|z(X)|\lesssim 1
, the Hamiltonian simply coincides with the one from the
z
-independent case. Take initial data of the form
X(0)=(0,1,z(0))
(
x(0)=0
can be taken without loss of generality) and
\Xi(0)=(\lambda,-\lambda,\Xi_{z}(0))
where
|z(0)|,|\Xi_{z}(0)|\lesssim 1
. As long as
|z(X)|\lesssim 1
, we have

\left\{\begin{aligned} \dot{z}&=\lambda y\frac{\Xi_{z}}{|\Xi|},\\
\dot{\Xi_{z}}&=0,\end{aligned}\right.

and hence we see that if we have
|y(X)|\lesssim 1
and
|\Xi_{z}|/|\Xi|\lesssim\lambda^{-1}
for some interval of time, we can guarantee that
|z(X)|\lesssim 1
as well. But then this means that the behavior of the bicharacteristics is the same as in the
z
-independent case; in particular,

\begin{split}|\Xi(t)|\simeq\lambda e^{\lambda t},\quad y(t)\simeq e^{-\lambda t}\end{split}

which in turn guarantees
|y(X)|\ll 1
and
|\Xi_{z}|/|\Xi|\ll\lambda^{-1}
! (This circular argument can be made rigorous with a continuity argument.) This shows that the bicharacteristics from the
z
-independent case still provides a good approximation within a time interval which is
independent
of
\lambda
, as long as the initial
z
-frequency is kept at
O(1)
. Essentially the same analysis can be repeated for the axisymmetric magnetic field of the form
f(r)\chi(z)\partial_{\theta}
with the caveat that it actually does not define a stationary solution to (
Hall-MHD
); see
1.6.5
below. This heuristic argument suggests that given a
z
-independent linear approximate solution, a simple cutoff in the
z
-direction still gives an approximate solution. Of course, this procedure gives rise to various technical difficulties, which shall be discussed in the following.

1.6.2.
Generalized energy identities.

Our main technical tool in the illposedness arguments (both linear and nonlinear) is the so-called
generalized energy identity
, which is a deceptively simple consequence of the (usual) energy identity. Let us briefly explain the ideas. Consider a linear equation of the form

(1.17)

\begin{split}\partial_{t}b+\mathcal{L}[b]=\mathcal{B}[b],\end{split}

where
\mathcal{L}
is antisymmetric and
\mathcal{B}
is a zeroth order operator;

\begin{split}\langle{\mathcal{L}[f],g}\rangle=-\langle{f,\mathcal{L}[g]}\rangle,\quad\|{\mathcal{B}[f]}\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\|{f}\|_{L^{2}}.\end{split}

Then one can see that not only the
L^{2}
-norm of a solution
b
is bounded in terms of the initial data, but also does the
L^{2}
-inner product of two solutions: for
b
and
\tilde{b}
satisfying (
1.17
), we have

\begin{split}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\langle{b,\tilde{b}}\rangle=\langle{\mathcal{B}[b],\tilde{b}}\rangle-\langle{\mathcal{L}[b],\tilde{b}}\rangle+\langle{b,\mathcal{B}[\tilde{b}]}\rangle-\langle{b,\mathcal{L}[\tilde{b}]}\rangle\end{split}

so that after cancellations,

(1.18)

\begin{split}\left|\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\langle{b,\tilde{b}}\rangle\right|\lesssim\|{b}\|_{L^{2}}\|{\tilde{b}}\|_{L^{2}}\end{split}

which implies (after integrating the above in time and then using that the
L^{2}
-norm is stable under (
1.17
))

\begin{split}\left|\langle{b(t),\tilde{b}(t)}\rangle-\langle{b_{0},\tilde{b}_{0}}\rangle\right|\lesssim t\|{b_{0}}\|_{L^{2}}\|{\tilde{b}_{0}}\|_{L^{2}}.\end{split}

Finally, if the “angle” between
b_{0}
and
\tilde{b}_{0}
is small; that is,
\langle{b_{0},\tilde{b}_{0}}\rangle\gtrsim\|{b_{0}}\|_{L^{2}}\|{\tilde{b}_{0}}\|_{L^{2}}
, we conclude that for some interval
I
of time,

(1.19)

\begin{split}\inf_{t\in I}\,\langle{b(t),\tilde{b}(t)}\rangle\geq c\|{b_{0}}\|_{L^{2}}\|{\tilde{b}_{0}}\|_{L^{2}}\end{split}

for some
c>0
. In the following we shall frequently refer to (
1.18
) (or its simple consequence (
1.19
)) as
generalized energy estimate
. The merit in the generalized energy estimate lies in the flexibility involved in its derivation; say we cannot solve (
1.17
) directly, but somehow are given an approximate solution
\tilde{b}
, satisfying instead

\begin{split}\partial_{t}\tilde{b}+\mathcal{L}[\tilde{b}]=\mathcal{B}^{\prime}[\tilde{b}]\end{split}

where
\mathcal{B}^{\prime}
is some bounded operator in
L^{2}
which does not need to be related in any way with
\mathcal{B}
. But note that in this case, the estimates (
1.18
) and (
1.19
) still hold under the same assumptions. Furthermore, one may observe that we actually do not need
\mathcal{B}^{\prime}
and
\mathcal{B}
to be bounded operators in
L^{2}
, but just need that
\|{\mathcal{B}^{*}[\tilde{b}]}\|_{L^{2}},\|{\mathcal{B}^{\prime}[\tilde{b}]}\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\|{\tilde{b}}\|_{L^{2}}
. In conclusion, as far as one is concerned with deriving the generalized energy estimate, one may regard
a priori
and
a posteriori
bounded terms as errors.

Having a generalized energy estimate in hand, the task of showing growth of
b
in high-regularity Sobolev spaces reduces to that of showing
decay
of an approximate solution
\tilde{b}
in low-regularity Sobolev spaces. As a simple example, if we are able to show
\|{\tilde{b}(t)}\|_{L^{1}}\lesssim e^{-t}\|{\tilde{b}_{0}}\|_{L^{2}}
, we conclude at once from (
1.19
) that
\|{b(t)}\|_{L^{\infty}}\gtrsim e^{t}\|{b_{0}}\|_{L^{2}}
. Of course in general showing degeneration in low regularity spaces could be a difficult task.

So far we have demonstrated that showing growth of a solution to (
1.17
) can be reduces to construction and analysis of an appropriate approximate solution. There is a general such procedure, commonly called as WKB analysis, which we briefly illustrate in
1.6.3
. Then we are in a position to discuss
additional
difficulties in proving the generalized energy estimate in the
z
-dependent case, over the
z
-independent case.

1.6.3.
WKB analysis.

Keeping the notation above, say we are interested in constructing an approximate solution
\tilde{b}
for (
1.17
). The discussion in the above suggest that we can completely neglect the a priori bounded terms from the beginning and consider
z
-independent solutions; say the latter independence assumption simplifies the operator
\mathcal{L}
into
\mathcal{L}^{\prime}
; now we want to approximately solve

(1.20)

\begin{split}\partial_{t}\tilde{b}+\mathcal{L}^{\prime}\tilde{b}=0.\end{split}

We use the standard WKB analysis, which can be viewed as an expansion of the solution in frequency. Take
\lambda\gg 1
and prepare an ansatz of the following form

\begin{split}\tilde{b}=e^{i\lambda\Phi}(h^{(0)}+\lambda^{-1}h^{(1)}+\cdots).\end{split}

We have a freedom of choice for the initial data of
\Phi,h^{(0)}
, and so on, which we can use to our advantage. Formally plugging in the ansatz into (
1.20
) and organizing the terms by the powers of
\lambda
, one obtains a hierarchy of evolution equations; the first one involving only
\Phi
, the second describing the evolution of
h^{(0)}
with coefficients depending on
\Phi
, and so on. While this inductively determines each function in the expansion, it turns out that in our concrete cases,
e^{i\lambda\Phi}h^{(0)}
is already provides a good approximate solution, in the
L^{2}
-sense.

1.6.4.
Bogovskiǐ operator and broken antisymmetry.

As we have mentioned in the above, we would like to declare that
\chi\tilde{b}
still provides a good approximation, where
\chi
is a simple cutoff function in
z
, and
\tilde{b}
is
z
-independent. An immediate problem one faces is that
\chi\tilde{b}
cannot be divergence free, a property which is essential for the principal operator
\mathcal{L}
to be anti-symmetric to begin with. Moreover, the initial data
b_{0}
should be divergence-free, close to
\chi\tilde{b}
at least in the
L^{2}
-norm, and have compact support. It is tempting to just apply the Leray projector on
\chi\tilde{b}
but in terms of the compact support property, a much better alternative is provided by the Bogovskiǐ operator introduced in
[
4
]
. This is also used in an essential way in the (
Hall-MHD
) case;
\chi\tilde{u}
is not divergence-free, so to remove the pressure term, we need to test
u
against the divergence-free part (defined by the Bogovskiǐ operator) of
\chi\tilde{u}
.

We still need to explain how the first difficulty is handled. The principal operator in the Hall current term is of second order and involves
\partial_{zx}
and
\partial_{zy}
derivatives. While going through the generalized energy identity, there appear terms with
\partial_{x}
and
\partial_{y}
on
\tilde{b}
when a
z
-derivatives falls on the cutoff (anti-symmetry is broken). However all such terms altogether cancel out to give rise an
L^{2}
-bounded term after some juggling of derivatives with the divergence-free condition on
b
. Of course this was anticipated from
1.6.1
, or can be alternatively seen by repeating the WKB analysis in the
z
-dependent case.

1.6.5.
A priori estimates for the axisymmetric Hall-MHD

Utilizing the axisymmetric background magnetic fields (over the translationally symmetric ones) is the key to obtain illposedness for compact data in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
. However, compared to the setting of
[
19
]
, one immediately sees a problem: there are no non-trivial axisymmetric
stationary
magnetic fields for (
E-MHD
) with compact support in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
. This forces us to work with time-dependent background solutions, which sounds like a nonsense because in the end, we have proven
nonexistence
of a solution to the initial value problem. Interestingly enough it is known that under the assumption of axisymmetry, existence and uniqueness of local-in-time smooth axisymmetric solutions can be established (
[
7
,
18
]
).
2
2

2

This statement should be interpreted carefully, as uniqueness is not guaranteed for (
E-MHD
) and (
Hall-MHD
), so that there is no reason to believe that the solution is axisymmetric even if the initial data is so. This point is emphasized in
[
18
]
.
We give a proof of a priori estimates both for the (
E-MHD
) and (
Hall-MHD
) cases, although some cases have been already covered in
[
18
]
. In the case of (
E-MHD
), the resulting equation for the axisymmetric magnetic field is exactly the (inviscid) Burgers equation in the
z
-direction, which is locally wellposed but definitely blows up in finite time for compact support initial data. This is not an issue for linear illposedness since the growth happens essentially instantaneously. Even though the magnetic field is time-dependent, all we need is that this background is constant in the
z
-direction for some intervals in
t
and
z
, which holds for solutions to the Burgers equation. In view of this, serious difficulties arise in the (
Hall-MHD
) case. Even if the background velocity is initially zero, the velocity becomes immediately nonzero by the axisymmetric magnetic field for
t>0
, which in turn breaks
z
-invariance in any small interval of
z
. This is handled by quantifying smallness of the
z
-variation of the magnetic field for small
t>0
. A much more serious problem is that, in general, the velocity field will immediately move the point (a hyperplane in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, strictly speaking) of degeneracy for the magnetic field. Recall that the illposedness mechanism is the convergence of wave packet solutions towards the degenerate point. The fact that the degenerate point is moving with time destroys the estimates for the wavepackets which were valid for an
O(1)
-interval of time; in the worst possible case, the estimates are only valid for a time interval of size
O(\lambda^{-1}\ln\lambda)
, where
\lambda\gg 1
is the frequency of the wave packet at the initial time. Even showing this requires proving smallness-in-time estimates for various quantities involving the background axisymmetric solution.

Remark 1.11
.

It is desirable (and could be of independent interest) to have a situation where the degenerate point is forced to be fixed, even under the axisymmetric Hall-MHD evolution. This is the case for axis-degenerate magnetic fields, which are simply steady magnetic fields which are degenerate on the axis
\{r=0\}
. One may perform the analysis of wave packets in this case, which gives admissible error estimates for
O(\lambda^{\alpha})
-timescales where
\alpha>0
is a factor depending on the order of axis degeneracy.

1.6.6.
Nonlinear illposedness

There is no general method for passing from a linear illposedness result to a corresponding nonlinear result; in certain cases a nonlinear system can be wellposed while it has a linearization which is illposed (
[
14
]
). In our case, we use the (generalized) energy estimate in a crucial way in deducing a nonlinear illposedness result from a linear one. Using a similar notation from the above, the equation for the perturbation takes the form

\begin{split}\partial_{t}b+\mathcal{L}[b]=\mathcal{B}[b]+Q[b,b]\end{split}

where we write
Q[b,b]
for the nonlinear terms. In practice, we have in
Q
terms of the form
b\nabla^{2}b
and
(\nabla b)^{2}
. Note that upon assuming towards contradiction that
b
belongs to some
H^{s}
with
s
large, we may bound the contribution from
Q
in deriving a generalized energy estimate for
b
using Sobolev inequalities:

\begin{split}\left|\langle{Q[b,b],\tilde{b}}\rangle\right|\lesssim\|{b}\|_{H^{s}}\|{b}\|_{L^{2}}\|{\tilde{b}}\|_{L^{2}}.\end{split}

This allows us to again deduce (
1.19
), which gives a contradiction to the boundedness of
b
upon taking the initial frequency support of
b
to be very large. This simple estimate makes precise the general belief that the nonlinear evolution follows approximately the linear one for a short time interval. In the three-dimensional case, one will see a slight difference in the minimum threshold for
s
(compared to the
z
-independent case) since one needs to use three-dimensional embedding results rather than two-dimensional ones.

The above gives an outline of how the unboundedness of the solution operator (assuming existence) can be proved. As we have done in
[
19
]
,
nonexistence
of the solution operator can be actually shown; the simple idea is to prepare a background solution with countably many points of degeneracy. Then for each degenerate point one puts a small (in some fixed
H^{s}
with
s
large enough) perturbation with increasing frequency. If the same argument as in the above can be repeated independently near each degenerate point, it will show that at some positive time moment (where the solution is assumed to
exist
), the
H^{s}
-norm should be greater than any large number, which is a contradiction.

Other than a few minor issues (related with ensuring the divergence-free conditions for some vector functions), there is not much additional difficulties in proving nonlinear illposedness in the
z
-dependent case over the
z
-independent case. On the contrary, having an unbounded direction
\mathbb{R}_{z}
can be used to our advantage in the nonexistence argument, as we can place countably many points of degeneracy along the
z
-direction which was definitely not possible in our previous work.

2.1.
Bogovskiǐ operator

In later sections, it will be important to be able to systematically “invert” the divergence operator to ensure divergence-free condition on the initial perturbations. One may simply apply the Leray projector to a vector field, but then the compact support property will be immediately lost. To avoid this problem we use the Bogovskiǐ operator introduced in
[
4
]
. Detailed information regarding the properties of this operator can be found in several textbooks; see for instance
[
1
,
13
]
. Given a bounded open set
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
, we write
d(U)=\sup_{\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}\in U}|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y}|
and
i(U)=\sup_{\exists\mathbf{x}\in{U},B(\mathbf{x},R)\subset{U}}R
.

Proposition 2.1

(
[
13
, Lemma 3.1]
)
.

Fix some smooth function
w\in C^{\infty}
supported in the unit ball in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
and satisfying
\int w=1
, and let
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
be a bounded open set with Lipschitz boundary. Take
\mathbf{x}_{0}\in U
such that
B(\mathbf{x}_{0},i(U))\subset\overline{U}
and
\overline{U}
is star-shaped with respect to
B(\mathbf{x}_{0},i(U))
(i.e., for every
{\bf x}\in\overline{U}
and
{\bf x}^{\prime}\in B(\mathbf{x}_{0},i(U))
, the line segment from
{\bf x}
to
{\bf x}^{\prime}
is contained in
\overline{U}
). Rescale
w
to define
w_{U}(\mathbf{x})=i(U)^{-3}w(i(U)^{-1}(\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{x}_{0}))
. We define the kernel

(2.1)

\begin{split}G_{U}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y}}{s}w\left(\mathbf{y}+\frac{\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y}}{s}\right)\frac{ds}{s^{3}}.\end{split}

Assume that
g\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
is supported in
U
and satisfies
\int_{U}g=0
. Then, we have that
h
defined by

\begin{split}&h(\mathbf{x})=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}G_{U}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})g(\mathbf{y})\mathrm{d}\mathbf{y}\end{split}

is supported in
U
and solves
\mathrm{div}\,h=g
, with the estimates

(2.2)

\begin{split}\|{h}\|_{L^{2}}\leq\frac{C_{n}}{d({U})}\left(\frac{d({U})}{i({U})}\right)^{n}\left(1+\frac{d({U})}{i({U})}\right)\|{g}\|_{L^{2}},\qquad\|{\nabla h}\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\left(\frac{d({U})}{i({U})}\right)^{3}\left(1+\frac{d({U})}{i({U})}\right)\|{g}\|_{L^{2}},\end{split}

for a universal constant
C>0
.

For our applications, we shall take
{\bf x}_{0}
to lie on the
z
-axis and
U
to be an (axisymmetric) cylinder of the form
U=\{(x,y,z):|{(x,y)}|<\ell,\,|{z}|<\ell\}
for some
\ell>0
. Abusing the terminology, we will refer to such cylinder
U
as a
cube
with side-length
\ell
. Choosing
i(U)=c\ell
for some universal small constant
c>0
, we simply have

(2.3)

\begin{split}\|{\nabla^{m+1}h}\|_{L^{2}}\leq C_{m}\|{\nabla^{m}g}\|_{L^{2}}\end{split}

for all
m\geq 0
. Indeed, observe that
d(U)
is comparable to
\ell
(and hence to
i(U)
, and that the star-shape condition follows from the convexity of
U
.

Moreover, we shall replace the kernel
G_{U}
in (
2.1
) by

\begin{split}\mathring{G}_{U}(r,\theta,z,r^{\prime},\theta^{\prime},z^{\prime}):=\int_{0}^{2\pi}G_{U}(r,\theta+\theta_{0},z,r^{\prime},\theta^{\prime}+\theta_{0},z^{\prime})\,\mathrm{d}\theta_{0}\end{split}

and define

\mathrm{div}^{-1}_{U}g({\bf x}):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\mathring{G}_{U}({\bf x},{\bf y})g({\bf y})\,\mathrm{d}{\bf y}.

With this choice, we keep the right-inverse and support-preserving properties of the operator in Proposition
2.1
, and have the additional convenient property that
[\partial_{\theta},\mathrm{div}^{-1}_{U}]=0
.
In what follows, if the choice of

is clear from the context, we will simply write
\mathrm{div}^{-1}
.

2.2.
A priori estimates for axisymmetric solutions

The purpose of subsection is to derive a few elementary a priori estimates for axisymmetric solutions to (

) and (

), which will serve as background solutions in our proof of instability/illposedness.

We begin by recalling that if
{\bf U}
be of the form
{\bf U}=U(r,z)\partial_{\theta}

In what follows, we write
{\mathbf{\Pi}}
instead

for an axisymmetric solution to (

). The system (

) for solutions of the form
{\mathbf{\Pi}}=\Pi(r,z)\partial_{\theta}
may be rewritten as

(2.4)

\partial_{t}\Pi-2\Pi\partial_{z}\Pi=0.

For an axisymmetric solution to (

), we write

instead

. The system (

) for solutions of the form

\left\{\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}\Omega+(V^{r}\partial_{r}+V^{z}\partial_{z})\Omega+2\Pi\partial_{z}\Pi&=\nu\not{\Delta}\Omega,\\
\partial_{t}\Pi+(V^{r}\partial_{r}+V^{z}\partial_{z})\Pi-2\Pi\partial_{z}\Pi&=0,\\
\partial_{r}V^{r}+\partial_{z}V^{z}&=0,\end{aligned}\right.

where
\nabla\times{\bf V}=\Omega(r,z)\partial_{\theta}

with respect to which we have

2.2.1.

We start with a lemma concerning the relationship between the standard

(1)

(2.7)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{r}V^{r}(r,z)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{r^{2}}\int_{0}^{r}(\partial_{x}{\bf V}^{x}+\partial_{y}{\bf V}^{y})(r^{\prime},z)r^{\prime}\,\mathrm{d}r^{\prime}.

(2)

For any nonnegative integer

(2.8)

\displaystyle\|{{\bf U}}\|_{H^{m}}^{2}

\displaystyle\simeq_{m}\|{rU^{\theta}}\|_{H^{m}(r\mathrm{d}r\mathrm{d}z)}^{2}+\|{U^{\theta}}\|_{H^{m-1}(r\mathrm{d}r\mathrm{d}z)}^{2},

Lemma 3.1

Proof.

\partial_{z}\Pi(t)=0
on the set
Z(t,[-10\ell,10\ell])
. Since
\chi
vanishes for
|z|\geq 2\ell
, it suffices that

2t=\int_{0}^{t}\|{2\Pi(s)}\|_{L^{\infty}}ds\leq 8\ell

for
\chi(z)\partial_{z}\Pi(t,r,z)=0
.
∎

In light of the above lemma, we shall (implicitly) assume that
\delta\leq 4\ell
.

We take the degenerating wave packets corresponding to
\widetilde{{\bf B}}=f(r)\partial_{\theta}
from the previous section, and introduce the simplifying notation
\tilde{b}=(-\nabla^{\perp}\tilde{\psi},\tilde{b}^{z})
. Here, we fix some universal smooth function
h(0,\cdot)
and use (
2.34
) to define
g_{0}
. We shall freely use the estimates
\|{g_{0}}\|_{H^{m}_{x,y}}\lesssim_{m}\ell^{-m}\|{g_{0}}\|_{L^{2}_{x,y}}
, and suppress from writing out the dependence of constants in
m,C_{m}
.

The equation for
\tilde{b}
can be written in the following economical form

\begin{split}\partial_{t}\tilde{b}+(\tilde{{\bf B}}\cdot\nabla)(\nabla\times\tilde{b})-((\nabla\times\tilde{b})\cdot\nabla)\tilde{{\bf B}}=\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{\tilde{b}},\end{split}

with

\sup_{t\in[0,\delta]}\|{\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{\tilde{b}}}\|_{L^{2}_{x,y}}\lesssim\ell^{-2}\|{g_{0}}\|_{L^{2}_{x,y}}.

We now fix our choice of initial data
b_{0}
. To take it to be close in
L^{2}
to
\chi(z)\tilde{b}_{0}
, under the divergence-free constraint, we write

\begin{split}&b_{0}=\chi(z)\tilde{b}_{0}+\zeta\end{split}

for some
\zeta
. Then, taking the divergence of both sides, we see that
\zeta
must satisfy

\begin{split}&0=\chi^{\prime}(z)\tilde{b}_{0}^{z}+\mathrm{div}\,\zeta,\end{split}

which suggests the choice
b_{0}:=\chi(z)\tilde{b}_{0}-\mathrm{div}_{U}^{-1}(\chi^{\prime}\tilde{b}_{0}^{z})
, where
U
can be taken to be any cube of side length
20\ell
(say) containing the support of
\chi^{\prime}\tilde{b}_{0}^{z}
. Using (
2.3
), we estimate that

\begin{split}\|{\partial_{\theta}(\mathrm{div}_{U}^{-1}\partial_{\theta}^{-1}(\chi^{\prime}\tilde{b}_{0}^{z}))}\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\lambda^{-1}\|{\chi^{\prime}\lambda\partial_{\theta}^{-1}\tilde{b}_{0}^{z}}\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\lambda^{-1}\ell^{\frac{1}{2}}\|{\tilde{b}_{0}^{z}}\|_{L^{2}_{x,y}}.\end{split}

Hence we obtain in particular that

(3.1)

\begin{split}&\langle{b_{0},\chi\tilde{b}_{0}}\rangle=\ell^{\frac{1}{2}}\|{b_{0}}\|_{L^{2}}\|{\tilde{b}_{0}}\|_{L^{2}_{x,y}}(1+O(\lambda^{-1})).\end{split}

3.1.2.
Generalized energy estimate

Now that we have specified
b
and
\tilde{b}
, let us proceed with an argument involving the general energy identity: we compute

\begin{split}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\langle{b,\chi\tilde{b}}\rangle&=-\langle{\nabla\times((\nabla\times b)\times\mathring{{\bf B}}),\chi\tilde{b}}\rangle-\langle{b,\chi\nabla\times((\nabla\times\tilde{b})\times\tilde{{\bf B}})}\rangle\\
&\qquad+\langle{((\nabla\times\mathring{{\bf B}})\cdot\nabla)b,\chi\tilde{b}}\rangle-\langle{(b\cdot\nabla)(\nabla\times\mathring{{\bf B}}),\chi\tilde{b}}\rangle+\langle{b,\chi\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{\tilde{b}}}\rangle.\end{split}

First, we take care of the terms that are trivially bounded in
L^{2}
:

\begin{split}\left|-\langle{(b\cdot\nabla)(\nabla\times\mathring{{\bf B}}),\chi\tilde{b}}\rangle+\langle{b,\chi\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{\tilde{b}}}\rangle\right|&\lesssim\|{\nabla^{2}\mathring{{\bf B}}}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|{b}\|_{L^{2}}\|{\chi\tilde{b}}\|_{L^{2}}+\|{b}\|_{L^{2}}\|{\chi\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{\tilde{b}}}\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\ell^{-4+\frac{1}{2}}\|{b}\|_{L^{2}}\|{g_{0}}\|_{L^{2}_{x,y}}\end{split}

Then, we rewrite the first term as

\begin{split}-\langle{\nabla\times((\nabla\times b)\times\mathring{{\bf B}}),\chi\tilde{b}}\rangle&=\langle{b,\nabla\times((\nabla\times(\chi\tilde{b}))\times\mathring{{\bf B}})}\rangle=\langle{b,(\mathring{{\bf B}}\cdot\nabla)(\nabla\times(\chi\tilde{b}))}\rangle-\langle{b,(\nabla\times(\chi\tilde{b}))\cdot\nabla\mathring{{\bf B}}}\rangle\end{split}

and organize the remaining terms as follows:

\begin{split}\mathrm{I}=\langle{((\nabla\times\mathring{{\bf B}})\cdot\nabla)b,\chi\tilde{b}}\rangle,\end{split}

\begin{split}\mathrm{II}=-\langle{b,(\nabla\times(\chi\tilde{b}))\cdot\nabla\mathring{{\bf B}}-\chi(\nabla\times\tilde{b})\cdot\nabla\tilde{{\bf B}}}\rangle,\end{split}

and

\begin{split}\mathrm{III}=\langle{b,(\mathring{{\bf B}}\cdot\nabla)(\nabla\times(\chi\tilde{b}))-\chi(\tilde{{\bf B}}\cdot\nabla)(\nabla\times\tilde{b})}\rangle.\end{split}

Estimate for I.
Using
\nabla\times\mathring{{\bf B}}=-r\partial_{z}\Pi\partial_{r}+r^{-1}\partial_{r}(r^{2}\Pi)\partial_{z}=r^{-1}\partial_{r}(r^{2}f)\partial_{z}
on the support of
\chi
,

\begin{split}|\mathrm{I}|=|\langle{r^{-1}\partial_{r}(r^{2}\Pi)\partial_{z}b,\chi\tilde{b}}\rangle|&\lesssim\ell^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\|{rf^{\prime}(r)}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|{f}\|_{L^{\infty}})\|{b}\|_{L^{2}}\|{g_{0}}\|_{L^{2}_{x,y}}\lesssim\ell^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|{b}\|_{L^{2}}\|{g_{0}}\|_{L^{2}_{x,y}}.\end{split}

Estimate for II.
Note that we obtain cancellations unless a derivative falls on
\chi
in the expression
\nabla\times(\chi\tilde{b})
, simply because
\mathring{{\bf B}}=\tilde{{\bf B}}
on the support of
\chi
. Then there are no derivatives falling on
b,\tilde{b}
, so that

\begin{split}&|\mathrm{II}|\lesssim\ell^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|{b}\|_{L^{2}}\|{g_{0}}\|_{L^{2}_{x,y}}.\end{split}

Estimate for III.
We begin with writing
\nabla\times(\chi\tilde{b})=\chi\nabla\times\tilde{b}+\chi^{\prime}(z)(-r\tilde{b}^{\theta}\partial_{r}+r^{-1}\tilde{b}^{r}\partial_{\theta})
and

\begin{split}\mathrm{III}&=-\langle{b^{r},\chi^{\prime}(z)f(r)\partial_{\theta}(r\tilde{b}^{\theta})}\rangle+\langle{rb^{\theta},r\chi^{\prime}(z)f(r)\partial_{\theta}(r^{-1}\tilde{b}^{r})}\rangle\\
&=\langle{b^{r},\chi^{\prime}(z)f(r)(r\partial_{r}\tilde{b}^{r}+\tilde{b}^{r})}\rangle+\langle{\partial_{r}b^{r}+r^{-1}b^{r}+\partial_{z}b^{z},\chi^{\prime}(z)f(r)r\tilde{b}^{r}}\rangle.\end{split}

Note that an integration by parts gives

\begin{split}&\langle{b^{r},\chi^{\prime}(z)f(r)r\partial_{r}\tilde{b}^{r}}\rangle=-\langle{\partial_{r}b^{r},\chi^{\prime}(z)f(r)r\tilde{b}^{r}}\rangle-2\langle{b^{r},\chi^{\prime}(z)f(r)\tilde{b}^{r}}\rangle-\langle{b^{r},\chi^{\prime}(z)f^{\prime}(r)r\tilde{b}^{r}}\rangle,\end{split}

so that after cancellations,

\begin{split}|\mathrm{III}|=|\langle{b^{r},\chi^{\prime}(z)f^{\prime}(r)r\tilde{b}^{r}}\rangle-\langle{b^{z},\chi^{\prime\prime}(z)f(r)r\tilde{b}^{r}}\rangle|&\lesssim(\ell^{-\frac{3}{2}}\|{rf}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\ell^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|{rf^{\prime}(r)}\|_{L^{\infty}})\|{b}\|_{L^{2}}\|{g_{0}}\|_{L^{2}_{x,y}}\\
&\lesssim\ell^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|{b}\|_{L^{2}}\|{g_{0}}\|_{L^{2}_{x,y}}.\end{split}

Collecting all the estimates, we conclude that

(3.2)

\begin{split}\left|\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\langle{b,\chi\tilde{b}}\rangle\right|\lesssim\ell^{-\frac{3}{2}}\|{b}\|_{L^{2}}\|{g_{0}}\|_{L^{2}_{x,y}}.\end{split}

3.1.3.
Growth of Sobolev norms

Restricting further to a time interval of length
\epsilon\ell^{2}
with some small absolute constant
\epsilon>0
, we may ensure that
\|{b}\|_{L^{2}}\leq 2\|{b_{0}}\|_{L^{2}}
. Within this time interval, we integrate (
3.2
) in time and use (
3.1
) to obtain

\begin{split}&\langle{b,\chi\tilde{b}}\rangle(t)\geq\frac{1}{2}\ell^{\frac{1}{2}}\|{b_{0}}\|_{L^{2}}\|{g_{0}}\|_{L^{2}_{x,y}},\end{split}

by further restricting the interval of time to satisfy
t\leq\epsilon\ell^{2}
. The rest of the proof is straightforward using the degeneration estimate from Proposition
2.12
and completely parallel to the corresponding argument from
[
19
, proof of Theorem
A
]
.

In the case
s=0
and
p>2
, we use the degeneration estimate (
2.35
) for
\|{\tilde{b}}\|_{L^{2}_{\theta}L^{p^{\prime}}_{rdr}}
where
p^{\prime}<2
is the dual exponent of
p
, and

\begin{split}&\|{b(t)}\|_{L^{p}}\|{\chi\tilde{b}(t)}\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}}\geq\langle{b,\chi\tilde{b}}\rangle(t)\geq\frac{1}{2}\ell^{\frac{1}{2}}\|{b_{0}}\|_{L^{2}}\|{g_{0}}\|_{L^{2}_{x,y}}\end{split}

with

\begin{split}\|{\chi\tilde{b}(t)}\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}}\lesssim_{\ell}\|{\tilde{b}(t)}\|_{L^{2}_{\theta}L^{p^{\prime}}_{rdr}}\end{split}

to deduce that, up to some powers of
\ell
,

\begin{split}&\|{b(t)}\|_{L^{p}}\gtrsim_{\ell}e^{c_{0}(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p})\lambda t}\|{b_{0}}\|_{L^{2}},\qquad t\in[0,\epsilon\ell^{2}].\end{split}

The general case of
s\geq 0
and
s+\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}>0
can be handled in a similar way. ∎

3.2.
Case of (
Hall-MHD
)

In this subsection, we treat the (
Hall-MHD
) case. While the computations are significantly more involved in this case, the overall strategy is essentially the same with the case of (
E-MHD
), and in particular, the initial data will be taken to be
u_{0}=0
and
b_{0}
the same as before. The key difference lies in the way a suitable lower bound is obtained in the
L^{2}
-inner product
\langle{b,\chi\tilde{b}}\rangle
.

For convenience, let us recall the system of equations (
1.10
) satisfied by an
L^{2}
-solution
(u,b)
around a time-dependent axisymmetric background:

(3.3)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}b+{\mathbf{\Pi}}\cdot\nabla(\nabla\times b)-(\nabla\times b)\cdot\nabla{\mathbf{\Pi}}-(\nabla\times{\mathbf{\Pi}})\cdot\nabla b+{\bf V}\cdot\nabla b-{\mathbf{\Pi}}\cdot\nabla u\\
&\quad=-b\cdot\nabla(\nabla\times{\mathbf{\Pi}})+b\cdot\nabla{\bf V}-u\cdot\nabla{\mathbf{\Pi}},\\
&\partial_{t}u+{\bf V}\cdot\nabla u+\nabla p-\nu\Delta u-(\nabla\times b)\times{\mathbf{\Pi}}=-u\cdot\nabla{\bf V}+(\nabla\times{\mathbf{\Pi}})\times b.\end{aligned}\right.

Here, we used the notation
{\mathbf{\Pi}}=\Pi\partial_{\theta}
and
{\bf V}=\nabla\times(-\Delta)^{-1}(\Omega\partial_{\theta})
, where the pair of scalar-valued functions
(\Pi,\Omega)
provides a solution to (
1.8
).

Furthermore, recall the system for
(\tilde{u},\tilde{b})
(omitting the superscript
(\nu)
for simplicity)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}\tilde{u}-f\partial_{\theta}\tilde{b}=\boldsymbol{\delta}_{\tilde{u}}\\
&\partial_{t}\tilde{b}+f\partial_{\theta}(\nabla\times\tilde{b})+r^{-1}f\partial_{\theta}\tilde{b}\partial_{\theta}=\boldsymbol{\delta}_{\tilde{b}}.\end{aligned}\right.

We shall need the following simple estimate, which is a straightforward consequence of Proposition
2.12
:

(3.4)

\begin{split}\|{\partial_{r}\tilde{b}}\|_{L^{2}_{x,y}}&\lesssim\lambda\ell^{-2}\|{f^{-1}}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathrm{supp}(\tilde{b}(t)))}\|{g_{0}}\|_{L^{2}_{x,y}}\lesssim\lambda\ell^{-2}e^{c_{1}\lambda t}\|{g_{0}}\|_{L^{2}_{x,y}}.\end{split}

Similarly, we have

(3.5)

\begin{split}\|{\partial_{\theta}\tilde{b}}\|_{L^{2}_{x,y}}&\lesssim\lambda\ell^{-2}\|{g_{0}}\|_{L^{2}_{x,y}},\qquad\|{\partial_{\theta}\partial_{r}\tilde{b}}\|_{L^{2}_{x,y}}\lesssim\lambda^{2}\ell^{-3}e^{c_{1}\lambda t}\|{g_{0}}\|_{L^{2}_{x,y}}.\end{split}

We write out the terms as follows:

\begin{split}&\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\left(\langle{u,\chi\tilde{u}-\mathrm{div}^{-1}(\chi^{\prime}\tilde{u}^{z})}\rangle+\langle{b,\chi\tilde{b}}\rangle\right)=\mathrm{I}+\mathrm{II}+\mathrm{III}+\mathrm{IV}+\mathrm{V}+\mathrm{VI},\end{split}

where

\begin{split}&\mathrm{I}=\langle{\partial_{t}u+\nabla p,-\mathrm{div}^{-1}(\chi^{\prime}\tilde{u}^{z})}\rangle+\langle{u,\mathrm{div}^{-1}(\chi^{\prime}\partial_{t}\tilde{u}^{z})}\rangle,\end{split}

\begin{split}&\mathrm{II}=\langle{u,\chi\boldsymbol{\delta}_{\tilde{u}}}\rangle+\langle{b,\chi\boldsymbol{\delta}_{\tilde{b}}}\rangle,\end{split}

\begin{split}&\mathrm{III}=\langle{-b\cdot\nabla(\nabla\times{\mathbf{\Pi}})+b\cdot\nabla{\bf V}-u\cdot\nabla{\mathbf{\Pi}},\chi\tilde{u}}\rangle+\langle{-u\cdot\nabla{\bf V}+(\nabla\times{\mathbf{\Pi}})\times b,\chi\tilde{b}}\rangle,\end{split}

\begin{split}&\mathrm{IV}=-\langle{{\bf V}\cdot\nabla u,\chi\tilde{u}}\rangle-\langle{{\bf V}\cdot\nabla b,\chi\tilde{b}}\rangle,\end{split}

\begin{split}&\mathrm{V}=\langle{\nu\Delta u,\chi\tilde{u}}\rangle+\langle{(\nabla\times b)\times{\mathbf{\Pi}},\chi\tilde{u}}\rangle+\langle{(\nabla\times{\mathbf{\Pi}})\cdot\nabla b,\chi\tilde{b}}\rangle,\end{split}

and finally further decompose the remaining terms as
\mathrm{VI}=\mathrm{VI}_{1}+\mathrm{VI}_{2}+\mathrm{VI}_{3}
where

\begin{split}\mathrm{VI}_{1}&=\langle{u,\chi f\partial_{\theta}\tilde{b}}\rangle+\langle{{\mathbf{\Pi}}\cdot\nabla u,\chi\tilde{b}}\rangle,\\
\mathrm{VI}_{2}&=-\langle{{\mathbf{\Pi}}\cdot\nabla\nabla\times b,\chi\tilde{b}}\rangle+\langle{b,-\chi f\partial_{\theta}\nabla\times\tilde{b}}\rangle,\\
\mathrm{VI}_{3}&=\langle{\nabla\times b\cdot\nabla{\mathbf{\Pi}},\chi\tilde{b}}\rangle-\langle{b,\chi r^{-1}f\partial_{\theta}\tilde{b}\partial_{\theta}}\rangle.\end{split}

We begin with rewriting
\mathrm{I}
as

\begin{split}\mathrm{I}&=\langle{{\bf V}\cdot\nabla u-\nu\Delta u-(\nabla\times b)\times{\mathbf{\Pi}}+u\cdot\nabla{\bf V}-(\nabla\times{\mathbf{\Pi}})\times b,\mathrm{div}^{-1}(\chi^{\prime}\tilde{u}^{z})}\rangle\\
&\quad+\langle{u,\mathrm{div}^{-1}(\chi^{\prime}f\partial_{\theta}\tilde{b})}\rangle+\langle{u,\mathrm{div}^{-1}(\chi^{\prime}\boldsymbol{\delta}_{\tilde{u}})}\rangle.\end{split}

Using

\begin{split}&\|{\nabla\mathrm{div}^{-1}(\chi^{\prime}\tilde{u}^{z})}\|_{L^{2}_{x,y}}\lesssim\|{\chi^{\prime}\tilde{u}^{z}}\|_{L^{2}_{x,y}}\lesssim\lambda^{-1}\ell^{\frac{1}{2}}\|{g_{0}}\|_{L^{2}_{x,y}},\quad\|{\mathrm{div}^{-1}(\chi^{\prime}\tilde{u}^{z})}\|_{L^{2}_{x,y}}\lesssim\ell^{-1}\|{\chi^{\prime}\tilde{u}}\|_{L^{2}_{x,y}}\lesssim\lambda^{-1}\ell^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|{g_{0}}\|_{L^{2}_{x,y}},\end{split}

we bound

\begin{split}&\left|\langle{{\bf V}\cdot\nabla u-\nu\Delta u-(\nabla\times b)\times{\mathbf{\Pi}}+u\cdot\nabla{\bf V}-(\nabla\times{\mathbf{\Pi}})\times b,\mathrm{div}^{-1}(\chi^{\prime}\tilde{u}^{z})}\rangle\right|\\
&\quad\lesssim\lambda^{-1}\ell^{\frac{1}{2}}\left((\|{\nabla{\bf V}}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\ell^{-1}\|{{\bf V}}\|_{L^{\infty}})\|{u}\|_{L^{2}}+(\|{\nabla{\mathbf{\Pi}}}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\ell^{-1}\|{{\mathbf{\Pi}}}\|_{L^{\infty}})\|{b}\|_{L^{2}}+\nu\|{\nabla u}\|_{L^{2}}\right)\|{g_{0}}\|_{L^{2}_{x,y}}\end{split}

and

\begin{split}&\left|\langle{u,\mathrm{div}^{-1}(\chi^{\prime}f\partial_{\theta}\tilde{b})}\rangle+\langle{u,\mathrm{div}^{-1}(\chi^{\prime}\boldsymbol{\delta}_{\tilde{u}})}\rangle\right|\lesssim\ell^{\frac{1}{2}}(1+\ell^{-1})\|{u}\|_{L^{2}}\|{g_{0}}\|_{L^{2}_{x,y}}.\end{split}

Terms in
\mathrm{II}
and
\mathrm{III}
can be handled in a straightforward manner: we have

\begin{split}&|\mathrm{II}|\lesssim\ell^{-\frac{3}{2}}(\|{u}\|_{L^{2}}+\|{b}\|_{L^{2}})\|{g_{0}}\|_{L^{2}_{x,y}}\end{split}

and

\begin{split}&|\mathrm{III}|\lesssim\ell^{\frac{1}{2}}\left((\|{\nabla^{2}{\mathbf{\Pi}}}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|{\nabla{\mathbf{\Pi}}}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|{\nabla{\bf V}}\|_{L^{\infty}})\|{b}\|_{L^{2}}+(\|{\nabla{\mathbf{\Pi}}}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|{\nabla{\bf V}}\|_{L^{\infty}})\|{u}\|_{L^{2}}\right)\|{g_{0}}\|_{L^{2}_{x,y}},\end{split}

respectively. We now treat the terms in
\mathrm{IV}
. Noting that

\begin{split}&\langle{{\bf V}\cdot\nabla b,\chi\tilde{b}}\rangle=-\langle{b,\chi V^{r}\partial_{r}\tilde{b}+V^{z}\chi^{\prime}\tilde{b}}\rangle,\quad\langle{{\bf V}\cdot\nabla u,\chi\tilde{u}}\rangle=-\langle{u,\chi V^{r}\partial_{r}\tilde{u}+V^{z}\chi^{\prime}\tilde{u}}\rangle\end{split}

and using (
3.4
) to bound
\langle{b,\chi V^{r}\partial_{r}\tilde{b}}\rangle
, we obtain

\begin{split}&|\mathrm{IV}|\lesssim\ell^{\frac{1}{2}}((1+\ell^{-1})+\lambda e^{c\lambda t})\|{{\bf V}}\|_{L^{\infty}}(\|{u}\|_{L^{2}}+\|{b}\|_{L^{2}})\|{g_{0}}\|_{L^{2}_{x,y}}.\end{split}

Next, recalling that

\begin{split}&\nabla\times(\Pi\partial_{\theta})=(-r\partial_{z}\Pi)\partial_{r}+(r^{-1}\partial_{r}(r^{2}\Pi))\partial_{z},\end{split}

we write

\begin{split}&\langle{(\nabla\times{\mathbf{\Pi}})\cdot\nabla b,\chi\tilde{b}}\rangle=\langle{-r\partial_{z}\Pi\partial_{r}b,\chi\tilde{b}}\rangle+\langle{r^{-1}\partial_{r}(r^{2}\Pi)\partial_{z}b,\chi\tilde{b}}\rangle\end{split}

and we may bound

\begin{split}&\left|\langle{-r\partial_{z}\Pi\partial_{r}b,\chi\tilde{b}}\rangle\right|\lesssim\left|\langle{b,\chi\partial_{r}(r\partial_{z}\Pi\tilde{b})}\rangle\right|\lesssim\ell^{\frac{1}{2}}(\ell^{-1}\|{{\mathbf{\Pi}}}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\lambda e^{c_{1}\lambda t}\|{\partial_{z}{\mathbf{\Pi}}}\|_{L^{\infty}})\|{b}\|_{L^{2}}\|{g_{0}}\|_{L^{2}_{x,y}}.\end{split}

The remaining terms from
\mathrm{V}
are easy to bound; we end up with

\begin{split}&\left|\mathrm{V}\right|\lesssim\ell^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\nu\|{\nabla u}\|_{L^{2}}+(\lambda e^{c_{1}\lambda t}\|{\partial_{z}{\mathbf{\Pi}}}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\ell^{-1}\|{{\mathbf{\Pi}}}\|_{L^{\infty}})\|{b}\|_{L^{2}}\right)\|{g_{0}}\|_{L^{2}_{x,y}}.\end{split}

Finally, we write

\begin{split}&\mathrm{VI}=\mathrm{VI}^{e}+\langle{({\mathbf{\Pi}}-{\mathbf{\Pi}}^{e})\cdot\nabla u,\chi\tilde{b}}\rangle-\langle{({\mathbf{\Pi}}-{\mathbf{\Pi}}^{e})\cdot\nabla\nabla\times b,\chi\tilde{b}}\rangle+\langle{\nabla\times b\cdot\nabla({\mathbf{\Pi}}-{\mathbf{\Pi}}^{e}),\chi\tilde{b}}\rangle\end{split}

where
{\mathbf{\Pi}}^{e}
is as in Lemma
2.9
and

\begin{split}\mathrm{VI}^{e}&:=\langle{u,\chi f\partial_{\theta}\tilde{b}}\rangle+\langle{{\mathbf{\Pi}}^{e}\cdot\nabla u,\chi\tilde{b}}\rangle-\langle{{\mathbf{\Pi}}^{e}\cdot\nabla\nabla\times b,\chi\tilde{b}}\rangle-\langle{b,\chi f\partial_{\theta}\nabla\times\tilde{b}}\rangle+\langle{\nabla\times b\cdot\nabla{\mathbf{\Pi}}^{e},\chi\tilde{b}}\rangle-\langle{b,\chi r^{-1}f\partial_{\theta}\tilde{b}\partial_{\theta}}\rangle.\end{split}

From the computations in the case of E-MHD in
3.1.2
,

\begin{split}&\left|\mathrm{VI}^{e}\right|\lesssim\ell^{-\frac{3}{2}}\|{b}\|_{L^{2}}\|{g_{0}}\|_{L^{2}_{x,y}}.\end{split}

For the other terms, we have

\begin{split}&\left|\langle{({\mathbf{\Pi}}-{\mathbf{\Pi}}^{e})\cdot\nabla u,\chi\tilde{b}}\rangle\right|=\left|\langle{r^{-1}({\mathbf{\Pi}}-{\mathbf{\Pi}}^{e})u,\chi\partial_{\theta}\tilde{b}}\rangle\right|\lesssim\lambda\ell^{\frac{1}{2}}\|{\nabla({\mathbf{\Pi}}-{\mathbf{\Pi}}^{e})}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|{u}\|_{L^{2}}\|{g_{0}}\|_{L^{2}_{x,y}},\end{split}

\begin{split}&\left|\langle{({\mathbf{\Pi}}-{\mathbf{\Pi}}^{e})\cdot\nabla\nabla\times b,\chi\tilde{b}}\rangle\right|=\left|\langle{b,\nabla\times(r^{-1}(\Pi-\Pi^{e})\chi\partial_{\theta}\tilde{b})}\rangle\right|\\
&\qquad\lesssim\ell^{\frac{1}{2}}(\lambda(1+\lambda)e^{c_{1}\lambda t}+\ell^{-1})\|{\nabla^{2}({\mathbf{\Pi}}-{\mathbf{\Pi}}^{e})}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|{b}\|_{L^{2}}\|{g_{0}}\|_{L^{2}_{x,y}},\end{split}

and

\begin{split}&\left|\langle{\nabla\times b\cdot\nabla({\mathbf{\Pi}}-{\mathbf{\Pi}}^{e}),\chi\tilde{b}}\rangle\right|=\left|\langle{b,\nabla\times((\chi\tilde{b})\cdot\nabla({\mathbf{\Pi}}-{\mathbf{\Pi}}^{e}))}\rangle\right|\\
&\qquad\lesssim\ell^{\frac{1}{2}}((\lambda e^{c\lambda t}+\ell^{-1})\|{\nabla({\mathbf{\Pi}}-{\mathbf{\Pi}}^{e})}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|{\nabla^{2}({\mathbf{\Pi}}-{\mathbf{\Pi}}^{e})}\|_{L^{\infty}})\|{b}\|_{L^{2}}\|{g_{0}}\|_{L^{2}_{x,y}}.\end{split}

Collecting the terms, and recalling our assumptions
\lambda\geq 1
,
\ell\leq 1
,

\begin{split}&\left|\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\left(\langle{u,\chi\tilde{u}-\mathrm{div}^{-1}(\chi^{\prime}\tilde{u}^{z})}\rangle+\langle{b,\chi\tilde{b}}\rangle\right)\right|\lesssim\ell^{-\frac{3}{2}}\left[1+\nu\|{\nabla u}\|_{L^{2}}+(\|{{\mathbf{\Pi}}}\|_{W^{2,\infty}}+\|{{\mathbf{\Pi}}-{\mathbf{\Pi}}^{e}}\|_{W^{2,\infty}}+\|{{\bf V}}\|_{W^{1,\infty}})\right.\\
&\qquad\left.+e^{c_{1}\lambda t}(\lambda\|{\partial_{z}\Pi}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\lambda^{2}\|{{\mathbf{\Pi}}-{\mathbf{\Pi}}^{e}}\|_{W^{1,\infty}}+\lambda\|{{\bf V}}\|_{L^{\infty}})(\|{u}\|_{L^{2}}+\|{b}\|_{L^{2}})\right]\|{g_{0}}\|_{L^{2}_{x,y}}.\end{split}

(Here we note that the norms on
{\mathbf{\Pi}},{\bf V}
, etc are taken only on the support of
\chi
.)

We now look for a time interval where the previous estimate is effective. To begin with, we use (
1.14
) to obtain

\begin{split}&\|{u(t)}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|{b(t)}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\nu\int_{0}^{t}\|{\nabla u(s)}\|_{L^{2}}\mathrm{d}s\leq 2\|{b_{0}}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.\end{split}

Furthermore, we shall restrict
t\in[0,T]
, where
T=c\ell^{m_{0}}
for some small absolute constant
c>0
and large
m_{0}\geq 1
(
m_{0}=10
suffices) so that

\begin{split}&\|{{\mathbf{\Pi}}}\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T];W^{2,\infty})}+\|{{\mathbf{\Pi}}-{\mathbf{\Pi}}^{e}}\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T];W^{2,\infty})}+\|{{\bf V}}\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T];W^{1,\infty})}\lesssim\ell^{-m_{0}}\end{split}

holds, using the estimate (
2.14
) from Proposition
2.6
. In what follows, we shall take
T
even smaller whenever it becomes necessary. Next, from Lemma
2.9
, we obtain smallness estimates

\begin{split}&\|{{\bf V}}\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim t\ell^{-m_{0}},\qquad\|{\partial_{z}\Pi}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathrm{supp}(\chi))}=\|{\partial_{z}(\Pi-\Pi^{e})}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathrm{supp}(\chi))}\lesssim\|{{\mathbf{\Pi}}-{\mathbf{\Pi}}^{e}}\|_{W^{1,\infty}}\lesssim t^{2}\ell^{-m_{0}}\end{split}

Furthermore, recalling the simple bound

\begin{split}&\left|\langle{u,\chi\tilde{u}-\mathrm{div}^{-1}(\chi^{\prime}\tilde{u}^{z})}\rangle\right|\lesssim\lambda^{-1}\ell^{\frac{1}{2}}\|{u}\|_{L^{2}}\|{g_{0}}\|_{L^{2}_{x,y}},\end{split}

we obtain that

\begin{split}&\left|\langle{b(t),\chi\tilde{b}(t)}\rangle-\langle{b_{0},\chi\tilde{b}_{0}}\rangle\right|\lesssim\ell^{-m_{0}+\frac{1}{2}}\left(\nu t^{\frac{1}{2}}+t+\int_{0}^{t}\left(1+\lambda s+(\lambda s)^{2}\right)e^{c_{1}\lambda s}\mathrm{d}s\right)\|{b_{0}}\|_{L^{2}}\|{g_{0}}\|_{L^{2}_{x,y}}.\end{split}

We estimate the time integral as

\begin{split}&\int_{0}^{t}\left(1+\lambda s+(\lambda s)^{2}\right)e^{c_{1}\lambda s}\mathrm{d}s\lesssim\int_{0}^{t}e^{2c_{1}\lambda s}\mathrm{d}s\lesssim\lambda^{-1}\ell(e^{2c_{1}\lambda t}-1)\end{split}

so that by taking
T
smaller than
c\lambda^{-1}\ln\lambda
for some small
c>0
and using

\begin{split}&\langle{b_{0},\chi\tilde{b}_{0}}\rangle=\ell^{\frac{1}{2}}\|{b_{0}}\|_{L^{2}}\|{g_{0}}\|_{L^{2}_{x,y}}(1+O(\lambda^{-1})),\end{split}

we deduce that, for
t\in[0,T]
,

\begin{split}&\langle{b,\chi\tilde{b}}\rangle(t)\geq\frac{1}{2}\ell^{\frac{1}{2}}\|{b_{0}}\|_{L^{2}}\|{g_{0}}\|_{L^{2}_{x,y}}.\end{split}

(Here it is implicit that
\lambda
is taken sufficiently large with respect to
\nu
and
\ell^{-1}
.) The rest of the argument is completely parallel to
3.1.3
. ∎

4.
Unboundedness of the solution map

In this section, we give the proof of Theorem
B*
. We first handle the simpler case of (
E-MHD
) in Section
4.1
and then the (
Hall-MHD
) case is treated in Section
4.2
.

4.1.
Case of (
E-MHD
)

As before, we take the background magnetic field to be
{\mathbf{\Pi}}(t)=\Pi(t,r,z)\partial_{\theta}
with initial data
f(r)\chi(z/10)
, where
f,\chi
are the same as in
1.3.1
. Towards a contradiction, we shall assume that the solution map is well-defined and bounded: to be precise, we are given some
\epsilon,\delta,s>0
,
s_{0}>7/2
, and
M>0
such that the solution map is defined as an operator

\begin{split}&\mathcal{B}_{\epsilon}({\mathbf{\Pi}}_{0};H^{s}_{comp})\rightarrow L^{\infty}_{t}([0,\delta];H^{s_{0}})\end{split}

with the
L^{\infty}_{t}([0,\delta];H^{s_{0}})
norm of the solution bounded by
M
.

We shall take
\lambda\gg\ell^{-1}
and derive a contradiction in the end by taking
\lambda\to\infty
. For a given
\lambda
, we take the same profile
b=b_{(\lambda)}
as in the proof of Theorem
A*
; note that we have at the initial time (using its explicit form in
3.1.1
)

\begin{split}&\|{b_{0}}\|_{H^{s}}\lesssim_{s}(\lambda+\ell^{-1})^{s}\|{b_{0}}\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\lambda^{s}\|{b_{0}}\|_{L^{2}}.\end{split}

Therefore, given
\epsilon>0
, we shall simply replace
b
by
c_{s}\epsilon\lambda^{-s}b
(
c_{s}>0
is a constant depending only on
s
) so that at the initial time we have
\|{b_{0}}\|_{H^{s}}<\epsilon
.

In particular,
{\mathbf{\Pi}}_{0}+b_{0}\in\mathcal{B}_{\epsilon}({\mathbf{\Pi}}_{0};H^{s}_{comp})
and there exists a solution
{\bf B}
belonging to
L^{\infty}_{t}([0,\delta];H^{s_{0}})
to (
E-MHD
) with initial data
{\mathbf{\Pi}}_{0}+b_{0}
. We then set
b:={\bf B}-{\mathbf{\Pi}}
, so that
b(t=0)=b_{0}
. We have that
b
solves

(4.1)

\begin{split}\partial_{t}b+({\mathbf{\Pi}}\cdot\nabla)(\nabla\times b)-((\nabla\times b)\cdot\nabla){\mathbf{\Pi}}+(b\cdot\nabla)(\nabla\times{\mathbf{\Pi}})-((\nabla\times{\mathbf{\Pi}})\cdot\nabla)b=\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{b},\end{split}

\begin{split}\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{b}:=-(b\cdot\nabla)\nabla\times b+(\nabla\times b)\cdot\nabla b.\end{split}

Next, we recall the
z
-independent approximate solution
\tilde{b}
:

\begin{split}\partial_{t}\tilde{b}+(\tilde{{\bf B}}\cdot\nabla)(\nabla\times\tilde{b})-((\nabla\times\tilde{b})\cdot\nabla)\tilde{{\bf B}}=\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{\tilde{b}},\end{split}

\begin{split}&\sup_{t\in[0,\delta]}\|{\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{\tilde{b}}}\|_{L^{2}_{x,y}}\lesssim\ell^{-2}\|{g_{0}}\|_{L^{2}_{x,y}},\end{split}

with
\tilde{{\bf B}}=f(r)\partial_{\theta}
. As before, the goal is to estimate the time derivative of
\langle{b,\chi\tilde{b}}\rangle
and the only additional term relative to the linear case from
3.1.2
is simply the contribution from the nonlinear error, which can be bounded by

\begin{split}&\left|\langle{\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{b},\chi\tilde{b}}\rangle\right|\lesssim\ell^{\frac{1}{2}}\|{\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{b}}\|_{L^{2}}\|{g_{0}}\|_{L^{2}_{x,y}}.\end{split}

We now observe that

\begin{split}&\|{b}\|_{H^{s_{0}}}\leq\|{{\bf B}}\|_{H^{s_{0}}}+\|{{\mathbf{\Pi}}}\|_{H^{s_{0}}}\lesssim_{s_{0}}M+\ell^{\frac{1}{2}-s_{0}}\end{split}

where we have used the assumption
M\sup_{t\in[0,\delta]}\|{{\bf B}}\|_{H^{s_{0}}}\leq M
and the a priori estimates from
2.2
, by shrinking
\delta>0
if necessary (in a way depending only on
\ell
). We then estimate

\begin{split}&\|{(b\cdot\nabla)\nabla\times b}\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\|{\nabla^{2}b}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|{b}\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim_{s_{0}}(M+\ell^{\frac{1}{2}-s_{0}})\|{b}\|_{L^{2}}\end{split}

and

\begin{split}&\|{(\nabla\times b)\cdot\nabla b}\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\|{\nabla b}\|_{L^{4}}^{2}\lesssim_{s_{0}}(M+\ell^{\frac{1}{2}-s_{0}})\|{b}\|_{L^{2}},\end{split}

where we have used simple estimates

\begin{split}&\|{\nabla^{2}b}\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim\|{b}\|_{H^{s_{0}}},\quad\|{\nabla b}\|_{L^{4}}\lesssim\|{b}\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|{b}\|_{H^{s_{0}}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\end{split}

which hold for
s_{0}>7/2
. This shows that

\begin{split}&\|{\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{b}}\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim(M+\ell^{\frac{1}{2}-s_{0}})\|{b}\|_{L^{2}}.\end{split}

Now recalling the estimate (
3.2
), we have

\begin{split}&\left|\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\langle{b,\chi\tilde{b}}\rangle\right|\lesssim\ell^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(M\ell^{-\frac{1}{2}}+\ell^{-s_{0}}+\ell^{-2}\right)\|{b}\|_{L^{2}}\|{g_{0}}\|_{L^{2}_{x,y}}.\end{split}

Since
\langle{\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{b},b}\rangle=0
, we may shrink
\delta>0
if necessary to guarantee that
\|{b}\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim 2\|{b_{0}}\|_{L^{2}}
on the time interval
[0,\delta]
. Then, proceeding in the same way as in
3.1.3
, we conclude that

\begin{split}&M+\ell^{\frac{1}{2}-s_{0}}\gtrsim_{s_{0}}\|{b(t)}\|_{H^{s_{0}}}\gtrsim_{s_{0}}e^{c_{0}s_{0}\lambda t}\|{b_{0}}\|_{H^{s_{0}}}\gtrsim_{s_{0},s}\epsilon e^{c_{0}s_{0}\lambda t}\lambda^{s_{0}-s}\end{split}

by further shrinking
\delta>0
if necessary, but in a way depending only on
f
and
M
. We obtain a contradiction since we may fix some
0<t^{*}<\delta
and take
\lambda\rightarrow+\infty
. The proof is complete. ∎

4.2.
Case of (
Hall-MHD
)

We take initial data
({\bf u}_{0},{\bf B}_{0})=(0,{\mathbf{\Pi}}_{0}+b_{0})
where
{\mathbf{\Pi}}_{0},b_{0}
are the same as in the case of (
E-MHD
). In particular,
b_{0}
is normalized in a way that
\|{b_{0}}\|_{H^{s}}<\epsilon
.

Assume that there exist some
\delta>0,s_{0}>7/2
, and a solution
({\bf u},{\bf B})
belonging to
L^{\infty}_{t}([0,\delta];H^{s_{0}-1}\times H^{s_{0}})
such that
{\bf u}(t=0)={\bf u}_{0}
and
{\bf B}(t=0)={\mathbf{\Pi}}_{0}+b_{0}
. We set
u(t)={\bf u}(t)-{\bf V}(t)
and
b(t)={\bf B}(t)-{\mathbf{\Pi}}(t)
. Due to the a priori estimates in Proposition
2.6
, we have
\|{\nabla{\bf V}}\|_{H^{s_{0}}}+\|{{\mathbf{\Pi}}}\|_{H^{s_{0}}}\leq 2\|{{\mathbf{\Pi}}_{0}}\|_{H^{s_{0}}}
on
[0,\delta]
by taking smaller
\delta=\delta(\ell)>0
if necessary; in particular, the pair
(u,b)
is well-defined on
[0,\delta]
and belongs to
L^{\infty}_{t}([0,\delta];H^{s_{0}-1}\times H^{s_{0}})
. As before, setting
M:=\sup_{t\in[0,\delta]}\left(\|{{\bf B}}\|_{H^{s_{0}}}+\|{{\bf u}}\|_{H^{s_{0}-1}}\right)
gives that

\begin{split}\|{u}\|_{H^{s_{0}-1}}+\|{b}\|_{H^{s_{0}}}\lesssim_{s_{0}}M+\ell^{\frac{1}{2}-s_{0}}.\end{split}

We then have that
(u,b)
satisfies

(4.2)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}u+{\bf V}\cdot\nabla u+\nabla{\bf p}-\nu\Delta u-(\nabla\times b)\times{\mathbf{\Pi}}=-u\cdot\nabla{\bf V}+(\nabla\times{\mathbf{\Pi}})\times b+\boldsymbol{\delta}_{u}\\
&\partial_{t}b+{\mathbf{\Pi}}\cdot\nabla(\nabla\times b)-(\nabla\times b)\cdot\nabla{\mathbf{\Pi}}-(\nabla\times{\mathbf{\Pi}})\cdot\nabla b+{\bf V}\cdot\nabla b-{\mathbf{\Pi}}\cdot\nabla u\\
&\quad=-b\cdot\nabla(\nabla\times{\mathbf{\Pi}})+b\cdot\nabla{\bf V}-u\cdot\nabla{\mathbf{\Pi}}+\boldsymbol{\delta}_{b},\\
\end{aligned}\right.

where

(4.3)

\begin{split}{\bf p}:=\sum_{i,j}R_{i}R_{j}({\bf u}^{i}{\bf u}^{j})-\sum_{i,j}R_{i}R_{j}({\bf B}^{i}{\bf B}^{j})-\frac{|{\bf B}|^{2}}{2}\end{split}

and

(4.4)

\left\{\begin{aligned} \boldsymbol{\delta}_{u}&:=-u\cdot\nabla u+b\cdot\nabla b,\\
\boldsymbol{\delta}_{b}&:=-(b\cdot\nabla)\nabla\times b+(\nabla\times b)\cdot\nabla b+b\cdot\nabla u-u\cdot\nabla b.\end{aligned}\right.

Now with

\begin{split}&\left|\langle{\boldsymbol{\delta}_{b},\chi\tilde{b}}\rangle\right|+\left|\langle{\boldsymbol{\delta}_{u},\chi\tilde{u}}\rangle\right|\lesssim\ell^{\frac{1}{2}}(M\ell^{-\frac{1}{2}}+\ell^{-s_{0}})(\|{u}\|_{L^{2}}+\|{b}\|_{L^{2}})\|{g_{0}}\|_{L^{2}_{x,y}},\end{split}

and recalling the estimates from Proposition
2.6
and Lemma
2.9
, we obtain the bound

\begin{split}&\left|\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}(\langle{u,\chi\tilde{u}-\mathrm{div}^{-1}(\chi^{\prime}\tilde{u}^{z})}\rangle+\langle{b,\chi\tilde{b}}\rangle)\right|\lesssim\ell^{\frac{1}{2}}(M\ell^{-\frac{1}{2}}+\ell^{-s_{0}}+\ell^{-m_{0}}(1+\lambda t)^{2}e^{c_{1}\lambda t})(\|{u}\|_{L^{2}}+\|{b}\|_{L^{2}})\|{g_{0}}\|_{L^{2}_{x,y}},\end{split}

for some large
m_{0}
, which can be taken to be
10
. Then, proceeding as in Section
3.2
, on the time interval
[0,T]
with
T=c\lambda^{-1}\ln\lambda
,

\begin{split}&\langle{b,\chi\tilde{b}}\rangle\geq(1-c)\ell^{\frac{1}{2}}\|{b_{0}}\|_{L^{2}}\|{g_{0}}\|_{L^{2}_{x,y}}\end{split}

by taking
\lambda\gg 1
depending on
\ell,M,s_{0},m_{0},
and also on
\nu
when
\nu>0
. Then, arguing as in
3.1.3
, we have that
\|{b(t)}\|_{H^{s_{0}}}\gtrsim_{s_{0}}\lambda^{s_{0}}e^{c_{0}s_{0}\lambda t}\|{b_{0}}\|_{L^{2}}\gtrsim_{s_{0},s}\epsilon\lambda^{s_{0}-s}e^{c_{0}s_{0}\lambda t}
so that at
t=T
,

\begin{split}&M+\ell^{\frac{1}{2}-s_{0}}\|{b(T)}\|_{H^{s_{0}}}\gtrsim_{s_{0},s}\epsilon\lambda^{(1+\alpha_{0})s_{0}-s}\end{split}

for an absolute constant
\alpha_{0}>0
. From the assumption
(1+\alpha_{0})s_{0}>s
, we get a contradiction by taking
\lambda\rightarrow+\infty
. ∎

5.
Nonexistence

5.1.
Case of (
E-MHD
)

We now prove Theorem
C*
in the (
E-MHD
) case. We may assume that
(\mathbb{T},\mathbb{R})_{z}=\mathbb{R}_{z}
since the case of
\mathbb{T}_{z}
was covered in
[
19
]
. Let
s>7/2,\delta>0
and
\epsilon>0
be given by the statement, and for simplicity fix an axisymmetric background of the form

\begin{split}&\tilde{{\mathbf{\Pi}}}_{0}=f_{0}(r)\chi_{0}(z/10)\partial_{\theta},\end{split}

where
f_{0}\in C^{\infty}_{comp}
satisfies the assumptions in Section
2.4
with
\ell=1/40
and
r_{0}=1/20=2\ell
; more specifically, we may choose
f_{0}
to satisfy
f_{0}(r)=r-1/20
for
1/40\leq r\leq 3/40
. Similarly, we take
0\leq\chi_{0}\leq 1
to be a smooth bump function supported in
[-1/100,1/100]
and
\chi_{0}=1
in
[-1/200,1/200]
. In particular,
\tilde{{\mathbf{\Pi}}}_{0}\in C^{\infty}_{comp}
. We then take

\begin{split}&{\mathbf{\Pi}}_{0}=\sum_{k=k_{0}}^{\infty}\tilde{{\mathbf{\Pi}}}_{0}^{(k)}:=\sum_{k=k_{0}}^{\infty}2^{-sk}\tilde{{\mathbf{\Pi}}}_{0}(2^{k}x,2^{k}y,2^{k}(z-z_{k})),\quad z_{k}:=1-2^{-k}\end{split}

so that

\begin{split}&\|{\tilde{{\mathbf{\Pi}}}_{0}^{(k)}}\|_{H^{s}}\lesssim 2^{-\frac{k}{2}}\end{split}

and in particular, by taking
k_{0}\gg 1
we may ensure that
\|{{\mathbf{\Pi}}_{0}}\|_{H^{s}}<\frac{\epsilon}{2}
. Here we remark in advance that
k_{0}
will be taken to be sufficiently large depending on given parameters
\epsilon,\delta,s
and also on a large parameter
N=N(s)\gg 1
to be introduced below. Note that (by redefining
\chi
if necessary)
{\mathbf{\Pi}}_{0}
is compactly supported and the supports of
\tilde{{\mathbf{\Pi}}}_{0}^{(k)}
are disjoint from each other; we shall also write

\begin{split}&\tilde{{\mathbf{\Pi}}}^{(k)}_{0}=f_{k}(r)\tilde{\chi}_{k}(z)\partial_{\theta},\end{split}

where

\begin{split}&f_{k}(r):=2^{-sk}f_{0}(2^{k}r),\quad\tilde{\chi}_{k}(z):=\chi_{0}(\frac{2^{k}}{10}(z-z_{k})).\end{split}

Note that
\ell_{f_{k}}\sim 2^{-k}
. Similarly, we set

\begin{split}&\chi_{k}(z):=\chi_{0}(2^{k}(z-z_{k}))\end{split}

to be a cutoff near
z=z_{k}
. Note that the lifespan of
\tilde{{\mathbf{\Pi}}}^{(k)}_{0}
under (
1.7
) increases as
k\rightarrow+\infty
and there is some non-empty interval of time during which the supports of the solutions
\{\tilde{{\mathbf{\Pi}}}^{(k)}\}_{k\geq 0}
do not overlap with each other. This guarantees that the unique local-in-time solution
{\mathbf{\Pi}}(t)
of (
1.7
) with initial data
{\mathbf{\Pi}}_{0}
(with some abuse of notation) is given by the superposition

\begin{split}&{\mathbf{\Pi}}(t)=\sum_{k=k_{0}}^{\infty}\tilde{{\mathbf{\Pi}}}^{(k)}(t):=\sum_{k=k_{0}}^{\infty}2^{-sk}\tilde{{\mathbf{\Pi}}}(2^{-(s-1)k}t,2^{k}x,2^{k}y,2^{k}(z-z_{k}))\end{split}

where
\tilde{{\mathbf{\Pi}}}(t)
is the solution of (
1.7
) with initial data
\tilde{{\mathbf{\Pi}}}_{0}
, which is smooth for an
O(1)
interval of time. Moreover, by taking
k_{0}\gg 1
, it is guaranteed that
{\mathbf{\Pi}}(t)
remains smooth in
[0,\delta]
for any
\delta>0
and
\|{\tilde{{\mathbf{\Pi}}}^{(k)}}\|_{L^{\infty}([0,\delta];H^{s})}\leq 2\|{\tilde{{\mathbf{\Pi}}}^{(k)}_{0}}\|_{H^{s}}
; in particular,
\|{{\mathbf{\Pi}}(t)}\|_{L^{\infty}([0,\delta];H^{s})}<\epsilon
. We can also make sure that
\chi_{k}\partial_{z}{\mathbf{\Pi}}(t)\equiv 0
for any
k\geq k_{0}
on
[0,\delta]
.

We now rescale the
z
-independent degenerating wave packet solutions
\{\tilde{b}_{(\lambda)}\}
associated with
f_{0}(r)\partial_{\theta}
as follows:

(5.1)

\begin{split}&\tilde{b}_{k}(t):=2^{\frac{k}{2}}\tilde{b}_{(2^{-k}\lambda_{k})}(2^{-sk}2^{2k}t,2^{k}x,2^{k}y),\quad\lambda_{k}:=2^{Nk}\end{split}

where
N=N(s)\gg 1
is to be specified later. Consider the initial data

(5.2)

\begin{split}{\bf B}_{0}={\mathbf{\Pi}}_{0}+\sum_{k=k_{0}}^{\infty}2^{-k}(2^{-k}\lambda_{k})^{-s}\left(\chi_{k}\tilde{b}_{k}(0)-\mathrm{div}_{U_{k}}^{-1}(\chi_{k}^{\prime}\tilde{b}^{z}_{k}(0))\right);\end{split}

here
U_{k}
is a cube of side-length
O(2^{-k})
containing the support of
\chi_{k}\tilde{b}_{k}(t)
for
t\in[0,\delta]
. For convenience we shall set
b_{k}=\chi_{k}\tilde{b}_{k}-\mathrm{div}_{U_{k}}^{-1}(\chi_{k}^{\prime}\tilde{b}^{z}_{k})
. It can be arranged that the cubes
U_{k}
do not overlap with each other. Note that we have

\begin{split}(2^{-k}\lambda_{k})^{-s}\|{\chi_{k}\tilde{b}_{k}(0)-\mathrm{div}_{U_{k}}^{-1}(\chi_{k}^{\prime}\tilde{b}^{z}_{k}(0))}\|_{H^{s}}\lesssim&\|{(2^{-k}\lambda_{k})^{-s}\chi_{k}\tilde{b}_{k}(0)}\|_{H^{s}}\lesssim 1\end{split}

and thanks to the extra factor of
2^{-k}
,
{\bf B}_{0}\in H^{s}
with
\|{{\bf B}_{0}}\|_{H^{s}}<\epsilon
by taking
k_{0}\gg 1
. From the normalizations in (
5.1
), we have that

(5.3)

\begin{split}\|{\tilde{b}_{k}(t)}\|_{L^{2}_{x,y}}+\|{\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{\tilde{b}}(t)}\|_{L^{2}_{x,y}}\lesssim 2^{C_{s}k}\end{split}

on
[0,\delta]
with some
C_{s}>0
depending only on
s
. The degeneration estimate takes the form

\begin{split}&\|{\tilde{b}_{k}(t)}\|_{L^{2}_{\theta}L^{1}_{rdr}}\lesssim 2^{C_{s}k}\exp(-2^{-c_{s}k}\lambda_{k}t).\end{split}

Now that we have fixed our choice of initial data, towards a contradiction, assume that there is a solution
{\bf B}\in L^{\infty}_{t}([0,\delta];H^{s})
to (
E-MHD
) with
{\bf B}(t=0)={\bf B}_{0}
given by (
5.2
). Denoting
b(t):={\bf B}(t)-{\mathbf{\Pi}}(t)
, we have that
b(t)
satisfies

(5.4)

\begin{split}&\partial_{t}b+(b\cdot\nabla)(\nabla\times{\mathbf{\Pi}})-(\nabla\times{\mathbf{\Pi}})\cdot\nabla b+({\mathbf{\Pi}}\cdot\nabla)(\nabla\times b)-(\nabla\times b)\cdot\nabla{\mathbf{\Pi}}=\nabla\times((\nabla\times b)\times b).\end{split}

Before we proceed to the proof of (localized) generalized energy identity, we need to localize the energy identity for
b
itself. Unfortunately, the
L^{2}
-norm of
b
localized to the support of
\tilde{{\mathbf{\Pi}}}^{(k)}
does not satisfy an energy identity by itself, as there are contributions from neighboring pieces. As in
[
19
, Section 5.2]
, we use cutoff functions with fast decaying tails which can accommodate such interactions. To this end, we prepare a
C^{\infty}
positive function
\widetilde{\chi}:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{+}
with the following properties:

•

\widetilde{\chi}(z)=1
for
z\in[-1/8,1/8]
,

•

|\widetilde{\chi}^{\prime}(z)|\leq|\widetilde{\chi}(z)|
for all
z\in\mathbb{R}
, and

•

\widetilde{\chi}\leq 2^{-|z|}
for
z>1/2
.

Note that instead of requiring
\widetilde{\chi}
to have compact support, we contend with exponential decay; this is why we can pointwise bound the derivative in terms of the function itself. We then define rescaled cutoffs by

\begin{split}&\widetilde{\chi}_{k}(z)=\widetilde{\chi}(2^{k}(z-z_{k})).\end{split}

Note that

•

\tilde{{\mathbf{\Pi}}}^{(k)}\widetilde{\chi}_{k}=\tilde{{\mathbf{\Pi}}}^{(k)}
and
{\mathbf{\Pi}}\widetilde{\chi}_{k}^{\prime}=0
,

•

\widetilde{\chi}_{k}=1
on the support of
\chi_{k}
; in particular
\widetilde{\chi}_{k}\chi_{k}^{\prime}=\widetilde{\chi}_{k}^{\prime}\chi_{k}=0
.

To proceed, we multiply both sides of (
5.4
) by
\widetilde{\chi}_{k}
and test against
\widetilde{\chi}_{k}b
. The first four terms involving
{\mathbf{\Pi}}
are easy to handle: we have

\begin{split}&\left|\langle{\widetilde{\chi}_{k}(b\cdot\nabla)(\nabla\times{\mathbf{\Pi}}),\widetilde{\chi}_{k}b}\rangle\right|\lesssim\|{{\mathbf{\Pi}}}\|_{W^{2,\infty}}\|{\widetilde{\chi}_{k}b}\|_{L^{2}}^{2},\end{split}

\begin{split}&\left|\langle{\widetilde{\chi}_{k}(\nabla\times{\mathbf{\Pi}})\cdot\nabla b,\widetilde{\chi}_{k}b}\rangle\right|\lesssim\|{{\mathbf{\Pi}}}\|_{W^{1,\infty}}\|{\widetilde{\chi}_{k}^{\prime}b}\|_{L^{2}}\|{\widetilde{\chi}_{k}b}\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim 2^{k}\|{{\mathbf{\Pi}}}\|_{W^{1,\infty}}\|{\widetilde{\chi}_{k}b}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\end{split}

(using the pointwise bound
|\widetilde{\chi}_{k}^{\prime}(z)|\leq 2^{k}|\widetilde{\chi}_{k}(z)|
), and similarly

\begin{split}&\left|\langle{\widetilde{\chi}_{k}\left[({\mathbf{\Pi}}\cdot\nabla)(\nabla\times b)-(\nabla\times b)\cdot\nabla{\mathbf{\Pi}}\right],\widetilde{\chi}_{k}b}\rangle\right|\\
&\qquad\lesssim(2^{k}\|{{\mathbf{\Pi}}}\|_{W^{1,\infty}}+2^{2k}\|{{\mathbf{\Pi}}}\|_{L^{\infty}})\|{\widetilde{\chi}_{k}b}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+2^{k}\|{{\mathbf{\Pi}}}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|{\widetilde{\chi}_{k}b}\|_{L^{2}}\|{\nabla(\widetilde{\chi}_{k}b)}\|_{L^{2}}.\end{split}

We further bound the last term as follows:

\begin{split}&2^{k}\|{{\mathbf{\Pi}}}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|{\widetilde{\chi}_{k}b}\|_{L^{2}}\|{\nabla(\widetilde{\chi}_{k}b)}\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim 2^{k}\|{{\mathbf{\Pi}}}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|{\widetilde{\chi}_{k}b}\|_{L^{2}}^{2-\frac{1}{s}}\|{\widetilde{\chi}_{k}b}\|_{H^{s}}^{\frac{1}{s}}\lesssim 2^{2k}\|{{\mathbf{\Pi}}}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|{b}\|_{H^{s}}^{\frac{1}{s}}\|{\widetilde{\chi}_{k}b}\|_{L^{2}}^{2-\frac{1}{s}}.\end{split}

Next, similar estimates hold for the nonlinear term: we have

\begin{split}&\left|\langle{\widetilde{\chi}_{k}\nabla\times((\nabla\times b)\times b),\widetilde{\chi}_{k}b}\rangle\right|\lesssim\left|\langle{\widetilde{\chi}_{k}^{\prime}b\nabla b,\widetilde{\chi}_{k}b}\rangle\right|+\|{\nabla^{2}b}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|{\widetilde{\chi}_{k}b}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\end{split}

(since there is no
\partial_{zz}
derivative) and therefore

\begin{split}&\left|\langle{\widetilde{\chi}_{k}\nabla\times((\nabla\times b)\times b),\widetilde{\chi}_{k}b}\rangle\right|\lesssim\|{\nabla^{2}b}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|{\widetilde{\chi}_{k}b}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+2^{k}\|{\nabla b}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|{\widetilde{\chi}_{k}b}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.\end{split}

Collecting the estimates, we have

(5.5)

\begin{split}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\|{\widetilde{\chi}_{k}b}\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim 2^{2k}\left(\|{\widetilde{\chi}_{k}b}\|_{L^{2}}^{1-\frac{1}{s}}+\|{\widetilde{\chi}_{k}b}\|_{L^{2}}\right)\end{split}

where the implicit constant depends on
\|{b}\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}H^{s}}
and
\|{{\mathbf{\Pi}}}\|_{W^{2,\infty}}
. At the initial time, we estimate the local energy:

\begin{split}\|{\widetilde{\chi}_{k}b(t=0)}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=c{2^{-4k}}\lambda_{k}^{-2s}+\sum_{k_{0}\leq k^{\prime},k^{\prime}\neq k}{2^{-2k^{\prime}}}\lambda_{k^{\prime}}^{-2s}\|{\widetilde{\chi}_{k}b_{k^{\prime}}(t=0)}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\end{split}

where
c\sim 1
is from the choice of
\widetilde{\chi}
. Note that the contribution for
k^{\prime}>k
is negligible relative to
{2^{-4k}}\lambda_{k}^{-2s}
from the decay of
{2^{-2k^{\prime}}}\lambda_{k^{\prime}}^{-2s}
in
k^{\prime}
. On the other hand, for
k^{\prime}<k
, we use the decay of
\chi_{k}
: for any
k^{\prime}<k
, the support of
b_{k^{\prime}}
is separated from
z_{k}
by at least
c2^{-\frac{k}{2}}
with
c>0
independent of
k
. Hence,

\begin{split}|\widetilde{\chi}_{k}|\lesssim 2^{-c2^{\frac{k}{2}}}\lesssim_{N,s}2^{-4Nsk}\ll{2^{-4k}}\lambda_{k}^{-2s}\end{split}

on the support of
b_{k^{\prime}}
with any
k^{\prime}>k
, and we obtain that
\|{\chi_{k}b(t=0)}\|_{L^{2}}{\lesssim 2^{-2k}}\lambda_{k}^{-s}
by choosing
k_{0}
sufficiently large with respect to
N,s
. Using this together with (
5.5
) yields that

(5.6)

\begin{split}\|{\widetilde{\chi}_{k}b{(t)}}\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim_{s}\left({2^{-\frac{k}{s}}}\lambda_{k}^{-1}+2^{2k}t\right)^{s}\lesssim_{s}{2^{-2k}}\lambda_{k}^{-s}+2^{2ks}t^{s}.\end{split}

We are in a position to prove a localized version of the generalized energy estimate: recall that

\begin{split}&\partial_{t}\tilde{b}+(\tilde{{\mathbf{\Pi}}}\cdot\nabla)(\nabla\times\tilde{b})-((\nabla\times\tilde{b})\cdot\nabla)\tilde{{\mathbf{\Pi}}}=\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{\tilde{b}}.\end{split}

where

\begin{split}&\tilde{{\mathbf{\Pi}}}=\sum_{k=k_{0}}^{\infty}\tilde{{\mathbf{\Pi}}}_{k}=\sum_{k=k_{0}}^{\infty}f_{k}(r)\partial_{\theta},\quad\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{\tilde{b}}=\sum_{k=k_{0}}^{\infty}\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{\tilde{b},k}\end{split}

with
\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{\tilde{b},k}
being the error associated with
\tilde{b}_{k}
. We use the above and (
5.4
) to compute

\begin{split}&\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\langle{\widetilde{\chi}_{k}b,\chi_{k}\tilde{b}_{k}}\rangle=-\langle{\widetilde{\chi}_{k}\left[(b\cdot\nabla)(\nabla\times{\mathbf{\Pi}})-(\nabla\times{\mathbf{\Pi}})\cdot\nabla b+({\mathbf{\Pi}}\cdot\nabla)(\nabla\times b)-(\nabla\times b)\cdot\nabla{\mathbf{\Pi}}\right],\chi_{k}\tilde{b}_{k}}\rangle\\
&\qquad+\langle{\widetilde{\chi}_{k}\nabla\times((\nabla\times b)\times b),\chi_{k}\tilde{b}_{k}}\rangle+\langle{\widetilde{\chi}_{k}b,-(\tilde{{\mathbf{\Pi}}}_{k}\cdot\nabla)(\nabla\times\tilde{b}_{k})+((\nabla\times\tilde{b}_{k})\cdot\nabla)\tilde{{\mathbf{\Pi}}}_{k}}\rangle+\langle{\widetilde{\chi}_{k}b,\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{\tilde{b},k}}\rangle.\end{split}

We estimate terms similarly as in the proof of the localized energy estimate; using that
\tilde{b}_{k}
vanishes on the support of
\widetilde{\chi}_{k}^{\prime}
and that
{\mathbf{\Pi}}=\tilde{{\mathbf{\Pi}}}_{k}
on the support of
\chi_{k}
cancels several terms. We end up (see
[
19
, Section 5.2]
for details) with

\begin{split}&\left|\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\langle{\widetilde{\chi}_{k}b,\chi_{k}\tilde{b}_{k}}\rangle\right|\lesssim 2^{C_{s}k}\|{\widetilde{\chi}_{k}b}\|_{L^{2}}\|{\chi_{k}\tilde{b}_{k}}\|_{L^{2}}\end{split}

where the implicit constant depends on
\|{b}\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}H^{s}}
and
\|{{\mathbf{\Pi}}}\|_{W^{2,\infty}}+\|{\tilde{{\mathbf{\Pi}}}}\|_{W^{2,\infty}}
. Combining (
5.6
) with the above gives that

\begin{split}\left|\langle{\widetilde{\chi}_{k}b(t),\chi_{k}\tilde{b}_{k}(t)}\rangle-2^{-ck}\lambda_{k}^{-s}\right|\lesssim_{s}{2^{C_{s}k}}\int_{0}^{t}\left({2^{-k}}\lambda_{k}^{-s}+2^{2ks}t^{\prime s}\right)dt^{\prime}\lesssim_{s}{2^{C_{s}k}}({2^{-k}}\lambda_{k}^{-s}t+2^{2ks}t^{s+1}),\end{split}

so that
\langle{\widetilde{\chi}_{k}b,\chi_{k}\tilde{b}_{k}}\rangle\geq{2^{-(ck+1)}}\lambda_{k}^{-s}
on
[0,t^{*}_{k}]
with

(5.7)

{t^{*}_{k}=2^{-c_{s}k}\lambda_{k}^{-\frac{s}{s+1}}}.

Taking
k_{0}
larger if necessary, we have
t^{*}_{k}\leq\delta
for all
k\geq k_{0}
. Then

\begin{split}2^{-(ck+1)}\lambda_{k}^{-s}&\lesssim\|{\chi_{k}\tilde{b}_{k}(t^{*}_{k})}\|_{L^{2}_{\theta,z}L^{1}_{rdr}}\|{\widetilde{\chi}_{k}b(t^{*}_{k})}\|_{L^{2}_{\theta,z}L^{\infty}_{r}}\lesssim\|{\chi_{k}\tilde{b}_{k}(t^{*}_{k})}\|_{L^{2}_{\theta,z}L^{1}_{rdr}}\|{\widetilde{\chi}_{k}b(t^{*}_{k})}\|_{L^{2}}^{1-\frac{1}{2s}}\|{\widetilde{\chi}_{k}b(t^{*}_{k})}\|_{H^{s}}^{\frac{1}{2s}}.\end{split}

By the degeneration property (
5.6
), it follows that

\|{\widetilde{\chi}_{k}b(t^{*}_{k})}\|_{H^{s}}\gtrsim_{s}2^{-C_{s}k}\lambda_{k}^{-\frac{3s^{2}}{s+1}}\exp\left(2^{-c_{s}k}\lambda_{k}^{\frac{1}{s+1}}\right).

By the algebra property of
H^{s}
, we may replace the LHS by
\|{b(t^{*}_{k})}\|_{H^{s}}
(with a different
C_{s}
). Taking
N=N(s)\gg 1
(recall that
\lambda_{k}=2^{Nk}
), we may ensure that

\|{b(t^{*}_{k})}\|_{H^{s}}\gtrsim_{s}2^{c_{s}k}

for some
c_{s}>0
independent of
k\geq k_{0}
, which contradicts boundedness of
\|{b}\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}H^{s}}
. ∎

5.2.
Case of (
Hall-MHD
)

We now give the proof of nonexistence in the Hall-MHD case. The general idea is the same as in the E-MHD case; fix some background magnetic field with countably many degenerate points and place a degenerating wave packet solution near each degenerate point to conclude unbounded growth in any short amount of time. Due to the strong non-locality in the velocity interactions (caused by the pressure term), we are only able to handle data with non-compact support in the Hall-MHD case. But as we shall see below, this will significantly simplify the analysis as we can take the points of degeneracy very far from each other.

To begin, let
s>7/2
,
\delta>0
, and
\epsilon>0
be given and take
\tilde{{\mathbf{\Pi}}}_{0}
exactly as in the above case of (
E-MHD
). We then take

(5.8)

\begin{split}&{{\mathbf{\Pi}}}_{0}=\sum_{k=k_{0}}^{\infty}\tilde{{\mathbf{\Pi}}}_{0}^{(k)}=\sum_{k=k_{0}}^{\infty}2^{-k}\tilde{{\mathbf{\Pi}}}_{0}(x,y,z-z_{k}),\quad z_{k}=z_{k-1}+\mu^{k}\end{split}

where
\mu=\mu(N,s)\gg 1
and
k_{0}=k_{0}(N,s,\epsilon,\delta)\gg 1
are to be determined. Note that there are no spatial rescalings. Next, we take the
z
-independent degenerating wave packet solutions
\{\tilde{b}_{(\lambda)},\tilde{u}_{(\lambda)}\}
associated with
f_{0}(r)\partial_{\theta}
and set

\begin{split}&\tilde{b}_{k}(t):=\tilde{b}_{(\lambda_{k})}(2^{-k}t,x,y,z-z_{k}),\quad\tilde{u}_{k}(t):=\tilde{u}_{(\lambda_{k})}(2^{-k}t,x,y,z-z_{k})\end{split}

where
\lambda_{k}=2^{Nk}
with
N=N(s)\gg 1
to be determined. This time, we simply translate
\chi_{0}
(which is the same as the previous section) to define
\chi_{k}(z):=\chi_{0}(z-z_{k}).
Then,
U_{k}
is defined to be a cube of side-length
O(1)
containing the support of
\chi_{k}\tilde{b}_{k}(t)
for
t\in[0,\delta]
. It is easy to guarantee that the cubes
U_{k}
do not overlap with each other, by taking
\mu>1
in (
5.8
) larger if necessary. We then take the initial data
(\mathbf{0},{\bf B}_{0})
to (
Hall-MHD
) where

(5.9)

\begin{split}{\bf B}_{0}:={\mathbf{\Pi}}_{0}+\sum_{k=k_{0}}^{\infty}2^{-k}\lambda_{k}^{-s}\left(\chi_{k}\tilde{b}_{k}(0)-\mathrm{div}_{U_{k}}^{-1}(\chi_{k}^{\prime}\tilde{b}^{z}_{k}(0))\right).\end{split}

Clearly, we have
\|{{\bf B}_{0}}\|_{H^{s}}\leq\epsilon
by taking
k_{0}=k_{0}(\epsilon)\gg 1
.

We use the letter
{\bf V}
to denote the corresponding velocity field, which is smooth on the time interval
[0,\delta]
. Next, applying Lemma
2.9
with a choice of
m=10
gives smallness in time estimate (
2.17
), (
2.18
).

Now we assume that there exists a solution
({\bf u},{\bf B})\in L^{\infty}_{t}([0,\delta];H^{s-1}\times H^{s})
with initial data
(0,{\bf B}_{0})
. Setting
u={\bf u}-{\bf V}
and
b={\bf B}-{\mathbf{\Pi}}
, we have that
(u,b)
belongs to
L^{\infty}([0,\delta];H^{s-1}\times H^{s})
and satisfies (
4.2
)–(
4.3
). We note the following simple estimate for the pressure:

\begin{split}\|{{\bf p}}\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\|{|{\bf u}|^{2}}\|_{L^{2}}+\|{|{\bf B}|^{2}}\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\|{{\bf u}}\|_{H^{s-1}}^{2}+\|{{\bf B}}\|_{H^{s}}^{2}\end{split}

where we have used the Sobolev embedding
H^{s-1}\subset L^{\infty}
. Next, we take cutoff functions

\begin{split}\widetilde{\chi}_{k}(z)=\chi_{0}(2\mu^{-k}(z-z_{k})).\end{split}

As we shall see below, the proof of a local version of the energy identity for
(u,b)
is straightforward since we may take
\mu\gg 1
: simply note that
\|{\widetilde{\chi}_{k}^{\prime}}\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim\mu^{-k}
, and we now compute

\begin{split}\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\left(\|{\widetilde{\chi}_{k}u}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|{\widetilde{\chi}_{k}b}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)\end{split}

using (
4.2
); after straightforward estimates using integration by parts, we first obtain

\begin{split}&\left|\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\|{\widetilde{\chi}_{k}u}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\langle{\widetilde{\chi}_{k}({\mathbf{\Pi}}\cdot\nabla b),\widetilde{\chi}_{k}u}\rangle-\langle{\widetilde{\chi}_{k}\boldsymbol{\delta}_{u},\widetilde{\chi}_{k}u}\rangle\right|\\
&\qquad\lesssim\|{\widetilde{\chi}_{k}^{\prime}}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|{\widetilde{\chi}_{k}u}\|_{L^{2}}\left(\|{{\bf p}}\|_{L^{2}}+\nu\|{\nabla u}\|_{L^{2}}+1\right)+\|{\widetilde{\chi}_{k}u}\|_{L^{2}}(\|{\widetilde{\chi}_{k}u}\|_{L^{2}}+\|{\widetilde{\chi}_{k}b}\|_{L^{2}})\\
&\qquad\lesssim(\mu^{-k}+\|{\widetilde{\chi}_{k}u}\|_{L^{2}}+\|{\widetilde{\chi}_{k}b}\|_{L^{2}})\|{\widetilde{\chi}_{k}u}\|_{L^{2}},\end{split}

where the implicit constant depends on
M+\|{{\bf V}}\|_{W^{1,\infty}}+\|{{\mathbf{\Pi}}}\|_{W^{2,\infty}}
. Similarly,

\begin{split}&\left|\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\|{\widetilde{\chi}_{k}b}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\langle{\widetilde{\chi}_{k}({\mathbf{\Pi}}\cdot\nabla u),\widetilde{\chi}_{k}b}\rangle-\langle{\widetilde{\chi}_{k}\boldsymbol{\delta}_{b},\widetilde{\chi}_{k}b}\rangle\right|\lesssim(\mu^{-k}+\|{\widetilde{\chi}_{k}u}\|_{L^{2}}+\|{\widetilde{\chi}_{k}b}\|_{L^{2}})\|{\widetilde{\chi}_{k}b}\|_{L^{2}}\end{split}

where again the implicit constant depends on
M+\|{{\bf V}}\|_{W^{1,\infty}}+\|{{\mathbf{\Pi}}}\|_{W^{2,\infty}}
. Then note that

\begin{split}\left|\langle{\widetilde{\chi}_{k}({\mathbf{\Pi}}\cdot\nabla u),\widetilde{\chi}_{k}b}\rangle+\langle{\widetilde{\chi}_{k}({\mathbf{\Pi}}\cdot\nabla b),\widetilde{\chi}_{k}u}\rangle\right|\lesssim\|{\widetilde{\chi}_{k}^{\prime}}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|{|{\mathbf{\Pi}}|b}\|_{L^{2}}\|{\widetilde{\chi}_{k}u}\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\mu^{-k}\|{\widetilde{\chi}_{k}u}\|_{L^{2}}\end{split}

after integrating by parts. Moreover, recall the cancellation property
\langle{\boldsymbol{\delta}_{u},u}\rangle+\langle{\boldsymbol{\delta}_{b},b}\rangle=0
: from this it is straightforward to see that

\begin{split}\left|\langle{\widetilde{\chi}_{k}\boldsymbol{\delta}_{b},\widetilde{\chi}_{k}b}\rangle+\langle{\widetilde{\chi}_{k}\boldsymbol{\delta}_{u},\widetilde{\chi}_{k}u}\rangle\right|\lesssim\|{\widetilde{\chi}_{k}^{\prime}}\|_{L^{\infty}}\left(\|{\widetilde{\chi}_{k}u}\|_{L^{2}}+\|{\widetilde{\chi}_{k}b}\|_{L^{2}}\right)\end{split}

where the implicit constant depends on
M
. Collecting the estimates, we conclude that

\begin{split}\left|\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\left(\|{\widetilde{\chi}_{k}u}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|{\widetilde{\chi}_{k}b}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)\right|\lesssim(\mu^{-k}+\|{\widetilde{\chi}_{k}u}\|_{L^{2}}+\|{\widetilde{\chi}_{k}b}\|_{L^{2}})(\|{\widetilde{\chi}_{k}u}\|_{L^{2}}+\|{\widetilde{\chi}_{k}b}\|_{L^{2}}).\end{split}

Since at the initial time, we have for some absolute constant
c>0
,

\begin{split}(\|{\widetilde{\chi}_{k}u}\|_{L^{2}}+\|{\widetilde{\chi}_{k}b}\|_{L^{2}})(t=0)=\|{\widetilde{\chi}_{k}b}\|_{L^{2}}(t=0)\simeq 2^{-ck-Nsk},\end{split}

we may take
\mu\gg 1
in a way depending only on
N
and
s
that
\mu^{-k}\lesssim\|{\widetilde{\chi}_{k}b}\|_{L^{2}}(t=0)
. Then from Gronwall’s inequality, we obtain

\begin{split}\|{\widetilde{\chi}_{k}u}\|_{L^{2}}+\|{\widetilde{\chi}_{k}b}\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\|{\widetilde{\chi}_{k}b(t=0)}\|_{L^{2}},\end{split}

uniformly on
[0,\delta]
with an implicit constant independent of
k
.

Lastly it only remains to localize a version of the generalized energy estimate; we claim that

\begin{split}\left|\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\left(\langle{\widetilde{\chi}_{k}u,\chi_{k}\tilde{u}_{k}-\mathrm{div}_{U_{k}}^{-1}(\chi_{k}^{\prime}\tilde{u}_{k}^{z})}\rangle+\langle{\widetilde{\chi}_{k}b,\chi_{k}\tilde{b}_{k}}\rangle\right)\right|\lesssim 2^{C_{s}k}\|{\widetilde{\chi}_{k}b(t=0)}\|_{L^{2}}\|{\chi_{k}\tilde{b}_{k}(t=0)}\|_{L^{2}}.\end{split}

The proof is completely parallel to Section
3.2
; the background solution
({\bf V},{\mathbf{\Pi}})
satisfies Sobolev and smallness estimates uniformly, both in
t\in[0,\delta]
and
k
. Moreover, the additional terms appearing in the above (in comparison with Section
3.2
) only come from the additional localization
\widetilde{\chi}_{k}
and hence such contributions can be bounded in terms of
\|{\widetilde{\chi}_{k}^{\prime}}\|_{L^{\infty}}
which is at the same order with
\|{\widetilde{\chi}_{k}b(t=0)}\|_{L^{2}}
.

Given the above, we have at once that

\begin{split}&\langle{\widetilde{\chi}_{k}b,\chi_{k}\tilde{b}_{k}}\rangle\geq(1-c)\|{\widetilde{\chi}_{k}b(t=0)}\|_{L^{2}}\|{\chi_{k}\tilde{b}_{k}(t=0)}\|_{L^{2}}\end{split}

on the time interval
[0,T_{k}]
where
T_{k}=c(2^{-k}\lambda_{k})^{-1}\ln(2^{-k}\lambda_{k})
with some absolute constant
c>0
. Certainly
T_{k}
is decreasing in
k
and
T_{k}\leq\delta
for
k
sufficiently large. Recall that the degeneration estimate is given by

\begin{split}&\|{\tilde{\psi}_{k}(t)}\|_{L^{2}_{x,y}}\lesssim\lambda_{k}^{-1}e^{-c_{0}2^{-k}\lambda_{k}t}\|{\tilde{b}_{k}(t=0)}\|_{L^{2}_{x,y}}.\end{split}

We then argue along the lines of Sections
3.2
and
4.2
, resulting in

\begin{split}&\|{\widetilde{\chi}_{k}b(T_{k})}\|_{H^{s}}\gtrsim\epsilon\lambda_{k}^{c}\|{\widetilde{\chi}_{k}b_{0}}\|_{H^{s}}\gtrsim\epsilon\lambda_{k}^{\frac{c}{2}}\end{split}

where
c>0
is an absolute constant. Finally, observing that

\begin{split}&\|{\widetilde{\chi}_{k}b(T_{k})}\|_{H^{s}}\leq\|{\widetilde{\chi}_{k}}\|_{H^{s}}\|{b(T_{k})}\|_{H^{s}}\lesssim(1+\mu^{-1})\|{b(T_{k})}\|_{H^{s}}\end{split}

and taking
k\rightarrow+\infty
shows that
\liminf_{t\rightarrow 0^{+}}\|{b(t)}\|_{H^{s}}=+\infty,
which is a contradiction. ∎

Deriving a kinetic uncertainty relation for piecewise deterministic processes: from classical to quantum
Fei Liu

Abstract
From the perspective of Markovian piecewise deterministic processes (PDPs), we investigate the derivation of a kinetic uncertainty relation (KUR), which was originally proposed in Markovian open quantum systems. First, stationary distributions of classical PDPs are explicitly constructed. Then, a tilting method is used to derive a rate functional of large deviations. Finally, based on an improved approximation scheme, we recover the KUR. These classical results are directly extended to the open quantum systems. We use a driven two-level quantum system to exemplify the quantum results. Keywords: nonequilibrium fluctuations, kinetic uncertainty relations, piecewise deterministic processes, trajectory, large deviation principle, open quantum systems

II.1
Liouville-master equation

The conventional approach of studying stochastic processes is to establish the integral of differential Chapman-Kolmogorov equations
Risken 1984
. For the PDPs, the corresponding equation for the probability density function
p(x,t)
is known
Davis 1984
;
Breuer and Petruccione 2002
. In the following, we derive the same result by an intuitive age-structure formalism used in semi-Markov processes
Ross 1995
;
Wang and Qian 2007
. This enables us to construct the stationary distribution of the PDPs.

Let
p(x,t;\alpha,\tau)
be the joint probability density when the state of the system at time
t
is
x
, the time of successive evolution is
\tau
, and the type of the last jump is
\alpha

\displaystyle\int{\rm d}x\left[\sum_{\beta=1}^{M}\int_{0}^{\infty}{\rm d}\tau p(x,t;\beta,\tau)\right]=1.

(7)

Note that the complete term in the squared brackets of Eq. (
7

of the system in state

at time
t

\tau
approaches infinity, the function

decays to zero; also see Eq. (
6

p(x,t;\alpha,\tau=0)
is also equal to the probability density that other continuous states jump to
x_{\alpha}
through
\alpha

t

\displaystyle p(x,t;\alpha,0)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\beta=1}^{M}\int{\rm d}y\int_{0}^{\infty}{\rm d}\tau p(y,t;\beta,\tau)w_{\alpha}(y|x_{\alpha})\delta(x-x_{\alpha})

(8)

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle c_{\alpha}(t)\delta(x-x_{\alpha}).

The defined functions

\alpha=1,\cdots,M

\alpha

in Eq. (

immediately after the

Let

\displaystyle p(x,t+h;\alpha,\tau+h)=\int{\rm d}yp(y,t;\alpha,\tau)\left[1-\Gamma(y)h\right]\delta(x-y-g(y)h)+{\it o}(h).

(9)

The reason for this is that if the system starts with the target state

after the

1

\displaystyle\partial_{t}p(x,t;\alpha,\tau)+\partial_{\tau}p(x,t;\alpha,\tau)=-\partial_{x}(g(x)p(x,t;\alpha,\tau))-\Gamma(x)p(x,t;\alpha,\tau).

(10)

Integrating and summing Eq. (

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\partial_{x}(g(x)p(x,t))+\int{\rm d}y[p(y,t)W(y|x)-p(x,t)W(x|y)].

Now, the reason for defining

IV
A kinetic uncertainty relation

Following Carollo et al.

, the time-extensive trajectory observable we are concerned with is a linear combination of the empirical rates
{k}_{\alpha}
which is the number of
\alpha
-jump happening in unit time along a trajectory

:

\displaystyle{J}[X_{T}]=\sum_{\alpha=1}^{M}\omega_{\alpha}{k}_{\alpha}[X_{T}].

(24)

Here,
\omega_{\alpha}
,
\alpha=1,\cdots,M
, represent certain
\alpha

We emphasize that these

are functionals of trajectory. Even if the system has been in the steady state, they usually do not equal
c_{\alpha}
because of fluctuations in finite time interval
T
. Of course, observing these large fluctuations is rare, and their values are mainly surrounding

\displaystyle{J}_{s}=\sum_{\alpha=1}^{M}\omega_{\alpha}c_{\alpha}.

(25)

To quantify their deviation, we introduce a dimensionless parameter

Assume that the LD principle is valid for the observable (
24

VI
Conclusion

In this paper, we investigated the derivation of the KUR originally found in open quantum systems. We find that this relation is in fact generally valid for the stochastic systems, either classical or quantum, that are described by the PDPs. The underlying cause is that the rates of jumps, rather than the types of the stochastic variables, determine the structure of fluctuations of these stochastic processes.

There are two possible questions worthy of further study. One question is to apply the KUR in practical classical systems that are described by the PDPs and are especially interesting in physics. One potential candidate is a certain type of molecular motors in biological physics: These macromolecules can be described by fully coupled discrete chemical states and continuous mechanical states, or so-called power-stroke model. The second question of further study is relevant to open quantum systems. Hasegawa has obtained another quantum uncertainty relation for the same Markovian open quantum systems. Clarifying their connections should be of interest.

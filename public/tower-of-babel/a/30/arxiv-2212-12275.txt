Abstract
. We prove the torsion freeness of the decomposable Orlik–Solomon algebra of a simple matroid on ground set [ n ] [n] . In the class of hypersolvable & non-supersolvable complex hyperplane arrangements, the torsion freeness, in a certain degree, of this combinatorially defined object, associated to the intersection lattice of the arrangement, impacts on the first non-vanishing higher homotopy group of the complement of the arrangement.



2010 Mathematics Subject Classification
Primary 52C35, 05B35; Secondary 55Q52

1.
Introduction

Let
\mathcal{A}
be an arrangement of hyperplanes in a complex vector space
V
, i.e., a finite set of hyperplanes in
V
. The
intersection lattice
of
\mathcal{A}
, denoted
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{A})
, is by definition the poset of intersections of subsets of
\mathcal{A}
, ordered by reverse inclusion, with a rank map given by codimension (see
[
8
]
for a comprehensive approach).
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{A})
is a geometric lattice, so it translates into the the existence of a simple matroid
M(\mathcal{A})
over the ground set
\mathcal{A}
, where dependency is defined as linear dependency. We will refer to a property or invariant associated to the arrangement as
combinatorially determined
(or simply,
combinatorial
), if they are determined by the intersection lattice of the arrangement.

A strongly combinatorial class of arrangements (in the above sense) is the class of
supersolvable arrangements
, introduced by Stanley in
[
10
]
, see also the equivalent notion of
fiber type
arrangements, topologically defined by Falk-Randell in
[
2
]
.
Supersolvability was generalized by Jambu-Papadima (
[
5
]
) to
hypersolvability
. Hypersolvable arrangements are characterised by the fact that they admit deformations into supersolvable arrangements (see
[
6
]
for the precise definition of such deformations).
A notable subclass of hypersolvable arrangements is the class of
2
-generic arrangements (arrangements whose intersection lattice has no dependency in rank
2
).
Complements of supersolvable arrangements are known to be
K(\pi,1)
spaces, property that no longer holds in the general hypothesis of hypersolvability. In fact, asphericity of the complement characterizes supersolvability inside the hypersolvable class, so it makes sense to study the occurrence and properties of higher non-trivial homotopy groups of the complement of a hypersolvable, non-supersolvable, arrangement.

This short note tries to answer a question raised in
[
7
]
concerning the torsion freeness for the
decomposable Orlik-Solomon
(OS) algebra associated to an arrangement of hyperplanes.

The motivation comes from a result in
[
7
]
(see Theorem
2.1
), which gives a topological meaning to the torsion freeness of the decomposable OS algebra (in a certain degree), for hypersolvable & non-supersolvable arrangements of hyperplanes, via a connection to the first non-vanishing higher homotopy group of the complement of the arrangement.

In general, the higher homotopy groups of a complement of an arrangement are very difficult to compute, and the same holds true even for their ’approximation’ by graded objects (see
[
9
]
). If
\pi_{1}:=\pi_{1}(X(\mathcal{A}))
is the fundamental group of the complement
X(\mathcal{A})
of an arrangement
\mathcal{A}
, then any higher homotopy group
\pi_{*>1}:=\pi_{*>1}(X(\mathcal{A}))
has a module structure over the group ring
R:=\mathbb{Z}\pi_{1}
, and one can consider the associated graded module

(1.1)

gr_{I}^{\bullet}(\pi_{p}):=\oplus_{q\geq 0}(\pi_{p}I^{q}/\pi_{p}I^{q+1})

over the graded ring
\gr_{I}^{\bullet}R
, where
I\subset R
is the augmentation ideal (in line with the notations from
[
7
]
, throughout the text,
-^{\bullet}
will signify the presence of a grading).

For hypersolvable arrangements
\mathcal{A}
consider the first non-vanishing higher homotopy group
\pi_{p(\mathcal{A})}
of the complement
X(\mathcal{A})
. Then
p(\mathcal{A})
is combinatorially determined, see
[
9
]
.
The graded ring
\gr_{I}^{\bullet}R
is combinatorial and torsion free (
[
1
]
), and so is the first graded piece
\gr_{I}^{0}\pi_{p(\mathcal{A})}
of the associated graded module (
1.1
) (
[
9
]
). In
[
7
]
the attention turns to the second graded piece of the graded module (
1.1
) associated to the first non-vanishing higher homotopy group,
\gr^{1}_{I}\pi_{p(\mathcal{A})}
, whose torsion freeness is characterised in terms of the torsion freeness, in a certain degree, of a combinatorially defined graded algebra, the so called decomposable OS algebra (see the next section for the definition).

Our main result, Theorem
3.5
, proves the torsion freeness of the decomposable OS algebra of an arbitrary simple matroid on ground set
[n]
. In particular, the decomposable OS algebra associated to an arrangement of hyperplanes is torsion free.
As a corollary, we obtain that
\gr^{1}_{I}\pi_{p(\mathcal{A})}
is also torsion free.

2.
Preliminaries

Let
\Lambda^{\bullet}
be the free exterior algebra over
\mathbb{Z}
on
n
generators
\{e_{1},\dots,e_{n}\}
.
The degree
m
component is generated by monomials
e_{X}:=e_{i_{1}}\wedge\dots\wedge e_{i_{m}}
, where
X\subseteq[n]
is the ordered set
\{i_{1}<\dots<i_{m}\}
and
e_{\emptyset}=1
.
Define the boundary map on
\Lambda^{\bullet},\;\partial:\Lambda^{\bullet}\rightarrow\Lambda^{\bullet-1}
to be the unique degree
-1
graded algebra derivation such that
\partial(e_{i})=1,\;i\in[n]
.

Let
M
be a simple matroid on the ground set
[n]
. The
Orlik-Solomon ideal
of
M
is the (graded) ideal
\mathcal{I}(M)
in
\Lambda^{\bullet}
generated by the boundaries
\{\partial(e_{C})\;|\;C\ \in\mathcal{C}(M)\}
of the circuits in
M
.
The
decomposable Orlik-Solomon ideal
is the product ideal
\Lambda^{+}\mathcal{I}(M)\subset\mathcal{I}(M)
, where
\Lambda^{+}
is the ideal of
\Lambda^{\bullet}
consisting of elements of strictly positive degree.
The
Orlik-Solomon algebra
of
M
is the graded quotient algebra
A^{\bullet}(M):=\Lambda^{\bullet}/\mathcal{I}(M)
. The
decomposable Orlik-Solomon algebra
of
M
is the graded quotient algebra
A_{+}^{\bullet}(M):=\Lambda^{\bullet}/\Lambda^{+}\mathcal{I}(M)
.
All the above objects can be defined over an arbitrary field
\mathbb{K}
. As ongoing convention, we will denote by
-_{\mathbb{K}}
the specialization to
-\mathbb{K}
coefficients.

We will be mainly interested in the case of matroids coming from arrangements of hyperplanes.
A
circuit

C
in an arrangement
\mathcal{A}
is a minimally (linearly) dependent subset of
\mathcal{A}
. Denote by
\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{A})
the OS ideal of
\mathcal{A}
, and by
A^{\bullet}(\mathcal{A}),\;A_{+}^{\bullet}(\mathcal{A})
the OS, respectively decomposable OS algebra of
\mathcal{A}
.

The OS algebra
A^{\bullet}(\mathcal{A})
of an arrangement is torsion free, since it admits a basis expressed solely in terms of circuits of
\mathcal{A}
, that does not depend on the field of coefficients, for any specialization
A^{\bullet}_{\mathbb{K}}(\mathcal{A})
(see for instance
[
3
,
11
]
). It also carries significant topological meaning, as it is isomorphic to the cohomology algebra, with integer coefficients, of the complement of the arrangement, by an emblematic result of Orlik and Solomon. If the arrangement
\mathcal{A}
is hypersolvable, the decomposable OS algebra of
\mathcal{A}
reflects some of the topology of the complement:

Theorem 2.1
.

(
[
7
]
)

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a hypersolvable and not supersolvable complex hyperplane arrangement with complement
X(\mathcal{A})
, and
p>1
minimal with the property that
\pi_{p}(X(\mathcal{A}))
is non-trivial. Then the following are equivalent:

(1)

The second graded piece of the graded module (
1.1
),
\gr^{1}_{I}\pi_{p}(X(\mathcal{A}))
, has no torsion.

(2)

The decomposable Orlik-Solomon algebra,
A_{+}^{\bullet}(\mathcal{A})
, is free in degree
\bullet=p+2
.

In the hypothesis of the previous theorem, we know that the minimal length of a circuit
C\in\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{A})
with
|C|>3
is equal to
p+2
. In particular, for a
2
-generic arrangement
\mathcal{A}
,
p+2
is the minimal length of a circuit in
\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{A})
.

3.
Torsion freeness for the decomposable OS algebra

Let
\Lambda^{\bullet}_{\mathbb{K}}
be the free exterior algebra on
n
generators
\{e_{1},\dots,e_{n}\}
over a field
\mathbb{K}
, defined in the previous section. A
monomial order
on
\Lambda^{\bullet}_{\mathbb{K}}
is a total order on the set of monomials
Mon(\Lambda^{\bullet}_{\mathbb{K}})
of
\Lambda^{\bullet}_{\mathbb{K}}
such that:

(1)

1<e_{X}
for any monomial
e_{X}\neq 1

(2)

if
e_{X}<e_{Y}
then
e_{X}\wedge e_{Z}<e_{Y}\wedge e_{Z}
, for
e_{X},e_{Y},e_{Z}
monomials such that
e_{X}\wedge e_{Z}\neq 0\neq e_{Y}\wedge e_{Z}
.

We say that a monomial
e_{X}

divides
a monomial
e_{Y}
iff
X\subseteq Y
.

Let
f=\sum_{i=1}^{m}a_{i}e_{X_{i}}\in\Lambda^{\bullet}_{\mathbb{K}}
, where
a_{i}\in\mathbb{K}\setminus\{0\}
and
e_{X_{i}}\in Mon(\Lambda^{\bullet}_{\mathbb{K}})
. The support of
f
is the set of monomials
supp(f):=\{e_{X_{1}},\dots,e_{X_{m}}\}
.

in_{\prec}(f)
, the
initial monomial
of
f
with respect to a monomial order
\prec
, is the biggest monomial with respect to
\prec
among
\{e_{X_{1}},\dots,e_{X_{m}}\}
.

A
Gröbner basis
for a non-zero ideal
\mathcal{I}
in the exterior algebra
\Lambda^{\bullet}_{\mathbb{K}}
, with respect to a monomial order
\prec
, is a finite set of elements
\mathcal{G}_{\prec}\subset\mathcal{I}
with the property that for any
f\in\mathcal{I}
there exists
g\in\mathcal{G}_{\prec}
such that
in_{\prec}(g)
divides
in_{\prec}(f)
.
A Gröbner basis is called
reduced
if, for all
g\in\mathcal{G}_{\prec},\;in_{\prec}(g)
has coefficient equal to
1
and, for all
g\neq g^{\prime}\in\mathcal{G}_{\prec}
, no monomial in
supp(g^{\prime})
is divisible by
in_{\prec}(g)
.
We refer to
[
4
]
for more details on the theory of Gröbner bases over exterior algebras.

In
[
11
]
, respectively
[
3
]
, one constructs Gröbner bases for OS ideals (for arrangements, respectively for simple matroids in general), which are then used to describe bases for the associated OS algebras, bases that do not depend on the chosen field of coefficients. This already proves torsion freeness for the considered OS algebras.

Let
\mathcal{C}(M)
denote the set of circuits of a simple matroid
M
on ground set
[n]
,
\mathcal{C}_{q}(M):=\{C\in\mathcal{C}(M)\;|\;|C|=q\}
the subset of circuits of length
q
and

\overline{\partial}_{q}:\langle e_{C}\;|\;C\in\mathcal{C}_{q+1}(M)\rangle_{\mathbb{K}}\rightarrow(\mathcal{I}(M)/\Lambda^{+}\mathcal{I}(M))^{q}_{\mathbb{K}}

the composition of the restriction
\partial|_{\langle e_{C}\;|\;C\in\mathcal{C}_{q+1}(M)\rangle_{\mathbb{K}}}
to the projection
\mathcal{I}^{q}_{\mathbb{K}}(M)\twoheadrightarrow(\mathcal{I}(M)/\Lambda^{+}\mathcal{I}(M))^{q}_{\mathbb{K}}
, where
\langle\rangle_{\mathbb{K}}
denotes
\mathbb{K}
-span.

In
[
7
]
one proves the torsion freeness in all degrees for the decomposable OS algebra associated to a graphic arrangement, by showing that the map
\overline{\partial}_{q}
, restricted to chordless
(q+1)
-circuits, is actually an isomorphism. It is not difficult to see, taking into account the description of circuits in signed graphic arrangements given by Zaslavsky (
[
12
]
), that the same technique can be used to prove the torsion freeness in all degrees for the decomposable OS algebra associated to a signed graphic arrangement.
The problem with this approach is that it does not extend to general arrangements, as shown by
[
7
, Ex. 4.7, Ex. 4.8]
.

We solve this problem by replacing the set of chordless
(q+1)
-circuits by a more suitable set of
(q+1)
-circuits, so the restriction of the map
\overline{\partial}_{q}
remains an isomorphism.
We essentially use a result of Forge (
[
3
]
) that describes the reduced Grobner basis of the OS ideal, for an arbitrary monomial order.

We recall some notions related to matroids, following
[
3
]
. Let
M
be a simple matroid on the ground set
[n]
, and
I
an independent set of
M
. An element
i\in[n]
is called
active
with respect to
I
if
I\cup\{i\}
contains a circuit with minimal element
i
.

Any permutation
\pi
of
[n]
defines an order on the elements of
[n]
,

\;\pi^{-1}(1)<_{\pi}\pi^{-1}(2)<_{\pi}\dots<_{\pi}\pi^{-1}(n),

and a monomial order
\prec_{\pi}
induced by

e_{\pi^{-1}(1)}\prec_{\pi}e_{\pi^{-1}(2)}\prec_{\pi}\dots\prec_{\pi}e_{\pi^{-1}(n)}.

Let
\mathcal{C}^{\pi}(M)
be the subset of circuits of
M
such that:

(1)

inf_{\prec_{\pi}}(C)=\alpha_{\pi}(C)

(2)

C\setminus\alpha_{\pi}(C)
is inclusion minimal with property (1),

where
\alpha_{\pi}(C)
is by definition the smallest active element with respect to the independent
C\setminus\{inf_{\prec_{\pi}}(C)\}
, relative to the order
\prec_{\pi}
.

Theorem 3.1
.

(
[
3
]
)

\mathcal{G}_{\pi}:=\{\partial(e_{C})|C\in\mathcal{C}^{\pi}(M)\}
is a reduced Gröbner basis for the ideal
\mathcal{I}(M)
with respect to the monomial order
\prec_{\pi}
.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
\pi
be a permutation such that

|\mathcal{C}_{q+1}^{\pi}(M)|=min\{|\mathcal{C}_{q+1}^{\pi^{\prime}}(M)|\;|\;\pi^{\prime}permutation\;of\;[n]\}.

Then the map

\overline{\partial}_{q}:\langle e_{C}\;|\;C\in\mathcal{C}^{\pi}_{q+1}(M)\rangle_{\mathbb{K}}\rightarrow(\mathcal{I}(M)/\Lambda^{+}\mathcal{I}(M))_{\mathbb{K}}^{q}

is an isomorphism.

Proof.

Since
\mathcal{G}_{\pi}
is a Gröbner basis for
\mathcal{I}(M)
, it’s also a system of generators for
\mathcal{I}(M)
, so it immediately follows that the map
\overline{\partial}_{q}
is a surjection.
Also, notice that the map
\partial_{q}:\langle e_{C}\;|\;C\in\mathcal{C}^{\pi}_{q+1}(M)\rangle_{\mathbb{K}}\rightarrow\mathcal{I}(M)_{\mathbb{K}}^{q}
is injective, following from the fact that
\mathcal{G}_{\pi}
is reduced.

To prove injectivity for
\overline{\partial}_{q}
, let

(3.1)

\mathfrak{e}=\sum_{C\in\mathcal{C}^{\pi}_{q+1}(M)}\mu_{C}e_{C}\in Ker(\overline{\partial}_{q}),\;\mu_{C}\in\mathbb{K},\;\overline{\partial}_{q}(\mathfrak{e})=0.

Then

(3.2)

\sum_{C\in\mathcal{C}^{\pi}_{q+1}(M)}\mu_{C}\partial(e_{C})=\sum_{S,\overline{C}}\xi_{\overline{C},S}e_{S}\partial(e_{\overline{C}}),\;\xi_{\overline{C},S}\in\mathbb{K},\;e_{S}\in\Lambda^{+},\;\overline{C}\in\mathcal{C}^{\pi}_{\leq q}(M).

Consider the initial monomials
in_{\prec_{\pi}}(\partial(e_{C}))
for all
C
appearing in the sum (
3.1
). All these monomials must be distinct, otherwise, there would exist two
(q+1)
-circuits, say
C_{1},C_{2}\in\mathcal{C}^{\pi}_{q+1}(M)
, such that
in_{\prec_{\pi}}(\partial(e_{C_{1}}))=in_{\prec_{\pi}}(\partial(e_{C_{2}}))
, then
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
would differ by an element,
inf_{\prec_{\pi}}(C_{1})\neq inf_{\prec_{\pi}}(C_{2})
. But this implies that
C_{1},C_{2}
cannot be simultaneously elements in
\mathcal{C}^{\pi}(M)
, since either
\alpha_{\pi}(C_{1})\prec_{\pi}inf_{\prec_{\pi}}(C_{1})
or
\alpha_{\pi}(C_{2})\prec_{\pi}inf_{\prec_{\pi}}(C_{2})
, contradiction.

In fact, since
C\in\mathcal{C}^{\pi}_{q+1}(M)
for all
(q+1)
-circuits
C
in (
3.1
), i.e.
\partial(e_{C})
are all elements of a reduced Gröbner basis for
\mathcal{I}(M)
, the monomials
in_{\prec_{\pi}}(e_{C})
do not cancel out with any other monomial in the left hand side of the equality (
3.2
). So, if the coefficient
\mu_{C}\neq 0
, then on the right side of (
3.2
) there exists
\overline{C}\in\mathcal{C}^{\pi}_{\leq q}(M)
such that
in_{\prec_{\pi}}(\partial(e_{C}))
is a monomial in
e_{S}\partial(e_{\overline{C}})
, for some
e_{S}\in\Lambda^{+},\;\overline{C}\in\mathcal{C}^{\pi}_{\leq q}(M)
.
Say
e_{X}
is the monomial in
\partial(e_{\overline{C}})
such that
in_{\prec_{\pi}}(e_{C})=e_{S}e_{X}
.

If
e_{X}=in_{\prec_{\pi}}(e_{\overline{C}})
, then
in_{\prec_{\pi}}(e_{\overline{C}})
divides
in_{\prec_{\pi}}(e_{C})
, contradiction, since both
\partial(e_{C})
and
\partial(e_{\overline{C}})
are elements in the reduced Grobner basis
\mathcal{G}_{\pi}
.
Then necessarily
e_{X}\neq in_{\prec_{\pi}}(e_{\overline{C}})
. Let
e_{\overline{C}}=e_{i_{1}}\wedge\dots\wedge e_{i_{p}},\;e_{i_{1}}\prec\dots\prec e_{i_{p}},\;p\leq q

and
e_{X}=e_{\overline{C}\setminus\{i_{j}\}}=e_{i_{1}}\wedge\dots\widehat{e_{i_{j}}}\dots\wedge e_{i_{p}}
, with
e_{i_{1}}\prec_{\pi}e_{i_{j}}
.
In this case, we will show that there is another monomial order, induced by a permutation
\pi_{0}
on
[n]
, such that
|\mathcal{C}_{q+1}^{\pi_{0}}(M)|<|\mathcal{C}_{q+1}^{\pi}(M)|
, thus arriving at a contradiction.

Take a permutation
\pi^{\prime}
on
[n]
such that
\pi^{\prime-1}(k)=\pi^{-1}(k)
for
k<\pi(i_{1})
or
k>\pi(i_{j})
,
\pi^{\prime-1}(\pi(i_{1}))=i_{j}
and
\pi^{\prime-1}(k)=\pi^{-1}(k-1)
, for
\pi(i_{1})<k\leq\pi(i_{j})
. Basically, the corresponding order
<_{\pi^{\prime}}
on
[n]
is obtained from the order
<_{\pi}
by moving
i_{j}
immediately to the left of
i_{1}
.

In order to prove
\overline{C}\in\mathcal{C}^{\pi^{\prime}}(M)
, it is enough to show that

i_{j}=\alpha_{\pi^{\prime}}(\overline{C})
.
Take
e_{m}\in\Lambda^{+},m\in[n]
, such that
(m,i_{1},i_{2},\dots,\hat{i_{j}},\dots,i_{p})
contains a circuit with minimal element
m
, in the ordering
\prec_{\pi^{\prime}}
. Then
m
is in the closure
cl(\{i_{1},i_{2},\dots,\hat{i_{j}},\dots,i_{p}\})=cl(\overline{C})=cl(\{i_{2},i_{3},\dots,i_{j},\dots,i_{p}\})
. This means that the set
\{m,i_{2},i_{3},\dots,i_{j},\dots,i_{p}\}
contains a circuit. If moreover we assume
m<_{\pi^{\prime}}i_{j}
, then
m<_{\pi}i_{1}
, contradiction to
i_{1}=\alpha_{\pi}(\overline{C})
. It follows that
i_{j}<_{\pi^{\prime}}m
, consequently
i_{j}=\alpha_{\pi^{\prime}}(\overline{C})
.

Notice that
\alpha_{\pi}(C)=\alpha_{\pi^{\prime}}(C)=e_{inf_{\prec_{\pi}}(C)}
.
But, since
in_{\prec_{\pi^{\prime}}}(e_{C})=e_{S}\wedge in_{\prec_{\pi^{\prime}}}(e_{\overline{C}})
,
C\notin\mathcal{C}_{q+1}^{\pi^{\prime}}(M)
.

We will show that any circuit in
\mathcal{C}_{q+1}^{\pi^{\prime}}(M)
, or, eventually, any circuit in
\mathcal{C}_{q+1}^{\pi_{0}}(M)
(corresponding to a suitable modification
\pi_{0}
of
\pi^{\prime}
) induces a circuit in
\mathcal{C}_{q+1}^{\pi}(M)
,
and the correspondence is injective, and, moreover,
\pi_{0}
still retains the properties of
\pi^{\prime}
above mentioned, related to the circuits
C,\overline{C}
. This injective correspondence, along with the fact that
C\in\mathcal{C}_{q+1}^{\pi}(M)
is not the correspondent of any circuit in
\mathcal{C}_{q+1}^{\pi_{0}}(M)
, would imply that
|\mathcal{C}_{q+1}^{\pi_{0}}(M)|<|\mathcal{C}_{q+1}^{\pi}(M)|
, contradiction to the hypothesis on the permutation
\pi
.

It is not hard to check that any circuit
C^{\prime}
in
\mathcal{C}_{q+1}^{\pi^{\prime}}(M)
such that
inf_{\prec_{\pi^{\prime}}}(C^{\prime})\neq i_{j}
is also a circuit in
\mathcal{C}_{q+1}^{\pi}(M)
, and
inf_{\prec_{\pi^{\prime}}}(C^{\prime})=inf_{\prec_{\pi}}(C^{\prime})
. If a circuit
C_{0}\in\mathcal{C}_{q+1}^{\pi^{\prime}}(M)
is such that
inf_{\prec_{\pi^{\prime}}}(C_{0}))=i_{j}
, then, if
C_{0}=(i_{j},a_{1},\dots,a_{q})
is not already a circuit in
\mathcal{C}_{q+1}^{\pi}(M)
, there are two situations to consider. Notice that if
C_{0}\notin\mathcal{C}_{q+1}^{\pi}(M)
, then
i_{j}
is no longer the minimal element of
C_{0}
, with respect to the order induced by
\pi
.

Firstly, if there exists
a^{\prime}<_{\pi}a_{1}
, such that
(a^{\prime},i_{j},\widehat{a_{1}},a_{2},\dots,a_{q})
contains a circuit
c
with
a^{\prime}=\alpha_{\prec_{\pi}}(c)
. Then
a^{\prime}<_{\pi^{\prime}}a_{1}
and the set
S:=(a^{\prime},a_{1},\dots,a_{q})
is dependent. But, since
C_{0}=(i_{j},a_{1},\dots,a_{q})
is a circuit in
\mathcal{C}_{q+1}^{\pi^{\prime}}(M)
, the set
S
must be a circuit in itself, otherwise we get a strict subset of
S
that is a circuit in
\mathcal{C}_{\leq q}^{\pi^{\prime}}(M)
with minimal element
a^{\prime}
,
a contradiction to the fact that
C_{0}\in\mathcal{G}_{\pi^{\prime}}
, which is reduced. Then, corresponding to the circuit
C_{0}\in\mathcal{C}_{q+1}^{\pi^{\prime}}(M)
we get the
(q+1)
-circuit
S
in
\mathcal{C}_{q+1}^{\pi}(M)
.

Secondly, assume there exists
a^{\prime}>_{\pi}a_{1}
, such that
(a^{\prime},i_{j},\widehat{a_{1}},a_{2}\dots,a_{q})
contains a circuit with
a^{\prime}
minimal element with respect to
<_{\pi}
. Then
a^{\prime}>_{\pi^{\prime}}a_{1}
and it is possible that
(a^{\prime},i_{j},\widehat{a_{1}},a_{2}\dots,a_{q})
contains a circuit with length strictly smaller than
q+1
. In this case we alter the permutation
\pi^{\prime}
by moving
a^{\prime}
immediately to the left of
a_{1}
, and we are back in the situation from the first case, so we can define a
(q+1)
-circuit associated to
C_{0}
in
\mathcal{C}_{q+1}^{\pi}(M)
. The newly obtained permutation, say
\pi^{\prime\prime}
, still retains the properties required for
\pi^{\prime}
, that is,
\overline{C}\in\mathcal{C}^{\pi^{\prime\prime}}(M)
and
in_{\prec_{\pi^{\prime}}}(e_{C})=e_{S}\wedge in_{\prec_{\pi^{\prime}}}(e_{\overline{C}})
, so
C\notin\mathcal{C}^{\pi^{\prime\prime}}(M)
. Moreover, each new modification, in the sense described before, of a permutation is situated to the right of the previous one in the order induced by the permutation.
Finally, notice that two distinct circuits in
\mathcal{C}_{q+1}^{\pi^{\prime\prime}}(M)
cannot correspond to the same circuit in
\mathcal{C}_{q+1}^{\pi}(M)
, due to the fact that
\mathcal{G}_{\pi^{\prime\prime}}
is reduced.

In this way, eventually after a number of moves as described before, we get a permutation
\pi_{0}
of
[n]
with the required properties.

Example 3.3
.

Let us revisit for instance
[
7
, Example 4.8]
. Let
\mathcal{A}\subset\mathbb{C}^{4}
be the arrangement of equation
xyzt(x+y+z+t)(x-y-z+t)=0
. Denote by
H,P
, the hyperplanes of equations
x+y+z+t=0
, respectively
x-y-z+t=0
. The set of circuits of
\mathcal{A}
is
\{(H,P,y,z),\;(H,P,x,t),\;(H,x,y,z,t),\;(P,x,y,z,t)\}
.
Let
\Lambda^{\bullet}(\mathcal{A}):=\Lambda(e_{H},e_{P},e_{x},e_{y},e_{z},e_{t})
be the exterior algebra with generators labelled by the hyperplanes of
\mathcal{A}
. Consider first the monomial order on
\Lambda^{\bullet}(\mathcal{A})
induced by
x<y<z<t<H<P
. The reduced Gröbner basis of the OS ideal
\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{A})
with respect to this order is given by the boundaries of the set of
4
circuits
\{(y,z,H,P),\;(x,t,H,P),\;(x,y,z,t,H),(x,y,z,t,P)\}
.

But for the monomial order induced by, say,
H<P<x<y<z<t
, the associated reduced Gröbner basis of
\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{A})
is given by the boundaries of the set of
3
circuits
\{(H,P,y,z),\;(H,P,x,t),\;(H,x,y,z,t)\}
, and
3
is the minimal cardinal for the reduced Gröbner bases of
\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{A})
obtained by varying the order among the hyperplanes of
\mathcal{A}
.

In this example,
dim_{\mathbb{K}}(\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{A})/\Lambda^{+}\mathcal{I}(A))_{\mathbb{K}}^{4}=1
,
(\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{A})/\Lambda^{+}\mathcal{I}(A))_{\mathbb{K}}^{q}=\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{A})_{\mathbb{K}}^{q}
, for
q<4
, and
(\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{A})/\Lambda^{+}\mathcal{I}(A))_{\mathbb{K}}^{q}=0
, for
q>4
.

Corollary 3.4
.

The graded abelian group
\mathcal{I}(M)/\Lambda^{+}\mathcal{I}(M)
is torsion free, in any degree
q
.

Proof.

Follows immediately from Proposition
3.2
.
∎

Theorem 3.5
.

The decomposable Orlik-Solomon algebra
A_{+}^{\bullet}(M)
is torsion free.

Proof.

It follows from the previous Corollary
3.4
and the split exact sequence:

0\rightarrow(\mathcal{I}(M)/\Lambda^{+}\mathcal{I}(M))^{\bullet}\rightarrow A_{+}^{\bullet}(M)\rightarrow A^{\bullet}(M)\rightarrow 0.

∎

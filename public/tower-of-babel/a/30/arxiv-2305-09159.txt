Tensor product of representations of quivers
Pradeep Das, Umesh V. Dubey and N. Raghavendra

Abstract
. In this article, we define the tensor product V ⊗ W V\otimes W of a representation V V of a quiver Q Q with a representation W W of an another quiver Q ′ Q^{\prime} , and show that the representation V ⊗ W V\otimes W is semistable if V V and W W are semistable. We give a relation between the universal representations on the fine moduli spaces N 1 , N 2 N_{1},N_{2} and N 3 N_{3} of representations of Q , Q ′ Q,Q^{\prime} and Q ⊗ Q ′ Q\otimes Q^{\prime} respectively over arbitrary algebraically closed fields. We further describe a relation between the natural line bundles on these moduli spaces when the base is the field of complex numbers. We then prove that the internal product Q ~ ⊗ Q ′ ~ \tilde{Q}\otimes\tilde{Q^{\prime}} of covering quivers is a sub-quiver of the covering quiver Q ⊗ Q ′ ~ \widetilde{Q\otimes Q^{\prime}} . We deduce the relation between stability of the representations V ⊗ W ~ \widetilde{V\otimes W} and V ~ ⊗ W ~ \tilde{V}\otimes\tilde{W} , where V ~ \tilde{V} denotes the lift of the representation V V of Q Q to the covering quiver Q ~ \tilde{Q} . We also lift the relation between the natural line bundles on the product of moduli spaces N 1 ~ × N 2 ~ \tilde{N_{1}}\times\tilde{N_{2}} .



Introduction

Whenever the tensor product of two objects exists in a category on
which there is a notion of semistability, it is natural to ask whether
the tensor product of two semistable objects is again semistable. In
the case of holomorphic vector bundles over a compact Riemann surface,
it follows from a theorem of Narasimhan and Seshadri
[
11
]
that the
tensor product of semistable vector bundles is semistable. To every
filtered complex vector space, Faltings and Wüstholz
[
4
]

associated a holomorphic vector bundle over a compact Riemann
surface, and using the result of
[
11
]
showed that the tensor
product of semistable filtered complex vector spaces is semistable.
Totaro
[
13
]
introduced a certain kind of metric called

good metric
on a filtered vector space over the field of
complex numbers and showed that the polystability of a filtered
vector space is equivalent to the existence of a good metric on the
vector space, and using this he gave an alternate proof of
Faltings’ result.

The theory of quivers and their representations is important in the
study of representation theory. In order to classify finite
dimensional modules over a finite dimensional algebra, King
[
8
]

translated this problem into classifying the representations of a
certain quiver and constructed the moduli spaces in question. These
moduli spaces are closely related to the moduli spaces of vector
bundles (see for example,
[
6
]
).

In this paper, we recall the notion of internal product
Q\otimes Q^{\prime}

of two quivers
Q
and
Q^{\prime}
as defined by Li and Lin in
[
10
]
, and
by Keller in
[
7
]
, and then we define the tensor product

V\otimes W
of a representation
V
of
Q
with a representation

W
of
Q^{\prime}
. We then show that the semistability of
V
and
W

implies the semistability of
V\otimes W
over arbitrary fields. As
a consequence we can get the similar result for geometrically stable
representations. When the base field is
\mathbf{C}
we give another proof of
the semistability of
V\otimes W
. This alternate proof is similar
to the proof of semistability of tensor product of semistable filtered
vector spaces by Totaro. In our case, the role of the good metric is
played by certain kind of Hermitian metrics on the representations
which we call
Einstein-Hermitian metrics
. An
Einstein-Hermitian metric on a representation of a quiver is a
solution of a certain differential equation called a
quiver
vortex equation
. The relation between stability and the solutions of
such equations has been studied by Álvarez-Cónsul and García-Prada
in the general context of “twisted quiver bundles” in
[
1
]
.

A consequence of King’s construction of the moduli spaces of quiver
representations is that they are quasi projective varieties. In
particular, when the base field is that of the complex numbers, there
exists a positive Hermitian holomorphic line bundle on each such moduli
space. In
[
3
,
2
]
the authors have constructed such a natural
line bundle on the moduli spaces of stable and semistable
representations of quivers respectively. In this paper we give a
relation between such natural line bundles on the moduli spaces of

Q,Q^{\prime}
and
Q\otimes Q^{\prime}
when the base field is
\mathbf{C}
. Furthermore,
we also describe a relation between the universal families of
representations on the fine moduli spaces of representations of
Q,Q^{\prime}

and
Q\otimes Q^{\prime}
over arbitrary algebraically closed fields. Note
that, here we are associating to an object of the product category

\mathbf{Rep}_{k}(Q)\times\mathbf{Rep}_{k}(Q^{\prime})
an object of the category

\mathbf{Rep}_{k}(Q\otimes Q^{\prime})
, where
\mathbf{Rep}_{k}(Q)
denotes the category of
representations of the quiver
Q
over the field
k
.

The fixed loci of torus action on a quiver moduli space can be described
using the Białynicki-Birula decomposition and these connected
components can be realised as moduli spaces of covering quivers, see

[
14
,
5
]
. We compare the semi-stability under tensor product
construction with the (abelian) covering quiver construction and observe
that these two constructions do not commute in general. Any
representation
V
of a quiver lifts to a representation

\widetilde{V}
of the covering quiver and becomes (semi-) stable
w.r.t. a suitable slope function. As a consequence we get a map

\tau_{N}
(see Proposition
4.4
) from the moduli space for the
covering quiver
\tilde{Q}
to the moduli space for the original quiver

Q
. We also find some relations between stability conditions of

\widetilde{V}\otimes\widetilde{W}
and
\widetilde{(V\otimes W)}

where
V
and
W
are representations of the quivers
Q
and
Q^{\prime}

respectively. We further prove the descent of the natural line bundle
similar to the descent under the tensor product map
\overline{\phi}
. We
combine these results with the map given by the tensor product to get
a commutative diagram (see Corollary
4.7
) and then use it to
give a relation between the natural line bundles on the moduli spaces of
representations of covering quivers and their internal product.

In Section
1
, we recall the basics on quivers and their
representations, and describe the moduli space of representations of
a quiver as constructed by King and mention some of its properties.
In Section
2
, we define the tensor product of two
representations and show that the tensor product of semistables is
semistable. In Section
3
, we describe the relations between
the natural line bundles, and between the universal families of
representations on the fine moduli spaces of the quivers involved. In
Section
4
, we recall the notion of (universal) covering quiver
of a quiver with a fixed dimension vector. We give the relation between
the internal product of quivers with the internal product of (universal)
covering quivers. This is used to get the relation between stability of
tensor product of representations on the (universal) covering quiver.
We also deduce the description of the pull back of the natural line
bundle on the moduli of internal product of covering
quivers in terms of suitable powers of the natural line bundles on the
individual quiver moduli spaces.

1.
The moduli space of quiver representations

1.1.
Quivers and their representations

A
quiver
is a quadruple
Q=(Q_{0},Q_{1},\mathrm{s},\mathrm{t})
consisting of two
finite sets
Q_{0}\neq\emptyset
and
Q_{1}
called the set of vertices
and the set of arrows respectively, and two functions

\mathrm{s},\mathrm{t}:Q_{1}\to Q_{0}
called the source map and the target map
respectively.

Let
k
be any field.

A
representation of
Q
over
k
is a pair
(V,\rho)
, where

V=(V_{a})_{a\in Q_{0}}
is a family of finite-dimensional
k
-vector
spaces and
\rho=(\rho_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in Q_{1}}
is a family of

k
-linear maps

\rho_{\alpha}:V_{\mathrm{s}(\alpha)}\to V_{\mathrm{t}(\alpha)}.

Let
(V,\rho)
and
(W,\sigma)
be two representations of
Q
. A

morphism from
(V,\rho)
to
(W,\sigma)
is a family

f=(f_{a})_{a\in Q_{0}}
of
k
-linear maps
f_{a}:V_{a}\to W_{a}
satisfying

\sigma_{\alpha}\circ f_{\mathrm{s}(\alpha)}=f_{\mathrm{t}(\alpha)}\circ\rho_{\alpha}
for all
\alpha\in Q_{1}
. The morphism
f
is an

isomorphism
if each
f_{a}
is an isomorphism.

1.2.
Stability and family of representations

Let
(V,\rho)
be a representation of
Q
. The
type
or the

dimension vector
of
(V,\rho)
is the element

(\mathrm{dim}_{k}(V_{a}))_{a\in Q_{0}}
of
\mathbf{N}^{Q_{0}}
, denoted
\mathrm{dim}(V,\rho)
.
The
rank
of
(V,\rho)
is the natural number

\sum_{a\in Q_{0}}\mathrm{dim}_{k}(V_{a})
, denoted
\mathrm{rk}(V,\rho)
.
A
weight
of
Q
is an element
\theta
of the
\mathbf{R}
-vector
space
\mathbf{R}^{Q_{0}}
.
The
\theta
-degree
of
(V,\rho)
is the real number

\deg_{\theta}(V,\rho)=\sum_{a\in Q_{0}}\theta_{a}\mathrm{dim}_{k}(V_{a}).

If
(V,\rho)
is non-zero, the
\theta
-slope
of
(V,\rho)
is
the real number

\mu_{\theta}(V,\rho)=\frac{\deg_{\theta}(V,\rho)}{\mathrm{rk}(V,\rho)}.

More generally, for any
d\in\mathbf{N}^{Q_{0}}\setminus\{0\}
, we define

\mathrm{rk}(d)=\sum_{a\in Q_{0}}d_{a},\deg_{\theta}(d)=\sum_{a\in Q_{0}}\theta_{a}d_{a}

and
\mu_{\theta}(d)=\frac{\deg_{\theta}(d)}{\mathrm{rk}(d)}
.

A non-zero representation
(V,\rho)
is called

\theta
-semistable
(respectively,
\theta
-stable
) if

\mu_{\theta}(W,\sigma)\leq\mu_{\theta}(V,\rho)\quad(\text{respectively,}~\mu_{\theta}(W,\sigma)<\mu_{\theta}(V,\rho))

for every non-zero proper subrepresentation
(W,\sigma)
of
(V,\rho)
.
The representation
(V,\rho)
is called
\theta
-polystable
if
it is
\theta
-semistable, and if it is a direct sum of a finite family
of
\theta
-stable representations of
Q
each having slope same as that
of
(V,\rho)
.

Let
d=(d_{a})_{a\in Q_{0}}
be an element of
\mathbf{N}^{Q_{0}}
such that

d_{a}>0
for all
a\in Q_{0}
. A
family
of
\theta
-semistable
representations of type
d
parametrised by a
k
-scheme
T
is a pair

(V,\rho)
, where
V=(V_{a})_{a\in Q_{0}}
is a family of locally free

\mathcal{O}_{T}
-modules,
\rho=(\rho_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in Q_{1}}
is a family
of morphisms
\rho_{\alpha}:V_{\mathrm{s}(\alpha)}\to V_{\mathrm{t}(\alpha)}
of

\mathcal{O}_{T}
-modules and for every closed point
t
of
T
, the
representation
(V(t),\rho(t))
of
Q
over
k
is
\theta
-semistable
and has type
d
.

We have an obvious notion of
morphism
between two families
parametrised by
T
which generalises the morphism of representations
of a quiver as defined earlier. Two families
(V,\rho)
and

(W,\sigma)
parametrised by
T
are called
equivalent
if there
is an invertible
\mathcal{O}_{T}
-module
L
such that

(L\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{T}}V,\mathbf{1}_{L}\otimes\rho)
is isomorphic to

(W,\sigma)
.

1.3.
The moduli of
\theta
-semistable representations of type
d

Let
\theta
be a rational weight of
Q
and
d\in\mathbf{N}^{Q_{0}}
be fixed,
and let
k
be an algebraically closed field. The

moduli functor of type
(\theta,d)
of
Q
is the presheaf

F(\theta,d)
on the category of
k
-schemes, which is defined by

\displaystyle F(\theta,d)(T)=\;

set of equivalence classes of families of
\theta
-semistable

\displaystyle\text{representations of type $d$ parametrised by $T$}.

Proposition 1.1
.

[
8
, Proposition 5.2]

There exists a connected normal quasi-projective
k
-scheme

M(\theta,d)
, which universally corepresents the functor

F(\theta,d)
.

The moduli space
M(\theta,d)
has the following properties.

(1)

The moduli space
M(\theta,d)
is projective if and only if
Q

is acyclic.

(2)

There is a canonical bijection from the set of S-equivalence
classes of
\theta
-semistable representations of
Q
, to the set

N(\theta,d)
of closed points of
M(\theta,d)
, where we say that two
representations are S-equivalent if they have filtrations with stable
quotients and the same associated graded representations.

(3)

There is an open subset
M^{\mathrm{s}}(\theta,d)
of
M(\theta,d)

such that the set

N^{\mathrm{s}}(\theta,d)=N(\theta,d)\cap M^{\mathrm{s}}(\theta,d)

of its closed points is the image of the set of isomorphism classes of

\theta
-stable representations of type
d
of
Q
, under the above
bijection.

(4)

If
d
is coprime, that is,

\gcd(d_{a}\,|\,a\in Q_{0})=1
,
then the moduli space
M^{\mathrm{s}}(\theta,d)
of
\theta
-stable
representations is a fine moduli space. Therefore, there exists a
universal family of representations
(U,\psi)
parametrised by

M^{\mathrm{s}}(\theta,d)
(see
[
8
, Proposition 5.3]
).

(5)

If
d
is
\theta
-coprime, that is,
\mu_{\theta}(d^{\prime})\neq\mu_{\theta}(d)
for all
0<d^{\prime}<d
, then
M(\theta,d)=M^{\mathrm{s}}(\theta,d)
.

(6)

If
d
is coprime, then there exists a dense open subset of

\mathbf{R}^{Q_{0}}
such that for any weight
\theta
in that subset
d
is also

\theta
-coprime.

1.4.
A description of the moduli space

Fix a family
V=(V_{a})_{a\in Q_{0}}
of
k
-vector spaces, such that

\mathrm{dim}_{k}(V_{a})=d_{a}
for all
a\in Q_{0}
. Let
\mathcal{A}
denote the

representation space
of type
d
of
Q
, that is, the
finite-dimensional
k
-vector space

\bigoplus_{\alpha\in Q_{1}}\mathrm{Hom}_{k}(V_{\mathrm{s}(\alpha)},V_{\mathrm{t}(\alpha)})
.
We give the vector space
\mathcal{A}
its usual structure of a variety.

Let
G
denote the algebraic group
\prod_{a\in Q_{0}}\mathrm{Aut}_{k}(V_{a})
.
There is a canonical algebraic right action of
G
on
\mathcal{A}
,
which is defined by

(\rho g)_{\alpha}=g_{\mathrm{t}(\alpha)}^{-1}\circ\rho_{\alpha}\circ g_{\mathrm{s}(\alpha)}

for all
\rho\in\mathcal{A}
,
g\in G
, and
\alpha\in Q_{1}
. The
stabiliser of any point
\rho
of
\mathcal{A}
is canonically
identified with the automorphism group of the representation
(V,\rho)

of
Q
. The orbits of this action is in bijection with the set of
isomorphism classes of representations of
Q
of type
d
.

Denote by
\Delta
the central algebraic subgroup of
G

consisting of all elements of the form
ce
, as
c
runs over

k^{\times}
, where
e=(\mathbf{1}_{V_{a}})_{a\in Q_{0}}
is the identity element
of
G
. Let
\overline{G}
denote the algebraic group

\Delta\backslash G
, and
\pi:G\to\overline{G}
the canonical
projection. Then, the action of
G
on
\mathcal{A}
induces an
algebraic right action of
\overline{G}
on
\mathcal{A}
.

Let
\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{ss}}
(respectively,
\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{s}}
) denote
the set of points
\rho
in
\mathcal{A}
, such that the
representation
(V,\rho)
of
Q
is
\theta
-semistable (respectively,

\theta
-stable). There is an obvious family of
\theta
-semistable
representations of type
d
of
Q
parametrised by

\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{ss}}
. Therefore, the universal property of

M(\theta,d)
gives a morphism of varieties

p^{\mathrm{ss}}:\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{ss}}\to M(\theta,d)
.

Proposition 1.2

(
[
8
, Proposition 5.2]
)
.

The morphism
p^{\mathrm{ss}}
is a
good quotient of
\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{ss}}
by
\overline{G}
, and its
restriction
p^{\mathrm{s}}:\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{s}}\to M^{\mathrm{s}}(\theta,d)
is
a geometric quotient of
\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{s}}
by
\overline{G}
.

1.5.
Moduli space over the field of complex numbers

Let
k=\mathbf{C}
. We then have an equivalent
statement for the polystability which we describe now. A

Hermitian metric
on a representation
(V,\rho)
of
Q
is a
family
h=(h_{a})_{a\in Q_{0}}
of Hermitian inner products

h_{a}:V_{a}\times V_{a}\to\mathbf{C}
.
We say that the metric
h
is
Einstein-Hermitian
with respect
to
\theta
if it satisfies

\sum_{\alpha\in\mathrm{t}^{-1}(a)}\rho_{\alpha}\circ\rho_{\alpha}^{*}-\sum_{\alpha\in\mathrm{s}^{-1}(a)}\rho_{\alpha}^{*}\circ\rho_{\alpha}=(\mu_{\theta}(V,\rho)-\theta_{a})\;\mathbf{1}_{V_{a}}

for all
a\in Q_{0}
, where, for each
\alpha\in Q_{1}
,

\rho_{\alpha}^{*}:V_{\mathrm{t}(\alpha)}\to V_{\mathrm{s}(\alpha)}
is the adjoint
of
\rho_{\alpha}:V_{\mathrm{s}(\alpha)}\to V_{\mathrm{t}(\alpha)}
with respect to
the Hermitian inner products
h_{\mathrm{s}(\alpha)}
and
h_{\mathrm{t}(\alpha)}

on
V_{\mathrm{s}(\alpha)}
and
V_{\mathrm{t}(\alpha)}
, respectively.

We then have the following proposition which is essentially a
consequence of
[
8
, Proposition 6.5]
.

Proposition 1.3
.

[
3
, Proposition 4.3]

Let
\theta
be a rational weight of
Q
, and
(V,\rho)
a non-zero
representation of
Q
. Then,
(V,\rho)
has an Einstein-Hermitian
metric with respect to
\theta
if and only if it is

\theta
-polystable. Moreover, if
h_{1}
and
h_{2}
are two
Einstein-Hermitian metrics on
(V,\rho)
with respect to
\theta
,
then there exists an automorphism
f
of
(V,\rho)
, such that

h_{1,a}(v,w)=h_{2,a}(f_{a}(v),f_{a}(w))

for all
a\in Q_{0}
and
v,w\in V_{a}
.

Moreover, in addition to the properties (1)–(6) as stated in Section

1.3
, the moduli spaces of quiver representations have the
following extra properties.

(7)

The moduli space
N(\theta,d)
of semistable representations
is a connected, normal and quasi-projective complex analytic space, and

N^{\mathrm{s}}(\theta,d)
is a dense open subspace in
N(\theta,d)
and has
a natural structure of a symplectic manifold (see

[
8
, Section 6]
).

(8)

There exist a natural Kähler metric
g^{\mathrm{s}}
, and a
natural Hermitian holomorphic line bundle

(L^{\mathrm{s}},h^{\mathrm{s}})
on
N^{\mathrm{s}}(\theta,d)
such that

c_{1}(L^{\mathrm{s}},h^{\mathrm{s}})=\frac{n}{2\pi}\Theta^{\mathrm{s}}
, where

n
is a positive integer such that
n(\theta_{a}-\mu_{\theta}(d))\in\mathbf{Z}

for all
a\in Q_{0}
, and
\Theta^{\mathrm{s}}
is the Kähler form of

g^{\mathrm{s}}
(see
[
3
, Theorem 7.8, 8.3]
).

(9)

There exist a natural holomorphic line bundle
L
on

N(\theta,d)
, a natural continuous Hermitian metric
h
on
L
and
a natural Kähler stratification (see
[
2
, Section 4c]
for
definition) of
N(\theta,d)
such that for each stratum
Z
, the
Hermitian metric
h_{Z}=h|_{Z}
on
L_{Z}=L|_{Z}
is
smooth and
c_{1}(L_{Z},h_{Z})=\frac{n}{2\pi}\Theta_{Z}
, where
\Theta_{Z}

is the Kähler form on
Z
(see
[
2
, Theorem 5.3, 5.4]
).

Remark 1.4
.

By a theorem of King
[
8
, Theorem 6.1, 6.5]
we also have that a
point
\rho\in\mathcal{A}
belongs to
\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{ss}}
if and
only if the closure, in the strong topology of
\mathcal{A}
, of the

G
-orbit
\rho G
contains a point
\rho^{\prime}\in\mathcal{A}
such that
the representation
(V,\rho^{\prime})
is polystable.

2.
Tensor products of representations and their semistability

Definition 2.1
.

The internal product of
Q
and
Q^{\prime}

\displaystyle\mathrm{s}_{Q\otimes Q^{\prime}}((\alpha,a^{\prime}))

\displaystyle=(\mathrm{s}_{Q}(\alpha),a^{\prime}),\quad\mathrm{t}_{Q\otimes Q^{\prime}}((\alpha,a^{\prime}))

\displaystyle=(\mathrm{t}_{Q}(\alpha),a^{\prime})\quad

Remark 2.3
.

(1)

The above product of quivers was defined by Li and Lin in

[
10
, Section 3.2]
where they called this product the “product
valued quiver”, and by Keller in
[
7
, Section 3.3]
where he
called this product the “tensor product” of the quivers.

(2)

For each vertex
a\in Q_{0}
, the full-subquiver on the vertex set

\{a\}\times Q_{0}^{\prime}
is isomorphic to the quiver
Q^{\prime}
, and for each
vertex
a^{\prime}\in Q_{0}^{\prime}
, the full-subquiver on the vertex set

Q_{0}\times\{a^{\prime}\}
is isomorphic to the quiver
Q
.

2.1.
Semistability of tensor product of representations over arbitrary fields

Let
k
be any field.

Let
\theta=(\theta_{a})_{a\in Q_{0}}
and

\theta^{\prime}=(\theta^{\prime}_{a^{\prime}})_{a^{\prime}\in Q_{0}^{\prime}}
be rational weights of
Q
and

Q^{\prime}
respectively. Let
\overline{\theta}=(\theta_{a}+\theta^{\prime}_{a^{\prime}})_{(a,a^{\prime})\in Q_{0}\times Q_{0}^{\prime}}
.

Let
(V,\rho)=\left((V_{a})_{a\in Q_{0}},(\rho_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in Q_{1}}\right)
and

(W,\sigma)=\left((W_{a^{\prime}})_{a^{\prime}\in Q_{0}^{\prime}},(\sigma_{\alpha^{\prime}})_{\alpha^{\prime}\in Q_{1}^{\prime}}\right)
be representations of
Q

and
Q^{\prime}
over
k
respectively.

Definition 2.4
.

The tensor product of
(V,\rho)
and
(W,\sigma)
is the
representation

(V,\rho)\otimes(W,\sigma)=((V_{a}\otimes W_{a^{\prime}})_{(a,a^{\prime})\in Q_{0}\times Q_{0}^{\prime}},(\tau_{\gamma})_{\gamma\in(Q\otimes Q^{\prime})_{1}}))

of the quiver
Q\otimes Q^{\prime}
, where

\tau_{\gamma}=\begin{cases}\mathbf{1}_{V_{a}}\otimes\sigma_{\alpha^{\prime}}\quad\mbox{~for}\quad\gamma=(a,\alpha^{\prime})\in Q_{0}\times Q_{1}^{\prime}\\
\rho_{\alpha}\otimes\mathbf{1}_{W_{a^{\prime}}}\quad\mbox{for}\quad\gamma=(\alpha,a^{\prime})\in Q_{1}\times Q_{0}^{\prime}.\end{cases}

Let
\mathrm{dim}(V,\rho)=d=(d_{a})_{a\in Q_{0}}
, and

\mathrm{dim}(W,\sigma)=d^{\prime}=(d_{a^{\prime}}^{\prime})_{a^{\prime}\in Q_{0}^{\prime}}
. Then we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{dim}((V,\rho)\otimes(W,\sigma))=\overline{d}=(d_{a}\,d_{a^{\prime}}^{\prime})_{(a,a^{\prime})\in Q_{0}\times Q_{0}^{\prime}},

\displaystyle\mathrm{rk}((V,\rho)\otimes(W,\sigma))=\sum_{a\in Q_{0},a^{\prime}\in Q_{0}^{\prime}}d_{a}\,d^{\prime}_{a^{\prime}}=\mathrm{rk}(V,\rho)\,\mathrm{rk}(W,\sigma),

\displaystyle\deg_{\overline{\theta}}{((V,\rho)\otimes(W,\sigma))}=\deg_{\theta}{(V,\rho)}\,\mathrm{rk}(W,\sigma)+\deg_{\theta^{\prime}}{(W,\sigma)}\,\mathrm{rk}(V,\rho),

and therefore,

\mu_{\overline{\theta}}((V,\rho)\otimes(W,\sigma))=\mu_{\theta}(V,\rho)+\mu_{\theta^{\prime}}(W,\sigma).

Proposition 2.5
.

Suppose that the representation
(V,\rho)
of
Q
is

\theta
-semistable and the representation
(W,\sigma)
of
Q^{\prime}
is

\theta^{\prime}
-semistable. Then the representation

(U,\tau)=(V,\rho)\otimes(W,\sigma)
of
Q\otimes Q^{\prime}
is

\overline{\theta}
-semistable.

Proof.

Let
(V,\rho)
be a
\theta
-semistable representation of the quiver

Q
and
(W,\sigma)
be a
\theta^{\prime}
-semistable representation of the
quiver
Q^{\prime}
. Suppose
(V,\rho)\otimes(W,\sigma)
is not

\overline{\theta}
-semistable. Then we get a quotient

(V,\rho)\otimes(W,\sigma)\rightarrow(U,\tau)
such that

\mu_{\overline{\theta}}((V,\rho)\otimes(W,\sigma))>\mu_{\overline{\theta}}(U,\tau)
.

By multiplying
\theta
and
\theta^{\prime}
by sufficiently large integer

d
we can assume that

\mu_{\overline{\theta}}((V,\rho)\otimes(W,\sigma))-\mu_{\overline{\theta}}(U,\tau)=\mu_{\theta}(V,\rho)+\mu_{\theta^{\prime}}(W,\sigma)-\mu_{\overline{\theta}}(U,\tau)>2,

as scaling does not change the semistability, see

[
12
, Section 5.1]
.

Now we can find the integers
m
and
n
such that

\mu_{\theta}(V,\rho)+m>0,~\mu_{\theta^{\prime}}(W,\sigma)+n>0\mbox{ and }m+n+\mu_{\overline{\theta}}(U,\tau)\leq 0.

If we replace the weight
\theta
by
\theta_{1}:=(\theta_{a}+m)_{a\in Q_{0}}
and
\theta^{\prime}
by
\theta_{1}^{\prime}:=(\theta_{a^{\prime}}+n)_{a^{\prime}\in Q_{0}^{\prime}}
, then the semistability does not
change following
[
12
, Section 5.1]
. Hence
(V,\rho)

(respectively
(W,\sigma)
) is
\theta_{1}
-semistable (respectively

\theta_{1}^{\prime}
-semistable) such that

\mu_{\theta_{1}}(V,\rho)>0,~\mu_{\theta_{1}^{\prime}}(W,\sigma)>0\mbox{ and }\mu_{\overline{\theta_{1}}}(U,\tau)\leq 0

are satisfied. In particular

\deg_{\theta_{1}}(V,\rho)>0,~\deg_{\theta_{1}^{\prime}}(W,\sigma)>0\mbox{ and }\deg_{\overline{\theta_{1}}}(U,\tau)\leq 0

holds by multiplying the ranks.

Now using the lemma
2.6
we get that

\deg_{\overline{\theta_{1}}}(U,\tau)>0
which is a contradiction.
Hence
(V,\rho)\otimes(W,\sigma)
is
\overline{\theta}
-semistable.
∎

Lemma 2.6
.

(i)

A direct sum of semistable representations of
positive degree of same slope is again a semistable
representation of positive degree.

(ii)

Any quotient of positive degree semistable
representation is again a representation of positive degree.

(iii)

For each vertex
(a,a^{\prime})\in Q_{0}\times Q_{0}^{\prime}
,
there exist restriction functors

R_{a,Q^{\prime}}:\mathbf{Rep}_{k}(Q\otimes Q^{\prime})\rightarrow\mathbf{Rep}_{k}(Q^{\prime});(U,\tau)\mapsto((U_{(a,a^{\prime})})_{a^{\prime}\in Q_{0}^{\prime}},(\tau_{(a,\alpha^{\prime})})_{\alpha^{\prime}\in Q_{1}^{\prime}})

\mbox{ and }R_{Q,a^{\prime}}:\mathbf{Rep}_{k}(Q\otimes Q^{\prime})\rightarrow\mathbf{Rep}_{k}(Q);(U,\tau)\mapsto((U_{(a,a^{\prime})})_{a\in Q_{0}},(\tau_{(\alpha,a^{\prime})})_{\alpha\in Q_{1}})

which are exact functors of abelian categories.

(iv)

For each vertices
a\in Q_{0}
, the restriction

R_{a,Q^{\prime}}((V,\rho)\otimes(W,\sigma))=((V_{a}\otimes W_{a^{\prime}})_{a^{\prime}\in Q_{0}^{\prime}},(\mathbf{1}_{V_{a}}\otimes\sigma_{\alpha^{\prime}})_{\alpha^{\prime}\in Q^{\prime}_{1}})\simeq(W,\sigma)^{\oplus d_{a}}
. Similarly, we have

R_{Q,a^{\prime}}((V,\rho)\otimes(W,\sigma))\simeq(V,\rho)^{\oplus d^{\prime}_{a^{\prime}}}
.

(v)

If
(U,\tau)
is a quotient of

(V,\rho)\otimes(W,\sigma)
which are semistable
representations of positive degree then

\deg_{\overline{\theta_{1}}}(U,\tau)>0
.

Proof.

The proof of the first four assertions easily follows from the
definition. Now to prove the last assertion we observe that

\displaystyle\deg_{\overline{\theta_{1}}}(U,\tau)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{(a,a^{\prime})\in Q_{0}\times Q_{0}^{\prime}}(\theta_{1,a}+\theta^{\prime}_{1,a^{\prime}})\mathrm{dim}(U_{(a,a^{\prime})})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{a^{\prime}\in Q_{0}^{\prime}}\Big[\sum_{a\in Q_{0}}(\theta_{1,a}\mathrm{dim}(U_{(a,a^{\prime})})\Big]+\sum_{a\in Q_{0}}\Big[\sum_{a^{\prime}\in Q_{0}^{\prime}}\theta^{\prime}_{1,a^{\prime}})\mathrm{dim}(U_{(a,a^{\prime})})\Big]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{a^{\prime}\in Q_{0}^{\prime}}[\deg_{\theta_{1}}(R_{Q,a^{\prime}}(U,\tau))]+\sum_{a\in Q_{0}}[\deg_{\theta_{1}^{\prime}}(R_{a,Q^{\prime}}(U,\tau))]

\displaystyle>

\displaystyle 0~(\mbox{ using }(i),(ii)\mbox{ and }(iv)).\hskip 184.9429pt\qed

Recall that a representation
(V,\rho)
of a quiver
Q
over a field

k
is said to be
\theta
-geometrically stable
if for any
field extension
k^{\prime}
of
k
, the representation

(V,\rho)\otimes k^{\prime}=((V_{a}\otimes_{k}k^{\prime})_{a\in Q_{0}},(\rho_{\alpha}\otimes_{k}\mathbf{1}_{k^{\prime}})_{\alpha\in Q_{1}})
is
\theta
-stable (see

[
6
, Definition 2.19]
). Since for any two
k
-vector spaces

V
and
W
we have

(V\otimes_{k}W)\otimes_{k}k^{\prime}\cong(V\otimes_{k}k^{\prime}\otimes_{k^{\prime}}W)\otimes_{k}k^{\prime}\cong(V\otimes_{k}k^{\prime})\otimes_{k^{\prime}}(W\otimes_{k}k^{\prime}),

we get the following corollary to Proposition
2.5
.

Corollary 2.7
.

If the representation
(V,\rho)
of
Q
is
\theta
-geometrically
stable and the representation
(W,\sigma)
of
Q^{\prime}
is

\theta^{\prime}
-geometrically stable with types
d
being

\theta
-coprime,
d^{\prime}
being
\theta^{\prime}
-coprime and
dd^{\prime}
being

\overline{\theta}
-coprime, then the representation

(V,\rho)\otimes(W,\sigma)
of
Q\otimes Q^{\prime}
is

\overline{\theta}
-geometrically stable.

2.2.
Semistability of tensor product of representations over the field of complex numbers

Assuming
k=\mathbf{C}
we give another proof of Proposition
2.5
. To this end, we first establish that the tensor product of polystables
is polystable.

Lemma 2.8
.

Suppose that the representation
(V,\rho)
of
Q
is

\theta
-polystable and the representation
(W,\sigma)
of
Q^{\prime}
is

\theta^{\prime}
-polystable. Then, the representation
(V,\rho)\otimes(W,\sigma)
of
Q\otimes Q^{\prime}
is
\overline{\theta}
-polystable.

Proof.

For a quiver
Q
and a representation
(V,\rho)
of
Q
, let us
write

X^{Q}_{a}(V,\rho)=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathrm{s}^{-1}(a)}\rho_{\alpha}^{*}\circ\rho_{\alpha}\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad Y^{Q}_{a}(V,\rho)=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathrm{t}^{-1}(a)}\rho_{\alpha}\circ\rho_{\alpha}^{*}.

Then by Proposition
1.3

we have

\displaystyle Y^{Q}_{a}(V,\rho)-X^{Q}_{a}(V,\rho)

\displaystyle=(\mu_{\theta}(V,\rho)-\theta_{a})\mathbf{1}_{V_{a}}

\displaystyle(a\in Q_{0})

\displaystyle Y^{Q^{\prime}}_{a^{\prime}}(W,\sigma)-X^{Q^{\prime}}_{a^{\prime}}(W,\sigma)

\displaystyle=(\mu_{\theta^{\prime}}(W,\sigma)-\theta^{\prime}_{a^{\prime}})\mathbf{1}_{W_{a^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle(a^{\prime}\in Q_{0}^{\prime}),

with respect to some Hermitian metrics
h
on
(V,\rho)
and
h^{\prime}
on

(W,\sigma)
. Note that,
\overline{\mathrm{s}}^{-1}(a,a^{\prime})=\{a\}\times\mathrm{s}^{\prime-1}(a^{\prime})\sqcup\mathrm{s}^{-1}(a)\times\{a^{\prime}\}
.
Therefore,

\displaystyle X^{Q\otimes Q^{\prime}}_{(a,a^{\prime})}((V,\rho)\otimes(W,\sigma))

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha^{\prime}\in\mathrm{s}^{\prime-1}(a^{\prime})}(\mathbf{1}_{V_{a}}\otimes\sigma_{\alpha^{\prime}})^{*}\circ(\mathbf{1}_{V_{a}}\otimes\sigma_{\alpha^{\prime}})+\sum_{\alpha\in\mathrm{s}^{-1}(a)}(\rho_{\alpha}\otimes\mathbf{1}_{W_{a^{\prime}}})^{*}\circ(\rho_{\alpha}\otimes\mathbf{1}_{W_{a^{\prime}}})

\displaystyle=\mathbf{1}_{V_{a}}\otimes(\sum_{\alpha^{\prime}\in\mathrm{s}^{\prime-1}(a^{\prime})}\sigma_{\alpha^{\prime}}^{*}\circ\sigma_{\alpha^{\prime}})+(\sum_{\alpha\in\mathrm{s}^{-1}(a)}\rho_{\alpha}^{*}\circ\rho_{\alpha})\otimes\mathbf{1}_{W_{a^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{1}_{V_{a}}\otimes X^{Q^{\prime}}_{a^{\prime}}(W,\sigma)+X^{Q}_{a}(V,\rho)\otimes\mathbf{1}_{W_{a^{\prime}}}.

Similarly,

Y^{Q\otimes Q^{\prime}}_{(a,a^{\prime})}((V,\rho)\otimes(W,\sigma))=\mathbf{1}_{V_{a}}\otimes Y^{Q^{\prime}}_{a^{\prime}}(W,\sigma)+Y^{Q}_{a}(V,\rho)\otimes\mathbf{1}_{W_{a^{\prime}}}.

Therefore, with respect to the Hermitian inner product

h_{a}\otimes h_{a^{\prime}}^{\prime}
on
V_{a}\otimes W_{a^{\prime}}
we have

\displaystyle Y^{Q\otimes Q^{\prime}}_{(a,a^{\prime})}((V,\rho)\otimes(W,\sigma))-X^{Q\otimes Q^{\prime}}_{(a,a^{\prime})}((V,\rho)\otimes(W,\sigma))

be the stable locus in the
representation space

\tilde{\mathcal{A}}=\mathcal{A}(\tilde{Q},\bf{1})
and

T=\Delta^{\prime}\backslash G(\tilde{Q},\bf{1})=\Delta^{\prime}\backslash(\prod_{(a,\mu)}\mathbb{G}_{m})

be the corresponding group, where
\Delta^{\prime}
is the one dimensional
subgroup
\{(\lambda)_{(a,\mu)}\,|\,\lambda\in k^{\times}\}
.
Then comparing the GIT quotients we get the following relation between
the moduli spaces.

Proposition 4.4
.

There is a surjective map

\tau_{N}:\tilde{N}\to N\,;\,[\tilde{V}]\mapsto[V]

satisfying

p\circ\varphi=\tau_{N}\circ\tilde{p},

where
\tilde{p}:\tilde{\mathcal{A}}^{\mathrm{s}}\to\tilde{N}
is the GIT
quotient by the group
T
and
\varphi
is the identification of

\tilde{\mathcal{A}}^{\mathrm{s}}
and
\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{s}}
.

Let
Q=(Q_{0},Q_{1},s,t)
be a quiver, and
Q^{\prime}
be a subquiver of
Q

with
Q_{0}^{\prime}=Q_{0}
, that is,
Q
and
Q^{\prime}
have the same set of vertices.
Let
d\in\mathbf{N}^{Q_{0}}
be a fixed dimension vector. For each
a\in Q_{0}

we also fix a vector space of dimension

. Let

be the representation spaces of
Q
and
Q^{\prime}
respectively of type

.

defined by

i_{d}(\sigma)_{\alpha}=\begin{cases}\sigma_{\alpha}\quad\mbox{if}\quad\alpha\in Q_{1}^{\prime}\\
0\quad\mbox{otherwise}\end{cases}

and

\pi_{d}(\rho)_{\alpha}=\rho_{\alpha}\quad\mbox{for all}\quad\alpha\in Q_{1}^{\prime}.

(3)

\pi_{d}\circ i_{d}=\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{A}^{\prime}}.

\rho\in R
, the pair
(V,\rho)
is a representation of
Q

of type
d
and
(V,\pi_{d}(\rho))

Q^{\prime}
of type

d
. Similarly, given any
\tau\in R^{\prime}
, the pair
(V,\tau)
is a
representation of
Q^{\prime}
of type
d
and
(V,i_{d}(\tau))
is a
representation of
Q
of type
d
.

Lemma 4.5
.

(1)

A representation
(V,\rho)
of
Q
of type
d
is

\theta
-semistable if the representation
(V,\pi_{d}(\rho))
of
Q^{\prime}
is

\theta
-semistable.

(2)

A representation
(V,\tau)
of
Q
of type
d
is

\theta
-semistable if and only if the representation
(V,i_{d}(\tau))

of
Q^{\prime}
is
\theta
-semistable.

Proof.

We note that if
(W,\sigma)
is a subrepresentation of
(V,\rho)
of
type
d^{\prime}
, then the representation
(W,\pi_{d^{\prime}}(\sigma))
of
Q^{\prime}
is
a subrepresentation of
(V,\pi_{d}(\rho))
of type
d^{\prime}
. Thus
(
1
) follows.

Similarly, if
(W,\kappa)
is a subrepresentation of
(V,\tau)
of
type
d^{\prime}
then
(W,i_{d^{\prime}}(\kappa))
is a subrepresentation of

(V,i_{d}(\tau))
of type
d^{\prime}
. Therefore it follows that

(4)

(V,i_{d}(\tau))\quad{\rm is~}\theta-{\rm semistable}\Rightarrow(V,\tau)\quad{\rm is~}\theta-{\rm semistable}.

We also have

(V,\tau)=(V,\pi_{d}\circ i_{d}(\tau)).

Therefore by (
1
),

(5)

(V,\tau)\quad{\rm is~}\theta-{\rm semistable}\Rightarrow(V,i_{d}(\tau))\quad{\rm is~}\theta-{\rm semistable}.

This proves (
2
).
∎

If

\mathbf{U}(\widetilde{V\otimes W})

4.1.
Relation between natural line bundles on the moduli of covering quivers over
\mathbf{C}

Let
k=\mathbf{C}
. Let
\tilde{F_{i}}

(i=1,2,3)
denote the natural line
bundle on the moduli space
\tilde{N_{i}}
, and

\tau_{i}:=\tau_{N_{i}}:\tilde{N_{i}}\to N_{i}
be the morphism as in
Proposition
4.4
.
Let
u_{i}:T_{i}\to\overline{G_{i}}
be the corresponding group homomorphism.
Then it can be seen that

\varphi_{i}(\tilde{\rho_{i}}\cdot\tilde{g_{i}})=\varphi_{i}(\tilde{\rho_{i}})\cdot u_{i}(\tilde{g_{i}})

for all
\tilde{\rho_{i}}\in\tilde{\mathcal{A}_{i}}^{\mathrm{s}}
and

\tilde{g_{i}}\in T_{i}
. Moreover the
T_{i}
-bundle

\tilde{\mathcal{A}_{i}}^{\mathrm{s}}\times\mathbf{C}
and the pullback bundle

\varphi_{i}^{*}(\mathcal{A}_{i}^{\mathrm{s}}\times\mathbf{C})
are isomorphic as

T_{i}
-bundles because the characters do not change. Hence by the
discussion in the beginning of the Section
3
, we get the
following.

Proposition 4.8
.

The line bundles
\tilde{F}_{i}
and
\tau_{i}^{*}F_{i}
are isomorphic.

Corollary 4.9
.

The line bundles
\tilde{\phi}^{*}\tilde{F_{3}}
and

\widetilde{\mathrm{pr}_{1}}^{*}(\tilde{F_{1}}^{\otimes n^{\prime}\,\mathrm{rk}(d^{\prime})})\otimes\widetilde{\mathrm{pr}_{2}}^{*}(\tilde{F_{2}}^{\otimes n\,\mathrm{rk}(d)})
on
\tilde{N_{1}}\times\tilde{N_{2}}
are isomorphic, where

\widetilde{\mathrm{pr}_{j}}:\tilde{N_{1}}\times\tilde{N_{2}}\to\tilde{N_{j}}

(
j=1,2
) is the projection map.

Proof.

We have

\mathrm{pr}_{i}\circ(\tau_{1}\times\tau_{2})=\tau_{i}\circ\widetilde{\mathrm{pr}_{i}}

for
i=1,2
. Therefore

(\tau_{1}\times\tau_{2})^{*}(\mathrm{pr}_{i}^{*}F_{i})=(\mathrm{pr}_{i}\circ(\tau_{1}\times\tau_{2}))^{*}F_{i}=(\tau_{i}\circ\widetilde{\mathrm{pr}_{i}})^{*}F_{i}=\widetilde{\mathrm{pr}_{i}}^{*}(\tau_{i}^{*}F_{i})=\widetilde{\mathrm{pr}_{i}}^{*}\tilde{F_{i}}.

Taking tensor products, and using Proposition
3.3
and Corollary
4.7
we get

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathrm{pr}_{1}}^{*}(\tilde{F_{1}}^{\otimes n^{\prime}\,\mathrm{rk}(d^{\prime})})\otimes\widetilde{\mathrm{pr}_{2}}^{*}(\tilde{F_{2}}^{\otimes n\,\mathrm{rk}(d)})

\displaystyle=(\tau_{1}\times\tau_{2})^{*}(\mathrm{pr}_{1}^{*}F_{1}^{\otimes n^{\prime}\,\mathrm{rk}(d^{\prime})}\otimes\mathrm{pr}_{2}^{*}F_{2}^{\otimes n\,\mathrm{rk}(d)})

\displaystyle=(\tau_{1}\times\tau_{2})^{*}\overline{\phi}^{*}F_{3}=(\tau_{3}\circ\tilde{\phi})^{*}F_{3}=\tilde{\phi}^{*}\tau_{3}^{*}F_{3}=\tilde{\phi}^{*}\tilde{F_{3}.}\qed

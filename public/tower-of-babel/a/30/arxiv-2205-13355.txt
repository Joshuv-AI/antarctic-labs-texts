Single-pass Nyström approximation in mixed precision
Erin Carson, Ieva Daužickaitė

Abstract
Low rank matrix approximations appear in a number of scientific computing applications. We consider the Nyström method for approximating a positive semidefinite matrix A A . In the case that A A is very large or its entries can only be accessed once, a single-pass version may be necessary. In this work, we perform a complete rounding error analysis of the single-pass Nyström method in two precisions, where the computation of the expensive matrix product with A A is assumed to be performed in the lower of the two precisions. Our analysis gives insight into how the sketching matrix and shift should be chosen to ensure stability, implementation aspects which have been commented on in the literature but not yet rigorously justified. We further develop a heuristic to determine how to pick the lower precision, which confirms the general intuition that the lower the desired rank of the approximation, the lower the precision we can use without detriment. We also demonstrate that our mixed precision Nyström method can be used to inexpensively construct limited memory preconditioners for the conjugate gradient method and derive a bound the condition number of the resulting preconditioned coefficient matrix. We present numerical experiments on a set of matrices with various spectral decays and demonstrate the utility of our mixed precision approach.

Given that in our particular setting, the matrix-matrix products with
A
are overwhelmingly the dominant cost, we thus seek to further reduce this cost through the use of low precision. We develop a mixed precision single-pass Nyström approach in which storage of and computation with
A
is performed at a precision lower than the working precision.

The natural question to ask is how does using the lower precision affect the quality of the approximation. We prove a bound on the error of
\|A-\widehat{A}_{N}\|_{F}
, where
\widehat{A}_{N}
is the approximation computed by the mixed precision algorithm. Intuitively, this can be bounded in terms of the deviation of the exact (infinite precision) Nyström approximation
A_{N}
from
A
and the deviation of
\widehat{A}_{N}
from
A_{N}
. These bounds are a large overestimate, but allow us to develop a practical heuristic to determine how low a precision can safely be used so that the error of the exact Nyström approximation remains dominant. Numerical experiments show that the heuristic is useful for a range of problems with various spectral decay curves.

We also consider the impact of the low-precision computations in preconditioning. Our focus is on limited memory preconditioners that can be constructed using the Nyström approximation and have been used in
[
10
,
14
]
. This is an area that can particularly benefit from using the single pass mixed precision approach by reducing the cost of constructing the preconditioner. We extend the bounds on the condition number of the preconditioned system given in
[
14
]
to account for finite precision error.

In summary, our contributions are as follows. We provide a full finite precision analysis for computing the Nyström approximation via Algorithm
2.1
, where two different precisions can be used and the computation of the matrix-matrix product can be performed in a lower precision. This analysis results in a deterministic bound for the Frobenius norm of the total finite precision error and allows us to formulate a practical heuristic for how to choose the lower precision. Insights into preserving stability when selecting the sketching matrix and a shift for the Cholesky decomposition are presented. We also extend bounds for the condition number of a matrix preconditoned by a limited memory preconditioner constructed using the Nyström approximation to account for the finite precision error. The theoretical results are illustrated with numerical experiments on synthetic and application problems.

The paper is structured as follows. In Section
2

3
we consider the application of our mixed precision approach to constructing limited memory preconditioners. Numerical examples are presented in Section
4

5

2.1
Finite precision analysis

We analyze Algorithm
2.1
which uses two precisions, a potentially lower precision with unit roundoff
u_{p}
for computing the expensive product with
A
, and a working precision
u
for all other steps of the algorithm. Throughout the paper, hats are used to denote the computed versions of quantities and thus the computed approximation is
\widehat{A}_{N}
. The finite precision error is denoted

\mathcal{E}=A_{N}-\widehat{A}_{N}.

(6)

We aim to bound the Frobenius norm of
\mathcal{E}
in order to give a bound on the total error of the approximation via

\|A-\widehat{A}_{N}\|_{F}=\|A-A_{N}+A_{N}-\widehat{A}_{N}\|_{F}\leq\|E\|_{F}+\|\mathcal{E}\|_{F}.

We use a standard model of floating point arithmetic; see, e.g.,
[
20
, Section 2.2]
. For matrix
A
,
\kappa(A)
will be used to denote the condition number
\kappa(A)=\|A^{\dagger}\|\|A\|
in an indicated norm. Note that in our analysis, we do not account for oversampling. We consider a general sketching matrix
\Omega\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times k}
and some small shift
\widehat{\nu}
. Discussion on how these should be chosen based on the finite precision analysis is presented in Section
2.5.2
.

Our analysis requires the following assumptions:

1.

the SVD in step
7
of Algorithm
2.1
is computed exactly;

2.

u\leq u_{p}
;

3.

no overflow or underflow occurs;

4.

\kappa_{2}(\widetilde{B})\ll u^{-1}
, where
\widetilde{B}=fl\left(\frac{1}{2}(\widehat{B}+\widehat{B}^{T})\right)
;

5.

\widehat{\nu}
and
\Omega
are set so that

\displaystyle\Omega^{T}(A+\widehat{\nu}I)\Omega\textrm{ is symmetric positive definite (SPD) };

(7)

\displaystyle\|\left(\Omega^{T}(A+\widehat{\nu}I)\Omega\right)^{-1}\Delta_{2}\|_{F}\leq\|\left(\Omega^{T}(A+\widehat{\nu}I)\Omega\right)^{-1}\|_{F}\|\Delta_{2}\|_{F}<1,

(8)

where
\Delta_{2}
is the finite precision error accumulated in steps
1
to
5
;

6.

\widehat{\nu}\leq c(n,k)u_{p}\|A\|_{F}\|\Omega\|_{F}^{2}
, where
c(n,k)
is a constant that depends on
k
and
n
; and

7.

the precisions
u_{p}
and
u
are chosen so that

\displaystyle\kappa_{2}(A_{k}+\widehat{\nu}I_{k})\widetilde{\kappa}(\Omega)^{2}

\displaystyle\ll u_{p}^{-2}\quad\textrm{and}

(9)

\displaystyle\kappa_{2}(A_{k}+\widehat{\nu}I_{k})\widetilde{\kappa}(\Omega)^{2}

\displaystyle\ll u^{-1},

(10)

where
A_{k}
is the best rank-
k
approximation of
A
and

\widetilde{\kappa}(\Omega)\coloneqq\|\Omega\|_{F}\|\left(W_{1}^{T}\Omega\right)^{\dagger}\|_{2}.

(11)

The first assumption does not have a significant effect on the final bound when a numerically stable algorithm is used to compute the SVD, but it simplifies the analysis; note that this same assumption is used in
[
9
]
. Assuming that the sketching is performed in a lower or the same precision as other computations allows us to make the analysis easier to read by ignoring terms of order

.
Note that since the sketching step is the only time

is accessed,
A
may also be stored in precision
u_{p}
. We assume all other quantities are stored in precision

. Assuming no overflow or underflow is a standard assumption, although we note that overflow and underflow can become increasingly common when very low precisions are used. The assumption on the condition number of
\widetilde{B}
is standard.
Assumption

\Omega

\widehat{\nu}
should be chosen to satisfy (
7
) and (
8
) in Section

. The bound for
\widehat{\nu}
ensures that the shift does not increase the approximation error significantly. The final assumption requires that the ideal rank-

approximation is well conditioned in the precisions used in Algorithm

, and
allows us to simplify the presentation of the bounds.

Following
[
20
]
, we define

\gamma_{n}^{(p)}=\frac{nu_{p}}{1-nu_{p}},\quad\widetilde{\gamma}_{n}^{(p)}=\frac{cnu_{p}}{1-cnu_{p}},

where

is a small constant independent of
n
. The superscript

is omitted when referring to the terms with

instead of

. The notations
\approx

2.2
Preliminary results

Before we delve into the finite precision analysis, we first explore the weighted pseudoinverse

X_{A}^{\dagger}\coloneqq AX\left(X^{T}AX\right)^{\dagger}

(12)

of a matrix
X\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times k}

independent of

are available
[

13

AX\left(X^{T}AX\right)^{\dagger}=A^{1/2}\left(X^{T}A^{1/2}\right)^{\dagger}

and considering the eigenvalue decomposition as in (

\left\lVert A^{1/2}\left(X^{T}A^{1/2}\right)^{\dagger}\right\rVert_{2}=\left\lVert\Lambda^{1/2}W^{T}\left(X^{T}W\Lambda^{1/2}W^{T}\right)^{\dagger}\right\rVert_{2}\leq\|\Lambda^{1/2}\|_{2}\left\lVert\left(X^{T}W\Lambda^{1/2}\right)^{\dagger}\right\rVert_{2}.

(13)

Recall that

\left\lVert\left(X^{T}W\Lambda^{1/2}\right)^{\dagger}\right\rVert_{2}=1/\sigma_{min}(X^{T}W\Lambda^{1/2})
,
where
\sigma_{min}(X^{T}W\Lambda^{1/2})
denotes the smallest singular value of
X^{T}W\Lambda^{1/2}
. We can write
X^{T}W\Lambda^{1/2}=\begin{bmatrix}X^{T}W_{1}\Lambda_{1}^{1/2}&X^{T}W_{2}\Lambda_{2}^{1/2}\end{bmatrix}

and using the singular value interlacing property and the inequality on the singular values of a product of two square matrices, we obtain

\sigma_{min}(X^{T}W\Lambda^{1/2})\geq\sigma_{min}\left(X^{T}W_{1}\Lambda_{1}^{1/2}\right)\geq\sigma_{min}(X^{T}W_{1})\sigma_{min}(\Lambda_{1}^{1/2})\\
=\|(X^{T}W_{1})^{\dagger}\|_{2}^{-1}\|\Lambda_{1}^{-1/2}\|_{2}^{-1}.

(14)

Combining this with (

A_{k}=W_{1}\Lambda_{1}W_{1}^{T}
, we obtain

\|X_{A}^{\dagger}\|_{2}\leq\kappa_{2}(A_{k})^{1/2}\|(X^{T}W_{1})^{\dagger}\|_{2}.

(15)

A bound on
\|\left(X^{T}AX\right)^{-1}\|_{2}
is required in our further analysis. Since
\sigma_{i}(X^{T}AX)=\sigma_{i}(X^{T}A^{1/2})^{2}
, we use (

\|\left(X^{T}AX\right)^{-1}\|_{2}\leq\|(X^{T}W_{1})^{\dagger}\|_{2}^{2}\|\Lambda_{1}^{-1/2}\|_{2}^{2}=\|(X^{T}W_{1})^{\dagger}\|_{2}^{2}\|A_{k}^{\dagger}\|_{2}.

(16)

2.3
Finite precision error bound

Our main result in this section is the following theorem.

Theorem 1
.

Let
A\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
be a symmetric positive semidefinite matrix stored in precision
u_{p}
, and
\widehat{A}_{N}
be its approximation computed by Algorithm
2.1
using precision
u_{p}
in step
1
and precision
u\leq u_{p}
in other steps. If the assumptions
1
-
11
are satisfied, then the total approximation error is bounded as

\|A-\widehat{A}_{N}\|_{F}\lesssim\|(A+\widehat{\nu}I)-(A+\widehat{\nu}I)_{N}\|_{F}+k^{1/2}\widetilde{\gamma}_{n}^{(p)}\kappa_{2}(A_{k}+\widehat{\nu}I_{k})\widetilde{\kappa}(\Omega)^{2}\|A\|_{F},

(17)

where
A_{k}
is the best rank-
k
approximation of
A
,
(A+\widehat{\nu}I)_{N}
is the exact Nyström approximation of
A+\widehat{\nu}I
and
\widetilde{\kappa}(\Omega)
is defined in (
11
).

We briefly comment on the bounds before stating the proof. The first term on the right-hand side bounds the error of the exact Nyström approximation of the shifted matrix
A+\widehat{\nu}I
. The bounds for the exact Nyström approximation error of
A
that depend on the eigenvalues of
A
can be easily adapted to bound the error of approximating
A+\widehat{\nu}I
as the eigenvalues are shifted by
\widehat{\nu}
and the extra error due to the shift is absorbed by the second term in (
17
). We now continue with the proof.

Proof.

The roadmap of the proof is as follows. First, we account for the finite precision error terms in every step of the algorithm, bound their norm and track their influence on the subsequent computations. We then backtrack all the steps of the algorithm to find out how the finite precision error influences the computed approximation
\widehat{A}_{N}
. In order to express
\widehat{A}_{N}
via
A_{N}
we approximate a perturbed inverse of
\Omega^{T}(A+\widehat{\nu}I)\Omega
to the first order. The final bound for the norm of the total finite precision error makes use of the bounds for the norms of the weighted pseudoinverse and the finite precision error terms obtained in the first part of the proof. We simplify the bound under reasonable assumptions. The proof is provided here with some extra details and cumbersome expressions deferred to Appendix
A
.

Part 1. Step-by-step analysis.

In step
1
, we compute

\displaystyle\widehat{Y}=A\Omega+\Delta,\textrm{ where }

(18)

\displaystyle\|\Delta\|_{F}\leq\gamma_{n}^{(p)}\|A\|_{F}\|\Omega\|_{F}.

(19)

Applying the shift
\widehat{\nu}
gives

\displaystyle\widehat{Y}_{\nu}=\widehat{Y}+\widehat{\nu}\Omega+\Delta_{\nu},\textrm{ where }

(20)

\displaystyle\|\Delta_{\nu}\|_{F}\leq u\|\widehat{Y}+\widehat{\nu}\Omega\|_{F}\leq\gamma_{n}(1+\gamma_{n}^{(p)})\|A\|_{F}\|\Omega\|_{F}+\gamma_{n}\widehat{\nu}\|\Omega\|_{F},

and the second inequality is due to (

) and (

The algorithm continues by forming a symmetric matrix

\displaystyle\,+k\left(1+2^{3/2}k(k+1)u\kappa(\widetilde{B})\right)^{2}\left(1+3\gamma_{n}\right)\widehat{\nu}\|\Omega\|_{F}^{2}.

\displaystyle\widehat{F}=F+\Delta_{F},\textrm{ where}

\displaystyle F=\widehat{Y}_{\nu}\widehat{C}^{-1},

\displaystyle\|\Delta_{F}\|_{F}\leq\frac{\gamma_{k}k^{1/2}\kappa_{2}(\widehat{C})}{1-\gamma_{k}k^{1/2}\kappa_{2}(\widehat{C})}\|F\|_{F},

and this gives

where

.
In the final step of the algorithm we remove the shift
\widehat{\nu}
and set all the computed eigenvalues that are smaller than the shift to zero. Then the computed approximation is

\displaystyle\Delta_{r}=U\textrm{diag}(\underbrace{0,\dots,0}_{\text{k-j}},\sigma_{k-j+1}^{2}-\widehat{\nu},\dots,\sigma_{k}^{2}-\widehat{\nu})U^{T}\textrm{ and }

\displaystyle\|\Delta_{r}\|_{F}\leq j^{1/2}\widehat{\nu},

for

\displaystyle\,-(A+\widehat{\nu}I)\Omega\left(\Omega^{T}(A+\widehat{\nu}I)\Omega\right)^{-1}\Delta_{2}\left(\Omega^{T}(A+\widehat{\nu}I)\Omega\right)^{-1}\Omega^{T}(A+\widehat{\nu}I)^{T}

2.4
A practical heuristic

We note that the bound in Theorem
1
overestimates the total error. However its structure, that is, it being a sum of the exact approximation error and an additional term for the finite precision error, gives us insight into when setting
u_{p}
to lower than the working precision may be appropriate. The finite precision error may be essentially ignored if
k^{1/2}\widetilde{\gamma}_{n}^{(p)}\kappa_{2}(A_{k}+\widehat{\nu}I_{k})\widetilde{\kappa}(\Omega)^{2}\|A\|_{F}\lesssim\|(A+\widehat{\nu}I)-(A+\widehat{\nu}I)_{N}\|_{F}
. The exact approximation error
\|(A+\widehat{\nu}I)-(A+\widehat{\nu}I)_{N}\|_{F}
is expected to decrease when the rank
k
of the approximation is increased, and hence the effect of the finite precision error may be important for large-rank approximations.

We can roughly estimate for which values of
u_{p}

\|\mathcal{E}\|_{F}
stays smaller than
\|E\|_{F}
.
We assume that the shift
\widehat{\nu}
is small enough to be ignored and replace
\|E\|_{F}
and
\|\mathcal{E}\|_{F}
with the bounds (
4
) and (
45
). We further simplify (
4
) using
\|\Lambda^{1/2}_{2}(W_{2}^{T}\Omega)(W_{1}^{T}\Omega)^{\dagger}\|_{2}\leq\|\Lambda^{1/2}_{2}\|_{2}\|(W_{2}^{T}\Omega)\|_{2}\|(W_{1}^{T}\Omega)^{\dagger}\|_{2}
and thus bound

\|E\|_{F}\leq ck^{1/2}\|\Lambda^{1/2}_{2}\|_{F}^{2}\|\Omega\|_{F}^{2}\|(W_{1}^{T}\Omega)^{\dagger}\|_{2}^{2},

where
c
is a small constant. Requiring (
45
) to be smaller than this, using the rule of thumb that
n
can be replaced by
n^{1/2}

[
20
]
, and ignoring
\widehat{\nu}
gives the constraint

u_{p}\ll n^{-1/2}\frac{\lambda_{k}}{\lambda_{max}}\frac{\sum_{i=k+1}^{n}\lambda_{i}}{(\sum_{j=1}^{n}\lambda_{j}^{2})^{1/2}}.

(46)

Note that we used large overestimates of both
\|\mathcal{E}\|_{F}
and
\|E\|_{F}
to obtain (
46
), so this should not be interpreted as a mathematically rigorous condition. Also, while it may be possible to compute or estimate
\lambda_{k}
and
\lambda_{max}
, estimating the fraction
\sum_{i=k+1}^{n}\lambda_{i}/(\sum_{j=1}^{n}\lambda_{j}^{2})^{1/2}
may be not achievable in practice. In this case, one may choose to omit this term and use the heuristic

u_{p}\ll n^{-1/2}\frac{\lambda_{k}}{\lambda_{max}}.

(47)

In Section
4
we demonstrate that, although not a rigorous constraint, this heuristic often gives a good indication of values of
u_{p}
that can be chosen without significant affecting approximation quality.

2.5
Satisfying the assumptions

The validity of our analysis depends on the conditions (
7
) and (
8
).
We tackle (
7
) by considering the smallest eigenvalue of
\Omega^{T}\left(A+\widehat{\nu}I\right)\Omega
and showing that it is larger than zero, and thus the matrix in question is SPD.
We use Weyl’s inequality and obtain

\lambda_{min}\left(\Omega^{T}\left(A+\widehat{\nu}I\right)\Omega\right)\geq\lambda_{min}(\Omega^{T}A\Omega)+\lambda_{min}(\widehat{\nu}\Omega^{T}\Omega).

Combining this with (

gives

\lambda_{min}\left(\Omega^{T}\left(A+\widehat{\nu}I\right)\Omega\right)\geq\sigma_{min}(\Omega^{T}W_{1})^{2}\lambda_{k}(A)+\widehat{\nu}\sigma_{min}(\Omega)^{2}.

(48)

All the quantities on the right hand side of the bound are non-negative and even in the case when
\lambda_{k}(A)=0
the distance between the smallest eigenvalue of
\Omega^{T}\left(A+\widehat{\nu}I\right)\Omega
and zero depends on the shift
\widehat{\nu}
and the smallest singular value of the sketching matrix
\Omega
.

We now consider condition (
8
). This is done by showing that

\displaystyle\left\lVert\left[\Omega^{T}\left(A+\widehat{\nu}I\right)\Omega\right]^{-1}\Delta_{2}\right\rVert_{2}\leq

\displaystyle\,\left\lVert\left[\Omega^{T}\left(A+\widehat{\nu}I\right)\Omega\right]^{-1}\right\rVert_{2}\left\lVert\Delta_{2}\right\rVert_{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\,\left\lVert\left[\Omega^{T}\left(A+\widehat{\nu}I\right)\Omega\right]^{-1}\right\rVert_{2}\left\lVert\Delta_{2}\right\rVert_{F}<1.

Using
\left\lVert\left(\Omega^{T}\left(A+\widehat{\nu}I\right)\Omega\right)^{-1}\right\rVert_{2}=\frac{1}{\lambda_{min}\left(\Omega^{T}\left(A+\widehat{\nu}I\right)\Omega\right)}
, (
48
), and (
35
) we have

\left\lVert\left(\Omega^{T}\left(A+\widehat{\nu}I\right)\Omega\right)^{-1}\right\rVert_{2}\|\Delta_{2}\|_{F}\leq\\
\frac{\left(\gamma_{n}^{(p)}+3\gamma_{n}+k\gamma_{k+1}+3\gamma_{n}\gamma_{n}^{(p)}+k\gamma_{k+1}\gamma_{n}^{(p)}\right)\|A\|_{F}\|\Omega\|_{F}^{2}}{\lambda_{k}(A)\sigma_{min}(W_{1}^{T}\Omega)^{2}+\widehat{\nu}\sigma_{min}(\Omega)^{2}}\\
\,+\frac{\left(3\gamma_{n}+k\gamma_{k+1}\right)\widehat{\nu}\|\Omega\|_{F}^{2}}{\lambda_{k}(A)\sigma_{min}(W_{1}^{T}\Omega)^{2}+\widehat{\nu}\sigma_{min}(\Omega)^{2}}.

Thus to satisfy (

\left(\gamma_{n}^{(p)}+3\gamma_{n}+k\gamma_{k+1}+3\gamma_{n}\gamma_{n}^{(p)}+k\gamma_{k+1}\gamma_{n}^{(p)}\right)\|A\|_{F}\|\Omega\|_{F}^{2}+\left(3\gamma_{n}+k\gamma_{k+1}\right)\widehat{\nu}\|\Omega\|_{F}^{2}\\

Ideally, this condition should be satisfied independently of
\lambda_{k}
and

2.5.2
Choosing the shift and the sketching matrix

Our analysis indicates that the shift and the sketching matrix have to be chosen such that

•

(
50

•

\widehat{\nu}\leq c(n,k)u_{p}\|\Omega\|_{F}^{2}\|A\|_{F}

From (

Rearranging the terms gives

which can be simplified to
\kappa(\Omega)\ll u^{-1/2}
. We note that sketching matrices are usually chosen to be well-conditioned and thus this condition is satisfied. We denote

\beta\coloneqq\sigma_{min}(\Omega)^{2}-\left(3\gamma_{n}+k\gamma_{k+1}\right)\|\Omega\|_{F}^{2}

\left(\gamma_{n}^{(p)}+3\gamma_{n}+k\gamma_{k+1}+3\gamma_{n}\gamma_{n}^{(p)}+k\gamma_{k+1}\gamma_{n}^{(p)}\right)\|A\|_{F}\|\Omega\|_{F}^{2}<\widehat{\nu}\beta.

Note that the computed versions of the shifts defined in
[
38
]
and
[
37
]
are

\displaystyle\widehat{\nu}=2u_{p}\|\widehat{Y}\|_{F}+\delta=2u_{p}\|A\Omega+\Delta\|_{F}+\delta_{1},\textrm{ where }

\displaystyle|\delta_{1}|\leq 2nu_{p}u\|\widehat{Y}\|_{F}

and

\displaystyle\widehat{\nu}=2u_{p}(\textrm{trace}(A)+\delta_{2})=2u_{p}\|A^{1/2}\|_{F}^{2}+2u_{p}\delta_{2},\textrm{ where }

\displaystyle|\delta_{2}|\leq\gamma_{n}^{(p)}\textrm{trace}(A).

Unfortunately we cannot show that (

that produces the terms on the left hand side of (

This shows that the choice of

depends on

In the case when

If however

n^{1/2}-1<(k-1)^{1/2}

\sigma_{min}(\Omega)<1
(see Figure

for an illustration) and thus
\sigma_{min}(\Omega)
has to be incorporated into the shift. This is in line with the comment in

that for large

3
Preconditioning

We now consider an important application area where low-rank matrix approximations are used, namely, preconditioning iterative solvers. A useful preconditioner has to be inexpensive to construct and apply while accelerating the convergence of an iterative solver. Using the mixed precision Algorithm
2.1
to construct the preconditioner may thus result in computational savings.

Let
A\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
be a symmetric positive semidefinite matrix, so that it can be approximated via the Nyström method, and consider a system of linear equations of the form

(A+\mu I)x=b,

(56)

where
\mu\geq 0
so that
A+\mu I
is positive definite,
I\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
is an identity matrix, and
x,b\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Preconditioned conjugate gradient (PCG) is a popular method for systems with symmetric positive definite coefficient matrices; see, e.g.,
[
33
]
.

We focus on the case where
A
has rapidly decreasing eigenvalues or a cluster of large eigenvalues; notice that the spectrum of
A+\mu I
has the same structure. In such settings, finite precision PCG convergence can be slow
[
6
]
and removing the largest eigenvalues with a preconditioner can accelerate convergence. This can be achieved using a spectral limited memory preconditioner (LMP), defined as

\displaystyle P=I-UU^{T}+\frac{1}{\alpha+\mu}U(\Theta+\mu I)U^{T},

(57)

\displaystyle P^{-1}=I-UU^{T}+(\alpha+\mu)U(\Theta+\mu I)^{-1}U^{T},

(58)

where the columns of
U\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times k}
are approximate eigenvectors of
A
and
U^{T}U=I
,
\Theta
is diagonal with approximations to the eigenvalues of
A
, and
\alpha\geq 0
.

The expression (
58
) is an instance of a general class of limited memory preconditioners studied in
[
41
,
18
,
40
]
and used in data assimilation
[
29
,
28
,
24
]
. Algorithm
2.1
returns an eigendecomposition of
A_{N}
and thus can be used to construct (
57
). The randomised version of the preconditioner is mentioned in
[
27
]
(Section 17) and is analysed in
[
14
]
, where it is called a randomised Nyström preconditioner. It is also explored in a data assimilation setting under the name randomised LMP in
[
10
]
.

It is easy to show that if (
58
) is constructed using exact eigenpairs, then the eigenvalues used to construct the preconditioner are mapped to
\alpha+\mu
and the other eigenvalues remain unchanged. This cannot be guaranteed when constructing the preconditioner with approximations to the eigenpairs, but a study of the eigenvalues of the preconditioned matrix when (
57
) is constructed with eigenvalues of
A+tE
, where
\|E\|_{2}=1
,
t\in\mathbb{R}
is small, and
\mu=0
, in
[
15
]
by Giraud and Gratton show that if the preconditioner is constructed using high quality approximations of not clustered or not small isolated eigenpairs, then the eigenvalues in the inexact case will be close to the exact ones.

If we have information on existing eigenvalue clusters of
A
, we may use it to choose
\alpha
and thus send the largest eigenvalues close to an already existing cluster. Martinsson and Tropp in
[
27
]
suggest setting
\alpha=\lambda_{k}
, where
\lambda_{k}
is the smallest nonzero eigenvalue of
A_{N}
. This is done with the hope that the spectrum of
P^{-1}(A+\mu I)
is then more clustered compared to
A
, and the condition number is reduced. Note that the condition number alone does not determine the PCG convergence behaviour and the same holds for the number of clusters of eigenvalues; see
[
6
]
and
[
5
]
for detailed commentary. A small condition number however does indicate fast convergence. In the following section, we consider the resulting condition number when the mixed precision Nyström approximation in Algorithm
2.1
is used to construct the preconditioner.

3.1
Bound on the condition number of the preconditioned coefficient matrix

The work in
[
14
]
provides bounds for the condition number of the preconditioned coefficient matrix in exact arithmetic. We extend them to include the finite precision error in the preconditioner

\widehat{P}^{-1}=I-\widehat{U}\widehat{U}^{T}+(\widehat{\lambda}_{k}+\mu)\widehat{U}(\widehat{\Theta}+\mu I)^{-1}\widehat{U}^{T},

(59)

where
\widehat{A}_{N}=\widehat{U}\widehat{\Theta}\widehat{U}^{T}
is a finite precision rank-
k
Nyström approximation of
A
obtained via Algorithm
2.1
and
\widehat{\lambda}_{k}
is the smallest eigenvalue of
\widehat{A}_{N}
. The columns of
\widehat{U}
are the eigenvectors of
\widehat{A}_{N}
and thus they are orthogonal. Then
\widehat{P}^{-1}
is symmetric positive definite and denoting the nonzero eigenvalues of
\widehat{A}_{N}
as
\widehat{\lambda}_{i}>\widehat{\lambda}_{i+1}
we can write

\displaystyle\widehat{P}^{-1}=

\displaystyle\,I-\widehat{U}\widehat{U}^{T}+(\widehat{\lambda}_{k}+\mu)\widehat{U}\textrm{diag}\left(\frac{1}{\widehat{\lambda}_{1}+\mu},\frac{1}{\widehat{\lambda}_{2}+\mu},\dots,\frac{1}{\widehat{\lambda}_{k}+\mu}\right)\widehat{U}^{T}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,I+\widehat{U}\textrm{diag}\left(\frac{\widehat{\lambda}_{k}+\mu}{\widehat{\lambda}_{1}+\mu}-1,\frac{\widehat{\lambda}_{k}+\mu}{\widehat{\lambda}_{2}+\mu}-1,\dots,\frac{\widehat{\lambda}_{k}+\mu}{\widehat{\lambda}_{k}+\mu}-1\right)\widehat{U}^{T}.

\widehat{P}^{-1}
thus has
n-k+1
eigenvalues equal to one and the rest are equal to
(\widehat{\lambda}_{k}+\mu)/(\widehat{\lambda}_{i}+\mu)
for
i=1,2,\dots,k-1
.

Theorem 2
.

Let
(A+\mu I)\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
be symmetric positive definite,
\widehat{P}^{-1}
as defined in (
59
), and
E
and
\mathcal{E}
as defined in (
2
) and (
6
), respectively. The condition number
\kappa(\widehat{P}^{-1/2}(A+\mu I)\widehat{P}^{-1/2})
can then be bounded as

\max\left\{1,\frac{\widehat{\lambda}_{k}+\mu-\|\mathcal{E}\|_{2}}{\mu+\lambda_{min}(A)}\right\}\leq\kappa(\widehat{P}^{-1/2}(A+\mu I)\widehat{P}^{-1/2})\leq 1+\frac{\widehat{\lambda}_{k}+\|E\|_{2}+2\|\mathcal{E}\|_{2}}{\mu-\|\mathcal{E}\|_{2}},

(60)

where the upper bound holds if
\mu>\|\mathcal{E}\|_{2}
. Regardless of this constraint,

\kappa(\widehat{P}^{-1/2}(A+\mu I)\widehat{P}^{-1/2})\leq\left(\widehat{\lambda}_{k}+\mu+\|E\|_{2}+\|\mathcal{E}\|_{2}\right)\left(\frac{1}{\widehat{\lambda}_{k}+\mu}+\frac{\|\mathcal{E}\|_{2}+1}{\lambda_{min}(A)+\mu}\right).

(61)

The proof closely follows the argument in
[
14
]
and is supplied in Appendix
B
.

If
\|\mathcal{E}\|_{2}=0
, then the bounds in Theorem
2
coincide with the bounds in
[
14
, Proposition 5.3]
. The lower bound is useful when
\widehat{\lambda}_{k}+\mu-\|\mathcal{E}\|_{2}>\mu+\lambda_{min}(A)
, that is, when
\widehat{\lambda}_{k}>\|\mathcal{E}\|_{2}+\lambda_{min}(A)
, which can be expected to hold when large eigenvalues are approximated. The finite precision error has an additive effect on the bounds and expands them, and a multiplicative effect appears in (
61
).

5
Conclusions

In this paper, we considered a mixed precision variant of the single-pass Nyström method for approximating an SPD matrix
A
, where the expensive product with
A
can be performed in lower precision than the other computations. We bound the total approximation error taking into account the finite precision error. A good quality approximation is obtained when the finite precision error is smaller than the error of the exact Nyström approximation itself. This corresponds to the case when only relatively large eigenvalues are approximated, as suggested by the practical heuristic developed in this work. Numerical examples with both synthetic problems and application problems confirm this observation, and indicate that the standard of using double precision throughout the algorithm may miss opportunities for improving performance.

We also analysed a randomised limited memory preconditioner constructed with the mixed precision Nyström approximation. We proved bounds on the condition number of the preconditioned coefficient matrix which take into account the finite precision error. The preconditioner is of most interest when it is constructed with a relatively low rank approximation, and in this case using low precision for the product with
A
does not diminish the quality of the preconditioner in terms of resulting iterations of the conjugate gradient method.

The limited range of low precision restricts the set of problems that can be considered in the mixed precision framework. This is problematic if setting the low precision to half or even quarter precision is desired. An algorithm that maps a matrix to half precision and preserves symmetry is proposed in
[
22
]
.
Future work on developing scaling algorithms that preserve the positive semidefiniteness property and the structure of the spectrum while also not requiring additionally accesses to the matrix
A
is of high interest.

Details of finite precision analysis

We provide the details for obtaining the bounds for
\|\widehat{C}\|_{F}
,

A.1
Bounding
\|\widehat{C}\|_{F}

We now investigate
\|\widehat{C}\|_{F}
by expressing
\widehat{C}
as a perturbed exact Cholesky factor of
\widetilde{B}
. We use
[
36

, which states that given the exact Cholesky decomposition
\widetilde{B}=C^{T}C

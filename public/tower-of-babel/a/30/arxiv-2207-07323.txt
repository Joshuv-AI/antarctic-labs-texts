Boundedness of solutions to Dirichlet, Neumann and Robin problems for elliptic equations in Orlicz spaces
Giuseppina Barletta, Andrea Cianchi, Greta Marino

Abstract
. Boundary value problems for second-order elliptic equations in divergence form, whose nonlinearity is governed by a convex function of non-necessarily power type, are considered. The global boundedness of their solutions is established under boundary conditions of Dirichlet, or Neumann, or Robin type. A decisive role in the results is played by optimal forms of Orlicz-Sobolev embeddings and boundary trace embeddings, which allow for critical growths of the coefficients.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
35J25, 35J60, 35B65.

1.
Introduction

We are concerned with the global boundedness of weak solutions to boundary value problems for second-order nonlinear elliptic equations of the form

(1.1)

-\divergenz\big(\mathcal{A}(x,u,\nabla u)\big)=\mathcal{B}(x,u,\nabla u)\quad\text{in }\Omega,

where
\Omega
is an open set in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
n\geq 2
, with finite Lebesgue measure
|\Omega|
.

This primary question in the regularity theory
was extensively analyzed as early as in the the classical monograph
[
LaUr
]
, where the boundedness of solutions to elliptic equations was also shown to be crucial for enjoying finer regularity properties.
Specifically, in
[
LaUr
, Theorem 7.1, Chapter 4, and Theorem 3.1, Chapter 5]
this problem for equations as in (
1.1
) is discussed in the standard case when Dirichlet boundary conditions are imposed and the nonlinearities of the functions
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}
in
u
and
\nabla u
are of polynomial type. This amounts to saying that the functions
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}
are subject to growth conditions depending on powers of
u
and
|\nabla u|
. Over the years, the results of
[
LaUr
]
have been sharpened, and extended to different boundary conditions in several contributions, such as
[
GaPe
,
GuVe
,
MMM
,
MaWi1
,
MaWi2
,
MaWi3
,
Wa
,
Wi
]
.

In the present paper, the restriction
on power type growths is removed, and the boundedness of solutions to boundary value problems for equations of type (
1.1
) driven by more general nonlinearities is established. A distinctive trait of our analysis is that the ellipticity condition on the principal part is dictated by a general nonnegative convex function vanishing at
0
– a Young function – of the modulus of the gradient. This calls for replacing customary Sobolev spaces with Orlicz-Sobolev spaces as a functional framework for solutions.

More specifically, we assume that
\mathcal{A}\colon\Omega\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\mathcal{B}\colon\Omega\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
are Carathéodory functions obeying:

(1.2)

\mathcal{A}(x,t,\xi)\cdot\xi\geq A(|\xi|)-B(|t|)

and

(1.3)

\sgn(t)\mathcal{B}(x,t,\xi)\leq f(x)+C(|t|)+D(|t|)E(|\xi|)

for a.e.
x\in\Omega
, for every
t\in\mathbb{R}
and every
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
Here, the dot
``\cdot"
stands for the scalar product in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
A:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
is a Young function,
B,C,D,E:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
are increasing functions,
and
f:\Omega\to[0,\infty)
is a measurable function whose integrability degree is prescribed by the membership in a weak Orlicz space defined by a Young function
M
.

Dirichlet, Neumann and Robin boundary value problems are included in our discussion. The datum
u_{0}
in the Dirichlet boundary condition

u=u_{0}\quad\text{on $\partial\Omega$}

is naturally assumed to be bounded. The weak formulation of the corresponding problem allows for arbitrary domains
\Omega
such that
|\Omega|<\infty
.

The Neumann condition to be imposed amounts to the vanishing of the co-normal derivative associated with the principal part of equation (
1.1
); namely:

\mathcal{A}(x,u,\nabla u)\cdot\nu=0\quad\text{on $\partial\Omega$,}

where
\nu
denotes the normal unit vector on
\partial\Omega
. The notion of weak solution to the corresponding Neumann problem avoids any a priori regularity assumption on
\Omega
. However, its degree of (ir)regularity enters the assumptions required on the functions appearing on the right-hand sides of inequalities (
1.2
) and (
1.3
). The regularity of
\Omega
will be prescribed in terms of its membership in Maz’ya type classes of bounded domains, defined via relative isoperimetric inequalities.

When a Robin condition

(1.4)

\mathcal{A}(x,u,\nabla u)\cdot\nu=\mathcal{C}(x,u)\quad\text{on $\partial\Omega$}

is assigned, boundary traces of solutions are as well involved in the definition of weak solution to the problem. The existence of traces is only guaranteed provided that
\Omega
is regular enough. Minimal assumptions on
\Omega
will be imposed in this connection.
The function
\mathcal{C}\colon\partial\Omega\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
in equation (
1.4
) is Carathéodory and is assumed to fulfill the bound

(1.5)

\sgn(t)\mathcal{C}(x,t)\leq g(x)+F(|t|)

for
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}
– a.e.
x\in\partial\Omega
and for every
t\in\mathbb{R}
. Here,
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}
denotes the
(n-1)
-dimensional measure,
F:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
is an increasing function, and the function
g:\partial\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
belongs to some weak Orlicz space, with respect to the measure
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}
, built upon a Young function
N
.

Our result on Dirichlet problems amounts to balance conditions among the growths of functions
A
,
B
,
C
,
D
,
E
,

M

for their solutions to be bounded. An analogous result holds for Neumann problems, save that the conditions also depend on an additional parameter, which appears as an exponent in the relative isoperimetric inequality supported by the domain
\Omega
. A conjugate of the function
A
from an optimal Orlicz-Sobolev inequality comes into the picture of these conditions. In fact, growth conditions in
u
which are critical in connection with the Sobolev conjugate of
A
are permitted.
As for Robin problems, the same assumptions as in the case of Dirichlet problems are required, plus additional balances between the functions
A
and
F
, and between the functions
A
and
N
. In this connection, a conjugate of
A
from a sharp Orlicz-Sobolev boundary trace inequality emerges as well. A critical growth in
u
according to the Sobolev trace conjugate of
A
is also entitled.

In the situation when the functions
A,B,C,D,E,F
appearing in inequalities (
1.2
), (
1.3
) and (
1.5
) are mere powers, the theorems to be presented recover several instances of those appearing in the contributions cited above, and also generalize them to the case of Neumann problems in possibly irregular domains.

The analysis of the global boundedness of solutions to elliptic problems under Orlicz type growth and Dirichlet boundary conditions traces back to
[
Ta1
]
in the case when
B=C=D=E=0
, and to
[
Ta2
]
in a complementary setting when
f=0
and
D=E=0
. The results of the latter paper were augmented in
[
Ci2
]
, where a sharp growth condition on the functions
B
and
C
was exhibited for solutions to the Dirichlet problems to be bounded. An extension to anisotropic equations is the subject of
[
Al
]
.

These are the contributions, dealing with elliptic equations ruled by Orlicz type nonlinearities, which are most closely related to the topic of our paper. On the other hand, the study of diverse
regularity properties of solutions to elliptic equations and systems with Orlicz growth has seen an increasing interest in the last decades. They are nowadays the subject of a vast literature, a nonexaustive sample of which includes

[
AbMo
,
AdiBF
,
BaSu
,
BaCi
,
Ba
,
BeMi
,
BHHK
,
BrMo
,
BSV
,
BGKS
,
CaKP
,
Ch
,
CeOk
,
CGZ
,
CGSW
,
Ci3
,
DeMi
,
DSV
,
Go
,
HHT
,
Ko
,
Li
,
Mar
,
MuTi
,
Wo
]
. The monograph
[
CGSW
]
is an up-to-date source of results and references in this area.

As far as proofs of our results are concerned, we point out that standard methods, such as Moser type iteration techniques, do not seem to yield optimal conclusions. This is related to the fact that, unlike the case when power type bounds in (
1.2
)–(
1.5
) are imposed, iterating optimal inequalities in the Orlicz framework need not produce an inequality which is again optimal. This drawback is also visible, for instance, in the iteration of Orlicz-Sobolev embeddings – see e.g.
[
CPS
]
for a discussion on this point.

Our approach makes use of identities and inequalities involving integrals over the level sets of the solutions. Starting with the work of De Giorgi, this is of course nowadays also customary in the regularity theory of PDEs. However, we have to depart from usual discretization techniques applied to the levels of integration, which apparently do not properly fit general growth conditions. As a replacement, we derive a differential inequality involving the distribution function of the solution, which can
be regarded as a continuous version of these discretization techniques, and
is
reminiscent of arguments from
[
Ci2
,
Ta1
,
Ta2
]
.

More specifically, we start with bounds for integrals involving
\nabla u
by integrals involving
u
over level sets of the latter. These bounds are, in a sense, global Caccioppoli type inequalities, and are deduced from assumptions (
1.2
)–(
1.5
) by testing the weak formulation of the problems under consideration by truncations of the solutions themselves. The resultant inequalities are coupled with inequalities in the opposite direction for integrals of
u
in terms of integrals of
\nabla u
.

The main novelties are to be found in this key step,
which strongly depends on the use of various sharp embeddings and respective inequalities in Orlicz-Sobolev spaces. Not only inequalities with an optimal Orlicz target space, but also improvements with a target of Orlicz-Lorentz type, which is in fact optimal among all rearrangement-invariant spaces, come into play. In dealing with Robin problems, Orlicz-Sobolev trace inequalities have equally a pivotal role. In addition, a new Sobolev trace inequality in the so called Lorentz
\Lambda
-spaces, depending on the Young function
N
, is needed.

The combination of these upper and lower bounds for energy integrals of the solutions provides us with an inequality which, via the coarea formula and isoperimetric inequalities applied to the level sets of the solutions, yields a differential inequality involving their distribution function. The regularity of the domain
\Omega
required for Neumann and Robin problems enters the game at this stage.
An analysis of the solutions to the relevant differential inequality finally dictates the conditions to be imposed on the functions appearing in assumptions (
1.2
)–(
1.3
), and, in the case of Robin problems, also in (
1.5
).

2.
Orlicz-Sobolev spaces

The function space background underlying the proofs of our main results is recalled in this section. Additional material is presented in Section
5
, where an optimal trace inequality in Lorentz
\Lambda
-spaces, taylored for our applications to Robin problems, is established.

2.1.
Young functions

A function
A\colon[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty]
is said to be a Young function if it is convex, left-continuous, vanishes at
0
, and is neither identically equal to zero, nor to infinity. Any Young function
A
takes the form

A(t)=\int_{0}^{t}a(\tau)\,d\tau\quad\text{for $t\geq 0$,}

where the function
a:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty]
is non-decreasing and left-continuous.
If
A
is a Young function, then the function

(2.1)

\frac{A(t)}{t}\quad\text{is non-decreasing in $(0,\infty)$.}

Moreover,

(2.2)

A(kt)\geq kA(t)\quad\text{for $k\geq 1$ and $t\geq 0$.}

The Young conjugate of
A
is denoted by
\widetilde{A}
, and is defined as

\widetilde{A}(s)=\sup\{st-A(t):\,t\geq 0\}\quad\text{for }s\geq 0.

One has that
\widetilde{\widetilde{A}}=A
. By the very definition of
\widetilde{A}
,

(2.3)

ts\leq A(t)+\widetilde{A}(s)\quad\text{for $s,t\geq 0$.}

Moreover, one has that

(2.4)

t\leq\widetilde{A}^{-1}(t)A^{-1}(t)\leq 2t\quad\text{for $t\geq 0$,}

where the superscript “
-1
” stands for the (generalized) right-continuous inverse.
In particular, inequality (
2.4
) yields

(2.5)

\frac{A(t)}{t}\leq\widetilde{A}^{-1}(A(t))\leq 2\frac{A(t)}{t}\quad\text{for }t>0.

The function
A
is said to satisfy the
\Delta_{2}
-condition near infinity (briefly,
A\in\Delta_{2}
near infinity) if
A
is finite-valued and there exist constants
c\geq 2
and
t_{0}\geq 0
such that

(2.6)

A(2t)\leq cA(t)\quad\text{for }t\geq t_{0}.

The function
A
is said to satisfy the
\nabla_{2}
-condition near infinity (briefly,
A\in\nabla_{2}
near infinity) if there exist constants
c>2
and
t_{0}\geq 0
such that

(2.7)

A(2t)\geq cA(t)\quad\text{for }t\geq t_{0}.

If inequality (
2.6
), or (
2.7
), holds with
t_{0}=0
, then
A
is said to satisfy the
\Delta_{2}
-condition globally, or the
\nabla_{2}
-condition globally, respectively.

One has that

(2.8)

A\in\nabla_{2}
near infinity [globally] if and only if
\widetilde{A}\in\Delta_{2}
near infinity [globally].

A Young function
A_{1}
is said to dominate another Young function
A_{2}
near infinity [globally] if there exist constants
c>0
and
t_{0}\geq 0
such that

A_{2}(t)\leq A_{1}(ct)\quad\text{for $t\geq t_{0}$ \,[$t\geq 0$].}

If the functions
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
dominate each other near infinity [globally], then they are said to be equivalent near infinity [globally]. The relations of domination and of equivalence between the functions
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
will be denoted by
A_{2}\lesssim A_{1}
and
A_{2}\approx A_{1}
, respectively.

2.2.
Orlicz and weak Orlicz spaces

Let
(\mathcal{R},m)
be a non-atomic,
\sigma
-finite measure space, equipped with a measure
m
. In our applications,
\mathcal{R}
will be either a subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, endowed with the Lebesgue measure, or a subset of
\partial\Omega
, endowed with the Hausdorff measure
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}
.
We denote by
\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{R})
the space of real-valued
m
-measurable functions on
\mathcal{R}
.

Given a Young function
A
,
the Orlicz class
K^{A}(\mathcal{R})
is defined as

K^{A}(\mathcal{R})=\bigg\{u\in{\mathcal{M}}(\mathcal{R}):\,\,\int_{\mathcal{R}}A(|u|)\,dm<\infty\bigg\}.

The set
K^{A}(\mathcal{R})
is, in general, just a convex subset of
{\mathcal{M}}(\mathcal{R})
. One the other hand, the Orlicz space
L^{A}(\mathcal{R})
, defined as

L^{A}(\mathcal{R})=\big\{u\in{\mathcal{M}}(\mathcal{R}):\,\,u/\lambda\in K^{A}(\mathcal{R})\,\,\text{for some}\,\,\lambda\in\mathbb{R}\big\},

is a Banach space, equipped with the norm

\|u\|_{L^{A}(\mathcal{R})}=\inf\left\{\lambda>0:\,\int_{\Omega}A\left(\frac{|u|}{\lambda}\right)dm\leq 1\right\}.

As a consequence of inequality (
2.3
), one has that

(2.9)

\int_{\mathcal{R}}|uv|\,dm\leq\int_{\mathcal{R}}A(|u|)\,dm+\int_{\mathcal{R}}\widetilde{A}(|v|)\,dm

for
u\in K^{A}(\mathcal{R})
and
v\in K^{\widetilde{A}}(\mathcal{R})
.

Assume that
m(\mathcal{R})<\infty
. Given two Young functions
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
, one has that

L^{A_{1}}(\mathcal{R})\to L^{A_{2}}(\mathcal{R})

if and only if
A_{1}
dominates
A_{2}
near infinity. Here, and in what follows, the arrow “
\to
” stands for continuous embedding.

Given a function
u\in{\mathcal{M}}(\mathcal{R})
, we denote by
u^{*}:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty]
its decreasing rearrangement, defined as

u^{*}(s)=\inf\{t\geq 0:m(\{|u|>t\})\leq s\}\quad\text{for $s\geq 0$.}

The Hardy-Littlewood inequality tells us that

(2.10)

\int_{\mathcal{R}}|uv|\,dm\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}u^{*}(s)v^{*}(s)\,ds

for
u,v\in\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{R})
.

The function
u^{**}:(0,\infty)\to[0,\infty]
is defined as

u^{**}(s)=\frac{1}{s}\int_{0}^{s}u^{*}(r)\;dr\quad\text{for }s>0.

Note that
u^{**}
is also non-increasing, and

u^{*}(s)\leq u^{**}(s)\quad\text{ for $s>0$.}

The signed decreasing rearrangement
u^{\circ}:[0,\infty)\to[-\infty,\infty]
is given by

u^{\circ}(s)=\inf\{t\geq 0:m(\{u>t\})\leq s\}\quad\text{for $s\geq 0$.}

The functions
u
,
u^{*}
and
u^{\circ}
are equimeasurable.

When
m(\mathcal{R})<\infty
, the increasing rearrangement
u_{*}:[0,m(\mathcal{R})]\to[0,\infty]
is defined as

u_{*}(s)=u^{*}(m(\mathcal{R})-s)\quad\text{for $s\in[0,m(\mathcal{R})]$.}

A reverse Hardy-Littlewood inequality reads

(2.11)

\int_{\mathcal{R}}|uv|\,dm\geq\int_{0}^{m(\mathcal{R})}u^{*}(s)v_{*}(s)\,ds

for
u,v\in\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{R})
.

Under the assumption that
m(\mathcal{R})<\infty
, we also set

{\rm med}(u)=u^{\circ}\big(\tfrac{m(\mathcal{R})}{2}\big),

a median of
u
.

Given a Young function
A
, the weak Orlicz space
L^{A,\infty}(\mathcal{R})
is defined as the set of functions
u\in{\mathcal{M}}(\mathcal{R})
for which the norm

\|u\|_{L^{A,\infty}(\mathcal{R})}=\sup_{s>0}\frac{u^{**}(s)}{A^{-1}(1/s)}

is finite. The space
L^{A,\infty}(\mathcal{R})
is a Banach space endowed with this norm. Its name stems from the fact that
L^{A}(\mathcal{R})\to L^{A,\infty}(\mathcal{R})
for every Young function
A
, and

\|u\|_{L^{A,\infty}(\mathcal{R})}\leq\|u\|_{L^{A}(\mathcal{R})}

for
u\in L^{A}(\mathcal{R})
. Weak Orlicz spaces are a special instance of the Marcinkiewicz spaces, whose definition will be recalled in Section
5
.

A variant of inequality (
2.9
), of critical use in our proofs, tells us what follows.
Let
\alpha>1
and let
A
be a Young function
such that

(2.12)

\int_{0}\left(\frac{t}{A(t)}\right)^{\frac{1}{\alpha-1}}dt<\infty

and

(2.13)

\int^{\infty}\left(\frac{t}{A(t)}\right)^{\frac{1}{\alpha-1}}dt=\infty.

Let
\widehat{A}_{\alpha}
be the Young function defined by

(2.14)

\widehat{A}_{\alpha}(t)=\int_{0}^{t}\widehat{a}_{\alpha}(\tau)\,d\tau\quad\text{for $t\geq 0$,}

and

(2.15)

\widehat{a}_{\alpha}^{-1}(s)=\bigg(\int_{a^{-1}(s)}^{\infty}\bigg(\int_{0}^{t}\bigg(\frac{1}{a(\tau)}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{\alpha-1}}d\tau\bigg)^{-\alpha}\frac{dt}{a(t)^{\frac{\alpha}{\alpha-1}}}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{1-\alpha}}\,\,\,\quad{\rm for}\,\,\,s\geq 0\,.

Then there exists a constant
\kappa_{1}=\kappa_{1}(\alpha)

such that

(2.16)

\int_{\mathcal{R}}|uv|\,dm\leq\kappa_{1}\int_{0}^{\infty}\widehat{A}_{\alpha}\left(s^{-1/\alpha}u^{*}(s)\right)ds+\kappa_{1}\int_{0}^{\infty}\widetilde{A}\left(s^{-1/\alpha^{\prime}}\int_{0}^{s}v^{*}(r)\,dr\right)ds

for every
u,v\in\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{R})
, see

[
Ci4
, eq. (4.46)]
. Note that the convergence of the inner integral on the right-hand side of equation (
2.15
) is equivalent to assumption (
2.12
).

2.3.
Orlicz-Sobolev spaces

Let
\Omega
be an open set in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and let
A
be a Young function. We define the Orlicz-Sobolev class
V^{1}K^{A}(\Omega)
as

V^{1}K^{A}(\Omega)=\left\{u\in{\mathcal{M}}(\mathcal{R}):\,\,u\text{ is weakly differentiable in $\Omega$ and $|\nabla u|\in K^{A}(\Omega)$}\right\}.

The subset of
V^{1}K^{A}(\Omega)
of those functions which vanish, in a suitable sense, on
\partial\Omega
can be defined as

\displaystyle V_{0}^{1}K^{A}(\Omega)=\bigl\{u\in{\mathcal{M}}(\mathcal{R})

\displaystyle:\text{the continuation of $u$ by $0$ outside of $\Omega$}

\displaystyle\qquad\text{ is weakly differentiable in $\mathbb{R}^{n}$ and belongs to $V^{1}K^{A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})$}\bigr\}.

The Orlicz-Sobolev space
W^{1,A}(\Omega)
, and its subspace
W^{1,A}_{0}(\Omega)
, are accordingly defined as

W^{1,A}(\Omega)=\left\{u\in L^{A}(\Omega):u/\lambda\in V^{1}K^{A}(\Omega)\,\,\text{for some $\lambda\in\mathbb{R}$}\right\},

and

W^{1,A}_{0}(\Omega)=\left\{u\in L^{A}(\Omega):u/\lambda\in V^{1}_{0}K^{A}(\Omega)\,\,\text{for some $\lambda\in\mathbb{R}$}\right\}.

The space
W^{1,A}(\Omega)
, equipped with the norm

(2.17)

\|u\|_{W^{1,A}(\Omega)}=\|u\|_{L^{A}(\Omega)}+\|\nabla u\|_{L^{A}(\Omega)},

is a Banach space. Here
\|\nabla u\|_{L^{A}(\Omega)}
stands for
\||\nabla u|\|_{L^{A}(\Omega)}
. The subspace
W^{1,A}_{0}(\Omega)
is closed in
W^{1,A}(\Omega)
with respect to this norm, and hence is also a Banach space. A Poincaré type inequality ensures that, if
|\Omega|<\infty
, then the functional
\|\nabla u\|_{L^{A}(\Omega)}
defines a norm on
W^{1,A}(\Omega)
equivalent to the norm (
2.17
).

2.4.
Orlicz-Sobolev embeddings

Let
\Omega
be an open set in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
|\Omega|<\infty
. Given a Young function
A
, let
H_{n}\colon[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
be the function defined as

(2.18)

H_{n}(t)=\left(\int_{0}^{t}\left(\frac{\tau}{A(\tau)}\right)^{\frac{1}{n-1}}d\tau\right)^{\frac{1}{n^{\prime}}}\quad\text{for }t\geq 0,

where
n^{\prime}=\frac{n}{n-1}
and
A
is modified, if necessary, near
0
in such a way that

(2.19)

\int_{0}\left(\frac{t}{A(t)}\right)^{\frac{1}{n-1}}\,dt<\infty.

The Sobolev conjugate of
A
is the Young function
A_{n}
, introduced in
[
Ci2
]
, and given by

(2.20)

A_{n}(t)=A(H_{n}^{-1}(t))\quad\text{for }t\geq 0.

Here,
H_{n}^{-1}
denotes the (generalized) left-continuous inverse of
H_{n}
. One has that

(2.21)

W_{0}^{1,A}(\Omega)\to L^{A_{n}}(\Omega),

and
L^{A_{n}}(\Omega)
is the optimal (i.e. smallest possible) Orlicz target space
[
Ci2
]
, see also
[
Ci1
]
for an alternate equivalent formulation. In particular, there exists a positive constant
\kappa_{2}=\kappa_{2}(n)
such that

(2.22)

\int_{\Omega}A_{n}\left(\frac{\kappa_{2}|u|}{\left(\int_{\Omega}A(|\nabla u|)\,dy\right)^{1/n}}\right)dx\leq\int_{\Omega}A(|\nabla u|)\,dx

for every
u\in V^{1}_{0}K^{A}(\Omega)
.

Notice that, if
A
grows so fast near infinity that

(2.23)

\int^{\infty}\left(\frac{t}{A(t)}\right)^{\frac{1}{n-1}}dt<\infty,

then

(2.24)

W_{0}^{1,A}(\Omega)\to L^{\infty}(\Omega).

Actually, condition (
2.23
) implies
that
H_{n}^{-1}(t)=\infty
for large
t
. Hence,
A_{n}(t)=\infty
as well, and
L^{A_{n}}(\Omega)=L^{\infty}(\Omega)
, up to equivalent norms.

On the other hand, the space
W_{0}^{1,A}(\Omega)
does contain unbounded functions in the opposite regime when

(2.25)

\int^{\infty}\left(\frac{t}{A(t)}\right)^{\frac{1}{n-1}}dt=\infty.

If assumption (
2.25
) is in force, embedding (
2.21
) can still be augmented,
provided that the class of admissible target spaces is enlarged as to include all rearrangement-invariant spaces. The optimal target
for embeddings of
W^{1,A}_{0}(\Omega)
into spaces from this
class is an Orlicz-Lorentz space, equipped with the norm given by

\|u\|_{L(\widehat{A}_{n},n)(\Omega)}=\big\|s^{-\frac{1}{n}}u^{*}(s)\big\|_{L^{\widehat{A}_{n}}(0,|\Omega|)}

for
u\in{\mathcal{M}}(\Omega)
, where
\widehat{A}_{n}
is the Young function defined as in (
2.14
)–(
2.15
), with
\alpha=n
.
Note that, as in
the case of definition (
2.18
) for the function
A_{n}
, the function
A
is modified near zero, if necessary, in such a way that assumption (
2.19
) is satisfied. Such an assumption is equivalent to requiring that the innermost integral on the right-hand side of equation (
2.15
), with
\alpha=n
, converges.

The relevant embedding reads

(2.26)

W_{0}^{1,A}(\Omega)\to L(\widehat{A}_{n},n)(\Omega).

Moreover, a positive constant
\kappa_{3}=\kappa_{3}(\Omega)
exists such that

(2.27)

\int_{0}^{|\Omega|}\widehat{A}_{\alpha}\big(\kappa_{3}s^{-\frac{1}{n}}u^{*}(s)\big)\,ds\leq\int_{\Omega}A(|\nabla u|)\,dx

for every
u\in V^{1}_{0}K^{A}(\Omega)
.

The optimal Orlicz target space for embeddings of the Orlicz-Sobolev space
W^{1,A}(\Omega)
is the same as in (
2.21
), provided that
\Omega
is sufficiently regular. Bounded Lipschitz domains, bounded domains satisfying the cone property, bounded John domains, and
(\varepsilon,\delta)
-domains are admissible, for instance. Any domain from these classes supports a relative isoperimetric inequality of the form

(2.28)

\kappa_{4}\min\{|E|,|\Omega\setminus E|\}^{\frac{1}{\alpha^{\prime}}}\leq\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\partial^{M}E\cap\Omega)

with
\alpha=n
, for some positive constant
\kappa_{4}>0
and every measurable set
E\subset\Omega
. Here,
\partial^{M}
stands for essential boundary in the sense of geometric measure theory, and
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\partial^{M}E\cap\Omega)
is the perimeter of
E
, relative to
\Omega
.

The name of inequality (
2.28
) stems from the fact that it is a version in an open set
\Omega
of the classical isoperimetric inequality in
\mathbb{R}^{n}

(2.29)

n\omega_{n}^{\frac{1}{n}}\min\{|E|,|\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus E|\}^{\frac{1}{n^{\prime}}}\leq\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\partial^{M}E)

for every measurable set
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, where
\omega_{n}
denotes the Lebesgue measure of the unit ball in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

The embedding

W^{1,A}(\Omega)\to L^{A_{n}}(\Omega),

for bounded domains satisfying inequality (
2.28
) with
\alpha=n
, is established in
[
CPS
, Theorem 6.12]
and in
[
Ci1
]
, with
A_{n}
replaced with an equivalent Young function.

In the same papers, Sobolev embeddings for the space
W^{1,A}(\Omega)
are also offered when
\Omega
is a bounded open set
fulfilling inequality (
2.28
) for some

\ \alpha>n
. The use of relative isoperimetric inequalities in the characterization of Sobolev embedding was discovered by V. Maz’ya more than sixty years ago
[
Ma1
]
(see also
[
Ma2
]
for an expanded treatment of this topic). We shall denote by
\mathcal{J}_{1/{\alpha^{\prime}}}
the Maz’ya class of open sets satisfying inequality (
2.28
).

The Orlicz target space in Orlicz-Sobolev embedding on these domains is defined in terms of the Young function
A_{\alpha}
given by

A_{\alpha}(t)=A(H_{\alpha}^{-1}(t))\quad\text{for }t\geq 0,

where
H_{\alpha}
is defined in analogy to (
2.18
), with
n
replaced by
\alpha
; namely

H_{\alpha}(t)=\left(\int_{0}^{t}\left(\frac{\tau}{A(\tau)}\right)^{\frac{1}{\alpha-1}}d\tau\right)^{\frac{1}{\alpha^{\prime}}}\quad\text{for $t\geq 0$,}

with a parallel convention on the modification of
A
near zero as in (
2.18
) in such a way that condition (
2.12
) is fulfilled.

Indeed,
if
\Omega\in\mathcal{J}_{1/{\alpha^{\prime}}}
for some
\alpha\geq n
, then

(2.30)

W^{1,A}(\Omega)\to L^{A_{\alpha}}(\Omega).

Furthermore, there exists a positive constant
\kappa_{5}=\kappa_{5}(\Omega)
such that

(2.31)

\int_{\Omega}A_{\alpha}\left(\frac{\kappa_{5}|u-{\rm med}(u)|}{\left(\int_{\Omega}A(|\nabla u|)\,dy\right)^{1/\alpha}}\right)dx\leq\int_{\Omega}A(|\nabla u|)\,dx

for every
u\in V^{1}K^{A}(\Omega)
.
In particular,

(2.32)

W^{1,A}(\Omega)\to L^{\infty}(\Omega)

if

(2.33)

\int^{\infty}\left(\frac{t}{A(t)}\right)^{\frac{1}{\alpha-1}}dt<\infty.

By contrast, embedding (
2.32
) is not guaranteed in the opposite regime corresponding to condition (
2.13
).

If the latter condition is in force, the target space in embedding (
2.30
) admits an improvement in the
class of rearrangement-invariant spaces, which parallels (
2.26
).
For an arbitrary domain
\Omega\in\mathcal{J}_{1/{\alpha^{\prime}}}
, the optimal target
for embeddings of
W^{1,A}(\Omega)
into rearrangement-invariant spaces is the Orlicz-Lorentz space
L(\widehat{A}_{\alpha},\alpha)(\Omega)
, endowed with the norm defined as

\|u\|_{L(\widehat{A}_{\alpha},\alpha)(\Omega)}=\big\|s^{-\frac{1}{\alpha}}u^{*}(s)\big\|_{L^{\widehat{A}_{\alpha}}(0,|\Omega|)}

for
u\in{\mathcal{M}}(\Omega)
, where
\widehat{A}_{\alpha}
is the Young function defined as in (
2.14
)–(
2.15
).
Analogously to the embeddings presented above,
the function
A
is suitably modified near zero, if necessary, for condition (
2.12
) to be fulfilled. This ensures that the right-hand side of equation (
2.15
) is well defined.

The embedding in question takes the form

W^{1,A}(\Omega)\to L(\widehat{A}_{\alpha},\alpha)(\Omega).

Moreover, a positive constant
\kappa_{6}=\kappa_{6}(\Omega)
exists such that

(2.34)

\int_{0}^{|\Omega|}\widehat{A}_{\alpha}\big(\kappa_{6}s^{-1/\alpha}(u-{\rm med}(u))^{*}(s)\big)\,ds\leq\int_{\Omega}A(|\nabla u|)\,dx

for every
u\in V^{1}K^{A}(\Omega)
.

A linear trace operator can be defined on any Orlicz-Sobolev space
W^{1,A}(\Omega)
under suitable regularity assumptions on
\Omega
. Minimal conditions on a bounded open set
\Omega
, which allow for the definition of a trace operator, are:

\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\partial\Omega)<\infty,\quad\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\partial\Omega\setminus\partial^{M}\Omega)=0,

and

\min\{\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\partial^{M}E\cap\partial\Omega)\,,\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\partial\Omega\setminus\partial^{M}E)\}\leq\kappa_{7}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\partial^{M}E\cap\Omega)

for some positive constant
\kappa_{7}>0
and every measurable set
E\subset\Omega
. An open set fulfilling these properties will be called an
admissible domain
in what follows.
In particular, any Lipschitz
domain is an admissible domain. In turn, any admissible domain belongs to the class
\mathcal{J}_{1/{n^{\prime}}}

The assumption
that
\Omega
be an admissible domain is necessary and sufficient
for the existence of a linear trace operator
{\rm Tr}:W^{1,1}(\Omega)\to L^{1}(\partial\Omega)
– see
[
Zi
, Section 5.10]
or
[
Ma2
, Theorem
9.5.2]
.

The Young function
A_{T}
defining the optimal Orlicz target space in Orlicz-Sobolev trace embeddings was exhibited in
[
Ci5
]
, and is given by

(2.35)

A_{T}(t)=\int_{0}^{t}\frac{A(H_{n}^{-1}(\tau))}{H_{n}^{-1}(\tau)}\,d\tau\quad\text{for $t\geq 0$,}

where
H_{n}
is as in (
2.18
) and, without loss of generality, the function
A
is assumed to fulfill condition (
2.19
). From
[
Ci5
, Theorem 3.1-(i)]
, we have that

(2.36)

\operatorname{Tr}:W^{1,A}(\Omega)\to L^{A_{T}}(\partial\Omega),

the space
L^{A_{T}}(\partial\Omega)
being optimal in (
2.36
) among all Orlicz target spaces.
Moreover, there exists a positive constant
\kappa_{8}=\kappa_{8}(\Omega)
such that

(2.37)

\int_{\partial\Omega}A_{T}\left(\frac{\kappa_{8}|\operatorname{Tr}u-{\rm med}(u)|}{\big(\int_{\Omega}A(|\nabla u|)\,dy\big)^{1/n}}\right)d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\leq\left(\int_{\Omega}A(|\nabla u|)\,dx\right)^{{1}/{n^{\prime}}}

for every
u\in V^{1}K^{A}(\Omega)
.

In particular, under assumption (
2.23
), one has that
L^{A_{T}}(\partial\Omega)=L^{\infty}(\partial\Omega)
, up to equivalent norms.

Let us notice that although
[
Ci5
, Theorem 3.1-(i)]
is stated for Lipschitz domains
\Omega
, it continues to hold for any admissible domain. Indeed, its proof makes use of an interpolation argument which relies upon endpoint boundedness properties of the operator
{\rm Tr}
, which are still enjoyed on any admissible domain.

3.
Main results

Our results about Dirichlet, Neumann and Robin problems for equation (
1.1
) are presented in Subsections
3.1
,
3.2
and
3.3
, respectively. Each result is illustrated by an example involving equations whose principal part has a power-times-logarithmic type growth.

3.1.
Dirichlet problems

Let
\Omega
be an open set in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, with
|\Omega|<\infty
, and let
u_{0}\in V^{1}K^{A}(\Omega)
. Consider the Dirichlet problem

(3.1)

\begin{cases}-\divergenz\big(\mathcal{A}(x,u,\nabla u)\big)=\mathcal{B}(x,u,\nabla u)\quad&\text{in }\Omega\\
u=u_{0}\quad&\text{on }\partial\Omega,\end{cases}

under the growth conditions (
1.2
) and (
1.3
).
A function
u\in V^{1}K^{A}(\Omega)
is said to be a weak solution to problem (
3.1
) if
u-u_{0}\in V_{0}^{1}K^{A}(\Omega)
and

(3.2)

\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{A}(x,u,\nabla u)\cdot\nabla\phi\;dx=\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{B}(x,u,\nabla u)\,\phi\;dx

for every
\phi\in V_{0}^{1}K^{A}(\Omega)
.

Our assumptions ensuring the boundedness of any solution to problem (
3.1
) read as follows.
The boundary datum
u_{0}
is required to fulfill the condition

(3.3)

u_{0}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega).

As far as the functions
B,C,D,E
appearing in equations (
1.2
) and (
1.3
) are concerned, we assume that

(3.4)

A\circ E^{-1}\quad\text{ is a Young function,}

and
there exist constants
\sigma>0
,
k>1
and
t_{0}>0
such that

(3.5)

B(t)\leq A_{n}(\sigma t)\quad\text{for $t>t_{0}$,}

(3.6)

C(t)\leq\frac{A_{n}(\sigma t)}{t}\quad\text{for $t>t_{0}$,}

(3.7)

D(t)\leq\frac{1}{kt}\left(\left(A\circ E^{-1}\right)^{\sim}\right)^{-1}\circ A_{n}(\sigma t)\quad\text{for $t>t_{0}$,}

where
A_{n}
is the Sobolev conjugate of
A
defined by (
2.20
).

Finally, we require that

(3.8)

f\in L^{M,\infty}(\Omega)

for some Young function
M
growing so fast near infinity that

(3.9)

\displaystyle\int_{0}A^{-1}\Bigg(\frac{1}{s}\left(\int_{0}^{s}\widetilde{A}\big(\lambda r^{1/n}M^{-1}(1/r)\big)dr+\int_{s}^{\infty}\widetilde{A}\big(\lambda r^{-1/{n^{\prime}}}sM^{-1}(1/s)\big)dr\right)\Bigg)\frac{ds}{s^{1/{n^{\prime}}}}<\infty

for every
\lambda>0
.

Let us emphasize that conditions (
3.5
)–(
3.9
) depend only on the asymptotic behaviour of the functions
A,B,C,D,E,M
and
A_{n}
near infinity.

In view of the Sobolev embedding (
2.24
), it suffices to consider the case when the Young function
A
fulfills condition (
2.25
), otherwise any weak solution to problem (
3.1
) is automatically bounded.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
\Omega
be an open set in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with finite measure. Suppose that the Young function
A
satisfies condition (
2.25
) and that assumptions (
3.3
)–(
3.8
) are in force. If
u
is a weak solution to the Dirichlet problem (
3.1
), then,
u\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
.

Remark 3.2
.

Under the additional assumption that
A\in\nabla_{2}
near infinity, inequality (
3.9
) holds for every
\lambda>0
if and only if it just holds for some
\lambda>0
. This is a consequence of property (
2.8
) and inequality (
2.2
).

Remark 3.3
.

A change of variables shows that

(3.10)

\int_{s}^{\infty}\widetilde{A}\big(\lambda r^{-\frac{1}{n^{\prime}}}sM^{-1}(1/s)\big)dr=n^{\prime}s^{n^{\prime}}M^{-1}(1/s)^{n^{\prime}}\int_{0}^{s^{\frac{1}{n}}M^{-1}(1/s)}\frac{\widetilde{A}(\lambda r)}{r^{1+n^{\prime}}}\,dr\quad\text{for $s>0$.}

The function on the right-hand side of equation (
3.10
) is usually handier than the one on the left-hand side in verifying condition (
3.9
).

Example 3.4
.

Assume that
A
is a Young function such that

(3.11)

A(t)\approx t^{p}(\log t)^{\delta}\quad\text{near infinity,}

where either
p>1
and
\delta\in\mathbb{R}
, or
p=1
and
\delta\geq 0
.

Assumption (
2.25
) amounts to requiring that

(3.12)

either
p<n
, or
p=n
and
\delta\leq n-1
.

One can verify that

A_{n}(t)\approx\begin{cases}t^{\frac{np}{n-p}}(\log t)^{\frac{n\delta}{n-p}}&\ \text{ if }\ 1\leq p<n\\
e^{t^{\frac{n}{n-1-\delta}}}&\ \text{ if }\ p=n\ \text{ and }\ \delta<n-1\\
e^{e^{t^{\frac{n}{n-1}}}}&\ \text{ if }\ p=n\ \text{ and }\ \delta=n-1\end{cases}\qquad\text{near infinity.}

Assume that the functions
B
and
C
, appearing in conditions (
1.2
) and (
1.3
), fulfill the inequalities

(3.13)

B(\sigma t)\leq\begin{cases}t^{\frac{np}{n-p}}(\log t)^{\frac{n\delta}{n-p}}&\ \text{ if }\ 1\leq p<n\\
e^{t^{\frac{n}{n-1-\delta}}}&\ \text{ if }\ p=n\ \text{ and }\ \delta<n-1\\
e^{e^{t^{\frac{n}{n-1}}}}&\ \text{ if }\ p=n\ \text{ and }\ \delta=n-1\end{cases}\qquad\text{near infinity,}\

and

(3.14)

C(\sigma t)\leq\begin{cases}t^{\frac{np}{n-p}-1}(\log t)^{\frac{n\delta}{n-p}}&\ \text{ if }\ 1\leq p<n\\
e^{t^{\frac{n}{n-1-\delta}}}&\ \text{ if }\ p=n\ \text{ and }\ \delta<n-1\\
e^{e^{t^{\frac{n}{n-1}}}}&\ \text{ if }\ p=n\ \text{ and }\ \delta=n-1\end{cases}\qquad\text{near infinity,}

for some constant
\sigma>0
. Also, suppose, for simplicity, that
D=E=0
.

Let
f\in L^{M,\infty}(\Omega)
, where
M
is a Young function such that

(3.15)

M(t)\approx t^{q}(\log t)^{\beta}\quad\text{near infinity,}

and either
q>1
and
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
, or
q=1
and
\beta>0
.

By Theorem
3.1
,
any weak solution
u
to the Dirichlet problem (
3.1
) is bounded, provided that, in addition, condition (
3.9
) is satisfied.
Since

\widetilde{A}(t)\approx t^{p^{\prime}}(\log t)^{-\frac{\delta}{p-1}},\qquad A^{-1}(t)\approx t^{1/p}(\log t)^{-\delta/p},\qquad M^{-1}(t)\approx t^{1/q}(\log t)^{-\beta/q}\quad\text{near infinity,}

this condition corresponds to demanding that

\begin{cases}\displaystyle\text{either }q>\frac{n}{p},\\
\text{ or }\,\,\displaystyle q=\frac{n}{p}>1\,\,\text{ and }\,\,\beta p+n\delta>n(p-1),\\
\text{ or }\,\,\displaystyle q=\frac{n}{p}=1,\,\delta\leq n-1\,\,\text{and}\,\,\beta+\delta>n-\frac{1}{n}.\\
\end{cases}

3.2.
Neumann problems

Given a bounded open set
\Omega
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, consider the Neumann problem

(3.16)

\begin{cases}-\divergenz\big(\mathcal{A}(x,u,\nabla u)\big)=\mathcal{B}(x,u,\nabla u)\quad&\text{in }\Omega\\
\mathcal{A}(x,u,\nabla u)\cdot\nu=0\quad&\text{on $\partial\Omega$,}\end{cases}

under the growth conditions (
1.2
) and (
1.3
).
A function
u\in V^{1}K^{A}(\Omega)
is said to be a weak solution to problem (
3.16
) if

(3.17)

\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{A}(x,u,\nabla u)\cdot\nabla\phi\;dx=\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{B}(x,u,\nabla u)\,\phi\;dx

for every
\phi\in V^{1}K^{A}(\Omega)
.

The conditions to be imposed on the functions appearing on the right-hand sides of equations (
1.2
) and (
1.3
) depend on the degree of regularity of the domain
\Omega
. The latter is assumed to satisfy

\Omega\in\mathcal{J}_{1/\alpha^{\prime}}

for some exponent
\alpha\geq n
. Here,
\mathcal{J}_{1/\alpha^{\prime}}
denotes the
Maz’ya class defined according to inequality (
2.28
).

Besides hypothesis (
3.4
), the functions
B,C,D,E
are requested to fulfill bounds parallel to (
3.5
)–(
3.7
), with the function
A_{n}
replaced by the Sobolev conjugate
A_{\alpha}
entering embedding (
2.30
). Namely:

(3.18)

B(t)\leq A_{\alpha}(\sigma t)\quad\text{for $t>t_{0}$,}

(3.19)

C(t)\leq\frac{A_{\alpha}(\sigma t)}{t}\quad\text{for $t>t_{0}$,}

(3.20)

D(t)\leq\frac{1}{kt}\left(\left(A\circ E^{-1}\right)^{\sim}\right)^{-1}\circ A_{\alpha}(\sigma t)\quad\text{for $t>t_{0}$,}

for some constants
\sigma>0
,
k>1
and
t_{0}\geq 0
.

Similarly, condition (
3.9
) on the Young function
M
defining an admissible ambient weak Orlicz space
L^{M,\infty}(\Omega)
for
f
turns into

(3.21)

\displaystyle\int_{0}A^{-1}\Bigg(\frac{1}{s}\left(\int_{0}^{s}\widetilde{A}\big(\lambda r^{1/\alpha}M^{-1}(1/r)\big)dr+\int_{s}^{\infty}\widetilde{A}\big(\lambda r^{-1/{\alpha^{\prime}}}sM^{-1}(1/s)\big)dr\right)\Bigg)\frac{ds}{s^{1/\alpha^{\prime}}}<\infty

for every
\lambda>0
.

Owing to embedding (
2.32
), which holds under assumption (
2.33
), in our result on Neumann problems it suffices to focus on the case when the function
A
fulfills the complementary condition (
2.13
).

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
\Omega
be an open bounded set in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
from the class
\mathcal{J}_{1/\alpha^{\prime}}
, for some
\alpha\geq n
. Let
A
be a Young function satisfying condition (
2.13
). Suppose that assumptions (
3.4
) and (
3.18
)–(
3.20
) are in force, and that condition (
3.8
) holds for some Young function
M
satisfying (
3.21
). If
u
is a weak solution to the Neumann problem (
3.16
), then,
u\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
.

Remark 3.6
.

Observations parallel to Remarks
3.2
and
3.3
apply with regard to
Theorem
3.5
.
In particular, the identity

(3.22)

\int_{s}^{\infty}\widetilde{A}\big(\lambda r^{-1/{\alpha^{\prime}}}sM^{-1}(1/s)\big)dr=\alpha^{\prime}s^{\alpha^{\prime}}M^{-1}(1/s)^{\alpha^{\prime}}\int_{0}^{s^{1/{\alpha}}M^{-1}(1/s)}\frac{\widetilde{A}(\lambda r)}{r^{1+\alpha^{\prime}}}\,dr\quad\text{for $s>0$}

can be of use in connection with condition (
3.21
).

Example 3.7
.

Assume that
A
is a Young function as in (
3.11
). In view of condition (
2.33
), assumptions (
3.12
) have to be replaced by

either
p=1
and
\delta\geq 0
, or
1<p<\alpha
, or
p=\alpha
and
\delta\leq\alpha-1
.

Moreover,

A_{\alpha}(t)\approx\begin{cases}t^{\frac{\alpha p}{\alpha-p}}(\log t)^{\frac{\alpha\delta}{\alpha-p}}&\ \text{ if }\ 1\leq p<\alpha\\

e^{e^{t^{\frac{\alpha}{\alpha-1}}}}&\ \text{ if }\ p=\alpha\ \text{ and }\ \delta=\alpha-1\end{cases}\qquad\text{near infinity.}

Assume that the functions
B
and

in conditions (
1.2
) and (

) satisfy the inequalities

B(\sigma t)\leq\begin{cases}t^{\frac{\alpha p}{\alpha-p}}(\log t)^{\frac{\alpha\delta}{\alpha-p}}&\ \text{ if }\ 1\leq p<\alpha\\

e^{e^{t^{\frac{\alpha}{\alpha-1}}}}&\ \text{ if }\ p=\alpha\ \text{ and }\ \delta=\alpha-1\end{cases}\quad\text{near infinity,}

and

C(\sigma t)\leq\begin{cases}t^{\frac{\alpha p}{\alpha-p}-1}(\log t)^{\frac{\alpha\delta}{\alpha-p}}&\ \text{ if }\ 1\leq p<\alpha\\
e^{t^{\frac{\alpha}{\alpha-1-\delta}}}&\ \text{ if }\ p=\alpha\ \text{ and }\ \delta<\alpha-1\\
e^{e^{t^{\frac{\alpha}{\alpha-1}}}}&\ \text{ if }\ p=\alpha\ \text{ and }\ \delta=\alpha-1\end{cases}\quad\text{near infinity,}

for some constant

. Moreover, let

.

Let
f\in L^{M,\infty}(\Omega)
, where
M
is a function obeying (

).

By Theorem

, in view of condition (

) any weak solution
u
to problem (

) is bounded provided that

\begin{cases}\displaystyle\text{either }q>\frac{\alpha}{p},\\

\end{cases}

3.3.
Robin problems

Assume now that
\Omega
is an admissible domain in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, as defined in Section
2
.
Here we focus on the Robin problem

(3.23)

\begin{cases}-\divergenz\big(\mathcal{A}(x,u,\nabla u)\big)=\mathcal{B}(x,u,\nabla u)\quad&\text{in }\Omega\\
\mathcal{A}(x,u,\nabla u)\cdot\nu=\mathcal{C}(x,u)\quad&\text{on $\partial\Omega$,}\end{cases}

under the growth conditions (
1.2
), (
1.3
) and (
1.5
).
A function
u\in V^{1}K^{A}(\Omega)
is said to be a weak solution to problem (
3.23
) if

(3.24)

\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{A}(x,u,\nabla u)\cdot\nabla\phi\;dx=\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{B}(x,u,\nabla u)\,\phi\;dx+\int_{\partial\Omega}\mathcal{C}(x,u)\,\phi\;d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}

for every
\phi\in V^{1}K^{A}(\Omega)
. For simplicity of notation, here, and in what follows, we just write
u
instead of
{\rm Tr}\,u
in integrals over
\partial\Omega
.

Our boundedness criterion for solutions to problem (
3.23
) requires the same assumptions on the functions
B,C,D,E,M
as those for the Dirichlet problem (
3.1
) and, of course, also entails bounds in a similar vein for the functions
F
and
g
involved in condition (
1.5
). The maximal admissible growth of the function
F
depends on the Sobolev trace conjugate
A_{T}
of
A
, defined by (
2.35
), and reads

(3.25)

F(t)\leq\frac{A_{T}(\sigma t)}{t}\quad\text{for $t>t_{0}$,}

for some constants
\sigma>0
and
t_{0}\geq 0
.
The integrability condition on the function
g
takes the form

(3.26)

g\in L^{N,\infty}(\partial\Omega),

for some Young function
N
such that

(3.27)

\int_{0}A^{-1}\bigg(\frac{1}{s^{n^{\prime}}}\int_{0}^{s}\widetilde{A}\bigg(\frac{1}{r}\int_{0}^{r}N^{-1}(1/\rho)\,d\rho\bigg)r^{\frac{1}{n-1}}\,dr\bigg)\frac{ds}{s^{\frac{n-2}{n-1}}}<\infty.

Finally, unlike the results for Dirichlet and Neumann problems presented above, the function
A
is required to additionally satisfy the
\nabla_{2}
-condition near infinity, which, loosely speaking, prevents
A
from having an almost linear growth.
This is due to the fact that the trace conjugate
A_{T}
of
A
boils down to
A
itself when the latter grows linearly. Indeed, classically
A_{T}=A
if
A(t)=t
for
t\geq 0
.

Theorem 3.8
.

Let
\Omega
be an admissible domain in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Assume that
A\in\nabla_{2}
near infinity and satisfies condition (
2.25
). Suppose
that assumptions (
3.3
)–(
3.8
) and (
3.25
)–(
3.26
)

4.
Proofs of Theorems

and

A detailed proof will be provided for Theorem

We begin with a technical lemma, dealing with the integrand appearing in condition (
3.21
). In preparation for its statement and proof, a couple of auxiliary functions are introduced.

Let
A
and
M
be Young functions fulfilling condition (
3.21
). Define, for
\lambda>0
, the function
\Psi_{\lambda}:(0,\infty)\to[0,\infty]
as

(4.1)

\Psi_{\lambda}(s)=\frac{1}{s}\bigg(\int_{0}^{s}\widetilde{A}\left(\lambda r^{1/\alpha}M^{-1}(1/r)\right)dr+\int_{s}^{\infty}\widetilde{A}\left(\lambda r^{-1/{\alpha^{\prime}}}sM^{-1}(1/s)\right)dr\bigg)\quad\text{for $s>0$.}

Hence, condition (
3.21
) takes the form

(4.2)

\displaystyle\int_{0}s^{-1/{\alpha^{\prime}}}A^{-1}\left(\Psi_{\lambda}(s)\right)ds<\infty

for every
\lambda>0
.
Next, let
\Phi_{\lambda}\colon[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty]
be the function defined by

(4.3)

\Phi_{\lambda}(s)=sA^{-1}\left(\Psi_{\lambda}(s)\right)\quad\text{for $s>0$.}

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
A
and
M
be Young functions fulfilling condition (
3.21
) for some
\alpha\geq n
. Assume that
M
is differentiable and

(4.4)

\Big(\frac{M(t)}{t}\Big)^{\prime}>0\qquad\text{for $t>0$.}

Let
\lambda>0
and let
\Phi_{\lambda}
be the function given by (
4.3
). Then,

(i)

\lim_{s\to 0^{+}}\Phi_{\lambda}(s)=0
;

(ii)

Either there exists
\overline{s}>0
such that
\Phi_{\lambda}
vanishes in
s\in(0,\overline{s})
, or there exists
\overline{s}>0
such that

\Phi_{\lambda}
is strictly increasing in
(0,\overline{s})
;

(iii)

Condition (
4.2
) is equivalent to

\int_{0}\frac{1}{\Phi_{\lambda}^{-1}(\tau)^{\frac{1}{\alpha^{\prime}}}}\;d\tau<\infty.

Proof.

Property (i) is a consequence of (ii) and of condition (
4.2
).
As for property (ii),
let us define the function
T_{\lambda}:(0,\infty)\to[0,\infty]
as

(4.5)

\displaystyle T_{\lambda}(s)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{s}\widetilde{A}\left(\lambda{r^{{1}/{\alpha}}}M^{-1}(1/r)\right)dr+\alpha^{\prime}s^{\alpha^{\prime}}M^{-1}(1/s)^{\alpha^{\prime}}\int_{0}^{s^{1/{\alpha}}M^{-1}(1/s)}\frac{\widetilde{A}(\lambda\varrho)}{\varrho^{1+\alpha^{\prime}}}\,d\varrho\quad\text{for $s>0$.}

By equations (
4.1
) and (
3.22
),

(4.6)

\displaystyle\Psi_{\lambda}(s)=\frac{1}{s}T_{\lambda}(s)\quad\text{for $s>0$.}

Thanks to assumption (
4.2
), there exists
s_{0}>0
such that
T_{\lambda}(s)<\infty
for
s\in(0,s_{0})
.

We claim that either
T_{\lambda}(s)>0
, or
T_{\lambda}(s)=0
for every

s\in(0,s_{1})
, for some
s_{1}\in(0,s_{0})
. Indeed, the first alternative trivially holds if
\widetilde{A}(t)>0
for every
t>0
. Assume now that there exists
t_{0}>0
such that

\widetilde{A}(t)=0
for
t\in[0,t_{0}]
and
\widetilde{A}(t)>0
for
t>t_{0}
. We may clearly assume that
s_{0}>t_{0}
. If there exists
s_{1}\in(0,t_{0})
such that

\lambda{r^{{1}/{\alpha}}}M^{-1}(1/r)\leq t_{0}\quad\text{for $r\in(0,s_{1})$,}

then, since the function
sM^{-1}(1/s)
is non-decreasing, we also have that

\lambda{r^{-{1}/{\alpha^{\prime}}}}sM^{-1}(1/s)\leq t_{0}\quad\text{for $s\in(0,s_{1})$ and $r\in(s_{1},\infty)$.}

Hence, owing to equation (
3.22
),

T_{\lambda}(s)=0
for
s\in(0,s_{1})
. On the other hand, if for every
s\in(0,t_{0})
there exists
r\in(0,s)
such that

\lambda{r^{{1}/{\alpha}}}M^{-1}(1/r)>t_{0},

then, by the continuity of the function
\lambda{r^{{1}/{\alpha}}}M^{-1}(1/r)
, the first integral on the right-hand side of equation (
4.5
) is positive, whence
T_{\lambda}(s)>0
for
s\in(0,t_{0})
.
Inasmuch as
\Phi_{\lambda}
vanishes if and only if
T_{\lambda}
vanishes, and
\Phi_{\lambda}=\infty
if and only if
T_{\lambda}=\infty
, we have shown that either there exists
\overline{s}>0
such that
\Phi_{\lambda}(s)=0
for
s\in(0,\overline{s})
, or there exists
\overline{s}>0
such that
0<\Phi_{\lambda}(s)<\infty
for
s\in(0,\overline{s})
.

In order to show that, in the latter case,
\Phi_{\lambda}
is strictly increasing, it suffices to show that

(4.7)

\Phi^{\prime}_{\lambda}(s)>0\quad\text{for $s\in(0,\overline{s})$.}

To this purpose, observe that

(4.8)

\displaystyle T_{\lambda}^{\prime}(s)

\displaystyle=\widetilde{A}\left(\lambda{s^{{1}/{\alpha}}}M^{-1}(1/s)\right)+\alpha^{\prime}\frac{s^{{1}/{\alpha^{\prime}}}}{M^{-1}(1/s)}\widetilde{A}\left(\lambda{s^{{1}/{\alpha}}}M^{-1}(1/s)\right)\Big(\frac{1}{\alpha}s^{-{1}/{\alpha^{\prime}}}M^{-1}(1/s)+s^{1/\alpha}\big(M^{-1}(1/s)\big)^{\prime}\Big)

\displaystyle\quad+\alpha^{\prime}\Big(s^{\alpha^{\prime}}M^{-1}(1/s)^{\alpha^{\prime}}\big)^{\prime}\int_{0}^{s^{1/{\alpha}}M^{-1}(1/s)}\frac{\widetilde{A}(\lambda\varrho)}{\varrho^{1+\alpha^{\prime}}}\,d\varrho

\displaystyle=\alpha^{\prime}\widetilde{A}\left(\lambda{s^{{1}/{\alpha}}}M^{-1}(1/s)\right)+\alpha^{\prime}\frac{s\big(M^{-1}(1/s)\big)^{\prime}}{M^{-1}(1/s)}\widetilde{A}\left(\lambda{s^{{1}/{\alpha}}}M^{-1}(1/s)\right)

\displaystyle\quad+\alpha^{\prime}\Big(s^{\alpha^{\prime}}M^{-1}(1/s)^{\alpha^{\prime}}\Big)^{\prime}\int_{0}^{s^{1/{\alpha}}M^{-1}(1/s)}\frac{\widetilde{A}(\lambda\varrho)}{\varrho^{1+\alpha^{\prime}}}\,d\varrho\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\text{for $s\in(0,\overline{s})$.}

On the other hand,

\displaystyle\frac{s\big(M^{-1}(1/s)\big)^{\prime}}{M^{-1}(1/s)}=\frac{-1/s\big(M^{-1}\big)^{\prime}(1/s)}{M^{-1}(1/s)}\geq-1\quad\text{for $s>0$,}

where the inequality holds since
M^{-1}:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
is a concave function. Moreover, thanks to assumption (
4.4
),

(4.9)

\Big(s^{\alpha^{\prime}}M^{-1}(1/s)^{\alpha^{\prime}}\Big)^{\prime}>0\quad\text{for $s\in(0,\overline{s})$.}

Combining inequalities (
4.8
)–(
4.9
) tells us that

(4.10)

T_{\lambda}^{\prime}(s)>0\qquad\text{for $s\in(0,\overline{s})$.}

Next, set

\mathcal{E}=\{s\in(0,\overline{s}):\,\Psi_{\lambda}^{\prime}(s)=0\}.

The set
\mathcal{E}
is closed, inasmuch as the function
\Psi_{\lambda}
is continuously differentiable. Hence, the set
(0,\overline{s})\setminus\mathcal{E}
is open and therefore there exists a set
K\subset\mathbb{N}
such that

(0,\overline{s})\setminus\mathcal{E}=\bigcup_{i\in K}(a_{i},b_{i}),

and
K=I\cup J
, where the sets
I
and
J
are such that
\Psi_{\lambda}^{\prime}>0
in
(a_{i},b_{i})
for
i\in I
, and
\Psi_{\lambda}^{\prime}<0
in
(a_{i},b_{i})
for
i\in J
.

If
s\in(a_{i},b_{i})
with

, then inequality (
4.7
) is a straightforward consequence of definition (
4.3
).

If
s\in(a_{i},b_{i})
with

, then coupling (
4.3
) with (

) tells us that

\Phi_{\lambda}(s)=\frac{A^{-1}(\Psi_{\lambda}(s))}{\Psi_{\lambda}(s)}T_{\lambda}(s),

whence inequality (
4.7
) follows, thanks to equation (

) and property (
2.1
).

Suppose now that

. Differentiating the right-hand side of equation (
4.3
) yields

\Phi_{\lambda}^{\prime}(s)=A^{-1}(\Psi_{\lambda}(s)),

thus establishing inequality (
4.7
) also in this case.

Finally, assertion (iii) is a consequence of the following chain, which relies upon Fubini’s theorem and a change of variables:

(4.11)

\displaystyle\int_{0}\frac{d\tau}{(\Phi_{\lambda}^{-1}(\tau))^{1/\alpha^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}\left(\alpha^{\prime}\int_{\Phi_{\lambda}^{-1}(\tau)}^{\infty}s^{-\frac{1}{\alpha^{\prime}}-1}\;ds\right)d\tau=\int_{0}s^{-\frac{1}{\alpha^{\prime}}-1}\left(\int_{0}^{\Phi_{\lambda}(s)}d\tau\right)ds

\displaystyle=\int_{0}s^{-\frac{1}{\alpha^{\prime}}-1}\Phi_{\lambda}(s)\,ds=\int_{0}s^{-\frac{1}{\alpha^{\prime}}}A^{-1}(\Psi_{\lambda}(s))\;ds.

∎

We are now in a position to prove Theorem
3.5
.

5.
A Sobolev trace inequality in Lorentz
\Lambda
-spaces

This section is devoted to a Sobolev trace inequality in Lorentz
\Lambda
-spaces to be employed in the proof of Theorem
3.8
on Robin problems. This is the content of Theorem
5.2
. We begin with some preliminary material on general rearrangement-invariant spaces and, more specifically, on Lorentz and Marcinkiewicz spaces.

Let
(\mathcal{R},m)
be a non-atomic, sigma-finite measure space. A rearrangement-invariant space
X(\mathcal{R})
on
\mathcal{R}
is a Banach function space, in the sense of Luxemburg, such that

\|u\|_{X(\mathcal{R})}=\|v\|_{X(\mathcal{R})}

whenever
u,v\in{\mathcal{M}}(\mathcal{R})
satisfy the equality
u^{*}=v^{*}
.
We refer to the monograph
[
BeSh
]
for a comprehensive treatment of the theory of rearrangement-invariant spaces.

The associate space of
X(\mathcal{R})
is denoted by
X^{\prime}(\mathcal{R})
. The latter is a kind of measure theoretic dual of
X(\mathcal{R})
, and enters a Hölder type inequality, since its norm is given by

\|u\|_{X^{\prime}(\mathbb{R})}=\sup_{v\in X(\Omega)}\frac{\int_{\mathcal{R}}uv\,dm}{\|v\|_{X(\Omega)}}.

In particular,
{(X^{\prime})^{\prime}(\mathcal{R})}=X(\mathcal{R})
.

The representation space
\overline{X}(\mathcal{R})
of
X(\mathcal{R})
is a rearrangement-invariant space on the interval
(0,m(\mathcal{R}))
, endowed with the Lebesgue measure. It has the property that

\|u\|_{X(\mathcal{R})}=\|u^{*}\|_{\overline{X}(0,m(\mathcal{R}))}

for every function
u\in X(\mathcal{R})
.

Lebesgue, Orlicz, Lorentz and Orlicz-Lorentz spaces are special instances of rearrangement-invariant spaces. The Lorentz and the Marcinkiewicz rearrangement-invariant spaces are built upon a general quasiconcave function.
In the present setting, a function
\varphi\colon[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
is called quasiconcave if it is increasing, vanishes only at
0
, and the function
\frac{\varphi(s)}{s}
is decreasing. Every quasiconcave function is equivalent, up to multiplicative constants, to a concave function
[
BeSh
, pg. 71]
. Moreover,

(5.1)

\min\{1,r\}\varphi(s)\leq\varphi(r{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}s})\leq\max\{1,r\}\varphi(s)\quad\text{for $r,s\geq 0$.}

If
\varphi
is a quasiconcave function, then the function
\widehat{\varphi}
, given by

(5.2)

\widehat{\varphi}(s)=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{s}{\varphi(s)}\quad&\text{if $s>0$}\\
0\quad&\text{if $s=0$,}\end{cases}

is also quasiconcave.

The Lorentz space
\Lambda_{\varphi}(\mathcal{R})
associated with a quasiconcave function
\varphi
is the collection of all functions
u\in{\mathcal{M}}(\mathcal{R})
making the norm

\|u\|_{\Lambda_{\varphi}(\mathcal{R})}=\int_{0}^{m(\mathcal{R})}u^{*}(s)\,d\varphi(s)

finite.

The Marcinkiewicz space
M_{\varphi}(\mathcal{R})
associated with
\varphi
is the collection of those functions
u\in\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{R})
such that the norm

\|u\|_{M_{\varphi}(\mathcal{R})}=\sup_{s\in(0,m(\mathcal{R}))}\varphi(s)u^{**}(s)

is finite.
One has that
[
Mu
]

(5.3)

\Lambda^{\prime}_{\varphi}(\mathcal{R})=M_{\widehat{\varphi}}(\mathcal{R})\quad\text{and}\quad M_{\varphi}^{\prime}(\mathcal{R})=\Lambda_{\widehat{\varphi}}(\mathcal{R}).

If
\Omega
is an open set in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we define the Sobolev type space built upon
X(\Omega)
as

W^{1}X(\Omega)=\{u\in X(\Omega):\,\text{$u$ is weakly differentiable and $|\nabla u|\in X(\Omega)$}\}.

Lemma 5.1
.

Assume that
\Omega
is an open set in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
\Omega\in\mathcal{J}_{1/{\alpha^{\prime}}}
for some
\alpha\geq n
.
Let
X(\Omega)
be a rearrangement-invariant space.
Then, there exists a positive constant
\kappa_{9}=\kappa_{9}(\Omega)>0
such that

(5.4)

\|u-{\rm med}(u)\|_{X(\Omega)}\leq\kappa_{9}\|\nabla u\|_{X(\Omega)}

for every function

u\in W^{1}X(\Omega)
.

Proof.

Let
u
be as in the statement. Owing to
[
CEG
, Lemma 6.6]
, its signed decreasing rearrangement
u^{\circ}
is locally absolutely continuous in
(0,|\Omega|)
. Moreover,

u^{\circ}\left(\frac{|\Omega|}{2}\right)={\rm med}(u)
. Hence,

u^{\circ}(s)-{\rm med}(u)=\int_{s}^{\frac{|\Omega|}{2}}-(u^{\circ})^{\prime}(r)\,dr\quad\text{for $s\in(0,|\Omega|)$.}

Let
T
be the linear operator defined as

Th(s)=\int_{s}^{\frac{|\Omega|}{2}}h(r)\min\{r,|\Omega|-r\}^{-1/{\alpha^{\prime}}}\,dr\quad\text{for $s\in(0,|\Omega|)$,}

for an integrable function
h:(0,|\Omega|)\to\mathbb{R}
. One can verify that
T
is bounded from
L^{1}(0,|\Omega|)
into
L^{1}(0,|\Omega|)
and from
L^{\infty}(0,|\Omega|)
into
L^{\infty}(0,|\Omega|)

|\Omega|
and on
\alpha

[
BeSh

ensures that it is also bounded from
\overline{X}(0,|\Omega|)
into
\overline{X}(0,|\Omega|)
, with the same dependence of the norm. Thus, there exists a constant
c=c(|\Omega|,\alpha)
such that

(5.5)

\|u^{\circ}-{\rm med}(u)\|_{\overline{X}(0,|\Omega|)}\leq c\|-(u^{\circ})^{\prime}(r)\min\{r,|\Omega|-r\}^{1/{\alpha^{\prime}}}\|_{\overline{X}(0,|\Omega|)}.

On the other hand, since
\Omega\in\mathcal{J}_{1/{\alpha^{\prime}}}
,
[
CiPi
, Lemma 4.1-(ii)]
ensures that

(5.6)

\|-(u^{\circ})^{\prime}(r)\min\{r,|\Omega|-r\}^{1/{\alpha^{\prime}}}\|_{\overline{X}(0,|\Omega|)}\leq c\|\nabla u\|_{X(\Omega)}

for some constant
c=c(\Omega)
. Inequality (
5.4
) follows from (
5.5
) and (
5.6
), owing to the fact that

\|u^{\circ}-{\rm med}(u)\|_{X(\Omega)}=\|u^{\circ}-{\rm med}(u)\|_{\overline{X}(0,|\Omega|)}.

∎

Assume that
\Omega

\mathbb{R}^{n}

(5.7)

\displaystyle\|u\|_{Y^{\prime}(\partial\Omega)}=\|u^{**}_{\mathcal{H}^{n-1}}(\gamma_{\Omega}s^{{1}/{n^{\prime}}})\|_{\overline{X}^{\prime}(0,|\Omega|)}

for a function

, where

. Here, the notation

means that the rearrangement of

Then

The general embedding (

Given a non-increasing function

The proof of Theorem

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
\varphi
be any quasiconcave function. Assume that

(5.13)

\sup_{s\in(0,\gamma\ell^{1/n^{\prime}})}h^{**}(s)\varphi(\gamma^{-n^{\prime}}s^{n^{\prime}})\leq\sup_{s\in(0,\ell)}\frac{\varphi(s)}{s}\int_{0}^{s}h^{**}(\gamma r^{\frac{1}{n^{\prime}}})\,dr\,\leq n\sup_{s\in(0,\gamma\ell^{1/n^{\prime}})}h^{**}(s)\varphi(\gamma^{-n^{\prime}}s^{n^{\prime}}).

Proof.

After rescaling, we may assume that

) will thus follow if we show that

for every

The first inequality in (

) holds since the function
h^{**}(s^{1/n^{\prime}})
is non-increasing.

The second inequality is a consequence of the following chain:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{s}\int_{0}^{s}h^{**}(r^{1/n^{\prime}})\;dr

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{s}\int_{0}^{s}\frac{1}{r^{1/n^{\prime}}}\biggl(\int_{0}^{r^{1/n^{\prime}}}h^{*}(\rho)\;d\rho\biggr)dr=\frac{n^{\prime}}{s}\int_{0}^{s^{1/n^{\prime}}}\varsigma^{\frac{1}{n-1}-1}\left(\int_{0}^{\varsigma}h^{*}(\rho)\;d\rho\right)d\varsigma

\displaystyle=\frac{n^{\prime}}{s}\int_{0}^{s^{1/n^{\prime}}}\biggl(\int_{\rho}^{s^{1/n^{\prime}}}\varsigma^{\frac{1}{n-1}-1}\;d\varsigma\biggr)h^{*}(\rho)\;d\rho=\frac{n}{s}\int_{0}^{s^{1/n^{\prime}}}\left(s^{1/n}-\rho^{1/(n-1)}\right)h^{*}(\rho)\;d\rho

\displaystyle\leq\frac{n}{s}\int_{0}^{s^{1/n^{\prime}}}s^{1/n}h^{*}(\rho)\;d\rho=ns^{1/n-1}\int_{0}^{s^{1/n^{\prime}}}h^{*}(\rho)\;d\rho=nh^{**}(s^{1/n^{\prime}})\quad\text{for $s>0$.}

Lemma 6.2

\displaystyle\inf_{t>0}\frac{tA_{T}^{\prime}(t)}{A_{T}(t)}

\displaystyle=\inf_{t>0}\frac{t\frac{A(H_{n}^{-1}(t))}{H_{n}^{-1}(t)}}{\int_{0}^{t}\frac{A(H_{n}^{-1}(\theta))}{H_{n}^{-1}(\theta)}\,d\theta}=\inf_{\tau>0}\frac{\frac{A(\tau)H_{n}(\tau)}{\tau}}{\int_{0}^{H(\tau)}\frac{A(H_{n}^{-1}(\theta))}{H_{n}^{-1}(\theta)}\,d\theta}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\frac{A(\tau)}{\tau^{1+\varepsilon}}\frac{d}{d\tau}\left(H_{n}(\tau)^{n^{\prime}}\right)\tau^{\varepsilon}H_{n}(\tau)^{-\frac{1}{n-1}}\,d\tau

\displaystyle\leq\frac{A(t)}{t^{1+\varepsilon}}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{d}{d\tau}\left(H_{n}(\tau)^{n^{\prime}}\right)H_{n}(\tau)^{-\frac{1}{n-1}}\,\tau^{\varepsilon}\,d\tau

\displaystyle=n^{\prime}\frac{A(t)}{t^{1+\varepsilon}}\int_{0}^{t}\tau^{\varepsilon}H_{n}^{\prime}(\tau)\;d\tau

\displaystyle=n^{\prime}\frac{A(t)}{t^{1+\varepsilon}}\left(H_{n}(t)t^{\varepsilon}-\varepsilon\int_{0}^{t}H_{n}(\tau)\tau^{\varepsilon-1}\,d\tau\right)\quad\text{for $t>0$.}

\text{the function }H_{n}(\tau)\tau^{\varepsilon-1}\text{ is decreasing}.

\begin{split}\frac{d}{d\tau}\left(\left(H_{n}(\tau)\tau^{\varepsilon-1}\right)^{n^{\prime}}\right)&=\frac{d}{d\tau}\left(\frac{1}{\tau^{n^{\prime}(1-\varepsilon)}}\int_{0}^{\tau}\left(\frac{\theta}{A(\theta)}\right)^{\frac{1}{n-1}}d\theta\right)\\

2.1
).

\int_{0}^{t}H_{n}(\tau)\tau^{\varepsilon-1}d\tau\geq H(t)t^{\varepsilon}\quad\text{for $t>0$.}

whence

\displaystyle(|\nabla u|\chi_{\Omega_{t}})^{*}(r)\,r^{-1/n^{\prime}}\int_{0}^{r^{1/n^{\prime}}}N^{-1}(1/\rho)\;d\rho\,dr

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{|\Omega|}(|\nabla u|\chi_{\Omega_{t}})^{*}(r)\,\chi_{[0,\mu(t)]}(r)\,r^{-1/n^{\prime}}\int_{0}^{r^{1/n^{\prime}}}N^{-1}(1/\rho)\;d\rho\,dr

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon\int_{0}^{|\Omega|}A((|\nabla u|\chi_{\Omega_{t}})^{*})\,dr+\varepsilon\int_{0}^{|\Omega|}\widetilde{A}\left(\chi_{[0,\mu(t)]}(r)\frac{r^{-1/n^{\prime}}}{\varepsilon}\int_{0}^{r^{1/n^{\prime}}}N^{-1}(1/\rho)\;d\rho\right)dr

\displaystyle=\varepsilon\int_{\Omega_{t}}A(|\nabla u|)\,dx+\varepsilon\int_{0}^{\mu(t)}\widetilde{A}\left(\frac{r^{-1/n^{\prime}}}{\varepsilon}\int_{0}^{r^{1/n^{\prime}}}N^{-1}(1/\rho)\;d\rho\right)dr

\displaystyle=\varepsilon\int_{\Omega_{t}}A(|\nabla u|)\,dx+\varepsilon n^{\prime}\int_{\mu(t)^{-1/n^{\prime}}}^{\infty}\widetilde{A}\left(\frac{\varsigma}{\varepsilon}\int_{0}^{1/\varsigma}N^{-1}(1/\rho)\;d\rho\right)\frac{d\varsigma}{\varsigma^{n^{\prime}+1}}.

Coupling equation (
6.17
) with (
6.18
) yields

(6.19)

\displaystyle\int_{E_{t}}g(x)(|u|-t)\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}

\displaystyle\leq\kappa_{12}\varepsilon\|g\|_{L^{N,\infty}(\partial\Omega)}\left[\int_{\Omega_{t}}A(|\nabla u|)\,dx+n^{\prime}\int_{\mu(t)^{-1/n^{\prime}}}^{\infty}\widetilde{A}\left(\frac{r}{\varepsilon}\int_{0}^{1/r}N^{-1}(1/\rho)\;d\rho\right)\frac{dr}{r^{n^{\prime}+1}}\right].

The second integral on the right-hand side of inequality (
6.16
) can be bounded as follows.
Thanks to assumption (
3.25
),

(6.20)

\displaystyle\int_{E_{t}}F(|u|)(|u|-t)\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\leq\int_{E_{t}}\frac{A_{T}(\sigma|u|)}{|u|}(|u|-t)\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{E_{t}}A_{T}(2\sigma(|u|-t))\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{E_{t}}\frac{A_{T}(2\sigma t)}{t}(|u|-t)\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{E_{t}}A_{T}\left(\frac{2\sigma\left(\int_{\Omega_{t}}A(|\nabla u|)\,dx\right)^{1/n}(|u|-t)}{\left(\int_{\Omega_{t}}A(|\nabla u|)\,dx\right)^{1/n}}\right)d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{E_{t}}\frac{A_{T}(2\sigma t)}{t}(|u|-t)\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}.

By Lemma
6.3
, for every
\varepsilon>0
there exists
\delta>0
such that
A_{T}(\theta\tau)<\varepsilon\theta A_{T}(\tau)
for every
\tau>0
and every
\theta\in(0,\delta)
. Let
\kappa_{8}
be the constant appearing in inequality (
2.37
). Let
t_{1}>t_{0}
be so large that

\frac{2\sigma}{\kappa_{8}}\left(\int_{\Omega_{t}}A(|\nabla u|)\,dx\right)^{1/n}<\delta.

Hence by inequality (
2.37
), if
t>t_{1}
, then

(6.21)

\displaystyle\int_{E_{t}}A_{T}

\displaystyle\left(\frac{2\sigma\left(\int_{\Omega_{t}}A(|\nabla u|)\,dx\right)^{1/n}(|u|-t)}{\left(\int_{\Omega_{t}}A(|\nabla u|)\,dx\right)^{1/n}}\right)d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{2\varepsilon\sigma}{\kappa_{8}}\left(\int_{\Omega_{t}}A(|\nabla u|)\,dx\right)^{1/n}\int_{E_{t}}A_{T}\left(\frac{\kappa_{8}(|u|-t)}{\left(\int_{\Omega_{t}}A(|\nabla u|)\,dx\right)^{1/n}}\right)d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2\varepsilon\sigma}{\kappa_{8}}\left(\int_{\Omega_{t}}A(|\nabla u|)\,dx\right)^{1/n}\left(\int_{\Omega_{t}}A(|\nabla u|)\,dx\right)^{1/n^{\prime}}=\frac{2\varepsilon\sigma}{\kappa_{8}}\int_{\Omega_{t}}A(|\nabla u|)\,dx.

Next, by inequality (
2.37
) with
A(t)=t
, which corresponds to a standard trace inequality for functions in
W^{1,1}(\Omega)
, there exists a constant
c
such that

(6.22)

\displaystyle\int_{E_{t}}\frac{A_{T}(2\sigma t)}{t}(|u|-t)\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}

\displaystyle\leq c\int_{\Omega_{t}}\frac{A_{T}(2\sigma t)}{t}|\nabla u|\,dx

\displaystyle\leq c\varepsilon\bigg(\int_{\Omega_{t}}\widetilde{A}\left(\frac{A_{T}(2\sigma t)}{\varepsilon t}\right)dx+\int_{\Omega_{t}}A(|\nabla u|)\,dx\bigg)

\displaystyle=c\varepsilon\mu(t)\widetilde{A}\left(\frac{A_{T}(2\sigma t)}{\varepsilon t}\right)+c\varepsilon\int_{\Omega_{t}}A(|\nabla u|)\,dx,

where the function
\mu
is defined as in (
4.15
). Since
A\in\nabla_{2}
,
property (
2.8
) ensures that
\widetilde{A}\in\Delta_{2}
. Hence

(6.23)

\displaystyle\widetilde{A}\left(\frac{A_{T}(2\sigma t)}{\varepsilon t}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\widetilde{A}\left(\frac{2\sigma}{\varepsilon}\frac{A(H^{-1}(2\sigma t))}{H^{-1}(2\sigma t)}\right)\leq c\widetilde{A}\left(\frac{A(H^{-1}(2\sigma t))}{H^{-1}(2\sigma t)}\right)

\displaystyle\leq cA(H^{-1}(2\sigma t))=cA_{n}(2\sigma t)\quad\text{for $t>0$,}

for some constant
c
,
where the first inequality holds by equation (
6.1
), the second one by the
\Delta_{2}
-property of
\widetilde{A}
, the third one by (
2.5
), and the equality by the very definition of the function
A_{n}
.

Combining inequalities (
6.20
)–(
6.23
) yields

(6.24)

\displaystyle\int_{E_{t}}F(|u|)(|u|-t)\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\leq\varepsilon c\int_{\Omega_{t}}A(|\nabla u|)\,dx+\varepsilon c\mu(t)A_{n}(\sigma t)\quad\text{for $t>t_{1}$,}

for some constant
c
.

From equations (
6.14
)–(
6.16
), (
6.19
), and (
6.24
) we infer that, if
\varepsilon
is chosen sufficiently small, then

(6.25)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{t}}A(|\nabla u|)\,dx

\displaystyle\leq c\Bigg\{\mu(t)A_{n}(ct)+\int_{0}^{\mu(t)}\widetilde{A}\left(r^{1/n}M^{-1}\left({1}/{r}\right)\right)dr

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{\mu(t)}^{\infty}\widetilde{A}\left(r^{-1/{n^{\prime}}}\mu(t)M^{-1}\left(1/\mu(t)\right)\right)dr+\int_{\mu(t)^{-1/n^{\prime}}}^{\infty}\widetilde{A}\left(r\int_{0}^{1/r}N^{-1}(1/\rho)\;d\rho\right)\frac{dr}{r^{n^{\prime}+1}}\Bigg\},

for
t>t_{1}
, for some constant
c
. Note that here we have again exploited the fact that
\widetilde{A}\in\Delta_{2}
.

Let
\Psi
be the function defined as in (
4.1
), with
\lambda=1
, and let

\Xi:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
be the function given by

\Xi(s)=\frac{1}{s}\int_{s^{-1/n^{\prime}}}^{\infty}\widetilde{A}\left(\frac{r}{\varepsilon}\int_{0}^{1/r}N^{-1}(1/\rho)\;d\rho\right)\frac{dr}{r^{n^{\prime}+1}}\quad\text{for $s>0$.}

Inequality (
6.25
) can be rewritten as

\frac{1}{\mu(t)}\int_{\Omega_{t}}A(|\nabla u|)\,dx\leq c\left(A_{n}(ct)+\Psi(\mu(t))+\Xi(\mu(t))\right)\quad\text{for $t>t_{1}$.}

Hence, via inequality (
4.32
) with
\alpha=n
and property (
2.2
) one obtains that

(6.26)

\int_{t}^{\infty}\mu(t)^{1/n^{\prime}}d\tau\leq\kappa_{4}\mu(t)A^{-1}\left(A_{n}(ct)+\Psi(\mu(t))+\Xi(\mu(t))\right)\quad\text{for $t>t_{1}$,}

for some constant
c
.
If
\eta
denotes the function defined as in (
4.36
), then
equation (
6.26
) in turn reads

(6.27)

\eta(t)\leq c\left(-\eta^{\prime}(t)\right)^{n^{\prime}}A^{-1}\left(A_{n}(ct)+\Psi\big((-\eta^{\prime}(t))^{n^{\prime}}\big)+\Xi\big((-\eta^{\prime}(t))^{n^{\prime}}\big)\right)\quad\text{for $t>t_{1}$.}

Now set

\displaystyle U

\displaystyle=\left\{t\geq t_{1}:\,\Psi(\mu(t))\geq\max\{A_{n}(ct),\Xi(\mu(t))\}\right\},

\displaystyle V

\displaystyle=\left\{t\geq t_{1}:\,\Xi(\mu(t))\geq\max\{A_{n}(ct),\Psi(\mu(t))\}\right\}.

We claim that

(6.28)

|U|<\infty\quad\text{and}\quad|V|<\infty.

One can show that
|U|<\infty
via inequalities analogous to (
4.38
) and (
4.40
).

In order to prove that
|V|<\infty
, from equation (
6.27
) and the definition of the set
V
one deduces that

\eta(t)\leq c\,(-\eta^{\prime}(t))^{n^{\prime}}A^{-1}\left(\Xi\big((-\eta^{\prime}(t))^{n^{\prime}}\big)\right)=c\,\Theta\big(((-\eta^{\prime}(t))^{n^{\prime}})\big)\quad\text{for $t>t_{1}$},

where
\Theta
denotes the function defined in (
6.10
). An argument analogous to the one which yields
|U|<\infty
, but relying upon
Lemma
6.4
instead of Lemma
4.1
,
tells us that
|V|<\infty
. Claim (
6.28
) hence follows.

We have thereby shown that the set
Z=[t_{1},\infty)\setminus\left(U\cup V\right)
is such that
|Z|=\infty
. Since inequality (
4.41
) continues to hold in the present framework, with
\alpha=n
and the set
W
replaced by
Z
, the same argument as in the proof of Theorem
3.5
leads to a contradiction. This completes the proof.
∎

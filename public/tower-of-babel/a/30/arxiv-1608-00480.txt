Introduction

Central configurations play an important role in the (Newtonian)
n
-body problem: to name two, they arise as configurations yielding homographic solutions, and as rest points in the flow on the McGehee collision manifold. Following the spirit of Albouy and Chenciner
[
2
]
, in this article we study the problem of central configurations from the point of view of mutual distances; but instead of lengths we consider the space of
differences
of positions, which turns out to be a suitable group of cochains
C^{1}
of degree
1
with coefficients in the Euclidean space
E
. Hence, we show that central configurations are critical points of a function defined on
C^{1}
and restricted to the subspace of
1
-cocycles, and show some consequences. The technique of embedding the central configurations problem into a suitable space of cocycles was actually already used by Moeckel in
[
12
]
, in an implicit way, and again by Moeckel and Montgomery in
[
15
]
. In this article we study this approach introducing cocycles and cohomology, and show that many calculations can be significantly simplified in this way. For further details and recent remarkable advances we refer to
[
3
,
8
]
.

More precisely, assume
n\geq 2
,
d\geq 1
. Let
E=\mathbb{R}^{d}
denote the
d
-dimensional Euclidean space. An element of
E^{n}
will be denoted by
{\bm{q}}=({\bm{q}}_{1},{\bm{q}}_{2},\ldots,{\bm{q}}_{n})
where
\forall\,j

{\bm{q}}_{j}\in E
. Let
\mathbb{F}_{n}(E)
denote as in

n

\displaystyle\mathbb{F}_{n}(E)=\big\{{\bm{q}}\in E^{n}\colon{\bm{q}}_{i}\neq{\bm{q}}_{j}\big\}.

If

\displaystyle\Delta=\bigcup_{i<j}\big\{{\bm{q}}\in E^{n}\colon{\bm{q}}_{i}={\bm{q}}_{j}\big\},

then

For

, let

be positive masses. Assume that the masses are normalized, i.e., that

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}m_{j}=1.

(1.1)

Let

denote the

\displaystyle{\langle{{\bm{v}}},{{\bm{w}}}\rangle}_{M}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}m_{j}{\bm{v}}_{j}\cdot{\bm{w}}_{j},

Let
\alpha>0
be a fixed homogeneity parameter, and
U\colon\mathbb{F}_{n}(E)\to\mathbb{R}
the potential function defined as

\displaystyle U({\bm{q}})=\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}\frac{m_{i}m_{j}}{\lvert{{\bm{q}}_{i}-{\bm{q}}_{j}}\rvert^{\alpha}}.

A

is a configuration

such that there exists
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
such that (
\forall\,j=1,\ldots,n
)

\displaystyle\lambda m_{j}{\bm{q}}_{j}=\frac{\partial U}{\partial{\bm{q}}_{j}}=-\alpha\sum_{k\neq j}m_{j}m_{k}\frac{{\bm{q}}_{j}-{\bm{q}}_{k}}{\lvert{{\bm{q}}_{j}-{\bm{q}}_{k}}\rvert^{\alpha+2}}.

(1.2)

If
{\bm{q}}
is a central configuration, then

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \lambda\sum_{j}m_{j}\lvert{{\bm{q}}_{j}}\rvert^{2}&=-\alpha\sum_{j=1}^{n}\sum_{k\neq j,k=1}^{n}m_{j}m_{k}\frac{({\bm{q}}_{j}-{\bm{q}}_{k})\cdot{\bm{q}}_{j}}{|{\bm{q}}_{j}-{\bm{q}}_{k}|^{\alpha+2}}\\
&=-\alpha\sum_{j<k}m_{j}m_{k}\frac{({\bm{q}}_{j}-{\bm{q}}_{k})\cdot{\bm{q}}_{j}+({\bm{q}}_{k}-{\bm{q}}_{j})\cdot{\bm{q}}_{k}}{\lvert{{\bm{q}}_{j}-{\bm{q}}_{k}}\rvert^{\alpha+2}}=-\alpha\sum_{j<k}\frac{m_{j}m_{k}}{\lvert{{\bm{q}}_{j}-{\bm{q}}_{k}}\rvert^{\alpha}}\\
\implies\lambda{\lVert{{\bm{q}}}\rVert}_{M}^{2}&=-\alpha U({\bm{q}}),\end{aligned}

and hence
\lambda=-\alpha\frac{U({\bm{q}})}{{\lVert{{\bm{q}}}\rVert}_{M}^{2}}<0
. By summing equation (
1.2
) in
j

\displaystyle\lambda\sum_{j}m_{j}{\bm{q}}_{j}=-\alpha\sum_{j<k}m_{j}m_{k}\frac{({\bm{q}}_{j}-{\bm{q}}_{k})+({\bm{q}}_{k}-{\bm{q}}_{j})}{\lvert{{\bm{q}}_{j}-{\bm{q}}_{k}}\rvert^{\alpha+2}}=\bm{0}

and hence

\displaystyle\sum_{j}m_{j}{\bm{q}}_{j}=\bm{0}.

If

is an abstract simplicial complex, recall that the simplicial chain complex of
K
with real coefficients, denoted by

, is defined as follows: for each

, the chain group

is the vector space of all the
\mathbb{R}
-linear combinations of
k
-dimensional simplexes of
K
; the boundary homomorphism
\partial_{k}\colon C_{k}(K;\mathbb{R})\to C_{k-1}(K;\mathbb{R})
is defined as
\partial_{k}(\sigma)=\sum\limits_{j=0}^{k}(-1)^{j}\sigma d_{j}
for each
k
-simplex
\sigma
of
K
and
0
otherwise, where

is the
j
-th face map. More precisely, all simplices in
K
can be ordered, and elements in

will be linear combinations of ordered
k
-simplices in

. An ordered

-simplex with vertices

will be denoted either as

or simply as

. With this notation, the
j

\sigma d_{j}=[x_{0},\ldots,\widehat{x}_{j},\ldots,x_{k}]
, where
\widehat{x}_{j}
means that the

-th element is canceled.

By taking homomorphisms valued in an
\mathbb{R}
-vector space

, the chain complex

yields the simplicial cochain complex with coefficients in

\delta^{k}\colon C^{k}(K;E)\to C^{k+1}(K;E)
are defined for each

\displaystyle\delta^{k}(\eta)=\eta\partial_{k+1}\colon\ C_{k+1}(K;\mathbb{R})\to E

for each cochain
\eta\colon C_{k}(K;\mathbb{R})\to E
. The kernel of
\delta^{k}
is the group of cocycles, and it is denoted as
Z^{k}(K;E)=\ker\delta^{k}\subset C^{k}(K;E)
.

Now, let
\Delta^{n-1}
denote the standard (abstract) simplex with
n

\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
. Then the vector spaces

defined above for

) of the simplicial complex
\Delta^{n-1}

and

. A
0
-simplex of
\Delta^{n-1}
is simply an element

, and hence a

n
-tuple

, i.e., a map
{\bm{q}}\colon{\bm{n}}\to E
. Furthermore, a

-dimensional cochain is a map
{\bm{q}}
defined with values in

-dimensional simplices of

In simpler terms, for each

such that

, let

denote the

, and for

is defined by the property that

The coboundary operator
\delta^{0}\colon C^{0}\to C^{1}
is the map defined by

for each

\displaystyle\delta^{0}({\bm{q}})({i,j})={\bm{q}}_{j}-{\bm{q}}_{i}\in E

\displaystyle\delta^{1}({\bm{q}})(ijk)={\bm{q}}\partial_{2}(ijk)={\bm{q}}(jk-ik+ij)={\bm{q}}_{ij}+{\bm{q}}_{jk}+{\bm{q}}_{ki}.

Moreover, since the simplex

\displaystyle Z^{k+1}\big(\Delta^{n-1};E\big)=\ker\delta^{k+1}=\operatorname{Im}\delta^{k}.

With an abuse of notation, when not necessary the subscript of

For each

Proposition 2.1
.

The
(

)

\displaystyle\left(P_{m}({\bm{Q}})\right)_{ij}=\sum_{k\not\in\{i,j\}}m_{k}({\bm{Q}}_{ik}+{\bm{Q}}_{kj}+{\bm{Q}}_{ji})+{\bm{Q}}_{ij}

Proof.

Consider the homomorphism

defined on the vector space of simplicial
1
-chains

with real coefficient, as

\displaystyle\pi_{m}([i,j])=[i,j]-\sum_{k\neq\{i,j\}}m_{k}\partial_{2}([i,j,k]),

where

is the boundary homomorphism in dimension

and any

\displaystyle P_{m}({\bm{Q}})[i,j]={\bm{Q}}(\pi_{m}[i,j]).

For each

\displaystyle\pi_{m}\partial_{2}[a,b,c]=\pi_{m}([a,b]+[b,c]+[c,a])=[a,b]-\sum_{k\neq\{a,b\}}m_{k}\partial_{2}[a,b,k]

\displaystyle\hphantom{\pi_m \partial_2 [a,b,c] =}{}+[b,c]-\sum_{k\neq\{b,c\}}m_{k}\partial_{2}[b,c,k]+[c,a]-\sum_{k\neq\{c,a\}}m_{k}\partial_{2}[c,a,k]

Therefore, also

\displaystyle P^{2}_{m}({\bm{Q}})[i,j]=P_{m}({\bm{Q}})(\pi_{m}[i,j])={\bm{Q}}(\pi_{m}^{2}[i,j])={\bm{Q}}(\pi_{m}[i,j])=P_{m}({\bm{Q}})[i,j].

The projection

3
Hessians and indices

Let
P\in\mathbb{F}_{n}(E)
be a central configuration, with mass-norm
r={\lVert{P}\rVert}_{M}
. As in the case
r=1
, seen in
(CC1)
, it is a critical point of the restriction of the potential function
U
to the inertia ellipsoid
S=\{{\bm{q}}\in\mathbb{F}_{n}(E)\colon{\lVert{{\bm{q}}}\rVert}_{M}=r\}
. As such, its Morse index is the Hessian of the restriction
U|_{S}
, which is a bilinear form defined on the tangent space
T_{P}S
. The Hessian of
f=U|_{S}
at a critical point
P\in S
can be computed in general as
D^{2}f(P)[{\bm{u}}(P),{\bm{v}}(P)]=((D_{\bm{u}}D_{\bm{v}}f-D_{D_{\bm{u}}{\bm{v}}})f)(P)=(D_{\bm{u}}(D_{\bm{v}}f))(P)
, where
{\bm{u}}
and
{\bm{v}}
are vector fields on
S
(see, e.g.,
[
10
, formula (1), p. 343]
). This yields the well-known formula of the Hessian in terms of second derivatives with respect to a local chart (see also
[
14
, Proposition 2.8.8, p. 136]
). Given the mass-metric, the Hessian can be written as
D^{2}f[{\bm{u}},{\bm{v}}]={\langle{D_{\bm{u}}\hat{\nabla}_{M}f},{{\bm{v}}}\rangle}_{M}
or as the self-adjoint endomorphism
T_{P}S\to T_{P}S
defined by
{\bm{u}}\in T_{P}S\mapsto D_{\bm{u}}\hat{\nabla}_{M}f(P)\in T_{P}S
, where
\hat{\nabla}_{M}
denotes the gradient induced by the mass-metric restricted to
S
(hence
\hat{\nabla}_{M}f(P)
is the projection of
\nabla_{M}U(P)
to
T_{P}S
, orthogonal with respect to
{\langle{-},{-}\rangle}_{M}
).

If
{\bm{N}}
denotes a vector normal to the tangent space
T_{P}S
(such as
P-O
, where
O
denotes the origin of the Euclidean space
E
), the projection
\hat{\nabla}_{M}f(P)
is equal to
\nabla_{M}U-\frac{{\langle{\nabla_{M}U},{{\bm{N}}}\rangle}_{M}}{{\lVert{{\bm{N}}}\rVert}_{M}^{2}}{\bm{N}}
evaluated at
P
. If
{\bm{N}}=P-O
, by Euler formula
{\langle{\nabla_{M}U},{{\bm{N}}}\rangle}_{M}=-\alpha U
, and because
P
is a critical point of
f=U|_{S}
and
{\bm{u}}\in T_{P}S
the derivative
D_{\bm{u}}\frac{\alpha U({\bm{q}})}{{\lVert{{\bm{q}}}\rVert}_{M}^{2}}
vanishes at
P
, and hence

\displaystyle D_{\bm{u}}\hat{\nabla}_{M}f(P)=D_{\bm{u}}\left(\nabla_{M}U({\bm{q}})+\frac{\alpha U({\bm{q}})}{{\lVert{{\bm{q}}}\rVert}_{M}^{2}}{\bm{N}}\right)(P)=D_{\bm{u}}(\nabla_{M}U)(P)+\frac{\alpha U(P)}{{\lVert{P}\rVert}_{M}^{2}}D_{\bm{u}}({\bm{q}})

\displaystyle\hphantom{D_{\bm{u}}\hat{\nabla}_Mf(P)}{}=D_{\bm{u}}\left(\nabla_{M}U-\lambda\nabla_{M}\frac{{\lVert{{\bm{q}}}\rVert}_{M}^{2}}{2}\right),

where
\lambda
is as above the constant
-\frac{\alpha U(P)}{{\lVert{P}\rVert}_{M}^{2}}
. Hence the following lemma holds.

Lemma 3.1
.

If
P
is a critical point of the restriction
U|_{S_{r}}
, with the inertia ellipsoid
S_{r}=\{{\bm{q}}\in\mathbb{F}_{n}(E)\colon{\lVert{{\bm{q}}}\rVert}_{M}=r\}
and with
\lambda
defined as
\lambda=-\frac{\alpha U(P)}{{\lVert{P}\rVert}_{M}^{2}}=-\frac{\alpha U(P)}{r^{2}}
, then
P
is a critical point of the function
U({\bm{q}})-\frac{\lambda}{2}{\lVert{{\bm{q}}}\rVert}_{M}^{2}
; moreover, the Hessian of
U|_{S_{r}}
at
P
is the restriction to the tangent space
T_{P}{S_{r}}
of the Hessian of the map
U({\bm{q}})-\frac{\lambda}{2}{\lVert{{\bm{q}}}\rVert}_{M}^{2}
defined on
\mathbb{F}_{n}(E)
, evaluated
P
.

Proposition 3.2
.

If
P\in\mathbb{F}_{n}(E)
is a central configuration, then the Morse index at
P
of the restriction
f=U|_{S_{r}}
is equal to the Morse index at
P
of the function
F({\bm{q}})=U({\bm{q}})-\frac{\lambda}{2}{\lVert{{\bm{q}}}\rVert}_{M}^{2}
, where
\lambda
and
S_{r}
are as above. Furthermore, the direction parallel to
P-O
is an eigenvector of the Hessian of
F
, with (positive) eigenvalue equal to
-\lambda(\alpha+2)>0
.

Proof.

Since
\nabla_{M}{U({\bm{q}})}
is homogeneous of degree
-\alpha-1
, if
{\bm{N}}=P-O
one has
D_{\bm{N}}(\nabla_{M}{U})(P)=-(\alpha+1)\nabla_{M}{U(P)}=-\lambda(\alpha+1){\bm{N}}
. Therefore

\displaystyle D_{\bm{N}}\left(\nabla_{M}U-\lambda\nabla_{M}\frac{{\lVert{{\bm{q}}}\rVert}_{M}^{2}}{2}\right)(P)=-\lambda(\alpha+1){\bm{N}}-\lambda{\bm{N}}=-\lambda(\alpha+2){\bm{N}}.

∎

Now, consider the function
f\colon C^{1}\to\mathbb{R}
defined on cochains in Theorem
2.3
as

\displaystyle f({\bm{q}})=\sum_{i<j}m_{i}m_{j}\left(\lvert{{\bm{q}}_{ij}}\rvert^{-\alpha}+\lvert{{\bm{q}}_{ij}}\rvert^{2}\right).

The following proposition links its Hessian with the Hessian of the function
F
of Proposition
3.2
, for
\lambda=-2
.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
{\bm{q}}\in\mathbb{F}_{n}(E)
be a central configuration which is a critical point of the function
F({\bm{q}})=U({\bm{q}})+{\lVert{{\bm{q}}}\rVert}_{M}^{2}
in
C^{0}

(
and hence
{\bm{q}}\in X)
. Let
H
be the Hessian of
F
at
{\bm{q}}

(
with respect to the mass-metric in
C^{0})
, and
\tilde{H}
the Hessian matrix of the composition
f\circ P_{m}
at
\delta^{0}({\bm{q}})\in Z^{1}\subset C^{1}

(
with respect to the mass-metric in
C^{1})
. Then the non-zero eigenvalues of
\tilde{H}
are the same as the non-zero eigenvalues of
H
, except for the eigenvalue
2
occurring in
H
with multiplicity

(
which corresponds to the group of translations in
E
, or equivalently the orthogonal complement of

in
C^{0})
.

Proof.

Since
U
is invariant with respect to translations in
C^{0}
,
H
has the autospace

(which is the tangent space of the group of translations acting on

, and is orthogonal to

with respect to the mass-metric), over which

vanishes and

; hence it is an eigenspace with eigenvalue

. The rest of eigenvalues of

correspond via the isometric embedding

to eigenvalues of the restriction of
f
to

, and hence to the eigenvalues in
Z^{1}
of the composition

. The orthogonal complement of
Z^{1}
, which is the kernel of
P_{m}
, yields zero eigenvalues to
\tilde{H}
.
∎

4
Simple proofs of some corollaries

Equations (
2.1
) can be written as the following:

\displaystyle\frac{\lambda}{\alpha}{\bm{q}}_{ij}+{\bm{Q}}_{ij}=\sum_{k\not\in\{i,j\}}m_{k}({\bm{Q}}_{ij}+{\bm{Q}}_{jk}+{\bm{Q}}_{ki}).

(4.1)

Now, consider for each triple
i
,
j
,
k
the corresponding term
{\bm{Q}}_{ijk}={\bm{Q}}_{ij}+{\bm{Q}}_{jk}+{\bm{Q}}_{ki}
. We give some very simple proofs to some well-known propositions (actually generalizing them to any homogeneity
\alpha
), that follow from the following simple geometric lemma.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
{\bm{q}}_{1}
,
{\bm{q}}_{2}
and
{\bm{q}}_{3}
be three non-collinear points in
E
. Then
{\bm{Q}}_{123}=\bm{0}
if and only if
{\bm{q}}_{1}
,
{\bm{q}}_{2}
and
{\bm{q}}_{3}
are vertices of an equilateral triangle.

Furthermore, there exists
c\in\mathbb{R}
such that
{\bm{Q}}_{123}=c{\bm{q}}_{12}
if and only if
\lvert{{\bm{q}}_{13}}\rvert=\lvert{{\bm{q}}_{23}}\rvert
, that is, if and only if the triangle with vertices in
{\bm{q}}_{1}
,
{\bm{q}}_{2}
and
{\bm{q}}_{3}
is isosceles in
{\bm{q}}_{3}
.

Proof.

If
{\bm{q}}_{1}
,
{\bm{q}}_{2}
and
{\bm{q}}_{3}
are not collinear (in
E
), then the differences
{\bm{q}}_{12}
,
{\bm{q}}_{13}
and
{\bm{q}}_{23}
generate a plane. Since
{\bm{q}}_{12}+{\bm{q}}_{23}+{\bm{q}}_{31}=\bm{0}
,
{\bm{Q}}_{123}=\bm{0}
implies

\displaystyle{\bm{Q}}_{123}={\bm{Q}}_{12}+{\bm{Q}}_{23}+{\bm{Q}}_{31}=\frac{{\bm{q}}_{12}}{\lvert{{\bm{q}}_{12}}\rvert^{\alpha+2}}+\frac{{\bm{q}}_{23}}{\lvert{{\bm{q}}_{23}}\rvert^{\alpha+2}}+\frac{{\bm{q}}_{31}}{\lvert{{\bm{q}}_{31}}\rvert^{\alpha+2}}=\bm{0}={\bm{q}}_{12}+{\bm{q}}_{23}+{\bm{q}}_{31}.

By taking barycentric coordinates in the plane generated by the three points, it follows that
{\bm{Q}}_{123}=\bm{0}
if and only if
\lvert{{\bm{q}}_{12}}\rvert^{\alpha+2}=\lvert{{\bm{q}}_{23}}\rvert^{\alpha+2}=\lvert{{\bm{q}}_{31}}\rvert^{\alpha+2}
, that is, if and only if the three points are vertices of an equilateral triangle.

If
c_{1}
,
c_{2}
and
c_{3}
are three non-zero real numbers such that
c_{3}{\bm{q}}_{12}+c_{1}{\bm{q}}_{23}+c_{2}{\bm{q}}_{31}=c{\bm{q}}_{12}
, then
(c_{3}-c){\bm{q}}_{12}+c_{1}{\bm{q}}_{23}+c_{2}{\bm{q}}_{31}=\bm{0}
, and as above this implies
c_{3}-c=c_{1}=c_{2}
. Hence if
{\bm{Q}}_{123}=c{\bm{q}}_{12}
, it holds that
\lvert{{\bm{q}}_{23}}\rvert^{\alpha+2}=\lvert{{\bm{q}}_{13}}\rvert^{\alpha+2}
as claimed.
∎

Corollary 4.2
.

For
n=3
,
d\geq 2
and
\alpha>0
, the only non-collinear central configuration is the equilateral configuration.

Proof.

Equation (
4.1
) implies that, if
{\bm{Q}}_{ijk}\neq\bm{0}
, then the configuration is collinear (since (
4.1
) implies there exist three real numbers
c_{12}
,
c_{23}
,
c_{31}
such that
c_{12}{\bm{q}}_{12}=m_{3}{\bm{Q}}_{123}
,
c_{23}{\bm{q}}_{23}=m_{1}{\bm{Q}}_{231}
,
c_{31}{\bm{q}}_{31}=m_{2}{\bm{Q}}_{312}
, and it is easy to see that
{\bm{Q}}_{123}={\bm{Q}}_{231}={\bm{Q}}_{312}
). Therefore, if the configuration is not collinear,
{\bm{Q}}_{ijk}=\bm{0}
, and by Lemma
4.1
the configuration is an equilateral triangle.
∎

Another easy consequence of Lemma
4.1
is the following proposition (see
[
1
,
11
,
17
]
for its importance in estimating the number non-degenerate planar central configurations of four bodies).

Corollary 4.3
.

For
n=4
,
d\geq 2
and
\alpha>0
, if a central configuration has three collinear bodies, then it is a collinear configuration.

Proof.

Assume that
{\bm{q}}_{1}
,
{\bm{q}}_{2}
and
{\bm{q}}_{3}
are collinear, and
{\bm{q}}_{4}
is not. Then, equation (
4.1
) implies that for suitable real numbers
c_{12}
,
c_{23}
and
c_{31}
the following equations hold:

\displaystyle c_{12}{\bm{q}}_{12}=m_{3}{\bm{Q}}_{123}+m_{4}{\bm{Q}}_{124},\qquad c_{23}{\bm{q}}_{23}=m_{1}{\bm{Q}}_{231}+m_{4}{\bm{Q}}_{234},

\displaystyle c_{31}{\bm{q}}_{31}=m_{2}{\bm{Q}}_{312}+m_{4}{\bm{Q}}_{314}.

This implies that there are
\tilde{c}_{12}
,
\tilde{c}_{23}
and
\tilde{c}_{31}

such that

\displaystyle{\bm{Q}}_{124}=\tilde{c}_{12}{\bm{q}}_{12},\qquad{\bm{Q}}_{234}=\tilde{c}_{23}{\bm{q}}_{23},\qquad{\bm{Q}}_{314}=\tilde{c}_{31}{\bm{q}}_{31},

and by Lemma
4.1
this implies that
\lvert{{\bm{q}}_{14}}\rvert=\lvert{{\bm{q}}_{24}}\rvert=\lvert{{\bm{q}}_{34}}\rvert
, which is not possible since
{\bm{q}}_{1}
,
{\bm{q}}_{2}
and
{\bm{q}}_{3}
are collinear.
∎

Corollary
4.3
can be easily generalized to arbitrary

as follows:

Corollary 4.4
.

For
n\geq 4
,
d\geq 2
and
\alpha>0
, if
n-1
of the bodies in the central configuration are collinear, then all of them are.

Corollary 4.5
.

For
n\geq 4
,
d\geq 3
and
\alpha>0
, if the first

{\bm{q}}_{1},\ldots,{\bm{q}}_{n-1}
in a central configuration belong to a plane

, and the

{\bm{q}}_{n}
does not belong to the plane

, then the distance between
{\bm{q}}_{n}
and any

does not depend on

such that
\lvert{{\bm{q}}_{n}-{\bm{q}}_{j}}\rvert=c

Proof.

For each

there exists

such that

\displaystyle c_{ij}{\bm{q}}_{ij}=\sum_{k\not\in\{i,j\}}m_{k}{\bm{Q}}_{ijk}=\sum_{k\not\in\{i,j,n\}}m_{k}{\bm{Q}}_{ijk}+m_{n}{\bm{Q}}_{ijn}.

The term

is parallel to the plane

, while the sum

is a vector parallel to the plane containing
{\bm{q}}_{i}
,

and

∎

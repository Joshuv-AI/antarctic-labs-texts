Equivariant chararacter bijections and the inductive Alperin–McKay condition
Julian Brough, Lucas Ruhstorfer

Abstract
. In this paper we consider the inductive Alperin–McKay condition for isolated blocks of groups of Lie type B B and C C . This finishes the verification of the inductive condition for groups of this type.



2010 Mathematics Subject Classification
20C33

In the representation theory of finite groups some of the most important open conjectures relate the representation theory of a finite group
G
and those of its
\ell
-local subgroups, for
\ell
a prime dividing the order of
G
.
One of these conjectures is the Alperin–McKay conjecture, which forms a blockwise generalisation of the McKay conjecture.
For an
\ell
-block
b
of
G
we denote by
{\rm Irr}_{0}(G,b)
the subset of height zero characters of
\operatorname{Irr}(G,b)
, the characters of the block
b
.

Conjecture A

(Alperin–McKay)
.

Let
b
be an
\ell
-block of
G
with defect group
D
and
B
its Brauer correspondent in
{\rm N}_{G}(D)
.
Then

|{\rm Irr}_{0}(G,b)|=|{\rm Irr}_{0}({\rm N}_{G}(D),B)|.

In
[
Spä13
]
, the Alperin–McKay conjecture was reduced to the verification of the so-called
inductive Alperin–McKay condition
(iAM) for all finite simple groups and primes
\ell
.
In previous papers
[
Ruh22a
]
for simple groups of Lie type, the second author has proven that it suffices to verify the iAM-condition for quasi-isolated blocks of groups of Lie type and in many cases just for isolated blocks.
Applying these techniques together with results from
[
BS19
]
, yielded a verification of the iAM-condition for all finite simple groups of type
A
and primes
\ell\geq 5
.

The present paper is concerned with considering the iAM-condition for the remaining classical quasi-simple groups, i.e., for groups of Lie type
B
,
C
and
D
defined over a field of characteristic
p\neq\ell
.

Due to the nature of the inductive proof using the iAM-condition, there is some flexibility in the choice of local subgroups that can be taken.
When
\ell\geq 5
the defect groups of
\ell
-blocks of these groups have a Cabanes subgroup, i.e. they have a unique maximal normal abelian subgroup.

With this in mind the first half of this paper is focused on the following result, which provides a method to verify the iAM-condition.

Theorem B
.

Assume that
\mathbf{G}
is a simple algebraic group of simply connected type
B_{n}
or
C_{n}
(
n\geq 2
) with Frobenius endomorphism
F:\mathbf{G}\to\mathbf{G}
and let
b
be a quasi-isolated
\ell
-block of
G:=\mathbf{G}^{F}
with
\ell\geq 5
.
Assume that the block
b
satisfies Assumption
4.5
.
Then the block
b
is AM-good relative to the Cabanes subgroup of its defect group.

The block
b
of
G
is labeled by a pair
(\mathbf{L},\lambda)
consisting of a
d
-split Levi subgroup
\mathbf{L}
of
(\mathbf{G},F)
and a
d
-cuspidal character
\lambda\in{\rm Irr}(\mathbf{L}^{F})
, where
d
is the order of
q
modulo
\ell
, see
2.1
.
Assumption
4.5
concerns the Clifford theory associated to
\mathbf{L}^{F}\lhd\operatorname{N}_{G}(\mathbf{L})
taking into account the action arising from the automorphisms of
G
stabilizing
\mathbf{L}
.

Assumption
4.5
was verified in type
C
with respect to any
d
-cuspidal pair
[
Bro22
]
and for blocks of maximal defect in type
B

[
CS19
]
. In types
B
and
C
, by
[
Ruh22a
, Theorem D]
it suffices to validate the iAM-condition for all isolated
\ell
-blocks.
The second half of this paper therefore focuses on verifying this assumption also for the isolated
\ell
-blocks in type
B_{n}
. In contrast to the computation in
[
Bro22
]
we make explicit use of the structure of the possible Levi subgroups that can occur for isolated blocks. This for one reduces the computational effort of verifying Assumption
4.5
and is helpful in overcoming technical difficulties related to the more complicated structure of the extended Weyl group (see e.g.
[
Bro22
, Remark 3.4]
). By verifying Assumption
4.5
we can therefore finish the verification of the iAM-condition for the considered groups.

Theorem C
.

Let
G
be a quasi-simple group of Lie type
B_{n}
or
C_{n}
, with
n\geq 2
, defined over the finite field
\mathbb{F}_{q}
for
q
a prime power of an odd prime and let
\ell\geq 5
not dividing
q
.
Then every
\ell
-block of
G
satisfies the iAM-condition.

Structure of the paper.

In Section
1
we provide some of the basic notation that will be used throughout the paper.
Sections
2
and
3
are dedicated to the parametrisation of the global, respectively local, height zero characters via the techniques developed by Enguehard together with
d
-Harish-Chandra theory.
This will then be used in Section
4
to prove Theorem

.
The remaining sections are then dedicated to verifying Assumption
4.5
in types

.

1.
notation

1.1.
Finite groups of Lie type

Throughout,
{\bf G}
denotes a connected reductive group over an algebraic closure
\mathbb{F}
of
\mathbb{F}_{p}
for a prime number
p
. Let
F:\mathbf{G}\rightarrow\mathbf{G}
be a Frobenius endomorphism defining an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-structure on
\mathbf{G}
where
q
is an integral power of
p
. We let
(\mathbf{G}^{\ast},F)
be a pair in duality with
(\mathbf{G},F)
. If
\mathbf{H}
is an
F
-stable subgroup of
\mathbf{G}
(or of
\mathbf{G}^{\ast}
), then we denote
H:=\mathbf{H}^{F}
.

An
F
-stable torus
\mathbf{T}
of
\mathbf{G}
is called an
e
-torus
if it splits
completely over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{e}}
but no non-trivial subtorus splits over any smaller field. In particular,
|\mathbf{T}^{F}|
is a power of
\Phi_{e}(q)
, where
\Phi_{e}
denotes the
e
th cyclotomic polynomial. For an
F
-stable torus
\mathbf{T}
we denote by
\mathbf{T}_{\phi_{e}}
its maximal
e
-split subtorus.
The
centralizers of
e
-tori of
\mathbf{G}
are called
e
-split Levi subgroups
.

1.2.
Character theory

For any
F
-stable Levi subgroup
\mathbf{L}
of
a (not necessarily
F
-stable) parabolic subgroup
\mathbf{P}
of
\mathbf{G}
Lusztig
defines linear maps

R_{\mathbf{L}\subset\mathbf{P}}^{\mathbf{G}}:\mathbb{Z}\operatorname{Irr}(L)\longrightarrow\mathbb{Z}\operatorname{Irr}(G),

{}^{\ast}R_{\mathbf{L}\subset\mathbf{P}}^{\mathbf{G}}:\mathbb{Z}\operatorname{Irr}(G)\longrightarrow\mathbb{Z}\operatorname{Irr}(L).

By the Mackey formula (see
[
GM20
, Theorem 3.3.7]
) these maps won’t depend in our cases on the choice of parabolic subgroups so that we will write
{}^{(\ast)}R_{\mathbf{L}}^{\mathbf{G}}
instead of
{}^{(\ast)}R_{\mathbf{L}\subset\mathbf{P}}^{\mathbf{G}}
.

A character
\chi\in\operatorname{Irr}(G)
is called
e
-cuspidal
if

{}^{\ast}R_{\mathbf{L}}^{\mathbf{G}}(\chi)=0
for every
e
-split proper Levi subgroup
\mathbf{L}
of
\mathbf{G}
. A pair
(\mathbf{L},\lambda)
consisting of an
e
-split Levi subgroup
\mathbf{L}
of
\mathbf{G}
and an

e
-cuspidal character
\lambda\in\operatorname{Irr}(L)
is then called an

e
-cuspidal pair
.
Given an
e
-cuspidal pair
(\mathbf{L},\lambda)
, we write

\mathcal{E}(G,(\mathbf{L},\lambda)):=\{\chi\in\operatorname{Irr}(G)\mid\langle{}^{\ast}R_{\mathbf{L}}^{\mathbf{G}}(\chi),\lambda\rangle\neq 0\}

for the set of constituents of
R_{\mathbf{L}}^{\mathbf{G}}(\lambda)
. This is called the

e
-Harish-Chandra series of
G
above
(\mathbf{L},\lambda)
.

For a semisimple element
s\in G^{\ast}
we denote by
\mathcal{E}(G,s)
the rational Lusztig series associated to it. For
{z}\in\operatorname{Z}(G^{\ast})
there exists a linear character
\hat{z}\in\operatorname{Irr}(G)
and multiplication with
\hat{z}
induces a bijection
\hat{z}:\mathcal{E}(G,1)\to\mathcal{E}(G,z)
, see
[
CE04
, Equation 8.19]
.

Moreover, if the center
\operatorname{Z}(\mathbf{G})
is connected

\psi_{G,s}:\mathcal{E}(\operatorname{C}_{G^{\ast}}(s),1)\to\mathcal{E}(G,s)

will denote the unique Jordan decomposition from Digne–Michel
[
GM20
, Theorem 4.7.1]
. In particular, we have a bijection
\psi_{G,1}:\mathcal{E}(G^{\ast},1)\to\mathcal{E}(G,1)
between the set of unipotent characters of
G
and
G^{\ast}
.

1.3.
Blocks of groups of Lie type

We let
\ell\neq p
be a fixed prime and denote by
\mathcal{E}(G,\ell^{\prime})
the union of Lusztig series
\mathcal{E}(G,s)
with
s\in G^{\ast}
a semisimple element of
\ell^{\prime}
-order. For such a semisimple element
s
of
\ell^{\prime}
-order, we denote by
\operatorname{C}_{G^{\ast}}(s)_{\ell}
the subset of
\ell
-power order elements of the centralizer of
s
. By a fundamental result of Broué–Michel
[
CE04
, Theorem 9.12]
, the set

\mathcal{E}_{\ell}(G,s)=\displaystyle\bigcup_{t\in\operatorname{C}_{G^{\ast}}(s)_{\ell}}\mathcal{E}(G,st)

is the set of characters associated to a sum of
\ell
-blocks. We denote by
\mathrm{Bl}(G,s)
the corresponding set of blocks.

2.
Global characters

2.1.
Parametrizing height zero characters

Throughout this section, we let
\mathbf{G}
be a connected reductive group with connected centre with all simple components of classical type. Suppose that
\ell
is a prime with
\ell\nmid q
and
\ell\geq 5
. We denote by
d
the order of
q
modulo
\ell
. According to
[
CE99
, Theorem 4.1]
to each
d
-cuspidal pair
(\mathbf{L},\lambda)
with
\lambda\in\mathcal{E}(L,\ell^{\prime})
, up to
\mathbf{G}^{F}
-conjugation, there exists a unique
\ell
-block
b=b_{\mathbf{G}^{F}}(\mathbf{L},\lambda)
of
\mathbf{G}^{F}
associated to it. Let
s\in L^{\ast}
a semisimple element of
\ell^{\prime}
-order such that
\lambda\in\mathcal{E}(L,s)
. Observe that since
(\mathbf{L},\lambda)
is
d
-cuspidal, we have
\operatorname{Z}^{\circ}(\operatorname{C}^{\circ}_{\mathbf{L}^{\ast}}(s))_{\Phi_{d}}=\operatorname{Z}^{\circ}(\mathbf{L}^{\ast})_{\Phi_{d}}
, see
[
KM15
, Remark 2.2]
. Since
\mathbf{L}^{\ast}
is
d
-split we have
\mathbf{L}^{\ast}=\operatorname{C}_{\mathbf{G}^{\ast}}(\operatorname{Z}^{\circ}(\mathbf{L}^{\ast})_{\Phi_{d}})
and so
\operatorname{C}_{\mathbf{L}^{\ast}}(s)=\operatorname{C}_{\operatorname{C}_{\mathbf{G}^{\ast}}(s)}(\operatorname{Z}^{\circ}(\operatorname{C}^{\circ}_{\mathbf{L}^{\ast}}(s))_{\Phi_{d}})
is a
d
-split Levi subgroup of
(\operatorname{C}_{\mathbf{G}^{\ast}}(s),F)
.

Lemma 2.1
.

With the assumptions and notation from above,
we have

\operatorname{Irr}_{0}(G,b)\subseteq\displaystyle\bigcup_{t\in\operatorname{Z}(\operatorname{C}_{L^{\ast}}(s))_{\ell}}\mathcal{E}(G,st).

Proof.

Let
\mathbf{G}(s)
be a connected reductive group in duality with
\operatorname{C}_{\mathbf{G}^{\ast}}(s)
(this group is connected by
[
CE04
, Proposition 13.16]
as
\operatorname{Z}(\mathbf{G})
is connected by assumption). According to
[
Eng08
, Theorem 1.6]
, there exists a bijection

\mathcal{B}_{G,s}:\mathrm{Bl}(\mathbf{G}(s)^{F},1)\to\mathrm{Bl}(\mathbf{G}^{F},s)

such that for
t\in\mathrm{C}_{G^{\ast}}(s)_{\ell}
we have

\psi_{G,st}\circ\psi_{G(s),t}^{-1}(\operatorname{Irr}(G(s),c)\cap\mathcal{E}(G(s),t))=\operatorname{Irr}(G,b)\cap\mathcal{E}(G,st),

where
\mathcal{B}_{G,s}(c)=b
.
By
[
Eng08
, Theorem 1.6]
this bijection yields a height preserving bijection
\operatorname{Irr}(G(s),c)\to\operatorname{Irr}(G,b)
.
The block
c
is determined from
b
by
[
Eng08
, Theorem 1.6(B.1.a)]
as follows: By Jordan decomposition
\lambda
corresponds to a unipotent character
\lambda_{s}\in\mathcal{E}(\operatorname{C}_{L^{\ast}}(s),1)
. Therefore, there is a Levi subgroup
\mathbf{L}(s)
of
\mathbf{G}(s)
in duality with the
d
-split Levi subgroup
\operatorname{C}_{\mathbf{L}^{\ast}}(s)
of
\operatorname{C}_{\mathbf{G}^{\ast}}(s)
. We let
\lambda(s):=\psi_{L(s),1}(\lambda_{s})
. Then
c
is the block associated to the unipotent
d
-cuspidal pair
(L(s),\lambda(s))
of
G(s)
.

By
[
BS19
, Theorem 5.2]
we therefore deduce that

\operatorname{Irr}_{0}(G(s),c)\subseteq\displaystyle\bigcup_{t\in\operatorname{Z}(L(s)^{\ast})_{\ell}}\mathcal{E}(G(s),st).

By definition
L(s)^{\ast}=\operatorname{C}_{L^{\ast}}(s)
, so
t\in\operatorname{Z}(\operatorname{C}_{L^{\ast}}(s))
. Using that the height preserving bijection
\operatorname{Irr}(G(s),c)\to\operatorname{Irr}(G,b)
preserves Lusztig series yields the claim.
∎

Lemma 2.2
.

Keep the notation and assumptions of Lemma
2.1
and let
\chi\in\mathcal{E}(G,st)\cap\operatorname{Irr}_{0}(G,b)
be a height zero character. Then
\chi
is a constituent of
R_{\mathbf{L}}^{\mathbf{G}}(\theta)
, where
\theta
is the
d
-cuspidal character defined by
\theta:=\psi_{L,st}(\psi_{L,s}^{-1}(\lambda))\in\mathcal{E}(L,st)
. Note that by Lemma
2.1
the character
\theta
is well-defined as
\mathrm{C}_{L^{\ast}}(st)=\mathrm{C}_{L^{\ast}}(s)
.

Proof.

Let
\mathbf{K}
be a Levi subgroup of
\mathbf{G}
in duality with the Levi subgroup
\operatorname{C}_{\mathbf{G}^{\ast}}(t)
of
\mathbf{G}^{\ast}
. Note that the latter is indeed a Levi subgroup of
\mathbf{G}^{\ast}
since
\ell\geq 5
(see
[
CE04
, Theorem 13.16]
).
By
[
Eng08
, Proposition 2.2.4]
there exists a
d
-cuspidal pair
(\mathbf{L}_{\mathbf{K}},\lambda_{\mathbf{K}})
of
\mathbf{K}
such that the map
R_{\mathbf{K}}^{\mathbf{G}}\hat{t}
yields a bijection

\operatorname{Irr}(b_{K}(\mathbf{L}_{\mathbf{K}},\lambda_{\mathbf{K}}))\cap\mathcal{E}(K,s)\to\operatorname{Irr}(b_{G}(\mathbf{L},\lambda))\cap\mathcal{E}(G,st).

Here, the
d
-cuspidal pair
(\mathbf{L}_{\mathbf{K}},\lambda_{\mathbf{K}})
of
\mathbf{K}
is constructed as follows: Let
(\operatorname{C}_{\mathbf{L}^{\ast}}(s),\lambda_{s})
be the unipotent
d
-cuspidal pair of
\operatorname{C}_{\mathbf{G}^{\ast}}(s)
corresponding under Jordan decomposition to the
d
-cuspidal pair
(\mathbf{L},\lambda)
of
G
. By
[
Eng08
, Definition and Proposition 1.4.7]
there exists a unipotent
d
-cuspidal pair
(\operatorname{C}_{\mathbf{L}^{\ast}}(s)_{\operatorname{C}_{\mathbf{K}^{\ast}}(s)},(\lambda_{s})_{\operatorname{C}_{\mathbf{K}^{\ast}}(s)})
of
\operatorname{C}_{\mathbf{K}^{\ast}}(s)
such that
[\operatorname{C}_{\mathbf{L}^{\ast}}(s),\operatorname{C}_{\mathbf{L}^{\ast}}(s)]=[\operatorname{C}_{\mathbf{L}^{\ast}}(s)_{\operatorname{C}_{\mathbf{K}^{\ast}}(s)},\operatorname{C}_{\mathbf{L}^{\ast}}(s)_{\operatorname{C}_{\mathbf{K}^{\ast}}(s)}]^{g}
for some
g\in\operatorname{C}_{G^{\ast}}(s)
, and the characters
\lambda_{s}
and
(\lambda_{s})_{\operatorname{C}_{\mathbf{K}^{\ast}}(s)}^{g}
both restrict to the same character of
[\operatorname{C}_{\mathbf{L}^{\ast}}(s),\operatorname{C}_{\mathbf{L}^{\ast}}(s)]^{F}
. Then
(\mathbf{L}_{\mathbf{K}},\lambda_{\mathbf{K}})
is defined as the
d
-cuspidal pair of
\mathbf{K}
with
\lambda_{K}\in\mathcal{E}(\mathbf{L}_{\mathbf{K}}^{F},s)
corresponding to
(\operatorname{C}_{\mathbf{L}^{\ast}}(s)_{\operatorname{C}_{\mathbf{K}^{\ast}}(s)},(\lambda_{s})_{\operatorname{C}_{\mathbf{K}^{\ast}}(s)})
under Jordan decomposition.

Observe that by Lemma
2.1
we have
t\in\operatorname{Z}(\operatorname{C}_{L^{\ast}}(s))
and so
\operatorname{C}_{\mathbf{G}^{\ast}}(t)\cap\operatorname{C}_{\mathbf{L}^{\ast}}(s)=\operatorname{C}_{\mathbf{L}^{\ast}}(s)
. We therefore have
\mathbf{K}(s)^{\ast}=\mathbf{L}(s)^{\ast}
and so
(\mathbf{L}(s)_{\mathbf{K}(s)^{\ast}},(\lambda_{s})_{\mathbf{K}(s)^{\ast}})=(\mathbf{L}(s)^{\ast},\lambda_{s})
. Thus,
\mathbf{L}_{\mathbf{K}}
is the Levi subgroup of
\mathbf{K}
in duality with the Levi subgroup
\operatorname{C}_{\mathbf{L}^{\ast}}(t)
of
\operatorname{C}_{\mathbf{G}^{\ast}}(t)
and
\lambda_{\mathbf{K}}=\psi_{K,s}(\lambda_{s})\in\mathcal{E}(K,s)
. Moreover,
\mathbf{L}_{\mathbf{K}}
is a Levi subgroup of
\mathbf{L}
.

Now the center of the Levi subgroup
\mathbf{K}
is again connected by
[
CE04
, Proposition 13.12]
. By
[
CE99
, Proposition 5.2]
(and its proof) we find that any
\psi\in\operatorname{Irr}(b_{K}(\mathbf{L}_{\mathbf{K}},\lambda_{\mathbf{K}}))\cap\mathcal{E}(K,s)
appears in the
d
-Harish-Chandra series of
(\mathbf{L}_{\mathbf{K}},\lambda_{\mathbf{K}})
. Hence,
\psi
is a constituent of
R_{\mathbf{L}_{\mathbf{K}}}^{\mathbf{K}}(\lambda_{\mathbf{K}})
and thus
\chi:=R_{\mathbf{K}}^{\mathbf{G}}(\hat{t}\psi)
is a constituent of

R_{\mathbf{K}}^{\mathbf{G}}(\hat{t}R_{\mathbf{L}_{\mathbf{K}}}^{\mathbf{K}}(\lambda_{\mathbf{K}}))=R_{\mathbf{L}_{\mathbf{K}}}^{\mathbf{G}}(\lambda_{\mathbf{K}}\hat{t})=R_{\mathbf{L}}^{\mathbf{G}}(R_{\mathbf{L}_{\mathbf{K}}}^{\mathbf{L}}(\lambda_{\mathbf{K}}\hat{t})).

Now
R^{\mathbf{L}}_{\mathbf{L}_{\mathbf{K}}}\circ\psi_{\mathbf{L}_{\mathbf{K}},st}=\psi_{\mathbf{L},st}
by the properties of Jordan decomposition (see
[
GM20
, Theorem 4.7.1]
). By what we said before,

is central in
\operatorname{C}_{L^{\ast}}(s)
. Hence,
\chi
is a constituent of
R_{\mathbf{L}}^{\mathbf{G}}(\theta)
with
\theta:=R_{\mathbf{L}_{\mathbf{K}}}^{\mathbf{L}}(\lambda_{\mathbf{K}}\hat{t})=\psi_{L,st}\psi_{L,s}^{-1}(\lambda)
. Finally, observe that
\lambda_{\mathbf{K}}
is a
d
-cuspidal character. Thus,
\lambda_{\mathbf{K}}\hat{t}
is also
d
-cuspidal and hence by the proof of
[
KM15
, Proposition 4.1]
the character
\theta
is
d
-cuspidal as well.
∎

Note that the proof of the previous lemma shows in particular that all irreducible constituents of
R_{\mathbf{L}}^{\mathbf{G}}(\theta)
lie in the block

. A similar description of the (height zero) characters of blocks of abelian defect using similar arguments was already obtained in

Mal14
, Theorem 2.9]
.

2.2.
d
-Harish-Chandra theory

The previous lemma can now be used as a parametrization of the global height zero characters in terms of
d
-Harish-Chandra theory. Recall that if
[\mathbf{G},\mathbf{G}]
is simply connected, we have by
[
CE04
, Equation 8.19]
a bijection

\operatorname{Z}(G^{\ast})\to\operatorname{Irr}(\mathbf{G}^{F}/[\mathbf{G},\mathbf{G}]^{F}),z\mapsto\hat{z}.

Proposition 2.3
.

In addition to the assumptions from before we assume that
[\mathbf{G},\mathbf{G}]
is simply connected. Let
(\mathbf{L},\theta)
,
\theta\in\mathcal{E}(L,st)
, be a
d
-cuspidal pair as in Lemma
2.2
. Let
\mathrm{Aut}_{\mathbb{F}}(\mathbf{G}^{F})
be defined as in
[
CS13
, 2.4]
.
There exist an
(\operatorname{Irr}(\mathbf{G}^{F}/[\mathbf{G},\mathbf{G}]^{F})\rtimes\mathrm{Aut}_{\mathbb{F}}(\mathbf{G}^{F}))_{(\mathbf{L},\theta)}
-equivariant bijection

\operatorname{Irr}(W_{G}(\mathbf{L},\theta))\to\mathcal{E}(\mathbf{G}^{F},(\mathbf{L},\theta)),\eta\mapsto R_{\mathbf{L}}^{\mathbf{G}}(\theta)_{\eta}.

Here,
(\hat{z},\sigma)\in(\operatorname{Irr}(\mathbf{G}^{F}/[\mathbf{G},\mathbf{G}]^{F})\rtimes\mathrm{Aut}_{\mathbb{F}}(\mathbf{G}^{F}))_{(\mathbf{L},\theta)}
acts by conjugation with
\sigma
on
\operatorname{Irr}(W_{G}(\mathbf{L},\theta))
.
In particular, we have
R_{\mathbf{L}}^{\mathbf{G}}(\theta)_{\eta}\in\operatorname{Irr}_{0}(G,b)
if and only if the following conditions are satisfied:

(i)

W_{G}(\mathbf{L},\theta)
contains a Sylow
\ell
-subgroup of
W_{G}(\mathbf{L},\lambda)
.

(ii)

\eta\in\operatorname{Irr}(W_{G}(\mathbf{L},\theta))
is an
\ell^{\prime}
-character.

Proof.

We follow the general ideas of
[
BS19
, Section 5.B]
.
Let
(\operatorname{C}_{\mathbf{L}^{\ast}}(st),\theta_{st})
be the unipotent
d
-cuspidal pair in
\operatorname{C}_{\mathbf{L}^{\ast}}(st)
associated to
(\mathbf{L},\theta)
.
Then as observed in the proof of
[
BS19
, Proposition 5.4]
we have as in
[
CS13
, Theorem 3.4]
an
\mathrm{Aut}_{\mathbb{F}}(\operatorname{C}_{\mathbf{G}^{\ast}}(st))_{(\operatorname{C}_{\mathbf{L}^{\ast}}(st),\theta_{st})}
-equivariant bijection

I_{\operatorname{C}_{\mathbf{L}^{\ast}}(st),\theta_{st}}^{\operatorname{C}_{\mathbf{G}^{\ast}}(st)}:\operatorname{Irr}(W_{\operatorname{C}_{G^{\ast}}(s)}(\operatorname{C}_{\mathbf{L}^{\ast}}(st),\theta_{st}))\to\mathcal{E}(\operatorname{C}_{\mathbf{G}^{\ast}}(st),(\operatorname{C}_{\mathbf{L}^{\ast}}(st),\theta_{st})).

As in the proof of
[
BS19
, Lemma 5.3]
we obtain for
\eta\in\operatorname{Irr}(W_{\operatorname{C}_{G^{\ast}}(s)}(\operatorname{C}_{\mathbf{L}^{\ast}}(st),\theta_{st}))
the equality

I_{\operatorname{C}_{\mathbf{L}^{\ast}}(st),\theta_{st}}^{\operatorname{C}_{\mathbf{G}^{\ast}}(st)}(\eta)(1)_{\ell}=|\operatorname{C}_{G^{\ast}}(st):\operatorname{N}_{\operatorname{C}_{G^{\ast}}(st)}(\operatorname{C}_{\mathbf{L}^{\ast}}(st))|_{\ell}\theta_{st}(1)_{\ell}\eta(1)_{\ell}.

By the uniqueness properties of Jordan decomposition (see also the proof of
[
Mal14
, Theorem 2.9]
) duality induces a natural isomorphism

\Phi:W_{\operatorname{C}_{G^{\ast}}(st)}(\operatorname{C}_{\mathbf{L}^{\ast}}(st),\theta_{st})\to W_{G}(\mathbf{L},\theta).

In particular, we also obtain in this case a bijection

\operatorname{Irr}(W_{G}(\mathbf{L},\theta))\to\mathcal{E}(G,(\mathbf{L},\theta)),\,\eta\mapsto\psi_{G,st}\circ I_{\operatorname{C}_{\mathbf{L}^{\ast}}(st),\theta_{st}}^{\operatorname{C}_{\mathbf{G}^{\ast}}(st)}\circ(\eta\circ\Phi),

and as in the Harish-Chandra case we denote by
R_{\mathbf{L}}^{\mathbf{G}}(\theta)_{\eta}
the character corresponding to
\eta\in\operatorname{Irr}(W_{G}(\mathbf{L},\theta))
under this bijection. As in
[
BS19
, Lemma 5.3]
the degree formula for the unipotent
d
-Harish-Chandra case yields that

R_{\mathbf{L}}^{\mathbf{G}}(\theta)_{\eta}(1)_{\ell}=|G:\operatorname{N}_{C_{G^{\ast}}(st)}(\operatorname{C}_{\mathbf{L}^{\ast}}(st))|_{\ell}\theta_{st}(1)_{\ell}\eta(1)_{\ell}.

By Jordan decomposition, we have
\theta(1)_{\ell}=|L:\operatorname{C}_{L^{\ast}}(st)|_{\ell}\theta_{st}(1)_{\ell}
. Replacing this in the formula above yields

R_{\mathbf{L}}^{\mathbf{G}}(\theta)_{\eta}(1)_{\ell}=\frac{|G:L|_{\ell}}{|W_{\operatorname{C}_{\mathbf{G}^{\ast}}(st)}(\operatorname{C}_{\mathbf{L}^{\ast}}(st))|_{\ell}}\theta(1)_{\ell}\eta(1)_{\ell}.

As in the proof of
[
BS19
, Theorem 5.6]
, we deduce that the minimum over all such
(t,\eta)
is obtained for
(1,1)

\frac{|\operatorname{C}_{G^{\ast}}(s)|_{\ell}}{\lambda_{s}(1)_{\ell}}.

It remains to show that the so-obtained bijection is

-equivariant. For this, let

be a bijective morphism commuting with the action of

. If
\sigma(\mathbf{L})=\mathbf{L}
, then there exists a dual morphism
\sigma^{\ast}:\mathbf{G}^{\ast}\to\mathbf{G}^{\ast}
commuting with

and such that
\sigma|_{\mathbf{L}}
and
\sigma^{\ast}|_{\mathbf{L}^{\ast}}
are in duality.
If

stabilizes
(\mathbf{L},\theta)
for some

, then

is

-conjugate to

. Hence, we can assume (by possibly replacing

by

for some suitable

it follows that

) and the equivariance properties of the parametrization of unipotent

-equivariant.
∎

3.

From now on we assume that
\mathbf{G}
is a simple, simply connected algebraic group of classical type
B_{n}
,
C_{n}
or
D_{n}
. In particular, the center of
\mathbf{G}
can be disconnected. Let
\iota:\mathbf{G}\hookrightarrow\widetilde{\mathbf{G}}
be a regular embedding, i.e. a closed embedding of algebraic groups with
\operatorname{Z}(\widetilde{\bf G})
connected and
\widetilde{\bf G}=\operatorname{Z}(\widetilde{\bf G}){\bf G}
, see
[
CE04
, Section 15.1]
.
Assume

is a Frobenius endomorphism extending the one of
\mathbf{G}
, as in
[

, Section 2]

Recall that
\Phi_{e}
denotes the

and observe that

by the remarks before
[
CE04

, where

is the order of

modulo

Lemma 3.1
.

Let

be a positve integer with

. Then
(q^{k}-1)_{\ell}\leq\Phi_{d}(q)_{\ell}
(resp.
(q^{k}+1)_{\ell}\leq\Phi_{d}(q)_{\ell}

(resp.

but

We write

resp.

. The property follows by the above characterization of

.
∎

Remark 3.2
.

Recall
[
GM20
, Example 3.5.20]
: If
F
defines an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-structure on
\mathbf{G}
, then
\mathbf{G}^{F^{d}}
inherits an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-structure but can also be considered as group over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{d}}
. Under this identification, the
d
-split Levi subgroups of
(\mathbf{G},F)
correspond to the
1
-split Levi subgroups of
(\mathbf{G},F^{d})
.

We claim that similarly for a positive integer

, the
d
-split Levi subgroups of
(\mathbf{G},F)
correspond to the
d/(d,k)
-split Levi subgroups of
(\mathbf{G},F^{k})
. For this it suffices to observe that if
(\mathbf{T},F)
is a
d
-split torus, then
(\mathbf{T},F^{k})
is a
d/(d,k)
-split torus. Indeed,
(\mathbf{T},F^{k})
splits completely over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{\mathrm{lcm}(d,k)}}
and if any non-trivial subtorus splits over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{ki}}
, then it also splits over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{(d,ki)}}
which forces

.

Recall that
d
is the order of
q
modulo
\ell
. Additionally, we set
d_{0}:=d
if

is odd and
d_{0}:=d/2
if

is even.

Lemma 3.3
.

Assume that
s
is quasi-isolated in
\mathbf{G}^{\ast}
and
(\mathbf{L},\lambda)
a

-cuspidal pair of

\operatorname{Z}(\operatorname{C}_{L^{\ast}}(s))_{\ell}=\operatorname{Z}(L^{\ast})_{\ell}

Proof.

By the properties of

-cuspidal pairs, see
[
KM15
, Remark 2.2]
, we have
\operatorname{Z}^{\circ}(\operatorname{C}_{\mathbf{L}^{\ast}}^{\circ}(s))_{\Phi_{d}}=\operatorname{Z}^{\circ}(\mathbf{L}^{\ast})_{\Phi_{d}}

defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
and

a set of integers we let
\mathbf{T}_{\phi_{E}}
the unique maximal
\Phi_{E}
-subgroup, see
[

, Definition 13.3, Proposition 13.5]

To prove the claim it is therefore by

Observe that

or

[
Mal17
, Table 2]
. In particular, by Remark
3.2
the polynomial order of

∎

6.1.

Let

The Chevalley generators

Proposition 9.2
.

There exists a

\Lambda
with respect to

Observe that

According to Lemma

9.2.

The group

Lemma 9.3
.

There exists a unique isomorphism

\Theta:L_{1}\to\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\varepsilon q^{d_{0}})

with
\Theta(N_{F^{d_{0}}/F}(\mathbf{x}_{\pm(e_{1}-e_{2})}(u)))=\mathbf{x}_{\pm(e_{1}-e_{2})}(u)
.

Proof.

We first claim that
v_{l}^{d_{0}}\in\operatorname{Z}(\mathbf{V}_{l})
. Firstly,
\rho(v_{l})^{d_{0}}\in\operatorname{Z}(\mathbf{W}_{l})
and
v_{l}^{d_{0}}=v_{l,0}^{l}
or
v_{l}^{d_{0}}=v_{l,0}^{2l}
. Hence, by
[
CS19
, Section 5.B]
, we have
\mathbf{H}_{l}\leq\operatorname{C}_{\mathbf{V}_{l}}(v_{l}^{d_{0}})
. By
[
CS19
, Lemma 5.4]
we deduce therefore that
v_{l}^{d_{0}}\in\operatorname{Z}(\mathbf{V}_{l})
.

Note that for
u\in\mathbb{F}
we have
{}^{v_{l}^{d_{0}}}\mathbf{x}_{e_{1}-e_{2}}(u)=\mathbf{x}_{\varepsilon(e_{1}-e_{2})}(\delta u)
for some
\delta\in\{\pm 1\}
by the Chevalley relations. Since
v_{l}^{d_{0}}\in\operatorname{Z}(\mathbf{V}_{l})
we have
{}^{v_{l}^{d_{0}}}\mathbf{n}_{e_{1}-e_{2}}(1)=\mathbf{n}_{e_{1}-e_{2}}(1)
. This implies
\delta=\varepsilon
by the Chevalley relations
[
Spä06
, Satz 2.1.6]
. Therefore,
F^{d_{0}}(\mathbf{x}_{e_{1}-e_{2}}(u))=\mathbf{x}_{\varepsilon(e_{1}-e_{2})}(\varepsilon u^{q^{d_{0}}})
. The claim of the lemma can now be deduced from this.
∎

Lemma 9.4
.

For
1\leq i\neq j\leq t_{l}
, the commutator
[L_{i},c_{j}^{\prime}]=1
.
Moreover,
[B_{m}(q),V^{\prime}]=1

Proof.

Denote by
\Phi_{A_{\mathcal{O}_{2i-1}}\sqcup\mathcal{O}_{2i}}
the type
A
subsystem as in
[
Bro22
, Notation 3.1]

L_{i}\leq\langle\mathbf{X}_{\alpha}\mid\alpha\in\Phi_{A_{\mathcal{O}_{2i-1}}\sqcup\mathcal{O}_{2i}}\rangle
and
c_{j}^{\prime}=x_{2j-1}x_{2j}
for
x_{i}\in\langle\mathbf{X}_{\alpha}\mid\alpha\in\Phi_{\mathcal{O}_{i}}\rangle
.
Fix
\alpha\in\Phi_{A_{\mathcal{O}_{2i-1}}\sqcup\mathcal{O}_{2i}}
and assume
i\neq j
.
Then
\alpha\perp\Phi_{\mathcal{O}_{i}}
.
By applying
[
Spä06
, Remark 2.1.7]
, it follows that
\mathbf{x}_{\alpha}(u)^{\mathbf{n}_{\beta}(1)}=\mathbf{x}_{\alpha}(u)
for all
\beta\in\Phi_{\mathcal{O}_{i}}

Thus
[\mathbf{x}_{\alpha}(u),c_{j}^{\prime}]=1

\alpha\in\Phi_{A_{\mathcal{O}_{2i-1}\sqcup\mathcal{O}_{2i}}}

The same argument also shows for

that

It remains to consider

Let

Again applying

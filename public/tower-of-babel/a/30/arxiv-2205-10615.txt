First Technique:

f\in M_{n}^{*}

\displaystyle f+M_{n+1}^{*}

\displaystyle\text{Clearly}\quad\Phi_{M}

5.
CI approximation: The general case

In this section we prove our general result regarding complete intersection (CI)
approximation; see

we showed that every
\mathfrak{m}
-primary ideal
\mathfrak{a}
in an equicharacteristic

-primary ideals; we have to take some care for

The essential point is to show existence of
homogeneous regular sequences in certain graded ideals.

This section is divided into two subsections. In the first subsection
R=\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}R_{n}
be a standard graded algebra
over a local ring
(R_{0},\mathfrak{m}_{0})

is Cohen-Macaulay and

is a finitely generated graded

-module generated by elements in

we give conditions to ensure a homogeneous regular sequence

where

).
In the second subsection we prove Theorem

.

Homogeneous regular sequence

Let
R=\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}R_{n}
be a standard algebra
over a local ring
(R_{0},\mathfrak{m}_{0})
and let
M=\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}M_{n}
be a finitely generated graded
R
-module.
In general a graded module

need not have homogeneous regular sequence
\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{c}\in R_{+}\cap\operatorname{ann}_{R}M
where

(see
5.1
).
When
R
is Cohen-Macaulay and
M=\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}M_{n}
is a finitely generated graded
R
-module generated by elements in
M_{0}
,
we give conditions to ensure a homogeneous regular sequence
\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{c}\in R_{+}\cap\operatorname{ann}_{R}M

where
c=\dim R-\dim M
.

We adapt an example from
[

, p. 34]
to show that a homogeneous regular sequence of length

\operatorname{grade}M

in

Example 5.1
.

Let
A=k[[X]]
and
T=A[Y]
. Set

. Notice that
R_{0}=A
. Set
\mathfrak{M}=(X,Y)R
the unique graded maximal ideal of

and let

. Clearly
\operatorname{grade}M=\operatorname{grade}(\mathfrak{M},R)=1

every homogeneous element in

is a zero-divisor.

The following result regarding existence of homogeneous regular sequence in
(\operatorname{ann}M)\cap R_{+}
(under certain conditions) is crucial in our proof of Theorem

.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
(R_{0},\mathfrak{m}_{0})
be a local ring and let
R=\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}R_{n}
be a
standard graded
R_{0}
-algebra and let
M=\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}M_{n}
be a finitely generated

R
-module. Assume
R
has a homogeneous s.o.p. Set
c=\dim R-\dim M
.
Assume

(1)

R_{0}
is Cohen-Macaulay and
R
is Cohen-Macaulay.

(2)

M
is generated as an
R
-module by some elements in
M_{0}
.

(3)

There exists

y_{1},\ldots,y_{s}\in R_{0}
a system of parameters for both
M_{0}
and
R_{0}
and a part of a homogeneous system of parameters for both
M
and
R
.

Then there exists a homogeneous regular sequence

\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{c}\in R_{+}
such that
\xi_{i}\in\operatorname{ann}_{R}M
for each
i
.

Proof.

We prove this by induction on
s=\dim R_{0}
.
When
s=0
,
R_{0}
is Artinian. So
\operatorname{grade}(\operatorname{ann}M,R)=\operatorname{grade}(\operatorname{ann}M\cap R_{+},R)
. As
R
is CM we have
\operatorname{grade}(\operatorname{ann}M,R)=c
.
The result follows from
[
2
, 1.5.11]
.

We prove the assertion for
s=r+1

assuming it to be valid for
s=r
. Set
S=R/y_{1}R=\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}S_{n}
and
N=M/y_{1}M
. Note that
N
is also generated in degree
0
. Let
\overline{y_{i}}=y_{i}\mod(y_{1})
with
2\leq i\leq s
. Then
\overline{y_{2}}\ldots\overline{y_{s}}

is a system of parameters for both
S_{0}
and
N_{0}=M_{0}/y_{1}M
. Notice
c=\dim R-\dim M=\dim S-\dim N
. Also note that
S
has h.s.o.p. By induction hypothesis
there exists a
S
-regular sequence

\overline{\xi_{1}},\ldots,\overline{\xi_{c}}\in S_{+}
such that
\overline{\xi_{i}}\in\operatorname{ann}_{S}N
for each
i
.

R
is CM. So
y_{1}
is
R
-regular.
Therefore
y_{1},\xi_{1},\xi_{2}\ldots,\xi_{c}
is an
R
-regular sequence.
Set
\mathfrak{q}=y_{1}R_{+}
. Let
M
be generated as an
R
-module by
u_{1},\ldots,u_{m}\in M_{0}
.
Fix
i\in\{1,\dots,c\}
and set
\xi=\xi_{i}
. By construction
\xi u_{j}\in y_{1}M
for each

j
. However
\xi\in R_{+}
and
y_{1}\in R_{0}
. So
\xi u_{j}\in\mathfrak{q}M
for each

j
. By the determinant trick (observing that each
u_{j}
has degree zero)
there exists a
homogeneous
polynomial

\Delta(\xi)=\xi^{n}+a_{1}\xi^{n-1}+\ldots+a_{n-1}\xi+a_{n}\in\operatorname{ann}_{R}M\quad\text{and}\ a_{i}\in\mathfrak{q}^{i}\ \text{for}\ i=1,\dots,n.

Clearly

.
Notice that
\Delta(\xi)=\xi^{n}\mod(y_{1})
. So

is a regular sequence,
[
21
, 16.1]
.
Therefore
\Delta(\xi_{1}),\Delta(\xi_{2})\ldots,\Delta(\xi_{c})

is an

-regular sequence. This proves the assertion when

.
∎

) the
second fundamental exact sequence.

6.9
.

Notice

and

(6.9.1)

\begin{split}0\longrightarrow\mathcal{B}^{I}(x,M)&\longrightarrow H^{0}(L^{I}(M))(-1)\longrightarrow H^{0}(L^{I}(M))\longrightarrow H^{0}(L^{I}(N))\\
&\longrightarrow H^{1}(L^{I}(M))(-1)\longrightarrow H^{1}(L^{I}(M))\longrightarrow H^{1}(L^{I}(N))\\
&\cdots\cdots\\
\end{split}

6.10
.

We will use the following well-known result regarding *-Artinian modules quite often:

Let
V
be a *-Artinian
\mathcal{S}
-module.

(a)

V_{n}=0
for all
n\gg 0

(b)

If
\psi\colon V(-1)\rightarrow V
is a monomorphism then
V=0
.

(c)

If
\phi\colon V\rightarrow V(-1)
is a monomorphism then
V=0
.

6.11
.

Recall an ideal
I
is said to be asymptotically normal if

is integrally closed for all
n\gg 0
.
If
I
is a asymptotically normal
\mathfrak{m}
-primary ideal and

then by a result of Huckaba and Huneke
[

, 3.1]
,
0ptG_{I^{l}}(A)\geq 2

for all

(also see
[

, 7.3]
). It also follows from

that in this case
H^{1}(L)_{n}=0
for

. In particular
\ell(H^{1}(L))<\infty
(here

).

6.12
.

Let
\mathcal{F}=\{M_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
be an
I
-stable filtration with
M_{n}=M
for
n\leq 0
. Set

. Then as before

is a
\mathcal{S}
-module.
All the results proved for

in the

with the same proofs.

6.13
.

We will also need the following fact for general filtration’s (and was proved for
I
-adic filtration’s in
[
27
, 9.2]
). Let
\mathcal{F}=\{M_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
be an

-stable filtration with

for

\geq 2

for

.

To see this set

. Let

-superficial with respect to

7.
Proof of Itoh’s-conjecture for normal ideals

In this section we give a proof of Itoh’s conjecture for normal ideals.

Theorem 7.1
.

Let
(A,\mathfrak{m})
be a Cohen-Macaulay local ring of dimension
d\geq 1
and let
\mathfrak{a}
be a normal
\mathfrak{m}
-primary ideal.
If
e_{3}^{\mathfrak{a}}(A)=0
then
G_{\mathfrak{a}}(A)
is Cohen-Macaulay.

Remark 7.2
.

The following assertion is well-known.
If
\mathfrak{a}^{n}
is integrally closed for all
n
then
\mathfrak{a}^{n}
is Ratliff-Rush for all
n\geq 1
.
It follows that
0ptG_{\mathfrak{a}}(A)\geq 1
, see
6.4
.

We first show that

Lemma 7.3
.

Theorem
7.1
holds if
\dim A=1,2
.

We note that
e_{3}^{\mathfrak{a}}(A)
is defined as in
2.2
.
This Lemma is certainly well-known to the experts. However we provide a proof
for the convenience of the reader.

Proof of Lemma
7.3
.

If
d=1
then by
7.2
the result holds.

Now assume
d=2
. Note that we may assume that
A
has an infinite residue field. Let
\mathfrak{c}
be a minimal reduction of
\mathfrak{a}
.
Set
\sigma_{i}=\ell(\mathfrak{a}^{i+1}/\mathfrak{c}\mathfrak{a}^{i})
. Then as
0ptG_{\mathfrak{a}}(A)
is positive it follows from a result of Huneke
[
10
, 2.4]

that the
h
-polynomial of
A
with respect to
\mathfrak{a}
is

h(t)=\ell(A/\mathfrak{a})+(\sigma_{0}-\sigma_{1})t+(\sigma_{1}-\sigma_{2})t^{2}+\cdots+(\sigma_{s-2}-\sigma_{s-1})t^{s-1}+\sigma_{s-1}t^{s}.

It follows that

\displaystyle e_{1}^{\mathfrak{a}}(A)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\geq 0}\sigma_{i},

\displaystyle e_{2}^{\mathfrak{a}}(A)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\geq 1}i\sigma_{i},\quad\text{and}

\displaystyle e_{3}^{\mathfrak{a}}(A)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\geq 2}\binom{i}{2}\sigma_{i}.

As
e_{3}^{\mathfrak{a}}(A)=0
we have
\sigma_{2}=0
. So we have
\mathfrak{a}^{3}=\mathfrak{c}\mathfrak{a}^{2}
. As
\mathfrak{a}
is integrally closed we also have

\mathfrak{a}^{2}\cap\mathfrak{c}=\mathfrak{c}\mathfrak{a}
, see
[
10
, Theorem 4.7 ]
(for rings containing a field) and
[
16
, Theorem 1]
. The result follows from Valabrega-Valla Theorem

[
33
, 2.3]
.
∎

7.4
.

Recall that an ideal
\mathfrak{a}
is said to be
asymptotically normal
if
\mathfrak{a}^{n}
is integrally closed for all
n\gg 0
.
The crucial result to prove Itoh’s conjecture is the following:

Lemma 7.5
.

Let
(A,\mathfrak{m})
be a Cohen-Macaulay local ring of dimension
3
and let
\mathfrak{a}
be an asymptotically normal
\mathfrak{m}
-primary ideal with
e_{3}^{\mathfrak{a}}(A)=0
. Set
L^{\mathfrak{a}}(A)=\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}A/\mathfrak{a}^{n+1}
considered as a module over the Rees algebra
\mathcal{S}=A[\mathfrak{a}t]
. Let
\mathfrak{M}=\mathfrak{m}\oplus\mathcal{S}_{+}
be the maximal homogeneous ideal of
\mathcal{S}
. Then the local cohomology modules
H^{i}_{\mathfrak{M}}(L^{\mathfrak{a}}(A))
vanish for
i=1,2

We will also need to extend Lemma
7.5
to dimensions
d\geq 4
.

7.6
.

Remark and a Convention:
Note that all the relevant graded modules considered upto Lemma
7.11
below are modules over the Rees algebra
\mathcal{S}=A[\mathfrak{a}t]
. Also all local cohomology will
taken over
\mathfrak{M}=\mathfrak{m}\oplus\mathcal{S}_{+}
the maximal homogeneous ideal of
\mathcal{S}
. Note
G_{\mathfrak{a}}(A)
is a quotient of
\mathcal{S}
.
We also note that if
x
is
A
-superficial with respect to
A
then the Rees algebra
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}=A/(x)[\mathfrak{a}/(x)t]
is a quotient of
\mathcal{S}
. As we are only interested in vanishing of certain local-cohomology modules, by the independence theorem of local cohomology
it does not matter if we take local cohomology of an
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
-module with respect to
\mathfrak{M}^{\prime}
or over
\mathfrak{M}
(and considering the module in question as an
\mathcal{S}
-module). So throughout we will only write
H^{i}(-)
to mean
H^{i}_{\mathfrak{M}}(-)
.

Lemma 7.7
.

Let
(A,\mathfrak{m})
be a Cohen-Macaulay local ring of dimension
d\geq 3
and let
\mathfrak{a}
be an asymptotically normal
\mathfrak{m}
-primary ideal with
e_{3}^{\mathfrak{a}}(A)=0
. Then the local cohomology modules
H^{i}(L^{\mathfrak{a}}(A))
vanish for
1\leq i\leq d-1
.

Proof.

We prove the result by induction on
d\geq 3
.
For
d=3
the result follows from Lemma
7.5
. Now assume
d\geq 4
and the result has been proved for
d-1
.
After passing through a general extension (see
2.9
(d)) we may choose
x
, an
A
-superficial element
with respect to
\mathfrak{a}
such that in the ring
B=A/(x)
the ideal
\mathfrak{b}=\mathfrak{a}/(x)
is asymptotically normal. Furthermore note that
e_{3}^{\mathfrak{b}}(B)=0
.
Set
L=L^{\mathfrak{a}}(A)
and
\overline{L}=L^{\mathfrak{b}}(B)
. By induction hypothesis we have
H^{i}(\overline{L})=0
for
1\leq i\leq d-2
. By
6.9.1
we get a surjective map

H^{1}(L)(-1)\rightarrow H^{1}(L)
and for
2\leq i\leq d-1
injections

H^{i}(L)(-1)\rightarrow H^{i}(L)
. By
6.10
it follows that
H^{i}(L)=0
for
i=2,\cdots d-1
. By
6.11
we get that
H^{1}(L)
has finite length. So the surjection

H^{1}(L)(-1)\rightarrow H^{1}(L)
induces an isomorphism
H^{1}(L)(-1)\cong H^{1}(L)
and this forces
H^{1}(L)=0
.
∎

We now give a proof of Theorem
7.1
assuming Lemma
7.5
.

Proof of Theorem
7.1
.

If
d=\dim A=1,2
, the result follows from Lemma
7.3
. If
d\geq 3

then by Lemma
7.7
we get
H^{i}(L^{\mathfrak{a}}(A))=0
for
1\leq i\leq d-1
. Also as
\mathfrak{a}
is normal, in-particular
\mathfrak{a}^{n}
is Ratliff-Rush for all

n\geq 1
. So by
6.5
we get
H^{0}(L^{\mathfrak{a}}(A))=0
. By taking cohomology of the first fundamental sequence
6.7.1
we get that
H^{i}(G_{\mathfrak{a}}(A))=0

for
0\leq i\leq d-1
. Thus
G_{\mathfrak{a}}(A)
is Cohen-Macaulay.
∎

7.8
.

Thus to prove Itoh’s conjecture all we have to do is to prove Lemma
7.5
. This requires several preparatory results.

For the rest of this section we will assume
\dim A=3
and
\mathfrak{a}
is an asymptotically normal ideal with
e_{3}^{\mathfrak{a}}(A)=0
. We will also assume that the residue field of
A
is infinite.
We first show

Lemma 7.9
.

(with hypotheses as in
7.8
.) Then

is Cohen-Macaulay for
n\gg 0

and
\operatorname{red}(\mathfrak{a}^{n})\leq 2
for

Remark 7.10
.

Lemma

is certainly known to the experts. We provide a proof for the convenience of the reader.

Proof of Lemma

Let
\mathfrak{c}=(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})

.
Notice that for all

By a result of Huckaba and Huneke

Fix

[

It follows that

\displaystyle e_{1}^{\mathfrak{a}^{n}}(A)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\geq 0}\sigma_{i},

\displaystyle e_{2}^{\mathfrak{a}^{n}}(A)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\geq 1}i\sigma_{i},\text{and}

\displaystyle e_{3}^{\mathfrak{a}^{n}}(A)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\geq 2}\binom{i}{2}\sigma_{i}.

As

Next we show

.

(with hypotheses as in

(2)

(3)

. By a result of Hoa,

(2) Set

.
Set

.

(3) As

(4) As

Remark 7.15

2.4.
Local factoriality

We do not know if

Lemma 2.3
.

(1)\ Cl(U_{Y}(n,d,L))\cong\mathbb{Z}\,.

(2) Let
\tilde{U}_{Y}(n,d,L)
denote a normalisation of
U_{Y}(n,D,L)
and
\pi:\tilde{U}_{Y}(n,d,L)\rightarrow U_{Y}(n,d,L)

{\rm Pic}\ \tilde{U}_{Y}(n,d,L)\ \cong\ \mathbb{Z}\,.

Proof.

(1) As

is nonsingular in codimension
1
, by
[
10
, Proposition 6.5, p.133]
, we have an isomorphism of class groups

Cl(U^{\prime}_{Y}(n,d,L))\cong Cl(U_{Y}(n,d,L))\,.

Since

is locally factorial, one has

Cl(U^{\prime}_{Y}(n,d,L))\cong\ {\rm Pic}\ U^{\prime}_{Y}(n,d,L)\cong\mathbb{Z}\,.
Hence

Cl(U_{Y}(n,d,L))\cong\mathbb{Z}\,.

(2) There is a commutative diagram

\begin{array}[]{ccc}{\rm Pic}\ (\tilde{U}_{Y}(n,d,L))&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\phi}}{{\longrightarrow}}&Cl(\tilde{U}_{Y}(n,d,L))\\
&&\\
\downarrow res_{P}&&\downarrow{res_{C}}\\
&&\\
{\rm Pic}\ \pi^{-1}(U^{\prime}_{Y}(n,d,L))&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\phi^{\prime}}}{{\longrightarrow}}&Cl(\pi^{-1}U^{\prime}_{Y}(n,d,L))\end{array}

As
\pi^{-1}U^{\prime}_{Y}(n,d,L)\cong U^{\prime}_{Y}(n,d,L),\tilde{U}_{Y}(n,d,L)
is nonsingular in codimension
1
. Hence by
[
10
, Proposition 6.5, p.133]
the map

is an isomorphism. Since
\pi^{-1}U^{\prime}_{Y}(n,d,L)
is locally factorial,
\phi^{\prime}
is an isomorphisms. The map

is a surjection as
{\rm Pic}\ \pi^{-1}U^{\prime}_{Y}(n,d,L)
is generated by the restriction of the line bundle

on

. Since

is normal and

is at least

is injective. Then

is an isomorphism. From the commutativity of the diagram, it follows that

3.4.
Stability of the Poincaré bundle on
Y\times U_{Y}(n,d,L)

The moduli space
U_{Y}(n,d,L)
is unirational
[
7
, Lemma 3.5(1)]
. Hence there is no non-constant map from
U_{Y}(n,d,L)
to Pic
Y
. Using the see-saw theorem
[
11
, Corollary 6, p. 54]
, one sees that every line bundle on
Y\times U_{Y}(n,d,L)
is the tensor product of the pull back of a line bundle on
U_{Y}(n,d,L)
and the pull back of a line bundle on
Y
. Hence we take the polarisation on
Y\times U_{Y}(n,d,L)
represented by a divisor of the form
a\alpha+b\theta_{L}
, where
\alpha
is a fixed ample Cartier divisor on
Y
and
a,b
are positive integers.

Theorem 3.5
.

The Poincaré bundle
\mathcal{U}_{L}
on
Y\times U_{Y}(n,d,L)
is stable with respect to the polarisation
a\alpha+b\theta_{L}
with
a,b
positive integers.

Proof.

Let
Q
be a torsionfree quotient sheaf of
\mathcal{U}_{L}
. It suffices to check that

r(\mathcal{U}_{L})d(Q)-r(Q)d(\mathcal{U}_{L})\ >\ 0\,.

Let
E\in U^{\prime}_{Y}(n,d,L)
be a general element. Let
Q_{Y}=Q|_{Y\times E}
. Then
Q_{Y}
is a quotient of the locally free sheaf
E
. The quotient
Q_{Y}
may not be torsionfree, let
Q^{\prime}_{Y}:=Q_{Y}/tor
be the quotient of
Q_{Y}
by its torsion subsheaf. Let
Y^{\prime}
be the set of smooth points of
Y
.

Since
Y^{\prime}\times U^{\prime}_{Y}(n,d,L)
is smooth, there is an open subset
Z^{\prime}\subset Y^{\prime}\times U^{\prime}_{Y}(n,d,L)
whose complement in
Y^{\prime}\times U^{\prime}_{Y}(n,d,L)
is of codimension at least
2
such that
Q
is locally free over
Z^{\prime}
. For a general
E\in U^{\prime}_{Y}(n,d,L)
,
Z^{\prime}
contains the curve
Y^{\prime}\times E
. This implies that
Q_{Y}
has torsion at most at nodes of
Y
and
r(Q)=r(Q_{Y})=r(Q^{\prime}),d(Q_{Y})\geq d(Q^{\prime}_{Y})
on
Y
. Since
E
is stable, one has
r(E)d(Q^{\prime}_{Y})-r(Q^{\prime}_{Y})d(E)>0
. This implies that

(3.6)

r(\mathcal{U}_{L})d(Q_{Y})-r(Q)d(E)>0\,.

Since the complement of
Z^{\prime}
in
Y^{\prime}\times U^{\prime}_{Y}(n,d,L)
is of codimension at least
2
, for a general
x\in Y^{\prime}
,
Z^{\prime}\cap(x\times U^{\prime}_{Y}(n,d,L))
has complement of codimension at least
2

x\times U^{\prime}_{Y}(n,d,L)
. Since
U_{Y}(n,d,L)-U^{\prime}_{Y}(n,d,L)
has codimension at least
3
(
[
6
, Proposition 2.1]
), it follows that the open subset
Z^{\prime}\cap(x\times U^{\prime}_{Y}(n,d,L))
has complement of codimension at least
2
in
x\times U_{Y}(n,d,L)
. Since
x
is a non-singular point, the sheaf
Q_{x}:=Q|_{x\times U_{Y}(n,d,L)}
is locally free on
Z^{\prime}\cap(x\times U_{Y}(n,d,L))
and
d(Q_{x})=d(Q_{x}|_{Z^{\prime}\cap(x\times U^{\prime}_{Y}(n,d,L))})
. For
x
a non-singular point,
\mathcal{U}_{L,x}=\mathcal{U}|_{x\times U_{Y}(n,d,L)}
is a stable vector bundle by Theorem
3.3
. Hence,

r(\mathcal{U}_{L,x})d(Q_{x})-r(Q_{x})d(\mathcal{U}_{L,x})>0
, i.e.

(3.7)

r(\mathcal{U}_{L})d(Q_{x})-r(Q)d(\mathcal{U}_{L,x})>0\,.

Let
m:=\ {\rm dim}\ U_{Y}(n,d,L)
.
One has

\begin{array}[]{ccl}d(\mathcal{U}_{L})&=&(c_{1}(\mathcal{U}_{L}).(b\theta_{L}+a\alpha)^{m})[Y\times U_{Y}(n,d,L)]\\
&=&c_{1}(\mathcal{U}_{L}).(mab^{m-1}\alpha\theta_{L}^{m-1}+b^{m}\theta_{L}^{m})[Y\times U_{Y}(n,d,L)]\\
&=&mab^{m-1}\alpha[Y]d(\mathcal{U}_{L,x})+b^{m}d(E)\theta_{L}^{m}[U_{Y}(n,d,L)]\\
\end{array}

Similarly,

d(Q)=mab^{m-1}\alpha[Y]d(\mathcal{Q}_{x})+b^{m}d(Q_{Y})\theta_{L}^{m}[U_{Y}(n,d,L)]\,.

Then

\begin{array}[]{ccl}r(\mathcal{U}_{L})d(Q)-r(Q)d(\mathcal{U}_{L})&=&mab^{m-1}\alpha[Y](r(\mathcal{U}_{L})d(\mathcal{Q}_{x})-r(Q)d(\mathcal{U}_{L,x}))\\
&&+b^{m}\theta_{L}^{m}[U_{Y}(n,d,L)](r(\mathcal{U}_{L})d(Q_{Y})-r(Q)d(E))\\
&>&0\,,\end{array}

by (
3.6
) and (
3.7
).

This completes the proof of the theorem.

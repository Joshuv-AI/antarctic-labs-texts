𝔸 1 \mathbb{A}^{1} -homotopy theory of log schemes
Doosung Park

Abstract
. We construct the 𝔸 1 \mathbb{A}^{1} -local stable motivic homotopy categories of fs log schemes. For schemes with the trivial log structure, our construction is equivalent to the original construction of Morel-Voevodsky. We prove the localization property. As a consequence, we obtain the Grothendieck six functors formalism for strict morphisms of fs log schemes. We extend 𝔸 1 \mathbb{A}^{1} -invariant cohomology theories of schemes to fs log schemes. In particular, we define motivic cohomology, homotopy K K -theory, and algebraic cobordism of fs log schemes. For any fs log scheme log smooth over a scheme, we express cohomology of its boundary in terms of cohomology of schemes.

4.4.
Cohomology theories

Throughout this subsection,
B
is a base scheme with an object
\mathbb{E}\in\mathcal{SH}(B)
.
For every morphism
p\colon X\to B
in
\mathbf{lSch}
and integers
p
and
q
, the
\mathbb{E}
-cohomology of
X
is defined to be

(4.4.1)

\mathbb{E}^{p,q}(X):=\Hom_{\mathcal{SH}(X)}(\Sigma^{p,q}\Sigma_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}^{\infty}X_{+},p^{*}\mathbb{E}).

Example 4.4.1
.

Consider the following three fundamental examples of
\mathbb{E}
:

(i)

motivic Eilenberg-MacLane spectrum
\mathrm{M}\Lambda
,

(ii)

homotopy
K
-theory spectrum
\mathrm{KGL}
,

(iii)

algebraic cobordism spectrum
\mathrm{MGL}
.

Voevodsky introduce these in
[
30
, Section 6]
, but the definition of
\mathrm{M}\Lambda
that we will use below is Spitzweck’s version
[
29
, Definition 4.27]
.
He defined
\mathrm{M}\mathbb{Z}
in
\mathcal{SH}(\Spec(\mathbb{Z}))
.
If
q\colon B\to\Spec(\mathbb{Z})
denotes the structure morphism, his definition of
\mathrm{M}\mathbb{Z}
in
\mathcal{SH}(B)
is
q^{*}\mathrm{M}\mathbb{Z}
.
The two different definitions of
\mathrm{M}\mathbb{Z}
are equivalent if
B
is smooth over a field by
[
29
, Theorem 8.22]
.
Spitzweck’s construction can be generalized to any ring
\Lambda
, see the statement of
[
29
, Corollary 10.4]
.

If
p\colon X\to B
is a morphism in
\mathbf{Sch}
, then
p^{*}\mathrm{KGL}\simeq\mathrm{KGL}
and
p^{*}\mathrm{MGL}\simeq\mathrm{MGL}
because
\mathrm{KGL}
and
\mathrm{MGL}
admit geometric models.
By definition,
p^{*}\mathrm{M}\Lambda\simeq\mathrm{M}\Lambda
.

Definition 4.4.2
.

Suppose
X\in\mathbf{lSch}/B
and
p
and
q
are integers.
The
motivic cohomology of
X
is defined to be

H_{\mathcal{M}}^{p}(X,\Lambda(q)):=\mathrm{M}\Lambda^{p,q}(X).

The
homotopy
K
-theory spectrum of
X
is defined to be

\mathrm{KH}(X):=\mathrm{map}_{\mathcal{SH}(X)}(\Sigma_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}^{\infty}X_{+},\mathrm{KGL}).

This is equivalent to the original definition when
X
has the trivial log structure due to
[
7
]
.
The
algebraic cobordism of
X
is defined to be
\mathrm{MGL}^{p,q}(X)
.

Corollary 4.4.3
.

Suppose
X\in\mathbf{lSm}/B
.
For every integer
q
, there is a canonical long exact sequence

\cdots\to\mathbb{E}^{p,q}(\partial X)\to\mathbb{E}^{p,q}(X-\partial X)\oplus\mathbb{E}^{p,q}(\underline{\partial X})\to\mathbb{E}^{p,q}(\underline{X})\to\mathbb{E}^{p+1,q}(\partial X)\to\cdots

Proof.

Immediate from Theorem
4.3.8
since
\mathbb{E}^{p,q}(X)\simeq\mathbb{E}^{p,q}(X-\partial X)
.
∎

Example 4.4.4
.

Suppose
X=\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{N},B}
.
Then Corollary
4.4.3
gives an isomorphism

\mathbb{E}^{p,q}(\mathrm{pt}_{\mathbb{N},B})\simeq\mathbb{E}^{p,q}(\mathbb{G}_{m,B})

for all integers
p
and
q
.

Remark 4.4.5
.

Suppose
k
is a field and
X\to\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{N}^{r},k}
is a strict smooth morphism in
\mathbf{lSch}
for some integer
r\geq 0
.
In general, our motivic cohomology
H_{\mathcal{M}}^{p}(\partial X,\mathbb{Z}(q))
is not isomorphic to the log-motivic cohomology
H_{\log-\mathcal{M}}^{p}(\partial X,\mathbb{Z}(q))
of Gregory-Langer in
[
14
]
.
For example, if
X=\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{N},k}
, then

H_{\mathcal{M}}^{1}(\partial X,\mathbb{Z}(1))\simeq H_{\mathcal{M}}^{1}(\mathbb{G}_{m,k},\mathbb{Z}(1))\simeq k^{*}\oplus\mathbb{Z}\simeq H^{0}(\partial X,\mathcal{M}_{\partial X}^{\mathrm{gp}})

by Example
4.4.4
, while

H_{\log-\mathcal{M}}^{1}(\partial X,\mathbb{Z}(1))\simeq H^{0}(\partial X,\mathcal{M}_{\partial X}^{\mathrm{gp}}/\mathbb{Z}_{\partial X})\simeq k^{*}.

Nevertheless, we expect that these two cohomology theories are closely related.
In the above case, the factor
\mathbb{Z}
in
k^{*}\oplus\mathbb{Z}\simeq H_{\mathcal{M}}^{1}(\partial X,\mathbb{Z}(1))
is the only difference.

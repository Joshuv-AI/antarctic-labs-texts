Stability of the non-perturbative O ⁡ ( D , D ) O(D,D) de-Sitter spacetime. The isotropic case.
Przemysław Bieniek, Jan Chojnacki, Jan H. Kwapisz, Krzysztof A. Meissner

Abstract
The existence of de-Sitter solutions and their stability within string theory became, in recent years, one of the central research questions within the string theory community. The so-called de-Sitter conjecture states that the de-Sitter vacua are unstable within string theory. Here, we study the de-Sitter solutions in string theory and their stability in the time direction using the O ⁡ ( d , d ) O(d,d) symmetry approach allowing for the calculation of the generic form of the string theory effective action. We study the undiscussed case of the constant generalized dilaton as well as the general form of dilaton couplings. Our findings suggest that de-Sitter solutions are unstable for the constant generalized dilaton both with and without matter. Furthermore, we found that in order for the theory to be weakly coupled the general form of dilaton coupling is in the form of a cosmological constant.

unless the constant potential.

For
f(H_{0})=0
we get

\displaystyle\dot{\Phi}^{2}+g(H_{0})

\displaystyle=-2V(\Phi),

(40)

\displaystyle 2\ddot{\Phi}-\dot{\Phi}^{2}-g(H_{0})

\displaystyle=2\left(V(\Phi)-\frac{\partial V}{\partial\Phi}\right),

adding the equations we get the equation for the dilaton evolution as

\displaystyle\ddot{\Phi}=-\frac{\partial V}{\partial\Phi}.

(41)

together with the constraint equation for
\dot{\Phi}

\displaystyle\dot{\Phi}^{2}

\displaystyle=-2V(\Phi)-g(H_{0}).

(42)

Note that for the above equation to hold, one requires
-1/2g(H_{0})>V(\Phi)
,
thus the potential has to be to bounded from above. Secondly note that the (
41
) is the equation for the particle in potential
V(\Phi)
. We have inspected the numerical solutions of the (
41
) for potential of the form
V(\Phi)=-c_{n}\Phi^{2n}
, for
n\in(2,\ldots,10)
as well as
V(\Phi)=-c\exp(\Phi)
with
c>0,c_{n}>0
. We found that the generalized dilaton necessary develops a negative value during the time evolution (the special case of
n=1
will be discussed below) and furthermore at some point
\Phi<-dHt
. On the other hand, in order for the theory to be weakly coupled one requires
e^{-\phi}\ll 1
and thus
\phi>0
, where
\phi=\Phi+dHt
for the de-Sitter solutions. In particular, for Minkowski spacetime
\phi=\Phi
. Thus for the de-Sitter spacetime the theory becomes strongly coupled for
n>1
.

Let us now discuss the special case of
V(\Phi)=-\frac{1}{2}m^{2}\Phi^{2}
the analytical solution of (
41
) is
\Phi=\mathbf{c}_{1}\cosh(mt)+\mathbf{c}_{2}\sinh(mt).
Plugging this solution into the (
42
) one gets
m=0
.

We conclude that for the weakly coupled theory, in order to have de-Sitter solution, one requires
\frac{\partial V}{\partial\Phi}=0.
We shall now discuss the cosmological constant case, where
V(\Phi)=\Lambda
.

III.2
The cosmological constant case

The cosmological constant term given by
\Lambda=\frac{d-d_{\mathrm{crit}}}{\alpha^{\prime}}
is in fact required for the noncritical strings to remain Weyl invariant and together with
\dot{\Phi}=0
is natural extension of the formalism discussed above to the non-critical strings case.
For the cosmological constant case from equations (
34
) and (
35
) we find:

\displaystyle\tilde{\rho}=-\Lambda,\quad\tilde{\sigma}=2\Lambda,\quad\tilde{p}=0.

(43)

Leading to equations of motion:

\displaystyle\dot{\Phi}^{2}+g(H)

\displaystyle=-2\Lambda,

(44)

\displaystyle f^{\prime}(H)\dot{H}-\dot{\Phi}f(H)

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle 2\ddot{\Phi}-\dot{\Phi}^{2}+Hf(H)-g(H)

\displaystyle=2\Lambda.

de-Sitter solution is now given by
g(H_{0})=-2\Lambda
and as before we have either
\dot{\Phi}=0
or
f(H_{0})=0
. In the
\dot{\Phi}=0
case, we get

\displaystyle g(H_{0})

\displaystyle=-2\Lambda,

(45)

\displaystyle Hf(H_{0})-g(H_{0})

\displaystyle=2\Lambda.

and thus
f(H_{0})=0
. Let us proceed the stability of the
f(H_{0})=0
case. Similarly to (
18
), we find the first derivative of Hubble parameter

\displaystyle\dot{H}=\pm\frac{f(H)}{f^{\prime}(H)}\sqrt{-2\kappa^{2}\Lambda-g(H)}.

(46)

It is vanishing at
H=H_{0}
. The rest of the reasoning leading to (
24
) remains unchanged.
This is true, however, for a constant value of the dilaton first derivative
\dot{\Phi}=0
. As previously described in
Bernardo and Franzmann 2020
, for the general, time dependent
\Phi(t)
, we have the stable branch of solutions with
\dot{\Phi}<0
and the unstable branch
\dot{\Phi}>0
. The instability is present, regardless of the sign of the cosmological constant.

The generalized dilaton is also unstable around the de-Sitter solution. To investigate it, let us start the evolution around a de-Sitter solution
H_{0}
by adding a small perturbation
\Delta
to it. As we have discussed before,
H
will grow with time. We further assume a general power-law behaviour of the Hubble parameter and approximate:

\displaystyle f(H)

\displaystyle=A\left(H-H_{0}\right),

(47)

\displaystyle H

\displaystyle=H_{0}+\Delta+at^{n}.

(48)

From the first equation in (
I.2
) we have

\displaystyle\dot{\Phi}=\frac{f^{\prime}(H)}{f(H)}\dot{H},

(49)

After integrating over time and changing the variables we are left with

\displaystyle\Phi\left(t\right)=\ln\left|\frac{f(H)}{f(H_{0}+\Delta)}\right|=\ln\left|1+\frac{a}{\Delta}t^{n}\right|.

(50)

Clearly, the dilaton grows unboundedly with time. which means that the system of differential equations is unstable in both ”directions”
H
and
\Phi
.
We wish to explain how our work relates to the investigations of
Bernardo and Franzmann 2020
. The authors present a thorough discussion of stability around equilibria for a general equation of state. They find eigenvalues of the matrix describing the linearized system (
37
):

\displaystyle\dot{H}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{F^{\prime\prime}}\left(yF^{\prime}-d\omega(y^{2}+HF^{\prime}-F)\right),

(51)

\displaystyle\dot{y}

\displaystyle=\frac{y^{2}}{2}-\frac{F}{2}+\frac{\lambda}{4}(y^{2}+HF^{\prime}-F).

Here,
F^{\prime}(H)=f(H)
,
y=\dot{\Phi}
,
\omega=p/\rho
,
\lambda=\sigma/\rho
. The linearization matrix
A=\partial_{H,y}C_{i}(X)
is then

\displaystyle A=\begin{pmatrix}y_{0}-dH_{0}\omega&\frac{1}{F^{\prime\prime}_{0}}(F^{\prime}_{0}-2d\omega y_{0})\\
-\frac{F^{\prime}_{0}}{2}+\frac{\lambda}{4}H_{0}F^{\prime\prime}_{0}&y_{0}(1+\frac{\lambda}{2})\end{pmatrix},

(52)

where the subscript zero relates to the values at the de-Sitter point
H=H_{0}
. In the cosmological constant case:
\omega=0,\quad\lambda=-2,\quad F^{\prime}_{0}=f(H_{0})=0
. The linearization matrix simplifies greatly:

\displaystyle A=\begin{pmatrix}y_{0}&0\\
-\frac{1}{2}H_{0}F^{\prime\prime}_{0}&0\end{pmatrix}.

(53)

The eigenvalues are
\alpha_{\pm}=\{0,y_{0}\}
, so the stability of the system depends on
y_{0}=\dot{\Phi}(H_{0})
. If it was negative, the system would be stable. From the second equation in (
37
) evaluated in
H=H_{0}
we have:

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\left(e^{-\Phi}f(H_{0})\right)=0,

(54)

hence
\dot{\Phi}(H_{0})=0
. Since both of the eigenvalues are zero, the linear analysis is not sufficient to conclude that the point
H_{0}
is stable. The second-order analysis described in the previous section shows that the de-Sitter vacuum is unstable. Nevertheless, the discussion of the solution with
\dot{\Phi}(H_{0})\neq 0
in
Bernardo and Franzmann 2020
is still valid and leads to a stable de-Sitter vacuum for
\dot{\Phi}(H_{0})<0
.

IV
Conclusions

In this paper we have studied the stability of the de-Sitter solutions in string theory using the non-perturbative effective action derived within the
O(d,d)
symmetry. In particular, we have studied the
\dot{\Phi}=0
vacuum solution, resembling the non-critical strings linear dilaton CFT solution, using the non-linear analysis and an exemplary solution
f(H)\sim\sin\left(\sqrt{\alpha^{\prime}}H\right)
. For the matter case we have shown that in order for the theory to be weakly coupled during evolution one requires the generic dilaton potential to be constant. We have built a simple framework employing the idea of the effective potential for the stability analysis in the T-dual theories. The approach is intuitive and less tedious, than more conventional expansion of the EOMs to the second-order variations. Our results concerning the constant generalized dilaton greatly impact de Sitter theories with rolling dilaton, yielding them unstable.

Quasi-fractionalization of spin in a cluster-based Haldane state supported by chirality of a triangular spin tube
Takanori Sugimoto, Takami Tohyama

Abstract
Fractionalization of quantum degrees of freedom holds the key to finding new phenomena in physics, e.g., the quark model in hadron physics, the spin-charge separation in strongly-correlated electron systems, and the fractional quantum Hall effect. A typical example of the fractionalization in quantum spin systems is the spin- 1 1 Haldane state, whose intriguing characteristics are well described by fractionalized S = 1 / 2 S=1/2 virtual spins in a bilinear-biquadratic spin- 1 1 chain, the so-called Affleck–Kennedy–Lieb–Tasaki model, delivering two individual spin- 1 / 2 1/2 degrees of freedom as edge states. Here we theoretically propose an exotic extension of the Haldane state and chirality in a triangular spin tube, inducing a quasi -fractionalization of spin- 1 / 2 1/2 degree of freedom, i.e., a quarter spin. Existence of the edge state is confirmed both analytically and numerically, combining a low-energy perturbation theory and variational matrix-product state method. Our study can not only propose a new quantum spin property but pave a way to novel quantum states of matter.

Historically, developments of physics have often been supported by discoveries of new fractionalization mechanism and derivative hidden degrees of freedom.

Main characteristics in the Haldane state, which is the ground state in an

In the AKLT model, an

1

To restore the

;

This procedure can be extended into more than two spins at each site, resulting in the cluster-based Haldane state (CBHS) in a spin cluster chain (SCC)

Meanwhile, with the open boundary condition, it is well known that the original Haldane state exhibits two edge states corresponding to spin-
1/2

1

Similarly, in the CBHS, there should be the edge states, while the edge site contains many
S=1/2

Hence, a naive question arises; if we obtain a way to detect only one of the spins at the edges, how does the spin degree of freedom behave in this state, delivering a new type of fractionalization?

To answer this question, in this letter, we consider a CBHS appearing in a triangular spin tube (TST) coupled with chirality degree of freedom [see Fig.
1

In this model, the chirality plays the role of an
S=1/2

;
Lüscher et al. 2004

If we add a Heisenberg-type coupling between the real and pseudo spins, it can split the four-fold ground-state degeneracy into a triplet and a singlet in a triangle cluster.
The triplet state is regarded as an
S=1
spin as a result of hybridization of the real and pseudo spins.
Thus, introducing antiferromagnetic interactions between neighboring triangles which are much smaller than the intra-cluster interactions induces a CBHS.
Since the chirality is not directly coupled with the magnetic field, only the real-spin component at edge site can be controlled with applying the magnetic field.

Lastly, we comment on a possibility of experimental setup of the TST model.
In our model, the Heisenberg terms in a TST, i.e.,
\mathcal{H}_{J}^{(j)}
and
\mathcal{H}_{J^{\prime}}^{(j)}

Hence, the coupling term of the real spin and the chirality

To understand this term in detail, we rewrite it in original spin operators, leading to

\displaystyle S_{\mathrm{tot},j}^{z}\chi_{j}^{z}=\frac{1}{4\sqrt{3}}\sum_{i,i^{\prime},i^{\prime\prime}}\epsilon_{ii^{\prime}i^{\prime\prime}}(\bm{S}_{i^{\prime},j}\times\bm{S}_{i^{\prime\prime},j})^{z},

\displaystyle S_{\mathrm{tot},j}^{+}\chi_{j}^{-}+\mathrm{H.c.}=-\mathcal{P}_{j}^{d}(S_{1,j}^{x}+S_{2,j}^{-\phi}+S_{3,j}^{\phi})\mathcal{P}_{j}^{d}.

Note that in this case, the intra-cluster interactions need to be approximately greater than the energy gap

Therefore, this term may be found in a chiral magnet holding a vector chirality, with applied the transverse herical magnetic field at low temperatures.

In conclusion, we have proposed an exotic extension of the Haldane state to show a novel mechanism of fractionalization of edge states.
In this model, the chirality degree of freedom is regarded as an
S=1/2
pseudo spin.
The real and pseudo spins are symmetrized in the Haldane state of our model, so that an
S=1/2
spin degree of freedom appearing as the edge states consists of the real and pseudo spin components.
Since the magnetic field is directly coupled with only the real spin, we can observe a half of the magnitude of edge states, corresponding to a quarter spin.
Our concept not only gives a new quantum spin feature, but also implies various possibilities of quantum fractionalization.

\displaystyle\chi_{j}^{x}=(\chi_{j}^{+}+\chi_{j}^{-})/2,\ \chi_{j}^{y}=(\chi_{j}^{+}-\chi_{j}^{-})/(2\imath),\ \chi_{j}^{z}=\chi_{j}/2

(19)

with the chirality ladder operators
\chi_{j}^{\pm}\equiv\sum_{\sigma=\pm\frac{1}{2}}\ket{\sigma,\pm 1}_{j}\bra{\sigma,\mp 1}_{j}
and the imaginary unit
\imath=\sqrt{-1}
. Here,
\ket{\sigma,c}
(
\sigma=\pm 1/2
and
c=\pm 1
) represents the simultaneous eigenstates of the total spin
S_{\mathrm{tot},j}^{z}
and the scalar chirality.

The triplet ground states with
J>0
and
K>0
in the cluster Hamiltonians (
16
) and (
18
) are given by,

\displaystyle\widetilde{\ket{1}}_{j}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}\left(e^{-\imath\phi}\ket{\uparrow\uparrow\downarrow}_{j}+e^{\imath\phi}\ket{\uparrow\downarrow\uparrow}_{j}+\ket{\downarrow\uparrow\uparrow}_{j}\right),

(20)

\displaystyle\widetilde{\ket{0}}_{j}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{6}}\big(e^{\imath\phi}\ket{\uparrow\uparrow\downarrow}_{j}+e^{-\imath\phi}\ket{\uparrow\downarrow\uparrow}_{j}+\ket{\downarrow\uparrow\uparrow}_{j}

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt\hskip 20.00003pt-e^{-\imath\phi}\ket{\downarrow\downarrow\uparrow}_{j}-e^{\imath\phi}\ket{\downarrow\uparrow\downarrow}_{j}-\ket{\uparrow\downarrow\downarrow}_{j}\big),

(21)

\displaystyle\widetilde{\ket{-1}}_{j}=-\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}\left(e^{\imath\phi}\ket{\downarrow\downarrow\uparrow}_{j}+e^{-\imath\phi}\ket{\downarrow\uparrow\downarrow}_{j}+\ket{\uparrow\downarrow\downarrow}_{j}\right),

(22)

with
\phi=2\pi/3
. The ket states in the right-hand side denote the direct products of one-spin eigenstates, e.g.,
\ket{\uparrow\downarrow\uparrow}_{j}=\ket{\uparrow}_{1,j}\ket{\downarrow}_{2,j}\ket{\uparrow}_{3,j}
.
By using the triplet states, the projection operator is defined by
\mathcal{P}_{j}=\sum_{m=0,\pm 1}\widetilde{\ket{m}}_{j}\widetilde{\bra{m}}_{j}
.

At low temperatures
T\ll J,K
, the low-energy physics is well described by the triplet states, neglecting the other states of cluster.
Since the triplet states correspond to the eigenstates of an
S=1
spin, we can write projected operators of the local spins with the
S=1
pseudo spin operator
\widetilde{\bm{S}}_{j}
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{j}\bm{S}_{1,j}\mathcal{P}_{j}=\mathcal{P}_{j}\begin{pmatrix}S_{1,j}^{x}\\
S_{1,j}^{y}\\
S_{1,j}^{z}\end{pmatrix}\mathcal{P}_{j}=\frac{1}{3}\begin{pmatrix}\widetilde{S}_{j}^{x}-(\widetilde{S}_{j}^{+})^{2}-(\widetilde{S}_{j}^{-})^{2}+2(\widetilde{S}_{j}^{z})^{2}-2\\
\widetilde{S}_{j}^{y}+\imath(\widetilde{S}_{j}^{+})^{2}-\imath(\widetilde{S}_{j}^{-})^{2}\\
\widetilde{S}_{j}^{z}-\widetilde{S}_{j}^{z}\widetilde{S}^{+}-\widetilde{S}_{j}^{z}\widetilde{S}^{-}-\widetilde{S}_{j}^{+}\widetilde{S}_{j}^{z}-\widetilde{S}_{j}^{-}\widetilde{S}_{j}^{z}\end{pmatrix},

(23)

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{j}\bm{S}_{2,j}\mathcal{P}_{j}=\mathcal{P}_{j}\begin{pmatrix}S_{2,j}^{x}\\
S_{2,j}^{y}\\
S_{2,j}^{z}\end{pmatrix}\mathcal{P}_{j}=\frac{1}{3}\begin{pmatrix}\widetilde{S}_{j}^{x}-e^{\imath\phi}(\widetilde{S}_{j}^{+})^{2}-e^{-\imath\phi}(\widetilde{S}_{j}^{-})^{2}-(\widetilde{S}_{j}^{z})^{2}+1\\
\widetilde{S}_{j}^{y}-e^{\imath\phi/4}(\widetilde{S}_{j}^{+})^{2}-e^{-\imath\phi/4}(\widetilde{S}_{j}^{-})^{2}-\sqrt{3}(\widetilde{S}_{j}^{z})^{2}+\sqrt{3}\\
\widetilde{S}_{j}^{z}-e^{\imath\phi}\widetilde{S}_{j}^{z}\widetilde{S}^{+}-e^{-\imath\phi}\widetilde{S}_{j}^{z}\widetilde{S}^{-}-e^{\imath\phi}\widetilde{S}_{j}^{+}\widetilde{S}_{j}^{z}-e^{-\imath\phi}\widetilde{S}_{j}^{-}\widetilde{S}_{j}^{z}\end{pmatrix},

(24)

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{j}\bm{S}_{3,j}\mathcal{P}_{j}=\mathcal{P}_{j}\begin{pmatrix}S_{3,j}^{x}\\
S_{3,j}^{y}\\
S_{3,j}^{z}\end{pmatrix}\mathcal{P}_{j}=\frac{1}{3}\begin{pmatrix}\widetilde{S}_{j}^{x}-e^{-\imath\phi}(\widetilde{S}_{j}^{+})^{2}-e^{\imath\phi}(\widetilde{S}_{j}^{-})^{2}-(\widetilde{S}_{j}^{z})^{2}+1\\
\widetilde{S}_{j}^{y}+e^{-\imath\phi/4}(\widetilde{S}_{j}^{+})^{2}+e^{\imath\phi/4}(\widetilde{S}_{j}^{-})^{2}+\sqrt{3}(\widetilde{S}_{j}^{z})^{2}-\sqrt{3}\\
\widetilde{S}_{j}^{z}-e^{-\imath\phi}\widetilde{S}_{j}^{z}\widetilde{S}^{+}-e^{\imath\phi}\widetilde{S}_{j}^{z}\widetilde{S}^{-}-e^{-\imath\phi}\widetilde{S}_{j}^{+}\widetilde{S}_{j}^{z}-e^{\imath\phi}\widetilde{S}_{j}^{-}\widetilde{S}_{j}^{z}\end{pmatrix}.

(25)

Although it is not so easy to calculate the inter-cluster interactions step by step with these operators, the total spin has a simple form
\mathcal{P}_{j}\bm{S}_{\mathrm{tot},j}\mathcal{P}_{j}=\widetilde{\bm{S}}_{j}
.
Therefore, we can easily obtain the effective Hamiltonian of an equivalent case of the inter-cluster interactions
J_{1}^{\prime}=J_{2}^{\prime}(\equiv J^{\prime})
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{eff}}^{(j)}|_{J_{1}^{\prime}=J_{2}^{\prime}=J^{\prime}}=(\mathcal{P}_{j}\mathcal{P}_{j+1})\mathcal{H}_{J^{\prime}}^{(j)}|_{J_{1}^{\prime}=J_{2}^{\prime}}(\mathcal{P}_{j}\mathcal{P}_{j+1})

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt=J^{\prime}(\mathcal{P}_{j}\mathcal{P}_{j+1})(\bm{S}_{\mathrm{tot},j}\cdot\bm{S}_{\mathrm{tot},j+1})(\mathcal{P}_{j}\mathcal{P}_{j+1})=J^{\prime}\widetilde{\bm{S}}_{j}\cdot\widetilde{\bm{S}}_{j+1}.

(26)

On the other hand, though a discord case
J_{1}^{\prime}\neq J_{2}^{\prime}
is not so easy, the case of
J_{2}^{\prime}=0
is relatively easy to obtain.
Moreover, if the effective Hamiltonian of (i)
J_{1}^{\prime}\neq 0
and
J_{2}^{\prime}=0
is obtained, we can also obtain the effective Hamiltonian for (ii)
J_{1}^{\prime}=0
and
J_{2}^{\prime}\neq 0
, because the equivalent case of effective Hamiltonian (
26
) is the sum of both the cases,

\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{eff}}^{(j)}|_{J_{1}^{\prime}=J_{2}^{\prime}=J^{\prime}}=\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{eff}}^{(j)}|_{J_{1}^{\prime}=J^{\prime}\neq 0,J_{2}^{\prime}=0}+\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{eff}}^{(j)}|_{J_{1}^{\prime}=0,J_{2}^{\prime}=J^{\prime}\neq 0}.

(27)

Then, to obtain the effective Hamiltonian in general case, it is sufficient to show the case of (i)
J_{1}^{\prime}\neq 0
and
J_{2}^{\prime}=0
.
With the relation
\sum_{i}\left(\mathcal{P}_{j}\bm{S}_{i,j}\mathcal{P}_{j}-\frac{1}{3}\widetilde{\bm{S}}_{j}\right)=0
, the effective Hamiltonian of the
J_{1}^{\prime}
term is rewritten by,

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{eff}}^{(j)}|_{J_{1}^{\prime}\neq 0,J_{2}^{\prime}=0}

\displaystyle=J_{1}^{\prime}\sum_{\alpha,i}(\mathcal{P}_{j}\mathcal{P}_{j+1})(S_{i,j}^{\alpha}S_{i,j+1}^{\alpha})(\mathcal{P}_{j}\mathcal{P}_{j+1})

\displaystyle=J_{1}^{\prime}\sum_{\alpha,i}\left[\left(\mathcal{P}_{j}S_{i,j}^{\alpha}\mathcal{P}_{j}-\frac{1}{3}\widetilde{S}_{j}^{\alpha}\right)+\frac{1}{3}\widetilde{S}_{j}^{\alpha}\right]\left[\left(\mathcal{P}_{j+1}S_{i,j+1}^{\alpha}\mathcal{P}_{j+1}-\frac{1}{3}\widetilde{S}_{j+1}^{\alpha}\right)+\frac{1}{3}\widetilde{S}_{j+1}^{\alpha}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{J_{1}^{\prime}}{3}\widetilde{\bm{S}}_{j}\cdot\widetilde{\bm{S}}_{j+1}+J_{1}^{\prime}\sum_{\alpha,i}\left(\mathcal{P}_{j}S_{i,j}^{\alpha}\mathcal{P}_{j}-\frac{1}{3}\widetilde{S}_{j}^{\alpha}\right)\left(\mathcal{P}_{j+1}S_{i,j+1}^{\alpha}\mathcal{P}_{j+1}-\frac{1}{3}\widetilde{S}_{j+1}^{\alpha}\right).

(28)

According to (
23
)–(
25
), we can rewrite the second term into

\displaystyle\sum_{\alpha,i}\left(\mathcal{P}_{j}S_{i,j}^{\alpha}\mathcal{P}_{j}-\frac{1}{3}\widetilde{S}_{j}^{\alpha}\right)\left(\mathcal{P}_{j+1}S_{i,j+1}^{\alpha}\mathcal{P}_{j+1}-\frac{1}{3}\widetilde{S}_{j+1}^{\alpha}\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt=\frac{1}{9}\sum_{\alpha,i}\sum_{m,n}(a_{i,m}^{\alpha}O_{j,m}^{\alpha})(a_{i,n}^{\alpha}O_{j+1,n}^{\alpha})

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt=\frac{1}{9}\sum_{\alpha}\sum_{m,n}O_{j,m}^{\alpha}\left(\sum_{i}a_{i,m}^{\alpha}a_{i,n}^{\alpha}\right)O_{j+1,n}^{\alpha},

(29)

with coefficient and operator vectors,
\bm{a}_{i}^{\alpha}=\{a_{i,m}^{\alpha}\}
and
\bm{O}_{j}^{\alpha}=\{O_{j,m}^{\alpha}\}
(
m=1,2,3,4
) given by,

\displaystyle\bm{a}_{1}^{x}=-\begin{pmatrix}1,&1,&-2,&2\end{pmatrix},

(30)

\displaystyle\bm{a}_{2}^{x}=-\begin{pmatrix}e^{\imath\phi},&e^{-\imath\phi},&1,&-1\end{pmatrix},

(31)

\displaystyle\bm{a}_{3}^{x}=-\begin{pmatrix}e^{-\imath\phi},&e^{\imath\phi},&1,&-1\end{pmatrix},

(32)

\displaystyle\bm{a}_{1}^{y}=\begin{pmatrix}\imath,&-\imath,&0,&0\end{pmatrix},

(33)

\displaystyle\bm{a}_{2}^{y}=-\begin{pmatrix}e^{\imath\phi/4},&e^{-\imath\phi/4},&\sqrt{3},&-\sqrt{3}\end{pmatrix},

(34)

\displaystyle\bm{a}_{3}^{y}=\begin{pmatrix}e^{-\imath\phi/4},&e^{\imath\phi/4},&\sqrt{3},&-\sqrt{3}\end{pmatrix},

(35)

\displaystyle\bm{a}_{1}^{z}=-\begin{pmatrix}1,&1,&1,&1\end{pmatrix},

(36)

\displaystyle\bm{a}_{2}^{z}=-\begin{pmatrix}e^{\imath\phi},&e^{-\imath\phi},&e^{\imath\phi},&e^{-\imath\phi}\end{pmatrix},

(37)

\displaystyle\bm{a}_{3}^{z}=-\begin{pmatrix}e^{-\imath\phi},&e^{\imath\phi},&e^{-\imath\phi},&e^{\imath\phi}\end{pmatrix},

(38)

and

\displaystyle\bm{O}_{j}^{x}=\bm{O}_{j}^{y}=\begin{pmatrix}(\widetilde{S}_{j}^{+})^{2},&(\widetilde{S}_{j}^{-})^{2},&(\widetilde{S}_{j}^{z})^{2},&1\end{pmatrix},

(39)

\displaystyle\bm{O}_{j}^{z}=\begin{pmatrix}\widetilde{S}_{j}^{z}\widetilde{S}_{j}^{+},&\widetilde{S}_{j}^{z}\widetilde{S}_{j}^{-},&\widetilde{S}_{j}^{+}\widetilde{S}_{j}^{z},&\widetilde{S}_{j}^{-}\widetilde{S}_{j}^{z}\end{pmatrix}.

(40)

The coefficient matrices in (
29
) are obtained as the direct product of the coefficient vectors,

\mathbf{A}_{i}^{\alpha}=\{(A_{i}^{\alpha})_{m,n}\}=\{a_{i,m}^{\alpha}a_{i,n}^{\alpha}\}=\bm{a}_{i}^{x}\otimes\bm{a}_{i}^{x}.

(41)

The sum of the coefficient matrices
\mathbf{A}_{\mathrm{tot}}^{\alpha}=\sum_{i}\mathbf{A}_{i}^{\alpha}
is easily calculated, leading to

\displaystyle\mathbf{A}_{\mathrm{tot}}^{x}=\begin{pmatrix}0&3&-3&3\\
3&0&-3&3\\
-3&-3&6&-6\\
3&3&-6&6\end{pmatrix},

(42)

\displaystyle\mathbf{A}_{\mathrm{tot}}^{y}=\begin{pmatrix}0&3&3&-3\\
3&0&3&-3\\
3&3&6&-6\\
-3&-3&-6&6\end{pmatrix},

(43)

\displaystyle\mathbf{A}_{\mathrm{tot}}^{z}=\begin{pmatrix}0&3&0&3\\
3&0&3&0\\
0&3&0&3\\
3&0&3&0\end{pmatrix}.

With these matrices, we can rewrite (

) into

\displaystyle\sum_{\alpha,i}\left(\mathcal{P}_{j}S_{i,j}^{\alpha}\mathcal{P}_{j}-\frac{1}{3}\widetilde{S}_{j}^{\alpha}\right)\left(\mathcal{P}_{j+1}S_{i,j+1}^{\alpha}\mathcal{P}_{j+1}-\frac{1}{3}\widetilde{S}_{j+1}^{\alpha}\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt=\frac{J_{1}^{\prime}}{9}\left[\bm{O}_{j}^{x}(\mathbf{A}_{\mathrm{tot}}^{x}+\mathbf{A}_{\mathrm{tot}}^{y})(\bm{O}_{j}^{x})^{T}+\bm{O}_{j}^{z}\mathbf{A}_{\mathrm{tot}}^{z}(\bm{O}_{j}^{z})^{T}\right].

(45)

Calculating the vector-matrix-vector products in the right-hand side, we finally obtain the effective Hamiltonian of (i)
J_{1}^{\prime}\neq 0
and
J_{2}^{\prime}=0
as follows,

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{eff}}^{(j)}|_{J_{1}^{\prime}\neq 0,J_{2}^{\prime}=0}

\displaystyle=J_{1}^{\prime}\Bigg\{\left(\frac{5}{3}\widetilde{S}_{j}^{z}\widetilde{S}_{j+1}^{z}+\widetilde{S}_{j}^{x}\widetilde{S}_{j+1}^{x}+\widetilde{S}_{j}^{y}\widetilde{S}_{j+1}^{y}\right)+\frac{8}{3}\left(\frac{1}{2}\widetilde{S}_{j}^{z}\widetilde{S}_{j+1}^{z}+\widetilde{S}_{j}^{x}\widetilde{S}_{j+1}^{x}+\widetilde{S}_{j}^{y}\widetilde{S}_{j+1}^{y}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\hskip 40.00006pt-\frac{2}{3}\left[2-(\widetilde{S}_{j}^{z})^{s}\right]\left[2-(\widetilde{S}_{j+1}^{z})^{s}\right]-\frac{4}{3}\Bigg\}.

(46)

Thus, the effective Hamiltonian of (ii)
J_{1}^{\prime}=0
and
J_{2}^{\prime}\neq 0
is obtained by,

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{eff}}^{(j)}|_{J_{1}^{\prime}=0,J_{2}^{\prime}\neq 0}=\frac{J_{2}^{\prime}}{J^{\prime}}\left(\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{eff}}^{(j)}|_{J_{1}^{\prime}\to J^{\prime},J_{2}^{\prime}\to J^{\prime}}-\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{eff}}^{(j)}|_{J_{1}^{\prime}\to J^{\prime},J_{2}^{\prime}=0}\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 30.00005pt=-J_{2}^{\prime}\Bigg\{\frac{2}{3}\widetilde{S}_{j}^{z}\widetilde{S}_{j+1}^{z}+\frac{8}{3}\left(\frac{1}{2}\widetilde{S}_{j}^{z}\widetilde{S}_{j+1}^{z}-\widetilde{S}_{j}^{x}\widetilde{S}_{j+1}^{x}+\widetilde{S}_{j}^{y}\widetilde{S}_{j+1}^{y}\right)^{2}+\frac{2}{3}\left[2-(\widetilde{S}_{j}^{z})^{s}\right]\left[2-(\widetilde{S}_{j+1}^{z})^{s}\right]-\frac{4}{3}\Bigg\}.

(47)

We have also confirmed the derivation of effective Hamiltonians from the original Hamiltonians with the matrix form in the two-cluster Hilbert spaces, corresponding to the projection of
(2^{3})^{2}\times(2^{3})^{2}
matrices to
3^{2}\times 3^{2}
matrices.

Appendix B
Matrix-product operator representations of the Hamiltonian

In this section, we show the matrix-product operator (MPO) representation of the Hamiltonian, which is used in the variational matrix-product state (VMPS) method.
For simplicity, we firstly devide the Hamiltonian (
15
) into two-body terms preserving the magnetization and three-body terms (including one-body terms) breaking the magnetization,

\mathcal{H}_{0}=\mathcal{H}_{2b}+\mathcal{H}_{3b}

(48)

with

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{L}\mathcal{H}_{J}^{(j)}+\sum_{j=1}^{L-1}\mathcal{H}_{J^{\prime}}^{(j)}-K\sum_{j=1}^{L}S_{\mathrm{tot},j}^{z}\chi_{j}^{z}

\displaystyle=J\sum_{j=1}^{L}\sum_{i<i^{\prime}}\bm{S}_{i,j}\cdot\bm{S}_{i^{\prime},j}+J_{1}^{\prime}\sum_{j=1}^{L-1}\sum_{i}\bm{S}_{i,j}\cdot\bm{S}_{i,j+1}+J_{2}^{\prime}\sum_{j=1}^{L-1}\sum_{i\neq i^{\prime}}\bm{S}_{i,j}\cdot\bm{S}_{i^{\prime},j+1}-\frac{K}{4\sqrt{3}}\sum_{j=1}^{L}\sum_{i,i^{\prime},i^{\prime\prime}}\epsilon_{ii^{\prime}i^{\prime\prime}}(\bm{S}_{i^{\prime},j}\times\bm{S}_{i^{\prime\prime},j})^{z}

(49)

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{3b}

\displaystyle=-\frac{K}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{L}(S_{\mathrm{tot},j}^{+}\chi_{j}^{-}+S_{\mathrm{tot},j}^{-}\chi_{j}^{+})=-\frac{2K}{3}\sum_{j=1}^{L}\left[S_{1}^{x}\,\mathrm{Hs}_{j}(2,3)+S_{2}^{\phi}\,\mathrm{Hs}_{j}(3,1)+S_{3}^{-\phi}\,\mathrm{Hs}_{j}(1,2)\right].

-th cluster

, and

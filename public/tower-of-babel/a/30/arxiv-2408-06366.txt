Linear maps on matrices preserving parallel pairs
Chi-Kwong Li, Ming-Cheng Tsai, Ya-Shu Wang and Ngai-Ching Wong

Abstract
. Two (real or complex) m × n m\times n matrices A A and B B are said to be parallel (resp. triangle equality attaining, or TEA in short) with respect to the spectral norm ∥ ⋅ ∥ \|\cdot\| if ‖ A + μ ​ B ‖ = ‖ A ‖ + ‖ B ‖ \|A+\mu B\|=\|A\|+\|B\| for some scalar μ \mu with | μ | = 1 |\mu|=1 (resp. μ = 1 \mu=1 ). We study linear maps T T on m × n m\times n matrices preserving parallel (resp. TEA) pairs, i.e., T ⁡ ( A ) T(A) and T ⁡ ( B ) T(B) are parallel (resp. TEA) whenever A A and B B are parallel (resp. TEA). It is shown that when m , n ≥ 2 m,n\geq 2 and ( m , n ) ≠ ( 2 , 2 ) (m,n)\neq(2,2) , a nonzero linear map T T preserving TEA pairs if and only if it is a positive multiple of a linear isometry, namely, T T has the form (1) A ↦ γ ​ U ​ A ​ V A\mapsto\gamma UAV or (2) A ↦ γ ​ U ​ A t ​ V A\mapsto\gamma UA^{{\mathrm{t}}}V (in this case, m = n m=n ), for a positive number γ \gamma , and unitary (or real orthogonal) matrices U U and V V of appropriate sizes. Linear maps preserving parallel pairs are those carrying form (1), (2), or the form (3) A ↦ f ⁡ ( A ) ​ Z A\mapsto f(A)Z for a linear functional f f and a fixed matrix Z Z . The case when ( m , n ) = ( 2 , 2 ) (m,n)=(2,2) is more complicated. There are linear maps of 2 × 2 2\times 2 matrices preserving parallel pairs or TEA pairs neither of the form (1), (2) nor (3) above. Complete characterization of such maps is given with some intricate computation and techniques in matrix groups.

1.
Introduction

Let
({\bf V},\|\cdot\|)
be a normed vector space over either the real field
{\mathbb{F}}={\mathbb{R}}
or
the complex field
{\mathbb{F}}={\mathbb{C}}
. Two vectors
x,y\in{\bf V}
form a
parallel
pair if

\|x+\mu y\|=\|x\|+\|y\|\quad\hbox{ for some }\mu\in{\mathbb{F}}\hbox{ with }|\mu|=1;

(1.1)

the vectors
x,y\in{\bf V}
form a
triangle equality attaining
(
TEA
) pairs if

\|x+y\|=\|x\|+\|y\|.

(1.2)

We are interested in the case when
{\bf V}={\mathbf{M}}_{m,n}({\mathbb{F}})
, the linear space of
m\times n
matrices over
{\mathbb{F}}

equipped with the spectral norm, that is, the operator norm

\|A\|=\max\{\|Ax\|:x\in{\mathbb{F}}^{n},\|x\|=1\},

while
{\mathbb{F}}^{m},{\mathbb{F}}^{n}
are equipped with the usual inner products and norms.
One may see
[
10
,
3
,
11
,
13
,
15
,
14
]
for the study of parallel pairs.

In this paper, wee study those linear maps

T:{\mathbf{M}}_{m,n}({\mathbb{F}})\rightarrow{\mathbf{M}}_{m,n}({\mathbb{F}})
preserving parallel pairs, that is,

\max_{|\nu|=1}\|T(A)+\nu T(B)\|=\|T(A)\|+\|T(B)\|\quad\hbox{whenever}\quad\max_{|\mu|=1}\|A+\mu B\|=\|A\|+\|B\|,

(1.3)

or preserving TEA pairs, that is,

\|T(A)+T(B)\|=\|T(A)\|+\|T(B)\|\quad\hbox{whenever }\quad\|A+B\|=\|A\|+\|B\|.

(1.4)

Evidently, if a linear map

T
satisfies (
1.4
), then it
also satisfies (
1.3
).
In other words, linear TEA pair preservers are also parallel pair preservers.

Note that if
m=1
or
n=1
,
the spectral norm reduce to the
\ell_{2}
-norm, which is strictly convex.
Consequently, two nonzero row matrices or
column matrices

A,B
are parallel (resp. form a TEA pair)
exactly when
A=\lambda B
for some scalar
\lambda
(resp. with
\lambda>0
).
Thus any linear map of row matrix spaces or column matrix spaces preserves parallel pairs as well as TEA pairs.
We will assume that
m,n\geq 2
throughout in this paper.

Note that
T
preserves TEA/parallel pairs if
T:{\mathbf{M}}_{m,n}({\mathbb{F}})\rightarrow{\mathbf{M}}_{m,n}({\mathbb{F}})
is a
scalar multiple of a linear isometry for the spectral norm.
In this case,

T
has the form
A\mapsto\gamma UAV
, or the form
A\mapsto\gamma UA^{{\mathrm{t}}}V
(only possible when
m=n
),
for some
U\in{\bf U}_{m}({\mathbb{F}})
and
V\in{\bf U}_{n}({\mathbb{F}})
, and
\gamma\geq 0
; see, e.g.,
[
6
]
.
Here,
{\bf U}_{k}({\mathbb{F}})
, or simply
{\bf U}_{k}
, denotes the group of
k\times k

complex unitary matrices or real orthogonal matrices
depending on
{\mathbb{F}}={\mathbb{C}}
or
{\mathbb{R}}
.
Indeed,
these are merely all linear preservers of TEA/parallel pairs on
{\mathbf{M}}_{m,n}({\mathbb{F}})
.
More precisely, we have the following.

Theorem 1.1
.

Suppose
m,n\geq 2
,
(m,n)\neq(2,2)
,
and
T:{\mathbf{M}}_{m,n}({\mathbb{F}})\rightarrow{\mathbf{M}}_{m,n}({\mathbb{F}})
is a nonzero linear map.
The following conditions are equivalent.

(a)

T
preserves TEA pairs.

(b)

T
preserves parallel pairs and the range space of
T
has dimension larger than one.

(c)

There are
U\in{\bf U}_{m}({\mathbb{F}})
,
V\in{\bf U}_{n}({\mathbb{F}})
, and
\gamma>0
such that

(1)

T
has the form
A\mapsto\gamma UAV
, or
(2)

m=n
and
T
has the form
A\mapsto\gamma UA^{{\mathrm{t}}}V
.

If the range space of a linear map
T:{\mathbf{M}}_{m,n}({\mathbb{F}})\rightarrow{\mathbf{M}}_{m,n}({\mathbb{F}})
has dimension
at most
1
, or equivalently,

T
has the form

(3)
A\mapsto\operatorname{trace}(FA)Z
for some
F\in{\mathbf{M}}_{n,m}({\mathbb{F}})
and
Z\in{\mathbf{M}}_{m,n}({\mathbb{F}})
,

then clearly
T
preserves parallel pairs (but not necessarily TEA pairs).

The proof of Theorem
1.1
will be presented in
Section
3
after some preliminary results are
given in Section
2
.
A key step of the proof is to show that
any nonzero linear TEA pair preserver, and any linear parallel pair preserver with range space
of dimension larger than one, is invertible (see Proposition
3.1
).
Then one can focus on invertible preservers.

In the study of linear preserver problems
on matrices, the
2\times 2
case often involves special maps and the proofs require special care.
One reason is that there are many
special subgroups and semi-groups of the linear maps on
{\mathbf{M}}_{2}({\mathbb{F}})
.
In our study, we experienced the same problem. Nevertheless, with intricate computation
and insights on some such special subgroups, we are able to give a complete
description of linear maps
T:{\mathbf{M}}_{2}({\mathbb{F}})\rightarrow{\mathbf{M}}_{2}({\mathbb{F}})
preserving TEA/parallel pairs.
It turns out that the structure are different for the two classes of preservers,
and they are also different in the real and complex cases.
To describe our main result, let

I_{2}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\cr 0&1\cr\end{pmatrix},\ \mathcal{C}_{1}=\mathcal{R}_{1}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\cr 0&-1\cr\end{pmatrix},\ \mathcal{C}_{2}=\mathcal{R}_{2}=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\cr 1&0\cr\end{pmatrix},\ \mathcal{C}_{3}=\begin{pmatrix}0&-i\cr i&0\cr\end{pmatrix},\ \mathcal{R}_{3}=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\cr-1&0\cr\end{pmatrix}.

It is well-known that
{\mathbf{C}}=\{I_{2}/\sqrt{2},\mathcal{C}_{1}/\sqrt{2},\mathcal{C}_{2}/\sqrt{2},\mathcal{C}_{3}/\sqrt{2}\}

is an orthonormal basis of
{\mathbf{M}}_{2}({\mathbb{C}})

with respect to the inner product

\langle A,B\rangle=\operatorname{trace}(B^{*}A)
, while

{\mathbf{R}}=\{I_{2}/\sqrt{2},\mathcal{R}_{1}/\sqrt{2},\mathcal{R}_{2}/\sqrt{2},\mathcal{R}_{3}/\sqrt{2}\}

is an orthonormal basis of
{\mathbf{M}}_{2}({\mathbb{R}})
with respect to the inner product
\langle A,B\rangle=\operatorname{trace}(B^{{\mathrm{t}}}A)
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
T:{\mathbf{M}}_{2}({\mathbb{C}})\rightarrow{\mathbf{M}}_{2}({\mathbb{C}})
be a complex linear map.

(a)

T
preserves TEA pairs if and only if there are
\gamma\geq 0
and

U,V\in{\bf U}_{2}({\mathbb{C}})
such that
T
has the form

A\mapsto\gamma UAV\qquad\hbox{ or }\qquad A\mapsto\gamma UA^{{\mathrm{t}}}V.

(b)

T
preserves parallel pairs if and only if one of the following holds.

(b.1)

T
has the form
A\mapsto\operatorname{trace}(FA)Z
for some
F,Z\in{\mathbf{M}}_{2}({\mathbb{C}})
.

(b.2)

There are complex unitary matrices

U_{1},U_{2},V_{1},V_{2}\in{\bf U}_{2}({\mathbb{C}})
such that the matrix representation of the linear transformation

\tilde{T}
defined by
A\mapsto U_{1}T(U_{2}AV_{2})V_{1}
with respect
to the basis
{\mathbf{C}}
has the form

\ \hskip 50.58878pt[\tilde{T}]_{{\mathbf{C}}}=\begin{pmatrix}d_{0}&\gamma_{1}i&\gamma_{2}i&\gamma_{3}i\\
0&d_{1}&0&0\cr 0&0&d_{2}&0\cr 0&0&0&d_{3}\cr\end{pmatrix}

(1.5)

for real scalars

d_{0},d_{1},d_{2},d_{3},\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2},\gamma_{3}
.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
T:{\mathbf{M}}_{2}({\mathbb{R}})\rightarrow{\mathbf{M}}_{2}({\mathbb{R}})
be a real linear map.

(a)

T
preserves TEA pairs if and only if

has the form

(a.1)

There are real orthogonal matrices

(a.2)

\tilde{T}
defined by

&d_{1}&&\\
&&d_{2}&\\

(1.6)

(b)

preserves parallel pairs if and only if

2.
Preliminaries

Let
m,n\geq 2
, and let
{\mathbb{F}}={\mathbb{R}}
or
{\mathbb{C}}
.
For notation simplicity, let
{\mathbf{M}}_{m,n}
,
{\mathbf{M}}_{n}
and
{\bf U}_{n}
denote

{\mathbf{M}}_{m,n}({\mathbb{F}})
,
{\mathbf{M}}_{n}({\mathbb{F}})
and
{\bf U}_{n}({\mathbb{F}})
, respectively, if the discussion applies to
both the real and complex cases. Denote by

A^{{\mathrm{t}}}
and
A^{*}
the transpose and the conjugate transpose of a matrix
A
.
We have
A^{*}=A^{{\mathrm{t}}}
if
{\mathbb{F}}={\mathbb{R}}
. Let
\{E_{11},E_{12},\dots,E_{mn}\}
denote the standard basis
for
{\mathbf{M}}_{m,n}
, i.e.,
E_{ij}\in{\mathbf{M}}_{m,n}
has the
(i,j)
entry equal to 1 and all other entries equal to 0.
Let
I_{n}\in{\mathbf{M}}_{n}
be the identity matrix, and
\mathbf{0}_{r,s}\in{\mathbf{M}}_{r,s}
be the zero matrix.
We simply write
I
and
\mathbf{0}
if the size is clear in the context.
We write
R\oplus S
for the

(r_{1}+s_{1})\times(r_{2}+s_{2})

block matrix
\begin{pmatrix}R&0\\
0&S\end{pmatrix}
for
R\in{\mathbf{M}}_{r_{1}\times r_{2}}

and
S\in{\mathbf{M}}_{s_{1}\times s_{2}}
.
Two matrices
A,B\in{\mathbf{M}}_{m,n}
are
said to be disjoint (a.k.a, orthogonal) if
A^{*}B=\mathbf{0}\in{\mathbf{M}}_{n}
and
AB^{*}=\mathbf{0}\in{\mathbf{M}}_{m}
. In some cases, we also write

A\oplus B
for the sum of disjoint matrices
A
and
B
.

Recall that every
rectangular matrix
A\in{\mathbf{M}}_{m,n}
admits a singular value decomposition, namely,

A=U\begin{pmatrix}D&0\cr 0&0\cr\end{pmatrix}V^{*}=\sum_{j=1}^{k}s_{j}u_{j}v_{j}^{*},

(2.1)

where
D={\rm diag}\,(s_{1},\dots,s_{k})
with
s_{1}\geq\cdots\geq s_{k}>0
, the nonzero singular values
of
A
, and

u_{1},\dots,u_{k}
and
v_{1},\dots,v_{k}
are the first
k
columns of the matrices
U\in{\bf U}_{m}
and

V\in{\bf U}_{n}
.
Note that
k\leq\min\{m,n\}
and
s_{1}^{2},\dots,s_{k}^{2}
are the nonzero eigenvalues of
AA^{*}
(or
A^{*}A
).
We will let
s_{j}(A)=s_{j}
for
j=1,\dots,k
,
and
s_{j}(A)=0
for
j>k
.

It is easy to see that two nonzero rectangular matrices
A,B\in{\mathbf{M}}_{m,n}
are parallel if and only if there is
a unit vector
v\in{\mathbb{F}}^{n}
and
\mu\in{\mathbb{F}}
with
|\mu|=1
such that

\|A+\mu B\|=\|Av+\mu Bv\|=\|A\|+\|B\|\geq\|Av\|+\|\mu Bv\|\geq\|Av+\mu Bv\|.

This is equivalent to the condition

|\langle Av,Bv\rangle|=\|Av\|\|Bv\|=\|A\|\|B\|>0.

Then the Cauchy-Schwartz inequality theorem tells us that

Av,Bv
are linearly dependent. Let
u=Av/\|Av\|
. Then
A=s_{1}(A)uv^{*}\oplus A_{1}
and

B=\overline{\mu}s_{1}(B)uv^{*}\oplus B_{1}
for some unimodular scalar
\mu
and matrices
A_{1},B_{1}

with
\|A_{1}\|\leq\|A\|=s_{1}(A)
and
\|B_{1}\|\leq\|B\|=s_{1}(B)
.
This leads to the following lemma; see, e.g.,
[
5
, Theorem 3.1 and Corollary 3.2]
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
A,B\in{\mathbf{M}}_{m,n}
be nonzero rectangular matrices.

(a)

A,B
are parallel if and only if there is a unit vector
v\in{\mathbb{F}}^{n}
such that

|\langle Av,Bv\rangle|=\|A\|\|B\|.

In this case,
A,B
form a TEA pair when
\langle Av,Bv\rangle=\|A\|\|B\|
.

(b)

A,B
are parallel if and only if there are unitary matrices
U\in{\bf U}_{m}
,
V\in{\bf V}_{n}
such that

A=U\begin{pmatrix}a&0\\
0&A_{1}\end{pmatrix}V\quad\text{and}\quad B=U\begin{pmatrix}b&0\\
0&B_{1}\end{pmatrix}V

for some
A_{1},B_{1}\in{\mathbf{M}}_{m-1,n-1}
with
\|A_{1}\|\leq a

and
\|B_{1}\|\leq|b|
. In this case,
A,B
form a TEA pair when
b>0
.

(c)

If
A,B
are parallel (resp. TEA), then so are
\alpha A,\beta B
for any scalars
\alpha,\beta
(resp. such that
\overline{\alpha}\beta\geq 0
).

(d)

A=U\begin{pmatrix}A_{1}&0\\
0&A_{2}\end{pmatrix}V
and
B=U\begin{pmatrix}B_{1}&0\\
0&B_{2}\end{pmatrix}V

are not parallel if
U\in{\bf U}_{m}
,
V\in{\bf V}_{n}
,

A_{1},B_{1}\in{\mathbf{M}}_{r,s}
and
A_{2},B_{2}\in{\mathbf{M}}_{m-r,n-s}
with

\|A_{2}\|<\|A_{1}\|
and
\|B_{1}\|<\|B_{2}\|
.

It follows from Lemma
2.1
(d) that two nonzero rectangular matrices

A,B
can not be simultaneously parallel and orthogonal.

Lemma 2.2
.

(a)

Let
T_{k}:{\mathbf{M}}_{m,n}\to{\mathbf{M}}_{m,n}
be a sequence of linear maps preserving parallel pairs (resp. TEA pairs) for
k\geq 1
.
If
T_{k}\to T
in norm then
T
preserves parallel pairs (resp. TEA pairs).

(b)

If
(A_{k},B_{k})
are parallel pairs (resp. TEA pairs)
and
A_{k}\to A
,
B_{k}\to B
in norm, then
(A,B)
is a parallel pair (resp. TEA pair).

Proof.

(a) Let
A,B\in{\mathbf{M}}_{m,n}
be parallel, and so are
T_{k}(A),T_{k}(B)
for
k\geq 1
.
In other words, there is a unimodular scalar
\mu_{k}
such that

\|T_{k}(A)+\mu_{k}T_{k}(B)\|=\|T_{k}(A)\|+\|T_{k}(B)\|,\quad\forall k\geq 1.

Since the unit circle in
{\mathbb{F}}
is compact, we can assume
\mu_{k}\to\mu
in
{\mathbb{F}}
with
|\mu|=1
.
Letting
k\to\infty
, we have

\|T(A)+\mu T(B)\|=\|T(A)\|+\|T(B)\|.

Consequently,
T
preserves parallel pairs. The case for TEA pair preservers is similar.

(b) The proof is similar to (a).

Example 2.3
.

(a)

Two linearly dependent matrices are always parallel.

(b)

Any two complex unitary matrices
U,V\in{\bf U}_{n}({\mathbb{C}})
are parallel. Indeed,

\|U+\mu V\|=\|I+\mu U^{*}V\|=2=\|U\|+\|V\|

whenever the complex conjugate
\overline{\mu}
belongs to the spectrum
\sigma(U^{*}V)
of the complex unitary
U^{*}V
.
They form a TEA pair when
\sigma(U^{*}V)
contains
1
.

(c)

Two real orthogonal matrices
U,V\in{\bf U}_{n}({\mathbb{R}})
are parallel if any only if
\sigma(U^{{\mathrm{t}}}V)
contains
1
or
-1
.
They form a TEA pair when
\sigma(U^{{\mathrm{t}}}V)
contains
1
.

(d)

Two real orthogonal matrices
F_{1},F_{2}\in{\bf U}_{2}({\mathbb{R}})
are parallel exactly when
F_{1}=\pm F_{2}
, or

\det(F_{1})=-\det(F_{2})
. In this case, they form a TEA pair unless
F_{1}=-F_{2}
.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
A,B\in{\mathbf{M}}_{m,n}
such that
\|A\|=\|B\|=1=\|A+B\|/2
.
Then
A+B
and
A-B
are parallel if and only if
A=B
.

Proof.

If
A=B
, then clearly
A+B=2A
and
A-B=0
are parallel.
Conversely, suppose
A+B
and
A-B
are parallel.
Since
\|A+B\|=2
, there are
U\in{\bf U}_{m},V\in{\bf V}_{n}
such that

U(A+B)V=(2I_{k})\oplus C
with
\|C\|<2
and
1\leq k\leq\min\{m,n\}
.
Since
\|A\|=\|B\|=1
, the
(j,j)
entry of
UAV
and
UBV
must be 1
for
j=1,\dots,k
.
Consequently,
UAV=I_{k}\oplus A_{1}
,
UBV=I_{k}\oplus B_{1}
,
and thus
U(A-B)V=0\oplus(A_{1}-B_{1})
.
With
\|C\|<2
,
it follows from Lemma
2.1
(d) that

A_{1}-B_{1}=0
. Thus,
A=B
.

Lemma 2.5
.

If
{\bf V}
is a subspace of

are parallel,

Let
A,B\in{\bf V}

5.
Related results and questions

In connection to our results,
one can consider other norms on
{\mathbf{M}}_{m,n}
. Suppose
1\leq k\leq\min\{m,n\}
.
Define the Ky-Fan
k
-norm on
{\mathbf{M}}_{m,n}
by

|A|_{k}=\sum_{j=1}^{k}s_{j}(A)
, the sum of the
k
largest singular values.
If
k=1
, it reduces to the spectral norm.
In
[
4
]
, the authors consider the
({\bf V},|\cdot|)=({\mathbf{M}}_{n},|\cdot|_{k})
,
for
2\leq k\leq n
,
and show that an invertible linear map preserves
parallel pairs if and only if it preserves TEA pairs, and
the map must be a positive multiple of an isometry for
|\cdot|_{k}
.

For the description of the isometries for the Ky-Fan
k
-norms on
{\mathbf{M}}_{m,n}({\mathbb{F}})
, one may see
[
6
]
.
Especially, it includes two
special maps
\tau_{1},\tau_{2}
when
{\mathbf{M}}_{m,n}({\mathbb{F}})={\mathbf{M}}_{4}({\mathbb{R}})
.
The proofs in
[
4
]
for square matrices can be readily extended to
{\mathbf{M}}_{m,n}
.
The authors of the paper
[
4
]
also characterize TEA and parallel preservers
with respect to the
k
-numerical radius on square matrices in a forthcoming paper.

Our study can be considered for more general operator spaces or operator algebras.
For example,
one may consider extensions of our results to

B(H,K)
, the set of bounded linear operators

A:H\rightarrow K
between real or complex Hilbert space over,
equipped with the operator norm

\|A\|=\sup\{\|Ax\|:x\in H,\ \|x\|=1\}.

In a forthcoming paper
[
9
]
, we will give the characterization of linear parallel pair
preservers of bounded operators,
as well as extensions to
C^{*}
-algebras and
JB^{*}
-triples.

It would also be interesting to consider our problems for
other (finite or infinite dimensional) normed spaces; e.g., see
[
6
]
.
In fact, one may consider the problems for linear maps
between two normed spaces
({\bf V}_{1},|\cdot|_{1})
and

({\bf V}_{2},|\cdot|_{2})
. An ambitious project is to determine those norms

|\cdot|_{1}
and
|\cdot|_{2}
such that bijective linear maps
preserving parallel pairs or TEA pairs
must be multiples of isometries for
|\cdot|_{1}
and
|\cdot|_{2}
.

One may also consider problems related to the parallel pairs,
or TEA pairs.
For instance, the Daugavet equation for operators
A
in
B(H)
is

\|A+I\|=\|A\|+1,

(5.1)

which is a well studied problem in function and operator theory
(see, e.g.,
[
1
,
2
,
12
]
).
One may consider linear maps of
B(H)
sending the set of
A
satisfying
(
5.1
) into or onto itself.

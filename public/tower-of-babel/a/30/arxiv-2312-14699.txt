The Hochschild cohomology ring of monomial algebras
Dalia Artenstein, Janina C. Letz, Amrei Oswald, Andrea Solotar

Abstract
. We give an explicit description of a diagonal map on the Bardzell resolution for any monomial algebra, and we use this diagonal map to describe the cup product on Hochschild cohomology. Then, we prove that the cup product is zero in positive degrees for triangular monomial algebras. Our proof uses the graded-commutativity of the cup product on Hochschild cohomology and does not rely on explicit computation of the Hochschild cohomology modules.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
16E40 (primary), 18G10

1.
Introduction

Given a field
\Bbbk
and an associative
\Bbbk
-algebra
A
, the Hochschild cohomology of
A
with coefficients in the
A
-bimodule
A
is the graded vector space

\operatorname{HH}^{*}(A)=\bigoplus_{i\geqslant 0}\operatorname{Ext}^{i}_{A\otimes A^{\rm op}}(A,A)\,.

The Hochschild cohomology of an associative algebra has a rich structure, which provides several derived invariants that are useful when studying the representations of the given algebra. The cup product,
\smile:\operatorname{HH}^{*}(A)\otimes\operatorname{HH}^{*}(A)\to\operatorname{HH}^{*}(A)
, gives
\operatorname{HH}^{*}(A)
the structure of a graded-commutative
\Bbbk
-algebra, as has been proved in several different ways. The proof of Murray Gerstenhaber
[
Ger63
]
gave rise to the definition of the Gerstenhaber bracket.
Both the cup product and the Gerstenhaber bracket were originally defined in terms of the bar resolution, which is usually very inefficient for explicit computations of the Hochschild cohomology. It has been known for a long time that the Hochschild cohomology can be computed by any resolution of
A
as an
A
-bimodule; see
[
CE56
, Chapter IX, §6]
. However, that the cup product and Gerstenhaber bracket can be computed using any resolution are more recent results; see
[
SW99
]
and
[
NW16
,
Vol19
]
respectively. In fact, the cup product coincides with the Yoneda product.

It is possible to avoid using the bar resolution for the computation of the cup product and use any resolution
P
—perhaps minimal—whenever it is equipped with a diagonal map
\Delta\colon P\to P\otimes_{A}P
; see for example
[
Wit19
]
. We use this strategy to compute the cup product for triangular monomial algebras and prove the following theorem.

Theorem A

(
Theorem

6.5
)
.

Consider a finite triangular monomial algebra,
A
. The cup product on
\operatorname{HH}^{*}(A)
is zero in positive degrees.

There are many classes of algebras for which the cup product is zero in positive degrees.
In
[
Cib98
, Lemma 3.1]
, Claude Cibils showed this for a finite dimensional path algebra
A=\Bbbk Q/I
with radical square zero and a finite quiver
Q
that is not a
crown
. In
loc. cit.
cycles are called crowns. As described in
[
GS02
]
, there exist self-injective algebras which have nonzero cup product in positive degrees. Moreover, in
[
GS02
]
, Edward Green and Øyvind Solberg construct many algebras that are not self-injective for which the cup product is nonzero in positive degrees. Next, Juan Carlos Bustamante
[
Bus06
]
proved that the cup product is zero in positive degrees for triangular quadratic string algebras and conjectured that this holds for any triangular monomial algebra as in
A
. In
[
RR14
, Theorem 5.3]
, María Julia Redondo and Lucrecia Román verified this conjecture for triangular string algebras.

For the description of the Hochschild cohomology, we use the Bardzell resolution introduced in
[
Bar97
]
. This is a minimal resolution of
A
as an
A
-bimodule, hence it is much more efficient than the bar resolution. We give an explicit description of a diagonal map on the Bardzell resolution for any monomial algebra in
Theorem

4.3
. We then use this diagonal map and the fact that the cup product is graded-commutative to prove
A
in
Section

5
. Our proof does not rely on an explicit computation of the Hochschild cohomology modules.

2.
Ambiguities

Let
\Bbbk
be a field and
A=\Bbbk Q/I
a finite dimensional monomial algebra. We denote by
\mathcal{B}
a basis of paths of
A
; such a basis is unique since
A
is monomial. In particular, a path is zero in
A
if and only if it is not contained in
\mathcal{B}
.

Notation 2.1
.

A finite quiver
Q=(Q_{0},Q_{1},s,t)
consists of

(1)

a finite set of
vertices

Q_{0}
,

(2)

a finite set of
arrows

Q_{1}
,

(3)

a source map
s\colon Q_{1}\to Q_{0}
, and

(4)

a target map
t\colon Q_{1}\to Q_{0}
.

A
path

p
is a sequence of arrows
\alpha_{n}\ldots\alpha_{1}
where
t(\alpha_{i})=s(\alpha_{i+1})
. Graphically the path can be depicted as

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\bullet}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\bullet}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\ldots}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\bullet\makebox[0.0pt][l]{\,.}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\alpha_{1}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\alpha_{2}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\alpha_{n}}

We set
s(p)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}s(\alpha_{1})
the
source of
p
and
t(p)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}t(\alpha_{n})
the
target of
p
. We say the
length
of the path
p=\alpha_{n}\ldots\alpha_{1}
is
n
. We identify a path of length zero with its vertex. We write
qp
for the concatenation of the paths
p
and
q
if
t(p)=s(q)
.

Let
p
and
q
be paths such that
p=bqa
. We say
q
is a
divisor
of
p
. In general the position of
q
in
p
, that is the paths
a
and
b
, need not be unique. We write
q\leq p
to mean that
q
is a divisor of
p
and the position of
q
in
p
is fixed. In this situation we set

\pre_{p}(q)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}a\quad\text{and}\quad\suf_{p}(q)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}b

for the
prefix
and
suffix of
q
in
p
, respectively. We drop the subscript when it is clear from the context. If
b=1
, we say
q
is a
suffix
of
p
, and if
a=1
, we say
q
is a
prefix
of
p
. If
q
is a divisor/suffix/prefix of
p
and
p\neq q
, we say
q
is a
proper
divisor/suffix/prefix, and we write
q\lneq p
.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
n\geq-1
be an integer. A path in
Q
is a
left
n
-ambiguity
if it decomposes as
p=u_{-1}\ldots u_{n}
such that

(1)

u_{-1}\in Q_{0}
,
u_{0}\in Q_{1}
,
u_{i}\in\mathcal{B}
, and

(2)

for any
0\leq i\leq n-1
we have
u_{i}u_{i+1}\in I
and any proper suffix of
u_{i}u_{i+1}
does not lie in
I
.

In practice, we drop the trivial path
u_{-1}
and write
u_{0}\ldots u_{n}
for the decomposition of a left ambiguity.

Dually, a path
p
is a
right
n
-ambiguity
if it decomposes as
p=v_{n}\ldots v_{-1}
such that the above properties hold when we replace suffix by prefix.

Left ambiguities are called
right
n
-chains
in
[
Skö08
, Section 3]
. Our use of the term ambiguity follows
[
CS15
, Definition 3.1]
and alludes to the fact that left and right ambiguities are equivalent; see
[
Bar97
, Lemma 3.1]
and also
[
Skö08
, Lemma 1]
. Hence we will simply say
n
-ambiguity instead of left or right
n
-ambiguity. Moreover, given an
n
-ambiguity
p
and decompositions as left and right ambiguities
p=u_{0}\ldots u_{n}=v_{n}\ldots v_{0}
, respectively, we have

(2.3)

v_{i}\leq u_{n-i}u_{n-i+1}\quad\text{and}\quad u_{i}\leq v_{n-i+1}v_{n-i}

for any
1\leq i\leq n
.

We denote the set of
n
-ambiguities by
\Gamma_{n}
. This set is denoted by
AP(n+1)
in
[
Bar97
]
and by
AP_{n+1}
in
[
RR18a
,
RR18b
]
.

For small
n
, we have the following descriptions of the sets of ambiguities
\Gamma_{n}
.

(1)

The
(-1)
-ambiguities are precisely the trivial paths of length zero, that is
\Gamma_{-1}=Q_{0}
.

(2)

The
0
-ambiguities are precisely the arrows, that is
\Gamma_{0}=Q_{1}
.

(3)

The
1
-ambiguities are the minimal generating set of paths of
I
.

For quadratic algebras the ambiguities have particularly nice decompositions:

Example 2.4
.

Let
A=\Bbbk Q/I
be a finite dimensional quadratic monomial algebra. Then
I
is generated by paths of length 2. For any left ambiguity
p=u_{0}\ldots u_{n}
each
u_{i}
is an arrow in
Q
and
u_{i}u_{i+1}
lies in the minimal generating set of
I
. In particular, setting
v_{i}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}u_{n-i}
yields a decomposition of
p
as a right ambiguity. Moreover, for any
0\leq i\leq n-1
the path
u_{i}u_{i+1}
is a 1-ambiguity.

The latter property needs not hold when
I
is generated by paths of length more than 2. In this case,
u_{i}u_{i+1}
can have a proper
prefix
that lies in
I
.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
m\geq n
be integers and let

p=u_{0}\ldots u_{m}\geq u^{\prime}_{0}\ldots u^{\prime}_{n}=q

be decompositions as left
m
- and
n
-ambiguities, respectively. We assume
\ell\leq m
is maximal such that
u_{\ell}\ldots u_{m}\geq q
. Then

(2.7)

\begin{aligned} u_{\ell+i}\geq u^{\prime}_{i}&\quad\text{when $i$ even}\\
u_{\ell+i}\leq u^{\prime}_{i}&\quad\text{when $i$ odd}\end{aligned}\quad\text{for}\quad 0\leq i\leq n\,.

In particular, this yields
m\geq l+n
and

(1)

if
n
is even, then
u_{\ell}\ldots u_{\ell+n}\geq q
and
u_{\ell}\ldots u_{\ell+n-1}\not\geq q
.

(2)

if
n
is odd, then
u_{\ell+1}\ldots u_{\ell+n}\leq q
.

Moreover, if
n
is odd and
q
a suffix of
u_{\ell}\ldots u_{m}
, then
q=u_{\ell}\ldots u_{\ell+n}
.

An analogous statement holds for right ambiguities.

Proof.

We show
Eq.

2.7
by induction on
i
. By assumption the claim holds for
i=0
. We assume
u_{\ell+i}\geq u^{\prime}_{i}
. Since no proper suffix of
u_{\ell+i}u_{\ell+i+1}
lies in
I
and
u^{\prime}_{i}u^{\prime}_{i+1}\in I
, we have
u_{\ell+i+1}\leq u^{\prime}_{i+1}
. A similar argument yields
u_{\ell+i+1}\geq u^{\prime}_{i+1}
when
u_{\ell+i}\leq u^{\prime}_{i}
.

Now let
n
be odd and
q
a suffix of
u_{\ell}\ldots u_{m}
. We show that, in this case, we have
u_{\ell}\ldots u_{\ell+i}=u^{\prime}_{0}\ldots u^{\prime}_{i}
when
i
is odd in the induction above. For
i=-1
there is nothing to show. We assume it holds for
i
. Then
u^{\prime}_{i+1}u^{\prime}_{i+2}
and
u_{\ell+i+1}u_{\ell+i+2}
have the same source, so one is the suffix of the other. Since both lie in
I
and neither has a proper suffix in
I
, they are equal. Taking
i=n
yields the desired statement.
∎

Corollary 2.8
.

Let
p
be an
n
-ambiguity.

(1)

No proper divisor of
p
is an
n
-ambiguity.

(2)

The decompositions of
p
as a left and right ambiguity are unique. ∎

Let
p
be a left
n
-ambiguity with decomposition
p=u_{0}\ldots u_{n}
. The suffix

\prescript{(m)}{}{p}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}u_{0}\ldots u_{m}

is, with this decomposition, a left
m
-ambiguity for every
-1\leq m\leq n
.

Analogously, if
p
is a right
n
-ambiguity with decomposition
p=v_{n}\ldots v_{0}
we set

p^{(m)}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}v_{m}\ldots v_{0}\,.

By
Eq.

2.3
, any
n
-ambiguity
p
has a decomposition

(2.9)

p=\prescript{(j)}{}{p}bp^{(i)}

for
i+j=n-1
and some
b\in B
with
b<u_{j+1}
and
b<v_{i+1}
. In general the remainder
b
in
Eq.

2.9
need not be trivial, as seen in the following example.

Example 2.10
.

In
Example

2.5
, the 3-ambiguity
p=\gamma\beta\alpha\delta\gamma\beta\alpha
has the decompositions as left and right ambiguity

\gamma|\beta\alpha|\delta\gamma|\beta\alpha\quad\text{and}\quad\gamma\beta|\alpha\delta|\gamma\beta|\alpha\,,

respectively. Then, we have
\prescript{(1)}{}{p}=\gamma\beta\alpha
and
p^{(1)}=\gamma\delta\alpha
, and the remainder is
b=\alpha
. In this example, for any
n
-ambiguity and
i+j=n-1
, the remainder is a path of length 1.

3.
Bardzell’s resolution

Let
A=\Bbbk Q/I
be a monomial algebra and
\Gamma_{n}
the set of
n
-ambiguities. We set
E\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\Bbbk Q_{0}\cong\Bbbk\Gamma_{-1}
and
{A}^{e}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}A\otimes_{k}A^{\rm op}
. In this section we describe a minimal resolution of
A
over
{A}^{e}
due to Bardzell
[
Bar97
]
that uses
n
-ambiguities as a basis in homological degree
n+1
. Since this resolution was first introduced, the notation has evolved and many arguments in the original paper can be made more concrete. In the sequel, we make use of some of these technical results. For this reason and the convenience of the reader, we will give many of the details.

We start with a description of the
(n-1)
-ambiguities contained in an
n
-ambiguity. This depends on the parity of
n
.

Notation 3.1
.

Let
p
be an
n
-ambiguity, i.e.
p\in\Gamma_{n}
. We define

\Sub(p)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\left\{q\in\Gamma_{n-1}\,\middle|\,q\leq p\right\}\,.

For every
q\in\Sub(p)
we have
p=\suf(q)q\pre(q)
. Clearly
\suf(q),\pre(q)\notin I
. By
Corollary

2.8
, we have two distinct elements

\left\{\prescript{(n-1)}{}{p},p^{(n-1)}\right\}\subseteq\Sub(p)\,.

We will see that the description of
\Sub(p)
depends on the parity of
n
. But first we need a technical
Lemma
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
n
be an odd integer and
q_{1}\neq q_{2}
be
n
-ambiguities with
q_{1}a=bq_{2}
. If
a\notin I
and
b\notin I
, then there exist
(n+1)
-ambiguities
p_{1}
and
p_{2}
such that
p_{1},p_{2}\leq q_{1}a=bq_{2}
and
\prescript{(n)}{}{p_{1}}=q_{1}
and
p_{2}^{(n)}=q_{2}
.

We assume
a\notin I
and
b\notin I
. Let

q_{1}=u_{0}\ldots u_{n}\quad\text{and}\quad q_{2}=u^{\prime}_{0}\ldots u^{\prime}_{n}

be decompositions as left

-ambiguities. This setup is illustrated in

Now we show
[
Bar97
, Lemma 3.3]
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
n
be an even integer and
p
an
n
-ambiguity. Then

\Sub(p)=\left\{\prescript{(n-1)}{}{p},p^{(n-1)}\right\}\,.

Proof.

Let
q\in\Sub(p)
with
q\neq\prescript{(n-1)}{}{p}
. We can write

\prescript{(n-1)}{}{p}a=bq\leq p\,.

Then
a\leq\pre(\prescript{(n-1)}{}{p})\notin I
and
b\leq\suf(p^{(n-1)})\notin I
. So, we can apply
Lemma

3.2
to obtain an
n
-ambiguity
\tilde{p}
with
\tilde{p}\leq bq\leq p
. Hence,
\tilde{p}=p
and
q=p^{(n-1)}
.
∎

The following
Lemma
recovers
[
RR18a
, Lemma 4.7]
.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
n
be an even integer and
p
a path in
Q
. We consider the ordered set

\left\{q\in\Gamma_{n}\,\middle|\,q\leq p\right\}=\{q_{1},\ldots,q_{N}\}

where
p=b_{i}q_{i}a_{i}
and
a_{i}\lneq a_{i+1}
for any
1\leq i\leq N-1
. Then
\prescript{(n-1)}{}{q_{i}}=q_{i+1}^{(n-1)}
for all
1\leq i\leq N-1
.

Proof.

If
\prescript{(n-1)}{}{q_{i}}\leq q_{i+1}
or
q_{i+1}^{(n-1)}\leq q_{i}
the claim holds by
Lemma

3.3
. On the other hand, if
\prescript{(n-1)}{}{q_{i}}\not\leq q_{i+1}
and
q_{i+1}^{(n-1)}\not\leq q_{i}
, then we have

b(\prescript{(n-1)}{}{q_{i}})=(q_{i+1}^{(n-1)})a\quad\text{with }a,b\notin I\,.

By
Lemma

3.2
there exists an
n
-ambiguity
q\leq b(\prescript{(n-1)}{}{q_{i}})
. By assumption, we have
q=q_{i}
or
q=q_{i+1}
. This is a contradiction.
∎

We can now describe the Bardzell resolution.

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
\Bbbk
be a field and
A
a monomial algebra over
\Bbbk
. The complex,

\displaystyle\Bardzell(A)_{n+1}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\begin{cases}0&\text{if }n<-1\\
A\otimes_{E}\Bbbk\Gamma_{n}\otimes_{E}A&\text{if }n\geq-1\end{cases}

with differential

(3.7)

\begin{gathered}d_{n+1}(1\otimes p\otimes 1)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\begin{cases}\displaystyle\sum_{q\in\Sub(p)}\suf(q)\otimes q\otimes\pre(q)&\text{if }n\text{ odd}\\
\begin{aligned} &\suf(p^{(n-1)})\otimes p^{(n-1)}\otimes 1\\
&\quad-1\otimes\prescript{(n-1)}{}{p}\otimes\pre(\prescript{(n-1)}{}{p})\end{aligned}&\text{if }n\text{ even}\end{cases}\end{gathered}

for
p\in\Gamma_{n}
, is a resolution of
A
over
{A}^{e}
with quasi-isomorphism

\varepsilon\colon\Bardzell(A)\to A\,,\quad b\otimes p\otimes a\mapsto\begin{cases}ba&\text{if }p\in\Gamma_{-1}\text{ and }t(a)=p=s(b)\\
0&\text{else}\,.\end{cases}

We need a few more technical results for the proof. First, we describe some constraints on the position of a smaller ambiguity in a bigger one.

Lemma 3.8
.

Let
n
be an integer and
i+j=n-1
. We assume
p
is an
n
-ambiguity with a decomposition
p=bqa
such that
q
is an
i
-ambiguity.

(1)

If
i
is odd, then
a=1
if and only if
\prescript{(j)}{}{p}\leq b
.

(2)

If
i
is even, then
a\lneq\pre(\prescript{(n-1)}{}{p})
if and only if
\prescript{(j)}{}{p}\leq b
.

The analogous statements for
p^{(j)}
hold as well:

(1)

If
i
is odd, then
b=1
if and only if
p^{(j)}\leq a
.

(2)

If
i
is even, then
b\lneq\suf(p^{(n-1)})
if and only if
p^{(j)}\leq a
.

Proof.

We decompose
p
and
q
as left ambiguities
p=u_{0}\ldots u_{n}
and
q=u^{\prime}_{0}\ldots u^{\prime}_{i}
,
and let
\ell
be maximal such that
u_{\ell}\ldots u_{n}\geq q
.

Let
i
be odd. The maximality of
\ell
implies that
u_{0}^{\prime}\leq u_{\ell}
. Therefore, by
Lemma

2.6
, we have
u_{\ell+i}\leq u^{\prime}_{i}
. Hence,

a=1\iff\ell+i=n\iff u_{j+1}\ldots u_{n}\geq q\iff\prescript{(j)}{}{p}\leq b\,.

Let
i
be even. Then
\pre(\prescript{(n-1)}{}{p})=u_{n}
. By
Lemma

2.6
we have
u_{\ell+i}\geq u^{\prime}_{i}
. Hence,

a\lneq\pre(\prescript{(n-1)}{}{p})\iff\ell+i=n\iff u_{j+1}\ldots u_{n}\geq q\iff\prescript{(j)}{}{p}\leq b\,.\qed

Lemma 3.9
.

Let
m<n
be integers and
p
an
n
-ambiguity. We assume
p\hat{a}=bqa
with
q
an
m
-ambiguity and
m
even. Then,
b\notin I
if and only if
q\leq\prescript{(m+1)}{}{p}
. In particular,
q\leq p
.

An analogous statement holds for
\hat{b}p=bqa
and
p^{(m+1)}
.

Proof.

Let
p=u_{0}\ldots u_{n}
and
q=u^{\prime}_{0}\ldots u^{\prime}_{m}
be decompositions as left ambiguities. We assume
b\notin I
. Then,
b<u_{0}u_{1}
. In particular, this means
u^{\prime}_{0}\leq u_{0}u_{1}
. If
u^{\prime}_{0}=u_{0}
, then
q=\prescript{(m)}{}{p}\leq\prescript{(m+1)}{}{p}
. If
u^{\prime}_{0}\leq u_{1}
, then by
Lemma

2.6
we have
q\leq u_{1}\ldots u_{m+1}\leq\prescript{(m+1)}{}{p}
since
m
is even.

The converse is clear from the definition of
\prescript{(m+1)}{}{p}
.
∎

Proof of
Theorem

3.6
.

In the definition of the differential, we use implicitly that for
n
even an
n
-ambiguity contains exactly two
(n-1)
-ambiguities; see
Lemma

3.3
. First, we show that
(\Bardzell(A),d)
is a complex, that is that
d^{2}=0
.

Step 3.10
.

We show that, for
n
odd, we have
d^{2}(1\otimes p\otimes 1)=0
when
p\in\Gamma_{n}
.

Using the definition of
d
in
Eq.

3.7
, we can write

\displaystyle d^{2}(1\otimes p\otimes 1)

\displaystyle=\sum_{q\in\Sub(p)}\left(\suf(q)\suf(q^{(n-2)})\otimes q^{(n-2)}\otimes\pre(q)\right.

\displaystyle\quad\quad\left.-\suf(q)\otimes\prescript{(n-2)}{}{q}\otimes\pre(\prescript{(n-2)}{}{q})\pre(q)\right)\,.

We order
\Sub(p)=\{\prescript{(n-1)}{}{p}=q_{1},\ldots,q_{N}=p^{(n-1)}\}
as in
Lemma

3.4
. Then
\prescript{(n-2)}{}{q_{i}}={q_{i+1}}^{(n-2)}
. Thus we obtain

\displaystyle\quad d^{2}(1\otimes p\otimes 1)

\displaystyle=\suf(p^{(n-1)})\suf(p^{(n-2)})\otimes p^{(n-2)}\otimes 1-1\otimes\prescript{(n-2)}{}{p}\otimes\pre(\prescript{(n-2)}{}{p})\pre(\prescript{(n-1)}{}{p})

\displaystyle=0\,,

since
\suf(p^{(n-1)})\suf(p^{(n-2)})\in I
and analogously for the second term.

Step 3.11
.

We show that, for
n
even, we have
d^{2}(1\otimes p\otimes 1)=0
when
p\in\Gamma_{n}
.

We can write

\displaystyle d^{2}(1\otimes p\otimes 1)

\displaystyle=\sum_{q\in\Sub(p^{(n-1)})}\suf(p^{(n-1)})\suf(q)\otimes q\otimes\pre(q)

\displaystyle\quad-\sum_{q\in\Sub(\prescript{(n-1)}{}{p})}\suf(q)\otimes q\otimes\pre(q)\pre(\prescript{(n-1)}{}{p})\,.

For
q\in\Sub(p^{(n-1)})\cap\Sub(\prescript{(n-1)}{}{p})
, the terms in the first and the second summand coincide and thus cancel. If
q\in\Sub(p^{(n-1)})\setminus\Sub(\prescript{(n-1)}{}{p})
, then
q\not\leq\prescript{(n-1)}{}{p}
, and by
Lemma

3.9
, we have
\suf(p^{(n-1)})\suf(q)\in I
. Analogously, the prefix of the second summand lies in
I
when
q\in\Sub(\prescript{(n-1)}{}{p})\setminus\Sub(p^{(n-1)})

5.
The cup product of Hochschild cohomology

Since
\Bardzell(A)
is a free resolution of
A
over
{A}^{e}
, we can use it to give an explicit description of Hochschild cohomology
\operatorname{HH}^{*}(A)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\operatorname{Ext}^{*}_{{A}^{e}}(A,A)
. Using the Hom-tensor adjunction we obtain

\operatorname{Hom}_{{A}^{e}}(\Bardzell_{n+1}(A),A)=\operatorname{Hom}_{{A}^{e}}(A\otimes_{E}\Bbbk\Gamma_{n}\otimes_{E}A,A)\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{{E}^{e}}(\Bbbk\Gamma_{n},A)\mathrel{{=}\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}}\Bbbk\Gamma_{n}\|\mathcal{B}\,,

and we denote the complex with modules
\Bbbk\Gamma_{n}\|\mathcal{B}
with the induced differential by
\Bbbk\Gamma\|\mathcal{B}
. A basis of
\Bbbk\Gamma_{n}\|\mathcal{B}
is given by the maps

(p\|b)(q)=\begin{cases}b&\text{if }q=p\\
0&\text{else}\end{cases}\quad\text{for }q\in\Gamma_{n},

where
p\in\Gamma_{n}
and
b\in\mathcal{B}
such that
s(p)=s(b)
and
t(p)=t(b)
. The notation
-\|-
indicates that the paths
p
and
b
are parallel, starting and ending at the same vertex.

We give an explicit description of the differential. We need to treat the even and odd case separately.

Case 1
.

Let
n
be an even integer and
p\|b\in\Bbbk\Gamma_{n-1}\|\mathcal{B}
. For an
n
-ambiguity
q
, the differential is given by

(5.1)

\partial^{n}(p\|b)(q)=(p\|b)(d_{n+1}(q))=\begin{cases}0&\text{if }p\not\leq q\\
cb-ba&\text{if }cp=pa=q\\
cb&\text{if }cp=q\text{ and $p$ not a suffix of $q$}\\
-ba&\text{if }pa=q\text{ and $p$ not a prefix of $q$}\,.\end{cases}

Case 2
.

Let
n
be an odd integer and
p\|b\in\Bbbk\Gamma_{n-1}\|\mathcal{B}
. For an
n
-ambiguity
q
, the differential is given by

(5.2)

\partial^{n}(p\|b)(q)=\begin{cases}0&\text{if }p\not\leq q\\
\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{N}c_{i}ba_{i}&\text{if }c_{i}pa_{i}=q\text{ and }a_{i}\neq a_{j}\text{ for }i\neq j\,.\end{cases}

The diagonal map on
\Bardzell(A)
given in
Theorem

4.3
induces a chain map

\smile\colon\operatorname{Hom}_{{A}^{e}}(\Bardzell(A),A)\times\operatorname{Hom}_{{A}^{e}}(\Bardzell(A),A)\to\operatorname{Hom}_{{A}^{e}}(\Bardzell(A),A)\,,

which induces the cup product on Hochschild cohomology; see the discussion before
Theorem

4.3
. The cup product is given by the formula

(5.3)

(p_{2}\|b_{2}\smile p_{1}\|b_{1})(q)=\sum_{q=ep_{2}cp_{1}a}eb_{2}cb_{1}a

for any
(i+j-1)
-ambiguity
q
where
p_{1}\|b_{1}\in\Bbbk\Gamma_{i}
and
p_{2}\|b_{2}\in\Bbbk\Gamma_{j}
.

By the discussion at the beginning of
Section

4
, preceding
Theorem

4.3
, this cup product coincides with the classical cup product coming from the bar resolution up to homotopy. Hence, we can use
Eqs.

5.1
and
5.2
to compute Hochschild cohomology and
Eq.

5.3
to describe the algebra structure on the Hochschild cohomology. We utilize the formulas to describe the Hochschild cohomology algebra in the following examples.

Example 5.4
.

Let
A=\Bbbk Q/I
be a finite dimensional quadratic monomial algebra. Then, any
n
-ambiguity is of length
(n+1)
, and in the formula for the diagonal map
Eq.

4.4
, there is at most one summand for every
i+j=n-1
given by
p=q_{2}q_{1}
. On
\Bbbk\Gamma\|\mathcal{B}
this yields the formula

p_{2}\|b_{2}\smile p_{1}\|b_{1}=\begin{cases}p_{2}p_{1}\|b_{2}b_{1}&\text{if }p_{2}p_{1}\in\Gamma_{i+j-1}\text{ and }b_{2}b_{1}\in\mathcal{B}\\
0&\text{else}\end{cases}

for any
p_{1}\|b_{1}\in\Bbbk\Gamma_{i}
and
p_{2}\|b_{2}\in\Bbbk\Gamma_{j}
. This is the same cup product that Redondo and Roman obtain using the comparison maps; see
[

, 4.2]
.

The Hochschild cohomology of the associated monomial algebra
A
was described in
[
Loc99
]
, and its algebra structure in
[
BLM00
]
. The latter defined a product on
\Bbbk\Gamma_{d}\|\mathcal{B}
and showed that, on cohomology, it coincides with the cup product. In the following, we will see, that our product on
\Bbbk\Gamma_{d}\|\mathcal{B}
, as described in
Eq.

5.3
, does not coincide with the product used in
[
BLM00
]
. However, on cohomology, the products are the same.

The
(2\ell-1)
-ambiguities of
A
are precisely the paths of length
\ell m
, and the
2\ell
-ambiguities the paths of length
\ell m+1
. For convenience, we will denote by
|p|
the length of the path
p
.

Let
p\|b\in\Gamma_{i-1}\|\mathcal{B}
and
p^{\prime}\|b^{\prime}\in\Gamma_{j-1}\|\mathcal{B}
. Then

p\|b\smile p^{\prime}\|b^{\prime}=pp^{\prime}\|bb^{\prime}\quad\text{if $i$ or $j$ even}\,.

If
i
and
j
are odd, then
|p|+|p^{\prime}|=m\frac{i+j}{2}-m+2
and every
(i+j-1)
-ambiguity is of length
m\frac{i+j}{2}
. Hence,

p\|b\smile p^{\prime}\|b^{\prime}=0\quad\text{if $i$ and $j$ odd and $|b|>0$ or $|b^{\prime}|>0$}\,.

In the remaining case, when
|b|=|b^{\prime}|=0
, the cup product can have multiple terms.

In comparison, the product on
\Bbbk\Gamma_{d}\|\mathcal{B}
given in
[
BLM00
]
is

p\|b\mathrel{\overline{\smile}}p^{\prime}\|b^{\prime}=\begin{cases}pp^{\prime}\|bb^{\prime}&\text{if }pp^{\prime}\in\Gamma_{i+j-1}\\
0&\text{else}\,.\end{cases}

That is, the cases where the products need not coincide are for
i
and
j
odd and
|b|=|b^{\prime}|=0
.

By
[
Loc99

\ker(\partial^{2\ell+1})=\bigoplus_{j=1}^{c-1}\Bbbk\Gamma_{2\ell-1}\|Q_{j}

Q_{j}

and the product
\mathrel{\overline{\smile}}
of
[

coincide on the cocycles and therefore on cohomology.

6.
Hochschild Cohomology of Triangular Monomial Algebras

A quiver algebra with relations is
triangular
if the underlying quiver has no oriented cycles. For a triangular monomial algebra
A
, the differentials
Eqs.

5.1
and
5.2
on the complex
\Bbbk\Gamma\|\mathcal{B}
can be simplified. When
n
is odd, the case
cp=pa=q
cannot occur. When
n
is even, the decomposition
cpa=q
is unique, and hence, there is exactly one summand whenever
p\leq q
. These simplifications give us some control over a generating set of the cocycles given by the irreducible cocycles.

Definition 6.1
.

We fix a cocycle

(6.2)

x=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\alpha_{i}(p_{i}\|b_{i})\in\Bbbk\Gamma_{n}\|\mathcal{B}

of degree
n+1
with
\alpha_{i}\neq 0
for
1\leq i\leq k
and
p_{i}\|b_{i}
pairwise distinct. We say
x
is
irreducible
if for each sequence
1\leq i_{1}<\ldots<i_{\ell}\leq k
with
\ell<k
and non-zero elements
\beta_{j}\in\Bbbk
for
1\leq j\leq\ell
one has

\partial\left(\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}\beta_{j}(p_{i_{j}}\|b_{i_{j}})\right)\neq 0\,.

The irreducible elements form a generating set of the cocycles in
\Bbbk\Gamma_{n}\|\mathcal{B}
.

Lemma 6.3
.

Given a triangular monomial algebra
A=\Bbbk Q/I
with basis
\mathcal{B}
and an irreducible cocycle
x=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\alpha_{i}(p_{i}\|b_{i})\in\Bbbk\Gamma_{n}\|\mathcal{B}
with
\alpha_{i}\in\Bbbk
for
n\geq 0
, there exist nontrivial paths
\tilde{p}
and
\tilde{b}
with
p_{i}=c_{i}\tilde{p}a_{i}
and
b_{i}=c_{i}\tilde{b}a_{i}
for
a_{i},c_{i}\in\mathcal{B}
for all
1\leq i\leq k
.

Before we prove the
Lemma
, we make an observation for general monomial algebras on overlapping elements of
\Gamma_{n}\|\mathcal{B}
for
n\geq 0
. Let
\partial(p\|b)(q)=\partial(p^{\prime}\|b^{\prime})(q)\neq 0
for

and an
(n+1)
-ambiguity
q
. We choose
q=cpa=c^{\prime}p^{\prime}a^{\prime}
such that
cba=c^{\prime}b^{\prime}a^{\prime}
. Without loss of generality, we may assume
a\leq a^{\prime}
. Then, there exist nontrivial paths
\tilde{p}\leq p,p^{\prime}
and
\tilde{b}\leq b,b^{\prime}
such that

p=\tilde{c}\tilde{p}\,,\quad p^{\prime}=\tilde{p}\tilde{a}\,,\quad b=\tilde{c}\tilde{b}\quad\text{and}\quad b^{\prime}=\tilde{b}\tilde{a}

for some
\tilde{a}\leq a^{\prime}
and
\tilde{c}\leq c
. This is depicted in

and
p^{\prime}
.

Proof of
Lemma

6.3
.

For

\partial(p\|b)(q)=\partial(p^{\prime}\|b^{\prime})(q)\neq 0
with
p\neq p^{\prime}
. Since

is triangular,
b=p
,

and
\partial(p\|p)(q)=\partial(p^{\prime}\|p^{\prime})(q)=q=0
as
Q\in\Gamma_{1}
is a relation in

. This is a contradiction. Hence any irreducible cocycle has at most one non-zero summand and the claim is satisfied.

Suppose
n\geq 1
and
k>1
. Given a sequence
i_{0},\ldots,i_{\ell}
of positive integers
\leq k
and
n
-ambiguities
q_{1},\ldots,q_{\ell}
such that:

\displaystyle\partial(p_{i_{j}}\|b_{i_{j}})(q_{j})=c_{j}b_{i_{j}}a_{j}=c^{\prime}_{j+1}b_{i_{j+1}}a^{\prime}_{j+1}=\partial(p_{i_{j+1}}\|b_{i_{j+1}})(q_{j})\neq 0

that there exist nontrivial paths
\tilde{p}
and
\tilde{b}
with
p_{i_{j}}=\tilde{a}_{j}\tilde{p}\tilde{c}_{j}
and

For

\ell

\partial(p_{i_{\ell}}\|b_{i_{\ell}})(q_{\ell})=c_{\ell}\tilde{c}_{\ell}\tilde{b}\tilde{a}_{\ell}a_{\ell}=c^{\prime}_{\ell+1}b_{i_{\ell+1}}a^{\prime}_{\ell+1}\neq 0

. Hence, there exists a path
\tilde{p}^{\prime}

Lemma 6.4
.

Let
A=\Bbbk Q/I
be a triangular monomial algebra and
x\in\Bbbk\Gamma_{m-1}\|\mathcal{B}
,
y\in\Bbbk\Gamma_{n-1}\|\mathcal{B}
irreducible cocycles with
n,m>0
. If
x\smile y\neq 0
, then
y\smile x=0
.

Proof.

We take
\tilde{p}
and
\tilde{p}^{\prime}
for

and
y
as in
Lemma

6.3
. Since
x\smile y\neq 0
, there exists
q\in\Gamma_{n+m-1}
such that
q=e\tilde{p}c\tilde{p}^{\prime}a
. Let us suppose that
y\smile x\neq 0
. Then, there exists
q^{\prime}\in\Gamma_{n+m-1}
such that
q^{\prime}=e^{\prime}\tilde{p}^{\prime}c^{\prime}\tilde{p}a
. Hence,
\tilde{p}c\tilde{p}^{\prime}c^{\prime}
is an oriented cycle in
Q
. This is a contradiction since
A
is a triangular monomial algebra.
∎

Theorem 6.5
.

Consider a triangular monomial algebra,
A=\Bbbk Q/I
, with a basis of paths
\mathcal{B}
. Then, the cup product on
\operatorname{HH}^{*}(A)
is zero in positive degrees.

Proof.

It is enough to show that the equivalence class of a cup product of irreducible cocycles is zero. Let
x\in\Bbbk\Gamma_{n-1}\|\mathcal{B}
and
y\in\Bbbk\Gamma_{m-1}\|\mathcal{B}
be irreducible cocycles. By
Lemma

6.4
, we have
x\smile y=0
or
y\smile x=0
. Since
\smile
is graded-commutative on
\operatorname{HH}^{n+m}(A)
, we conclude that
\overline{x\smile y}
is zero in
\operatorname{HH}^{m+n}(A)
.
∎

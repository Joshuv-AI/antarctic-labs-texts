Nonlinear magnetotransport in MoTe 2
A.C. Marx, H. Jafari, E.K. Tekelenburg, M.A. Loi, J. Sławińska, M.H.D. Guimarães

Abstract
The shape of the Fermi surface influences many physical phenomena in materials and a growing interest in how the spin-dependent properties are related to the fermiology of crystals has surged. Recently, a novel current-dependent nonlinear magnetoresistance effect, known as bilinear magnetoelectric resistance (BMR), has been shown to be not only sensitive to the spin-texture in spin-polarized non-magnetic materials, but also dependent on the convexity of the Fermi surface in topological semimetals. In this paper, we show that the temperature dependence of the BMR signal strongly depends on the crystal axis of the semimetallic MoTe 2 . For the a-axis, the amplitude of the signal remains fairly constant, while for the b-axis it reverses sign at about 100 K 100\text{\,}\mathrm{K} . We calculate the BMR efficiencies at 10 K 10\text{\,}\mathrm{K} to be χ J A = {\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\chi$}}}_{J}^{A}= 173 ± 3 nm 2 T − 1 A − 1 173\pm 3\text{\,}{\mathrm{nm}}^{2}\text{\,}{\mathrm{T}}^{-1}\text{\,}{\mathrm{A}}^{-1} and χ J B = {\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\chi$}}}_{J}^{B}= − 364 ± 13 nm 2 T − 1 A − 1 -364\pm 13\text{\,}{\mathrm{nm}}^{2}\text{\,}{\mathrm{T}}^{-1}\text{\,}{\mathrm{A}}^{-1} for the a- and b-axis, respectively, and we find that they are comparable to the efficiencies measured for WTe 2 . We use density functional theory calculations to compute the Fermi surfaces of both phases at different energy levels and we observe a change in convexity of the outer-most electron pocket as a function of the Fermi energy. Our results suggest that the BMR signal is mostly dominated by the change in the Fermi surface convexity.



The bilinear magnetoelectric resistance (BMR) effect is a powerful technique to extract important information on the band structure of quantum materials, such as Fermi surface convexity and spin textures
He et al. 2018a
;
He et al. 2018b
;
Zhang et al. 2022
;
He et al. 2019
;
Liu et al. 2023
.
The BMR effect causes a modulation of the material’s resistance depending on the relative angle between applied electric and magnetic fields
Zhang and Vignale 2018
.
This effect is also sometimes referred as the unidirectional magnetoresistance (UMR)
Guillet et al. 2020
;
Calavalle et al. 2022
or electrical magnetochiral anisotropy
Rikken et al. 2001
;
Wang et al. 2022
;
Yokouchi et al. 2023
and, as the name suggests, it has a linear dependence with both current and magnetic field.
The term UMR is also commonly used in literature to address the effect in magnetic materials
Avci et al. 2015
.
To avoid confusion, we will address this effect as BMR from now on.
This effect has been used to explore the spin-dependent Fermi surface of systems with different electronic properties
He et al. 2018a
;
He et al. 2018b
;
Zhang et al. 2022
, and to give information on the shape and topology of the electron and hole pockets of semimetallic systems
He et al. 2019
.

Weyl semimetals present an interesting platform to explore new physical phenomena due to their topologically protected states
Burkov 2016
.
These states, called Fermi arcs, appear at the surface of materials and connect the conduction and valence band
Deng et al. 2016
.
A lot of work has been done in understanding the influence of the topologically protected states on the transport properties of Weyl semimetals
Chen et al. 2016a
;
Qian et al. 2014
;
Zhang et al. 2018a
;
Chen et al. 2018
;
Zhang et al. 2018b
;
Khim et al. 2016
;
Sharma et al. 2017
, but a complete understanding of the role of bulk bands is still lacking.

The Weyl semimetal candidate MoTe
2
is a van der Waals material showing low crystal symmetry
Zhang et al. 2016
;
Chen et al. 2016b
;
Stiehl et al. 2019
and strong spin-orbit coupling (SOC)
Naylor et al. 2016
;
Cui et al. 2019
.
Moreover, it undergoes a crystallographic phase transition as a function of temperature

MoTe
2
crystallizes in the monoclinic
1T^{\prime}
phase at room temperature and transitions to the orthorrombic
T{}_{d}
phase below
240\text{\,}\mathrm{K}

;

(Fig.
1

The crystallographic lattice shows a metallic zigzag chain along the
b

ab

.
The phase transition also leads to a change on the crystal symmetry group, from space group
P2_{1}/m
to space group
Pmn2_{1}

;
Roy et al. 2022
;
Tenzin et al. 2023
.
It is important to note that
1T^{\prime}
-MoTe
2

;
Yuan et al. 2013
;
Riley et al. 2014
.
Interestingly, the

phase remains a trivial semimetal

;
Wang et al. 2016
;
Jiang et al. 2017
.
Although a large magnetoresistance has been measured in

-MoTe
2

Chen et al. 2016a
;
Lee et al. 2018
;
Pei et al. 2017
, the origins of the effect are not yet clear, since theoretical calculations indicate that MoTe
2
is an uncompensated semimetal
Thirupathaiah et al. 2017
.
Moreover, despite these interesting properties and promising spintronic
Lim et al. 2018
;
Safeer et al. 2019
;

;
Zhou et al. 2019
;
Ontoso et al. 2023

Hoque et al. 2021
and topological applications

;
Luo et al. 2016
;
Zhang et al. 2018b
;
Zhang et al. 2018a
;
Chen et al. 2018
;
Singh et al. 2020

In this work, we report on the BMR effect observed for the two main crystal axis of semimetallic MoTe
2
as a function of temperature.
We observe a change in the BMR signal as we cool down from room temperature to
10\text{\,}\mathrm{K}
which is strongly anisotropic with respect to the crystal axes (Fig.
1
).
For some of our devices we observe a crystal-phase change as a function of temperature, which apparently is partially correlated to the change in the BMR signal.
Despite this crystal phase change, density-functional theory (DFT) calculations show little changes on the overall band structure or band convexity at the Fermi level for both phases.
This indicates that the strong dependence of the BMR signal we observe could be attributed to a change on the Fermi surface convexity resulting from a Fermi level shift as a function of temperature, as observed in the sister material
T_{d}
-WTe
2

He et al. 2019
.

Our devices consist of
1T^{\prime}
-MoTe
2

The hBN/

-MoTe
2

2

2
crystals from degrading.
Two perpendicular Hall bars were patterned on the stack, on the same MoTe
2
flake, using electron-beam lithography and followed by Ti/Au contact deposition by conventional techniques.
The channel of each Hall bar was designed to be aligned with one of the main axis of the crystal.
The measurements were performed using conventional harmonic low-frequency (
177\text{\,}\mathrm{Hz}
) lock-in techniques with current biases below
I_{0}=

500\text{\,}\mathrm{\SIUnitSymbolMicro A}
in a variable-temperature insert in Helium gas.
The in-plane angle (
\phi

B=

7\text{\,}\mathrm{T}
) and the current direction was varied while measuring the second-harmonic voltage in the longitudinal direction (Fig.

The measurements for the transverse direction can be found in the supplementary information

We have measured three different sets of devices with different MoTe

The change in the BMR signal as a function of temperature is clearly observed in two of our samples,

The BMR effect arises from the interplay between the applied current and magnetic field.
Mathematically, we can express the resistance of a material by a resistance term R
0
, plus a term linear on the current and magnetic field:
\text{I}\text{R}_{1}\left(\mathbf{B}\right)
.
For an ac-current
\text{I}=\text{I}_{0}\text{sin}(\omega t)
applied through the material, therefore, the longitudinal voltage can be easily written by Ohm’s law, yielding:

\text{V}=\text{R}_{0}\text{I}_{0}\text{sin}\left(\omega t\right)+\frac{1}{2}\text{R}_{1}\text{I}_{0}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\text{R}_{1}\text{I}_{0}^{2}\text{sin}\left(2\omega t-\frac{\pi}{2}\right)

(1)

with the first-harmonic, DC and second-harmonic components of the longitudinal voltage signal being the first, second and third terms on the right, respectively.
As can be seen, both DC and
\text{V}_{xx}^{2\omega}
are proportional to the BMR coefficient
\text{R}_{1}
.
Here we focus on the second-harmonic response, which is less prone to additional artifacts.

We observe a remarkably different behavior on the second-harmonic longitudinal resistance (
\text{R}_{xx}^{2\omega}
) as a function of temperature for the two crystal axes.
Here we define
\text{R}_{xx}^{2\omega}=\frac{\text{V}_{xx}^{2\omega}}{(I_{0})^{2}}
.
Figure
1
d and e show the results for
I_{0}
parallel to the
a
- and
b
-axis, respectively, for a magnetic field of +
7\text{\,}\mathrm{T}
and two different temperatures (
10\text{\,}\mathrm{K}
and
210\text{\,}\mathrm{K}
).
Experimental data is represented by the scattered points, while the full lines are the corresponding fits according to:

\text{R}_{xx}^{2\omega}=\Delta\text{R}_{xx}^{2\omega}\text{sin}[2\left(\phi+\phi_{0}\right)]+y_{0},

(2)

where
\Delta\text{R}_{xx}^{2\omega}
is the amplitude of the signal, and
\phi_{0}
and
y_{0}
are the angular and vertical offsets, respectively.
For both axes we notice that
\text{R}_{xx}^{2\omega}
shows a sinusoidal behaviour with a periodicity of
2\pi
.
Regions of high and low resistance states can be found at angles of about
90\text{\,}\mathrm{\SIUnitSymbolDegree}
and
180\text{\,}\mathrm{\SIUnitSymbolDegree}
.
This agrees with a picture of a BMR signal arising from a Rashba-like spin-texture for the Fermi surface of MoTe
2
.
Strikingly, the two axes show very different behaviours as the temperature is reduced.
For the
a
-axis, we observe only a small difference between the amplitudes at
10\text{\,}\mathrm{K}
and
210\text{\,}\mathrm{K}
, while for the
b
-axis the amplitude is not only
\sim 2.5
times larger at
10\text{\,}\mathrm{K}
, but it also changes sign compared to the measurement at
210\text{\,}\mathrm{K}
.
Further characterization of our signals, showing the magnetic field and current dependence of
\text{R}_{xx}^{2\omega}
for both a- and b-axis can be found in the supplementary information

These values and temperature behavior are comparable to the ones reported for the sister material WTe

However, we point out that different from MoTe

The results for WTe

Nonetheless, it is known that BMR can also depend on the spin texture of the Fermi surfaces

;

;

To further understand the origins of the behavior we observe, we performed DFT calculations to compute the Fermi contours of the hole and electron bands at different Fermi levels and for both crystal phases. Details of the calculations can be found in the supplementary material
sup
(see also references
Giannozzi et al. 2009
;
Giannozzi et al. 2017
;
Kresse and Furthmüller 1996
;

;

;

As shown in Fig.
3

1T^{\prime}
and
T_{d}

Both show a semimetallic behavior, with electron and hole pockets at the Fermi level.
Due to the presence of inversion symmetry in the
1T^{\prime}
phase, we do not see any spin splitting in the DFT calculations.
However, the
T_{d}
lacks inversion symmetry and shows a Rashba-like spin texture, i.e. with the spins perpendicular to the crystal momentum (see supplementary material for the spin-dependent electronic dispersion).
For this reason, we rule out that the BMR signal is dominated by a spin-dependent contribution, since the second-harmonic signal reverses sign with temperature which would imply a reversal on the spin direction.

Similar to WTe
2
, it has been demonstrated that MoTe
2
can show a shift on the Fermi level as a function of temperature
Wu et al. 2015
;
Xu et al. 2018
;
Beaulieu et al. 2021
;
Kim et al. 2021
.
To explore the implications of this to our measurements, we obtain the Fermi-surfaces at different energies (Fig.
3
) for both phases.
The overall behaviour of the electron and hole pockets with the change in energy is similar for both
1T^{\prime}
and
T_{d}
MoTe
2
, however the latter shows two extra hole pockets appearing at low energies.
Remarkably, we obtain a change in convexity of the electron pockets for both phases along the
\Gamma-Y
direction.
This change in convexity is consistent with the sign change we observe in our measurements, revealing that our measurements are dominated by a change in the Fermi surface convexity.

Our observations of a BMR signal dominated by the Fermi-surface convexity in
\text{MoTe}_{2}
are an important step for the understanding of the band structure of this Weyl semimetal candidate.
Here we report an anisotropic behavior of the BMR signal as a function of temperature, with a clear inversion below
100\text{\,}\mathrm{K}
for the current along the
b
-axis while no sign reversal is observed for the current along the
a
-axis.
This observation is in agreement with a change in the Fermi surface convexity, induced by a change in the Fermi level due to temperature, similar to what has been reported for the sister material WTe
2

He et al. 2019
.
We envision that our demonstration of large BMR signals in
\text{MoTe}_{2}
can be exploited in spintronic devices consisting of
\text{MoTe}_{2}
interfaced with two-dimensional magnets, for which the magnetic field role could be played by the magnetic exchange. This would allow one to obtain important information on the magnetization direction even on magnetic insulators, while also exploiting the unusual spin torque symmetries
Stiehl et al. 2019
provided by
\text{MoTe}_{2}
for magnetization manipulation.

On the geometric Ramanujan conjecture
Dario Beraldo

Abstract
. We prove two main results relevant for the geometric Langlands program. The first result is an analogue of the Ramanujan conjecture: any cuspidal D-module on Bun G \operatorname{Bun}_{G} is tempered. We actually prove a more general statement: any D-module that is ∗ * -extended from a quasi-compact open substack of Bun G \operatorname{Bun}_{G} is tempered. Then the assertion about cuspidal objects is an immediate consequence of a theorem of Drinfeld-Gaitsgory. Building up on this, we prove our second main result, the automorphic gluing theorem for the group S ​ L 2 SL_{2} : it states that any D-module on Bun S ​ L 2 \operatorname{Bun}_{SL_{2}} is determined by its tempered part and its constant term. This theorem (vaguely speaking, an analogue of Langlands’ classification for the group S ​ L 2 ​ ( ℝ ) SL_{2}({\mathbb{R}}) ) corresponds under geometric Langlands to the spectral gluing theorem of Arinkin-Gaitsgory and the author. In the final part of the paper, we argue that the above results pave the way for a potential proof of the geometric Langlands conjecture for G = S ​ L 2 G=SL_{2} .

1.6.
Infinitely connective objects

Let
{\mathcal{C}}
be a DG category equipped with a t-structure.
Essential to this paper is the notion of
infinitely connective object
. Let us review this notion, provide some examples and explain the role it plays here.

1.6.1.

First, let us recall the main definition, following our discussion in
[
10
, Sections 1.5 and 6.1]
. When dealing with t-structures, we use the
cohomological notation
. Given a DG category
{\mathcal{C}}
equipped with a t-structure, let

{\mathcal{C}}^{\leq-\infty}:=\bigcap_{n\gg 0}{\mathcal{C}}^{\leq-n}.

This is the full subcategory of

1.6.2.

At first glance, it might seem that
{\mathcal{C}}^{\leq-\infty}
is always zero. For instance, this is the case for
{\mathcal{C}}=\operatorname{Vect}
and much more generally for
{\mathcal{C}}={\operatorname{QCoh}}({\mathcal{Y}})
where
{\mathcal{Y}}
is a derived algebraic stack.
Even more generally,
{\mathcal{C}}^{\leq-\infty}\simeq 0
whenever the t-structure on
{\mathcal{C}}
is left-complete.

However, nonzero infinitely connective objects play an important role in the algebraic geometry of (even mildly) singular varieties: for instance, they are responsible for the difference between quasi-coherent and ind-coherent sheaves on a singular quasi-smooth scheme.

1.6.3.

Namely, in the particular case of
{{\operatorname{LS}}_{\check{G}}}
mentioned above (and much more generally), the DG category
{\operatorname{IndCoh}}_{\check{{\mathcal{N}}}}({{\operatorname{LS}}_{\check{G}}})
possesses a natural t-structure and
{\operatorname{IndCoh}}_{\check{{\mathcal{N}}}}({{\operatorname{LS}}_{\check{G}}})^{\leq-\infty}
is equivalent to the kernel of the standard projection functor

\Psi_{0\hookrightarrow\check{{\mathcal{N}}}}:{\operatorname{IndCoh}}_{\check{{\mathcal{N}}}}({{\operatorname{LS}}_{\check{G}}})\twoheadrightarrow{\operatorname{QCoh}}({{\operatorname{LS}}_{\check{G}}}).

Remark 1.6.4
.

The above is a precise manifestation of the fact that the difference between
{\operatorname{IndCoh}}
and

lies cohomologically at

[
25
]

1.6.5.

On the other side of the geometric Langlands conjecture, we will see that the DG category

\mathfrak{D}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G})^{\leq-\infty}\simeq 0

1.6.6.

The above observations force the conjectural Langlands functor

infinitely connective objects on the spectral side should correspond to anti-tempered objects on the automorphic side

[

2.1.
Some standard t-structures

Here we review certain definitions given in
[
25
]
,
[
17
]
and
[
2
]
.
We start with the definition of the natural t-structure on the DG category of ind-coherent sheaves in the quasi-smooth case. Then we pass to the discussion of t-structures on DG categories of D-modules on stacks and, afterwards, on ind-stacks.

2.1.1.

Our conventions on stacks follow those of
[
15
]
and
[
17
]
, namely we will consider algebraic stacks that are locally QCA. The “local QCA”, as opposed to just “QCA”, is allowed to accommodate for
\operatorname{Bun}_{G}
.

2.1.2.

Let
{\mathcal{Y}}
be a quasi-smooth QCA derived stack: in practice, we will be interested in only one example:
{{\operatorname{LS}}_{\check{G}}}:={{\operatorname{LS}}_{\check{G}}}(X)
. Attached to
{\mathcal{Y}}
is its stack of singularities
{\operatorname{Sing}}({\mathcal{Y}}):=H^{-1}(T^{*}{\mathcal{Y}})
.
Given any conical closed subset
M\subseteq{\operatorname{Sing}}({\mathcal{Y}})
, consider the DG category
{\operatorname{IndCoh}}_{M}({\mathcal{Y}})
of
ind-coherent sheaves with singular support contained in
M
. This is a full subcategory of
{\operatorname{IndCoh}}({\mathcal{Y}})
. If
M
contains the zero section of
{\operatorname{Sing}}({\mathcal{Y}})
, then there is a colocalization

\Xi_{0\to M}:{\operatorname{QCoh}}({\mathcal{Y}})
{\operatorname{IndCoh}}_{M}({\mathcal{Y}}):\Psi_{0\to M}.

2.1.3.

Consider the standard t-structure on
{\operatorname{QCoh}}({\mathcal{Y}})
: an object
{\mathcal{F}}
belongs to
{\operatorname{QCoh}}({\mathcal{Y}})^{\leq 0}
iff its pullback to some (equivalently, any) atlas is connective.
By reducing to affine DG schemes and then to
\operatorname{Vect}
, one checks the well-known fact that
{\operatorname{QCoh}}({\mathcal{Y}})^{\leq-\infty}\simeq 0
.

2.1.4.

Using the above t-structure on
{\operatorname{QCoh}}({\mathcal{Y}})
, one defines a t-structure on
{\operatorname{IndCoh}}_{M}({\mathcal{Y}})
by setting

{\operatorname{IndCoh}}_{M}({\mathcal{Y}})^{\leq 0}:=\{{\mathcal{F}}\in{\operatorname{IndCoh}}_{M}({\mathcal{Y}})\;|\;\Psi_{0\to M}({\mathcal{F}})\in{\operatorname{QCoh}}({\mathcal{Y}})^{\leq 0}\}.

In other words,
\Psi_{0\to M}
is right t-exact by design. One quickly deduces that
\Xi_{0\to M}
is right t-exact too. It then follows that
\Psi_{0\to M}
is t-exact: indeed, being right adjoint to a right t-exact functor, it is left t-exact.
The following simple observation is crucial for us.

Lemma 2.1.5
.

In the situation above, we have:

{\operatorname{IndCoh}}_{M}({\mathcal{Y}})^{\leq-\infty}\simeq\ker(\Psi_{0\to M}).

In particular,
{\operatorname{IndCoh}}_{M}({\mathcal{Y}})^{\leq-\infty}
is the right orthogonal of the inclusion
\Xi_{0\to M}:{\operatorname{QCoh}}({\mathcal{Y}})\hookrightarrow{\operatorname{IndCoh}}_{M}({\mathcal{Y}})
.

Proof.

The inclusion
\ker(\Psi_{0\to M})\subseteq{\operatorname{IndCoh}}_{M}({\mathcal{Y}})^{\leq-\infty}
is obvious. The opposite inclusion follows from the fact that
{\operatorname{QCoh}}({\mathcal{Y}})^{\leq-\infty}\simeq 0
.
∎

2.1.6.

Now let
{\mathcal{Y}}
be an algebraic stack, not necessarily quasi-compact and not necessarily quasi-smooth. Recall that
{\operatorname{IndCoh}}({\mathcal{Y}})
is equipped with a t-structure defined in the same as above, that is, by requiring that
\Psi_{\mathcal{Y}}:{\operatorname{IndCoh}}({\mathcal{Y}})\to{\operatorname{QCoh}}({\mathcal{Y}})
be right t-exact.
This t-structure is used in the following definition.

2.1.7.

Our next task is to discuss t-structures on categories of D-modules.
Let
{\mathcal{Y}}
be an algebraic stack as above. We define a t-structure on
\mathfrak{D}({\mathcal{Y}})
by declaring that the forgetful functor
{\mathsf{oblv}}_{{\mathcal{Y}},R}:\mathfrak{D}({\mathcal{Y}})\to{\operatorname{IndCoh}}({\mathcal{Y}})
be left t-exact.

Lemma 2.1.8
.

Let
f:\mathcal{X}\to{\mathcal{Y}}
be a map of locally QCA algebraic stacks. We have:

(1)

If
f=j
is an open embedding, then
j^{*}:\mathfrak{D}({\mathcal{Y}})\twoheadrightarrow\mathfrak{D}(\mathcal{X})
is t-exact.

(2)

If
f=i
is a closed embedding, then
i_{*}:\mathfrak{D}(\mathcal{X})\hookrightarrow\mathfrak{D}({\mathcal{Y}})
is t-exact.

(3)

The t-structure on
\mathfrak{D}({\mathcal{Y}})
is Zariski-local.

(4)

If
f
is smooth of dimension
d
, then
f^{!}[-d]:\mathfrak{D}({\mathcal{Y}})\to\mathfrak{D}(\mathcal{X})
is t-exact.

(5)

If
f
is affine, then
f_{*}:\mathfrak{D}(\mathcal{X})\to\mathfrak{D}({\mathcal{Y}})
is right t-exact.

(6)

\mathfrak{D}({\mathcal{Y}})^{\leq-\infty}\simeq 0
.

Proof.

Assertion (1) is obvious:
j^{*}
and
j_{*}
are both left t-exact by definition; the latter facts implies by adjunction that
j^{*}
is also right t-exact. Assertion (3) now follows from (1) via the argument of
[
24
, Lemma 7.8.7]
.
If
f
is a smooth atlas, assertion (4) follows from
[
17
, Lemma 6.3.7]
.
Then the general case of (4), as well as (2) and (5), all reduce to the case of schemes where they are well-known.
Finally, for (6), we can proceed as follows: by (4), it suffices to prove that
\mathfrak{D}(U)^{\leq-\infty}\simeq 0
for any scheme
U
locally of finite type. By (3), this can be checked Zariski-locally, and so we are reduced to the affine case. Then, by (2), we can embed
\mathfrak{D}(U)
in a t-exact manner into
\mathfrak{D}({\mathbb{A}}^{n})
for some
n
. Hence it remains to show that
\mathfrak{D}({\mathbb{A}}^{n})^{\leq-\infty}\simeq 0
. For this, note that the forgetful functor
\mathfrak{D}({\mathbb{A}}^{n})\to{\operatorname{QCoh}}({\mathbb{A}}^{n})\xrightarrow{H^{*}({\mathbb{A}}^{n},-)}\operatorname{Vect}
is conservative and t-exact up to a finite shift.
∎

2.1.9.

Now we extend the above definitions to DG categories of D-modules on ind-stacks.

Definition 2.1.10
.

An ind-stack is a filtered colimit of locally QCA stacks under closed embeddings.

2.1.11.

For an ind-stack
{\mathcal{Y}}\simeq\colim_{j}{\mathcal{Y}}_{j}
as defined above, we have
\mathfrak{D}({\mathcal{Y}})\simeq\colim_{j}\mathfrak{D}({\mathcal{Y}}_{j})
, as well as
\mathfrak{D}({\mathcal{Y}})\simeq\lim_{j}\mathfrak{D}({\mathcal{Y}}_{j})
.
The colimit is taken along the structure pushforward functors, the limit under their right adjoints (the
!
-pullback functors).

We define a t-structure on
\mathfrak{D}({\mathcal{Y}})
by declaring
\mathfrak{D}({\mathcal{Y}})^{\leq 0}
to be the full subcategory generated by the essential images of the inclusions
\mathfrak{D}({\mathcal{Y}}_{j})^{\leq 0}\subseteq\mathfrak{D}({\mathcal{Y}})
for all
j
.
These t-structures enjoy properties similar to those of Lemma
2.1.8
.

Remark 2.1.12
.

As an example, this construction yields a t-structure on the DG category
\mathfrak{D}(\operatorname{Hecke}_{G,x}^{{\operatorname{glob}}})
of D-modules on the ind-stack
\operatorname{Hecke}_{G,x}^{{\operatorname{glob}}}
.

2.1.13.

It is clear that the above t-structure on
\mathfrak{D}({\mathcal{Y}})
agrees with the usual one when
{\mathcal{Y}}
happens to be an ind-scheme, see
[
10
, Section 2.4.2]
and references therein.
Accordingly, we have t-structures on
\mathfrak{D}(\mathsf{Gr}_{G,x})
and on
\mathfrak{D}(G[\Sigma])
. We would like to have a t-structure on
\operatorname{Sph}_{G}\simeq\mathfrak{D}(\operatorname{Hecke}_{G,x}^{\operatorname{loc}})
as well. Since
\operatorname{Hecke}_{G,x}^{\operatorname{loc}}
is not an ind-stack, this is not covered by the above paradigm. Therefore, we give a definition “by hands”:

Definition 2.1.14
.

We put a t-structure on
\operatorname{Sph}_{G}:=\mathfrak{D}(\mathsf{Gr}_{G})^{G({\mathbb{O}})}
by declaring the forgetful functor

{\mathsf{oblv}}^{G({\mathbb{O}})}:\operatorname{Sph}_{G}\to\mathfrak{D}(\mathsf{Gr}_{G})

to be right t-exact: an object
{\mathcal{F}}\in\operatorname{Sph}_{G}
is connective iff so is
{\mathsf{oblv}}^{G({\mathbb{O}})}({\mathcal{F}})
.

Lemma 2.1.15
.

The functor
{\mathsf{oblv}}^{G({\mathbb{O}})}:\operatorname{Sph}_{G}\to\mathfrak{D}(\mathsf{Gr}_{G})
is t-exact with respect to the above t-structures.

Proof.

We need to prove that
{\mathsf{oblv}}^{G({\mathbb{O}})}
is left t-exact. We can exhaust
\mathsf{Gr}_{G}
with
{G({\mathbb{O}})}
-invariant subschemes
Y_{n}
with the following property: the
{G({\mathbb{O}})}
-action on
Y_{n}
factors through a finite dimensional quotient group
H_{n}
with pro-unipotent kernel. Define a t-structure on
\mathfrak{D}(Y_{n})^{G({\mathbb{O}})}
by declaring the functor

(2.1)

{\mathsf{oblv}}^{{G({\mathbb{O}})}}:\mathfrak{D}(Y_{n})^{G({\mathbb{O}})}\to\mathfrak{D}(Y_{n})

to be right t-exact.
Note that the fully faithful embedding
i_{!}:\mathfrak{D}(Y_{n})^{G({\mathbb{O}})}\hookrightarrow\mathfrak{D}(\mathsf{Gr}_{G})^{G({\mathbb{O}})}
is right t-exact, and so its right adjoint is left t-exact.
Thus, by the definition of the t-structure on
\mathfrak{D}(\mathsf{Gr}_{G})
, it remains to prove that (
2.1
) is left t-exact.
By pro-unipotence, we have a natural equivalence

\mathfrak{D}(Y_{n})^{G({\mathbb{O}})}\simeq\mathfrak{D}(Y_{n})^{H_{n}}\simeq\mathfrak{D}(Y_{n}/H_{n}).

Assertion (4) of Lemma
2.1.8
shows that the t-structure on
\mathfrak{D}(Y_{n})^{G({\mathbb{O}})}
corresponds, under the above equivalence, to a shift by
\dim(H_{n})
of the usual t-structure on
\mathfrak{D}(Y_{n}/H_{n})
. In particular, the pullback functor along
Y_{n}\twoheadrightarrow Y_{n}/H_{n}
is t-exact.
∎

Lemma 2.1.16
.

The above t-structure on
\operatorname{Sph}_{G}
is compatible with filtered colimits.

Proof.

By definition, a t-structure on
{\mathcal{C}}
is compactible with filtered colimits if
{\mathcal{C}}^{\geq 0}
is closed under filtered colimits. We know this is the case for
{\mathcal{C}}=\mathfrak{D}(\mathsf{Gr}_{G})
: a way to see this is to observe that
\mathfrak{D}(\mathsf{Gr}_{G})^{\leq 0}
is generated by objects that are compact in
\mathfrak{D}(\mathsf{Gr}_{G})
. Then the lemma follows from the fact that
{\mathsf{oblv}}^{G({\mathbb{O}})}:\operatorname{Sph}_{G}\to\mathfrak{D}(\mathsf{Gr}_{G})
is left t-exact, continuous and conservative.
∎

Remark 2.1.17

(Not used in what follows)
.

It is easy to see that
\operatorname{Sph}_{G}^{\leq-\infty}
is generated by
\mathsf{Av}_{!}^{{G({\mathbb{O}})}}({\mathcal{F}})
for all
{\mathcal{F}}\in\mathfrak{D}(\mathsf{Gr}_{G})
infinitely connective and ind-holonomic. Indeed, any such object
\mathsf{Av}_{!}^{{G({\mathbb{O}})}}({\mathcal{F}})
is infinite connective because
\mathsf{Av}_{!}^{{G({\mathbb{O}})}}
is right t-exact. On the other hand, given
{\mathcal{A}}\in\operatorname{Sph}_{G}^{\leq-\infty}

\mathsf{Av}_{!}^{{G({\mathbb{O}})}}{\mathsf{oblv}}^{G({\mathbb{O}})}{\mathcal{A}}\simeq(p_{G})_{!}(\omega_{G})\otimes{\mathcal{A}}\simeq H_{*}(G)\otimes{\mathcal{A}}

with
{\mathsf{oblv}}^{G({\mathbb{O}})}{\mathcal{A}}

\operatorname{Sph}_{G}
is ind-holonomic.)

2.2.
t-good and t-excellent maps

The following definitions will play an important role in the proof of Theorem
B
.

Definition 2.2.1
.

Let
\mathcal{X}
and
\mathcal{X}^{\prime}
be ind-stacks, so that their DG categories of D-modules are equipped with t-structures. We say that a map
f:\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{X}^{\prime}
is

•

t-good
if
f^{!}
sends
\mathfrak{D}(\mathcal{X}^{\prime})^{\leq-\infty}
to
\mathfrak{D}(\mathcal{X})^{\leq-\infty}
;

•

t-excellent
if the D-module pullback
f^{!}:\mathfrak{D}(\mathcal{X}^{\prime})\to\mathfrak{D}(\mathcal{X})
is right t-exact up to a finite shift.

Remark 2.2.2
.

We can extend the context of the above definitions to the case where
\mathcal{X}^{\prime}=\operatorname{Hecke}_{G,x}^{\operatorname{loc}}
. In this case
\mathfrak{D}(\mathcal{X}^{\prime})\simeq\operatorname{Sph}_{G,x}
is equipped with the t-structure of Definition
2.1.14
.

2.2.3.

A t-excellent map is in particular t-good.
The opposite implication is false: the map
r
of Section
1.6.11
is a counterexample.
A composition of t-good (respectively, t-excellent) maps is t-good (respectively, t-excellent).

Example 2.2.4
.

Let
{\mathcal{Y}}
be an ind-scheme whose dualizing D-module is infinitely connective and let
y\in{\mathcal{Y}}(\mathbbm{k})
.
As mentioned, relevant examples of such
{\mathcal{Y}}
are
{\mathbb{A}}^{\infty}
,
\mathsf{Gr}_{N}
,
\mathsf{Gr}_{G}
,
G[\Sigma]
for
G
non-abelian and
\Sigma
a smooth affine curve.
Then the closed embedding
i_{y}:{\operatorname{pt}}\hookrightarrow{\mathcal{Y}}
determined by
y\in{\mathcal{Y}}(\mathbbm{k})
is not t-good.
On the other hand, the structure map
{\mathcal{Y}}\to{\operatorname{pt}}
is t-excellent (as well as t-good for a trivial reason).
Actually, in this simple case the map
p_{\mathcal{Y}}^{!}
sends the entire
\operatorname{Vect}

Lemma 2.2.7
.

The property of being t-good can be checked smooth-locally on the source.

Proof.

Let

be a t-good map and
\rho:{\mathcal{U}}\to\mathcal{X}
a smooth map. Since

is t-good.
For the other direction, suppose that

is a smooth cover and that

is t-good: we wish to show that

is t-good.
To this end, take
{\mathcal{F}}\in\mathfrak{D}({\mathcal{Y}})^{\leq-\infty}
and
{\mathcal{G}}\in\mathfrak{D}(\mathcal{X})^{\geq m}
. Since

is arbitrary, it suffices to show that

{\mathcal{H}}{om}_{\mathfrak{D}(\mathcal{X})}(f^{!}({\mathcal{F}}),{\mathcal{G}})\in\operatorname{Vect}^{>0}.

By smooth descent for D-modules, we have

{\mathcal{H}}{om}_{\mathfrak{D}(\mathcal{X})}(f^{!}({\mathcal{F}}),{\mathcal{G}})\simeq\lim_{[n]\in\boldsymbol{\Delta}}{\mathcal{H}}{om}_{\mathfrak{D}({\mathcal{U}}_{n})}\Big((\pi_{n})^{!}f^{!}({\mathcal{F}}),(\pi_{n})^{!}{\mathcal{G}}\Big),

where the

\operatorname{Vect}^{>0}

4.2.1.

Geometric Langlands for

In

In the present informal

Next, one should prove that these three functors are compatible with gluing, and therefore they assemble to yield a functor

{\operatorname{St}}_{\check{G}}
is remarkable in at least three respects.

•

The
\mathbbm{k}
-fibers of
{\operatorname{St}}_{\check{G}}
are either
1
-dimensional or zero, depending on whether the
{\check{G}}
-local system in question is semi-simple or not. The full formulas appeared in
[
12
, Theorem D’]
; here we just give the three main examples in case
G=SL_{2}
:

(6.2)

\left.{\operatorname{St}}_{{\check{G}}}\right|_{\sigma}\simeq\begin{cases}\mathbbm{k}&\mbox{ if $\sigma$ is irreducible};\\
\mathbbm{k}[3]&\mbox{ if $\sigma$ is trivial};\\
0&\mbox{ if $\sigma$ is not semi-simple}.\end{cases}

•

Even though the first two arrows of the composition (
6.1
) are not at all fully faithful, the functor
\mathsf{DL}_{{\check{G}}}
is fully faithful when restricted to
{\operatorname{Coh}}_{\check{{\mathcal{N}}}}({{\operatorname{LS}}_{\check{G}}})
. This is
[
12
, Theorem E]
. In particular, the functor

(6.3)

\operatorname{Perf}({{\operatorname{LS}}_{\check{G}}})\xrightarrow{\underline{{\operatorname{St}}}_{{\check{G}}}\otimes-}{\operatorname{QCoh}}({{\operatorname{LS}}_{\check{G}}})

is fully faithful.

•

Finally,
{\operatorname{St}}_{\check{G}}
is intimately connected to the space of
{\check{G}}
-opers
. This connection (discussed below in Section
6.3
) is work-in-progress for general
G
, but it is almost obvious for
G
of rank
1
.

6.3.
Steinberg and opers

6.3.1.

Let us elaborate on the third item above. Consider the prestack
{\operatorname{LS}}_{{\check{G}}}^{\operatorname{Op}\!\operatorname{-}\!\mathit{gen}}
of
{\check{G}}
-local systems on
X
equipped with a generic oper structure
. We do not define this prestack in general, but point out that, for
{\check{G}}=PGL_{2}
, an
S
-point of
{\operatorname{LS}}_{{\check{G}}}^{\operatorname{Op}\!\operatorname{-}\!\mathit{gen}}
is:

•

an
S
-point
(E,\nabla)
of
{{\operatorname{LS}}_{\check{G}}}
;

•

a generic
\check{B}
-reduction of
E
, in the sense of
[
5
]
, which is not preserved by
\nabla
.

Denote by
\pi:{\operatorname{LS}}_{{\check{G}}}^{\operatorname{Op}\!\operatorname{-}\!\mathit{gen}}\to{{\operatorname{LS}}_{\check{G}}}
the map that forgets the generic oper structure. Similarly to the case of the prestack
Y[X]^{\mathsf{gen}}
of generic maps from
X
to a scheme
Y
, the DG category
\mathfrak{D}({\operatorname{LS}}_{{\check{G}}}^{\operatorname{Op}\!\operatorname{-}\!\mathit{gen}})
is well-defined and equipped with a renormalized push-forward functor

\pi_{*,{\operatorname{ren}}}:\mathfrak{D}({\operatorname{LS}}_{{\check{G}}}^{\operatorname{Op}\!\operatorname{-}\!\mathit{gen}})\longrightarrow\mathfrak{D}({{\operatorname{LS}}_{\check{G}}}).

Now we claim:

Theorem 6.3.2
.

For
{\check{G}}=PGL_{2}
, there is a natural isomorphism

\pi_{*,{\operatorname{ren}}}\big(\omega_{{\operatorname{LS}}_{{\check{G}}}^{\operatorname{Op}\!\operatorname{-}\!\mathit{gen}}}\big)\simeq{\operatorname{St}}_{\check{G}}.

Remark 6.3.3
.

We expect this result to be true for all groups
{\check{G}}
(it is work in progress at the moment): see
[
1
]
, and
[
30
]
for a related result for classical groups.

6.3.4.

Rather than giving a construction and a proof of this statement (these will appear elsewhere), we find it more instructive to look at three examples that match the three cases of (
6.2
).
Let
\sigma
be a
\mathbbm{k}
-point of
{{\operatorname{LS}}_{\check{G}}}
and set

\operatorname{Op}^{\mathsf{gen}}_{G,\sigma}:={\operatorname{LS}}_{{\check{G}}}^{\operatorname{Op}\!\operatorname{-}\!\mathit{gen}}\utimes_{{{\operatorname{LS}}_{\check{G}}}}\sigma.

One of the features of the renormalized push-forward is base-change: the pullback of
\pi_{*,{\operatorname{ren}}}\big(\omega_{{\operatorname{LS}}_{{\check{G}}}^{\operatorname{Op}\!\operatorname{-}\!\mathit{gen}}}\big)
along
\sigma:\operatorname{Spec}(\mathbbm{k})\to{{\operatorname{LS}}_{\check{G}}}
computes the Borel-Moore homology of
\operatorname{Op}^{\mathsf{gen}}_{G,\sigma}
. So, let us check that such Borel-Moore homology matches the results of (
6.2
) in the three cases of
\sigma
irreducible, trivial and not semi-simple.

6.3.5.

It follows from the definition that (at the reduced level, which is enough for the computations of Borel-Moore homology)

(6.4)

\operatorname{Op}^{\mathsf{gen}}_{G,\sigma}\simeq\begin{cases}{\mathbb{P}}^{1}[X]^{\mathsf{gen}}&\mbox{ if $\sigma$ is irreducible};\\
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}[X]^{\mathsf{gen}}-{\mathbb{P}}^{1}&\mbox{ if $\sigma$ is trivial};\\
{\mathbb{A}}^{1}[X]^{\mathsf{gen}}&\mbox{ if $\sigma$ is not semi-simple}.\end{cases}

Indeed, if
\sigma
is irreducible, no
\check{B}
-reduction of the underlying
{\check{G}}
-bundle can be preserved by the connection. Similarly, if
\sigma
is not semi-simple and
\check{B}
-reduced, no other
\check{B}
-reduction of the underlying
{\check{G}}
-bundle can be preserved by the connection: otherwise these two
\check{B}
-reductions would yield a
{\check{T}}
-reduction of
\sigma
, contradicting semi-simplicity.

6.3.6.

Now,
H_{\operatorname{BM}}({\mathbb{P}}^{1}[X]^{\mathsf{gen}})\simeq H_{*}({\mathbb{P}}^{1}[X]^{\mathsf{gen}})\simeq\mathbbm{k}
: this is the homological contractibility of the space of rational maps into a flag variety, proven in
[
5
]
building up on
[
23
]
.
We deduce that

H_{\operatorname{BM}}({\mathbb{P}}^{1}[X]^{\mathsf{gen}}-{\mathbb{P}}^{1})\simeq\operatorname{cone}\Big(H_{*}({\mathbb{P}}^{1})\longrightarrow H_{*}({\mathbb{P}}^{1}[X]^{\mathsf{gen}})\Big)\simeq\operatorname{cone}(\mathbbm{k}\oplus\mathbbm{k}[2]\to\mathbbm{k})\simeq\mathbbm{k}[3]

and that

H_{\operatorname{BM}}({\mathbb{A}}^{1}[X]^{\mathsf{gen}})\simeq H_{\operatorname{BM}}({\mathbb{P}}^{1}[X]^{\mathsf{gen}}-{\operatorname{pt}})\simeq\operatorname{cone}\Big(H_{*}({\operatorname{pt}})\longrightarrow H_{*}({\mathbb{P}}^{1}[X]^{\mathsf{gen}})\Big)\simeq\operatorname{cone}(\mathbbm{k}\to\mathbbm{k})\simeq 0,

as desired.

6.4.
Fully faithfulness

Let us now return to Conjecture
6.1.4
and in particular to the study of

{\mathop{\operatorname{{\mathcal{H}}\it om}}}_{{\operatorname{QCoh}}({\operatorname{LS}}_{{\check{G}}})}({\mathcal{F}},{\mathcal{O}}_{{\operatorname{LS}}_{{\check{G}}}})

for
{\mathcal{F}}\in\operatorname{Perf}({\operatorname{LS}}_{{\check{G}}})
.

6.4.1.

By the fully faithfulness of (
6.3
), we see that

{\mathop{\operatorname{{\mathcal{H}}\it om}}}_{{\operatorname{QCoh}}({\operatorname{LS}}_{{\check{G}}})}({\mathcal{F}},{\mathcal{O}}_{{\operatorname{LS}}_{{\check{G}}}})\simeq{\mathop{\operatorname{{\mathcal{H}}\it om}}}_{{\operatorname{QCoh}}({\operatorname{LS}}_{{\check{G}}})}({\mathcal{F}}\otimes\underline{{\operatorname{St}}}_{\check{G}},\underline{{\operatorname{St}}}_{\check{G}})

and we wish to compute the latter in automorphic terms. The object
{\mathcal{F}}\otimes\underline{{\operatorname{St}}}_{\check{G}}
is, like any other object of
{\operatorname{QCoh}}({\operatorname{LS}}_{{\check{G}}})
, generated under colimits by perfect objects, so it suffices to study the Hom spaces

{\mathop{\operatorname{{\mathcal{H}}\it om}}}_{{\operatorname{QCoh}}({\operatorname{LS}}_{{\check{G}}})}({\mathcal{P}},\underline{{\operatorname{St}}}_{\check{G}})\hskip 11.38092pt\mbox{for all ${\mathcal{P}}\in\operatorname{Perf}({{\operatorname{LS}}_{\check{G}}})$}.

6.4.2.

Now, it is known by the so-called
localization principle
, see
[
22
]
, that a collection of perfect generators of
{\operatorname{QCoh}}({{\operatorname{LS}}_{\check{G}}})
is given by objects coming from
{\mathop{\operatorname{\rm Rep}}}({\check{G}})_{\mathsf{Ran}}
. Taking one of these for
{\mathcal{P}}
, and expressing
\underline{{\operatorname{St}}}_{{\check{G}}}
via generic opers as above, brings the computation of

{\mathop{\operatorname{{\mathcal{H}}\it om}}}_{{\operatorname{QCoh}}({\operatorname{LS}}_{{\check{G}}})}({\mathcal{P}},\underline{{\operatorname{St}}}_{\check{G}})

to (a variant of) the main commutative diagram of
[
27
, Corollary 10.4.5]
, called
fundamental commutative diagram
there.

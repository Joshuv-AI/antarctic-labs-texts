Lagrangian combinatorics of matroids
Federico Ardila, Graham Denham, June Huh

Abstract
. The Lagrangian geometry of matroids was introduced in [ ADH20 ] through the construction of the conormal fan of a matroid M \M . We used the conormal fan to give a Lagrangian-geometric interpretation of the h h -vector of the broken circuit complex of M \M : its entries are the degrees of the mixed intersections of certain convex piecewise linear functions γ \gamma and δ \delta on the conormal fan of M \M . By showing that the conormal fan satisfies the Hodge-Riemann relations, we proved Brylawski’s conjecture that this h h -vector is a log-concave sequence. This sequel explores the Lagrangian combinatorics of matroids , further developing the combinatorics of biflats and biflags of a matroid, and relating them to the theory of basis activities developed by Tutte, Crapo, and Las Vergnas. Our main result is a combinatorial strengthening of the h h -vector computation: we write the k k -th mixed intersection of γ \gamma and δ \delta explicitly as a sum of biflags corresponding to the 𝑛𝑏𝑐 \nbc -bases of internal activity k + 1 k+1 .



1.
Introduction.

Let
\M
be a matroid of rank
r+1
on
n+1
elements with no loops and no coloops.
The
Lagrangian geometry of matroids
was introduced in

[
ADH20
]
through the construction of the
conormal fan
of
\M
. The conormal fan of
\M
is a Lagrangian analogue of the Bergman fan of
\M
, which
in turn is a tropical geometric model of
\M
. We used the conormal fan
to give a tropical geometric interpretation of
the
h
-vector
of the broken circuit complex
\BC(\M)
. Explicitly, we identified convex piecewise linear functions
\gamma
and
\delta
such that

\gamma^{k}\delta^{n-k-1}\cap 1_{\M,\M^{\perp}}=h_{r-k}(\BC(\M))\ \ \text{for all $k$,}

where
1_{\M,\M^{\perp}}
is the top-dimensional constant Minkowski weight
1
on the conormal fan of
\M

[
ADH20
, Theorem 1.2]
. We also showed that the conormal fan is
Lefschetz
, and in particular, satisfies the Hodge–Riemann
relations
[
ADH20
, Theorem 5.27]
.
Combining these results, we proved Brylawski’s conjecture from
[
Bry82
]
that
the
h
-vector
of the broken circuit complex
forms a log-concave sequence,
that is,

h_{i}(\BC(\M))^{2}\geq h_{i-1}(\BC(\M))h_{i+1}(\BC(\M))\ \ \text{for all $i$.}

In this followup paper, we explore the
Lagrangian combinatorics
of matroids
, which studies the combinatorial structure of
the conormal fan. We further develop the study of biflats and biflags
of matroids, initiated in
[
ADH20
]
, unveiling a strong connection
to the theory of basis activities developed by Tutte
[
Tut67
]
,
Crapo
[
Cra69
]
, and Las Vergnas

[
LV13
]
.
In particular, we directly relate the mixed intersections
\gamma^{k}\delta^{n-k-1}
to the reduced broken circuit complex using the
canonical expansion
in Section
3
,
obtaining the following bijective strengthening of
[
ADH20
, Theorem 1.2]
in the
conormal Chow ring
of Definition
1.7
.

Theorem 1.1
.

For
0\leq k\leq r
,
in the conormal Chow ring of
\M
, we have

\gamma^{k}\delta^{n-k-1}=\sum_{B}x_{{\mathscr{F}}^{+}(B)|{\mathscr{G}}^{+}(B)},

where
the sum is over the
\nbc
-bases of
\M
of internal activity
k+1
.

The symbol
{\mathscr{F}}^{+}(B)|{\mathscr{G}}^{+}(B)
stands for the extended
\nbc
-biflag associated to
B
in Section
2.2
. By construction, for every
B
,

x_{{\mathscr{F}}^{+}(B)|{\mathscr{G}}^{+}(B)}\cap 1_{\M,\M^{\perp}}=1.

Since
\M
has exactly
h_{r-k}(\BC(\M))
bases with internal activity
k+1
,
Theorem
1.1
implies
[
ADH20
, Theorem 1.2]
.
The original proof of
[
ADH20
, Theorem 1.2]
relied on the special case
k=0
of Theorem
1.1

[
ADH20
, Proposition 4.9]
and
on the theory of Chern–Schartz–MacPherson cycles of matroids
introduced by López de Medrano, Rincón, and Shaw
[
LdMRS20
]
.

1.1.
Enumerative combinatorics of matroids.

For the remainder of this paper, we fix a total ordering on the ground set
E
of
\M
and identify
E
with the set
\{0,1,\ldots,n\}
.
We refer to
[
Wel76
]
and
[
Oxl11
]
for any undefined matroid terminology.
We are interested in the following
r
-dimensional simplicial complexes associated to
\M
:

\bullet

The
independence complex

\IN(\M)
, the collection of subsets of
E
which do not contain any circuit of
\M
.

\bullet

The
broken circuit complex

\BC(\M)
, the collection of subsets of
E
which do not contain any broken circuit of
\M
.

A
broken circuit
is a subset obtained from a circuit of
\M
by deleting the least element in the fixed ordering on
E
.
The broken circuit complex
\BC(\M)
is the cone over the
reduced broken circuit complex

\RBC(\M)
with apex
0
.

For a simplicial complex
\Delta
of dimension
r
, its
f
-vector

f(\Delta)=(f_{0},f_{1},\ldots,f_{r+1})
is defined by

f_{i}(\Delta)=\text{the number of faces in $\Delta$ with $i$ vertices}.

The
f
-vector is often stored more compactly in the
h
-vector

h(\Delta)=(h_{0},h_{1},\ldots,h_{r+1})
, given by

\sum_{i=0}^{r+1}f_{i}(\Delta)q^{r-i+1}=\sum_{i=0}^{r+1}h_{i}(\Delta)(q+1)^{r-i+1}.

The
h
-vector of the broken circuit complex is given by

h_{r-k}(\BC(\M))=h_{r-k}(\RBC(\M))=t_{k+1,0}(\M)\ \ \text{for all $k$},

where
t_{i,j}(\M)
is the coefficient of
x^{i}y^{j}
in the Tutte polynomial
T_{\M}(x,y)

[
Bjö92
]
. In particular,
h_{r+1}(\BC(\M))
is zero, and
h_{r}(\BC(\M))
is Crapo’s
beta invariant

\beta_{\M}=t_{1,0}(\M).

The authors of
[
AHK18
]
and
[
ADH20
]
proved the following results,
conjectured by Mason and Hoggar

[
Mas72
,
Hog74
]
and by Brylawski and Dawson
[
Bry82
,
Daw84
]
, respectively.

Theorem 1.2
.

The following hold for any matroid
\M
.

(1)

The
f
-vectors of
\IN(\M)
and
\BC(\M)
are log-concave
[
AHK18
]
.

(2)

The
h
-vectors of
\IN(\M)
and
\BC(\M)
are log-concave
[
ADH20
]
.

We note that the independence complex of any matroid is the reduced broken circuit complex of another matroid
[
Bry77
, Theorem 4.2]
, and,
for any simplicial complex, the log-concavity of its
h
-vector implies the log-concavity of its
f
-vector
[
Bre94
, Corollary 8.4]
.
Also, for Theorem
1.2
, we may suppose that
\M
has no loops and no coloops.
We thus focus on the
h
-vector of the broken circuit complex of a matroid with no loops and no coloops.
One of the main ingredients in the proof of Theorem
1.2
in this case is the above-mentioned formula for the
h
-vector
[
ADH20
, Theorem 1.2]
, which we strengthen in Theorem
1.1
.

Remark 1.3
.

For matroids representable over the field of complex numbers, the intersection theoretic formula for the
h
-vector of the broken circuit complex was given in

[
DGS12
]
, and
the connection to the Chern–Schwartz–MacPherson classes was observed in
[
Huh13
]
and
[
Huh15
]
.
Varchenko’s conjecture on the number of critical points of products of linear forms
[
Var95
]
,
proved by Orlik and Terao in
[
OT95
]
,
is equivalent to the central special case of the formula

\delta^{n-1}\cap 1_{\M,\M^{\perp}}=h_{r}(\BC(\M)).

Recently, for any matroid, Berget, Eur, Spink, and Tseng proved a very general and closely related formula

\sum_{i+j+k+l=n}\Bigg(\int_{X_{E}}\alpha^{i}\beta^{j}c_{k}(\mathscr{S}^{\vee}_{\M})c_{l}(\mathscr{Q}_{\M})\Bigg)(x+y)x^{i}y^{j}z^{k}w^{l}=(y+z)^{r}(x+w)^{n-r+1}T_{\M}\Big(\frac{x+y}{y+z},\frac{x+y}{x+w}\Big),

where
c_{k}(\mathscr{S}^{\vee}_{\M})
and
c_{l}(\mathscr{Q}_{\M})
are
tautological Chern classes
of
\M

[
BEST21
, Theorem A]
, and they
used it to give another proof of Theorem
1.2
.
To deduce Theorem
1.2
from their formula, they used the fact
[
AFR10
,
DF10
]
that many functions of matroids behave valuatively under matroid polytope subdivisions. This allowed them to reduce key computations to the case of representable matroids and prove them using an algebro-geometric argument, thus avoiding the combinatorics of biflats and biflags.
The final part of their proof also employs the fact, proved in
[
ADH20
]
,
that the conormal fan of a matroid is a Lefschetz fan. We refer to
[
BEST21
, Remark 9.9]
for a detailed comparison of the two proofs of Theorem
1.2
.

1.2.
Lagrangian combinatorics of matroids.

This paper explores the algebraic-combinatorial structure of the
conormal Chow ring
{A_{\M,\M^{\perp}}}
introduced in
[
ADH20
, Section 3.5]
.
The conormal Chow ring is an extension
of the Chow ring
A_{\M}
studied in

[
FY04
]
and
[
AHK18
]
.
We recall the central combinatorial notions from
[
ADH20
]
.

\bullet

A
biflat

F|G
of
\M
consists of a flat
F
of
\M
and a
flat
G
of the dual matroid
\M^{\perp}
such that they are nonempty, they are not both
equal to
E
, and their union is
E
.

\bullet

Two biflats
F|G
and
F^{\prime}|G^{\prime}
of
\M
are
compatible
if

\text{($F\subseteq F^{\prime}$ and $G\supseteq G^{\prime}$)}\ \ \text{or}\ \ \text{($F\supseteq F^{\prime}$ and $G\subseteq G^{\prime}$)}.

\bullet

A
biflag
of
\M
is a collection
{\mathscr{F}}|{\mathscr{G}}
of pairwise compatible biflats of
\M
satisfying

\bigcup_{F|G\in{\mathscr{F}}|{\mathscr{G}}}F\cap G\neq E.

The
length
of
{\mathscr{F}}|{\mathscr{G}}
is
k
, the number of biflats it contains.

Remark 1.5
.

Let
{\mathscr{F}}
be an increasing sequence of
k
nonempty flats of
\M
, say

{\mathscr{F}}=(\emptyset\subsetneq F_{1}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq F_{k}\subseteq E),

and let
{\mathscr{G}}
be a decreasing sequence of
k
nonempty flats of
\M^{\perp}
, say

{\mathscr{G}}=(E\supseteq G_{1}\supseteq\cdots\supseteq G_{k}\supsetneq\emptyset).

Then the collection
{\mathscr{F}}|{\mathscr{G}}
consisting of the pairs
F_{1}|G_{1},\ldots,F_{k}|G_{k}
is a biflag of
\M
if and only if

F_{j}\cup G_{j}=E
for all
1\leq j\leq k
and
F_{j}\cup G_{j+1}\neq E
for some
0\leq j\leq k.

See
[
ADH20
, Proposition 2.15]
for a straightforward verification.

Example 1.6
.

We write
{\mathscr{F}}|{\mathscr{G}}
as a table with rows
{\mathscr{F}}
and
{\mathscr{G}}
, augmented with the columns
\emptyset|E
and
E|\emptyset
.
For example, for the square pyramid graph
G
of Figure
1
,
01256|1347

is a biflat and

\begin{array}[]{|c|c|c|}\hline\cr\emptyset&01256&E\\
E&1347&\emptyset\\
\hline\cr\end{array}\subset\begin{array}[]{|c|cccc|c|}\hline\cr\emptyset&5&56&01256&E&E\\
E&E&E&1347&3&\emptyset\\
\hline\cr\end{array}\subset\begin{array}[]{|c|cccccc|c|}\hline\cr\emptyset&5&56&01256&01256&E&E&E\\
E&E&E&1347&347&347&3&\emptyset\\
\hline\cr\end{array}

are three biflags of biflats; the third one is maximal.

The biflats and biflags of a matroid encode the combinatorics of the
conormal fan, described in
[
ADH20
, Section 3.4]
. Our main
algebraic object of interest is the Chow ring of the conormal fan, which we
now define independently. We begin with a polynomial ring
{S_{\M,\M^{\perp}}}
with real coefficients and variables
x_{F|G}

indexed by
the biflats of
\M
.
For any set of biflats
{\mathscr{F}}|{\mathscr{G}}
, we consider the monomial

\displaystyle x_{{\mathscr{F}}|{\mathscr{G}}}=\prod_{F|G\in{\mathscr{F}}|{\mathscr{G}}}x_{F|G}.

We also define, for every element
i
in the ground set
E
, the linear forms

\gamma_{i}=\sum_{i\in F,\,F\neq E}x_{F|G},\qquad\overline{\gamma}_{i}=\sum_{i\in G,\,G\neq E}x_{F|G},\qquad\delta_{i}=\sum_{i\in F\cap G}x_{F|G}.

These linear forms correspond to certain convex piecewise linear functions on the conormal fan of
\M

[
ADH20
, Section 3.4]
.
We write

\bullet

{I_{\M,\M^{\perp}}}
for the ideal generated by the monomials
x_{{\mathscr{F}}|{\mathscr{G}}}
, where
{\mathscr{F}}|{\mathscr{G}}
is not a biflag, and

\bullet

{J_{\M,\M^{\perp}}}
for the ideal generated by the linear forms
\gamma_{i}-\gamma_{j}
and
\overline{\gamma}_{i}-\overline{\gamma}_{j}
, for any
i
and
j
in
E
.

Definition 1.7
.

The
conormal Chow ring
of
\M
is the quotient

{A_{\M,\M^{\perp}}}={S_{\M,\M^{\perp}}}/({I_{\M,\M^{\perp}}}+{J_{\M,\M^{\perp}}}).

The equivalence classes of
\gamma_{i}
and
\overline{\gamma}_{i}
in the conormal Chow ring do not depend on
i
.
We denote these classes by
\gamma
and
\overline{\gamma}
, respectively.

We note that the equivalence class of
\delta_{i}
in the conormal Chow ring also do not depend on
i
:
For every biflat
F|G
, the element
i
must be in
F
or in
G
, and hence

\delta_{i}=\gamma_{i}+\overline{\gamma}_{i}-\sum_{F\neq E,G\neq E}x_{F|G}.

We write
\delta
for the class of these elements in the conormal Chow ring. In
[
ADH20
, Section 3.5]
,
we constructed the
degree map

\deg:A_{\M,\M^{\perp}}^{n-1}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R},\qquad x_{{\mathscr{F}}|{\mathscr{G}}}\longmapsto x_{{\mathscr{F}}|{\mathscr{G}}}\cap 1_{\M,\M^{\perp}}=\begin{cases}1&\text{if ${\mathscr{F}}|{\mathscr{G}}$ is a biflag,}\\
0&\text{if ${\mathscr{F}}|{\mathscr{G}}$ is not a biflag.}\end{cases}

In
[
ADH20
, Theorem 1.2]
,
we gave the following interpretation of
h(\BC(\M))
in terms of the conormal intersection theory of
\M
:

\deg(\gamma^{k}\delta^{n-k-1})=\gamma^{k}\delta^{n-k-1}\cap 1_{\M,\M^{\perp}}=h_{r-k}(\BC(\M))\ \ \text{for $0\leq k\leq r$.}

Our goal is to give a bijective proof of this numerical identity.

2.
Biflats and biflags of matroids.

Throughout the paper, we will fix a loopless and coloopless matroid
\M
with ground set
E
of size
n+1
and rank
r+1
. The dual matroid
\M^{\perp}
has rank
r^{\perp}+1\coloneq(n+1)-(r+1)=n-r
.

2.1.
Combinatorics of biflags.

The following lemma is the combinatorial manifestation of the fact that the conormal fan is pure of dimension
n-1
.
Its proof introduces several ideas that will be useful in what follows.

Lemma 2.1
.

Every maximal biflag of
\M
has length
n-1
.

Proof.

Let
{\mathscr{F}}|{\mathscr{G}}
be a biflag consisting of biflats
F_{1}|G_{1},\ldots,F_{k}|G_{k}
, say

{\mathscr{F}}=(\emptyset\subsetneq F_{1}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq F_{k}\subseteq E)\ \ \text{and}\ \ {\mathscr{G}}=(E\supseteq G_{1}\supseteq\cdots\supseteq G_{k}\supseteq\emptyset).

For every
i
, we write
r_{i}
for the rank of
F_{i}
in
\M
and
r_{i}^{\perp}
for the rank of
G_{i}
in
\M^{\perp}
.
We have

\sum_{i=1}^{k+1}\left((r_{i}-r_{i-1})+(r^{\perp}_{i-1}-r^{\perp}_{i})\right)=(r+1)+(r^{\perp}+1)=n+1.

If
j
is an index satisfying
F_{j}\cup G_{j+1}\neq E
, then we must have
F_{j}\neq F_{j+1}
and
G_{j}\neq G_{j+1}
, so

(r_{j}-r_{j-1})+(r^{\perp}_{j-1}-r^{\perp}_{j})\geq 2.

Since every summand of
n+1
is positive, there can be at most
n
summands, so
k\leq n-1
.

We now show that
{\mathscr{F}}|{\mathscr{G}}
is not maximal if
k<n-1
. We consider two cases separately.

(1)

We have
r_{i}-r_{i-1}\geq 2
or
r^{\perp}_{i-1}-r^{\perp}_{i}\geq 2
for some
i
.

Suppose
r_{i}-r_{i-1}\geq 2
.
Choose any flat
F
with
F_{i-1}\subsetneq F\subsetneq F_{i}
.
If
F\cup G_{i}\neq E
, then
F|G_{i-1}
is a biflat, and hence
{\mathscr{F}}^{+}|{\mathscr{G}}^{+}\coloneq{\mathscr{F}}|{\mathscr{G}}\cup F|G_{i-1}
is a biflag. On the other hand, if
F\cup G_{i}=E
, then
F|G_{i}
is a biflat, and
{\mathscr{F}}^{+}|{\mathscr{G}}^{+}\coloneq{\mathscr{F}}|{\mathscr{G}}\cup F|G_{i}
is a biflag.

(2)

We have
r_{i}-r_{i-1}\leq 1
and
r^{\perp}_{i-1}-r^{\perp}_{i}\leq 1
for all
i
.

In this case,
(r_{i}-r_{i-1})+(r^{\perp}_{i-1}-r^{\perp}_{i})\leq 2

for all
i
, so at least two summands of
n+1
are equal to

2
. Therefore, at least two values
i=j,j^{\prime}
satisfy
r_{i}-r_{i-1}=r^{\perp}_{i-1}-r^{\perp}_{i}=1
, and hence
F_{i-1}\subsetneq F_{i}
and

G_{i-1}\supsetneq G_{i}
. At least one of these, say
j^{\prime}
, satisfies

F_{j^{\prime}-1}\cup G_{j}^{\prime}\neq E
. Consider the other one;
F_{j}|G_{j-1}
is
a biflat, and
{\mathscr{F}}^{+}|{\mathscr{G}}^{+}\coloneq{\mathscr{F}}|{\mathscr{G}}\cup F_{j}|G_{j-1}
is a biflag,
because it satisfies
F_{j^{\prime}-1}\cup G_{j}^{\prime}\neq E
.
∎

We will often use the following basic result
[
ADH20
, Lemma 3.15]
.

Lemma 2.2
.

If
F
and
G
are nonempty flats of
\M
and
\M^{\perp}

respectively, then
\left|F\cup G\right|\neq n
.

We close this section with some definitions that will be useful throughout the paper. Let
{\mathscr{F}}|{\mathscr{G}}
be a biflag of
\M
. As above, we write
{\mathscr{F}}
and
{\mathscr{G}}
for the flags

{\mathscr{F}}=(\emptyset\subsetneq F_{1}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq F_{k}\subseteq E)\ \ \text{and}\ \ {\mathscr{G}}=(E\supseteq G_{1}\supseteq\cdots\supseteq G_{k}\supseteq\emptyset),

where
k
is the length of
{\mathscr{F}}|{\mathscr{G}}
, and
F_{0}=G_{k+1}=\emptyset
and
F_{k+1}=G_{0}=E
.

Definition 2.3
.

The
gap sequence
of
{\mathscr{F}}|{\mathscr{G}}
, denoted
\D({\mathscr{F}}|{\mathscr{G}})
,
is the sequence of
gaps

D_{0}|D_{1}|\cdots|D_{k},\ \ \text{where }\ \ D_{j}\coloneq(F_{j+1}-F_{j})\cap(G_{j}-G_{j+1})=E-(F_{j}\cup G_{j+1}).

The notion of gaps appears naturally in the model of the conormal fan as a
configuration space
[
ADH20
, Section 2]
.
Each gap must have size at least
2
, as we noted above.

Definition 2.4
.

The
jump sets
of
{\mathscr{F}}|{\mathscr{G}}
are

\displaystyle\,\mathsf{J}({\mathscr{F}})

\displaystyle=\left\{j\mid 0\leq j\leq k\text{~and~}F_{j}\subsetneq F_{j+1}\right\},

\displaystyle\,\mathsf{J}({\mathscr{G}})

\displaystyle=\left\{j\mid 0\leq j\leq k\text{~and~}G_{j}\supsetneq G_{j+1}\right\}.

The
double jump set
is
\,\mathsf{J}({\mathscr{F}})\cap\,\mathsf{J}({\mathscr{G}})
; the elements of these sets are called
jumps
of
{\mathscr{F}}
and
{\mathscr{G}}
and
double jumps
of
{\mathscr{F}}|{\mathscr{G}}
, respectively.

If the gap
D_{j}
is nonempty, then
j
must be a double jump. Thus, by definition, every biflag has at least one double jump.
The double jumps of a biflag
{\mathscr{F}}|{\mathscr{G}}
play an important role throughout the paper, so we mark them

\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{\subsetneq}
and
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{\supsetneq}
in the table for
{\mathscr{F}}|{\mathscr{G}}
.

3.
Lower bound for
\gamma^{k}\delta^{n-k-1}
.

We aim to compute the degree of
\gamma^{k}\delta^{n-k-1}
by expressing it as a sum of square-free monomials
x_{{\mathscr{F}}|{\mathscr{G}}}
.
One fundamental feature of the computation of
\gamma^{k}\delta^{n-k-1}
, which is simultaneously an advantage and a difficulty, is that there are many different ways to carry it out, since we have
n+1
different definitions of
\gamma
and
\delta
; namely
\gamma=\gamma_{i}
and
\delta=\delta_{i}
for every
i\in E
. It is not clear from the outset how one should organize this computation.

To have control over the computation, we require some structure amidst that freedom. To achieve this, we introduce two key tools in this section:

\bullet

(Definition
3.3
)
a canonical way of expanding powers of
\delta
, and

\bullet

(Lemma
3.11
)
a criterion on a monomial
m
that guarantees that
m\cdot\gamma^{k}=0
.

As we will see in Section
4
, the criterion in Lemma
3.11
shows that most expressions of the form
m\cdot\gamma^{k}
vanish in degree
n-1
. The procedure in Definition
3.3
will provide the combinatorial structure necessary to describe the terms that remain.

3.1.
The canonical expansion of
\delta^{m}
.

Let
{\mathscr{F}}|{\mathscr{G}}
be a biflag and
x_{{\mathscr{F}}|{\mathscr{G}}}
be the corresponding square-free monomial. Recall from Lemma

2.6
that the union of the gaps of
{\mathscr{F}}|{\mathscr{G}}
is nonempty and equals

D_{0}\sqcup\cdots\sqcup D_{k}=E-\bigcup_{j=1}^{k}(F_{j}\cap G_{j}).

Definition 3.1
.

(
Canonical expansion of
x_{{\mathscr{F}}|{\mathscr{G}}}\,\delta
)
For a monomial
x_{{\mathscr{F}}|{\mathscr{G}}}
, let

e=e({\mathscr{F}}|{\mathscr{G}})\coloneq\max\big(E-\bigcup_{j=1}^{k}(F_{j}\cap G_{j})\big),

Define the
canonical expansion
of
x_{{\mathscr{F}}|{\mathscr{G}}}\delta
to be the expression

x_{{\mathscr{F}}|{\mathscr{G}}}\,\delta_{e}=x_{{\mathscr{F}}|{\mathscr{G}}}\sum_{e\in F\cap G}x_{F|G}.

We recursively obtain the
canonical expansion
of
x_{{\mathscr{F}}|{\mathscr{G}}}\delta^{m}
by multiplying each monomial in the canonical expansion of
x_{{\mathscr{F}}|{\mathscr{G}}}\delta^{m-1}
by
\delta
, again using the canonical expansion.

Lemma 3.2
.

The canonical expansion of
x_{{\mathscr{F}}|{\mathscr{G}}}\,\delta
is the sum of the monomials
x_{({\mathscr{F}}\cup F)|({\mathscr{G}}\cup G)}
corresponding to the biflags of the form
{({\mathscr{F}}\cup F)|({\mathscr{G}}\cup G)}\supsetneq{{\mathscr{F}}|{\mathscr{G}}}
such that

.
If
j
is the unique index for which

. Furthermore, the nonzero terms in the canonical expansion correspond to the biflats

with

and

.

Proof.

The first statement follows directly from definition. For the second one, assume

but

e\notin G_{j+1}

but

Finally, if

(

3.3.
Multiplying by a high power of
\gamma
eradicates non-initial monomials.

Lemma 3.9
.

Consider a monomial
x_{{\mathscr{F}}|{\mathscr{G}}}
and let
l
be the largest index for which
F_{l}\neq E
. If
c\notin F_{l}
, then
x_{{\mathscr{F}}|{\mathscr{G}}}\,\gamma_{c}
is the sum of the monomials
x_{({\mathscr{F}}\cup F)|({\mathscr{G}}\cup G)}
corresponding to
the variables
x_{F|G}
with
F_{l}\cup c\subseteq F\subsetneq E
and
G_{l}\supseteq G\supseteq G_{l+1}
.

Proof.

We have

x_{{\mathscr{F}}|{\mathscr{G}}}\,\gamma_{c}=x_{{\mathscr{F}}|{\mathscr{G}}}\sum_{c\in F\neq E}x_{F|G}
.
Now, if
{({\mathscr{F}}\cup F)|({\mathscr{G}}\cup G)}
is a biflag with
c\in F\neq E
, then we must have
F_{l}\subsetneq F\subsetneq E=F_{l+1}
, so the biflat
F|G
must be added in between indices
l
and
l+1
of
{{\mathscr{F}}|{\mathscr{G}}}
. Conversely, any such biflat arises in this expansion.
∎

Definition 3.10
.

Let us call a monomial
x_{{\mathscr{F}}|{\mathscr{G}}}

initial
if
the distinct flats in
{\mathscr{F}}
have ranks
1,2,\ldots,i,
and
r+1
for some
i
.

The following technical lemma will play an important role.
It shows that the multiplication by a high power of
\gamma
eradicates non-initial monomials.

Lemma 3.11
.

Let
{\mathscr{F}}|{\mathscr{G}}
be a biflag of
\M
, and let
s
be the number of distinct proper flats in
{\mathscr{F}}
.

(1)

If
s+k>r
, then

x_{{\mathscr{F}}|{\mathscr{G}}}\,\gamma^{k}=0
.

(2)

If
s+k=r
and
{\mathscr{F}}
is not initial, then
x_{{\mathscr{F}}|{\mathscr{G}}}\,\gamma^{k}=0
.

Proof.

The proof of the first part is nearly identical and simpler than the proof of the second part.
For the second part, as in Lemma
3.9
, let
l
be the largest index for which
F_{l}\neq E
, and let
c\notin F_{l}
.
We proceed by descending induction on
s
.

The largest possible value of

for a non-initial flag is
r-1
.
Suppose first that
s=r-1
and
k=1
. If
{\mathscr{F}}
is not initial, we must have
\rank F_{l}=r
. Lemma
3.9
then implies that

x_{{\mathscr{F}}|{\mathscr{G}}}\,\gamma=x_{{\mathscr{F}}|{\mathscr{G}}}\,\gamma_{c}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}F_{l}\cup c\subseteq F\neq E\\
G_{l}\supseteq G\supseteq G_{l+1}\end{subarray}}x_{({\mathscr{F}}\cup F)|({\mathscr{G}}\cup G)}=0,

since the only flat containing
F_{l}
, which has corank 1, and
c
, which is not in
F_{l}
, is
E
.

Now suppose that the result is true for some value of
s\leq r-1
, and consider any biflag
{\mathscr{F}}|{\mathscr{G}}
, where
{\mathscr{F}}
has
s-1
distinct proper flats. We have

x_{{\mathscr{F}}|{\mathscr{G}}}\,\gamma^{r-(s-1)}=(x_{{\mathscr{F}}|{\mathscr{G}}}\,\gamma_{c})\gamma^{r-s}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}F_{l}\cup c\subseteq F\neq E\\
G_{l}\supseteq G\supseteq G_{l+1}\end{subarray}}x_{({\mathscr{F}}\cup F)|({\mathscr{G}}\cup G)}\gamma^{r-s}=0,

where each summand is

∎

\nbc

4.
Upper bound for
\gamma^{k}\delta^{n-k-1}
.

Proposition
3.5
shows that the terms
x_{{\mathscr{F}}|{\mathscr{G}}}
in the canonical expansion of

are given by the set
{\mathscr{T}}_{\M,\M^{\perp}}
of combinatorially determined tables
({\mathscr{F}}|{\mathscr{G}},\mathcal{E})
. Lemma
3.11
shows that multiplication by high powers of
\gamma
eradicates many of these monomials. The main goal of this section will be to characterize those monomials in the canonical expansion of
\delta^{n-k-1}
that resist multiplication by
\gamma^{k}
.

Definition 4.1
.

A monomial
x_{{\mathscr{F}}|{\mathscr{G}}}
of degree
n-k-1
and the corresponding to table
({\mathscr{F}}|{\mathscr{G}},\mathcal{E})\in{\mathscr{T}}_{\M,\M^{\perp}}

are said to be

if

x_{{\mathscr{F}}|{\mathscr{G}}}\,\gamma^{k}\neq 0

in the conormal Chow ring of
\M
.

We saw in Proposition
3.12
that any
\nbc
monomial does resist multiplication by
\gamma^{k}
, and gives rise to its corresponding extended
\nbc
monomial. We will eventually show in Proposition
4.15
that these are in fact the only resistant monomials, and hence that
\deg(\gamma^{k}\delta^{n-k-1})=h_{r-k}(\BC(\M))
.
This proof will require several steps, which we carry out in the following subsections.

4.1.
The jump sets of a resistant term of
\delta^{n-k-1}
.

Recall the notion of jump sets of
{\mathscr{F}}|{\mathscr{G}}
from Definition
2.4
.
We write
\Tr_{k}\M
for the
k
-th truncation of
\M
.

Lemma 4.2
.

If the monomial
x_{{\mathscr{F}}|{\mathscr{G}}}
arises in the canonical expansion of
\delta^{n-k-1}
and resists multiplication by
\gamma^{k}
, then

(1)

{\mathscr{F}}|{\mathscr{G}}
has a unique double jump, and

(2)

{\mathscr{F}}
and
{\mathscr{G}}
are complete flags of nonempty flats in
\Tr_{k}\M
and
\M^{\perp}
, with possible repetitions.

Proof.

Since
x_{{\mathscr{F}}|{\mathscr{G}}}\gamma^{k}
is nonzero, the first part of Lemma
3.11
tells us that
{\mathscr{F}}
contains
s\leq r-k
distinct proper flats, so
\left|\,\mathsf{J}({\mathscr{F}})\right|=s+1\leq r-k+1
. Also, since
\M^{\perp}
has rank
r^{\perp}+1=n-r
,
we have
\left|\,\mathsf{J}({\mathscr{G}})\right|\leq n-r
.
On the other hand, since a square-free monomial cannot contain repeated terms,
\,\mathsf{J}({\mathscr{F}})\cup\,\mathsf{J}({\mathscr{G}})=\left\{0,1,\ldots,n-k-1\right\}
.
Therefore,

n-k\leq\left|\,\mathsf{J}({\mathscr{F}})\cup\,\mathsf{J}(G)\right|+\left|\,\mathsf{J}({\mathscr{F}})\cap\,\mathsf{J}({\mathscr{G}})\right|=\left|\,\mathsf{J}({\mathscr{F}})\right|+\left|\,\mathsf{J}({\mathscr{G}})\right|\leq n-k+1,

and the number of double jumps is
\left|\,\mathsf{J}({\mathscr{F}})\cap\,\mathsf{J}({\mathscr{G}})\right|\leq 1
. But
{\mathscr{F}}|{\mathscr{G}}
has at least one nonempty gap
D_{d}
, which guarantees a double jump
d
.

The above analysis also implies that
{\mathscr{F}}
contains
s=r-k
distinct proper flats – which must have ranks
1,2,\ldots,r-k
by the second part of Lemma
3.11
–
and that
{\mathscr{G}}
has flats in every rank of
\M^{\perp}
. ∎

It follows that any table
({\mathscr{F}}|{\mathscr{G}},\mathcal{E})
arising in the canonical expansion of
\delta^{n-k-1}
that resists multiplication by
\gamma^{k}
has the form

\begin{array}[]{|cc|ccccccccccc|cc|}\hline\cr\emptyset&\subsetneq&F_{1}&\subseteq&\cdots&\subseteq&F_{d}&\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{\subsetneq}&F_{d+1}&\subseteq&\cdots&\subseteq&F_{n-k-1}&\subseteq&E\\
E&\supseteq&G_{1}&\supseteq&\cdots&\supseteq&G_{d}&\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{\supsetneq}&G_{d+1}&\supseteq&\cdots&\supseteq&G_{n-k-1}&\supsetneq&\emptyset\\
\hline\cr&&e_{1}&&\cdots&&e_{d}&\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{D}&e_{d+1}&&\cdots&&e_{n-k-1}&&\\
\hline\cr\end{array}\,

where
d
is the unique double jump of
{\mathscr{F}}|{\mathscr{G}}
.
We write

D\coloneq D_{d}=E-(F_{d}\cup G_{d+1})=(F_{d+1}-F_{d})\cap(G_{d}-G_{d+1})

for the unique nonempty gap. In every column other than the

-th, one inclusion is strict and the other one is an equality.
From now on, we will also record the nonempty gap
D
in the bottom row of the table
({\mathscr{F}}|{\mathscr{G}};\mathcal{E})
. This is redundant information, but it will be useful visually in the proofs that follow.

Remark 4.3
.

For each index
i
, the flat
F_{i}
contains the bottom row entries below it and to its left; namely,
e_{1},\ldots,e_{i}
, and
D
if
i\geq d+1
. Similarly, the coflat
G_{i}
contains the bottom row entries below it and to its right;
e_{i},\ldots,e_{n-k-1}
, and
D
if
i\leq d
.

We continue with two more easy but important properties of the canonical expansion. The first one tells us that the arrival sequence
\mathcal{E}
and the index
d
of the double jump completely determine which inclusions are strict in the table of
({\mathscr{F}}|{\mathscr{G}},\mathcal{E})
. We define the
descent set
and
ascent set
of
\mathcal{E}
by

\Des(\mathcal{E})=\{i\,:\,e_{i}>e_{i+1}\}\ \ \text{and}\ \ \Asc(\mathcal{E})=\{i\,:\,e_{i}<e_{i+1}\}.

Lemma 4.4
.

If
i\in\,\mathsf{J}({\mathscr{F}})-\,\mathsf{J}({\mathscr{G}})
, then
e_{i}>e_{i+1}
. If
i\in\,\mathsf{J}({\mathscr{G}})-\,\mathsf{J}({\mathscr{F}})
, then
e_{i}<e_{i+1}
. Therefore,

\,\mathsf{J}({\mathscr{F}})=\Des(\mathcal{E})\cup\{0,d\}\ \ \text{and}\ \ \,\mathsf{J}({\mathscr{G}})=\Asc(\mathcal{E})\cup\{d,n-k-1\}.

Proof.

It is clear from the definitions that
0\in\,\mathsf{J}({\mathscr{F}})
and
n-k-1\in\,\mathsf{J}({\mathscr{G}})
.
By symmetry, it is enough to prove the first assertion. Assume for the sake of contradiction that
i\in\,\mathsf{J}({\mathscr{F}})-\,\mathsf{J}({\mathscr{G}})
and
e_{i}<e_{i+1}
, so the table of
({\mathscr{F}}|{\mathscr{G}},\mathcal{E})
contains

\begin{array}[]{|ccccc|}\hline\cr\cdots&F_{i}&\subsetneq&F_{i+1}&\cdots\\
\cdots&G_{i}&=&G_{i+1}&\cdots\\
\hline\cr\cdots&e_{i}&<&e_{i+1}&\cdots\\
\hline\cr\end{array}\,.

Then the biflat
F_{i}|G_{i}
arrives to the monomial
x_{{\mathscr{F}}|{\mathscr{G}}}
after the biflat
F_{i+1}|G_{i+1}
does, so
e_{i}\notin F_{i+1}\cap G_{i+1}
. This contradicts the fact that
e_{i}\in F_{i}\cap G_{i}\subseteq F_{i+1}\cap G_{i+1}
.
∎

Lemma 4.5
.

If
i<j
and
e_{i}<e_{j}
, then
e_{i}\not\in G_{j}
. If
i<j
and
e_{i}>e_{j}
, then

e_{j}\not\in F_{i}
.

Proof.

It suffices to prove the first assertion. A part of the table of
({\mathscr{F}}|{\mathscr{G}},\mathcal{E})
reads

\begin{array}[]{|ccccc|}\hline\cr F_{i}&\subseteq&\cdots&\subseteq&F_{j}\\
G_{i}&\supseteq&\cdots&\supseteq&G_{j}\\
\hline\cr e_{i}&&<&&e_{j}\\
\hline\cr\end{array}\ ,

and hence
F_{i}|G_{i}
appears in the term
x_{{\mathscr{F}}|{\mathscr{G}}}
after
F_{j}|G_{j}
and
e_{i}\notin F_{j}\cap G_{j}
. Since
e_{i}\in F_{i}\subseteq F_{j}
, we must have
e_{i}\notin G_{j}
.
∎

4.2.
The resistant terms of
\delta^{n-k-1}
are determined by the bottom row of their table.

We now show that any resistant table
({\mathscr{F}}|{\mathscr{G}},\mathcal{E})
is completely determined by the bottom row of its table, that is, by
\mathcal{E}
,
d
, and
D

Proposition 4.6
.

Let
({\mathscr{F}}|{\mathscr{G}},\mathcal{E})
,
D
, and
d
be as above.

(1)

For any
x\in D
, we have

\displaystyle B(x)

\displaystyle\coloneq\text{$\{e_{i+1}\mid i\in\,\mathsf{J}({\mathscr{F}})-d\}\cup x$ is a basis of $\Tr_{k}\M$, and}

\displaystyle B^{\perp}(x)

\displaystyle\coloneq\text{$\{e_{i}\mid i\in\,\mathsf{J}({\mathscr{G}})-d\}\cup x$ is a basis of $\M^{\perp}$}.

(2)

For any
x\in D
, the flags
{\mathscr{F}}
and
{\mathscr{G}}
are given by

\displaystyle F_{j}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\cl(\{e_{i+1}\mid i\in\,\mathsf{J}({\mathscr{F}})-d,\,i+1\leq j\})&\textrm{ if }j\leq d,\\
\cl(x\cup\{e_{i+1}\mid i\in\,\mathsf{J}({\mathscr{F}})-d,\,i+1\leq j\})&\textrm{ if }j>d,\end{cases}

\displaystyle G_{j}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\cl^{\perp}(x\cup\{e_{i}\mid i\in\,\mathsf{J}({\mathscr{G}})-d,\,i\geq j\})&\textrm{ if }j\leq d,\\
\cl^{\perp}(\{e_{i}\mid i\in\,\mathsf{J}({\mathscr{G}})-d,\,i\geq j\})&\textrm{ if }j>d.\end{cases}

(3)

The ranks of the flags
{\mathscr{F}}
and
{\mathscr{G}}
are given by

\rank(F_{j})=\left|\,\mathsf{J}({\mathscr{F}})_{<j}\right|\ \ \text{and}\ \ \rank^{\perp}(G_{j})=\left|\,\mathsf{J}({\mathscr{G}})_{\geq j}\right|.

The jump sets
\,\mathsf{J}({\mathscr{F}})
and
\,\mathsf{J}({\mathscr{G}})
are given by Lemma
4.4
, and hence
{\mathscr{F}}
and
{\mathscr{G}}
are determined by
\mathcal{E}
,
d
, and
D
.
Thus, the biflag
{\mathscr{F}}|{\mathscr{G}}
is determined by the arrival sequence
\mathcal{E}
, the double jump
d
, and the nonempty gap

.

Proof.

Let us treat
B(x)
as an ordered set, ordered from left to
right.
If
i\in\,\mathsf{J}({\mathscr{F}})-\{d\}
then
F_{i}\subsetneq F_{i+1}
and
\rank(F_{i+1})=\rank(F_{i})+1
by the first part of Lemma
3.11
.
Also, Lemmas
4.4
and
4.5
tell us that

and
e_{i+1}\notin F_{i}
. This implies that

, and that

is independent from the earlier terms in

and

The third part follows from

\left|\,\mathsf{J}({\mathscr{F}})_{<j}\right|=\begin{cases}\left|(\,\mathsf{J}({\mathscr{F}})-d)_{<j}\right|&\textrm{ if }j\leq d,\\

\end{cases}\qquad\left|\,\mathsf{J}({\mathscr{G}})_{\geq j}\right|=\begin{cases}\left|(\,\mathsf{J}({\mathscr{F}})-d)_{\geq j}\right|+1&\textrm{ if }j\leq d,\\

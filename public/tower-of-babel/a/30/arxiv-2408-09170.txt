Peridynamics and Anisotropic Fractional Sobolev Spaces with Variable Exponents
Sabri Bahrouni, Julián Fernández Bonder, Ignacio Ceresa Dussel, Olimpio Miyagaki

Abstract
. In this paper, our primary objective is to develop the peridynamic fractional Sobolev space and establish novel BBM-type results associated with it. We also address the peridynamic fractional anisotropic p − p- Laplacian. A secondary objective is to explore anisotropic fractional Sobolev spaces with variable exponents, where we also derive new BBM-type results. Additionally, we address the eigenvalue problem in the isotropic case.

35R09, 46E36, 47G20, 45P05

1.
Introduction

Given a measurable function
u
on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, and an index vector
\overrightarrow{p}=\left(p_{1},p_{2},\ldots,p_{N}\right)
, where
1\leq p_{j}\leq\infty
for each
j
, we can calculate the numbers
\|u\|_{\overrightarrow{p}}
by first calculating the
L^{p_{1}}
-norm of
u\left(x_{1},\ldots,x_{N}\right)
with respect to
x_{1}
, and then the
L^{p_{2}}
-norm of the result with respect to
x_{2}
, and so on finishing with the
L^{p_{N}}
-norm with respect to
x_{N}
:

\|u\|_{\overrightarrow{p}}=\left\|\cdots\left\|\|u\|_{L^{p_{1}}\left(dx_{1}\right)}\right\|_{L^{p_{2}}\left(dx_{2}\right)}\cdots\right\|_{L^{p_{N}}\left(dx_{N}\right)}

where

\|f\|_{L^{p}(dt)}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}{\left(\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|f(\ldots,t,\ldots)|^{p}dt\right)^{1/p}\text{ if }1<p<\infty}\\
\underset{t}{\operatorname{ess}\sup}|f(\ldots,t,\ldots)|\text{ if }p=\infty.\end{array}\right.

If all the numbers
p_{j}
are finite, then

\|u\|_{\overrightarrow{p}}=\left(\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\ldots\left(\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\left(\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\left|u\left(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\right)\right|^{p_{1}}\mathrm{~d}x_{1}\right)^{p_{2}/p_{1}}\mathrm{~d}x_{2}\right)^{p_{3}/p_{2}}\ldots\mathrm{d}x_{N}\right)^{1/p_{N}}.

We will denote by
L^{\overrightarrow{p}}=L^{\overrightarrow{p}}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)
the Banach space of set of functions
u
for which
\|u\|_{\overrightarrow{p}}<\infty,
where equivalence classes of almost everywhere equal. We refer to Benedek and Panzone
[
2
]
for general information on spaces
L^{\overrightarrow{p}}
.

We define the anisotropic Sobolev space as the class of function
u
in
L^{\overrightarrow{p}}
whose partial derivatives
\partial_{i}u
are elements of
L^{p_{i}},
see
[
1
]
and references therein for more details.

This type of anisotropy led to two possible generalizations: one for Sobolev spaces with a variable exponent and the other for fractional Sobolev spaces.

For Sobolev spaces with a variable exponent, variational problems have been extensively studied, with a particular focus on the isotropic variable exponent Sobolev spaces, as detailed in
[
7
,
8
,
9
,
10
]
. In
[
3
]
, Fan carried out a systematic investigation of anisotropic variable exponent Sobolev spaces, providing an important theoretical framework for studying anisotropic and parabolic equations involving the operator
-\Delta_{\overrightarrow{p}(x)}u:=-\sum_{i=1}^{N}\partial_{i}\left(\left|\partial_{i}u\right|^{p_{i}(x)-2}\partial_{i}u\right),
(see
[
4
,
5
,
6
]
).

While variable exponent Sobolev spaces extend the classical theory by allowing the integrability order to vary, the fractional framework further generalizes this concept by using non-integer orders of differentiability, leading to a richer and more nuanced analysis of function spaces. Fractional order Sobolev spaces have gained significant attention recently, particularly following the influential papers
[
11
]
and
[
12
]
. In the seminal paper
[
13
]
, Bourgain, Brezis and Mironescu proved that for any smooth bounded domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N},u\in W^{1,p}(\Omega),1\leq p<\infty
, the well-known fractional Gagliardo seminorm recovers its local counterpart as
s
goes to 1, in the sense that

\lim_{s\uparrow 1}(1-s)\iint_{\Omega\times\Omega}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}dxdy=K(N,p)\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{p}dx,

where the constant
K(N,p)
is defined as

K(N,p)=\frac{1}{p}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{N-1}}|\boldsymbol{\omega}\cdot h|^{p}d\mathcal{H}^{N-1}(h).

Here
\mathbb{S}^{N-1}\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
denotes the unit sphere,
\mathcal{H}^{N-1}
is the (
N-1)
-dimensional Hausdorff measure and
\omega
is an arbitrary unit vector of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
. See
[
14
]
for a survey on this and related results.

In
[
17
]
, the authors studied the fractional anisotropic Sobolev space, where they considered different fractional regularity and integrability in each coordinate direction. They introduced
N
different fractional parameters
s_{1},\cdots,s_{N}
and
N
different integrability parameters
p_{1},\cdots,p_{N}
, defining a space of functions
u(x)=u(x_{1},\cdots,x_{N})
such that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{|u(x+he_{i})-u(x)|^{p_{i}}}{|h|^{1+s_{i}p_{i}}}\,dhdx<\infty\quad\text{for}\quad i=1,\cdots,N.

A Bourgain-Brezis-Mironescu (BBM) type result for an energy functional strongly related to the fractional anisotropic
p-
Laplacian has been recently proven in
[
15
]
.

One of the primary goals of this paper is, based on
[
27
,
28
]
, to study the peridynamic problem associated with the fractional anisotropic
p
-Laplacian. Peridynamics is distinguished by its nonlocality, which allows points separated by a positive distance to exert forces on each other. This feature sets peridynamics apart from classical theories that rely on gradients. As a result, peridynamics is particularly effective for addressing problems involving discontinuities, such as fractures, dislocations, or multi-scale materials. Hence, for a given real number
\delta>0
,
0<s<1
, and
1<p<\infty
, we will analyze the behavior when
\delta\rightarrow 0
of the functional of the form

(1)

[u]_{s,p,\delta}^{i}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{|h|\leq\delta}\frac{|u(x+he_{i})-u(x)|^{p}}{|h|^{1+sp}}\,dh\,dx,

and the associated nonlocal operator, namely

(-\Delta_{p})_{\delta}^{s}u(x)=p.v.\int_{|h|\leq\delta}\frac{|u(x+he_{i})-u(x)|^{p-2}(u(x+he_{i})-u(x))}{|h|^{1+sp}}\,dh.

It can be seen as a
peridynamic fractional anisotropic p-laplacian
because
(-\Delta_{p})_{\delta}^{s}
represents a truncation of the fractional anisotropic p-laplacian analyzed in the paper
[
15
]
.

Recently, a novel category of fractional Sobolev spaces with variable exponents, denoted as
W^{s,\bar{p},p(.,.)}(\Omega)
, was introduced by U. Kaufmann
et al.

[
18
]
. Initially proposed in their work, this framework has subsequently undergone refinement in
[
19
]
, where it is precisely defined as

W^{s,\bar{p},p(.,.)}(\Omega)=\bigg\{u\in L^{\bar{p}}(\Omega):\ \int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p(x,y)}}{|x-y|^{N+sp(x,y)}}dxdy<\infty\bigg\},

where
\Omega
is an open subset of
\mathbb{R}^{N}

(N\geq 2)
,
s\in(0,1)
,
\bar{p}(x)=p(x,x)
and
p
satisfying

(P)

\displaystyle p\in C\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N},(1+\infty)\right),

\displaystyle p(x,y)=p(y,x)\ \text{for all}\ x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{N}.

A second goal of this paper is to explore the anisotropic properties of fractional Sobolev spaces with variable exponents, which will be discussed in Section
4
.

Definition 1.1
.

Given
i=1,\cdots,N,

p_{0}\in C\left(\mathbb{R}^{N},(1+\infty)\right),

\Omega
an open subset of
\mathbb{R}^{N},
and
p_{i}
satisfying (
P
).
Let
\vec{s}=(s_{1},\cdots,s_{N}),
and
\vec{p}(\cdot,\cdot)=(p_{1}(\cdot,\cdot),\cdots,p_{N}(\cdot,\cdot)).
We introduce the
anisotropic fractional Sobolev spaces with variable exponent

W^{\vec{s},\vec{p}(\cdot,\cdot)}_{p_{0}}(\Omega)
as

W^{\vec{s},\vec{p}(\cdot,\cdot)}_{p_{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})=\left\{u\in L^{p_{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{N}):\quad\sum_{i=1}^{N}J_{s_{i},p_{i}}(u)<\infty\right\},

equipped with the norm

\|u\|_{\vec{s},p_{0},\vec{p}}=\|u\|_{p_{0}}+\sum_{i=1}^{N}[u]_{s_{i},p_{i}},

where

J_{s_{i},p_{i}}(u)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{|u(x+he_{i})-u(x)|^{p_{i}(x,x+he_{i})}}{|h|^{1+s_{i}p_{i}(x,x+he_{i})}}\,dhdx,

and

[u]_{s_{i},p_{i}}=\inf\left\{\lambda>0:\quad J_{s_{i},p_{i}}\left(\frac{u}{\lambda}\right))\leq 1\right\}.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows: Section 2 is a preliminary section to establish the notation. In Section 3, we address the Peridynamics case. In Section 4, we cover the anisotropic variable case. Note that in the last section, we sometimes do not specify the exponent as variable in the notations if it is clear from the context.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we briefly review the definitions and list some basic properties of the
Lebesgue spaces with variable exponent and the fractional Sobolev spaces with variable exponent.

2.1.
Variable exponent Lebesgue spaces

Let
\Omega
be a Lipschitz domain in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
and
r\in C_{+}(\overline{\Omega})
. We define the variable exponent Lebesgue space

L^{r(\cdot)}(\Omega):=\bigg\{u:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R},\ \text{measurable}\ \text{and}\ \int_{\Omega}|u(x)|^{r(x)}dx<\infty\bigg\},

endowed with the Luxemburg norm

\|u\|_{r,\Omega}:=\inf\bigg\{\lambda>0:\ \int_{\Omega}\left|\frac{u(x)}{\lambda}\right|^{r(x)}\frac{dx}{r(x)}\leq 1\bigg\}.

In the Luxemburg norm definition, we used
\frac{dx}{r(x)},
recognizing that this choice is inconsequential; it remains equivalent to using
dx
alone and streamlines the equations slightly. When there is no confusion we shall often write
\|\cdot\|_{r}
instead of
\|\cdot\|_{r,\Omega}.

Some basic properties of
L^{r(x)}(\Omega)
are listed in the following two propositions.

Proposition 2.1

(
[
9
,
10
]
)
.

The space
L^{r(\cdot)}(\Omega)
is a separable, uniformly convex Banach space and its dual space is
L^{r^{{}^{\prime}}(\cdot)}(\Omega)
, where
\frac{1}{r(x)}+\frac{1}{r^{{}^{\prime}}(x)}=1
. Furthermore, for any
u\in L^{r(\cdot)}(\Omega)
and
v\in L^{r^{{}^{\prime}}(x)}(\Omega)
, we have

\bigg|\int_{\Omega}uv\ dx\bigg|\leq 2\|u\|_{r,\Omega}\|v\|_{r^{{}^{\prime}},\Omega}.

Proposition 2.2

(
[
9
]
)
.

Define the modular
\rho_{r}:L^{r(\cdot)}(\Omega)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
by

\rho_{r}(u):=\int_{\Omega}|u|^{r(x)}dx,\ \text{for all}\ u\in L^{r(\cdot)}(\Omega).

Then, we have the following relations between norm and modular.

(i)

u\in L^{r(\cdot)}(\Omega)\setminus\{0\}
if and only if
\rho_{r}\big(\frac{u}{\|u\|_{r,\Omega}}\big)=1
.

(ii)

\rho_{r}(u)>1\ (=1,\ <1)
if and only if
\|u\|_{r,\Omega}>1,\ (=1,\ <1)
, respectively.

(iii)

If
\|u\|_{r,\Omega}>1
, then
\|u\|_{r,\Omega}^{r_{-}}\leq\rho_{r}(u)\leq\|u\|_{r,\Omega}^{r_{+}}
.

(iv)

If
\|u\|_{r,\Omega}<1
, then
\|u\|_{r,\Omega}^{r_{+}}\leq\rho_{r}(u)\leq\|u\|_{r,\Omega}^{r_{-}}
.

(v)

For a sequence
(u_{n})\subset L^{r(\cdot)}(\Omega)
and
u\in L^{r(x)}(\Omega)
, we have

\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\|u_{n}-u\|_{r,\Omega}=0\ \ \text{if and only if}\ \ \lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\rho_{r}(u_{n}-u)=0.

2.2.
Fractional Sobolev spaces with variable exponents

Let
s\in(0,1)
and
p\in C(\overline{\Omega}\times\overline{\Omega})
be such that
p
is symmetric, i.e.,
p(x,y)=p(y,x)
for all
x,y\in\overline{\Omega}
and

1<p^{-}(\Omega):=\inf_{(x,y)\in\overline{\Omega}\times\overline{\Omega}}p(x,y)\leq p^{+}(\Omega):=\sup_{(x,y)\in\overline{\Omega}\times\overline{\Omega}}p(x,y)<+\infty.

Let
\bar{p}(x)=p(x,x),
then
\bar{p}\in C_{+}(\overline{\Omega})
, and we define

W^{s,\bar{p},p(.,.)}(\Omega)=\bigg\{u\in L^{\bar{p}(x)}(\Omega):\ \int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p(x,y)}}{|x-y|^{N+sp(x,y)}}dxdy<\infty\bigg\},

and for

[u]_{s,p(.,.),\Omega}=\inf\bigg\{\lambda>0:\ \rho_{p(.,.)}\bigg(\frac{u}{\lambda}\bigg)<1\bigg\},

where

\rho_{p(.,.)}(u)=\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p(x,y)}}{|x-y|^{N+s\tilde{p}(x,y)}}\frac{dxdy}{p(x,y)}.

Then,
W^{s,\bar{p},p(.,.)}(\Omega)
endowed with the norm

\|u\|_{s,\bar{p},p,\Omega}:=\|u\|_{\bar{p},\Omega}+[u]_{s,p(.,.),\Omega},

is a separable and reflexive Banach space (see
[
18
,
21
,
22
]

Theorem 2.3
.

(
[
19
]
)
Assume that
sp^{+}(\Omega)<N.

Then, it holds that

(1)

Invoking the continuity of

3.
Peridynamics

In this section we will developed the theory for the peridynamic fractional anisotropic Sobolev space and the
peridynamic fractional anisotropic p-laplacian
.

3.1.
Peridynamic fractional anisotropic Sobolev space

Given a real number
\delta>0
,
0<s<1
and
1<p<\infty
. We will analyze, the behavior as
\delta\rightarrow 0
of functional of the form

(3)

[u]_{s,p,\delta}^{i}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{|h|\leq\delta}\frac{|u(x+he_{i})-u(x)|^{p}}{|h|^{1+sp}}\,dh\,dx.

First, we need to define an appropriate functional space to work within; therefore, we introduce the anisotropic peridynamical Sobolev space as follows:

(4)

W^{s,p,\delta}_{i}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\colon=\{u\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),\text{ such that }[u]_{s,p,\delta}^{i}<\infty\}.

This space is endowed with the norm

\|u\|_{s,p,\delta}^{i}=\left([u]_{s,p,\delta}^{i}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}+\|u\|_{p},

is a reflexive and separeble Banach space.

Now, in order to control every variable, we proceed as follows. Let
\vec{s}=(s_{1},\dots,s_{n})
,
\vec{p}=(p_{1},\dots,p_{n})
and
\vec{\delta}=\{\delta_{1},\dots,\delta_{n}\}
with
s_{i}\in(0,1]
,
p_{i}\in(1,\infty)
and
\delta_{i}>0
for
i=1,\dots,n
. Then define

W^{\vec{s},\vec{p},\vec{\delta}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})=\bigcap_{i=1}^{n}W^{s_{i},p_{i},\delta_{i}}_{i}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

This anisotropic Sobolev space is then a separable and reflexive Banach space with norm

\|u\|_{\vec{s},\vec{p},\vec{\delta}}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|u\|_{s_{i},p_{i},\delta_{i}}^{i}.

Observe that the case when each
\delta_{i}
tends to infinity corresponds to the Sobolev space studied in
[
17
]
. In that paper, the authors define the quantities

\overline{\vec{s}}=\left(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{s_{i}}\right)^{-1},\qquad\overline{\vec{s}\vec{p}}=\left(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{s_{i}p_{i}}\right)^{-1}.

Then, assuming that
\overline{\vec{s}\vec{p}}<n
the following critical Sobolev exponent is introduced

(5)

\vec{p}_{\vec{s}}\ ^{*}=\vec{p}\ ^{*}=\frac{n\overline{\vec{s}\vec{p}}/\overline{\vec{s}}}{n-\overline{\vec{s}\vec{p}}}.

The following theorem is a straightforward adaptation of Theorem 1.1 of
[
17
]
and the proof is omitted.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
s_{1},\ldots,s_{n}\in[s_{0},1)
for some
s_{0}\in(0,1)
and
p_{1},\ldots,p_{n}>1
be such that
\overline{\vec{s}\vec{p}}<n
. Then
W^{\vec{s},\vec{p},\vec{\delta}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\subset\subset L^{p}_{\text{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for every
1\leq p<\vec{p}\ ^{*}
. Moreover,
W^{\vec{s},\vec{p},\vec{\delta}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\subset L^{p}_{\text{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
continuously for every
1\leq p\leq\vec{p}\ ^{*}
.

3.2.
BBM-type results for
W_{i}^{s,p,\delta}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

Let us start by revising several classical lemmas to establish the BBM-type results.

In the rest of this section we will use the following notation
D_{i}(u)(x)=|u(x+he_{i})-u(x)|.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
u\in W^{1,p}_{i}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. Therefore the following estimate holds

[u]_{s,p,\delta}^{i}\leq 2\frac{\delta^{p(1-s)}}{p(1-s)}\left\|\partial_{i}u\right\|_{p}^{p}.

Proof.

Given
u\in C^{1}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
it is a well known fact that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|D_{i}(u)(x)|^{p}\,dx\leq|h|^{p}\|\partial_{i}u\|_{p}^{p},

then

\displaystyle\int_{|h|<\delta}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|D_{i}(u,x)|^{p}}{|h|^{1+sp}}\,dxdh=

\displaystyle\int_{|h|<\delta}\frac{1}{|h|^{1+sp}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|D_{i}(u)(x)|^{p}\,dx\right)\,dh

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\partial_{i}u\right\|_{p}^{p}2\int_{0}^{\delta}\frac{h^{p}}{|h|^{1+sp}}\,dh

\displaystyle=2\frac{\delta^{p(1-s)}}{p(1-s)}\left\|\partial_{i}u\right\|_{p}^{p}.

We conclude the proof using the density of smooth functions with compact support in
W^{1,p}_{i}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
.
∎

The next lemma shows that the functional
[\cdot]_{s,p,\delta}^{i}
decreases when one regularizes a function
u
with a standard mollifier and shows the behavior with respect to truncations. The proof is in
[
15
, Lemma 2.3]
.

Lemma 3.3
.

On one hand, let
u\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
,
\rho_{\varepsilon}
is the standard mollifier, then
u_{\epsilon}=\rho_{\varepsilon}*u
verifies

[u_{\varepsilon}]_{s,p,\delta}^{i}\leq[u]_{s,p,\delta}^{i}

for every
\varepsilon>0
.

On the other hand, given
\eta\in C^{1}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
be such that
\eta=1
on
B_{1}
,
\eta=0
on
B_{2}^{c}
,
0\leq\eta\leq 1
and
\|\nabla\eta\|_{\infty}\leq 2
. Given
k\in\mathbb{N}
, we define
\eta_{k}(x)=\eta(x/k)
. If
u_{k}=\eta_{k}u
is the truncation of
u
by
\eta_{k}
, then

[u_{k}]_{s,p,\delta}^{i}\leq 2^{p-1}[u]_{s,p,\delta}^{i}+\frac{2^{2p}\delta^{p(1-s)}}{k^{p}p(1-s)}\|u\|_{p}^{p}.

The following theorems are the main important in this section.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
u\in C^{2}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
,
0<s<1,
and
\delta>0
. Then

(6)

\lim_{\delta\rightarrow 0}\frac{1}{\delta^{p(1-s)}}[u]_{s,p,\delta}^{i}=\frac{2}{p(1-s)}\|\partial_{i}u\|_{p}.

Proof.

From the well-known estimate for smooth functions

(7)

\left|\frac{|D_{i}(u)(x)|^{p}}{|h|^{p}}-|\partial_{i}u|^{p}\right|\leq C|h|,

where
C
depends of the smoothness of
u
, we get that

\displaystyle\int_{|h|<\delta}\left|\frac{|D_{i}(u)(x)|^{p}}{|h|^{1+sp}}-\frac{|\partial_{i}u|^{p}}{|h|^{1+p(s-1)}}\right|\,dh\leq\frac{2C\delta^{p(1-s)+1}}{p(1-s)+1}.

Hence, when
\delta\rightarrow 0
we conclude that

\displaystyle\lim_{\delta\rightarrow 0}\int_{|h|<\delta}\frac{|D_{i}(u)(x)|^{p}}{|h|^{1+sp}}\,dh=\lim_{\delta\rightarrow 0}\int_{|h|<\delta}\frac{|\partial_{i}u|^{p}}{|h|^{1+p(s-1)}}\,dh=\lim_{\delta\rightarrow 0}2|\partial_{i}u|^{p}\frac{\delta^{p(1-s)}}{p(1-s)}.

Therefore

\displaystyle\lim_{\delta\rightarrow 0}\frac{1}{\delta^{p(1-s)}}\int_{|h|<\delta}\frac{|D_{i}(u)(x)|^{p}}{|h|^{1+sp}}\,dh=\frac{2|\partial_{i}u|^{p}}{p(1-s)}.

To end the proof we need to find an integrable majorant for

F_{\delta}(x)=\int_{|h|<\delta}\frac{|D_{i}(u)(x)|^{p}}{|h|^{1+sp}}\,dh.

Then since
u\in C^{2}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, there exists
R>\delta>0
such that
\mathop{\text{supp}}(u)\subset Q_{R}
, where
Q_{R}=[-R,R]^{n}
. If
|x_{i}|\leq 2R

(8)

Theorem 3.6

3.3.
Applications

In most applications, what turns out to be more useful than the norm or semi-norms are the energy functionals

J_{\delta}^{i}(u)\colon=\frac{1}{\delta^{p(1-s)}}[u]_{s,p,\delta}^{i}\text{ and }J^{i}(u)\colon=\frac{2}{p(1-s)}\|u_{x_{i}}\|_{p}^{p}.

Observe that we avoid using the notation
J_{s,p,\delta}^{i}(u)
because it would be cumbersome. However, the reader should understand that the functional also depends on
p
and
s
.

Theorems
3.6
and
3.7
immediately imply

J_{\delta}^{i}(u)\overset{\Gamma}{\longrightarrow}J_{0}^{i}(u)

as
\delta\rightarrow 0
in
L^{p}(\Omega)
for every
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
bounded. Just observe that Theorem
3.7
is exactly the lim-inf inequality and for the
lim sup inequality one just has to take the constant sequence
u_{k}=u
as a recovery
sequence and apply Theorem
3.6
.

Now, given
\vec{p}=(p_{1},\dots,p_{n})
and
\vec{s}=(s_{1},\dots,s_{n})
,
s_{i}\in(0,1)
and
\vec{\delta}=(\delta_{1},\dots,\delta_{n})
We define the functional

J_{\vec{\delta}}(u)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}J_{\delta_{i}}^{i}(u).

In the context of the application, establishing the Gamma-convergence of the entire functional is crucial. The following corollary ensures this.

Corollary 3.8
.

Let
\vec{p}=(p_{1},\dots,p_{n})
and
\vec{s}=(s_{1},\dots,s_{n})
with
p_{i}\in(1,\infty)
and
s_{i}\in(0,1)
for every

. Let
\delta_{i}^{k}\rightarrow 0
as

for

and let
\overline{\delta}_{k}=(\delta_{1}^{k},\dots,\delta_{j}^{k},\delta_{j+1},\dots,\delta_{n})
. Let
\vec{\delta}_{0}=(\underbrace{0,\dots,0}_{j},\delta_{j+1},\dots,\delta_{n})
. Then
J_{\vec{\delta}_{k}}
Gamma converges to
J_{\vec{\delta}_{0}}
in
L^{p_{\text{max}}}(\Omega)
for any
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
bounded.

Proof.

The
\liminf

follows from the
\liminf
inequality for every
J^{i}_{\delta_{i}^{k}}

(i=1,\dots,j)
and the continuity of
J^{i}_{\delta_{i}}
for

.

The
\limsup
inequality follows since the constant sequence
u_{k}=u
is a recovery sequence for
every

J^{i}_{\delta_{i}^{k}}
,
i=1,\dots,j
.
∎

3.3.1.
Peridynamic fractional anisotropic p-laplacian

In this subsection, we will show the variational nature of the problem by defining the Euler-Lagrange equation for the functionals.

Definition 3.9
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a bounded open subset. Then we define the anisotropic Sobolev space of functions vanishing at the boundary as

\displaystyle W^{\vec{s},\vec{p},\vec{\delta}}_{0}(\Omega)

\displaystyle=\{u\in W^{\vec{s},\vec{p},\vec{\delta}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\colon u|_{\Omega^{c}}=0\},

\displaystyle W^{s_{i},p_{i},\delta_{i}}_{i,0}(\Omega)

\displaystyle=\{u\in W^{s_{i},p_{i},\delta_{i}}_{i}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\colon u|_{\Omega^{c}}=0\}.

The topological dual spaces of
W^{\vec{s},\vec{p},\vec{\delta}}_{0}(\Omega)
and
W^{s_{i},p_{i},\delta_{i}}_{i,0}(\Omega)
will be denoted by
W^{-\vec{s},\vec{p}^{\prime},\vec{\delta}}(\Omega)
and
W_{i}^{-s_{i},p^{\prime}_{i},\delta_{i}}(\Omega)
respectively.

Now, it is easy to see that the functionals
J^{i}_{\delta_{i}}
are Fréchet differentiable for every
i=1,\dots,n
, every
s\in(0,1]
,
\delta_{i}>0
and every
p\in(1,\infty)
. Even more
(J^{i}_{\delta_{i}})^{\prime}\colon W^{s_{i},p_{i},\delta_{i}}_{i,0}(\Omega)\rightarrow W^{-s_{i},p_{i}^{\prime},\delta_{i}}_{i}(\Omega)
is continuous and is given by

\displaystyle\langle(J^{i}_{\delta_{i}})^{\prime}(u),v\rangle=\frac{p}{\delta^{p(1-s)}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{|h|\leq\delta}\frac{|D_{i}(u)(x)|^{p-2}D_{i}(u)(x)D_{i}(v)(x)}{|h|^{1+sp}}\,dhdx.

\langle(J^{i})^{\prime}(u),v\rangle=\frac{2}{(1-s)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\partial_{x_{i}}u|^{p-2}\partial_{x_{i}}u\ \partial_{x_{i}}v\,dx,

where
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
denotes the duality pairing between
W^{s_{i},p_{i},\delta}_{i,0}(\Omega)
and its dual
W^{-s_{i},p_{i}^{\prime},\delta}_{i}(\Omega)
.

The operator

J_{\vec{\delta}}(u)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}J_{\delta_{i}}^{i}(u).

is Fréchet differentiable with derivative given by

J_{\vec{\delta}}^{\prime}(u)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{p_{i}}(J^{i}_{\delta_{i}})^{\prime}(u).

Finally, we define the
Peridynamic fractional anisotropic
\vec{p}
-laplacian
as

(-\widetilde{\Delta}_{\vec{p}})^{\vec{s}}_{\vec{\delta}}u:=(J_{\vec{\delta}})^{\prime}(u).

Therefore, we want to study the equation

(11)

\begin{cases}(-\widetilde{\Delta}_{\vec{p}})^{\vec{s}}_{\vec{\delta}}u=f&\text{in }\Omega,\\
u=0&\text{in }\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega.\end{cases}

We say that
u\in W^{\vec{s},\vec{p}}_{0}(\Omega)
is a weak solution of (
11
) if

\langle(-\widetilde{\Delta}_{\vec{p}})^{\vec{s}}_{\vec{\delta}}u,v\rangle=\int_{\Omega}fv\,dx

for all
v\in W^{\vec{s},\vec{p},\vec{\delta}}_{0}(\Omega)
.

For a weak solution to be well defined, as usual, we need to impose some integrability conditions on the source term
f
. Hence, we consider the case where
f=f(x)
and
f\in L^{(\vec{p}\ ^{*})^{\prime}}(\Omega)
.

The existence and uniqueness of weak solutions to (
11
) is then a direct consequence of the direct method in the calculus of variations since the solution is the unique minimizer of the functional

I(v):=J_{\vec{\delta}}(v)-\int_{\Omega}fv\,dx,

which is a strictly convex, coercive, and continuous functional in
W^{\vec{s},\vec{p},\vec{\delta}}_{0}(\Omega)
.

Let us summarize all of these facts into a single statement.

Proposition 3.10
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a bounded open set, let
\vec{\delta}=(\delta_{1},\dots,\delta_{n})
,
\delta_{i}>0
,
\vec{s}=(s_{1},\dots,s_{n})
,
s_{i}\in(0,1)
,
\vec{p}=(p_{1},\dots,p_{n})
,
p_{i}\in(1,\infty)
and
\vec{p}\ ^{*}
the critical Sobolev exponent defined in the beginning of this section. Assume that
p_{\text{max}}<\vec{p}\ ^{*}
and
\overline{\vec{s}\vec{p}}<n
.

Then, for every
f\in L^{(\vec{p}\ ^{*})^{\prime}}(\Omega)
, there exists a unique weak solution
u\in W^{\vec{s},\vec{p},\vec{\delta}}_{0}(\Omega)

of (
11
).

Now, we will analyze the asymptotic behavior of the solution to (
11
) when (or some of the)
\delta_{i}\rightarrow 0
.

To this end assume that
\delta_{1}^{k},\dots,\delta_{j}^{k}\rightarrow 0
(as
k\rightarrow\infty
) and
\delta_{j+1},\dots,\delta_{n}
remain fixed and consider
\vec{\delta}_{k}:=(\delta_{1}^{k},\dots,\delta_{j}^{k},\delta_{j+1},\dots,\delta_{n})
and the functionals

I_{k}(v):=J_{\vec{\delta}_{k}}(v)-\int_{\Omega}fv\,dx.

By Proposition
3.10
, there exists a unique minimizer
u_{k}
of
I_{k}
in
W^{\vec{s},\vec{p},\vec{\delta}}_{0}(\Omega)
. Hence we want to study the behavior of the sequence
\{u_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
as
k\rightarrow\infty
.

To this end, recall that
\vec{\delta}_{0}:=(\underbrace{0,\dots,0}_{j},\delta_{j+1},\dots,\delta_{n})
and

I_{0}(v):=J_{\vec{\delta}_{0}}(v)-\int_{\Omega}fv\,dx,

where

J_{\vec{\delta}_{0}}(v):=\sum_{i=1}^{j}J^{i}(v)+\sum_{i=j+1}^{n}J^{i}_{\delta_{i}}(v).

Next, we make full use of the BBM results to conclude the following theorem:

Theorem 3.11
.

With the preceding notation, the functionals
\bar{I}_{k}\colon L^{p_{\text{min}}}(\Omega)\rightarrow\bar{\mathbb{R}}
defined as

\bar{I}_{k}(v)=\begin{cases}I_{k}(v)&\text{if }v\in W^{\vec{s},\vec{p},\vec{\delta}}_{0}(\Omega),\\
\infty&\text{elsewhere}.\end{cases}

Gamma-converges to

defined as

\bar{I}(v)=\begin{cases}I_{0}(v)&\text{if }v\in W^{\vec{s},\vec{p},\vec{\delta}}_{0}(\Omega),\\
\infty&\text{elsewhere}.\end{cases}

Proof.

By Corollary
3.8
we have that
J_{\vec{\delta}_{k}}
gamma converge to
J_{\vec{\delta}_{0}}
as
k\rightarrow\infty
. So the result follows since the functional
u\mapsto\int_{\Omega}fu\,dx
is continuous in
L^{p_{\text{min}}}(\Omega)
.
∎

An immediate consequence of this result is the convergence of the solutions of (

) to the corresponding limiting problem.

Corollary 3.12
.

Let
u_{k}\in W^{\vec{s},\vec{p},\vec{\delta}}_{0}(\Omega)
be a weak solution to

\begin{cases}(-\widetilde{\Delta}_{\vec{p}})^{\vec{s}}_{\vec{\delta}_{k}}u_{k}=f&\text{in }\Omega,\\

Then
u_{k}\rightarrow u_{0}
in

where

4.
Anisotropic fractional Sobolev Spaces with Variable Exponents

4.1.
Properties and Inequalities

We define the fractional
i^{th}-
Sobolev variable exponent space as

W_{i}^{s_{i},p_{i}(\cdot,\cdot)}(\mathbb{R}^{N}):=\left\{u\in L^{\bar{p}_{i}}(\mathbb{R}^{N}):\quad J_{s_{i},p_{i}(\cdot,\cdot)}(u)<\infty\right\}.

We can see without difficulty that the space
W_{i}^{s_{i},p_{i}(\cdot,\cdot)}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
constitutes a Banach space, characterized by the norm

\|u\|_{s_{i},p_{i}}=\|u\|_{\bar{p}_{i}}+[u]_{s_{i},p_{i}},

which is both separable and reflexive.
Define

W^{\vec{s},\vec{p}(\cdot,\cdot)}(\mathbb{R}^{N})=\bigcap_{i=1}^{N}W_{i}^{s_{i},p_{i}(\cdot,\cdot)}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),

then
W^{\vec{s},\vec{p}(\cdot,\cdot)}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
is a Banach, separable and reflexive space.

Definition 4.1
.

A function
u\in L^{1}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
belongs to the
homogeneous anisotropic fractional Sobolev spaces with variable exponent

\dot{W}^{\vec{s},\vec{p}(\cdot,\cdot)}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
if
\sum_{i=1}^{N}[u]_{s_{i},p_{i}}<\infty.

Proposition 4.2
.

If
u\in C^{1}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),
then
u\in\dot{W}^{\vec{s},\vec{p}(\cdot,\cdot)}(\mathbb{R}^{N}).

Proof.

Let
u\in C^{1}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),
there exists
R>0
such that
u(x)=0
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N},

|x|\geq R.
By the mean value Theorem,

|u(x)-u(y)|\leq\|\nabla u\|_{\infty}|x-y|,\quad\forall\ x,y\in B_{R}(0).

By Tonelli’s theorem and relabeling the variables of integration, we can write

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{|u(x+he_{i})-u(x)|^{p_{i}(x,x+he_{i})}}{|h|^{1+s_{i}p_{i}(x,x_{i}(h))}}\,dhdx

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{R}(0)}\int_{I_{i}}\frac{|u(x+he_{i})-u(x)|^{p_{i}(x,x+he_{i})}}{|h|^{1+s_{i}p_{i}(x,x+he_{i})}}\,dhdx

\displaystyle\leq\int_{B_{R}(0)}\int_{I_{i}}\frac{\|\nabla u\|_{\infty}^{p_{i}(x,x+he_{i})}|h|^{p_{i}(x,x+he_{i})}}{|h|^{1+s_{i}p_{i}(x,x+he_{i})}}dhdx

\displaystyle\leq\max\left(\|\nabla u\|_{\infty}^{p^{-}},\|\nabla u\|_{\infty}^{p^{+}}\right)\int_{I_{i}}\frac{\max\left(|h|^{p^{-}},|h|^{p^{+}}\right)}{\min\left\{|h|^{1+s_{i}(p_{i})^{-})},|h|^{1+s_{i}(p_{i})^{+})}\right\}}dh

\displaystyle:=C_{i}

where
C_{i}>0,
and
I_{i}=\left\{h\in\mathbb{R}:\ \ x+he_{i}\in B_{R}(0),\ \forall x\in B_{R}(x)\right\}.
It is not difficult to see that
I_{i}
will be a bounded subset of
\mathbb{R}.
This completes the proof.
∎

Definition 4.3
.

We define
W^{\vec{s},\vec{p}(\cdot,\cdot)}_{\bar{p}}(\Omega)
as a
C^{1}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
-module if, for any
u\in W^{\vec{s},\vec{p}(\cdot,\cdot)}_{\bar{p}}(\Omega)
and
v\in C^{1}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, the product
uv
also belongs to
W^{\vec{s},\vec{p}(\cdot,\cdot)}_{\bar{p}}(\Omega)
.

Possessing the
C^{1}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
-module property is considered advantageous. Let’s investigate the conditions under which
W^{\vec{s},\vec{p}(\cdot,\cdot)}_{\bar{p}}(\Omega)
is indeed a
C^{1}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
-module.

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded subset of
\mathbb{R}^{N}.

W^{\vec{s},\vec{p}(\cdot,\cdot)}_{\bar{p}}(\Omega)
is a
C^{1}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
-module if

\bar{p}(x)\geq p_{M}(x)\quad\text{for a.e.}\ x\in\Omega,

where
p_{M}(x):=\max\left\{p_{1}(x,x),\cdots,p_{N}(x,x)\right\}.

Proof.

Since
p_{i}(x)\leq\bar{p}(x),
then
L^{\bar{p}}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{p_{i}(\cdot)}(\Omega).
Let
u\in W^{\vec{s},\vec{p}(\cdot,\cdot)}_{\bar{p}}(\Omega)\subset L^{\bar{p}}(\Omega)
and
v\in C^{1}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\subset L^{p_{0}(\cdot)}(\Omega)
, then, obviously
uv\in L^{p_{0}(\cdot)}(\Omega).
From Proposition
4.2
,
uv\in\dot{W}^{\vec{s},\vec{p}(\cdot,\cdot)}(\mathbb{R}^{N}).
Thus
W^{\vec{s},\vec{p}(\cdot,\cdot)}_{p_{0}(\cdot)}(\Omega)
is a
C^{1}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
-module.
∎

The space
W^{\vec{s},\vec{p}(\cdot,\cdot)}_{p_{M}(\cdot)}(\Omega)
is written simply by

namely

W^{\vec{s},\vec{p}(\cdot,\cdot)}(\Omega)=\left\{u\in L^{p_{M}(\cdot)}(\Omega):\quad\sum_{i=1}^{N}J_{s_{i},p_{i},\Omega}(u)<\infty\right\}.

4.2.
BBM-type results for
W_{i}^{s,p(\cdot,\cdot)}(\mathbb{R}^{N})

In this section, we are operating with a constant direction and fixed parameters. Consequently, we will dispense with the use of the index ”

and

Theorem 4.5
.

Let

\lim_{s\rightarrow 1}J_{s,p(\cdot,\cdot)}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{2}{\overline{p}(x)}|\partial_{1}u|^{\bar{p}(x)}\,dx.

Lemma 4.6
.

Let

Then

(12)

Proof.

Since

there exists
C>0,
depending on the

such that

(13)

|u(x+he_{1})-u(x)-\partial_{1}u(x).h|\leq C|h|^{2},\quad\text{for all}\quad h\in\mathbb{R}.

Consider fixing
\varepsilon>0
and selecting

The continuity of the function

at

this value satisfies the condition that

(1-\varepsilon)|\partial_{1}u(x)|^{\bar{p}(x)}\leq|\partial_{1}u(x)|^{p(x,x+he_{1})}\leq(1+\varepsilon)|\partial_{1}u(x)|^{\bar{p}(x)},

for all

Also we have the existence of

Using the inequality

we get

|\partial_{1}u(x).h|^{p(x,x+he_{1})}\leq(1+\varepsilon)|u(x+he_{1})-u(x)|^{p(x,x+he_{1})}+C|h|^{2p(x,x+he_{1})}.

If we consider

4.3.
Eigenvalue problems: Isotropic case

The fractional
p(x)
-Laplacian
(-\triangle)^{s}_{p(.,.)}
is given by

(-\triangle)^{s}_{p(.,.)}u(x):=P.V.\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p(x,y)-2}(u(x)-u(y))}{|x-y|^{N+sp(x,y)}}dy,\ \text{for all}\ x\in\Omega.

P.V.
is a commonly used abbreviation in the principal value sense.

The eigenvalue problem associated to
(-\triangle)^{s}_{p(.,.)}
is the following:

(24)

\begin{cases}(-\triangle)^{s}_{p(.,.)}u=\Lambda|u|^{\bar{p}-2}u&\mbox{in }\Omega\\
u=0&\mbox{in}\ \mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash\Omega.\end{cases}

The natural variational approach to study problem (
24
) is to consider critical points of the non-homogeneous Rayleigh type quotient

(25)

R_{s}(u)=\frac{\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\displaystyle\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p(x,y)}}{|x-y|^{N+sp(x,y)}}dxdy}{\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|u(x)|^{\bar{p}(x)}dx}.

When looking in the literature, no prior studies have delved into the exploration of problems associated with the fractional
p(x)-
Laplacian, offering a comprehensive examination of its spectrum. However, we posit that addressing this issue may not be overly challenging, as a parallel investigation to that of the
p(x)-
Laplacian could be undertaken. Nevertheless, a notable complication arises in tackling the eigenvalue problem (
24
) due to its lack of homogeneity. This difficulty manifests particularly when dealing with operators featuring nonstandard growth. Specifically, eigenvalues in equation (
25
) are contingent on the magnitude of
\int_{\Omega}|u(x)|^{\bar{p}(x)}dx=\mu,
wherein distinct constraints may yield disparate eigenvalues.

An innovative approach, as proposed by Franzina and Lindqvist in
[
24
]
, involves incorporating norms into the Rayleigh-type quotient instead of modulars. The overarching objective is to introduce homogeneity into the analysis, thereby mitigating the challenges associated with nonstandard growth operators. The focus of their investigation centered on the local
p(x)-
Laplacian. In contrast, we introduce an exploration into a nonlocal, homogeneous Rayleigh-type quotient, expanding the scope of the study. We propose the study of the following quotient:

(26)

H_{s}(u):=\frac{K(u)}{k(u)},

where
K(u)=[u]_{s,p(.,.)}
and
k(u)=\|u\|_{\bar{p},\Omega}
(Refer to Section 2 for definitions).
Notice that the homogeneity of the quotient in (
26
) implies that eigenvalues are independent of the energy level chosen to perform the minimization, we can assume
\|u\|_{\bar{p},\Omega}=1.

We are led to consider the following operator

\mathcal{L}u(x):=P.V.\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p(x,y)-2}(u(x)-u(y))}{K(u)^{p(x,y)-1}|x-y|^{N+sp(x,y)}}dy,\ \text{for all}\ x\in\Omega.

We consider the homogeneous counterpart of (
24
):

(27)

\begin{cases}\mathcal{L}u=\Lambda\left|\frac{u}{k(u)}\right|^{\bar{p}-2}\frac{u}{k(u)}&\mbox{in }\Omega\\
u=0&\mbox{in}\ \mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash\Omega.\end{cases}

The Euler-Lagrange equation:

We define

\Lambda_{1}=\inf_{v\in X\backslash\{0\}}H_{s}(u).

Proposition 4.10
.

There exists a non-negative minimizer
u\in X\backslash\{0\},
of
\Lambda_{1}
.

Proof.

The Sobolev inequality (
2
) shows that
\Lambda_{1}>0.
Let
(v_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a minimizing sequence of
\Lambda_{1}
such that
\|v_{n}\|_{\bar{p},\Omega}=1.
Then

\Lambda_{1}=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}[v_{n}]_{s,p(.,.)}.

By Theorem
2.5
, we can extract a subsequence still denoted
(v_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
and find a function
u\in X
such that

v_{n}\rightarrow u\quad\text{strongly in}\quad L^{\bar{p}(.)}(\Omega),

v_{n}\rightharpoonup u\quad\text{weakly in}\quad X.

Since the norm is weakly sequentially lower semi-continuous, we obtain

\frac{[u]_{s,p(.,.)}}{\|u\|_{\bar{p},\Omega}}\leq\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{[v_{n}]_{s,p(.,.)}}{\|v_{n}\|_{\bar{p},\Omega}}=\Lambda_{1}.

Therefore,
u
serves as a minimizer, and the same holds true for
|u|
. With this, the proof is complete.

∎

To obtain the Euler-Lagrange equation for the minimizer, we select an arbitrary test function
\varphi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)

Before proceeding with our studies, let us compute the preceding identities.

Proposition 4.11

Let

Proof.

The proof for the derivation of

Subtracting the preceding two expressions and employing the variables

\frac{\nabla_{s}v_{\varepsilon}}{K(v_{\varepsilon})},

and

Approximation by T T means with respect to Vilenkin system in Lebesgue spaces and Lipschitz classes Thanks: The research was supported by Shota Rustaveli National Science Foundation (SRNSF) grant no. FR-24-698.

Abstract
. In this paper we present and prove some new results concerning approximation properties of T T means with respect to the Vilenkin system in Lebesgue spaces and Lipschitz classes for any 1 ≤ p < ∞ 1\leq p<\infty . As applications, we obtain extension of some known approximation inequalities.

2.
Preliminaries

Let
\mathbb{N}_{+}
denote the set of the positive integers,
\mathbb{N}:=\mathbb{N}_{+}\cup\{0\}.
Let

m=:(m_{0},m_{1},...)

be a sequence of positive integers not less than 2. Denote by

Z_{m_{k}}:=\{0,1,...,m_{k}-1\}

the additive group of integers modulo
m_{k}.
Define the group
G_{m}
as the complete direct product of the group
Z_{m_{k}}
with the product of the discrete topologies of
Z_{m_{k}}\textrm{'s}
.

The direct product
\mu
of the measures

\mu_{k}\left(\{j\}\right):=1/m_{k}\text{ \ }(j\in Z_{m_{k}})

is the Haar measure on
G_{m}
with
\mu\left(G_{m}\right)=1.

If
\sup_{k\in\mathbb{N}}m_{k}<+\infty
, then we call
G_{m}
a bounded Vilenkin group. If the sequence
\{m_{k}\}_{k\geq 0}
is unbounded, then
G_{m}
is said to be unbounded Vilenkin group. In this paper we consider only bounded Vilenkin groups.

The elements of
G_{m}
are represented by the sequences

x:=(x_{0},x_{1},\dots,x_{k},\dots)\qquad\left(\text{ }x_{k}\in Z_{m_{k}}\right).

It is easy to give a base for the neighborhood of
G_{m}
, namely

\displaystyle I_{0}\left(x\right):=G_{m},\ \ \ I_{n}(x):=\{y\in G_{m}\mid y_{0}=x_{0},\dots,y_{n-1}=x_{n-1}\}\text{ }(x\in G_{m},\text{ }n\in\mathbb{N}).

For simplicity we also define
I_{n}:=I_{n}(0).

Next, we define a generalized number system based on
m
in the following way:

M_{0}=:1,\quad M_{k+1}=:m_{k}M_{k}\quad(k\in\mathbb{N})

Then every
n\in\mathbb{N}
can be uniquely expressed as

n=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}n_{j}M_{j},\ \ \ \text{where }\ \ \ n_{j}\in Z_{m_{j}}\ \ \ (j\in\mathbb{N})

and only a finite number of
n_{j}`
s differ from zero. Let

|n|=:\max\{j\in\mathbb{N},n_{j}\neq 0\}.

Moreover, Vilenkin (see
[
35
,
36
,
37
]
) investigated the group
G_{m}
and introduced the Vilenkin system
\{{\psi}_{j}\}_{j=0}^{\infty}
as

\psi_{n}\left(x\right):=\prod_{k=0}^{\infty}r_{k}^{n_{k}}\left(x\right)\text{ \quad}\left(n\in\mathbb{N}\right).

where
r_{k}(x)
are the generalized Rademacher functions defined by

r_{k}(x):=\exp(2\pi ix_{k}/m_{k}),\text{ \ \ }\left(k\in\mathbb{N}\right).

These systems include as a special case the Walsh system when
m_{k}=2
for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
.

The norms (or quasi-norms)
\left\|f\right\|_{p},

0<p<\infty,
of the Lebesgue spaces
L^{p}(G_{m})
are defined by

\left\|f\right\|_{p}^{p}:=\int_{G_{m}}\left|f\right|^{p}d\mu.

The Vilenkin system is orthonormal and complete in
L^{2}\left(G_{m}\right)\,
(see e.g.
[
1
]
and
[
31
]
).

If
f\in L^{1}\left(G_{m}\right)
, we can define the Fourier
coefficients, the partial sums of the Fourier series, the Fejér means, the Dirichlet and Fejér kernels
with respect to the Vilenkin system in
the usual manner:

\displaystyle\widehat{f}\left(k\right)

\displaystyle:

\displaystyle=\int_{G_{m}}f\overline{\psi}_{k}d\mu,\,\text{\quad}\left(k\in\mathbb{N}\right),

\displaystyle S_{n}f

\displaystyle:

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\widehat{f}\left(k\right)\psi_{k},\text{
\quad}\left(n\in\mathbb{N}_{+},\text{ }S_{0}f:=0\right),

\displaystyle\sigma_{n}f

\displaystyle:

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}S_{k}f,\text{ \quad\ \ }\left(n\in\mathbb{N}_{+}\right).

\displaystyle D_{n}

\displaystyle:

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\psi_{k},\text{ \quad\ \ }\left(n\in\mathbb{N}_{+}\right).

\displaystyle K_{n}

\displaystyle:

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}D_{k},\text{ \quad\ \ }\left(n\in\mathbb{N}_{+}\right).\

Recall that (see e.g.
[
1
]
and
[
29
]
),

(6)

\quad\hskip 0.0ptD_{M_{n}}\left(x\right)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\text{ }M_{n},\text{\thinspace\thinspace\thinspace if\thinspace\thinspace}x\in I_{n},\\
\text{ }0,\text{\thinspace\thinspace\thinspace\thinspace\thinspace\thinspace\thinspace\thinspace if
\thinspace\thinspace}x\notin I_{n},\end{array}\right.

(7)

\displaystyle D_{M_{n}-j}\left(x\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle D_{M_{n}}\left(x\right)-\overline{\psi}_{M_{n}-1}(-x)D_{j}(-x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle D_{M_{n}}\left(x\right)-\psi_{M_{n}-1}(x)\overline{D_{j}}(x),\,\,\ 0\leq j<M_{n}.

(8)

n\left|K_{n}\right|\leq 2R^{2}\sum_{l=0}^{|n|}M_{l}\left|K_{M_{l}}\right|,

and

(9)

\displaystyle\int_{G_{m}}K_{n}(x)d\mu(x)=1,\ \ \ \ \ \sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\int_{G_{m}}\left|K_{n}(x)\right|d\mu(x)\leq R^{5}.

where
R:=\sup_{k\in\mathbb{N}}m_{k}.
Moreover, if
n>t,

t,n\in\mathbb{N},
then

(10)

K_{M_{n}}\left(x\right)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{M_{t}}{1-r_{t}(x)},&x\in I_{t}\backslash I_{t+1},\quad x-x_{t}e_{t}\in I_{n},\\
\frac{M_{n}+1}{2},&x\in I_{n},\\
0,&\text{otherwise.}\end{array}\right.

The
n
-th Nörlund mean
t_{n}
and
T
mean
T_{n}
of
f\in L^{1}(G_{m})
are defined by

t_{n}f:=\frac{1}{Q_{n}}\overset{n}{\underset{k=1}{\sum}}q_{n-k}S_{k}f

and

T_{n}f:=\frac{1}{Q_{n}}\overset{n-1}{\underset{k=0}{\sum}}q_{k}S_{k}f,

where

Q_{n}:=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}q_{k}.

Here
\{q_{k},\ k\geq 0\}
is a sequence of nonnegative numbers, where
q_{0}>0
and

(11)

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}Q_{n}=\infty.

Then, a
T
mean generated by
\{q_{k},\ k\geq 0\}
is regular if and only if the condition (
11
) is fulfilled (see
[
29
]
).

It is evident that

T_{n}f\left(x\right)=\underset{G_{m}}{\int}f\left(t\right)F_{n}\left(x-y\right)d\mu\left(y\right)

where

(12)

F_{n}:=\frac{1}{Q_{n}}\overset{n-1}{\underset{k=0}{\sum}}q_{k}D_{k},

which are called the kernels of the
T
means.

By applying Abel transformation we get the following two useful identities:

(13)

\displaystyle Q_{n}:=\overset{n-1}{\underset{k=0}{\sum}}q_{k}\cdot 1=\overset{n-2}{\underset{k=0}{\sum}}(q_{k}-q_{k+1})k+q_{n-1}(n-1)

and

(14)

T_{n}f=\frac{1}{Q_{n}}\left(\overset{n-2}{\underset{k=0}{\sum}}(q_{k}-q_{k+1})k\sigma_{k}f+q_{n-1}(n-1)\sigma_{n-1}f\right).

3.
The Main Results

Our first main result reads:

Theorem 1
.

Let
f\in L^{p}(G_{m}),
where
1\leq p<\infty
and
{{T}_{n}}
are
T
means generated by a non-increasing sequence
\{q_{k},\ k\in\mathbb{N}\}.

Then, for any
n,N\in\mathbb{N},

M_{N}\leq n<M_{N+1},
we have the following inequality:

(15)

\displaystyle\ \ \ \|T_{n}f-f\|_{p}\leq\frac{6R^{6}}{Q_{n}}\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}M_{j}q_{{}_{M_{j}}}\omega_{p}\left(1/M_{j},f\right)+4R^{6}\omega_{p}\left(1/M_{N},f\right).

Next we state and prove a similar inequality for non-decreasing sequences but under some restrictions.

Theorem 2
.

Let
f\in L^{p}(G_{m}),
where
1\leq p<\infty
and
T_{n}
are regular
T
means generated by a non-decreasing sequence
\{q_{k},\ k\in\mathbb{N}\}
.
Then, for any
n,N\in\mathbb{N},

M_{N}\leq n<M_{N+1},
we have the following inequality:

(16)

\|T_{n}f-f\|_{p}\leq\frac{6R^{6}q_{n-1}}{Q_{n}}\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}M_{j}\omega_{p}\left(1/M_{j},f\right)+\frac{4R^{6}q_{n-1}M_{N}}{Q_{n}}\omega_{p}\left(1/M_{N},f\right).

If, in addition, the sequence
\{q_{k},\ k\in\mathbb{N}\}
satisfies the condition
(
2
), then the inequality

(17)

\displaystyle\|T_{n}f-f\|_{p}\leq C_{p}\sum_{j=0}^{N}\frac{M_{j}}{M_{N}}\omega_{p}\left(1/2^{j},f\right)

holds for some constant
C_{p}
only depending on
p.

Finally, we state and prove the third main result for the non-decreasing sequences, where we prove a more precise result then that in (
17
) and without the restriction (
2
) but only for subsequences.

Theorem 3
.

Let
f\in L^{p}(G_{m}),
where
1\leq p<\infty
and
{{T}_{k}}
are regular
T
means generated by a non-decreasing sequence
\{q_{k},\ k\in\mathbb{N}\}
.
Then, for any
n\in\mathbb{N},
the following inequality holds:

(18)

\displaystyle\|T_{M_{n}}f-f\|_{p}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle R^{2}\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\frac{M_{j}}{M_{n}}\omega_{p}\left(1/M_{j},f\right)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{2R^{4}}{q_{0}}\overset{n-1}{\underset{j=0}{\sum}}\frac{(n-j)(q_{{}_{M_{n}-M_{j}}})M_{j}}{M_{n}}\omega_{p}\left(1/M_{j},f\right)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\left(R^{2}+2\right)\omega_{p}\left(1/M_{n},f\right).

\displaystyle\|T_{n}f-f\|_{p}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{Q_{n}}\left(\overset{n-2}{\underset{j=0}{\sum}}(q_{j}-q_{j+1})j\|\sigma_{j}f-f\|_{p}+q_{n-1}(n-1)\|\sigma_{n-1}f-f\|_{p}\right)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle I+II.

Moreover,

(21)

\displaystyle I

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{Q_{n}}\overset{M_{N}-1}{\underset{j=1}{\sum}}\left(q_{j}-q_{j+1}\right)j\|\sigma_{j}f-f\|_{p}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{1}{Q_{n}}\overset{n-1}{\underset{j=M_{N}}{\sum}}\left(q_{j}-q_{j+1}\right)j\|\sigma_{j}f-f\|_{p}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle I_{1}+I_{2}.

Now we estimate all terms separately. By applying estimate (
1
) for
I_{1}
we obtain that

(22)

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{2R^{5}}{Q_{n}}\overset{N-1}{\underset{k=0}{\sum}}\overset{M_{k+1}-1}{\underset{j=M_{k}}{\sum}}\left(q_{j}-q_{j+1}\right)j\sum_{s=0}^{k}\frac{M_{s}}{M_{k}}\omega_{p}\left(1/M_{s},f\right)

\displaystyle\leq

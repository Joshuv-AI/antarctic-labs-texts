Emergence of Wulff-Crystals from atomistic systems on the FCC and HCP lattices
Marco Cicalese, Leonard Kreutz, Gian Paolo Leonardi

Abstract
. We consider a system of N N hard spheres sitting on the nodes of either the FCC \mathrm{FCC} or HCP \mathrm{HCP} lattice and interacting via a sticky-disk potential. As N N tends to infinity (continuum limit), assuming the interaction energy does not exceed that of the ground-state by more than N 2 / 3 N^{2/3} (surface scaling), we obtain the variational coarse grained model by Γ \Gamma -convergence. More precisely, we prove that the continuum limit energies are of perimeter type and we compute explicitly their Wulff shapes. Our analysis shows that crystallization on FCC \mathrm{FCC} is preferred to that on HCP \mathrm{HCP} for N N large enough. The method is based on integral representation and concentration-compactness results that we prove for general periodic lattices in any dimension.

A fundamental problem in crystallography is to understand why ensembles of large number of atoms arrange themselves into crystals at low temperatures. From the mathematical point of view, proving that equilibrium configurations of certain phenomenological interaction energies exhibit these structures is referred to as the crystallization problem
[

At zero temperature the internal energy of a configuration of atoms is expected to be solely governed by its geometric arrangement.
Within the framework of molecular mechanics

\displaystyle=\langle e_{2},\nu\rangle+\langle e_{1}-e_{2},\nu\rangle+\langle e_{3}+e_{2},\nu\rangle+|\langle e_{3}+e_{2}-e_{1},\nu\rangle|

\displaystyle=2\langle e_{1},\nu\rangle+\langle e_{3}+e_{2}-e_{1},\nu\rangle+|\langle e_{3}+e_{2}-e_{1},\nu\rangle|\geq 2\langle e_{1},\nu\rangle\,.

Hence, we see that (

) holds true. This together with (

Step 2
.(Calculation of
\varphi^{\circ}_{\mathrm{HCP}}
) In order to calculate
\varphi^{\circ}_{\mathrm{HCP}}
, we exploit the symmetries of
\varphi^{\circ}_{\mathrm{HCP}}
. Note that

(36)

\displaystyle\varphi_{\mathrm{HCP}}\left(-\begin{pmatrix}\nu_{1}\\
\nu_{2}\\
\nu_{3}\end{pmatrix}\right)=\varphi_{\mathrm{HCP}}\left(\begin{pmatrix}\nu_{1}\\
\nu_{2}\\
-\nu_{3}\end{pmatrix}\right)=\varphi_{\mathrm{HCP}}\left(\begin{pmatrix}-\nu_{1}\\
\nu_{2}\\
\nu_{3}\end{pmatrix}\right)=\varphi_{\mathrm{HCP}}\left(\begin{pmatrix}\nu_{1}\\
\nu_{2}\\
\nu_{3}\end{pmatrix}\right)\,.

Given

R=T_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\circ T_{2}^{\alpha_{2}}\circ T_{3}^{\alpha_{3}}
,
\alpha_{i}\in\{0,1\}
such that
(R\zeta)_{i}\geq 0
for all
i
. Thus,

(37)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\varphi_{\mathrm{HCP}}^{\circ}(\zeta)&=\max_{\varphi_{\mathrm{HCP}}(\nu)\leq 1}\langle\nu,\zeta\rangle=\max_{\varphi_{\mathrm{HCP}}(\nu)\leq 1}\langle R\nu,R\zeta\rangle\\
&=\max_{\varphi_{\mathrm{HCP}}(R^{-1}\nu)\leq 1}\langle\nu,R\zeta\rangle=\max_{\varphi_{\mathrm{HCP}}(\nu)\leq 1}\langle\nu,R\zeta\rangle=\varphi_{\mathrm{HCP}}^{\circ}(R\zeta)\,.\end{split}

It therefore suffices to calculate
\varphi_{\mathrm{HCP}}^{\circ}
for
\zeta\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
such that
\zeta_{i}\geq 0
. This together with (
36

\nu=(\nu_{1},\nu_{2},\nu_{3})

\varphi_{{\mathrm{HCP}}}(\nu)\leq 1
and

\displaystyle\langle\nu,\zeta\rangle=\max_{\varphi_{\mathrm{HCP}}(\nu)\leq 1}\langle\nu,\zeta\rangle\,,

then
\nu_{i}\geq 0
for all
i
. Additionally, a maximizer
\nu
can be chosen such that
\varphi_{\mathrm{HCP}}
is not differentiable at
\nu
. Therefore, there are the following cases to consider:

(a)

\langle e_{1}-e_{2},\nu\rangle=0
;

(b)

\langle e_{1}-e_{3},\nu\rangle=0,\langle e_{1}-e_{2},\nu\rangle\geq 0
;

(c)

\langle e_{2}-e_{3},\nu\rangle=0,\langle e_{3}-e_{1},\nu\rangle\geq 0
;

(d)

\langle e_{3},\nu\rangle=0
;

(e)

\langle e_{1},\nu\rangle=0
.

Here, we point out that the points on the boundary
\nu_{2}=0

\nu
such that
\nu_{2}=0

\varphi_{\mathrm{HCP}}(\nu)
would either be differentiable and thus
\nu
would not be a maximum point or
\nu
would satisfy one of the cases (a)-(e).

Maximum of case

. Hence,

26

\displaystyle\varphi_{\mathrm{HCP}}(\nu)=\sqrt{2}\left(2\nu_{1}+\frac{1}{3}\sqrt{6}\nu_{3}+\max\{\nu_{1},\frac{2}{3}\sqrt{6}\nu_{3}\}\right)\,.

(a.1)
t\geq\frac{2}{3}\sqrt{6}s
:
Since the maximum is attained for
\varphi_{\mathrm{HCP}}(\nu)=1
, we have
t=\frac{1}{3\sqrt{2}}-\frac{1}{9}\sqrt{6}s
. Now,
t\geq 0
together with
t\geq\frac{2}{3}\sqrt{6}s

\displaystyle\langle\nu,\zeta\rangle=t\left(\zeta_{1}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}\zeta_{2}\right)+s\zeta_{3}=\left(\frac{1}{3\sqrt{2}}-\frac{1}{9}\sqrt{6}s\right)\left(\zeta_{1}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}\zeta_{2}\right)+s\zeta_{3}\,,

(38)

\displaystyle\max_{\nu\text{ sat. (a.1)}}\langle\nu,\zeta\rangle=\max\left\{\frac{2}{7\sqrt{2}}\left(\zeta_{1}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}\zeta_{2}+\frac{3}{2\sqrt{6}}\zeta_{3}\right),\frac{1}{3\sqrt{2}}\left(\zeta_{1}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}\zeta_{2}\right)\right\}\,.

(a.2)
t\leq\frac{2}{3}\sqrt{6}s
:
Using
\varphi_{\mathrm{HCP}}(\nu)=1
, we obtain
t=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{2}}-\frac{\sqrt{6}}{2}s
. Now,
t\geq 0
together with
t\leq\frac{2}{3}\sqrt{6}s
implies
\frac{3}{14\sqrt{3}}\leq s\leq\frac{1}{2\sqrt{3}}
. Noting that

\displaystyle\langle\nu,\zeta\rangle=t\left(\zeta_{1}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}\zeta_{2}\right)+s\zeta_{3}=\left(\frac{1}{3\sqrt{2}}-\frac{1}{9}\sqrt{6}s\right)\left(\zeta_{1}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}\zeta_{2}\right)+s\zeta_{3}\,,

Maximum of case
(b)
.

\frac{1}{2}\nu_{1}+\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{3}\nu_{2}=\frac{2}{3}\sqrt{6}\nu_{3}

|\nabla\varphi_{\mathrm{HCP}}^{\circ}(\zeta)|=\frac{1}{14}(41/3)^{1/2}

\mathcal{L}^{3}
-a.e.. Due to the coarea formula, we have

(56)

\displaystyle\begin{split}|C_{e}\cap W_{\mathrm{HCP}}|&=14\left(\frac{3}{41}\right)^{1/2}\int_{C_{e}\cap W_{\mathrm{HCP}}}|\nabla\varphi_{\mathrm{HCP}}^{\circ}(\zeta)|\,\mathrm{d}\zeta\\
&=14\left(\frac{3}{41}\right)^{1/2}\int_{0}^{1}\mathcal{H}^{2}(C_{e}\cap\{\varphi_{\mathrm{HCP}}^{\circ}(\zeta)=s\})\,\mathrm{d}s\\
&=\frac{1}{3}14\left(\frac{3}{41}\right)^{1/2}\mathcal{H}^{2}(A_{e})=\frac{2^{3}\cdot 5\cdot 7}{3}\,.\end{split}

Using (

Proof of Lemma
4.1
.

We split the proof of the lemma into four steps. First, we prove (
57
). In the second step, we show that each face is a rhombus and calculate its area. Lastly, we show that each neighboring Voronoi cell
V_{\mathrm{FCC}}(b)
,
b\in\mathcal{N}_{\mathrm{FCC}}
shares one face with the Voronoi cell
V_{\mathrm{FCC}}(0)
.

Step 1
.(Proof of (
57
)) To check (
57
), since
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{FCC}}
is a Bravais-lattice (see (
8
)), it suffices to consider the case
x=0
. Let
\mathcal{V}_{{\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{FCC}}}}(0)
denote the Voronoi cell of
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{FCC}}
at
x=0
defined according to (
13
).

Step 1.1
.(
\mathcal{V}_{{\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{FCC}}}}(0)\subset V_{\mathrm{FCC}}
) Let
y\in\mathcal{V}_{{\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{FCC}}}}(0)
. By the very definition of Voronoi cell we have that for all
b\in{\mathcal{N}_{\mathrm{FCC}}}
it holds

|y|\leq|y-b|\,.

Noting that
|b|=1
for all
b\in\mathcal{N}_{\mathrm{FCC}}\subset\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{FCC}}
, we have

\displaystyle|y|\leq|y-b|\iff|y|^{2}\leq|y-b|^{2}=|y|^{2}-2\langle b,y\rangle+|b|^{2}\iff\langle b,y\rangle\leq\frac{1}{2}\,,

that is the inclusion
\mathcal{V}_{\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{FCC}}}(0)\subset V_{\mathrm{FCC}}
.

Step 1.2
.(
V_{\mathrm{FCC}}\subset\mathcal{V}_{\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{FCC}}}(0)
) We show that for
y\in V_{\mathrm{FCC}}
we have
|y|\leq|y-z|
for all
z\in\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{FCC}}
. This is equivalent to

(61)

\displaystyle y\in V_{\mathrm{FCC}}\implies\langle y,z\rangle\leq\tfrac{1}{2}|z|^{2}\quad\text{ for all }z\in\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{FCC}}\,.

We first observe that if
z\in\mathcal{N}_{\mathrm{FCC}}
, (
61
) is trivial since
|z|=1
. Next, we prove (
61
) for all
z\in\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{FCC}}\setminus\mathcal{N}_{\mathrm{FCC}}
. We distinguish two cases:

(a)

z=\lambda_{1}b_{j}+\lambda_{2}b_{k}

\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}
,
j,k\in\{1,2,3\},j\neq k\,
;

(b)

z=\lambda_{1}b_{1}+\lambda_{2}b_{2}+\lambda_{3}b_{3}

\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\lambda_{3}\in\mathbb{Z}\,
.

Proof in case
(a)
.
We only show the statement for
z=\lambda_{1}b_{1}+\lambda_{2}b_{2}
for
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}
, the cases with any other combination of two vectors being analogous. If
\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}\geq 0
, since
\langle b_{1},b_{2}\rangle\geq 0
, we have

\displaystyle\langle y,z\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle y,\lambda_{1}b_{1}+\lambda_{2}b_{2}\rangle\leq\frac{1}{2}|\lambda_{1}b_{1}|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}|\lambda_{2}b_{2}|^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}|\lambda_{1}b_{1}+\lambda_{2}b_{2}|^{2}-\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}\langle b_{1},b_{2}\rangle\leq\frac{1}{2}|\lambda_{1}b_{1}+\lambda_{2}b_{2}|^{2}=\frac{1}{2}|z|^{2}\,.

On the other hand, if
\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}\leq 0
and without loss of generality
|\lambda_{1}|\leq|\lambda_{2}|
, noting that
b_{1}-b_{2}\in\mathcal{N}_{\mathrm{FCC}}
, we have

\displaystyle\langle y,z\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle y,\lambda_{1}b_{1}+\lambda_{2}b_{2}\rangle=\langle y,(\lambda_{2}+\lambda_{1})b_{2}+\lambda_{1}(b_{1}-b_{2})\rangle\leq\frac{1}{2}|(\lambda_{2}+\lambda_{1})b_{2}|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}|\lambda_{1}(b_{1}-b_{2})|^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}|\lambda_{1}b_{1}+\lambda_{2}b_{2}|^{2}-\lambda_{1}(\lambda_{2}+\lambda_{1})\langle(b_{1}-b_{2}),b_{2}\rangle\leq\frac{1}{2}|\lambda_{1}b_{1}+\lambda_{2}b_{2}|^{2}\,.

Here, the last inequality follows, since
|b_{1}|=|b_{2}|
and therefore
\lambda_{1}(\lambda_{2}+\lambda_{1})\langle(b_{1}-b_{2}),b_{2}\rangle\geq 0
. This concludes case (a).

Proof in case
(b)
.
We now show that (
57
) holds true in the case of
b=\lambda_{1}b_{1}+\lambda_{2}b_{2}+\lambda_{3}b_{3}
with
\lambda_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}
. We restrict to the case
\lambda_{1}\geq 0,\lambda_{2}\geq 0
and
\lambda_{3}\leq 0
, since if all
\lambda_{i}
are of the same sign, (
57
) can be deduced from the fact that it holds true for
b\in\mathcal{N}_{\mathrm{FCC}}
and the fact that
\langle b_{j},b_{k}\rangle\geq 0
. Without loss of generality, we assume
|\lambda_{2}|\leq|\lambda_{3}|
. Hence, observing that
b_{2}-b_{3}\in\mathcal{N}_{\mathrm{FCC}}
, we have

\displaystyle\langle y,z\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle y,\lambda_{1}b_{1}+\lambda_{2}b_{2}+\lambda_{3}b_{3}\rangle=\langle y,\lambda_{1}b_{1}+(\lambda_{3}+\lambda_{2})b_{3}+\lambda_{2}(b_{2}-b_{3})\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}|\lambda_{1}b_{1}+(\lambda_{3}+\lambda_{2})b_{3}|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}|\lambda_{2}(b_{2}-b_{3})|^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}|\lambda_{1}b_{1}+\lambda_{2}b_{2}+\lambda_{3}b_{3}|^{2}-\langle(\lambda_{1}b_{1}+(\lambda_{3}+\lambda_{2})b_{3}),\lambda_{2}(b_{2}-b_{3})\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}|\lambda_{1}b_{1}+\lambda_{2}b_{2}+\lambda_{3}b_{3}|^{2}-(\lambda_{3}+\lambda_{2})\lambda_{2}\langle b_{2}-b_{3},b_{3}\rangle\leq\frac{1}{2}|\lambda_{1}b_{1}+\lambda_{2}b_{2}+\lambda_{3}b_{3}|^{2}\,.

Here, the last inequality follows from
|b_{2}|=|b_{3}|
and
\lambda_{3}+\lambda_{2}\leq 0
whereas the equality in the last line is due to
\langle b_{1},b_{2}\rangle=\langle b_{1},b_{3}\rangle=\langle b_{2},b_{3}\rangle
. This concludes case (b) and with that Step 1.2.

Step 2
.(The faces of the Voronoi cell) To show that each face of the Voronoi cell
V_{\mathrm{FCC}}
is a rhombus with area
\frac{1}{4}\sqrt{2}
we first exploit its symmetries. Let
i\in\{1,2,3\}
and let
T_{i}\colon\mathbb{R}^{3}\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
be the linear mapping that flips the
i
-th entry, i.e.

\displaystyle(T_{i}x)_{j}=\begin{cases}-x_{i}&\text{if }i=j\,,\\
x_{j}&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

We observe that

\displaystyle T_{i}\mathcal{N}_{\mathrm{FCC}}=\{T_{i}b\colon b\in\mathcal{N}_{\mathrm{FCC}}\}=\mathcal{N}_{\mathrm{FCC}}\,,\text{ for all }i\in\{1,2,3\}.

Moreover, given a permutation
\pi\in S_{3}
we have that

\displaystyle\pi\mathcal{N}_{\mathrm{FCC}}=\{\pi b\colon b\in\mathcal{N}_{\mathrm{FCC}}\}=\mathcal{N}_{\mathrm{FCC}}\,.

It therefore suffices to restrict only to the case in which the vector
b_{0}
agrees with the vector
b_{1}\in\mathcal{N}_{\mathrm{FCC}}
. We claim that this face has corners given by

(62)

\displaystyle c_{1}=\left(\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{2},0,0\right)\,,c_{2}=\left(0,\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{2},0\right)\,,c_{3}=\frac{1}{4}\left(\sqrt{2},\sqrt{2},\sqrt{2}\right)\,,c_{4}=\frac{1}{4}\left(\sqrt{2},\sqrt{2},-\sqrt{2}\right)\,.

Note that, if this were true then it is easy to see that
S_{b_{0}}
is a rhombus and
\mathcal{H}^{2}(S_{b_{0}})=\frac{1}{4}\sqrt{2}
. It remains to prove (
62

y
a corner of
S_{b_{0}}
. We can assume that
y_{1},y_{2}\geq 0
. Were this not the case, then there could be
b^{\prime}\in\mathcal{N}_{\mathrm{FCC}}
such that
\langle b^{\prime},y\rangle>\langle b,y\rangle
, thus contradicting the definition of
S_{b_{0}}
in (
58
). If
y_{1}=0
(or
y_{2}=0
), then
y_{2}=\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{2}
(resp.
y_{1}=\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{2}
) and since,
\langle b^{\prime},y\rangle\leq\frac{1}{2}
for all

and

y_{1}>0
and
y_{2}>0

Step 3
.(Neighbors share faces) We want to show that for each
b_{0}\in\mathcal{N}_{\mathrm{FCC}}
we have that the face
S_{b_{0}}
of
V_{\mathrm{FCC}}(0)
is shared with the Voronoi cell
V_{\mathrm{FCC}}(b_{0})
. By the symmetries shown in Step 2 it suffices to prove this statement only for

. Using (

of the Voronoi cell
V_{\mathrm{FCC}}(0)
coincide with the corners of the face

of the Voronoi cell
V_{\mathrm{FCC}}(b_{0})
.

Step 4
.(Volume of the Voronoi cell) In order to calculate the volume of the Voronoi cell we note that
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{FCC}}
is a Bravais-lattice with spanning vectors
b_{1},b_{2},b_{3}
. Since, the Voronoi cells of all the points are the same, it suffices to calculate the fraction of points per unit volume. This, then gives also the volume per point. Since, the Voronoi cells are space filling the volume per point is equal to the volume of each Voronoi cell. Due to (
15
) we have that

\displaystyle|T_{\mathrm{FCC}}|=\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{2}\,.

Furthermore, we have that

\displaystyle\bigcup_{x\in\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{FCC}}}(x+T_{{\mathrm{FCC}}})=\mathbb{R}^{3}\,,\text{ and }\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{FCC}}\cap T_{\mathrm{FCC}}=\{0\}\,.

Hence, each points of the lattice occupies a volume
|T_{\mathrm{FCC}}|=\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{2}
and the volume of the Voronoi cell must be the same. This concludes Step 3 and thus the proof of the lemma.

Proof of Lemma

We split the proof of the lemma into four steps. First, we prove (

6

Step 1

|b|=1

(

b\in\mathcal{N}_{\mathrm{HCP}}
. Next, we prove (

(b)

Proof in case

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{3}\langle y,v_{1}+v_{1}-e_{1}+v_{1}-e_{2}\rangle

Here, the last inequality follows by calculating the norms of

and
e_{3}
by using (
7

Proof of case

Proof of case

.

,
\lambda_{3}\geq 0

\displaystyle\langle y,z\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle y,v_{1}+\lambda_{1}e_{1}+\lambda_{2}e_{2}+\lambda_{3}e_{3}\rangle\leq\frac{1}{2}|v_{1}+\lambda_{1}e_{1}+\lambda_{2}e_{2}|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}|\lambda_{3}e_{3}|^{2}

.(Volume of the Voronoi cell)

\displaystyle|T_{\mathrm{HCP}}|=\sqrt{2}\,.

5.
General periodic lattices

This section deals with integral representation and concentrated-compactness properties of energies defined on general periodic lattices.

Definition 5.1
.

Let
\Sigma\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a countable set of points in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. We call
\Sigma
an
admissible
set of points if the following two conditions hold:

(L1)

There exists
R>0
such that
\inf_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\#(\Sigma\cap B_{R}(x))\geq 1
;

(L2)

There exists
r>0
such that
\mathrm{dist}(x,\Sigma\setminus\{x\})\geq r
for all
x\in\Sigma
.

Definition 5.2
.

We define the Voronoi cell of
x\in\Sigma
as

(70)

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}(x):=\{z\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\colon|x-z|\leq|y-z|\text{ for all }y\in\Sigma\}\,.

The set of nearest neighbors of
\Sigma
is defined by

\displaystyle\mathcal{NN}(\Sigma):=\{(x,y)\in\Sigma\times\Sigma\colon\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\mathcal{V}(x)\cap\mathcal{V}(y))>0\}\,,

We set
\mathcal{NN}(x)=\{y\in\Sigma\colon(x,y)\in\mathcal{NN}(\Sigma)\}
. Given
\varepsilon>0
we denote by
\varepsilon\Sigma:=\{\varepsilon x\colon x\in\Sigma\}
and for
x\in\varepsilon\Sigma
we set
\mathcal{V}_{\varepsilon}(x)=\varepsilon\mathcal{V}(\varepsilon^{-1}x)
the Voronoi cell of
x\in\varepsilon\Sigma
, and
\mathcal{NN}_{\varepsilon}(x)=\{y\in\varepsilon\Sigma\colon\varepsilon^{-1}(x,y)\in\mathcal{NN}(\Sigma)\}
the set of nearest neighbors of
x
in
\varepsilon\Sigma
.

We now define for
u\colon\Sigma\to\{0,1\}
the two energy functionals given by

(71)

\displaystyle F_{\varepsilon}(u,A):=\underset{\varepsilon x\in A}{\sum_{(x,y)\in\mathcal{NN}(\Sigma)}}\varepsilon^{n-1}c_{nn}(x-y)|u(\varepsilon x)-u(\varepsilon y)|\,

and

(72)

\displaystyle\hat{F}_{\varepsilon}(u,A):=\underset{\varepsilon x,\varepsilon y\in A}{\sum_{(x,y)\in\mathcal{NN}(\Sigma)}}\varepsilon^{n-1}c_{nn}(x-y)|u(\varepsilon x)-u(\varepsilon y)|\,,

where
c_{nn}\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to[0,+\infty]
satisfies

(73)

\displaystyle C^{-1}\leq c_{nn}(x)\leq C\,,\quad\text{for all }x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\,.

When
A=\mathbb{R}^{n}
we omit the dependence on it and write
F_{\varepsilon}(u)=F_{\varepsilon}(u,\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
\hat{F}_{\varepsilon}(u)=\hat{F}_{\varepsilon}(u,\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

Remark 5.3
.

(Difference between
F_{\varepsilon}
and
\hat{F}_{\varepsilon}
) We want to point out the difference between
F_{\varepsilon}
and
\hat{F}_{\varepsilon}
: In the formula defining
F_{\varepsilon}
the sum is taken over all
(x,y)\in\mathcal{NN}(\Sigma)
such that
\varepsilon x\in A
. Instead in the case of
\hat{F}_{\varepsilon}
the sum takes only those
(x,y)\in\mathcal{NN}(\Sigma)
such that both
\varepsilon x\in A
and
\varepsilon y\in A
. The functional
F_{\varepsilon}(u,\cdot)
is an additive set function on disjoint sets, i.e., given
A,B\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
A\cap B=\emptyset
, we have

\displaystyle F_{\varepsilon}(u,A\cup B)=F_{\varepsilon}(u,A)+F_{\varepsilon}(u,B)\,,

whereas
\hat{F}_{\varepsilon}(u,\cdot)
is only super-additive on disjoint sets. Our
\Gamma
-convergence result will be stated for the functional
F_{\varepsilon}
. The reason for us to introduce
\hat{F}_{\varepsilon}
is that our proof will use the integral representation result proven in
[
3
]
, see Theorem
5.7
. However, we will show later on that the
\Gamma
-convergence of
F_{\varepsilon}
is equivalent to that of
\hat{F}_{\varepsilon}
.

Given
X\subset\varepsilon\Sigma
we write with a slight abuse of notation

\displaystyle F_{\varepsilon}(X,A)=F_{\varepsilon}(\chi_{X},A)\,.

Note that, due to (
73
), we have

\displaystyle C^{-1}\sum_{x\in X}\varepsilon^{n-1}\#(\mathcal{NN}_{\varepsilon}(x)\setminus X)\leq F_{\varepsilon}(X,A)\leq C\sum_{x\in X}\varepsilon^{n-1}\#(\mathcal{NN}_{\varepsilon}(x)\setminus X)\,.

Hypothesis (
73
) corresponds to
[
3
, Hypothesis 1]
in the case that, according to the notation in
[
3
, Equation (5.23)]
,
c_{nn}^{\varepsilon}(x,y)=c_{nn}(x-y)
and
c_{lr}^{\varepsilon}(x,y)=0
. It is worth observing that in
[
3
]
a more general class of functionals was investigated, namely those for which also certain long-range interactions between points in
\Sigma
contribute to the energy, i.e.,
c_{lr}(x,y)\neq 0
. For the sake of exposition and simplicity, here we consider the case
c_{lr}=0
, that is the energy accounts only for the nearest neighbor interactions. However, with some more involved multi-scale constructions, all the statements below extend to the more general case where also long-range interactions are considered.

Definition 5.4
.

Given
X\subset\varepsilon\mathcal{L}
we define the rescaled empirical measures associated to
X
as

(74)

\displaystyle\mu_{\varepsilon}=\varepsilon^{n}\sum_{x\in X}\delta_{x}\,.

Furthermore, recalling (
70
), we define

(75)

\displaystyle V_{\varepsilon}(X):=\bigcup_{x\in X}{\mathcal{V}}_{\varepsilon}(x)\,.

Henceforth, we drop the dependence on
X
and simply write
V_{\varepsilon}
.
Given
A\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
open with
\partial A\in\mathrm{Lip}
, with slight abuse of notation we define
F_{\varepsilon}\colon\mathcal{M}_{+}(A)\to[0,+\infty]
(similarly
\hat{F}_{\varepsilon}\colon\mathcal{M}_{+}(A)\to[0,+\infty]
) by

\displaystyle F_{\varepsilon}(\mu,A)=\begin{cases}F_{\varepsilon}(X,A)&\mu\text{ is given by }\eqref{def:empiricalmeasures}\text{ for some }X\subset\varepsilon\mathcal{L}\,;\\
+\infty&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Additionally, we define
F_{\varepsilon}\colon L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(A)\to[0,+\infty]
(similarly
\hat{F}_{\varepsilon}\colon L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(A)\to[0,+\infty]
) by

\displaystyle F_{\varepsilon}(u,A)=\begin{cases}F_{\varepsilon}(X,A)&u=\chi_{V_{\varepsilon}}\text{ and }V_{\varepsilon}\text{ is given by }\eqref{def:piecewiseconstantinterpolant}\text{ for some }X\subset\varepsilon\mathcal{L}\,;\\
+\infty&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

It is necessary for us to introduce two different domains of definition for the extended functional

, since we want to make use of
[

. As it will turn out the two types of extension are equivalent, cf. Lemma

Let
(\Omega,\mathbb{P},\mathcal{F})
be a probability space. Hereafter we recall some definitions from
[

]
(Definition 5.1 and Definition 5.4):

Definition 5.5
.

We say that a family
(\tau_{z})_{z\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}
,
\tau_{z}\colon\Omega\to\Omega
, is an
additive group action
on
\Omega
if

\displaystyle\tau_{z_{1}+z_{2}}=\tau_{z_{2}}\circ\tau_{z_{1}}\quad\text{for all }z_{1},z_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}\,.

Such an additive group action is called
measure preserving
if

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(\tau_{z}B)=\mathbb{P}(B)\quad\text{for all }B\in\mathcal{F},z\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}\,.

If in addition, for all
B\in\mathcal{F}
we have

\displaystyle(\tau_{z}(B)=B\quad\text{for all }z\in\mathbb{Z}^{n})\implies\mathbb{P}(B)\in\{0,1\}\,,

then
(\tau_{z})_{z\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}
is called
ergodic
.

Definition 5.6
.

A random variable
\mathcal{L}\colon\Omega\to(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{\mathbb{Z}^{n}}
,
\omega\mapsto\mathcal{L}(\omega)=\{\mathcal{L}(\omega)(i)\}_{i\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}
is called a stochastic
lattice. We say that
\mathcal{L}
is admissible if
\mathcal{L}(\omega)
is admissible in the sense of Definition
5.1
and the constants

can be chosen independent of
\omega

\mathbb{P}
-almost surely. The stochastic lattice
\mathcal{L}
is said to be
stationary
if
there exists a measure preserving group action
(\tau_{z})_{z\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}
on
\Omega
such that, for
\mathbb{P}
-almost every
\omega\in\Omega
,

\mathcal{L}(\tau_{z}\omega)=\mathcal{L}(\omega)+z
.
If in addition
(\tau_{z})_{z\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}
is ergodic, then
\mathcal{L}
is called ergodic, too.

We now state a simplified version of
[
3
, Theorem 5.5]
which is enough for our purposes.

Theorem 5.7
.

(Stochastic homogenization of spin systems) Let
\mathcal{L}
be a stationary and ergodic stochastic lattice and let
\hat{F}_{\varepsilon}
be defined by (
72
). Let
A\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be open and bounded with
\partial A\in\mathrm{Lip}
. For
\mathbb{P}
-almost every
\omega
the functionals
F_{\varepsilon}(\omega)

\Gamma
-converge with respect to the strong
L^{1}(A)
-topology to the functional
F_{\mathrm{hom}}\colon L^{1}(A)\to[0,+\infty]
defined by

\displaystyle F_{\mathrm{hom}}(u,A):=\begin{cases}\int_{J(u)\cap A}\varphi_{\mathrm{hom}}(\nu_{u})\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}&\text{if }u\in BV(A;\{0,1\}),\\
+\infty&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

The function
\varphi_{\mathrm{hom}}\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to[0,+\infty]
is given by

\displaystyle\varphi_{\mathrm{hom}}(\nu)=\lim_{T\to+\infty}\frac{1}{T^{n-1}}\int_{\Omega}\inf\big\{

\displaystyle F(u,Q_{T}^{\nu})\colon u\colon\mathcal{L}(\omega)\to\{0,1\},

\displaystyle u(i)=u_{\nu}(i)\text{ for }i\in\mathcal{L}(\omega)\setminus Q^{\nu}_{T-l_{T}}\big\}\,\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}(\omega)\,,

where
l_{T}\to+\infty
and
l_{T}/T\to 0
as
T\to+\infty
.

Definition 5.8
.

Let
\mathcal{L}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be an admissible set of points. We say that
\mathcal{L}
is periodic if there exists a basis
\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{n}\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}+e_{k}=\mathcal{L}\text{ for all }k=1,\ldots,n\,.

We denote by

\displaystyle Q:=\left\{\sum_{k=1}^{n}\lambda_{k}e_{k}\colon 0\leq\lambda_{k}<1\right\}

the periodicity cell of
\mathcal{L}
. We set

(76)

\displaystyle\rho:=\frac{\#(\mathcal{L}\cap Q)}{|Q|}\,.

In the following we assume, up to a change of coordinates that
\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{n}\}
is the standard orthonormal basis of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

We collect the following general properties of periodic admissible set of points.

Lemma 5.9
.

(Properties of periodic admissible sets) Let
\mathcal{L}
be a periodic admissible set of points. The following holds true:

(i)

B_{r/2}(x)\subset\mathcal{V}(x)\subset B_{R}(x)
for all
x\in\mathcal{L}
;

(ii)

\mathcal{NN}(x)\subset B_{2R}(x)
for all
x\in\mathcal{L}
;

(iii)

There exists
C=C(n,r,R)\in(0,+\infty)
such that
\displaystyle\sup_{x\in\mathcal{L}}\#\mathcal{NN}(x)\leq C
. In particular,
\partial\mathcal{V}(x)
is made out of finitely many
(n-1)
-dimensional polyhedral faces;

(iv)

There exists
C=C_{\mathcal{L}}>0
such that for all
X\subset\varepsilon\mathcal{L}
and
A\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
there holds

\displaystyle F_{\varepsilon}(X,A)\leq C\,\mathrm{Per}(V_{\varepsilon},(A)_{R\varepsilon})\text{ and }\,\mathrm{Per}(V_{\varepsilon},A)\leq CF_{\varepsilon}(X,(A)_{R\varepsilon})\,.

Proof.

Apart from (iv) all of these facts are classical. We collect their proof here for completeness.

proof of (i), (ii):
Let
x\in\mathcal{L}
. The inclusion
B_{r/2}(x)\subset\mathcal{V}(x)
follows from (L2) since for all
y\in B_{r/2}(x)
and
z\in\mathcal{L}\setminus\{x\}

\displaystyle|z-y|\geq|x-z|-|x-y|\geq r-r/2=r/2\geq|x-y|\,.

As for the inclusion
\mathcal{V}(x)\subset B_{R}(x)
assume that there exists
y\in\mathcal{V}(x)\setminus B_{R}(x)
. We have for all
z\in\mathcal{L}\setminus\{x\}

\displaystyle|y-z|\geq|y-x|\geq R\,.

This implies that
B_{R}(y)\cap\mathcal{L}=\emptyset
contradicting (L1). Finally
\mathcal{NN}(x)\subset B_{2R}(x)
since for
y\in\mathcal{NN}(x)
we have that
\mathcal{V}(x)\cap\mathcal{V}(y)\neq\emptyset
which implies
B_{R}(x)\cap B_{R}(y)\neq\emptyset
.

proof of (iii):
Due to (i) and (ii) we have that
B_{r/2}(y)\cap B_{r/2}(z)=\emptyset

y,z\in\mathcal{NN}(x)
,
y\neq z
and
B_{r/2}(y)\subset B_{2R+r}(x)
for all
y\in\mathcal{NN}(x)
. Therefore

\displaystyle\omega_{n}\left(\frac{r}{2}\right)^{n}\#\mathcal{NN}(x)=\bigcup_{y\in\mathcal{NN}(x)}|B_{r/2}(y)|\leq|B_{2R+r}(x)|\leq\omega_{n}(2R+r)^{n}

and thus the claim follows with
C=(2+4R/r)^{n}
.

proof of (iv):
The desired inequalities follow from the following observation: Given
x\in X
, we have that

(77)

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\mathcal{V}_{\varepsilon}(x)\cap\partial V_{\varepsilon})>0\iff\mathcal{NN}_{\varepsilon}(x)\setminus X\neq\emptyset\,.

Additionally, we note that, for
x\in X
such that
\mathcal{NN}_{\varepsilon}(x)\setminus X\neq\emptyset
, there exists
C>0
such that

(78)

\displaystyle C^{-1}\varepsilon^{n-1}\leq\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\mathcal{V}_{\varepsilon}(x)\cap\partial V_{\varepsilon})\leq C\varepsilon^{n-1}\,.

Now, summing over all
x\in X\cap A
and noting that each Voronoi cell intersects only a finite number of other Voronoi cells, using (
77
),(
78
),(i), and (iii), we obtain

\displaystyle F_{\varepsilon}(X,A)=\sum_{x\in X\cap A}\varepsilon^{n-1}\#(\mathcal{NN}_{\varepsilon}(x)\setminus X)

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{x\in X\cap A}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\mathcal{V}_{\varepsilon}(x)\cap\partial V_{\varepsilon})

\displaystyle\leq C\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\partial V_{\varepsilon}\cap(A)_{R\varepsilon})=C\,\mathrm{Per}(V_{\varepsilon},(A)_{R\varepsilon})\,.

This yields the first inequality in (iv). On the other hand, owing to (i) we have
\partial V_{\varepsilon}\cap A\subset\bigcup_{x\in X\cap(A)_{R\varepsilon}}(\mathcal{V}_{\varepsilon}(x)\cap\partial V_{\varepsilon})
, and thus, by (
77
) and (
78
),

\displaystyle\mathrm{Per}(V_{\varepsilon},A)=\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\partial V_{\varepsilon}\cap A)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{x\in X\cap(A)_{R\varepsilon}}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\mathcal{V}_{\varepsilon}(x)\cap\partial V_{\varepsilon})

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon^{n-1}\sum_{x\in X\cap(A)_{R\varepsilon}}\#(\mathcal{NN}_{\varepsilon}(x)\setminus X)=CF_{\varepsilon}(X,(A)_{R\varepsilon})\,.

This shows the second inequality in (iv) and concludes the proof.

Lemma 5.11
.

(Equivalence of convergences) Let
\mathcal{L}
be an admissible periodic set of points and
F_{\varepsilon}
defined in (
71
) with
\mathcal{L}
in place of
\Sigma
. Let
A\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be open and let
V\subset A
be a set of finite perimeter and let
\{X_{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon}\subset\varepsilon\mathcal{L}
for each
\varepsilon>0
be such that

(79)

\displaystyle\sup_{\varepsilon>0}F_{\varepsilon}(X_{\varepsilon},A)<+\infty\,.

Then, setting
\mu_{\varepsilon}
and
V_{\varepsilon}
as in (
74
) and (
75
), the following are equivalent:

(i)

\mu_{\varepsilon}\overset{*}{\rightharpoonup}\mu
with respect to the weak star topology of measures and
\mu=\rho\mathcal{L}^{n}\llcorner V
.

(ii)

\chi_{V_{\varepsilon}}\to\chi_{V}
with respect to the strong
L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(A)
-topology.

Proof.

We proceed in two steps. First, we construct a sequence of auxiliary measure
\nu_{\varepsilon}
and show that its weak*-convergence is equivalent to the weak*-convergence of the sequence of measures
\mu_{\varepsilon}
. Then, for this sequence of measures we show that its weak*-convergence is equivalent to (ii).

Step 1
.(Construction of the auxiliary measure)
Let
\{X_{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon}
be as in the assumptions of the lemma and let
v\in C_{c}(A)
such that
\mathrm{supp}\,v\subset\subset A
. We assume that
\varepsilon>0
is small enough such that for all
k\in\varepsilon\mathbb{Z}^{n}
there holds

(80)

\displaystyle Q_{(3+R)\varepsilon}(k)\cap\mathrm{supp}\,v\neq\emptyset\implies Q_{(3+R)\varepsilon}(k)\subset\subset A\,.

Fix
R_{0}>0
such that
\mathrm{supp}\,v\subset B_{R_{0}}
. Since
v
is uniformly continuous, it admits a modulus of continuity
\omega=\omega_{v}\colon[0,+\infty)\to[0,+\infty)
, i.e., an increasing function such that
\omega(0)=0
and

(81)

\displaystyle|v(x)-v(y)|\leq\omega(|x-y|)\text{ for all }x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\,.

We set

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\mathcal{I}_{\varepsilon}^{\mathrm{full}}:=\{k\in\varepsilon\mathbb{Z}^{d}\colon Q_{(3+R)\varepsilon}(k)\cap X=Q_{(3+R)\varepsilon}(k)\cap\varepsilon\mathcal{L}\}\,,\\
&\mathcal{I}_{\varepsilon}^{\mathrm{empty}}:=\{k\in\varepsilon\mathbb{Z}^{d}\colon Q_{(3+R)\varepsilon}(k)\cap X=\emptyset\}\,,\end{split}

and
\mathcal{I}_{\varepsilon}^{\mathrm{bad}}:=\{k\in\varepsilon\mathbb{Z}^{n}\colon Q_{(3+R)\varepsilon}(k)\cap\mathrm{supp}\,v\neq\emptyset\}\setminus(\mathcal{I}_{\varepsilon}^{\mathrm{full}}\cup\mathcal{I}_{\varepsilon}^{\mathrm{empty}})
. We now set

\displaystyle\nu_{\varepsilon}:=\sum_{k\in\mathcal{I}_{\varepsilon}^{\mathrm{full}}}\varepsilon^{n}\#(\mathcal{L}\cap Q)\delta_{k}\,.

Our goal is to show that

(82)

\displaystyle\mu_{\varepsilon}\overset{*}{\rightharpoonup}\mu\quad\text{in the sense of measures}\quad\iff\quad\nu_{\varepsilon}\overset{*}{\rightharpoonup}\mu\quad\text{in the sense of measures.}

First of all we claim that

(83)

\displaystyle\#\mathcal{I}_{\varepsilon}^{\mathrm{bad}}\leq C\varepsilon^{1-n}\,.

In fact, due to its definition and (
80
), we have that for all
k\in\mathcal{I}_{\varepsilon}^{\mathrm{bad}}
there exists
x\in X\cap Q_{(3+R)\varepsilon}(k)\subset A
such that
\#(\mathcal{NN}(x)\setminus X)\neq\emptyset
. Therefore, since for
k\in\varepsilon\mathbb{Z}^{n}
fixed
Q_{(3+R)\varepsilon}(k)\cap Q_{(3+R)\varepsilon}(j)\neq\emptyset
for only finitely many
j
independent of
\varepsilon
, we have

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{n-1}\#\mathcal{I}_{\varepsilon}^{\mathrm{bad}}\leq\sum_{k\in\mathcal{I}_{\varepsilon}^{\mathrm{bad}}}\sum_{x\in Q_{(3+R)\varepsilon}(k)\cap X}\varepsilon^{n-1}\#(\mathcal{NN}(x)\setminus X)\leq CF_{\varepsilon}(X_{\varepsilon},A)\,.

Using (
79
) yields (
83
).
Let now
k\in\mathcal{I}_{\varepsilon}^{\mathrm{full}}
. Then

\displaystyle\int_{Q_{\varepsilon}(k)}v(x)\,\mathrm{d}\nu_{\varepsilon}(x)=v(k)\varepsilon^{n}\#(\mathcal{L}\cap Q)

\displaystyle=\sum_{x\in Q_{\varepsilon}(k)\cap\varepsilon\mathcal{L}}\varepsilon^{n}v(k)

\displaystyle=\sum_{x\in Q_{\varepsilon}(k)\cap X}\varepsilon^{n}v(x)+\sum_{x\in Q_{\varepsilon}(k)\cap\varepsilon\mathcal{L}}\varepsilon^{n}(v(k)-v(x))

\displaystyle=\int_{Q_{\varepsilon}(k)}v(x)\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{\varepsilon}(x)+\sum_{x\in Q_{\varepsilon}(k)\cap\varepsilon\mathcal{L}}\varepsilon^{n}(v(k)-v(x))\,.

Thus,

\displaystyle\left|\int_{Q_{\varepsilon}(k)}v(x)\,\mathrm{d}(\nu_{\varepsilon}-\mu_{\varepsilon})(x)\right|\leq\sum_{x\in Q_{\varepsilon}(k)\cap\varepsilon\mathcal{L}}\varepsilon^{n}|v(k)-v(x)|\leq\omega(\varepsilon\sqrt{n})|Q_{\varepsilon}(k)|

and, recalling that
\mathrm{supp}\,v\subset B_{R_{0}}
and (
81
), we have for
\varepsilon>0
small enough

(84)

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\mathcal{I}_{\varepsilon}^{\mathrm{full}}}\left|\int_{Q_{\varepsilon}(k)}v(x)\,\mathrm{d}(\nu_{\varepsilon}-\mu_{\varepsilon})(x)\right|\leq\omega(\varepsilon\sqrt{n})|B_{2R_{0}}|\,.

Noting that both
|\mu_{\varepsilon}|(Q_{\varepsilon}(k))
and
|\nu_{\varepsilon}|(Q_{\varepsilon}(k))
are bounded above by

\varepsilon^{n}\#(\mathcal{L}\cap Q)\leq C\varepsilon^{n}
for all
k\in\varepsilon\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, using (
83
), we observe

(85)

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\mathcal{I}_{\varepsilon}^{\mathrm{bad}}}\left|\int_{Q_{\varepsilon}(k)}v(x)\,\mathrm{d}(\nu_{\varepsilon}-\mu_{\varepsilon})(x)\right|\leq 2\|v\|_{\infty}\#\mathcal{I}_{\varepsilon}^{\mathrm{bad}}\varepsilon^{n}\#(\mathcal{L}\cap Q)\leq C\varepsilon\|v\|_{\infty}\,.

Therefore, noting that
\mu_{\varepsilon}\lfloor_{Q_{\varepsilon}(k)}=\nu_{\varepsilon}\lfloor_{Q_{\varepsilon}(k)}
for
k\in\mathcal{I}_{\varepsilon}^{\mathrm{empty}}
, using (
84
) and (
85
), we obtain

\displaystyle\begin{split}\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}v(x)\,\mathrm{d}(\nu_{\varepsilon}-\mu_{\varepsilon})(x)\right|&\leq\omega(\varepsilon\sqrt{n})|B_{2R}|+C\varepsilon\|v\|_{\infty}\,.\end{split}

This shows (
82
).

Step 2
.(Equivalence of convergence) We now prove that

\displaystyle\chi_{V_{\varepsilon}}\to\chi_{V}\quad\text{in }L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(A)\quad\iff\quad\nu_{\varepsilon}\overset{*}{\rightharpoonup}\mu\quad\text{in the sense of measures.}

First of all, recalling
\rho
defined in (
76
), we note

(86)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}v\,\mathrm{d}\nu_{\varepsilon}&=\sum_{k\in\mathcal{I}_{\varepsilon}^{\mathrm{full}}}\varepsilon^{n}\#(\mathcal{L}\cap Q)v(k)=\frac{\#(\mathcal{L}\cap Q)}{|Q|}\sum_{k\in\mathcal{I}_{\varepsilon}^{\mathrm{full}}}|Q_{\varepsilon}(k)|v(k)\\
&=\rho\sum_{k\in\mathcal{I}_{\varepsilon}^{\mathrm{full}}}\int_{Q_{\varepsilon}(k)}v(y)\,\mathrm{d}y+\rho\sum_{k\in\mathcal{I}_{\varepsilon}^{\mathrm{full}}}\int_{Q_{\varepsilon}(k)}(v(k)-v(y))\,\mathrm{d}y\,.\end{split}

Now, due to (
81
), we have for
\varepsilon>0
small enough

(87)

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{k\in\mathcal{I}_{\varepsilon}^{\mathrm{full}}}\int_{Q_{\varepsilon}(k)}(v(k)-v(y))\,\mathrm{d}y\right|\leq\sum_{k\in\mathcal{I}_{\varepsilon}^{\mathrm{full}}}\int_{Q_{\varepsilon}(k)}|v(k)-v(y)|\,\mathrm{d}y\leq C\omega(\varepsilon\sqrt{n})|B_{2R_{0}}|\,.

Note that, by Lemma
5.9
(i), we have

(88)

\displaystyle\rho\sum_{k\in\mathcal{I}_{\varepsilon}^{\mathrm{full}}}\int_{Q_{\varepsilon}(k)}v(y)\,\mathrm{d}y=\rho\sum_{k\in\mathcal{I}_{\varepsilon}^{\mathrm{full}}}\int_{Q_{\varepsilon}(k)\cap V_{\varepsilon}}v(y)\,\mathrm{d}y

and also
V_{\varepsilon}\cap Q_{\varepsilon}(k)=\emptyset
for
k\in\mathcal{I}_{\varepsilon}^{\mathrm{empty}}
. Note that by (
83
) we have

(89)

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\mathcal{I}_{\varepsilon}^{\mathrm{bad}}}\int_{Q_{\varepsilon}(k)}\left|v(y)\right|\,\mathrm{d}y\leq\varepsilon^{n}\#\mathcal{I}_{\varepsilon}^{\mathrm{bad}}\|v\|_{\infty}\leq C\varepsilon\|v\|_{\infty}\,.

Due to (
86
)–(
89
), we obtain that

\displaystyle\rho\chi_{V_{\varepsilon}}\overset{*}{\rightharpoonup}\mu\quad\text{in the sense of measures}\quad\iff\quad\nu_{\varepsilon}\overset{*}{\rightharpoonup}\mu\quad\text{in the sense of measures.}

Now clearly (ii) implies (i), since the
L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(A)
convergence of the characteristic functions implies their weak* convergence as measures. As for the implication (i) to (ii) we proceed as follows. Let
V\subset A
be a set of finite perimeter and assume that
\mu_{\varepsilon}\overset{*}{\rightharpoonup}\mu
and
\mu=\rho\mathcal{L}^{n}\llcorner V
. By Step 1 this is equivalent to
\nu_{\varepsilon}\overset{*}{\rightharpoonup}\mu
, hence to
\rho\chi_{V_{\varepsilon}}\overset{*}{\rightharpoonup}\rho\chi_{V}
in the sense of measures (which is to say that
\chi_{V_{\varepsilon}}\overset{*}{\rightharpoonup}\chi_{V}
) Take now an arbitrary subsequence (not relabeled) of
\{X_{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon}
. We show that there exists a further subsequence (again not relabeled) such that
\chi_{V_{\varepsilon}}\to\chi_{V}
with respect to the strong
L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(A)
-topology. Since
L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(A)
-topology satisfies the Urysohn property this implies the claim. By the compactness statement in Proposition
5.10
we have that there exists a set of finite perimeter
V^{\prime}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
and a further subsequence
\{X_{\varepsilon_{k}}\}_{k}\subset\{X_{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon}
such that
\chi_{V_{\varepsilon}}\to\chi_{V^{\prime}}
with respect to the strong
L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(A)
-topology. Since this implies their weak* convergence as measures and we already know that the whole sequence converges to
\chi_{V}
we deduce
V=V^{\prime}
which implies the claim and concludes the proof of the lemma.

∎

Theorem 5.14
.

(
\Gamma
-convergence for periodic admissible lattices) Let
\mathcal{L}
be an admissible periodic set of points and let
F_{\varepsilon}
be defined by (
71
) with
\mathcal{L}
in place of
\Sigma
. Let
A\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be bounded open set with
\partial A\in\mathrm{Lip}
or

. The functionals
F_{\varepsilon}

\Gamma

\displaystyle F_{\mathrm{hom}}(\mu,A):=\begin{cases}\int_{\partial^{*}V\cap A}\varphi_{\mathrm{hom}}(\nu)\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}&\text{if }\mu=\rho\mathcal{L}^{n}\llcorner V\,;\\

The function
\varphi_{\mathrm{hom}}\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to[0,+\infty]
is given by

\displaystyle\varphi_{\mathrm{hom}}(\nu)=\lim_{T\to+\infty}\frac{1}{T^{n-1}}\inf\left\{F(X,Q_{T}^{\nu})\colon X\subset\mathcal{L},\chi_{X}(i)=u_{\nu}(i)\text{ for }i\in\mathcal{L}\setminus Q^{\nu}_{T-l_{T}}\right\}\,,

where
l_{T}\to+\infty
and
l_{T}/T\to 0
as

.

Proof.

Step 1
.(Probabilistic setup)
We exploit the integral representation result (Theorem
5.7
) to obtain the specific form of the
\Gamma
-limit. We fix
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})=(\{0\},\{\{0\},\emptyset\},\delta_{0})
to be a probability space and a trivial additive and ergodic group action (see Definition 5.1 in
[
3
]
)
\tau_{z}\colon\{0\}\to\{0\},z\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}
given by
\tau_{z}(0)=0
. With respect to this group action
\mathcal{L}(0)=\mathcal{L}
is an admissible stationary and ergodic stochastic lattice according to Definition
5.6
. In fact, since
\mathcal{L}
is periodic according to Definition
5.8
, for all
z=(z_{1},z_{2},z_{3})\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}
we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(0)=\mathcal{L}\,,\quad\mathcal{L}(\tau_{z}(0))=\mathcal{L}(0)=\mathcal{L}(0)+\sum_{k=1}^{n}z_{k}e_{k}\,.

Therefore, all conditions of Theorem
5.7
are satisfied.
This shows that for
\hat{F}_{\varepsilon}\colon L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(A)\to[0,+\infty]
we have

\displaystyle\Gamma\text{-}\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\hat{F}_{\varepsilon}(\chi_{V},A)=F_{\mathrm{hom}}(\chi_{V},A)

for all
A\subset\mathbb{R}^{n},\partial A\in\mathrm{Lip},\chi_{V}\in BV(A,\{0,1\})
. Note that, by Corollary
5.12
, this is equivalent to saying that for
\hat{F}_{\varepsilon}\colon\mathcal{M}_{+}(A)\to[0,+\infty]
we have

\displaystyle\Gamma\text{-}\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\hat{F}_{\varepsilon}(\mu,A)=F_{\mathrm{hom}}(\mu,A)\,.

This concludes Step 1.

Step 2
.(
\Gamma
-convergence of
F_{\varepsilon}
) We use the
\Gamma
-convergence of
\hat{F}_{\varepsilon}
obtained in Step 1 in order to prove the
\Gamma
-convergence of
F_{\varepsilon}
. Let us first prove the result for
A\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
open and bounded with
\partial A\in\mathrm{Lip}
. We note that

\displaystyle\hat{F}_{\varepsilon}(X,A)\leq F_{\varepsilon}(X,A)\,

and therefore for all
\mu_{\varepsilon}\overset{*}{\rightharpoonup}\mu
, we have

\displaystyle\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}F_{\varepsilon}(\mu_{\varepsilon},A)\geq\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\hat{F}_{\varepsilon}(\mu_{\varepsilon},A)\geq F_{\mathrm{hom}}(\mu,A)\,.

Therefore

\Gamma\text{-}\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}F_{\varepsilon}(\mu,A)\geq F_{\mathrm{hom}}(\mu,A)\,.

Next, we show the Limsup-inequality. Let
V\subset A
be such that

(90)

\displaystyle F_{\mathrm{hom}}(\rho\chi_{V},A)<+\infty\,

since otherwise there is nothing to prove. Using Lemma
5.9
(iv), we obtain that
\mathrm{Per}(V,A)<+\infty
, i.e.,
V
is a set of finite perimeter. In particular, by
[
25
, Theorem 1.3]
(noting that
\partial A\in\mathrm{Lip}
implies that
A
is an extension domain), there exists an extension
V_{\mathrm{e}}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that

(i)

up to a set of zero
\mathcal{L}^{n}
-measure;

(ii)

\mathrm{Per}(V_{\mathrm{e}},\mathbb{R}^{n})\leq C\mathrm{Per}(V,A)\,;

(iii)

\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(\partial^{*}V_{\mathrm{e}}\cap\partial A)=0\,.

Now (ii) together with (
90
) implies, again by Lemma
5.9
(iv), that

\displaystyle F_{\mathrm{hom}}(\rho\chi_{V_{\mathrm{e}}},\mathbb{R}^{n})=\int_{\partial^{*}V_{\mathrm{e}}}\varphi_{\mathrm{hom}}(\nu)\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}<+\infty\,.

Fix
\delta>0
and let
\mu_{\varepsilon}^{\delta}\to\rho\chi_{V_{\mathrm{e}}}
weakly* in
\mathcal{M}_{+}((A)_{\delta})

\displaystyle\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}\hat{F}_{\varepsilon}(\mu_{\varepsilon}^{\delta},(A)_{\delta})\leq F_{\mathrm{hom}}(\rho\chi_{V_{\mathrm{e}}},(A)_{\delta})\,.

\displaystyle F_{\varepsilon}(X,A)\leq\hat{F}_{\varepsilon}(X,(A)_{\delta})\,.

Therefore, recalling Definition

, we obtain,

\displaystyle\Gamma\text{-}\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}F_{\varepsilon}(\rho\chi_{V},A)\leq\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}F_{\varepsilon}(\mu_{\varepsilon}^{\delta},A)\leq\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}\hat{F}_{\varepsilon}(\mu_{\varepsilon}^{\delta},(A)_{\delta})\leq F_{\mathrm{hom}}(\rho\chi_{V_{\mathrm{e}}},(A)_{\delta})\,.

\displaystyle\Gamma\text{-}\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}F_{\varepsilon}(\rho\chi_{V},A)\leq F_{\mathrm{hom}}(\rho\chi_{V_{\mathrm{e}}},\overline{A})=F_{\mathrm{hom}}(\rho\chi_{V_{\mathrm{e}}},A)=F_{\mathrm{hom}}(\rho\chi_{V},A)\,,

where the last equality follows by properties (i) and (iii) of
V_{\mathrm{e}}
. This shows the desired integral representation for all

such that
\partial A\in\mathrm{Lip}
.

Step 3
.(Integral representation on unbounded sets)
It remains to prove the integral representation of the
\Gamma
-limit for
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. The Liminf-inequality follows by monotonicity since for all
R>0
and

we have

\displaystyle F_{\varepsilon}(X,B_{R})\leq F_{\varepsilon}(X)

and therefore, given
\mu_{\varepsilon}\to\rho\chi_{V}
weakly* in
\mathcal{M}_{+}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, we have

\displaystyle\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}F_{\varepsilon}(\mu_{\varepsilon})\geq\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}F_{\varepsilon}(\mu_{\varepsilon},B_{R})\geq F_{\mathrm{hom}}(\rho\chi_{V},B_{R})\,.

The claim follows by taking the supremum over
R>0
. We now turn our attention to the Limsup inequality. We can assume without loss of generality that
V\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a set of finite perimeter and

(91)

\displaystyle C\mathrm{Per}(V,\mathbb{R}^{n})\leq\int_{\partial^{*}V}\varphi(\nu)\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}=F_{\mathrm{hom}}(\rho\chi_{V},\mathbb{R}^{n})<+\infty

since otherwise there is nothing to prove. By the isoperimetric inequality there exists
C>0
such that

\displaystyle\min\{|V|,|\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus V|\}^{\frac{n-1}{n}}\leq C\mathrm{Per}(V,\mathbb{R}^{n})\,.

Without loss of generality we assume that
|V|<+\infty
. By the Fleming-Rishel formula we can find
\{R_{k}\}_{k}
such that
R_{k}\to+\infty
and

(92)

\displaystyle{\rm(i)}\quad\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(V\cap\partial B_{R_{k}})\leq\frac{1}{k}\quad\text{and}\quad{\rm(ii)}\quad|V\cap B_{R_{k}}^{c}|\leq\frac{1}{k}\,.

We define

Lemma 5.15
.

(Nucleation Lemma) For every
(p_{0},v_{0})\in(0,+\infty)^{2}
there exists
m=m(p_{0},v_{0})>0
such that if
V\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
\mathrm{Per}(V)\leq p_{0}
,
|V|\geq v_{0}
, then there exists
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that

\displaystyle|V\cap B_{1}(x)|\geq m\,.

Proof.

Assume that
|V\cap B_{1}(x)|<\delta\leq\omega_{n}/2
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Then, noting that
\chi_{B_{1}(x)}(y)=\chi_{B_{1}(y)}(x)
for all
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we obtain

(95)

\displaystyle\omega_{n}|V|=\int_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\chi_{V}(x)\chi_{B_{1}(x)}(y)\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x=\int_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\chi_{V\cap B_{1}(y)}(x)\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x\,.

Due to the assumption we have
|V\cap B_{1}(x)|<\delta
and therefore due to the relative isoperimetric inequality and Fubini’s Theorem, we obtain

(96)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\int_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\chi_{V\cap B_{1}(y)}(x)\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x=\int_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}|V\cap B_{1}(y)|\,\mathrm{d}y&\leq\delta^{1/n}\int_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}|V\cap B_{1}(x)|^{1-\frac{1}{n}}\,\mathrm{d}y\\
&\leq\frac{\delta^{1/n}}{C_{B}}\int_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\partial^{*}V\cap B_{1}(y))\,\mathrm{d}y\,,\end{split}

where
C_{B}>0
denotes the relative isoperimetric constant of
B_{1}
. Now, again due to Fubini’s Theorem, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\partial^{*}V\cap B_{1}(y))\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=\int_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\chi_{B_{1}(y)}(x)\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\lfloor_{\partial^{*}V}(x)\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=\int_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\chi_{B_{1}(x)}(y)\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\lfloor_{\partial^{*}V}(x)=\omega_{n}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\partial^{*}V)

This together with (
95
) and (
96
) leads to

\displaystyle|V|\leq\frac{\delta^{1/n}}{C_{B}}\mathrm{Per}(V)\,.

Then, we conclude that
\delta\geq\left(C_{B}|V|/\mathrm{Per}(V)\right)^{n}
and thus for any
m<\min\{\omega_{n}/2,\left(C_{B}v/p_{0}\right)^{n}\}
the thesis holds.

A_{r_{\varepsilon},r_{\varepsilon}+s_{\varepsilon}}
where
s_{\varepsilon}=k_{\varepsilon}\varepsilon
(here
k_{\varepsilon}

k_{\varepsilon}\to+\infty
and
k_{\varepsilon}\varepsilon\to 0

\hat{X}_{\varepsilon}\cap B_{r_{\varepsilon}}
and
\hat{X}_{\varepsilon}\setminus B_{r_{\varepsilon}+s_{\varepsilon}}
(resp.
X_{\varepsilon}\cap B_{r_{\varepsilon}}
and
X_{\varepsilon}\setminus B_{r_{\varepsilon}+s_{\varepsilon}}

\varepsilon\to 0

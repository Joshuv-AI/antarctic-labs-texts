Cosmological dynamics and observational constraints on a viable f ⁡ ( Q ) f(Q) non-metric gravity model
A. Oliveros, Mario A. Acero

Abstract
Inspired by an exponential f ⁡ ( R ) f(R) gravity model studied in the literature, in this work we introduce a new and viable f ⁡ ( Q ) f(Q) gravity model, which can be represented as a perturbation of Λ \Lambda CDM. Typically, within the realm of f ⁡ ( Q ) f(Q) gravity, the customary approach to investigate cosmological evolution involves employing a parametrization of the Hubble expansion rate in terms of the redshift, H ⁡ ( z ) H(z) , among other strategies. In this work we have implemented a different strategy, deriving an analytical approximation for H ⁡ ( z ) H(z) , from which we deduce approximated analytical expressions for the parameters w DE w_{\rm{DE}} , w eff w_{\rm{eff}} , and Ω DE \Omega_{\rm{DE}} , as well as the deceleration parameter q q . In order to verify the viability of this approximate analytical solution, we examined the behavior of the these parameters in the late-time regime , in terms of the free parameter of the model, b b . We find that for b > 0 b>0 , w DE w_{\rm{DE}} shows a quintessence-like behavior, while for b < 0 b<0 , it shows a phantom-like behavior. However, regardless of the sign of b b , w eff w_{\rm{eff}} exhibits a quintessence-like behavior. Furthermore, it has been deduced that as the magnitude of the parameter b b increases, the present model deviates progressively from Λ \Lambda CDM. We have also performed a Markov Chain Monte Carlo statistical analysis to test the model predictions with the Hubble parameter, the Pantheon supernova (SN) observational data, and the combination of those samples, obtaining constraints on the parameters of the model and the current values of the Hubble parameter and the matter density. Our findings indicate that this f ⁡ ( Q ) f(Q) gravity model is indeed a viable candidate for describing the late-time evolution of the Universe at the background level.

The
f(Q)
gravity: a brief review

In general, the action for an
f(Q)
gravity model in the presence of matter components is given by

S=\int{d^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\left(\frac{f(Q)}{2\kappa^{2}}+\mathcal{L}_{\rm{M}}\right)},

(2.1)

where
g
denotes the determinant of the metric tensor,
g^{\mu\nu}
,
\kappa^{2}=8\pi G=1/M_{\rm{p}}^{2}
, with
G
being the Newton’s constant, and
M_{\rm{p}}
the reduced Planck mass.
\mathcal{L}_{\rm{M}}
represents the Lagrangian density for the matter components (relativistic and non-relativistic perfect matter fluids). The term
f(Q)
is for now an arbitrary function of the non-metricity scalar
Q
. This scalar it is obtained by the contraction of the non-metricity tensor, which is defined as
[
16
,
12
,
13
]

Q_{\gamma\mu\nu}\equiv\nabla_{\gamma}g_{\mu\nu}=\partial_{\gamma}g_{\mu\nu}-\Gamma^{\beta}_{\mkern 10.0mu\gamma\mu}g_{\beta\nu}-\Gamma^{\beta}_{\mkern 10.0mu\gamma\nu}g_{\beta\mu},

(2.2)

where
\nabla_{\gamma}
represents the covariant derivative with respect to a general affine connection
\Gamma^{\alpha}_{\mkern 10.0mu\gamma\beta}
, which can be written as
[
56
,
57
]

\Gamma^{\lambda}\\
_{\mu\nu}=\left\{{}^{\lambda}\\
_{\mu\nu}\right\}+K^{\lambda}\\
_{\mu\nu}+L^{\lambda}\\
_{\mu\nu},

(2.3)

with the Levi-Civita connection of the metric given by

\left\{{}^{\lambda}\\
_{\mu\nu}\right\}\equiv\frac{1}{2}g^{\lambda\beta}\left(\partial_{\mu}g_{\beta\nu}+\partial_{\nu}g_{\beta\mu}-\partial_{\beta}g_{\mu\nu}\right);

(2.4)

the contortion tensor is

K^{\lambda}\\
_{\mu\nu}\equiv\frac{1}{2}T^{\lambda}\\
_{\mu\nu}+T^{\,\,\,\,\,\lambda}_{(\mu\,\,\,\,\nu)},

(2.5)

with the torsion tensor
T^{\lambda}_{\,\,\,\,\mu\nu}\equiv 2\Gamma^{\lambda}_{\,\,\,\,[\mu\nu]}
, and the disformation tensor
L^{\lambda}_{\,\,\,\,\mu\nu}
, which can be written in terms of the non-metricity tensor as

L^{\beta}_{\mkern 10.0mu\mu\nu}=\frac{1}{2}Q^{\beta}_{\mkern 10.0mu\mu\nu}-Q_{(\mu\nu)}^{\mkern 35.0mu\beta}.

(2.6)

The contraction of the tensor
Q_{\gamma\mu\nu}
is given by

Q=-Q_{\gamma\mu\nu}P^{\gamma\mu\nu},

(2.7)

where the tensor
P^{\gamma\mu\nu}
is the non-metricity conjugate, defined as

P^{\beta}_{\mkern 10.0mu\mu\nu}=-\frac{1}{2}L^{\beta}_{\mkern 10.0mu\mu\nu}+\frac{1}{4}(Q^{\beta}-\tilde{Q}^{\beta})g_{\mu\nu}-\frac{1}{4}\delta^{\beta}_{\,\,\,(\mu}Q_{\nu)},

(2.8)

the other quantities present in Eq. (
2.8
) are given by

Q_{\beta}=g^{\mu\nu}Q_{\beta\mu\nu},\quad\tilde{Q}_{\beta}=g^{\mu\nu}Q_{\mu\beta\nu}.

(2.9)

Variation of the action (
2.1
) with respect to the metric gives the equation of motion
[
12
,
13
]
,

\frac{2}{\sqrt{-g}}\nabla_{\beta}(f_{Q}\sqrt{-g}P^{\beta}_{\mkern 10.0mu\mu\nu})+\frac{1}{2}fg_{\mu\nu}+f_{Q}(P_{\mu\beta\lambda}Q_{\nu}^{\mkern 10.0mu\beta\lambda}-2Q_{\beta\lambda\mu}P^{\beta\lambda}\mkern 1.0mu\nu)=-T_{\mu\nu},

(2.10)

where
f_{Q}\equiv\frac{df}{dQ}
, and as usual, the energy momentum tensor
T_{\mu\nu}
is given by

T_{\mu\nu}=-\frac{2}{\sqrt{-g}}\frac{\delta\sqrt{-g}\mathcal{L}_{\rm{M}}}{\delta g^{\mu\nu}},

(2.11)

which in this case is considered to be a perfect fluid, i.e.,
T_{\mu\nu}=(\rho+p)u_{\mu}u_{\nu}+pg_{\mu\nu}
, where
p
and
\rho
are the total pressure and total energy density of any perfect fluid of matter, radiation and DE, respectively. The quantity
u^{\mu}
represents the 4-velocity of the fluid.

Considering the flat Friedman-Robertson-Walker (FRW) metric,

ds^{2}=-dt^{2}+a(t)^{2}\delta_{ij}dx^{i}dx^{j},

(2.12)

with
a(t)
representing the scale factor, the time and spatial components of Eq. (
2.10
) are given, respectively, by
[
12
,
13
]

6f_{Q}H^{2}-\frac{1}{2}f=\rho_{\rm{m}}+\rho_{\rm{r}},

(2.13)

and

(12H^{2}f_{QQ}+f_{Q})\dot{H}=-\frac{1}{2}\left(\rho_{\rm{m}}+\frac{4}{3}\rho_{\rm{r}}\right),

(2.14)

where
\rho_{\rm{m}}
is the matter density and
\rho_{\rm{r}}
denotes the density of radiation.
f_{QQ}=\frac{d^{2}f}{dQ^{2}}
, the over-dot denotes a derivative with respect to the cosmic time
t
and
H\equiv\dot{a}/a
is the Hubble parameter. Additionally, we have considered
\kappa^{2}=1
. Further, the non-metricity scalar associated with the metric (
2.12
) is given by

Q=6H^{2}.

(2.15)

It should be noted that in this scenario, the standard Friedmann equations of General Relativity (GR) plus the cosmological constant are recovered when assuming that
f(Q)=Q+2\Lambda
.

If there is no interaction between non-relativistic matter and radiation, then these components obey separately the conservation laws

\dot{\rho}_{\rm{m}}+3H\rho_{\rm{m}}=0,\quad\dot{\rho}_{\rm{r}}+4H\rho_{\rm{r}}=0.

(2.16)

As usual in the literature, it is possible to rewrite the field equations (
2.13
) and (
2.14
) in the Einstein-Hilbert form:

3H^{2}=\rho,

(2.17)

-2\dot{H}^{2}=\rho+p,

(2.18)

where
\rho=\rho_{\rm{m}}+\rho_{\rm{r}}+\rho_{\rm{DE}}
and
p=p_{\rm{m}}+p_{\rm{r}}+p_{\rm{DE}}
correspond to the total effective energy pressure densities of the cosmological fluid, respectively. In this case, the dark energy component has a geometric origin, and after some manipulation in Eqs. (
2.13
) and (
2.14
), we obtain the effective dark energy and pressure corresponding to the
f(Q)
-theory given by

\rho_{\rm{DE}}=\frac{1}{2}f+3H^{2}(1-2f_{Q}),

(2.19)

and

p_{\rm{DE}}=2\dot{H}(12H^{2}f_{QQ}+f_{Q}-1)-\rho_{\rm{DE}}.

(2.20)

It is easy to show that
\rho_{\rm{DE}}
and
p_{\rm{DE}}
defined in this way satisfy the usual energy conservation equation

\dot{\rho}_{\rm{DE}}+3H(\rho_{\rm{DE}}+p_{\rm{DE}})=0.

(2.21)

In this case, we assume that the equation of state parameter for this effective dark energy satisfies the following relation:

w_{\rm{DE}}=\frac{p_{\rm{DE}}}{\rho_{\rm{DE}}}=-1+\frac{2\dot{H}(12H^{2}f_{QQ}+f_{Q}-1)}{\frac{1}{2}f+3H^{2}(1-2f_{Q})}.

(2.22)

4
Parameter constraints

To constraint the parameters of the model we implement a statistical analysis based in the Markov Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) method, considering the observational data of the Hubble parameter,
H_{{\rm obs}}(z)
, obtained from the cosmic chronometers and the radial BAO size methods (see Ref.
[
58
]
for the complete list of data points and information about the observational methods), and of the magnitude-redshift relation,
m_{{\rm obs}}(z)
, for the Pantheon sample including 1048 observational data
[
59
]
.

As exposed by Eq. (
3.1
), our model depends on two dimensionless parameters (
n,b
); however, we are considering the specific case of
n=1
. Then, for the statistical analysis presented in this section, we also decided to include the current value of the Hubble parameter,
H_{0}\equiv H(z=0)
, as well as the current value of the matter density parameter,
\Omega_{m0}\equiv\Omega_{m}(z=0)
. With this, we compute the value of the Hubble parameter at different redshifts as predicted by the model,
H_{{\rm fQ}}(z)
, as well as the apparent magnitude
m_{{\rm fQ}}(z)
(which is a function of the expansion rate,
H_{{\rm fQ}}(z)
through the luminosity distance; see
[
59
]
for details), considering the approximate solution given by Eq. (
3.10
).

We implement the MCMC method by using the
emcee
package
[
60
]
, which employs a suitable
\chi^{2}
function as an input for the applied Maximum Likelihood (ML) method. In our case, the likelihood function reads

\mathcal{L}(D|z;\bm{\theta})=-\ln p(D|z;\bm{\theta})=-\frac{1}{2}\chi^{2}(z;\bm{\theta}),

(4.1)

with the
\chi^{2}
function defined as

\chi^{2}(z;\bm{\theta})=\sum_{k=1}^{N_{d}}\frac{\left(D_{{\rm fQ},k}(z;\bm{\theta})-D_{{\rm obs},k}(z)\right)^{2}}{\sigma_{k}^{2}},

(4.2)

where
\bm{\theta}=\left(H_{0},\Omega_{m0},b\right)
is the vector of the parameters to be fit,
\sigma_{k}
is the standard deviation of the corresponding data, and
N_{d}
is the number of data points. Depending on the set of observational data,
D_{{\rm fQ}}
corresponds to the expansion rate,
H(z)
(with
N_{d}=40
), or to the apparent magnitude,
m
(with
N_{d}=1048
).

The final important aspect to mention here is the introduction of appropriate priors for the parameters, which are displayed in Tab.
1
and are used for both of the analyses. Notice that for
H_{0}
the prior is uniform and large enough for the MCMC to be able to explore values around the two reported observations which are currently under scrutiny (see a discussion bellow).

5
Conclusions

Until now, a definitive and satisfactory resolution to the enigma of DE remains elusive within the cosmological community. Consequently, cosmologists continue to propose new and often exotic theories in their pursuit of someday attaining a comprehensive explanation for this perplexing problem. Among these proposals, the
f(Q)
non-metric gravity has recently gained prominence as an alternative framework for elucidating phenomena in both early and late-time cosmology. Remarkably, this theory achieves this without necessitating the inclusion of dark energy, the inflaton field, or dark matter. This innovative approach represents a significant step forward in our quest to understand the fundamental nature of the universe.

In this sense, here we have introduce a new and viable
f(Q)
gravity model which can be represented as a perturbation of the
\Lambda
CDM model (see Eq. (
3.1
)). Usually, within the realm of
f(Q)
gravity, the customary approach to investigate cosmological evolution involves employing a parametrization of the Hubble parameter in terms of redshift, among other strategies. In this work as an alternative, we have derived an analytical approximation for the expansion rate
H(z)
(Eq. (
3.10
)), and from it, we have deduced approximated analytical expressions for the parameters
w_{\rm{DE}}
,
w_{\rm{eff}}
, and
\Omega_{DE}
, as well as the deceleration parameter
q
in terms of the redshift
z
(Eqs. (
3.15
)-(
3.18
)).

In order to verify the viability of this approximate analytical solution for
H(z)
, we have examined the behaviour of the these parameters in the late-time regime. It should be noted that for
b>0
,
w_{\rm{DE}}
shows a quintessence like behavior and for
b<0
, it shows a phantom like behavior (see Fig.
1
). However, regardless of the sign of
b
,
w_{\rm{eff}}
shows a quintessence like behavior (shown in Fig.
3
). Furthermore, it has been deduced that in general as the magnitude of the parameter
b
increases, the present model deviates progressively from the
\Lambda
CDM model (depicted in Figs.
1
-
4
). This is the expected behavior, since by construction our approximated solution for
H(z)
was built as a perturbation of the
\Lambda
CDM model solution.

The MCMC analysis considering the Hubble parameter and the Pantheon data samples allowed us to find constraints on the perturbation parameter
b
, as well as on the current values of the Hubble expansion rate (
H_{0}
) and the matter density (
\Omega_{m0}
), as presented in Table
2
and Fig.
5
. The combined datasets (
H(z)+
Pantheon) analysis indicates a preference for negative values of
b
, implying a phantom-like behavior for
w_{{\rm DE}}
, and making evident that the model deviates only marginally from the
\Lambda
CDM predictions (see Figs.
6
and
7
), a conclusion which is also supported by the predicted values of the DE equation of state (
w_{\rm{DE}0}
) and density (
\Omega_{\rm{DE}0}
), at present times (
z=0
), as reported in Table
3
. Then, our findings indicate that this
f(Q)
gravity model is indeed a viable candidate for describing the late-time evolution of the Universe at the background level.

Umbilical submanifolds of ℍ k × 𝕊 n − k + 1 \mathbb{H}^{k}\times\mathbb{S}^{n-k+1} .


Abstract
. In this article we complete the classification of the umbilical submanifolds of a Riemannian product of space forms, addressing the case of a conformally flat product ℍ k × 𝕊 n − k + 1 \mathbb{H}^{k}\times\mathbb{S}^{n-k+1} , which has not been covered in previous works on the subject. We show that there exists precisely a p p -parameter family of congruence classes of umbilical submanifolds of ℍ k × 𝕊 n − k + 1 \mathbb{H}^{k}\times\mathbb{S}^{n-k+1} with substantial codimension p p , which we prove to be at most min ​ { k + 1 , n − k + 2 } \mbox{min}\,\{k+1,n-k+2\} . We study more carefully the cases of codimensions one and two and exhibit, respectively, a one-parameter family and a two-parameter family (together with three extra one-parameter families) that contain precisely one representative of each congruence class of such submanifolds. In particular, this yields another proof of the classification of all (congruence classes of) umbilical submanifolds of 𝕊 n × ℝ \mathbb{S}^{n}\times\mathbb{R} , and provides a similar classification for the case of ℍ n × ℝ \mathbb{H}^{n}\times\mathbb{R} . We determine all possible topological types, actually, diffeomorphism types, of a complete umbilical submanifold of ℍ k × 𝕊 n − k + 1 \mathbb{H}^{k}\times\mathbb{S}^{n-k+1} . We also show that umbilical submanifolds of the product model of ℍ k × 𝕊 n − k + 1 \mathbb{H}^{k}\times\mathbb{S}^{n-k+1} can be regarded as rotational submanifolds in a suitable sense, and explicitly describe their profile curves when k = n k=n . As a consequence of our investigations, we prove that every conformal diffeomorphism of ℍ k × 𝕊 n − k + 1 \mathbb{H}^{k}\times\mathbb{S}^{n-k+1} onto itself is an isometry.

1.
Introduction

An isometric immersion
f\colon M\to N
of a Riemannian manifold
M
into another Riemannian manifold
N
is
umbilical
if there exists a normal vector field
H
along
f
such that its second fundamental form

\alpha_{f}\in\Gamma(T^{*}M\otimes T^{*}M\otimes N_{f}M)

with values in the normal bundle is given by
\alpha_{f}(X,Y)=\langle X,Y\rangle H

for all
X,Y\in\mathfrak{X}(M)
.
Geometrically, this roughly means that
f(M)
is equally curved in
N
in all tangent directions. Umbilical submanifolds are the simplest possible submanifolds of a Riemannian manifold after the totally geodesic ones, for which
\alpha_{f}
vanishes identically.

It is apparent that a generic Riemannian manifold does not carry any umbilical submanifold of dimension
n\geq 2
. For instance, it was shown by Souam and Toubiana
[
8
]
that of the eight three-dimensional model geometries of Thurston, only the spaces of constant curvature, the product spaces
\mathbb{S}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}
and
\mathbb{H}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}
, and the
\emph{Sol}
geometry admit umbilical surfaces, in the latter case a unique non totally geodesic one, up to ambient isometries. Here
\mathbb{S}^{n}
and
\mathbb{H}^{n}
stand for the sphere and hyperbolic space of dimension
n
, respectively. On the other hand, by a result of Leung and Nomizu
[
2
]
(see also Section
1.7
of
[
1
]
), if
N
is a Riemannian manifold that satisfies the axiom of
r
-spheres, that is, for any point
x\in N
and for any integer
r\geq 2
, there always exists an extrinsic sphere of
N
tangent at
x
to any prescribed
r
-dimensional subspace
V\subset T_{x}N
, then
N
must have constant sectional curvature. Recall that an extrinsic sphere of a Riemannian manifold
N
is an umbilical submanifold of
N
whose mean curvature vector field is parallel in the normal connection. It is also pointed out in
[
2
]
that if the requirement on the mean curvature vector field is dropped in the axiom of
r
-spheres, then the corresponding weaker axiom for
n>4
and
3<r<n
implies that M is conformally flat.

Besides the spaces of constant curvature, there are few Riemannian manifolds for which a classification of all its umbilical submanifolds is known. For a general symmetric space
N
, it was shown by Nikolayevsky (see Theorem
1
of
[
6
]
) that any umbilical submanifold of
N
is an umbilical submanifold of a product of space forms totally geodesically embedded in
N
. This was one of our motivations for studying umbilical submanifolds of a product of space forms.

For submanifolds of dimension
m\geq 3
of a product
\mathbb{Q}_{k_{1}}^{n_{1}}\times\mathbb{Q}_{k_{2}}^{n_{2}}
of space forms whose curvatures satisfy
k_{1}+k_{2}\neq 0
, the problem was reduced in
[
4
]
to the classification of
m
-dimensional umbilical submanifolds with codimension two of
\mathbb{S}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}
and
\mathbb{H}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}
. The case of
\mathbb{S}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}
was carried out in
[
5
]
, extending previous results in
[
8
]
and
[
9
]
for hypersurfaces. The case
m=2
was studied in
[
7
]
, where it was shown that, in addition to the examples that appear already in higher dimensions, there are precisely two distinct two–parameter families of complete embedded flat umbilical surfaces that lie substantially in
\mathbb{H}_{k}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}
and
\mathbb{H}_{k_{1}}^{3}\times\mathbb{H}_{k_{2}}^{3}
, respectively.

In this article we address the classification of the umbilical submanifolds of the products
\mathbb{Q}_{k_{1}}^{n_{1}}\times\mathbb{Q}_{k_{2}}^{n_{2}}
of space forms in the cases that have not been covered in
[
4
]
,
[
5
]
and
[
7
]
, namely, the cases in which either
k_{1}+k_{2}=0
or
k_{1}<0
and
n_{2}=1
. Together with the case in which
k_{1}>0
and
n_{2}=1
, already considered in
[
5
]
, these are precisely the products of Riemannian manifolds that are conformally flat. In fact, there exists a global conformal diffeomorphism of

\mathbb{H}^{k}\times\mathbb{S}^{n-k+1}
onto the complement
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\setminus\mathbb{R}^{k-1}
of a
(k-1)
-dimensional subspace of
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
(see Section
3
). Since umbilical submanifolds are preserved under a conformal change of the metric of the ambient manifold, one might consider this to imply already a classification of the umbilical submanifolds of
\mathbb{H}^{k}\times\mathbb{S}^{n-k+1}
: any umbilical isometric immersion
f\colon M^{m}\to\mathbb{H}^{k}\times\mathbb{S}^{n-k+1}
is produced by composing the standard inclusion into
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\setminus\mathbb{R}^{k-1}
of an open subset of either
\mathbb{S}^{m}
or
\mathbb{R}^{m}
with the inverse of such diffeomorphism.

However, for a complete classification of the umbilical submanifolds of
\mathbb{H}^{k}\times\mathbb{S}^{n-k+1}
, one should be able to decide when two such umbilical isometric immersions
f,\tilde{f}\colon M^{m}\to\mathbb{H}^{k}\times\mathbb{S}^{n-k+1}
are congruent, that is, when there exists an isometry
\Phi\colon\mathbb{H}^{k}\times\mathbb{S}^{n-k+1}\to\mathbb{H}^{k}\times\mathbb{S}^{n-k+1}
such that
\tilde{f}=\Phi\circ f
. In other words, a complete classification of the umbilical submanifolds of
\mathbb{H}^{k}\times\mathbb{S}^{n-k+1}
requires a description of all congruence classes of such submanifolds as well as the knowledge of a representative of each of them. Other natural questions one may pose are: what is the maximal substantial codimension of an umbilical submanifold of
\mathbb{H}^{k}\times\mathbb{S}^{n-k+1}
, that is, the maximal codimension of an umbilical submanifold of
\mathbb{H}^{k}\times\mathbb{S}^{n-k+1}
that is not contained in a totally geodesic submanifold? What are its geometry and topology?

We now outline the contents of the paper and the results we have obtained while investigating these problems. In Section
2
, we recall some basic facts on the model of Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
as a hypersurface of the light cone in the Lorentz space

\mathbb{L}^{n+3}
, a convenient setting to deal with such problems. In Section
3
, we introduce the conformal model
(\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\setminus\mathbb{R}^{k-1},g)
of
\mathbb{H}^{k}\times\mathbb{S}^{n-k+1}
, and describe its isometries (Theorem
1
) and totally geodesic submanifolds (Proposition
4
). As a consequence of independent interest of Theorem
1
, we see that every conformal diffeomorphism of
\mathbb{H}^{k}\times\mathbb{S}^{n-k+1}
is an isometry (Corollary
2
), which extends a similar result by Souam and Toubiana
[
8
]
for the particular cases of the product spaces
\mathbb{S}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}
and
\mathbb{H}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}
as well as a well-known property of hyperbolic space. The conformal model
(\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\setminus\mathbb{R}^{k-1},g)
of
\mathbb{H}^{k}\times\mathbb{S}^{n-k+1}
also allows us to easily determine all possible topological types, actually, diffeomorphism types, of a complete umbilical submanifold of
\mathbb{H}^{k}\times\mathbb{S}^{n-k+1}
(Proposition
6
).

In Section
4
we address the congruence problem for umbilical submanifolds of

\mathbb{H}^{k}\times\mathbb{S}^{n-k+1}
. First we show that any such submanifold with codimension
p
arises as the intersection of
\mathbb{H}^{k}\times\mathbb{S}^{n-k+1}\subset\mathbb{L}^{n+3}
with a codimension
p
time-like subspace of
\mathbb{L}^{n+3}
(Proposition
8
). Then we obtain an algebraic criterion to decide when two such submanifolds are congruent and use it to prove that there exists precisely a
p
-parameter family of congruence classes of umbilical submanifolds of
\mathbb{H}^{k}\times\mathbb{S}^{n-k+1}
with substantial codimension
p
(Theorem
15
). Along the way, we show that the substantial codimension of an umbilical submanifold of
\mathbb{H}^{k}\times\mathbb{S}^{n-k+1}
is at most
\mbox{min}\,\{k+1,n-k+2\}
(Corollary
14
). Then we study more carefully the cases of codimensions one and two. We give necessary and sufficient conditions for two umbilical submanifolds of
(\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\setminus\mathbb{R}^{k-1},g)
with codimensions one and two to be congruent in terms of their Euclidean data (Propositions
4
and
5
, respectively) and exhibit, respectively, a one-parameter family and a two-parameter family (together with three extra one-parameter families) that contain precisely one representative of each congruence class of such submanifolds (Corollaries
17
and
22
, respectively). In particular, we recover the main result of
[
4
]
for the particular case of
\mathbb{S}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}
(Corollary
20
) and obtain, as a particular case of Corollary
22
, a similar classification of all (congruence classes of) umbilical submanifolds of
\mathbb{H}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}
.

In Section
5
, we show that umbilical submanifolds of the product model of

\mathbb{H}^{k}\times\mathbb{S}^{n-k+1}
can be regarded as rotational submanifolds in a suitable sense. In particular, umbilical submanifolds of
\mathbb{H}^{n}\times\mathbb{S}^{1}
(and hence of its universal cover
\mathbb{H}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}
) are rotational submanifolds with curves as profiles, and the latter are explicitly described by means of the conformal model of
\mathbb{H}^{n}\times\mathbb{S}^{1}
, providing an alternative description to that in
[
4
]
of the profile curves of umbilical submanifolds of
\mathbb{S}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}
.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section we summarize some basic facts on the model of Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
as a hypersurface of the light cone in the Lorentz space
\mathbb{L}^{n+3}
that make it a useful tool to study Moebius geometric problems. We refer to Chapter
9
of
[
1
]
for details.

Let
\mathbb{L}^{n+3}
be the
(n+3)
-dimensional Lorentzian space
with the metric induced by the inner product

\langle v,w\rangle=-v_{0}w_{0}+v_{1}w_{1}+\cdots+v_{n+2}w_{n+2}

for
v=(v_{0},\dots,v_{n+2})
and
w=(w_{0},\ldots,w_{n+2})
.
The set of light-like vectors

\mathbb{V}^{n+2}=\{v\in\mathbb{L}^{n+3}:\langle v,v\rangle=0,\;v\neq 0\}

is called the
light cone
of
\mathbb{L}^{n+3}
.
For any given
w\in\mathbb{V}^{n+2}
, the intersection

\mathbb{E}^{n+1}=\{v\in\mathbb{V}^{n+2}:\langle v,w\rangle=1\}

of
\mathbb{V}^{n+2}
with the affine hyperplane

\{u\in\mathbb{L}^{n+3}:\langle u,w\rangle=1\}

is a model of the
(n+1)
-dimensional Euclidean space.
Namely, for a fixed
v\in\mathbb{E}^{n+1}
and a linear isometry

C\colon\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\to(\mbox{span}\{v,w\})^{\perp}\subset\mathbb{L}^{n+3},

the map
\Psi\colon\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\to\mathbb{L}^{n+3}
,
given by

(1)

\Psi(x)=v+Cx-\frac{1}{2}\|x\|^{2}w,

is an isometric embedding such that
\Psi(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})=\mathbb{E}^{n+1}
. From now on we assume that a triple
(v,w,C)
has been fixed with
w_{0}<0
, so that

\mathbb{E}^{n+1}\subset\mathbb{V}^{n+2}_{+}
, the upper half of
\mathbb{V}^{n+2}
.

For any hypersphere
\mathcal{S}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}

with (constant) mean curvature
h
with respect to a unit normal vector
field
N
, the map

x\in\mathcal{S}\mapsto\Psi_{*}(x)N(x)+h\Psi(x)\in\mathbb{L}^{n+3}

has a constant value
z
, a unit space-like vector satisfying

\langle\Psi(x),z\rangle=0
for all
x\in\mathcal{S}
. It follows that

\Psi(\mathcal{S})=\mathbb{E}^{n+1}\cap\{z\}^{\perp}.

Observe that
\mathcal{S}
is an affine hyperplane if and only if

0=h=\langle z,w\rangle.

If
\mathcal{S}
is a hypersphere with (Euclidean) center
x_{0}

and radius
r
, oriented by its inward pointing unit normal vector field

x\in\mathcal{S}\mapsto N(x)=\frac{1}{r}(x_{0}-x)

with corresponding mean curvature
h=1/r
, then its associated unit space-like vector is

z=\frac{1}{r}\Psi(x_{0})+\frac{r}{2}w.

On the other hand, the unit space-like vector associated to an affine hyperplane
\mathcal{S}

oriented by a unit normal vector
N
is

z=CN-cw,

where
c\in\mathbb{R}
is the oriented distance from
\mathcal{S}
to the origin in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
.

If
\mathcal{S}_{1}
and
\mathcal{S}_{2}
are hyperspheres or affine hyperplanes with corresponding unit space-like vectors
z_{1}
and
z_{2}
, respectively. and if
N^{1}_{x}
and
N^{2}_{x}
are the
unit normal vectors of
\mathcal{S}_{1}
and
\mathcal{S}_{2}
, respectively, at
x\in\mathcal{S}_{1}\cap\mathcal{S}_{2}
, then

\langle N^{1}_{x},N^{2}_{x}\rangle=\langle z_{1},z_{2}\rangle.

In particular,
\mathcal{S}_{1}
and
\mathcal{S}_{2}
intersect orthogonally if and only if

\langle z_{1},z_{2}\rangle=0
.

More generally, any sphere or affine subspace
\mathcal{S}
with codimension
k
of
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
is given by

\Psi(\mathcal{S})=\mathbb{E}^{n+1}\cap V^{\perp}
for some space-like subspace

V\subset\mathbb{L}^{n+3}
of dimension
k
, affine subspaces being characterized by the fact
that
w\in V^{\perp}
. We refer to
V
in the sequel as the space-like subspace of
\mathbb{L}^{n+3}
correspondent to
\mathcal{S}
.

If
f\colon M^{m}\to\mathbb{V}^{n+2}
is an immersion of a
differentiable manifold and
\mu\in C^{\infty}(M)
is any positive smooth function, then the
map
h\colon M^{m}\to\mathbb{V}^{n+2}
given by
h=\mu f
is also an
immersion, and the induced metrics
\langle\,,\,\rangle_{f}
and
\langle\,,\,\rangle_{h}

are related by

\langle\,,\,\rangle_{h}=\mu^{2}\langle\,,\,\rangle_{f}.

As a consequence, any conformal immersion
f\colon M^{m}\to\mathbb{R}^{n+1}

with conformal factor
\varphi\in C^{\infty}(M)
gives rise to an
isometric immersion
{\mathcal{I}}(f)\colon M^{m}\to\mathbb{V}_{+}^{n+2}
given by

{\mathcal{I}}(f)=\frac{1}{\varphi}\Psi\circ f.

Conversely, any isometric immersion

F\colon M^{m}\to\mathbb{V}_{+}^{n+2}\smallsetminus\mathbb{R}w
, where
\mathbb{R}w=\{tw:t<0\}
,
gives rise to a conformal immersion
{\mathcal{C}}(F)\colon M^{m}\to\mathbb{R}^{n+1}

given by

\Psi\circ{\mathcal{C}}(F)=\Pi\circ F,

whose conformal factor is
1/\langle F,w\rangle
. Here
\Pi\colon\mathbb{V}_{+}^{n+2}\smallsetminus\mathbb{R}w\to\mathbb{E}^{n+1}
denotes the projection
onto
\mathbb{E}^{n+1}
given by

\Pi(u)=u/\langle u,w\rangle
.

In particular, if
T\in\mathbb{O}_{1}(n+3)
, then

{\mathcal{C}}(T\circ\Psi)
is a conformal transformation of
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
,
and every conformal transformation of
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
is given in this way.
For instance, if
R\in\mathbb{O}_{1}(n+3)
is the reflection

R(u)=u-2\langle u,z\rangle z

with respect to the hyperplane in
\mathbb{L}^{n+3}
orthogonal to a unit
space-like vector
z
, with
\langle z,w\rangle\neq 0
, then

{\mathcal{C}}(R\circ\Psi)=I

is the inversion with respect to the hypersphere such that
\Psi(\mathcal{S})=\mathbb{E}^{n+1}\cap\{z\}^{\perp}
. If
G\in\mathbb{O}_{1}(n+3)
satisfies
G(w)=\lambda w
for some

\lambda\in(0,+\infty)
, then

{\mathcal{C}}(G\circ\Psi)=L

is a similarity of ratio
\lambda
. In particular, isometries
of
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
correspond to the elements of
\mathbb{O}_{1}(n+3)
that fix
w
.

3.
The conformal model of
\mathbb{H}^{k}\times\mathbb{S}^{n-k+1}

Let
\mathbb{R}^{k-1}
be a subspace of dimension
k-1
of
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
, which we assume to be

\mathbb{R}^{k-1}=\{(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n+1})\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\,:\,x_{k}=\cdots=x_{n+1}=0\}.

Given
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
, we write
x=x^{T}+x^{\perp}
according to the orthogonal decomposition
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}=\mathbb{R}^{k-1}\oplus\mathbb{R}^{n-k+2}
. Now let
v,w\in\mathbb{L}^{n+3}
be vectors in
\mathbb{V}^{n+2}\subset\mathbb{L}^{n+3}
with
\langle v,w\rangle=1
, let

C\colon\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\to(\mbox{span}\{v,w\})^{\perp}\subset\mathbb{L}^{n+3}
be a linear isometry, and set

V_{1}=C(\mathbb{R}^{k-1})
and
V_{2}=C(\mathbb{R}^{n-k+2})
. Let
\mathbb{L}^{n+3}=W_{1}\oplus W_{2}
be the orthogonal decomposition with
W_{1}=V_{1}\oplus\mbox{span}\{v,w\}
and
W_{2}=V_{2}
. If
\Psi\colon\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\to\mathbb{E}^{n+1}\subset\mathbb{L}^{n+3}
is the isometric embedding given by (
1
), then the map
\Theta\colon\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\setminus\mathbb{R}^{k-1}\to\mathbb{H}^{k}\times\mathbb{S}^{n-k+1}\subset W_{1}\oplus W_{2}=\mathbb{L}^{k+1}\times\mathbb{R}^{n-k+2}=\mathbb{L}^{n+3}
defined by

(2)

\displaystyle\Theta(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|x^{\perp}\|^{-1}\Psi(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|x^{\perp}\|^{-1}\left(v-\frac{1}{2}\|x\|^{2}w+Cx^{T}+Cx^{\perp}\right)

is a conformal diffeomorphism with induced metric
g=\varphi^{2}g_{0}
whose conformal factor with respect to the Euclidean metric
g_{0}
is given by
\varphi(x)=\|x^{\perp}\|^{-1}=(\sum_{i=k}^{n+1}x_{i}^{2})^{-1/2}
, that is,
\varphi(x)
is the inverse of the distance from
x
to
\mathbb{R}^{k-1}
. We refer to
(\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\setminus\mathbb{R}^{k-1},g)
as the
conformal model
of
\mathbb{H}^{k}\times\mathbb{S}^{n-k+1}
. The above notations will be used throughout the paper.

3.1.
The isometries of
(\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\setminus\mathbb{R}^{k-1},g)

The next result describes the isometries of
(\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\setminus\mathbb{R}^{k-1},g)
.

Theorem 1
.

The following assertions on the map
F\colon\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\setminus\mathbb{R}^{k-1}\to\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\setminus\mathbb{R}^{k-1}
are equivalent for any
1\leq k\leq n+1
:

(i)

F
is an isometry with respect to
g
;

(ii)

\Theta\circ F=T\circ\Theta
, where
T\in O_{1}(n+3)
leaves
W_{1}
and
W_{2}
invariant;

(iii)

F=\mathcal{C}(T\circ\Psi)
, where
T\in O_{1}(n+3)

leaves
W_{1}
and
W_{2}
invariant;

(iv)

F
is a conformal diffeomorphism with respect to the Euclidean metric
g_{0}
;

(v)

F
is a conformal diffeomorphism with respect to
g
;

(vi)

F
is given by
F=I\circ L
, where
L
is a composition
L=G\circ H\circ A
of an orthogonal transformation
A\in O(n+1)
that leaves
\mathbb{R}^{k-1}
invariant, a homothety with respect to the origin and a translation by a vector
v\in\mathbb{R}^{k-1}
, and
I
is an inversion with respect to a hypersphere centered at a point of
\mathbb{R}^{k-1}
.

Proof:
(i)\Longrightarrow(ii)
: Let
F
be an isometry of
(\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\setminus\mathbb{R}^{k-1},g)
. Since
\Theta\colon(\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\setminus\mathbb{R}^{k-1},g)\to\mathbb{H}^{k}\times\mathbb{S}^{n-k+1}
is an isometry, then
\Theta\circ F=T\circ\Theta
for some element
T
of the isometry group

\mbox{Iso}\,(\mathbb{H}^{k}\times\mathbb{S}^{n-k+1})=\mbox{Iso}\,(\mathbb{H}^{k})\times\mbox{Iso}\,(\mathbb{S}^{n-k+1})
of
\mathbb{H}^{k}\times\mathbb{S}^{n-k+1}\subset W_{1}\oplus W_{2}=\mathbb{L}^{n+3}
, which is an orthogonal transformation
T\in O_{1}(n+3)
that leaves
W_{1}
and
W_{2}
invariant.

(ii)\Longrightarrow(iii)
: Since
\Theta(x)=\|x^{\perp}\|^{-1}\Psi(x)
, then
\Pi\circ\Theta=\Psi
, and hence

\Pi\circ T\circ\Psi=\Pi\circ T\circ\Theta=\Pi\circ\Theta\circ F=\Psi\circ F.

That
(iii)
implies
(iv)
is clear, as is the equivalence between
(iv)
and
(v)
.

(iv)\Longrightarrow(iii)
: The assumption that
F(\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\setminus\mathbb{R}^{k-1})\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\setminus\mathbb{R}^{k-1}
means that
(F(x))^{\perp}\neq 0
whenever
x^{\perp}\neq 0
. Since

\Psi(x)=v-\frac{1}{2}\|x\|^{2}w+Cx^{T}+Cx^{\perp}

and
\Psi\circ F=\Pi\circ T\circ\Psi
, this implies that
\pi_{W_{2}}(Ty)\neq 0
whenever
\pi_{W_{2}}(y)\neq 0
, where
\pi_{W_{2}}\colon\mathbb{L}^{n+3}\to W_{2}=V_{2}
is the orthogonal projection. In other words,
\ker(\pi_{W_{2}}\circ T)\subset\ker\pi_{W_{2}}=W_{1}
, or equivalently,
y\in W_{1}
whenever
Ty\in W_{1}
. Thus
W_{1}
is invariant under
T^{-1}
, and hence both
W_{2}
and
W_{1}
are invariant under
T
.

(iii)\Longrightarrow(vi)
: Define
(\bar{v},\bar{w},\bar{C})

3.2.

Proposition 4

(3)

\alpha^{f_{1}}(x)(X,Y)=\alpha^{f_{0}}(x)(X,Y)-\frac{1}{\varphi(f(x))}f^{*}g_{0}(X,Y)(\mbox{grad}_{0}\varphi(f(x)))_{N_{f}M(x)}

with respect to

.

For
\tilde{M}^{m}=\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\setminus\mathbb{R}^{k-1}
,
g_{0}
the standard Euclidean metric and

3.3.

3

or

The next result gives an algebraic criterion to decide whether an umbilical submanifold of
(\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\setminus\mathbb{R}^{k-1},g)
is substantial.

Proposition 13
.

An umbilical submanifold
S
of
(\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\setminus\mathbb{R}^{k-1},g)
with corresponding space-like subspace

is substantial if and only if
V\cap W_{1}
and
V\cap W_{2}
are trivial.

Proof:
Since
\Psi(S)=\mathbb{E}^{n+1}\cap V^{\perp}
, then
S
is contained in a totally geodesic submanifold
\tilde{S}
, with
\Psi(\tilde{S})=\mathbb{E}^{n+1}\cap{\tilde{V}}^{\perp}
for a space-like subspace
\tilde{V}\subset\mathbb{L}^{n+3}
, if
V\supset\tilde{V}
. By Corollary
5
,

\tilde{V}=\tilde{V}_{1}\oplus\tilde{V}_{2}
with

\tilde{V}_{1}\subset W_{1}
and
\tilde{V}_{2}\subset W_{2}
.
Therefore,
S
is contained in some totally geodesic submanifold
\tilde{S}
if and only if either
V\cap W_{1}
or
V\cap W_{2}
is non trivial.

Corollary 14
.

The substantial codimension of an umbilical submanifold of
\mathbb{H}^{k}\times\mathbb{S}^{n-k+1}
is at most
\mbox{min}\,\{k+1,n-k+2\}
.

-parameter family of congruence classes of umbilical submanifolds of

, two umbilical submanifolds

Corollary 19

Corollary 20
.

For any
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\setminus\{0\}
and any unit vector
N\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
with
\langle x_{0},N\rangle=0
, the two-parameter family

\{\mathbb{S}^{n}(x_{c};r)\cap\mathcal{H}(N;c),\;x_{c}=x_{0}+cN,c\geq 0,r>0\}
, together with the one-parameter family
\{\mathbb{S}^{n}(x_{0};r)\cap\mathcal{H}(x_{0}/\|x_{0}\|;\|x_{0}\|),\;r>0\}
, contain precisely one representative of each congruence class of umbilical non totally geodesic submanifolds of codimension two of
(\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\setminus\{0\},g)=\mathbb{S}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}
. The sphere
\mathbb{S}^{n}(x_{c};r)\cap\mathcal{H}(N;c)
has substantial codimension two if and only if
c>0
.

Proof:
By Proposition
19
, if
\mathbb{S}^{n}(x_{c};r)\cap\mathcal{H}(N;c)
and
\mathbb{S}^{n}(x_{\tilde{c}};\tilde{r})\cap\mathcal{H}(\tilde{N};\tilde{c})
, with
c,\tilde{c}>0
and
r,\tilde{r}>0
, are congruent, then
{\displaystyle\frac{\|{x}_{\tilde{c}}\|}{\|{x}_{c}\|}=\frac{\tilde{r}}{r}=\frac{\tilde{c}}{c}}
. Thus

{\displaystyle\frac{\tilde{c}^{2}+\|x_{0}\|^{2}}{{c}^{2}+\|x_{0}\|^{2}}=\frac{\|{x}_{\tilde{c}}\|^{2}}{\|{x}_{c}\|^{2}}=\frac{\tilde{c}^{2}}{c^{2}}}
,
which implies that
\tilde{c}=c
, and hence
\tilde{r}=r
.

Similarly, Proposition
19
also implies that
\mathbb{S}^{n}(x_{0};r)\cap\mathcal{H}(x_{0}/\|x_{0}\|;\|x_{0}\|)
and
\mathbb{S}^{n}(x_{0};\tilde{r})\cap\mathcal{H}(x_{0}/\|x_{0}\|;\|x_{0}\|)
are congruent if and only if
r=\tilde{r}
.

Now, given any
\mathbb{S}^{n}(\tilde{x}_{0};\tilde{r})\cap\mathcal{H}(\tilde{N};\tilde{c})
with

\|\tilde{x}_{0}\|\neq 0
, set
{\displaystyle\lambda^{2}=\|\tilde{x}_{0}\|^{2}}/{\tilde{c}^{2}}
.
Notice that
\lambda\geq 1
by the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, for
\langle\tilde{N},\tilde{x}_{0}\rangle=\tilde{c}
.
If
\lambda^{2}=1
, that is,
\tilde{c}^{2}=\|\tilde{x}_{0}\|^{2}
, then

\mathbb{S}^{n}(\tilde{x}_{0};\tilde{r})\cap\mathcal{H}(\tilde{N};\tilde{c})
is congruent to

\mathbb{S}^{n}(x_{0};r)\cap\mathcal{H}(x_{0}/\|x_{0}\|;\|x_{0}\|)
for
{\displaystyle r=\frac{\tilde{r}\|x_{0}\|}{\|\tilde{x}_{0}\|}}
.

If
\lambda^{2}>1
, choose
{\displaystyle c=\sqrt{\frac{\|x_{0}\|^{2}}{\lambda^{2}-1}}}
and
{\displaystyle r=\frac{c\tilde{r}}{\tilde{c}}}
. Then

{\displaystyle\frac{\|\tilde{x}_{0}\|}{\|x_{c}\|}=\frac{\tilde{r}}{r}=\frac{\tilde{c}}{c},}

hence
\mathbb{S}^{n}(\tilde{x}_{0};\tilde{r})\cap\mathcal{H}(\tilde{N};\tilde{c})
is congruent to

\mathbb{S}^{n}(x_{c};r)\cap\mathcal{H}(N;c)
by Proposition
19
.

Finally, any
\mathcal{H}(N_{1};c_{1})\cap\mathcal{H}(N_{2};0)
with
c_{1}\neq 0
is congruent to

\mathbb{S}^{n}(x_{c};r)\cap\mathcal{H}(N;c)
with
c=0
and
r=\|x_{0}\|
.

In the remaining of this section we prove a version of Corollary

for the conformal model

of

Lemma 21
.

Let
v\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
be such that
\|v\|\leq 1
. Then, for any
w\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
, the smallest eigenvalue of the Gram matrix
G
of
\{v,w\}
satisfies
0\leq\bar{\lambda}\leq 1
, and
\bar{\lambda}=1
if and only if
\langle v,w\rangle=0
,
\|v\|=1
and
\|w\|\geq 1
.

Proof:
Given any
w\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
, let
A\colon\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
be the linear map such that
Ae_{1}=v
and
Ae_{2}=w
, where
\{e_{1},e_{2}\}
is the canonical basis of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. Then the eigenvalues of
G
are the eigenvalues of
A^{t}A\colon\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
. Thus its smallest eigenvalue
\bar{\lambda}
satisfies

1\geq\|v\|^{2}=\langle A^{t}Ae_{1},e_{1}\rangle\geq\mbox{min}\left\{\frac{\langle A^{t}Av,v\rangle}{\langle v,v\rangle}\;:\>v\neq 0\right\}=\bar{\lambda}\geq 0.

Moreover, if
\bar{\lambda}=1
, then
\|v\|=1
and
e_{1}
is an eigenvector of
A^{t}A
associated to
\bar{\lambda}
. Thus
e_{2}
is also an eigenvector of
A^{t}A
, and hence
\langle v,w\rangle=\langle A^{t}Ae_{1},e_{2}\rangle=0
.
In particular, the eigenvalues of
A^{t}A
are
1=\|v\|^{2}
and
\|w\|^{2}
, and hence
\|w\|\geq 1
. The “if” part is trivial.

Corollary 22
.

Let
p_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\setminus\mathbb{R}^{k-1}

Then the two-parameter family

(7)

\displaystyle\{\mathbb{S}^{n}(x_{c},\sqrt{1+c^{2}}\|p_{0}\|)

contain, together, precisely one representative of each congruence class of umbilical non-totally geodesic submanifolds of codimension two in
(\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\setminus\mathbb{R}^{k-1},g)

First notice that in the family (

Assume that

and

. Similarly, one can check that distinct elements in any of the one-parameter families in (

distinct families.

Let

5.1.
The profile curves of umbilical submanifolds of
\mathbb{H}^{n}\times\mathbb{S}^{1}

We first consider the codimension

\mathbb{H}^{n}\times\mathbb{S}^{1}

(\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\setminus\mathbb{R}^{n-1},g)

\mathbb{H}^{n}\times\mathbb{S}^{1}

spans
\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
. By Corollary

\{\mathbb{S}^{n}(x_{0},r),r>0\}
centered at a point

with

The hypersphere
\mathbb{S}^{n}(e_{n+1},r)

Let

Notice that the image

Since the the subgroup of isometries

acts on each slice,
and this action is transitive on the intersection
\Theta(\mathbb{S}^{n}(e_{n+1},r))\cap\mathbb{H}^{n}\times\{q\}
of the umbilical hypersurface
\Theta(\mathbb{S}^{n}(e_{n+1},r))
of
\mathbb{H}^{n}\times\mathbb{S}^{1}
with each slice
\mathbb{H}^{n}\times\{q\}
, it follows that
\Theta(\mathbb{S}^{n}(e_{n+1},r))
is generated by the action of

O(U_{1}^{\perp})\times\{I\}
on the curve
\Theta(c(I))
. Notice also that if
\Theta(c(\theta))
lies in the slice
\mathbb{H}^{n}\times\{q\}
, then the orbit
\Theta(\mathbb{S}^{n}(e_{n+1},r))\cap\mathbb{H}^{n}\times\{q\}
of
\Theta(c(\theta))
under
O(U_{1}^{\perp})\times\{I\}
is an equidistant hypersurface, horosphere or sphere in that slice (which can degenerate into a point), depending on whether we are in the hyperbolic, parabolic or spherical case, respectively.

Let us now consider the case of umbilical submanifolds with codimension
2
. By Corollary
22
, it is enough to consider the elements of the two families of umbilical submanifolds given by (
7
) and (
22
) that have substantial codimension
2
. Following the notations in that proposition, set
p_{0}=e_{n+1}
,
e=e_{n}
,
\eta=e_{1}
and let
x_{c}
and
N_{c}
be given by

x_{c}=e_{n+1}+\sqrt{1+c^{2}}\,e_{1}\;\;\mbox{and}\;\;N_{c}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+c^{2}}}e_{n}+\frac{c}{\sqrt{1+c^{2}}}e_{1},\,\,\,c>0.

Let
z_{1}
and
z_{2}
be the unit space-like vectors associated to

\mathbb{S}^{n}(x_{c},r)
and
\mathcal{H}(N_{c},c)
, respectively. Then

\{z_{1},z_{2}\}
is an orthonormal basis of the space-like subspace

V\subset\mathbb{L}^{n+3}
associated to the umbilical submanifold

\mathbb{S}^{n}(x_{c},r)\cap\mathcal{H}(N_{c},c)
. Notice that the
W_{1}

components
z_{1}^{T}
and
z_{2}^{T}
are orthogonal, and that
z_{2}^{T}
is
always space-like. Moreover,
\langle z_{1}^{T},z_{1}^{T}\rangle=1-\frac{1}{r^{2}}
.
Thus the subspace
U_{1}\subset W_{1}
is time-like, degenerate or
space-like depending on whether
r>1
,
r=1
or
r<1
, respectively.
We call these cases accordingly
hyperbolic
,

parabolic
or
spherical
.

Consider the curve
c\colon I\to\mathcal{H}(N_{c},c)
given by

c(\theta)=x_{c}+r\cos(\theta)e_{n+1}+r\sin(\theta)\eta_{c},

where

(9)

\eta_{c}=-\frac{c}{\sqrt{1+c^{2}}}e_{n}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+c^{2}}}e_{1}.

Here
I=(-\pi,\pi]
,
I=(-\pi,\pi)
or
I=[-\pi+\arccos(r),\pi-\arccos(r)]
,
depending on whether we are in the hyperbolic, parabolic or spherical case, respectively.
Notice that
\Theta(c(I))
is contained in a totally geodesic
\mathbb{H}^{2}\times\mathbb{S}^{1}\subset\mathbb{H}^{n}\times\mathbb{S}^{1}
and that, for each
\theta\in I
, the point
\Theta(c(\theta))
lies in a different slice
\mathbb{H}^{n}\times\{q\}
,
q\in\mathbb{S}^{1}
.
Since the the subgroup of isometries
O(U_{1}^{\perp})\times\{I\}
acts on each slice, and this action is transitive on the intersection
\Theta(\mathbb{S}^{n}(x_{c},r)\cap\mathcal{H}(N_{c},c))\cap\mathbb{H}^{n}\times\{q\}
of the umbilical submanifold
\Theta(\mathbb{S}^{n}(x_{c},r)\cap\mathcal{H}(N_{c},c))
with each slice
\mathbb{H}^{n}\times\{q\}
, it follows as before that
\Theta(\mathbb{S}^{n}(x_{c},r)\cap\mathcal{H}(N_{c},c))
is generated by the action of
O(U_{1}^{\perp})\times\{I\}
on the curve

\Theta(c(I))
.

As for the umbilical submanifolds in the one-parameter family
\{\mathbb{S}^{n}(x_{c},\sqrt{1+c^{2}})\cap\mathcal{H}(N_{c},c)
,
c>0
, they are always hyperbolic. Here we define
c\colon(-\pi,\pi]\to\mathcal{H}(N_{c},c)
by

c(\theta)=x_{c}+\sqrt{1+c^{2}}\cos(\theta)e_{n+1}+\sqrt{1+c^{2}}\sin(\theta)\eta_{c},

where
\eta_{c}
is given by (
9
). Then we see as in the previous case that the submanifold
\Theta(\{\mathbb{S}^{n}(x_{c},\sqrt{1+c^{2}})\cap\mathcal{H}(N_{c},c))
is generated by the action of
O(U_{1}^{\perp})\times\{I\}
on the curve
\Theta(c(I))
.

Abstract
. A circulant graph is a Cayley graph of a finite cyclic group. The Weisfeiler-Leman-dimension of a circulant graph X X with respect to the class of all circulant graphs is the smallest positive integer m m such that the m m -dimensional Weisfeiler-Leman algorithm correctly tests the isomorphism between X X and any other circulant graph. It is proved that for a circulant graph of order n n this dimension is less than or equal to Ω ⁡ ( n ) + 3 \Omega(n)+3 , where Ω ⁡ ( n ) \Omega(n) is the total number of prime divisors of n n .



1.
Introduction

The Weisfeiler-Leman algorithm was initially proposed in
[
19
]
for testing graph isomorphism. It is in this capacity that a multidimensional variant of this algorithm was used in Babai’s fundamental result
[
1
]
on the graph isomorphism problem. For a positive integer
m
and an arbitrary graph
X
(directed or undirected), the
m
-dimensional Weisfeiler-Leman algorithm (the
m
-dim
\WL
algorithm) canonically constructs a special coloring of the
m
-tuples consisting of vertices of
X
. Two given graphs are declared isomorphic if the resulting colorings do not distinguish these graphs; in this case the graphs are said to be
\WL_{m}
-equivalent
. The algorithm works correctly if the
\WL_{m}
-equivalence of the input graphs implies that they are isomorphic. In many cases, the
m
-dim
\WL
algorithm works correctly already for small
m
, for example, if one of the input graphs is planar, one can take
m=3

[
14
]
. However, there are pairs of
n
-vertex graphs with arbitrary large
n
, for which
m
cannot be taken less than
cn
for some constant
0<c<1
, see
[
2
]
.

A natural formalism closely related with the above discussion was proposed in monograph
[
11
]
. A
Weisfeiler-Leman dimension
(
\WL
-dimension
) of a graph
X
is defined to be the smallest positive integer
m
such that any graph
X^{\prime}
which is
\WL_{m}
-equivalent to the graph
X
is isomorphic to
X
. More or less recent results about the
\WL
-dimension of graphs in some special classes can be found in, e.g.,
[
12
]
. When
X
belongs to a fixed class
{\mathfrak{K}}
of graphs and
X^{\prime}
runs over
{\mathfrak{K}}
, we speak of the
\WL
-dimension of
X
with respect to
{\mathfrak{K}}
.

In the present paper we focus on the following question: how large can be the
\WL
-dimension of a circulant graph, i.e., a Cayley graph of a cyclic group. It is known that the
\WL
-dimension of a random circulant graph is at most
2

[
18
]
, and that of every circulant graph of prime power order is at most
3

[
17
]
; the latter upper bound is tight. At present, it is not clear whether it is possible to bound the
\WL
-dimension of an arbitrary circulant graph by a constant not depending on the order of a graph. Our main result (Theorem
1.1
below) shows that the
\WL
-dimension of any circulant graph of order
n
with respect to the class of all circulant graphs is at most
O(\log n)
.

Theorem 1.1
.

The
\WL
-dimension of any circulant graph of order
n
with respect to the class of all circulant graphs is at most
\Omega(n)+3
, where
\Omega(n)
is the total number of prime divisors of
n
.

It seems that the bound
\Omega(n)+3
in Theorem
1.1
is not tight. At least, it can be reduced to
3
if
n
is a power of a prime. Moreover, it is not known whether the
\WL
-dimension of any circulant graph with respect to the class of all circulant graphs can be bounded from above by a constant. Finally, it is not clear whether Theorem
1.1
remains true if one removes the phrase “with respect to the class of all circulant graphs”.

Let us discuss the main ideas in the proof of Theorem
1.1
. First, we note that each graph
X
is associated with a coherent configuration
{\cal X}=\WL(X)
which can be thought as an arc-colored graph of a special form (the exact definition of coherent configuration is given in Section
2
). The concept of the
\WL
-dimension is naturally extended to coherent configurations (see Section
3
), and then we need to estimate the
\WL
-dimension
\dim_{\scriptscriptstyle\WL}({\cal X})
of the coherent configuration
{\cal X}
. In this case,
{\cal X}
is a Cayley scheme over a cyclic group or, in other terminology, a circulant scheme, and we can take advantage of the theory of such schemes, developed in series of papers
[
6
,
7
,
8
,
9
,
10
]
. The relevant details of this theory are presented in Section
4
and Subsection
5.1
.

When passing from graphs to coherent configurations, the condition of
\WL_{m}
-equivalence of two graphs
X
and
X^{\prime}
transforms into the condition of the existence of an algebraic isomorphism
\varphi:\WL(X)\to\WL(X^{\prime})
, the restriction of which to any
m
-vertex subset is induced by isomorphism (Lemma
7.2
). By Muzychuk’s theorem (Theorem
4.1
), algebraically isomorphic circulant schemes are isomorphic and therefore we may assume that
\WL(X)=\WL(X^{\prime})
, and that
\varphi
is an algebraic automorphism of the scheme
{\cal X}=\WL(X)
.

In a special class of quasinormal circulant schemes, each algebraic isomorphism is uniquely defined by a multiplier (Lemma
7.3
), that is, a system of consistent automorphisms of some sections of the underlying cyclic group. The set of these sections is uniquely determined by the scheme
{\cal X}
and the cardinality of this set does not exceed
\Omega(n)
. A careful analysis of the multiplier corresponding to the algebraic automorphism
\varphi
shows that if
m\geq\Omega(n)+3
, then
\varphi
is induced by the isomorphism (Theorem
7.4
). Thus if the scheme
{\cal X}
is quasinormal, then
\dim_{\scriptscriptstyle\WL}(X)=\dim_{\scriptscriptstyle\WL}({\cal X})\leq\Omega(n)+3
.

A reduction from arbitrary circulant schemes to quasinormal ones is established in Section
5
. The key point here is that any non-quasinormal scheme
{\cal X}
can be extended to a larger circulant scheme
{\cal X}^{\star}
the structure of which is, in a sense, under control. From Theorem
5.4
, it follows that
\dim_{\scriptscriptstyle\WL}({\cal X})\leq\dim_{\scriptscriptstyle\WL}({\cal X}^{\star})
. Since the scheme
{\cal X}^{\star}
is strictly larger than
{\cal X}
, we arrive at quasinormal case after finitely many extensions.

From the above discussion, it should be clear that the proof of our main result heavily uses coherent configurations, some tools to pass from
\WL_{m}
-equivalent graphs to algebraic isomorphisms, and circulant schemes. To make the paper as selfcontained as possible, we cite relevant facts in Sections
2
,
3
, and
4
.

2.
Coherent configurations

In the further presentation of the basics of coherent configurations, we follow the monograph
[
3
]
; all facts not explained in detail can be found there.

2.1.
Notation

Throughout the paper,
\Omega
denotes a finite set. For a set
\Delta\subseteq\Omega
, the Cartesian product
\Delta\times\Delta
and its diagonal are denoted by
{\bf 1}_{\Delta}
and
1_{\Delta}
, respectively. If
\Delta=\{\alpha\}
, we abbreviate
1_{\alpha}:=1_{\{\alpha\}}
. For a relation
s\subseteq{\bf 1}_{\Omega}
, we set
s^{*}=\{(\alpha,\beta):(\beta,\alpha)\in s\}
,
\alpha s=\{\beta\in\Omega:\ (\alpha,\beta)\in s\}
for all
\alpha\in\Omega
, and define
\langle{s}\rangle
as the minimal (with respect to inclusion) equivalence relation on
\Omega
, containing
s
. The
support
of
s
is defined to be the smallest set
\Delta\subseteq\Omega
such that
s\subseteq{\bf 1}_{\Omega}
.

For any collection
S
of relations, we denote by
S^{\cup}
the set of all unions of elements of
S
, and consider
S^{\cup}
as a poset with respect to inclusion. The
dot product
of relations
r,s\subseteq\Omega\times\Omega
is denoted by

r\cdot s=\{(\alpha,\beta)\!:\ (\alpha,\gamma)\in r,\ (\gamma,\beta)\in s\text{ for some }\gamma\in\Omega\}.

The set of classes of an equivalence relation
e
on
\Omega
is denoted by
\Omega/e
. For
\Delta\subseteq\Omega
, we set
\Delta/e=\Delta/e_{\Delta}
, where
e_{\Delta}={\bf 1}_{\Delta}\cap e
. If the classes of
e_{\Delta}
are singletons,
\Delta/e
is identified with
\Delta
. Given a relation
s\subseteq{\bf 1}_{\Omega}
, we put

(1)

s_{\Delta/e}=\{(\Gamma,\Gamma^{\prime})\in{\bf 1}_{\Delta/e}:s_{\Gamma,\Gamma^{\prime}}\neq\varnothing\},

where
s_{\Gamma,\Gamma^{\prime}}=s\cap(\Gamma\times\Gamma^{\prime})
, and abbreviate
s_{\Gamma}:=s_{\Gamma,\Gamma}
. For a fixed
s
, the set of all equivalence relations
e
on
\Omega
, such that
e\cdot s=s\cdot e=s
, contains the largest (with respect to inclusion) element which is denoted by
\rad(s)
and called the
radical
of
s
. Obviously,
\rad(s)\subseteq\langle{s}\rangle
.

For a set
B
of bijections
f:\Omega\to\Omega^{\prime}
, subsets
\Delta\subseteq\Omega
and
\Delta^{\prime}\subseteq\Omega^{\prime}
, equivalence relations
e
and
e^{\prime}
on
\Omega
and
\Omega^{\prime}
, respectively, we put

B^{\Delta/e,\Delta^{\prime}/e^{\prime}}=\{f^{\Delta/e}:\ f\in B,\ \Delta^{f}=\Delta^{\prime},\ e^{f}=e^{\prime}\},

where
f^{\Delta/e}
is the bijection from
\Delta/e
onto
\Delta^{\prime}/e^{\prime}
induced by
f
; we also abbreviate
B^{\Delta/e}:=B^{\Delta/e,\Delta^{\prime}/e}
if
\Delta^{\prime}
is clear from context.

Under a tuple on
\Omega
, we mean an
m
-tuple
x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{m})
for some natural number
m
. We put
\Omega(x):=\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{m}\}
. For a mapping
f
from
\Omega
to another set, we put
x^{f}=({x_{1}}^{f},\ldots,{x_{m}}^{f})
and extend this notation to sets of
m
-tuples in a natural way.

2.2.
Coherent configurations

Let
S
be a partition of
{\bf 1}_{\Omega}
. A pair
\mathcal{X}=(\Omega,S)
is called a
coherent configuration
on
\Omega
if

(CC1)

1_{\Omega}\in S^{\cup}
,

(CC2)

s^{*}\in S
for all
s\in S
,

(CC3)

given
r,s,t\in S
, the number
c_{rs}^{t}=|\alpha r\cap\beta s^{*}|
does not depend on
(\alpha,\beta)\in t
.

The number
|\Omega|
is called the
degree
of
{\cal X}
. A coherent configuration
{\cal X}
is said to be
trivial
if
S=S({\cal X})
consists of
1_{\Omega}
and its complement (unless
\Omega
is a singleton),
discrete
if
S
consists of singletons,
homogeneous
or a
scheme
if
1_{\Omega}\in S
.

With any permutation group
G\leq\sym(\Omega)
, one can associate a coherent configuration
\inv(G)=(\Omega,S)
, where
S
consists of the relations
(\alpha,\beta)^{G}=\{(\alpha^{g},\beta^{g}):\ g\in G\}
for all
\alpha,\beta\in\Omega
. A coherent configuration
{\cal X}
is said to be
regular
if
{\cal X}=\inv(G)
, where the group
G
is regular, or equivalently, if
|\alpha s|=1
for all
\alpha\in\Omega
and
s\in S
.

2.3.
Isomorphisms and algebraic isomorphisms

A
combinatorial isomorphism
or, briefly,
isomorphism
from a coherent configuration
{\cal X}=(\Omega,S)
to a coherent configuration
{\cal X}^{\prime}=(\Omega^{\prime},S^{\prime})
is defined to be a bijection
f:\Omega\rightarrow\Omega^{\prime}
such that
S^{f}=S^{\prime}
, where
S^{f}=\{s^{f}:\ s\in S\}
. In this case,
{\cal X}
and
{\cal X}^{\prime}
are said to be
isomorphic
; the set of all isomorphisms from
{\cal X}
to
{\cal X}^{\prime}
is denoted by
\iso({\cal X},{\cal X}^{\prime})
, and by
\iso({\cal X})
if
{\cal X}={\cal X}^{\prime}
. The group
\iso({\cal X})
contains a normal subgroup

\aut({\cal X})=\{f\in\sym(\Omega):\ s^{f}=s\text{ for all }s\in S\}

called the
automorphism group
of
{\cal X}
.

A bijection
\varphi:S\rightarrow S^{\prime}
is called an
algebraic isomorphism
from
{\cal X}
to
{\cal X}^{\prime}
if for all
r,s,t\in S
, we have

c_{\varphi(r)\varphi(s)}^{\varphi(t)}=c_{rs}^{t}.

In this case,
|\Omega^{\prime}|=|\Omega|
and
1_{\Omega^{\prime}}=\varphi(1_{\Omega})
. The set of all algebraic isomorphisms from
{\cal X}
to
{\cal X}^{\prime}
is denoted by
\iso_{alg}({\cal X},{\cal X}^{\prime})
, and by
\aut_{alg}({\cal X})
if
{\cal X}={\cal X}^{\prime}
.

Any isomorphism
f\in\iso({\cal X},{\cal X}^{\prime})
induces the algebraic isomorphism
\varphi_{f}:{\cal X}\to{\cal X}^{\prime}
,
s\mapsto s^{f}
. We put

\iso({\cal X},{\cal X}^{\prime},\varphi)=\{f\in\iso({\cal X},{\cal X}^{\prime}):\ \varphi_{f}=\varphi\}

and abbreviate
\iso({\cal X},\varphi):=\iso({\cal X},{\cal X},\varphi)
. Note that
\aut({\cal X})=\iso({\cal X},\id_{\cal X})
, where
\id_{\cal X}
is the trivial (identical) algebraic automorphism of
{\cal X}
. If the scheme
{\cal X}
is regular, then
\iso({\cal X},{\cal X}^{\prime},\varphi)\neq\varnothing
for any
\varphi
(see
[
3
, Theorem 2.3.33]
).

2.4.
Tensor product

Given two arbitrary coherent configurations
{\cal X}_{1}=(\Omega_{1},S_{1})
and
{\cal X}_{2}=(\Omega_{2},S_{2})
, denote by
S_{1}\otimes S_{2}
the set of all relations

s_{1}\otimes s_{2}=\left\{((\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}),(\beta_{1},\beta_{2}))\in(\Omega_{1}\times\Omega_{2})^{2}:\ (\alpha_{1},\beta_{1})\in s_{1},\ (\alpha_{2},\beta_{2})\in s_{2}\right\},

where
s_{1}\in S_{1}
and
s_{2}\in S_{2}
. Then the pair
{\cal X}_{1}\otimes{\cal X}_{2}=(\Omega_{1}\times\Omega_{2},S_{1}\otimes S_{2})
is a coherent configuration. It is called the

tensor product
of
{\cal X}_{1}
and
{\cal X}_{2}
.

2.5.
Extensions

There is a natural partial ordering
\leq
on the set of all coherent configurations on
\Omega
. Namely, given two such coherent configurations
{\cal X}
and
{\cal Y}
, we set

{\cal X}\leq{\cal Y}\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad S({\cal X})^{\cup}\subseteq S({\cal Y})^{\cup},

and say that
{\cal Y}
is the
extension
(or fission) of
{\cal X}
. The minimal and maximal elements with respect to this order are the
trivial
and
discrete
coherent configurations, respectively. It holds true that
\inv(\aut({\cal X}))\geq{\cal X}
.

The coherent configuration
\WL(X)
of a graph
X
, mentioned in the Introduction, is
precisely the minimal coherent configuration on the vertex set of
X
that contains the arc
set of
X
as the union of basis relations.

2.6.
Relations and fibers

Let
{\cal X}=(\Omega,S)
be a coherent configuration. The elements of
S
and of
S^{\cup}
are called
basis relations
and
relations
of
{\cal X}
, respectively. The basis relation containing a pair
(\alpha,\beta)\in{\bf 1}_{\Omega}
is denoted by
r_{\cal X}(\alpha,\beta)
or simply by
r(\alpha,\beta)
. The set of all relations of
{\cal X}
is closed with respect to intersections, unions, and the dot product.

Any set
\Delta\subseteq\Omega
such that
1_{\Delta}\in S
is called a
fiber
of
{\cal X}
. In view of condition (CC1), the set
F=F({\cal X})
of all fibers forms a partition of
\Omega
. Every basis relation is contained in the Cartesian product of two uniquely determined fibers.

Every algebraic isomorphism
\varphi\in\iso_{alg}({\cal X},{\cal X}^{\prime})
can be extended in a natural way to a poset isomorphism
S^{\cup}\to(S^{\prime})^{\cup}
, denoted also by
\varphi
. This poset isomorphism respects the set theoretical operations and the dot product. Furthermore,
\varphi
induces a fiber preserving poset isomorphism
F^{\cup}\to(F^{\prime})^{\cup}
,
\Delta\mapsto\Delta^{\varphi}
, where the set
\Delta^{\varphi}
is defined by the equality
\varphi(1_{\Delta})=1_{\Delta^{\varphi}}
.

Let
{\cal X}\leq{\cal Y}
and
{\cal X}^{\prime}\leq{\cal Y}^{\prime}
. We say that the algebraic isomorphism
\varphi

is extended
to an algebraic isomorphism
\psi\in\iso_{alg}({\cal Y},{\cal Y}^{\prime})
or that
\psi

extends

\varphi
if
\psi(s)=\varphi(s)
for all
s\in S
.

2.7.
Parabolics and quotients

A relation
e
of
{\cal X}
that is an equivalence relation on the support of
e
is called a
partial parabolic
of
{\cal X}
, and a
parabolic
of
{\cal X}
if the support coincides with
\Omega
. The relations
\langle{s}\rangle
and
\rad(s)
are partial parabolics for all
s\in S^{\cup}
. Given a partial parabolic
e
, we put
S_{\Omega/e}=\{s_{\Omega/e}:\ s\in S,\ s_{\Omega/e}\neq\varnothing\}
, and put
S_{\Delta}=\{s_{\Delta}:\ s\in S,\ s_{\Delta}\neq\varnothing\}
for any
\Delta\in\Omega/e
. Then

{\cal X}_{\Omega/e}=(\Omega/e,S_{\Omega/e})\quad\text{and}\quad{\cal X}_{\Delta}=(\Delta,S_{\Delta})

are coherent configurations, called a
quotient
of
{\cal X}
modulo
e
and a
restriction
of
{\cal X}
to
\Delta
, respectively. We have

F({\cal X}_{\Omega/e})=\{\Gamma_{\Omega/e}:\ \Gamma\in F,\ \Gamma_{\Omega/e}\neq\varnothing\}\quad\text{and}\quad F({\cal X}_{\Delta})=\{\Gamma\cap\Delta:\ \Gamma\in F,\ \Gamma\cap\Delta\neq\varnothing\},

respectively. In particular,
{\cal X}_{\Omega/e}
and
{\cal X}_{\Delta}
are schemes if so is
{\cal X}
.

Let
\varphi\in\iso_{alg}({\cal X},{\cal X}^{\prime})
be an algebraic isomorphism and
e
a (partial) parabolic of
{\cal X}
. Then
\varphi(e)
is a (partial) parabolic of
{\cal X}^{\prime}
with the same number of classes. Moreover,

(2)

\varphi(\langle{s}\rangle)=\langle{\varphi(s)}\rangle\quad\text{and}\quad\varphi(\rad(s))=\rad(\varphi(s)).

The algebraic isomorphism
\varphi
induces the algebraic isomorphism

(3)

\varphi_{\Omega/e}:{\cal X}_{\Omega/e}\to{\cal X}^{\prime}_{\Omega^{\prime}/e^{\prime}},\ s_{\Omega/e}\mapsto s^{\prime}_{\Omega^{\prime}/e^{\prime}},

where
e^{\prime}=\varphi(e)
and
s^{\prime}=\varphi(s)
. Now let
\Delta\in\Omega/e
and
\Delta^{\prime}\in\Omega/e^{\prime}
. Assume that there is
\Gamma\in F
such that
\Delta\cap\Gamma\neq\varnothing\neq\Delta^{\prime}\cap\Gamma^{\prime}
, where
\Gamma^{\prime}=\Gamma^{\varphi}
. Then
\varphi
induces an algebraic isomorphism

(4)

\varphi_{\Delta,\Delta^{\prime}}:{\cal X}_{\Delta}\to{\cal X}^{\prime}_{\Delta^{\prime}},\ s_{\Delta}\mapsto s^{\prime}_{\Delta^{\prime}}.

This algebraic isomorphism always exists for all
\Delta
and
\Delta^{\prime}
if
{\cal X}
(and hence
{\cal X}^{\prime}
) is a scheme.

2.8.
Sections

Let
{\cal X}
be a coherent configuration,
e
a partial parabolic of
{\cal X}
, and
\Delta
a class of a partial parabolic containing
e
. The quotient set
{\mathfrak{S}}=\Delta/e
is called a
section
of
{\cal X}
. Any element of
{\mathfrak{S}}
is of the form
\alpha_{\mathfrak{S}}:=\alpha e
for some
\alpha\in\Delta
; we extend this notation by setting
\alpha_{\mathfrak{S}}=\varnothing
for all
\alpha\not\in\Delta
. For any
s\in S
, we define the relation
s_{\mathfrak{S}}
on
{\mathfrak{S}}
by formula (
1
).

The set of all sections of
{\cal X}
is denoted by
\secc({\cal X})
. It is partially ordered, namely,
\Delta/e\preceq\Delta^{\prime}/e^{\prime}
, whenever
\Delta\subseteq\Delta^{\prime}
and
e^{\prime}\subseteq e
. For a section
{\mathfrak{S}}=\Delta/e\in\secc({\cal X})
, we put
{\cal X}_{\mathfrak{S}}=({\cal X}_{\Delta})_{\Delta/e}
.

Let
\varphi\in\iso_{alg}({\cal X},{\cal X}^{\prime})
,
e^{\prime}=\varphi(e)
, and
\Delta^{\prime}
a class of the
\varphi
-image of the partial parabolic of
{\cal X}
, containing the class
\Delta
. The algebraic isomorphisms (
3
) and (
4
) induce the algebraic isomorphism

\varphi_{{\mathfrak{S}},{\mathfrak{S}}^{\prime}}=(\varphi_{\Delta,\Delta^{\prime}})_{\Delta/e,\Delta^{\prime}/e^{\prime}}

from the coherent configurations
{\cal X}_{\mathfrak{S}}
to the coherent configuration
{\cal X}^{\prime}_{{\mathfrak{S}}^{\prime}}
.

2.9.
Point extensions

Let
m\geq 1
be an integer and
x\in\Omega^{m}
.
The
point extension

{\cal X}_{x}
of the coherent configuration
{\cal X}
with respect to
x
is defined to be the smallest coherent configuration that is an extension of
{\cal X}
, and contains the singleton relations
1_{x_{1}},\ldots,1_{x_{m}}
. Clearly, the point extension
{\cal X}_{x}
depends on the set
\Omega(x)
only. When the tuple is irrelevant, we use the term “point extension”, and “one-point extension” if
m=1
.

Let
\varphi\in\iso_{alg}({\cal X},{\cal X}^{\prime})
, and let
x\in\Omega^{m}
,
x^{\prime}\in{\Omega^{\prime}}^{m}
. An algebraic isomorphism
\psi\in\iso_{alg}({\cal X}_{x},{\cal X}^{\prime}_{x^{\prime}})
is called an
(x,x^{\prime})
-extension
of
\varphi
if it extends
\varphi
and

\psi(1_{x_{i}})=1_{x^{\prime}_{i}},\quad i=1,\ldots,m.

Note that the
(x,x^{\prime})
-extension is unique if it exists. The following statement is straightforward.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
{\cal X}
and
{\cal X}^{\prime}
be schemes,
{\mathfrak{S}}\in\secc({\cal X})
and
{\mathfrak{S}}^{\prime}\in\secc({\cal X}^{\prime})
, and

\varphi\in\iso_{alg}({\cal X},{\cal X}^{\prime})\quad\text{and}\quad\varphi_{{\mathfrak{S}},{\mathfrak{S}}^{\prime}}\in\iso_{alg}({\cal X}_{{\mathfrak{S}}},{\cal X}^{\prime}_{{\mathfrak{S}}^{\prime}}).

If
\varphi
has the
(x,x^{\prime})
-extension for some tuples
x
and
x^{\prime}
, then
\varphi_{{\mathfrak{S}},{\mathfrak{S}}^{\prime}}
has the
(x_{{\mathfrak{S}}},x^{\prime}_{{\mathfrak{S}}^{\prime}})
-extension, where
x_{{\mathfrak{S}}}
(respectively,
x^{\prime}_{{\mathfrak{S}}^{\prime}}
) is a tuple with nonempty entries
(x_{i})_{{\mathfrak{S}}}
(respectively,
(x_{i}^{\prime})_{{\mathfrak{S}}^{\prime}}
).

3.
The WL-dimension of coherent configurations

In this section, we give the definition of the
\WL
-dimension of coherent configuration in terms of
m
-ary coherent configurations,
m\geq 2
. The details can be found in
[
4
,
5
,
17
]
.

3.1.
m
-ary coherent configurations

Let
m\geq 1
be an integer. For a tuple
x\in\Omega^{m}
, denote by
\rho(x)
the equivalence relation on
M=\{1,\ldots,m\}
such that
(i,j)\in\rho(x)
if and only if
x_{i}=x_{j}
. The monoid of all maps from
M
to itself is denoted by
\mon(M)
. Given
\sigma\in\mon(M)
, we set

x^{\sigma}=(x_{1^{\sigma}},\ldots,x_{m^{\sigma}}).

Let
{\mathfrak{X}}
be a partition of
\Omega^{m}
. The class of
{\mathfrak{X}}
, containing
x\in\Omega^{m}
, is denoted by
[x]
. Given
X_{1},\ldots,X_{m}\in{\mathfrak{X}}
, we denote by
n(x;X_{1},\ldots,X_{m})
the number of all
\alpha\in\Omega
such that
x_{i\leftarrow\alpha}\in X_{i}
for all
i\in M
, where

x_{i\leftarrow\alpha}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{i-1},\alpha,x_{i+1},\ldots,x_{m}).

A partition
{\mathfrak{X}}
of
\Omega^{m}
is called an
m
-ary coherent configuration
on
\Omega
if the following conditions are satisfied for all
X\in{\mathfrak{X}}
:

(CC1’)

\rho(x)
does not depend on
x\in X
,

(CC2’)

X^{\sigma}\in{\mathfrak{X}}

4.
Circulant schemes

There is a one-to-one correspondence between circulant schemes (defined below) and the Schur rings over cyclic groups (see, e.g.,
[
3
, Theorem 2.4.16]
). One should not be embarrassed that most results cited in the present paper were formulated and proved in the language of Schur rings. In the rest of this section, we will change our terminology slightly to match that used for circulant schemes (and Schur rings). In this section,
G
is a cyclic group,
{{\gothfamily 1}}_{G}
denotes the identity element of
G
, and
G_{right}\leq\sym(G)
is a group induced by right multiplications of
G

[
3

.

4.1.
Definitions

A coherent configuration
{\cal X}
on the elements of
G
is said to be
circulant scheme
over
G
if the group
\aut({\cal X})
contains the subgroup
G_{right}
, or equivalently

{\cal X}\leq\inv(G_{right}).

Every relation of
{\cal X}
defines a Cayley graph of
G
, i.e., a circulant graph. Vice versa, if
X
is a circulant graph, then
\WL(X)
is a circulant scheme on the same group.

Every subgroup
H\leq G
defines an equivalence relation
e:=e(H)
on the elements of
G
, the classes of which are the cosets of
H
in
G
. In particular,
e({{\gothfamily 1}}_{G})=1_{G}
and
e(G)={\bf 1}_{G}
. Furthermore,
H=\alpha e
with
\alpha={{\gothfamily 1}}_{G}
, and
H\leq H^{\prime}
if and only if
e(H)\subseteq e(H^{\prime})
. A subgroup
H\leq G
is called an
{\cal X}
-group
if the equivalence relation
e(H)
is a parabolic of
{\cal X}
. The mapping
H\mapsto e(H)
defines a one-to-one correspondence between the
{\cal X}
-groups and parabolics of
{\cal X}
. If
s
is a relation of
{\cal X}
and
\alpha={{\gothfamily 1}}_{G}

and

correspond to the parabolics
\langle{s}\rangle
and
\rad(s)
, respectively. Put

\secc_{1}({\cal X})=\{{\mathfrak{S}}\in\secc({\cal X}):\ {{\gothfamily 1}}_{\mathfrak{S}}\neq\varnothing\}.

Let
{\mathfrak{S}}\in\secc_{1}({\cal X})
. We associate with
{\mathfrak{S}}
the groups

and

, for which

-groups and

can naturally be identified with cyclic group

Thus

{\mathfrak{S}}
is

if the scheme

is trivial. Note that
{\mathfrak{S}}
is a subsection of a section

L({\mathfrak{T}})\leq L
and

.

Let
U
and

be
{\cal X}
-groups such that

satisfies the

or

{{\gothfamily 1}}_{G}\in\Omega(x)
and for each section
{\mathfrak{S}}\in\secc_{1}({\cal X})
of prime power order, there is
g\in\Omega(x)
such that
{\mathfrak{S}}=\langle{g_{\mathfrak{S}}}\rangle
. A trivial example of a base tuple is given by any
x
for which
\Omega(x)=G
.

Lemma 6.3
.

For any circulant scheme of degree
n
, there exists a base
m
-tuple with
m\leq\Omega(n)+1
.

Proof.

Denote by
{\cal H}
the set of all subgroups of
G
of prime power order. For each
H\in{\cal H}
, we choose an arbitrary generator
g_{H}
of the group
H
. Then any point tuple
x
with
\Omega(x)=\{g_{H}:\ H\in{\cal H}\}
is a base tuple for
{\cal X}
: indeed, if
{\mathfrak{S}}\in\secc_{1}({\cal X})
is of prime power order
p^{k}
for some
k\geq 1
, then the set
U({\mathfrak{S}})\setminus L({\mathfrak{S}})
contains
g_{H}
, where
H
is the Sylow
p
-subgroup of
U({\mathfrak{S}})
. Since the group
G
is cyclic, we have
|{\cal H}|\leq\Omega(n)+1
, as required.
∎

The following statement shows that if
x
is a base tuple for a quasinormal scheme
{\cal X}
, then the set
\Omega(x)
controls, in a sense, the sections belonging to the set
\secc_{0}({\cal X})
.

Theorem 6.4
.

Let
x
be a base tuple for a quasinormal circulant scheme
{\cal X}
. Then for any section
{\mathfrak{S}}\in\secc_{0}({\cal X})
, the coherent configuration
{\cal X}_{x,{\mathfrak{S}}}
is discrete.

Proof.

First, let
{\mathfrak{S}}\in\secc_{0}({\cal X})
be a normal principle section. Then for any prime
p
dividing
|{\mathfrak{S}}|
, the Sylow
p
-subgroup
{\mathfrak{S}}_{p}
of
{\mathfrak{S}}
and its complement subgroup
{\mathfrak{S}}_{p^{\prime}}
are
{\cal X}_{\mathfrak{S}}
-groups. Since
x
is a base tuple, there exists
g\in\Omega(x)
such that
U({\mathfrak{S}}_{p})=\langle{g}\rangle
. Put
{\mathfrak{s}}_{p}=g_{\mathfrak{S}}
. Then

\langle{{\mathfrak{s}}_{p}}\rangle={\mathfrak{S}}_{p}\quad\text{and}\quad\{{\mathfrak{s}}_{p}\}\in F({\cal X}_{x,{\mathfrak{S}}}),

where the latter holds true, because
g\in\Omega(x)
and hence the singleton
\{g\}
is a fiber of the coherent configuration
{\cal X}_{g}\leq{\cal X}_{x}
. By the same reason,
\{{{\gothfamily 1}}_{\mathfrak{S}}\}\in F({\cal X}_{x,{\mathfrak{S}}})
, and hence
{\mathfrak{S}}_{p^{\prime}}
is a homogeneity set of
{\cal X}_{x,{\mathfrak{S}}}
. It easily follows that

(23)

{\mathfrak{s}}_{p}{\mathfrak{S}}_{p^{\prime}}\in F({\cal X}_{x,{\mathfrak{S}}})^{\cup}.

Note that the intersection of the sets
{\mathfrak{s}}_{p}{\mathfrak{S}}_{p^{\prime}}\subseteq{\mathfrak{S}}
over all
p
dividing
|{\mathfrak{S}}|
is equal to a singleton
\{{\mathfrak{s}}\}
, where
{\mathfrak{s}}=\prod_{p}{\mathfrak{s}}_{p}\in{\mathfrak{S}}
. In view of (
23
), this singleton is a homogeneity set of the coherent configuration
{\cal X}_{x,{\mathfrak{S}}}
; in particular,
\{{\mathfrak{s}}\}\in F({\cal X}_{x,{\mathfrak{S}}})
. On the other hand, the element
{\mathfrak{s}}
generates the group
{\mathfrak{S}}
(see above). Thus, the coherent configuration

{\cal X}_{x,{\mathfrak{S}}}\geq({\cal X}_{\mathfrak{S}})_{{{\gothfamily 1}}_{\mathfrak{S}},{\mathfrak{s}}}

is discrete by Lemma
4.3
(1).

Now, let
{\mathfrak{S}}\in\secc_{0}({\cal X})
be an arbitrary section. It is of the form described in Lemma
4.4
. In the notation of that lemma, from
[
3
, Theorem 3.2.17]
, it follows that

{\cal X}_{x,{\mathfrak{S}}}\geq({\cal X}_{x,{\mathfrak{S}}})_{{\mathfrak{S}}_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes({\cal X}_{x,{\mathfrak{S}}})_{{\mathfrak{S}}_{k}}={\cal X}_{x,{\mathfrak{S}}_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes{\cal X}_{x,{\mathfrak{S}}_{k}}.

It suffices to verify that the coherent configuration
{\cal X}_{x,{\mathfrak{S}}_{i}}
is discrete,
i=1,\ldots,k
. Recall that the section
{\mathfrak{S}}_{i}
is projectively equivalent to a subsection
{\mathfrak{T}}
of some principal normal section
{\mathfrak{T}}^{\prime}\in\secc_{0}({\cal X})
. By above, the coherent configuration
{\cal X}_{x,{\mathfrak{T}}^{\prime}}
and hence the coherent configuration
{\cal X}_{x,{\mathfrak{T}}}=({\cal X}_{x,{\mathfrak{T}}^{\prime}})_{\mathfrak{T}}
is discrete. By Lemma
6.2
(2), this implies that all fibers of the coherent configuration
{\cal X}_{x,{\mathfrak{S}}}
are singletons, i.e., this coherent configuration is discrete.
∎

7.
Extendable algebraic isomorphisms and multipliers

7.1.
Extendable algebraic isomorphisms

Let
{\cal X}
and
{\cal X}^{\prime}
be coherent configurations and
x
a point tuple of
{\cal X}
. An algebraic isomorphism
\varphi\in\iso_{alg}({\cal X},{\cal X}^{\prime})
is said to be
extendable
at
x
if
\varphi
has the
(x,x^{\prime})
-extension for some point tuple
x^{\prime}
of
{\cal X}^{\prime}
. Let
m
be a nonnegative integer. If the algebraic isomorphism
\varphi
is extendable at every
m
-tuple, then
\varphi
is said to be
m
-extendable
. Thus the usual algebraic isomorphisms are
0
-extendable, whereas the sesquiclosed algebraic isomorphisms defined in
[
17
]
are
1
-extendable.

Lemma 7.1
.

Let
{\cal X}
and
{\cal X}^{\prime}
be schemes,
\varphi\in\iso_{alg}({\cal X},{\cal X}^{\prime})
, and
m
a nonnegative integer. Assume that
\varphi
is
m
-extendable. Then

(i)

\varphi
is
k
-extendable for all
0\leq k\leq m
,

(ii)

if
{\mathfrak{S}}\in\secc({\cal X})
,
{\mathfrak{S}}^{\prime}\in\secc({\cal X}^{\prime})
, and
\varphi_{{\mathfrak{S}},{\mathfrak{S}}^{\prime}}\in\iso_{alg}({\cal X}_{{\mathfrak{S}}},{\cal X}^{\prime}_{{\mathfrak{S}}^{\prime}})
is defined, then
\varphi_{{\mathfrak{S}},{\mathfrak{S}}^{\prime}}
is
m
-extendable.

Proof.

Let
y
be a point
k
-tuple of
{\cal X}
. Choose any
m
-tuple
x
such that
\pr_{k}(x)=y
. By the lemma condition, there is an
(x,x^{\prime})
-extension
\psi
of the algebraic isomorphism
\varphi
for some point
m
-tuple
x^{\prime}
of
{\cal X}^{\prime}
. The restriction of
\psi
to the coherent configuration
{\cal X}_{y}
is obviously the
(y,y^{\prime})
-extension of
\varphi
. This proves statement (i). Statement (ii) follows from Lemma
2.1
.
∎

The concept of
m
-extendable algebraic isomorphism can be used to establish a necessary condition for two schemes to be
\WL_{m}
-equivalent. More exactly, the following statement immediately follows from
[
17
, Theorem 3.7]
.

Lemma 7.2
.

Let
m\geq 2
be an integer. Assume that two schemes are
\WL_{m}
-equivalent with respect to an algebraic isomorphism. Then it is
(m-2)
-extendable.

7.2.
Multipliers of circulant schemes

In the rest of this section we show that if
{\cal X}
is a quasinormal circulant scheme, then every
m
-extendable algebraic automorphism of
{\cal X}
for sufficiently large
m
is induced by an isomorphism. We begin with an auxiliary statement showing that in this case the algebraic automorphism in question is associated with a special system of group automorphisms parametrized by the
{\cal X}
-sections belonging to the set
\secc_{0}({\cal X})
.

Lemma 7.3
.

Let
{\cal X}
be a quasinormal circulant scheme over a group
G
, and
\varphi
an algebraic automorphism of
{\cal X}
. Assume that
\varphi
is extendable at a base tuple
x\in G^{m}
. Then there exists a family

(24)

{\mathfrak{M}}={\mathfrak{M}}(\varphi,x)=\{\sigma_{\mathfrak{S}}\in\aut({\mathfrak{S}}):\ {\mathfrak{S}}\in\secc_{0}({\cal X})\}

such that for all sections
{\mathfrak{S}},{\mathfrak{T}}\in\secc_{0}({\cal X})
the following conditions are satisfied:

(M1)

\sigma_{\mathfrak{S}}\in\iso({\cal X}_{\mathfrak{S}},\varphi_{\mathfrak{S}})
,

(M2)

if
{\mathfrak{T}}\succeq{\mathfrak{S}}
, then
(\sigma_{\mathfrak{T}})^{\mathfrak{S}}=\sigma_{\mathfrak{S}}
,

(M3)

if
{\mathfrak{T}}\sim{\mathfrak{S}}
, then
(\sigma_{{\mathfrak{T}}})^{f_{{\mathfrak{T}},{\mathfrak{S}}}}=\sigma_{\mathfrak{S}}
,
where
f_{{\mathfrak{T}},{\mathfrak{S}}}
is the mapping (
11
).

Proof.

Let
x^{\prime}\in G^{m}
be an arbitrary
m
-tuple such that the algebraic automorphism
\varphi
can be extended to an algebraic isomorphism
\psi\in\iso_{alg}({\cal X}_{x},{\cal X}_{x^{\prime}})
. Let
{\mathfrak{S}}\in\secc_{0}({\cal X})
. By Theorem
6.4
, the coherent configuration
{\cal X}_{x,{\mathfrak{S}}}
is discrete. Consequently, the algebraic isomorphism
\psi_{\mathfrak{S}}:{\cal X}_{x,{\mathfrak{S}}}\to{\cal X}_{x^{\prime},{\mathfrak{S}}}
is induced by a (uniquely defined) permutation
\sigma_{\mathfrak{S}}\in\sym({\mathfrak{S}})
such that

(25)

\psi_{\mathfrak{S}}(1_{\mathfrak{s}})=1_{{\mathfrak{s}}^{\sigma}}\ \,\text{for all}\ \,{\mathfrak{s}}\in{\mathfrak{S}},

where
\sigma=\sigma_{\mathfrak{S}}
.
First we verify that this permutation satisfies the conditions (M1), (M2), (M3).

To prove (M1), let
{\mathfrak{S}}\in\secc_{0}({\cal X})
. Since
{\cal X}_{x,{\mathfrak{S}}}\geq{\cal X}_{\mathfrak{S}}
and
\psi
extends
\varphi
, we conclude that
\psi_{\mathfrak{S}}
extends
\varphi_{\mathfrak{S}}
, and hence
\iso({\cal X}_{x,{\mathfrak{S}}},\psi_{\mathfrak{S}})\subseteq\iso({\cal X}_{\mathfrak{S}},\varphi_{\mathfrak{S}})
. On the other hand,
\sigma_{\mathfrak{S}}\in\iso({\cal X}_{x,{\mathfrak{S}}},\psi_{\mathfrak{S}})
. Thus,
\sigma_{\mathfrak{S}}\in\iso({\cal X}_{\mathfrak{S}},\varphi_{\mathfrak{S}})
.

To prove (M2), let
{\mathfrak{S}},{\mathfrak{T}}\in\secc_{0}({\cal X})
be such that
{\mathfrak{T}}\succeq{\mathfrak{S}}
. Then, obviously,
(\psi_{\mathfrak{T}})_{\mathfrak{S}}=\psi_{\mathfrak{S}}
. Take any
{\mathfrak{s}}\in{\mathfrak{S}}
and choose any
{\mathfrak{t}}\in{\mathfrak{T}}
such that
{\mathfrak{t}}_{\mathfrak{S}}={\mathfrak{s}}
. Then, by formula (
25
), we have

1_{({\mathfrak{s}})^{\mu^{\mathfrak{S}}}}=1_{{({\mathfrak{t}}_{\mathfrak{S}})}^{\mu^{\mathfrak{S}}}}=(1_{{\mathfrak{t}}^{\mu}})_{\mathfrak{S}}=(\psi_{\mathfrak{T}}(1_{\mathfrak{t}}))_{\mathfrak{S}}=[(\psi_{\mathfrak{T}})_{\mathfrak{S}}](1_{\mathfrak{s}})=\psi_{\mathfrak{S}}(1_{\mathfrak{s}})=1_{{\mathfrak{s}}^{\sigma}},

where
\mu=\sigma_{\mathfrak{T}}
. Hence,
{\mathfrak{s}}^{\mu^{\mathfrak{S}}}={\mathfrak{s}}^{\sigma}
for all
{\mathfrak{s}}\in{\mathfrak{S}}
, whence
(\sigma_{\mathfrak{T}})^{\mathfrak{S}}=\mu^{\mathfrak{S}}=\sigma=\sigma_{\mathfrak{S}}
.

To prove (M3), let
{\mathfrak{S}},{\mathfrak{T}}\in\secc_{0}({\cal X})
be such that
{\mathfrak{T}}\sim{\mathfrak{S}}
. Without loss of generality, we may assume that
{\mathfrak{S}}
is a multiple of
{\mathfrak{T}}
: indeed, the general case reduces to the case when one of the sections
{\mathfrak{S}}
and
{\mathfrak{T}}
is a multiple of the other, and then we make use of the fact that
f_{{\mathfrak{S}},{\mathfrak{T}}}=(f_{{\mathfrak{T}},{\mathfrak{S}}})^{-1}
. Thus we need to verify that

{\mathfrak{t}}^{\sigma_{\mathfrak{T}}f_{{\mathfrak{T}},{\mathfrak{S}}}}={\mathfrak{s}}^{\sigma_{\mathfrak{S}}}\ \,\text{for all}\ \,{\mathfrak{t}}\in{\mathfrak{T}},

where
{\mathfrak{s}}={\mathfrak{t}}^{f_{{\mathfrak{T}},{\mathfrak{S}}}}
. By Lemma
6.2
(1), the set
{\mathfrak{t}}
treated as a subset of
G
is a homogeneity set of the coherent configuration
{\cal X}_{x}
. Therefore the set
{\mathfrak{t}}^{\psi}
is also a homogeneity set of
{\cal X}_{x}
. Furthermore,

({\mathfrak{t}}^{\psi})_{\mathfrak{T}}=({\mathfrak{t}}_{\mathfrak{T}})^{\psi_{\mathfrak{T}}}=\{{\mathfrak{t}}\}^{\psi_{\mathfrak{T}}}=\{{\mathfrak{t}}^{\sigma_{\mathfrak{T}}}\}.

Consequently,
{\mathfrak{t}}^{\psi}\subseteq{\mathfrak{t}}^{\sigma_{\mathfrak{T}}}
. The converse inclusion can be verified analogously. Thus,
{\mathfrak{t}}^{\psi}={\mathfrak{t}}^{\sigma_{\mathfrak{T}}}
and, similarly,
{\mathfrak{s}}^{\psi}={\mathfrak{s}}^{\sigma_{\mathfrak{S}}}
. Now, since
{\mathfrak{s}}={\mathfrak{t}}^{f_{{\mathfrak{T}},{\mathfrak{S}}}}
, Lemma
6.1
implies that
{\mathfrak{t}}\supseteq{\mathfrak{s}}
. Consequently,
{\mathfrak{t}}^{\psi}\supseteq{\mathfrak{s}}^{\psi}
, which by above shows that
{\mathfrak{t}}^{\sigma_{\mathfrak{T}}}\supseteq{\mathfrak{s}}^{\sigma_{\mathfrak{S}}}
. Thus,
{\mathfrak{t}}^{\sigma_{\mathfrak{T}}f_{{\mathfrak{T}},{\mathfrak{S}}}}={\mathfrak{s}}^{\sigma_{\mathfrak{S}}}
again by Lemma
6.1
.

It remains to verify that
\sigma_{\mathfrak{S}}\in\aut({\mathfrak{S}})
for all
{\mathfrak{S}}\in\secc_{0}({\cal X})
. This follows from condition (M1) and Lemma
4.3
(2) if
{\mathfrak{S}}
is a principal normal section. Let
{\mathfrak{S}}
be a subsection of a principal normal section
{\mathfrak{S}}^{\prime}\in\secc_{0}({\cal X})
such that
{\mathfrak{S}}\sim{\mathfrak{T}}\preceq{\mathfrak{S}}^{\prime}
for some section
{\mathfrak{T}}\in\secc_{1}({\cal X})
. By condition (M2), we have

\sigma_{\mathfrak{T}}=(\sigma_{{\mathfrak{S}}^{\prime}})^{\mathfrak{T}}\in\aut({\mathfrak{S}}^{\prime})^{\mathfrak{T}}=\aut({\mathfrak{T}}).

By condition (M3) and Lemma
4.2
, this implies that

\sigma_{\mathfrak{S}}=f_{{\mathfrak{S}},{\mathfrak{T}}}\,\sigma_{\mathfrak{T}}\,{f_{{\mathfrak{S}},{\mathfrak{T}}}}^{-1}\in f_{{\mathfrak{S}},{\mathfrak{T}}}\,\aut({\mathfrak{T}})\,{f_{{\mathfrak{S}},{\mathfrak{T}}}}^{-1}=\aut({\mathfrak{S}}).

Finally, assume that the section
{\mathfrak{S}}\in\secc_{0}({\cal X})
is arbitrary. It is of the form (
12
), see Lemma
4.4
. In the notation of that lemma, from the condition (M2) and the above, it follows that

\sigma_{\mathfrak{S}}=(\sigma_{\mathfrak{S}})^{{\mathfrak{S}}_{1}}\cdot\ldots\cdot(\sigma_{\mathfrak{S}})^{{\mathfrak{S}}_{k}}=\sigma_{{\mathfrak{S}}_{1}}\cdot\ldots\cdot\sigma_{{\mathfrak{S}}_{k}}\in\aut({\mathfrak{S}}_{1})\times\cdots\times\aut({\mathfrak{S}}_{k})=\aut({\mathfrak{S}}),

as required.
∎

From the proof of Lemma
7.3
, it follows that the automorphisms
\sigma_{\mathfrak{S}}
in the statement of that lemma depend not only on the
m
-tuple
x
but also on the choice of the
m
-tuple
x^{\prime}
. However, this dependence is not essential for further exposition.

At this point, we make use of some results proved in papers
[
9
]
and
[
10
]
. We apply these results to quasinormal circulant schemes. It should be noted that any such scheme is
quasidence
in terms of that papers. Indeed, every trivial
{\cal X}
-section is projectively equivalent to a subsection of a normal section and hence is of prime order. Moreover, the quasinormality implies also that the set
\secc_{0}({\cal X})
defined in our paper coincides with the set
{\mathfrak{S}}_{0}({\cal X})
used in that papers. Thus any family (
24
) satisfying the conditions (M1), (M2), and (M3) is (in terms of that papers) an
{\cal X}
-multiplier
, see
[
10
, Definition 5.1]
. Based on the theory of
{\cal X}
-multipliers and using Lemma
7.3
, we can prove the main result of this section.

Theorem 7.4
.

Let
{\cal X}
be a quasinormal circulant scheme. Then any algebraic automorphism of
{\cal X}
, that is extendable at a base tuple for
{\cal X}
, is induced by an isomorphism.

Proof.

Assume that
\varphi\in\aut_{alg}({\cal X})
is extendable at a base tuple
x
for
{\cal X}
. By Lemma
7.3
, there exists an
{\cal X}
-multiplier
{\mathfrak{M}}={\mathfrak{M}}(\varphi,x)
. Denote by
{\cal X}_{0}
the
coset closure
of the scheme
{\cal X}
; there is no need to cite here the exact definition that was given in
[
9
]
. For our purposes, it suffices to know just that
{\cal X}_{0}
is a circulant scheme and
{\cal X}_{0}\geq{\cal X}
. In accordance with Lemma 5.2 and Theorem 3.4 proved in
[
10
]
, there exists an algebraic automorphism
\varphi_{0}\in\aut_{alg}({\cal X}_{0})
such that

(26)

\{\sigma_{\mathfrak{S}}\}=\iso({\cal X}_{0,{\mathfrak{S}}},\varphi_{0,{\mathfrak{S}}})\quad\text{for all}\ \,{\mathfrak{S}}\in\secc_{0}({\cal X}),

where
\sigma_{\mathfrak{S}}
is the component of
{\mathfrak{M}}
at the section
{\mathfrak{S}}
,
{\cal X}_{0,{\mathfrak{S}}}=({\cal X}_{0})_{\mathfrak{S}}
, and
\varphi_{0,{\mathfrak{S}}}=(\varphi_{0})_{\mathfrak{S}}
. By
[
10
, Theorem 1.1]
, it suffices to verify that
\varphi_{0}
extends
\varphi
. To this end, let
s
be a basis relation of the scheme
{\cal X}
. Denote by
{\mathfrak{S}}
the section of
\secc_{0}({\cal X})
such that
U({\mathfrak{S}})
and
L({\mathfrak{S}})
are the subgroups of
G
corresponding to the parabolics
\langle{s}\rangle
and
\rad(s)
in the sense of Subsection
4.1
. From formula (
26
), it follows that

\varphi_{0}(s)_{\mathfrak{S}}=\varphi_{0,{\mathfrak{S}}}(s_{\mathfrak{S}})=(s_{\mathfrak{S}})^{\sigma_{\mathfrak{S}}}=\varphi(s_{\mathfrak{S}}).

Taking into account that
\varphi_{0}(\rad(s))=\rad(s)=\varphi(\rad(s))
, we conclude that
\varphi_{0}(s)=\varphi(s)
.
∎

8.
Proof of Theorem
1.1

Let
X
be a circulant graph of order
n
. Then the coherent configuration
{\cal X}=\WL(X)
is a circulant scheme. In accordance with
[
4
, Subsection 3.2]
, the
\WL
-dimension of
X
is equal to
\dim_{\scriptscriptstyle\WL}({\cal X})
. We estimate the last number in two steps. First, we use Theorem
5.4
to reduce the general circulant schemes to quasinormal schemes.

Theorem 8.1
.

For any circulant scheme
{\cal X}
, there exists a quasinormal circulant scheme
{\cal X}^{\prime}
(of the same degree) such that
\dim_{\scriptscriptstyle\WL}({\cal X})\leq\dim_{\scriptscriptstyle\WL}({\cal X}^{\prime})
.

Proof.

If the scheme
{\cal X}
is quasinormal, then it is nothing to prove. Otherwise,
{\cal X}
has a singular class of order at least
4
(Lemma
5.1
). Let
{\cal X}^{\star}
be a singular extension of
{\cal X}
. Since
{\cal X}^{\star}
is circulant and
\rk({\cal X}^{\star})>\rk({\cal X})
(Lemma
5.2
), we may assume by induction on
\rk({\cal X})
that

(27)

\dim_{\scriptscriptstyle\WL}({\cal X}^{\star})\leq\dim_{\scriptscriptstyle\WL}({\cal X}^{\prime}):=m

for some quasinormal circulant scheme
{\cal X}^{\prime}
with the same degree as
{\cal X}^{\star}
.

Now let
{\cal Y}
be a circulant scheme
\WL_{m}
-equivalent to
{\cal X}
with respect to an algebraic isomorphism
\varphi\in\iso_{alg}({\cal X},{\cal Y})
. By the Muzychuk theorem (Theorem
4.1
), we may assume that

(28)

{\cal Y}={\cal X}\quad\text{and}\quad\varphi\in\aut_{alg}({\cal X}).

Then
{\cal X}
and
\varphi
satisfy the condition of Theorem
5.4
. Hence there exists an algebraic automorphism
\varphi^{\star}\in\aut_{alg}({\cal X}^{\star})
that extends
\varphi
and such that
{\cal X}^{\star}
is
\WL_{m}
-equivalent to itself with respect to
\varphi^{\star}
. By virtue of (
27
), this shows that

\iso({\cal X}^{\star},\varphi^{\star})\neq\varnothing.

Since
\iso({\cal X}^{\star},\varphi^{\star})\subseteq\iso({\cal X},\varphi)
, we conclude that
\iso({\cal X},\varphi)\neq\varnothing
. This proves the required inequality
\dim_{\scriptscriptstyle\WL}({\cal X})\leq m=\dim_{\scriptscriptstyle\WL}({\cal X}^{\prime})
.
∎

To complete the proof, let
m=\Omega(n)+3
. By Theorem
8.1
, we may assume that the scheme
{\cal X}
is quasinormal. Let
{\cal Y}
be an arbitrary circulant scheme
\WL_{m}
-equivalent to
{\cal X}
with respect to an algebraic isomorphism
\varphi\in\iso_{alg}({\cal X},{\cal Y})
. As above, we may also assume that relations (
28
) hold. Then the algebraic isomorphism
\varphi
is
(m-2)
-extendable by Lemma
7.2
. From Lemma
6.3
, it follows that there exists a base
(m-2)
-tuple for
{\cal X}
. Since
\varphi
is extendable at this tuple, Theorem
7.4
implies that
\iso({\cal X},\varphi)\neq\varnothing
. Thus,
\dim_{\scriptscriptstyle\WL}({\cal X})\leq m=\Omega(n)+3
, as required.

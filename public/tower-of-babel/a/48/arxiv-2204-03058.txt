On the action of the Σ ⁡ ( 2 , 3 , 7 ) \Sigma(2,3,7) homology sphere group on its space of left-orders
Kathryn Mann and Michele Triestino

Abstract
. We show that the action of the group Γ = ⟨ a , b , c ∣ a 2 = b 3 = c 7 = a b c ⟩ \Gamma=\langle a,b,c\mid a^{2}=b^{3}=c^{7}=abc\rangle on its space of left-orders has exactly two minimal components. MSC2020: Primary 37C85, 57M60,37B05. Secondary 37E05.



A
left-order
on a group
G
is a total order
\prec
on
G
which is preserved by the left-multiplication of
G
on itself. A left-order is uniquely determined by its cone of positive elements
P_{\prec}=\{g\in G:g\succ\mathsf{id}\}
, which forms a semi-group, and satisfies
G=P_{\prec}\sqcup P^{-1}_{\prec}\sqcup\{\mathsf{id}\}
. Conversely, any semi-group satisfying such properties is the positive cone for some left-order. Because of this, one can see the set
\LO(G)
of left-orders on
G
as the set of positive cones on
G
, so that it is naturally a closed subset of
2^{G}
.
A fundamental system of neighborhoods at a given left-order
\prec
is given by the subsets

V_{\prec,F}=\{\prec^{\prime}\in\LO(G):P_{\prec}\cap F=P_{\prec^{\prime}}\cap F\},

where
F
runs over finite subsets of
G
.

The conjugacy action of
G
on itself induces an action by homeomorphisms on
\{0,1\}^{G}
which preserves the subset of positive cones, and thus
G
has a natural action by homeomorphisms on
\LO(G)
. Given an element
g\in G
and a left-order
\prec\in\LO(G)
we denote by
\prec_{g}
the left-order obtained using this action. By definition, this is determined by the condition
P_{\prec_{g}}=gP_{\prec}g^{-1}
.

A general approach to understand the qualitative dynamical behavior of this action has been developed by Clay
[
3
]
. Notably he proved (see also Rivas
[
11
]
) that in the case of nonabelian finite rank free groups, the action is topologically transitive, namely there exists a dense orbit.
When
G
is a non-trivial group such that
\LO(G)
is finite (such groups have been classified by Tararin, and they are a particular class of polycyclic groups), the action of
G
on
\LO(G)
has exactly two orbits. Using this, Clay gave an example of countable, not finitely generated, group for which the action is minimal, namely every orbit is dense. The problem of finding a non-trivial finitely generated group with this property is well-known to experts, see for instance Navas’s survey
[
9
, Question 6]
.
One of the main difficulties is that there are often obstructions to make the orbit of a positive cone
P
accumulate on
P^{-1}
(see for instance
[
3
, Proposition 6]
for an obstruction related to convex generators for Conradian orderings).

The purpose of this note is to describe the action on the space of left-orders of the group

\Gamma:=\langle a,b,c\mid a^{2}=b^{3}=c^{7}=abc\rangle.

By a classical result of Milnor
[
8
]
, this is isomorphic to the fundamental group of the Brieskorn homology 3-sphere
\Sigma(2,3,7)
, and it is also obtained by considering the
\mathbb{Z}
-central extension in
\widetilde{\PSL}(2,\mathbb{R})
of the cocompact Fuchsian
(2,3,7)
-triangle group
\Delta:=\Delta(2,3,7)
in
\PSL(2,\mathbb{R})
, as one sees from the standard presentation
\Delta\cong\langle a,b,c\mid a^{2},b^{3},c^{7},abc\rangle
. (See
[
8
, Corollary 2.5]
. Here we adopt a more common notation for these groups, as in Calegari
[
2
]
). The group
\Gamma
is a quite remarkable left-orderable group, for instance Thurston
[
12
]
noticed that it is an example of finitely generated, perfect left-orderable group.

We prove that the obstruction mentioned above is the only one for this group.

Theorem 1
.

For any
\prec,\prec^{\prime}\in\LO(\Gamma)
such that
abc\in P_{\prec}\cap P_{\prec^{\prime}}
, we have
\overline{\orb_{\Gamma}(\prec)}=\overline{\orb_{\Gamma}(\prec^{\prime})}
. In particular, the action of
\Gamma
on
\LO(\Gamma)
has exactly two minimal components.

Let us comment that
\Gamma
gives the first known example of finitely generated group with this property, and admitting uncountably many left-orders. The proof of this result resembles somehow the proof of
[
10
, Proposition 2.12]
, and it relies on the tight relation between left-orders and orientation-preserving actions on the real line. Indeed, given any left-order
\prec
on a countable group
G
, one can construct a faithful action of
G
on the line, by the so-called
dynamical realization
. More precisely, there exists an action
\varphi_{\prec}:G\to\homeo_{+}(\mathbb{R})
and a base point
p\in\mathbb{R}
such that
\varphi_{\prec}(g)(p)>p
if and only if
g\in P_{\prec}
.

Remark 2
.

Note that for any
g\in G
, the action
\varphi_{\prec}
with base point
\varphi_{\prec}(g)(p)
provides a dynamical realization of the left-order
\prec_{g}
. This follows easily from the fact that the action of
G
on the line is by orientation preserving homeomorphisms, so
\varphi_{\prec}(h)(p)>p\Leftrightarrow\varphi_{\prec}(gh)(p)>\varphi_{\prec}(g)(p)\Leftrightarrow\varphi_{\prec}(ghg^{-1})\varphi_{\prec}(g)(p)>\varphi_{\prec}(g)(p)
.

Being naturally a subgroup of
\widetilde{\PSL}(2,\mathbb{R})
, the group
\Gamma
admits a faithful action on the real line.
More precisely, the action of
\Gamma
is the
\mathbb{Z}
-central lift of the action of
\Delta\in\PSL(2,\mathbb{R})
by Möbius transformations on
\mathbb{R}\mathrm{P}^{1}
(or, equivalently, the action on the boundary of the hyperbolic disk
\partial\mathbb{H}^{2}
induced by isometries of
\mathbb{H}^{2}
), where the center is generated by the element
abc
.

Remark 3
.

Thus, this action has the property that every point stabilizer is either trivial or cyclic (because
\Delta
is a discrete subgroup of
\PSL(2,\mathbb{R})
), and in the latter case one can find a generator of the stabilizer which contracts any sufficiently small neighborhood of the point (because
\Delta
is cocompact in
\PSL(2,\mathbb{R})
).

We will refer to the action of
\Gamma
coming from the embedding on
\widetilde{\PSL}(2,\mathbb{R})
, as to the
standard action

\rho:\Gamma\to\homeo_{+}(\mathbb{R})
. The remarkable (and well-known) fact that we will use is that the standard action is essentially the unique action of
\Gamma
on the line.

Theorem 4
.

Let
\varphi:\Gamma\to\homeo_{+}(\mathbb{R})
be an action without global fixed points. Then there exists a continuos monotone map
h:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
such that
h\circ\varphi(g)=\rho(g)\circ h
for every
g\in\Gamma
.

Proof.

As explained in Calegari
[
2
, Remark 4.3.4]
, by a combination of the Milnor–Wood inequality and a theorem of Matsumoto
the group
\Delta
admits a unique non-trivial action on the circle up to semi-conjugacy. We claim that for any action
\varphi
of
\Gamma
on
\mathbb{R}
as in Theorem
4
, the central element
abc
acts without fixed points. Thus, there is an induced action of
\Delta=\Gamma/\langle abc\rangle
on the circle
\mathbb{R}/\langle\varphi(abc)\rangle
, which is nontrivial (else
abc
would act trivially on the line), so unique up to semi-conjugacy. Since the standard action is minimal, such a semi-conjugacy can be realized by a continuous monotone map (projected to the circle) as in the statement of the theorem, showing that
\Gamma
has a unique action up to semi-conjugacy as desired.

To prove the claim, assume that
p\in\mathbb{R}
is fixed by
\varphi(abc)
. Since we are assuming that
\varphi
has no global fixed points, there must be a generator
s\in\{a,b,c\}
such that
\varphi(s)(p)\neq p
. Assume, say, that
s=a
and
\varphi(a)(p)>p
(the other cases can be treated similarly). Then we have
\varphi(a^{2})(p)>\varphi(a)(p)>p
, but
\varphi(a^{2})(p)=\varphi(abc)(p)=p
, contradicting the assumption.
∎

The conclusion in the statement is often rephrased by saying that
\varphi
and
\rho
are
semi-conjugate
actions. We will say that they are
positively
semi-conjugate if the map
h
is increasing. When
h
is not injective, we also say that
\varphi
is a
blow up
of
\rho
.

Remark 5
.

In general, if
\psi:G\to\homeo_{+}(\mathbb{R})
is a blow up of a dynamical realization
\varphi_{\prec}
with base point
p
, with positive semi-conjugacy
h:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
, then for any
q\in h^{-1}(p)
, the linear order of points in the orbit
\psi(G)(q)
agrees with that of
\varphi_{\prec}(G)(p)
.

Proof of Theorem
1
.

Fix two left-orders
\prec
and
\prec^{\prime}
on
\Gamma
and a finite subset
F\subset\Gamma
. We write
P
and
P^{\prime}
for the corresponding positive cones. Assume that
abc\in P\cap P^{\prime}
. We want to find an element
g\in\Gamma
such that
\prec_{g}^{\prime}
belongs to the neighborhood
V_{\prec,F}
.
Let
\varphi:\Gamma\to\homeo_{+}(\mathbb{R})
and
\varphi^{\prime}:\Gamma\to\homeo_{+}(\mathbb{R})
be dynamical realizations of
\prec
and
\prec^{\prime}
respectively, with corresponding base points
p
and
p^{\prime}
.
As
abc\in P\cap P^{\prime}
, by Theorem
4
, the dynamical realizations
\varphi
and
\varphi^{\prime}
are positively semi-conjugate. As any two positively semi-conjugate actions admit a common blow up (see for instance
[
5
, Theorem 2.2]
), by Remark
5
we can actually assume
\varphi=\varphi^{\prime}
, and we will simply write
g(x)
instead of
\varphi(g)(x)
for any
g\in\Gamma
,
x\in\mathbb{R}
. We denote by
\Lambda\subset\mathbb{R}
the minimal invariant closed subset for the action
\varphi
.
When
p
or
p^{\prime}
is not in
\Lambda
, we let
J
(respectively,
J^{\prime}
) be the closure of the connected component of
\mathbb{R}\smallsetminus\Lambda
containing the base point
p
(respectively,
p^{\prime}
), and we write
K=\stab(J)
(respectively,
K^{\prime}=\stab(J^{\prime})
). In case
p
or
p^{\prime}
is in
\Lambda
, we simply put
J=\{p\}
(or
J^{\prime}=\{p^{\prime}\}
), in which case
K
(respectively,
K^{\prime}
) is trivial. Note that as for the standard action
\rho
point stabilizers are either trivial or cyclic (Remark
3
), the subgroup
K
(and so
K^{\prime}
) is either trivial or cyclic.

As an easy first case, assume to start that
K
is trivial. By continuity of the action, we can find a neighborhood
V
of
J
, such that
f(x)>x
if and only if
f(p)>p
for every
f\in F
and
x\in V
.
Let
g\in\Gamma
be any element such that
g(p^{\prime})\in V
, in this case, by Remark
2
, we have
\prec_{g}^{\prime}\in V_{\prec,F}
, as wanted.

When
K
is non-trivial, we need to take some care to choose such an element
g
to send
p^{\prime}
to the correct side of
J
.
By Remark
3
, we can take a generator
k
of
K
such that for every sufficiently small neighborhood
U
of
J
, one has
k(U)\subset U
. That is to say, if
u
is close to
J
but strictly to the left of
J
, we have
k(u)>u
, and if
u
is close to
J
but strictly to the right, then
k(u)<u
.
Suppose first that
k\in P
. Then choose
g\in\Gamma
such that
g(p^{\prime})
lies to the left of
J
in
U-J
, chosen sufficiently close so that
f(x)>x
if and only if
f(p)>p
for any
f\in F
(note that our choice of the left side ensures this holds even if
K\cap F\neq\varnothing
),
and such that
gK^{\prime}g^{-1}\cap F=\varnothing
. The last condition may easily be satisfied because each side of
U-J
intersects the minimal set for the action. Then, by Remark
2
again,
\prec_{g}^{\prime}\in V_{\prec,F}
, as desired. If instead we have
k\in P^{-1}
, one makes the same argument choosing
g(p^{\prime})
to be to the right.
∎

As a final comment, let us mention that other finitely generated groups are known to admit a unique nontrivial action on the line, up to semi-conjugacy. One interesting example is provided by the central extension
\widetilde{T}
of Thompson’s group
T
of dyadic piecewise linear circle homeomorphisms (see the work of Matte Bon and the second author
[
7
]
). Understanding this example was our original motivation, as we were wondering whether one could find examples of groups whose action on the space of orders is minimal by considering the groups of Hyde and Lodha
[
4
]
(or their more conceptual generalization presented by Le Boudec and Matte Bon in
[
6
]
), which are “quasi-periodic” versions of
\widetilde{T}
.
However, point stabilizers for the standard action of
\widetilde{T}
are very large, as they are always isomorphic to a subgroup of Thompson’s group
F
containing
[F,F]
, and the classification of actions of such groups (even up to semi-conjugacy, see the work of Brum, Matte Bon, Rivas and the second author
[
1
]
) is quite complex. Because of this, the classification of actions of
\widetilde{T}

up to conjugacy
is also complex, and there is little hope to understand the minimal components of the action of
\widetilde{T}
on
\LO(\widetilde{T})
(and even worse in the case of the groups of Hyde and Lodha and generalizations).

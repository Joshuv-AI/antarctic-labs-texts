Dynamics of Chebyshev endomorphisms on some affine algebraic varieties
Keisuke Uchimura

Abstract
. The Chebyshev polynomials T d T_{d} in one variable are typical chaotic maps on ℂ {\mathbb{C}} . Chebyshev endomorphisms P A n d : ℂ n → ℂ n P_{A_{n}}^{d}:{\mathbb{C}}^{n}\to{\mathbb{C}}^{n} are also chaotic. We consider the action of the dihedral group D n + 1 D_{n+1} on ℂ n {\mathbb{C}}^{n} . The endomorphism P A n d P_{A_{n}}^{d} maps any D n + 1 − D_{n+1}- orbit of 𝐳 ∈ ℂ n {\bf z}\in{\mathbb{C}}^{n} to a D n + 1 D_{n+1} -orbit of P A n d ​ ( 𝐳 ) P_{A_{n}}^{d}({\bf z}) . The endomorphism P A n d P_{A_{n}}^{d} induces a mapping on ℂ n / D n + 1 {\mathbb{C}}^{n}/D_{n+1} . Using invariant theory we embed ℂ n / D n + 1 {\mathbb{C}}^{n}/D_{n+1} as an affine subvariety X X in ℂ m {\mathbb{C}}^{m} . Then we have morphisms g d g_{d} on X X . We study the cases n = 2 n=2 and 3 3 . In these cases the morphisms g d g_{d} are defined over ℤ {\mathbb{Z}} . We find a class of affine subvarieties V V of X X which are invariant under g d g_{d} . These varieties are concerned with branch loci or critical loci.The class contains ℂ 2 {\mathbb{C}}^{2} , a cuspidal cubic, a parabola, a quadric hypersurface in ℂ 4 {\mathbb{C}}^{4} , an affine algebraic surface in ℂ 4 {\mathbb{C}}^{4} which is birationally equivalent to an affine quadric cone in ℂ 3 {\mathbb{C}}^{3} , and others. 2.
From holomorphic maps to morphisms on varieties

2.1.
Chebyshev endomorphisms and dihedral groups

Chebyshev endomorphism

of degree

is associate to the Lie algebra of type

. For short, we denote

We begin with constructing Chebyshev endomorphisms

Let

Let

-th elementary symmetric polynomial in

We define a map
\Phi

to
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}

\Phi(t_{1},\dots,t_{n+1})=(z_{1},\dots,z_{n}).

Let

Since

is a symmetric polynomial in

{\mathbb{Z}}
, each

can be expressed as a polynomial in

{\mathbb{Z}}

See
[

23
]

Note that

z_{n+1-k}=\sum t_{i_{1}}\cdot\cdot\cdot t_{i_{n+1-k}}=\sum 1/(t_{j_{1}}\cdot\cdot\cdot t_{j_{k}}),

\{i_{1},\dots,i_{n+1-k},j_{1},\dots,j_{k}\}=\{1,2,\dots,n+1\}.

Considering the correspondence of

If

\overline{z_{j}^{(d)}(z_{1},\dots,z_{n})}=z_{j}^{(d)}(z_{n},\dots,z_{1}).

{z_{n+1-j}^{(d)}}=\overline{z_{j}^{(d)}},

.

For any

(2.4)

Proof.

We have

R^{d}\circ T_{d}(z_{1},\dots,z_{n})=(\zeta^{d}z_{1}^{(d)},\ \zeta^{2d}z_{2}^{(d)},\ \dots,\ \zeta^{nd}z_{n}^{(d)}),\ (z_{1},\dots,z_{n})\in{\mathbb{C}}^{n}.

Any element
(z_{1},\dots,z_{n})
determines a set
\{t_{1},\dots,t_{n+1}\}
satisfying (2.1) as roots of the following equation ;

t^{n+1}+(-1)z_{1}t^{n}+\ ...\ +(-1)^{n}z_{n}t+(-1)^{n+1}=0.

Since

V_{F^{\prime}}
to
V_{F^{\prime}}
. Then we will show that
g^{\prime}_{d}:V_{F^{\prime}}\to V_{F^{\prime}}
is conjugate to
g_{d}:V_{F}\to V_{F}
via the isomorphism

\varphi\mid_{V_{F}}
in the following proposition.

Proposition 4.30
.

Let
\{p^{\prime},q^{\prime},r^{\prime},s^{\prime}\}
be any other fundamental system of invariants of
{\mathbb{C}}[x,y,z]^{D_{4}}
. Set

F^{\prime}=(p^{\prime},q^{\prime},r^{\prime},s^{\prime}).
By Definition 2.4 we define the map
g^{\prime}_{d}

from
V_{F^{\prime}}
to
V_{F^{\prime}}
. Let

\varphi\mid_{V_{F}}
be the map in Lemma 4.29. Then we have
\varphi\mid_{V_{F}}\circ g_{d}=g^{\prime}_{d}\circ\varphi\mid_{V_{F}}
.

Proof.

From (2.11), we have

g_{d}\circ\tilde{F}({\bf x})=\tilde{F}\circ f_{d}({\bf x}),\ {\mbox{and}}\ g^{\prime}_{d}\circ\tilde{F}^{\prime}({\bf x})=\tilde{F}^{\prime}\circ f_{d}({\bf x}),

where
\tilde{F}^{\prime}
is the polynomial map from
{\mathbb{C}}^{3}
to
V_{F^{\prime}}
in Theorem 2.3.
Since
\varphi(p,q,r,s)=(p^{\prime},q^{\prime},r^{\prime},s^{\prime}),
it follows that
\tilde{F}^{\prime}=\varphi\circ\tilde{F}
. Then

\varphi\circ g_{d}\circ\tilde{F}=\varphi\circ\tilde{F}\circ f_{d}=\tilde{F}^{\prime}\circ f_{d}=g^{\prime}_{d}\circ\tilde{F}^{\prime}=g^{\prime}_{d}\circ\varphi\circ\tilde{F}.

By Theorem 2.3(1), we know that
\tilde{F}
is surjective. Then

\varphi\mid_{V_{F}}\circ g_{d}=g^{\prime}_{d}\circ\varphi\mid_{V_{F}}.

∎

Scattering below ground states for a class of systems of nonlinear Schrodinger equations
Satoshi MASAKI, Ryusei TSUKUDA

Abstract
. In this paper, we consider the scattering problem for a class of N N -coupled systems of the cubic nonlinear Schrödinger equations in three space dimensions. We prove the scattering of solutions which have a mass-energy quantity less than that for the ground states. This result is previously obtained by Duyckaerts-Holmer-Roudenko for the single cubic nonlinear Schrödinger equation in three space dimensions. It turns out that the result can be extended to a wide class of N N -coupled systems.

1.
Introduction

In this paper, we study the Cauchy problem of the following
N
-coupled system of nonlinear
Schördinger equations:

(gNLS)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\,i\partial_{t}{u_{j}}+\Delta{u_{j}}+F_{j}(\bm{u})=0,\quad 1\leqslant j\leqslant N,\quad(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{3},\\
&\,\bm{u}(t_{0})=(u_{1,0},u_{2,0},\dots,u_{N,0})\in(H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))^{N},\\
\end{aligned}\right.

where
N\geqslant 2
and
\bm{u}=\bm{u}(t,x)=(u_{1}(t,x),u_{2}(t,x),\dots,u_{N}(t,x))
is a
\mathbb{C}^{N}
-valued unknown.

\Delta=\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x_{1}^{2}}+\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x_{2}^{2}}+\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x_{3}^{2}}

is the standard Laplacian.

F_{j}
is a cubic nonlinearity.
We assume that the nonlinearity is given by the relation

(HS)

\displaystyle F_{j}:=\tfrac{1}{2}\partial_{\overline{z_{j}}}g

from a
\mathbb{R}
-valued quartic polynomial
g\colon\mathbb{C}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}
with real coefficients which satisfies
the gauge condition

(GC)

\displaystyle g(e^{i\theta}z_{1},e^{i\theta}z_{2},\dots,e^{i\theta}z_{N})=g(z_{1},z_{2},\dots,z_{N})

for all
(z_{1},z_{2},\dots,z_{N})\in\mathbb{C}^{N}
and
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
.
One consequence of assumption (
HS
) is that (
gNLS
) possesses the Hamiltonian structure.
Indeed, (
gNLS
) has the conserved energy

(1.1)

E(\bm{u}):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left(\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{N}|\nabla u_{j}|^{2}-\frac{1}{4}g(\bm{u})\right)dx,

at least formally. By (
GC
) and (
HS
),
there are two more conserved quantities:

\displaystyle M(\bm{u}):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{N}|u_{j}|^{2}dx,

\displaystyle P(\bm{u}):=\sum_{j=1}^{N}\im{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\overline{u}_{j}\nabla u_{j}dx}.

They correspond to the mass (or charge) and the moment, respectively.
We denote
\bm{F}(\bm{u})=(F_{1}(\bm{u}),F_{2}(\bm{u}),\dots,F_{N}(\bm{u}))
.
For the existence of bound states, we suppose

(1.2)

\displaystyle g_{\text{max}}:=\max_{|z_{1}|^{2}+|z_{2}|^{2}+\dots|z_{N}|^{2}=1}g(z_{1},z_{2},\dots,z_{N})>0.

The system (
gNLS
) contains several well-known systems.
The first one is the Manakov system or the vector valued nonlinear
Schördinger equation:

i\partial_{t}{u_{j}}+\Delta{u_{j}}+u_{j}\sum_{j=1}^{N}|u_{j}|^{2}=0,\quad 1\leqslant j\leqslant N,\quad(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}.

This system is given by
g(u_{1},u_{2},\dots,u_{N})=(\sum_{j=1}^{N}|u_{j}|^{2})^{2}
.
It is known that the system is an integrable system in one space dimension
[
Manakov
]
(see also
[
APT1
,
APT2
]
).
The second example is as follows:

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\,i\partial_{t}{u_{1}}+\Delta{u_{1}}+(a+b)(|u_{1}|^{2}+|u_{2}|^{2})u_{1}+(a-b)|u_{3}|^{2}u_{1}+bu_{2}^{2}\overline{u_{3}}=0,\\
&\,i\partial_{t}{u_{2}}+\Delta{u_{2}}+(a+b)(|u_{1}|^{2}+|u_{3}|^{2})u_{2}+a|u_{2}|^{2}u_{2}+2bu_{1}\overline{u_{2}}u_{3}=0,\\
&\,i\partial_{t}{u_{3}}+\Delta{u_{3}}+(a+b)(|u_{2}|^{2}+|u_{3}|^{2})u_{3}+(a-b)|u_{1}|^{2}u_{3}+b\overline{u_{1}}u_{2}^{2}=0,\\
\end{aligned}\right.

where
(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}
and
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
are constants.
This is a model system in the spinor Bose-Einstein condensate (see
[
Wadati-Tsuchida
]
, for instance).
This system corresponds to the choice
N=3
and

\displaystyle g(z_{1},z_{2},z_{3})={}

\displaystyle a(|z_{1}|^{2}+|z_{2}|^{2}+|z_{3}|^{2})^{2}

\displaystyle+b((|z_{1}|^{2}-|z_{3}|^{2})^{2}+2|z_{2}|^{2}(|z_{1}|^{2}+|z_{3}|^{2})+4\operatorname{Re}(\overline{z_{1}}z_{2}^{2}\overline{z_{3}})).

One easily deduces from this form that there exists
a_{0}=a_{0}(b)
such that (
1.2
) holds for
a>a_{0}
.

The existence and the characterization of the ground states of (
gNLS

[
30

.
The instability of the ground states and
a sharp criteria for the global existence are also obtained by using the variational characterization of the ground states.
The global existence result is a global-existence-below-ground-state type result.
The aim of this paper is to establish the scattering of the global solutions.

Before going into details, we briefly recall the results for the single NLS equation:

(NLS)

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &\,i\partial_{t}{u}+\Delta{u}+\mu|u|^{p-1}u=0,\ \ t\in\mathbb{R},\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\\
&\,u(0,x)=u_{0}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),\end{aligned}\right.

The local well-posedness in

[

For an

-solution

\displaystyle M(u):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{1}{2}|u|^{2}dx,\ \ \ E(u):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\frac{1}{2}|\nabla u|^{2}-\frac{1}{p+1}|u|^{p+1}\right)dx

exists globally forward (resp. backward) in time and

(resp.

) such that

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}\left\lVert U(-t)u(t)-u_{+}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}=0

\displaystyle\left(\text{resp. }\lim_{t\to-\infty}\left\lVert U(-t)u(t)-u_{-}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}=0\right)

holds. This definition is naturally extended to the system case.

There are large number of literature on the scattering problem of (

When

A typical example of the non-scattering solution is a soliton.

It is known that one can obtain a sharp sufficient condition for scattering by means of variational characterization of

As for the mass-supercritical and energy-subcritical case

Let

\displaystyle\left\lVert u_{0}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\left\lVert\nabla u_{0}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}<\left\lVert Q\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\left\lVert\nabla Q\right\rVert_{L^{2}},

\displaystyle M(u)E(u)<M(Q)E(Q)

1.1.
Main result

Let us make notations.
Let

For a Banach space

Definition 1.2

Our main result is the following.

Theorem 1.3
.

If the data
\bm{u}_{0}\in(H^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{3}))^{N}
satisfies

(1.4)

\displaystyle M(\bm{u}_{0})E(\bm{u}_{0})<M(\bm{Q})E(\bm{Q}),

(1.5)

\displaystyle\left\lVert\bm{u}_{0}\right\rVert_{(L^{2})^{N}}\left\lVert\nabla\bm{u}_{0}\right\rVert_{(L^{2})^{N}}<\left\lVert\bm{Q}\right\rVert_{(L^{2})^{N}}\left\lVert\nabla\bm{Q}\right\rVert_{(L^{2})^{N}}

then the corresponding
H^{1}
-solution
\bm{u}
to (
gNLS
) scatters for both time directions,
where
\bm{Q}\in\mathcal{G}
.

Remark 1.4
.

By (
1.3
), one has
M(\bm{Q})E(\bm{Q})=g_{\max}^{-2}M(Q)E(Q)
and
\left\lVert\bm{Q}\right\rVert_{(L^{2})^{N}}\left\lVert\nabla\bm{Q}\right\rVert_{(L^{2})^{N}}=g_{max}^{-1}\left\lVert Q\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\left\lVert\nabla Q\right\rVert_{L^{2}}
for all
\bm{Q}\in\mathcal{G}
, where
Q
is the (normalized) ground state in the single case.

The proof of the main theorem is done by extending the argument in
[
Duyckaerts-Holmer-Roudenko
]
to the system setup.
We use the concentration-compactness/rigidity argument by Kenig and Merle.
It will turn out that the proof for the single case works with a slight modification.
It can be said that
a large class of NLS systems is handled in a unified way by our formulation.

The outline of the proof is as follows.
We first reformulate the above theorem as a variational problem.
The starting point is that, under the assumption (
1.5
),
a solution scatters if the value of the mass-energy quantity of the solution is sufficiently small.
Then, let
A_{c}
be the critical value of the mass-energy quantity such that
the scattering of solutions is assured together with (
1.5
).
One sees by definition that this value does not exceed the value of the mass-energy quantity of the ground state,
that is, one has
A_{c}\leqslant M(\bm{Q})E(\bm{Q})
.
Then, the matter is reduced to showing that

\displaystyle A_{c}=M(\bm{Q})E(\bm{Q}).

We prove this by contradiction.
If it fails then one can show the existence of a critical element
\bm{u}_{c}(t)
.
This
\bm{u}_{c}(t)
is a global non-scattering solution to (
gNLS
)
of which value of the mass-energy quantity is exactly
A_{c}
and
of which orbit is precompact modulo space translation.
We use a profile decomposition to prove this.
Finally, we control the space translation by the analysis of the moment of the critical element
and apply the truncated virial estimate to deduce a contradiction.

In Section
2

In particular, we obtain the local well-posedness, the equivalent characterization of the scattering, and the long time perturbation.
In Section

The property of the ground states are briefly recalled in Section

Then, we prove the main result in Section

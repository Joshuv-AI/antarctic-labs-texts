Strichartz estimates for Maxwell equations in media: The partially anisotropic case
Robert Schippa

Abstract
. We prove Strichartz estimates for solutions to Maxwell equations in three dimensions with rough permittivities, which have less than three different eigenvalues. To this end, Maxwell equations are conjugated to half-wave equations in phase space. We use the Strichartz estimates in a known combination with energy estimates to show the new well-posedness results for quasilinear Maxwell equations.

Introduction

In the following Maxwell equations in media in three spatial dimensions, the physically most relevant case (cf.
[
6
,
12
]
), are analyzed. These describe the propagation of
electric and magnetic fields

(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{B}):\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}
, and
displacement and magnetizing fields

(\mathcal{D},\mathcal{H}):\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}
. The system of equations is given by

(1)

\left\{\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}\mathcal{D}&=\nabla\times\mathcal{H}-\mathcal{J}_{e},\quad\nabla\cdot\mathcal{D}&&=\rho_{e},\\
\partial_{t}\mathcal{B}&=-\nabla\times\mathcal{E}-\mathcal{J}_{m},\quad\nabla\cdot\mathcal{B}&&=\rho_{m},\\
\mathcal{D}(0,\cdot)&=\mathcal{D}_{0},\quad\qquad\quad\quad\mathcal{B}(0,\cdot)&&=\mathcal{B}_{0}.\end{aligned}\right.

(\rho_{e},\rho_{m}):\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}\to\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}
denote
electric and magnetic charges
and
(\mathcal{J}_{e},\mathcal{J}_{m}):\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}\to\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}

electric and magnetic currents
. There is no physical evidence for the existence of magnetic charges or magnetic currents, but we include them to highlight a key aspect of the analysis.

The notations follow the previous work
[
19
]
on Maxwell equations in two spatial dimensions. We denote space-time coordinates
x=(x^{0},x^{1},\ldots,x^{n})=(t,x^{\prime})\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
and the dual variables in Fourier space by
\xi=(\xi^{0},\xi^{1},\ldots,\xi^{n})=(\tau,\xi^{\prime})\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

In this work we supplement Maxwell equations with time-instantaneous material laws, relating
\mathcal{E}
with
\mathcal{D}
and
\mathcal{H}
with
\mathcal{B}

\begin{split}\mathcal{D}(x)&=\varepsilon(x)\mathcal{E}(x),\quad\varepsilon:\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3},\\
\mathcal{B}(x)&=\mu(x)\mathcal{H}(x),\quad\mu:\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3}.\end{split}

\varepsilon
is referred to as
permittivity
, and
\mu
is referred to as
permeability
. In some cases we shall assume that

, which means that the considered material is magnetically isotropic. This is a common assumption in nonlinear optics (cf.
[
17
]
). Like in the preceding work
[
19
]
, we want to describe the propagation in possibly anisotropic and inhomogeneous media. We suppose that
\varepsilon
,
\mu

\varepsilon,\mu:\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3}
with
\Lambda_{1},\Lambda_{2}>0

\xi^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}

\Lambda_{1}|\xi^{\prime}|^{2}\leq\sum_{i,j=1}^{3}\kappa^{ij}(x)\xi_{i}^{\prime}\xi_{j}^{\prime}\leq\Lambda_{2}|\xi^{\prime}|^{2},\quad\kappa^{ij}(x)=\kappa^{ji}(x),\quad\kappa\in\{\varepsilon,\mu\}.

The case of diagonal
\varepsilon=\text{diag}(\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2},\varepsilon_{3})
,
\mu=1_{3\times 3}
covers the physically relevant case

(4)

\varepsilon(\mathcal{E})=(1+|\mathcal{E}|^{2})1_{3\times 3}

(5)

\mathcal{C}(D)=\begin{pmatrix}0&-\partial_{3}&\partial_{2}\\
\partial_{3}&0&-\partial_{1}\\
-\partial_{2}&\partial_{1}&0\end{pmatrix},\quad P(x,D)=\begin{pmatrix}\partial_{t}1_{3\times 3}&-\mathcal{C}(D)\mu^{-1}\\
\mathcal{C}(D)\varepsilon^{-1}&\partial_{t}1_{3\times 3}\end{pmatrix}.

(
1

P(x,D)\begin{pmatrix}\mathcal{D}\\
\mathcal{H}\end{pmatrix}=-\begin{pmatrix}\mathcal{J}_{e}\\
\mathcal{J}_{m}\end{pmatrix},\quad\begin{cases}\nabla\cdot\mathcal{D}=\rho_{e},\\
\nabla\cdot\mathcal{B}=\rho_{m}.\end{cases}

i(\xi_{0},\xi_{0}-\|\xi^{\prime}\|_{\varepsilon},\xi_{0}+\|\xi^{\prime}\|_{\varepsilon})\text{ for }\|\xi^{\prime}\|_{\varepsilon}\sim 1.

\varepsilon(x)=(\varepsilon_{1}(x),\varepsilon_{2}(x),\varepsilon_{2}(x))

\varepsilon_{1}\neq\varepsilon_{2}\neq\varepsilon_{3}\neq\varepsilon_{1}

We compute

\begin{split}\partial_{t}(\psi(|\mathcal{D}|^{2})\mathcal{D})&=\psi(|\mathcal{D}|^{2})\partial_{t}\mathcal{D}+(2\psi^{\prime}(|\mathcal{D}|^{2})\mathcal{D}\otimes\mathcal{D})\partial_{t}\mathcal{D}=:\tilde{\psi}_{1}(\mathcal{D})\partial_{t}\mathcal{D},\\
\nabla\times(\psi(|\mathcal{D}|^{2})\mathcal{D})&=[\psi(|\mathcal{D}|^{2})\nabla\times+(2\psi^{\prime}(|\mathcal{D}|^{2})(\mathcal{D}\otimes(\mathcal{D}\times\nabla))^{t})]\mathcal{D}=:\tilde{\psi}_{2}(\mathcal{D})\mathcal{D}.\end{split}

After a diagonalization in phase space, we shall see that
\tilde{\psi}_{1}(\mathcal{D})
and
\tilde{\psi}_{2}(\mathcal{D})
have at most two different eigenvalues.

Passing to the second order systen yields the system of wave equations:

(18)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}\partial_{t}^{2}\mathcal{D}&=-\nabla\times(\tilde{\psi}_{2}(\mathcal{D})\nabla\times\mathcal{D}),\quad\nabla\cdot\mathcal{D}=\nabla\cdot\mathcal{H}=0,\\
\partial_{t}^{2}\mathcal{H}&=-\nabla\times(\tilde{\psi}_{1}(\mathcal{D})\nabla\times\mathcal{H}).\end{array}\right.

We shall first consider the simplified Kerr system, which is obtained by replacing
\tilde{\psi}_{i}
with
\psi(|\mathcal{D}|^{2})
:

(19)

\partial_{t}^{2}\mathcal{D}=-\nabla\times(\psi(|\mathcal{D}|^{2})\nabla\times\mathcal{D}),\quad\nabla\cdot\mathcal{D}=0.

In this case we can apply the Strichartz estimates for isotropic permittivity to prove the following:

Theorem 1.8

(Local well-posedness for the simplified Kerr system)
.

(
19
) is locally well-posed for
s>\frac{13}{6}
.

We remark that we could likewise treat the system

\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}\partial_{t}^{2}\mathcal{D}&=-\nabla\times(\psi(|\mathcal{D}|^{2})\nabla\times\mathcal{D}),\quad\nabla\cdot\mathcal{D}=0,\\
\partial_{t}^{2}\mathcal{H}&=-\nabla\times(\psi(|\mathcal{D}|^{2})\nabla\times\mathcal{H},\quad\nabla\cdot\mathcal{H}=0.\end{array}\right.

with the additional estimates for
\mathcal{H}
being carried out in similar spirit.

In the case of partially anisotropic permittivity, we can use the Strichartz estimates from Theorem
1.5
directly:

Theorem 1.9

(Local well-posedness for Maxwell equations with partially anisotropic permittivity)
.

Let
\varepsilon^{-1}=\text{diag}(\psi(|\mathcal{D}_{1}|^{2}),1,1)
with
\psi:\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 1}
smooth, monotone increasing, and
\psi(0)=1
. Then, the Maxwell system

\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}\partial_{t}\mathcal{D}&=\nabla\times\mathcal{H},\quad\nabla\cdot\mathcal{D}=\nabla\cdot\mathcal{H}=0,\\
\partial_{t}\mathcal{H}&=-\nabla\times(\varepsilon^{-1}\mathcal{D}),\;(\mathcal{D}(0),\mathcal{H}(0))\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{3};\mathbb{R}^{6})\end{array}\right.

is locally well-posed for
s>9/4
.

In the two-dimensional case we have shown that the derivative loss for Strichartz estimates with rough coefficients is sharp (cf.
[
19
, Section 7]
). In the three dimensional case we do not have an example showing sharpness. However, the fact that the derivative loss in the isotropic case matches the loss for second order hyperbolic operators indicates sharpness of the Strichartz estimates in the isotropic case.

Preliminaries

In this section we collect basic facts about pseudo-differential operators and the FBI transform to be used in the sequel.

2.1.
Pseudo-differential operators with rough symbols

In the following we clarify the quantization and recall the composition formulae for pseudo-differential operators presently considered. We refer to
[
7
,
28
,
29
]
for further reading.

Recall the standard Hörmander class of symbols:

S^{m}_{\rho,\delta}=\{a\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}):|\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\partial_{\xi}^{\beta}a|\lesssim(1+|\xi|)^{m-\rho|\beta|+|\alpha|\delta}\}

for
m\in\mathbb{R}
,
0\leq\delta\leq\rho\leq 1
. In the following we obtain pseudo-differential operators via the quantization:

a(x,D)f=(2\pi)^{-m}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}e^{ix.\xi}a(x,\xi)\hat{f}(\xi)d\xi.

The
L^{p}
-boundedness of
a(x,D)
with
a\in S^{0}_{1,\delta}
,
0\leq\delta<1
is standard (cf.
[
29
, Section 0.11]
). In the present context of rough coefficients, we shall also consider symbols which are rough in the spatial variable. After a Littlewood-Paley decomposition and a paradifferential decomposition, we can reduce to Hörmander symbols. We record the following quantification of
L^{p}L^{q}
-boundedness for symbols, which are smooth and compactly supported in the fiber variable and possibly rough in the spatial variable:

Lemma 2.1

(
[
19
, Lemma 2.3]
)
.

Let
1\leq p,q\leq\infty
and
a\in C^{s}_{x}C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{m})
with
a(x,\xi)=0
for
\xi\notin B(0,2)
. Suppose that

\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{m}}\sum_{0\leq|\alpha|\leq m+1}\|D_{\xi}^{\alpha}a(x,\cdot)\|_{L^{1}_{\xi}}\leq C.

Then, we find the following estimate to hold:

\|a(x,D)f\|_{L^{p}L^{q}}\lesssim C\|f\|_{L^{p}L^{q}}.

We recall the Kohn–Nirenberg theorem on symbol composition. Denote

\partial_{x}^{\alpha}=\partial_{x_{1}}^{\alpha_{1}}\ldots\partial_{x_{m}}^{\alpha_{m}}\text{ and }D_{\xi}^{\alpha}=\partial_{\xi}^{\alpha}/(i^{|\alpha|})

for
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{m}
.

Theorem 2.2

(
[
29
, Proposition 0.3C]
)
.

Let
m_{1},m_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
,
0\leq\delta_{i}<\rho_{i}\leq 1
for
i=1,2
. Given
P(x,\xi)\in S^{m_{1}}_{\rho_{1},\delta_{1}}
,
Q(x,\xi)\in S^{m_{2}}_{\rho_{2},\delta_{2}}
, suppose that

0\leq\delta_{2}<\rho\leq 1\text{ with }\rho=\min(\rho_{1},\rho_{2}).

Then,
(P\circ Q)(x,D)\in OPS^{m_{1}+m_{2}}_{\rho,\delta}
with
\delta=\max(\delta_{1},\delta_{2})
, and
P(x,D)\circ Q(x,D)
satisfies the asymptotic expansion

(20)

(P\circ Q)(x,D)=\sum_{\alpha}\frac{1}{\alpha!}(D_{\xi}^{\alpha}P\partial_{x}^{\alpha}Q)(x,D)+R,

where
R:\mathcal{S}^{\prime}\to C^{\infty}
is smoothing.

Lemma
2.1
quantifies the
L^{p}L^{q}
-bounds for the expansion (
20
) (see
[
19
, Section 2]
). From truncating the expansion to

(P\circ Q)(x,D)=\sum_{|\alpha|\leq N}\frac{1}{\alpha!}(D_{\xi}^{\alpha}P\partial_{x}^{\alpha}Q)(x,D)+R_{N}(x,D),

we can find error bounds for
R_{N}
decaying in
\lambda
. This can be proved again by Lemma
2.1
. We recall the Calderon–Vaillancourt theorem (cf.
[
2
,
28
]
) to bound
OPS^{0}_{\rho,\rho}
. The following quantification is due to Kato
[
9
]
:

Theorem 2.3

(Calderon–Vaillancourt)
.

Let
0\leq\rho<1
and
a(x,\xi)\in S^{0}_{\rho,\rho}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
with

|\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\partial_{\xi}^{\beta}a(x,\xi)|\leq C(1+|\xi|)^{\rho(|\alpha|-|\beta|)}

for
|\alpha|\leq\lfloor\frac{d}{2}\rfloor+1
,
|\beta|\leq\lfloor\frac{d}{2}\rfloor+2
. Then,

\|a(x,D)\|_{L^{2}\to L^{2}}\lesssim_{\rho,d}C.

2.2.
The FBI transform

We shall make use of the FBI transform to conjugate the evolution to phase space (cf.
[
4
,
27
]
). For
\lambda\in 2^{\mathbb{Z}}
, we define the FBI transform of
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{m};\mathbb{C})
by

\begin{split}T_{\lambda}f(z)&=C_{m}\lambda^{\frac{3m}{4}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}e^{-\frac{\lambda}{2}(z-y)^{2}}f(y)dy,\qquad z=x-i\xi\in T^{*}\mathbb{R}^{m}\equiv\mathbb{R}^{2m},\\
C_{m}&=2^{-\frac{m}{2}}\pi^{-\frac{3m}{4}}.\end{split}

The FBI transform is an isometric mapping
T_{\lambda}:L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{m})\to L^{2}_{\Phi}(T^{*}\mathbb{R}^{m})
with
\Phi(z)=e^{-\lambda\xi^{2}}
. The range of
T_{\lambda}
are holomorphic functions, thus there are many inversion formulae. One is given by the adjoint in
L^{2}_{\Phi}
:

T_{\lambda}^{*}F(y)=C_{m}\lambda^{\frac{3m}{4}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2m}}e^{-\frac{\lambda}{2}(\bar{z}-y)^{2}}\Phi(z)F(z)dxd\xi.

By decomposing a function into coherent states, the FBI transform allows us to find an approximate conjugate of pseudo-differential operators. Let
s\geq 0
,
a(x,\xi)\in C^{s}_{x}C^{\infty}_{c}
be smooth and compactly supported in
\xi
. We assume that

a(x,\xi)=0\text{ for }\xi\notin B(0,2).

Let
a_{\lambda}(x,\xi)=a(x,\xi/\lambda)
denote the scaled symbol and
A_{\lambda}=a_{\lambda}(x,D)
be the corresponding pseudo-differential operator. We have the following asymptotic for analytic symbols:

T_{\lambda}A_{\lambda}(x,D)\approx\sum_{\alpha,\beta}(\partial_{\xi}-\lambda\xi)^{\alpha}\frac{\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\partial_{\xi}^{\beta}a(x,\xi)}{|\alpha|!|\beta|!(-i\lambda)^{|\alpha|}\lambda^{|\beta|}}\big(\frac{1}{i}\partial_{x}-\lambda\xi)^{\beta}T_{\lambda}.

We consider truncations of the asymptotic expansion. For
s\leq 1
, we let

\tilde{a}^{s}_{\lambda}=a,

and for
1<s\leq 2
, let

\tilde{a}^{s}_{\lambda}=a+\frac{1}{-i\lambda}a_{x}(\partial_{\xi}-\lambda\xi)+\frac{1}{\lambda}a_{\xi}(\frac{1}{i}\partial_{x}-\lambda\xi)=a+\frac{2}{\lambda}(\bar{\partial}a)(\partial-i\lambda\xi)

with
\partial=\frac{1}{2}(\partial_{x}+i\partial_{\xi})
and
\bar{\partial}=\frac{1}{2}(\partial_{x}-i\partial_{\xi})
. We define the remainder

R^{s}_{\lambda,a}=T_{\lambda}A_{\lambda}-\tilde{a}^{s}_{\lambda}T_{\lambda}.

Tataru
[
25
,
26
]
proved the following approximation result:

Theorem 2.4

(
[
26
, Theorem 5, p. 393]
)
.

Let
0<s\leq 2
, and
a\in C^{s}_{x}C^{\infty}_{c}
. Then,

\begin{split}\|R^{s}_{\lambda,a}\|_{L^{2}\to L^{2}_{\Phi}}&\lesssim\lambda^{-\frac{s}{2}},\\
\|(\partial_{\xi}-\lambda\xi)R^{s}_{\lambda,a}\|_{L^{2}\to L^{2}_{\Phi}}&\lesssim\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{s}{2}}.\end{split}

Moreover, if
a\in X^{1}C^{\infty}_{c}
with
X^{1}=\{f\in L_{t}^{2}L_{x^{\prime}}^{\infty}\,:\,\partial f\in L_{t}^{2}L_{x^{\prime}}^{\infty}\}
, then

\|R^{1}_{\lambda,a}\|_{L^{\infty}L^{2}\to L^{2}_{\Phi}}\lesssim\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}.

2.3.
The characteristic set depending on the permittivity

In this section we summarize the characteristic set of Maxwell equations depending on the number of different eigenvalues of
\varepsilon=\text{diag}(\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2},\varepsilon_{3})
and
\mu=\text{diag}(\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\mu_{3})
. For this discussion suppose that
\varepsilon
and
\mu
are homogeneous. The partially anisotropic case (and isotropic case as special case) was detailed in
[
18
]
and the fully anisotropic case was analyzed in
[
16
]
.

2.3.1.
Isotropic case

For
\mu
and
\varepsilon
proportional to the unit matrix we can diagonalize the principal symbol to the diagonal matrix as will be carried out in Section
3
:

d(x,\xi)=i\text{diag}(\xi_{0},\xi_{0},\xi_{0}-(\varepsilon\mu)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|\xi^{\prime}\|,\xi_{0}+(\varepsilon\mu)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|\xi^{\prime}\|,\xi_{0}-(\varepsilon\mu)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|\xi^{\prime}\|,\xi_{0}+(\varepsilon\mu)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|\xi^{\prime}\|).

This shows that the characteristic set, without the contribution of the charges, is given by

\{\xi_{0}^{2}-(\varepsilon\mu)^{-1}\|\xi^{\prime}\|^{2}=0\}

with multiplicity two.

2.3.2.
Partially anisotropic case

In the case
\varepsilon=\text{diag}(\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2},\varepsilon_{2})
,
\varepsilon_{1}\neq\varepsilon_{2}
,
\mu=\mu 1_{3\times 3}
the diagonalization in the constant-coefficient case with
L^{p}
-bounded multipliers is still possible. We obtain the diagonal matrix:

d(x,\xi)=i\text{diag}(\xi_{0},\xi_{0},\xi_{0}-\varepsilon_{2}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|\xi^{\prime}\|,\xi_{0}+\varepsilon_{2}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|\xi^{\prime}\|,\xi_{0}-\|\xi^{\prime}\|_{\varepsilon},\xi_{0}+\|\xi^{\prime}\|_{\varepsilon})

with
\|\xi^{\prime}\|_{\varepsilon}=(\varepsilon_{2}^{-1}\xi_{1}^{2}+\varepsilon_{1}^{-1}\xi_{2}^{2}+\varepsilon_{1}^{-1}\xi_{3}^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}
. Clearly, we have

\varepsilon_{2}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|\xi^{\prime}\|=\|\xi^{\prime}\|_{\varepsilon}\;\Leftrightarrow\;\xi_{2}^{\prime}=\xi_{3}^{\prime}=0.

The characteristic set is given by the sphere

(\xi_{0}^{2}-\|\xi^{\prime}\|_{\varepsilon}^{2})(\xi_{0}^{2}-\varepsilon_{2}^{-1}\|\xi^{\prime}\|^{2})=0

and describes two ellipsoids, which are smoothly intersecting at the
\xi_{1}
-axis.

2.3.3.
Fully anisotropic case

To find the characteristic set in the fully anisotropic case, we symmetrize

\begin{pmatrix}i\xi_{0}&-i\mathcal{C}(\xi^{\prime})\mu^{-1}\\
i\mathcal{C}(\xi^{\prime})\varepsilon^{-1}&i\xi_{0}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\hat{\mathcal{D}}\\
\hat{\mathcal{B}}\end{pmatrix}=0

by multiplying with the matrix (cf.
[
16
, Proposition 1.3, p. 1835]
)

\begin{pmatrix}i\xi_{0}&i\mathcal{C}(\xi^{\prime})\mu^{-1}\\
-i\mathcal{C}(\xi^{\prime})\varepsilon^{-1}&i\xi_{0}\end{pmatrix}

to find

\begin{pmatrix}-\xi_{0}^{2}-\mathcal{C}(\xi^{\prime})\mu^{-1}\mathcal{C}(\xi^{\prime})\varepsilon^{-1}&0\\
0&-\xi_{0}^{2}-\mathcal{C}(\xi^{\prime})\varepsilon^{-1}\mathcal{C}(\xi^{\prime})\mu^{-1}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\hat{\mathcal{D}}\\
\hat{\mathcal{H}}\end{pmatrix}=0.

We compute

\begin{split}p(\xi)&=\det(-\xi_{0}^{2}-\mathcal{C}(\xi^{\prime})\mu^{-1}\mathcal{C}(\xi^{\prime})\varepsilon^{-1})\\
&=\det(-\xi_{0}^{2}-\mathcal{C}(\xi^{\prime})\varepsilon^{-1}\mathcal{C}(\xi^{\prime})\mu^{-1})=-\xi_{0}^{2}(\xi_{0}^{4}-\xi_{0}^{2}q_{0}(\xi)+q_{1}(\xi))\end{split}

with

\begin{split}q_{0}(\xi)&=\xi_{1}^{2}\big(\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{2}\mu_{3}}+\frac{1}{\mu_{2}\varepsilon_{3}}\big)+\xi_{2}^{2}\big(\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{1}\mu_{3}}+\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{3}\mu_{1}}\big)+\xi_{3}^{2}\big(\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{2}\mu_{1}}+\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{1}\mu_{2}}\big),\\
q_{1}(\xi)&=\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{1}\varepsilon_{2}\varepsilon_{3}\mu_{1}\mu_{2}\mu_{3}}(\varepsilon_{1}\xi_{1}^{2}+\varepsilon_{2}\xi_{2}^{2}+\varepsilon_{3}\xi_{3}^{2})(\mu_{1}\xi_{1}^{2}+\mu_{2}\xi_{2}^{2}+\mu_{3}\xi_{3}^{2}).\end{split}

It
[
16
, Section 3]
was proved that the condition for full anisotropy
\varepsilon=\text{diag}(\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2},\varepsilon_{3})
and
\mu=\text{diag}(\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\mu_{3})
is given by

(21)

\frac{\varepsilon_{1}}{\mu_{1}}\neq\frac{\varepsilon_{2}}{\mu_{2}}\neq\frac{\varepsilon_{3}}{\mu_{3}}\neq\frac{\varepsilon_{1}}{\mu_{1}}.

If this fails, then the characteristic set will be like in the isotropic or partially anisotropic case.

If (
21
) holds, then the characteristic set ceases to be smooth and becomes the Fresnel wave surface with conical singularities.

.

It can be conceived as union of three components:

•

a smooth and regular component with two principal curvatures bounded from below,

•

one-dimensional submanifolds with vanishing Gaussian curvature and one principal curvature,

•

neighbourhoods of (four) conical singularities.

This is classical and was already pointed out by Darboux
[
3
]
. The curvature was precisely quantified in
[
16
]
. Dispersive properties in the constant-coefficient case were first analyzed by Liess
[

]
. The conical singularities lead to the dispersive properties of the
2d
wave equation. We prove Strichartz estimates for rough coefficients in the companion paper
[

.

3.
Strichartz estimates in the isotropic case

The purpose of this section is to reduce (
1
) to half-wave equations in the isotropic case. The Strichartz estimates then follow from Proposition
1.7
. The key point is to diagonalize the principal symbol of

P(x,D)=\begin{pmatrix}\partial_{t}1_{3\times 3}&-\mathcal{C}(D)\mu^{-1}\\
\mathcal{C}(D)\varepsilon^{-1}&\partial_{t}1_{3\times 3}\end{pmatrix}.

The diagonalization argument follows the two-dimensional case, but is more involved. The eigenpairs in the partially anisotropic case had been computed in case of constant coefficients in
[
18
]
. This suffices for constant-coefficients, but for variable coefficients this diagonalization appears to lose regularity. However, in the isotropic case, we can find a regular diagonalization after an additional microlocalization.

Further reductions are standard, i.e., reduction to high frequencies and localization to a cube of size
1
, reduction to dyadic estimates, truncating frequencies of the coefficients. We start with diagonalizing the principal symbol:

3.1.
Diagonalizing the principal symbol in the isotropic case

We begin with the isotropic case
\varepsilon=\text{diag}(\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{1})
and
\mu=\text{diag}(\mu_{1},\mu_{1},\mu_{1})
. In the following we abuse notation and write
\varepsilon=\varepsilon_{1}
and
\mu=\mu_{1}
for sake of brevity.
In the isotropic case the diagonalization is as regular as in two dimensions after an additional localization in phase space. It turns out we have to distinguish one non-degenerate direction to find non-degenerate eigenvectors.

We use the block matrix structure to find eigenvectors of
p/i
.
We have

p/i=\begin{pmatrix}\xi_{0}&-\mathcal{C}(\xi^{\prime})\mu^{-1}\\
\mathcal{C}(\xi^{\prime})\varepsilon^{-1}&\xi_{0}\end{pmatrix}\text{ with }\mathcal{C}(\xi^{\prime})_{ij}=-i\varepsilon_{ijk}\xi_{k},

where

We find eigenvectors

\begin{pmatrix}\xi_{0}&-\mathcal{C}(\xi^{\prime})\mu^{-1}\\

v_{2}\end{pmatrix}=\lambda\begin{pmatrix}v_{1}\\
v_{2}\end{pmatrix}.

Let
\lambda^{\prime}=\lambda-\xi_{0}

(22)

\displaystyle-\mathcal{C}(\xi^{\prime})\mu^{-1}v_{2}

(23)

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}(\xi^{\prime})\varepsilon^{-1}v_{1}

In the following let
\xi^{\prime}\neq 0

\xi^{\prime}=0

\xi_{i}^{*}=\xi_{i}/|\xi^{\prime}|

\lambda=\xi_{0}

because
\text{span}(\xi^{\prime})=\ker(\mathcal{C}(\xi^{\prime}))

\lambda\neq\xi_{0}

\lambda^{\prime}=\lambda-\xi_{0}

) and (

(24)

\displaystyle-\mathcal{C}(\xi^{\prime})\mu^{-1}\mathcal{C}(\xi^{\prime})\varepsilon^{-1}v_{1}

(25)

\displaystyle-\mathcal{C}(\xi^{\prime})\varepsilon^{-1}\mathcal{C}(\xi^{\prime})\mu^{-1}v_{2}

\displaystyle={\lambda^{\prime}}^{2}v_{2}.

Since
\varepsilon
and
\mu
are isotropic and elliptic, we can define

\lambda^{*}=(\varepsilon\mu)^{\frac{1}{2}}\lambda^{\prime},

and write (

-\mathcal{C}^{2}(\xi^{\prime})v_{i}={\lambda^{*}}^{2}v_{i},\quad i=1,2.

We have
\mathcal{C}^{2}(\xi^{\prime})=-\|\xi^{\prime}\|^{2}1_{3\times 3}+\xi^{\prime}(\xi^{\prime})^{t}

\langle v_{i},\xi^{\prime}\rangle=0

\xi^{\prime}

\lambda^{\prime}\neq 0

\lambda^{*}\in\{-\|\xi^{\prime}\|;\|\xi^{\prime}\|\}

We shall construct eigenvectors depending on the non-degenerate direction of
\xi^{*}=\frac{\xi^{\prime}}{\|\xi^{\prime}\|}\in\mathbb{S}^{2}

\xi_{ij}^{2}=\xi_{i}^{2}+\xi_{j}^{2}
for

.

Eigenvectors for
|\xi_{1}^{*}|\gtrsim 1
:

We let

v_{1}^{(1)}=\begin{pmatrix}\frac{\xi_{2}}{\xi_{12}}\\
-\frac{\xi_{1}}{\xi_{12}}\\
0\end{pmatrix},\quad v_{2}^{(1)}=\frac{\mathcal{C}(\xi^{\prime})\varepsilon^{-1}}{\lambda^{\prime}}v_{1}^{(1)}=\pm\big(\frac{\mu}{\varepsilon}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\begin{pmatrix}\frac{\xi_{1}\xi_{3}}{\xi_{12}\|\xi^{\prime}\|}\\

The choice of

Secondly, we let

0\\
-\frac{\xi_{1}}{\xi_{13}}\end{pmatrix},\quad v_{2}^{(2)}=\frac{\mathcal{C}(\xi^{\prime})\varepsilon^{-1}}{\lambda^{\prime}}v_{1}^{(2)}=\pm\big(\frac{\mu}{\varepsilon}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\begin{pmatrix}\frac{-\xi_{1}\xi_{2}}{\xi_{13}\|\xi^{\prime}\|}\\

m^{(1)}(\xi)=\begin{pmatrix}\xi_{1}^{*}&0&\frac{\xi_{2}}{\xi_{12}}&\frac{\xi_{2}}{\xi_{12}}&\frac{\xi_{3}}{\xi_{13}}&\frac{\xi_{3}}{\xi_{13}}\\

\|S_{B}v\|_{L^{2}_{x}}\lesssim\|\mathcal{N}_{\lambda}\tilde{S}_{B}\mathcal{M}_{\lambda}S_{B}v\|_{L^{2}_{x}}+\|E_{\lambda}S_{B}v\|_{L^{2}_{x}}\lesssim\|\mathcal{M}_{\lambda}S_{B}v\|_{L^{2}_{x}}+\lambda^{-1}\|S_{B}v\|_{L^{2}_{x}}.

Absorbing
\lambda^{-1}\|S_{B}v\|_{L^{2}_{x}}
into the left hand-side finishes the proof.
∎

Proof of Theorem

.

4.3.1.
\partial\varepsilon\in L_{T}^{2}L_{x^{\prime}}^{\infty}

Reduction to dyadic estimate and frequency truncation.

It suffices to prove estimates close to the forbidden endpoint
(p,q)=(2,\infty)
,
\delta=0

(49)

\lambda^{-\frac{5}{4}-\delta}\|S_{\lambda}S^{\prime}_{\lambda}u\|_{L_{T}^{p}L^{q}_{x^{\prime}}}\lesssim_{T,\delta}\|S_{\lambda}S^{\prime}_{\lambda}u\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{2}_{x^{\prime}}}+\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|P_{\lambda}S_{\lambda}S^{\prime}_{\lambda}u\|_{L_{T}^{2}L^{2}_{x^{\prime}}}+\lambda^{-\frac{3}{4}}\|S^{\prime}_{\lambda}\rho_{em}\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x^{\prime}}^{2}}.

In the above display the coefficients of
P
are truncated at frequencies
\lesssim\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}
,
(p,q)
denotes a sharp Strichartz pair and
\delta>0
.

To see how (
49

14

\tilde{S}_{\lambda}\tilde{S}^{\prime}_{\lambda}P_{\lambda}S_{\lambda}S^{\prime}_{\lambda}u=P_{\lambda}S_{\lambda}S^{\prime}_{\lambda}u.

By
\tilde{S}_{\lambda}
the mildly enlarged frequency projection is denoted, likewise for

. Now we write

\tilde{S}_{\lambda}\tilde{S}^{\prime}_{\lambda}P_{\lambda}S_{\lambda}S^{\prime}_{\lambda}=\tilde{S}_{\lambda}\tilde{S}^{\prime}_{\lambda}PS_{\lambda}S^{\prime}_{\lambda}-\tilde{S}_{\lambda}\tilde{S}^{\prime}_{\lambda}P_{\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}\lesssim\cdot\lesssim\lambda}S_{\lambda}S^{\prime}_{\lambda}.

The contribution of the second term is estimated by

\begin{split}\|\nabla\times(\varepsilon^{-1}_{\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}\lesssim\cdot\lesssim\lambda}S_{\lambda}S^{\prime}_{\lambda}\mathcal{D}))\|_{L^{2}_{x}}&\lesssim\|\partial\varepsilon^{-1}\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x^{\prime}}^{\infty}}\|S_{\lambda}S^{\prime}_{\lambda}u\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x^{\prime}}^{2}}+\lambda\|\varepsilon^{-1}_{\gtrsim\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}}\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x^{\prime}}^{\infty}}\|S_{\lambda}S^{\prime}_{\lambda}u\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x^{\prime}}^{2}}\\

For the first term, we note that

\begin{split}\|\tilde{S}_{\lambda}\tilde{S}^{\prime}_{\lambda}PS_{\lambda}S^{\prime}_{\lambda}u\|_{L^{2}_{x}}&\leq\|\tilde{S}_{\lambda}\tilde{S}^{\prime}_{\lambda}Pu\|_{L^{2}_{x}}+\|\tilde{S}_{\lambda}\tilde{S}^{\prime}_{\lambda}\nabla\times([\varepsilon^{-1},S_{\lambda}S^{\prime}_{\lambda}]\mathcal{D})\|_{L^{2}_{x}}\\

The second estimate follows from

\|\tilde{S}_{\lambda}\tilde{S}^{\prime}_{\lambda}\nabla\times([\varepsilon^{-1},S_{\lambda}S^{\prime}_{\lambda}]\mathcal{D})\|_{L^{2}_{x}}\lesssim\lambda\|[\varepsilon^{-1},S_{\lambda}S^{\prime}_{\lambda}]\mathcal{D}\|_{L^{2}_{x}}\lesssim\|\mathcal{D}\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x^{\prime}}^{2}},

which is based on the commutator estimate

as a consequence of the kernel estimate (
[
25

). Hence, taking the supremum gives

\sup_{\lambda\geq 1}\big(\lambda^{-\frac{5}{4}-\delta}\|S^{\prime}_{\lambda}S_{\lambda}u\|_{L_{T}^{p}L_{x^{\prime}}^{q}}\big)\lesssim\|u\|_{L_{T}^{\infty}L_{x^{\prime}}^{2}}+\||D|^{-\frac{1}{2}}Pu\|_{L^{2}_{x}}+\|\langle D^{\prime}\rangle^{-\frac{3}{4}}\rho_{em}\|_{L_{T}^{\infty}L_{x^{\prime}}^{2}}.

Recall the estimate for the contribution away from the characteristic surface:

\|\langle D^{\prime}\rangle^{-\rho}S_{|\tau|\gg\|\xi^{\prime}\|}u\|_{L_{T}^{p}L_{x^{\prime}}^{q}}\lesssim\|u\|_{L_{T}^{\infty}L_{x^{\prime}}^{2}}+\|Pu\|_{L_{T}^{2}L_{x^{\prime}}^{2}}.

Since

\|\langle D^{\prime}\rangle^{-\frac{5}{4}-\delta}S_{|\tau|\lesssim\|\xi^{\prime}\|}u\|_{L_{T}^{p}L_{x^{\prime}}^{q}}\lesssim\|u\|_{L_{T}^{\infty}L_{x^{\prime}}^{2}}+\||D|^{-\frac{1}{2}}Pu\|_{L_{T}^{2}L_{x^{\prime}}^{2}}+\|\langle D^{\prime}\rangle^{-\frac{3}{4}}\rho_{em}\|_{L_{T}^{\infty}L_{x^{\prime}}^{2}}

Applying this to homogeneous solutions (together with the better estimate away from the characteristic surface), we find

\|\langle D^{\prime}\rangle^{-\frac{5}{4}-\delta}u\|_{L_{T}^{p}L_{x^{\prime}}^{q}}\lesssim\|u(0)\|_{L_{x^{\prime}}^{2}}+\|\langle D^{\prime}\rangle^{-\frac{3}{4}}\rho_{em}(0)\|_{L_{x^{\prime}}^{2}}.

We interpolate the above display for the sharp Strichartz exponents with

with the energy estimate

\begin{split}\|\langle D^{\prime}\rangle^{-\rho-\frac{1}{2p}-\delta}u\|_{L_{T}^{p}L_{x^{\prime}}^{q}}&\lesssim\|u\|_{L_{T}^{\infty}L_{x^{\prime}}^{2}}+\|Pu\|_{L_{T}^{1}L^{2}_{x^{\prime}}}\\
&\quad+\|\langle D^{\prime}\rangle^{-\frac{3}{4}}\rho_{em}(0)\|_{L_{x^{\prime}}^{2}}+\|\langle D^{\prime}\rangle^{-\frac{3}{4}}\partial_{t}\rho_{em}\|_{L_{T}^{1}L_{x^{\prime}}^{2}}.\end{split}

Proof.

For the proof of (
52

\frac{1}{4}
derivatives (again essentially due to (

4.3.2.
Lipschitz coefficients

Now we shall see how to modify the above argument to deal with Lipschitz coefficients and show estimates with slightly less derivative loss. After the usual reductions, we shall prove the dyadic estimate

(55)

\lambda^{-\frac{7}{6}-\delta}\|S_{\lambda}S^{\prime}_{\lambda}u\|_{L_{T}^{p}L_{x^{\prime}}^{q}}\lesssim_{T}\|S_{\lambda}S^{\prime}_{\lambda}u\|_{L_{T}^{2}L_{x^{\prime}}^{2}}+\lambda^{-\frac{1}{3}}\|P_{\lambda^{\frac{2}{3}}}S_{\lambda}S^{\prime}_{\lambda}u\|_{L_{T}^{2}L_{x^{\prime}}^{2}}+\lambda^{-\frac{2}{3}}\|S^{\prime}_{\lambda}\rho_{em}\|_{L_{T}^{\infty}L_{x^{\prime}}^{2}}.

The coefficients of
P
are truncated at frequencies
\lambda^{\frac{2}{3}}
. The frequency truncation at
\lambda^{\frac{2}{3}}
will not be emphasized in the following anymore, and we simply write
P_{\lambda}
. We observe like above

\tilde{S}_{\lambda}\tilde{S}^{\prime}_{\lambda}P_{\lambda}S_{\lambda}S^{\prime}_{\lambda}u=P_{\lambda}S_{\lambda}S^{\prime}_{\lambda}u

and write

\tilde{S}_{\lambda}\tilde{S}^{\prime}_{\lambda}P_{\lambda}S_{\lambda}S^{\prime}_{\lambda}=\tilde{S}_{\lambda}\tilde{S}^{\prime}_{\lambda}PS_{\lambda}S^{\prime}_{\lambda}-\tilde{S}_{\lambda}\tilde{S}^{\prime}_{\lambda}P_{\lambda^{\frac{2}{3}}\lesssim\cdot\lesssim\lambda}S_{\lambda}S^{\prime}_{\lambda}.

We estimate the contribution of the second term by

\begin{split}\|\nabla\times(\varepsilon^{-1}_{\lambda^{\frac{2}{3}}\lesssim\cdot\lesssim\lambda}S_{\lambda}S^{\prime}_{\lambda}\mathcal{D})\|_{L^{2}_{x}}&\lesssim\|\partial\varepsilon^{-1}\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\|S_{\lambda}S^{\prime}_{\lambda}u\|_{L^{2}_{x}}\\
&\quad+\lambda\|\varepsilon^{-1}_{\geq\lambda^{\frac{2}{3}}}\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\|S_{\lambda}S^{\prime}_{\lambda}u\|_{L^{2}_{x}}\\
&\lesssim\lambda^{\frac{1}{3}}(1+\|\partial\varepsilon\|_{L_{T,x^{\prime}}^{\infty}})\|S_{\lambda}S^{\prime}_{\lambda}u\|_{L^{2}_{x}}.\end{split}

For the first term we use a commutator argument

\begin{split}\|\tilde{S}_{\lambda}\tilde{S}^{\prime}_{\lambda}PS_{\lambda}S^{\prime}_{\lambda}u\|_{L^{2}_{x}}&\leq\|\tilde{S}_{\lambda}\tilde{S}^{\prime}_{\lambda}Pu\|_{L^{2}_{x}}+\|\tilde{S}_{\lambda}\tilde{S}^{\prime}_{\lambda}\nabla\times([\varepsilon^{-1},S_{\lambda}S^{\prime}_{\lambda}])\mathcal{D})\|_{L^{2}_{x}}\\
&\lesssim\|\tilde{S}_{\lambda}\tilde{S}_{\lambda}^{\prime}Pu\|_{L^{2}_{x}}+\|u\|_{L^{2}_{x}}.\end{split}

The second estimate follows by

\|\tilde{S}_{\lambda}\tilde{S}^{\prime}_{\lambda}\nabla\times([\varepsilon^{-1},S_{\lambda}S^{\prime}_{\lambda}]\mathcal{D})\|_{L^{2}_{x}}\lesssim\lambda\|[\varepsilon^{-1},S_{\lambda}S^{\prime}_{\lambda}]\mathcal{D}\|_{L^{2}_{x}}\lesssim\|\mathcal{D}\|_{L^{2}_{x}},

which is a consequence of

\|[\varepsilon^{-1},S_{\lambda}S^{\prime}_{\lambda}]\|_{L^{2}_{x}\to L^{2}_{x}}\lesssim\lambda^{-1}\|\partial\varepsilon\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}

from a kernel estimate (cf.
[
25
, Eq. (3.21)]
). Hence, we find like in the beginning of Subsection
4.3.1
:

\|\langle D^{\prime}\rangle^{-\frac{7}{6}-\delta}S_{|\tau|\lesssim\|\xi^{\prime}\|}u\|_{L_{T}^{p}L_{x^{\prime}}^{q}}\lesssim_{T,\delta}\|u\|_{L_{T}^{2}L_{x^{\prime}}}+\||D|^{-\frac{1}{3}}Pu\|_{L_{T}^{2}L_{x^{\prime}}^{2}}+\|\langle D^{\prime}\rangle^{-\frac{3}{4}}\rho_{em}\|_{L_{T}^{\infty}L_{x^{\prime}}^{2}}.

Together with the better estimate for the contribution away from the characteristic surface, we find like in Subsection
4.3.1

\begin{split}\|\langle D^{\prime}\rangle^{-\rho-\frac{1}{2p}-\delta}u\|_{L_{T}^{p}L_{x^{\prime}}^{q}}&\lesssim\|u(0)\|_{L_{x^{\prime}}^{2}}+\|Pu\|_{L_{T}^{1}L_{x^{\prime}}^{2}}\\

Lemma 5.6

Proof.

\begin{split}\frac{d}{dt}E^{s}[u]&=2\langle\langle D^{\prime}\rangle^{s-1}\partial_{t}u,\langle D^{\prime}\rangle^{s-1}u\rangle+2\langle\langle D^{\prime}\rangle^{s-1}\partial_{t}^{2}u,\langle D^{\prime}\rangle^{s-1}\partial_{t}u\rangle\\

&=I+II+III+IV.\end{split}

The first and last term are estimated by Cauchy-Schwarz and Hölder’s inequality as

\begin{split}|\langle\langle D^{\prime}\rangle^{s-1}\partial_{t}u,\langle D^{\prime}\rangle^{s-1}u\rangle|&\lesssim E^{s}[u],\\

We summarize the second and third component as

(II+III)/2=\langle\nabla\times(\varepsilon(u)\langle D^{\prime}\rangle^{s-1}\nabla\times u)+\langle D^{\prime}\rangle^{s-1}\nabla\times(\varepsilon(u)\nabla\times u,\langle D^{\prime}\rangle^{s-1}\partial_{t}u\rangle.

Before we compute the commutator, we note that

\nabla\times(\varepsilon(u)\nabla\times u)=O(\partial_{x^{\prime}}\varepsilon)\nabla\times u-\varepsilon(u)\Delta u.

The first term is lower order because by the fractional Leibniz rule we find

\begin{split}\|\langle D^{\prime}\rangle^{s-1}(\partial_{x^{\prime}}\varepsilon)(\nabla\times u)\|_{L_{x^{\prime}}^{2}}&\lesssim\|\partial\varepsilon\|_{L_{x^{\prime}}^{\infty}}\|\langle D^{\prime}\rangle^{s-1}\nabla\times u\|_{L_{x^{\prime}}^{2}}+\|\nabla\times u\|_{L_{x^{\prime}}^{\infty}}\|\langle D^{\prime}\rangle^{s-1}\partial\varepsilon\|_{L^{2}_{x^{\prime}}}\\
&\lesssim\|\nabla_{x}u\|_{L_{x^{\prime}}^{\infty}}\|\langle D^{\prime}\rangle^{s-1}\partial u\|_{L^{2}_{x^{\prime}}}.\end{split}

The ultimate inequality is a consequence of Moser estimates (assuming a priori bounds on

The second term can be argued to be lower order like in (

The ultimate estimate follows from
E^{s}[u]\approx_{\|u\|_{L_{x^{\prime}}^{\infty}}}\|u\|_{H^{s}_{x^{\prime}}}
for divergence-free functions. Applying Grönwall’s inequality yields

E^{s}[u](t)\lesssim e^{C\int_{0}^{t}(1+\|\nabla_{x}u\|_{L_{x^{\prime}}^{\infty}})ds}E^{s}[u](0).

Let
s>\frac{13}{6}
and
u
be a smooth solution to (
61
). Then, there is a lower semicontinuous
T=T(\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s}_{x^{\prime}}})
such that the following estimate holds:

\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|u(t)\|_{H^{s}_{x^{\prime}}}\lesssim\|u(0)\|_{H^{s}_{x^{\prime}}}.

Proof.

Since
u
is divergence free and for

we have

, it suffices to show an a priori estimate for the energy functional

. To this end, we control
\|\nabla_{x}u\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x^{\prime}}^{\infty}}
by Strichartz estimates.

We define the auxiliary function

and apply Strichartz estimates to find

(71)

\|\langle D^{\prime}\rangle^{-\rho-\frac{1}{6p}-\varepsilon}\nabla_{x}v\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x^{\prime}}^{\infty}}\lesssim\|\nabla_{x}v\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x^{\prime}}^{2}}+\|P(x,u,D)v\|_{L_{t}^{1}L_{x^{\prime}}^{2}}.

Since

, (

\|\nabla_{x}u\|_{L_{T}^{2}L_{x^{\prime}}^{\infty}}\lesssim_{T}\|\langle D^{\prime}\rangle^{s-1}\nabla_{x}u\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x^{\prime}}^{2}}+\|[P(x,u,D),\langle D^{\prime}\rangle^{s-1}]u\|_{L^{2}_{x^{\prime}}}.

By the commutator estimate from above, we find

(72)

\|\nabla_{x}u\|_{L_{T}^{2}L_{x^{\prime}}^{\infty}}\lesssim_{T}(1+T^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\nabla u\|_{L_{T}^{2}L_{x^{\prime}}^{\infty}})\|\langle D^{\prime}\rangle^{s-1}\nabla_{x}u\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x^{\prime}}^{2}}.

Moreover, the energy estimate gives

(73)

E^{s}[u](t)\lesssim_{\|u\|_{L_{x}^{\infty}}}e^{C(t+\int_{0}^{t}\|\nabla_{x}u(s)\|_{L_{x^{\prime}}^{\infty}}ds)}E^{s}[u](0).

(

) and (
73

and

∎

We turn to estimates for differences of solutions in

Lemma 5.8
.

Let

be smooth solutions to (

The difference

\begin{split}P(x,u,D)w&=\partial_{t}^{2}(u-v)+\nabla\times[\varepsilon(u)\nabla\times u-\varepsilon(u)\nabla\times v]\\

with

The above notation means that

(\nabla_{x^{\prime}}u,\nabla_{x^{\prime}}v)
and
A_{0}
is linear in

and quadratic in
\nabla_{x^{\prime}}u
and
\nabla_{x^{\prime}}v
. To prove (

for some

.
For

we use interpolation to bound

Other quadratic expressions
(\nabla_{x^{\prime}}u,\nabla_{x^{\prime}}v)
and

We conclude the argument by frequency envelopes:

Definition 5.9
.

(c_{N})_{N\in 2^{\mathbb{N}_{0}}}\in\ell^{2}
is a frequency envelope for functions
(u_{0},u_{1})\in H^{s}_{x}\times H^{s-1}_{x}
if we have the following two properties:

a)

Energy bound:

\|S^{\prime}_{N}(u_{0},u_{1})\|_{H^{s}\times H^{s-1}}\leq c_{N},

b)

Slowly varying property:

\frac{c_{N}}{c_{J}}\lesssim\big[\frac{N}{J}\big]^{\delta}.

We use the notation
\big[\frac{N}{J}\big]=\min(\frac{N}{J},\frac{J}{N})
.

S^{\prime}_{N}
denote the spatial Littlewood-Paley projections. Envelopes are sharp if

\|u\|^{2}_{H^{s}\times H^{s-1}}\approx\sum_{N}c_{N}^{2}.

To construct an envelope for
(u_{0},u_{1})\in H^{s}\times H^{s-1}
, we let

\tilde{c}_{N}=\|S^{\prime}_{N}(u_{0},u_{1})\|_{H^{s}}\text{ and }c_{N}=\sup_{J}\big[\frac{N}{J}\big]^{\delta}c_{J}.

We use the following regularization: Let
(u_{0},u_{1})\in H^{s}\times H^{s-1}
with size
L
and let
(c_{N})
be a sharp frequency envelope. For
u_{0}
we consider
(u_{0},u_{1})^{M}=S^{\prime}_{\leq M}(u_{0},u_{1})
frequency truncations as regularization. We note the following properties:

•

Uniform bounds:

\|S^{\prime}_{N}(u_{0}^{M},u_{1}^{M})\|_{H^{s}\times H^{s-1}}\lesssim c_{N},

•

High frequency bounds:

\|(u_{0}^{M},u_{1}^{M})\|_{H^{s+j}\times H^{s+j-1}}\lesssim M^{j}c_{M}\quad(j\geq 0).

•

Difference bounds:

\|(u_{0},u_{1})^{2M}-(u_{0},u_{1})^{M}\|_{H^{1}\times L^{2}}\lesssim M^{-s}c_{M}.

•

Limit:

(u_{0},u_{1})=\lim_{M\to\infty}(u_{0}^{M},u_{1}^{M})\text{ in }H^{s}\times H^{s-1}.

We obtain for the regularized initial data a family of smooth solutions. The existence depends only on
L=\|(u_{0},u_{1})\|_{H^{s}\times H^{s-1}}
. We have the following:

i)

A priori estimates at high regularity:

\|u^{M}\|_{C(0,T;H^{s+j})}\lesssim M^{j}c_{M},\quad j\geq 0,

ii)

Difference bounds:

\|u^{2M}-u^{M}\|_{C(0,T;H^{1}\times L^{2})}\lesssim M^{-s}c_{M}.

From the difference bounds and a telescoping sum argument, we have the convergence of
u^{M}
as
M\to\infty
in
C_{T}L^{2}
. Writing

u-u^{M}=\sum_{K=M}^{\infty}u^{2K}-u^{K}

we can argue by estimates at higher regularity and difference bounds that
u^{2K}-u^{K}
is essentially concentrated at frequencies
K
. This yields the estimate

\|u-u^{M}\|_{C(0,T;H^{s})}\lesssim c_{\geq M}

and convergence of
u^{M}
in
C_{T}H^{s}
. A variant of the argument also gives continuity of the data-to-solution mapping. The proof of Theorem
1.8
is complete.

5.2.
Partially anisotropic permittivity

In this section we improve the local well-posedness for quasilinear Maxwell equations in the case of partially anisotropic permittivity
\varepsilon^{-1}=(\psi(|\mathcal{D}_{1}|^{2}),1,1)
. To prove energy estimates, we have to rewrite the Maxwell system

(77)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}\partial_{t}\mathcal{D}&=\nabla\times\mathcal{H},\\
\partial_{t}\mathcal{H}&=-\nabla\times(\varepsilon^{-1}(\mathcal{D})\mathcal{D})\end{array}\right.

into non-divergence form. We compute

\nabla\times(\varepsilon^{-1}(\mathcal{D})\mathcal{D})=\begin{pmatrix}\partial_{2}\mathcal{D}_{3}-\partial_{3}\mathcal{D}_{2}\\
\big(\psi(|\mathcal{D}_{1}|^{2})+2\psi^{\prime}(|\mathcal{D}_{1}|^{2})\mathcal{D}_{1}^{2}\big)\partial_{3}\mathcal{D}_{1}-\partial_{1}\mathcal{D}_{3}\\
\partial_{1}\mathcal{D}_{2}-\big(\psi(|\mathcal{D}_{1}|^{2})+2\psi^{\prime}(|\mathcal{D}_{1}|^{2})\mathcal{D}_{1}^{2}\big)\partial_{2}\mathcal{D}_{1}\end{pmatrix}.

This suggests to work with the modified permittivity

(78)

\tilde{\varepsilon}^{-1}(\mathcal{D})=(\psi(|\mathcal{D}_{1}|^{2})+2\psi^{\prime}(|\mathcal{D}_{1}|^{2})\mathcal{D}_{1}^{2},1,1),

for which we prove Strichartz estimates in divergence form. It turns out that these yield suitable Strichartz estimates for the equation in non-divergence form.

We shall prove Theorem
1.9
following the same steps like above.
We begin with a priori estimates for solutions for
s>9/4
: We consider the energy functional

(79)

E^{s}[u](t)=\langle\langle D^{\prime}\rangle^{s}u(t),C(u)\langle D^{\prime}\rangle^{s}u(t)\rangle\approx_{\|u\|_{L_{x}^{\infty}}}\|u(t)\|_{H^{s}_{x^{\prime}}},

for which we want to prove the estimate

(80)

E^{s}[u](t)\leq C(\|u\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}})e^{c(\|u\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}})\int_{0}^{t}\|\nabla_{x^{\prime}}u(s)\|_{L^{\infty}_{x^{\prime}}}ds}E^{s}[u](0).

To cancel the top-order terms, we define symmetric
C(u)
such that we find the estimate

\frac{d}{dt}E^{s}[u](t)\leq C(\|u\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}})\|\nabla_{x}u\|_{L^{\infty}_{x^{\prime}}}E^{s}[u](t)

to hold. To this end, we rewrite (
77
) as
\partial_{t}u=\mathcal{A}_{j}(u)\partial_{j}u
and require

(81)

C(u)\mathcal{A}^{j}(u)=\mathcal{A}^{j}(u)^{*}C(u).

The matrices
\mathcal{A}^{j}(u)
take the form

\mathcal{A}^{j}(u)=\begin{pmatrix}0&A^{j}_{1}(u)\\
A^{j}_{2}(u)&0\end{pmatrix}

and we have
(A_{1}^{j})_{mn}=-\varepsilon_{jmn}
with
\varepsilon
denoting the Levi–Civita symbol. For
A_{2}^{j}
we find
(A_{2}^{1})_{mn}=\varepsilon_{1mn}
and

A_{2}^{2}=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&-1\\
0&0&0\\
\psi(|\mathcal{D}_{1}|^{2})+2\psi^{\prime}(|\mathcal{D}_{1}|^{2})\mathcal{D}_{1}^{2}&0&0\end{pmatrix},\quad A_{2}^{3}=\begin{pmatrix}0&1&0\\
-\psi(|\mathcal{D}_{1}|^{2})-2\psi^{\prime}(|\mathcal{D}_{1}|^{2})\mathcal{D}_{1}^{2}&0&0\\
0&0&0\end{pmatrix}.

With the ansatz

C(u)=\begin{pmatrix}C_{1}(u)&0\\
0&1_{3\times 3}\end{pmatrix}

(
81
) becomes
A_{2}^{j}=(A_{1}^{j})^{t}C_{1}(u)
. A straight-forward computation yields

C_{1}(u)=\begin{pmatrix}\psi(|\mathcal{D}_{1}|^{2})+2\psi^{\prime}(|\mathcal{D}_{1}|^{2})\mathcal{D}_{1}^{2}&0&0\\
0&1&0\\
0&0&1\end{pmatrix}.

We are ready for the proof of the following proposition:

Proposition 5.10
.

Let
s\geq 0
and
u=(\mathcal{D},\mathcal{H})
be a smooth solution to (
77
). Then, (
80
) holds true. For
s>9/4
, there is a time
T=T(\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s}})
, which is lower semicontinuous such that

(82)

\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|u(t)\|_{H^{s}_{x^{\prime}}}\lesssim\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s}_{x^{\prime}}}.

Proof.

We compute

\begin{split}\frac{d}{dt}E^{s}[u](t)&=\langle\langle D^{\prime}\rangle^{s}\sum_{j=1}^{3}\mathcal{A}^{j}(u)\partial_{j}u,C(u)\langle D^{\prime}\rangle^{s}u\rangle+\langle\langle D^{\prime}\rangle^{s}u,(\frac{d}{dt}C(u))\langle D^{\prime}\rangle^{s}u\rangle\\
&\quad+\langle\langle D^{\prime}\rangle^{s}u,C(u)\langle D^{\prime}\rangle^{s}\sum_{j=1}^{3}\mathcal{A}^{j}(u)\partial_{j}u\rangle=I+II+III.\end{split}

Clearly, by using the equation and Hölder’s inequality, we find

II\lesssim_{\|u\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}}\|\nabla_{x^{\prime}}u\|_{L^{\infty}}\|u\|^{2}_{H^{s}_{x^{\prime}}}.

Via integration by parts, the Kato-Ponce commutator estimate, and Moser estimates, we find

\begin{split}|I+III|&\lesssim_{\|u\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}}\|\nabla_{x^{\prime}}u\|_{L^{\infty}_{x^{\prime}}}\|u\|^{2}_{H^{s}_{x^{\prime}}}\\
&\;+\big|\sum_{j=1}^{3}(\langle\langle D^{\prime}\rangle^{s}\partial_{j}u,\mathcal{A}^{j}(u)C(u)\langle D^{\prime}\rangle^{s}u\rangle-\langle\langle D^{\prime}\rangle^{s}\partial_{j}u,C(u)\mathcal{A}^{j}(u)\langle D^{\prime}\rangle^{s}u\rangle\big|.\end{split}

The term in the second line vanishes by (
81
).
Taking the estimates together, we have

\frac{d}{dt}E^{s}[u](t)\lesssim_{\|u\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}}\|\nabla_{x^{\prime}}u(t)\|_{L^{\infty}_{x^{\prime}}}E^{s}[u](t)

and (
80
) follows from Grønwall’s argument.

To prove a priori estimates for
s>9/4
, we use Strichartz estimates and a continuity argument. We require that
\|\nabla_{x^{\prime}}u\|_{L^{2}(0,T_{0};L^{\infty}_{x^{\prime}})}\leq K
for fixed
K>0
and a maximally defined
T_{0}>0
. Note that this gives

\|\nabla_{x}\varepsilon\|_{L^{2}(0,T_{0};L^{\infty}_{x^{\prime}})}\lesssim_{A}K\text{ and }\|\nabla_{x}\varepsilon\|_{L^{1}(0,T_{0};L^{\infty}_{x^{\prime}})}\lesssim_{A}T_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}K.

Hence, we have uniform constants in the energy and Strichartz estimates

\|\langle D^{\prime}\rangle^{-\alpha}w\|_{L^{p}(0,T;L^{\infty})}\lesssim\|w\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x^{\prime}}^{2}}+\|\tilde{P}(x,D)w\|_{L_{t}^{1}L_{x^{\prime}}^{2}}

for
\alpha>\rho+\frac{1}{3p}
from Theorem
1.5
in the charge-free case, where
\tilde{P}
is the time-dependent Maxwell operator with permittivity
\tilde{\varepsilon}^{-1}
as defined in (
78
). This can be recast as (using
\|\partial\tilde{\varepsilon}\|_{L_{T}^{1}L_{x^{\prime}}^{\infty}}<\infty
)

(83)

\|\langle D^{\prime}\rangle^{-\alpha}w\|_{L^{p}(0,T;L^{q})}\lesssim\|w\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x^{\prime}}^{2}}+\|Q(x,D)w\|_{L_{t}^{1}L_{x^{\prime}}^{2}}

for
Q(x,D)=\partial_{t}1_{6\times 6}-\mathcal{A}^{j}(u)\partial_{j}
denoting the time-dependent Maxwell operator in non-divergence form.

By applying this estimate to
w=\langle D^{\prime}\rangle^{\alpha+1}u
and the Kato–Ponce commutator estimate (for which it is necessary to change to the non-divergence form), we obtain the estimate

\|\nabla_{x^{\prime}}u\|_{L^{4}(0,T;L^{\infty}_{x^{\prime}})}\lesssim_{\|u\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}}(1+T^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\nabla_{x^{\prime}}u\|_{L^{2}_{T}L_{x^{\prime}}^{\infty}})\|u\|_{L_{T}^{\infty}H^{s}_{x^{\prime}}}.

Together with (
80
), this can be bootstrapped to prove the claim.
∎

By similar arguments to
[
19
]
, we prove the following
L^{2}
-bound for differences:

Proposition 5.11
.

Let
u^{1}
and
u^{2}
be two smooth solutions to (
77
), and set
v=u^{1}-u^{2}
. Then, the estimate

\|v(t)\|_{2}\lesssim_{A}e^{c(A)\int_{0}^{t}B(s)ds}\|v(0)\|_{L^{2}}

holds true with
A=\|u^{1}\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}+\|u^{2}\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}
and

. For
s>9/4

such that

\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|v(t)\|_{L^{2}_{x^{\prime}}}\lesssim_{\|u^{i}(0)\|_{H^{s}_{x^{\prime}}}}\|v(0)\|_{L^{2}_{x^{\prime}}}.

Proof.

First we note

\begin{split}\frac{d}{dt}v(t)&=\sum_{j=1}^{3}\mathcal{A}^{j}(u)\partial_{j}u^{1}-\sum_{j=1}^{3}\mathcal{A}^{j}(u^{2})\partial_{j}u^{2}\\
&=\sum_{j=1}^{3}\mathcal{A}^{j}(u^{1})\partial_{j}v+\sum_{j=1}^{3}[\mathcal{A}^{j}(u^{1})-\mathcal{A}^{j}(u^{2})]\partial_{j}u^{2}\\
&=\sum_{j=1}^{3}\mathcal{A}^{j}(u^{1})\partial_{j}v+\sum_{j=1}^{3}\mathcal{B}^{j}(u^{1},u^{2})v\partial_{j}u^{2}.\end{split}

Let
E^{0}[v](t)=\langle v(t),C(u^{1})v(t)\rangle
and compute

\begin{split}\frac{d}{dt}E^{0}[v](t)&=\langle\sum_{j=1}^{3}\mathcal{A}^{j}(u^{1})\partial_{j}v,C(u^{1})v\rangle+\langle v,(\frac{d}{dt}C(u^{1}))v\rangle+\langle v,C(u^{1})\sum_{j=1}^{3}\mathcal{A}^{j}(u^{1})\partial_{j}v\rangle\\

The main terms

5.10
via integration by parts and Moser estimates:

|I+III|\lesssim_{\|u^{1}\|_{L^{\infty}}}\|\nabla_{x^{\prime}}u\|_{L^{\infty}_{x^{\prime}}}\|v(t)\|_{L^{2}_{x^{\prime}}}^{2}.

We find like above by Hölder’s inequality

|II|\lesssim_{\|u^{1}\|_{L^{\infty}}}\|\nabla_{x^{\prime}}u^{1}\|_{L^{\infty}_{x^{\prime}}}\|v(t)\|_{L^{2}_{x^{\prime}}}^{2},

and

and

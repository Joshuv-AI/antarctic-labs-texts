Hubble Tension and Cosmological Imprints of U ​ ( 1 ) X U(1)_{X} Gauge Symmetry: U ​ ( 1 ) B 3 − 3 ​ L i U(1)_{B_{3}-3L_{i}} as a case study
Dilip Kumar Ghosh, Purusottam Ghosh, Sk Jeesun, Rahul Srivastava

Abstract
The current upper limit on N eff N_{\rm eff} at the time of CMB by Planck 2018 can place stringent constraints in the parameter space of BSM paradigms where their additional interactions may affect neutrino decoupling. Motivated by this fact in this paper we explore the consequences of light gauge boson ( Z ′ Z^{\prime} ) emerging from local U ​ ( 1 ) X U(1)_{X} symmetry in N eff N_{\rm eff} at the time of CMB. First, we analyze the generic U ​ ( 1 ) X U(1)_{X} models with arbitrary charge assignments for the SM fermions and show that, in the context of N eff N_{\rm eff} the generic U ​ ( 1 ) X U(1)_{X} gauged models can be broadly classified into two categories, depending on the charge assignments of first generation leptons. We then perform a detailed analysis with two specific U ​ ( 1 ) X U(1)_{X} models: U ​ ( 1 ) B 3 − 3 ​ L e U(1)_{B_{3}-3L_{e}} and U ​ ( 1 ) B 3 − 3 ​ L μ U(1)_{B_{3}-3L_{\mu}} and explore the contribution in N eff N_{\rm eff} due to the presence of Z ′ Z^{\prime} realized in those models. For comparison, we also showcase the constraints from low energy experiments like: Borexino, Xenon 1T, neutrino trident, etc. We show that in a specific parameter space, particularly in the low mass region of Z ′ Z^{\prime} , the bound from N eff N_{\rm eff} (Planck 2018) is more stringent than the experimental constraints. Additionally, a part of the regions of the same parameter space may also relax the H 0 H_{0} tension.

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2E_{\chi}}\int d\Pi_{i}d\Pi_{j}d\Pi_{k}(2\pi)^{4}\delta^{4}(p_{\chi}+p_{i}-p_{j}-p_{k})\times

Temperature evolution

Now with all the collision terms, we are set to formulate the energy evaluation equations.
Following the prescription in appendix-
A
we can write the following energy density evaluation equations:

\displaystyle\frac{d\rho_{\nu_{i}}}{dt}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\left(4H\rho_{\nu_{i}}-\left(\frac{\delta\rho_{\nu_{i}\to e}}{\delta t}\right)_{\rm tot}-\sum_{j\neq i}\left(\frac{\delta\rho_{\nu_{i}\to\nu_{j}}}{\delta t}\right)_{\rm tot}+\frac{\delta\rho_{Z^{\prime}\to\nu_{i}}}{\delta t}\right)

(53)

\displaystyle\frac{d\rho_{Z^{\prime}}}{dt}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\left(3H(\rho_{Z^{\prime}}+P_{Z^{\prime}})-\sum_{i}\frac{\delta\rho_{Z^{\prime}\to\nu_{i}}}{\delta t}-\frac{\delta\rho_{Z^{\prime}\to e}}{\delta t}\right)

(54)

\displaystyle\frac{d\rho_{\gamma}}{dt}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\left(4H\rho_{\gamma}+3H\left(\rho_{e}+p_{e}\right)+\sum_{i}\left(\frac{\delta\rho_{\nu_{i}\to e}}{\delta t}\right)_{\rm tot}+\frac{\delta\rho_{Z^{\prime}\to e}}{\delta t}\right),

(55)

where the
\sum_{i}
indicates the summation of energy density transfer rates over all
3

\nu~(e,\mu,\tau)

\left(\frac{\delta\rho_{\nu_{i}\to\nu_{j}}}{\delta t}\right)_{\rm tot}

\displaystyle\frac{dT_{\nu_{i}}}{dt}

\displaystyle-\left(4H\rho_{\nu_{i}}-\left(\frac{\delta\rho_{\nu_{i}\to e}}{\delta t}\right)_{\rm tot}-\sum_{j\neq i}\left(\frac{\delta\rho_{\nu_{i}\to\nu_{j}}}{\delta t}\right)_{\rm tot}+\frac{\delta\rho_{Z^{\prime}\to\nu_{i}}}{\delta t}\right)\left(\frac{\partial\rho_{\nu_{i}}}{\partial T_{\nu_{i}}}\right)^{-1}

(56)

\displaystyle\frac{dT_{Z^{\prime}}}{dt}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\left(3H(\rho_{Z^{\prime}}+P_{Z^{\prime}})-\sum_{i}\frac{\delta\rho_{Z^{\prime}\to\nu_{i}}}{\delta t}-\frac{\delta\rho_{Z^{\prime}\to e}}{\delta t}\right)\left(\frac{\partial\rho_{Z^{\prime}}}{\partial T_{Z^{\prime}}}\right)^{-1}

\displaystyle\frac{dT_{\gamma}}{dt}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\left(4H\rho_{\gamma}+3H\left(\rho_{e}+p_{e}\right)+\sum_{i}\left(\frac{\delta\rho_{\nu_{i}\to e}}{\delta t}\right)_{\rm tot}+\frac{\delta\rho_{Z^{\prime}\to e}}{\delta t}\right)\left(\frac{\partial\rho_{\gamma}}{\partial T_{\gamma}}+\frac{\partial\rho_{e}}{\partial T_{\gamma}}\right)^{-1}

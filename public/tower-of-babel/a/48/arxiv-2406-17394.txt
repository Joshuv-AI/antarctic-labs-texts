Abstract :
The moments of the charge distributions obtained by the sum-of-Gaussians(SOG)-analysis
of electron-scattering data are examined in
3
H and
3
He, together with those by
the Fourier-Bessel(FB)-one.
The SOG- and FB-methods reproduce well the experimental form-factors available at present,
but provide different charge-distributions from each other.
As a result, they do not yield the same values of the moments
of the charge distribution,
although their analysis are called ‘model-independent’.
The moments are sensitive to the tail of the charge distribution.
The present experimental data are not enough for SOG- and FB-analyses
to determine the reasonable shape of the tails, in a quantum mechanical point of view.
The new accurate experimental data at small momentum transfer squared
less than
0.1
fm
-2
are desired.

Introduction

Electron-scattering experiments have provided continuously us
with fundamental information of nuclear structure
for long time
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
4
]
.
Since electromagnetic interaction is well
understood theoretically
[
1
,
2
]
,
the reaction mechanism
is almost completely separated from assumptions on the nuclear structure
which is dominated by strong interaction.
Hence, the observed form factors of

3
H and
3
He, as mirror nuclei with the least number of constituent nucleons,
have played a role of touchstones for understanding structure of more complex nuclei
from the beginning of electron-scattering history
[
5
,
6
]
.
Around in 1980, in addition to the nucleonic degrees of freedom,
the mesonic effects on the form factors of the few-body systems were extensively
studied along with new experiment
[
7
,
8
,
9
,
10
]

and development of the calculation-method
based on realistic nuclear forces
[
11
,
12
,
13
,
14
,
15
]
.
The meson exchange current(MEC) has been shown to be necessary for understanding
the experimental form-factors
[
10
]
.
The form factor provides the charge density distribution,
\rho_{c}(r)
, in the nucleus.
The gross profiles of
\rho_{c}(r)
in the three-nucleon systems
were thus established about 30 years ago, using the world data at that time
[
10
]
.

The
n
th moment of
\rho_{c}(r)
is defined by

\langle\,r^{n}\,\rangle_{c}=\int dr^{3}r^{n}\rho_{c}(r)/Z
,

Z
denoting the number of the protons in the nucleus.
The second moment(
n=2
)
is called the mean square radius(msr)
1
1
endnote:

1

The abbreviation
of the ‘rms’(root mean square)-radius is frequently used in the literature,
but it is convenient for the present purpose to employ ‘msr’ for
the mean square radius, because electron scattering observes the value of the msr
(
\langle\,r^{2}\,\rangle_{c}
), together with the higher moments,
\langle\,r^{2n}\,\rangle_{c},(n=2,3,\cdots)
,
rather than the square root of the msr.
,
and is one of the key quantities in studying nuclear structure
[
3
]
.
The msrs of
3
H and
3
He, as fundamental systems of the complex nuclei, should be
determined experimentally as accurately as possible.
In fact, however, we can not say that the values of the msrs are accurate enough for
recent requirements in nuclear physics, in spite of the fact that
the shape of
\rho_{c}(r)
is explored with the use of the ‘world data’.

There are two ways at present to deduce the shape of
\rho_{c}(r)

from the experimental cross sections of electron scattering.
They are called the sum-of-Gaussians(SOG)
[
17
]
- and
the Fourier-Bessel(FB)
[
18
,
19
]
-method,
which are referred frequently as a ‘model-independent’ way
[
4
]
.
If experimental cross sections were available for the values of
q

0
to
\infty
,

electron to the nucleus, both ways would yield
\rho_{c}(r)
‘model-independently’,
which reproduces cross sections, according the phase-shift analysis
of the experiments,
and then the values of the msrs would uniquely be determined.
The
q
-region in the experiments performed so far, however,
is limited even in ‘world data’ for
3
H and
3
H
[
10
]
.
Hence, strictly speaking,
the obtained
\rho_{c}(r)
, and therefore the value of the msr depends on the analysis-methods.
Indeed, Ref.
[
4
]
lists the two different values of the square root of the msr
by the SOG- and the FB-method. For
3
H, the former yields
1.76(4)
and the latter

1.68(3)
, while for
3
He, the former
1.844(45)

and the latter
1.877(19)
in units of fm.
Ref.
[
10
]
provides
1.753(86)
for
3
H and
1.959(30)
fm for
3
He
by the SOG-analysis with the use of the
‘world data’, adding new experimental results after Ref.
[
4
]
.
Recent papers
[
16
,
20
]
have referred a different value(1.973(14) fm)
for
3
He obtained by the SOG-method in Ref.
[
21
]
.
In this way, the values of the msrs are strongly dependent on
the methods such as SOG and FB.
The SOG- and FB-methods were successful in studying the gross shape of
\rho_{c}(r)
[
10
]
,
but not enough for providing accurate values of the msrs.

The purpose of the present paper is to explore in detail
how the values of the moments depend on the analysis-methods of electron-scattering data
in
3
H and
3
He.
Such an investigation is desirable at present, because
recent detailed calculations on few-body systems require
the accurate experimental values of the msr to be reproduced
[
16
,
22
]
,
while those aiming
ab initio
calculations employ the value of the msr of
a few-body system as a reference in constructing nuclear interactions
[
23
]
.
Moreover, Refs.
[
24
,
25
,
26
]
have shown that
if the values of the higher moments,
\langle\,r^{n}\,\rangle_{c}(n\geq 4)
, are determined accurately,
both the proton- and neutron-distributions are investigated using the same experimental
results obtained through electromagnetic interaction.
The trinucleon-systems,
3
H and
3
He, are mirror nuclei,
and at the same time, the most extremely neutron-rich and proton-rich among nuclei
explored experimentally so far in electron scattering.
We note also that recent experiment
[
20
]
on the laser spectroscopy
of muonic
3
He-ions
provides the square root of the msr to be 1.97007(94) fm
with remarkable small errors,
against
1.959(30)
in electron scattering
[
10
]
.
The corrections for the msr from the muonic atom depends on the values of
the Zemach moments
which are related to the higher moments determined via electron scattering
[

,
21
]
.

In particular, both methods fail to describe the tails of

.
The MEC is necessary for understanding

In §

.
In §

2
Definition of the charge distribution and its moments

In order to describe the charge density with the relativistic corrections,
we use the Foldy-Wouthuysen(FW)-method
[
1
]

which makes the unitary transformation of the four-component
framework to the two-component one.
Because we do not know the original relativistic Hamiltonian, however,
we employ the Dirac equation with electromagnetic field,
in the same way as the previous authors in
[
27
,
28
,
29
,
25
]
.

When writing the form factor in the plane-wave
Born approximation(PWBA) in elastic electron scattering as

F_{c}(q)=\frac{1}{Z}\langle\,0\,|\,\hat{\rho}(q)\,|\,0\,\rangle

(1)

with the matrix element provided by the
the ground-state wave function in the two component framework,
the charge operator,
\hat{\rho}(q)
, given by the FW-transformation
up to order
1/M^{2}
,
M
being the nucleon mass,
is written as
[
25
]

\displaystyle\hat{\rho}(q)=\sum_{k=1}^{A}e^{i\bm{q}\cdot\bm{r_{k}}}\Bigl(D_{1k}(q^{2})+iD_{2k}(q^{2})\bm{q}\!\cdot\!(\,\bm{p}_{k}\times\bm{\sigma}_{k}\,)\Bigr).

(2)

Here,
Z
and
A
denote the numbers of the protons and nucleons of the system, respectively.
In the above equation, the first and the second term in the right-hand side
are called the Darwin-Foldy(DF) and the spin-orbit terms, respectively
[
28
,
30
]
.
They are defined by

\displaystyle D_{1k}(q^{2})

\displaystyle=F_{1k}(q^{2})-\frac{q^{2}}{2}D_{2k}(q^{2}),

(3)

\displaystyle D_{2k}(q^{2})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4M^{2}}\left(F_{1k}(q^{2})+2\mu_{k}F_{2k}\right),

(4)

with the Dirac form factor,
F_{1\tau}(q^{2})
, related to the Sachs,
G_{E\tau}(q^{2})
,
and Pauli,
F_{2\tau}(q^{2})
, form factor as
[
1
]

F_{1\tau}(q^{2})=G_{E\tau}(q^{2})+\mu_{\tau}q^{2}F_{2\tau}(q^{2})/(4M^{2}),

(5)

where
\tau=p(n)
indicates
k
being the proton(neutron).
Moreover,
\mu_{\tau}
denots the anomalous
magnetic moment to be
\mu_{\tau}=1.793
for
p
and
-1.913
for
n
.
The momentum-transfer dependence of the nucleon form factors
is still under discussions theoretically
[
31
,
32
,
33
]
.
Experimentally also there are various functional forms to fit
the electron scattering data at present
[
34
,
35
]
.
In the present paper,
the following Sachs and Pauli form factors
will be employed
[
25
,
26
]
,

\displaystyle G_{Ep}(q^{2})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(1+r_{p}^{2}q^{2}/12)^{2}},\qquad F_{2p}=\frac{G_{Ep}(q^{2})}{1+q^{2}/4M^{2}},

(6)

\displaystyle G_{En}(q^{2})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(1+r_{+}^{2}q^{2}/12)^{2}}-\frac{1}{(1+r_{-}^{2}q^{2}/12)^{2}},\qquad F_{2n}=\frac{G_{Ep}(q^{2})-G_{En}(q^{2})/\mu_{n}}{1+q^{2}/4M^{2}},

with the values used in Ref.
[
36
]

r_{p}=0.877\,\textrm{fm},\qquad r_{\pm}^{2}=(0.830)^{2}\mp 0.058\,\textrm{fm}^{2}.

(7)

In Ref.
[
36
]
,
G_{En}(q^{2})
is given by the form

G_{En}(q^{2})=-\frac{r_{n}^{2}q^{2}/6}{1+q^{2}/M^{2}}\frac{1}{(1+r_{p}^{2}q^{2}/12)^{2}},

(8)

with
r_{n}^{2}=-0.116\,{\rm fm}^{2}
. This is numerically almost equal to

G_{En}(q^{2})
in Eq.(
6
) with the values of Eq.(
7
),
and the values of the first and the second derivative of these form factors
are taken to be equal to each other at
q^{2}=0
.
There are still discussions on the values of
r_{p}
and
r_{\pm}^{2}

themselves
[
37

38
,
39
,
40
]
.
The value

fm is almost equal to the upper bound

r_{p}=0.887
fm of the proton size at present
[
37
]
.
The uncertainty of the nucleon-size, however, does not change our main conclusions
with respect to the SOG- and FB-methods in the present paper.
The charge density,
\rho_{c}(r)
, with the relativistic corrections
is given by the Fourier transform of Eq.(
1
) as

\rho_{c}(r)=Z\int\frac{d^{3}q}{(2\pi)^{3}}\exp(-i\bm{q}\!\cdot\!\bm{r})F_{c}(q).

(9)

The mean
2n
th-order moment,
\langle\,r^{2n}\,\rangle_{c}
,
of
\rho_{c}(r)
is given for
n=1,2,3\cdots
by

\langle\,r^{2n}\,\rangle_{c}=\sum_{\tau}\langle\,r^{2n}\,\rangle_{c\tau},\qquad\langle\,r^{2n}\,\rangle_{c\tau}=\frac{1}{Z}\int d^{3}r\,r^{2n}\rho_{c\tau}(r),

(10)

where
\rho_{c\tau}(r)
stands for the proton(neutron) charge distribution for
\tau=p(n)
,
as
\rho_{c}(r)=\rho_{cp}(r)+\rho_{cn}(r)
.
In calculating
\langle\,r^{2n}\,\rangle_{c}
, it is convenient to use the following
identity instead of the above equation itself
[
24
]
,

\langle\,r^{2n}\,\rangle_{c\tau}=(-\bm{\nabla}_{q}^{2})^{n}F_{c\tau}(q)|_{q=0},

(11)

where
F_{c\tau}(q)
for
\tau=p(n)
denotes the part of Eq.(
1
)
due to the protons(neutrons) as

F_{c\tau}(q)=\frac{1}{Z}\int dr^{3}\exp(i\bm{q}\!\cdot\!\bm{r})\rho_{c\tau}(r).

(12)

In the present discussions on
3
H and
3

relativistic corrections to the form factors are negligible
[
11
]
,
but the DF-terms will be kept in some calculations of the moments,
while the spin-orbit term is neglected, because it is expected
not to be necessary for the present purpose
[
25
,
26
]
.
In denoting the

(13)

\displaystyle\langle\,r^{6}\,\rangle_{c}

\displaystyle\langle\,r^{6}\,\rangle_{cp}-\langle\,r^{6}\,\rangle_{cn},

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad+\frac{35}{2}r^{4}_{p}\left(\langle\,r^{2}\,\rangle_{p}+\frac{3}{4M^{2}_{p}}\right)+\frac{35}{3}r^{6}_{p},

\displaystyle\left.\qquad\qquad\qquad+\frac{35}{3}(r^{6}_{+}-r^{6}_{-})\right],

\displaystyle\langle\,r^{8}\,\rangle_{c}

\displaystyle\langle\,r^{8}\,\rangle_{cp}-\langle\,r^{8}\,\rangle_{cn},

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad+63r^{4}_{p}\left(\langle\,r^{4}\,\rangle_{p}+\frac{5}{2M_{p}^{2}}\langle\,r^{2}\,\rangle_{p}\right)+140r^{6}_{p}\left(\langle\,r^{2}\,\rangle_{p}+\frac{3}{4M_{p}^{2}}\right)+\frac{175}{2}r^{8}_{p}\,,

According to Eq.(

, is expanded in terms of the moments as

F_{c}(q)=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{m}\frac{1}{(2m+1)!}q^{2m}\langle\,r^{2m}\,\rangle_{c}.

There is no constraint on the expansion in a physical point of view,
but in a practical use with
the limited number of terms,
we should know its convergence and the magnitude of the reminder term.
For this purpose, the Leibnitz criterion is useful, which requires the sufficient
condition for the convergence of the alternating series,

\frac{1}{(2m+1)!}q^{2m}\langle\,r^{2m}\,\rangle_{c}>\frac{1}{(2m+3)!}q^{2(m+1)}\langle\,r^{2(m+1)}\,\rangle_{c}\,\,\,\,\,\,\,(\rightarrow 0\,\,\,{\rm for}\,\,\,m\rightarrow\infty).

(31)

In this case, for the sum of the limited number of the terms:

F_{k}(q)=\sum_{m=0}^{k}(-1)^{m}\frac{1}{(2m+1)!}q^{2m}\langle\,r^{2m}\,\rangle_{c}\,,

(32)

there is the alternating-series-estimation-theorem as

|F_{k}(q)-F_{c}(q)|\leq\frac{1}{(2m+3)!}q^{2(m+1)}\langle\,r^{2(m+1)}\,\rangle_{c},

(33)

which gives an approximate magnitude on the sum of the neglected terms.

The sum-of-Gaussians(SOG)
[
17
]

[

to analyze the electron-scattering cross sections
have widely been used as a ‘model-independent’ way
[
4

because they need not assume a specific function like Fermi-type
for the charge density distribution,
\rho_{c}(r)
,
which is required as an input in the phase-shift analysis of experimental data.
It is not necessary for the ‘model-independent’ analyses, however, to determine uniquely

\rho_{c}(r)
, because experimental data available at present are
limited in a region of the momentum transfer,
q
, as
q^{2}_{\rm min}\leq q^{2}\leq q^{2}_{\rm max}
.
As a result, the values of the moment,
\langle\,r^{n}\,\rangle_{c}=\int d^{3}r\,r^{n}\rho_{c}(r)/Z
,
depend on the analysis-method to deduce
\rho_{c}(r)
from the experimental cross-sections.
In the present paper, we have investigated how the values of the moments depend on
the SOG- and FB-method in
3
H and
3
He.

It has been shown
that because the SOG- and the FB-method do not describe well the tails of
\rho_{c}(r)
,
in a quantum mechanical point of view,
the both analyses provide smaller values
of
\langle\,r^{n}\,\rangle_{c}
than those by detailed calculations with AV8’+
3N
,
as shown in Table
3

n
th moments derived from
the SOG(FB)-analyses to those of the AV8’+
3N
-calculations
without small relativistic corrections listed in Tables

2

The disagreement between those of the AV8’+
3N
- and SOG(FB)-analyses
increases with increasing

In the eighth moments, the SOG-analysis provides
less than
50\%

3N
-values, while the FB-analysis less than
15\%
.
The values of the msr(the second moment) seem to be obtained rather well

3N
-ones, but they are not determined independently of
the disagreement of the higher moments.
The SOG(FB) form factors to reproduce the experimental ones are functions of not only
the value of the msr, but also the values of the higher moments.
These results are not affected by the contributions of the meson-exchange currents(MEC)
to the cross sections, because the MEC-contributions appear around the center of the nucleus.

Non simple blow ups for the Nirenberg problem on half spheres
Mohameden Ahmedou & \& Mohamed Ben Ayed

Abstract




dedicated to the memory of Prof. Louis Nirenberg

Abstract

In this paper we study a Nirenberg type problem on standard half spheres
(\mathbb{S}^{n}_{+},g_{0})
consisting of finding conformal metrics of prescribed scalar curvature and zero boundary mean curvature. This problem amounts to solve the following boundary value problem involving the critical Sobolev exponent:

(\mathcal{P})\quad\begin{cases}-\Delta_{g_{0}}u\,+\,\frac{n(n-2)}{4}u\,=K\,u^{\frac{n+2}{n-2}},\,u>0\quad\mbox{in }\mathbb{S}^{n}_{+},\\
\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}\,=\,0\quad\mbox{on }\partial\mathbb{S}^{n}_{+},\end{cases}

where
K\in C^{3}(\mathbb{S}^{n}_{+})
is a positive function.
We construct, under generic conditions on the function
K
, finite energy solutions of a subcritical approximation of
(\mathcal{P})
on half spheres of dimension
n\geq 5
, which exhibit multiple blow up of
cluster-type
at the same boundary point. These solutions may have zero or non zero weak limit and may develop clusters at different boundary points. Such a blow up phenomena on half spheres drastically contrast with the case of the Nirenberg problem on spheres, where non simple blow up for finite energy solutions cannot occur and unveils an unexpected connection with vortex type problems arising in Euler equations in fluid dynamic and mean fields type equations in mathematical physics.
We construct also, under suitable conditions on the restriction of
K
on
\partial\mathbb{S}^{n}_{+}
, approximate solutions of arbitrarily large energy and Morse index.

Key Words:
Lyapunov Schmidt reduction, Critical Sobolev exponent, Morse index, Non-simple blow up points, Vortex problems.

AMS subject classification: 35A01, 58J05, 58E05.

1
Introduction and statement of the results

For the
n-
dimensional sphere
\mathbb{S}^{n},\,n\geq 3
endowed with its standard metric
g_{0}
and a given positive function
K\in C^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{n})
, Nirenberg asked in the early seventieth the following question:
Can the function
K
be realized as the scalar curvature of a metric
g
, conformally equivalent to
g_{0}
?

Writing the conformal metric as
g:=u^{{4}/{(n-2)}}g_{0}
, the above question amounts to solve the following nonlinear elliptic equation involving the Sobolev critical exponent:

(1)

(\mathcal{NP})\quad-\Delta_{g_{0}}u\,+\,\frac{n(n-2)}{4}u\,=K\,u^{\frac{n+2}{n-2}},\quad\,u>0\,\mbox{ in }\,\mathbb{S}^{n}.

This problem has a variational structure, however the associated Euler-Lagrange functional does not satisfy the Palais-Smale condition due to the critical growth of the nonlinearity.

To overcome such a difficulty, one can lower the critical exponent by considering the following subcritical approximation of the Nirenberg problem
(\mathcal{NP})
:

(2)

(\mathcal{NP}_{\varepsilon})\quad-\Delta_{g_{0}}u\,+\,\frac{n(n-2)}{4}u\,=K\,u^{\frac{n+2}{n-2}-\varepsilon},\quad\,u>0\,\mbox{ in }\,\mathbb{S}^{n},

where
\varepsilon>0
is a small parameter.
In this way one recovers the compactness and then studies the behavior of a solution
u_{\varepsilon}
of
(\mathcal{NP}_{\varepsilon})
as the parameter
\varepsilon
goes to zero. Thanks to elliptic estimates there are two alternatives: either
||u_{\varepsilon}||_{L^{\infty}}
remains uniformly bounded and in this case the solutions
u_{\varepsilon}
converge in the
C^{2,\alpha}-
topology as
\varepsilon\to 0
to a solution of
(\mathcal{NP})
or the sequence of solutions
u_{\varepsilon}
blows up. In this latter case, assuming that
u_{\varepsilon}
is a family of energy-bounded solutions, it follows from the concentration compactness principle that
u_{\varepsilon}^{{2n}/{(n-2)}}\mathcal{L}^{n}
, where
\mathcal{L}^{n}
denotes the
n
-dimensional Lesbegue measure, converges in the sense of measures to a sum of Dirac masses, see
[
22
,
28
]
. Actually a refined blow up analysis of such blowing up solutions has been initiated by R. Schoen
[
26
,
27
,
18
]
and developed by Y.Y. Li
[
20
,
21
]
, C.C. Chen and C.S Lin
[
13
,
14
]
) and Druet-Hebey-Robert
[
16
]
. It follows from such a blow up analysis that solutions concentrate at critical points of
K
. Moreover, in dimension
n=3,4
and under the assumption that
\Delta K
does not vanish at any critical point, it has been proved that all blow up points are
isolated simple
, that is locally the blowing up solution has the energy of
one bubble
, see please definition 0.3 in
[
20
]
. Moreover concentration occurs only at critical points of
K
with
\Delta K<0
. This has been proved by Yanyan Li
[
20
,
21
]
for the dimensions
n=3,4
. Furthermore on spheres of dimensions
n\geq 5
and under the additional assumption that the energy of the solutions is uniformly bounded, A. Malchiodi and M. Mayer
[
23
]
proved that all blow up points are isolated simple. See also A. Bahri
[
5
]
where a corresponding property has been proved for
critical points at infinity
of the associated variational problem. Furthermore the assumption that the energy is bounded is necessary to rule out
non simple blow up
as shown by C.C. Chen and C.S. Lin
[
12
]
.

In this paper we consider a Nirenberg type problem on standard half spheres
(\mathbb{S}^{n}_{+},g_{0})
consisting of prescribing simultaneously the scalar curvature to be a function
K\in C^{3}(\mathbb{S}^{n}_{+})
and the boundary mean curvature to be zero. This amounts to solve the following boundary value problem

(3)

(\mathcal{P})\quad\begin{cases}-\Delta_{g_{0}}u\,+\,\frac{n(n-2)}{4}u\,=K\,u^{\frac{n+2}{n-2}},\,u>0&\mbox{ in }\mathbb{S}^{n}_{+},\\
\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}\,=\,0&\mbox{on }\partial\mathbb{S}^{n}_{+}.\end{cases}

This problem has been studied on half spheres of dimensions
n=3,4
. See the papers
[
19
,
15
,
9
,
10
,
11
]
and the references therein.

Here also in order to recover compactness one considers the following subcritical approximation

(4)

(\mathcal{P}_{\varepsilon})\quad\begin{cases}-\Delta_{g_{0}}u\,+\,\frac{n(n-2)}{4}u\,=K\,u^{\frac{n+2}{n-2}-\varepsilon},\,u>0&\mbox{ in }\mathbb{S}^{n}_{+},\\
\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}\,=\,0&\mbox{on }\partial\mathbb{S}^{n}_{+}.\end{cases}

Just as above, there are two alternatives for the behavior of a sequence of solutions
u_{\varepsilon}
of
(\mathcal{P}_{\varepsilon})
. Either the
||u_{\varepsilon}||_{L^{\infty}}
remains uniformly bounded or it blows up and if it does, assuming that
u_{\varepsilon}
is a family of energy-bounded solutions, then it follows that
u^{{2n}/{(n-2)}}_{\varepsilon}\mathcal{L}^{n}
converges to a sum of Dirac masses, some of them are in the interior and the others are located on the boundary. The interior points are critical points of
K
satisfying that
\Delta K\leq 0
and the boundary points are critical points of
K_{1}
the restriction of
K
on the boundary satisfying that
\partial_{\nu}K\geq 0
. See
[
15
]
and
[
10
]
.
Moreover a refined blow up analysis, under the assumption that
\Delta K\neq 0
at interior critical points of
K
and that
\partial_{\nu}K\neq 0
at critical points of
K_{1}
, shows that in the dimension
n=3
all blow up points are isolated simple, see
[
19
]
and
[
15
]
. Furthermore, under additional condition on
K_{1}
, it has been proved in
[
11
]
that on four dimensional half spheres all blow up points are isolated simple.

On half spheres of dimension
n\geq 5
the problem
(\mathcal{P}_{\varepsilon})
admits blowing up solutions having only
isolated simple blow points
. Indeed we have:

Theorem 1.1

Let
K
be a positive smooth function on
\overline{\mathbb{S}^{n}_{+}}
with
n\geq 5
and let
z_{1},\cdots,z_{m}\in\partial\mathbb{S}^{n}_{+}
be non degenerate critical points of
K_{1}:=K_{\lfloor\partial\mathbb{S}^{n}_{+}}
which satisfy that
(\partial_{\nu}K)(z_{i})>0
for each
i
and let
y_{m+1},\cdots,y_{m+\ell}
be non degenerate critical points of
K
with
\Delta K(y_{i})<0
for each
i>m
.

Then there exists a sequence of solutions
u_{\varepsilon}
of
(\mathcal{P}_{\varepsilon})
which converges weakly to
0
and blows up at
z_{1},\cdots,z_{m},y_{m+1},\cdots,y_{m+\ell}
and for each
i
, it holds

\lim_{r\to 0}\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{B_{r}(z_{i})}Ku_{\varepsilon}^{\frac{2n}{n-2}}\,=\,\underbrace{\frac{S_{n}}{K(z_{i})^{(n-2)/2}}}_{\text{Energy of 1 boundary bubble}}\mbox{ and }\lim_{r\to 0}\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{B_{r}(y_{i})}Ku_{\varepsilon}^{\frac{2n}{n-2}}\,=\,\underbrace{\frac{2\,S_{n}}{K(y_{i})^{(n-2)/2}}}_{\text{Energy of 1 interior bubble}}

where
B_{r}(z)
is a geodesic ball and the universal constant
S_{n}:=c_{0}^{2n/(n-2)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}\frac{1}{(1+|y|^{2})^{n}}
, where
c_{0}:=(n(n-2))^{(n-2)/4}
, is the energy of one
boundary bubble
(for
K\equiv 1
).
Hence each blow up point is an isolated simple blow up one.

Remark 1.1

1.

The above theorem holds true on three and four dimensional half spheres. See
[
15
,
11
]
.

2.

The above theorem gives the existence of blowing up solutions having only interior blow up points (by taking
m=0
) or only boundary blow up points (by taking
\ell=0
).

Before resuming our investigation of the blow up phenomena we recall the definition of non degenerate solution of
(\mathcal{P})
:

A solution
\omega
of
(\mathcal{P})
is said to be non degenerate if the linearized operator

(5)

\mathcal{L}_{\omega}(\varphi):=-\Delta_{g_{0}}\varphi\,+\frac{n(n-2)}{4}\varphi\,-\,\frac{n+2}{n-2}K\omega^{\frac{4}{n-2}}\varphi

does not admit zero as an eigenvalue.

Next we want to consider the question of existence of blowing up solutions which are close to a combination of a sum of bubbles and a solution of
(\mathcal{P})
.
Namely we prove that there are blowing up solutions of
(\mathcal{P}_{\varepsilon})
with non zero weak limit having interior as well as boundary blow ups which are all
isolated simple
. More precisely our result can be stated as follows:

Theorem 1.2

Let
K
be a positive smooth function on
\overline{\mathbb{S}^{n}_{+}}
,
z_{1},\cdots,z_{m}\in\partial\mathbb{S}^{n}_{+}
be non degenerate critical points of
K_{1}:=K_{\lfloor\partial\mathbb{S}^{n}_{+}}
which satisfy that
(\partial_{\nu}K)(z_{i})>0
for each
i
(
m\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
) and
y_{m+1},\cdots,y_{m+\ell}
be non degenerate critical points of
K
with
\Delta K(y_{i})<0
for each
i>m
(
\ell\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
). Furthermore let
\omega
be a non degenerate solution of
(\mathcal{P})
.

(i)

If
n\geq 5

there exists a sequence of solutions
u_{\varepsilon}
of
(\mathcal{P}_{\varepsilon})
which converges weakly to
\omega
and blows up at
(z_{1},\cdots,z_{m})
(with
m\geq 1
) and for each
i
, it holds

\lim_{r\to 0}\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{B_{r}(z_{i})}Ku_{\varepsilon}^{({2n}/{(n-2)})}\,=\,\frac{S_{n}}{K(z_{i})^{(n-2)/2}}.

(ii)

If
n\geq 7

there exists a sequence of solutions
u_{\varepsilon}
of
(\mathcal{P}_{\varepsilon})
which converges weakly to
\omega
and blows up at
z_{1},\cdots,z_{m},y_{m+1},\cdots,y_{m+\ell}
(with
m\geq 0
and
\ell\geq 1
) and for each
i
, it holds

\lim_{r\to 0}\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{B_{r}(z_{i})}Ku_{\varepsilon}^{\frac{2n}{n-2}}\,=\,\frac{S_{n}}{K(z_{i})^{(n-2)/2}}\quad\mbox{ and }\quad\lim_{r\to 0}\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{B_{r}(y_{i})}Ku_{\varepsilon}^{\frac{2n}{n-2}}\,=\,\frac{2\,S_{n}}{K(y_{i})^{(n-2)/2}}.

All the blow up points are isolated simple.

We call such a blow up behavior,
blow up with residual mass
.

Remark 1.2

The existence of blow up with residual mass in dimension
n=6
is an open question even for the case of closed spheres. See please the remarks ending the proof of Theorem
1.5
for the analytical features behind the failure of our argument in this dimension.

In contrast to the above results and also to the case of closed spheres, we construct in this paper a sequence of finite energy solutions of
(\mathcal{P}_{\varepsilon})
on half spheres of dimension
n\geq 5
, which exhibit multiple blow up at the same boundary point.

Indeed it turns out that the existence of solutions blowing up at the same boundary point with the energy of
m
boundary bubbles,
m\geq 2
is related to the existence of critical points of the following Kirchhoff-Routh type function:

(6)

\mathcal{F}_{z,m}:\mathbb{F}_{m}(T_{z}(\partial\mathbb{S}^{n}_{+}))\to\mathbb{R};\,\mathcal{F}_{z,m}(\xi_{1},\cdots,\xi_{m}):=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{m}D^{2}K_{1}(z)(\xi_{i},\xi_{i})+\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq m}\frac{1}{|\xi_{i}-\xi_{j}|^{n-2}}.

where
\mathbb{F}_{m}(T_{z}(\partial\mathbb{S}^{n}_{+})):=\{(\xi_{1},\cdots,\xi_{m});\,\xi_{i}\neq\xi_{j}\in T_{z}(\partial\mathbb{S}^{n}_{+})\,\,\mbox{ for }i\neq j\}
.

We point out that similar hamiltonian type functions appear in the characterization of the location of the concentration points in mean field type equations in mathematical physics and Euler equation in fluid dynamic. The relevance of such vortex type problems in Yamabe type equations has been discovered by Thizy-Vétois
[
29
]
and Pistoia-Vaira
[
25
]
.

Next we prove that every non-degenerate critical point of the functional
\mathcal{F}_{z,m}
, where
z
is a boundary point satisfying the assumption of Theorem
1.1
gives rise to bubbling off solutions of high energy and morse index. We recall that the
morse index

Morse(\omega)
of a solution
\omega
of
(\mathcal{P})
is the dimension of the space of negativity of the bilinear form associated to the linearized operator
\mathcal{L}_{\omega}
(defined in (
5
)).
Namely we have

Theorem 1.3

Let
K
be a positive smooth function on
\overline{\mathbb{S}^{n}_{+}}
with
n\geq 5
and
z\in\partial\mathbb{S}^{n}_{+}
be a non degenerate critical point of
K_{1}:=K_{\lfloor\partial\mathbb{S}^{n}_{+}}
satisfying
(\partial_{\nu}K)(z)>0
.

For
m\geq 2
, assume that the function
\mathcal{F}_{z,m}
has a non degenerate critical point. Then there exists a sequence of solutions
u_{\varepsilon}
of
(\mathcal{P}_{\varepsilon})
having the energy of
m
boundary bubbles, which blows up at
z
. That is

\lim_{r\to 0}\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{B_{r}(z)}Ku_{\varepsilon}^{({2n}/{(n-2)})}\,=\,\underbrace{m\,{\frac{1}{K(z)^{(n-2)/2}}S_{n}}}_{\text{Energy of m boundary bubbles}}.

Furthermore, the morse index of the solution
u_{\varepsilon}
is lower bounded by
m
.

We call such a blow up point non simple of order m.

Remark 1.3

•

The above non simple blow up is of cluster type and we point out that non simple blow up of cluster type have been recently constructed for some perturbations of some Yamabe type equations on manifolds which are not locally conformally flat by Thizy-Vétois
[
29
]
and Pistoia-Vaira
[
25
]
.

•

We point out that assuming the function
\mathcal{F}_{z,m}
has a critical point implies that the boundary point
z
, where the concentration occurs, is not a local maxima. See statement
(ii)
in Proposition
4.1
.

•

A non simple blow up phenomenon has been proved by P.Esposito and J.Wei
[
17
]
for the Sinh-Gordon equation under Neumann boundary conditions.

As an immediate corollary of Theorem
1.3
we have the following result

Corollary 1.1

Let
K
be a positive smooth function on
\overline{\mathbb{S}^{n}_{+}}
with
n\geq 5
and let

be a non-degenerate critical point of
K_{1}:=K_{\lfloor\partial\mathbb{S}^{n}_{+}}

Suppose that

has at least one simple positive eigenvalue. Then there exists a sequence of solutions

of

having the energy of 2 boundary bubbles and which blows up at the boundary point

\lim_{r\to 0}\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{B_{r}(z)}Ku_{\varepsilon}^{({2n}/{(n-2)})}\,=\,2\,\underbrace{\frac{1}{K(z)^{(n-2)/2}}S_{n}}_{\text{Energy of 1 boundary bubble}}.

Furthermore, as a corollary of Theorem
1.3
and Proposition
4.2
we obtain the existence result of blowing up solutions of
(\mathcal{P}_{\varepsilon})
with arbitrarily large energy and Morse index. More precisely we prove the following theorem:

Corollary 1.2

Let
n\geq 5
and assume that the function
K_{1}:=K_{\lfloor{\partial\mathbb{S}^{n}_{+}}}
has a non degenerate critical point
z\in\partial\mathbb{S}^{n}_{+}
such that
\partial_{\nu}K(z)>0
and the hessian matrix
D^{2}K_{1}(z)
has only one simple positive eigenvalue and the others are negative. Then for every
m\in\mathbb{N}
there exists a solution

of
(\mathcal{P}_{\varepsilon})
such that

\lim_{m\to\infty}\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}_{+}}Ku_{\varepsilon,m}^{\frac{2n}{n-2}}\,=\,+\infty\quad\mbox{ and }\quad Morse(u_{\varepsilon,m})\to\infty,\mbox{ as }m\to\infty.

In the next result we generalize Theorem
1.3
by constructing a solution of
(\mathcal{P}_{\varepsilon})
having
clusters
of non simple blow up points at different boundary points.
Furthermore, the solution can have also interior blow up points. Namely we prove:

Theorem 1.4

Let
K
be a positive smooth function on
\overline{\mathbb{S}^{n}_{+}}
with
n\geq 5
and let
z_{1},\cdots,z_{m}\in\partial\mathbb{S}^{n}_{+}
(with
m\geq 0
) be non degenerate critical points of
K_{1}:=K_{\lfloor\partial\mathbb{S}^{n}_{+}}
, which satisfy that
(\partial_{\nu}K)(z_{i})>0
for each
i
and

be non degenerate critical points of
K
with
\Delta K(y_{i})<0
for each
i>m
(
\ell\geq 0
).

For
1\leq i\leq m
let
q_{i}\geq 1
and suppose that if
q_{i}\geq 2
then the function
\mathcal{F}_{z_{i},q_{i}}
has a non degenerate critical point. Then there exists a sequence of solutions

of
(\mathcal{P}_{\varepsilon})
which converges weakly to
0

,

, it holds

\lim_{r\to 0}\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{B_{r}(z_{i})}Ku_{\varepsilon}^{({2n}/{(n-2)})}\,=\,q_{i}\,\frac{S_{n}}{K(z_{i})^{(n-2)/2}}\mbox{ and }\lim_{r\to 0}\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{B_{r}(y_{i})}Ku_{\varepsilon}^{({2n}/{(n-2)})}\,=\,\frac{2\,S_{n}}{K(y_{i})^{(n-2)/2}}.

and

Regarding the method of the proof of the above theorems some remarks are in order. Although we use a finite dimensional framework similar to the one used by Bahri-Li-Rey
[
8
]
to deal with Yamabe type equations on domains, there is a major difference in the analytical feature of the two frameworks. Indeed Bahri-Li-Rey used in crucial way the refined blow up analysis of R. Schoen
[
26
,
18
]
which proves that all blow up points of the Yamabe equation are
isolated simple
. Such a fact simplifies in a drastic way the asymptotic expansion of the gradient near potential blow up points and hence the construction of blowing up solutions. In particular it implies that the mutual interaction of two bubbles
\delta_{a_{i},\lambda_{i}}
and
\delta_{a_{j},\lambda_{j}}
is of the order of
{G(a_{i},a_{j})}/{(\lambda_{i}\lambda_{j})^{{(n-2)}/{2}}}
, where
G(.,.)
denotes the Green’s function. In contrast with the situation considered by Bahri-Li-Rey
[
8
]
we are in a situation where the points are very close to each other and it is very challenging to compute the leading term in the mutual interaction. In fact with respect to the cases of the Nirenberg problem on spheres and of the Yamabe problem, the main analytical feature relies in that fact that at every critical point
z\in\partial\mathbb{S}^{n}_{+}
of the restriction of
K
on the boundary
K_{1}:=K_{\lfloor{\partial\mathbb{S}^{n}_{+}}}
, which is not a local maximum and satisfying that
\frac{\partial K}{\partial\nu}(z)>0
, a
balancing phenomenon
between the self interaction and the mutual interaction of bubbles sitting near of
z
occurs. This balancing phenomenon is the key point of our construction of multibubbling solutions of the approximated problem
(\mathcal{P}_{\varepsilon})
accumulating at the same boundary point
z
. Furthermore the goals of the two papers are different. Indeed the main result of Bahri-Li-Rey is the computation of the difference of topology induced by
simple blow points
while our goal in this paper is to construct
non simple blow up points.
In a subsequent paper, we will compute the difference of topology induced by these
non simple blow up points
and show that the computation is much more involved compared with the
simple blow up
case.

The sequel of this paper is organized as follows: in Section 2 we set up the variational framework associated to equation
(\mathcal{P}_{\varepsilon})
, recall its related Euler-Lagrange functional and its finite dimensional reduction. In Section 3 we expand the gradient of the Euler-Lagrange functional in the neighborhood of highly concentrated
bubbles
. Section 4 is devoted to the study of the critical point of a Kirchhoff-Routh type functional while Section 5 is devoted to prove our main results. Finally we collect in the appendix useful estimates of the
standard bubble
.

2
Variational framework and preliminaries

In this section we set up the general framework and introduce some notation.

We start by recalling the variational framework of the boundary value problem
(\mathcal{P}_{\varepsilon})
. Namely we recall that solutions of
(\mathcal{P}_{\varepsilon})
are in one to one correspondence with the positive critical points of the functional

I_{\varepsilon}(u)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}_{+}}|\nabla u|^{2}+\frac{n(n-2)}{8}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}_{+}}u^{2}-\frac{1}{p+1-\varepsilon}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}_{+}}K|u|^{p+1-\varepsilon},\,\,u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{n}_{+})\mbox{ and }p:=\frac{n+2}{n-2}.

For convenience matter, we perform a stereographic projection to reduce our problem to
\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
. Let
\mathcal{D}^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
denote the completion of
C^{\infty}_{c}(\overline{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}})
with respect to Dirichlet norm. The stereographic projection
\pi_{a}
through a point
a\in\partial\mathbb{S}^{n}_{+}
induces an isometry
\iota:H^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{n}_{+})\to\mathcal{D}^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
according to the following formula

(7)

\displaystyle(\iota v)(x)=\Big(\frac{2}{1+|x|^{2}}\Big)^{(n-2)/{2}}v(\pi_{a}^{-1}(x)),\qquad v\in H^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{n}_{+}),\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}.

In particular we have that for every
v\in H^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{n}_{+})

(8)

\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}_{+}}(|\nabla v|^{2}+\frac{n(n-2)}{4}v^{2})=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}|\nabla(\iota v)|^{2}\qquad\mbox{and }\qquad\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}_{+}}|v|^{p+1}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}|\iota v|^{p+1}.

In the sequel, we will identify the function
K
and its composition with the stereographic projection
\pi_{a}
. We will also identify a point
b
of
\mathbb{S}^{n}_{+}
and its image by
\pi_{a}
.
We define

(9)

\delta_{a,\lambda}(x):=c_{0}\frac{\lambda^{n-2/2}}{(\lambda^{2}+1+(1-\lambda^{2})\cos d(a,x))^{n-2/2}}\qquad\mbox{ with }c_{0}:=(n(n-2))^{(n-2)/4}

where
d
is the geodesic distance on
\overline{\mathbb{S}^{n}_{+}}
and the constant
c_{0}
is chosen such that

-\Delta\delta_{a,\lambda}\,+\,\frac{n(n-2)}{4}\delta_{a,\lambda}\,=\,\delta_{a,\lambda}^{{(n+2)}/{(n-2)}}\quad\mbox{ in }\mathbb{S}^{n}_{+}.

We notice that this function satisfies
c/\lambda^{(n-2)/2}\leq\delta_{a,\lambda}\leq c\lambda^{(n-2)/2}
uniformly in
\mathbb{S}^{n}_{+}
, which implies that

(10)

|\delta_{a,\lambda}^{-\varepsilon}-1|\leq c\,\varepsilon\ln\lambda\qquad\mbox{ if }\varepsilon\ln\lambda\mbox{ is small}.

Note that, in some computations, we need a precise estimate of
\delta_{a,\lambda}^{-\varepsilon}
which is given in Lemma
6.1
.

Moreover, it is easy to see that, using (
7
) with
\pi_{-a}
, the function
\iota\delta_{(a,\lambda)}
is equal to

(11)

\iota\delta_{a,\lambda}(x)=c_{0}\frac{\lambda^{(n-2)/2}}{(1+\lambda^{2}|x|^{2})^{(n-2)/2}}\,;\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}

(see the proof of Lemma
6.1
for the details of this change of variables).

Note that, if we use
\pi_{b}
(that is with
b\in\partial\mathbb{S}^{n}_{+}
instead of
-a
),
the function
\iota\delta_{a,\lambda}
will be

c_{0}\frac{\mu^{(n-2)/2}}{(1+\mu^{2}|x-\tilde{a}|^{2})^{(n-2)/2}}\qquad\mbox{ with }\quad\begin{cases}\tilde{a}:=\frac{(\lambda^{2}-1)\mbox{Proj}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}a}{2+(\lambda^{2}-1)(1-\cos d(a,b))}\\
\mu:=\frac{2+(\lambda^{2}-1)(1-\cos d(a,b))}{2\lambda}\end{cases}

where
\mbox{Proj}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
denotes the projection onto
\mathbb{R}^{n}
(for the proof, see page 14 of
[
6
]
). In the following we will use
\delta_{a,\lambda}
even if we are in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. These notation will be assumed to be understood in the sequel.

Notice that, if the point
a\in\mathbb{S}^{n}_{+}
then the normal derivative on the boundary of the function
\delta_{a,\lambda}
is not
0
. Hence we need to modify this function as follows:

For
a\in\overline{\mathbb{S}^{n}_{+}},
we define
projected bubble

\varphi_{a,\lambda}
to be the unique solution of

-\Delta\varphi_{a,\lambda}\,+\,\frac{n(n-2)}{4}\varphi_{a,\lambda}\,=\,\delta_{a,\lambda}^{{(n+2)}/{(n-2)}}\quad\mbox{ in }\,\mathbb{S}^{n}_{+};\quad\frac{\partial\varphi_{a,\lambda}}{\partial\nu}\,=0\mbox{ on }\partial\mathbb{S}^{n}_{+}.

We point out that
\varphi_{a,\lambda}=\delta_{a,\lambda}
if
a\in\partial\mathbb{S}^{n}_{+}
.

For
\varepsilon=0
, the functional
I_{0}
does not satisfy the Palais-Smale condition
(PS)
and non converging
(PS)
-sequences of positive functions belong to the set
V(q,\ell,\tau)
if their weak limit is zero and in
V(\omega,q,\ell,\tau)
if their weak limit is
\omega
. These sets called in the sequel
neighborhood at infinity
are defined as follows:

For
q,\ell\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
(with
q+\ell\geq 1
) and
\tau>0
small

(12)

\displaystyle V(q,\ell,\tau):=

\displaystyle\{u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{n}_{+}):\,\exists\,\lambda_{i}>\tau^{-1},\,\exists\,a_{i}\in\overline{\mathbb{S}^{n}_{+}}\,\mbox{ for }i=1\cdots,q+\ell\,\,\mbox{ s.t. }\,\lambda_{i}d(a_{i},\partial\mathbb{S}^{n}_{+})<\tau

\displaystyle\mbox{ for }i\leq q\,;\,\,\lambda_{i}d(a_{i},\partial\mathbb{S}^{n}_{+})>\tau^{-1}\mbox{ for }i>q\,\mbox{ and }\|u-\sum K(a_{i})^{(2-n)/4}\varphi_{a_{i},\lambda_{i}}\|<\tau\},

(13)

\displaystyle V(\omega,q,\ell,\tau):=

\displaystyle\{u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{n}_{+}):u-\omega\in V(q,\ell,\tau)\},

where
\omega
is a positive solution of
(\mathcal{P})
and where
\varepsilon_{ij}
denotes the mutual interaction between two different bubbles and it is defined by

(14)

\varepsilon_{ij}:=[\lambda_{i}/\lambda_{j}+\lambda_{j}/\lambda_{i}+({1}/{2})\lambda_{i}\lambda_{j}(1-\cos d(a_{i},a_{j}))]^{(2-n)/2}.

2.1
Finite dimensional reduction in the zero weak limit case

In the case where the weak limit of non converging
(PS)
-sequences of positive functions is zero, we proceed as follows to parameterize the neighborhood at infinity
V(q,\ell,\tau)
. Namely we have the following proposition whose proof is, up to minor modifications, identical to the proof of Proposition 7 in
[
7
]
.

Proposition 2.1

Let
u\in V(q,\ell,\tau)
, then the following minimization problem

\min_{\alpha_{i}>0;\lambda_{i}>0;a_{i}\in\partial\mathbb{S}^{n}_{+}(i\leq q);a_{i}\in\mathbb{S}^{n}_{+}(i>q)}\|u-\sum_{1\leq i\leq q+\ell}\alpha_{i}\varphi_{a_{i},\lambda_{i}}\|

has a unique solution (up to permutations on the indices).
Hence, each function
u\in V(q,\ell,\tau)
can be written as

(15)

u=\sum_{1\leq i\leq q+\ell}\alpha_{i}\varphi_{a_{i},\lambda_{i}}+v

where the parameters
\alpha_{i},a_{i},\lambda_{i}
are the solution of the previous minimization problem satisfying

(16)

|\alpha_{i}^{4/(n-2)}K(a_{i})-1|<c\,\tau\,\,\,\forall\,\,1\leq i\leq q+\ell\quad;\quad\lambda_{i}d(a_{i},\partial\mathbb{S}^{n}_{+})>c\,\tau^{-1}\,\,\,\forall i>q

and
v
satisfies

(17)

\displaystyle\|v\|\leq\tau\mbox{ and }\langle v,\psi\rangle=0\,\,\mbox{ for each }

\displaystyle\psi\in\{\varphi_{i};\partial\varphi_{i}/\partial\lambda_{i};\partial\varphi_{i}/\partial a_{i}^{k},\,\,i\geq q+1,\,k\leq n\}\cup\{\delta_{i};\partial\delta_{i}/\partial\lambda_{i};\partial\delta_{i}/\partial a_{i}^{k},\,\,i\leq q,\,k\leq n-1\}.

Regarding the infinite dimensional part
v
we have the following estimate:

Proposition 2.2

Let
u:=\sum_{i=1}^{q+\ell}\alpha_{i}\varphi_{a_{i},\lambda_{i}}\in V(q,\ell,\tau)
with
\varepsilon\ln\lambda_{i}
is small for each
i
. Then there exists a unique function
\overline{v}:=\overline{v}_{\varepsilon,\alpha_{i},a_{i},\lambda_{i}}
such that

(18)

\langle\nabla I_{\varepsilon}(u+\overline{v}),v\rangle=0\quad\mbox{ for each }v\mbox{ satisfying \eqref{V0}}.

Furthermore, it holds that :
\|\overline{v}\|\leq cR(\varepsilon,a,\lambda)
where

R(\varepsilon,a,\lambda):=\varepsilon+\sum_{i=1}^{q+p}\Big(\frac{|\nabla K(a_{i})|}{\lambda_{i}}+\frac{1}{\lambda_{i}^{2}}\Big)+\,\begin{cases}\sum\varepsilon_{ij}^{\frac{n+2}{2(n-2)}}(\ln\varepsilon_{ij}^{-1})^{\frac{n+2}{2n}}+\sum_{i>q}\frac{\ln(\lambda_{i}d_{i})}{(\lambda_{i}d_{i})^{(n+2)/2}}\mbox{ if }n\geq 6,\\
\sum\varepsilon_{ij}(\ln\varepsilon_{ij}^{-1})^{{3}/{5}}+\sum_{i>q}\frac{1}{(\lambda_{i}d_{i})^{3}}\mbox{ if }n=5.\end{cases}

The above proposition follows using similar arguments as in the proof of Proposition
2.4
below.

2.2
The non zero weak limit case

In this subsection we deal with non converging
PS
-sequences having non zero weak limit. Let
\omega
be a non degenerate solution of
(\mathcal{P})
. Arguing as above we have the following parametrization:

Proposition 2.3

Let
u\in V(\omega,q,\ell,\tau)
, then the following minimization problem

\min_{\alpha_{i}>0;\lambda_{i}>0;a_{i}\in\partial\mathbb{S}^{n}_{+}(i\leq q);a_{i}\in\mathbb{S}^{n}_{+}(i>q)}\|u-\alpha_{0}\omega-\sum_{1\leq i\leq q+\ell}\alpha_{i}\varphi_{a_{i},\lambda_{i}}\|

has a unique solution (up to permutations on the indices).

Hence, each function
u\in V(\omega,q,\ell,\tau)
can be written as

(19)

u=\alpha_{0}\omega+\sum_{1\leq i\leq q+\ell}\alpha_{i}\varphi_{a_{i},\lambda_{i}}+v

where the parameters
\alpha_{i},a_{i},\lambda_{i}
are the solution of the previous minimization problem satisfying

(20)

|\alpha_{i}^{4/(n-2)}K(a_{i})-1|<c\,\tau\,\,\,\forall\,\,1\leq i\leq q+\ell\quad;\quad|\alpha_{0}-1|<c\,\tau\quad;\quad\lambda_{i}d(a_{i},\partial\mathbb{S}^{n}_{+})>c\,\tau^{-1}\,\,\,\forall i>q

and
v
satisfies

(21)

\|v\|\leq\tau\,\,;\quad\langle v,\omega\rangle=0\quad\mbox{ and }\quad v\mbox{ satisfies \eqref{V0} }.

Regarding the infinite dimensional part
v
we prove the following proposition:

Proposition 2.4

Assume that
\omega
is a non degenerate solution of
(\mathcal{P})
and let
u:=\alpha_{0}\omega+\sum_{i=1}^{q+\ell}\alpha_{i}\varphi_{a_{i},\lambda_{i}}\in V(\omega,q,\ell,\tau)
with
\varepsilon\ln\lambda_{i}
is small for each
i
. Then there exists a unique function
\overline{v}:=\overline{v}_{\varepsilon,\alpha_{i},a_{i},\lambda_{i}}
such that

(22)

\langle\nabla I_{\varepsilon}(u+\overline{v}),v\rangle=0\quad\mbox{ for each }v\mbox{ satisfying \eqref{wV0}}.

Furthermore, it holds

\|\overline{v}\|\leq c\,R(\varepsilon,a,\lambda)+c\,R(\lambda)\quad\mbox{ with }R(\lambda):=\sum_{i=1}^{q+p}\chi(\lambda_{i})\quad\mbox{ where }\chi(\lambda):=\begin{cases}{1}/{\lambda^{3/2}}&\mbox{ if }n=5,\\
({\ln^{2/3}\lambda})/{\lambda^{2}}&\mbox{ if }n=6,\\
{1}/{\lambda^{2}}&\mbox{ if }n\geq 7.\end{cases}

(We remark that the term
R(\lambda)
does not appear if
\omega=0
).

Proof
.
Observe that, for
b\geq 0
,
c\in\mathbb{R}
and
\gamma>2
, it holds

|b+c|^{\gamma}=b^{\gamma}+\gamma b^{\gamma-1}c+\frac{1}{2}\gamma(\gamma-1)b^{\gamma-2}c^{2}+O\big(|c|^{\gamma}+b^{\gamma-3}|c|^{3}(\mbox{if }\gamma>3)\big).

Thus we derive that, for each
v
satisfying (
21
),

\displaystyle I_{\varepsilon}(u+v)=I_{\varepsilon}(u)-f_{\varepsilon}(v)+(1/2)Q_{\varepsilon}(v)+o(\|v\|^{2}),\quad\mbox{ where }

\displaystyle f_{\varepsilon}(v)=\int Ku^{p-\varepsilon}v\quad\mbox{ and }\quad Q_{\varepsilon}(v):=\|v\|^{2}-((n+2)/(n-2))\int Ku^{p-1-\varepsilon}v^{2}.

Moreover, using (
20
), easy computations imply that

Q_{\varepsilon}(v)=\overline{Q}_{0}(v)+o(\|v\|^{2})
where

\overline{Q}_{0}(v):=\|v\|^{2}-\frac{n+2}{n-2}\int K\omega^{p-1}v^{2}-\frac{n+2}{n-2}\sum_{i=1}^{q+\ell}\int\delta_{i}^{p-1}v^{2}.

Since we assumed that
\omega
is a non degenerate solution of
(\mathcal{P}_{0})
, arguing as in
[
5
]
(pages 354-355) we derive that
\overline{Q}_{0}
is a non degenerate quadratic form and so
Q_{\varepsilon}
. Hence the existence of
\overline{v}
satisfying the equality of the proposition follows. Concerning the estimate of
\|\overline{v}\|
, we get that

\|\overline{v}\|\leq c\|f_{\varepsilon}\|.

Note that, for each
v
satisfying (
21
), it holds (we denote by
\overline{u}:=\sum\alpha_{i}\varphi_{i}
)

f_{\varepsilon}(v)=\int K\overline{u}^{p-\varepsilon}v+\int K\omega^{p-\varepsilon}v+\sum O\Big(\int\delta_{i}^{p-1}\omega|v|+\int\delta_{i}\omega^{p-1}|v|\Big).

Now we need to estimate the previous terms. For the second one, since
v\perp\omega
, it holds

(23)

\int K\omega^{p-\varepsilon}v=\int K\omega^{p}v+O(\varepsilon\|v\|)=O(\varepsilon\|v\|).

For the third and the fourth ones, observe that

\Big(\int_{B(a,1)}\delta_{a,\lambda}^{8n/(n^{2}-4)}\Big)^{(n+2)/(2n)}+\Big(\int_{B(a,1)}\delta_{a,\lambda}^{2n/(n+2)}\Big)^{(n+2)/(2n)}\leq c\chi(\lambda).

\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B(a,1)}\delta_{a,\lambda}^{2n/(n-2)}\Big)^{2/n}\leq\frac{c}{\lambda^{2}}\qquad;\qquad\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B(a,1)}\delta_{a,\lambda}^{2n/(n-2)}\Big)^{(n-2)/(2n)}\leq\frac{c}{\lambda^{(n-2)/2}}.

Hence, using the Holder’s inequalities and, in
B(a_{i},1)
, the fact that
\omega
is
L^{\infty}
bounded, we obtain

(24)

\int\delta_{i}^{p-1}\omega|v|+\int\delta_{i}\omega^{p-1}|v|\leq c\|v\|\chi(\lambda_{i})

It remains the first one. (We remark that in the case
\omega=0
, this integral is the unique term in the linear form
f_{\varepsilon}
). Recall that we have
\varepsilon\ln\lambda_{i}
is small for each
i
. It holds that

(25)

\int_{\mathbb{S}_{+}^{n}}K\overline{u}^{\frac{n+2}{n-2}-\varepsilon}v=\sum\alpha_{i}^{\frac{n+2}{n-2}-\varepsilon}\int_{\mathbb{S}_{+}^{n}}K\varphi_{i}^{\frac{n+2}{n-2}-\varepsilon}v+O\Big(\sum_{i\neq j}\int_{\mathbb{S}_{+}^{n}}\sup(\varphi_{j},\varphi_{i})^{\frac{4}{n-2}}\inf(\varphi_{j},\varphi_{i})|v|\Big).

Observe that, for
n\geq 6
, it follows that
4/(n-2)\leq 1
. Hence, using Holder’s inequality and (
99
), we get

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{S}_{+}^{n}}\sup(\varphi_{j},\varphi_{i})^{\frac{4}{n-2}}\inf(\varphi_{j},\varphi_{i})|v|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{S}_{+}^{n}}(\varphi_{j}\varphi_{i})^{\frac{n+2}{2(n-2)}}|v|\leq c\|v\|\Big(\int_{\mathbb{S}_{+}^{n}}(\delta_{j}\delta_{i})^{\frac{n}{n-2}}\Big)^{\frac{n+2}{2n}}

\displaystyle\leq c\|v\|\varepsilon_{ij}^{\frac{n+2}{2(n-2)}}(\ln\varepsilon_{ij}^{-1})^{\frac{n+2}{2n}}\quad\mbox{ if }n\geq 6,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{S}_{+}^{5}}\sup(\varphi_{j},\varphi_{i})^{{4}/{3}}\inf(\varphi_{j},P\delta_{i})|v|

\displaystyle\leq c\|v\|\varepsilon_{ij}(\ln\varepsilon_{ij}^{-1})^{{3}/{5}}\mbox{ if }n=5.

It remains to estimate the first integral in (
25
). Recall that
v
satisfies (
21
) which implies that
\int\delta_{i}^{p}v=0
for each
i\leq q+\ell
. Thus, it follows that

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{\mathbb{S}_{+}^{n}}K

\displaystyle\delta_{i}^{\frac{n+2}{n-2}-\varepsilon}v\Big|=\Big|K(a_{i})\int_{\mathbb{S}_{+}^{n}}\delta_{i}^{\frac{n+2}{n-2}-\varepsilon}v+O\Big(\int|K(x)-K(a_{i})|\delta_{i}^{p}|v|\Big)\Big|

\displaystyle\leq c\int|\delta_{i}^{-\varepsilon}-c_{0}^{-\varepsilon}\lambda_{i}^{-\varepsilon(n-2)/2}|\delta_{i}^{p}|v|+c|\nabla K(a_{i})|\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}_{+}}d(x,a_{i})\delta_{i}^{\frac{n+2}{n-2}}|v|+c\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}_{+}}d(x,a_{i})^{2}\delta_{i}^{\frac{n+2}{n-2}}|v|

(26)

\displaystyle\leq c\,\|v\|\Big(\varepsilon+\frac{|\nabla K(a_{i})|}{\lambda_{i}}+\frac{1}{\lambda_{i}^{2}}\Big),

where we have used the Holder’s inequality and Lemma
6.1
.

Thus the estimate of the first integral of (
25
) follows for
i\leq q
(since in this case we have
\varphi_{i}=\delta_{i}
).

Now, for
i\geq q+1
(that is
a_{i}\in\mathbb{S}^{n}_{+}
), we get

(27)

\int_{\mathbb{S}_{+}^{n}}K\varphi_{i}^{\frac{n+2}{n-2}-\varepsilon}v=\int_{\mathbb{S}_{+}^{n}}K\delta_{i}^{\frac{n+2}{n-2}-\varepsilon}v+O\Big(\int_{\mathbb{S}_{+}^{n}}\delta_{i}^{\frac{4}{n-2}}|\varphi_{i}-\delta_{i}||v|\Big)

The first integral is computed in (
26
). Finally, using the fact that
|\varphi_{i}-\delta_{i}|\leq c\min(1/(\lambda_{i}^{(n-2)/2}d_{i}^{n-2})\,;\,\delta_{i})
(see Lemma
6.2
) and denoting by
B_{i}:=B(a_{i},d_{i})
, we get that

\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}\setminus B_{i}}\delta_{i}^{\frac{4}{n-2}}|\varphi_{i}-\delta_{i}||v|\leq c\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}\setminus B_{i}}\delta_{i}^{\frac{n+2}{n-2}}|v|\leq c\frac{\|v\|}{(\lambda_{i}d_{i})^{(n+2)/2}},

\int_{B_{i}}\delta_{i}^{\frac{4}{n-2}}|\varphi_{i}-\delta_{i}||v|\leq c\begin{cases}\frac{1}{(\lambda_{i}d_{i}^{2})^{3/2}}\int_{B_{i}}\delta_{i}^{\frac{4}{3}}|v|\leq c\frac{\|v\|}{(\lambda_{i}d_{i})^{3}}\quad\mbox{ if }n=5,\\
\frac{1}{(\lambda_{i}d_{i}^{2})^{(n+2)/4}}\int_{B_{i}}\delta_{i}^{\frac{n+2}{2(n-2)}}|v|\leq c\|v\|\frac{\ln^{(n+2)/(2n)}(\lambda_{i}d_{i})}{(\lambda_{i}d_{i})^{(n+2)/2}}\quad\mbox{ if }n\geq 6.\end{cases}

The result follows.

3
Asymptotic expansion of the gradient

3.1
Expansion in the neighborhood at infinity
V(\omega,q,\ell,\tau)

In the following propositions, we will give the asymptotic expansion of the gradient in the set
V(\omega,q,\ell,\tau)
. Note that, in this paper we do not need some precisions given in these propositions, but we present some details for possible future use.

Proposition 3.1

Let
u:=\alpha_{0}\omega+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\alpha_{i}\varphi_{a_{i},\lambda_{i}}+\overline{v}\in V(\omega,q,\ell,\tau)
(with
N:=q+\ell
). Assume that
\varepsilon\ln\lambda_{i}
is small for each
i
. For each

, there hold

\displaystyle\langle\nabla I_{\varepsilon}(u),\delta_{i}\rangle=\,\alpha_{i}S_{n}\,\Big(1-\frac{\alpha_{i}^{p-1}K(a_{i})}{\lambda_{i}^{\varepsilon(n-2)/2}}\Big)+O\Big(R_{1,i}(\varepsilon,a,\lambda)\Big),

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad-\frac{\alpha_{j}^{p-1}K(a_{j})}{\lambda_{j}^{\varepsilon(n-2)/2}}\Big)-\overline{c}_{6}\frac{\omega(a_{i})}{\lambda_{i}^{(n-2)/2}}\alpha_{0}\Big(1-\alpha_{0}^{p-1}-\frac{\alpha_{i}^{p-1}K(a_{i})}{\lambda_{i}^{\varepsilon(n-2)/2}}\Big)+O\Big(\frac{1}{\lambda_{i}^{2}}+R_{2,i}(\varepsilon,a,\lambda)\Big),

\displaystyle\langle\nabla I_{\varepsilon}(u),\frac{1}{\lambda_{i}}\frac{\partial\delta_{i}}{\partial a_{i}}\rangle_{\lfloor T_{a_{i}}(\partial\mathbb{S}^{n}_{+})}=\frac{c_{2}}{2}\sum_{j\leq q;j\neq i}\frac{\alpha_{j}}{\lambda_{i}}\frac{\partial\varepsilon_{ij}}{\partial a_{i}}\Big(1-\frac{\alpha_{i}^{p-1}K(a_{i})}{\lambda_{i}^{\varepsilon(n-2)/2}}-\frac{\alpha_{j}^{p-1}K(a_{j})}{\lambda_{j}^{\varepsilon(n-2)/2}}\Big)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad-\frac{\alpha_{i}^{p}}{\lambda_{i}^{\varepsilon(n-2)/2}}c_{5}\frac{\nabla_{T}K(a_{i})}{\lambda_{i}}+O\Big(\frac{1}{\lambda_{i}^{2}}+R_{3,i}(\varepsilon,a,\lambda)\Big),

where

\displaystyle R_{1,i}(\varepsilon,a,\lambda):=\varepsilon+\frac{|\nabla K(a_{i})|}{\lambda_{i}}+\frac{1}{\lambda_{i}^{2}}+\sum\varepsilon_{ij}+R^{2}(\varepsilon,a,\lambda)+O_{\omega}\Big(\frac{1}{\lambda_{i}^{(n-2)/2}}+\sum\frac{\ln\lambda_{k}}{\lambda_{k}^{n/2}}\Big),

\displaystyle R_{2,i}(\varepsilon,a,\lambda):=R_{4}(\varepsilon,a,\lambda)+\sum_{j\geq q+1}\varepsilon_{ij},

\displaystyle R_{3,i}(\varepsilon,a,\lambda):=R_{4}(\varepsilon,a,\lambda)+\sum_{j\geq q+1}\varepsilon_{ij}+\sum_{j\leq q;j\neq i}\lambda_{j}d(a_{j},a_{i})\varepsilon_{ij}^{\frac{n+1}{n-2}},

\displaystyle R_{4}(\varepsilon,a,\lambda):=R^{2}(\varepsilon,a,\lambda)+\sum_{j\neq k}\varepsilon_{jk}^{\frac{n}{n-2}}\ln(\varepsilon_{jk}^{-1})+O_{\omega}\Big(\sum\frac{\ln\lambda_{k}}{\lambda_{k}^{n/2}}\Big),

S_{n}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}}\frac{c_{0}^{2n/(n-2)}}{(1+|x|^{2})^{n}}dx\,\,;\,\,c_{3}:=(n-2)c_{0}^{2n/(n-2)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}\frac{x_{n}(|x|^{2}-1)}{(1+|x|^{2})^{n+1}}dx>0

c_{4}:=c_{0}^{2n/(n-2)}\frac{(n-2)^{2}}{8}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{(|x|^{2}-1)\ln(1+|x|^{2})}{(1+|x|^{2})^{n+1}}dx>0\,\,;\,\,c_{2}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{c_{0}^{2n/(n-2)}}{(1+|x|^{2})^{(n+2)/2}}dx

and where
R(\varepsilon,a,\lambda)
is defined in Proposition
2.2
,
T_{a_{i}}(\partial\mathbb{S}^{n}_{+})
denotes the tangent space at the point
a_{i}
.

(We remark that
O_{\omega}(.)
does not appear if
\omega=0
.)

Proof
.
From the definition of
I_{\varepsilon}
, it is easy to deduce that

(28)

\langle\nabla I_{\varepsilon}(u),h\rangle=\langle u,h\rangle-\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}_{+}}K|u|^{p-1-\varepsilon}uh\qquad\mbox{ for each }h\in H^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{n}_{+}).

We will focus on the second assertion and we will give the principal changes for the other ones. Taking
h=\lambda_{i}\partial\delta_{i}/\partial\lambda_{i}
, it holds:
\langle\overline{v},\lambda_{i}\partial\delta_{i}/\partial\lambda_{i}\rangle=0
. To estimate the first term of (
28
), using (
97
) for
j\geq q+1
and (
98
) for
j\leq q
, there hold

(29)

\displaystyle\langle\varphi_{j},\lambda_{i}\frac{\partial\delta_{i}}{\partial\lambda_{i}}\rangle

\displaystyle=O(\int\delta_{j}^{p}\delta_{i})=O(\varepsilon_{ij})\quad\forall\,\,j\geq q+1\quad,\quad\langle\delta_{i},\lambda_{i}\frac{\partial\delta_{i}}{\partial\lambda_{i}}\rangle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}\delta_{i}^{p}\lambda_{i}\frac{\partial\delta_{i}}{\partial\lambda_{i}}=0,

(30)

\displaystyle\langle\delta_{j},\lambda_{i}\frac{\partial\delta_{i}}{\partial\lambda_{i}}\rangle

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}\delta_{j}^{\frac{n+2}{n-2}}\lambda_{i}\frac{\partial\delta_{i}}{\partial\lambda_{i}}=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\delta_{j}^{\frac{n+2}{n-2}}\lambda_{i}\frac{\partial\delta_{i}}{\partial\lambda_{i}}=\frac{c_{2}}{2}\lambda_{i}\frac{\partial\varepsilon_{ij}}{\partial\lambda_{i}}+O\big(\varepsilon_{ij}^{\frac{n}{n-2}}\ln(\varepsilon_{ij}^{-1})\big)\mbox{ if }j\leq q,

\displaystyle\langle\omega,\lambda_{i}\frac{\partial\delta_{i}}{\partial\lambda_{i}}\rangle

\displaystyle=p\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}_{+}}\delta_{i}^{p-1}\lambda_{i}\frac{\partial\delta_{i}}{\partial\lambda_{i}}\omega=p\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}\delta_{i}^{p-1}\lambda_{i}\frac{\partial\delta_{i}}{\partial\lambda_{i}}(\iota\omega)

4
Critical points of the Kirchoff-Routh type functional
\mathcal{F}_{z,m}

In this section we study the existence of critical points of the Kirchoff-Routh type functional
\mathcal{F}_{z,m}
. Our main result states as follows

Proposition 4.1

Let
z\in\partial\mathbb{S}^{n}_{+}
be a non degenerate critical point of
K_{1}:=K_{\lfloor{\partial\mathbb{S}^{n}_{+}}}
and
\mathcal{F}_{z,m}
be defined in (
6
). Then we have

(i)

If
z
is a local minimum then
\mathcal{F}_{z,m}
achieves its minimum.

(ii)

If
z
is a local maximum then
\mathcal{F}_{z,m}
does not have any critical point.

iii)

If the hessian matrix
D^{2}K_{1}(z)
has at least a positive eigenvalue
\lambda
, then the function
\mathcal{F}_{z,2}
admits at least one critical point.

iv)

For each simple positive eigenvalue
\lambda
of the hessian matrix
D^{2}K_{1}(z)
the function
\mathcal{F}_{z,2}
has a non-degenerate critical point
(x_{\lambda},y_{\lambda})
.

Furthermore, if
\lambda_{1}
and
\lambda_{2}
are two simple positive eigenvalues, then
(x_{\lambda_{1}},y_{\lambda_{1}})\neq(x_{\lambda_{2}},y_{\lambda_{2}})
.

Proof
.  The proof of
(i)
is trivial since
D^{2}K_{1}(z)
is a positive definite matrix. Indeed in this case
\mathcal{F}_{z,m}
is coercive.

The proof of statement
(ii)
is as follows: writing
(\xi_{1},\cdots,\xi_{m}):=r\Lambda
with
r>0
and
\Lambda:=(\Lambda_{1},\cdots,\Lambda_{m})\in(\mathbb{R}^{n-1})^{m}
with
\|\Lambda\|=1
, the function
\mathcal{F}_{z,m}
reads as follows:

\mathcal{F}_{z,m}(r,\Lambda):=\frac{1}{2}r^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{m}D^{2}K_{1}(z)(\Lambda_{i},\Lambda_{i})+\frac{1}{r^{n-2}}\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq m}\frac{1}{|\Lambda_{i}-\Lambda_{j}|^{n-2}}.

Hence, under the assumption that
D^{2}K_{1}(z)(\Lambda_{i},\Lambda_{i})\leq 0
for each
\Lambda_{i}
, we have

\frac{\partial\mathcal{F}_{z,m}}{\partial r}=r\sum_{i=1}^{m}D^{2}K_{1}(z)(\Lambda_{i},\Lambda_{i})-\frac{n-2}{r^{n-1}}\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq m}\frac{1}{|\Lambda_{i}-\Lambda_{j}|^{n-2}}<0

Hence the function
\mathcal{F}_{z,m}
does not have any critical point if the point
z
is a local maximum.

To prove the statement
(iii)
let
\lambda
be a positive eigenvalue of
M:=D^{2}K_{1}(z)
(which exists by our assumption). To find a critical point of
\mathcal{F}_{z,2}
, we need to solve the equation
D\mathcal{F}_{z,2}(x,y)=0
which is equivalent to

(60)

M.x-(n-2)\frac{x-y}{|x-y|^{n}}=0\quad\mbox{ and }\quad M.y-(n-2)\frac{y-x}{|x-y|^{n}}=0.

Summing the two equations, we get
M.(x+y)=0
which implies that
x=-y
(since
M
is assumed to be non-degenerate). Inserting this information in the first equation of (
60
), we obtain

(61)

M.x=\frac{(n-2)}{2^{n-1}}\frac{x}{|x|^{n}}:={\underline{c}}\frac{x}{|x|^{n}}.

It is easy to see that
{\overline{x}}:=({\underline{c}}/\lambda)^{1/n}u_{\lambda}
is a solution of (
61
) (where
u_{\lambda}
is an unit eigenvector corresponding to the eigenvalue
\lambda
). Hence the proof of the first claim.

For the last claim, we have by assumption that the eigenspace
V_{\lambda}
of the eigenvalue
\lambda
is one dimensional and our goal is to prove that the critical point
({\overline{x}},-{\overline{x}})
obtained in the statement
(iii)
of this proposition is non-degenerate. To that aim, we observe that

D^{2}\mathcal{F}_{z,2}({\overline{x}},{\overline{y}})((x,y),.)=\begin{pmatrix}M.x-{(n-2)}\frac{x-y}{|\overline{x}-\overline{y}|^{n}}+n(n-2)\frac{\langle\overline{x}-\overline{y},x-y\rangle}{|\overline{x}-\overline{y}|^{n+2}}(\overline{x}-\overline{y})\\
M.y-(n-2)\frac{y-x}{|\overline{y}-\overline{x}|^{n}}+n(n-2)\frac{\langle\overline{y}-\overline{x},y-x\rangle}{|\overline{x}-\overline{y}|^{n+2}}(\overline{y}-\overline{x})\end{pmatrix}.

We claim that the unique solution of
D^{2}\mathcal{F}_{z,2}({\overline{x}},{\overline{y}})((x,y),.)=0
is
(0,0)
. Indeed, summing up the two components, we obtain that
M.(x+y)=0
which implies that
x=-y
. The first component hence becomes:

(62)

M.x=\frac{(n-2)}{2^{n-1}}\frac{x}{|\overline{x}|^{n}}-\frac{n(n-2)}{2^{n-1}}\frac{\langle\overline{x},x\rangle}{|\overline{x}|^{n+2}}\overline{x}.

Taking the scalar product of (
62
) with
\overline{x}
, we get

\langle M.x,\overline{x}\rangle=\frac{(n-2)}{2^{n-1}}\frac{\langle x,\overline{x}\rangle}{|\overline{x}|^{n}}-\frac{n(n-2)}{2^{n-1}}\frac{\langle\overline{x},x\rangle}{|\overline{x}|^{n}}=\frac{(n-2)}{2^{n-1}}(1-n)\frac{\langle x,\overline{x}\rangle}{|\overline{x}|^{n}}.

Using the fact that
M
is a symmetric matrix, we derive that
\langle M.x,\overline{x}\rangle=\langle M.\overline{x},{x}\rangle
. Using the fact that
\overline{x}
is a solution of (
61
), the previous equation implies

\frac{(n-2)}{2^{n-1}}(1-n)\frac{\langle x,\overline{x}\rangle}{|\overline{x}|^{n}}=\frac{(n-2)}{2^{n-1}}\frac{\langle\overline{x},x\rangle}{|\overline{x}|^{n}}

which implies that
\langle x,\overline{x}\rangle=0
. Putting this information in (
62

|\overline{x}|^{n}=\underline{c}/\lambda
we derive that
M.x=\lambda x
which implies that
x\in V_{\lambda}
. Now, since we assumed that
\mbox{dim }V_{\lambda}=1
and using the fact that
\langle x,\overline{x}\rangle=0
and
\overline{x}\in V_{\lambda}
we obtain that
x=0
and therefore
(x,y)=(0,0)
. Hence
D^{2}F({\overline{x}},{\overline{y}})
is non-degenerate. This completes the proof.

Proposition 4.2

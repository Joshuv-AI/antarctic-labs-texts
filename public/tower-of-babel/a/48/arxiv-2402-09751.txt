Long-time behavior towards viscous-dispersive shock for Navier-Stokes equations of Korteweg type Thanks: Acknowledgment. S. Han, M.-J. Kang and H. Lee were partially supported by the National Research Foundation of Korea (NRF-2019R1C1C1009355 and NRF-2019R1A5A1028324)
Sungho Han, Moon-Jin Kang, Jeongho Kim, Hobin Lee

Abstract
. We consider the so-called Naiver-Stokes-Korteweg(NSK) equations for the dynamics of compressible barotropic viscous fluids with internal capillarity. We handle the time-asymptotic stability in 1D of the viscous-dispersive shock wave that is a traveling wave solution to NSK as a viscous-dispersive counterpart of a Riemann shock. More precisely, we prove that when the prescribed far-field states of NSK are connected by a single Hugoniot curve, then solutions of NSK tend to the viscous-dispersive shock wave as time goes to infinity. To obtain the convergence, we extend the theory of a a -contraction with shifts, used for the Navier-Stokes equations, to the NSK system. The main difficulty in analysis for NSK is due to the third-order derivative terms of the specific volume in the momentum equation. To resolve the problem, we introduce an auxiliary variable that is equivalent to the derivative of the specific volume.

1.
Introduction

A study on the fluid model with an internal capillarity effect dates back to the works of Van der Waals and Korteweg
[
28
,
35
]
, where the stress tensor may depend on the high-order derivative of the density. Later, Duun and Serrin
[
14
]
introduced a thermodynamically consistent fluid model for internal capillarity, called the Navier-Stokes-Korteweg(NSK) equations. After its introduction, the NSK system has drawn a lot of attention and there has been numerous literature on the mathematical theory and application, due to its strong relationship with the quantum fluid models. We refer to the following literature and references therein for the readers who are interested in the state-of-the-art results on the Korteweg type fluids
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
11
]
.

In this paper, we are interested in the time-asymptotic stability of the one-dimensional compressible fluid model of the Korteweg type. Consider the one-dimensional barotropic NSK equations in the Lagrangian mass coordinates:

(1.1)

\displaystyle v_{t}-u_{x}=0,\quad(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R},

\displaystyle u_{t}+p(v)_{x}=\mu\left(\frac{u_{x}}{v}\right)_{x}+\kappa\left(\frac{-v_{xx}}{v^{5}}+\frac{5v_{x}^{2}}{2v^{6}}\right)_{x},

where the unknown functions
v=v(t,x)
and
u=u(t,x)
represent the specific volume and velocity of the fluid, respectively. The pressure
p=p(v)
is given by the
\gamma
-law, that is,

p(v)=bv^{-\gamma},\quad b>0,\quad\gamma>1.

Here, the constants
\mu>0
and
\kappa>0
represent the viscosity coefficient and capillary coefficient of the fluid, respectively. For simplicity, we normalize the coefficients so that
b=1,\mu=1
, and
\kappa=1
. The initial data of the NSK system (
1.1
) is given by
(v_{0},u_{0})
, whose far-field states are prescribed as constants:

\lim_{x\to\pm\infty}(v_{0}(x),u_{0}(x))=(v_{\pm},u_{\pm}).

When
\kappa=0
, that is, the capillarity effect is ignored, the NSK system (
1.1
) is reduced to the standard compressible Navier-Stokes(NS) equations:

(1.2)

\displaystyle

\displaystyle v_{t}-u_{x}=0,\quad(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R},

\displaystyle u_{t}+p(v)_{x}=\mu\left(\frac{u_{x}}{v}\right)_{x}.

Among many interesting topics on the NS equations (
1.2
), the large-time behavior of solutions to (
1.2
) is one of the most important and motivated problems, as it is related to the inviscid limit to the Euler equation. Due to its significance, there has been a lot of previous literature on the time asymptotic behavior of the NS equations. Among the numerous results on the time-asymptotic stability of the NS equations (
1.2
), we refer to
[
16
,
17
,
26
,
31
,
32
,
33
]
, although the list is totally not exhaustive. These results naturally motivate us to study the time-asymptotic behavior of the solution to the NSK equations (
1.1
). The large-time behavior of the NSK equations has a close relationship with the solution to the Euler equation

(1.3)

\displaystyle v_{t}-u_{x}=0,\quad(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R},

\displaystyle u_{t}+p(v)_{x}=0,

subject to the Riemann initial data

(1.4)

(v(0,x),u(0,x))=\begin{cases}(v_{-},u_{-}),&x<0,\\
(v_{+},u_{+}),&x>0,\end{cases}

as in the Navier-Stokes equations case
[
30
]
. We focus on the case when the end states
(v_{\pm},u_{\pm})
are connected by a single Hugoniot curve. Without loss of generality, we only handle the case of a 2-shock curve. In other words, for a given right-end state
(v_{+},u_{+})
we consider the left-end state
(v_{-},u_{-})
that is on the 2-shock curve
S_{2}(v_{+},u_{+})
satisfying the following Rankine-Hugoniot conditions:

(1.5)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}-\sigma(v_{+}-v_{-})-(u_{+}-u_{-})=0,\\
-\sigma(u_{+}-v_{-})-(p(v_{+})-p(v_{-}))=0,\end{cases}\quad\sigma:=\sqrt{-\frac{p(v_{+})-p(v_{-})}{v_{+}-v_{-}}}>0,

and the entropy condition:

v_{-}<v_{+},\quad u_{-}>u_{+}.

Then the Riemann solution
(\overline{v},\overline{u})
to the Euler equations (
1.3
)–(
1.4
) is given by 2-shock wave

(1.6)

(\overline{v}(t,x),\overline{u}(t,x))=\begin{cases}(v_{-},u_{-})&\text{if}\quad x<\sigma t,\\
(v_{+},u_{+})&\text{if}\quad x>\sigma t.\end{cases}

For the case of NSK equations (
1.1
), the counterpart of the Riemann solution (
1.6
) is a viscous-dispersive shock, as a traveling wave
(\widetilde{v},\widetilde{u})(x-\sigma t)
solution to (
1.1
), that satisfies the following ODEs:

(1.7)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &-\sigma\widetilde{v}^{\prime}-\widetilde{u}^{\prime}=0,\\
&-\sigma\widetilde{u}^{\prime}+p(\widetilde{v})^{\prime}=\left(\frac{\widetilde{u}^{\prime}}{\widetilde{v}}\right)^{\prime}+\left(\frac{-\widetilde{v}^{\prime\prime}}{\widetilde{v}^{5}}+\frac{5(\widetilde{v}^{\prime})^{2}}{2\widetilde{v}^{6}}\right)^{\prime},\\
&(\widetilde{v},\widetilde{u})(\pm\infty)=(v_{\pm},u_{\pm}).\end{aligned}

Similar to the Navier-Stokes equations, the time-asymptotic stability of the NSK system has been investigated in many literature. A first study on the stability of the NSK equations is due to
[
4
]
, where the authors provided the stability and the large-time behavior of the solutions toward the rarefaction wave, followed by the analysis on the large-time behavior of the solution perturbed from the viscous-dispersive shock wave
[
6
]
. We also mention several results on the stability of the non-isentropic Navier-Stokes-Kortweg system for the case of contact wave
[
8
]
and the composition of contact and rarefaction waves
[
7
]
. We also refer to the stability result for the planar rarefaction wave for the three-dimensional NSK equations
[
29
]
.

In particular, the authors in
[
6
]
used a classical anti-derivative method (cf.
[
32
]
) for obtaining the time-asymptotic stability of viscous-dispersive shock wave, where the zero-mass condition for the initial perturbation is crucially imposed. For the NS system as in
[
17
,
26
]
, this zero-mass constraint on the initial data was removed by using the theory of
a
-contraction with shifts.

Therefore, the goal of the paper is to prove the time-asymptotic stability of the viscous-dispersive shock wave for the NSK equation (
1.1
) without the zero mass condition, based on the theory of
a
-contraction with shifts.

The method of
a
-contraction with shifts was developed in
[
20
]
for the stability of extremal shocks in the hyperbolic system of conservation laws, especially for the Euler system. The first extension of the method to a viscous system was done in the 1D scalar case
[
21
]
(
[
19
]
for a more general case), and then in the multi-D case
[
25
]
. In the context of the one-dimensional barotropic NS system, this method was used to prove the contraction property of any large perturbations for a single viscous shock in
[
22
,
23
]
, and for a composite wave of two shocks in
[
24
]
. Furthermore, the method was also used in
[
26
]
to show the long-time behavior of the barotropic NS system for the composition of shock and rarefaction under the 1D perturbation, and for a single shock under the multi-D perturbation in
[
36
]
. Its extension to the Navier-Stokes-Fourier system was discussed in
[
27
]
. As for applications of the method to other viscous hyperbolic systems, we also refer to
[
9
,
10
]
, particularly in the context of the viscous hyperbolic system arising from a chemotaxis model.

Our main theorem reads as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

For a given state
(v_{+},u_{+})\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}
, there exist positive constants
C_{0},\delta_{0}
, and
\varepsilon_{1}
such that the following holds.
For any
(v_{-},u_{-})
on the 2-shock curve
S_{2}(v_{+},u_{+})
, that is, satisfying the Rankine-Hugoniot condition (
1.5
), such that
|v_{+}-v_{-}|<\delta_{0}
, denote
(\widetilde{v},\widetilde{u})(x-\sigma t)
the 2-viscous-dispersive shock defined in (
1.7
). Let
(v_{0},u_{0})
be any initial data such that

\sum_{\pm}\left(\|v_{0}-v_{\pm}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{\pm})}+\|u_{0}-u_{\pm}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{\pm})}\right)+\|v_{0x}\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R})}+\|u_{0x}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}<\varepsilon_{0},

where
\mathbb{R}_{-}:=-\mathbb{R}_{+}=(-\infty,0)
. Then, the Navier-Stokes-Korteweg system (
1.1
) admits a unique global-in-time solution
(v,u)
. Moreover, there exists a Lipschitz continuous shift
X(t)
such that

\displaystyle v(t,x)-\widetilde{v}(x-\sigma t-X(t))\in C(0,\infty;H^{2}(\mathbb{R})),

\displaystyle u(t,x)-\widetilde{u}(x-\sigma t-X(t))\in C(0,\infty;H^{1}(\mathbb{R})).

In addition, we have

\lim_{t\to\infty}\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\left|(v,u)(t,x)-(\widetilde{v},\widetilde{u})(x-\sigma t-X(t))\right|=0

and

(1.8)

\lim_{t\to\infty}|\dot{X}(t)|=0.

Remark 1.1
.

Since (
1.8
) implies

\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{X(t)}{t}=0,

the shift function
X(t)
grows at most sub-linearly as
t\to\infty
. Thus, the shifted wave
\widetilde{U}(x-\sigma t-X(t))
tends to the original wave
\widetilde{U}(x-\sigma t)
time-asymptotically.

Remark 1.2
.

The results of Theorem
1.1
still hold for the NSK system with a general pressure
p(v)>0
satisfying
p^{\prime}(v)<0,p^{\prime\prime}(v)>0
for
v>0
, and smooth viscosity
\mu=\mu(v)
and smooth capillary
\kappa=\kappa(v)
, without meaningful added difficulties, since we consider small
H^{2}
-perturbations for
v
variables. So, our result especially includes the cases of
\mu(v)=v^{-\alpha}
and
\kappa(v)=v^{-\beta}
for
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{R}
as (cf.
[
5
]
)

(1.9)

\displaystyle

\displaystyle v_{t}-u_{x}=0,\quad(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R},

\displaystyle u_{t}+p(v)_{x}=\left(\frac{u_{x}}{v^{\alpha+1}}\right)_{x}+\left(-\frac{v_{xx}}{v^{\beta+5}}+\frac{\beta+5}{2}\frac{v_{x}^{2}}{v^{\beta+6}}\right)_{x}.

In particular, when
\beta=-1
, then the system (
1.9
) represents the one-dimensional quantum fluid model in the Lagrangian coordinate.

1.7

-contraction with shift, and then we obtain the estimates on the high-order terms in Section

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we present several preliminary estimates on the relative quantities for the pressure and the internal energy. We also provide the existence and properties of viscous-dispersive shock in this section. Finally, we introduce several
O(1)
-constants and related estimates on them.

2.1.
Estimates on the relative quantities

We present several upper and lower bounds on the relative quantities that will be used in estimating the relative entropy. For any function
F:(0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
and
v,w\in(0,\infty)
, we define the relative quantity
F(v|w)
as

F(v|w):=F(v)-F(w)-F^{\prime}(w)(v-w).

In particular, when
F
is convex, then the relative quantity is always positive. In the following lemma, we present several lower and upper bounds on the relative quantities for the pressure
p(v)=v^{-\gamma}
and the internal energy
Q(v)=\frac{v^{1-\gamma}}{\gamma-1}
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\gamma>1
and
v_{+}
be given constants. Then, there exists constants
C,\delta_{*}
such that the following assertions hold:

(1)

For any
v,\bar{v}
satisfying
0<\bar{v}<2v_{+}
and
0<v<3v_{+}
,

|v-\bar{v}|^{2}\leq CQ(v|\bar{v}),\quad|v-\bar{v}|^{2}\leq Cp(v|w).

(2)

For any
v,\bar{v}
satisfying
v,\bar{v}>v_{+}/2
,

|p(v)-p(\bar{v})|\leq C|v-\bar{v}|.

(3)

For any
0<\delta<\delta_{*}
and any
(v,\bar{v})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}
satisfying
|p(v)-p(\bar{v})|<\delta
and
|p(\bar{v})-p(v_{+})|<\delta,

\displaystyle p(v|\bar{v})\leq\left(\frac{\gamma+1}{2\gamma}\frac{1}{p(\bar{v})}+C\delta\right)|p(v)-p(\bar{v})|^{2},

\displaystyle Q(v|\bar{v})\geq\frac{p(\bar{v})^{-\frac{1}{\gamma}-1}}{2\gamma}|p(v)-p(\bar{v})|^{2}-\frac{1+\gamma}{3\gamma^{2}}p(\bar{v})^{-\frac{1}{\gamma}-2}(p(v)-p(\bar{v}))^{3},

\displaystyle Q(v|\bar{v})\leq\left(\frac{p(\bar{v})^{-\frac{1}{\gamma}-1}}{2\gamma}+C\delta\right)|p(v)-p(\bar{v})|^{2}.

Proof.

Since the proofs are duplicates of those of
[
22
, Lemma 2.4, 2.5, and 2.6]
, we omit the proof.
∎

2.2.
Viscous-dispersive shock wave

In the following lemma, we present the existence of the viscous-dispersive shock wave, and its properties that are useful in our analysis. We consider a 2-shock connecting
(v_{-},u_{-})
and
(v_{+},u_{+})
such that
(v_{-},u_{-})\in S_{2}(v_{+},u_{+})
. The existence of viscous-dispersive shock wave for the Navier-Stokes-Korteweg equations was already studied in
[
6
]
, but the condition on the end states for the existence is too complicated. In particular, the existence of the viscous-dispersive shock wave is not guaranteed even for a weak shock, that is,
|v_{+}-v_{-}|\ll 1
. On the other hand, the authors in
[
15
]
provide the method of slow manifold theory to show the existence of viscous-dispersive shock wave, which satisfies ODE that is similar to ours. Moreover, the only condition for the existence is the smallness of shock strength, as in our case. Therefore, we provide the existence and several important properties of the wave in the same spirit as in
[
15
]
.

Lemma 2.2
.

For a given right-end state
(v_{+},u_{+})
, there exists a positive constant
\delta_{0}
such that the following statement holds. For any left end state
(v_{-},u_{-})\in S_{2}(v_{+},u_{+})
with
|v_{+}-v_{-}|\sim|u_{+}-u_{-}|=:\delta_{S}<\delta_{0}
, there exists a unique solution
(\widetilde{v},\widetilde{u})(\xi)
to (
1.7
) such that
\widetilde{v}(0)=\frac{v_{-}+v_{+}}{2}
. Moreover, the following estimates hold: there exists a positive constant
C
such that

(2.1)

\displaystyle

\displaystyle\widetilde{u}^{\prime}<0,\quad\widetilde{v}^{\prime}>0,

\displaystyle C^{-1}\widetilde{v}^{\prime}(x-\sigma t)\leq\widetilde{u}^{\prime}(x-\sigma t)\leq C\widetilde{v}^{\prime}(x-\sigma t),\quad x\in\mathbb{R},t>0,

\displaystyle|\widetilde{v}(\xi)-v_{\pm}|\leq C\delta_{S}e^{-C\delta_{S}|\xi|},\quad\pm\xi>0,

\displaystyle|\widetilde{v}^{\prime}(\xi)|\leq C\delta_{S}^{2}e^{-C\delta_{S}|\xi|},\quad|\widetilde{v}^{\prime\prime}(\xi)|\leq C\delta_{S}|\widetilde{v}^{\prime}(\xi)|.

Proof.

Since the proof is technical and lengthy, we postpone the proof to Appendix
B
.
∎

Remark 2.1
.

We remark that the monotonicity of the viscous-dispersive shock is a consequence of the smallness of shock strength. Indeed, when the shock strength
\delta_{S}
is not small, we cannot guarantee the monotonicity of the shock profile, and there might exist oscillation in the profile. See Figure
1
for the numerical simulation of the viscous-dispersive shocks with different shock strengths.

Figure 1.
Profiles of
\widetilde{v}
for weak shock
\delta_{S}=0.05
(left) and for strong shock
\delta_{S}=0.5
(right), when the right-end state is fixed as
v_{+}=0.7
. The profile of the small shock is monotone, while that of the large shock has an oscillation. We also note that, even in the small shock case, the viscous-dispersive shock is not symmetric with respect to the inflection point, unlike the viscous shock of the classical Navier-Stokes equations.

2.3.
Useful
O(1)
-constants

In the later analysis, we will use the following
O(1)
-constants defined as

(2.2)

\sigma_{\ell}:=\sqrt{-p^{\prime}(v_{-})},\quad\alpha_{\ell}:=\frac{\gamma+1}{2\gamma\sigma_{\ell}p(v_{-})}=\frac{p^{\prime\prime}(v_{-})}{2|p^{\prime}(v_{-})|^{2}\sigma_{\ell}}.

These constants are indeed independent of the small shock strength
\delta_{S}
since
v_{+}/2\leq v_{-}\leq v_{+}
. Then, the following estimates on the
O(1)
-constants hold:

(2.3)

|\sigma-\sigma_{\ell}|=\left|\sqrt{-\frac{p(v_{+})-p(v_{-})}{v_{+}-v_{-}}}-\sqrt{-p^{\prime}(v_{-})}\right|\leq C\delta_{S}.

Moreover, thanks to Lemma
2.2
, the shock profile is monotone for the weak shock, and therefore
v_{-}\leq\widetilde{v}(x-\sigma t)\leq v_{+}
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}
. This yields the following estimates

(2.4)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\|\sigma_{\ell}^{2}+p^{\prime}(\widetilde{v})\|_{L^{\infty}}=\|p^{\prime}(\widetilde{v})-p^{\prime}(v_{-})\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C\delta_{S},\\
&\left\|\frac{1}{\sigma_{\ell}^{2}}-\frac{p(\widetilde{v})^{-\frac{1}{\gamma}-1}}{\gamma}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}=\left\|\frac{(v_{-})^{\gamma+1}}{\gamma}-\frac{p(\widetilde{v})^{-\frac{1}{\gamma}-1}}{\gamma}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C\delta_{S}.\end{aligned}

Throughout the paper,
C
denotes a positive
O(1)
-constant which may change from line to line, but which is independent of the small constants like
\delta_{S},\varepsilon_{1}
and the lifespan
T
given in Proposition
3.3
.

2.4.
Augmented system

We close this section, by introducing the augmented system for (
1.1
). Our first observation is that the natural dissipative entropy (or energy) for the NSK system (
1.1
) is given by

\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(\frac{|u|^{2}}{2}+Q(v)+\frac{(v_{x})^{2}}{2v^{5}}\right)\,dx.

Therefore, it is natural to introduce an auxiliary variable
w
defined as

w=-\frac{v_{x}}{v^{5/2}},

so that the entropy can be written in terms of extended variable
U=(v,u,w)
as

(2.5)

\eta(U):=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(\frac{|u|^{2}}{2}+Q(v)+\frac{|w|^{2}}{2}\right)\,dx,

whose relative functional would have a natural quadratic structure with respect to the variables
(v,u,w)
. Therefore, in order to use the well-established relative entropy method to control the
L^{2}
-perturbation, it is more convenient to consider an augmented system consisting of three variables
(v,u,w)
, instead of the original NSK equations (
1.1
). Using the equation of
v
, we deduce that
w
satisfies

w_{t}=\left(-\frac{v_{t}}{v^{5/2}}\right)_{x}=\left(-\frac{u_{x}}{v^{5/2}}\right)_{x},

and the Korteweg term in the momentum equation can be represented in terms of
w
as

-\frac{v_{xx}}{v^{5}}+\frac{5v^{2}_{x}}{2v^{6}}=\frac{w_{x}}{v^{5/2}}.

Thus, the NSK system (
1.1
) can be transformed into the following extended system with respect to
(v,u,w)
:

(2.6)

\begin{cases}v_{t}-u_{x}=0,\\
u_{t}+p(v)_{x}=\left(\frac{u_{x}}{v}\right)_{x}+\left(\frac{w_{x}}{v^{5/2}}\right)_{x},\\
w_{t}=\left(-\frac{u_{x}}{v^{5/2}}\right)_{x}.\end{cases}

Henceforth, we refer (
2.6
) as the NSK system, instead of the original system (
1.1
), unless otherwise specified. We also extend the viscous-dispersive shock wave
(\widetilde{v},\widetilde{u})
to
(\widetilde{v},\widetilde{u},\widetilde{w})
, which obviously satisfies

(2.7)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &-\sigma\widetilde{v}_{\xi}-\widetilde{u}_{\xi}=0,\\
&-\sigma\widetilde{u}_{\xi}+(p(\widetilde{v}))_{\xi}=\left(\frac{\widetilde{u}_{\xi}}{\widetilde{v}}\right)_{\xi}+\left(\frac{\widetilde{w}_{\xi}}{\widetilde{v}^{5/2}}\right)_{\xi},\\
&-\sigma\widetilde{w}_{\xi}=\left(-\frac{\widetilde{u}_{\xi}}{\widetilde{v}^{5/2}}\right)_{\xi}.\end{aligned}

As we will see in Section
4
, the system (
2.6
) can be written as a general hyperbolic equation, so that the classical relative entropy estimates
[
12
,
13
]
can be directly applied.

3.
A priori estimate and Proof of Theorem
1.1

In this section, we first provide the a priori estimate for the perturbation, which is the key estimate for the main theorem. The proof of a priori estimate is presented in the next two sections. After stating the a priori estimate, we prove the global existence and time-asymptotic behavior of the solution, completing the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

3.1.
Local existence

We first provide the local existence of strong solutions to the original NSK system (
1.1
), or equivalently, the NSK system (
2.6
).

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
\underline{v}
and
\underline{u}
be smooth monotone functions such that

\underline{v}(x)=v_{\pm},\quad\underline{u}(x)=u_{\pm},\quad\mbox{for}\quad\pm x\geq 1.

Then, for any constants
M_{0}
,
M_{1}
,
\underline{\kappa}_{0}
,
\overline{\kappa}_{0}
,
\underline{\kappa}_{1}
, and
\overline{\kappa}_{1}
with

0<M_{0}<M_{1},\quad\mbox{and}\quad 0<\underline{\kappa}_{1}<\underline{\kappa}_{0}<\overline{\kappa}_{0}<\overline{\kappa}_{1},

there exists a finite time
T_{0}>0
such that if the initial data
(v_{0},u_{0})
satisfy

\|v_{0}-\underline{v}\|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{R})}+\|u_{0}-\underline{u}\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R})}\leq M_{0},\quad\mbox{and}\quad\underline{\kappa}_{0}\leq v_{0}(x)\leq\overline{\kappa}_{0},\quad\forall x\in\mathbb{R},

the Navier-Stokes-Korteweg equations (
1.1
) admit a unique solution
(v,u)
on
[0,T_{0}]
satisfying

\displaystyle v-\underline{v}\in L^{\infty}([0,T_{0}];H^{2}(\mathbb{R}))\cap L^{2}([0,T_{0}];H^{3}(\mathbb{R})),\quad u-\underline{u}\in L^{\infty}([0,T_{0}];H^{1}(\mathbb{R}))\cap L^{2}([0,T_{0}];H^{2}(\mathbb{R})),

\|v-\underline{v}\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T_{0}];H^{2}(\mathbb{R}))}+\|u-\underline{u}\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T_{0}];H^{1}(\mathbb{R}))}\leq M_{1}

and

\underline{\kappa}_{1}\leq v(t,x)\leq\overline{\kappa}_{1},\quad\forall(t,x)\in[0,T_{0}]\times\mathbb{R}.

Proof.

The proof of the local existence can be obtained by using the standard argument of generating a sequence of approximate solutions and the Cauchy estimate, see for example
[
34
]
. For the brevity of the paper, we omit the proof.
∎

3.2.
Construction of shift

Next, we introduce the shift
X:\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}
as a solution to the following ODE:

(3.1)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \dot{X}(t)&=-\frac{M}{\delta_{S}}\Bigg(\int_{\mathbb{R}}a\left(x-\sigma t-X(t)\right)\widetilde{u}_{x}\left(x-\sigma t-X(t)\right)\big(u-\widetilde{u}(x-\sigma t-X(t)\big)\,dx\\
&\hskip 56.9055pt+\frac{1}{\sigma}\int_{\mathbb{R}}a(x-\sigma t-X(t))\partial_{x}p\big(\widetilde{v}(x-\sigma t-X(t))\big)\big(v-\widetilde{v}(x-\sigma t-X(t))\big)\,dx\Bigg),\end{aligned}

where
M=\frac{5\sigma_{\ell}^{3}\alpha_{\ell}}{4}
. Then, the standard existence theorem for the ODE can be applied to guarantee the existence of the shift.

Proposition 3.2
.

For any
c_{1},c_{2},c_{3}>0
, there exists a constant
C>0
such that the following is true. For any
T>0
, and any function
v,u\in L^{\infty}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R})
with

c_{1}\leq v(t,x)\leq c_{2},\quad|u(t,x)|\leq c_{3},\quad(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R},

the ODE (
3.1
) has a unique Lipschitz continuous solution
X
on
[0,T]
. Moreover, we have

|X(t)|\leq Ct,\quad t\in[0,T].

As the name implies, the constructed shift
X(t)
will play an important role in the theory of
a
-contraction with shift. In the following, we use the following abbreviated notation for the shifted function. For any function
g:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
, we define

g^{X}(\cdot):=g(\cdot-X(t)),\quad t\geq 0.

3.3.
A priori estimate

We now state the a priori estimate, which is the key estimate for obtaining the time-asymptotic behavior of the NSK equations.

Proposition 3.3
.

For a given state
(v_{+},u_{+})\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}
, there exist positive constants
C_{0},\delta_{0}
, and
\varepsilon_{1}
such that the following holds:

Suppose that
(v,u,w)
is the solution to (
2.6
) on
[0,T]
for some
T>0
, and
(\widetilde{v},\widetilde{u},\widetilde{w})
is defined in (
2.7
). Let
X
be the Lipschitz continuous solution to (
3.1
) with weight function
a
defined in (
4.2
). Assume that the shock strength
\delta_{S}
is less than
\delta_{0}
and that

\displaystyle v-\widetilde{v}^{X}\in L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{2}(\mathbb{R})),

\displaystyle u-\widetilde{u}^{X}\in L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{1}(\mathbb{R}))\cap L^{2}(0,T;H^{2}(\mathbb{R})),

and

(3.2)

\|v-\widetilde{v}^{X}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{2}(\mathbb{R}))}+\|u-\widetilde{u}^{X}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{1}(\mathbb{R}))}\leq\varepsilon_{1}.

Then, for all
0\leq t\leq T
,

(3.3)

\displaystyle

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left(\left\lVert v-\widetilde{v}^{X}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}+\left\lVert u-\widetilde{u}^{X}\right\rVert_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}+\left\lVert w-\widetilde{w}^{X}\right\rVert_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}\right)+\delta_{S}\int_{0}^{t}|\dot{X}(s)|^{2}\,ds

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\left(G_{1}+G_{3}+G^{S}\right)\,ds+\int_{0}^{t}\left(D_{u_{1}}+D_{u_{2}}+G_{w}+G_{w_{1}}+G_{w_{2}}\right)\,ds

\displaystyle\leq C_{0}\left(\left\lVert v_{0}-\widetilde{v}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}+\left\lVert u_{0}-\widetilde{u}\right\rVert_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}+\left\lVert w_{0}-\widetilde{w}\right\rVert_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}\right),

where
C_{0}
is independent of
T
, and

\displaystyle G_{1}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}}|a_{x}^{X}|\left|p(v)-p(\widetilde{v}^{X})-\frac{u-\widetilde{u}^{X}}{2C_{*}}\right|^{2}\,dx,

\displaystyle G_{3}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}}|a^{X}_{x}||w-\widetilde{w}^{X}|^{2}\,dx,

\displaystyle G^{S}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\widetilde{v}_{x}^{X}||u-\widetilde{u}^{X}|^{2}\,dx,

\displaystyle D_{u_{1}}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}}|(u-\widetilde{u}^{X})_{x}|^{2}\,dx,\quad D_{u_{2}}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}}|(u-\widetilde{u}^{X})_{xx}|^{2}\,dx,

\displaystyle G_{w}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}}|w-\widetilde{w}^{X}|^{2}\,dx,\quad G_{w_{1}}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}}|(w-\widetilde{w}^{X})_{x}|^{2}\,dx,\quad G_{w_{2}}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}}|(w-\widetilde{w}^{X})_{xx}|^{2}\,dx.

Here,
C_{*}
is a positive constant defined in (
4.9
).

Remark 3.1
.

By the small perturbation of
v
in

, and the definition of
w
-variable,
w
is equivalent to the derivative of
v
. Therefore, the estimate (
3.3
) is equivalent to the following another formulation for the a priori estimate:

(3.4)

\displaystyle

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left(\left\lVert v-\widetilde{v}^{X}\right\rVert_{H^{2}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}+\left\lVert u-\widetilde{u}^{X}\right\rVert_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}\right)+\delta_{S}\int_{0}^{t}|\dot{X}(s)|^{2}\,ds

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\left(G_{1}+G_{3}+G^{S}\right)\,ds+\int_{0}^{t}\left(D_{u_{1}}+D_{u_{2}}+G_{w}+G_{w_{1}}+G_{w_{2}}\right)\,ds

\displaystyle\leq C_{0}\left(\left\lVert v_{0}-\widetilde{v}\right\rVert_{H^{2}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}+\left\lVert u_{0}-\widetilde{u}\right\rVert_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}\right),

where
(v,u)
is the solution to the original NSK equations (
1.1
) and
(\widetilde{v},\widetilde{u})
is defined in (
1.7
)

3.4.
Global existence of perturbed solution

Using the a priori estimate (
3.3
) and the equivalent form (
3.4
), we can extend the local solution obtained from Proposition
3.1
to the global one by using the standard continuation argument. We first choose smooth functions
\underline{v}
and
\underline{u}
that satisfy

(3.5)

\sum_{\pm}\left(\|\underline{v}-v_{\pm}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{\pm})}+\|\underline{u}-u_{\pm}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{\pm})}\right)+\|\partial_{x}\underline{v}\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R})}+\|\partial_{x}\underline{u}\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R})}\leq C\delta_{S}.

Then, we use the estimates on the shock wave (
2.1
) to obtain

(3.6)

\displaystyle

\displaystyle\left\lVert\underline{v}-\widetilde{v}^{X}\right\rVert_{H^{2}(\mathbb{R})}+\left\lVert\underline{u}-\widetilde{u}^{X}\right\rVert_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R})}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\pm}\left(\left\lVert\underline{v}-v_{\pm}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{\pm})}+\left\lVert\underline{u}-u_{\pm}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{\pm})}\right)+\|\widetilde{v}^{X}-v_{+}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}+\|\widetilde{v}^{X}-v_{-}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{-})}

\displaystyle+\|\partial_{x}\underline{v}\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R})}+\|\widetilde{v}^{X}_{x}\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R})}+\|\widetilde{u}^{X}-u_{+}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}+\|\widetilde{u}^{X}-u_{-}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{-})}+\|\partial_{x}\underline{u}\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R})}+\|\widetilde{u}^{X}_{x}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}

\displaystyle\leq C\sqrt{\delta_{S}}

Now, for sufficiently small
\delta_{S}
we choose
\varepsilon_{0}
as

\varepsilon_{0}<\frac{\varepsilon_{1}}{3}-C\sqrt{\delta_{S}}.

Consider any initial data
(v_{0},u_{0})
such that

\sum_{\pm}\left(\|v_{0}-v_{\pm}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{\pm})}+\|u_{0}-u_{\pm}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{\pm})}\right)+\|v_{0x}\|_{H^{1}}+\|u_{0x}\|_{L^{2}}<\varepsilon_{0}.

Then, we use (
3.5
) to obtain

\displaystyle\|v_{0}-\underline{v}\|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{R})}+\|u_{0}-\underline{u}\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R})}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\pm}\left(\|v_{0}-v_{\pm}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{\pm})}+\|u_{0}-u_{\pm}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{\pm})}+\|\underline{v}-v_{\pm}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{\pm})}+\|\underline{u}-u_{\pm}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{\pm})}\right)

\displaystyle\quad+\|v_{0x}\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R})}+\|u_{0x}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}+\|\underline{v}_{x}\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R})}+\|\underline{u}_{x}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon_{0}+C\sqrt{\delta_{S}}<\frac{\varepsilon_{1}}{3}.

From the smallness of
\varepsilon_{1}
and Sobolev embedding, we have

\frac{v_{-}}{2}\leq v_{0}(x)\leq 2v_{+},\quad x\in\mathbb{R}

and by the local existence result in Proposition
3.1
, there exists
T_{0}>0
such that

(3.7)

\|v-\underline{v}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T_{0};H^{2}(\mathbb{R}))}+\|u-\underline{u}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T_{0};H^{1}(\mathbb{R}))}\leq\frac{\varepsilon_{1}}{2}

and

\frac{v_{-}}{3}\leq v(t,x)\leq 3v_{+},\quad(t,x)\in[0,T_{0}]\times\mathbb{R}.

On the other hand, we estimate the difference between
(\underline{v},\underline{u})
and
(\widetilde{v}^{X},\widetilde{u}^{X})
by using similar estimate as in (
3.6
) and (
2.1
) as

\displaystyle\|\underline{v}-\widetilde{v}^{X}(t,\cdot)\|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{R})}+\|\underline{u}-\widetilde{u}^{X}(t,\cdot)\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R})}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\pm}\left(\|\underline{v}-v_{\pm}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{\pm})}+\|\underline{u}-u_{\pm}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{\pm})}+\|\widetilde{v}^{X}-v_{\pm}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{\pm})}+\|\widetilde{u}^{X}-u_{\pm}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{\pm})}\right)

\displaystyle\quad+\|\widetilde{v}^{X}_{x}\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R})}+\|\widetilde{u}^{X}_{x}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}+\|\underline{v}_{x}\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R})}+\|\underline{u}_{x}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}

\displaystyle\leq C\sqrt{\delta_{S}}(1+\sqrt{|X(t)|})\leq C\sqrt{\delta_{S}}(1+\sqrt{t}).

Taking
T_{1}\in(0,T_{0})
small enough so that
C\sqrt{\delta_{S}}(1+\sqrt{T_{1}})\leq\frac{\varepsilon_{1}}{2}
, we have

(3.8)

\|\underline{v}-\widetilde{v}^{X}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T_{1};H^{2}(\mathbb{R}))}+\|\underline{u}-\widetilde{u}^{X}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T_{1};H^{1}(\mathbb{R}))}\leq\frac{\varepsilon_{1}}{2}.

Combining (
3.7
) and (
3.8
) yields

\|v-\widetilde{v}^{X}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T_{1};H^{2}(\mathbb{R}))}+\|u-\widetilde{u}^{X}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T_{1};H^{1}(\mathbb{R}))}\leq\varepsilon_{1}.

Then, the a priori estimate (
3.4
) implies that
T_{1}
can be extended to
+\infty
, and the global existence is proved. In particular, we have

(3.9)

\displaystyle

\displaystyle\sup_{t>0}\left(\left\lVert v-\widetilde{v}^{X}\right\rVert_{H^{2}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}+\left\lVert u-\widetilde{u}^{X}\right\rVert_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}\right)+\delta_{S}\int_{0}^{\infty}|\dot{X}(t)|^{2}\,dt

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(G_{1}+G_{3}+G^{S}\right)\,dt+\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(D_{u_{1}}+D_{u_{2}}+G_{w}+G_{w_{1}}+G_{w_{2}}\right)\,dt

\displaystyle\leq C_{0}\left(\left\lVert v_{0}-\widetilde{v}\right\rVert_{H^{2}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}+\left\lVert u_{0}-\widetilde{u}\right\rVert_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}\right)<\infty

and, for
t>0
,

(3.10)

|\dot{X}(t)|\leq C_{0}\left(\|(v-\widetilde{v}^{X})(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}+\|(u-\widetilde{u}^{X})(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}\right).

3.5.
Time-asymptotic behavior

We are now ready to prove the time-asymptotic behavior of the perturbation. We first define

g(t):=\|(v-\widetilde{v}^{X})_{x}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}+\|(u-\widetilde{u}^{X})_{x}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}.

We will show that
g\in W^{1,1}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
which implies
\lim_{t\to\infty}g(t)=0
. Then, the Gagliardo-Nirenberg interpolation inequality and the uniform bound estimate (
3.9
) implies

(3.11)

\lim_{t\to\infty}\left(\|v-\widetilde{v}^{X}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}+\|u-\widetilde{u}^{X}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}\right)=0.

Furthermore, (
3.10
) and (
3.11
) imply that

\lim_{t\to\infty}|\dot{X}(t)|\leq C_{0}\lim_{t\to\infty}\left(\left\lVert(v-\widetilde{v}^{X})(t,\cdot)\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}+\left\lVert(u-\widetilde{u}^{X})(t,\cdot)\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}\right)=0.

Therefore, it remains to show that
g\in W^{1,1}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
.

(1)
g\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
: We use the definition of
w
variable to observe that

\displaystyle|(v-\widetilde{v}^{X})_{x}|

\displaystyle=|wv^{5/2}-\widetilde{w}^{X}(\widetilde{v}^{X})^{5/2}|\leq v^{5/2}|w-\widetilde{w}^{X}|+|\widetilde{w}^{X}||v^{5/2}-(\widetilde{v}^{X})^{5/2}|

\displaystyle\leq C|w-\widetilde{w}^{X}|+C|\widetilde{w}^{X}||v-\widetilde{v}^{X}|\leq C|w-\widetilde{w}^{X}|+C|\widetilde{v}_{x}^{X}||v-\widetilde{v}^{X}|

\displaystyle\leq C|w-\widetilde{w}^{X}|+C|\widetilde{v}_{x}^{X}||p(v)-p(\widetilde{v}^{X})|

\displaystyle\leq C|w-\widetilde{w}^{X}|+C|\widetilde{v}_{x}^{X}|\left|p(v)-p(\widetilde{v}^{X})-\frac{u-\widetilde{u}^{X}}{2C^{*}}\right|+C|\widetilde{v}_{x}^{X}||u-\widetilde{u}^{X}|.

Therefore, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}|g(t)|\,dt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|(v-\widetilde{v}^{X})_{x}|^{2}+|(u-\widetilde{u}^{X})_{x}|^{2}\,dxdt

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|w-\widetilde{w}^{X}|^{2}+|\widetilde{v}_{x}^{X}|^{2}\left|p(v)-p(\widetilde{v}^{X})-\frac{u-\widetilde{u}^{X}}{2C^{*}}\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt+|\widetilde{v}^{X}_{x}|^{2}|u-\widetilde{u}^{X}|^{2}+|(u-\widetilde{u}^{X})_{x}|^{2}\,dxdt

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{0}^{\infty}(G_{w}+G_{1}+G^{S}+D_{u_{1}})\,dt<\infty,

where we used (
3.9
) in the last inequality. This implies
g\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
.

(2)
g^{\prime}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
: We combine the system (
2.6
) and (
2.7
) to obtain

(3.12)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &(v-\widetilde{v}^{X})_{t}-(u-\widetilde{u}^{X})_{x}=\dot{X}(t)\widetilde{v}_{x}^{X},\\
&(u-\widetilde{u}^{X})_{t}+(p(v)-p(\widetilde{v}^{X}))_{x}=\left(\frac{u_{x}}{v}-\frac{\widetilde{u}_{x}^{X}}{\widetilde{v}^{X}}\right)_{x}+\left(\frac{w_{x}}{v^{5/2}}-\frac{\widetilde{w}_{x}^{X}}{(\widetilde{v}^{X})^{5/2}}\right)_{x}+\dot{X}(t)\widetilde{u}_{x}^{X},\\
&(w-\widetilde{w}^{X})_{t}=-\left(\frac{u_{x}}{v^{5/2}}-\frac{\widetilde{u}_{x}^{X}}{(\widetilde{v}^{X})^{5/2}}\right)_{x}+\dot{X}(t)\widetilde{w}_{x}^{X}.\end{aligned}

Then, using the equation (
3.12
), we estimate the time-integration of
g^{\prime}
as

(3.13)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}

\displaystyle|g^{\prime}(t)|\,dt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}2\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}}(v-\widetilde{v}^{X})_{x}(v-\widetilde{v}^{X})_{xt}\,dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}}(u-\widetilde{u}^{X})_{x}(u-\widetilde{u}^{X})_{xt}\,dx\right|\,dt

\displaystyle\leq 2\int_{0}^{\infty}\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}}(v-\widetilde{v}^{X})_{x}\left((u-\widetilde{u}^{X})_{xx}+\dot{X}(t)\widetilde{v}^{X}_{xx}\right)\,dx\right|\,dt

\displaystyle+2\int_{0}^{\infty}\Bigg|\int_{\mathbb{R}}(u-\widetilde{u}^{X})_{x}\Bigg(-(p(v)-p(\widetilde{v}^{X}))_{xx}+\left(\frac{u_{x}}{v}-\frac{\widetilde{u}_{x}^{X}}{\widetilde{v}^{X}}\right)_{xx}

\displaystyle+\left(\frac{w_{x}}{v^{5/2}}-\frac{\widetilde{w}_{x}^{X}}{(\widetilde{v}^{X})^{5/2}}\right)_{xx}+\dot{X}(t)\widetilde{u}_{xx}^{X}\Bigg)\Bigg|\,dt

\displaystyle=2\int_{0}^{\infty}\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}}(v-\widetilde{v}^{X})_{x}\left((u-\widetilde{u}^{X})_{xx}+\dot{X}(t)\widetilde{v}^{X}_{xx}\right)\,dx\right|\,dt

\displaystyle+2\int_{0}^{\infty}\Bigg|\int_{\mathbb{R}}(u-\widetilde{u}^{X})_{xx}\Bigg((p(v)-p(\widetilde{v}^{X}))_{x}-\left(\frac{u_{x}}{v}-\frac{\widetilde{u}_{x}^{X}}{\widetilde{v}^{X}}\right)_{x}-\left(\frac{w_{x}}{v^{5/2}}-\frac{\widetilde{w}_{x}^{X}}{(\widetilde{v}^{X})^{5/2}}\right)_{x}\Bigg)

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathbb{R}}(u-\widetilde{u}^{X})_{x}\dot{X}(t)\widetilde{u}^{X}_{xx}\Bigg|\,dt

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{0}^{\infty}(G_{w}+G_{1}+G^{S}+D_{u_{1}}+D_{u_{2}}+|\dot{X}(t)|^{2})\,dt

\displaystyle+C\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left|\left(\frac{u_{x}}{v}-\frac{\widetilde{u}_{x}^{X}}{\widetilde{v}^{X}}\right)_{x}\right|^{2}+\left|\left(\frac{w_{x}}{v^{5/2}}-\frac{\widetilde{w}_{x}^{X}}{(\widetilde{v}^{X})^{5/2}}\right)_{x}\right|^{2}\,dxdt.

Since the first term in the right-hand side of (
3.13
) can be bounded by (
3.9
), we only need to estimate the last two terms. The first one of the last two terms can be estimated as in the usual Navier-Stokes equations. However, as we obtain a uniform
H^{2}
-norm of
v
perturbation, we can directly control the
L^{\infty}
-norm of
(v-\widetilde{v}^{X})_{x}
, which makes the estimate simpler. Precisely, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left|\left(\frac{u_{x}}{v}-\frac{\widetilde{u}^{X}_{x}}{\widetilde{v}^{X}}\right)_{x}\right|^{2}\,dxdt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Bigg|\frac{1}{v}(u-\widetilde{u}^{X})_{xx}+\widetilde{u}^{X}_{xx}\left(\frac{1}{v}-\frac{1}{\widetilde{v}^{X}}\right)-\frac{1}{v^{2}}(v-\widetilde{v}^{X})_{x}(u-\widetilde{u}^{X})_{x}

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt-\frac{\widetilde{v}^{X}_{x}}{v^{2}}(u-\widetilde{u}^{X})_{x}-\frac{\widetilde{u}^{X}_{x}}{v^{2}}(v-\widetilde{v}^{X})_{x}-\widetilde{v}^{X}_{x}\widetilde{u}^{X}_{x}\left(\frac{1}{v^{2}}-\frac{1}{(\widetilde{v}^{X})^{2}}\right)\Bigg|^{2}\,dxdt

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Bigg(|(u-\widetilde{u}^{X})_{xx}|^{2}+|\widetilde{u}_{x}^{X}|^{2}|v-\widetilde{v}^{X}|^{2}+|(u-\widetilde{u}^{X})_{x}|^{2}|(v-\widetilde{v}^{X})_{x}|^{2}

\displaystyle\hskip 85.35826pt+|\widetilde{v}_{x}^{X}|^{2}|(u-\widetilde{u}^{X})_{x}|^{2}+|\widetilde{u}_{x}^{X}|^{2}|(v-\widetilde{v}^{X})_{x}|^{2}+|\widetilde{v}^{X}_{x}|^{2}|\widetilde{u}^{X}_{x}|^{2}|v-\widetilde{v}^{X}|^{2}\Bigg)\,dxdt,

and consequently,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left|\left(\frac{u_{x}}{v}-\frac{\widetilde{u}^{X}_{x}}{\widetilde{v}^{X}}\right)_{x}\right|^{2}\,dxdt

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(G_{w}+G_{1}+G^{S}+D_{u_{1}}+D_{u_{2}}\right)\,dt

\displaystyle\quad+C\|(v-\widetilde{v}^{X})_{x}\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}((0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R})}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|(u-\widetilde{u}^{X})_{x}|^{2}\,dxdt

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(G_{w}+G_{1}+G^{S}+D_{u_{1}}+D_{u_{2}}\right)\,dt+C\int_{0}^{\infty}D_{u_{1}}\,dt<+\infty.

Finally, we estimate the last term as

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left|\left(\frac{w_{x}}{v^{5/2}}-\frac{\widetilde{w}^{X}_{x}}{(\widetilde{v}^{X})^{5/2}}\right)_{x}\right|^{2}\,dxdt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Bigg|\frac{(w-\widetilde{w}^{X})_{xx}}{v^{5/2}}+\widetilde{w}^{X}_{xx}\left(\frac{1}{v^{5/2}}-\frac{1}{(\widetilde{v}^{X})^{5/2}}\right)-\frac{5}{2}\frac{1}{v^{7/2}}(v-\widetilde{v}^{X})_{x}(w-\widetilde{w}^{X})_{x}

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt-\frac{5}{2}\frac{\widetilde{v}^{X}_{x}}{v^{7/2}}(w-\widetilde{w}^{X})_{x}-\frac{5}{2}\frac{\widetilde{w}^{X}_{x}}{v^{7/2}}(v-\widetilde{v}^{X})_{x}-\frac{5}{2}\widetilde{v}^{X}_{x}\widetilde{w}^{X}_{x}\left(\frac{1}{v^{7/2}}-\frac{1}{(\widetilde{v}^{X})^{7/2}}\right)\Bigg|^{2}\,dxdt

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Bigg(|(w-\widetilde{w}^{X})_{xx}|^{2}+|\widetilde{w}^{X}_{xx}|^{2}|v-\widetilde{v}^{X}|^{2}+|(v-\widetilde{v}^{X})_{x}|^{2}|(w-\widetilde{w}^{X})_{x}|^{2}

\displaystyle\hskip 85.35826pt+|\widetilde{w}^{X}_{x}|^{2}|v-\widetilde{v}^{X}|^{2}+|\widetilde{v}^{X}_{x}|^{2}|(w-\widetilde{w}^{X})_{x}|^{2}+|\widetilde{v}^{X}_{x}|^{2}|\widetilde{w}^{X}_{x}|^{2}|v-\widetilde{v}^{X}|^{2}\Bigg)\,dxdt.

However, using the definition of
w
variable
\widetilde{w}=-\frac{\widetilde{v}_{x}}{\widetilde{v}^{5/2}}
, we derive

\widetilde{w}_{x}=-\frac{\widetilde{v}_{xx}}{\widetilde{v}^{5/2}}+\frac{5}{2}\frac{(\widetilde{v}_{x})^{2}}{\widetilde{v}^{7/2}},\quad\widetilde{w}_{xx}=-\frac{\widetilde{v}_{xxx}}{\widetilde{v}^{5/2}}+\frac{15}{2}\frac{\widetilde{v}_{x}\widetilde{v}_{xx}}{\widetilde{v}^{7/2}}-\frac{35}{4}\frac{(\widetilde{v}_{x})^{2}}{\widetilde{v}^{9/2}},

which, together with (
2.1
), implies

|\widetilde{w}_{x}|\leq C|\widetilde{v}_{x}|,\quad|\widetilde{w}_{xx}|\leq C|\widetilde{v}_{x}|.

Therefore, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left|\left(\frac{w_{x}}{v^{5/2}}-\frac{\widetilde{w}^{X}_{x}}{(\widetilde{v}^{X})^{5/2}}\right)_{x}\right|^{2}\,dxdt

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(G_{1}+G^{S}+D_{w_{1}}+D_{w_{2}}\right)\,dt+C\int_{0}^{\infty}|(v-\widetilde{v}^{X})_{x}|^{2}|(w-\widetilde{w}^{X})_{x}|^{2}\,dxdt

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(G_{1}+G^{S}+D_{w_{1}}+D_{w_{2}}\right)\,dt+C\|v-\widetilde{v}^{X}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,\infty;H^{2}(\mathbb{R}))}\int_{0}^{\infty}D_{w_{1}}\,dt<+\infty.

which proves
g^{\prime}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
. Thus, we have shown that
g\in W^{1,1}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
. This completes the proof of the asymptotic behavior of the NSK equations. Therefore, once we have the a priori estimate in Proposition
3.3
, we prove the time asymptotic behavior of the NSK equations.

Lemma 4.1

There exists a positive constant

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{5}(U)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}a^{X}\partial_{x}(u-\widetilde{u}^{X})\frac{(v^{5/2}-(\widetilde{v}^{X})^{5/2})\partial_{x}\widetilde{w}^{X}}{v^{5/2}(\widetilde{v}^{X})^{5/2}}\,dx,

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{6}(U)=-\int_{\mathbb{R}}a^{X}\partial_{x}(w-\widetilde{w}^{X})\frac{(v^{5/2}-(\widetilde{v}^{X})^{5/2})\partial_{x}\widetilde{u}^{X}}{v^{5/2}(\widetilde{v}^{X})^{5/2}}\,dx.

We note that the terms
\mathcal{K}_{i}(U)
are the terms from the Korteweg force, compared to the classical NS equations. The decomposition of the good terms is also defined as follows:

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{1}(U):=C_{*}\int_{\mathbb{R}}a^{X}_{x}\left|p(v)-p(\widetilde{v}^{X})-\frac{u-\widetilde{u}^{X}}{2C_{*}}\right|^{2}\,dx,\quad\mathcal{G}_{2}(U):=\frac{\sigma}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}a^{X}_{x}|u-\widetilde{u}^{X}|^{2}\,dx,

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{3}(U):=\frac{\sigma}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}a^{X}_{x}|w-\widetilde{w}^{X}|^{2}\,dx,\quad\mathcal{D}(U):=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{a^{X}}{v}|\partial_{x}(u-\widetilde{u}^{X})|^{2}\,dx.

On the other hand, since
Y(U)
is expanded as

\displaystyle Y(U)

\displaystyle=-\int_{\mathbb{R}}a^{X}_{x}\eta(U|\widetilde{U}^{X})\,dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}}a^{X}D^{2}\eta(\widetilde{U}^{X})(\widetilde{U})^{X}_{x}(U-\widetilde{U}^{X})\,dx

\displaystyle=-\int_{\mathbb{R}}a^{X}_{x}\left(\frac{|u-\widetilde{u}^{X}|^{2}}{2}+\frac{|w-\widetilde{w}^{X}|^{2}}{2}+Q(v|\widetilde{v}^{X})\right)\,dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}}a^{X}\widetilde{u}^{X}_{x}(u-\widetilde{u}^{X})\,dx

\displaystyle\quad-\int_{\mathbb{R}}a^{X}p^{\prime}(\widetilde{v}^{X})\widetilde{v}^{X}_{x}(v-\widetilde{v}^{X})\,dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}}a^{X}\widetilde{w}^{X}_{x}(w-\widetilde{w}^{X})\,dx,

we decompose
Y
as

Y=\sum_{i=1}^{7}Y_{i},

where

\displaystyle Y_{1}

\displaystyle:=\int_{\mathbb{R}}a^{X}\widetilde{u}^{X}_{x}(u-\widetilde{u}^{X})\,dx,\quad Y_{2}:=\frac{1}{\sigma}\int_{\mathbb{R}}a^{X}p^{\prime}(\widetilde{v}^{X})\widetilde{v}^{X}_{x}(v-\widetilde{v}^{X})\,dx,

\displaystyle Y_{3}

\displaystyle:=-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}a^{X}_{x}\Big(u-\widetilde{u}^{X}-2C_{*}\big(p(v)-p(\widetilde{v}^{X})\big)\Big)\Big(u-\widetilde{u}^{X}+2C_{*}\big(p(v)-p(\widetilde{v}^{X})\big)\Big)\,dx,

\displaystyle Y_{4}

\displaystyle:=-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}a^{X}_{x}4C_{*}^{2}\big(p(v)-p(\widetilde{v}^{X})\big)^{2}\,dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}}a^{X}_{x}Q(v|\widetilde{v}^{X})dx,

\displaystyle Y_{5}

\displaystyle:=-\int_{\mathbb{R}}a^{X}p^{\prime}(\widetilde{v}^{X})\widetilde{v}^{X}_{x}\left(v-\widetilde{v}^{X}+\frac{2C_{*}}{\sigma}(p(v)-p(\widetilde{v}^{X}))\right)\,dx,

\displaystyle Y_{6}

\displaystyle:=\int a^{X}p^{\prime}(\widetilde{v}^{X})\widetilde{v}^{X}_{x}\frac{2C_{*}}{\sigma}\left(p(v)-p(\widetilde{v}^{X})-\frac{u-\widetilde{u}^{X}}{2C_{*}}\right)\,dx,

\displaystyle Y_{7}

\displaystyle:=-\int_{\mathbb{R}}a^{X}_{x}\frac{|w-\widetilde{w}^{X}|^{2}}{2}\,dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}}a^{X}\widetilde{w}^{X}_{x}(w-\widetilde{w}^{X})\,dx.

We now define a shift function
X(t)
so that it satisfies the following ODE:

(4.11)

\dot{X}=-\frac{M}{\delta_{S}}(Y_{1}+Y_{2}),\quad X(0)=0.

With this choice of shift
X
, the term
\dot{X}(t)Y(U)
in (
4.10
) can be written as

\dot{X}(t)Y(U)=-\frac{\delta_{S}}{M}|\dot{X}|^{2}+\dot{X}\sum_{i=3}^{7}Y_{i}.

In conclusion, we decompose the right-hand side of (
4.10
) as

(4.12)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{R}}a^{X}\eta(U|\widetilde{U}^{X})\,dx&=\underbrace{-\frac{\delta_{S}}{2M}|\dot{X}|^{2}+\mathcal{B}_{1}-\mathcal{G}_{2}-\frac{3}{4}\mathcal{D}}_{=:\mathcal{R}_{1}}\\
&\quad\underbrace{-\frac{\delta_{S}}{2M}|\dot{X}|^{2}+\dot{X}\sum_{i=3}^{7}Y_{i}+\sum_{i=2}^{6}\mathcal{B}_{i}+\sum_{i=1}^{6}\mathcal{K}_{i}-\mathcal{G}_{1}-\mathcal{G}_{3}-\frac{1}{4}\mathcal{D}}_{=:\mathcal{R}_{2}}.\end{aligned}

In the following subsections, we estimate the terms in
\mathcal{R}_{1}
and
\mathcal{R}_{2}
respectively.

4.4.
Estimate of
\mathcal{R}_{1}

Estimation of
\mathcal{R}_{1}
is the most important part in the proof of Lemma
4.1
, in which the Poincaré-type inequality is crucially used. For a fixed
t\geq 0
, we define an auxiliary variable
y
as

y:=\frac{u_{-}-\widetilde{u}(x-\sigma t-X(t))}{\delta_{S}}.

Then it follows from the definition that the map
x\mapsto y=y(x)
is one-to-one and

\frac{dy}{dx}=-\frac{1}{\delta_{S}}\widetilde{u}^{X}_{x}>0,\quad\mbox{and}\quad\lim_{x\to-\infty}y=0,\quad\lim_{x\to\infty}y=1.

Using the new variable
y
, we will apply the following Poincarè-type inequality:

Lemma 4.4
.

[
22
, Lemma 2.9]
For any
f:[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}
with
\int_{0}^{1}y(1-y)|f^{\prime}|^{2}\,dy<\infty
,

\int_{0}^{1}\left|f-\int_{0}^{1}f\,dy\right|^{2}dy\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{1}y(1-y)|f^{\prime}|^{2}\,dy.

We apply Lemma
4.4
to the perturbation
f
of the following form:

f:=\left(u(t,\cdot)-\widetilde{u}(\cdot-\sigma t-X(t)\right)\circ y^{-1},

that is
f(y)=u(t,x)-\widetilde{u}(x-\sigma t-X(t))
. Therefore, the goal of this subsection is to represent
\mathcal{R}_{1}
in terms of
f
and then use Lemma
4.4
to estimate it. In the following, we estimate the terms in
\mathcal{R}_{1}
separately.

\bullet
(Estimate of
\frac{\delta_{S}}{2M}|\dot{X}|^{2}
):
We use the definition of
Y_{1}
and change of variables for
y
to observe that

Y_{1}=\int_{\mathbb{R}}a^{X}\widetilde{u}_{x}^{X}(u-\widetilde{u}^{X})\,dx=-\delta_{S}\int_{0}^{1}a^{X}f\,dy.

Then, using (
2.3
) and
\|a^{X}-1\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R})}\leq\sqrt{\delta_{S}}
, we have

\left|Y_{1}+\delta_{S}\int_{0}^{1}f\,dy\right|\leq\delta_{S}\int_{0}^{1}|a^{X}-1||f|dy\leq\delta_{S}^{3/2}\int_{0}^{1}|f|\,dy.

To estimate
Y_{2}
, we first use the relation
\sigma\widetilde{v}^{X}_{x}=-\widetilde{u}^{X}_{x}
and change of variables for
y
to yield

Y_{2}=-\frac{1}{\sigma^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}a^{X}p^{\prime}(\widetilde{v}^{X})\widetilde{u}^{X}_{x}(u-\widetilde{u}^{X})\,dx=\frac{\delta_{S}}{\sigma^{2}}\int_{0}^{1}a^{X}p^{\prime}(\widetilde{v}^{X})f\,dy.

This, together with the estimates (
2.3
), (
2.4
) and
\|a^{X}-1\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R})}\leq\sqrt{\delta_{S}}
, implies

\displaystyle\left|Y_{2}+\delta_{S}\int_{0}^{1}f\,dy\right|\leq\delta_{S}\int_{0}^{1}\left|\frac{a^{X}p^{\prime}(\widetilde{v}^{X})}{\sigma^{2}}+1\right||f|dy\leq C\delta_{S}(\sqrt{\delta_{S}}+\delta_{S})\int_{0}^{1}|f|\,dy.

Since
\dot{X}=-\frac{M}{\delta_{S}}(Y_{1}+Y_{2})
, we combine the estimates for
Y_{1}
and
Y_{2}
to obtain

\displaystyle\left|\dot{X}-2M\int_{0}^{1}f\,dy\right|\leq\frac{M}{\delta_{S}}\left(\left|Y_{1}+\delta_{S}\int_{0}^{1}f\,dy\right|+\left|Y_{2}+\delta_{S}\int_{0}^{1}f\,dy\right|\right)\leq C(\sqrt{\delta_{S}}+\delta_{S})\int_{0}^{1}|f|\,dy,

which implies

\displaystyle\left(\left|2M\int_{0}^{1}f\,dy\right|-|\dot{X}|\right)^{2}\leq C(\sqrt{\delta_{S}}+\delta_{S})^{2}\int_{0}^{1}|f|^{2}\,dy\leq C\delta_{S}\int_{0}^{1}|f|^{2}\,dy.

We use an elementary inequality
\frac{p^{2}}{2}-q^{2}\leq(p-q)^{2}
for
p,q\in\mathbb{R}
to obtain

\displaystyle 2M^{2}\left(\int_{0}^{1}f\,dy\right)^{2}-|\dot{X}|^{2}\leq C\delta_{S}\int_{0}^{1}|f|^{2}\,dy,

and by rearranging the terms, we obtain

(4.13)

-\frac{\delta_{S}}{2M}|\dot{X}|^{2}\leq-M\delta_{S}\left(\int_{0}^{1}f\,dy\right)^{2}+C\delta_{S}\int_{0}^{1}|f|^{2}\,dy.

\bullet
(Estimates of
\mathcal{B}_{1}
and
\mathcal{G}_{2}
):
Recall that
\mathcal{B}_{1}
and
\mathcal{G}_{2}
are

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{1}:=\frac{1}{4C_{*}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}a^{X}_{x}|u-\widetilde{u}^{X}|^{2}\,dx,

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{2}:=\frac{\sigma}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}a^{X}_{x}|u-\widetilde{u}^{X}|^{2}\,dx.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{1}-\mathcal{G}_{2}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{1}{4C_{*}}-\frac{\sigma}{2}\right)\int_{\mathbb{R}}a_{x}^{X}|u-\widetilde{u}^{X}|^{2}\,dx=-\left(\frac{1}{4C_{*}}-\frac{\sigma}{2}\right)\frac{1}{\sqrt{\delta_{S}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\widetilde{u}_{x}^{X}|u-\widetilde{u}^{X}|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\delta_{S}}\left(\frac{1}{4C_{*}}-\frac{\sigma}{2}\right)\int_{0}^{1}f^{2}\,dy.

where
C_{*}
defined in (
4.9
) can be written as

C_{*}=\frac{1}{2\sigma_{\ell}}-(\sqrt{\delta_{S}}+\delta_{S})\alpha_{\ell}\sigma_{\ell},

with simple notation
\alpha_{\ell}=\frac{\gamma+1}{2\gamma\sigma_{\ell}p(v_{-})}
in (
2.2
). On the other hand, using (
2.3
), (
2.4
), we obtain

\displaystyle\sqrt{\delta_{S}}\left(\frac{1}{4C_{*}}-\frac{\sigma}{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\sigma_{\ell}}{2}\frac{\sqrt{\delta_{S}}}{1-2(\sqrt{\delta_{S}}+\delta_{S})\sigma_{\ell}^{2}\alpha_{\ell}}-\frac{\sigma}{2}\sqrt{\delta_{S}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\sqrt{\delta_{S}}}{2}\left(\frac{1}{1-2(\sqrt{\delta_{S}}+\delta_{S})\sigma_{\ell}^{2}\alpha_{\ell}}(\sigma_{\ell}-\sigma)+\sigma\left(\frac{1}{1-2(\sqrt{\delta_{S}}+\delta_{S})\sigma_{\ell}^{2}\alpha_{\ell}}-1\right)\right)

\displaystyle\leq C\delta_{S}^{3/2}+\delta_{S}\sigma_{\ell}^{3}\alpha_{\ell}.

Therefore, we have

(4.14)

\mathcal{B}_{1}-\mathcal{G}_{2}\leq C\delta_{S}^{3/2}\int_{0}^{1}f^{2}\,dy+\sigma_{\ell}^{3}\alpha_{\ell}\delta_{S}\int_{0}^{1}f^{2}\,dy.

\bullet
(Estimate of
\mathcal{D}(U)
):
First, using
a\geq 1
and change of variables, we estimate the diffusion term
\mathcal{D}
in terms of
f
:

\mathcal{D}\geq\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{1}{v}|\partial_{x}(u-\widetilde{u}^{X})|^{2}dx=\int_{0}^{1}|\partial_{y}f|^{2}\frac{1}{v}\left(\frac{dy}{dx}\right)dy.

Similar to the NS system
[
26
, Lemma 4.5]
, there exists
C>0
such that the following estimate holds:

(4.15)

\left|\frac{1}{y(1-y)}\frac{1}{\widetilde{v}^{X}}\left(\frac{dy}{dx}\right)-\frac{\sigma}{2\sigma_{\ell}}\frac{\delta_{S}v^{\prime\prime}(p_{-})}{|v^{\prime}(p_{-})|^{2}}\right|\leq C\delta_{S}^{2}.

We present the proof of the estimate (
4.15
) in Appendix
A
. On the other hand, since
C^{-1}\leq v\leq C
, we have

(4.16)

\left|\frac{\widetilde{v}^{X}}{v}-1\right|\leq C\left|\widetilde{v}^{X}-v\right|\leq C\varepsilon_{1}.

Then, using (
4.15
) and (
4.16
), we obtain the lower bound for
\mathcal{D}
as

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}

\displaystyle\geq\int_{0}^{1}|\partial_{y}f|^{2}\frac{\widetilde{v}^{X}}{v}\frac{1}{\widetilde{v}^{X}}\left(\frac{dy}{dx}\right)dy

\displaystyle\geq(1-C\varepsilon_{1})\left(\frac{\sigma}{2\sigma_{\ell}}\frac{\delta_{S}v^{\prime\prime}(p_{-})}{|v^{\prime}(p_{-})|^{2}}-C\delta_{S}^{2}\right)\int_{0}^{1}y(1-y)\left|\partial_{y}f\right|^{2}dy.

Finally, since

\displaystyle\sigma_{\ell}^{3}\alpha_{\ell}=\frac{1}{2}(1+\gamma)\frac{1}{v_{-}}=\frac{1}{2}\frac{v^{\prime\prime}(p_{-})}{|v^{\prime}(p_{-})|^{2}},

we obtain

(4.17)

\mathcal{D}\geq\sigma_{\ell}^{3}\alpha_{\ell}\delta_{S}(1-C(\delta_{0}+\varepsilon_{1}))\int_{0}^{1}y(1-y)|\partial_{y}f|^{2}\,dy.

\bullet
(Estimate of
\mathcal{R}_{1}
): We now combine the estimates (
4.14
) and (
4.17
), we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{1}-\mathcal{G}_{2}-\frac{3}{4}\mathcal{D}\leq\sigma_{\ell}^{3}\alpha_{\ell}\delta_{S}\left((1+C\sqrt{\delta_{S}})\int_{0}^{1}f^{2}\,dy-\frac{3}{4}(1-C(\delta_{0}+\varepsilon_{1}))\int_{0}^{1}y(1-y)|\partial_{y}f|^{2}\,dy\right),

which together with the smallness of
\delta_{0},\varepsilon_{1}
yields

\mathcal{B}_{1}-\mathcal{G}_{2}-\frac{3}{4}\mathcal{D}\leq\sigma_{\ell}^{3}\alpha_{\ell}\delta_{S}\left(\frac{9}{8}\int_{0}^{1}f^{2}\,dy-\frac{5}{8}\int_{0}^{1}y(1-y)|\partial_{y}f|^{2}\,dy\right).

Then, using Lemma
4.4
with the identity

\int_{0}^{1}|f-\overline{f}|^{2}\,dy=\int_{0}^{1}f^{2}\,dy-\overline{f}^{2},\quad\overline{f}:=\int_{0}^{1}f\,dy,

we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{1}-\mathcal{G}_{2}-\frac{3}{4}\mathcal{D}\leq-\frac{\sigma_{\ell}^{3}\alpha_{\ell}\delta_{S}}{8}\int_{0}^{1}f^{2}\,dy+\frac{5\sigma_{\ell}^{3}\alpha_{\ell}\delta_{S}}{4}\left(\int_{0}^{1}f\,dy\right)^{2}.

Finally, using (
4.13
) with the choice
M=\dfrac{5\sigma_{\ell}^{3}\alpha_{\ell}}{4}
, we have

\displaystyle-\frac{\delta_{S}}{2M}|\dot{X}|^{2}+\mathcal{B}_{1}-\mathcal{G}_{2}-\frac{3}{4}\mathcal{D}\leq-\frac{\sigma_{\ell}^{3}\alpha_{\ell}\delta_{S}}{16}\int_{0}^{1}f^{2}\,dy,

which implies

(4.18)

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{1}\leq-C_{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\widetilde{v}^{X}_{x}||u-\widetilde{u}^{X}|^{2}\,dx=:-C_{1}G^{S}.

4.5.
Estimate of remaining terms

We now estimate the remaining terms in
\mathcal{R}_{2}
. We first substitute the estimate of
\mathcal{R}_{1}
(
4.18
) to (
4.12
) and use the Young’s inequality

\dot{X}\sum_{i=3}^{7}Y_{i}\leq\frac{\delta_{S}}{4M}|\dot{X}|^{2}+\frac{C}{\delta_{S}}\sum_{i=3}^{7}|Y_{i}|^{2}

to have

(4.19)

\displaystyle{}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{R}}a\eta(U|\widetilde{U})\,dx\leq-C_{1}G^{S}-\frac{\delta_{S}}{4M}|\dot{X}|^{2}+\frac{C}{\delta_{S}}\sum_{i=3}^{7}|Y_{i}|^{2}+\sum_{i=2}^{6}\mathcal{B}_{i}+\sum_{i=1}^{6}\mathcal{K}_{i}-\mathcal{G}_{1}-\mathcal{G}_{3}-\frac{1}{4}\mathcal{D}.

Therefore, to close the estimate of the weighted relative entropy, it suffices to control the remaining terms
|Y_{i}|^{2}
,
\mathcal{B}_{i}
, and
\mathcal{K}_{i}
. Below, we estimate each term separately.

\bullet
(Estimate of
\frac{C}{\delta_{S}}|Y_{i}|^{2}
for
i=3,\ldots,7
): We use Cauchy-Schwarz inequality to estimate
Y_{3}
as

\displaystyle|Y_{3}|^{2}

\displaystyle=\left|-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}a^{X}_{x}\Big(u-\widetilde{u}^{X}-2C_{*}\big(p(v)-p(\widetilde{v}^{X})\big)\Big)\Big(u-\widetilde{u}^{X}+2C_{*}\big(p(v)-p(\widetilde{v}^{X})\big)\Big)\,dx\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}a^{X}_{x}\left|p(v)-p(\widetilde{v}^{X})-\frac{u-\widetilde{u}^{X}}{2C_{*}}\right|^{2}\,dx\right)\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}a^{X}_{x}\left|p(v)-p(\widetilde{v}^{X})+\frac{u-\widetilde{u}^{X}}{2C_{*}}\right|^{2}\,dx\right)

\displaystyle\leq C\mathcal{G}_{1}\|a^{X}_{x}\|_{L^{\infty}}\left(\|u-\widetilde{u}^{X}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}))}^{2}+\|v-\widetilde{v}^{X}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}))}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C\delta^{3/2}_{S}\varepsilon_{1}^{2}\mathcal{G}_{1},

where we used
\|\widetilde{v}^{X}_{x}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C\delta_{S}^{2}
to obtain
\|a^{X}_{x}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{\delta_{S}}}\|\widetilde{v}^{X}_{x}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C\delta_{S}^{3/2}
. This yields

\frac{C}{\delta_{S}}|Y_{3}|^{2}\leq C\sqrt{\delta_{S}}\varepsilon_{1}^{2}\mathcal{G}_{1}.

On the other hand, using
Q(v|\widetilde{v}^{X})\leq C|p(v)-p(\widetilde{v}^{X})|
, we estimate
Y_{4}
as

\displaystyle|Y_{4}|

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{\mathbb{R}}|a_{x}^{X}||p(v)-p(\widetilde{v}^{X})|^{2}\,dx,

which implies

\displaystyle\frac{C}{\delta_{S}}|Y_{4}|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{\delta_{S}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}|a^{X}_{x}||p(v)-p(\widetilde{v}^{X})|^{2}\,dx\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{\delta_{S}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}|a^{X}_{x}|\left|p(v)-p(\widetilde{v}^{X})-\frac{u-\widetilde{u}^{X}}{2C_{*}}\right|^{2}\,dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}}|a^{X}_{x}||u-\widetilde{u}^{X}|^{2}\,dx\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{\delta_{S}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}|a^{X}_{x}|\left|p(v)-p(\widetilde{v}^{X})-\frac{u-\widetilde{u}^{X}}{2C_{*}}\right|^{2}\,dx\right)^{2}+\frac{C}{\delta_{S}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}|a^{X}_{x}||u-\widetilde{u}^{X}|^{2}\,dx\right)^{2}.

Then, using the estimates
\|a^{X}_{x}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}\leq C\delta^{3/2}_{S}
and
\|v-\widetilde{v}^{X}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}+\|u-\widetilde{u}^{X}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\leq C\varepsilon_{1}
, we derive the following estimate for
Y_{4}
:

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \frac{C}{\delta_{S}}|Y_{4}|^{2}&\leq\frac{C}{\delta_{S}}\|a^{X}_{x}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}\left(\|v-\widetilde{v}^{X}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}+\|u-\widetilde{u}^{X}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}\right)\int_{\mathbb{R}}|a^{X}_{x}|\left|p(v)-p(\widetilde{v}^{X})-\frac{u-\widetilde{u}^{X}}{2C_{*}}\right|^{2}\,dx\\
&\quad+\frac{C}{\delta_{S}^{3/2}}\|a^{X}_{x}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}\|u-\widetilde{u}^{X}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\widetilde{v}^{X}_{x}||u-\widetilde{u}^{X}|^{2}\,dx\\
&\leq C\sqrt{\delta_{S}}\varepsilon_{1}^{2}\mathcal{G}_{1}+C\varepsilon_{1}^{2}G^{S}.\end{aligned}

To estimate
Y_{5}
, we first note that

\displaystyle Y_{5}

\displaystyle=-\int_{\mathbb{R}}a^{X}p^{\prime}(\widetilde{v}^{X})\widetilde{v}^{X}_{x}\left(v-\widetilde{v}^{X}+\frac{p(v)-p(\widetilde{v}^{X})}{\sigma\sigma_{l}}-\frac{2}{\sigma}\alpha_{l}\sigma_{l}(\sqrt{\delta_{S}}+\delta_{S})(p(v)-p(\widetilde{v}^{X}))\right)\,dx.

Since
v=p(v)^{-\frac{1}{\gamma}}
, we use Taylor expansion of the map
z\mapsto z^{-\frac{1}{\gamma}}
to observe

\left|v-\widetilde{v}^{X}+\frac{p(v)-p(\widetilde{v}^{X})}{\gamma p^{1+\frac{1}{\gamma}}(\widetilde{v}^{X})}\right|\leq C|p(v)-p(\widetilde{v}^{X})|^{2}.

This, together with (
2.3
) and (
2.4
), implies

\left|v-\widetilde{v}^{X}+\frac{p(v)-p(\widetilde{v}^{X})}{\sigma\sigma_{l}}\right|\leq C(\varepsilon_{1}+\delta_{S})|p(v)-p(\widetilde{v}^{X})|.

Therefore, we estimate
Y_{5}
as

\displaystyle|Y_{5}|

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\widetilde{v}^{X}_{x}|\left|v-\widetilde{v}^{X}+\frac{p(v)-p(\widetilde{v}^{X})}{\sigma\sigma_{l}}\right|\,dx+\sqrt{\delta_{S}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\widetilde{v}^{X}_{x}||p(v)-p(\widetilde{v}^{X})|\,dx\right)

\displaystyle\leq C(\varepsilon_{1}+\delta_{S}+\sqrt{\delta_{S}})\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\widetilde{v}^{X}_{x}|\left|p(v)-p(\widetilde{v}^{X})\right|\,dx

\displaystyle\leq C(\varepsilon_{1}+\sqrt{\delta_{S}})\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\widetilde{v}^{X}_{x}|\left|p(v)-p(\widetilde{v}^{X})-\frac{u-\widetilde{u}^{X}}{2C_{\ast}}\right|\,dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\widetilde{v}^{X}_{x}|\left|u-\widetilde{u}^{X}\right|\,dx\right)

\displaystyle\leq C(\varepsilon_{1}+\sqrt{\delta_{S}})\sqrt{\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\widetilde{v}^{X}_{x}|dx}\left(\sqrt{\int_{\mathbb{R}}|a^{X}_{x}|\left|p(v)-p(\widetilde{v}^{X})-\frac{u-\widetilde{u}^{X}}{2C_{\ast}}\right|^{2}\,dx}+\sqrt{\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\widetilde{v}^{X}_{x}|\left|u-\widetilde{u}^{X}\right|^{2}\,dx}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C(\varepsilon_{1}+\sqrt{\delta_{S}})\sqrt{\delta_{S}}(\sqrt{\mathcal{G}_{1}}+\sqrt{G^{S}}),

which yields

\displaystyle\frac{C}{\delta_{S}}|Y_{5}|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C(\varepsilon_{1}+\sqrt{\delta_{S}})^{2}(\mathcal{G}_{1}+G^{S}).

For
Y_{6}
, we use Hölder inequality as

\displaystyle|Y_{6}|

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\widetilde{v}^{X}_{x}|\left|p(v)-p(\widetilde{v}^{X})-\frac{u-\widetilde{u}^{X}}{2C_{*}}\right|\,dx

\displaystyle\leq C\sqrt{\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\widetilde{v}^{X}_{x}|\,dx}\sqrt{\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\widetilde{v}^{X}_{x}|\left|p(v)-p(\widetilde{v}^{X})-\frac{u-\widetilde{u}^{X}}{2C_{*}}\right|^{2}\,dx},

which gives

\displaystyle\frac{C}{\delta_{S}}|Y_{6}|^{2}\leq\frac{C}{\delta_{S}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\widetilde{v}^{X}_{x}|\,dx\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\widetilde{v}^{X}_{x}|\left|p(v)-p(\widetilde{v}^{X})-\frac{u-\widetilde{u}^{X}}{2C_{*}}\right|^{2}\,dx\leq C\sqrt{\delta_{S}}\mathcal{G}_{1}.

Finally, for
Y_{7}
, we use the relations

|\widetilde{w}^{X}_{x}|\leq C\left(|\widetilde{v}^{X}_{xx}|+|\widetilde{v}^{X}_{x}|\right)\leq C|\widetilde{v}^{X}_{x}|,\quad|\widetilde{v}^{X}_{x}|\sim\sqrt{\delta_{S}}|a^{X}_{x}|

and Hölder’s inequality to obtain

\displaystyle|Y_{7}|

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}|a^{X}_{x}||w-\widetilde{w}^{X}|^{2}\,dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\widetilde{v}^{X}_{x}||w-\widetilde{w}^{X}|\,dx\right)

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}|a^{X}_{x}||w-\widetilde{w}^{X}|^{2}\,dx+\sqrt{\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\widetilde{v}^{X}_{x}|\,dx}\sqrt{\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\widetilde{v}^{X}_{x}||w-\widetilde{w}^{X}|^{2}\,dx}\,\right)

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}|a^{X}_{x}||w-\widetilde{w}^{X}|^{2}\,dx+\sqrt{\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\widetilde{v}^{X}_{x}|\,dx}\sqrt{\sqrt{\delta_{S}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|a^{X}_{x}||w-\widetilde{w}^{X}|^{2}\,dx}\,\right),

which gives

\displaystyle\frac{C}{\delta_{S}}|Y_{7}|^{2}\leq\frac{C}{\delta_{S}}\left(\|a^{X}_{x}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}\|w-\widetilde{w}^{X}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}+\delta_{S}^{3/2}\right)\mathcal{G}_{3}\leq C\sqrt{\delta_{S}}\mathcal{G}_{3},

where the last inequality, we used
\|a^{X}_{x}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}<C\delta_{S}^{\frac{3}{2}}
and
\|w-\widetilde{w}^{X}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}\leq C\|(v-\widetilde{v}^{X})_{x}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}\leq C\varepsilon_{1}^{2}
.

Combining all the estimates of
Y_{i}
with
i=3,4,\ldots,7
, and use the smallness of the parameters, we conclude
that

\displaystyle\frac{C}{\delta_{S}}\sum_{i=3}^{7}|Y_{i}|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C(\sqrt{\delta_{S}}+\varepsilon_{1}^{2})\mathcal{G}_{1}+C\sqrt{\delta_{S}}\mathcal{G}_{3}+C\left(\varepsilon_{1}^{2}+\delta_{S}^{2}\right)G^{S}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{100}(\mathcal{G}_{1}+\mathcal{G}_{3}+C_{1}G^{S}),

where the coefficient
\frac{1}{100}
is chosen as a small coefficient, but still order of
O(1)
.

\bullet
(Estimate of
\mathcal{B}_{i}(U)
for
i=2,3,4,5,6
): We estimate
\mathcal{B}_{2}
by using Young’s inequality,
\|a^{X}_{x}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}\leq C\delta^{3/2}_{S}
and

as

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{\mathbb{R}}|a_{x}^{X}||u-\widetilde{u}^{X}||(u-\widetilde{u}^{X})_{x}|\,dx,

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{100}\mathcal{D}+C\delta_{S}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\widetilde{v}^{X}_{x}||u-\widetilde{u}^{X}|^{2}\,dx\leq\frac{1}{100}(\mathcal{D}+C_{1}G^{S}).

\displaystyle\leq C\delta_{S}^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|a^{X}_{x}||u-\widetilde{u}^{X}||p(v)-p(\widetilde{v}^{X})|\,dx

\displaystyle\leq C\delta_{S}^{2}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}|a^{X}_{x}|\left|p(v)-p(\widetilde{v}^{X})-\frac{u-\widetilde{u}^{X}}{2C_{*}}\right|^{2}\,dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}}|a^{X}_{x}|\left|u-\widetilde{u}^{X}\right|^{2}\,dx\right)

\displaystyle\leq C\delta_{S}^{2}\mathcal{G}_{1}+C\delta^{3/2}_{S}G^{S}\leq\frac{1}{100}(\mathcal{G}_{1}+C_{1}G^{S}).

We use
|\widetilde{u}^{X}_{x}|\sim|\widetilde{v}^{X}_{x}|
, Young’s inequality,
\|\widetilde{v}^{X}_{x}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}\leq C\delta_{S}^{2}
, and
|a^{X}_{x}|\sim\frac{|\widetilde{v}^{X}_{x}|}{\sqrt{\delta_{S}}}

\displaystyle\leq C\delta_{S}(\mathcal{G}_{1}+G^{S}+\mathcal{D})\leq\frac{1}{100}\left(\mathcal{D}+C_{1}G^{S}+\mathcal{G}_{1}\right).

We estimate
\mathcal{B}_{5}
by using similar argument as before

\displaystyle|\mathcal{B}_{5}(U)|

\displaystyle\leq C\delta_{S}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|a_{x}^{X}|\left|p(v)-p(\widetilde{v}^{X})-\frac{u-\widetilde{u}^{X}}{2C_{\ast}}\right|^{2}\,dx+C\sqrt{\delta_{S}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\widetilde{v}_{x}^{X}||u-\widetilde{u}^{X}|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle|\mathcal{B}_{6}(U)|

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon_{1}\mathcal{G}_{1}+C\lVert u-\widetilde{u}^{X}\rVert_{L^{2}{(\mathbb{R})}}\lVert(u-\widetilde{u}^{X})_{x}\rVert_{L^{2}{(\mathbb{R})}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{|\widetilde{v}_{x}^{X}|}{\sqrt{\delta_{S}}}|u-\widetilde{u}^{X}|dx

\mathcal{K}_{4}

\displaystyle|\mathcal{K}_{4}(U)|

\displaystyle|\mathcal{K}_{6}(U)|

Proof of Lemma
4.1

We are now ready to prove the key lemma, Lemma
4.1
. We combine all the estimates in (
4.19
), (
4.20
), and (
4.21
) to derive the following control on the weighted relative entropy:

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{R}}a^{X}\eta(U|\widetilde{U}^{X})\,dx

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{\delta_{S}}{4M}|\dot{X}|^{2}-\frac{93}{100}\mathcal{G}_{1}-\frac{93}{100}\mathcal{G}_{3}

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{93}{100}C_{1}G^{S}-\frac{19}{100}\mathcal{D}+C\sqrt{\delta_{S}}\|(w-\widetilde{w}^{X})_{x}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}.

After integrating the above inequality on
[0,t]
for any

, we conclude that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}a^{X}(t,x)\eta(U(t,x)|\widetilde{U}^{X}(t,x))\,dx+\int_{0}^{t}\left(\delta_{S}|\dot{X}|^{2}+\mathcal{G}_{1}+\mathcal{G}_{3}+G^{S}+\mathcal{D}\right)\,ds

\displaystyle\quad\leq C\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}a(0,x)\eta(U_{0}(x)|\widetilde{U}(0,x))\,dx+\sqrt{\delta_{S}}\int_{0}^{t}\|(w-\widetilde{w}^{X})_{x}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}\right).

However, since the bounds
1/2\leq a\leq 2
,
D(U)\leq C\mathcal{D}(U)
,
G_{1}(U)\leq C\mathcal{G}_{1}(U)
,
G_{3}(U)\leq C\mathcal{G}_{3}(U)
, and the relation

\|U-\widetilde{U}^{X}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}\sim\int_{\mathbb{R}}\eta(U|\widetilde{U}^{X})\,dx,\quad\forall t\in[0,T]

holds, this completes the proof of Lemma
4.1
.

In this section, we provide the estimate on the

-perturbation, and complete the proof of Proposition
3.3
. To achieve this, in addition to the estimate of the
L^{2}
-perturbation between
(v,u,w)
and
(\widetilde{v},\widetilde{u},\widetilde{w})
obtained in the previous section, we need to derive higher-order estimates for the

-perturbation between

and

. Therefore, our goal of this section is to establish the following lemma.

Lemma 5.1
.

Under the hypotheses of Proposition
3.3
, there exists a positive constant
C
, independent of
\delta_{S},\varepsilon_{1},T
such that for all
t\in[0,T],

(5.1)

\displaystyle

\displaystyle\left\lVert(v-\widetilde{v})(t,\cdot)\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}+\left\lVert(u-\widetilde{u})(t,\cdot)\right\rVert_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}+\left\lVert(w-\widetilde{w})(t,\cdot)\right\rVert_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}+\delta_{S}\int_{0}^{t}|\dot{X}(s)|^{2}\,ds

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\left(G_{1}+G_{3}+G^{S}\right)\,ds+\int_{0}^{t}\left(D_{u_{1}}+D_{u_{2}}+G_{w}+D_{w_{1}}+D_{w_{2}}\right)\,ds

\displaystyle\leq C\left\lVert(v-\widetilde{v})(0,\cdot)\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}+\left\lVert(u-\widetilde{u})(0,\cdot)\right\rVert_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}+\left\lVert(w-\widetilde{w})(0,\cdot)\right\rVert_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R})}^{2},

or equivalently,

\displaystyle\left\lVert(v-\widetilde{v})(t,\cdot)\right\rVert_{H^{2}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}+\left\lVert(u-\widetilde{u})(t,\cdot)\right\rVert_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}+\delta_{S}\int_{0}^{t}|\dot{X}(s)|^{2}\,ds

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\left(G_{1}+G_{3}+G^{S}\right)\,ds+\int_{0}^{t}\left(D_{u_{1}}+D_{u_{2}}+G_{w}+G_{w_{1}}+G_{w_{2}}\right)\,ds

\displaystyle\leq C\left\lVert(v-\widetilde{v})(0,\cdot)\right\rVert_{H^{2}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}+\left\lVert(u-\widetilde{u})(0,\cdot)\right\rVert_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R})}^{2},

where

\displaystyle G_{1}

\displaystyle:=\int_{\mathbb{R}}|a_{x}|\left|p(v)-p(\widetilde{v})-\frac{u-\widetilde{u}}{2C_{*}}\right|^{2}\,dx,

\displaystyle G_{3}

\displaystyle:=\int_{\mathbb{R}}|a_{x}||w-\widetilde{w}|^{2}\,dx,

\displaystyle G^{S}

\displaystyle:=\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\widetilde{v}_{x}||u-\widetilde{u}|^{2}\,dx,

\displaystyle D_{u_{1}}

\displaystyle:=\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\partial_{x}(u-\widetilde{u})|^{2}\,dx,\quad D_{u_{2}}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\partial_{xx}(u-\widetilde{u})|^{2}\,dx,

\displaystyle G_{w}

\displaystyle:=\int_{\mathbb{R}}|(w-\widetilde{w})|^{2}\,dx,\quad D_{w_{1}}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\partial_{x}(w-\widetilde{w})|^{2}\,dx,\quad D_{w_{2}}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\partial_{xx}(w-\widetilde{w})|^{2}\,dx.

For simplicity, we introduce the following notations for the perturbation:

(5.2)

\phi(t,x):=v(t,x)-\widetilde{v}(t,x),\ \psi(t,x):=u(t,x)-\widetilde{u}(t,x),\ \omega(t,x):=w(t,x)-\widetilde{w}(t,x).

Then the triplet
(\phi,\psi,\omega)
satisfies

(5.3)

\displaystyle\phi_{t}-\psi_{x}=\dot{X}\widetilde{v}_{x},

\displaystyle\psi_{t}+\left(p(v)-p(\widetilde{v})\right)_{x}=\left(\frac{u_{x}}{v}-\frac{\widetilde{u}_{x}}{\widetilde{v}}\right)_{x}+\left(\frac{w_{x}}{v^{5/2}}-\frac{\widetilde{w}_{x}}{\widetilde{v}^{5/2}}\right)_{x}+\dot{X}\widetilde{u}_{x},

\displaystyle\omega_{t}=-\left(\frac{u_{x}}{v^{5/2}}-\frac{\widetilde{u}_{x}}{\widetilde{v}^{5/2}}\right)_{x}+\dot{X}\widetilde{w}_{x}.

For later reference, we will represent some of the nonlinear terms in the above systems (
5.3
) as follows:

(5.4)

\displaystyle\left(p(v)-p(\widetilde{v})\right)_{x}=p^{\prime}(v)\phi_{x}+\widetilde{v}_{x}\left(p^{\prime}(v)-p^{\prime}(\widetilde{v})\right),

\displaystyle\left(\frac{u_{x}}{v}-\frac{\widetilde{u}_{x}}{\widetilde{v}}\right)_{x}=\frac{\psi_{xx}}{v}-\frac{\psi_{x}v_{x}}{v^{2}}+\widetilde{u}_{xx}\left(\frac{1}{v}-\frac{1}{\widetilde{v}}\right)+\widetilde{u}_{x}\left(\frac{\widetilde{v}_{x}}{\widetilde{v}^{2}}-\frac{v_{x}}{v^{2}}\right),

\displaystyle\left(\frac{w_{x}}{v^{5/2}}-\frac{\widetilde{w}_{x}}{\widetilde{v}^{5/2}}\right)_{x}=v^{-5/2}\omega_{xx}-\frac{5}{2}v^{-7/2}v_{x}\omega_{x}

\displaystyle+\widetilde{w}_{xx}\left(v^{-5/2}-\widetilde{v}^{-5/2}\right)+\frac{5}{2}\widetilde{w}_{x}\left(\widetilde{v}^{-7/2}\widetilde{v}_{x}-v^{-7/2}v_{x}\right).

We note that the a priori assumptions (
3.2
) and the Sobolev inequality imply the control on the
L^{\infty}
-norms for the perturbations

(5.5)

\left\lVert\phi\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R})}+\left\lVert\phi_{x}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R})}+\left\lVert\psi\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R})}\leq C\varepsilon_{1}.

The following lemma will be used throughout this section.

Lemma 5.2
.

Under the hypotheses of Proposition
3.3
, there exists a positive constants
C
that is independent of
\delta_{S}
and
\varepsilon_{1}
, such that for all
t\in[0,T],
and for

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(|\widetilde{v}_{x}|+|\widetilde{u}_{x}|)^{1+p}\left|u-\widetilde{u}\right|^{2}\,dxds\leq C\delta_{S}^{2p}\int_{0}^{t}G^{S}\,ds,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(|\widetilde{v}_{x}|+|\widetilde{u}_{x}|)^{1+p}\left|v-\widetilde{v}\right|^{2}\,dxds\leq C\delta_{S}^{2p}\int_{0}^{t}\left(G_{1}+G^{S}\right)ds.

Proof.

It follows from (
2.1
) that
|\widetilde{v}_{x}|\sim|\widetilde{u}_{x}|
, and
\|\widetilde{v}_{x}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C\delta_{S}^{2}
, which implies

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(|\widetilde{v}_{x}|+|\widetilde{u}_{x}|)^{1+p}\left|u-\widetilde{u}\right|^{2}\,dxds\leq C\left\lVert\widetilde{v}_{x}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R})}^{p}\int_{0}^{t}G^{S}\,ds\leq C\delta_{S}^{2p}\int_{0}^{t}G^{S}\,ds.

Similarly, we use the equivalence between
|v-\widetilde{v}|
and
|p(v)-p(\widetilde{v})|
to obtain

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(|\widetilde{v}_{x}|+|\widetilde{u}_{x}|)^{1+p}\left|v-\widetilde{v}\right|^{2}\,dxds\leq C\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\widetilde{v}_{x}|^{1+p}\left|p(v)-p(\widetilde{v})\right|^{2}\,dxds

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\widetilde{v}_{x}|^{1+p}\left|p(v)-p(\widetilde{v})-\frac{u-\widetilde{u}}{2C_{*}}\right|^{2}\,dxds+C\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\widetilde{v}_{x}|^{1+p}\left|u-\widetilde{u}\right|^{2}\,dxds

\displaystyle\leq C\left\lVert\widetilde{v}_{x}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R})}^{p}\int_{0}^{t}(G_{1}+G^{S})\,ds\leq C\delta_{S}^{2p}\int_{0}^{t}(G_{1}+G^{S})\,ds.

∎

Introduction and main results

Consider the set
\{\phi_{i}\}_{i\geq 0}
of

-normalized Laplace-Beltrami eigenfunctions

For

We are interested in the following question: if

In

to deduce the exponential decay of

. However, it is unclear how the constants

depend on
\phi_{j}
,
\phi_{k}
and

Motivated by questions from number theory, Sarnak

proved the following result: if

later replaced the power loss with a logarithmic loss (see also
[

In the case of general compact real-analytic manifolds, Zelditch
[

proved that
c_{i}(\phi_{j}^{2})\leq Ce^{-R\lambda_{i}}
where
R
is strictly less than the maximal analytic Grauert tube radius, and

M
and
R

These results describe the behavior of

when
f
is a product of eigenfunctions as
i
goes to infinity, but they do not describe what happens for smaller values of
i
. For example, in the case of the flat torus
\mathbb{T}^{d}
,
c_{i}(\phi_{j}\phi_{k})=0
if
{\lambda_{i}}>{\lambda_{j}}+{\lambda_{k}}
. A natural question to ask is the following: for a general analytic manifold, what are the conditions on
i
,
j
, and

c_{i}(\phi_{j}\phi_{k})

In the case of smooth manifolds, some recent results indicate when

To the best of our knowledge, it is an open problem whether exponential decay holds for

1.1.
Main results

Our first main result shows quantitatively the exponential decay of
c_{i}\!\biggl(\prod\limits_{j\leq n}\phi_{k_{j}}\biggr)
in the real-analytic setting:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
(M,g)
be a real-analytic closed Riemannian manifold of dimension
d
. Let
\{\phi_{k}\}
be an orthonormal basis of Laplace-Beltrami eigenfunctions on
M
with eigenvalues
\lambda_{k}^{2}
in increasing order.

Let
n\geq 2
and consider a finite set of positive indices
\{{k_{1}},\dots,{k_{n}}\}
. There exist
c(M)
and
C(M)
such that the following holds: for any
i>0
,

(1)

\displaystyle\left|\int_{M}\phi_{i}\prod_{j\leq n}\phi_{k_{j}}\right|\leq e^{-c\lambda_{i}}\prod_{j\leq n}e^{C\lambda_{k_{j}}}\,.

If we only consider indices
i
large enough we get the following straightforward Corollary:

Corollary 1.2
.

Let
(M,g)
be a real-analytic closed Riemannian manifold of dimension
d
. Let
\{\phi_{k}\}
be an orthonormal basis of Laplace-Beltrami eigenfunctions on
M
with eigenvalues
\lambda_{k}^{2}
in increasing order. Let
n\geq 2
and consider a finite set of positive indices
\{{k_{1}},\dots,{k_{n}}\}
. There exists
C_{1}(M)
and
C_{2}(M)
such that if
\lambda_{i}\geq C_{1}\sum\limits_{j\leq n}\lambda_{k_{j}}
, then

(2)

\displaystyle\left|\int_{M}\phi_{i}\prod_{j\leq n}\phi_{k_{j}}\right|\leq e^{-C_{2}\lambda_{i}}\,.

The second result that we prove is a Lemma well-known to specialists: the
L^{2}
norm of a product of Laplace-Beltrami eigenfunctions cannot be too small.

Lemma 1.3
.

Let
(M,g)
be a smooth closed Riemannian manifold of dimension
d
. Let
\{\phi_{k}\}
be an orthonormal basis of Laplace-Beltrami eigenfunctions on
M
with eigenvalues
\lambda_{k}^{2}
in increasing order. Let
n\geq 2
and consider a finite set of positive integers
\{{k_{1}},\dots,{k_{n}}\}
. There exists
C_{3}(M)
and
C_{4}(M,n)
such that:

(3)

\left\|\prod\limits_{j\leq n}\phi_{k_{j}}\right\|_{L^{2}(M)}\geq C_{3}\prod_{j\leq n}e^{-C_{4}\lambda_{k_{j}}}\,.

Remark 1.4
.

We note that on
\mathbb{S}^{2}
, the
L^{2}
norm of the product of
\Re(x+iy)^{k}
,
\Re(x+iz)^{k}
and
\Re(y+iz)^{k}
decays exponentially fast in
k
.

In our last result, we show, by combining Corollary
1.2
and Lemma
1.3
, that one can recover most of the
L^{2}
mass of a product of Laplace-Beltrami eigenfunctions using only finitely many terms in the Fourier expansion:

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
(M,g)
be a real-analytic closed Riemannian manifold of dimension
d
. Let
\{\phi_{k}\}
be an orthonormal basis of Laplace-Beltrami eigenfunctions on
M
with eigenvalues
\lambda_{k}^{2}
in increasing order. Let
n\geq 2
, consider a finite set of positive integers
\{{k_{1}},\dots,{k_{n}}\}
. Denote
f:=\prod_{j\leq n}\phi_{k_{j}}
and denote by
c_{i}:=\left\langle f,\phi_{i}\right\rangle_{L^{2}(M)}
its Fourier coefficients. There exists
C_{5}(M,n)
such that
99\%
of the
L^{2}
mass of

is concentrated in the following way:

(4)

\displaystyle\left\|\sum_{i\in A_{n}}c_{i}\phi_{i}\right\|_{L^{2}(M)}\geq 0.99\left\|f\right\|_{L^{2}(M)}\quad\quad\mbox{ where }A_{n}:=\left\{i:\lambda_{i}\leq C_{5}\sum\limits_{j\leq n}\lambda_{k_{j}}\right\}.

Remark 1.6

To fix the ideas, we chose

Theorem

4.
Proof of Theorem
1.1

4.1.
Outline of the proof

The proof of Theorem
1.1
naturally decomposes into two parts. The first part (sections
4.2
to
4.5
) consists of finding a harmonic extension of a product of eigenfunctions with quantitative estimates.

We note that the existence and uniqueness of the harmonic extension are guaranteed by the theorem of Cauchy-Kovalevskaya. However, the classical real-valued version of this theorem does not give any (quantitative) information on the extension nor on the size of the domain where this extension is defined. On the other hand, there exists a lesser-known complex version of Cauchy-Kovalevskaya which includes quantitative estimates on both the supremum of this extension as well as on the size of the domain on which this extension is defined.

In Section
4.2
, we show that for each
p\in M
, there is a normal chart at
p
which includes a polydisk in which the coefficients of
\Delta_{g}
have a bounded holomorphic extension.

In Section
4.3
, we show that on each chart, there exists a possibly smaller polydisk in which the Laplace-Beltrami eigenfunctions have a bounded holomorphic extension. We also estimate the supremum of the holomorphic extension of an eigenfunction in a fixed complex polydisk in terms of its eigenvalue.

The results from these two previous sections now allow us to consider a complex Cauchy problem. In Section
4.4
, we show, using a complex version of Cauchy-Kovalevskaya’s theorem, the existence of a unique holomorphic harmonic extension of the product of eigenfunctions on a complex polydisk. We also find a quantitative estimate on the supremum of the extension and on the size of the polydisk in which this extension is well-defined.

In Section
4.5
, we go back to the real setting and finish the construction.

In the second part (Section
4.6
), we use this harmonic extension together with Green’s formula to estimate the Fourier coefficients.

4.2.
Radius of holomorphic extension of the coefficients of the Laplacian

In this section, we show that for each
p\in M
, there is a chart
O_{p}
containing a real polydisk of fixed radius (i.e., independent of
p
) and a complex extension of
\Delta_{g}
on the complexification of this polydisk. More precisely, we have the following Proposition:

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
(M,g)
be a closed real-analytic manifold of dimension
d
.
There exists
R_{1}(M)
such that for any

, there exists a chart

with the following properties:

•

The polydisk

is fully contained in

•

Let
\Delta_{g}=\sum_{|\alpha|\leq 2}a_{\alpha}\partial^{\alpha}_{x}
be the local expression for the Laplace-Beltrami operator. Then, for any multi-index

As a consequence, we can define the operator
L_{g}:=\sum_{|\alpha|\leq 2}a_{\alpha}(z)\partial_{z}^{\alpha}

PD_{\mathbb{C}^{d}}(R_{1})

Proof.

Let
O_{p}=B_{R_{inj}}
a normal coordinate chart.
We immediately see that
PD_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\frac{R_{inj}}{\sqrt{d}}\right)\subset B_{R_{inj}}
. Since the metric is analytic, for each
p\in M
the coefficients of the metric (in normal coordinates around

R^{\prime}(p)
, but does not converge absolutely on any larger polydisk. We set

\displaystyle R^{\prime}:=\inf_{p\in M}R^{\prime}(p)>0.

We know that each coefficient
a_{\alpha}
is determined only by the matrix
g
, its derivatives and its inverse. Therefore, the radius of convergence of the Taylor series of
a_{\alpha}
is the same as the radius of convergence of the coefficients of
g

a_{\alpha}
has a holomorphic extension to
PD_{\mathbb{C}^{d}}(R^{\prime})
.

Denote
R^{\prime\prime}:=\min\left(\frac{R_{inj}}{\sqrt{d}},R^{\prime}\right)
. By construction,
PD_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(R^{\prime\prime})\subset O_{p}
and each coefficient
a_{\alpha}(x)
has a holomorphic extension
a_{\alpha}(z)
in
PD_{\mathbb{C}^{d}}(R^{\prime\prime})

R_{1}:=\frac{R^{\prime\prime}}{2}
ensures that each

is bounded and holomorphic in
PD_{\mathbb{C}^{d}}(R_{1})
. This completes the proof of Proposition
4.1
.
∎

4.4.2.
Step 2: Changing boundary conditions

We rewrite the Cauchy problem (

) in a way that allows us to use Theorem

4.4.3.
Step 3: Finding a suitable
\delta

To use Theorem
4.8
, we need condition (
15

. Note that for any index

2(2^{d+1}e)^{2}\sum_{|\alpha|\leq 2}(2R_{3})^{2-|\alpha|}\delta^{2}|a_{\alpha}(z)|\leq 1,\quad z\in PD_{\mathbb{C}^{d+1}}(2R_{3}).

Recall that by the definition of

(see Step 1 above), every coefficients

is holomorphic and bounded on

for any
\delta\geq 0
. Therefore, we can make the following choice for

p\in M,
define

\displaystyle\delta_{0,p}:=2(2^{d+1}e)^{2}\sup_{z\in PD_{\mathbb{C}^{d}}(2R_{3})}\left(\sum_{|\alpha|\leq 2}|a_{\alpha}(z)|(2R_{3})^{2-|\alpha|}\right),

Step 4: Back to the original problem

We will now go back to our initial Cauchy problem (

We note that

4.4.5.
Step 5: The bounds on

It remains to estimate the right-hand side of (

\displaystyle\sup_{PD_{\mathbb{C}^{d}}(R_{2})}|\phi_{k_{j}}|\leq C_{6}e^{R_{1}\lambda_{k_{j}}}(C_{7}\lambda_{k_{j}})^{\frac{d-1}{2}}.

Since

, we get the following bound:

(28)

\sup_{PD_{\mathbb{C}^{d}}(4R_{3})}\left|\prod\limits_{j\leq n}\phi_{k_{j}}\right|\leq\prod_{j\leq n}C_{6}e^{R_{1}\lambda_{k_{j}}}\left(C_{7}\lambda_{k_{j}}\right)^{\frac{d-1}{2}}.

We will use Cauchy’s estimate to bound the derivatives of a holomorphic function:

Lemma 4.9

(Cauchy’s estimates)
.

Let
k\geq 1
, let
R,\delta>0
and let

be holomorphic and bounded in

(29)

\displaystyle\sup_{PD_{\mathbb{C}^{k+1}}(R,\delta)}|\partial_{z}^{\alpha}f|\leq\alpha!R^{-|\alpha|}\delta^{-\alpha_{k+1}}\sup_{PD_{\mathbb{C}^{k+1}}(2R,\delta)}|f|.

Estimate (

(30)

\sup_{PD_{\mathbb{C}^{d}}(2R_{3})}\left|\partial^{\alpha}_{z}\left(\prod\limits_{j\leq n}\phi_{k_{j}}\right)\right|\leq\alpha!(2R_{3})^{-|\alpha|}\sup_{PD_{\mathbb{C}^{d}}(4R_{3})}\left|\prod\limits_{j\leq n}\phi_{k_{j}}\right|.

Using (

\displaystyle 2(2R_{3}\delta)^{2}\sup_{PD_{\mathbb{C}^{d}}(2R_{3})}\left|L_{g}\left(\prod_{j\leq n}\phi_{k_{j}}\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leq 2(2R_{3}\delta)^{2}\sup_{PD_{\mathbb{C}^{d}}(2R_{3})}\sum_{|\alpha|\leq 2}|a_{\alpha}|\left|\partial^{\alpha}_{z}\left(\prod\limits_{j\leq n}\phi_{k_{j}}\right)\right|

(31)

\displaystyle\leq 4(2R_{3}\delta)^{2}\left(\sup_{PD_{\mathbb{C}^{d}}(2R_{3})}\sum_{|\alpha|\leq 2}|a_{\alpha}|(2R_{3})^{-|\alpha|}\right)\sup_{PD_{\mathbb{C}^{d}}(4R_{3})}\left|\prod\limits_{j\leq n}\phi_{k_{j}}\right|.

By the definition of
\delta
in (

we obtain the following:

\displaystyle 2(2R_{3}\delta)^{2}\sup_{PD_{\mathbb{C}^{d}}(2R_{3})}\left|L_{g}\left(\prod_{j\leq n}\phi_{k_{j}}\right)\right|\leq\frac{1}{2^{2d+1}e^{2}}\sup_{PD_{\mathbb{C}^{d}}(4R_{3})}\left|\prod\limits_{j\leq n}\phi_{k_{j}}\right|

(32)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2^{2d+1}e^{2}}\prod_{j\leq n}C_{6}e^{R_{1}\lambda_{k_{j}}}(C_{7}\lambda_{k_{j}})^{\frac{d-1}{2}},

the last inequality following from (

(33)

\sup_{PD_{\mathbb{C}^{d+1}}(R_{3},\delta)}|u_{p}|\leq\left(\frac{1}{2^{2d+1}e^{2}}+1\right)\prod_{j\leq n}C_{6}e^{R_{1}\lambda_{k_{j}}}(C_{7}\lambda_{k_{j}})^{\frac{d-1}{2}}.

This completes the upper bound (

.
Finally, the bound (

4.5.
Reduction to the real setting

So far, for any
p\in M
, we have constructed a holomorphic function

solving

on a complex neighborhood of

in

to

on

Proposition 4.10
.

With
R_{3}
and
\delta
defined as in Proposition
4.7
, let us define
T:=\frac{R_{3}\delta}{2}
. Then, there exists a real-analytic solution
H
to the following problem:

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{rll}\Delta_{g^{\prime}}H&=0&\mbox{ on }M\times(-T,T),\\
H&=\prod\limits_{j\leq n}\phi_{k_{j}}&\mbox{ on }\{t=0\},\\
\partial_{t}H&=0&\mbox{ on }\{t=0\}.\end{array}\right.

Furthermore, we have the following bounds for
H
:

(37)

\displaystyle\sup_{M\times(-T,T)}|H|\leq C_{8}\prod\limits_{j\leq n}C_{6}e^{R_{1}\lambda_{k_{j}}}(C_{7}\lambda_{k_{j}})^{\frac{d-1}{2}},\quad\sup_{M\times(-T,T)}|\partial_{t}H|\leq\frac{1}{T}C_{8}\prod\limits_{j\leq n}C_{6}e^{R_{1}\lambda_{k_{j}}}(C_{7}\lambda_{k_{j}})^{\frac{d-1}{2}},

where
C_{6},C_{7},C_{8},R_{1}
as in Proposition
4.7
.

We will first construct this function

on charts by restricting the holomorphic functions
u_{p}
constructed in Section
4.4
to the real setting. To this end, we will use the following elementary property of holomorphic functions and (finite-order) differential operators with holomorphic coefficients:

Fact:
Let
A
be a finite subset of
\mathbb{N}^{k}
, let
R>0
and let
b_{\alpha}^{\mathbb{C}}:PD_{\mathbb{C}^{k}}(R)\to\mathbb{C}
be bounded holomorphic functions for
\alpha\in A
. Let
u^{\mathbb{C}}:PD_{\mathbb{C}^{k}}(R)\to\mathbb{C}
be bounded and holomorphic as well. Let
b_{\alpha}^{\mathbb{R}}:=\left.b_{\alpha}^{\mathbb{C}}\right|_{PD_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}(R)}
and
u^{\mathbb{R}}:=\left.u^{\mathbb{C}}\right|_{PD_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}(R)}
. Then,

(38)

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\alpha\in A}b_{\alpha}^{\mathbb{C}}(z)\partial_{z}^{\alpha}u^{\mathbb{C}}=0,\hskip 5.69046pt\forall z\in PD_{\mathbb{C}^{k}}(R)\quad\implies\quad\sum\limits_{\alpha\in A}b_{\alpha}^{\mathbb{R}}(x)\partial_{x}^{\alpha}u^{\mathbb{R}}=0,\hskip 5.69046pt\forall x\in PD_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}(R).

This fact can easily be verified by expanding both Taylor series and comparing the coefficients.

Now, let
p\in M
,
PD_{\mathbb{R}^{d+1}}(R_{3},\delta)\subset O_{p}\times\mathbb{R}
and
u_{p}:PD_{\mathbb{C}^{d+1}}(R_{3},\delta)\to\mathbb{C}
be the solution to the Cauchy problem (
4.4
) constructed using Proposition
4.7
. Let
H_{p}
be the restriction of
u_{p}
to
PD_{\mathbb{R}^{d+1}}(R_{3},\delta)\subset O_{p}\times\mathbb{R}
.

By (
38

H_{p}
solves the following real-analytic Cauchy problem with real-valued boundary conditions:

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{rll}\Delta_{g^{\prime}}H_{p}&=0&\mbox{ on }PD_{\mathbb{R}^{d+1}}(R_{3},\delta),\\
H_{p}&=\prod\limits_{j\leq n}\phi_{k_{j}}&\mbox{ on }\{x_{d+1}=0\},\\
\partial_{x_{d+1}}H_{p}&=0&\mbox{ on }\{x_{d+1}=0\}.\end{array}\right.

Note that
H_{p}
is real-valued by the real version of Cauchy-Kovalevskaya’s Theorem (since the imaginary part of
H_{p}
solves the same Cauchy problem (
4.5

Using again the real version of Cauchy-Kovalevskaya’s Theorem, we note that the restriction of
H_{p}
to any polydisk
Q\subset PD_{\mathbb{R}^{d+1}}(R_{3},\delta)
that intersects
\{x_{d+1}=0\}
is the only analytic solution to the following Cauchy problem:

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{rll}\Delta_{g^{\prime}}f&=0&\mbox{ on }Q,\\
f&=\prod\limits_{j\leq n}\phi_{k_{j}}&\mbox{ on }\{x_{d+1}=0\},\\
\partial_{x_{d+1}}f&=0&\mbox{ on }\{x_{d+1}=0\}.\end{array}\right.

Now, recall that for any

as the normal map associated with

. Let us take two points

such that

This allows us to patch all the solutions

into one global solution
H
on
M\times[-R_{3}\delta,R_{3}\delta]
. We now choose

, and remark that

solves the Cauchy problem (
4.10
).
Finally, the bounds (
37

follow from the bounds (

) on

did not depend on the choice of of

4.6.
The final step

We are now ready to finish the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Let
T
and
H
be defined as in Proposition
4.10
. Let
M_{T}:=M\times[0,T]
and denote
f_{i}(x,t):=\phi_{i}(x)e^{-\lambda_{i}t}
for
(x,t)\in M_{T}
.

Since both
H
and
f_{i}
are harmonic on
M_{T}
, Green’s formula yields

(45)

\displaystyle 0=\int_{M_{T}}\left(f_{i}\Delta_{g^{\prime}}H-H\Delta_{g^{\prime}}f_{i}\right)=\int_{\partial M_{T}}\left(f_{i}\partial_{n}H-H\partial_{n}f_{i}\right)\,,

where
\partial_{n}
denotes the outwards normal derivative on
\partial M_{T}
. Since
M
is a manifold without boundary,
\partial M_{T}=M\times\{0\}\bigcup M\times\{T\}
and therefore

(46)

\displaystyle\int_{M\times\{0\}}\left(f_{i}\partial_{t}H-H\partial_{t}f_{i}\right)=\int_{M\times\{T\}}\left(f_{i}\partial_{t}H-H\partial_{t}f_{i}\right)\,.

Since
H
solves the Cauchy problem (
4.10
), (
46
) becomes

\displaystyle\int_{M\times\{0\}}\lambda_{i}\phi_{i}\prod\limits_{j\leq n}\phi_{k_{j}}=\int_{M\times\{T\}}\left(\phi_{i}e^{-T\lambda_{i}}\partial_{t}H+H\lambda_{i}\phi_{i}e^{-T\lambda_{i}}\right).

Since
\lambda_{i}>0
for all
i>0
, we obtain

\displaystyle\left|\int_{M}\phi_{i}\prod\limits_{j\leq n}\phi_{k_{j}}\right|\leq e^{-T\lambda_{i}}\frac{{\text{Vol}(M)}^{1/2}}{\lambda_{i}}\left(\|\partial_{t}H\|_{L^{\infty}(M_{T})}\|\phi_{i}\|_{L^{2}(M)}+\lambda_{i}\|H\|_{L^{\infty}(M_{T})}\|\phi_{i}\|_{L^{2}(M)}\right).

Since
\phi_{i}
is normalized in
L^{2}(M)
and applying the bounds on
H
from Proposition
4.10
, we obtain

(47)

\left|\int_{M}\phi_{i}\prod\limits_{j\leq n}\phi_{k_{j}}\right|\leq e^{-T\lambda_{i}}\frac{{\text{Vol}(M)}^{1/2}}{\lambda_{i}}C_{8}\left(\frac{1}{T}+\lambda_{i}\right)\prod_{j\leq n}C_{6}e^{R_{1}\lambda_{k_{j}}}\left(C_{7}\lambda_{k_{j}}\right)^{\frac{d-1}{2}}\,.

By noticing that for
x>0,

x^{(d-1)/2}\leq d!e^{x}
, by denoting
\lambda_{1}
the first non-zero eigenvalue of
\Delta_{g}
on
M
, and by recalling that eigenvalues are arranged in increasing order, we obtain

(48)

\left|\int_{M}\phi_{i}\prod\limits_{j\leq n}\phi_{k_{j}}\right|\leq{\text{Vol}(M)}^{1/2}C_{8}\left(\frac{1}{T\lambda_{1}}+1\right)e^{-T\lambda_{i}}\prod_{j\leq n}C_{6}d!\,e^{(R_{1}+C_{7})\lambda_{k_{j}}}\,.

Now, since
C_{6},C_{8},T
depend only on
M
, there exists
C_{9}(M)>0
such that

{\text{Vol}(M)}^{1/2}C_{8}\left(\frac{1}{T\lambda_{1}}+1\right)C_{6}d!\leq e^{C_{9}\lambda_{1}}.

Since eigenvalues are labelled in increasing order, by choosing
c:=T
and
C:=R_{1}+C_{7}+C_{9}
, we finally obtain

\displaystyle\left|\int_{M}\phi_{i}\prod\limits_{j\leq n}\phi_{k_{j}}\right|\leq e^{-c\lambda_{i}}\prod_{j\leq n}e^{C\lambda_{k_{j}}}\,,

which completes the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

∎

5.

In this Section, we prove Lemma
1.3
, on a lower bound for the

norm of a product of Laplace-Beltrami eigenfunctions. To do that, we will find a set of fixed measure where all eigenfunctions appearing in the product will be bounded from below. This relies on estimates for Laplace-Beltrami eigenfunctions from
[
4
]
and for the size of sub-level sets of harmonic functions from
[

]
:

Theorem 5.1
.

[
4
, Proposition 4.1]

Let
(M,g)
be a smooth compact manifold without boundary. Let
\phi_{k}
be a Laplace-Beltrami eigenfunction on
M

\lambda_{k}^{2}
. Then, there exists

depending only on

such that the following holds: for any

\displaystyle\sup_{B(p,R_{4})}|\phi_{k}|\geq e^{-C_{9}\lambda_{k}-C_{10}}\|\phi_{k}\|_{L^{\infty}(M)}.

Theorem 5.2
.

[
4
, Theorem 4.2 (ii)]

Let
(M,g)
be a smooth compact manifold without boundary. Let
\phi_{k}
be a Laplace-Beltrami eigenfunction on
M

\lambda_{k}^{2}

\displaystyle N\big(\phi_{k},B(p,r)\big):=\ln\left(\frac{\sup_{B(p,2r)}|\phi_{k}|}{\sup_{B(p,r)}|\phi_{k}|}\right)\leq C_{11}\lambda_{k}+C_{12}.

The two previous results were proved by Donnelly and Fefferman in their work on the size of the nodal sets of Laplace-Beltrami eigenfunctions on real-analytic manifolds. We will also need the following Remez inequality due to Logunov and Malinnikova:

Theorem 5.3
.

[
9

A

E_{a}:=\left\{x\in\frac{1}{2}Q:\,|u(x)|<e^{-a}\right\}.

We have the following bound:

(49)

\mu_{d}(E_{a})\leq C_{R}e^{-\frac{\beta a}{N}}s(Q)^{d}

We will start the proof of Lemma

\mu_{d}\Big(\left\{|\phi_{\lambda_{k}}|<e^{-C_{14}\lambda_{k}-C_{15}}\right\}\cap Q/2\Big)\leq\frac{1}{2n}{\mu_{d}(Q/2)}.

Let
n
be fixed.

be fixed and

From now on, every constant that will appear in the proof of Proposition

\mu_{d}\Big(\left\{|\phi_{k}|<e^{-\frac{r}{4}{\lambda_{k}}}L\right\}\cap Q/2\Big)\leq\frac{2}{r}\mu_{d+1}\Big(\left\{|h_{k}|<L\right\}\cap Q^{\prime}/2\Big).

Note that the cube

B\left(0,r\sqrt{d}\right)
. Hence

N(\phi_{k},Q)\leq\ln\left(\frac{\sup_{B(0,2r\sqrt{d})}|\phi_{k}|}{\sup_{B(0,r)}|\phi_{k}|}\right).

Therefore, by applying Theorem
5.2

\lfloor\ln_{2}\left(\sqrt{d}\right)\rfloor+2
times, we have that

N(\phi_{k},Q)\leq C_{11}{\lambda_{k}}+C_{12},

and therefore

(52)

\displaystyle N(h_{k},Q)\leq C_{16}{\lambda_{k}}+C_{17}.

Similarly, since
B(0,r)\subset Q
, it comes by applying Theorem
5.2

\lfloor\ln_{2}\left(\frac{R_{4}}{r}\right)\rfloor+1
times that

\sup_{Q}|\phi_{k}|\geq\sup_{B(0,r)}|\phi_{k}|\geq\sup_{B(0,R_{4})}|\phi_{k}|e^{-C_{18}{\lambda_{k}}-C_{19}}.

Hence, by Theorem
5.1
and since
\|\phi_{k}\|_{L^{\infty}(M)}\geq\frac{1}{Vol(M)^{1/2}}
(as
\|\phi_{k}\|_{L^{2}(M)}=1
), we get

\sup_{Q}|\phi_{k}|\geq e^{-C_{20}{\lambda_{k}}-C_{21}}

which implies that

(53)

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{Q^{\prime}}|h_{k}|\geq e^{-C_{22}{\lambda_{k}}-C_{23}}.

The cube
Q^{\prime}
and the function
{h_{k}}(\sup\limits_{Q^{\prime}}|h_{k}|)^{-1}
both satisfy the conditions of theorem
5.4
. Therefore, by (
52
), (
53
) and by theorem
5.4
, there exists
\beta
and
C_{R}
, such that for any
a>0
, we have the following inequality:

(54)

\mu_{d+1}\Big(\left\{|h_{k}|<e^{-a}e^{-C_{22}{\lambda_{k}}-C_{23}}\right\}\cap Q^{\prime}/2\Big)\leq C_{R}e^{\frac{-\beta a}{C_{16}{\lambda_{k}}+C_{17}}}.

We now set
a:=C_{24}(n){\lambda_{k}}
where
C_{24}
is large enough (and independent of
\lambda_{k}
) such that

C_{R}e^{-C_{24}\frac{\beta{\lambda_{1}}}{C_{16}{\lambda_{1}}+C_{17}}}\leq\frac{1}{2n}\mu_{d+1}(Q^{\prime}/2).

With this choice of
a
, we obtain using (
54
) that

(55)

\mu_{d+1}\Big(\big\{|h_{k}|<e^{-(C_{24}+C_{22}){\lambda_{k}}-C_{23}}\big\}\cap Q^{\prime}/2\Big)\leq\frac{1}{2n}\mu_{d+1}(Q^{\prime}/2).

Combining equations (
51
) and (
55
), and noticing that
\mu_{d+1}(Q^{\prime}/2)=r/2\mu_{d}(Q/2)
, we obtain the following inequality:

(56)

\mu_{d}\Big(\big\{|\phi_{k}|<e^{-(C_{24}+C_{22}+\frac{r}{4}){\lambda_{k}}-C_{23}}\big\}\cap Q/2\Big)\leq\frac{1}{2n}\mu_{d}(Q/2)\,.

Choosing
C_{14}:=C_{24}+C_{22}+\frac{r}{4}
and
C_{15}:=C_{23}
, and noticing that they both only depend on
M,Q
and
n
, we finally obtain

\mu_{d}\Big(\big\{|\phi_{k}|<e^{-C_{14}{\lambda_{k}}-C_{15}}\big\}\cap Q/2\Big)\leq\frac{1}{2n}\mu_{d}(Q/2)\,.

which completes the proof of Proposition
5.4
.
∎

Now that we have found a small set where each eigenfunction is small, we can find a large set where all the eigenfunctions in the product are bounded from below.

Corollary 5.5
.

Let
(M,g)
be a smooth closed manifold. Let
n\geq 1
. For any
0<k_{1}<\ldots<k_{n}
, there exists a set
E
of measure at least
C_{25}
where
C_{25}>0
is independent of
n
and of the choice of
k_{j}
and there exists
C_{26}(M,n)>0
with the following property: for any
1\leq j\leq n
, we have

(57)

\inf\limits_{E}|\phi_{k_{j}}|>e^{-C_{26}\lambda_{k_{j}}}.

Proof.

Choose
O_{p}
and
Q
as in Proposition
5.4
. By Proposition
5.4
, we know that ,

\mu_{d}\left(\bigcup_{j\leq n}\left\{|\phi_{k_{j}}|<e^{-C_{14}\lambda_{k_{j}}-C_{15}}\right\}\cap Q/2\right)\leq\frac{1}{2}{\mu_{d}(Q/2)},

and therefore,

\mu_{d}\left(\bigcap_{j\leq n}\left\{|\phi_{k_{j}}|\geq e^{-C_{14}\lambda_{k_{j}}-C_{15}}\right\}\cap Q/2\right)\geq\frac{1}{2}{\mu_{d}(Q/2)}.

Then, denote

E^{\prime}:=\bigcap_{j\leq n}\left\{|\phi_{k_{j}}|\geq e^{-C_{14}\lambda_{k_{j}}-C_{15}}\right\}\cap Q/2\,.

Set
E:=\psi_{p}^{-1}(E^{\prime})
and observe that
e^{-C_{14}\lambda_{k_{j}}-C_{15}}\geq e^{-C_{26}\lambda_{k_{j}}}
where
C_{26}(M,n):=C_{14}+\frac{C_{15}}{\lambda_{1}}>0
. This finishes the proof of Corollary
5.5
.
∎

We can now complete the proof of Lemma
1.3
:

Proof of Lemma
1.3
.

For
n
fixed, let the set
E
be as in Corollary
5.5
. Let
\mu_{g}
be the Lebesgue measure on
M
induced by
g
. We have the following inequalities:

\displaystyle\left\|\prod_{j\leq n}\phi_{k_{j}}\right\|_{L^{2}(M)}\geq\left\|\prod_{j\leq n}\phi_{k_{j}}\right\|_{L^{2}(E)}\geq\mu_{g}(E)^{1/2}\,\inf\limits_{E}\left|\prod_{j\leq n}\phi_{k_{j}}\right|\geq\mu_{g}(E)^{1/2}\,\prod_{j\leq n}e^{-C_{26}{\lambda_{k_{j}}}}

where the last inequality follows from Corollary
5.5
. We now set
C_{3}(M):=\mu_{g}(E)^{1/2}
and
C_{4}(M,n):=C_{26}
to finish the proof of Lemma
1.3
.
∎

6.
The finite approximation

In this Section, we prove Theorem
1.5
about the finite approximation in
L^{2}
for a product of Laplace-Beltrami eigenfunctions.

Proof of Theorem
1.5
.

Let
S:=\sum\limits_{i\geq 1}e^{-C_{2}\lambda_{i}}
, where
C_{2}
comes from Corollary
1.2
. Note that
S<\infty
by Weyl’s asymptotic formula (see for instance
[
2
]
).

Let
C_{5}(M,n)\geq C_{1}
be a large constant that will be defined later. Denote

A_{n}:=\left\{i:\lambda_{i}\leq C_{5}\sum\limits_{j\leq n}\lambda_{k_{j}}\right\}

and let
A_{n}^{c}=\mathbb{N}\backslash A_{n}
. Recall that we defined
c_{i}:=\int\limits_{M}\phi_{i}\prod\limits_{j\leq n}\phi_{k_{j}}.
Since
\{\phi_{i}\}
is an orthonormal basis of
L^{2}(M)
,

\displaystyle\left\|\prod\limits_{j\leq n}\phi_{k_{j}}-\sum_{i\in A_{n}}c_{i}\phi_{i}\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}(M)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in A_{n}^{c}}|c_{i}|^{2}\,,

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i\in A_{n}^{c}}e^{-2C_{2}\lambda_{i}}\,

where the second inequality follows from Corollary
1.2
and the fact that
C_{5}\geq C_{1}
. Hence, using Lemma
1.3
, we get that

\left\|\prod\limits_{j\leq n}\phi_{k_{j}}-\sum_{i\in A_{n}}c_{i}\phi_{i}\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}(M)}\leq\left\|\prod\limits_{j\leq n}\phi_{k_{j}}\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}(M)}\,\frac{1}{C_{3}^{2}}\prod_{j\leq n}e^{2C_{4}\lambda_{k_{j}}}\sum_{i\in A_{n}^{c}}e^{-2C_{2}\lambda_{i}}.

By the definition of
A_{n}
and
S
,

\displaystyle\left\|\prod\limits_{j\leq n}\phi_{k_{j}}-\sum_{i\in A_{n}}c_{i}\phi_{i}\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}(M)}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\prod\limits_{j\leq n}\phi_{k_{j}}\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}(M)}\,\frac{1}{C_{3}^{2}}\prod_{j\leq n}e^{(2C_{4}-C_{2}C_{5})\lambda_{k_{j}}}\,\sum_{i\in A_{n}^{c}}e^{-C_{2}\lambda_{i}}

(58)

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\prod\limits_{j\leq n}\phi_{k_{j}}\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}(M)}\,\frac{S}{C_{3}^{2}}\prod_{j\leq n}e^{(2C_{4}-C_{2}C_{5})\lambda_{k_{j}}}.

If
2C_{4}-C_{2}C_{5}\leq 0
, (
6
) becomes

(59)

\displaystyle\left\|\prod\limits_{j\leq n}\phi_{k_{j}}-\sum_{i\in A_{n}}c_{i}\phi_{i}\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}(M)}\leq\left\|\prod\limits_{j\leq n}\phi_{k_{j}}\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}(M)}\,\frac{S}{C_{3}^{2}}e^{n(2C_{4}-C_{2}C_{5})\lambda_{1}}.

Now, choose
C_{5}
large enough so that
2C_{4}-C_{2}C_{5}\leq 0
and

\frac{S}{C_{3}^{2}}e^{n(2C_{4}-C_{2}C_{5})\lambda_{1}}\leq\frac{1}{100^{2}}.

With this choice of
C_{5}
, inequality (
59
) becomes

\left\|\prod\limits_{j\leq n}\phi_{k_{j}}-\sum_{i\in A_{n}}c_{i}\phi_{i}\right\|_{L^{2}(M)}\leq\frac{1}{100}\left\|\prod\limits_{j\leq n}\phi_{k_{j}}\right\|_{L^{2}(M)},

which completes the proof of Theorem
1.5
.
∎

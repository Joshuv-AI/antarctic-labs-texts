Distribution Dependent Stochastic Differential Equations Supported in part by NNSFC (11771326, 11831014, 11921001, 11801406).
Xing Huang, Panpan Ren, Feng-Yu Wang

Abstract
Due to their intrinsic link with nonlinear Fokker-Planck equations and many other applications, distribution dependent stochastic differential equations (DDSDEs for short) have been intensively investigated. In this paper we summarize some recent progresses in the study of DDSDEs, which include the correspondence of weak solutions and nonlinear Fokker-Planck equations, the well-posedness, regularity estimates, exponential ergodicity, long time large deviations, and comparison theorems.



AMS subject Classification: 60B05, 60B10.

Keywords: DDSDE, nonlinear Fokker-Planck equation, Bismut formula, Wasserstein distance, gradient estimate.

1
Introduction

To characterize nonlinear PDEs in Vlasov’s kinetic theory, Kac
[
27
,
28
]
proposed the
``
propagation of chaos” of mean field particle systems, which stimulated McKean
[
33
]
to study nonlinear Fokker-Planck equations using stochastic differential equations with distribution dependent drifts, see
[
45
]
for a theory on mean field particle systems and applications.

In general, a nonlinear Fokker-Planck equation can be characterized by the following distribution dependent stochastic differential equations (DDSDEs for short):

(1.1)

\text{\rm{d}}X_{t}=b(t,X_{t},\mathscr{L}_{X_{t}})\text{\rm{d}}t+\sigma(t,X_{t},\mathscr{L}_{X_{t}})\text{\rm{d}}W_{t},

where
W_{t}
is an
m
-dimensional Brownian motion on a complete filtration probability space
(\Omega,\{\mathscr{F}_{t}\}_{t\geq 0},\mathbb{P})
,
\mathscr{L}_{\xi}
is the distribution (i.e. the law) of a random variable
\xi
,

\displaystyle b=(b_{i})_{1\leq i\leq d}:[0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathscr{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d},

\displaystyle\sigma=(\sigma_{ij})_{1\leq i\leq d,1\leq j\leq m}:[0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathscr{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}\otimes\mathbb{R}^{m}

are measurable, and
\mathscr{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is the space of probability measures on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
equipped with the weak topology. Due to the pioneering work
[
33
]
of McKean, the DDSDE (
1.1
) is also called McKean-Vlasov SDE or mean field SDE.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
s\geq 0
.

(1)

A continuous adapted process
(X_{s,t})_{t\geq s}
is called a solution of (
1.1
) from time
s
, if

\int_{s}^{t}\mathbb{E}\big[|b(r,X_{s,r},\mathscr{L}_{X_{s,r}})|+\|\sigma(r,X_{s,r},\mathscr{L}_{X_{s,r}})\|^{2}\big]\text{\rm{d}}r<\infty,\ \ t\geq s,

and
\mathbb{P}
-a.s.

X_{s,t}=X_{s,s}+\int_{s}^{t}b(r,X_{s,r},\mathscr{L}_{X_{s,r}})\text{\rm{d}}r+\int_{s}^{t}\sigma(r,X_{s,r},\mathscr{L}_{X_{s,r}})\text{\rm{d}}W_{r},\ \ t\geq s.

When
s=0
we simply denote
X_{t}=X_{0,t}
.

(2)

A couple
(\tilde{X}_{s,t},\tilde{W}_{t})_{t\geq s}
is called a weak solution of (
1.1
) from time
s
, if
\tilde{W}_{t}
is the
m
-dimensional Brownian motion on a complete filtration probability space
(\tilde{\Omega},\{\tilde{\mathscr{F}}_{t}\}_{t\geq 0},\tilde{\mathbb{P}})
such that
(\tilde{X}_{s,t})_{t\geq s}
is a solution of (
1.1
) from time
s

for
(\tilde{W}_{t},\tilde{\mathbb{P}})
replacing
(W_{t},\mathbb{P})
. (
1.1
) is called weakly unique for an initial distribution
\nu\in\mathscr{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, if all weak solutions with distribution
\nu
at time
s
are equal in law.

(3)

Let
\hat{\mathscr{P}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
be a subspace of
\mathscr{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. (
1.1
) is said to have strong (respectively, weak) well-posedness for initial distributions in
\hat{\mathscr{P}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, if for any
\mathscr{F}_{s}
-measurable
X_{s,s}
with
\mathscr{L}_{X_{s,s}}\in\hat{\mathscr{P}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
(respectively, any initial distribution
\nu\in\hat{\mathscr{P}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
at time
s
), it has a unique strong (respectively, weak) solution.
We call the equation well-posed if it is both strongly and weakly well-posed.

According to Yamada-Watanabe principle, for classical SDEs the strong well-posedness implies the weak one. But this does not apply to
DDSDEs, see Theorem
3.2
below for a modified Yamada-Watanabe principle.

In this paper, we summarize the following recent progress in the study of the DDSDE (
1.1
): the correspondence between the weak solution of (
1.1
) and the associated nonlinear Fokker-Planck equation (Section 2), criteria on the well-posedness (i.e. existence and uniqueness of solutions) (Section 3), regularity of distributions (Section 4), exponential ergodicity (Section 5), long time large deviations (Section 6), and comparison theorems (Section 7). Corresponding results for general models of path-distribution dependent SDEs/SPDEs can be found in
[
2
,
19
,
37
]
.

2
Weak solution and nonlinear Fokker-Planck equation

In this part, we first introduce the
``
superposition principle” which provides a correspondence between the weak solution of (
1.1
) and the solution of the associated nonlinear Fokker-Planck equation on
\mathscr{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, then present some typical examples.

2.1
Superposition principle

Consider the following nonlinear Fokker-Planck equation on
\mathscr{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
:

(2.1)

\partial_{t}\mu_{t}=L_{t,\mu_{t}}^{*}\mu_{t},

where for any
(t,\mu)\in[0,\infty)\times\mathscr{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, the Kolmogorov operator
L_{t,\mu}
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
is given by

L_{t,\mu}:=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}(\sigma\sigma^{*})_{ij}(t,\cdot,\mu)\partial_{i}\partial_{j}+\sum_{i=1}^{d}b_{i}(t,\cdot,\mu)\partial_{i},

for
\sigma^{*}
being the transposition of
\sigma
.

Definition 2.1
.

For
s\geq 0
,
\mu_{\cdot}\in C([s,\infty);\mathscr{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
is called a solution of (
2.1
) from time
s
, if

\int_{s}^{t}\text{\rm{d}}r\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\big\{\|\sigma(r,x,\mu_{r})\|^{2}+|b(r,x,\mu_{r})|\big\}\mu_{r}(\text{\rm{d}}x)<\infty,\ \ t>s,

and for any
f\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,

(2.2)

\mu_{t}(f):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f\text{\rm{d}}\mu_{t}=\mu_{s}(f)+\int_{s}^{t}\mu_{r}(L_{r,\mu_{r}}f)\text{\rm{d}}r,\ \ t\geq s.

Now, assume that
(\tilde{X}_{t},\tilde{W}_{t})_{t\geq s}
is a weak solution of (
1.1
) from time
s
under a complete filtration probability space
(\tilde{\Omega},\{\tilde{\mathscr{F}}_{t}\}_{t\geq s},\tilde{\mathbb{P}})
, and let

\mu_{t}=\mathscr{L}_{\tilde{X}_{t}|\tilde{\mathbb{P}}}:=\tilde{\mathbb{P}}\circ(\tilde{X}_{t})^{-1}
be the distribution of
\tilde{X}_{t}
under the probability
\tilde{\mathbb{P}}
. By Itô’s formula we have

\text{\rm{d}}f(\tilde{X}_{t})=\big\{L_{t,\mu_{t}}f(\tilde{X}_{t})\big\}\text{\rm{d}}t+\langle\nabla f(\tilde{X}_{t}),\sigma(t,\tilde{X}_{t},\mu_{t})\text{\rm{d}}\tilde{W}_{t}\rangle.

Integrating both sides over
[s,t]
and taking expectations, we obtain (
2.2
) so that
\mu_{\cdot}
solves (
2.1
) by definition.
Indeed, the following result due to
[
5
,
6
]
also ensures the converse, i.e. a solution of (
2.1
) gives a weak solution of (
1.1
),
see Section 2 of
[
6
]
(and
[
5
]
).

Theorem 2.1

(
[
5
,
6
]
)
.

Let
(s,\zeta)\in[0,\infty)\times\mathscr{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Then the DDSDE (
1.1
) has a weak solution
(\tilde{X}_{t},\tilde{W}_{t})_{t\geq s}
starting from
s
with
\mathscr{L}_{\tilde{X}_{s}|\tilde{\mathbb{P}}}=\zeta
, if and only if (
2.1
) has a solution
(\mu_{t})_{t\geq s}
starting from
s
with
\mu_{s}=\zeta
. In this case
\mu_{t}=\mathscr{L}_{\tilde{X}_{t}|\tilde{\mathbb{P}}}
,
t\geq s
.

2.2
Some examples

In this part, we introduce some typical nonlinear PDES and state their corresponding DDSDEs.

Example 2.1 (Landau type equations).

Consider the following nonlinear PDE for probability density functions
(f_{t})_{t\geq 0}
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
:

(2.3)

\partial_{t}f_{t}=\frac{1}{2}\text{div}\bigg\{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}a(\cdot-z)\big(f_{t}(z)\nabla f_{t}-f_{t}\nabla f_{t}(z)\big)\text{\rm{d}}z\bigg\},

where
a:\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}\otimes\mathbb{R}^{d}
has weak derivatives.
For the real-world model of homogenous Landau equation we have
d=3
and

a(x)=|x|^{2+\gamma}\Big(I-\frac{x\otimes x}{|x|^{2}}\Big),\ \ x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}

for some constant
\gamma\in[-3,1].

In this case (
2.3
) is a limit version of Boltzmann equation (for thermodynamic system) when all collisions become grazing.
To characterize this equation using SDE, let
m=d
,
b=\frac{1}{2}\text{div}a
and
\sigma=\sqrt{a}
. Consider the DDSDE

(2.4)

\text{\rm{d}}X_{t}=(b*\mathscr{L}_{X_{t}})(X_{t})\text{\rm{d}}t+(\sigma*\mathscr{L}_{X_{t}})(X_{t})\text{\rm{d}}B_{t},

where

(f*\mu)(x):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(x-z)\mu(\text{\rm{d}}z).

Then the distribution density

f_{t}(x):=\frac{\mathscr{L}_{X_{t}}(\text{\rm{d}}x)}{\text{\rm{d}}x}
solves the Landau type equation (
2.3
).

Example 2.2 (Porous media equation).

Consider the following nonlinear PDE for probability density functions on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
:

(2.5)

\partial_{t}f_{t}=\Delta f_{t}^{3}.

Then for any solution to the DDSDE (
1.1
) with coefficients

b=0,\ \ \sigma(x,\mu)=\sqrt{2}\frac{\text{\rm{d}}\mu}{\text{\rm{d}}x}(x)I_{d\times d},

the probability density function solves the porous media equation (
2.5
).

Example 2.3 (Granular media equation).

Consider the following nonlinear PDE for probability density functions on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
:

(2.6)

\partial_{t}f_{t}=\Delta f_{t}+{\rm div}\big\{f_{t}\nabla V+f_{t}\nabla(W*f_{t})\big\}.

Then the associated DDSDE (
1.1
) has coefficients

b(x,\mu)=-\nabla V(x)-\nabla(W*\mu)(x),\ \ \sigma(x,\mu)=\sqrt{2}I_{d\times d},

where

(W*\mu)(x):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}W(x-y)\mu(\text{\rm{d}}y).

3
Well-posedness

We first introduce a fixed-point argument in distribution and a modified Yamada-Watanabe principle, then present results on the existence and uniqueness for monotone and singular coefficients respectively.

3.1
Fixed-point in distribution and Yamada-Watanabe principle

Let
\hat{\mathscr{P}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
be a subspace of
\mathscr{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, and let
\hat{\rho}
be a complete metric on
\hat{\mathscr{P}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
inducing the Borel sigma algebra of the weak topology. Typical examples include

\hat{\mathscr{P}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})=\mathscr{P}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}):=\big\{\mu\in\mathscr{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d}):\mu(|\cdot|^{p})<\infty\big\}

for
p>0
, with
L^{p}
-Wasserstein distance

\mathbb{W}_{p}(\mu,\nu):=\inf_{\pi\in\mathscr{C}(\mu,\nu)}\bigg(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}|x-y|^{p}\pi(\text{\rm{d}}x,\text{\rm{d}}y)\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p\lor 1}},\ \ \mu,\nu\in\mathscr{P}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

When
p=0
this reduces to the total variation norm

\|\mu-\nu\|_{TV}:=2\sup_{A\in\mathscr{B}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}|\mu(A)-\nu(A)|.

For any
T>s\geq 0
and
\nu\in\hat{\mathscr{P}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, consider the path space over
\hat{\mathscr{P}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

\hat{\mathscr{C}}_{s,T}^{\nu}:=\big\{\mu_{\cdot}\in C([s,T];\hat{\mathscr{P}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})):\mu_{s}=\nu\big\},

which is then complete under the metric

\hat{\rho}_{s,T}(\mu_{\cdot},\nu_{\cdot}):=\sup_{t\in[s,T]}\hat{\rho}(\mu_{t},\nu_{t}).

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
T>s\geq 0
, and let
X_{s}
be an
\mathscr{F}_{s}
-measurable random variable with
\nu:=\mathscr{L}_{X_{s}}\in\hat{\mathscr{P}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).
Assume that for any

\mu\in\hat{\mathscr{C}}_{s,T}^{\nu},
the classical SDE

(3.1)

\text{\rm{d}}X_{t}^{\mu}=b(t,X_{t}^{\mu},\mu_{t})\text{\rm{d}}t+\sigma(t,X_{t}^{\mu},\mu_{t})\text{\rm{d}}W_{t},\ \ t\in[s,T],X_{s}^{\mu}=X_{s}

has a unique solution, and the map

\mu\in\hat{\mathscr{C}}_{s,T}^{\nu}\mapsto\Phi_{s,T}\mu:=(\mathscr{L}_{X_{t}^{\mu}})_{t\in[s,T]}\in\hat{\mathscr{C}}_{s,T}^{\nu}

is contractive. Then the DDSDE
(
1.1
)
has well-posedness for initial distributions in
\hat{\mathscr{P}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

Proof.

By the fixed-point theorem, the map
\Phi_{s,T}
has a unique fixed point
\mu
in
\hat{\mathscr{C}}_{s,T}^{\mu}
, so that by the definition of
\Phi_{s,T}
we have

\mathscr{L}_{X_{t}^{\mu}}=\mu_{t},t\in[s,T]
, i.e. in this case
(X_{t}^{\mu})_{t\in[s,T]}
is a solution of (
1.1
) from time
s
starting at
X_{s}.
If (
1.1
) has another solution
(\hat{X}_{t})_{t\in[s,T]}
with
\mathscr{L}_{\hat{X}_{\cdot}}\in\hat{C}_{s,T}^{\mu}
, then
\mu:=\mathscr{L}_{\hat{X}_{\cdot}}
is a fixed point of
\Phi_{s,T}
so that
\mathscr{L}_{X_{t}}=\mathscr{L}_{\hat{X}_{t}}=:\mu_{t},t\in[s,T].

Therefore, by the uniqueness of (
3.1
) we have
\mathscr{L}_{X_{t}}=\mathscr{L}_{\hat{X}_{t}}=X_{t}^{\mu}
, which implies the uniqueness of (
1.1
) with
\mathscr{L}_{X_{\cdot}}\in\hat{\mathscr{C}}_{s,T}^{\mu}
.
Since the strong well-posedness of (
3.1
) implies the weak one, the same argument leads to the weak well-posedness of the DDSDE (
1.1
) starting from
\nu
at time
s
.
∎

The Yamada-Watanabe principle
[
55
]
(see
[
30
]
for a general version) is a fundamental tool in the study of well-posedness for SDEs with singular coefficients. In the present distribution dependent setting, the original statement does not apply, but we have the following modified version due to
[
21
, Lemma 3.4]
.

Theorem 3.2

(
[
21
]
)
.

Let
T>s\geq 0
, and let
X_{s}
be an
\mathscr{F}_{s}
-measurable random variable with
\nu:=\mathscr{L}_{X_{s}}\in\hat{\mathscr{P}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).
Assume that for any

\mu\in\hat{\mathscr{C}}_{s,T}^{\nu},
the classical SDE
(
3.1
)
has a unique solution with initial value
X_{s}
at time
s
. If (
1.1
) for
t\in[s,T]
has a weak solution with initial distribution
\nu
at time
s
, and has pathwise uniqueness with initial value
X_{s}
at times
s
, then it has well-posedness for initial distributions in
\hat{\mathscr{P}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

3.2
The monotone case

(H_{3}^{1})

For every
t\geq 0
,
b_{t}
is continuous on
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathscr{P}_{\theta}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
b
is bounded on bounded sets in
[0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathscr{P}_{\theta}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Moreover, there exists

K\in L^{1}_{loc}([0,\infty);(0,\infty))
such that

\displaystyle\|\sigma(t,x,\mu)-\sigma(t,y,\nu)\|^{2}\leq K(t)\big\{|x-y|^{2}+\mathbb{W}_{\theta}(\mu,\nu)^{2}\big\},

\displaystyle\langle b(t,x,\mu)-b(t,y,\nu),x-y\rangle\leq K(t)\big\{|x-y|^{2}+\mathbb{W}_{\theta}(\mu,\nu)|x-y|\big\},

\displaystyle|b(t,0,\delta_{0})|+\|\sigma(t,0,\delta_{0})\|_{HS}^{2}\leq K(t),\ \ t\geq 0,x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\mu,\nu\in\mathscr{P}_{\theta}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),

where
\delta_{0}
is the Dirac measure at
0\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Under this monotone condition we have the following result essentially due to
[
51
]
, where a stronger growth condition
on
|b(t,0,\mu)|
is assumed.
See also
[
17
]
for the well-posedness under integrated Lyapunov
conditions which may cover more examples.

Theorem 3.3

(
[
51
]
)
.

Assume
(H_{3}^{1})
for some
\theta\in[1,\infty)
, and let
\sigma(t,x,\mu)
does not depend on
\mu
when
\theta<2
.

(1)

The DDSDE
(
1.1
)
has well-posedness for initial distributions in
\mathscr{P}_{\theta}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
Moreover, for any
p\geq\theta
and
s\geq 0
,
\mathbb{E}|X_{s,s}|^{p}<\infty
implies

\mathbb{E}\sup_{t\in[s,T]}|X_{s,t}|^{p}<\infty,\ \ T\geq t\geq s\geq 0.

(2)

There exists increasing
\psi:[0,\infty)\rightarrow[0,\infty)
such that for any two solutions
X_{s,t}
and
Y_{s,t}
of
(
1.1
)
with
\mathscr{L}_{X_{s,s}},\mathscr{L}_{Y_{s,s}}\in\mathscr{P}_{\theta}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,

(3.2)

\mathbb{E}|X_{s,t}-Y_{s,t}|^{\theta}\leq\big(\mathbb{E}|X_{s,s}-Y_{s,s}|^{\theta}\big)\text{\rm{e}}^{\int_{s}^{t}\psi(r)\text{\rm{d}}r},\ \ t\geq s\geq 0.

Consequently,

(3.3)

\lim_{\mathbb{E}|X_{s,s}-Y_{s,s}|^{\theta}\rightarrow 0}\mathbb{P}\Big(\sup_{r\in[s,t]}|X_{s,r}-Y_{s,r}|\geq\varepsilon\Big)=0,\ \ t>s\geq 0,\varepsilon>0;

and

(3.4)

\mathbb{W}_{\theta}(P_{s,t}^{*}\mu_{0},P_{s,t}^{*}\nu_{0})^{\theta}\leq\mathbb{W}_{2}(\mu_{0},\nu_{0})^{\theta}\text{\rm{e}}^{\int_{s}^{t}\psi(r)\text{\rm{d}}r},\ \ t\geq s\geq 0.

Proof.

We briefly explain the proof of Theorem
3.3
(1), while (2) can be easily proven by using Itô’s formula. For any
T>s\geq 0
,
\nu\in\mathscr{P}_{\theta}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
\mu\in\hat{\mathscr{C}}_{s,T}^{\nu}
,
(H_{3}^{1})
implies that (
3.1
) for
t\in[s,T]
is well-posed with initial distribution
\nu
at
s
.
Moreover, by Itô’s formula, and
(H_{3}^{1})
with
\sigma(t,x,\mu)
not depending on
\mu
when
\theta<2
, we find a large enough constant

\lambda>0
such that
\Phi_{s,T}
is contractive on
\hat{\mathscr{C}}_{s,T}^{\nu}
under the complete metric

\hat{\rho}_{s,T}(\mu,\tilde{\mu}):=\sup_{t\in[s,T]}\text{\rm{e}}^{-\lambda(t-s)}\mathbb{W}_{\theta}(\mu_{t},\tilde{\mu}_{t}),\ \ \mu,\tilde{\mu}\in\hat{C}_{s,T}^{\nu}.

Then the well-posedness follows from Theorem
3.1
. ∎

3.3
The singular case

In this part, we consider the existence and uniqueness of (
1.1
) with singular drift and non-degenerate noise.
We first introduce some results derived in
[
58
,
43
,
57
]
for distribution dependent drifts satisfying local integrability conditions in time and space but bounded in distribution,
in
[
24
]
for the case with locally integrable drifts having linear growth in distribution, and in
[
22
]
for drifts with
an integrable term and a Lipchitz term. These three situations are mutually incomparable.

3.3.1
Integrability in time-space and boundedness in distribution

When the noise is possibly degenerate, the strong/weak well-posedness will be discussed in the next section under a monotone condition.

We will consider weak solutions having finite
\phi
-moment, for
\phi
in the following class:

\mathbf{\Phi}:=\big\{\phi\in C^{\infty}([0,\infty);[1,\infty)):0\leq\phi^{\prime}\leq c\phi\ \text{for\ some\ constant\ }c>0\big\}.

Let

\mathscr{P}_{\phi}(\mathbb{R}^{d}):=\big\{\mu\in\mathscr{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d}):\|\mu\|_{\phi}:=\mu(\phi(|\cdot|))<\infty\big\},

which is equipped with the
\phi
-total variation norm

\|\mu-\nu\|_{\phi,TV}:=\sup_{|f|\leq\phi(|\cdot|)}\big|\mu(f)-\nu(f)\big|,\ \ \mu,\nu\in\mathscr{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

We denote
\|\cdot\|_{\phi,TV}
by
\|\cdot\|_{\theta,TV}
when
\phi=1+|\cdot|^{\theta}
for some
\theta\geq 0
.
For fixed
T>0
, let

\mathscr{C}_{T,\phi}=C([0,T];\mathscr{P}_{\phi}(\mathbb{R}^{d})):=\Big\{\mu:[0,T]\rightarrow\mathscr{P}_{\phi}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),\lim_{t\rightarrow s}\|\mu_{t}-\mu_{s}\|_{\phi,TV}=0,\ s\in[0,T]\Big\},

which is a complete space under the metric

\rho_{T,\phi}(\mu,\nu):=\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|\mu_{t}-\nu_{t}\|_{\phi,TV}.

For any
\mu\in\mathscr{C}_{T,\phi}
, denote

b^{\mu}(t,x):=b(t,x,\mu_{t}),\ \ \sigma^{\mu}(t,x):=\sigma(t,x,\mu_{t}),\ \ a^{\mu}(t,x):=\frac{1}{2}\big\{\sigma^{\mu}(\sigma^{\mu})^{*}\big\}(t,x),\ \ (t,x)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Definition 3.1

(Linear Functional Derivative)
.

Let
\phi\in\mathbf{\Phi}.
A function
f:\mathscr{P}_{\phi}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\rightarrow\mathbb{R}

is said to have linear functional derivative

D^{F}f:\mathscr{P}_{\phi}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\rightarrow\mathbb{R},
if it is measurable, and

(i)

D^{F}f
is measurable with

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}D^{F}f(\mu)\text{\rm{d}}\mu=0;

(ii)

For any compact
K\subset\mathscr{P}_{\phi}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,

\sup_{\mu\in K}|D^{F}f(\mu)|\leq k\phi(|\cdot|)
holds for some constant
k>0
;

(iii)

For any
\mu,\nu\in\mathscr{P}_{\phi}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),

\lim_{s\downarrow 0}\frac{f((1-s)\mu+s\nu)-f(\mu)}{s}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}D^{F}f(\mu)(y)(\nu-\mu)(\text{\rm{d}}y).

By taking
\nu=\delta_{y}
, we see that if
f
has linear functional derivative, then the convex extrinsic derivative

\tilde{D}^{E}f(\mu)(y):=\lim_{s\downarrow 0}\frac{f((1-s)\mu+s\delta_{y})-f(\mu)}{s}=D^{F}f(\mu)(y)-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}D^{F}f(\mu)(y)\text{\rm{d}}\mu

exists. See
[
39
]
for links of more derivatives in measure. For
i=1,2
, let

(3.5)

\displaystyle\mathscr{I}_{i}=\Big\{(p,q)\in(1,\infty)\times(1,\infty):\frac{d}{p}+\frac{2}{q}<i\Big\}.

Definition 3.2
.

For any
p\geq 1
, let
\tilde{L}_{p}
be the space of all measurable functions
g
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that

\|g\|_{\tilde{L}_{p}}:=\sup_{z\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bigg(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|g(x)|^{p}1_{\{|x-z|\leq 1\}}\text{\rm{d}}x\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}<\infty.

4
Regularity estimates

In this section, we introduce some results on the regularity of distributions for the DDSDE (
1.1
). We first establish the log-Harnack inequality, which implies the
``
gradient estimate” and entropy estimate, then establish the Bismut formula for the Lions derivative of the distribution, and finally study the derivative estimate
on the distribution. In the first two cases the noise does not depend on the distribution, while the last part applies also to distribution dependent noise.

4.1

The dimension-free Harnack inequality was founded in
[

for diffusion semigroups on Riemannian manifolds, and as a weaker version the log-Harnack inequality was introduced in
[

for (reflecting) diffusion processes and SDEs. See the monograph
[

]
for the study of these type inequalities and applications.
In this part, we introduce the log-Harnack inequality established in
[

]
and
[

]
for DDSDEs with non-degenerate and degenerate noise respectively. We will only consider
distribution, independent noise, since the log-Harnack inequality is not yet available for DDSDEs with distribution dependent noise.

such that

\langle D^{I}f(\mu),\phi\rangle_{L^{2}(\mu)}=D^{I}_{\phi}f(\mu),\ \ \phi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d};\mu).

We call
D^{I}f(\mu)
the intrinsic derivative of
f
at
\mu
. If
f
is intrinsic differentiable at all
\mu\in\mathscr{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, we call it intrinsic differentiable on
\mathscr{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and denote

\|D^{I}f(\mu)\|:=\|D^{I}f(\mu)\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}=\bigg(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|D^{I}f(\mu)|^{2}\text{\rm{d}}\mu\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

(2)

If
f
is intrinsic differentiable and for any
\mu\in\mathscr{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,

\lim_{\|\phi\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}\rightarrow 0}\frac{f(\mu\circ({\rm Id}+\phi)^{-1})-f(\mu)-D^{I}_{\phi}f(\mu)}{\|\phi\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}}=0,

we call
f

L
-differentiable on
\mathscr{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. In this case,
D^{I}f(\mu)
is also denoted by
D^{L}f(\mu)
, and is called the
L
-derivative of
f
at
\mu
.

Intrinsic derivative was first introduced in
[
1
]
in the configuration space over a Riemannian manifold, while the
L
-derivative appeared in the Lecture notes
[
9
]
for the study of mean field games and is also called Lions derivative in references.

Note that the derivative
D^{I}f(\mu)\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d};\mu)
is
\mu
-a.e. defined. In applications, we take its continuous version if exists.
The following classes of
L
-differentiable functions are often used in analysis:

(a)

f\in C^{1}(\mathscr{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})):
if
f
is
L
-differentiable such that for every

,
there exists a
\mu
-version

such that

is jointly continuous in
(x,\mu)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathscr{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

(b)

if
f\in C^{1}(\mathscr{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
and

D^{L}f(\mu)(x)
is bounded.

(c)

f\in C^{2}(\mathscr{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})):
if
f\in C^{1}(\mathscr{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
and
Df(\mu)(x)
is
L
-differentiable in
\mu
and differentiable in

, such that

and

are jointly continuous in

(d)

,

are jointly
continuous in

If moreover these derivatives are bounded, we denote

f\in C_{b}^{1,1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathscr{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})).

(f)

f\in C^{2,2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathscr{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
, if
f
is a continuous function on
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathscr{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

for

f(x,\cdot)\in C^{2}(\mathscr{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
for

exists, and all derivatives

\displaystyle\nabla f(x,\mu),\ \nabla^{2}f(x,\mu),\ D^{L}f(x,\mu)(y),\ (D^{L}\nabla f)(x,\mu)(y)

\displaystyle\nabla\{D^{L}f(x,\mu)(\cdot)\}(y),\ (D^{L})^{2}f(x,\mu)(y,z)

are jointly
continuous in

If moreover these derivatives are bounded, we denote

5
Exponential ergodicity in entropy

The convergence in entropy for stochastic systems is an important topic in both probability theory and mathematical physics, and has been well studied for Markov processes by using the log-Sobolev inequality, see for instance
[
7
]
and references therein. However, the existing results derived in the literature do not apply to DDSDEs.
In 2003, Carrillo, McCann and Villani
[
11
]
proved the exponential convergence in a mean field entropy of the following granular media equation for probability density functions
(\rho_{t})_{t\geq 0}
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
:

(5.1)

\partial_{t}\rho_{t}=\Delta\rho_{t}+{\rm div}\big\{\rho_{t}\nabla(V+W*\rho_{t})\big\},

where the internal potential
V\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
satisfies
\text{\rm{Hess}}_{V}\geq\lambda I_{d\times d}
for a constant
\lambda>0
and the
d\times d
-unit matrix
I_{d\times d}
, and the interaction potential
W\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
satisfies

W(-x)=W(x)
and
\text{\rm{Hess}}_{W}\geq-\delta I_{d\times d}

for some constant
\delta\in[0,\lambda/2)
. Recall that we write
M\geq\lambda I_{d}
for a constant
\lambda
and a
d\times d
-matrix
M
, if
\langle Mv,v\rangle\geq\lambda|v|^{2}
holds for any
v\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
To introduce the mean field entropy, let
\mu_{V}(\text{\rm{d}}x):=\frac{\text{\rm{e}}^{-V(x)}\text{\rm{d}}x}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\text{\rm{e}}^{-V(x)}\text{\rm{d}}x}
, recall the classical relative entropy

{\rm Ent}(\nu|\mu):=\begin{cases}\mu(\rho\log\rho),&\text{if}\ \nu=\rho\mu,\\
\infty,&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

for
\mu,\nu\in\mathscr{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, and consider the free energy functional

E^{V,W}(\mu):={\rm Ent}(\mu|\mu_{V})+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}W(x-y)\mu(\text{\rm{d}}x)\mu(\text{\rm{d}}y),\ \ \mu\in\mathscr{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),

where we set
E^{V,W}(\mu)=\infty
if either
{\rm Ent}(\mu|\mu_{V})=\infty
or the integral term is not well defined. Then the
associated mean field entropy
{\rm Ent}^{V,W}

is defined by

(5.2)

{\rm Ent}^{V,W}(\mu):=E^{V,W}(\mu)-\inf_{\nu\in\mathscr{P}}E^{V,W}(\nu),\ \ \mu\in\mathscr{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

According to
[
11
]
, for
V
and
W
satisfying the above mentioned conditions,
E^{V,W}
has a unique minimizer
\mu_{\infty},
and
\mu_{t}(\text{\rm{d}}x):=\rho_{t}(x)\text{\rm{d}}x
for probability density
\rho_{t}
solving (
5.1
) converges to
\mu_{\infty}
exponentially in the mean field entropy:

{\rm Ent}^{V,W}(\mu_{t})\leq\text{\rm{e}}^{-(\lambda-2\delta)t}{\rm Ent}^{V,W}(\mu_{0}),\ \ t\geq 0.

Recently, this result was generalized in
[
15
]
by establishing the uniform log-Sobolev inequality for the associated mean field particle systems, such that
{\rm Ent}^{V,W}(\mu_{t})
decays exponentially for a class of non-convex
V\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
W\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
, where
W(x,y)=W(y,x)
and
\mu_{t}(\text{\rm{d}}x):=\rho_{t}(x)\text{\rm{d}}x
for
\rho_{t}
solving the nonlinear PDE

(5.3)

\partial_{t}\rho_{t}=\Delta\rho_{t}+{\rm div}\big\{\rho_{t}\nabla(V+W\circledast\rho_{t})\big\},

where

(5.4)

W\circledast\rho_{t}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}W(\cdot,y)\rho_{t}(y)\text{\rm{d}}y.

In this case,
{\rm Ent}^{V,W}
is defined in (
5.2
) for the free energy functional

E^{V,W}(\mu):={\rm Ent}(\mu|\mu_{V})+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}W(x,y)\mu(\text{\rm{d}}x)\mu(\text{\rm{d}}y),\ \ \mu\in\mathscr{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

To study (
5.3
) using probability methods, we consider the
following DDSDE with initial distribution
\mu_{0}
:

(5.5)

\text{\rm{d}}X_{t}=\sqrt{2}\text{\rm{d}}B_{t}-\nabla\big\{V+W\circledast\mathscr{L}_{X_{t}}\big\}(X_{t})\text{\rm{d}}t,

where
B_{t}
is the
d
-dimensional Brownian motion,
\mathscr{L}_{X_{t}}
is the distribution of
X_{t}
, and

(5.6)

(W\circledast\mu)(x):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}W(x,y)\mu(\text{\rm{d}}y),\ \ x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\mu\in\mathscr{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

provided the integral exists.
Let
\rho_{t}(x)=\frac{(\mathscr{L}_{X_{t}})(\text{\rm{d}}x)}{\text{\rm{d}}x},\ t\geq 0.
By Itô’s formula and the integration by parts formula, we have

\displaystyle\frac{\text{\rm{d}}}{\text{\rm{d}}t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(\rho_{t}f)(x)\text{\rm{d}}x=\frac{\text{\rm{d}}}{\text{\rm{d}}t}\mathbb{E}[f(X_{t})]=\mathbb{E}\big[\big(\Delta-\nabla V-\nabla\{W\circledast\rho_{t}\}\big)f(X_{t})\big]

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho_{t}(x)\big\{\Delta f-\langle\nabla V+\nabla\{W\circledast\rho_{t}\},\nabla f\rangle\big\}(x)\text{\rm{d}}x

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(x)\{\Delta\rho_{t}+{\rm div}[\rho_{t}\nabla V+\rho_{t}\nabla(W\circledast\rho_{t})]\big\}(x)\text{\rm{d}}x,\ \ t\geq 0,\ f\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

Therefore,
\rho_{t}
solves (
5.3
). On the other hand, by this fact and the uniqueness of (
5.3
) and (
5.5
), if
\rho_{t}
solves (
5.3
) with
\mu_{0}(\text{\rm{d}}x):=\rho_{0}(x)\text{\rm{d}}x
,
then
\rho_{t}(x)\text{\rm{d}}x=\mathscr{L}_{X_{t}}(\text{\rm{d}}x)
for
X_{t}
solving (
5.5
) with
\mathscr{L}_{X_{0}}=\mu_{0}.

To extend the study of
[
11
,
15
]
, we investigate the exponential convergence in entropy for the following DDSDE on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
:

(5.7)

\text{\rm{d}}X_{t}=b(X_{t},\mathscr{L}_{X_{t}})\text{\rm{d}}t+\sigma(X_{t})\text{\rm{d}}W_{t},

where
W_{t}
is the
m
-dimensional Brownian motion on a complete filtration probability space
(\Omega,\{\mathscr{F}_{t}\}_{t\geq 0},\mathbb{P})
,

\sigma:\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}\otimes\mathbb{R}^{m},\ \ b:\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathscr{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}

are measurable.

Unlike in
[
11
,
15
]
where the mean field particle systems are used to estimate the mean field entropy, we use
the log-Harnack inequality introduced in
[
49
,
42
]
and the Talagrand inequality developed in
[
46
,
7
,
35
]
.
Since the log-Harnack inequality is not yet available when
\sigma
depends on the distribution, in (
5.7
) we only consider distribution-free
\sigma
.

In the following subsections, we first present a criterion on the exponential convergence for DDSDEs by using the log-Harnack and Talagrand inequalities, then prove the exponential convergence for granular media type equations which generalizes the framework of
[
15
]
, and finally consider exponential convergence for (
5.7
) with non-degenerate and degenerate noises respectively.

5.1
A criterion with application to Granular media type equations

In general, we consider the following DDSDE:

(5.8)

\text{\rm{d}}X_{t}=\sigma(X_{t},\mathscr{L}_{X_{t}})\text{\rm{d}}W_{t}+b(X_{t},\mathscr{L}_{X_{t}})\text{\rm{d}}t,

where
W_{t}
is the
m
-dimensional Brownian motion and

\sigma:\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathscr{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}\otimes\mathbb{R}^{m},\ \ b:\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathscr{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}

are measurable. We assume that this SDE is strongly and weakly well-posed
for square integrable initial values. It is in particular the case if
b
is continuous on
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathscr{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and there exists a constant
K>0
such that

(5.9)

\begin{split}&\langle b(x,\mu)-b(y,\nu),x-y\rangle^{+}+\|\sigma(x,\mu)-\sigma(y,\nu)\|^{2}\leq K\big\{|x-y|^{2}+\mathbb{W}_{2}(\mu,\nu)^{2}\},\\
&|b(0,\mu)|\leq K\Big(1+\sqrt{\mu(|\cdot|^{2})}\Big),\ \ x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\mu,\nu\in\mathscr{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),\end{split}

see for instance
[
51
]
. See also
[
24
,
58
]
and references therein for the well-posedness of DDSDEs with singular coefficients. For any
\mu\in\mathscr{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, let
P_{t}^{*}\mu=\mathscr{L}_{X_{t}}
for the solution
X_{t}
with initial distribution
\mathscr{L}_{X_{0}}=\mu
. Let

P_{t}f(\mu)=\mathbb{E}[f(X_{t})]=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f\text{\rm{d}}P_{t}^{*}\mu,\ \ t\geq 0,f\in\mathscr{B}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

We have the following equivalence on the exponential convergence of
P_{t}^{*}\mu
in
{\rm Ent}
and
\mathbb{W}_{2}
.

Theorem 5.1

(
[
41
]
)
.

Assume that
P_{t}^{*}
has a unique invariant probability measure
\mu_{\infty}\in\mathscr{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that for some constants
t_{0},c_{0},C>0
we have the log-Harnack inequality

(5.10)

P_{t_{0}}(\log f)(\nu)\leq\log P_{t_{0}}f(\mu)+c_{0}\mathbb{W}_{2}(\mu,\nu)^{2},\ \ \mu,\nu\in\mathscr{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),f\in\mathscr{B}^{+}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

and the Talagrand inequality

(5.11)

\mathbb{W}_{2}(\mu,\mu_{\infty})^{2}\leq C{\rm Ent}(\mu|\mu_{\infty}),\ \ \mu\in\mathscr{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

(1)

If there exist constants
c_{1},\lambda,t_{1}\geq 0
such that

(5.12)

\mathbb{W}_{2}(P_{t}^{*}\mu,\mu_{\infty})^{2}\leq c_{1}\text{\rm{e}}^{-\lambda t}\mathbb{W}_{2}(\mu,\mu_{\infty})^{2},\ \ t\geq t_{1},\mu\in\mathscr{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),

then

(5.13)

\begin{split}&\max\big\{c_{0}^{-1}{\rm Ent}(P_{t}^{*}\mu|\mu_{\infty}),\mathbb{W}_{2}(P_{t}^{*}\mu,\mu_{\infty})^{2}\big\}\\
&\leq c_{1}\text{\rm{e}}^{-\lambda(t-t_{0})}\min\big\{\mathbb{W}_{2}(\mu,\mu_{\infty})^{2},C{\rm Ent}(\mu|\mu_{\infty})\big\},\ \ t\geq t_{0}+t_{1},\mu\in\mathscr{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).\end{split}

(2)

If for some constants
\lambda,c_{2},t_{2}>0

(5.14)

{\rm Ent}(P_{t}^{*}\mu|\mu_{\infty})\leq c_{2}\text{\rm{e}}^{-\lambda t}{\rm Ent}(\mu|\mu_{\infty}),\ \ t\geq t_{2},\mu\in\mathscr{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),

then

(5.15)

\begin{split}&\max\big\{{\rm Ent}(P_{t}^{*}\mu,\mu_{\infty}),C^{-1}\mathbb{W}_{2}(P_{t}^{*}\mu,\mu_{\infty})^{2}\big\}\\
&\leq c_{2}\text{\rm{e}}^{-\lambda(t-t_{0})}\min\big\{c_{0}\mathbb{W}_{2}(\mu,\mu_{\infty})^{2},{\rm Ent}(\mu|\mu_{\infty})\big\},\ \ t\geq t_{0}+t_{2},\mu\in\mathscr{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).\end{split}

When
\sigma\sigma^{*}
is invertible and does not depend on the distribution, the log-Harnack inequality (
5.10
) has been established in
[
51
]
.
The Talagrand inequality was first found in
[
46
]
for
\mu_{\infty}
being the Gaussian measure, and extended in
[
7
]
to

\mu_{\infty}
satisfying the log-Sobolev inequality

(5.16)

\mu_{\infty}(f^{2}\log f^{2})\leq C\mu_{\infty}(|\nabla f|^{2}),\ \ f\in C_{b}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),\mu_{\infty}(f^{2})=1,

see
[
35
]
for an earlier result under a curvature condition, and see
[
48
]
for further extensions.

To illustrate this result, we consider the granular media type equation for probability density functions
(\rho_{t})_{t\geq 0}
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
:

(5.17)

\partial_{t}\rho_{t}={\rm div}\big\{a\nabla\rho_{t}+\rho_{t}a\nabla(V+W\circledast\rho_{t})\big\},

where
W\circledast\rho_{t}
is in (
5.4
), and the functions

a:\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}\otimes\mathbb{R}^{d},\ \ V:\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R},\ \ W:\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}

satisfy the following assumptions.

(H_{5}^{1})

a:=(a_{ij})_{1\leq i,j\leq d}\in C_{b}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}\otimes\mathbb{R}^{d})
, and

a\geq\lambda_{a}I_{d\times d}
for some constant
\lambda_{a}>0
.

(H_{5}^{2})

V\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),W\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
with
W(x,y)=W(y,x)
, and there exist constants
\kappa_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
and
\kappa_{1},\kappa_{2},\kappa_{0}^{\prime}>0
such that

(5.18)

\text{\rm{Hess}}_{V}\geq\kappa_{0}I_{d\times d},\ \ \kappa_{0}^{\prime}I_{2d\times 2d}\geq\text{\rm{Hess}}_{W}\geq\kappa_{0}I_{2d\times 2d},

(5.19)

\langle x,\nabla V(x)\rangle\geq\kappa_{1}|x|^{2}-\kappa_{2},\ \ x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Moreover, for any
\lambda>0
,

(5.20)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}\text{\rm{e}}^{-V(x)-V(y)-\lambda W(x,y)}\text{\rm{d}}x\text{\rm{d}}y<\infty.

(H_{5}^{3})

There exists a function
b_{0}\in L^{1}_{loc}([0,\infty))
with

r_{0}:=\frac{\|\text{\rm{Hess}}_{W}\|_{\infty}}{4}\int_{0}^{\infty}\text{\rm{e}}^{\frac{1}{4}\int_{0}^{t}b_{0}(s)\text{\rm{d}}s}\text{\rm{d}}t<1

such that for any
x,y,z\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\displaystyle\big\langle y-x,\nabla V(x)-\nabla V(y)+\nabla W(\cdot,z)(x)-\nabla W(\cdot,z)(y)\big\rangle\leq|x-y|b_{0}(|x-y|).

For any
N\geq 2
, consider the Hamiltonian for the system of
N
particles:

H_{N}(x_{1},\cdots,x_{N})=\sum_{i=1}^{N}V(x_{i})+\frac{1}{N-1}\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq N}^{N}W(x_{i},x_{j}),

and the corresponding finite-dimensional Gibbs measure

\mu^{(N)}(\text{\rm{d}}x_{1},\cdots,\text{\rm{d}}x_{N})=\frac{1}{Z_{N}}\text{\rm{e}}^{-H_{N}(x_{1},\cdots,x_{N})}\text{\rm{d}}x_{1}\cdots\text{\rm{d}}x_{N},

where
Z_{N}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{dN}}\text{\rm{e}}^{-H_{N}(x)}\text{\rm{d}}x<\infty
due to (
5.20
) in
(H_{2})
.
For any
1\leq i\leq N
, the conditional marginal of
\mu^{(N)}
given
z\in\mathbb{R}^{d(N-1)}
is given by

\displaystyle\mu^{(N)}_{z}(\text{\rm{d}}x):=\frac{1}{Z_{N}(z)}\text{\rm{e}}^{-H_{N}(x|z)}\text{\rm{d}}x,\ \ Z_{N}(z):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\text{\rm{e}}^{-H_{N}(x|z)}\text{\rm{d}}x,

\displaystyle H_{N}(x|z):=V(x)-\log\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d(N-1)}}\text{\rm{e}}^{-\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}\{V(z_{i})+\frac{1}{N-1}W(x,z_{i})\}}\text{\rm{d}}z_{1}\cdots\text{\rm{d}}z_{N-1}.

We have the following result.

Theorem 5.2

(
[
41
]
)
.

Assume
(H_{5}^{1})
-
(H_{5}^{3})
. If there is a constant
\beta>0
such that the uniform log-Sobolev inequality

(5.21)

\mu^{(N)}_{z}(f^{2}\log f^{2})\leq\frac{1}{\beta}\mu^{(N)}_{z}(|\nabla f|^{2}),\ \ f\in C_{b}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),\mu^{(N)}_{z}(f^{2})=1,N\geq 2,z\in\mathbb{R}^{d(N-1)}

holds,
then there exists a unique
\mu_{\infty}\in\mathscr{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and a constant
c>0
such that

(5.22)

\mathbb{W}_{2}(\mu_{t},\mu_{\infty})^{2}+{\rm Ent}(\mu_{t}|\mu_{\infty})\leq c\text{\rm{e}}^{-\lambda_{a}\beta(1-r_{0})^{2}t}\min\big\{\mathbb{W}_{2}(\mu_{0},\mu_{\infty})^{2}+{\rm Ent}(\mu_{0}|\mu_{\infty})\big\},\ \ t\geq 1

holds for any probability density functions
(\rho_{t})_{t\geq 0}
solving (
5.17
), where
\mu_{t}(\text{\rm{d}}x):=\rho_{t}(x)\text{\rm{d}}x,t\geq 0.

This result allows
V
and W to be non-convex. For instance, let
V=V_{1}+V_{2}\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that

\|V_{1}\|_{\infty}\land\|\nabla V_{1}\|_{\infty}<\infty
,
\text{\rm{Hess}}_{V_{2}}\geq\lambda I_{d\times d}
for some
\lambda>0
, and
W\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
with
\|W\|_{\infty}\land\|\nabla W\|_{\infty}<\infty
. Then the uniform log-Sobolev inequality
(
5.21
) holds for some constant
\beta>0
.

5.2
The non-degenerate case

In this part, we make the following assumptions:

(H_{5}^{4})

b
is continuous on
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathscr{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and there exists a constant
K>0
such that
(
5.9
)
holds.

(H_{5}^{5})

\sigma\sigma^{*}
is invertible with
\lambda:=\|(\sigma\sigma^{*})^{-1}\|_{\infty}<\infty
, and there exist constants
K_{2}>K_{1}\geq 0
such that for any
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\mu,\nu\in\mathscr{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,

\|\sigma(x)-\sigma(y)\|_{HS}^{2}+2\langle b(x,\mu)-b(y,\nu),x-y\rangle\leq K_{1}\mathbb{W}_{2}(\mu,\nu)^{2}-K_{2}|x-y|^{2}.

According to Theorem
3.3
, if
(H_{3}^{1})
holds, then for any initial value
X_{0}\in L^{2}(\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathscr{F}_{0},\mathbb{P})
, (
5.7
) has a unique solution which satisfies

\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|X_{t}|^{2}\Big]<\infty,\ \ T\in(0,\infty).

Let
P_{t}^{*}\mu=\mathscr{L}_{X_{t}}
for the solution
X_{t}
with
\mathscr{L}_{X_{0}}=\mu.

We have the following result.

Theorem 5.3

(
[
41
]
)
.

Assume
(H_{5}^{4})
and
(H_{5}^{5})
. Then
P_{t}^{*}
has a unique invariant probability measure
\mu_{\infty}
such that

(5.23)

\max\big\{\mathbb{W}_{2}(P_{t}^{*}\mu,\mu_{\infty})^{2},{\rm Ent}(P_{t}^{*}\mu|\mu_{\infty})\big\}\leq\frac{c_{1}}{t\land 1}\text{\rm{e}}^{-(K_{2}-K_{1})t}\mathbb{W}_{2}(\mu,\mu_{\infty})^{2},\ \ t>0,\mu\in\mathscr{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

holds for some constant
c_{1}>0
.
If moreover
\sigma\in C_{b}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}\otimes\mathbb{R}^{m})
, then there exists a constant
c_{2}>0
such that for any
\mu\in\mathscr{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),t\geq 1
,

(5.24)

\max\big\{\mathbb{W}_{2}(P_{t}^{*}\mu,\mu_{\infty})^{2},{\rm Ent}(P_{t}^{*}\mu|\mu_{\infty})\big\}\leq c_{2}\text{\rm{e}}^{-(K_{2}-K_{1})t}\min\big\{\mathbb{W}_{2}(\mu,\mu_{\infty})^{2},{\rm Ent}(\mu|\mu_{\infty})\big\}.

To illustrate this result, we consider the granular media equation (
5.3
), for which we take

(5.25)

\sigma=\sqrt{2}I_{d\times d},~~~~~~b(x,\mu)=-\nabla\big\{V+W\circledast\mu\big\}(x),\ \ (x,\mu)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathscr{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

The following example is not included by Theorem
5.2
since the function
W
may be non-symmetric.

Example 5.1 (Granular media equation).

Consider (
5.3
) with
V\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
W\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
satisfying

(5.26)

\text{\rm{Hess}}_{V}\geq\lambda I_{d\times d},\ \ \text{\rm{Hess}}_{W}\geq\delta_{1}I_{2d\times 2d},\ \ \|\text{\rm{Hess}}_{W}\|\leq\delta_{2}

for some constants
\lambda_{1},\delta_{2}>0
and
\delta_{1}\in\mathbb{R}
.
If
\lambda+\delta_{1}-\delta_{2}>0
, then there exists a unique
\mu_{\infty}\in\mathscr{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and a
constant
c>0
such that for any probability density functions
(\rho_{t})_{t\geq 0}
solving (
5.3
),
\mu_{t}(\text{\rm{d}}x):=\rho_{t}(x)\text{\rm{d}}x
satisfies

(5.27)

\begin{split}&\max\big\{\mathbb{W}_{2}(\mu_{t},\mu_{\infty})^{2},{\rm Ent}(\mu_{t}|\mu_{\infty})\big\}\\
&\leq c\text{\rm{e}}^{-(\lambda+\delta_{1}-\delta_{2})t}\min\big\{\mathbb{W}_{2}(\mu_{0},\mu_{\infty})^{2},{\rm Ent}(\mu_{0}|\mu_{\infty})\big\},\ \ t\geq 1.\end{split}

Proof.

Let
\sigma
and
b
be in (
5.25
). Then (
5.26
) implies

(H_{3}^{1})
and

\langle b(x,\mu)-b(y,\nu),x-y\rangle\leq-(\lambda_{1}+\delta_{1})|x-y|^{2}+\delta_{2}|x-y|\mathbb{W}_{1}(\mu,\nu),

where we have used the formula

\mathbb{W}_{1}(\mu,\nu)=\sup\{\mu(f)-\nu(f):\ \|\nabla f\|_{\infty}\leq 1\}.

So, by taking
\alpha=\frac{\delta_{2}}{2}
and noting that
\mathbb{W}_{1}\leq\mathbb{W}_{2}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\langle b(x,\mu)-b(y,\nu),x-y\rangle\leq-\big(\lambda+\delta_{1}-\alpha\big)|x-y|^{2}+\frac{\delta_{2}^{2}}{4\alpha}\mathbb{W}_{1}(\mu,\nu)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq-\Big(\lambda+\delta_{1}-\frac{\delta_{2}}{2}\Big)|x-y|^{2}+\frac{\delta_{2}}{2}\mathbb{W}_{2}(\mu,\nu)^{2},\ \ x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\mu,\nu\in\mathscr{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

Therefore, if (
5.26
) holds for
\lambda+\delta_{1}-\delta_{2}>0
, Theorem
5.3
implies that
P_{t}^{*}
has a unique invariant probability measure
\mu_{\infty}\in\mathscr{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, such that
(
5.27
) holds for
\mu_{0}\in\mathscr{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
When
\mu_{0}\notin\mathscr{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, we have
\mathbb{W}_{2}(\mu_{0},\mu_{\infty})^{2}=\infty
since
\mu_{\infty}\in\mathscr{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
Combining this with the Talagrand inequality

\mathbb{W}_{2}(\mu_{0},\mu_{\infty})^{2}\leq C{\rm Ent}(\mu_{0}|\mu_{\infty})

for some constant
C>0
, see the proof of Theorem
5.3
, we have
{\rm Ent}(\mu_{0}|\mu_{\infty})=\infty
for
\mu_{0}\notin\mathscr{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, so that (
5.27
) holds for all
\mu_{0}\in\mathscr{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.∎

5.3
The degenerate case

When
\mathbb{R}^{k}
with some
k\in\mathbb{N}
is considered, to emphasize the space we use
\mathscr{P}(\mathbb{R}^{k})
(
\mathscr{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{k})
) to denote the class of probability measures (with finite second moment) on
\mathbb{R}^{k}
.
Consider the following McKean-Vlasov stochastic Hamiltonian system for
(X_{t},Y_{t})\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}+d_{2}}:=\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}\times\mathbb{R}^{d_{2}}:

(5.28)

\begin{cases}\text{\rm{d}}X_{t}=BY_{t}\text{\rm{d}}t,\\
\text{\rm{d}}Y_{t}=\sqrt{2}\text{\rm{d}}W_{t}-\Big\{B^{*}\nabla V(\cdot,\mathscr{L}_{(X_{t},Y_{t})})(X_{t})+\beta B^{*}(BB^{*})^{-1}X_{t}+Y_{t}\Big\}\text{\rm{d}}t,\end{cases}

where
\beta>0
is a constant,
B
is a
d_{1}\times d_{2}
-matrix such that
BB^{*}
is invertible, and

V:\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}\times\mathscr{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}+d_{2}})\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d_{2}}

is measurable.
Let

\displaystyle{\psi_{B}}((x,y),(\bar{x},\bar{y})):=\sqrt{|x-\bar{x}|^{2}+|B(y-\bar{y})|^{2}},\ \ (x,y),(\bar{x},\bar{y})\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}+d_{2}},

\displaystyle\mathbb{W}_{2}^{\psi_{B}}(\mu,\nu):=\inf_{\pi\in\mathscr{C}(\mu,\nu)}\bigg\{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}+d_{2}}\times\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}+d_{2}}}{\psi_{B}}^{2}\text{\rm{d}}\pi\bigg\}^{\frac{1}{2}},\ \ \mu,\nu\in\mathscr{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}+d_{2}}).

We assume

(H_{5}^{6})

V(x,\mu)
is differentiable in
x
such that
\nabla V(\cdot,\mu)(x)
is Lipschitz continuous in
(x,\mu)\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}\times\mathscr{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}+d_{2}}).

Moreover, there exist constants
\theta_{1},\theta_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
with

(5.29)

\theta_{1}+\theta_{2}<\beta,

such that for any
(x,y),(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}+d_{2}}
and
\mu,\mu^{\prime}\in\mathscr{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}+d_{2}})
,

6
Donsker-Varadhan large deviations

The LDP (large deviation principle) is a fundamental tool characterizing the asymptotic behaviour of probability measures
\{\mu_{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon>0}
on a topological space
E
, see
[
13
]
and references within.
Recall that
\mu_{\varepsilon}
for small
\varepsilon>0
is said to
satisfy the LDP with speed
\lambda(\varepsilon)\rightarrow+\infty
(as
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
) and rate
function
I:E\rightarrow[0,+\infty]
, if
I
has compact level sets (i.e.
\{I\leq r\}
is compact for
r\in{\mathbb{R}}^{+}
),
and for any Borel subset
A
of
E
,

-\inf_{A^{o}}I\leq\liminf_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\frac{1}{\lambda(\varepsilon)}\log\mu_{\varepsilon}(A)\leq\limsup_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\frac{1}{\lambda(\varepsilon)}\log\mu_{\varepsilon}(A)\leq-\inf_{\bar{A}}I,

where
A^{o}
and
\bar{A}
stand for the
interior and the closure of
A
in
E
respectively.

In this part, we consider the Donsker-Varadhan type long time LDP
[
12
]
for
\mu_{\varepsilon}:=\mathscr{L}_{L_{\varepsilon^{-1}}},
where

L_{t}:=\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\delta_{X(s)}\text{\rm{d}}s,\ \ t>0

is the empirical measure for a path-distribution dependent SPDE.

Let
(\mathbb{H},\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle,|\cdot|)
be a separable Hilbert space. For a fixed constant
r_{0}>0
,
a path
\xi\in\mathscr{C}:=C([-r_{0},0];\mathbb{H})
stands for a sample of the history with time length
r_{0}
. Recall that
\mathscr{C}
is a Banach space with the uniform norm

\|\xi\|_{\infty}:=\sup_{\theta\in[-r_{0},0]}|\xi(\theta)|,\ \ \xi\in\mathscr{C}.

For any map
\xi(\cdot):[-r_{0},\infty)\rightarrow\mathbb{H}
and any time
t\geq 0
, its segment
\xi_{\cdot}:[0,\infty)\rightarrow\mathscr{C}
is defined by

\xi_{t}(\theta):=\xi(t+\theta),\ \ \theta\in[-r_{0},0],t\geq 0.

Let
\mathscr{P}(\mathscr{C})
denote the space of all probability measures on
\mathscr{C}
equipped with the weak topology, and let

\mathscr{L}_{\eta}
stand for the distribution of a random variable
\eta
.
Consider the following path-distribution dependent SPDE on
\mathbb{H}
:

(6.1)

\text{\rm{d}}X(t)=\{AX(t)+b(X_{t},\mathscr{L}_{X_{t}})\}\text{\rm{d}}t+\sigma(\mathscr{L}_{X_{t}})\text{\rm{d}}W(t),~~t\geq 0,

where

\bullet

(A,\mathscr{D}(A))
is a negative definite self-adjoint operator on
\mathbb{H}
;

\bullet

W(t)
is the cylindrical Brownian motion on a separable Hilbert space
\tilde{\mathbb{H}}
; i.e.

W(t)=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}B_{i}(t)\tilde{e}_{i},\ \ t\geq 0

for an orthonormal basis
\{\tilde{e}_{i}\}_{i\geq 1}
on
\tilde{\mathbb{H}}
and a sequence of independent one-dimensional
Brownian motions
\{B_{i}\}_{i\geq 1}

on a complete filtration probability space
(\Omega,\mathscr{F},\{\mathscr{F}_{t}\}_{t\geq 0},\mathbb{P})
,
where
\mathscr{F}_{0}
is rich enough such that for any
\pi\in\mathscr{P}(\mathscr{C}\times\mathscr{C})
there exists a
\mathscr{C}\times\mathscr{C}
-valued random variable
\xi
on
(\Omega,\mathscr{F}_{0},\mathbb{P})
such that

.

\bullet

b:\mathscr{C}\times\mathscr{P}(\mathscr{C})\rightarrow\mathbb{H},\ \ \sigma:\mathscr{P}(\mathscr{C})\rightarrow\mathbb{L}(\tilde{\mathbb{H}};\mathbb{H})
are
measurable.

Let
X_{t}^{\nu}
denote the mild segment solution with initial distribution

Definition 6.1
.

Let
\mathscr{P}(\mathscr{C})
be equipped with the weak topology, let
\mathscr{A}\subset\mathscr{P}(\mathscr{C})
, and let

(1)

if for any closed

\limsup_{t\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{t}\sup_{\nu\in\mathscr{A}}\log\mathbb{P}(L_{t}^{\nu}\in A)\leq-\inf_{A}J.

(2)

\{L_{t}^{\nu}\}_{\nu\in\mathscr{A}}
is said to satisfy the lower bound uniform LDP with rate function
J
, denoted by
\{L_{t}^{\nu}\}_{\nu\in\mathscr{A}}\in LDP_{l}(J),

(3)

\{L_{t}^{\nu}\}_{\nu\in\mathscr{A}}
is said to satisfy the uniform LDP with rate function

To state establish the LDP, we recall the Feller property, the strong Feller property and the irreducibility for a (sub-) Markov operator

7
Comparison theorem

The order preservation of stochastic processes is a crucial property for one to compare a complicated process with simpler ones, and a result to ensure this property is called
``
comparison theorem” in the literature. There are two different type order preservations, one is in the distribution (weak) sense and the other is in the pathwise (strong) sense, where the latter implies the former.
The weak order preservation has been investigated for diffusion-jump Markov processes in
[
10
,
54
]
and references within, as well as a class of super processes in
[
53
]
. There are also plentiful results on the strong order preservation, see, for instance,

[
4
,
14
,
26
,
32
,
34
,
56
]
and references within for comparison theorems on forward/backward SDEs (stochastic differential equations), with jumps and/or with memory. Recently, sufficient and necessary conditions have been derived in
[
20
]
for the order preservation of SDEs with memory.

On the other hand, path-distribution dependent SDEs have been investigated in
[
19
]
, see also
[
51
]
and references within for distribution-dependent SDEs without memory. In this section, sufficient and necessary conditions of the order preservations for path-distribution dependent SDEs are presented.

Let
r_{0}\geq 0
be a constant and
d\geq 1
be a natural number. The path space

{\bf C}=C([-r_{0},0];\mathbb{R}^{d})
is Polish under the uniform norm
\|\cdot\|_{\infty}
.
For any continuous map
f:[-r_{0},\infty)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
and

t\geq 0
, let
f_{t}\in{\bf C}
be such that
f_{t}(\theta)=f(\theta+t)
for
\theta\in[-r_{0},0]
. We call
(f_{t})_{t\geq 0}
the segment of
(f(t))_{t\geq-r_{0}}.

Next, let
\mathscr{P}({\bf C})
be the set of probability measures on
{\bf C}
equipped with the weak topology.
Finally, let
W(t)
be an
m
-dimensional Brownian motion on a complete
filtration probability space
(\Omega,\{\mathscr{F}_{t}\}_{t\geq 0},\mathbb{P})
.

We consider the following Distribution-dependent SDEs with memory:

(7.1)

\begin{cases}\text{\rm{d}}X(t)=b(t,X_{t},\mathscr{L}_{X_{t}})\,\text{\rm{d}}t+\sigma(t,X_{t},\mathscr{L}_{X_{t}})\,\text{\rm{d}}W(t),\\
\text{\rm{d}}\bar{X}(t)=\bar{b}(t,\bar{X}_{t},\mathscr{L}_{\bar{X}_{t}})\,\text{\rm{d}}t+\bar{\sigma}(t,\bar{X}_{t},\mathscr{L}_{\bar{X}_{t}})\,\text{\rm{d}}W(t),\end{cases}

where

b,\bar{b}:[0,\infty)\times\mathscr{C}\times\mathscr{P}({\bf C})\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d};\ \ \sigma,\bar{\sigma}:[0,\infty)\times\mathscr{C}\times\mathscr{P}({\bf C})\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}\otimes\mathbb{R}^{m}

are measurable.

For any
s\geq 0
and
\mathscr{F}_{s}
-measurable
{\bf C}
-valued random variables
\xi,\bar{\xi}

) for

(X(t),\bar{X}(t))_{t\geq s}
such that for all

\begin{split}&X(t)=\xi(0)+\int_{s}^{t}b(r,X_{r},\mathscr{L}_{X_{r}})\text{\rm{d}}r+\int_{s}^{t}\sigma(r,X_{r},\mathscr{L}_{X_{r}})\text{\rm{d}}W(r),\\
&\bar{X}(t)=\bar{\xi}(0)+\int_{s}^{t}\bar{b}(r,\bar{X}_{r},\mathscr{L}_{\bar{X}_{r}})\text{\rm{d}}r+\int_{s}^{t}\bar{\sigma}(r,\bar{X}_{r},\mathscr{L}_{\bar{X}_{r}})\text{\rm{d}}W(r),\end{split}

where
(X_{t},\bar{X}_{t})_{t\geq s}
is the segment process of
(X(t),\bar{X}(t))_{t\geq s-r_{0}}
with

(X_{s},\bar{X}_{s})=(\xi,\bar{\xi})
.

Following the line of
[
19
]
, we consider the class of probability measures of finite second moment:

\mathscr{P}_{2}({\bf C})=\bigg\{\nu\in\mathscr{P}({\bf C}):\nu(\|\cdot\|_{\infty}^{2}):=\int_{{\bf C}}\|\xi\|_{\infty}^{2}\nu(\text{\rm{d}}\xi)<\infty\bigg\}.

It is a Polish space under the Wasserstein distance

\mathbb{W}_{2}(\mu_{1},\mu_{2}):=\inf_{\pi\in\mathscr{C}(\mu_{1},\mu_{2})}\left(\int_{{\bf C}\times{\bf C}}\|\xi-\eta\|_{\infty}^{2}\pi(\text{\rm{d}}\xi,\text{\rm{d}}\eta)\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},\ \ \mu_{1},\mu_{2}\in\mathscr{P}_{2}({\bf C}),

where
\mathscr{C}(\mu_{1},\mu_{2})
is the set of all couplings for
\mu_{1}
and
\mu_{2}.

To investigate the order preservation, we make the following assumptions.

(H_{7}^{1})

(Continuity)
There exists an increasing function
\alpha:\mathbb{R}_{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{+}
such that for any
t\geq 0;\xi,\eta\in{\bf C};\mu,\nu\in\mathscr{P}_{2}({\bf C})
,

\displaystyle|b(t,\xi,\mu)-b(t,\eta,\nu)|^{2}+|\bar{b}(t,\xi,\mu)-\bar{b}(t,\eta,\nu)|^{2}+\|\sigma(t,\xi,\mu)-\sigma(t,\eta,\nu)\|_{HS}^{2}

\displaystyle+\|\bar{\sigma}(t,\xi,\mu)-\bar{\sigma}(t,\eta,\nu)\|_{HS}^{2}\leq\alpha(t)\big(\|\xi-\eta\|_{\infty}^{2}+\mathbb{W}_{2}(\mu,\nu)^{2}\big).

(H_{7}^{1})

(Growth) There exists an increasing function
K:\mathbb{R}_{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{+}
such that

\displaystyle|b(t,0,\delta_{0})|^{2}+|\bar{b}(t,0,\delta_{0})|^{2}+\|\sigma(t,0,\delta_{0})\|_{HS}^{2}+\|\bar{\sigma}(t,0,\delta_{0})\|^{2}_{HS}\leq K(t),\ \ t\geq 0,

where
\delta_{0}
is the Dirac measure at point
0\in{\bf C}
.

It is easy to see that these two conditions imply assumptions
(H1)
-
(H3)
in
[
19
]
, so that by
[
19
, Theorem 3.1]
, for any
s\geq 0
and
\mathscr{F}_{s}
-measurable
{\bf C}
-valued random variables
\xi,\bar{\xi}
with finite second moment,
the equation (

) has a unique solution

Moreover, the segment process

(7.2)

\mathbb{E}\sup_{t\in[s,T]}\big(\|X(s,\xi)_{t}\|_{\infty}^{2}+\|\bar{X}(s,\bar{\xi})_{t}\|_{\infty}^{2}\big)<\infty,\ \ T\in[s,\infty).

To characterize the order-preservation for solutions of (
7.1
), we introduce the partial-order on
{\bf C}.
For
x=(x^{1},\cdots,x^{d})
and
y=(y^{1},\cdots,y^{d})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we write
x\leq y
if
x^{i}\leq y^{i}
holds for all
1\leq i\leq d.

Similarly, for
\xi=(\xi^{1},\cdots,\xi^{d})
and
\eta=(\eta^{1},\cdots,\eta^{d})\in{\bf C}
, we write
\xi\leq\eta
if
\xi^{i}(\theta)\leq\eta^{i}(\theta)
holds for all
\theta\in[-r_{0},0]
and
1\leq i\leq d.
A function
f
on
{\bf C}
is called increasing if
f(\xi)\leq f(\eta)
for
\xi\leq\eta
. Moreover, for any
\xi_{1},\xi_{2}\in{\bf C}
,
\xi_{1}\land\xi_{2}\in{\bf C}
is defined by

(\xi_{1}\land\xi_{2})^{i}:=\min\{\xi^{i}_{1},\xi_{2}^{i}\},\ \ 1\leq i\leq d.

For two probability measures
\mu,\nu\in\mathscr{P}({\bf C})
, we write
\mu\leq\nu
if
\mu(f)\leq\nu(f)
holds for any increasing function
f\in C_{b}({\bf C})
. According to
[
29
, Theorem 5]
,
\mu\leq\nu
if and only if there exists
\pi\in\mathscr{C}(\mu,\nu)
such that

\pi(\{(\xi,\eta)\in{\bf C}^{2}:\xi\leq\eta\})=1.

Definition 7.1
.

The stochastic differential system
(\ref{E1N})
is called order-preserving, if for any
s\geq 0
and
\xi,\bar{\xi}\in L^{2}(\Omega\rightarrow{\bf C},\mathscr{F}_{s},\mathbb{P})
with
\mathbb{P}(\xi\leq\bar{\xi})=1
,

\mathbb{P}\big(X(s,\xi;t)\leq\bar{X}(s,\bar{\xi};t),\ t\geq s\big)=1.

We first present the following sufficient conditions for the order preservation, which reduce back to the corresponding ones in
[
20
]
when the system is distribution-independent.

Theorem 7.1
.

Assume
(H_{7}^{1})
and
(H_{7}^{2})
. The system (
7.1
) is order-preserving provided the following two conditions are satisfied:

(1)

For any
1\leq i\leq d
,
\mu,\nu\in\mathscr{P}_{2}({\bf C})
with
\mu\leq\nu
,
\xi,\eta\in{\bf C}
with
\xi\leq\eta

\xi^{i}(0)=\eta^{i}(0)

b^{i}(t,\xi,\mu)\leq\bar{b}^{i}(t,\eta,\nu),\ \ \text{a.e.}\ t\geq 0.

For a.e.

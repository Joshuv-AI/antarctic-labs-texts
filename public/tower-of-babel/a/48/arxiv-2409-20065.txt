Double-Side Polarization and Beamforming Alignment in Polarization Reconfigurable MISO System with Deep Neural Networks Thanks: Joongheon Kim and Sean Kwon is the corresponding author.
Paul Seungcheol Oh, Han Han, Joongheon Kim, Sean Kwon

Abstract
Polarization reconfigurable (PR) antennas enhance spectrum and energy efficiency between next-generation node B (gNB) and user equipment (UE). This is achieved by tuning the polarization vectors for each antenna element based on channel state information (CSI). On the other hand, degree of freedom increased by PR antennas yields a challenge in channel estimation with pilot training overhead. This paper pursues the reduction of pilot overhead, and proposes to employ deep neural networks (DNNs) on both transceiver ends to directly optimize the polarization and beamforming vectors based on the received pilots without the explicit channel estimation. Numerical experiments show that the proposed method significantly outperforms the conventional first-estimate-then-optimize scheme by maximum of 20% in beamforming gain.

On the other hand, the system design for the optimal polarization vectors depends crucially on the knowledge of the channel state information (CSI). In practice, obtaining the sufficient CSI in a multi-PR-antenna communication system is difficult, since the PR wireless channels typically provide higher degrees-of-freedom than the conventional ones with the corresponding pilot signal design.

This paper considers a double-side polarization and beamforming alignment problem within a PR multiple-input-single-output (PR-MISO) system. In this setting, a single-antenna user equipment (UE) first sends pilots to a multi-antenna next generation node B (gNB), and the gNB designs its polarization and the beamforming vectors based on the received pilots. Subsequently, the gNB sends pilots towards the UE, enabling the UE to design its polarization vectors accordingly. By transmitting and receiving pilots from both transceiver sides, the gNB and UE learn to implicitly coordinate with each other without additional feedback cost.

The double-side polarization and beamforming alignment problem is very challenging to solve since it involves the estimation of the PR channel which is inherently higher-dimensional than what is directly observed through pilots at both the gNB and UE.
This is because the PR systems exploit the PR antennas based on the channel depolarization matrix
[
7
]
; which comes with the cost of increase in the dimensionality of the channel compared to what can be observed through pilots. With such increase in the channel dimension, the channel estimation becomes highly non-trivial at both tranceiver sides; thus, it leads to excessive pilot training overhead as the scale of the multi-antenna PR system grows.

The main idea of this paper is that significant savings on pilots overhead is possible by leveraging deep neural networks (DNNs) to bypass explicit channel estimation at both transceiver sides. Specifically, we employ two separate DNNs at the gNB and UE to directly optimize polarization and beamforming vectors at the gNB as well as the optimal polarization vector at the UE directly based on their received pilots. Simulation results show that the proposed end-to-end data-driven approach significantly reduces the pilot overhead compared to the conventional first-estimate-then-optimize scheme.

II-B

Double-side Pilot Protocol

This section describes a double-side pilot protocol in which the gNB first sends a sequence of
L
downlink pilots
\{x_{{\rm gNB},l}\}^{L}_{l=1}
, under a power constraint
\mathbb{E}[|x_{{\rm gNB},l}|^{2}]\leq\rho_{\rm gNB}
, to the UE during a designated pilot frame. Subsequently, in the next pilot frame, the UE sends a sequence of uplink
\{x_{{\rm UE},l}\}^{L}_{l=1}
pilots, under a power constraint
\mathbb{E}[|x_{{\rm UE},l}|^{2}]\leq\rho_{\rm UE}
, to the gNB. Without loss of generality, we set
x_{{\rm gNB},l}=\sqrt{\rho_{\rm gNB}}
and
x_{{\rm UE},l}=\sqrt{\rho_{\rm UE}}
. Owe to channel reciprocity, we denote the channel from the gNB to UE and the channel from UE to gNB as
\mathbb{H}_{\rm dp}
and
\mathbb{H}_{\rm dp}^{H}
, respectively. Then, the
l^{\rm th}
received pilot at the gNB is described as follows

\displaystyle\mathbb{y}_{{\rm gNB},l}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}_{{\rm gNB},l}^{\top}\mathbb{H}_{\rm dp}^{H}\mathbb{p}_{\rm UE}x_{{\rm UE},l}+\mathbf{n}_{{\rm gNB},l}\in\mathbb{C}^{N_{t}},

(10)

where
\mathbb{p}_{\rm UE}
is randomly generated and fixed across different
l
and
\mathbb{P}_{{\rm gNB},l}
is randomly generated for each
l
. Here,
\mathbf{n}_{{\rm gNB},l}\sim\mathcal{C}\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^{2}_{\rm gNB}\bf{I})
is the additive white Gaussian noise (AWGN) at the gNB. In an analogous manner,
l^{\rm th}
received pilots at the UE is described as

\displaystyle y_{{\rm UE},l}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{p}_{\rm UE}^{\top}\mathbb{H}_{\rm dp}\mathbb{P}_{\rm gNB}\mathbb{w}_{l}x_{{\rm gNB},l}+n_{{\rm UE},l}\in\mathbb{C},

(11)

where
\mathbb{p}_{\rm UE}
and
\mathbb{P}_{\rm gNB}
are randomly generated and fixed for all
l
and
\mathbb{w}_{l}
is generated randomly for each
l
and
n_{{\rm UE},l}\sim\mathcal{C}\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^{2}_{\rm UE})
is the AWGN during the pilot stage at the UE side.

II-C

Problem Formulation

This paper proposes to design the beamformer
\mathbb{w}
and polarization vectors at the gNB (
7
) and polarization vector at UE (
4
) based on the received pilots
\mathbb{Y}_{\rm gNB}=[\mathbb{y}_{{\rm gNB},1},\mathbb{y}_{{\rm gNB},2},\ldots,\mathbb{y}_{{\rm gNB},L}]\in\mathbb{C}^{N_{t}\times L}
and
\mathbb{Y}_{\rm UE}=[y_{{\rm UE},1},y_{{\rm UE},2},\ldots,y_{{\rm UE},L}]\in\mathbb{C}^{L}
.
The problem of interest is then described as

\displaystyle\max_{\mathcal{F}(.),~\mathcal{G}(.)}

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg[\big|\mathbb{p}^{\top}_{\rm UE}\mathbb{H}_{\rm dp}\mathbb{P}_{\rm gNB}\mathbb{w}\big|^{2}\bigg]

(12)

\displaystyle\textrm{s.t.}

\displaystyle||\mathbb{w}||^{2}=1,

\displaystyle\{\mathbb{w},\theta_{1},\ldots,\theta_{N_{t}}\}=\mathcal{F}(\mathbb{Y}_{\rm gNB}),

\displaystyle\theta_{\rm UE}=\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{Y}_{\rm UE}),

where
\mathcal{F}:\mathbb{C}^{N_{t}\times L}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{N_{t}}\times\mathbb{R}^{N_{t}}
and
\mathcal{G}:\mathbb{C}^{L}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
. Note that (
12
) is a challenging variational optimization problem in which the objective is maximized over the high dimensional functional mapping
\mathcal{F}
and the functional mapping
\mathcal{G}
. As a solution, we propose to learn
\mathcal{F}
and
\mathcal{G}
directly through DNNs based on received pilots.

III
Least Square Solution for Channel Estimation

This section describes the conventional first-estimate-then-optimize approach to solve (
9
) with double-side pilot protocol via least square (LS) channel estimation. We first concatenating downlink pilots into a matrix to form
\mathbb{Y}_{\rm UE}=[y_{{\rm UE},1},y_{{\rm UE},2},\ldots,y_{{\rm UE},L}]^{\top}\in\mathbb{C}^{L\times 1}
, expressed as

\mathbb{Y}_{\rm UE}=\mathbb{W}^{\top}\mathbb{P}^{\top}_{\rm gNB}\mathbb{H}_{\rm dp}^{\top}\mathbb{p}_{\rm UE}+\mathbb{n}_{\rm UE},

(13)

with
\mathbb{W}=[\mathbb{w}_{1},\mathbb{w}_{2},\ldots,\mathbb{w}_{L}]\in\mathbb{C}^{N_{t}\times L}
, being the matrix of concatenated downlink beamforming vectors used in each pilot frame and

\mathbb{n}_{\rm UE}=[n_{{\rm UE},1},n_{{\rm UE},2},\ldots,n_{{\rm UE},L}]^{\top}\in\mathbb{C}^{L\times 1}
being the downlink noise vector. With (
13
), we can obtain the estimate of the downlink channel by solving the LS problem formulated as

\displaystyle\min_{\mathbb{H}_{\rm dp}}

\displaystyle\big|\big|\mathbb{Y}_{\rm UE}-\mathbb{W}^{\top}\mathbb{P}^{\top}_{\rm gNB}\mathbb{H}_{\rm dp}^{\top}\mathbb{p}_{\rm UE}\big|\big|^{2}.

(14)

The optimal solution to (
14
) is given as

\hat{\mathbb{H}}_{\rm dp,{\rm dl}}=\big[(\mathbb{A}^{H}\mathbb{A})^{-1}\mathbb{A}^{H}\mathbb{Y}_{\rm UE}\mathbb{p}_{\rm UE}^{\top}(\mathbb{p}_{\rm UE}\mathbb{p}_{\rm UE}^{\top})^{-1}\big]^{\top},

(15)

where
\mathbb{A}\triangleq\mathbb{W}^{T}\mathbb{P}^{T}_{\rm gNB}
is effective gNB receiving sensor.

In a similar manner, we formulate the uplink pilots as

\displaystyle\bar{\mathbb{Y}}_{\rm gNB}

\displaystyle=[\mathbb{y}_{{\rm gNB},1}^{\top},\mathbb{y}_{{\rm gNB},2}^{\top},\ldots,\mathbb{y}_{{\rm gNB},L}^{\top}]^{\top}\in\mathbb{C}^{LN_{t}\times 1}

(16)

\displaystyle\bar{\mathbb{Y}}_{\rm gNB}

\displaystyle=\bar{\mathbb{P}}_{\rm gNB}^{\top}\mathbb{H}_{\rm up}^{\top}\mathbb{p}_{\rm UE}+\mathbb{N}_{\rm gNB},

where
\bar{\mathbb{P}}_{\rm gNB}=[\mathbb{P}_{{\rm gNB},1},\mathbb{P}_{{\rm gNB},2},\ldots,\mathbb{P}_{{\rm gNB},L}]\in\mathbb{R}^{2N\times LN_{t}}
is a concatenated matrix of (
7
) in each pilot frame and
\mathbb{N}_{\rm gNB}=[\mathbf{n}_{{\rm gNB},1},\mathbf{n}_{{\rm gNB},2},\ldots,\mathbf{n}_{{\rm gNB},L}]
is the concatenated uplink noise matrix. To this end, uplink LS problem for estimation of
\mathbb{H}_{\rm up}
is formulated as,

\displaystyle\min_{\mathbb{H}_{\rm dp}}

\displaystyle\big|\big|\bar{\mathbb{Y}}_{\rm gNB}-\bar{\mathbb{P}}_{\rm gNB}\mathbb{H}_{\rm dp}^{\top}\mathbb{p}_{\rm UE}\big|\big|^{2}.

(17)

The optimal solution to (
17
) is

\hat{\mathbb{H}}_{\rm dp,{\rm ul}}=\big[(\bar{\mathbb{P}}_{\rm gNB}\bar{\mathbb{P}}_{\rm gNB}^{\top})^{-1}\bar{\mathbb{P}}_{\rm gNB}\bar{\mathbb{Y}}_{\rm gNB}\mathbb{p}_{\rm UE}^{\top}(\mathbb{p}_{\rm UE}\mathbb{p}_{\rm UE}^{\top})\big]^{\top}.

(18)

By utilizing the estimated channels on both ends, the gNB can design the optimal polarization and beamforming vector using (
18
), while UE can also design its optimal polarization vector with (
15
). Given that there are
4N_{t}
unknown parameters in
\mathbb{H}_{\rm dp}
and the gNB receives
N_{t}
dimensions with each pilot signal, four uplink pilots are sufficient to recover the channel if the noise is not present. In contrast, the UE observes only a single dimension per pilot signal and requires
4N_{t}
pilots to recover the channel. This leads to increase in pilot overhead.

IV
Proposed Deep Learning Solution

The goal of the proposed DNN framework is to maximize the beamforming gain in (
12
) from
\mathbb{Y}_{\rm gNB}
and
\mathbb{Y}_{\rm UE}
directly based on the received pilots so as to bypass explicit channel estimation. As shown in Fig.
2
, the mapping functions
\mathcal{F}
and
\mathcal{G}
are learned separately at both sides with their respective DNNs.

Fig. 2:
Proposed DNN based PR-MISO system

Specifically,
\mathcal{F}
is parameterized by a DNN with the size
2LN_{t}\times h_{1}\times\ldots\times h_{j}\times 3N_{t}
, where it has
j
hidden layers with each consisting of
h_{j}
neurons. Particularly,
the input of the network is described as

\tilde{\mathbb{Y}}_{\rm gNB}=\big[\text{vec}(\Re\{\mathbb{Y}_{\rm gNB}\})^{\top},\text{vec}(\Im\{\mathbb{Y}_{\rm gNB}\})^{\top}\big]\in\mathbb{C}^{2LN_{t}},

(19)

which is a concatenated and flattened vector consisting of real and imaginary parts of
\mathbb{Y}_{\rm gNB}
.

The output of the network is separated and passed to sigmoid and normalization layers to produce
\mathbb{P}_{\rm gNB}
and
\mathbb{w}
. Let
\mathbb{z}\in\mathbb{R}^{3N_{t}}
denote the output vector of the gNB’s DNN and define
[\mathbb{z}]_{m_{1}:m_{2}}
as subvector of
\mathbb{z}
index from
m_{1}
to
m_{2}
. To generate the polarization angles, we first extract
[\mathbb{z}]_{1:N_{t}}
and process it through a sigmoid layer to produce
\theta_{i}=\sigma([\mathbb{z}]_{1:N_{t}})\cdot\frac{\pi}{2}
, where
\sigma(x)=\frac{1}{1+e^{-x}}
is the sigmoid function. This yields
N_{t}
polarization angles, which are used to construct
\mathbb{P}_{\rm gNB}
according to (
3
). Next, we handle the remaining
2N_{t}
output of gNB’s DNN, i.e.,
[\mathbb{z}]_{N_{t}+1:3N_{t}}
. These components are processed through a normalization layer defined by

\mathbb{w}=\frac{[\mathbb{z}]_{N_{t}+1:2N_{t}}+j[\mathbb{z}]_{2N_{t}+1:3N_{t}}}{\big|\big|[\mathbb{z}]_{N_{t}+1:3N_{t}}\big|\big|_{2}}.

(20)

This normalization layer satisfies the power constraint of the downlink beamformer and produces
\mathbb{w}
.

In a similar way, UE’s mapping
\mathcal{G}
, is parameterized by UE’s DNN with the size
2L\times h_{1}\times\ldots\times h_{j}\times 1
. The input of UE’s DNN is described as

\tilde{\mathbb{Y}}_{\rm UE}=\big[{\rm vec}(\Re\{\mathbb{Y}_{\rm UE}\})^{\top},{\rm vec}(\Im\{\mathbb{Y}_{\rm UE}\})^{\top}\big]\in\mathbb{C}^{2L}.

(21)

Let
\mathbb{g}\in\mathbb{R}
denote output of UE’s DNN. We process this through the sigmoid layer, described as
\theta_{\rm UE}=\sigma(\mathbb{g})\cdot\frac{\pi}{2}
, to produce the UE polarization angle. This gets employed to construct
\mathbb{p}_{\rm UE}
with (
4
). With the sigmoid and normalization layers at gNB’s side and a sigmoid layer at UE’s side, the network is trained in unsupervised manner with loss function set as
-\mathbb{E}\big[\big|\mathbb{p}^{\top}_{\rm UE}\mathbb{H}_{\rm dp}\mathbb{P}_{\rm gNB}\mathbb{w}\big|^{2}\big]
.

VI
Conclusion

The PR-MISO system takes advantage of the additional degrees-of-freedom of polarization by effectively exploiting the channel depolarization matrix. However, conventional first-estimate-then-optimize scheme confronts a challenge in channel estimation, due to the required higher dimensional channel estimation from the lower dimensional pilot measurements. This paper resolves the challenge via proposing the employment of DNNs at both transceiver sides to learn optimal polarization and beamforming vectors at the gNB and polarization vectors at the UE based on received pilots. Simulation results illustrate that the proposed method of bypassing explicit channel estimation significantly outperforms the conventional first-estimate-then-optimize scheme.

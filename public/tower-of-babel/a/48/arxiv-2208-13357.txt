Application of Ramsey theory to localization of set of product states via multicopies
Xing-Chen Guo, Mao-Sheng Li

Abstract
It is well known that any N N orthogonal pure states can always be perfectly distinguished under local operation and classical communications (LOCC) if ( N − 1 ) (N-1) copies of the state are available [ Phys. Rev. Lett. 85 , 4972 (2000) ]. It is important to reduce the number of quantum state copies that ensures the LOCC distinguishability in terms of resource saving and nonlocality strength characterization. Denote f r ​ ( N ) f_{r}(N) the least number of copies needed to LOCC distinguish any N N orthogonal r r -partite product states. This work will be devoted to the estimation of the upper bound of f r ​ ( N ) f_{r}(N) . In fact, we first relate this problem with Ramsey theory, a branch of combinatorics dedicated to studying the conditions under which orders must appear. Subsequently, we prove f 2 ​ ( N ) ≤ ⌈ N 6 ⌉ + 2 f_{2}(N)\leq\lceil\frac{N}{6}\rceil+2 , which is better than f 2 ​ ( N ) ≤ ⌈ N 4 ⌉ f_{2}(N)\leq\lceil\frac{N}{4}\rceil obtained in [Eur. Phys. J. Plus 136 , 1172 (2021)] when N > 24 N>24 . We further exhibit that for arbitrary ϵ > 0 \epsilon>0 , f r ​ ( N ) ≤ ⌈ ϵ ​ N ⌉ f_{r}(N)\leq\lceil\epsilon N\rceil always holds for sufficiently large N N .

Orthogonal pure states can always be perfectly distinguished by means of global measurement

In such setting, we may instead perform local measurements on constituent subsystems and communicate the results mutually through classical communications, that is, local operations and classical communications(LOCC), to distinguish those states. For any positive integers

III
Cases for bipartite system

Denote an unknown product state of a given bipartite system by
|\psi\rangle
, and assume that
|\psi\rangle\in\{|a_{i}\rangle\otimes|b_{i}\rangle\}_{i=1}^{n}

First, we review Shu’s method of analyzing orthogonal product states by polygons. Regarding the
N

\langle b_{j}|b_{k}\rangle=0
. Since
\{|a_{i}\rangle\otimes|b_{i}\rangle\}_{i=1}^{N}
is an orthogonal set, each edge of the graph

must either be colored by red or by blue. That is, there is a correspondence between orthogonal set
\{|a_{i}\rangle\otimes|b_{i}\rangle\}_{i=1}^{N}
and edge 2-coloring
K_{N}

shu2021locality

Shu obtained one of his results by proving the lemma that

or 3 states in

or a blue
K_{3}

shu2021locality

m

Theorem 1

\bullet

From our results, it is possible to exclude more than 4 states via LOCC protocols with a single copy of
N
orthogonal bipartite product states
\{|a_{i}\rangle\otimes|b_{i}\rangle\}_{i=1}^{N}
when
N
is large enough. Before going further, we give the following definition.

Definition 1
.

For positive integers
N,\ r\geq 2
, we define
f_{r}(N)
as the least number of copies which ensures the LOCC distinguishability of any
N
orthogonal
r
-partite product states.

So
f_{r}(N)
is the unique positive integer that satisfies the following two conditions:

(i)

any
N
orthogonal
r
-partite product states are LOCC distinguishable provided
f_{r}(N)
copies of given states;

(ii)

there exist
N
orthogonal
r
-partite product states which are LOCC indistinguishable even provided
f_{r}(N)-1
copies of given states.

Shu
shu2021locality
obtained an upper bound of
f_{r}(N)
. Especially, he proved that
f_{2}(N)\leq\lceil\frac{N}{4}\rceil
. In the remainder of this section, we dedicate to obtain a smaller upper bound of
f_{2}(N)
for large
N
by applying our results obtained above.

Theorem 2
.

f_{2}(N)\leq\lceil\frac{N}{6}\rceil+2
.

Proof.

First, by Shu’s result,
f_{2}(N)\leq\lceil\frac{N}{4}\rceil.
We can check that
\lceil\frac{N}{4}\rceil\leq\lceil\frac{N}{6}\rceil+2
when
N\leq 17.
For every integer
N\geq 18,
it can be written as

N=6q+r,\ q,r\in\mathbb{N},q\geq 3,\text{and }0\leq r\leq 5.

By
Corollary 1
, if
q\geq 3,

we can exclude 6 states via a single copy. And so we
can use
q-2
copies to exclude
6(q-2)
states and left
12+r
states. To distinguish the remaining
12+r
states,

\lceil\frac{12+r}{4}\rceil

more copies are enough. Therefore, the total number of copies needed are at most

(q-2)+\lceil\frac{12+r}{4}\rceil\leq(q-2)+\lceil\frac{12+r}{6}\rceil+2=q+\lceil\frac{r}{6}\rceil+2=\lceil\frac{N}{6}\rceil+2.

IV
Cases for multipartite system

In this section, we consider the more general
r
-partite system(
r\geq 2
). We claim that our results of bipartite system can be naturally generalized to
r
-partite case, by contemplating the correspondence between
N
orthogonal
r
-partite product states and edge
r
-coloring
K_{N}
, which allows us to make use of the language of
r
-color Ramsey number, similar to the bipartite case.

For convenience, we first clarify some notations used in the rest of the paper.

Notations

(1)

“WLG” stands for “Without loss of generality”;

(2)

Let
S
be an
r
-partite system which is composed of
r
subsystems
S_{1},\cdots,S_{r}
. Assume that the state of
S
is prepared in one of
N
orthogonal
r
-partite product states
\{|\psi_{i}\rangle=|\psi_{i}^{1}\rangle\otimes\cdots\otimes|\psi_{i}^{r}\rangle\}_{i=1}^{N}
, where
|\psi_{i}^{j}\rangle
corresponds to the
j
-th subsystem
S_{j}
. We say the
k
product states
|\psi_{n_{1}}\rangle,\cdots,|\psi_{n_{k}}\rangle
are orthogonal to each other with respect to
S_{j}
if
|\psi_{n_{1}}^{j}\rangle,\cdots,|\psi_{n_{k}}^{j}\rangle
are orthogonal to each other;

(3)

If
|\psi_{n_{1}}\rangle,\cdots,|\psi_{n_{k}}\rangle
are orthogonal to each other with respect to
S_{j}
, then we use symbol
M_{n_{1},\cdots,n_{k}}^{j}
to denote the local measurement

\{|\psi_{n_{i}}^{j}\rangle\langle\psi_{n_{i}}^{j}|\}_{i=1}^{k}\cup\{{\mathbb{I}-\sum_{i=1}^{k}|\psi_{n_{i}}^{j}\rangle\langle\psi_{n_{i}}^{j}|}\}

on the
j
-th subsystem
S_{j}
.

Now we illuminate the correspondence between
N
orthogonal
r
-partite product states and edge
r
-coloring
K_{N}
in detail, as well as introducing the usage of the language of
r
-color Ramsey number, just as the bipartite case. Regard the
N
orthogonal
r
-partite product states
\{|\psi_{i}\rangle=|\psi_{i}^{1}\rangle\otimes\cdots\otimes|\psi_{i}^{r}\rangle\}_{i=1}^{N}
as the
N
vertexes of a complete graph
K_{N}
, and if two states are orthogonal to each other with respect to the
j
-th subsystem
S_{j}
, then color the edge that connects the corresponding two vertexes by the
j
-th color. So each edge of the corresponding
K_{N}
must be colored by one of the
r
colors, that is, there is a correspondence between
\{|\psi_{i}\rangle=|\psi_{i}^{1}\rangle\otimes\cdots\otimes|\psi_{i}^{r}\rangle\}_{i=1}^{N}
and edge
r
-coloring
K_{N}
. Then
N\geq R(i_{1},\cdots,i_{r})
means that there exists
k\in\{1,\cdots,r\}
, s.t.,
K_{N}
contains a subgraph
K_{i_{k}}
whose edges are all colored by the
k
-th color. That is, there exists a subsystem
S_{k}
, s.t., there exist
i_{k}
states in
\{|\psi_{i}\rangle=|\psi_{i}^{1}\rangle\otimes\cdots\otimes|\psi_{i}^{r}\rangle\}_{i=1}^{N}
which are orthogonal to each other with respect to
S_{k}
.

With the statements above, we can naturally derive the
r
-partite versions of
Theorem 1
and
Corollary 1
. The techniques applied to prove the
r
-partite results are just similar to those used in the bipartite case.

Theorem 3
.

Given any
R_{r}(m)
orthogonal
r
-partite product states and a positive integer
k\geq m
, if for any
0\leq t\leq k-m\ (t\in\mathbb{N})
the following inequalities

R_{r}(m)-(m+t)\geq R(m+1+t,k-m+1-t,\cdots,k-m+1-t;\ r),

(2)

are satisfied, then a single copy is sufficient to exclude at least
k
states via LOCC protocols.

Proof.

Denote the
R_{r}(m)
orthogonal
r
-partite product states as
\{|\psi_{i}\rangle=|\psi_{i}^{1}\rangle\otimes\cdots\otimes|\psi_{i}^{r}\rangle\}_{i=1}^{R_{r}(m)}
, and assume that the unknown state from this set is
|\psi\rangle\in\{|\psi_{i}\rangle\}_{i=1}^{R_{r}(m)}
. Suppose that there are exactly
M_{j}
states which are orthogonal to each other with respect to subsystem
S_{j}
,
j=1,\cdots,r
. Define
M=\mathop{\rm max}\{M_{j}\}_{j=1}^{r}
. By the definition of
R_{r}(m)
, there must be a subsystem
S_{k}
, s.t., the set
\{|\psi_{i}\rangle\}_{i=1}^{R_{r}(m)}
must have
m
states which are orthogonal to each other with respect to
S_{k}
. So we have
M\geq m
. Setting
t=k-m
in inequality (
2
), we have
R_{r}(m)-k\geq R(k+1,1,\cdots,1;r)=1
, that is,
R_{r}(m)\geq k+1
. Therefore, the cardinality of the set
\{|\psi_{i}\rangle\}_{i=1}^{R_{r}(m)}
is greater than
k+1
. Now we separate our proof into two cases according to the number
M
.

(1)

M\geq k.
That is, there must be a subsystem
s.t.
there are at least
k
states which are orthogonal to each other with respect to that subsystem. WLG let
\{|\psi_{i}^{1}\rangle\}_{i=1}^{k}
be an orthogonal set. Then taking
M_{1,\cdots,k}^{1}
, the outcome of the measurement have the following different possible cases:

\bullet

The outcome is not one of
1,\cdots,k
(that is, the outcome corresponds to
\mathbb{I}-\sum_{i=1}^{k}|\psi_{i}^{1}\rangle\langle\psi_{i}^{1}|
). So we can exclude
k
states.

\bullet

The outcome is one of
1,\cdots,k
(that is, the outcome corresponds to one of
\{|\psi_{i}^{1}\rangle\langle\psi_{i}^{1}|\}_{i=1}^{k}
). WLG, we assume that the outcome is
k
. Then we can exclude the following
(k-1)
states

|\psi_{1}\rangle,\cdots,|\psi_{k-1}\rangle.

What’s more, we consider the following two cases:

\bullet

If
\exists\ j>k
, s.t.,
\langle\psi_{k}^{1}|\psi_{j}^{1}\rangle=0
, then we can also exclude
|\psi_{j}\rangle
via the outcome
k
.

\bullet

If
\forall\ j>k
,
\langle\psi_{k}^{1}|\psi_{j}^{1}\rangle\neq 0
, then there must exist
s\in\{2,\cdots,r\}
, s.t.,
\langle\psi_{k}^{s}|\psi_{k+1}^{s}\rangle=0
. Then taking
M_{k,k+1}^{s},
each outcome of this measurement could exclude one more state. Hence, we could exclude at least
k
states totally.

(2)

M=k+1-n(n\in\mathbb{N}).
Notice that
M=k+1-n\geq m
. Therefore, we only need to consider the cases:
0\leq n\leq k-m+1
. That is, there must be a subsystem, s.t., there are
k+1-n
(
n\in\mathbb{N}
) states which are orthogonal to each other with respect to that subsystem. WLG let
\{|\psi_{i}^{1}\rangle\}_{i=1}^{k+1-n}
be an orthogonal set. Now we will prove our statement by induction on
n
. Notice that the cases
n=0
and
n=1
have been proved by the previous one case. Now suppose that the result is true for
n\leq N
(where
N+1\leq k-m+1
), i.e., when
M=\mathop{\rm max}\{M_{j}\}_{j=1}^{r}=k+1-N
, a single copy is sufficient to exclude at least
k
states via LOCC protocols (inductive assumption).

In the following, we will show that our statement is true for
n=N+1
. That is, when
M=\mathop{\rm max}\{M_{j}\}_{j=1}^{r}=k+1-(N+1)=k-N
, a single copy is sufficient to exclude at least
k
states via LOCC protocols. WLG let
\{|\psi_{i}^{1}\rangle\}_{i=1}^{k-N}
be an orthogonal set. Taking
M_{1,\cdots,k-N}^{1}
, we have two different possible outcomes:

(i)

The outcome is not among
\{1,\cdots,k-N\}
. Then we exclude
(k-N)
states
|\psi_{1}\rangle,\cdots,|\psi_{k-N}\rangle
. The condition

R_{r}(m)-(k-N)\geq R(k-N+1,N+1,\cdots,N+1;r),

implies that the number of the remaining states is not less than
R(k-N+1,N+1,\cdots,N+1;r)
. Regarding the remaining
R_{r}(m)-(k-N)
states
\{|\psi_{i}\rangle\}_{i=k-N+1}^{R_{r}(m)}
as an edge r-coloring
K_{R_{r}(m)-(k-N)}
, by the definition of Ramsey number, we know that there must be either
k-N+1
states which are orthogonal to each other with respect to
S_{1}
, or
\exists\ q\in\{2,\cdots,r\}
, s.t., there are
N+1
states which are orthogonal to each other with respect to
S_{q}
. For the first setting, by the inductive assumption, we know that our statement is true. For the latter setting, WLG let
\{|\psi_{i}^{q}\rangle\}_{i=k-N+1}^{k+1}
be an orthogonal set. Taking
M_{k-N+1,\cdots,k+1}^{q},
we can exclude at least
N
more states for each possible outcome. Hence, we could exclude at least
k-N+N=k
states in total.

(ii)

The outcome is one of
1,\cdots,k-N
. WLG assume that the outcome is
k-N
. So we can exclude the
k-N-1
states
|\psi_{1}\rangle,\cdots,|\psi_{k-N-1}\rangle
. Furthermore, we consider the following different cases:

\bullet

If there are only
s
(where
1\leq s\leq N+1
) states in
\{|\psi_{i}^{1}\rangle\}_{i=k-N+1}^{R_{r}(m)}
which are orthogonal to
|\psi_{k-N}^{1}\rangle
, WLG let

\langle\psi_{k-N}^{1}|\psi_{k-N+1}^{1}\rangle=\cdots=\langle\psi_{k-N}^{1}|\psi_{k-N+s}^{1}\rangle=0.

Then for the outcome
k-N
, we can also exclude
s
more states

|\psi_{k-N+1}\rangle,\cdots,|\psi_{k-N+s}\rangle.

Moreover, setting
t=k-m-(N+1-s)
in inequality (
2
), we have

R_{r}(m)-(k-N-1+s)\geq R(k-N+s,N+2-s,\cdots,N+2-s;r).

This implies that the number of the remaining states
\{|\psi_{k-N}\rangle,|\psi_{i}\rangle\}_{i=k-N+s+1}^{R_{r}(m)}
is not less than
R(k-N+s,N+2-s,\cdots,N+2-s;r)
. By the definition of Ramsey number, there are either
k-N+s
states in
\{|\psi_{k-N}\rangle,|\psi_{i}\rangle\}_{i=k-N+s+1}^{R_{r}(m)}
which are orthogonal to each other with respect to
S_{1}
, or
\exists\ q\in\{2,\cdots,r\}
, s.t., there are
N+2-s
states in
\{|\psi_{k-N}\rangle,|\psi_{i}\rangle\}_{i=k-N+s+1}^{R_{r}(m)}
that are orthogonal to each other with respect to
S_{q}
. For the first setting, as
s\geq 1
, by the inductive assumption, we know that our statement is true. For the latter setting, WLG let
\{|\psi_{k-N}^{q}\rangle\}\cup\{|\psi_{i}^{q}\rangle\}_{i=k-N+s+1}^{k+1}
be an orthogonal set. Taking
M_{k-N,k-N+s+1,k-N+s+2,\cdots,k+1}^{q},
we can exclude at least
(N+1-s)
more states for each possible outcome. Hence, we could exclude at least
(k-N-1)+s+(N+1-s)=k
states in total.

\bullet

If there is no state in
\{|\psi_{i}^{1}\rangle\}_{i=k-N+1}^{R_{r}(m)}
that is orthogonal to
|\psi_{k-N}^{1}\rangle
. Then from the condition

R_{r}(m)-(k-N)\geq R(k-N+1,N+1,\cdots,N+1;r),

there must be either
k-N+1
states in
\{|\psi_{i}\rangle\}_{i=k-N+1}^{R_{r}(m)}
which are orthogonal to each other with respect to
S_{1}
, or
\exists\ q\in\{2,\cdots,r\}
, s.t., there are
N+1
states in
\{|\psi_{i}\rangle\}_{i=k-N+1}^{R_{r}(m)}
which are orthogonal to each other with respect to
S_{q}
. If it is the first case, then by the inductive assumption we know the statement is true; if it is the latter case, WLG let
\{|\psi_{i}^{q}\rangle\}_{i=k-N+1}^{k+1}
be an orthogonal set. Taking
M_{k-N+1,\cdots,k+1}^{q}
, if the outcome is not one of
k-N+1,\cdots,k+1
, then we exclude
N+1
more states, that is, we can totally exclude
k-N-1+(N+1)=k
states; if the outcome is one of
k-N+1,\cdots,k+1
, WLG let the outcome be
k+1
, then we can exclude
N
states
|\psi_{k-N+1}\rangle,\cdots,|\psi_{k}\rangle
, and if
\langle\psi_{k+1}^{q}|\psi_{k-N}^{q}\rangle=0
, then exclude
|\psi_{k-N}\rangle
and so we have already excluded
(k-N-1)+N+1=k
states; if
\langle\psi_{k+1}^{q}|\psi_{k-N}^{q}\rangle\neq 0
, then there must be
v\in\{1,\cdots,r\}
(
v\neq 1,q
), s.t.,
\langle\psi_{k+1}^{v}|\psi_{k-N}^{v}\rangle=0
, then
M_{k+1,k-N}^{v}
is sufficient to exclude at least one more state, then we can totally exclude
(k-N-1)+N+1=k
states. Hence, we can exclude
(k-N-1)+(N+1)=k
states totally.

By induction we can conclude that the result is true for all cases.

Notice that
Theorem 1
is just the special form of
Theorem 3
when
r=2
. From
Theorem 3
we can obtain the following
Corollary 2
by means of
r
-color Ramsey number, just similar to
Corollary 1
.

Corollary 2
.

(1) For any
R_{r}(m)
orthogonal
r
-partite product states
(m\geq 4,m\in\mathbb{N^{+}})
, a single copy is sufficient to exclude
m+1
states via LOCC protocols; (2) For any
R(m,m,m)
orthogonal tripartite product states
(m\geq 5,m\in\mathbb{N^{+}})
, a single copy is sufficient to exclude
m+2
states via LOCC protocols.

Proof.

We just need to check the relevant inequalities of
Theorem 3
.

(1)

First, we only need to check

\begin{array}[]{l}R_{r}(m)-m\geq R(m+1,2,\cdots,2;r)=m+1,\\
R_{r}(m)-(m+1)\geq R(m+2,1,\cdots,1;r)=1,\\
\end{array}

when
m\geq 4
. From
Proposition 3
we can conclude that
R_{r}(m)\geq R(m,m)
, and it’s easy to check that
R(m,m)\geq 2m+1\geq m+2
when
m\geq 4
. So the two inequalities hold when
m\geq 4
;

(2)

Moreover, we need to check that when
m\geq 5
we have

\begin{array}[]{l}R(m,m,m)-m\geq R(m+1,3,3),\\
R(m,m,m)-(m+1)\geq R(m+2,2,2),\\
R(m,m,m)-(m+2)\geq R(m+3,1,1).\end{array}

\bullet

The last two inequalities. We know
R(m,m,m)\geq R(m,m)
, and it’s easy to see that
R(m,m)\geq 2m+3
when

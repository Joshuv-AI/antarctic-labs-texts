Abstract
. In this note we give explicit computations of certain types of Curtis homomorphisms and interpret them in terms of Gelfand-Tsetlin diagrams. Introduction

In this note we are concerned with the Gelfand-Graev representation of
GL(n,{\mathbb{F}}_{q})
,
n>1
. There are two equivalent ways of describing irreducible representations entering the Gelfand-Graev representations. In 1970, S.I. Gelfand
[
7
]
introduced Bessel functions associated to each irreducible representation entering Gelfand-Graev representation of group
GL(n,{\mathbb{F}}_{q})
and gave explicit computations for certain cases of
GL(3,{\mathbb{F}}_{q})
. Another way exploits properties of the Hecke algebra
{\mathcal{H}}
(or
GL(n,{\mathbb{F}}_{q})-
endomorphism algebra) of the Gelfand-Graev representation. In 1993, Curtis proved the following factorization theorem (Theorem 4.2 in
[
2
]
) for the homomorphisms between
\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}_{l}}
-algebras:

(0.1)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{H}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}_{l}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}_{l}}[T]}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f_{T,\theta}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f_{T}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widehat{\theta}}

where
T
is a maximal torus in
GL(n,{\mathbb{F}}_{q})
,
\theta:T\rightarrow\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}_{l}}
is a character of the group
T
and
\widehat{\theta}
is the linear extension of
\theta
to the group algebra:
\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}_{l}}[T]
.

Since
\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}_{l}}
is isomorphic to
{\mathbb{C}}
as a field, in the following sections we shall see that our computation can be implemented for both
\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}_{l}}
and
{\mathbb{C}}
interchangeably. Therefore throughout this note we shall regard the Hecke algebra
{\mathcal{H}}
and the group algebra of
T
as algebras over
{\mathbb{C}}
.

We refer the algebra homomorphism
f_{T}
in (0.1) as
Curtis homomorphism
. In the factorization theorem, the Curtis homomorphism was constructed using the Green function over finite fields whose general theory was developed by Deligne and Lusztig
[
5
]
via an geometric approach of constructing all irreducible representations of
G
using
l
-adic cohomology. It is a natural problem of computing
f_{T}
explicitly which is directly related to explicit computations of Bessel functions over finite fields (defined for
GL(n,{\mathbb{F}}_{q})
by Gelfand in
[
7
]
). Furthermore, it is expected by Curtis
[
3
]
that it is probable to find a combinatorial proof of the existence of
f_{T}
, which is also closely related to the explicit computations of structure constants of the Hecke algebra
{\mathcal{H}}
. Following his own construction, Curtis computed
f_{T}
explicitly when
G=SL_{2}
in
[
2
]
and later with Shinoda
[
4
]
they gave explicit computation of
f_{T}
on maximal parabolics for
G=GL_{n}
. We note that both explicit computations in
[
2
]
and
[
4
]
exploited properties of Green functions over finite fields and they are hard to generalize to other cases.

In this note we propose a new approach to the problem following the ideas of
[
8
]
for Archimedean Whittaker functions. Namely we derive the Gelfand-Tsetlin formulas for
GL(n,{\mathbb{F}}_{q})
-Whittaker functions in Section 3 and 4 which share the same formalism and combinatorial interpretations in terms of Gelfand-Tsetlin diagrams with the
GL(n,{\mathbb{R}})
-Whittaker functions. As a consequence, we are able to give explicit computation of values of Curtis homomomorphisms for
G_{n}=GL(n,{\mathbb{F}}_{q})
and
T
being its maximal split tori (we shall fix
T=T_{n}
to be the subgroup of diagonal matrices) for the case of maximal and minimal parabolics (defined in Section 1). The main results of this note are stated in Theorems 2.1-2.3 of Section 2, which illustrate that the Curtis homomophisms are indeed ”sums” over the corresponding Gelfand-Tsetlin diagrams.

We first note that by work of Gelfand
[
7
]
, the homomorphisms
f_{T,\theta}
are in one-to-one correspondence with the Bessel functions of the generic representations of
G_{n}
. We also note that for every character
\underline{\chi}_{n}
of
T_{n}
, (
T_{n}
,
\underline{\chi}_{n}
) are in one-to-one correspondence with the principal series representations of
G_{n}
. Therefore the problem reduces to the explicit computation of Bessel functions corresponding to principal series representations.

In Section 1 we identify the Bessel functions (up to multiple of constants) with matrix coefficients of the corresponding principal series representations. We call these matrix coefficients
GL(n,{\mathbb{F}}_{q})
-Whittaker functions due to similarity of constructions for
GL_{n}
over Archimedean local
fields
[
8
]
. Namely, a recursive formula for
GL(n,{\mathbb{R}})
-Whittaker functions with respect to
n
was derived and a combinatorial interpretation of this recursion in terms of Gelfand-Tsetlin diagrams was explained. We propose (in Section 3 and 4) that the same result (in terms of formalism and combinatorial interpretation) in
[
8
]
also holds for their finite field counterparts defined in Section 1. Thus, we refer to as Gelfand-Tsetlin formulas in this note. Explicit formulas of Curtis homomophisms would easily follow once the Whittaker (Bessel) functions were explicitly computed by the Gelfand-Tsetlin formulas.

We also remark that these two types of values of Whittaker functions for the maximal and minimal parabolics were considered as Kloosterman sums previously. In particular, the values of Whittaker functions on anti-diagonals were called long element Kloosterman sums in
[
9
]
, or big cell Kloosterman sums in
[
1
,
6
]
.
GL_{3}
-long element Kloosterman sums was computed in
[

]
. An algorithm of computing
GL_{n}
-long element Kloosterman sums was described in
[
6
]
, however, in general it is hard to have explicit formulas for arbitrary

following this algorithm. Combinatorial interpretation of Kloosterman sums in terms of Gelfand-Tsetlin diagrams as well as recursion over the ranks were not considered previously as far as the author’s knowledge.

The plan of this note is as follows. We first review the notions of Bessel functions following the work of Gelfand
[
7
]
and identify them with Whittaker functions defined over finite fields. The main results in this note are explicit formulas of Curtis homomorphisms (Theorems 2.1-2.3) and the Gelfand-Tsetlin formulas of
GL(n,{\mathbb{F}}_{q})
-Whittaker functions(Theorem 3.1, 3.2 and 4.1). The values of Curtis homomorphisms are products of recursion kernels
\widetilde{Q}
, therefore they are ”sums” over the corresponding Gelfand-Tsetlin diagrams. In Section 3 and 4 we give Gelfand-Tsetlin formulas for the values of Whittaker functions on maximal and minimal parabolics respectively. The formulas are expressed recursively over the ranks, namely in both cases a
GL_{n}
-Whittaker function could be expressed in terms of
GL_{n-1}
-Whittaker functions and the recursion kernel is summed over a Gelfand-Tsetlin diagram. Therefore by resolving the recursive relations obtained we have a explicit formula of values of Whittaker functions and its combinatorial interpretation, as explained in Theorem 3.1, 3.2 and 4.1. In the last section we prove the proposition serving as key step while we derive recursions of anti-diagonal matrices, which has the same forms as its Archimedean counterparts (see Part 2 of
[
8
]
). We show that this is not merely a coincidence, and verify the appropriate expressions using Cauchy-Binet formula, which works for arbitrary fields.

1.
Preliminaries: Whittaker models, Bessel functions and Whittaker functions

Let
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
be a finite field with
q
elements, where
q
is a power of an odd prime, and
\psi:{\mathbb{F}}_{q}\rightarrow{\mathbb{C}}^{\times}
be a nontrivial additive character. Let
U_{n}
be the upper-triangular unipotent subgroup of general linear group
G_{n}=GL(n,{\mathbb{F}}_{q})
, therefore
\psi
defines a character on
U_{n}
by

\psi\begin{pmatrix}1&a_{1}&*&\cdots&*\\
&1&a_{2}&\ddots&\vdots\\
&&\ddots&\ddots&*\\
&&&1&a_{n-1}\\
&&&&1\end{pmatrix}=\psi\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}a_{i}\right).

Consider the Gelfand-Graev representation

Ind_{U_{n}}^{G_{n}}\psi=\{f:G_{n}\rightarrow{\mathbb{C}}\mid f(ug)=\psi(u)f(g)\quad\forall u\in U_{n},\forall g\in G_{n}\},

where
Ind_{U_{n}}^{G_{n}}\psi
is a subrepresentation of the right regular representation of
G_{n}
. Steinberg
[
12
]
showed that
Ind_{U_{n}}^{G_{n}}\psi
is multiplicity-free, i.e., every irreducible subrepresentations of
Ind_{U_{n}}^{G_{n}}\psi
appear exactly once. Following
[
7
]
, we call an irreducible representation
\pi
of
G_{n}
generic if it is isomorphic to a subrepresentation of
Ind_{U_{n}}^{G_{n}}\psi
, and we denote the unique subspace of
Ind_{U_{n}}^{G_{n}}\psi
which is isomorphic to
\pi
by
{\mathcal{W}}(\pi,\psi)
. We call
{\mathcal{W}}(\pi,\psi)
the
(
\psi
-)Whittaker model
of
\pi
, with respect to character
\psi
.

There are two ways of descibing irreducible representations entering Gelfand-Graev represenation. Gelfand
[
7
]
showed that for any irreducible generic representation
\pi
of
G_{n}
, there exists a unique element of
{\mathcal{W}}(\pi,\psi)
satisfying the following properties, which is called the
Bessel function
of
\pi
.

Definition 1.1
.

For any irreducible generic representation
\pi
of
G_{n}
, there exists an unique element
J_{\pi}\in{\mathcal{W}}(\pi,\psi)
such that:

(1)

J_{\pi}(I_{n})=1
, where
I_{n}
is the identity matrix;

(2)

J_{\pi}(u_{1}gu_{2})=\psi(u_{1}u_{2})J_{\pi}(g)
for every
u_{1},u_{2}\in U_{n}
,
g\in G_{n}
. (This property is also called
U_{n}
-bi-equivariance.)

Let
T_{n}
be the subgroup of diagonal matrices and
W_{n}
is the subgroup of permutation matrices (which is isomorphic to the Weyl group of
G_{n}
), we have the Bruhat decomposition of
G_{n}
following
[
7
]

(1.1)

\displaystyle G_{n}=U_{n}T_{n}W_{n}U_{n}.

Let
B_{n}^{-}
be a Borel subgroup of
G_{n}=GL(n,{\mathbb{F}}_{q})
consisting of all lower-triangular matrices, therefore (1.1) is equivalent to the following:

(1.2)

\displaystyle G_{n}=\bigsqcup\limits_{w\in W_{n}}B_{n}^{-}wU_{n}.

From (1.1) the (
U_{n},U_{n}
)-double cosets in
G_{n}
are parametrized by
N_{n}=T_{n}W_{n}
(which is the subgroup consisting of all monomial matrices). Therefore by the properties of Bessel functions stated above, we have the following theorem for the support of Bessel function
J_{\pi}
:

Theorem 1.1
.

(
[
7
]
, Proposition 4.9)

J_{\pi}
is supported on double cosets of the form

U_{n}g_{n_{1},\cdots,n_{s}}(c_{1},\cdots,c_{s})U_{n},

where for
n_{1},\cdots,n_{s}>0
with
n_{1}+\cdots+n_{s}=n
and
c_{1},\cdots,c_{s}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{\times}
,

g_{n_{1},\cdots,n_{s}}(c_{1},\cdots,c_{s})=\begin{pmatrix}0&\cdots&0&c_{1}I_{n_{1}}\\
\vdots&\iddots&c_{2}I_{n_{2}}&0\\
0&\iddots&\iddots&\vdots\\
c_{s}I_{n_{s}}&0&\cdots&0\par\end{pmatrix},

where
I_{n}
denotes the identity
n\times n
matrix.

Therefore by the property (2) of the Bessel function
J_{\pi}
mentioned above we may concentrate on the values of
J_{\pi}
on
g_{n_{1},\cdots,n_{s}}(c_{1},\cdots,c_{s})
.

In this note we concentrate on two cases of the support of the Bessel functions, namely matrices of the form
g_{1,n-1}(c_{1},1)
and
g_{n-1,1}(c_{1},1)
; and matrices of the form
g_{1,\cdots,1}(c_{1},\cdots,c_{n})
, i.e., anti-diagonal matrices. Since each partition of
n=n_{1}+\cdots+n_{s}
determines a parabolic subgroup of
G_{n}
, therefore we refer to the cases of
g_{1,n-1}(c_{1},1)
and
g_{n-1,1}(c_{1},1)
as
maximal parabolics
; and the cases of
g_{1,\cdots,1}(c_{1},\cdots,c_{n})
as
minimal parabolics
.

We first recall the notion of principal series representations of general linear groups over finite fields. Let
\underline{\chi}_{n}
be a character of the Borel subgroup
B_{n}^{-}
defined by

\underline{\chi}_{n}\begin{pmatrix}a_{1}&0&\cdots&0\\
*&a_{2}&\ddots&\vdots\\
\vdots&\ddots&\ddots&0\\
*&\cdots&*&a_{n}\\
\end{pmatrix}=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\chi_{i}(a_{i}),

where
\chi_{i}
,
1\leq i\leq n
, are multiplicative characters of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
. A principal series representation (
\pi_{\underline{\chi}_{n}},{\mathcal{V}}_{\underline{\chi}_{n}})
of
G_{n}
with parameter
\underline{\chi}_{n}=(\chi_{1},\cdots,\chi_{n})
is given by

{\mathcal{V}}_{\underline{\chi}_{n}}=Ind_{B_{n}^{-}}^{G_{n}}\underline{\chi}_{n}=\{f:G_{n}\rightarrow{\mathbb{C}}\mid f(bg)=\underline{\chi}_{n}(b)f(g)\quad\forall b\in B_{n}^{-},\forall g\in G_{n}\}.

From (1.2) we see the (
B_{n}^{-},B_{n}^{-}
)-double cosets in
G_{n}
are parametrized by
W_{n}
.

For
f_{1},f_{2}\in{\mathcal{V}}_{\underline{\chi}_{n}}
, we define the following inner product on
{\mathcal{V}}_{\underline{\chi}_{n}}
:

(1.3)

\displaystyle<f_{1},f_{2}>=\frac{1}{|G_{n}|}\sum_{g\in G_{n}}f_{1}(g)\overline{f_{2}(g)}.

By (1.1), we may rewrite (1.3) as follows:

(1.4)

\displaystyle<f_{1},f_{2}>=\frac{1}{[G_{n}:B_{n}^{-}]}\sum_{u\in U_{n}}\sum_{w\in W_{n}}f_{1}(wu)\overline{f_{2}(wu)}.

Consider
\psi_{n}\in{\mathcal{V}}_{\underline{\chi}_{n}}
defined by the following:

(1.5)

\displaystyle\psi_{n}(wu)=\begin{cases}\psi(u),\quad\quad w=I_{n}\\
0,\quad\quad\text{otherwise}\end{cases}.

\psi_{n}
is called the (
\psi
-)
Whittaker vector
of
{\mathcal{V}}_{\underline{\chi}_{n}}
. We have the following observation on
\psi_{n}
:

Lemma 1.1
.

For every
u^{\prime}\in U_{n}
, we have
\pi_{\underline{\chi}_{n}}(u^{\prime})\psi_{n}=\psi(u^{\prime})\psi_{n}
.

Proof.

For any given
u^{\prime}\in U_{n}
, we have
\pi_{\underline{\chi}_{n}}(u^{\prime})\psi_{n}(wu)=\psi_{n}(wuu^{\prime})
, where
w\in W_{n}
and
u\in U_{n}
are fixed. If
w=I_{n}
, then clearly we have
\pi_{\underline{\chi}_{n}}(u^{\prime})\psi_{n}(u)=\psi_{n}(uu^{\prime})=\psi_{n}(u^{\prime})\psi_{n}(u)
. If
w\neq I_{n}
, then
\psi_{n}(wuu^{\prime})=0
and
\psi_{n}(wu)=0
, therefore we still have
\pi_{\underline{\chi}_{n}}(u^{\prime})\psi_{n}(wu)=\psi(u^{\prime})\psi_{n}(wu)
as both sides are 0.

Note that
\psi_{n}
is determined by its values on
wu\in G_{n}
, therefore we conclude that
\pi_{\underline{\chi}_{n}}(u^{\prime})\psi_{n}=\psi(u^{\prime})\psi_{n}
.
∎

It is a standard result that (
\pi_{\underline{\chi}_{n}},{\mathcal{V}}_{\underline{\chi}_{n}})
is an irreducible generic representation if
\chi_{i}
(
1\leq i\leq n
) are all distinct. Kilmoyer
[
10
]
showed that
{\mathcal{V}}_{\underline{\chi}_{n}}
and
Ind_{U_{n}}^{G_{n}}\psi
have exactly one irreducible constituent in common, denoted by (
\pi_{\underline{\chi}_{n}}^{S},{\mathcal{V}}_{\underline{\chi}_{n}}^{S}
) which is called generalized Steinberg representation in
[
10
]
. Moreover,
\psi_{n}\in{\mathcal{V}}_{\underline{\chi}_{n}}^{S}
. We could identify
{\mathcal{V}}_{\underline{\chi}_{n}}^{S}
with its Whittaker model
{\mathcal{W}}(\pi_{\underline{\chi}_{n}}^{S},\psi)
via certain matrix coefficients of the representation (
\pi_{\underline{\chi}_{n}}^{S},{\mathcal{V}}_{\underline{\chi}_{n}}^{S}
):

Definition 1.2
.

For any
f\in{\mathcal{V}}_{\underline{\chi}_{n}}
, we have the following matrix coefficient of (
\pi_{\underline{\chi}_{n}},{\mathcal{V}}_{\underline{\chi}_{n}})

(1.6)

\displaystyle W_{\psi}(f):G_{n}\rightarrow{\mathbb{C}},\quad g\mapsto<\pi_{\underline{\chi}_{n}}(g)f,\psi_{n}>.

Moreover, by (1.3)-(1.5) we have

(1.7)

\displaystyle W_{\psi}(f)(g)=\frac{1}{[G_{n}:B_{n}^{-}]}\sum_{u\in U_{n}}(\pi_{\underline{\chi}_{n}}(g)f)(u)\overline{\psi_{n}(u)}.

In particular, we denote
W_{\psi}(\psi_{n})
by
\Psi_{\underline{\chi}_{n}}^{GL_{n}}
, which is called the
Whittaker function
associated with principal series representation (
\pi_{\underline{\chi}_{n}},{\mathcal{V}}_{\underline{\chi}_{n}})
.

Note that
W_{\psi}(f)
is a matrix coefficient of (
\pi_{\underline{\chi}_{n}}^{S},{\mathcal{V}}_{\underline{\chi}_{n}}^{S}
) if
f\in{\mathcal{V}}_{\underline{\chi}_{n}}^{S}
, in particular,
W_{\psi}(\psi_{n})
is a matrix coefficient of both (
\pi_{\underline{\chi}_{n}},{\mathcal{V}}_{\underline{\chi}_{n}})
and (
\pi_{\underline{\chi}_{n}}^{S},{\mathcal{V}}_{\underline{\chi}_{n}}^{S})
. We have the following explicit identification of the generalized Steinberg representation with its Whittaker model:

Proposition 1.1
.

We have the following identification of (
\pi_{\underline{\chi}_{n}}^{S},{\mathcal{V}}_{\underline{\chi}_{n}}^{S})
with its Whittaker model
{\mathcal{W}}(\pi_{\underline{\chi}_{n}}^{S},\psi)
:

W_{\psi}:{\mathcal{V}}_{\underline{\chi}_{n}}^{S}\rightarrow{\mathcal{W}}(\pi_{\underline{\chi}_{n}}^{S},\psi),\quad\quad f\mapsto W_{\psi}(f),

where for any
g\in G_{n}
,

W_{\psi}(f)(g)=<\pi_{\underline{\chi}_{n}}(g)f,\psi_{n}>=\frac{1}{[G_{n}:B_{n}^{-}]}\sum_{u\in U_{n}}(\pi_{\underline{\chi}_{n}}(g)f)(u)\overline{\psi_{n}(u)}.

Proof.

For any given
u\in U_{n}
and
g\in G_{n}
, we have

W_{\psi}(f)(ug)=<\pi_{\underline{\chi}_{n}}(ug)f,\psi_{n}>=<\pi_{\underline{\chi}_{n}}(g)f,\pi_{\underline{\chi}_{n}}(u^{-1})\psi_{n}>.

By Lemma 1.1 we have

\pi_{\underline{\chi}_{n}}(u^{-1})\psi_{n}=\psi_{n}(u^{-1})\psi=\overline{\psi(u)}\psi.

Therefore

W_{\psi}(f)(ug)=<\pi_{\underline{\chi}_{n}}(g)f,\overline{\psi(u)}\psi_{n}>=\psi(u)<\pi_{\underline{\chi}_{n}}(g)f,\psi_{n}>.

Hence we deduce that

W_{\psi}(f)(ug)=\psi(u)W_{\psi}(f)(ug),

whence for all
f\in{\mathcal{V}}_{\underline{\chi}_{n}}^{S}
,
W_{\psi}(f)\in Ind_{U_{n}}^{G_{n}}\psi

It is clear that
W_{\psi}
is a linear homomorphism between the underlying spaces. For any given
g,g^{\prime}\in G_{n}
we have

\displaystyle W_{\psi}(\pi_{\underline{\chi}_{n}}(g^{\prime})f)(g)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{[G_{n}:B_{n}^{-}]}\sum_{u\in U_{n}}\left(\pi_{\underline{\chi}_{n}}(g)(\underline{\pi}_{n}(g^{\prime})f)\right)(u)\overline{\psi_{n}(u)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{[G_{n}:B_{n}^{-}]}\sum_{u\in U_{n}}\left(\pi_{\underline{\chi}_{n}}(gg^{\prime})f)\right)(u)\overline{\psi_{n}(u)}

\displaystyle=<\pi_{\underline{\chi}_{n}}(gg^{\prime})f,\psi_{n}>

\displaystyle=g^{\prime}\cdot W_{\psi}(f)(g).

Hence we deduce that
W_{\psi}
embeds (
\pi_{\underline{\chi}_{n}}^{S},{\mathcal{V}}_{\underline{\chi}_{n}}^{S})
into the Gelfand-Graev representation.
Remark that

(1.8)

\displaystyle W_{\psi}(\psi_{n})(I_{n})=\frac{1}{[G_{n}:B_{n}^{-}]}\sum_{u\in U_{n}}\psi_{n}(u)\overline{\psi_{n}(u)}=\frac{|U_{n}|}{[G_{n}:B_{n}^{-}]}.

Therefore
W_{\psi}\neq 0
. Hence by Schur’s lemma we conclude that
W_{\psi}
is an isomorphism of representations.
∎

One key observation in this note is that for subrepresentations principal series representations entering the Gelfand-Graev representation, the Bessel function defined in Definition 1.1 and the Whittaker function defined in Definition 1.2 are proportional to each other. Namely we have the following proposition.

Proposition 1.2
.

For any given (
\pi_{\underline{\chi}_{n}}^{S},{\mathcal{V}}_{\underline{\chi}_{n}}^{S}
) we have

(1.9)

\displaystyle J_{\pi_{\underline{\chi}_{n}}^{S}}=\frac{[G_{n}:B_{n}^{-}]}{|U_{n}|}\Psi_{\underline{\chi}_{n}}^{GL_{n}}.

Proof.

For any given
u_{1},u_{2}\in U_{n}
and
g\in G_{n}
, we have

\Psi_{\underline{\chi}_{n}}^{GL_{n}}(u_{1}gu_{2})=<\pi_{\underline{\chi}_{n}}(u_{1}gu_{2})\psi_{n},\psi_{n}>=<\pi_{\underline{\chi}_{n}}(g)\left(\pi_{\underline{\chi}_{n}}(u_{2})\psi_{n}\right),\pi_{\underline{\chi}_{n}}(u_{1}^{-1})\psi_{n}>.

By Lemma 1.1 we have

\pi_{\underline{\chi}_{n}}(u_{2})\psi_{n}=\psi(u_{2})\psi,

and

\pi_{\underline{\chi}_{n}}(u_{1}^{-1})\psi_{n}=\overline{\psi(u_{1})}\psi.

Therefore

\Psi_{\underline{\chi}_{n}}^{GL_{n}}(u_{1}gu_{2})=<\pi_{\underline{\chi}_{n}}(g)\left(\psi(u_{2})\psi_{n}\right),\overline{\psi(u_{2})}\psi_{n}>=\psi(u_{1}u_{2})<\pi_{\underline{\chi}_{n}}(g)\psi_{n},\psi_{n}>.

Hence
\Psi_{\underline{\chi}_{n}}^{GL_{n}}
is
U_{n}
-bi-equivariant. By (1.8) we have

\frac{[G_{n}:B_{n}^{-}]}{|U_{n}|}\Psi_{\underline{\chi}_{n}}^{GL_{n}}(I_{n})=1.

Therefore the function
\frac{[G_{n}:B_{n}^{-}]}{|U_{n}|}\Psi_{\underline{\chi}_{n}}^{GL_{n}}
satisfies the two properties stated in Definition 1.1. Hence by the uniqueness of Bessel function we have

J_{\pi_{\underline{\chi}_{n}}^{S}}=\frac{[G_{n}:B_{n}^{-}]}{|U_{n}|}\Psi_{\underline{\chi}_{n}}^{GL_{n}}.

∎

From Proposition 1.2 we conclude that for every irreducible generic representations which appear as subrepresentations of principal series representations we may replace their Bessel functions by the Whittaker functions, which is an important observation while deriving the main results stated in the next section.

2.
Curtis homomorphisms and Gelfand-Tsetlin diagrams

Another way to describe all irreducible generic representations is to consider the endomorphism algebra of
Ind_{U_{n}}^{G_{n}}\psi
,
End_{G_{n}}(Ind_{U_{n}}^{G_{n}}\psi)
, which is called the
unipotent Hecke algebra
. Steinberg
[
12
]
showed that this unipotent Hecke algebra is commutative, which is equivalent to the multiplicity-free property of the representation
Ind_{U_{n}}^{G_{n}}\psi
. By Mackey’s theorem,
End_{G_{n}}(Ind_{U_{n}}^{G_{n}}\psi)
is isomorphic to the
U_{n}
-bi-equivariant functions on
G_{n}
, namely

{\mathcal{H}}_{\psi}(G_{n},U_{n})=\{f:G_{n}\rightarrow{\mathbb{C}}\mid f(u_{1}gu_{2})=\psi(u_{1}u_{2})f(g)\quad\forall u_{1},u_{2}\in U_{n},\forall g\in G_{n}\},

where the multiplicative structure of
{\mathcal{H}}_{\psi}(G_{n},U_{n})
is convolution of functions. Clearly we see that
\{J_{\pi}\mid\text{$\pi$ is an irreducible generic representation of $G_{n}$}\}
is a basis of
{\mathcal{H}}_{\psi}(G_{n},U_{n})
. Therefore the study of unipotent Hecke algebras is closely related to constructions of Bessel functions.

Let
e_{\psi_{n}}{\mathbb{C}}[G_{n}]e_{\psi_{n}}
be a subalgebra of the group algebra
{\mathbb{C}}[G_{n}]
, where
e_{\psi_{n}}
is an idempotent element in
{\mathbb{C}}[G_{n}]
given by

e_{\psi_{n}}=\frac{1}{|U_{n}|}\sum_{u\in U_{n}}\overline{\psi(u)}u.

It can be shown by Theorem 1.1 that
e_{\psi_{n}}g_{n_{1},\cdots,n_{s}}(c_{1},\cdots,c_{s})e_{\psi_{n}}
is a basis for
e_{\psi_{n}}{\mathbb{C}}[G_{n}]e_{\psi_{n}}
. Moreover, from Section 3 of
[
2
]
, we can identify
e_{\psi_{n}}{\mathbb{C}}[G_{n}]e_{\psi_{n}}
with the unipotent Hecke algebra
{\mathcal{H}}_{\psi}(G_{n},U_{n})

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathbb{C}}[T]}

4.
Gelfand-Tsetlin formula for minimal parabolics

In this section we aim to give explicit formula for

Theorem 4.1

n\geq 3
,let

(4.1)

\displaystyle\Psi_{\underline{\chi}_{n}}^{GL_{n}}(a_{1},\cdots,a_{n})=C_{n}\sum_{t_{1},\cdots,t_{n-1}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{\times}}Q_{GL_{n-1}}^{GL_{n}}(a_{1},\cdots,a_{n};t_{1},\cdots,t_{n-1}|\chi_{n})\Psi_{\underline{\chi}_{n-1}}^{GL_{n-1}}(t_{1},\cdots,t_{n-1}),

where

\displaystyle Q_{GL_{n-1}}^{GL_{n}}(a_{1},\cdots,a_{n};t_{1},\cdots,t_{n-1}|\chi_{n})

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces a_{n}}

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces y_{n-2,n-2}}

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces a_{n-1}}

.

For every

\displaystyle\Delta_{i}(u^{(n)}w_{0}^{(n)})=(-1)^{\frac{i(i-1)}{2}}\prod_{k=i}^{n-1}\prod_{j=k-i+1}^{k}y_{k,j},

and

(5.2)

\displaystyle\frac{\Delta_{i,i+1}(u^{(n)}w_{0}^{(n)})}{\Delta_{i}(u^{(n)}w_{0}^{(n)})}=\sum_{k=i}^{n-1}\prod_{m=k}^{n-1}\frac{y_{m+1,m-i+1}}{y_{m,m-i+1}},

Theorem 5.1

(5.3)

\displaystyle\Delta_{i}(u^{(n)}w_{0}^{(n)})=\prod_{k=1}^{i}y_{n-1,n-k}\Delta_{i}(u^{(n-1)}w_{0}^{(n-1)}).

.

(5.4)

\displaystyle\frac{\Delta_{i,i+1}(u^{(n)}w_{0}^{(n)})}{\Delta_{i}(u^{(n)}w_{0}^{(n)})}=\frac{1}{y_{n-1,n-i}}+\frac{y_{n-1,n-i-1}}{y_{n-1,n-i}}\frac{\Delta_{i,i+1}(u^{(n-1)}w_{0}^{(n-1)})}{\Delta_{i}(u^{(n-1)}w_{0}^{(n-1)})},

Theorem 5.3

(Cauchy-Binet formula)

and

i

\displaystyle|(MN)_{IJ}|=\sum_{K}|M_{IK}||N_{KJ}|,

[

]
. Using formula (5.5) we are able to prove both Theorem 5.1 and Theorem 5.2.

(Theorem 5.1)

A note on a result of Watson on the boundary behavior of positive solutions of the Heat equation
Utsav Dewan

Abstract
. By the classical Fatou-Kato Theorem, it is well-known that positive solutions of the Heat equation on the Euclidean upper half-space ℝ + n + 1 \mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+} , admit finite normal limit at almost every point on the boundary ℝ n \mathbb{R}^{n} . Watson estimated the size of the set in the boundary where a positive solution of the Heat equation blows up faster than a prescribed rate along normal limits, in terms of its outer Hausdorff measure (of a suitable dimension). The aim of this note is to give a corresponding sharpness result.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
35K08

1.
Introduction

We consider the Heat equation

(1.1)

\Delta u(x,t)=\frac{\partial}{\partial t}u(x,t)\>,

on the Euclidean upper half-space
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}=\{(x,t):x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},t>0\}
, where

\Delta=\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x^{2}_{i}}\>,

is the Laplacian on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

By the classical Fatou-Kato Theorem
[
5
]
,
it is well-known that positive solutions of the Heat equation (
1.1
) admit finite normal limit at almost every point on the boundary
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Then a natural question to ask is: how does a positive solution of the Heat equation behave along normal limits, on the complement of this full measure subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. More precisely, how quickly can a positive solution of the Heat equation grow or how large can the exceptional set (in the boundary) be where a positive solution of the Heat equation blows up faster than a prescribed rate.

To address these questions, we first note that a positive solution of the Heat equation
u
on
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}
, is represented by a unique non-negative Borel measure
\mu
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, in the following way
[
7
, P. 93-99]
:

u(x,t)=W[\mu](x,t)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}W(x-y,t)\>d\mu(y)\>,\text{ for }(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}\>,

such that the integral is well-defined for all
(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}
, where

(1.2)

W(x,t):=\frac{1}{{(4\pi t)}^{n/2}}\>e^{-\frac{{\|x\|}^{2}}{4t}}\>,\text{ for }(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}\>,

is the Gauss-Weierstrass kernel or the Heat kernel of
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}
. The kernel is normalized so that

(1.3)

W[\lambda]\equiv 1\>,

where
\lambda
is the Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. For non-negative
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, we will denote the corresponding Gauss-Weierstrass integral by
W[f]
, in stead of
W[f\>d\lambda]
.

Now for any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and any
t\in(0,1)
, the expression (
1.2
) yields

(1.4)

u(x,t)\leq\frac{1}{{(4\pi t)}^{n/2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{-\frac{{\|x-y\|}^{2}}{4}}\>d\mu(y)={\left(\frac{1}{t}\right)}^{n/2}\>W[\mu](x,1)\>.

Then as
0<W[\mu](x,1)<+\infty
, (
1.4
) motivates one to estimate the size of the following set:

S_{\alpha}(u):=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\displaystyle\limsup_{t\to 0}t^{\alpha/2}u(x,t)=+\infty\}\>,\text{ for }0\leq\alpha<n\>.

In
[
6
, Theorem 6]
, Watson showed that for any positive solution of the Heat equation
u
on
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}
, the
(n-\alpha)
-dimensional outer Hausdorff measure of the set
S_{\alpha}(u)
is zero, that is

\mathcal{H}^{n-\alpha}\left(S_{\alpha}(u)\right)=0\>.

Then it is natural to ask whether the above result is sharp in the following sense: given a realizable set
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
\mathcal{H}^{n-\alpha}\left(E\right)=0
, does there exist a positive solution of the Heat equation
u
on
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}
such that
E\subset S_{\alpha}(u)
. This article answers the above question in the affirmative:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
0\leq\alpha<n
, and
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
\mathcal{H}^{n-\alpha}(E)=0
, then there exists a positive solution of the Heat equation
u
on
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}
, such that
E\subset S_{\alpha}(u)
.

Theorem
1.1
is motivated by recent works on the similar problem of estimating the size of exceptional sets along radial limits for positive superharmonic functions, by Hirata on the Euclidean unit ball
[
3
]
, on the real Hyperbolic ball
[
4
]
and by the author on non-positively curved Harmonic manifolds of purely exponential volume growth
[
1
]
.

This article is organized as follows. In section
2
, we recall the definitions of some notions related to Hausdorff measure. In section
3
, we first see an auxiliary lemma and then prove Theorem
1.1
.

Throughout this article
C_{1},C_{2},\dots
will be used to denote positive constants, with dependence on dimension or parameters made explicit. The indicator function of a set
A
will be denoted by
\chi_{A}
. For
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
r>0
,
B(x,r)
will denote the Euclidean ball with center
x
and radius
r
.

2.
Preliminaries on Hausdorff Measure

In this section, we briefly recall the definitions of Hausdorff dimensions, Hausdorff measure. These can be found in
[
2
]
.

Let
(X,d)
be a metric space. Then for a set
E\subset X
,
\textsf{d}(E)
will denote the diameter of
E
. For
\varepsilon>0
, an
\varepsilon
-cover of
E\subset X
is a countable (or finite) collection of sets
\{U_{i}\}
with

0<\textsf{d}\left(U_{i}\right)\leq\varepsilon\text{, for all }i\text{ such that }E\subset\displaystyle\bigcup_{i}U_{i}\>.

For
s\geq 0
, we recall that

\mathcal{H}^{s}_{\varepsilon}(E):=\inf\left\{\displaystyle\sum_{i}{\left(\textsf{d}\left(U_{i}\right)\right)}^{s}:\{U_{i}\}\text{ is an }\varepsilon\text{-cover of }E\right\}\>.

Then the
s
-dimensional Hausdorff outer measure of
E
is defined by,

\mathcal{H}^{s}(E):=\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\mathcal{H}^{s}_{\varepsilon}(E)\>.

The above value remains unaltered if one only considers covers consisting of balls. This outer measure restricts to a measure (also denoted by)
\mathcal{H}^{s}
on a
\sigma
-algebra that contains all Borel sets. It is called the
s
-dimensional Hausdorff measure.

The Hausdorff dimension of
E
is defined by

dim_{\mathcal{H}}E:=\inf\left\{s\geq 0:\mathcal{H}^{s}(E)=0\right\}\>.

If
s=dim_{\mathcal{H}}E
, then
\mathcal{H}^{s}(E)
may be zero or infinite or
\mathcal{H}^{s}(E)\in(0,\infty)
.

3.
Proof of the main theorem

We first start off with the following auxiliary lemma:

Lemma 3.1
.

There exists a constant
C_{1}\in(0,1)
, depending only on the dimension
n
such that for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\>,\delta>0
and
t\in(0,C^{2}_{1}\delta^{2}]
,

W\left[\chi_{B(x,\delta)}\right](x,t)\geq\frac{1}{2}\>.

Proof.

Choose and fix
t\in(0,C^{2}_{1}\delta^{2}]
. By (
1.3
), we have

W\left[\chi_{B(x,\delta)}\right](x,t)=1-\frac{1}{{(4\pi t)}^{n/2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B(x,\delta)}e^{-\frac{{\|x-y\|}^{2}}{4t}}\>d\lambda(y)\>.

Let
\omega_{n}
denote the surface area of the unit sphere
S^{n-1}
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. By integration in polar coordinates, some elementary change of variables and simplification of the integration in terms of Gamma functions yield the existence of a positive constant
C_{2}
which depends only on the dimension
n
, such that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B(x,\delta)}e^{-\frac{{\|x-y\|}^{2}}{4t}}\>d\lambda(y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\omega_{n}\int_{\delta}^{+\infty}e^{-\frac{r^{2}}{4t}}\>r^{n-1}\>dr

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2^{n}\>\omega_{n}\>t^{n/2}\int_{\frac{\delta}{2\sqrt{t}}}^{+\infty}e^{-r^{2}}\>r^{n-1}\>dr

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2^{n}\>\omega_{n}\>t^{n/2}\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-\left(r+\frac{\delta}{2\sqrt{t}}\right)^{2}}\>{\left(r+\frac{\delta}{2\sqrt{t}}\right)}^{n-1}\>dr

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2^{n}\>\omega_{n}\>t^{n/2}\>e^{-\frac{\delta^{2}}{4t}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-\left(\frac{r\delta}{\sqrt{t}}\right)}\>{\left(r+\frac{\delta}{2\sqrt{t}}\right)}^{n-1}\>dr

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2^{n}\>\omega_{n}\>t^{n/2}\>e^{-\frac{\delta^{2}}{4t}}\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\binom{n-1}{j}{\left(\frac{\delta}{2\sqrt{t}}\right)}^{j}\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-\left(\frac{r\delta}{\sqrt{t}}\right)}\>r^{n-1-j}\>dr

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2^{n}\>\omega_{n}\>t^{n/2}\>e^{-\frac{\delta^{2}}{4t}}\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\binom{n-1}{j}\frac{1}{2^{j}}{\left(\frac{\delta}{\sqrt{t}}\right)}^{2j-n}\Gamma(n-j)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2^{n}\>C_{2}\>t^{n/2}\>e^{-\frac{\delta^{2}}{4t}}\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}{\left(\frac{\delta}{\sqrt{t}}\right)}^{2j-n}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2^{n}\>C_{2}\>e^{-\frac{\delta^{2}}{4t}}\>\delta^{n}\>\left(\frac{1}{\left(\frac{\delta}{\sqrt{t}}\right)-1}\right)\>.

Hence setting
C_{3}=C_{2}\>\pi^{-n/2}\>,
we get that

\frac{1}{{(4\pi t)}^{n/2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B(x,\delta)}e^{-\frac{{\|x-y\|}^{2}}{4t}}\>d\lambda(y)\\
\leq C_{3}\>e^{-\frac{\delta^{2}}{4t}}\>{\left(\frac{\delta}{\sqrt{t}}\right)}^{n}\>\left(\frac{1}{\left(\frac{\delta}{\sqrt{t}}\right)-1}\right)\>.

Now note that for
C_{4}>1
, sufficiently large (depending only on the dimension
n
), there exist positive constants
C_{5},C_{6}
depending only on the dimension
n
, such that for

\frac{\delta}{\sqrt{t}}\geq C_{4}\>,

one has

(3.1)

\left(\frac{\delta}{\sqrt{t}}-1\right)\geq C_{5}\>\frac{\delta}{\sqrt{t}}\>,\text{ and }e^{-\frac{\delta^{2}}{4t}}\>{\left(\frac{\delta}{\sqrt{t}}\right)}^{n}\leq C_{6}\>.

Thus setting

C_{1}=\min\left\{\frac{1}{C_{4}},\frac{C_{5}}{2C_{3}C_{6}}\right\}\>,

and plugging (
3.1
) in (
3
), the result follows as

\displaystyle\frac{1}{{(4\pi t)}^{n/2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B(x,\delta)}e^{-\frac{{\|x-y\|}^{2}}{4t}}\>d\lambda(y)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{C_{3}C_{6}}{C_{5}}\left(\frac{\sqrt{t}}{\delta}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\>.

∎

We now prove Theorem
1.1
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Since
\mathcal{H}^{n-\alpha}(E)=0
, for any
m\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists a covering of
E
by balls
\{B^{m}_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
such that
their diameters satisfy

(3.2)

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}{(\textsf{d}\left(B^{m}_{j}\right))}^{n-\alpha}<2^{-m}\>.

If
B
is a ball in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with center
x
and radius
r
, then for notational convenience,
2B
will denote the ball with center
x
and radius
2r
.

Now define,

f:=\displaystyle\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}m\>{\left(\textsf{d}\left(B^{m}_{j}\right)\right)}^{-\alpha}\>\chi_{2B^{m}_{j}}\>.

Then there exists a positive constant
C_{7}
depending only on the dimension
n
, such that by (
3.2
),

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f\>d\lambda

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}m\>{\left(\textsf{d}\left(B^{m}_{j}\right)\right)}^{-\alpha}\>\lambda\left(2B^{m}_{j}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{7}\displaystyle\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}m\>{\left(\textsf{d}\left(B^{m}_{j}\right)\right)}^{n-\alpha}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{7}\displaystyle\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}m\>2^{-m}<+\infty\>.

Hence
f\>d\lambda
defines a finite, non-negative Borel measure on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and thus
W[f]
defines a positive solution of the Heat equation on
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}
.

Now let
x\in E
and
m\in\mathbb{N}
. Then there exists
j_{m}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
x\in B^{m}_{j_{m}}
. If
r_{m}
is the radius of
B^{m}_{j_{m}}
then

B(x,r_{m})\subset 2B^{m}_{j_{m}}\>.

Then by Lemma
3.1
, one has

(3.3)

W\left[\chi_{2B^{m}_{j_{m}}}\right](x,t)\geq W\left[\chi_{B(x,r_{m})}\right](x,t)\geq 1/2\>,

whenever
t\leq C^{2}_{1}r^{2}_{m}
.

Thus defining
t_{m}:=C^{2}_{1}r^{2}_{m}
, we have by (
3.3
),

\displaystyle W[f](x,t_{m})

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle m{\left(\textsf{d}\left(B^{m}_{j_{m}}\right)\right)}^{-\alpha}\>W\left[\chi_{2B^{m}_{j_{m}}}\right](x,t_{m})

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle m\left(2^{-(\alpha+1)}C^{\alpha}_{1}\right)t^{-\alpha/2}_{m}\>.

So there exists a positive constant
C_{8}
, depending only on the dimension
n
and the parameter
\alpha
, such that

t^{\alpha/2}_{m}\>W[f](x,t_{m})\geq C_{8}\>m\>.

By (
3.2
),
t_{m}\to 0
as
m\to+\infty
, and hence we get the result.
∎

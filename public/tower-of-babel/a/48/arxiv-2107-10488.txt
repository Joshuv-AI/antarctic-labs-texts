High dimensional expansion implies amplified local testability
Tali Kaufman, Izhar Oppenheim

Abstract
In this work we show that high dimensional expansion implies locally testable code. Specifically, we define a notion that we call high-dimensional-expanding-system (HDE-system). This is a set system defined by incidence relations with certain high dimensional expansion relations between its sets. We say that a linear code is modelled over HDE-system, if the collection of linear constraints that the code satisfies could by described via the HDE-system. We show that a code that can be modelled over HDE-system is locally testable. This implies that high dimensional expansion phenomenon solely implies local testability of codes. Prior work had to rely to local notions of local testability to get some global forms of testability (e.g. co-systolic expansion from local one, global agreement from local one), while our work infers global testability directly from high dimensional expansion without relying on some local form of testability. The local testability result that we obtain from HDE-systems is, in fact, stronger than standard one, and we term it amplified local testability . Roughly speaking in an amplified locally testable code, the rejection probability of a corrupted codeword, that is not too far from the code, is amplified by a k k factor compared to the guarantee in standard testing, where k k is the length of the test (the guarantee for a corrupted codeword that is very far from the code is the same as in standard testing). Amplified testing is stronger than standard local testability, but weaker than the notion of optimal testing as defined by Bhattacharyya et al., that roughly requires amplified local testability without assuming that the corrupted codeword is not too far from the code. We further show that most of the well studied locally testable codes as Reed-Muller codes and more generally affine invariant codes with single-orbit property fall into our framework. Namely, it is possible to show that they are modelled over an HDE-system, and hence the family of all p-ary affine invariant codes is amplified locally testable. This yields the strongest known testing results for affine invariant codes with single orbit, strengthening the work of Kaufman and Sudan.

Introduction

High dimensional expansion implies testability.

The aim of this work is to show that codes arising from high dimensional expanding set systems are locally testable. Specifically, we define the notion of
High Dimensional Expanding System (
\operatorname{HDE-System}
)
that is a two layer expanding set system that generalizes two layer set systems arising from high dimensional expanders. Using this new concept, we show that codes whose constraints form an
\operatorname{HDE-System}
are locally testable.

Testability of well studied codes via high dimensional expansion.

We further show that most well studied locally testable codes as Reed-Muller codes and more generally affine-invariant codes are, in fact,
\operatorname{HDE-System}
codes! Hence, their local testability could be re-inferred using our current work; and could be attributed to the high dimensional expansion phenomenon. Specifically, we give a high dimension expansion based proof to the local testability of single orbit affine invariant codes, that strengthen the well known result of Kaufman and Sudan
[
KS08
]
.

High dimensional expansion implies amplified testability.

In the following we define locally testable codes, and a strictly stronger notion of testability that we term amplified locally testable codes.
Roughly speaking in an amplified locally testable code, the rejection probability of a corrupted codeword, that is not too far from the code, is amplified by a
k
factor compared to the guarantee in standard testing, where
k
is the length of the test (the guarantee for a corrupted codeword that is very far from the code is the same as in standard testing). Our main goal would be to show that local testability could be inferred by high dimensional expansion machinery. Furthermore, in cases where high dimensional expansion implies locally testable codes, the derived codes are not only locally testable, but rather they are also amplified locally testable. By applying this machinery to single orbit affine invariant codes, we get that these codes are amplified locally testable, which is the strongest notion of testability known for such codes, strengthening the well known work of Kaufman and Sudan
[
KS08
]
.

Definition 1.1

(Locally testable code)

Given a linear code

-query tests, define

-query tests that

Let

The best we hope for amplified local testing is

In this work we show that a code which can be described via
\operatorname{HDE-System}
, not only we can infer local testability for it, but rather we can infer amplified local testability for it!

Local testability via unique neighbor expansion.

We show that
\lambda
-expanding
\operatorname{HDE-System}
has some form of unique neighbor expansion property associated with it (see Definition
3.3
). We also show that if the HDE-system has a strong enough unique neighbor expansion property, then a linear code defined based on this system is amplified locally testable. We prove that this is the case for affine-invariant codes with the single orbit property. Thus, HDE-system provides a mechanism to get amplified local testability of codes. Prior to our work, there was not a general phenomenon that explains local testability. In this work we show that local testability, and in fact a stronger notion of amplified local testability, is a high dimensional expansion phenomenon!

1.1
Comparison to prior works

A recent work of Dikstein at el.
[
DDHRZ20
]
seems superficially close to the methods of this paper, since both works deduce local testability using ideas stemming from high dimensional expansion. The reader should note that there are major difference between the works:

•

The work of Dikstein at el.
[
DDHRZ20
]
relies on the idea that ”global” local testability can be inferred from ”local” local testability. I.e., in
[
DDHRZ20
]
, the assumption is that the code contains many small (i.e., ”local”) locally testable codes and by expansion considerations, it follows that the global code is locally testable. This is also the point of view of
[
KKL14
,
EK16
,
KM18
]
that considered what can be thought of as ”co-cycle codes” and the global testability was be derived assuming they are composed of small local codes that are locally testable (aka ”the links” code). In contrast to
[
DDHRZ20
]
(and to
[
KKL14
,
EK16
,
KM18
]
), the focus of this current work is to get local testability of codes
directly
from high dimensional expansion phenomenon. Deducing local testability of codes directly from high dimensional expansion (without relying on any local code that is locally testable) is new and is achieved here for the first time.

•

Our work has the benefit of deducing not only local testability, but rather amplified local testability which was not achieved in
[
DDHRZ20
]
.

•

As far as we know, the work of
[
DDHRZ20
]
does not apply to the family of affine invariant codes, but only to a sub-family of lifted codes. Thus, in terms of generality, our work seems to apply in a more general setting.

It is also beneficial to compare the results of this paper to previous results regarding single orbit affine invariant codes. In
[
KS08
, Theorem 2.9]
, it was shown that single orbit affine invariant codes are locally testable. Using our new machinery, we improve on this result, showing the the family of all affine invariant codes has amplified local testability. As noted above, a stronger result was known for Reed-Muller codes (which is a sub-family of the family of affine invariant codes), but, prior to our work, no general treatment was available to the entire family of single orbit affine invariant codes.

1.2
High Dimensional Expanding System (
\operatorname{HDE-System}
)

Our main definition towards defining high dimensional expander codes is called High-Dimensional-Expanding-System or
\operatorname{HDE-System}
for short.

We start by defining a
(s,k,K)
-Two layer system:

Definition 1.6

(
(s,k,K)
-Two layer system)
.

A two layer system
X
is a system
X=(V,E,T)
of three sets:

1.

A finite set

whose elements are called vertices.

2.

A set

such that
|\tau|=k
for every
\tau\in E

\bigcup_{\tau\in E}\tau=V
.

3.

A set
T\subseteq 2^{E}
such that
|\sigma|=K
for every
\sigma\in T
and
\bigcup_{\sigma\in T}\sigma=E
.

4.

By abuse of notation, we will denote
v\in\sigma
for
v\in V,\sigma\in T
if there is
\tau\in\sigma
such that
v\in\tau
. Using this notation, for every
\sigma\in T
and every

2\leq|\{\tau\in\sigma:v\in\tau\}|\leq s.

Roughly speaking,
\operatorname{HDE-System}
is a two layer system with good expansion properties. In order to give the definition, we need to define several graphs associated with a two layer system. We note that all the graphs defined below will be actually considered as weighted graphs with a weight function induced by weights on
T

Definition 1.7

(The ground graph)
.

For a two layer system
X=(V,E,T)
, the ground graph of
X
is the graph whose vertices are
V
and edges are
\{\{v,u\}:\exists\tau\in E,u,v\in\tau\}
.

Definition 1.8

(Link of a vertex)
.

For a two layer system
X=(V,E,T)
and

is the graph whose vertex set is
E_{v}=\{\tau\in E:v\in\tau\}
and whose edge set is

T_{v}=\{\{\tau,\tau^{\prime}\}:\tau\neq\tau^{\prime}\text{ and }\exists\sigma\in T\text{ such that }\tau,\tau^{\prime}\in\sigma\}.

Definition 1.9

(The non-intersecting graph)
.

For a two layer system
X=(V,E,T)

is a graph whose vertex set is
E
and edge set is

\{\{\tau,\tau^{\prime}\}:\tau\cap\tau^{\prime}=\emptyset\text{ and }\exists\sigma\in T,\text{ such that }\tau,\tau^{\prime}\in\sigma\}.

This graph corresponds to the Non-Intersecting Walk, i.e., to the walk from a between elements of
E
that do NOT intersect (as subsets of

An
\operatorname{HDE-System}
is a two layer system

in which all these graphs are expanding. More precisely, for

\lambda
-expander is it is connected and either the second largest eigenvalue of the is

Definition 1.10
.

(High Dimensional Expanding System (
\operatorname{HDE-System}
)) [informal, for formal see Definition
4.8
]
For

, a (weighted) two layer system

is called
\lambda
-expanding-
\operatorname{HDE-System}
if the ground graph and the links of all the vertices are
\lambda
-expanders and the non-intersecting graph is either totally disconnect (i.e., it has no edges) or a

-expander.

High Dimensional expanders imply
\operatorname{HDE-System}

Expanding HDE’s have unique neighbor expansion for small sets that are also locally small.

Our main motivation for the definition of

.

(Main Theorem 1: Unique neighbor expansion property for

Given a

On the ability to get unique neighbor expansion from HDE-systems.

The idea behind the proof of Main Theorem 1 is to use the expansion of the links in order to derive unique neighbor expansion. The links are very good expanders so a set that is locally small has the property that its local views in the links expand a lot. Each link induces by its local view many ”potential unique neighbors”. However, it could be that the local views of the links will interfere and the ”potential unique neighbors” by the ”links opinion” will turn out to be non unique neighbors. Since the system is expanding the total interference between links is small and thus the overall unique neighbor property is implied.

1.3
\operatorname{HDE-System}
Codes

Given a two layer system
X=(V,E,T)
as above, we want to use it as a ”foundation” and for constructing a code. Such a construction it is not unique and cannot be done for every
X
. However, for a code that ”could be constructed via

.

Before describing this construction, we need to establish some terminology and notation: Let
C\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{p}^{V}
be a linear code (where
V
is a finite set) with a check matrix
H
.

•

We denote by
\mathcal{E}=\mathcal{E}(H)
the rows
H
and we refer to
\mathcal{E}
as the constraints of the code (or
k
-constraints if they all have a support of size
k

\mathcal{E}
are

\underline{c}\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{V}

\underline{c}\in C
if and only if for every
\underline{e}\in\mathcal{E}

\underline{e}\cdot\underline{c}=0
(recall that
\underline{e},\underline{c}
are indexed by the elements in

\underline{e}\cdot\underline{c}=\sum_{v}\underline{e}(v)\underline{c}(v)

•

For
\underline{e}\in\mathcal{E}

\underline{e}
as

\operatorname{supp}(\underline{e})=\{v\in V:\underline{e}(v)\neq 0\}.

•

A
linear dependency
of
\mathcal{E}
is a function
\operatorname{ld}:\mathcal{E}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}_{p}
such that for every
\underline{c}\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{V}

\sum_{\underline{e}\in\mathcal{E}}\operatorname{ld}(\underline{e})(\underline{e}\cdot\underline{c})=0
. In other words, if we think of the row vector
\underline{\operatorname{ld}}=(\operatorname{ld}(\underline{e}))_{\underline{e}\in\mathcal{E}}
, then
\underline{\operatorname{ld}}H=\underline{0}
. As above, the support of
\operatorname{ld}
is the set

\operatorname{supp}(\operatorname{ld})=\{\underline{e}\in\mathcal{E}:\operatorname{ld}(\underline{e})\neq 0\}.

.

Consider

If

On the ability to get local testability from unique neighbor expansion.

We assume we are in a situation that we have a code that is modelled over an HDE-system. Thus, we know that each
k
-constraint of the code is participating in a linear dependency.
This means that on every dependency, if there is one violated constraint that touches it, there must be another one that touches it.

We are given a vector that falsifies few constraints from the code and we want to show that such a vector is close to the code.
We can try to correct it by flipping variables such that this flipping reduces the number of violated constraints.
Clearly, such a procedure will not flip more variables then the total number of unsatisfied constraints which we assume to be small.

If you arrived at a situation when no more flipping can be done then you know that on each variable (assuming we work over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
) the fraction of violating constraints is at most
\frac{p-1}{p}
of the constraints that it participates in (making it
\frac{p-1}{p}
-locally small). However, the unique neighbor expansion implies that there are linear dependencies that ”sees” only one violating constraint! but as we said, this is not possible. So when we arrived at a situation where no more flipping is possible, we, in fact, arrived at a codeword that is close to our initial vector as required.

where

is the size of the support of the constraint defining

\operatorname{HDE-System}
with

\operatorname{rej}(\underline{c})\geq k(C)\frac{1}{2^{15}p^{4}}\min\left\{\min_{\underline{c}^{\prime}\in C}\|\underline{c}-\underline{c}^{\prime}\|,\frac{1}{k(C)^{3}}\right\}.

We compare this result to the (non-amplified) local testing for affine invariant codes of Kaufman and Sudan

.

[

For every

Local testability when
s>2
.

The main focus of this work is proving amplified local testability for codes modelled over HDE-systems with
s=2
. We further have a more general treatment for codes modelled over HDE-system with general
s\geq 3
under some extra-assumptions. Roughly speaking, for the case of
s\geq 3
we need the extra assumption that the code is composed of local small codes that are locally testable. The difficulty in the case where
s\geq 3
is that the bit flipping argument we described above can only correct the code to be
\frac{p-1}{p}
-locally small, while ”The unique neighbor Theorem” says that we can deduce the
(\delta,\varepsilon_{0})
-unique neighbor property from expansion given that
\delta<\frac{1}{s-1}
. Thus, in the case where
s\geq 3
, we may not be able correct a corrupted codeword by bit flipping to a setting in which we can apply our unique neighbor argument. This difficulty is dealt by adding the assumption of ”local” local testability that grantees that correcting by bit flipping converges to a word that is
\delta
-locally small (and thus we can use our previous machinery). This new method requires some additional definitions and we refer the reader to Section
7
for further details.

Distance of HDE codes.

An additional result is that for codes modelled over HDE-systems, the distance of the code can be bounded in terms of the expansion of the HDE system. This is done explicitly in Section
8
(see Theorem
8.1
).

Organization of this paper:

In Section
2
, we define weighted two layer systems and the induced weight functions on the graphs associated to it (the ground graph, the non-intersecting graph and the links). In Section
3
, we prove Main Theorem 2 above, showing how unique neighbor expansion implies (amplified) local testability. In Section
4
, we state and prove Main Theorem 1 above. showing that an expanding
\operatorname{HDE-System}
has the unique neighbor property. In Section
5
, we show how to combine both our main Theorems in order to deduce amplified local testability for codes modelled over HDE-systems. In Section
6
, we show that our new machinery can be used to show that the family of p-ary single orbit affine invariant codes is amplified locally testable. In Section
7
, we explain how to deduce local testability for codes modelled over HDE-systems with
s\geq 3
under additional assumptions of ”local” local testability. In Section
8
, we show that codes modelled over
\operatorname{HDE-System}
also have a bound on the distance of the code coming from expansion considerations.

Two layer set systems

Let
X=(V,E,T)
be a two layer system (see Definition
1.6
). We fix a function
w:T\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{+}
and define the weight function
w:V\cup E\cup T\rightarrow\mathbb{N}
as follows:

\forall\tau\in E,w(\tau)=\sum_{\tau\in\sigma}w(\sigma),

\forall v\in V,w(v)=\sum_{v\in\sigma}w(\sigma).

Also, for a set
A\subseteq V\cup E\cup T
, we define

w(A)=\sum_{\eta\in A}w(\eta).

Proposition 2.1
.

For every
v\in V
,

2w(v)\leq w(\{\tau\in E:v\in\tau\})\leq sw(v).

Proof of Proposition
2.1
.

Fix
v\in V
. By definition of
w
it holds that

w(\{\tau\in E:v\in\tau\})=\sum_{\tau\in E,v\in\tau}w(\tau)=\sum_{\tau\in E,v\in\tau}\left(\sum_{\sigma\in T,\tau\in\sigma}m(\sigma)\right)=\sum_{\sigma\in T,v\in\sigma}m(\sigma)\left(\sum_{\tau\in E,v\in\tau\subseteq\sigma}1\right)\geq\sum_{\sigma\in T,v\in\sigma}2m(\sigma)=2w(v),

and the proof of the second inequality is similar, using the fact that

\sum_{\tau\in E,v\in\tau\subseteq\sigma}1\leq s.

∎

Corollary 2.2
.

For every
X
as above,

\frac{2}{k}w(V)\leq w(E)\leq\frac{s}{k}w(V).

Proof of Corollary
2.2
.

By Proposition
2.1
,

w(V)=\sum_{v\in V}w(v)\leq\sum_{v\in V}\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\tau\in E,v\in\tau}w(\tau)=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\tau\in E}w(\tau)\sum_{v\in\tau}1=\frac{k}{2}w(E),

and the proof of the second inequality is similar.
∎

2.1
Weighted graphs

For a finite graph
G=(V,E)
, a

on the edges is any positive function

. This induces a positive function
m:V\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{+}
by

\forall v\in V,m(v)=\sum_{e\in E,v\in e}m(e).

Also, for
\emptyset\neq U\subseteq V
, we denote

m(U)=\sum_{v\in U}m(v).

A
weighted graph
is a graph
(V,E)
a weight function.

2.2
The ground graph

Recall that the ground graph of
X
is the
1
-skeleton of the hyper-graph
(V,E)
. Concretely, the ground graph is the graph with the vertex set
V
such that for every
u,v\in V
,
v
and
u
are connected by an edge if there is
\tau\in E
such that
v,u\in\tau
. Define a weight function
m_{\operatorname{gr}}
on this graph as

m_{\operatorname{gr}}(\{v,u\})=\sum_{\tau\in E,v,u\in\tau}w(\tau).

Proposition 2.3
.

For every
v\in V
,

2(k-1)w(v)\leq m_{\operatorname{gr}}(v)\leq s(k-1)w(v)
.

Proof of Proposition
2.3
.

Fix
v\in V
. By the definition of
m_{\operatorname{gr}}
,

m_{\operatorname{gr}}(v)=\sum_{u\in V,u\neq v}m_{\operatorname{gr}}(\{v,u\})=\sum_{u,u\neq v}\left(\sum_{\tau\in E,v,u\in\tau}\left(\sum_{\sigma\in T,\tau\in\sigma}w(\sigma)\right)\right)=\sum_{\sigma\in T,v\in\sigma}w(\sigma)\left(\sum_{\tau\in E,v\in\tau\in\sigma}\left(\sum_{u,u\neq v,u\in\tau}1\right)\right)=\sum_{\sigma\in T,v\in\sigma}w(\sigma)\left(\sum_{\tau\in E,v\in\tau\in\sigma}k-1\right).

Recall that for every
\sigma\in T
and every
v\in\sigma
it holds that

2\leq|\{\tau\in E:v\in\tau\in\sigma\}|\leq s

and thus

\sum_{\sigma\in T,v\in\sigma}w(\sigma)\left(\sum_{\tau\in E,v\in\tau\in\sigma}k-1\right)\geq\sum_{\sigma\in T,v\in\sigma}2(k-1)=2(k-1)w(v),

\sum_{\sigma\in T,v\in\sigma}w(\sigma)\left(\sum_{\tau\in E,v\in\tau\in\sigma}k-1\right)\leq\sum_{\sigma\in T,v\in\sigma}s(k-1)=s(k-1)w(v).

∎

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
U\subseteq V
be a non-empty set. For
1\leq i\leq k
, denote

E^{i}_{U}=\{\tau\in E:|\tau\cap U|=i\}.

Then

m_{\operatorname{gr}}(U,U)=\sum_{i=2}^{k}i(i-1)w(E^{i}_{U}).

Proof of Proposition
2.4
.

By direct computation,

m_{\operatorname{gr}}(U,U)=\sum_{u\in U}\left(\sum_{v\in U,u\neq v}m_{\operatorname{gr}}(\{u,v\})\right)=\sum_{u\in U}\left(\sum_{v\in U,u\neq v}\left(\sum_{\tau\in E,u,v\in\tau}w(\tau)\right)\right)=\sum_{u\in U}\left(\sum_{v\in U,u\neq v}\left(\sum_{\tau\in E,u,v\in\tau}w(\tau)\right)\right)=\sum_{\tau\in E}w(\tau)\left(\sum_{u\in U,u\in\tau}\left(\sum_{v\in U,u\neq v,v\in\tau}1\right)\right)=\sum_{i=2}^{k}\sum_{\tau\in E^{i}_{U}}w(\tau)\left(\sum_{u\in U,u\in\tau}\left(\sum_{v\in U,u\neq v,v\in\tau}1\right)\right)=\sum_{i=2}^{k}\sum_{\tau\in E^{i}_{U}}w(\tau)i(i-1)=\sum_{i=2}^{k}i(i-1)w(E^{i}_{U}).

∎

2.3
The non-intersecting graph

Given
X
as above,
\tau,\tau^{\prime}\in E
will be called

and we will denote
\tau\leftrightarrow\tau^{\prime}
if
\tau\cap\tau^{\prime}=\emptyset
and there is
\sigma\in T
with
\tau,\tau^{\prime}\in\sigma
.

We recall that the non-intersecting graph of
X
is the graph with the vertex set
E
and two elements
\tau,\tau^{\prime}\in E
are connected by an edge if they are non-intersecting. Define a weight function on the edges of this graph as

m_{\operatorname{nint}}(\{\tau,\tau^{\prime}\})=\sum_{\sigma\in T,\tau,\tau^{\prime}\in\sigma}w(\sigma).

Define the constants
Q_{\operatorname{nint}}^{\max},Q_{\operatorname{nint}}^{\min}
of
X
as

Q_{\operatorname{nint}}^{\max}=\max_{\tau\in E}\max_{\sigma\in T,\tau\in\sigma}|\{\tau^{\prime}\in E:\tau^{\prime}\in\sigma\text{ and }\tau\leftrightarrow\tau^{\prime}\}|,

Q_{\operatorname{nint}}^{\min}=\min_{\tau\in E}\min_{\sigma\in T,\tau\in\sigma}|\{\tau^{\prime}\in E:\tau^{\prime}\in\sigma\text{ and }\tau\leftrightarrow\tau^{\prime}\}|.

Proposition 2.5
.

For every
A\subseteq E
,

Q_{\operatorname{nint}}^{\min}w(A)\leq m_{\operatorname{nint}}(A)\leq Q_{\operatorname{nint}}^{\max}w(A).

Proof of Proposition
2.5
.

It is enough to prove that for every
\tau\in E
,
Q_{\operatorname{nint}}^{\min}w(\tau)\leq m_{\operatorname{nint}}(\tau)\leq Q_{\operatorname{nint}}^{\max}w(\tau)
.
Fix
\tau\in E
, then

m_{\operatorname{nint}}(\tau)=\sum_{\tau^{\prime},\tau\leftrightarrow\tau^{\prime}}m_{\operatorname{nint}}(\{\tau,\tau^{\prime}\})=\sum_{\tau^{\prime},\tau\leftrightarrow\tau^{\prime}}\sum_{\sigma\in T,\tau,\tau^{\prime}\in\sigma}w(\sigma)=\sum_{\sigma\in T,\tau\in\sigma}w(\sigma)\sum_{\tau^{\prime},\tau\leftrightarrow\tau^{\prime},\tau^{\prime}\in\sigma}1

and the inequalities follow from the fact that

Q_{\operatorname{nint}}^{\min}\leq\sum_{\tau^{\prime},\tau\leftrightarrow\tau^{\prime},\tau^{\prime}\in\sigma}1\leq Q_{\operatorname{nint}}^{\max}.

∎

We define the

of the non-intersecting graph to be

R_{\operatorname{nint}}=\frac{Q_{\operatorname{nint}}^{\min}}{Q_{\operatorname{nint}}^{\max}}
.

Remark 2.6
.

Note that

and

if and only if the non-intersecting graph has no isolated vertices.

The following Corollary readily follows from Proposition

Corollary 2.7

Let
X
as above and assume that

. Then for every two non-empty sets

\frac{1}{R_{\operatorname{nint}}}\frac{w(A)}{w(B)}\geq\frac{m_{\operatorname{nint}}(A)}{m_{\operatorname{nint}}(B)}.

Recall that for every

Define a weight function on

Proposition 2.8
.

For every

w(\tau)\leq m_{v}(\tau)\leq(s-1)w(\tau),

2w(v)\leq m_{v}(E_{v})\leq sw(v).

Proof of Proposition
2.8
.

Fix

m_{v}(\tau)=\sum_{\tau^{\prime}\in E_{v},\tau^{\prime}\neq\tau}m_{v}(\{\tau,\tau^{\prime}\})=\sum_{\tau^{\prime}\in E,v\in\tau^{\prime},\tau^{\prime}\neq\tau}\left(\sum_{\sigma\in T,\tau,\tau^{\prime}\in\sigma}w(\sigma)\right)=\sum_{\sigma\in T,\tau\in\sigma}w(\sigma)\left(\sum_{\tau^{\prime}\in E,\tau^{\prime}\neq\tau,v\in\tau^{\prime}\in\sigma}1\right).

By our assumptions on

1\leq\sum_{\tau^{\prime}\in E,\tau^{\prime}\neq\tau,v\in\tau^{\prime}\in\sigma}1\leq s-1,

and thus

Also,

m_{v}(E_{v})=\sum_{\tau\in E_{v}}m_{v}(\tau)={w(\{\tau\in E:v\in\tau\})},

and thus by Proposition

2w(v)\leq m_{v}(E_{v})\leq sw(v).

∎

For a set
A\subseteq E

.

Proposition 2.9
.

Let
A\subseteq E
and
U\subseteq V

A_{U}^{i}\subseteq A
as

A_{U}^{i}=\{\tau\in A:|U\cap\tau|=i\}.

Then

\sum_{i=1}^{k}iw(A_{U}^{i})\leq\sum_{v\in U}m_{v}(A_{v})\leq(s-1)\sum_{i=1}^{k}iw(A_{U}^{i}).

In particular, for every set
A\subseteq E
and every set
U\subseteq V

\sum_{v\in U}m_{v}(A_{v})\leq(s-1)kw(A).

Proof of Proposition
2.9
.

Let
A\subseteq E
and
U
as above. By Proposition
2.8
, for every
\tau\in A
and every
v\in\tau

w(\tau)\leq m_{v}(\tau)\leq(s-1)w(\tau)

\sum_{v\in U}m_{v}(A_{v})=\sum_{v\in U}\left(\sum_{\tau\in A,v\in\tau}m_{v}(\tau)\right)=\sum_{v\in U}\left(\sum_{\tau\in A,v\in\tau}w(\tau)\right)\leq^{m_{v}(\tau)\leq(s-1)w(\tau)}(s-1)\sum_{\tau\in A}w(\tau)\sum_{v\in U,v\in\tau}1=(s-1)\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sum_{\tau\in A_{U}^{i}}w(\tau)\sum_{v\in U,v\in\tau}1=(s-1)\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sum_{\tau\in A_{U}^{i}}iw(\tau)=(s-1)\sum_{i=1}^{k}iw(A_{U}^{i}).

\min_{\underline{c}^{\prime}\in C}\|\underline{c}-\underline{c}^{\prime}\|\leq\frac{s}{k}\frac{\varepsilon}{2(\delta-\frac{p-1}{p})}.

.

Let

Fix

\underline{c}\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{V}

∎

Next, we will prove Theorem

:

Proof.

and

4
Main Expansion Theorem

Below we prove the main expansion theorem of this paper, namely that a
\lambda
-expanding
\operatorname{HDE-System}
is has unique neighbor expansion (given that
\lambda
is sufficiently small). We start by recalling some definitions and results concerning expansion of weighted graphs.

4.1
Expansion of weighted graphs - basic definitions and results

The aim of this section is to review the basic definitions regarding weighted graph and to state the Cheeger inequality and Alon-Chung Lemma in this setting. This section does not contain any new results and we provide the proofs merely for the sake of completeness.

Let
G=(V,E)
be a weighted graph with a weight function
m
. For
\emptyset\neq U_{1},U_{2}\subseteq V
, we denote

m(U_{1},U_{2})=\sum_{(u_{1},u_{2})\in U_{1}\times U_{2},\{u_{1},u_{2}\}\in E}m(\{u_{1},u_{2}\}).

We will work with the following variation on the Cheeger constant:

Definition 4.1
.

Let
G=(V,E)
be a finite graph. The (generalized) Cheeger constant of
G
is

h_{G}=\min_{\emptyset\neq U\subsetneqq V}\frac{m(U,V\setminus U)m(V)}{m(U)m(V\setminus U)}.

Note that
m(U,V\setminus U)\leq\min\{m(U),m(V\setminus U)\}
and therefore
h_{G}\leq 2
.

We will use the following (unorthodox) definition of a
\lambda
-expander:

Definition 4.2

(
\lambda
-expander)
.

A graph
G
will be called a
\lambda
-expander if
1-h_{G}\leq\lambda
.

The Cheeger inequality shows that this definition follows from
\lambda
-spectral expansion - see Theorem
4.4
below.

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
G
be a
\lambda
-expander with
\lambda\geq 0
, then for every
\emptyset\neq U\subsetneqq V
,

m(U)\left(\lambda+\frac{m(U)}{m(V)}\right)\geq m(U,U).

Proof of Proposition
4.3
.

Let
\emptyset\neq U\subsetneqq V
, then

m(U)=m(U,V\setminus U)+m(U,U).

Thus, by the definition of
h_{G}
,

h_{G}\frac{m(U)m(V\setminus U)}{m(V)}\leq m(U,V\setminus U)=m(U)-m(U,U).

It follows that

m(U)\left(1-h_{G}+h_{G}\frac{m(U)}{m(V)}\right)=m(U)\left(1-h_{G}\frac{m(V\setminus U)}{m(V)}\right)\geq m(U,U),

and substituting
h_{G}
by
1-\lambda
and using the fact that
\lambda\geq 0
finished the proof.
∎

Below, we define the (weighted) random walk on a weighted graph and state the Cheeger inequality in this setting.

Define
\ell^{2}(V)
to be the space of functions
\{\phi:V\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\}
with the inner-product

\langle\phi,\psi\rangle=\sum_{v\in V}m(v)\phi(v)\psi(v),

and the induced norm

\|\phi\|^{2}=\sum_{v\in V}m(v)\phi(v)^{2}.

The random walk on the
(V,E)
is the operator
M:\ell^{2}(V)\rightarrow\ell^{2}(V)
defined by

M\phi(v)=\frac{1}{m(v)}\sum_{u,\{u,v\}\in E}m(\{u,v\})\phi(u).

We note that when
m:E\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{+}
is the constant function
1
, then
M
is the simple random walk on the graph.

The standard facts regarding this operator are:

•

The operator
M
is self-adjoint and of operator norm
1
with respect to the inner product on
\ell^{2}(V)
defined above.

•

For every
\phi\in\ell^{2}(V)
,

\langle(I-M)\phi,\phi\rangle=\sum_{\{u,v\}\in E}m(\{u,v\})(\phi(u)-\phi(v))^{2}.

•

For the constant function
\mathbbm{1}\in\ell^{2}(V)
it holds that
M\mathbbm{1}=\mathbbm{1}
, i.e., this is an eigenfunction with the eigenvalue
1
.

•

The graph
(V,E)
is connected if and only if
1
is an eigenvalue of
M
with multiplicity
1
.

Theorem 4.4

(The Cheeger inequality, Alon-Chung Lemma)
.

Let
(V,E)
be a connected graph with a weight function
m
. Denote by
\lambda
the second largest eigenvalue of
M
. Let
\emptyset\neq U\subsetneqq V
be a set of vertices. Then the following inequalities hold:

1.

The Cheeger inequality:

h_{G}\geq 1-\lambda.

In other words,
G
is
\lambda
-Cheeger-expanding.

2.

Alon-Chung Lemma:

m(U)\left(\lambda+(1-\lambda)\frac{m(U)}{m(V)}\right)\geq m(U,U).

Proof of Theorem
4.4
.

We start by proving The Cheeger inequality. For a set
U
as above, define
\phi\in\ell^{2}(V)
by

\phi(v)=\begin{cases}m(V\setminus U)&v\in U\\
-m(U)&v\in V\setminus U\end{cases}.

We observe that
\phi\perp\mathbbm{1}
, indeed:

\langle\phi,\mathbbm{1}\rangle=\sum_{v\in U}m(v)m(V\setminus U)+\sum_{v\in V\setminus U}m(v)(-m(U))=m(U)m(V\setminus U)-m(V\setminus U)m(U)=0.

Thus,

\langle(I-M)\phi,\phi\rangle\geq(1-\lambda)\|\phi\|^{2}.

A direct computation shows that

\|\phi\|^{2}=m(U)m(V\setminus U)(m(U)+m(V\setminus U))=m(U)m(V\setminus U)m(V).

By the fact stated above regarding
\langle(I-M)\phi,\phi\rangle
, we get that

(1-\lambda)m(U)m(V\setminus U)(m(V))\leq\langle(I-M)\phi,\phi\rangle=\sum_{\{u,v\}\in E}m(\{u,v\})(\phi(u)-\phi(v))^{2}=\sum_{\{u,v\}\in E,u\in U,v\in V\setminus U}m(\{u,v\})(m(U)+m(V\setminus U))^{2}=m(U,V\setminus U)m(V)^{2}.

Dividing this inequality by
m(V)^{2}
yields the Cheeger inequality stated in the Theorem.

The Alon-Chung Lemma is a consequence of the Cheeger inequality and Proposition
4.3
above.
∎

We note that in the case where the graph is “almost complete”, there is an easy bound on the Cheeger constant:

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
G=(V,E)
be a weighted graph. If there is a constant
0\leq\beta<1
such that for every
v\in V
, such that
m(v)\geq(\max_{e^{\prime}\in E}m(e^{\prime}))(1-\beta)|V|
, then
h_{G}\geq 1-2\beta
, i.e.,
G
is
2\beta
-expander.

Proof of Lemma
4.5
.

We start by defining a normalized weight function
m^{\prime}(e)=\frac{m(e)}{\max_{e^{\prime}\in E}m(e^{\prime})}
. With this normalization, we get that for every
e
,
m^{\prime}(e)\leq 1
and for every
v\in V
,
m^{\prime}(v)\geq(1-\beta)|V|
. We note that this normalization does not change the Cheeger constant of the graph.

Let
U\subseteq V
be some non-empty subset such that
|U|\leq\frac{1}{2}
. By the assumption
m^{\prime}(v)\geq(1-\beta)|V|
it follows that

m^{\prime}(V)\geq(1-\beta)|V|^{2}
. Also,

m^{\prime}(U,V\setminus U)=\sum_{u\in U}m(\{u\},V\setminus U)\geq\sum_{u\in U}(m^{\prime}(u)-m^{\prime}(\{u\},U))\geq\sum_{u\in U}(m^{\prime}(u)-|U|)\geq|U|\left((1-\beta)|V|-|U|\right).

Note that for every
v\in V
,
m^{\prime}(v)\leq|V|
and thus

m^{\prime}(U)\leq|V||U|,m^{\prime}(V\setminus U)\leq|V|(|V|-|U|).

From the above inequalities, it follows that

\frac{m(U,V\setminus U)m(V)}{m(U)m(V\setminus U)}=\frac{m^{\prime}(U,V\setminus U)m^{\prime}(V)}{m^{\prime}(U)m^{\prime}(V\setminus U)}\geq\frac{|U|((1-\beta)|V|-|U|)(1-\beta)|V|^{2}}{|V||U|||V|(|V|-|U|)}=1-\frac{\beta|V|}{|V|-|U|}\geq 1-\frac{\beta|V|}{|V|-\frac{1}{2}|V|}=1-2\beta.

The above inequality holds for every non-empty
U\subseteq V
with
|U|\leq\frac{1}{2}|V|
and thus
h_{G}\geq 1-2\beta
.
∎

Below, in applications, we will also need a result for the Cheeger constant of (weak) graph covers.

Definition 4.6

(Weak cover)
.

Given two finite weighted graphs
G^{\prime}=(V^{\prime},E^{\prime},m^{\prime}),G=(V,E,m)
we say that
G^{\prime}
is a weak cover of
G
if there is a surjective function
p:V^{\prime}\rightarrow V
such that

•

For any
\{v^{\prime},u^{\prime}\}\in E^{\prime}
it follows that
\{p(v^{\prime}),p(u^{\prime})\}\in E
and the induced map
p:E^{\prime}\rightarrow E
is surjective.

•

For every
e\in E
,
m(e)=\sum_{e^{\prime}\in p^{-1}(e)}m(e^{\prime})
.

Proposition 4.7
.

Let
G^{\prime}=(V^{\prime},E^{\prime},m^{\prime}),G=(V,E,m)
be two finite weighted graphs such that
G^{\prime}
is a weak cover of
G
. Then
h_{G}\geq h_{G^{\prime}}
.

Proof.

By the definition of a weak cover, we have for every two sets
U_{1},U_{2}\subseteq V
that

m(U_{1},U_{2})=\sum_{(u_{1},u_{2})\in U_{1}\times U_{2},\{u_{1},u_{2}\}\in E}m(\{u_{1},u_{2}\})=\sum_{(u_{1},u_{2})\in U_{1}\times U_{2},\{u_{1},u_{2}\}\in E}\sum_{u_{1}^{\prime}\in p^{-1}(u_{1}),u_{2}^{\prime}\in p^{-1}(u_{2}),\{u_{1}^{\prime},u_{2}^{\prime}\}\in E^{\prime}}m(\{u_{1}^{\prime},u_{2}^{\prime}\})=\sum_{(u_{1}^{\prime},u_{2}^{\prime})\in p^{-1}(U_{1})\times p^{-1}(U_{2}),\{u_{1}^{\prime},u_{2}^{\prime}\}\in E^{\prime}}m(\{u_{1}^{\prime},u_{2}^{\prime}\})=m^{\prime}(p^{-1}(U_{1}),p^{-1}(U_{2})).

Thus it follows that for every non-empty set
U\subseteq V
,

m(U,V\setminus U)=m^{\prime}(p^{-1}(U),p^{-1}(V\setminus U))=m^{\prime}(p^{-1}(U),V^{\prime}\setminus p^{-1}(U))

and

m(U)=m^{\prime}(p^{-1}(U)),m(V\setminus U)=m^{\prime}(V^{\prime}\setminus p^{-1}(U)),m(V)=m^{\prime}(V^{\prime}).

Thus for every non-empty set
U\subseteq V
, if we denote
U^{\prime}=p^{-1}(U)
it holds that

\frac{m(U,V\setminus U)m(V)}{m(U)m(V\setminus U)}=\frac{m^{\prime}(U^{\prime},V^{\prime}\setminus U^{\prime})m^{\prime}(V^{\prime})}{m^{\prime}(U^{\prime})m^{\prime}(V^{\prime}\setminus U^{\prime})}\geq h_{G^{\prime}}

and it follows that
h_{G}\geq h_{G^{\prime}}
.
∎

4.2
Main Expansion Theorem

After the above definition of
\lambda
-expanders, we can give the formal definition of an
\operatorname{HDE-System}
:

Definition 4.8
.

Let
X
be a
(s,k,K)
-two layer system. For a constant
\lambda<1
, we say that
X
is a
\lambda
-expanding
\operatorname{HDE-System}
, if the ground graph and the links of all the vertices are all
\lambda
-expanders and the non-intersecting graph is either a totally disconnected (i.e., contains no edges) or a
\lambda
-expander.

Our main theorem is regarding HDE-systems is for every
k,K\in\mathbb{N}
and every
0<\delta<1
there is a
\lambda(k,K,\delta)>0
such that every
(s,k,K)
-two layer system that is a
\lambda
-expanding
\operatorname{HDE-System}
has
\delta
-unique neighbor expansion. We state (and prove) this theorem formally below, but before doing so, we will need some additional lemmas.

Lemma 4.9
.

Let
\lambda_{\operatorname{gr}}\geq 0
be a constant and
X
a
(s,k,K)
-two layer system such that the ground graph of
X
is a
\lambda_{\operatorname{gr}}
-expander. Also, let

be a non-empty set,
0<\mu<1
be a constant and
U\subseteq V_{\mu\text{-large}}

If
\frac{w(A)}{w(E)}\leq\frac{4\mu^{2}}{s^{3}(s-1)^{2}}
, then

\frac{s(s-1)(k-1)}{2\mu}\lambda_{\operatorname{gr}}+\frac{s^{3}(s-1)^{2}}{4\mu^{2}}\frac{w(A)}{w(E)}\geq\left(1-\frac{s(s-1)(k-1)}{2\mu}\lambda_{\operatorname{gr}}\right)\sum_{i=2}^{k}(i-1)\frac{w(A_{U}^{i})}{w(A)}.

By Proposition

(1)

By Proposition

m_{\operatorname{gr}}(U,U)=\sum_{i=2}^{k}i(i-1)w(E^{i}_{U})\geq\sum_{i=2}^{k}i(i-1)w(A_{U}^{i}).

(2)

By Proposition

and Corollary

m_{\operatorname{gr}}(V)\geq 2(k-1)w(V)\geq\frac{2k(k-1)}{s}w(E).

(3)

By the fact that of

, for every

we have that
\frac{m_{v}(A_{v})}{m_{v}(E_{v})}\geq\mu
.
Using Proposition

:

\sum_{i=1}^{k}iw(A_{U}^{i})\geq\frac{1}{s-1}\sum_{v\in U}m_{v}(A_{v})\geq\frac{1}{s-1}\sum_{v\in U}\mu m_{v}(E_{v})\geq^{\text{Proposition }\ref{m_v (tau), m_v (X_v) prop}}\frac{1}{s-1}\sum_{v\in U}2\mu w(v)=\frac{2}{s-1}\mu w(U)\geq^{\text{Proposition }\ref{m_gr (v) prop}}\frac{2}{s-1}\frac{\mu}{s(k-1)}m_{\operatorname{gr}}(U).

(4)

Using the fact that

w(A)=\sum_{i=0}^{k}w(A_{U}^{i}),

(5)

and (
4
), we have that

\frac{s(s-1)(k-1)}{2\mu}\left(w(A)+\sum_{i=2}^{k}(i-1)w(A_{U}^{i})\right)\geq m_{\operatorname{gr}}(U).

(6)

Using this inequality and (
3

m_{\operatorname{gr}}(U)\left(\lambda_{\operatorname{gr}}+\frac{m_{\operatorname{gr}}(U)}{m_{\operatorname{gr}}(V)}\right)\\
\leq\frac{s(s-1)(k-1)}{2\mu}\left(w(A)+\sum_{i=2}^{k}(i-1)w(A_{U}^{i})\right)\left(\lambda_{\operatorname{gr}}+\frac{s(s-1)(k-1)}{2\mu}\frac{s}{2k(k-1)w(E)}\left(w(A)+\sum_{i=2}^{k}(i-1)w(A_{U}^{i})\right)\right)=w(A)\left(1+\sum_{i=2}^{k}(i-1)\frac{w(A_{U}^{i})}{w(A)}\right)\left(\frac{s(s-1)(k-1)}{2\mu}\lambda_{\operatorname{gr}}+\frac{s^{3}(s-1)^{2}(k-1)}{8\mu^{2}k}\frac{w(A)}{w(E)}\left(1+\sum_{i=2}^{k}(i-1)\frac{w(A_{U}^{i})}{w(A)}\right)\right)\leq w(A)\left(1+\sum_{i=2}^{k}(i-1)\frac{w(A_{U}^{i})}{w(A)}\right)\left(\frac{s(s-1)(k-1)}{2\mu}\lambda_{\operatorname{gr}}+\frac{s^{3}(s-1)^{2}}{8\mu^{2}}\frac{w(A)}{w(E)}\left(1+\sum_{i=2}^{k}(i-1)\frac{w(A_{U}^{i})}{w(A)}\right)\right).

(7)

Combining this inequality with (

2
) yields

w(A)\left(1+\sum_{i=2}^{k}(i-1)\frac{w(A_{U}^{i})}{w(A)}\right)\left(\frac{s(s-1)(k-1)}{2\mu}\lambda_{\operatorname{gr}}+\frac{s^{3}(s-1)^{2}}{8\mu^{2}}\frac{w(A)}{w(E)}\left(1+\sum_{i=2}^{k}(i-1)\frac{w(A_{U}^{i})}{w(A)}\right)\right)\geq\sum_{i=2}^{k}i(i-1)w(A_{U}^{i}).

After dividing by
w(A)
and re-arranging, we get

\frac{s(s-1)(k-1)}{2\mu}\lambda_{\operatorname{gr}}+\frac{s^{3}(s-1)^{2}}{8\mu^{2}}\frac{w(A)}{w(E)}\left(1+\sum_{i=2}^{k}(i-1)\frac{w(A_{U}^{i})}{w(A)}\right)^{2}\geq\sum_{i=2}^{k}\left(i-\frac{s(s-1)(k-1)}{2\mu}\lambda_{\operatorname{gr}}\right)(i-1)\frac{w(A_{U}^{i})}{w(A)}.

(8)

We note that

\left(1+\sum_{i=2}^{k}(i-1)\frac{w(A_{U}^{i})}{w(A)}\right)^{2}=\left(1\cdot 1+\sum_{i=2}^{k}\sqrt{\frac{w(A_{U}^{i})}{w(A)}}\cdot(i-1)\sqrt{\frac{w(A_{U}^{i})}{w(A)}}\right)^{2}\leq^{\text{Cauchy-Schwarz}}\left(1+\sum_{i=2}^{k}\frac{w(A_{U}^{i})}{w(A)}\right)\left(1+\sum_{i=2}^{k}(i-1)^{2}\frac{w(A_{U}^{i})}{w(A)}\right)\leq 2\left(1+\sum_{i=2}^{k}(i-1)^{2}\frac{w(A_{U}^{i})}{w(A)}\right),

(9)

where the last inequality is due to (
5
). Combining inequalities (
8
) and (
9
) yields

\frac{s(s-1)(k-1)}{2\mu}\lambda_{\operatorname{gr}}+\frac{s^{3}(s-1)^{2}}{4\mu^{2}}\frac{w(A)}{w(E)}\left(1+\sum_{i=2}^{k}(i-1)^{2}\frac{w(A_{U}^{i})}{w(A)}\right)\geq\sum_{i=2}^{k}\left(i-\frac{s(s-1)(k-1)}{2\mu}\lambda_{\operatorname{gr}}\right)(i-1)\frac{w(A_{U}^{i})}{w(A)}.

After re-arranging, we get

\frac{s(s-1)(k-1)}{2\mu}\lambda_{\operatorname{gr}}+\frac{s^{3}(s-1)^{2}}{4\mu^{2}}\frac{w(A)}{w(E)}\geq\sum_{i=2}^{k}\left(i-\frac{s(s-1)(k-1)}{2\mu}\lambda_{\operatorname{gr}}-\frac{s^{2}(s-1)^{2}}{2\mu^{2}}\frac{w(A)}{w(E)}(i-1)\right)(i-1)\frac{w(A_{U}^{i})}{w(A)}.

Recall we assumed that
\frac{s^{3}(s-1)^{2}}{4\mu^{2}}\frac{w(A)}{w(E)}\leq 1
and therefore the above inequality yields

\frac{s(s-1)(k-1)}{2\mu}\lambda_{\operatorname{gr}}+\frac{s^{3}(s-1)^{2}}{4\mu^{2}}\frac{w(A)}{w(E)}\geq\left(1-\frac{s(s-1)(k-1)}{2\mu}\lambda_{\operatorname{gr}}\right)\sum_{i=2}^{k}(i-1)\frac{w(A_{U}^{i})}{w(A)},

as needed.

∎

Lemma 4.10
.

Let
\lambda_{\operatorname{loc}}\geq 0
be a constant and
X
be a
(s,k,K)
-two layer system such that all the links are
\lambda_{\operatorname{loc}}
-expanders. Also, let
A\subseteq E
be a non-empty set,
0<\mu<1
be a constant and
U\subseteq V_{\mu\text{-small}}
be a set. Then

(s-1)\left(\lambda_{\operatorname{loc}}+\mu\right)\sum_{i=1}^{k}iw(A_{U}^{i})\geq\sum_{v\in U}m_{v}(A_{v},A_{v}).

Proof.

By Proposition
4.3
, for every
v\in U
,

m_{v}(A_{v})\left(\lambda_{\operatorname{loc}}+\frac{m_{v}(A_{v})}{m_{v}(E_{v})}\right)\geq m_{v}(A_{v},A_{v}).

By the assumption that
U\subseteq V_{\mu\text{-small}}
,

\frac{m_{v}(A_{v})}{m_{v}(E_{v})}\leq\mu.

Summing over all
v\in U
yields

\sum_{v\in U}m_{v}(A_{v})\left(\lambda_{\operatorname{loc}}+\mu\right)\geq\sum_{v\in U}m_{v}(A_{v},A_{v}).

By Proposition
2.9
,

\sum_{v\in U}m_{v}(A_{v})\leq(s-1)\sum_{i=1}^{k}iw(A_{U}^{i}),

and the needed inequality follows.
∎

Lemma 4.11
.

Let
\lambda_{\operatorname{nint}}\geq 0
be a constant and
X
be a
(s,k,K)
-two layer system such that the non-intersecting graph of
X
is
\lambda_{\operatorname{nint}}
-expander. For a non-empty set
A\subseteq E
, denote

D_{\operatorname{nint}}^{\geq 2}=\{\sigma\in T:\exists\tau,\tau^{\prime}\in A\cap\sigma,\tau\leftrightarrow\tau^{\prime}\}.

Then

\frac{1}{2R_{\operatorname{nint}}}\left(\lambda_{\operatorname{nint}}+\frac{1}{R_{\operatorname{nint}}}\frac{w(A)}{w(E)}\right)\geq\frac{w(D_{\operatorname{nint}}^{\geq 2})}{w(A)}.

Proof.

Recall that
Q_{\operatorname{nint}}^{\max},Q_{\operatorname{nint}}^{\min}
denote the constants such that for every
\sigma\in T
and every
\tau\in\sigma
,

Q_{\operatorname{nint}}^{\min}\leq|\{\tau^{\prime}\in\sigma:\tau^{\prime}\leftrightarrow\tau\}|\leq Q_{\operatorname{nint}}^{\max}.

By Proposition
4.3
,

m_{\operatorname{nint}}(A)\left(\lambda_{\operatorname{nint}}+\frac{m_{\operatorname{nint}}(A)}{m_{\operatorname{nint}}(E)}\right)\geq m_{\operatorname{nint}}(A,A).

By Proposition
2.5
and Corollary
2.7
, we have that

m_{\operatorname{nint}}(A)\left(\lambda_{\operatorname{nint}}+\frac{m_{\operatorname{nint}}(A)}{m_{\operatorname{nint}}(E)}\right)\leq Q_{\operatorname{nint}}^{\max}w(A)\left(\lambda_{\operatorname{nint}}+\frac{1}{R_{\operatorname{nint}}}\frac{w(A)}{w(E)}\right).

Thus we are left to prove that

m_{\operatorname{nint}}(A,A)\geq 2Q_{\operatorname{nint}}^{\min}w(D_{\operatorname{nint}}^{\geq 2}).

Indeed,

m_{\operatorname{nint}}(A,A)=\sum_{\tau\in A}\left(\sum_{\tau^{\prime}\in A,\tau\leftrightarrow\tau^{\prime}}\left(\sum_{\sigma\in T,\tau,\tau^{\prime}\in\sigma}w(\sigma)\right)\right)=\sum_{\sigma\in T}w(\sigma)\left(\sum_{\tau\in A,\tau\in\sigma}\left(\sum_{\tau^{\prime}\in A,\tau\leftrightarrow\tau^{\prime},\tau^{\prime}\in\sigma}1\right)\right)=\sum_{\sigma\in D_{\operatorname{nint}}^{\geq 2}}w(\sigma)\left(\sum_{\tau\in A,\tau\in\sigma}\left(\sum_{\tau^{\prime}\in A,\tau\leftrightarrow\tau^{\prime},\tau^{\prime}\in\sigma}1\right)\right)=\sum_{\sigma\in D_{\operatorname{nint}}^{\geq 2}}w(\sigma)\left(\sum_{\tau\in A,\tau\in\sigma}Q_{\operatorname{nint}}^{\min}\right)\geq 2Q_{\operatorname{nint}}^{\min}w(D_{\operatorname{nint}}^{\geq 2}).

∎

After this, we can state and prove our main expansion theorem:

Theorem 4.12
.

Let
s,k,K\in\mathbb{N}
,
0<\delta<\frac{1}{s-1}
,
0\leq\alpha<1
be constants. Let
X
be a
(s,k,K)
-two layer system such that the following holds:

1.

The ground graph in of
X
is a
\lambda_{\operatorname{gr}}
-expander with

\lambda_{\operatorname{gr}}=\frac{(1-\alpha)(1-(s-1)\delta)}{4s(s-1)^{2}k(k-1)}\min\left\{\frac{7(1-(s-1)\delta)}{4(1+15(s-1)\delta)},\frac{1}{2}\right\}.

2.

All the vertices of
X
are
\lambda_{\operatorname{loc}}
-expanders with

\lambda_{\operatorname{loc}}=\frac{(1-\alpha)(1-(s-1)\delta)}{8k(s-1)}.

3.

The non-intersecting graph is either totally disconnected or a
\lambda_{\operatorname{nint}}
-expander with

\lambda_{\operatorname{nint}}=\frac{R_{\operatorname{nint}}(1-\alpha)(1-(s-1)\delta)}{4K}.

Denote

\varepsilon_{0}=\min\{\frac{R_{\operatorname{nint}}^{2}(1-\alpha)(1-(s-1)\delta)}{4}\frac{1}{K},\frac{7(1-\alpha)^{2}(1-(s-1)\delta)^{3}}{64(1+15(s-1)\delta)s^{3}(s-1)^{4}}\frac{1}{k^{2}}\}
. For every
A\subseteq E
, if
A
is non-empty,
(\delta,\alpha)
-locally small and

\frac{w(A)}{w(E)}<\varepsilon_{0},

then there exists
\sigma\in T
that contains exactly one element of
A
, i.e.,

w(\{\sigma\in T:|\sigma\cap A|=1\})>0,

i.e.,
X
has the
((\delta,\alpha),\varepsilon_{0})
-unique neighbor expansion property.

Proof.

Let
A\subseteq E
be a non-empty set such that
A
is
(\delta,\alpha)
-locally small and

\frac{w(A)}{w(E)}<\varepsilon_{0}.

Define the following sets:

D=\{\sigma\in T:\sigma\cap A\neq\emptyset\},

D_{\operatorname{nint}}^{\geq 2}=\{\sigma\in T:\exists\tau,\tau^{\prime}\in A\cap\sigma,\tau\leftrightarrow\tau^{\prime}\},

D_{\operatorname{loc}}=D\setminus D_{\operatorname{nint}}^{\geq 2},

D_{\operatorname{loc}}^{i}=\{\sigma\in D_{\operatorname{loc}}:|\sigma\cap A|=i,\forall\tau_{1},\tau_{2}\in\sigma\cap A,\tau_{1}\cap\tau_{2}\neq\emptyset\},

D^{1}=\{\sigma\in T:|\sigma\cap A|=1\}.

We note that by this notation

D=D^{1}\dot{\cup}D_{\operatorname{nint}}^{\geq 2}\dot{\cup}\bigcup_{i=2}^{K}D_{\operatorname{loc}}^{i},

and by the definition of the weight function
w
, we have that

w(A)\leq w(D^{1})+Kw(D_{\operatorname{nint}}^{\geq 2})+\sum_{i=2}^{K}iw(D_{\operatorname{loc}}^{i}).

Therefore, in order to prove the Theorem, it is sufficient to prove that

w(A)>Kw(D_{\operatorname{nint}}^{\geq 2})+\sum_{i=2}^{K}iw(D_{\operatorname{loc}}^{i}).

(10)

We will start by showing that

Kw(D_{\operatorname{nint}}^{\geq 2})<\frac{(1-\alpha)(1-(s-1)\delta)}{4}w(A).

If the non-intersecting graph is totally disconnected, then
w(D_{\operatorname{nint}}^{\geq 2})=0
and this holds trivially. Otherwise, by Lemma
4.11
, we have that

\frac{K}{2R_{\operatorname{nint}}}\left(\lambda_{\operatorname{nint}}+\frac{1}{R_{\operatorname{nint}}}\frac{w(A)}{w(E)}\right)\geq\frac{Kw(D_{\operatorname{nint}}^{\geq 2})}{w(A)}.

By our assumptions,
\lambda_{\operatorname{nint}}=\frac{R_{\operatorname{nint}}(1-\alpha)(1-(s-1)\delta)}{4K}
and

\frac{w(A)}{w(E)}<\frac{R_{\operatorname{nint}}^{2}(1-\alpha)(1-(s-1)\delta)}{4K}

and therefore:

\frac{(1-\alpha)(1-(s-1)\delta)}{4}=\frac{K}{2R_{\operatorname{nint}}}\left(\frac{R_{\operatorname{nint}}(1-\alpha)(1-(s-1)\delta)}{4K}+\frac{R_{\operatorname{nint}}(1-\alpha)(1-(s-1)\delta)}{4K}\right)>\frac{K}{2R_{\operatorname{nint}}}\left(\lambda_{\operatorname{nint}}+\frac{1}{R_{\operatorname{nint}}}\frac{w(A)}{w(E)}\right)\geq\frac{Kw(D_{\operatorname{nint}}^{\geq 2})}{w(A)},

i.e.,

Kw(D_{\operatorname{nint}}^{\geq 2})<\frac{(1-\alpha)(1-(s-1)\delta)}{4}w(A).

Thus, in order to prove (
10
) and complete the proof, it is enough to prove that

(1-\frac{(1-\alpha)(1-(s-1)\delta)}{4})w(A)>\sum_{i=2}^{K}iw(D_{\operatorname{loc}}^{i})

or equivalently that

(\alpha+\frac{(1-\alpha)(3+(s-1)\delta)}{4})w(A)>\sum_{i=2}^{K}iw(D_{\operatorname{loc}}^{i}).

(11)

We note that

\sum_{v\in V}m_{v}(A_{v},A_{v})=\sum_{v\in V}\sum_{\tau\in A_{v}}\sum_{\tau^{\prime}\in A_{v},\tau^{\prime}\neq\tau}\sum_{\sigma\in T,\tau,\tau^{\prime}\subseteq\sigma}w(\sigma)=\sum_{\sigma\in T}w(\sigma)\sum_{v\in\sigma}\sum_{\tau\in A_{v},\tau\subseteq\sigma}\sum_{\tau^{\prime}\in A_{v},\tau^{\prime}\neq\tau,\tau^{\prime}\subseteq\sigma}1=\sum_{\sigma\in T}w(\sigma)\sum_{\tau\in A\cap\sigma}\sum_{\tau^{\prime}\in A\cap\sigma,|\tau\cap\tau^{\prime}|=1}1\geq\sum_{i=2}^{K}\sum_{\sigma\in D_{\operatorname{loc}}^{i}}w(\sigma)\sum_{\tau\in A\cap\sigma}\sum_{\tau^{\prime}\in A\cap\sigma,|\tau\cap\tau^{\prime}|=1}1=\sum_{i=2}^{K}i(i-1)w(D_{\operatorname{loc}}^{i})\geq\sum_{i=2}^{K}iw(D_{\operatorname{loc}}^{i}).

Thus, in order to prove (
11
), it is sufficient to prove

\sum_{v\in V}m_{v}(A_{v},A_{v})<(\alpha+\frac{(1-\alpha)(3+(s-1)\delta)}{4})w(A).

(12)

We observe that

\sum_{v\in V}m_{v}(A_{v},A_{v})=\sum_{v\in V_{\delta\text{-small}}}m_{v}(A_{v},A_{v})+\sum_{v\in V_{\delta\text{-large}}}m_{v}(A_{v},A_{v})\leq\sum_{v\in V_{\delta\text{-small}}}m_{v}(A_{v},A_{v})+\sum_{v\in V_{\delta\text{-large}}}m_{v}(A_{v})\leq^{A\text{ is }(\delta,\alpha)-\text{locally small}}\sum_{v\in V_{\delta\text{-small}}}m_{v}(A_{v},A_{v})+\alpha w(A)

Thus, in order to prove (
12
), it is enough to prove that

\sum_{v\in V_{\delta\text{-small}}}m_{v}(A_{v},A_{v})<\frac{(1-\alpha)(3+(s-1)\delta)}{4}w(A).

(13)

Let
\mu=\frac{(1-\alpha)(1-(s-1)\delta)}{8k(s-1)}
. Applying Lemma
4.10
on the set
V_{\mu\text{-small}}
yields

(s-1)\left(\lambda_{\operatorname{loc}}+\mu\right)kw(A)\geq(s-1)\left(\lambda_{\operatorname{loc}}+\mu\right)k\sum_{i=0}^{k}w(A_{V_{\mu\text{-small}}}^{i})\geq(s-1)\left(\lambda_{\operatorname{loc}}+\mu\right)\sum_{i=1}^{k}iw(A_{V_{\mu\text{-small}}}^{i})\geq^{\text{Lemma }\ref{links lemma}}\sum_{v\in V_{\mu\text{-small}}}m_{v}(A_{v},A_{v}).

Using the fact that
\mu=\frac{(1-\alpha)(1-(s-1)\delta)}{8k(s-1)}
and that
\lambda_{\operatorname{loc}}=\frac{(1-\alpha)(1-(s-1)\delta)}{8k(s-1)}
, we get that

\frac{(1-\alpha)(1-(s-1)\delta)}{4}w(A)\geq\sum_{v\in V_{\mu\text{-small}}}m_{v}(A_{v},A_{v}).

(14)

Note that since
V_{\delta\text{-small}}\subseteq V_{\mu\text{-small}}\dot{\cup}(V_{\mu\text{-large}}\cap V_{\delta\text{-small}})
, we have that

\sum_{v\in V_{\delta\text{-small}}}m_{v}(A_{v},A_{v})\leq\sum_{v\in V_{\mu\text{-small}}}m_{v}(A_{v},A_{v})+\sum_{v\in V_{\mu\text{-large}}\cap V_{\delta\text{-small}}}m_{v}(A_{v},A_{v}),

and thus, in order to prove (
13
), we are left to show that

\sum_{v\in V_{\mu\text{-large}}\cap V_{\delta\text{-small}}}m_{v}(A_{v},A_{v})<\frac{(1-\alpha)(3+(s-1)\delta)}{4}w(A)-\frac{(1-\alpha)(1-(s-1)\delta)}{4}w(A)=(1-\alpha)\frac{1+(s-1)\delta}{2}w(A).

(15)

Lemma
4.10
applied to
V_{\mu\text{-large}}\cap V_{\delta\text{-small}}\subseteq V_{\delta\text{-small}}
yields that

(s-1)\left(\lambda_{\operatorname{loc}}+\delta\right)\sum_{i=1}^{k}iw(A_{V_{\mu\text{-large}}\cap V_{\delta\text{-small}}}^{i})\geq\sum_{v\in V_{\mu\text{-large}}\cap V_{\delta\text{-small}}}m_{v}(A_{v},A_{v}).

Recall that
\lambda_{\operatorname{loc}}=\frac{(1-\alpha)(1-(s-1)\delta)}{8k(s-1)}\leq\frac{(1-\alpha)(1-(s-1)\delta)}{16(s-1)}
and thus we have that

\left(\frac{(1-\alpha)(1-(s-1)\delta)}{16}+(s-1)\delta\right)\sum_{i=1}^{k}iw(A_{V_{\mu\text{-large}}\cap V_{\delta\text{-small}}}^{i})\geq\sum_{v\in V_{\mu\text{-large}}\cap V_{\delta\text{-small}}}m_{v}(A_{v},A_{v}).

Note that

w(A)\geq\sum_{i=1}^{k}w(A_{V_{\mu\text{-large}}}^{i}),

and combining this with the above inequality yields

\left(\frac{(1-\alpha)(1-(s-1)\delta)}{16}+(s-1)\delta\right)\left(w(A)+\sum_{i=2}^{k}(i-1)w(A_{V_{\mu\text{-large}}}^{i})\right)\geq\sum_{v\in V_{\mu\text{-large}}}m_{v}(A_{v},A_{v}),

(16)

or equivalently,

(1-\alpha)\left(\frac{1+15(s-1)\delta}{16}\right)\left(w(A)+\sum_{i=2}^{k}(i-1)w(A_{V_{\mu\text{-large}}}^{i})\right)\geq\sum_{v\in V_{\mu\text{-large}}}m_{v}(A_{v},A_{v}).

(17)

For
\mu=\frac{(1-\alpha)(1-(s-1)\delta)}{8k(s-1)}
, by the condition on
\frac{w(A)}{w(E)}
we have that
\frac{w(A)}{w(E)}\leq\frac{4\mu^{2}}{s^{3}(s-1)^{2}}
and thus we can apply Lemma
4.9

w(A)\left(\frac{s(s-1)(k-1)}{2\mu}\lambda_{\operatorname{gr}}+\frac{s^{3}(s-1)^{2}}{4\mu^{2}}\frac{w(A)}{w(E)}\right)\geq\left(1-\frac{s(s-1)(k-1)}{2\mu}\lambda_{\operatorname{gr}}\right)\sum_{i=2}^{k}(i-1)w(A_{U}^{i}).

Recall that
\mu=\frac{(1-\alpha)(1-(s-1)\delta)}{8k(s-1)}
and thus

w(A)\left(\frac{4s(s-1)^{2}k(k-1)}{(1-\alpha)(1-(s-1)\delta)}\lambda_{\operatorname{gr}}+\frac{16s^{3}(s-1)^{4}k^{2}}{(1-\alpha)^{2}(1-(s-1)\delta)^{2}}\frac{w(A)}{w(E)}\right)\geq\left(1-\frac{4s(s-1)^{2}k(k-1)}{(1-\alpha)(1-(s-1)\delta)}\lambda_{\operatorname{gr}}\right)\sum_{i=2}^{k}(i-1)w(A_{U}^{i}).

(18)

By our choice of
\lambda_{\operatorname{gr}}
, we have that

1-\frac{4s(s-1)^{2}k(k-1)}{(1-\alpha)(1-(s-1)\delta)}\lambda_{\operatorname{gr}}\geq\frac{1}{2},

(19)

and

\frac{4s(s-1)^{2}k(k-1)}{(1-\alpha)(1-(s-1)\delta)}\lambda_{\operatorname{gr}}\leq\frac{7(1-(s-1)\delta)}{4(1+15(s-1)\delta)}

(20)

By the fact that

\frac{w(A)}{w(E)}<\frac{7(1-\alpha)^{2}(1-(s-1)\delta)^{3}}{64(1+15(s-1)\delta)s^{3}(s-1)^{4}k^{2}},

we have that

\frac{16s^{3}(s-1)^{4}k^{2}}{(1-\alpha)^{2}(1-(s-1)\delta)^{2}}\frac{w(A)}{w(E)}<\frac{7(1-(s-1)\delta)}{4(1+15(s-1)\delta)}.

(21)

Combining (
18
), (
19
), (
20
), (
21
) yields

w(A)\frac{7(1-(s-1)\delta)}{1+15(s-1)\delta}>\sum_{i=2}^{k}(i-1)w(A_{V_{\mu\text{-large}}}^{i})

Combining this inequality with (
17
) yields,

\sum_{v\in V_{\mu\text{-large}}\cap V_{\delta\text{-small}}}m_{v}(A_{v},A_{v})<(1-\alpha)\left(\frac{1+15(s-1)\delta}{16}\right)\left(w(A)+w(A)\frac{7(1-(s-1)\delta)}{1+15(s-1)\delta}\right)=(1-\alpha)\left(\frac{1+15(s-1)\delta}{16}\right)\left(\frac{1+15(s-1)\delta+7(1-(s-1)\delta)}{1+15(s-1)\delta}\right)w(A)=(1-\alpha)\frac{1+(s-1)\delta}{2}w(A),

and thus (
15
) is proven and the proof is complete.
∎

Here we combine Theorem
4.12
and Corollary
3.7
to deduce amplified local testability for codes modelled over
\operatorname{HDE-System}
’s

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
p

The code

-two layer system

-expanding

with

yields that every code

,

.

6
Example: Single orbit affine invariant codes are locally testable

Below, we show how to use Theorem
5.1
in order to prove that single orbit affine invariant codes over a field of characteristic
p
are locally testable. This fact was already proved by the Kaufman and Sudan
[
KS08
]
, but our proof is different and we show more: we show that the family of single orbit affine invariant codes is amplified locally testable.

We start with defining single orbit affine invariant codes. Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
be a positive integer,
p
be a prime and
\mathbb{K}
be a finite field. We recall that the following:

1.

The
affine group

\operatorname{Aff}(\mathbb{K}^{n})=\mathbb{K}^{n}\rtimes\operatorname{GL}(\mathbb{K}^{n})
(where
\operatorname{GL}(\mathbb{K}^{n})
are the invertible linear transformations on
\mathbb{K}^{n}
) is the group of couples of the form
(\underline{y},L)
where
\underline{y}\in\mathbb{K}^{n}
and
L\in\operatorname{GL}(\mathbb{K}^{n})
acting on
\mathbb{K}^{n}
by

(\underline{y},L).\underline{x}=L\underline{x}+\underline{y}.

2.

The group
\operatorname{Aff}(\mathbb{K}^{n})
acts on functions
f\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\mathbb{K}^{n}}
by

((\underline{y},L).f)(\underline{x})=f((\underline{y},L)^{-1}.\underline{x})=f((-L^{-1}\underline{y},L^{-1}).\underline{x})=f(L^{-1}\underline{x}-L^{-1}\underline{y}).

3.

For
k^{\prime}\in\mathbb{N},1\leq k^{\prime}\leq n+1
, a set of
k^{\prime}
vectors
\underline{x}_{1},...,\underline{x}_{k^{\prime}}
are said to be in
general position
if
\underline{x}_{2}-\underline{x}_{1},...,\underline{x}_{k^{\prime}}-\underline{x}_{1}

4.

For every

A

is linear code that is defined as follows: Fix some

such that

Define

to be the linear code defined by

\mathcal{E}=\{(\underline{y},L).\underline{e}_{0}:(\underline{y},L)\in\operatorname{Aff}(\mathbb{K}^{n})\}.

In other words,
\underline{c}\in C
if and only if for every

((\underline{y},L).\underline{e}_{0})\cdot\underline{c}=0.

Again, note that every equation in

is uniquely determined by its support.

We will want to show that any single orbit affine invariant code is modelled over a two layer system

. Defining

Thus, we can define

. Note the following:

•

For
\Phi

•

The parameter

In summation, any choice of a set

defines a single orbit affine invariant code as above and two sets

Claim 6.1
.

There is a constant
2\leq k^{\prime}\leq\min\{k,n+1\}
such that for every admissible set

,
|S|=k^{\prime}
.

Proof.

We will prove that for every two admissible sets

it holds that
|S_{1}|=|S_{2}|
and thus we can take

where

is any admissible set. Let

be admissible sets. By definition

are in the

-dimensional affine subspace spanned by the vectors in

are in general position, it follows that they span the same affine subspace as the vectors in

and thus

. The fact that

are in general position, the fact that

Proposition 6.2
.

For every

Abusing notation, we say that a set

Proposition 6.3
.

Let
\underline{e}_{0}\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\mathbb{K}^{n}}
be as in the definition of the code
C\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\mathbb{K}^{n}}
and denote
\tau_{0}=\operatorname{supp}(\underline{e}_{0})
with
|\tau_{0}|=k
. Also denote
E=\{(\underline{y},L).\tau_{0}:(\underline{y},L)\in\operatorname{Aff}(\mathbb{K}^{n})\}
as above.

For every admissible set
S\subseteq\{1,...,k\}
there are linear functions
\ell_{1}^{S},...,\ell_{k}^{S}:(\mathbb{K}^{n})^{S}\rightarrow\mathbb{K}^{n}
such that the following holds:

•

For every
(\underline{y}_{j})_{j\in S}\in(\mathbb{K}^{n})^{S}
and every
i\in S
,
\ell_{i}^{S}((\underline{y}_{j})_{j\in S})=\underline{y}_{i}
.

•

For a set
\tau\subseteq V
it holds that
\tau\in E
if and only if there are
\{\underline{y}_{j}\in\mathbb{K}^{n}:j\in S\}
in general position such that

\tau=\{\ell_{1}^{S}((\underline{y}_{j})_{j\in S}),...,\ell_{k}^{S}((\underline{y}_{j})_{j\in S})\}.

Proof.

Denote
\tau_{0}=\{\underline{x}_{1},...,\underline{x}_{k}\}
as above. Fix
S
to be an admissible set and fix some
j_{0}\in S
. By the definition of admissible sets, for every
1\leq i\leq k
there are (unique) scalars
\alpha_{ij}\in\mathbb{K},j\in S\setminus\{j_{0}\}
such that

\underline{x}_{i}-\underline{x}_{j_{0}}=\sum_{j\in S\setminus\{j_{0}\}}\alpha_{ij}(\underline{x}_{j}-\underline{x}_{j_{0}}),

or equivalently

\underline{x}_{i}=\underline{x}_{j_{0}}+\sum_{j\in S\setminus\{j_{0}\}}\alpha_{ij}(\underline{x}_{j}-\underline{x}_{j_{0}}).

Define
\ell_{i}^{S}:(\mathbb{K}^{n})^{S}\rightarrow\mathbb{K}^{n}
by

\ell_{i}^{S}((\underline{y}_{j})_{j\in S})=\underline{y}_{j_{0}}+\sum_{j\in S\setminus\{j_{0}\}}\alpha_{ij}(\underline{y}_{j}-\underline{y}_{j_{0}}).

(22)

Note that it holds that for every
i\in S
,

\ell_{i}^{S}((\underline{y}_{j})_{j\in S})=\underline{y}_{i}.

We are left to prove that
\tau\in E
if and only if there are
\{\underline{y}_{j}\in\mathbb{K}^{n}:j\in S\}
in general position such that

\tau=\{\ell_{1}^{S}((\underline{y}_{j})_{j\in S}),...,\ell_{k}^{S}((\underline{y}_{j})_{j\in S})\}.

First, assume that
\tau=\{\underline{y}_{1},...,\underline{y}_{k}\}\in E
, then (after re-indexing if needed) there is
(\underline{y},L)\in\operatorname{Aff}(\mathbb{K}^{n})
such that for every
1\leq i\leq k
,
(\underline{y},L).\underline{x}_{i}=\underline{y}_{i}
. Thus, for every
1\leq i\leq k
,

\ell_{i}^{S}((\underline{y}_{j})_{j\in S})=\underline{y}_{j_{0}}+\sum_{j\in S\setminus\{j_{0}\}}\alpha_{ij}(\underline{y}_{j}-\underline{y}_{j_{0}})=(\underline{y},L).\underline{x}_{j_{0}}+\sum_{j\in S\setminus\{j_{0}\}}\alpha_{ij}((\underline{y},L).\underline{x}_{j}-(\underline{y},L).\underline{x}_{j_{0}})=L\underline{x}_{j_{0}}+\underline{y}+\sum_{j\in S\setminus\{j_{0}\}}\alpha_{ij}((L\underline{x}_{j}+\underline{y})-(L\underline{x}_{j_{0}}+\underline{y}))=\underline{y}+L(\underline{x}_{j_{0}}+\sum_{j\in S\setminus\{j_{0}\}}\alpha_{ij}(\underline{x}_{j}-\underline{x}_{j_{0}})=\underline{y}+L\ell_{i}^{S}((\underline{x}_{j})_{j\in S})=\underline{y}+L\underline{x}_{i}=(\underline{y},L).\underline{x}_{i}=\underline{y}_{i}.

(23)

Thus,

\tau=\{\ell_{1}^{S}((\underline{y}_{j})_{j\in S}),...,\ell_{k}^{S}((\underline{y}_{j})_{j\in S})\},

as needed.

Conversely, let
(\underline{y}_{j})_{j\in S}
be vectors in general position. We will show that

\{\ell_{1}^{S}((\underline{y}_{j})_{j\in S}),...,\ell_{k}^{S}((\underline{y}_{j})_{j\in S})\}\in E.

Indeed, there is
(\underline{y},L)\in\operatorname{Aff}(\mathbb{K}^{n})
such that for every
j\in S
,
(\underline{y},L).\underline{x}_{i}=\underline{y}_{i}
, thus by (
23
), for every
1\leq i\leq k

\ell_{i}^{S}((\underline{y}_{j})_{j\in S})=(\underline{y},L).\underline{x}_{i}

and it follows that

\{\ell_{1}^{S}((\underline{y}_{j})_{j\in S}),...,\ell_{k}^{S}((\underline{y}_{j})_{j\in S})\}\in E.

∎

For an admissible set
S
denote
\ell^{S}:(\mathbb{K}^{n})^{S}\rightarrow(\mathbb{K}^{n})^{k}
to by the linear transformation

\ell^{S}((\underline{y}_{j})_{j\in S})=(\ell^{S}_{1}((\underline{y}_{j})_{j\in S}),...,\ell^{S}_{k}((\underline{y}_{j})_{j\in S})).

Denote
M_{S}
to be the
k\times k^{\prime}
matrix representing

. Explicitly, by equation (
22
), we have that

\ell_{i}^{S}((\underline{y}_{j})_{j\in S})=(1-\sum_{j\in S\setminus\{j_{0}\}}\alpha_{ij})\underline{y}_{j_{0}}+\sum_{j\in S\setminus\{j_{0}\}}\alpha_{ij}\underline{y}_{j}.

Thus, if we denote

\beta{ij}=\begin{cases}\alpha_{ij}&j\neq j_{0}\\
(1-\sum_{j\in S\setminus\{j_{0}\}}\alpha_{ij})&j=j_{0}\end{cases},

where
1\leq i\leq k
and
j\in S
, we have that
M_{S}=(\beta_{ij})_{1\leq i\leq k,j\in S}
. From the above Proposition it follows that if we treat
\underline{y}_{S}=(\underline{y}_{j})_{j\in S}
as a column vector, then if
(\underline{y}_{j})_{j\in S}
is in general position, then the support of column vector
M_{S}(\underline{y}_{j})_{j\in S}
is in
E
. Vice versa, if
\{\underline{y}_{i}:1\leq i\leq k\}\in E
, then (up to re-indexing the
\underline{y}_{i}
’s) is holds for any admissible set
S
,
M_{S}(\underline{y}_{j})_{j\in S}=(\underline{y}_{i})_{1\leq i\leq k}
(where
(\underline{y}_{i})_{1\leq i\leq k}
is a column vector).

Next, we define the concept of general position of matrices.

Definition 6.4

(General position for
k^{\prime}\times k^{\prime}
matrix)
.

Let
S_{1},S_{2}
be two admissible sets and let
B=(\underline{y}_{j_{1},j_{2}})_{j_{1}\in S_{1},j_{2}\in S_{2}}
be a
k^{\prime}\times k^{\prime}
matrix with entries in
\mathbb{K}^{n}
. We note that
M_{S_{1}}B
is a
k\times k^{\prime}
matrix,
BM_{S_{2}}^{t}
is a
k^{\prime}\times k
matrix and
M_{S_{1}}BM_{S_{2}}^{t}
is a
k\times k
matrix.
We say that
B
is in general position with respect to
S_{1},S_{2}
if:

1.

Every row in
M_{S_{1}}B
is composed of
k^{\prime}
vectors in
\mathbb{K}^{n}
that are in general position.

2.

Every column in
BM_{S_{2}}^{t}
is composed of
k^{\prime}
vectors in
\mathbb{K}^{n}
that are in general position.

3.

The entries of
M_{S_{1}}BM_{S_{2}}^{t}
are pairwise different.

Proposition 6.5
.

If
B=(\underline{y}_{j_{1},j_{2}})_{j_{1}\in S_{1},j_{2}\in S_{1}}
is in general position, then the support of every row and every column in
M_{S_{1}}BM_{S_{2}}^{t}
are in
E
.

Proof.

By the definition of general position of
B
, every column in
BM_{S_{2}}^{t}
is composed of
k^{\prime}
vectors in
\mathbb{K}^{n}
that are in general position. Thus, the support of every column of
M_{S_{1}}(BM_{S_{2}}^{t})
is in
E
. Also, every row in
M_{S_{1}}B
is composed of
k^{\prime}
vectors in
\mathbb{K}^{n}
that are in general position. Thus every column of
(M_{S_{1}}B)^{t}=B^{t}M_{S_{1}}^{t}
is in general position and as above the support of every column of
M_{S_{2}}(B^{t}M_{S_{1}}^{t})
is in
E
. By transposing, it follows that the support of every row in
M_{S_{1}}BM_{S_{2}}^{t}
is in
E
.
∎

After this set-up, we can finally define of linear dependencies: Fix two admissible sets
S_{1},S_{2}
and for every
B=(\underline{y}_{j_{1},j_{2}})_{j_{1}\in S_{1},j_{2}\in S_{2}}
is in general position define
\operatorname{ld}_{B,S_{1},S_{2}}
to be

\operatorname{ld}_{B,S_{1},S_{2}}(\underline{e})=\begin{cases}1&\operatorname{supp}(\underline{e})\text{ is a support of a row vector in }M_{S_{1}}BM_{S_{2}}^{t}\\
-1&\operatorname{supp}(\underline{e})\text{ is a support of a column vector in }M_{S_{1}}BM_{S_{2}}^{t}\\
0&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}.

This is a linear dependency since every entry of
M_{S_{1}}BM_{S_{2}}^{t}
is contained in a row vector and in a column vector and thus they cancel each other. More formally, for
v\in V=\mathbb{K}^{n}
, we denote
v\in M_{S_{1}}BM_{S_{2}}^{t}
if
v
is an entry of
M_{S_{1}}BM_{S_{2}}^{t}
. We also denote
\operatorname{suppRow}(M_{S_{1}}BM_{S_{2}}^{t}),\operatorname{suppCol}(M_{S_{1}}BM_{S_{2}}^{t})
to denote the supports rows and columns in
M_{S_{1}}BM_{S_{2}}^{t}
. Then for every
\underline{c}\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{V}
it holds that

\sum_{\underline{e}\in\mathcal{E}}\operatorname{ld}_{B,S_{1},S_{2}}(\underline{e})(\underline{e}\cdot\underline{c})=\sum_{\underline{e}\in\mathcal{E}}\operatorname{ld}_{B,S_{1},S_{2}}(\underline{e})(\sum_{v\in\operatorname{supp}(\underline{e})}\underline{c}(v))=\sum_{\underline{e}\in\mathcal{E},\operatorname{supp}(\underline{e})\in\operatorname{suppRow}(M_{S_{1}}BM_{S_{2}}^{t})}(\sum_{v\in\operatorname{supp}(\underline{e})}\underline{c}(v))-\sum_{\underline{e}\in\mathcal{E},\operatorname{supp}(\underline{e})\in\operatorname{suppCol}(M_{S_{1}}BM_{S_{2}}^{t})}(\sum_{v\in\operatorname{supp}(\underline{e})}\underline{c}(v))=\sum_{v\in M_{S_{1}}BM_{S_{2}}^{t}}\underline{c}(v)-\underline{c}(v)=0.

The following Proposition shows that the linear dependencies do not depend on the choice of
S_{1},S_{2}
.

Proposition 6.6
.

For a
k\times k
matrix
M
and two admissible sets
S_{1},S_{2}
, denote
M|_{S_{1},S_{2}}
to be the
k^{\prime}\times k^{\prime}
matrix minor of
M
with rows indices in
S_{1}
and column indices in
S_{2}
.

Let
S_{1},S_{2},S_{1}^{\prime},S_{2}^{\prime}
be admissible sets and
B
be in general position with respect to
S_{1},S_{2}
. Then
(M_{S_{1}}BM_{S_{2}}^{t})|_{S_{1}^{\prime},S_{2}^{\prime}}
is in general position with respect to
S_{1}^{\prime},S_{2}^{\prime}
and

M_{S_{1}^{\prime}}(M_{S_{1}}BM_{S_{2}}^{t})|_{S_{1}^{\prime},S_{2}^{\prime}}M_{S_{2}^{\prime}}^{t}=M_{S_{1}}BM_{S_{2}}^{t}.

In particular, for every
B
be in general position with respect to
S_{1},S_{2}
there is a unique
B^{\prime}
in general position with respect to
S_{1}^{\prime},S_{2}^{\prime}
such that
\operatorname{ld}_{B,S_{1},S_{2}}=\operatorname{ld}_{B^{\prime},S_{1}^{\prime},S_{2}^{\prime}}
.

Proof.

If
S
is an admissible set and
\{\underline{y}_{i}:1\leq i\leq k\},\{\underline{y}_{i}^{\prime}:1\leq i\leq k\}\in E
, then by Proposition
6.3
if for every

it holds that

,
\underline{y}_{i}=\underline{y}_{i}^{\prime}

agree on their admissible set, they agree everywhere. Thus, in order to show that

it is sufficient to show that the row vectors of the two matrices agree on

and the column vectors of the matrices agree on

, and this also follows readily from Proposition

.
∎

Fix some admissible sets
S_{1},S_{2}
and define

We observe that the assuming that

: Namely,

(2,k,2k)
-system with

.

Next, we will prove that given that

or

Lemma 6.7
.

Let

be admissible sets. Then

|\{B:B\text{ is in general position with respect to }S_{1},S_{2}\}|\geq(|\mathbb{K}|^{n})^{(k^{\prime})^{2}}(1-\frac{k^{2}}{|\mathbb{K}|^{n}}).

Recall that
B
is in general position if the following holds

Every row in

is composed of
k^{\prime}
vectors in
\mathbb{K}^{n}
that are in general position.

Every column in

k^{\prime}

\mathbb{K}^{n}

The entries of

Below, we will show that the following:

Theorem 6.9

if

. The weight function on this graph is defined by

m_{\operatorname{opp}}(\{v,\tau\})=\sum_{\sigma\in T,v\in\sigma,\tau\in\sigma}w(\sigma).

Denote

to be the spectral expansion of this graph (note that here we are using spectral expansion and not the more relaxed Cheeger constant expansion defined above).

Proposition 7.4
.

If
X
is locally spherical, then for every
v\in V
,

\frac{1}{K}m_{\operatorname{opp}}(v)\leq w(v)\leq m_{\operatorname{opp}}(v).

Also, it holds for every
\tau\in E
that

\frac{1}{kK}m_{\operatorname{opp}}(\tau)\leq w(\tau).

Proof.

By the assumption that
X
is locally spherical, for every
\sigma\in T
such that
v\in\sigma
, there is at least one and at most
K

\tau\in E_{\operatorname{sph}(v)}
such that
v\in\sigma
and
\tau\in\sigma
. It follows that

w(v)=\sum_{\sigma\in T,v\in\sigma}w(\sigma)\leq\sum_{\sigma\in T,v\in\sigma}\sum_{\tau\in E_{\operatorname{sph}(v)}}w(\sigma)=\sum_{\tau\in E,\{v,\tau\}\in E_{\operatorname{opp}}}m_{\operatorname{opp}}(\{v,\tau\})=m_{\operatorname{opp}}(v),

and that

w(v)=\sum_{\sigma\in T,v\in\sigma}w(\sigma)\geq\frac{1}{K}\sum_{\sigma\in T,v\in\sigma}\sum_{\tau\in E_{\operatorname{sph}(v)}}w(\sigma)=\frac{1}{K}\sum_{\tau\in E,\{v,\tau\}\in E_{\operatorname{opp}}}m_{\operatorname{opp}}(\{v,\tau\})=\frac{1}{K}m_{\operatorname{opp}}(v).

Next, for
\tau\in E
, it holds for every
\sigma\in T
such that
\tau\in\sigma
that there is at most
kK
vertices such that
v\in\sigma
and
\tau\in E_{\operatorname{sph}(v)}
. Thus,

w(\tau)=\sum_{\sigma\in T,\tau\in\sigma}w(\sigma)\geq\frac{1}{kK}\sum_{\sigma\in T,\tau\in\sigma}w(\sigma)\sum_{v\in V,v\in\sigma,\tau\in E_{\operatorname{sph}(v)}}1=\frac{1}{kK}\sum_{v\in V,\tau\in E_{\operatorname{sph}(v)}}\sum_{\sigma\in T,\tau\in\sigma,v\in\sigma}w(\sigma)=\frac{1}{kK}m_{\operatorname{opp}}(\tau).

∎

In order to prove this Theorem, we will need the following general Lemmas regarding bipartite graphs:

Lemma 7.7

(Sampling in bipartite graphs)
.

Let
(V,E)
be a connected bipartite graph with
V=V_{1}\cup V_{2}
and a weight function
m
. Denote
\lambda
to be the spectral expansion of this graph. Fix
U\subseteq V_{1}
non-empty and denote
N(U)=\{v\in V_{2}:\exists u\in U,\{u,v\}\in E\}
. For
0<\alpha<1
, define
N(U)_{\geq\alpha}
to be the set of vertices
v\in N(U)
such that

\left|\frac{m(\{\{u,v\}:u\in U\})}{m(v)}-\frac{m(U)}{m(V_{1})}\right|\geq\alpha.

Then

m(N(U)_{\geq\alpha})\leq\frac{\lambda^{2}}{\alpha^{2}}m(U).

Proof.

Define

\phi_{U}:V\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
as

\phi_{U}(v)=\begin{cases}1-\frac{m(U)}{m(V_{1})}&v\in U\\
-\frac{m(U)}{m(V_{1})}&v\in V_{1}\setminus U\\
0&v\in V_{2}\end{cases}.

Since the graph is bipartite, it follows that if
\phi_{U}\perp\mathbbm{1}_{V_{1}},\mathbbm{1}_{V_{2}}
(where
\mathbbm{1}_{V_{i}}
is the indicator function on
V_{i}
), then
\|M\phi_{U}\|^{2}\leq\lambda^{2}\|\phi_{U}\|^{2}
. It is obvious that
\phi_{U}\perp\mathbbm{1}_{V_{2}}
and we leave it to the reader to check that
\phi_{U}\perp\mathbbm{1}_{V_{1}}
. We note that

\|\phi_{U}\|^{2}=m(U)\left(1-\frac{m(U)}{m(V_{1})}\right)^{2}+m(V_{1}\setminus U)\left(-\frac{m(U)}{m(V_{1})}\right)^{2}=m(U)\left(1-\frac{m(U)}{m(V_{1})}\right)\leq m(U).

For every
v\in V_{2}
,

M\phi_{U}(v)={\frac{1}{m(v)}\sum_{u\in U,\{u,v\}\in E}m(\{u,v\})\left(1-\frac{m(U)}{m(V_{1})}\right)+\sum_{u\in V_{1}\setminus U,\{u,v\}\in E}m(\{u,v\})\left(-\frac{m(U)}{m(V_{1})}\right)}=\left(\frac{1}{m(v)}\sum_{u\in U,\{u,v\}\in E}m(\{u,v\})1\right)-\frac{m(U)}{m(V_{1})}=\frac{m(\{\{u,v\}:u\in U\})}{m(v)}-\frac{m(U)}{m(V_{1})}.

Thus

\|M\phi_{U}\|^{2}=\sum_{v\in V_{2}}m(v)\left|\frac{m(\{\{u,v\}:u\in U\})}{m(v)}-\frac{m(U)}{m(V_{1})}\right|^{2}.

Note that it follows that

\|M\phi_{U}\|^{2}\geq m(N(U)_{\geq\alpha})\alpha^{2}.

Thus for the inequality
\|M\phi_{U}\|^{2}\leq\lambda^{2}\|\phi_{U}\|^{2}
, we deduce that

m(N(U)_{\geq\alpha})\leq\frac{\lambda^{2}}{\alpha^{2}}m(U)

as needed.
∎

Lemma 7.8
.

Let
(V,E)
be a connected bipartite graph with
V=V_{1}\cup V_{2}
and a weight function
m
. Denote
\lambda
to be the spectral expansion of this graph. For

and let

\frac{\sum_{v\in W,\{u,v\}\in E}m(\{v,u\})}{m(u)}\geq\delta.

\frac{m(U)}{m(W)}\leq\frac{4}{3\delta}(\frac{2\lambda}{\sqrt{\delta}}+\frac{m(U)}{m(V_{1})}).

Proof.

We note that

\sum_{u\in U}\sum_{v\in W,\{u,v\}\in E}m(\{v,u\})\geq\delta m(U).

Fix
\alpha=2\frac{\lambda}{\sqrt{\delta}}

N(U)_{\geq\alpha}
be as in Lemma
7.7
and
N(U)_{<\alpha}=N(U)\setminus N(U)_{\geq\alpha}
. By Lemma
7.7
,
m(N(U)_{\geq\alpha})\leq\frac{\delta}{4}m(U)
. Therefore

\delta m(U)\leq\sum_{u\in U}\sum_{v\in W,\{u,v\}\in E}m(\{v,u\})=\sum_{u\in U}\sum_{v\in W\cap N(U)_{<\alpha},\{u,v\}\in E}m(\{v,u\})+\sum_{u\in U}\sum_{v\in W\cap N(U)_{\geq\alpha},\{u,v\}\in E}m(\{v,u\})\leq\sum_{u\in U}\sum_{v\in W\cap N(U)_{<\alpha},\{u,v\}\in E}m(\{v,u\})+m(N(U)_{\geq\alpha})\leq\sum_{u\in U}\sum_{v\in W\cap N(U)_{<\alpha},\{u,v\}\in E}m(\{v,u\})+\frac{\delta}{4}m(U)={\sum_{v\in W\cap N(U)_{<\alpha}}m(\{\{u,v\}:u\in U\})+\frac{\delta}{4}m(U)}.

It follows that

m(U)\leq\frac{4}{3\delta}\sum_{v\in W\cap N(U)_{<\alpha}}m(\{\{u,v\}:u\in U\}).

(26)

Note that for every

\frac{m(\{\{u,v\}:u\in U\})}{m(v)}-\frac{m(U)}{m(V_{1})}<\alpha

m(\{\{u,v\}:u\in U\})<(\alpha+\frac{m(U)}{m(V_{1})})m(v).

Summing over all

yields

\sum_{v\in W\cap N(U)_{<2\lambda}}\sum_{\{v,u\}\in E,u\in U}m(\{v,u\})\leq(\alpha+\frac{m(U)}{m(V_{1})})m(W\cap N(U)_{<2\lambda}).

Combining this with (

as needed.
∎

7.2
Local to global error correction from spheres

Let
C\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{p}^{V}
be a code modelled over a two layer system
X
that is locally spherical. For every vertex
v\in V
, define the sphere code
C_{\operatorname{sph}(v)}\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{p}^{V_{\operatorname{sph}(v)}}
to be the code defined by

\mathcal{E}_{\operatorname{sph}(v)}=\{\underline{e}\in\mathcal{E}:\operatorname{supp}(\underline{e})\in E_{\operatorname{sph}(v)}\}.

Define a weight function
m_{\operatorname{sph}(v)}:E_{\operatorname{sph}(v)}\cup V_{\operatorname{sph}(v)}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
by

m_{\operatorname{sph}(v)}(\tau)=\sum_{\sigma\in T,\tau\in\sigma,v\in\sigma}w(\sigma)

and

m_{\operatorname{sph}(v)}(u)=\sum_{\tau\in E_{\operatorname{sph}(v)},u\in\tau}m_{\operatorname{sph}(v)}(\tau).

Use
m_{\operatorname{sph}(v)}
as the weight function for the code
C_{\operatorname{sph}(v)}\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{p}^{V_{\operatorname{sph}(v)}}
.

Definition 7.9

(Locally spherical LTC)
.

Let
C\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{p}^{V}
be a code modelled over a two layer system
X
that is locally spherical. We say that the code
C
is locally spherical LTC if the following holds:

1.

(Spheres are LTC) The sphere codes of all the vertices are uniformly locally testable.

2.

(Extendibility) For every
\underline{c}\in C_{\operatorname{sph}(v)}
there is
a\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
such that
\underline{c}_{\operatorname{ext}}\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{V_{\operatorname{sph}(v)}\cup\{v\}}
defined as

\underline{c}_{\operatorname{ext}}(u)=\begin{cases}\underline{c}(u)&u\in V_{\operatorname{sph}(v)}\\
a&u=v\end{cases},

fulfils all the equations on
\mathcal{E}
containing
v
, i.e., that for every
\underline{e}\in\mathcal{E}
, if
v\in\operatorname{supp}(\underline{e})
, then
\underline{e}\cdot\underline{c}_{\operatorname{ext}}=0
(note that the support of
\underline{e}
is in
V_{\operatorname{sph}(v)}\cup\{v\}
and therefore this equality is meaningful even though formally
\underline{c}_{\operatorname{ext}}
is not defined in
V\setminus(V_{\operatorname{sph}(v)}\cup\{v\})
).

Our main theorem in this section is the following.

Theorem 7.10
.

Let
C\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{p}^{V}
be a code modelled over a
(s,k,K)
two layer system
X
that is locally spherical. Assume that
C
is locally spherical LTC.There is
\lambda>0
such that if
X
is a
\lambda
-expanding
\operatorname{HDE-System}
and
\lambda_{\operatorname{opp}}\leq\lambda
, then
C
is locally testable.

Sketch of proof: For
\underline{c}\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{V}
, we denote

\mathcal{A}(\underline{c})=\{\underline{e}\in\mathcal{E}:\underline{e}\cdot\underline{c}\neq 0\},

and

A(\underline{c})=\{\tau\in E:\exists\underline{e}\in\mathcal{A}(\underline{c}),\operatorname{supp}(\underline{e})=\tau\}.

Our objective is to show that if
\frac{w(A)}{w(E)}
is small, then
\underline{c}
is close to
\underline{c}^{\prime}\in C
. We proceed via the following steps:

1.

We fix
\delta<\frac{1}{s-1}
.

2.

By locally spherical LTC, every vertex with a small sphere with respect to
A
can be corrected such that it has a
\delta
-small link.

3.

From the previous step it follows that we have a correction scheme that corrects every vertex with a small sphere to a vertex with a
\delta
-small link. We bound the weight of the corrected vertices in the scheme (similar to the proof of Theorem
3.5
) and show that there is
\underline{c}^{\prime}
close to
\underline{c}
such that every vertex with a
\delta
-small sphere with respect to
A(\underline{c}^{\prime})
has a small link with respect to
A(\underline{c}^{\prime})
. We will show that
\underline{c}^{\prime}\in C
.

4.

For
\underline{c}^{\prime}
above, the weight of
A(\underline{c}^{\prime})
summed in the
\delta
-large links is bounded by the weight of spherically large vertices. Thus, by Theorem
7.6
(using the expansion of the opposite graph) the weight of
\delta
-large links is negligible with respect to
A(\underline{c}^{\prime})
.

5.

By Theorem
4.12
, the
\lambda
-expansion of
X
implies that every set
A(\underline{c}^{\prime})
with negligible weight of
\delta
-large links has the unique neighbor property. It follows by Lemma
3.4
, that
\underline{c}^{\prime}\in C
as needed.

8
Distance of codes modelled over two layer systems

Here we show that we can bound the minimal distance of codes modelled over a
(s,k,K)
-two layer system based on the expansion of the ground graph. Namely, we show the following:

Theorem 8.1
.

Let
X=(V,E,T)
be a
(s,k,K)
-two layer system and
C\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{p}^{V}
be a linear code modelled over
X
. Then for every
\underline{c}\in C\setminus\{\underline{0}\}
it holds that

\|\underline{c}\|\geq\frac{16}{s^{4}(s-1)^{2}k}\left(1-s(s-1)(k-1)\lambda_{\operatorname{gr}}\right).

Proof.

Let
\underline{c}\in C\setminus\{\underline{0}\}
. Denote

U=\operatorname{supp}(\underline{c})=\{v\in V:\underline{c}(v)\neq 0\},

then
\|\underline{c}\|=\frac{w(U)}{w(V)}
.
Define
A\subseteq E
to be

A=\{\tau\in E:U\cap\tau\neq\emptyset\}.

Denote

A_{U}^{i}=\{\tau\in A:|U\cap\tau|=i\}.

Since
\underline{c}
is a code word it follows that
A_{U}^{1}
is empty: Otherwise, there is an equation that meets the support of
\underline{c}
at exactly one variable and thus not satisfied. Therefore

w(A)=\sum_{i=2}^{k}w(A_{U}^{i}).

Thus,

w(A)=\sum_{i=2}^{k}w(A_{U}^{i})=\sum_{i=2}^{k}\sum_{v\in U}\frac{1}{i}\sum_{\tau\in A_{U}^{i},v\in\tau}w(\tau)\leq\frac{1}{2}\sum_{v\in U}\sum_{i=2}^{k}\sum_{\tau\in A_{U}^{i},v\in\tau}w(\tau)\leq\frac{1}{2}\sum_{v\in U}\sum_{\tau\in A,v\in\tau}w(\tau)\leq\frac{1}{2}\sum_{v\in U}\sum_{\tau\in E,v\in\tau}w(\tau)\leq^{\text{Proposition }\ref{w(v) as sum of w(tau)'s prop}}\frac{1}{2}\sum_{v\in U}sw(v)=\frac{s}{2}w(U).

By Corollary
2.2
,
\frac{k}{2}w(E)\geq w(V)
and thus

\|\underline{c}\|=\frac{w(U)}{w(V)}\geq\frac{4}{sk}\frac{w(A)}{w(E)}.

It follows that in order to prove the assertion stated above, we need to prove that

\frac{w(A)}{w(E)}\geq\frac{4}{s^{3}(s-1)^{2}}\left(1-s(s-1)(k-1)\lambda_{\operatorname{gr}}\right).

If
\frac{w(A)}{w(E)}>\frac{4}{s^{3}(s-1)^{2}}
we are done, and thus we can assume that
\frac{w(A)}{w(E)}\leq\frac{4}{s^{3}(s-1)^{2}}
. Note that by definition, every
v\in U
is
1
-large with respect to
A
. Thus, by Lemma
4.9
(with
\mu=1
), we get that if
\frac{w(A)}{w(E)}\leq\frac{4}{s^{3}(s-1)^{2}}
, then

\frac{s(s-1)(k-1)}{2}\lambda_{\operatorname{gr}}+\frac{s^{3}(s-1)^{2}}{4}\frac{w(A)}{w(E)}\geq\left(1-\frac{s(s-1)(k-1)}{2}\lambda_{\operatorname{gr}}\right)\sum_{i=2}^{k}(i-1)\frac{w(A_{U}^{i})}{w(A)}\geq\left(1-\frac{s(s-1)(k-1)}{2}\lambda_{\operatorname{gr}}\right)\frac{1}{w(A)}\sum_{i=2}^{k}w(A_{U}^{i})=1-\frac{s(s-1)(k-1)}{2}\lambda_{\operatorname{gr}}.

It follows that

\frac{w(A)}{w(E)}\geq\frac{4}{s^{3}(s-1)^{2}}\left(1-s(s-1)(k-1)\lambda_{\operatorname{gr}}\right),

as needed.
∎

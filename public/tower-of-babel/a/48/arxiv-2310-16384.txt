Distributed Uncertainty Quantification of Kernel Interpolation on Spheres Thanks: The corresponding author is Di Wang.
Shao-Bo Lin, Xingping Sun, Di Wang

Abstract
For radial basis function (RBF) kernel interpolation of scattered data, Schaback [ 30 ] in 1995 proved that the attainable approximation error and the condition number of the underlying interpolation matrix cannot be made small simultaneously. He referred to this finding as an “uncertainty relation”, an undesirable consequence of which is that RBF kernel interpolation is susceptible to noisy data. In this paper, we propose and study a distributed interpolation method to manage and quantify the uncertainty brought on by interpolating noisy spherical data of non-negligible magnitude. We also present numerical simulation results showing that our method is practical and robust in terms of handling noisy data from challenging computing environments.

Kernel interpolation, distributed uncertainty mitigation, scattered spherical data

Uncertainty Relation of Kernel Interpolation on Spheres

Let
L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{d})
be the Hilbert space with inner product

\langle f,g\rangle=\int\limits_{\mathbb{S}^{d}}f(x)g(x)d\omega(x),\quad f,g\in L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{d}),

where
d\omega
denotes the rotational invariant probability measure on
\mathbb{S}^{d}
.
Let
\mathbb{H}^{d}_{k}
be the subspace of
L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{d})
consisting of all the homogeneous spherical harmonics of degree
k
. Suppose that
\{Y_{k,\ell}\}_{\ell=1}^{Z(d,k)}
is an orthonormal basis of
\mathbb{H}_{k}^{d}
. Here
Z(d,k):=\mbox{dim}\ \mathbb{H}^{d}_{k}
. Let
P_{k}^{d+1}
denote the Gegenbauer polynomial of order
\frac{d-1}{2}
and degree

normalized so that
P_{k}^{d+1}(1)=1
. It is known
[
25
]
that

Z(d,k)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{2k+d-1}{k+d-1}{{k+d-1}\choose{k}},&\mbox{if}\ k\geq 1,\\
1,&\mbox{if}\ k=0,\end{array}\right.

(4)

and that

\sum_{\ell=1}^{Z(d,k)}Y_{k,\ell}(x)Y_{k,\ell}(x^{\prime})=\frac{Z(d,k)}{\Omega_{d}}P_{k}^{d+1}(x\cdot x^{\prime}),\quad x,x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{S}^{d},

(5)

in which
\Omega_{d}=\frac{2\pi^{\frac{d+1}{2}}}{\Gamma(\frac{d+1}{2})}
is the volume of
\mathbb{S}^{d}
. Equation (
5
) is recognized in the literature as the addition formula for homogeneous spherical harmonics. For
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{d}),
its Fourier coefficients
\hat{f}_{k,\ell}
are defined as
\hat{f}_{k,\ell}:=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}}f(x)Y_{k,\ell}(x)d\omega(x).

We take the liberty of assuming that the Fourier coefficient definition above is extended in a routine manner so that it is applicable to all the tempered distributions on
\mathbb{S}^{d}.

For a nonnegative integer
s
, denote by
\mathcal{P}_{s}^{d}
the set of all algebraic polynomials with degree at most
s
defined on
\mathbb{S}^{d}
. Then we have that
\mathcal{P}_{s}^{d}=\bigoplus_{k=0}^{s}\mathbb{H}^{d}_{k}
and that dim
\mathcal{P}_{s}^{d}=\sum_{k=0}^{s}Z(d,k)=Z(d+1,s)\sim s^{d}
. Here and hereafter by writing
A(p)\sim B(p)
between the two nonnegative quantities
A(p)
and
B(p)
depending on a parameter
p
other than the dimension
d
, we mean that there exist two constants
0<C_{1}<C_{2}
depending only on
d
such that
C_{1}B(p)\leq A(p)\leq C_{2}B(p).

A univariate tampered distribution
\phi
supported on
[-1,1]
is called a spherical basis distribution (SBD)
[
27
]
, if
its Gegenbauer series expansion

\phi(u)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\hat{\phi}_{k}\frac{Z(d,k)}{\Omega_{d}}P_{k}^{d+1}(u)
has all positive coefficients, i.e.,

0<\hat{\phi}_{k}<\infty
for all
k=0,1,\ldots.
Here the series convergence is under the topology of tampered distributions.
If in addition
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\hat{\phi}_{k}{Z(d,k)}<\infty
, then
\phi
is called a spherical basis function (SBF). Given an SBD
\phi
, we define

\mathcal{N}_{\phi}:=\left\{f(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\sum_{\ell=1}^{Z(d,k)}\hat{f}_{k,\ell}Y_{k,\ell}(x):\|f\|_{\phi}^{2}:=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\hat{\phi}_{k}^{-1}\sum_{\ell=1}^{Z(d,k)}\hat{f}_{k,\ell}^{2}<\infty\right\}

as the native space of
\phi
. In particular, if
\hat{\phi}_{k}=1
for all

then
\mathcal{N}_{\phi}
coincides with
L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{d})
.

The approximation power of the kernel interpolation scheme defined as in (
2

\Lambda:=\{x_{i}\}_{i=1}^{|D|}
on
\mathbb{S}^{d}
and the spectrum of

; see e.g.
[
28
,
27
]

on

: the mesh norm

, the separation radius
q_{\Lambda}
, and the mesh ratio
\rho_{\Lambda}
:

\displaystyle h_{\Lambda}

\displaystyle:=\max_{x\in\mathbb{S}^{d}}\min_{x_{i}\in\Lambda}\mbox{dist}(x,x_{i}),

(6)

\displaystyle q_{\Lambda}

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{2}\min_{i\neq i^{\prime}}\mbox{dist}(x_{i},x_{i^{\prime}}),

(7)

\displaystyle\rho_{\Lambda}

\displaystyle:=\frac{h_{\Lambda}}{q_{\Lambda}}.

(8)

Here
\mbox{dist}(x,x^{\prime})

is the geodesic (great circle) distance between
x
and
x^{\prime}
on
\mathbb{S}^{d}
. If there is a constant
\tau=\tau(d)
depending only on

or simply that

We point out here that
the mesh norm is actually the Hausdorff distance between the two sets
\Lambda
and
\mathbb{S}^{d}.
In the sequel, we will also refer to the points in

as sampling sites.

If we have in (

\varepsilon_{i}=0
almost surely, then we refer to this as the “noise-free model”, which

Lemma 1

Suppose that

However, a different picture emerges when one deals with noisy data. As is well-known from basic numerical linear algebra, the stability or the lack thereof for the kernel interpolation algorithm depend on the condition number of the kernel matrix
\Phi_{D}
. In the RBF setting, Shaback
[
30
]
established the following interesting inequality:

P^{2}(x)\left(\sigma_{\min}(\Phi^{(x)}_{D})\right)^{-1}\geq 1,\quad x\in\mathbb{S}^{d}\setminus\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{|D|}\}.

(11)

Here
\sigma_{\min}(A)
denotes the smallest eigenvalue of a positive definite matrix
A,

\Phi^{(x)}_{D}
the

(|D|+1)\times(|D|+1)
kernel matrix associated with the
(|D|+1)
points
x,x_{1},\ldots,x_{|D|}

the so called “power function” defined by

P(x):=\|\phi_{x}-\mathcal{P}_{\Lambda}(\phi_{x})\|_{\phi},

in which
\mathcal{P}_{\Lambda}
denotes the projector from
\mathcal{N}_{\phi}
to its subspace
S_{\Lambda}:=\mbox{span}\{\phi_{x_{j}}\}^{|D|}_{j=1}.
Due to the reminiscence of (
11

P(x)
has a direct bearing on the rate of attainable approximation of the kernel interpolant; 2. Schaback’s uncertainty relation carries over to the SBF setting in a routine fashion; 3. The two quantities
\sigma_{\min}(\Phi^{(x)}_{D})
and
\sigma_{\min}(\Phi_{D})
are indistinguishably close for appropriately chosen
x\in\mathbb{S}^{d}\setminus\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{|D|}\}.

Simply put, if the rate of attainable approximation of the kernel interpolant is
R(h_{\Lambda})
, where
R:(0,\infty)\rightarrow(0,\infty)
is an increasing function and satisfies
\lim_{t\downarrow 0}R(t)=0,
then Schaback’s uncertainty relation asserts that
\left(\sigma_{\min}(\Phi_{D})\right)^{-1}
grows at least the magnitude
R^{-1/2}(h_{\Lambda})
, resulting in a noise propagation of a sizable proportion and accentuating the notion that for the type of noisy data specified in (
1
), the approximation error of kernel interpolation of our concern based on quasi-uniformly distributed sampling sites does not decrease statistically when the sample size increases. The following result and our numerical experiments further lend support to Schaback’s uncertainty relation; see Figure

3
Distributed Kernel Interpolation

Corollary
2
shows that kernel interpolation performs poorly while confronting noisy data of non-negligible magnitude. To overcome this major drawback, we propose and study in this section a distributed kernel interpolation (DKI) method, which is motivated by the “distributed learning” in the literature
[
37
,
19
]
. Figuratively speaking, this is a divide-and-conquer strategy for uncertainty quantification. To elaborate, we
describe the method in three steps.

•

Step 1. Data set decomposition:
For a fixed
1\leq m\leq|D|
, decompose the data set
D
into
m
disjoint subsets
D_{j}:=\{(x_{i,j},y_{i,j}\}_{i=1}^{|D_{j}|}
so that
D=\bigcup_{j=1}^{m}D_{j}
,
D_{j}\cap D_{j^{\prime}}=\varnothing
for
j\neq j^{\prime}
, and the input set
\Lambda_{j}
of

is
\tau
-quasi-uniform for some
\tau>2
.

•

Step 2. Kernel interpolation on data sub-sets:
On each subset

, carry out the minimal norm interpolation as follows.

f_{D_{j}}:=\arg\min\limits_{f\in\mathcal{N}_{\phi}}\|f\|_{\phi},\qquad\mbox{s.t.}\quad f(x_{i,j})=y_{{i,j}},\quad(x_{i,j},y_{i,j})\in D_{j}.

(13)

We will henceforth refer to the interpolant
f_{D_{j}}
as an “interpolet”.

•

Step 3. Synthesization of interpolets:
Build the DKI estimator by synthesizing interpolets via weighted averaging

\overline{f}_{D}=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\frac{|D_{j}|}{|D|}f_{D_{j}}.

(14)

Write

S^{T}_{D,W}{\bf c}=S_{D}^{T}{\sqrt{W}}{\bf c}=\sum_{i=1}^{|D|}\sqrt{w_{i}}c_{i}\phi_{x_{i}},

and

L_{\phi,D,W}f=S^{T}_{D,W}S_{D,W}f=\sum_{i=1}^{|D|}w_{i}f(x_{i})\phi_{x_{i}}

(19)

as the positive operator from
\mathcal{N}_{\phi}
to
\mathcal{N}_{\phi}
,
where
W={\rm diag}(w_{1},\dots,w_{|D|})
.
As usual, we will refer to the above
w_{i}
’s as “weights”. If
w_{1}=\dots=w_{|D|}=1/|D|
, we denote
S^{T}_{D,W}
and
L_{\phi,D,W}
as
S_{D}^{T}
and
L_{\phi,D}
respectively. Since

\frac{1}{|D|}\Phi_{D}=S_{D}S_{D}^{T},

it follows from (
3
) that

f_{D}=S_{D}^{T}(S_{D}S_{D}^{T})^{-1}S_{D}{\bf y}_{D}=S_{D,W}^{T}(S_{D,W}S_{D,W}^{T})^{-1}{\bf y}_{D,W},

(20)

where
{\bf y}_{D,W}=(\sqrt{w_{1}}y_{1},\dots,\sqrt{w_{|D|}}y_{|D|})^{T}.

We call
\mathcal{Q}_{\Lambda,s}:=\{(w_{i,s},x_{i}):w_{i,s}>0\hbox{~and~}x_{i}\in\Lambda\}

a positive quadrature rule of order
s
on
\mathbb{S}^{d}
if

\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}}P(x)d\omega(x)=\sum_{x_{i}\in\Lambda}w_{i,s}P(x_{i}),\qquad\forall P\in\mathcal{P}_{s}^{d}.

(21)

Positive spherical quadrature rules such as the ones in (
21
) are discussed in
[
23
,
3
]
. In particular, Theorem 3.1
[
3
]
asserts that for a
\tau
-quasi uniform point set
\Lambda
, there is a positive quadrature rule of order
s
with
s\leq\bar{c}_{1}|\Lambda|^{1/d}
and
0<w_{i,s}\leq\bar{c}_{2}|\Lambda|^{-1}
for some constants
\bar{c}_{1},\bar{c}_{2}
depending only on
\tau,d,s
, which we will refer to as a D-type quadrature rule for short, as it plays a crucial role in our analysis in the present paper. For a D-type quadrature rule, we denote:

W_{s}:={\rm diag}(w_{1,s},\dots,w_{|\Lambda|,s})\quad{\rm and}\quad W^{1/2}_{s}:={\rm diag}(w^{1/2}_{1,s},\dots,w^{1/2}_{|\Lambda|,s}).

Our main result in this section is the following theorem, which gives upper bounds for norms of products of pertinent operators when the weights in (
19
) are positive.

Theorem 9
.

Let
\mathcal{Q}_{\Lambda,s}:=\{(w_{i,s},x_{i}):w_{i,s}>0\hbox{~and~}x_{i}\in\Lambda\}
be a D-type
quadrature rule of order
s
on
\mathbb{S}^{d}
.
If
\hat{\phi}_{k}\sim k^{-2\gamma}
with
\gamma>d/2
,
then there is a constant
C^{\prime}
depending only on
\gamma
and
d
, such that for any
\lambda\geq C^{\prime}s^{-2\gamma}
, there holds

\|(L_{\phi,D,W_{s}}+\lambda I)^{-1/2}(L_{\phi}+\lambda I)^{1/2}\|\leq 2\sqrt{3}/3

(22)

and

\|(L_{\phi,D,W_{s}}+\lambda I)^{1/2}(L_{\phi}+\lambda I)^{-1/2}\|\leq\sqrt{6}/2,

(23)

where
g(L_{\phi,D,W_{s}})
and
g(L_{\phi})
are also defined by spectral calculus for
g:R_{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
.

The proof of Theorem
9
is involved and will be given in the next subsection. Here we emphasize its usefulness in deriving approximation error estimates for KI’s (
2
) and DKI’s (
14
). In particular, we use Theorem
9
to prove a class of Sobolev-type sampling inequalities.

Corollary 10
.

Let
\Lambda
be a
\tau
-quasi-uniform set and
\mathcal{Q}_{\Lambda,s}:=\{(w_{i,s},x_{i}):w_{i,s}>0\hbox{~and~}x_{i}\in\Lambda\}
a D-type
quadrature rule of order
s
on
\mathbb{S}^{d}
.
Suppose that
\phi,\psi
are SBDs satisfying
\hat{\phi}\sim k^{-2\gamma}
with
\gamma>d/2
and
\hat{\psi}\sim(\hat{\phi})^{\beta}
for some
0\leq\beta\leq 1.

Then there exists a constant
C^{*}
depending only on
\beta,\gamma
, and
d,
such that for any

\lambda\geq C^{*}s^{-2\gamma}
and
any
f\in\mathcal{N}_{\phi}
, there holds

\|f\|_{\psi}\leq(4/3)^{(1-\beta)/2}\left(\lambda^{(1-\beta)/2}\|f\|_{\phi}+\lambda^{-\beta/2}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{|D|}w_{i,s}(f(x_{i}))^{2}\right)^{1/2}\right).

For a quasi-uniform point set
\Lambda
, we have
h_{\Lambda}\sim q_{\Lambda}\sim{|D|}^{-1/d}
.
Corollary
10
then yields the following sampling inequality

\|f\|_{\psi}\leq C_{2}^{*}\left(\lambda^{1/2}\|f\|_{\phi}+\left(\frac{1}{|D|}\sum_{i=1}^{|D|}(f(x_{i}))^{2}\right)^{1/2}\right),

where
C_{2}^{*}
is a constant independent of
|D|
. In particular, the case
\beta=0
gives rise to the
L^{2}
sampling inequality on spheres established in
[
11
, Theorem 5.1]
. We caution, however, that this implication is valid only under the assumption that the point set
\Lambda
is quasi-uniform.

Proof of Corollary
10
.

For a given
f\in\mathcal{N}_{\phi}
and
0\leq\beta\leq 1
, we apply Lemma
7
and Theorem
9
to get

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\psi}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|L_{\phi}^{(1-\beta)/2}f\|_{\phi}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|(L_{\phi}+\lambda I)^{(1-\beta)/2}(L_{\phi,D,W_{s}}+\lambda I)^{-(1-\beta)/2}\|\|(L_{\phi,D,W_{s}}+\lambda I)^{(1-\beta)/2}f\|_{\phi}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(4/3)^{(1-\beta)/2}\lambda^{-\beta/2}\|(L_{\phi,D,W_{s}}+\lambda I)^{1/2}f\|_{\phi}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(4/3)^{(1-\beta)/2}\lambda^{-\beta/2}\|L_{\phi,D,W_{s}}^{1/2}f\|_{\phi}+(4/3)^{(1-\beta)/2}\lambda^{-(1-\beta)/2}\|f\|_{\phi}.

In developing the above inequalities, we have also used
the basic inequality
(a+b)^{1/2}\leq a^{1/2}+b^{1/2}
for nonnegative real numbers
a,b
and the well-known Cordes inequality
[
1
]
for positive operators
A,B
,

\|A^{u}B^{u}\|\leq\|AB\|^{u},\qquad 0\leq u\leq 1.

(24)

By the definition of
L_{\phi,D,W_{s}}
, we deduce

\displaystyle\|L_{\phi,D,W_{s}}^{1/2}f\|_{\phi}^{2}=\langle L_{\phi,D,W_{s}}^{1/2}f,L_{\phi,D,W_{s}}^{1/2}f\rangle_{\phi}=\langle f,L_{\phi,D,W_{s}}f\rangle_{\phi}=\langle f,S_{D}^{T}W_{s}S_{D}f\rangle_{\phi}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\sqrt{W_{s}}S_{D}f,\sqrt{W_{s}}S_{D}f\rangle_{\phi}=\sum_{i=1}^{|D|}w_{i,s}(f(x_{i}))^{2}.

It then follows that

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\psi}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(4/3)^{(1-\beta)/2}\lambda^{-\beta/2}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{|D|}w_{i,s}(f(x_{i}))^{2}\right)^{1/2}+(4/3)^{(1-\beta)/2}\lambda^{-(1-\beta)/2}\|f\|_{\phi},

which is the desired result.

4.2
Quadrature rule for products of functions

To prove Theorem
9
, we need
a quadrature rule for products of two functions in
\mathcal{N}_{\phi}
associated with certain positive operators of our interest.

Proposition 11
.

Let
\hat{\phi}_{k}\sim k^{-2\gamma}
with
\gamma>d/2
and
u,v\in[0,1/2]
. If
\Lambda=\{x_{i}\}_{i=1}^{|\Lambda|}
is
\tau
-quasi uniform and
s\leq\tilde{c}_{1}|\Lambda|^{1/d}
, then for a D-type quadrature rule
\mathcal{Q}_{\Lambda,s}=\{(w_{i,s},x_{i}):\hbox{~and~}x_{i}\in\Lambda\}
of order
s
and
each pair of
f,g\in\mathcal{N}_{\phi}
, there holds

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}}(\eta_{\lambda,u}(L_{\phi})f)(x)(\eta_{\lambda,v}(L_{\phi})g)(x)d\omega(x)-\sum_{x_{i}\in\Lambda}w_{i,s}\left[(\eta_{\lambda,u}(L_{\phi})f)(x_{i})(\eta_{\lambda,v}(L_{\phi})g)(x_{i})\right]\right|

\displaystyle\leq\tilde{c}^{\prime}\lambda^{-\max\{u,v\}}s^{-\gamma}\|f\|_{\phi}\|g\|_{\phi},

in which
\eta_{\lambda,u}(L_{\phi})f=(L_{\phi}+\lambda I)^{-u}f
for
0\leq u\leq 1/2
and
0<\lambda\leq 1
, and
\tilde{c}^{\prime}
is a constant depending only on
\gamma,\kappa,u,v,\tau
and
d
.

Proposition
11
extends
[
2
, Corollary 3.6]
which asserts that

\left|\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}}f(x)d\omega(x)-\sum_{x_{i}\in\Lambda}w_{i,s}f(x_{i})\right|\leq\tilde{c}_{1}^{\prime}s^{-\gamma}\|f\|_{\phi},\quad f\in\mathcal{N}_{\phi},

(25)

where
\tilde{c}_{1}^{\prime}
is a constant depending only on
\gamma,d
.
As a matter of fact, setting
u=v=0
and
g(x)\equiv 1
in Proposition
11
recovers (
25
). Furthermore, the results of Proposition
11
improve those of
[
8
, Proposition 1]
, which gives the following estimate:

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}}(\eta_{\lambda,u}(L_{\phi})f)(x)(\eta_{\lambda,v}(L_{\phi})g)(x)d\omega(x)-\sum_{x_{i}\in\Lambda}w_{i,s}\left[(\eta_{\lambda,u}(L_{\phi})f)(x_{i})(\eta_{\lambda,v}(L_{\phi})g)(x_{i})\right]\right|

\displaystyle\leq\tilde{c}\|f\|_{\phi}\|g\|_{\phi}\begin{cases}\lambda^{-u-v}s^{-2\gamma}+s^{-(1-u-v)\gamma},&\mbox{if}\ u+v<1,\\
\lambda^{-u-v}s^{-2\gamma}+\log s,&\mbox{if}\ u+v=1.\end{cases}

Obtaining the sharper upper bound here requires the following two lemmas from respectively Narcowich et al
[
27
, Corollary 5.4]
and Dai
[
5
]
.

Lemma 12
.

Let
\hat{\phi}_{k}\sim k^{-2\gamma}
with
\gamma>d/2
. Let
\Lambda
be a finite subset of
\mathbb{S}^{d}
. Then there exists a constant
\tilde{c}_{1}
depending only on
\gamma
and
d
, such that for any
f\in\mathcal{N}_{\phi}
and
s^{*}\geq\tilde{c}_{1}/q_{\Lambda}
, there is a
P_{s^{*}}\in\mathcal{P}_{s^{*}}^{d}
satisfying
\|P_{s^{*}}\|_{\phi}\leq 6\|f\|_{\phi}
and
\|P_{s^{*}}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{d})}\leq 6\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{d})}
.
Moreover, we have

f(x_{i})=P_{s^{*}}(x_{i}),\qquad i=1,\dots,|\Lambda|,

(26)

and

\|f-P_{s^{*}}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{d})}\leq\tilde{c}_{2}h_{\Lambda}^{\gamma}\|f\|_{\phi},

(27)

where
\tilde{c}_{2}
is a constant depending only on
d,\beta
and
\gamma
.

Lemma 13
.

Let
s\in\mathbb{N}
and
\Lambda
be quasi-uniform. Suppose that
\mathcal{Q}_{\Lambda,s}:=\{(w_{i,s},x_{i}):x_{i}\in\Lambda\}
is a D-type quadrature rule of order
s
on
\mathbb{S}^{d}
. Then for any
P\in\Pi_{s^{\prime}}^{d}
with
s^{\prime}\in\mathbb{N}
, there holds

\displaystyle\sum_{x_{i}\in\Lambda}w_{i,s}|P(x_{i})|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\tilde{c}_{1}(s^{\prime}/s)^{d}\|P\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{d})},\qquad s^{\prime}>\lfloor s/3\rfloor,

where
\tilde{c}_{1}
is a constant depending only on
d
.

The above lemma can be
derived by combining
[
5
, Lemma 4.4]
and
[
5
, Lemma 4.5]
(see also
[
6
, Theorem 2.1]
and
[
24
, Theorem 3.3]
).
With the help of above lemmas, we are in a position to prove Proposition

.

Proof of Proposition
11
.

Let
\tilde{c}\geq\tilde{c_{1}}
be a positive constant properly chosen so that
s:=\tilde{c}/q_{\Lambda}
satisfies the pertinent interpolation condition in Lemma
12
. Let
P_{f}^{*}
and
P_{g}^{*}
be, respectively, the interpolating polynomials (in accordance to Lemma
12
) from
\Pi^{d}_{s}

for
\eta_{\lambda,u}(L_{\phi})f
and
\eta_{\lambda,v}(L_{\phi})g\in\mathcal{N}_{\phi}
.
Using a standard “zero-sum” technique and Hölder’s inequality, we derive
that

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}}(\eta_{\lambda,u}(L_{\phi})f)(x)(\eta_{\lambda,v}(L_{\phi})g)(x)d\omega(x)-\sum_{x_{i}\in\Lambda}w_{i,s}\left[(\eta_{\lambda,u}(L_{\phi})f)(x_{i})(\eta_{\lambda,v}(L_{\phi})g)(x_{i})\right]\right|

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{A}_{1}+\mathcal{A}_{2}+\mathcal{A}_{3},

in which

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{1}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\|(\eta_{\lambda,u}(L_{\phi})f)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{d})}\|(\eta_{\lambda,v}(L_{\phi})g)-P_{g}^{*}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{d})},

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{2}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\|P_{g}^{*}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{d})}\|(\eta_{\lambda,u}(L_{\phi})f)-P_{f}^{*}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{d})},

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{3}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}}P_{f}^{*}(x)P_{g}^{*}(x)d\omega(x)-\sum_{x_{i}\in\Lambda}w_{i,s}P_{f}^{*}(x_{i})P_{g}^{*}(x_{i})\right|.

Since
0\leq u,v\leq 1/2
, we get from (
16
) that

\|(\eta_{\lambda,u}(L_{\phi})f)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{d})}\|\leq\kappa^{1-2u}\|f\|_{\phi},\quad\|(\eta_{\lambda,v}(L_{\phi})g)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{d})}\|\leq\kappa^{1-2v}\|g\|_{\phi}.

We use the result of Lemma
12
to bound
\mathcal{A}_{1}
and
\mathcal{A}_{2}
as follows.

\mathcal{A}_{1}\leq\tilde{c}_{2}\kappa^{1-2u}h_{\Lambda}^{\gamma}\|\eta_{\lambda,v}(L_{\phi})g\|_{\phi}\|f\|_{\phi}\leq\tilde{c}_{2}\kappa^{1-2u}\lambda^{-v}h_{\Lambda}^{\gamma}\|f\|_{\phi}\|g\|_{\phi},\quad\mbox{and}

(28)

\mathcal{A}_{2}\leq 6\tilde{c}_{2}\kappa^{1-2v}h_{\Lambda}^{\gamma}\|\eta_{\lambda,u}(L_{\phi})f\|_{\phi}\|g\|_{\phi}\leq 6\tilde{c}_{2}\kappa^{1-2v}\lambda^{-u}h_{\Lambda}^{\gamma}\|f\|_{\phi}\|g\|_{\phi}.

(29)

It remains to bound
\mathcal{A}_{3}
. To this end, we let

Q^{*}_{1},Q_{2}^{*}\in\mathcal{P}_{\lfloor s/6\rfloor}^{d}
be the
L^{2}
-projections of
P_{f}^{*}
and
P_{g}^{*}
onto

\mathcal{P}_{\lfloor s/6\rfloor}^{d}
. The the following inequalities are immediate.

\displaystyle\|P_{f}^{*}-Q_{1}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{d})}\leq\tilde{c}_{3}s^{-\gamma}\|\eta_{\lambda,u}(L_{\phi})f\|_{\phi},\quad{\rm and}

(30)

\displaystyle\|P_{g}^{*}-Q_{2}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{d})}\leq\tilde{c}_{3}s^{-\gamma}\|\eta_{\lambda,v}(L_{\phi})g\|_{\phi},

where
\tilde{c}_{3}
is a constant depending only on
\gamma
. We then break up
\mathcal{A}_{3}
into five terms and use Hölder’s inequality to write

\mathcal{A}_{3}\leq\sum_{k=1}^{5}\mathcal{A}_{3,k},

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{3,1}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\|P^{*}_{f}-Q_{1}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{d})}\|P^{*}_{g}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{d})},

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{3,2}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\|P^{*}_{g}-Q_{2}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{d})}\|Q_{1}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{d})},

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{3,3}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{x_{i}\in\Lambda}w_{i,s}(P^{*}_{f}(x_{i})-Q_{1}(x_{i}))^{2}\right)^{1/2}\left(\sum_{x_{i}\in\Lambda}w_{i,s}(P^{*}_{g}(x_{i}))^{2}\right)^{1/2},

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{3,4}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{x_{i}\in\Lambda w_{i,s}}w_{i,s}(P^{*}_{g}(x_{i})-Q_{2}(x_{i}))^{2}\right)^{1/2}\left(\sum_{x_{i}\in\Lambda}w_{i,s}(Q_{1}(x_{i}))^{2}\right)^{1/2},

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{3,5}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}}Q_{1}(x)Q_{2}(x)d\omega(x)-\sum_{x_{i}\in\Lambda}w_{i,s}Q_{1}(x_{i})Q_{2}(x_{i})\right|.

The fact that
Q_{1}Q_{2}\in\mathcal{P}_{s}^{d}
implies
\mathcal{A}_{3,5}=0
. Furthermore,
(
30
) together with

\|Q_{1}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{d})}\leq\|P_{f}^{*}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{d})}
and
\|Q_{2}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{d})}\leq\|P_{g}^{*}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{d})}
yields

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{3,1}\leq 6\tilde{c}_{3}s^{-\gamma}\|\eta_{\lambda,u}(L_{\phi})f\|_{\phi}\|\eta_{\lambda,v}(L_{\phi})g\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{d})}\leq 6\tilde{c}_{3}\kappa^{1-2v}\lambda^{-u}s^{-\gamma}\|f\|_{\phi}\|g\|_{\phi},\;{\rm and}

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{3,2}\leq 6\tilde{c}_{3}\kappa^{1-2u}\lambda^{-v}s^{-\gamma}\|f\|_{\phi}\|g\|_{\phi}.

Using Lemma
13
with
s^{\prime}=s^{*}=\tilde{c}q_{\Lambda}^{-1}
, we have

\displaystyle A_{3,3}\leq\tilde{c}_{1}(\tilde{c}q_{\Lambda}^{-1}s^{-1})^{2d}\mathcal{A}_{3,1}\leq 6\tilde{c}_{1}(\tilde{c}q_{\Lambda}^{-1}s^{-1})^{2d}\tilde{c}_{3}\kappa^{1-2v}\lambda^{-u}s^{-\gamma}\|f\|_{\phi}\|g\|_{\phi},\;{\rm and}

\displaystyle A_{3,4}\leq\tilde{c}_{1}(\tilde{c}q_{\Lambda}^{-1}s^{-1})^{2d}\mathcal{A}_{3,2}\leq 6\tilde{c}_{1}(\tilde{c}q_{\Lambda}^{-1}s^{-1})^{2d}\tilde{c}_{3}\kappa^{1-2u}\lambda^{-v}s^{-\gamma}\|f\|_{\phi}\|g\|_{\phi}.

\mathcal{A}_{3}\leq 12(\tilde{c}_{1}(\tilde{c}q_{\Lambda}^{-1}s^{-1})^{2d}\tilde{+}2)\tilde{c}_{3}(\kappa+1)\lambda^{-\max\{u,v\}}s^{-\gamma}\|f\|_{\phi}\|g\|_{\phi}.

(31)

We then get from (

), (

) and (

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}}(\eta_{\lambda,u}(L_{\phi})f)(x)(\eta_{\lambda,v}(L_{\phi})g)(x)d\omega(x)-\sum_{x_{i}\in\Lambda}w_{i,s}\left[(\eta_{\lambda,u}(L_{\phi})f)(x_{i})(\eta_{\lambda,v}(L_{\phi})g)(x_{i})\right]\right|

\displaystyle\leq 12(\tilde{c}_{1}(\tilde{c}q_{\Lambda}^{-1}s^{-1})^{2d}\tilde{+}2)\tilde{c}_{3}(\kappa+1)\lambda^{-\max\{u,v\}}s^{-\gamma}\|f\|_{\phi}\|g\|_{\phi}

\displaystyle\;\;+7\tilde{c}_{2}(\kappa+1)\lambda^{-\max\{u,v\}}h_{\Lambda}^{\gamma}\|f\|_{\phi}\|g\|_{\phi}.

Since

is
\tau

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}}(\eta_{\lambda,u}(L_{\phi})f)(x)(\eta_{\lambda,v}(L_{\phi})g)(x)d\omega(x)-\sum_{x_{i}\in\Lambda}w_{i,s}\left[(\eta_{\lambda,u}(L_{\phi})f)(x_{i})(\eta_{\lambda,v}(L_{\phi})g)(x_{i})\right]\right|

\displaystyle\leq\tilde{c}^{\prime}\lambda^{-\max\{u,v\}}s^{-\gamma}\|f\|_{\phi}\|g\|_{\phi},

where
\tilde{c}^{\prime}
is a constant depending only on
\tau,u,v,\kappa
,
\gamma
and
d
.

.

4.3
Operator differences and products

Here we first use Proposition

to bound
\|L_{\phi}-L_{\phi,D,W_{s}}\|

.

Lemma 14
.

Let
\mathcal{Q}_{\Lambda,s}:=\{(w_{i,s},x_{i}):w_{i,s}>0\hbox{~and~}x_{i}\in\Lambda\}
be a positive
quadrature rule on

\hat{\phi}_{k}\sim k^{-2\gamma}

,

\displaystyle\|(L_{\phi}+\lambda I)^{-u}(L_{\phi,D,W_{s}}-L_{\phi})(L_{\phi}+\lambda I)^{-v}\|\leq\tilde{c}^{\prime}\lambda^{-\max\{u,v\}}s^{-\gamma}.

(32)

Proof.

Due to the definition of operator norm, we have from the reproducing property of
\phi
, i.e.
\langle\phi_{x},f\rangle_{\phi}=f(x)
for any

that

\displaystyle\|(L_{\phi}+\lambda I)^{-u}(L_{\phi,D,W_{s}}-L_{\phi})(L_{\phi}+\lambda I)^{-v}\|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{\|f\|_{\phi}\leq 1}\|(L_{\phi}+\lambda I)^{-u}(L_{\phi,D,W_{s}}-L_{\phi})(L_{\phi}+\lambda I)^{-v}f\|_{\phi}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{\|f\|_{\phi}\leq 1}\sup_{\|g\|_{\phi}\leq 1}\langle(L_{\phi}+\lambda I)^{-u}(L_{\phi,D,W_{s}}-L_{\phi})(L_{\phi}+\lambda I)^{-v}f,g\rangle_{\phi}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{\|f\|_{\phi}\leq 1}\sup_{\|g\|_{\phi}\leq 1}\langle(L_{\phi,D,W_{s}}-L_{\phi})(L_{\phi}+\lambda I)^{-u}f,(L_{\phi}+\lambda I)^{-v}g\rangle_{\phi}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{\|g\|_{\phi}\leq 1,\|f\|_{\phi}\leq 1}\left|\left\langle\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}}(L_{\phi}+\lambda I)^{-u}f(x^{\prime})\phi_{x^{\prime}}d\omega(x^{\prime})\right.\right.

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\left.\left.\sum_{i=1}^{|D|}w_{i,s}(L_{\phi}+\lambda I)^{-u}f(x_{i})\phi_{x_{i}},(L_{\phi}+\lambda I)^{-v}g\right\rangle_{\phi}\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{\|g\|_{\phi}\leq 1,\|f\|_{\phi}\leq 1}\left|\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}}(L_{\phi}+\lambda I)^{-u}f(x^{\prime})\langle\phi_{x^{\prime}},(L_{\phi}+\lambda I)^{-v}g\rangle_{\phi}d\omega(x^{\prime})\right.

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\left.\sum_{i=1}^{|D|}w_{i,s}(L_{\phi}+\lambda I)^{-u}f(x_{i})\langle\phi_{x_{i}},(L_{\phi}+\lambda I)^{-v}g\rangle_{\phi}\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{\|g\|_{\phi}\leq 1,\|f\|_{\phi}\leq 1}\left|\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}}(L_{\phi}+\lambda I)^{-u}f(x^{\prime})(L_{\phi}+\lambda I)^{-v}g(x^{\prime})d\omega(x^{\prime})\right.

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\left.\sum_{i=1}^{|D|}w_{i,s}(L_{\phi}+\lambda I)^{-u}f(x_{i})(L_{\phi}+\lambda I)^{-v}g(x_{i})\right|.

Then,
proposition

11
implies

\|(L_{\phi}+\lambda I)^{-1/2}(L_{\phi,D,W_{s}}-L_{\phi})(L_{\phi}+\lambda I)^{-1/2}\|\leq\tilde{c}^{\prime}\lambda^{-\max\{u,v\}}s^{-\gamma}.

This completes the proof of
lemma

14
.

Proof of Theorem
9
.

For
\lambda\geq 16\tilde{c}^{\prime 2}s^{-2}
, Lemma
14
yields

\|(L_{\phi}+\lambda I)^{-1/2}(L_{\phi,D,W_{s}}-L_{\phi})(L_{\phi}+\lambda I)^{-1/2}\|\leq 1/4.

(33)

Then,

\displaystyle(L_{\phi}+\lambda I)^{1/2}(L_{\phi,D,W_{s}}+\lambda I)^{-1}(L_{\phi}+\lambda I)^{1/2}

Besov-type spaces of variable smoothness on rough domains
A. I. Tyulenev

Abstract
The paper puts forward new Besov spaces of variable smoothness B p , q φ 0 ​ ( G , { t k } ) B^{\varphi_{0}}_{p,q}(G,\{t_{k}\}) and B ~ p , q , r l ​ ( Ω , { t k } ) \widetilde{B}^{l}_{p,q,r}(\Omega,\{t_{k}\}) on rough domains. A domain G G is either a bounded Lipschitz domain in ℝ n \mathbb{R}^{n} or the epigraph of a Lipschitz function, a domain Ω \Omega is an ( ε , δ ) (\varepsilon,\delta) -domain. These spaces are shown to be the traces of the spaces B p , q φ 0 ​ ( ℝ n , { t k } ) B^{\varphi_{0}}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\{t_{k}\}) and B ~ p , q , r l ​ ( ℝ n , { t k } ) \widetilde{B}^{l}_{p,q,r}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\{t_{k}\}) on domains G G and Ω \Omega , respectively. The extension operator Ext 1 : B p , q φ 0 ​ ( G , { t k } ) → B p , q φ 0 ​ ( ℝ n , { t k } ) \operatorname{Ext}_{1}:B^{\varphi_{0}}_{p,q}(G,\{t_{k}\})\to B^{\varphi_{0}}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\{t_{k}\}) is linear, the operator Ext 2 : B ~ p , q , r l ​ ( Ω , { t k } ) → B ~ p , q , r l ​ ( ℝ n , { t k } ) \operatorname{Ext}_{2}:\widetilde{B}^{l}_{p,q,r}(\Omega,\{t_{k}\})\to\widetilde{B}^{l}_{p,q,r}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\{t_{k}\}) is nonlinear. As a corollary, an exact description of the traces of 2-microlocal Besov-type spaces and weighted Besov-type spaces on rough domains is obtained.

The purpose of the present paper is to provide an exact description of the traces of Besov spaces of variable smoothness on domains with nonsmooth boundary.

The starting points for the Fourier-analytic approach to Besov spaces of variable smoothness were the papers

Definition 2.2

Let

To be able to work with more involved weight sequences, a definition of a new weighted class (see Definition

3

on Lipschitz domains

Throughout this section we fix parameters
p,q,\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}\in(0,\infty]
,
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
,
\alpha_{3}\geq 0

and a
p

.

Like in
[
27
]
for a Lipschitz domain, we shall consider either a special or a bounded Lipschitz domain.

A special Lipschitz domain is defined as an open set
G
lying above the graph of a Lipschitz function. More precisely,

G=\{x=(x^{\prime},x_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:x_{n}>\varpi(x^{\prime})\},

where
\varpi
is a function satisfying the Lipschitz condition on
\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
with constant
M\geq 1
. We set
K:=\{x=(x^{\prime},x_{n})|x_{n}>M|x^{\prime}|\}
.

A bounded Lipschitz domain is a bounded domain
G
, whose boundary
\partial G
can be covered by a finite number of balls
B_{k}
so that, possibly after a proper rotation,

\partial G\bigcap B_{k}
for each

is a part of the graph of a Lipschitz function.

Lemma 3.1

Let
\omega\in D
,
f\in B^{\varphi_{0}}_{p,q}(\{t_{k}\})
. Next, let
\varphi_{0}\in D(-K)
have nonzero integral and let
\varphi(x)=\varphi_{0}(x)-2^{-n}\varphi_{0}(\frac{x}{2})
,

,
\sigma_{2}\geq p
. Then
\omega f\in B^{\varphi_{0}}_{p,q}(\{t_{k}\})
and

\|\omega f|B^{\varphi_{0}}_{p,q}(\{t_{k}\})\|\leq C\|f|B^{\varphi_{0}}_{p,q}(\{t_{k}\})\|,

(3.1)

where the constant
C>0
in independent of
f
.

Remark 3.1

Lemma

enables one to easily reduce the problem on the intrinsic description of Besov spaces of variable smoothness on a bounded Lipschitz domain

to a similar problem on a special Lipschitz domain

at the end of this section.

In view of Remark

Lemma 3.2

(the local reproducing formula)

Let

be a special Lipschitz domain, let

For a proof, see

]

Remark 3.2

Of course, the local reproducing formula similar to (

Let

,

,

Let

, a function
\varphi_{0}\in D
,

\hbox{ for}\ m\in\mathbb{Z}^{n},\ j\in\mathbb{N}_{0},\ c\geq 1.

(3.3)

(\varepsilon,\delta)
-
domain
if, for any
x,y\in\Omega
such that
|x-y|<\delta
, there exists
a rectifiable path
\Gamma\subset\Omega
of length
<\frac{1}{\varepsilon}|x-y|
connecting
x
and
y
such that, for each
z\in\Gamma
,

\operatorname{dist}(z,\partial\Omega)>\varepsilon\frac{|z-x||z-y|}{|x-y|}.

We fix throughout this section some
(\varepsilon,\delta)
-domain
\Omega
. We shall also assume that
\operatorname{rad}(\Omega)=\inf\limits_{\alpha}\inf\limits_{x\in\Omega_{\alpha}}\sup\limits_{y\in\Omega_{\alpha}}|x-y|>0
. Here,

\Omega_{\alpha}
denotes the connected components of an open set
\Omega
.

We shall frequently use the following notations. For any cube
Q
, we set
Q^{\ast}:=\frac{9}{8}Q
.
By
F
and
F_{c}
we shall denote, respectively, the Whitney decomposition of the open sets

\Omega
and
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\overline{\Omega}
(see
[
9
]
for details).

Following
[
9
]
, for a cube
Q\in F_{c}
, we let
Q^{s}
denote any cube from
F
of maximal diameter such that

\operatorname{dist}(Q^{s},Q)<2\operatorname{dist}(Q,\partial\Omega)
. The cube
Q^{s}
will be called the reflection of
Q
. By
\mathcal{F}_{c}

we shall denote the cubes from the family
F_{c}
whose diameters are at most
\delta
.

Lemma 4.1

For every cube
Q\in\mathcal{F}_{c}
we have

1)

\frac{1}{C(n,\varepsilon,\delta)}l(Q)\leq l(Q^{s})\leq C(n,\varepsilon,\delta)l(Q);

(4.1)

2)

\operatorname{dist}(Q,Q^{s})\leq C(n,\varepsilon,\delta)l(Q);

(4.2)

3)
each cube
Q^{s}\in F
is a reflected cube for at most
C(n,\varepsilon,\delta)
cubes from the family
\mathcal{F}_{c}
.

The proof of this lemma is contained in § 5 of
[
9
]
(see the arguments succeeding estimate (5.3)).

Following
[
9
]
, we construct a nonlinear (since the local near-best approximations depend on
f
) extensions of the operator from
the
(\varepsilon,\delta)
-domain
\Omega
to the entire
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
Let
r\in(0,1]
and assume that
f\in L_{r}(Q\bigcap\Omega)
for every cube
Q
. We set

\widetilde{f}:=\operatorname{Ext}[f]=\chi_{\Omega}f+\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}Q\in F_{c}\\
\hbox{diam}(Q)\leq\delta\end{subarray}}P_{Q^{s}}[f]\phi_{Q},

(4.3)

where
\{\phi_{Q}\}_{Q\in F_{c}}
is the partition of unity for the open set
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\overline{\Omega}
,
and
P_{Q^{s}}[f]
is a polynomial of near-best approximation with constant
\lambda\geq 1
(see formula (2.8) in
[
9
]
for details) to a function
f
in the quasi-norm
L_{r}(Q^{s})
(see
[
9
]
for more details).

Actually, (
4.3
) defines a family of extension operators, since each choice of near
best approximants
P_{Q^{s}}[f]
give an extension
\widetilde{f}
. The results that follow apply to
any such extension operator
\operatorname{Ext}
with the restriction that the constant
\lambda\geq 1
is fixed

The proof of the main result depends on two auxiliary lemmas.

Lemma 4.2

(
[
9
]
)

Let
R_{0}
and

be two cubes from

with
\hbox{diam}(Q)\leq\hbox{diam}(R_{0})

\operatorname{dist}(Q,R_{0})\leq\min\{\delta,C_{1}\hbox{diam}(R_{0})\}

a fixed constant. Then, there
is a sequence of cubes

, from

, such that each

touches
R_{j-1}
,
j=l,\dotsc,m
, and for each

,
R_{j}\subset cR_{0}
and for
each

,
Q\subset cR_{j}
with

depending only on

and
\Omega
.

Lemma 4.3

(
[
9
]
)

Let
\beta>0
and
\operatorname{Ext}[f]
be defined by (
4.1
). Let
R
be a cube with
\operatorname{dist}(R,\partial\Omega)\leq\hbox{diam}R\leq a\delta
where
a
is a fixed constant depending only on
\varepsilon,\delta
and
n
. Let
f\in L_{r}(Q\bigcap\Omega)
for every cube
Q
,
\beta\leq r\leq 1
. Then we have

\left(E_{l}(\widetilde{f},R)_{r}\right)^{r}\leq C\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}S\in F\\
S\subset cR\end{subarray}}\left(E_{l}(f,S^{\ast})_{r}\right)^{r}

(4.4)

where
c,C
depend only on
l,n,\beta,\lambda
and
\varepsilon,\delta
.

For convenience, for
0<r\leq p\leq\infty
we set
p_{r}=\frac{p}{r}
. The exponent

is determined from the equation
\frac{1}{p_{r}}+\frac{1}{p^{\prime}_{r}}=1
.

The main result of this section is as follows

Theorem 4.1

Let
0<\alpha_{1}\leq\alpha_{2}<l
,
r\in(0,p]
,
0<p,q\leq\infty
,
p\geq r
,
\sigma_{1}\geq rp^{\prime}_{r}
,
\sigma_{2}\geq p
.
Next, let
p
be an admissible weight sequence
\{t_{k}\}
such that
\{\overline{t}_{k}\}\in\mathcal{X}^{\alpha_{3}}_{\alpha,\sigma,p}
. Then

\begin{split}&\|f|\widetilde{B}^{l}_{p,q,r}(\Omega,\{t_{k}\})\|\approx\Bigl(\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\Bigl(\int\limits_{\Omega}t^{p}_{k}(x)\left[\delta^{l}_{r}(x+2^{-k}I,\Omega)f\right]^{p}\,dx\Bigr)^{\frac{q}{p}}\Bigr)^{\frac{1}{q}}+\\
&+\Bigl(\int\limits_{\Omega}t^{p}_{0}(x)\left\|f|L_{r}\Bigl((x+I)\bigcap\Omega\Bigr)\right\|^{p}\,dx\Bigr)^{\frac{1}{p}}.\end{split}

(4.5)

(the modifications in the cases
p=\infty
or
q=\infty
are clear.)

Proof
. The estimate ‘
\geq
’ in (
4.5
) is clear. To establish the estimate ‘
\leq
’, we only consider the case
p,q\in(0,\infty)
,
because in the remaining cases the proof follows the same ideas, but is technically simpler.

For further purposes we note that in view of Remark
2.1
we may assume without loss of generality that
\sigma_{1}=rp^{\prime}_{r}
,
\sigma_{2}=p
. Moreover in view of Corollary 4.4
[
33
]
we have
\|\cdot|\widetilde{B}^{l}_{p,q,r}\|\approx\|\cdot|\widetilde{B}^{l}_{p,q,\tilde{r}}\|
for
r<\tilde{r}\leq p
and
\sigma_{1}>\tilde{r}p^{\prime}_{\tilde{r}}>rp^{\prime}_{r}
. Hence, without loss of generality we may assume that
r\in(0,\min{1,p}]
.

Until the end of the proof we shall fix the number
k_{0}=\min\{k\in\mathbb{N}|\sqrt{n}2^{-k}<a\delta\}
(the constant
a
is the same as in Lemma
4.3
).

In view of Remark
2.6
it suffices to prove the estimate

\begin{split}&\Bigl(\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\Bigl(\sum\limits_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}t^{p}_{k,m}\Bigl(\mathcal{E}_{l}(\widetilde{f},\widetilde{c}Q_{k,m})_{r}\Bigr)^{p}\Bigr)^{\frac{q}{p}}\Bigr)^{\frac{1}{q}}+\Bigl(\sum\limits_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}t^{p}_{0,m}\left\|\widetilde{f}|L_{r}(Q_{k_{0},m})\right\|^{p}\Bigr)^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq\\
&\leq C\Bigl(\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\Bigl(\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}\\
Q^{\ast}_{k,m}\subset\Omega\end{subarray}}t^{p}_{k,m}\Bigl(\mathcal{E}_{l}(f,Q^{\ast}_{k,m})_{r}\Bigr)^{p}\Bigr)^{\frac{q}{p}}\Bigr)^{\frac{1}{q}}+\Bigl(\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}\\
Q_{0,m}\subset\Omega\end{subarray}}t^{p}_{0,m}\left\|f|L_{r}\Bigl(Q_{0,m}\bigcap\Omega\Bigr)\right\|^{p}\Bigr)^{\frac{1}{p}}\end{split}

(4.6)

in which the constant
C>0
is independent of function
f
, and
\widetilde{c}\in(1,\frac{9}{8})
.

We fix
k\geq k_{0}
and estimate
\mathcal{E}_{l}(\widetilde{f},\widetilde{c}Q_{k,m})_{r}
for different
m\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
. Similarly to
[
9
]
,
we have three different cases to consider.

In the first case
, we consider the cubes such that
\operatorname{dist}(Q_{k,m},\partial\Omega)\leq\sqrt{n}2^{-k}
(the constant
a
is the same as in Lemma
4.3
).
We let
A_{1}(k)
denote the corresponding set of indexes
m
.

Let
\mu\in(0,\min\{1,p,q\}]
be fixed. Then, clearly by (
4.3
) and since the
l_{q}
-norm is monotone in
q
, we have for
m\in A_{1}(k)

E_{l}(\widetilde{f},\widetilde{c}Q_{k,m})_{r}\leq\Bigl(\sum\limits_{j=k}^{\infty}\Bigl(\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\widetilde{m}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}\\
Q_{j,\widetilde{m}}\subset c\widetilde{c}Q_{k,m}\end{subarray}}E_{l}(f,Q_{j,\widetilde{m}}^{\ast})_{r}\Bigr)^{\mu}\Bigr)^{\frac{1}{\mu}}.

In the case when a cube
Q_{j,\widetilde{m}}
does not lie in the family
F
it will be useful
to write
E_{l}(f,Q_{j,\widetilde{m}})_{r}=0
to have the shorthand for some inequalities.
Hence, using the Minkowski inequality (since
\frac{p}{\mu}\geq 1
), we find that

\Bigl(\sum\limits_{m\in A_{1}(k)}t^{p}_{k,m}\Bigl(\mathcal{E}_{l}(\widetilde{f},\widetilde{c}Q_{k,m})_{r}\Bigr)^{p}\Bigr)^{\frac{\mu}{p}}\leq\\
\leq C\sum\limits_{j=k}^{\infty}\Bigl(\sum\limits_{m\in A_{1}(k)}t^{p}_{k,m}2^{\frac{knp}{r}}\Bigl(\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\widetilde{m}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}\\
Q_{j,\widetilde{m}}\subset c\widetilde{c}Q_{k,m}\end{subarray}}E_{l}(f,Q^{\ast}_{j,\widetilde{m}})_{r}\Bigr)^{p}\Bigr)^{\frac{\mu}{p}}.

(4.7)

Let us estimate the inside sum in the outer brackets on the right of (
4.7
). To this end we shall use the monotonicity of the
l_{q}
-norm in
q

(taking into account that
0<r\leq 1
), apply the Hölder inequality (since
\frac{p}{r}\geq 1
). We have

\sum\limits_{m\in A_{1}(k)}t^{p}_{k,m}2^{\frac{knp}{r}}\Bigl(\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\widetilde{m}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}\\
Q_{j,\widetilde{m}}\subset c\widetilde{c}Q_{k,m}\end{subarray}}E_{l}(f,Q^{\ast}_{j,\widetilde{m}})_{r}\Bigr)^{p}\leq\\
\leq\sum\limits_{m\in A_{1}(k)}t^{p}_{k,m}2^{\frac{knp}{r}}\Bigl(\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\widetilde{m}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}\\
Q_{j,\widetilde{m}}\subset c\widetilde{c}Q_{k,m}\end{subarray}}\Bigl(E_{l}(f,Q^{\ast}_{j,\widetilde{m}})_{r}\Bigr)^{r}\Bigr)^{\frac{p}{r}}\leq\\
\leq\sum\limits_{m\in A_{1}(k)}t^{p}_{k,m}2^{\frac{(k-j)np}{r}}\Bigl(\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\widetilde{m}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}\\
Q_{j,\widetilde{m}}\subset c\widetilde{c}Q_{k,m}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{t^{\sigma_{1}}_{j,\widetilde{m}}}\Bigr)^{\frac{p}{\sigma_{1}}}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\widetilde{m}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}\\
Q_{j,\widetilde{m}}\subset c\widetilde{c}Q_{k,m}\end{subarray}}t^{p}_{j,\widetilde{m}}2^{\frac{jnp}{r}}(E_{l}(f,Q^{\ast}_{j,\widetilde{m}})_{r})^{p}.

(4.8)

where the constant
c>1
is the same as in Lemma
4.1
.

From (
4.7
), (
4.8
) in view of (
2.6
), (
2.7
) (note that
\frac{n}{p}+\frac{n}{\sigma_{1}}=\frac{n}{r}
and
that the overlap multiplicity of the cubes
c\widetilde{c}Q_{k,m}
is finite with fixed
k
and variable

) we have

\sum\limits_{m\in A_{1}(k)}t^{p}_{k,m}\Bigl(\mathcal{E}_{l}(\widetilde{f},\widetilde{c}Q_{k,m})_{r}\Bigr)^{p}\leq C\Bigl(\sum\limits_{j=k}^{\infty}2^{\mu(k-j)\alpha_{1}}\Bigl(\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\widetilde{m}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}\\
Q^{\ast}_{j,\widetilde{m}}\subset\Omega\end{subarray}}t^{p}_{j,\widetilde{m}}\Bigl(\mathcal{E}_{l}(f,Q^{\ast}_{j,\widetilde{m}})_{r}\Bigr)^{p}\Bigr)^{\frac{\mu}{p}}\Bigr)^{\frac{p}{\mu}}

(4.9)

In the
second case
, we have
\operatorname{dist}(Q_{k,m},\partial\Omega)>\sqrt{n}2^{-k}
and
\widetilde{c}Q_{k,m}\subset\Omega
.
So,
\widetilde{f}=\operatorname{Ext}[f]\equiv f
and there is nothing to prove.

In the
third case
,
\operatorname{dist}(Q_{k,m},\partial\Omega)>\sqrt{n}2^{-k}
and
Q_{k,m}\subset\Omega^{c}
.
We let
A_{3}(k)
denote the set of indexes
m
in this case. Clearly, for
m\in A_{3}(k)
the cube
Q_{k,m}
is contained in a unique Whitney cube
R\in F_{c}
. We have two cases to consider: in the first case
\operatorname{dist}(R,\partial\Omega)<a\delta
, and in the second case

\operatorname{dist}(R,\partial\Omega)\geq a\delta
. Accordingly, we split the index set
A_{3}(k)
into two disjoint subsets

A^{1}_{3}(k)
and
A^{2}_{3}(k)
. Clearly,
\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}\\
\mbox{diam}(Q)<\delta\end{subarray}}\phi_{Q}=1
on
R

if
\operatorname{dist}(R,\partial\Omega)<a\delta
and

is sufficiently small (the parameter

can
always be reduced without sacrificing the validity of Lemma
4.3
). Hence, for
m\in A^{1}_{3}(k)
and
Q_{k,m}\subset R
,
l(R)=2^{-j}
,
j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,
we have

\Bigl(\delta^{l}_{r}(\widetilde{c}Q_{k,m},\widetilde{c}Q_{k,m})\Bigr)^{r}\leq 2^{-jkr}\sup\limits_{|\alpha|=l}\sup\limits_{x\in\widetilde{c}Q_{k,m}}|D^{\alpha}\widetilde{f}(x)|^{r}\leq\\
\leq C2^{r(j-k)l}\sup\limits_{Q\in\Lambda_{R}}|P_{R^{s}}[f]-P_{Q^{s}}[f]|,

(4.10)

where
\Lambda_{R}
is the set of cubes from the family
F_{c}
which have common boundary points with the cube
R
.

By Lemma
4.1
and Lemma
4.2
, for any cube
Q\in\Lambda_{R}
there exists a chain of pairewisely touching cubes
R^{s}:=R_{0},\dotsc,Q^{s}
.
Besides, each cube from this chain lies in the family
F
, and the quantity of cubes in this chain is bounded from above by
C(n,\varepsilon,\delta)
.
We let
T_{Q}
denote this chain of cubes. An application of estimate 4.26 from
[
9
]
shows that

|P_{R^{s}}[f]-P_{Q^{s}}[f]|\leq C2^{\frac{jn}{r}}\Bigl(\sum\limits_{S\in T_{Q}}E_{l}(f,S^{\ast})_{r}\Bigr)^{\frac{1}{r}}.

(4.11)

Using (
4.10
), (
4.11
), this establishes

\mathcal{E}_{l}(\widetilde{f},cQ_{k,m})^{r}_{r}\leq C2^{r(j-k)l}\sum\limits_{Q\in\Lambda_{R}}\sum\limits_{S\in T_{Q}}\mathcal{E}_{l}(f,S^{\ast})^{r}_{r}.

(4.12)

We raise the left and right-hand side of (
4.12
) to the power
\frac{p}{r}
and apply the
Hölder inequality to the right-hand side (because
\frac{p}{r}\geq 1
). We also note that
on the right of (
4.12
) the number of terms is finite and is bounded above by a constant, which depends only on
n,\varepsilon,\delta
. As a result, we have

(\mathcal{E}_{l}(\widetilde{f},cQ_{k,m})_{r})^{p}\leq C2^{p(j-k)l}\sum\limits_{Q\in\Lambda_{R}}\sum\limits_{S\in T_{Q}}\Bigl(\mathcal{E}_{l}(f,S^{\ast})_{r}\Bigr)^{p}.

(4.13)

Next, using (
4.13
),

\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\in A^{1}_{3}(k)\\
Q_{k,m}\subset R\end{subarray}}t^{p}_{k,m}\Bigl(\mathcal{E}_{l}(\widetilde{f},Q_{k,m})_{r}\Bigr)^{p}\leq C2^{p(j-k)l}\Bigl(\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\in A^{1}_{3}(k)\\
Q_{k,m}\subset R\end{subarray}}t^{p}_{k,m}\Bigr)\sum\limits_{Q\in\Lambda_{R}}\sum\limits_{S\in T_{Q}}\Bigl(\mathcal{E}_{l}(f,S^{\ast})_{r}\Bigr)^{p}.

(4.14)

We now sum estimate (
4.14
) over all cubes
R\in\mathcal{F}_{c}
that contain the cubes
Q_{k,m}
,
m\in A^{1}_{3}(k)
. In view of (
2.5
), we have

\sum\limits_{m\in A^{1}_{3}(k)}t^{p}_{k,m}\mathcal{E}_{l}(\widetilde{f},\widetilde{c}Q_{k,m})^{p}_{r}\leq C\sum\limits_{S\in F}\Bigl(\frac{l(S)}{2^{k}}\Bigr)^{p(l-\alpha_{2})}t^{p}_{S}\Bigl(\mathcal{E}_{l}(f,S^{\ast})_{r}\Bigr)^{p}.

(4.15)

If we now formally put
\mathcal{E}_{l}(f,Q_{j,m}^{\ast})_{r}=0
for the cubes
Q_{j,m}
not lying in the family
F
,
then estimate (
4.15
) can be rewritten in the form (recall that we fixed

\Bigl(\sum\limits_{m\in A^{1}_{3}(k)}t^{p}_{k,m}\Bigl(\mathcal{E}_{l}(\widetilde{f},\widetilde{c}Q_{k,m})_{r}\Bigr)^{p}\Bigr)^{\frac{\mu}{p}\frac{1}{\mu}}\leq\\
\leq C\Bigl(\sum\limits_{j=k_{0}}^{k}2^{\mu(l-\alpha_{2})(j-k)}\Bigl(\sum\limits_{\widetilde{m}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}t^{p}_{j,\widetilde{m}}\Bigl(\mathcal{E}_{l}(f,Q_{j,\widetilde{m}}^{\ast})_{r}\Bigr)^{p}\Bigr)^{\frac{\mu}{p}}\Bigr)^{\frac{1}{\mu}}.

(here we also employ the condition

-norm in

).

Arguing as in the proof of Theorem 5.3 of
[
9
]

R\in\mathcal{F}_{c}
,
\operatorname{dist}(R,\partial\Omega)\geq a\delta

\sup\limits_{x\in R}|D^{\alpha}\widetilde{f}(x)|\leq C\|f|L_{r}(R^{\prime})\|^{p}

\Bigl(\delta^{l}_{r}(\widetilde{c}Q_{k,m},\widetilde{c}Q_{k,m})\widetilde{f}\Bigr)^{p}\leq C2^{-klp}\|f|L_{r}(R^{\prime})\|^{p},\quad m\in A^{2}_{3}(k).

can be represented as a union of pairwise nonoverlapping cubes

), when

runs over the cubes from

for which

) and (

\sum\limits_{m\in A^{2}_{3}(k)}t^{p}_{k,m}\Bigl(\mathcal{E}_{l}(\widetilde{f},\widetilde{c}Q_{k,m})_{r}\Bigr)^{p}\leq C2^{(k_{0}-k)lp}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}\\
Q_{k_{0},m}\subset\Omega\end{subarray}}t^{p}_{k_{0},m}\|f|L_{r}(Q_{k_{0},m})\|^{p}.

\Bigl(\sum\limits_{k=k_{0}}^{\infty}\Bigl(\sum\limits_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}t^{p}_{k,m}\Bigl(\mathcal{E}_{l}(\widetilde{f},\widetilde{c}Q_{k,m})_{r}\Bigr)^{p}\Bigr)^{\frac{q}{p}}\Bigr)^{\frac{1}{q}}\leq\\

\begin{split}&\Bigl(\sum\limits_{k=k_{0}}^{\infty}\Bigl(\sum\limits_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}t^{p}_{k,m}\Bigl(\mathcal{E}_{l}(\widetilde{f},\widetilde{c}Q_{k,m})_{r}\Bigr)^{p}\Bigr)^{\frac{q}{p}}\Bigr)^{\frac{1}{q}}\leq\\

Next, from (

\begin{split}&\Bigl(\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\Bigl(\sum\limits_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}t^{p}_{k,m}\Bigl(\mathcal{E}_{l}(\widetilde{f},\widetilde{c}Q_{k,m})_{r}\Bigr)^{p}\Bigr)^{\frac{q}{p}}\Bigr)^{\frac{1}{q}}\leq C\Bigl(\sum\limits_{k=k_{0}}^{\infty}\Bigl(\sum\limits_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}t^{p}_{k,m}\Bigl(\mathcal{E}_{l}(\widetilde{f},\widetilde{c}Q_{k,m})_{r}\Bigr)^{p}\Bigr)^{\frac{q}{p}}\Bigr)^{\frac{1}{q}}+\\

Now from (

Introduction

Let
M
be a closed connected smooth manifold. In the sequel,
H:T^{*}M\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
will be a given continuous function, called the
Hamiltonian
, where
T^{*}M
is the cotangent bundle of
M
. We denote by
(x,p,u)
points of
T^{*}M\times\mathbb{R}
. We will assume that
H
is convex and superlinear in the variable
p
.

We are interested in the following approximation problem for Hamilton–Jacobi equations of the form

H(x,d_{x}u_{\lambda},\lambda u_{\lambda}(x))=c\ \ \textup{in $M$}

(1.1)

where
\lambda>0
is called a discount factor in view of stochastic optimal control and differential games. Note that in the limit case
\lambda=0
, there is a unique value
c\in\mathbb{R}
, the so-called
critical value
, such that

H(x,d_{x}u,0)=c\ \ \textup{in $M$}

(1.2)

admits solutions, so we will use this value
c
in (
1.1
) as well. The notion of
solution, subsolution and supersolution
adopted in this paper is in the viscosity sense
[
CL83
]
, see also the books
[
Bar94
,
BCD97
]
.

Our goal in this paper is to explore the asymptotic behavior, as
\lambda\to 0^{+}
, of the viscosity solutions of (
1.1
) in a setting where
H(x,p,u)
can be nonlinear in
u
, and not necessarily strictly increasing in
u
everywhere. Such equations will be called
nonlinear degenerate discounted H-J equations
. Note that this asymptotic problem is not easy because the critical equation (
1.2
) has infinitely many solutions, even up to additive constants in general. As will see later, Mather (minimizing) measures play a central role in the convergence of
u_{\lambda}
(as
\lambda\rightarrow 0^{+}
).

Our approximation problem originates from the seminal paper by Lions, Papanicolaou and Varadhan
[
LPV87
]
in 1987 on homogenization of Hamilton–Jacobi equations. In this work, they considered the
vanishing discount problem
(also called
ergodic approximation
)

\lambda u_{\lambda}(x)+G(x,d_{x}u_{\lambda})=0\ \ \textup{in $M$}

(1.3)

and discussed the asymptotic behavior of the solutions as
\lambda\to 0^{+}
. This is of course a particular case of our setting by choosing
H(x,p,u)=u+G(x,p)
. Equation (
1.3
) obeys the comparison principle and admits a viscosity solution denoted by
u_{\lambda}
. They pointed out
\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow 0_{+}}\lambda u_{\lambda}=-c(G)
with
c(G)
being the critical value of the system
G
. Therefore, the next step is to investigate the convergence of
u_{\lambda}+c(G)/\lambda
as
\lambda\rightarrow 0^{+}
as well. It turns out that
\{u_{\lambda}+c(G)/\lambda\}_{\lambda>0}
are equibounded and equi-Lipschitz, thus, by the Ascoli-Arzelà theorem and the stability property of viscosity solutions
[
CEL84
]
, they converge,
along subsequences
as
\lambda
goes to
0
, to viscosity solutions of the critical equation

G(x,d_{x}u)=c(G)\ \ \textup{in $M$.}

(1.4)

At that time, it was not clear if different subsequences yield the same limit solution of (
1.4
). This problem remained unsolved for quite some time. Some constraints on the possible limit solutions were found in
[
Gom08
,
ISM11
]
. The essential breakthrough was made by
[
DFIZ16b
]
, where they established the convergence to a unique limit solution as well as the characterization of the limit, by using tools from weak KAM theory. We also refer to
[
MS17
,
AS22
]
for geometric interpretation of the convergence.

The result of asymptotic convergence has been subsequently generalized to the second order HJ setting
[
MT17
,
IMT17a
,
IMT17b
]
. Nowadays, this topic is still very active and progress has been made in many different directions. We mention some here:
[
DFIZ16a
]
for a discrete time version,
[
CP19
]
for mean field games,

[
CCIZ19
,
GMT18
,
WYZ21
,
Che21
]
for contact Hamilton–Jacobi versions,
[
DSZ18
,
DZ21
,
Ish21
,
IJ20
]
for weakly coupled systems,
[
IS20
,
DD22
]
for a noncompact setting,
[
DW21
,
WYZ22
]
for HJ equations with negative discount factors.

We point out that all papers mentioned above required a strict monotonicity hypothesis. A recent work
[
Zav22
]
first studied some degenerate HJ equations of the form

\lambda\alpha(x)u_{\lambda}(x)+G(x,d_{x}u_{\lambda})=c(G)\ \ \textup{in $M$,}

(1.5)

where
\alpha:M\to\mathbb{R}
is non-negative and may vanish at some places. The author proved that the convergence still holds if
\alpha>0
on the projected Aubry set. This sheds some light on dealing with the convergence of solutions for systems with degeneracy.

In the present paper, we will establish an asymptotic convergence result for nonlinear discounted HJ equations (
1.1
) with degenerate discounting. We will show the importance of Mather measures in controlling the convergence of solutions. As merely a subset of the Aubry set, the Mather set allows a better understanding of systems, see our condition
(L4)
. As far as we know such a condition
(L4)
is new in the literature. This condition may be optimal for our convergence problem (see Section
10
for a counterexample showing some optimality), and in addition, we also discuss how to use
(L4)
to obtain a generalized comparison principle. As the Hamiltonians
H(x,p,u)
involved in our equation (
1.1
) depends implicitly or nonlinearly on the unknown function
u
, there is in general no explicit representation formula for the value function
u_{\lambda}
to equation (
1.1
). To overcome this difficulty, we borrow tools from weak KAM theory and use arguments that do not rely on explicit representation formulas of the value functions. That is another novelty comparing to previous works such as
[
DFIZ16b
,
Zav22
]
and provides a chance to further apply our results.

1.1
Setting

Let
M
be a closed connected smooth manifold endowed with a Riemannian metric. As
M
is compact, all such Riemannian metrics are equivalent and all our results are independent of this choice. Let
T^{*}M
and
TM
be the cotangent and tangent bundles of
M
, respectively. For
x\in M
, we will use
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{x}
to denote the norm associated to
g
, either on
T^{*}M
or on
TM
.

On this compact manifold
M
, we consider a continuous Lagrangian
L(x,v,u):TM\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
which satisfies the following conditions:

(L0)

(Monotonicity) For every given
(x,v)\in TM
,
L(x,v,u)
is non-increasing in

u\in\mathbb{R}
.

(L1)

(Convexity) For every given
x\in M
and
u\in\mathbb{R}
,
L(x,v,u)
is convex in
v\in T_{x}M
.

(L2)

(Superlinearity) For every
K,u_{0}\in[0,+\infty[
, we have

\sup\{K\lVert v\rVert_{x}-L(x,v,u)\mid(x,v,u)\in TM\times[-u_{0},u_{0}]\}<+\infty.

Note that condition
(L0)
implies
L(x,v,u)\geq L(x,v,u_{0})
for all
u\leq u_{0}
, so it suffices to assume the superlinearity for
L(x,v,u_{0})
in
(L2)
.

By Fenchel’s formula, the Lagrangian
L
has a conjugated Hamiltonian
H:T^{*}M\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
, i.e.,

H(x,p,u):=\sup_{v\in T_{x}M}p(v)-L(x,v,u),\text{ for every $(x,p,u)\in T^{*}M\times\mathbb{R}$}.

(1.6)

The Hamiltonian
H
is continuous, and conditions
(L0)
–
(L2)
on
L
directly lead to the following conditions on
H
:

(H0)

(Monotonicity) For every given
(x,p)\in T^{*}M
,
H(x,p,u)
is non–decreasing in

u\in\mathbb{R}
.

(H1)

(Convexity) For every given
x\in M
and
u\in\mathbb{R}
,
H(x,p,u)
is convex in
p\in T^{*}_{x}M
.

(H2)

(Superlinearity) For every
K,u_{0}\in[0,+\infty[
, we have

\sup\{K\lVert p\rVert_{x}-H(x,p,u)\mid(x,p,u)\in T^{*}M\times[-u_{0},u_{0}]\}<+\infty.

For brevity, we can introduce the Lagrangian
L^{r}:TM\to\mathbb{R},(x,v)\mapsto L(x,v,r)
for each fixed
r\in\mathbb{R}
, and its associated Hamiltonian
H^{r}:T^{*}M\to\mathbb{R}
,
(x,p)\mapsto H(x,p,r)
. Accordingly, we denote by
c(H^{r})
the critical value of
H^{r}
.

We aim to consider Hamilton–Jacobi equations of the form

H(x,d_{x}u_{\lambda},\lambda u_{\lambda}(x))=c(H^{0})\quad\textup{in $M$}

(HJ
λ
)

where
\lambda>0
. We are interested in understanding the asymptotic behavior of the global continuous viscosity solutions
u_{\lambda}:M\to\mathbb{R}
as
\lambda
goes to zero.

To this aim, we now introduce another condition
(L3)
on the Lagrangian
L
.

(L3)

(
u
-derivative at
0
)
For every
(x,v)\in TM
, the partial derivative
\partial L/\partial u(x,v,0)
exists. Moreover, for every compact subset
S\subset TM
, we can find a modulus of continuity
\eta_{S}
such that

\left\lvert L(x,v,u)-L(x,v,0)-u\frac{\partial L}{\partial u}(x,v,0)\right\rvert\leq\lvert u\rvert\eta_{S}(\lvert u\rvert),\text{ for all $(x,v,u)\in S\times\mathbb{R}.$}

Recall that a modulus of continuity is a non–decreasing function
\eta_{S}:[0,+\infty[\to[0,+\infty[
with
\lim_{u\to 0}\eta_{S}(u)=0
.

Actually, condition
(L3)
is quite general, in view of the fact that it holds whenever
L
is
C^{1}
.

In order to formulate the last condition, we will need to use the notion of Mather measure (see Section
2
) for the critical Lagrangian
L^{0}
. We denote by
\mathfrak{M}(L^{0})
the set of all Mather measures of
L^{0}
.

(L4)

(Non–degeneracy on Mather set)

\int_{TM}\frac{\partial L}{\partial u}(x,v,0)\,{\rm d}\tilde{\mu}(x,v)<0,\text{ for all Mather measures
$\tilde{\mu}\in\mathfrak{M}(L^{0})$}.

Such an integral form of monotonicity condition w.r.t.
u
concerning Mather measures is new, as far as we know. It is crucial in the study of the asymptotic behavior of solutions, and is also the key to obtaining a comparison principle for equation (
HJλ
) (see Section
8
for more discussion about
(L4)
).

Main results

1.2.1
Convergence of the solutions of (
HJλ
)

Let us first present the following result:

Theorem 1.1
.

If the Lagrangian
L:TM\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
satisfies conditions
(L0)
,
(L1)
,
(L2)
,
(L3)
and

equation (

Moreover, the viscosity solutions of (

of (

In addition, for every given

, the family of viscosity solutions of (
HJλ

Since the Hamiltonian

is not necessarily strictly increasing in

) with

obeys a comparison principle. Nonetheless, thanks to condition

, the solutions

Theorem 1.2
.

If the Lagrangian
L:TM\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
satisfies conditions
(L0)
,
(L1)
,
(L2)
,
(L3)

1.

The family of all possible viscosity solutions

2.

The particular function
u_{0}
is a viscosity solution of the critical Hamilton–Jacobi equation

H^{0}(x,d_{x}u)=c(H^{0})\quad\textup{in $M$}.

(1.7)

The function
u_{0}:M\to\mathbb{R}
is also the largest viscosity subsolution
w
of (

) satisfying

\int_{TM}w(x)\frac{\partial L}{\partial u}(x,v,0)\,{\rm d}\tilde{\mu}(x,v)\geq 0,\ \ \text{for all Mather measures $\tilde{\mu}\in\mathfrak{M}(L^{0})$.}

Remark 1.3
.

For each
\lambda>0
, let us denote by
\mathcal{F}_{\lambda}
the set of all continuous viscosity solutions of equation (

implies that

\mathrm{dist}_{H}(\mathcal{F}_{\lambda},\{u_{0}\})\to 0,\quad\textup{as $\lambda\to 0^{+}$}

denotes the Hausdorff distance between two sets in

\sup_{w_{\lambda}\in\mathcal{F}_{\lambda}}\|w_{\lambda}-u_{0}\|_{\infty}\to 0,\quad\textup{as $\lambda\to 0^{+}$}.

We also provide another formula for the limit

. This formula involves the Peierls barrier (see Section

) and Mather measures.

Theorem 1.4
.

The limit solution
u_{0}:M\to\mathbb{R}
obtained in Theorem

satisfies

u_{0}(x)=\inf_{\tilde{\mu}\in\mathfrak{M}(L^{0})}\frac{\int_{TM}h(y,x)\frac{\partial L}{\partial u}(y,v,0)\,{\rm d}\tilde{\mu}(y,v)}{\int_{TM}\frac{\partial L}{\partial u}(y,v,0)\,{\rm d}\tilde{\mu}(y,v)}.

where

is the Peierls barrier of the Lagrangian
L^{0}
.

Remarks 1.5
.

We now give some explanations about our hypotheses.

1.

Our analysis strongly relies on the convexity condition
(L1)
or equivalently
(H1)
, because we will use a dynamical systems approach in light of weak KAM theory for convex superlinear systems. We also refer to

]

2.

By the superlinearity condition

-C(K,u_{0})=\sup\{K\lVert v\rVert_{x}-L(x,v,u)\mid(x,v,u)\in TM\times[-u_{0},u_{0}]\},

then

is finite and

L(x,v,u)\geq K\lVert v\rVert_{x}+C(K,u_{0}),\text{ for all $(x,v,u)\in TM\times[-u_{0},u_{0}]$.}

(1.8)

As above, by condition

if we set

-C^{*}(K,u_{0})=\sup\{K\lVert p\rVert_{x}-H(x,p,u)\mid(x,p,u)\in T^{*}M\times[-u_{0},u_{0}]\},

we have
C^{*}(K,u_{0})
is finite and

H(x,p,u)\geq K\lVert p\rVert_{x}+C^{*}(K,u_{0}),\text{ for all $(x,p,u)\in T^{*}M\times[-u_{0},u_{0}]$.}

By standard arguments as in
[

]
, hypothesis

can also be weakened to a coercivity assumption:

(H2
′

For every given

.

Note that due to the compactness of

3.

Condition

is equivalent to the following one:

•

For every

1.3
Organization of the paper

The proofs of our results mainly use a dynamical systems approach in light of weak KAM theory, combined with some ideas from
[
Zav22
,
WYZ21
,
Che21
]
.

In Section
2
, we recall some basic facts on viscosity solutions of the Hamilton–Jacobi equations, and some useful results and tools from weak KAM theory or Aubry-Mather theory. In Section
3
we reveal properties of the viscosity solutions for specific equation (
HJλ
). In particular, we will use that any solution of (
HJλ
) is in fact a solution of certain non-discounted equation, to which we can directly apply the results in Section
2
. In Section
4
we prove Lemma
4.1
, which plays an important role in obtaining lower and upper bounds for the family of solutions
\{u_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda>0}
, in the subsequent Section
5
. With such a boundness estimate we then derive in Section
6
an equi-Lipschitz property for the family
\{u_{\lambda}\}
as well as their calibrated curves.

In Section
7
we deal with the crux of the proof for Theorem
1.2
. In view of conditions
(L3)
and
(L4)
we will establish several technical lemmas about the
u_{\lambda}
-calibrated curves and their asymptotic properties. It allows us to introduce a family of Borel probability measures associated with the
u_{\lambda}
-calibrated curves, and in particular, we obtain a selection principle for Mather measures. Such a kind of asymptotic analysis plays a crucial role in proving the convergence.

Section
8
is devoted to giving a comparison result for the HJ equation (

). Using Lemma
4.1
again and combining with condition

, we prove Theorem

and Theorem

. In Section

.

2
Preliminaries on
u

We will need results from weak KAM theory of convex autonomous Lagrangians and Hamiltonians independent of
u\in\mathbb{R}
. These results in the classical Aubry-Mather theory and weak KAM theory were originally established,
see
[
Mat91
,
Mat93
,
Fat08
]
, for Tonelli Lagrangians or Hamiltonians, which are by definition C
2
. As is well known, they can be generalized to continuous Lagrangians or Hamiltonians, due to the arguments in
[
Bar94
,
FS04
,
FS05
,
Fat12
,
DFIZ16b
]
and references therein.

Let us consider a continuous Lagrangian
{\mathfrak{L}}:TM\to\mathbb{R}
which satisfies:

(
{\mathfrak{L}}1
)

(Convexity) For every given
x\in M
, the Lagrangian

{\mathfrak{L}}(x,v)
is convex in
v\in T_{x}M
.

(
{\mathfrak{L}}2
)

(Superlinearity) For every
K\in[0,+\infty[
, we have

\sup\{K\lVert v\rVert_{x}-{\mathfrak{L}}(x,v)\mid(x,v)\in TM\}<+\infty.

The Hamiltonian
{\mathfrak{H}}:T^{*}M\to\mathbb{R}
associated to the Lagrangian
{\mathfrak{L}}:TM\to\mathbb{R}
is

{\mathfrak{H}}(x,p):=\sup_{v\in T_{x}M}p(v)-{\mathfrak{L}}(x,v),\text{ for every $(x,p)\in T^{*}M$}.

This Hamiltonian
{\mathfrak{H}}
is continuous and satisfies:

(
{\mathfrak{H}}1
)

(Convexity) For every given
x\in M
, the Hamiltonian
{\mathfrak{H}}(x,p)

is convex in
p\in T^{*}_{x}M
.

(
{\mathfrak{H}}2
)

(Superlinearity) For every
K\in[0,+\infty[
, we have

We now proceed to define the Mather (minimizing) measures for the Lagrangian
\mathfrak{L}
. This notion was first introduced by J. N. Mather in the case of a
C^{2}
Tonelli Lagrangian
[
Mat91
]
, where the minimizing measures were obtained by minimizing the integral of the Lagrangian with respect to a family of probability measures invariant with respect to the Euler-Lagrange flow. However, as realized by R. Mañé
[
Mn96
]
, this minimizing problem yields the same minimizers if it is done to a larger family of closed measures. This equivalent definition therefore can be adapted to the current paper’s setting since the Euler-Lagrange flow cannot be defined, see also
[
FS05
]
.

Definition 2.10
.

A Borel probability measure
\tilde{\mu}
on
TM
is called
closed
if it satisfies:

a)

\displaystyle{\int_{TM}\|v\|_{x}\,{\rm d}\tilde{\mu}(x,v)<+\infty}
;

b)

\displaystyle{\int_{TM}{\rm d}_{x}\varphi(v)\,{\rm d}\tilde{\mu}(x,v)=0}
for every
\varphi\in\mbox{\rm C}^{1}(M)
.

There is a way to obtain closed measures from Lipschitz curves: if
\gamma:[a,b]\to M
is a Lipschitz curve, we define the probability measure

\tilde{\mu}_{\gamma}
on
TM
by

\int_{TM}f(x,v)\,{\rm d}\tilde{\mu}_{\gamma}:=\frac{1}{b-a}\int_{a}^{b}f(\gamma(s),\dot{\gamma}(s))\,{\rm d}s,\quad\textup{for every $f\in C(TM)$.}

The support of the measure, denoted by
\operatorname{supp}\tilde{\mu}_{\gamma}
, is contained in the closure of the graph of the speed curve of
\gamma
, that is

\operatorname{supp}\tilde{\mu}_{\gamma}=\overline{\{(\gamma(s),\dot{\gamma}(s))\mid s\in[a,b],\text{where $\dot{\gamma}(s)$ exists}\}}.

Since
\gamma
is Lipschitz, if
K
is a Lipschitz constant for
\gamma
, we get

\operatorname{supp}\tilde{\mu}_{\gamma}\subset\{(x,v)\in TM\mid\lVert v\rVert_{x}\leq K\}.

Hence the measure
\tilde{\mu}_{\gamma}
has compact support. It is not difficult to check the following lemma.

Lemma 2.11
.

Suppose
\gamma_{n}:[a_{n},b_{n}]\to M,n\geq 0
is a sequence of equi-Lipschitz curves with a common Lipschitz constant
K
, then

\operatorname{supp}\tilde{\mu}_{\gamma_{n}}\subset\{(x,v)\in TM\mid\lVert v\rVert_{x}\leq K\},\text{ for all $n\geq 0$.}

In particular, the sequence
\tilde{\mu}_{\gamma_{n}}
is relatively compact for the weak topology on the space of probability measures on
TM
. Therefore, if
b_{n}-a_{n}\to+\infty
, we can extract a subsequence

\tilde{\mu}_{\gamma_{n_{i}}}
which weakly converges to a probability measure
\tilde{\mu}
on
TM
.
Moreover, any such limit measure
\tilde{\mu}
is a closed measure.

Proof.

The relative compactness is obvious. To verify that
\tilde{\mu}
is closed, we observe that: for any
C^{1}
function
\Phi:M\to\mathbb{R}
,

\displaystyle\int_{TM}{\rm d}_{x}\Phi(v)\,{\rm d}\tilde{\mu}(x,v)=\lim_{n_{i}\to+\infty}\int_{TM}{\rm d}_{x}\Phi(v)\,{\rm d}\tilde{\mu}_{\gamma_{n_{i}}}(x,v)=

\displaystyle\lim_{n_{i}\to+\infty}\frac{1}{b_{n_{i}}-a_{n_{i}}}\int_{a_{n_{i}}}^{b_{n_{i}}}\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}s}\Phi(\gamma(s))\,{\rm d}s

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{n_{i}\to+\infty}\frac{\Phi(b_{n_{i}})-\Phi(\gamma(a_{n_{i}}))}{b_{n_{i}}-a_{n_{i}}}=0,

since
\Phi
is bounded on the compact manifold
M
. This proves the desired result.
∎

Proposition 2.12
.

The following holds:

\min_{\tilde{\mu}}\int_{TM}{\mathfrak{L}}(x,v)\,{\rm d}\tilde{\mu}(x,v)=-c(\mathfrak{H})

where
\tilde{\mu}
varies in the set of closed probability measures. Hence,

\int_{TM}{\mathfrak{L}}(x,v)+c({\mathfrak{H}})\,{\rm d}\tilde{\mu}(x,v)\geq 0,
for any closed measure
\tilde{\mu}
.

Definition 2.13
.

A closed probability measure
\tilde{\mu}
on
TM
is called a
Mather measure
for the Lagrangian
{\mathfrak{L}}
if

\int_{TM}{\mathfrak{L}}(x,v)+c({\mathfrak{H}})\,{\rm d}\tilde{\mu}(x,v)=0.

The set of all Mather measures of
{\mathfrak{L}}
is denoted by
\mathfrak{M}({\mathfrak{L}})
. A
projected Mather measure
is a probability measure on
M
of the form
\mu=\pi\tilde{\mu}
, where
\tilde{\mu}\in\mathfrak{M}({\mathfrak{L}})
and
\pi:TM\to M
is the canonical projection.

The set
{\mathfrak{L}}
is non-empty. The Mather set
\widetilde{\cal M}_{\mathfrak{L}}\subset TM
defined as

\widetilde{\cal M}_{\mathfrak{L}}:=\overline{\bigcup_{\tilde{\mu}\in\mathfrak{M}({\mathfrak{L}})}\operatorname{supp}\tilde{\mu}}

is compact. This also implies that the set of Mather measures is compact for the weak topology on the space of probability measures on
TM
. We denote by
{\cal M}_{\mathfrak{L}}=\pi\circ\widetilde{\cal M}_{\mathfrak{L}}
the projected Mather set. It is a classical conclusion in Aubry-Mather theory that
{\cal M}_{\mathfrak{L}}\subset{\cal A}_{\mathfrak{L}}
, and
\cal A_{\mathfrak{L}}\backslash{\cal M}_{\mathfrak{L}}\neq\emptyset
could happen in rather wide occasions.

Finally, we summarize the properties of Mather measures.

Proposition 2.14
.

Suppose that
\mathfrak{L}:TM\to\mathbb{R}
is a Lagrangian satisfying (
{\mathfrak{L}}1
) and (
{\mathfrak{L}}2
). Then,

•

The set
\mathfrak{M}({\mathfrak{L}})
is not empty, i.e. there always exists a closed probability measure
\tilde{\mu}

such that
\int_{TM}\mathfrak{L}(x,v)+c(\mathfrak{H})\,{\rm d}\tilde{\mu}(x,v)=0
.

•

The Mather set
\widetilde{\cal M}_{\mathfrak{L}}=\overline{\bigcup_{\tilde{\mu}\in\mathfrak{M}({\mathfrak{L}})}\operatorname{supp}\tilde{\mu}}

is compact. In addition,
{\cal M}_{\mathfrak{L}}\subset{\cal A}_{\mathfrak{L}}
.

•

The set
\mathfrak{M}({\mathfrak{L}})
is compact for the weak topology on the space of probability measures on
TM
.

All the results above can be applied to any one of the Lagrangian
L^{r}:TM\to\mathbb{R},r\in\mathbb{R}
introduced in Section

.

We now consider a Lagrangian

(L0)
,
(L1)
and
(L2)
. The associated Hamiltonian

(H0)

(H1)

(H2)

By Theorem

H^{0}(x,d_{x}\hat{u})=c(H^{0})

H^{0}(x,p)=H(x,p,0)

\hat{u}_{+}=\hat{u}-\min_{M}\hat{u}\geq 0

\hat{u}_{+}

\hat{u}_{+}

HJλ
) for all

\hat{u}_{-}=\hat{u}-\max_{M}\hat{u}\leq 0

HJλ
) for all

\hat{u}_{-}\leq\hat{u}_{+}

u_{\lambda}(x):=\sup\{w(x)\mid\hat{u}_{-}\leq w\leq\hat{u}_{+},w\text{~is a continuous viscosity subsolution of~}\eqref{HJlambda}\}.

is indeed a viscosity solution. Therefore, we conclude that:

Proposition 3.1
.

For every
\lambda>0
, we can find a continuous viscosity solution
u_{\lambda}:M\to\mathbb{R}
of the discounted Hamilton–Jacobi equation (
HJλ
) such that
\hat{u}-\max_{M}\hat{u}\leq u_{\lambda}\leq\hat{u}-\min_{M}\hat{u}

Remark 3.2
.

However, equation (
HJλ
) may fail to have the uniqueness property of solutions because by condition

is not necessarily strictly increasing. For more on uniqueness of the solutions, see Section

Next, we will interpret a solution of equation (

{\bf H}^{\psi}(x,p):=H(x,p,\psi(x)).

(3.2)

(H1)
and
(H2)

{\bf H}^{\psi}

{\mathfrak{H}}1
) and (

Then we can easily verify the following conclusion:

Proposition 3.3
.

Any viscosity solution

An important Lemma

This section is devoted to proving Lemma
4.1
, a very useful conclusion in the following sections. As previously we consider the Lagrangian
L:TM\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
satisfying conditions
(L0)
,
(L1)
and
(L2)
, and the associated Hamiltonian
H:T^{*}M\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
.

Given two continuous functions
\varphi,\psi:M\to\mathbb{R}
, as done above we define two Hamiltonians

{\bf H}^{\varphi},{\bf H}^{\psi}:T^{*}M\to\mathbb{R}
by

{\bf H}^{\varphi}(x,p)=H(x,p,\varphi(x))~\text{ and }~{\bf H}^{\psi}(x,p)=H(x,p,\psi(x)).

Their associated Lagrangians
{\bf L}^{\varphi},{\bf L}^{\psi}:TM\to\mathbb{R}
are given by

{\bf L}^{\varphi}(x,v)=L(x,v,\varphi(x))~\text{ and }~{\bf L}^{\psi}(x,v)=L(x,v,\psi(x)).

Lemma 4.1
.

Suppose that
u:M\to\mathbb{R}
is a subsolution of

{\bf H}^{\varphi}(x,d_{x}u)=c

and
w:M\to\mathbb{R}
is a solution of

{\bf H}^{\psi}(x,d_{x}w)=c.

Then one of the following two holds:

(1)

The maximum of
u-w
can be attained at a point
x_{\max}\in M
where
\varphi(x_{\max})-\psi(x_{\max})\leq 0
.

(2)

We can find a Lipschitz curve
\gamma:]-\infty,0]\to M
which is both
u
-calibrated for

{\bf L}^{\varphi}+c
and
w
-calibrated for
{\bf L}^{\psi}+c
, such that for all
t\in[0,+\infty[
,

\displaystyle u(\gamma(-t))-w(\gamma(-t))=\max(u-w),\qquad\varphi(\gamma(-t))-\psi(\gamma(-t))>0.

Moreover, if (1) does not hold, denoting by
K<+\infty
a Lipschitz constant
for the Lipschitz curve
\gamma:]-\infty,0]\to M
obtained in (2),
we can find
a
closed
measure
\tilde{\mu}
on
TM
, whose support,
\operatorname{supp}\tilde{\mu}
, is contained in the compact subset
\{(x,v)\in TM\mid\lVert v\rVert_{x}\leq K\}
,
such that

\int_{TM}\big[L(x,v,\varphi(x))+c\big]\,{\rm d}\tilde{\mu}(x,v)=\int_{TM}\big[L(x,v,\psi(x))+c\big]\,{\rm d}\tilde{\mu}(x,v)=0,

and for all
(x,v)\in\operatorname{supp}\tilde{\mu}
,

u(x)-w(x)=\max_{M}(u-w),\ \ \varphi(x)-\psi(x)>0.

Proof.

Pick
x_{0}\in M
such that

u(x_{0})-w(x_{0})=\max_{M}(u-w)
. If
\varphi(x_{0})-\psi(x_{0})\leq 0
, then alternative (1) of the lemma holds.

Suppose now that
\varphi(x_{0})-\psi(x_{0})>0
. Since
w
is a solution of

{\bf H}^{\psi}(x,d_{x}w)=c
,
by Theorem
2.4
and Lemma
2.6
, we can find a Lipschitz (with Lipschitz constant
K<\infty
) curve
\gamma:\,]-\infty,0]\to M
, with

\gamma(0)=x_{0}
, which is
w
-calibrated for
{\bf L}^{\psi}
. In particular,

w(x_{0})-w(\gamma(-t))=\int_{-t}^{0}L(\gamma(s),\dot{\gamma}(s),\psi(\gamma(s)))+c\,{\rm d}s,\text{ for every $t\geq 0$.}

(4.1)

Since
\varphi(x_{0})-\psi(x_{0})>0
, we can define
t_{0}\in[0,+\infty]
by

t_{0}=\sup\{t\geq 0\mid\varphi(\gamma(-s))-\psi(\gamma(-s))>0,\text{ for all $s\in[0,t]$}\}.

(4.2)

By continuity of
s\mapsto\varphi(\gamma(-s))-\psi(\gamma(-s))
,
we have
t_{0}>0
and
(\varphi-\psi)(\gamma(-t_{0}))=0
if

is finite.

Claim:
We have

u(x_{0})-w(x_{0})=u(\gamma(-t))-w(\gamma(-t)),\text{ for all $t\in[0,t_{0}[$},

(4.3)

and
\gamma:[0,t_{0}[\to M
is not only

w
-calibrated for
{\bf L}^{\psi}
but also
u
-calibrated for
{\bf L}^{\varphi}
.
Moreover

L\bigl(\gamma(-t),\dot{\gamma}(-t),\varphi(\gamma(-t))\bigr)=L\bigl(\gamma(-t),\dot{\gamma}(-t),\psi(\gamma(-t))\bigr),\text{ for almost all $t\in[0,t_{0}[$}.

(4.4)

We now prove the Claim.
In fact by condition (L0), since
\varphi(\gamma(-s))-\psi(\gamma(-s))>0
, for all
s\in[0,t_{0}[
, we obtain

which finally finishes establishing the Claim.

\bullet
If

, when alternative (1) of the lemma does not hold. In fact, when alternative (2) holds, we have

(\varphi-\psi)(\gamma(-t))>0,\text{ for all $t\in[0,+\infty[$,}

(4.7)

and by (

), we obtain

u(x_{0})-w(x_{0})=u(\gamma(-t))-w(\gamma(-t)),\text{ for all $t\in[0,+\infty[$},

5

’s are equibounded

Even though the Hamilton–Jacobi equation (
HJλ
),
\lambda>0
, may not have the uniqueness property of solutions under conditions
(L0)
–
(L4)
, we can still show that all possible solutions are uniformly bounded from below and above.

Theorem 5.1
.

Suppose that the Lagrangian
L:TM\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
satisfies conditions
(L0)
,
(L1)
,
(L2)
,
(L3)
and
(L4)
.
If
\hat{u}:M\to\mathbb{R}
is a global viscosity solution of

H^{0}(x,d_{x}\hat{u})=c(H^{0}).

Then for every global continuous viscosity solution
u_{\lambda}
of (
HJλ
), with
\lambda>0
, we have

\hat{u}-\max_{M}\hat{u}\leq u_{\lambda}\leq\hat{u}-\min_{M}\hat{u}.

In particular, the solutions of (
HJλ
), for all
\lambda>0
, are equibounded.

We split the proof of Theorem
5.1
in two lemmas, one where we show the upper bound and the other one where we show the lower bound. The key of the proof is to make use of Lemma
4.1
.

Lemma 5.2
.

For every global continuous viscosity solution
u_{\lambda}

of (
HJλ
), with
\lambda>0
, the function
u_{\lambda}-\hat{u}
achieves its maximum at a point

x_{\max}\in M
where
u_{\lambda}\leq 0
.

Therefore,
u_{\lambda}\leq\hat{u}-\min_{M}\hat{u}
for every global continuous viscosity solution
u_{\lambda}
of (
HJλ
), with
\lambda>0
.

Proof.

The last part of the lemma follows from the first part, since

u_{\lambda}-\hat{u}\leq\max_{M}(u_{\lambda}-\hat{u})=u_{\lambda}(x_{\max})-\hat{u}(x_{\max})\leq-\hat{u}(x_{\max})\leq-\min_{M}\hat{u}.

Let us now prove the first part. Note that the functions
u_{\lambda}
and
\hat{u}
are respectively viscosity solutions of

{\bf H}^{\lambda u_{\lambda}}(x,d_{x}w)=c(H^{0})
and of

{\bf H}^{0}(x,d_{x}w)=c(H^{0})
.
Therefore, we can apply Lemma
4.1
to conclude the following dichotomy:

(I)

Either the maximum of
u_{\lambda}-\hat{u}
can be achieved at a point where
\lambda u_{\lambda}-0
is
\leq 0
.

(II)

Or we can find a closed measure
\tilde{\mu}
with compact support on

TM
such that

u_{\lambda}(x)-\hat{u}(x)=\max_{M}(u_{\lambda}-\hat{u})~\text{ and }~\lambda u_{\lambda}(x)>0,\text{ for all
$(x,v)\in\operatorname{supp}{\tilde{\mu}}$,}

and

\int_{TM}\big[L(x,v,\lambda u_{\lambda}(x))+c(H^{0})\big]\,{\rm d}\tilde{\mu}=\int_{TM}\big[L(x,v,0)+c(H^{0})\big]\,{\rm d}\tilde{\mu}=0.

(5.1)

Case (I) implies right away the lemma.

If Case (II) happens, to finish the proof of the lemma, it suffices to show that the existence of this measure
\tilde{\mu}
leads to a contradiction.

Note that the second equality in (
5.1
) implies that
\tilde{\mu}
is a Mather measure for
L^{0}
. Moreover, since
\lambda u_{\lambda}(x)>0
for all
(x,v)\in\operatorname{supp}{\tilde{\mu}}
, condition
(L0)
implies that

L(x,v,0)\leq L(x,v,t)\leq L(x,v,\lambda u_{\lambda}(x)),\text{ for all $(x,v,t)$ with $(x,v)\in\operatorname{supp}{\tilde{\mu}}$ and $t\in[0,\lambda u_{\lambda}(x)]$}.

This together with the first equality in (
5.1
) implies that

L(x,v,0)=L(x,v,t)=L(x,v,\lambda u_{\lambda}(x)),\text{ for all $(x,v,t)$ with
$(x,v)\in\operatorname{supp}{\tilde{\mu}}$ and $t\in[0,\lambda u_{\lambda}(x)]$}.

Since the partial derivative
\partial L/\partial u(x,v,0)
exists (by condition
(L3)
), using again that
\lambda u_{\lambda}(x)>0
for all

(x,v)\in\operatorname{supp}{\tilde{\mu}}
, we conclude that

\frac{\partial L}{\partial u}(x,v,0)=0,\text{ for all
$(x,v)\in\operatorname{supp}{\tilde{\mu}}$},

which in turn implies

\int_{TM}\frac{\partial L}{\partial u}(x,v,0)\,{\rm d}\tilde{\mu}=0.

This contradicts condition
(L4)
, since we have observed above that
\tilde{\mu}
is a Mather measure for
L^{0}
.
∎

Lemma 5.3
.

For every continuous viscosity solution

The proof is very similar to that of Lemma

and

10
A counterexample

We conclude with a simple example showing the necessity of the non–degeneracy hypothesis
(L4)
.

Let us start with a
1
-periodic Hamiltonian of the pendulum on
\mathbb{R}
:

H(x,p)=\frac{1}{2}|p|^{2}+\cos(2\pi x).

Its critical value is
c(H)=1
(whether we see it as a
1
-periodic function a
2
-periodic function or a
4
-periodic function).

Let
\alpha:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
be a non-negative continuous function that is supported on
[1/2,3/2]
and positive on
]1/2,3/2[
. For each integer
n\geq 2
, we will denote by
\alpha_{n}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
the
n
-periodic function that coincides with
\alpha
on
[0,n]
. We will denote by
H_{n}(x,p,u)=H(x,p)+\alpha_{n}(x)u
.

If
\lambda>0
, let
u_{\lambda}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
be a
2
-periodic function solution of
H_{2}(x,d_{x}u_{\lambda},\lambda u_{\lambda}(x))=1
. Such a function exists by Perron’s method, with arguments already presented before. Such a function is differentiable at
x=2
with
u_{\lambda}^{\prime}(x)=0
.

We consider the equation
H(x,u^{\prime}(x))=1
, and pick two
2
-periodic solutions of this equation. The first one is actually the only
1
-periodic solution up to constants, and we only write it on
[0,1]
:

v^{1}(x)=\begin{cases}\int_{0}^{x}\sqrt{2-2\cos(2\pi s)}\,{\rm d}s,&x\in[0,1/2],\\
\int_{1}^{x}-\sqrt{2-2\cos(2\pi s)}\,{\rm d}s,&x\in[1/2,1];\end{cases}

so
v^{1}(0)=v^{1}(1)=v^{1}(2)=0
.
The second one is
C^{1}
and
2
-periodic, and we write it on
[0,2]
:

v^{2}(x)=\begin{cases}\int_{0}^{x}\sqrt{2-2\cos(2\pi s)}\,{\rm d}s,&x\in[0,1],\\
\int_{2}^{x}-\sqrt{2-2\cos(2\pi s)}\,{\rm d}s,&x\in[1,2];\end{cases}

so
v^{2}(0)=v^{2}(2)=0
.

We now construct
4
-periodic functions (we give the definition on
[0,4]
) that are solutions of
H_{4}(x,v^{\prime}(x),\lambda v(x))=1
on
M=\mathbb{R}/4\mathbb{Z}
, by gluing a piece of
u_{\lambda}
with
v^{1}
or
v^{2}
. More precisely, for
\lambda>0
, we define

v^{1}_{\lambda}(x)=\begin{cases}u_{\lambda}(x),&x\in[0,2],\\
v^{1}(x)+u_{\lambda}(2),&x\in[2,4];\end{cases}\quad\qquad v^{2}_{\lambda}(x)=\begin{cases}u_{\lambda}(x),&x\in[0,2],\\
v^{2}(x)+u_{\lambda}(2),&x\in[2,4].\end{cases}

Note that the gluing are differentiable at junction points.

With these constructions, it is obvious that solutions of
H_{4}(x,v^{\prime}(x),\lambda v(x))=1
do not necessarily converge as
\lambda\to 0
. Note that the Mather measures of
H_{4}^{0}
are convex combinations of the Dirac measures
\delta_{(0,0)}
,
\delta_{(1,0)}
,
\delta_{(2,0)}
and
\delta_{(3,0)}
supported on hyperbolic equilibria. However, the non–degeneracy condition
(L4)
is only verified for the measure
\delta_{(1,0)}
.

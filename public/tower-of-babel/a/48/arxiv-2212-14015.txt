Dupin Cyclides as a Subspace of Darboux Cyclides
Jean Michel Menjanahary and Raimundas Vidunas

Abstract
Dupin cyclides are interesting algebraic surfaces used in geometric design and architecture to join canal surfaces smoothly and to construct model surfaces. Dupin cyclides are special cases of Darboux cyclides, which in turn are rather general surfaces in ℝ 3 \mathbb{R}^{3} of degree 3 or 4. This article derives the algebraic conditions for recognition of Dupin cyclides among the general implicit form of Darboux cyclides. We aim at practicable sets of algebraic equations on the coefficients of the implicit equation, each such set defining a complete intersection (of codimension 4) locally. Additionally, the article classifies all real surfaces and lower dimensional degenerations defined by the implicit equation for Dupin cyclides.



Keywords: Dupin cyclides, Darboux cyclides, canal surfaces, geometric design, architecture.

6
Classification of real cases of Dupin cyclides

The torus equation (
2.15
) is defined over
\mathbb{R}
also when
r^{2}<0
or
R^{2}<0
.
Similarly, the canonical equation (
1.2
) is defined over
\mathbb{R}
if
\alpha,\beta,\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
,
or if exactly two of these numbers are on the imaginary line
\sqrt{-1}\,\mathbb{R}\subset\mathbb{C}
.
Then we may obtain degenerations to surfaces with a few (if any) real points.

Section
6.3
classifies all degenerations of Dupin cyclides.
For that purpose, Section
6.1
defines general Möbius isomorphisms between Dupin cyclides and toruses,
and follows the cases when they are defined over
\mathbb{R}
.
Section
6.2
defines a Möbius invariant
J_{0}
of Dupin cyclides.
This invariant and a few semi-algebraic conditions classify the Dupin cyclides up to real Möbius transformations.

6.1
Möbius isomorphisms to the torus

Spherical inversions or Möbius transformations between Dupin cyclides and a general torus
have been constructed geometrically
[
16
, Theorem 3.5]
and computed
[
23
, §2.3]
.
An explicit Möbius isomorphism that maps a canonical Dupin cyclide (
1.2
)
to the torus equation (
2.15
) is given by

\displaystyle(x,y,z)\mapsto

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha\delta+\beta\varepsilon}{\gamma}\,(1,0,0)+\frac{2\beta\varepsilon}{(x-\gamma)^{2}+y^{2}+z^{2}}\,(x-\gamma,y,z),

(6.1)

where
\beta=\sqrt{\alpha^{2}-\gamma^{2}}
,
\varepsilon=\sqrt{\delta^{2}-\gamma^{2}}
.
This Möbius transformation is defined over
\mathbb{R}
if and only if
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}
and
\beta\varepsilon\in\mathbb{R}
.
If
\gamma=0
, the canonical equation (
1.2
) coincides already with the torus equation (
2.15
)
with
\alpha=R
,
\delta=r
. With
\beta\varepsilon\in\mathbb{R}
, the minor and major radiuses of the torus are given by

r=\frac{\gamma^{2}\varepsilon}{\alpha\varepsilon+\beta\delta},\qquad R=\frac{\gamma^{2}\beta}{\alpha\varepsilon+\beta\delta},

(6.2)

We can further apply scaling by the factor

\frac{\gamma^{2}}{\alpha\varepsilon+\beta\delta}

(6.3)

to the immediate torus equation
if either all
\alpha,\delta,\beta,\varepsilon\in\mathbb{R}
or all
\alpha,\delta,\beta,\varepsilon\in\sqrt{-1}\,\mathbb{R}
.
The resulting torus equation has
r=\varepsilon
,
R=\beta
.
Otherwise the scaling can be adjusted by the factor
\sqrt{-1}
,
and the radiuses become
r^{2}=\gamma^{2}-\delta^{2}
,
R^{2}=\gamma^{2}-\alpha^{2}
.

On the other hand, the canonical equation (
1.2
)
is symmetrical
[
19
, (1)-(2)]

with respect to the simultaneous interchange
y\leftrightarrow z
,
\alpha\leftrightarrow\gamma
.
This symmetry implies a Möbius equivalence to the torus with the minor radius
\sqrt{R^{2}-r^{2}}

(and the same major radius
R
) as well.
Up to scaling, this Möbius equivalence is symmetric to (
6.1
):

\displaystyle(x,y,z)\mapsto

\displaystyle\;\frac{\gamma\delta+\sqrt{(\gamma^{2}-\alpha^{2})(\delta^{2}-\alpha^{2})}}{\alpha}\,(1,0,0)

\displaystyle\;+\frac{2\sqrt{(\gamma^{2}-\alpha^{2})(\delta^{2}-\alpha^{2})}}{(x-\alpha)^{2}+y^{2}+z^{2}}\,(x-\alpha,z,y),

(6.4)

Both Möbius isomorphisms are defined over
\mathbb{R}
if
\alpha\gamma\neq 0
and

\delta^{2}
is between
\alpha^{2}
and
\gamma^{2}
on the real line.
This means that
0<r/R<1
, because (
6.2
) implies

\frac{r}{R}=\sqrt{\frac{\delta^{2}-\gamma^{2}}{\alpha^{2}-\gamma^{2}}}.

(6.5)

A composition of the two Möbius isomorphisms relates two toruses with the minor radiuses
r
and
\sqrt{R^{2}-r^{2}}
, when
r<R
.
This transformation is obtained explicitly by applying (
6.1
) with

\alpha=R
,
\gamma=0
,
\delta=r
. After additional scaling of
(x,y,z)
by
r/R
we obtain
the Möbius transformation

\displaystyle(x,y,z)\mapsto

\displaystyle\big(\sqrt{R^{2}-r^{2}},0,0\big)+\frac{2r\,\sqrt{R^{2}-r^{2}}}{(x-r)^{2}+y^{2}+z^{2}}\,(x-r,z,y),

(6.6)

that brings torus (
2.15
) to the torus

(x^{2}+y^{2}+z^{2}+r^{2})^{2}-4R^{2}(x^{2}+y^{2})=0.

(6.7)

When

, this Möbius duality is not defined over
\mathbb{R}
. If

, then (

[

and the surface (

If

horn

while (

6.2
The toroidic invariant for
r/R

Any torus (
2.15
) has two clear families of circles on it, namely on the vertical planes
ax+by=0

or horizontal planes
z=c
. These circles are the principal curvature lines on the torus, and are known as

principal circles
. Less known are two families of
Villarceau circles

[
24
]

on the bitangent planes
z=ax+by
with
a^{2}+b^{2}=r^{2}/(R^{2}-r^{2})
, on smooth toruses with
r<R
.
The angles
\theta
,
\frac{\pi}{2}-\theta
between principal and Villarceau circles depend only on the families of the involved circles.
The sine (or the complementary cosine) of
\theta
equals
[
24
]
to the quotient
r/R
.
The duality (
6.6
) of toruses with the minor radiuses
r
and
\sqrt{R^{2}-r^{2}}

underscores constancy of the angle pair
\theta
,
\frac{\pi}{2}-\theta

under the (conformal!) Möbius transformations.
Krasauskas noted to us that the numbers

r^{2}/R^{2}
and
1-r^{2}/R^{2}
are equal to the two possible cross-ratios
within the quaternionic representation
[
28
]
of Dupin cyclides.

The symmetry between
r/R
and
\sqrt{1-r^{2}/R^{2}}
leads to this invariant under the Möbius transformations:

J_{0}=\frac{r^{2}}{R^{2}}\left(1-\frac{r^{2}}{R^{2}}\right).

(6.8)

We define the invariant
J_{0}
for general Dupin cyclides by the Möbius equivalence.
The maximal value
J_{0}=1/4
gives the “most round" cyclides (with
R=\sqrt{2}\,r
)
that optimize the Willmore energy
[
25
]

\int\!\!\int_{S\,}H^{2}dA

(6.9)

for the smooth real surfaces
S
with the torus topology. The integrand
H^{2}
is the mean curvature
H
squared,
and
dA
is the infinitesimal area element.
The Willmore energy is conformally invariant,
and it equals
[
26
, pg 275]

\frac{\pi^{2}R^{2}}{r\,\sqrt{R^{2}-r^{2}}}=\frac{\pi^{2}}{\sqrt{J_{0}}}

(6.10)

for a smooth torus (
2.15
).
The duality breaks down for
J_{0}\leqslant 0
, as Möbius transformation (
6.6
) is then not defined over
\mathbb{R}
.
The singular horn torus (with
r=R
) is paired to the degeneration to a circle (
r=0
),
and the spindle toruses (with
r>R
) are paired with algebraic surfaces with no real points (
r\in\sqrt{-1}\,\mathbb{R}
).

We apply our results to compute the invariant
J_{0}
for all Dupin cyclides.
In the case of a canonical quartic cyclide (
3.1
),
we choose Möbius equivalence (
6.1
) with a torus,
and obtain

\displaystyle\frac{r^{2}}{R^{2}}=\frac{\delta^{2}-\gamma^{2}}{\alpha^{2}-\gamma^{2}}=\frac{2A_{2}+A_{3}-A_{1}}{A_{2}-A_{3}}

(6.11)

due to (

) and (

\displaystyle J_{0}

\displaystyle=-\frac{(\delta^{2}-\gamma^{2})(\delta^{2}-\alpha^{2})}{(\alpha^{2}-\gamma^{2})^{2}}

(6.12)

\displaystyle=\frac{(A_{1}-2A_{2}-A_{3})(A_{2}+2A_{3}-A_{1})}{(A_{2}-A_{3})^{2}}.

(6.13)

To obtain expressions of
J_{0}
for the general quartic cyclide (
2.1
),
we first eliminate
A_{2},A_{3}
as in Lemma
3.5
. The result is

\displaystyle J_{0}=\frac{7A_{1}^{2}-8C_{0}A_{1}+2C_{0}^{2}+W_{1}}{3A_{1}^{2}-2C_{0}A_{1}-C_{0}^{2}+4W_{1}}.

(6.14)

After the elimination of
A_{1}

A Ramsey theorem for the reals
Tanmay C. Inamdar

Abstract
. We prove that for every colouring of pairs of reals with finitely-many colours, there is a set homeomorphic to the rationals which takes no more than two colours. This was conjectured by Galvin in 1970, and a colouring of Sierpiński from 1933 witnesses that the number of colours cannot be reduced to one. Previously in 1985 Shelah had shown that a stronger statement is consistent with a forcing construction assuming the existence of large cardinals. Then in 2018 Raghavan and Todorčević had proved it assuming the existence of large cardinals. We prove it in ZFC . In fact Raghavan and Todorčević proved, assuming more large cardinals, a similar result for a large class of topological spaces. We prove this also, again in ZFC .

Before we state Theorem D, let us recall the definitions. The first is standard, the second

Definition 1.1

Let

(i)

Recall also the Urysohn Metrisation Theorem which states that any second countable regular Hausdorff space is metrisable.

Finally we justify some statements from the introduction. Let

be uncountable subset of the reals, then there is

is dense in itself. Indeed, let

denote the set of all rational intervals

We also mentioned that any

which is homeomorphic to

contains a set order-isomorphic to

, so let us quickly check the details. First we define an equivalence relation

on

. Then each equivalence class has size at most

. So let

must be countably infinite too. Then for any

from

For the rest of this section, fix

and functions

When

.

is the least ordinal

such that for every

It should be clear that for

-complete ideals

on

and

,

\rk_{\mathcal{I}}(f)\leq\rk_{\mathcal{J}}(f).

In particular, for

both elements of
\mathcal{I}^{+}
for
\mathcal{I}
an
\aleph_{1}
-complete ideal on
\pom(H)
, as
\mathcal{I}+B\subseteq\mathcal{I}+A
,

\rk_{\mathcal{I}+B}(f)\leq\rk_{\mathcal{I}+A}(f).

For the rest of this section, fix
\mathcal{I}
an
\aleph_{1}
-complete ideal on

and

Definition 3.6

.

Lemma 3.7

Let

and let
\mathcal{I}

(i)

(ii)

Proof.

It is clear that
\mathbf{I}[f,\mathcal{I}]\supseteq\mathcal{I}

\pom(H)\in\mathcal{I}^{*}

\mathcal{I}+\pom(H)=\mathcal{I}

\pom(H)\notin\mathbf{I}[f,\mathcal{I}]

For
A\subseteq B
both in
\mathcal{I}^{+}

\rk_{\mathcal{I}+B}(f)\leq\rk_{\mathcal{I}+A}(f)

\mathbf{I}[f,\mathcal{I}]

For

and

Given a sequence
\langle A_{n}\mid n<\omega\rangle

and some others not, but combined with the previous observations and the fact that

Claim 3.7.1
.

Let
\langle B_{n}\mid n<\omega\rangle

(i)

It is clear that (i) implies (ii). We will prove (i) by induction on

\zeta

\mathcal{B}_{n+1}:=\{\sigma_{m}\mid m\leq n+1\}.

Now note that by the nature of the enumeration
\vec{\sigma}
, for every
\rho\in{}^{<\omega}\omega
such that
\rho\sqsubset\sigma_{n+1}
, we have that
\rho\in\mathcal{B}_{n}
. So it follows that
\sigma_{n+1}\in\mathcal{L}_{n}
. Then by the nature of
\mathcal{L}_{n}
, for every
\rho\in\mathcal{L}_{n}
,
\rho\not\sqsubset\sigma_{n+1}
and
\sigma_{n+1}\not\sqsubset\rho
. So, taking

\mathcal{L}_{n+1}:=(\mathcal{L}_{n}\cup\{\sigma_{n+1}{}^{\smallfrown}\langle k\rangle\mid k<\omega\})\setminus\{\sigma_{n+1}\},

we see that the tree requirement at stage
n+1
is taken care of.

In order to ensure continuity between the stages of the recursion, we will ensure that
y_{\sigma_{n+1}}=F(M)
for some
M\in h_{n}(\sigma_{n+1})
and also, for
\rho\in\mathcal{L}_{n}\setminus\{\sigma_{n+1}\}
,
h_{n+1}(\rho)\subseteq h_{n}(\rho)
. We will refer to this as the
continuity promise
.
This combined with the convergence promise at stage
n
will immediately ensure that the convergence requirement at stage
n+1
is met.

Let
B:=h_{n}(\sigma_{n+1})
, and for
\rho\in\mathcal{L}_{n}\setminus\{\sigma_{n+1}\}
, let
D_{\rho}:=h_{n}(\rho)
.
Suppose that
\rho\in\mathcal{L}_{n}\setminus\{\sigma_{n+1}\}
is such that
\sigma_{n+1}<_{\mathrm{lex}}\rho
. The first Ramsey inductive hypothesis at stage
n
ensures that
(D_{\rho},B)
is
(i,j)
-saturated over
\mathcal{I}
. By Lemma
5.3
(i), we have that

\forall^{\mathcal{I}}M\in B\exists^{\mathcal{I}}N\in D_{\rho}\,[c(F(M),F(N))=j].

Similarly, for
\rho\in\mathcal{L}_{n}\setminus\{\sigma_{n+1}\}
such that
\rho<_{\mathrm{lex}}\sigma_{n+1}
, the first Ramsey inductive hypothesis at stage
n
ensures that
(B,D_{\rho})
is
(i,j)
-saturated over
\mathcal{I}
. By Lemma
5.3
(i), we have that

\forall^{\mathcal{I}}M\in B\exists^{\mathcal{I}}N\in D_{\rho}\,[c(F(M),F(N))=i].

So, using the fact that
\mathcal{I}
is
\aleph_{1}
-complete, let
C\subseteq B
be an element of
\mathcal{I}^{+}
such that for every
M\in C
,

•

if
\rho\in\mathcal{L}_{n}\setminus\{\sigma_{n+1}\}
is such that
\sigma_{n+1}<_{\mathrm{lex}}\rho
, then

\exists^{\mathcal{I}}N\in D_{\rho}\,[c(F(M),F(N))=j],

•

and if
\rho\in\mathcal{L}_{n}\setminus\{\sigma_{n+1}\}
is such that
\rho<_{\mathrm{lex}}\sigma_{n+1}
, then

\exists^{\mathcal{I}}N\in D_{\rho}\,[c(F(M),F(N))=i].

Now,
C\subseteq B\subseteq A
and
C\in\mathcal{I}^{+}
, so by the hypotheses we have that there is an
(i,j)
-winner below
C
over
\mathcal{I}
. So, fix
M\in C
such that
F(M)
is such an
(i,j)
-winner below
C
over
\mathcal{I}
and then let
y_{\sigma_{n+1}}:=F(M)
, and so by our notation, also
y_{n+1}:=F(M)
.

It should be clear from the injectivity promise at stage
n
that the sequence
\langle y_{m}\mid m\leq n+1\rangle
is injective, so the injectivity requirement at stage
n+1
is taken care of. The second, third, and fourth Ramsey inductive hypotheses at stage
n
ensure that the Ramsey requirement is met at stage
n+1
. So we have taken care of the requirements (i)-(iv).

Since
y_{n+1}
is an
(i,j)
-winner below
C
over
\mathcal{I}
, fix a sequence
\langle T_{k}\mid k<\omega\rangle
such that

•

for every
k<\omega
,
T_{k}\subseteq C
and

;

•

for every

and

;

•

for every

(i,j)
-saturated over
\mathcal{I}
;

•

for every
k<\omega
, for every

Now to define

. First,
h_{n+1}\restriction\{\sigma_{n+1}{}^{\smallfrown}\langle k\rangle\mid k<\omega\}
is just the function
\sigma_{n+1}{}^{\smallfrown}\langle k\rangle\mapsto T_{k}
. At this point note that for every
k<\omega

This, the continuity promise, and the choice of

and the first Ramsey inductive hypothesis up to stage

n+1

It should also be clear that the convergence promise has been taken care of by the continuity promise and the fact that

(i,j)
-winner below

over

Lastly, to describe
h_{n+1}\restriction(\mathcal{L}_{n}\setminus\{\sigma_{n+1}\})

•

•

Now for the third and fourth Ramsey inductive hypotheses. It should be clear from the construction that we only need to verify them for

6.
A decisive pair

The main result of this section is Lemma
6.7
which provides us with the decisive starting point for the main lemma, Lemma

, in Section
7
. The exhaustion argument in Lemma

is similar to one in Raghavan and Todorčević
[
19
]

. The notion of unsaturation (see Definition
6.1
) plays a more prominent role here than in
[
19
]
for this reason.

Let us recall our collection of fixed objects:

•

(X,\tau)
is a regular topological space which is not left-separated and has a point-countable basis
\mathcal{B}
.

•

K
is a positive natural number and

is a colouring of pairs from
X
.

•

\Omega
is an uncountable regular cardinal such that
X,\tau,\mathcal{B}\in H(\Omega)
.

•

\langle U_{x,k}\mid x\in X,k<\omega\rangle
is such that for every
x\in X
,
\langle U_{x,k}\mid k<\omega\rangle
enumerates
\mathcal{B}_{\{x\}}
.

•

\Lambda_{1}\subseteq\pom(H(\Omega))
is a fixed stationary subset of
\pom(H(\Omega))
such that for every
M\in\Lambda_{1}
,
M\prec H(\Omega)
and
X,\tau,\mathcal{B}\in M
, and
\overline{M\cap X}\setminus M\neq\emptyset
.

•

F:\Lambda_{1}\rightarrow X
is such that for every

,

.

•

\mathbb{I}
is the collection of fine normal ideals on

.

Recall that saturation and weak saturation were defined in Definition
5.2
. The following is related to these.

Definition 6.1
.

Let
(A,B,\mathcal{I})\in\mathcal{C}_{2}
and
F\subseteq K^{2}
. We say that
(A,B)
is
F
-unsaturated over
\mathcal{I}
if for every
(i,j)\in F
,
(A,B)
is not weakly
(i,j)
-saturated over
\mathcal{I}
.

For the next lemma we again remind the reader of our convention that if

is defined, that is, has a specific value, then we implicitly assume that also

.

Lemma 6.2
.

For any
(A,B,\mathcal{I})\in\mathcal{C}_{2}
, for some
(i,j)\in K^{2}
we have that
(A,B)
is weakly
(i,j)
-saturated over
\mathcal{I}
.

Proof.

Fix for the moment an
M\in A
, and let
x:=F(M)
. By Lemma
4.5
,
\{N\in B\mid F(N)=x\}\in\mathcal{I}
. So
C\in\mathcal{I}^{+}
where

C:=\{N\in B\mid F(N)\neq x\}.

So for some
i_{M}<K
we have that

\{N\in C\mid c(x,F(N))=i_{M}\}\in\mathcal{I}^{+}.

To summarise, for every
M\in A
, for some
i_{M}<K
we have that

\{N\in B\mid F(M)\neq F(N)\ \&\ c(F(M),F(N))=i_{M}\}\in\mathcal{I}^{+}.

So stabilising on

we obtain an
i<K
such that

\{M\in A\mid i_{M}=i\}\in\mathcal{I}^{+}.

So,

\exists^{\mathcal{I}}M\in A\exists^{\mathcal{I}}N\in B\,[c(F(M),F(N))=i].

Similarly we can find a
j<K
such that

\exists^{\mathcal{I}}N\in B\exists^{\mathcal{I}}M\in A\,[c(F(M),F(N))=j],

and conclude the proof.
∎

Lemma 6.3
.

Let
(C,D,\mathcal{J})\leq_{2}(A,B,\mathcal{I})
be elements of
\mathcal{C}_{2}
. Let
(i,j)\in K^{2}
.

If
(A,B)
is
\{(i,j)\}
-unsaturated over
\mathcal{I}
, then also
(C,D)
is
\{(i,j)\}
-unsaturated over
\mathcal{J}
.

Proof.

As
\mathcal{I}\subseteq\mathcal{J}
, we have that
\mathcal{I}^{+}\supseteq\mathcal{J}^{+}
. Suppose that
(A,B)
is not weakly
(i,j)
-saturated over
\mathcal{I}
. There are two possibilities for why. The first is that

\{M\in A\mid\exists^{\mathcal{I}}N\in B\,[c(F(M),F(N))=i]\}\in\mathcal{I},

in which case as
C\subseteq A
and
D\subseteq B
and
\mathcal{J}^{+}\subseteq\mathcal{I}^{+}
, also

\{M\in C\mid\exists^{\mathcal{J}}N\in D\,[c(F(M),F(N))=i]\}\in\mathcal{J},

(C,D)
is not weakly
(i,j)
-saturated over
\mathcal{J}

The other possibility is that

\{N\in D\mid\exists^{\mathcal{J}}M\in C\,[c(F(M),F(N))=j]\}\in\mathcal{J},

(C,D)
is not weakly
(i,j)
-saturated over
\mathcal{J}
.
∎

Lemma 6.5
.

Let
\mathcal{I}\subseteq\mathcal{J}

be such that

is

-unsaturated over

.

Then, there is
(S,T,\mathcal{J})\leq_{2}(C,D,\mathcal{J})

is

-unsaturated over
\mathcal{I}
.

Proof.

Note that as
\mathcal{I}\subseteq\mathcal{J}
, we have that
\mathcal{J}^{+}\subseteq\mathcal{I}^{+}
. We will prove the lemma by induction on the size of

(C,D)
is not weakly
(i,j)
-saturated over
\mathcal{J}

\neg(\exists^{\mathcal{J}}M\in C\exists^{\mathcal{J}}N\in D\,[c(F(M),F(N))=i]).

\{M\in C\mid\{N\in D\mid c(F(M),F(N))=i\}\in\mathcal{J}^{+}\}.

So,

and hence also

Now for every

,

\forall^{\mathcal{I}}N\in T\,[c(F(M),F(N))\neq i]

(S,T,\mathcal{J})\leq_{2}(C,D,\mathcal{J})

(S,T)

(i,j)
-saturated over
\mathcal{I}
. This completes the base step.

Now, for the inductive step. Suppose that
|F|>1
and
(i,j)\in F
. First appealing to the inductive hypotheses, we obtain
(S^{\prime},T^{\prime},\mathcal{J})\leq_{2}(C,D,\mathcal{J})
such that
(S^{\prime},T^{\prime})
is
(F\setminus\{(i,j)\})
-unsaturated over
\mathcal{I}
. By Lemma

we have that
(S^{\prime},T^{\prime})
is not weakly
(i,j)
-saturated over
\mathcal{J}
. Then we can appeal to the base case applied to
(S^{\prime},T^{\prime},\mathcal{J})
to obtain
(S,T,\mathcal{J})\leq_{2}(S^{\prime},T^{\prime},\mathcal{J})
and
(S,T)
is not weakly
(i,j)
-saturated over
\mathcal{I}
, and hence
(S,T)
is
F
-unsaturated over
\mathcal{I}
by Lemma
6.3
.
∎

Remark 6.6
.

The above lemma shows that for
\mathcal{I}\subseteq\mathcal{J}
ideals in
\mathbb{I}
and

and
(i,j)\in K^{2}
, if
(A,B)
is
(i,j)
-saturated over
\mathcal{I}
then also
(A,B)
is
(i,j)
-saturated over
\mathcal{J}
.

Lemma 6.7
.

There is a pair
(A,\mathcal{I})\in\mathcal{C}_{1}
and
(i,j)\in K^{2}
such that

•

for every
\mathcal{J}\in\mathbb{I}
with
\mathcal{I}\subseteq\mathcal{J}
,

•

for every
B\subseteq A
with
B\in\mathcal{J}^{+}
,

there are
S,T\subseteq B
with
S,T\in\mathcal{J}^{+}
such that
(S,T)
is
(i,j)
-saturated over
\mathcal{I}
.

Proof.

Let
A
,
\mathcal{I}
,
F
, and
(i,j)
be as in Lemma
6.4
. Then for every
(B,\mathcal{J})\leq_{1}(A,\mathcal{I})
, for some
(C,D,\mathcal{J}_{1})\leq_{2}(B,B,\mathcal{J})
, we have that
(C,D)
is
F
-unsaturated over
\mathcal{J}_{1}
. By Lemma

, we can find

7.
The main lemma

We prove now our main lemma, which as we have mentioned earlier uses an idea we learned from Shelah’s
[
30
]
.

Let us recall again the objects we have fixed:

•

(X,\tau)
is a regular topological space which is not left-separated and has a point-countable basis
\mathcal{B}
.

•

K
is a positive natural number and
c:[X]^{2}\rightarrow K
is a colouring of pairs from
X
.

•

\Omega
is an uncountable regular cardinal such that
X,\tau,\mathcal{B}\in H(\Omega)
.

•

\langle U_{x,k}\mid x\in X,k<\omega\rangle
is such that for every
x\in X
,
\langle U_{x,k}\mid k<\omega\rangle
enumerates
\mathcal{B}_{\{x\}}
.

•

\Lambda_{1}
is a fixed stationary subset of
\pom(H(\Omega))
such that for every
M\in\Lambda_{1}
,
M\prec H(\Omega)
and
X,\tau,\mathcal{B}\in M
, and
\overline{M\cap X}\setminus M\neq\emptyset
.

•

F:\Lambda_{1}\rightarrow X
is such that for every
M\in\Lambda_{1}
,
F(M)\in\overline{M\cap X}\setminus M
.

•

\mathbb{I}
is the collection of fine normal ideals on
\pom(H(\Omega))
.

Lemma 7.1
.

There is an
\mathcal{I}\in\mathbb{I}
and an
A\in\mathcal{I}^{+}
such that
(A,\mathcal{I})
is a winning pair for
c
.

Proof.

Using Lemma
6.7
, let
(A,\mathcal{I})\in\mathcal{C}_{1}
and
(i,j)\in K^{2}
be such that

•

for every
\mathcal{J}\in\mathbb{I}
with
\mathcal{I}\subseteq\mathcal{J}
,

•

for every
B\subseteq A
with
B\in\mathcal{J}^{+}
,

there are
S,T\subseteq B
with
S,T\in\mathcal{J}^{+}
such that
(S,T)
is
(i,j)
-saturated over
\mathcal{I}
.

We will prove that
(A,\mathcal{I})
is an
(i,j)
-winning pair. Let us recall from Definition
5.4
what this requires. We need to show that for every
B\in\mathcal{I}^{+}
with
B\subseteq A
, there is an
(i,j)
-winner
x
below
B
over
\mathcal{I}
. This in turn requires us to show that there is an
M\in B
with
F(M)=x
and there is a sequence
\langle T_{k}\mid k<\omega\rangle
such that

•

for every
k<\omega
,
T_{k}\subseteq B
and
T_{k}\in\mathcal{I}^{+}
;

•

for every
N\in\bigcup_{k<\omega}T_{k}
,
F(N)\neq x
and
c(x,F(N))=i
;

•

for every
k<l<\omega
,
(T_{l},T_{k})
is
(i,j)
-saturated over
\mathcal{I}
;

•

for every
k<\omega
, for every
N\in T_{k}
,
F(N)\in U_{x,k}
.

So, fix
B\in\mathcal{I}^{+}
with
B\subseteq A
.
We want to show that there is an
(i,j)
-winner below
B
over
\mathcal{I}
, so suppose towards a contradiction that there is no such element.

Let
\mathcal{T}
be the set of all finite sequences
\sigma
such that for some
l<\omega
,

\sigma=\langle(S_{k},T_{k})\mid k<l\rangle,

and such that

(i)

for all
k<l
,
S_{k},T_{k}\in\mathcal{I}^{+}
with
S_{k}\cup T_{k}\subseteq B
;

(ii)

for all
k<l
,
(S_{k},T_{k})
is
(i,j)
-saturated over
\mathcal{I}
;

(iii)

for all
0<k<l
,
S_{k}\cup T_{k}\subseteq S_{k-1}
;

(iv)

for all
k<l
, there is a
U\in\mathcal{B}
such that for every
M\in S_{k}\cup T_{k}
,
U=U_{F(M),k}
;

(v)

for all
k<l
and every
M\in S_{k}
,
\{N\in T_{k}\mid c(F(M),F(N))=i\}\in\mathcal{I}^{+}
.

Note that
\mathcal{T}
is closed under initial segments, so it is a tree of finite sequences.

For every
M\in B
, let
\mathcal{T}^{M}
consist of those
\sigma\in\mathcal{T}
such that if
\sigma=\langle(S_{k},T_{k})\mid k<l\rangle
for some
l<\omega
, then for every
k<l
,
M\in S_{k}
. Note that
\mathcal{T}^{M}
is also closed under initial segments, so it is a subtree of
\mathcal{T}
. In particular for every
M\in B
, the empty sequence
\langle\rangle
is in
\mathcal{T}^{M}
.

Claim 7.1.1
.

For every
M\in B
,
\mathcal{T}^{M}
is well-founded.

Proof.

Suppose that this is not so, so let
M\in B
be such that we can find an infinite sequence

b=\langle(S_{k},T_{k})\mid k<\omega\rangle

such that all of the initial segments of
b
are in
\mathcal{T}^{M}
.
It follows that
M\in\bigcap_{k<\omega}S_{k}
. Let
x:=F(M)
. Now for every
k<\omega
, let

\widetilde{T}_{k}:=\{N\in T_{k}\mid c(x,F(N))=i\}.

Then for every
k<\omega
we have that
\widetilde{T}_{k}\in\mathcal{I}^{+}
and
\widetilde{T}_{k}\subseteq T_{k}\subseteq B
, and also, for every
N\in\widetilde{T}_{k}
,
x\neq F(N)
and
c(x,F(N))=i
.

Also, for
k<l<\omega
, we have that

\widetilde{T}_{l}\subseteq T_{l}\subseteq S_{l-1}\subseteq S_{k},

and then as
(S_{k},T_{k})
is
(i,j)
-saturated over
\mathcal{I}
, also
(\widetilde{T}_{l},\widetilde{T}_{k})
is
(i,j)
-saturated over
\mathcal{I}
.

Lastly, for
k<\omega
, as
\{M\}\cup\widetilde{T}_{k}\subseteq S_{k}\cup T_{k}
, for every
N\in\widetilde{T}_{k}
we have that
U_{F(N),k}=U_{F(M),k}=U_{x,k}
, and hence also,
F(N)\in U_{x,k}
.

Altogether, we have that
x
is an
(i,j)
-winner below
B
over
\mathcal{I}
as witnessed by
M
and the sequence
\langle\widetilde{T}_{k}\mid k<\omega\rangle
. This is a contradiction.

∎

For every
M\in B
the tree
\mathcal{T}^{M}
is well-founded, so we define a rank
\rk^{M}:\mathcal{T}^{M}\rightarrow\ord
via: for
\sigma\in\mathcal{T}^{M}
,

\rk^{M}(\sigma):=\sup\{\rk^{M}(\sigma^{\prime})+1\mid\sigma^{\prime}\in\mathcal{T}^{M},\,\sigma\sqsubset\sigma^{\prime}\}.

We now get a chance to use a beautiful idea of Shelah
[
30
]
.
Let
\mathbf{P}
be the set of all
creatures

\mathfrak{p}
where a creature is a triple
\mathfrak{p}=(\sigma,Z,f)
such that

•

\sigma\in\mathcal{T}
;

•

Z\in\mathcal{I}^{+}
with
Z\subseteq B
, and more specifically, if
|\sigma|=0
then
Z:=B
, and if
\sigma=\langle(S_{k},T_{k})\mid k<l\rangle
for some
0<l<\omega
, then
Z:=S_{l-1}
;

•

f\in{}^{Z}\ord
is the function
M\mapsto\rk^{M}(\sigma)
, which is legitimate as
\sigma\in\mathcal{T}^{M}
for every
M\in Z
.

Note that
\mathbf{P}
is non-empty, since
(\langle\rangle,B,f)\in\mathbf{P}
where
f\in{}^{B}\ord
is the function
M\mapsto\rk^{M}(\langle\rangle)
.

Now, given a creature
\mathfrak{p}=(\sigma,Z,f)
in
\mathbf{P}
, as
\mathcal{I}+Z
is an
\aleph_{1}
-complete ideal and
Z\in(\mathcal{I}+Z)^{*}
and
f\in{}^{Z}\ord
, the rank
\rk_{\mathcal{I}+Z}(f)
is well-defined, and we will call this the
hybrid rank
of
\mathfrak{p}
to avoid confusion among all the ranks.
16
16

16

The terminology is due to Assaf Rinot from
[
22
]
.

So, among all the creatures in
\mathbf{P}
, let
\mathfrak{p}=(\sigma,Z,f)
be chosen such that its hybrid rank is minimal. Let
k:=|\sigma|
.

Now consider the ideal
\mathcal{J}:=\mathbf{I}[f,\mathcal{I}+Z]
.
By Lemma
3.7
,

\mathcal{I}\subseteq\mathcal{I}+Z\subseteq\mathcal{J},

and
\mathcal{J}
is a fine normal ideal on
\pom(H(\Omega))
so
\mathcal{J}\in\mathbb{I}
.
As
Z\in\mathcal{J}^{*}
, or more relevant, as
(Z,\mathcal{J})\in\mathcal{C}_{1}
, by Lemma
4.4
there is
U\in\mathcal{B}
and
(Y,\mathcal{J})\leq_{1}(Z,\mathcal{J})
such that for every
M\in Y
,
U_{F(M),k}=U
. Now,
(Y,\mathcal{J})\leq_{1}(A,\mathcal{I})
, and by the choice of
(A,\mathcal{I})
we can find
S^{\prime},T\subseteq Y
such that
S^{\prime},T\in\mathcal{J}^{+}
and
(S^{\prime},T)
is
(i,j)
-saturated over
\mathcal{I}
.
We have by Lemma
5.3
that

\forall^{\mathcal{I}}M\in S^{\prime}\exists^{\mathcal{I}}N\in T[c(F(M),F(N))=i].

So, let
S\subseteq S^{\prime}
be such that
S^{\prime}\setminus S\in\mathcal{I}
and

\forall M\in S\exists^{\mathcal{I}}N\in T[c(F(M),F(N))=i].

Since
\mathcal{I}\subseteq\mathcal{J}
and
S^{\prime}\in\mathcal{J}^{+}
, note that
S\in\mathcal{J}^{+}
too, and of course
(S,T)
is also
(i,j)
-saturated over
\mathcal{I}
.

Let
\widetilde{\sigma}:=\sigma{}^{\smallfrown}\langle(S,T)\rangle
, and then we have that
\widetilde{\sigma}\in\mathcal{T}
.
Now, let
\widetilde{\mathfrak{p}}:=(\widetilde{\sigma},\widetilde{Z},\widetilde{f})
, where

•

\widetilde{Z}:=S
, and

•

\widetilde{f}\in{}^{S}\ord
is the function
M\mapsto\rk^{M}(\widetilde{\sigma})
.

Clearly
\widetilde{\mathfrak{p}}\in\mathbf{P}
. Now, let us compare
\rk_{\mathcal{I}+Z}(f)
and
\rk_{\mathcal{I}+\widetilde{Z}}(\widetilde{f})
.
As
S\in\mathcal{J}^{+}=(\mathbf{I}[f,\mathcal{I}+Z])^{+}
and
S\subseteq Z
, we have that

\rk_{\mathcal{I}+Z}(f)=\rk_{(\mathcal{I}+Z)+S}(f)=\rk_{\mathcal{I}+S}(f)>\rk_{\mathcal{I}+S}(\widetilde{f})=\rk_{\mathcal{I}+\widetilde{Z}}(\widetilde{f}).

To see why the inequality is true, note that for every
M\in S
, as
\sigma,\widetilde{\sigma}\in\mathcal{T}^{M}
and
\sigma\sqsubset\widetilde{\sigma}
,

f(M)=\rk^{M}(\sigma)>\rk^{M}(\widetilde{\sigma})=\widetilde{f}(M),

so
\widetilde{f}<_{\mathcal{I}+S}f
.
So we have that the hybrid rank of
\widetilde{\mathfrak{p}}
is strictly less than the hybrid rank of
\mathfrak{p}
. This contradicts the choice of
\mathfrak{p}
and we finish.
∎

We are now in a position to prove Theorem D.

Corollary 7.2
.

Let
(X,\tau)
be a regular topological space which is not left-separated and has a point-countable basis. Let
K
be a positive natural number and
c:[X]^{2}\rightarrow K
a colouring of pairs of elements of
X
. Then there is a
Y\subseteq X
homeomorphic to
\mathbb{Q}
such that
|c``[Y]^{2}|\leq 2
.

Proof.

By Lemma
7.1
, there is an
\mathcal{I}\in\mathbb{I}
and an
A\in\mathcal{I}^{+}
such that
(A,\mathcal{I})
is a winning pair for
c
. By Theorem
5.5
this gives the existence of a
Y\subseteq X
as required.
∎

Enhancement of High Harmonic Generation in Bulk Floquet Systems
Abhishek Kumar, Yantao Li, Babak Seradjeh

Abstract
We formulate a theory of bulk optical current for a periodically driven system, which accounts for the mixing of external drive and laser field frequencies and, therefore, the broadening of the harmonic spectrum compared to the undriven system. We express the current in terms of Floquet-Bloch bands and their non-adiabatic Berry connection and curvature. Using this expression, we relate spatio-temporal symmetries of the driven model to selection rules for current harmonics. We illustrate the application of this theory by studying high harmonic generation in the periodically driven Su-Schrieffer-Heeger model. At high frequencies and low field amplitudes, we find analytical expressions for current harmonics. We also calculate the current numerically beyond the high frequency limit and verify that when the drive breaks a temporal symmetry, harmonics forbidden in the undriven model become available. Moreover, we find significant enhancement in higher harmonics when the system is driven, even for low field amplitudes. Our work offers a unified Floquet approach to nonlinear optical properties of solids, which is useful for realistic calculations of high harmonic spectra of electronic systems subject to multiple periodic drives.

Optical current in Floquet Systems

II.1
Primer on Floquet theory

For a time-periodic Hamiltonian,
\hat{H}(t)=\hat{H}(t+T)
, with period
T
, the solution of Schrödinger equation
takes the form
\mathinner{|{\Psi_{\alpha}(t)}\rangle}=e^{-i\Xi_{\alpha}t}\mathinner{|{\Phi_{\alpha}(t)}\rangle}
, where
\Xi_{\alpha}\in(-\pi/T,\pi/T]
is the quasienergy, a conserved quantity and the Floquet state,
\mathinner{|{\Phi_{\alpha}(t)}\rangle}
, is also periodic in time with the same period
T
, and satisfies the Floquet-Schrödinger equation

[\hat{H}(t)-i\partial_{t}]\mathinner{|{\Phi_{\alpha}(t)}\rangle}=\Xi_{\alpha}\mathinner{|{\Phi_{\alpha}(t)}\rangle}.

(1)

Also, the time-ordered evolution operator

\displaystyle\hat{U}(t,t_{0})

\displaystyle:=\mathrm{T}\hskip-2.84526pt\exp{\left[-i\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\hat{H}(s)ds\right]}

(2)

\displaystyle=e^{-i(t-t_{0})\hat{H}_{F}(t)}\hat{P}(t,t_{0}),

(3)

is decomposed into a periodic micromotion operator

\displaystyle\hat{P}(t,t_{0})

\displaystyle=\hat{P}(t+T,t_{0})=\hat{P}(t,t_{0}+T)

\displaystyle\equiv\sum_{\alpha}\mathinner{|{\Phi_{\alpha}(t)}\rangle}\mathinner{\langle{\Phi_{\alpha}(t_{0})}|}

(4)

and the evolution under the Floquet Hamiltonian

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{F}(t)=\sum_{\alpha}\Xi_{\alpha}\mathinner{|{\Phi_{\alpha}(t)}\rangle}\mathinner{\langle{\Phi_{\alpha}(t)}|}.

(5)

For a Hamiltonian that depends on two commensurate frequencies (
\omega
,
\Omega
), we can define
\gamma=\omega t
and
\Gamma=\Omega t
so that

\hat{H}(\gamma,\Gamma)=\hat{H}(\gamma+2\pi,\Gamma)=\hat{H}(\gamma,\Gamma+2\pi)
.
We can then write the multi-mode Floquet-Schrödinger equation

\left[\hat{H}(\gamma,\Gamma)-i(\omega\partial_{\gamma}+\Omega\partial_{\Gamma})\right]\mathinner{|{\Phi_{\alpha}(\gamma,\Gamma)}\rangle}=\Xi_{\alpha}\mathinner{|{\Phi_{\alpha}(\gamma,\Gamma)}\rangle},

(6)

where
\mathinner{|{\Phi_{\alpha}(\gamma,\Gamma)}\rangle}
is periodic in
\gamma
and
\Gamma
with period
2\pi
.

We may expand any such function,
f(\gamma,\Gamma)
in Fourier modes,
f(\gamma,\Gamma)=\sum_{m,M}e^{-im\gamma-iM\Gamma}f^{(m,M)}
with

f^{(m,M)}=\oint e^{im\gamma+iM\Gamma}f(\gamma,\Gamma)\frac{d\gamma}{2\pi}\frac{d\Gamma}{2\pi},

(7)

where
\oint
is over a cycle of each integration variable. The single-time Schrödinger equation and its solutions are obtained simply by replacing
\gamma=\omega t
and
\Gamma=\Omega t
in the multi-mode Schrödinger equation and its solutions.

II.2
Optical current and its harmonics

We now derive an expression for optical current in a time periodic system using Floquet theory. The density matrix of the system at some initial time
t_{0}
is
\hat{\rho}(t_{0})
, which evolves in time through a unitary operator
\hat{U}(t,t_{0})
as

\hat{\rho}(t)=\hat{U}(t,t_{0})\hat{\rho}(t_{0})\hat{U}^{\dagger}(t,t_{0})
.

For further simplification, we assume that all the operators can be written in the single-particle fermionic basis and take periodic boundary conditions. So, the Hamiltonian
\hat{H}(t)=\sum_{krs}\hat{c}^{\dagger}_{kr}h_{rs}(k,t)\hat{c}_{ks}
,
where
\hat{c}^{\dagger}_{kr}
and
\hat{c}_{kr}
are the creation and annihilation operators, respectively, at lattice momentum
k
and quantum number
r
, and
h(k,t)
is the time-periodic Bloch Hamiltonian. Similarly, the current operator
\hat{J}(t)=\sum_{krs}\hat{c}^{\dagger}_{kr}j_{rs}(k,t)\hat{c}_{ks}
, where
j(k,t)=\partial{h}(k,t)/\partial k
.

The current is

\displaystyle J(t,t_{0})

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Tr}\left[\hat{\rho}(t)\hat{J}(t)\right]

(8)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k}\mathrm{tr}\left[g(k)j^{H}(k,t,t_{0})\right],

(9)

where
g_{rs}(k,t_{0})=\mathrm{Tr}[\hat{\rho}(t_{0})c^{\dagger}_{ks}c^{\vphantom{{\dagger}}}_{kr}]
, ‘
\mathrm{Tr}
’ is taken over the many-body Hilbert space, and ‘
\mathrm{tr}
’ is over the single-particle Hilbert space for each momentum
k
. Here,
j^{H}(k,t,t_{0})=u^{\dagger}(k,t,t_{0})j(k,t)u(k,t,t_{0})
is the (single-particle) current in the Heisenberg picture and
u(k,t,t_{0})=\mathrm{T}\hskip-2.84526pt\exp[-i\int_{t_{0}}^{t}h(k,s)ds]
. In the Floquet basis
\mathinner{|{\phi_{\alpha}(k,t)}\rangle}
of
h(k,t)
, we find

J(t,t_{0})=\sum_{k\alpha\beta}g_{\alpha\beta}(k,t_{0})j^{F}_{\beta\alpha}(k,t)e^{-i(\epsilon_{\alpha}-\epsilon_{\beta})(t-t_{0})},

(10)

where
j^{F}_{\beta\alpha}(k,t)=\mathinner{\langle{\phi_{\beta}(k,t)}|}j(k,t)\mathinner{|{\phi_{\alpha}(k,t)}\rangle}
is periodic in time, and
\epsilon_{\alpha}
are the single-particle quasienergies.

In the following, we specify the time dependence of the Hamiltonian through a periodically driven internal parameter
\delta(t)=\sum_{M}\delta^{(M)}e^{-iM\Omega t}
and an external electric field via the gauge potential
A(t)=\sum_{m}A^{(m)}e^{-im\omega t}
. Thus, the Hamiltonian
h(k,t)
depends on the two frequencies
\Omega
of the drive and
\omega
of the external field. Therefore, we may employ the Fourier expansion

j^{F}_{\beta\alpha}(k,t)=\sum_{m,M}j_{\beta\alpha}^{(m,M)}(k)e^{-i(m\omega+M\Omega)t}
to write

\displaystyle J(t,t_{0})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\alpha\beta\\
mM\end{subarray}}g_{\alpha\beta}(k,t_{0})j^{(m,M)}_{\beta\alpha}(k)e^{-i(m\omega+M\Omega)t}

\displaystyle\hskip 71.13188pt\times e^{-i(\epsilon_{\alpha}-\epsilon_{\beta})(t-t_{0})}.

(11)

The dependence on the initial time
t_{0}
in Eqs. (
10
) and (
II.2
), where we set the occupation of the quasi-energy bands, is the result of our assumption that the ensuing dynamics is fully coherent. However, in reality the occupation of the quasienergy bands,
g_{\alpha\beta}(k,t_{0})
, depends on a specific relaxation process for a given system. We are interested in situations in which the relaxation process, e.g. through suitable coupling to a thermal bath, results in a diagonal population
g_{\alpha\beta}=g_{\alpha}\delta_{\alpha\beta}
independent of
t_{0}
. This removes the dependence of the optical current on the initial time. Physically, this means the system approaches a Floquet steady state. Formally, we can obtain this result by assuming
g_{\alpha\beta}
is independent of
t_{0}
and average the current over
t_{0}
, to which only the diagonal occupations in Floquet states contribute.

Then the current becomes periodic in time
J(t)=J(t+T)
, and

J(t)=\sum_{k\alpha mM}g_{\alpha}(k)j^{(m,M)}_{\alpha\alpha}(k)e^{-i(m\omega+M\Omega)t}.

(12)

This form of the optical current clearly shows the frequency mixing in the generation of its harmonics. For example, if the drive frequency is an integer multiple of the field frequency,
\Omega=N\omega
, the current acquires harmonics
m+NM
of the field frequency. Conversely, when
\omega=n\Omega
for integer
n
, the current acquires fractional harmonics
m+M/n
of the field frequency. As we shall demonstrate in concrete examples, some of these harmonics may vanish or be enhanced depending on the order of the process and selection rules imposed by spatiotemporal symmetries.

We also note that this diagonal optical current may appear to correspond to only the intra-band contributions. However, it is important to bear in mind that each quasienergy band is obtained by a reconstruction of the original energy bands of static system. Therefore, the optical current, Eq. (
12
), already includes both intra- and inter-band contributions of the static system.

II.3
Optical current from Berry connection and curvature

We would like to recast the optical current in terms of geometrical quantities like Berry curvature and Berry connection.
First, we note that in the Floquet basis, the current matrix elements,

\displaystyle j^{F}_{\beta\alpha}(k,t)

\displaystyle\equiv\mathinner{\langle{\phi_{\beta}(k,t)}|}\frac{\partial{h(k,t)}}{\partial k}\mathinner{|{\phi_{\alpha}(k,t)}\rangle}

\displaystyle=[\epsilon_{\beta}(k)-\epsilon_{\alpha}(k)+i\partial_{t}]\mathinner{\langle{\phi_{\beta}(t)}|{\partial_{k}\phi_{\alpha}(k,t)}\rangle}

\displaystyle\quad+\delta_{\alpha\beta}\partial_{k}\epsilon_{\beta}(k).

(13)

So the full expression of the current, Eq. (
10
), with diagonal Floquet population becomes

J(t)=\sum_{k\alpha}g_{\alpha}(k)\left[\partial_{t}{A}_{\alpha,k}(k,t)+\partial_{k}\epsilon_{\alpha}(k)\right],

(14)

where
{A}_{\alpha,k}(k,t)=\mathinner{\langle{\phi_{\alpha}(k,t)}|{i\partial_{k}\phi_{\alpha}(k,t)}\rangle}
is the Berry connection of the Floquet band
\alpha
. This expression makes it clear that all time dependence and, therefore, all higher harmonics of the optical current result from the time dependence of the Floquet Berry connection. As in the static case, this also shows that a completely filled Floquet band cannot contribute to DC current.

On the other hand, the gauge invariant of the optical current is not manifested in Eq. (
14
), since the Berry connection
{A}_{\alpha,k}(k,t)
itself is not a gauge invariant quantity. Therefore, we now further recast this expression in terms of the gauge-invariant Floquet Berry curvature,

\displaystyle{E}_{\alpha}

\displaystyle=\partial_{t}{A}_{\alpha,k}-\partial_{k}{A}_{\alpha,t},

(15)

\displaystyle=i\mathinner{\langle{\partial_{t}\phi_{\alpha}}|{\partial_{k}\phi_{\alpha}}\rangle}-i\mathinner{\langle{\partial_{k}\phi_{\alpha}}|{\partial_{t}\phi_{\alpha}}\rangle}

(16)

where
{A}_{\alpha,t}(k,t)=\mathinner{\langle{\phi_{\alpha}(k,t)}|{i\partial_{t}\phi_{\alpha}(k,t)}\rangle}
is the non-adiabatic (Aharonov-Anandan) connection of the states in Floquet band
\alpha
.
Aharonov and Anandan 1987

Numerically, we can also calculate the Berry curvature more efficiently and accurately without the need of fixing a special gauge.
Matsuoka et al. 2005

To proceed, note that

\displaystyle\partial_{t}{A}_{\alpha,k}

\displaystyle={E}_{\alpha}+\partial_{k}{\mathinner{\langle{\phi_{\alpha}}|{i\partial_{t}\phi_{\alpha}}\rangle}}

(17)

\displaystyle={E}_{\alpha}+\partial_{k}\mathinner{\langle{\phi_{\alpha}}|{h}|{\phi_{\alpha}}\rangle}{-\partial_{k}\epsilon_{\alpha}}.

(18)

Therefore, we find

J(t)=\sum_{k\alpha}g_{\alpha}(k)\left[{E}_{\alpha}(k,t)+\partial_{k}\eta_{\alpha}(k,t)\right],

(19)

where
\eta_{\alpha}(k,t)=\mathinner{\langle{\phi_{\alpha}(k,t)}|}h(k,t)\mathinner{|{\phi_{\alpha}(k,t)}\rangle}
is the expectation value of the instantaneous Hamiltonian in the Floquet band
\alpha
. As before, due to periodicity in crystal momentum
k
, this last term does not contribute in a completely filled band. However, in partially filled bands, it can contribute to DC as well as the higher harmonics of optical current.

We note that these expressions can be readily extended to higher dimensions,

\displaystyle\mathbf{J}(t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mathbf{k}\alpha}g_{\alpha}(\mathbf{k})\left[\partial_{t}{{A}}_{\alpha,\mathbf{k}}(\mathbf{k},t)+\partial_{\mathbf{k}}\epsilon(\mathbf{k})\right]

(20)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mathbf{k}\alpha}g_{\alpha}(\mathbf{k})\left[\boldsymbol{{E}}_{\alpha}(\mathbf{k},t)+\partial_{\mathbf{k}}\eta(\mathbf{k},t)\right],

(21)

where
\boldsymbol{{E}}_{\alpha}=\partial_{t}{{A}}_{\alpha,\mathbf{k}}-\partial_{\mathbf{k}}{{A}}_{\alpha t}
and the Floquet Berry connection

{{A}}_{\alpha,\mathbf{k}}=\mathinner{\langle{\phi_{\alpha}}|{i\partial_{\mathbf{k}}\phi_{\alpha}}\rangle}
and
{{A}}_{\alpha,t}=\mathinner{\langle{\phi_{\alpha}}|{i\partial_{t}\phi_{\alpha}}\rangle}
.

II.4
Selection rules

Here, we analyze the constraints on the current and its harmonics due to symmetries for the ideal Floquet occupation with each band either fully occupied or empty. In particular, we consider a unitary mirror (
I_{F}
) and an antiunitary time-reflection (
\Theta_{F}
) symmetries, which include time glide operations,

\displaystyle h(k,t)

\displaystyle=I_{F}^{\dagger}h(-k,t+t_{I})I_{F},

(22)

\displaystyle h(k,t)

\displaystyle=\Theta_{F}^{\dagger}h(-k,-t+t_{R})\Theta_{F}.

(23)

Under
I_{F}
symmetry, the occupied Floquet bands
\alpha\in\text{occ}
satisfy

I_{F}\mathinner{|{\phi_{\alpha}(k,t)}\rangle}=\sum_{\beta\in\text{occ}}\mathfrak{I}_{\beta\alpha}(k,t)\mathinner{|{\phi_{\beta}(-k,t+t_{I})}\rangle}
, where
\mathfrak{I}(k,t)
is a unitary matrix. This leads to a relation between the current at different times,

J(t)=-J(t+t_{I}).

(24)

Therefore, the current vanishes at an odd number of times in any interval of length
t_{I}
in the cycle, at the boundary of which the current is nonzero.
For
t_{I}=\pi/\omega
, we get the following condition for the harmonics of the current,

J^{(p)}=(-1)^{p+1}J^{(p)},

(25)

which means in the presence of
I_{F}
symmetry, only odd harmonics will survive.

Under
\Theta_{F}
symmetry, the occupied Floquet bands satisfy
\Theta_{F}\mathinner{|{\phi_{\alpha}(k,t)}\rangle}=\sum_{\beta\in\text{occ}}\mathfrak{T}_{\beta\alpha}(k,t)\mathinner{|{\phi_{\alpha}(-k,-t+t_{R})}\rangle}
, where
\mathfrak{T}(k,t)
is an orthogonal matrix.
This also leads to a relation between current at different times,

J(t)=-J(-t+t_{R}).

(26)

As a result, the current must vanish at
J(t_{R}/2)=J(T/2+t_{R}/2)=0
.
For
t_{R}=\pi/\omega
we have the following properties of the harmonics of the current,

J^{(p)}=(-1)^{p+1}J^{(-p)},

(27)

which means the odd harmonics of the current will be real and even harmonics of the current will be imaginary, and the DC component
J^{(0)}=0
. If
t_{R}=0
instead, the current
J(t)=-J(-t)
is an odd function of time and we find

J^{(p)}=-J^{(-p)},

(28)

which means
J^{(0)}=0
and all the other harmonics will be imaginary.

When both
I_{F}
and
\Theta_{F}
symmetries are present, and taking
t_{I}=t_{R}=\pi/\omega
, we have

J^{(p)}=J^{(-p)}=(-1)^{p+1}J^{(p)},

(29)

which means that only odd harmonics are nonzero and they are real.

II.5
Relation to topology

The expressions of the current in terms of the Floquet Berry connection and curvature, Eqs. (
14
) and (
19
), provide a link between the optical current and the quantum geometry of Floquet bands. This geometry is also responsible for nontrivial topological properties of the system
Morimoto and Nagaosa 2016
. Here, we examine the connection between the optical current and nontrivial topology in different frequency regimes.

In the low-frequency limit, the Floquet states
\mathinner{|{\phi_{\alpha}(k,t)}\rangle}
approach, up to a smooth gauge
Rodriguez-Vega et al. 2018
, the adiabatic states
\mathinner{|{\psi_{\alpha}^{\text{ad}}(k,t)}\rangle}
, i.e. the instantaneous eigenstates of the driven Hamiltonian,
h(k,t)\mathinner{|{\psi_{\alpha}^{\text{ad}}(k,t)}\rangle}=E_{\alpha}(k,t)\mathinner{|{\psi_{\alpha}^{\text{ad}}(t)}\rangle}
, assuming the instantaneous energy bands
E_{\alpha}(k,t)
remain gapped. Then, the optical current of fully occupied bands approaches
J(t)\to\sum_{\alpha\in\text{occ}}\oint{E}^{\text{ad}}_{\alpha}(k,t)dk
, where
{E}^{\text{ad}}_{\alpha}(k,t)
is the adiabatic Berry curvature. This is, of course, the Thouless pump, in which a charge

Q=\oint J(t)dt=\sum_{\alpha\in\text{occ}}\oint{E}_{\alpha}^{\text{ad}}(k,t)dkdt\equiv\text{Ch}^{\text{ad}}_{\text{occ}},

(30)

equal to the Chern number
\text{Ch}^{\text{ad}}_{\text{occ}}
of the occupied adiabatic bands in
(k,t)
space, is pumped in each cycle.

While Eqs. (
14
) and (
19
) hold formally at all frequencies, the gap structure of Floquet bands deviates significantly away from the adiabatic limit. As a result, the Floquet bands in the intermediate frequency range become partially filled. In the high frequency limit, other approximations such as rotating-wave frame and Floquet Magnus expansion become available. In certain cases, one can also justify or specifically engineer the Floquet bands to be be fully occupied or empty. For example, for high frequencies that are off-resonant with gapped static bands, one may take the occupation of Floquet bands to be nearly the same as the original static bands. In such case,
the optical current is obtained from the non-adiabatic Berry connection and curvature of Floquet bands.

We would like to note a subtlety related to the choice of gauge in using Eqs. (
14
) and (
19
).
Consider a driven one-dimensional Hamiltonian
h(k,t)
with time-reflection symmetry, e.g.
h(k,-t)=h(k,t)
, and chiral symmetry
\{C,h(k,t)\}=0
satisfying
C=C^{\dagger}=C^{-1}
. Then, the Floquet Hamiltonians
h_{F}(k,t_{*})
at time-reflection symmetric times
t_{*}=0,T/2
, inherit the chiral symmetry
C
. The topology of the Floquet bands is then characterized by two gauge-invariant winding numbers
w(t_{*})
,

w(t_{*})=\frac{1}{\pi}\sum_{\alpha\in\text{occ}}\oint\mathinner{\langle{C\phi_{\alpha}(k,t_{*})}|{i\partial_{k}\phi_{\alpha}(k,t_{*})}\rangle}dk\in\mathbb{Z}.

(31)

There is a bulk-boundary correspondence between
w(t_{*})
and the number of midgap bound states,
\nu_{0}
and
\nu_{\pi}
, respectively, at quasienergies
0
and
\pi/T
,

\nu_{0}=\frac{w(T/2)+w(0)}{2},\quad\nu_{\pi}=\frac{w(T/2)-w(0)}{2}.

(32)

It is possible to choose a smooth gauge at each
t_{*}
,
\mathinner{|{\phi_{\alpha}(k,t_{*})}\rangle}\to e^{i\Lambda_{\alpha}(k,t_{*})}\mathinner{|{\phi_{\alpha}(k,t_{*})}\rangle}
, such that

w(t_{*})=\frac{1}{\pi}\sum_{\alpha\in\text{occ}}\oint\mathinner{\langle{\phi_{\alpha}(k,t_{*})}|{i\partial_{k}\phi_{\alpha}(k,t_{*})}\rangle}dk.

It is then tempting to related
w(T/2)-w(0)
to
\int_{0}^{T/2}j(t)dt
. However, while both of these quantities are gauge-invariant, this would not be in general correct because it is not in general possible to find a gauge
\mathinner{|{\phi_{\alpha}(k,t)}\rangle}\to e^{i\Lambda_{\alpha}(k,t)}\mathinner{|{\phi_{\alpha}(k,t)}\rangle}
that smoothly connects
\Lambda_{\alpha}(k,0)
to
\Lambda_{\alpha}(k,T/2)
in the cycle. The degree to which this smooth gauge condition is broken is indeed what
\nu_{\pi}
quantifies.

III
HHG in Periodically Driven Su-Schrieffer-Heeger model

III.1
Model and symmetries

In order to illustrate the physics of high harmonic generation in Floquet systems concretely, we consider the Su-Schrieffer-Heeger (SSH) model with time-periodic hopping amplitudes. In addition, an external time-periodic and spatially uniform gauge field,
A(t)
, is introduced via Peierls substitution
k\rightarrow k-A(t)
. The full loch Hamiltonian is

\displaystyle h(k,t)

\displaystyle=w\left\{1+m(t)+[1-m(t)]\cos[k-A(t)]\right\}\sigma_{x}

\displaystyle\quad+w\left[1-m(t)\right]\sin[k-A(t)]\sigma_{y}.

(33)

Here,
w
is the average hopping amplitude. In the following, we take the driven field
A(t)=A_{0}\sin(\omega t+\theta)
and the driven hopping modulation
m(t)=m_{0}+m_{1}\cos(\Omega t)
.

The static SSH model (
m_{1}=0,A_{0}=0
) has a trivial (
m_{0}>0
) and a topological phase (
m_{0}<0
) phase protected by the chiral symmetry,
\{h,C\}=0
with
C=\sigma_{z}
.
In addition, the static model has both unitary inversion (or mirror) symmetry (
I=\sigma_{x},k\to-k
) and antiunitary time-reversal symmetry (
\Theta=K,k\to-k
, where
K
is the complex conjugation).

In the driven model, these symmetries are in general lifted by the external field. However, for certain drive protocols, we may recover these symmetries upon a suitable mapping within the cycle. That is, the driven system may have related
spatiotemporal
symmetries, in which the temporal part of the Hamiltonian is also transformed. Indeed, the inversion symmetry can be restored for the driven system if there exists a time glide
\tau(t_{I}):t\mapsto t+t_{I}
within the cycle, for which
A(t+t_{I})=-A(t)
is odd and
m(t+t_{I})=m(t)
is even. Similarly, the time-reversal symmetry is restored if there exists a reflection time
t_{R}
, for which
A(t_{R}-t)=-A(t)
is odd and
m(t_{R}-t)=m(t)
is even. The spatiotemporal symmetries of the driven system are, then,

I_{F}^{-1}h(-k,t+t_{I})I_{F}=h(k,t),\quad I_{F}=\tau(t_{I})I,

(34a)

and

\Theta_{F}^{-1}h(-k,-t+t_{R})\Theta_{F}=h(k,t),\quad\Theta_{F}=\tau(t_{R})\Theta.

(34b)

When the drive frequency is an even multiple of the field frequency (
\Omega=2n\omega
,
n\in\mathbb{Z}
), we may choose
t_{I}=\pi/\omega
for any
\theta
to obtain symmetry under
I_{F}
, yielding

J(t+T/2)=-J(t).

For
\theta=\pm\frac{\pi}{2}
, we can take
t_{R}=\pi/\omega
to obtain symmetry under
\Theta_{F}
, whereby

J(t+T/2)=-J(-t).

Similarly, if
\theta=0
or
\pi
, we can take
t_{R}=0
to find
J(t)=-J(-t)
. Therefore, selection rules for the ideal occupation of the Floquet bands apply to the harmonics of the current in these cases as discussed in Sec.
II.4
.

V
Conclusions and Outlook

We have developed a theoretical framework using Floquet theory to calculate the optical current in a periodically driven system.
The Floquet optical current mixes the field and drive frequencies,
\omega
and
\Omega
, respectively, generating harmonics
m\omega+M\Omega
. The frequency mixing broadens the spectrum of harmonic orders and enhances the values of harmonics even for a low field amplitude.

This formulation naturally contains the Floquet theoretic approach to understanding high harmonic generation in initially undriven systems and the Floquet linear response theory
Kumar et al. 2020
as limiting cases when the drive amplitude and the field amplitude, respectively, approach zero. In the latter case this work provides a nonlinear Floquet theoretic framework for optical current for any time periodic field.

The optical current can be recast in terms of the occupation of Floquet-Bloch bands and their non-adiabatic Berry connection and curvature. This formulation naturally brings out a relation between topology and optical current for sufficiently low drive and field frequencies and exposes and clarifies subtleties of gauge fixing that prevent a direct relation between current harmonics and Floquet topological invariants for higher frequencies.

In the presence of spatio-temporal symmetries like inversion and time reversal with time glide, we obtained the properties of optical current and selection rules for the harmonics.
As an application of our formulation, we studied the optical current and in the driven SSH model and found analytical expressions for its harmonics at sufficiently high frequency and weak field amplitude.
Symmetry analysis and numerical calculations show that by altering the temporal part of the spatio-temporal symmetry, we can obtain harmonics that are forbidden in undriven cases. The broader implication of this phenomenon is that without changing the spatial structure or symmetry of a material, we can procure forbidden harmonics by driving the system.

Importantly, frequency mixing between the drive and the field opens a way to enhance the HHG spectrum. This Floquet enhancement can be accessed naturally in pump-probe setups, where the pump and drive frequencies are commensurate. For non-optical drives, a
more realistic setup is when the drive frequency is a small commensurate fraction of the field frequency, which always breaks the temporal symmetry and leads to a broad-spectrum HHG.
Additionally, our calculations pertaining to the driven SSH model may be tested in synthetic quantum simulators.
Kanungo et al. 2021

Our results demonstrates that we can broaden and enhance the spectrum of harmonic order by driving the system. This work encourages further theoretical and experimental investigations of harmonic generation in bulk driven quantum systems.

(56)

The occupied Floquet states are mapped explicitly as
I_{F}\mathinner{|{\phi_{\alpha}(k,t)}\rangle}=\sum_{\beta\in\text{occ}}\mathfrak{I}_{\beta\alpha}(k,t)\mathinner{|{\phi_{\alpha}(-k,t+t_{I})}\rangle}
, with a unitary matrix
\mathfrak{I}(k,t)
: for
\lambda,\beta\in\text{occ}
,

\displaystyle[\mathfrak{I}(k,t)\mathfrak{I}^{\dagger}(k,t)]_{\lambda\beta}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha\in\text{occ}}\mathfrak{I}_{\lambda\alpha}(k,t)\mathfrak{I}_{\beta\alpha}^{*}(k,t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha\in\text{occ}}\mathinner{\langle{\phi_{\lambda}(-k,t+t_{I})}|{I_{F}}|{\phi_{\alpha}(k,t)}\rangle}\mathinner{\langle{\phi_{\beta}(-k,t+t_{I})}|{I_{F}}|{\phi_{\alpha}(k,t)}\rangle}^{*}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha\in\text{occ}}\mathinner{\langle{\phi_{\lambda}(-k,t+t_{I})}|{I_{F}}|{\phi_{\alpha}(k,t)}\rangle}\mathinner{\langle{\phi_{\alpha}(k,t)}|{I_{F}^{\dagger}}|{\phi_{\beta}(-k,t+t_{I})}\rangle}

\displaystyle=\mathinner{\langle{\phi_{\lambda}(-k,t+t_{I})}|{I_{F}P_{\text{occ}}(k,t)I_{F}^{\dagger}}|{\phi_{\beta}(-k,t+t_{I})}\rangle}

\displaystyle=\mathinner{\langle{\phi_{\lambda}(-k,t+t_{I})}|{P_{\text{occ}}(-k,t+t_{I})}|{\phi_{\beta}(-k,t+t_{I})}\rangle}

\displaystyle=\delta_{\lambda\beta}.

(57)

Then, the current has the symmetry

\displaystyle J(t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha\in\text{occ}}\oint\mathinner{\langle{\phi_{\alpha}(k,t)}|{\partial_{k}h(k,t)}|{\phi_{\alpha}(k,t)}\rangle}dk=\oint\mathrm{tr}[P_{\text{occ}}(k,t)\partial_{k}h(k,t)]dk

\displaystyle=\oint\mathrm{tr}[P_{\text{occ}}(k,t)I_{F}^{\dagger}\partial_{k}h(-k,t+t_{I})I_{F}]dk=\oint\mathrm{tr}[I_{F}P_{\text{occ}}(k,t)I_{F}^{\dagger}\partial_{k}h(-k,t+t_{I})]dk

\displaystyle=\oint\mathrm{tr}[P_{\text{occ}}(-k,t+t_{I})\partial_{k}h(-k,t+t_{I})]dk=-\oint\mathrm{tr}[P_{\text{occ}}(k,t+t_{I})\partial_{k}h(k,t+t_{I})]dk

\displaystyle=-J(t+t_{I}).

(58)

The antiunitary symmetry
\Theta_{F}
transforms the Floquet unitary as

\displaystyle\Theta_{F}U_{F}(k,t)\Theta_{F}^{\dagger}

\displaystyle=\prod_{s:t\to t+T}\Theta_{F}e^{-ih(k,s)ds}\Theta_{F}^{\dagger}

\displaystyle=\prod_{s:t\to t+T}e^{+ih(-k,-s+t_{R})ds}

\displaystyle=\left(\prod_{s:-t-T+t_{R}\to-t+t_{R}}e^{-ih(-k,s)ds}\right)^{\dagger}

\displaystyle=U_{F}^{\dagger}(-k,-t+t_{R}).

(59)

Here,
\prod_{s:t_{1}\to t_{2}}
denotes the time-ordered product over
t_{1}<t_{2}
. Thus,
\Theta_{F}^{\dagger}h_{F}(-k,-t+t_{R})\Theta_{F}=h_{F}(k,t)
and

\Theta_{F}P_{\text{occ}}(k,t)\Theta_{F}^{\dagger}=P_{\text{occ}}(-k,-t+t_{R}).

(60)

Explicitly, the occupied Floquet states are mapped as
\Theta_{F}\mathinner{|{\phi_{\alpha}(k,t)}\rangle}=\sum_{\beta\in\text{occ}}\mathfrak{T}_{\beta\alpha}(k,t)\mathinner{|{\phi_{\alpha}(-k,-t+t_{R})}\rangle}
, with an orthogonal matrix
\mathfrak{T}(k,t)
: for
\lambda,\beta\in\text{occ}
,

\displaystyle[\mathfrak{T}(k,t)\mathfrak{T}^{\raisebox{-1.0pt}{{\scriptsize{T}}}}(k,t)]_{\lambda\beta}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha\in\text{occ}}\mathfrak{T}_{\lambda\alpha}(k,t)\mathfrak{T}_{\beta\alpha}(k,t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha\in\text{occ}}\mathinner{\langle{\phi_{\lambda}(-k,-t+t_{R})}|{\Theta_{F}}|{\phi_{\alpha}(k,t)}\rangle}\mathinner{\langle{\phi_{\beta}(-k,-t+t_{R})}|{\Theta_{F}}|{\phi_{\alpha}(k,t)}\rangle}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha\in\text{occ}}\mathinner{\langle{\phi_{\lambda}(-k,-t+t_{R})}|{\Theta_{F}}|{\phi_{\alpha}(k,t)}\rangle}\mathinner{\langle{\phi_{\alpha}(k,t)}|{\Theta_{F}^{\dagger}}|{\phi_{\beta}(-k,-t+t_{I})}\rangle}

\displaystyle=\mathinner{\langle{\phi_{\lambda}(-k,-t+t_{R})}|{\Theta_{F}P_{\text{occ}}(k,t)\Theta_{F}^{\dagger}}|{\phi_{\beta}(-k,-t+t_{R})}\rangle}

\displaystyle=\mathinner{\langle{\phi_{\lambda}(-k,-t+t_{R})}|{P_{\text{occ}}(-k,-t+t_{R})}|{\phi_{\beta}(-k,-t+t_{R})}\rangle}

\displaystyle=\delta_{\lambda\beta},

(61)

where we have used the identity
\mathinner{\langle{\psi}|{\Theta_{F}}|{\chi}\rangle}=\mathinner{\langle{\chi}|{\Theta_{F}^{\dagger}}|{\psi}\rangle}
for antiunitary
\Theta_{F}
.
Similarly, the current has the symmetry

\displaystyle J(t)

\displaystyle=\oint\mathrm{tr}[P_{\text{occ}}(k,t)\partial_{k}h(k,t)]dk

\displaystyle=\oint\mathrm{tr}[P_{\text{occ}}(k,t)\Theta_{F}^{\dagger}\partial_{k}h(-k,-t+t_{R})\Theta_{F}]dk=\oint\mathrm{tr}[\Theta_{F}P_{\text{occ}}(k,t)\Theta_{F}^{\dagger}\partial_{k}h(-k,-t+t_{R})]dk

\displaystyle=\oint\mathrm{tr}[P_{\text{occ}}(-k,-t+t_{R})\partial_{k}h(-k,-t+t_{R})]dk=-\oint\mathrm{tr}[P_{\text{occ}}(k,-t+t_{R})\partial_{k}h(k,-t+t_{R})]dk

\displaystyle=-J(-t+t_{R}),

(62)

as stated in he main text.

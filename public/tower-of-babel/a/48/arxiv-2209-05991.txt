Trigonometric chaos and
X

Abstract
. In the line of previous work by Naor, we establish new forms of metric X p \mathrm{X}_{p} inequalities in group algebras under very general assumptions. Our results’ applicability goes beyond the previously known setting in two directions. In first place, we find continuous forms of the X p \mathrm{X}_{p} inequality in the n n -dimensional torus. Second, we consider transferred forms of the sharp scalar valued metric X p \mathrm{X}_{p} inequality in the von Neumann algebra ℒ ⁡ ( G ) \mathcal{L}(\mathrm{G}) of a discrete group G \mathrm{G} . As a byproduct of our results, some metric consequences and their relation with bi-Lipschitz nonembeddability of Banach spaces are explored in the context of noncommutative L p L_{p} spaces.



Abstract

Introduction

In functional analysis, it is a fundamental question to know when a given space is isomorphic to a linear subspace of another. The corresponding area, Banach space embedding theory, dates back to the first half of the twentieth century. For the important example of
L_{p}
and
L_{q}
the landscape is well known.
L_{2}
is isomorphic to a subspace of
L_{p}
for all
p
in the Banach range, but there is no linear embedding from
L_{q}
to
L_{p}
if either
q<\min\{2,p\}
or
q>\max\{2,p\}
. Banach conjectured a positive answer for
\min\{2,p\}<q<\max\{2,p\}
. Kadec proved it for
p<q<2
in
[
6
]
, while Paley disproved it for
2<q<p
in
[
10
]
.

Sometimes, properties of Banach spaces that are local (that is, ones that can be formulated in terms of vectors in finite dimensional subspaces) do not rely on the whole strength of the linear structure. Instead, they may only need the fact that the norm defines a metric. Determining for which properties is this the case is known as the Ribe program, initiated after
[
11
]
and explicitly formulated in
[
1
]
. Replacing the linear maps that yield inclusions between Banach spaces by bi-Lipschitz maps becomes therefore natural. Regarding
L_{p}
spaces, the qualitative results mentioned above do not change, and notably one gets again that
L_{q}
cannot be embedded into
L_{p}
in the range
2<q<p
. This happens in spite of the fact that the classical argument that shows that there is no bi-Lipschitz embedding from
L_{q}
into
L_{p}
uses explicitly the linear structure of the spaces. This is so because it goes through differentiability —of Lipschitz maps— to reduce the metric statement to a linear one. However, the result can be proved by other means and indeed a different quantitative proof can be found in the work of Naor and Schechtman
[
9
]
. One can consult the Introduction in that work for a more detailed context and references regarding the history of the problem and its connections with other areas.

A key innovation in
[
9
]
is the introduction of a family of inequalities, termed
\mathrm{X}_{p}
inequalities. Let us briefly recall the results in
[
9
]
, since they are going to be the starting point of our investigation in the present manuscript. Denote by
[n]
the initial segment
[n]:=\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
. We view the set
\mathbb{Z}_{8m}^{n}
as a probability space equipped with the normalized counting measure. In the same vein we identify
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n}
with
\{-1,1\}^{n}
using multiplicative notation. A metric space
(\mathbb{X},d)
is said to be a metric
\mathrm{X}_{p}
space if for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
k\in[n]
, there exists
m\in\mathbb{N}
such that every mapping
f:\mathbb{Z}_{8m}^{n}\to\mathbb{X}
satisfies the following estimate for
p\geq 2

\displaystyle\frac{1}{{n\choose k}}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathsf{S}\subset[n]\\
|\mathsf{S}|=k\end{subarray}}\mathsf{E}

\displaystyle\big[d\big(\mathrm{M}_{4m\varepsilon_{\mathsf{S}}}f(x),f(x)\big)^{p}\big]

(
\mathrm{MX}_{p}
)

\displaystyle\lesssim_{p}m^{p}\left(\frac{k}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\mathsf{E}\big[d\big(\mathrm{M}_{e_{j}}f(x),f(x)\big)^{p}\big]+\Big(\frac{k}{n}\Big)^{\frac{p}{2}}\mathsf{E}\big[d\big(\mathrm{M}_{\varepsilon}f(x),f(x)\big)^{p}\big]\right).

Above, the dummy variable
(\varepsilon,x)
belongs to
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n}\times\mathbb{Z}_{8m}^{n}
,
\varepsilon_{\mathsf{S}}
denotes the
\mathsf{S}
-truncated vector
\sum_{j\in\mathsf{S}}\varepsilon_{j}e_{j}
, the expectation
\mathsf{E}
is taken in the product probability space, and the operators
\mathrm{M}_{v}
are just translations given by

\mathrm{M}_{v}f(x)=f(x+v).

They are Fourier multipliers, which will become relevant later. The main result in
[
9
]
then asserts that
L_{p}
is an
\mathrm{X}_{p}
space, while
L_{q}
is not if
2<q\neq p
. This and the fact that being an
\mathrm{X}_{p}
metric space is invariant under the action of bi-Lipschitz maps provides the quantitative nonembeddability argument alluded above. Note that the result is not true independently of the value of
m
. It is necessary that it scales at least so that
m\gtrsim\sqrt{n/k}
, even though the bound was not found yet to be sharp in
[
9
]
. The above discussion justifies why
\mathrm{X}_{p}
inequalities are a tool to study Lipschitz nonembeddability results between Banach spaces.

Little after it, Naor found in
[
7
]
a different proof of the metric
\mathrm{X}_{p}
inequality which was quantitatively optimal. Namely, it turns out that the necessary bound
m\gtrsim\sqrt{n/k}
is, indeed, sufficient. This entails a number of significant consequences in metric geometry, including distortion estimates of discrete
\ell_{q}
-grids into
L_{p}
and sharp snowflake
L_{p}
-estimates. A key step towards the main result in
[
7
]
is the inequality below for balanced averages of Fourier truncations of the Fourier-Walsh expansions. Those are expansions of the form

f=\sum_{\mathsf{A}\subset[n]}\widehat{f}(\mathsf{A})W_{\mathsf{A}}.

Given
2\leq p<\infty
,
k\in[n]
, and
f\in L_{p}(\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n})
mean zero, it holds

(
\mathrm{N}_{p}
)

\frac{1}{{n\choose k}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathsf{S}\subset[n]\\
|\mathsf{S}|=k\end{subarray}}\Big\|\sum_{\mathsf{A}\subset\mathsf{S}}\widehat{f}(\mathsf{A})W_{\mathsf{A}}\Big\|_{p}^{p}\lesssim_{p}\frac{k}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left\|\partial_{j}f\right\|_{p}^{p}+\Big(\frac{k}{n}\Big)^{\frac{p}{2}}\|f\|_{p}^{p}.

Above, the symbol
\partial_{j}
denotes the
j
-th directional discrete derivative

\partial_{j}f(\varepsilon):=f(\varepsilon)-f(\varepsilon_{1},\ldots,\varepsilon_{j-1},-\varepsilon_{j},\varepsilon_{j+1},\ldots,\varepsilon_{n}).

Once (

N
p

) has been established, the metric
\mathrm{X}_{p}
inequality for all classical
L_{p}
spaces follows from it plus the natural injection of the multiplicative group
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n}
into
\mathbb{Z}_{2m}^{n}
needed to define the multipliers
\mathrm{M}_{\varepsilon}(f)(x)=f(x+\varepsilon)
for
f:\mathbb{Z}_{2m}^{n}\to\mathbb{C}
. This is the starting point of our work. We seek new metric
\mathrm{X}_{p}
inequalities that can be proven using appropriate versions of (

N
p

). In
[
2
]
, a wide range of inequalities of this kind is established for the (noncommutative)
L_{p}
spaces associated with the group von Neumann algebra
\mathcal{L}(\mathrm{G})
of a discrete group
\mathrm{G}
. Elements in
L_{p}(\mathcal{L}(\mathrm{G}))
are not functions for nonabelian
\mathrm{G}
and making sense of pointwise evaluation is not immediate, though necessary. Seeking the greatest possible generality, we shall consider below different choices of abelian groups
\mathrm{H}
that will play the role of
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n}
in
[
7
]
. Therefore, in the evaluation
\mathrm{M}_{\varepsilon}f(x):=f(x+\varepsilon)
, the variable
\varepsilon\in\mathrm{H}
but that may not be the case for
x
, which imposes a compatibility condition between the two variables. This is a crucial difficulty and the main contribution in this paper is to clarify when a chaos-type inequality like (

N
p

) —as those obtained in
[
2
]
— can be used to deduce a new metric
\mathrm{X}_{p}
-type inequality.

Let us now explain our abstract result, we refer to
[
2
]
for notation regarding analysis on group von Neumann algebras. Let
\mathrm{H}
be a discrete abelian group which can be written as a direct product:

\mathrm{H}=\mathrm{H}_{1}\times\mathrm{H}_{2}\times\cdots\times\mathrm{H}_{n},

equipped with its counting measure. Given
\mathsf{S}\subset[n]
, we define the truncation of
\mathrm{H}
on
\mathsf{S}
as the subgroup
\mathrm{H}_{\mathsf{S}}=\times_{j\in\mathsf{S}}\mathrm{H}_{j}\hookrightarrow\mathrm{H}
. Truncations are transferred to the dual group
\widehat{\mathrm{H}}
, which is a probability space with Haar measure
dx
. Assume also that
\mathrm{G}
is an arbitrary discrete group, and let
p\geq 2
. We say that
(\mathrm{H},\mathrm{G})
is an
\mathrm{X}_{p}
-representable pair
if the following conditions hold:

(RP-1)

Balanced truncations.
We have

\qquad\frac{1}{{n\choose k}}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathsf{S}\subset[n]\\
|\mathsf{S}|=k\end{subarray}}\big\|(\mathsf{E}_{[n]\setminus\mathsf{S}}\otimes\mathrm{id})h\big\|^{p}_{p}\lesssim_{p}\frac{k}{n}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}\big\|(\partial_{j}\otimes\mathrm{id})h\big\|^{p}_{p}+\Big(\frac{k}{n}\Big)^{\frac{p}{2}}\|h\|^{p}_{p},

for
k\in[n]
, any mean zero
h\in L_{p}(\widehat{\mathrm{H}};L_{p}(\mathcal{L}(\mathrm{G})))
, and where

\qquad\mathsf{E}_{[n]\setminus\mathsf{S}}f(x)=\int_{\widehat{\mathrm{H}}}f(x_{\mathsf{S}}+z_{[n]\setminus\mathsf{S}})\ dz

is the conditional expectation onto functions which only depend on variables in
\widehat{\mathrm{H}}_{\mathsf{S}}
. The
\partial_{j}
are linear maps which play the role of directional derivatives.

(RP-2)

Compatibility conditions.
There exists an abelian group

\qquad\Gamma=\Gamma_{1}\times\Gamma_{2}\times\cdots\times\Gamma_{n}

and a map
\eta:\widehat{\mathrm{H}}\rightarrow\widehat{\Gamma}
such that the following compatibility conditions hold:

i)

Symmetric inclusion.
Given
\mathsf{S}\subset[n]
, the variables
\eta_{\mathsf{S}}(y):=(\eta(y))_{\mathsf{S}}
and
-\eta_{\mathsf{S}}(y)
(using here additive notation) have the same distribution.

ii)

Uniformly bounded translations.
There exists a group of Fourier multipliers
\{\mathrm{M}_{\gamma}\}_{\gamma\in\widehat{\Gamma}}
, completely and uniformly bounded on
L_{p}(\mathcal{L}(\mathrm{G}))
.

Theorem A
.

Let
p\geq 2
and
(\mathrm{H},\mathrm{G})
be an
\mathrm{X}_{p}
-representable pair. Let
k\in[n]
and suppose that

satisfies

. Then, for every
f\in L_{p}(\mathcal{L}(\mathrm{G}))
there holds

\displaystyle\frac{1}{{n\choose k}}

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathsf{S}\subset[n]\\
|\mathsf{S}|=k\end{subarray}}\int_{\widehat{\mathrm{H}}}\big\|\mathrm{M}_{4m\eta_{\mathsf{S}}(y)}f-f\big\|^{p}_{p}\ dy

\displaystyle\lesssim_{p}m^{p}\Bigg(\frac{k}{n}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}\int_{\widehat{\mathrm{H}}}\big\|\partial_{j}\mathrm{M}_{2\eta(y)}f\big\|_{p}^{p}\ dy+\Big(\frac{k}{n}\Big)^{\frac{p}{2}}\int_{\widehat{\mathrm{H}}}\big\|\mathrm{M}_{\eta(y)}f-f\big\|_{p}^{p}\ dy\Bigg).

-representable pair

Proof of Theorem A.
For each
\mathsf{S}\subset[n]
with
|\mathsf{S}|=k
and
y\in\mathrm{H}

\displaystyle\big\|\mathrm{M}_{4m\eta_{\mathsf{S}}(y)}f-f\big\|_{p}^{p}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{p}

\displaystyle\big\|T_{[n]\setminus\mathsf{S}}f-f\big\|_{p}^{p}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\big\|\mathrm{M}_{4m\eta_{\mathsf{S}}(y)}T_{[n]\setminus\mathsf{S}}f-T_{[n]\setminus\mathsf{S}}f\big\|_{p}^{p}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\big\|\mathrm{M}_{4m\eta_{\mathsf{S}}(y)}f-\mathrm{M}_{4m\eta_{\mathsf{S}}(y)}T_{[n]\setminus\mathsf{S}}f\big\|_{p}^{p}\ =:\ \mathrm{A}+\mathrm{B}+\mathrm{C}.

First, we claim that

(1.1)

\big\|T_{\mathsf{S}}f-f\big\|_{p}^{p}\lesssim_{p}\int_{\widehat{\mathrm{H}}}\big\|\mathrm{M}_{\eta(y)}f-f\big\|_{p}^{p}\,dy.

Indeed, by convexity of the
L_{p}
norm

\displaystyle\big\|T_{\mathsf{S}}f-f\big\|_{p}^{p}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big\|\int_{\widehat{\mathrm{H}}}\mathrm{M}_{2\eta_{\mathsf{S}}(y)}f\,dy-f\Big\|^{p}_{p}\ \lesssim_{p}\ \int_{\widehat{\mathrm{H}}}\big\|\mathrm{M}_{2\eta_{\mathsf{S}}(y)}f-f\big\|_{p}^{p}\,dy

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2^{p-1}\left(\int_{\widehat{\mathrm{H}}}\big\|\mathrm{M}_{2\eta_{\mathsf{S}}(y)}f-\mathrm{M}_{\eta(y)}f\big\|_{p}^{p}\,dy+\int_{\widehat{\mathrm{H}}}\big\|\mathrm{M}_{\eta(y)}f-f\big\|_{p}^{p}\,dy\right).

Now,
\eta(y)=\eta_{S}(y)+\eta_{[n]\setminus S}(y)

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\frac{k}{n}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}\int_{\widehat{\mathrm{H}}}\big\|\partial_{j}\mathrm{M}_{2\eta(y)}f\big\|^{p}_{p}\,dy+\Big(\frac{k}{n}\Big)^{\frac{p}{2}}\int_{\widehat{\mathrm{H}}}\big\|\mathrm{M}_{\eta(y)}f-f\big\|^{p}_{p}\,dy.

To prove (

(1.3)

\displaystyle\Big(\int_{\widehat{\mathrm{H}}}\big\|\mathrm{M}_{4m\eta_{\mathsf{S}}(y)}T_{[n]\setminus\mathsf{S}}f-T_{[n]\setminus\mathsf{S}}f\big\|_{p}^{p}\,dy\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}}

we get

\displaystyle\hskip-20.0pt\frac{1}{{n\choose k}}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathsf{S}\subset[n]\\
|\mathsf{S}|=k\end{subarray}}\int_{\widehat{\mathrm{H}}}\big\|\mathrm{M}_{4m\eta_{\mathsf{S}}(y)}T_{[n]\setminus\mathsf{S}}f-T_{[n]\setminus\mathsf{S}}f\big\|_{p}^{p}\,dy

\displaystyle\lesssim_{p}

\displaystyle\frac{m^{p}}{{n\choose k}}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathsf{S}\subset[n]\\
|\mathsf{S}|=k\end{subarray}}\int_{\widehat{\mathrm{H}}}\big\|\mathsf{E}_{[n]\setminus\mathsf{S}}h(y)\big\|^{p}_{p}\,dy

\displaystyle\lesssim_{p}

\displaystyle m^{p}\Bigg(\frac{k}{n}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}\int_{\widehat{\mathrm{H}}}\big\|(\partial_{j}\otimes\mathrm{id})h(y)\big\|_{p}^{p}\,dy+\Big(\frac{k}{n}\Big)^{\frac{p}{2}}\int_{\widehat{\mathrm{H}}}\|h(y)\|_{p}^{p}\,dy\Bigg)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle m^{p}\Bigg(\frac{k}{n}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}\int_{\widehat{\mathrm{H}}}\big\|\partial_{j}\mathrm{M}_{2\eta(y)}f\big\|_{p}^{p}\,dy+\Big(\frac{k}{n}\Big)^{\frac{p}{2}}\int_{\widehat{\mathrm{H}}}\|\mathrm{M}_{\eta(y)}f-f\|_{p}^{p}\,dy\Bigg).

Therefore, putting altogether we get

\displaystyle\frac{1}{{n\choose k}}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathsf{S}\subset[n]\\
|\mathsf{S}|=k\end{subarray}}\int_{\widehat{\mathrm{H}}}\big\|\mathrm{M}_{4m\eta_{\mathsf{S}}(y)}f-f\big\|_{p}^{p}\,dy

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle m^{p}\Bigg(\frac{k}{n}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}\int_{\widehat{\mathrm{H}}}\big\|\partial_{j}\mathrm{M}_{2\eta(y)}f\big\|^{p}_{p}\,dy+\Big(\Big(\frac{k}{n}\Big)^{\frac{p}{2}}+\frac{1}{m^{p}}\Big)\int_{\widehat{\mathrm{H}}}\big\|\mathrm{M}_{\eta(y)}f-f\big\|^{p}_{p}\,dy\Bigg).

This completes the proof for any
m\geq\sqrt{n/k}
, as imposed in the statement.
\square

Remark 1.1
.

A close inspection of the proof of Theorem
A
shows that one can exchange requirement
(RP-2)
by the existence of a family of invertible operators
\{\mathrm{M}_{y}\}_{y\in\widehat{\mathrm{H}}}
satisfying the following properties:

•

Product structure.
\mathrm{M}_{y}=\mathrm{M}_{y_{\mathsf{S}}}\mathrm{M}_{y_{[n]\setminus\mathsf{S}}}
.

•

Uniform boundedness.
\displaystyle\max_{|k|\leq 4m}\sup_{y\in\widehat{\mathrm{H}}}\big\|\mathrm{M}_{y}^{k}\big\|_{p\to p}<\infty
.

•

Symmetry.
\mathrm{M}_{y}^{k}f
and
\mathrm{M}_{y}^{-k}f
have the same distribution function for each
f
.

The above conditions are formally weaker than
(RP-2)
, and more importantly, they clarify the fact that the auxiliary group
\Gamma
is not necessary in the definition of
\mathrm{X}_{p}
-representable pairs. However, in the paper we have chosen to work with the stronger condition
(RP-2)
because in all of our examples we are able to identify the associated group
\Gamma
and this makes checking the conditions of Theorem
A
simpler.

Remark 1.2
.

In many of the cases of interest there holds

(1.4)

\int_{\widehat{\mathrm{H}}}\big\|\partial_{j}\mathrm{M}_{2\eta(y)}f\big\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{L}(\mathrm{G}))}^{p}\,dy\lesssim_{p}\big\|\mathrm{M}_{e_{j}}f-f\big\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{L}(\mathrm{G}))}^{p},

for each
j=1,\ldots,n
. This improves the conclusion of Theorem
A
to a truly metric inequality, which resembles the one in
[
7
]
more closely. This highlights the importance of the choice of the family of derivatives when checking conditions
(RP-1)
-
(RP-2)
. One case in which this holds is when derivatives and conditional expectations are related by

(1.5)

\partial_{j}=\mathrm{id}-\mathsf{E}_{\{j\}}\quad\mbox{for}\quad 1\leq j\leq n.

In turn, (
1.5
) holds when
\partial_{j}\mbox{\large$\chi$}_{w}(x)=\delta_{w_{j}\neq 0}\,\mbox{\large$\chi$}_{w}(x)
for any character
\mbox{\large$\chi$}_{w}
on
\widehat{\mathrm{H}}
.

2.
Intrinsic
\mathrm{X}_{p}
inequalities

In this section we explore intrinsic
\mathrm{X}_{p}
inequalities in the framework provided by Theorem
A
. The term “intrinsic” means here that there exists a natural inclusion of the index group
\mathrm{H}
into the
\mathrm{X}_{p}
-group
\mathrm{G}
, so there is no need to use an auxiliary group
\Gamma
other than
\mathrm{G}
. In our setting, this unfortunately forces us to work with
\mathrm{G}=\Gamma
abelian. An extension of Theorem
A
including noncommutative translations indexed by nonabelian groups
\mathrm{H}
would open a door to potential applications in the metric geometry of noncommutative
L_{p}
-spaces. Intrinsic
\mathrm{X}_{p}
inequalities will be complemented below with “transferred
\mathrm{X}_{p}
inequalities” which refer to those which admit some
\Gamma\neq\mathrm{G}
, including nonabelian
\mathrm{X}_{p}
-groups in the picture. We will illustrate this scenario in the context of free groups below. In the language of quantum group theory, intrinsic
\mathrm{H}
-translations are given by
\mathrm{G}
-comultiplications, while transferred
\mathrm{H}
-translations require more general
\mathrm{G}
-corepresentations in terms of
\Gamma
.

2.1.
Continuous
\mathrm{X}_{p}
inequalities

Let
\mathrm{H}=\mathrm{G}=\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, so that the dual groups are
n
-dimensional tori, which we identify with
[-1/2,1/2)^{n}
for convenience. We also pick
\Gamma=\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, and we explore two different choices for
\eta:\mathbb{T}^{n}\to\mathbb{T}^{n}
. First, we take the map
\eta(y)_{j}=y_{j}/4m
, so each component of
\eta
maps
\mathbb{T}^{n}
to
[-1/(8m),1/(8m))
. The following encodes what follows from Theorem
A
in this case.

Proposition 2.1
.

If
p\geq 2
,
k\in[n]
and
m\geq\sqrt{n/k}
, every
f:\mathbb{T}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
satisfies

\displaystyle\frac{1}{{n\choose k}}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathsf{S}\subset[n]\\
|\mathsf{S}|=k\end{subarray}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}\hskip-3.0pt\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}\big|f(x+y_{\mathsf{S}})-f(x)\big|^{p}\,dydx

\displaystyle\lesssim_{p}

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!m^{p}\hskip-3.0pt\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}\hskip-3.0pt\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}\hskip-3.0pt\Bigg\{\frac{k}{n}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}\Big|f\big(x\hskip-1.0pt+\hskip-1.0pt\frac{y_{j}e_{j}}{4m}\big)-f(x)\Big|^{p}\hskip-3.0pt+\Big(\frac{k}{n}\Big)^{\frac{p}{2}}\Big|f\big(x\hskip-1.0pt+\hskip-1.0pt\frac{y}{4m}\big)-f(x)\Big|^{p}\Bigg\}dydx.

Proof.
We start checking that
(\mathbb{Z}^{n},\mathbb{Z}^{n})
is an
\mathrm{X}_{p}
-representable pair. Taking the group
\Gamma=\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, it is clear that
(RP-2)
holds with our choice of
\eta
and multipliers given by the usual translations
\mathrm{M}_{\gamma}f(x)=f(x+\gamma)
which are unitary modulations at the Fourier side. Second, if
\chi_{w}=\exp(2\pi i\langle w,\cdot\rangle)
is the character associated with
w\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, we consider the differential operators

\partial_{j}\chi_{w}=\delta_{w_{j}\neq 0}\,\chi_{w}\quad\mbox{for}\quad j\in[n].

By
[
2
, Subsection 2.1]
, we have

\frac{1}{{n\choose k}}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathsf{S}\subset[n]\\
|\mathsf{S}|=k\end{subarray}}\big\|\mathsf{E}_{[n]\setminus\mathsf{S}}f\big\|^{p}_{L_{p}(\mathbb{T}^{n})}\lesssim_{p}\frac{k}{n}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}\|\partial_{j}f\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{T}^{n})}^{p}+\Big(\frac{k}{n}\Big)^{\frac{p}{2}}\|f\|^{p}_{L_{p}(\mathbb{T}^{n})}.

In particular, by Fubini’s theorem we get
(RP-1)
. Then, Theorem
A
yields

(2.1)

\displaystyle\hskip-5.0pt\frac{1}{{n\choose k}}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathsf{S}\subset[n]\\
|\mathsf{S}|=k\end{subarray}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}\hskip-3.0pt\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}\big|f(x+y_{\mathsf{S}})-f(x)\big|^{p}\,dydx

\displaystyle\lesssim_{p}

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!m^{p}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}\hskip-3.0pt\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}\frac{k}{n}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}\Big|\partial_{j}^{y}f\big(x+\frac{y}{2m}\big)\Big|^{p}+\Big(\frac{k}{n}\Big)^{\frac{p}{2}}\Big|f\big(x+\frac{y}{4m}\big)-f(x)\Big|^{p}dydx,

using the superscripts in the partial derivatives to indicate the variable over which differentiation is performed. It only remains to estimate (
2.1
) to establish the result. In order to do that, observe that (
1.5
) holds in this case. Applying it to
h(y)=f(x+y/2m)
and using the properties of translations we get

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}\hskip-3.0pt\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}\Big|\partial_{j}^{y}f\Big(x+\frac{y}{2m}\Big)\Big|^{p}dydx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}\hskip-3.0pt\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}\Big|f\Big(x+\frac{y}{2m}\Big)-\mathsf{E}_{\{j\}}^{y}f\Big(x+\frac{y}{2m}\Big)\Big|^{p}dydx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}\hskip-3.0pt\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}\Big|f\Big(x+\frac{y}{2m}\Big)-\int_{\mathbb{T}}f\Big(x+\frac{y_{[n]\setminus\{j\}}+te_{j}}{2m}\Big)dt\Big|^{p}dydx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}\hskip-3.0pt\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}\hskip-3.0pt\int_{\mathbb{T}}\Big|f\Big(x+\frac{y}{2m}\Big)-f\Big(x+\frac{y_{[n]\setminus\{j\}}+te_{j}}{2m}\Big)\Big|^{p}dtdydx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}\hskip-3.0pt\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}\hskip-3.0pt\int_{\mathbb{T}}\Big|f\Big(x+\frac{y_{j}e_{j}}{2m}\Big)-f\Big(x+\frac{te_{j}}{2m}\Big)\Big|^{p}dtdydx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}\hskip-3.0pt\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}\Big|f\Big(x+\frac{y_{j}e_{j}}{2m}\Big)-f(x)\Big|^{p}dydx\leq 2\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}\hskip-3.0pt\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}\Big|f\Big(x+\frac{y_{j}e_{j}}{4m}\Big)-f(x)\Big|^{p}dydx.

Inserting that in the outcome of Theorem
A
yields the assertion.
\square

2.2.
Cyclic groups with the word length

We now focus on a discrete inequality that is a strict generalization of (

MX
p

). Consider the inclusion
\eta_{\ell}:\mathbb{Z}_{2\ell}^{n}\to\mathbb{Z}_{8\ell m}^{n}
given by

(2.3)

\displaystyle\eta_{\ell}(x)=\big(\beta_{\ell}(x_{1}),\ldots,\beta_{\ell}(x_{n})\big),

where
\beta_{\ell}(y)=y-\ell
when
0\leq y\leq\ell-1
and
\beta_{\ell}(y)=y-(\ell-1)
when
\ell\leq y\leq 2\ell-1
.

Proposition 2.4
.

If
p\geq 2
,
k\in[n]
and
m\geq\sqrt{n/k}
, every
f:\mathbb{Z}_{8\ell m}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
satisfies

\displaystyle\hskip-60.0pt\frac{m^{-p}}{{n\choose k}}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathsf{S}\subset[n]\\
|\mathsf{S}|=k\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{(8\ell m)^{n}(2\ell)^{n}}\sum\limits_{x\in\mathbb{Z}_{8\ell m}^{n}}\sum\limits_{y\in\mathbb{Z}_{2\ell}^{n}}\big|f\big(x+4m\eta_{\ell}(y)_{\mathsf{S}}\big)-f(x)\big|^{p}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{p}

\displaystyle(4\ell)^{p-1}\frac{k}{n}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}\frac{1}{(8\ell m)^{n}}\sum\limits_{x\in\mathbb{Z}_{8\ell m}^{n}}\big|f(x+e_{j})-f(x)\big|^{p}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\Big(\frac{k}{n}\Big)^{\frac{p}{2}}\frac{1}{(8\ell m)^{n}(2\ell)^{n}}\sum\limits_{x\in\mathbb{Z}_{8\ell m}^{n}}\sum\limits_{y\in\mathbb{Z}_{2\ell}^{n}}\big|f(x+\eta_{\ell}(y))-f(x)\big|^{p}.

Proof.
Choose
\ell\geq 1
and take
(\mathrm{H},\mathrm{G})=(\mathbb{Z}_{2\ell}^{n},\mathbb{Z}_{8\ell m}^{n})
with auxiliary group
\Gamma=\mathrm{G}
. We want to check that this is an
\mathrm{X}_{p}
-representable pair. It is clear that the range of
\beta_{\ell}
is
-[\ell]\cup[\ell]\subset\mathbb{Z}_{8\ell m}^{n}
. When
\ell=1
, it recovers the map that sends
0
to
-1
and
1
to
1
. In addition, a simple observation yields that
-\beta_{\ell}(y)=\beta_{\ell}(2\ell-1-y)
which implies condition
(RP-2)
i). Next, denoting again characters by
\chi_{w}
for each
w\in\mathbb{Z}_{2\ell}^{n}
, we choose conditional expectations given by

\mathsf{E}_{[n]\setminus\mathsf{S}}\chi_{w}=\delta_{w\in\mathbb{Z}_{2\ell}^{\mathsf{S}}}\chi_{w}.

By
[
2
, Subsection 2.2]
and Fubini’s theorem,
(RP-1)
holds with derivatives given again by
\partial_{j}:\chi_{w}\mapsto\delta_{w_{j}\neq 0}\chi_{w}
. Finally, the map
\mathrm{M}_{\gamma}:\chi_{w}\mapsto\chi_{w}(\gamma)\chi_{w}
is a completely bounded multiplier on
L_{p}(\mathbb{Z}_{8\ell m}^{n})
, so the family
\{\mathrm{M}_{\gamma}\}_{\gamma\in\Gamma}
satisfies
(RP-2)
ii). The application of Theorem
A
yields

(2.4)

\displaystyle\hskip-40.0pt\frac{m^{-p}}{{n\choose k}}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathsf{S}\subset[n]\\
|\mathsf{S}|=k\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{(8\ell m)^{n}(2\ell)^{n}}\sum\limits_{x\in\mathbb{Z}_{8\ell m}^{n}}\sum\limits_{y\in\mathbb{Z}_{2\ell}^{n}}\big|f\big(x+4m\eta_{\ell}(y)_{\mathsf{S}}\big)-f(x)\big|^{p}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{p}

\displaystyle\frac{k}{n}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}\frac{1}{(8\ell m)^{n}(2\ell)^{n}}\sum\limits_{x\in\mathbb{Z}_{8\ell m}^{n}}\sum\limits_{y\in\mathbb{Z}_{2\ell}^{m}}\big|\partial_{j}^{y}f\big(x+2\eta_{\ell}(y)\big)\big|^{p}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\Big(\frac{k}{n}\Big)^{\frac{p}{2}}\frac{1}{(8\ell m)^{n}(2\ell)^{n}}\sum\limits_{x\in\mathbb{Z}_{8\ell m}^{n}}\sum\limits_{y\in\mathbb{Z}_{2\ell}^{n}}\big|f(x+\eta_{\ell}(y))-f(x)\big|^{p}.

Since (
1.5
) holds in this setting too, we get

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{x\in\mathbb{Z}_{8\ell m}^{n}}\sum\limits_{y\in\mathbb{Z}_{2\ell}^{n}}

\displaystyle\big|\partial_{j}^{y}f(x+2\eta_{\ell}(y))\big|^{p}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{x\in\mathbb{Z}_{8\ell m}^{n}}\sum\limits_{y\in\mathbb{Z}_{2\ell}^{n}}\big|f(x+2\eta_{\ell}(y))-\mathsf{E}_{\{j\}}^{y}f(x+2\eta_{\ell}(y))\big|^{p}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{x\in\mathbb{Z}_{8\ell m}^{n}}\sum\limits_{y\in\mathbb{Z}_{2\ell}^{n}}\Big|\frac{1}{2\ell}\sum\limits_{t\in\mathbb{Z}_{2\ell}}f(x+2\eta_{\ell}(y))-f(x+2\eta_{\ell}(y_{[n]\setminus\{j\}}+te_{j}))\Big|^{p}

\displaystyle\leq\sum\limits_{x\in\mathbb{Z}_{8\ell m}^{n}}\sum\limits_{y\in\mathbb{Z}_{2\ell}^{n}}\frac{1}{2\ell}\sum\limits_{t\in\mathbb{Z}_{2\ell}}\big|f(x+2\eta_{\ell}(y))-f(x+2\eta_{\ell}(y_{[n]\setminus\{j\}}+te_{j}))\big|^{p}

\displaystyle=(2\ell)^{n-2}\sum\limits_{x\in\mathbb{Z}_{8\ell m}^{n}}\sum\limits_{y_{j}\in\mathbb{Z}_{2\ell}}\sum\limits_{t\in\mathbb{Z}_{2\ell}}\big|f(x+2\beta_{\ell}(y_{j})e_{j})-f(x+2\beta_{\ell}(t)e_{j})\big|^{p},

by the definition of
\mathsf{E}_{[n]\setminus\mathsf{S}}
and convexity. Since
|\beta_{\ell}(y_{j})-\beta_{\ell}(t)|\leq 2\ell
, the translation invariance of the Haar measure yields

\displaystyle\hskip-100.0pt(2\ell)^{n-2}\sum\limits_{x\in\mathbb{Z}_{8\ell m}^{n}}\sum\limits_{y_{j},t\in\mathbb{Z}_{2\ell}}\big|f(x+2\beta_{\ell}(y_{j})e_{j})-f(x+2\beta_{\ell}(t)e_{j})\big|^{p}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(2\ell)^{n}(4\ell)^{p-1}\sum\limits_{x\in\mathbb{Z}_{8\ell m}^{n}}\big|f(x+e_{j})-f(x)\big|^{p},

which yields the result when inserted in (
2.4
). This completes the proof.
\square

3.
Transferred
\mathrm{X}_{p}
inequalities

Now we apply Theorem
A
to some pairs
(\mathrm{H},\mathrm{G})
with
\mathrm{H}=\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n}
and nonabelian
\mathrm{G}
. Clearly, the choices of the auxiliary group
\Gamma
and
\mathrm{G}
in each case must be related. We give the details for one particular choice and comment on a few more later. We choose
\Gamma=\mathbb{Z}_{8m}^{n}
and

\mathrm{G}=\mathbb{Z}_{8m}^{*n}:=\mathbb{Z}_{8m}*\ldots*\mathbb{Z}_{8m},

the free product of
n
copies of
\mathbb{Z}_{8m}
. For
j\in[n]
, denote the generator of its
j
-th factor by
g_{j}
. Elements
w\in\mathbb{Z}_{8m}^{*n}
can and will be described as reduced words in the form

w=g_{k_{1}}^{\ell_{1}}g_{k_{2}}^{\ell_{2}}\ldots g_{k_{r}}^{\ell_{r}}\quad\mbox{with}\quad k_{i}\neq k_{i+1}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\ell_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus 8m\mathbb{Z}.

Given
u\in\mathbb{Z}_{8m}^{n}
, we consider the character
\mbox{\large$\chi$}_{u}:\mathbb{Z}_{8m}^{*n}\to\mathbb{C}
determined by

\mbox{\large$\chi$}_{u}(w)=\exp\Bigg(\frac{2\pi i}{8m}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}u_{j}\sum\limits_{t:k_{t}=j}\ell_{t}\Bigg)\quad\mbox{for}\quad w=g_{k_{1}}^{\ell_{1}}g_{k_{2}}^{\ell_{2}}\ldots g_{k_{r}}^{\ell_{r}}.

It is clear that
\mbox{\large$\chi$}_{u}(w)\mbox{\large$\chi$}_{v}(w)=\mbox{\large$\chi$}_{u+v}(w)
. Next, we define the Fourier multipliers
\mathrm{M}_{u}:\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{Z}_{8m}^{*n})\rightarrow\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{Z}_{8m}^{*n})
determined by
\lambda(w)\mapsto\mbox{\large$\chi$}_{u}(w)\lambda(w)
for the left regular representation
\lambda
on
\mathbb{Z}_{8m}^{*n}
.

Lemma 3.1
.

\mathrm{M}_{u}
is a normal unital trace-preserving
*-
homomorphism on
\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{Z}_{8m}^{*n})
.

Proof.
\mathrm{M}_{u}
is clearly unital and

\mathrm{M}_{u}(\lambda(w)^{*})=\mathrm{M}_{u}(\lambda(w^{-1}))=\overline{\mbox{\large$\chi$}_{u}(w)}\ \lambda(w)^{*}=\mathrm{M}_{u}(\lambda(w))^{*}.

Next we claim that
\mathrm{M}_{u}(\lambda(ww^{\prime}))=\mathrm{M}_{u}(\lambda(w))\mathrm{M}_{u}(\lambda(w^{\prime}))
. It is clear that this identity holds when both
w
and
w^{\prime}
are powers of a fixed generator
g_{j}
. More precisely, we have

\mathrm{M}_{u}(\lambda(g_{j}^{\ell_{1}+\ell_{2}}))=\mathrm{M}_{u}(\lambda(g_{j}^{\ell_{1}}))\ \mathrm{M}_{u}(\lambda(g_{j}^{\ell_{2}})).

Now, let us consider two reduced words given by

w=g_{k_{1}}^{\ell_{1}}g_{k_{2}}^{\ell_{2}}\ldots g_{k_{r}}^{\ell_{r}}\quad\mbox{and}\quad w^{\prime}=g_{k_{1}^{\prime}}^{\ell_{1}^{\prime}}g_{k_{2}^{\prime}}^{\ell_{2}^{\prime}}\ldots g_{k_{s}^{\prime}}^{\ell_{s}^{\prime}}.

If
k_{r}\neq k_{1}^{\prime}
the claim trivially holds. If
k_{r}=k_{1}^{\prime}
and
\ell_{r}+\ell_{1}^{\prime}\not\equiv 0\ (\mbox{mod}\ 8m)
, then

\mathrm{M}_{u}(\lambda(ww^{\prime}))=\mathrm{M}_{u}(\lambda(g_{k_{1}}^{\ell_{1}}\ldots g_{k_{r-1}}^{\ell_{r-1}}))\ \mathrm{M}_{u}(\lambda(g_{k_{r}}^{\ell_{r}+\ell_{1}^{\prime}}))\ \mathrm{M}_{u}(\lambda(g_{k_{2}^{\prime}}^{\ell_{2}^{\prime}}\ldots g_{k_{s}^{\prime}}^{\ell_{s}^{\prime}}))

which yields the same conclusion. For the remaining case, we may write
ww^{\prime}
as

ww^{\prime}=\underbrace{g_{k_{1}}^{\ell_{1}}\ldots g_{k_{r-1}}^{\ell_{r-1}}}_{\rho}\underbrace{g_{k_{2}^{\prime}}^{\ell_{2}^{\prime}}\ldots g_{k_{s}^{\prime}}^{\ell_{s}^{\prime}}}_{\rho^{\prime}}.

Arguing as above, if
k_{r-1}\neq k^{\prime}_{2}
or
\ell_{r-1}+\ell_{2}^{\prime}\not\equiv 0\ (\mbox{mod}\ 8m)
we get

\displaystyle\mathrm{M}_{u}(\lambda(ww^{\prime}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{M}_{u}(\lambda(\rho))\mathrm{M}_{u}(\lambda(\rho^{\prime}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{M}_{u}(\lambda(\rho))\mathrm{M}_{u}(\lambda(g_{k_{r}}^{\ell_{r}+\ell_{1}^{\prime}}))\mathrm{M}_{u}(\lambda(\rho^{\prime}))\ =\ \mathrm{M}_{u}(\lambda(w))\mathrm{M}_{u}(\lambda(w^{\prime})).

One can iterate this process to deduce the claim. Thus,
\mathrm{M}_{u}
is a
*-
homomorphism on
\mathrm{span}\{\lambda(w):w\in\mathbb{Z}_{8m}^{*n}\}
. In particular, it is a completely positive unital map.
Moreover, it is trace-preserving since
\tau(\mathrm{M}_{u}(\lambda(w)))=\mbox{\large$\chi$}_{u}(w)\tau(\lambda(w))
and
\mbox{\large$\chi$}_{u}(e)=1
. Finally, note that for any
f,g\in\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{Z}_{8m}^{*n})
there holds

\displaystyle\tau(\mathrm{M}_{u}(f)g^{*})\!\!\!\!

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\tau\Big[\Big(\sum\limits_{w\in\mathbb{Z}_{8m}^{*n}}\widehat{f}(w\ \mbox{\large$\chi$}_{u}(w\ \lambda(w)\Big)\Big(\sum\limits_{\eta\in\mathbb{Z}_{8m}^{*n}}\widehat{g}(\eta)\ \lambda(\eta)\Big)^{*}\Big]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\sum\limits_{w\in\mathbb{Z}_{8m}^{*n}}\widehat{f}(w)\mbox{\large$\chi$}_{u}(w)\overline{\widehat{g}(w)}\,=\sum\limits_{w\in\mathbb{Z}_{8m}^{*n}}\widehat{f}(w)\overline{\mbox{\large$\chi$}_{-u}(w)}\overline{\widehat{g}(w)}\,=\,\tau(f(\mathrm{M}_{-u}g)^{*}).

Since
\mathrm{M}_{-u}
extends to a bounded map on
L_{1}(\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{Z}_{8m}^{*n}))
,
\mathrm{M}_{u}
is
w^{*}-
continuous.
\square

Before exploring
\mathrm{X}_{p}
inequalities for free groups, we shall need to use Theorem B in
[
2
]
with values in a noncommutative
L_{p}
-space over a QWEP von Neumann algebra
\mathcal{M}
. More precisely, given a mean-zero
f:\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n}\to\mathcal{M}
we claim that

(3.1)

\displaystyle\hskip-30.0pt\frac{1}{{n\choose k}}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathsf{S}\subset[n]\\
|\mathsf{S}|=k\end{subarray}}\big\|(\mathsf{E}_{[n]\setminus\mathsf{S}}\otimes\mathrm{id})f\big\|^{p}_{L_{p}(\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n};L_{p}(\mathcal{M}))}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{p}

\displaystyle\frac{k}{n}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}\big\|(\partial_{j}\otimes\mathrm{id})f\big\|^{p}_{L_{p}(\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n};L_{p}(\mathcal{M}))}+\Big(\frac{k}{n}\Big)^{\frac{p}{2}}\|f\|^{p}_{L_{p}(\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n};L_{p}(\mathcal{M}))},

where
\mathsf{E}_{[n]\setminus\mathsf{S}}
and
\partial_{j}
stand for the original conditional expectation and directional derivative used by Naor in
[
7
]
. To check that (
3.1
) holds, one can see that the proof in
[
2
]
goes through in the operator valued setting as long as dimension-free bounds hold for operator valued Riesz transforms
[
4
]
, which just requires to use the same argument together with Fubini’s theorem from
[
3
]
.

Proposition 3.2
.

If
p\geq 2
,
k\in[n]
and
m\geq\sqrt{n/k}
, every
f\in L_{p}(\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{Z}_{8m}^{*n}))
satisfies

\displaystyle\hskip-40.0pt\frac{1}{{n\choose k}}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathsf{S}\subset[n]\\
|\mathsf{S}|=k\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{2^{n}}\sum\limits_{\varepsilon\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n}}\big\|\mathrm{M}_{4m\varepsilon_{\mathsf{S}}}f-f\big\|^{p}_{L_{p}(\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{Z}_{8m}^{*n}))}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{p}

\displaystyle m^{p}\Bigg(\frac{k}{n}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}\big\|\mathrm{M}_{e_{j}}f-f\big\|_{p}^{p}+\Big(\frac{k}{n}\Big)^{\frac{p}{2}}\frac{1}{2^{n}}\sum\limits_{\varepsilon\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n}}\big\|\mathrm{M}_{\varepsilon}f-f\big\|_{p}^{p}\Bigg).

Proof.
We apply Theorem
A
to
(\mathrm{H},\mathrm{G})=(\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n},\mathbb{Z}_{8m}^{*n})
, so we need to check that it is an
\mathrm{X}_{p}
-representable pair. Condition
(RP-1)
follows from (
3.1
) since
\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{Z}_{8m}^{*n})
is a QWEP von Neumann algebra. Consider the auxiliary group
\Gamma=\mathbb{Z}_{8m}^{n}
with the inclusion
\eta
defined as in (
2.3
) for
\ell=1
. Condition
(RP-2)
i) is then automatically satisfied. Finally, according to Lemma
3.1
we know that
\mathrm{M}_{\gamma}
extends to a completely contractive map on
L_{p}(\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{Z}_{8m}^{*n}))
for any
1\leq p\leq\infty
. Therefore, the family
\{\mathrm{M}_{\gamma}\}_{\gamma\in\mathbb{Z}_{8m}^{n}}
can be used to check
(RP-2)
ii) and Theorem
A
leads to

\displaystyle\frac{1}{{n\choose k}}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathsf{S}\subset[n]\\
|\mathsf{S}|=k\end{subarray}}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2^{n}}\sum\limits_{\varepsilon\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n}}\big\|\mathrm{M}_{4m\varepsilon_{\mathsf{S}}}f-f\big\|^{p}_{L_{p}(\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{Z}_{8m}^{*n}))}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{p}m^{p}\Bigg(\frac{k}{n}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}\frac{1}{2^{n}}\sum_{\varepsilon\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n}}\big\|(\partial_{j}\otimes\mathrm{id})\mathrm{M}_{2\varepsilon}f\big\|^{p}_{p}+\Big(\frac{k}{n}\Big)^{\frac{p}{2}}\frac{1}{2^{n}}\sum\limits_{\varepsilon\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n}}\big\|\mathrm{M}_{\varepsilon}f-f\big\|_{p}^{p}\Bigg),

for every
f\in L_{p}(\mathbb{Z}_{8m}^{n};L_{p}(\mathbb{Z}_{8m}^{*n}))
. Finally, the computations for discrete derivatives from Subsection
2.2
imply that condition (
1.4
) holds, yielding the result.
\square

The example above is clearly not the only possible choice that we can make for the groups
\Gamma
and
\mathrm{G}
in the pair. In general, given abelian groups
\Gamma_{1},\ldots,\Gamma_{n}
and
\Gamma=\Gamma_{1}\times\Gamma\times\cdots\times\Gamma_{n}
so that there is a nice map

\eta:\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n}\to\widehat{\Gamma},

one can take
\mathrm{G}=\Gamma_{1}*\ldots*\Gamma_{n}
and construct a family of multipliers
\{\mathrm{M}_{\gamma}\}_{\gamma\in\widehat{\Gamma}}
as above. Once the properties of the family are checked, the above scheme yields a metric
\mathrm{X}_{p}
inequality for the pair
(\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n},\Gamma,\mathrm{G})
. For example, the interested reader can check that a representation in the spirit of Lemma
3.1
for
\Gamma=\mathbb{Z}^{n}
and
\mathrm{G}=\mathbb{F}_{n}
(the free group of
n
generators) holds. This yields the following free metric
\mathrm{X}_{p}
inequality in the free group algebra

\displaystyle\frac{1}{{n\choose k}}

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathsf{S}\subset[n]\\
|\mathsf{S}|=k\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{2^{n}}\sum\limits_{\varepsilon\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n}}\big\|\mathrm{M}_{4m\varepsilon_{\mathsf{S}}}f-f\big\|^{p}_{L_{p}(\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{F}_{n}))}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{p}m^{p}\Bigg(\frac{k}{n}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}\big\|\mathrm{M}_{e_{j}}f-f\big\|^{p}_{L_{p}(\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{F}_{n}))}+\Big(\frac{k}{n}\Big)^{\frac{p}{2}}\frac{1}{2^{n}}\sum\limits_{\varepsilon\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n}}\big\|\mathrm{M}_{\varepsilon}f-f\big\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{F}_{n}))}^{p}\Bigg).

4.
Metric consequences

We conclude by collecting a few metric consequences of the results in the previous sections. In
[
7
]
, Naor gave two sufficient hypothesis for a Banach space
\mathbb{X}
to be a metric
\mathrm{X}_{p}
space: a Banach
\mathrm{X}_{p}
inequality and operator valued dimension-free estimates for Riesz transforms. In particular, any mean-zero
f:\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n}\to\mathbb{X}
must satisfy

\frac{1}{2^{n}}\sum_{\varepsilon\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n}}\Big\|\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}\varepsilon_{j}(\partial_{j}\otimes\mathrm{id})f\Big\|^{p}_{L_{p}(\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n};\mathbb{X})}\simeq_{p}\big\|(\Delta^{-1/2}\otimes\mathrm{id})f\big\|^{p}_{L_{p}(\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n};\mathbb{X})}.

When
\mathbb{X}=L_{p}(\mathcal{M})
, the Banach
\mathrm{X}_{p}
inequality is
[
2
, Theorem 1.2]
, while the Riesz transform estimates can be found in
[
4
]
when
\mathcal{M}
is QWEP, as explained in the previous section. This latter fact had previously been announced for the Schatten classes
S_{p}
in
[
7
,
8
]
. The result that we get is the following:

Theorem 4.1

(Noncommutative
L_{p}
spaces are metric
\mathrm{X}_{p}
spaces)
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a
QWEP
von Neuman algebra. Then, if
p\geq 2
,
k\in[n]
and
m\geq\sqrt{n/k}
, every
f:\mathbb{Z}_{8m}^{n}\rightarrow L_{p}(\mathcal{M})
satisfies

\displaystyle\hskip-60.0pt\frac{m^{-p}}{{n\choose k}}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathsf{S}\subset[n]\\
|\mathsf{S}|=k\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{(16m)^{n}}\sum\limits_{x\in\mathbb{Z}_{8m}^{n}}\sum\limits_{\varepsilon\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n}}\big\|f(x+4m\varepsilon_{\mathsf{S}})-f(x)\big\|^{p}_{L_{p}(\mathcal{M})}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{p}

\displaystyle\frac{k}{n}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}\frac{1}{(8m)^{n}}\sum\limits_{x\in\mathbb{Z}_{8m}^{n}}\big\|f(x+e_{j})-f(x)\big\|^{p}_{L_{p}(\mathcal{M})}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\Big(\frac{k}{n}\Big)^{\frac{p}{2}}\frac{1}{(16m)^{n}}\sum\limits_{x\in\mathbb{Z}_{8m}^{n}}\sum\limits_{\varepsilon\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n}}\big\|f(x+\varepsilon)-f(x)\big\|^{p}_{L_{p}(\mathcal{M})}.

Theorem
4.1
provides a large class of examples of metric
\mathrm{X}_{p}
spaces beyond the class of (commutative)
L_{p}
spaces. Also, as shown in
[
7
]
, being a metric
\mathrm{X}_{p}
space implies lower estimates on the distorsion of bi-Lipschitz embeddings of nonlinear sets. Indeed, let
c_{L_{p}(\mathcal{M})}(\mathbf{X})
denote the smallest norm of a bi-Lipschitz map
\mathbf{X}\to L_{p}(\mathcal{M})
. Then we have the following:

Corollary 4.2
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be
QWEP
and
2<q<p
. Then

i)

\displaystyle c_{L_{p}(\mathcal{M})}\big([m]_{q}^{n}\big)\,\asymp_{p,q}\,\min\Big\{n^{\frac{(p-q)(q-2)}{q^{2}(p-2)}},m^{1-\frac{2}{q}}\Big\}
.

ii)

If
c_{L_{p}(\mathcal{M})}\big((L_{q},\|x-y\|_{q}^{\theta})\big)<\infty
, then necessarily
\theta\leq q/p
.

We refer to
[
7
,
9
]
for precise definitions on
[m]_{q}^{n}
and
\theta
-snowflakes. We end with a note about the optimality of the discrete cyclic inequalities from Section
2
. Given
m,n\geq 2
, we know from
[
9
, Lemma 3.1]
that there exists
h_{m}^{n}:\mathbb{Z}_{m}^{n}\rightarrow\{0,\ldots,4m\}^{2n}
such that

\Bigg(\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}\Big|\exp\Big(\frac{2\pi ix_{j}}{m}\Big)-\exp\Big(\frac{2\pi iy_{j}}{m}\Big)\Big|^{q}\Bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}\,\sim\,\big\|h_{m}^{n}(x)-h_{m}^{n}(y)\big\|_{q}

holds (up to absolute constants) for any
x,y\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}^{n}
and
q\geq 2
. Given a bi-Lipschitz map
g:[16m]^{2n}_{q}\rightarrow L_{p}
with bi-Lipschitz norm
D
, set
F=g\circ h_{4m,n}
. Then, arguing as in
[
9
, Theorem 1.14]
we get

\frac{1}{{n\choose k}}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathsf{S}\subset[n]\\
|\mathsf{S}|=k\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{(4m)^{2n}}\sum\limits_{x,y\in\mathbb{Z}_{4m}^{n}}\big\|F(x+y_{\mathsf{S}})-F(x)\big\|_{L_{p}}^{p}\,\gtrsim_{p}\,m^{p}k^{\frac{p}{q}}\big|e^{\frac{2\pi i}{4m}}-1\big|.

Next, we make the choices
k=\lceil n^{\frac{p(q-2)}{q(p-2)}}\rceil
and
m=\lceil n^{\frac{p-q}{q(p-2)}}\rceil
, which ensure that
m\gtrsim\sqrt{n/k}
, and so we can apply Proposition
2.4
to
F
in order to get the following statement: if
2<q<p
and
m,n,\ell\in\mathbb{N}
, there holds

c_{L_{p}}([m]_{q}^{n})\gtrsim_{p,q}\min\Big\{|e^{i\pi/\ell}-1|\,n^{\frac{(p-q)(q-2)}{q^{2}(p-2)}},m^{1-\frac{2}{q}}\Big\}\gtrsim_{p,q,\ell}\min\Big\{n^{\frac{(p-q)(q-2)}{q^{2}(p-2)}},m^{1-\frac{2}{q}}\Big\}.

Thus, up to constants (which get worse as
\ell\to\infty
) we recover the optimal distortions found in
[
7
]
. Also, Naor’s result for
\theta
-snowflakes in
[
7
]
follow from Proposition
2.4
.

Problem 4.3
.

According to
[
8
, Theorem 12]

(4.1)

c_{S_{p}}(\mathrm{X})\leq\dim(\mathrm{X})^{\frac{q}{2}(\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p})}

for every linear subspace
\mathrm{X}\subset S_{q}^{n}
and any
2<q<p<\infty
. Is there an embedding of
\mathrm{X}
into
S_{p}(\ell_{q}^{m})
with
m=\dim(\mathrm{X})
and constants independent of
m
? This is trivially true for row, column or diagonal subspaces and we know from
[
5
, Theorem 2]
that it holds for the full space
\mathrm{X}=S_{q}^{n}
with optimal
m
being
\dim(\mathrm{X})=n^{2}
. If this was the case for general
\mathrm{X}
, we would get
c_{S_{p}}(\mathrm{X})\lesssim c_{S_{p}}(S_{p}(\ell_{q}^{m}))
whose rightmost term is dominated by an operator space
(
matrix amplification
)
form of
c_{S_{p}}(\ell_{q}^{m})
. Using that
S_{p}
is a metric
\mathrm{X}_{p}
-space and arguing as above

(4.2)

c_{S_{p}}(\ell_{q}^{m})\asymp_{p,q}m^{\frac{(p-q)(q-2)}{q^{2}(p-2)}}.

Does
c_{S_{p}}(S_{p}(\ell_{q}^{m}))
behave like
c_{S_{p}}(\ell_{q}^{m})
? If so, one could guess that

(4.3)

c_{S_{p}}(\mathrm{X})\leq_{p,q}\dim(\mathrm{X})^{\frac{(p-q)(q-2)}{q^{2}(p-2)}}\quad\mbox{for every}\quad\mathrm{X}\subset S_{q}^{n}?

This would already improve (
4.1
) and it is best possible for
\mathrm{X}=\ell_{q}^{n}=\mathrm{diag}(S_{q}^{n})
. It is still open to find the optimal distortion
c_{S_{p}}(\mathrm{X})
for the full space
\mathrm{X}=S_{q}^{n}
. In this case, we have the following bounds

(4.4)

\mathrm{A}:=n^{\frac{(p-q)(q-2)}{q^{2}(p-2)}}\leq c_{S_{p}}(S_{q}^{n})\leq\min\Big\{n^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q}},n^{\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p}}\Big\}.

Indeed, the lower bound follows from (
4.2
) and the inclusion
\ell_{q}^{n}\subset S_{q}^{n}
. The upper bound follows easily using Hölder inequality. Note that (
4.3
) gives
c_{S_{p}}(S_{q}^{n})\leq\mathrm{A}^{2}
but (
4.4
) implies that no bound
c_{S_{p}}(S_{q}^{n})\leq\mathrm{A}^{\beta}
could be optimal for any
\beta>1
.

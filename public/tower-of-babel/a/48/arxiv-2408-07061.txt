Abstract
. We give an extension of a criterion of van der Corput on uniform distribution of sequences. Namely, we prove that a sequence x n x_{n} is uniformly distributed modulo 1 if it is weakly monotonic and satisfies the conditions Δ 2 ​ x n → 0 , n 2 ​ Δ 2 ​ x n → ∞ \Delta^{2}x_{n}\to 0,\quad n^{2}\Delta^{2}x_{n}\to\infty . Our proof is straightforward and uses a Diophantine approximation by rational numbers, while van der Corput’s approach is based on some estimates of exponential sums.

1.
Introduction

Let
X=\{x_{n},\,n=1,2,\ldots\}
be a sequence of real numbers. Denote by
A_{N}(X,I)
the counting function that is the number of elements
x_{n}
,
n=1,2,\ldots,N
, which fractional parts
\{x_{n}\}
are contained in certain interval
I=[a,b)\subset[0,1)
. A sequence
X
is said to be uniformly distributed modulo 1 if

(1)

\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{A_{N}(X,I)}{N}=b-a

for every interval
I\subset[0,1)
. The classical Weyl criterion (
[
5
]
*page Theorem 2.1) states that
a sequence
\{x_{n}\}
is uniformly distributed if and only if for any integer
h\neq 0
we have

(2)

\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\exp(2\pi ihx_{k})=0.

A classical example of a uniformly distributed sequence is
\{n\theta\}
, where
\theta
is an irrational number, that immediately follows from Weyl’s criterion. In fact, Weyl’s criterion is a powerful tool in the study of uniform distribution of sequences. It enables to reduce many problems in the uniform distribution theory to estimates of exponential sums. In many criteria of uniform distribution properties of the first and higher order differences of sequences play significant role. Those are defined inductively as follows:

(3)

\Delta x_{n}=x_{n+1}-x_{n},\quad\Delta^{k+1}x_{n}=\Delta(\Delta^{k}x_{n}),\quad k=1,2,\ldots.

The following criteria are well-known in the theory of uniform distribution.

Theorem A

(van der Corput,
[
10
]
)
.

If a sequence
\{x_{n}\}
satisfies the condition
\Delta x_{n}\to\theta
, where

\theta
is an irrational number, then
\{x_{n}\}
is uniformly distributed.

Theorem B

(van der Corput,
[
10
]
)
.

If a sequence
\{x_{n}\}
satisfies the conditions

(4)

\Delta^{k}x_{n}\searrow 0,\quad n\Delta^{k}x_{n}\to\infty\text{ as }n\to\infty

for some
k\geq 1
, then it is uniformly distributed.

Theorem C

(van der Corput,
[
7
]
)
.

A sequence
\{x_{n}\}
is uniformly distributed whenever

(5)

\displaystyle\Delta x_{n}\to\infty,\quad\Delta^{2}x_{n}\searrow 0,

(6)

\displaystyle\frac{n^{2}\Delta^{2}x_{n}}{(\Delta x_{n})^{2}}\to\infty\text{ as }n\to\infty.

Theorems
A
and
B
can be proved using either Weyl’s criterion or applying straightforward approach, while the proof of Theorem
C
follows from subtle quantitative estimates of certain exponential sums due to van der Corput
[
7
]
(see also
[
5
]
*pp. 15-18). In fact, van der Corput’s method of exponential sums developed in papers
[
7
,
8
,
9
,
10
]
have had applications in many different number-theoretic problems (see
[
3
,
4
,
6
]
). In the present paper we give an extension of Theorem
C
, applying a different approach, without using trigonometric sums. Namely, we divide our sequence
x_{n}
into segment groups
\{x_{j}:\,n_{k}<j\leq n_{k+1}\}
having
\varepsilon
-discrepancy, where integers
n_{k}
satisfy the bound
n_{k}<n_{k+1}<n_{k}(1+\varepsilon)
and are determined by an iteration as follows: having
n_{k}
, we apply a suitable Diophantine approximation to the first difference
\Delta x_{n_{k}}
, then
n_{k+1}
is defined depending on certain properties of this approximation. We use two key lemmas (Lemma
4
and
5
) in the estimations of discrepancies of segment groups.

Definition
.

A sequence of numbers
\{x_{n}\}
is said to be weakly-decreasing if
x_{n}\geq 0
and there exists a constant
\mathcal{K}\geq 1
such that

(7)

\max_{j>k}x_{j}\leq\mathcal{K}\min_{j\leq k}x_{j},\quad k=1,2,\ldots,

or equivalently
x_{j}\leq\mathcal{K}x_{n}
whenever
j>n
. We say that
\{x_{n}\}
is weakly-increasing if
\{-x_{n}\}
is weakly-decreasing.

Theorem
.

A sequence
\{x_{n}\}
is uniformly distributed if
\Delta^{2}x_{n}
is weakly-monotonic and satisfies the conditions

(8)

\Delta^{2}x_{n}\to 0,\quad n^{2}\Delta^{2}x_{n}\to\infty\text{ as }n\to\infty.

Remark 1.1
.

The condition
n^{2}\Delta^{2}x_{n}\to\infty
in the theorem is sharp, i.e. it may not be replaced by

(9)

n^{2}\omega(n)\Delta^{2}x_{n}\to\infty

for any sequence
\omega(n)\to\infty
. Namely, the sequence
x_{n}=\log n
is not uniformly distributed, but satisfies (
9
) whenever
\omega(n)\to\infty
.

2.
Notations and auxiliary lemmas

Lemma 1
.

Let
Y=\{y_{1},y_{2},\ldots,y_{m}\}
be a sequence such that both
y_{k}
and
\Delta y_{k}
are monotonic. Then for any interval
[a,b]
we have

(10)

\#(Y\cap[a,b])\leq\left(\frac{2(b-a)}{\min_{k}|\Delta^{2}y_{k}|}\right)^{1/2}+2.

Proof.

Without loss of generality we can suppose that both
y_{k}
and
\Delta y_{k}
are increasing and so
\Delta^{2}y_{k}\geq 0
. If
\#(Y\cap[a,b])\leq 2
, then (
10
) is immediate. So we can suppose
Y\cap[a,b]=\{y_{t+1},y_{t+2},\ldots,y_{t+s}\}
,
s\geq 3
. We have

(11)

\displaystyle b-a

\displaystyle\geq y_{t+s}-y_{t+1}

(12)

\displaystyle=\Delta y_{t+1}+\Delta y_{t+2}+\ldots+\Delta y_{t+s-1}

(13)

\displaystyle=(s-1)\Delta y_{t+1}+\sum_{j=1}^{s-2}(s-j-1)\Delta^{2}y_{t+j}

(14)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{(s-2)(s-1)}{2}\cdot\min_{k}\Delta^{2}y_{k}

(15)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{(s-2)^{2}}{2}\cdot\min_{k}\Delta^{2}y_{k},

which implies (
10
).
∎

Lemma 2
.

If a sequence
Y=\{y_{1},y_{2},\ldots,y_{m}\}
satisfies the conditions of Lemma
1
, then

(16)

\#Y=m\geq\left(\frac{2|y_{m}-y_{1}|}{\max\{\max_{k}|\Delta^{2}y_{k}|,\min_{k}|\Delta y_{k}|\}}\right)^{1/2}.

Proof.

Likewise to the proof of the previous lemma we can suppose that both
y_{k}
and
\Delta y_{k}
are increasing. So we have
\min_{k}|\Delta y_{k}|=\Delta y_{1}
. Thus we obtain

(17)

\displaystyle y_{m}-y_{1}

\displaystyle=(m-1)\Delta y_{1}+\sum_{j=1}^{m-2}(m-j-1)\Delta^{2}y_{j}

(18)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{m(m-1)}{2}\cdot\max\{\max_{k}|\Delta^{2}y_{k}|,\Delta y_{1}\}

(19)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{m^{2}}{2}\cdot\max\{\max_{k}|\Delta^{2}y_{k}|,\min_{k}|\Delta y_{k}|\}

and so (

) follows.
∎

Lemma 3
.

Let a sequence
Y=\{y_{1},y_{2},\ldots,y_{m}\}
be such that both
y_{k}
and
\Delta y_{k}
are increasing. Then for any two intervals
J=[a,b)
and
I=[c,d)
with
J<I
(i.e.

) and

we have

(20)

\frac{\#(Y\cap I)-1}{|I|}\leq\frac{\#(Y\cap J)+1}{|J|}.

Proof.

If
\#(Y\cap I)\leq 1
, then (
20
) is trivial. So we can suppose that
\#(Y\cap I)\geq 2
. One can check,

(21)

\frac{\#(Y\cap I)-1}{|I|}\leq\frac{1}{\min_{[y_{j},y_{j+1})\subset I}\Delta y_{j}}.

If
\#(Y\cap J)=0
, then
J\subset[y_{k},y_{k+1})
for some
k
. Then we can write

(22)

|J|\leq\Delta y_{k}\leq\min_{[y_{j},y_{j+1})\subset I}\Delta y_{j}

and combining this with (
21
), we obtain

(23)

\frac{\#(Y\cap I)-1}{|I|}\leq\frac{1}{|J|}=\frac{\#(Y\cap J)+1}{|J|}.

If
\#(Y\cap J)=1
, then, applying the condition
J<I
and that
\Delta y_{k}
is increasing, we can write

(24)

|J|\leq 2\min_{[y_{j},y_{j+1})\subset I}\Delta y_{j}.

Thus, using also (
21
), we obtain

(25)

\frac{\#(Y\cap I)-1}{|I|}\leq\frac{2}{|J|}=\frac{\#(Y\cap J)+1}{|J|}.

Hence, we can suppose
\#(Y\cap J)\geq 2
. One can check

(26)

\frac{\#(Y\cap J)+1}{|J|}\geq\frac{1}{\max_{[y_{j},y_{j+1})\cap J\neq\varnothing}\Delta y_{j}}\geq\frac{1}{\min_{[y_{j},y_{j+1})\subset I}\Delta y_{j}}\geq\frac{\#(Y\cap I)-1}{|I|}.

∎

Lemma 8
.

Let
Y_{k}\subset\mathbb{R}

. Then,

\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{8}n^{2}}\leq\Delta^{2}x_{n}<\varepsilon^{12}\text{ for all }n>n(\varepsilon)>\varepsilon^{-5}.

Then we fix
n>n(\varepsilon)
. According to Lemma
7
it is enough to prove that there exists an integer
m\leq 2n\varepsilon
, such that

(81)

\mathcal{D}\{x_{k}:\,n<k\leq n+m\}\lesssim\varepsilon.

Suppose that
p_{k}/q_{k}
is the sequence of continued fraction approximation of
\Delta x_{n}
. Namely, there are sequences of coprime integers
p_{k}
and
q_{k}
such that

(82)

\displaystyle\left|\Delta x_{n}-\frac{p_{k}}{q_{k}}\right|\leq\frac{1}{q_{k}q_{k+1}},

(83)

\displaystyle 1=q_{0}<q_{1}<q_{2}<\ldots

(see for example
[
1
]
chap. 24 or
[
2
]
chap. 3). We may find a unique integer
k=k(n)
such that
q_{k}\leq\varepsilon^{-4}<q_{k+1}
. Denote

p=p_{k}
,
q=q_{k}
,
q^{\prime}=q_{k+1}
. Hence we have

(84)

\displaystyle\left|\Delta x_{n}-\frac{p}{q}\right|\leq\frac{1}{qq^{\prime}},

(85)

\displaystyle q\leq\varepsilon^{-4}<q^{\prime}.

To prove (
81
) we will consider two possible cases of
q
. Besides, for the second case three different sub-cases will be discussed.

Case 1:
q>\varepsilon^{-1}
.
For any
0\leq j\leq q
we have

\Delta x_{n+j}=\Delta x_{n}+\sum_{i=n}^{n+j-1}\Delta^{2}x_{i}

and so, using (
80
), (
84
) and (
85
), we obtain

(86)

\displaystyle\left|\Delta x_{n+j}-\frac{p}{q}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\Delta x_{n}-\frac{p}{q}\right|+\sum_{i=n}^{n+q-1}\Delta^{2}x_{i}\leq\frac{1}{qq^{\prime}}+q\varepsilon^{12}

(87)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{q^{2}}+q\cdot\frac{1}{q^{3}}=\frac{2}{q^{2}}.

Therefore,

(88)

\displaystyle\left|x_{n+j}-x_{n}-\frac{jp}{q}\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\sum_{i=n}^{n+j-1}\left(\Delta x_{i}-\frac{p}{q}\right)\right|

(89)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=n}^{n+j-1}\left|\Delta x_{i}-\frac{p}{q}\right|\leq\frac{2}{q}

for all
1\leq j\leq q
. Thus, using assumption
q>\varepsilon^{-1}
, for the sequences

(90)

\displaystyle Y=\{y_{j}=x_{n+j}-x_{n}:\,j=1,2,\ldots,q\},

(91)

\displaystyle Z=\left\{z_{j}=\frac{jp}{q}:\,j=1,2,\ldots,q\right\}

we have
|y_{j}-z_{j}|<2/q<2\varepsilon
and, applying Lemma
6
, we get

(92)

\mathcal{D}(Y)\leq\mathcal{D}(Z)+4\varepsilon.

On the other hand, since
p
and
q
are coprime integers, we have

\bar{Z}=\left\{\left\{\frac{jp}{q}\right\}:\,j=1,2,\ldots,q\right\}=\left\{\frac{s}{q}:\,s=0,1,\ldots,q-1\right\}

and so
\mathcal{D}(Z)=\mathcal{D}(\bar{Z})=1/q<\varepsilon
. Thus, we obtain

(93)

\mathcal{D}\{x_{n+j}:\,j=1,2,\ldots,q\}=\mathcal{D}(Y)\leq\mathcal{D}(Z)+4\varepsilon<5\varepsilon.

Since
q\leq n\varepsilon
(see (
80
), (
85
)), we get (
81
) with
m=q
.

Case 2:
q\leq\frac{1}{\varepsilon}
.

We claim there is an integer
m\leq 2n\varepsilon^{2}
such that

(94)

\mathcal{D}\{x_{n+r},x_{n+r+q},\ldots,x_{n+r+(m-1)q}\}\lesssim\varepsilon,\quad m\leq 2n\varepsilon^{2},

for all
1\leq r\leq q
. Having (
94
), we may obtain (
81
). Indeed, applying Lemma
8
, from (
94
) it follows that

(95)

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}\{x_{n+1},x_{n+2},

\displaystyle\ldots,x_{n+qm}\}

(96)

\displaystyle\leq\max_{1\leq r\leq q}\mathcal{D}\{x_{n+r},x_{n+r+q},\ldots,x_{n+r+(m-1)q}\}\lesssim\varepsilon.

It remains just note that
mq\leq 2qn\varepsilon^{2}\leq 2n\varepsilon
.
In order to prove (
94
) consider the sequences

y_{k}(r)=-kp+x_{n+r+(k-1)q},\quad k=1,2,\ldots,

for the parameters
1\leq r\leq q
. Observe that

(97)

\mathcal{D}\{x_{n+r},x_{n+r+q},\ldots,x_{n+r+(m-1)q}\}=\mathcal{D}\{y_{k}(r):\,k=1,2,\ldots,m\}.

Let us state some properties of the sequences
y_{k}(r)
.

P1) First, observe that

(98)

\displaystyle\Delta y_{k}(r)

\displaystyle=y_{k+1}(r)-y_{k}(r)=-p+x_{n+r+kq}-x_{n+r+(k-1)q}

(99)

\displaystyle=-p+\sum_{j=0}^{q-1}\Delta x_{n+r+(k-1)q+j}

(100)

\displaystyle=-p+\sum_{j=0}^{q-1}\left(\Delta x_{n}+\sum_{i=n}^{n+r+(k-1)q+j-1}\Delta^{2}x_{i}\right)

(101)

\displaystyle=-p+q\Delta x_{n}+\sum_{j=0}^{q-1}\,\,\sum_{i=n}^{n+r+(k-1)q+j-1}\Delta^{2}x_{i}

(102)

\displaystyle=\alpha+\sum_{j=0}^{q-1}\,\,\sum_{i=n}^{n+r+(k-1)q+j-1}\Delta^{2}x_{i},

where

(103)

|\alpha|=|q\Delta x_{n}-p|<\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}<\varepsilon^{-4}\text{ (see }\eqref{a6},\eqref{a5}).

P2) From (
80
) and (
102
) it follows that

(104)

\displaystyle\Delta^{2}y_{k}(r)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{q-1}\,\,\sum_{i=n+r+(k-1)q+j}^{n+r+kq+j-1}\Delta^{2}x_{i}

(105)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{q^{2}}{(n+(k+2)q)^{2}\varepsilon^{8}}\gtrsim\frac{1}{n^{2}\varepsilon^{8}}\text{ if }k\leq 3n.

Since
\Delta^{2}x_{k}
is weakly-decreasing, one can easily check that so is
\Delta^{2}y_{k}(r)
with the same constant
\mathcal{K}
. Moreover, we have

(106)

\displaystyle\Delta^{2}y_{k}(r)\leq q^{2}\varepsilon^{12}\leq\varepsilon^{10},\quad k=1,2,\ldots,\text{(see }\eqref{x5}),

(107)

\displaystyle\Delta^{2}y_{k}(r)\leq\mathcal{K}\Delta^{2}y_{s}(r^{\prime})\text{ if }r^{\prime}+sq\leq r+kq,\,1\leq r,r^{\prime}\leq q.

P3) From (
102
) we can also see that

(109)

\displaystyle\Delta y_{k}(r^{\prime})\leq\Delta y_{k}(r)\leq\Delta y_{k+1}(r^{\prime}),\quad 0\leq r^{\prime}\leq r\leq q,

(110)

\displaystyle\Delta y_{k+1}(r)=\Delta y_{k}(r+q).

Then, using also the representations

\displaystyle y_{k}(r)-y_{s}(r)=\Delta y_{s}(r)+\Delta y_{s+1}(r)+\ldots+\Delta y_{k-1}(r),

\displaystyle y_{k}(0)-y_{s}(0)=\Delta y_{s}(0)+\Delta y_{s+1}(0)+\ldots+\Delta y_{k-1}(0),

we conclude

(111)

y_{k}(0)-y_{s}(0)\leq y_{k}(r)-y_{s}(r)\leq y_{k+1}(0)-y_{s}(0)-\Delta y_{s}(0).

P4) Note that all the terms in (
102
) are non-negative except
\alpha
, which can be either positive or negative.
Besides, using (
109
), one can check that
\Delta y_{k}(r)\nearrow\beta
, where
\beta
doesn’t depend on
r
(
\beta
can also be infinite). In the case
\beta>0
we may find an integer
h(r)\geq 0
such that

(112)

\displaystyle\Delta y_{k}(r)\leq 0

\displaystyle\hbox{ if }k\leq h(r),

(113)

\displaystyle\Delta y_{k}(r)\geq 0

\displaystyle\hbox{ if }k>h(r).

If
\beta\leq 0
, then we set
h(r)=\infty
. From (
109
) and (
110
), we can conclude that
h(r)
is non-increasing in
r
and
h(0)=h(q)+1
. So we have

(114)

h(0)\geq h(r)\geq h(0)-1,\quad 1\leq r\leq q.

Taking into account (
102
) and (
103
), so we can write

(115)

|\Delta y_{k}(r)|<|\alpha|<\varepsilon^{4},\text{ whenever }k\leq h(r).

We will consider the following three possible sub-cases of Case 2, applying some restrictions to the number
h(0)
.

Case 2.1:
h(0)>n\varepsilon^{2}
.
Choose
m=[n\varepsilon^{2}]-1<n/2
(see (
79
)). From (
114
) we get
h(r)>m
. So according to (
112
) the sequence
\{y_{k}(r):\,k=1,2,\ldots,m\}
satisfies the conditions of Lemma
1
, namely,
\Delta y_{k}(r)
is increasing and negative.
On the other hand, using (
105
), we can write

(116)

\min_{1\leq k\leq m}\Delta^{2}y_{k}(r)\gtrsim\frac{1}{n^{2}\varepsilon^{8}}.

First, applying Lemma
1
, we obtain

(117)

\#(\{y_{1}(r),y_{2}(r),\ldots,y_{m}(r)\}\cap[k,k+1])\lesssim\frac{1}{\sqrt{(n^{2}\varepsilon^{8})^{-1}}}=n\varepsilon^{4}.

Then, applying Lemma
4
(see also (
44
)) together with (
97
) and (
115
), we conclude

(118)

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}\{x_{n+r},x_{n+r+q},\ldots,x_{n+r+(m-1)q}\}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{D}\{y_{k}(r):\,k=1,2,\ldots,m\}

(119)

\displaystyle\lesssim\max_{1\leq j<m}|\Delta y_{j}(r)|+\frac{n\varepsilon^{4}}{m}\lesssim\varepsilon^{4}+\varepsilon^{2}\lesssim\varepsilon

that gives (
94
) .

Case 2.2:

1/\varepsilon\sqrt{\delta}<h(0)\leq n\varepsilon^{2}
, where

(120)

\delta=\Delta^{2}y_{h(0)-1}(0)\lesssim\varepsilon^{10}\text{ (see }\eqref{x70}).

In this case we will choose
m=h(0)-2
. Using the weakly-decreasing property of
\Delta^{2}y_{k}(r)
and relation (
107
), we can write

(121)

\displaystyle\min_{1\leq k\leq m}\Delta^{2}y_{k}(r)\geq\frac{\Delta^{2}y_{m}(r)}{\mathcal{K}}\geq\frac{\Delta^{2}y_{m+1}(0)}{\mathcal{K}^{2}}

(122)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad=\frac{\Delta^{2}y_{h(0)-1}(0)}{\mathcal{K}^{2}}=\frac{\delta}{\mathcal{K}^{2}}

By (
112
) we have
m<h(r)
. Therefore the sequence
\{y_{1}(r),y_{2}(r),\ldots,y_{m}(r)\}
satisfies the conditions of Lemma
1
, since
\Delta y_{k}(r)
is increasing and by (
114
)
\Delta y_{k}(r)\leq 0
for
k=1,2,\ldots,m
. Thus, applying Lemma
1
, we conclude

(123)

\#(\{y_{1}(r),y_{2}(r),\ldots,y_{m}(r)\}\cap[k,k+1])\lesssim\frac{1}{\sqrt{\delta}}.

Once again applying Lemma
4
(with (
44
)), (
115
) as well as the assumption

(124)

m=h(0)-2>1/\varepsilon\sqrt{\delta}-2\gtrsim 1/\varepsilon\sqrt{\delta},

we get

(125)

\displaystyle\{y_{k}(r):\,

\displaystyle k=1,2,\ldots,m\}

(126)

\displaystyle\lesssim\varepsilon^{4}+\frac{\max_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\#(\{y_{1}(r),y_{2}(r),\ldots,y_{m}(r)\}\cap[k,k+1])}{m}\lesssim\varepsilon

and so (
94
).

Case 2.3:
h(0)\leq\min\left\{\frac{1}{\varepsilon\sqrt{\delta}},n\varepsilon^{2}\right\}
.

Observe that

(127)

h(0)-1\leq h(r)\leq h(0)\leq\min\left\{\frac{1}{\varepsilon\sqrt{\delta}},n\varepsilon^{2}\right\}\text{ (see }\eqref{x58})

and the sequence

(128)

Y=\{y_{k}(r):\,h(0)+1\leq k\leq l=2[n\varepsilon^{2}]\}

satisfies the conditions of Lemmas
1
and
2
, since both

and
\Delta y_{k}(r)
are increasing in this range according to (
113
) and (
114
). We know that the sequence
\Delta^{2}y_{k}
is weakly-decreasing, so the sequence
Y
satisfies also conditions of Lemma
5
. Besides, we have

(129)

\displaystyle\min_{h(0)+1\leq k\leq l}|\Delta^{2}y_{k}(r)|\gtrsim\frac{1}{n^{2}\varepsilon^{8}},\,(\text{see }\eqref{x74})

(130)

\displaystyle\max_{h(0)+1\leq k\leq l}|\Delta^{2}y_{k}(r)|\leq\mathcal{K}\Delta^{2}y_{h(0)-1}(0)=\mathcal{K}\delta,(\text{see }\eqref{x67})

(131)

\displaystyle\min_{k\geq h(0)+1}\Delta y_{k}(r)=\Delta y_{h(0)+1}(r)

(132)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad=\Delta y_{h(0)-1}(r)+\Delta^{2}y_{h(0)-1}(r)+\Delta^{2}y_{h(0)}(r)

(133)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad\leq\Delta^{2}y_{h(0)-1}(r)+\Delta^{2}y_{h(0)}(r)\,(\text{see }\eqref{x28},\eqref{x59})

(134)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad\leq\mathcal{K}(\Delta^{2}y_{h(0)-1}(0)+\Delta^{2}y_{h(0)}(0))\,(\text{see }\eqref{x69})

(135)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad\leq\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{K}+1)\Delta^{2}y_{h(0)-1}(0)=\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{K}+1)\delta,

(136)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\text{ whenever }k\geq h(0)+1.

Using Lemma
1
with
[a,b]=[y_{h(r)+1}(r),y_{l}(r)],
as well as bounds (
127
) and (
129
), we obtain

(137)

y_{l}(r)-y_{h(r)+1}(r)\gtrsim(l-h(r))^{2}\cdot\frac{1}{n^{2}\varepsilon^{8}}\gtrsim(n\varepsilon^{2})^{2}\cdot\frac{1}{n^{2}\varepsilon^{8}}=\varepsilon^{-4}.

Then since sequence (
128
) is increasing, we may find an integer
m\leq l
such that

(138)

y_{m-1}(0)-y_{h(0)+1}(0)\lesssim\varepsilon^{-4}\lesssim y_{m}(0)-y_{h(0)+1}(0).

From (
111
) and (
138
) we get

(139)

\displaystyle y_{m-2}(r)-y_{h(0)+1}(r)\leq y_{m-1}(0)-y_{h(0)+1}(0)-\Delta y_{h(0)+1}(0)

(140)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\leq y_{m-1}(0)-y_{h(0)+1}(0)\lesssim\varepsilon^{-4},

(141)

\displaystyle y_{m}(r)-y_{h(0)+1}(r)\geq y_{m}(0)-y_{h(0)+1}(0)\gtrsim\varepsilon^{-4}.

From (
140
) and (
106
) it follows that

(142)

\displaystyle\Delta y_{m-3}(r)\leq\Delta y_{h(0)+1}(r)+\Delta y_{h(0)+2}(r)+\ldots+\Delta y_{m-3}(r)

(143)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad=y_{m-2}(r)-y_{h(0)+1}(r)\lesssim\varepsilon^{-4},

(144)

\displaystyle\Delta y_{m-2}(r)\leq\Delta y_{m-1}(r)=\Delta y_{m-3}(r)+\Delta^{2}y_{m-3}(r)+\Delta^{2}y_{m-2}(r)\lesssim\varepsilon^{-4}.

Thus, using (
140
) and (

), we obtain

(145)

y_{m}(r)-y_{h(0)+1}(r)=y_{m-2}(r)-y_{h(0)+1}(r)+\Delta y_{m-2}(r)+\Delta y_{m-1}(r)\lesssim\varepsilon^{-4}.

From bounds (
130
) and (
136
) we obtain

(146)

\max\left\{\max_{h(0)+1\leq k\leq l}|\Delta^{2}y_{k}(r)|,\min_{h(0)+1\leq k\leq l}|\Delta y_{k}(r)|\right\}\lesssim\delta.

Thus, applying Lemma
2
together with (
141
), we obtain

(147)

m^{2}\delta\geq(m-h(0))^{2}\delta\gtrsim y_{m}(r)-y_{h(0)+1}(r)\geq\varepsilon^{-4}

and then, combining the restriction
h(r)\leq\frac{1}{\varepsilon\sqrt{\delta}}
(see (
127
)), we obtain

(148)

m\geq\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}\sqrt{\delta}}>\frac{h(r)}{\varepsilon}\gtrsim\frac{h(0)}{\varepsilon}.

Applying Lemma
5
and bounds (
120
), (
141
), (
145
), we get

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}\{y_{h(0)+1}(r),y_{h(0)+2}(r),\ldots,y_{m}(r)\}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{y_{m}(r)-y_{h(0)+1}(r)}{m}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{y_{m}(r)-y_{h(0)+1}(r)}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\varepsilon^{-4}\cdot\varepsilon^{2}\sqrt{\delta}+\varepsilon^{2}\lesssim\varepsilon.

Finally, applying (
148
), we obtain

(149)

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}\{y_{1}(r),y_{2}(r),

\displaystyle\ldots,y_{m}(r)\}

(150)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{h(0)}{m}+\mathcal{D}\{y_{h(0)+1}(r),y_{h(0)+2}(r),\ldots,y_{m}(r)\}\lesssim\varepsilon.

Taking into account (
97
) and that
m\leq l\leq 2n\varepsilon^{2}
, this completes the proof of (
94
) and so the theorem.

The advantage of the

In the following we do such computations for the tiling number elliptic curves.
It can also be computed in terms of graph theoretic language

for the tiling number elliptic curves

3.1.1.

Let
m
be a square-free integer and
E^{(m)}
the elliptic curve over
{\mathbb{Q}}
defined by the Weierstrass equation
y^{2}=x(x-m)(x+3m)
.
Let
\Sigma
be the set of primes containing
2,\infty

and bad places for
E
, namely,
\Sigma=\{2,3,\infty\}
.
Let
S
be the set of primes dividing
6m\infty
,
and
\Sigma_{1}:=S\setminus\Sigma
.
Let
{\mathbb{Q}}(S,2)
be the subgroup
of
{\mathbb{Q}}^{\times}/({\mathbb{Q}}^{\times})^{2}
supported on
S
, or equivalently, the set of square-free integers with prime factors in
S
. Then elements in
{\mathrm{Sel}}_{2}(E^{(m)}/{\mathbb{Q}})
can be realized as curves
C_{\Lambda}/{\mathbb{Q}}
with
C_{\Lambda}({\mathbb{A}}_{\mathbb{Q}})\neq\emptyset
. Here for
\Lambda=(b_{1},b_{2})\in{\mathbb{Q}}(S,2)^{\oplus 2}
, define
C_{\Lambda}
to be

C_{\Lambda}:\qquad\begin{cases}b_{1}z_{1}^{2}-b_{2}z_{2}^{2}=mt^{2}\\
b_{1}z_{1}^{2}-b_{1}b_{2}z_{3}^{2}=-3mt^{2}\end{cases}

Note that
C_{\Lambda}({\mathbb{A}}_{\mathbb{Q}})\neq\emptyset
if and only if
C_{\Lambda}({\mathbb{Q}}_{v})\neq\emptyset
for all
v\in S
.
The four
2
-torsion points
O
,
(0,0)
,
(m,0)
, and
(-3m,0)
correspond to

(b_{1},b_{2})=(1,1)
,
(-3,-m)
,
(m,1)
, and
(-3m,-m)
.

For any
p\in\Sigma_{1}
,
C_{\Lambda}({\mathbb{Q}}_{p})\neq\emptyset
if and only if

\begin{cases}\left(\frac{b_{1}}{p}\right)=\left(\frac{b_{2}}{p}\right)=1,&\text{if }p\nmid b_{1}b_{2},\\
\left(\frac{m/b_{1}}{p}\right)=\left(\frac{b_{2}}{p}\right)=1,&\text{if }p\mid b_{1},p\nmid b_{2},\\
\left(\frac{-3b_{1}}{p}\right)=\left(\frac{-m/b_{2}}{p}\right)=1,&\text{if }p\nmid b_{1},p\mid b_{2},\\
\left(\frac{-3m/b_{1}}{p}\right)=\left(\frac{-m/b_{2}}{p}\right)=1,&\text{if }p\mid b_{1},p\mid b_{2}.\end{cases}

For
p\in\Sigma
, it turns out that
C_{\Lambda}({\mathbb{Q}}_{p})\neq\emptyset

if and only if
{\mathrm{loc}}_{p}(\Lambda)
is contained in certain subspace

W_{p}
of
({\mathbb{Q}}_{p}^{\times}/({\mathbb{Q}}_{p}^{\times})^{2})^{\oplus 2}

which only depends on the image of
m
in
{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}^{\times}/({\mathbb{Q}}_{p}^{\times})^{2}
.
For example,
if
3\nmid m
, then
C_{\Lambda}({\mathbb{Q}}_{3})\neq\emptyset
if and only if

3\nmid b_{2}
and

\begin{cases}\left(\frac{b_{2}}{3}\right)=1,&\text{if }3\nmid b_{1},\\
\left(\frac{-b_{2}m}{3}\right)=1,&\text{if }3\mid b_{1},\end{cases}

and if
3\mid m
, then
C_{\Lambda}({\mathbb{Q}}_{3})\neq\emptyset
if and only if

\begin{cases}\left(\frac{b_{1}}{3}\right)=\left(\frac{b_{2}}{3}\right)=1,&\text{if }3\nmid b_{1}b_{2},\\
\left(\frac{m/b_{1}}{3}\right)=\left(\frac{b_{2}}{3}\right)=1,&\text{if }3\mid b_{1},3\nmid b_{2},\\
\left(\frac{-m/3b_{1}}{3}\right)=\left(\frac{-m/b_{2}}{3}\right)=1,&\text{if }3\nmid b_{1},3\mid b_{2},\\
\left(\frac{-b_{1}/3}{3}\right)=\left(\frac{-m/b_{2}}{3}\right)=1,&\text{if }3\mid b_{1},3\mid b_{2}.\end{cases}

It’s easy to see that
C_{\Lambda}({\mathbb{R}})\neq\emptyset
if and and only if

\begin{cases}b_{1}b_{2}>0,\quad&\text{if }m>0,\\
b_{2}>0,&\text{if }m<0.\end{cases}

If
2\nmid m
, then it is easy to see that
if
C_{\Lambda}({\mathbb{Q}}_{2})\neq\emptyset
then
b_{1}
must be odd, and one may compute the precise condition for
C_{\Lambda}({\mathbb{Q}}_{2})\neq\emptyset
.
The followings are some examples.

•

In the case
m\equiv 3\ \mathrm{mod}\ 4
,
C_{\Lambda}({\mathbb{Q}}_{2})\neq\emptyset
if and only if

2\nmid b_{1}
and
b_{2}\equiv 1\ \mathrm{mod}\ 4
.

•

In the case
m\equiv 5\ \mathrm{mod}\ 8
,
C_{\Lambda}({\mathbb{Q}}_{2})\neq\emptyset
if and only if

b_{1}\equiv 1\ \mathrm{mod}\ 4
and
2\nmid b_{2}
.

•

In the case
m\equiv 1\ \mathrm{mod}\ 8
,
C_{\Lambda}({\mathbb{Q}}_{2})\neq\emptyset
if and only if

(b_{1}\ \mathrm{mod}\ 8,b_{2}\ \mathrm{mod}\ 8)=(1,1)
,
(1,5)
,
(3,2)
,
(5,3)
,
(5,7)
or
(7,6)
.
Equivalently,
2\nmid b_{1}

and
\left(\left[\frac{-1}{b_{1}}\right],\left[\frac{2}{b_{1}}\right],\left[\frac{-1}{b_{2}/2^{{\mathrm{ord}}_{2}(b_{2})}}\right],\left[\frac{2}{b_{2}/2^{{\mathrm{ord}}_{2}(b_{2})}}\right],{\mathrm{ord}}_{2}(b_{2})\right)^{\mathrm{T}}

lies in the right kernel of

\begin{pmatrix}1&1&1&0&0\\
1&0&0&0&1\end{pmatrix}
.

3.1.2.

We introduce some matrices with entries in
{\mathbb{F}}_{2}
.
If
n=\ell_{1}\cdots\ell_{k}
is an odd positive integer,
then define the matrix
A=A(n)
associated to
n

to be
A=(a_{ij})\in M_{k\times k}({\mathbb{F}}_{2})
,
where
a_{ij}=\left[\frac{\ell_{j}}{\ell_{i}}\right]:=\frac{1}{2}\left(1-\left(\frac{\ell_{j}}{\ell_{i}}\right)\right)

(the additive Legendre symbol) if
i\neq j
and
\sum_{j}a_{ij}=0
for all
i
,
namely
a_{ii}=\left[\frac{n/\ell_{i}}{\ell_{i}}\right]
.
For an integer
d
prime to
n
, define the column vector
z_{d}=z_{d}(n)
associated to
n

to be
z_{d}:=\left(\left[\frac{d}{\ell_{1}}\right],\cdots,\left[\frac{d}{\ell_{k}}\right]\right)^{\mathrm{T}}
.
Finally, denote
D_{d}:={\mathrm{diag}}(z_{d})
.
It’s easy to see that
A+A^{\mathrm{T}}=z_{-1}z_{-1}^{\mathrm{T}}+D_{-1}
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
A
be the matrix associated to
n
with
n\equiv 3\ \mathrm{mod}\ 4
.
Then
\rank(A+D_{-1})=\rank(A)+1
.

Proof.

Since
n
has odd number of prime factors
\equiv 3\ \mathrm{mod}\ 4
,
it’s easy to see that we also have
\sum_{i}a_{ij}=0
for all
j
.
Therefore

\rank(A+D_{-1})=\rank\begin{pmatrix}A+D_{-1}\\
z_{-1}^{\mathrm{T}}\end{pmatrix}=\rank\begin{pmatrix}A+D_{-1}+z_{-1}z_{-1}^{\mathrm{T}}\\
z_{-1}^{\mathrm{T}}\end{pmatrix}=\rank\begin{pmatrix}A^{\mathrm{T}}\\
z_{-1}^{\mathrm{T}}\end{pmatrix}=\rank(A)+1,

where the last equality holds since
the elements of
z_{-1}^{\mathrm{T}}
sum to
1

while the elements of any row of
A^{\mathrm{T}}
sum to
0
.
∎

Remark 3.2
.

When
n\equiv 3\ \mathrm{mod}\ 4
,
it’s known that
\rank_{4}{\mathrm{Cl}}({\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt{-n}))=\corank(A)-1=\corank(A+D_{-1})
.
Here for a matrix
M
, the

is the dimension
of the right kernel of
M
.
In particular,
g(n)\equiv\det(A+D_{-1})\ \mathrm{mod}\ 2

where

.

Lemma 3.10
.

Let
\Sigma
be a finite set consisting of
-1
and some primes of
{\mathbb{Q}}
,

be a prime not in
\Sigma

\Sigma^{\prime}:=\Sigma\cup\{q\}

Let

be an integer,

\psi_{k}:\Omega_{k,\Sigma^{\prime}}/S_{k}\to\Omega_{k+1,\Sigma}/S_{k+1}

be the unique map which makes the following diagram commutes

\textstyle{~S_{k}(\Sigma^{\prime})~\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{n\mapsto qn}
\textstyle{S_{k+1}(\Sigma)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\Omega_{k,\Sigma^{\prime}}/S_{k}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\psi_{k}}
\textstyle{\Omega_{k+1,\Sigma}/S_{k+1}}

where

and

are the set of positive square-free integers
coprime to
\Sigma^{\prime}
with
k
prime factors,
resp. coprime to
\Sigma
with
k+1
prime factors.
Let
\Omega_{0}
be a subset of
\Omega_{k+1,\Sigma}
stable by
S_{k+1}
-action.
Then

\left|\frac{\#\psi_{k}^{-1}(\Omega_{0})}{\#\Omega_{k,\Sigma^{\prime}}}-\frac{\#\Omega_{0}}{\#\Omega_{k+1,\Sigma}}\right|\leq 2^{\#\Sigma}\cdot\frac{\log(k+1)+13/12}{\sqrt{k+1}}.

Proof.

The map
\psi_{k}
can be given by the map

\widetilde{\psi}_{k}:\Omega_{k,\Sigma^{\prime}}\to\Omega_{k+1,\Sigma}

which is

\big((a_{ij})_{1\leq i<j\leq k},(z_{i}^{(p)})_{1\leq i\leq k,p\in\Sigma^{\prime}}\big)\mapsto\big((b_{ij})_{1\leq i<j\leq k+1},(w_{i}^{(p)})_{1\leq i\leq k+1,p\in\Sigma}\big),

where

b_{ij}=\begin{cases}a_{ij},&\text{if }1\leq i<j\leq k,\\
z_{i}^{(q)},&\text{if }1\leq i\leq k\text{ and }j=k+1,\end{cases}\qquad\text{and}\qquad w_{i}^{(p)}=\begin{cases}z_{i}^{(p)},&\text{if }1\leq i\leq k,\\
\left[\frac{p}{q}\right],&\text{if }i=k+1.\end{cases}

It’s clear that this map is injective, and whose image is the set
of
\big((b_{ij}),(w_{i}^{(p)})\big)
such that

w_{k+1}^{(p)}=\left[\frac{p}{q}\right]
for all
p\in\Sigma
.
By this way we view
\Omega_{k,\Sigma^{\prime}}
as a subset of
\Omega_{k+1,\Sigma}
,
and
\psi_{k}^{-1}(\Omega_{0})=\Omega_{0}\cap\Omega_{k,\Sigma^{\prime}}
.

Write
\Omega_{k+1,\Sigma}=\bigsqcup_{s=0}^{k+1}\Omega_{k+1,\Sigma,s}

where
\Omega_{k+1,\Sigma,s}
consists of elements

\big((b_{ij}),(w_{i}^{(p)})\big)
of
\Omega_{k+1,\Sigma}
such that

\#\{1\leq i\leq k+1\mid w_{i}^{(p)}=\left[\frac{p}{q}\right]\text{ for all }p\in\Sigma\}=s
.
Then
\Omega_{k+1,\Sigma,s}
is also a union of
S_{k+1}
-orbits, and

\#\Omega_{k+1,\Sigma,s}=2^{k(k+1)/2}\binom{k+1}{s}(2^{\#\Sigma}-1)^{k+1-s}.

It’s known that (see for example
[
17
]
, Theorem 4.12)
for
N\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 1}
,
0<p<1
and
\delta>0
, we have

\sum_{|n-Np|\geq N\delta}\binom{N}{n}p^{n}(1-p)^{N-n}\leq 2\exp(-2N\delta^{2}),

hence

R:=\sum_{|s-(k+1)/2^{\#\Sigma}|\geq(k+1)\delta}\frac{\#\Omega_{k+1,\Sigma,s}}{\#\Omega_{k+1,\Sigma}}\leq 2\exp(-2(k+1)\delta^{2}).

On the other hand,
if
\omega
is an element of
\Omega_{k+1,\Sigma,s}
,
consider the
S_{k+1}
-orbit
[\omega]
, it’s easy to see that

\frac{\#([\omega]\cap\Omega_{k,\Sigma^{\prime}})}{\#[\omega]}=\frac{s}{k+1}.

Therefore, if we
write
\Omega_{0,s}:=\Omega_{0}\cap\Omega_{k+1,\Sigma,s}
, then we have

\#(\Omega_{0}\cap\Omega_{k,\Sigma^{\prime}})=\sum_{s=0}^{k+1}\#(\Omega_{0,s}\cap\Omega_{k,\Sigma^{\prime}})=\sum_{s=0}^{k-1}\frac{s}{k+1}\#\Omega_{0,s}.

Hence

\displaystyle\#(\Omega_{0}\cap\Omega_{k,\Sigma^{\prime}})

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{|s-(k+1)/2^{\#\Sigma}|<(k+1)\delta}\frac{s}{k+1}\#\Omega_{0,s}+R\cdot\#\Omega_{k+1,\Sigma}

\displaystyle\leq(2^{-\#\Sigma}+\delta)\cdot\#\Omega_{0}+2\exp(-2(k+1)\delta^{2})\cdot\#\Omega_{k+1,\Sigma}

as well as

\displaystyle\#(\Omega_{0}\cap\Omega_{k,\Sigma^{\prime}})

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{|s-(k+1)/2^{\#\Sigma}|<(k+1)\delta}\frac{s}{k+1}\#\Omega_{0,s}\geq(2^{-\#\Sigma}-\delta)\cdot(\#\Omega_{0}-R\cdot\#\Omega_{k+1,\Sigma})

\displaystyle\geq(2^{-\#\Sigma}-\delta)\cdot\#\Omega_{0}-2\exp(-2(k+1)\delta^{2})\cdot\#\Omega_{k+1,\Sigma},

therefore

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\#\psi_{k}^{-1}(\Omega_{0})}{\#\Omega_{k,\Sigma^{\prime}}}-\frac{\#\Omega_{0}}{\#\Omega_{k+1,\Sigma}}\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\frac{\#(\Omega_{0}\cap\Omega_{k,\Sigma^{\prime}})-2^{-\#\Sigma}\cdot\#\Omega_{0}}{\#\Omega_{k,\Sigma^{\prime}}}\right|\leq\frac{\delta\cdot\#\Omega_{0}+2\exp(-2(k+1)\delta^{2})\cdot\#\Omega_{k+1,\Sigma}}{\#\Omega_{k,\Sigma^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{\#\Sigma}\big(\delta+2\exp(-2(k+1)\delta^{2})\big).

Taking

it’s easy to obtain the
desired result.
∎

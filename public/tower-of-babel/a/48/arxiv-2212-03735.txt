h ​ p hp -optimal interior penalty discontinuous Galerkin methods for the biharmonic problem
Z. Dong, L. Mascotto

Abstract
We prove h ​ p hp -optimal error estimates for interior penalty discontinuous Galerkin methods (IPDG) for the biharmonic problem with homogeneous essential boundary conditions. We consider tensor product-type meshes in two and three dimensions, and triangular meshes in two dimensions. An essential ingredient in the analysis is the construction of a global H 2 H^{2} piecewise polynomial approximants with h ​ p hp -optimal approximation properties over the given meshes. The h ​ p hp -optimality is also discussed for 𝒞 0 \mathcal{C}^{0} -IPDG in two and three dimensions, and the stream formulation of the Stokes problem in two dimensions. Numerical experiments validate the theoretical predictions and reveal that p p -suboptimality occurs in presence of singular essential boundary conditions. AMS subject classification : 65N12; 65N30; 65N50. Keywords : discontinuous Galerkin; optimal convergence; a priori error estimation; p p -version.



1
Introduction

The numerical approximation of solutions to the biharmonic problem is a challenging task tracing back to the 60ties
[
3
]
;
see also the later work
[
16
]
.
The main difficulty in designing standard Galerkin methods for this problem resides in the construction of global
H^{2}
conforming polynomial spaces
over the given meshes.
A possible way to circumvent this issue is based on using nonconforming finite element spaces.
The Morley element is the first and
one of the most popular among them;
see, e.g.,
[
28
,
38
]
.

Another option to avoid
H^{2}
conformity is to use

H^{1}
conforming piecewise polynomial spaces.
This approach goes under the name of
\mathcal{C}^{0}
-interior penalty discontinuous Galerkin method (
\mathcal{C}^{0}
-IPDG)
and has been a very active area of research over the last
20
years;
see, e.g.,

[
7
,
9
,
11
,
17
]
.
The continuity constraint can we be further weakened
leading to the use of fully discontinuous test and trial functions.
This approach goes under the name of interior penalty discontinuous Galerkin method (IPDG)
and was first investigated
for the biharmonic problem
by Baker
[
5
]

in the late 70ties;
amongst others, we recall the following contributions,
including the
p
- and
hp
-versions of the scheme:

[
20
,
14
,
15
,
23
,
29
,
30
,
35
]
.

Standard results in the theory of the IPDG for the biharmonic problem
state that the convergence is optimal with respect to the mesh size,
and one order and a half suboptimal with respect to the polynomial degree.
The reason for this is the use of polynomial inverse estimates
while handling the penalization of the bilinear form.
A milder suboptimality of half an order occurs also in the approximation
of elliptic partial differential equations of second order
but it can be removed under two assumptions:
(
i
) the essential boundary conditions are homogeneous;
(
ii
) the underlying mesh allows for the existence of a global
H^{1}
piecewise polynomial
with
hp
-optimal approximation properties.
This was first observed in
[
31
]

using the error analysis in the
L^{2}
norm
for the local discontinuous Galerkin method
and later analyzed in
[
33
]

using the error analysis in the energy norm for the IPDG.

The aim of this paper is to prove that an analogous result holds true using the IPDG for the biharmonic problem
with homogeneous essential boundary conditions.
We shall mainly focus on the Hessian-Hessian formulation in
[
11
]
,
and show that similar
hp
-optimal results are valid also for
the
\mathcal{C}^{0}
-IPDG for the biharmonic problem in two and three dimensions,
and the stream formulation of the Stokes problem in two dimensions.
More precisely,
the main result of this contribution is that the optimality with respect to the polynomial degree
can be recovered using conforming tensor product-type meshes
in two and three dimensions,
and triangular meshes in two dimensions.

The analysis hinges upon showing the existence
of global
H^{2}
piecewise polynomial
hp
-optimal approximants
over the given meshes.
On tensor product-type meshes,
we shall exhibit explicit
hp
-optimal bounds in the anisotropic Sobolev scale
that are fully explicit with respect to the Sobolev regularity index.
We are not aware of existing results in the literature covering the three dimensional case of this;
so, this topic is interesting
per se
.
On triangular meshes, such an
H^{2}
approximant
can be constructed following
[
36
]

but the error estimates depend on the Sobolev regularity index implicitly;
we are not aware of a counterpart of this approach on tetrahedral meshes.

If essential boundary conditions are nonhomogeneous,
for elliptic partial differential equations of second order,
it is known that the method is
p
-suboptimal if essential boundary conditions are singular;
see
[
19
]
.
A similar result might be proven for the biharmonic problem.
Showing this goes beyond the scopes of this work,
but we shall provide numerical evidence that indeed
p
-suboptimality occurs.

Notation.

Let
D
be a domain in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
d=1,2,3
.
We denote the Sobolev space of order
s\in\mathbb{N}
by
H^{s}(D)
.
Fractional order and negative order Sobolev spaces are defined using interpolation theory and duality, respectively.
We denote the Lebesgue space of square integrable functions over
D
by
L^{2}(D)
.
We endow the above spaces with the inner product
(\cdot,\cdot)_{s,D}
,
which induces the norm and seminorm
{\left\|{\cdot}\right\|}_{s,D}
and
{\left|{\cdot}\right|}_{s,D}
.
Further, given
\ell\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
and
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
we introduce the anisotropic Sobolev space:

\begin{split}H^{\ell,k}(D):=\Big\{v\in H^{\ell}(D)\Big|&{\left\|{\partial^{\alpha_{1}}_{x_{1}}\dots\partial^{\alpha_{d}}_{x_{d}}u}\right\|}_{0,D}<\infty\quad\forall\boldsymbol{\alpha}=(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{d})\in\mathbb{N}^{d}\text{ s. th.}\\
&|\boldsymbol{\alpha}|=k,\ \alpha_{j}\leq 2\ \forall j\in\{1,\dots,d\}\setminus\{J\}\text{ for one }J\in\{1,\dots,d\}\Big\}.\end{split}

(1)

The space
H^{\ell,k}(D)
contains
H^{\ell}
functions
with square integrable mixed derivatives of order
k
,
where such mixed derivatives have fewer
than three partial derivatives along all directions but one.

We introduce several weak differential operators:

\nabla
as the gradient;

\Delta=\nabla\cdot\nabla
as the Laplacian;

\Delta^{2}
as the bilaplacian;

D^{2}=\nabla\nabla^{T}
as the Hessian.

Given
p\in\mathbb{N}
,
let
\mathbb{P}_{p}(D)
and
\mathbb{Q}_{p}(D)
represent the spaces of polynomials of degree at most
p
over a domain
D

and degree at most
p
in each variable over a
d
-dimensional cube
D
, respectively.

We denote the scalar product between two vectors
\mathbf{u}
and
\mathbf{v}

in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
by
\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{v}
.
Further, we denote the entrywise product between two tensors
\mathbf{u}
and
\mathbf{v}
in
\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
by
\mathbf{u}:\mathbf{v}
.

Given two positive quantities
a
and
b
,
we use the short-hand notation “
a\lesssim b
”
if there exists a positive constant
c
independent of the discretization parameters
such that “
a\leq c\ b
”.
Moreover, we write “
a\approx b
” if and only if “
a\lesssim b
”
and “
b\lesssim a
”.

The continuous problem.

Given
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
d=2,3
, a bounded polygonal/polyhedral domain
with unit outward normal vector
\mathbf{n}_{\Omega}

and
f\in L^{2}(\Omega)
,
we consider the following biharmonic problem:
find
u:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
such that

\begin{cases}\Delta^{2}u=f&\text{in }\Omega,\\
u=0,\quad\mathbf{n}_{\Omega}\cdot\nabla u=0&\text{on }\partial\Omega.\end{cases}

(2)

Introduce

V:=H^{2}_{0}(\Omega),\qquad\qquad B(u,v)=\int_{\Omega}D^{2}u:D^{2}v\quad\forall u,v\in V.

A weak formulation of problem (
2
) reads

\begin{cases}\text{find }u\in V\text{ such that}\\
B(u,v)=(f,v)_{0,\Omega}\qquad\forall v\in V.\end{cases}

(3)

Problem (
3
) is well posed;
see, e.g.,
[
10
, Section 5.9]
.

Remark 1
.

We only consider homogeneous boundary conditions
as they allow for fewer cumbersome technicalities
when deriving
hp
-optimal error estimates with the IPDG.
However, the
hp
-optimal results that we shall discuss in this paper
can be extended to the case of piecewise smooth boundary conditions.
Moreover, handling natural boundary conditions does not hide additional technicalities
nor impact the optimal convergence rate in
p
;
see, e.g.,
[
8
]
.

2
Interior penalty discontinuous Galerkin methods for the biharmonic problem

In this section, we introduce the IPDG for problem (
3
).
More precisely, we introduce regular tensor product-type and triangular meshes in Section
2.1
;
Section
2.2
is devoted to design the method;
in Section
2.3
,
we discuss some properties of the bilinear forms involved in the method.

2.1
Meshes

We consider sequences
\{\mathcal{T}_{n}\}_{n}
of decompositions of
\Omega
into parallelograms/parallelepipeds in two and three dimensions,
and triangular meshes in two dimensions.
Henceforth, we shall refer to the case of parallelograms/parallelepipeds meshes
as tensor product-type meshes.
We denote the set of (
d-1
)-dimensional faces of each
\mathcal{T}_{n}
by
\mathcal{F}_{n}
.
We further distinguish interior and boundary faces
introducing
\mathcal{F}_{n}^{I}
and
\mathcal{F}_{n}^{B}
, respectively.
The set of vertices of
\mathcal{T}_{n}
is
\mathcal{V}_{n}
.

With each element
K\in\mathcal{T}_{n}
, we associate its diameter
h_{K}

and collect all the diameters in
\mathbf{h}:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{+}

defined as

\mathbf{h}:=\begin{cases}h_{K}&\text{if }\mathbf{x}\in K\in\mathcal{T}_{n},\\
h_{F}&\text{if }\mathbf{x}\in F\in\mathcal{F}_{n}.\\
\end{cases}

We denote the unit normal vector to any interior face
F

with respect to the global orientation of
F
by
\mathbf{n}
.
We always fix a outward pointing vector for boundary faces.

Throughout, we assume quasi-uniformity of the mesh
\mathcal{T}_{n}
, i.e.,
there exists
c_{\text{mesh}}>0
such that

c_{\text{mesh}}^{-1}h_{K_{1}}\leq h_{K_{2}}\leq c_{\text{mesh}}h_{K_{1}}\qquad\forall K_{1},\ K_{2}\in\mathcal{T}_{n}.

In what follows, we consider a uniform distribution of the polynomial degree
p\in\mathbb{N}
over
\mathcal{T}_{n}
.
Variable polynomial degrees can be used
[
32
,
15
]

but are not the main focus of this work.

Define the scalar broken Sobolev space

\begin{split}&H^{1}(\mathcal{T}_{n},\Omega):=\{v\in L^{2}(\Omega)\mid v_{|K}\in H^{1}(K)\},\\
&H^{\ell,k}(\mathcal{T}_{n}):=\{v\in L^{2}(\Omega)\mid v_{|K}\in H^{\ell,k}(K)\}\quad\forall\ell\in\mathbb{R}^{+},\ k\in\mathbb{N}.\end{split}

We introduce the jump
\llbracket\cdot\rrbracket:L^{2}(F)\to\mathbb{R}

and the average
\{\cdot\}:L^{2}(F)\to\mathbb{R}
operators
over a face
F\in\mathcal{F}_{n}
as follows:
if the face
F\in\mathcal{F}_{n}^{I}
is an interior face shared by the elements
K^{+}
and
K^{-}
,
given
v\in H^{1}(\mathcal{T}_{n},\Omega)
with restrictions over the elements
K^{+}
and
K^{-}
denoted by
v^{+}
and
v^{-}
, respectively,

\llbracket v\rrbracket_{F}(\mathbf{x})=\llbracket v\rrbracket(\mathbf{x}):=v^{+}(\mathbf{x})-v^{-}(\mathbf{x}),\qquad\{v\}_{F}(\mathbf{x})=\{v\}(\mathbf{x}):=\frac{1}{2}(v^{+}(\mathbf{x})+v^{-}(\mathbf{x}))\qquad\forall\mathbf{x}\in F.

On boundary faces
F\in\mathcal{F}_{n}^{B}
, we set
\llbracket v\rrbracket=\{v\}=v
.

The definition of the two operators above can be extended to vector and tensor functions.
We avoid using different notation, for the presentation’s sake.

For any differential operator introduced in Section
1
,
we denote its elementwise version associated with
\mathcal{T}_{n}

by adding a subscript
n
;
for instance,
\nabla_{n}
is the piecewise gradient over
\mathcal{T}_{n}
.

2.2
The method

For each mesh
\mathcal{T}_{n}
and polynomial degree
p
,
we introduce the space

V_{n}:=\{v_{n}\in L^{2}(\Omega)\mid v_{n}{}_{|K}\in\mathbb{P}_{p}(K)\;\forall K\in\mathcal{T}_{n}\}.

We endow
V_{n}
with the broken Sobolev norm and seminorm of order
s>0

{\left\|{\cdot}\right\|}_{s,\mathcal{T}_{n}}^{2}:=\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{n}}{\left\|{\cdot}\right\|}_{s,K}^{2},\qquad{\left|{\cdot}\right|}_{s,\mathcal{T}_{n}}^{2}:=\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{n}}{\left|{\cdot}\right|}_{s,K}^{2},

and introduce the lifting operator
\mathcal{L}:V_{n}\oplus V\to[V_{n}]^{d\times d}
,
d=2,3
, as

\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{L}(u_{n}):\mathbf{v}_{n}:=\int_{\mathcal{F}_{n}}\{\mathbf{n}\cdot(\nabla\cdot\mathbf{v}_{n})\}\llbracket u_{n}\rrbracket-\int_{\mathcal{F}_{n}}\{(\mathbf{v}_{n})\mathbf{n}\}\cdot\llbracket\nabla u_{n}\rrbracket\qquad\forall\mathbf{v}_{n}\in[V_{n}]^{d\times d}.

(4)

Let
\boldsymbol{\sigma}:\mathcal{F}_{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
and
\boldsymbol{\tau}:\mathcal{F}_{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{+}

two piecewise constant stabilization parameters, which we shall fix in what follows; see Section
2.3
below.
Given
D_{n}^{2}
the piecewise Hessian over
\mathcal{T}_{n}
,
we define the bilinear form
B_{n}:V_{n}\times V_{n}\to\mathbb{R}
as

\begin{split}B_{n}(u_{n},v_{n})&:=\int_{\Omega}D_{n}^{2}u_{n}:D_{n}^{2}v_{n}+\int_{\Omega}\left(\mathcal{L}(u_{n}):D_{n}^{2}v_{n}+\mathcal{L}(v_{n}):D_{n}^{2}u_{n}\right)\\
&\quad+\int_{\mathcal{F}_{n}}\left(\boldsymbol{\sigma}\llbracket u_{n}\rrbracket\llbracket v_{n}\rrbracket+\boldsymbol{\tau}\llbracket\nabla u_{n}\rrbracket\cdot\llbracket\nabla v_{n}\rrbracket\right)\qquad\forall u_{n},v_{n}\in V_{n}.\end{split}

(5)

We consider the following IPDG for the approximation of solutions to (
3
); see
[
15
]
:

\begin{cases}\text{find }u_{n}\in V_{n}\text{ such that}\\
B_{n}(u_{n},v_{n})=(f,v_{n})\qquad\forall v_{n}\in V_{n}.\end{cases}

(6)

Other dG formulations might be used
[
11
,
20
]
;
yet, we stick to (
6
) as it is the most similar to that of
[
33
]
.

Eventually, we define the natural norm associated with the bilinear form
B_{n}(\cdot,\cdot)
:
for all
v\in V_{n}\oplus V
,

\begin{split}{\left\|{v}\right\|}_{\dG}^{2}&:={\left\|{D_{n}^{2}v}\right\|}_{0,\Omega}^{2}+\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{n}}{\left\|{\boldsymbol{\tau}^{\frac{1}{2}}\llbracket\nabla v\rrbracket}\right\|}_{0,F}^{2}+\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{n}}{\left\|{\boldsymbol{\sigma}^{\frac{1}{2}}\llbracket v\rrbracket}\right\|}_{0,F}^{2}\\
&=:{\left\|{D_{n}^{2}v}\right\|}_{0,\Omega}^{2}+{\left\|{\boldsymbol{\tau}^{\frac{1}{2}}\llbracket\nabla v\rrbracket}\right\|}_{0,\mathcal{F}_{n}}^{2}+{\left\|{\boldsymbol{\sigma}^{\frac{1}{2}}\llbracket v\rrbracket}\right\|}_{0,\mathcal{F}_{n}}^{2},\end{split}

(7)

where

(u,v)_{0,\mathcal{F}_{n}}:=\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{n}}(u,v)_{0,F},\qquad{\left\|{u}\right\|}_{0,\mathcal{F}_{n}}^{2}:=(u,u)_{0,\mathcal{F}_{n}}\qquad\qquad\forall u,v\in V_{n}\oplus V.

2.3
Properties of the lifting operator, bilinear form, and right-hand side

Following
[
20
, Lemma 5.1]
,
we have the following continuity result for the lifting operator
\mathcal{L}(\cdot)
in (
4
).

Lemma 2.1
.

There exists a positive constant
c_{S}
independent of
\mathbf{h}
and
p

such that

{\left\|{\mathcal{L}(v_{n})}\right\|}_{0,\Omega}\leq c_{S}\left({\left\|{\left(\frac{p^{6}}{\mathbf{h}^{3}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\llbracket v_{n}\rrbracket}\right\|}_{0,\mathcal{F}_{n}}+{\left\|{\left(\frac{p^{2}}{\mathbf{h}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\llbracket\nabla v_{n}\rrbracket}\right\|}_{0,\mathcal{F}_{n}}\right)\qquad\forall v_{n}\in V_{n}.

Consequences of Lemma
2.1
are the coercivity and continuity of the dG bilinear form
B_{n}(\cdot,\cdot)

under suitable choices of the dG parameters
\boldsymbol{\sigma}
and
\boldsymbol{\tau}
,
namely, given
c_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}
,
c_{\boldsymbol{\tau}}>2c_{S}+\frac{1}{2}
,

\boldsymbol{\sigma}:=c_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}\frac{p^{6}}{\mathbf{h}^{3}},\qquad\boldsymbol{\tau}:=c_{\boldsymbol{\tau}}\frac{p^{2}}{\mathbf{h}}.

(8)

See, e.g.,
[
20
, Lemma 5.2]
.

Lemma 2.2
.

The bilinear form
B_{n}(\cdot,\cdot)
is coercive and continuous:

B_{n}(v,v)\geq\frac{1}{2}{\left\|{v}\right\|}_{\dG}^{2},\qquad B_{n}(u,v)\leq 2{\left\|{u}\right\|}_{\dG}{\left\|{v}\right\|}_{\dG}\qquad\qquad\forall u,\ v\in V_{n}\oplus V.

(9)

We also have the continuity of the right-hand side in (
6
).

Lemma 2.3
.

There exists a positive constant
c_{C}
independent of
\mathbf{h}
and
p

such that

(f,v)_{0,\Omega}\leq c_{C}{\left\|{f}\right\|}_{0,\Omega}{\left\|{v}\right\|}_{\dG}\qquad\forall v\in V_{n}\oplus V.

Proof.

The assertion follows from the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality
and Poincaré-Friedrichs type inequalities for piecewise
H^{2}
functions;
see, e.g.,
[
12
, Corollary
4.2
]
.
∎

The well posedness of method (
6
) follows combining
the coercivity of
B_{n}(\cdot,\cdot)
and the continuity of
(f,\cdot)_{0,\Omega}

with respect to the
{\left\|{\cdot}\right\|}_{\dG}
norm.

Theorem 2.4
.

Method (
6
) is well posed with a priori estimates

{\left\|{u_{n}}\right\|}_{\dG}\leq 2c_{C}{\left\|{f}\right\|}_{0,\Omega}.

Polynomial approximation results on tensor product-type meshes

Preliminary technical results.

We recall a result concerned with approximation properties of the
L^{2}
and
H^{1}
projectors in one dimension;
see, e.g.,
[
32
,
13
]
for details.
Given
\widehat{I}:=(-1,1)
,
let
\Pi^{0}_{p}:L^{2}(\widehat{I})\to\mathbb{P}_{p}(\widehat{I})

and
\Pi^{1}_{p}:H^{1}(\widehat{I})\to\mathbb{P}_{p}(\widehat{I})

be the

and

\mathbb{P}_{p}(\widehat{I})

for all

(q_{p},v-\Pi^{0}_{p}v)_{0,\widehat{I}}=0\quad\forall v\in L^{2}(\widehat{I}),\qquad\begin{cases}(q_{p}^{\prime},(v-\Pi^{1}_{p}v)^{\prime})_{0,\widehat{I}}=0\\
(v-\Pi^{1}_{p}v)(\pm 1)=0\end{cases}\quad\forall v\in H^{1}(\widehat{I}).

The following approximation estimates are valid;
see, e.g.,
[
32
, Chapter 3]

and
[
18
, Lemma 3.5]
.

Lemma 3.1
.

For all
p\in\mathbb{N}
,
given
u\in H^{k}(\widehat{I})

with
k\geq 1
and
s:=\min(k,p+1)
,
there exists a positive constant
c
independent of
p
and
k

such that

\begin{split}{\left\|{u-\Pi^{0}_{p}u}\right\|}_{0,\widehat{I}}&\leq cp^{-s}{\left|{u}\right|}_{s,\widehat{I}},\\
{\left\|{(u-\Pi^{1}_{p}u)^{\prime}}\right\|}_{0,\widehat{I}}&\leq cp^{-s+1}{\left|{u}\right|}_{s,\widehat{I}},\\
{\left\|{(u-\Pi^{0}_{p}u)^{\prime}}\right\|}_{0,\widehat{I}}&\leq cp^{-s+\frac{3}{2}}{\left|{u}\right|}_{s,\widehat{I}}.\end{split}

(10)

Next, we recall best approximation estimates of the elementwise
L^{2}
projector
\Pi^{0}_{p}:L^{2}(K)\to\mathbb{Q}_{p}(K)

with respect to the boundary

norm
on a

-dimensional tensor product-element

;

]

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
d=2,3
be a tensor product-type element,
i.e., a parallelogram if
d=2
and a parallelepiped if
d=3
,

h_{K}
its diameter,
and

,
k\geq 1
.
Then, for all
1\leq s\leq\min(p+1,k)
,
p\in\mathbb{N}
,
there exists a positive constant
c>0

independent of
h_{K}
,
k
, and
p
such that

{\left\|{u-\Pi^{0}_{p}u}\right\|}_{0,\partial K}\leq c\left(\frac{h_{K}}{p+1}\right)^{s-\frac{1}{2}}{\left\|{u}\right\|}_{s,K}.

Now, we discuss

-optimal approximation properties
by means of global

\mathcal{T}_{n}
.
We proceed in three steps.

H^{2}

We recall the existence of a polynomial matching the values of a given function and its derivative

-optimal

Lemma 3.3

Let
\widehat{I}:=(-1,1)

Then, for all

(\mathcal{P}_{p}^{1}u)(\pm 1)=u(\pm 1),\qquad\qquad(\mathcal{P}_{p}^{1}u)^{\prime}(\pm 1)=u^{\prime}(\pm 1).

(11)

Moreover, the following upper bounds are valid:
given

,

\begin{split}{\left\|{(u-\mathcal{P}_{p}^{1}u)^{\prime\prime}}\right\|}_{0,\widehat{I}}^{2}&\leq\frac{(p-s-1)!}{(p+s-1)!}{\left\|{u^{(s+2)}}\right\|}_{0,\widehat{I}}^{2},\\
{\left\|{(u-\mathcal{P}_{p}^{1}u)^{\prime}}\right\|}_{0,\widehat{I}}^{2}&\leq\frac{(p-s-1)!}{(p+s-1)!}\frac{1}{(p-1)p}{\left\|{u^{(s+2)}}\right\|}_{0,\widehat{I}}^{2},\\
{\left\|{u-\mathcal{P}_{p}^{1}u}\right\|}_{0,\widehat{I}}^{2}&\leq\frac{(p-s-1)!}{(p+s-1)!}c(p){\left\|{u^{(s+2)}}\right\|}_{0,\widehat{I}}^{2},\end{split}

(12)

where

is independent of

\begin{split}c(p)\approx p^{-4}.\end{split}

Proof.

We provide the details of the proof in Appendix

for completeness.
∎

[

, Corollary

]
,

-optimal approximation result
for the projector
\mathcal{P}_{p}^{1}
.

Corollary 3.4
.

Let
\widehat{I}:=(-1,1)
and

.
Then, there exists a positive constant
c_{1D}>0

Thus, we only need to derive estimates for the inconsistency term,

Proceeding as in
[

]

we set

\begin{split}B_{n}(u-u_{n},\xi)&=\int_{\mathcal{F}_{n}}\big\{\mathbf{n}\cdot(\nabla\cdot D_{n}^{2}\eta)-\mathbf{n}\cdot(\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{\Pi}^{0}_{p}D_{n}^{2}\eta)\big\}\llbracket\xi\rrbracket\\

Since

7

(

\begin{split}A_{1}&\lesssim\frac{h^{\frac{3}{2}}}{p^{3}}{\left\|{\nabla\cdot(D_{n}^{2}\eta-\boldsymbol{\Pi}^{0}_{p}D_{n}^{2}\eta)}\right\|}_{0,\mathcal{F}_{n}}{\left\|{\boldsymbol{\sigma}^{\frac{1}{2}}\llbracket\xi\rrbracket}\right\|}_{0,\mathcal{F}_{n}}\lesssim\frac{h^{\frac{3}{2}}}{p^{3}}{\left\|{\nabla\cdot(D_{n}^{2}u-\boldsymbol{\Pi}^{0}_{p}D_{n}^{2}u)}\right\|}_{0,\mathcal{F}_{n}}{\left\|{\xi}\right\|}_{\dG}.\end{split}

as the global counterparts follow immediately.
The triangle inequality implies

{\left\|{\partial_{x}(\partial_{x}^{2}u-\Pi^{0}_{p}\partial_{x}^{2}u)}\right\|}_{0,F}\leq{\left\|{\partial_{x}^{3}u-\Pi^{0}_{p-1}\partial_{x}^{3}u}\right\|}_{0,F}+{\left\|{\Pi^{0}_{p-1}\partial_{x}^{3}u-\partial_{x}\Pi^{0}_{p}\partial_{x}^{2}u}\right\|}_{0,F}=:Z_{1}+Z_{2}.

(30)

As for the term
Z_{1}
, Lemma
3.2
yields

Z_{1}\lesssim\left(\frac{h}{p}\right)^{s-\frac{3}{2}}{\left\|{\partial_{x}^{3}u}\right\|}_{s-1,K}\leq\left(\frac{h}{p}\right)^{s-\frac{3}{2}}{\left\|{u}\right\|}_{s+2,K}\qquad\forall s\geq 0.

(31)

As for the term
Z_{2}
, we use the polynomial inverse trace inequality
[
32
, eq. (4.6.4)]

(which gives an order suboptimality in
p
)
and the triangle inequality:

\begin{split}Z_{2}&\lesssim\frac{p}{h^{\frac{1}{2}}}{\left\|{\Pi^{0}_{p-1}\partial_{x}^{3}u-\partial_{x}\Pi^{0}_{p}\partial_{x}^{2}u}\right\|}_{0,K}\\
&\leq\frac{p}{h^{\frac{1}{2}}}\left[{\left\|{\partial_{x}^{3}u-\Pi^{0}_{p-1}\partial_{x}^{3}u}\right\|}_{0,K}+{\left\|{\partial_{x}(\partial_{x}^{2}u-\Pi^{0}_{p}\partial_{x}^{2}u)}\right\|}_{0,K}\right]=Z_{2,1}+Z_{2,2}.\end{split}

(32)

Using the approximation properties of the
L^{2}
projector yields

Z_{2,1}\lesssim\frac{p}{h^{\frac{1}{2}}}\left(\frac{h}{p}\right)^{s-1}{\left\|{\partial_{x}^{3}u}\right\|}_{s-1,K}\leq\frac{p}{h^{\frac{1}{2}}}\left(\frac{h}{p}\right)^{s-1}{\left\|{u}\right\|}_{s+2,K}.

On the other hand, standard manipulations entail

\begin{split}Z_{2,2}&\lesssim\frac{p}{h^{\frac{1}{2}}}\left[{\left\|{\partial_{x}(\partial_{x}^{2}u-\Pi^{0,y}_{p}\partial_{x}^{2}u)}\right\|}_{0,K}+{\left\|{\partial_{x}(\Pi^{0,y}_{p}\partial_{x}^{2}u-\Pi^{0,y}_{p}\Pi^{0,x}_{p}\partial_{x}^{2}u)}\right\|}_{0,K}\right]\\
&\leq\frac{p}{h^{\frac{1}{2}}}\left[{\left\|{\partial_{x}(\partial_{x}^{2}u-\Pi^{0,y}_{p}\partial_{x}^{2}u)}\right\|}_{0,K}+{\left\|{\partial_{x}(\partial_{x}^{2}u-\Pi^{0,x}_{p}\partial_{x}^{2}u)}\right\|}_{0,K}\right].\end{split}

Using the approximation properties of the
L^{2}
projection on the first term,
see Lemma
3.1
,
and (
10
) (which gives half an order suboptimality in
p
) on the second term imply

Z_{2,2}\lesssim\frac{p^{\frac{3}{2}}}{h^{\frac{1}{2}}}\left(\frac{h}{p}\right)^{s-1}{\left\|{\partial_{x}^{3}u}\right\|}_{s-1,K}\lesssim\frac{p^{\frac{3}{2}}}{h^{\frac{1}{2}}}\left(\frac{h}{p}\right)^{s-1}{\left\|{u}\right\|}_{s+2,K}.

We plug the estimates on
Z_{2,1}
and
Z_{2,2}
in (
32
) and arrive at

Z_{2}\lesssim\frac{p^{\frac{3}{2}}}{h^{\frac{1}{2}}}\left(\frac{h}{p}\right)^{s-1}{\left\|{u}\right\|}_{s+2,K},\qquad s\geq 0.

(33)

Inserting next (
31
) and (
33
) in (
30
) gives

{\left\|{\partial_{x}(\partial_{x}^{2}u-\Pi^{0}_{p}\partial_{x}^{2}u)}\right\|}_{0,F}\lesssim\frac{p^{\frac{3}{2}}}{h^{\frac{1}{2}}}\left(\frac{h}{p}\right)^{s-1}{\left\|{u}\right\|}_{s+2,K}=\frac{p^{\frac{5}{2}}}{h^{\frac{3}{2}}}\left(\frac{h}{p}\right)^{s}{\left\|{u}\right\|}_{s+2,K},\qquad s\geq 0.

In overall, also recalling the factor
\frac{h^{\frac{3}{2}}}{p^{3}}
in front of
T_{1}
,
we deduce

T_{1}\lesssim p^{-\frac{1}{2}}\left(\frac{h}{p}\right)^{s}{\left\|{u}\right\|}_{s+2,\Omega}.

(34)

As for the term
T_{2}
on the right-hand side of (
29
),
we use Lemma
3.2
and obtain

T_{2}\lesssim p^{-\frac{1}{2}}\left(\frac{h}{p}\right)^{s}{\left\|{u}\right\|}_{s+2,\Omega}.

(35)

Inserting (
34
) and (
35
) in (
29
) leads to

\sup_{\xi\in V_{n}}\frac{|B_{n}(u-u_{n},\xi)|}{{\left\|{\xi}\right\|}_{\dG}}\lesssim p^{-\frac{1}{2}}\left(\frac{h}{p}\right)^{s}{\left\|{u}\right\|}_{s+2,\Omega}\leq\left(\frac{h}{p}\right)^{s}{\left\|{u}\right\|}_{s+2,\Omega}.

(36)

A combination of (
25
), (
24
), and (
36
) yields the assertion.
∎

4.1
states that

6
) delivers
hp

3
)

,

4.2
Optimal convergence on tensor product-type meshes in weaker norms

For tensor product-type meshes,
we discuss
hp
-optimal error estimates in weaker norms
under the assumption that
\Omega
is convex.
Notably, we are interested in the
L^{2}
and broken-
H^{1}
error estimates.
Since analogous techniques are employed, we focus on
L^{2}
error estimates only.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
\mathcal{T}_{n}
be a quasi-uniform tensor product-type mesh of either parallelograms (2D) or parallelepipeds (3D)
as in Section
2.1
.
Let
u
and
u_{n}
be the solutions to (
3
) and (
6
), respectively.
Then, if
\Omega
is convex,
there exists a positive constant
c
independent of
h
and
p

such that

{\left\|{u-u_{n}}\right\|}_{0,\Omega}\leq c\left(\frac{h}{p}\right)^{2}{\left\|{u-u_{n}}\right\|}_{\dG}.

Together with Theorem
4.1
,
this implies
hp
-optimal convergence in the
L^{2}
norm
if additional regularity on
u
is granted.

Proof.

Consider the auxiliary problem: find
\Phi
such that

\begin{cases}\Delta^{2}\Phi=u-u_{n}&\text{in }\Omega,\\
u=0,\quad\mathbf{n}_{\Omega}\cdot\nabla u=0&\text{on }\partial\Omega.\end{cases}

In weak formulation, this problem reads

\begin{cases}\text{find }u\in V\text{ such that}\\
B(u,v)=(u-u_{n},v)_{0,\Omega}\qquad\forall v\in V.\end{cases}

(37)

Due to the convexity of
\Omega
, the solution
\Phi
to (
37
) belongs to
H^{4}(\Omega)
and the following regularity estimate holds true:

{\left\|{\Phi}\right\|}_{4,\Omega}\lesssim{\left\|{u-u_{n}}\right\|}_{0,\Omega};

(38)

see, e.g.,
[
22
]
and
[
26
, Theorem
6
, page
182
]

for the two and three dimensional cases, respectively.

Using (
37
),
splitting the integrals elementwise,
and integrating by parts imply

{\left\|{u-u_{n}}\right\|}_{0,\Omega}^{2}=(u-u_{n},\Delta^{2}\Phi)_{0,\Omega}=\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{n}}\left(-(\nabla(u-u_{n}),\nabla\Delta\Phi)_{0,K}+(u-u_{n},\nabla\Delta\Phi\cdot\mathbf{n}_{K})_{0,\partial K}\right).

Integrating by parts, and
using (
4
) and the smoothness of
\Phi
give

\begin{split}{\left\|{u-u_{n}}\right\|}_{0,\Omega}^{2}&=\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{n}}(D^{2}(u-u_{n}),D^{2}\Phi)_{0,K}+(\llbracket u-u_{n}\rrbracket,\{\nabla\Delta\Phi\cdot\mathbf{n}\})_{0,\mathcal{F}_{n}}\\
&\quad+(\llbracket\Phi\rrbracket,\{\nabla\Delta(u-u_{n})\cdot\mathbf{n}\})_{0,\mathcal{F}_{n}}-(\llbracket\nabla(u-u_{n})\rrbracket,\{D^{2}\Phi\cdot\mathbf{n}\})_{0,\mathcal{F}_{n}}\\
&\quad-(\llbracket\nabla\Phi\rrbracket,\{D^{2}(u-u_{n})\cdot\mathbf{n}\})_{0,\mathcal{F}_{n}}+(\boldsymbol{\sigma}^{\frac{1}{2}}\llbracket u_{n}\rrbracket,\llbracket v_{n}\rrbracket)_{0,\mathcal{F}_{n}}+(\boldsymbol{\tau}^{\frac{1}{2}}\llbracket\nabla u_{n}\rrbracket,\llbracket\nabla v_{n}\rrbracket)_{0,\mathcal{F}_{n}}.\end{split}

As, e.g., in
[
21
, Theorem
4.5
]
,
we write the right-hand side as a combination of
B_{n}(u-u_{n},\Phi)
and a reminder
R
.
Standard computations reveal that

\begin{split}&R=\left[(\llbracket u-u_{n}\rrbracket,\{\nabla\Delta\Phi\cdot\mathbf{n}\})_{0,\mathcal{F}_{n}}-(\llbracket\nabla(u-u_{n})\rrbracket,\{D^{2}\Phi\cdot\mathbf{n}\})_{0,\mathcal{F}_{n}}-(\mathcal{L}(u-u_{n}),\Pi^{0}_{p}D_{n}^{2}\Phi)_{0,\Omega}\right]\\
&\!+\!\left[(\llbracket\Phi\rrbracket,\{\nabla\Delta(u-u_{n})\cdot\mathbf{n}\})_{0,\mathcal{F}_{n}}\!-\!(\llbracket\nabla\Phi\rrbracket,\{D^{2}(u-u_{n})\cdot\mathbf{n}\})_{0,\mathcal{F}_{n}}\!-\!(\mathcal{L}\Phi,\Pi^{0}_{p}(D_{n}^{2}(u-u_{n})))_{0,\Omega}\right]\\
&=A+B.\end{split}

Due to the regularity of
\Phi
, we have
B=0
.
We deduce

{\left\|{u-u_{n}}\right\|}_{0,\Omega}^{2}=B_{n}(u-u_{n},\Phi)+R,

(39)

where

\begin{split}R&=(\llbracket u-u_{n}\rrbracket,\{\mathbf{n}\cdot\nabla(D^{2}\Phi-\Pi^{0}_{p}D^{2}\Phi)\})_{0,\mathcal{F}_{n}}-(\llbracket\nabla(u-u_{n})\rrbracket,\{\mathbf{n}\cdot(D^{2}\Phi-\Pi^{0}_{p}D^{2}\Phi)\})_{0,\mathcal{F}_{n}}.\end{split}

Thanks to the smoothness of
\mathcal{P}_{p}\Phi
,
where
\mathcal{P}_{p}
is as in Theorem
3.8
,
we can write

B_{n}(u-u_{n},\Phi)=B_{n}(u-u_{n},\Phi-\mathcal{P}_{p}\Phi).

In fact,
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{P}_{p}\Phi)=0
.
Using the continuity property (
9
),
the smoothness and the homogeneous essential boundary conditions of
\Phi-\mathcal{P}_{p}\Phi
,
the approximation properties of
\mathcal{P}_{p}
in (
22
),
and the stability property (
38
),
we deduce

\begin{split}B_{n}(u-u_{n},\Phi)&\lesssim{\left\|{u-u_{n}}\right\|}_{\dG}{\left\|{D_{n}^{2}(\Phi-\mathcal{P}_{p}\Phi)}\right\|}_{0,\Omega}\lesssim\left(\frac{h}{p}\right)^{2}{\left\|{u-u_{n}}\right\|}_{\dG}{\left\|{\Phi}\right\|}_{4,\Omega}\\
&\lesssim\left(\frac{h}{p}\right)^{2}{\left\|{u-u_{n}}\right\|}_{\dG}{\left\|{u-u_{n}}\right\|}_{0,\Omega}.\end{split}

(40)

We are left with estimating the remainder
R
on the right-hand side of (
39
).
To this aim, we proceed as in the proof of Theorem
4.1
.
More precisely, we apply the estimates (
34
) and (
35
) with
s=2

and use the stability bound (
38
) to deduce

R\lesssim{\left\|{u-u_{n}}\right\|}_{\dG}\left(\frac{h}{p}\right)^{2}{\left\|{\Phi}\right\|}_{4,\Omega}\lesssim\left(\frac{h}{p}\right)^{2}{\left\|{u-u_{n}}\right\|}_{\dG}{\left\|{u-u_{n}}\right\|}_{0,\Omega}.

(41)

The assertion follows combining (
40
) and (
41
) in (
39
).
∎

Remark 5
.

As customary, the convexity assumption in Theorem
4.2
can be relaxed by demanding that
the domain
\Omega
implies
H^{4}
regularity on the solution of the auxiliary problem (
37
).

4.3
Optimal convergence on triangular meshes

In this section, we provide the guidelines to extend
Theorems
4.1
and
4.2

to the case of triangular meshes.
As in the analysis above,

hp
-optimal convergence estimates follow from
the existence of an
H^{2}
conforming polynomial interpolant
over a given triangular mesh with
hp
-optimal approximation properties.
We undertake a different approach compared to the case of tensor product-type meshes
as the arguments therein employed do not apply on simplicial meshes.

In
[
36
]
, Suri constructed an interpolant
\mathcal{I}_{p}^{\rm BS}
of
H^{2}
functions
into the space of global
H^{2}
piecewise polynomials on triangular meshes.
Such an interpolant satisfies the following approximation properties:
there exists a positive constant
c
only depending on
\mathcal{T}_{n}
such that

{\left|{u-\mathcal{I}_{p}^{\rm BS}u}\right|}_{\ell,\Omega}\leq cp^{-(k-\ell)}{\left|{u}\right|}_{k,\Omega}\qquad\qquad\ell=0,1,2.

(42)

The interpolant
\mathcal{I}_{p}^{\rm BS}
is constructed generalizing to the
H^{2}
case
the techniques for the
H^{1}
case
developed by Babuška and Suri in
[
4
]
.
First, a polynomial with
p
-optimal approximation properties is constructed on each triangle.
Then, global
H^{2}
regularity is recovered
using an
H^{2}
stable lifting operator,
which preserves essential polynomial conditions on the boundary of a triangle.
So,
p
-optimal error bounds of the IPDG on triangular meshes for the biharmonic problem
follow using (
42
) in the analysis of
Section
4.1
.

We report the convergence results for triangular meshes.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
\mathcal{T}_{n}
be a quasi-uniform triangular mesh as in Section
2.1
.
Let
u
and
u_{n}
be the solutions to (
3
) and (
6
), respectively.
Assume that
u\in H^{s+2}(\Omega)
,
s:=\min(k,p-1)
,
p\geq 2
.
Then, there exists a positive constant
c

independent of
h
and
p

such that

{\left\|{u-u_{n}}\right\|}_{\dG}\leq c\left(\frac{h}{p}\right)^{s}{\left\|{u}\right\|}_{s+2,\Omega},\qquad\qquad{\left\|{u-u_{n}}\right\|}_{0,\Omega}\leq c\left(\frac{h}{p}\right)^{2}{\left\|{u-u_{n}}\right\|}_{\dG}.

In other words, for homogeneous essential boundary conditions,
method (
6
) is
hp
-optimal in the energy and
L^{2}
norms
also on triangular meshes.

Remark 6
.

The
p
-optimal interpolant in (
42
) is derived based on the trigonometric polynomials approximation theory.
The error bound is
p
-optimal with respect to the Sobolev index
k

but contains unknown constants depending on
k
;
this is due to the use of the Stein’s extension operator for Lipschitz domains
[
34
]
.
The corresponding bound cannot be used to derive exponential convergence for the
hp
-version of Galerkin methods
but is enough to study
p
-optimal algebraic convergence.
So, one of the reasons why we developed the theory of Section
3

is that the
hp
-optimal error estimates are explicit in the Sobolev index
k
on tensor product-type meshes.

Remark 7
.

To the best of our knowledge, so far,
the Suri-type
p
-optimal interpolation operator in (
42
)
can be constructed in two dimensions only.
There are two main reasons for this fact:
(
i
) an explicit construction of general order
H^{2}
conforming piecewise polynomial spaces on tetrahedral meshes
is still missing;
(
ii
)
H^{2}
stable lifting operators for tetrahedra are also unknown.
Notwithstanding, it is possible to use
H^{1}
-conforming
hp
-optimal polynomial interpolation operator on tetrahedral meshes.
The resulting error bound for the IPDG is then suboptimal only by half an order of
p
,
thus improving the expected one and a half an order suboptimality.

Remark 8
.

There exist at least other two different constructions for
H^{2}
stable lifting operators.
In
[
27
]
, an
H^{2}
stable lifting operator has been derived to prove the
p
-robustness of the inf-sup constant
in H(div)-FEM for the Stokes problem in two dimensions.
In
[
1
, Theorem
2.2
]
,
the existence of an
H^{2}
stable lifting operator was shown,
which preserves essential polynomial conditions on the boundary of a triangle
to design preconditioners for high-order
H^{2}
FEM on triangular meshes
[
2
]
.

5
Convergence for other IPDG formulations of the biharmonic problem

In this section, we discuss other IPDG formulations that can be proven to be
hp
-optimal
using similar techniques to those of Section
4
.

5.1
\mathcal{C}^{0}
-IPDG for the biharmonic problem

In this section, we consider
\mathcal{C}^{0}
-IPDG for the biharmonic problem;
see
[
17
,
11
]
.
Given the space
V_{n}
of piecewise continuous polynomials over a mesh
\mathcal{T}_{n}
as in Section
2.1
,
we introduce the lifting operator
\mathcal{L}:V_{n}\oplus V\to[V_{n}]^{d\times d}
as

\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{L}(u_{n}):\mathbf{v}_{n}:=-\int_{\mathcal{F}_{n}}(\{(\mathbf{v}_{n})\mathbf{n}\}\cdot\mathbf{n})\;(\llbracket\nabla u_{n}\rrbracket\cdot\mathbf{n})\qquad\forall\mathbf{v}_{n}\in[V_{n}]^{d\times d}

and the discrete bilinear form

\begin{split}B_{n}(u_{n},v_{n})&:=\int_{\Omega}D_{n}^{2}u_{n}:D_{n}^{2}v_{n}+\int_{\Omega}\left(\mathcal{L}(u_{n}):D_{n}^{2}v_{n}+\mathcal{L}(v_{n}):D_{n}^{2}u_{n}\right)\\
&\quad+\int_{\mathcal{F}_{n}}\boldsymbol{\tau}(\llbracket\nabla u_{n}\rrbracket\cdot\mathbf{n})(\llbracket\nabla v_{n}\rrbracket\cdot\mathbf{n})\qquad\forall u_{n},v_{n}\in V_{n}.\end{split}

(43)

With an abuse of notation,
we denote the norm induced by the bilinear form in (
43
) by
{\left\|{\cdot}\right\|}_{\dG}
.

The bilinear form
B_{n}(\cdot,\cdot)
is equal to that in (
5
)
without the scalar jump terms.
Moreover, the jump of the gradients appear only along the normal direction to the faces.
Thus,
p
-optimal error estimates follow as
(and are in fact easier to prove than) in Section
4
.

For completeness, we report the convergence result.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
\mathcal{T}_{n}
be either a quasi-uniform triangular mesh,
or a quasi-uniform tensor product-type mesh of either parallelograms (2D) or parallelepipeds (3D)
as in Section
2.1
.
Let
u
and
u_{n}
be the solutions to (
3
) and the corresponding
\mathcal{C}^{0}
-IPDG, respectively.
Assume that
u\in H^{s+2}(\Omega)
,
s:=\min(k,p-1)
,
p\geq 2
.
Then, there exists a positive constant
c

independent of
h
, and
p

such that

{\left\|{u-u_{n}}\right\|}_{\dG}\leq c\left(\frac{h}{p}\right)^{s}{\left\|{u}\right\|}_{s+2,\Omega},\qquad\qquad{\left\|{u-u_{n}}\right\|}_{0,\Omega}\leq c\left(\frac{h}{p}\right)^{2}{\left\|{u-u_{n}}\right\|}_{\dG}.

5.2
Divergence-conforming DG methods for the stream formulation of the Stokes problem in two dimensions

In this section,
we discuss how to extend the optimal results of Section
4

to divergence-conforming DG methods for
the stream formulation of the Stokes problem
in two dimensions;

see
[
25
,
37
]
.

We start with the standard formulation of the Stokes problem in two dimensions:
given a polygonal domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}

and
\mathbf{f}\in[L^{2}(\Omega)]^{2}
,
we seek
\widetilde{\mathbf{u}}\in[H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)]^{2}

and
\rho\in L^{2}_{0}(\Omega)
that solve weakly

\begin{cases}-\Delta\widetilde{\mathbf{u}}+\nabla\rho=\mathbf{f}&\text{in }\Omega,\\
\nabla\cdot\widetilde{\mathbf{u}}=0&\text{in }\Omega,\\
\widetilde{\mathbf{u}}=\mathbf{0}&\text{on }\partial\Omega.\end{cases}

(44)

The velocity
\widetilde{\mathbf{u}}
is divergence free.
So, there exists
\psi\in H^{2}_{0}(\Omega)
such that

\widetilde{\mathbf{u}}=\nabla\times\psi
.

Introduce the Stokes kernel

\mathbf{Z}:=\left\{\mathbf{v}\in[H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)]^{2}\ \middle|\ \div\mathbf{v}=0\right\}.

Testing the first equation in (
44
)
with
\widetilde{\mathbf{v}}\in\mathbf{Z}
gives

(\nabla\widetilde{\mathbf{u}},\nabla\widetilde{\mathbf{v}})_{0,\Omega}=(\nabla\widetilde{\mathbf{u}},\nabla\widetilde{\mathbf{v}})_{0,\Omega}+(\nabla\rho,\widetilde{\mathbf{v}})_{0,\Omega}=(\mathbf{f},\widetilde{\mathbf{v}})_{0,\Omega}.

Since
\widetilde{\mathbf{v}}\in\mathbf{Z}
,
there exists
\phi\in H^{2}_{0}(\Omega)
such that

\widetilde{\mathbf{v}}=\nabla\times\phi
.
We deduce the stream formulation of the Stokes problem in (
44
):

(\nabla\ (\nabla\times\psi),\nabla\ (\nabla\times\phi))_{0,\Omega}=(\mathbf{f},\nabla\times\phi)_{0,\Omega}\qquad\forall\phi\in H^{2}_{0}(\Omega).

(45)

This is a weak formulation of the biharmonic problem (
2
)
which is alternative to that in (
3
).
We can rewrite the right-hand side of (
45
) integrating by parts:

(\mathbf{f},\nabla\times\phi)_{0,\Omega}=(\nabla\times\mathbf{f},\phi)_{0,\Omega}.

The strong formulation corresponding to (
45
) reads as follow:
we seek
\psi\in H^{2}_{0}(\Omega)
satisfying

\Delta^{2}\psi=\nabla\times\mathbf{f}\text{ in }\Omega.

We derive the
\mathcal{C}^{0}
-IPDG formulation of this problem
using standard dG techniques;
see, e.g.,
[
25
]
.
On piecewise polynomial spaces,
we define the discrete bilinear form

\begin{split}B_{n}(\psi_{n},\phi_{n})&:=\int_{\mathcal{T}_{n}}(\nabla\times\nabla\psi_{n}):(\nabla\times\nabla\phi_{n})-\int_{\mathcal{F}_{n}}\{\nabla\times\nabla\psi_{n}\}\cdot\llbracket\nabla\phi_{n}\times\mathbf{n}\rrbracket\\
&\quad-\int_{\mathcal{F}_{n}}\{\nabla\times\nabla\phi_{n}\}\cdot\llbracket\nabla\psi_{n}\times\mathbf{n}\rrbracket+\int_{\mathcal{F}_{n}}\boldsymbol{\tau}\llbracket\nabla\psi_{n}\times\mathbf{n}\rrbracket\cdot\llbracket\nabla\phi_{n}\times\mathbf{n}\rrbracket\qquad\forall\psi_{n},\phi_{n}\in V_{n}.\end{split}

With an abuse of notation,
we denote the norm induced by the bilinear form in (
43
) by
{\left\|{\cdot}\right\|}_{\dG}
.

Following the same steps as in Section
4
,

hp
-optimal error estimates can be proved.
In fact, the problem is
the bilinear form appearing in
\mathcal{C}^{0}
-IPDG (
43
) with rotated components.

For completeness, we report the convergence result.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
\mathcal{T}_{n}
be either a quasi-uniform triangular mesh,
or a quasi-uniform tensor product-type mesh of either parallelograms (2D) or parallelepipeds (3D)
as in Section
2.1
.
Let
\psi
and
\psi_{n}
be the solutions to (
45
) and the corresponding
\mathcal{C}^{0}
-IPDG, respectively.
Assume that
\psi\in H^{s+2}(\Omega)
,
s:=\min(k,p-1)
,
p\geq 2
.
Then, there exists a positive constant
c

independent of
h
, and
p

such that

{\left\|{\psi-\psi_{n}}\right\|}_{\dG}\leq c\left(\frac{h}{p}\right)^{s}{\left\|{\psi}\right\|}_{s+2,\Omega},\qquad\qquad{\left\|{\psi-\psi_{n}}\right\|}_{0,\Omega}\leq c\left(\frac{h}{p}\right)^{2}{\left\|{\psi-\psi_{n}}\right\|}_{\dG}.

Conclusions

We proved that the IPDG for the biharmonic problem
is
hp
-optimal if essential boundary conditions are homogeneous,
and triangular and tensor product-type meshes (in two and three dimensions) are employed.
The analysis hinges upon proving the existence
of a global
H^{2}
piecewise polynomial over the given meshes
with
hp
-optimal approximation properties.
The results discussed for the biharmonic problem
were extended to other methods,
e.g.,
\mathcal{C}^{0}
-IPDG, and problems,
e.g., the stream formulation of the Stokes problem.
The theoretical results were validated on several test cases,
which also showed
p
-suboptimality for solutions with singular essential boundary conditions.

Appendix A
Proof of Lemma
3.3

For the sake of presentation, throughout the proof, we shall write
u_{p}
instead of
\mathcal{P}_{p}^{1}u
.

We set
u_{p}^{\prime\prime}
as the Legendre expansion of
u^{\prime\prime}
truncated at order
p-2
.
In other words, expanding
u^{\prime\prime}
as a series of Legendre polynomials
L_{j}(\xi)
,
j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,

u^{\prime\prime}(\xi)=\sum_{i=0}^{+\infty}b_{j}L_{j}(\xi),

(46)

we define

u_{p}^{\prime\prime}(\xi):=\sum_{i=0}^{p-2}b_{j}L_{j}(\xi).

(47)

Standard properties of Legendre polynomials,
see, e.g.,
[
32
, Appendix C]
, imply

b_{j}=\frac{2j+1}{2}\int_{-1}^{1}u^{\prime\prime}(\xi)L_{j}(\xi)d\xi.

(48)

For
k\geq 0
, we recall
[
32
, Lemma
3.10
]
that

\sum_{i=k}^{+\infty}\frac{2}{2i+1}\frac{(i+k)!}{(i-k)!}|b_{i}|^{2}=\int_{\widehat{I}}(1-\xi^{2})^{k}|u^{(k+2)}(\xi)|^{2}d\xi\leq{\left\|{u^{(k+2)}}\right\|}_{0,\widehat{I}}^{2}.

(49)

Using the orthogonality properties of Legendre polynomials
[
32
, eq. (C.24)]
,
the fact that
s\leq p-2
,
and (
49
),
we obtain

\begin{split}{\left\|{u^{\prime\prime}-u_{p}^{\prime\prime}}\right\|}_{0,\widehat{I}}^{2}&=\sum_{i=p-1}^{+\infty}\frac{2}{2i+1}|b_{i}|^{2}=\sum_{i=p-1}^{+\infty}\frac{2}{2i+1}\frac{(i+s)!}{(i-s)!}|b_{i}|^{2}\frac{(i-s)!}{(i+s)!}\\
&\leq\frac{(p-s-1)!}{(p+s-1)!}\sum_{i=s}^{+\infty}\frac{2}{2i+1}\frac{(i+s)!}{(i-s)!}|b_{i}|^{2}\leq\frac{(p-s-1)!}{(p+s-1)!}{\left\|{u^{(s+2)}}\right\|}_{0,\widehat{I}}^{2}.\end{split}

The two above equations yield the first bound in (
12
).

Next, we introduce

u_{p}^{\prime}(\xi)=\int_{-1}^{\xi}u_{p}^{\prime\prime}(t)dt+u^{\prime}(-1).

(50)

We have
u_{p}^{\prime}(-1)=u^{\prime}(-1)
.
Moreover, recalling that
u_{p}^{\prime\prime}
and
u^{\prime\prime}
have the same average over
\widehat{I}
, we also have

u_{p}^{\prime}(1)=\int_{-1}^{1}u_{p}^{\prime\prime}(t)dt+u^{\prime}(-1)=\int_{-1}^{1}u^{\prime\prime}(t)dt+u^{\prime}(-1)=u^{\prime}(1),

which proves (
11
) for the derivative of
u_{p}
at the endpoints of
\widehat{I}
.

At this point, we observe

u^{\prime}(\xi)-u_{p}^{\prime}(\xi)=\int_{-1}^{\xi}(u^{\prime\prime}(t)-u_{p}^{\prime\prime}(t))dt=\sum_{i=p-1}^{+\infty}b_{i}\int_{-1}^{\xi}L_{i}(t)dt=:\sum_{i=p-1}^{+\infty}b_{i}\phi_{i}(\xi).

(51)

Recall the Legendre differential equation
[
32
, eq. (C.2.3)]

((1-\xi^{2})L_{i}^{\prime}(\xi))^{\prime}+i(i+1)L_{i}(\xi)=0\qquad\forall i\in\mathbb{N}_{0}.

Integrating the above identity over
(-1,\xi)
,
\xi\in(-1,1)
, yields

\phi_{i}(\xi)=-\frac{1}{i(i+1)}(1-\xi^{2})L_{i}^{\prime}(\xi).

(52)

Recall the orthogonality property of the derivatives of Legendre polynomials
[
32
, eq. (3.39)]
:

\int_{-1}^{1}(1-\xi^{2})L_{i}^{\prime}(\xi)L_{j}^{\prime}(\xi)d\xi=\frac{2\delta_{i,j}}{2i+1}\frac{(i+1)!}{(i-1)!}.

(53)

Combining (
52
) and (
53
),
for all
i
,
j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, we deduce

\begin{split}\int_{-1}^{1}(1-\xi^{2})^{-1}\phi_{i}(\xi)\phi_{j}(\xi)d\xi&=\frac{1}{i(i+1)j(j+1)}\int_{-1}^{1}(1-\xi^{2})L_{i}^{\prime}(\xi)L_{j}^{\prime}(\xi)d\xi\\
&=\frac{1}{i^{2}(i+1)^{2}}\frac{2\delta_{i,j}}{2i+1}\frac{(i+1)!}{(i-1)!}=\frac{2\delta_{i,j}}{i(i+1)(2i+1)}.\end{split}

(54)

Using
s\leq p-2
,
we write

\begin{split}&\int_{-1}^{1}|u^{\prime}(\xi)-u_{p}^{\prime}(\xi)|^{2}d\xi=\int_{-1}^{1}|\!\!\!\sum_{i=p-1}^{+\infty}b_{i}\phi_{i}(\xi)|^{2}d\xi\leq\int_{-1}^{1}(1-\xi^{2})^{-1}|\!\!\!\sum_{i=p-1}^{+\infty}b_{i}\phi_{i}(\xi)|^{2}d\xi\\
&\overset{\eqref{phi-orthogonality}}{\leq}\sum_{i=p-1}^{+\infty}\frac{2|b_{i}|^{2}}{i(i+1)(2i+1)}=\sum_{i=p-1}^{+\infty}\left(\frac{2|b_{i}|^{2}}{2i+1}\frac{(i+s)!}{(i-s)!}\right)\frac{(i-s)!}{(i+s)!}\frac{1}{i(i+1)}\\
&\leq\frac{(p-s-1)!}{(p+s-1)!}\frac{1}{(p-1)p}\left(\sum_{i=s}^{+\infty}\frac{2|b_{i}|^{2}}{2i+1}\frac{(i+s)!}{(i-s)!}\right)\overset{\eqref{Vk-norm-identity}}{\leq}\frac{(p-s-1)!}{(p+s-1)!}\frac{1}{(p-1)p}{\left\|{u^{(s+2)}}\right\|}_{0,\widehat{I}}^{2},\end{split}

which is the second bound in (
12
).

Finally, we introduce

u_{p}(\xi)=\int_{-1}^{\xi}u_{p}^{\prime}(t)dt+u(-1).

(55)

We observe that
u_{p}(-1)=u(-1)
.
Since
L_{1}^{\prime}(t)=1=L_{0}(t)
, standard manipulations imply

\begin{split}u_{p}(1)-u_{p}(-1)&=\int_{-1}^{1}u_{p}^{\prime}(t)dt\overset{\eqref{writing:u:2}}{=}2u^{\prime}(-1)+\int_{-1}^{1}\left(\int_{-1}^{t}u_{p}^{\prime\prime}(x)dx\right)dt\\
&\overset{\eqref{expanding:u-second},\eqref{difference-derivatives}}{=}2u^{\prime}(-1)+\int_{-1}^{1}\sum_{i=0}^{p-2}b_{i}\phi_{i}(t)dt=2u^{\prime}(-1)+\sum_{i=0}^{p-2}b_{i}\int_{-1}^{1}\phi_{i}(t)dt\\
&\overset{\eqref{identity:phi-i}}{=}2u^{\prime}(-1)-\sum_{i=0}^{p-1}b_{i}\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{1}{i(i+1)}(1-t^{2})L_{i}^{\prime}(t)L_{1}^{\prime}(t)dt\\
&\overset{\eqref{orthogonality:L-prime}}{=}2u^{\prime}(-1)-\sum_{i=0}^{+\infty}b_{i}\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{1}{i(i+1)}(1-t^{2})L_{i}^{\prime}(t)L_{1}^{\prime}(t)dt\\
&\overset{\eqref{identity:phi-i},\eqref{expanding:u-second}}{=}2u^{\prime}(-1)+\int_{-1}^{1}\left(\int_{-1}^{t}u^{\prime\prime}(x)dx\right)dt=\int_{-1}^{1}u^{\prime}(t)dt=u(1)-u(-1).\end{split}

Using that
u_{p}(-1)=u(-1)
, we deduce
u_{p}(1)=u(1)
.

We are left with proving error estimates in the
L^{2}
norm.
To this aim, observe that

u(\xi)-u_{p}(\xi)=\int_{-1}^{\xi}(u^{\prime}(t)-u_{p}^{\prime}(t))dt.

We arrive at

u(\xi)-u_{p}(\xi)\overset{\eqref{difference-derivatives}}{=}\sum_{i=p-1}^{+\infty}b_{i}\int_{-1}^{\xi}\phi_{i}(x)\ dx\ dt=:\sum_{i=p-1}^{+\infty}b_{i}\psi_{i}(\xi).

(56)

We prove certain orthogonality properties of the
\psi_{i}
functions.
Recall the identity
[
32
, eq. (C.2.5)]
:

L_{i}(\xi)=\frac{L^{\prime}_{i+1}(\xi)-L^{\prime}_{i-1}(\xi)}{2i+1}\qquad\forall i\geq 2,\qquad\qquad L_{0}(\xi)=L_{1}^{\prime}(\xi).

Integrating over
(-1,t)
,
t\in(-1,1)
,
and using
L_{i+1}(-1)=L_{i-1}(-1)
,
see
[
32
, eq. (C.2.6)]
,
we deduce

\phi_{i}(t)=\frac{L_{i+1}(\xi)-L_{i-1}(\xi)}{2i+1}.

Upon integrating the above identity over
(-1,\xi)
,
\xi\in(-1,1)
, we arrive at

\psi_{i}(\xi)=\frac{\int_{-1}^{\xi}L_{i+1}(t)\ dt-\int_{-1}^{\xi}L_{i-1}(t)\ dt}{2i+1}\overset{\eqref{difference-derivatives}}{=}\frac{\phi_{i+1}(\xi)-\phi_{i-1}(t)}{2i+1}.

With the notation
\phi_{-1}(\xi)=0
,
we get

\begin{split}&\psi_{i}(\xi)\psi_{j}(\xi)\\
&=\frac{1}{(2i+1)(2j+1)}\left(\phi_{i+1}(\xi)\phi_{j+1}(\xi)+\phi_{i-1}(\xi)\phi_{j-1}(\xi)-\phi_{i+1}(\xi)\phi_{j-1}(\xi)-\phi_{i-1}(\xi)\phi_{j+1}(\xi)\right).\end{split}

In (
54
), we proved that the
\phi_{i}
functions are orthogonal with respect to the
(1-\xi^{2})
-weighted
L^{2}
inner product.
Therefore, testing the above identity by
(1-\xi^{2})^{-1}

and integrating over
(-1,1)
we arrive at

\int_{-1}^{1}(1-\xi^{2})^{-1}\psi_{i}(\xi)\psi_{j}(\xi)\ d\xi=\frac{1}{(2i+1)(2j+1)}\left(\aleph_{i+1,j+1}+\aleph_{i-1,j-1}-\aleph_{i+1,j-1}-\aleph_{i-1,j+1}\right),

(57)

where, from (
54
),

\aleph_{\ell,k}=\begin{cases}0&\text{if }\ell\leq 0\text{ or }k\leq 0,\\
\frac{2\delta_{\ell,k}}{\ell(\ell+1)(2\ell+1)}&\text{if }\ell,\ k>0.\end{cases}

We provide explicit values for the expression in (
57
).
If
j\in\{i-2,i,i+2\}
, then

\int_{-1}^{1}(1-\xi^{2})^{-1}\psi_{i}(\xi)\psi_{j}(\xi)\ d\xi=0.

If
j=i
, then

\int_{-1}^{1}(1-\xi^{2})^{-1}\psi_{i}(\xi)\psi_{i}(\xi)\ d\xi=\beth^{i}_{1}+\beth^{i}_{2},

where

\beth_{1}^{i}:=\frac{1}{(2i+1)^{2}}\frac{2}{(i+1)(i+2)(2i+3)}\quad\forall i\in\mathbb{N};\qquad\beth_{2}^{i}:=\begin{cases}0&\text{if }i=1,\\
\frac{1}{(2i+1)^{2}}\frac{2}{(i-1)i(2i-1)}&\text{if }i\geq 2.\end{cases}

(58)

If
j=i+2
, then

\int_{-1}^{1}(1-\xi^{2})^{-1}\psi_{i}(\xi)\psi_{i+2}(\xi)\ d\xi=-\frac{1}{(2i+1)(2i+5)}\cdot\frac{2}{(i+1)(i+2)(2i+3)}=:-\beth^{i}_{3}.

(59)

If
j=i-2
, then

\int_{-1}^{1}(1-\xi^{2})^{-1}\psi_{i}(\xi)\psi_{i-2}(\xi)\ d\xi=-\beth^{i}_{4}:=\begin{cases}-\frac{1}{(2i+1)(2i-3)}\cdot\frac{2}{(i-1)i(2i-1)}&\text{if }i\geq 3,\\
0&\text{if }i\leq 2.\end{cases}

(60)

In light of the above orthogonality properties,
we can estimate the
L^{2}
approximation error as follows:

\begin{split}{\left\|{u-u_{p}}\right\|}^{2}_{0,\widehat{I}}&\overset{\eqref{expansion:psi}}{=}{\left\|{\sum_{i=p-1}^{{+\infty}}b_{i}\psi_{i}}\right\|}^{2}_{0,\widehat{I}}\leq\sum_{i,j=p-1}^{+\infty}{\left|{b_{i}\ b_{j}\int_{-1}^{1}(1-\xi^{2})^{-1}\psi_{i}(\xi)\psi_{j}(\xi)d\xi}\right|}\\
&\overset{\eqref{beth-1-2},\eqref{beth-3},\eqref{beth-4}}{\leq}\sum_{i=p-1}^{+\infty}|b_{i}|^{2}\left(\beth^{i}_{1}+\beth^{i}_{2}+\beth^{i}_{3}+\beth^{i}_{4}\right).\end{split}

We cope with the four terms on the right-hand side separately.
We begin with
\beth^{i}_{1}
:

\begin{split}\sum_{i=p-1}^{+\infty}|b_{i}|^{2}\beth^{i}_{1}&\overset{\eqref{beth-1-2}}{=}\sum_{i=p-1}^{+\infty}|b_{i}|^{2}\frac{1}{(2i+1)^{2}}\cdot\frac{2}{(i+1)(i+2)(2i+3)}\\
&=\sum_{i=p-1}^{+\infty}\left(\frac{2}{2i+1}|b_{i}|^{2}\frac{(i+s)!}{(i-s)!}\right)\left(\frac{(i-s)!}{(i+s)!}\frac{1}{(i+1)(i+2)(2i+1)(2i+3)}\right)\\
&\overset{\eqref{Vk-norm-identity}}{\leq}\frac{(p-s-1)!}{(p+s-1)!}\frac{1}{p(p+1)(2p-1)(2p+1)}{\left\|{u^{(s+2)}}\right\|}_{0,\widehat{I}}^{2}.\end{split}

Next, we focus on
\beth^{i}_{2}
,
i\geq 2
:

\begin{split}\sum_{i=\max(2,p-1)}^{+\infty}|b_{i}|^{2}\beth^{i}_{2}&\overset{\eqref{beth-1-2}}{=}\sum_{i=\max(2,p-1)}^{+\infty}|b_{i}|^{2}\frac{1}{(2i+1)^{2}}\cdot\frac{2}{(i-1)i(2i-1)}\\
&=\sum_{i=\max(2,p-1)}^{+\infty}\left(\frac{2}{2i+1}|b_{i}|^{2}\frac{(i+s)!}{(i-s)!}\right)\left(\frac{(i-s)!}{(i+s)!}\frac{1}{(i-1)i(2i-1)(2i+1)}\right)\\
&\overset{\eqref{Vk-norm-identity}}{\leq}\frac{(p-s-1)!}{(p+s-1)!}\frac{1}{(p-2)(p-1)(2p-3)(2p-1)}{\left\|{u^{(s+2)}}\right\|}_{0,\widehat{I}}^{2}.\end{split}

As for the term
\beth^{i}_{3}
, we proceed as follows:

\begin{split}\sum_{i=p-1}^{+\infty}|b_{i}|^{2}\beth^{i}_{3}&\overset{\eqref{beth-3}}{=}\sum_{i=p-1}^{+\infty}|b_{i}|^{2}\frac{1}{(2i+1)(2i+5)}\cdot\frac{2}{(i+1)(i+2)(2i+3)}\\
&=\sum_{i=p-1}^{+\infty}\left(\frac{2}{2i+1}|b_{i}|^{2}\frac{(i+s)!}{(i-s)!}\right)\left(\frac{(i-s)!}{(i+s)!}\frac{1}{(i+1)(i+2)(2i+3)(2i+5)}\right)\\
&\overset{\eqref{Vk-norm-identity}}{\leq}\frac{(p-s-1)!}{(p+s-1)!}\frac{1}{p(p+2)(2p+1)(2p+3)}{\left\|{u^{(s+2)}}\right\|}_{0,\widehat{I}}^{2}.\end{split}

Eventually, we cope with the term
\beth^{i}_{4}
,
i\geq 3
:

\begin{split}\sum_{i=\max(3,p-1)}^{+\infty}|b_{i}|^{2}\beth^{i}_{4}&\overset{\eqref{beth-4}}{=}\sum_{i=\max(3,p-1)}^{+\infty}|b_{i}|^{2}\frac{1}{(2i+1)(2i-3)}\cdot\frac{2}{(i-1)i(2i-1)}\\

&\leq\frac{(p-s-1)!}{(p+s-1)!}\frac{1}{(p-2)(p-1)(2p-5)(2p-3)}{\left\|{u^{(s+2)}}\right\|}_{0,\widehat{I}}^{2}.\end{split}

Collecting the five bounds above concludes the proof.

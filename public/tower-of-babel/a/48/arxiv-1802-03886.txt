Local and Global Existence of Solutions to Scalar Equations on Spatially Flat Universe as a Background with Non-minimal Coupling
Fiki T. Akbar, Bobby E. Gunara, Muhammad Iqbal, Hadi Susanto

Abstract
We prove the wellposedness of scalar wave equations on spatially flat universe as a background with nonminimal coupling with the scalar potential turned on by introducing the k k -order linear energy and the corresponding energy norm. In the local case, we show that both the k k -order linear energy and the energy norm are bounded for finite time with initial data in H k + 1 × H k H^{k+1}\times H^{k} . Whereas in the global case, we have to add three assumptions related to the nonminimal coupling constant, the scale factor of spacetimes, and the form of the scalar potential that has to be a polynomial with a small positive parameter. Then, we show that the solution does globally exist with a particular decay estimate that depends on the scale factor of the spacetimes. Finally, we provide some physical models that support our general setup.

Introduction

It is of interest to study the Klein-Gordon equation because it describes the dynamics of the spinless particle in our universe at quantum level or it can be viewed as the scalar wave equation at classical level in our universe. Moreover, to get a more realistic picture we have to include the influence of geometrical properties of the universe which may also determine by its matter distribution. It is extremely difficult, however, to solve the Klein-Gordon equation on the family of four dimensional Friedmann-Robertson-Walker spacetimes with general couplings even at the classical level. Therefore, we have to specify both the spacetime and the coupling in order to get a solvable model. Several simple models in four dimensions have been studied, for example, in
[

,
2
,
3
]
.

In this paper we prove the local and global existence of solutions of the Klein-Gordon equation in higher dimensional spatially flat Friedmann-Robertson-Walker spacetimes with non-minimal coupling between the scalar curvature and the scalar field, and the scalar potential turned on. This additional non-minimal coupling is the simplest generalization of the scalar field theory on curved spacetimes
[

,
5
]
, which can be viewed, for example, as a result of quantum corrections

The starting point of proving the local and global existence of solutions is by introducing the
k

for finite time

Using these assumptions, we prove that the supremum of the energy norm is bounded and thus, we could have a decay estimate.

Real Scalar Field in Spatially Flat Universe

In this section, we discuss some local properties of the real scalar field on the higher dimensional spatially flat universe as a background with additional non-minimal coupling where the coupling interaction of the scalar field
\phi
is proportional to the scalar curvature of the spacetime. We show that the nonlinear terms and the
k
-order linear energy are bounded if the energy norm is also bounded.

The action of our theory has the form

\mathcal{S}=\int d\tau dx\sqrt{-g}\left(\frac{1}{2}\partial_{\mu}\phi\>\partial^{\mu}\phi+\frac{\xi}{2}R\phi^{2}-V(\phi)\right)\>,

(3.1)

where
g
and
R
are the determinant and the scalar curvature of the metric (
2.4
), respectively. The non-minimal coupling is introduced in the second term of the right hand side of (
3.1
) with positive constant
\xi

1
1

1

Model with positive
\xi
is called canonical, while that with negative
\xi
is called phantom.
. The real function
V(\phi)
denotes the scalar potential which is assumed to be smooth and satisfy the following conditions,

\displaystyle(1)

\displaystyle V(0)=0~,

\displaystyle(2)

\displaystyle\partial_{\phi}V(0)=0~.

(3.2)

The conditions in (
3.2

\phi^{4}

The equation of motions of scalar field in this case is given by

\nabla_{\mu}\nabla^{\mu}\phi-\xi R\phi+\partial_{\phi}V(\phi)=0\>,

(3.3)

where
\nabla_{\mu}
is a covariant derivative with respect to the metric (
2.3
). Using (
2.3
) and (
2.5
), we can write the equation of motion in form of nonlinear waves equation

\partial_{\tau}^{2}\phi-\Delta\phi=F(\phi,\partial_{\tau}\phi)\>,

(3.4)

F(\phi,\partial_{\tau}\phi)\equiv-(D-2)H~\partial_{\tau}\phi-\xi(D-1)\left(2\dot{H}+(D-2)H^{2}\right)\phi+a^{2}\partial_{\phi}V(\phi)\>,

(3.5)

and
\Delta
is Laplacian in
\mathrm{I\hskip-1.99168ptR}^{D-1}
. The energy-momentum tensor for system (
3.1
) is given by

\displaystyle T_{\mu\nu}=\nabla_{\mu}\phi\;\nabla_{\nu}\phi-\frac{1}{2}g_{\mu\nu}\nabla_{\lambda}\phi\;\nabla^{\lambda}\phi-g_{\mu\nu}V(\phi)-\xi G_{\mu\nu}\phi^{2}-\xi\left(g_{\mu\nu}\nabla_{\lambda}\nabla^{\lambda}\phi^{2}-\nabla_{\mu}\nabla_{\nu}\phi^{2}\right)\>,

where
G_{\mu\nu}
is Einstein tensor of
\mathcal{M}^{D}
. Then, we could define an energy functional
E=a^{2D-2}\int T_{00}~d^{D-1}x
. However, such a functional covers only
L^{2}
-norm functions and it is difficult to obtain a decay estimate of the scalar field from it in the global case (see Section

k

\mathcal{H}_{k}[\phi]=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{|\alpha|\leq k}\int_{\mathrm{I\hskip-1.99168ptR}^{D-1}}\>\left[(\partial^{\alpha}\partial_{\tau}\phi)^{2}+\left|\nabla\partial^{\alpha}\phi\right|^{2}\right]\>dx\>,

(3.7)

with
\alpha
being a multi index, which will be used in this paper. We also define an energy norm as

|\phi(\tau,\cdot)|_{k}:=\|\phi(\tau,\cdot)\|_{H^{k+1}(\mathrm{I\hskip-1.99168ptR}^{D-1})}+\|\partial_{\tau}\phi(\tau,\cdot)\|_{H^{k}(\mathrm{I\hskip-1.99168ptR}^{D-1})}

(3.8)

This form of energy can be used as a bound for the nonlinear term. In the following lemmas, we prove the properties of the nonlinear term and the linear energy.

Lemma 1
.

Let
\phi
be a real function such that for all
\tau\in[\tau_{0},\tau_{0}+T]
and
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,

|\phi(\tau,\cdot)|_{k}\leq C\>.

(3.9)

Then for all
\tau\in[\tau_{0},\tau_{0}+T]
and
k>(D-1)/2
, we have

\left(\sum_{|\alpha|\leq k}\int_{\mathrm{I\hskip-1.99168ptR}^{D-1}}[\partial^{\alpha}F(\phi,\partial_{\tau}\phi)]^{2}\>dx\right)^{1/2}\leq C\>,

(3.10)

where
C
depends only on the initial data,
T
,
k
and the bound of the scalar potential.

Proof.

First, we consider the case of
|\alpha|>0
. The spatial derivative of Equation (
3.5
) gives

\partial^{\alpha}F(\phi,\partial_{\tau}\phi)=-(D-2)H~\partial^{\alpha}\partial_{\tau}\phi-\xi(D-1)\left(2\dot{H}+(D-2)H^{2}\right)\partial^{\alpha}\phi+a^{2}\partial^{\alpha}\partial_{\phi}V\>.

(3.11)

Since
\tau\in[\tau_{0},\tau_{0}+T]
,
a(\tau)
is a regular
C^{n}
-function, and using the hypothesis that the first and second terms are bounded, we thus have

\left(\sum_{|\alpha|\leq k}\int_{\mathrm{I\hskip-1.99168ptR}^{D-1}}[\partial^{\alpha}F(\phi,\partial_{\tau}\phi)]^{2}\>dx\right)^{1/2}\leq C\left\{1+\|\partial_{\phi}V\|_{H^{k}(\mathrm{I\hskip-1.99168ptR}^{D-1})}\right\}

(3.12)

To estimate the derivative of the scalar potential, we write down
\partial^{\alpha}\partial_{\phi}V
as

\partial^{\alpha}\partial_{\phi}V=\left(\partial_{\phi}^{\beta}\partial_{\phi}V\right)\;\partial^{\gamma_{1}}\phi\;\partial^{\gamma_{2}}\phi\ldots\partial^{\gamma_{i}}\phi\>,

(3.13)

where
\gamma_{1}+\gamma_{2}+\ldots+\gamma_{i}=\alpha
. Since the scalar potential is a smooth function, then by Sobolev embedding theorem for
k>(D-1)/2
, the hypothesis of the lemma implies that
\partial_{\phi}^{\beta}\partial_{\phi}V
is bounded. Thus,

\|\partial_{\phi}V\|_{H^{k}(\mathrm{I\hskip-1.99168ptR}^{D-1})}\leq C\|\phi\|_{H^{k}(\mathrm{I\hskip-1.99168ptR}^{D-1})}\leq C\>,

(3.14)

and we obtain Equation (
3.10
).

For
|\alpha|=0
, we have

\left(\int_{\mathrm{I\hskip-1.99168ptR}^{D-1}}\>[F(\phi,\partial_{\tau}\phi)]^{2}\>dx\right)^{1/2}\leq C\left\{\|\phi\|_{L^{2}(\mathrm{I\hskip-1.99168ptR}^{D-1})}+\|\partial_{\tau}\phi\|_{L^{2}(\mathrm{I\hskip-1.99168ptR}^{D-1})}+\|\partial_{\phi}V\|_{L^{2}(\mathrm{I\hskip-1.99168ptR}^{D-1})}\right\}\>.

(3.15)

The first and second terms at the right hand side are bounded to a constant by the hypothesis. Using the assumption that
\partial_{\phi}V(0)=0
, we obtain the estimate

\|\partial_{\phi}V\|_{L^{2}(\mathrm{I\hskip-1.99168ptR}^{D-1})}\leq C\|\phi\|_{L^{2}(\mathrm{I\hskip-1.99168ptR}^{D-1})}\leq C\>,

(3.16)

where the constant
C
depends on the bound of the scalar potential. Hence, the proof is complete.
∎

\displaystyle\|F(\phi_{l},\partial_{\phi}\tau\phi_{l})-F(\phi_{l-1},\partial_{\phi}\tau\phi_{l-1})\|_{H^{k}(\mathrm{I\hskip-1.99168ptR}^{D-1})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left[\|\phi_{l}-\phi_{l-1}\|_{H^{k}(\mathrm{I\hskip-1.99168ptR}^{D-1})}+\|\partial_{\tau}\phi_{l}-\partial_{\tau}\phi_{l-1}\|_{H^{k}(\mathrm{I\hskip-1.99168ptR}^{D-1})}\right.

(4.15)

\displaystyle\qquad\left.+\|\partial_{\phi}V(\phi_{l})-\partial_{\phi}V(\phi_{l-1})\|_{H^{k}(\mathrm{I\hskip-1.99168ptR}^{D-1})}\right]\>.

We can estimate the first and second terms as

\displaystyle\|\phi_{l}-\phi_{l-1}\|_{H^{k}(\mathrm{I\hskip-1.99168ptR}^{D-1})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|\phi_{l}-\phi_{l-1}\|_{L^{2}(\mathrm{I\hskip-1.99168ptR}^{D-1})}+\mathcal{H}^{1/2}_{k}[\phi_{l}-\phi_{l-1}]

\displaystyle\|\partial_{\tau}\phi_{l}-\partial_{\tau}\phi_{l-1}\|_{H^{k}(\mathrm{I\hskip-1.99168ptR}^{D-1})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{1/2}_{k}[\phi_{l}-\phi_{l-1}]\>.

(4.16)

To estimate the third term, we write

\partial_{\phi}V(\phi_{l})-\partial_{\phi}V(\phi_{l-1})=\int_{0}^{1}\partial_{\phi}^{2}V[\sigma\phi_{l}+(1-\sigma)\phi_{l-1}]d\sigma\;\left(\phi_{l}-\phi_{l-1}\right)\>.

(4.17)

Using Lemma
3
and the fact that the scalar potential is a smooth function, for
k>(D-1)/2
we obtain

\displaystyle\|\partial_{\phi}V(\phi_{l})-\partial_{\phi}V(\phi_{l-1})\|_{H^{k}(\mathrm{I\hskip-1.99168ptR}^{D-1})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|\phi_{l}-\phi_{l-1}\|_{H^{k}(\mathrm{I\hskip-1.99168ptR}^{D-1})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left(\|\phi_{l}-\phi_{l-1}\|_{L^{2}(\mathrm{I\hskip-1.99168ptR}^{D-1})}+\mathcal{H}^{1/2}_{k}[\phi_{l}-\phi_{l-1}]\right)\>,

where the constant
C
depends on the bound of the scalar potential. Hence, we obtain the estimate

\|F(\phi_{l},\partial_{\phi}\tau\phi_{l})-F(\phi_{l-1},\partial_{\phi}\tau\phi_{l-1})\|_{H^{k}(\mathrm{I\hskip-1.99168ptR}^{D-1})}\leq C\left(\|\phi_{l}-\phi_{l-1}\|_{L^{2}(\mathrm{I\hskip-1.99168ptR}^{D-1})}+\mathcal{H}^{1/2}_{k}[\phi_{l}-\phi_{l-1}]\right)\leq CE_{l,k}\>.

(4.19)

Now, similar to the proof of Lemma
3
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{d}{d\tau}\mathcal{H}_{k}[\phi_{l}-\phi_{l-1}]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|F(\phi_{l},\partial_{\tau}\phi_{l})-F(\phi_{l-1},\partial_{\tau}\phi_{l-1})\|_{H^{k}(\mathrm{I\hskip-1.99168ptR}^{D-1})}\;\|\partial_{\tau}\phi_{l}-\partial_{\tau}\phi_{l-1}\|_{H^{k}(\mathrm{I\hskip-1.99168ptR}^{D-1})}

(4.20)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle CE_{l,k}\mathcal{H}^{1/2}_{k}[\phi_{l}-\phi_{l-1}]\>.

Integrating the inequality, we obtain

\mathcal{H}^{1/2}_{k}[\phi_{l}-\phi_{l-1}](\tau)\leq\mathcal{H}^{1/2}_{k}[\phi_{l}-\phi_{l-1}](\tau_{0})+\frac{1}{2}CE_{l,k}T\>,

(4.21)

for all
\tau\in[\tau_{0},\tau_{0}+T]
and the proof is finished.

∎

Next, we prove the estimate of
E_{l,k}
.

Lemma 5
.

Let
\{\phi_{l}\}
be solutions of (
4.2
) and (
4.3
). Let
E_{l,k}
be given by Equation (
4.13
). For
k>(D-1)/2
, there exist constants
C_{0}>1
and
T
such that,

E_{l,k}\leq\frac{C_{0}}{2^{l}}\>,

(4.22)

for all
l
.

Proof.

The lemma is true for
l=1
by assuming that the constant
C_{0}
is big enough. Let us consider

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial\tau}\int_{\mathrm{I\hskip-1.99168ptR}^{D-1}}\>|\phi_{l+1}-\phi_{l}|^{2}\>dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\int_{\mathrm{I\hskip-1.99168ptR}^{D-1}}\>|\phi_{l+1}-\phi_{l}|\;\partial_{\tau}|\phi_{l+1}-\phi_{l}|\>dx

(4.23)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\|\phi_{l+1}-\phi_{l}\|_{L^{2}(\mathrm{I\hskip-1.99168ptR}^{D-1})}\|\partial_{\tau}(\phi_{l+1}-\phi_{l})\|_{L^{2}(\mathrm{I\hskip-1.99168ptR}^{D-1})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2^{3/2}\|\phi_{l+1}-\phi_{l}\|_{L^{2}(\mathrm{I\hskip-1.99168ptR}^{D-1})}\mathcal{H}^{1/2}_{k}[\phi_{l+1}-\phi_{l}]\>,

Integrating this inequality and since
k>(D-1)/2
, we obtain

\|\phi_{l+1}-\phi_{l}\|_{L^{2}(\mathrm{I\hskip-1.99168ptR}^{D-1})}(\tau)\leq\|\phi_{l+1}-\phi_{l}\|_{L^{2}(\mathrm{I\hskip-1.99168ptR}^{D-1})}(\tau_{0})+2^{1/2}\left|\int_{\tau_{0}}^{\tau}\mathcal{H}^{1/2}_{k}[\phi_{l+1}-\phi_{l}](s)\>ds\right|\>.

(4.24)

Assuming
T<1/2
and using Lemma
4
, we have the estimate

E_{l+1,k}\leq\|\phi_{l+1}-\phi_{l}\|_{L^{2}(\mathrm{I\hskip-1.99168ptR}^{D-1})}(\tau_{0})+2\mathcal{H}^{1/2}_{k}[\phi_{l+1}-\phi_{l}](\tau_{0})+CE_{l,k}T\>.

(4.25)

The first and second terms depend only on the initial data. However, we can choose them as such that,

\|\phi_{l+1}-\phi_{l}\|_{L^{2}(\mathrm{I\hskip-1.99168ptR}^{D-1})}(\tau_{0})+2\mathcal{H}^{1/2}_{k}[\phi_{l+1}-\phi_{l}](\tau_{0})\leq\frac{1}{2^{l+2}}\>.

(4.26)

Hence, by assuming
CT<1/4
, Equation (
4.25
) and the induction hypothesis give

E_{l+1,k}\leq\frac{1}{2^{l+2}}+\frac{C_{0}}{2^{l+2}}\leq\frac{C_{0}}{2^{l+1}}\>.

(4.27)

Thus, Equation (
4.22
) is true for all
l
and the proof is finished.

∎

Let us consider, for all
|\alpha|\leq k+1
,

\displaystyle\|\nabla\partial^{\alpha}(\phi_{l}-\phi_{l-1})(\tau,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\mathrm{I\hskip-1.99168ptR}^{D-1})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathrm{I\hskip-1.99168ptR}^{D-1}}\left|\nabla\partial^{\alpha}(\phi_{l}-\phi_{l-1})(\tau,\cdot)\right|^{2}dx\right)^{1/2}

(4.28)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\mathcal{H}_{k}[\phi_{l}-\phi_{l-1}]^{1/2}(\tau)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle CE_{l,k}\>.

In other words,
\phi_{l}-\phi_{l-1}\in H^{k+1}(\mathrm{I\hskip-1.99168ptR}^{D-1})
. However, using Lemma
5
, we have

\sup_{\tau\in[\tau_{0},\tau_{0}+T]}\|\phi_{l}-\phi_{l-1}\|_{H^{k+1}(\mathrm{I\hskip-1.99168ptR}^{D-1})}\leq\frac{C_{0}}{2^{l}}\>,

(4.29)

which show that
\{\phi_{l}\}
is a Cauchy sequence on
C\left([\tau_{0},\tau_{0}+T],H^{k+1}(\mathrm{I\hskip-1.99168ptR}^{D-1})\right)
. Using a similar method, we can show that
\{\partial_{\tau}\phi_{l}\}
is also a Cauchy sequence on
C\left([\tau_{0},\tau_{0}+T],H^{k}(\mathrm{I\hskip-1.99168ptR}^{D-1})\right)
. Thus, we have proven the existence of generalized solutions of Equation (
3.4
) such that

\phi\in C\left([\tau_{0},\tau_{0}+T],H^{k+1}(\mathrm{I\hskip-1.99168ptR}^{D-1})\right)\cap C^{1}\left([\tau_{0},\tau_{0}+T],H^{k}(\mathrm{I\hskip-1.99168ptR}^{D-1})\right)\>.

(4.30)

Furthermore, using Lemma
5
, we also get that
\{\partial_{i}\partial_{j}\phi_{l}\}
and
\{\partial_{i}\partial_{\tau}\phi_{l}\}
are Cauchy sequences on
C\left([\tau_{0},\tau_{0}+T],H^{k-1}(\mathrm{I\hskip-1.99168ptR}^{D-1})\right)
. In fact, we have

\displaystyle\|\partial_{\tau}^{2}(\phi_{l+1}-\phi_{l})\|_{H^{k-1}(\mathrm{I\hskip-1.99168ptR}^{D-1})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|F(\phi_{l},\partial_{\tau}\phi_{l})-F(\phi_{l-1},\partial_{\tau}\phi_{l-1})\|_{H^{k-1}(\mathrm{I\hskip-1.99168ptR}^{D-1})}

(4.31)

\displaystyle\qquad+\|\partial_{i}\partial^{i}(\phi_{l+1}-\phi_{l})\|_{H^{k-1}(\mathrm{I\hskip-1.99168ptR}^{D-1})}\>.

On the right hand side, the first term is bounded by
E_{l,k}
and the second term is bounded by a constant since it is a Cauchy sequence on
H^{k-1}(\mathrm{I\hskip-1.99168ptR}^{D-1})
. Then using Lemma
5
, we have that
\{\partial_{\tau}^{2}\phi_{l}\}
is also a Cauchy sequence in
H^{k-1}(\mathrm{I\hskip-1.99168ptR}^{D-1})
. Thus, for
k>(D-1)/2
, there exists a real function
\phi
such that,

\sup_{\tau\in[\tau_{0},\tau_{0}+T]}\|\partial^{2}_{\tau}\phi\|_{H^{k-1}(\mathrm{I\hskip-1.99168ptR}^{D-1})}\leq C\>.

(4.32)

The above inequality implies that
\partial_{\tau}^{2}\phi\in C\left([\tau_{0},\tau_{0}+T],H^{k-1}(\mathrm{I\hskip-1.99168ptR}^{D-1})\right)
.

Let us consider a fixed point
(\tau,x)\in\mathcal{M}^{D}
. We make a sequance
(t_{l},x_{l})\rightarrow(\tau,x)
, where
\tau_{0}\leq\tau_{l},\tau\leq\tau_{0}+T
. Now, we have the estimate

\left|\partial_{\tau}^{2}\phi(\tau,x)-\partial_{\tau}^{2}\phi(\tau_{l},x_{l})\right|\leq\left|\partial_{\tau}^{2}\phi(\tau,x)-\partial_{\tau}^{2}\phi(\tau,x_{l})\right|+\left|\partial_{\tau}^{2}\phi(\tau,x_{l})-\partial_{\tau}^{2}\phi(\tau_{l},x_{l})\right|\>.

(4.33)

By Sobolev embedding theorem, for
k>(D-1)/2
we have
\partial_{\tau}^{2}\phi
to be a continuous function in
x
, and thus, the first term on the right hand side vanishes as
l\rightarrow\infty
. The second term can be estimated by a constant times
\|\partial_{\tau}^{2}\phi(\tau,\cdot)-\partial_{\tau}^{2}\phi(\tau_{l},\cdot)\|_{H^{k-1}(\mathrm{I\hskip-1.99168ptR}^{D-1})}
. Since
\partial_{\tau}^{2}\phi\in C\left([\tau_{0},\tau_{0}+T],H^{k-1}(\mathrm{I\hskip-1.99168ptR}^{D-1})\right)
, then the second term also goes to zero as
l\rightarrow\infty
. Hence, we conclude that,
\partial_{\tau}^{2}\phi\in C\left([\tau_{0},\tau_{0}+T]\times\mathrm{I\hskip-1.99168ptR}^{D-1}\right)
, thus

\phi\in C^{2}\left([\tau_{0},\tau_{0}+T]\times\mathrm{I\hskip-1.99168ptR}^{D-1}\right)\>.

(4.34)

To show the uniqueness, consider
\phi,\phi^{\prime}
as solutions of Equation (
4.1
) with the same initial data. Similar to the proof of Lemma
4
, we have the estimate

\displaystyle\frac{d}{d\tau}\mathcal{H}_{k}[\phi^{\prime}-\phi]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|F(\phi^{\prime},\partial_{\tau}\phi^{\prime})-F(\phi,\partial_{\tau}\phi)\|_{H^{k}(\mathrm{I\hskip-1.99168ptR}^{D-1})}\;\|\partial_{\tau}\phi^{\prime}-\partial_{\tau}\phi\|_{H^{k}(\mathrm{I\hskip-1.99168ptR}^{D-1})}

(4.35)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\mathcal{H}_{k}[\phi^{\prime}-\phi]\>.

Using Gronwall lemma and the fact that
\phi,\phi^{\prime}
have the same initial data, then for all
\tau\in[\tau_{0},\tau_{0}+T]
, we conclude that
\phi^{\prime}(\tau)=\phi(\tau)
and the uniqueness follows.

Thus, we have proven,

Theorem 1
.

Let
f\in H^{k+1}(\mathrm{I\hskip-1.99168ptR}^{D-1})
and
g\in H^{k}(\mathrm{I\hskip-1.99168ptR}^{D-1})
be initial data with compact support. Assume that the scalar potential is a smooth function satisfying
V(0)=0
and
\partial_{\phi}V(0)=0
and that
k>(D-1)/2
. Then, there exist
T>0
and a unique
\phi\in C^{2}\left([\tau_{0},\tau_{0}+T]\times\mathrm{I\hskip-1.99168ptR}^{D-1}\right)
being a local solution to the Equation (
3.4
) such that

\phi\in C\left([\tau_{0},\tau_{0}+T],H^{k+1}(\mathrm{I\hskip-1.99168ptR}^{D-1})\right)\cap C^{1}\left([\tau_{0},\tau_{0}+T],H^{k}(\mathrm{I\hskip-1.99168ptR}^{D-1})\right)\>.

(4.36)

4.2
Smoothness Properties

The local solution, which we have discussed above, is
\phi\in C^{2}\left([\tau_{0},\tau_{0}+T],\mathrm{I\hskip-1.99168ptR}^{D-1}\right)
throughout their interval of existence. In general, however they are actually smoother than this. In this section, we prove the smoothness properties of the solution of equation (
4.1
).

We claim that the solution is
C^{(m-1)}\left([\tau_{0},\tau_{0}+T],\mathrm{I\hskip-1.99168ptR}^{D-1}\right)
for
m\geq 3
. We will prove the statement using the induction argument. For
m=3
, the statement is true. Now, assume that it is also true for
m=n
, Then, we have

\partial^{r}_{\tau}\partial_{j_{1}}\partial_{j_{2}}\cdots\partial_{j_{i-r-1}}\phi\in C\left([\tau_{0},\tau_{0}+T]\times\mathrm{I\hskip-1.99168ptR}^{D-1}\right)\>,

(4.37)

where
r=0,1,\cdots,n-1
. For
m=n+1
, we need to prove that the limit of sequence defined by equations (
4.2
) and (
4.3
) satisfies

\partial_{j_{1}}\partial_{j_{2}}\cdots\partial_{j_{i-p}}\partial^{p}_{\tau}\phi\in C\left([\tau_{0},\tau_{0}+T]\times\mathrm{I\hskip-1.99168ptR}^{D-1}\right)\>,

(4.38)

for
p=0,1,\cdots,n-1
.

Since
n+1>3
, using Lemma
5
, we have

E_{n+1}[\phi_{l}-\phi_{l-1}]\leq C\>,

(4.39)

for some constant
C
. In other words, we have the estimate

\|\partial_{j}(\phi_{l}-\phi_{l-1})(\tau,\cdot)\|_{H^{n+1}(\mathrm{I\hskip-1.99168ptR}^{D-1})}+\|\partial_{\tau}(\phi_{l}-\phi_{l-1})(\tau,\cdot)\|_{H^{n+1}(\mathrm{I\hskip-1.99168ptR}^{D-1})}\leq C\>.

(4.40)

From the first term on the right hand side, we obtain

\|\partial_{j_{1}}\cdots\partial_{j_{n-1}}\partial_{j}(\phi_{l}-\phi_{l-1})(\tau,\cdot)\|_{H^{2}(\mathrm{I\hskip-1.99168ptR}^{D-1})}\leq C\|\partial_{j}(\phi_{l}-\phi_{l-1})(\tau,\cdot)\|_{H^{n+1}(\mathrm{I\hskip-1.99168ptR}^{D-1})}\leq C\>.

(4.41)

Using Sobolev embedding theorem, we conclude

\partial_{j_{1}}\cdots\partial_{j_{n}}\phi\in C\left([\tau_{0},\tau_{0}+T]\times\mathrm{I\hskip-1.99168ptR}^{D-1}\right)\>.

(4.42)

For the second term, using similar methods, we have

\partial_{j_{1}}\cdots\partial_{j_{n-1}}\partial_{\tau}\phi\in C\left([\tau_{0},\tau_{0}+T]\times\mathrm{I\hskip-1.99168ptR}^{D-1}\right)\>.

(4.43)

Hence, we have shown that Equation (
4.38
) is satisfied for
p=0
and
p=1
.

From Equation (
4.3
), we have

\displaystyle\partial^{p}_{\tau}\partial_{j_{1}}\cdots\partial_{j_{i-p}}(\phi_{l}-\phi_{l-1})=\partial^{p-2}_{\tau}\partial_{j_{1}}\cdots\partial_{j_{i-p}}\hat{F}_{l}+\partial^{p-2}_{\tau}\partial_{j_{1}}\cdots\partial_{j_{i-p}}\partial_{k}\partial^{k}(\phi_{l}-\phi_{l-1})\>,

(4.44)

with

\hat{F}_{l}=F(\phi_{l},\partial_{\tau}\phi_{l})-F(\phi_{l-1},\partial_{\tau}\phi_{l-1})\>.

(4.45)

The first term of Equation (
4.44
) can be written as

\displaystyle\partial^{p-2}_{\tau}\partial_{j_{1}}\cdots\partial_{j_{i-p}}\hat{F}_{l}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{12\xi}{\tau^{2}}\partial^{p-2}_{\tau}\partial_{j_{1}}\cdots\partial_{j_{i-p}}(\phi_{l}-\phi_{l-1})-\frac{4}{\tau}\partial^{p-1}_{\tau}\partial_{j_{1}}\cdots\partial_{j_{i-p}}(\phi_{l}-\phi_{l-1})

(4.46)

\displaystyle-~\tau^{4}\partial^{p-2}_{\tau}\partial_{j_{1}}\cdots\partial_{j_{i-p}}\left[\partial_{\phi}V(\phi_{l})-\partial_{\phi}V(\phi_{l-1})\right]\>.

From the induction hypothesis, the first and second terms are
C\left([\tau_{0},\tau_{0}+T]\times\mathrm{I\hskip-1.99168ptR}^{D-1}\right)
. To estimate the last term, define

G(\phi_{l},\phi_{l-1})=\int_{0}^{1}\partial_{\sigma}\partial_{\phi}V[\sigma\phi_{l}+(1-\sigma)\phi_{l-1}]\>d\sigma\>,

(4.47)

and we have the estimate,

\displaystyle\partial^{p-2}_{\tau}\partial_{j_{1}}\cdots\partial_{j_{i-p}}\left[G(\phi_{l},\phi_{l-1})\hat{\phi}_{l-1}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{c+d\leq i-p}~~\sum_{s+t\leq p-2}(\partial^{s}_{\tau}\partial_{j_{1}}\cdots\partial_{j_{c}}G)

\displaystyle~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~(\partial^{t}_{\tau}\partial_{j_{c+1}}\cdots\partial_{j_{c+d}}(\phi_{l}-\phi_{l-1}))\>,

for
c\geq 1
and
d\geq 0
. Since the scalar potential is a smooth function and
E_{c}[\phi_{l}]
is bounded for
c\leq n-p
by Lemma
3
, therefore we can bound
(\partial^{s}_{\tau}\partial_{j_{1}}\cdots\partial_{j_{c}}G)
by a constant. Because the last factor is
C\left([\tau_{0},\tau_{0}+T]\times\mathrm{I\hskip-1.99168ptR}^{D-1}\right)
by induction hypothesis, then
\partial^{p-2}_{\tau}\partial_{j_{1}}\cdots\partial_{j_{i-p}}\hat{F}_{l}
is also
C\left([\tau_{0},\tau_{0}+T]\times\mathrm{I\hskip-1.99168ptR}^{D-1}\right)
, and we conclude

\displaystyle\partial^{p}_{\tau}\partial_{j_{1}}\partial_{j_{2}}\cdots\partial_{j_{i-p}}\phi\in C\left([\tau_{0},\tau_{0}+T]\times\mathrm{I\hskip-1.99168ptR}^{D-1}\right)\>,

(4.49)

for
p=0,1,\cdots,n-1
.

Hence, we have proven

Theorem 2
.

Let
f\in H^{k+1}(\mathrm{I\hskip-1.99168ptR}^{D-1})
and
g\in H^{k}(\mathrm{I\hskip-1.99168ptR}^{D-1})
be the initial data with compact support. Assume that the scalar potential is a smooth function satisfying
V(0)=0
and
\partial_{\phi}V(0)=0
and that
k>(D-1)/2
. Then, there exist
T>0
and a unique
\phi\in C^{(k-1)}\left([\tau_{0},\tau_{0}+T]\times\mathrm{I\hskip-1.99168ptR}^{D-1}\right)
, which is a local solution to the Equation (
3.4
), such that

\phi\in C\left([\tau_{0},\tau_{0}+T],H^{k+1}(\mathrm{I\hskip-1.99168ptR}^{D-1})\right)\cap C^{1}\left([\tau_{0},\tau_{0}+T],H^{k}(\mathrm{I\hskip-1.99168ptR}^{D-1})\right)\>.

(4.50)

5
Global Existence

5.1
General Setup

In Section
4
, we proved the existence a unique local solution of Equation (
3.4
), i.e.

\begin{cases}\partial_{\tau}^{2}\phi+(D-2)H~\partial_{\tau}\phi-\Delta\phi=-\xi(D-1)\left(2\dot{H}+(D-2)H^{2}\right)\phi+a^{2}\partial_{\phi}V(\phi)\\
\phi(\tau_{0},\cdot)=f\in H^{k+1}(\mathrm{I\hskip-1.99168ptR}^{D-1})\\
\partial_{\tau}\phi(\tau_{0},\cdot)=g\in H^{k}(\mathrm{I\hskip-1.99168ptR}^{D-1})\>.\end{cases}

(5.1)

Here, we show that it is possible to have a set of global solutions of (
3.4
) for
T\to+\infty
. To proceed, let us define a new field,
\psi=a^{(D-2)/2}\phi
where
a\equiv a(\tau,\tau_{0})
such that Equation (
3.4
) can be written down as

\begin{cases}\partial_{\tau}^{2}\psi-\Delta\psi=h(\tau)\psi+a^{D}\partial_{\psi}V(\psi)\\
\psi(0,\cdot)=a(\tau_{0})^{(D-2)/2}f\\
\partial_{\tau}\psi(0,\cdot)=\frac{1}{2}(D-2)a(\tau_{0})^{(D-4)/2}\dot{a}(\tau_{0})f+a(\tau_{0})^{(D-2)/2}g\>,\end{cases}

(5.2)

where

h(\tau)=\left[\frac{1}{2}(D-2)-2\xi(D-1)\right]\left[\dot{H}+\frac{D-2}{2}H^{2}\right]~.

(5.3)

Note that Equation (
5.3
) is Riccati’s form of the Hubble parameter
H(\tau)
. In particular,
H(\tau)
could be thought of as a solution of Riccati’s equation, see for example,
[
9
]
.
In the rest of the paper we simply take several assumptions as follows.

Assumption 1
.

h(\tau)\leq 0
and
\partial_{\tau}h(\tau)\geq 0
for all
\tau\in\mathrm{I\hskip-1.99168ptR}^{+}
.

This assumption follows that
h(\tau)
tends to vanish as
\tau\to+\infty
. For example, the function
h(\tau)
may have the form of either
-m\mathrm{e}^{-n\tau}
or
-m\tau^{-n}
with
m,n\in\mathrm{I\hskip-1.99168ptR}^{+}
. The latter function for
n=2
could be related to a cosmological model where the scale factor
a(\tau)
has a polynomial form. This occurs, for example, in the standard cosmological models discussed in Section
2
.

Assumption 2
.

The scalar potential has the form

V(\phi)=-\frac{\epsilon}{p+1}\>\phi^{p+1}\>,

(5.4)

with
\epsilon
is a small positive parameter and
p\in\mathrm{I\hskip-1.99168ptR}^{+}
.

Assumption 3
.

\int_{0}^{\infty}a^{[D+2-(D-2)p]/2}d\tau=A(\tau_{0})<+\infty
for all
\tau_{0}\in\mathrm{I\hskip-1.99168ptR}^{+}
.

Thus, we can write the differential equation as

\partial_{\tau}^{2}\psi-\Delta\psi=h(\tau)\psi+\epsilon P(\tau,\psi)\>,

(5.5)

where
h(\tau)
is given by (
5.3
) and
P(\tau,\psi)=-a^{[D+2-(D-2)p]/2}~\psi^{p}
.

Now, suppose we have the nonhomogenous linear equation

\begin{cases}\partial_{\tau}^{2}\eta-\Delta\eta=h(\tau)\eta+P(\tau,x)\\
\eta(0,\cdot)=a(\tau_{0})^{(D-2)/2}f\\
\partial_{\tau}\eta(0,\cdot)=\frac{1}{2}(D-2)a(\tau_{0})^{(D-4)/2}\dot{a}(\tau_{0})f+a(\tau_{0})^{(D-2)/2}g\>,\end{cases}

(5.6)

where
h(\tau)
is given by (
5.3
) and
\tau_{0}>0
is arbitary real number. First, we prove the following lemma,

Lemma 6
.

Let
\eta
be a solution of linear Equation (
5.6
) with compact support. Let
P(\tau,x)
be a
C^{0}\left(\mathrm{I\hskip-1.99168ptR}^{+},H^{k}(\mathrm{I\hskip-1.99168ptR}^{D-1})\right)
function such that
\int_{0}^{\infty}\>\|P(s)\|_{H^{k}(\mathrm{I\hskip-1.99168ptR}^{D-1})}\>ds<\infty
. If Assumption
1
holds, then we have the following inequality,

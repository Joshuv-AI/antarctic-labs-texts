Anomalous quantum oscillations from boson-mediated interband scattering
Léo Mangeolle, Johannes Knolle

Abstract
Quantum oscillations (QO) in metals refer to the periodic variation of thermodynamic and transport properties as a function of inverse applied magnetic field. QO frequencies are normally associated with semi-classical trajectories of Fermi surface orbits but recent experiments challenge the canonical description. We develop a theory of composite frequency quantum oscillations (CFQO) in two-dimensional Fermi liquids with several Fermi surfaces and interband scattering mediated by a dynamical boson, e.g. phonons or spin fluctuations. Specifically, we show that CFQO arise from oscillations in the fermionic self-energy with anomalous frequency splitting and distinct strongly non-Lifshitz-Kosevich temperature dependencies. Our theory goes beyond the framework of semi-classical Fermi surface trajectories highlighting the role of many-body effects. We provide experimental predictions and discuss the effect of non-equilibrium boson occupation in driven systems.



Introduction:

Magneto-oscillations – or “quantum” oscillations (QO) – have been instrumental in probing Fermi surfaces since their discovery in 1930.
They appear in both thermodynamic quantities such as the magnetic susceptibility – the de Haas - van Alphen (dHvA) oscillations
[
1
]
–
and transport properties such as the electric conductivity – the Shubnikov - de Haas (SdH) oscillations
[
2

The semiclassical theory developed by Onsager
[
3
]
and the subsequent calculations by Lifshitz and Kosevich
[
4
]

relate the fundamental frequencies appearing in QO experiments, as well as the temperature dependence of their amplitude – the Lifshitz-Kosevich (LK) behavior –,
to geometric properties of the Fermi surface (FS). Thus, QO allow for the precise determination of FSs
[
5
,
6
]
and the inclusion of Berry phase effects in its semi-classical description established the field of “fermiology”
[
7
,
8
]
.

Other QO frequencies in addition to the fundamental ones often appear in magnetic breakdown situations
[
9
,
10
]
,
and are proportional to the extremal enclosed area of tunneling-allowed semi-classical orbits.
Their temperature dependence still follows the usual LK dependence,
R_{\rm LK}^{[m]}(T)\equiv\frac{2\pi^{2}Tm}{\sinh(2\pi^{2}Tm)}
,
with an effective mass
m
consistent with the semi-classical picture.
However, recent experiments
[
11
,
12
]
have observed unusual CFQO frequencies set by the difference of two fundamental frequencies not connected via magnetic breakdown. These
difference frequency
quantum oscillations (DFQO)
[
13
]
do not have an intuitive semi-classical picture à la Onsager and are, in that sense,
a purely quantum effect induced by scattering between distinct FSs.
Their temperature dependence follows the LK law with a typical temperature set by the difference between the masses of the two FSs involved in the scattering, allowing them to survive thermal smearing to much higher temperatures than the fundamental frequency QOs.
More broadly, CFQO can be thought of as generalizations of magneto-intersubband QO
[
14
,
15
]
known in coupled 2DEGs and quasi-two-dimensional materials, which may also arise from inter-band interactions
[
16
]
.
Remarkably, a whole range of known (bulk) metals possibly display CFQO, see Ref.
[
17
]
for a recent review, and other correlated quantum materials with unusual QO temperature dependence
[
18
,
19
,
20
]
have been reported. The complex QO spectrum of FeSe displays possible DFQO
[
21
]
which could originate from two FSs connected by strong spin fluctuation scattering observed experimentally
[
22
]
calling for a better understanding of many-body effects.

has two competing effects: it increases the boson population

, thereby enhancing the additional DFQO amplitude, and for
\zeta\neq 0
it increases thermal smearing following
R_{\rm LK}^{[m_{\delta}]}(T_{f})
. For fixed
T_{b}
, even at
\zeta=0
the presence of a finite density of bosons suppresses the thermal activation behavior at
T_{0}\rightarrow 0
and we, again, obtain a strong non-LK behavior. Interestingly, a controlled way of tuning the boson occupation in experiment would permit a determination of the electron boson coupling strength or extraction of the subsystems effective temperatures.

Conclusion:

We have shown that interband scattering of electrons mediated by a dynamical boson generates difference frequency quantum oscillations (DFQO) which do not correspond to any semiclassical trajectory. More specifically, they exhibit a frequency splitting and anomalous temperature dependence, both of which are directly related to the boson dispersion
\Omega_{\lambda,\bm{k}}
, and their amplitude is proportional to the fermion-boson coupling strength
|g_{s,\overline{s}}^{\lambda,\bm{k}}|^{2}
. Thus boson-mediated DFQO carry information about the interacting fermion-boson system, and could be a useful experimental tool for probing electron interaction mechanisms in correlated metals.

Our study poses a whole range of further questions, for example the possibility of such oscillations occurring in
single-band
models on the verge of a density wave instability. There, the presence of a low-energy dynamical bosons could lead to precursor QO prior to Fermi surface reconstruction. In addition, a microscopic treatment of non-equilibrium effects on DFQO will be essential for quantitative predictions. Finally, we have shown that long-overlooked QO phenomena appear from boson scattering within the Fermi liquid regime. Exploring the case of critical Fermi surfaces, where self-energy effects on QO are large
[
33
,
49
]
, promises even more surprises.

Appendix A
Free theory in a magnetic field

We consider the free Hamiltonian:

\displaystyle H_{0}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\bm{p}}\sum_{s=\pm}\left[\frac{1}{2m_{s}}\left(\alpha_{s}^{2}(p_{x}-\text{p}_{s}^{x}-y)^{2}+{\alpha_{s}^{-2}}(p_{y}-\text{p}_{s}^{y})^{2}\right)-\mu_{s}\right]|\bm{p},s\rangle\langle\bm{p},s|.

(9)

A.1
Diagonalization

Start from the standard diagonalization identity (where
p_{y}\equiv-i\partial_{y}
),

\displaystyle\left[-\left(\partial_{y}\right)^{2}+\left(y-p_{x}\right)^{2}-\big(2n+1\big)\right]\psi_{n,p_{x}}(\bm{r})

\displaystyle=0,

(10)

with eigenfunctions
\psi_{n,p_{x}}(\bm{r})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{L_{x}}}{e}^{ip_{x}x}\psi_{n}\big(y-p_{x}\big)
,
where
\psi_{n}(\xi)=e^{-\xi^{2}/2}H_{n}\left[\xi\right]
and
H_{n}
is the
n
th Hermite polynomial
normalized such that
\int\text{d}\xi\,\psi_{n^{\prime}}(\xi)\psi_{n}(\xi)=\delta_{n,n^{\prime}}
.
With the change of variables
y\rightarrow\alpha y
, it is straightforward to show that

\displaystyle\left[{\alpha^{-2}}\left(-i\partial_{y}\right)^{2}+\alpha^{2}\left(y-p_{x}\right)^{2}-\big(2n+1\big)\right]\psi_{n,\alpha p_{x}}(x/\alpha,y\alpha)

\displaystyle=0,

(11)

and using
(-i\partial_{y}-\text{p}_{y})\left(e^{i\text{p}_{y}y}\;\cdot\,\right)=e^{i\text{p}_{y}y}(-i\partial_{y})
,

\displaystyle\left[{\alpha^{-2}}\left(-i\partial_{y}-\text{p}_{y}\right)^{2}+\alpha^{2}\left(y-(p_{x}-\text{p}_{x})\right)^{2}-\big(2n+1\big)\right]e^{i\text{p}_{y}y}\psi_{n,\alpha(p_{x}-\text{p}_{x})}(x/\alpha,y\alpha)

\displaystyle=0.

(12)

Introducing orthogonal basis states
|s\rangle
for the quantum number
s
,
this puts the free Hamiltonian into a diagonal form
H_{0}=\sum_{s,n,p_{x}}\varepsilon_{s,n}|s,n,p_{x}\rangle\langle s,n,p_{x}|
,
with energies
\varepsilon_{s,n}=\frac{{\mathsf{e}}B}{m_{s}}\left(n+\tfrac{1}{2}\right)
,
and eigenstates
|s,n,p_{x}\rangle=\psi_{s,n,p_{x}}\otimes|s\rangle
, with eigenfunctions

\displaystyle\psi_{s,n,p_{x}}(\bm{r})=\sqrt{\alpha_{s}/L_{x}}\,e^{i(p_{x}-\text{p}_{s}^{x})x}e^{i\text{p}_{s}^{y}y}\psi_{n}\big(\alpha_{s}(y-(p_{x}-\text{p}_{s}^{x}))\big)

(13)

normalized such that
\langle s^{\prime},n^{\prime},p_{x}^{\prime}|s,n,p_{x}\rangle=\delta_{s,s^{\prime}}\delta_{n,n^{\prime}}\frac{2\pi}{L_{x}}\delta(p_{x}-p_{x}^{\prime})
.

A.2
Ladder operator

Because of the recurrence properties

\displaystyle\partial_{\xi}\psi_{n}(\alpha\xi)

\displaystyle=\alpha\sqrt{n/2}\psi_{n-1}(\alpha\xi)-\alpha\sqrt{(n+1)/2}\psi_{n+1}(\alpha\xi),

(14)

\displaystyle\alpha\xi\psi_{n}(\alpha\xi)

\displaystyle=\sqrt{n/2}\psi_{n-1}(\alpha\xi)+\sqrt{(n+1)/2}\psi_{n+1}(\alpha\xi),

(15)

the creation-destruction operators in the basis of Landau eigenstates of the band indexed by
s
are

\displaystyle b_{s}^{\dagger}

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(\alpha_{s}(p_{x}-y-\text{p}_{s}^{x})+\alpha_{s}^{-1}i(p_{y}-\text{p}_{s}^{y})\right),

(16)

\displaystyle b_{s}

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(\alpha_{s}(p_{x}-y-\text{p}_{s}^{x})-\alpha_{s}^{-1}i(p_{y}-\text{p}_{s}^{y})\right).

(17)

They verify

\displaystyle b_{s}^{\dagger}(-1)^{n}\psi_{s,n,p_{x}}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{n+1}(-1)^{n+1}\psi_{s,n+1,p_{x}},

(18)

\displaystyle b_{s}(-1)^{n}\psi_{s,n,p_{x}}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{n}(-1)^{n-1}\psi_{s,n-1,p_{x}}.

(19)

The velocity operator along axis
x
, defined as
\bm{v}_{x}=\partial_{\bm{p}_{x}}H_{0}
, reads in band
s

\displaystyle v_{s}^{x}

\displaystyle=\alpha_{s}^{2}(p_{x}-y-\text{p}_{s}^{x})/m_{s}=\alpha_{s}\frac{b_{s}+b_{s}^{\dagger}}{\sqrt{2}m_{s}}.

(20)

One can also check that
H_{0}=\sum_{s}\frac{{\mathsf{e}}B}{m_{s}}\left(b_{s}^{\dagger}b_{s}+\tfrac{1}{2}\right)
.

A.3
Free propagator

The non-interacting electron Green’s function is

\displaystyle G_{0}(z)

\displaystyle=\sum_{s=\pm}\sum_{n,p_{x}}\frac{|s,n,p_{x}\rangle\langle s,n,p_{x}|}{z-\varepsilon_{s,n}},

(21)

with
z
the complex frequency variable. Explicitly, written in components this reads

\displaystyle(G_{0})_{s,s^{\prime}}(\bm{r},\bm{r}^{\prime},z)

\displaystyle=\delta_{s,s^{\prime}}\sum_{n,p_{x}}\psi_{s,n,p_{x}}(\bm{r})\left[z-\varepsilon_{s,n}\right]^{-1}\psi^{*}_{s,n,p_{x}}(\bm{r}^{\prime}).

(22)

Introducing
\bm{R}=\tfrac{1}{2}(\bm{r}+\bm{r}^{\prime})
, one can write after a few manipulations

\displaystyle\textstyle{\sum_{p_{x}}}\psi_{s,n,p_{x}}(\bm{r})\psi^{*}_{s,n,p_{x}}(\bm{r}^{\prime})

(23)

\displaystyle=e^{i\text{p}_{s}^{y}(y-y^{\prime})}\int\frac{\text{d}^{2}\bm{k}}{(2\pi)^{2}}e^{i\bm{k}(\bm{r}-\bm{r}^{\prime})}2\,(-1)^{n}\int\text{d}\xi e^{-ik_{y}(2/\alpha_{s})(\xi-\alpha_{s}(R_{y}-k_{x}))}\psi_{n}(\xi-2\alpha_{s}(R_{y}-k_{x}))\psi_{n}(\xi).

We now apply the formula

\displaystyle\int\text{d}\xi e^{i\xi\lambda_{x}}\psi_{n}(\xi)\psi_{n}(\xi+\lambda_{y})=L_{n}(\tfrac{1}{2}\bm{\lambda}^{2})e^{-\tfrac{1}{4}\bm{\lambda}^{2}}e^{-i\lambda_{x}\lambda_{y}/2}

(24)

where
L_{n}
is the
n
th Laguerre polynomial, normalized such that
\int_{0}^{\infty}\text{d}te^{-t}L_{n}(t)L_{n^{\prime}}(t)=\delta_{n,n^{\prime}}
,
to the particular case
\lambda_{x}=-(2/\alpha_{s})k_{y}
and
\lambda_{y}=-2\alpha_{s}(R_{y}-k_{x})
.
This yields

\displaystyle\textstyle{\sum_{p_{x}}}\psi_{s,n,p_{x}}(\bm{r})\psi^{*}_{s,n,p_{x}}(\bm{r}^{\prime})

\displaystyle=e^{i\text{p}_{s}^{y}(y-y^{\prime})}\int\frac{\text{d}^{2}\bm{k}}{(2\pi)^{2}}e^{i\bm{k}(\bm{r}-\bm{r}^{\prime})}e^{i(y+y^{\prime})(x-x^{\prime})/2}(-1)^{n}2L_{n}(2r_{s}^{2}(\bm{k}))e^{-r_{s}^{2}(\bm{k})},

(25)

where we introduce the shorthand
r_{s}^{2}(\bm{p})=p_{y}^{2}/\alpha_{s}^{2}+p_{x}^{2}\alpha_{s}^{2}
. Thus,

\displaystyle(G_{0})_{s,s^{\prime}}(\bm{r},\bm{r}^{\prime},z)

\displaystyle=\delta_{s,s^{\prime}}e^{i\varphi(\bm{r},\bm{r}^{\prime})}e^{i\text{p}_{s}^{y}(y-y^{\prime})}\int\frac{\text{d}^{2}\bm{p}}{(2\pi)^{2}}e^{i\bm{p}(\bm{r}-\bm{r}^{\prime})}(\bar{G}_{0})_{s,s}(\bm{p},z),

(26)

\displaystyle\varphi(\bm{r},\bm{r}^{\prime})

\displaystyle:=(y+y^{\prime})(x-x^{\prime})/2,

(27)

\displaystyle(\bar{G}_{0})_{s,s}(\bm{p},z)

\displaystyle=2\sum_{n}(-1)^{n}L_{n}(2r_{s}^{2}(\bm{p}))e^{-r_{s}^{2}(\bm{p})}\left[z-\epsilon_{s,n}\right]^{-1},

(28)

and notably the translational dependence of
G_{0}(z)
can be factorized as a mere phase factor.

Appendix B
Interacting theory in a field

B.1
Disorder scattering

We consider intraband local-in-space impurity scattering
H_{\rm dis}=\sum_{s,\mathbf{r}}u(\mathbf{r})c^{\dagger}_{s,\mathbf{r}}c^{\vphantom{\dagger}}_{s,\mathbf{r}}
,
where the disorder potential
u(\mathbf{r})
is a gaussian random variable with zero mean and two-point correlation

\langle u(\mathbf{r})u(\mathbf{r}^{\prime})\rangle=\bar{u}_{0}^{2}\delta(\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}^{\prime})
.

The corresponding self-energy in real space, obtained by averaging over disorder realization, is

\displaystyle\Sigma^{\rm dis}_{s,s^{\prime}}(\bm{r},\bm{r}^{\prime},z)

\displaystyle=\delta_{s,s^{\prime}}\langle u(\bm{r})u(\bm{r}^{\prime})\rangle G^{\rm dis}_{s,s^{\prime}}(\bm{r},\bm{r}^{\prime},z)=\bar{u}_{0}^{2}\delta_{s,s^{\prime}}\delta(\bm{r}-\bm{r}^{\prime})G^{\rm dis}_{s,s}(\bm{r},\bm{r},z)

(29)

where
G_{\rm dis}^{-1}=G_{0}^{-1}-\Sigma_{\rm dis}
and we assumed the non-crossing approximation
and did not include any corrections from boson scattering.
We now assume, and will find self-consistently, a pure imaginary diagonal form of
\Sigma_{\rm dis}
in the basis of Landau eigenstates:

\displaystyle(\Sigma^{\rm dis})_{s,s^{\prime}}^{n,n^{\prime}}(p_{x},p_{x}^{\prime};z)

\displaystyle=-i\Gamma_{s}^{\rm dis}\,{\rm sign}({\rm Im}(z))\,\delta_{s,s^{\prime}}\delta_{n,n^{\prime}}\frac{2\pi}{L_{x}}\delta(p_{x}-p_{x}^{\prime}),

(30)

where
\Gamma_{s}^{\rm dis}
is to be determined.
Expressing
G_{\rm dis}
in the basis of Landau eigenstates and using the Poisson formula

\displaystyle\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}f(l)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=-\infty}^{+\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}\text{d}l\,e^{i2\pi kl}f(l),

(31)

to resum the Landau level summation, and using
\sum_{p_{x}}\psi_{s,n,p_{x}}(\bm{r})\psi^{*}_{s,n,p_{x}}(\bm{r})=\frac{1}{2\pi}
, one obtains

\displaystyle G^{\rm dis}_{s,s}(\bm{r},\bm{r},z)

\displaystyle\approx\frac{1}{2\pi}\sum_{p\in\mathbb{Z}}(-1)^{p}\int_{1/2}^{\infty}\text{d}u\frac{e^{i2\pi pu}}{z+\mu_{s}+i\Gamma_{s}^{\rm dis}\,{\rm sign}({\rm Im}(z))-\omega_{{\rm c},s}u}.

(32)

Upon extending the lower bound of the integral to
-\infty
and contour integration,
the
p
contribution in the above sum comes with a
|p|
th power of the Dingle factor.
Thus we keep only
|p|\leq 1
, and absorb the real part of the
p=0
component into a redefinition of
\mu_{s}
. This yields

\displaystyle G^{\rm dis}_{s,s}(\bm{r},\bm{r},z)

\displaystyle\approx-\frac{i}{2\omega_{{\rm c},s}}\,{\rm sign}({\rm Im}(z))\,\left[1-2e^{-2\pi\Gamma_{s}^{\rm dis}/\omega_{{\rm c},s}}e^{i2\pi\tfrac{z+\mu_{s}}{\omega_{{\rm c},s}}\,{\rm sign}({\rm Im}(z))}\right].

(33)

The oscillating term in Eq.(
33
) oscillates with the frequency of the
s
band, therefore it cannot generate difference frequency quantum oscillations
to the leading order in Dingle factors, as we show later. Neglecting this contribution by keeping only the zeroth power of Dingle factors in Eq.(
33
),
one finds the self-consistent value
\Gamma_{s}^{\rm dis}\equiv\overline{u}_{0}^{2}/2\omega_{{\rm c},s}
.
The fermionic Green’s function dressed by disorder then reads

\displaystyle(G_{\rm dis})_{s,s^{\prime}}(\bm{r},\bm{r}^{\prime},z)

\displaystyle=\delta_{s,s^{\prime}}e^{i\varphi(\bm{r},\bm{r}^{\prime})}e^{i\text{p}_{s}^{y}(y-y^{\prime})}\int\frac{\text{d}^{2}\bm{p}}{(2\pi)^{2}}e^{i\bm{p}(\bm{r}-\bm{r}^{\prime})}(\bar{G}_{\rm dis})_{s,s}(\bm{p},z),

(34)

\displaystyle(\bar{G}_{\rm dis})_{s,s}(\bm{p},z)

\displaystyle=2\sum_{n}(-1)^{n}L_{n}(2r_{s}^{2}(\bm{p}))e^{-r_{s}^{2}(\bm{p})}\left[z-\epsilon_{s,n}+i\Gamma_{s}^{\rm dis}\,{\rm sign}({\rm Im}(z))\right]^{-1}.

(35)

B.2
Electron-boson self-energy

We now consider the fermion-boson interaction hamiltonian
H_{\rm int}
defined in the main text.
Because the boson changes the fermionic quantum number
s\leftrightarrow\overline{s}
, the fermionic self-energy is diagonal in
s
.
Because the boson is neutral, the fermionic self-energy factorizes in the same way as the free propagator,
and can be expressed as

\displaystyle\Sigma^{\rm int}_{s,s^{\prime}}(\bm{r},\bm{r}^{\prime},z)

\displaystyle=e^{i\varphi(\bm{r},\bm{r}^{\prime})}e^{i\text{p}_{s}^{y}(y-y^{\prime})}\int\frac{d^{2}\bm{p}}{(2\pi)^{2}}e^{i\bm{p}(\bm{r}-\bm{r}^{\prime})}\,\delta_{s,s^{\prime}}\,\bar{\Sigma}^{\rm int}_{s,s}(\bm{p},z).

(36)

To the order of one loop, using the bare bosonic propagator
(D_{0})_{\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}}(\bm{k},i\omega_{\rm m})=\delta_{\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}}\frac{2\Omega_{\lambda,\bm{k}}}{(i\omega_{\rm m})^{2}-\Omega_{\lambda,\bm{k}}^{2}}

and the dressed fermionic propagator
G_{\rm dis}
for internal lines, one finds

\displaystyle\bar{\Sigma}^{\rm int}_{s,s}(\bm{p},z)

\displaystyle=-T\sum_{\omega_{\rm m}}\sum_{\lambda}\int\frac{d^{2}\bm{k}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\frac{1}{2\Omega_{\lambda,\bm{k}}}\left|g_{s,\overline{s},\bm{p}}^{\lambda,\bm{k}}\right|^{2}\,(D_{0})_{\lambda,\lambda}(\bm{k},i\omega_{\rm m})\,\bar{G}^{\rm dis}_{\overline{s},\overline{s}}(\bm{p}+\bm{k},z+i\omega_{\rm m}),

(37)

where
\omega_{\rm m}=2\text{m}\pi T,\rm m\in\mathbb{Z}
.

The Matsubara summation yields, up to a correction which vanishes in the limit
\Gamma_{s}^{\rm dis}\rightarrow 0
,

\displaystyle\bar{\Sigma}^{\rm int}_{s,s^{\prime}}(\bm{p},z)

\displaystyle\approx\delta_{s,s^{\prime}}\int\frac{\text{d}^{2}\bm{k}}{(2\pi)^{2}}e^{-r_{\overline{s}}^{2}(\bm{p}+\bm{k})}\sum_{\lambda}\frac{1}{2\Omega_{\lambda,\bm{k}}}\left|g_{s,\overline{s}}^{\lambda,\bm{k}}\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\times 2\sum_{n}(-1)^{n}L_{n}\left(2r_{\overline{s}}^{2}(\bm{p}+\bm{k})\right)\left[\frac{n_{\rm B}(\Omega_{\lambda,\bm{k}})+n_{\rm F}(\tilde{\varepsilon}_{\overline{s},n})}{z+\Omega_{\lambda,\bm{k}}-\tilde{\varepsilon}_{\overline{s},n}}-(\Omega_{\lambda,\bm{k}}\leftrightarrow-\Omega_{\lambda,\bm{k}})\right],

(38)

with
\tilde{\varepsilon}_{\overline{s},n}\equiv\varepsilon_{\overline{s},n}-i\Gamma_{s}^{\rm dis}\,{\rm sign}({\rm Im}(z))
.
The neglected contribution has poles at
z=i(2{\rm n}+1)\pi T-\Omega_{\lambda,\bm{k}},{\rm n}\in\mathbb{Z}
, away from the real axis,
therefore it does not contain magneto-oscillations of
\bar{\Sigma}^{\rm int}_{s,s^{\prime}}(\bm{p},z)
and is thus not relevant in the present study.

Expressed in the basis of eigenfunctions of the free hamiltonian,

\displaystyle(\Sigma^{\rm int})_{s,s^{\prime}}^{n,n^{\prime}}(p_{x},p_{x}^{\prime};z)

\displaystyle=\int\frac{\text{d}^{2}\bm{q}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\bar{\Sigma}^{\rm int}_{s,s}(\bm{q},z)\times I_{s,s}^{n,n^{\prime}}(p_{x},p_{x}^{\prime};\bm{q})

(39)

where

\displaystyle I_{s,s}^{n,n^{\prime}}(p_{x},p_{x}^{\prime};\bm{q})

\displaystyle\equiv\int\text{d}^{2}\bm{r}\text{d}^{2}\bm{r}^{\prime}e^{i\varphi(\bm{r},\bm{r}^{\prime})}e^{i\text{p}_{s}^{y}(y-y^{\prime})}e^{i\bm{q}(\bm{r}-\bm{r}^{\prime})}\psi^{*}_{s,n,p_{x}}(\bm{r})\psi_{s,n^{\prime},p_{x}^{\prime}}(\bm{r}^{\prime})

\displaystyle=(-1)^{n}\;\frac{2\pi}{L_{x}}\delta(p_{x}-p_{x}^{\prime})\,4\pi\int\text{d}\xi\,e^{-i\xi 2q_{y}/\alpha_{s}}\;\psi_{n}(\xi+\alpha_{s}q_{x})\psi_{n^{\prime}}(\xi-\alpha_{s}q_{x})

\displaystyle=(-1)^{n}\;\frac{2\pi}{L_{x}}\delta(p_{x}-p_{x}^{\prime})\,4\pi\left(-2\alpha_{s}q_{x}-i2q_{y}/\alpha_{s}\right)^{n^{\prime}-n}\;L_{n}^{(n^{\prime}-n)}(2r_{s}^{2}(\bm{q}))\;e^{-r_{s}^{2}(\bm{q})},

(40)

where the second line was obtained after a few simple manipulations,
and the third line results from the identity

\displaystyle\int\text{d}\xi e^{i\xi\lambda_{x}}\psi_{n}(\xi)\psi_{n^{\prime}}(\xi+\lambda_{y})

\displaystyle=e^{-i\lambda_{x}\lambda_{y}/2}e^{-\tfrac{1}{4}\bm{\lambda}^{2}}\left(\lambda_{y}+i\lambda_{x}\right)^{n^{\prime}-n}L_{n}^{(n^{\prime}-n)}(\tfrac{1}{2}\bm{\lambda}^{2}),

(41)

applied to the specific case
\lambda_{x}=-2q_{y}/\alpha_{s}
and
\lambda_{y}=-2\alpha_{s}q_{x}
,
where
L_{n}^{(m)}
the
(n,m)
th associated Laguerre polynomial.

Combined with Eq.(
38
), this yields Eq.(
3
) in the main text, where explicitly

\displaystyle I_{s,l}^{n,n^{\prime}}(\bm{k})

\displaystyle\equiv 2\int\frac{\text{d}^{2}\bm{q}}{(2\pi)^{2}}e^{-r_{s}^{2}(\bm{q})}e^{-r_{\overline{s}}^{2}(\bm{q}+\bm{k})}\left(-2\alpha_{s}q_{x}-i2q_{y}/\alpha_{s}\right)^{n^{\prime}-n}\;(-1)^{n}L_{n}^{(n^{\prime}-n)}(2r_{s}^{2}(\bm{q}))\,(-1)^{l}L_{l}\left(2r_{\overline{s}}^{2}(\bm{q}+\bm{k})\right).

(42)

B.3
Dressed fermionic Green’s function

The full inverse Green’s function
G^{-1}=G_{\rm dis}^{-1}-\Sigma^{\rm int}
, expressed in the basis of Landau eigenstates, is

\displaystyle(G^{-1})_{s,s^{\prime}}^{n,n^{\prime}}(p_{x},p_{x}^{\prime};z)

\displaystyle=(G_{0}^{-1})_{s,s^{\prime}}^{n,n^{\prime}}(p_{x},p_{x}^{\prime};z)-(\Sigma^{\rm dis})_{s,s^{\prime}}^{n,n^{\prime}}(p_{x},p_{x}^{\prime};z)-(\Sigma^{\rm int})_{s,s^{\prime}}^{n,n^{\prime}}(p_{x},p_{x}^{\prime};z).

(43)

It is diagonal in its
s
and
p_{x}
indices, as well as
p_{x}
-independent, therefore the Green’s function has the form

\displaystyle G_{s,s^{\prime}}^{n,n^{\prime}}(p_{x},p_{x}^{\prime};z)

\displaystyle=\delta_{s,s^{\prime}}\frac{2\pi}{L_{x}}\delta(p_{x}-p_{x}^{\prime})\,\left(\delta_{n,n^{\prime}}(z-\varepsilon_{s,n})-\Delta_{n,n^{\prime}}^{s}(z)+i\Gamma_{n,n^{\prime}}^{s}(z)\right)^{-1}.

(44)

Besides, the off-diagonal in
(n,n^{\prime})
coefficients of
G^{-1}
are always much smaller than its diagonal ones
provided that
\left|\Sigma^{\rm int}_{s,s}\right|\lesssim\Gamma^{\rm dis}_{s}
, which we will assume.
Now, for any matrix
\mathsf{M}_{n,n^{\prime}}
such that
\mathsf{M}_{n,n}=O(1)
whereas
\mathsf{M}_{n\neq n^{\prime}}=O(\delta)
with
\delta
a small parameter,
it is easy to show that
(\mathsf{M}^{-1})_{n,n}=1/\mathsf{M}_{n,n}+O(\delta^{2})
– for instance by using the general inversion formula

\mathsf{M}^{-1}=\tilde{\mathsf{M}}^{\top}/\left|\mathsf{M}\right|
, with
\tilde{\mathsf{M}}
the comatrix and
\left|\mathsf{M}\right|
the determinant of
\mathsf{M}
.
Applying this to the particular case
\mathsf{M}=G^{-1}
, we obtain the diagonal elements

\displaystyle\Delta_{n,n}^{s}(z)

\displaystyle\approx{\rm Re}\,(\Sigma^{\rm int})_{s,s}^{n,n}(z),

(45)

\displaystyle\Gamma_{n,n}^{s}(z)

\displaystyle\approx-{\rm Im}\,(\Sigma^{\rm int})_{s,s}^{n,n}(z)+\Gamma_{s}^{\rm dis}\,{\rm sign}({\rm Im}(z)).

(46)

Appendix C
Self-energy oscillations

C.1
General calculation

The summation over level index
n
in Eq.(
38
) (relabeled

here) can be performed using Poisson resummation.
This step relies on the fact that
(-1)^{l}L_{l}(x)
can be approximated by a smooth function of

a
continuous
variable around

,
in terms of the Airy function.
[

We find

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{\overline{s}}^{\lambda}(\bm{q},\bm{k};z)

\displaystyle\equiv\sum_{l}\frac{n_{\rm B}(\Omega_{\lambda,\bm{k}})+n_{\rm F}(\tilde{\varepsilon}_{\overline{s},l})}{z+\Omega_{\lambda,\bm{k}}-\tilde{\varepsilon}_{\overline{s},l}}\,(-1)^{l}L_{l}\left(2r_{\overline{s}}^{2}(\bm{q}+\bm{k})\right)

(47)

\displaystyle\approx\frac{1}{\omega_{{\rm c},\overline{s}}}\sum_{p\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{1/2}^{\infty}\text{d}l\;e^{i2\pi pl}\,\frac{n_{\rm B}(\Omega_{\lambda,\bm{k}})+n_{\rm F}(\tilde{\varepsilon}_{\overline{s},l})}{l-\xi_{\lambda,\overline{s}}(\bm{k},z)-i(\Gamma_{\overline{s}}^{\rm dis}/\omega_{{\rm c},\overline{s}}){\rm sign}({\rm Im}(z))}\,(-1)^{l}L_{l}\left(2r_{\overline{s}}^{2}(\bm{q}+\bm{k})\right),

.

The

Extending the lower integration bound to

and performing the contour integration, one obtains

\mathcal{S}_{\overline{s}}^{\lambda}(\bm{q},\bm{k};z)=\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{\overline{s},\lambda}(\bm{q},\bm{k};z)+\mathcal{S}^{\prime\prime}_{\overline{s},\lambda}(\bm{q},\bm{k};z)+\dots
,
where “+ …” contains the

non-oscillatory term and
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
(resp.
\mathcal{S}^{\prime\prime}
) results from zeros of the denominator (resp. poles of the numerator) in Eq.(

Explicitly, we find

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{\overline{s},\lambda}(\bm{q},\bm{k};z)

D.1.1
General calculations

We compute

\displaystyle=\int\text{d}\epsilon\frac{\hat{\sigma}_{xx}(\epsilon)}{4T\cosh^{2}\left(\frac{\epsilon}{2T}\right)},

\displaystyle\hat{\sigma}_{xx}(\epsilon)

\displaystyle=-\frac{\mathsf{e}^{2}}{\pi}\frac{1}{L_{x}L_{y}}\,\mathbf{Tr}_{n,p_{x},s}\left[\hat{v}_{x}\,{\rm Im}G(\epsilon)\,\hat{v}_{x}\,{\rm Im}G(\epsilon)\right].

n
in Eq.(

p\in\mathbb{Z}
the harmonics index.

-\infty

\displaystyle\alpha_{s}^{2}\frac{\Gamma^{s}_{\xi_{s}(\epsilon)}(\epsilon)/\omega_{{\rm c},s}}{1+4\left(\Gamma^{s}_{\xi_{s}(\epsilon)}(\epsilon)/\omega_{{\rm c},s}\right)^{2}}

D.2.3
Sum frequency oscillations

Because the main dHvA difference frequency oscillation decays as rapidly as the sum frequency when increasing temperature, we also compute the leading two sum frequency terms:

\displaystyle\tilde{\rho}_{\rm sf}(\epsilon)

\displaystyle\approx-N_{\phi}\sum_{s}\frac{1}{\omega_{{\rm c},s}^{2}}\,R_{\rm D}^{s}\,\sum_{\lambda}\sum_{\eta=\pm}\Big(e^{i2\pi\left(\xi_{s}(\epsilon)+\xi_{\overline{s}}(0)\right)}({\sf\Sigma}^{\prime\prime})_{s,\overline{s}}^{\lambda,\eta}(\epsilon)-e^{i2\pi\left(\xi_{s}(\epsilon)+\xi_{\lambda,\overline{s}}^{\eta}(\epsilon)\right)}i({\sf\Sigma}^{\prime})_{s,\overline{s}}^{\lambda,\eta}(\epsilon)\Big)\;+{\rm h.c.}

(84)

With
\sum_{\eta=\pm}({\sf\Sigma}^{\prime\prime})_{s,\overline{s}}^{\lambda,\eta}(\epsilon)=(\hat{\sf\Sigma}^{\prime\prime})_{s,\overline{s}}^{\lambda}\sum_{\eta=\pm}\eta(\epsilon+\eta\bar{\Omega}_{\lambda}+i\Gamma_{\overline{s}}^{\rm dis})^{-1}

and
({\sf\Sigma}^{\prime})_{s,\overline{s}}^{\lambda,\eta}(\epsilon)=\left[n_{\rm B}(\bar{\Omega}_{\lambda})+n_{\rm F}(\eta\epsilon+\bar{\Omega}_{\lambda})\right]\,(\hat{\sf\Sigma}^{\prime})_{s,\overline{s}}^{\lambda}
,
we evaluate the energy integrals using Eq.(
82
) and the integrals

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\text{d}\epsilon\,n_{\rm F}(\epsilon)\,e^{iX\epsilon}

\displaystyle=\frac{-2i\pi T}{2\sinh(\pi XT)},

(85)

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\text{d}\epsilon\,n_{\rm F}(\epsilon)\,n_{\rm F}(\eta\epsilon+\Omega)\,e^{iX\epsilon}

\displaystyle=\frac{+2i\pi T}{2\sinh(\pi XT)}\left(n_{\rm B}(\Omega)+\eta\,n_{\rm B}(-\eta\Omega)\,e^{-i\eta\Omega X}\right),

(86)

which together with
\eta\,n_{\rm B}(-\eta\Omega)=-\left(n_{\rm B}(\Omega)+\tfrac{1+\eta}{2}\right)
yields

\displaystyle\tilde{n}_{\rm p}^{\rm sf}

\displaystyle\approx\sum_{s,\lambda}\,(\mathsf{A}^{\prime})_{n_{\rm p}}^{s,\lambda}(T)\,\sum_{\eta=\pm}\exp\left(\tfrac{1+\eta}{2}\bar{\Omega}_{\lambda}/T\right)\,\cos\left(2\pi(\xi_{s}(0)+\xi_{\overline{s}}(0)-\eta\bar{\Omega}_{\lambda}/\omega_{{\rm c},s})\right)

\displaystyle+\sum_{s,\lambda}\,(\mathsf{A}^{\prime\prime})_{n_{\rm p}}^{s,\lambda}(T)\,\sin\left(2\pi(\xi_{s}(0)+\xi_{\overline{s}}(0))\right),

(87)

\displaystyle(\mathsf{A}^{\prime})_{n_{\rm p}}^{s,\lambda}(T)

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{1}{2\pi^{2}}\frac{1}{\omega_{{\rm c},s}^{2}}\,R_{\rm D}^{s}\,(\hat{\sf\Sigma}^{\prime})_{s,\overline{s}}^{\lambda}\,n_{\rm B}(\bar{\Omega}_{\lambda})\;\frac{2\pi^{2}T}{\sinh\left(2\pi^{2}(m_{s}+m_{\overline{s}})T\right)},

(88)

\displaystyle(\mathsf{A}^{\prime\prime})_{n_{\rm p}}^{s,\lambda}(T)

\displaystyle\equiv-\frac{1}{2\pi^{2}}\frac{1}{\omega_{{\rm c},s}}\,^{-2\pi\Gamma_{\overline{s}}^{\rm dis}/\omega_{{\rm c},s}}\,(\hat{\sf\Sigma}^{\prime\prime})_{s,\overline{s}}^{\lambda}\;\frac{2\bar{\Omega}_{\lambda}}{\bar{\Omega}_{\lambda}^{2}+(\Gamma_{\overline{s}}^{\rm dis}+\pi T)^{2}}\,R_{\rm LK}^{[m_{s}]}(T).

(89)

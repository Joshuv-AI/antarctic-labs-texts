John’s blow up examples and scattering solutions for semi-linear wave equations
Louie Bernhardt, Volker Schlue, Dongxiao Yu

Abstract
In light of recent work of the third author, we revisit a classic example given by Fritz John of a semi-linear wave equation which exhibits finite in time blow up for all compactly supported data. We present the construction of future global solutions from asymptotic data given in [ 12 ] for this specific example, and clarify the relation of this result of Yu to John’s theorem in [ 6 ] . Furthermore we present a novel blow up result for finite energy solutions satisfying a sign condition due to the first author, and invoke this result to show that the constructed backwards in time solutions blow up in the past.



1
Introduction

At the MATRIX workshop on
Hyperbolic PDEs and Nonlinear Evolution Problems
, in September 2023, Dongxiao Yu presented several global existence results
from asymptotic data
for a class of quasi-linear wave equations
[
12
]
. These
backwards in time
results are compelling because they apply in particular to wave equations for which
forward in time
global existence is not known, or worse, are known to admit blow up solutions.

The topic of this note is the classic example

-\Box u=(\partial_{t}u)^{2}\qquad\text{: on }\mathbb{R}^{3+1}\,.

(1)

This is a
semi-linear
wave equation for which Fritz John showed that
forward in time solutions arising from data of compact support
blow up in finite time
[
6
]
. We recall the statement of this well-known fact, and his argument briefly in Section
2
; excellent expositions of John’s results in
[
6
]
can be found in the lecture notes
[
1
,
2
]
.

The forward in time results use the initial value formulation for (
1
),
and rely on the method of spherical means.
The
backward in time solutions
constructed by Yu
use
Hörmander’s asymptotic system

[
3
,
4
,
5
]
to find an approximate solution,
and rely on energy estimates for the remainder.

A similar philosophy has been applied by Lindblad and Schlue
[
11
]

to obtain global existence from
scattering data, backwards in time
,
for
semi-linear
wave equations which admit
forward in time
global solutions.
The classic examples are here wave equations satisfying the null condition
[
7
]
,

\Box u=Q(\partial u,\partial u)\,

(2)

which, in contrast to (
1
) admit global solutions which asymptote to a given
linear
solution, and

u(t,x)\sim\varepsilon r^{-1}U(q,\omega)\qquad(r\sim t,t\to\infty)

(3)

where
r=|x|
,
q=r-t
, and
\omega=x/|x|
.
In particular, scattering solutions can be constructed from knowledge of the
radiation field

U(q,\omega)
.

The situation is very different for (
1
).
As we will discuss in this note, the existence results for (
1
) that go beyond
almost global existence
for small data, depend on carefully chosen data, either asymptotically or initially.

In Section
3
,
we demonstrate that there are solutions which approach a solution of the asymptotic system,
in the sense that

u(t,x)\sim\varepsilon r^{-1}U(s,q,\omega)\qquad(r\sim t,t\to\infty)

(4)

where
s=\varepsilon\ln(t)
, and
U
satisfies

2U_{sq}+U_{q}^{2}=0.

(5)

Following
[
12
]
,
we first prepare a solution to (
5
) which does not blow up,
and then find a corresponding solution to (
1
).
This construction is a special case of the results in
[
12
]
which apply more generally to
quasi-linear wave equations
. In contrast to (
3
), these solutions
do not have a radiation field
.

The main result that motivates this note is Theorem
3
, in Section
3
:
There do exist
\mathrm{C}^{k}
solutions of (
1
) for
t\geq 0
.
We will call these solutions
future global
.

In view of John’s blow up result
these solutions cannot be compactly supported at
t=0
.
Indeed, while Theorem
1
states that the only
global

C^{k}
solution that arises from compactly supported data is the trivial solution,
it is clear from John’s argument in Section
2
that solutions of (
1
) arising from compactly supported data
blow up both to the future, and to the past
. (They are neither future nor past global.)

This also shows that the blow-up results of John
[
6
]
do not trivially extend from compactly supported data to general finite energy solutions. As we have seen, there are
some
finite energy solutions, namely the solutions of Theorem
3
which are future global.
However, this raises the question if these solutions are also
past global
.

In Section
4
,
we show with an argument that is due to the first author,
that the solutions of Theorem
3
are not
global
:
While they are future global, they
blow up in the past
.
Theorem
11
gives a sufficient sign condition for blow up of finite energy solutions to (
1
), and Corollary
12
shows that the solutions constructed in
[
12
]
satisfy that sign condition.

The occurence of a sign condition is expected in this setting:
Already for the asympototic equation (
5
) the sign of the initial data for
U_{q}
at
s=0
is relevant for existence, for
s>0
.

Thus this note clarifies the relation between the global existence results of the third author,
and the blow up results of John, in the special case of the equation (
1
).
We leave open the question if there are
any
global finite energy solutions to (
1
).

2
John’s blow-up result for the forward solution

Fritz John’s classical paper
[
6
]
contains the result that is most relevant to this note:

Theorem 1
(John ’81)

Suppose
u:\mathds{R}^{3+1}\to\mathds{R}
is a solution of

\displaystyle\partial_{t}^{2}u-\Delta u=(\partial_{t}u)^{2}\,,

(6)

\displaystyle u(0,x)=u_{0}\,,\quad\partial_{t}u(0,x)=u_{1}

(7)

with
smooth data of compact support

u_{0},u_{1}\in\mathrm{C}^{\infty}_{0}
.

If
u\in\mathrm{C}^{2}(\mathds{R}^{3}\times[0,\infty)
, then
u=0
identically.

Remark 2

The theorem refers to
general solutions
with compactly supported data.
The more frequently referred to statement, which is also easier to prove, is the fact that there are
some
solutions that blow up.
These are constructed from
spatially homogeneous
solutions,
u(t,x)=u(t)
, which satisfy the ODE

\partial_{t}u_{t}=(u_{t})^{2}\,.

(8)

This ODE should be compared to (
5
).
In both cases the
sign
of the initial data is crucial:
In (
8
) it is chosen to ensure blow-up,
and for (
5
) it is chosen to
prevent
blow-up.
In view of these simple examples it is also not surprising that a
sign condition
is relevant in the general setting of Section
4
.

The proof of Theorem
1
is a spherical means argument,
and relies on the fact that in
3+1
dimensions
the spherical mean

M_{t,r}[u](x)=\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{|\xi|=1}u(t,x+r\xi)\mathrm{d}\sigma(\xi)

(9)

of any solution to (
6
) satisfies

\partial_{t}^{2}M_{t,r}[ru](x)-\partial_{r}^{2}M_{t,r}[ru](x)=M_{t,r}[r(\partial_{t}u)^{2}](x)\,.

(10)

This well-known reduction to a
1+1
-dimensional wave equation in
(t,r)

then yields the representation formula

M_{t,r}[ru](0)=\frac{1}{2}\bigl((r+t)M_{r+t}[u_{0}](0)+(r-t)M_{r-t}[u_{0}](0)\bigr)+\frac{1}{2}\int_{r-t}^{r+t}\rho M_{\rho}[u_{1}](0)\mathrm{d}\rho\\
+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{r-(t-s)}^{r+(t-s)}\rho M_{s,\rho}[(\partial_{t}u)^{2}](0)\mathrm{d}\rho\mathrm{d}s

(11)

For data of compact support
, say contained in the ball
|r|\leq R
,
and any
(t,r)
with
t>R+|r|
,
r>0
,
it then holds

M_{t,r}[ru](0)\geq\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{|t-r-s|}^{r+t-s}\rho M_{s,\rho}^{2}[\partial_{t}u](0)\mathrm{d}\rho\mathrm{d}s\,.

(12)

Given that the integrand is non-negative it can be suitably restricted further.
Most importantly it is an integral in space
and time
,
over the spherical means of
\partial_{t}u
;
since by the compact support assumption
u
vanishes for
r>R+t
,
the latter integral in time can be carried out,
and by the fundamental theorem of Calculus and Cauchy-Schwarz this leads to

M_{t,r}[u](0)\geq\frac{1}{2}\int_{t-r}^{r}\tfrac{\rho}{r(R+t-r)}\bigl|M_{\rho+(t-r),\rho}[u](0)\bigr|^{2}\mathrm{d}\rho\,,

(13)

for
(t,r)\in\Sigma_{R}=\{(t,r):R<R+r<t<2r\}
.
For a detailed derivation of this inequality we refer the reader to
[
1
,
2
]
.

This now shows that
along a characteristic emanating
from
(t,r)
,
the quantity

\beta(\rho)=\int_{-q}^{\rho}\sigma\bigl|M_{\sigma-q,\sigma}[u]\bigr|^{2}\mathrm{d}\sigma

(14)

satisfies the differential inequality

\beta^{\prime}(\rho)=\rho\bigl|M_{\rho-q,\rho}[u]\bigr|^{2}\geq\frac{1}{4(R-q)^{2}\rho}\beta^{2}(\rho)\,.

(15)

It is then easy to show that this quantity blows up, unless
M_{\rho-q,\rho}[u]=0
for all
\rho\geq r
.

Note that the blow up here is at the level of the solution
u
itself.
This is distinct from the blow-up argument in Section
4
,
which yields blow up at the level of
\partial_{t}u
.

3
Existence of the backwards solution

Consider the scalar semilinear wave equation (
1
) in
\mathbb{R}^{1+3}
:

\displaystyle-\Box u=(\partial_{t}^{2}-\Delta)u=(\partial_{t}u)^{2}.

We have seen in the previous section,
that according to the result of John
[
6
]
,
any nontrivial
C^{3}
solution to (
1
) with compactly supported initial data blows up in finite time. In this section, however, we discuss a recent result in
(
12
, Corollary 8.3)
on the construction of nontrivial future global solutions to (
1
).

Theorem 3
(Yu ’23)

There exist a large family of nontrivial future global solutions to (
1
) for
t\geq 0
. Moreover, for each of these future global solutions, the solution and its time derivative cannot be both compactly supported at
t=0
.

Remark 4

For each constant
C>0
, the function
-\ln(t+C)
is a future global solution to (
1
); cf. Remark
2
. However, the future global solutions constructed in
[
12
]
decay as
|x|\to\infty
and thus are different from these constant-in-
x
solutions. See, e.g., the estimates (
23
) below.

Remark 5

To be more precise, in this paper, we prove that for each nonzero function
A(q,\omega)\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{S}^{2})
with
A\geq 0
, there exists a nontrivial future global solution
u
to (
1
). The solution
u
is associated to the function
A
in the following way. At infinite time, the solution
u
matches a future global solution
U(s,q,\omega)
to the asymptotic equation (
17
). Meanwhile, this
U
is uniquely determined by the data
U_{q}|_{s=0}=A
and the boundary condition
\lim_{q\to-\infty}U(s,q,\omega)=0
.

Remark 6

Note that the second half of Theorem
3
follows directly Theorem
1
. Meanwhile, we can also prove that the data of these nontrivial future global solutions are not compactly supported without applying John’s finite time blowup result. See Section
3.3
.

Remark 7

Theorem
3
can be generalized. First, it was mentioned in Remark
5
that we start with some function
A
and construct a corresponding future global solution to (
1
). The assumptions of
A
can be relaxed. Instead of assuming
A\in C_{c}^{\infty}
, we only need to assume that
A
along with its derivatives decays faster than certain negative powers of
\langle q\rangle
as
|q|\to\infty
.

Besides, the construction used in the proof of Theorem
3
also applies to a large family of quasilinear wave equations in
\mathbb{R}^{1+3}
.
We refer our readers to
[
12
]
for more details.

3.1
Hörmander’s asymptotic equation

Let us discuss how the construction works. Here we make use of an asymptotic equation for (
1
) which was first introduced by Hörmander
[
3
,
4
,
5
]
. In its derivation, we assume that we already have a future global solution
u=u(t,x)
for
t\geq 0
to (
1
) and that
u
has a special form

\displaystyle u(t,x)=\varepsilon r^{-1}U(s,q,\omega)

(16)

where
s=\varepsilon\ln t
,
r=|x|
,
\omega=x/|x|
, and
q=r-t
. Assuming that
t=r\to\infty
, we plug
u=\varepsilon r^{-1}U
into (
1
) and compare the coefficients of terms of order
\varepsilon^{2}t^{-2}
. This gives us the following asymptotic equation for(
1

\displaystyle 2U_{sq}+U_{q}^{2}=0.

(17)

We can solve this equation explicitly:

\displaystyle U_{q}(s,q,\omega)

\displaystyle=\frac{2U_{q}(0,q,\omega)}{U_{q}(0,q,\omega)s+2}

(18)

Remark 8

If
U|_{s=0}\in C_{c}^{\infty}
is nonzero, we have
U_{q}(0,q,\omega)<0
for some
(q,\omega)
, so the denominator in (
18
) equals
0
for some positive
s
. In this case, we have finite time blowup for the asymptotic equation (
17
) when the data are
C_{c}^{\infty}
and nonzero.

We may compare this observation to Remark
2
.
Indeed the asymptotic equation (
18
) is precisely of the form (
8
).
In both cases the blow-up is sensitive to the sign of the initial data.

3.2
Construction of nontrivial future global solution

In our construction, we want to obtain a future global solution to (
17
) for
s\geq 0
. To achieve this goal, we fix a function
A=A(q,\omega)
such that
A\geq 0
everywhere. For simplicity, in this note we also assume that
A\in C_{c}^{\infty}
. By setting
U_{q}(0,q,\omega)=A(q,\omega)
in (

\lim_{q\to-\infty}U(s,q,\omega)=0

U
to (

\displaystyle U(s,q,\omega)

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{q}\frac{2A(\rho,\omega)}{A(\rho,\omega)s+2}\ d\rho,\qquad\forall(s,q,\omega)\in[0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}.

(19)

Since
A\in C_{c}^{\infty}

R>0

for

whenever

Using this

as follows. Fix a small constant
\delta\in(0,1)
and set

\displaystyle u_{app}(t,x)

\displaystyle=\varepsilon r^{-1}\eta(t)\psi(r/t)U(\varepsilon\ln t-\delta,r-t,\omega).

(20)

Here
\eta=\eta(t)
and
\psi=\psi(s)
are two cutoff functions. We choose them so that
u_{app}\equiv\varepsilon r^{-1}U
in the region where
t\gtrsim 1
and
|r-t|<t/4
and that
u_{app}\equiv 0
whenever
t\lesssim 1
or
|r-t|>t/2
. And since
U
vanishes for

, we have
u_{app}\equiv 0
whenever

. In

, it is shown that with this choice of

, we have for

the pointwise estimates

\displaystyle|Z^{I}u_{app}|\lesssim_{I}\varepsilon(1+t)^{-1+C_{I}\varepsilon},\qquad\forall I,\ \forall(t,x)\in[0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{3}

and
u_{app}
is indeed an approximate solution to (
1
) in the following sense:

\displaystyle|Z^{I}(\Box u_{app}+(\partial_{t}u_{app})^{2})|\lesssim_{I}\varepsilon(1+t)^{-3+C_{I}\varepsilon},\qquad\forall I,\ \forall(t,x)\in[0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{3}.

Here
Z^{I}
denotes a product of
|I|
commuting vector fields: translations
\partial_{\alpha}
for
\alpha=0,1,2,3
, scaling
S=t\partial_{t}+r\partial_{r}
, rotations
\Omega_{ij}=x_{i}\partial_{j}-x_{j}\partial_{i}
for
i,j=1,2,3
and Lorentz boosts
\Omega_{0i}=x_{i}\partial_{t}+t\partial_{i}
for

Next, we fix a large time

and consider the following backward Cauchy problem

\displaystyle-\Box v=(v_{t}+2\partial_{t}u_{app})v_{t}-\chi(t/T)(-\Box u_{app}-(\partial_{t}u_{app})^{2})\qquad\text{ in }[0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{3}

with

for

\chi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(-2,2)

\chi|_{[-1,1]}\equiv 1
. It is not hard to see that

solves (

for

, in our proof, we can use the unweighted energy
E(v)(t):=\left\lVert\partial v(t)\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}
and the standard energy estimate for
\Box
.

3.3
Proof that the data are not compactly supported

We finally remark that the future global solution
u
constructed above cannot have compactly supported initial data at
t=0
as long as
A\not\equiv 0
. In fact, the pointwise bound for
\partial(u-u_{app})
above shows that

\displaystyle|u-u_{app}|\lesssim\varepsilon(1+t)^{-3/2+C\varepsilon}(1+|t-|x||)^{1/2},\qquad\text{whenever }|x|<2t.

(24)

Recall that
u_{app}=\varepsilon r^{-1}U
in the region
|r-t|\leq t/4
, so for each
\gamma\in(0,1)
, we have

\displaystyle|u(t,x)-\varepsilon r^{-1}U(\varepsilon\ln t-\delta,r-t,\omega)|\lesssim\varepsilon t^{-(3+\gamma)/2+C\varepsilon},\qquad\forall t\geq 1,\ |t-|x||\leq t^{\gamma}.

The power of
t
is less than
-1
for
\varepsilon\ll_{\gamma}1
. Meanwhile, if
A(q^{0},\omega^{0})>0
for some
(q^{0},\omega^{0})
, we have for all
q>q^{0}
,

\displaystyle U(s,q,\omega^{0})

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{q}\frac{2A(\rho,\omega^{0})}{A(\rho,\omega^{0})s+2}\ d\rho\geq\int_{-\infty}^{q^{0}}\frac{2A(\rho,\omega^{0})}{A(\rho,\omega^{0})s+2}\ d\rho\gtrsim_{q^{0},\omega^{0}}(1+s)^{-1}.

As a result, for all
t\gtrsim 1
,
r-t>q^{0}
, and
|r-t|\leq t^{\gamma}
with
\gamma\in(0,2/3)
, we have

\displaystyle u(t,r\omega^{0})\geq C_{q^{0},\omega^{0}}^{-1}\varepsilon r^{-1}\cdot(1+\varepsilon\ln t)^{-1}-C\varepsilon t^{-(3+\gamma)/2+C\varepsilon}\gtrsim_{q^{0},\omega^{0},\gamma}\frac{\varepsilon}{t(\varepsilon\ln t+1)}.

However, if the data of

is compactly supported, we have
u\equiv 0
whenever
r-t\geq R_{0}
for some constant
R_{0}>0
. Note that the intersection of
\{r-t\geq R_{0}\}
and
\{t>1,r-t>q^{0},|r-t|<t^{\gamma}\}
is not empty for all sufficiently large
t
, so here is a contradiction. We conclude that the solution
u
constructed above cannot have compactly supported initial data.

4
Blowup criteria for solutions with non-compactly supported initial data

In this section we give sufficient conditions for the blow up in finite time of solutions to the semilinear wave equation

\square u=-(\partial_{t}u)^{2}.

(25)

These conditions amount to assumptions on the sign of the solution to the linear wave equation with the same initial data, as well as its time derivative. Unlike previous proofs of blowup for solutions to (
25
), including John’s original proof in
[
6
]
, we do not assume that the data is compactly supported. In fact, Theorem
11
shows that a large class of finite-energy solutions blow up in finite time. We note that the sign conditions that imply blowup, namely (
28
), (
29
) in Lemma
9
, constitute sufficient conditions for solutions to (
25
) to blow up in finite positive time or finite negative time respectively.

First we show that, under suitable decay assumptions on our initial data, solutions to the linear equation satisfy one of three sign conditions. The proof of Lemma
9
(and of Theorem
11
) rely heavily on taking spherical means of solutions, and so we introduce the following notation. Given a function
f\in C(\mathbb{R}^{3})
, we denote the spherical mean of radius
r\geq 0
of
f
about the point
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
as

M_{x_{0}}[f](r)=\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{|\xi|=1}f(x_{0}+r\xi)\mathrm{d}\sigma(\xi).

(26)

Lemma 9

Let
u_{0},u_{1}\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
such that

\lim_{r\rightarrow\infty}\partial_{r}(ru_{0})=\lim_{r\rightarrow\infty}ru_{1}=0,

(27)

where
r=|x|
. Assume one of
u_{0},u_{1}
are nonzero. Let
u_{\text{lin}}
be the unique solution to the linear wave equation

\square u_{\text{lin}}=0

with initial data
u_{\text{lin}}(0,x)=u_{0}(x)
,
\partial_{t}u_{\text{lin}}(0,x)=u_{1}(x)
. Then
u_{\text{lin}}
satisfies at least one of the following three sign conditions:

1.

There exists
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
,
q\geq 0
,
r_{0}\geq q
, such that

M_{x_{0}}[\partial_{t}u_{\text{lin}}(r-q)](r)>0

(28)

for all
r\geq r_{0}
.

2.

There exists
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
,
q\geq 0
,
r_{0}\geq q
such that

M_{x_{0}}[\partial_{t}u_{\text{lin}}(-(r-q))](r)<0,

(29)

for all
r\geq r_{0}
.

3.

For all
t\in\mathbb{R},x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
, we have

u_{\text{lin}}(t,x)\geq 0.

(30)

Proof

We write explicitly

M_{x_{0}}[u_{\text{lin}}(t)](r)=\frac{1}{2r}\Big((r+t)M_{x_{0}}[u_{0}](r+t)+(r-t)M_{x_{0}}[u_{0}](r-t)\Big)\\
+\frac{1}{2r}\int_{r-t}^{r+t}\rho M_{x_{0}}[u_{1}](\rho)\mathrm{d}\rho.

Differentiating in
t
, we have

M_{x_{0}}[\partial_{t}u_{\text{lin}}(t)](r)=\frac{1}{2r}\Big(\partial_{r}(rM_{x_{0}}[u_{0}])(r+t)-\partial_{r}(rM_{x_{0}}[u_{0}])(r-t)\Big)\\
+\frac{1}{2r}\Big((r+t)M_{x_{0}}[u_{1}](r+t)+(r-t)M_{x_{0}}[u_{1}](r-t)\Big).

The right hand side can be written entirely in terms of the solution
u_{\text{lin}}
as

M_{x_{0}}[\partial_{t}u_{\text{lin}}(t)](r)=\frac{1}{2r}\Big(u_{\text{lin}}(r+t,x_{0})-u_{\text{lin}}(-(r-t),x_{0})\Big).

(31)

One may see this from Kirchhoff’s formula for
u_{\text{lin}}
:

\displaystyle u_{\text{lin}}(t,x_{0})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4\pi t^{2}}\int_{\partial B_{t}(x_{0})}\partial_{r}(ru_{0})(t,y)+tu_{1}(t,y)\mathrm{d}\sigma(y)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{|\xi|=1}\partial_{r}(ru_{0})(t,x_{0}+t\xi)+tu_{1}(t,x_{0}+t\xi)\mathrm{d}\sigma(\xi)

\displaystyle=\partial_{r}(rM_{x_{0}}[u_{0}])(t)+tM_{x_{0}}[u_{1}](t),

where the radial coordinate
r
is centered at
x_{0}
. Letting
q=r-t
, we write (
31
) as

M_{x_{0}}[\partial_{t}u_{\text{lin}}(r-q)](r)=\frac{1}{2r}\Big(u_{\text{lin}}(2r-q,x_{0})-u_{\text{lin}}(-q,x_{0})\Big).

We now claim that the first sign condition (
28
) is satisfied if there exists some
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
,
q\geq 0
such that
u_{\text{lin}}(-q,x_{0})<0
. The decay condition (
27
) implies
u_{\text{lin}}(2r-q)\rightarrow 0
as
r\rightarrow\infty
, since

u_{\text{lin}}(2r-q,x_{0})=\partial_{r}(rM_{x_{0}}[u_{0}])(2r-q)+(2r-q)M_{x_{0}}[u_{1}](2r-q).

and so if
\partial_{r}(ru_{0}),ru_{1}\rightarrow 0
as
r\rightarrow\infty
, so do their spherical means, therefore there exists some
r_{0}\geq q
such that

|u_{\text{lin}}(2r-q,x_{0})|<\frac{1}{2}|u_{\text{lin}}(-q,x_{0})|

for all
r\geq r_{0}
. It follows that

M_{x_{0}}[\partial_{t}u_{\text{lin}}(r-q)](r)>-\frac{1}{2}u_{\text{lin}}(-q,x_{0})>0

for all
r\geq r_{0}
, and thus (
28
) is satisfied. Similarly, from (
31
) we have

M_{x_{0}}[\partial_{t}u_{\text{lin}}(-(r-q))](r)=\frac{1}{2r}\Big(u_{\text{lin}}(q,x_{0})-u_{\text{lin}}(-(2r-q),x_{0})\Big),

and so by the same argument the second sign condition (
29
) is satisfied if there exists some
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
,
q\geq 0
such that
u_{\text{lin}}(q,x_{0})<0
. It follows that if neither of (
28
),(
29
) hold, then the third sign condition (
30
) is necessarily satisfied.

Remark 10

We point out that the decay condition (
27
) is satisfied by all continuous differentiable initial data with finite energy. Moreover, for each sign condition, there are finite-energy solutions to the homogeneous wave equation that satisfy that one sign condition and not the other two. For example, consider the solution
u_{\text{lin}}
with initial data

u_{\text{lin}}(0,x)=0,\quad\partial_{t}u_{\text{lin}}(0,x)=\chi(x),

where
\chi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
is some nonnegative bump function. Then for all
t\geq 0,x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
, we have

u_{\text{lin}}(-t,x)=-tM_{x}[\chi](t),

and

\partial_{t}u_{\text{lin}}(t,x)=\partial_{t}u_{\text{lin}}(-t,x)=M_{x}[\chi](t).

Therefore
\partial_{t}u_{\text{lin}}\geq 0
everywhere and
u_{\text{lin}}\leq 0
for all negative
t
, and so
u_{\text{lin}}
satisfies the first sign condition (
28
), and not the other two. Similarly, the solution
v_{\text{lin}}(t,x)=-u_{\text{lin}}(t,x)
satisfies the second sign condition (
29
) only. For the final sign condition, we consider the solution
u_{\text{lin}}
with initial data

u_{\text{lin}}(0,x)=\frac{1}{(1+|x|^{2})^{1/2}},\quad\partial_{t}u_{\text{lin}}(0,x)=0.

Then we have

u_{\text{lin}}(t,x)=\frac{1}{2|x|}\Big(\frac{|x|+t}{(1+|x+t|^{2})^{1/2}}+\frac{|x|-t}{(1+|x-t|^{2})^{1/2}}\Big).

Then
u_{\text{lin}}\geq 0
everywhere, and one can show that for all
t\geq 0
, we have

\partial_{t}u_{\text{lin}}(t,x)\leq 0\leq\partial_{t}u_{\text{lin}}(-t,x).

Hence
u_{\text{lin}}
satisfies only the third sign condition (
30
).

We now show that the first two sign conditions (
28
), (
29
) are sufficient conditions for finite-time blowup.

Theorem 11
(Bernhardt ’24)

Suppose
u
is a nonzero solution of the initial value problem (
6
) with initial data
u(0,x)=u_{0}(x)
,
\partial_{t}u(0,x)=u_{1}(x)
. Suppose
u_{0},u_{1}\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
satisfy the decay assumption (
27
), and let
u_{\text{lin}}
be the solution to the homogeneous wave equation
\square u_{\text{lin}}=0
with matching initial data
u_{0},u_{1}
. If
u_{\text{lin}}
satisfies the sign condition (
28
), then
\partial_{t}u\rightarrow\infty
in finite positive time. Alternatively, if
u_{\text{lin}}
satisfies (
29
), then
\partial_{t}u\rightarrow-\infty
in finite negative time.

Proof

First, we assume that the first sign condition (
28
) holds for some
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{3},q\geq 0,r_{0}\geq q
. The spherical mean
M_{x_{0}}[u]
satisfies Darboux’s equation on
\mathbb{R}^{3}
:

\partial_{r}^{2}M_{x_{0}}[u]+\frac{2}{r}\partial_{r}M_{x_{0}}[u]=\Delta_{x}M_{x_{0}}[u],

and so by (
25
)
M_{x_{0}}[u]
satisfies the
\mathbb{R}^{1+1}
nonlinear wave equation

-\partial_{t}^{2}(rM_{x_{0}}[u])+\partial_{r}^{2}(rM_{x_{0}}[u])=-rM_{x_{0}}[(\partial_{t}u)^{2}].

For all
t,r\geq 0
such that
r\geq t
, the Duhamel formula for
M_{x_{0}}[u]
is

M_{x_{0}}[u(t)](r)=M_{x_{0}}[u_{\text{lin}}(t)](r)+\frac{1}{2r}\int_{T(t,r)}\rho M_{x_{0}}[(\partial_{t}u(s))^{2}](\rho)\mathrm{d}\rho\mathrm{d}s,

(32)

where
u_{\text{lin}}
is the solution to the homogeneous equation
\square u_{\text{lin}}=0
with the same initial data
u_{0},u_{1}
, and
T(t,r)
is the triangle with corners
(0,r-t),(t,r),(0,t+r)
. We split the integral of
M[(\partial_{t}\phi)^{2}]
over
T(t,r)
in (
32
) into parts
\rho\leq r
, and
\rho\geq r
, so that

\int_{T(t,r)}\rho M[(\partial_{t}u(s))^{2}](\rho)\mathrm{d}s\mathrm{d}\rho=\int_{r-t}^{r}\int_{0}^{\rho-(r-t)}\rho M[(\partial_{t}u(s))^{2}](\rho)\mathrm{d}s\mathrm{d}\rho\\
+\int_{r}^{r+t}\int_{0}^{r+t-\rho}\rho M[(\partial_{t}u(s))^{2}](\rho)\mathrm{d}s\mathrm{d}\rho.

(33)

We then differentiate (
32
) with respect to
t
, which by (
33
) gives

M_{x_{0}}[\partial_{t}u(t)](r)=M_{x_{0}}[\partial_{t}u_{\text{lin}}(t)](r)+\frac{1}{2r}\int_{r-t}^{r}\rho M[(\partial_{t}u(\rho-(r-t)))^{2}](\rho)\mathrm{d}\rho\\
+\frac{1}{2r}\int_{r}^{r+t}\rho M[(\partial_{t}u(r+t-\rho))^{2}](\rho)\mathrm{d}\rho.

(34)

Clearly the second integral in (
34
) is nonnegative, moreover we have by Cauchy Schwarz that

(M_{x_{0}}[f](r))^{2}\leq M_{x_{0}}[f^{2}](r),

and so from (
34
) we obtain the lower bound

M_{x_{0}}[\partial_{t}u(r-q)](r)\geq M_{x_{0}}[\partial_{t}u_{\text{lin}}(r-q)](r)+\frac{1}{2r}\int_{q}^{r}\rho\Big(M[\partial_{t}u(\rho-q)](\rho)\Big)^{2}\mathrm{d}\rho,

where we set
t=r-q
. Given that the first sign condition (
28
) is satisfied, we have

M_{x_{0}}[\partial_{t}u(r-q)](r)\geq\frac{1}{2r}\int_{q}^{r}\rho\Big(M[\partial_{t}u(\rho-q)](\rho)\Big)^{2}\mathrm{d}\rho

(35)

for all
r\geq r_{0}
. Define the function

N(r)=\int_{q}^{r}\rho\Big(M[\partial_{t}u(\rho-q)](\rho)\Big)^{2}\mathrm{d}\rho.

By (
35
), the following ODE inequality holds for all
r\geq r_{0}
:

\displaystyle N^{\prime}(r)

\displaystyle=r\Big(M[\partial_{t}u(r-q)](r)\Big)^{2}

\displaystyle\geq r\Big(\frac{1}{2r}\int_{q}^{r}\rho\Big(M[\partial_{t}u(\rho-q)](\rho)\Big)^{2}\mathrm{d}\rho\Big)^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4r}N^{2}(r).

(36)

We then integrate this inequality on the interval
[r_{0},r]
, giving

\frac{1}{N(r_{0})}-\frac{1}{N(r)}\geq\frac{1}{4}\log\Big(\frac{r}{r_{0}}\Big).

(37)

Since
u_{0},u_{1}\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
, the property (
28
) implies that there exists some small
\epsilon>0
such that
M_{x_{0}}[\partial_{t}u_{\text{lin}}(r-q)](r)>0
for all
r\in[r_{0}-\epsilon,r_{0}]
, and so

\displaystyle N(r_{0})

\displaystyle=\int_{q}^{r_{0}}\rho\Big(M[\partial_{t}u(\rho-q)](\rho)\Big)^{2}\mathrm{d}\rho

\displaystyle\geq\int_{r_{0}-\epsilon}^{r_{0}}\rho\Big(M[\partial_{t}u_{\text{lin}}(\rho-q)](\rho)\Big)^{2}\mathrm{d}\rho>0.

It then follows from (
36
) that
N(r)\geq N(r_{0})>0
, and so we rearrange (
37
) to obtain

N(r)\geq\frac{N(r_{0})}{N(r_{0})-\frac{1}{4}\log(r/r_{0})},

(38)

Therefore
N(r)\rightarrow+\infty
for some
r\leq r_{*}
, where
r_{*}
is the value for which the right hand side blows up to infinity, given by

r_{*}=r_{0}\exp\Big(\frac{4}{N(r_{0})}\Big).

(39)

By (
34
), this implies that the spherical mean
M_{x_{0}}[\partial_{t}u(r-q)](r)
blows up, and hence
\partial_{t}u\rightarrow\infty
at some point
(t,x)\in(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{3}
.

If alternatively (
29
) is satisfied, then observe that the function
v(t)=u(-t)
also satisfies the semilinear wave equation (
25
) with initial data

v(0,x)=u_{0}(x),\quad\partial_{t}v(0,x)=-u_{1}(x).

Let
v_{\text{lin}}(t)
denote the solution to the homogeneous wave equation with the same initial data as
v
. By uniqueness, we have for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
that
v_{\text{lin}}(t)=u_{\text{lin}}(-t)
. As
\partial_{t}v_{\text{lin}}(t)=-\partial_{t}u_{\text{lin}}(-t)
, it is clear that if
u_{\text{lin}}
satisfies the second sign condition, then
v_{\text{lin}}
satisfies the first sign condition. From the previous argument,
\partial_{t}v\rightarrow\infty
in finite positive time , and so since
\partial_{t}u(t)=-\partial_{t}v(-t)
, it follows that
\partial_{t}u\rightarrow-\infty
in finite negative time.

Finally we show that the solutions constructed in
[
12
]
do not satisfy the third sign condition (
30
), and therefore blow up in finite negative time.

Corollary 12

Let
u
be a
C^{2}
solution to (
25
) constructed in Theorem
3
, and let
u_{\text{lin}}
be the solution to the homogeneous wave equation
\square u_{\text{lin}}=0
with the same initial data as
u
. Then
u_{\text{lin}}
does not satisfy the third sign condition (
30
). That is, there exists some
t\in\mathbb{R},x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
such that
u_{\text{lin}}(t,x)<0
. Moreoever,
\partial_{t}u
blows up in finite negative time.

Proof

We begin by stating that if
u_{\text{lin}}
does not satisfy (
30
), then by Lemma
9
,
u_{\text{lin}}
satisfies either of the first two sign conditions (
28
),(
29
). It follows by Theorem
11
that
u
blows up in finite negative time, since
u
is a future global solution. To show
u_{\text{lin}}
does not satisfy the third sign condition, we proceed by contradiction. Suppose
u_{\text{lin}}(t,x)\geq 0
for all
(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}^{1+3}
. One immediate consequence is that
u_{0}\geq 0
. For
t>0
, by Kirchhoff’s formula, we write

u_{\text{lin}}(t,x)=\partial_{r}(rM_{x}[u_{0}])(t)+tM_{x}[u_{1}](t)>0.

(40)

Rearranging this we can bound

\partial_{r}(rM_{x}[u_{0}])(t)\geq-tM_{x}[u_{1}](t),

Similarly,

u_{\text{lin}}(-t,x)=\partial_{r}(rM_{x}[u_{0}])(t)-tM_{x}[u_{1}](t),

and therefore we have

\partial_{r}(rM_{x}[u_{0}])(t)\geq|tM_{x}[u_{1}](t)|.

(41)

The d’Alembert formula for the spherical means of
u_{\text{lin}}
is

M_{x}[u_{\text{lin}}(t)](r)=\frac{1}{2r}\Big((r+t)M_{x}[u_{0}](r+t)+(r-t)M_{x}[u_{0}](r-t)\Big)\\
+\frac{1}{2r}\int_{r-t}^{r+t}\rho M_{x}[u_{1}](\rho)\mathrm{d}\rho.

We bound the final integral using (
41
), so that

\displaystyle\int_{r-t}^{r+t}\rho M_{x}[u_{1}](\rho)\mathrm{d}\rho

\displaystyle\geq-\int_{r-t}^{r+t}|\rho M_{x}[u_{1}](\rho)|\mathrm{d}\rho

\displaystyle\geq-\int_{r-t}^{r+t}\partial_{\rho}(\rho M_{x}[u_{0}])(\rho)\mathrm{d}\rho

\displaystyle=(r-t)M_{x_{0}}[u_{0}](r-t)-(r+t)M_{x}[u_{0}](r+t).

Hence, by (
41
) we have

M_{x}[u_{\text{lin}}(t)](r)\geq\frac{(r-t)M_{x}[u_{0}](r-t)}{r}.

Note that
u_{0}
must be nonzero somewhere, otherwise by the representation formula, (
40
),
u_{\text{lin}}
must have a point
(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}^{1+3}
where
u_{\text{lin}}(t,x)<0<u_{\text{lin}}(-t,x)
. Therefore, we may fix
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
,
q=r-t\geq 0
such that
M_{x_{0}}[u_{0}](q)>0
, and so for all
r\geq q

M_{x_{0}}[u_{\text{lin}}(r-q)](r)\geq\frac{C}{r}

for some constant
C>0
. By the Duhamel formula for
u
(
32
), we have
u\geq u_{\text{lin}}
for all positive time, and so

M_{x_{0}}[u(r-q)](r)\geq\frac{C}{r}

(42)

On the other hand, for the solutions
u
constructed in Theorem
3
, for all
q\geq 0
,
\omega\in\mathbb{S}^{2}
,
r
sufficiently large we may bound
u
like

\displaystyle|u(r-q,r\omega)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\epsilon r^{-1}U(\epsilon\ln(r-q)-\delta,q,\omega)+(1+r-q)^{-3/2+C\epsilon}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{r\ln(r-q)}.

(43)

These upper and lower bounds cannot both be true, hence
u_{\text{lin}}(t,x)<0
for some
(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}^{1+3}
.

Remark 13

The solutions to (
25
) that are constructed in Theorem
3
are a subset of the more general solutions constructed in
[
12
]
. In Theorem
3
it is assumed that for solutions
U
to the asymptotic system (
17
), the initial data
U_{q}(0,q,\omega)=A(q,\omega)
is compactly supported in
q
. More generally one may assume decay like
A=O(\langle q\rangle^{-2-})
when
q<0
and
A=O(\langle q\rangle^{-1-})
when
q>0
, as was shown in
[
12
]
. In this case, we have (assuming
A=O(\langle q\rangle^{-\gamma})
with
\gamma>1
) that

|U(q,\omega)|\leq\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{2A(p,\omega)}{A(p,\omega)s+2}\ \mathrm{d}p\\
\leq\int_{|p|\leq s^{1/\gamma}}\min\{\frac{1}{s},1\}\ \mathrm{d}p+\int_{|p|\geq s^{1/\gamma}}\langle p\rangle^{-\gamma}\ \mathrm{d}p\lesssim\frac{1}{(1+s)^{1-1/\gamma}}.

(44)

Then in lieu of (
43
) we have, for large
r
,

|u(r-q,r\omega)|\lesssim\frac{1}{r(\ln(r-q))^{1-1/\gamma}}.

As
\gamma>1
,
u
still decays faster than
O(r^{-1})
, which is incompatible with the lower bound (
42
). Hence the solutions constructed in
[
12
]
also blow up in finite negative time.

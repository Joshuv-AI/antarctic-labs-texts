On application of slowly varying functions with remainder in the theory of Markov Branching Processes with mean one and infinite variance Thanks: © 2019 Imomov A.A and Meyliyev A.Kh.
Azam Imomov and Abror Meyliyev

Abstract
. We investigate an application of slowly varying functions (in sense of Karamata) in the theory of Markov branching process. We treat the critical case so that the infinitesimal generating function of the process has the infinite second moment, but it regularly varies with remainder. We improve the Basic Lemma of the theory of critical Markov branching process and refine well-known limit results.

Introduction and main results

1.1.
Preliminaries

We consider the Markov Branching Process (MBP) to be the homogeneous continuous-time Markov process

\left\{Z(t),t\geq{0}\right\}
with the state space
{\mathcal{S}}_{0}=\{0\}\cup{\mathcal{S}}
,
where
{\mathcal{S}}\subset\mathbb{N}
and
\mathbb{N}=\{1,2,\ldots\}
. The transition probabilities
of the process

P_{ij}(t):=\mathbb{P}\left\{{{Z(t)=j}\bigl|{Z(0)=i}\bigr.}\right\}

satisfy the following branching property:

P_{ij}(t)=P_{1j}^{i*}(t)\quad\parbox{79.6678pt}{{for all} {} $i,j\in{\mathcal{S}}$,}

(1.1)

where the asterisk denotes convolution. Herein transition
probabilities
P_{1j}(t)
are expressed by relation

P_{1j}(\varepsilon)=\delta_{1j}+a_{j}\varepsilon+o(\varepsilon)\quad\parbox{56.9055pt}{{as} {} $\varepsilon\downarrow 0$,}

(1.2)

where
\delta_{ij}
is Kronecker’s delta function and
\left\{{a_{j}}\right\}
are intensities of
individuals’ transformation such that
a_{j}\geq 0
for
j\in{\mathcal{S}}_{0}\backslash\{1\}
and

0<a_{0}<-a_{1}=\sum\limits_{j\in{\mathcal{S}}_{0}\backslash\{1\}}{a_{j}}<\infty.

The MBP was defined first by Kolmogorov and Dmitriev
[
8
]
; for more
detailed information see
[
2
, Ch. III]
and
[
5
, Ch. V]
.

Defining the generating function (GF)
F(t;s)=\sum\nolimits_{j\in{\mathcal{S}}_{0}}{P_{1j}(t)s^{j}}

it follows from (
1.1
) and (
1.2
) that the process
\left\{Z(t)\right\}
is determined
by the infinitesimal GF
f(s)=\sum\nolimits_{j\in{\mathcal{S}}_{0}}{a_{j}s^{j}}
for
s\in[0,1)
.
Moreover it follows from (
1.2
) that GF
F(t;s)
is unique solution of the backward
Kolmogorov equation
{\partial{F}\mathord{\left/{\vphantom{{\partial{F}}{\partial{t}}}}\right.\kern-1.2pt}{\partial{t}}}=f\left({F}\right)
with the boundary
condition
F(0;s)=s
; see
[
2
, p. 106]
. If
m:=\sum\nolimits_{j\in{\mathcal{S}}}{ja_{j}}=f^{\prime}(1-)
is finite then
F(t;1)=1
and due to Kolmogorov equation it can be calculated
that
\mathbb{E}\bigl[{{Z(t)}\left|{Z(0)=i}\right.}\bigr]=\sum\nolimits_{j\in{\mathcal{S}}}{jP_{ij}(t)}=ie^{mt}
. Last formula shows that long-term properties of MBP are various
depending on value of parameter
m
. Hence the MBP is classified as
critical

if
m=0
and
sub-critical
or
supercritical
if
m<0
or
m>0

respectively. Monographs
[
1
]
–
[
3
]
and
[
5
]

are general references for mentioned and other classical facts on theory of MBP.

In the paper we consider the critical case. Let
R(t;s)=1-F(t;s)
and

q(t):=R(t;0)=\mathbb{P}\left\{{\mathcal{H}>t}\right\},

where the variable
\mathcal{H}=\inf\left\{t:Z(t)=0\right\}
denotes an extinction
time of MBP. Then
q(t)
is the survival probability of the process. Sevastyanov
[
11
]

proved that if
f{{}^{\prime\prime\prime}}(1-)<\infty
then the following asymptotic representation holds:

{{1}\over{R(t;s)}}-{{1}\over{1-s}}={{f{{}^{\prime\prime}}(1-)}\over{2}}{t}+{\mathcal{O}\bigl(\ln{t}\bigr)}\quad\parbox{62.59596pt}{{as} {} $t\rightarrow\infty$}

(1.3)

for all

; see
[
11

.

Later on Zolotarev
[
12

has found a principally new result on asymptotic

without the assumption of

. Namely
providing that

is a regularly varying function at zero that is

\lim_{x\downarrow{0}}{{xg^{\prime}(x)}\over{g(x)}}=\gamma

{{q(t)}\over{f\left({1-q(t)}\right)}}\sim{\alpha}t\quad\parbox{56.9055pt}{{as} {} $t\rightarrow{\infty}$.}

(1.4)

Further we assume that the infinitesimal GF

Pakes

and

is GF of invariant

1.2.
Aim and Basic assumptions

The representation (

Let

Note that the function

{{y\Lambda^{\prime}(y)}\over{\Lambda(y)}}=\nu+\delta(y),

[formula]

Throughout the paper
\left[{f_{\nu}}\right]
and

\left[{\Lambda_{\delta}}\right]

Basic assumptions

Since
{{{\mathcal{L}\left({\lambda x}\right)}}\mathord{\left/{\vphantom{{{\mathcal{L}\left({\lambda x}\right)}}{{\mathcal{L}\left(x\right)}}}}\right.\kern-1.2pt}{{\mathcal{L}\left(x\right)}}}\to{1}

(1.9)

where
\varrho(x)\to 0
as
x\to\infty
. If there is some positive function

and

as

,
then

is said to be
SV-function with remainder
at infinity;
see
[
3
, p. 185, condition SR1]
. As we can see below, if the function

\delta(y)
is known it will be possible to estimate a decreasing rate of the remainder
\varrho(x)
.

Using that
\Lambda(1)=\mathcal{L}(1)=a_{0}
integration
\left[{\Lambda_{\delta}}\right]
yields

\Lambda(y)=a_{0}y^{\nu}\exp{\int\limits_{1}^{y}{{{\delta(u)}\over{u}}\textrm{d}u}}.

Therefore we have

Changing variable as

in the integrand gives

(1.10)

where
\varepsilon(t)=-\delta(1/t)
and
\varepsilon(t)\to 0
as

.
It follows from (

{{\mathcal{L}(\lambda x)}\over{\mathcal{L}(x)}}=\exp\int\limits_{x}^{\lambda x}{{{\varepsilon(t)}\over t}\textrm{d}t}=1+\varrho(x)\quad\parbox{62.59596pt}{{as} {} $x\to\infty$}

, where
\varrho(x)\to 0
as

\int\limits_{x}^{\lambda x}{{{\varepsilon(t)}\over t}\textrm{d}t}=\ln\left[{1+\varrho(x)}\right]=\varrho(x)+{\mathcal{O}}\left({\varrho^{2}(x)}\right)\quad\parbox{62.59596pt}{{as} {} $x\to\infty$.}

Applying the mean value theorem to the left-hand side of the
last equality we can assert that

\varepsilon(x)={\mathcal{O}}\left({\varrho(x)}\right)\quad\parbox{62.59596pt}{{as} {} $x\to\infty$.}

(1.11)

Thus the assumption
\left[{\Lambda_{\delta}}\right]
provides that
\mathcal{L}(s)

to be an SV-function at infinity with the remainder in the form of

\varrho(x)={\mathcal{O}}\left({\delta\bigl(1/x\bigr)}\right)
as

.

Improved analysis of double J / ψ J/\psi production in Z Z -boson decay
Guang-Yu Wang a, Xing-Gang Wu a, Xu-Chang Zheng a, Jiang Yan a, Jia-Wei Zhang b

Abstract
In this paper, we present an improved calculation for the decay rate of the rare Z Z -boson decay into J / ψ + J / ψ J/\psi+J/\psi . This decay is dominated by the photon fragmentation mechanism, i.e., the transition Z → J / ψ + γ ∗ Z\to J/\psi+\gamma^{*} followed by the fragmentation γ ∗ → J / ψ \gamma^{*}\to J/\psi . In our calculation, the amplitude of γ ∗ → J / ψ \gamma^{*}\to J/\psi is extracted from the measured value of Γ ⁡ ( J / ψ → e + ​ e − ) \Gamma(J/\psi\to e^{+}e^{-}) , and the amplitude of Z → J / ψ + γ ∗ Z\to J/\psi+\gamma^{*} is calculate through the light-cone approach. The higher-order QCD and relativistic corrections in the amplitude of γ ∗ → J / ψ \gamma^{*}\to J/\psi and the large logarithms of m Z 2 / m c 2 m_{{}_{Z}}^{2}/m_{c}^{2} that appear in the amplitude of Z → J / ψ + γ ∗ Z\to J/\psi+\gamma^{*} are resummed in our calculation. Besides, the non-fragmentation amplitude is calculated based on the NRQCD factorization, and the next-to-leading order QCD and relativistic corrections are included. The obtained branching fraction for this Z Z decay channel is 8.66 − 0.69 + 1.48 × 10 − 11 8.66^{+1.48}_{-0.69}\times 10^{-11} .



I
Introduction

The rare
Z
-boson decay into double
J/\psi
provides a good platform for studying the heavy quarkonium production and for searching of new physics beyond the standard model (SM). In 2019, the CMS collaboration presented the first search for the decay
Z\to J/\psi+J/\psi
based on the data from
pp
collisions with an integrated luminosity of
37.5\,{\rm fb}^{-1}
at
\sqrt{s}=13\,{\rm TeV}
at the large hadron collider (LHC). The observed upper limit on the branching fraction for the decay is
{\rm Br}(Z\to J/\psi+J/\psi)<2.2\times 10^{-6}

CMS:2019wch
. In 2022, the CMS collaboration updated the upper limit on this branching fraction by using a data set of
pp
collisions corresponding to an integrated luminosity of
138\,{\rm fb}^{-1}
. Their updated upper limit is
{\rm Br}(Z\to J/\psi+J/\psi)<1.4\times 10^{-6}

CMS:2022fsq
. Although this decay process has not been observed up to now, the prospect of observing this decay at future colliders is optimistic
dEnterria:2023wjq
. As is well-known, there are several lepton colliders, e.g., ILC
ILC:2013jhg
, CEPC
CEPCStudyGroup:2018ghi
, FCC-ee
FCC:2018evy
, and super Z factory
zfactory
, are under consideration. These lepton colliders are planed to operate at the
Z
pole for a period of time, and a large number of
Z
bosons are expected to be produced, e.g., at the FCC-ee, about
5\times 10^{12}

Z
bosons will be produced
FCC:2018evy
. Therefore, these lepton colliders provide new opportunities for studying the rare decay
Z\to J/\psi+J/\psi
.

On the theoretical side, the decay
Z\to J/\psi+J/\psi
was first studied in Ref.
Bergstrom:1990bu
in 1990. In Ref.
Likhoded:2017jmx
, this decay was reanalysed at leading order (LO) in
\alpha_{s}
and
v
(
v
stands for the typical velocity of the
c
quark or the
\bar{c}
quark in the rest frame of
J/\psi
) based on the nonrelativistic QCD (NRQCD) factorization. In these references, only the contribution of the LO QCD Feynman diagrams were considered. In Ref.
Gao:2022mwa
, Gao and Gong pointed out that besides the LO QCD diagrams, the other transition via
Z\to J/\psi+\gamma^{*}
followed by the fragmentation
\gamma^{*}\to J/\psi
can also bring significant contribution. Their calculation shows that the branching fraction of
Z\to J/\psi+J/\psi
can be enhanced to
1.1\times 10^{-10}
from
1.5\times 10^{-13}
after including the single photon fragmentation contribution. Recently, the next-to-leading order (NLO) QCD correction to the decay rate of
Z\to J/\psi+J/\psi
has been calculated in Refs.
Li:2023tzx
;
Luo:2022ugd
. The results there show that the NLO QCD correction can diminish the decay rate significantly
Li:2023tzx
.

In this paper, we devote ourselves to presenting an improved calculation for the decay width of
Z\to J/\psi+J/\psi
. As the photon fragmentation contribution dominates this decay process, we first focus on the amplitude for the photon fragmentation diagrams. We express the amplitude of the photon fragmentation diagrams into two parts: one for
Z\to J/\psi+\gamma^{*}
, and the other for
\gamma^{*}\to J/\psi
. For the amplitude of
\gamma^{*}\to J/\psi
, we directly extract it from the experimentally measured leptonic decay width of the
J/\psi
, rather than calculating it order by order in
\alpha_{s}
and
v
. Using this approach, the QCD and relativistic corrections to the amplitude of
\gamma^{*}\to J/\psi
are resummed up to all orders. For the amplitude of
Z\to J/\psi+\gamma^{*}
, we adopt the light-cone approach suggested by Refs.
Lepage:1980fj
;
Chernyak:1983ej
, in which the amplitude is expressed as the convolution of the hard-scattering kernel with the
J/\psi
light-cone distribution amplitude (LCDA). The hard-scattering kernel can be calculated with the perturbative QCD (pQCD), and the LCDA for
J/\psi
can be calculated through the NRQCD factorization
Bodwin:1994jh
. Under the light-cone approach, logarithms of
m_{{}_{Z}}^{2}/m_{c}^{2}
appearing in the amplitude can be resummed through solving the evolution equation of the LCDA. In addition to the contribution of the photon fragmentation diagrams, we also calculate the contribution from the non-fragmentation diagrams. We shall adopt the fixed-order approach under the NRQCD factorization, which is adopted in previous calculations on the decay rate of
Z\to J/\psi+J/\psi
, to calculate the non-fragmentation amplitude.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. In Sec.
II
, we give the calculation formalism for the photon fragmentation amplitude, the non-fragmentation amplitude, and the decay width of
Z\to J/\psi+J/\psi
. In Sec.
III
, we present the numerical results and discussions. Section
IV
is reserved as a summary.

\displaystyle N_{n}=\frac{4(2n+3)}{(n+1)(n+2)}.

\displaystyle T_{H}(x,\mu)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}N_{n}b_{n}(\mu)C_{n}^{(3/2)}(2x-1).

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}dx\,T_{H}(x,\mu)\phi_{J/\psi}^{\parallel}(x,\mu)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}b_{n}(\mu)a_{n}(\mu)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{n}b_{n}(\mu)U_{nk}(\mu,\mu_{0})a_{k}(\mu_{0}),

Then, we keep 20 nonzero terms in the second line of Eq.(

In this subsection, we present the key formulas for calculating the non-fragmentation amplitude. Compared to the fragmentation amplitude, the non-fragmentation amplitude is significantly suppressed

;

Under the NRQCD factorization
Bodwin:1994jh
, the non-fragmentation amplitude up to order-

for the decay

can be written as

\displaystyle{\cal M}^{\rm nfr}_{Z\to J/\psi+J/\psi}

\displaystyle=2m_{J/\psi}\left(c_{0}+c_{2}\langle v^{2}\rangle_{J/\psi}\right)\langle J/\psi|\psi^{\dagger}{\bm{\sigma}}\cdot{\bm{\epsilon}}\chi|0\rangle^{2},

(30)

where
c_{0}
and
c_{2}
are short-distance coefficients (SDCs) that can be expanded in powers of
\alpha_{s}
. In this work, we calculate the SDC
c_{0}
up to NLO in
\alpha_{s}
, and calculate the SDC
c_{2}
at LO in
\alpha_{s}
.
\langle J/\psi|\psi^{\dagger}{\bm{\sigma}}\cdot{\bm{\epsilon}}\chi|0\rangle
is the LDME for
J/\psi
.
\langle v^{2}\rangle_{J/\psi}
is a ratio of LDMEs, which has been defined in Eq.(
12
). The factor
2m_{J/\psi}
arises from that we use the relativistic normalization for the
J/\psi
in
{\cal M}^{\rm nfr}_{Z\to J/\psi+J/\psi}
on the left-hand side of Eq.(
30
), while we use conventional nonrelativistic normalization for the NRQCD matrix element on the right-hand side of Eq.(
30
).

The SDCs can be determined through the matching method. According to this method, we first replace the
J/\psi
state in Eq.(
30
) by an on-shell
(c\bar{c})
pair with a small relative velocity

, i.e.,

\displaystyle{\cal M}^{\rm nfr}_{Z\to(c\bar{c})[^{3}S_{1}^{[1]}]+(c\bar{c})[^{3}S_{1}^{[1]}]}

\displaystyle=\left(c_{0}+c_{2}\,v^{2}\right)\langle(c\bar{c})[^{3}S_{1}^{[1]}]|\psi^{\dagger}{\bm{\sigma}}\cdot{\bm{\epsilon}}\chi|0\rangle^{2},

(31)

where the amplitude
{\cal M}^{\rm nfr}_{Z\to(c\bar{c})[^{3}S_{1}^{[1]}]+(c\bar{c})[^{3}S_{1}^{[1]}]}
can be calculated using perturbative theory in QCD, and the LDME
\langle(c\bar{c})[^{3}S_{1}^{[1]}]|\psi^{\dagger}{\bm{\sigma}}\cdot{\bm{\epsilon}}\chi|0\rangle
for an on-shell
(c\bar{c})

production.

To calculate the amplitude
{\cal M}^{\rm nfr}_{Z\to(c\bar{c})[^{3}S_{1}^{[1]}]+(c\bar{c})[^{3}S_{1}^{[1]}]}
up to order
\alpha_{s}
and order
v^{2}
, we assign the momenta of the
c
and
\bar{c}
quarks as
p_{i}=\frac{1}{2}P_{i}+q_{i}
and
\bar{p}_{i}=\frac{1}{2}P_{i}-q_{i}
, where
i=1,2
. Then we have the relations

\displaystyle p_{i}^{2}=\bar{p}_{i}^{2}=m_{c}^{2},\,P_{i}^{2}=4E_{q}^{2},\,P_{i}\cdot q_{i}=0,

\displaystyle q_{i}^{2}=m_{c}^{2}-E_{q}^{2}=-m_{c}^{2}v^{2}.

(32)

In the rest frame of a
(c\bar{c})
pair, the total and relative momenta of the
(c\bar{c})
pair are
P_{i}=(2E_{q},\bm{0})
and
q_{i}=(0,\bm{q}_{i})
where
E_{q}=\sqrt{\bm{q}_{i}^{2}+m_{c}^{2}}
. In deriving these relations, we have implicitly adopted the relation
\bm{q}_{1}^{2}=\bm{q}_{2}^{2}=m_{c}^{2}v^{2}
.

It is convenient to adopt the covariant projection method to enforce the produced
(c\bar{c})
pairs to be the corresponding spin and color state. The spin projector for the spin-triplet state is
Bodwin:2010fi

\displaystyle\Pi=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4\sqrt{2}E_{q}(E_{q}+m_{c})}(\not{\bar{p}}_{i}-m_{c})\not{\epsilon}^{*}(\not{P}_{i}+2E_{q})

(33)

\displaystyle\cdot(\not{p}_{i}+m_{c}),

where
\epsilon^{*}
is the polarization vector of the spin-triplet state. The color projector for color-singlet state is

\displaystyle\Lambda_{1}=\frac{\bf 1}{\sqrt{3}},

(34)

where
{\bf 1}
stands for the unit matrix of the
SU(3)_{c}
group.

To obtain the amplitude in terms of powers of
v
, we first expand the amplitude in powers of
q_{i}
to the required order,

\displaystyle{\cal M}^{\rm nfr}_{Z\to(c\bar{c})[^{3}S_{1}^{[1]}]+(c\bar{c})[^{3}S_{1}^{[1]}]}

\displaystyle={\cal M}\Big|_{q_{1}=q_{2}=0}+\sum_{i=1}^{2}q_{i}^{\alpha}\frac{\partial{\cal M}}{\partial q_{i}^{\alpha}}\Big|_{q_{1}=q_{2}=0}

\displaystyle~~~+\frac{1}{2!}\sum_{i,j=1}^{2}q_{i}^{\alpha}q_{j}^{\beta}\frac{\partial^{2}{\cal M}}{\partial q_{i}^{\alpha}\partial q_{j}^{\beta}}\Big|_{q_{1}=q_{2}=0}+\cdots,

(35)

where
{\cal M}^{\rm nfr}_{Z\to(c\bar{c})[^{3}S_{1}^{[1]}]+(c\bar{c})[^{3}S_{1}^{[1]}]}
is the non-fragmentation amplitude, which has been abbreviated as
{\cal M}
on right hand side of Eq.(
35
). To project out the
S
-wave contribution, we make the replacement

\displaystyle q_{i}^{\mu}q_{i}^{\nu}\to\frac{{\bm{q}}_{i}^{2}}{(D-1)}\Pi^{\mu\nu}.

(36)

where

\displaystyle\Pi^{\mu\nu}=-g^{\mu\nu}+\frac{P_{i}^{\mu}P_{i}^{\nu}}{P_{i}^{2}},

(37)

The odd-power terms of
q_{i}
vanish for the S-wave contribution. Then we obtain the amplitude up to order
v^{2}
,

\displaystyle{\cal M}^{\rm nfr}_{Z\to(c\bar{c})[^{3}S_{1}^{[1]}]+(c\bar{c})[^{3}S_{1}^{[1]}]}

\displaystyle={\cal M}\Big|_{q_{1}=q_{2}=0}+\frac{1}{2!}\sum_{i=1}^{2}\frac{{\bm{q}}_{i}^{2}\Pi_{\alpha\beta}}{(D-1)}\frac{\partial^{2}{\cal M}}{\partial q_{i}^{\alpha}\partial q_{i}^{\beta}}\Big|_{q_{1}=q_{2}=0}.

(38)

In the calculation, we expand the amplitude in powers of the relative momenta
q_{i}
before carrying out the loop integration. This amounts to calculating the contributions arising from the hard region in the language of the strategy of region
Beneke:1997zp
. Consequently, the Coulomb divergences arising from the potential region do not appear in our calculation.

There are ultraviolet (UV) and infrared (IR) divergences in the loop-diagram calculation. We utilize dimensional regularization to regularize both the UV and IR divergences, with spacetime dimension
D=4-2\epsilon
. The UV divergences are removed through renormalization. We define the renormalized charm-quark field, charm-quark mass, and gluon field in the on-shell (OS) scheme, while define the renormalized strong coupling constant in the modified minimal subtraction (
\overline{\rm MS}
) scheme. The corresponding renormalization constants are
Z_{i}=1+\delta Z_{i}
, and

\displaystyle\delta Z_{2}^{\rm OS}=

\displaystyle-C_{F}\dfrac{\alpha_{s}}{4\pi}\left[\dfrac{1}{\epsilon_{UV}}+\frac{2}{\epsilon_{IR}}-3\gamma_{E}+3\ln\frac{4\pi\mu_{r}^{2}}{m_{c}^{2}}+4\right],

\displaystyle\delta Z_{m}^{\rm OS}=

\displaystyle-3C_{F}\dfrac{\alpha_{s}}{4\pi}\left[\dfrac{1}{\epsilon_{UV}}-\gamma_{E}+\ln\dfrac{4\pi\mu_{r}^{2}}{m_{c}^{2}}+\frac{4}{3}\right],

\displaystyle\delta Z_{3}^{\rm OS}=

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha_{s}}{4\pi}\bigg[(\beta^{\prime}_{0}-2C_{A})\left(\dfrac{1}{\epsilon_{UV}}-\frac{1}{\epsilon_{IR}}\right)

\displaystyle-\frac{4}{3}T_{F}\left(\dfrac{1}{\epsilon_{UV}}-\gamma_{E}+\ln\dfrac{4\pi\mu_{r}^{2}}{m_{c}^{2}}\right)\bigg],

\displaystyle\delta Z_{g}^{\overline{\mathrm{MS}}}=

\displaystyle-\dfrac{\beta_{0}}{2}\dfrac{\alpha_{s}}{4\pi}\left[\dfrac{1}{\epsilon_{UV}}-\gamma_{E}+\ln(4\pi)\right],

(39)

where
\gamma_{E}
is the Euler constant,
\mu_{r}
is the renormalization scale,
\beta_{0}=11/3-2n_{f}/3
,
\beta^{\prime}_{0}=11/3-2n_{lf}/3
,
n_{f}
is the number of active quark flavors and
n_{lf}=3
is the number of light quark flavors.

In dimensional regularization, the
\gamma_{5}
matrix should be noted. In this work, we employ the Larin scheme
Larin:1993tq
, where the product of a
\gamma_{\mu}
matrix and a
\gamma_{5}
matrix is expreesed as:

\displaystyle\gamma_{\mu}\gamma_{5}=\frac{i}{6}\epsilon_{\mu\rho\sigma\tau}\gamma^{\rho}\gamma^{\sigma}\gamma^{\tau}.

(40)

In this
\gamma_{5}
scheme, a finite renormalization is required to restore the axial current Ward identity for the axial vector vertex. The corresponding finite renormalization constant
Z_{5}
up to order
\alpha_{s}
for the axial vector vertex is given by:

\displaystyle Z_{5}=1-\frac{\alpha_{s}}{\pi}C_{F}.

(41)

In order to obtain the SDCs using Eq.(
31
), in addition to the amplitude
{\cal M}^{\rm nfr}_{Z\to(c\bar{c})[^{3}S_{1}^{[1]}]+(c\bar{c})[^{3}S_{1}^{[1]}]}
, we also need to calculate the matrix element
\langle(c\bar{c})[^{3}S_{1}^{[1]}]|\psi^{\dagger}{\bm{\sigma}}\cdot{\bm{\epsilon}}\chi|0\rangle
. The matrix element up to order
\alpha_{s}
is

\displaystyle\sum_{\lambda}|\langle(c\bar{c})[^{3}S_{1}]|\psi^{\dagger}{\bm{\sigma}}\cdot{\bm{\epsilon}}(\lambda)\chi|0\rangle|^{2}=2N_{c}(D-1)(2E_{q})^{2},

(42)

where
\lambda
denotes the helicity of the
(c\bar{c})
pair.

In the calculation of the non-framentation amplitude, the packages FeynArts
feynarts
is adopted to generate Feynman diagrams, the package Feyncalc
feyncalc1
;
feyncalc2
is adopted to carry out the traces over the Dirac and color matrices, the package $Apart
apart
is adopted to do partial fraction, and the package FIRE
fire
is adopted to do integration-by-parts (IBP) reduction for the loop integrals. After the IBP reduction, the one-loop integral are reduced to mater integrals, and these master integrals are calculated numerically using the package LoopTools
looptools
.

II.3
Decay width

The total amplitude for the decay
Z\to J/\psi+J/\psi
is the sum of the fragmentation and non-fragmentation amplitudes, i.e.,

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{Z\to J/\psi+J/\psi}=

\displaystyle\,\mathcal{M}^{\rm fr}_{Z\to J/\psi+J/\psi}+\mathcal{M}^{\rm nfr}_{Z\to J/\psi+J/\psi}.

(43)

Then the decay width can be calculated through

\displaystyle\Gamma_{Z\to J/\psi+J/\psi}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2!}\frac{1}{3}\frac{\sqrt{m_{{}_{Z}}^{2}/4-m_{J/\psi}^{2}}}{8\pi m_{{}_{Z}}^{2}}\sum\left|\mathcal{M}_{Z\to J/\psi+J/\psi}\right|^{2},

(44)

where the factor
1/2!
comes from that there are two identical particles in the final state,
1/3
comes from the average over the polarizations of the
Z
boson,
\sum
denotes that the polarizations of the initial and final states are summed over.

IV
summary

In this paper, we have presented a calculation for the decay rate of
Z\to J/\psi+J/\psi
. The decay process
Z\to J/\psi+J/\psi
is dominated by the single photon fragmentation mechanism, i.e., the transition
Z\to J/\psi+\gamma^{*}
followed by the fragmentation
\gamma^{*}\to J/\psi
. The amplitude of
\gamma^{*}\to J/\psi
has already been calculated up to
{\cal O}(\alpha_{s}^{3})
under the NRQCD factorization in Refs.
Marquard:2014pea
;
Egner:2022jot
. It seems that the perturbative expansion of the amplitude of
\gamma^{*}\to J/\psi
does not converge in those calculations. Moreover, the higher-order corrections of the amplitude of
Z\to J/\psi+\gamma^{*}
involve the large logarithms of
m_{{}_{Z}}^{2}/m_{c}^{2}
. Considering these problems, we have presented an improved calculation for the decay rate of
Z\to J/\psi+J/\psi
compared to the fixed-order calculation. In our calculation, the amplitude for the photon fragmentation is expressed as the product of the amplitude of
Z\to J/\psi+\gamma^{*}
and the amplitude of
\gamma^{*}\to J/\psi
. Then, the amplitude of
\gamma^{*}\to J/\psi
is extracted from the measured value of
\Gamma(J/\psi\to e^{+}e^{-})
, and the amplitude of
Z\to J/\psi+\gamma^{*}
is calculated by the light-cone approach where the large logarithms of
m_{{}_{Z}}^{2}/m_{c}^{2}
are resummed through solving the evolution equation of the
J/\psi
LCDA. The advantage of our calculation is that the higher-order QCD and relativistic corrections in the amplitude of
\gamma^{*}\to J/\psi
and the large logarithms of
m_{{}_{Z}}^{2}/m_{c}^{2}
in the amplitude of
Z\to J/\psi+\gamma^{*}
are resummed. For the non-fragmentation amplitude, we adopt the fixed-order approach under the NRQCD factorization. In our calculation of the non-fragmentation amplitude, the NLO QCD and NLO relativistic corrections are included simultaneously.

The obtained branching fraction for
Z\to J/\psi+J/\psi
is
8.66^{+1.48}_{-0.69}\times 10^{-11}
, which is compatible with the current experimental upper limit
{\rm Br}(Z\to J/\psi+J/\psi)=1.4\times 10^{-6}
given by the CMS collaboration
CMS:2022fsq
. In recent years, several high-luminosity
e^{+}e^{-}
colliders, such as ILC, CEPC, FCC-ee, and Super
Z
factory are proposed. A large number of
Z
boson events are expected to be generated at these future
e^{+}e^{-}
colliders. For instance, about
5\times 10^{12}

Z
bosons can be generated at the FCC-ee
FCC:2018evy
. According to the branching fraction obtained in this work, about
400

Z\to J/\psi+J/\psi
events are expected to be generated at the FCC-ee. One may expect that this decay can be studied at these future
e^{+}e^{-}
colliders.

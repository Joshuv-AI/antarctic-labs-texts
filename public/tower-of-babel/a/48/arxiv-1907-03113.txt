. In this article we discuss the notion of γ \gamma - H ∞ H^{\infty} -bounded calculus, strong γ \gamma - m m - H ∞ H^{\infty} -bounded calculus on half-plane and weak- γ \gamma -Gomilko-Shi-Feng condition and give a connection between them. 1.
INTRODUCTION

The
H^{\infty}
-functional calculus for a sectorial operator and a strip-type operator have played and important role in the spectral theory and evolutions equations
[
6
]
.
The
H^{\infty}
-functional calculus for a half-plane type operator is a recent tool studied in
[
4
]
.
As for sectorial and strip-type operators, it is natural to construct a holomorphic functional calculus for
a half-plane type operator
A
via the Dunford formula

f(A)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\partial R}f(\lambda)R(\lambda,A)d\lambda.

Here,
R
is a half-plane and
f
is a bounded analytic function on
R

with good properties which ensure that
f(A)
is bounded. This construction
allows to define a notion of bounded
H^{\infty}
-functional calculus for a half-plane type operator.
Contrary to bounded
H^{\infty}
-functional calculus for a sectorial
or a strip-type operator, the bounded
H^{\infty}
-functional calculus for a half-plane type operator has no characterization with simple estimates, even on Hilbert space. However, a weaker notions of bounded
H^{\infty}
-functional calculus,
called strong
m
-bounded functional calculus (Definition
2.7
) turn out to be equivalent to a
condition studied independently by Gomilko
[
5
]
and Shi and Feng
[
18
]
(Definition
2.5
),
called GFS condition in this paper. Furthermore, they show that this condition is sufficient for
the generation of bounded
C_{0}
-semigroups. Therefore, the set (containing all
sectorial operators of type
<\frac{\pi}{2}
) of all half-plane type operators
which have GFS is included (equal when
X
is Hilbert)
in the set of all negative generators of bounded
C_{0}
-semigroup.

The aim of this paper is to study the generation of a
\gamma
-bounded
C_{0}
-semigroups on a Banach space
X
,
that is, a
C_{0}
-semigroup
(T_{t})_{t\geqslant 0}
on a Banach space
X
such that the set
\{T(t)\,:\,t\geq 0\}
is
\gamma
-bounded. A first step is to consider a stronger condition than
the GFS condition (however equivalent on Hilbert spaces), that we call
weak
\gamma
-Gomilko-Shi-Feng (Definition
3.4
) and abreviate as the
W\gamma
-GFS condition.
It turns out that the
W\gamma
-GFS condition
is equivalent to a notion of
\gamma
-bounded strong
m
-bounded functional calculus.
Furthermore the latter
condition is sufficient for the generation of weak
\gamma
-bounded
C_{0}
-semigroups, and hence
for the generation of
\gamma
-bounded
C_{0}
-semigroups when the underlying space is
K
-convex.
Therefore when
X
is
K
-convex, the set (containing all
\gamma
-sectorial operators of
\gamma
-type
<\frac{\pi}{2}
) of all operators which satisfy the
W\gamma
-GFS condition is equal to the set of
all negative generators of
\gamma
-bounded
C_{0}
-semigroups.
This last statement is contained in our main result
(Theorem
4.1
), which gives equivalence of the
generation of
\gamma
-bounded
C_{0}
-semigroup with the
\gamma
-m-bounded functional calculus,
as well as with some estimates of the resolvent of the negative generator and its adjoint.
From this theorem, we deduce, when
X
is
K
-convex space,
a
\gamma
-bounded version of the Gearhart-Prüss Theorem (Corollary
5.3
)
and the following result: if
(T_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is a bounded
C_{0}
-semigroup on a

K
-convex space and if the set
\{e^{-\delta t}T_{t}\,:\,t\geq 0\}

is
\gamma
-bounded for one
\delta>0
, then it is
\gamma
-bounded for each
\delta>0
.
Moreover it is possible to find an example of bounded
C_{0}
-semigroup
such that
\{e^{-\delta t}T_{t}\,:\,t\geq 0\}
is
\gamma
-bounded for all
\delta>0
but not
for
\delta=0
.

Now we describe the structure of the paper. Section 2 only contains preliminary results.
We recall important results of
[
4
]
, then we collect some results about

\gamma
-boundedness and weak
\gamma
-boundedness and generalized square functions.
In Section 3, we discuss the
W\gamma
-GFS condition and strong
\gamma
-
m
-bounded
H^{\infty}
-functional calculus. We give our main result in Section 4, namely a version of Gomilko-Shi-Feng Theorem on
K
-convex spaces. Section 5 is devoted
to some consequences of the results of Section 4, in particular
we state a version of the Gearhart-Prüss Theorem in
K
-convex spaces.
Finally, Section 6 is dedicated to an overview of the implications between the different notions of
H^{\infty}
-bounded functional calculus for half-plane type operator and the generation of bounded and
\gamma
-bounded
C_{0}
-semigroups.

2.
BACKGROUND AND PRELIMINARY RESULTS

For any Banach spaces
X,Y
, we let
\mathcal{L}(X,Y)
denote the space of
all bounded linear operators from
X
into
Y
. If
Y=X
, we write

\mathcal{L}(X)
instead of
\mathcal{L}(X,X)
.
If
A
is a closed operator on
X
, we denote by
Dom(A)
,
\rho(A)
and
\sigma(A)
the domain,
the resolvent set and the spectrum of
A
,
respectively. When
\lambda\in\rho(A)
, we let
R(\lambda,A)=(\lambda I-A)^{-1}

denote the corresponding resolvent operator.

2.1.  Half-plane type operator.

In this subsection we review the definitions of half-plane type operators
and their functional calculi,
following
[
4
]
.

We fix a real number
\omega
. For any
\alpha\leq\omega
, we consider
the right open half-plane

R_{\alpha}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}\,:\,Re(z)>\alpha\}.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
A
be a closed and densely defined operator on
X
. We will say that
A

is of
half-plane type

\omega
if
\sigma(A)\subset\overline{R_{\omega}}
and

\forall\,\alpha<\omega,\qquad\sup\{\left\|R(z,A)\right\|\,:\,Re(z)\leq\alpha\}<\infty.

We will say that a
C_{0}
-semigroup
(T_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is of type
\omega\in\mathbb{R}
if
there exists a constant
C>0
such that
\left\|T_{t}\right\|\leq Ce^{-\omega t}
for all
t\geq 0
.
Note that such
\omega
always exists
[
17
, Theorem 2.2 section 1.2]
.
It follows from the Laplace formula (see e.g.
[
17
, Formula (7.1) section 1.7]
)
that if
-A
generates a
C_{0}
-semigroup
(T_{t})_{t\geq 0}
of type
\omega
, then
A
is an
operator of half-plane type
\omega

(in fact
A
is even strong half-plane in the sense of
[
4
, Definition 2.1]
).

Throughout the rest of this subsection, we let
A
be an operator of half-plane type
\omega
.

For any
\alpha\leq\omega
, let

H^{\infty}(R_{\alpha})
be the space of all bounded analytic functions
f:R_{\alpha}\to\mathbb{C}
,
equipped with the norm
\left\|f\right\|_{H^{\infty}(R_{\alpha})}:=\underset{z\in R_{\alpha}}{\sup}|f(z)|
.
Then
H^{\infty}(R_{\alpha})
is a Banach algebra.

Next we consider the auxiliary space

\mathcal{E}(R_{\alpha}):=\{f\in H^{\infty}(R_{\alpha})\,:\,\exists s>0,f(z)=O(|z|^{-(1+s)})\text{ as }|z|\rightarrow\infty\}.

Whenever
\alpha<\delta<\omega
and
f\in\mathcal{E}(R_{\alpha})
, the integral

(1)

f(A):=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}f(\delta+it)R(\delta+it,A)dt

is absolutely convergent in
\mathcal{L}(X)
.
Further its value is independent of
\delta\in(\alpha,\omega)
. This is due
to Cauchy’s theorem for vector-valued holomorphic functions.

If
f\in H^{\infty}(R_{\alpha})
, we can define a closed, densely defined, operator

f(A)
by regularisation as follows (see
[
4
]
and
[
6
]
for more details).
Let
\mu<\alpha
and set
e(z):=(\mu-z)^{-2}
. Then

e\in\mathcal{E}(R_{\alpha})
,
ef\in\mathcal{E}(R_{\alpha})
and

e(A)=R(\mu,A)^{2}
is injective. Then
f(A)
is defined by

f(A)=e(A)^{-1}(ef)(A),

with
Dom(f(A))
equal to the space of all
x\in X
such that
[(ef)(A)](x)
belongs
to the range of
e(A)
(=
Dom(A^{2})
).
It turns out that this definition does not depend on the choice of
\mu
.

In particular, for any
t\geq 0
, the function
z\mapsto e^{-tz}
belongs to
H^{\infty}(R_{\alpha})
,
hence the above construction provides an operator

e^{-tA}:=(e^{-tz})(A).

The following proposition (see
[
4
, Proposition 2.5]
) gives an expected link
between the generator of a
C_{0}
-semigroup and its exponential in the previous sense.

Proposition 2.2
.

The operator
-A
is the generator of a
C_{0}
-semigroup
(T_{t})_{t\geq 0}
if
and only if
e^{-tA}
is a bounded operator for all
t\in[0,1]
and

\underset{t\in[0,1]}{\sup}\left\|e^{-tA}\right\|<\infty
. In this case, we have

T_{t}=e^{-tA}
for all
t\geq 0
.

Definition 2.3
.

We say that
A
has a bounded
H^{\infty}
-functional calculus of type
\omega

if there exists
C>0
such that for any
\alpha<\omega
and for any
f\in H^{\infty}(R_{\alpha})
,

f(A)
belongs to
\mathcal{L}(X)
and

(2)

\left\|f(A)\right\|\leq C\left\|f\right\|_{H^{\infty}(R_{\omega})}.

Remark 2.4
.

The reasoning at the beginning of
[
4
, Section 5]
and the so-called convergence
lemma (see
[
4
, Theorem 3.1]
) show that to prove
that an operator
A
has a bounded
H^{\infty}
-functional calculus of type
\omega
,
it suffices to prove an estimate (
2
) for any

f\in{\mathcal{E}}(R_{\alpha})
and any
\alpha<\omega
.

As a direct consequence of Proposition
2.2
,
we see that if
A
has a bounded
H^{\infty}
-functional calculus of type
\omega
,
then
-A
generates a
C_{0}
-semigroup of type
\omega
.
The converse does not hold true, even on Hilbert space (see Section 6).

The following condition, called Gomilko-Shi-Feng condition (GFS), is important to connect

C_{0}
-semigroups and functional calculi.

Definition 2.5
.

Let
m\geq 1
be an integer. We say that

A
satisfies
(GFS)_{m,\omega}
if there exists a constant

C>0
such that

\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\langle R(\alpha+it,A)^{m+1}x,y\rangle|dt\leq\frac{C}{(\omega-\alpha)^{m}}\left\|x\right\|\left\|y\right\|

for any
x\in X
and any
y\in X^{*}
.

Gomilko
[
5
]
and Shi-Feng
[
18
]
have shown
the following two results: if
A
has
(GFS)_{1,0}
, then

-A
generates a bounded
C_{0}
-semigroup
(T_{t})_{t\geq 0}
;
conversely if
X
is a Hilbert space, the negative generator
of a bounded
C_{0}
-semigroup satisfies
(GFS)_{1,0}
.
This is now known as the Gomilko-Shi-Feng Theorem.

Remark 2.6
.

It is easy to check that a sectorial operator of type
<\frac{\pi}{2}
has
(GFS)_{1,0}
.
We refer e.g. to
[
6
]
for information about sectorial operators.
We recall that
A
is a sectorial operator of type
<\frac{\pi}{2}
if
and only if
-A
generates a bounded analytic semigroup and that in this case,
there exists a constant
C>0
such that

\forall\,\lambda\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{R_{0}},\quad\left\|\lambda R(\lambda,A)\right\|\leq C.

This implies that for any
\alpha<0
and any
t\in\mathbb{R}
,
\left\|R(\alpha+it,B)\right\|\leq\frac{C}{|\alpha+it|}
.
Hence for any
\alpha<0
and arbitrary
x\in X
and
y\in X^{*}
, we have

\displaystyle(-\alpha)\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\langle R(\alpha+it,A)^{2}x,y\rangle|dt

\displaystyle\leq(-\alpha)\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left\|R(\alpha+it,A)\right\|^{2}dt\left\|x\right\|\left\|y\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{-C\alpha}{(-\alpha)^{2}+t^{2}}dt\left\|x\right\|\left\|y\right\|

\displaystyle=C\pi\left\|x\right\|\left\|y\right\|,

which proves the result.

It is noticed in
[
4
, Lemma 5.4]
that for any
\alpha<\beta<\omega

and any
f\in H^{\infty}(R_{\alpha})
, the
m
-th derivative
f^{(m)}
of
f

belongs to
H^{\infty}(R_{\beta})
for any integer
m\geq 1
. Thus it makes sense to
define
f^{(m)}(A)
.

Definition 2.7
.

Let
m\geq 1
be an integer.
We say that
A
has a
strong
m
-bounded functional calculus

of type
\omega
if there exists a constant
C>0
such that for each

\alpha<\omega
and each
f\in H^{\infty}(R_{\alpha})
,

f^{(m)}(A)\in\mathcal{L}(X)\text{ and }\left\|f^{(m)}(A)\right\|\leq\frac{C}{(\omega-\alpha)^{m}}\left\|f\right\|_{H^{\infty}(R_{\alpha})}.

The following remarkable results are proved in
[
4
, Theorem 6.4]
:
A
has

(GFS)_{m,\omega}
if and only if
A
has a strong
m
-bounded functional calculus
of type
\omega
, if and only if
A
has a strong
1
-bounded functional calculus
of type
\omega
. (In particular,
(GFS)_{m,\omega}
does not depend on
m
.)
Further if
A
has a strong
m
-bounded functional calculus of type
\omega
, then
-A

generates a
C_{0}
-semigroup of type
\omega
. It is further shown in
[
4
, Theorem 7.1]

that if
X
is a Hilbert space, then conversely,
A
has a strong
m
-bounded functional calculus of type
\omega

if
-A
generates a
C_{0}
-semigroup of type
\omega
. This converse is wrong in general, see
Section 6 for more on this.

The following is implicit in
[
4
]
.

Proposition 2.8
.

If
A
has a bounded
H^{\infty}
-functional calculus of type
\omega
,
then
A
has a strong
m
-bounded functional calculus of type
\omega
, for any
m\geq 1
.

Proof.

According to
[
4
, Lemma 5.4]
, there is a constant

K>0
, such that for any

\alpha<\omega
and any
f\in H^{\infty}({R_{\alpha}})
, we have

\left\|f^{(m)}\right\|_{H^{\infty}(R_{\omega})}\leq\frac{K}{(\omega-\alpha)^{m}}\left\|f\right\|_{H^{\infty}(R_{\alpha})}
.

Assume that
A
has a bounded
H^{\infty}
-functional calculus of type
\omega
.
Then for any
f
as above,
f^{(m)}(A)\in{\mathcal{L}}(X)
and we have

\left\|f^{(m)}(A)\right\|\leq C\left\|f^{(m)}\right\|_{H^{\infty}(R_{\omega})}\leq\frac{KC}{(\omega-\alpha)^{m}}\left\|f\right\|_{H^{\infty}(R_{\alpha})}.

This proves that
A
has a strong
m
-bounded functional calculus of type
\omega
.

2.2.  The Sun Dual of a
C_{0}
-Semigroup

In this subsection we collect a few facts from
[
16
]

which are useful when dealing with non reflexive Banach
spaces.

Let
X
be a Banach space and let
Z\subset X^{*}
be a closed subspace. We say that

Z
is norming if there exists a constant
c>0
such that for any
x\in X
,

c\left\|x\right\|\leq\sup\bigl\{|\langle x,y\rangle|\,:\,y\in Z,\ \left\|y\right\|\leq 1\bigr\}.

Let
(T_{t})_{t\geq 0}
be a
C_{0}
-semigroup on
X
, with generator
-A
. It may happen that
the dual semigroup
(T_{t}^{*})_{t\geq 0}
is not strongly continuous on
X^{*}
.
We denote by

X^{\odot}
(pronounced
X
-sun) the set

(3)

X^{\odot}:=\{x\in X^{*},\quad\left\|T^{*}_{t}x-x\right\|\underset{t\rightarrow 0}{\longrightarrow}0\}.

This set trivially satisfies
T^{*}_{t}(X^{\odot})\subset X^{\odot}
for every
t\geq 0
.
Moreover
X^{\odot}
is a closed and weak*-dense
subspace of
X^{*}
. Indeed we have

X^{\odot}=\overline{D(A^{*})}
.

We let
T^{\odot}_{t}
denote the restriction of
T^{*}_{t}
to
X^{\odot}
. Then by definition,

(T^{\odot}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is a
C_{0}
-semigroup on
X^{\odot}
.
It is called the
sun dual of
(T_{t})_{t\geq 0}
. Let
A^{\odot}
be its negative generator.
Then
A^{\odot}
is the part of
A^{*}
in
X^{\odot}
(see
[
6
, page 6]
for definition).

We will use the following two results.

Theorem 2.9
.

Let
(T_{t})_{t\geq 0}
be a
C_{0}
-semigroup on
X
, with generator
-A
.

(1)

We have
\rho(A)=\rho(A^{*})=\rho(A^{\odot})
and
R(\lambda,A)^{*}y=R(\lambda,A^{*})y=R(\lambda,A^{\odot})y
for all
\lambda\in\rho(A)
and
for any
y\in X^{\odot}
.

(2)

The space
X^{\odot}
is norming.

About (2), we note that more precisely, if we define

\left\|x\right\|^{\prime}:=\sup\big\{|\langle x,x^{\odot}\rangle|,\,x^{\odot}\in X^{\odot},\,\left\|x^{\odot}\right\|\leq 1\big\},

and if we let
M:=\varlimsup_{t\rightarrow 0}\left\|T(t)\right\|
, then we have

\left\|x\right\|^{\prime}\leq\left\|x\right\|\leq M\left\|x\right\|^{\prime},\qquad x\in X.

2.3.
\gamma
-boundedness on Banach spaces.

In recent years,
\gamma
-boundedness and
\mathcal{R}
-boundedness played an
important role in the operator valued harmonic analysis, multiplier theory and functional calculi
(see
[
10
]
for more details). Throughout
X,Y
denote arbitrary Banach spaces and
we let
(\gamma_{n})_{n\geq 1}
be a sequence of independent
complex valued standard Gaussian variables on some probability space
\Sigma
.
We denote by
G(X)
the closure of

\big\{\sum_{k=1}^{n}\gamma_{k}\otimes x_{k}\,:\,x_{k}\in X,\,n\in\mathbb{N}\big\}

in
L^{2}(\Sigma,X)
. For
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\in X
, we let

\left\|\sum_{k=1}^{n}\gamma_{k}\otimes x_{k}\right\|_{G(X)}:=\bigg(\underset{\Sigma}{\mathop{\text{\Huge$\int$}}\nolimits}{\left\|\sum_{k=1}^{n}\gamma_{k}(\lambda)x_{k}\right\|^{2}d\lambda}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}

denote the induced norm.

Definition 2.10
.

Let
\mathcal{T}\subset\mathcal{L}(X,Y)
be a set of operators. We say that

\mathcal{T}
is
\gamma
-bounded if
there exists a constant
C\geq 0
such that for all finite sequences

(T_{n})_{n=1}^{N}\subset\mathcal{T}
and
(x_{n})_{n=1}^{N}\subset X
, the following inequality holds:

(4)

\left\|\sum_{n=1}^{N}\gamma_{n}\otimes T_{n}x_{n}\right\|_{G(Y)}\leq C\left\|\sum_{n=1}^{N}\gamma_{n}\otimes x_{n}\right\|_{G(X)}.

The least admissible constant in the above inequality is called the

\gamma
-bound of
\mathcal{T}
and we denote this quantity by
\gamma(\mathcal{T})
.
If
\mathcal{T}
fails to be
\gamma
-bounded, we set
\gamma(\mathcal{T})=\infty
.

Replacing the sequence
(\gamma_{k})_{k\geq 1}
by a sequence of independent Rademacher
variables
(\epsilon_{k})_{k\geq 1}
in the above definition, we obtain the definition of

\mathcal{R}
-boundedness. It is well known that any
\mathcal{R}
-bounded set is
\gamma
-bounded, and that
these notions are equivalent when
X
has finite cotype (see
[
10
, theorem 8.6.4]
for a more general result).
Furthermore if
X
has cotype
2
and
Y
has type 2 (especially when
X=Y
is an Hilbert space)
then
\mathcal{R}
-boundedness,
\gamma
-boundedness and uniform boundedness are equivalent.

Assume that
X
and
Y
are Banach lattices with finite cotype.
By the Khintchine-Maurey inequality
[
10
, Theorem 7.2.13]
,
there exist
c,C>0
such that for every
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\in X
,

c\left\|\big(\sum_{n=1}^{N}|x_{n}|^{2}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right\|_{X}\leq\left\|\sum_{n=1}^{N}\gamma_{n}\otimes x_{n}\right\|_{G(X)}\leq C\left\|\big(\sum_{n=1}^{N}|x_{n}|^{2}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right\|_{X},

and
Y
satisfies a similar property.
Hence a set
\mathcal{T}\subset\mathcal{L}(X,Y)
is
\gamma
-bounded if
and only if there exists
C\geq 0
such that for all finite sequences

(T_{n})_{n=1}^{N}\subset\mathcal{T}
and
(x_{n})_{n=1}^{N}\subset X
,

(5)

\left\|\big(\sum_{n=1}^{N}|T_{n}x_{n}|^{2}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right\|_{Y}\leq C\left\|\big(\sum_{n=1}^{N}|x_{n}|^{2}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right\|_{X}.

We recall for further use that
\gamma
-boundedness is stable
under the strong operator topology.

Proposition 2.11
.

Let
\mathcal{T}\subset\mathcal{L}(X,Y)
be a
\gamma
-bounded set. Then
the closure
\overline{\mathcal{T}}^{so}
of
\mathcal{T}
in the
strong operator topology is
\gamma
-bounded with

\gamma(\overline{\mathcal{T}}^{so})=\gamma(\mathcal{T})
.

We will need the following lemma, for which we refer to
[
10
, Theorem 8.5.4]
.

Lemma 2.12

(
L^{\infty}
-integral means)
.

Let
(\Omega,\mu)
be a measure space and let
F:\Omega\rightarrow\mathcal{L}(X,Y)

be an operator-valued function. Assume that
F(\cdot)x
belongs to
L^{1}(\Omega;Y)

for any
x\in X
and that there exists a constant
K>0
such that

\left\|F(\cdot)x\right\|_{L^{1}(\Omega;X)}\leq K\left\|x\right\|,\quad x\in X.

Then, for any
\phi\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
, we can define a
bounded operator
T^{F}_{\phi}\in\mathcal{L}(X,Y)
by

T^{F}_{\phi}x=\int_{\Omega}\phi(s)F(s)xd\mu(s),\quad x\in X,

and for any
C>0
, the set

\mathcal{T}^{F}_{\infty}:=\big\{T^{F}_{\phi}\,:\,\phi\in L^{\infty}(\Omega),\,\left\|\phi\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq C\big\}

is
\gamma
-bounded.

The adjoint set of a
\gamma
-bounded set may not be
\gamma
-bounded
(
[
10
, example 8.4.2]
). Following
[
11
]
, we introduce
weaker notions to circumvent this difficulty.

Definition 2.13
.

1)

Let
\mathcal{T}\subset\mathcal{L}(X,Y)
be a set of operators.
We say that
\mathcal{T}
is weak
\gamma
-bounded (
W\gamma

2)

Lemma 2.14

(weak
L^{1}
-integral means)

Let

With these two facts in hand,

Details are left to the reader.

We will need the notion of
K
-convexity,
for which we refer to
[
14
]
or
[

]
.
We recall that a Banach space

is

-convex if and only if there exists a constant

such that for all

the following inequality holds:

(8)

\left\|\sum_{n=1}^{N}\gamma_{n}\otimes x_{n}\right\|_{G(X)}\leq K\sup\Big\{\big|\sum_{n=1}^{N}\langle x_{n},y_{n}\rangle\big|\,:\,y_{1},\ldots,y_{N}\in X^{*},\ \left\|\sum_{n=1}^{N}\gamma_{n}\otimes y_{n}\right\|_{G(X^{*})}\leq 1\Big\}.

It turns out that
X
is
K
-convex if and only if
X^{*}
is
K
-convex. If this is the case, then
according to
[
10
, Corollary 7.4.6]
, there exists a constant

K^{\prime}>0
such that for all
y_{1},\ldots,y_{n}\in X^{*}
,

(9)

\left\|\sum_{n=1}^{N}\gamma_{n}\otimes y_{n}\right\|_{G(X^{*})}\leq K^{\prime}\sup\Big\{\big|\sum_{n=1}^{N}\langle x_{n},y_{n}\rangle\big|\,:\,x_{1},\ldots,x_{N}\in X,\ \left\|\sum_{n=1}^{N}\gamma_{n}\otimes x_{n}\right\|_{G(X)}\leq 1\Big\}.

We recall that all UMD spaces are
K
-convex. In particular,
L^{p}
-spaces are

K
-convex for any
1<p<\infty
. Further any closed subspace of a

K
-convex space is
K
-convex.
We also recall that any
K
-convex
space has a finite cotype. In particular, a
K
-convex Banach space
cannot contain
c_{0}
. A fundamental result on
K
-convexity is
Pisier’s Theorem
[
10
, Theorem 7.4.23]

which asserts that
X
is
K
-convex if and only if
X
has non-trivial type.

We note that
there exist non reflexive
K
-convex Banach spaces.
It readily follows from (
8
)
that if

is a
K
-convex space, then a set
\mathcal{T}\subset\mathcal{L}(X,Y)
is

-bounded if and only if it is
\gamma
-bounded. Likewise using (

we obtain that if

is

-convex, then a set

is

\gamma
-bounded.

We now turn to the definition of
\gamma
-spaces, which play a fundamental role in this paper.
Let
H
be a Hilbert space.
A linear operator
T:H\rightarrow X
is called
\gamma
-summing if

\left\|T\right\|_{\gamma}:=\sup\left\|\sum_{n=1}^{N}\gamma_{n}\otimes Th_{n}\right\|_{G(X)}<\infty,

where the supremum is taken over all finite orthonormal system
\{h_{1},...,h_{n}\}
in
H
.
We let
\gamma_{\infty}(H;X)
denote the space of all
\gamma
-summing operator and
we endow it with the norm
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\gamma}
. Then

\gamma_{\infty}(H;X)
is a Banach space. Clearly any finite rank (bounded) operator
is a
\gamma
-summing operator. We let
\gamma(H;X)
be
the closure in
\gamma_{\infty}(H;X)
of the space of finite rank operators from
H
into
X
.
The spaces
\gamma_{\infty}(H;X)
and
\gamma(H;X)

do not coincide in general
[
10
, Example 9.1.21]
but when
X
does not contain a copy
of
c_{0}
(in particular when

is
K
-convex) then these spaces coincide.

Let
(S,\mu)
be a measure space. We say that a function

f:S\rightarrow X
is weakly
L^{2}
if for each
x^{*}\in X^{*}
,
the function
s\mapsto\langle f(s),x^{*}\rangle
is measurable and belongs to
L^{2}(S)
.
If
f:S\rightarrow X
is measurable and weakly
L^{2}
, one can define an opertor
\mathbb{I}_{f}:L^{2}(S)\to X
,
given by

\mathbb{I}_{f}(g):=\int_{S}g(s)f(s)ds,\quad g\in L^{2}(S),

where this integral is defined in the Pettis sense.

We let
\gamma(S;X)
be the space of all measurable and weakly
L^{2}

functions
f:S\rightarrow X
such that
\mathbb{I}_{f}

belongs to
\gamma(L^{2}(S);X)
. We endow it with
\left\|f\right\|_{\gamma(S;X)}:=\left\|\mathbb{I}_{f}\right\|_{\gamma(L^{2}(S);X)}
. A remarkable fact is the density
of simple function in the set
\gamma(S;X)

[
10
, Proposition 9.2.5]
.

Now we collect some important results, which will be useful in the next sections.
We start with the so-called Multiplier Theorem
[
10
, Theorem 9.5.1]
,
a high ranking result involving the
\gamma
-boundedness.
We state it under the assumption that
X
does not contain
c_{0}
.
Thus the following statement applies to
K
-convex spaces.

Theorem 2.15

(
\gamma
-Multiplier theorem)
.

Let
X
be a Banach space not containing
c_{0}
.
Let
M:S\rightarrow\mathcal{L}(X)
be a strongly mesurable function
and assume that its range
\mathcal{M}:=\{M(s):s\in S\}
is
\gamma
-bounded.
Then for every function
\psi:S\rightarrow X
in
\gamma(S;X)
,
the function
M\psi:S\rightarrow X
belongs to
\gamma(S;X)
, and we have

\left\|M\psi\right\|_{\gamma(S;X)}\leq\gamma(\mathcal{M})\left\|\psi\right\|_{\gamma(S;X)}.

The next result is an inequality of Hölder type
[
10
, Theorem 9.2.14 (1)]
.

Theorem 2.16

(
\gamma
-Hölder inequality)
.

If
f:S\rightarrow X
and
g:S\rightarrow X^{*}
belongs to

\gamma(S;X)
and
\gamma(S;X^{*})
, respectively, then
\langle f,g\rangle
belongs to
L^{1}(S)
and we have

\left\|\langle f,g\rangle\right\|_{L^{1}(S)}\leq\left\|f\right\|_{\gamma(S;X)}\left\|g\right\|_{\gamma(S;X^{*})}.

Now we give an extension result, for which we refer to
[
10
, Theorem 9.6.1]
.
We identify the algebraic tensor product
H^{*}\otimes X
with the space
of finite rank bounded operator operators from
H
into
X
in the usual way, that
is, we set
(h^{*}\otimes x)(h)=h^{*}(h)x
for any
h\in H,\,h^{*}\in H^{*}

and
x\in X
.

Theorem 2.17
.

[Extension theorem]
Let
H_{1}
and
H_{2}
be Hilbert spaces. For any bounded operator
U:H_{1}^{*}\rightarrow H_{2}^{*}
, the mapping

U\otimes I_{X}:H_{1}^{*}\otimes X\longrightarrow H_{2}^{*}\otimes X,

taking
h^{*}\otimes x
to
U(h^{*})\otimes x
for any
h^{*}\in H_{1}^{*}
and
x\in X
,
has an unique extension to a bounded linear operator

\tilde{U}:\gamma(H_{1};X)\rightarrow\gamma(H_{2};X)
of the same norm.
Furthermore for all
T\in\gamma(H_{1};X)
,

(10)

\tilde{U}(T)=T\circ\,^{t}U,

where
{}^{t}U
denotes the Banach space adjoint of
U
.

To conclude this part, we apply the above principles to
the Fourier-Plancherel transform
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})\to L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
. We identify the dual of
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})

with
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
via the usual duality map provided by integration on
\mathbb{R}
.

Lemma 2.18
.

For any
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R};X)
, let
\hat{f}
be its Fourier transform defined by

\hat{f}(t)=\,\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-its}f(s)ds.

Let
\mathcal{F}:L^{2}(\mathbb{R})\rightarrow L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
be the Fourier-Plancherel transform
(which coincides with
f\mapsto\hat{f}
on
L^{1}(\mathbb{R})\cap L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
). Let

\tilde{\mathcal{F}}:\gamma(L^{2}(\mathbb{R});X)\rightarrow\gamma(L^{2}(\mathbb{R});X)

be its extension provided by Theorem
2.17
. If
f\in\gamma(\mathbb{R};X)\cap L^{1}(\mathbb{R};X)
, then we have

\widehat{f}\in\gamma(\mathbb{R};X)\qquad\hbox{and}\qquad\tilde{\mathcal{F}}(\mathbb{I}_{f})=\mathbb{I}_{\widehat{f}}

and further,

\left\|\widehat{f}\right\|_{\gamma(\mathbb{R};X)}=\sqrt{2\pi}\,\left\|f\right\|_{\gamma(\mathbb{R};X)}.

Proof.

Obviously
\widehat{f}
is measurable, and as
f
is weakly

L^{2}
,
\widehat{f}
is also weakly
L^{2}
. Indeed for
x\in X^{*}
one has by Fourier-Plancherel theorem :

\displaystyle\left\|\langle\widehat{f},x^{*}\rangle\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\big|\langle\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(s)e^{-ist}ds,x^{*}\rangle\big|^{2}dt=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\big|\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-its}\langle f(s),x^{*}\rangle ds\big|^{2}dt

\displaystyle=\left\|\mathcal{F}(\langle f,x^{*}\rangle)\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}=2\pi\,\left\|\langle f,x^{*}\rangle\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}.

It follows that
\mathbb{I}_{\widehat{f}}
is well defined and bounded.
Now let
g\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})\cap L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
. Then by Fubini theorem, using equality

{}^{t}\mathcal{F}=\mathcal{F}
and (
10
):

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathcal{F}}(\mathbb{I}_{f})(g)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{I}_{f}\circ\mathcal{F}(g)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathcal{F}(g)(t)f(t)dt=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\big(\int_{\mathbb{R}}g(s)e^{-ist}ds\big)f(t)dt

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}g(s)\big(\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(t)e^{-its}dt\big)ds=\mathbb{I}_{\widehat{f}}(g).

By density and since
\tilde{\mathcal{F}}(\mathbb{I}_{f})
and
\mathbb{I}_{\widehat{f}}

are bounded, the equality
\tilde{\mathcal{F}}(\mathbb{I}_{f})=\mathbb{I}_{\widehat{f}}

follows. Hence
\widehat{f}\in\gamma(\mathbb{R};X)
.

Finally since
(\sqrt{2\pi})^{-1}\mathcal{F}
is an isometry,
extension principle yields the equalities

\left\|\widehat{f}\right\|_{\gamma(\mathbb{R};X)}=\left\|\mathbb{I}_{\widehat{f}}\right\|_{\gamma(L^{2}(\mathbb{R});X)}=\left\|\tilde{\mathcal{F}}(\mathbb{I}_{f})\right\|_{\gamma(L^{2}(\mathbb{R});X)}=\sqrt{2\pi}\left\|\mathbb{I}_{f}\right\|_{\gamma(L^{2}(\mathbb{R});X)}=\sqrt{2\pi}\left\|f\right\|_{\gamma(\mathbb{R};X)}.

Example 3.1
.

It is easy to exhibit

and let

(-\alpha)\int_{\mathbb{R}}\epsilon(t)R(\alpha+it,A)^{2}dt\,=\int_{\mathbb{R}}(-\alpha)(\alpha+it)^{-2}\epsilon(t)\bigl((\alpha+it)R(\alpha+it,A)\bigr)^{2}dt.

\left\|t\mapsto(-\alpha)(\alpha+it)^{-2}\epsilon(t)\right\|_{L^{1}}\leq\pi
, Lemma
2.12
ensures that
the set

\big\{(-\alpha)\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\epsilon(t)R(\alpha+it,A)^{2}dt\,:\,\epsilon:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\overline{\mathbb{D}}\text{ measurable},\,\alpha<0\big\}

is
W^{*}\gamma
-bounded. Hence the result follows from the above Lemma
3.5
.

Proposition 3.7
.

Let
m\geq 1
be an integer and
assume that
A
has property
(W\gamma\text{-}GFS)_{m,\omega}
.
Then for any integer
1\leq p\leq m
,

A
has property
(W\gamma\text{-}GFS)_{p,\omega}
.

Proof.

We proceed by induction, showing that if
m\geq 2
, then
(W\gamma\text{-}GFS)_{m,\omega}

implies
(W\gamma\text{-}GFS)_{m-1,\omega}
.

Suppose that
A
has property
(W\gamma\text{-}GFS)_{m,\omega}
, with
m\geq 2
.
Let
\epsilon:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\overline{\mathbb{D}}
be a measurable function
and let
\alpha<\omega
. Applying
[
4
, Proposition 6.3. (a)]
, we have

R(\alpha+it,A)^{m}\,=\,-m\int_{-\infty}^{\alpha}R(u+it,A)^{m+1}du

for any
t\in\mathbb{R}
. Hence
for any
x\in X
, for any
y\in X^{*}
and for any
t\in\mathbb{R}
,

\displaystyle\langle\epsilon(t)R(\alpha+it,A)^{m}x,y\rangle

\displaystyle=-\int_{-\infty}^{\alpha}m\langle\epsilon(t)R(u+it,A)^{m+1}x,y\rangle du

\displaystyle=-\int_{-\infty}^{0}m\langle\epsilon(t)R(\alpha+u+it,A)^{m+1}x,y\rangle du.

We now integrate over
t
. Property
(GFS)_{m,\omega}
ensures that
we can apply Fubini’s theorem in the following
computation:

\displaystyle(\omega-\alpha)^{m-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}}

\displaystyle\langle\epsilon(t)R(\alpha+it,A)^{m}x,y\rangle dt

\displaystyle=-\int_{\mathbb{R}}m(\omega-\alpha)^{m-1}\int_{-\infty}^{0}\langle\epsilon(t)R(\alpha+u+it,A)^{m+1}x,y\rangle dudt

\displaystyle=-\int_{-\infty}^{0}m(\omega-\alpha)^{m-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\langle\epsilon(t)R(\alpha+u+it,A)^{m+1}x,y\rangle dtdu

\displaystyle=-\int_{-\infty}^{0}m\frac{(\omega-\alpha)^{m-1}}{(\omega-\alpha-u)^{m}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\langle(\omega-\alpha-u)^{m}\epsilon(t)R(\alpha+u+it,A)^{m+1}x,y\rangle dtdu.

Let
\mathcal{T}
be the set (
12
). By assumption,
\mathcal{T}
is
W^{*}\gamma
-bounded hence by
Lemma
2.14
,
the set

\displaystyle\Gamma:=\big\{\int_{-\infty}^{0}\phi(u)\int_{\mathbb{R}}

\displaystyle(\omega-\alpha-u)^{m}\epsilon(t)R(\alpha+u+it,A)^{m+1}dtdu\,:\,

\displaystyle\epsilon:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\overline{\mathbb{D}}\text{ measurable},\,\alpha<\omega,\,\phi\in L^{1}((-\infty,0)),\,\left\|\phi\right\|_{L^{1}}\leq 2\big\}

is
W^{*}\gamma
-bounded. Since

\left\|m\frac{(\omega-\alpha)^{m-1}}{(\omega-\alpha-\,\cdot)^{m}}\right\|_{L^{1}((-\infty,0))}=\frac{m}{m-1}\leq 2,

the above calculation shows that the set

\big\{(\omega-\alpha)^{m-1}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\epsilon(t)R(\alpha+it,A)^{m}dt\,:\,\epsilon:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\overline{\mathbb{D}}\text{ measurable},\,\alpha<\omega\big\}

is included in
\Gamma
, hence is
W^{*}\gamma
-bounded. Hence, by Lemma
3.5
,
the operator
A
has property
(W\gamma\text{-}GFS)_{m-1,\omega}
.
∎

We recalled that

is equivalent
to strong
m
-bounded functional calculus of type

The following theorem
provides a similar statement in the context of

Theorem 3.8
.

The following assertions are equivalent for
m\geq 1
:

(i)

A
has
(W\gamma\text{-}GFS)_{m,\omega}
;

(ii)

A
has a strong
W\gamma
-
1
-bounded functional calculus of type
\omega
;

(iii)

A
has a strong
W\gamma
-
m
-bounded functional calculus of type
\omega
.

Moreover, if
A
satisfies these conditions, then
-A
generates a
C_{0}
-semigroup of
W\gamma
-type
\omega
.

Proof.

(i)\Rightarrow(ii)
: First, by Proposition
3.7
,
A
has
(W\gamma\text{-}GFS)_{1,\omega}
.

Let
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{N}<\omega
,

\delta_{1}<\alpha_{1},\ldots,\delta_{N}<\alpha_{N}
, and
f_{1}\in\mathcal{E}(R_{\delta_{1}}),\ldots,f_{N}\in\mathcal{E}(R_{\delta_{N}})
with
\left\|f_{k}\right\|_{H^{\infty}(R_{\alpha_{k}})}\leq 1
. Let

x_{1},\ldots,x_{N}\in X
and
y_{1},\ldots,y_{N}\in X^{*}
. It follows from the proof of

[
4
, Theorem 5.6 (a)]
) that for any
k=1,\ldots,N
,

f^{\prime}_{k}(A)=\frac{-1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(\alpha_{k}+it)R(\alpha_{k}+it,A)^{2}dt.

Consequently,

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{N}|\langle(\omega-\alpha_{k})f^{\prime}_{k}(A)x_{k},y_{k}\rangle|

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{N}\big|\bigl\langle\frac{-1}{2\pi}\Bigl(\int_{\mathbb{R}}(\omega-\alpha_{k})f_{k}(\alpha_{k}+it)R(\alpha_{k}+it,A)^{2}dt\Bigr)x_{k},y_{k}\bigr\rangle\big|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2\pi}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(\omega-\alpha_{k})|f_{k}(\alpha_{k}+it)||\langle R(\alpha_{k}+it,A)^{2}x_{k},y_{k}\rangle|dt

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2\pi}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\left\|f_{k}\right\|_{H^{\infty}(R_{\alpha_{k}})}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\big|\langle(\omega-\alpha_{k})R(\alpha_{k}+it,A)^{2}x_{k},y_{k}\rangle\big|dt.

Since
\left\|f_{k}\right\|_{H^{\infty}(R_{\alpha_{k}})}\leq 1
and
A
has
(W\gamma\text{-}GFS)_{1,\omega}
,
this yields an estimate

\sum_{k=1}^{N}|\langle(\omega-\alpha_{k})f^{\prime}_{k}(A)x_{k},y_{k}\rangle|\leq C\left\|\sum_{k=1}^{N}\gamma_{k}\otimes x_{k}\right\|_{G(X)}\left\|\sum_{k=1}^{N}\gamma_{k}\otimes y_{k}\right\|_{G(X^{*})}.

According to Remark
3.3
(1),
this implies that
A
has a strong
W\gamma
-1-bounded functional calculus of type
\omega
.

(ii)\Rightarrow(iii)
: It follows from the assumption that the set

\Delta:=\underset{\alpha<\beta<\omega}{\bigcup}\big\{(\omega-\beta)g^{(m)}(A)\,:\,g\in H^{\infty}(R_{\alpha}),\,\left\|g^{(m-1)}\right\|_{H^{\infty}(R_{\beta})}\leq 1\big\}

is
W\gamma
-bounded. For any
\alpha<\beta<\omega
and
f\in H^{\infty}(R_{\alpha})

with
\left\|f\right\|_{H^{\infty}(R_{\alpha})}\leq 1
, we have

\left\|\frac{(\beta-\alpha)^{m-1}}{(m-1)!}f^{(m-1)}\right\|_{H^{\infty}(R_{\beta})}\leq 1

by
[
4
, Lemma 5.4]
. Hence

\underset{\alpha<\beta<\omega}{\bigcup}\bigg\{\frac{(\omega-\beta)(\beta-\alpha)^{m-1}}{(m-1)!}f^{(m)}(A),\;f\in H^{\infty}(R_{\alpha}),\left\|f\right\|_{H^{\infty}(R_{\alpha})}\leq 1\bigg\}\subset\Delta.

Taking
\beta=\frac{\alpha+\omega}{2}
in the above set, we obtain

\underset{\alpha<\omega}{\bigcup}\bigg\{\frac{(\omega-\alpha)^{m}}{2^{m}(m-1)!}f^{(m)}(A)\,:\,f\in H^{\infty}(R_{\alpha}),\left\|f\right\|_{H^{\infty}(R_{\alpha})}\leq 1\bigg\}\subset\Delta.

Hence the above is
W\gamma
-bounded.
Thus
A
has a strong
W\gamma
-
m
-bounded functional calculus of type
\omega
.

(iii)\Rightarrow(i)
: Let
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{N}<\omega
,

x_{1},\ldots,x_{N}\in X
and
y_{1},\ldots,y_{N}\in X^{*}
. Arguing as in
the proof of
[
4
, Theorem 5.6 (b)]
, we consider
\beta_{k}\in(\alpha_{k},w)
for
any
k=1,\ldots,N
and introduce measurable functions

\epsilon_{1},...,\epsilon_{N}:\mathbb{R}\to\overline{\mathbb{D}}
such that

|\langle R(\alpha_{k}+it,A)^{m+2}x_{k},y_{k}\rangle|=\langle R(\alpha_{k}+it,A)^{m+2}x_{k},y_{k}\rangle\epsilon_{k}(t)

for all
k=1,\ldots,N
and all
t\in\mathbb{R}
. Next for any
R>0
and any
Re(z)>\beta_{k}
, we set

\phi_{k,R}(z):=\frac{\beta_{k}-\alpha_{k}}{\pi}\int_{-R}^{R}\frac{\epsilon_{k}(t)}{(\alpha_{k}+it-z)^{2}}dt

Since
R
is finite, it is easy to show that
\phi_{k,R}(z)=O(|z|^{-2})
as
|z|\rightarrow\infty
, and hence

\phi_{k,R}\in\mathcal{E}(R_{\alpha})
. Furthermore, it follows from
the proof of
[
4
, Theorem 5.6 (b)]

that
\left\|\phi_{k,R}\right\|_{H^{\infty}(R_{\beta_{k}})}\leq 1
and

\phi_{k}^{(m)}(A)=\int_{-R}^{R}\frac{\beta_{k}-\alpha_{k}}{\pi}(m+1)!\epsilon_{k}(t)R(\alpha_{k}+it,A)^{m+2}dt.

It therefore follows from
(iii)
that we have an estimate

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{N}

\displaystyle\int_{-R}^{R}\big|\langle\frac{(\beta_{k}-\alpha_{k})(\omega-\beta_{k})^{m}}{\pi}(m+1)!R(\alpha_{k}+it,A)^{m+2}x_{k},y_{k}\rangle\big|dt

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{N}\big|\big\langle(\omega-\beta_{k})^{m}\phi_{k,R}^{(m)}(A)x_{k},y_{k}\big\rangle\big|

\displaystyle\leq C\left\|\sum_{k=1}^{N}\gamma_{k}\otimes x_{k}\right\|_{G(X)}\left\|\sum_{k=1}^{N}\gamma_{k}\otimes y_{k}\right\|_{G(X^{*})}.

Passing to the limit when
R\to\infty
, one obtains

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\big|\langle\frac{(\beta_{k}-\alpha_{k})(\omega-\beta_{k})^{m}}{\pi}(m+1)!R(\alpha_{k}+it,A)^{m+2}x_{k},y_{k}\rangle\big|dt

\displaystyle\leq C\left\|\sum_{k=1}^{N}\gamma_{k}\otimes x_{k}\right\|_{G(X)}\left\|\sum_{k=1}^{N}\gamma_{k}\otimes y_{k}\right\|_{G(X^{*})}.

Now we choose
\beta_{k}=\frac{\omega+\alpha_{k}}{2}
in the above estimate.
We obtain the following inequality

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\langle(\omega-\alpha_{k})^{m+1}R(\alpha_{k}+it,A)^{m+2}x_{k},y_{k}\rangle|dt

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2^{m+1}C\pi}{(m+1)!}\left\|\sum_{k=1}^{N}\gamma_{k}\otimes x_{k}\right\|_{G(X)}\left\|\sum_{k=1}^{N}\gamma_{k}\otimes y_{k}\right\|_{G(X^{*})}.

This shows that
A
has
(W\gamma\text{-}GFS)_{m+1,\omega}
.
Then by Proposition
3.7
,
A
has
(W\gamma\text{-}GFS)_{m,\omega}
.

Finally, assume that
(ii)
holds true.
In particular
A
has a strong
m
-1-bounded functional calculus of type
\omega
hence
by Theorem
[
4
, Theorem 6.4]
,
-A
generates a
C_{0}
-semigroup
(T_{t})_{t\geq 0}
of type
\omega
.

For any
t\geq 0
and
\alpha<\omega
, let
\phi_{\alpha,t}(z)=e^{-tz}e^{t\alpha}
for
z\in R_{\alpha}
.
Then
\left\|\phi_{\alpha,t}\right\|_{H^{\infty}(R_{\alpha})}=1
. Hence
by
(ii)
, the set

\displaystyle\underset{\alpha<\omega}{\bigcup}\big\{(\omega-\alpha)\phi^{\prime}_{\alpha,t}(A)\,:\,t\geq 0\big\}\text{ is W$\gamma$-bounded}.

Therefore the set

\displaystyle\underset{\alpha<\omega}{\bigcup}\big\{(\omega-\alpha)(-t)e^{-tA}e^{t\alpha}\,:\,t>0\big\}\text{ is $W\gamma$-bounded}.

We noticed in Proposition
2.2
that
e^{-tA}=T_{t}
for any

.
Hence taking
\alpha=\omega-\frac{1}{t}
for any

, we deduce that the set

\{e^{\omega t}T_{t}\,:\,t\geq 0\}\text{ is $W\gamma$-bounded.}

Hence
(T_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is a
C_{0}
-semigroup of
W\gamma
-type
\omega
.
∎

4.
A SHI-FENG-GOMILKO THEOREM ON
K
-CONVEX SPACES

Let
(\Omega,\mu)
be a measure space, let
N\in\mathbb{N}
and let
(e_{1},\ldots,e_{N})

denote the canonical basis of
l^{2}_{N}
. Let
l^{2}_{N}\overset{2}{\otimes}L^{2}(\Omega)

denote the Hilbert space tensor product of
l^{2}_{N}
and
L^{2}(\Omega)
. With the
notation
\mathbb{N}_{N}=\{1,\ldots,N\}
,
we have a unitary isomorphism

l^{2}_{N}\overset{2}{\otimes}L^{2}(\Omega)=L^{2}(\Omega\times\mathbb{N}_{N}).

Let
X
be a Banach space.
For any bounded operator
u:l^{2}_{N}\overset{2}{\otimes}L^{2}(\Omega)\longrightarrow X
, let

u_{k}:L^{2}(\Omega)\rightarrow X
be defined by
u_{k}(f)=u(e_{k}\otimes f)
, for any
k=1,\ldots,N
.
Then the mapping
u\mapsto\sum_{k}e_{k}\otimes u_{k}
induces an algebraic isomorphism

\mathcal{L}(l^{2}_{N}\overset{2}{\otimes}L^{2}(\Omega),X)\simeq l^{2}_{N}\otimes\mathcal{L}(L^{2}(\Omega),X).

It is easy to check that
u:l^{2}_{N}\otimes L^{2}(\Omega)\rightarrow X
belongs
to
\gamma(L^{2}(\Omega\times\mathbb{N}_{N});X)
if and only if
u_{k}
belongs to
\gamma(L^{2}(\Omega);X)

for any
k=1,\ldots,N
. This leads to an algebraic isomorphism

\gamma(L^{2}(\Omega\times\mathbb{N}_{N});X)\simeq l^{2}_{N}\otimes\gamma(L^{2}(\Omega);X).

Likewise, a function
f:\Omega\times\mathbb{N}_{N}\rightarrow X
belongs to

\gamma(\Omega\times\mathbb{N}_{N};X)
if and only if
f(\cdot,k)
belongs to
\gamma(\Omega;X)
for any
k=1,\ldots,N
.

Recall the sun dual
X^{\odot}
from Subsection
2.2
.
We now state and prove the main result of this article.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
X
be a
K
-convex Banach space and let
m\geq 1
be an integer. Let
A

be an operator of half-plane type
\omega
on
X
.
The following assertions are equivalent:

(i)

-A
generates a
C_{0}
-semigroup
(T_{t})_{t\geq 0}
of
\gamma
-type
\omega
;

(ii)

There exists a constant
C>0
such that for all
N\in\mathbb{N}
,
for all
x_{1},\ldots,x_{N}\in X
, for all
y_{1},\ldots,y_{N}\in\overline{dom(A^{*})}=X^{\odot}
, and
for all
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{N}<\omega
,
the functions

(t,k)\mapsto\sqrt{\omega-\alpha_{k}}R(\alpha_{k}+it,A)x_{k}
and

(t,k)\mapsto\sqrt{\omega-\alpha_{k}}R(\alpha_{k}+it,A^{*})y_{k}

belong to
\gamma(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{N}_{N};X)
and
\gamma(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{N}_{N};X^{*})
, respectively, and satisfy

(14)

\left\|(t,k)\mapsto\sqrt{\omega-\alpha_{k}}R(\alpha_{k}+it,A)x_{k}\right\|_{\gamma(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{N}_{N};X)}\leq C\left\|\sum_{k=1}^{N}{\gamma_{k}\otimes x_{k}}\right\|_{G(X)}

and

(15)

\left\|(t,k)\mapsto\sqrt{\omega-\alpha_{k}}R(\alpha_{k}+it,A^{*})y_{k}\right\|_{\gamma(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{N}_{N};X^{*})}\leq C\left\|\sum_{k=1}^{N}{\gamma_{k}\otimes y_{k}}\right\|_{G(X^{*})};

(iii)

A
has a strong
\gamma
-
1
-bounded functional calculus of type
\omega
;

(iv)

A
has a strong
\gamma
-
m
-bounded functional calculus of type
\omega
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, one may assume
\omega=0
.

The equivalence
(iii)\Leftrightarrow(iv)
and the
implication
(iv)\Rightarrow(i)
follow from
Theorems
3.8

and
3.10
.

(i)\Rightarrow(ii)
:
Since
\mathcal{T}:=\{T_{t}\,:\,t\geq 0\}
is
\gamma
-bounded, the strongly
measurable function
M:\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{N}_{N}\longrightarrow\mathcal{L}(X)
defined
by
M(t,k):=T_{t}
has
\gamma
-bounded range
\mathcal{T}
.
Then by Theorem
2.15
, for each
\psi\in\gamma(\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{N}_{N};X)
,
the function
M\psi
belongs to
\gamma(\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{N}_{N};X)
, with

(16)

\left\|M\psi\right\|_{\gamma(\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{N}_{N};X)}\leq\gamma(\mathcal{T})\left\|\psi\right\|_{\gamma(\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{N}_{N};X)}.

Consider
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{N}<0
and
x_{1},\ldots,x_{N}\in X
, and define

\psi(t,k)=\sqrt{-\alpha_{k}}e^{\alpha_{k}t}x_{k},\qquad t\in\mathbb{R}_{+},\ k=1,\ldots,N.

Then
\psi\in\gamma(\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{N}_{N};X)
and

(17)

\left\|\psi\right\|_{\gamma(\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{N}_{N};X)}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left\|\sum_{k=1}^{N}\gamma_{k}\otimes x_{k}\right\|_{G(X)}.

Indeed,
\psi=\sum_{k=1}^{N}h_{k}\otimes x_{k}
where
h_{k}:\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{N}_{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is defined by

h_{k}\colon(t,j)\longmapsto\begin{cases}\sqrt{-\alpha_{k}}e^{\alpha_{k}t}&\text{if }j=k\\
0&\text{if }j\neq k.\end{cases}

Further

are pairwise orthogonal with

\left\|h_{k}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{N}_{N})}=\left\|\sqrt{-\alpha_{k}}e^{\alpha_{k}t}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}
. Hence (
17
) follows from
[
10
, Example 9.2.4]
.

Recall that for each

, we have

R(\alpha_{k}+it,A)x_{k}=-\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{its}e^{\alpha_{k}s}T_{s}x_{k}ds.

Applying Lemma
2.18
, we deduce that

(t,k)\longmapsto R(\alpha_{k}+it,A)x_{k}
belongs to
\gamma(\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{N}_{N};X)
, with

(18)

\left\|(t,k)\mapsto\sqrt{-\alpha_{k}}R(\alpha_{k}+it,A)x_{k}\right\|_{\gamma(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{N}_{N},X)}=\sqrt{2\pi}\left\|(t,k)\mapsto\sqrt{-\alpha_{k}}e^{\alpha_{k}t}T_{t}x_{k}\right\|_{\gamma(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{N}_{N},X)}.

Combining (
16
), (
17
) and (
18
), we actually obtain

\left\|(t,k)\mapsto\sqrt{-\alpha_{k}}R(\alpha_{k}+it,A)x_{k}\right\|_{\gamma(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{N}_{N},X)}\leq\sqrt{\pi}\gamma(\mathcal{T})\left\|\sum_{k=1}^{N}\gamma_{k}\otimes x_{k}\right\|_{G(X)},

which proves (
14
).

Finally, since
X
is
K
-convex, the set
\{T_{t}^{*}\,:\,t\geq 0\}
is
\gamma
-bounded.
Further
(T_{t}^{\odot})_{t\geq 0}
is a
C_{0}
-semigroup on the sun dual
X^{\odot}
, with
generator equal to
-A^{\odot}
.
Then the above computations together with Theorem
2.9
lead to (
15
).

(ii)\Rightarrow(iii)
: Let
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{N}<0
,
x_{1},\ldots,x_{N}\in X
and
y_{1},\ldots,y_{N}\in X^{\odot}
.
Applying Theorem
2.16
, one obtains

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\langle-\alpha_{k}R(\alpha_{k}+it,A)^{2}x_{k},y_{k}\rangle|dt

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\langle\sqrt{-\alpha_{k}}R(\alpha_{k}+it,A)x_{k},\sqrt{-\alpha_{k}}R(\alpha_{k}+it,A)^{*}y_{k}\rangle|dt

\displaystyle=\left\|(t,k)\mapsto<\sqrt{-\alpha_{k}}R(\alpha_{k}+it,A)x_{k},\sqrt{-\alpha_{k}}R(\alpha_{k}+it,A^{*})y_{k}>\right\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{N}_{N})}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|(t,k)\mapsto\sqrt{-\alpha_{k}}R(\alpha_{k}+it,A)x_{k}\right\|_{\gamma(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{N}_{N};X)}\left\|(t,k)\mapsto\sqrt{-\alpha_{k}}R(\alpha_{k}+it,A^{*})y_{k}\right\|_{\gamma(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{N}_{N};X^{*})}

\displaystyle\leq C^{2}\left\|\sum_{k=1}^{N}{\gamma_{k}\otimes x_{k}}\right\|_{G(X)}\left\|\sum_{k=1}^{N}{\gamma_{k}\otimes y_{k}}\right\|_{G(X^{*})}.

Since
X^{\odot}
is norming in
X
, it follows from the above
estimate and Theorem
3.10
that
A
has a strong
\gamma
-
1
-bounded calculus of type
0
.
∎

Remark 4.2
.

Let
(S,\mu)
be a measure space and let
E(S)
be a
K
-convex
Banach function space over
(S,\mu)

(see
[
10
, appendix F]
for definition). Then

has finite cotype hence
according to
[

, Proposition 9.3.8]
, there exist

and

such

,

(19)

c\left\|f\right\|_{E(S;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{N}_{N}))}\leq\left\|f\right\|_{\gamma(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{N}_{N};E(S))}\leq C\left\|f\right\|_{E(S;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{N}_{N}))}.

Furthermore, the following equality holds,

\left\|f\right\|_{E(S;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{N}_{N}))}=\left\|\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\sum_{k=1}^{N}|f(\cdot,k)|^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right\|_{E(S)}.

The space

satisfies similar properties.
Hence using (

) and the Khintchine-Maurey inequality

[

, Theorem 7.2.13]
, the condition

of Theorem

can be replaced by:

(ii)^{\prime}

There exists a constant

such that for all

,
for all

, for all

, and

,

\left\|\Big(\sum_{k=1}^{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(\omega-\alpha_{k})|R(\alpha_{k}+it,A)x_{k}|^{2}dt\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right\|_{E(S)}\leq C\left\|\big(\sum_{k=1}^{N}|x_{k}|^{2}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right\|_{E(S)}

\left\|\Big(\sum_{k=1}^{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(\omega-\alpha_{k})|R(\alpha_{k}+it,A^{*})y_{k}|^{2}dt\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right\|_{E(S)^{*}}\leq C\left\|\big(\sum_{k=1}^{N}|y_{k}|^{2}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right\|_{E(S)^{*}}.

-semigroup of

-type

Of course the above applies when

.

.

5.
A GEARHART-PRÜSS THEOREM ON
K
-CONVEX SPACES

Let
A
be a half-plane type operator on some Banach space
X
.
Its abscissa of uniform boundedness
s_{0}(A)
is defined by

s_{0}(A):=\sup\big\{\alpha\in\mathbb{R}\,:\,\sigma(A)\subset R_{\alpha}\ \hbox{and}\ \underset{Re(z)\leq\alpha}{\sup}\left\|R(z,A)\right\|<\infty\big\}.

If
-A
generates a
C_{0}
-semigroup
(T_{t})_{t\geq 0}
, then the exponential growth bound
\omega(A)
if defined
as the supremum of all
\omega\in\mathbb{R}
such that
(T_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is of type
\omega
, that is,

\omega(A):=\sup\big\{\omega\in\mathbb{R}\,:\,\text{there exists $M_{\omega}>0$ such that $\left\|T_{t}\right\|\leq M_{\omega}e^{-\omega t}$ for all }t\geq 0\big\}.

We introduce
\gamma
-bounded analogues of these notions, as follows. First we set

s_{0}^{\gamma}(A):=\sup\big\{\alpha\in\mathbb{R}\,:\,\sigma(A)\subset R_{\alpha}\ \hbox{and}\ \text{the set }\{R(z,A)\,:\,Re(z)\leq\alpha\}\text{ is $\gamma$-bounded}\big\},

with the convention that
s_{0}^{\gamma}(A)=-\infty
if no set
\{R(z,A)\,:\,Re(z)\leq\alpha\}

is
\gamma
-bounded.
Second, if
-A
generates a
C_{0}
-semigroup
(T_{t})_{t\geq 0}
, and if the latter
admits a
\gamma
-type, then we set

\omega^{\gamma}(A):=\sup\big\{\omega\in\mathbb{R}\,:\,\text{the set }\{e^{\omega t}T_{t}\,:\,t\geq 0\}\text{ is $\gamma$-bounded}\big\}.

By convention we set
\omega^{\gamma}(A)=-\infty
if
(T_{t})_{t\geq 0}
has no
\gamma
-type.
See Example
3.1
for simple examples of such semigroups.

When
X
is a Hilbert space, the Gearhart-Prüss Theorem
[
1
, Theorem 5.2.1]

asserts that
\omega(A)=s_{0}(A)
. The main purpose of this section is to give
an analogous equality
\omega^{\gamma}(A)=s_{0}^{\gamma}(A)
on
K
-convex
Banach spaces.

It is obvious that
\omega^{\gamma}(A)\leq\omega(A)
and

s_{0}^{\gamma}(A)\leq s_{0}(A)
. The next inequality is more significant.

Lemma 5.1
.

Assume that
-A
generates a
C_{0}
-semigroup. Then

\omega(A)\leq s_{0}^{\gamma}(A)
.

Proof.

Let
\omega<\omega_{0}<\omega(A)
. By assumption, there exists a constant
M>0
such that

\left\|T_{s}\right\|\leq Me^{-\omega_{0}s}
for any
s\geq 0
.
Writing
e^{\omega s}T_{s}=e^{(\omega-\omega_{0})s}e^{\omega_{0}s}T_{s}
, we obtain that

s\mapsto e^{\omega s}T_{s}x
belongs to
L^{1}((0,\infty),X)
for any
x\in X
, with

(22)

\left\|s\mapsto e^{\omega s}T_{s}x\right\|_{L^{1}((0,\infty),X)}\,\leq\frac{M}{\omega-\omega_{0}}\,\left\|x\right\|.

For any
\alpha\leq\omega
and any
t\in\mathbb{R}
, we have

\displaystyle R(\alpha+it,A)x

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{its}e^{\alpha s}T(s)xds

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{its}e^{(\alpha-\omega)s}e^{\omega s}T_{s}xds.

Since
|e^{its}e^{(\alpha-\omega)s}|\leq 1
for any
s>0
,
we derive that the set

(23)

\{R(\alpha+it,A)\,:\,t\in\mathbb{R},\,\alpha\leq\omega\}

is included in the set

\Big\{\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\psi(s)e^{\omega s}T_{s}ds,\,:\,\psi\in L^{\infty}((0,\infty)),\;\left\|\psi\right\|_{\infty}\leq 1\Big\}.

By Lemma
2.12
and (
22
), the above set is

\gamma
-bounded. Therefore the set (
23
)
is
\gamma
-bounded. Hence
\omega<s_{0}^{\gamma}(A)
. Passing to the
supremum, this yields
\omega(A)\leq s_{0}^{\gamma}(A)
.
∎

Summarizing, we have

(24)

\omega^{\gamma}(A)\leq\omega(A)\leq s_{0}^{\gamma}(A)\leq s_{0}(A)

whenever
-A
generates a
C_{0}
-semigroup.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
X
be a
K
-convex Banach space. Let
-A
be the generator of a
C_{0}
-semigroup
of
\gamma
-type
\omega
on
X
.
Then
A
has a strong
\gamma
-1-bounded functional
calculus of type
s
for each
s<s_{0}^{\gamma}(A)
.

Proof.

We fix some
s<s_{0}^{\gamma}(A)
. If
s\leq\omega
, then
A
has a strong
\gamma
-1-bounded functional
calculus of type
s
by Theorem
4.1
. Thus we may now assume that
\omega<s
.

Let
N\in\mathbb{N}
, let
x_{1},\ldots,x_{N}\in X
and let
y_{1},\ldots,y_{N}\in X^{\odot}
.
According to Theorem
4.1
, an estimate (
14
) is satisfied
for any
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{N}<\omega
.
Consider
s_{1},...,s_{N}<s
and let
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{N}<\omega
be chosen such that

(25)

\omega-\alpha_{k}=s-s_{k},\qquad k\in\mathbb{N}_{N}.

By the resolvent identity, we have

R(s_{k}+it,A)x_{k}=\big(I+(\alpha_{k}-s_{k})R(s_{k}+it,A)\big)R(\alpha_{k}+it,A)x_{k}

for any
k\in\mathbb{N}_{N}
and any
t\in\mathbb{R}
.
According to (
25
), this implies that

\sqrt{s-s_{k}}R(s_{k}+it,A)x_{k}=\big(I+(\omega-s)R(s_{k}+it,A)\big)\sqrt{\omega-\alpha_{k}}R(\alpha_{k}+it,A)x_{k}.

Now define
M_{s_{1},...,s_{n}}:\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{N}_{N}\rightarrow\mathcal{L}(X)
by

M_{s_{1},...,s_{n}}(t,k):=I+(\omega-s)R(s_{k}+it,A),\quad t\in\mathbb{R},\ k\in\mathbb{N}_{N}.

The range of
M_{s_{1},...,s_{n}}
is included in the set

\{I+(\omega-s)R(\alpha+it,A),\;\alpha\leq s\},

which is independent of
s_{1},\cdots,s_{k}
. The latter set is
\gamma
-bounded, by the definition of
s_{0}^{\gamma}(A)
.
Let
K>0
denote its
\gamma
-bounded constant.
Applying Theorem
2.15
and (
14
), we obtain that

\displaystyle\left\|(t,k)\mapsto\sqrt{s-s_{k}}R(s_{k}+it,A)x_{k}\right\|_{\gamma(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{N}_{N};X)}

\displaystyle\leq K\left\|(t,k)\mapsto\sqrt{\omega-\alpha_{k}}R(\alpha_{k}+it,A)x_{k}\right\|_{\gamma(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{N}_{N};X)}

\displaystyle\leq KC\left\|\sum_{k=1}^{N}{\gamma_{k}\otimes x_{k}}\right\|_{G(X)}.

Since
X
is
K
-convex, the set
\{I+(\omega-s)R(\alpha+it,A)^{*},\;\alpha\leq s\}

is
\gamma
-bounded as well. Hence using (
15
) we obtain a similar estimate

\displaystyle\left\|(t,k)\mapsto\sqrt{s-s_{k}}R(s_{k}+it,A^{*})y_{k}\right\|_{\gamma(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{N}_{N};X^{*})}\leq K^{*}C\left\|\sum_{k=1}^{N}{\gamma_{k}\otimes y_{k}}\right\|_{G(X^{*})}.

Now applying the implication “
(ii)\Rightarrow(iii)
” of
Theorem
4.1
, we obtain the desired result.
∎

Corollary 5.3
.

Let
X
be a
K
-convex Banach space and let

-A
be the generator of a
C_{0}
-semigroup
on
X
.
If
\omega^{\gamma}(A)>-\infty
, then we have

\omega^{\gamma}(A)=\omega(A)=s_{0}^{\gamma}(A).

Proof.

If
\omega^{\gamma}(A)>-\infty
, then by
Theorem
5.2
,
A
has a strong
\gamma
-1-bounded functional calculus of type

for
any

. According to Theorem

, this implies that

(T_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is of
\gamma
-type
s
for
any
s<s_{0}^{\gamma}(A)
.
Thus
\omega^{\gamma}(A)\geq s_{0}^{\gamma}(A)
. Combining with (
24
), we obtain the result.
∎

6.
AN OVERVIEW

Let
\omega\in\mathbb{R}
and let
A
be a half-plane type operator on some Banach space
X
.
Either in
[
4
]
or in the present paper, the following six
properties are considered:

(i)

A
has a bounded
H^{\infty}
-functional calculus of type
\omega
.

(ii)

A
has a strong
1
-bounded functional calculus of type
\omega
.

(iii)

-A
generates a
C_{0}
-semigroup of type
\omega
.

(i
)_{\gamma}

A
has a
\gamma
-bounded
H^{\infty}
-functional calculus of type
\omega
.

(ii
)_{\gamma}

A
has a strong
\gamma
-
1
-bounded functional calculus of type
\omega
.

(iii
)_{\gamma}

-A
generates a
C_{0}
-semigroup of
\gamma
-type
\omega
.

The aim of this last section is to give an overview of the relations between these properties,
at least on
K
-convex spaces.
This will require the analysis of a specific example, see Proposition
6.1

below. In the above list, we have deliberately omitted
the strong
m
-bounded and
\gamma
-
m
-bounded functional calculi.

It follows from Proposition
2.8
and
[
4
, Theorem 6.4]
that

(i)\Rightarrow(ii)\Rightarrow(iii).

Likewise it follows from Remark
3.3
(3) and Remark
3.9
that

(i)_{\gamma}\Rightarrow(ii)_{\gamma}\Rightarrow(iii)_{\gamma}.

The implication “
(ii)\Rightarrow(i)
” is wrong. Indeed it follows from either

[
3
]
or
[
15
]
that on any infinite dimensional Hilbert space

H
, there exists a bounded
C_{0}
-semigroup
(T_{t})_{t\geq 0}
on
H
whose
negative generator
A
does not have a bounded
H^{\infty}
-functional calculus of type
0
.
Thus with
\omega=0
,
A
satisfies (iii) and does not satisfy (i). Moreover
(ii) and (iii) are equivalent on Hilbert space, by
[
4
, Theorem 7.1]
.
This proves the result.

Since
\gamma
-boundedness and uniform boundedness are equivalent on Hilbert space,
the above also shows that the implication “
(ii)_{\gamma}\Rightarrow(i)_{\gamma}
” is wrong.

The implication “
(iii)\Rightarrow(ii)
” is wrong. Indeed let
1<p\neq 2<+\infty
and
let
(T_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
be the right translation group on
L^{p}(\mathbb{R})
, which is a bounded
C_{0}
-group.
Let
-A
denote its generator. It follows from Gomilko’s paper
[
5
]
that either

A
or
-A
does not have a strong
1
-bounded functional calculus of type
0
.

We have shown in Theorem
4.1

that if
X
is
K
-convex, then the implication “
(iii)_{\gamma}\Rightarrow(ii)_{\gamma}
”
holds true. We do not know whether “
(iii)_{\gamma}\Rightarrow(ii)_{\gamma}
” holds true on any Banach space.

The implication “
(iii)_{\gamma}\Rightarrow(ii)
” holds true, by
[
8
, Theorem 6.4]
(see
Remark
4.3
for more on this.)

We noticed above that
(iii)
does not imply
(i)
on Hilbert space. Consequently,
The implication “
(iii)_{\gamma}\Rightarrow(i)
” is wrong.

The only remaining question is whether
(i)
implies
(iii)_{\gamma}
. We are going to show that
this is wrong on sufficently bad spaces, see Example
6.2
below.

For this purpose we introduce a class of
C_{0}
-(semi)groups of independent interest.
Recall the Gaussian space
G(X)
from Subsection 2.3. We will use the
so-called ‘contraction principle’
[
10
, Theorem 6.1.13]
, which says
that for any
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\in X
and any
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n}\in\mathbb{C}
, we have

(26)

\left\|\sum_{k=1}^{n}\alpha_{k}\gamma_{k}\otimes x_{k}\right\|_{G(X)}\leq\underset{k}{\sup}|\alpha_{k}|\left\|\sum_{k=1}^{n}\gamma_{k}\otimes x_{k}\right\|_{G(X)}.

We recall that
X
has property
(\alpha)
(see
[
10
, section 7.5]
for more details)
if there exists a constant
C\geq 1
such that for any finite family

(x_{ij})
in
X
and any finite family
(t_{ij})
in
\mathbb{C}
, we have

(27)

\left\|\sum_{i,j}\gamma_{i}\otimes\gamma_{j}\otimes t_{ij}x_{ij}\right\|_{G(G(X))}\leq C\underset{i,j}{\sup}|t_{ij}|\left\|\sum_{i,j}\gamma_{i}\otimes\gamma_{j}\otimes x_{ij}\right\|_{G(G(X))}.

Banach spaces with property
(\alpha)
have a finite cotype, thus

\mathcal{R}
-boundedness and
\gamma
-boundedness are equivalent on such spaces.
We recall that the class of all Banach spaces with property
(\alpha)

is stable under taking subspaces and that
all Banach lattices with a finite cotype have property
(\alpha)
.
In particular, for any
1\leq p<\infty
,
L^{p}
-spaces
and their subspaces have property
(\alpha)
.

Let
(\xi_{k})_{k\geq 1}
be a sequence of distinct points of
\mathbb{R}
.
For any finite Gaussian sum
\sum_{k=1}^{n}\gamma_{k}\otimes x_{k}
, with
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\in X
, we let

(28)

T_{t}\Bigl(\sum_{k=1}^{n}\gamma_{k}\otimes x_{k}\Bigr):=\sum_{k=1}^{n}e^{-it\xi_{k}}\gamma_{k}\otimes x_{k},\quad t\in\mathbb{R}.

Then we have

\left\|T_{t}\Bigl(\sum_{k=1}^{n}\gamma_{k}\otimes x_{k}\Bigr)\right\|\leq\left\|\sum_{k=1}^{n}\gamma_{k}\otimes x_{k}\right\|,

by (
26
).
Since the finite Gaussian sums are dense in
G(X)
, each
T_{t}
extends to a bounded
linear operator on
G(X)
(still denoted by
T_{t}
), with
\left\|T_{t}\right\|=1
.
Furthermore
(T_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
is a
C_{0}
-group.
Indeed it is plain that for any finite Gaussian sum
z=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\gamma_{k}\otimes x_{k}
,
T_{t}(z)\to z
when
t\to 0
.
Then the strong continuity of
(T_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
follows from the
uniform boundedness of
(T_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
and the density of the set of all
finite Gaussian sums in
G(X)
.

Proposition 6.1
.

Let
-A
denote the generator of the
C_{0}
-group
(T_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
defined by (
28
).

(1)

A
has a bounded
H^{\infty}
-functional calculus of type
0
.

(2)

The
C_{0}
-group
(T_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
is
\gamma
-bounded if and only if
X
has property
(\alpha)
.

Proof.

For any
b\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
, we let
\int_{\mathbb{R}}b(t)T_{t}dt\,\in\mathcal{L}(G(X))
denote the operator defined by

\bigl(\int_{\mathbb{R}}b(t)T_{t}dt\bigr)(z)\,=\,\int_{0}^{\infty}b(t)T_{t}(z)dt,\quad z\in G(X).

If
b\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
, we let
\mathcal{L}b
denote the Laplace transform of
b
, that is,

\mathcal{L}b(z)=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-zt}b(t)dt,\;z\in\overline{R_{0}}.

Obviously
\mathcal{L}b
is continuous and bounded on
\overline{R_{0}}
and
its restriction to
R_{0}
belongs to
H^{\infty}(R_{0})
.

For any
b\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
and any
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\in X
, we have

(29)

\big(\int_{\mathbb{R}}b(t)T_{t}dt\big)(\sum_{k=1}^{n}\gamma_{k}\otimes x_{k})=\int_{\mathbb{R}}b(t)\sum_{k=1}^{n}e^{-i\xi_{k}t}\gamma_{k}\otimes x_{k}dt=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\widehat{b}(\xi_{k})\gamma_{k}\otimes x_{k}.

If
b\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
, this implies, using
(
26
), that

(30)

\left\|\int_{0}^{\infty}b(t)T_{t}dt\right\|_{\mathcal{L}(G(X))}\leq\left\|\mathcal{L}b\right\|_{H^{\infty}(R_{0})}.

Now let
\alpha<0
and let
f\in\mathcal{E}(R_{\alpha})
. According to

[
7
, Lemma 5.1]
, there is a (necessarily unique)

b\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
such that
f=\mathcal{L}b
on
R_{0}
and

f(A)=\int_{0}^{\infty}b(t)T_{t}dt.

The estimate (
30
) therefore implies that

\left\|f(A)\right\|\leq\left\|\mathcal{L}b\right\|_{H^{\infty}(R_{0})}=\left\|f\right\|_{H^{\infty}(R_{0})}.

This shows (1).

We now turn to the proof of (2). First assume that

X
has property
(\alpha)
. Let
(t_{j})_{j}
be a finite family of real numbers and
for any
j
, let
z_{j}=\sum_{k}\gamma_{j}\otimes x_{jk}
be a finite Gaussian sum.
We have

\sum_{j}\gamma_{j}\otimes T_{t_{j}}(z_{j})\,=\,\sum_{i,j}e^{-it_{j}\xi_{k}}\gamma_{j}\otimes\gamma_{k}\otimes x_{jk}.

Applying (
27
) we deduce that

\left\|\sum_{j}\gamma_{j}\otimes T_{t_{j}}(z_{j})\right\|_{G(G(X))}\,\leq\,C\left\|\sum_{i,j}\gamma_{j}\otimes\gamma_{k}\otimes x_{jk}\right\|_{G(G(X))}\,=\,C\left\|\sum_{j}\gamma_{j}\otimes z_{j}\right\|_{G(G(X))}.

Since the set of all finite Gaussian sums is dense in
G(X)
, this shows that

(T_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
is
\gamma
-bounded.

Assume on the contrary that
X
does not have property
(\alpha)
. By (
26
), there exists a
(necessarily unique) contractive,
non degenerate, homomorphism

w:C_{0}(\mathbb{R})\rightarrow\mathcal{L}(G(X))

such that

w(f)(\sum_{k=1}^{n}\gamma_{k}\otimes x_{k})=\sum_{k=1}^{n}f(\xi_{k})\gamma_{k}\otimes x_{k}

for any
n\geq 1
and any
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\in X
. We claim that
the set

S=\bigl\{w(f)\,:\,f\in C_{0}(\mathbb{R}),\ \left\|f\right\|_{\infty}\leq 1\bigr\}

is not
\gamma
-bounded.
Indeed let
(t_{ik})
and
(x_{ik})
be finite families in

\mathbb{C}
and
X
, respectively, and assume that
|t_{ik}|\leq 1

for any
i,k
. There exist
f_{i}\in C_{0}(\mathbb{R})
such that
f_{i}(\xi_{k})=t_{ik}

and
\left\|f_{i}\right\|_{\infty}\leq 1
for any
i,k
. Then

\sum_{i,k}\gamma_{i}\otimes\gamma_{k}\otimes t_{ik}x_{ik}\,=\,\sum_{i}\gamma_{i}\otimes w(f_{i})\Bigl(\sum_{k}\gamma_{k}\otimes x_{ik}\Bigr).

If

S
were
\gamma
-bounded, this would imply that the norm of the left hand side
is dominated by the norm of
\sum_{i,k}\gamma_{i}\otimes\gamma_{k}\otimes x_{ik}
,
which would imply property
(\alpha)
.

Note that the homomorphismm
w
‘extends’
(T_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
in the sense
of
[
13
, Definition 2.4]
. Indeed, according to (
29
), we have

\int_{\mathbb{R}}b(t)T_{t}dt=w(\hat{b})

for any
b\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
. Therefore
if
(T_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
were
\gamma
-bounded, then according to
[
13
, Theorem 4.4]
,
the above set
S
would be
\gamma
-bounded. We just noticed
that this does not hold true. Hence,

(T_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
is not
\gamma
-bounded.
∎

Example 6.2
.

Proposition

6.1
provides an example of an operator which satisfies
(i)
without satisfying

(iii)_{\gamma}
, for
\omega=0
.
Indeed, assume that
X
does not have property
(\alpha)

and let
(T_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
(with generator
-A
)
be given by the above proposition. Changing
T_{t}
into
T_{-t}
, part (1) of Proposition

6.1
shows that
A
and
-A
have a bounded

H^{\infty}
-bounded functional calculus of type
0
. However by part (2) of Proposition

6.1
, either
(T_{t})_{t\geq 0}
or
(T_{-t})_{t\geq 0}
is not
\gamma
-bounded.

We do not know if
(i)
implies
(iii)_{\gamma}
on non Hilbertian Banach spaces with property
(\alpha)
.
In particular, we do not know if
(i)
implies
(iii)_{\gamma}
on
L^{p}
-spaces, for
1<p\not=2<\infty
.

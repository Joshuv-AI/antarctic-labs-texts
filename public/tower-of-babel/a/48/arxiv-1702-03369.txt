Abstract
Let G G be some generalized π \pi -soluble groups and ℱ {\cal F} be a Fitting set of G G . In this paper, we prove the existence and conjugacy of ℱ {\cal F} -injectors of G G , and give a description of the structure of the injectors.

Introduction

Throughout this paper, all groups are finite and
p
is a prime.
G
always denotes a group,
|G|
is the order of
G
,
\sigma(G)
is the set of all primes dividing
|G|
,
\pi
denotes a set of some primes. Let
\mathbb{P}
be the set of all primes and
\pi^{\prime}=\mathbb{P}\setminus\pi
. For any set
{\cal X}
of subgroups of
G
, we let
\sigma({\cal X})=\bigcup_{G\in{\cal X}}\sigma(G)
.

Recall that a class
\mathfrak{F}
of groups is called a Fitting class if
\mathfrak{F}
is closed under taking normal subgroups and products of normal
\mathfrak{F}
-subgroups.
As usual, we denote by
\mathfrak{E},\mathfrak{S},\mathfrak{N}
the classes of all groups, all soluble groups, all nilpotent groups, respectively;
\mathfrak{E}_{\pi},\mathfrak{S}_{\pi},\mathfrak{N}_{\pi}
denote the classes of all
\pi
-groups, all soluble
\pi
-groups, all nilpotent
\pi
-groups, respectively; and
\mathfrak{S}^{\pi}
and
\mathfrak{N}^{\pi}
to denote the class of all
\pi
-soluble groups and the class of all
\pi
-nilpotent groups, respectively. It is well known that all the above classes are Fitting classes.

Following Anderson
[
1
]
(see also
[
2
, VIII, (2.1)]
), a nonempty set
\mathcal{F}
of subgroups of
G
is called a Fitting set of
G

if the following three conditions are satisfied: i) If
T\unlhd S\in\mathcal{F}
, then
T\in\mathcal{F}
; ii) If
S,\ T\in\mathcal{F}
and
S,\ T\unlhd ST
, then
ST\in\mathcal{F}
; iii) If
\ S\in\mathcal{F}
and
x\in G
, then
S^{x}\in\mathcal{F}
.

For a set
{\cal X}
of subgroups of
G
, the join
G_{\cal X}
of all normal
{\cal X}
-subgroups of
G
is called the
{\cal X}
-radical of
G
. From the definitions of Fitting class
\mathfrak{F}
and Fitting set
{\cal F}
of
G
, for each nonempty Fitting class
\mathfrak{F}
(nonempty Fitting set
{\cal F}
of
G
, resp.) of
G
,
G_{\mathfrak{F}}
(
G_{\cal F}
, resp.) is the unique maximal
normal
\mathfrak{F}
-subgroup (
{\cal F}
-subgroup resp.) of
G
. In particular,
G_{\mathfrak{N}}=F(G)
is the Fitting subgroup of
G
.

It is well known that the theory of Fitting classes play an important role in the theory of groups. The importance of the theory of Fitting classes can firstly be seen in the following theorem proved by Fischer, Gasch
\ddot{u}
tz and Harley
[
3
]
:
For every Fitting class
\mathfrak{F}
, every soluble group possesses exactly one conjugacy class of
\mathfrak{F}
-injectors.
This
result is in fact a graceful generalization of the classical Sylow theorem and Hall theorem (The Hall Theorem shown that every soluble group possesses a Hall
\pi
-subgroup and any two Hall
\pi
-subgroups are conjugated in
G
for any
\pi\subseteq\sigma(G)
).

Recall that, for any class
\mathfrak{F}
of groups, a subgroup
V
of
G
is said to be
\mathfrak{F}
-maximal if
V\in\mathfrak{F}
and
U=V
whenever
V\leq U\leq G
and
U\in\mathfrak{F}
.
A subgroup

of a group

is said to be an
\mathfrak{F}
-injector of

is an

\mathfrak{F}
-maximal subgroup of

for every subnormal subgroup

of
G
. For a Fitting set
{\cal F}
of
G
, the
{\cal F}
-injector of
G
is similarly defined (see
[
2
, VIII, (2.5)]
).
Clearly, every
\mathfrak{F}
-injector (resp.
{\cal F}
-injector) of
G
is an
\mathfrak{F}
-maximal (resp.
{\cal F}
-maximal) subgroup of
G
.
Note that if
\mathfrak{F}=\mathfrak{N}_{p}
is the Fitting class of all
p
-groups, then the
\mathfrak{F}
-injectors of a group
G
are Sylow
p
-subgroups of
G
; if
\mathfrak{F}=\mathfrak{E}_{\pi}
and
G
has a Hall
\pi
-soluble group, then the
\mathfrak{F}
-injectors of

are Hall
\pi
-subgroups of

(see
[
4

[
5
, p.328]

As a development of the theorem of Fischer, Gasch
\ddot{u}
tz and Harley
[
3
]

6

If

is a Fitting class, then the set

is a Fitting set, which is denoted by

and called the trace of

in

, the

-injectors and

-injectors of

[
2

, not every Fitting set of
G
is the trace of a Fitting class. Hence, in view of the theorem of Shemetkov in
[
6
]
and the theorem of Fischer, Gasch
\ddot{u}
tz and Harley
[
3
]
, the following question naturally arise:

Problem 1.1.
For an arbitrary Fitting set
{\cal F}
of a non-soluble group
G
(in particular, some generalized
\pi
-soluble groups
G
, for example, the groups in Theorem A below), whether
G
possesses an
{\cal F}
-injector and any two
{\cal F}
-injectors are conjugate ?

It is clear that for a Fitting class
\mathfrak{F}
, every
\mathfrak{F}
-injector of
G
contains the
\mathfrak{F}
-radical
G_{\mathfrak{F}}
of
G
. In
[
7
]
Fischer gave the characterization the
\mathfrak{N}
-injectors by means of the nilpotent radical
G_{\mathfrak{N}}=F(G)
. He proved that the set of
\mathfrak{N}
-injectors is exactly the set of all maximal nilpotent subgroups of
G
containing
F(G).

The product
\mathfrak{FH}
of two Fitting classes
\mathfrak{F}
and

\mathfrak{H}
is the class
(G\mid G/G_{\mathfrak{F}}\in\mathfrak{H}).
It is
well known that the product of any two Fitting classes is also a
Fitting class and the multiplication of Fitting classes satisfies
associative law (see
[
2
, IX, (1.12)(a)(c)]
).

Hartley
[
8
]
proved that, for the Fitting class of type
\mathfrak{XN}
(where
\mathfrak{X}
is a nonempty Fitting class), a subgroup
V
of a soluble group
G
is an
\mathfrak{XN}
-injecotr of
G
if and only if
V/G_{\mathfrak{X}}
is a nilpotent subgroup of
G
. As a further improvement, the authors in
[
9
]
proved that for a Hartley class
\mathfrak{H}
, a subgroup
V
of soluble group
G
is
\mathfrak{H}
-injector of
G
if and only if
V/G_{h}
is a
D
-injector of
G/G_{h}
, where
D=\bigcap_{i\in I}\mathfrak{S}_{\pi^{\prime}}\mathfrak{S}_{\pi}
and
G_{h}=\prod_{i\in I}G_{h(\pi_{i})}
. Moreover, in
[
9
]
, it was proved that the set of
\mathfrak{H}
-injectors coincide with the set of of all
\mathfrak{H}
-maximal subgroups containing the
\mathfrak{H}
-radical of
G
.

Following
[
5
, §7.2]
, a Fitting set
{\cal F}
is said to be injective if every group
G
possesses at least one
{\cal F}
-injector.

In connection with above, the following more general question naturally arise.

Problem 1.2.
Let
{\cal F}
be a injective Fitting set of
G
in some universe. What is the structure of the
{\cal F}
-injector?

In order to resolve the Problems 1.1 and 1.2, we need to develop and extend the local method of Hartley
[
8
]
(which is for Fitting classes and in the universe of soluble groups) to for Fitting sets and in a more general universe (not necessary in the universe of soluble groups).

Firstly, for a Fitting set
{\cal F}
of
G
and a Fitting class
\mathfrak{X}
, we call the set
\{H\leq G|H/H_{\cal F}\in\mathfrak{X}\}
of subgroups of
G
the product of
{\cal F}
and
\mathfrak{X}
and denote it by
{\cal F}\circ\mathfrak{X}.

Following
[
10
]
, a function
f
:
\mathbb{P}\longrightarrow\{\rm{Fitting\ sets\ of\ G}\}
is called a Hartley function (or in brevity, H-function).

Definition 1.3.
Let
\emptyset\neq\pi\subseteq\mathbb{P}
and

SLR(f)=\bigcap_{p\in\pi}f(p)\circ\mathfrak{E}_{p^{\prime}}.
A Fitting set
{\cal F}
of
G
called:
\pi
-semilocal
if
{\cal F}=SLR(f)
for some
H
-function
f
;
\pi
-local

if
{\cal F}=SLR(f)
for some
H
-function
f
such that
f(p)\circ\mathfrak{N}_{p}=f(p)
for all
p\in\pi
. In this case when
{\cal F}=SLR(f)
,
{\cal F}
is said to be defined by the H-function
f
or
f
is an H-function of
{\cal F}
.

In particular, if
\pi=\mathbb{P}
, then a
\pi
-semilocal Fitting set (a
\pi
-local Fitting set, respectively) is said to be a semilocal Fitting set (a local Fitting set, respectively).

Definition 1.4.
Let
{\cal F}=SLR(f)
be a
\pi
-semilocal Fitting set of
G
. Then
f
is said to be

1) integrated if
f(p)\subseteq{\cal F}
for all
p\in\pi
;

2) full if
f(p)=f(p)\circ\mathfrak{N}_{p}
for all
p\in\pi
;

3) invariable if
f(p)=f(q)
for all
p,q\in\pi
.

For a
\pi
-semilocal Fitting set

of
G
, the subgroup
G_{f}=\Pi_{p\in\pi}G_{f(p)}
is said the

-radical of

.

The following two theorems resolved the Problems 1.1 and 1.2 for some generalized
\pi
-soluble groups and some
\pi
-semilocal Fitting sets of

.

Theorem A.
Let
{\cal F}
be a Fitting set of
G
. Then
G
possesses an
{\cal F}
-injector and any two
{\cal F}
-injectors are conjugate if one of the following conditions:

(1)
G\in{\cal F}\circ\mathfrak{S}^{\pi}
, where
\pi=\sigma({\cal F});

(2)
{\cal F}
is
\pi
-semilocal and
G\in\mathfrak{S}^{\pi};

(3)
{\cal F}
is
\pi
-semilocal and
G\in{\cal F}\circ\mathfrak{S}^{\pi}
, where
\sigma(G_{\cal F})\subseteq\pi
.

Moreover, the index of every
{\cal F}
-injector in
G
is a
\pi
-number in the case (2).

Theorem B.
Let
{\cal F}
be a
\pi
-semilocal Fitting set of
G
defined by a full and invariable H-function
f
and
G_{f}
the
f
-radical of
G
.
If

is

-soluble, then the following statements hold:

(1) A subgroup

of

-injector of

if and only if

is a Hall

-subgroup of

(2)

{\cal F}
-injector and any two

-injectors are conjugate in

;

(3) A subgroup

of

is an
{\cal F}
-injector of

if and only if

is an
{\cal F}
-maximal subgroup of

(4) If

-injector of

-subgroup of

3
Properties of the product of a Fitting set and a Fitting class

In this section, we discuss the properties of the product of a Fitting set and a Fitting class, which are also useful in the proof of Theorems A and B.

Proposition 3.1.
Support that
{\cal F}
is a Fitting set of
G
and
\mathfrak{H}
is a nonempty Fitting class. Then
{\cal F}\circ\mathfrak{H}
is a Fitting set of
G
.

Proof.
Let
{\cal M}={\cal F}\circ\mathfrak{H}
. Assume that
H\leq G
,
K\unlhd H\in{\cal M}.
Then
H/H_{{\cal F}}\in\mathfrak{H}
. By Lemma 2.1,
K_{{\cal F}}=K\cap H_{{\cal F}}.
Hence
K/K_{{\cal F}}\simeq KH_{{\cal F}}/H_{{\cal F}}\unlhd H/H_{{\cal F}}\in\mathfrak{H}
. Consequently,
K/K_{{\cal F}}\in\mathfrak{H}
, and so
K\in{\cal M}.

Now assume that
H,L
are subgroup of
G
,
H,L\in{\cal M}
and
H,L\unlhd HL
. Then
H/H_{\cal F}\in\mathfrak{H}
. By Lemma 2.1,
H_{\cal F}=(HL)_{\cal F}\cap H
. Similarly,
L_{\cal F}=(HL)_{\cal F}\cap L
. By the isomorphism

H/H_{\cal F}\simeq H(HL)_{\cal F}/(HL)_{\cal F}
and
L/L_{\cal F}\simeq L(HL)_{\cal F}/(HL)_{\cal F}
, we have that

HL/(HL)_{\cal F}\in\mathfrak{H}.
Hence
HL\in{\cal M}.

Final assume that
H\leq G
and
H\in{\cal M}.
Then
\bar{H}=H/H_{{\cal F}}\in\mathfrak{H}
and
\bar{H}=(\bar{H})^{x}=H^{x}/(H_{{\cal F}})^{x}.
In order to prove that
H^{x}\in{\cal M}
for all
x\in G
, it only need to prove that
(H^{x})_{{\cal F}}=(H_{{\cal F}})^{x}.

In fact, since
H_{{\cal F}}\in{\cal F}
and
{\cal F}
is a Fitting set of
G
,
(H_{{\cal F}})^{x}\in{\cal F}
. Moreover,
(H_{{\cal F}})^{x}\unlhd H^{x}
, so
(H_{{\cal F}})^{x}\leq(H^{x})_{{\cal F}}.
On the other hand,
(H^{x})_{{\cal F}}\unlhd H^{x}
, so
((H^{x})_{{\cal F}})^{x^{-1}}\unlhd H
. But as
{\cal F}
is a Fitting set of
G
and
(H^{x})_{{\cal F}}\in{\cal F},
we have that
((H^{x})_{{\cal F}})^{x^{-1}}\in{\cal F}
, and so
((H^{x})_{{\cal F}})^{x^{-1}}\leq H_{{\cal F}}.
It follows that
(H^{x})_{{\cal F}}=(H_{{\cal F}})^{x}.

The above shows that
{\cal F}\circ\mathfrak{H}
is a Fitting set of
G
. The proposition is proved.

Proposition 3.2.
Let
H\leq G
and
{\cal F}
be a Fitting set of
G
, and
\mathfrak{H}
a nonempty Fitting class. Then:

(1)
{\cal F}\subseteq{\cal F}\circ\mathfrak{H}.

(2)
H_{{\cal F}\circ\mathfrak{H}}/H_{{\cal F}}=(H/H_{{\cal F}})_{\mathfrak{H}}.

Proof.
(1) Assume that
H\in{\cal F}
. Then
H=H_{{\cal F}},
so
H/H_{{\cal F}}=1\in\mathfrak{H}
. It follows that
H\in{\cal F}\circ\mathfrak{H}.
Hence (1) holds.

(2) Since
{\cal F}\subseteq{\cal F}\circ\mathfrak{H}
by (1),
H_{{\cal F}}\leq H_{{\cal F}\circ\mathfrak{H}}\leq H.
Then by Lemma 2.1(1),
H_{{\cal F}}=H_{{\cal F}\circ\mathfrak{H}}\cap H_{\cal F}=(H_{{\cal F}\circ\mathfrak{H}})_{{\cal F}}.

As
H_{{\cal F}\circ\mathfrak{H}}\in{\cal F}\circ\mathfrak{H},

H_{{\cal F}\circ\mathfrak{H}}/(H_{{\cal F}\circ\mathfrak{H}})_{{\cal F}}\in\mathfrak{H}.
Hence
H_{{\cal F}\circ\mathfrak{H}}/H_{{\cal F}}\leq(H/H_{{\cal F}})_{\mathfrak{H}}.

Now let
L/H_{{\cal F}}=(H/H_{{\cal F}})_{\mathfrak{H}}.

Then
L/H_{{\cal F}}\unlhd H/H_{{\cal F}},
so
L\unlhd H
. By Lemma 2.1,
L_{{\cal F}}=L\cap H_{{\cal F}}=H_{{\cal F}}.
Hence
L/H_{{\cal F}}=L/L_{{\cal F}}\in\mathfrak{H}
. Consequently,
L\in{\cal F}\circ\mathfrak{H}.
But as
L\unlhd H
, we obtain that
L\leq H_{{\cal F}\circ\mathfrak{H}}.
Thus
L/H_{{\cal F}}\leq H_{{\cal F}\circ\mathfrak{H}}/H_{{\cal F}}.

Proposition 3.3.
Let
{\cal F}
be a Fitting set of
G
,
H\leq G
and
\mathfrak{M,H}
be nonempty Fitting classes. Then

({\cal F}\circ\mathfrak{M})\circ\mathfrak{H}={\cal F}\circ\mathfrak{MH}.

Proof.
Let
H\in({\cal F}\circ\mathfrak{M})\circ\mathfrak{H}
. Then
H/H_{{\cal F}\circ\mathfrak{M}}\in\mathfrak{H}.
Hence by Proposition 3.2,

H/H_{{\cal F}\circ\mathfrak{M}}\simeq(H/H_{{\cal F}})/(H_{{\cal F}\circ\mathfrak{M}}/H_{{\cal F}})=(H/H_{{\cal F}})/(H/H_{{\cal F}})_{\mathfrak{M}}\in\mathfrak{H}.

Hence
H/H_{{\cal F}}\in\mathfrak{MH}
, and so
H\in{\cal F}\circ(\mathfrak{MH}).
This implies that
({\cal F}\circ\mathfrak{M})\circ\mathfrak{H}\subseteq{\cal F}\circ\mathfrak{MH}.

Since the above derivation process is reversible, we have also that
{\cal F}\circ\mathfrak{MH}\subseteq({\cal F}\circ\mathfrak{M})\circ\mathfrak{H}.
Thus the proposition holds.

Recall that a class
\mathfrak{X}
of groups is called a formation if it is closed under taking homomorphic images and subdirect products. Clearly, for a nonempty formation, every group
G
has the least normal subgroup
G^{\mathfrak{X}}
such that
G/G^{\mathfrak{X}}\in\mathfrak{X}.
A Fitting formation is a class of groups which is both a formation and a Fitting class. A Fitting class which is closed under homomorphic image is called a radical homomorph (see, for example,
[
16
, p. 2 and p. 324]
).

Proposition 3.4.
Let
{\cal F,H}
be two Fitting sets of
G
. Then

(1) If
\mathfrak{M}
is a nonempty radical homomorph and
{\cal F}\subseteq{\cal H},
then
{\cal F}\circ\mathfrak{M}\subseteq{\cal H}\circ\mathfrak{M}.

(2) If
\mathfrak{M}
is a nonempty Fitting formation, then
({\cal F}\cap{\cal H})\circ\mathfrak{M}=({\cal F}\circ\mathfrak{M})\cap({\cal H}\circ\mathfrak{M}).

(3) If
\mathfrak{M}
and
\mathfrak{H}
are nonempty Fitting classes, then
{\cal F}\circ(\mathfrak{M}\cap\mathfrak{H})=({\cal F}\circ\mathfrak{M})\cap({\cal F}\circ\mathfrak{H}).

Proof.
The proof is similar to the proof of
[
17
, Lemma 4]
.

. Therefore by Lemma 2.2(3), we obtain that

.

Now assume that

,

-injector of

Thus the statement (1) of Theorem B holds.

(2) Since

Corollary 6.1.
The
\pi
-closed injectors of a
\pi
-soluble group
G
are just the subgroups of the type
G_{\pi^{\prime}}O_{\pi}(G)
. Hence the
\pi
-closed injectors are the maximal
\pi
-closed subgroups of
G
containing the
\pi^{\prime}
-closed radical
G_{\pi^{\prime}}
of
G
.

Proof.
Let
\mathfrak{F}
be the class of all
\pi
-closed groups, that is,
\mathfrak{F}=\mathfrak{E}_{\pi}\mathfrak{E}_{\pi^{\prime}}
. Since
G
is
\pi
-soluble, every Hall
\pi
-subgroup of
G
is soluble. Let
{\cal F}=Tr_{\mathfrak{E}_{\pi}\mathfrak{E}_{\pi^{\prime}}}(G)
. Then
{\cal F}=Tr_{\mathfrak{S}_{\pi}\mathfrak{E}_{\pi^{\prime}}}(G)
. If we can prove that
{\cal F}
is a
\pi
-semilocal Fitting set of
G
defined by a full and invariable H-function
f
, then by Theorem B(4), every
{\cal F}
-injector of
G
is of the type
G_{\pi^{\prime}}G_{f}
, where
G_{\pi^{\prime}}
is some Hall
\pi^{\prime}
-subgroup of
G
.

Let
{\cal X}=Tr_{\mathfrak{S}_{\pi}}(G)
and
f(p)={\cal X}
for all
p\in\pi
. Then
f
is invariable.
We now prove that
f
is a full H-function of
{\cal F}
.
Clearly,
{\cal X}\subseteq{\cal X}\circ\mathfrak{N}_{p}
for any
p\in\pi
. Let
H\leq G
and
H\in{\cal X}\circ\mathfrak{N}_{p}
. Then
H/H_{\cal X}\in\mathfrak{N}_{p}\subseteq\mathfrak{S}_{\pi}
and
H_{\cal X}\in{\cal X}\subseteq\mathfrak{S}_{\pi}.
Hence
H\in\mathfrak{S}_{\pi}
, so
H\in{\cal X}.
Thus
{\cal X}\circ\mathfrak{N}_{p}={\cal X}
, that is,
f
is a full H-function.

We now prove that
{\cal F}=SLR(f)=\bigcap_{p\in\pi}{\cal X}\circ\mathfrak{E}_{p^{\prime}}={\cal X}\circ\mathfrak{E}_{\pi^{\prime}}
.

If
H\in{\cal F}
, then
H\in\mathfrak{S}_{\pi}\mathfrak{E}_{\pi^{\prime}}
and so
H/H_{\mathfrak{S}_{\pi}}\in\mathfrak{E}_{\pi^{\prime}}.
Since
H_{\mathfrak{S}_{\pi}}=H_{\cal X}
,
H\in{\cal X}\circ\mathfrak{E}_{\pi^{\prime}}
.
This shows that
{\cal F}\subseteq{\cal X}\circ\mathfrak{E}_{\pi^{\prime}}.
Conversely, assume that
L\leq G
and
L\in{\cal X}\circ\mathfrak{E}_{\pi^{\prime}}.
Then
L/L_{\cal X}\in\mathfrak{E}_{\pi^{\prime}}.

As
L_{\cal X}=L_{\mathfrak{S}_{\pi}},
we have that
L\in\mathfrak{S}_{\pi}\mathfrak{E}_{\pi^{\prime}}.
Hence
L\in{\cal F}
. This shows that
{\cal F}={\cal X}\circ\mathfrak{E}_{\pi^{\prime}}.
This completes the proof.

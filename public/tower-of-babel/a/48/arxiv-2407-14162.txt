Isotropic cosmic birefringence from an oscillating axion-like field Preprint: TU-1241
Kai Murai

Abstract
We propose a new mechanism for isotropic cosmic birefringence with an axion-like field that rapidly oscillates during the recombination epoch. In conventional models, the field oscillation during the recombination epoch leads to a cancellation of the birefringence effect and significantly suppresses the EB spectrum of the cosmic microwave background (CMB) polarization. By introducing an asymmetric potential to the axion, this cancellation becomes incomplete, and a substantial EB spectrum can be produced. This mechanism also results in a washout of the EE spectrum, which can be probed in future CMB observations. Our findings suggest the possibility that an axion-like field responsible for isotropic cosmic birefringence can also account for a significant fraction of dark matter.

Since the ALP is a parity-odd field coupled to photons, its temporal and spatial variations induce a parity violation in CMB.
When the ALP induces cosmic birefringence, the birefringence angle,
\beta

For appreciable ICB, the ALP field must start to oscillate after the recombination, which requires the ALP mass of

Otherwise, the birefringence angle oscillates depending on the emission time of CMB photons, and the ICB angle averaged over the line of sight is highly suppressed

The oscillation of the birefringence angle also washes out the linear polarizations and reduces the TE and EE spectra, although they are parity-even quantities.
This washout effect provides an upper bound on the ALP-photon coupling for ALP dark matter

;

Moreover, the ALP oscillation at the observer causes an oscillation in the polarization angle across the entire sky

Recently, a hint of such an oscillating feature in linear polarization has been reported at the significance level of
2.5\sigma

In this paper, we propose a new mechanism to cause ICB by an ALP that rapidly oscillates during the recombination epoch.
We consider an ALP potential asymmetric under
\phi\leftrightarrow-\phi
.
Then, the field oscillation is also distorted, and the cancellation of the EB spectrum becomes incomplete.
On the other hand, the oscillating birefringence and washout of the TE and EE spectra arise in a similar way to the conventional scenario.
To validate this mechanism, we numerically evaluate the CMB polarization spectra including cosmic birefringence.
We find that the ICB of
\beta\sim 0.3
deg can be explained without violating the constraint from the washout effect.
We also provide approximate formulae to obtain the polarization spectra from the spectra without cosmic birefringence.

II
Cosmic Birefringence

In general, an axion-like field,
\phi
, is coupled with photons in a parity-violating manner.
We consider a Lagrangian given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}=-\frac{1}{2}(\partial_{\mu}\phi)^{2}-V(\phi)-\frac{1}{4}F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu}-\frac{1}{4}g\phi F_{\mu\nu}\tilde{F}^{\mu\nu}\ ,

(1)

where
V(\phi)
is the potential for
\phi
,
F_{\mu\nu}
is the field strength of the photon field,
\tilde{F}^{\mu\nu}
is its dual, and
g
is the ALP-photon coupling constant with mass dimension
-1
.

Under temporal and spatial variations of
\phi
, the polarization plane of linear polarization of photons rotates by an angle,
\beta
, given by
Carroll:1989vb
;
Carroll:1991zs
;
Harari:1992ea

\displaystyle\beta=\frac{g}{2}\left[\phi(t_{\mathrm{obs}},\bm{x}_{\mathrm{obs}})-\phi(t_{\mathrm{emit}},\bm{x}_{\mathrm{emit}})\right]\ ,

(2)

where
(t_{\mathrm{obs}},\bm{x}_{\mathrm{obs}})
and
(t_{\mathrm{emit}},\bm{x}_{\mathrm{emit}})
are the spacetime coordinates at the observation and emission of photons, respectively.
While the spatial dependence of
\phi(t_{\mathrm{emit}},\bm{x}_{\mathrm{emit}})
induces anisotropic birefringence, the difference between
\phi(t_{\mathrm{obs}},\bm{x}_{\mathrm{obs}})
and the spatial average of
\phi(t_{\mathrm{emit}},\bm{x}_{\mathrm{emit}})
induces ICB.
In the following, we focus on the homogeneous component of
\phi(t_{\mathrm{emit}},\bm{x}_{\mathrm{emit}})
to obtain the isotropic component of the birefringence angle.
Moreover, we are interested in the ALP that starts to oscillate before the recombination, and thus we expect
|\phi(t_{\mathrm{obs}},\bm{x}_{\mathrm{obs}})|
to be negligibly small.

Throughout this work, we assume that
\phi
oscillates around the potential minimum at
\phi=0
.

Then, the birefringence angle depends only on
\phi
at the emission and is given by

\displaystyle\beta(t_{\mathrm{emit}})\simeq-\frac{g}{2}\bar{\phi}(t_{\mathrm{emit}})\ ,

(3)

where we decompose
\phi
into its background and fluctuations as
\phi(t,\bm{x})=\bar{\phi}(t)+\delta\phi(t,\bm{x})
.

To describe the effects of cosmic birefringence on the CMB polarization, we consider the Stokes parameters of linear polarization,
Q\pm iU
, and their Fourier transform,
{}_{\pm 2}\Delta_{P}(\eta,q,\mu)
.
Here,
\eta
is the conformal time,
q
is a wavenumber vector of the Fourier mode, and
\mu\equiv\bm{q}\cdot\bm{k}/(qk)
is a parameter of the angle between
\bm{q}
and the photon wavenumber
\bm{k}
.
The Boltzmann equation of
{}_{\pm 2}\Delta_{P}(\eta,q,\mu)
is given by
Liu:2006uh
;
Finelli:2008jv
;
Gubitosi:2014cua
;
Lee:2016jym

{}_{\pm 2}\Delta_{P}^{\prime}+iq\mu{}_{\pm 2}\Delta_{P}

\displaystyle=\tau^{\prime}\left[-_{\pm 2}\Delta_{P}+\sqrt{\frac{6\pi}{5}}{}_{\pm 2}Y_{2}^{0}(\mu)\Pi(\eta,q)\right]\pm 2i\beta^{\prime}{}_{\pm 2}\Delta_{P}\,,

(4)

where the prime denotes a derivative with respect to
\eta
,
{}_{\pm 2}Y_{l}^{m}
is the spin-2 spherical harmonics,
\Pi
is the polarization source term
Zaldarriaga:1996xe
, and
\tau^{\prime}\equiv a(\eta)n_{e}(\eta)\sigma_{T}
is the differential optical depth with the scale factor
a
, the electron number density
n_{e}
, and the Thomson scattering cross section
\sigma_{T}
.
To solve the Boltzmann equation, it is convenient to expand
{}_{\pm 2}\Delta_{P}
with
{}_{\pm 2}Y_{l}^{0}
as

{}_{\pm 2}\Delta_{P}(\eta,q,\mu)\equiv\sum_{l}i^{-l}\sqrt{4\pi(2l+1)}_{\pm 2}\Delta_{P,l}(\eta,q){}_{\pm 2}Y_{l}^{0}(\mu)\ .

(5)

Then, we can formally integrate the Boltzmann equation as

{}_{\pm 2}\Delta_{P,l}(\eta_{0},q)

\displaystyle=-\frac{3}{4}\sqrt{\frac{(l+2)!}{(l-2)!}}\int_{0}^{\eta_{0}}\mathrm{d}\eta\,\tau^{\prime}e^{-\tau(\eta)}\Pi(\eta,q)\frac{j_{l}(x)}{x^{2}}e^{\pm 2i\beta(\eta)}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\eta_{0}}\mathrm{d}\eta\,F(\eta,q)e^{\pm 2i\beta(\eta)}\ ,

(6)

where
\eta_{0}
is the current conformal time,
\tau(\eta)=\int_{\eta}^{\eta_{0}}\mathrm{d}\eta_{1}\,\tau^{\prime}(\eta_{1})
,
j_{l}
is the spherical Bessel function, and
x\equiv q(\eta_{0}-\eta)
.
We simplified the factors other than the birefringence effect by
F
for simplicity of notation.

We further decompose
{}_{\pm 2}\Delta_{P,l}
into parity eigenstates,
E
and
B
modes, given by
Zaldarriaga:1996xe
;
Kamionkowski:1996ks

\displaystyle\Delta_{E,l}\pm\Delta_{B,l}\equiv-{}_{\pm 2}\Delta_{P,l}(\eta_{0},q)\ .

(7)

Then, we obtain the polarization angular power spectra as

\displaystyle C_{l}^{XY}\equiv 4\pi\int\mathrm{d}(\ln q)\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{R}}(q)\Delta_{X,l}(q)\Delta_{Y,l}(q)\ ,

(8)

where
X,Y=E
or
B
, and
\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{R}}
is the primordial curvature power spectrum.

II.1
Isotropic Cosmic Birefringence

First, we discuss ICB induced by the time evolution of
\bar{\phi}
.
If
\bar{\phi}
starts to evolve after the last scattering of the CMB photons, all photons experience the same birefringence angle,
\beta\simeq g(\bar{\phi}(t_{0})-\bar{\phi}_{\mathrm{in}})/2
, where
\bar{\phi}_{\mathrm{in}}
is the initial value of
\bar{\phi}
.
Then, we can factor out
e^{\pm 2i\beta}
in Eq. (
6
) and obtain

\displaystyle\Delta_{E,l}\pm\Delta_{B,l}=e^{\pm 2i\beta}\left(\Delta_{E,l,0}\pm\Delta_{B,l,0}\right)\ ,

(9)

where the subscript “0” denotes quantities without cosmic birefringence.
Then, the polarization angular power spectra under cosmic birefringence are given by
Lue:1998mq
;
Feng:2004mq
;
Liu:2006uh

\displaystyle C_{l}^{EE}

\displaystyle=C_{l,0}^{EE}\cos^{2}(2\beta)+C_{l,0}^{BB}\sin^{2}(2\beta)\ ,

(10)

\displaystyle C_{l}^{BB}

\displaystyle=C_{l,0}^{EE}\sin^{2}(2\beta)+C_{l,0}^{BB}\cos^{2}(2\beta)\ ,

(11)

\displaystyle C_{l}^{EB}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(C_{l,0}^{EE}-C_{l,0}^{BB}\right)\sin(4\beta)\ ,

(12)

where we assumed
C_{l,0}^{EB}=0
.
From these relations, we obtain
C_{l}^{EB}\sim 0.01C_{l}^{EE}
for
\beta\sim 0.3
deg and
C_{l,0}^{EE}\gg C_{l,0}^{BB}
.
As a result, by taking an appropriate value of
g
, the reported ICB can be explained by the ALP that evolves only after the recombination epoch.
On the other hand, if
\bar{\phi}
evolves during the recombination epoch, these relations do not hold. In particular, if
\bar{\phi}
oscillates, positive and negative contributions to the EB spectrum cancel each other.

II.2
Washout of CMB polarization

Next, we analytically estimate the washout effect following Ref.
Fedderke:2019ajk
.
If the ALP rapidly oscillates during the recombination epoch,
\beta(\eta)
evolves much faster than
F(\eta,q)
.
Then, we can approximate
{}_{\pm 2}\Delta_{P,l}
by dividing the integration over
\eta
into a sum of the integration over one period of the ALP oscillation:

{}_{\pm 2}\Delta_{P,l}(\eta_{0},q)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}\int_{\eta_{i}}^{\eta_{i}+\delta\eta_{i}}\mathrm{d}\eta\,F(\eta,q)e^{\pm 2i\beta(\eta)}

\displaystyle\simeq\sum_{i}F(\eta_{i},q)\int_{\eta_{i}}^{\eta_{i}+\delta\eta_{i}}\mathrm{d}\eta\,e^{\pm 2i\beta(\eta)}\ ,

(13)

where
i
is the label of the ALP oscillation, and
\delta\eta_{i}
is the oscillation period.
Then, we approximate
\beta
in each period by

\displaystyle\beta\simeq\langle\beta\rangle_{i}\sin\left(2\pi\frac{\eta-\eta_{i}}{\delta\eta_{i}}\right)\ ,

(14)

where
\langle\beta\rangle_{i}
is the amplitude of
\beta
at
\eta=\eta_{i}
.
Consequently, we obtain

{}_{\pm 2}\Delta_{P,l}(\eta_{0},q)

\displaystyle\simeq\sum_{i}F(\eta_{i},q)\delta\eta_{i}j_{0}(2\langle\beta\rangle_{i})

\displaystyle\simeq\int\mathrm{d}\eta\,F(\eta,q)j_{0}(g\langle\phi\rangle(\eta))

\displaystyle\sim j_{0}(g\langle\phi\rangle_{*}){}_{\pm 2}\Delta_{P,l,0}(\eta_{0},q)\ ,

(15)

where
\langle\phi\rangle
is the oscillation amplitude of the ALP field.
In the last line, we made a further approximation by replacing
j_{0}(g\langle\phi\rangle(\eta))
with a representative value at
z=z_{*}=1090
.
Finally, we obtain the analytical estimate for the polarization spectra as

\displaystyle C_{l,\mathrm{ana}}^{TE}

\displaystyle=j_{0}(g\langle\phi\rangle_{*})C_{l,0}^{TE}\ ,

(16)

\displaystyle C_{l,\mathrm{ana}}^{EE}

\displaystyle=j_{0}^{2}(g\langle\phi\rangle_{*})C_{l,0}^{EE}\ ,

(17)

which is reduced to

\displaystyle C_{l,\mathrm{ana}}^{TE}

\displaystyle\simeq\left(1-\frac{1}{4}g^{2}\langle\phi\rangle_{*}^{2}\right)C_{l,0}^{TE}\ ,

(18)

\displaystyle C_{l,\mathrm{ana}}^{EE}

\displaystyle\simeq\left(1-\frac{1}{2}g^{2}\langle\phi\rangle_{*}^{2}\right)C_{l,0}^{EE}\ ,

(19)

in the limit of a small oscillation angle.

III
Numerical results

Here, we show the numerical results for the CMB polarization spectra under oscillating birefringence.
Let us consider an asymmetric potential.
Since this study aims to generally investigate the effects of an asymmetric potential, we adopt a simple potential given by

\displaystyle V(\phi)=\frac{m_{\phi}^{2}}{2}\phi^{2}+C_{3}\phi^{3}+C_{4}\phi^{4}\ ,

(20)

where
C_{3}
and
C_{4}
are constants.

Such an asymmetric potential can be realized, e.g., in the context of axion monodromy

Alternatively, a superposition of cosine potentials with different scales and phases also gives an asymmetric potential around its minima.

In numerical simulations, we normalize the ALP field by
\varphi\equiv\phi/\phi_{\mathrm{in}}
with the initial field value
\phi_{\mathrm{in}}

\displaystyle V(\varphi)

\displaystyle=\frac{m_{\phi}^{2}\phi_{\mathrm{in}}^{2}}{2}\left(\varphi^{2}+c_{3}\varphi^{3}+c_{4}\varphi^{4}\right)

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{m_{\phi}^{2}\phi_{\mathrm{in}}^{2}}{2}\tilde{V}(\varphi)\ ,

In the intermediate region, the shape of the potential can be highly distorted.

\displaystyle\ddot{\varphi}+3H\dot{\varphi}+m_{\phi}^{2}\frac{\partial\tilde{V}(\varphi)}{\partial\varphi}=0\ ,

Finally, we comment on the dependence of the EB spectrum on the model parameters.
To obtain a substantial EB signal, the asymmetric oscillation during the recombination epoch is essential (see Fig.
4

To realize such a situation, the quadratic, cubic, and quartic terms should contribute comparably to the potential at that time, which is not the case for general choices of

,
c_{3}
, and
c_{4}
.
If there are higher-order terms in the potential, they should not dominate over the lower-order terms during the recombination epoch to realize the asymmetric oscillation.
Note that the dependence on
\phi_{\mathrm{in}}
is absorbed to

and
c_{4}
for fixed
m_{\phi}
in our notation of the potential (

IV
Summary

In this paper, we investigated the effect of oscillating birefringence on the CMB polarization spectra.
First, we numerically evaluated the EE and EB spectra for the mass potential and validated the analytical estimate.
Then, we investigated an asymmetric potential and found that the oscillating birefringence by an asymmetric potential can explain the ICB with
\beta\sim 0.3
deg without violating the constraint from the washout effect.
We also provided analytical formulae for the EE and EB spectra with an asymmetric potential.

Although we used
m_{\phi}=10^{-26}
eV as an example, we have checked that the result is almost independent of
m_{\phi}
as long as
\phi
rapidly oscillates during the recombination epoch.
Rather, the resultant EE and EB spectra are largely determined by
\langle\phi\rangle_{*,\mathrm{eff}}
and
\bar{\phi}_{*}
given in Eqs. (
25
) and (
26
).
Thus,
\phi
will be able to explain the ICB with
\beta\sim 0.3
deg even for
m_{\phi}\gg 10^{-26}
eV.
This opens up the possibility that
\phi
is a significant fraction of dark matter and the origin of the ICB at the same time.
Considering the X-ray constraint of
g<6.3\times 10^{-13}\,\mathrm{GeV}^{-1}
for the ultra-light mass region
Reynes:2021bpe
, we require
\bar{\phi}_{*}\gtrsim 10^{10}
GeV to obtain
\beta\sim 0.3
deg.
Since the energy density of the ALP is at most that of dark matter, we require that
m_{\phi}^{2}\bar{\phi}_{*}^{2}/2
should be smaller than the dark matter density at the recombination as a rough necessary condition.
Consequently, we obtain the upper bound of

In this work, we assumed that the ALP is a subdominant component in the universe and adopted a toy-model potential.
To test the possibility of the ICB by ALP dark matter, we need to solve the background evolution including the ALP and the CMB spectra consistently.
Since the time scale of the ALP oscillation is much shorter than that of the CMB physics, it will be computationally expensive.
However, an approximate analytical solution for the evolution of
\phi(t)
could be used to speed up the calculations
Galaverni:2023zhv
.
Moreover, the ALP potential should be modified for dark matter because the ALP does not behave as non-relativistic matter at the beginning of oscillations with the quartic potential.
For instance, the combination of cosine-type potentials can lead to a potential distorted only in a certain region between the initial value and the potential minimum.
Further detailed investigation of this possibility is left for future work.

Relations Between Abs-Normal NLPs and MPCCs. Part 1: Strong Constraint Qualifications
\name L.C. Hegerhorst-Schultchen a ∗ and C. Kirches b and M.C. Steinbach a

Abstract
This work is part of an ongoing effort of comparing non-smooth optimization problems in abs-normal form to MPCCs. We study the general abs-normal NLP with equality and inequality constraints in relation to an equivalent MPCC reformulation. We show that kink qualifications and MPCC constraint qualifications of linear independence type and Mangasarian-Fromovitz type are equivalent. Then we consider strong stationarity concepts with first and second order optimality conditions, which again turn out to be equivalent for the two problem classes. Throughout we also consider specific slack reformulations suggested in [ 9 ] , which preserve constraint qualifications of linear independence type but not of Mangasarian-Fromovitz type.

Introduction

Nonsmoothness arises frequently
in practical optimization problems from various areas.
In finite dimensions, certain nonsmooth functions
like
\ell_{1}
and
\ell_{\infty}
norms
can be avoided by smooth remodeling,
but models that involve (possibly nested) absolute value,
maximum, and minimum functions,
models with piecewise definitions and
models with equilibrium conditions or complementarity conditions
lead to genuine nonsmoothness.
An important sub-class of such nonsmooth finite-dimensional optimization problems
is essentially characterized as “NLPs with finitely many kinks”,
which gives rise to more general standard problem classes like
MPCCs
[
10
]

and optimization problems in abs-normal form
[
2
]
.

The latter are non-smooth nonlinear optimization problems of the form

\displaystyle\smash[b]{\min_{x}}\ f(x)\quad\textup{s.t.}\quad g(x)

\displaystyle=0,

(NLP)

\displaystyle h(x)

\displaystyle\geq 0,

where
D^{x}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
is open, the objective
f\in C^{d}(D^{x},\mathbb{R})
is a smooth function
(
d\geq 1
)
and the equality and inequality constraints
g\in C^{d}_{\text{abs}}(D^{x},\mathbb{R}^{m_{1}})
and

h\in C^{d}_{\text{abs}}(D^{x},\mathbb{R}^{m_{2}})
are non-smooth functions with the non-smoothness exposed in
abs-normal form
[
2
]
.
Thus, there exist functions

c_{\mathcal{E}}\in C^{d}(D^{x,\lvert z\rvert},\mathbb{R}^{m_{1}})
,

c_{\mathcal{I}}\in C^{d}(D^{x,\lvert z\rvert},\mathbb{R}^{m_{2}})
and

c_{\mathcal{Z}}\in C^{d}(D^{x,\lvert z\rvert},\mathbb{R}^{s})
with
D^{x,\lvert z\rvert}=D^{x}\times D^{\lvert z\rvert}
,

D^{\lvert z\rvert}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{s}
open and
symmetric

(i.e.,
z\in D^{\lvert z\rvert}
implies
\Sigma z\in D^{\lvert z\rvert}

for every signature matrix
\Sigma
, see
Definition

1
) such that

\displaystyle g(x)

\displaystyle=c_{\mathcal{E}}(x,\lvert z\rvert),

\displaystyle h(x)

\displaystyle=c_{\mathcal{I}}(x,\lvert z\rvert),

(ANF)

\displaystyle z

\displaystyle=c_{\mathcal{Z}}(x,\lvert z\rvert)\quad\textup{with $\partial_{2}c_{\mathcal{Z}}(x,\lvert z\rvert)$ strictly lower triangular}.

Note that we introduce one joint
switching constraint

c_{\mathcal{Z}}

for
g
and
h
and reuse
switching variables

z_{i}

if the same argument occurs inside an absolute value in
g
and
h
.
Here, components of
z
can be computed one by one from
x
and
z_{i}
,
i<j
,
since
\partial_{2}c_{\mathcal{Z}}(x,\lvert z\rvert)
is strictly lower triangular.
In the following we write
z(x)
to denote this dependence explicitly.
However,
z
is implicitly defined by
z=c_{\mathcal{Z}}(x,\lvert z\rvert)
.
To consider solvability of this system, we use the reformulation
\lvert z_{i}\rvert=\sign(z_{i})z_{i}
.

Definition 1

(Signature of
z
)
.

Let
x\in D^{x}
. We define the
signature

\sigma(x)

and the associated
signature matrix

\Sigma(x)
as

\displaystyle\sigma(x)

\displaystyle\mathrel{{\mathop{:}}{=}}\sign(z(x))\in\{-1,0,1\}^{s},

\displaystyle\Sigma(x)

\displaystyle\mathrel{{\mathop{:}}{=}}\diag(\sigma(x)).

A signature vector
\sigma(x)\in\{-1,1\}^{s}
is called
definite
,
otherwise
indefinite
.

With
Definition

1
,
we can write
\lvert z(x)\rvert=\Sigma(x)z(x)
and consider the system
z=c_{\mathcal{Z}}(x,\Sigma z)
, which for any
fixed signature

\Sigma=\Sigma(\hat{x})
becomes a differentiable system.
Then, application of the implicit function theorem yields the existence of a locally unique solution
z(x)
with Jacobian

\partial_{x}z(\hat{x})=[I-\partial_{2}c_{\mathcal{Z}}(\hat{x},\lvert z(\hat{x})\rvert)\Sigma]^{-1}\partial_{1}c_{\mathcal{Z}}(\hat{x},\lvert z(\hat{x})\rvert)\in\mathbb{R}^{s\times n}.

Definition 2

(Active Switching Set)
.

We call the switching variable
z_{i}

active
if
z_{i}(x)=0
.
The
active switching set

\alpha

consists of all indices of active switching variables, i.e.

\alpha(x)\mathrel{{\mathop{:}}{=}}\{i\in\{1,\dots,s\}\colon z_{i}(x)=0\}.

We denote the number of active switching variables by
\lvert\alpha(x)\rvert

and the number of inactive ones by
\lvert\sigma(x)\rvert\mathrel{{\mathop{:}}{=}}s-\lvert\alpha(x)\rvert
.

Unconstrained optimization problems in abs-normal form
have recently been introduced by
[
3
]
,
and have been shown to come with favorable theoretical properties
that lead to globally convergent solution methods
based on piecewise linearizations
that can be generated by algorithmic differentiation
[
5
,
6
]
.
When nonsmooth equality and inequality constraints are added,
the resulting abs-normal NLPs can be compared to MPCCs,
for which a well-established theoretical framework has been developed over the past decades
[
10
]
.
Comparing the two classes of nonsmooth optimization problems,
the authors reported in
[
8
]

that they basically form the same problem class,
although with a different representation of nonsmoothness
and not necessarily with the same regularity properties.

Literature

The abs-normal NLPs considered here are a direct generalization of
unconstrained abs-normal problems developed by Griewank and Walther

[
2
,
3
]
.
These problems offer particularly attractive theoretical features
when generalizing KKT theory and stationarity concepts,
and they are tractable by sophisticated algorithms with guaranteed convergence
based on piecewise linearizations
and using algorithmic differentiation techniques
[
5
,
6
]
.

Another important class of nonsmooth optimization problems are Mathematical Programs with Complementarity (or Equilibrium) Constraints (MPCCs, MPECs); an overview can be found in the book
[
10
]
.
Since standard theory for smooth optimization problems cannot be applied, new constraint qualifications and corresponding optimality conditions were introduced.
By now, there is a large body of literature on MPCCs, and we refer to
[
12
]
for an overview of the basic concepts and theory.
In this paper, constraint qualifications for MPCCs in the sense of linear independence and Mangasarian Fromowitz are considered.
Further, the corresponding stationarity concept (S-stationarity) as well as first and second order optimality conditions are studied.
Details can be found in
[
11
]
,
[
10
]
and
[
1
]
.

In
[
8
]
we have shown that unconstrained
optimization problems in abs-normal form are a subclass of MPCCs
and we have studied regularity concepts of linear independence type,
Mangasarian-Fromovitz type and Abadie type.
We have also shown that abs-normal NLPs with general constraints
are equivalent to the class of MPCCs.
In
[
9
]
we have generalized
optimality conditions of unconstrained abs-normal problems
to the case with equality and inequality constraints
under the linear independence kink qualification.
More details and additional information about these results as well as about the results in this paper can be found in
[
7
]
.

Contributions

We develop a deeper understanding of the commonalities
of these problem classes of NLPs in abs-normal form on the one hand, and MPCCs on the other. We provide a detailed comparative study
of general abs-normal NLPs and MPCCs,
considering constraint qualifications
of linear independence type and Mangasarian Fromovitz type
for the standard formulation
and for a reformulation with absolute value slacks
that was suggested in
[
9
]
.
In particular, we show that corresponding constraint qualifications
of abs-normal NLPs and MPCCs are equivalent
and that the linear independence type constraint qualifications
are preserved by the slack reformulation
while this is not the case for a Mangasarian-Fromovitz type constraint qualification.
We then compare optimality conditions of first and second order
for abs-normal NLPs and MPCCs
under the respective linear independence type constraint qualifications.
We show equivalence of the respective first order necessary conditions, kink stationarity and strong stationarity.
We also show how second order conditions for MPCCs can be carried over to abs-normal NLPs. Under suitable additional assumptions, we prove equivalence
of positive (semi-)definiteness of the associated reduced Hessians,
which gives correspondences of second order necessary and sufficient conditions.

We expect that our theoretical results will contribute
to the understanding and further development
of rigorous solution algorithms for abs-normal NLPs. We also expect the results to
facilitate a possible transferral of active-signature methods for abs-normal forms, such as SALMIN
[
5
]
, to MPCCs.

2
Abs-Normal NLPs

In this section we consider two formulations for non-smooth NLPs in abs-normal form
that differ in the treatment of inequality constraints.

2.1
General Abs-Normal NLPs

In this paragraph we consider abs-normal NLPs with equalities and inequalities,
obtained by substituting the constraints representation
in abs-normal form (
ANF
)
into the general non-smooth problem (
NLP
).
Note that we use the variables
(t,z^{t})
instead of
(x,z)
.

Definition 3

(Abs-Normal NLP)
.

Let
D^{t}
be an open subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n_{t}}
.
A non-smooth NLP is called an
abs-normal NLP

if functions
f\in C^{d}(D^{t},\mathbb{R})
,
c_{\mathcal{E}}\in C^{d}(D^{t,\lvert z^{t}\rvert},\mathbb{R}^{m_{1}})
,

c_{\mathcal{I}}\in C^{d}(D^{t,\lvert z^{t}\rvert},\mathbb{R}^{m_{2}})
, and
c_{\mathcal{Z}}\in C^{d}(D^{t,\lvert z^{t}\rvert},\mathbb{R}^{s_{t}})
with
d\geq 1
exist
such that the NLP reads

\displaystyle\smash[b]{\min_{t,z^{t}}}\ f(t)\quad\textup{s.t.}\quad

\displaystyle c_{\mathcal{E}}(t,\lvert z^{t}\rvert)=0,

\displaystyle c_{\mathcal{I}}(t,\lvert z^{t}\rvert)\geq 0,

(I-NLP)

\displaystyle c_{\mathcal{Z}}(t,\lvert z^{t}\rvert)-z^{t}=0,

where
D^{\lvert z^{t}\rvert}
is open and symmetric and

\partial_{2}c_{\mathcal{Z}}(x,\lvert z^{t}\rvert)
is strictly lower triangular.

The feasible set of (
I-NLP
) is denoted by

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\textup{abs}}

\displaystyle\mathrel{{\mathop{:}}{=}}\left\{\,(t,z^{t})\,\left|\begin{array}[]{l}c_{\mathcal{E}}(t,\lvert z^{t}\rvert)=0,\ c_{\mathcal{I}}(t,\lvert z^{t}\rvert)\geq 0,\\
c_{\mathcal{Z}}(t,\lvert z^{t}\rvert)-z^{t}=0\end{array}\right.\!\right\}

\displaystyle:=\{(t,z^{t}(t))\colon t\in D^{t},\ c_{\mathcal{E}}(t,\lvert z^{t}(t)\rvert)=0,\ c_{\mathcal{I}}(t,\lvert z^{t}(t)\rvert)\geq 0\}.

In contrast to standard NLP theory,
we do not count equalities as active constraints.

Definition 4

(Active Inequality Set)
.

Let
(t,z^{t}(t))\in\mathcal{F}_{\textup{abs}}
. We call the constraint

i\in\mathcal{I}

active
if
c_{i}(t,\lvert z^{t}(t)\rvert)=0
.
The
active set

\mathcal{A}(t)
consists of all indices of active constraints,

\mathcal{A}(t)=\{i\in\mathcal{I}\colon c_{i}(t,\lvert z^{t}(t)\rvert)=0\}.

We denote the number of active inequality constraints by
\lvert\mathcal{A}(t)\rvert
.

To define the linear independence kink qualification as well as the interior direction kink qualification
for (
I-NLP
) we need its Jacobians.

Definition 5

(Jacobians)
.

Consider the abs-normal NLP (
I-NLP
).
For
(t,z^{t}(t))\in\mathcal{F}_{\textup{abs}}
set
\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{A}(t)
,
\alpha=\alpha(t)
,
\sigma=\sigma(t)
,

\Sigma=\diag(\sigma)
, and
c_{\mathcal{A}}=[c_{i}]_{i\in\mathcal{A}}
.
The
equality-constraints Jacobian
is

\displaystyle J_{\mathcal{E}}(t)\mathrel{{\mathop{:}}{=}}\partial_{t}c_{\mathcal{E}}(t,\Sigma z^{t}(t))

\displaystyle=\partial_{1}c_{\mathcal{E}}(t,\Sigma z^{t}(t))+\partial_{2}c_{\mathcal{E}}(t,\Sigma z^{t}(t))\Sigma\partial_{t}z^{t}(t)

\displaystyle=\partial_{1}c_{\mathcal{E}}(t,\lvert z^{t}(t)\rvert)+\partial_{2}c_{\mathcal{E}}(t,\lvert z^{t}(t)\rvert)\Sigma\partial_{t}z^{t}(t),

the
active inequality Jacobian
is

\displaystyle J_{\mathcal{A}}(t)\mathrel{{\mathop{:}}{=}}\partial_{t}c_{\mathcal{A}}(t,\Sigma z^{t}(t))

\displaystyle=\partial_{1}c_{\mathcal{A}}(t,\Sigma z^{t}(t))+\partial_{2}c_{\mathcal{A}}(t,\Sigma z^{t}(t))\Sigma\partial_{t}z^{t}(t)

\displaystyle=\partial_{1}c_{\mathcal{A}}(t,\lvert z^{t}(t)\rvert)+\partial_{2}c_{\mathcal{A}}(t,\lvert z^{t}(t)\rvert)\Sigma\partial_{t}z^{t}(t),

and the
active switching Jacobian
is

\displaystyle J_{\alpha}(t)\mathrel{{\mathop{:}}{=}}\left[e_{i}^{T}\partial_{t}z^{t}(t)\right]_{i\in\alpha}=\left[e_{i}^{T}[I-\partial_{2}c_{\mathcal{Z}}(t,\lvert z^{t}(t)\rvert)\Sigma]^{-1}\partial_{1}c_{\mathcal{Z}}(t,\lvert z^{t}(t)\rvert)\right]_{i\in\alpha}.

Definition 6

(Linear Independence Kink Qualification)
.

Let
(t,z^{t}(t))\in\mathcal{F}_{\textup{abs}}
.
We say that the
linear independence kink qualification (LIKQ)

holds for (
I-NLP
) at
t
if

\displaystyle J_{\textup{abs}}(t)=\begin{bmatrix}J_{\mathcal{E}}(t)\\
J_{\mathcal{A}}(t)\\
J_{\alpha}(t)\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}\partial_{t}c_{\mathcal{E}}(t,\lvert z^{t}(t)\rvert)\\
\partial_{t}c_{\mathcal{A}}(t,\lvert z^{t}(t)\rvert)\\
[e_{i}^{T}\partial_{t}z^{t}(t)]_{i\in\alpha}\end{bmatrix}\in\mathbb{R}^{(m_{1}+\lvert\mathcal{A}\rvert+\lvert\alpha\rvert)\times n_{t}}

has full row rank
m_{1}+\lvert\mathcal{A}\rvert+\lvert\alpha\rvert
.

Definition 7

(Interior Direction Kink Qualification)
.

Let
(t,z^{t}(t))\in\mathcal{F}_{\textup{abs}}
.
We say that the
interior direction kink qualification (IDKQ)

holds for (
I-NLP
) at
t
if

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}J_{\mathcal{E}}(t)\\
J_{\alpha}(t)\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}\partial_{t}c_{\mathcal{E}}(t,\lvert z^{t}(t)\rvert)\\
[e_{i}^{T}\partial_{t}z^{t}(t)]_{i\in\alpha}\end{bmatrix}\in\mathbb{R}^{(m_{1}+\lvert\alpha\rvert)\times n_{t}}

has full row rank
m_{1}+\lvert\alpha\rvert
and if there exists a vector
d\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{t}}
such that

J_{\mathcal{E}}(t)d=0,\quad J_{\alpha}(t)d=0,\quad\text{and}\quad J_{\mathcal{A}}(t)d>0.

For the general abs-normal NLP (
I-NLP
) considered here,
IDKQ actually generalizes MFCQ from the smooth case
and corresponds to MPCC-MFCQ, as we will show below.
We cannot use the canonical name MFKQ, however,
since Griewank and Walther have already defined MFKQ
as a different weakening of LIKQ in
[
4
]
.
We believe that other possible names like
“Abs-normal MFKQ” or “Constrained MFKQ”
would produce confusion rather than clarification
and hence suggest the descriptive name “Interior Direction KQ”.

The following example from
[
11
]
(converted from MPCC form to abs-normal NLP form) shows that IDKQ is weaker than LIKQ
in the presence of inequality constraints.

Example 8

(IDKQ is weaker than LIKQ)
.

Consider the problem

\displaystyle\smash[b]{\min_{t\in\mathbb{R}^{3},z^{t}\in\mathbb{R}}}\ t_{1}+t_{2}-t_{3}\quad\textup{s.t.}\quad

\displaystyle t_{1}+t_{2}-\lvert z^{t}\rvert=0,

\displaystyle 4t_{1}-t_{3}\geq 0,

\displaystyle 4t_{2}-t_{3}\geq 0,

\displaystyle t_{1}-t_{2}-z^{t}=0,

with solution
t^{*}=(0,0,0)
and
(z^{t})^{*}=0
. We compute

\displaystyle J_{\mathcal{A}}(t^{*})=\begin{bmatrix}4&0&-1\\
0&4&-1\end{bmatrix},\quad J_{\mathcal{E}}(t^{*})=\begin{bmatrix}1&1&0\end{bmatrix},\quad\text{and}\quad J_{\alpha}(t^{*})=\begin{bmatrix}1&-1&0\end{bmatrix}.

Here, LIKQ is not satisfied but IDKQ is satisfied with
d=(0,0,-1)
.

2.2
Abs-Normal NLPs with Inequality Slacks

In this paragraph we consider abs-normal NLPs with slack variables introduced for all
inequalities. We make use of the absolute value of a slack variable, an idea due to Griewank.
This results in a class of purely equality-constrained abs-normal NLPs,
which simplifies the derivation of optimality conditions under the LIKQ,
see
[
9
]
and
Section

5
.

Using slack variables
w\in\mathbb{R}^{m_{2}}
,
we obtain the following reformulation of (
NLP
):

\displaystyle\smash[b]{\min_{t,w}}\ f(t)\quad\textup{s.t.}\quad

\displaystyle g(t)=0,

\displaystyle h(t)-\lvert w\rvert=0.

Then, we express
g
and
h
in abs-normal form as in (
ANF
)
and introduce additional switching variables
z^{w}
to handle
\lvert w\rvert
.
This approach leads to the next definition.

Definition 9

(Abs-Normal NLP with Inequality Slacks)
.

An abs-normal NLP posed in the following form is called
an
abs-normal NLP with inequality slacks
:

\displaystyle\smash[b]{\min_{t,w,z^{t},z^{w}}}\ f(t)\quad\textup{s.t.}\quad

\displaystyle c_{\mathcal{E}}(t,\lvert z^{t}\rvert)=0,

\displaystyle c_{\mathcal{I}}(t,\lvert z^{t}\rvert)-\lvert z^{w}\rvert=0,

(E-NLP)

\displaystyle c_{\mathcal{Z}}(t,\lvert z^{t}\rvert)-z^{t}=0,

\displaystyle w-z^{w}=0,

where
D^{\lvert z^{t}\rvert}
is open and symmetric and

\partial_{2}c_{\mathcal{Z}}(x,\lvert z^{t}\rvert)
is strictly lower triangular.

The feasible set of (
E-NLP
) is a lifting of
\mathcal{F}_{\textup{abs}}
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\textup{e-abs}}

\displaystyle\mathrel{{\mathop{:}}{=}}\left\{\,(t,w,z^{t},z^{w})\,\left|\begin{array}[]{l}c_{\mathcal{E}}(t,\lvert z^{t}\rvert)=0,\ c_{\mathcal{I}}(t,\lvert z^{t}\rvert)-\lvert z^{w}\rvert=0,\\
c_{\mathcal{Z}}(t,\lvert z^{t}\rvert)-z^{t}=0,\ w-z^{w}=0\end{array}\right.\!\right\}

\displaystyle:=\left\{(t,w,z^{t},z^{w})\colon(t,z^{t})\in\mathcal{F}_{\textup{abs}},\ w=z^{w},\ \lvert z^{w}\rvert=c_{\mathcal{I}}(t,\lvert z^{t}\rvert)\right\}.

Using the dependence of
z^{t}
and
z^{w}
of
t
and
w
, the feasible set can be written as

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\textup{e-abs}}

\displaystyle=\left\{\,(t,w,z^{t}(t),z^{w}(w))\,\left|\begin{array}[]{l}t\in D^{t},\ c_{\mathcal{E}}(t,\lvert z^{t}(t)\rvert)=0,\\
c_{\mathcal{I}}(t,\lvert z^{t}(t)\rvert)-\lvert z^{w}(w)\rvert=0,\ w-z^{w}(w)=0\end{array}\right.\!\right\}

\displaystyle=\left\{(t,w,z^{t}(t),z^{w}(w))\colon(t,z^{t}(t))\in\mathcal{F}_{\textup{abs}},\ w=z^{w}(w),\ \lvert z^{w}(w)\rvert=c_{\mathcal{I}}(t,\lvert z^{t}(t)\rvert)\right\}.

We split the active switching set into subsets for variables
t
and
w

as
\alpha=(\alpha^{t},\alpha^{w})
.

Remark 10
.

Note that introducing
\lvert w\rvert
converts inequalities
to pure equalities without a nonnegativity condition
for the slack variables
w
.
However, the slack reformulation has some subtle issues.
Subsequently we will show that, in contrast to LIKQ,
IDKQ is
not
preserved.
Moreover, one cannot eliminate
the equation
w-z^{w}=0
(and hence
z^{w}
or
w
) in (
E-NLP
)
since this would destroy the abs-normal form.
Finally, the slack
w
is not uniquely determined
since the signs of nonzero components
w_{i}
can be chosen arbitrarily,
yielding a set of
2^{m_{2}-\lvert\alpha^{w}\rvert}
choices,

W(t)\mathrel{{\mathop{:}}{=}}\{w\colon\lvert w\rvert=c_{\mathcal{I}}(t,\lvert z^{t}(t)\rvert)\}
.

Lemma 11
.

Consider
(t,w,z^{t}(t),z^{w}(w))\in\mathcal{F}_{\textup{e-abs}}
. Then, LIKQ for (
E-NLP
) at
(t,w)

is full row rank of

\displaystyle J_{\textup{e-abs}}(t,w)=\begin{bmatrix}\partial_{t}c_{\mathcal{E}}(t,\lvert z^{t}(t)\rvert)&0\\
\partial_{t}c_{\mathcal{I}}(t,\lvert z^{t}(t)\rvert)&-\Sigma^{w}\\
[e_{i}^{T}\partial_{t}z^{t}(t)]_{i\in\alpha^{t}}&0\\
0&[e_{i}^{T}I]_{i\in\alpha^{w}}\end{bmatrix}\in\mathbb{R}^{(m_{1}+m_{2}+\lvert\alpha^{t}\rvert+\lvert\alpha^{w}\rvert)\times(n_{t}+m_{2})}.

Proof.

Set
x=(t,w)
,
z=(z^{t},z^{w})
,
\bar{f}(x)=f(t)
,

\bar{c}_{\mathcal{E}}(x,\lvert z\rvert)=(c_{\mathcal{E}}(t,\lvert z^{t}\rvert),c_{\mathcal{I}}(t,\lvert z^{t}\rvert)-\lvert z^{w}\rvert)
, and

\bar{c}_{\mathcal{Z}}(x,\lvert z\rvert)=(c_{\mathcal{Z}}(t,\lvert z^{t}\rvert),w)
.
Then, we can write (
E-NLP
) compactly as

\displaystyle\smash[b]{\min_{x,z}}\ \bar{f}(x)\quad\textup{s.t.}\quad

\displaystyle\bar{c}_{\mathcal{E}}(x,\lvert z\rvert)=0,

(
\overline{\text{E-NLP}}
)

\displaystyle\bar{c}_{\mathcal{Z}}(x,\lvert z\rvert)-z=0,

and compute
\bar{J}_{\mathcal{E}}
and
\bar{J}_{\alpha}
from
Definition

5
using the special
structure of (
E-NLP
).
The resulting matrix
J_{\textup{e-abs}}(x)=\begin{bmatrix}\bar{J}_{\mathcal{E}}(x)^{T}&\bar{J}_{\alpha}(x)^{T}\end{bmatrix}^{T}

in
Definition

6
has the form above.
∎

Remark 12
.

Clearly, the rank of
J_{\textup{e-abs}}
does not depend on the signs
of
\pm 1
entries in
\Sigma^{w}
but only on their positions.
Hence, LIKQ does not depend on the particular choice of
w
.
Otherwise it would not make sense to consider (
E-NLP
).

Note that, since the abs-normal NLP (
E-NLP
)
does not contain any inequalities,
the concept of IDKQ is equivalent to LIKQ here.
This is in contrast to the standard reformulation
of smooth NLP inequalities as equalities with nonnegative slacks
where the validity of LICQ and MFCQ are both unaffected.

2.3
Relations of Kink Qualifications for Abs-Normal NLPs

In this paragraph we discuss the relations of kink qualifications
for the two different formulations of abs-normal NLPs.
We use the set
W(t)
from above.

Theorem 13
.

LIKQ for (
I-NLP
) holds at
(t,z^{t}(t))\in\mathcal{F}_{\textup{abs}}
if and only if LIKQ for (
E-NLP
) holds at

(t,w,z^{t}(t),z^{w}(w))\in\mathcal{F}_{\textup{e-abs}}
for any (and hence all)
w\in W(t)
.

Proof.

This follows immediately by comparison of
J_{\textup{abs}}
and
J_{\textup{e-abs}}
using that

\alpha^{w}(w)=\{i\in\mathcal{I}\colon w_{i}=0\}=\{i\in\mathcal{I}\colon c_{i}(t,z^{t}(t))=0\}=\mathcal{A}(t)

and

\Sigma^{w}=\diag(\sigma^{w})\quad\text{with}\quad\sigma^{w}_{i}=\sign(w_{i})=\begin{cases}0,&i\in\mathcal{A}(t),\\
\pm 1,&i\notin\mathcal{A}(t).\end{cases}\qed

Theorem 14
.

IDKQ for (
I-NLP
) holds at
(t,z^{t}(t))\in\mathcal{F}_{\textup{abs}}
if
IDKQ for (
E-NLP
) holds at
(t,w,z^{t}(t),z^{w}(w))\in\mathcal{F}_{\textup{e-abs}}

for any (and hence all)
w\in W(t)
.
The converse is not true.

Proof.

Since (
E-NLP
) has no inequalities,
the concepts of IDKQ and LIKQ coincide.
LIKQ for (
E-NLP
) is equivalent to
LIKQ for (
I-NLP
) by
Theorem

13
, and
LIKQ for (
I-NLP
) implies IDKQ for (
I-NLP
).
The converse does not hold since LIKQ for (
I-NLP
)
is stronger then IDKQ as we have shown in
Example

8
.
∎

3
Counterpart MPCCs

In this section we introduce MPCC counterpart problems for the two formulations
(
I-NLP
) and (
E-NLP
). Then, we have a quick look at relations between them.

3.1
Counterpart MPCC for the General Abs-Normal NLP

To reformulate (
I-NLP
) as an MPCC, we partition
z^{t}
into its nonnegative part and the modulus of its
nonpositive part,
u^{t}\mathrel{{\mathop{:}}{=}}[z^{t}]^{+}\mathrel{{\mathop{:}}{=}}\max(z^{t},0)
and
v^{t}\mathrel{{\mathop{:}}{=}}[z^{t}]^{-}\mathrel{{\mathop{:}}{=}}\max(-z^{t},0)
.
Then, we require complementarity of these two variables
to replace
\lvert z^{t}\rvert
by
u^{t}+v^{t}
and
z^{t}
itself by
u^{t}-v^{t}
.

Definition 15

(Counterpart MPCC of (
I-NLP
))
.

The
counterpart MPCC
of the abs-normal NLP (
I-NLP
) reads

\displaystyle\smash[b]{\min_{t,u^{t},v^{t}}}\ f(t)\quad\textup{s.t.}\quad

\displaystyle c_{\mathcal{E}}(t,u^{t}+v^{t})=0,

\displaystyle c_{\mathcal{I}}(t,u^{t}+v^{t})\geq 0,

(I-MPCC)

\displaystyle c_{\mathcal{Z}}(t,u^{t}+v^{t})-(u^{t}-v^{t})=0,

\displaystyle 0\leq u^{t}\perp v^{t}\geq 0,

where
u^{t},v^{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{s_{t}}
.

The feasible set of (
I-MPCC
) is denoted by

\mathcal{F}_{\textup{mpcc}}\mathrel{{\mathop{:}}{=}}\left\{\,(t,u^{t},v^{t})\,\left|\begin{array}[]{l}c_{\mathcal{E}}(t,u^{t}+v^{t})=0,\ c_{\mathcal{I}}(t,u^{t}+v^{t})\geq 0,\\
c_{\mathcal{Z}}(t,u^{t}+v^{t})=u^{t}-v^{t},\ 0\leq u^{t}\perp v^{t}\geq 0\end{array}\right.\!\right\}.

Lemma 16
.

Given an abs-normal NLP (
I-NLP
)
and its counterpart MPCC (
I-MPCC
),
we have a homeomorphism
\phi\colon{}\mathcal{F}_{\textup{mpcc}}\to\mathcal{F}_{\textup{abs}}
defined as

\displaystyle\phi(t,u^{t},v^{t})

\displaystyle=(t,u^{t}-v^{t}),

\displaystyle\phi^{-1}(t,z^{t})

\displaystyle=(t,[z^{t}]^{+},[z^{t}]^{-}).

Proof.

Obvious.
∎

Just like the active switching set of the abs-normal NLP, we define index sets of the counterpart MPCC.

Definition 17

(Index Sets)
.

We denote by
\mathcal{U}^{t}_{0}\mathrel{{\mathop{:}}{=}}\{i\in\{1,\dots,s_{t}\}\colon u^{t}_{i}=0\}

the set of indices of active inequalities
u^{t}_{i}\geq 0
,
and by
\mathcal{U}^{t}_{+}\mathrel{{\mathop{:}}{=}}\{i\in\{1,\dots,s_{t}\}\colon u^{t}_{i}>0\}

the set of indices of inactive inequalities
u^{t}_{i}\geq 0
.
Analogous definitions hold of
\mathcal{V}^{t}_{0}
and
\mathcal{V}^{t}_{+}
.
By
\mathcal{D}^{t}\mathrel{{\mathop{:}}{=}}\mathcal{U}^{t}_{0}\cap\mathcal{V}^{t}_{0}
we denote
the set of indices of non-strict (degenerate) complementarity pairs.
Thus we have the partitioning

\{1,\dots,s_{t}\}=\mathcal{U}^{t}_{+}\cup\mathcal{V}^{t}_{+}\cup\mathcal{D}^{t}
.

In the following we define constraint qualifications for the counterpart MPCC.
The standard definitions say that MPCC-LICQ and MPCC-MFCQ are LICQ and MFCQ,
respectively, for the so-called
tightened NLP
(see
[
11
]
)
with associated Jacobian

\displaystyle J(t,u^{t},v^{t})=\begin{bmatrix}\partial_{1}c_{\mathcal{E}}&\partial_{2}c_{\mathcal{E}}P_{\mathcal{U}^{t}_{+}}^{T}&\partial_{2}c_{\mathcal{E}}P_{\mathcal{U}^{t}_{0}}^{T}&\partial_{2}c_{\mathcal{E}}P_{\mathcal{V}^{t}_{+}}^{T}&\partial_{2}c_{\mathcal{E}}P_{\mathcal{V}^{t}_{0}}^{T}\\
\partial_{1}c_{\mathcal{A}}&\partial_{2}c_{\mathcal{A}}P_{\mathcal{U}^{t}_{+}}^{T}&\partial_{2}c_{\mathcal{A}}P_{\mathcal{U}^{t}_{0}}^{T}&\partial_{2}c_{\mathcal{A}}P_{\mathcal{V}^{t}_{+}}^{T}&\partial_{2}c_{\mathcal{A}}P_{\mathcal{V}^{t}_{0}}^{T}\\
\partial_{1}c_{\mathcal{Z}}&[\partial_{2}c_{\mathcal{Z}}-I]P_{\mathcal{U}^{t}_{+}}^{T}&[\partial_{2}c_{\mathcal{Z}}-I]P_{\mathcal{U}^{t}_{0}}^{T}&[\partial_{2}c_{\mathcal{Z}}+I]P_{\mathcal{V}^{t}_{+}}^{T}&[\partial_{2}c_{\mathcal{Z}}+I]P_{\mathcal{V}^{t}_{0}}^{T}\\
0&0&I&0&0\\
0&0&0&0&I\end{bmatrix},

where
P_{\mathcal{S}}\in\mathbb{R}^{\lvert\mathcal{S}\rvert\times s_{t}}
denotes the projector
onto the subspace defined by
\mathcal{S}\subseteq\{1,\dots,s_{t}\}

and all partial darivatives are evaluated at
(t,u^{t}+v^{t})
.
This Jacobian will be needed in
Section

5.2

to formulate second order conditions.
Here we exploit the two unit blocks
to state constraint qualifications in a more compact form as in
[
8
]
.

Definition 18

(MPCC-LICQ for (
I-MPCC
), see
[
11
]
)
.

We say that the
MPCC-LICQ
holds for (
I-MPCC
)
at a feasible point
(t,u^{t},v^{t})
if

\displaystyle J_{\textup{mpcc}}(t,u^{t},v^{t})

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}\partial_{1}c_{\mathcal{E}}&\partial_{2}c_{\mathcal{E}}P_{\mathcal{U}^{t}_{+}}^{T}&\partial_{2}c_{\mathcal{E}}P_{\mathcal{V}^{t}_{+}}^{T}\\
\partial_{1}c_{\mathcal{A}}&\partial_{2}c_{\mathcal{A}}P_{\mathcal{U}^{t}_{+}}^{T}&\partial_{2}c_{\mathcal{A}}P_{\mathcal{V}^{t}_{+}}^{T}\\
\partial_{1}c_{\mathcal{Z}}&[\partial_{2}c_{\mathcal{Z}}-I]P_{\mathcal{U}^{t}_{+}}^{T}&[\partial_{2}c_{\mathcal{Z}}+I]P_{\mathcal{V}^{t}_{+}}^{T}\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle\in\mathbb{R}^{(m_{1}+\lvert\mathcal{A}\rvert+s_{t})\times(n_{t}+\lvert\mathcal{U}^{t}_{+}\rvert+\lvert\mathcal{V}^{t}_{+}\rvert)}

has full row rank
m_{1}+\lvert\mathcal{A}\rvert+s_{t}
.
Here all partial derivatives are evaluated at
(t,u^{t}+v^{t})
.

Definition 19

(MPCC-MFCQ for (
I-MPCC
), see
[
11
]
)
.

We say that the
MPCC-MFCQ
holds for (
I-MPCC
)
at a feasible point
(t,u^{t},v^{t})
if

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}\partial_{1}c_{\mathcal{E}}&\partial_{2}c_{\mathcal{E}}P_{\mathcal{U}^{t}_{+}}^{T}&\partial_{2}c_{\mathcal{E}}P_{\mathcal{V}^{t}_{+}}^{T}\\
\partial_{1}c_{\mathcal{Z}}&[\partial_{2}c_{\mathcal{Z}}-I]P_{\mathcal{U}^{t}_{+}}^{T}&[\partial_{2}c_{\mathcal{Z}}+I]P_{\mathcal{V}^{t}_{+}}^{T}\end{bmatrix}\in\mathbb{R}^{(m_{1}+s_{t})\times(n_{t}+\lvert\mathcal{U}^{t}_{+}\rvert+\lvert\mathcal{V}^{t}_{+}\rvert)}

has full row rank
m_{1}+s_{t}
and if there exists a vector
d\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{t}+\lvert\mathcal{U}^{t}_{+}\rvert+\lvert\mathcal{V}^{t}_{+}\rvert}
such that

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}\partial_{1}c_{\mathcal{E}}&\partial_{2}c_{\mathcal{E}}P_{\mathcal{U}^{t}_{+}}^{T}&\partial_{2}c_{\mathcal{E}}P_{\mathcal{V}^{t}_{+}}^{T}\\
\partial_{1}c_{\mathcal{Z}}&[\partial_{2}c_{\mathcal{Z}}-I]P_{\mathcal{U}^{t}_{+}}^{T}&[\partial_{2}c_{\mathcal{Z}}+I]P_{\mathcal{V}^{t}_{+}}^{T}\end{bmatrix}d

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}\partial_{1}c_{\mathcal{A}}&\partial_{2}c_{\mathcal{A}}P_{\mathcal{U}^{t}_{+}}^{T}&\partial_{2}c_{\mathcal{A}}P_{\mathcal{V}^{t}_{+}}^{T}\end{bmatrix}d

\displaystyle>0.

Again all partial derivatives are evaluated at
(t,u^{t}+v^{t})
.

As with LIKQ and IDKQ for (
I-NLP
), MPCC-MFCQ is weaker then MPCC-LICQ for the counterpart MPCC of
(
I-NLP
).
The latter fact is well known, and can also be seen easily by rewriting
Example

8
as the counterpart MPCC and checking the above
conditions.

3.2
Counterpart MPCC for the Abs-Normal NLP with Inequality Slacks

Using the same approach as in the preceding paragraph, we formulate the counterpart MPCC of (
E-NLP
).

Definition 20

(Counterpart MPCC of (
E-NLP
))
.

The
counterpart MPCC
of the abs-normal NLP (
E-NLP
) reads:

\displaystyle\smash[b]{\min_{t,w,u^{t},v^{t},u^{w},v^{w}}}\ f(t)\quad\textup{s.t.}\quad

\displaystyle c_{\mathcal{E}}(t,u^{t}+v^{t})=0,

\displaystyle c_{\mathcal{I}}(t,u^{t}+v^{t})-(u^{w}+v^{w})=0,

\displaystyle c_{\mathcal{Z}}(t,u^{t}+v^{t})-(u^{t}-v^{t})=0,

(E-MPCC)

\displaystyle w-(u^{w}-v^{w})=0,

\displaystyle 0\leq u^{t}\perp v^{t}\geq 0,

\displaystyle 0\leq u^{w}\perp v^{w}\geq 0,

where
u^{t},v^{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{s_{t}}
and
u^{w},v^{w}\in\mathbb{R}^{m_{2}}
.

The feasible set of (
E-MPCC
) is a lifting of
\mathcal{F}_{\textup{mpcc}}
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\textup{e-mpcc}}

\displaystyle\mathrel{{\mathop{:}}{=}}\left\{\,(t,w,u^{t},v^{t},u^{w},v^{w})\,\left|\begin{array}[]{l}c_{\mathcal{E}}(t,u^{t}+v^{t})=0,\ c_{\mathcal{I}}(t,u^{t}+v^{t})=u^{w}+v^{w},\\
c_{\mathcal{Z}}(t,u^{t}+v^{t})=u^{t}-v^{t},\ w=u^{w}-v^{w},\\
0\leq u^{t}\perp v^{t}\geq 0,\ 0\leq u^{w}\perp v^{w}\geq 0\end{array}\right.\!\right\}

\displaystyle:=\left\{\,(t,w,u^{t},v^{t},u^{w},v^{w})\,\left|\begin{array}[]{l}(t,u^{t},v^{t})\in\mathcal{F}_{\textup{mpcc}},\ c_{\mathcal{I}}(t,u^{t}+v^{t})=u^{w}+v^{w},\\
w=u^{w}-v^{w},\ 0\leq u^{w}\perp v^{w}\geq 0\end{array}\right.\!\right\}.

Clearly, the homeomorphism between
\mathcal{F}_{\textup{mpcc}}
and
\mathcal{F}_{\textup{abs}}

extends to
\mathcal{F}_{\textup{e-mpcc}}
and
\mathcal{F}_{\textup{e-abs}}
.

Lemma 21
.

Given an abs-normal NLP (
E-NLP
)
and its counterpart MPCC (
E-MPCC
),
we have a homeomorphism
\bar{\phi}:\mathcal{F}_{\textup{e-mpcc}}\to\mathcal{F}_{\textup{e-abs}}
defined as

\displaystyle\bar{\phi}(t,w,u^{t},v^{t},u^{w},v^{w})

\displaystyle=(t,w,u^{t}-v^{t},u^{w}-v^{w}),

\displaystyle\bar{\phi}^{-1}(t,w,z^{t},z^{w})

\displaystyle=(t,w,[z^{t}]^{+},[z^{t}]^{-},[z^{w}]^{+},[z^{w}]^{-}).

Proof.

Obvious.
∎

Lemma 22
.

MPCC-LICQ for (
E-MPCC
) at a feasible point

y=(t,w,u^{t},v^{t},u^{w},v^{w})
is full row rank of

\displaystyle J_{\textup{e-mpcc}}(y)

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}\partial_{1}c_{\mathcal{E}}&0&\partial_{2}c_{\mathcal{E}}P_{\mathcal{U}^{t}_{+}}^{T}&\partial_{2}c_{\mathcal{E}}P_{\mathcal{V}^{t}_{+}}^{T}&0&0\\
\partial_{1}c_{\mathcal{I}}&0&\partial_{2}c_{\mathcal{I}}P_{\mathcal{U}^{t}_{+}}^{T}&\partial_{2}c_{\mathcal{I}}P_{\mathcal{V}^{t}_{+}}^{T}&-P_{\mathcal{U}^{w}_{+}}^{T}&-P_{\mathcal{V}^{w}_{+}}^{T}\\
\partial_{1}c_{\mathcal{Z}}&0&[\partial_{2}c_{\mathcal{Z}}-I]P_{\mathcal{U}^{t}_{+}}^{T}&[\partial_{2}c_{\mathcal{Z}}+I]P_{\mathcal{V}^{t}_{+}}^{T}&0&0\\
0&I&0&0&-P_{\mathcal{U}^{w}_{+}}^{T}&+P_{\mathcal{V}^{w}_{+}}^{T}\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle\in\mathbb{R}^{(m_{1}+m_{2}+s_{t}+m_{2})\times(n_{t}+m_{2}+\lvert\mathcal{U}^{t}_{+}\rvert+\lvert\mathcal{V}^{t}_{+}\rvert+\lvert\mathcal{U}^{w}_{+}\rvert+\lvert\mathcal{V}^{w}_{+}\rvert)},

where all partial derivatives are evaluated at
(t,u^{t}+v^{t})
.

Proof.

We set
x=(t,w)
,
u=(u^{t},u^{w})
,
v=(v^{t},v^{w})
as well as
\bar{f}(x)=f(t)
,

\bar{c}_{\mathcal{E}}(x,u+v)=\begin{pmatrix}c_{\mathcal{E}}(t,u^{t}+v^{t})\\
c_{\mathcal{I}}(t,u^{t}+v^{t})-(u^{w}+v^{w}))\end{pmatrix},\quad\bar{c}_{\mathcal{Z}}(x,u+v)=\begin{pmatrix}c_{\mathcal{Z}}(t,u^{t}+v^{t})\\
w\end{pmatrix}.

Then, (
E-MPCC
) becomes

\displaystyle\smash[b]{\min_{x,u,v}}\ \bar{f}(x)\quad\textup{s.t.}\quad

\displaystyle\bar{c}_{\mathcal{E}}(x,u+v)=0,

\displaystyle\bar{c}_{\mathcal{Z}}(x,u+v)-(u-v)=0,

(
\overline{\text{E-MPCC}}
)

\displaystyle 0\leq u\perp v\geq 0,

and we can compute the Jacobian from
Definition

18
using the special
structure of (
E-MPCC
).
The resulting matrix has the stated form, except that the last four columns
belong to variables
(u^{t},v^{t},u^{w},v^{w})
rather than
(u,v)=(u^{t},u^{w},v^{t},v^{w})
.
∎

Like LIKQ for (

and like IDKQ for (
E-NLP

is equivalent to MPCC-LICQ since no inequalities

In this section we have a closer look at relations between abs-normal NLPs

4.1
Relations of General Abs-Normal NLP and MPCC

Here we use the variables
x
and
z
instead of
t
and
z^{t}

to shorten notation because we do not consider inequality slacks.
Then the general abs-normal NLP (
I-NLP
) becomes:

\displaystyle\smash[b]{\min_{x,z}}\ f(x)\quad\textup{s.t.}\quad

\displaystyle c_{\mathcal{E}}(x,\lvert z\rvert)=0,

\displaystyle c_{\mathcal{I}}(x,\lvert z\rvert)\geq 0,

\displaystyle c_{\mathcal{Z}}(x,\lvert z\rvert)-z=0.

The counterpart MPCC (
I-MPCC
) reads:

\displaystyle\smash[b]{\min_{x,u,v}}\ f(x)\quad\textup{s.t.}\quad

\displaystyle c_{\mathcal{E}}(x,u+v)=0,

\displaystyle c_{\mathcal{I}}(x,u+v)\geq 0,

\displaystyle c_{\mathcal{Z}}(x,u+v)-(u-v)=0,

\displaystyle 0\leq u\perp v\geq 0.

We obtain the following relations of kink qualifications
and MPCC constraint qualifications.

Theorem 25

(LIKQ
\iff
MPCC-LICQ)
.

LIKQ for (
I-NLP
) at
x\in\mathcal{F}_{\textup{abs}}
is equivalent to
MPCC-LICQ for (
I-MPCC
) at

(x,u,v)=(x,[z(x)]^{+},[z(x)]^{-})\in\mathcal{F}_{\textup{mpcc}}
.

Proof.

Setting
y\mathrel{{\mathop{:}}{=}}(x,u+v)
and
r\mathrel{{\mathop{:}}{=}}m_{1}+\lvert\mathcal{A}\rvert+s
,
MPCC-LICQ for the counterpart MPCC is

\displaystyle\rank\begin{bmatrix}\partial_{1}c_{\mathcal{E}}(y)&\partial_{2}c_{\mathcal{E}}(y)P_{\mathcal{U}_{+}}^{T}&\partial_{2}c_{\mathcal{E}}(y)P_{\mathcal{V}_{+}}^{T}\\
\partial_{1}c_{\mathcal{A}}(y)&\partial_{2}c_{\mathcal{A}}(y)P_{\mathcal{U}_{+}}^{T}&\partial_{2}c_{\mathcal{A}}(y)P_{\mathcal{V}_{+}}^{T}\\
\partial_{1}c_{\mathcal{Z}}(y)&[\partial_{2}c_{\mathcal{Z}}(y)-I]P_{\mathcal{U}_{+}}^{T}&[\partial_{2}c_{\mathcal{Z}}(y)+I]P_{\mathcal{V}_{+}}^{T}\end{bmatrix}=r.

By negating the second column and combining it with the third column,
this is equivalent to

\displaystyle\rank\begin{bmatrix}\partial_{1}c_{\mathcal{E}}(y)&-\partial_{2}c_{\mathcal{E}}(y)\Sigma P_{\mathcal{U}_{+}\cup\mathcal{V}_{+}}^{T}\\
\partial_{1}c_{\mathcal{A}}(y)&-\partial_{2}c_{\mathcal{A}}(y)\Sigma P_{\mathcal{U}_{+}\cup\mathcal{V}_{+}}^{T}\\
\partial_{1}c_{\mathcal{Z}}(y)&[I-\partial_{2}c_{\mathcal{Z}}(y)\Sigma]P_{\mathcal{U}_{+}\cup\mathcal{V}_{+}}^{T}\end{bmatrix}=r

and, by non-singularity of
I-\partial_{2}c_{\mathcal{Z}}(y)\Sigma
, to

\displaystyle\rank\begin{bmatrix}\partial_{1}c_{\mathcal{E}}(y)&-\partial_{2}c_{\mathcal{E}}(y)\Sigma P_{\mathcal{U}_{+}\cup\mathcal{V}_{+}}^{T}\\
\partial_{1}c_{\mathcal{A}}(y)&-\partial_{2}c_{\mathcal{A}}(y)\Sigma P_{\mathcal{U}_{+}\cup\mathcal{V}_{+}}^{T}\\
[I-\partial_{2}c_{\mathcal{Z}}(y)\Sigma]^{-1}\partial_{1}c_{\mathcal{Z}}(y)&P_{\mathcal{U}_{+}\cup\mathcal{V}_{+}}^{T}\end{bmatrix}=r.

Next, we use the third row to eliminate the entries above

P_{\mathcal{U}_{+}\cup\mathcal{V}_{+}}^{T}
to obtain

\displaystyle\rank\begin{bmatrix}\partial_{1}c_{\mathcal{E}}(y)+\partial_{2}c_{\mathcal{E}}(y)\Sigma[I-\partial_{2}c_{\mathcal{Z}}(y)\Sigma]^{-1}\partial_{1}c_{\mathcal{Z}}(y)&0\\
\partial_{1}c_{\mathcal{A}}(y)+\partial_{2}c_{\mathcal{A}}(y)\Sigma[I-\partial_{2}c_{\mathcal{Z}}(y)\Sigma]^{-1}\partial_{1}c_{\mathcal{Z}}(y)&0\\
[I-\partial_{2}c_{\mathcal{Z}}(y)\Sigma]^{-1}\partial_{1}c_{\mathcal{Z}}(y)&P_{\mathcal{U}_{+}\cup\mathcal{V}_{+}}^{T}\end{bmatrix}=r,

which we can write with
u+v=\lvert z\rvert=\lvert z(x)\rvert
as

\displaystyle\rank\begin{bmatrix}\partial_{x}c_{\mathcal{E}}(x,\lvert z(x)\rvert)&0\\
\partial_{x}c_{\mathcal{A}}(x,\lvert z(x)\rvert)&0\\
\partial_{x}z(x)&P_{\mathcal{U}_{+}\cup\mathcal{V}_{+}}^{T}\end{bmatrix}=r.

Finally, since
\alpha=\mathcal{D}
is the complement of
\mathcal{U}_{+}\cup\mathcal{V}_{+}
,
this is equivalent to

\displaystyle\rank\begin{bmatrix}\partial_{x}c_{\mathcal{E}}(x,\lvert z(x)\rvert)\\
\partial_{x}c_{\mathcal{A}}(x,\lvert z(x)\rvert)\\
[e_{i}^{T}\partial_{x}z(x)]_{i\in\alpha}\end{bmatrix}=m_{1}+\lvert\mathcal{A}\rvert+\lvert\alpha\rvert,

which is LIKQ for the abs-normal NLP.
∎

Theorem 26

(IDKQ
\iff
MPCC-MFCQ)
.

IDKQ for (
I-NLP
) at
x\in\mathcal{F}_{\textup{abs}}
is equivalent to
MPCC-MFCQ for (
I-MPCC
) at

(x,u,v)=(x,[z(x)]^{+},[z(x)]^{-})\in\mathcal{F}_{\textup{mpcc}}
.

Proof.

Again with
y\mathrel{{\mathop{:}}{=}}(x,u+v)
,
MPCC-MFCQ for the counterpart MPCC is

1.

full row rank of

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}\partial_{1}c_{\mathcal{E}}(y)&\partial_{2}c_{\mathcal{E}}(y)P_{\mathcal{U}_{+}}^{T}&\partial_{2}c_{\mathcal{E}}(y)P_{\mathcal{V}_{+}}^{T}\\
\partial_{1}c_{\mathcal{Z}}(y)&[\partial_{2}c_{\mathcal{Z}}(y)-I]P_{\mathcal{U}_{+}}^{T}&[\partial_{2}c_{\mathcal{Z}}(y)+I]P_{\mathcal{V}_{+}}^{T}\end{bmatrix}\in\mathbb{R}^{(m_{1}+s)\times(n+\lvert\mathcal{U}_{+}\cup\mathcal{V}_{+}\rvert)}.

As in the proof of
Theorem

25
,
this is seen to be full row rank of

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}\partial_{x}c_{\mathcal{E}}(x,\lvert z(x)\rvert)\\
[e_{i}^{T}\partial_{x}z(x)]_{i\in\alpha}\end{bmatrix}\in\mathbb{R}^{(m_{1}+\lvert\alpha\rvert)\times n}.

2.

the existence of a vector

d=(d_{x},d_{u},d_{v})\in\mathbb{R}^{n+\lvert\mathcal{U}_{+}\cup\mathcal{V}_{+}\rvert}
such that

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}\partial_{1}c_{\mathcal{E}}(y)&\partial_{2}c_{\mathcal{E}}(y)P_{\mathcal{U}_{+}}^{T}&\partial_{2}c_{\mathcal{E}}(y)P_{\mathcal{V}_{+}}^{T}\\
\partial_{1}c_{\mathcal{Z}}(y)&[\partial_{2}c_{\mathcal{Z}}(y)-I]P_{\mathcal{U}_{+}}^{T}&[\partial_{2}c_{\mathcal{Z}}(y)+I]P_{\mathcal{V}_{+}}^{T}\end{bmatrix}d=0,

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}\partial_{1}c_{\mathcal{A}}(y)&\partial_{2}c_{\mathcal{A}}(y)P_{\mathcal{U}_{+}}^{T}&\partial_{2}c_{\mathcal{A}}(y)P_{\mathcal{V}_{+}}^{T}\\
\end{bmatrix}d>0.

We combine
d_{u}
and
-d_{v}
to
d_{uv}\in\mathbb{R}^{\lvert\mathcal{U}_{+}\cup\mathcal{V}_{+}\rvert}
.
Then this is equivalent to

\displaystyle\partial_{1}c_{\mathcal{E}}(y)d_{x}+\partial_{2}c_{\mathcal{E}}(y)\Sigma P_{\mathcal{U}_{+}\cup\mathcal{V}_{+}}^{T}d_{uv}

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle\partial_{1}c_{\mathcal{Z}}(y)d_{x}-[I-\partial_{2}c_{\mathcal{Z}}(y)\Sigma]P_{\mathcal{U}_{+}\cup\mathcal{V}_{+}}^{T}d_{uv}

6
Conclusions and Outlook

We have shown that general abs-normal NLPs
are essentially the same problem class as MPCCs.
The two problem classes have corresponding constraint qualifications,
stationarity concepts, and optimality conditions of first and second order.
We have also shown that the slack reformulation
from
[
9
]
,
which is useful to simplify derivations under LIKQ,
does not preserve IDKQ and has other subtle drawbacks
like non-uniqueness of slack variables.
We have not considered counterpart abs-normal NLPs of general MPCCs
as in
[
8
]
.
This would provide a different perspective on the equivalence
of the two problem classes but no additional insight.
It is hoped that the identities revealed may serve to transfer algorithms for the solution of MPCCs to the young field of abs-normal forms and abs-normal NLP. Vice versa, active signature algorithms for abs-normal forms, such as
SALMIN
[
5
]
, may be applicable to MPCCs.
Relations between the two problem classes under weaker
constraint qualifications of Abadie type and Guignard type
are the subject of part two of this research and are put forward in a companion article.

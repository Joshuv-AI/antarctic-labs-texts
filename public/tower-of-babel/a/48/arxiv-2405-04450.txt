A natural model for curved inflation
Quentin Vigneron, Julien Larena

Abstract
Inflationary models with a non-zero background curvature require additional hypothesis or parameters compared to flat inflation and the procedure to construct them cannot be as simple as in the flat case. For this reason, there is no consensus on the primordial power spectrum that should be considered at large scales in a curved Universe. In this letter, we propose a model of curved inflation in which the usual canonical quantization and Bunch–Davies vacuum choice of the flat case can be considered. The framework is a recently proposed modification of general relativity in which a non-dynamical topological term is added to the Einstein equation. The model is universal as it is the same for any background curvature, and no additional parameters or hypothesis on the initial spatial curvature are introduced. This gives a natural and simple solution to the problem of constructing curved inflation, and at the same time provides an additional argument for this topological modification of general relativity.

Cosmological inflation successfully explains the Cosmic Microwave Background (CMB) anisotropies, and provides at the same time a solution to the flatness and horizon problems. The goal of this letter is to propose a curved inflationary scenario bypassing the problems previously mentioned, as simple as flat inflation, and without introducing any additional parameters. The philosophy of our approach lies in a slight modification of the Einstein equation that was proposed by

Details of the derivations can be found in the Appendices. Throughout this letter, we use Greek letters for spacetime indices, and Roman letters for spatial indices.

Topo-GR

Figure 1
:
Qualitative difference on the role of matter between GR (left) and topo-GR (right). In GR, spacetime is Ricci flat in vacuum, i.e.
R_{\mu\nu}=0
, and matter induces a deviation from flatness. In topo-GR, in vacuum, spacetime is curved by topology, i.e.
R_{\mu\nu}=\bar{R}_{\mu\nu}

The original motivation for the modification of GR proposed in
[
24

is not related to inflation, but is to obtain a relativistic field equation which admits a non-relativistic limit in any topology, something not possible in GR (see Sec. 4 in
[
24

R_{\mu\nu}
in the Einstein equation is replaced by the difference between that tensor and a reference Ricci curvature
\bar{R}_{\mu\nu}
that is fixed and depends on the spatial covering space of the spatial topology, i.e.
R_{\mu\nu}\rightarrow R_{\mu\nu}-\bar{R}_{\mu\nu}
, leading to equation (
1

1
. We direct the reader to
[

24

1

The precise way
\bar{R}_{\mu\nu}
is chosen as function of the topology is described in Sec. 5.2.2 of
[
24
]

\bar{R}_{\mu\nu}
takes the form
[

\displaystyle\bar{R}_{\mu\nu}=2K\left(\gamma_{\mu\nu}+\mathcal{L}_{X}\gamma_{\mu\nu}\right),

(3)

where

is the background comoving spatial metric. For

= 0, we have

Qualitatively, within topo-GR, matter does not directly curve spacetime, as in GR, but only induces deviations (not necessarily small) from the curvature “imposed” by topology, i.e. deviations from
\bar{R}_{\mu\nu}

1

Curvature deviation perturbation

We define the
curvature deviation perturbation
as

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathcal{R}}\coloneqq\mathcal{R}-K\mathcal{C}.

(18)

Physically,
\tilde{\mathcal{R}}
is related to the difference between the first order spatial scalar curvature
\delta\left({{}^{(3)}R}\right)
and the first order spatial reference curvature

\displaystyle\left[\delta\left({\mathcal{R}^{(3)}}\right)-\delta\left(g^{ij}\bar{R}_{ij}\right)\right]_{\rm c}

\displaystyle=\frac{4}{a^{2}}\Delta\tilde{\mathcal{R}}.

(19)

For a flat background,

reduces to
\mathcal{R}
. But while in GR,
\mathcal{R}
is the natural variable to use to study inflation for

, in topo-GR this becomes

for any

There are three main reasons for this:

1.

\tilde{\mathcal{R}}
is conserved on super-Hubble scales for shear-free adiabatic perturbations or single field inflation, without assuming additionally the curvature scale to be super-Hubble. This results from

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}\tilde{\mathcal{R}}^{\prime}=\frac{a^{2}\kappa}{3(1+w)}\left[c_{\rm s}^{2}\delta\rho_{\rm c}+\delta p_{\not=ad}+\frac{2}{3}\left(\Delta+3K\right)\Pi\right].

(20)

For adiabatic perturbations, that formula also shows that the density perturbation after inflation is directly generated by
\tilde{\mathcal{R}}
for any
K
, as with
\mathcal{R}
in GR for
K=0
.

2.

With a single scalar field as a source,
\tilde{\mathcal{R}}
is solution of the Mukhanov-Sasaki equation for any
K
:

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathcal{R}}^{\prime\prime}+\frac{2z^{\prime}}{z}\tilde{\mathcal{R}}^{\prime}-\Delta\tilde{\mathcal{R}}=0\qquad\forall K,

(21)

where

. This equation for
\tilde{\mathcal{R}}
is formally equivalent to that of
\mathcal{R}
in GR for a flat background, but with the Laplacian depending on spatial curvature.

3.

\tilde{\mathcal{R}}
is one of the two free scalar fields to appear in the action at second order (see next section).

The fact that
\tilde{\mathcal{R}}
is the relevant variable to consider in topo-GR rather than
\mathcal{R}
is consistent with the main idea of the theory: what matters for the dynamics is not the (spatial/spacetime) curvature, but the difference between that curvature and the reference curvature.

is the Hubble rate during inflation, and we have defined the usual slow-roll parameters
\epsilon\coloneqq-\dot{H}/H^{2}
and
\eta\coloneqq\epsilon-\ddot{\varphi}/(H\dot{\varphi})
; moreover, we set
\mu\coloneqq 3/2+\epsilon
and
\nu\coloneqq 3/2+3\epsilon-\eta
.

This is is an unambiguous way, free from new parameters, of defining the scaling and amplitude of a spectrum resulting from a single field slow-roll inflation in any curved space, something out of reach in GR. The power spectrum of the additional scalar mode
\mathcal{C}
is fully fixed by the slow-roll parameters and the curvature, and no additional parameters are needed. Initial conditions on the density perturbation are obtained from
\mathcal{P}_{\tilde{\mathcal{R}}}
using relation (
20
).

At first order in the slow-roll parameters, we have the following ratios

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{K\mathcal{C}}/\mathcal{P}_{\tilde{\mathcal{R}}}

\displaystyle=\epsilon{\left(\frac{K}{k^{2}-3K}\right)^{2}},

(30)

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{h}/\mathcal{P}_{\tilde{\mathcal{R}}}

\displaystyle=16\epsilon\left(\frac{k}{\sqrt{k^{2}+6K}}\right)^{3}.

(31)

The tensor-to-scalar ratio
r
features a scale dependence for scales approaching the curvature scale, with an enhancement for
K<0
, similarly to the GR case
[
17
]
. While there is a divergence for
k^{2}=-6K
, it might be avoided for specific closed hyperbolic topologies for which the minimal wave number of tensor modes would be
k_{\rm min}^{2}>-6K
(e.g.
[
27
]
). Additionally, the consistency relation between
r
and the scaling index
n_{h}=-2\epsilon
of tensor modes features a dependence on the wave number for
K\not=0
. This prediction would be observable only for a current curvature scale comparable to the CMB scale.

But most importantly, while topo-GR features one additional gauge invariant variable
\mathcal{C}
, the amplitude of its power spectrum is negligible compared to that of the curvature deviation perturbation. Additionally,
\mathcal{C}
decays during radiation and matter dominations on sub-Hubble scales. Therefore, its effects on scalar mode fluctuations can be neglected from the start of inflation to the late time Universe, and considering
\mathcal{C}=0
is a good approximation. In this case
\tilde{\mathcal{R}}
exactly corresponds to the usual curvature perturbation
\mathcal{R}
, and the situation is even closer to that of GR for
K=0
. Especially, the scale dependence and the amplitude of
\mathcal{R}
is equivalent for any
K
. This is the main difference between our model and other curved inflationary scenarios in which
\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{R}}
depends on
K
(see e.g.
[
10
]
)

For this reason, observations will only be able to distinguish between the inflationary model presented in this work (and therefore the topo-GR theory) and current curved inflationary models in GR if
\Omega_{K}
is large enough. Currently,
\Omega_{K}
is constrained to be of the order of
10^{-3}
or less within GR
[
1
]
or topo-GR
[
25
]
, and future surveys such as the Euclid space telescope might allow us to gain one order of magnitude on this constraint
[
28
]
. Since this leads to a very large curvature scale, this might already be too small due to the cosmic variance present at large scales. If so, this model of inflation would still have a theoretical interest, using Occam’s Razor argument, as no additional parameters or hypothesis on the initial curvature are needed compared to curved inflationary models in GR to incorporate curvature into inflation. Post-inflationary constraints would then have to be used to probe topo-GR. With this regard, studying the dynamics of reheating might be an interesting perspective. The main difference we expect with reheating scenarios in GR would come from the presence of the second reference connection
\bar{\nabla}
in topo-GR. This offers the possibility of reheating scenarios based on a coupling between the inflaton and that connection, as has been studied within the broader context of Metric Affine Gravity theories
[
29
, e.g.]
. We let this study for a future work.

Conclusion

Curved inflationary scenarios are essential to the coherence of the inflation hypothesis, but cannot be easily constructed within general relativity.
In this letter, we have proposed a model of curved inflation which keeps the simplicity of flat inflation while allowing for canonical quantization with any background spatial curvature. For this, a non-dynamical topological term is added to the Einstein equation.
The main features of the model are the following: (i) no bounce and no upper bound on the Hubble radius exists during inflation and a Bunch-Davis vacuum condition is well-defined for any
K
; (ii) there exists a variable
\tilde{\mathcal{R}}
, reducing to the curvature perturbation for
K=0
, that is conserved on super-Hubble scales and is solution of the Mukhanov-Sasaki equation for any
K
; (iii) a unique well-defined primordial power spectrum is obtained for any
K
, with the same scale dependence for scalar modes in each case.

This model of curved inflation has the same number of degrees of freedom as in the
Standard Model,
and is universal in the sense it is the same regardless of the background spatial curvature, and this with the same procedure than in the flat GR case. Since one of the successes of (flat) inflation is its simplicity, the fact that our model manages to keep this simplicity for any curvature is a strength, which gives a natural and simple answer to how curved inflation should be built.

We advocate that this is also a strong argument for considering a non-dynamical topological term in the Einstein equation in the form proposed by the topo-GR theory, when non-asymptotically flat spacetimes and non-Euclidean topologies are considered.

Wave equation for

Consider a scalar field

\displaystyle T^{\mu}{}_{\nu}=\nabla^{\mu}\phi\nabla_{\nu}\phi-\frac{1}{2}\nabla^{\alpha}\phi\nabla_{\alpha}\phi\,\delta^{\mu}_{\nu}-V\,\delta^{\mu}_{\nu}.

(C1)

We define the perturbation of the scalar field in the longitudinal gauge as

\displaystyle\delta\rho_{l}=\frac{1}{a^{2}}\left(\varphi^{\prime}\delta\varphi^{\prime}-\varphi^{\prime 2}\Phi\right)+\partial_{\varphi}V\delta\varphi\quad;\quad\mathcal{V}=-\frac{\delta\varphi}{\varphi^{\prime}}\quad;\quad\Pi=\Pi^{i}=\Pi^{ij}=0\ ;

(C2)

The first order field equations (
1

1

(C3)

\displaystyle\Psi^{\prime}+\mathcal{H}\Psi-\frac{1}{2}\varphi^{\prime}\delta\varphi=4K\mathcal{H}\mathcal{C}+K\mathcal{C}^{\prime},

(C4)

\displaystyle\Phi-\Psi=-4K\mathcal{C}.

(C5)

The derivative of equation (

\displaystyle\Psi^{\prime\prime}+\mathcal{H}\Psi^{\prime}+\mathcal{H}^{\prime}\Psi-\frac{1}{2}\left[\varphi^{\prime}\delta\varphi^{\prime}-\left(2\mathcal{H}\varphi^{\prime}+a^{2}\partial_{\varphi}V\right)\delta\varphi\right]=K\left(\mathcal{C}^{\prime\prime}+4\mathcal{H}\mathcal{C}^{\prime}+4\mathcal{H}^{\prime}\mathcal{C}\right).

The left-hand-sides of equations (

\displaystyle\left\{\eqref{eq_3}+\frac{\mathcal{H}}{\varphi^{\prime}}\eqref{eq_4}+\frac{2z^{\prime}}{z}\eqref{eq_2}-\eqref{eq_1}\right\},

Appendix D
Second order action

The action at second order of topo-GR can be calculated following the same approach as in GR, within the ADM formalism
[
30
]
. From the action (
22
) of topo-GR, we obtain

\displaystyle S=\frac{1}{2}\int\textrm{d}\tau\textrm{d}x^{3}\sqrt{s}\Big[

\displaystyle N\left(\mathcal{R}^{(3)}_{ij}-\bar{R}_{ij}\right)s^{ij}+\frac{1}{N}\left(E^{ij}E_{ij}-E^{2}\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{N}\left(\phi^{\prime}+N^{i}\partial_{i}\phi\right)^{2}+ND_{i}\phi D^{i}\phi-2NV

\displaystyle\ +\frac{1}{N}N^{i}N^{j}\bar{R}_{ij}-\frac{2}{N}N^{i}\bar{R}_{i0}+\frac{1}{N}\bar{R}_{00}\Big],

(D1)

where
s_{ij}
is the spatial metric with
\mathcal{R}^{(3)}_{ij}
its Ricci curvature and
\sqrt{s}\coloneqq\sqrt{{\rm det}\,s_{ij}}
,
N
is the lapse,
N^{i}
is the shift, and

\displaystyle E_{ij}\coloneqq\frac{1}{2}\gamma^{\prime}_{ij}-D_{(i}N_{j)},\quad E\coloneqq E^{i}{}_{i}.

(D2)

We first consider only scalar modes. The goal is to develop the action (
D1
) up to second order in the first order variables
\mathcal{R}
and
\mathcal{C}
. For this, the first and second orders of the reference curvature as functions of these variables are needed. Because the reference curvature is a fixed tensor, its perturbation orders are pure gauge. This means that in a general gauge, up to second order,

is of the form
[
25
]

\displaystyle\bar{R}_{\mu\nu}=\overset{(0)}{R}_{\mu\nu}+\left[\mathcal{L}_{X}\overset{(0)}{R}_{\mu\nu}\right]+\left[\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{L}_{X}^{2}\overset{(0)}{R}_{\mu\nu}+\mathcal{L}_{Y}\overset{(0)}{R}_{\mu\nu}\right],

(D3)

\overset{(0)}{R}_{\mu\nu}=2K\gamma_{ij}

X^{\mu}
in the gauge we are choosing. The time component
X^{0}
is not present in the first order of
\bar{R}_{\mu\nu}
, and in the second order is only present in a spatial boundary term. Hence, it can be assumed to be zero in the calculation of the second order action, leading to
X^{\mu}=\delta^{\mu}_{i}X^{i}

In the comoving gauge, for which
\delta\varphi=0

\displaystyle s_{ij}=a^{2}\left[\left(1-2\mathcal{R}\right)\gamma_{ij}+2D_{i}D_{j}E\right],

(D4)

where the choice of

changes the shift but not the foliation, hence the gauge remains comoving. The choice

leads to

is defined as
\mathcal{C}\coloneqq\mathcal{X}-E

X^{i}\eqqcolon D^{i}\mathcal{X}

[
25
]
. The choice

is the standard one, and the easiest in which calculating the second order action. For this choice, we have

, and the first and second orders of the reference curvature take the form

\displaystyle\overset{(1)}{\bar{R}}_{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=4K\delta_{(\mu}^{0}\delta_{\nu)}^{i}\partial_{i}\mathcal{C}^{\prime}+4K\delta_{\mu}^{i}\delta_{\nu}^{j}\,D_{i}D_{j}\mathcal{C},

(D5)

\displaystyle\overset{(2)}{\bar{R}}_{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=2K\delta^{0}_{\mu}\delta^{0}_{\nu}\,D_{k}\mathcal{C}^{\prime}D^{k}\mathcal{C}^{\prime}+2K\left(D^{k}\mathcal{C}D_{k}D_{i}D_{j}\mathcal{C}+2D_{k}D_{i}\mathcal{C}D^{k}D_{j}\mathcal{C}\right)\delta^{i}_{\mu}\delta^{j}_{\nu}+2\overset{(2)}{\bar{R}}_{0i}\delta^{0}_{(\mu}\delta^{i}_{\nu)},

(D6)

where
\overset{(2)}{\bar{R}}_{0i}
is not needed for the calculation of the second order action.

We define the perturbations of the lapse and shift respectively as

\displaystyle N=a\left(1+\alpha\right)\quad;\quad N_{i}=a^{2}\partial_{i}\beta.

(D7)

The variation of the first order action (
D1
) with respect to
\alpha
and
\beta
gives the constraints equations, respectively as

\displaystyle\alpha=-\frac{\tilde{\mathcal{R}}^{\prime}}{\mathcal{H}},\quad\quad\Delta\left(\beta+\mathcal{C}^{\prime}\right)=-3K\mathcal{C}^{\prime}+\frac{1}{\mathcal{H}}\Delta\tilde{\mathcal{R}}-\epsilon\tilde{\mathcal{R}}^{\prime},

(D8)

which also correspond, respectively, to equations (
1
) and (
1

D1
) at second order in
\tilde{\mathcal{R}}
and
\mathcal{C}
. The gauge choice (

\displaystyle a^{2}s^{ij}

\displaystyle=\gamma^{ij}+\left[2\mathcal{R}\gamma^{ij}\right]+\left[4\mathcal{R}^{2}\gamma^{ij}\right],

(D9)

\displaystyle\overset{(1)}{\Gamma}{}^{a}_{bc}

\displaystyle=-2\delta^{a}_{(b}D_{c)}\mathcal{R}+\gamma_{bc}D^{a}\mathcal{R},

\displaystyle\frac{1}{a^{3}}\sqrt{{\rm det}\,s_{ij}}

Using

\displaystyle\int\textrm{d}\tau\textrm{d}x^{3}\sqrt{s}\left\{\frac{1}{N}\left(E^{i}{}_{j}E^{j}{}_{i}-E^{2}\right)+\left[\frac{1}{N}\left(\phi^{\prime}+N^{i}D_{i}\phi\right)^{2}+ND_{i}\phi D^{i}\phi-2NV\right]+\frac{1}{N}N^{i}N^{j}\bar{R}_{ij}\right.

Appendix E
Canonical quantization of
C

The Lagrangian of
C
features spatial derivatives of the velocity field
C^{\prime}
which cannot be removed with integration by parts. Therefore, to calculate the conjugate momentum of the field
C
, we need to come back to the general definition of the canonical momentum.

We consider a general Lagrangian depending on a field
q
, its time derivative
\dot{q}
and the spatial derivatives (first order or higher) of
q
and
\dot{q}
, i.e.
\mathcal{L}\left(q,D_{i}q,D_{i}D_{j}q,...,\dot{q},D_{i}\dot{q},D_{i}D_{j}\dot{q},...\right)
. The conjugate momentum
\pi
of
q
is defined as the functional derivative of
\mathcal{L}
with respect to
\dot{q}
, i.e. for a trial function
\phi
we have

\displaystyle\int\textrm{d}x^{3}\pi\,\phi

\displaystyle\coloneqq\int\textrm{d}x^{3}\frac{\delta\mathcal{L}}{\delta\dot{q}}\,\phi

\displaystyle:=\int\textrm{d}x^{3}\left[\partial_{\dot{q}}\mathcal{L}+\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{D_{i}\dot{q}}D_{i}+\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{D_{i}D_{j}\dot{q}}D_{i}D_{j}+...\right]\,\phi,

(E1)

which becomes, after integration by parts,

\displaystyle\int\textrm{d}x^{3}\,\pi\,\phi

\displaystyle=\int\textrm{d}x^{3}\left[\partial_{\dot{q}}\mathcal{L}-D_{i}\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{D_{i}\dot{q}}+D_{i}D_{j}\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{D_{i}D_{j}\dot{q}}+...\right]\phi.

(E2)

Up to second spatial derivatives, this yields

\displaystyle\pi\coloneqq\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial\dot{q}}-D_{i}\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial{D_{i}\dot{q}}}+D_{i}D_{j}\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial{D_{i}D_{j}\dot{q}}}+...

(E3)

Due to the dependence of
\mathcal{L}
in
D_{i}\dot{q}
, we get extra terms in the definition of
\pi
, compared to the usual formula
\pi=\partial\mathcal{L}/\partial{\dot{q}}
. Eq (
E1
) also shows that adding a boundary term in the Lagrangian, e.g.
\mathcal{L}\rightarrow\mathcal{L}+\Delta\dot{q}^{2}
, does not change the expression for the momentum.

Now using Eq (
E3
) for the Lagrangian of
C
, in the limit
\tau\rightarrow-\infty
, so that we neglect
a^{\prime\prime}/a
, i.e.

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{C}\coloneqq-\frac{1}{2}\left(3C^{\prime 2}+\frac{1}{K}C^{\prime}\Delta C^{\prime}+3C\Delta C+\frac{1}{K}C\Delta\Delta C\right),

(E4)

the conjugate momentum of
C
is

\displaystyle\pi_{C}=-\left(\Delta/K+3\right)C^{\prime}.

(E5)

Performing a canonical quantization of
C

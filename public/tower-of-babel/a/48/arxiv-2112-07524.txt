Abstract
We introduce the graph theoretical parameter of edge treewidth. This parameter occurs in a natural way as the tree-like analogue of cutwidth or, alternatively, as an edge-analogue of treewidth. We study the combinatorial properties of edge-treewidth. We first observe that edge-treewidth does not enjoy any closeness properties under the known partial ordering relations on graphs. We introduce a variant of the topological minor relation, namely, the weak topological minor relation and we prove that edge-treewidth is closed under weak topological minors. Based on this new relation we are able to provide universal obstructions for edge-treewidth. The proofs are based on the fact that edge-treewidth of a graph is parametetrically equivalent with the maximum over the treewidth and the maximum degree of the blocks of the graph. We also prove that deciding whether the edge-treewidth of a graph is at most k \color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}k is an NP -complete problem.

Introduction

A vibrant area of research in graph algorithms is dedicated to the study of structural graph parameters and their algorithmic applications. Perhaps the most prominent graph parameter is treewidth (hereafter denoted by
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{{\bf tw}}
). The importance of treewidth resides on both its combinatorial and algorithmic applications. From the algorithmic point of view, treewidth
has readily became important due to Courcelle’s theorem
[
14
]
, asserting that
every problem definable by some sentence
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}\textphi
in Monadic Second Order Logic (MSOL) can be solved in time
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\cal O}_{{\bf tw}(G),|\textphi|}(|G|)
.
1
1

1

Let
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\bf t}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{l})\in\mathbb{N}^{l}
and
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}\chi,\textpsi:\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}.

We say that
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}\chi(n)={\cal O}_{{\bf t}}(\textpsi(n))
if there exists a computable
function
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}\textphi:\mathbb{N}^{l}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}

such that
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}\chi(n)={\cal O}(\textphi({\bf t})\cdot\textpsi(n)).

Using the terminology of parameterized complexity, this means that every MSOL-definable problem
admits a fixed parameter algorithm (in short, an
FPT
-algorithm) when parameterized by treewidth.

Interestingly, there are several problems where Courcelle’s theorem does not apply.
As mentioned in
[
23
]
, problems such as
Capacitated Vertex Cover
,
Capacitated Dominating Set
,
List Coloring
, and
Boolean CSP

are
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\sf W}[1]
-hard, when parameterized by
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\bf tw}
, which mens that an
FTP
-algorithm may not be expected for them. The emerging question for such problems is whether they may admit an
FTP
-algorithm when parameterized by some alternative structural parameter. As many problems escaping the expressibility
power of MSOL are defined using certificates that are edge sets, the investigation has been oriented to edge-analogues of treewidth. The first candidate
for this was the parameter of
tree-cut width
, denoted by
twc
, defined by Wollan in
[
45
]
.
Indeed tree-cut width enjoys combinatorial properties that parallel those of treewidth and, most importantly,
it yelds
FPT
-algorithms for several such problems including the above mentioned ones
[
23
]
. Interestingly, this landscape appears to be more complicated when it comes
to the
Edge Disjoint Paths
problem. Ganian and Ordyniak, in
[
24
]
, proved
that this problem is
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\sf W}[1]
-hard when parameterized by
twc
. This means that some other, alternative to
twc
, parameter should be considered for this problem. This paper was motivated by this question.
We give the definition of a different parameter, called
edge-treewidth
and denoted by
etw
. As we see, edge-treewidtht is parametrically incomparable to tree-cut width and can be seen in a natural way as an “edge-analogue” of
treewidth or as a “tree-analogue” of cutwidth, based on their layout definitions.
Moreover, it seems that it is the right choice as a parameter for the
Edge Disjoint Paths
problem: this problem
admits an
FPT
-algorithm when parameterized by
etw
.

Some definitions on graphs and on graph parameters.

Before we proceed with the definition of edge-treewidth and its relation to other parameters, we need some definitions.

All graphs that we consider are finite, loop-less, and may have multiple edges. Given a graph
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G
, we let
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}V(G)
and
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}E(G)
respectively denote its vertex set and edge set. As we permit multi-edges,
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}E(G)
is a multiset and the
multiplicity
of each edge is the number of times that it appears in
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}E(G)
.
We use
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}|G|=|V(G)|
in order to denote the
size
of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G
.
For a subset of vertices
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}S\subseteq V(G)
, we denote by
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G[S]
the subgraph of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G
induced by
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}S
. We also define
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}E_{G}(S)
as the set of edges with one vertex in
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}S
and one vertex not in
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}S

and
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}N_{G}(S)
as the set of all vertices not in
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}S
that are adjacent to a vertex in
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}S
.
The
vertex-degree
(resp.
edge-degree
) of a vertex
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}v
is defined as
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\sf vdeg}_{G}(v)=|N_{G}(\{v\})|
(resp.
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\sf edeg}_{G}(v)=|E_{G}(\{v\})|
). We also set

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}\Delta_{\sf v}(v)=\max\{{\sf vdeg}_{G}(v)\mid v\in V(G)\}
and

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}\Delta_{\sf e}(v)=\max\{{\sf edeg}_{G}(v)\mid v\in V(G)\}
.
Given a graph
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G
, a set of vertices
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}S
and a vertex
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}v\in S
, we define

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}C_{G}(S,v)
as the vertex set of the connected component of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G[S]
containing the vertex
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}v
.

Graph parameters.

A
graph parameter
is any function mapping graphs to non-negative integers.
Let
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\bf p}_{1},{\bf p}_{2}
be two graph parameters. We write
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\bf p}_{1}={\bf p}_{2}
if, for every graph
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G
,
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\bf p}_{1}(G)={\bf p}_{2}(G)
.
We say that
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\bf p}_{1}
is
upper bounded
by
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\bf p}_{2}
, denoted by
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\bf p}_{1}\lesssim{\bf p}_{2}
, if there is a
function
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}f:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
such that for every
graph
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G
,
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\bf p}_{1}(G)\leq f({\bf p}_{2}(G))
. We also say that
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\bf p}_{1}
and
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\bf p}_{2}
are
parametrically equivalent
, denoted by
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\bf p}_{1}\sim{\bf p}_{2}
, if
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\bf p}_{2}\lesssim{\bf p}_{1}

and
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\bf p}_{1}\lesssim{\bf p}_{2}
. Also, we say that
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\bf p}_{1}
and
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\bf p}_{2}
are
parametrically incomparable
, denoted by
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\bf p}_{1}\not\sim{\bf p}_{2}
, if neither
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\bf p}_{2}\lesssim{\bf p}_{1}

nor
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\bf p}_{1}\lesssim{\bf p}_{2}
.

Layouts.

A
layout
of an
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}n
-vertex graph
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G
is a linear ordering
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\sf L}=\langle x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\rangle
of its vertices.
We define
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}\mathcal{L}(G)
as the set of layouts of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G
. For
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}i\in\{1,\dots,n\}
, we let
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{{\sf L}(i)}
denote the
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}i
-th vertex of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\sf L}
. If clear from the context,
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\sf L}(i)
may be denoted
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}x_{i}
. For two vertices
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}x
and
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}y
, we write
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}x\prec_{{\sf L}}y
if
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}x
occurs before
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}y
in
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\sf L}
.
Given
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\sf L}=\langle x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\rangle\in{\cal L}(G)
and
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
, we define
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}S_{i}=\{x_{i},\ldots,x_{n}\}
. Finally, if
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}S\subseteq V(G)
, we denote by
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{{\sf L}[S]}
the layout of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G[S]
such that, for every
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}x,y\in S
,
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}x\prec_{{\sf L}[S]}y
if and only if
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}x\prec_{{\sf L}}y
.

Given an
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}n
-vertex graph
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G
and a layout
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\sf L}=\langle x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\rangle\in\mathcal{L}(G)
, we call
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}(G,{\sf L})
-cost function
every
function
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}\delta_{G,{\sf L}}:[n]\to\mathbb{N}
that assigns a non-negative integer to each position of the layout
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\sf L}
. From now on, for simplicity, we will use
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}\delta
instead of the heavier notation
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}\delta_{G,{\sf L}}
.
Given a
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}(G,{\sf L})
-cost function

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}\delta
, we set
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\bf p}_{\delta}(G,{\sf L})=\max\{\delta(i)\mid i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}\}

and we define the following general graph parameter:

{\bf p}_{\delta}(G)=\min\{{\bf p}_{\delta}(G,{\sf L})\mid{\sf L}\in\mathcal{L}(G)\}.

The
treewidth
of a graph measures the topological resemblance of a graph to the structure of a tree
[
36
,
2
,
28
]
. The
pathwidth
of a graph, denoted by
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\bf pw}
, measures the topological resemblance of a graph to the structure of a path
[
35
,
21
]
. It holds that
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\bf p}_{\delta^{\sf v}}={\bf pw}

[
32
]
and
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\bf p}_{\delta^{\sf vc}}={{\bf tw}}

[
17
]
. Finally,
the parameter
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\bf p}_{\delta^{\sf e}}
is known as the
cutwidth
of a graph
[
13
,
30
]
, denoted
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\bf cw}
. From the layout characterization of pathwidth, we can consider the cutwidth as the edge-analogue of pathwidth. Likewise, we may see
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\bf p}_{\textdelta^{\sf ec}}
as the edge-analogue of treewidth. We call
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\bf p}_{\textdelta^{\sf ec}}

edge-treewidth
and we denote it by
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{{\bf etw}}
.
Interestingly, this parameter is, so far, completely non-investigated. The purpose of this note is to initiate the study of edge-treewidth, as an “edge-analogue” of treewidth.

As we already mentioned, an alternative edge-analogue of treewidth is
tree-cut width
. The reason why tree-cut width can be seen as an edge-analogue of treewidth is that it is parametrically equivalent to the
maximum size of a wall contained as an immersion in the same way that treewidth is parametrically equivalent to the maximum
size of a wall contained as topological minor
[
45
]
(see
Section 2
for the formal definitions of these graph containment relations).
Using the terminology that we introduce in
Section 5
, the family of walls may serve as
universal minor-obstruction
for
treewidth as well as universal immersion-obstruction for tree-cut width.

All four parameters
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\bf tw},{\bf pw},{\bf cw},{\bf tcw}

have been extensively studied both from the combinatorial and the algorithmic point of view
[
27
,
45
,
15
,
3
,
4
,
5
]
. For all of them, the corresponding decision problem is
NP
-complete
[

,
8
,
1
,
26
,

]
. Moreover, all of them enjoy nice closeness properties under known
partial ordering relations on graphs: treewidth and pathwidth are minor-closed, while cutwidth and tree-cut are immersion-closed. These closeness relations imply that all of them
are fixed parameter tractable (in short,
FPT
) when parameterized by their values. In other words, for every
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\bf p}\in\{{\bf tw},{\bf pw},{\bf cw},{\bf tcw}\}
, there is an algorithm computing
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\bf p}(G)
in time
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\cal O}_{{\bf p}(G)}(|G|)

[
9
,
7
,
43
,
44
,
11
]
. (For more on parameterized algorithms and complexity, see
[

,
22
,

]

Our results.

Unfortunately, the above combinatorial/algorithmic properties do not copy for
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\bf etw}
.
As we see in
Section 2
, none of the above closeness properties holds for edge-treewidth.
In
Section 2
we define a new partial ordering relation, namely the
weak topological minor

relation
and we show that edge-treewidth is closed under this relation.
Let
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\cal G}_{k}=\{G\mid{\bf etw}(G)\leq k\}
,
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}k\in\mathbb{N}
.
We consider the
weak-topological minor obstruction set

of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\cal G}_{k}
that is defined as the set
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\sf obs}({\cal G}_{k})
containing all weak-topological minor minimal graphs that do not belong in
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\cal G}_{k}
. In
Section 3
, we identify the (finite)
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\sf obs}({\cal G}_{k})
for
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}k\leq 2

and we show that, for
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}k=3
,
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\sf obs}({\cal G}_{k})
is infinite.
This comes in (negative) contrast to the fact that the corresponding obstructions sets for the graphs of treewidth/pathwidth/cutwidth at most
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}k
is always finite because of the seminal results of Robertson and Semour in their Graph Minors series
[
39
]
. On the positive side, we prove that
edge-treewidth admits a
finite
“long term” obstruction characterization. As our main combinatorial result (
Section 5
),
we give a set of
five
parameterized
graph families that can act as
universal obstructions
for edge-treewidth in the sense
that edge-treewidth is parametrically equivalent to the maximum size of a graph in these families that
is contained as weak-topological minor.

The algorithmic motivation of our study of edge treewidth is the following problem.

Edge Disjoint Path problem (EDP)

Input:

A graph
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G
and a set
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}(s_{1},t_{1}),\dots,(s_{k},t_{k})
of pairs of vertices, called terminals, of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G
.

Question:

Does
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G
contains pairwise edge disjoint paths between every pair of terminals?

The vertex disjoint counterpart of
EDP
is the
Vertex Disjoint Path
problem (
VDP
).
Both
EDP
and
VDP
are known to be
NP
-Complete
[
31
]
. Both problems
are
FPT
when parameterized by the number
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}k
of the terminals
[
38
]
. Another relevant question is whether (and when)
VDP
and
EDP
remain
FPT
when parameterized by certain graph parameters that are independent from the number of terminals.
In other words, the question is for which parameter
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\bf p}
the above problems can be solved in time
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}|G|^{{\cal O}_{{\bf p}(G)}(1)}
or, even better, in time
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\cal O}_{{\bf p}(G)}(|G|^{{\cal O}(1)})
.
In the former case, the corresponding parameterized problem is classified in the parameterized complexity class
XP
and, as we already mentioned, in the later in the parameterized parameterized complexity class
FPT
.

Naturally, the first parameter to consider is treewidth. It is known that
VDP
, parameterized by treewidth,
is in
FPT
because of the time
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\cal O}_{{\bf tw}(G)}(|G|)
algorithm of Scheffler in
[
41
]
. However, this is not any more the case for
EDP

as it has been shown by Nishizeki, Vygen, and Zhou in
[
34
]
that the
EDP
is
NP
-hard even on graphs of treewidth two. Therefore, one may not expect that
EDP
, parameterized by treewidth, is in
FPT
or even in
XP
. This induces the question on whether there is some alternative graph parameter
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{{\bf p}}
such that
EDP
is
FPT
,
when parameterized by
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\bf p}
.
The first parameter to be considered was tree-cut width, seen as an edge-analogue of treewidth.
In this direction Ganian and Ordyniak, in
[
24
]
, proved that
EDP
, when parameterized by
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\bf tcw},
is in

Obstructions

Given a graph class
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\cal G}
and a partial relation
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}\leq

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\cal G}
with respect to
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}\leq
, denoted by

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\sf obs}_{\leq}({\cal G})
, as the set of all
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}\leq
-minimal graphs not in
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\cal G}
. Clearly, if
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\cal G}
is closed under
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}\leq
, then

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\sf obs}_{\leq}({\cal G})
can be seen as a complete characterisation of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\cal G}
, as
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G\in{\cal G}
iff
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}\forall H\in{\sf obs}({\cal G})\ H\not\leq G

Hereafter, for
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}i\in\mathbb{N},
we denote by
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\sf obs}_{i}
the weak-topological minor obstruction set of the family of graphs of edge treewidth at most
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}i

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\sf obs}_{i}={\sf obs}_{\leq_{\sf wtp}}(\{G\mid{\bf etw}(G)\leq i\})

We next prove that
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\sf obs}_{3}

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\sf obs}_{1}

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\sf obs}_{2}

4
A parametric equivalence

Our next step is to show that edge-treewidth can be parametrically expressed using the maximum edge-degree parameter.
For this we will define a new parameter, using the edge-degree as basic ingredient, and we will prove its parameteric equivalence with edge-treewidth.

Lemma 7
.

Let
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G=(V,E)
be a biconnected graph. For every vertex
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}u
of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G
, we have
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\bf etw}(G,u)\leq{\bf etw}(G)^{2}+2\cdot{\bf etw}(G)
, where
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\bf etw}(G,u)=\max\{\mathbf{p}_{\delta^{\sf ce}}(G,{\sf L})\>|\>{\sf L}\in\mathcal{L}(G)\land{\sf L}{(1)}=u\}
.

Proof.

Let
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\sf L}=\langle x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\rangle
be a layout of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G
. Suppose that
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}u=x_{i}
. Consider the layout
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\sf L}^{\prime}=\langle x_{i},x_{1},\dots,x_{i-1},x_{i+1},\dots x_{n}\rangle
. We observe that for every vertex
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}x\in\{x_{i+1},\dots,x_{n}\}
, we have
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}\delta^{\sf ce}_{G,{\sf L}}(x)=\delta^{\sf ce}_{G,{\sf L}^{\prime}}(x)
. However, for a vertex
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}x\in\{x_{1},\dots,x_{i-1}\}
, we have that
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}\delta^{\sf ce}_{G,{\sf L}^{\prime}}(x)\leq\delta^{\sf ce}_{G,{\sf L}}(x)+\ell
, where
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}\ell
is upper bounded by the degree of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}x_{i}
in
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G
.

First observe that
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}|N(x_{i})\cap\{x_{1},\dots,x_{i-1}\}|
is at most
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}\delta^{\sf ce}_{G,{\sf L}}(x_{i})\leq\mathbf{p}_{\delta^{\sf ce}}(G,{\sf L})
. It remains to bound
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}|N(x_{i})\cap S_{i+1}|
. Let
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}H
be the subgraph of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G
induced by
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}C_{G}(S_{i},x_{i})
. As
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G
is biconnected, every connected component of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}H-x_{i}
has at least one neighbor that appears prior to
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}x_{i}
in
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\sf L}
. It follows that the number of connected components of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}H-x_{i}
is at most
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}\delta^{\sf ce}_{G,{\sf L}}(x_{i})\leq\mathbf{p}_{\delta^{\sf ce}}(G,{\sf L})
. Let
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}C
be one of these connected components and
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}x_{C}
be the first vertex of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}C
in
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\sf L}
. Then observe that
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}|N(x_{i})\cap C|\leq\delta^{\sf ce}_{G,{\sf L}}(x_{C})\leq\mathbf{p}_{\delta^{\sf ce}}(G,{\sf L})
.
It follows that
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}|N(x_{i})\cap S_{i+1}|\leq\mathbf{p}_{\delta^{\sf ce}}(G,{\sf L})^{2}

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}\ell\leq\mathbf{p}_{\delta^{\sf ce}}(G,{\sf L})^{2}+\mathbf{p}_{\delta^{\sf ce}}(G,{\sf L})
, proving the result.
∎

Universal obstructions.

By the grid minor theorem
[
36
,
40
]
(see also
[
18
]
), we know that
the treewidth is parametrically equivalent to the size of the largest wall contained as a topological minor.
That way, we can claim that the walls as a set of “universal minor-obstructions” for treewidth. Before proving a similar result for edge-treewidth and the weak topological minor relation, let us formalize the notion
universal obstructions
.

Let
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}\leq
be some partial ordering relation. Given a set of graphs
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\cal A}
and a
graph
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G
, we say that
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\cal A}\leq G
if
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}\exists H\in{\cal A}\ H\leq G
.
A
parameterized set of graphs
is a set
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\cal H}=\{H_{i}\mid i\in\mathbb{N}\}
of graphs, indexed by non-negative integers.
Given an partial ordering relation
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}\leq
, we say that
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\cal H}
is
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}\leq
-monotone
if for every
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}i\in\mathbb{N}
,
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}H_{i}\leq{H}_{i+1}
.
Let
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}\mathcal{H}^{1}
and
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}\mathcal{H}^{2}
be two
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}\leq
-monotone parameterized sets of graphs. We say that
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\cal H}^{1}\leq{\cal H}^{2}
if there is a function
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}f:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
such that for every
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}i\in\mathbb{N}
,
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}H_{i}^{1}\leq H_{f(i)}^{2}
.
We say that
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\cal H}^{1}
and
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\cal H}^{2}
are
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}\leq
-equivalent
if
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\cal H}^{1}\leq{\cal H}^{2}
and
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\cal H}^{2}\leq{\cal H}^{1}
.
For instance, we may define the two parameterized sets of graphs
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\cal W}=\{W_{i}\mid i\in{\mathbb{N}}\}
and
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\cal G}=\{\textGamma_{i}\mid i\in{\mathbb{N}}\}

and observe that
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\cal W}
and
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\cal G}
are
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}\leq_{\sf mn}
-equivalent. However, they are not
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\cal W}
are
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}\leq_{\sf tp}
-equivalent
as
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\cal W}\leq_{\sf tp}{\cal G}
, while
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\cal G}\not\leq_{\sf tp}{\cal W}
.

A set
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}\mathfrak{A}
of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}\leq
-monotone parameterized sets of graphs is a
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}\leq
-antichain
if for every two distinct
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}\mathcal{H}^{1}\in\mathfrak{A}
and
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}\mathcal{H}^{2}\in\mathfrak{A}
, neither
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\cal H}^{1}\leq{\cal H}^{2}
nor
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\cal H}^{2}\leq{\cal H}^{1}
.
Given that
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}\mathfrak{A}=\{{\cal H}^{1},\ldots,{\cal H}^{r}\}
, we define the
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}i
-th layer
of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}\mathfrak{A}
, denoted by
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}\mathfrak{A}_{i}
as the set consisting of the
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}i
-th graph in each of the parameterized set of graphs in
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}\mathfrak{A}
, that is
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}\mathfrak{A}_{i}=\{H_{i}^{1},\ldots,H_{r}^{i}\}
.
We also define the graph parameter
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\bf p}_{\mathfrak{A},\leq}
so that

{\bf p}_{\mathfrak{A}}(G)=\max\{i\mid\mathfrak{A}_{i}\leq G\}.

Definition 11
.

Let
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}\leq
be a graph inclusion relation,
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}\mathfrak{A}

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}\leq
-antichain, and

Claim 13
.

For every
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}j
,
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}1\leq j\leq 5

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}\mathcal{H}^{j}

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}\leq_{\sf wtp}
-monotone parameterized set of graphs.

Proof of claim.

Observe that

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}\theta_{i}
is obtained from
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}\theta_{i+1}
by deleting one edge and that
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}\dot{\theta}_{i}
is obtained from
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}\dot{\theta}_{i+1}
by deleting one vertex of vertex-degree
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}2
. We note that every graph
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}H^{j}_{i+1}
(
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}j\in\{3,4\}
) in one of the two fan families
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}\mathcal{H}^{3}
,
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}\mathcal{H}^{4}
has an induced path
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}P
of at least
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}4+i
vertices. Let
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}x
be one of the two extreme vertices of that path. To obtain
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}H^{j}_{i}
from
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}H^{j}_{i+1}
, we first delete
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}x
. If this generate edges between vertices of degree two in the resulting graph, then we contract these (one or two) edges.
One can easily check that, in the weak subdivision
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}\dot{W}_{4+i}
of the wall, performing vertex deletions and edges contraction between degree-two vertices along the top row and the left-most column, we can obtain
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}\dot{W}_{(3+i)\times(3+i)}
.

Claim 14
.

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}\mathfrak{A}=\{\mathcal{H}^{j}\mid 1\leq j\leq 5\}
is a
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}\leq_{\sf wtp}
-antichain.

Proof of claim.

1.

Consider
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}j\in[1,4]
. It is well-known that the treewidth of any subdivision of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}W_{i\times i}
is
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}i
. Observe that for every
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}i\in\mathbb{N}
,
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\bf tw}(H^{j}_{i})=2
. As
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G\leq_{\sf wtp}H
implies that
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\bf tw}(G)\leq{\bf tw}(H)
, we have that
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}\mathcal{H}^{j}\not\leq_{\sf wtp}\mathcal{H}^{5}
. Moreover, if
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G\leq_{\sf wtp}H
, then
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}\Delta_{\sf e}(G)\leq\Delta_{\sf e}(H)
. This implies that
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}\mathcal{H}^{5}\not\leq_{\sf wtp}\mathcal{H}^{j}
.

2.

We observe that a weak topological minor of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}\theta_{i}
is either the single vertex graph or a theta graph
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}\theta_{i^{\prime}}
for some
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}i^{\prime}<i
. It follows that for every
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}j\in\{2,3,4\}
,
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}\mathcal{H}^{j}\not\leq_{\sf wtp}\mathcal{H}^{1}
.

3.

We observe that every weak topological minor of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}\dot{\theta}_{i}
that is biconnected is either the single vertex graph or
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}\dot{\theta}_{i^{\prime}}
for some
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}i^{\prime}<i
. It follows that for every
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}j\in\{1,3,4\}
,
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}\mathcal{H}^{j}\not\leq_{\sf wtp}\mathcal{H}^{2}
.

4.

We observe that for
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}j\in\{3,4\}
, every weak topological minor of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}H^{j}_{i}
that is biconnected is either the
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}K_{3}
, the fan
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}\varphi_{3}
or contains an induced path on at least
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}5
vertices. It follows that for every
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}j^{\prime}\in\{1,2\}

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}\mathcal{H}^{j^{\prime}}\not\leq_{\sf wtp}\mathcal{H}^{j}

Finally, we observe that every weak topological minor of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}\tilde{\varphi}_{j}\in\mathcal{H}^{4}

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}\mathcal{H}^{4}\not\leq_{\sf wtp}\mathcal{H}^{3}

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}\diamond

Claim 16
.

There exists a function
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}f:\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}
such that if for every
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}j\in[1,5]
,
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}H^{j}_{k}\not\leq_{\sf wtp}G
, then
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\bf etw}(G)\leq f(k)
.

Proof of claim.

By the parametric equivalence between
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\bf etw}(G)
and
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}\mathbf{p}(G)=\max\big\{\{\Delta_{\sf e}(B)\>|\>B\in{\sf bc}(G)\}\cup\{{{\bf tw}}(B)\>|\>B\in{\sf bc}(G)\}\big\}
(see
Section 4
), it suffices to prove that there exists a function
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}f:\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}
such that if for every
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}j\in[1,5]
,
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}H^{j}_{k}\not\leq_{\sf wtp}G
, then
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}\mathbf{p}(G)\leq f(k)
. Let
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}B
be the block of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G
such that
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}\mathbf{p}(G)=\max\{\Delta_{\sf e}(B),{\bf tw}(B)\}
. We have two cased to consider:

•

Suppose first that
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}\mathbf{p}(G)={\bf tw}(B)
. Observe that by the wall theorem (see
[
37
,
12
]
), if
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}B
contains a subdivision of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}W_{k\times k}
as a subgraph, then
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\bf tw}(G)\geq k^{{\cal O}(1)}
.

•

So let us consider the case where
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}\mathbf{p}(G)=\Delta_{\sf e}(B)
. Let
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}x
be a vertex of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}B
such that
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\sf edeg}_{B}(x)=\Delta_{\sf e}(B)
and suppose that
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\sf edeg}_{G}(x)\geq k\cdot k^{2k-3}
. As by assumption,
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}\theta_{k}\not\leq_{\sf wtp}G
, we have that
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\sf vdeg}_{B}(x)\geq k^{2k-3}
. Let
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}T^{\prime}
be a (simple) subtree of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}B\setminus\{x\}
spanning all the neighbors of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}x
in
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}B
. We consider the tree
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}T
obtained from
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}T^{\prime}
by disolving every vertex of vertex-degree two that is not a neighbor of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}x
in
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G
. It follows that every vertex of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}T
is a neighbor of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}x
or a branching vertex. Observe that every vertex
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}y
in
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}T
has vertex-degree
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\sf vdeg}_{T}(y)<k
, as otherwise we would have
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}\dot{\theta}_{k}\leq_{\sf wtp}G
. It follows that
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}T
is a tree spanning at least
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}k^{2k-3}
vertices and its maximum vertex-degree
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}\Delta_{\sf v}(T)
is at most
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}k
. Moreover, the number of vertices in
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}T
is at most
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}(\Delta_{\sf v}(T))^{{\lceil\sf diam}(T)/2\rceil}
, where
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\sf diam}(T)
is the diameter of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}T
. This implies that
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}T
contains a path
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}P
of length at least
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}4k-6

We now prove that the parameters of edge-treewidth and tree-cut width are parametrically incomparable, i.e.,
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\bf tcw}\not\sim{\bf etw}

For this, we may avoid giving the (lengthy) definition of tree-cut width and use instead the main result of Wollan in

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\bf tcw}\sim{\bf p}_{\leq_{\sf im},{\{\cal W}\}}

Section 5
. This implies that
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\bf etw}\not\lesssim{\bf p}_{\leq_{\sf im},{\{\cal W}\}}

Let
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}Z_{i}
be the graph obtained after taking

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}|E(W_{i})|
copies of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}\texttheta_{3}

6
On the complexity of computing edge-treewidth

We discuss the computational complexity of the following decision problem:

Edge-Treewidth

Input:

A graph
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G
and an integer
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}k
.

Question:

Decide if
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\bf etw}(G)\leq k
?

Theorem 18

(
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\sf NP}
-completeness)
.

The problem
Edge-treewidth
is
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\sf NP}
-complete.

Proof.

Clearly, the problem
Edge-treewidth
belongs to
NP
. Indeed given a layout
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\sf L}
of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G
, one can check in polynomial time if
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}\mathbf{p}_{\delta^{\sf ce}}(G,{\sf L})\leq k
.
The
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\sf NP}
-hardness proof is based on a reduction from the
Min-Bisection
problem.

Min-bisection problem

Input:

A graph
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G
and an integer
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}k
.

Question:

Is there a bipartition
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}V_{1},V_{2}
of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}V(G)
such that
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}||V_{1}|-|V_{2}||\leq 1
and
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}|E_{G}(V_{1})|\leq k

?

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G

Figure 10
:
The graph
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}H
is obtained by adding to the graph
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G
an independent set of size
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}n^{2}
and making each of the vertices of the independent set universal to the vertices of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G
.

Let
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}(G,k)
be an instance of
Min Bisection
where
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G
is a graph on
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}n
vertices. We build an instance
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}(H,w)
of
Edge-treewidth
as follows (see
Figure 10
):

•

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}V(H)=V(G)\cup Q
, where
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}Q
is an independent set of size
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}n^{2}
;

•

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}E(H)=E(G)\cup\{\{x,y\}\mid x\in V(G),y\in Q\}
, that is we add to
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}E(G)
all possible edges between
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}Q
and
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}V(G)
;

•

and
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}w=\frac{n^{3}}{2}+k
.

Claim 19
.

If
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}(G,k)
is a
YES
-instance of
Min-Bisection
, then
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}(H,w)
is a
YES
-instance of
Edge-treewidth
.

Proof of claim.

Let
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}V_{1},V_{2}
be a bipartition of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}V(G)
such that
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}||V_{1}|-|V_{2}||\leq 1
and
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}|E_{G}(V_{1})|\leq k
. Let us consider a layout
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\sf L}
of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}H
such that for every triple
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}x,y,z\in V(H)
such that
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}x\in V_{1}
,
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}y\in Q
,
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}z\in V_{2}
, then
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}x\prec_{{\sf L}}y\prec_{{\sf L}}z
, (in other words
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\sf L}=\langle V_{1}\cdot Q\cdot V_{2}\rangle
) (here we use
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}\cdot
for the sequence concatenation operation). Observe that
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}{\textdelta^{\sf ec}_{H,{\sf L}}(\frac{n}{2}+1)}\leq\frac{n^{3}}{2}+k=w
.
Indeed,
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}S_{\frac{n}{2}+1}=Q\cup V_{2}
induces a connected subgraph of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}G
, that is
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}C_{H}(S_{\frac{n}{2}+1},x_{\frac{n}{2}+1})=Q\cup V_{2}
. Moreover, there are
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}n^{2}\cdot\frac{n}{2}
edges between
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}V_{1}
and
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}Q
and at most
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}k
edges between
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}V_{1}
and
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}V_{2}
. Let us prove that for every
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}i\neq\frac{n}{2}+1
,
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}\textdelta^{\sf ec}(H,{\sf L},i)\leq w
. We consider three cases:

•

Suppose that
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}i\leq\frac{n}{2}
(i.e.
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}x_{i}\in V_{1}
). Observe that
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}C_{H}(S_{i},x_{i})=S_{i}
and thereby
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}E_{H}(C_{H}(S_{i},x_{i}))
contains
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}(i-1)\cdot n^{2}
between
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}V(H)\setminus S_{i}
and
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}Q
, and
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}c
edges between
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}V(H)\setminus S_{i}
and
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}S_{i}\setminus Q
with
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}c\leq\frac{n^{2}}{2}
. All in all, we have that
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}\textdelta^{\sf ec}(H,{\sf L},i)\leq(i-1)\cdot n^{2}+\frac{n^{2}}{2}\leq(\frac{n}{2}-1)\cdot n^{2}+\frac{n^{2}}{2}=\frac{n^{3}}{2}-\frac{n^{2}}{2}
.

•

Suppose that
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}i>n^{2}+\frac{n}{2}
(i.e.
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}x_{i}\in V_{2}
). A symmetric argument proves that
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}\textdelta^{\sf ec}(H,{\sf L},i)\leq w
.

•

Suppose that
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}\frac{n}{2}+1<i\leq n^{2}+\frac{n}{2}
(i.e.
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}x_{i}\in Q
). Then
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}E_{H}(C_{H}(S_{i},x_{i}))
contains at most

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}k
edges between the vertices of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}V_{1}
and
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}V_{2}
,
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}(i-\frac{n}{2})\cdot\frac{n}{2}
edges between the vertices of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}Q
and
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}V_{2}
, and
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}(n^{2}+\frac{n}{2}-i)\cdot\frac{n}{2}
edges between the vertices of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}V_{1}
and
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}Q
. In total, we obtain that
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}\textdelta^{\sf ec}(H,{\sf L},i)\leq w
.

So we proved that
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}\textdelta^{\sf ec}(H,{\sf L})\leq w
and thereby
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}(H,w)
is a
YES
-instance of
Edge-treewidth
.
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}\diamond

Claim 20
.

If
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}(H,w)
is a
YES
-instance of
Edge-treewidth
, then
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}(G,k)
is a
YES
-instance of
Min-Bisection
.

Proof of claim.

Let
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}L
be a layout of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}H
such that
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}\textdelta^{\sf ec}(H,{\sf L})\leq w=\frac{n^{3}}{2}+k
with
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}k\leq\frac{n^{2}}{2}
. Let
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}i
be the smallest index for which
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}|(V(H)\setminus S_{i})\cap V(G)|=|S_{i}\cap V(G)|=\frac{n}{2}
. We denote
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}V_{1}=(V(H)\setminus S_{i})\cap V(G)
and
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}V_{2}=S_{i}\cap V(G)
. Let us prove that the bipartition
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}V_{1},V_{2}
certifies that
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}(G,k)
is a
YES
-instance of
Min-Bisection
.

We first argue that
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}S_{i}\cap Q\neq\emptyset
. For the sake of contradiction, suppose that
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}S_{i}\cap Q=\emptyset
.
Let
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}j<i
be the largest integer such that
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}x_{j}\in Q
. By the minimality of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}i
, we know that
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}v_{i-1}\notin Q
and thereby
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}j<i-1
. We observe that
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}C_{H}(S_{j-1},x_{j-1}))=S_{j-1}
and moreover
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}Q\setminus\{v_{j}\}\subseteq V(H)\setminus S_{j}
and
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}|S_{i-1}\cap V(G)|\geq\frac{n}{2}+1
. It follows that
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}E_{H}(S_{j-1})
contains at least
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}(n^{2}-1)\cdot(\frac{n}{2}+1)
edges, that is
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}\textdelta^{\sf ec}(H,{\sf L},i)>\frac{n^{3}}{2}+\frac{n^{2}}{2}
, contradicting the assumption that
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}\textdelta^{\sf ec}(H,{\sf L})\leq w=\frac{n^{3}}{2}+k
with
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}k\leq\frac{n^{2}}{2}
.

As a consequence of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}Q\cap S_{i}\neq\emptyset
, we have that
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.34}C_{H}(S_{i},x_{i})=S_{i}
. This implies that at least half of the edges incident to a vertex

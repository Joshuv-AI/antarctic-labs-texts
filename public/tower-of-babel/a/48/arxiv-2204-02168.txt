Rational Root Theorem
. If a polynomial with integral coefficients
P=\sum_{k=0}^{n}c_{k}X^{k}
has a nonzero rational root
\frac{a}{b}
where the integers

and
b
are relatively prime, then
a
divides
c_{0}
and
b
divides
c_{n}

Note that in

, the authors give a direct proof in the case

due to P. Turán but, even if it uses the same tools, the following is shorter, requires fewer calculations and provides a more general statement. Indeed, we not only prove the irrationality of
\tan(r\pi)

\tan^{2}(r\pi)
is an irrational number unless it is equal to

,
1
,
3
or
\frac{1}{3}

\cos^{2}(r\pi)
and
\cos(r\pi)
are irrational numbers except in usual cases.

Assume that
\tan^{2}(r\pi)
is a rational number not equal to
0
or
1

\tan^{2}(2r\pi)=\frac{4\tan^{2}(r\pi)}{(1-\tan^{2}(r\pi))^{2}}

(1)

is also a rational number not equal to

\tan^{2}(2r\pi)
is a nonzero rational number and
\tan^{2}(2r\pi)\neq 1

\frac{4x}{(1-x)^{2}}=1
are the irrational numbers
x=3\pm 2\sqrt{2}
. Thus, possibly multiplying by a suitable power of 2, we can assume that
n
is odd and write
n=2m+1
where
m

\tan^{2}(r\pi)\neq 0

Let us put

(1+\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{i}\mathop{}\mathopen{}t)^{n}=\left(\frac{\mathrm{e}^{\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{i}\mathop{}\mathopen{}\frac{2d}{n}\pi}}{\cos(\frac{2d}{n}\pi)}\right)^{n}=\frac{1}{\cos^{n}(\frac{2d}{n}\pi)}=\left(\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{i}\mathop{}\mathopen{}\frac{2d}{n}\pi}}{\cos(-\frac{2d}{n}\pi)}\right)^{n}=(1-\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{i}\mathop{}\mathopen{}t)^{n},

Hence,

Since

Furthermore, since
s

1

2r

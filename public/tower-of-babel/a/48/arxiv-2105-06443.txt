Energy stability of plane Couette and Poiseuille flows and Couette paradox
Paolo Falsaperla, Giuseppe Mulone & Carla Perrone

Abstract
We study the nonlinear stability of plane Couette and Poiseuille flows with the Lyapunov second method by using the classical L 2 L_{2} -energy. We prove that the streamwise perturbations are L 2 L_{2} -energy stable for any Reynolds number. This contradicts the results of Joseph [ 10 ] , Joseph and Carmi [ 12 ] and Busse [ 4 ] , and allows us to prove that the critical nonlinear Reynolds numbers are obtained along two-dimensional perturbations, the spanwise perturbations, as Orr [ 16 ] had supposed. This conclusion combined with recent results by Falsaperla et al. [ 8 ] on the stability with respect to tilted rolls, provides a possible solution to the Couette-Sommerfeld paradox.



Key words: Plane Couette, Plane Poiseuille, Nonlinear stability, Weighted energy, Couette-Sommerfeld paradox

2
Laminar flows between two parallel planes

Given a reference frame
Oxyz
, with unit vectors
{\bf i},{\bf j},{\bf k}
, consider the layer
\mathcal{D}={\mathbb{R}}^{2}\times[-1,1]
of thickness
2
with horizontal coordinates
x,y
and vertical coordinate
z
.

Plane parallel shear flows are solutions of the stationary Navier-Stokes equations

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}{\bf U}\!\cdot\!\nabla{\bf U}={\rm Re}^{-1}\Delta{\bf U}-\nabla P\\[5.0pt]
\nabla\cdot{\bf U}=0,\\
\end{array}\right.

characterized by the functional form

{\bf U}=\begin{pmatrix}\par f(z)\\
0\\
0\end{pmatrix}=f(z){\bf i},

(4)

where
{\bf U}
is the velocity field and
P
the pressure field, and
{\rm Re}
is the Reynolds number.
The function
f(z):[-1,1]\to\mathbb{R}
is assumed to be sufficiently smooth and is called the shear profile. All the variables are written in a non-dimensional form. To non-dimensionalize the equations and the gap of the layer we use a Reynolds number based on the average shear and half gap
d
(see
[
8
]
).

In particular, for fixed velocity at the boundaries
z=\pm 1
, we have the well known profiles:

a) Couette
f(z)=z
,

b) Poiseuille
f(z)=1-z^{2}
.

2.1
Perturbation equations

The perturbation equations to the plane parallel shear flows, in non-dimensional form, are

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}u_{t}=-{\bf u}\!\cdot\!\nabla u+{\rm Re}^{-1}\Delta u-(fu_{x}+f^{\prime}w)-\dfrac{\partial p}{\partial x}\\[5.0pt]
v_{t}=-{\bf u}\!\cdot\!\nabla v+{\rm Re}^{-1}\Delta v-fv_{x}-\dfrac{\partial p}{\partial y}\\[5.0pt]
w_{t}=-{\bf u}\!\cdot\!\nabla w+{\rm Re}^{-1}\Delta w-fw_{x}-\dfrac{\partial p}{\partial z}\\[5.0pt]
\nabla\cdot{\bf u}=0,\\
\end{array}\right.

where
{\bf u}=u{\bf i}+v{\bf j}+w{\bf k}
is the perturbation to the velocity field,
p
is the perturbation to the pressure field.

Throughout the paper, we use the symbol
h_{x}
as
\frac{\partial h}{\partial x}
,
h_{t}
as
\frac{\partial h}{\partial t}
, etc., for any function

.

To system (
2.1
) we append the rigid boundary conditions

{\bf u}(x,y,\pm 1,t)=0,\quad(x,y,t)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\times(0,+\infty),

and the initial condition

{\bf u}(x,y,z,0)={\bf u_{0}}(x,y,z),\quad{\rm in}\quad\mathcal{D},

with
{\bf u_{0}}(x,y,z)
solenoidal vector which vanishes at the boundaries.

Definition 2.1
.

We define streamwise (or longitudinal) perturbations the perturbations
{\bf u},p
which do not depend on
x
.

Definition 2.2
.

We define spanwise (or transverse) perturbations the perturbations
{\bf u},p
which do not depend on
y
.

2.2
Linear stability/instability

Assume that both
{\bf u}
and
\nabla p
are

-periodic with periods

and

(a,b)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{+}

\Omega=[0,\frac{2\pi}{a}]\times[0,\frac{2\pi}{b}]\times[-1,1].

As the basic function space, we take

(g,h)=\int_{0}^{\frac{2\pi}{a}}\int_{0}^{\frac{2\pi}{b}}\int_{-1}^{1}g(x,y,z)h(x,y,z)dxdydz,

and the norm given by

\|g\|=\Bigl[\int_{0}^{\frac{2\pi}{a}}\int_{0}^{\frac{2\pi}{b}}\int_{-1}^{1}g^{2}(x,y,z)dxdydz\Bigr]^{\frac{1}{2}}.

We recall that the classical results of Romanov
[
23
]
prove that Couette flow is
linearly stable
for
any Reynolds
number. Instead, Poiseuille flow is unstable for any Reynolds number bigger that

(Orszag
[

The linear stability/instability is obtained by studying the linearised system by neglecting the terms
{\bf u}\!\cdot\!\nabla u
,
{\bf u}\!\cdot\!\nabla v
,
{\bf u}\!\cdot\!\nabla w
in (
2.1

We observe that, in the linear case, the Squire theorem
[
25
]
holds and the most destabilizing perturbations are two-dimensional, in particular the spanwise perturbations (see Drazin and Reid
[
7
]

[
7
]

3
Nonlinear energy stability

Here we study
the nonlinear energy stability with the Lyapunov method
, by using the classical energy

V(t)=\dfrac{1}{2}[\|u\|^{2}+\|v\|^{2}+\|w\|^{2}].

We obtain
sufficient conditions of global nonlinear stability
.

Taking into account the solenoidality of
{\bf u}
and the boundary condition, we write the energy identity
[
21
]

\dot{V}=-(f^{\prime}w,u)-{\rm Re}^{-1}[\|\nabla u\|^{2}+\|\nabla v\|^{2}+\|\nabla w\|^{2}],

(10)

and we have

\begin{array}[]{l}\dot{V}=-(f^{\prime}w,u)-{\rm Re}^{-1}[\|\nabla u\|^{2}+\|\nabla v\|^{2}+\|\nabla w\|^{2}]=\\[8.53581pt]
=\left(\dfrac{-(f^{\prime}w,u)}{\|\nabla u\|^{2}+\|\nabla v\|^{2}+\|\nabla w\|^{2}}-\dfrac{1}{{\rm Re}}\right)\|\nabla{\bf u}\|^{2}\leq\\[8.53581pt]
\leq\left(\dfrac{1}{\rm Re}_{c}-\dfrac{1}{{\rm Re}}\right)\|\nabla{\bf u}\|^{2},\end{array}

(11)

where

\dfrac{1}{\rm Re}_{c}=m=\max_{\cal S}\dfrac{-(f^{\prime}w,u)}{\|\nabla u\|^{2}+\|\nabla v\|^{2}+\|\nabla w\|^{2}},

(12)

and
\cal S
is the space of the
kinematically admissible fields

\begin{array}[]{l}{\cal S}=\{u,v,w\in H^{2}(\Omega),\;u=v=w=w_{z}=0\hbox{ on the boundaries,}\\[8.53581pt]
\hbox{ periodic in {x}, and {y},}\quad u_{x}+v_{y}+w_{z}=0,\quad\|\nabla{\bf u}\|>0\},\end{array}

(13)

and
H^{2}(\Omega)
is the Sobolev space of the functions which are in
L_{2}(\Omega)
together with their first and second generalized derivatives.

The Euler-Lagrange equations of this maximum problem are given by

(f^{\prime}w{\bf i}+f^{\prime}u{\bf k})-2m\Delta{\bf u}=\nabla\lambda,

(14)

where
\lambda
is a Lagrange multiplier. Assuming as Orr
[
16
]
that the maximum is achieved for spanwise perturbations,
\dfrac{\partial}{\partial y}\equiv 0

double-curl
of (
14
) and by using the solenoidality condition

\dfrac{{\rm Re}_{c}}{2}(f^{\prime\prime}w_{x}+2f^{\prime}w_{xz})+\Delta\Delta w=0.

By solving this equation we obtain the Orr results: for Couette and Poiseuille flows, we have

3.1
Stability of streamwise perturbations for any Reynolds number

We assume that the perturbations are
streamwise

do not depend on

\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\equiv 0

Therefore the perturbation equations (

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}u_{t}=-{\bf u}\!\cdot\!\nabla u+{\rm Re}^{-1}\Delta u-f^{\prime}w\\[5.0pt]
v_{t}=-{\bf u}\!\cdot\!\nabla v+{\rm Re}^{-1}\Delta v-\dfrac{\partial p}{\partial y}\\[5.0pt]
w_{t}=-{\bf u}\!\cdot\!\nabla w+{\rm Re}^{-1}\Delta w-\dfrac{\partial p}{\partial z}\\[5.0pt]
v_{y}+w_{z}=0.\\
\end{array}\right.

i)
Weighted energy

We define a
weighted energy
(Lyapunov function) equivalent to the classical energy norm and show that the streamwise perturbations cannot destabilize the basic Couette or Poiseuille flows.

First we introduce an arbitrary positive number
\beta
. Then, we multiply (
3.1
)
1
by
\beta u
and integrate over
\Omega
. Besides, we multiply (
3.1
)
2
and (
3.1
)
3
by
v
and
w
and integrate over
\Omega
.
By taking into account the solenoidality of
{\bf u}
, the boundary conditions and the periodicity, we have

\dfrac{d}{dt}[\dfrac{\beta\|u\|^{2}}{2}]=-\beta(f^{\prime}w,u)-\beta{\rm Re}^{-1}\|\nabla u\|^{2}

\dfrac{d}{dt}[\dfrac{\|v\|^{2}+\|w\|^{2}}{2}]=-{\rm Re}^{-1}(\|\nabla v\|^{2}+\|\nabla w)\|^{2}.

By using the arithmetic-geometric mean inequality, we have

-\beta(f^{\prime}w,u)\leq\beta\dfrac{M^{2}}{2\epsilon}\|w\|^{2}+\beta\dfrac{\epsilon}{2}\|u\|^{2},

where
M=\max_{[-1,1]}|f^{\prime}(z)|
and
\epsilon
is an arbitrary positive number to be chosen.

Now we define the Lyapunov function (weighted energy norm)

E(t)=\dfrac{1}{2}[\beta\|u\|^{2}+\|v\|^{2}+\|w\|^{2}],

(21)

and choose
\epsilon=\dfrac{\pi^{2}}{{\rm 4Re}}.
From the above inequality and the use of the Poincaré inequality
\dfrac{\pi^{2}}{4}\|g\|^{2}\leq\|\nabla g\|^{2}
(
g=u
,
g=v
,
g=w
), we have

\dot{E}\leq\dfrac{1}{2}[(\beta\dfrac{4M^{2}{\rm Re}}{\pi^{2}}-\dfrac{\pi^{2}}{{2\rm Re}})\|w\|^{2}-\dfrac{\pi^{2}}{{2\rm Re}}\|v\|^{2}-\beta\dfrac{\pi^{2}}{4\rm Re}\|u\|^{2}].

By choosing
\beta=\dfrac{\pi^{4}}{16M^{2}\rm Re^{2}},
we finally have

\dot{E}\leq-\dfrac{\pi^{2}}{{4\rm Re}}E.

Integrating this inequality, we have
the exponential decay

E(t)\leq E(0)\exp\{-\dfrac{\pi^{2}}{{4\rm Re}}t\}.

(22)

This inequality implies global nonlinear exponential stability of the basic Couette or Poiseuille flows with respect to the streamwise perturbations for any Reynolds number.

. We have the following inequalities:

\begin{split}\dfrac{d}{dt}\dfrac{\|u\|^{2}}{2}&=-(f^{\prime}u,w)-{\rm Re}^{-1}\|\nabla u\|^{2}\leq{M\|u\|\|w\|-\rm Re}^{-1}\|\nabla u\|^{2}\leq\\
&\leq M(\dfrac{\|u\|^{2}}{2\epsilon}+\dfrac{\epsilon}{2}{\|w\|^{2}})-{\rm Re}^{-1}\|\nabla u\|^{2}\leq M(\dfrac{\|u\|^{2}}{2\epsilon}+\dfrac{\epsilon}{2}{\|w\|^{2}})-\\
&-C\|u\|^{2}=(\dfrac{M}{2\epsilon}-C)\|u\|^{2}+\dfrac{\epsilon}{2}M\|w\|^{2}=-\dfrac{C}{2}\|u\|^{2}+\dfrac{M^{2}}{C}\dfrac{\|w\|^{2}}{2}\end{split}

(24)

where
\epsilon=\dfrac{M}{C}
, and
C=\dfrac{\pi^{2}}{4{\rm Re}}.

We use this inequality and (
23
) to obtain

\begin{split}\dfrac{d}{dt}\|u\|^{2}&\leq-C\|u\|^{2}+\dfrac{M^{2}}{C}\|w\|^{2}\leq-C\|u\|^{2}+\dfrac{M^{2}}{C}(\|v\|^{2}+\|w\|^{2})\leq\\
&\leq-C\|u\|^{2}+\dfrac{M^{2}}{C}H_{0}e^{-2Ct}.\end{split}

(25)

Integrating last inequality, we have

\|u\|^{2}\leq e^{-Ct}[k-\dfrac{M^{2}}{C^{2}}H_{0}e^{-Ct}]=ke^{-Ct}-\dfrac{M^{2}}{C^{2}}H_{0}e^{-2Ct},

(26)

with
k=K_{0}+\dfrac{M^{2}}{C^{2}}H_{0},\quad K_{0}=\|u_{0}\|^{2}
.

We introduce the classical energy

L(t)=\dfrac{1}{2}[\|u\|^{2}+\|v\|^{2}+\|w\|^{2}],

(27)

and observe that the initial energy is given by
L_{0}=\dfrac{H_{0}+K_{0}}{2}
. Adding the (
23
) and the (
26
) we finally have:

\begin{split}L(t)&\leq H_{0}e^{-2Ct}+(K_{0}+\dfrac{M^{2}}{C^{2}}H_{0})e^{-Ct}-\dfrac{M^{2}}{C^{2}}H_{0}e^{-2Ct}\leq\\
&\leq L_{0}e^{-2Ct}+(L_{0}+\dfrac{M^{2}}{C^{2}}L_{0})e^{-Ct}=\\
&=L_{0}(e^{-2Ct}+e^{-Ct}+\dfrac{M^{2}}{C^{2}}e^{-Ct}).\end{split}

(28)

This inequality implies global nonlinear exponential stability of the basic Couette or Poiseuille flows with respect to the streamwise perturbations for any Reynolds number.

Therefore, using two different approaches, we have proved:

Theorem 3.1
.

Assuming the perturbations to the basic shear flows (
4
) are streamwise, then we have nonlinear stability according to (
22
) and (
28
), both with the weighted and the classical energy.

Denoting by
{\rm Re}^{y}
the critical Reynolds number with respect to streamwise perturbations, from this Theorem we have

{\rm Re}^{y}=+\infty
, i.e.,
the streamwise perturbations cannot destabilize the basic flows.

Remark 3.1
.

We note that this result is also in agreement with what observe Reddy et al.
[
20
]
: “
Linear stability analysis implies that the disturbance energy of sufficiently small perturbations to laminar flow decays to
0
as
t\to\infty
. But for finite times the energy of disturbances may grow linearly… For a given streamwise and spanwise wave number one can determine a disturbance, called an optimal, which yields the greatest transient linear growth. In channel flows, the optimals which yield the most disturbance growth are independent, or nearly independent, of the streamwise coordinate
”.

3.2
Nonlinear stability with respect to three-dimensional perturbations

We study the nonlinear stability with the Lyapunov method, by using the classical energy

V(t)=\dfrac{1}{2}[\|u\|^{2}+\|v\|^{2}+\|w\|^{2}].

By writing the energy identity

\dot{V}=-(f^{\prime}w,u)-{\rm Re}^{-1}[\|\nabla u\|^{2}+\|\nabla v\|^{2}+\|\nabla w\|^{2}],

(29)

we have

\begin{array}[]{l}\dot{V}=-(f^{\prime}w,u)-{\rm Re}^{-1}[\|\nabla u\|^{2}+\|\nabla v\|^{2}+\|\nabla w\|^{2}]=\\[8.53581pt]
=\left(\dfrac{-(f^{\prime}w,u)}{\|\nabla u\|^{2}+\|\nabla v\|^{2}+\|\nabla w\|^{2}}-\dfrac{1}{{\rm Re}}\right)\|\nabla{\bf u}\|^{2}\leq\\[8.53581pt]
\leq\left(m-\dfrac{1}{{\rm Re}}\right)\|\nabla{\bf u}\|^{2},\end{array}

(30)

where

\dfrac{1}{{\rm Re}_{c}}=m=\max_{\cal S}\dfrac{-(f^{\prime}w,u)}{\|\nabla u\|^{2}+\|\nabla v\|^{2}+\|\nabla w\|^{2}},

(31)

and
\cal S
is the space of the
kinematically admissible fields
(
54
).

The Euler-Lagrange equations of this maximum problem are given by

-f^{\prime}w{\bf i}-f^{\prime}u{\bf k}+2m\Delta{\bf u}=\nabla\lambda,

(32)

where
\lambda
is a Lagrange multiplier.

We define

\zeta=v_{x}-u_{y}

(it is linked to the toroidal part of the decomposition of the velocity vector
{\bf u}
in the poloidal, toroidal and the mean flow, see
[
13
]
,
[
14
]
)
and take the third component of the
double curl
of (
32
) and the third component of the
curl
of (
32
). We obtain the system of the Euler-Lagrange equations written in terms of
\zeta
and
w
:

\begin{cases}f^{\prime}(\zeta_{y}+2w_{xz})+f^{\prime\prime}w_{x}+2m\Delta\Delta w=0\\
f^{\prime}w_{y}+2m\Delta\zeta=0,\end{cases}

(33)

with the boundary conditions

w=w_{z}=0,\;\zeta=0.

(34)

The eigenvalue problem (
33
) - (
34
) is solved with the Chebyshev method by using 80 polynomials. The maximum we obtain corresponds exactly to the critical Reynolds numbers obtained by Joseph
[
10
]
and Joseph and Carmi
[
12
]
and Busse
[
4
]
(i.e. the minimum Reynolds number is reached for the streamwise perturbations). We report these results in Figure 1.

Figure 1:
Plane Couette energy Orr-Reynolds number
{\rm Re}={\rm Re}_{c}
as function of the wave numbers
\rm a
and
\rm b
, for system (
33
) with rigid boundary conditions. The absolute minimum is
\rm R_{c}=20.6
and it is achieved for wavenumbers
\rm a=0
,
\rm b=1.6
. For Poiseuille flow a similar picture can be drawn.

This conclusion and the results we have obtained in Subsection 3.1 for streamwise perturbations are in an
obvious contradiction
. Probably this contradiction is due to the choice of the space of
kinematically admissible
perturbations where we look for the maximum. This space is too large and likely contains perturbations which are not admissible as
physical perturbations
competing for the maximum.

How can we solve this contradiction?

We first observe that if the streamwise perturbations (now
w_{x}=0
and
\zeta_{x}=0
) are stable for any Reynolds number, than, from (
33
)
1
,
m=0
and this implies that
\zeta_{y}=0
.
Equation (
33
)
2
implies that also
w_{y}=0
. If now we consider plan-form perturbations (see Chandrasekhar
[
5
]
, p.24 formula (111)) we have
v=\dfrac{1}{a^{2}+b^{2}}[w_{yz}-\zeta_{x}]=0
. So we are led to speculate:

Conjecture

A
possible answer
to the contradiction is this: we introduce the
subspace
{\cal S}_{0}
of the physical admissible perturbations
which is the subspace of
{\cal S}
consisting of maximizing functions
u,v,w\in{\cal S}
such that
v=0
and we
conjecture
that the maximum
m
is assumed among the functions of this subspace. Furthermore, we observe that in the numerator of (
53
) does not appear explicitly the field
v
.

With this conjecture, we see immediately that the streamwise perturbations are always stable and the maximum is achieved on the spanwise perturbations.

In fact, we have

m=\max_{{\cal S}_{0}}\dfrac{-(f^{\prime}w,u)}{\|\nabla u\|^{2}+\|\nabla w\|^{2}}.

(35)

The Euler-Lagrange equations of this maximum problem are given by

-f^{\prime}w{\bf i}-f^{\prime}u{\bf k}+2m\Delta{\bf u}=\nabla\lambda,

(36)

where
\lambda
is a Lagrange multiplier,
\nabla\lambda=(\lambda_{x},0,\lambda_{z})^{T}
.

We take the third component of the double curl of (
36
) and the third component of the curl of (
36
). We obtain the system of the Euler-Lagrange equations written in terms of
\zeta
and
w
:

\begin{cases}f^{\prime}(\zeta_{y}+2w_{xz})+f^{\prime\prime}w_{x}+2m\Delta\Delta w=0\\
f^{\prime}w_{y}+2m\Delta\zeta=0,\end{cases}

(37)

with the boundary conditions

w=w_{z}=\zeta=0,

(38)

where now
\zeta=-u_{y}
.

We take the second component of the double curl of (
36
) to get

f^{\prime}\zeta_{z}+f^{\prime\prime}\zeta-f^{\prime}w_{xy}=0.

(39)

From this equation and (
37
)
2
we have that
\zeta
and all its derivative with respect to
z
are zero on the boundaries. This implies that
\zeta\equiv 0
, hence
u_{y}=0
and from (
37
)
2
also
w_{y}=0
. Therefore,
u=u(x,z),v=0,w=w(x,z)
and the less stabilizing perturbations which satisfy the equation

2f^{\prime}w_{xz}+f^{\prime\prime}w_{x}+2m\Delta\Delta w=0,

(40)

with boundary conditions
w=w_{z}=0
on
z=\pm 1
, are the spanwise perturbations, as Orr
[
16
]
had supposed.
Moreover, if we consider streamwise perturbations, from the equation

m\|\Delta w\|^{2}=0,

we immediately find
m=0
, i.e.
{\rm Re}^{y}=+\infty
. We report these results in Figure 2 where the critical Reynolds number versus wave numbers for spanwise perturbations are shown.

4
Two-dimensional tilted perturbations

We have seen that the least stabilizing perturbations in the linear case are two-dimensional (the Squire
[
25
]
theorem). In the nonlinear case, we have seen, in the previous section, that the least stabilizing perturbations are the two-dimensional spanwise perturbations. Since the experiments of Prigent et al.
[
19
]
show that at the onset of instability some tilted perturbations (rolls with axes in the
Oxy
-plane) appear (see Prigent et al.
[
19
]
, Fig. 3), it is obvious and important to study linear and nonlinear energy stability with respect to two-dimensional perturbations and look for those perturbations which first appear at the onset of instability and compare the critical Reynolds numbers
obtained with the experimental ones. This could solve the Couette-Sommerfeld paradox, because now it is proved that the least stable perturbations at the onset of turbulence are two-dimensional perturbations. For this reason here we briefly recall the main results of
[
8
]
.

Falsaperla et al.
[
8
]
considered tilted perturbations (rolls with axes in the plane
Oxy
) of an angle
\theta
with the
x
-direction, i.e., the perturbations
{\bf u},p
along the
x^{\prime}
axis which forms an angle
\theta\in(0,\frac{\pi}{2}]
with the direction
x
and does not depend on
x^{\prime}
.
For this, they considered an arbitrary inclined perturbation which forms an angle
\theta
with the direction of motion
{\bf i}
(the
x
-direction).

The system for these perturbations is

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}u^{\prime}_{t}=-{\bf u}\!\cdot\!\nabla u^{\prime}+{\rm Re}^{-1}\Delta u^{\prime}-(fu^{\prime}_{x}+f^{\prime}\cos\theta\,w)-\dfrac{\partial p}{\partial x^{\prime}}\\[5.0pt]
v^{\prime}_{t}=-{\bf u}\!\cdot\!\nabla v^{\prime}+{\rm Re}^{-1}\Delta v^{\prime}-fv^{\prime}_{x}+f^{\prime}\sin\theta\,w-\dfrac{\partial p}{\partial y^{\prime}}\\[5.0pt]
w_{t}=-{\bf u}\!\cdot\!\nabla w+{\rm Re}^{-1}\Delta w-fw_{x}-\dfrac{\partial p}{\partial z}\\[5.0pt]
\dfrac{\partial u^{\prime}}{\partial x^{\prime}}+\dfrac{\partial v^{\prime}}{\partial y^{\prime}}+\dfrac{\partial w}{\partial z}=0,\\
\end{array}\right.

where

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}u^{\prime}=\cos\theta\,u+\sin\theta\,v\\[5.69054pt]
v^{\prime}=-\sin\theta\,u+\cos\theta\,v,\end{array}\right.

(46)

and

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}x^{\prime}=\cos\theta\,x+\sin\theta\,y\\[5.69054pt]
y^{\prime}=-\sin\theta\,x+\cos\theta\,y.\end{array}\right.

(47)

We note that when
\theta\to 0
then
x^{\prime}\to x
,
y^{\prime}\to y
,
u^{\prime}\to u
,
v^{\prime}\to v
.

Now we consider
tilted (stream) perturbations in the
x^{\prime}
-direction
, i.e, those with
\dfrac{\partial}{\partial x^{\prime}}\equiv 0
. The first equation of (
4
) becomes

u^{\prime}_{t}=-{\bf u}\!\cdot\!\nabla u^{\prime}+{\rm Re}^{-1}\Delta u^{\prime}-(fu^{\prime}_{x}+f^{\prime}\cos\theta\,w).

(48)

We have the energy equation

\dfrac{d}{dt}[\dfrac{\beta\|u^{\prime}\|^{2}}{2}]=-\beta(f^{\prime}\cos\theta u^{\prime},w)-\beta{\rm Re}^{-1}\|\nabla u^{\prime}\|^{2},

(49)

where
\beta
is an arbitrary positive number to be chosen.

Moreover, we have

\dfrac{d}{dt}[\dfrac{\|v^{\prime}\|^{2}+\|w\|^{2}}{2}]=-{\rm Re}^{-1}(\|\nabla v^{\prime}\|^{2}+\|\nabla w)\|^{2}+(f^{\prime}\sin\theta\,v^{\prime},w).

(50)

We note that as
\theta\to 0
the energy equations tend to the energy equations obtained for the streamwise perturbations. Moreover, in the case
\dfrac{\partial}{\partial x^{\prime}}\equiv 0
, if
\theta\to\dfrac{\pi}{2}
, since
y^{\prime}\to-x
,
v^{\prime}\to-u
,
x^{\prime}\to y
,

\dfrac{\partial v^{\prime}}{\partial y^{\prime}}\to\dfrac{\partial u}{\partial x}
, the energy equation (
50
) becomes that for spanwise perturbations
[
8
]
.

Defining

H=\dfrac{1}{2}[\|v^{\prime}\|^{2}+\|w\|^{2}],

(51)

we have

\begin{array}[]{l}\dot{H}=(f^{\prime}\sin\theta\,v^{\prime},w)-{\rm Re}^{-1}[\|\nabla v^{\prime}\|^{2}+\|\nabla w\|^{2}]\leq\\[8.53581pt]
\leq\left(\dfrac{1}{{\rm Re}_{c}}-\dfrac{1}{{\rm Re}}\right)[\|\nabla v^{\prime}\|^{2}+\|\nabla w\|^{2}],\end{array}

(52)

where

\dfrac{1}{{\rm Re}_{c}}=\max_{{\cal S}_{0}}\dfrac{(f^{\prime}\sin\theta\,v^{\prime},w)}{\|\nabla v^{\prime}\|^{2}+\|\nabla w\|^{2}},

(53)

and
{\cal S}_{0}
is the space of the
physically admissible fields

\begin{array}[]{l}{\cal S}_{0}=\{v^{\prime},w\in H^{1}(\Omega),\;v^{\prime}=w=0\hbox{ on the boundaries},\\[8.53581pt]
\quad v^{\prime}_{y^{\prime}}+w_{z}=0,\quad\|\nabla v^{\prime}\|+\|\nabla w\|>0\}.\end{array}

(54)

Falsaperla et al.
[
8
]
proved that, for a fixed wavelength
\lambda=\dfrac{2\pi}{a}
and a given angle
\theta
, one has

{\rm Re}_{c}={\rm Re}_{Orr}(\frac{2\pi}{\lambda\sin\theta})/\sin\theta,

(55)

where
{\rm Re}_{Orr}(\frac{2\pi}{\lambda\sin\theta})
is the critical Reynolds number for given wavelength
\lambda
and angle
\theta
.

In particular we note that for
\theta\to\frac{\pi}{2}
we obtain the critical Orr Reynolds number for spanwise perturbations. For
\theta\to 0
we obtain that the critical Reynolds number tends to
+\infty
. Falsaperla et al.
[
8
]
also proved that
\|u^{\prime}\|\to 0
as
t\to\infty
.

Formula (
55
) gives the critical value for a fixed positive wavelength
\lambda
. The minimum with respect to
\lambda
in
(0,+\infty)
is the nonlinear critical Reynolds number for tilted perturbations of an angle
\theta
:

{\rm Re}_{c}=\min_{\lambda>0}{\rm Re}_{Orr}(\frac{2\pi}{\lambda\sin\theta})/\sin\theta={\rm Re}_{Orr}/\sin\theta,

(56)

where
{\rm Re}_{Orr}=44.3
for plane Couette flow and
{\rm Re}_{Orr}=87.6
for plane Poiseuille flow. These results are shown in Figure 3 for Couette case.

The meaning of (
56
) is that when the Reynolds number is less than
{\rm Re}_{c}
the linear and nonlinear energy of tilted perturbations of an angle
\theta
and arbitrary wavelength
\lambda
decay exponentially fast as
t\to+\infty
. For instance, if we consider tilted perturbations of an angle
\theta=25^{\circ}
, the critical energy Reynolds number is
{\rm Re}_{c}=104.82
for plane Couette flow and
{\rm Re}_{c}=207.51
for plane Poiseuille flow, for any
\lambda
. These values though lower than the experimental or numerical values however are bigger than the classical ones.

In order to compare the critical Reynolds numbers with the experiments and the numerical simulations, Falsaperla et al.
[
8
]
considered (
55
) with fixed wavelengths given by the experiments of Prigent et al.
[
19
]
and numerical calculations
[
3
]
. For instance, for

i)
\theta=25^{\circ}
,
\lambda=46
, experimental Reynolds number is about
395
, they obtained approximately
{\rm Re}_{Orr}(\frac{2\pi}{46\sin 25^{\circ}})/\sin 25^{\circ}=369
,

ii)
\theta=26^{\circ}
,
\lambda=48
, experimental Reynolds number is about
385
, they obtained approximately
{\rm Re}_{Orr}(\frac{2\pi}{48\sin 26^{\circ}})/\sin 26^{\circ}=383
(for other values see
[
8
]
).

Therefore, they obtained that the energy stability limit are in a very good agreement with the experiments. This gives a possible solution to the Couette-Sommerfeld paradox.

Plane Couette tilted energy Orr-Reynolds number

\lambda
and angle
\theta
. The two points light blue and red correspond to the cases i) and ii). For Poiseuille flow a similar picture can be drawn.

5
Conclusion

We have proved sufficient conditions of global nonlinear stability of plane Couette and Poiseuille flows for any Reynolds number less than the ordinary limit of spanwise perturbations. For streamwise perturbations we have rigorously proved nonlinear
L_{2}
-energy stability results for any Reynolds number and we have suggested a way to overcome an obvious contradiction with classical numerical results.
We have introduced a space of physical perturbations for the maximum problem and we have proved that the least stabilizing perturbations are two-dimensional (spanwise perturbations). This result justifies the previous study of Falsaperla et al.
[
8
]
on the stability of tilted rolls with axes in the
Oxy
-plane, and gives a possible solution of the Couette-Sommerfeld paradox.

We finally note that some similar results hold for shear flows in magneto-hydrodynamics (forthcoming paper).

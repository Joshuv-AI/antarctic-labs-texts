Polarized Hodge Structures for Clemens Manifolds
Chi Li

Abstract
Let X X be a Calabi-Yau threefold. A conifold transition first contracts X X along disjoint rational curves with normal bundles of type ( − 1 , − 1 ) (-1,-1) , and then smooth the resulting singular complex space X ¯ \bar{X} to a new compact complex manifold Y Y . Such Y Y is called a Clemens manifold and can be non-Kähler. We prove that any small smoothing Y Y of X ¯ \bar{X} satisfies ∂ ∂ ¯ \partial\bar{\partial} -lemma. We also show that the resulting pure Hodge structure of weight three on H 3 ​ ( Y ) H^{3}(Y) is polarized by the cup product. These results answer some questions of R. Friedman.



1
Introduction and main results

Let
X
be a projective Calabi-Yau threefold. Let
C_{1},\dots,C_{r}
be
r
disjoint smooth rational curves such that their normal bundles
N_{C_{i}}X\cong\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(-1,-1)
. Then they can be contracted to
r
ordinary double points to give a singular complex space
\bar{X}
.
If the class
[C_{1}],\dots,[C_{r}]
span
H^{4}(X;\mathbb{C})
and satisfy a linear relation
\sum_{i}m_{i}[C_{i}]=0
with all
m_{i}\neq 0
, then
\bar{X}
is smoothable and all smoothings of
\bar{X}
are compact complex manifolds of dimension three with second Betti number
b_{2}=0
and trivial canonical bundle.
Such non-Kähler complex threefolds were first constructed by H. Clemens and are thus called Clemens manifolds. The details of this construction appeared in R. Friedman
[
4
]
under an assumption on the obstruction group of deformation, which was later removed in
[
8
,
11
,
16
]
. The transition from
X
to smoothings of
\bar{X}
is called the conifold transition, which is a process for connecting different moduli spaces of projective Calabi-Yau threefolds (see
[
4
,
12
]
). For this reason, Clemens manifolds have been studied extensively in complex geometry (see
[
17
]
).

For a given Clemens manifold
Y
, it is a natural question whether
Y
satisfies the
\partial\bar{\partial}
-lemma.
According to the work
[
3
]
, this is equivalent to asking whether the Hodge-de Rham spectral sequence degenerates at the
E_{1}
-page and
whether Hodge filtrations on the cohomology groups induce pure Hodge structures. We can further ask whether the possible induced weight 3 Hodge structure on
H^{3}(Y)
is polarized by the cup product. As pointed out by Friedman in
[
6
]
, Clements manifolds are not deformation equivalent to complex manifolds bimeromorphic to Kähler manifolds, so that the standard method for endowing (polarized) Hodge structures on cohomology groups do not apply. Despite this fact, Friedman proved in
[
6
]
that the general Clemens manifolds satisfy the
\partial\bar{\partial}
-lemma.
Here general roughly means that the
\partial\bar{\partial}
-lemma holds outside of a proper real analytic subvariety in the moduli space. Note that satisfying
\partial\bar{\partial}
-lemma is not a closed condition under deformation (see
[
1
]
for concrete examples of nilmanifolds).
Furthermore, Friedman wrote that

“… although it seems likely that in fact it holds for all small smoothings of
\bar{X}
. Unfortunately, the variational methods of this paper do not seem well suited to deciding if the resulting weight three Hodge structures are polarized.”

The goal of this note is to confirm and answer the above speculation:

Theorem 1.1
.

Any small smoothing
Y
of
\bar{X}
satisfies the
\partial\bar{\partial}
-lemma. Moreover, the resulting weight three Hodge structure on
H^{3}(Y)
is polarized by the cup product.

Let
Y
be a smoothing of
\bar{X}
and let
\{F^{p}\}
be the Hodge filtration of
H^{3}(Y;\mathbb{C})
. Then based on the deep theory of the (limiting) mixed Hodge structures, Friedman already showed in
[
6
]
that the Hodge-de Rham spectral sequence degenerates at the
E_{1}
page, which implies
F^{1}\cap\overline{F^{3}}=0
(
[
6
, Lemma 1.5]
) and
H^{k}(Y;\mathbb{C})
trivially admits a pure Hodge structure if
k\neq 3
. As a consequence, as explained in
[
6
, Corollary 1.6]
, it follows from the work of Deligne-Griffiths-Morgan-Sullivan
[
3
]
that
Y
satisfies the
\partial\bar{\partial}
-lemma if and only if
F^{2}\cap\overline{F^{2}}=0
. Recall that a pure Hodge structure on
H^{3}(Y;\mathbb{C})
is (
\mathbb{Q}
)-polarized if there is non-degenerate bilinear form (defined over
\mathbb{Q}
) that satisfies Hodge-Riemann (HR) bilinear relation.
Friedman showed that, for the resulting pure Hodge structure on the general Clemens manifolds, the first set of HR bilinear relation follows from the degeneration of Hodge-de Rham spectral sequence at the
E_{1}
-page (
[
6
, Lemma 1.4]
):

\langle F^{1},F^{3}\rangle=0,\quad\langle F^{2},F^{2}\rangle=0

(1)

where
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
is the standard (alternating) intersection form on
H^{3}(Y;\mathbb{Q})
.
Moreover the (easy)
H^{3,0}
-part of the second second set of HR bilinear relation is true by a direct calculation:

\sqrt{-1}\langle\omega,\bar{\omega}\rangle>0,

for any
\omega\in H^{3,0}
.

Given these discussion, the precise statement of our result is the following:

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
Y
be any small smoothing of
\bar{X}
.
The subspace
F^{2}_{Y}=F^{2}H^{3}(Y;\mathbb{C})
satisfies:

F^{2}_{Y}\cap\overline{F^{2}_{Y}}=0.

(2)

We have a Hodge decomposition
H^{3}(Y;\mathbb{C})=H^{3,0}\oplus H^{0,3}\oplus H^{2,1}\oplus H^{1,2}
where
H^{p,q}=F^{p}_{Y}\cap\overline{F^{q}_{Y}}
satisfies
H^{q,p}=\overline{H^{p,q}}
.
Moreover, the following Hodge-Riemann bilinear relation is true: for any
\eta\in H^{2,1}
,

-\sqrt{-1}\langle\eta,\bar{\eta}\rangle>0.

(3)

The proof of Theorem
1.2
uses Friedman’s calculation of limiting mixed Hodge structure
H^{3}_{\lim}
associated to certain semistable model of degeneration (as stated in Theorem
2.2
below). For the proof of (
2
), some part of our calculations is motivated by the calculation in
[
6
]
. However our use of the limiting mixed Hodge structure is different (see Remark
3.2
). The extra tool we use is Deligne’s (weak) splitting of mixed Hodge structures (see Theorem
2.3
).
The key new observation and an interesting phenomenon (based on elementary linear algebra) is that, the invariance of certain well-chosen basis sections of
F^{2}
under the monodromy operator implies that their wedge product has a leading term given by
\mathrm{Im}(z)\cdot|D|^{2}
where
z
is in the upper half-plane such that
\mathrm{Im}(z)\gg 1
when the smoothing is small and
D
is a smooth function on the base of deformation which is bounded away from zero (see (
20
)).
The proof of Hodge-Riemann bilinear relation (
3
) is more delicate.
We first observe that
\{H^{2,1}_{s}=F^{2}_{Y_{s}}\cap\overline{F^{1}_{Y_{s}}};s\in S\}
, where
S
is the base of deformations, form a sub-vector bundle which allows us to extend a well-chosen basis from
H^{2,1}_{\lim}
and consider intersection pairing on
Y
as a block matrix. The restriction of intersection pairing to a one-dimensional subspace can be shown to be positive by a similar technique as before. We then reduce the problem to proving the positive definiteness of a sub-matrix on the complement after a suitable change of basis.
The key is to show that this sub-matrix is essentially a perturbation of the intersection pairing on
X
(Lemma
3.6
), which allows to transmit the Hodge-Riemann relation on
H^{3}(X)
to the positive definiteness of this sub-matrix. The method in this paper should also work for some other examples of non-Kähler manifolds obtained in similar smoothing process, and this will be studied elsewhere.

,
it is clear that, to prove the positive definiteness of
Q
, it suffices to prove that the sub-matrix
P:=\hat{Q}-\rho^{-1}\phi\phi^{*}
is positive definite.
For simplicity of notation, denote
y=\mathrm{Im}(z)>0
. Let us calculate by using (
28
):

\displaystyle\rho\cdot P_{ij}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho Q_{ij}-\phi_{i}\overline{\phi_{j}}=Q(v_{h+1},\overline{v_{h+1}})Q(v_{i},\overline{v_{j}})-Q(v_{i},\overline{v_{h+1}})Q(v_{h+1},\overline{v_{j}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(Q(v^{\prime}_{h+1},\overline{v^{\prime}_{h+1}})+2y|D_{h+1}|^{2}+\sqrt{-1}(-C_{h+1}\overline{D_{h+1}}+D_{h+1}\overline{C_{h+1}}\right)

\displaystyle\cdot\left(Q(v^{\prime}_{i},\overline{v^{\prime}_{j}})+2yD_{i}\overline{D_{j}}+\sqrt{-1}(-C_{i}\overline{D_{j}}+D_{i}\overline{C_{j}})\right)

\displaystyle-\left(Q(v^{\prime}_{i},\overline{v^{\prime}_{h+1}})+2yD_{i}\overline{D_{h+1}}+\sqrt{-1}(-C_{i}\overline{D_{h+1}}+D_{i}\overline{C_{h+1}})\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt\cdot\left(Q(v^{\prime}_{h+1},\overline{v^{\prime}_{j}})+2yD_{h+1}\overline{D_{j}}+\sqrt{-1}(-C_{h+1}\overline{D_{j}}+D_{h+1}\overline{C_{j}})\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2y|D_{h+1}|^{2}Q(v^{\prime}_{i},\overline{v^{\prime}_{j}})+2y|D_{h+1}|^{2}\sqrt{-1}(-C_{i}\overline{D_{j}}+D_{i}\overline{C_{j}})

\displaystyle+2yQ(v^{\prime}_{h+1},\overline{v^{\prime}_{h+1}})D_{i}\overline{D_{j}}+2yD_{i}\overline{D_{j}}\sqrt{-1}(-C_{h+1}\overline{D_{h+1}}+D_{h+1}\overline{C_{h+1}})

\displaystyle-2yD_{i}\overline{D_{h+1}}Q(v^{\prime}_{h+1},\overline{v^{\prime}_{j}})-2yD_{i}\overline{D_{h+1}}\sqrt{-1}(-C_{h+1}\overline{D_{j}}+D_{h+1}\overline{C_{j}})

\displaystyle-2yD_{h+1}\overline{D_{j}}Q(v^{\prime}_{i},\overline{v^{\prime}_{h+1}})-2yD_{h+1}\overline{D_{j}}\sqrt{-1}(-C_{i}\overline{D_{h+1}}+D_{i}\overline{C_{h+1}})

\displaystyle+O(1).

The points of this calculation are that the
y^{2}
-term get (surprisingly) cancelled, and there is a (leading) term
2y|D_{h+1}|^{2}Q(v^{\prime}_{i},\overline{v^{\prime}_{j}})
while the other
y
-terms all contain at least one factor from
\{C_{i},D_{i},C_{j},D_{j}\}
with
i,j\in\{1,\dots,h\}
and hence can be written as
y\cdot O(|s|)
thanks to (
30
).
So we can write

\displaystyle P_{ij}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho^{-1}\cdot\rho P_{ij}=\frac{1}{2y(1+O(s))+O(1)}\left(2y|D_{h+1}|^{2}Q(v^{\prime}_{i},\overline{v^{\prime}_{j}})+y\cdot O(|s|)+O(1)\right)

(32)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|D_{h+1}|^{2}Q(v^{\prime}_{i},\overline{v^{\prime}_{j}})+O(|s|)+O(y^{-1}).

Because both terms
O(|s|)
and
O(y^{-1})
are negligible if
|s|\ll 1
is sufficiently small (see (
13
)), to prove that
\{P_{ij}\}
is positive definite, we just need to prove that
\{Q(v^{\prime}_{i},\overline{v^{\prime}_{j}})\}
is uniformly positive definite by which we mean that it is positive definite and is bounded from below by
\epsilon\cdot\mathrm{Id}_{h}
for some
\epsilon>0
independent of
s
.

Remark 3.5
.

If
\dim S=1
so that
s=\zeta
and
y=\mathrm{Im}(z)=-\frac{1}{2\pi}\log|s|
, then the calculations/estimates can be greatly simplified (as shown in the first arXiv-version of this paper).

Because
v^{\prime}_{i}
are small perturbations of
u_{i}
(see the property (i) after (
17
)), we see that
\{Q(v^{\prime}_{i},\overline{v^{\prime}_{j}})\}
is a small perturbation of the
h\times h
-matrix

\{Q^{\prime}_{ij}\}=\{Q(u_{i},\overline{u_{j}})\}_{i,j\in\{1,\dots,h\}}
which is just matrix of the restriction
Q|_{I^{2,1}}
with respect to the basis
\{u_{i};i=1,\dots,h\}
of
I^{2,1}
.
So it suffices to show that
\{Q^{\prime}_{ij}\}
is positive definite.

To prove this positivity, we first remark that
Q=-\sqrt{-1}\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
naturally induces a bilinear form
\bar{Q}
on
W_{3}/W_{2}
. In other words, for any
[u],[v]\in W_{3}/W_{2}
, let
u,v\in W_{3}
be their liftings and set:

\bar{Q}([u],[v])=Q(u,v)=-\sqrt{-1}\langle u,v\rangle.

(33)

Because
W_{2}=W_{3}^{\perp}
, the right-hand-side does not depend on the liftings of
[u],[v]
. Now according to the splitting (
7
), there is a canonical identification of
V=I^{3,0}\oplus I^{0,3}\oplus I^{2,1}\oplus I^{1,2}
with
W_{3}/W_{2}
which preserves the Hodge decomposition.
It is immediate from the defintion that under this identification the bilinear form
Q_{V}(\cdot,\cdot):=-\sqrt{-1}\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle|_{V}
is nothing but the the induced bilinear form on
W_{3}/W_{2}
. Now the key is the following lemma.

Lemma 3.6
.

Under the natural isomorphism of Hodge structures
W_{3}/W_{2}\cong H^{3}(\tilde{X})
, the bilinear form
\bar{Q}(\cdot,\cdot)
is isomorphic to the bilinear form
-\sqrt{-1}\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\tilde{X}}
on
H^{3}(\tilde{X})
.

This lemma would complete the proof of Theorem
1.2
. Indeed, with the identification
(V,Q_{V}(\cdot,\cdot))\cong(W_{3}/W_{2},\bar{Q}(\cdot,\cdot))
,
we know that the matrix
Q^{\prime}=\{Q(u_{i},\overline{u_{j}})\}=\{Q_{V}(u_{i},\overline{u_{j}})\}
is indeed positive definite by the second Hodge-Riemann bilinear relation for
\tilde{X}
.

Finally we only need to prove Lemma
3.6
.

Proof of Lemma
3.6
.

We first note that the morphisms of mixed Hodge structures from Theorem
2.2
.(i)-(ii) are induced by natural geometric maps.
Indeed, the quotient morphism
H^{3}(Y_{0})\rightarrow H^{0}(\tilde{X})
is simply induced by the closed embedding
\tilde{X}\rightarrow Y_{0}
.
On the other hand, the injective morphism
\iota:H^{3}(Y_{0})\cong H^{3}(\mathcal{Y})\rightarrow H^{3}_{\lim}\cong H^{3}(Y_{s})
(with
s\in S^{*}
) is induced by the composition
Y_{s}\rightarrow\mathcal{Y}^{*}\rightarrow\mathcal{Y}
and we used the fact that
Y_{0}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{Y}
is a homotopy equivalence thus inducing an isomorphism
H^{3}(Y_{0})\cong H^{3}(\mathcal{Y})
.

This description motivates our method to verify the statement of the lemma.
First we will lift any two elements
[u],[u^{\prime}]\in H^{3}(\tilde{X})=W_{3}/W_{2}
to elements in
H^{3}(\mathcal{Y})\cong H^{3}(Y_{0})=W_{3}
, and then restrict to get
u,u^{\prime}\in H^{3}(Y_{s})
. After this lifting, we will verify that the cup product of
u,u^{\prime}
on
Y_{s}
agree with the cup product of
[u]
and
[u^{\prime}]
on
\tilde{X}
.

This is indeed not difficult to achieve in by using de Rham cohomology. We first represent
[u]
by a closed differential form
\psi
. Let
U
be the disjoint union of open neighborhoods of exceptional divisors
E_{i}\cong\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}
in
\tilde{X}
. Then
U
is homotopic to a disjoint union of
\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}
. As a consequence,
H^{3}(U;\mathbb{C})=0
, and hence the closed form
\left.\psi\right|_{U}
is exact. So there exists a smooth 2-form
\theta
on
U
such that
\psi|_{U}=d\theta
. Let
\eta
be a cut-off function supported on
U
and is identically equal to 1 on a smaller open neighborhood
U_{1}
of
E_{i}
such that
\overline{U_{1}}
is a compact subset of
U
.
Then the differential form
\hat{\psi}=\psi-d(\eta\theta)
vanishes on
U_{1}
and represents
[u]
.
We now want to extend
\hat{\psi}
to get a closed two form
\hat{\Psi}
on
\mathcal{Y}
such that
\left.\hat{\Psi}\right|_{\tilde{X}_{0}}=\hat{\psi}
.
For simplicity of notation, set
\overline{U_{1}}^{c}:=\tilde{X}\setminus\overline{U_{1}}
so that
\hat{\psi}
is supported on
\overline{U_{1}}^{c}
.
By possibly shrinking
S
, we can then construct a deformation retraction
r:\mathcal{Y}\rightarrow Y_{0}
such that
r^{-1}(\overline{U_{1}}^{c})\cong\overline{U_{1}}^{c}\times S
and the retraction is given by the projection. Then we define
\hat{\Psi}=r^{*}\hat{\psi}
on
r^{-1}(\overline{U_{1}}^{c})
and is equal to 0 elsewhere. Then
\hat{\Psi}|_{Y_{s}}
represents
u\in\iota(H^{3}(\mathcal{Y}))\subset H^{3}(Y_{s})=H_{\lim}
.

If
[u^{\prime}]
is another element of
H^{3}(X;\mathbb{C})
, we can use the same process to get a lifting in
H^{3}(\mathcal{Y};\mathbb{C})
that is represented by a closed differential form
\hat{\Psi}^{\prime}
on
\mathcal{Y}
. So
u^{\prime}\in\iota(H^{3}(\mathcal{Y}))\subset H^{3}(Y_{s})=H_{\lim}
is represented by
\hat{\Psi}^{\prime}|_{Y_{s}}
.

Then by the change of variable formula for integrals, we get the wanted identity:

\langle[u],[u^{\prime}]\rangle_{\tilde{X}}=\int_{\tilde{X}}\psi\wedge\psi^{\prime}=\int_{Y_{s}}\hat{\Psi}|_{Y_{s}}\wedge\hat{\Psi}^{\prime}|_{Y_{s}}=\langle u,u^{\prime}\rangle_{H_{\lim}}.

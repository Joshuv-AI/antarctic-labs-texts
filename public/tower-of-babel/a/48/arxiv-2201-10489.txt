Sphere2Vec: Multi-Scale Representation Learning over a Spherical Surface for Geospatial Predictions
Gengchen Mai*, Yao Xuan, Wenyun Zuo, Krzysztof Janowicz, Ni Lao

Abstract
Generating learning-friendly representations for points in a 2D space is a fundamental and long-standing problem in machine learning. Recently, multi-scale encoding schemes (such as Space2Vec) were proposed to directly encode any point in 2D space as a high-dimensional vector, and has been successfully applied to various (geo)spatial prediction tasks. However, a map projection distortion problem rises when applying location encoding models to large-scale real-world GPS coordinate datasets (e.g., species images taken all over the world) - all current location encoding models are designed for encoding points in a 2D (Euclidean) space but not on a spherical surface, e.g., earth surface . To solve this problem, we propose a multi-scale location encoding model called S ​ p ​ h ​ e ​ r ​ e ​ 2 ​ V ​ e ​ c Sphere2Vec which directly encodes point coordinates on a spherical surface while avoiding the map projection distortion problem. We provide theoretical proof that the S ​ p ​ h ​ e ​ r ​ e ​ 2 ​ V ​ e ​ c Sphere2Vec encoding preserves the spherical surface distance between any two points. We also developed a unified view of distance-reserving encoding on spheres based on the Double Fourier Sphere (DFS). We apply S ​ p ​ h ​ e ​ r ​ e ​ 2 ​ V ​ e ​ c Sphere2Vec to the geo-aware image classification task. Our analysis shows that S ​ p ​ h ​ e ​ r ​ e ​ 2 ​ V ​ e ​ c Sphere2Vec outperforms other 2D space location encoder models especially on the polar regions and data-sparse areas for image classification tasks because of its nature for spherical surface distance preservation.

Problem Formulation

Distributed representation of point-features on the spherical surface
can be formulated as follows.
Given a set of points
\mathcal{P}=\{\boldsymbol{\mathbf{x}}_{i}\}
on the surface of a sphere
\mathbb{S}^{2}
, e.g., species occurrences all over the world, where
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{x}}_{i}=(\lambda_{i},\phi_{i})\in\mathbb{S}^{2}
indicates a point with longitude
\lambda_{i}\in[-\pi,\pi)
and latitude
\phi_{i}\in[-{\pi}/{2},{\pi}/{2}]
.
Define a function
Enc_{\mathcal{P},\theta}(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{x}}):\mathbb{S}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
which is parameterized by
\theta
and maps any coordinate
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{x}}
in a spherical surface
\mathbb{S}^{2}
to a vector representation of
d
dimension.

Let
Enc(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{x}})=\mathbf{NN}(PE_{S}(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{x}}))

where
\mathbf{NN}()
is a learnable multi-layer perceptron with
h
hidden layers and
k
neurons per layer. We want to find a function
PE_{S}(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{x}})
which does a one-to-one mapping from each point
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{x}}_{i}=(\lambda_{i},\phi_{i})\in\mathbb{S}^{2}
to a multi-scale representation with
S
as the total number of scales such that it satisfy the following requirement:

\displaystyle\langle PE_{S}(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{x}}_{1}),PE_{S}(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{x}}_{2})\rangle=f(\Delta D),\forall\boldsymbol{\mathbf{x}}_{1},\boldsymbol{\mathbf{x}}_{2}\in\mathbb{S}^{2},

(1)

where
\Delta D\in[0,\pi R]
is the spherical surface distance between
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{x}}_{1},\boldsymbol{\mathbf{x}}_{2}
,
R
is the radius of this sphere, and
f(x)
is a strictly monotonically decreasing function for
x\in[0,\pi R]
. In other words, we expect to find a function
PE_{S}(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{x}})
such that the resulting multi-scale representation of
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{x}}
preserves the spherical surface distance while it is more learning-friendly for the downstream neuron network model
\mathbf{NN}()
.

Pseudospectral Methods on Spheres

Much study has been done for the numerical solutions on spheres, for example, in weather prediction

[
29
,
30
,
26
]
.
The main idea is so called pseudospectral methods which leverage truncated discrete Fourier transformation on spheres to achieve computation efficiency while avoiding the error caused by projection distortion.
The particular set of basis functions to be used depends on the particular problem.
However, they do not aim at learning good representations in machine learning models. In this study we try to make connections to these approaches and explore how their insights can be realized in a deep learning model.

Sphere2Vec

5.1
Proof of Theorem 1

Proof.

Since
PE_{1,sphereC}(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{x}}_{i})=[\sin(\phi_{i}),\cos(\phi_{i})\cos(\lambda_{i}),\cos(\phi_{i})\sin(\lambda_{i})]
for
i=1,2
,
the inner product

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\langle PE_{1,sphereC}(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{x}}_{1}),PE_{1,sphereC}(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{x}}_{2})\rangle\\
&=\sin(\phi_{1})\sin(\phi_{2})+\cos(\phi_{1})\cos(\lambda_{1})\cos(\phi_{2})\cos(\lambda_{2})+\cos(\phi_{1})\sin(\lambda_{1})\cos(\phi_{2})\sin(\lambda_{2})\\
&=\sin(\phi_{1})\sin(\phi_{2})+\cos(\phi_{1})\cos(\phi_{2})\cos(\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2})\\
&=\cos(\Delta\delta)=\cos(\Delta D/R),\end{split}

(11)

where
\Delta\delta
is the central angle between
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{x}}_{1}
and
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{x}}_{2}
, and the spherical law of cosines is applied to derive the second last equality.
So,

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\|PE_{1,sphereC}(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{x}}_{1})-PE_{1,sphereC}(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{x}}_{2})\|^{2}\\
&=\langle PE_{1,sphereC}(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{x}}_{1})-PE_{1,sphereC}(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{x}}_{2}),PE_{1,sphereC}(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{x}}_{1})-PE_{1,sphereC}(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{x}}_{2})\rangle\\
&=2-2\cos(\Delta D/R)\\
&=4\sin^{2}(\Delta D/2R).\end{split}

(12)

So
\|PE_{1,sphereC}(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{x}}_{1})-PE_{1,sphereC}(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{x}}_{2})\|=2\sin(\Delta D/2R)
since
\Delta D/2R\in[0,\frac{\pi}{2}]
.
By Taylor expansion,
\|PE_{1,sphereC}(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{x}}_{1})-PE_{1,sphereC}(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{x}}_{2})\|\approx\Delta D/R
when
\Delta D
is small w.r.t.
R
.
∎

7
Conclusion

In this work, we propose a general purpose multi-scale spherical location encoder -
Sphere2Vec
which can encode any location on the spherical surface into a high dimension vector which is learning-friendly for downstream neuron network models. We provide theoretical proof that
Sphere2Vec
is able to preserve the spherical surface distance between points.
We conduct experiments on the geo-aware image classification with 5 large-scale real-world datasets.
Results shows that
Sphere2Vec
can outperform the state-of-the-art 2D location encoders on both tasks. Further analysis shows that
Sphere2Vec
is especially excel at polar regions as well data-sparse areas.

The Map Projection Distortion Problem

Map projection distortion is unavoidable when projecting spherical coordinates into 2D space.
There are no map projection can preserve distances at all direction.
The so-called equidistant projection can only preserve distance on one direction, e.g., the longitude direction for the equirectangular projection (See Figure

9.2
A Brief Overview of Geo-Aware Image Classification Task

If we assume
\mathbf{I}
and
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{x}}
are conditionally independent given
y
, then based on Bayes’ theorem, we have

\displaystyle P(y|\mathbf{I},\boldsymbol{\mathbf{x}})=\dfrac{P(\mathbf{I},\boldsymbol{\mathbf{x}}|y)P(y)}{P(\mathbf{I},\boldsymbol{\mathbf{x}})}=\dfrac{P(\mathbf{I}|y)P(\mathbf{I})}{P(y)}\dfrac{P(y|\boldsymbol{\mathbf{x}})P(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{x}})}{P(y)}\dfrac{P(y)}{P(\mathbf{I},\boldsymbol{\mathbf{x}})}\;\propto\;P(y|\boldsymbol{\mathbf{x}})P(y|\mathbf{I})

(18)

Fig
1(a)
illustrates the whole workflow. The major objective for this task is to learn a geographic prior distribution
P(y|\boldsymbol{\mathbf{x}})\;\propto\;\sigma(Enc(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{x}})\mathbf{T}_{:,y})
such that all observed species occurrences (all image locations
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{x}}
as well as their associated species class
y
) have maximum probability. Mac Aodha et al.
[
21
]
used a loss function which is based on maximum likelihood estimation (MLE). Given a set of training samples - data points and their associated class labels
\mathbb{X}=\{(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{x}},y)\}
, the loss function
\mathcal{L}^{image}(\mathbb{X})
is defined as:

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathcal{L}^{image}(\mathbb{X})=\sum_{(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{x}},y)\in\mathbb{X}}\sum_{\boldsymbol{\mathbf{x}}^{-}\in\mathcal{N}(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{x}})}\Big(&\beta\log(\sigma(Enc(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{x}})\mathbf{T}_{:,y}))+\\
&\sum_{i=1,i\neq y}^{c}\log(1-\sigma(Enc(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{x}})\mathbf{T}_{:,i}))+\\
&\sum_{i=1}^{c}\log(1-\sigma(Enc(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{x}}^{-})\mathbf{T}_{:,i}))\Big)\end{split}

(19)

Here,
\beta
is a hyperparameter to enlarge the weight of positive samples.
\mathcal{N}(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{x}})
represents the negative sample set of point
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{x}}
while
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{x}}^{-}\in\mathcal{N}(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{x}})
is a negative sample uniformly generated from the spherical surface given each data point
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{x}}
. We follow the same model set up but replace the
wrap
location encoder with our
Sphere2Vec
model.

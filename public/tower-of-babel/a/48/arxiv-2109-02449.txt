Ricci curvature integrals, local functionals, and the Ricci flow
Yuanqing Ma, Bing Wang

Abstract
Consider a Riemannian manifold ( M m , g ) (M^{m},g) whose volume is the same as the standard sphere ( S m , g r ​ o ​ u ​ n ​ d ) (S^{m},g_{round}) . If p > m 2 p>\frac{m}{2} and ∫ M { R ​ c − ( m − 1 ) ​ g } − p ​ 𝑑 v \int_{M}\left\{Rc-(m-1)g\right\}_{-}^{p}dv is sufficiently small, we show that the normalized Ricci flow initiated from ( M m , g ) (M^{m},g) will exist immortally and converge to the standard sphere. The choice of p p is optimal.



1
Introduction

The classical Myer’s theorem states that if a closed Riemannian manifold
(M^{m},g)
satisfies

\displaystyle[left=\empheqlbrace\,]

\displaystyle Rc\geq m-1,

(1.1a)

\displaystyle|M|_{g}=(m+1)\omega_{m+1},

(1.1b)

where
\omega_{m}
is the volume of unit ball in the Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{m}
. Then the inequality (
1.1a
) must be an equality, and the manifold
(M^{m},g)
must be isometric to
(S^{m},g_{round})
.
It is a natural question to find a quantative version of this rigidity theorem.
If we replace (
1.1
) by the following conditions

\displaystyle[left=\empheqlbrace\,]

\displaystyle Rc\geq m-1-\delta,

(1.2a)

\displaystyle|M|_{g}=(m+1)\omega_{m+1},

(1.2b)

for some small number
\delta<\delta_{0}(m)
, we can still declare many properties of
M
.
For example, by the deep work of Perelman
[
29
]
, we know that such
M
must be homeomorphic to
S^{m}
.
The seminal work of Colding
[
10
]

[
11
]
proved that
(M,g)
is Gromov-Hausdorff close to the standard sphere
(S^{m},g_{round})
.
Furthermore, it is proved by the foundational work of Cheeger-Colding
[
9
]
that
M
is diffeomorphic to
S^{m}
, and
(M,g)
is uniformly bi-Hölder equivalent to
(S^{m},g_{round})
.
Their proof relies on the Reifenberg method.
In
[
40
]
, the second named author proved that the normalized Ricci flow initiated from
(M,g)
exists immortally and converges to a round metric
g_{\infty}
,
and the identity map from
(M,g)
to
(M,g_{\infty})
is uniformly bi-Hölder.
Therefore,
[
40
]
provides an alternative proof of Cheeger-Colding’ result, via Ricci flow smoothing, which seems to be natural and was intensively studied (e.g. See
[
14
]

[
37
]

[
24
]
and the references therein.).

The results mentioned above are based on the point-wise Ricci lower bound.
It is interesting to investigate whether this point-wise Ricci lower bound can be replaced by an integral Ricci lower bound.
Actually, many important results based on integral Ricci lower bound were established by the work of Petersen-Wei
[
32
]

[
33
]
, D. Yang
[
45
]

[
46
]

[
47
]
, S. Gallot
[
18
]
, etc.
The Ricci flow behavior with initial data satisfying Ricci or other curvature’s integral pinching conditions was also studied by many people, e.g., see
[
43
]

[
44
]

[
6
]
.
Under an appropriate integral Ricci curvature condition(cf. Definition
2.1
for precise definitions), we have the following theorem.

Theorem 1.1

(
Main Theorem
)
.

For each
m\geq 3
and
p>\frac{m}{2}
, there is a constant
\delta_{0}=\delta_{0}(m,p)
with the following property.

Suppose
(M^{m},g)
is a Riemannian manifold satisfying

\displaystyle[left=\empheqlbrace\,]

\displaystyle\int_{M}\left\{Rc-(m-1)g\right\}_{-}^{p}dv<\delta,

(1.3a)

\displaystyle|M|_{g}=(m+1)\omega_{m+1},

(1.3b)

where
|\cdot|
means volume,
\omega_{m+1}
is the volume of unit ball in
\mathbb{R}^{m+1}
, and
\delta\in(0,\delta_{0})
.
Then the normalized Ricci flow initiated from
(M^{m},g)
exists immortally and converges to a round metric
g_{\infty}
exponentially fast.
The limit metric
(M,g_{\infty})
has constant sectional curvature
1
.

Furthermore, for each pair of points
x,y\in M
satisfying
d_{g}(x,y)\leq 1
, the following distance bi-Hölder estimate holds:

\displaystyle e^{-\psi}d_{g}^{1+\psi}(x,y)\leq d_{g_{\infty}}(x,y)\leq e^{\psi}d_{g}^{1-\psi}(x,y),

(1.4)

where
\psi=\psi(\delta|m,p)
such that
\displaystyle\lim_{\delta\to 0^{+}}\psi(\delta|m,p)=0
.

Note that Theorem
1.1
implies the following topological rigidity result.

Corollary 1.2

(Petersen-Sprouse
[
31
]
, Aubry
[
1
]
)
.

If
(M^{m},g)
satisfies the condition (
1.3
), then
M
is diffeomorphic to standard sphere
S^{m}
and is close to the standard sphere in the Gromov-Hausdorff topology:

\displaystyle d_{GH}\left\{(M^{m},g),(S^{m},g_{round})\right\}<\psi(\delta|m,p).

(1.5)

We remark that the conclusion of Corollary
1.2
was obtained by Petersen-Sprouse
[
31
]
under the condition (cf. Definition
2.2
)

\displaystyle\kappa(1,p,1)\coloneqq\sup_{x\in M}\left\{\frac{\int_{B(x,1)}\{Rc-(m-1)g\}_{-}^{p}dv}{|B(x,1)|}\right\}^{\frac{1}{p}}<\xi(m,p)

(1.6)

for some small number
\xi=\xi(m,p)
. The above condition can be deduced from (
1.3
) by the diameter bound of Aubry
[
1
]
.
One may wonder whether
p
can be chosen as
\frac{m}{2}
.
However, this expectation is destroyed by the construction of Aubry.
In view of Theorem 9.2 of
[
1
]
, for arbitrary closed Riemannian manifold
(N^{m},h)
satisfying
|N|=(m+1)\omega_{m+1}
and
\delta>0
, one can construct a Riemannian metric
(M,g)

of the same volume satisfying

\displaystyle d_{GH}\left\{(M,g),(N,h)\right\}<\delta,\quad\textrm{and}\quad\int_{M}\left\{Rc-(m-1)g\right\}_{-}^{\frac{m}{2}}dv<\delta.

(1.7)

Also, it is not hard to see from the construction in
[
1
]
that the topology of
M
could be far away from
S^{m}
.
Therefore, the coefficient
p>\frac{m}{2}
in Theorem
1.1
is optimal.
It would be a possible generalization to replace
\left\{Rc-(m-1)g\right\}_{-}
in equation (
1.7
) by
\left\{Rc-(m-1)g\right\}
.
If such generalization holds, then Corollary
1.2
can be improved to a topological rigidity result in the similar spirit as in Margerin
[
28
]
, Bour-Carron
[
3
]
, and Chang-Gursky-Yang
[
7
]
, etc.

We briefly discuss the proof of Theorem
1.1
.
The first thing need to be done is to obtain uniform existence time of the Ricci flow solution initiated from
(M,g)
, say
t\geq 1
.
Then we shall show that the volume closeness at
t=0
implies the Gromov-Hausdorff closeness at
t=0
, and consequently
C^{0}
-closeness
for all
t\in[0.5,1]
. By standard regularity improvement, we then obtain
C^{\infty}
-closeness between
(M,g(1))
and
(S^{m},g_{round})
.
Then it is well-known (cf.
[
25
]

[
2
]

[
4
]
) that the normalized Ricci flow started from
g(1)

exists forever and converges to the standard sphere
(S^{m},g_{round})
.
The strategy described above is the same as the one used in
[
40
]
. However, there exist essential technical difficulties to be overcome.

The first difficulty
is to obtain uniform existence time for the Ricci flow initiated from
(M,g)
.
In
[
40
]
, this is achieved through the application of the improved pseudo-locality, whose key is
the estimate of the local
\boldsymbol{\mu}
-functional. Note that in
[
40
]
, the
\boldsymbol{\mu}
-functional can be obtained either through
the blowup analysis, or it can be derived through the isoperimetric constant estimate via the needle-decomposition method for RCD space (cf.
[
5
]
).
However, in the current setting, the Ricci curvature is not bounded from below point-wisely.
Thus the RCD theory can not be applied directly here. Fortunately, the blowup analysis method survives, as it only requires gradient estimates, volume comparison theorems and compactness properties,
all of which are available.

The second difficulty
is the distance distortion estimate.
Note that it is a key ingredient for the distance distortion estimate to obtain the the smallness of the space-time integral
\iint|R|dvdt
, which is the almost Einstein condition.
In
[
40
]
, this condition is obtained through applying maximum principle for the scalar curvature
R
, since the almost non-negativity of
R
is preserved under the Ricci flow. In the current paper, we only have an integral estimate of
|Rc_{-}|
at initial time.
Therefore, the maximum principle argument fails. We overcome this difficulty by delicately applying the curvature condition

\displaystyle t|Rm|(x,t)\leq\epsilon,

(1.8)

where
\epsilon=\epsilon(m)
is a small number far less than
1
.
We construct new cutoff functions and develop an ODE system for rough volume ratio upper bound and
\int|R_{-}|^{p}
.
Then it follows from ODE comparison that both of them are well-controlled. A further interpolation argument then implies the almost Einstein condition.

In conclusion, the first difficulty can be overcome if we are able to apply the pseudo-locality property in Theorem 1.2 of
[
40
]
.
Then it suffices to show a delicate
\boldsymbol{\mu}
-functional estimate under the initial metric, which is achieved by the following theorem.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
(M,g)
be a closed Riemannian manifold of dimension
m\geq 3
and
p>\frac{m}{2}
. For
x_{0}\in M
and each pair of positive numbers
(\eta,A)
, there exists a constant
\delta=\delta(m,p,\eta,A)
with the following properties.

Suppose

\displaystyle[left=\empheqlbrace\,]

\displaystyle\int_{B(x_{0},\delta^{-1})}Rc_{-}^{p}dv\leq\frac{1}{2}\omega_{m}\delta^{4p-m},

(1.9a)

\displaystyle\left|B\left(x_{0},\delta^{-1}\right)\right|\geq(1-\delta)\omega_{m}\delta^{-m}.

(1.9b)

Then we have

\displaystyle\inf\limits_{\tau\in\left[\frac{1}{3},3\right]}\boldsymbol{\bar{\mu}}\left(B(x_{0},A),g,\tau\right)\geq-\frac{\eta}{2},

(1.10)

\displaystyle\inf\limits_{\tau\in\left[\frac{1}{2},2\right]}\boldsymbol{\mu}\left(B(x_{0},A),g,\tau\right)\geq-\eta.

(1.11)

Note that (
1.9
) is a version of the “almost Euclidean” condition.
(
1.9b
) means the volume ratio is very close to the Euclidean one, (
1.9a
) means that Ricci curvature is almost non-negative in the integral sense.
The coefficient
\frac{1}{2}\omega_{m}
in (
1.9a
) is only for technical convenience and could be replaced by other small constant.
The entropy estimate (
1.11
) assures us to apply the pseudo-locality theorem. In particular, we shall have the curvature estimate (
1.8
).
Therefore, under the help of condition (
1.8
), we are able to overcome the second difficulty in light of the following theorem.

Theorem 1.4
.

Suppose
\{(M^{m},g(t)),0\leq t\leq 1\}
is a Ricci flow solution,
r_{0}\geq 3
and
p>\frac{m}{2}
.
Suppose

\displaystyle[left=\empheqlbrace\,]

\displaystyle\left|B_{g(0)}(x_{0},r_{0})\right|_{dv_{g(0)}}\leq V_{0},

(1.12a)

\displaystyle\int_{B_{g(0)}(x_{0},r_{0})}R_{-,g(0)}^{p}dv_{g(0)}\leq\epsilon,

(1.12b)

and

\displaystyle R_{-,g(t)}(x)\leq\frac{\epsilon}{t},\quad\forall\;x\in B_{g(0)}(x_{0},r_{0}),\;t\in(0,1].

(1.13)

Then we have

\displaystyle[left=\empheqlbrace\,]

\displaystyle\left|B_{g(0)}(x_{0},r_{0}-2)\right|_{dv_{g(t)}}\leq V_{0}+\frac{(V_{0}+\epsilon)\left(e^{2Ct}-1\right)}{2},

(1.14a)

\displaystyle\int_{B_{g(0)}(x_{0},r_{0}-2)}R_{-,g(t)}^{p}dv_{g(t)}\leq\epsilon+\frac{(V_{0}+\epsilon)\left(e^{2Ct}-1\right)}{2},

(1.14b)

for all
t\in[0,1]
. Furthermore,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}dt\int_{B_{g(0)}(x_{0},r_{0}-2)}R_{-,g(t)}dv_{g(t)}\leq\psi(\epsilon|m,p,V_{0}),

(1.15)

where
\psi=\psi(\epsilon|m,p)
such that
\displaystyle\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\psi(\epsilon|m,p)=0
.

By Theorem
1.4
, we actually obtain the volume ratio upper bound and the
L^{p}
-integral bound of scalar curvature along the flow, under appropriate initial conditions.
If we have the almost Euclidean condition (
1.9
) at initial time, then conditions (
1.12
) and (
1.13
) in Theorem
1.4
are all satisfied automatically.
Furthermore, the inequality (
1.15
) implies the local almost Einstein condition, via another volume comparison argument.

Theorem 1.5
.

Suppose
\{(M^{m},g(t)),0\leq t\leq 1\}
is a Ricci flow solution,
m\geq 3
and
p>\frac{m}{2}
.
For
x_{0}\in M
and each pair of positive numbers
(\xi,A)
, there exists a constant
\delta=\delta(m,p,\xi,A)
with the following property.

If (
1.9
) is satisfied with respect to the initial metric
g(0)
, then we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\int_{B_{g(0)}(x_{0},A)}|R|dv_{g(t)}dt\leq\xi.

(1.16)

In light of the local almost Einstein condition (
1.16
), we are able to develop the distance distortion estimate.
Thus the second difficulty is overcome. Consequently, we can apply the distance distortion estimate and
C^{0}
-continuous dependence of initial data
for the Ricci-Deturck flow, as done in
[
40
]
, to obtain the continuous dependence of the metric with respect to the Gromov-Hausdorff topology.

Theorem 1.6

(
Continuous dependence in the
C^{\infty}
-Cheeger-Gromov topology
)
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{N}=\left\{\left(N^{m},h(t)\right),0\leq t\leq T\right\}
is a (normalized) Ricci flow solution on a closed manifold
N
, initiated from
h(0)=h
, and
p>\frac{m}{2}
.
For each
\epsilon
small, there exists
\eta=\eta(\mathcal{N},p,\epsilon)
with the following properties.

Suppose
\left(M^{m},g\right)
is a Riemannian manifold satisfying

\displaystyle\kappa(p,\epsilon)\leq\epsilon^{2-\frac{m}{p}},\quad d_{GH}\left\{(M,g),(N,h)\right\}<\eta

(1.17)

where
p>\frac{m}{2}
. Then the (normalized) Ricci flow initiated from
(M,g)
exists on
\left[0,T\right]
. Furthermore, there exists a family of diffeomorphisms
\left\{\Phi_{t}:N\longrightarrow M,\epsilon\leq t\leq T\right\}
such that

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{t\in\left[\epsilon,T\right]}\left\|\Phi_{t}^{*}g(t)-h(t)\right\|_{C^{\left[\epsilon^{-1}\right]}(N,h(t))}<\epsilon.

(1.18)

In Theorem 1.3 of
[
40
]
, the author proved the continuous dependence when
Rc_{M}>-(m-1)A
.
In the current paper, this condition is replaced by the integral condition
\kappa(p,\epsilon)<\epsilon^{2-\frac{m}{p}}
.
Note that if
\epsilon<\left(\frac{1}{(m-1)A}\right)^{\frac{p}{m}}
, then
Rc>-(m-1)A
implies that
\kappa(p,\epsilon)<\epsilon^{2-\frac{m}{p}}
.
Therefore, Theorem
1.18
is an improvement of Theorem 1.3 in
[
40
]
.

It is not hard to see that Theorem
1.1
follows from Theorem
1.6
.
In fact, if condition (
1.3
) is satisfied for sufficiently small
\delta
, then it follows from Petersen-Sprouse
[
31
]
and Aubry
[
1
]
that (
1.17
) holds with
(N^{m},h)=(S^{m},g_{round})
.
Thus we can apply Theorem
1.6
to obtain that the normalized Ricci flow started from
(M,g)
exists on
[0,1]
. Furthermore, the metric
g(1)
is very close to
g_{round}
in
C^{5}
-topology.
In particular, the curvature operator of
g(1)
is nearby the standard one. Thus we can apply Huisken’s theorem
[
25
]
to obtain the convergence of the normalized Ricci flow.
The remaining argument is the same as that in
[
40
]
.

Reviewing the proof of Theorem
1.1
, it is clear that whenever the major two difficulties (almost Euclidean entropy estimate and almost Einstein condition) are overcome,
most of the theorems in
[
40
]
can be naturally extended to the current situation. For example, when the metric is Gromov-Hausdorff close to a stable Einstein manifold,
then the normalized Ricci flow has global existence and convergence. The details will be provided at the end of this paper in Section
6
.
We also remark that Theorem
1.3
, Theorem
1.4
and Theorem
1.5
have their independent interests, rather than only being intermediate steps to obtain Theorem
1.1
.

The structure of the paper is described as follows. In Section
2
, we review some elementary materials needed in the paper.
In Section
3
, we settle the first difficulty by developing the local
\boldsymbol{\mu}
-functional estimate in Theorem
1.3
.
In Section
4
, we overcome the second difficulty and obtain the almost Einstein condition, by proving Theorem
1.4
and Theorem
1.5
.
In Section
5
, we follow the strategy of
[
40
]
to obtain the distance distortion estimate and we improve the rough distance distortion estimate to
C^{\infty}
-closeness, i.e., Theorem
1.6
.
Finally, in Section
6
, we study the stability of the Ricci flow and prove Theorem
1.1
.

Preliminaries

In this section, we fix notations and overview important results under the integral Ricci curvature condition.

Let
(M^{m},g)
be a closed Riemannian manifold of dimension
m
. Let
p
be a positive number for studying
L^{p}
-norm of Ricci curvature.
Without further explanation, we always assume by default that

\displaystyle m\geq 3,\quad\textrm{and}\quad p>\frac{m}{2}.

(2.1)

We shall use
|\cdot|_{dv_{g}}
to denote volume of domains in the Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
, and the subscription
g
will be omitted if
g
is clear in the context.
When no confusion is possible, we also use
|\cdot|
to denote the area of hypersurfaces in a Riemannian manifold.
In the situation we want to highlight the underlying metric to define the volume, we also use
vol_{g}(\cdot)
to denote volume.
We use
\omega_{m}
to denote the volume of unit ball in
\mathbb{R}^{m}
. Therefore, the standard round sphere of sectional curvature
1
has volume
(m+1)\omega_{m+1}
.

The following notations are commonly used in the study of comparison geometry in terms of the integral Ricci curvature condition.

Definition 2.1
.

For any
x\in M
, let
\zeta(x)
be the smallest eigenvalue of the Ricci tensor
Rc:T_{x}M\rightarrow T_{x}M
.
Then we define

\displaystyle Rc_{-}(x)\coloneqq\left|\min\left\{0,\zeta(x)\right\}\right|,

(2.2)

\displaystyle\{Rc-(m-1)g\}_{-}(x)\coloneqq\left|\min\left\{0,\zeta(x)-(m-1)\right\}\right|,

(2.3)

\displaystyle R_{-}(x)\coloneqq\left|\min\left\{{0,R}\right\}\right|,

(2.4)

where
R=tr_{g}Rc
is the scalar curvature.

By definition, it is clear that

\displaystyle 0\leq R_{-}\leq mRc_{-}.

(2.5)

Definition 2.2
.

For
p,r>0
, we define

\displaystyle\kappa_{g}(x,p,r):=r^{2}\left(\fint_{B_{g}(x,r)}Rc_{-,g}^{p}dv_{g}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}=r^{2}\left(\frac{\int_{B_{g}(x,r)}Rc_{-,g}^{p}dv_{g}}{vol_{g}\left(B_{g}(x,r)\right)}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},

(2.6)

\displaystyle\kappa_{g}(p,r):=\sup\limits_{x\in M}\kappa_{g}(x,p,r).

(2.7)

For a constant
\lambda
, we can similarly define

\displaystyle\kappa_{g}(x,\lambda,p,r):=r^{2}\left(\fint_{B_{g}(x,r)}\left|\min\left\{0,\zeta(x)-(m-1)\lambda\right\}\right|^{p}dv_{g}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},

(2.8)

\displaystyle\kappa_{g}(\lambda,p,r):=\sup\limits_{x\in M}\kappa_{g}(x,\lambda,p,r).

(2.9)

The subscription
g
will be omitted if there is no ambiguity.

It is clear that
\kappa_{g}(\lambda,p,r)=\kappa_{g}(p,r)
when
\lambda=0
.
Note that
\kappa_{g}(p,r)
is scaling invariant. Namely,
\kappa_{g}(p,r)=\kappa_{a^{2}g}(p,ar)
for each
a>0
.

Definition 2.3
.

For a function
f
on
(M,g)
, we define

\displaystyle\left|\left|f\right|\right|^{*}_{p,B(x,r)}:=\left(\fint_{B(x,r)}\left|f\right|^{p}dv\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}=\left(\frac{\int_{B(x,r)}\left|f\right|^{p}dv}{vol\left(B(x,r)\right)}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},

(2.10)

\displaystyle\left|\left|f\right|\right|_{p,B(x,r)}:=\left(\int_{B(x,r)}\left|f\right|^{p}dv\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

(2.11)

Here
\fint
denotes the mean value of integration.

The Sobolev constant estimate and the non-collapsing condition are closely related.
The existence of uniform Sobolev constant naturally implies the non-collapsing condition(e.g. Proposition 2.4 of
[
42
]
).
On the other hand, the non-collapsing condition also implies the estimate of isoperimetric constant and hence the Sobolev constant(e.g. Theorem 3 of
[
18
]
and Theorem 7.4 of
[
45
]
).
If we consider the normalized volume, then non-collapsing always holds true formally and so does the Sobolev constant.
The following estimate was part of Corollary 4.6 of
[
15
]
.

Lemma 2.4
.

There exists a small constant
\epsilon=\epsilon(m,p)>0
and some
C_{S}=C(m)r
such that if
\kappa(p,1)\leq\epsilon
, there holds that

\left|\left|f\right|\right|^{*}_{\frac{2m}{m-2},B(x,r)}\leq C_{S}\left|\left|\nabla f\right|\right|^{*}_{2,B(x,r)}

(2.12)

for any
r\in(0,1)
,
f\in C^{\infty}_{0}(B(x,r))
and consequently, for all
f\in W^{1,2}_{0}(B(x,r))
.

Given
z\in M
, let
r(y)=d(y,z)
be the distance function and

\displaystyle\iota(y)\coloneqq\left(\Delta r-\frac{m-1}{r}\right)_{+}.

The classical Laplacian comparison states that, if the Ricci curvature
Rc_{M}
of
M
satisfies
Rc_{M}\geq 0
, then
\Delta r\leq\frac{m-1}{r}
, i.e.
\iota\equiv 0
. In
[
32
]
, this result is generalized to the case of integral Ricci bounds.

Lemma 2.5

(
[
32
]
, Lemma 2.2)
.

For any
p>\frac{m}{2}
and
r>0
, there hold

\displaystyle\left|\left|\iota\right|\right|_{2p,B(z,r)}\leq\left(\frac{(m-1)(2p-1)}{2p-m}\left|\left|Rc_{-}\right|\right|_{p,B(z,r)}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},

(2.13)

\displaystyle\left|\left|\iota\right|\right|^{*}_{2p,B(z,r)}\leq\left(\frac{(m-1)(2p-1)}{2p-m}\left|\left|Rc_{-}\right|\right|^{*}_{p,B(z,r)}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

(2.14)

We also need the following important consequences about volume comparison.

Lemma 2.6

(
Volume doubling property
,
[
33
]
, Theorem 2.1)
.

Let
(M,g)
be a Riemannian manifold of dimension
m\geq 3
and
p>\frac{m}{2}
. For a point
x_{0}\in M
, if
\kappa(x_{0},p,r_{2})\leq\epsilon
, there exists a constant

Step 10. Derive the desired contradiction.

Note that both
\left\|\varphi_{\infty}\right\|_{2,B(x_{\infty},A)}
and
\left\|\nabla\varphi_{\infty}\right\|_{2,B(x_{\infty},A)}
are finite by (
3.61
) and (
3.62
). Thus we have
\varphi_{\infty}\in W^{1,2}(B(x_{\infty},A))
.
In view of estimate (
3.62
), by setting
\varphi_{\infty}|_{\partial B(x_{\infty},A)}\equiv 0
, it is clear that
\varphi_{\infty}
is continuous up to boundary
\partial B(x_{\infty},A)
, which is clearly smooth.
Applying Theorem 2 of section 5.5 of
[
17
]
, we have
\varphi_{\infty}\in W^{1,2}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{m})
by setting
\varphi_{\infty}|_{\mathbb{R}^{m}\backslash B(x_{\infty},A)}\equiv 0
.
It follows from (
3.61
), (
3.60
), (
3.59
) and (
3.5
) that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}\left\{4\left|\nabla\varphi_{\infty}\right|^{2}-2\varphi^{2}_{\infty}\log\varphi_{\infty}-m\left(1+\log\sqrt{4\pi}\right)\varphi^{2}_{\infty}\right\}dv=\boldsymbol{\bar{\mu}}_{\infty}<\frac{-\eta}{2}<0,

(3.64)

which contradicts the Logarithm Sobolev inequality of Euclidean space (cf.
[
21
]
), which says the left hand side of the above equation is non-negative. This contradiction establishes the proof of (
3.3
).
∎

Next we want to prove the following inequality

\displaystyle\inf\limits_{\tau\in\left[\frac{1}{2},2\right]}\boldsymbol{\mu}\left(B(x_{0},A),g,\tau\right)\geq-\eta.

(3.65)

It suffices to show the following lemma:

Lemma 3.2
.

Suppose
p>\frac{m}{2}
,
C_{S}
is the
L^{2}
-Sobolev constant of
\Omega\subset M
.

If
\epsilon_{0}\coloneqq\left\{\int_{\Omega}R_{-}^{p}dv\right\}^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq\frac{1}{10C_{S}}
, then for any
\tau\in\left[\frac{1}{2},2\right]
and
\sigma=\tau e^{-C_{S}\epsilon_{0}}
, we have

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\mu}(\Omega,g,\tau)-\boldsymbol{\bar{\mu}}(\Omega,g,\sigma)\geq-C(m,C_{s})\epsilon_{0}.

(3.66)

Proof.

Suppose
\varphi
is a minimizer of
\boldsymbol{\mu}\left(\Omega,g,\tau\right)
. Namely, we have
\boldsymbol{\mu}\left(\Omega,g,\tau\right)=\boldsymbol{\mathcal{W}}(\Omega,g,\varphi,\tau)
and
\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{2}dv=1
.
Fix
\sigma>0
, it follows from definition(cf. Definition
2.9
) that

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\mu}\left(\Omega,g,\tau\right)-\boldsymbol{\bar{\mu}}\left(\Omega,g,\sigma\right)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\mathcal{W}}(\Omega,g,\varphi,\tau)-\boldsymbol{\overline{\mathcal{W}}}(\Omega,g,\varphi,\sigma)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{m}{2}\log\left(\frac{\tau}{\sigma}\right)+\int_{\Omega}\tau R\varphi^{2}dv+4(\tau-\sigma)\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\varphi|^{2}dv

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle-\frac{m}{2}\log\left(\frac{\tau}{\sigma}\right)-\int_{\Omega}\tau R_{-}\varphi^{2}dv+4(\tau-\sigma)\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\varphi|^{2}dv

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle-\frac{m}{2}\log\left(\frac{\tau}{\sigma}\right)-\tau\epsilon_{0}\left(\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{\frac{2p}{p-1}}dv\right)^{\frac{p-1}{p}}+4(\tau-\sigma)\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\varphi|^{2}dv,

(3.67)

where we applied Hölder’s inequality and the condition
\epsilon_{0}=\left\{\int_{\Omega}R_{-}^{p}dv\right\}^{\frac{1}{p}}
in the last step.

Since
p>\frac{m}{2}
, we can choose
\theta=1-\frac{m}{2p}\in(0,1)
such that
\frac{\theta}{1}+\frac{1-\theta}{\frac{m}{m-2}}=\frac{1}{\frac{p}{p-1}}
.
Then Hölder’s inequality and Young’s inequality yield that

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{\frac{2p}{p-1}}dv\right)^{\frac{p-1}{p}}\leq\left(\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{2}dv\right)^{\theta}\left(\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{\frac{2m}{m-2}}dv\right)^{\frac{(1-\theta)(m-2)}{m}}\leq\theta\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{2}dv+(1-\theta)\left(\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{\frac{2m}{m-2}}dv\right)^{\frac{m-2}{m}}.

Applying the normalization condition and the Sobolev inequality, we arrive at

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{\frac{2p}{p-1}}dv\right)^{\frac{p-1}{p}}\leq\theta+(1-\theta)C_{S}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\varphi|^{2}dv.

(3.68)

Putting (
3.68
) into (
3.67
), we obtain

\displaystyle\quad\boldsymbol{\mu}(\Omega,g,\tau)-\boldsymbol{\bar{\mu}}(\Omega,g,\sigma)

\displaystyle\geq-\frac{m}{2}\log\left(\frac{\tau}{\sigma}\right)-\tau\epsilon_{0}\theta+\left\{4(\tau-\sigma)-\tau\epsilon_{0}(1-\theta)C_{s})\right\}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\varphi|^{2}dv.

(3.69)

Plugging the particular value
\sigma=e^{-\epsilon_{0}C_{S}}\tau
into the above inequality yields that

\displaystyle\quad\boldsymbol{\mu}(\Omega,g,\tau)-\boldsymbol{\bar{\mu}}(\Omega,g,\sigma)

\displaystyle\geq-\frac{m}{2}C_{S}\epsilon_{0}-\tau\epsilon_{0}\theta+\left\{4(1-e^{-\epsilon_{0}C_{S}})-\epsilon_{0}C_{S}+\epsilon_{0}\theta C_{s}\right\}\tau\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\varphi|^{2}dv.

(3.70)

Since
\epsilon_{0}<\frac{1}{10C_{S}}
, it is clear that
4(1-e^{-\epsilon_{0}C_{S}})-\epsilon_{0}C_{S}>0
.
Therefore, the term inside the parenthesis of (
3.70
) is positive.
Recall that
\theta=1-\frac{m}{2p}\in(0,1)
and
\tau\in[\frac{1}{2},2]
. Then (
3.66
) follows directly from (
3.70
).
The proof of Lemma
3.2
is complete.
∎

Theorem 3.3
.

Same conditions as in Proposition
3.1
. If (
3.1a

3.1b

\displaystyle\inf\limits_{\tau\in\left[\frac{1}{2},2\right]}\boldsymbol{\mu}\left(B(x_{0},A),g,\tau\right)\geq-\eta,

(3.71)

\displaystyle\inf_{t\in(0,1]}\boldsymbol{\mu}\left(B_{g(0)}\left(x_{0},A\sqrt{t}\right),g(0),t\right)\geq-\eta.

(3.72)

Proof.

) and (

\delta

4
Estimate of volume and scalar curvature integral

Suppose
\{(M^{m},g(t)),t\in[0,1]\}
is a solution of Ricci flow with an initial data
(M,g(0))
.
In Section
3
, we prove that if
(M,g(0))
satisfies the integral Ricci curvature condition (
3.75a
) and the volume lower bound condition (
3.75b
), then we have the pseudo-locality estimate (
3.76
).
In this section, we shall provide local estimates of volume and
L^{p}
-norm of scalar curvature along the Ricci flow. In
[
40
]
, similar estimates were obtained under the conditions

\displaystyle|Rc|\leq\frac{\epsilon}{t},\quad\textrm{and}\quad R_{g(0)}\geq-\epsilon,

with the help of localized maximum principle(cf. Theorem 5.1 of
[
40
]
). In this section, we use the conditions

\displaystyle R_{-}\leq\frac{\epsilon}{t},\quad\textrm{and}\quad\int_{B_{g(0)}(x_{0},r_{0})}R_{-}^{p}dv\leq\epsilon,

which can be deduced from (
3.75a
) and (
3.75b
) naturally.

The local estimates of volume and
L^{p}
-norm of scalar curvature are key new ingredients of this paper.
Before we delve into the details of proving these estimates, let us detour for elementary technical preparations.

Lemma 4.1
.

Suppose
\{(M^{m},g(t)),0\leq t\leq 1\}
is a smooth flow.
\Omega
is a compact subset of
M
.
f\in C^{\infty}\left(M\times[0,1]\right)
and
\eta\in C^{1}\left(M\times[0,1]\right)
. Let
p>1
and
\Omega_{0}(t)=\left\{x\in\Omega:f(x,t)\geq 0\right\}
. Then we have

\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega_{0}}\eta f^{p}dv=\int_{\Omega_{0}}\frac{d}{dt}\left(\eta f^{p}\right)dv+\int_{\Omega_{0}}\eta f^{p}\left(\frac{d}{dt}dv\right)

(4.1)

for any
t\in[0,1]
.

Proof.

We remind the reader that the boundary of
\Omega_{0}(t)
may not be smooth, which may cause unexpected difficulty.
We shall prove
4.1
by definition. Namely, we shall prove the following equation.

\displaystyle\quad\lim_{\Delta t\to 0}\frac{1}{\Delta t}\left(\int_{\Omega_{0}(t+\Delta t)}\eta(x,t+\Delta t)f^{p}(x,t+\Delta t)dv_{t+\Delta t}-\int_{\Omega_{0}(t)}\eta(x,t)f^{p}(x,t)dv_{t}\right)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega_{0}(t)}\eta(x,t)f^{p}(x,t)\left(\frac{d}{dt}dv_{t}\right)+\int_{\Omega_{0}(t)}\frac{d}{dt}\left(\eta(x,t)f^{p}(x,t)\right)dv_{t}.

(4.2)

In order to prove (
4.2
), it suffices to prove the following three equations.

\displaystyle\lim_{\Delta t\to 0}\frac{1}{\Delta t}\left(\int_{\Omega_{0}(t+\Delta t)}\eta f^{p}(x,t+\Delta t)dv_{t+\Delta t}-\int_{\Omega_{0}(t)}\eta f^{p}(x,t+\Delta t)dv_{t+\Delta t}\right)=0,

(4.3)

\displaystyle\lim_{\Delta t\to 0}\frac{1}{\Delta t}\left(\int_{\Omega_{0}(t)}\eta f^{p}(x,t+\Delta t)dv_{t+\Delta t}-\int_{\Omega_{0}(t)}\eta f^{p}(x,t+\Delta t)dv_{t}\right)=\int_{\Omega_{0}(t)}\eta f^{p}(x,t)\left(\frac{d}{dt}dv_{t}\right),

(4.4)

\displaystyle\lim_{\Delta t\to 0}\frac{1}{\Delta t}\left(\int_{\Omega_{0}(t)}\eta f^{p}(x,t+\Delta t)dv_{t}-\int_{\Omega_{0}(t)}\eta f^{p}(x,t)dv_{t}\right)=\int_{\Omega_{0}(t)}\frac{d}{dt}\left(\eta f^{p}(x,t)\right)dv_{t}.

(4.5)

We shall prove them step by step.

Step 1. Equation (
4.3
) holds.

Note that

\displaystyle\lim_{\Delta t\to 0}\frac{1}{\Delta t}\left(\int_{\Omega_{0}(t+\Delta t)}\eta f^{p}(x,t+\Delta t)dv_{t+\Delta t}-\int_{\Omega_{0}(t)}\eta f^{p}(x,t+\Delta t)dv_{t+\Delta t}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{\Delta t\to 0}\frac{1}{\Delta t}\left(\int_{\Omega_{0}(t+\Delta t)\backslash\Omega_{0}(t)}\eta f^{p}(x,t+\Delta t)dv_{t+\Delta t}-\int_{\Omega_{0}(t)\backslash\Omega_{0}(t+\Delta t)}\eta f^{p}(x,t+\Delta t)dv_{t+\Delta t}\right).

(4.6)

When
\Delta t\rightarrow 0_{+}
, in
\Omega_{0}(t+\Delta t)\backslash\Omega_{0}(t)
, there holds

f(x,t)<0,\quad f(x,t+\Delta t)\geq 0.

Since
\left|\frac{df}{dt}\right|\leq C
in
\Omega\times[0,1]
, there holds

0\leq f(x,t+\Delta t)<C\Delta t

in
\Omega_{0}(t+\Delta t)\backslash\Omega_{0}(t)
.

Similarly, in
\Omega_{0}(t)\backslash\Omega_{0}(t+\Delta t)
, there holds

f(x,t)\geq 0,\quad f(x,t+\Delta t)<0,

which implies that

-C\Delta t\leq f(x,t+\Delta t)<0

in
\Omega_{0}(t+\Delta t)\backslash\Omega_{0}(t)
. Thus we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\Delta t}\left|\int_{\Omega_{0}(t+\Delta t)\backslash\Omega_{0}(t)}\eta f^{p}(x,t+\Delta t)dv_{t+\Delta t}-\int_{\Omega_{0}(t)\backslash\Omega_{0}(t+\Delta t)}\eta f^{p}(x,t+\Delta t)dv_{t+\Delta t}\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\Delta t}\left|\int_{\Omega_{0}(t+\Delta t)\backslash\Omega_{0}(t)}\eta f^{p}(x,t+\Delta t)dv_{t+\Delta t}\right|+\frac{1}{\Delta t}\left|\int_{\Omega_{0}(t)\backslash\Omega_{0}(t+\Delta t)}\eta f^{p}(x,t+\Delta t)dv_{t+\Delta t}\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\Delta t}CV(\Delta t)^{p}\leq C(\Delta t)^{p-1},

where
V
is the upper bound of
vol_{g(t)}\Omega
when
t\in[0,1]
. As
p>1
, it is clear that

\lim_{\Delta t\to 0_{+}}\frac{1}{\Delta t}\left(\int_{\Omega_{0}(t+\Delta t)}\eta f^{p}(x,t+\Delta t)dv_{t+\Delta t}-\int_{\Omega_{0}(t)}\eta f^{p}(x,t+\Delta t)dv_{t+\Delta t}\right)=0.

(4.7)

Similar deduction yields that

\lim_{\Delta t\to 0_{-}}\frac{1}{\Delta t}\left(\int_{\Omega_{0}(t+\Delta t)}\eta f^{p}(x,t+\Delta t)dv_{t+\Delta t}-\int_{\Omega_{0}(t)}\eta f^{p}(x,t+\Delta t)dv_{t+\Delta t}\right)=0.

(4.8)

Therefore, (
4.3
) follows from the combination of (
4.7
) and (
4.8
).

Step 2. Equation (
4.4
) holds.

Since
\frac{d}{dt}\log dv_{t}=\frac{1}{2}tr_{g}\dot{g}
and
\{(M^{m},g(t)),0\leq t\leq 1\}
is a smooth flow, it is clear that

\displaystyle dv_{t+\Delta t}-dv_{t}=\left(\frac{1}{2}tr_{g}\dot{g}\Delta t+o(\Delta t)\right)dv_{t}.

Consequently, by continuity of
\eta f^{p}
, there holds that

\displaystyle\lim_{\Delta t\to 0}\frac{1}{\Delta t}\left(\int_{\Omega_{0}(t)}\eta f^{p}(x,t+\Delta t)dv_{t+\Delta t}-\int_{\Omega_{0}(t)}\eta f^{p}(x,t+\Delta t)dv_{t}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{\Delta t\to 0}\frac{1}{\Delta t}\int_{\Omega_{0}(t)}\eta f^{p}(x,t+\Delta t)\left(\frac{1}{2}tr_{g}\dot{g}\Delta t+o(\Delta t)\right)dv_{t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{0}(t)}\eta f^{p}(x,t)\left(\frac{d}{dt}dv_{t}\right),

which is nothing but (
4.4
).

It is obvious that the equation (
4.5
) holds. Therefore, equation (
4.2
) follows immediately from the combination of (
4.3
), (
4.4
) and (
4.5
).
∎

Lemma 4.2
.

Suppose
M^{m}
is a smooth manifold.
\Omega\subset M
is a bounded domain,
f\in C^{\infty}\left(M\right)
and
\eta\in C^{1}\left(M\right)
.
Let
p>1
and
\Omega_{h}\coloneqq\left\{x\in\Omega:f(x)\geq h\right\}
. Then we have

\int_{\Omega_{0}}\eta\Delta f^{p}dv=-\int_{\Omega_{0}}\langle\nabla\eta,\nabla f^{p}\rangle dv.

(4.9)

Proof.

We first assume
\Omega
has smooth boundary.

Recall that
\Omega_{0}=\left\{x\in\Omega:f(x)\geq 0\right\}
.
If
\partial\Omega_{0}
is smooth, then equation (
4.9
) holds obviously.
Otherwise, we shall show (
4.9
) by approximation.
Suppose
h
is a regular value of
f
, then
\partial\Omega_{h}
is smooth.
Integration by parts yields that

\int_{\Omega_{h}}\eta\Delta f^{p}dv+\int_{\Omega_{h}}\langle\nabla\eta,\nabla f^{p}\rangle dv=\int_{\partial\Omega_{h}}\langle\eta\nabla f^{p},\vec{n}\rangle d\sigma=\int_{\partial\Omega_{h}}\langle\eta f^{p-1}\nabla f,\vec{n}\rangle d\sigma.

(4.10)

Since
f=h
on
\partial\Omega_{h}
, there holds

\int_{\partial\Omega_{h}}\langle\eta f^{p-1}\nabla f,\vec{n}\rangle d\sigma=h^{p-1}\int_{\partial\Omega_{h}}\langle\eta\nabla f,\vec{n}\rangle d\sigma=h^{p-1}\int_{\Omega_{h}}\left\{\eta\Delta f+\langle\nabla\eta,\nabla f\rangle\right\}dv.

(4.11)

Consequently we have

\left|\int_{\Omega_{h}}\left\{\eta\Delta f^{p}+\langle\nabla\eta,\nabla f^{p}\rangle\right\}dv\right|\leq h^{p-1}Cvol(\Omega).

(4.12)

In
\Omega_{0}\backslash\Omega_{h}
, since
0\leq f\leq h
, there holds

\langle\nabla\eta,\nabla f^{p}\rangle+\eta\Delta f^{p}=pf^{p-1}\langle\nabla\eta,\nabla f\rangle+\eta\left(p(p-1)f^{p-2}|\nabla f|^{2}+pf^{p-1}\Delta f\right)\geq-Ch^{p-1}.

(4.13)

By Sard’s theorem, we can choose regular values
h\to 0
. It follows from (
4.12
) and (
4.13
) that
\int_{\Omega_{0}}\eta\Delta f^{p}dv+\int_{\Omega_{0}}\langle\nabla\eta,\nabla f^{p}\rangle dv
exists and

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{0}}\eta\Delta f^{p}dv+\int_{\Omega_{0}}\langle\nabla\eta,\nabla f^{p}\rangle dv=\lim_{h\to 0}\left(\int_{\Omega_{h}}\eta\Delta f^{p}dv+\int_{\Omega_{h}}\langle\nabla\eta,\nabla f^{p}\rangle dv\right)=0,

which is equivalent to (
4.9
).

Now we consider the general situation. Note that
\Omega
can be exhausted by a sequence of domains
\tilde{\Omega}_{k}
, which have smooth boundaries (cf. the proof of Lemma 2.6 of
[
39
]
).
Namely, we have

\displaystyle\tilde{\Omega}_{1}\subset\tilde{\Omega}_{2}\subset\cdots\tilde{\Omega}_{k}\subset\tilde{\Omega}_{k+1}\subset\cdots,\quad\Omega=\cup_{k=1}^{\infty}\tilde{\Omega}_{k}.

From previous argument, we know that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{0}\cap\tilde{\Omega}_{k}}\eta\Delta f^{p}dv=-\int_{\Omega_{0}\cap\tilde{\Omega}_{k}}\langle\nabla\eta,\nabla f^{p}\rangle dv.

Since
\left\{\Omega_{0}\cap\tilde{\Omega}_{k}\right\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
is an exhaustion of
\Omega_{0}
, we can take limit of both sides of the above equation to obtain (
4.9
).
The proof of Lemma
4.2
is complete.
∎

Now we are ready to prove the main theorem of this section.

Theorem 4.3
.

Suppose
\{(M^{m},g(t)),0\leq t\leq 1\}
is a Ricci flow solution. Let
r_{0}\geq 3
and
p>\frac{m}{2}
.
Suppose

\displaystyle[left=\empheqlbrace\,]

\displaystyle vol_{g(0)}\left(B_{g(0)}(x_{0},r_{0})\right)\leq V_{0},

(4.14a)

\displaystyle\int_{B_{g(0)}(x_{0},r_{0})}R_{-,g(0)}^{p}dv\leq\epsilon,

(4.14b)

and

\displaystyle R_{-}(x,t)\leq\frac{\epsilon}{t},\quad\forall\;x\in B_{g(0)}(x_{0},r_{0}),\;t\in(0,1].

(4.15)

Then for each
t\in[0,1]
we have

\displaystyle[left=\empheqlbrace\,]

\displaystyle vol_{g(t)}\left(B_{g(0)}(x_{0},r_{0}-2)\right)\leq V_{0}+\frac{(V_{0}+\epsilon)\left(e^{2Ct}-1\right)}{2},

(4.16a)

\displaystyle\int_{B_{g(0)}(x_{0},r_{0}-2)}R_{-,g(t)}^{p}dv\leq\epsilon+\frac{(V_{0}+\epsilon)\left(e^{2Ct}-1\right)}{2}.

(4.16b)

Proof.

The proof consists of five steps. We shall first construct a cutoff function
\eta
with proper properties in Step 1.
Then we calculate the evolution of
\int_{M}\eta dv
and
\int_{M}\eta^{\frac{3}{2}}R_{-}^{p}dv
in Step 2 and Step 3, respectively.
In Step 4, we dominate
\int_{M}\eta dv
and
\int_{M}\eta^{\frac{3}{2}}R_{-}^{p}dv
by ODE solutions which
can be calculated explicitly. Finally, in Step 5, we focus on the domain where
\eta\equiv 1
and finish the proof of the estimate (
4.16a
) and (
4.16b
).
All constants
C
in this proof depend only on
m
and
p
and may vary from line to line.

Step 1. Construction of a proper cutoff function
\eta
.

Define a cut-off function
\eta:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
satisfying
\eta^{\prime}\leq 0
and

\displaystyle\eta(s)=\begin{cases}0,&s\in[r_{0},\infty);\\
\left(\frac{1}{4p+4}(r_{0}-x)\right)^{4p+4},&s\in\left[r_{0}-\frac{1}{3},r_{0}\right];\\
1,&s\in(-\infty,r_{0}-1].\end{cases}

(4.17)

Furthermore, we can choose an
\eta\in C^{3}
such that

\displaystyle\eta^{\prime}=-\eta^{\frac{4p+3}{4p+4}},\quad\textrm{if}\;s\in\left[r_{0}-\frac{1}{3},r_{0}\right];

(4.18)

\displaystyle-\eta^{\prime\prime}\leq C\eta,\quad\eta^{\frac{2p-3}{2p-2}}\leq C\eta,\quad 0\leq-\eta^{\prime}\leq C\eta^{\frac{4p+3}{4p+4}},\quad\textrm{if}\;s\in\left[0,r_{0}-\frac{1}{3}\right].

(4.19)

Abusing notation, we define

\displaystyle\alpha\coloneqq\frac{p-1}{2p+2},

(4.20)

\displaystyle\eta\coloneqq\eta(d_{g(0)}(x_{0},x)+t^{\alpha}).

(4.21)

Step 2. It holds that

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{M}\eta dv\leq C\left\{\int_{M}\eta dv+\int_{M}\eta^{\frac{3}{2}}R_{-}^{p}dv\right\}.

(4.22)

Direct calculation shows that

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{M}\eta dv=\int_{M}\left(\frac{d}{dt}\eta\right)dv+\int_{M}\eta\left(\frac{d}{dt}dv\right)=\int_{M}\eta^{\prime}\alpha t^{\alpha-1}dv+\int_{M}\eta(-R)dv.

(4.23)

Since
\eta^{\prime}\leq 0
and
\alpha\in(0,1)
, it follows from (
4.15
) and (
4.20
) that

\displaystyle\alpha\eta^{\prime}t^{\alpha-1}\leq\alpha\eta^{\prime}\frac{R_{-}^{1-\alpha}}{\epsilon^{1-\alpha}}=\frac{\alpha}{\epsilon^{1-\alpha}}\cdot\eta^{\prime}\cdot R_{-}^{1-\alpha}\leq\eta^{\prime}R_{-}^{1-\alpha}=\eta^{\prime}R_{-}^{\frac{p+3}{2p+2}}.

(4.24)

Plugging (

) and noting that

, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{M}\eta dv\leq\int_{M}\eta^{\prime}R_{-}^{\frac{p+3}{2p+2}}dv+\int_{M}\eta R_{-}dv.

(4.25)

Now we divide the support of
\eta
into two parts:

\displaystyle M_{1}\coloneqq\left\{x\in M:d_{g(0)}(x_{0},x)+t^{\alpha}\in\left[0,r_{0}-\frac{1}{3}\right]\right\},

\displaystyle M_{2}\coloneqq\left\{x\in M:d_{g(0)}(x_{0},x)+t^{\alpha}\in\left[r_{0}-\frac{1}{3},r_{0}\right]\right\}.

It follows from (
4.18
) that
\eta^{\prime}=-\eta^{\frac{4p+3}{4p+4}}
on
M_{2}
. Then (
4.25
) can be written as

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{M}\eta dv

\displaystyle\leq\int_{M_{1}}\eta^{\prime}R_{-}^{\frac{p+3}{2p+2}}dv+\int_{M_{2}}\eta^{\prime}R_{-}^{\frac{p+3}{2p+2}}dv+\int_{M_{1}}\eta R_{-}dv+\int_{M_{2}}\eta R_{-}dv

\displaystyle\leq\int_{M_{2}}-\eta^{\frac{4p+3}{4p+4}}R_{-}^{\frac{p+3}{2p+2}}dv+\int_{M_{1}}\eta R_{-}dv+\int_{M_{2}}\eta R_{-}dv.

(4.26)

Hölder’s inequality and Young’s inequality imply that

\displaystyle\int_{M_{1}}\eta R_{-}dv

\displaystyle=\int_{M_{1}}(\eta^{\frac{3}{2p}}R_{-})\cdot\eta^{\frac{2p-3}{2p}}dv\leq\bigg(\int_{M_{1}}\eta^{\frac{3}{2}}R_{-}^{p}dv\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}\bigg(\int_{M_{1}}\eta^{\frac{2p-3}{2p-2}}dv\bigg)^{\frac{p-1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{M_{1}}\eta^{\frac{3}{2}}R_{-}^{p}dv+\int_{M_{1}}\eta^{\frac{2p-3}{2p-2}}dv.

(4.27)

Similarly, we have

\displaystyle\int_{M_{2}}\eta R_{-}dv

\displaystyle=\int_{M_{2}}\left(\eta^{\frac{3}{4p+6}}R_{-}^{\frac{p}{2p+3}}\right)\cdot\left(\eta^{\frac{4p+3}{4p+6}}R_{-}^{\frac{p+3}{2p+3}}\right)dv

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{M_{2}}\eta^{\frac{3}{2}}R_{-}^{p}dv\right)^{\frac{1}{2p+3}}\left(\int_{M_{2}}\eta^{\frac{4p+3}{4p+4}}R_{-}^{\frac{p+3}{2p+2}}dv\right)^{\frac{2p+2}{2p+3}}

(4.28)

Plugging (
4.27
) and (
4.28
) into (
4.26
) and applying (
4.19
), we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{M}\eta dv

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{M}\eta^{\frac{3}{2}}R_{-}^{p}dv+\int_{M_{1}}\eta^{\frac{2p-3}{2p-2}}dv\leq C\left\{\int_{M}\eta dv+\int_{M}\eta^{\frac{3}{2}}R_{-}^{p}dv\right\},

(4.29)

Step 3. It holds that

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{M}\eta^{\frac{3}{2}}R_{-}^{p}dv\leq C\left\{\int_{M}\eta dv+\int_{M}\eta^{\frac{3}{2}}R_{-}^{p}dv\right\}.

For each time

\displaystyle M_{-}(t)\coloneqq\{x\in M:R(x,t)\leq 0\}.

Then
R_{-}\equiv 0
on

\displaystyle\int_{M}\eta^{\frac{3}{2}}R_{-}^{p}dv=\int_{M_{-}}\eta^{\frac{3}{2}}(-R)^{p}dv.

\displaystyle\quad\frac{d}{dt}\int_{M}\eta^{\frac{3}{2}}R_{-}^{p}dv=\frac{d}{dt}\int_{M_{-}}\eta^{\frac{3}{2}}(-R)^{p}dv

\displaystyle=\int_{M_{-}}\left(\frac{d}{dt}\eta^{\frac{3}{2}}\right)(-R)^{p}dv+\int_{M_{-}}\eta^{\frac{3}{2}}p(-R)^{p-1}\frac{d}{dt}(-R)dv+\int_{M_{-}}\eta^{\frac{3}{2}}(-R)^{p}\left(\frac{d}{dt}dv\right).

Along the Ricci flow, we have

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\eta^{\frac{3}{2}}=\frac{3}{2}\eta^{\frac{1}{2}}\eta^{\prime}\alpha t^{\alpha-1}\leq 0,

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}dv=-Rdv,

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}R=\Delta R+2|Rc|^{2}\geq\Delta R+\frac{2R^{2}}{m}.

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{M}\eta^{\frac{3}{2}}R_{-}^{p}dv\leq\int_{M_{-}}\eta^{\frac{3}{2}}p(-R)^{p-1}\Delta(-R)dv-\left(\frac{2p}{m}-1\right)\int_{M_{-}}\eta^{\frac{3}{2}}(-R)^{p+1}dv.

\displaystyle\Delta(-R)^{p}=p(p-1)(-R)^{p-2}|\nabla(-R)|^{2}+p(-R)^{p-1}\Delta(-R).

Plugging the above equation into (

\displaystyle\quad\frac{d}{dt}\int_{M}\eta^{\frac{3}{2}}R_{-}^{p}dv

\displaystyle\leq\int_{M_{-}}\eta^{\frac{3}{2}}\Delta(-R)^{p}dv-\int_{M_{-}}\eta^{\frac{3}{2}}p(p-1)(-R)^{p-2}|\nabla(-R)|^{2}dv-\left(\frac{2p}{m}-1\right)\int_{M_{-}}\eta^{\frac{3}{2}}(-R)^{p+1}dv.

\displaystyle\quad\int_{M_{-}}\eta^{\frac{3}{2}}\Delta(-R)^{p}dv

\displaystyle\leq\int_{M_{-}}\eta^{\frac{3}{2}}p(p-1)(-R)^{p-2}|\nabla(-R)|^{2}dv+\frac{9p}{16(p-1)}\int_{M_{-}}\frac{|\nabla\eta|^{2}}{\eta^{\frac{1}{2}}}(-R)^{p}dv.

) and (
4.19

.
Thus

\displaystyle\frac{|\nabla\eta|^{2}}{\eta^{\frac{1}{2}}}\leq C\eta^{\frac{3p+2}{2p+2}}.

(4.35)

Combining (
4.33

4.34
) and (
4.35

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{M}\eta^{\frac{3}{2}}R_{-}^{p}dv\leq C\int_{M_{-}}\eta^{\frac{3p+2}{2p+2}}R_{-}^{p}dv-\left(\frac{2p}{m}-1\right)\int_{M_{-}}\eta^{\frac{3}{2}}R_{-}^{p+1}dv.

Hölder’s inequality yields that

\displaystyle\int_{M_{-}}\eta^{\frac{3p+2}{2p+2}}R_{-}^{p}dv=\int_{M_{-}}\left\{\eta^{\frac{3p}{2p+2}}R_{-}^{p}\right\}\cdot\eta^{\frac{1}{p+1}}dv\leq\left(\int_{M_{-}}\eta^{\frac{3}{2}}R_{-}^{p+1}dv\right)^{\frac{p}{p+1}}\bigg(\int_{M_{-}}\eta dv\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p+1}}.

Consequently, we have

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{M}\eta^{\frac{3}{2}}R_{-}^{p}dv\leq C\left(\int_{M_{-}}\eta^{\frac{3}{2}}R_{-}^{p+1}dv\right)^{\frac{p}{p+1}}\bigg(\int_{M_{-}}\eta dv\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p+1}}-\left(\frac{2p}{m}-1\right)\int_{M}\eta^{\frac{3}{2}}R_{-}^{p+1}dv.

\displaystyle C\bigg(\int_{M}\eta^{\frac{3}{2}}R_{-}^{p+1}dv\bigg)^{\frac{p}{p+1}}\bigg(\int_{M}\eta dv\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p+1}}\leq\left(\frac{2p}{m}-1\right)\int_{M}\eta^{\frac{3}{2}}R_{-}^{p+1}dv+C\int_{M}\eta dv.

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{M}\eta^{\frac{3}{2}}R_{-}^{p}dv\leq C\int_{M}\eta dv,

5

Lemma 5.1

(

]

(

Theorem 5.4

.
For each
\epsilon
small, there exists an
\eta=\eta(\mathcal{N},p,\epsilon)
with the following properties.

Suppose
\left(M^{m},g\right)
is a Riemannian manifold satisfying

\displaystyle\kappa(p,\epsilon)<\epsilon^{2-\frac{m}{p}},\quad d_{GH}\left\{(M,g),(N,h)\right\}<\eta.

(5.6)

Then the Ricci flow initiated from
(M,g)
exists on
\left[0,T\right]
. Furthermore, there exists a family of diffeomorphisms
\left\{\Phi_{t}:N\longrightarrow M,\epsilon\leq t\leq T\right\}
such that

\sup\limits_{t\in\left[\epsilon,T\right]}\left\|\Phi_{t}^{*}g(t)-h(t)\right\|_{C^{\left[\epsilon^{-1}\right]}(N,h(t))}<\epsilon.

(5.7)

Sketch of the proof:

Since the proof is very similar to that in section 6 of
[
40
]
, we shall only sketch the proof and highlight the key points.

In
[
40
]
, the second named author proved estimate (
5.7
) when initial Ricci curvature has a uniform point-wise lower bound.
The key idea there is to construct diffeomorphisms between locally almost flat manifolds with rough Gromov-Hausdorff approximations(cf. Lemma 6.2 and 6.3 of
[
40
]
).
In the current situation, the point-wise Ricci lower bound condition

Rc_{M}>-(m-1)A
is replaced by the weaker integral Ricci curvature condition
\kappa(p,\epsilon)<\epsilon^{2-\frac{m}{p}}
.
The almost flatness and rough Gromov-Hausdorff approximation conditions are realized by the curvature-injectivity-radius estimate in Theorem
3.4
.

By the smoothness and compactness of
(N,h)
, for each small
\delta
, there exists a small
r_{1}(\delta,N,h)
satisfying

\displaystyle\left(1-\frac{\delta}{2}\right)\omega_{m}r^{m}\leq\left|B(x,r)\right|\leq\left(1+\frac{\delta}{2}\right)\omega_{m}r^{m},\quad\forall\;x\in N,\;0<r<r_{1}.

Choosing
\eta=\eta(h,\epsilon)
sufficiently small.
Since (
5.6
) holds, the volume continuity (cf. Lemma
2.15
) and volume comparison (cf. Lemma 2.3 of
[
32
]
) guarantees that

\displaystyle(1-\delta)\omega_{m}r^{m}\leq\left|B(y,r)\right|\leq(1+\delta)\omega_{m}r^{m},\quad\forall y\in M,\;0<r<\frac{r_{1}}{8}.

Define
r_{2}\coloneqq\min\{\epsilon,\frac{r_{1}}{8}\}
. Then we have

\displaystyle\kappa(p,r)\leq 2\left(\frac{r}{\epsilon}\right)^{2-\frac{m}{p}}\kappa(p,\epsilon)\leq 2r^{2-\frac{m}{p}},\quad\forall\;0<r\leq r_{2}.

Thus we can choose an
r_{3}(\delta,\epsilon)
which satisfies
\kappa(p,r_{3})\leq\delta^{2}
.
By scaling, on the manifold
(M,\frac{g}{\delta^{2}r_{3}^{2}})
, (
3.75a
) and (
3.75b
) holds. Thus we can apply the pseudo-locality property in Theorem
3.4
and obtain the distance distortion estimate in Theorem
5.3
.
Note that this estimate is the key point (cf. Lemma 6.2 in
[
40
]
) for the construction of diffeomorphism
\Phi:N\to M
.
Furthermore,
\Phi^{*}(g(\xi))
is very close to
h
in
C^{0}
-topology, for a very small time
\xi=\xi(\mathcal{N},\epsilon)
.
Then we apply the Ricci-Deturck flow technique to obtain (
5.7
), following exactly the same steps as that in Theorem 6.1 of
[
40
]
.
∎

Theorem
5.7
can be generalized to a version under normalized Ricci flow.
The following lemma of volume estimate is needed to achieve such generalization.

Lemma 5.5
.

Same conditions as in Theorem
5.7
. Suppose
\epsilon
is a small number satisfying
k(p,\epsilon)\leq\epsilon^{2-\frac{m}{p}}
and
\left|Rm\right|_{h(t)}\leq\epsilon^{-1}
.
Then for each small positive number
\xi<\frac{\epsilon}{100}
, there exists an
\eta=\eta(\xi,\epsilon,h)
with the following property.

If
d_{GH}\left\{(M,g),(N,h)\right\}<\eta
, then

\left|\log\frac{\left|M\right|_{dv_{g(t)}}}{\left|M\right|_{dv_{g(0)}}}\right|<C(h,m)\epsilon^{-1}\xi,\quad\forall t\in\left(0,\xi\right).

(5.8)

Proof.

By volume continuity (cf. (
2.44
) in Lemma
2.15
), we have

\displaystyle\left|\left|M\right|_{g(0)}-\left|N\right|_{g(0)}\right|\leq\psi(\eta|\epsilon,h),

which is equivalent to

\displaystyle\left|\log\frac{\left|M\right|_{dv_{g(0)}}}{\left|N\right|_{dv_{h(0)}}}\right|\leq\psi(\eta|\epsilon,h).

(5.9)

By Vitali covering method, there exists a constant
C(m)
such that

\left|\left|Rc_{-}\right|\right|_{p,M}\leq C(m)\epsilon^{-1}\left|M\right|_{g(0)}\leq C(h,m)\epsilon^{-1}.

By (
5.6
) and Theorem
4.3
, we know for
\xi
small enough, there holds

\displaystyle\left\|R_{-}(t)\right\|_{p,M}<C(h,m)\epsilon^{-1},\quad\forall t\in\left(0,\xi\right).

Then we calculate

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\left|M\right|_{dv_{g(t)}}=\int_{M}-Rdv_{g(t)}\leq\int_{M}R_{-}dv_{g(t)}\leq\left\|R_{-}\right\|_{p,M}\left|M\right|^{\frac{p-1}{p}}_{dv_{g(t)}}\leq\frac{C(h,m)}{\epsilon}\left|M\right|^{\frac{p-1}{p}}_{dv_{g(t)}},

which implies

\displaystyle\left(\left|M\right|^{\frac{1}{p}}_{dv_{g(\xi)}}-\frac{C(h,m)(\xi-t)}{\epsilon p}\right)^{p}\leq\left|M\right|_{dv_{g(t)}}\leq\left(\left|M\right|^{\frac{1}{p}}_{dv_{g(0)}}+\frac{C(h,m)t}{\epsilon p}\right)^{p}.

(5.10)

As for the Ricci flow
\left(N,h(t)\right)
, it is clear that

\displaystyle\left|Rm\right|_{h(t)}\leq 2\sup\limits_{z\in N}\left|Rm\right|_{h(0)}(z)\leq 2\epsilon^{-1},\quad\forall x\in N,\;t\in\left(0,\xi\right].

Thus direct estimate of volume element yields that

\displaystyle\frac{\left|N\right|_{dv_{h(\xi)}}}{\left|N\right|_{dv_{h(0)}}}\geq e^{-2m(m-1)\epsilon^{-1}\xi}.

(5.11)

On the other hand, the smooth closeness in Theorem
5.7
imply

\displaystyle\left|\log\frac{\left|M\right|_{dv_{g(\xi)}}}{\left|N\right|_{dv_{h(\xi)}}}\right|\leq\psi(\eta|\xi,\epsilon,m,p).

(5.12)

For each
t\in(0,\xi)
, it follows from the combination of (
5.10
) and (
5.12
) that

\displaystyle\left|M\right|_{dv_{g(t)}}^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\geq\left|M\right|^{\frac{1}{p}}_{dv_{g(\xi)}}-\frac{C(h,m)(\xi-t)}{\epsilon p}\geq\left\{e^{-\psi}\left|N\right|_{dv_{h(\xi)}}\right\}^{\frac{1}{p}}-\frac{C(h,m)\xi}{\epsilon p}.

Plugging (
5.11
) into the above inequality and applying (
5.9
), we obtain

\displaystyle\quad\left|M\right|_{dv_{g(t)}}^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\geq\left\{e^{-\psi-2m(m-1)\epsilon^{-1}\xi}\left|N\right|_{dv_{h(0)}}\right\}^{\frac{1}{p}}-\frac{C(h,m)\xi}{\epsilon p}

\displaystyle\geq\left\{e^{-\psi-2m(m-1)\epsilon^{-1}\xi}\left|M\right|_{dv_{g(0)}}\right\}^{\frac{1}{p}}-\frac{C(h,m)\xi}{\epsilon p},

which implies (
5.8
) immediately.
∎

Theorem 5.6

(
Continuous dependence in the case of normalized Ricci flow
)
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{N}=\left\{\left(N^{m},\tilde{h}(\tilde{t})\right),0\leq\tilde{t}\leq\tilde{T}\right\}
is a normalized Ricci flow solution on the closed m-manifold
N
, and
p>\frac{m}{2}
.
For each
\epsilon
small, there exists an
\eta=\eta(\mathcal{N},p,\epsilon)
with the following properties.

If
\left(M^{m},g\right)
is a Riemannian manifold satisfying (
5.6
), then the Ricci flow initiated from
(M,g)
exists on
\left[0,\tilde{T}\right]
.
Furthermore, there exists a family of diffeomorphisms
\left\{\tilde{\Phi}_{\tilde{t}}:N\longrightarrow M,\epsilon\leq\tilde{t}\leq\tilde{T}\right\}
such that

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{\tilde{t}\in\left[\epsilon,\tilde{T}\right]}\left\|\tilde{\Phi}_{\tilde{t}}^{*}g(t)-\tilde{h}(\tilde{t})\right\|_{C^{\left[\epsilon^{-1}\right]}(N,\tilde{h}(\tilde{t}))}<\epsilon.

(5.13)

Proof.

Let
\left\{(N,h(t)),t\in[0,T]\right\}
be the unnormalized Ricci flow corresponding to the normalized Ricci flow
\mathcal{N}
. In other words, define

\displaystyle\lambda_{N}(t)\coloneqq\left(\frac{|N|_{dv_{h(t)}}}{|N|_{dv_{h(0)}}}\right)^{\frac{-2}{m}},\quad\tilde{t}\coloneqq\int_{0}^{t}\lambda_{N}(s)ds,\quad\tilde{T}\coloneqq\int_{0}^{T}\lambda_{N}(s)ds,\quad\tilde{h}(\tilde{t})\coloneqq\lambda_{N}(t)h(t).

(5.14)

Then
\left(N^{m},\tilde{h}(\tilde{t})\right)
satisfies the volume normalized Ricci flow equation and
|N|_{dv_{\tilde{h}(\tilde{t})}}=|N|_{dv_{h(0)}}
. Similarly, we can define
\tilde{g}
and
\lambda_{M}
.

Since
\left\{(N,h(t)),t\in[0,T]\right\}
is a smooth compact space-time, we can extend the Ricci flow to
\left\{(N,h(t)),t\in[0,T+\epsilon^{\prime}]\right\}
.
By Theorem
5.7
and Lemma
5.5
, for each small
\xi
, the Ricci flow initiated from
(M,g)
shall exist on
[0,T+\epsilon^{\prime}]
and satisfy

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{t\in[0,T]}\left|\lambda_{M}(t)-\lambda_{N}(t)\right|<\xi.

(5.15)

If
\eta\rightarrow 0
, then
\lambda_{M}(t)\rightarrow\lambda_{N}(t)
uniformly on
[0,T+\epsilon^{\prime}]
. Consequently, we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T+\epsilon^{\prime}}\lambda_{M}(s)ds\rightarrow\int_{0}^{T+\epsilon^{\prime}}\lambda_{N}(s)ds>\int_{0}^{T}\lambda_{N}(s)ds=\tilde{T}.

Thus the normalized Ricci flow initiated from
(M,g)
exists on
[0,\tilde{T}]
.
Since
\tilde{g}(\tilde{t})=\lambda_{M}(t)g(t)
and
\lambda_{M}(t)
is uniformly bounded by (
5.15
), up to adjusting constant slightly, it is clear that (
5.13
) follows immediately from (
5.7
).
∎

Note that Theorem
1.6
is nothing but the combination of Theorem
5.7
and Theorem
5.6
.
Therefore, we have already finished the proof of Theorem
1.6
.

6
Proof of the main theorem

In this section, we study the global behavior of the normalized Ricci flow near a given immortal solution initiated from
(N,h)
.
In particular, we shall show a stability theorem nearby a weakly stable Einstein manifold under appropriate
L^{p}
-Ricci curvature conditions.
Then we apply this stability theorem to prove Theorem
1.1
.

First we introduce the definition of stability of Ricci flow.

Definition 6.1
.

A closed Einstein metric
\left(N,h_{E}\right)
is called weakly stable if there exists an
\epsilon=\epsilon\left(N,h_{E}\right)
with the following properties.

For any smooth Riemannian metric
h
satisfying

|N|_{dv_{h}}=|N|_{dv_{h_{E}}},\quad||h-h_{E}||_{C^{[\epsilon^{-1}]}\left(h_{E}\right)}<\epsilon,

(6.1)

the normalized Ricci flow initiated from
(N,h)
exists immortally and converges (in smooth topology) to an Einstein metric
\left(N,h_{E}^{\prime}\right)
.

Furthermore,
\left(N,h_{E}\right)
is called strictly stable if each
h_{E}^{\prime}
is isometric to
h_{E}
for some sufficiently small
\epsilon
.

Theorem 6.2

(
Stability nearby a stable Ricci flow
)
.

Suppose
\left\{(N^{m},h(t)),t\in[0,\infty)\right\}
is an immortal solution of normalized Ricci flow with the initial metric
h(0)=h
, and
p>\frac{m}{2}
.
The immortal solution
(N,h(t))
converges to a weakly stable Einstein manifold
\left(N,h_{E}\right)
. Then for any
\epsilon
small, there exists an
\eta=\eta\left(N,h,p,\epsilon\right)
with the following properties.

Suppose a Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
satisfying

\kappa(p,\epsilon)<\epsilon^{2-\frac{m}{p}},\quad d_{GH}\left\{(M,g),(N,h)\right\}<\eta.

(6.2)

Then the normalized Ricci flow solution initiated from
(M,g)
exists immortally and converges to an Einstein manifold
\left(N,h_{E}^{\prime}\right)
. Moreover, if
h_{E}
is strictly stable, then
h_{E}^{\prime}
is homothetic to
h_{E}
.

Proof.

Because the immortal solution
(N,h(t))
converges to an Einstein manifold
\left(N,h_{E}\right)
, we can choose
T=T(\epsilon)
such that

||h(T)-h_{E}||_{C^{[\epsilon^{-1}]}\left(h_{E}\right)}<\epsilon.

(6.3)

On the other hand, by Theorem
5.6
, we can find
\eta=\eta\left(N,h,p,\epsilon\right)
such that if
(M,g)
satisfies

\kappa(p,\epsilon)<\epsilon^{2-\frac{m}{p}},\quad d_{GH}\left\{(M,g),(N,h)\right\}<\eta,

then the normalized Ricci flow initiated from
(M,g)
exists on
[0,T]
and

||h(T)-\Phi^{*}g(T)||_{C^{[\epsilon^{-1}]}\left(h_{E}\right)}<\epsilon

(6.4)

for a diffeomorphism
\Phi:N\rightarrow M
.

Combining (
6.3
) and (
6.4
), we have

||h_{E}-\Phi^{*}g(T)||_{C^{[\epsilon^{-1}]}\left(h_{E}\right)}<2\epsilon.

(6.5)

Scaling the metric
(N,\Phi^{*}g(T))
slightly, we have

|N|_{dv_{\left(C\Phi^{*}g(T)\right)}}=|N|_{dv_{h_{E}}},\quad||h_{E}-C\Phi^{*}g(T)||_{C^{[\epsilon^{-1}]}\left(h_{E}\right)}<3\epsilon

for an
\epsilon=\epsilon(N,h_{E})
which is sufficiently small. Because
(N,h_{E})
is weakly stable (cf. Definition
6.1
),
the normalized Ricci flow initiated from
(N,\Phi^{*}g(T))
exists immortally and converges to an Einstein manifold
\left(N,h_{E}^{\prime}\right)
. If
(N,h_{E})
is strictly stable, then we have
Ch_{E}^{\prime}=h_{E}
where
C
is the small scaling number.
Concatenating the flows, we obtain a normalized Ricci flow which initiates from
(M,g)
and converges to
(N,h_{E}^{\prime})
.
∎

In Theorem
6.2
, if
(N,h)
itself is a weakly stable Einstein manifold, then we have the following corollary.

Corollary 6.3

(
Stability nearby a stable Einstein manifold
)
.

Suppose
(N^{m},h)
is a weakly stable Einstein manifold,
p>\frac{m}{2}
and
\xi<\epsilon_{0}(m,p)
is sufficiently small.
Then there exists
\delta=\delta(h,p,\xi)
with the following properties.

If
(M^{m},g)
is a Riemannian manifold satisfying

\displaystyle\kappa(p,\xi)<\xi^{2-\frac{m}{p}},\quad\textrm{and}\quad d_{GH}\{(M,g),(N,h)\}<\delta,

then
(M,g)
can be smoothly deformed to an Einstein metric
(N,h_{E})
by normalized Ricci flow.
Moreover, if
(N,h)
is strictly stable, then the limit metric is homothetic to
(N,h)
.

Now we are ready to finish the proof of main theorem.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
:

By the diameter estimate of Aubry
[
1
]
, we know from (
1.3
) that the diameter of
(M,g)
is bounded by
2\pi
.
Then we can apply the volume comparison of Petersen-Wei (cf. Theorem 1.1 of
[
32
]
) to obtain

\displaystyle\inf_{x\in M}|B(x,1)|\geq c_{0}(m,p),

which in turn means that the average
L^{p}
-norm of
\{Rc-(m-1)g\}_{-}
is small. Namely, (
1.6
) holds.
Consequently, the work of Petersen and Sprouse
[
31
]
applies and we have

\displaystyle d_{GH}\{(M,g),(S^{m},g_{round})\}\leq\psi(\epsilon|m,p).

Note that the space form
(S^{m},g_{round})
is strictly stable by the work of Huisken
[
25
]
.
Therefore, the convergence of the normalized Ricci flow initiated from
(M^{m},g)
follows from Corollary
6.3
.
The exponential convergence follows from the argument of Hamilton
[
22
]
.
∎

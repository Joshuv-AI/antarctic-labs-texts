1.
Introduction

The study of nodal sets of functions has long been a prominent research topic, particularly concerning the zero sets of solutions to partial differential equations. In this paper, we systematically investigate the properties of nodal sets of general parabolic equations. Let us consider a solution
u(x,t)
to the parabolic equations with time-dependent coefficients

\partial_{t}u=\partial_{i}(a^{ij}(x,t)\partial_{j}u)+b^{i}(x,t)\partial_{i}u+c(x,t)u,

(1.1)

in
Q_{2}:=\{(x,t):|x|<2,-4<t\leq 0\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}
.
where the coefficients
a^{ij}
are elliptic and the coefficients
b,c
are bounded,

(1+\lambda)^{-1}\delta^{ij}\leq a^{ij}\leq(1+\lambda)\delta^{ij},\quad|b^{i}|,|c|\leq\lambda.

(1.2)

We assume
a^{ij}
satisfies Lipschitz regularity condition with respect to the parabolic distance

\displaystyle|a^{ij}(x,t)-a^{ij}(y,s)|\leq\lambda\cdot d((x,t),(y,s))\equiv\lambda\cdot(||x-y||^{2}+|s-t|)^{1/2}.

(1.3)

1.1.
Finite Measure Theorem

First we study the measure of nodal sets of
u(\cdot,t)
for each fixed
t
.

Particularly, when
u(x,t)
and all coefficients are time-independent,
u
satisfies an elliptic equation, whose nodal set has been extensively studied. If the coefficients are analytic, Donnelly and Fefferman
[
20
]
obtained optimal upper and lower bound estimates for the Haudorff measure of nodal sets of eigenfunctions of Laplacian, and Lin
[
36
]
provided another proof for the optimal upper bound. The non-analytic conditions are much more complicated. To name a few, there are several explicit but not optimal upper bound estimates obtained by Hardt-Simon
[
32
]
and also by Han-Lin
[
29
]
with another proof, and also lower bounds by
[
13
,
54
]
. Recently, Logunov
[
41
]
proved the optimal lower bound for the measure, thereby resolving Yau’s conjecture, and also obtained polynomial growth upper bounds
[
42

[

As for the solutions to parabolic equations in domain of
\mathbb{R}^{n}

[
36
]
, Lin proved the measure of nodal sets at each time slice is finite for the equations with analytic and time-independent coefficients. Nonetheless, with general non-analytic and time-dependent coefficients, very few results have been discovered. In the case
n=1
, Angenent
[
3
]
proved that the nodal point set at each time slice is discrete. For higher dimensions, Han-Lin
[
30
]
proved that the nodal set has finite space-time codimension-one Hausdorff measure under doubling assumption on the solution. However, at each time slice, controlling the measure of the nodal sets remains an open problem.

As our first result, we address this case in full generality: for any dimension, with time-dependent coefficients and with merely Lipschitz regular leading coefficients
a^{ij}
, we can prove that the
(n-1)
-dimensional Hausdorff measure is finite at each time slice.

Theorem 1.4

Let

1.2.
Dimension Monotonicity Theorem

Next we study the evolutionary behavior of nodal sets over time. According to the work of Angenent
[
3
]
, when
n=1
and additionally
a_{t}
is bounded, the number of zero points of
u(x,t)
, which is a solution to
u_{t}=a(x,t)u_{xx}+b(x,t)u_{x}+c(x,t)u
with Dirichlet boundary condition, is non-increasing in
t
. This result suggests that in principle the complexity of solutions to parabolic equations decays over time, naturally raising interest in the behavior of such solutions in higher dimensions. However, as far as we know, nothing has been established for general dimensions yet.

In this subsection, we continue the study of evolutionary properties of parabolic solutions in higher dimensions. Unlike the case
n=1
in
[
3
]
, in general, one cannot expect the Hausdorff measure of the nodal set to be non-increasing. We construct examples demonstrating that the Hausdorff measure of the nodal sets can increase over time, even for solutions to heat equations when
n\geq 2
.

Example 1.17
.

Let
f(s)=\frac{3}{2}s-\frac{1}{2}s^{2}-1
when
0\leq s\leq 3/2
and
f(s)\equiv 1/8
when
s\geq 3/2
. Then
f
is
C^{1}
-smooth and
s=1
is the only zero point. Consider the heat solution

u(x,t)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}f(|y|^{2})\rho_{t}(x,y)dy.

It is easy to check that
u(x,t)=u(x^{\prime},t)
for
|x|=|x^{\prime}|
. Denote
f(r^{2},t)=u(x,t)
with
|x|=r
. Then
f(s,0)=f(s)
, noting that
f(1)=0
and
f^{\prime}(1)=1/2>0
,
\Delta f(|y|^{2})|_{\{|y|=1\}}=-2<0
, we can see that
\partial_{s}f(1,t)>1/4>0
,
\partial_{t}f(1,t)<-1<0
for
t
sufficiently small. This implies
f(1,t)<0
and
f(r^{2}_{t},t)=0
for some
r_{t}>1
. Hence the nodal set
Z_{t}=\{x:|x|=r_{t}\}
and
\mathcal{H}^{1}(Z_{t})=2\pi r_{t}
is increasing near
0
.

Example 1.18
.

On the other hand, one can also construct solution with Dirichlet boundary. Let
f(s)=\frac{3}{2}s-\frac{1}{2}s^{2}-1
when
0\leq s\leq 3/2
and
f(s)\equiv 1/8
when
3/2\leq s\leq 2
and
f(s)=\frac{1}{24}(s^{2}-8s+15)
when
2\leq s\leq 3
. By smoothing
f
near
s=3/2
and
s=2
, we get a smooth
\tilde{f}
. Consider the solution
u(x,t)
of
(\partial_{t}-\Delta)u=0
in
B_{3}(0^{2})\times[0,1]
with Dirichlet boundary
u=0
on
\partial B_{3}\times[0,1]
and
u(x,0)=\tilde{f}(|x|^{2})
. One can check as above that near
t=0
the Hausdorff measure of
Z_{t}=\{x:u(x,t)=0,|x|<3\}
is increasing. Actually, one can use Dirichlet heat kernel estimate to show that
u>0
near the boundary (
[
44
]
). Hence we only need to consider the situation near
|x|=1
.

These examples reveal that the evolutionary behavior of parabolic solutions is too complicated to expect any monotonicity in the size of nodal sets in higher dimensions. However, while measure monotonicity appears unrealistic, dimension monotonicity is a more practical expectation. In our second result, we provide an affirmative answer to this dimension monotonicity. Similar to the case when
n=1
, if we assume
a^{ij}
is Lipschitz in the time direction, i.e.,
|a^{ij}(x,t)-a^{ij}(x,s)|\leq\lambda|s-t|
, then we can show that the Hausdorff dimension of nodal sets is non-increasing over time.

Theorem 1.19
.

Let
u
be a nonzero solution of (
1.1
) in
Q_{2}
with Dirichlet boundary condition
u=0
on
\{|x|=2,-4<t\leq 0\}
. Assume that the coefficients satisfy (
1.2
) and (
1.3
) and
a^{ij}
is Lipschitz in time direction. Then the Hausdorff dimension of the nodal set
Z_{t}:=\{x:u(x,t)=0,|x|<2\}
is non-increasing,

\displaystyle\dim Z_{t}\leq\dim Z_{s}\leq n-1

(1.20)

for any
0\geq t\geq s>-4
.

Remark 1.21
.

It should be noted that the Lipschitz assumption in the time direction is sharp, as it ensures the unique continuation property for backward uniqueness of parabolic equations. According to the counter-examples in
[
45
,
46
]
, if
a^{ij}
is only Hölder continuous in time, i.e.
|a^{ij}(x,t)-a^{ij}(x,s)|\leq\lambda|s-t|^{\alpha}
for
\alpha\in(0,1)
, then there exists solution
u
such that
u(\cdot,T)\equiv 0
while
u(\cdot,t)
is not for
t<T
.

This is the first monotonicty property over time for nodal sets of higher-dimensional parabolic solutions. Similar to the applications in the
n=1
case to curve shortening flows or mean curvature flows, as seen in
[
2
,
4
,
5
,
12
,
16
,
17
,
26
]
, more geometric applications are expected. Furthermore, as noted in Remark
1.7
, Theorem
1.19
holds in the more general inequality setting. This broadens its applicability to more nonlinear equations.

Outline

2.
Background and Heat Equations

In this section, we will recall and prove some results which will be used in our proofs. First we will recall some results of caloric functions.

2.1.
Caloric Function and monotone frequency

In this subsection, we consider the solution to the standard heat equation in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times[-T,0]
,

\partial_{t}u=\Delta u.

Noting that if there is no any growth control the solution may not be unique with given initial data. Let us assume some appropriate growth assumption on
u
, say polynomial growth.
First we recall the monotonicity formula for
u
as in Poon
[
53
]
.
Fix some point
(x_{0},t_{0})
. Consider

\begin{split}E_{x_{0},t_{0}}(r)&=2r^{2}\int_{t=t_{0}-r^{2}}|\nabla u|^{2}G_{x_{0},t_{0}}\\
H_{x_{0},t_{0}}(r)&=\int_{t=t_{0}-r^{2}}u^{2}G_{x_{0},t_{0}}\end{split}

where

G_{x_{0},t_{0}}(x,t)\equiv(4\pi(t_{0}-t))^{-\frac{n}{2}}\text{e}^{-\frac{|x-x_{0}|^{2}}{4(t_{0}-t)}}.

We define the frequency for
r\geq 0
to be

N_{x_{0},t_{0}}(r)=\frac{E_{x_{0},t_{0}}(r)}{H_{x_{0},t_{0}}(r)}.

(2.1)

It turns out that
N
is non-increasing in
r
. For the sake of completeness, here we include the proof, which follows Poon
[
53
]
.

Theorem 2.2

(Poon)
.

N_{x_{0},t_{0}}^{\prime}(r)\geq 0
for any
(x_{0},t_{0})
and any
r>0
.

Proof.

Observe that

\nabla G_{x_{0},t_{0}}=\frac{x-x_{0}}{2(t-t_{0})}G_{x_{0},t_{0}}\quad\text{ and }\quad\partial_{t}G_{x_{0},t_{0}}=-\Delta G_{x_{0},t_{0}}.

For simplicity we omit the subscripts. Then integration by parts implies that

E(r)=-2r^{2}\int_{t=t_{0}-r^{2}}u(\Delta u+\nabla u\cdot\frac{x-x_{0}}{2(t-t_{0})})G_{x_{0},t_{0}}=-2r^{2}\int_{t=t_{0}-r^{2}}u(u_{t}+\nabla u\cdot\frac{x-x_{0}}{2(t-t_{0})})G_{x_{0},t_{0}}

H^{\prime}(r)=-2r\int_{t=t_{0}-r^{2}}2uu_{t}G_{x_{0},t_{0}}-u^{2}\Delta G_{x_{0},t_{0}}=-4r\int_{t=t_{0}-r^{2}}u(u_{t}+\nabla u\cdot\frac{x-x_{0}}{2(t-t_{0})})G_{x_{0},t_{0}}=\frac{2E(r)}{r}.

\begin{split}E^{\prime}(r)&=4r\int_{t=t_{0}-r^{2}}|\nabla u|^{2}G_{x_{0},t_{0}}-4r^{3}\int_{t=t_{0}-r^{2}}2\nabla u\cdot\nabla u_{t}G_{x_{0},t_{0}}+4r^{3}\int_{t=t_{0}-r^{2}}|\nabla u|^{2}\Delta G_{x_{0},t_{0}}\\
&=4r\int_{t=t_{0}-r^{2}}|\nabla u|^{2}G_{x_{0},t_{0}}-8r^{3}\int_{t=t_{0}-r^{2}}(\nabla u\cdot\nabla u_{t}+\nabla^{2}u(\nabla u,\frac{x-x_{0}}{2(t-t_{0})}))G_{x_{0},t_{0}}\\
&=-8r^{3}\int_{t=t_{0}-r^{2}}(\nabla u\cdot\nabla(u_{t}+\nabla u\cdot\frac{x-x_{0}}{2(t-t_{0})})G_{x_{0},t_{0}}\\
&=8r^{3}\int_{t=t_{0}-r^{2}}(u_{t}+\nabla u\cdot\frac{x-x_{0}}{2(t-t_{0})})^{2}G_{x_{0},t_{0}}.\end{split}

Therefore,

\begin{split}&\quad E^{\prime}(r)H(r)-E(r)H^{\prime}(r)\\
&=8r^{3}\Big(\int_{t=t_{0}-r^{2}}u^{2}G_{x_{0},t_{0}}\Big)\Big(\int_{t=t_{0}-r^{2}}\Big(u_{t}+\nabla u\cdot\frac{x-x_{0}}{2(t-t_{0})}\Big)^{2}G_{x_{0},t_{0}}\Big)-8r^{3}\Big(\int_{t=t_{0}-r^{2}}u(u_{t}+\nabla u\cdot\frac{x-x_{0}}{2(t-t_{0})})G_{x_{0},t_{0}}\Big)^{2}.\end{split}

Hence, by Cauchy-Schwarz inequality we have
N^{\prime}(r)\geq 0
.
∎

Since
H^{\prime}(r)=2E(r)/r
, we have
H^{\prime}(r)/H(r)=2N(r)/r
. Integrating from
r_{1}
to
r_{2}
we have

\log\frac{H(r_{2})}{H(r_{1})}=2\int_{r_{1}}^{r_{2}}\frac{N(s)}{s}ds.

(2.3)

If we define the doubling index

as

D(r)\equiv\log_{4}\frac{H(2r)}{H(r)}.

Then from (

we can establish the equivalence between frequency and doubling index

N(r)\leq D(r)\leq N(2r).

(2.4)

This is an important global doubling control with respect to frequency. It could be used to prove the unique continuation for heat equation.

The following Lemma proves the orthogonality of homogeneous caloric polynomial of different orders(see also

14
]

Lemma 2.13
.

Suppose
P
,
Q
be two homogeneous caloric polynomials with order
d_{1}\neq d_{2}
. Then we have

\int_{t=t_{0}}PQG_{0,0}=\int_{t=t_{0}}(\nabla P\cdot\nabla Q)G_{0,0}=0.

Proof.

Note that

d_{1}\int_{t=t_{0}}PQG_{0,0}=\int_{t=t_{0}}(2\partial_{t}P\cdot t+\nabla P\cdot x)QG_{0,0}=\int_{t=t_{0}}(2t_{0}\Delta P+\nabla P\cdot x)QG_{0,0}.

Integration by parts gives

2t_{0}\int_{t=t_{0}}\Delta PQG_{0,0}=-2t_{0}\int_{t=t_{0}}(\nabla P\cdot\nabla Q)G_{0,0}-\int_{t=t_{0}}(\nabla P\cdot x)QG_{0,0}.

Hence we have

d_{1}\int_{t=t_{0}}PQG_{0,0}=-2t_{0}\int_{t=t_{0}}(\nabla P\cdot\nabla Q)G_{0,0}.

On the other hand, by similar arguments we have

d_{2}\int_{t=t_{0}}PQG_{0,0}=-2t_{0}\int_{t=t_{0}}(\nabla P\cdot\nabla Q)G_{0,0}.

Since
d_{1}\neq d_{2}
, the result follows.
∎

Remark 2.14
.

Let
P(x,t)
be a homogeneous caloric polynomial of order
d
. Then
P(x,-1)
is an eigenfunction of the Ornstein-Uhlenbeck operator
\mathcal{L}(u)=\Delta u-\frac{1}{2}\langle\nabla u,x\rangle
with eigenvalue

is self-adjoint with respect to the weighted volume

Next we prove that the frequency of a polynomial is bounded by its order. This is a direct consequence of the previous orthogonality lemma.

Corollary 2.15
.

Let
P
be a caloric polynomial of order
d
. Then we have

for all

.

Proof.

Write

, where each
P_{i}
is a homogeneous caloric polynomial of order
i
. Then by previous Lemma
2.13

\begin{split}\int_{t=-r^{2}}|\nabla P|^{2}G_{0,0}&=\int_{t=-r^{2}}\langle\sum_{i}\nabla P_{i},\sum_{j}\nabla P_{j}\rangle G_{0,0}=\sum_{i}\int_{t=-r^{2}}|\nabla P_{i}|^{2}G_{0,0}\\
\int_{t=-r^{2}}P^{2}G_{0,0}&=\int_{t=-r^{2}}(\sum P_{i})^{2}G_{0,0}=\sum_{i}\int_{t=-r^{2}}P_{i}^{2}G_{0,0}.\end{split}

By Lemma
2.5
or the calculation in Lemma

we have
2r^{2}\int_{t=-r^{2}}|\nabla P_{i}|^{2}G_{0,0}=i\int_{t=-r^{2}}P_{i}^{2}G_{0,0}

. This implies that

Thus

.
∎

Corollary 2.16
.

Let
P
be a caloric polynomial of order
d
. For any

, if

then for any

\displaystyle\sup_{Q_{R}}|P(x,t)-a_{0}|\leq C(n,d,R)|a_{0}|\sqrt{\epsilon}

(2.17)

for some nonzero constant

.

Proof.

Write

, where each

\displaystyle=\int_{t=-1}\langle\sum_{i}a_{i}\nabla P_{i},\sum_{j}a_{j}\nabla P_{j}\rangle G_{0,0}=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\int_{t=-1}a_{i}^{2}|\nabla P_{i}|^{2}G_{0,0}=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\int_{t=-1}a_{i}^{2}|\nabla P_{i}|^{2}G_{0,0}.

2.3.
Maximum Principle

In this subsection, we recall the maximum principle of parabolic equation with weak coefficients.
Let us consider the following equation in

) (

Here

Remark 3.6
.

Write

satisfies the following rescaled equation

\partial_{s}\tilde{u}=\partial_{i}(\tilde{a}^{ij}(y,s)\partial_{j}\tilde{u})+\tilde{b}^{i}(y,s)\partial_{i}\tilde{u}+\tilde{c}\tilde{u},

where

\begin{split}\tilde{a}(y,s)&=a(x,t)^{-1}\cdot a(x+rA_{x,t}(y),t+\ell^{2}s)\\
\tilde{b}(y,s)&=\ell\cdot\sqrt{a}(x,t)^{-1}\cdot b(x+\ell A_{x,t}(y),t+\ell^{2}s)\\
\tilde{c}(y,s)&=\ell^{2}\cdot c(x+\ell A_{x,t}(y),t+\ell^{2}s)\end{split}

Hence we have
\tilde{a}(0,0)=(\delta^{ij})
and moreover

|\tilde{a}^{ij}(Y)-\tilde{a}^{ij}(Z)|\leq C(\lambda,\alpha)\ell^{\alpha}d(Y,Z)^{\alpha},(1+C(\lambda,\alpha)\ell^{\alpha})^{-1}\delta^{ij}\leq\tilde{a}^{ij}\leq(1+C(\lambda,\alpha)){\ell}^{\alpha})\delta^{ij},|\tilde{b}^{i}|\leq(1+\lambda)^{-1/2}{\ell},|c|\leq\lambda{\ell}^{2}.

Here

is the parabolic distance define by
d((x,t),(y,s))=(||x-y||^{2}+|s-t|)^{1/2}
.

3.1.
Almost Monotonicity for Localized Frequency

In this section, we prove that the localized frequency function is almost non-decreasing. First we introduce an important approximation Lemma.

Lemma 3.15
.

(Caloric approximation)
Let
u
be a solution to (
3.1
) (
3.2
) on
Q_{2}
with doubling assumption (
3.3
). Let
\epsilon\in(0,1/10)

and

, there exists an ancient caloric polynomial

, such that

\fint_{Q_{1}}h^{2}=1,\quad\text{ and }\quad||h-u_{x,0;\ell}||_{C^{1;1}(Q_{1/\epsilon})}\leq\epsilon.

(3.16)

Proof.

We prove the result by contradiction argument. Suppose the result be false. Consider
u_{x,0;1/i}
with

. Since
\fint_{Q_{1}}u_{x,0;1/i}^{2}=1
, we have
\fint_{Q_{2^{k}}}u_{x,0;1/i}^{2}\leq C(n,\lambda)^{k\Lambda}
for any

and any

by doubling assumption

. Then by interior estimates,

\sup_{i}||u_{x,0;1/i}||_{C^{1+\alpha;1+\alpha/2}(Q_{2^{k-1}})}\leq C(n,\lambda,\alpha)^{k\Lambda}.

For each

, there exists some

such that
||u_{x,0;1/i}-h_{k}||_{C^{1;1}(Q_{2^{k-1}})}\to 0

by passing to a subsequence. Moreover each

is a caloric function according to the estimates of

. By taking

, we have

in

on compact sets with

satisfying the polynomial growth condition. Then we can conclude by Lemma
2.7
that

is indeed a caloric polynomial of order

. The contradiction arises.
∎

\displaystyle N^{u}_{x,0}(r_{i_{3}})\leq N^{u}_{x,0}(r_{i_{2}+1})+\delta^{2}\leq N^{u}_{x,0}(r_{i_{2}})-\delta+\delta^{2}\leq N^{u}_{x,0}(r_{i_{1}})-2(\delta-\delta^{2}).

By iteration we get

\displaystyle N^{u}_{x,0}(r_{i_{k}})\leq N^{u}_{x,0}(r_{i_{1}})-(k-1)(\delta-\delta^{2}).

s\in[\tilde{r},1/2]
and for some
\gamma
if
\ell_{0}(n,\lambda,\alpha,\Lambda,\epsilon)
small. Now we choose the largest integer
d
such that
d<\gamma+\alpha
. Hence
d
satisfies

\gamma+\alpha-d>0\quad\text{ and }\quad\gamma+\alpha-(d+1)\leq 0.

With this
d
, we construct the function
\phi
as follows: For any
(x,t)\in B_{\eta}\times[-1,0]
, we define

\phi(x,t)=\int_{Q^{\eta}_{1}}(G(x,t;y,s)-G(0,0;y,s))f(y,s)dyds-\sum_{k=1}^{d}\int_{Q^{\eta}_{1}\setminus Q^{\eta}_{\tilde{r}}}P^{k}_{y,s}(x,t)f(y,s)dyds

(4.26)

where
P^{k}_{y,s}(x,t)
is defined as in last section, see Lemma
4.19
. Here
f=(\partial_{t}-\Delta)\tilde{u}
. Note that
f
may not be well-defined pointwisely due to the low regularity of
u
. However we will address this issue using integral by parts in the proof. We will prove the uniform bound for
\phi
provided that only the Hölder continuity of
a^{ij}
is assumed. For simplicity, we assume the coefficients
b^{i}=c=0
without loss of generality, as the terms involving
b^{i}
or
c
would have growth of higher order.

Set
r=|(x,t)|_{\eta}\equiv\max\{\eta^{-1}|x|,\sqrt{|t|}\}
. We have the following claim about the growth estimates for
\phi(x,t)
:

Claim :
If
r\geq\tilde{r}
, we have

|\phi(x,t)|\leq C(n,\lambda,\alpha,\Lambda,\eta)r_{1}^{\alpha}|(x,t)|^{d+\alpha/2}.

(4.27)

If
r\leq\tilde{r}
, we have

|\phi(x,t)|\leq C(n,\lambda,\alpha,\Lambda,\eta)r_{1}^{\alpha}\tilde{r}^{d+\alpha/2}.

(4.28)

The claim implies
\phi
is well-defined and finite. Noting that
(\partial_{t}-\Delta)\phi(x,t)=f(x,t)
we have the function
h\equiv\tilde{u}-\phi
is the caloric function we want defined in
Q^{\eta}_{1}
. In the following we prove the first estimate and the second one can proved in a similar way.

Suppose
(x,t)\in Q^{\eta}_{1}
and
r=|(x,t)|_{\eta}
.
If
r\geq\tilde{r}
, we can split the integral into the following three parts

\begin{split}I_{1}&=\int_{Q^{\eta}_{2r}}(G(x,t;y,s)-G(0,0;y,s))f(y,s)dyds\\
I_{2}&=-\sum_{k=1}^{d}\int_{Q^{\eta}_{2r}\setminus Q^{\eta}_{\tilde{r}}}P^{k}_{y,s}(x,t)f(y,s)dyds\\
I_{3}&=\int_{Q^{\eta}_{1}\setminus Q^{\eta}_{2r}}\Big(G(x,t;y,s)-\sum_{k=0}^{d}P^{k}_{y,s}(x,t)\Big)f(y,s)dyds.\end{split}

(4.29)

If
r<\tilde{r}
, we can split the integral as above by replacing
r
by
\tilde{r}
. We will only consider the case
r\geq\tilde{r}
. One will see that the same argument implies the estimate in the claim for
r<\tilde{r}
.

Note that

f(y,s)=\partial_{s}\tilde{u}-\Delta\tilde{u}=(\partial_{s}\tilde{u}-\Delta\tilde{u})-(\partial_{s}\tilde{u}-\partial_{i}(\tilde{a}^{ij}\partial_{j}\tilde{u}))=\partial_{i}\Big((\tilde{a}^{ij}-\delta^{ij})\partial_{j}\tilde{u}\Big).

Using integration by parts, we can write

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle=\int_{Q^{\eta}_{2r}}G(x,t;y,s)f(y,s)dyds-\int_{Q^{\eta}_{2r}}G(0,0;y,s)f(y,s)dyds

\displaystyle=-\int_{Q^{\eta}_{2r}}\partial_{y_{i}}G(x,t;y,s)(\tilde{a}^{ij}(y,s)-\delta^{ij})\partial_{y_{j}}\tilde{u}(y,s)dyds+\int_{\partial B_{2\eta r}\times[-(2r)^{2},0]}G(x,t;y,s)(\tilde{a}^{ij}(y,s)-\delta^{ij})\partial_{y_{j}}\tilde{u}(y,s)\cdot n_{i}dyds

\displaystyle+\int_{Q^{\eta}_{2r}}\partial_{y_{i}}G(0,0;y,s)(\tilde{a}^{ij}(y,s)-\delta^{ij})\partial_{y_{j}}\tilde{u}(y,s)dyds+\int_{\partial B_{2\eta r}\times[-(2r)^{2},0]}G(0,0;y,s)(\tilde{a}^{ij}(y,s)-\delta^{ij})\partial_{y_{j}}\tilde{u}(y,s)\cdot n_{i}dyds

where
n_{i}
is
i
-th component of the spatial outer normal.

Note that

\displaystyle\quad\int_{Q^{\eta}_{2r}}\partial_{y_{i}}G(x,t;y,s)(\tilde{a}^{ij}(y,s)-\delta^{ij})\partial_{y_{i}}\tilde{u}(y,s)dyds

\displaystyle\leq Cr_{1}^{\alpha}\int_{Q^{\eta}_{2r}}|\nabla_{y}G(x,t;y,s)||(y,s)|^{\alpha}|\nabla\tilde{u}(y,s)|dyds

\displaystyle\leq Cr_{1}^{\alpha}r^{\gamma-1+\alpha}\int_{Q^{\eta}_{2r}}|(x-y,t-s)|_{\eta}^{-n-1}dyds

\displaystyle\leq Cr_{1}^{\alpha}r^{\gamma-1+\alpha}\int_{Q^{\eta}_{3r}}|(y,s)|_{\eta}^{-n-1}dyds\leq Cr_{1}^{\alpha}r^{\gamma+\alpha}.

Next we deal with the boundary term.

\begin{split}&\quad\int_{\partial B_{2\eta r}\times[-(2r)^{2},0]}G(x,t;y,s)(\tilde{a}^{ij}(y,s)-\delta^{ij})\partial_{y_{j}}\tilde{u}(y,s)\cdot n_{i}dyds\\
&\leq Cr_{1}^{\alpha}r^{\gamma+\alpha-1}\int_{\partial B_{2\eta r}\times[-(2r)^{2},0]}G(x,t;y,s)dyds\leq Cr_{1}^{\alpha}r^{\gamma+\alpha}.\end{split}

(4.30)

The remaining two terms of
I_{1}
could be estimated in the same way. Therefore we prove that

|I_{1}|\leq Cr_{1}^{\alpha}r^{\gamma+\alpha}.

Next we deal with
I_{2}
. Integration by parts gives

\displaystyle I_{2}=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{d}\int_{Q^{\eta}_{2r}\setminus Q^{\eta}_{\tilde{r}}}\partial_{y_{i}}(P^{k}_{y,s}(x,t))(\tilde{a}^{ij}(y,s)-\delta^{ij})\partial_{y_{j}}\tilde{u}(y,s)dyds

\displaystyle-\sum_{k=1}^{d}\int_{\partial B_{2\eta r}\times[-(2r)^{2},0]}(P^{k}_{y,s}(x,t))(\tilde{a}^{ij}(y,s)-\delta^{ij})\partial_{y_{j}}\tilde{u}(y,s)\cdot n_{i}dyds

\displaystyle+\sum_{k=1}^{d}\int_{\partial B_{\eta\tilde{r}}\times[-\tilde{r}^{2},0]}(P^{k}_{y,s}(x,t))(\tilde{a}^{ij}(y,s)-\delta^{ij})\partial_{y_{j}}\tilde{u}(y,s)\cdot n_{i}dyds.

For the first term, we have

\displaystyle\quad\sum_{k=1}^{d}

\displaystyle\int_{Q^{\eta}_{2r}\setminus Q^{\eta}_{\tilde{r}}}\partial_{y_{i}}(P^{k}_{y,s}(x,t))(\tilde{a}^{ij}(y,s)-\delta^{ij})\partial_{y_{j}}\tilde{u}(y,s)dyds

\displaystyle\leq Cr_{1}^{\alpha}\sum_{k=1}^{d}\int_{Q^{\eta}_{2r}\setminus Q^{\eta}_{\tilde{r}}}|(y,s)|^{\alpha}|\nabla_{y}(P^{k}_{y,s}(x,t)||\nabla\tilde{u}(y,s)|dyds

\displaystyle\leq Cr_{1}^{\alpha}\sum_{k=1}^{d}\sum_{i\geq 0,2^{-i+1}r\geq\tilde{r}}\int_{Q^{\eta}_{2^{1-i}r}\setminus Q^{\eta}_{2^{-i}r}}|(y,s)|^{\alpha}|\nabla_{y}(P^{k}_{y,s}(x,t)||\nabla\tilde{u}(y,s)|dyds

\displaystyle\leq Cr_{1}^{\alpha}\sum_{k=1}^{d}r^{k}\sum_{i\geq 0,2^{-i+1}r\geq\tilde{r}}\int_{Q^{\eta}_{2^{1-i}r}\setminus Q^{\eta}_{2^{-i}r}}|(y,s)|^{-n-k-1+\alpha}|(y,s)|^{\gamma-1}dyds

\displaystyle\leq Cr_{1}^{\alpha}\sum_{k=1}^{d}r^{k}\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}(\frac{r}{2^{i}})^{\gamma+\alpha-k}\leq Cr_{1}^{\alpha}r^{\gamma+\alpha}\sum_{k=1}^{d}\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}(\frac{1}{2^{\gamma+\alpha-k}})^{i}\leq Cr_{1}^{\alpha}r^{\gamma+\alpha},

where the last inequality follows from the fact that
\gamma+\alpha-d>0
. The two boundary terms could be estimates similar as in (
4.30
).

Next we deal with
I_{3}
. We have

\displaystyle I_{3}=

\displaystyle-\int_{Q^{\eta}_{1}\setminus Q^{\eta}_{2r}}\partial_{y_{i}}R^{d}_{y,s}(x,t)(\tilde{a}^{ij}(y,s)-\delta^{ij})\partial_{y_{j}}\tilde{u}(y,s)dyds

\displaystyle+\int_{\partial B_{\eta}\times[-1,0]}R^{d}_{y,s}(x,t)(\tilde{a}^{ij}(y,s)-\delta^{ij})\partial_{y_{j}}\tilde{u}(y,s)\cdot n_{i}dyds

\displaystyle-\int_{\partial B_{2\eta r}\times[-(2r)^{2},0]}R^{d}_{y,s}(x,t)(\tilde{a}^{ij}(y,s)-\delta^{ij})\partial_{y_{j}}\tilde{u}(y,s)\cdot n_{i}dyds

For the first term, by Lemma
4.19
,

\displaystyle\quad\int_{Q^{\eta}_{1}\setminus Q^{\eta}_{2r}}\partial_{y_{i}}R^{d}_{y,s}(x,t)(\tilde{a}^{ij}(y,s)-\delta^{ij})\partial_{y_{j}}\tilde{u}(y,s)dyds

\displaystyle\leq Cr_{1}^{\alpha}\int_{Q^{\eta}_{1}\setminus Q^{\eta}_{2r}}|\nabla_{y}R^{d}_{y,s}(x,t)||(y,s)|^{\alpha}|\nabla\tilde{u}(y,s)|dyds

\displaystyle\leq Cr_{1}^{\alpha}\sum_{i=1}^{M}\int_{Q^{\eta}_{2^{i+1}r}\setminus Q^{\eta}_{2^{i}r}}|\nabla_{y}(R^{k}_{y,s}(x,t)||(y,s)|^{\alpha}||\nabla\tilde{u}(y,s)|dyds

\displaystyle\leq Cr_{1}^{\alpha}\sum_{j=d+1}^{2(d+1)}r^{j}\sum_{i=1}^{M}\int_{Q^{\eta}_{2^{i+1}r}\setminus Q^{\eta}_{2^{i}r}}|(y,s)|^{-n-j-1}\cdot|(y,s)|^{\alpha}|\cdot|(y,s)|^{\gamma-1}dyds

\displaystyle\leq Cr_{1}^{\alpha}r^{\gamma+\alpha}\sum_{j=d+1}^{2(d+1)}\sum_{i=1}^{M}(2^{\gamma+\alpha-j})^{i},

where
M
is an integer such that
2^{M}r\leq 1<2^{M+1}r
.

Recall that
\gamma+\alpha-(d+1)\leq 0
. If
\gamma+\alpha<d+1
, we have

\sum_{j=d+1}^{2(d+1)}\sum_{i=1}^{M}(2^{\gamma+\alpha-j})^{i}\leq C(\alpha,\Lambda).

If
\gamma+\alpha=d+1
, then we have

\displaystyle\sum_{j=d+1}^{2(d+1)}\sum_{i=1}^{M}(2^{\gamma+\alpha-j})^{i}=M+\sum_{j=d+2}^{2(d+1)}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}(2^{d+1-j})^{i}\leq\log_{2}(1/r)+C(\alpha,\Lambda).

To handle the boundary terms is similar. Therefore, we have the following estimate for
I_{3}
,

I_{3}\leq Cr_{1}^{\alpha}r^{\gamma+\frac{\alpha}{2}}.

Therefore, we have proved that

|\phi(x,t)|\leq Cr_{1}^{\alpha}r^{\gamma+\alpha/2}.

(4.31)

For
|(x,t)|_{\eta}=r\leq\tilde{r}
the same argument as above by replacing
r
by
\tilde{r}
we get

|\phi(x,t)|\leq Cr_{1}^{\alpha}\tilde{r}^{\gamma+\alpha/2}.

(4.32)

Hence we finish the proof of the claim.

Noting that
Q_{r}^{\eta}=\{(x,t):|(x,t)|_{\eta}\leq r\}
, as a direct consequence of the claim, we get for any
r\in[\tilde{r},1]
that

\displaystyle\sup_{Q_{r}^{\eta}}|\phi(x,t)|\leq Cr_{1}^{\alpha}r^{\gamma+\alpha/2}.

(4.33)

On the other hand, Since
D^{\tilde{u}}(r)\leq\gamma+\alpha/2
for
r\in[\tilde{r},1/2]
and by Lemma
3.51
, we have

H^{\tilde{u}}(r)\geq Cr^{2\gamma+\alpha}H^{\tilde{u}}(1)\geq Cr^{2\gamma+\alpha}.

If we pick
\ell_{0}(n,\alpha,\lambda,\Lambda,\eta,\epsilon)
small enough and note that
r_{1}\leq\ell_{0}
, then for any
r\in[\tilde{r},1]
we have

\sup_{Q_{r}^{\eta}}|\phi(x,t)|\leq Cr_{1}^{\alpha}r^{\gamma+\alpha/2}\leq Cr_{1}^{\alpha}(H^{\tilde{u}}(r))^{1/2}\leq\epsilon^{2}(H^{\tilde{u}}(r))^{1/2}.

(4.34)

Also, by Lemma
3.51
we have

\sup_{Q_{r}^{\eta}}|\phi(x,t)|\leq Cr_{1}^{\alpha}(H^{\tilde{u}}(r))^{1/2}\leq\epsilon^{2}(\fint_{Q_{r}}\tilde{u}^{2})^{1/2}.

Therefore, for any
r\in[\tilde{r},1]
we have

\displaystyle\frac{\fint_{Q_{2r}}h^{2}}{\fint_{Q_{r}}h^{2}}\leq 100\frac{\fint_{Q_{2r}}\tilde{u}^{2}+\fint_{Q_{2r}}\phi^{2}}{\fint_{Q_{r}}\tilde{u}^{2}-\fint_{Q_{r}}\phi^{2}}\leq 100\frac{(1+C\epsilon^{2})\fint_{Q_{2r}}\tilde{u}^{2}}{(1-C\epsilon^{2})\fint_{Q_{r}}\tilde{u}^{2}}.

This proves the doubling bound for
h
.
∎

Next we will prove the frequency closeness between
\tilde{u}
and the approximated caloric function
h
. Note that the definition of
H^{\tilde{u}}
requires the integral over the ball
B_{1/r_{1}}
. However we only have the approximation over the ball
B_{\eta}
. The following two lemmas show that there is no essential difference between these two integrals.

Lemma 4.35
.

Let
P
be a caloric polynomial of order at most
d
. Then for any
\epsilon
, there exists some
\eta_{0}(n,d,\epsilon)>0
such that for any
\eta>\eta_{0}
and
s>0

\int_{\{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{\eta s}\}\cap\{t=-s^{2}\}}P^{2}G_{0,0}\leq\epsilon\int_{\{t=-s^{2}\}}P^{2}G_{0,0}.

Proof.

First we suppose
P
be a caloric homogeneous polynomial of order
d
. By change of variable formula, we have

\int_{\{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{\eta s}\}\cap\{t=-s^{2}\}}P^{2}G_{0,0}=\frac{s^{2d}}{(4\pi)^{n/2}}\int_{\{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{\eta}\}\cap\{t=-1\}}P^{2}(x,-1)\exp(-\frac{|x|^{2}}{4})dx.

Similarly, we have

\int_{\{t=-s^{2}\}}P^{2}G_{0,0}=\frac{s^{2d}}{(4\pi)^{n/2}}\int_{\{t=-1\}}P^{2}(x,-1)\exp(-\frac{|x|^{2}}{4})dx.

By normalization we may assume

\int_{\{t=-1\}}P^{2}(x,-1)\exp(-\frac{|x|^{2}}{4})dx=1.

By lemma
3.51
and standard estimate, we have

|P(x,-1)|\leq C(n,d)(|x|^{d}+1).

Therefore, by taking
\eta
large enough we have

\int_{\{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{\eta}\}\cap\{t=-1\}}P^{2}(x,-1)\exp(-\frac{|x|^{2}}{4})dx\leq C^{\prime}(n,d)\int_{\eta}^{\infty}r^{d+n-1}\exp(-\frac{r^{2}}{4})dr\leq\epsilon.

This finishes the proof for caloric homogeneous polynomials.

For general case, we can write
P=\sum_{i=0}^{d}P_{i}
with each
P_{i}
homogeneous polynomial of order
i
. Then by lemma
2.13
, we have

\int_{\{t=-s^{2}\}}P^{2}G_{0,0}=\sum_{i}\int_{\{t=-s^{2}\}}P_{i}^{2}G_{0,0}.

And Hölder inequality gives

\int_{\{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{\eta s}\}\cap\{t=-s^{2}\}}P^{2}G_{0,0}\leq(d+1)\sum_{i}\int_{\{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{\eta s}\}\cap\{t=-s^{2}\}}P_{i}^{2}G_{0,0}.

The proof is now finished using homogeneous case.
∎

The polynomial case will be used in the proof of the following general case.

Lemma 4.36
.

Let
u
be a solution to (
3.1
) (
3.2
) on
Q_{2}
with doubling assumption (
3.3
). Let
x\in B_{1}
. Then for any
\epsilon>0
there exists some
\ell_{0}(n,\lambda,\alpha,\Lambda,\epsilon)
and
\eta_{0}(n,\lambda,\alpha,\Lambda,\epsilon)
such that for any
\ell\leq\ell_{0}
and
\eta\geq\eta_{0}
with
\tilde{u}=u_{x,0;\ell}
and any
0\leq r\leq 1

\int_{\{B_{1/\ell}\setminus B_{\eta r}\}\cap\{t=-r^{2}\}}\tilde{u}^{2}G_{0,0}\leq\epsilon\int_{\{B_{1/\ell}\}\cap\{t=-r^{2}\}}\tilde{u}^{2}G_{0,0}\equiv\epsilon H^{\tilde{u}}(r).

In particular, for
r\in(0,1]
we have

\Big|\frac{\int_{\{B_{\eta r}\}\cap\{t=-r^{2}\}}\tilde{u}^{2}G_{0,0}}{H^{\tilde{u}}(r)}-1\Big|\leq\epsilon.

Proof.

We prove this by contradiction. Suppose the result be false with some
\epsilon_{0}>0
. Then we can find a sequence
\tilde{u}_{i}\equiv u_{x,0;1/i}
with
i\to\infty
and each
\tilde{u}_{i}
has polynomial growth of order
C(n,\lambda,\alpha)\Lambda
. By Lemma
3.15
, there exists some caloric polynomial
P
of order
C(n,\lambda,\alpha)\Lambda
such that
||\tilde{u}_{i}-P||_{C^{1;1}(\Omega)}\to 0
in any compact
\Omega\subset Q_{1/i}
.

Take
\eta(n,\lambda,\alpha,\Lambda,\epsilon_{0})
as in Lemma
4.35
with order
d=C(n,\lambda,\alpha)\Lambda
. Then we have

\int_{\{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{\eta s}\}\cap\{t=-s^{2}\}}P^{2}G_{0,0}\leq\frac{\epsilon_{0}}{2}\int_{\{t=-s^{2}\}}P^{2}G_{0,0}.

Since
\tilde{u}_{i}
and
P
has polynomial growth, then as
i\to\infty
we have

\int_{\{B_{i}\}\cap\{t=-s^{2}\}}\tilde{u}^{2}_{i}G_{0,0}\to\int_{\{t=-s^{2}\}}P^{2}G_{0,0}\quad\text{ and }\quad\int_{\{B_{\eta s}\}\cap\{t=-s^{2}\}}\tilde{u}^{2}_{i}G_{0,0}\to\int_{\{B_{\eta s}\}\cap\{t=-s^{2}\}}P^{2}G_{0,0}.

This proves that for large
i

\int_{\{B_{i}\setminus B_{\eta s}\}\cap\{t=-s^{2}\}}\tilde{u}_{i}^{2}G_{0,0}\leq\epsilon_{0}\int_{\{t=-s^{2}\}}\tilde{u}_{i}^{2}G_{0,0}.

The contradiction arises.
∎

With the lemma above, we can prove that the closeness of frequency between
\tilde{u}
and
h
in Theorem
4.23
.

Proposition 4.37
.

Let
u
be a solution to (
3.1
) (
3.2
) on
Q_{2}
with doubling assumption (
3.3
). Let
x\in B_{1}
and
\epsilon\leq\alpha/10
. There exists some
\eta(n,\lambda,\alpha,\Lambda,\epsilon)
and
\ell_{0}(n,\lambda,\alpha,\Lambda,\epsilon)
such that if
|N^{u}_{x,0}(r_{1})-N^{u}_{x,0}(r_{2})|\leq\alpha/4
for
10r_{2}\leq r_{1}\leq\ell_{0}
, let
h
be the approximated caloric function as in Theorem
4.23
. Then we have the following frequency closeness

|N^{\tilde{u},1/r_{1}}(s)-N^{h,\eta}(s)|\leq\epsilon.

(4.38)

Proof.

By Theorem
4.23
, for any
r\in[\tilde{r},1]
we have

\sup_{Q_{r}^{\eta}}|\tilde{u}(y,s)-h(y,s)|\leq\epsilon^{2}(H^{\tilde{u}}(r))^{1/2}.

Hence for any
r\in[\tilde{r},1]
, we have

\int_{B_{\eta r}\cap\{t=-r^{2}\}}|\tilde{u}-h|^{2}G_{0,0}\leq\epsilon^{4}H^{\tilde{u}}(r)

and

\int_{B_{\eta r}\cap\{t=-r^{2}\}}|\tilde{u}+h|^{2}G_{0,0}\leq 10H^{\tilde{u}}(r).

Therefore we have

\int_{B_{\eta r}\cap\{t=-r^{2}\}}|\tilde{u}^{2}-h^{2}|G_{0,0}\leq\int_{\{B_{\eta r}\}\cap\{t=-r^{2}\}}\frac{|\tilde{u}-h|^{2}}{\epsilon^{2}}G_{0,0}+\int_{B_{\eta r}\cap\{t=-r^{2}\}}\frac{\epsilon^{2}|\tilde{u}+h|^{2}}{4}G_{0,0}\leq 4\epsilon^{2}H^{\tilde{u}}(r).

This proves that

\int_{B_{\eta r}\cap\{t=-r^{2}\}}\tilde{u}^{2}G_{0,0}-4\epsilon^{2}H^{\tilde{u}}(r)\leq\int_{B_{\eta r}\cap\{t=-r^{2}\}}h^{2}G_{0,0}\leq\int_{B_{\eta r}\cap\{t=-r^{2}\}}\tilde{u}^{2}G_{0,0}+4\epsilon H^{\tilde{u}}(r).

Now we take
\eta(n,\lambda,\alpha,\Lambda,\epsilon)
as in Lemma
4.36
. We have

(1-5\epsilon^{2})H^{\tilde{u}}(s)\leq\int_{B_{\eta s}\cap\{t=-s^{2}\}}h^{2}G_{0,0}\leq(1+5\epsilon^{2})H^{\tilde{u}}(s).

One can obtain the same estimates for gradient terms
|\nabla h|
. This proves the closeness of frequency.
∎

Combining with Theorem
2.21
, we prove that
u
is uniformly almost symmetric when frequency is pinched.

Theorem 4.39
.

Let
u
be a solution to (
3.1
) (
3.2
) on
Q_{2}
with doubling assumption (
3.3
). Let
x\in B_{1}
and
\epsilon\leq c(n,\lambda,\alpha,\Lambda)
. Then there exist some
\ell_{0}(n,\lambda,\alpha,\Lambda,\epsilon)
and
\delta(n,\lambda,\alpha,\Lambda,\epsilon)
such that if
|N^{u}_{x,0}(r_{1})-N^{u}_{x,0}(r_{2})|\leq\delta
for
10\delta^{-1}r_{2}\leq r_{1}\leq\ell_{0}
, there exists some integer
d
such that:

(1)

For any
r\in[r_{2},r_{1}]
we have
|N^{u}_{x,0}(r)-d|\leq\epsilon
.

(2)

There exists some homogeneous caloric polynomial
P_{d}
with normalization
\fint_{Q_{1}}|P_{d}|^{2}=1
such that for any
r\in[r_{2},\delta r_{1}]

\sup_{Q_{1}}|u_{x,0;r}-P_{d}|\leq\epsilon.

Proof.

The first conclusion comes from Lemma
3.36
. We prove the second conclusion. Let
\tilde{u}=u_{x,0;r_{1}}
. Denote
\hat{r}=r/r_{1}
. Let
h
be the approximated caloric function as in Theorem
4.23
. By Proposition
4.37
, we can apply Theorem
2.21
to find a homogeneous caloric polynomial
P_{d}
of order
d
such that for any
r\in[2r_{2},\delta r_{1}]
such that

\displaystyle\sup_{Q_{1}}|h_{0,0;\hat{r}}-P_{d}|\leq\epsilon.

(4.40)

On the other hand, by the scaling property of rescaled maps we have

u_{x,0;r}(y,s)=\frac{\tilde{u}(\hat{r}z,\hat{r}^{2}s)}{(\fint_{Q_{\hat{r}}}\tilde{u}^{2})^{1/2}}\quad\text{ and }\quad h_{0,0;r}(y,s)=\frac{h(\hat{r}y,\hat{r}^{2}s)}{(\fint_{Q_{\hat{r}}}h^{2})^{1/2}}.

By Theorem
4.23
, we have

\Big|\fint_{Q_{\hat{r}}}\tilde{u}^{2}-\fint_{Q_{\hat{r}}}h^{2}\Big|\leq C\epsilon^{2}\fint_{Q_{\hat{r}}}\tilde{u}^{2}.

Hence

\displaystyle\quad\sup_{Q_{1}}|u_{x,0;r}-h_{0,0;\hat{r}}|=\sup_{Q_{\hat{r}}}|\frac{\tilde{u}}{(\fint_{Q_{\hat{r}}}\tilde{u}^{2})^{1/2}}-\frac{h}{(\fint_{Q_{\hat{r}}}h^{2})^{1/2}}|

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{Q_{\hat{r}}}|\frac{\tilde{u}}{(\fint_{Q_{\hat{r}}}\tilde{u}^{2})^{1/2}}-\frac{h}{(\fint_{Q_{\hat{r}}}\tilde{u}^{2})^{1/2}}|+|\frac{h}{(\fint_{Q_{\hat{r}}}\tilde{u}^{2})^{1/2}}-\frac{h}{(\fint_{Q_{\hat{r}}}h^{2})^{1/2}}|

\displaystyle\leq C\epsilon^{2}.

By triangle inequality, now we have

\sup_{Q_{1}}|u_{x,0;r}-P_{d}|\leq\sup_{Q_{1}}|u_{x,0;r}-h_{0,0;\hat{r}}|+|h_{0,0;\hat{r}}-P_{d}|\leq\epsilon.

The proof is now finished.
∎

5.
Cone-Splitting in a Fixed Time Slice

Recall that we say a continuous function
u
is uniformly
(k,\eta,x)
-symmetric in
[r_{2},r_{1}]
if there exists a
k
-symmetric homogeneous polynomial
P
with
\fint_{B_{1}}|P|^{2}=1
such that for any

6.1
.

By Lemma
3.51
, we have
\sup_{x\in B_{1}}N_{x,0}(1)\leq C_{0}(n,\lambda,\alpha,\Lambda)
for some integer
C_{0}
. We can cover
B_{1}
using Vitali covering with radius
r=c(n,\lambda)\delta
. Then we can apply Proposition
6.12
and
6.20
to decompose
c
-balls and
d
-balls iteratively. Then we obtain the following covering

B_{1}\subset\bigcup_{a}\big(\mathcal{C}_{0,a}\cup\mathcal{N}_{a}\cap B_{a}(x_{a})\big)\cup\bigcup_{b}B_{r_{b}}(x_{b})\cup\bigcup_{e}B_{r_{e}}(x_{e})\cup S_{d},

(6.27)

where
\mathcal{H}^{k}(S_{d})=0
and
\sum_{a}r^{k}_{a}+\sum_{b}r^{k}_{b}+\sum_{e}r^{k}_{e}+\mathcal{H}^{k}(\bigcup_{a}\mathcal{C}_{0,a})\leq C(n,\lambda,\alpha,\Lambda,\delta)
.

Next we deal with
e
-balls. According to the definition of
e
-ball, we know
\mathcal{V}_{\delta,d,r_{e}}(x_{e},0)=\emptyset
. In other words,
\sup_{x\in B_{e}}N_{x,0}(s)\leq C_{0}(n,\lambda,\alpha,\Lambda)-\delta
for some
s\geq 10^{-2}r_{e}
. By Lemma
3.36
we get
\sup_{x\in B_{e}}N_{x,0}(s)\leq C_{0}(n,\lambda,\alpha,\Lambda)-1+\delta
for
s\geq c(n,\lambda,\alpha,\Lambda,\delta)r_{e}
. And we can cover
B_{r_{e}}
with Vitali covering with this radius
c(n,\lambda,\alpha,\Lambda,\delta)r_{e}
. Then apply the arguments above again to refine the covering lemmas of
c
-balls and
d
-balls and continue the process for finitely many (at most
C_{0}(n,\lambda,\alpha,\Lambda))
times. Finally we obtain the result.

The second conclusion comes from the Quantitative Cone-splitting theorem
5.2
. Actually, if
x\in\mathcal{N}^{a}
or
x\in B_{r_{b}}(x_{b})
, by Corollary
3.37
and Theorem
5.2
we have
u
is
(k+1,\epsilon,r^{\prime},x)
-symmetric for some small
r^{\prime}
provided
\eta
and
\delta
is sufficiently small. This proves that
\mathcal{S}^{k}_{\epsilon}\cap B_{1}\subset\Big(S_{0}\cup\bigcup_{a}\mathcal{C}_{0,a}\Big)
.
∎

7.
Proof of the Main Theorems

In this section we will prove our main theorems.

7.1.
Proof of Theorem
1.14

For any
\epsilon\leq 1/10
denote
\mathcal{S}^{n-1}_{\epsilon}=\mathcal{S}^{n-1}_{\epsilon}(u(\cdot,0))
. We will see that
Z_{0}\cap B_{1}\subset\mathcal{S}^{n-1}_{\epsilon}\cap B_{1}
where
Z_{0}=\{x:u(x,0)=0\}
. To see this, it suffices to show that
u(y,0)\neq 0
for any
y\notin\mathcal{S}_{\epsilon}^{n-1}
.Let
y\notin\mathcal{S}_{\epsilon}^{n-1}
we have for some
s>0
that
u(\cdot,0)
is
(n,\epsilon,s,y)
-symmetric. This means that
f(x)=\frac{u(y+sx,0)}{\left(\fint_{B_{1}}|u(y+sx,0)|^{2}dx\right)^{1/2}}
is
\epsilon
-close to
1
. Hence
|f(0)-1|\leq\epsilon
and thus
u(y,0)\neq 0
if
\epsilon<1/2
. Hence we have
Z_{0}\cap B_{1}\subset\mathcal{S}^{n-1}_{\epsilon}\cap B_{1}
. The Hausdorff estimate follows now directly since
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\mathcal{S}_{\epsilon}^{n-1}\cap B_{1})\leq C<\infty
by Theorem
6.1
. Hence we complete the proof. ∎

Let

Let us now turn to the proof of Theorem
1.19
. The following result is standard.

Lemma 7.5
.

Let
u
be a continuous function in
B_{1}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Assume both
\{x:u(x)>0\}
and
\{x:u(x)<0\}
are not empty. Then

\displaystyle\dim Z_{u}\geq n-1,

(7.6)

where
Z_{u}=\{x:u(x)=0\}.

Proof.

Assume
x_{0},y_{0}\in B_{1}
satisfy
u(x_{0})>0
and
u(y_{0})<0
. Since
u\in C^{0}
, then there exists
r_{0}>0
such that
\inf_{B_{r_{0}}(x_{0})}u(x)>0
and
\sup_{B_{r_{0}}(y_{0})}u(x)<0
. Consider two hyperplanes
H_{1}
and
H_{2}
satisfying

•

H_{1}
and
H_{2}
are perpendicular to
v:=x_{0}-y_{0}

•

x_{0}\in H_{1}
and
y_{0}\in H_{2}
.

Denote line
\ell_{x,y}
to be the line segment which is parallel to
v
and
\ell_{x,y}\cap H_{1}=\{x\}
and
\ell_{x,y}\cap H_{2}=\{y\}
. For any
x\in H_{1}\cap B_{r_{0}}(x_{0})
there exists a unique
y_{x}\in H_{2}\cap B_{r_{0}}(y_{0})
such that
\ell_{x,y_{x}}//v
.
Noting that
u|_{H_{1}\cap B_{r_{0}}(x_{0})}>0
and
u|_{H_{1}\cap B_{r_{0}}(y_{0})}<0
and
u
is continuous, there must exist
z_{x}\in\ell_{x,y_{x}}
such that
u(z_{x})=0
. Define a map
\tau:H_{1}\cap B_{r_{0}}(x_{0})\to Z_{u}
by
\tau(x)=z_{x}
. Then
|\tau(x)-\tau(y)|\geq|x-y|
. Hence by the definition of Hausdorff measure, we have

\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\{z:u(z)=0,z\in\ell_{x,y_{x}},x\in H_{1}\cap B_{r_{0}}(x_{0})\})\geq\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(H_{1}\cap B_{r_{0}}(x_{0}))\geq c(n)r_{0}^{n-1}>0.

In particular,
\dim Z_{u}\geq n-1.

∎

Proof of Theorem
1.19
.

By the standard backward uniqueness (see
[
15
,
22
,
37
,
55
]
), we know that
u
is not vanishing at any
t>-4
otherwise
u\equiv 0
in

. Therefore by Theorem
1.4
we know that
\dim Z_{t}\leq n-1
for any

(see also
[
11
]
). Therefore, we only need to consider the following two cases.

Case 1:
Assume
s=-4
and
t>-4
. If
\dim Z_{-4}\geq n-1
, then
\dim Z_{t}\leq n-1\leq\dim Z_{-4}
. If
\dim Z_{-4}<n-1
, then by Lemma

we can assume

. By maximum principle Theorem

we get
u(x,t)>0
for any
t>-4
. In particular
Z_{t}=\emptyset
. Hence
\dim Z_{t}\leq\dim Z_{s}
.

Case 2:
Assume
-4<s<t\leq 0
. We have

and
\dim Z_{s}\leq n-1
. If
\dim Z_{s}=n-1

\dim Z_{s}<n-1

High frequency non-gyrokinetic turbulence at tokamak edge parameters
M. Raeth, K. Hallatschek

Abstract
First of a kind 6D-Vlasov computer simulations of high frequency ion Bernstein wave turbulence for parameters relevant to the tokamak edge show transport comparable to sub-Larmor-frequency gyrokinetic turbulence. The customary restriction of magnetized plasma turbulence studies to the gyrokinetic approximation may not be based on physics but only a practical constraint due to computational cost.



Five-dimensional gyrokinetic turbulence simulations have become the gold
standard for theoretical transport studies in the core of magnetic confinement
systems. Yet, their descriptive power still falls short of the tokamak
edge-plasma and particularly the high confinement mode (H-mode) transition,
likely due to the presence of high gradients (
\rho_{i}/L_{n}=O(1)
) and elevated
fluctuation levels (
\delta n/n=O(1)
). On the other hand, the axioms of
gyrokinetics, that the distribution function is nearly constant on a Larmor
circle and the turbulence time scales long compared to the Larmor period, are
ultimately just assumptions.
Time and again, confined plasma has proven to evade preconceptions, such as
with the unexpected finding of the importance of “hyperfine turbulence”

Jenko 2004
on the extremely small electron Larmor radius scale.
Nevertheless, owing to the many extremely disparate time scales for strongly
magnetized plasmas (e.g. for transport, gyration, Alfven-waves), there
have been only steps in the direction of full 6D simulations, consisting of
only two space dimensions
Deng et al. 2016
and/or linear
verification with standard gyro-kinetic ion temperature gradient (ITG) modes

Sturdevant et al. 2016
, or the study of waves
Yu et al. 2022
in a
gyrokinetic background. (The situation is very different from astrophysics or
inertial fusion, where 6D simulations are common place.)

Using the novel 6D turbulence code BSL6D, which is particularly efficient in
simulating ion gyrations
Kormann et al. 2019
, we demonstrate that indeed
genuinely non-gyrokinetic instabilities and turbulence have to be taken into
account for high gradients such as those typical for a tokamak edge.

The target of our 6D simulations is the ion kinetic equation in dimensionless
(
\rho_{i}=v_{\text{th}}=n=T=1
) variables

\partial_{t}f+\bm{v}\cdot\nabla f+(-\nabla\phi+\bm{v}\times\bm{B})\cdot\nabla_{\bm{v}}f=0

(1)

where for simplicity we restrict ourselves to electrostatic perturbations in
“slab geometry”, i.e., to a homogeneous constant magnetic field

\bm{B}=\hat{z}
. Furthermore, the system is assumed to be quasineutral
(
n_{i}=n_{e}
) and the electrons to be adiabatic,
n_{e}=\phi
.
The initial state is chosen to be in (unstable) equilibrium, which is why it
has to be a function of the gyrocenter coordinate

\bm{r}_{gc}=\bm{r}-\bm{\rho}
,
\bm{\rho}=\hat{z}\times\bm{v}
, in particular,

x_{gc}=x+v_{y}
. We choose the gradients to be in x direction, such as

f_{0}:=f|_{t=0}=g(x_{gc},v),\quad g(x,v)=f_{M}(n(x),T(x)),

(2)

with the Boltzmann distribution

f_{M}(n,T)=n(2\pi T)^{-3/2}e^{-\frac{v^{2}}{2T}}
, where
n(x),T(x)
represent
the background density and temperature profiles as function of the gyrocenter
position.

Linear equations

To study the stability of gyrokinetic and
non-gyrokinetic modes for given density and temperature gradients we linearize
(
1
) around
h=0
with
f=h-\phi g_{0}+f_{0}
,
g_{0}=f_{M}(1,1)
,
resulting in

\partial_{t}h+\bm{v}\cdot\nabla h+\bm{v}\times\hat{z}\cdot\nabla_{\bm{v}}h=\partial_{t}\phi g_{0}+\nabla\phi\cdot\nabla_{v}(f_{0}-g_{0}).

(3)

Using the Boussinesq approximation

\nabla_{v}(f_{0}-g_{0})\approx\nabla_{v}(f_{0}-g_{0})|_{x_{gc}=0}=:\bm{v}^{*}g_{0},

(4)

\text{with}\;\bm{v}^{*}=\hat{y}\left[\kappa_{T}\partial_{T}(\ln f_{M})+\kappa_{n}\right],

(5)

where
\kappa_{n}=L_{n}^{-1}=(\ln n)^{\prime}
,
\kappa_{T}=L_{T}^{-1}=(\ln T)^{\prime}
,

\partial_{T}\ln f_{M}=(v^{2}-3)/2
, the linearized equation becomes translation
invariant and the response of the velocity integral over
h
to an electric
potential on the right hand side of (
3
) can be analyzed in
Fourier space.

At
\bm{v}^{*}=0
the response is the well-known Gordeyev integral

Gordeev 1952
;
Brambilla 1998

\int\frac{h}{\phi}d^{3}v=G_{\omega}(k_{\perp}\sqrt{T},k_{z}\sqrt{T})=

(6)

=-i\omega\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-2Tk_{\perp}^{2}\sin^{2}\frac{t^{\prime}}{2}-\frac{k_{z}^{2}Tt^{\prime 2}}{2}+i\omega t^{\prime}}dt^{\prime},

(7)

where the
T
dependence has been explicitly indicated. Noting the structure
of the right hand side, the response for finite gradient term
\bm{v}^{*}
can
be expressed using a
T
-derivative,

\int\frac{h}{\phi}d^{3}v=\left[1-\frac{k_{y}}{\omega}(\kappa_{T}\partial_{T}+\kappa_{n})\right]G_{\omega}(k_{\perp}\sqrt{T},k_{z}\sqrt{T})=

(8)

=\left[-\omega+{k_{y}}(\kappa_{T}\partial_{T}+\kappa_{n})\right]\sum_{p\in\mathds{Z}}\frac{\Gamma_{p}(T{k_{\perp}}^{2})}{|{k_{z}}|\sqrt{2T}}Z\left(\frac{\omega-p}{|{k_{z}}|\sqrt{2T}}\right),

(9)

where the series expansion
Brambilla 1998
of
G_{\omega}
has been
inserted,
\Gamma_{p}(x)=I_{p}(x)e^{-x}
,

Z(x)=e^{-x^{2}}(i\sqrt{\pi}-2\int_{0}^{x}e^{x^{\prime 2}}dx^{\prime})
is the Fried-Conte plasma
dispersion function, and the expressions are to be evaluated at
T=1
.
The electrostatic dispersion relation follows from quasi-neutrality

n_{i}=n_{e}
, where
n_{i}=\int hd^{3}v-\phi
and
n_{e}=\phi
, as

2=\int hd^{3}v\,/\,\phi.

(10)

In the absence of gradients this equation describes non-gyrokinetic
“neutralized” ion Bernstein waves (IBW) close to the harmonics of the Larmor
frequency with a frequency shift depending on the wavenumbers

Schmitt 1973
. Already for relatively small gradient parameters

\kappa_{n},\kappa_{T}<1
IBW for many harmonics are destabilized, as depicted by
the numerical solutions of (
10
) in
Fig.
1
, easily exceeding the growth rate of the
(gyrokinetic) ITG mode. The IBWs at higher harmonics tend to have a higher
wavenumber of maximum growth rate.

Critical gradients of ion Bernstein waves

In principle, unstable non-gyrokinetic modes can exist for arbitrary complex

\omega
, since the real and imaginary part of (
10
)
can always be satisfied by choosing suitable real parameters

\kappa_{n},\kappa_{T}
, which in general turn out to be of order of the inverse
Larmor radius. However, we would like to know under which conditions there are
also low gradient non-gyrokinetic instabilities with

|k_{y}\kappa_{n}|,|k_{y}\kappa_{T}|\ll 1
and
\omega
arbitrarily close to the Larmor
resonances – similar to the well-known gradient threshold for the (slab)
gyrokinetic ITG modes at very small frequencies. Cauchy’s argument principle

Hasegawa 1968
implies that threshold parameters for the
instability fulfill the dispersion relation exactly at real

\omega
. Conversely, stability is implied, if no such threshold parameters
can be found.

Due to the Gaussian dependence of the imaginary part of
Z
, it can be
neglected in all but one Larmor resonance
p
for frequencies close to the
gyro resonances, i.e., if
\delta\omega=(\omega-p)\ll 1
and
|k_{z}|\ll 1
, the
latter of which must be verified a posteriori. The imaginary part of
(
10
,
9
) then turns into

[-\omega+k_{y}(\kappa_{n}+\kappa_{T}\partial_{T})]\frac{\Gamma_{p}(T{k_{\perp}}^{2})}{\sqrt{T}}e^{-\frac{\delta\omega^{2}}{2{k_{z}}^{2}T}}=0,

(11)

equivalent to

-\frac{\omega}{k_{y}}+\kappa_{n}+\left(\frac{\delta\omega^{2}}{k_{z}^{2}}+\frac{d\ln\Gamma_{p}({k_{\perp}}^{2})}{d\ln{k_{\perp}}}-1\right)\frac{\kappa_{T}}{2}=0,

(12)

while the real part of the dispersion relation is

\chi_{\text{nr}}+\left[-\omega+{k_{y}}(\kappa_{T}\partial_{T}+\kappa_{n})\right]\frac{\Gamma_{p}(T{k_{\perp}}^{2})}{|{k_{z}}|\sqrt{2T}}Z\left(\frac{\delta\omega}{|{k_{z}}|\sqrt{2T}}\right)=0

(13)

with the weakly
\omega
-dependent non-resonant charge density response
defined as

\chi_{\text{nr}}=-2+\left[-\omega+{k_{y}}(\kappa_{T}\partial_{T}+\kappa_{n})\right]\sum_{q\in\mathds{Z},q\neq p}\frac{\Gamma_{q}(T{k_{\perp}}^{2})}{|{k_{z}}|\sqrt{2T}}Z\left(\frac{\omega-q}{|{k_{z}}|\sqrt{2T}}\right).

(14)

In the limit
\omega=p
,
k_{z}\rightarrow 0
and small gradients, the constant
term dominates the expression for
\chi_{\text{nr}}
. Direct numerical
inspection for varying
p
and arbitrary
{k_{\perp}}
shows that

|\chi_{\text{nr}}+2|<1.29|1-k_{y}\kappa_{n}/\omega|+0.52|k_{y}\kappa_{T}/\omega|

for
p\neq 0
, and
|\chi_{\text{nr}}+2|<|k_{y}\kappa_{T}|
for
p=0
. Hence, for
sufficiently small gradients
\chi_{\text{nr}}<0
, and more specifically

-2.3\lesssim\chi_{\text{nr}}\lesssim-0.7
. Solving (
11
) for

\kappa_{n}
and inserting it into (
13
) cancels the plasma dispersion
function,

\chi_{\text{nr}}+\frac{1}{2}\frac{\delta\omega}{k_{z}^{2}}\kappa_{T}k_{y}\Gamma_{p}({k_{\perp}}^{2})=0,

(15)

where the identity
Z^{\prime}(x)=-2(1+xZ(x))
has been used.

Solving (
15
) for
k_{z}^{2}
one can replace
k_{z}
as a parameter by

\delta\omega
provided that
\delta\omega\,k_{y}\kappa_{T}\chi_{\text{nr}}<0
.
With this, (
11
) results in

-\frac{p}{k_{y}}+\kappa_{n}+\left(\frac{d\ln\Gamma_{p}({k_{\perp}}^{2})}{d\ln{k_{\perp}}}-1\right)\frac{\kappa_{T}}{2}-\delta\omega\left(1+\frac{\chi_{\text{nr}}}{\Gamma_{p}}\right)=0.

(16)

Fixing the signs of
k_{y},\kappa_{T}
to be positive, the sign of the term
proportional to
\delta\omega
is always positive for low gradients, since

\chi_{\text{nr}}<-\Gamma_{p}
, because
\Gamma_{0}\leq 1
,
\Gamma_{p}<0.25/p
for any

{k_{\perp}}
. Thus, the existence of a threshold requires the rest of
(
16
) to be negative, in other words,

\kappa_{n}<\frac{p}{k_{y}}+\left(1-\frac{d\ln\Gamma_{0}({k_{\perp}}^{2})}{d\ln{k_{\perp}}}\right)\frac{\kappa_{T}}{2}.

(17)

For the gyrokinetic ITG mode
p=0
, and the standard threshold condition
results. For the instability of non-gyrokinetic modes (
17
)
requires
p>0
and no conditions on the gradients themselves besides their
assumed smallness, and the condition that
\kappa_{T}\neq 0
, since otherwise
(
15
) cannot be solved for
k_{z}^{2}
. Then, for an arbitrarily small

\delta\omega
a marginally stable threshold mode exists at small
k_{y},{k_{\perp}}
,
since

\frac{k_{y}\chi_{\text{nr}}}{\Gamma_{p}({k_{\perp}}^{2})}=-O(k_{y}^{1-2p})\rightarrow-\infty\mbox{
for }k_{y}={k_{\perp}}\rightarrow 0

(18)

and another one at large
{k_{\perp}}
at fixed
k_{y}
, since

\frac{k_{y}\chi_{\text{nr}}}{\Gamma_{p}({k_{\perp}}^{2})}=-O({k_{\perp}})\rightarrow+\infty\mbox{
for }{k_{\perp}}\rightarrow\infty,k_{y}=const.

(19)

Since according to (
15
) in both limits
{k_{z}}\rightarrow 0
, the initial
assumption of a single resonance contributing to the imaginary part of the ion
response is justified.
On the other hand, if (
17
) does not hold,
(
16
) has no valid solutions in
{k_{\perp}}
and
\delta\omega
, and
hence there are no real solutions for any
\omega
,
k_{z}
,
k_{y}
, and
{k_{\perp}}

for the system of equations (
11
,
13
) and therefore according
to Cauchy’s argument theorem
Hasegawa 1968
there are no
low-gradient instabilities close to the resonance in the system.

Since the condition
p>0
only concerns the sign of the phase velocity and not
the gradients themselves, the non-gyrokinetic high frequency IBW instabilities
occur
even when the gyrokinetic ITG modes are ruled out by the
thresholds
– the IBWs just require a non-zero temperature gradient.

6D turbulence simulations

Having executed extensive linear and nonlinear benchmarking simulations

Raeth 2023
;
Raeth et al. 2023
with BSL6D to solve (
1

the dispersion relation and reproducing results analogous to

Sturdevant et al. 2016

run showing the most relevant features of the high-frequency turbulence.

Since there is no equivalent to the gyrokinetic flux-tube scenario for the
non-gyrokinetic equations without the additional approximation
(
4
), we use periodic boundary conditions. The ion
distribution function
f({\bm{x}},{\bm{v}})
is initialized with a Maxwellian

f_{0}=f_{M}(n,T)
with a background density and temperature profile similar to
(
2

n=1+g_{n}\sin\frac{2\pi x_{gc}}{L_{x}}+\text{ran}(\bm{r}_{gc}),\;T=1+g_{T}\sin\frac{2\pi x_{gc}}{L_{x}},

(20)

white noise density perturbation
\text{ran}(\bm{r}_{gc})

The profile amplitudes have been set to
g_{T}=0.6

g_{n}=0.49

resulting in the maximum inverse gradient lengths
\max(\kappa_{T})=0.375

The computation has been carried out in a phase space region covering the

[-4,4]^{3}

150000

In the following, band pass filters have been used diagnostically to separate

Discussion

Using a novel 6D kinetic simulation code specifically designed for strongly
magnetized plasmas we have shown that high frequency non-gyrokinetic
instabilities (ion Bernstein waves, IBWs) can produce transport-relevant
turbulence, even in competition to gyrokinetic ITG turbulence, which is
usually exclusively considered in those plasmas.
The simulations have been simplified by assuming electrostatic fields,
adiabatic electrons and a homogeneous magnetic field. They refer therefore
mostly to the ion turbulence component of low-
\beta
, high-gradient plasmas,
where the magnetic fluctuations are small and the magnetic field
inhomogeneities are unimportant compared to the plasma gradients.
Nevertheless, the IBWs can be analytically shown to be unstable at arbitrarily
low gradients and wavenumbers. They require only the presence of a temperature
gradient, and not the excess of a temperature gradient threshold like the ITG
modes.

Gradient lengths such as in the simulation detailed above,
\sim 3\rho_{i}
, are
regularly encountered in the edge of tokamak H-mode discharges

Wolfrum et al. 2007
, where also the temperature gradients are typically
below the ITG instability threshold, since

\eta=L_{n}/L_{T}\sim 0.6-1.2
.
This, together with the potential breakdown of gyrokinetic theory for short
gradient lengths and the lack of a satisfying explanation for the
L/H-transition, suggests a full 6D treatment of turbulence to be very relevant
to the edge region of magnetic confinement devices.
In future work, more detailed electron physics and inhomogeneous, fluctuating
magnetic fields will be included in such simulations. It seems likely that the
inclusion of additional degrees of freedom would rather increase than decrease
the intensity of the non-gyrokinetic modes.

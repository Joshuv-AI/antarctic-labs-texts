Some Analytical Properties of the Hyperbolic Sine Integral
Kwara Nantomah

Abstract
. By using some tools of analysis, we establish some analytical properties such as monotonicity and inequalities involving the hyperbolic sine integral function. As applications of some of the established properties, we obtain some rational bounds for the hyperbolic tangent function.

2.
Some Properties of the Hyperbolic Sine Integral

The hyperbolic sine integral function may also be defined for
z\in(-\infty,\infty)
by the following equivalent forms.

\displaystyle\mathrm{Shi}(z)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\sinh(zt)}{t}\,dt,

(3)

\displaystyle=\sum_{r=0}^{\infty}\frac{z^{2r+1}}{(2r+1)(2r+1)!}.

(4)

By change of variable, representation (
3
) is obtained from (
2
) and representation (
4
) is obtained from either (
2
) or (
3
) by using the series representation of
\frac{\sinh(z)}{z}
.

By utilizing representation (
3
), the derivatives of
\mathrm{Shi}(z)
are obtained as follows.

\mathrm{Shi}^{(k)}(z)=\int_{0}^{1}t^{k-1}\sinh(zt)dt,\quad k\in\{2m:m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\},

(5)

\mathrm{Shi}^{(k)}(z)=\int_{0}^{1}t^{k-1}\cosh(zt)dt,\quad k\in\{2m+1:m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\},

(6)

where
\mathbb{N}_{0}=\{0,1,2,3,...\}
. In particular, the first and second derivatives are

\mathrm{Shi}^{\prime}(z)=\int_{0}^{1}\cosh(zt)dt=\frac{\sinh(z)}{z},

(7)

\mathrm{Shi}^{\prime\prime}(z)=\int_{0}^{1}t\sinh(zt)dt=\frac{\cosh(z)}{z}-\frac{\sinh(z)}{z^{2}}.

(8)

Remark 2.1
.

Identity (
8
) implies that

\cosh(z)>\frac{\sinh(z)}{z}

(9)

for
z>0
and this is well known in the literature.

supperadditive or subadditive on

Theorem 2.3
.

The function
\mathrm{Shi}(z)
is supperadditive on
(0,\infty)
. That is, the inequality

\mathrm{Shi}(u+v)>\mathrm{Shi}(u)+\mathrm{Shi}(v)

(10)

holds for
u>0
and
v>0
.

First Proof.

Let
A(z)=\frac{\mathrm{Shi}(z)}{z}
for
z>0
. Then

\displaystyle z^{2}A^{\prime}(z)

\displaystyle=z\mathrm{Shi}^{\prime}(z)-\mathrm{Shi}(z)

\displaystyle=\sum_{r=0}^{\infty}\frac{z^{2r+1}}{(2r+1)!}-\sum_{r=0}^{\infty}\frac{z^{2r+1}}{(2r+1)(2r+1)!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{r=0}^{\infty}\left[1-\frac{1}{2r+1}\right]\frac{z^{2r+1}}{(2r+1)!}

\displaystyle>0.

Hence
A(z)
is increasing and the conclusion follows from Lemma
2.2
.
∎

Second Proof.

Let
u>0
and
v>0
. Then

\displaystyle\mathrm{Shi}(u+v)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\sinh(ut+vt)}{t}\,dt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\sinh(ut)\cosh(vt)}{t}\,dt+\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\cosh(ut)\sinh(vt)}{t}\,dt

\displaystyle>\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\sinh(ut)}{t}\,dt+\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\sinh(vt)}{t}\,dt

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Shi}(u)+\mathrm{Shi}(v)

since
\cosh(z)>1
for all
z\neq 0
.
∎

Third Proof.

Let
\phi(u,v)=\mathrm{Shi}(u+v)-\mathrm{Shi}(u)-\mathrm{Shi}(v)
for
u>0
and
v>0
. Without loss of generality, let
v
be fixed. Then

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial u}\phi(u,v)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Shi}^{\prime}(u+v)-\mathrm{Shi}^{\prime}(u)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\cosh(ut+vt)dt-\int_{0}^{1}\cosh(ut)dt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\left[\cosh(ut)\cosh(vt)+\sinh(ut)\sinh(vt)\right]\,dt-\int_{0}^{1}\cosh(ut)\,dt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\cosh(ut)[\cosh(vt)-1]dt+\int_{0}^{1}\sinh(ut)\sinh(vt)dt

\displaystyle>0

since
\cosh(z)>1
for all
z\neq 0
. Thus,
\phi(u,v)
is increasing and so

\phi(u,v)>\lim_{u\to 0}\phi(u,v)=0

which gives the desired result.
∎

Theorem 2.4
.

The inequality

\mathrm{Shi}(u)+\mathrm{Shi}(v)>u+v

(11)

holds for
u>0
and
v>0
, and the inequality

\frac{\mathrm{Shi}(u)}{\mathrm{Shi}(v)}\leq\frac{u}{v}

(12)

holds for
0<u\leq v
.

Proof.

The monotonicity property of the function
\frac{\mathrm{Shi}(z)}{z}
implies that, for
z>0
, we have

\frac{\mathrm{Shi}(z)}{z}>\lim_{z\to 0^{+}}\frac{\mathrm{Shi}(z)}{z}=1.

That is,

\mathrm{Shi}(z)>z.

Hence for
u>0
and
v>0
, we have
\mathrm{Shi}(u)>u
and
\mathrm{Shi}(v)>v
which results to (
11
). Likewise, for
0<u\leq v
, we have

\frac{\mathrm{Shi}(u)}{u}\leq\frac{\mathrm{Shi}(v)}{v}

which results to (
12
).
∎

Theorem 2.5
.

Let
z>0
and
\lambda\in(0,1)
. Then the inequality

\mathrm{Shi}(\lambda z)>\lambda\mathrm{Shi}(z)

(13)

holds. If
\lambda>1
, then the inequality is reversed.

Proof.

Let
\alpha(z)=\mathrm{Shi}(\lambda z)-\lambda\mathrm{Shi}(z)
for
z>0
and
\lambda\in(0,1)
. Then

\displaystyle\alpha^{\prime}(z)

\displaystyle=\lambda\left[\mathrm{Shi}^{\prime}(\lambda z)-\mathrm{Shi}^{\prime}(z)\right]<0

since
\mathrm{Shi}^{\prime}(z)
is increasing for
z>0
. Hence
\alpha(z)
is decreasing and then, we have

\alpha(z)>\lim_{z\to 0^{+}}\alpha(z)=0

which gives (
13
).
∎

Theorem 2.6
.

For
z>0
, the inequality

\mathrm{Shi}(z)+\mathrm{Shi}(1/z)\geq 2\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\sinh(t)}{t}dt\approx 2.11450

(14)

holds. Equality is attained if
z=1
.

Proof.

The case for
z=1
is easily seen. Because of this, let
P(z)=\mathrm{Shi}(z)+\mathrm{Shi}(1/z)
for
z\in(0,1)\cup(1,\infty)
. Then

P^{\prime}(z)=\mathrm{Shi}^{\prime}(z)-\frac{1}{z^{2}}\mathrm{Shi}^{\prime}(1/z),

which means that

\displaystyle zP^{\prime}(z)

\displaystyle=\sinh(z)-\sinh(1/z):=E(z)

Since
\sinh(z)
is increasing, then
E(z)<0
if
z\in(0,1)
and
E(z)>0
if
z\in(1,\infty)
. Thus,
P(z)
is decreasing on
(0,1)
and increasing on
(1,\infty)
. Therefore, on both intervals, we have

P(z)>\lim_{z\to 1}P(z)=2\mathrm{Shi}(1)=2\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\sinh(t)}{t}dt\approx 2.11450

completing the proof.
∎

Lemma 2.7

(
[
13
]
)
.

Let
-\infty\leq u<v\leq\infty
and
p
and
q
be continuous functions that are differentiable on
(u,v)
, with
p(u+)=q(u+)=0
or
p(v-)=q(v-)=0
. Suppose that
q(z)
and
q^{\prime}(z)
are nonzero for all
z\in(u,v)
. If
\frac{p^{\prime}(z)}{q^{\prime}(z)}
is increasing (or decreasing) on
(u,v)
, then
\frac{p(x)}{q(x)}
is also increasing (or decreasing) on
(u,v)
.

In the literature, Lemma
2.7
is referred to as l’Hospital rule for monotonicy. It has become a remarkable tool in proving various results in mathematical analysis.

Lemma 2.8
.

For
z>0
, the function
T(z)=\frac{\sinh(z)}{\mathrm{Shi}(z)}
is increasing.

Proof.

For
z\in(0,\infty)
, we have

T(z)=\frac{\sinh(z)}{\mathrm{Shi}(z)}=\frac{p_{1}(z)}{q_{1}(z)},

where
p_{1}(z)=\sinh(z)
,
q_{1}(z)=\mathrm{Shi}(z)
and
p_{1}(0)=q_{1}(0)=0
. Then

\frac{p^{\prime}_{1}(z)}{q^{\prime}_{1}(z)}=\frac{z\cosh(z)}{\sinh(z)}=\frac{p_{2}(z)}{q_{2}(z)}

where
p_{2}(z)=z\cosh(z)
,
q_{2}(z)=\sinh(z)
and
p_{2}(0)=q_{2}(0)=0
. Then

\displaystyle\sinh^{2}(z)\left(\frac{p_{2}(z)}{q_{2}(z)}\right)^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\left[\cosh(z)+z\sinh(z)\right]\sinh(z)-z\cosh^{2}(z)

\displaystyle=\cosh(z)\sinh(z)+z\left[\sinh^{2}(z)-\cosh^{2}(z)\right]

\displaystyle=\cosh(z)\sinh(z)-z

\displaystyle>0

since
\cosh(z)>1
and
\sinh(z)>z
for
z>0
.
Thus,
\frac{p_{1}^{\prime}(z)}{q_{1}^{\prime}(z)}
is increasing. Hence by Lemma
2.7
, the function
\frac{p_{1}(z)}{q_{1}(z)}
is also increasing. This completes the proof.
∎

Theorem 2.9
.

For
z>0
, the inequality

\mathrm{Shi}(z)\mathrm{Shi}(1/z)\geq\left(\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\sinh(t)}{t}dt\right)^{2}\approx 1.11778

(15)

holds. Equality is attained if
z=1
.

Proof.

The case for
z=1
is easily seen. And so, let
Q(z)=\mathrm{Shi}(z)\mathrm{Shi}(1/z)
and
\theta(z)=\ln Q(z)
for
z\in(0,1)\cup(1,\infty)
. Then

\displaystyle z\theta^{\prime}(z)

\displaystyle=z\frac{\mathrm{Shi}^{\prime}(z)}{\mathrm{Shi}(z)}-\frac{1}{z^{2}}\frac{\mathrm{Shi}^{\prime}(1/z)}{\mathrm{Shi}(1/z)}

\displaystyle=\frac{\sinh(z)}{\mathrm{Shi}(z)}-\frac{\sinh(1/z)}{\mathrm{Shi}(1/z)}

\displaystyle:=H(z).

Because of Lemma
2.8
, then
H(z)<0
if
z\in(0,1)
and
H(z)>0
if
z\in(1,\infty)
. Subsequently,
Q(z)
is decreasing on
(0,1)
and increasing on
(1,\infty)
. Therefore, on both intervals, we have

Q(z)>\lim_{z\to 1}Q(z)=(\mathrm{Shi}(1))^{2}=\left(\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\sinh(t)}{t}dt\right)^{2}\approx 1.11778

completing the proof.
∎

Lemma 2.10
.

For
z>0

\mathrm{Shi}(z)-\sinh(z)<0

(16)

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle V^{\prime}(z)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Shi}^{\prime}(z)-\cosh(z)

\displaystyle=\frac{\sinh(z)}{z}-\cosh(z)<0

as a result of (
9
). Hence

V(z)<\lim_{z\to 0}V(z)=0

which proves (
16

Lemma 2.11
.

For
z>0
, the function
K(z)=\frac{\sinh(z)}{\mathrm{Shi}^{2}(z)}
is decreasing.

Proof.

For
z\in(0,\infty)
, we have

K(z)=\frac{\sinh(z)}{\mathrm{Shi}^{2}(z)}=\frac{p_{1}(z)}{q_{1}(z)},

where
p_{1}(z)=\sinh(z)
,
q_{1}(z)=\mathrm{Shi}^{2}(z)
and
p_{1}(0)=q_{1}(0)=0
. Then

\frac{p^{\prime}_{1}(z)}{q^{\prime}_{1}(z)}=\frac{z\cosh(z)}{2\mathrm{Shi}(z)\sinh(z)}=\frac{p_{2}(z)}{q_{2}(z)}

where
p_{2}(z)=z\cosh(z)
,
q_{2}(z)=2\mathrm{Shi}(z)\sinh(z)
and
p_{2}(0)=q_{2}(0)=0
. Then

\displaystyle 2\mathrm{Shi}^{2}(z)\left(\frac{p_{2}(z)}{q_{2}(z)}\right)^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Shi}(z)\coth(z)-z\mathrm{Shi}(z)\mathrm{cosech}^{2}(z)-\cosh(z)

\displaystyle=\cosh(z)\left[\frac{\mathrm{Shi}(z)}{\sinh(z)}-1\right]-z\mathrm{Shi}(z)\mathrm{cosech}^{2}(z)

\displaystyle<0

as a result of (
16
).
Thus,
\frac{p_{1}^{\prime}(z)}{q_{1}^{\prime}(z)}
is decreasing. Hence by Lemma
2.7
, the function
\frac{p_{1}(z)}{q_{1}(z)}
is also decreasing. This completes the proof.
∎

Remark 2.12
.

The increasing property of the function
\frac{\sinh(z)}{\mathrm{Shi}(z)}
is equivalent to

z\mathrm{Shi}(z)\cosh(z)-\sinh^{2}(z)>0.

(17)

Also, the decreasing property of the function
\frac{\sinh(z)}{\mathrm{Shi}^{2}(z)}
is equivalent to

z\mathrm{Shi}(z)\cosh(z)-2\sinh^{2}(z)<0.

(18)

Combining (
17
) and (
18
) yields

\sinh^{2}(z)<z\mathrm{Shi}(z)\cosh(z)<2\sinh^{2}(z)

(19)

which is also equivalent to

\frac{\tanh(z)}{z}<\frac{\mathrm{Shi}(z)}{\sinh(z)}<2\frac{\tanh(z)}{z}.

(20)

Theorem 2.13
.

For
z>0
, the inequality

\frac{z}{2}+\frac{\cosh(z)-1}{z}<\mathrm{Shi}(z)<2\left(\frac{\cosh(z)-1}{z}\right)

(21)

holds.

Proof.

Recall that
t<\mathrm{Shi}(t)<\sinh(t)

t>0
. Then, integrating over the interval
(0,z)

\int_{0}^{z}tdt<\int_{0}^{z}\mathrm{Shi}(t)dt<\int_{0}^{z}\sinh(t)dt

\frac{z^{2}}{2}<z\mathrm{Shi}(z)-\cosh(z)+1<\cosh(z)-1

Theorem 2.14
.

For
z>0
, the inequality

\frac{2\mathrm{Shi}(z)\mathrm{Shi}(1/z)}{\mathrm{Shi}(z)+\mathrm{Shi}(1/z)}\leq\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\sinh(t)}{t}dt\approx 1.05725

(22)

holds. Equality is attained if
z=1
.

Proof.

The case for
z=1
is easily seen. On that note, let
\Psi(z)=\frac{2\mathrm{Shi}(z)\mathrm{Shi}(1/z)}{\mathrm{Shi}(z)+\mathrm{Shi}(1/z)}
and
h(z)=\ln\Psi(z)
for
z\in(0,1)\cup(1,\infty)
. Then

h^{\prime}(z)=\frac{\mathrm{Shi}^{\prime}(z)}{\mathrm{Shi}(z)}-\frac{1}{z^{2}}\frac{\mathrm{Shi}^{\prime}(1/z)}{\mathrm{Shi}(1/z)}-\frac{\mathrm{Shi}^{\prime}(z)-\frac{1}{z^{2}}\mathrm{Shi}^{\prime}(1/z)}{\mathrm{Shi}(z)+\mathrm{Shi}(1/z)}

which implies that

z\left[\mathrm{Shi}(z)+\mathrm{Shi}(1/z)\right]h^{\prime}(z)=z\frac{\mathrm{Shi}^{\prime}(z)}{\mathrm{Shi}(z)}\mathrm{Shi}(1/z)-\frac{1}{z}\frac{\mathrm{Shi}^{\prime}(1/z)}{\mathrm{Shi}(1/z)}\mathrm{Shi}(z).

This further gives rise to

\displaystyle z\left[\frac{1}{\mathrm{Shi}(z)}+\frac{1}{\mathrm{Shi}(1/z)}\right]h^{\prime}(z)

\displaystyle=z\frac{\mathrm{Shi}^{\prime}(z)}{\mathrm{Shi}^{2}(z)}-\frac{1}{z}\frac{\mathrm{Shi}^{\prime}(1/z)}{\mathrm{Shi}^{2}(1/z)}

\displaystyle=\frac{\sinh(z)}{\mathrm{Shi}^{2}(z)}-\frac{\sinh(1/z)}{\mathrm{Shi}^{2}(1/z)}

\displaystyle=D(z).

Owing to Lemma
2.11
, we have
D(z)>0
if
z\in(0,1)
and
D(z)<0
if
z\in(1,\infty)
. Thus,
h(z)
is increasing on
(0,1)
and decreasing on
(1,\infty)
. Accordingly,
\Psi(z)
is increasing on
(0,1)
and decreasing on
(1,\infty)
. Therefore, on both intervals, we have

\Psi(z)<\lim_{z\to 1}\Psi(z)=\mathrm{Shi}(1)=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\sinh(t)}{t}dt\approx 1.05725

completing the proof.
∎

3.
Rational Bounds for the Hyperbolic Tangent Function

In this section, as applications of the hyperbolic sine integral, we obtain some rational bounds for the hyperbolic tangent function.

Theorem 3.1
.

For
z>0
, the inequalities

\frac{2z}{z^{2}+2}<\tanh(z)<\frac{z^{3}+6z}{3z^{2}+6}

(25)

hold.

Proof.

By direct computations, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathrm{Shi}^{(3)}(z)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}t^{2}\cosh(zt)dt

\displaystyle=\frac{(z^{2}+2)\sinh(z)-2z\cosh(z)}{z^{3}}>0.

Upon rearrangement, we obtain

\tanh(z)>\frac{2z}{z^{2}+2}

which gives the left hand side of (
25
). Also,

\displaystyle\mathrm{Shi}^{(4)}(z)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}t^{3}\sinh(zt)dt

\displaystyle=\frac{(z^{3}+6z)\cosh(z)-(3z^{2}+6)\sinh(z)}{z^{4}}>0.

Hence

\tanh(z)<\frac{z^{3}+6z}{3z^{2}+6}

which gives the right hand side of (
25
). This completes the proof.
∎

.

Proof.

By direct computations, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathrm{Shi}^{(5)}(z)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}t^{4}\cosh(zt)dt

\displaystyle=\frac{(z^{4}+12z^{2}+24)\sinh(z)-(4z^{3}+24z)\cosh(z)}{z^{5}}>0.

This implies that

\tanh(z)>\frac{4z^{3}+24z}{z^{4}+12z^{2}+24}

which gives the left hand side of (
26
). Also,

\displaystyle\mathrm{Shi}^{(6)}(z)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}t^{5}\sinh(zt)dt

\displaystyle=\frac{(z^{5}+20z^{3}+120z)\cosh(z)-(5z^{4}+60z^{2}+120)\sinh(z)}{z^{6}}

\displaystyle>0.

Hence

\tanh(z)<\frac{z^{5}+20z^{3}+120z}{5z^{4}+60z^{2}+120}

which gives the right hand side of (
26
). This completes the proof.
∎

\displaystyle\mathrm{Shi}^{(8)}(z)

Theorem 3.4
.

For
z>0
, the inequalities

\frac{8z^{7}+336z^{5}+6720z^{3}+40320z}{z^{8}+56z^{6}+1680z^{4}+20160z^{2}+40320}<\tanh(z)\\
<\frac{z^{9}+72z^{7}+3024z^{5}+60480z^{3}+362880z}{9z^{8}+504z^{6}+15120z^{4}+181440z^{2}+362880}

hold.

Proof.

\displaystyle\mathrm{Shi}^{(9)}(z)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}t^{8}\cosh(zt)dt

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{z^{9}}\left[(z^{8}+56z^{6}+1680z^{4}+20160z^{2}+40320)\sinh(z)\right.

\displaystyle\quad\left.-(8z^{7}+336z^{5}+6720z^{3}+40320z)\cosh(z)\right]

\displaystyle>0.

This implies that

\tanh(z)>\frac{8z^{7}+336z^{5}+6720z^{3}+40320z}{z^{8}+56z^{6}+1680z^{4}+20160z^{2}+40320}

which gives the left hand side of (

\displaystyle\mathrm{Shi}^{(10)}(z)

Remark 3.6

Due to their monotonicity properties, for

\mathrm{Shi}^{(m)}(z)

Corollary 3.7
.

For
z>0
, the inequalities

\frac{z^{2}+2}{2}<\cosh(z)<e^{\frac{z^{2}}{6}}\left(\frac{z^{2}+2}{2}\right)^{\frac{2}{3}}

Proof.

By integrating (

\int_{0}^{z}\frac{2t}{t^{2}+2}dt<\int_{0}^{z}\tanh(t)dt<\int_{0}^{z}\frac{t^{3}+6t}{3t^{2}+6}dt

\ln\frac{z^{2}+2}{2}<\ln\cosh(z)<\ln\left\{e^{\frac{z^{2}}{6}}\left(\frac{z^{2}+2}{2}\right)^{\frac{2}{3}}\right\}

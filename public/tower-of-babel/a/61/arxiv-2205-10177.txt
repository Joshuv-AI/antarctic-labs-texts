Stability of black solitons in optical systems with intensity-dependent dispersion
Dmitry E. Pelinovsky, Michael Plum

Abstract
. Black solitons are identical in the nonlinear Schrödinger (NLS) equation with intensity-dependent dispersion and the cubic defocusing NLS equation. We prove that the intensity-dependent dispersion introduces new properties in the stability analysis of the black soliton. First, the spectral stability problem possesses only isolated eigenvalues on the imaginary axis. Second, the energetic stability argument holds in Sobolev spaces with exponential weights. Third, the black soliton persists with respect to addition of a small decaying potential and remains spectrally stable when it is pinned to the minimum points of the effective potential. The same model exhibits a family of traveling dark solitons for every wave speed and we incorporate properties of these dark solitons for small wave speeds in the analysis of orbital stability of the black soliton.



1.
Introduction

The canonical model of nonlinear optics for dark and black solitons
is the cubic defocusing NLS equation
[
19
,
25
]
, which can be written in the form

(1.1)

iu_{t}+u_{xx}+2(1-|u|^{2})u=0,

where
u(t,x):\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}\mapsto\mathbb{C}
is normalized by the boundary conditions

(1.2)

|u(t,x)|\to 1\quad\mbox{\rm as}\;\;|x|\to\infty.

Dark solitons are traveling wave solutions of the form

(1.3)

u(t,x)=\gamma\tanh(\gamma(x-2ct))+ic,\quad\gamma:=\sqrt{1-c^{2}},

where
c\in(-1,1)
is the parameter for the half wave speed. The limiting
solution for
c=0
,

(1.4)

u(t,x)=\tanh(x),

is referred to as the black soliton as the intensity
I:=|u|^{2}
reaches zero at a point of odd symmetry in
x
. The dark solitons (
1.3
) can be extended with two
translational parameters due to the basic symmetries of the NLS equation (
1.1
):

(1.5)

\mbox{\rm If }u(t,x)\;\;\mbox{\rm is a solution, so is }e^{i\theta_{0}}u(t,x+\zeta_{0}),\quad\theta_{0},\zeta_{0}\in\mathbb{R}.

The cubic defocusing NLS equation (
1.1
) is integrable with inverse scattering such that the dark solitons can be associated with eigenvalues of a self-adjoint linear (Dirac) operator
[
33
]
. Orbital and asymptotic
stabilities of the dark and black solitons were studied in many mathematical papers, e.g. with functional-analytic methods
[
3
,
4
,
15
]
, the inverse scattering transform
[
9
,
14
]
, and higher-order conserved quantities
[
11
]
. Lower-order conserved quantities were recently constructed in
[
20
,
21
]
to derive global solutions of the cubic NLS equation (
1.1
) in spaces of lower regularity.

Extended models for dark solitons were considered by using the generalized defocusing NLS equation, e.g. with the cubic–quintic and saturable nonlinearities
[
29
]
. Many results were obtained in the mathematical analysis of the generalized NLS models such as the local and global existence of solutions to the initial-value problem in energy space
[
12
,
13
]
, orbital stability of dark and black solitons
[
2
,
5
,
23
]
, and persistence of the black soliton under a small exponentially decaying potential
[
28
]
.

1.1.
NLS models with intensity-dependent dispersion

The objective of this work is to study orbital stability of the black soliton in a novel NLS model, which we take in the normalized form

(1.6)

i(1-|\psi|^{2})\psi_{t}+\psi_{xx}=0,

where
\psi(t,x):\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}\mapsto\mathbb{C}
is the wave function. We assume that
\psi(t,\cdot)\in\mathcal{F}
for every
t\in\mathbb{R}
, where

(1.7)

\mathcal{F}:=\left\{f\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}):\quad|f(x)|<1,\;\;x\in\mathbb{R}\quad\mbox{\rm and}\quad|f(x)|\to 1\;\;{\rm as}\;\;|x|\to\infty\right\}.

After the standing wave transformation

\psi(t,x)=e^{-2it}u(t,x),

the new model is equivalent
to the modified cubic defocusing NLS equation in the form

(1.8)

i(1-|u|^{2})u_{t}+u_{xx}+2(1-|u|^{2})u=0,

which has the same solution (
1.4
) for the black soliton. The black soliton can be extended with two translational parameters
\theta_{0}
and
\zeta_{0}
due to the same symmetries (
1.5
). In addition, it can be extended to the family of
traveling wave solutions with the wave speed
c
:

(1.9)

\psi(t,x)=e^{-2it}U_{c}(x-2ct),

where
U_{c}(\xi)
with
\xi:=x-2ct
is a solution of the normalized equation

(1.10)

U_{c}^{\prime\prime}-2ic(1-|U_{c}|^{2})U_{c}^{\prime}+2(1-|U_{c}|^{2})U_{c}=0.

Dark solitons with the profile
U_{c}
are solutions of the differential equation (
1.10
) in the set
\mathcal{F}
satisfying
the boundary conditions

(1.11)

U_{c}(\xi)\to e^{i\theta_{\pm}(c)}\quad\mbox{\rm as}\;\;\xi\to\pm\infty

for some phases
\theta_{\pm}(c)\in[0,2\pi)
. If
c=0
, then

(1.12)

\varphi(\xi):=U_{c=0}(\xi)=\tanh(\xi)

is the black soliton with
\theta_{+}(0)=0
and
\theta_{-}(0)=\pi
. In addition, equation (
1.6
) is invariant under the scaling transformation:

(1.13)

\mbox{\rm If }\psi(t,x)\;\;\mbox{\rm is a solution, so is }\psi(\omega^{2}t,\omega x),\quad\omega>0.

The starting model (
1.6
) represents a class of NLS equations
with intensity-dependent dispersion, which have been used for modeling of coherently prepared multistate atoms
[
16
]
, quantum well waveguides
[
22
]
, and fiber-optics communication systems
[
24
]
. Bright solitons of the NLS model with normalized intensity-dependent dispersion,

(1.14)

i\psi_{t}+(1-|\psi|^{2})\psi_{xx}=0,

were studied in
[
30
,
31
]
. Bright solitons have cusped singularities at the unit intensity and the nature of singularities was incorporated in the study of existence and energetic stability of bright solitons in the energy space
[
30
,
31
]
. Note that the energetic stability results were conditional to the existence of local solutions to the initial-value problem for the NLS model (
1.14
) in the energy space and the energy conservation, which was left as an open problem.

1.2.
Motivations

The NLS model (
1.6
) with the inverse behavior of the normalized intensity-dependent dispersion compared to the NLS model (
1.14
) features both the dark and black solitons and is the subject of the present work. Similar to the scopes of
[
30
,
31
]
, we will not address local well-posedness of the initial-value problem but will focus on the analysis of energetic stability of the black soliton and the novelty compared to the case of the cubic defocusing NLS equation (
1.1
).

It is particularly interesting to see how the intensity-dependent dispersion introduces exponential weights in the construction of a Lyapunov functional for the black soliton.

For the cubic defocusing NLS equation (
1.1
), it is well-known that the phase of complex perturbations to the black soliton cannot be controlled in the energy space due to nonzero boundary conditions. Related to this property, the second variation of the Lyapunov functional is not coercive in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
under the constraint of fixed (
renormalized
) momentum and the continuous spectrum of the linearized operator does not possess a spectral gap near the zero eigenvalue. To remedy the lack of coercivity, an exponentially weighted
H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
space was introduced in
[
15
]
, in which the orbital stability of the black soliton was obtained with the standard Lyapunov method. The phase of complex perturbations can be controlled in the weighted
H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
space. A generalization of the ideas of
[
15
]
was used in the weighted
H^{2}(\mathbb{R})
space in
[
11
]
due to integrability of the cubic defocusing NLS equation (
1.1
).

The same method of weighted
H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
spaces was then used in
[
8
]
to study orbital stability of the domain walls which are minimizers of energy with nonzero boundary conditions at infinity
[
1
]
. Recently, the same idea of the exponential weighted
H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
space was used in
[
2
]
to study orbital stability of the black soliton in the quintic defocusing NLS equation.

1.3.
Summary of results

As the main outcome of this work, we will show that the intensity-dependent dispersion gives a natural definition of the exponentially weighted
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
space in which the second variation of the Lyapunov functional is coercive
under the constraints of fixed momentum and mass. The exponentially weighted
H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
space appears as the form domain of the linearized operator.

The orbital stability of the black soliton is established
in the exponentially weighted
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
space
with the Lyapunov method that incorporates the additional scaling transformation (
1.13
). Furthermore, the spectrum of the linearized operator in the exponentially weighted
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
space is shown to be purely discrete with a spectral gap near the zero eigenvalue. One important ingredient in the analysis
of orbital stability of the black soliton is the asymptotic behavior of the dark
solitons with the profile
U_{c}\in\mathcal{F}
satisfying (
1.10
) and (
1.11
) as
c\to 0
. We establish the asymptotic behavior with arguments based on the implicit function theorem as the explicit expressions for
U_{c}
are not available.

As a consequence of the spectral gap near the zero eigenvalue, spectral stability of the black soliton can also be studied for the extended NLS model with a small decaying potential as in

(1.15)

i(1-|\psi|^{2})\psi_{t}+\psi_{xx}=\varepsilon V(x)\psi,

where
\varepsilon
is a small parameter and
V
is a fixed real-valued potential. By using Lyapunov–Schmidt reduction methods,
we will show for
V\in W^{2,\infty}(\mathbb{R})\cap L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
that the black soliton persists at the non-degenerate extremal points of the effective potential

(1.16)

\mathcal{V}(s):=\int_{\mathbb{R}}V(x+s){\rm sech}^{2}(x)dx.

Moreover, we will show for
V\in W^{2,\infty}(\mathbb{R})\cap L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
that the black soliton is linearly stable if the extremal point is a local minimum of
\mathcal{V}(s)
and linearly unstable
if it is a local maximum of
\mathcal{V}(s)
. This suggests robust pinning of the black soliton to the minima of the effective potential
\mathcal{V}(s)
. This outcome of the NLS model (
1.6
) is a great practical improvement compared to the cubic NLS equation (
1.1
),
where pinning of the black soliton is linearly unstable both at the maximum and minimum points of the effective potential
\mathcal{V}(s)

[
28
]
.

, respectively.

2.
Conserved quantities

Assume that the NLS equation (
1.6
) admits a smooth solution
\psi(t,\cdot)\in\mathcal{F}\cap C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
for some
t\in\mathbb{R}
satisfying

(2.1)

\psi(t,x)=e^{i\theta_{\pm}}\left[1+A_{\pm}e^{-\alpha_{\pm}|x|}+{\rm o}(e^{-\alpha_{\pm}|x|})\right]\qquad\mbox{\rm as}\;\;x\to\pm\infty,

for some real
\alpha_{\pm}>0
,
A_{\pm}<0
, and
\theta_{\pm}\in[0,2\pi)
which may depend on time
t\in\mathbb{R}
. The asymptotic expansion (
2.1
) is assumed to be differentiable term by term. Substituting (
2.1
) into (
1.6
) shows that
\theta_{\pm}
changes in time as
\theta_{\pm}^{\prime}(t)=-\frac{1}{2}\alpha_{\pm}(t)^{2}
.

The NLS model (
1.6
) has formally conserved quantities

(2.2)

M(\psi):=\int_{\mathbb{R}}(1-|\psi|^{2})^{2}dx\quad\mbox{\rm and}\quad E(\psi):=\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\psi_{x}|^{2}dx,

which have the meaning of mass and energy, respectively. This can be checked directly with

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}M(\psi)

\displaystyle=-2\int_{\mathbb{R}}(1-|\psi|^{2})(\bar{\psi}\psi_{t}+\bar{\psi}_{t}\psi)dx

\displaystyle=-2i(\bar{\psi}\psi_{x}-\bar{\psi}_{x}\psi)|_{x\to-\infty}^{x\to+\infty}=0,

and

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}E(\psi)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}(\bar{\psi}_{x}\psi_{xt}+\bar{\psi}_{xt}\psi_{x})dx

\displaystyle=i\frac{\bar{\psi}_{x}\psi_{xx}-\bar{\psi}_{xx}\psi_{x}}{1-|\psi|^{2}}|_{x\to-\infty}^{x\to+\infty}=0,

where in the last line we have used that
\psi(t,\cdot)\in\mathcal{F}\cap C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
has
the exponential decay (
2.1
) which ensures that

\bar{\psi}_{x}\psi_{xx}-\bar{\psi}_{xx}\psi_{x}
converges to zero at infinity with the double exponential rate compared to
1-|\psi|^{2}
.

In addition, the NLS equation (
1.6
) has formally conserved
momentum

(2.3)

P(\psi)=\frac{1}{2i}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{(1-|\psi|^{2})^{2}}{|\psi|^{2}}(\bar{\psi}\psi_{x}-\bar{\psi}_{x}\psi)dx,

provided that
\psi(t,x)\neq 0
everywhere. To confirm conservation of the momentum, we write
P=P_{1}+P_{2}
, where

(2.4)

P_{1}(\psi)=\frac{i}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(1-|\psi|^{2})(\bar{\psi}\psi_{x}-\bar{\psi}_{x}\psi)dx,\quad P_{2}(\psi)=\frac{1}{2i}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(1-|\psi|^{2})\left(\frac{\psi_{x}}{\psi}-\frac{\bar{\psi}_{x}}{\bar{\psi}}\right)dx,

and obtain after straightforward computations,

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}P_{1}(\psi)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(\bar{\psi}\psi_{xxx}-\bar{\psi}_{x}\psi_{xx}-\bar{\psi}_{xx}\psi_{x}+\bar{\psi}_{xxx}\psi)dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{|\psi|^{2}(\bar{\psi}_{x}\psi_{xx}+\bar{\psi}_{xx}\psi_{x})}{1-|\psi|^{2}}dx

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}(\bar{\psi}\psi_{xx}-2|\psi_{x}|^{2}+\bar{\psi}_{xx}\psi)|_{x\to-\infty}^{x\to+\infty}-\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{|\psi|^{2}(|\psi_{x}|^{2})_{x}}{1-|\psi|^{2}}dx

and

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}P_{2}(\psi)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(\frac{\psi_{xxx}}{\psi}-\frac{\psi_{x}\psi_{xx}}{\psi^{2}}-\frac{\bar{\psi}_{x}\bar{\psi}_{xx}}{\bar{\psi}^{2}}+\frac{\bar{\psi}_{xxx}}{\bar{\psi}}\right)dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{(\bar{\psi}_{x}\psi_{xx}+\bar{\psi}_{xx}\psi_{x})}{1-|\psi|^{2}}dx

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\psi_{xx}}{\psi}+\frac{\bar{\psi}_{xx}}{\bar{\psi}}\right)|_{x\to-\infty}^{x\to+\infty}+\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{(|\psi_{x}|^{2})_{x}}{1-|\psi|^{2}}dx,

from which it follows that
P(\psi)=P_{1}(\psi)+P_{2}(\psi)
conserves
if
\psi(t,\cdot)\in\mathcal{F}\cap C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
satisfies (
2.1
) and
\psi(t,x)\neq 0
everywhere.

Remark 2.1
.

P_{1}(\psi)
and
P_{2}(\psi)
in (
2.4
) generalize the conserved momentum and phase quantities of the cubic NLS equation (
1.1
) in the same way as
M(\psi)
in (
2.2
) generalizes the conserved mass
of the cubic NLS equation. The renormalized momentum
P=P_{1}+P_{2}
is introduced in the cubic NLS equation (
1.1
) from the two conserved quantities as
the technical tool to prove orbital stability of dark solitons
[
3
,
4
,
5
,
23
]
. In the context of the NLS model (
1.6
), neither
P_{1}
nor
P_{2}
are conserved quantities but their sum is the conserved quantity bearing the same meaning as
the renormalized momentum of the cubic NLS equation (
1.1
).

Remark 2.2
.

Using the decomposition
P=P_{1}+P_{2}
given by (
2.4
), we can rewrite the momentum
P(\psi)
in the form

(2.5)

\displaystyle P(\psi)=\frac{i}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(2-|\psi|^{2})(\bar{\psi}\psi_{x}-\bar{\psi}_{x}\psi)dx+\arg(\psi)|_{x\to-\infty}^{x\to+\infty},

where the last term is
\theta_{+}-\theta_{-}
if
\psi(t,\cdot)\in\mathcal{F}\cap C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
satisfies (
2.1
). The form (
2.5
) is defined even if
\psi(t,x)
vanishes at some
(t,x)
.

The conservation of the momentum
P(\psi)
in the form (
2.5
) can be verified independently from the NLS model (
1.6
) and the exponential decay (
2.1
) which suggests
\theta_{\pm}^{\prime}(t)=-\frac{1}{2}\alpha_{\pm}(t)^{2}
. Differentiating (
2.5
) and using (
1.6
) yield

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}P(\psi)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\alpha_{+}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\alpha_{-}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{2-|\psi|^{2}}{1-|\psi|^{2}}\left(\bar{\psi}_{x}\psi_{xx}+\bar{\psi}_{xx}\psi_{x}-\bar{\psi}\psi_{xxx}-\bar{\psi}_{xxx}\psi\right)dx

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{2-|\psi|^{2}}{(1-|\psi|^{2})^{2}}(\bar{\psi}\psi_{xx}+\bar{\psi}_{xx}\psi)dx+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{1}{1-|\psi|^{2}}(\bar{\psi}\psi_{xx}-\bar{\psi}_{xx}\psi)(\bar{\psi}\psi_{x}-\bar{\psi}_{x}\psi)dx

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\alpha_{+}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\alpha_{-}^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\frac{2-|\psi|^{2}}{1-|\psi|^{2}}\left(\bar{\psi}\psi_{xx}+\bar{\psi}_{xx}\psi\right)\biggr|_{x\to-\infty}^{x\to+\infty}+4|\psi_{x}|^{2}\biggr|_{x\to-\infty}^{x\to+\infty}=0,

where the exponential decay (
2.1
) was used to get cancellation of terms at infinity. Hence, the momentum
P(\psi)
in the form (
2.5
) is also conserved in time
t
. Compared to (
2.3
), the form (
2.5
) does not require
\psi(t,x)\neq 0
everywhere.

3.
Main results

We recall that
\psi(t,x)=e^{-2it}\tanh(x)
is the standing wave
of the NLS model (
1.6
) corresponding to the black soliton.
From the conserved quantities
M(\psi)
and
E(\psi)
in (
2.2
),
we construct the Lyapunov functional for the black soliton in the form

(3.1)

\Lambda(\psi):=E(\psi)+M(\psi)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left[|\psi_{x}|^{2}+(1-|\psi|^{2})^{2}\right]dx.

Since it coincides with the Lyapunov functional for the cubic NLS equation
(
1.1
), it is clear that the black soliton
\varphi(x)=\tanh(x)
is a critical point of
\Lambda(\psi)
in the energy space

(3.2)

\Sigma:=\left\{\psi\in H^{1}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}):\quad\psi_{x}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}),\;\;1-|\psi|^{2}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})\right\},

equipped with the distance between two elements
\psi_{1},\psi_{2}\in\Sigma
:

\mathcal{D}_{\Sigma}(\psi_{1},\psi_{2}):=\sqrt{\|\psi_{1}^{\prime}-\psi_{2}^{\prime}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\||\psi_{1}|^{2}-|\psi_{2}|^{2}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}}.

The critical point
\varphi\in\Sigma
of
\Lambda(\psi)
is degenerate only due to the translational and phase symmetries (
1.5
). We introduce the perturbation
u+iv\in H^{1}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R})
to
\varphi
according to the expansion

(3.3)

\psi(t,x)=e^{-2it}\left[\varphi(x)+u(t,x)+iv(t,x)\right].

The expansion of the Lyapunov functional
\Lambda(\psi)
is given by

(3.4)

\Lambda(\psi)-\Lambda(\varphi)=Q_{+}(u)+Q_{-}(v)+R(u,v),

where the quadratic forms are given by

(3.5)

\displaystyle Q_{+}(u)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left[(u_{x})^{2}+2(3\varphi^{2}-1)u^{2}\right]dx,

(3.6)

\displaystyle Q_{-}(v)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left[(v_{x})^{2}+2(\varphi^{2}-1)v^{2}\right]dx,

and the remainder term is given by

(3.7)

R(u,v)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left[(2\varphi u+u^{2}+v^{2})^{2}-4\varphi^{2}u^{2}\right]dx.

Expansion (
3.4
) is the same as in the cubic NLS equation (
1.1
), where the form of the remainder term (
3.7
)
uses the quantity

(3.8)

\eta:=|\varphi+u+iv|^{2}-\varphi^{2}=2\varphi u+u^{2}+v^{2},

which belongs to
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
if
\psi
belongs to the energy space
\Sigma
. What appears to be new
is the linearized equations which arise when the decomposition
(
3.3
) is substituted to the NLS model (
1.6
) and the quadratic and cubic terms with respect to perturbation
u+iv
are crossed out. Separation of the real and imaginary parts gives the following linearized equations:

(1-\varphi^{2})u_{t}=L_{-}v,\qquad(1-\varphi^{2})v_{t}=-L_{+}u.

where the linear operators
L_{\pm}
are defined by the differential expressions

(3.9)

\displaystyle L_{+}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\partial_{x}^{2}+6\varphi^{2}-2,

(3.10)

\displaystyle L_{-}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\partial_{x}^{2}+2\varphi^{2}-2.

Separation of variables gives the spectral stability problem in the form

(3.11)

\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}0&L_{-}\\
-L_{+}&0\end{array}\right]\left[\begin{array}[]{c}u\\
v\end{array}\right]=\lambda(1-\varphi^{2})\left[\begin{array}[]{c}u\\
v\end{array}\right]\qquad\Leftrightarrow\qquad\begin{array}[]{c}L_{-}v=\lambda(1-\varphi^{2})u,\\
-L_{+}u=\lambda(1-\varphi^{2})v,\end{array}

which naturally suggests to consider the linear operators
L_{\pm}

in the Hilbert space

(3.12)

\mathcal{H}:=\left\{f\in L^{2}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}):\quad\sqrt{1-\varphi^{2}}f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})\right\},

with the associated inner product

(3.13)

(f,g)_{\mathcal{H}}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}}(1-\varphi^{2})\bar{f}gdx.

and the induced norm
\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{H}}
. The Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
is nothing but an exponentially weighted
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
space since

\sqrt{1-\varphi^{2}}(x)={\rm sech}(x).

Related to
\mathcal{H}
, the form domain and the operator domain
for the linear operators
L_{\pm}
are defined as follows:

(3.14)

\mathcal{H}^{1}_{+}:=H^{1}(\mathbb{R}),\quad\mathcal{H}^{1}_{-}:=\left\{f\in\mathcal{H}:\quad f^{\prime}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})\right\},

and

(3.15)

\mathcal{H}^{2}_{\pm}:=\left\{f\in\mathcal{H}_{\pm}^{1}:\quad\cosh^{2}(\cdot)\;L_{\pm}f\in\mathcal{H}\right\}.

Writing
\mathcal{L}_{\pm}:=\cosh^{2}(\cdot)L_{\pm}
defines linear operators

\mathcal{L}_{\pm}:\mathcal{H}^{2}_{\pm}\subset\mathcal{H}\mapsto\mathcal{H}
. The linear stability problem (
3.11
) can be rewritten
with the use of these operators in the form

(3.16)

\mathcal{L}_{-}v=\lambda u,\qquad-\mathcal{L}_{+}u=\lambda v.

The quadratic forms
Q_{\pm}
in (
3.5
)–(
3.6
)
are defined by
\mathcal{L}_{\pm}
in
\mathcal{H}^{1}_{\pm}
.
We will next present four main results proven in this work.

The first result (Section
4
) concerns with the spectrum of the linear operators
\mathcal{L}_{\pm}
in
\mathcal{H}
and the linear stability problem (
3.16
) in
\mathcal{H}\times\mathcal{H}
.

Theorem 1
.

The spectrum of
\mathcal{L}_{\pm}
in
\mathcal{H}
with the dense domain
\mathcal{H}^{2}_{\pm}\subset\mathcal{H}
consists of simple isolated eigenvalues
as follows

(3.17)

\sigma_{\mathcal{H}}(L_{+})=\{0,\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\dots\},\qquad 0<\mu_{1}<\mu_{2}<\dots

and

(3.18)

\sigma_{\mathcal{H}}(L_{-})=\{-2,0,\nu_{1},\nu_{2},\dots\},\qquad 0<\nu_{1}<\nu_{2}<\dots

The spectrum of the linear stability problem (
3.16
) in
\mathcal{H}\times\mathcal{H}
consists of pairs of isolated eigenvalues

(3.19)

\{\pm i\omega_{1},\pm i\omega_{2},\dots\},\qquad\qquad 0<\omega_{1}\leq\omega_{2}\leq\dots

and a quadruple zero eigenvalue associated with the symmetries (
1.5
).

Remark 3.1
.

Eigenvalues of
\mathcal{L}_{+}
and
\mathcal{L}_{-}
in
\mathcal{H}
can be found explicitly as

\mu_{n}:=n(n+5),\quad\nu_{n}:=(n+1)(n+2)-2,\quad n\in\mathbb{N}.

Remark 3.2
.

4.
Linear operators
\mathcal{L}_{\pm}
in the exponentially weighted
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
space

Here we analyze the spectra of linear operators
\mathcal{L}_{\pm}
and the linearized operator

(4.1)

\mathcal{L}=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}0&\mathcal{L}_{-}\\
-\mathcal{L}_{+}&0\end{array}\right]

in the Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
defined in (
3.12
). We provide the proof of Theorem
1
which gives spectral stability of the black soliton and specifies that the spectra of
\mathcal{L}_{-}
and
\mathcal{L}_{+}
in
\mathcal{H}
and
\mathcal{L}
in
\mathcal{H}\times\mathcal{H}
are purely discrete.

4.1.
Spectrum of
\mathcal{L}_{-}
in
\mathcal{H}

It follows from (
3.10
) that
L_{-}=-\partial_{x}^{2}-2\,{\rm sech}^{2}(x)
. The spectral problem for
\mathcal{L}_{-}
in
\mathcal{H}
take the form

(4.2)

-v^{\prime\prime}(x)-2\;{\rm sech}^{2}(x)\;v(x)=\nu\;{\rm sech}^{2}(x)\;v(x),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}.

The first two eigenvalues of the spectral problem (
4.2
) are available explicitly:

\displaystyle\nu=-2:

\displaystyle\quad v(x)=1,

\displaystyle\nu=0:

\displaystyle\quad v(x)=\tanh(x).

The bounded solutions of the second-order differential equation (
4.2
) belong to the form domain
\mathcal{H}^{1}_{-}
given by (
3.14
), whereas the linearly growing solutions do not belong to
\mathcal{H}^{1}_{-}
. Hence, eigenvalues of the spectral problem
(
4.2
) coincide with the admissible values of
\nu

for which the Schrödinger operator

\mathcal{S}_{\nu}:=-\partial_{x}^{2}-(2+\nu){\rm sech}^{2}(x):H^{2}(\mathbb{R})\subset L^{2}(\mathbb{R})\to L^{2}(\mathbb{R})

admits the end-point resonance at the zero energy level with a bounded eigenfunction
v(x)
. This problem is well-known
in mathematical physics (see
[
26
, Chapter 6, pp.768–769]
), for which the differential equation (
4.2
) is converted to the hypergeometric equation. It is also well-known that the power series for the hypergeometric function is truncated into a polynomial which admits a bounded eigenfunction if and only if

2+\nu=n(n+1),\quad\mbox{\rm where}\;\;n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}:=\{0,1,2,\dots\}.

This gives the exact location of the simple
eigenvalues at
\nu=n(n+1)-2
(Remark
3.1
).

Remark 4.1
.

Each eigenvalue is simple because if one solution of the second-order differential equation (
3.18
) is bounded, the second solution is linearly growing in
x
at infinity. Moreover, Sturm’s oscillation theorem states that the bounded eigenfunctions for the
n
-th eigenvalue has
(n-1)
zeros on
\mathbb{R}
. Hence
0
is the second eigenvalue of
\mathcal{L}_{-}
in
\mathcal{H}
.

We are going to make the picture above precise and give the proof that
the linear operator
\mathcal{L}_{-}
in the Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
has a purely discrete spectrum consisting of simple isolated eigenvalues. In order to do so, we define a positive linear operator

M_{-}:=-\partial_{x}^{2}+{\rm sech}^{2}(x)

or equivalently,

(4.3)

\mathcal{M}_{-}:\mathcal{H}^{2}_{-}\to\mathcal{H},\quad\mathcal{M}_{-}:=-\cosh^{2}(x)\partial_{x}^{2}+1,

where the operator domain
\mathcal{H}_{-}^{2}
is given by (
3.15
).
It follows from the triangle inequality that the operator domain
\mathcal{H}_{-}^{2}
can be written in the equivalent form

(4.4)

\mathcal{H}^{2}_{-}=\left\{f\in\mathcal{H}:\quad f^{\prime}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}),\;\;\cosh(\cdot)f^{\prime\prime}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})\right\}

equipped with the norm

(4.5)

\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{2}_{-}}:=\sqrt{\|\cosh(\cdot)f^{\prime\prime}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|f^{\prime}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\|f\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}}}.

The first result insures that
0
does not belong to the spectrum of the operator
\mathcal{M}_{-}
in
\mathcal{H}
.

Lemma 4.1
.

The linear operator
\mathcal{M}_{-}
given by (
4.3
) is bijective
and symmetric, whereas
\mathcal{M}^{-1}_{-}
is a bounded operator from
\mathcal{H}
to
\mathcal{H}^{2}_{-}
.

Proof.

For injectivity, we use the integration by parts formula (Lemma
A.2
) which ensures that if
u\in\mathcal{H}^{2}_{-}
, then

\langle-u^{\prime\prime},u\rangle_{L^{2}}=\|u^{\prime}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}
. Hence, if there exists
u\in\mathcal{H}^{2}_{-}
such that
\mathcal{M}_{-}u=0
, then

\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(|u^{\prime}(x)|^{2}+{\rm sech}^{2}(x)|u(x)|^{2}\right)dx=0

which implies that
u=0
in
\mathcal{H}
. Hence,
{\rm Ker}(\mathcal{M}_{-})
is trivial in
\mathcal{H}
.

For surjectivity, let
f\in\mathcal{H}
and consider the resolvent equation for
\mathcal{M}_{-}
in the weak form:

(4.6)

\left(u,\phi\right)_{\mathcal{H}^{1}_{-}}=\left(f,\phi\right)_{\mathcal{H}},\quad\forall\phi\in\mathcal{H}^{1}_{-},

where the inner product in
\mathcal{H}^{1}_{-}
is defined by

\left(u,\phi\right)_{\mathcal{H}^{1}_{-}}:=\left(u,\phi\right)_{\mathcal{H}}+\langle u^{\prime},\phi^{\prime}\rangle_{L^{2}},\quad\forall u,\phi\in\mathcal{H}^{1}_{-},

together with the induced norm
\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{H}^{1}_{-}}
.
Due to the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality

|\left(f,\phi\right)_{\mathcal{H}}|\leq\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}}\|\phi\|_{\mathcal{H}}\leq\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}}\|\phi\|_{\mathcal{H}^{1}_{-}},\quad\forall\phi\in\mathcal{H}^{1}_{-}

and the Riesz Representation Theorem, there exists a unique solution
u\in\mathcal{H}^{1}_{-}
of (
4.6
) such that

\|u\|_{\mathcal{H}^{1}_{-}}\leq\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}}
. This yields

\langle u^{\prime},\phi^{\prime}\rangle_{L^{2}}=\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\rm sech}^{2}(\cdot)(\bar{f}-\bar{u})\phi dx,\quad\forall\phi\in\mathcal{H}^{1}_{-}

with
{\rm sech}^{2}(\cdot)(f-u)\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
. Therefore, if we take
\phi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R})\subset\mathcal{H}^{1}_{-}
, then
u^{\prime}
is weakly differentiable and
-u^{\prime\prime}={\rm sech}^{2}(\cdot)(f-u)\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
.
Hence,
-\cosh(\cdot)u^{\prime\prime}={\rm sech}(\cdot)(f-u)\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
so that

u\in\mathcal{H}^{2}_{-}
is a strong solution of

\mathcal{M}_{-}u=f
for every
f\in\mathcal{H}
.

The bound on the inverse operator
\mathcal{M}_{-}^{-1}
from
\mathcal{H}
to
\mathcal{H}^{2}_{-}
follows from
\|u\|_{\mathcal{H}^{1}_{-}}\leq\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}}
and

\|\cosh(\cdot)u^{\prime\prime}\|_{L^{2}}\leq\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}}+\|u\|_{\mathcal{H}}\leq 2\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}},

so that the definition (
4.5
) implies that
\|\mathcal{M}_{-}^{-1}f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{2}_{-}}\leq\sqrt{5}\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}}
holds for every
f\in\mathcal{H}
.

Finally, the integration by parts
formula (Lemma
A.2
) ensures for any
u,v\in\mathcal{H}^{2}_{-}
that

\left(\mathcal{M}_{-}u,v\right)_{\mathcal{H}}=\int_{\mathbb{R}}(-\bar{u}^{\prime\prime}+{\rm sech}^{2}(\cdot)\bar{u})vdx=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\bar{u}(-v^{\prime\prime}+{\rm sech}^{2}(\cdot)v)dx=\left(u,\mathcal{M}_{-}v\right)_{\mathcal{H}},

so that the linear operator (
4.3
) is symmetric.
∎

The next goal is to prove compactness of the embedding of
\mathcal{H}^{2}_{-}
into
\mathcal{H}
. This will ensure that the spectrum of the operator
\mathcal{M}_{-}
in
\mathcal{H}
is purely discrete.

Lemma 4.2
.

The embedding
\mathcal{H}^{2}_{-}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{H}
is compact.

Proof.

Let
\{u_{n}\}
denotes a bounded sequence in
\mathcal{H}^{2}_{-}
so that
\{\cosh(\cdot)u_{n}^{\prime\prime}\}
,
\{u_{n}^{\prime}\}
, and

\{{\rm sech}(\cdot)u_{n}\}
are bounded in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
. In particular,
since
\cosh(\cdot)\geq 1
,
\{u_{n}^{\prime}\}
is bounded in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
.
Since the mapping
H^{1}(\mathbb{R})\ni v\mapsto{\rm sech}(\cdot)v\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
is compact, there exists a subsequence
\{u_{n_{k}}\}
such that
\{{\rm sech}(\cdot)u_{n_{k}}^{\prime}\}
is convergent in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
.

Furthermore,
\left({\rm sech}(\cdot)u_{n}\right)^{\prime}={\rm sech}(\cdot)u_{n}^{\prime}-\tanh(\cdot){\rm sech}(\cdot)u_{n}
is bounded in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
, hence

\{{\rm sech}(\cdot)u_{n}\}
is bounded in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
. By Sobolev’s embedding
of
H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
into
C^{0}(\mathbb{R})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
,
\{u_{n}(0)\}
is bounded in
\mathbb{C}
so that without loss of generality,

\{u_{n_{k}}(0)\}
converges in
\mathbb{C}
. Using

u_{n}(x)=u_{n}(0)+\int_{0}^{x}\cosh(t){\rm sech}(t)u_{n}^{\prime}(t)dt,

we obtain

\displaystyle{\rm sech}(x)|u_{n_{k}}(x)-u_{n_{j}}(x)|

\displaystyle\leq{\rm sech}(x)|u_{n_{k}}(0)-u_{n_{j}}(0)|

\displaystyle+{\rm sech}(x)\int_{0}^{x}\cosh(t){\rm sech}(t)|u^{\prime}_{n_{k}}(t)-u^{\prime}_{n_{j}}(t)|dt.

The first term in the right-hand side is bounded in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
by

\sqrt{2}|u_{n_{k}}(0)-u_{n_{j}}(0)|,

which vanishes in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
as
k,j\to\infty
due to convergence of
\{u_{n_{k}}(0)\}
. Using the bound (
A.3
) (Lemma
A.1
), the second term in the right-hand side is bounded in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
by

2\|{\rm sech}(\cdot)(u_{n_{k}}^{\prime}-u_{n_{j}}^{\prime})\|_{L^{2}},

which vanishes as
k,j\to\infty
due to convergence of
\{{\rm sech}(\cdot)u_{n_{k}}^{\prime}\}
. Hence,
\{{\rm sech}(\cdot)u_{n_{k}}\}
converges in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
, and thus

\{u_{n_{k}}\}
converges in
\mathcal{H}
which verifies that
the embedding
\mathcal{H}^{2}_{-}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{H}
is compact.
∎

Combining the inverse operator
\mathcal{M}_{-}^{-1}:\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H}^{2}_{-}
in Lemma
4.1
and
the compact embedding
\mathcal{H}^{2}_{-}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{H}

in Lemma
4.2
, we can define a compact
operator

(4.7)

\mathcal{K}_{-}=\mathcal{M}_{-}^{-1}:\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H}^{2}_{-}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{H}.

The following corollary
gives the desired result for the proof of Theorem
1
.

Corollary 4.1
.

The spectrum of
\mathcal{L}_{-}
in
\mathcal{H}
consists of isolated eigenvalues.

Proof.

The compact operator
\mathcal{K}_{-}
has a purely discrete spectrum of
eigenvalues accumulating at
0
. Hence the linear operator

\mathcal{M}_{-}:\mathcal{H}^{2}_{-}\subset\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H}
has a purely discrete spectrum of eigenvalues. Since
\mathcal{L}_{-}=\mathcal{M}_{-}-3
, the spectrum of
\mathcal{L}_{-}
in
\mathcal{H}
consists of isolated eigenvalues.
∎

Remark 4.2
.

Isolated eigenvalues of
\mathcal{L}_{-}
in
\mathcal{H}
given by Corollary
4.1
satisfy the ordering (
3.18
) because each eigenvalue is simple and
\nu=0
is the second eigenvalue of the spectral problem (
4.2
) (Remark
4.1
).

4.2.
Spectrum of
\mathcal{L}_{+}
in
\mathcal{H}

It follows from (
3.9
) that
L_{+}=-\partial_{x}^{2}+4-6\,{\rm sech}^{2}(x)
. The spectral problem for
\mathcal{L}_{+}
in
\mathcal{H}
take the form

(4.8)

-u^{\prime\prime}(x)+4u(x)-6\;{\rm sech}^{2}(x)\;u(x)=\mu\;{\rm sech}^{2}(x)\;u(x),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}.

The first eigenvalue of the spectral problem (
4.8
) is available explicitly:

\displaystyle\mu=0:

\displaystyle\quad u(x)={\rm sech}^{2}(x).

The differential equation (
4.8
) has exponentially growing and exponentially decaying solutions at infinity. By Levinston’s theorem (Proposition 8.1 in
[
7
]
), the growth and decay rates are
\pm 2
, hence
the exponentially growing solutions do not belong to
\mathcal{H}
.
Thus, we conclude that eigenvalues of the spectral problem
(
4.8
) coincide with the admissible values of
\mu

for which the Schrödinger operator

\mathcal{R}_{\mu}:=-\partial_{x}^{2}-(6+\mu){\rm sech}^{2}(x):H^{2}(\mathbb{R})\subset L^{2}(\mathbb{R})\to L^{2}(\mathbb{R})

admits an eigenvalue at the energy level
-4
with a bounded and exponentially decaying eigenfunction
u(x)
. By using the exact solution from the hypergeometric equation (see
[
26
, Chapter 6, pp.768–769]
), the bounded and exponentially decaying eigenfunction exists at the energy level
-4
if and only if

\mu=n(n+5),\quad\mbox{\rm where}\;\;n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}:=\{0,1,2,\dots\}.

This gives the exact location of the simple eigenvalues (Remark
3.1
).

Remark 4.3
.

Each eigenvalue is simple because if one solution of the second-order differential equation
(
4.8
) is exponentially decaying, then the second solution is exponentially growing in
x
at infinity. Moreover, Sturm’s oscillation theorem states that the bounded eigenfunctions for the
n
-th eigenvalue has
(n-1)
zeros on
\mathbb{R}
. Hence
0
is the first eigenvalue of
\mathcal{L}_{-}
.

We will complete the picture above with the proof that
the linear operator
\mathcal{L}_{+}
in the Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
has a purely discrete spectrum consisting of simple isolated eigenvalues. In order to do so, we define a positive linear operator

M_{+}:=-\partial_{x}^{2}+4

or equivalently,

(4.9)

\mathcal{M}_{+}:\mathcal{H}^{2}_{+}\to\mathcal{H},\quad\mathcal{M}_{+}:=\cosh^{2}(x)(-\partial_{x}^{2}+4),

where the operator domain
\mathcal{H}_{+}^{2}
given by (
3.15
) can be rewritten in the equivalent form

(4.10)

\mathcal{H}^{2}_{+}=\left\{f\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R})\;\;\cosh(\cdot)(-\partial_{x}^{2}+4)f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})\right\},

equipped with the norm

(4.11)

\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{2}_{+}}:=\sqrt{\|\cosh(\cdot)(-f^{\prime\prime}+4f)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|f^{\prime}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+4\|f\|^{2}_{L^{2}}}.

We also recall that
\mathcal{H}^{1}_{+}\equiv H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
.

The next two lemmas give equivalent results to those in Lemma
4.1
and
4.2
.

Lemma 4.3
.

The linear operator
\mathcal{M}_{+}
given by (
4.9
) is bijective
and symmetric, whereas
\mathcal{M}^{-1}_{+}
is a bounded operator from
\mathcal{H}
to
\mathcal{H}^{2}_{+}
.

Proof.

For injectivity, we again use the integration by parts formula (Lemma
A.3
) which yields for every
u\in\mathcal{H}^{2}_{+}
:

\left(\mathcal{M}_{+}u,u\right)_{\mathcal{H}}=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(|u^{\prime}(x)|^{2}+4|u(x)|^{2}\right)dx.

If
u\in\mathcal{H}^{2}_{+}
is a solution of
\mathcal{M}_{+}u=0
, then
u=0
in
\mathcal{H}
and
{\rm Ker}(\mathcal{M}_{+})
is trivial in
\mathcal{H}
.

For surjectivity, let
f\in\mathcal{H}
and consider the resolvent equation for
\mathcal{M}_{+}
in the weak form:

(4.12)

\left(u,\phi\right)_{\mathcal{H}^{1}_{+}}=\left(f,\phi\right)_{\mathcal{H}},\quad\forall\phi\in\mathcal{H}^{1}_{+},

where we recall that
\mathcal{H}^{1}_{+}\equiv H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
and use the inner product

\left(u,\phi\right)_{\mathcal{H}^{1}_{+}}:=4\langle u,v\rangle_{L^{2}}+\langle u^{\prime},\phi^{\prime}\rangle_{L^{2}},\quad\forall u,\phi\in\mathcal{H}^{1}_{+},

together with the induced norm
\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{H}^{1}_{+}}
. By the Riesz Representation Theorem and the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, there exists a unique solution
u\in\mathcal{H}^{1}_{+}
of (
4.12
) such that
\|u\|_{\mathcal{H}^{1}_{+}}\leq\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}}
. This yields

\langle u^{\prime},\phi^{\prime}\rangle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}({\rm sech}^{2}(\cdot)\bar{f}-4\bar{u})\phi dx,\quad\forall\phi\in\mathcal{H}^{1}_{+}.

In particular, this holds for
\phi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R})

implying
-u^{\prime\prime}={\rm sech}^{2}(\cdot)f-4u\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})

and
\cosh(\cdot)(-u^{\prime\prime}+4u)={\rm sech}(\cdot)f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
so that

u\in\mathcal{H}^{2}_{+}
is a strong solution of the resolvent equation

\mathcal{M}_{+}u=f
and this holds for every
f\in\mathcal{H}
.

The bound on the inverse operator
\mathcal{M}_{+}^{-1}
from
\mathcal{H}
to
\mathcal{H}^{2}_{+}
follows from

\|\cosh(\cdot)(-u^{\prime\prime}+4u)\|_{L^{2}}=\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}}

so that the definition (
4.11
) implies that

\|\mathcal{M}_{+}^{-1}f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{2}_{-}}\leq\sqrt{2}\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}}
holds for every
f\in\mathcal{H}
.

The symmetry of
\mathcal{M}_{+}:\mathcal{H}^{2}_{+}\subset\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H}
follows from the integration by parts
formula (Lemma
A.3
).
∎

Lemma 4.4
.

The embedding
\mathcal{H}^{2}_{+}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{H}
is compact.

Proof.

Since the mapping
H^{1}(\mathbb{R})\ni v\mapsto{\rm sech}(\cdot)v\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
is compact and
\mathcal{H}^{1}_{+}\equiv H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
,
the embedding
\mathcal{H}^{1}_{+}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{H}
is compact and so is the embedding
\mathcal{H}^{2}_{+}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{H}

since
\mathcal{H}^{2}_{+}\subset\mathcal{H}^{1}_{+}
with continuous embedding.
∎

Combining the inverse operator
\mathcal{M}_{+}^{-1}:\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H}^{2}_{+}
in Lemma
4.3
and
the compact embedding
\mathcal{H}^{2}_{+}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{H}

in Lemma
4.4
, we can define a compact
operator

(4.13)

\mathcal{K}_{+}=\mathcal{M}_{+}^{-1}:\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H}^{2}_{+}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{H}.

The following corollary
gives the desired result for the proof of Theorem
1
.

Corollary 4.2
.

The spectrum of
\mathcal{L}_{+}
in
\mathcal{H}
consists of isolated eigenvalues.

Proof.

The compact operator
\mathcal{K}_{+}
has a purely discrete spectrum of
eigenvalues accumulating to
0
. Hence the linear operator

\mathcal{M}_{+}:\mathcal{H}^{2}_{+}\subset\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H}
has a purely discrete spectrum of eigenvalues. Since
\mathcal{L}_{+}=\mathcal{M}_{+}-6
, the spectrum of
\mathcal{L}_{+}
in
\mathcal{H}
consists of isolated eigenvalues.
∎

.

4.3.
Spectrum of
\mathcal{L}
in
\mathcal{H}\times\mathcal{H}

The following lemma characterizes the quadruple zero eigenvalue of the stability problem (
3.16
).

Lemma 4.5
.

The zero eigenvalue
\lambda=0
of the stability problem (
3.16
) has double geometric multiplicity and quadruple algebraic multiplicity.

Proof.

Recall the two eigenvectors (
3.20
) of the stability problem (
3.16
) for
\lambda=0
.
Since the linearized operator
\mathcal{L}
in (
4.1
) is anti-diagonal, eigenvectors for
\lambda=0

are given by solutions of
\mathcal{L}_{+}u=0
and
\mathcal{L}_{-}v=0
in
\mathcal{H}
. Hence the two eigenvectors (
3.20
) are the only solutions
of these equations in
\mathcal{H}
(Remarks
4.2
and
4.4
) so that
\lambda=0
is an eigenvalue of geometric multiplicity
two
.

Generalized eigenvectors of the stability problem (
3.16
)
for
\lambda=0
can be written in the form

(4.14)

\vec{w}_{g1}=\left[\begin{array}[]{c}0\\
v_{\varphi}\end{array}\right],\quad\vec{w}_{g2}=\left[\begin{array}[]{c}u_{\varphi}\\
0\end{array}\right],\qquad\begin{array}[]{c}\mathcal{L}_{-}v_{\varphi}=\varphi^{\prime},\\
-\mathcal{L}_{+}u_{\varphi}=\varphi.\end{array}

Since
\varphi(x)=\tanh(x)
, the inhomogeneous equations
(
4.14
) can be solved in
\mathcal{H}
exactly as

(4.15)

v_{\varphi}(x)=\frac{1}{4}{\rm sech}^{2}(x)-\frac{1}{2},\quad u_{\varphi}(x)=-\frac{1}{4}x\;{\rm sech}^{2}(x),

where the homogeneous solutions are set to zero without loss of generality. In order to prove that
\lambda=0
is an eigenvalue of algebraic multiplicity
four
, we need to show that the inhomogeneous linear equations

(4.16)

\begin{array}[]{c}-\mathcal{L}_{+}\tilde{u}=v_{\varphi},\\
\mathcal{L}_{-}\tilde{v}=u_{\varphi},\end{array}

do not admit solutions in
\mathcal{H}
. Since
(\mathcal{L}_{+}\tilde{u},\varphi^{\prime})_{\mathcal{H}}=0
for every
\tilde{u}\in\mathcal{H}^{2}_{+}
due to integration by parts (Lemma
A.3
) and
\mathcal{L}_{+}\varphi^{\prime}=0
, it follows that

\tilde{u}\in\mathcal{H}^{2}_{+}\subset\mathcal{H}
in (
4.16
) exists if and only if
(v_{\varphi},\varphi^{\prime})_{\mathcal{H}}=0
.
However, this is a contradiction since
(v_{\varphi},\varphi^{\prime})_{\mathcal{H}}<0

because
v_{\varphi}(x)<0
and
\varphi^{\prime}(x)>0
for every
x\in\mathbb{R}
. One can compute exactly that
(v_{\varphi},\varphi^{\prime})_{\mathcal{H}}=-\frac{2}{5}
.

Since
(\mathcal{L}_{-}\tilde{v},\varphi)_{\mathcal{H}}=0
for every
\tilde{v}\in\mathcal{H}^{2}_{-}
due to integration by parts (Lemma
A.2
) and
\mathcal{L}_{-}\varphi=0
, it follows that
\tilde{v}\in\mathcal{H}^{2}_{-}\subset\mathcal{H}
in (
4.16
) exists if and only if

(u_{\varphi},\varphi)_{\mathcal{H}}=0
.
However, this is again a contradiction since
(u_{\varphi},\varphi)_{\mathcal{H}}<0

because
u_{\varphi}(x)<0
and
\varphi(x)>0
for every
x>0
and
u_{\varphi}
,
\varphi
are odd functions. One can compute exactly that
(u_{\varphi},\varphi)_{\mathcal{H}}=-\frac{1}{12}
.

Thus, the Jordan chain of generalized eigenvectors of the stability problem (
3.16
) is truncated at the two generalized eigenvectors (
4.14
) so that
\lambda=0
is eigenvalue of algebraic multiplicity
four
.
∎

We will further prove that the spectrum of the stability problem (
3.11
) is purely discrete and consists of eigenvalues
with the eigenfunctions
(u,v)\in\mathcal{H}^{2}_{+}\times\mathcal{H}^{2}_{-}
. To do so, we rewrite the resolvent equation in the form

(4.17)

\begin{array}[]{c}\mathcal{L}_{-}v=\lambda u+f,\\
-\mathcal{L}_{+}u=\lambda v+g,\end{array}

for a given
(f,g)\in\mathcal{H}\times\mathcal{H}
. Since the quadruple eigenvalue
\lambda=0
is prescribed by Lemma
4.5
, the resolvent equation (
4.17
) is considered for
\lambda\neq 0
.

By using the compact operators
\mathcal{K}_{\pm}
given by (
4.7
) and (
4.13
), we rewrite the resolvent equation (
4.17
) in the equivalent form

(4.18)

\begin{array}[]{c}(I-3\mathcal{K}_{-})v=\lambda\mathcal{K}_{-}u+\mathcal{K}_{-}f,\\
-(I-6\mathcal{K}_{+})u=\lambda\mathcal{K}_{+}v+\mathcal{K}_{+}g,\end{array},

where
I
is the identity in
\mathcal{H}
. Before progressing further, we recall the generalized kernel of the stability problem in Lemma
4.5
from solutions of the linear equations

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{+}\varphi^{\prime}=0,\quad\mathcal{L}_{-}\varphi=0,\quad\mathcal{L}_{+}u_{\varphi}=-\varphi,\quad\mathcal{L}_{-}v_{\varphi}=\varphi^{\prime},

which we can reproduce in the equivalent form

\displaystyle(I-6\mathcal{K}_{+})\varphi^{\prime}=0,\quad(I-3\mathcal{K}_{-})\varphi=0,\quad(I-6\mathcal{K}_{+})u_{\varphi}=-\mathcal{K}_{+}\varphi,\quad(I-3\mathcal{K}_{-})v_{\varphi}=\mathcal{K}_{-}\varphi^{\prime}.

The following lemma will ensure that projections to the eigenfunctions of the generalized kernel are non-degenerate.

Lemma 4.6
.

The following two matrices are invertible:

(4.19)

A_{+}:=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}(\varphi^{\prime},\mathcal{K}_{+}\varphi)_{\mathcal{H}}&(\varphi^{\prime},\mathcal{K}_{+}v_{\varphi})_{\mathcal{H}}\\
(u_{\varphi},\mathcal{K}_{+}\varphi)_{\mathcal{H}}&(u_{\varphi},\mathcal{K}_{+}v_{\varphi})_{\mathcal{H}}\end{array}\right]

and

(4.20)

A_{-}:=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}(\varphi,\mathcal{K}_{-}\varphi^{\prime})_{\mathcal{H}}&(\varphi,\mathcal{K}_{-}u_{\varphi})_{\mathcal{H}}\\
(v_{\varphi},\mathcal{K}_{-}\varphi^{\prime})_{\mathcal{H}}&(v_{\varphi},\mathcal{K}_{-}u_{\varphi})_{\mathcal{H}}\end{array}\right]

Proof.

The first diagonal entries of
A_{\pm}
are zero because

(\varphi^{\prime},\mathcal{K}_{+}\varphi)_{\mathcal{H}}=(\mathcal{K}_{+}\varphi^{\prime},\varphi)_{\mathcal{H}}=\frac{1}{6}(\varphi^{\prime},\varphi)_{\mathcal{H}}=0

and

(\varphi,\mathcal{K}_{-}\varphi^{\prime})_{\mathcal{H}}=(\mathcal{K}_{-}\varphi,\varphi^{\prime})_{\mathcal{H}}=\frac{1}{3}(\varphi,\varphi^{\prime})_{\mathcal{H}}=0

We will show that the off-diagonal entries of
A_{\pm}
are all non-zero.
This will ensure invertibility of
A_{\pm}
irrespectively
of the last diagonal entries of
A_{\pm}
. For two off-diagonal entries,
we obtain

(\varphi^{\prime},\mathcal{K}_{+}v_{\varphi})_{\mathcal{H}}=(\mathcal{K}_{+}\varphi^{\prime},v_{\varphi})_{\mathcal{H}}=\frac{1}{6}(\varphi^{\prime},v_{\varphi})_{\mathcal{H}}<0

and

(\varphi,\mathcal{K}_{-}u_{\varphi})_{\mathcal{H}}=(\mathcal{K}_{-}\varphi,u_{\varphi})_{\mathcal{H}}=\frac{1}{3}(\varphi,u_{\varphi})_{\mathcal{H}}<0,

where the signs of the last terms are computed in the proof
of Lemma
4.5
. For the other two off-diagonal entries,
we claim the following explicit expressions

(4.21)

\displaystyle(\mathcal{K}_{-}\varphi^{\prime})(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{4}{7}+\frac{1}{7}{\rm sech}^{2}(x),

(4.22)

\displaystyle(\mathcal{K}_{+}\varphi)(x)

\displaystyle=x\cosh(2x)-\sinh(2x)\log(2\cosh(x))+\frac{1}{2}\tanh(x).

The expression (
4.21
) is obtained by solving the inhomogeneous equation

(-\partial_{x}^{2}+{\rm sech}^{2}(x))(\mathcal{K}_{-}\varphi^{\prime})=(-\partial_{x}^{2}-2{\rm sech}^{2}(x))v_{\varphi}={\rm sech}^{4}(x).

from which we obtain
(v_{\varphi},\mathcal{K}_{-}\varphi^{\prime})_{\mathcal{H}}<0
since
v_{\varphi}(x)<0
and
(\mathcal{K}_{-}\varphi^{\prime})(x)>0
for every
x\in\mathbb{R}
.

The expression (
4.22
) is obtained by solving the inhomogeneous equation

(-\partial_{x}^{2}+4)(\mathcal{K}_{+}\varphi)=-(-\partial_{x}^{2}+4-6{\rm sech}^{2}(x))u_{\varphi}=\tanh(x){\rm sech}^{2}(x)

with the homogeneous solution chosen to satisfy the requirement
that
\mathcal{K}_{+}\varphi\in\mathcal{H}
. To show this, we can rewrite the right-hand-side of (
4.22
) for
x>0
as

(4.23)

xe^{-2x}-\frac{1}{2}g(x)+\frac{1}{2}(g(x)+1)e^{-4x}-\frac{e^{-2x}}{1+e^{-2x}},

where

g(x):=e^{2x}\left[\log(2\cosh(x))-x\right]-1=e^{2x}\log(1+e^{-2x})-1.

Taylor expansion shows that
g(x)=-\frac{1}{2}e^{-2x}+\mathcal{O}(e^{-4x})
as
x\to+\infty
so that the explicit expression (
4.23
) decays to
0
as
x\to+\infty
exponentially fast. Since it is odd in
x
,
it belongs to
H^{1}(\mathbb{R})\subset\mathcal{H}

so that it coincides with
(\mathcal{K}_{+}\phi)
. Moreover, since
(\mathcal{K}_{+}\varphi)(x)\to 0
as
x\to+\infty

and

\frac{d}{dx}\frac{(\mathcal{K}_{+}\varphi)(x)}{\sinh(2x)}=\frac{\tanh(x)-x}{2\sinh^{2}(x)\cosh^{2}(x)}<0,\quad x>0,

we have
(\mathcal{K}_{+}\varphi)(x)>0
for
x>0
, from which we obtain

(u_{\varphi},\mathcal{K}_{+}\varphi)_{\mathcal{H}}<0

since
u_{\varphi}(x)<0
for
x>0
and both functions are odd. Thus, all four off-diagonal terms in
A_{\pm}
are nonzero so that
A_{\pm}
are invertible.
∎

Let us now define the following two subspaces of
\mathcal{H}
:

\mathcal{U}:={\rm span}(\varphi^{\prime},u_{\varphi})^{\perp},\quad\mathcal{V}:={\rm span}(\varphi,v_{\varphi})^{\perp},

and denote the orthogonal projection operators
\Pi_{\mathcal{U}}:\mathcal{H}\mapsto\mathcal{U}
and
\Pi_{\mathcal{V}}:\mathcal{H}\mapsto\mathcal{V}
.

The following lemma describes decompositions of
\mathcal{H}
into two direct sums, which are generally non-orthogonal.

Lemma 4.7
.

\mathcal{H}
can be decomposed into the following direct sums

\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{U}\oplus{\rm span}(\mathcal{K}_{+}\varphi,\mathcal{K}_{+}v_{\varphi})=\mathcal{V}\oplus{\rm span}(\mathcal{K}_{-}\varphi^{\prime},\mathcal{K}_{-}u_{\varphi}).

Proof.

First we show that

\xi_{1}:=\mathcal{K}_{+}\varphi-\Pi_{\mathcal{U}}(\mathcal{K}_{+}\varphi),\qquad\xi_{2}:=\mathcal{K}_{+}v_{\varphi}-\Pi_{\mathcal{U}}(\mathcal{K}_{+}v_{\varphi})

are linearly independent. Letting
(c_{1},c_{2})\in{\mathbb{C}}^{2}
such that
c_{1}\xi_{1}+c_{2}\xi_{2}=0
implies

c_{1}\mathcal{K}_{+}\varphi+c_{2}\mathcal{K}_{+}v_{\varphi}\in\mathcal{U}.

Taking inner products in
\mathcal{H}
with
\varphi^{\prime}
and
u_{\varphi}
yields
the linear homogeneous system

A_{+}\left[\begin{array}[]{c}c_{1}\\
c_{2}\end{array}\right]=\left[\begin{array}[]{c}0\\
0\end{array}\right],

which has no nontrivial solutions by Lemma
4.6
. Hence,
c_{1}=c_{2}=0
. Thus,
\{\xi_{1},\xi_{2}\}
is a basis of

\mathcal{U}^{\perp}:={\rm span}(\varphi^{\prime},u_{\varphi})
so that every
f\in\mathcal{H}
can be written as

f=u+\alpha\xi_{1}+\beta\xi_{2}=\tilde{u}+\alpha\mathcal{K}_{+}\varphi+\beta\mathcal{K}_{+}v_{\varphi},

with uniquely determined
u\in\mathcal{U}
and
(\alpha,\beta)\in{\mathbb{C}}^{2}
,
and with
\tilde{u}:=u-\Pi_{\mathcal{U}}(\alpha\mathcal{K}_{+}\varphi+\beta\mathcal{K}_{+}v_{\varphi})
.

The decomposition of
\mathcal{H}

into
\mathcal{V}\oplus{\rm span}(\mathcal{K}_{-}\varphi^{\prime},\mathcal{K}_{-}u_{\varphi})
follows analogously but with using invertibility of
A_{-}
in Lemma
4.6
.
∎

With the help of Lemma
4.7
, we define the projection operator
P_{\mathcal{U}}
from
\mathcal{H}
to
\mathcal{U}
along
{\rm span}(\mathcal{K}_{+}\varphi,\mathcal{K}_{+}v_{\varphi})
as

P_{\mathcal{U}}(u+\alpha\mathcal{K}_{+}\varphi+\beta\mathcal{K}_{+}v_{\varphi})=u,\quad\forall u\in\mathcal{U},\quad\forall(\alpha,\beta)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}.

Similarly, the projection operator
P_{\mathcal{V}}
from
\mathcal{H}
to
\mathcal{V}
along
{\rm span}(\mathcal{K}_{-}\varphi^{\prime},\mathcal{K}_{-}u_{\varphi})
is defined as

P_{\mathcal{V}}(v+\alpha\mathcal{K}_{-}\varphi^{\prime}+\beta\mathcal{K}_{-}u_{\varphi})=v,\quad\forall v\in\mathcal{V},\quad\forall(\alpha,\beta)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}.

The following lemma gives invertibility
of the linear operators in the resolvent equation (
4.18
)
after appropriate projection operators.

Lemma 4.8
.

The following two operators

\mathcal{S}_{+}:=P_{\mathcal{U}}(I-6\mathcal{K}_{+})|_{\mathcal{U}}:\mathcal{U}\mapsto\mathcal{U}

and

\mathcal{S}_{-}:=P_{\mathcal{V}}(I-3\mathcal{K}_{-})|_{\mathcal{V}}:\mathcal{V}\mapsto\mathcal{V}

are invertible with bounded inverse.

Proof.

Since
\mathcal{K}_{+}
and
\mathcal{K}_{-}
are compact,
the operators
\mathcal{S}_{+}
and
\mathcal{S}_{-}
are compact perturbations of the identity operators in
\mathcal{U}
and
\mathcal{V}
. Therefore, the assertion follows from Fredholm’s alternative theorem, once we have proven injectivity of
\mathcal{S}_{+}
and
\mathcal{S}_{-}
.

To prove injectivity of
\mathcal{S}_{+}
, we let
u\in\mathcal{U}
satisfy
\mathcal{S}_{+}u=0
which implies

(I-6\mathcal{K}_{+})u=\alpha\mathcal{K}_{+}\varphi+\beta\mathcal{K}_{+}v_{\varphi},

for some
(\alpha,\beta)\in{\mathbb{C}}^{2}
. Taking the inner product in
\mathcal{H}
with
\varphi^{\prime}
yields

0=\alpha(\varphi^{\prime},\mathcal{K}_{+}\varphi)_{\mathcal{H}}+\beta(\varphi^{\prime},\mathcal{K}_{+}v_{\varphi})_{\mathcal{H}}.

Since
(\varphi^{\prime},\mathcal{K}_{+}\varphi)_{\mathcal{H}}=0

and
(\varphi^{\prime},\mathcal{K}_{+}v_{\varphi})_{\mathcal{H}}\neq 0
by Lemma
4.6
, we have
\beta=0
. With the help of
(I-6\mathcal{K}_{+})u_{\varphi}=-\mathcal{K}_{+}\varphi
, we obtain

u=-\alpha u_{\varphi}+\gamma\varphi^{\prime},

for some
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
, but since
u\in\mathcal{U}
, we obtain
\alpha=\gamma=0
so that
u=0
and
\mathcal{S}_{+}
is injective.

Injectivity of
\mathcal{S}_{-}
follows analogously.
∎

With the preliminary results in Lemmas
4.6
,
4.7
, and
4.8
, we now obtain
the desired result in the following theorem.

Theorem 5
.

The spectrum of the linear stability problem (
3.11
) consists only of isolated eigenvalues of finite multiplicity
and admits no finite accumulation points.

Proof.

By Lemma
4.6
,

\{\mathcal{K}_{+}\varphi,\mathcal{K}_{+}v_{\varphi}\}
is linearly independent of
\{\varphi^{\prime},u_{\varphi}\}
so that

{\rm Null}(P_{\mathcal{U}})\cap{\rm Null}(I-\Pi_{\mathcal{U}})={\rm Null}(P_{\mathcal{V}})\cap{\rm Null}(I-\Pi_{\mathcal{V}})=\{0\}.

As a result, the resolvent problem (
4.18
) is identical to the following two systems:

(4.24)

\begin{array}[]{l}P_{\mathcal{V}}(I-3\mathcal{K}_{-})v=P_{\mathcal{V}}(\lambda\mathcal{K}_{-}u+\mathcal{K}_{-}f),\\
-P_{\mathcal{U}}(I-6\mathcal{K}_{+})u=P_{\mathcal{U}}(\lambda\mathcal{K}_{+}v+\mathcal{K}_{+}g)\end{array}

and

(4.25)

\begin{array}[]{l}(I-\Pi_{\mathcal{V}})(I-3\mathcal{K}_{-})v=(I-\Pi_{\mathcal{V}})(\lambda\mathcal{K}_{-}u+\mathcal{K}_{-}f),\\
-(I-\Pi_{\mathcal{U}})(I-6\mathcal{K}_{+})u=(I-\Pi_{\mathcal{U}})(\lambda\mathcal{K}_{+}v+\mathcal{K}_{+}g)\end{array}

Since
\mathcal{H}
is given by the orthogonal sums
\mathcal{U}\oplus\mathcal{U}^{\perp}
and
\mathcal{V}\oplus\mathcal{V}^{\perp}
, we decompose

(4.26)

u=\tilde{u}+\alpha\varphi^{\prime}+\beta u_{\varphi},\quad v=\tilde{v}+\gamma\varphi+\delta v_{\varphi},

where
\tilde{u}\in\mathcal{U}
,
\tilde{v}\in\mathcal{V}
, and

\alpha,\beta,\gamma,\delta\in{\mathbb{C}}
. Since

\begin{array}[]{l}P_{\mathcal{U}}(I-6\mathcal{K}_{+})\varphi^{\prime}=0,\quad P_{\mathcal{U}}(I-6\mathcal{K}_{+})u_{\varphi}=0,\quad P_{\mathcal{U}}(\mathcal{K}_{+}\varphi)=0,\quad P_{\mathcal{U}}(\mathcal{K}_{+}v_{\varphi})=0,\\
P_{\mathcal{V}}(I-3\mathcal{K}_{-})\varphi=0,\quad P_{\mathcal{V}}(I-3\mathcal{K}_{-})v_{\varphi}=0,\quad P_{\mathcal{V}}(\mathcal{K}_{-}\varphi^{\prime})=0,\quad P_{\mathcal{V}}(\mathcal{K}_{-}u_{\varphi})=0,\end{array}

the first system (
4.24
) with the decomposition (
4.26
) is equivalent to

\begin{array}[]{l}P_{\mathcal{V}}(I-3\mathcal{K}_{-})\tilde{v}=P_{\mathcal{V}}(\lambda\mathcal{K}_{-}\tilde{u}+\mathcal{K}_{-}f),\\
-P_{\mathcal{U}}(I-6\mathcal{K}_{+})\tilde{u}=P_{\mathcal{U}}(\lambda\mathcal{K}_{+}\tilde{v}+\mathcal{K}_{+}g).\end{array}

By Lemma
4.8
, the linear operators in the left-hand-side can be inverted so that the system can be rewritten in the equivalent form:

\left[\begin{array}[]{l}\tilde{u}\\
\tilde{v}\end{array}\right]=\lambda\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}0&-\mathcal{S}_{+}^{-1}P_{\mathcal{U}}\mathcal{K}_{+}|_{\mathcal{V}}\\
\mathcal{S}_{-}^{-1}P_{\mathcal{V}}\mathcal{K}_{-}|_{\mathcal{U}}&0\end{array}\right]\left[\begin{array}[]{l}\tilde{u}\\
\tilde{v}\end{array}\right]+\left[\begin{array}[]{c}-\mathcal{S}_{+}^{-1}P_{\mathcal{U}}\mathcal{K}_{+}g\\
\mathcal{S}_{-}^{-1}P_{\mathcal{V}}\mathcal{K}_{-}f\end{array}\right].

Since the matrix operator in the right-hand side is a compact operator
from
\mathcal{U}\times\mathcal{V}
to itself, Fredholm’s alternative theorem
tells us that either the inhomogeneous system has a unique solution
(\tilde{u},\tilde{v})\in\mathcal{U}\times\mathcal{V}
for every
(f,g)\in\mathcal{H}\times\mathcal{H}
or
\lambda
is an isolated eigenvalue of finite multiplicity of the homogeneous system with
f=g=0
. Moreover, isolated
eigenvalues have no finite accumulation points.

Let us show that the Fredholm’s alternative carries over to the original system (
4.18
). To do so, we analyze the second system (
4.25
) with the decomposition (
4.26
). Since

(\varphi^{\prime},(I-6\mathcal{K}_{+})u)_{\mathcal{H}}=0,\quad(\varphi,(I-3\mathcal{K}_{-})v)_{\mathcal{H}}=0,

and

Proof of Theorem

5.
Existence of traveling dark solitons

Here we study the construction of traveling dark solitons for arbitrary wave speed
c
and in the limit
c\to 0
and thus provide the proof of Theorem
2
.

If
U_{c}
is a solution
of the differential equation (
1.10
), then
\bar{U}_{c}
is
the solution of the same equation for
-c
. Therefore, it is sufficient to
prove the existence result for
c>0
. The following
lemma yields the existence of traveling dark solitons.

Lemma 5.1
.

For every
c\in(0,\infty)
, there exists the dark soliton with the profile
U_{c}\in\mathcal{F}\cap C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
satisfying (
1.10
) and (
1.11
) such that the mapping
c\mapsto U_{c}
is
C^{\infty}
on
(0,\infty)
.

Proof.

By using the polar form
U_{c}=\sqrt{\rho_{c}}e^{i\phi_{c}}
, we rewrite the differential equation (
1.10
) as the following system:

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}(\rho_{c}\phi_{c}^{\prime})^{\prime}-c(1-\rho_{c})\rho_{c}^{\prime}=0,\\
2\rho_{c}\rho_{c}^{\prime\prime}-(\rho_{c}^{\prime})^{2}+8(1-\rho_{c})\rho_{c}^{2}+8c(1-\rho_{c})\rho_{c}^{2}\phi_{c}^{\prime}-4\rho_{c}^{2}(\phi_{c}^{\prime})^{2}=0.\end{array}\right.

The first equation in system (
5
) can be integrated under the boundary conditions (
1.11
)
to the form

(5.3)

\phi_{c}^{\prime}=-\frac{c}{2\rho_{c}}(1-\rho_{c})^{2},

so that
\lim\limits_{|\xi|\to\infty}\phi_{c}^{\prime}(\xi)=0
if
\lim\limits_{|\xi|\to\infty}\rho_{c}(\xi)=1
.

Substituting (
5.3
) to the second equation in system (
5
)
and using the transformation
\rho_{c}=q_{c}^{2}
, we obtain the second-order differential equation

q_{c}^{\prime\prime}+2(1-q_{c}^{2})q_{c}-\frac{c^{2}}{q_{c}}(1-q_{c}^{2})^{3}-\frac{c^{2}}{4q_{c}^{3}}(1-q_{c}^{2})^{4}=0,

which can be integrated under the boundary conditions (
1.11
) to the form

(q_{c}^{\prime})^{2}-(1-q_{c}^{2})^{2}+\frac{c^{2}}{4q_{c}^{2}}(1-q_{c}^{2})^{4}=0.

Unfolding the transformation
\rho_{c}=q_{c}^{2}
yields

(5.4)

(\rho_{c}^{\prime})^{2}=(1-\rho_{c})^{2}\left[4\rho_{c}-c^{2}(1-\rho_{c})^{2}\right],

which is the first-order invariant of the second-order equation

(5.5)

\rho_{c}^{\prime\prime}+2(1-\rho_{c})(3\rho_{c}-1-c^{2}(1-\rho_{c})^{2})=0,

with the unique choice of the integration constant due to the boundary conditions (
1.11
).

The critical point
(\rho,\rho^{\prime})=(1,0)
is a saddle point
of the second-order equation (
5.5
)
with two homoclinic orbits located in
[\rho_{-}(c),1]\times\mathbb{R}
and
[1,\rho_{+}(c)]\times\mathbb{R}
, where
\rho_{\pm}(c)
are turning points of the homoclinic orbits. The turning points correspond to the values of
\xi_{0}
for which
\rho^{\prime}(\xi_{0})=0
and hence are obtained from the roots of the quadratic equation
4\rho=c^{2}(1-\rho)^{2}
that follows from (
5.4
). The turning points are given explicitly by

\rho_{\pm}(c)=\frac{c^{2}+2\pm 2\sqrt{1+c^{2}}}{c^{2}}

and they exist for every
c>0
. Moreover,

\rho_{-}(c)=\frac{1}{4}c^{2}+\mathcal{O}(c^{4}),\quad\rho_{+}(c)=\frac{4}{c^{2}}+\mathcal{O}(1),\quad\mbox{\rm as}\;\;c\to 0.

There exists exactly one (up to translation) solution
\rho_{c}\in[\rho_{-}(c),1]
of the first-order invariant (
5.4
) in the set
\mathcal{F}
. For every
c\in(0,\infty)
, the solution satisfies

\rho_{c}(\xi)\geq\rho_{-}(c)>0,\quad\forall\xi\in\mathbb{R}

so that
U_{c}\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
in the polar form.
Moreover,
\rho_{c}
,
\phi_{c}
, and thus
U_{c}
are
C^{\infty}

with respect to
c
on
(0,\infty)
.
∎

Remark 5.1
.

Another solution of the first-order invariant (
5.4
) exists for
\rho\in[1,\rho_{+}(c)]
. This solution is not used for the scopes of this work.

Remark 5.2
.

Linearization of the second-order equation (
5.5
) at
(\rho,\rho^{\prime})=(1,0)
shows that the saddle point is associated with the same exponential rate
\pm 2
independently of the speed parameter

. Consequently, up to translation in

,

satisfies

\rho_{c}(\xi)=1+A_{c}e^{-2|\xi|}+{\rm o}(e^{-2|\xi|})
as

with a unique choice for

.

Remark 5.3
.

The
C^{\infty}
smoothness of
U_{c}
at
c=0
cannot be concluded from the polar form. Indeed, we have
\rho_{c}=\rho_{0}+\mathcal{O}_{H^{2}}(c^{2})
, see the proof of Theorem
6
, where

\rho_{0}(\xi)=\tanh^{2}(\xi)
is the suitable solution of
(\rho_{0}^{\prime})^{2}=4\rho_{0}(1-\rho_{0})^{2}

\rho_{0}(0)=0
, the
phase factor
\phi_{c}(\xi)
is singular at
\xi=0
. If we use the formal expansion

\phi_{c}^{\prime}=c\phi_{1}^{\prime}+\mathcal{O}(c^{3})
, then

\phi_{1}^{\prime}=-\frac{(1-\rho_{0})^{2}}{2\rho_{0}}

so that
\phi_{1}(\xi)=\coth(2\xi)
is singular at

. The polar form

gives formally

U_{c}(\xi)=\tanh(\xi)+\frac{ic\cosh(2\xi)}{2\cosh^{2}(\xi)}+\mathcal{O}(c^{2}),

where the second term coincides with the explicit expression
for
-2icv_{\varphi}
, see (
4.15
).
This formal expansion cannot be justified from the polar form because of the singularity at
\xi=0
.

The following theorem justifies the expansion of
U_{c}
as
c\to 0

by using the real and imaginary coordinates for
U_{c}=u_{c}+iv_{c}
in addition
to the variable
\rho_{c}=|U_{c}|^{2}

Theorem 6
.

There exists
c_{0}>0
and
A_{0}>0
such that for every

(5.6)

\|U_{c}-\varphi+2icv_{\varphi}\|_{\mathcal{H}^{2}_{-}}\leq A_{0}c^{2},

Proof.

Let us substitute

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}u_{c}^{\prime\prime}+2(1-u_{c}^{2}-v_{c}^{2})u_{c}+2c(1-u_{c}^{2}-v_{c}^{2})v_{c}^{\prime}=0,\\

The relevant analysis is obtained in three steps.

Step 1.

Lemma 6.1

7.
Persistence and stability of black solitons in potentials

Here we consider a small and decaying potential in the framework of the perturbed NLS model (
1.15
) and prove persistence and stability of the black soliton. This yields the proof of Theorem
4
. The following two lemmas give the persistence and stability results separately.

The standing wave solution
\psi(t,x)=e^{-2it}\phi_{\varepsilon}(x)
of the perturbed NLS model (
1.15
) are found from the second-order differential equation

(7.1)

\phi_{\varepsilon}^{\prime\prime}+2(1-|\phi_{\varepsilon}|^{2})\phi_{\varepsilon}=\varepsilon V(x)\phi_{\varepsilon}.

Since
V(x):\mathbb{R}\mapsto\mathbb{R}
, we consider real solutions
\phi_{\varepsilon}(x):\mathbb{R}\mapsto\mathbb{R}
which converge to
\varphi
pointwise in
x
as
|\varepsilon|\to 0
, where
\varphi(x):=\tanh(x)
. The following lemma uses the Lyapunov–Schmidt reduction method to get the existence result.

Lemma 7.1
.

Assume that
V\in W^{2,\infty}(\mathbb{R})\cap L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
and that
s\in\mathbb{R}
is a simple root of
\mathcal{V}^{\prime}
, where
\mathcal{V}
is given by (
1.16
). There exists
\varepsilon_{0}>0
such that for every
\varepsilon\in(-\varepsilon_{0},\varepsilon_{0})
, there exists a real solution of equation (
7.1
) in the form
\phi_{\varepsilon}=\varphi_{s}+\tilde{\varphi}_{\varepsilon}
,
where
\varphi_{s}(x):=\varphi(x-s)
and
\tilde{\varphi}_{\varepsilon}\in H^{2}(\mathbb{R})
satisfies
\|\tilde{\varphi}_{\varepsilon}\|_{H^{2}}\leq C|\varepsilon|
for some
\varepsilon
-independent positive constant
C
.

Proof.

We use the decomposition

(7.2)

\phi_{\varepsilon}=\varphi_{s+a}+\psi,

where
s\in\mathbb{R}
is a simple root of
\mathcal{V}^{\prime}(s)
,

is a parameter to be determined, and
\psi\in H^{2}(\mathbb{R})
is a correction term to be determined. The decomposition (
7.2
) allows us to rewrite (
7.1
) in the equivalent form

(7.3)

L_{+}\psi+\varepsilon V\left[\varphi_{s+a}+\psi\right]+N(\psi)=0,

where
L_{+}:=-\partial_{x}^{2}+6\varphi^{2}_{s+a}-2
is the translated version of the linearized operator
L_{+}
in (
3.9
) and

is the nonlinear part of the cubic nonlinearity.

The linearized operator
L_{+}
can be considered in

, where
0
is a simple isolated eigenvalue of
L_{+}
at the bottom of
\sigma(L_{+})
with
{\rm Null}(L_{+})={\rm span}(\varphi^{\prime}_{s+a})
. The Lyapunov–Schmidt reduction method relies on the orthogonal decomposition by using the orthogonal projection

\Pi_{a}\psi:=\psi-\frac{\langle\varphi^{\prime}_{s+a},\psi\rangle}{\|\varphi^{\prime}_{s+a}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}}\varphi^{\prime}_{s+a},

where
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
is the inner product in

.
Assuming

(that is,
\langle\varphi^{\prime}_{s+a},\psi\rangle=0
) for uniqueness of definitions of

and
\psi\in H^{2}(\mathbb{R})
allows us to split (
7.3
) into two parts:

(7.4)

\displaystyle F(\varepsilon,a,\psi)

\displaystyle:=L_{+}\psi+\Pi_{a}\left(\varepsilon V\left[\varphi_{s+a}+\psi\right]+Z(\psi)\right)=0,

(7.5)

\displaystyle f(\varepsilon,a,\psi)

\displaystyle:=\langle\varphi^{\prime}_{s+a},\varepsilon V\left[\varphi_{s+a}+\psi\right]+Z(\psi)\rangle=0.

Since

•

F(\varepsilon,a,\psi):\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}\times H^{2}(\mathbb{R})|_{\{\varphi^{\prime}_{s+a}\}^{\perp}}\to L^{2}(\mathbb{R})|_{\{\varphi^{\prime}_{s+a}\}^{\perp}}
is
C^{\infty}
for every
V\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
,

•

F(0,0,0)=0
,

•

is an invertible operator with a bounded inverse from

to
H^{2}(\mathbb{R})|_{\{\varphi^{\prime}_{s+a}\}^{\perp}}
,

the implicit function theorem gives the existence of a unique
C^{\infty}
mapping
(\varepsilon,a)\mapsto\psi_{\varepsilon,a}\in H^{2}(\mathbb{R})|_{\{\varphi^{\prime}_{s+a}\}^{\perp}}
which solves
(
7.4
) for every small
(\varepsilon,a)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}
with the estimate

(7.6)

\|\psi_{\varepsilon,a}\|_{H^{2}}\leq C|\varepsilon|(1+|a|),

for some
(\varepsilon,a)
-independent constant
C>0
.

The first term of

can be simplified after integration by parts:

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}V(x)\varphi(x-s-a)\varphi^{\prime}(x-s-a)dx

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}V^{\prime}(x)\left[1-\varphi^{2}(x-s-a)\right]dx

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}V^{\prime}(x){\rm sech}^{2}(x-s-a)dx

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{V}^{\prime}(s+a).

Since
V\in W^{2,\infty}(\mathbb{R})
,
\mathcal{V}
is a
C^{2}
function of its argument. If
s\in\mathbb{R}
is a simple root of
\mathcal{V}^{\prime}
, then

\lim_{a\to 0}\frac{\mathcal{V}^{\prime}(s+a)-\mathcal{V}^{\prime\prime}(s)a}{a}=0.

Substituting the
C^{\infty}
mapping

(\varepsilon,a)\mapsto\psi_{\varepsilon,a}\in H^{2}(\mathbb{R})|_{\{\varphi^{\prime}_{s+a}\}^{\perp}}

from equation (
7.4
) into (

)
gives the implicit equation

(7.7)

\hat{f}(\varepsilon,a):=\varepsilon^{-1}f(\varepsilon,a,\psi_{\varepsilon,a})=0,

where

is

in its arguments,
\hat{f}(0,0)=0
, and

\partial_{a}\hat{f}(0,0)=\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{V}^{\prime\prime}(s)\neq 0.

The implicit function theorem gives the existence of a unique
C^{1}
mapping
\varepsilon\mapsto a_{\varepsilon}\in\mathbb{R}
which solves (

) for every small
\varepsilon\in\mathbb{R}
with the estimate

for some
\varepsilon

∎

) is positive. However, the numerator in (

) attains negative values since
\mathcal{L}_{-}(\varepsilon):=(1-\phi_{\varepsilon}^{2})^{-1}L_{-}(\varepsilon)
has a simple negative eigenvalue in
\mathcal{H}_{\varepsilon}
and
v\in\mathcal{H}_{\varepsilon}
does not satisfy any orthogonality condition. Therefore, the smallest eigenvalue
-\lambda_{0}^{2}
is negative so that there exists a pair of real eigenvalues
\{\lambda_{0},-\lambda_{0}\}
.

Remark 7.3
.

The method based on the Rayleigh quotient (
7.13
) is inconclusive if
\mathcal{V}^{\prime\prime}(s)>0
because both the numerator and the denominator attain negative values for some
v\in\mathcal{H}_{\varepsilon}
.

8.
Conclusion

This work can be summarized as follows. We have considered a new NLS model with intensity-dependent dispersion (
1.6
) as an alternative of the cubic defocusing NLS equation (
1.1
). We have shown that the same black soliton has better stability properties in the NLS model (
1.6
) in several aspects. The NLS model provides a natural Hilbert
L^{2}
space with exponential weights, where the linearized stability problem admits only isolated eigenvalues of finite multiplicity and no finite accumulation points. The energetic stability argument is obtained with four rather than three orthogonality conditions, where the additional constraint is due to the new scaling symmetry (
1.13
). The black soliton is continued to the family of traveling dark solitons for every speed compared to the finite speed cutoff in the cubic NLS equation (
1.1
).
Finally, the black soliton remains orbitally stable in the presence of a small decaying potential if it is pinned to the minimum point of the effective potential compared to either oscillatory or monotone instability of the black soliton of the cubic NLS equation with a small decaying potential.

We end this paper with a list of further open problems.

1.
We have not attempted to address local well-posedness
of the new NLS model (
1.6
) in
\Sigma\cap\mathcal{H}
,
where
\Sigma
is the energy space (
3.2
) and
\mathcal{H}
is the exponentially weighted
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
space (
3.12
).
We conjecture that the initial-value problem is locally well-posed
in the set of functions
\mathcal{F}
in (
1.7
) for which
|u(t,x)|\leq 1
for
(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}
, but this problem is a subject on its own.
Recent results on local well-posedness of such NLS models
with zero boundary conditions can be found in
[
18
]
.

2.
Another related problem is the asymptotic stability of
black solitons. By using ideas of
[
15
]
, the asymptotic stability
analysis can be developed provided that the local well-posedness problem
for the NLS model (
1.6
) is solved first.

3.
Orbital stability of the continuous family of dark solitons
with any speed
c\in\mathbb{R}
can be considered by using the
exponentially weighted Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{c,\omega=1}
in Remark
6.1
. According to the standard orbital
stability criterion
[
5
,
23
]
, the dark soliton with profile
U_{c}

is orbitally stable if the mapping
c\mapsto P(U_{c})
is monotonically increasing. It follows from the expansion (
5.22
) that the stability is satisfied for small
c\in(-c_{0},c_{0})
with some
c_{0}>0
.
However, since
U_{c}
is only available implicitly, the precise stability
criterion can only be checked numerically for larger values of
c\in\mathbb{R}

or verified with computer-assisted proofs
[
27
]
.

4.
It would be interesting to justify the new NLS model (
1.6
)
as a reduction of the Maxwell equations in the framework of either temporal or spatial nonlinear optics. Justification of the extended NLS models of the type

i(1-\partial_{x}^{2})\psi_{t}+\psi_{xx}+2(1-|\psi|^{2})\psi=0

was considered in
[
10
, Section 4.1.4]
in the framework of the NLS models with a full dispersion relation. However, the relevant analysis did not incorporate the intensity-dependent dispersion. Justification of the NLS model (
1.6
) is open for further studies.

5.
One can think of the regularization of the NLS model (
1.6
)
with bounded intensity-dependent dispersion, similar to the one used in

[
31
]
for the NLS model (
1.14
). Local well-posedness of the regularized model in the space of functions with nonzero boundary conditions at infinity can be shown by using the general analysis of
[
12
]
. It is expected that the stability properties
of the black solitons analyzed in our work will persist in the time evolution of the regularized version of the NLS model (
1.6
).

Appendix A
Integration by parts in
\mathcal{H}

We start with the following technical estimates.

Lemma A.1
.

For every
w\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
, it is true that

(A.1)

\displaystyle\left\|\cosh(\cdot)\int_{\cdot}^{\infty}{\rm sech}(t)w(t)dt\right\|_{L^{2}(0,\infty)}\leq 2\|w\|_{L^{2}(0,\infty)},

(A.2)

\displaystyle\left\|\cosh(\cdot)\int_{-\infty}^{\cdot}{\rm sech}(t)w(t)dt\right\|_{L^{2}(-\infty,0)}\leq 2\|w\|_{L^{2}(-\infty,0)},

and

(A.3)

\displaystyle\left\|{\rm sech}(\cdot)\int_{0}^{\cdot}\cosh(t)w(t)dt\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\leq 2\|w\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}.

Proof.

In order to prove (
A.1
), we integrate by parts and obtain for each
y>0
:

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{y}\cosh^{2}(x)\left|\int_{x}^{\infty}{\rm sech}(t)w(t)dt\right|^{2}dx

\displaystyle=\left[\frac{1}{2}(x+\cosh(x)\sinh(x))\left|\int_{x}^{\infty}{\rm sech}(t)w(t)dt\right|^{2}\right]\biggr|_{x=0}^{x=y}

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{y}(x{\rm sech}(x)+\sinh(x)){\rm Re}\left(\bar{w}(x)\int_{x}^{\infty}{\rm sech}(t)w(t)dt\right)dx

The first and second terms are estimated separately with Cauchy–Schwarz inequality as

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}(y+\cosh(y)\sinh(y))\left|\int_{y}^{\infty}{\rm sech}(t)w(t)dt\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}(y+\cosh(y)\sinh(y))(1-\tanh(y))\int_{y}^{\infty}|w(t)|^{2}dt

\displaystyle\leq\int_{y}^{\infty}|w(t)|^{2}dt

and

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{y}(x{\rm sech}(x)+\sinh(x))|\bar{w}(x)|\left|\int_{x}^{\infty}{\rm sech}(t)w(t)dt\right|dx

\displaystyle\leq 2\int_{0}^{y}\cosh(x)|\bar{w}(x)|\left|\int_{x}^{\infty}{\rm sech}(t)w(t)dt\right|dx

\displaystyle\leq 2\left(\int_{0}^{y}\cosh^{2}(x)\left|\int_{x}^{\infty}{\rm sech}(t)w(t)dt\right|^{2}dx\right)^{1/2}\left(\int_{0}^{y}|w(x)|^{2}dx\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{y}\cosh^{2}(x)\left|\int_{x}^{\infty}{\rm sech}(t)w(t)dt\right|^{2}dx+2\int_{0}^{y}|w(x)|^{2}dx,

where we have used
2ab\leq\frac{1}{2}a^{2}+2b^{2}
in the last line.
Combining the estimates, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{y}\cosh^{2}(x)\left|\int_{x}^{\infty}{\rm sech}(t)w(t)dt\right|^{2}dx\leq 2\int_{y}^{\infty}|w(x)|^{2}dx+4\int_{0}^{y}|w(x)|^{2}dx,

which yields the bound (
A.1
) in the limit
y\to\infty
. A similar bound (
A.2
) follows analogously for
y<0
.

In order to prove (
A.3
), we again integrate by parts and obtain for each
y>0
:

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{y}{\rm sech}^{2}(x)\left|\int_{0}^{x}\cosh(t)w(t)dt\right|^{2}dx

\displaystyle=\left[(\tanh(x)-1)\left|\int_{0}^{x}\cosh(t)w(t)dt\right|^{2}\right]\biggr|_{x=0}^{x=y}

\displaystyle+2\int_{0}^{y}(\cosh(x)-\sinh(x)){\rm Re}\left(\bar{w}(x)\int_{0}^{x}\cosh(t)w(t)dt\right)dx

The first term is negative while the second term is again estimated with Cauchy–Schwarz inequality as

\displaystyle 2\int_{0}^{y}{\rm sech}(x)|w(x)|\left|\int_{0}^{x}\cosh(t)w(t)dt\right|dx

\displaystyle\leq 2\left(\int_{0}^{y}{\rm sech}^{2}(x)\left|\int_{0}^{x}\cosh(t)w(t)dt\right|^{2}dx\right)^{1/2}\left(\int_{0}^{y}|w(x)|^{2}dx\right)^{1/2}.

In the limit
y\to\infty
, this gives the bound

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}{\rm sech}^{2}(x)\left|\int_{0}^{x}\cosh(t)w(t)dt\right|^{2}dx\leq 4\int_{0}^{\infty}|w(t)|^{2}dt.

A similar is obtained analogously for
y<0
, which yields together the bound (
A.3
).
∎

Using the estimates in Lemma
A.1
, we prove the integration by parts formula for both function spaces. Here
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
denotes the standard inner product in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
.

Lemma A.2
.

For every
f\in\mathcal{H}^{2}_{-}
and
g\in\mathcal{H}^{1}_{-}
, it is true that
\langle f^{\prime},g^{\prime}\rangle=-\langle f^{\prime\prime},g\rangle
.

Proof.

First, we check that if
f\in\mathcal{H}^{2}_{-}
, then
\cosh(\cdot)f^{\prime}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
and so
\sinh(\cdot)f^{\prime}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
. Indeed, if
f\in\mathcal{H}^{2}_{-}
, then
f^{\prime}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
and
\cosh(\cdot)f^{\prime\prime}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
. It is then clear that
f^{\prime}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
so that
f^{\prime}\in C(\mathbb{R})
and
f^{\prime}(x)\to 0
as
|x|\to\infty
by Sobolev’s embedding. Therefore, we write

f^{\prime}(x)=-\int_{x}^{\infty}f^{\prime\prime}(t)dt=-\int_{x}^{\infty}{\rm sech}(t)\left[\cosh(t)f^{\prime\prime}(t)\right]dt,

so that
\cosh(\cdot)f^{\prime}\in L^{2}(0,\infty)
by the bound (
A.1
) with
w:=\cosh(\cdot)f^{\prime\prime}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
. Similarly, we obtain

\cosh(\cdot)f^{\prime}\in L^{2}(-\infty,0)
by the bound (
A.2
).

Next, since

(A.4)

\frac{d}{dx}\left[\cosh(x)f^{\prime}(x)\right]=\cosh(x)f^{\prime\prime}(x)+\sinh(x)f^{\prime}(x),

we infer that if
f\in\mathcal{H}^{2}_{-}
, then
\cosh(\cdot)f^{\prime}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
, which implies that
\cosh(x)f^{\prime}(x)\to 0
as
|x|\to\infty
. Similarly, if
g\in\mathcal{H}^{1}_{-}
, we have
{\rm sech}(\cdot)g\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
since
{\rm sech}(\cdot)g\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
,

{\rm sech}(\cdot)g^{\prime}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
, and

(A.5)

\frac{d}{dx}\left[{\rm sech}(x)g(x)\right]={\rm sech}(x)g^{\prime}(x)-{\rm sech}(x)\tanh(x)g(x).

This implies that
{\rm sech}(x)g(x)\to 0
as
|x|\to\infty
so that

f^{\prime}(x)g(x)=\left[\cosh(x)f^{\prime}(x)\right]\left[{\rm sech}(x)g(x)\right]\to 0\quad\mbox{\rm as}\;\;|x|\to\infty

and integration by parts yields
\langle f^{\prime},g^{\prime}\rangle=-\langle f^{\prime\prime},g\rangle
.
∎

Lemma A.3
.

For every
f\in\mathcal{H}^{2}_{+}
and
g\in\mathcal{H}^{1}_{+}
, it is true that
\langle f^{\prime},g^{\prime}\rangle=-\langle f^{\prime\prime},g\rangle
.

Proof.

Here we recall that
\mathcal{H}^{1}_{+}\equiv H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
and that if
f\in\mathcal{H}^{2}_{+}
, then
f\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
and
\cosh(\cdot)(-f^{\prime\prime}+4f)\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
. This implies that
(-f^{\prime\prime}+4f)\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
and hence
f\in H^{2}(\mathbb{R})

so that
f(x),f^{\prime}(x)\to 0
as
|x|\to\infty
and the integration by parts holds.
∎

\displaystyle\hat{P}_{1}:=\frac{i}{2}\int\left[(2-|U_{c,\omega}|^{2})\nu-(\bar{U}_{c,\omega}U^{\prime}_{c,\omega}-\bar{U}^{\prime}_{c,\omega}U_{c,\omega})\eta\right]dx+\theta_{1}(R)-\theta_{1}(-R)

and

\displaystyle\hat{P}_{2}:=-\int(2-|U_{c,\omega}|^{2})(uv^{\prime}-u^{\prime}v)dx+\frac{1}{2i}\int\eta\nu dx+\theta_{2}(R)-\theta_{2}(-R),

and the integration is performed over
[-R,R]
. Substituting
\nu
into
\hat{P}_{1}
and integrating
u^{\prime}
and
v^{\prime}
by parts yield
after simplifications:

\displaystyle\hat{P}_{1}

\displaystyle=(2-|U_{c,\omega}|^{2})\left({\rm Im}(U_{c,\omega})u-{\rm Re}(U_{c,\omega})v\right)\biggr|^{x=R}_{x=-R}

\displaystyle\quad+4\int(1-|U_{c,\omega}|^{2})(-{\rm Im}(U^{\prime}_{c,\omega})u+{\rm Re}(U^{\prime}_{c,\omega})v)dx

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{2i}\int(\bar{U}_{c,\omega}U^{\prime}_{c,\omega}-\bar{U}^{\prime}_{c,\omega}U_{c,\omega})(u^{2}+v^{2})dx+\theta_{1}(R)-\theta_{1}(-R).

The quadratic term in
\hat{P}_{1}
is bounded by
\|u\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}}+\|v\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}}
because there is
C_{R}>0
such that

\int_{-R}^{R}\left|\bar{U}_{c,\omega}U^{\prime}_{c,\omega}-\bar{U}^{\prime}_{c,\omega}U_{c,\omega}\right|(u^{2}+v^{2})dx\leq C_{R}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(1-\varphi^{2})\left|\bar{U}_{c,\omega}U^{\prime}_{c,\omega}-\bar{U}^{\prime}_{c,\omega}U_{c,\omega}\right|(u^{2}+v^{2})dx,

and
U_{c,\omega},U_{c,\omega}^{\prime}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
.
The linear integral term in
\hat{P}_{1}
is combined together with
a similar term in
P_{1}
yields

4\int_{\mathbb{R}}(1-|U_{c,\omega}|^{2})(-{\rm Im}(U^{\prime}_{c,\omega})u+{\rm Re}(U^{\prime}_{c,\omega})v)dx,

which vanish due to the second and fourth orthogonality conditions (
6.11
). Finally, the terms at
x=\pm R

are combined together in
P_{1}
and
\hat{P}_{1}
to yield

\displaystyle\frac{{\rm Im}(U_{c,\omega})u-{\rm Re}(U_{c,\omega})v}{|U_{c,\omega}|^{2}}\biggr|^{x=R}_{x=-R}+\theta_{1}(R)-\theta_{1}(-R)=0.

All integral terms in
\hat{P}_{2}
are bounded by

\|u^{\prime}+iv^{\prime}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\|u+iv\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}}+\|\eta\|^{2}_{L^{2}}
because integration on
[-R,R]
is bounded by integration on
\mathbb{R}

with the weight
(1-\varphi^{2})
. Similarly, by using the Sobolev embedding
of
H^{1}([-R,R])
to
L^{\infty}([-R,R])
and the Taylor expansion with
|U_{c,\omega}(\pm R)|^{2}
being bounded away from zero, we obtain

|\theta_{2}(\pm R)|\leq C_{R}(|u(\pm R)|^{2}+|v(\pm R)|^{2})\leq C_{R}\|u+iv\|^{2}_{H^{1}([-R,R])},

which is bounded by

\|u^{\prime}+iv^{\prime}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\|u+iv\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}}
since
[-R,R]
is compact and
1-\varphi^{2}
is bounded away from zero on
[-R,R]
.

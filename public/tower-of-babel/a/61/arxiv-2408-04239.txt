1
Introduction

In this paper we are concerned with relationships between
non-commutative harmonic oscillators (NcHO) and
two-photon quantum Rabi models from an
operator-theoretic point of view, and study the asymptotic behaviors of the spectral zeta function of
the two-photon quantum Rabi models using path measures.
For brevity, we refer to the two-photon quantum Rabi model as 2pQRM.

The NcHO is a second order differential operator
and had been introduced by Parmeggiani and Wakayama
[
29
,
30
,
31
]
as a non-commutative extension of the one-dimensional harmonic oscillator through
the oscillator representation of Lie algebra
{\mathfrak{s}l}_{2}({R})
, and
the spectrum of the NcHO are studied so far from a purely mathematical point of view.
In the Bargmann representation the eigenvalue equation of the NcHO
can be reduced to studying Heun ordinary differential equations
[
24
]
.
While the spectrum of the NcHO is purely discrete and one can define its spectral zeta function.
A meromorphic continuation and special values of the spectral zeta function
have been investigated in
[
14
,
15
,
21
,
16
,
25
,
35
,
22
]
.

On the other hand the standard quantum Rabi model describes a two-level atom coupled with a single photon.
In this paper the standard quantum Rabi model is called one-photon quantum Rabi model and we abbreviate as 1pQRM.
It is shown in
[
2
]
that the 1pQRM is integrable due to the parity symmetry,
and as a result, the spectrum of the 1pQRM has been extensively studied.
There are many generalizations of the 1pQRM which describe various implementations within cavity and circuit QED as well as quantum simulation platforms.
See
[
3
]
and references therein.
The so-called two-photon quantum Rabi model, 2pQRM,
is one generalization, which is the main subject of the present paper.
The 2pQRM is also a second order differential operator
and the spectrum of the 2pQRM are studied so far but mainly from a physical point of view.
The amount of research on the 2pQRM is vast, and it is difficult to cover it all here.
For example, the spectrum of the 2pQRM is studied in
[
5
]
,
the kpQRM (
k\geq 3
) is discussed in
[
4
]
,
the hybrid of the 2pQRM and the 1pQRM is introduced in
[
44
]
,
the degeneracy of the spectral curve is investigated in
[
43
]
,
and the case of critical coupling
g=\frac{1}{2}

is studied in
[
37
]
.

Although the NcHO and the 2pQRM appear quite similar, as shown below, they have often been studied independently. In particular, the 2pQRM has been valued as the simplest yet nontrivial model in physics, whereas the NcHO has
been primarily discussed in terms of number theory and complex differential equations.
It is remarkable, however, that
[
23
]
was recently published as a paper bridging the NcHO and
the 2pQRM, where it was shown that the eigenvectors of the NcHO coincide with those of a certain 2pQRM.
This result serves as one of the motivations for the present paper.

In this paper we discuss (1) relationships between
Q_{\alpha\beta}^{\rm NH}
and
H_{\triangle,g}^{\rm 2p}
,
(2) Feynman-Kac formulas of
e^{-tH_{\triangle,g}^{\rm 2p}}
and
e^{-tQ_{\alpha\beta}^{\rm NH}}
,
(3) the asymptotic behaviors of the spectral zeta functions of
H_{\triangle,g}^{\rm 2p}
.
In what follows we explain (1), (2) and (3).

(1) We show that the NcHO can be decomposed into the 2pQRM.
The non-commutative harmonic oscillator

Q_{\alpha\beta}^{\rm NH}=\begin{pmatrix}\alpha&0\\
0&\beta\end{pmatrix}\otimes\left(a^{\ast}a+\frac{1}{2}\right)+\frac{1}{2}\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\
1&0\end{pmatrix}\otimes(a^{2}-{a^{\ast}}^{2})

is defined by two parameters
\alpha,\beta>0
, and
two-photon quantum Rabi model

H_{\triangle,g}^{\rm 2p}=\triangle\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&-1\end{pmatrix}\otimes{\mathchoice{\rm 1\mskip-4.0mul}{\rm 1\mskip-4.0mul}{\rm 1\mskip-4.5mul}{\rm 1\mskip-5.0mul}}+{\mathchoice{\rm 1\mskip-4.0mul}{\rm 1\mskip-4.0mul}{\rm 1\mskip-4.5mul}{\rm 1\mskip-5.0mul}}\otimes\left(a^{\ast}a+\frac{1}{2}\right)+g\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
1&0\end{pmatrix}\otimes(a^{2}+{a^{\ast}}^{2})

by
\triangle>0
and a coupling constant
g\in{R}
.
See Section
2
for the detail.
Both
Q_{\alpha\beta}^{\rm NH}
and
H_{\triangle,g}^{\rm 2p}
are defined as self-adjoint operators in
the Hilbert space:

\mathcal{H}={C}^{2}\otimes{L^{2}({R})}
.
We can see that
H_{\triangle,g}^{\rm 2p}
is unitarily equivalent to the form

\displaystyle H_{\triangle,g}^{\rm 2p}\cong H_{0,g}^{\rm 2p}+\triangle\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
1&0\end{pmatrix},

(1.1)

where

H_{0,g}^{\rm 2p}
is diagonalized and its spectrum is exactly specified.
On the other hand
Q_{\alpha\beta}^{\rm NH}
can be represented as

\displaystyle Q_{\alpha\beta}^{\rm NH}=\begin{pmatrix}\sqrt{\alpha}&0\\
0&\sqrt{\beta}\end{pmatrix}K_{\alpha\beta}^{\rm NH}\begin{pmatrix}\sqrt{\alpha}&0\\
0&\sqrt{\beta}\end{pmatrix},

(1.2)

where
K_{\alpha\beta}^{\rm NH}

is also diagonalized and its spectrum is exactly specified as is
H_{0,g}^{\rm 2p}
.
Note that

(\begin{smallmatrix}\sqrt{\alpha}&0\\
0&\sqrt{\beta}\end{smallmatrix})
is not unitary whenever
\alpha\neq\beta
.
See Lemma
2.2
for
H_{0,g}^{\rm 2p}
and Lemma
2.5
for
K_{\alpha\beta}^{\rm NH}
.
Both models have very simple forms, and when a mathematician looks at them for the first time, they may appear trivial. Their spectral analysis is however not straightforward, as demonstrated in this paper.
When
\alpha=\beta
and
\triangle=0
,
by (
1.1
) and (
1.2
) it is trivial to see that

Q_{\alpha\alpha}^{\rm NH}\cong\frac{\sqrt{\alpha^{2}-1}}{\sqrt{1-4g^{2}}}H_{0,g}^{\rm 2p},\quad|\alpha|>1,\quad|g|<\frac{1}{2}.

The main purpose of this paper is to give relationships
between
Q_{\alpha\beta}^{\rm NH}
and
H_{\triangle,g}^{\rm 2p}
but for
\alpha\neq\beta
and
\triangle>0
.
New ingredient in this paper is to
introduce a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{\alpha\beta}
for each pair
\alpha,\beta>0
in (
3.5
).
It is given by

(f,g)_{\alpha\beta}=(f,\gamma_{\alpha\beta}g)_{\mathcal{H}},

where
\gamma_{\alpha\beta}
is the matrix defined by

\gamma_{\alpha\beta}=\begin{pmatrix}1/\alpha&0\\
0&1/\beta\end{pmatrix}\otimes{\mathchoice{\rm 1\mskip-4.0mul}{\rm 1\mskip-4.0mul}{\rm 1\mskip-4.5mul}{\rm 1\mskip-5.0mul}}.

Then
for each eigenvalue
\lambda
of
Q_{\alpha\beta}^{\rm NH}

one defines a two-photon quantum Rabi model
H_{\frac{\alpha-\beta}{2\alpha\beta}\lambda,\frac{1}{2\sqrt{\alpha\beta}}}^{\rm 2p}
with
\triangle=\frac{\alpha-\beta}{2\alpha\beta}\lambda
and
g=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{\alpha\beta}}
, which acts in
\mathcal{H}_{\alpha\beta}
, and it can be shown that

\displaystyle{\rm I}_{\alpha\beta}Q_{\alpha\beta}^{\rm NH}{\rm I}_{\alpha\beta}^{-1}=\frac{2\alpha\beta}{\alpha+\beta}\bigoplus_{\lambda\in{\rm spec}(Q_{\alpha\beta}^{\rm NH})}H_{\frac{\alpha-\beta}{2\alpha\beta}\lambda,\frac{1}{2\sqrt{\alpha\beta}}}^{\rm 2p}{\rm P}_{\frac{\alpha+\beta}{2\alpha\beta}\lambda}

(1.3)

holds on
\mathcal{H}_{\alpha\beta}
in Theorem
3.8
.
Here
{\rm I}_{\alpha\beta}
is a unitary operator from
\mathcal{H}
to
\mathcal{H}_{\alpha\beta}
and

{\rm P}_{\frac{\alpha+\beta}{2\alpha\beta}\lambda}

denotes the projection onto the eigenspace of

H_{\frac{\alpha-\beta}{2\alpha\beta}\lambda,\frac{1}{2\sqrt{\alpha\beta}}}^{\rm 2p}
corresponding to eigenvalue
\frac{\alpha+\beta}{2\alpha\beta}\lambda
.
In this paper we also introduce
one-particle non-commutative harmonic oscillator

Q_{\alpha\beta}^{\rm 1pNH}=\begin{pmatrix}\alpha&0\\
0&\beta\end{pmatrix}\otimes\left(a^{\ast}a+\frac{1}{2}\right)+\frac{1}{2}\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\
1&0\end{pmatrix}(a-a^{\ast})

and establish relationships between
Q_{\alpha\beta}^{\rm 1pNH}
and
one-photon quantum Rabi model

H_{\triangle,g}^{\rm 1p}=\triangle\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&-1\end{pmatrix}\otimes{\mathchoice{\rm 1\mskip-4.0mul}{\rm 1\mskip-4.0mul}{\rm 1\mskip-4.5mul}{\rm 1\mskip-5.0mul}}+{\mathchoice{\rm 1\mskip-4.0mul}{\rm 1\mskip-4.0mul}{\rm 1\mskip-4.5mul}{\rm 1\mskip-5.0mul}}\otimes\left(a^{\ast}a+\frac{1}{2}\right)+g\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
1&0\end{pmatrix}\otimes(a+{a^{\ast}})

in
Theorem
5.1
. The result is

\displaystyle{\rm I}_{\alpha\beta}Q_{\alpha\beta}^{\rm 1pNH}{\rm I}_{\alpha\beta}^{-1}=\frac{2\alpha\beta}{\alpha+\beta}\bigoplus_{\lambda\in{\rm spec}(Q_{\alpha\beta}^{\rm 1pNH})}H_{\frac{\alpha-\beta}{2\alpha\beta}\lambda,\frac{1}{2\sqrt{\alpha\beta}}}^{\rm 1p}\tilde{\rm P}_{\frac{\alpha+\beta}{2\alpha\beta}\lambda},

(1.4)

where
\tilde{\rm P}_{\frac{\alpha+\beta}{2\alpha\beta}\lambda}

denotes the projection onto the eigenspace of

H_{\frac{\alpha-\beta}{2\alpha\beta}\lambda,\frac{1}{2\sqrt{\alpha\beta}}}^{\rm 1p}
corresponding to eigenvalue
\frac{\alpha+\beta}{2\alpha\beta}\lambda
.
From (
1.3
) and (
1.4
) one can see
that
Q_{\alpha\beta}^{\rm NH}
can be decomposed into
H_{\triangle,g}^{\rm 2p}
, while
Q_{\alpha\beta}^{\rm 1pNH}
can be decomposed into
H_{\triangle,g}^{\rm 1p}
.

(2) We discuss the NcHO and the 2pQRM using path measures.
As mentioned above, the 2pQRM is a physical model whose coefficients are expressed as

2\times 2
Pauli matrices, making it suitable for path integrals.
In
[
13
]
, the Feynman-Kac formula for

e^{-tH_{\triangle,g}^{\rm 1p}}
was constructed.
Similarly, we construct a Feynman-Kac formula for

e^{-tH_{\triangle,g}^{\rm 2p}}
in Theorem
4.1
.
However, since the interaction term of the 1pQRM is first-order with respect to the annihilation and creation operator, whereas the interaction term of the 2pQRM is second-order. Therefore,
constructing the Feynman-Kac formula for
e^{-tH_{\triangle,g}^{\rm 2p}}
is nontrivial.
On the other hand, the NcHO is a mathematical model and appears somewhat artificial.
As a result, it has been challenging to apply the theory of path integrals to

e^{-tQ_{\alpha\beta}^{\rm NH}}
except in special cases such as when
\alpha=\beta

[
39
]
.
However, in this paper, we elucidate the relationship between the 2pQRM and the NcHO, as shown in (
1.3
), and derive a path integral for

e^{-tQ_{\alpha\beta}^{\rm NH}}
based on (
1.3
).

(3) We discuss spectral zeta functions.

denotes the spectrum of a self-adjoint operator

\zeta_{2p}(s;\tau)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\mu_{n}(\triangle,g)^{s}},\quad s>1

We study the asymptotic behaviors of the spectral zeta function of

H_{\triangle,g}^{\rm 2p}
using the Feynman-Kac formula. A similar asymptotic behaviors of the spectral zeta function of

H_{\triangle,g}^{\rm 1p}
have been investigated in
[
36
,
13
]
using path measures.

.
We include appendices, Sections

2
Spectrum of NcHO and 2pQRM

2.1
Preliminaries

Let

a
and
a^{\ast}
be the annihilation operator
and the creation operator in
{L^{2}({R})}
, respectively, which are given by

a=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}x}+x\right)
and

a^{\ast}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(-\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}x}+x\right)
.
They satisfy the canonical commutation relation

[a,a^{\ast}]={\mathchoice{\rm 1\mskip-4.0mul}{\rm 1\mskip-4.0mul}{\rm 1\mskip-4.5mul}{\rm 1\mskip-5.0mul}}
.
Note that

a^{\ast}a=-\frac{1}{2}\frac{{\rm d}^{2}}{{\rm d}x^{2}}+\frac{1}{2}x^{2}-\frac{1}{2}

is the harmonic oscillator, and
{\rm spec}(a^{\ast}a)={N}\cup\{0\}
.
Let
L_{n}
be the eigenspace of
a^{\ast}a
for the eigenvalue
n
.
It is actually given by
L_{n}={\rm LH}\{\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}{a^{\ast}}^{n}\varphi_{\rm g}\}
, where

\varphi_{\rm g}(x)=\pi^{-1/4}e^{-x^{2}/2}
. Note that
a\varphi_{\rm g}=0
.
Then
\dim L_{n}=1
for any
n\geq 0
and

{L^{2}({R})}
can be represented as a Fock space:

{L^{2}({R})}=\bigoplus_{n=0}^{\infty}L_{n}.

Thus
a:L_{n}\to L_{n-1}
for
n\geq 1
and
a^{\ast}:L_{n}\to L_{n+1}
for
n\geq 0
.
On the other hand
a^{\ast}a
leaves
L_{n}
invariant.
Let

{\rm U}_{t}=e^{-ita^{\ast}a}.

\{{\rm U}_{t}\}_{t\in{R}}
is a strongly continuous one-parameter unitary group and leaves
L_{n}
invariant for each
n\geq 0
.
It can be shown that

{\rm U}_{t}
maps
{\rm D}(a)
to itself and

{\rm U}_{t}a{\rm U}_{t}^{-1}=e^{it}a
on
{\rm D}(a)
,
and

{\rm U}_{t}
maps
{\rm D}(a^{\ast})
to itself and

{\rm U}_{t}a^{\ast}{\rm U}_{t}^{-1}=e^{-it}a^{\ast}
on
{\rm D}(a^{\ast})
.
In particular
e^{-i(\pi/2)a^{\ast}a}
is the Fourier transform on

\bigoplus_{n=0}^{\infty}L_{n}
.
We set

p=-i\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}x},\quad q=x.

Then

e^{-i(\pi/2)a^{\ast}a}(a+a^{\ast})e^{i(\pi/2)a^{\ast}a}=i(a-a^{\ast})
, which implies that

e^{-i(\pi/2)a^{\ast}a}qe^{i(\pi/2)a^{\ast}a}=-p.

Moreover

e^{-i(\pi/4)a^{\ast}a}
is very useful in this paper.
It satisfies that

e^{-i(\pi/4)a^{\ast}a}(a^{2}-{a^{\ast}}^{2})e^{i(\pi/4)a^{\ast}a}=i(a^{2}+{a^{\ast}}^{2})

which implies that

e^{-i(\pi/4)a^{\ast}a}(pq+qp)e^{i(\pi/4)a^{\ast}a}=-p^{2}+q^{2}.

Next we explain the spin part.
Let
\sigma_{x}
,
\sigma_{y}
and
\sigma_{z}
be the
2\times 2
Pauli matrices given by

\displaystyle\sigma_{x}=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
1&0\end{pmatrix},\quad\sigma_{y}=\begin{pmatrix}0&-i\\
i&0\end{pmatrix},\quad\sigma_{z}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&-1\end{pmatrix}.

The set
\{\sigma_{x},\sigma_{y},\sigma_{z}\}
forms a basis for the Lie-algebra
{\mathfrak{s}}{\mathfrak{u}}(2)
.
The
3
-dimensional rotation group
SO({R}^{3})

has an adjoint representation on
SU(2)
.
Let
n\in{R}^{3}
be a unit vector and let
\theta\in[0,2\pi)
.
Define
\bm{\sigma}=(\sigma_{x},\sigma_{y},\sigma_{z})
, and for
v=(v_{1},v_{2},v_{3})\in{R}^{3}
,
we set
n\cdot\bm{\sigma}=n_{1}\sigma_{x}+n_{2}\sigma_{y}+n_{3}\sigma_{z}
.
It follows that
e^{(i/2)\theta n\cdot\sigma}(v\cdot\bm{\sigma})e^{-(i/2)\theta n\cdot\sigma}=Rv\cdot\bm{\sigma}
, where

R\in SO(3)
denotes the
3\times 3
matrix representing
the rotation around
n
by an angle
\theta
.
As a specific example, let
n=(0,1,0)
and
\theta=\pi/2
. In this case
we have

{\iota}_{y}\sigma_{y}{\iota}_{y}^{-1}=\sigma_{y}
and

{\iota}_{y}\sigma_{x}{\iota}_{y}^{-1}=\sigma_{z}
and

{\iota}_{y}\sigma_{z}{\iota}_{y}^{-1}=-\sigma_{x}
,
where

{\iota}_{y}=e^{i\frac{\pi}{4}\sigma_{y}}
.
In the same way we can construct
{\iota}_{x}
and
{\iota}_{z}
.
In this paper, we frequently use such unitary transformations

\displaystyle{\iota}_{x}\sigma_{x}{\iota}_{x}^{-1}=\sigma_{x},\,\,{\iota}_{x}\sigma_{y}{\iota}_{x}^{-1}=\sigma_{z},\,\,{\iota}_{x}\sigma_{z}{\iota}_{x}^{-1}=-\sigma_{y},

(2.1)

\displaystyle{\iota}_{y}\sigma_{x}{\iota}_{y}^{-1}=\sigma_{z},\,\,{\iota}_{y}\sigma_{y}{\iota}_{y}^{-1}=\sigma_{y},\,\,{\iota}_{y}\sigma_{z}{\iota}_{y}^{-1}=-\sigma_{x},

(2.2)

\displaystyle{\iota}_{z}\sigma_{x}{\iota}_{z}^{-1}=-\sigma_{y},\,\,{\iota}_{z}\sigma_{y}{\iota}_{z}^{-1}=\sigma_{x},\,\,{\iota}_{z}\sigma_{z}{\iota}_{z}^{-1}=\sigma_{z}

(2.3)

on spins in the following sections. Combining
{\rm U}_{t}
and
{\iota}_{\cdot}
, we have the unitary equivalences:

\sigma_{y}(-i)\otimes(a-a^{\ast})\cong\sigma_{x}\otimes(a+a^{\ast}),\quad\sigma_{y}(-i)\otimes(a^{2}-{a^{\ast}}^{2})\cong\sigma_{x}\otimes(a^{2}+{a^{\ast}}^{2}).

We shall use these equivalences in this paper.

2.2
Two-photon quantum Rabi model

The state space for the 2pQRM is given by the Hilbert space:

\mathcal{H}={C}^{2}\otimes{L^{2}({R})}.

We use identifications:

\mathcal{H}\cong{L^{2}({R})}\oplus{L^{2}({R})}\cong L^{2}({R};{C}^{2})

unless it causes confusion.
The 1pQRM describes
a two-level atom coupled to a single photon, which
is introduced by I.I.Rabi
[
32
]
in the semiclassical region
and then
the quantized version is introduced by E.T. Jaynes and F.W. Cumming
[
17
]
.
The Hamiltonian is given by

H_{\triangle,g}^{\rm 1p}=\triangle\sigma_{z}\otimes{\mathchoice{\rm 1\mskip-4.0mul}{\rm 1\mskip-4.0mul}{\rm 1\mskip-4.5mul}{\rm 1\mskip-5.0mul}}+{\mathchoice{\rm 1\mskip-4.0mul}{\rm 1\mskip-4.0mul}{\rm 1\mskip-4.5mul}{\rm 1\mskip-5.0mul}}\otimes\left(a^{\ast}a+\frac{1}{2}\right)+g\sigma_{x}\otimes(a+{a^{\ast}})

as a self-adjoint operator in
\mathcal{H}
, where

g\in{R}
stands for a coupling constant,
and
\triangle>0
is also a constant.
In physics
{\rm spec}(\triangle\sigma_{z})=\{-\triangle,\triangle\}
describes the eigenvalues of the two-level atom.
While the Hamiltonian of the 2pQRM, which is the main subject in this paper, is defined
by
H_{\triangle,g}^{\rm 1p}
with the interaction term
a+a^{\ast}
replaced by
a^{2}+{a^{\ast}}^{2}
:

\displaystyle H_{\triangle,g}^{\rm 2p}=\triangle\sigma_{z}\otimes{\mathchoice{\rm 1\mskip-4.0mul}{\rm 1\mskip-4.0mul}{\rm 1\mskip-4.5mul}{\rm 1\mskip-5.0mul}}+{\mathchoice{\rm 1\mskip-4.0mul}{\rm 1\mskip-4.0mul}{\rm 1\mskip-4.5mul}{\rm 1\mskip-5.0mul}}\otimes\left(a^{\ast}a+\frac{1}{2}\right)+g\sigma_{x}\otimes(a^{2}+{a^{\ast}}^{2}).

(2.4)

In physics
the 2pQRM is defined by

H_{\triangle,g}^{\rm 2p}
with

a^{\ast}a+\frac{1}{2}
replaced by

a^{\ast}a
, but in this paper
we add an extra term
\frac{1}{2}
and adopt (
2.4
) for the latter convenience.
Notice that

a^{2}+{a^{\ast}}^{2}=\frac{{\rm d}^{2}}{{\rm d}x^{2}}+x^{2}=-p^{2}+q^{2}.

For
{\mathchoice{\rm 1\mskip-4.0mul}{\rm 1\mskip-4.0mul}{\rm 1\mskip-4.5mul}{\rm 1\mskip-5.0mul}}\otimes a^{\ast}a
,
we use identifications:

{\mathchoice{\rm 1\mskip-4.0mul}{\rm 1\mskip-4.0mul}{\rm 1\mskip-4.5mul}{\rm 1\mskip-5.0mul}}\otimes a^{\ast}a=a^{\ast}a\oplus a^{\ast}a=(\frac{1}{2}p^{2}+\frac{1}{2}q^{2}+\frac{1}{2})\oplus(\frac{1}{2}p^{2}+\frac{1}{2}q^{2}+\frac{1}{2}).

Let
\mathcal{D}={\rm D}({\mathchoice{\rm 1\mskip-4.0mul}{\rm 1\mskip-4.0mul}{\rm 1\mskip-4.5mul}{\rm 1\mskip-5.0mul}}\otimes a^{\ast}a)
.
We also identify
\mathcal{D}
with several subspaces below:

\mathcal{D}={C}^{2}\otimes{\rm D}(a^{\ast}a)={\rm D}(a^{\ast}a)\oplus{\rm D}(a^{\ast}a)={\rm D}(a^{\ast}a\oplus a^{\ast}a)={\rm D}(p^{2}+q^{2})\oplus{\rm D}(p^{2}+q^{2}).

We use these identifications unless any confusion arises.
We emphasize that

{\rm D}(a^{\ast}a)={\rm D}\left(\frac{{\rm d}^{2}}{{\rm d}x^{2}}\right)\cap{\rm D}(x^{2}).

The proposition below shall be often times used in this paper.

Proposition 2.1

Let
t,s\in{R}
and
T_{t,s}=(p+tq)^{2}+sq^{2}
.

(s>0)

T_{t,s}
is self-adjoint on
{\rm D}(p^{2}+q^{2})
,
T_{t,s}\cong\sqrt{s}(p^{2}+q^{2})
and

{\rm spec}(T_{t,s})=\{\sqrt{s}(2n+1)\}
.

(s=0)

T_{t,s}
is self-adjoint on
\mathcal{U}_{t}{\rm D}(p^{2})
,
T_{t,s}\cong p^{2}
and
{\rm spec}(T_{t,s})=[0,\infty)
, where
\mathcal{U}_{t}=e^{itq^{2}/2}
.

(s<0)

T_{t,s}
is essentially self-adjoint on
{\rm D}(p^{2}+q^{2})
,
T_{t,s}\cong\sqrt{-s}(p^{2}-q^{2})

.

Proof.

See Theorems

,

∎Let

Lemma 2.2

Lemma 2.3

(self-adjointness of
H_{\triangle,g}^{\rm 2p}

(|g|<\frac{1}{2})

\displaystyle\inf{\rm spec}(H_{\triangle,g}^{\rm 2p})\geq\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{1-4g^{2}}-\triangle.

(2.5)

(|g|=\frac{1}{2})

is self-adjoint on
{\rm D}(q^{2})\oplus{\rm D}(p^{2})

and

\displaystyle\inf{\rm spec}(H_{\triangle,g}^{\rm 2p})\geq-\triangle.

(2.6)

(|g|<\frac{1}{2})

is essentially self-adjoint on

Proof.

Suppose that
|g|<\frac{1}{2}

\displaystyle{\iota}_{y}H_{\triangle,g}^{\rm 2p}{\iota}_{y}^{-1}=\begin{pmatrix}(\frac{1}{2}-g)p^{2}+(\frac{1}{2}+g)q^{2}&-\triangle\\

(2.7)

and

(2.8)

We consider the parity symmetry.
The parity symmetry
of
H_{\triangle,g}^{\rm 1p}
is very useful for studying the spectrum of
H_{\triangle,g}^{\rm 1p}
.
For
H_{\triangle,g}^{\rm 2p}
there is a similar symmetry.
Let

P_{1}=\sigma_{z}\otimes e^{i\pi{a^{\ast}a}}
and

P_{2}=\sigma_{z}\otimes e^{i(\pi/2){a^{\ast}a}}
.
It can be verified that

[P_{1},H_{\triangle,g}^{\rm 1p}]=0
and
[P_{2},H_{\triangle,g}^{\rm 2p}]=0
.
The former symmetry is known as
{Z}_{2}
-symmetry or parity symmetry,
while the latter is referred to as
{Z}_{4}
-symmetry.
Since the spectrum of
P_{2}
is
\{\pm 1,\pm i\}
,
the state space
\mathcal{H}
can be decomposed into
four subspaces:

\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{H}_{+1}\oplus\mathcal{H}_{-1}\oplus\mathcal{H}_{i}\oplus\mathcal{H}_{-i},

where

\mathcal{H}_{k}
is the eigenspace of
P_{2}
corresponding to eigenvalue
k\in\{\pm 1,\pm i\}
.
Let
{C}^{2}\otimes L_{n}=L_{n+}\oplus L_{n-}
, where

L_{n+}=\{\binom{f}{0}\mid f\in L_{n}\}
and

L_{n-}=\{\binom{0}{f}\mid f\in L_{n}\}
.
Then each
\mathcal{H}_{k}
is defined by

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{+1}=\bigoplus_{m=0}^{\infty}L_{4m+}\oplus L_{4m+2-},\quad\mathcal{H}_{-1}=\bigoplus_{m=0}^{\infty}L_{4m+2+}\oplus L_{4m-},

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{+i}=\bigoplus_{m=0}^{\infty}L_{4m+1+}\oplus L_{4m+3-},\quad\mathcal{H}_{-i}=\bigoplus_{m=0}^{\infty}L_{4m+3+}\oplus L_{4m+1-}.

H_{\triangle,g}^{\rm 2p}
can be reduced by
\mathcal{H}_{k}
.
In the case of
g=0
it can be observed that the ground state
\binom{0}{\varphi_{\rm g}}
belongs to
\mathcal{H}_{-1}
.
We shall show in Corollary
4.3
,
using path measures, that the ground state
\Phi
of
H_{\triangle,g}^{\rm 2p}
satisfies

\Phi\in\mathcal{H}_{-1}
for any
g\in{R}
.

2.3
Non-commutative harmonic oscillator

In this section we are concerned with the NcHO.
Let
\alpha,\beta\in{R}
. The NcHO is a self-adjoint operator
in

\mathcal{H}
,
which is defined by

Q_{\alpha\beta}^{\rm NH}=\begin{pmatrix}\alpha&0\\
0&\beta\end{pmatrix}\otimes\left(a^{\ast}a+\frac{1}{2}\right)+\frac{1}{2}\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\
1&0\end{pmatrix}\otimes(a^{2}-{a^{\ast}}^{2}).

We notice that the interaction term in
Q_{\alpha\beta}^{\rm NH}
is

\frac{1}{2}(a^{2}-{a^{\ast}}^{2})=\frac{1}{2}\left(x\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}x}+\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}x}x\right)=x\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}x}+\frac{1}{2}=i\frac{1}{2}(pq+qp).

Note that
{\rm spec}((-i)(a^{2}-{a^{\ast}}^{2}))={R}
.

Lemma 2.4

(essential self-adjointness of
Q_{\alpha\beta}^{\rm NH}
)

Let
\alpha,\beta\in{R}
.
Then
Q_{\alpha\beta}^{\rm NH}
is essentially self-adjoint on any core of
{\mathchoice{\rm 1\mskip-4.0mul}{\rm 1\mskip-4.0mul}{\rm 1\mskip-4.5mul}{\rm 1\mskip-5.0mul}}\otimes a^{\ast}a
.

Proof.

We have

Q_{\alpha\beta}^{\rm NH}=\begin{pmatrix}\alpha&0\\
0&\beta\end{pmatrix}\otimes\frac{1}{2}(p^{2}+q^{2})+\sigma_{y}\otimes\frac{1}{2}(pq+qp).

Note that

Q_{\alpha\beta}^{\rm NH}
is symmetric on
\mathcal{D}
.
Let
N={\mathchoice{\rm 1\mskip-4.0mul}{\rm 1\mskip-4.0mul}{\rm 1\mskip-4.5mul}{\rm 1\mskip-5.0mul}}\otimes(a^{\ast}a+1)
.
Thus directly we see that

[N,Q_{\alpha\beta}^{\rm NH}]=2i\sigma_{y}\otimes(q^{2}-p^{2})
.
Then we have

\displaystyle|(f,Q_{\alpha\beta}^{\rm NH}g)|\leq C\|N^{\frac{1}{2}}f\|\|N^{\frac{1}{2}}g\|,

\displaystyle|(Nf,Q_{\alpha\beta}^{\rm NH}g)-(Q_{\alpha\beta}^{\rm NH}f,Ng)|=2|(f,i\sigma_{y}\otimes(q^{2}-p^{2})g)|\leq C\|N^{\frac{1}{2}}f\|\|N^{\frac{1}{2}}g\|.

By the Nelson commutator theorem
[
33
, Theorem X.36’]
,

Q_{\alpha\beta}^{\rm NH}
is essentially self-adjoint on any core of
{\mathchoice{\rm 1\mskip-4.0mul}{\rm 1\mskip-4.0mul}{\rm 1\mskip-4.5mul}{\rm 1\mskip-5.0mul}}\otimes a^{\ast}a
.
∎In the special case where
\alpha,\beta>0

we can
provide further details
on the spectrum of
Q_{\alpha\beta}^{\rm NH}
.
Let

A_{\alpha\beta}=\begin{pmatrix}\sqrt{\alpha}&0\\
0&\sqrt{\beta}\end{pmatrix}\otimes{\mathchoice{\rm 1\mskip-4.0mul}{\rm 1\mskip-4.0mul}{\rm 1\mskip-4.5mul}{\rm 1\mskip-5.0mul}}.

Then

A_{\alpha\beta}\mathcal{D}=\mathcal{D}

and

\displaystyle Q_{\alpha\beta}^{\rm NH}=A_{\alpha\beta}K_{\alpha\beta}^{\rm NH}A_{\alpha\beta}

(2.9)

on
\mathcal{D}
, where

K_{\alpha\beta}^{\rm NH}={\mathchoice{\rm 1\mskip-4.0mul}{\rm 1\mskip-4.0mul}{\rm 1\mskip-4.5mul}{\rm 1\mskip-5.0mul}}\otimes\frac{1}{2}(p^{2}+q^{2})+\frac{1}{2\sqrt{\alpha\beta}}\sigma_{y}\otimes(pq+qp).

The following lemmas (Lemmas
2.5
and
2.6
) may be well-known, but we include their proofs for the sake of self-consistency, as the proofs are short and fundamental

Lemma 2.5

(spectrum of
K_{\alpha\beta}^{\rm NH}
)

Operator
K_{\alpha\beta}^{\rm NH}
is self-adjoint on

{\rm D}(p^{2}+q^{2})\oplus{\rm D}(p^{2}+q^{2})
for

\alpha\beta>~1
,
self-adjoint on
{\rm D}((p+q)^{2})\oplus{\rm D}((p-q)^{2})
for
\alpha\beta=1
,
and
essentially self-adjoint on
{\rm D}(p^{2}+q^{2})\oplus{\rm D}(p^{2}+q^{2})
for

0<\alpha\beta<1
.
Moreover we have

K_{\alpha\beta}^{\rm NH}\cong\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{1-\frac{1}{\alpha\beta}}\begin{pmatrix}p^{2}+q^{2}&0\\
0&p^{2}+q^{2}\end{pmatrix}&\alpha\beta>1,\\
\frac{1}{2}\begin{pmatrix}(p+q)^{2}&0\\
0&(p-q)^{2}\end{pmatrix}&\alpha\beta=1,\\
\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\frac{1}{\alpha\beta}-1}\begin{pmatrix}p^{2}-q^{2}&0\\
0&p^{2}-q^{2}\end{pmatrix}&0<\alpha\beta<1.\end{array}\right.

In particular

{\rm spec}(K_{\alpha\beta}^{\rm NH})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\sqrt{1-\frac{1}{\alpha\beta}}\left\{n+\frac{1}{2}\right\}_{n=0}^{\infty}&\alpha\beta>1,\\
{\rm spec}(K_{\alpha\beta}^{\rm NH})=[0,\infty)&\alpha\beta=1,\\
{\rm spec}(K_{\alpha\beta}^{\rm NH})={R}&0<\alpha\beta<1.\end{array}\right.

Proof.

Suppose that
\alpha\beta>1
.
We see that

K_{\alpha\beta}^{\rm NH}=\frac{1}{2}\left({\mathchoice{\rm 1\mskip-4.0mul}{\rm 1\mskip-4.0mul}{\rm 1\mskip-4.5mul}{\rm 1\mskip-5.0mul}}\otimes p+\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha\beta}}\sigma_{y}\otimes q\right)^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\left(1-\frac{1}{\alpha\beta}\right){\mathchoice{\rm 1\mskip-4.0mul}{\rm 1\mskip-4.0mul}{\rm 1\mskip-4.5mul}{\rm 1\mskip-5.0mul}}\otimes q^{2}.

By
{\iota}_{x}
in (
2.1
) we have

\displaystyle{\iota}_{x}K_{\alpha\beta}^{\rm NH}{\iota}_{x}^{-1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left({\mathchoice{\rm 1\mskip-4.0mul}{\rm 1\mskip-4.0mul}{\rm 1\mskip-4.5mul}{\rm 1\mskip-5.0mul}}\otimes p+\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha\beta}}\sigma_{z}\otimes q\right)^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\left(1-\frac{1}{\alpha\beta}\right){\mathchoice{\rm 1\mskip-4.0mul}{\rm 1\mskip-4.0mul}{\rm 1\mskip-4.5mul}{\rm 1\mskip-5.0mul}}\otimes q^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\begin{pmatrix}\left(p+\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha\beta}}q\right)^{2}+\left(1-\frac{1}{\alpha\beta}\right)q^{2}&0\\
0&\left(p-\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha\beta}}q\right)^{2}+\left(1-\frac{1}{\alpha\beta}\right)q^{2}\end{pmatrix}.

\left(p\pm\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha\beta}}q\right)^{2}+\left(1-\frac{1}{\alpha\beta}\right)q^{2}

is self-adjoint on
{\rm D}(p^{2}+q^{2})
by Proposition
2.1
. Then

K_{\alpha\beta}^{\rm NH}
is self-adjoint on
{\rm D}(p^{2}+q^{2})\oplus{\rm D}(p^{2}+q^{2})
.
Moreover

\displaystyle{\iota}_{x}K_{\alpha\beta}^{\rm NH}{\iota}_{x}^{-1}

\displaystyle\cong\frac{1}{2}\begin{pmatrix}p^{2}+\left(1-\frac{1}{\alpha\beta}\right)q^{2}&0\\
0&p^{2}+\left(1-\frac{1}{\alpha\beta}\right)q^{2}\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle\cong\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{1-\frac{1}{\alpha\beta}}\begin{pmatrix}p^{2}+q^{2}&0\\
0&p^{2}+q^{2}\end{pmatrix}

by Proposition
2.1
.
Then

{\rm spec}(K_{\alpha\beta}^{\rm NH})=\left\{\sqrt{1-\frac{1}{\alpha\beta}}\left(n+\frac{1}{2}\right)\right\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
.
Suppose that
\alpha\beta=1
.
Then

\displaystyle{\iota}_{x}K_{\alpha\beta}^{\rm NH}{\iota}_{x}^{-1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\begin{pmatrix}(p+q)^{2}&0\\
0&(p-q)^{2}\end{pmatrix}

and
K_{\alpha\beta}^{\rm NH}
is self-adjoint on
{\rm D}((p+q)^{2}\oplus(p-q)^{2})
with

{\rm spec}(K_{\alpha\beta}^{\rm NH})=[0,\infty)
by Proposition
2.1
.
Finally
suppose that
0<\alpha\beta<~1
.
By Proposition
2.1
again we see that
K_{\alpha\beta}^{\rm NH}
is essentially self-adjoint on
{\rm D}(p^{2}+q^{2})\oplus{\rm D}(p^{2}+q^{2})
.
Moreover we have

\displaystyle K_{\alpha\beta}^{\rm NH}

\displaystyle\cong\frac{1}{2}\begin{pmatrix}p^{2}+\left(1-\frac{1}{\alpha\beta}\right)q^{2}&0\\
0&p^{2}+\left(1-\frac{1}{\alpha\beta}\right)q^{2}\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle\cong\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\frac{1}{\alpha\beta}-1}\begin{pmatrix}p^{2}-q^{2}&0\\
0&p^{2}-q^{2}\end{pmatrix}.

Then

{\rm spec}(K_{\alpha\beta}^{\rm NH})={R}
.
∎

Lemma 2.6

(self-adjointness of
Q_{\alpha\beta}^{\rm NH}
)

(
\alpha\beta>1
)

Q_{\alpha\beta}^{\rm NH}
is self-adjoint on

{\rm D}(p^{2}+q^{2})\oplus{\rm D}(p^{2}+q^{2})
and
{\rm spec}(K_{\alpha\beta}^{\rm NH})
is purely discrete and

\displaystyle\left(n+\frac{1}{2}\right)\min\{\alpha,\beta\}\sqrt{1-\frac{1}{\alpha\beta}}\leq\lambda_{2n}\leq\lambda_{2n+1}\leq\left(n+\frac{1}{2}\right)\max\{\alpha,\beta\}\sqrt{1-\frac{1}{\alpha\beta}}

(2.10)

for
n\geq 0
.
In particular

\displaystyle\inf{\rm spec}(Q_{\alpha\beta}^{\rm NH})\geq\frac{\min\{\alpha,\beta\}}{2}\sqrt{1-\frac{1}{\alpha\beta}}.

(
\alpha\beta=1
)

Q_{\alpha\beta}^{\rm NH}
is self-adjoint on
{\rm D}((p+q)^{2}\oplus(p-q)^{2})
and

\inf{\rm spec}(Q_{\alpha\beta}^{\rm NH})=0.

(
0<\alpha\beta<1
)

Q_{\alpha\beta}^{\rm NH}
is essentially self-adjoint on
{\rm D}(p^{2}+q^{2})\oplus{\rm D}(p^{2}+q^{2})

and

\inf{\rm spec}(Q_{\alpha\beta}^{\rm NH})=-\infty.

Proof.

Suppose that
\alpha\beta>1
.
It can be seen that
A_{\alpha\beta}K_{\alpha\beta}^{\rm NH}A_{\alpha\beta}
is symmetric.
Let
g\in{\rm D}((A_{\alpha\beta}K_{\alpha\beta}^{\rm NH}A_{\alpha\beta})^{\ast})
and
f\in\mathcal{D}
. Then there exists
h
such that

(A_{\alpha\beta}K_{\alpha\beta}^{\rm NH}A_{\alpha\beta}f,g)=(f,h)
.
On the other hand

(K_{\alpha\beta}^{\rm NH}A_{\alpha\beta}f,A_{\alpha\beta}g)=(f,h)
.
Since

A_{\alpha\beta}\mathcal{D}=\mathcal{D}
,

(K_{\alpha\beta}^{\rm NH}A_{\alpha\beta}f,A_{\alpha\beta}g)=(A_{\alpha\beta}f,A_{\alpha\beta}^{-1}h)
implies that
A_{\alpha\beta}g\in{\rm D}(K_{\alpha\beta}^{\rm NH})
and

K_{\alpha\beta}^{\rm NH}A_{\alpha\beta}g=A_{\alpha\beta}^{-1}h
.
Hence
A_{\alpha\beta}K_{\alpha\beta}^{\rm NH}A_{\alpha\beta}g=h
and
g\in{\rm D}(A_{\alpha\beta}K_{\alpha\beta}^{\rm NH}A_{\alpha\beta})
.
Thus
{\rm D}(A_{\alpha\beta}K_{\alpha\beta}^{\rm NH}A_{\alpha\beta})={\rm D}((A_{\alpha\beta}K_{\alpha\beta}^{\rm NH}A_{\alpha\beta})^{\ast})
. Then
A_{\alpha\beta}K_{\alpha\beta}^{\rm NH}A_{\alpha\beta}
is self-adjoint
on
\mathcal{D}
.
Let
{\rm spec}(K_{\alpha\beta}^{\rm NH})=\{e_{n}\}
.
By the minmax principle
we have the
n
th eigenvalue of
Q_{\alpha\beta}^{\rm NH}
is given by

\displaystyle\lambda_{n}

\displaystyle=\sup_{f_{0},\ldots,f_{n}\in{\rm D}(Q_{\alpha\beta}^{\rm NH})}\inf_{f\in\{{\rm LH}[f_{0},\ldots,f_{n}]\}^{\perp}}\frac{(f,Q_{\alpha\beta}^{\rm NH}f)}{\|f\|^{2}}

\displaystyle=\sup_{f_{0},\ldots,f_{n}\in{\rm D}(A_{\alpha\beta}K_{\alpha\beta}^{\rm NH}A_{\alpha\beta})}\inf_{f\in\{{\rm LH}[f_{0},\ldots,f_{n}]\}^{\perp}}\frac{\|A_{\alpha\beta}f\|^{2}}{\|f\|^{2}}\frac{(A_{\alpha\beta}f,K_{\alpha\beta}^{\rm NH}A_{\alpha\beta}f)}{\|A_{\alpha\beta}f\|^{2}}\geq\min\{\alpha,\beta\}e_{n}.

Similarly we also have

\lambda_{n}\leq\max\{\alpha,\beta\}e_{n}
.
Since
e_{n}\to\infty
as
n\to\infty
,

\lambda_{n}\to\infty
as
n\to\infty
. Thus

{\rm spec}(Q_{\alpha\beta}^{\rm NH})
is purely discrete, and (
2.10
) follows.
Finally we estimate the lowest eigenvalue
\lambda_{0}
of
Q_{\alpha\beta}^{\rm NH}
.
Since

\lambda_{0}=\inf_{f\in{\rm D}(A_{\alpha\beta}K_{\alpha\beta}^{\rm NH}A_{\alpha\beta})}\frac{(A_{\alpha\beta}f,K_{\alpha\beta}^{\rm NH}A_{\alpha\beta}f)}{\|A_{\alpha\beta}f\|^{2}}\frac{\|A_{\alpha\beta}f\|^{2}}{\|f\|^{2}}\geq\min\{\alpha,\beta\}\inf{\rm spec}(K_{\alpha\beta}^{\rm NH}),

hence (
2.10
) follows.
Next suppose that
\alpha\beta=1
.
The self-adjointness can be proven in a similar way to the case of
\alpha\beta>1
.
Note that
A_{\alpha\beta}
is invertible. There exists
\{f_{n}\}
such that
(f_{n},K_{\alpha\beta}^{\rm NH}f_{n})\to 0
as
n\to\infty
.
Let
A_{\alpha\beta}^{-1}f_{n}=g_{n}
. Then

(g_{n},Q_{\alpha\beta}^{\rm NH}g_{n})\to 0
as
n\to\infty

and

\inf{\rm spec}(Q_{\alpha\beta}^{\rm NH})=0
follows.
Finally suppose that
0<\alpha\beta<1
. We also see that
there exists
f_{n}
such that
\|f_{n}\|=1
and

(f_{n},Q_{\alpha\beta}^{\rm NH}f_{n})=(A_{\alpha\beta}f_{n},K_{\alpha\beta}^{\rm NH}A_{\alpha\beta}f_{n})\to-\infty
as
n\to\infty
by Lemma
2.5
.
Then
\inf{\rm spec}(Q_{\alpha\beta}^{\rm NH})=-\infty
follows.
∎

2.4
Concavity of the lowest eigenvalue curves

In this section we show that the lowest eigenvalue curves of
H_{\triangle,g}^{\rm 2p}
,
H_{\triangle,g}^{\rm 1p}
and
Q_{\alpha\beta}^{\rm NH}
are
concave. More precisely, the lowest eigenvalue
E
of these models is a function of
the coupling constant
g
, and we shall show that
the function

g\mapsto E(g)

is concave.
For
H_{\triangle,g}^{\rm 1p}
this can be shown using Feynman-Kac formula (see Remark
2.11
),
but
for
H_{\triangle,g}^{\rm 2p}
and
Q_{\alpha\beta}^{\rm NH}
it is hard to show this in a similar manner to
H_{\triangle,g}^{\rm 1p}
.
We begin with demonstrating perturbative computations of the lowest eigenvalues of
H_{\triangle,g}^{\rm 2p}
,

H_{\triangle,g}^{\rm 1p}
and
Q_{\alpha\beta}^{\rm NH}
,
since these models are analytic family of type (A)
[
34
, p.16]
under certain conditions.
Consequently the lowest eigenvalues and their corresponding eigenvectors are analytic in
the coupling constant.
A similar result for
perturbative computations of the lowest eigenvalues of
Q_{\alpha\beta}^{\rm NH}
is given in
[
26
, Section 3.2]
.

An abstract procedure for the computation is as follows.
Let
H(0)
be a self-adjoint operator and
V
is symmetric.
Define
H(g)=H(0)+gV
for
g\in{R}
such that

H(g)
is an analytic family of type (A).
Set
\inf{\rm spec}(H(g))=E(g)
and
H(g)\Phi(g)=E(g)\Phi(g)
with
\|\Phi(g)\|=1
for any
g\in{R}
.
Suppose that
E(g)
is discrete and simple for any
g\in{R}
.
Then Kato-Rellich theorem yields that
E(g)
is analytic in
g
and
\Phi(g)
is also analytic in
g

[
34
, Theorem XII.8]
.
Suppose that
E(g)=E(-g)
and

H(0)\Phi_{0}=E(0)\Phi_{0}
.
Then we have

\displaystyle E^{(2)}(0)=-2(V\Phi_{0},(H(0)-E(0))^{-1}V\Phi_{0}),

(2.14)

where
E^{(n)}
and
\Phi^{(n)}
denote the
n
th derivative of
E
and
\Phi
, respectively.
This formula can be derived by considering
the eigenvalue equation

H(g)\Phi(g)=E(g)\Phi(g)
.
Note that
\Phi(0)=\Phi_{0}
.
We have

\displaystyle V\Phi(g)+(H(0)+gV)\Phi^{(1)}(g)=E^{(1)}(g)\Phi(g)+E(g)\Phi^{(1)}(g),

\displaystyle 2V\Phi^{(1)}(g)+(H(0)+gV)\Phi^{(2)}(g)=E^{(2)}(g)\Phi(g)+2E^{(1)}(g)\Phi^{(1)}(g)+E(g)\Phi^{(2)}(g).

By
E^{(1)}(0)=0
, it follows that
E^{(2)}(0)=(2V\Phi^{(1)}(0),\Phi_{0})
.
(
2.14
) follows from
\Phi^{(1)}(0)=-(H(0)-E(0))^{-1}V\Phi_{0}
.
This procedure can be extended straightforwardly as follows.
Set
\Phi(0)=\Phi
,
\Phi^{(n)}(0)=\Phi^{(n)}
,
E^{(n)}(0)=E^{(n)}

and
H(0)^{-1}=\frac{1}{K}
, and assume that
E(0)=E^{(2m+1)}(0)=0
for
m\geq 0
.
Then

\displaystyle nV\Phi^{(n-1)}+H(0)\Phi^{(n)}=\sum_{k=1}^{[n/2]}\binom{n}{2k}E^{(2k)}\Phi^{(n-2k)},

\displaystyle\Phi^{(n)}=\frac{1}{K}\left(-nV\Phi^{(n-1)}+\sum_{k=1}^{[n/2]}\binom{n}{2k}E^{(2k)}\Phi^{(n-2k)}\right).

The first several terms are given by

\displaystyle 2(V\Phi,\Phi^{(1)})=E^{(2)},

\displaystyle 3(V\Phi,\Phi^{(2)})=\binom{3}{2}E^{(2)}(\Phi,\Phi^{(1)}),

\displaystyle 4(V\Phi,\Phi^{(3)})=\binom{4}{2}E^{(2)}(\Phi,\Phi^{(2)})+E^{(4)}

and

\displaystyle\Phi^{(1)}=-\frac{1}{K}V\Phi,

\displaystyle\Phi^{(2)}=\frac{1}{K}(-2V\Phi^{(1)}+E^{(2)}\Phi)=\frac{1}{K}Y\Phi,

\displaystyle\Phi^{(3)}=\frac{1}{K}(-3V\Phi^{(2)}+3E^{(2)}\Phi^{(1)})=-3\frac{1}{K}V\frac{1}{K}Y\Phi-3E^{(2)}\frac{1}{K}\frac{1}{K}V\Phi,

where

Y=2V\frac{1}{K}V+E^{(2)}
.
Together with (
2.14
) we see that

\displaystyle E^{(2)}=-2(V\Phi,\frac{1}{k}V\Phi),

(2.15)

\displaystyle E^{(4)}=4(V\Phi,\Phi^{(3)})-6E^{(2)}(\Phi,\Phi^{(2)})=-6(Y\Phi,\frac{1}{K}Y\Phi)-12E^{(2)}\|\frac{1}{K}V\Phi\|^{2}.

(2.16)

Remark 2.8

We introduce notation
T_{\rm ren}=T-\langle T\rangle
, where
\langle T\rangle=(\Phi,T\Phi)
.
Then

\frac{1}{2!}E^{(2)}=-\langle V\frac{1}{K}V\rangle.

Notice that
V\frac{1}{K}V\Phi\not\in{\rm D}(\frac{1}{K})
but

(V\frac{1}{K}V)_{\rm ren}\Phi\in{\rm D}(\frac{1}{K})
. Thus

\frac{1}{4!}E^{(4)}=-\langle(V\frac{1}{K}V)_{\rm ren}\frac{1}{K}(V\frac{1}{K}V)_{\rm ren}\rangle+\langle V\frac{1}{K}V\rangle\langle V\frac{1}{K^{2}}V\rangle

is well defined.

2.4.1
2pQRM

We apply (
2.15
) and (
2.16
) to compute the Taylor expansion of the lowest eigenvalue of
H_{\triangle,g}^{\rm 2p}
.
We see that
H_{\triangle,g}^{\rm 2p}
is an analytic family of type (A) for
|g|<\frac{1}{2}
by Lemma
2.3
.
Let
e_{\triangle}^{\rm 2p}(g)
be the lowest eigenvalue of
H_{\triangle,g}^{\rm 2p}
.
We shall show that
e_{\triangle}^{\rm 2p}(g)
is simple in Corollary
4.2
.
At
g=0
we observe that
e^{\rm 2p}_{\triangle}(0)=\frac{1}{2}-\triangle
and
the expansion is given by

e_{0}^{\rm 2p}(g)=\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{1-4g^{2}}=\frac{1}{2}-g^{2}-g^{4}+\mathcal{O}(g^{5}).

Using (
2.15
) we can compute

{e_{\triangle}^{\rm 2p}}^{(2)}(0)
. Let
\Phi=\binom{0}{\varphi_{\rm g}}
. Then

H_{\triangle,0}^{\rm 2p}\Phi=(\frac{1}{2}-\triangle)\Phi
.
The result is

\displaystyle{e^{\rm 2p}_{\triangle}}^{(2)}(0)

\displaystyle=-2(\sigma_{x}\otimes(a^{2}+{a^{\ast}}^{2})\Phi,(\triangle\sigma_{z}\otimes{\mathchoice{\rm 1\mskip-4.0mul}{\rm 1\mskip-4.0mul}{\rm 1\mskip-4.5mul}{\rm 1\mskip-5.0mul}}+{\mathchoice{\rm 1\mskip-4.0mul}{\rm 1\mskip-4.0mul}{\rm 1\mskip-4.5mul}{\rm 1\mskip-5.0mul}}\otimes a^{\ast}a+\triangle)^{-1}\sigma_{x}\otimes(a^{2}+{a^{\ast}}^{2})\Phi)_{\mathcal{H}}

\displaystyle=-2\left(\binom{{a^{\ast}}^{2}\varphi_{\rm g}}{0},\begin{pmatrix}(a^{\ast}a+2\triangle)^{-1}&0\\
0&(a^{\ast}a)^{-1}\end{pmatrix}\binom{{a^{\ast}}^{2}\varphi_{\rm g}}{0}\right)_{\mathcal{H}}

\displaystyle=-2({a^{\ast}}^{2}\varphi_{\rm g},(a^{\ast}a+2\triangle)^{-1}{a^{\ast}}^{2}\varphi_{\rm g})_{{L^{2}({R})}}=-\frac{2}{2+2\triangle}\|{a^{\ast}}^{2}\varphi_{\rm g}\|_{{L^{2}({R})}}^{2}=-\frac{2}{1+\triangle}.

(2.17)

Similar result can be found in e.g.,
[
40
, (34)]
.
By setting

V=\sigma_{x}\otimes(a^{2}+{a^{\ast}}^{2})
,

E^{(2)}=\frac{-2}{1+\triangle}
,

\Phi=\binom{0}{\varphi_{\rm g}}

and

\frac{1}{k}=(\begin{smallmatrix}(a^{\ast}a+2\triangle)^{-1}&0\\
0&(a^{\ast}a)^{-1}\end{smallmatrix})
in (
2.16
),
we can compute
{e_{\triangle}^{\rm 2p}}^{(4)}(0)
.
Note that

\displaystyle 2\frac{1}{K}V\Phi=\frac{1}{1+\triangle}\binom{{a^{\ast}}^{2}\varphi_{\rm g}}{0},

\displaystyle 2V\frac{1}{K}V\Phi=\frac{1}{1+\triangle}\binom{0}{({a^{\ast}}^{4}+2)\varphi_{\rm g}},

\displaystyle Y\Phi=2V\frac{1}{K}V\Phi+E^{(2)}\Phi=\frac{1}{1+\triangle}\binom{0}{{a^{\ast}}^{4}\varphi_{\rm g}},

\displaystyle\frac{1}{K}Y\Phi=\frac{1}{4}\frac{1}{1+\triangle}\binom{0}{{a^{\ast}}^{4}\varphi_{\rm g}}.

Since
\|{a^{\ast}}^{2n}\Phi\|^{2}=n!
, we have

\displaystyle-12E^{(2)}\|\frac{1}{K}V\Phi\|^{2}=12\left(\frac{1}{1+\triangle}\right)^{3},

\displaystyle-6(Y\Phi,\frac{1}{K}Y\Phi)=-36\left(\frac{1}{1+\triangle}\right)^{2}.

Substituting these terms into (
2.16
) we can see that

\displaystyle{e^{\rm 2p}_{\triangle}}^{(4)}(0)=-36\left(\frac{1}{1+\triangle}\right)^{2}+12\left(\frac{1}{1+\triangle}\right)^{3}.

(2.18)

Lemma 2.9

We have

\displaystyle e^{\rm 2p}_{\triangle}(g)=\frac{1}{2}-\triangle-\frac{1}{1+\triangle}g^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{1}{1+\triangle}\right)^{2}\left(\frac{2+3\triangle}{1+\triangle}\right)g^{4}+\mathcal{O}(g^{5}).

(2.19)

Proof.

Since

e^{\rm 2p}_{\triangle}(g)=e^{\rm 2p}_{\triangle}(0)+\frac{1}{2!}{e^{\rm 2p}_{\triangle}}^{(2)}(0)g^{2}+\frac{1}{4!}{e^{\rm 2p}_{\triangle}}^{(4)}(0)g^{4}+\mathcal{O}(g^{5})
,
the lemma follows from (
2.17
) and (
2.18
).
∎

Theorem 2.10

(concavity of eigenvalue curves of
H_{\triangle,g}^{\rm 2p}
)

Both of the lowest eigenvalue curves
f_{1}:g\to e^{\rm 2p}_{\triangle}
and

f_{2}:g^{2}\to e^{\rm 2p}_{\triangle}
are concave for sufficiently small
|g|
.

Proof.

The coefficient of the
g^{2}
term of the expansion of

e^{\rm 2p}_{\triangle}
with respect to
g
is

-\frac{1}{1+\triangle}

by (
2.19
). Then
f_{1}
is concave.
Similarly
the coefficient of the
g^{4}
term of the expansion of

e^{\rm 2p}_{\triangle}
with respect to
g^{2}
is

-\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{1}{1+\triangle}\right)^{2}\left(\frac{2+3\triangle}{1+\triangle}\right)<~0
.
Then
f_{2}
is also
concave.
∎

2.4.3
NcHO

For
H_{\triangle,g}^{\rm 2p}
we could compute the lowest eigenvalues.
We can also obtain similar results for
Q_{\alpha\beta}^{\rm NH}
.
We have

Q_{\alpha\beta}^{\rm NH}\cong\frac{\alpha+\beta}{2}{\mathchoice{\rm 1\mskip-4.0mul}{\rm 1\mskip-4.0mul}{\rm 1\mskip-4.5mul}{\rm 1\mskip-5.0mul}}\otimes\left(a^{\ast}a+\frac{1}{2}\right)+\sigma_{z}\otimes\frac{1}{2}(a^{2}+{a^{\ast}}^{2})-\frac{\alpha-\beta}{2}\sigma_{y}\otimes\left(a^{\ast}a+\frac{1}{2}\right).

We fix
A=\frac{\alpha+\beta}{2}
and
g=\frac{\alpha-\beta}{2}
.
Hence

\displaystyle Q_{\alpha\beta}^{\rm NH}\cong\frac{1}{2}\begin{pmatrix}A_{-}p^{2}+A_{+}q^{2}&0\\
0&A_{+}p^{2}+A_{-}q^{2}\end{pmatrix}-\frac{1}{2}g\begin{pmatrix}0&-i(p^{2}+q^{2})\\
i(p^{2}+q^{2})&0\end{pmatrix}

with
A_{+}=A+1
and
A_{-}=A-1
.
We regard
A
as a constant and
g
a coupling constant.
Note that
Q_{\alpha\beta}^{\rm NH}
is an analytic family of type (A) for sufficiently small
|g|
by Lemma
2.6
.
Let
\lambda_{0}(g)
be the lowest eigenvalue of
Q_{\alpha\beta}^{\rm NH}
.
It is known that
\lambda_{0}(g)
is simple by
[
11
,
12
]
.
By (
2.13
) we see that
\lambda_{0}(g)=\lambda_{0}(-g)
and
it is represented as

\lambda_{0}(g)=\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{A^{2}-1}+\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{0}^{(2)}(0)g^{2}+\mathcal{O}(g^{3}).

Let
D_{\varepsilon}
be the dilation such that
D_{\varepsilon}f(x)=f(\varepsilon x)/\sqrt{\varepsilon}
.
Thus
D_{\varepsilon}qD_{\varepsilon}^{-1}=\varepsilon q
and

D_{\varepsilon}pD_{\varepsilon}^{-1}=~p/\varepsilon
, and

D_{\varepsilon}(p^{2}+q^{2})D_{\varepsilon}^{-1}=\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}p^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}q^{2}.

Set
D_{\varepsilon^{1/4}}\varphi_{\rm g}=\varphi_{\rm g}(\varepsilon)
. Hence
we have

\displaystyle(p^{2}+\varepsilon q^{2})\varphi_{\rm g}(\varepsilon)=\sqrt{\varepsilon}\varphi_{\rm g}(\varepsilon),

\displaystyle(p^{2}+q^{2})\varphi_{\rm g}(\varepsilon)=(\sqrt{\varepsilon}+(1-\varepsilon)q^{2})\varphi_{\rm g}(\varepsilon).

Then

\displaystyle((\alpha\mp 1)p^{2}+(\alpha\pm 1)q^{2})\varphi_{\rm g}(\frac{\alpha\pm 1}{\alpha\mp 1})=\sqrt{\alpha^{2}-1}\varphi_{\rm g}(\frac{\alpha\pm 1}{\alpha\mp 1}).

Define the map
\xi
on
[0,\infty)
by

\xi(u)=\pi^{-1/2}\left(e^{-|x|^{2}u/2},\left(1+\left(1-u\right)q^{2}\right)\left(p^{2}+q^{2}-1\right)^{-1}\left(1+\left(1-u\right)q^{2}\right)e^{-|x|^{2}u/2}\right).

Note that

\xi
can be expressed differently:

\xi(u)=\pi^{-1/2}\left(e^{-|x|^{2}/2},\left(1+\left(\frac{1}{u}-1\right)q^{2}\right)\left(p^{2}+\frac{1}{u^{2}}q^{2}-\frac{1}{u}\right)^{-1}\left(1+\left(\frac{1}{u}-1\right)q^{2}\right)e^{-|x|^{2}/2}\right).

Lemma 2.12

Recall that
A=\frac{\alpha+\beta}{2}
,
g=\frac{\beta-\alpha}{2}

and

A_{\pm}=A\pm 1
. Then
we have

\lambda_{0}(g)=\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{A^{2}-1}-\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\xi(A_{+}/A_{-})}{A_{-}}+\frac{\xi(A_{-}/A_{+})}{A_{+}}\right)g^{2}+\mathcal{O}(g^{3}).

Proof.

Let

\Phi=\binom{\varphi_{\rm g}(A_{+}/A_{-})}{\varphi_{\rm g}(A_{-}/A_{+})}=\binom{\Phi_{+}}{\Phi_{-}}.

Then

\frac{1}{2}\begin{pmatrix}A_{-}p^{2}+A_{+}q^{2}&0\\
0&A_{+}p^{2}+A_{-}q^{2}\end{pmatrix}\Phi=\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{A_{+}A_{-}}\Phi.

Simply we set

D_{+}=D_{(A_{+}/A_{-})^{1/4}}
and

D_{-}=D_{(A_{-}/A_{+})^{1/4}}
.
Then
D_{\pm}^{-1}=D_{\mp}
and

\Phi_{\pm}=D_{\pm}\varphi_{\rm g}
.
We have

\displaystyle(p^{2}+q^{2})\Phi_{\pm}=\left(\sqrt{\frac{A_{\pm}}{A_{\mp}}}+\left(1-\frac{A_{\pm}}{A_{\mp}}\right)q^{2}\right)\Phi_{\pm}.

(2.20)

By formula (
2.15
) we have

\displaystyle\lambda_{0}^{(2)}(0)=-2\left(\sigma_{y}h\Phi,\left(Ah+\sigma_{z}\frac{1}{2}(a^{2}+{a^{\ast}}^{2})-\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{A_{+}A_{-}}\right)^{-1}\sigma_{y}h\Phi\right)

\displaystyle=-4\left(\binom{-h\Phi_{-}}{h\Phi_{+}},\begin{pmatrix}\left(A_{-}p^{2}+A_{+}q^{2}-\sqrt{A_{+}A_{-}}\right)^{-1}&0\\
0&\left(A_{+}p^{2}+A_{-}q^{2}-\sqrt{A_{+}A_{-}}\right)^{-1}\end{pmatrix}\binom{-h\Phi_{-}}{h\Phi_{+}}\right)

\displaystyle=-(\Phi_{-},C_{-}\Phi_{-})-(\Phi_{+},C_{+}\Phi_{+}),

where
h=\frac{1}{2}(p^{2}+q^{2})

and

\displaystyle C_{\pm}

\displaystyle=\left(\sqrt{\frac{A_{\pm}}{A_{\mp}}}+\left(1-\frac{A_{\pm}}{A_{\mp}}\right)q^{2}\right)\left(A_{\pm}p^{2}+A_{\mp}q^{2}-\sqrt{A_{+}A_{-}}\right)^{-1}\left(\sqrt{\frac{A_{\pm}}{A_{\mp}}}+\left(1-\frac{A_{\pm}}{A_{\mp}}q^{2}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{A_{+}A_{-}}}\left(\sqrt{\frac{A_{\pm}}{A_{\mp}}}+\left(1-\frac{A_{\pm}}{A_{\mp}}\right)q^{2}\right)D_{\mp}\left(p^{2}+q^{2}-1\right)^{-1}D_{\mp}^{-1}\left(\sqrt{\frac{A_{\pm}}{A_{\mp}}}+\left(1-\frac{A_{\pm}}{A_{\mp}}q^{2}\right)\right).

Here we used
(
2.20
).
We also see that

\displaystyle D_{+}^{-1}\left(\sqrt{\frac{A_{-}}{A_{+}}}+\left(1-\frac{A_{-}}{A_{+}}\right)q^{2}\right)\Phi_{-}=\sqrt{\frac{A_{-}}{A_{+}}}\left(1+\left(1-\frac{A_{-}}{A_{+}}\right)q^{2}\right)D_{-}^{2}\varphi_{\rm g}

Hence

\displaystyle(\Phi_{-},C_{-}\Phi_{-})

\displaystyle={\frac{A_{-}}{A_{+}}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{A_{+}A_{-}}}\left(\left(1+\left(1-\frac{A_{-}}{A_{+}}\right)q^{2}\right)D_{-}^{2}\varphi_{\rm g},(p^{2}+q^{2}-1)^{-1}\left(1+\left(1-\frac{A_{-}}{A_{+}}\right)q^{2}\right)D_{-}^{2}\varphi_{\rm g}\right)

\displaystyle={\frac{1}{A_{+}}}\sqrt{\frac{A_{-}}{A_{+}}}\left(\left(1+\left(1-\frac{A_{-}}{A_{+}}\right)q^{2}\right)D_{-}^{2}\varphi_{\rm g},\left(p^{2}+q^{2}-1\right)^{-1}\left(1+\left(1-\frac{A_{-}}{A_{+}}\right)q^{2}\right)D_{-}^{2}\varphi_{\rm g}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(D_{-}^{2}\varphi_{\rm g},M_{-}D_{-}^{2}\varphi_{\rm g}\right),

and we can also see that

(\Phi_{+},C_{+}\Phi_{+})=\left(D_{+}^{2}\varphi_{\rm g},M_{+}D_{+}^{2}\varphi_{\rm g}\right)
.
Here

D_{\pm}^{2}\varphi_{\rm g}(x)=\pi^{-1/4}\exp\left(-\frac{|x|^{2}}{2}\frac{A_{\pm}}{A_{\mp}}\right)\left(\frac{A_{\mp}}{A_{\pm}}\right)^{1/4}

and

\displaystyle M_{\pm}=\frac{1}{A_{\mp}}\sqrt{\frac{A_{\pm}}{A_{\mp}}}\left(1+\left(1-\frac{A_{\pm}}{A_{\mp}}\right)q^{2}\right)\left(p^{2}+q^{2}-1\right)^{-1}\left(1+\left(1-\frac{A_{\pm}}{A_{\mp}}\right)q^{2}\right).

Then we obtain that

\lambda_{0}^{(2)}(0)=-\xi(A_{+}/A_{-})/A_{-}-\xi(A_{-}/A_{+})/A_{+}
and lemma follows.
∎

Theorem 2.13

(concavity of eigenvalue curves of
Q_{\alpha\beta}^{\rm NH}
)

The lowest eigenvalue curve
g\to\lambda_{0}(g)
is concave for sufficiently small
|g|
.

Proof.

Since

p^{2}+q^{2}-1
is a nonnegative self-adjoint operator,
we see that

\left(\left(1+\left(1-u\right)q^{2}\right)e^{-|x|^{2}u/2},\left(p^{2}+q^{2}-1\right)^{-1}\left(1+\left(1-u\right)q^{2}\right)e^{-|x|^{2}u/2}\right)>0

for any
u\in{R}
.
The coefficient of the
g^{2}
term of the expansion of

\lambda_{0}(g)
with respect to
g
is

-\frac{1}{2}(\xi(A_{+}/A_{-})/A_{-}+\xi(A_{-}/A_{+})/A_{+})<0
by Lemma
2.12
.
Then
the theorem follows.
∎

In this section we decompose

’s.

.

We have

\displaystyle K_{\alpha\beta}^{\rm NH}=({\iota}_{z}\otimes{\rm U})^{-1}H_{0,\frac{1}{2\sqrt{\alpha}\beta}}^{\rm 2p}({\iota}_{z}\otimes{\rm U}).

(3.1)

Then by

Proposition 3.1

is unitary on

unfortunately

\alpha=\beta

(2)
Suppose that

\displaystyle\kappa_{t}(y,x)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi(1-e^{-2t})}}\exp\left(-\frac{|y-e^{-t}x|^{2}}{1-e^{-2t}}\right).

(4.2)

Then

{E}^{x}_{\rm P}[f({\rm X}_{t})]=\int_{{R}}f(y)\kappa_{t}(y,x){\rm d}y
.

In this section we construct a Feynman-Kac formula of

In

e^{-tH_{\triangle,g}^{\rm 1p}}

[

]
.

e^{-tH_{\triangle,g}^{\rm 2p}}

(4.3)

\displaystyle{\rm J}H_{\triangle,g}^{\rm 2p}{\rm J}^{-1}=L_{\triangle,g},

Let

T_{s}=S_{\triangle s}=(-1)^{N_{\triangle s}}\sigma.

We define an
({R}\times{Z}_{2})
-valued stochastic process
(q_{t})_{t\geq 0}
on
\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{Y}

and
V({\rm q}_{s})

\displaystyle{\rm q}_{t}=({\rm X}_{t(1-2gT_{t})},T_{t}):\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{Y}\to{R}\times{Z}_{2},

\displaystyle V({\rm q}_{s})=(2{\rm X}_{s(1-2gT_{s})}^{2}-1)T_{s}.

\triangle
and
g

,

Theorem 4.1

(Feynman-Kac formulas)

Suppose that

. Then
we have

\displaystyle(f,e^{-tL_{\triangle,g}}g)_{L^{2}({R}\times{Z}_{2},{\rm d}\mu)}=2e^{\triangle t}e^{-\frac{t}{2}}{\bf E}\left[\overline{f({\rm q}_{0})}g({\rm q}_{t})e^{-g\int_{0}^{t}V({\rm q}_{s}){\rm d}s}\right],

Proof.

Let

\displaystyle\tilde{L}=({\mathchoice{\rm 1\mskip-4.0mul}{\rm 1\mskip-4.0mul}{\rm 1\mskip-4.5mul}{\rm 1\mskip-5.0mul}}-2g\sigma_{z})\otimes b^{\ast}b+g\sigma_{z}\otimes(2x^{2}-{\mathchoice{\rm 1\mskip-4.0mul}{\rm 1\mskip-4.0mul}{\rm 1\mskip-4.5mul}{\rm 1\mskip-5.0mul}})+\frac{1}{2}.

(4.5)

Under the identification
\mathcal{H}\cong L^{2}({R}\times{Z}_{2},{\rm d}\mu)
,
we can see that

(\tilde{L}f)(x,\sigma)=(1-2g\sigma)(b^{\ast}bf)(x,\sigma)+g\sigma(2x^{2}-1)f(x,\sigma)+\frac{1}{2}f(x,\sigma),\quad(x,\sigma)\in{R}\times{Z}_{2}.

By the Trotter-Kato product formula
[
41
,
20
]
we see that

e^{-tL_{\triangle,g}}=s-\lim_{n\to\infty}\left(e^{\frac{t}{n}\triangle\sigma_{x}}e^{-\frac{t}{n}\tilde{L}}\right)^{n}.

Since we observe that

together with them we have

By the Markov properties of
({\rm X}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
and
(N_{t})_{t\geq 0}

\displaystyle(e^{\frac{t}{n}\triangle\sigma_{x}}e^{-\frac{t}{n}\tilde{L}}e^{\frac{t}{n}\triangle\sigma_{x}}e^{-\frac{t}{n}\tilde{L}}f)(x,\sigma)

\displaystyle=e^{\frac{2t}{n}\triangle}e^{-\frac{2t}{n}\frac{1}{2}}{E}_{\Pi}^{\sigma}{E}_{{\rm P}}^{x}\left[e^{-g\int_{0}^{\frac{t}{n}}(2X^{2}_{(1-2gS_{\frac{t}{n}\triangle})s}-1)S_{\frac{t}{n}\triangle}{\rm d}s}\right.

\displaystyle\hskip 85.35826pt\times\left.{E}_{\Pi}^{S_{\frac{t}{n}\triangle}}{E}_{{\rm P}}^{{\rm X}_{(1-2gS_{\frac{t}{n}\triangle})\frac{t}{n}}}\left[e^{-g\int_{0}^{\frac{t}{n}}(2X^{2}_{(1-2gS_{\frac{t}{n}\triangle})s}-1)S_{\frac{t}{n}\triangle}{\rm d}s}f({\rm q}_{\frac{t}{n}})\right]\right]

\displaystyle=e^{\frac{2t}{n}\triangle}e^{-\frac{2t}{n}\frac{1}{2}}{E}_{\Pi}^{\sigma}{E}_{{\rm P}}^{x}\left[e^{-g\int_{0}^{\frac{t}{n}}(2X^{2}_{(1-2gS_{\frac{t}{n}\triangle})s}-1)S_{\frac{t}{n}\triangle}{\rm d}s}e^{-g\int_{0}^{\frac{t}{n}}(2X^{2}_{(1-2gS_{\frac{2t}{n}\triangle})s+(1-2gS_{\frac{2t}{n}\triangle})\frac{t}{n}}-1)S_{\frac{2t}{n}\triangle}{\rm d}s}f({\rm q}_{\frac{2t}{n}})\right]

\displaystyle=e^{\frac{2t}{n}\triangle}e^{-\frac{2t}{n}\frac{1}{2}}{E}_{\Pi}^{\sigma}{E}_{{\rm P}}^{x}\left[e^{-g\int_{0}^{\frac{t}{n}}(2X^{2}_{(1-2gS_{\frac{t}{n}\triangle})s}-1)S_{\frac{t}{n}\triangle}{\rm d}s}e^{-g\int_{\frac{t}{n}}^{\frac{2t}{n}}(2X^{2}_{(1-2gS_{\frac{2t}{n}\triangle})s}-1)S_{\frac{2t}{n}\triangle}{\rm d}s}f({\rm q}_{\frac{2t}{n}})\right].

\displaystyle\left(\left(e^{\frac{t}{n}\triangle\sigma_{x}}e^{-\frac{t}{n}\tilde{L}}\right)^{n}f\right)(x,\sigma)=e^{t\triangle}e^{-\frac{t}{2}}{E}_{\Pi}^{\sigma}{E}_{{\rm P}}^{x}\left[e^{-g\sum_{j=1}^{n}\int_{\frac{t(j-1)}{n}}^{\frac{tj}{n}}(2X^{2}_{(1-2gS_{\frac{tj}{n}\triangle})s}-1)S_{\frac{tj}{n}\triangle}{\rm d}s}f({\rm q}_{t})\right].

As

We can see that

\displaystyle(g,e^{-tL_{\triangle,g}}f)

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to\infty}e^{t\triangle}e^{-\frac{t}{2}}2{\mathbf{E}}\left[\bar{g}({\rm q}_{0})e^{-g\sum_{j=1}^{n}\int_{\frac{t(j-1)}{n}}^{\frac{tj}{n}}(2X^{2}_{(1-2gS_{\frac{tj}{n}\triangle})s}-1)S_{\frac{tj}{n}\triangle}{\rm d}s}f({\rm q}_{t})\right]

\displaystyle=e^{t\triangle}e^{-\frac{t}{2}}2{\mathbf{E}}\left[\bar{g}({\rm q}_{0})e^{-g\int_{0}^{t}(2X^{2}_{(1-2gS_{s\triangle})s}-1)S_{s\triangle}{\rm d}s}f({\rm q}_{t})\right].

Then the proof of the first statement is complete. The second statement follows from

{\rm J}H_{\triangle,g}^{\rm 2p}{\rm J}^{-1}=L_{\triangle,g}
.

f\in L^{2}({R}\times{Z}_{2},{\rm d}\mu)

f\geq 0
almost everywhere.
We say
f\in L^{2}({R}\times{Z}_{2},{\rm d}\mu)

almost everywhere.
We denote the set of nonnegative functions by
\mathcal{P}
,
and that of positive functions by
\c{\rP}_{+}
.
A bounded operator

on

is positivity preserving if and only if

and
positivity improving if and only if

.

(uniqueness of the ground state)

4.3

H_{\triangle,g}^{\rm 2p}

Let
{\rm spec}(H_{\triangle,g}^{\rm 2p})=\{\mu_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
.
From a number-theoretical perspective, special attention is given to studying the spectral zeta function, defined as

\zeta_{\rm 2p}(s)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\mu_{n}^{s}}.

In
[
38
,
13
,
36
]
the spectral zeta function of 1pQRM is studied.

]

The eigenvalues of

\triangle

We have

\displaystyle\mu_{2m}(0,\triangle)=m+\frac{1}{2}-\triangle,

\displaystyle\mu_{2m}(g,0)=\mu_{2m+1}(g,0)=\sqrt{1-4g^{2}}\left(m+\frac{1}{2}\right).

Notice that

We have

\displaystyle\|L_{\triangle,g}^{-1}\phi\|\leq\left(1+\frac{\triangle}{\mu_{g}-\triangle}\right)\|L_{0,g}^{-1}\phi\|.

\|\overline{(p+tq)^{2}+sq^{2}}f_{n}-\overline{(p+tq)^{2}+sq^{2}}f_{m}\|\leq a\|h(f_{n}-f_{m})\|+b\|f_{n}-f_{m}\|\to 0

as
n,m\to\infty
with some
a,b\geq 0
,
we see that
\mathcal{U}_{t}f\in{\rm D}(p^{2}+q^{2})
and

\mathcal{U}_{t}^{-1}(p^{2}+sq^{2})\mathcal{U}_{t}f=\overline{(p+tq)^{2}+sq^{2}}f

by the closedness of
p^{2}+sq^{2}
, but

\overline{(p+tq)^{2}+sq^{2}}={(p+tq)^{2}+sq^{2}}
on
{\rm D}(p^{2}+q^{2})
.
Then the lemma follows.
∎

Theorem A.5

Suppose that
t\in{R}
and
s>0
. Then

{(p+tq)^{2}+sq^{2}}
is self-adjoint on
{\rm D}(p^{2}+q^{2})
.

Proof.

By Lemma
A.4
, we have

\mathcal{U}_{t}^{-1}(p^{2}+sq^{2})\mathcal{U}_{t}={(p+tq)^{2}+sq^{2}}
on
\mathcal{U}_{t}^{-1}{\rm D}(p^{2}+q^{2})
.
The left hand side is self-adjoint on
\mathcal{U}^{-1}{\rm D}(p^{2}+q^{2})

but

\mathcal{U}_{t}^{-1}{\rm D}(p^{2}+q^{2})={\rm D}(p^{2}+q^{2})
.
Then
the right-hand side is also self-adjoint on
{\rm D}(p^{2}+q^{2})
.
∎

Appendix B
Spectral zeta function of
Q_{\alpha\beta}^{\rm NH}

Let
{\rm spec}(Q_{\alpha\beta}^{\rm NH})=\{\lambda_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
.
Each eigenvalue
\lambda_{n}
depends on
\alpha
and
\beta
:

\lambda_{n}=\lambda_{n}(\alpha,\beta)
.
For each
\alpha,\beta>0

the spectral zeta function
\zeta_{\rm NH}
of
Q_{\alpha\beta}^{\rm NH}
is defined by

\zeta_{\rm NH}(s)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\lambda_{n}^{s}}.

In the case of
\alpha=\beta
, we see that
\lambda_{2n}=\lambda_{2n+1}=\sqrt{\alpha^{2}-1}\left(n+\frac{1}{2}\right)
,
n\geq 0
, and
\zeta_{\rm NH}
is given by

\zeta_{\rm NH}(s)=2(\alpha^{2}-1)^{-s/2}\zeta\left(s;\frac{1}{2}\right).

We consider the asymptotic behavior of
the spectral zeta function
\zeta_{\rm NH}
for
\alpha\neq\beta

as
\beta\to\alpha
.

Theorem B.1

Suppose that
\alpha
is sufficiently large.
Then

\lim_{\beta\to\alpha}\zeta_{\rm NH}(s)=2({\alpha^{2}-1})^{s/2}\zeta\left(s;\frac{1}{2}\right).

Proof.

Let
z\in{C}
with
\,\mathrm{Im}z\neq 0
.
Since
\alpha
is sufficiently large,

\left(a^{\ast}a+\frac{1}{2}\right)(Q_{\alpha,\alpha}^{\rm HN}-z)^{-1}
is bounded.
Then the operator norm of

(Q_{\alpha\beta}^{\rm NH}-z)^{-1}-(Q_{\alpha,\alpha}^{\rm HN}-z)^{-1}
can be estimated as

\displaystyle\|(Q_{\alpha\beta}^{\rm NH}-z)^{-1}-(Q_{\alpha,\alpha}^{\rm HN}-z)^{-1}\|

\displaystyle=|\beta-\alpha|\left\|(Q_{\alpha\beta}^{\rm NH}-z)^{-1}\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}\left(a^{\ast}a+\frac{1}{2}\right)(Q_{\alpha,\alpha}^{\rm HN}-z)^{-1}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{|\beta-\alpha|}{|\,\mathrm{Im}z|}\left\|\left(a^{\ast}a+\frac{1}{2}\right)(Q_{\alpha,\alpha}^{\rm HN}-z)^{-1}\right\|\to 0

as
\beta\to\alpha
.
Then
\lim_{\beta\to\alpha}\lambda_{n}(\alpha,\beta)=\lambda_{n}(\alpha,\alpha)
for each
n
.
Since

(n+\frac{1}{2})\min\{\alpha,\beta\}\sqrt{1-\frac{1}{\alpha\beta}}\leq\lambda_{2n}\leq\lambda_{2n+1}
by Lemma
2.6
, by the Lebesgue dominated convergence theorem
we have

\displaystyle\lim_{\beta\to\alpha}\zeta_{\rm NH}(s)=\lim_{\beta\to\alpha}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\lambda_{n}(\alpha,\beta)^{s}}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\lambda_{n}(\alpha,\alpha)^{s}}=2({\alpha^{2}-1})^{-s/2}\zeta\left(s;\frac{1}{2}\right).

∎

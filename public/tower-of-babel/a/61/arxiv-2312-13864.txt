Orbits of Jacobi forms and Theta relations
Valery Gritsenko, Haowu Wang

Abstract
. Jacobi theta functions with rational characteristics can be viewed as vector-valued Jacobi forms. Theta relations usually correspond to different constructions of certain Jacobi forms. From this observation, we extract a new approach, which is called orbits of Jacobi forms, to produce identities on Jacobi theta functions. Our approach not only provides simple proofs of many known theta relations but also produces a large number of new identities, which can be considered as generalizations of Riemann’s theta relations.

11F50, 14K25

1.
Introduction

The Jacobi theta function with rational characteristic defined as

\vartheta_{a,b}(\tau,z)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}e^{\pi i(n+a)^{2}\tau+2\pi i(n+a)(z+b)}

is a holomorphic function on
\mathbb{H}\times\mathbb{C}
and plays a vital role in many areas
[
11
]
, including the constructions of modular forms, the research of Abelian varieties and moduli spaces, and the theory of quadratic forms. There are a lot of interesting and useful identities on Jacobi theta functions, which are called theta relations. In this paper we introduce a new method to construct theta relations. Our approach is based on the theory of Jacobi forms.

Jacobi forms
[
3
]
are holomorphic functions on
\mathbb{H}\times\mathbb{C}
which are modular in the first variable
\tau\in\mathbb{H}
and quasi doubly-periodic in the second variable
z\in\mathbb{C}
. The automorphic group of Jacobi forms is the Jacobi group which is the semidirect product of
\mathop{\mathrm{SL}}\nolimits_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
with the integral Heisenberg group. In particular, the real Heisenberg group acts on a Jacobi form of weight
k
and index
t
via

\left(\varphi\lvert_{k,t}[x,y;r]\right)(\tau,z)=e^{2\pi it(x^{2}\tau+2xz+xy+r)}\varphi(\tau,z+x\tau+y).

It is known
[
8
]
that the Jacobi triple product formula

\vartheta(\tau,z)=q^{\frac{1}{8}}(\zeta^{\frac{1}{2}}-\zeta^{-\frac{1}{2}})\prod_{n\geq 1}(1-q^{n}\zeta)(1-q^{n}\zeta^{-1})(1-q^{n})

defines a holomorphic Jacobi form of weight
\frac{1}{2}
and index
\frac{1}{2}
with a
multiplier system of order
8
, where
q=e^{2\pi i\tau}
and
\zeta=e^{2\pi iz}
. All Jacobi
theta functions with rational characteristics can be expressed by
\vartheta
. More precisely,

\vartheta_{a+\frac{1}{2},b+\frac{1}{2}}
equals
\vartheta\lvert_{\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}}[a,b;0]
up to a constant factor. From which we observe that Jacobi theta functions with certain characteristics constitute a vector-valued function. We analyze in §
2
the
behavior of this vector with respect to the full Jacobi modular group. Note that the
quadratic form in the Jacobi theta functions is the odd form
x^{2}
.
Theta relations should come from different constructions of some Jacobi forms in terms of the vectors of theta-functions with characteristics. We next give an accurate description of this observation. We first embed the set

R_{N}:=\left(\frac{1}{N},\frac{1}{N}\right)\cdot\mathop{\mathrm{SL}}\nolimits_{2}(\mathbb{Z})/\mathbb{Z}^{2}=\left\{\left(\frac{u}{N},\frac{v}{N}\right)\in\frac{1}{N}\mathbb{Z}^{2}:0\leq u,v<N,\,(u,v)\neq(0,0)\right\}

in the rational Heisenberg group.
Then the vector-valued function
(\vartheta\lvert_{\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}}Y)_{Y\in R_{N}}
, which is called
the orbit
of
\vartheta
, transforms like a vector-valued Jacobi form with additional non-regular correcting factors (see (
3.3
), (
3.4
)). Therefore, we are able to construct Jacobi forms by taking special combinations of vector-valued functions
(\vartheta\lvert_{\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}}Y)(\tau,0)
and
(\vartheta\lvert_{\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}}Y)(\tau,mz)
with
m\geq 1
.
A general example of such combinations is given below.

Theorem

(see Theorem
4.4
)
.

Let
N\geq 2
and
a
,
b
,
c
,
d
be non-negative integers satisfying that
N\lvert(b+2c)
and
a+b+c+d\equiv 0\operatorname{mod}2N
. Then the function

\operatorname{Tr}_{a,b,c,d}^{(N)}:=\sum_{Y\in R_{N}}(\vartheta\lvert Y)^{a}(\tau,0)(\vartheta\lvert Y)^{b}(\tau,z)(\vartheta\lvert Y)^{c}(\tau,2z)(\vartheta\lvert Y)^{d}(\tau,Nz)

is a holomorphic Jacobi form of weight
\frac{a+b+c+d}{2}
and index
\frac{b+4c+N^{2}d}{2}
with a character of finite order.

Given parameters, if
\operatorname{Tr}_{a,b,c,d}^{(N)}=0
then we get a theta relation. If
\operatorname{Tr}_{a,b,c,d}^{(N)}\neq 0
, then it is possible to find basic Jacobi forms, such as Jacobi–Eisenstein series or nice generators of a certain space of Jacobi forms, to represent
\operatorname{Tr}_{a,b,c,d}^{(N)}
, because the corresponding space of Jacobi forms is finite dimensional. In this case, we obtain a generalized theta relation. By applying the above theorem to different parameters, more than fifty identities are derived (see Corollaries
4.5
,
4.6
,
4.7
).

In section
4
we also establish formulas to represent holomorphic Jacobi forms of weight
6
and weak Jacobi forms of weight
0
in terms of Jacobi theta functions of order
2
(see Proposition
4.8
and Corollary
4.11
).

The paper is organized as follows. In section
2
, we recall some basics on Jacobi forms of integral and half-integral index, as well as Jacobi theta functions. We also explain the action of the real Heisenberg group on Jacobi forms. Section
3
is devoted to the introduction of orbits of Jacobi forms. In section
4
, we apply the technique of orbits of
\vartheta
to the constructions of Jacobi forms and of theta relations. In section
5
, we present several applications of our formulas.

2.
Jacobi forms and Jacobi theta functions

2.1.
Definition of Jacobi forms

In this subsection we introduce Jacobi forms of integral and half-integral index. We refer to
[
3
,
7
,
8
]
for more details.

Jacobi forms appear naturally as Fourier–Jacobi coefficients of Siegel modular forms of genus 2. In what follows we use this fact to explain the action of the real Jacobi group on the space of holomorphic functions on
\mathbb{H}\times\mathbb{C}
. Let

\mathbb{H}_{2}=\left\{Z\in M_{2}(\mathbb{C}):Z={Z^{t}},\;\mathrm{Im}(Z)>0\right\}

be the Siegel upper-half space of genus 2, and let
\mathop{\mathrm{Sp}}\nolimits_{2}(\mathbb{R})
be the real symplectic group of rank 2. We define the standard slash operator
\lvert_{k}
(
k\in\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{Z}
) on the space of functions on
\mathbb{H}_{2}
as

(F\lvert_{k}M)(Z):=\det(CZ+D)^{-k}F(M{\langle{Z}\rangle})

where

M=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}A&B\\
C&D\end{array}\right)\in\mathop{\mathrm{Sp}}\nolimits_{2}(\mathbb{R})\quad\text{and}\quad M{\langle{Z}\rangle}:=(AZ+B)(CZ+D)^{-1}.

Let us consider the real Jacobi group
\Gamma^{J}(\mathbb{R})=\Gamma_{\infty}(\mathbb{R})/\{\pm I_{4}\}
, where
\Gamma_{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
is a maximal parabolic subgroup of the real symplectic group defined by

\Gamma_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}):=\left\{\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}*&0&*&*\\
&*&*&*\\
&0&*&*\\
0&0&0&*\end{array}\right)\in\mathop{\mathrm{Sp}}\nolimits_{2}(\mathbb{R})\right\}.

The homomorphism

(2.1)

A=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}a&b\\
c&d\end{array}\right)\in\mathop{\mathrm{SL}}\nolimits_{2}(\mathbb{R})\longmapsto\{A\}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}a&0&b&0\\
0&1&0&0\\
c&0&d&0\\
0&0&0&1\end{array}\right)\in\Gamma^{J}(\mathbb{R})

defines an embedding of
\mathop{\mathrm{SL}}\nolimits_{2}(\mathbb{R})
into
\Gamma^{J}(\mathbb{R})
.
The second standard subgroup of
\Gamma^{J}(\mathbb{R})
is the real Heisenberg group

H(\mathbb{R}):=\{[x,y;r]:x,y,r\in\mathbb{R}\}

where

(2.2)

[x,y;r]=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}1&0&0&y\\
x&1&y&r\\
0&0&1&-x\\
0&0&0&1\end{array}\right).

The multiplication in
H(\mathbb{R})
is given by

(2.3)

[x_{1},y_{1};r_{1}]\cdot[x_{2},y_{2};r_{2}]=[x_{1}+x_{2},y_{1}+y_{2};r_{1}+r_{2}+x_{1}y_{2}-x_{2}y_{1}].

The group
\mathop{\mathrm{SL}}\nolimits_{2}(\mathbb{R})
acts on
H(\mathbb{R})
via

(2.4)

A\cdot[x,y;r]=\{A\}[x,y;r]\{A^{-1}\}=[(x,y)A^{-1};r]=[dx-cy,ay-bx;r].

The real Jacobi group
\Gamma^{J}(\mathbb{R})
is the semidirect product
\mathop{\mathrm{SL}}\nolimits_{2}(\mathbb{R})\ltimes H(\mathbb{R})
with the multiplication

(2.5)

(A,h_{1})\cdot(B,h_{2})=(AB,(B^{-1}\cdot h_{1})\cdot h_{2}).

Let
\Gamma^{J}(\mathbb{Z})=\mathop{\mathrm{SL}}\nolimits_{2}(\mathbb{Z})\ltimes H(\mathbb{Z})
be the integral Jacobi group, where

H(\mathbb{Z}):=\{[x,y;r]:x,y,r\in\mathbb{Z}\}

is the integral Heisenberg group.
We describe the characters of the integral Jacobi group. Let
\chi:\Gamma^{J}(\mathbb{Z})\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{\times}
be a character (or a multiplier system) of finite order. By
[
2
]
, its restriction to
\mathop{\mathrm{SL}}\nolimits_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
is
\upsilon_{\eta}^{D}
, where
D\in\mathbb{N}
and
\upsilon_{\eta}
is the multiplier system (or a projective character) of order
24
of the Dedekind
\eta
-function

\eta(\tau)=q^{\frac{1}{24}}\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}(1-q^{n}).

Moreover, one has

(2.6)

\chi(\{A\}\cdot[x,y;r])=\upsilon_{\eta}^{D}(A)\cdot\upsilon_{H}^{\varepsilon}([x,y;r]),

where
\varepsilon=0,1
and
\upsilon_{H}
is the unique binary character of
H(\mathbb{Z})
defined as

(2.7)

\upsilon_{H}([x,y;r])=(-1)^{x+y+xy+r}.

We now introduce the action of the Jacobi group on the space of functions on
\mathbb{H}\times\mathbb{C}
. Fix

Z=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\tau&z\\
z&\omega\end{array}\right)\in\mathbb{H}_{2},\quad q=e^{2\pi i\tau},\quad\zeta=e^{2\pi iz}.

Let
k\in\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{Z}
and
t\in\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{N}
. Assume that

\varphi(\tau,z)
is a holomorphic function on
\mathbb{H}\times\mathbb{C}
. Then

(2.8)

\widetilde{\varphi}(Z):=\varphi(\tau,z)\exp(2\pi itw)

is a holomorphic function on
\mathbb{H}_{2}
. The action of the Jacobi group
\Gamma^{J}(\mathbb{R})
on
\varphi(\tau,z)
is defined as

(2.9)

(\varphi\lvert_{k,t}g)(\tau,z):=(\widetilde{\varphi}\lvert_{k}g)(Z)\exp(-2\pi it\omega).

In particular, the generators of
\Gamma^{J}(\mathbb{R})
act on
\varphi(\tau,z)
via

(2.10)

\displaystyle\big(\varphi\lvert_{k,t}\{A\}\big)(\tau,z)

\displaystyle=(c\tau+d)^{-k}e^{-2\pi it\tfrac{cz^{2}}{c\tau+d}}\varphi\left(\frac{a\tau+b}{c\tau+d},\frac{z}{c\tau+d}\right),

(2.11)

\displaystyle\big(\varphi\lvert_{k,t}[x,y;r]\big)(\tau,z)

\displaystyle=e^{2\pi it(x^{2}\tau+2xz+xy+r)}\varphi(\tau,z+x\tau+y).

Definition 2.1
.

Let
k\in\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{Z}
and
t\in\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{N}
. A holomorphic function
\varphi(\tau,z)
on
\mathbb{H}\times\mathbb{C}
is called a
weak Jacobi form of weight
k
and index
t
with a multiplier system (or a character)
\chi:\Gamma^{J}(\mathbb{Z})\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{\times}
if it satisfies the functional equation

(\varphi\lvert_{k,t}g)(\tau,z)=\chi(g)\varphi(\tau,z),\quad\forall g\in\Gamma^{J}(\mathbb{Z}),

and if it has a Fourier expansion of the form

\varphi(\tau,z)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\geq 0,\,n\equiv\frac{D}{24}\operatorname{mod}\mathbb{Z}\\
l\in\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{Z}\end{subarray}}f(n,l)\exp\big(2\pi i(n\tau+lz)\big),

where
0\leq D<24
is given by
\chi\lvert_{\mathop{\mathrm{SL}}\nolimits_{2}(\mathbb{Z})}=\upsilon_{\eta}^{D}
.
If
\varphi
further satisfies the condition

f(n,l)\neq 0\Longrightarrow 4nt-l^{2}\geq 0

then it is called a
holomorphic Jacobi form
. If
\varphi
further satisfies the stronger condition

f(n,l)\neq 0\Longrightarrow 4nt-l^{2}>0

then it is called a
Jacobi cusp form
.

We denote by
J^{\mathrm{w}}_{k,t}(\chi)
the finite dimensional vector space of all weak Jacobi forms of weight
k
, index
t
and character
\chi
. The corresponding spaces of holomorphic Jacobi forms and Jacobi cusp forms are denoted by
J_{k,t}(\chi)
and
J^{\mathrm{cusp}}_{k,t}(\chi)
, respectively. If the character is trivial, we also write
J_{k,t}=J_{k,t}(1)
for short.

We remark that if
\varphi\in J_{k,t}^{\mathrm{w}}(\chi)
is nonzero then
\chi=\upsilon_{\eta}^{D}\cdot\upsilon_{H}^{2t}
. By definition, we have

J^{\mathrm{w}}_{k,t}(\upsilon_{\eta}^{D}\cdot\upsilon_{H}^{2t})=\eta^{D}\cdot J^{\mathrm{w}}_{k-\frac{D}{2},t}(\upsilon_{H}^{2t}),\quad\text{and}\quad k-D/2\in\mathbb{Z}.

2.2.
Jacobi theta functions

Let
(a,b)\in(\mathbb{Q}^{2}\backslash\mathbb{Z}^{2})\cup\{(0,0)\}
. The Jacobi theta function with rational characteristic
(a,b)
(see
[
11
, Chapter 1]
) is defined as

(2.12)

\vartheta_{a,b}(\tau,z)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}e^{\pi i(n+a)^{2}\tau+2\pi i(n+a)(z+b)}.

It is a holomorphic function on
\mathbb{H}\times\mathbb{C}
. We set

\theta_{a,b}(\tau)=\vartheta_{a,b}(\tau,0).

In the notation, the classical Jacobi theta functions of order
2
have the following form

\displaystyle\vartheta_{00}=\vartheta_{0,0}

\displaystyle\vartheta_{01}=\vartheta_{0,\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\vartheta_{10}=\vartheta_{\frac{1}{2},0}

\displaystyle\vartheta_{11}=\vartheta_{\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\theta_{00}=\theta_{0,0}

\displaystyle\theta_{01}=\theta_{0,\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\theta_{10}=\theta_{\frac{1}{2},0}

For
ab=00
,
01
,
10
we also put

\xi_{ab}(\tau,z)=\vartheta_{ab}(\tau,z)/\theta_{ab}(\tau).

Let
q=e^{2\pi i\tau}
and
\zeta=e^{2\pi iz}
. The Jacobi triple product formula

(2.13)

\vartheta(\tau,z)=q^{\frac{1}{8}}(\zeta^{\frac{1}{2}}-\zeta^{-\frac{1}{2}})\prod_{n\geq 1}(1-q^{n}\zeta)(1-q^{n}\zeta^{-1})(1-q^{n})

defines a holomorphic Jacobi form of weight
\frac{1}{2}
and index
\frac{1}{2}

with a multiplier system of order
8
, that is,
\vartheta\in J_{\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}}(\upsilon_{\eta}^{3}\cdot\upsilon_{H})
(see
[
8
]
). Note that
\vartheta(\tau,z)=-i\vartheta_{11}(\tau,z)
and it vanishes precisely on

\{(\tau,z)\in\mathbb{H}\times\mathbb{C}:z\in\mathbb{Z}\tau+\mathbb{Z}\}.

Moreover, these zeros are all simple.

The Jacobi theta functions with rational characteristics can be expressed by means of
\vartheta
.

(2.14)

\displaystyle\vartheta(\tau,z+a\tau+b)=

\displaystyle\exp\Big(-\pi i\big(a^{2}\tau+2az+2ab+a+\frac{1}{2}\big)\Big)\vartheta_{a+\frac{1}{2},b+\frac{1}{2}}(\tau,z),

(2.15)

\displaystyle\Big(\vartheta\lvert_{\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}}[a,b;0]\Big)(\tau,z)=

\displaystyle\exp\Big(-\pi i\big(ab+a+\frac{1}{2}\big)\Big)\vartheta_{a+\frac{1}{2},b+\frac{1}{2}}(\tau,z),

(2.16)

\displaystyle\vartheta_{a+x,b+y}(\tau,z)=

\displaystyle\exp\bigl(2\pi iay\bigr)\vartheta_{a,b}(\tau,z),\quad\forall\,x,y\in\mathbb{Z}.

2.3.
The space of Jacobi forms

For convenience, we introduce some notation of modular forms and Jacobi forms. The function
E_{2k}=1+O(q)
denotes the
\mathop{\mathrm{SL}}\nolimits_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
-Eisenstein series of weight
2k
for
k\geq 1
. The function
\Delta=\eta^{24}
is the cusp form of weight 12 on
\mathop{\mathrm{SL}}\nolimits_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
. The function
E_{2k,m}
denotes the Jacobi–Eisenstein series of weight
2k
and index
m
for
k,m\geq 1
introduced in
[
3
, §2]
. The functions
\phi_{0,1}
,
\phi_{0,2}
,
\phi_{0,3}
,
\phi_{0,4}
are generators of the graded ring of weak Jacobi forms of weight
0
with integral Fourier coefficients and their indices are respectively
1
,
2
,
3
,
4
(see
[
5
,
6
,
10
]
). The function
\phi_{-2,1}
is the unique weak Jacobi form of weight
-2
and index
1
up to constant (see
[
3
]
). The following constructions can be found in
[
3
,
6
,
10
]
.

(2.17)

\displaystyle\phi_{0,1}(\tau,z)

\displaystyle=\frac{(E_{4}^{2}E_{4,1}-E_{6}E_{6,1})(\tau,z)}{144\Delta(\tau)}=\zeta+\zeta^{-1}+10+O(q)\in J_{0,1}^{\mathrm{w}},

(2.18)

\displaystyle\phi_{0,2}(\tau,z)

\displaystyle=2\left[(\xi_{00}\xi_{01})^{2}+(\xi_{00}\xi_{10})^{2}+(\xi_{10}\xi_{01})^{2}\right](\tau,z)=\zeta+\zeta^{-1}+4+O(q)\in J_{0,2}^{\mathrm{w}},

(2.19)

\displaystyle\phi_{0,3}(\tau,z)

\displaystyle=\frac{\vartheta^{2}(\tau,2z)}{\vartheta^{2}(\tau,z)}=\zeta+\zeta^{-1}+2+O(q)\in J_{0,3}^{\mathrm{w}},

(2.20)

\displaystyle\phi_{0,4}(\tau,z)

\displaystyle=\frac{\vartheta(\tau,3z)}{\vartheta(\tau,z)}=\zeta+\zeta^{-1}+1+O(q)\in J_{0,4}^{\mathrm{w}},

(2.21)

\displaystyle\phi_{-2,1}(\tau,z)

\displaystyle=\frac{\vartheta^{2}(\tau,z)}{\eta^{6}(\tau)}=\zeta+\zeta^{-1}-2+O(q)\in J_{-2,1}^{\mathrm{w}}.

For ease of use later, we recall the structure of the space of Jacobi forms. From
[
3
, Theorem 9.4]
, we know that the bigraded ring of all weak Jacobi forms of even weight and integral index is a polynomial algebra in
\phi_{0,1}
and
\phi_{-2,1}
over the ring of
\mathop{\mathrm{SL}}\nolimits_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
modular forms. Moreover, for
k\in\mathbb{Z}
and
t\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

(2.22)

\displaystyle J_{2k+1,t}^{\mathrm{w}}=\phi_{-1,2}\cdot J_{2k+2,t-2}^{\mathrm{w}},

\displaystyle\phi_{-1,2}(\tau,z)=\frac{\vartheta(\tau,2z)}{\eta^{3}(\tau)}\in J_{-1,2}^{\mathrm{w}}.

Let
k\in\mathbb{Z}
and
t\in\mathbb{N}
. By
[
5
, Lemma 1.4]
, we have

(2.23)

\displaystyle J_{2k,t+\frac{1}{2}}^{\mathrm{w}}=\phi_{0,\frac{3}{2}}\cdot J_{2k,t-1}^{\mathrm{w}},

\displaystyle J_{2k+1,t+\frac{1}{2}}^{\mathrm{w}}=\phi_{-1,\frac{1}{2}}\cdot J_{2k+2,t}^{\mathrm{w}},

where
\phi_{0,\frac{3}{2}}
and
\phi_{-1,\frac{1}{2}}
are defined as

(2.24)

\displaystyle\phi_{0,\frac{3}{2}}(\tau,z)=\frac{\vartheta(\tau,2z)}{\vartheta(\tau,z)}\in J_{0,\frac{3}{2}}^{\mathrm{w}},

\displaystyle\phi_{-1,\frac{1}{2}}(\tau,z)=\frac{\vartheta(\tau,z)}{\eta^{3}(\tau)}\in J_{-1,\frac{1}{2}}^{\mathrm{w}}.

3.
Orbits of Jacobi forms

In this section we introduce the notion of orbits of Jacobi forms. Let us fix
X=(p,q)\in\mathbb{Q}^{2}
. We consider the set

(3.1)

X\cdot\mathop{\mathrm{SL}}\nolimits_{2}(\mathbb{Z})=\{XM:M\in\mathop{\mathrm{SL}}\nolimits_{2}(\mathbb{Z})\},

where
XM
is the usual matrix multiplication.
We choose a finite system of representatives

(3.2)

R_{X}=X\cdot\mathop{\mathrm{SL}}\nolimits_{2}(\mathbb{Z})/\mathbb{Z}^{2}.

For arbitrary
Y_{1}\in R_{X}
and
M\in\mathop{\mathrm{SL}}\nolimits_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
, we can choose
Y_{2}\in R_{X}
such that
Y_{1}M-Y_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
. We compute in the real Heisenberg group (
2.2
)

\displaystyle[Y_{1};0]\cdot\{M\}

\displaystyle=\{M\}\cdot(\{M^{-1}\}[Y_{1};0]\{M\})

\displaystyle=\{M\}\cdot[Y_{1}M;0]

\displaystyle=\{M\}\cdot[Y_{1}M-Y_{2};0][0,0;\det(Y_{2};Y_{1}M)][Y_{2};0],

where
\det(Y_{2};Y_{1}M)
is the determinant of the matrix whose first row is
Y_{2}
and second row is
Y_{1}M
.

Let
\varphi\in J_{k,t}(\upsilon_{\eta}^{D}\cdot\upsilon^{2t}_{H})
. From discussions above, we derive the transformation formula under the action of
\mathop{\mathrm{SL}}\nolimits_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
as follows

(3.3)

\big(\varphi\lvert_{k,t}[Y_{1};0]\big)\lvert_{k,t}\{M\}=\upsilon_{\eta}^{D}(M)\upsilon^{2t}_{H}\big([Y_{1}M-Y_{2};0]\big)\exp\big(2\pi it\det(Y_{2};Y_{1}M)\big)\varphi\lvert_{k,t}[Y_{2};0].

Let
Y=(y_{1},y_{2})\in R_{X}
. For any
h=[h_{1},h_{2};0]\in H(\mathbb{Z})
, we have

[Y;0]\cdot h=h\cdot[0,0;2(y_{1}h_{2}-h_{1}y_{2})]\cdot[Y;0],

which yields the transformation formula under the action of the integral Heisenberg group

(3.4)

\big(\varphi\lvert_{k,t}[Y;0]\big)\lvert_{k,t}h=\upsilon^{2t}_{H}(h)\exp\big(4\pi it(y_{1}h_{2}-h_{1}y_{2})\big)\varphi\lvert_{k,t}[Y;0].

Let
f(n,l)
denote the Fourier coefficients of
\varphi
. The order of
\varphi
is defined as

(3.5)

\mathop{\mathrm{Ord}}\nolimits(\varphi)=\min\{4nt-l^{2}:f(n,l)\neq 0\}.

Then

(3.6)

\varphi\lvert_{k,t}[Y;0]\equiv 0\quad\text{or}\quad\mathop{\mathrm{Ord}}\nolimits(\varphi\lvert_{k,t}[Y;0])\geq\mathop{\mathrm{Ord}}\nolimits(\varphi).

The following two formulas will be used later. Their proofs are straightforward.

(3.7)

\displaystyle\big[\big(\varphi\lvert_{k,t}[Y;0]\big)(\tau,mz)\big]\lvert_{k,m^{2}t}\{M\}

\displaystyle=\big(\varphi\lvert_{k,t}[Y;0]\lvert_{k,t}\{M\}\big)(\tau,mz),

(3.8)

\displaystyle\big[\big(\varphi\lvert_{k,t}[Y;0]\big)(\tau,mz)\big]\lvert_{k,m^{2}t}h

\displaystyle=\big(\varphi\lvert_{k,t}[Y;0]\lvert_{k,t}h^{\prime}\big)(\tau,mz),

where
m
is a positive integer and
h=[h_{1},h_{2};r]\in H(\mathbb{Z})
,

h^{\prime}=[mh_{1},mh_{2};m^{2}r]
.

From now on, we abbreviate
\varphi\lvert_{k,t}[Y;0]
to
\varphi\lvert Y

when there is no confusion. Equations (
3.3
) and
(
3.4
) describe the transformations of the vector-valued function

\left(\varphi\lvert Y\right)_{Y\in R_{X}}
with respect to the integral Jacobi
group. We call this vector-valued function the
orbit
of

\varphi
. We note that the transformation formulae depend on the choice
of the system representatives
R_{X}
. This paper aims to construct new
Jacobi forms by orbits of basic Jacobi forms.
We consider the special combinations of two types of functions:

\left(\varphi\lvert Y\right)(\tau,0)
and

\left(\varphi\lvert Y\right)(\tau,mz)
,
m\geq 1
. Here, the adjective

special
means that by this combination one can cancel the bad
additional factors coming from

and

in (

and (

4.
Theta relations and their generalizations

Let us consider
X=(\frac{1}{N},\frac{1}{N})
, where
N\geq 2
is a positive integer. Then we fix

R_{N}=\left(\frac{1}{N},\frac{1}{N}\right)\cdot\mathop{\mathrm{SL}}\nolimits_{2}(\mathbb{Z})/\mathbb{Z}^{2}=\left\{\left(\frac{u}{N},\frac{v}{N}\right)\in\frac{1}{N}\mathbb{Z}^{2}:0\leq u,v<N,\,(u,v)\neq(0,0)\right\}.

In this section, we use the orbit
(\vartheta\lvert Y)_{Y\in R_{N}}
of
\vartheta
to construct many Jacobi forms. When the constructed Jacobi form is identically zero, we get an identity on Jacobi theta functions with rational characteristics, which is in fact a theta relation. When the constructed Jacobi form is not zero, we obtain a generalized Riemann’s theta relation. For instance, we get formulae that represent basic Jacobi forms (Jacobi–Eisenstein series, generators of the ring of Jacobi forms, …) in terms of Jacobi theta functions with rational characteristics.

We now introduce our first result. The product
\prod_{Y\in R_{N}}\vartheta\lvert Y
of the functions in the orbit of
\vartheta
transforms like a Jacobi form. More precisely, we prove the following proposition.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
N\geq 2
be a positive integer. Then we have the following identity

(4.1)

\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq u,v<N\\
(u,v)\neq(0,0)\end{subarray}}\vartheta_{\frac{u}{N}+\frac{1}{2},\frac{v}{N}+\frac{1}{2}}(\tau,z)=(-1)^{N-1}\eta^{N^{2}-1}(\tau)\frac{\vartheta(\tau,Nz)}{\vartheta(\tau,z)}.

In particular, let
z=0
, we get

(4.2)

\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq u,v<N\\
(u,v)\neq(0,0)\end{subarray}}\theta_{\frac{u}{N}+\frac{1}{2},\frac{v}{N}+\frac{1}{2}}(\tau)=(-1)^{N-1}N\eta^{N^{2}-1}(\tau).

Proof.

Recall from (
2.15
) that
\vartheta_{\frac{u}{N}+\frac{1}{2},\frac{v}{N}+\frac{1}{2}}
equals
\vartheta|Y
up to constant factor for
Y=(u/N,v/N)
.
Although we can compute the transformations of
\prod_{Y\in R_{N}}\vartheta\lvert Y
under the action of the integral Jacobi group, the computations are very complicated. To pass through this difficulty, we consider the function
\prod_{Y\in R_{N}}\vartheta^{2N}\lvert Y
because the additional bad factors in (
3.3
) and (
3.4
) will be cancelled.
For any
M\in\mathop{\mathrm{SL}}\nolimits_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
and any
h\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
, we have

\displaystyle(\vartheta^{2N}\lvert Y)\lvert M

\displaystyle=\upsilon_{\eta}^{6N}(M)\vartheta^{2N}\lvert Y^{\prime},

\displaystyle(\vartheta^{2N}\lvert Y)\lvert[h;0]

\displaystyle=\vartheta^{2N}\lvert Y,

where
Y^{\prime}\in R_{N}
such that
Y^{\prime}-YM\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
. Therefore,

\Phi(\tau,z)=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq u,v<N\\
(u,v)\neq(0,0)\end{subarray}}\vartheta_{\frac{u}{N}+\frac{1}{2},\frac{v}{N}+\frac{1}{2}}^{2N}(\tau,z)=c\prod_{Y\in R_{N}}\vartheta^{2N}\lvert Y\in J_{N^{3}-N,N^{3}-N}(\upsilon_{\eta}^{2(N^{3}-N)}),

where
c
is a constant.
We know from
[
4
, Lemma 3]
that
\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq u,v<N\\
(u,v)\neq(0,0)\end{subarray}}\vartheta_{\frac{u}{N}+\frac{1}{2},\frac{v}{N}+\frac{1}{2}}(\tau,0)
has the lead term of
q
-expansion:
(-1)^{N-1}Nq^{\frac{N^{2}-1}{24}}
. It follows that
\frac{\Phi}{\eta^{2(N^{3}-N)}}\in J_{0,N^{3}-N}^{\mathrm{w}}
. Moreover,
\frac{\Phi}{\eta^{2(N^{3}-N)}}
has the same zeros as
\left(\dfrac{\vartheta(\tau,Nz)}{\vartheta(\tau,z)}\right)^{2N}
and their zeros have the same multiplicity. We hence obtain

\Big(\Phi/\eta^{2(N^{3}-N)}\Big)/\left(\frac{\vartheta(\tau,Nz)}{\vartheta(\tau,z)}\right)^{2N}\in J_{0,0}^{\mathrm{w}}.

Therefore, the above function is constant, which clearly forces that the function

\left(\Big(\prod\vartheta_{\frac{u}{N}+\frac{1}{2},\frac{v}{N}+\frac{1}{2}}(\tau,z)\Big)/\eta^{N^{2}-1}(\tau)\right)/\left(\frac{\vartheta(\tau,Nz)}{\vartheta(\tau,z)}\right)

is also constant. We then prove the desired formula by comparing the lead terms of
q
-expansion.
∎

X_{2}=\left(0,\frac{1}{2}\right)
,

.

\displaystyle A_{a,b,c}^{(1)}=\frac{1}{6}\left(S_{12}s_{13}s_{23}+S_{13}s_{12}s_{23}+S_{23}s_{13}s_{12}\right),

\displaystyle A_{a,b,c}^{(2)}=\frac{1}{6}\left(S_{12}S_{13}s_{23}+S_{13}s_{12}S_{23}+S_{23}s_{13}S_{12}\right),

\displaystyle A_{a,b,c}^{(3)}=\frac{1}{2}S_{12}S_{13}S_{23}.

Then when

A_{a,b,c}^{(i)}=1+O(q)\in J_{\frac{3(a+b+c)}{2},2ic+\frac{ib}{2}},\quad\text{for}\;i=1,2,3.

When

A_{a,b,c}^{(i)}\in J_{\frac{3(a+b+c)}{2},2ic+\frac{ib}{2}}(\upsilon_{\eta}^{12}),\quad\text{for}\;i=1,2,3.

Proof.

Denote
T=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&1\\
0&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)
and
S=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&-1\\
1&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)
. We find

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{c}S_{12}|T\\
S_{13}|T\\
S_{23}|T\end{array}\right)=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}0&0&-u\\
0&-u&0\\
-u&0&0\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{c}S_{12}\\
S_{13}\\
S_{23}\end{array}\right),

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{c}S_{12}|S\\
S_{13}|S\\
S_{23}|S\end{array}\right)=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}-v&0&0\\
0&0&v\\
0&v&0\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{c}S_{12}\\
S_{13}\\
S_{23}\end{array}\right),

where

and
v=\upsilon_{\eta}(S)^{3(a+b+c)}
.
It is clear that
S_{ij}
is invariant under the action of the integral Heisenberg group. We then complete the proof by direct computation.
∎

Remark 4.12
.

Our approach can be generalized to the case of several variables. In other words, we can define the orbits of Jacobi forms of lattice index and use them to construct more general theta relations. For example, the well known
Riemann’s theta formula
(see
[
11
]
)

(4.68)

\prod_{i=1}^{4}\vartheta_{00}(\tau,z_{i})+\prod_{i=1}^{4}\vartheta(\tau,z_{i})-\prod_{i=1}^{4}\vartheta_{01}(\tau,z_{i})-\prod_{i=1}^{4}\vartheta_{10}(\tau,z_{i})=2\prod_{i=1}^{4}\vartheta(\tau,x_{i}),

where
x_{1}=\frac{z_{1}+z_{2}+z_{3}+z_{4}}{2}
,
x_{2}=\frac{z_{1}+z_{2}-z_{3}-z_{4}}{2}
,
x_{3}=\frac{z_{1}-z_{2}+z_{3}-z_{4}}{2}
,
x_{4}=\frac{z_{1}-z_{2}-z_{3}+z_{4}}{2}
,
can be deduced by the orbit of
\prod_{i=1}^{4}\vartheta(\tau,z_{i})
which is a holomorphic Jacobi form of weight
2
and index
1
associated to the root lattice
D_{4}
. The interesting formula

(4.69)

\prod_{i=1}^{8}\vartheta(\tau,z_{i})+\prod_{i=1}^{8}\vartheta_{00}(\tau,z_{i})+\prod_{i=1}^{8}\vartheta_{01}(\tau,z_{i})+\prod_{i=1}^{8}\vartheta_{10}(\tau,z_{i})=2\vartheta_{E_{8}},

where

\vartheta_{E_{8}}(\tau,\mathfrak{z}_{8})=\sum_{v\in E_{8}}e^{\pi i((v,v)\tau+2(v,\mathfrak{z}_{8}))},

is the theta function of root lattice
E_{8}
, can be concluded from the orbit of
\prod_{i=1}^{8}\vartheta(\tau,z_{i})
which is a holomorphic Jacobi form of weight
4
and index
1
with respect to the root lattice
D_{8}
. Besides, the following formula which is a generalization of
(\ref{c03})

(4.70)

\sum_{Y\in R_{3}}(\vartheta\lvert Y)^{3}(\tau,z_{1})(\vartheta\lvert Y)^{3}(\tau,z_{2})=2\vartheta^{3}(\tau,z_{1})\vartheta^{3}(\tau,z_{2}),

can be derived from the orbit of
\vartheta(\tau,z_{1})\vartheta(\tau,z_{2})
which is a Jacobi form of weight
1
and index
\frac{1}{2}
with respect to the lattice
{\langle{2}\rangle}\oplus{\langle{2}\rangle}
.
In some sense, we can view (
4.70
) as a Riemann’s theta formula of order
3
. We will systematically investigate the orbits of Jacobi forms of lattice index in a separate article.

5.
Applications

In this section, we present several applications of our formulas. Firstly, as an application of (
4.1
), we give a simple proof of the following interesting formulae in
[
12
, Lemma 1]
.

Corollary 5.1
.

Assume that
N
is odd and
v_{1},v_{2}
run through a set denoted by
\mathfrak{A}
of representatives of
\frac{1}{N}(\mathbb{Z}\tau+\mathbb{Z})/(\mathbb{Z}\tau+\mathbb{Z})
. Then we have

\displaystyle\prod_{v_{1}\neq v_{2}}[\wp(\tau,z+v_{1})-\wp(\tau,z+v_{2})]

\displaystyle=c_{1}\left(\frac{\vartheta(\tau,2Nz)}{\vartheta^{4}(\tau,Nz)}\right)^{N^{2}-1}\eta^{(4N^{2}+3)(N^{2}-1)}(\tau),

\displaystyle\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}v_{1},v_{2}\neq 0\\
v_{1}\neq v_{2}\end{subarray}}[\wp(\tau,v_{1})-\wp(\tau,v_{2})]

\displaystyle=c_{2}\Delta^{\frac{(N^{2}-1)(N^{2}-3)}{6}}(\tau),

where
c_{1},c_{2}
are nonzero constants depending only on
N
and
\wp
is the Weierstrass
\wp
-function

\wp(\tau,z)=\frac{1}{z^{2}}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\omega\in\mathbb{Z}\tau+\mathbb{Z}\\
\omega\neq 0\end{subarray}}\left((z+\omega)^{-2}-\omega^{-2}\right).

Proof.

By the following identity

\wp(\tau,z_{1})-\wp(\tau,z_{2})=\frac{4\pi^{2}\eta^{6}(\tau)\vartheta(\tau,z_{1}+z_{2})\vartheta(\tau,z_{1}-z_{2})}{\vartheta^{2}(\tau,z_{1})\vartheta^{2}(\tau,z_{2})},

we check at once

\displaystyle\prod_{v_{1}\neq v_{2}}[\wp(\tau,z+v_{1})-\wp(\tau,z+v_{2})]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle*\eta^{6N^{2}(N^{2}-1)}\prod_{v_{1}\neq v_{2}}\frac{\vartheta(\tau,2z+v_{1}+v_{2})\vartheta(\tau,v_{1}-v_{2})}{\vartheta^{2}(\tau,z+v_{1})\vartheta^{2}(\tau,z+v_{2})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle*\eta^{6N^{2}(N^{2}-1)}\prod_{v_{1}\neq v_{2}}\frac{\vartheta_{v_{1}+v_{2}}(\tau,2z)\theta_{v_{1}-v_{2}}(\tau)}{\vartheta_{v_{1}}^{2}(\tau,z)\vartheta_{v_{2}}^{2}(\tau,z)}

where
*
are constants. The notation
\vartheta_{v}
stands for
\vartheta_{a+1/2,b+1/2}
if
v=a\tau+b
. One can check that the set
\{v_{1}+v_{2}:v_{1}\neq v_{2}\in\mathfrak{A}\}
contains every element of
\mathfrak{A}
and each element appears
N^{2}-1
times.
Similarly, we find that the set
\{v_{1}-v_{2}:v_{1}\neq v_{2}\in\mathfrak{A}\}
contains every element of
\mathfrak{A}
except
0
and each element appears
N^{2}
times.
We thus prove the first formula by (
4.1
) and (
4.2
). The proof of the second formula is similar.
∎

Remark 5.2
.

When
N=2
, we can prove the following identities in the same way

\displaystyle\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq 4}[\wp(\tau,z+v_{i})-\wp(\tau,z+v_{j})]

\displaystyle=c_{3}\eta^{30}\left(\frac{\vartheta(\tau,4z)}{\vartheta^{4}(\tau,2z)}\right)^{2},

\displaystyle\prod_{1<i<j\leq 4}[\wp(\tau,v_{i})-\wp(\tau,v_{j})]

\displaystyle=c_{4}\eta^{12},

where
c_{3}
,
c_{4}
are constants,

,

,

and

N>2
is even because the set
\{v_{1}+v_{2}:v_{1}\neq v_{2}\in\mathfrak{A}\}
does not satisfy the condition in the above proof.

As another application, we generalize the Jacobi’s derivative formula:
\vartheta^{{}^{\prime}}(\tau,0)=\pi i\theta_{00}\theta_{01}\theta_{10}

Lemma 5.3

\vartheta^{\prime}(\tau,0)=2\pi i\eta(\tau)^{3},\;\vartheta{{}^{\prime\prime}}(\tau,0)=0,\;\vartheta{{}^{\prime\prime\prime}}(\tau,0)=48\pi^{3}i\eta(\tau)^{3}G_{2}(\tau).

6

is the quasi-modular Eisenstein series of weight

.
We can check that the function

is a modular form of weight

on
\mathop{\mathrm{SL}}\nolimits_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
. Hence
f_{1}(\tau)
is a constant and
f_{2}=f_{3}=0
, which imply the lemma.
∎

It is obvious that
\vartheta_{01}^{\prime}(\tau,0)=\vartheta{{}^{\prime}}_{10}(\tau,0)=\vartheta{{}^{\prime}}_{00}(\tau,0)=0

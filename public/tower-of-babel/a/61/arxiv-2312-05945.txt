4-Chromatic Graphs Have At Least 4 Cycles of Length 0 mod 3 3
Sean Kim, Michael E. Picollelli

Abstract
A 2018 conjecture of Brewster, McGuinness, Moore, and Noel [ 2 ] asserts that for k ≥ 3 k\geq 3 , if a graph has chromatic number greater than k k , then it contains at least as many cycles of length 0 mod k 0\bmod k as the complete graph on k + 1 k+1 vertices. Our main result confirms this in the k = 3 k=3 case by showing every 4 4 -critical graph contains at least 4 4 cycles of length 0 mod 3 0\bmod 3 , and that K 4 K_{4} is the unique such graph achieving the minimum. We make progress on the general conjecture as well, showing that ( k + 1 ) (k+1) -critical graphs with minimum degree k k have at least as many cycles of length 0 mod r 0\bmod r as K k + 1 K_{k+1} , provided k + 1 ≠ 0 mod r k+1\neq 0\bmod r . We also show that K k + 1 K_{k+1} uniquely minimizes the number of cycles of length 1 mod k 1\bmod k among all ( k + 1 ) (k+1) -critical graphs, strengthening a recent result of Moore and West [ 12 ] and extending it to the k = 3 k=3 case.

Introduction

The study of cycles with a given length modulo an integer
k
began with work of Burr and Erdős
[
8
]
, who conjectured that for odd values of
k
, graphs of sufficiently large average degree contain cycles of all lengths modulo
k
. This conjecture was proven by Bollobás
[
1
]
, with the bound subsequently improved by Thomassen
[
16
]
. Further work, building in part on conjectures of Thomassen
[
16
]
, has largely focused on the existence of such cycles under minimum degree or connectivity assumptions – see, for example,
[
5
]
,
[
7
]
,
[
9
]
, or
[
15
]
.

This paper is concerned with bounding the number of cycles of length
0\bmod k
and
1\bmod k
in non-
k
-colorable graphs. The first result in this direction is due to Tuza
[
17
]
, who showed that graphs without cycles of length
1\bmod k
are
k
-colorable, generalizing König’s classic characterization of bipartite graphs. For cycles of length
0\bmod k
, Chen and Saito
[
3
]
showed that graphs without cycles of length
0\bmod 3
are
3
-colorable; Dean, Lesniak and Saito
[
6
]
achieved the same conclusion for graphs without cycles of length
0\bmod 4
. Chen, Ma and Zang
[
4
]
established that any non-
k
-colorable graph is guaranteed cycles of every length
\ell\bmod k
except possibly
\ell=2
, settling the existence case in the
\ell=0
case. Very recent work has essentially settled the existence question entirely: Gao, Huo, and Ma
[
10
]
showed every
(k+1)
-critical non-complete graph has cycles of all lengths modulo
k
when
k\geq 6
. The same conclusion for the
k=3
case was implied by work in
[
3
]
,
[
13
]
, and
[
14
]
, and the case where

is the subject of recent work of Huo
[
11
]
.

The question of how many such cycles must occur has received considerably less attention. Brewster, McGuinness, Moore and Noel
[
2
]
generalized a proof of Chen and Saito’s result due to Wrochna to show that any graph which is not
k
-colorable has at least
(k-1)!/2
cycles of length
0\bmod k
. They further conjectured that the complete graph
K_{k+1}
achieves the minimum number of such cycles among all non-
k
-colorable graphs.

Conjecture 1

(Brewster, McGuinness, Moore, and Noel,
[
2
]
)
.

If

, then

contains at least
(k+1)(k-1)!/2
cycles of length

.

We will establish this conjecture for
(k+1)
-critical graphs with minimum degree

(see Corollary

), but our main contribution is to settle it in the affirmative in the

Theorem 1.1
.

If
G
is
4
-critical, then
G
has at least
4
cycles of length
0\mod 3
, with equality only for
K_{4}
.

Building on the arguments in
[
2
]
, Moore and West
[
12
]
established bounds on the number of cycles of length
0\bmod r
and
1\bmod r
in non-
k
-colorable graphs, where
3\leq r\leq k
. While their bound on cycles of length
0\bmod k
was the same as in
[
2
]
, they were able to bound the number of cycles of length
1\bmod k
in non-
k
-colorable graphs below by
k!/2
. This is best possible as it is achieved by

. Their argument, a probabilistic application of Tuza’s strengthening of Minty’s Theorem, also established a structural condition for equality when

Theorem 1.2

(Moore and West
[
12
]
, Theorem 5)
.

For
k\geq 3
, a non-
k
-colorable graph has at least
k!/2
cycles with lengths
congruent to 1 modulo
k
, with equality for
k\geq 4
only when these cycles all have length

k+1
.

By modifying their arguments, we achieve a slight strengthening of their result that extends to the
k=3

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
k\geq 3
and let
G
be a
(k+1)
-critical graph. Then

has at least
k!/2
cycles of length
1\bmod k
, with equality only for the complete graph

The remainder of this paper is organized as follows: in Section

-critical graphs, followed by the proof of Theorem

Cycles in
(k+1)
-critical graphs

-chromatic graphs. We remark that our notation largely follows

The results in this section are primarily established by modifying the approach to generalized Kempe chains used by Moore and West in
[

. To that end, we will use the the following definition from

Definition 2.1

(Moore and West
[

]
)
.

Let

be a

-colorable graph and let

be a proper
k
-coloring of

, where we assume the set of colors is

\sigma

1.

V(D_{\sigma})=V(G)
.

2.

For

if and only if

\sigma(\varphi(u))=\varphi(v)
.

For

, let

be the set of vertices accessible from

along directed paths in

. A key observation in

is that we may “shift” the colors in

by recoloring each

, contradicting

.

As an illustration, we begin with a slight strengthening of Theorem 4 from
[
12

that bounds the number of cycles of a given modular length in a graph

is
(k+1)
-chromatic but

Theorem 2.2

(See Moore and West
[
12
]
, Theorem 4)
.

Fix

\chi(G)=k+1
but
\chi(G-e)=k
, then letting

be an endpoint of

contains at least
\frac{1}{2}\prod_{i=1}^{r-1}(k-i)

0\bmod r
.

Proof.

Letting

, we fix a proper
k
-coloring
\varphi
of
G-e
using colors

and let
\sigma
be a cyclic
r
-permutation of
[k]
that includes
\varphi(x)
. We note that
\varphi(x)=\varphi(y)
must hold, else
\varphi
is a proper
k
-coloring of
G
.

Consider the
\sigma
-subdigraph
D_{\sigma}
of
G
under
\varphi
and
\sigma
, and let
A_{x}
be the set of vertices accessible from
x
via directed paths. By construction of

, the mapping
\varphi^{\prime}
formed by setting
\varphi^{\prime}(v)=\sigma(\varphi(v))

\varphi^{\prime}(v)=\varphi(v)

If

then

yields a proper

-coloring of

contains a directed path

from

to

Thus,

has a directed cycle containing

be its underlying cycle in

visits every color from

-permutations,

.
∎

Proof of Theorem

Our aim now is to prove Theorem
1.3
: let

and let
G
be a
(k+1)
-critical graph. Let
v
be an arbitrary vertex, and let
\varphi
be a

-coloring of

using colors

. We first show that

contains at least
k!/2
cycles of length
1\bmod k
that include

.

For each
i\in[k]
, let

be the set of neighbors of
v
with color
i
under
\varphi
, noting

since
G
is not
k
-colorable. Let
G_{i}
be the subgraph of
G
formed by deleting the edges between
v
and
N_{i}
, and let
\varphi^{i}
denote the extension of
\varphi
to a proper
k
-coloring of
G_{i}
by setting
\varphi^{i}(v)=i
.

Let
\sigma
be a cyclic permutation of
[k]
, and consider the
\sigma
-subdigraph
D^{i}_{\sigma}
of
G_{i}
under
\varphi^{i}
. We claim that
D^{i}_{\sigma}
must contain a directed path from

into
N_{i}
. Otherwise, letting

denote the set of vertices in
D^{i}_{\sigma}
accessible from
v
, we can recolor
G_{i}
by shifting the colors in
W
according to
\sigma
, producing a proper
k
-coloring
\varphi^{\prime}
of
G_{i}
in which all vertices in
N_{i}
have color

and
v
has color
\sigma(i)
. But in that case
\varphi^{\prime}
is a proper coloring of
G
, a contradiction.

With foresight we let
v_{i}\in N_{i}
be a vertex on a shortest directed path
P

D_{\sigma}^{i}
from
v
to
N_{i}
, noting
P
has length
0\bmod k
since it begins and ends on a vertex of color
i
. Then
P
along with the edge
v_{i}v
forms a directed cycle
\widehat{C}_{\sigma}^{i}
in
G
of length
1\bmod k
; let
C^{i}_{\sigma}
be its underlying cycle in
G
. Since
P
visits all the colors of
\sigma
in order and contains the edge

,
\widehat{C}_{\sigma}^{i}
, viewed now as an oriented subgraph of

, determines

and
\sigma
. Consequently,

could also be the underlying cycle of
\widehat{C}_{\sigma^{-1}}^{\sigma(i)}
, but of no other such directed cycle constructed this way. So as

and
\sigma

.

Suppose now that

\sigma
of

and an

, the constructed cycle

is spanning, and therefore so is the shortest directed path from

to

. Consequently,

, implying that

, completing the proof.

4.1
4-critical graphs with minimum degree at least 4

To finish the proof of Theorem
1.1
, we must now show that every 4-critical graph
G
with minimum degree at least 4 contains at least 5 cycles of length
0\bmod 3
. In this case, the argument used in Theorem
2.4
does not directly adapt as it obtained cycles from closed directed walks. A key to constructing these walks was that every neighbor of a vertex
v
of degree 3 in a 4-critical graph receives a different color under a proper 3-coloring of
G-v
, which does not apply once
\delta(G)\geq 4
.

Instead, our arguments will rely on two other results. The first is a sufficient condition for cycles of length
0\bmod 3
to exist due to Chen and Saito
[
3
]
:

Theorem 4.2

(Chen and Saito
[
3
]
, Theorem 1)
.

If
G
is a graph with
n\geq 2
vertices and at most one vertex of degree 2 or less, then
G
contains a cycle of length
0\bmod 3
.

We remark that in
[
3
]
, the authors refer to cycles with length divisible by
3
as
good cycles
, and we will do so for the remainder of this section. Our first application of this will be to handle the case where a vertex lies on at least three good cycles.

Lemma 4.3
.

If
G
is a 4-critical graph with
\delta(G)\geq 4
and there exists a vertex
v\in V(G)
such that
v
lies on at least 3 good cycles, then
G
contains at least 5 good cycles.

Proof.

Suppose
v\in V(G)
lies on at least 3 good cycles, call them
C^{1},C^{2},C^{3}
. Since
\delta(G)\geq 4
,
\delta(G-v)\geq 3
, so
G-v
contains at least one good cycle
C^{4}
by Theorem
4.2
.

If
V(C^{4})\nsubseteq N(v)
then
C^{4}
contains an edge
e
with at most one endpoint in
N(v)
. Deleting
e
from
G-v
produces a subgraph with at most one vertex of degree 2, and thus it contains a good cycle
C^{5}\neq C^{4}
by Theorem
4.2
, yielding the result.

If
V(C^{4})\subseteq N(v)
, then every edge of
C^{4}
forms a triangle with
v
, and we’re done if
|V(C^{4})|\geq 5
. But if
|V(C^{4})|<5
, then
|V(C^{4})|=3
since
C^{4}
is good. This implies
G
contains a
K_{4}
on
C^{4}\cup\{v\}
, contradicting
G
’s 4-criticality and completing the proof.

∎

We’ll also appeal to a recent result of Gao, Huo, Liu and Ma
[
9
]
, which was used to resolve a number of conjectures regarding the existence of cycles of prescribed lengths.

Definition 4.4

(Gao, Huo, Liu and Ma
[
9
]
)
.

A collection of
\ell
paths is
admissible
if the length of every path is at least two, and the lengths form an arithmetic progression with common difference one or two.

Theorem 4.5

(Gao, Huo, Liu, and Ma
[
9
]
, Theorem 1.2 )
.

Let
G
be a 2-connected graph and let
x,y
be distinct vertices of
G
. If every vertex in
G
other than
x
and
y
has degree at least
\ell+1
, then there exist
\ell
admissible paths from
x
to
y
in
G
.

We note that Theorem
2.2
implies that every vertex
v
in a 4-critical graph lies on at least two good cycles, by applying the Theorem first with an arbitrary edge incident with
v
, then with an edge incident with
v
on the found cycle.

Lemma 4.6
.

If
G
is
4
-critical with
\delta(G)\geq 4
and every vertex is on at most two good cycles, then:

1.

G
is
4
-regular, and

2.

Every edge of
G
lies on exactly one good cycle.

Proof.

It suffices to argue that every edge lies on at least one good cycle. This will imply that every vertex lies on at least
d(v)/2
good cycles, yielding a maximum degree of at most 4 under the assumptions given and therefore
G
is
4
-regular. Furthermore, if any edge
uv
lies on at least two good cycles, then those two cycles only cover three edges incident with
v
, implying
v
lies on a third good cycle, a contradiction.

To that end, pick any edge
e=uv
. It is routine to show that a color-critical graph is
2
-connected (e.g., see
[
18
]
, Exercise 8.2.11), so by Theorem
4.5
there exist
3
admissible paths from
u
to
v
. Since the common difference is one or two, their lengths cover the congruence classes modulo 3, so one of these paths has length
2\bmod 3
and forms a good cycle with
e
.
∎

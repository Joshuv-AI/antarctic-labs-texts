Irreducibility of eventually 2 2 -periodic curves in the moduli space of cubic polynomials
Niladri Patra

Abstract
. Consider the moduli space, ℳ 3 , \mathcal{M}_{3}, of cubic polynomials over ℂ {\mathbbm{C}} , with a marked critical point. Let 𝒮 k , n \mathscr{S}_{k,n} be the set of all points in ℳ 3 \mathcal{M}_{3} for which the marked critical point is strictly ( k , n ) (k,n) -preperiodic. Milnor conjectured that the affine algebraic curves 𝒮 k , n \mathscr{S}_{k,n} are irreducible, for all k ≥ 0 , n > 0 k\geq 0,n>0 . In this article, we show the irreducibility of eventually 2 2 -periodic curves, i.e. 𝒮 k , 2 , k ≥ 0 \mathscr{S}_{k,2},\;k\geq 0 curves. We also note that the curves, 𝒮 k , 2 , k ≥ 0 \mathscr{S}_{k,2},\;k\geq 0 , exhibit a possible splitting-merging phenomenon that has not been observed in earlier studies of 𝒮 k , n \mathscr{S}_{k,n} curves. Finally, using the irreducibility of 𝒮 k , 2 \mathscr{S}_{k,2} curves, we give a new and short proof of Galois conjugacy of unicritical points lying on 𝒮 k , 2 \mathscr{S}_{k,2} , for even natural number k k .

1980s

]
,

,

]
). It began with the moduli space of quadratic polynomials and its subsets of dynamical nature, like the Mandelbrot set. In two consecutive papers (
[
BH88
]
,
[
BH92
]
), Branner and Hubbard pioneered the study of the moduli space of cubic polynomials. Later in a
1991
preprint, published as
[
Mil09
]
, Milnor restructured this moduli space by considering cubic polynomials with a
marked
critical point. This space is a ramified
2
to
1
cover of the moduli space of cubic polynomials. In this article, we will partially answer a question, raised by Milnor (
[
Mil09
]
), about the irreducibility of some dynamically interesting affine algebraic curves in the moduli space of cubic polynomials with a marked critical point.

Let
f
be a polynomial over
{\mathbbm{C}}
. For any integer
m\geq 0
, we denote the iteration of
f
with itself
m
times, as
f^{m}
,
\;\textit{i}.\textit{e}.\;\;f^{0}=Id,\;f^{m}=f^{m-1}\circ f,\text{ for all }m\in{\mathbbm{N}}
. For
x\in{\mathbbm{C}}
, the
forward orbit
of
x
is defined to be the set of images of
x
under all the iterates of
f
,
\;\textit{i}.\textit{e}.\;\;\{f^{m}(x)\;|\;m\geq 0\}
.

A point
x\in{\mathbbm{C}}
is called a
periodic point
of period
n
if
f^{n}(x)=x
. It is called
strictly

n
-
periodic point
if
n
is the smallest positive integer for which
f^{n}(x)=x
. A point
x\in{\mathbbm{C}}
is called a
(k,n)
-
preperiodic point
if
f^{k}(x)
is a periodic point of period
n
,
\;\textit{i}.\textit{e}.\;\;f^{k+n}(x)=f^{k}(x)
. It is called
strictly

(k,n)
-
preperiodic point
if
f^{k}(x)
is strictly
n
-periodic and
f^{l}(x)
is not periodic for any
0\leq l<k
.

For a polynomial
f\in{\mathbbm{C}}[z]
, the roots of the derivative of
f
are called the
finite critical points
of
f
. Let us consider the set
S_{3}
of all cubic polynomials over
{\mathbbm{C}}
, with a marked (finite) critical point. Two polynomials that are affine conjugate to each other, exhibit the same dynamical behaviour. So, we consider the quotient space of
S_{3}
, by identifying polynomials that are affine conjugate to each other, and the affine conjugation map sends the marked critical point of the first to the marked critical point of the latter. This space,
\mathcal{M}_{3}
, is called
the moduli space of cubic polynomials with a marked critical point
. In other words,
\mathcal{M}_{3}
is the space

\{(f,a)\;|\;f\in S_{3},a\text{ is a finite critical point of }f\}/\sim,

where the equivalence relation
\sim
is given by
(f,a)\sim(g,a^{\prime})
iff there is
h\in Aut({\mathbbm{C}})
such that
h\circ f\circ h^{-1}=g
and
h(a)=a^{\prime}
.

A polynomial in
{\mathbbm{C}}[z]
is called
monic
if its leading coefficient is one, and called
reduced
(or,
centered
) if the sum of its roots is zero. Observe that, any polynomial is affine conjugate to a monic, reduced polynomial. Hence,
\mathcal{M}_{3}
can be seen as set of affine conjugacy classes of monic, reduced, cubic polynomials over
{\mathbbm{C}}
, with a marked critical point. From
[
Mil09
]
, every monic, reduced cubic polynomial, with a marked critical point, can be written in the
modified Branner-Hubbard normal form
as,

f_{a,b}(z)=z^{3}-3a^{2}z+2a^{3}+b,

(1.1)

with
\pm a
as its finite critical points,
a
is the marked critical point and
f(a)=b
is a finite critical value. Let
a,b,a^{\prime},b^{\prime}\in{\mathbbm{C}}
. Brief calculation shows that,
f_{a,b}
and
f_{a^{\prime},b^{\prime}}
are affine conjugate to each other iff either
(a,b)=(a^{\prime},b^{\prime})
or
(a,b)=(-a^{\prime},-b^{\prime})
. Hence, the moduli space
\mathcal{M}_{3}
can be identified as,

\mathcal{M}_{3}\longleftrightarrow{\mathbbm{C}}^{2}/\left((a,b)\sim(-a,-b)\right).

The space
{\mathbbm{C}}^{2}/\left((a,b)\sim(-a,-b)\right)
is isomorphic to the image of
{\mathbbm{C}}^{2}
under the affine Veronese map,
\;{\mathbbm{C}}^{2}\rightarrow{\mathbbm{C}}^{3},\;(a,b)\mapsto(a^{2},ab,b^{2})
. Hence,
\mathcal{M}_{3}={\mathbbm{C}}^{2}/\left((a,b)\sim(-a,-b)\right)
is a
2
-dimensional affine variety.

Let
k\geq 0
and
n>0
be integers. Consider the set,
\mathscr{S}_{k,n}
, of all points
(a,b)\in\mathcal{M}_{3}
such that the marked critical point
a
is strictly
(k,n)
-preperiodic under the polynomial map
f_{a,b}
. The set
\mathscr{S}_{k,n}
is an affine algebraic curve in
\mathcal{M}_{3}
. Milnor
[
Mil09
]
conjectured that the
\mathscr{S}_{0,n},n\in{\mathbbm{N}}
curves are all irreducible. In general, it is conjectured that,

Conjecture 1.1
.

For any choice of integers
k\geq 0
and
n>0
, the curve
\mathscr{S}_{k,n}
is irreducible.

Buff, Epstein, and Koch (
[
BEK18
]
) proved this conjecture for
\mathscr{S}_{k,1}
curves. Arfeux and Kiwi (
[
AK20
]
) have shown that
\mathscr{S}_{0,n}
curves are irreducible, for any

. In this article, we will prove this conjecture for
\mathscr{S}_{k,2}
curves, for any non-negative integer

(see Theorem
5.10
). We state the theorem below.

Theorem 1.2
.

For any non-negative integer
k
, the curve
\mathscr{S}_{k,2}
is irreducible.

Our proof of Theorem
1.2
is of arithmetic nature and mimics the approach taken for the unicritical case in
[
BEK18
]
. Let
k\geq 0,n>0
. We form polynomials
h_{k,n}\in{\mathbbm{Z}}[a,b]
such that
\mathscr{S}_{k,n}
is Zariski dense in the curve of
h_{k,n}
in
\mathcal{M}_{3}
, with finite complement. We form the polynomials
h_{0,2}
and
h_{1,2}
explicitly and show that they are irreducible over
{\mathbbm{C}}
. For
k\geq 2
, We show that
h_{k,2}
polynomials are generalised
3
-Eisenstein polynomials with respect to
h_{1,2}
. Hence, they are irreducible over
{\mathbbm{Q}}
. If the polynomials
h_{k,2},\;k\geq 0
are irreducible over
{\mathbbm{C}}
, then we are done. Analogs of these polynomials in the
(k,1),\;k\geq 0
case (studied in
[
BEK18
]
) and
(0,n),\;n\in{\mathbbm{N}}
case (studied in
[
AK20
]
) has turned out to be irreducible. But in the eventually
2
-periodic case, we observe a possible
splitting-merging phenomenon
. The polynomial
h_{1,2}
is
reducible
. In general, the polynomials
h_{k,2}
can be reducible over
{\mathbbm{C}}
. If
h_{k,2}
is reducible for some
k\geq 0
, then it can split into at most two factors over the field
{\mathbbm{Q}}[i]
. We show that both of these factors, that lie in
{\mathbbm{Q}}[i][a,b]
, have a smooth
{\mathbbm{Q}}[i]
-rational point. Using extension of irreducibility (Corollary
4.8
), we get that both of these factors are irreducible over
{\mathbbm{C}}
. Moreover, we show that the irreducible curves in
{\mathbbm{C}}^{2}
corresponding to these two factors merge together under the equivalence relation
(a,b)\sim(-a,-b)
, generating one irreducible curve in
\mathcal{M}_{3}
, which contains
\mathscr{S}_{k,2}
as a Zariski dense subset. This completes the proof of Theorem
1.2
.

As a consequence of this approach, we find an explicit form of
h_{k,2}
polynomials.

Proposition 1.3
.

The polynomial
h_{0,2}
is
(b-a)(b+2a)+1
. Let

. For

h_{k,2}=\dfrac{f_{k,2}\cdot f_{k-1,1}}{h_{0,2}\cdot f_{k-1,2}\cdot f_{k,1}},

(1.2)

and for

h_{k,2}=\dfrac{f_{k,2}\cdot f_{k-1,1}}{f_{k-1,2}\cdot f_{k,1}}.

(1.3)

A polynomial over
{\mathbbm{C}}
is called

in Equation (

. The finite set of values of

(k,n)
-preperiodic under

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
k\geq 0,n>0
. The polynomial
h_{k,n}
kills
\mathscr{S}_{k,n}
. Moreover,
\mathscr{S}_{k,n}
is Zariski dense in the curve of

in
\mathcal{M}_{3}
, with finitely many points in its complement.

Proof.

Any point in
\mathscr{S}_{k,n}
lies on the curve of

but not on the curve of
g_{i}
, for any

appearing in Equation (

). Hence,
\mathscr{S}_{k,n}
is a subset of the curve of
h_{k,n}
in
\mathcal{M}_{3}
. The complement of
\mathscr{S}_{k,n}
in the curve of

consists of points

for which

is
(k,n)
-preperiodic but not strictly. So, each of them is a solution of some

appearing in Equation (

). By definition of

,

is coprime to

over
{\mathbbm{Z}}
, for every

. The polynomials

and

’s are all monic as polynomials in

From Lemma

For any

.

By definition of

, coincide. Replacing

) in Lemma

4.4.
Extension of irreducibility

In this subsection, we relate the irreducibility of a multivariate polynomial over a number field and over
{\mathbbm{C}}

[

by Buff, Epstein and Koch. We should mention that while we state Theorem

Theorem 4.6

[

]

Let

, and the linear part of

is irreducible in
{\mathbbm{C}}[a,b]

Corollary 4.7

Let

be an element of
{\mathbbm{Q}}[a,b]

has a smooth
{\mathbbm{Q}}
-rational point, i.e. there exists a point

, and

is smooth at
(a^{0},b^{0})

{\mathbbm{C}}[a,b]

By an affine change of coordinate, sending

to

, from

, such that constant term of

is zero and

has non-zero linear part. Also,

is irreducible over
{\mathbbm{C}}
(or, over
{\mathbbm{Q}})\iff g^{\prime}
is irreducible over
{\mathbbm{C}}
(or, over
{\mathbbm{Q}}
). Applying Theorem
4.6
on

, one obtains the corollary.
∎

Corollary 4.8
.

Let
F
be a number field, which means finite extension over
{\mathbbm{Q}}
. Let

be an element of

. Let us assume that

Replacing

with

∎

4.5.
Even and odd polynomials

Definition 4.9
.

Let
g
be an element in
{\mathbbm{C}}[a,b]
. we say
g
is
even polynomial
iff
g(a,b)=g(-a,-b)
, and
g
is
odd polynomial
iff
g(a,b)=-g(-a,-b)
.

Every non-zero polynomial
g\in{\mathbbm{C}}[a,b]
, can be written as
g=g_{e}+g_{o}
, where
g_{e}\in{\mathbbm{C}}[a,b]
is an even polynomial, and
g_{o}\in{\mathbbm{C}}[a,b]
is an odd polynomial.

Let
G_{e}(\text{and},\;G_{o})
denote the set of all even (and, odd) polynomials in
{\mathbbm{C}}[a,b]
.

Lemma 4.10
.

The sets
G_{e},G_{o}
are additive subgroups of
{\mathbbm{C}}[a,b]
. The set
G:=G_{e}\cup G_{o}
is closed under multiplication. Also, if
g_{1},g_{2}\in G
, and
g_{1}=g_{2}\cdot h
, for some
h\in{\mathbbm{C}}[a,b]
, then
h
belongs to
G
.

Proof.

Only the last part of the lemma is non-trivial. We will prove the last part by contradiction. Let us assume that
h
is neither even nor odd polynomial. So,
h
admits an even-odd decomposition
h=h_{e}+h_{o}
, where
h_{e}
is even polynomial,
h_{o}
is odd polynomial and
h_{e}\neq 0\neq h_{o}
. Now,
g_{2}
being even or odd polynomial,
g_{1}
admits an even-odd decomposition
g_{1}=g_{2}\cdot h_{e}+g_{2}\cdot h_{o}
, where
g_{2}\cdot h_{e}\neq 0\neq g_{2}\cdot h_{o}
. Hence, we arrive at a contradiction.
∎

Lemma 4.11
.

For
k\in{\mathbbm{N}}\cup\{0\},n\in{\mathbbm{N}}
, the polynomials
f_{k,n}
are odd polynomials.

Proof.

Let
l\geq 0
. Consider the polynomial
f^{l}(z)\in{\mathbbm{Z}}[z,a,b]
. Every monomial term of
f^{l}(z)
is of odd degree. Hence, same is true for
f^{l}(a)
. Therefore,
f^{l}(a)
is an odd polynomial in
{\mathbbm{Z}}[a,b]
for any
l\geq 0
and so is
f_{k,n}=f^{k+n}(a)-f^{k}(a)
, for any
k\geq 0,n\in{\mathbbm{N}}
.
∎

Corollary 4.12
.

Let
k\in{\mathbbm{N}}\cup\{0\},n\in{\mathbbm{N}}
. If the polynomials
h_{l,m}
are irreducible over
{\mathbbm{Q}}
for all
0\leq l\leq k,\;1\leq m\leq n,\;(l,m)\neq(k,n)\in{\mathbbm{Z}}^{2}
, then the polynomial
h_{k,n}
is even or odd polynomial.

Proof.

If the polynomials
h_{l,m}
are irreducible over
{\mathbbm{Q}}
for all
0\leq l\leq k,\;1\leq m,\;m|n,\;(l,m)\neq(k,n)\in{\mathbbm{Z}}^{2}
, then for any such
(l,m)
(including
(k,n)
), one can write

f_{l,m}=h_{l,m}\cdot\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq j\leq l,\;1\leq r,\;r|m,\\
(j,r)\neq(l,m)\in{\mathbbm{Z}}^{2}\end{subarray}}h_{j,r}^{a_{j,r}},\text{ for some }a_{j,r}\in{\mathbbm{N}}

Observe that
h_{0,1}=f_{0,1}=b-a
is an odd polynomial. Applying induction on both
l
and
m
such that
0\leq l\leq k,1\leq m,m|n
, and using the last part of Lemma
4.10
, one gets that
h_{k,n}
is even or odd polynomial.
∎

Lemma 4.13
.

Let
g\in{\mathbbm{C}}[a,b]
be an even or odd polynomial. Let
h\in{\mathbbm{C}}[a,b]
be an irreducible polynomial, having a decomposition
h=h_{e}+h_{o},
where
h_{e}
is even polynomial, and
h_{o}
is odd polynomial. Let

. Then the following statements are true,

1)
h^{\diamond}
is irreducible.

2) In
{\mathbbm{C}}[a,b]
,
h
divides
g\iff h^{\diamond}
divides
g
.

3) If
h
is not even or odd polynomial, then
h
and
h^{\diamond}
are distinct (which means not equal upto associates) irreducible polynomials in
{\mathbbm{C}}[a,b]
.

Proof.

First two parts of the lemma follows from using the change of variable

.

For the third part of the lemma, if

is not even or odd polynomial, then

are constant multiple of each other, then

, which are the polynomials

. As either

or

∎

8.
On

In this section, we study the obstructions to direct generalization of our technique to study irreducibility of
\mathscr{S}_{k,q}
curves, where
k\geq 0,q
odd prime.

Lemma 8.1
.

For any prime
q\in{\mathbbm{N}}
, we have
h_{1,q}=(f^{q}(a)+2a)/(b+2a)
. In another form,
h_{1,q}\equiv h_{0,q}\equiv\sum_{i=0}^{q-1}(b-a)^{3^{i}-1}(\text{mod }3)
.

Proof.

We deduce,

\displaystyle f_{1,q}

\displaystyle=f^{q+1}(a)-f(a)

\displaystyle=(f^{q}(a)-a)\left((f^{q}(a))^{2}+af^{q}(a)+a^{2}-3a^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=(f^{q}(a)-a)^{2}(f^{q}(a)+2a)

\displaystyle=(f_{0,q})^{2}(f^{q}(a)+2a).

So,
h_{1,q}
divides
f^{q}(a)+2a
. As
f^{q}(a)+2a\equiv 3a\;(\text{mod }f_{0,q})
, the polynomials
f^{q}(a)+2a
and
f_{0,q}
are coprime.

As we obtain
h_{1,q}
by factoring out irreducible factors of
f_{1,1}
and
f_{0,q}
from
f_{1,q}
, each raised to their highest power that divides
f_{1,q}
, we get that

h_{1,q}=\frac{f^{q}(a)+2a}{(h_{1,1})^{s}},

where
s
is the highest power of
h_{1,1}
that divides
f^{q}(a)+2a
.

By Equation (
5.4
), we have
h_{1,1}=b+2a
. Putting
b=-2a
, we see that
f^{n}(a)=-2a,
for any
n\in{\mathbbm{N}}
. So,
b+2a
divides
f^{q}(a)+2a
. We need to check if
(b+2a)^{2}
divides
f^{q}(a)+2a
. As
f\equiv z^{3}-a^{3}+b\;(\text{mod }3)
, we have

f^{q}(a)+2a\equiv f^{q}(a)-a\equiv\sum_{i=0}^{p-1}(b-a)^{3^{i}}\;(\text{mod }3).

As
(b-a)^{2}
does not divide
f^{q}(a)+2a
in modulo
3
, we get that
(b+2a)^{2}
does not divide
f^{q}(a)+2a
in
{\mathbbm{Z}}[a,b]
. Hence,

h_{1,q}=\frac{f^{q}(a)+2a}{b+2a}.

Reducing this equation in modulo
3
, we obtain the other form of
h_{1,q}
mentioned in the lemma. To show
h_{1,q}\equiv h_{0,q}(\text{mod }3)
, observe that

f_{0,q}\equiv(b-a)\sum_{i=0}^{q-1}(b-a)^{3^{i}-1}\;(\text{mod }3).

Hence, the lemma is proved.
∎

Lemma 8.2
.

Let
k\in{\mathbbm{N}}
and
q\in{\mathbbm{N}},q
prime. If
h_{1,q}
is irreducible in
{\mathbbm{F}}_{3}[a,b]
, then
h_{k,q}\equiv(h_{1,q})^{N_{k,q}}(\text{mod }3)
, for some
N_{k,q}\in{\mathbbm{N}}
.

Proof.

Let
k,q\in{\mathbbm{N}},q
prime. Then,

\displaystyle f_{k,q}

\displaystyle=f^{k+q}(a)-f^{k}(a)

\displaystyle\equiv\sum_{i=k}^{k+q-1}(b-a)^{3^{i}}(\text{mod }3)

\displaystyle\equiv(b-a)^{3^{k}}\left(\sum_{j=0}^{q-1}(b-a)^{3^{j}-1}\right)^{3^{k}}(\text{mod }3)

As
f_{k,1}\equiv(b-a)^{3^{k}}(\text{mod }3)
, we have
h_{k,q}
divides
\left(\sum_{j=0}^{q-1}(b-a)^{3^{j}-1}\right)^{3^{k}}
in modulo
3
. As
h_{1,q}\equiv\sum_{j=0}^{q-1}(b-a)^{3^{j}-1}(\text{mod }3)
is irreducible modulo
3
, the lemma follows.
∎

The next lemma shows that this method of showing irreducibility of
h_{k,q}
in
{\mathbbm{Q}}[a,b]
, does not extend for any prime
q
other than
2
.

Lemma 8.3
.

The polynomial
h_{1,q}(\text{mod }3)
is irreducible in
{\mathbbm{F}}_{3}[a,b]\iff q=2
.

Proof.

By
\tilde{h}_{1,q},
we will denote the image of
h_{1,q}
under the quotient map
{\mathbbm{Z}}[a,b]\rightarrow{\mathbbm{F}}_{3}[a,b]
. The polynomial
\tilde{h}_{1,q}=\sum_{i=0}^{q-1}(b-a)^{3^{i}-1}
is reducible in
{\mathbbm{F}}_{3}[a,b]
iff
g(x):=\sum_{i=0}^{q-1}x^{3^{i}-1}
is reducible in
{\mathbbm{F}}_{3}[x]
. Consider the polynomial
xg(x)=\sum_{i=0}^{q-1}x^{3^{i}}
. Consider the extension
{\mathbbm{F}}_{3^{q}}
over
{\mathbbm{F}}_{3}
. The orders of the two fields in this extension imply that there are non-zero elements in
{\mathbbm{F}}_{3^{q}}
, for which the trace is
0
under the extension
{\mathbbm{F}}_{3^{q}}/{\mathbbm{F}}_{3}
. Any such element is a root of
xg(x)
, as
Gal({\mathbbm{F}}_{3^{q}}/{\mathbbm{F}}_{3})
is generated by the Frobenius elemnent,
x\mapsto x^{3}
. So,
g(x)
is irreducible in
{\mathbbm{F}}_{3}[x]
if and only if
deg(g(x))
is same as

[{\mathbbm{F}}_{3^{q}}:{\mathbbm{F}}_{3}]=q
. Now,
deg(g(x))=3^{q-1}-1
is equal to
q\iff q=2
. Hence, the lemma is proved.
∎

On Massey products and Rational homogeneous varieties
Luca Rizzi

Abstract
. We study the equivalence between infinitesimal Torelli theorem for smooth hypersurfaces in rational homogeneous varieties with Picard number one and the theory of generalized Massey products. This equivalence shows that the differential of the period map vanishes on an infinitesimal deformation if and only if certain twisted differential forms are elements of the Jacobian ideal of the hypersurface. We also prove an infinitesimal Torelli theorem result for smooth hypersurfaces in log parallelizable varieties.

1.
Introduction

Let
V\subset\mathbb{P}^{n}
be a smooth hypersurface defined by a homogeneous polynomial
F
of degree
d
. Denote by
S
the polynomial ring in
n+1
variables and by
\mathcal{J}
the Jacobian ideal of
F
, that is the ideal of
S
generated by the partial derivatives of
F
. The Jacobian ring is by definition the quotient
R=S/\mathcal{J}
and it is naturally graded.

Griffiths in
[
Griff
]
showed that the graded pieces of the ring
R
give all the pieces of the Hodge structure of
V
. Furthermore, the piece of degree
d
gives exactly the space of infinitesimal deformations of
V
. Using this results he proved that the infinitesimal Torelli problem for projective hypersurfaces is reduced to the study of the injectivity of suitable maps given by polynomial multiplication.

This idea was later developed by Green in
[
Gr1
]
to deal with the general case of smooth sufficiently ample hypersurfaces of a smooth projective variety. Green introduced the notions of pseudo-Jacobian ideal and of pseudo-Jacobian ring and developed an approach to the problem extending the one by Griffiths. In this paper Green was able to solve the infinitesimal and generic Torelli problem for smooth sufficiently ample hypersurfaces.

Now take a smooth projective variety
Y
with
\mathrm{Pic}(Y)=[H]\cdot\mathbb{Z}
where
H
is an effective divisor. Hence for any effective divisor
X\subset Y
there exists a unique
d\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 0}
such that
X
is an element of the linear system
|dH|
. Thus a natural problem is to concretely specify the ampleness condition required by Green and find the minimum
d\in\mathbb{N}
such that the differential of the period map
d\mathcal{P}_{X}
is injective for
X
a smooth element of
|dH|
.

In
[
RZ2
]
and
[
RZ3
]
an interesting equivalence between this problem and the theory of
Massey products
is developed in the case of
Y
a projective space and
Y
a Grassmannian variety respectively. In this paper we generalize these results for the case
Y
rational homogeneous variety of Picard number one.

Let us briefly recall the notion of generalized Massey product, more details of the general theory are discussed in Section
4
; see also the therein quoted bibliography.
Denote by
\mathcal{O}_{Y}(1)
the ample generator of
\mathrm{Pic}(Y)
and by
\mathcal{O}_{Y}(a)=\mathcal{O}_{Y}(1)^{\otimes a}
its twist by an integer
a
and take an infinitesimal deformation
\xi\in H^{1}(X,T_{X})
of
X
. If
X=(\tau=0)
,
\tau\in H^{0}(Y,\mathcal{O}_{Y}(d))
, any infinitesimal deformation is induced by a local family
X+tR=0
where
R\in H^{0}(Y,\mathcal{O}_{Y}(d))
; see: Proposition
5.3
.
We twist by
\mathcal{O}_{X}(2)
the exact sequence associated to the infinitesimal deformation
\xi\in H^{1}(X,T_{X})
to obtain

(1.1)

0\to\mathcal{O}_{X}(2)\to\Omega^{1}_{\mathcal{X}}(2)|_{X}\to\Omega^{1}_{X}(2)\to 0.

Now take
n=\dim X
and a generic
n+1
-dimensional vector space
W<H^{0}(X,\Omega^{1}_{X}(2))
contained in the kernel of the cup-product homomorphism
\delta_{\xi}\colon H^{0}(X,\Omega^{1}_{X}(2))\to H^{1}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(2))
and denote by
\lambda^{i}W
the image of
\bigwedge^{i}W
through the natural homomorphism
\lambda^{i}\colon\bigwedge^{i}H^{0}(X,\Omega^{1}_{X}(2))\to H^{0}(X,\bigwedge^{i}(\Omega^{1}_{X}(2)))
.

Let
\mathcal{B}:=\langle\eta_{1},\ldots,\eta_{n+1}\rangle
be a basis of
W
and
s_{1},\ldots,s_{n+1}\in H^{0}(X,\Omega^{1}_{\mathcal{X}}(2)|_{X})
liftings of, respectively,
\eta_{1},\ldots,\eta_{n+1}
, then the map

\Lambda^{n+1}\colon\bigwedge^{n+1}H^{0}(X,\Omega^{1}_{\mathcal{X}}(2)|_{X})\to H^{0}(X,{\rm{det}}(\Omega^{1}_{\mathcal{X}}(2)|_{X}))

gives the twisted differential form
\Omega:=\Lambda^{n+1}(s_{1}\wedge s_{2}\wedge\ldots\wedge s_{n+1})\in H^{0}(X,{\rm{det}}(\Omega^{1}_{\mathcal{X}}(2)|_{X}))
which is called
generalized adjoint form or Massey product associated to

\xi
,
W
, and
\mathcal{B}
. On the other hand we can also consider the
n+1
global forms
\omega_{i}:=\lambda^{n}(\eta_{1}\wedge\ldots\wedge\eta_{i-1}\wedge{\widehat{\eta_{i}}}\wedge\eta_{i+1}\wedge\ldots\wedge\eta_{n+1})\in\lambda^{n}W
.

By Sequence (
1.1
),

\det(\Omega^{1}_{X}(2))\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(2)=\det(\Omega^{1}_{\mathcal{X}}(2)|_{X})
, and we can construct an obvious homomorphism:

(1.2)

H^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(2))\otimes\lambda^{n}W\to H^{0}(X,{\rm{det}}(\Omega^{1}_{\mathcal{X}}(2)|_{X}).

The Generalized Adjoint Theorem, see
4.4
, fully characterizes the condition of
\Omega
being in the image of this map, that is

\Omega\in\mathrm{Im}\,(H^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(2)\otimes\lambda^{n}W\to H^{0}(X,{\rm{det}}(\Omega^{1}_{\mathcal{X}}(2)|_{X}).

The important point is that to check this condition is equivalent to check if
d\mathcal{P}_{X}(\xi)=0
.

More deeply, we can construct an explicit space of generalized adjoint forms associated to
\xi
in the following way.
First we lift the sections
\eta_{i}

H^{0}(X,\Omega^{1}_{X}(2))
to
H^{0}(Y,\Omega^{1}_{Y}(2))
, then we take the wedge product to obtain a twisted volume form

, independent from
\xi
, such that

\widetilde{\Omega}\in H^{0}(Y,\Omega_{Y}^{N}(2N))
, where
N=\dim Y
. Finally by taking the form
R\in H^{0}(Y,\mathcal{O}_{Y}(d))
which induces the deformation
\xi
, we consider the class
R\cdot{\widetilde{\Omega}}\in H^{0}(Y,\Omega_{Y}^{N}(2N+d))
which by adjunction restricts to
\Omega\in H^{0}(X,\Omega^{N-1}_{X}(2N))=H^{0}(X,{\rm{det}}(\Omega^{1}_{X}(2))\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(2))
.

Recall that a log parallelizable variety is given by a pair

2.
Rational homogeneous varieties with Picard number 1

In this section we briefly review the theory of rational homogeneous varieties while we fix the notation. For representation theory and Lie algebras we refer to
[
FH
]
and
[
H
]
, see also
[
O
]
for some notes on rational homogeneous varieties.

A classical theorem of Borel and Remmert
[
BR
]
asserts that a homogeneous projective complex manifold is a direct product of an abelian variety by a rational homogeneous space. The latter can be described as a quotient
G/P
, where
G
is a semi-simple algebraic group and
P
a parabolic subgroup.
Moreover such rational homogeneous space can be decomposed as

(2.1)

G/P=G_{1}/P_{1}\times\dots\times G_{k}/P_{k}

where the
G_{i}
are simple algebraic groups and
P_{i}<G_{i}
are parabolic subgroups.

As customary we will denote by
\mathfrak{g}
the Lie algebra associated to an algebraic group
G
, that is
\mathfrak{g}=T_{e}G
, and by
\text{ad}\colon\mathfrak{g}\to\text{End}(\mathfrak{g})
its adjoint representation, defined via the Lie bracket by
\text{ad}(x)(y)=[x,y]
. The Killing form on
\mathfrak{g}
is then
K(x,y)=\text{tr}(\text{ad}(x)\circ\text{ad}(y))
.

Let
\mathfrak{h}
be the Cartan subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}
and recall that an element
\alpha
in its dual
\mathfrak{h}^{*}
is called a
root
if

\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}=\{x\in\mathfrak{g}\mid\text{ad}(h)(x)=\alpha(h)x\quad\forall h\in\mathfrak{h}\}

is non trivial. In this case it is well known that
\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}
is a 1-dimensional subspace of
\mathfrak{g}
.
The set of roots is usually denoted by
\Phi
and one has the
Cartan decomposition
of
\mathfrak{g}

\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{h}\oplus\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\Phi}\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}.

Choosing an hyperplane not containing any root, we identify a decomposition
\Phi=\Phi^{+}\cup\Phi^{-}
into positive and negative roots.
An element of
\Phi^{+}
is called a simple root if it cannot be written as the sum of two elements of
\Phi^{+}
, denote by
\Delta=\{\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{l}\}
a set of simple roots for
\mathfrak{g}
.
Every root
\alpha\in\Phi
is a linear combination of elements of
\Delta
with integer coefficients which are either all non-negative or all non-positive.

Since the Killing form
K
is nondegenerate, it gives an isomorphism
\mathfrak{h}\cong\mathfrak{h}^{*}
and also induces a nondegenerate form on
\mathfrak{h}^{*}
which we will denote using simple parenthesis
(\alpha,\beta)
.
This form is positive definite on the real span of the roots
\mathfrak{h}_{\mathbb{R}}^{*}
: it is known that at most two root lengths occur in
\Phi
, hence we will speak of long roots and short roots.
We also denote by
W
be the
Weyl group of
\mathfrak{g}
generated by the reflections
\sigma_{\alpha}
, with respect to the hyperplane orthogonal to
\alpha\in\Phi
. The fundamental Weyl chamber
\mathcal{C}
is the convex set

\mathcal{C}=\{\lambda\in\mathfrak{h}_{\mathbb{R}}^{*}\mid(\lambda,\alpha)>0\quad\forall\alpha\in\Delta\}.

Given a representation
V
of
\mathfrak{g}
,
\lambda\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}
is called a weight if the subspace
V_{\lambda}=\{v\in V\mid h\cdot v=\lambda(h)v,\ \forall h\in\mathfrak{h}\}
is non trivial.
Weights have many important properties, in particular they form the
group of weights

\Lambda=\{\lambda\in\mathfrak{h}_{\mathbb{R}}^{*}\mid\langle\lambda,\alpha\rangle:=2(\lambda,\alpha)/(\alpha,\alpha)\in\mathbb{Z}\quad\forall\alpha\in\Phi\}.

This is a lattice generated by the
fundamental weights

\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{l}
defined by the property that
\langle\lambda_{i},\alpha_{j}\rangle=\delta_{ij}
.
A weight
\lambda
is
dominant
if
\langle\lambda,\alpha_{i}\rangle\geq 0
for every
\alpha_{i}\in\Delta
, equivalently
\lambda
is a combination with non-negative integers of the fundamental weights. The weight

(2.2)

\delta=\sum_{i=1}^{l}\lambda_{i}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\alpha\in\Phi^{+}}\alpha

is dominant and satisfies
\langle\delta,\alpha_{i}\rangle=1
for every
\alpha_{i}
.
The lattice
\Lambda
is partially ordered:
\lambda_{1}>\lambda_{2}
if
\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}
is a sum of positive roots, hence for every representation of
\mathfrak{g}
, it make sense to consider its highest and lowest weights.
If such a representation is irreducible, the highest and lowest weight are unique and they uniquely identify the representation (up to isomorphism).

Now consider again a semi-simple group
G
. Every parabolic subgroup is identified, up to conjugation, by a subset of the simple roots,
\Sigma\subset\Delta
. The cardinality of this subset
\Sigma
determines the rank of the Picard group of
G/P
and since in this paper we are interested in varieties with
\mathrm{Pic}\cong\mathbb{Z}
, it will be enough consider varieties
G/P
where
G
is a simple group and
P
is a parabolic subgroup generated by only one simple root.
Hence we recall that all the simple Lie algebras are classically known and fall in four families
A_{n},B_{n},C_{n}
, and
D_{n}
with five exceptions

Now the subgroup
P
is constructed in the following way.
Take
\alpha_{r}
,
1\leq r\leq l
, a simple root and define these three sets:

(2.3)

\Phi_{1}=\{\alpha\in\Phi\mid\alpha=\sum_{i=1}^{l}n_{i}\alpha_{i},\ n_{r}=0\},

(2.4)

\Phi(\mathfrak{n}^{+})=\{\alpha\in\Phi^{+}\mid\alpha=\sum_{i=1}^{l}n_{i}\alpha_{i},\ n_{r}>0\},

(2.5)

\Phi(\mathfrak{n})=\Phi_{1}\cup\Phi(\mathfrak{n}^{+})

From these and the Cartan decomposition we obtain three subalgebras of
\mathfrak{g}
:

(2.6)

\mathfrak{g}_{1}=\mathfrak{h}\oplus\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\Phi_{1}}\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha},\quad\mathfrak{n}^{+}=\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\Phi(\mathfrak{n}^{+})}\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha},\quad\mathfrak{n}=\mathfrak{h}\oplus\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\Phi(\mathfrak{n})}\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}

In particular
\mathfrak{n}
is the subalgebra which corresponds to the parabolic subgroup
P
, that is
T_{e}P=\mathfrak{n}
.

Since all the rational homogeneous varieties with
\mathrm{Pic}\cong\mathbb{Z}
are constructed in this way, in this paper every variety will be associated to a pair
(\mathfrak{g},\alpha_{r})
. Note that different pairs can still produce isomorphic varieties.

As an important example we have the irreducible Hermitian symmetric spaces of compact type which in turn contain well known classes like Grassmannians
(A_{l},\alpha_{r})
and Quadric hypersurfaces
(B_{l},\alpha_{1})
and
(D_{l},\alpha_{1})
.

We finish this section with some important results on homogeneous vector bundles.
Homogeneous vector bundles are constructed in the following way:
take
\rho\colon P\to\text{GL}(V)
a representation of
P
, then the associated bundle
E_{\rho}
is given by the quotient of
G\times V
by the following action of
P

(g,v)\to(gp,\rho(p^{-1})v).

We will say that
E_{\rho}
is irreducible if
\rho
is an irreducible representation.
It is known, see
[
K
, Page 89]
, that there is a one to one correspondence between irreducible representations of
P
and irreducible representations of
\mathfrak{g}_{1}
with lowest weight
-\lambda
, where
\lambda
is in the sublattice
\Lambda^{+}_{1}=\{\lambda\in\Lambda\mid(\lambda,\alpha_{i})\geq 0,\ \forall i\neq r\}
.
Hence every irreducible vector bundle will be denoted by
E_{-\lambda}
to highlight its corresponding lowest weight.

Definition 2.1
.

A weight
\lambda\in\Lambda
is called

-

singular
if
(\lambda,\alpha)=0
for some
\alpha\in\Phi

-

regular with index
p
if it is not singular and there exist exactly
p
roots
\alpha\in\Phi^{+}

with
(\lambda,\alpha)<0
.

The following theorem, originally proved by Bott in
[
B
]
and then generalized by Kostant in
[
Kos
]
, is fundamental to compute the cohomology of irreducible homogeneous vector bundle.

Theorem 2.2

(Generalized Borel-Weil)
.

Let
E_{-\lambda}
an irreducible homogeneous vector bundle as above. Then

(1)

if the weight
\lambda+\delta
is singular,
H^{q}(G/P,E_{-\lambda})=0
for all
q

(2)

if the weight
\lambda+\delta
is regular of index
p
,
H^{q}(G/P,E_{-\lambda})=0
for
q\neq p
and
H^{p}(G/P,E_{-\lambda})
is an irreducible
G
-module with lowest weight
-\mu
. Here
\mu
is the uniquely determined element of the closure of the fundamental Weyl chamber such that
\mu+\delta
is congruent to
\lambda+\delta
under the Weyl group.

Remark 2.3
.

In particular if
\lambda+\delta
is regular of index
0
,
\lambda
is dominant, therefore
H^{0}(G/P,E_{-\lambda})
is the irreducible module of lowest weight
-\lambda
, which we will denote by
G_{-\lambda}
.

The dimension of such representations can be computed using the Weyl formula
[
H
, Page 139]

(2.7)

\dim G_{-\lambda}=\prod_{\alpha\in\Phi^{+}}\frac{(\lambda+\delta,\alpha)}{(\delta,\alpha)}=\prod_{\alpha\in\Phi^{+}}\frac{\langle\lambda+\delta,\alpha\rangle}{\langle\delta,\alpha\rangle}.

3.
The Cotangent sheaf and its twists

In this section we study the cotangent sheaf
\Omega^{1}_{G/P}
and its twists.
From now on we will denote
G/P
simply by
Y
; as seen before it is given by a pair
(\mathfrak{g},\alpha_{r})
. Denote by
\mathcal{O}_{Y}(1)
the ample generator of
\mathrm{Pic}(Y)
and by
\mathcal{O}_{Y}(a)=\mathcal{O}_{Y}(1)^{\otimes a}
its twist by an integer
a
.
Note that
\mathcal{O}_{Y}(1)
is induced by the irreducible representation of
P
with lowest weight
-\lambda_{r}
, hence
\mathcal{O}_{Y}(a)
has lowest weight
-a\lambda_{r}
.

It is well known that the tangent sheaf of
Y
is a homogeneous bundle and the representation of
P
associated to this bundle comes from the adjoint representation:
P\to\text{GL}(\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{n})
. Hence we can identify the cotangent bundle with the
P
-module
\mathfrak{n}^{+}
and, more generally, the sheaf
\Omega^{p}_{Y}
of
p
-forms on
Y
will be defined by the module
\bigwedge^{p}\mathfrak{n}^{+}
.

The problem with these modules is that they are not irreducible in general and therefore we can not apply directly the Generalized Borel-Weil Theorem
2.2
when we need to compute their cohomology.
In the case of irreducible Hermitian symmetric spaces of compact type, the modules
\bigwedge^{p}\mathfrak{n}^{+}
are at least completely reducible and their decomposition is given by Kostant in
[
Kos
, Corollary 8.2]
, so it is still possible to apply the Borel-Weil theorem to their direct summands.

In the general case however they are not even completely reducible and the idea of how to deal with them is given in
[
K1
]
.
Konno in his works introduces a filtration of the modules
\bigwedge^{p}\mathfrak{n}^{+}
as follows: for every positive integer
i
, define
F^{i}\bigwedge^{p}\mathfrak{n}^{+}
the linear subspace of
\bigwedge^{p}\mathfrak{n}^{+}
spanned by vectors whose weights are
\lambda=\sum_{j=1}^{l}n_{j}\alpha_{j}
with
n_{r}\geq i
. It is known that for two roots
\alpha
and
\beta

[\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha},\mathfrak{g}_{\beta}]=\begin{cases}\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha+\beta}&if $\alpha+\beta$ is a root\\
0&otherwise\end{cases}

hence it is not difficult to see that
F^{i}\bigwedge^{p}\mathfrak{n}^{+}
is invariant under the adjoint action and if we set

G^{i}\bigwedge^{p}\mathfrak{n}^{+}:=F^{i}\bigwedge^{p}\mathfrak{n}^{+}/F^{i+1}\bigwedge^{p}\mathfrak{n}^{+}

this is a completely reducible
P
-module and as a
\mathfrak{g}_{1}
module it can be identified with the subspace of
\bigwedge^{p}\mathfrak{n}^{+}
spanned by the vectors whose weight satisfies
n_{r}=i
.
We use the notation
G^{i}\Omega^{p}_{Y}
to indicate the homogeneous vector bundle induced by
G^{i}\bigwedge^{p}\mathfrak{n}^{+}
.
From this filtration we get a spectral sequence with the following properties, see
[
K
, Proposition 2.2.2]

Proposition 3.1
.

There is a spectral sequence

(3.1)

E^{i,q-i}_{1}=H^{q}(Y,G^{i}\Omega^{p}_{Y}(a))\Rightarrow H^{q}(Y,\Omega^{p}_{Y}(a))

where
G^{i}\Omega^{p}_{Y}(a)=G^{i}\Omega^{p}_{Y}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{Y}(a)
.

If the irreducible decomposition of
G^{i}\bigwedge^{p}\mathfrak{n}^{+}
is

G^{i}\bigwedge^{p}\mathfrak{n}^{+}=\bigoplus_{j}E_{-\mu_{ij}}

with
E_{-\mu_{ij}}
irreducible with lowest weight
-\mu_{ij}
, then
-\mu_{ij}
is a sum of
p
distinct roots in
\Phi(\mathfrak{n}^{+})
and
n_{r}=i
.
Moreover if
E_{-\mu_{ij}}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{Y}(a)=E_{-(\mu_{ij}+a\lambda_{r})}
is the irreducible homogeneous vector bundle with lowest weight
-(\mu_{ij}+a\lambda_{r})
we have

(3.2)

H^{q}(Y,G^{i}\Omega^{p}_{Y}(a))=\bigoplus_{j}H^{q}(Y,E_{-(\mu_{ij}+a\lambda_{r})}).

Using this spectral sequence we can give a first estimate on the cohomology
h^{0}(Y,\Omega^{p}_{Y}(a))

Corollary 3.2

h^{0}(Y,\Omega^{p}_{Y}(a))\leq\sum_{i}h^{0}(Y,G^{i}\Omega^{p}_{Y}(a))

However we can get a more precise result on

Lemma 3.4

The weights

(1)

-\alpha_{r}+a\lambda_{r}+\delta
are singular if

and regular of index 0 if

(2)

-\lambda_{r}+\lambda^{\prime}+a\lambda_{r}+\delta
are regular of index 0

(3)

-2\lambda_{r}+\lambda^{\prime}+a\lambda_{r}+\delta
are singular if

and regular of index 0 if

Proof.

This is a simple computation on the bilinear form
(,)
. Recall that
\langle\lambda_{i},\alpha_{j}\rangle=\delta_{ij}
and
(\lambda_{i},\alpha_{j})=\delta_{ij}(\alpha_{j},\alpha_{j})/2
and that
\delta=\sum\lambda_{i}
.
For every simple root
\alpha_{j}
we have

(-\alpha_{r}+a\lambda_{r}+\delta,\alpha_{j})=-(\alpha_{r},\alpha_{j})+a(\lambda_{r},\alpha_{j})+(\delta,\alpha_{j})=\begin{cases}(a-1)/2(\alpha_{r},\alpha_{r})&if $r=j$\\
-(\alpha_{r},\alpha_{j})+(\lambda_{j},\alpha_{j})&if $r\neq j$\end{cases}

Since it is well known that
(\alpha_{r},\alpha_{j})\leq 0
for
\alpha_{r},\alpha_{j}
distinct simple roots, the case

is always positive. If
a>1
also the

case is positive, hence we have that the weight is regular of index 0. On the other hand if
a=1
,
(-\alpha_{r}+a\lambda_{r}+\delta,\alpha_{r})=0
hence the weight is singular.

In the case of
-\lambda_{r}+\lambda^{\prime}+a\lambda_{r}+\delta
we have

(-\lambda_{r}+\lambda^{\prime}+a\lambda_{r}+\delta,\alpha_{j})=(a\lambda_{r}+\lambda^{\prime\prime},\alpha_{j})=\begin{cases}a(\lambda_{r},\alpha_{r})&if $r=j$\\
(\lambda^{\prime\prime},\alpha_{j})&if $r\neq j$\end{cases}

which is always positive and shows that
-\lambda_{r}+\lambda^{\prime}+a\lambda_{r}+\delta
is regular of index 0.

Lastly in the case of

we have

(-2\lambda_{r}+\lambda^{\prime}+a\lambda_{r}+\delta,\alpha_{j})=((a-1)\lambda_{r}+\lambda^{\prime\prime},\alpha_{j})=\begin{cases}(a-1)(\lambda_{r},\alpha_{r})&if $r=j$\\
(\lambda^{\prime\prime},\alpha_{j})&if $r\neq j$\end{cases}

and the weight is regular of index 0 if

while if

it is singular because
(-2\lambda_{r}+\lambda^{\prime}+a\lambda_{r}+\delta,\alpha_{r})=0
.

4.
Review of the Generalized adjoint theory

The theory of adjoint forms was introduced in
[
CP
]
and
[
PZ
]
and later developed and applied in
[
Ra
]
,
[
PR
]
,
[
CNP
]
,
[
G-A
]
,
[
BGN
]
,
[
RZ1
]
, and recently
[
CRZ
]
. The basic idea is to study global
1
-forms and
n
-forms of a complex smooth variety of dimension
n
to get some information on its infinitesimal deformations.
Even if the variety is regular, that is there are no global
1
-forms, this theory can be generalized and applied, see
[
RZ2
]
,
[
RZ3
]
. This is the generalization that we need in this paper, hence we will recall the basic construction.
Since adjoint forms are also called Massey products, see
[
PT
]
,
[
RZ4
]
, in this paper we stick mostly with the second denomination to avoid possible confusion with the adjoint representation of a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
.

Let
X
and
\xi
be respectively a smooth compact complex variety of dimension
m
and a class
\xi\in\text{Ext}^{1}(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{L})
where

\mathcal{F}
and
\mathcal{L}
are two locally free sheaves on
X
of rank
n
and
1
respectively. Then the extension class
\xi
gives a rank
n+1
vector bundle
\mathcal{E}
on
X
which fits in an exact sequence:

(4.1)

0\to\mathcal{L}\to\mathcal{E}\to\mathcal{F}\to 0.

By taking the highest wedge product of (
4.1
), the determinant sheaf
\det\mathcal{F}:=\bigwedge^{n}\mathcal{F}
fits into the exact sequence:

(4.2)

0\to\bigwedge^{n-1}\mathcal{F}\otimes\mathcal{L}\to\bigwedge^{n}\mathcal{E}\to\det\mathcal{F}\to 0,

which still corresponds to
\xi
via the isomorphisms
\text{Ext}^{1}(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{L})\cong\text{Ext}^{1}(\det\mathcal{F},\bigwedge^{n-1}\mathcal{F}\otimes\mathcal{L})\cong H^{1}(X,\mathcal{F}^{\vee}\otimes\mathcal{L})
.

A natural problem is to find conditions on the behaviour of the global sections of the involved vector bundles in order to have the splitting of (
4.1
). From now on, assume that the connecting homomorphism

\delta_{\xi}\colon H^{0}(X,\mathcal{F})\to H^{1}(X,\mathcal{L})
has a kernel of dimension sufficiently high. More precisely assume that there exists a subspace
W\subset\ker\delta_{\xi}
of dimension
n+1
. Choose a basis
\mathcal{B}:=\{\eta_{1},\ldots,\eta_{n+1}\}
of
W
. By definition we can take liftings
s_{1},\ldots,s_{n+1}\in H^{0}(X,\mathcal{E})
of the sections
\eta_{1},\ldots,\eta_{n+1}
. If we consider the natural map

\Lambda^{n}\colon\bigwedge^{n}H^{0}(X,\mathcal{E})\to H^{0}(X,\bigwedge^{n}\mathcal{E})

we can define the sections

(4.3)

\Omega_{i}:=\Lambda^{n}(s_{1}\wedge\ldots\wedge\widehat{s_{i}}\wedge\ldots\wedge s_{n+1})

for
i=1,\ldots,n+1
. Denote by
\omega_{i}
, for
i=1,\ldots,n+1
, the corresponding sections in
H^{0}(X,\det\mathcal{F})
. By commutativity between evaluation of wedge product and restriction it easily follows that
\omega_{i}=\lambda^{n}(\eta_{1}\wedge\ldots\wedge\widehat{\eta_{i}}\wedge\ldots\wedge\eta_{n+1})
, where
\lambda^{n}
is the natural morphism

\lambda^{n}\colon\bigwedge^{n}H^{0}(X,\mathcal{F})\to H^{0}(X,\det\mathcal{F}).

Definition 4.1
.

We denote by
\lambda^{n}W
the subspace of
H^{0}(X,\det\mathcal{F})
generated by
\omega_{1},\ldots,\omega_{n+1}
.
If
\lambda^{n}W
is nontrivial we call
D_{W}
the fixed divisor of the induced sublinear system
|\lambda^{n}W|\subset\mathbb{P}(H^{0}(X,\det\mathcal{F}))
and
Z_{W}
the base locus of its moving part
|M_{W}|\subset\mathbb{P}(H^{0}(X,\det\mathcal{F}(-D_{W})))
.

Definition 4.2
.

The form
\Omega\in H^{0}(X,\det\mathcal{E})
corresponding to
s_{1}\wedge\ldots\wedge s_{n+1}
via

(4.4)

\Lambda^{n+1}\colon\bigwedge^{n+1}H^{0}(X,\mathcal{E})\to H^{0}(X,\det\mathcal{E})

is called
generalized adjoint form or Massey product associated to W
.

Remark 4.3
.

This section vanishes on
D_{W}
and
Z_{W}
, that is
\Omega
in the image of the natural injection
\det\mathcal{E}(-D_{W})\otimes\mathcal{I}_{Z_{W}}\to\det\mathcal{E}
.

The basic idea is that, in a split exact sequence, generalized Massey products as
\Omega
do not add any information which is not already given by the top forms
\omega_{i}\in H^{0}(X,\det\mathcal{F})
,
i=1,\dots,n+1
. Conversely, what can we say about the exact sequence if generalized Massey product do not add any information?
More precisely, since
{\rm{det}}(\mathcal{E})={\rm{det}}(\mathcal{F})\otimes\mathcal{L}
, we will focus on the condition

(4.5)

\Omega\in\mathrm{Im}\,(H^{0}(X,\mathcal{L})\otimes\lambda^{n}W\to H^{0}(X,\det\mathcal{E})).

By
[
RZ2
, Theorem [A] and Theorem [B]]
we have:

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
\Omega\in H^{0}(X,\det\mathcal{E})
be a generalized Massey product associated to
W
as above.
If
\Omega\in\mathrm{Im}\,(H^{0}(X,\mathcal{L})\otimes\lambda^{n}W\to H^{0}(X,\det\mathcal{E}))

then
\xi\in\ker(H^{1}(X,\mathcal{F}^{\vee}\otimes\mathcal{L})\to H^{1}(X,\mathcal{F}^{\vee}\otimes\mathcal{L}(D_{W})))
. Viceversa if
H^{0}(X,\mathcal{L})\cong H^{0}(X,\mathcal{L}(D_{W}))
and if

\xi\in\ker(H^{1}(X,\mathcal{F}^{\vee}\otimes\mathcal{L})\to H^{1}(X,\mathcal{F}^{\vee}\otimes\mathcal{L}(D_{W})))
, then
\Omega\in\mathrm{Im}\,(H^{0}(X,\mathcal{L})\otimes\lambda^{n}W\to H^{0}(X,\det\mathcal{E}))
.

Remark 4.5
.

Note that if
D_{W}=0
the above theorem is a criterion for the vanishing of
\xi
and the splitting of Sequence
4.1
.

To put things in perspective, in this paper the variety
X
is a smooth member of the linear system
|\mathcal{O}_{Y}(d)|
and from now on we will assume that

is not a projective space, since this case has already been covered in
[

]
, and that

is

. The class

. This infinitesimal deformation gives an exact sequence

0\to\mathcal{O}_{X}\to\Omega^{1}_{\mathcal{X}}|_{X}\to\Omega^{1}_{X}\to 0

but we can not study this sequence directly using the theory of Massey products because the sheaf

does not have in general global sections, whereas we need at least

The idea is of course to twist this sequence by

After doing this we obtain the sequence

which still associated to

Once this is done we are in the setting required to apply Theorem

, in fact note that every subspace

is also automatically in

by the following easy

.

The cohomology map

Proof.

This is easy because

In fact by the exact sequence defining

Recall the following definition from

Definition 5.1
.

We say that a property holds for a sufficiently ample line bundle

Given the sheaf

6.
The dimension of
H^{0}(X,\Omega^{1}_{X}(2))

As discussed in Section
4
, we want to use the theory of generalized Massey product, but to do so we have to choose an appropriate

such that

has a high enough number of liftable sections.
As pointed out in Proposition

such that

Both in the case of smooth hypersurfaces of the projective spaces and of Grassmannians, studied in

and in
[

respectively, the choice

does the trick. Hence

We will start with the following

Lemma 6.1

If

is a smooth hypersurface of degree

in

,

, then
H^{0}(Y,\Omega^{1}_{Y}(2))=H^{0}(X,\Omega^{1}_{X}(2))

Proof.

We look at the short exact sequences

(6.1)

0\to\mathcal{O}_{X}(2-d)\to\Omega^{1}_{Y}(2)|_{X}\to\Omega^{1}_{X}(2)\to 0

and

(6.2)

0\to\Omega^{1}_{Y}(2-d)\to\Omega^{1}_{Y}(2)\to\Omega^{1}_{Y}(2)|_{X}\to 0

Using the first we get the isomorphism
H^{0}(X,\Omega^{1}_{Y}(2)|_{X})=H^{0}(X,\Omega^{1}_{X}(2))
since

for

by the sequence

and the vanishings of
[
K
, Theorem 3.1.1]
.
In the same fashion from the second we get the isomorphism

H^{i}(Y,\Omega^{1}_{Y}(2-d))=0
for

.
∎

By this simple Lemma it will be enough to look at the dimension of the spaces

and this will be done using the machinery introduced in Section

and in particular in Proposition

.

Theorem 6.2
.

Let

given by

be one of the varieties in Table

, and let

be a smooth hypersurface of degree

Proof.

Since the computations are often similar, we only show some meaningful cases and simply give an overview of the others. We also show how in some cases we can use classically know cohomological dimensions as

and

Case

These are Grassmannian varieties. The result for this class is the content of

, see also

Case

These are quadric hypersurfaces

in

. The result can be easily obtained by a cohomological analysis of the twisted conormal exact sequence

Case
(B_{l},\alpha_{r})
with

For this case the computation is very similar to the case

Case
(B_{l},\alpha_{r})
with

Also in this case the dimension can be directly computed, but since Konno in

hence the inequality to prove is

For simplicity we prove that

\binom{2l+1}{r-2}\geq 2r(l-r)+r(r+1)=r(2l-r+1)

which of course implies the previous inequality. Developing the binomial coefficient we have

\frac{(2l+1)(2l)\cdots(2l-r+4)}{(r-2)!}\geq r(2l-r+1)

and since
2l-r+4>2l-r+1
this can be simplified to

\frac{(2l+1)(2l)\cdots(2l-r+5)}{(r-2)!}\geq r.

Dividing by

we get

\frac{2l+1}{r}\cdot\frac{2l}{r-2}\cdots\frac{2l-r+5}{3}\cdot\frac{1}{2}\geq 1

By

Case
(C_{l},\alpha_{r})

It is not difficult to see that we have

\langle\delta,\alpha\rangle=\begin{cases}j-i&if $\alpha\in A$\\
l-i+1&if $\alpha\in B$\\
2l-i-j+2&if $\alpha\in C$\end{cases}

Now if

and

\langle\lambda_{r-1},\alpha\rangle=\begin{cases}1&if $\alpha_{r-1}$ appears in $\alpha$\\
0&otherwise\end{cases}

hence the only

(6.4)

\begin{split}\prod_{\alpha\in A}\frac{2\langle\lambda_{r-1},\alpha\rangle+\langle\delta,\alpha\rangle}{\langle\delta,\alpha\rangle}=\prod_{i=1}^{r-1}\prod_{j=r}^{l}\frac{2+j-i}{j-i}=\prod_{i=1}^{r-1}\frac{(2+r-i)(3+r-i)\cdots(2+l-i)}{(r-i)(r+1-i)\cdots(l-i)}=\\

This is equivalent to

\frac{(l+1)(l)\cdots(l-r+3)}{r!}\cdot\frac{(l)(l-1)\cdots(l-r+2)}{r!}\geq(2l-r+1)

and, recalling that

Hypersurfaces in log parallelizable varieties

As we mentioned earlier, in
[
RZ2
]
the case of smooth hypersurfaces in projective spaces is studied and then as generalization we have tackled the case of smooth hypersurfaces in Grassmannians in
[

and the case of smooth hypersurfaces in rational homogeneous varieties with Picard number one in this paper.
A different road that can be taken starting form the case of projective spaces is the study of smooth hypersurfaces in toric varieties and, more in general, of smooth hypersurfaces in log parallelizable varieties.
This is what we will do now.

Consider a pair
(X,D)
where
X
is a smooth variety and
D
is a reduced normal crossing divisor in
X
. That is,
D
is an effective divisor locally given on an open set
U
by
z_{1}z_{2}\cdots z_{k}=0
. We can define the sheaf
\Omega^{1}_{X}(\text{log }D)
of

as the locally free
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-module generated by
dz_{1}/z_{1},\ldots,dz_{k}/z_{k},dz_{k+1},\ldots,dz_{n}
and its dual
T_{X}(-\text{log }D)
is the subsheaf of the tangent sheaf
T_{X}
consisting of those derivations that preserve the ideal sheaf
\mathcal{O}_{X}(-D)
. This is also locally free and locally is generated by
z_{1}\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{1}},\dots,z_{k}\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{k}},\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{k+1}},\dots,\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{n}}
.

Now assume that a connected algebraic group
G
acts on
X
and preserves
D
, then from the natural morphism of Lie algebras

\mathfrak{g}\to\Gamma(X,T_{X})

we have a map of sheaves

\mathcal{O}_{X}\otimes\mathfrak{g}\to T_{X}

which factors through a map

\mathcal{O}_{X}\otimes\mathfrak{g}\to T_{X}(-\text{log }D).

The variety
X
is said
log homogeneous under
G
with boundary
D
if this map is surjective and
log parallelizable under
G
with boundary
D
if this map is an isomorphism.
Actually if
X
is complete, it is log homogeneous (resp. log parallelizable) under some group
G
with boundary
D
if and only if the sheaf
T_{X}(-\text{log }D)
is generated by its global sections (resp. is trivial). We then say that
X
is log homogeneous (resp. log parallelizable) with boundary
D
without specifying the group
G
. For more details on log homogeneous varieties we refer to
[
Br1
]
,
[
Br2
]
.

Here we deal with log parallelizable varieties and we recall some useful facts for later use.
Take

From the fact that

\Omega_{X}^{n}=\mathcal{O}_{X}(-D)=\mathcal{O}_{X}(-\sum_{j=1}^{r}D_{j})

Furthermore we have the exact sequences given by the residue

Remark 8.2

Note that the canonical sheaf of

We recall that toric varieties are an important subclass of log parallelizable varieties and some important cohomological results of toric varieties extend in fact to all log parallelizable varieties. The first one is the following

Proposition 8.3
.

Let
L
be an ample invertible sheaf on a log parallelizable variety
X
. Then

H^{i}(X,L)=0

for all
i>0
.

Proof.

We work using induction on
n=\dim X
.
Consider the exact sequence

(8.5)

0\to L(-D)\to L\to L|_{D}\to 0

Recall that the canonical sheaf of
X
is
\mathcal{O}_{X}(-D)
hence
H^{i}(X,L(-D))
vanishes for

by Kodaira vanishing.

If
n=1
it immediately follows that
H^{1}(X,L)=0
.

If
n>1
we only have to prove the vanishings of
H^{i}(D,L|_{D})
which come from the induction hypothesis with some caution on the number of components of the divisor
D
. In fact if
D
has only one irreducible component this vanishing is immediate by induction because by Proposition
8.1
we have that
D
is log parallelizable. Otherwise we write
D=D_{1}+D^{1}
as before and by the exact sequence

0\to\mathcal{O}_{D_{1}}(-D^{1})\to\mathcal{O}_{D}\to\mathcal{O}_{D^{1}}\to 0

tensored by
L
we have

0\to L(-D^{1})|_{D_{1}}\to L|_{D}\to L|_{D^{1}}\to 0.

The vanishing of the higher cohomology of
L(-D^{1})|_{D_{1}}
comes again by Kodaira vanishing, on the other hand for
L|_{D^{1}}
we can iterate the process and reduce again the number of components

0\to L(-D^{1,2})|_{D_{2}}\to L|_{D^{1}}\to L|_{D^{1,2}}\to 0.

Now note that the vanishing of the higher cohomology of
L(-D^{1,2})|_{D_{2}}
is not immediate by Kodaira vanishing because
\mathcal{O}_{D_{2}}(-D^{1,2})
is not the canonical sheaf of
D_{2}
, in fact
\mathcal{O}_{D_{2}}(-D^{2})
is. Nevertheless by the exact sequence

0\to L(-D^{2})|_{D_{2}}\to L(-D^{1,2})|_{D_{2}}\to L(-D^{1,2})|_{D_{1,2}}\to 0

we see that the higher cohomologies of
L(-D^{1,2})|_{D_{2}}
vanish because those of
L(-D^{2})|_{D_{2}}
and
L(-D^{1,2})|_{D_{1,2}}
vanish by Kodaira on
D_{2}
and

respectively.
Hence it is not difficult to see that we can iterate this process until we obtain all the necessary vanishings.
∎

Note that the vanishing of

. Similarly the vanishing of

for

and

follows by Kodaira vanishing theorem.

Now we use decreasing induction on the number of irreducible components of

using Sequence

tensored by

If we denote as before

, then the vanishing of

We end this section with an infinitesimal Torelli result for certain smooth hypersurfaces in log parallelizable varieties. The analogue for hypersurfaces in toric varieties is in

Take an ample line bundle
L
on
X
and
Z
a smooth element in the linear system
|L|
. We assume that
\Omega^{n}_{X}\otimes L
is ample and that

(8.6)

H^{0}(X,\Omega^{n}_{X}\otimes L)\otimes H^{0}(X,\Omega^{n}_{X}\otimes L^{n-1})\to H^{0}(X,{\Omega^{n}_{X}}\otimes\Omega^{n}_{X}\otimes L^{n})

is surjective. This second hypothesis is strictly related to the following problem which is, at least for toric varieties, deeply studied:

Problem 8.5
.

For every
L_{1}
and
L_{2}
ample line bundles on a smooth projective toric variety
X
the multiplication map

(8.7)

H^{0}(X,L_{1})\otimes H^{0}(X,L_{2})\to H^{0}(X,L_{1}\otimes L_{2})

is surjective.

Fakhruddin
[

proved that this question has a positive answer for an ample line

L_{1}
and a globally generated line bundle
L_{2}
on a smooth toric surface.
Moreover it is well known that for an ample line bundle L on a toric variety of dimension

H^{0}(X,L^{m})\otimes H^{0}(X,L)\to H^{0}(X,L^{m+1})

is surjective for

. Hence our second hypothesis is very natural since we assume that
\Omega^{n}_{X}\otimes L
is ample.

Theorem 8.6
.

Let
Z
be a smooth element in the linear system
|L|
with

Z

Proof.

To prove that the infinitesimal Torelli holds for

is injective. In fact this map is the highest piece of the derivative of the period map, hence if (

The idea is to prove the surjectivity of the dual of (

(8.9)

that is by Serre duality

First note that from the isomorphism

composed with the residue map we obtain a map

\phi\colon H^{0}(X,\Omega^{n}_{X}(Z))\cong H^{0}(X,\Omega^{n}_{X}(\textnormal{log }Z))\stackrel{{\scriptstyle Res}}{{\rightarrow}}H^{0}(Z,\Omega^{n-1}_{Z}).

Now consider the exact sequence

0\to\Omega^{2}_{X}(\textnormal{log }Z)\to\Omega^{2}_{X}(Z)\to\Omega^{3}_{X}(2Z)|_{Z}\to\Omega^{4}_{X}(3Z)|_{Z}\to\dots\to\Omega^{n}_{X}((n-1)Z)|_{Z}\to 0

This gives in cohomology a map

H^{0}(Z,\Omega^{n}_{X}((n-1)Z)|_{Z})\to H^{n-2}(X,\Omega^{2}_{X}(\textnormal{log }Z))

which composed with restriction and the residue gives

\phi^{\prime}\colon H^{0}(X,\Omega^{n}_{X}((n-1)Z))\to H^{0}(Z,\Omega^{n}_{X}((n-1)Z)|_{Z})\to H^{n-2}(X,\Omega^{2}_{X}(\textnormal{log }Z))\stackrel{{\scriptstyle Res}}{{\rightarrow}}H^{n-2}(Z,\Omega^{1}_{Z}).

Taking the tensor product of Sequence (
8.11
) with the ample line bundle
\Omega^{n}_{X}\otimes L
we obtain the exact sequence

(8.12)

\begin{split}0\to\Omega^{2}_{X}(\textnormal{log }Z)\otimes\Omega^{n}_{X}(Z)\to\Omega^{2}_{X}(Z)\otimes\Omega^{n}_{X}(Z)\to\Omega^{3}_{X}(2Z)|_{Z}\otimes\Omega^{n-1}_{Z}\to\dots{\\
}\dots\to\Omega^{n}_{X}((n-1)Z)|_{Z}\otimes\Omega^{n-1}_{Z}\to 0.\end{split}

By Theorem
8.4
and sequence

0\to\Omega_{X}^{j}((j-2)Z)\otimes\Omega^{n}_{X}(Z)\to\Omega_{X}^{j}((j-1)Z)\otimes\Omega^{n}_{X}(Z)\to\Omega_{X}^{j}((j-1)Z)|_{Z}\otimes\Omega^{n-1}_{Z}\to 0

it is not difficult to see that
H^{i}(Z,\Omega_{X}^{j}((j-1)Z)|_{Z}\otimes\Omega^{n-1}_{Z})=0
for
i\geq 1
and since also
H^{n-1}(X,\Omega^{2}_{X}(Z)\otimes\Omega^{n}_{X}(Z))=0
we have a surjective map

(8.13)

H^{0}(Z,\Omega^{n}_{X}((n-1)Z)|_{Z}\otimes\Omega^{n-1}_{Z})\to H^{n-2}(X,\Omega^{2}_{X}(\textnormal{log }Z)\otimes\Omega^{n}_{X}(Z)).

By the exact sequence

0\to\Omega^{2}_{X}\otimes\Omega^{n}_{X}(Z)\to\Omega^{2}_{X}(\textnormal{log }Z)\otimes\Omega^{n}_{X}(Z)\to\Omega^{1}_{Z}\otimes\Omega^{n-1}_{Z}\to 0

and the vanishing of
H^{n-1}(X,\Omega^{2}_{X}\otimes\Omega^{n}_{X}(Z))
we have that the residue map

H^{n-2}(X,\Omega^{2}_{X}(\textnormal{log }Z)\otimes\Omega^{n}_{X}(Z))\to H^{n-2}(Z,\Omega^{1}_{Z}\otimes\Omega^{n-1}_{Z})

is also surjective.

Finally
H^{1}(X,\Omega^{n}_{X}\otimes\Omega^{n}_{X}((n-1)Z))=0
and the restriction

H^{0}(X,\Omega^{n}_{X}((n-1)Z)\otimes\Omega^{n}_{X}(Z))\to H^{0}(Z,\Omega^{n}_{X}((n-1)Z)|_{Z}\otimes\Omega^{n-1}_{Z})

is also surjective.

Hence taking the composition of (
8.13
) together with the residue and the restriction we have a surjective map

\psi\colon H^{0}(X,\Omega^{n}_{X}\otimes\Omega^{n}_{X}(nZ))\to H^{n-2}(Z,\Omega^{1}_{Z}\otimes\Omega^{n-1}_{Z}).

We can write the following commutative diagram

(8.14)

\textstyle{H^{0}(Z,\Omega^{n-1}_{Z})\otimes H^{n-2}(Z,\Omega^{1}_{Z})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

Since

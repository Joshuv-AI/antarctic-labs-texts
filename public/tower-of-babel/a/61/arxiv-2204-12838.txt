Remarks on some integral formulas for G 2 \operatorname{\textsl{G}}_{2} -structures
Francisco Martín Cabrera

Abstract
. For seven-dimensional Riemannian manifolds equipped with a G 2 \operatorname{\textsl{G}}_{2} -structure, we show in a full detailed way that all integral formulas and divergence equations, given by diverse authors, are agree with the ones displayed here in terms of the intrinsic torsion of the G 2 \operatorname{\textsl{G}}_{2} -structure. Likewise the components of such an intrinsic torsion is expressed by means of exterior algebra. Keywords and phrases: G G -structure, intrinsic torsion, minimal connection, G 2 \operatorname{\textsl{G}}_{2} -structure, vector cross product, G 2 \operatorname{\textsl{G}}_{2} -map 2000 MSC : 53C10, 53C15

1.
Introduction

By using different tools, diverse authors, Bryant in
[
5
]
, Bor and Hernández Lamoneda in
[
3
]
, Friedrich and Ivanov in
[
9
]
and Niedziałomski in
[
16
]
, have deduced integral formulas and divergence equations for Riemannian manifolds equipped with a
\operatorname{\textsl{G}}_{2}
-structure. They express the scalar curvature of the manifold in terms related with components of the intrinsic torsion of the
\operatorname{\textsl{G}}_{2}
-structure. Such terms are different in each formula. Thus it is natural to ask for the way in which all mentioned formulas are agree and the correlation between them.

Our purpose here is to show in a detailed way that all integral formulas, given by the above mentioned authors, are agree with

\int_{M}s\,\mathrm{vol}_{M}=\int_{M}9\|\xi_{(1)}\|^{2}-\frac{3}{2}\|\xi_{(2)}\|^{2}-\frac{3}{2}\|\xi_{(3)}\|^{2}+\frac{15}{2}\|\xi_{(4)}\|^{2}\;\mathrm{vol}_{M},

where
s
is the scalar curvature,
M
is a closed manifold equipped with a
\operatorname{\textsl{G}}_{2}
-structure and
\xi
denotes the intrinsic torsion. The terms
\xi_{(i)}
denote
\operatorname{\textsl{G}}_{2}
-components of
\xi
. Likewise, we also prove that all divergence equations, given by such authors, are agree with

s=9\|\xi_{(1)}\|^{2}-\frac{3}{2}\|\xi_{(2)}\|^{2}-\frac{3}{2}\|\xi_{(3)}\|^{2}+\frac{15}{2}\|\xi_{(4)}\|^{2}-6\,\mathrm{div}\,\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}_{7}}\xi_{e_{i}}e_{i}.

Correlations between the integral formulas and between the divergence equations have already been pointed out by Niedziałomski in
[
16
]
. Here we give a description of them. This is done following notations for
\operatorname{\textsl{G}}_{2}
-structures used by Fernández and Gray in
[
7
]
and by the present author in
[
13
,
12
,
14
]
. In our view, the present text illustrates the advantages of such notations.

Using the map
\xi\longrightarrow-\xi\varphi=\nabla\varphi
, where
\varphi
is the fundamental three-form of the
\operatorname{\textsl{G}}_{2}
-structure, it is found that all information about the intrinsic torsion is contained in the covariant derivative
\nabla\varphi
. From this it is deduced that all information about the intrinsic torsion is contained in the exterior derivatives
d\varphi
and
d\star\varphi
(
\star
denotes Hodge star operator). This is described in
[
15
]
(see also Table I in
[
12
]
). Thus a useful alternative way to find such information is by studying
d\varphi
and
d\star\varphi
. Because all the necessary tools are displayed in first sections, in last section we take the chance to express the components of the intrinsic torsion
\xi
in terms of
d\varphi
and of
d\star\varphi
.

2.
Preliminaries

Let
\{e_{0},\dots,e_{6}\}
denote the standard positive orthonormal basis of
\mathbb{R}^{7}
. The exceptional Lie group
\operatorname{\textsl{G}}_{2}
is the subgroup of
\operatorname{\textsl{SO}}(7)
consisting of those elements which fix the three-form
\varphi
defined by

(2.1)

\varphi=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}_{7}}e_{i}^{*}{\mathchoice{\,{\scriptstyle\wedge}\,}{{\scriptstyle\wedge}}{{\scriptscriptstyle\wedge}}{{\scriptscriptstyle\wedge}}}e_{i+1}^{*}\wedge e_{i+3}^{*},

where we have denoted by
e_{i}^{*}
the dual one-forms of
e_{i}
. A
Cayley basis
is a
\{u_{0}^{*},\dots,u_{6}^{*}\}
for covectors such that, in terms of this basis,
\varphi
is written as in (
2.1
). The name Cayley basis is used because, in this context, it is well known the identification of
\mathbb{R}^{7}
with the imaginary octonions or Cayley numbers
\mathrm{Im}\,\mathbb{O}
.
The dual basis, denoted by
\{u_{0},\dots,u_{6}\}
of a Cayley basis, is also called
Cayley basis
for vectors. Thus the standard basis
\{e_{0},\dots,e_{6}\}
is a Cayley basis.

Here we fix
\mathrm{Vol}=e_{0}^{*}{\mathchoice{\,{\scriptstyle\wedge}\,}{{\scriptstyle\wedge}}{{\scriptscriptstyle\wedge}}{{\scriptscriptstyle\wedge}}}\dots{\mathchoice{\,{\scriptstyle\wedge}\,}{{\scriptstyle\wedge}}{{\scriptscriptstyle\wedge}}{{\scriptscriptstyle\wedge}}}e_{6}^{*}
as volume form and, denoting the corresponding Hodge star operator by
\star
, the four-form

\star\varphi=-\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}_{7}}e_{i+2}^{*}{\mathchoice{\,{\scriptstyle\wedge}\,}{{\scriptstyle\wedge}}{{\scriptscriptstyle\wedge}}{{\scriptscriptstyle\wedge}}}e_{i+4}^{*}\wedge e_{i+5}^{*}{\mathchoice{\,{\scriptstyle\wedge}\,}{{\scriptstyle\wedge}}{{\scriptscriptstyle\wedge}}{{\scriptscriptstyle\wedge}}}e_{i+6}^{*}

will play a relevant rôle along the present text.

The euclidean metric
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
on
\mathbb{R}^{7}
induces the following inner product on skew symmetric
p
-forms:

(\alpha,\beta)=\frac{1}{p!}\sum_{i_{1},\dots,i_{p}\in\mathbb{Z}_{7}}\alpha(e_{i_{1}},\dots,e_{i_{p}})\beta(e_{i_{1}},\dots,e_{i_{p}}),

for all
\alpha,\beta\in\Lambda^{p}\mathbb{R}^{7*}
. The corresponding norm of
(\cdot,\cdot)
will be denoted by
|\cdot|
. The inner product
(\cdot,\cdot)
is widely used in the literature and satisfies the identity

\alpha{\mathchoice{\,{\scriptstyle\wedge}\,}{{\scriptstyle\wedge}}{{\scriptscriptstyle\wedge}}{{\scriptscriptstyle\wedge}}}\star\beta=(\alpha,\beta)\,\mathrm{Vol}.

It follows
(\varphi,\varphi)=(\star\varphi,\star\varphi)=7
and
\mathrm{Vol}=\frac{1}{7}\,\varphi\,{\mathchoice{\,{\scriptstyle\wedge}\,}{{\scriptstyle\wedge}}{{\scriptscriptstyle\wedge}}{{\scriptscriptstyle\wedge}}}\star\varphi
. Note also that
(\star\alpha,\star\beta)=(\alpha,\beta)
.

For general tensors, it is used the inner product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
given by

\langle\Psi,\Phi\rangle=\sum_{i_{1},\dots,i_{r},j_{1},\dots,j_{s}\in\mathbb{Z}_{7}}\Psi^{i_{1}\dots i_{r}}_{j_{1}\dots j_{r}}\Phi^{i_{1}\dots i_{r}}_{j_{1}\dots j_{r}},

for all
(r,s)
-tensors
\Psi
and
\Phi
on
\mathbb{R}^{7}
, where
\Psi^{i_{1}\dots i_{r}}_{j_{1}\dots j_{r}}
and
\Phi^{i_{1}\dots i_{r}}_{j_{1}\dots j_{r}}
are computed by using an orthonormal basis for vectors and its dual one. The corresponding norm of
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
will be denoted by
\|\cdot\|
.

Remark 2.1
.

Let us recall the well known converse fact that if one has a three-form
\varphi

\mathbb{R}^{7}

Using

(u,v)\longrightarrow u\times v=\sum_{i\in Z_{7}}\varphi(u,v,e_{i})e_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{7}

\langle u\times v,u\rangle=\langle u\times v,v\rangle=0,\qquad\|u\times v\|^{2}=\|u\|\|v\|-\langle u,v\rangle^{2},

for all

, which are the conditions of the definition of such a product. From these initial conditions, the following identities are derived

\displaystyle\langle u\times v,w\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle u,v\times w\rangle,

(2.2)

\displaystyle u\times(u\times v)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle u,v\rangle u-\|u\|^{2}v,

(2.3)

\displaystyle u\times(v\times w)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-v\times(u\times w)+\langle v,w\rangle u+\langle w,u\rangle v-2\langle u,v\rangle w

Note that for a Cayley basis, as
\{e_{0},\dots,e_{6}\}

\operatorname{\mathfrak{g}}_{2}=\left\{a\in\operatorname{\mathfrak{so}}(7)\,|\,a(u\times v)=a(u)\times v+u\times a(v),\,\forall\,u,v\in\mathbb{R}^{7}\right\}.

\operatorname{\mathfrak{g}}_{2}=\left\{a\in\operatorname{\mathfrak{gl}}(7)\,|\,a(u\times v)=a(u)\times v+u\times a(v),\,\forall\,u,v\in\mathbb{R}^{7}\right\}.

a(e_{i})=a(e_{i+1}\times e_{i+3})=a(e_{i+1})\times e_{i+3}+e_{i+1}\times a(e_{i+1}),\qquad i\in\mathbb{Z}_{7},

\displaystyle\mathbb{R}\,\mathrm{Id}_{\mathbb{R}^{7}}

3.
Some invariants of an endomorphism in
\operatorname{\mathfrak{gl}}(7)
under the action of
\operatorname{\textsl{G}}_{2}

For our purposes, it is necessary to consider several invariants (used in
[
16
]
) of an endomorphism

under the action of
\operatorname{\textsl{G}}_{2}
. There are some of them which are also invariant under the wider action of
\operatorname{\textsl{SO}}(7)
.

A first one is the norm of
a
. In fact,
\|a\|^{2}=\|ga\|^{2}
, for all
g\in\operatorname{\textsl{SO}}(7)
. Other invariants are the coefficients of the characteristic polynomial. They are denoted by
\sigma_{i}(a)
, where

\det(a-\lambda\,\mathrm{Id})=\textstyle\sum_{i=1}^{7}(-1)^{i}\sigma_{7-i}(a)\lambda^{i}.

Thus,
\sigma_{0}(a)=1
,
\sigma_{1}(a)=\tr(a)
,
\sigma_{2}(a)=\tfrac{1}{2}\sum_{i,j\in\mathbb{Z}_{7}\,i\neq j}(a_{i\,i}a_{j\,j}-a_{i\,j}a_{j\,i})
.

In relation with the
\operatorname{\textsl{G}}_{2}
-components of
a
, one has the following result.

Lemma 3.1
.

For all
a\in\operatorname{\mathfrak{gl}}(7)
, it is satisfied

\displaystyle(\sigma_{1}(a))^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 7\|a_{(1)}\|^{2},

\displaystyle 2\sigma_{2}(a)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 6\|a_{(1)}\|^{2}+\|a_{(2)}\|^{2}-\|a_{(3)}\|^{2}+\|a_{(4)}\|^{2}.

Proof.

The expression for

follows directly from (

). To see the expression for
\sigma_{2}(a)
, taking

\displaystyle\|a_{(2)}+a_{(4)}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\textstyle\sum_{i,j\in\mathbb{Z}_{7}\,i\neq j}(a_{i\,j}^{2}+a_{j\,i}^{2})-\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\textstyle\sum_{i,j\in\mathbb{Z}_{7}\,i\neq j}a_{i\,j}a_{j\,i},

\displaystyle\|a_{(1)}+a_{(3)}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\textstyle\sum_{i,j\in\mathbb{Z}_{7}\,i\neq j}(a_{i\,j}^{2}+a_{j\,i}^{2})+\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\textstyle\sum_{i,j\in\mathbb{Z}_{7}\,i\neq j}a_{i\,j}a_{j\,i}+\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}_{7}}a_{i\,i}^{2}

4.
\operatorname{\textsl{G}}_{2}
-structures

We recall briefly some facts about
{\sl G}_{2}
-structures and
display some results we will need later.

A
{\sl G}_{2}
-structure on a Riemannian seven-manifold

(M,\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle)
is by
definition a

\operatorname{\textsl{G}}_{2}
-reduction
\mathcal{P}(M)
of the orthonormal frame
\operatorname{\textsl{SO}}(7)
-bundle
\mathcal{SO}(M)
. This is equivalent to the existence of a global three-form

\varphi
which may be locally written as in (
2.1
). For all

m\in M
, the
tangent space
T\,M
is then associated to the representation
\mathbb{R}^{7}
of
\operatorname{\textsl{G}}_{2}
.
Hence, in each point of a Riemannian manifold with a
{\sl G}_{2}
-structure, there are local orthonormal coframe fields

\{\-e_{0}^{*},\-\ldots,\-e_{6}^{*}\}
such that

\varphi
is written as in (
2.1
), we call them
Cayley
coframes
. The dual frame, denoted also by
\{e_{0},\dots,e_{6}\}
, of a Cayley coframe is called a
Cayley frame
.

When
M
is equipped with a
\operatorname{\textsl{G}}_{2}
-structure, one can also consider the two-fold vector cross product
\times:TM\times TM\to TM
given by
\langle X\times Y,Z\rangle=\varphi(X,Y,Z)

[
7
]
.

If
M
is equipped with a

\operatorname{\textsl{G}}_{2}
-structure, then there always exists a
\operatorname{\textsl{G}}_{2}
-connection

\widetilde{\nabla}
defined on
M
. Doing the difference

\widetilde{\xi}_{X}=\widetilde{\nabla}_{X}-\nabla_{X}
, where

\nabla_{X}
is the Levi-Civita connection of
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
, a tensor
\widetilde{\xi}_{X}\in\operatorname{\mathfrak{so}}(M)
is
obtained. Decomposing
\widetilde{\xi}_{X}=(\widetilde{\xi}_{X})_{\operatorname{\mathfrak{g}}_{2}}+(\widetilde{\xi}_{X})_{\operatorname{\mathfrak{g}}_{2}^{\perp}}
,

(\widetilde{\xi}_{X})_{\operatorname{\mathfrak{g}}_{2}}\in\operatorname{\mathfrak{g}}_{2}

and
(\widetilde{\xi}_{X})_{\operatorname{\mathfrak{g}}_{2}^{\perp}}\in\operatorname{\mathfrak{g}}_{2}^{\perp}
, a new
\operatorname{\textsl{G}}_{2}
-connection
\nabla^{\operatorname{\textsl{G}}_{2}}
, defined by

\nabla^{\operatorname{\textsl{G}}_{2}}_{X}=\widetilde{\nabla}_{X}-(\tilde{\xi}_{X})_{\operatorname{\mathfrak{g}}_{2}}
, can be considered. Because the difference
between two
\operatorname{\textsl{G}}_{2}
-connections must be in
\operatorname{\mathfrak{g}}_{2}
,

\nabla^{\operatorname{\textsl{G}}_{2}}
is the unique
\operatorname{\textsl{G}}_{2}
-connection on
M
such that its
torsion
\xi_{X}=(\widetilde{\xi}_{X})_{\operatorname{\mathfrak{g}}_{2}^{\perp}}=\nabla^{\operatorname{\textsl{G}}_{2}}_{X}-\nabla_{X}
is in

\operatorname{\mathfrak{g}}_{2}^{\perp}
.
\nabla^{\operatorname{\textsl{G}}_{2}}
is called the
minimal
connection
and
\xi
is referred to as the
intrinsic
torsion
of the
\operatorname{\textsl{G}}_{2}
-structure on
M

[
6
]
.

By one hand, as it is pointed out in
[
16
]
, since
\xi_{X}\in\operatorname{\mathfrak{g}}_{2}^{\perp}
, one has

\xi_{X}Y=A_{T(X)}(Y)=Y\times T(X),

where
T\in\mathrm{T}M\otimes\mathrm{T}^{*}M
(see the description of
\operatorname{\mathfrak{g}}_{2}^{\perp}
given in Section
2
).

On the other hand, the covariant derivative
\nabla\varphi
satisfies
\nabla\varphi=-\xi\varphi
and
\xi\to-\xi\varphi
is a
\operatorname{\textsl{G}}_{2}
-map. Thus the space of possible intrinsic torsions can be identified with the space
\mathfrak{X}\subseteq\mathrm{T}^{*}M\otimes\Lambda^{3}\mathrm{T}^{*}M
of covariant derivatives of
\varphi
. Consider now the
\operatorname{\textsl{G}}_{2}
-map
r:\mathfrak{X}\longrightarrow\mathrm{T}^{*}M\times\mathrm{T}^{*}M
defined by
[
12
,
13
]

r(\alpha)(X,Y)=\frac{1}{4}(X\lrcorner\alpha,Y\lrcorner\star\varphi)=\frac{1}{24}\langle X\lrcorner\alpha,Y\lrcorner\star\varphi\rangle.

Then an explicit expression
[
14
]
for
\xi
is given by

\xi_{X}Y=-\frac{1}{3}\textstyle\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}_{7}}r(\nabla\varphi)(X,e_{i})e_{i}\times Y.

Thus, comparing with the identity
\xi_{X}Y=Y\times T(X)
, it is found
[
16
]

T(X)=\frac{1}{3}\textstyle\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}_{7}}r(\nabla\varphi)(X,e_{i})e_{i}.

Note that
T_{i\,j}=\langle e_{i},T(e_{j})\rangle=\frac{1}{3}r(\nabla\varphi)(e_{j},e_{i})
.

The space
\mathfrak{X}=\mathrm{T}^{*}M\otimes\operatorname{\mathfrak{g}}_{2}^{\perp}\subseteq\mathrm{T}^{*}M\otimes(\mathrm{T}M\otimes\mathrm{T}^{*}M)
of posible intrinsic torsions, under the action of
\operatorname{\textsl{G}}_{2}
, is decomposed into four irreducible
\operatorname{\textsl{G}}_{2}
-modules
\operatorname{\mathfrak{X}}=\operatorname{\mathfrak{X}}_{1}\oplus\operatorname{\mathfrak{X}}_{2}\oplus\operatorname{\mathfrak{X}}_{3}\oplus\operatorname{\mathfrak{X}}_{4}
. This gives place to a natural classification of types of
\operatorname{\textsl{G}}_{2}
-structure. This was shown by Fernández and Gray, by using
\nabla\varphi
, in
[
7
]
.
Using the composition of maps
\xi\to-\xi\varphi=\nabla\varphi\to r(-\xi\varphi)\to T
, it is derived
\operatorname{\mathfrak{X}}\cong\operatorname{\mathfrak{gl}}(\mathrm{T}M)
,

\operatorname{\mathfrak{X}}_{1}\cong\mathbb{R}\,\mathrm{Id}_{\mathrm{T}M}
,
\operatorname{\mathfrak{X}}_{3}\cong S_{0}^{2}\mathrm{T}^{*}M
,
\operatorname{\mathfrak{X}}_{2}\cong\operatorname{\mathfrak{g}}_{2}
, and
\operatorname{\mathfrak{X}}_{4}\cong\operatorname{\mathfrak{g}}_{2}^{\perp}
.

5.
The different integral formulas

By using different tools, some authors have derived integral formulas for
\operatorname{\textsl{G}}_{2}
-structures. Our purpose here is to display the correlations between them. The existence of theses correlations was indicated by Kiedzialomski in
[
16
]
. Firstly, it is shown the integral formula more recently proved.

Theorem 5.1

(
[
16
]
)
.

On a closed (i.e., compact without boundary) manifold
M
equipped with a
\operatorname{\textsl{G}}_{2}
-structure, the following integral formula holds

\frac{1}{6}\int_{M}s\,\mathrm{vol}_{M}=\int_{M}-\frac{3}{2}i_{0}(T)+6\sigma_{2}(T)\;\mathrm{vol}_{M},

where

denotes the scalar curvature.

This is shown by deducing the divergence formula given by

(5.1)

\textstyle\mathrm{div}\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}_{7}}\xi_{e_{i}}e_{i}=\displaystyle-\frac{1}{6}s-\frac{3}{2}i_{0}(T)+6\sigma_{2}(T)

(see Lemma 2.2, Lemma 4.1 and Lemma 4.2 in
[
16
]

\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}_{7}}\xi_{e_{i}}e_{i}

(5.2)

\textstyle\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}_{7}}\xi_{e_{i}}e_{i}=\displaystyle-p(T)=-\frac{1}{6}(pd^{\star}\varphi)^{\sharp},

[
12
,
13
]

\alpha\in\mathrm{T}^{*}_{m}M
,
x\in\mathrm{T}_{m}M
and
m\in M
. In fact, considering a Cayley frame
\{e_{0},\dots,e_{6}\}
(this frame will be also used as Cayley frame in the sequel),

\displaystyle\textstyle\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}_{7}}\xi_{e_{i}}e_{i}=

\displaystyle\textstyle\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}_{7}}e_{i}\times T(e_{i})

\displaystyle\textstyle\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}_{7}}\left(T_{i+1\,i}\,e_{i}\times e_{i+1}+T_{i+2\,i}\,e_{i}\times e_{i+2}+T_{i+3\,i}\,e_{i}\times e_{i+3}+T_{i+4\,i}\,e_{i}\times e_{i+4}\right.

\displaystyle-\textstyle\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}_{7}}\left(T_{i+1\,i+3}-T_{i+3\,i+1}+T_{i+4\,i+5}-T_{i+5\,i+4}+T_{i+2\,i+6}-T_{i+6\,i+2}\right)e_{i}

\displaystyle\c{c}=

\displaystyle-p(T).

(see
[

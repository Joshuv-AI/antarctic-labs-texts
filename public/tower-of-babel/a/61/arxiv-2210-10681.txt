Abstract

We study the dynamics of waves, oscillations, and other spatio-temporal patterns in stochastic evolution systems, including SPDE and stochastic integral equations.
Representing a given pattern as a smooth, stable invariant manifold of the deterministic dynamics, we reduce the stochastic dynamics to a finite dimensional SDE on this manifold using the isochronal phase.
The isochronal phase is defined by mapping a neighbourbhood of the manifold onto the manifold itself, analogous to the isochronal phase defined for finite-dimensional oscillators by A.T. Winfree and J. Guckenheimer.
We then determine a probability measure that indicates the average position of the stochastic perturbation of the pattern/wave as it wanders over the manifold.
It is proved that this probability measure is accurate on time-scales greater than
O(\sigma^{-2})
, but less than
O(\exp(C\sigma^{-2}))
, where
\sigma\ll 1
is the amplitude of the stochastic perturbation.
Moreover, using this measure, we determine the expected velocity of the difference between the deterministic and stochastic motion on the manifold.

Introduction

This paper presents results on stochastic perturbations of spatio-temporal patterns in stochastic PDE and integral equations.
Specifically, we determine a probability distribution that characterizes the long-time behaviour of certain patterns that are stationary or periodic in time under small noise perturbations.
These results are obtained using an abstract framework that encompasses travelling waves in nerve axons

3.1

\pi_{t}=\pi(X_{t})
be the isochronal phase of
X_{t}
(as long as

(98)

(Furthermore we require

(see

]

[

Theorem 3.17
.

For any orthonormal basis

\displaystyle\pi(X_{t\wedge\tau})

\displaystyle+\sigma\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau}D\pi(X_{s})B(X_{s})\,dW_{s},

Proof.

First, we prove that the Itô integral

.
Since

is a mild solution of (

\displaystyle X_{t^{*}_{i+1}}-X_{t^{*}_{i}}

\displaystyle=\,\Lambda_{t^{*}_{i+1}-t^{*}_{i}}X_{t^{*}_{i}}-X_{t^{*}_{i}}+\int_{t^{*}_{i}}^{t^{*}_{i+1}}\Lambda_{t^{*}_{i+1}-s}N(X_{s})\,ds+\sigma\int_{t^{*}_{i}}^{t^{*}_{i+1}}\Lambda_{t^{*}_{i+1}-s}B(X_{s})\,dW_{s}

Inserting this into (

\displaystyle\pi(X_{t^{*}})-\pi(X_{0})

\displaystyle=\,\sum_{i=1}^{M}D\pi(X_{t^{*}_{i}})\left[U^{1}_{i}\right]+\sum_{i=1}^{M}D\pi(X_{t^{*}_{i}})\left[U^{2}_{i}\right]+\sigma\sum_{i=1}^{M}D\pi(X_{t^{*}_{i}})\left[U_{i}^{3}\right]

\displaystyle(I+II)\,

\displaystyle\rightarrow\,\int_{0}^{t^{*}}\tilde{N}\big(\pi(X_{s})\big)\,ds,

(104)

\displaystyle III\,

\displaystyle\rightarrow\,\int_{0}^{t^{*}}D\pi(X_{s})B(X_{s})\,dW_{s},

(105)

\displaystyle IV\,

\displaystyle\rightarrow\,0,

(106)

\displaystyle V\,

\displaystyle\rightarrow\,0,

(107)

\displaystyle VI\,

\displaystyle\rightarrow\,\int_{0}^{t^{*}}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}D^{2}\pi(X_{s})\left[B(X_{s})e_{k},B(X_{s})e_{k}\right]\,ds.

(108)

We first handle the nonzero limits. For a fixed partition
\{t_{i}\}_{i=1}^{M}
with mesh size
h>0
, choose arbitrary

and let
(\hat{X}_{s})_{s\in[t_{i},t_{i+1}]}
be given by

\hat{X}_{t_{i}}\,=\,X_{t_{i}},\quad\hat{X}_{s}\,=\,\Lambda_{s-t_{i}}X_{t_{i}}+\int_{t_{i}}^{s}\Lambda_{t-r}N(\hat{X}_{r})\,dr\quad\text{ for }\quad s\in[t_{i},t_{i+1}].

3.7
, for
s\in[t_{i},t_{i+1}]

\displaystyle\pi\big(\hat{X}_{s}\big)

\displaystyle=\hat{\pi}(s)\text{ where }

(109)

\displaystyle\frac{d\hat{\pi}}{ds}(s)

\displaystyle=\tilde{N}\big(\hat{\pi}(s)\big)\text{ and }\hat{\pi}(t_{i})=\pi\big(X_{t_{i}}\big).

(110)

Taking

is continuous, we obtain (
104
).
For the limit of

\|\Lambda_{t^{*}_{i+1}-s}-\mathcal{I}\|=O(h)

\int_{t^{*}_{i}}^{t^{*}_{i+1}}\Lambda_{t^{*}_{i+1}-s}B(X_{s})\,dW_{s}\,=\,B(X_{t^{*}_{i}})[W_{t^{*}_{i+1}}-W_{t^{*}_{i}}]+o(h).

, we have

\displaystyle III

\displaystyle=\,\sum_{i=1}^{M}D\pi(X_{t^{*}_{i}})\left[\int_{t^{*}_{i}}^{t^{*}_{i+1}}\Lambda_{t^{*}_{i+1}-s}B(X_{s})\,dW_{s}\right]

\displaystyle=\,\sum_{i=0}^{K}D\pi(X_{t^{*}_{i}})B(X_{t^{*}_{i}})[W_{t^{*}_{i+1}}-W_{t^{*}_{i}}]+o(h)

\displaystyle\xrightarrow[h\rightarrow 0]{}\,\int_{0}^{t^{*}}D\pi(X_{s})B(X_{s})\,dW_{s}.

To obtain
VI
, we formally decompose
(W_{t})_{t\geq 0}
as

W_{t}=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}e_{k}\beta^{k}_{t}

(111)

for some collection of independent identically distributed Brownian motions
\{\beta^{k}_{t}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
, and we recall that
\{e_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
is the orthonormal basis of
H
from Assumption
D
.

We thus find that

\displaystyle D^{2}\pi(w_{i})\left[U^{3}_{i},U^{3}_{i}\right]=D^{2}\pi(w_{i})\left[\int_{t^{*}_{i}}^{t^{*}_{i+1}}\Lambda_{t^{*}_{i+1}-s}B(X_{s})\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}e_{k}d\beta_{s}^{k},\,\int_{t^{*}_{i}}^{t^{*}_{i+1}}\Lambda_{t^{*}_{k+1}-s}B(X_{s})\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb{N}}e_{\ell}d\beta_{s}^{\ell}\right].

(112)

Now as
h\to 0
, it must be that for any
a\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
,

\displaystyle\sum_{k,\ell\leq a}\sum_{i=1}^{M}D^{2}\pi(w_{i})\left[\int_{t^{*}_{i}}^{t^{*}_{i+1}}\Lambda_{t^{*}_{i+1}-s}B(X_{s})e_{k}d\beta_{s}^{k},\,\int_{t^{*}_{i}}^{t^{*}_{i+1}}\Lambda_{t^{*}_{k+1}-s}B(X_{s})\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb{N}}e_{\ell}d\beta_{s}^{\ell}\right]

\displaystyle\to\sum_{k\leq a}\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau}D^{2}\pi(X_{s})[B(X_{s})e_{k},B(X_{s})e_{k}]ds.

(One proves this by taking expectations of the square difference and proving that it goes to zero). Furthermore, thanks to Lemma
3.15
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg[\bigg\{\sum_{k,\ell>a}\sum_{i=0}^{M-1}D^{2}\pi(w_{i})\left[\int_{t^{*}_{i}}^{t^{*}_{i+1}}\Lambda_{t^{*}_{i+1}-s}B(X_{s})e_{k}d\beta_{s}^{k},\,\int_{t^{*}_{i}}^{t^{*}_{i+1}}\Lambda_{t^{*}_{k+1}-s}B(X_{s})\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb{N}}e_{\ell}d\beta_{s}^{\ell}\right]\bigg\}^{2}\bigg]

(113)

becomes arbitrarily small as
a\to\infty
. This implies VI.
Finally, one easily checks that
IV
and
V
tend to zero.

∎

4
Concentration & Metastability of the Isochronal Phase

In this section, we determine a measure
P_{*}
that describes the average behaviour of
\pi_{t}\coloneqq\pi(X_{t})
on a particular large time scale in the small noise regime.
The measure
P_{*}
is explicitly determined (for small values of
\sigma
) in terms of the first and second derivatives of the isochron map and the diffusion coefficient
B
, as stated in Theorem
4.19
.

We assume throughout this section
\Gamma
consists of fixed points. That is, we assume that

\tilde{\phi}_{t}(\alpha)=\alpha\text{ for all }t\geq 0.

(114)

We define the exit time from
\Gamma_{\delta}
(the
\delta
neighbourhood of
\Gamma
) to be

\tau_{\delta}\,\coloneqq\,\inf\left\{t>0\,:\,\left\|X_{t}-\gamma_{\pi(X_{t})}\right\|_{E}=\delta\right\},

(115)

and it is assumed that
\delta
is sufficiently small that all of the regularity results of Section 3 hold. Recall that in (
3
), we assumed that the following lemma holds.

Lemma 4.18
.

There exist constants
c>0
,
\sigma_{*}>0
, and
\delta_{*}>0
such that if
\sigma\in(0,\sigma_{*})
and
\delta\in(\sigma^{2},\delta^{*})
, then for
t>0
it holds that

\mathbb{P}\left(t>\tau_{\delta}\right)\,\leq\,t\exp\left(-c\sigma^{-2}\delta^{2}\right).

(116)

Lemma
4.18
has been proved in
[
66
]
in the case that
\Gamma
consists of stationary points, so long as the isochronal and variational phases are close to one another. Such a closeness result is proven in Appendix
A
.

In the proof of the next theorem, we demonstrate the existence of the measure
P_{*}
on
\mathcal{S}
alluded to above. The measure
P_{*}
can be characterized as the unique invariant measure of a Markov process
\grave{\pi}_{t}
on
\mathcal{S}
. To specify this process, for any
\alpha\in\mathcal{S}
, define
\mathcal{H}(\alpha)\in\mathbb{R}^{m-1\times m-1}
to be the square matrix, with components, for
1\leq j,k\leq m-1
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{jk}(\alpha)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\left\langle\mathcal{B}(\alpha)e_{k},\mathfrak{v}_{\alpha,j}\right\rangle\left\langle\mathcal{B}(\alpha)e_{k},\mathfrak{v}_{\alpha,k}\right\rangle,

(117)

and
\{e_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}\subset E
is any orthonormal basis of
H
, and in the above
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
is the Euclidean dot product over
\mathbb{R}^{m}
. It is immediate from the definition that
\mathcal{H}
is symmetric and positive semi-definite, and we assume that there exists a non-zero lower bound for its eigenvalues, which holds uniformly for all
\alpha\in\mathcal{S}
. Write
G(\alpha):=\big(G_{jk}(\alpha)\big)_{1\leq j,k\leq m-1}
to be the
\mathbb{R}^{(m-1)\times(m-1)}
square matrix that is (i) co-diagonal with
H(\alpha)
, and (ii) with eigenvalues that are the positive square roots of the eigenvalues of
H(\alpha)
. Define
\grave{\pi}_{t}=(\grave{\pi}^{i}_{t})_{1\leq i\leq m}
to be the folllowing
\mathbb{R}^{m}
-valued stochastic process,

\displaystyle d\grave{\pi}^{i}_{t}=

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}_{i}(\grave{\pi}_{t})dt+\sum_{1\leq j,k\leq m-1}\mathfrak{v}_{\grave{\pi}_{t},j}^{i}G_{jk}(\grave{\pi}_{t})dW^{k}_{t}\text{ where }

(118)

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}_{i}\big(\grave{\pi}_{t}\big)=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\sum_{j=1}^{m-1}\mathfrak{v}_{\grave{\pi}_{t},j}^{i}D^{2}\pi_{j}(\gamma_{\grave{\pi}_{t}})[B(\gamma_{\grave{\pi}_{t}})e_{k},B(\gamma_{\grave{\pi}_{t}})e_{k}]

(119)

and
\{W^{k}_{t}\}_{1\leq k\leq m-1}
are independent
\mathbb{R}
-valued Brownian Motions, and
\grave{\pi}_{0}^{i}
is any constant in
\mathcal{S}
.

Theorem 4.19
.

With unit probability a modification of
\grave{\pi}_{t}
stays on
\mathcal{S}\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}
for all time. Furthermore, the process
\grave{\pi}_{t}
possesses a unique invariant measure
P_{*}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{S})
, such that the following holds:

Fix arbitrary
M>0
and
\epsilon\ll 1
. There exist
\sigma_{\epsilon},\,\delta_{\epsilon},C_{\epsilon}>0
(depending on both
\epsilon
and
M
) such that if
\sigma\leq\sigma_{\epsilon}
,
\delta<\delta_{\epsilon}
, and
g\in C^{2}(\mathcal{S})
has first and second derivatives bounded in Euclidean norm by
M
, then

\mathbb{P}\left(\left|\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}g\big(\pi_{s}\big)\,ds-P_{*}(g)\right|<\epsilon,\,t<\tau_{\delta}\right)\,\geq\,1-t\exp\left(-c\sigma^{-2}\delta^{2}\right)-\exp\left(-C_{\epsilon}\sigma^{2}t\right),

(120)

and we recall that
\pi_{s}=\pi(X_{s})
.

The following is then an immediate consequence.

Corollary 4.20
.

In the setting of Theorem
4.19
, if
(t_{\sigma})_{\sigma>0}
is any family of times such that

\sigma^{2}t_{\sigma}\xrightarrow[\sigma\rightarrow 0]{}\infty\quad\text{ and }\quad t_{\sigma}\,\leq\,\exp\left(c\sigma^{-2}\delta^{2}\right),

(121)

then

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\left|\frac{1}{t_{\sigma}}\int_{0}^{t_{\sigma}}g\big(\pi_{s}\big)\,ds-P_{*}(g)\right|<\epsilon,\,t_{\sigma}<\tau_{\delta}\right)

\displaystyle\xrightarrow[\sigma\rightarrow 0]{}\,1.

(122)

Theorem
4.19
indicates that, for each small
\sigma>0
, the measure
P_{*}
is “observable” on the time scale indicated by (
121
), in the sense that on this time scale, a sufficient convergence to
P_{*}
before escape from
\Gamma_{\delta}
is observed with high probability.
In Section
4.2
, we demonstrate how one may use Theorem
4.19
to compute the noise-induced drift of the isochronal phase when
\mathcal{S}=\mathbb{S}^{1}
.

Since
\Gamma
is assumed to consist of fixed points in this section,
\tilde{N}=0
, and the Itô formula (
99
) therefore assumes the form

d\pi_{t}\,=\,\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}D^{2}\pi(X_{t})[B(X_{t})e_{k},B(X_{t})e_{k}]\,dt+\sigma D\pi(X_{t})B(X_{t})\,dW_{t}.

(123)

Define

v_{t}\,\coloneqq\,X_{t}-\gamma_{\pi_{t}},

so
\left\|v_{t}\right\|\lesssim\left\|v_{t}\right\|_{E}<\delta
for
t<\tau
.
Thus, for
\delta>0
sufficiently small, one expects that the following approximations are good for
t<\tau_{\delta}
:

\pi(X_{t})\,\simeq\,\pi(\gamma_{\pi(X_{t})}),\quad D\pi(X_{t})\,\simeq\,D\pi(\gamma_{\pi(X_{t})}),\quad D^{2}\pi(X_{t})\,\simeq\,D^{2}\pi(\gamma_{\pi(X_{t})}).

(124)

We wish to explicitly identify a finite-dimensional diffusion on
\mathcal{S}
whose probability law is very close to that of
\pi(X_{t})
. Define

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}(\alpha)

\displaystyle\in L(H,T_{\alpha}\mathcal{S}^{*})

(125)

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}(\alpha)\,

\displaystyle\coloneqq\,D\pi(\gamma_{\alpha})B(\gamma_{\alpha}).

(126)

We then consider the Itô diffusion on
\mathcal{S}
governed by

d\hat{\pi}_{t}\,=\,\mathcal{V}(\hat{\pi}_{t})\,dt+\mathcal{B}(\hat{\pi}_{t})\,d\tilde{W}_{t},\quad\hat{\pi}_{0}\,=\,\alpha,

(127)

where
\tilde{W}_{t}
is a Wiener process that is equivalent in law to
W_{t}
and independent of
W_{t}
. This stochastic process is identical (in probability distribution) to the process
\grave{\pi}_{t}
in (
118
). The only difference is that (
127
) is driven by the same infinite-dimensional Wiener Process as the original system, whereas
\grave{\pi}_{t}
is driven by
(m-1)

\mathbb{R}
-valued Brownian Motions. When we want to emphasize that
\hat{\pi}
has initial condition
\alpha\in\mathcal{S}
, we write
\hat{\pi_{t}}=\hat{\pi}_{t}(\alpha)
.

A priori
,
\hat{\pi}_{t}
is an
\mathbb{R}^{m}
-valued diffusion.
However, one can verify that there is a version of this stochastic process such that for any
\alpha\in\mathcal{S}
we have
\hat{\pi}_{t}(\alpha)\in\mathcal{S}
for all
t\leq t_{\delta}
almost surely.
For further details see Remark V.1.1 of Ikeda & Watanabe
[
48
]
.

We also wish to consider a rescaled version of
\hat{\pi}
defined on finite time intervals - doing this helps clarify the ergodic timescale for the induced phase dynamics on
\mathcal{S}
.
Fix a time interval
\Delta=\Delta_{\sigma,\epsilon,\eta}>0
(taken to be a precise value following the proof of Lemma
4.21
, below), and let
t_{a}\coloneqq a\Delta
for
a\in\mathbb{N}
(note that the sequence
\{t_{a}\}_{a\in\mathbb{N}}
used here is distinct from that in Section
3
).
Then, define
\tilde{\pi}^{a}_{t}
for
t\in[t_{a},t_{a+1}]
as the solution to

d\tilde{\pi}^{a}_{t}\,=\,\sigma^{2}\mathcal{V}(\tilde{\pi}^{a}_{t})\,dt+\sigma\mathcal{B}(\tilde{\pi}^{a}_{t})\,dW_{t},\quad\tilde{\pi}_{t_{a}}^{a}\,=\,\pi_{t_{a}},

(128)

where
W_{t}
in (
128
) is pathwise identical to the process in (
1
).
Note that
\tilde{\pi}^{a}_{t}
is identical in law to
\hat{\pi}_{\sigma^{2}t}(\pi_{t_{a}})
for each
t\in[t_{a},t_{a+1}]
, due to the fact that the two Markov processes have the same infinitesimal generator.

The remainder of this section is as follows.
In Lemma
4.21
, we show that
P_{*}
exists as the unique invariant measure of (
127
) to which the distribution of
\hat{\pi}_{t}
converges uniformly.
In Lemma
4.22
, we then show that
\tilde{\pi}_{t}^{a}
closely approximates
\pi_{t}
on each
[t_{a},t_{a+1}]
.
Using this, we demonstrate that (
120
) holds, completing the proof of Theorem
4.19
.

4.1
Proof of Theorem
4.19

In this section, we make extensive use of the regularity of the isochron map provided for in Section
3
.
To begin, we note that the existence of a unique, uniformly convergent invariant measure of (
127
) is a well-established result under certain assumptions, which we state in the following lemma.

Lemma 4.21
.

The system (
127
) possesses a unique invariant measure
P_{*}
on
\mathcal{S}
to which it converges at an exponential rate in total variation norm.
Moreover, for arbitrary
\epsilon,\eta>0
, there exists a constant
\tilde{\Delta}=\tilde{\Delta}_{\epsilon,\eta}>0
such that if
t\geq\tilde{\Delta}
and
g\in C^{2}(\mathcal{S})
then

\sup_{\alpha\in\mathcal{S}}\mathbb{P}\left(\left|\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}g(\hat{\pi}_{s}(\alpha))\,ds-P_{*}(g)\right|\geq\epsilon/2\right)\,\leq\,\eta.

(129)

Proof.

It is well-known that (
127
) possesses a unique invariant measure such that (
129
) holds. This follows from the fact that
\mathcal{S}
is compact and the diffusion coefficient is uniformly bounded from below, which implies that
\grave{\pi}_{t}
must satisfy a Doeblin Condition. See for instance
[
5
, Section 1.10.1]
.
∎

We henceforth fix
\Delta=\Delta_{\sigma,\epsilon,\eta}\coloneqq\sigma^{-2}\tilde{\Delta}_{\epsilon,\eta}
, and let
t_{a}=a\Delta
for
a\in\mathbb{N}
.
Below, we show that
P_{*}
in Lemma
4.21
satisfies the properties described in Theorem
4.19
.
To this end, the following lemma is needed, which places a bound on the difference between
\pi_{t}
and
\tilde{\pi}^{a}_{t}
on each
[t_{a},t_{a+1}]
.
From these bounds on time intervals of length
\Delta
, we will obtain the long time bounds in
Theorem
4.19
.

Lemma 4.22
.

For any
\epsilon,\eta>0
, there exist
\delta_{0},\sigma_{0}>0
such that for
\sigma<\sigma_{0}
and
\delta<\delta_{0}
,

\mathbb{P}\left(\sup_{t\in[t_{a},t_{a+1}]}\left\|\pi_{t}-\tilde{\pi}^{a}_{t}\right\|\geq\epsilon,\,\sup_{t\in[t_{a},t_{a+1}]}\left\|v_{t}\right\|\leq\delta\right)\,<\,\eta.

(130)

Proof.

Using the Lipschitz continuity of the map
\phi
, and the Lipschitz continuity of
\mathcal{V}
, we observe that so long as
v_{t}<\delta
we have

\displaystyle\left\|\pi_{t}-\tilde{\pi}_{t}^{a}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\,\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}\left\|\int_{t_{a}}^{t}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}D^{2}\pi(X_{s})[B(X_{s})e_{k},B(X_{s})e_{k}]-\mathcal{V}(\tilde{\pi}_{s}^{a})\,ds\right\|

(131)

\displaystyle+\sigma\left\|\int_{t_{a}}^{t}D\pi(X_{s})B(X_{s})-\mathcal{B}(\tilde{\pi}_{s}^{a})\,dW_{s}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\,\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}\left\|\int_{t_{a}}^{t}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}D^{2}\pi(X_{s})[B(X_{s})e_{k},B(X_{s})e_{k}]-\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}D^{2}\pi(\gamma_{\pi_{s}})[B(\gamma_{\pi_{s}})e_{k},B(\gamma_{\pi_{s}})e_{k}]\,ds\right\|

\displaystyle+\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}\left\|\int_{t_{a}}^{t}\mathcal{V}(\pi_{s})-\mathcal{V}(\tilde{\pi}_{s}^{a})\,ds\right\|

\displaystyle+\sigma\left\|\int_{t_{a}}^{t}D\pi(X_{s})B(X_{s})-D\pi(\gamma_{\tilde{\pi}_{s}^{a}})B(\gamma_{\tilde{\pi}_{s}^{a}})\,dW_{s}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\,\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}C_{\pi}\left\|\int_{t_{a}}^{t}X_{s}-\gamma_{\pi_{s}}\,ds\right\|+\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}C_{\mathcal{V}}\left\|\int_{t_{a}}^{t}\pi_{s}-\tilde{\pi}_{s}^{a}\,ds\right\|

\displaystyle+\sigma\left\|\int_{t_{a}}^{t}D\pi(X_{s})B(X_{s})-D\pi(\gamma_{\tilde{\pi}_{s}^{a}})B(\gamma_{\tilde{\pi}_{s}^{a}})\,dW_{s}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\,\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}C_{\pi}\Delta\delta+\sigma\left\|\int_{t_{a}}^{t}D\pi(X_{s})B(X_{s})-D\pi(\gamma_{\tilde{\pi}_{s}^{a}})B(\gamma_{\tilde{\pi}_{s}^{a}})\,dW_{s}\right\|+\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}C_{\mathcal{V}}\int_{t_{a}}^{t}\left\|\pi_{s}-\tilde{\pi}_{s}^{a}\right\|\,ds

\displaystyle\eqqcolon\,K_{0}+\sigma\left\|\mathfrak{B}_{t}\right\|+K_{1}\int_{t_{a}}^{t}\|\pi_{s}-\tilde{\pi}_{s}^{a}\|\,ds.

Then, by Grönwall’s inequality, we have

\displaystyle\left\|\pi_{t}-\tilde{\pi}_{t}^{a}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\,\sigma\left\|\mathfrak{B}_{t}\right\|+K_{0}+\int_{t_{a}}^{t}(\sigma\left\|\mathfrak{B}_{s}\right\|+K_{0})K_{1}e^{K_{1}(t-s)}\,ds

(132)

\displaystyle=\,\sigma\left\|\mathfrak{B}_{t}\right\|+K_{0}+K_{0}K_{1}\int_{t_{a}}^{t}e^{K_{1}(t-s)}\,ds+\sigma K_{1}\int_{t_{a}}^{t}\left\|\mathfrak{B}_{s}\right\|e^{K_{1}(t-s)}\,ds

\displaystyle\leq\,\sigma\left\|\mathfrak{B}_{t}\right\|+K_{0}+K_{0}(e^{K_{1}(t-t_{a})}-1)+\sigma K_{1}\int_{t_{a}}^{t}\left\|\mathfrak{B}_{s}\right\|e^{K_{1}(t-s)}\,ds

\displaystyle\leq\,\sigma\left\|\mathfrak{B}_{t}\right\|+K_{0}e^{K_{1}\Delta}+\sigma K_{1}\int_{t_{a}}^{t}\left\|\mathfrak{B}_{s}\right\|e^{K_{1}\Delta}\,ds.

It therefore follows that, so long as
\left\|v_{t}\right\|\leq\delta
,

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[t_{a},t_{a+1}]}\left\|\pi_{t}-\tilde{\pi}_{t}^{a}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\,\sigma\sup_{t\in[t_{a},t_{a+1}]}\left\|\mathfrak{B}_{t}\right\|+K_{0}e^{K_{1}\Delta}+\sigma K_{1}\Delta e^{K_{1}\Delta}\sup_{t\in[t_{a},t_{a+1}]}\left\|\mathfrak{B}_{t}\right\|

\displaystyle\eqqcolon\,K_{0}e^{K_{1}\Delta}+K_{2}\sup_{t\in[t_{a},t_{a+1}]}\left\|\mathfrak{B}_{t}\right\|.

Then, by a union of events bound we find that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\sup_{t\in[t_{a},t_{a+1}]}\left\|\pi_{t}-\tilde{\pi}_{t}^{a}\right\|\geq\epsilon,\,\,\,\left\|v_{t}\right\|\leq\delta\right)

\displaystyle\leq\,\mathbb{P}\left(K_{0}e^{K_{1}\Delta}\geq\frac{\epsilon}{2}\right)+\mathbb{P}\left(\sup_{t\in[t_{a},t_{a+1}]}\left\|\mathfrak{B}_{t}\right\|\geq\frac{\epsilon}{2K_{2}}\right).

(133)

For any
\epsilon,\eta,\sigma>0
, we can take
\delta_{0}>0
small enough such that for
\delta<\delta_{0}
we have

K_{0}e^{K_{1}\Delta}\,=\,\delta\frac{C_{\pi}}{2}\sigma^{2}\Delta e^{{\frac{C_{\mathcal{V}}}{2}\sigma^{2}\Delta}}\,<\,\frac{\epsilon}{2}.

(134)

Indeed, recalling that
\Delta=O(\sigma^{-2})
, we may choose
\delta_{0}
independently of
\sigma
.
This implies that the first term in the right hand side of (
133
) is zero.
To handle the second term, fix
\delta<\delta_{0}
.
Then, we apply Markov’s inequality for third moments, and the Burkholder-Davis-Gundy inequality to find that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\sup_{t\in[t_{a},t_{a+1}]}\left\|\mathfrak{B}_{t}\right\|\geq\frac{\epsilon}{2K_{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\,\frac{8K_{2}^{3}}{\epsilon^{3}}\ExpOp\left[\left(\sup_{t\in[t_{a},t_{a+1}]}\left\|\mathfrak{B_{t}}\right\|\right)^{3}\right]

\displaystyle=\,\frac{8K_{2}^{3}}{\epsilon^{3}}\ExpOp\left[\sup_{t\in[t_{a},t_{a+1}]}\left\|\int_{t_{a}}^{t}D\pi(X_{s})B(X_{s})-D\pi(\gamma_{\pi_{s}})B(\gamma_{\pi_{s}})\,dW_{s}\right\|^{3}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\,\frac{8K_{2}^{3}}{\epsilon^{3}}\ExpOp\left[\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\sup_{t\in[t_{a},t_{a+1}]}\left\|\int_{t_{a}}^{t}\big[D\pi(X_{s})B(X_{s})-D\pi(\gamma_{\pi_{s}})B(\gamma_{\pi_{s}})\big]e_{k}\,d\beta_{\mathbb{S}}^{k}\right\|^{3}\right]

\displaystyle+R_{t}

\displaystyle\leq\,\frac{8K_{2}^{3}}{\epsilon^{3}}C_{3}\ExpOp\left[\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\int_{t_{a}}^{t_{a+1}}\left\|\big[D\pi(X_{s})B(X_{s})-D\pi(\gamma_{\pi_{s}})B(\gamma_{\pi_{s}})\big]e_{k}\right\|^{3/2}\,ds\right]

\displaystyle\leq\,\frac{16K_{2}^{3}}{\epsilon^{3}}C_{3}\Delta\sup_{x\in\Gamma_{\delta}}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\left\|\big[D\pi(x)B(x)e_{k}\right\|^{3/2}

\displaystyle\leq\,\big(16C_{3}M_{\pi}\big)K_{2}^{3}\Delta\epsilon^{-3},

where
C_{3}
is the constant appearing in the Burkholder-Davis-Gundy inequality.
The remainder term
R_{t}
in the third line is the off diagonal terms appearing in the expression for the cube of a sum, and can be identified with zero.
Since
K_{2}=O(\sigma)
and
\Delta=O(\sigma^{-2})
, we see that for arbitrary
\epsilon,\eta>0
, we can obtain

\mathbb{P}\left(\sup_{t\in[t_{a},t_{a+1}]}\left\|\mathfrak{B}_{t}\right\|\geq\frac{\epsilon}{2K_{2}}\right)\,\leq\,\frac{\eta}{2}

by taking
\sigma
less than or equal to some
\sigma_{0}
proportional to
\epsilon^{3}\eta
.
Note that
\sigma_{0}
depends on
\delta_{0}
via
M_{\pi}
.
This completes the proof.
∎

In the next lemma we ‘combine’ the previous two lemmas, showing that the distribution of
\pi_{t}
becomes arbitrarily close to that of
P_{*}
over sufficiently long time intervals.

Lemma 4.23
.

For any
\epsilon,\eta>0
and
a\in\mathbb{N}
, for
\delta<\delta_{0}
and sufficiently small
\sigma>0
we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\left|\Delta^{-1}\int_{t_{a}}^{t_{a+1}}g(\pi_{s})\,ds-P_{*}(g)\right|\geq\epsilon,\,\tau_{\delta}\geq t_{a+1}\right)\leq\eta.

(135)

Proof.

Using a union of events bound,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\left|\Delta^{-1}\int_{t_{a}}^{t_{a+1}}g(\pi_{s})\,ds-P_{*}(g)\right|\geq\epsilon,\,\tau_{\delta}\geq t_{a+1}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\,\mathbb{P}\left(\left|\Delta^{-1}\int_{t_{a}}^{t_{a+1}}g(\tilde{\pi}_{s})\,ds-\,P_{*}(g)\right|\,\geq\,\epsilon\right)+\mathbb{P}\left(\Delta^{-1}\int_{t_{a}}^{t_{a+1}}\left|g(\pi_{s})-g(\tilde{\pi}_{s})\right|\,ds\geq\epsilon,\,\tau_{\delta}\geq t+\Delta\right).

Since
\hat{\pi}_{\sigma^{2}(t-t_{a})}
with initial condition
\pi_{t_{a}}
has an identical probability law to
\tilde{\pi}_{t-t_{a}}^{a}
, for any
\eta>0

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\left|\frac{1}{\Delta}\int_{t_{a}}^{t_{a+1}}g(\tilde{\pi}_{s})\,ds-P_{*}(g)\right|\geq\epsilon/2\right)

\displaystyle=\,\mathbb{P}\left(\left|\frac{1}{\Delta\sigma^{-2}}\int_{0}^{\Delta\sigma^{-2}}g(\hat{\pi}_{s})\,ds-P_{*}(g)\right|\geq\epsilon/2\right)

\displaystyle\leq\,\eta,

so long as
\sigma^{-2}\Delta_{\sigma,\epsilon,\eta}\geq\tilde{\Delta}_{\epsilon/2,\eta}
, by (
129
), which can be achieved by taking
\sigma
is sufficiently small.
Now, since the Lipschitz constant of
g
is bounded by
m
, Lemma
4.22
implies that there exists
\sigma_{0}>0
such that if
\sigma<\sigma_{0}

then

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\frac{1}{\Delta}\int_{t_{a}}^{t_{a+1}}\left|g(\pi_{s})-g(\tilde{\pi}_{s})\right|\,ds\geq\epsilon/2,\,\tau_{\delta}>t_{a+1}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\,\mathbb{P}\left(\sup_{t\in[t_{a},t_{a+1}]}\left\|\pi_{t}-\tilde{\pi}_{t}\right\|\geq\epsilon/(2m),\,\tau_{\delta}\geq t_{a+1}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\,\eta.

∎

We now combine the above events to prove a bound on the probability of the event “either the average of
g(\pi_{s})
is close to
P_{*}
, or
t>\tau_{\delta}
”.
Hence, the proof of Theorem
4.19
follows from a simple property of probability measures.

Lemma 4.24
.

Fix arbitrary
M>0
and small
\epsilon>0
.
There exist
\sigma_{\epsilon},\,\delta_{\epsilon},C_{\epsilon}>0
(depending on both
\epsilon
and
M
) such that if
\sigma\leq\sigma_{\epsilon}
,
\delta<\delta_{\epsilon}
, and
g\in C^{2}(\mathcal{S})
has first and second derivatives bounded in Euclidean norm by
M
, then

\mathbb{P}\left(\left|\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}g\big(\pi_{s}\big)\,ds-P_{*}(g)\right|>\epsilon,\,\,t<\tau_{\delta}\right)\,\leq\,\exp\left(-C\sigma^{2}t\right).

(136)

Proof.

Without loss of generality, take
g
to be non-negative and uniformly bounded above by
1
.
Following Lemma
4.21
, the convergence to ergodic averages of (
127
) is uniform. For
\sigma,\epsilon,\eta>0
, let
\Delta=\sigma^{-2}\tilde{\Delta}_{\epsilon,\eta}
be as in Lemma
4.23
, and for
a\in\mathbb{N}
set
t_{a}\coloneqq a\Delta
. By Chernoff’s bound, for arbitrary
\kappa>0
and
t>\Delta
, the left hand side of (
136
) may be bounded as

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\left|\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}g\big(\pi_{s}\big)\,ds-P_{*}(g)\right|>\epsilon,\,\,t<\tau_{\delta}\right)\,\leq\,

(137)

\displaystyle\ExpOp\bigg[\chi(\tau_{\delta}\geq t)\bigg(\exp\left(\kappa\int_{0}^{t}g(\pi_{s})\,ds-\kappa tP_{*}(g)-\kappa t\epsilon\right)

\displaystyle+\exp\left(-\kappa\int_{0}^{t}g(\pi_{s})\,ds+\kappa tP_{*}(g)-\kappa\epsilon t\right)\bigg)\bigg].

We only bound the first term on the right hand side (the bound of the other term is very similar). We are going to show that there exists a constant
\tilde{\kappa}_{\epsilon}
(depending on
\epsilon
but not
\sigma
) such that

\ExpOp\left[\chi(\tau_{\delta}\geq t)\exp\left(\tilde{\kappa}_{\epsilon}\sigma^{2}\int_{0}^{t}g(\pi_{s})\,ds-\tilde{\kappa}_{\epsilon}\sigma^{2}tP_{*}(g)\right)\right]\,\leq\,\exp\big(\epsilon\tilde{\kappa}_{\epsilon}\sigma^{2}t/2\big).

(138)

Once we have established (
138
), the result (
136
) follows from substituting
\kappa=\tilde{\kappa}_{\epsilon}\sigma^{2}
into (
137
).
Towards our goal of proving (
138
), define the random variable

H_{a}\,\coloneqq\,\chi(\tau_{\delta}\geq t_{a+1})\exp\left(\kappa\left(\int_{t_{a}}^{t_{a+1}}g(\pi_{s})\,ds-\kappa(t_{a+1}-t_{a})P_{*}(g)\right)\right),

and define
\hat{\mathfrak{a}}
as the random index such that
\tau_{\delta}\in[t_{\hat{\mathfrak{a}}},t_{\hat{\mathfrak{a}}+1}]
.
Then,

\displaystyle\ExpOp\left[\chi(\tau_{\delta}\geq t)\exp\left(\kappa\int_{0}^{t}g(\pi_{s})\,ds-\kappa\tau_{\delta}P_{*}(g)\right)\right]

(139)

\displaystyle=\,\ExpOp\left[\chi(\tau_{\delta}\geq t)\prod_{a=0}^{\hat{\mathfrak{a}}}H_{a}\times\exp\left(\kappa\int_{t_{\hat{\mathfrak{a}}}}^{\tau_{\delta}}g(\pi_{s})\,ds-\kappa(\tau_{\delta}-t_{a})P_{*}(g)\right)\right]

\displaystyle\leq\,\ExpOp\left[\chi(\tau_{\delta}\geq t)\prod_{a=0}^{\mathfrak{a}}H_{a}\right]\exp(2\kappa\Delta),

and
\mathfrak{a}\coloneqq\lfloor t/\Delta\rfloor-1
.
Taking the conditional expectation on events up to the stopping time, we have

\mathbb{E}\bigg[\chi(\tau_{\delta}\geq t)\exp(-\kappa\tau_{\delta}\epsilon)\prod_{a=0}^{\mathfrak{a}}H_{a}\bigg]=\mathbb{E}\bigg[\prod_{a=1}^{\mathfrak{a}-1}H_{a}\mathbb{E}\big[\chi\big\{\tau_{\delta}\geq t\big\}H_{\mathfrak{a}}|\mathcal{F}_{t_{\mathfrak{a}-1}}\big]\bigg].

(140)

We first bound the conditional expectation. Write

q=\int_{t_{\mathfrak{a}}}^{t_{\mathfrak{a}+1}}g(\pi_{s})ds-\big\{t_{\mathfrak{a}+1}-t_{\mathfrak{a}}\big\}P_{*}(g).

Then, assuming that
\tau_{\delta}\geq t_{\mathfrak{a}-1}
, for any
\alpha>0
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big[\chi\big\{\tau_{\delta}\geq t\big\}H_{\mathfrak{a}}|\mathcal{F}_{t_{\mathfrak{a}-1}}

\displaystyle\big]=\mathbb{E}\big[\chi\big\{\tau_{\delta}\geq t\text{ and }q\geq\alpha\big\}H_{\mathfrak{a}}|\mathcal{F}_{t_{\mathfrak{a}-1}}\big]+\mathbb{E}\big[\chi\big\{\tau_{\delta}\geq t\text{ and }q<\alpha\big\}H_{\mathfrak{a}}|\mathcal{F}_{t_{\mathfrak{a}-1}}\big]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}\big(q\geq\alpha,\tau_{\delta}\geq t_{\mathfrak{a}+1}\big)\exp(\kappa\Delta)+\mathbb{P}(q<\alpha,\tau_{\delta}\geq t_{\mathfrak{a}+1})\exp(\alpha\kappa\Delta)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}\big(q\geq\alpha,\tau_{\delta}\geq t_{\mathfrak{a}+1}\big)\big\{\exp(\kappa\Delta)-\exp(\alpha\kappa\Delta)\big\}+\exp(\alpha\kappa\Delta),

(141)

since evidently

\mathbb{P}\big(q\geq\alpha,\tau_{\delta}\geq t_{\mathfrak{a}+1}\big)+\mathbb{P}\big(q<\alpha,\tau_{\delta}\geq t_{\mathfrak{a}+1}\big)\leq 1.

Now, thanks to Lemma
4.23
, we can take
\eta
and
\alpha=\epsilon
to be such that

\mathbb{P}\big(q\geq\epsilon,\tau_{\delta}\geq t_{\mathfrak{a}}\big)\leq\eta,

and we thus obtain from (
4.24
) that

\displaystyle\ExpOp\left[\chi\big\{\tau_{\delta}\geq t\big\}H_{\mathfrak{a}}|\mathcal{F}_{t_{\mathfrak{a}-1}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\exp\big\{\epsilon\Delta+\eta\exp(\kappa\Delta)-\eta\exp(\epsilon\Delta)\big\}

\displaystyle\leq\exp\big\{\epsilon\kappa\Delta+2\eta\kappa\Delta\big\},

(142)

as long as
\kappa\Delta
is sufficiently small (by taking a first order Taylor expansion).
Since
\Delta\simeq O(\sigma^{-2})
, it suffices to take
\kappa=\sigma^{2}\tilde{\kappa}_{\epsilon}
, where
\tilde{\kappa}_{\epsilon}>0
is a constant independent of
\sigma
such that
\tilde{\kappa}_{\epsilon}\to 0
as
\epsilon\to 0
.
We thereby obtain

\mathbb{E}\big[\chi\big\{\tau_{\delta}\geq t\big\}H_{\mathfrak{a}}|\mathcal{F}_{t_{\mathfrak{a}-1}}\big]\leq\exp\big(\epsilon\kappa\Delta+2\eta\kappa\Delta\big).

(143)

It thus follows from (
139
) that

\displaystyle\ExpOp\left[\chi(\tau_{\delta}\geq t)\exp\left(\kappa\int_{0}^{t}g(\pi_{s})\,ds-\kappa\tau_{\delta}P_{*}(g)\right)\right]\leq\mathbb{E}\bigg[\prod_{a=1}^{\mathfrak{a}-1}H_{a}\bigg]\exp\big(\epsilon\kappa\Delta+2\eta\kappa\Delta\big).

(144)

We iterate this inequality, from
a=\mathfrak{a}
down to
a=1
, and obtain that

\displaystyle\ExpOp\left[\chi(\tau_{\delta}\geq t)\exp\left(\kappa\int_{0}^{t}g(\pi_{s})\,ds-\kappa\tau_{\delta}P_{*}(g)\right)\right]\leq

\displaystyle\exp\big((\mathfrak{a}+1)(2\eta+\epsilon)\kappa\Delta\big)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\exp\big(\kappa(2\eta+\epsilon)\kappa t/\Delta\big),

(145)

where we have substituted
\mathfrak{a}\coloneqq\lfloor t/\Delta\rfloor-1
in the penultimate line.

Substituting (
145
) into (
137
) and using
\kappa/\Delta=\tilde{\kappa}_{\epsilon}/\tilde{\Delta}<1
for sufficiently small
\epsilon>0
yields

\mathbb{P}\left(\left|\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}g(\pi_{s})\,ds-P_{*}(g)\right|>\epsilon,\,t<\tau_{\delta}\right)\,\leq\,2\exp\left(\sigma^{2}\tilde{\kappa}\left(\frac{2\tilde{\kappa}}{\tilde{\Delta}}\eta+\left(\frac{\tilde{\kappa}}{\tilde{\Delta}}-1\right)\epsilon\right)t\right).

Since
\eta
is a free parameter used in this proof only to determine the length of the time intervals
\Delta
, we may take
\eta
to be small enough for the constant within the second exponential above to be negative.
We have thus proven that, for arbitrary
t>0
and any
\epsilon>0
, there exists a constant
C=C_{\epsilon,\eta}
, which can be taken to be positive such that

\mathbb{P}\left(\left|\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}g(\pi_{s})\,ds-P_{*}(g)\right|>\epsilon,\,t<\tau_{\delta}\right)\,\leq\,2\exp\left(-C\sigma^{2}t\right).

(146)

∎

Lemma
4.24
then allows us to prove Theorem
4.19
using basic facts from probability theory.

Proof of Theorem
4.19
.

Define the events

\displaystyle F_{1}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\,\left\{\left|\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}g\big(\pi_{s}\big)\,ds-P_{*}(g)\right|<\epsilon\right\},

\displaystyle F_{2}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\,\left\{t<\tau_{\delta}\right\}.

Then, using Theorem
4.18
and Lemma
4.24
, observing that

\mathbb{P}(F_{1}\cap F_{2})=\mathbb{P}(F_{2})-\mathbb{P}(F_{1}^{c}\cap F_{2})

completes the proof.
∎

4.2
Noise-Induced Drift on a Circle

In this section, we consider the example where
\mathcal{S}
is the circle
\mathbb{S}^{1}
,
such as when
\Gamma
is a limit-cycle. Defining the following vector field
\mathcal{Z}:E\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}(X)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}D^{2}\pi(X)[B(X)e_{k},B(X)e_{k}]

(147)

we assume throughout this section that for small enough
\delta
, and all
X,Y\in\Gamma_{\delta}
, there is a constant such that

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{Z}(X)-\mathcal{Z}(Y)\|_{E}\leq\rm{Const}\|X-Y\|_{E}.

(148)

Giacomin, Poquet and Shapira
[
40
]
have obtained an analogous result to this for finite-dimensional limit cycles. Infinite dimensional examples that this result applies to include the noise-induced wandering of neural bumps in the visual cortex
[
54
,
67
]
, and noise-induced deviation in the speed of travelling waves in the Fitzhugh-Nagumo system on a periodic spatial domain
[
31
]
.

Rather than viewing
\mathbb{S}^{1}
as a subset of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, in this section we identify
\mathbb{S}^{1}
with the half open interval
[0,2\pi)
.
For any continuous function
f:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow[0,2\pi)
(this interval being endowed with the natural topology on
\mathbb{S}^{1}
that identifies the endpoints), we let
\overline{f}:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be the unique continuous function such that

f(t)\,=\,\overline{f}(t)\,\,\text{mod}\,2\pi.

In more concrete terms,
\overline{\pi}_{t}
is the arc-length covered by the isochronal phase at time
t>0
. (If one wants more details, refer to Propositions 1.33 & 1.34 of Hatcher
[
46
]
.)

In this section, we emphasize the dependence of the isochronal phase on
\sigma\geq 0
by writing
\pi_{t}=\pi_{t}^{\sigma}

Note that over any closed subinterval
B_{\zeta}=[\zeta,2\pi-\zeta]\subset[0,2\pi)
for
\zeta>0
, we have

d\overline{\pi}_{t}^{\sigma}\,=\,d\pi_{t}^{\sigma},

(149)

where
d\pi_{t}^{\sigma}
is as in (
99
). Now write, for
t>0
,

\nu_{t}(B_{\zeta})=\rm{Lebesgue}\big(\{s\leq t:\pi_{s}\notin B_{\zeta}\}\big).

As
\zeta\to 0^{+}
, it must be that
\nu_{t}(B^{c}_{\zeta})\to 0
almost surely. Furthermore the measure
P_{*}
from Theorem
4.19
can be interpreted as a measure on
[0,2\pi)
.
This motivates the following corollary, which implies Corollary D of Section
1.1
just as in the proof of Theorem
4.19
.

Corollary 4.25
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{S}=\mathbb{S}^{1}
and that the vector field
\mathcal{V}
defined in (
119
) is
C^{2}
.
Then

\mathbb{P}\left(\left\|\frac{1}{\sigma^{2}t}\left(\overline{\pi}_{t}^{\sigma}-\overline{\pi}_{t}^{0}\right)-P_{*}\left(\mathcal{V}\right)\right\|<\epsilon,T_{\sigma}<\tau_{\delta}\right)\,\geq\,1-t\exp\left(-c\sigma^{-2}\delta^{2}\right)-\exp\left(-C_{\epsilon}\sigma^{2}t\right).

(150)

Proof.

We first prove

\mathbb{P}\left(\left\|\frac{1}{\sigma^{2}t}\left(\overline{\pi}_{t}^{\sigma}-\overline{\pi}_{t}^{0}\right)-P_{*}\left(\mathcal{V}\right)\right\|>\epsilon,T_{\sigma}<\tau_{\delta}\right)\,\leq\,\exp\left(-C\sigma^{2}t\right),

(151)

from which (
150
) follows just as in the proof of Theorem
4.19
at the end of Section
4.1
.
Noting that
D\pi(x)V(x)
is constant with respect to
x\in B(\Gamma)
.
Hence, using (
149
) and the Itô formula (
123
) for
\pi
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\left\|\frac{1}{\sigma^{2}t}\left(\overline{\pi}_{t}^{\sigma}-\overline{\pi}_{t}^{0}\right)-P_{*}(\mathcal{V})\right\|>\epsilon,t<\tau_{\delta}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\,\mathbb{P}\left(\left\|\frac{1}{2t}\int_{0}^{t}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}D^{2}\pi(X_{s})\left[B(X_{s})e_{k},B(X_{s})e_{k}\right]\,ds-P_{*}(\mathcal{V})\right\|>\epsilon/2,t<\tau_{\delta}\right)

\displaystyle+\mathbb{P}\left(\left\|\frac{1}{\sigma t}\int_{0}^{t}D\pi(X_{s})B(X_{s})\,dW_{s}\right\|>\epsilon/2,t<\tau_{\delta}\right)

\displaystyle\eqqcolon\,\mathbb{P}(F_{1})+\mathbb{P}(F_{2}).

Then, observe that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(F_{1})

\displaystyle\leq\,\mathbb{P}\left(\left\|\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}D^{2}\pi(X_{s})[B(X_{s})e_{k},B(X_{s})e_{k}]-\mathcal{V}(\pi_{s})\,ds\right\|>\epsilon/4,t<\tau_{\delta}\right)

\displaystyle+\mathbb{P}\left(\left\|\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{V}(\pi_{s})\,ds-P_{*}(\mathcal{V})\right\|>\epsilon/4,t<\tau_{\delta}\right)

\displaystyle\eqqcolon\,\mathbb{P}(F_{1,1})+\mathbb{P}(F_{1,2}).

By the Lipschitz property of the vector field in (

), we have that there is a constant
C_{\pi}>0
,

\left\|\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}D^{2}\pi(X_{s})[B(X_{s})e_{k},B(X_{s})e_{k}]-\mathcal{V}(\pi_{s})\,ds\right\|\,\leq\,C_{\pi}\sup_{s\in[0,t]}\left\|v_{s}\right\|\,\leq\,C_{\pi}\delta,

the last inequality holding when
t<\tau_{\delta}
.
Hence, so long as we take
\delta<\delta_{\epsilon}\coloneqq\epsilon/4C_{\pi}
, we have that
\mathbb{P}(F_{1,1})=0
.
Then, under the assumption that
\mathcal{V}
is
C^{2}
, the bound on
\mathbb{P}(F_{1,2})
follows from the first part of this theorem with

, for a possibly different value of
m>0
.
The bound on
\mathbb{P}(F_{2})
is in Lemma
4.26
.
∎

Lemma 4.26
.

There exists
K_{F_{2}}>0
such that for all
t>0
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(F_{2})

\displaystyle\equiv\,\mathbb{P}\left(\left\|\frac{1}{\sigma t}\int_{0}^{t}D\pi(X_{s})B(X_{s})\,dW_{s}\right\|>\epsilon/2,\,t<\tau_{\delta}\right)\,

\displaystyle\leq\,\exp\left(-K_{F_{2}}t^{3}\right).

Proof.

Recall that
\int_{0}^{t}D\pi(X_{s})B(X_{s})\,dW_{s}
is an
\mathcal{S}
-valued continuous martingale. By the Dambins-Dubins-Schwarz theorem (see Karatzas and Shreve
[
52
]
, Theorem 3.4.6), there exist
m

\mathbb{R}
-valued Brownian Motions
\tilde{W}^{j}(t)
such that the
j
th component of
\int_{0}^{t}D\pi(X_{s})B(X_{s})\,dW_{s}
can be pathwise-identified with
\tilde{W}^{j}\big(\zeta^{j}_{t}\big)
, where
\zeta^{j}_{t}
is defined as the quadratic variation of the
j
th component of
\int_{0}^{t}D\pi(X_{s})B(X_{s})\,dW_{s}
at time
t\geq 0
,

\zeta_{t}^{j}\,=\,\left\langle\left(\int_{0}^{\cdot}\pi(X_{s})B(X_{s})\,dW_{s}\right)_{j}\right\rangle_{t}.

(152)

Observing that

\displaystyle\zeta_{t}^{j}

\displaystyle\leq\,\left\langle\int_{0}^{\cdot}\pi(X_{s})B(X_{s})\,dW_{s}\right\rangle_{t}

(153)

\displaystyle=\,\int_{0}^{t}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\left\|D\pi(X_{s})B(X_{s})e_{k}\right\|^{2}\,ds

\displaystyle\leq\,tM_{\pi}M_{B}

for
t<\tau
and setting
c\coloneqq M_{\pi}M_{B}
, we find

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(F_{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\,\mathbb{P}\left(\sup_{0\leq s\leq t}\sup_{1\leq j\leq m}\big|\tilde{W}^{j}\big(\zeta_{s}^{j}\big)\big|>\frac{\sigma\epsilon t}{2m},t<\tau\right)

(154)

\displaystyle\leq\,\mathbb{P}\left(\sup_{0\leq s\leq t}\sup_{1\leq j\leq m}\big|\tilde{W}^{j}(cs)\big|>\frac{\sigma\epsilon t}{2m},t<\tau\right).

Now, for any constant
k>0
and
0\leq s\leq t
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\tilde{W}^{j}(cs)>\frac{\sigma\epsilon t}{2m},t<\tau\right)

\displaystyle=\,\mathbb{P}\left(\sup_{0\leq s\leq t}\exp\left(k\tilde{W}^{j}(cs)\right)>\exp\left(k\frac{\sigma\epsilon t}{2m}\right),t<\tau\right)

(155)

\displaystyle\leq\,\ExpOp\left[\exp\left(k\tilde{W}^{j}(ct)-k\frac{\sigma\epsilon t}{2m}\right)\chi\{t<\tau\}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\,\exp\left(\frac{k^{2}}{2ct}-\frac{k\sigma\epsilon t}{2m}\right)

using Doob’s submartingale inequality in the penultimate line (noting that
\exp\big(k\tilde{W}^{j}(ct)\big)
is a submartingale, since
\exp
is a convex function), and using the fact that
\tilde{W}^{j}(ct)
is normally distributed with variance
ct
in the last line. To optimize the bound in (
155
), choose

k\,=\,\frac{c\sigma\epsilon}{2m}t^{2}.

(156)

After substitution, this yields the bound

\mathbb{P}\left(\tilde{W}^{j}(cs)>\frac{\sigma\epsilon t}{2m},t<\tau\right)\,\leq\,\exp\left(-\frac{c(\sigma\epsilon)^{2}}{8m^{2}}t^{3}\right)\,\eqqcolon\,\exp\left(-K_{F_{2}}t^{3}\right).

(157)

∎

Appendix A
Closeness of the Isochronal and Variational Phases

Let
m^{\prime}\leq m
denote the intrinsic dimension of
\mathcal{S}
(and therefore
\Gamma
), and for
\alpha\in\mathcal{S}
let
\{\alpha_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m^{\prime}}
denote a local coordinate system at
\alpha
.We recall the
\delta
neigbourhood of the manifold
\Gamma
,

\Gamma_{\delta}=\big\{x\in H:\|x-u\|\leq\delta\text{ for some }u\in\Gamma\big\}.

(158)

The variational phase is defined
[
66
]
to be the map
\beta:\Gamma_{\delta}\rightarrow\mathcal{S}
defined for sufficiently small
\delta>0
for
x\in\Gamma_{\delta}
as the unique point
\beta(x)\in\mathcal{S}
such that

P_{\beta(x)}[x-\gamma_{\beta(x)}]\,=\,0.

(159)

It is well-known from the theory of orbital stability that the variational phase is well-defined as long as
\delta
is sufficiently small
[
51
, Lemma 4.3.3]
. We note this in the following Lemma.

Lemma A.27
.

There exists
\hat{\delta}
such that for all
\delta\leq\hat{\delta}
, and all
x\in\Gamma_{\delta}
, there exists a unique
\beta(x)
satisfying (
159
).

Lemma A.28
.

There exists
\delta_{*}>0
such that for
\delta\in(0,\delta_{*})
, if
\left\|x-\gamma_{\pi(x)}\right\|\leq\delta
then

\left\|\gamma_{\pi(x)}-\gamma_{\beta(x)}\right\|\,\leq\,\delta^{2}.

Proof.

It is well-established in the theory of orbital stability that for all sufficiently small
\delta
and all
x\in\Gamma_{\delta}
,

\displaystyle\left\|\pi(x)-\beta(x)\right\|\leq\text{Const}\left\|x-\gamma_{\beta(x)}\right\|^{2}.

(160)

See the proof of Theorem 4.3.5 in
[
51
]
for more details. Since the map
\beta\to\gamma_{\beta}
is Lipschitz, we have proved the lemma.
∎

By using Lemma
A.28
, we are able to directly apply Theorem 5.1 of
[
66
]
to prove
4.18
.

Proof of Lemma
4.18
.

Let
\beta_{t}\coloneqq\beta(X_{t})
be the variational phase of
X_{t}
.
Then, by a union of events bound we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}[t>\tau_{\delta}]

\displaystyle=\,\mathbb{P}\left[\sup_{s\in[0,t]}\left\|X_{s}-\gamma_{\pi_{s}}\right\|>\delta\right]

\displaystyle\leq\,\mathbb{P}\left[\sup_{s\in[0,t]}\left\|X_{s}-\gamma_{\beta_{s}}\right\|>\frac{\delta}{2}\right]+\mathbb{P}\left[\sup_{s\in[0,t]}\left\|\gamma_{\beta_{s}}-\gamma_{\pi_{s}}\right\|>\frac{\delta}{2}\right].

By Lemma
A.28
, we know that so long as
t<\tau_{\delta}
we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left[\sup_{s\in[0,t]}\left\|\gamma_{\beta_{s}}-\gamma_{\pi_{s}}\right\|>\frac{\delta}{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\,\mathbb{P}\left[\sup_{s\in[0,t]}\left\|X_{s}-\gamma_{\pi_{s}}\right\|^{2}>\frac{\delta}{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\,\mathbb{P}\left[\delta^{2}>\frac{\delta}{2}\right].

Taking
\delta
sufficiently small, we thus obtan

\mathbb{P}[t>\tau_{\delta}]\,\leq\,\mathbb{P}\left[\sup_{s\in[0,t]}\left\|X_{s}-\gamma_{\beta_{s}}\right\|>\frac{\delta}{2}\right].

Applying Theorem 5.1 of
[
66
]
completes the proof.
∎

On Métivier’s Lax-Mizohata theorem and extensions to weak defects of hyperbolicity. Part one.
Karim Ndoumajoud, Benjamin Texier

Abstract
We prove that, for first-order, fully nonlinear systems of partial differential equations, under an hypothesis of ellipticity for the principal symbol, the Cauchy problem has no solution within a range of Sobolev indices depending on the regularity of the initial datum. This gives a new and greatly detailed proof of a result of G. Métivier [ Remarks on the Cauchy problem , 2005]. We then extend this result to systems experiencing a transition from hyperbolicity and ellipticity, in the spirit of recent work by N. Lerner, Y. Morimoto, and C.-J. Xu, [ Instability of the Cauchy-Kovalevskaya solution for a class of non-linear systems , 2010], and N. Lerner, T. Nguyen and B. Texier [ The onset of instability in first-order systems , 2018].



1
Introduction

We study the fully nonlinear, order-one Cauchy problem in
\mathbb{R}^{d}:

(1.1)

\left\{\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}u+F(t,x,u,\partial_{x}u)&=0,\\
u_{|t=0}&=u_{in}\in H^{\sigma}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\end{aligned}\right.

where
t\geq 0
and
d\geq 1.
The map

F:\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{Nd}\to\mathbb{R}^{N}

is smooth in a neighborhood of some
(0,x^{0},u^{0},v^{0}),
where
N\geq 1.
The principal symbol is defined as

(1.2)

A(t,x,u,v,\xi):=\xi\cdot\partial_{4}F(t,x,u,v)=\sum_{1\leq j\leq d}\xi_{j}\partial_{4_{j}}F(t,x,u,v),

where
\partial_{4_{j}}F
refers to the derivative of
F
with respect to the
j
-th component
v_{j}
of its fourth argument
(v_{1},\dots,v_{d})\in(\mathbb{R}^{N})^{d}.

Under an assumption of initial ellipticity (strong defect of hyperbolicity, see Assumption
2.1
below) for
A(0,\omega^{0}),
for some
\omega^{0}=(x^{0},u^{0},v^{0},\xi^{0})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{Nd}\times\mathbb{S}^{d-1},
we prove in Theorem
1
that if
s>1+d/2,
then no local-in-time
H^{s}
solution exists to the Cauchy problem (
1.1
), even on arbitrarily small time intervals, for some data with a Sobolev degree of regularity
\sigma\in[s,2s-1-d/2).

Under an assumption of a transition from hyperbolicity to ellipticity (weak defect of hyperbolicity; see Assumption
2.2
below) for
A(0,\omega^{0}),
for some
\omega^{0},
we prove in Theorem
2
that if
s>3/2+3/4+d/2,
then no local-in-time
H^{s}
solution exists to the Cauchy problem (
1.1
), even on arbitrarily small time intervals, for some data with a Sobolev degree of regularity
\sigma\in[s,2s-3/2-d/2).

Our results recover and extend an elliptic theorem of G. Métivier
[
3
]
. The analysis of weak defects of hyperbolicity was pioneered in the article of N. Lerner, Y. Morimoto and C.-J. Xu
[
11
]
. These authors proved that for complex scalar equations exhibiting a weak defect of hyperbolicity, no solutions could exist above a certain level of regularity. Following
[
11
]
, strong Hadamard instabilities were proved by N. Lerner, T. Nguyen and B. Texier
[
12
]
for systems exhibiting weak defects of hyperbolicity. Our second result (Theorem
2
) extends Theorem 1.6 from
[
12
]
: while the result of
[
12
]
, under an assumption analogous to Assumption
2.2
, proved a lack of regularity of an hypothetical flow, we prove that such a flow does not exist. The functional spaces considered in
[
12
]
are however larger: the lack of Hölder continuity is from
W^{1,\infty}
to
H^{m},
any
m\in\mathbb{R},
while here we restrict to sufficiently small Sobolev spaces.

2
Assumptions and results

2.1
Elliptic initial-value problems

Consider the ellipticity assumption:

Assumption 2.1
.

For some
(x^{0},u^{0},v^{0},\xi^{0})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{Nd}\times\mathbb{S}^{d-1},
the spectrum of the principal symbol
A(0,x^{0},u^{0},v^{0},\xi^{0})
defined in (
1.2
) is not entirely contained in
\mathbb{R}.

The above ellipticity assumption implies non-existence of Sobolev solutions to the initial-value problem (
1.1
), in the following sense:

Theorem 1
.

Under Assumption
2.1
,
for any
s>d/2+1,
any
s\leq\sigma<2s-1-d/2,
for some
u_{in}\in H^{\sigma}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),
there is no
T>0
and no ball
B_{x^{0}}
centered at
x^{0}
such that the initial-value problem (
1.1
) has a solution in
C^{0}([0,T],H^{s}(B_{x^{0}})).

Theorem
1
recovers the ellipticity theorem of Métivier (Theorem 4.2
[
3
]
), which extends the classical linear ellipticity theorems of Lax
[
9
]
and Mizohata
[
14
]
. The merit of our proof might be to clarify Métivier’s and prepare for extensions to weak defects of hyperbolicity (see Theorem
2
below), and to Gevrey spaces in the spirit of the work of B. Morisse
[
5
,
6
,
7
]
. The proof of Theorem
1
is given in Section
3
.

2.2
Transition to ellipticity

Weak defects of hyperbolicity
were first explored for scalar equations in
[
11
]
. The results of
[
11
]
were extended to quasi-linear systems in
[
12
]
. Roughly speaking, an assumption of a weak defect of hyperbolicity consists in relaxing Assumption
2.1
into an assumption of initial hyperbolicity that is instantaneously lost: the principal symbol is hyperbolic at
t=0
but not for any
t>0.
This kind of assumption was given precise formulations for systems in
[
12
]
, and shown to induce strong Hadamard instabilities in initial-value problems for quasi-linear systems.

Introduce
P
the characteristic polynomial of
A
evaluated along a solution
u
of (
1.1
):

(2.1)

P(t,x,\xi,\lambda)=\mbox{det}\,(A(t,x,u(t,x),\partial_{x}u(t,x),\xi)-\lambda{\rm Id}\big).

Via the equation (
1.1
), at
t=0
the characteristic polynomial
P
and its partial derivatives can all be expressed in terms of the initial datum. Denoting
v_{in}=(u_{in},\partial_{x}u_{in}),
and
A_{in}(x,\xi)=A(0,x,v_{in}(x),\xi),

G_{in}(x)=F(0,x,v_{in}(x)),
we see, in particular, that
P(0,x,\xi,\lambda)
is equal to
\mbox{det}\,\big(A_{in}(x,\xi)-\lambda{\rm Id}\big),
and
\partial_{t}P(0,x,\xi,\lambda)
to

(2.2)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}P(0,x,\xi,\lambda)=\mbox{det}^{\prime}\big(A_{in}(x,\xi)

\displaystyle-\lambda{\rm Id}\big)

\displaystyle\cdot\Big(\partial_{1}A(0,x,v_{in}(x),\xi)+\partial_{3}A(0,x,v_{in}(x),\xi)G_{in}(x)

\displaystyle+\partial_{4}A(0,x,v_{in}(x),\xi)\cdot\partial_{x}G_{in}(x)\Big).

The transition from hyperbolicity to ellipticity is formulated in
[
12
]
in terms of the jet of
P
(the first terms in its Taylor expansion) at
t=0.
These conditions thus bear on the datum, and involve the nonlinear terms in the equation.

We formulate here an assumption that is analogous to Hypothesis 1.5 in
[
12
]
, and describes a time-differentiable branching of eigenvalues of
A
from the hyperbolic to the elliptic region.
Given
U
an open set in
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{S}^{d-1},
denote
{\mathcal{S}}_{U}
the spectrum at
t=0
above
U:

{\mathcal{S}}_{U}:=\big\{(x,\xi,\lambda)\in U\times\mathbb{C},\quad P(0,x,\xi,\lambda)=0\big\}.

Assumption 2.2
.

For the principal symbol
A
(
1.2
) we assume the following conditions:

(i)

initial hyperbolicity:
for some open set
U,
we have
\omega=(x,\xi,\lambda)\in{\mathcal{S}}_{U}
only if
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}.

(ii)

Smooth diagonalizability:
for some
(x^{0},\xi^{0})\in U,
the matrix
A(0,x,\xi)
is smoothly diagonalizable for all
(x,\xi)
near
(x^{0},\xi^{0})
in
U.

(iii)

Existence of double eigenvalues:
for some
\omega^{0}=(x^{0},\xi^{0},\lambda^{0})\in{\mathcal{S}}_{U},
we have
\partial_{\lambda}P(0,\omega^{0})=0.

(iv)

C^{1}
bifurcations of the spectrum away from
\mathbb{R}:
given any
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
such that
\omega=(x^{0},\xi^{0},\lambda)\in{\mathcal{S}}_{U}
and
\partial_{\lambda}P(0,\omega)=0:
we have
\partial_{t}P(0,\omega)=0
and

(2.3)

\big(\partial_{t\lambda}^{2}P(0,\omega)\big)^{2}<(\partial_{t}^{2}P\partial_{\lambda}^{2}P)(0,\omega),

and
\partial_{t}P(0,\omega^{\prime})=0,

\partial_{\lambda}P(0,\omega^{\prime})=0
for all
\omega^{\prime}
near
\omega
in
{\mathcal{S}}_{U}.

In condition (ii) above, by “smooth” we mean “as smooth as
A_{|t=0}
”, which by assumption on
F
means “as smooth as the datum”.

We describe in Section
4.1
how Assumption
2.2
paints a picture of the spectrum of
A
evaluated along a putative solution
u
of (
1.1
) for very small time: at
t
goes from
0
to positive values, some real eigenvalues of
A
stay real, while pairs of eigenvalues branch out of the real axis into the rest of the complex plane, in a
C^{1}
fashion, so that the real parts of the branching eigenvalues is
O(t).

Theorem 2
.

Under Assumption
2.2
,
for any
s>3/2+3/4+d/2,
any
\sigma
such that
3+d/2\leq\sigma<2s-3/2-d/2,
for some
u_{in}\in H^{\sigma}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),
there is no
T>0
and no ball
B_{x^{0}}
centered at
x^{0}
such that the initial-value problem (
1.1
) has a solution in
C^{0}([0,T],H^{s}(B_{x^{0}})).

Examples of systems and data satisfying Assumption
2.2
are given in
[
12
]
. Those include in particular systems of coupled Burgers equations.

In Assumption
2.2
the instability is weaker than in Assumption
2.1
. As a consequence, the instability is slower to develop, and the condition on
\sigma-s
in Theorem
2
is stronger than the one in Theorem
1
.

Condition
\sigma>3+d/2
is required in order to make sense of two time derivatives (corresponding, via the equation, to three spatial derivatives) at
t=0,
which we need in Assumption
2.2
(iv). For the proof to go through we only need
s>2+d/2,
but then the conditions bearing on
\sigma
impose that
s
is actually greater than
3/2+3/4+d/2.

The proof of Theorem
2
is given in Section
4
.

2.3
Remarks

In the companion paper
[
15
]
, we get rid of the upper bound on
\sigma,
that is prove non-existence of Sobolev solutions above a certain level of regularity
no matter how regular the datum is,
under an extra assumption of semi-simplicity and constant multiplicity for the purely imaginary eigenvalues of the principal symbol. We do so in the elliptic case and in the case of a transition to ellipticity. This extends Theorem 4.4 in
[
3
]
.

A lengthy appendix (pages
A.1
to
B
) gives some standard and not-so-standard results pertaining to pseudo- and para-differential operators. In the course of the analysis, we indeed need an estimate for the action on Sobolev maps of the difference between a pseudo-differential operator in semiclassical quantization and its para-differential counterpart. This issue was examined earlier in
[
8
]
and
[
16
]
. We correct here small errors from
[
8
]
and
[
16
]
; see Proposition
A.3
. We also show how Gårding’s inequality translates into rates of growth for the flows of para-differential operators of order zero, in semiclassical quantization, for symbols with Hölder spatial regularity (see Proposition
B.1
).

Note finally that the condition
s>1+d/2
is critical, in the following sense: in the favorable case of symmetric hyperbolic systems, well-posedness is known only under that condition on the regularity of the datum; moreover, for the related (but nonlocal, hence distinct) incompressible Euler equations in two dimensions, well-posedness fails to hold in
H^{2}

[
1
]
. Here the assumption
s>1+d/2
means that the posited solution
u
of (
1.1
) (the existence of which we endeavor to disprove) does not have a high level of pointwise regularity. Since
F
depends on
(u,\partial_{x}u),
the symbols are only Hölder continuous in space. In particular, this forces us to rely on Gårding’s inequality in order to derive bounds for the flows of relevant order-zero para-differential operators, as opposed to the high-regularity approach of
[
17
,
13
]
.

3
Ellipticity: proof of Theorem
1

We work by contradiction and
assume
the existence of
T>0,
a ball
B_{x_{0}}
centered at
x_{0}
and
u\in C^{0}([0,T],H^{s}(B_{x_{0}}))
which solves the initial-value problem (
1.1
). The contradiction will eventually come from the high but finite regularity of the datum
u_{in}.

Our assumption puts forward a distinguished point
(x^{0},u^{0},v^{0},\xi^{0})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{Nd}\times\mathbb{S}^{d-1}.
We will choose the datum
u_{in}
(see Section
3.7
), so that
u_{in}(x^{0})\simeq u^{0},
and
\partial_{x}u_{in}(x^{0})=v^{0}.

We identify the solution
u
with its extension to the whole space
\mathbb{R}^{d}
via a linear bounded extension operator
H^{s}(B_{x_{0}})\to H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

3.1
Hyperbolic rescaling

Define the spatial rescaling map:

(3.1)

h_{j}:\quad f\in H^{s}\to(x\to f(2^{-j}x))\in H^{s},

for some
j
that will eventually be chosen large, and let

(3.2)

\tilde{u}(t,x):=u(2^{-j}t,x),\qquad u_{h}:=h_{j}\tilde{u},

where
u
is the posited solution. Then,
u_{h}
belongs to
C^{0}([0,2^{j}T],H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})),
and solves
the initial-value problem

(3.3)

\left\{\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}u_{h}+2^{-j}F(2^{-j}t,2^{-j}x,u_{h},2^{j}\partial_{x}u_{h})&=0,\\
u_{h}(0,x)=u_{in}(2^{-j}x).\end{aligned}\right.

3.2
Paralinearization

Our second step is to para-linearize the system. Since
u_{h}
belongs to
H^{s}
for
t\in[0,2^{j}T],
we have (see for instance Theorem 5.2.4 in
[
4
]
):

(3.4)

F(2^{-j}t,2^{-j}x,u_{h},2^{j}\partial_{x}u_{h})=T_{\partial_{3}F(u_{h},2^{j}\partial_{x}u_{h})}u_{h}+T_{\partial_{4}F(u_{h},2^{j}\partial_{x}u_{h})}2^{j}\partial_{x}u_{h}+R_{j}^{para},

where
T_{a}b
refers to the para-product of
b
by
a,
and
\partial_{3}F
refers to the partial derivative of
F
with respect to its third (
u
) argument. The remainder satisfies

(3.5)

R_{j}^{para}\in L^{\infty}([0,2^{j}T],H^{2(s-1)-d/2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})).

The proof of Theorem 5.2.4 in
[
4
]
shows moreover that

(3.6)

\|R_{j}^{para}\|_{H^{2(s-1)-d/2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\lesssim C_{F}(\|u_{h},2^{j}\partial_{x}u_{h}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})})\|(u_{h},2^{j}\partial_{x}u_{h})\|_{H^{s-1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},

where
C_{F}:\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
is nondecreasing and depends only on
F.
In (
3.4
), we omitted the arguments
(2^{-j}t,2^{-j}x)
of
\partial_{3}F
and
\partial_{4}F
in the right-hand side.

3.3
Semiclassical quantization

Given a symbol
a,
our preferred notation for the para-differential operator with symbol
a
is
{\rm op}(a):

(3.7)

T_{a}={\rm op}(a),

where the spatial convolution with the partial inverse Fourier transform of some admissible cut-off function is implicit. For details on our notation, and classical results pertaining to para-differential operators, see Appendix
A
.

By definition, the para-differential operator with symbol
a
in
2^{-j}
-semiclassical quantization is

{\rm op}_{j}(a):=h_{j}^{-1}{\rm op}(h_{j}a)h_{j},

where
h_{j}
is defined in (
3.1
).
Classical results in semiclassical quantization are recalled in Appendix
A
.
We remark that, using notation (
3.7
), we can write (
3.4
) in the form

(3.8)

2^{-j}h_{j}F(\tilde{u},\partial_{x}\tilde{u})=2^{-j}{\rm op}\big(h_{j}\partial_{3}F(\tilde{u},\partial_{x}\tilde{u})\big)h_{j}\tilde{u}+{\rm op}\big(h_{j}\partial_{4}F(\tilde{u},\partial_{x}\tilde{u})\big)\partial_{x}(h_{j}\tilde{u})+2^{-j}R^{para}_{j}.

That is,

(3.9)

\displaystyle 2^{-j}F(\tilde{u},\partial_{x}\tilde{u})

\displaystyle=h_{j}^{-1}{\rm op}\Big(2^{-j}h_{j}\partial_{3}F(\tilde{u},\partial_{x}\tilde{u})\Big)h_{j}\tilde{u}+h_{j}^{-1}{\rm op}\Big(h_{j}\partial_{4}F(\tilde{u},\partial_{x}\tilde{u})\cdot i\xi\Big)h_{j}\tilde{u}

\displaystyle+2^{-j}h_{j}^{-1}R^{para}_{j}.

Thus
\tilde{u}
solves the initial-value problem

(3.10)

\left\{\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}\tilde{u}+{\rm op}_{j}(\tilde{A})\tilde{u}&=\tilde{R}^{para}_{j},\\
\tilde{u}(0)&=u_{in},\end{aligned}\right.

where
\tilde{R}^{para}_{j}
is

(3.11)

\tilde{R}^{para}_{j}:=-2^{-j}h_{j}^{-1}R^{para}_{j},

and the symbol
\tilde{A}
is defined by

(3.12)

\displaystyle\tilde{A}(t,x,\tilde{u},\partial_{x}\tilde{u},\xi):=2^{-j}\partial_{3}F(2^{-j}t,x,\tilde{u},\partial_{x}\tilde{u})+\partial_{4}F(2^{-j}t,x,\tilde{u},\partial_{x}\tilde{u})\cdot i\xi,

where
\tilde{u}
is evaluated at
(t,x).
Recall that
j
will be chosen to be large. Thus the symbol
\tilde{A}
appears as a small perturbation of the principal symbol
iA,
with
A
defined in (
1.2
).

3.4
Localization near the distinguished point in the cotangent space

We use a Littlewood-Paley dyadic decomposition
(\phi_{j})_{j\geq 0}.
That is, a smooth frequency truncation function
\phi_{0}
is given, with
0\leq\phi_{0}\leq 1,
and
\phi_{0}(\xi)\equiv 1
for
|\xi|\leq\varepsilon_{1}
and
\phi_{0}(\xi)\equiv 0
for
|\xi|\geq\varepsilon_{2},
for some real numbers
\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2}
satisfying
0<\varepsilon_{1}<\varepsilon_{2}<1<2\varepsilon_{1}.
We let

(3.13)

\phi_{j}(\xi):=\phi_{0}(2^{-j}\xi)-\phi_{0}(2^{-j+1}\xi),\qquad\mbox{for $j\geq 1.$}

In particular, the cut-off
\phi_{1}
is supported in the neighborhood
\{\varepsilon_{1}\leq|\xi|\leq 2\varepsilon_{2}\}
of the unit disk; the cut-off
\phi_{k}
is supported in the annulus
\{2^{k}\varepsilon_{1}\leq|\xi|\leq 2^{1+k}\varepsilon_{2}\}.
We have the decomposition

(3.14)

f=\sum_{j\geq 0}\phi_{j}(D)f,

where the operator
\phi_{j}(D)
is the Fourier multiplier with symbol
\phi_{j}(\xi).

We localize in
(x,\xi)
near
(x^{0},\xi^{0}),
within the annulus
|\xi|\simeq 2^{-j},
for some integer
j
which will eventually be chosen large. In this view, we let
\psi
be a smooth space-frequency cut-off supported in
(x^{0},\xi^{0})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}_{x}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}_{\xi}:
for some
\delta>0,
we have

(3.15)

\psi(x,\xi)=\left\{\begin{aligned} 1,&\quad|x-x^{0}|+|\xi-\xi^{0}|\leq\delta/2,\\
0,&\quad|x-x^{0}|+|\xi-\xi^{0}|\geq\delta.\end{aligned}\right.

We may choose
\psi
to be a tensor product
\psi(x,\xi)=\psi_{1}(x)\psi_{2}(\xi).

We also let

(3.16)

v:={\rm op}_{j}(\psi)\tilde{u}

where
\tilde{u}
is defined in (
3.2
) and was found in Section
3.3
to solve the initial-value problem (
3.10
).
The initial-value problem in
v
is

(3.17)

\left\{\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}v+{\rm op}_{j}(\tilde{A})v&=f,\\
v(0)&={\rm op}_{j}(\psi)u_{in},\end{aligned}\right.

where

(3.18)

f=-[{\rm op}_{j}(\psi),{\rm op}_{j}(\tilde{A})]\tilde{u}+{\rm op}_{j}(\psi)\tilde{R}^{para}_{j}.

We observe that the operator in (
3.17
) is further localized, up to a remainder. Indeed, we have

{\rm op}_{j}(\tilde{A})v={\rm op}_{j}(\psi^{\sharp}\tilde{A})v-f_{1},

where

(3.19)

f_{1}:={\rm op}_{j}(\tilde{A})\big({\rm op}_{j}(\psi^{\sharp}){\rm op}_{j}(\psi)-{\rm op}_{j}(\psi)\big)\tilde{u}+({\rm op}_{j}(\psi^{\sharp}\tilde{A})-{\rm op}_{j}(\tilde{A}){\rm op}_{j}(\psi^{\sharp})\big)v,

and
f_{1}
will be seen to be appropriately sense.

Above, we used notation
\chi\prec\chi^{\sharp},
borrowed from
[
12
]
:

(3.20)

\chi\prec\chi^{\sharp}\quad\mbox{means}\quad(1-\chi^{\sharp})\chi\equiv 0,

with
\chi,\chi^{\sharp}\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{2d};[0,1]).
Whenever we use (
3.20
), it is implicit that the support of the extension
\chi^{\sharp}
is
not much bigger
than the support of
\chi.
The context usually makes clear what is meant by “not much bigger”.

We let

(3.21)

g:=f+f_{1},

and the initial-value problem in
v
now appears as

(3.22)

\left\{\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}v+{\rm op}_{j}(M)v&=g,\\
v(0)&={\rm op}_{j}(\psi)u_{in}.\end{aligned}\right.

The symbol
M
is, up to a small perturbation, a localized version of the principal symbol
A
defined in (
1.2
):

(3.23)

\displaystyle M(t,x,\xi)=\psi^{\sharp}(x,\xi)\Big(

\displaystyle A(2^{-j}t,x,\tilde{u},\partial_{x}\tilde{u},i\xi)+2^{-j}\partial_{3}F(2^{-j}t,x,\tilde{u},\partial_{x}\tilde{u})\Big),

where
\tilde{u}
is evaluated at
(t,x).
By regularity of
F,
the posited regularity of
u,
and Lemma
A.1
, we have
M(t)\in C^{[\theta],\theta-[\theta]}S^{0},
for all
t
in the interval of definition of
\tilde{u},
where

(3.24)

\theta:=s-1-d/2.

3.5
Spectral decomposition

Assumption
2.1
states that the spectrum of
A(0,x^{0},u^{0},v^{0},\xi^{0})
is not entirely contained in
\mathbb{R}.

The datum will be chosen to satisfy
v(0,x^{0})\simeq u^{0},

\partial_{x}v(0,x^{0})\simeq v^{0}.
(A precise choice is made in Section
3.7
). By continuity of the spectrum (a consequence of Rouché’s theorem, see for instance Proposition 1.1 in
[
18
]
), if

(3.25)

j
is large enough,  and
|v(0,x^{0})-u^{0}|+|\partial_{x}v(0,x^{0})-v^{0}|
is small enough,

then the spectrum of
M(0,x^{0},\xi^{0})
is not entirely contained in
i\mathbb{R}.

Note that
M
is defined in (
3.23
) in terms of
A
evaluated at
i\xi,
so that it is indeed the real parts of the eigenvalues that matter in this ill-posedness proof. Since
M
appears in the left-hand side of (
3.22
), an eigenvalue with a negative real part is susceptible to generate growth.

We denote

\gamma:=\Re e\,\lambda_{0}<\Re e\,\lambda_{1}<\dots<\Re e\,\lambda_{p_{0}}

the spectrum of
M(0,x^{0},\xi^{0}).
The most unstable eigenvalue is
\lambda_{0}.
By reality of the coefficients of
A,
the real part of the spectrum of
M
is symmetric with respect to 0. Thus we have
\gamma=\Re e\,\lambda_{0}<0.

The elliptic eigenvalue
\lambda_{0}
might correspond to a coalescing point
(0,x^{0},\xi^{0},\lambda_{0})
in the spectrum.
We denote
\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{p}
the eigenvalues of
M
which coalesce at
z^{0}=(0,x^{0},\xi^{0}),
with value
\lambda_{0}
at
z^{0}:

(3.26)

\mu_{j}(z^{0})=\lambda_{0},\qquad 1\leq j\leq p.

We say that the eigenvalues
\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{p}
are
elliptic
, and call
hyperbolic
the other eigenvalues of
M.
The hyperbolic eigenvalues are thus those which take values
\Re e\,\lambda_{1},\dots,\Re e\,\lambda_{p_{0}}
at
z^{0}.
We denote
\mu_{p+1},\dots,\mu_{N}
the other branches of eigenvalues of
M.
It may be that
\Re e\,\mu_{j}\neq 0
for some
j\in[p+1,N],
even though we call
\mu_{j}
hyperbolic, but it will be a convenient way of referring to this part of the spectrum.

If
j\leq p<j^{\prime},
then

(3.27)

\Re e\,\mu_{j}<\Re e\,\mu_{j^{\prime}},

uniformly in a small neighborhood of
z^{0},
by continuity of the eigenvalues. We may and do assume that the support of
\psi^{\sharp}
is small enough so that (
3.27
) holds over the support of
\psi^{\sharp}.

The eigenvalues
\mu_{j}
may not be smooth, but satisfy the Hölder estimates (see for instance Proposition 3.1 in
[
18
]
, or
[
2
]
):

(3.28)

|\mu_{j}(z)-\mu_{j}(z^{0})|\leq c|z-z_{0}|^{1/p^{\prime}},

for some
c>0
and some
p^{\prime}\geq p.

We denote
E=E(t,x,\xi)
the direct sum of the generalized eigenspaces associated with the eigenvalues
\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{p}
which coalesce at
z^{0}=(0,x^{0},\xi^{0})
with value
\lambda_{0},
and
H=H(t,x,\xi)
the direct sum of the generalized eigenspaces associated with the other eigenvalues, so that

(3.29)

\mathbb{C}^{N}=E(t,x,\xi)+H(t,x,\xi),\qquad\mbox{for all $(t,x,\xi)$ near $(0,x^{0},\xi^{0}).$}

3.6
Projections

We denote
P_{H}
the projector onto
H
parallel to
E,
and
P_{E}
the projector onto
E
parallel to
H,
so that

(3.30)

{\rm Id}=P_{E}+P_{H}.

We have integral representations for
P_{E}
and
P_{H}
in terms of
M
(see for instance Proposition 2.1 in
[
18
]
):

(3.31)

P_{\star}=\frac{1}{2i\pi}\int_{{\mathcal{C}}_{\star}}(z-M)^{-1}\,dz,\qquad\star\in\{H,E\},

where
{\mathcal{C}}_{H}
is a contour enclosing all the hyperbolic eigenvalues and only those, and
{\mathcal{C}}_{E}
is a contour enclosing the elliptic eigenvalues. By spectral separations (
3.27
), we can choose non-overlapping contours
{\mathcal{C}}_{E}
and
{\mathcal{C}}_{H}.
The representations (
3.31
) then ensure that
P_{E}
and
P_{H}
are as smooth as
M,
so that
P_{E},P_{H}\in C^{0,\theta}S^{0},
pointwise in time.

The symbol
M,
and the projectors
P_{E}
and
P_{H}
depend on
\partial_{x}u,
which a priori is only continuous in time. This will be an issue in view of the change of variable (
3.32
). Hence we regularize the projectors. Let
P_{H}^{\varepsilon}
be
P_{H}
evaluated at
k_{\varepsilon}\star(u,\partial_{x}u),
where
k_{\varepsilon}
is a smoothing kernel, just like in (
A.23
). The small parameter
\varepsilon
will be chosen in terms of
j
in the proof of Lemma
3.5
.

The associated components of the unknown
v
are

(3.32)

v_{H}:={\rm op}_{j}(\psi^{\flat}P_{H}^{\varepsilon})v,\qquad v_{E}={\rm op}_{j}(\psi^{\flat}P_{E}^{\varepsilon})v,

with

(3.33)

\psi\prec\psi\prec\psi^{\sharp},

where we used notation (
3.20
).
We denote

(3.34)

M_{H}:=P_{H}M,\qquad M_{E}:=P_{E}M,

so that

(3.35)

M_{H}\equiv M_{H}P_{H},\qquad M_{E}\equiv M_{E}P_{E},

and now derive the equations in
v_{H}
and
v_{E}.
We have

\displaystyle\partial_{t}v_{H}

\displaystyle={\rm op}_{j}(\psi\partial_{t}P^{\varepsilon}_{H})v+{\rm op}_{j}(\psi P^{\varepsilon}_{H})\partial_{t}v

\displaystyle={\rm op}_{j}(\psi\partial_{t}P^{\varepsilon}_{H})v-{\rm op}_{j}(\psi^{\flat}P_{H}^{\varepsilon}){\rm op}_{j}(M)v+{\rm op}_{j}(\psi^{\flat}P_{H}^{\varepsilon})g.

Next, by composition of para-differential operators in semiclassical quantization, and approximation of
P_{H}
by
P_{H}^{\varepsilon},

{\rm op}_{j}(\psi^{\flat}P_{H}^{\varepsilon}){\rm op}_{j}(M)={\rm op}_{j}(\psi^{\flat}M_{H})+\mbox{a smaller term.}

Precisely, using the composition result described in Section
A.5
,

{\rm op}_{j}(\psi^{\flat}P_{H}^{\varepsilon}){\rm op}_{j}(M)={\rm op}_{j}(\psi^{\flat}M_{H})+2^{-j\theta}R_{\theta}(\psi^{\flat}P_{H}^{\varepsilon},M)+{\rm op}_{j}(\psi^{\flat}(P_{H}^{\varepsilon}-P_{H})){\rm op}_{j}(M),

where we recall (
3.24
)
\theta=s-1-d/2.

Besides, by (
3.35
),

\displaystyle{\rm op}_{j}(\psi^{\flat}M_{H})

\displaystyle={\rm op}_{j}(M_{H}){\rm op}_{j}(\psi^{\flat}P_{H}^{\varepsilon})+2^{-j\theta}R_{\theta}(M_{H},\psi^{\flat}P_{H})+{\rm op}_{j}(M_{H}){\rm op}_{j}(\psi^{\flat}(P_{H}-P_{H}^{\varepsilon})).

We denote
R_{H}
the sum of the remainders in the last two equations, as we let

(3.36)

\begin{aligned} R_{H}&:=2^{-j\theta}\big(R_{\theta}(\psi^{\flat}P_{H}^{\varepsilon},M)+R_{\theta}(M_{H},\psi^{\flat}P_{H})\big)\\
&\quad+{\rm op}_{j}(M_{H}){\rm op}_{j}(\psi^{\flat}(P_{H}-P_{H}^{\varepsilon}))++{\rm op}_{j}(\psi^{\flat}(P_{H}^{\varepsilon}-P_{H})){\rm op}_{j}(M)\end{aligned}.

Substituting
E
for
H,
we obtain a system in
(v_{H},v_{E}):

(3.37)

\left\{\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}v_{H}+{\rm op}_{j}(M_{H})v_{H}&=g_{H},\\
\partial_{t}v_{E}+{\rm op}_{j}(M_{E})v_{E}&=g_{E},\end{aligned}\right.

with notation

(3.38)

g_{\star}:={\rm op}_{j}(\psi^{\flat}P_{\star}^{\varepsilon})g-(R_{\star}+{\rm op}_{j}(\psi^{\flat}\partial_{t}P_{\star}^{\varepsilon}))v,\qquad\star\in\{H,E\}.

In the next Section, we choose the datum
u_{in}
and examine what our choice entails for the data
v_{E}(0)
and
v_{H}(0).

3.7
The initial datum

We carefully choose the polarization of the datum, and its frequency localization. This Section ends with Corollary
3.3
, where a lower bound for an
L^{2}
norm of
v_{E}(0)
is proved.

3.7.1
Polarization

Here we choose the polarization of the initial datum
u_{in},
meaning its direction in the state space
\mathbb{R}^{N}.
The most obvious choice
u_{in}(x^{0})=u^{0}
is not always appropriate. Indeed, it could be that the elliptic component of
u^{0}
(in the sense of (
3.29
)) is zero. For instance, with
d=2
and
N=3
and

A(u,i\xi)=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}0&iu_{3}\xi_{1}&0\\
-iu_{3}\xi_{1}&0&0\\
0&0&iu_{1}\xi_{2}\end{array}\right),

then
A
is elliptic at
(u^{0},\xi^{0})
with
u^{0}=(0,0,1)
and
\xi^{0}=(1,0).
The elliptic subspace
E,
however, satisfies
E(u^{0},\xi^{0})=\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\{0\},
so that
P_{E}(u^{0},\xi^{0})u^{0}=0.

Thus choosing
u_{in}(x^{0})=u^{0}
would be an issue in the perspective of proving a bound from below for the elliptic component
v_{E}(0)
of the datum. The following Lemma shows that an appropriate choice for
u_{in}(x^{0})
is always possible:

Lemma 3.1
.

Under Assumption
2.1
,
for some
u^{1}\in\mathbb{R}^{d},
for
u_{in}
such that
u_{in}(x^{0})=u^{1}
and
\partial_{x}u_{in}(x^{0})=v^{0},
the spectrum of
M(0,x^{0},\xi^{0})
is not included in
i\mathbb{R}
and we have
P_{E}(0,x^{0},\xi^{0})u^{1}\neq 0,
where
P_{E}
is the projection onto the elliptic space and parallel to the hyperbolic space (
3.29
).

Recall that the symbol
M
is defined in (
3.23
). It depends on
(t,x)
through
(u,\partial_{x}u),
where
u
is our posited solution. In particular, the symbol
M
depends on the datum
u_{in}.
Accordingly, the symbol
P_{E}
depends on the datum.

Proof.

Let
(x^{0},u^{0},v^{0},\xi^{0})
as in Assumption
2.1
. Let
M_{0}
be the matrix-valued symbol (
3.23
) at
(t,x,\xi)=(0,x^{0},\xi^{0}),
with the choice
u_{in}(x^{0})=u^{0},

\partial_{x}u_{in}(x^{0})=v^{0}.
Let
E_{0}
be the associated elliptic space, as defined in Section (
3.27
). If
P_{E_{0}}u^{0}\neq 0,
then we may choose
u^{1}=u^{0}.

Otherwise,
P_{E_{0}}u^{0}=0.
Let
\vec{e}
be a unitary elliptic eigenvector of
M_{0},
and posit

u_{in}(x^{0})=u^{1}:=u^{0}+\alpha\vec{e},

for some small
\alpha>0,
and
\partial_{x}u_{in}(x^{0})=v^{0}.
If
\alpha
is small enough, by continuity of the spectrum, the eigenvalues of
M(0,x^{0},\xi^{0})
are not all purely imaginary, so that the space
E(0,x^{0},\xi^{0})
is not reduced to
\{0\}.

By definition of
M
in (
3.23
), the projector
P_{E}
takes the form

P_{E(0,x,\xi)}=\underline{P}_{E}(x,u(0,x),\partial_{x}u(0,x),\xi).

Since the elliptic eigenvalues are separated from the hyperbolic eigenvalues, the map
\underline{P}
is smooth in its arguments (see for instance Proposition 2.1 in
[
18
]
). We may further choose
\vec{e}
so that

(3.39)

\vec{e}+\partial_{u}\underline{P}_{E}(0,u^{0},v^{0},\xi^{0})\cdot u^{0}\neq 0.

Indeed, if
\partial_{u}\underline{P}_{E}(0,u^{0},v^{0},\xi^{0})\cdot u^{0}\neq 0
does not generates the whole elliptic eigenspace
E(0,x^{0},\xi^{0}),
then we choose for
\vec{e}
to be any other (unitary) elliptic eigenvector. If
\partial_{u}\underline{P}(0,u^{0},v^{0},\xi^{0})\cdot u^{0}
happens to generate
E(0,x^{0},\xi^{0})
in its entirety, then we let
\vec{e}=\lambda\partial_{u}\underline{P}_{E}(0,u^{0},v^{0},\xi^{0})\cdot u^{0}\neq 0,
and we may choose
\lambda\neq-1
so that
\vec{e}
is unitary.

Then,

\displaystyle P_{E(0,x^{0},\xi^{0})}u^{1}

\displaystyle=P_{E_{0}}(u^{0}+\alpha\vec{e})+(P_{E(0,x^{0},\xi^{0})}-P_{E_{0}})(u^{0}+\alpha\vec{e})

\displaystyle=\alpha\vec{e}+(P_{E(0,x^{0},\xi^{0})}-P_{E_{0}})(u^{0}+\alpha\vec{e})

\displaystyle=\alpha\big(\vec{e}+\partial_{u}\underline{P}_{E}(x^{0},u^{0},\xi^{0})\cdot u^{0}\big)+O(\alpha^{2}),

which implies
P_{E(0,x^{0},\xi^{0})}u^{1}\neq 0
by (
3.39
), if
\alpha
is small enough.
∎

3.7.2
Oscillations

Let
w
be a scalar map in the Schwartz class
{\mathcal{S}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{x}),
such that
\hat{w}\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{\xi}),
and
w(x^{0})\neq 0.
Associated with
w,
we let

(3.40)

w_{in}(x):=\sum_{j\geq 0}a_{j}e^{ix\cdot 2^{j}\xi^{0}}w,

where
a_{j}>0
is a
j
-dependent amplitude.

Lemma 3.2
.

With the choice

(3.41)

a_{j}:=2^{-j\sigma}(1+j)^{-1},\qquad j>0,

we have

c_{1}a_{j}\leq\|\phi_{j+1}(D)v_{in}\|_{L^{2}}\leq c_{2}a_{j},

for
j\geq j^{0}>0,
for some
j^{0}
and some
c_{1}>0
and
c_{2}>0
which depend only
w
and the radii
\varepsilon_{1}
and
\varepsilon_{2}
that appear in the Littlewood-Paley decomposition
(\phi_{j})_{j\geq 0}
introduced in Section
3.4
.

In particular, the scalar map
w_{in}
belongs to
H^{\sigma}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\setminus\big(\cup_{\varepsilon>0}H^{\sigma+\varepsilon}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\big).
Moreover, if
|a_{0}|
is large enough, then
w_{in}(x^{0})\neq 0.

Proof.

Given
j_{0}>0,
we examine
\phi_{j_{0}+1}(D_{x})v_{in}.
We note that
\phi_{j_{0}+1}\equiv\phi_{1}(2^{-j_{0}}\cdot).
The term of index
j
in the sum is

\phi_{j_{0}+1}(D)\big(a_{j}e^{ix\cdot 2^{j}\xi^{0}}w\big)=a_{j}e^{ix\cdot 2^{j}\xi^{0}}{\mathcal{F}}^{-1}\Big(\phi_{1}(2^{-j_{0}+j}\xi_{0}+2^{-j_{0}}\cdot)\hat{w}\,\Big).

Let
R>0
such that the support of
\hat{w}
is supported in
\{|\xi|\leq R\}.
Then, we observe that for
\phi_{1}(2^{-j_{0}+j}\xi_{0}+2^{-j_{0}}\xi)\hat{w}(\xi)
to be different from zero, we must have

(3.42)

|\xi|\leq R,\qquad 2^{j_{0}}\varepsilon_{1}\leq|2^{j}\xi_{0}+\xi|\leq 2^{j_{0}+1}\varepsilon_{2}.

(Recall indeed that
\phi_{1}
is supported in
\{\varepsilon_{1}\leq|\xi|\leq 2\varepsilon_{2}\}.
) If
j_{0}
is large enough so that

2^{-j_{0}}R<\varepsilon_{1}-1/2,\quad\mbox{and}\quad 2^{-(j_{0}-1)}R+\varepsilon_{2}<1,

then there is no
\xi
such that (
3.42
) holds unless
j=j_{0}.
Now

\|\phi_{1}(2^{-j}D)\big(a_{j}e^{ix\cdot 2^{j}\xi^{0}}w\big)\|_{L^{2}}=a_{j}w_{j},

where

w_{j}=\|\phi_{1}(\xi^{0}+2^{-j}\cdot)\hat{w}\|_{L^{2}},

so that
w_{j}\leq|w|_{L^{2}}.
Besides, since
\hat{w}\neq 0
(indeed,
w\neq 0
), for some
|\eta|\leq R,
we have
\hat{w}(\eta)\neq 0.
If
j
is large enough, then
\varepsilon_{1}\leq|\xi^{0}+2^{-j}\eta|\leq 2\varepsilon_{2},
so
that
\phi_{1}(\xi^{0}+2^{-j}\eta)\neq 0,
hence
w_{j}>0,
with a lower bound which does not depend on
j.
Indeed, we have
|\hat{w}|\geq\gamma_{0}>0
in the ball
B(\eta,r)
for some
\gamma_{0}>0
and some
r>0
which depend only on the choice of
w.
Then,
\phi_{1}(\xi^{0}+2^{-j}\xi)\equiv 1
on this ball if
j
is large enough. Hence the lower bound for
w_{j}
is independent on
j.

Finally, if
|a_{0}|
is large enough, then

|w_{in}(x^{0})|\geq|w(x^{0})|\Big(|a_{0}|-\sum_{j\geq 1}|a_{j}|\Big)>0,

since
w(x^{0})\neq 0.

∎

3.7.3
Localization

The datum is defined as

(3.43)

\displaystyle u_{in}(x)

\displaystyle=u^{1}\frac{w_{in}(x)}{|w_{in}(x^{0})|}+(x-x^{0})\cdot(v^{0}-u^{1}\frac{\partial_{x}w_{in}(x^{0})}{|w_{in}(x^{0})|}\big)\psi_{1}(x),

where the vector
u^{1}
is introduced in Lemma
3.1
and the scalar function
w_{in}
in Section
3.7.2
; the spatial cut-off
\psi_{1}
is introduced in Section
3.4
: we have
\psi_{1}\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d};[0,1]),
with
\psi_{1}\equiv 1
in a neighborhood of
0,
and the support of
\psi_{1}
is appropriately small.

With the choice (
3.43
), we have, by Lemma
3.2
,

(3.44)

u_{in}\in H^{\sigma}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\setminus\big(\cup_{\varepsilon>0}H^{\sigma+\varepsilon}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\big),

and also

(3.45)

u_{in}(x^{0})=u^{1},\qquad\partial_{x}u_{in}(x^{0})=v^{0}.

Moreover, the elliptic component of the datum is bounded from below:

Corollary 3.3
.

If the parameter
\delta
measuring the size of the support of the cut-off
\psi
(
3.15
) is small enough, and if
\varepsilon>0
is small enough, then the datum
v_{E}(0)={\rm op}_{j}(\psi^{\flat}P_{E}^{\varepsilon}){\rm op}_{j}(\psi)u_{in}
for the elliptic component
v_{E}
satisfies

(3.46)

\|{\rm op}_{j}(\tilde{\psi})v_{E}(0)\|_{L^{2}(B(x^{0},\delta))}\geq C2^{-j\sigma}(1+j)^{-1},

for
j
large enough and some constant
C>0
which depends on
\delta
but not on
j,
for some cut-off
\tilde{\psi}
such that
\tilde{\psi}\prec\psi^{\flat},
with notation introduced in (
3.20
).

Above,
\delta>0
is the small localization radius introduced in Section
3.4
.

Proof.

Throughout this proof, the projector
P_{E}
(defined at the beginning of Section
3.6
) is evaluated at
t=0.
In particular,
P_{E}
depends on
x
through the datum
u_{in}\in H^{\sigma},
and
\|P_{E}^{\varepsilon}\|_{0,0,1+[d/2]}<\infty
since
\sigma>d/2.
Here we used the symbolic norms introduced in (
A.9
). This implies, by (
A.17
), the bound

(3.47)

\|{\rm op}_{j}(\psi^{\flat}P_{E}^{\varepsilon})\|_{L^{2}\to L^{2}}\lesssim 1.

The elliptic datum is

v_{E}(0)={\rm op}_{j}(\psi^{\flat}P_{E}^{\varepsilon}){\rm op}_{j}(\psi)u_{in}.

The second term in
u_{in}
does not contribute much to the
L^{2}
norm of
v_{E}(0).

Indeed, the difference
u_{in}-w_{in}(u^{1}/|w_{in}(x^{0})|)
is compactly supported in
x.
Denote it
\tilde{\psi}_{1}.
We have

{\rm op}_{j}(\psi)\tilde{\psi}_{1}={\rm op}_{j}(\psi_{1})\psi_{2}(2^{-j}D)\tilde{\psi}_{1},

and since the Fourier transform of
\tilde{\psi}_{1}
belongs to the Schwartz space, the
L^{2}
norm of
\psi_{2}(2^{-j}D)\tilde{\psi}_{1}
is controlled by
2^{-jN^{\prime}},
for
N^{\prime}
arbitrarily large, up to an
N^{\prime}
-dependent constant. Thus we may focus on
{\rm op}_{j}(\psi^{\flat}P_{E}^{\varepsilon}){\rm op}_{j}(\psi)(w_{in}u^{1})
in the rest of this proof.

At this point it is convenient to use pseudo-differential operators, as opposed to para-differential operators. By Proposition
A.3
and regularity of
\psi,

\|({\rm op}_{j}(\psi)-\mathrm{pdo}_{j}(\psi))\|_{L^{2}\to L^{2}}\lesssim 2^{-j(\sigma^{\prime}-d)},

for any
\sigma^{\prime}>0,
and

\|({\rm op}_{j}(\psi^{\flat}P_{E}^{\varepsilon})-\mathrm{pdo}_{j}(\psi^{\flat}P_{E}^{\varepsilon}))\mathrm{pdo}_{j}(\psi)v\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim 2^{-j(\sigma-1-d/2)}\|\mathrm{pdo}_{j}(\psi)v\|_{j,d/2},

for any
v\in H^{d/2}.

Since
\mathrm{pdo}_{j}(\psi)=\psi_{1}\psi_{2}(2^{-j}D),
and
\psi_{1}\in C^{\infty}_{c},
we have

\|\mathrm{pdo}_{j}(\psi)w_{in}\|_{j,d/2}\lesssim\|\psi_{2}(2^{-j}D)w_{in}\|_{j,d/2},

and by regularity of
w_{in}
and localization of the support of
\psi_{2},

\|\psi_{2}(2^{-j}D)w_{in}\|_{j,d/2}\lesssim 2^{-j\sigma}.

With the above and (
3.47
), we find

(3.48)

\|({\rm op}_{j}(\psi^{\flat}P_{E}^{\varepsilon}){\rm op}_{j}(\psi)-\mathrm{pdo}_{j}(\psi^{\flat}P_{E}^{\varepsilon})\mathrm{pdo}_{j}(\psi))(w_{in}u^{1})\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim 2^{-j(2\sigma-1-d/2)},

and we now focus on
\mathrm{pdo}_{j}(\psi^{\flat}P_{E}^{\varepsilon})\mathrm{pdo}_{j}(\psi)(w_{in}u^{1}).
We have

\mathrm{pdo}_{j}(\psi)(w_{in}u^{1})(x)=\psi_{1}(x)\sum_{j^{\prime}\geq 0}\psi_{2}(2^{-j}D)(a_{j}e^{ix\cdot 2^{j^{\prime}}\xi^{0}}wu^{1})(x).

Just like in the proof of Lemma
3.2
, we have
\psi_{2}(2^{-j^{\prime}}D)(e^{ix\cdot 2^{j^{\prime}}\xi^{0}}w)\neq 0
only if
j^{\prime}=j.
Thus

\displaystyle\mathrm{pdo}_{j}(\psi)w_{in}

\displaystyle=\psi_{1}(x)\psi_{2}(2^{-j}D)(a_{j}e^{ix\cdot 2^{j}\xi^{0}}w)

\displaystyle=a_{j}e^{ix\cdot 2^{j}\xi^{0}}a_{j}\psi_{1}(x)\psi_{2}(\xi^{0}+2^{-j}D)w,

and

(3.49)

\mathrm{pdo}_{j}(\psi^{\flat}P_{E}^{\varepsilon})\mathrm{pdo}_{j}(\psi)(w_{in}u^{1})=a_{j}e^{ix\cdot 2^{j}\xi^{0}}\psi_{1}^{\flat}(x)\mathrm{pdo}((\psi_{2}^{\flat}P_{E}^{\varepsilon})(\xi^{0}+2^{-j}\cdot))\tilde{w}(x),

where we denote
\tilde{w}:=u^{1}\psi_{1}\psi_{2}(\xi^{0}+2^{-j}D)w.
We approximate

(3.50)

\mathrm{pdo}((\psi_{2}^{\flat}P_{E}^{\varepsilon})(\xi^{0}+2^{-j}\cdot))\tilde{w}(x)\simeq P_{E}^{\varepsilon}(x,\xi^{0})\tilde{w}.

The error in (
3.50
) is

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{ix\cdot\xi}\big(\psi_{2}^{\flat}(\xi^{0}+2^{-j}\xi)P_{E}^{\varepsilon}(x,\xi^{0}+2^{-j}\xi)-P_{E}^{\varepsilon}(x,\xi^{0})\big){\mathcal{F}}(\tilde{w})(\xi)\,d\xi.

We have, using
\psi_{2}^{\flat}(\xi^{0})=1,

|\psi_{2}^{\flat}(\xi^{0}+2^{-j}\xi)P_{E}^{\varepsilon}(x,\xi^{0}+2^{-j}\xi)-P_{E}^{\varepsilon}(x,\xi^{0})|\leq 2^{-j}\|\partial_{\xi}(\psi_{2}^{\flat}P_{E}^{\varepsilon}(x,\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{\xi})}|\xi|.

Thus the error in (
3.50
) is bounded, in
L^{2}(B(x^{0},1))
norm, by

2^{-j}\|\partial_{\xi}(\psi_{2}^{\flat}P_{E}^{\varepsilon})\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})}\|{\mathcal{F}}(\partial_{x}\tilde{w})\|_{L^{1}}.

By regularity of the datum and
P_{E}^{\varepsilon},
the norm
\|\partial_{\xi}(\psi_{2}^{\flat}P_{E}^{\varepsilon})\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})}
is finite (and independent of
j
). Besides, since
\psi_{1}
is smooth and compactly supported,

\|{\mathcal{F}}(\partial_{x}\tilde{w})\|_{L^{1}}\lesssim\|\psi_{2}(\xi^{0}+2^{-j}D)w))\|_{H^{1+d/2^{+}}},

where we used the bound
|\hat{f}\|_{L^{1}}\lesssim\|f\|_{H^{d/2^{+}}},
where
d/2^{+}
is any real number strictly greater than
d/2.
Since
\hat{w}
is smooth and compactly supported, the norm
\|\psi_{2}(\xi^{0}+2^{-j}D)w))\|_{H^{1+d/2^{+}}}
is finite; it is also bounded independently of
j.
Thus the error in (
3.50
) appears to be

3.12
Bounds for the forward-in-time solution operator

Recall that

In computing bounds for the solution operators

Indeed, this simplification results in an extra remainder term

Lemma 3.4
.

For some
\gamma_{E}>0
such that
\gamma_{E}\to\Re e\,\lambda_{0}
as
\delta\to 0,
some
\gamma_{H}\geq 0
with
\gamma_{E}>\gamma_{H},
for
0\leq t^{\prime}\leq t,
we have the bounds

\|S_{\star}(t^{\prime};t)\|_{L^{2}\to L^{2}}\lesssim e^{(t-t^{\prime})\gamma_{\star}},\qquad\star\in\{H,E\},

with an implicit constant which depends on
\delta.

Above,
\lambda_{0}
is the most unstable eigenvalue of
M,
as defined in Section
3.5
, and
\delta
measures the radius of the space-frequency truncation
\psi
(or
\psi^{\sharp}
) which enters in the definition of
M_{E}
and
M_{H}
(see Section
3.4
for the definition of
\psi
and Section
3.6
for the definitions of
M_{H}
and
M_{E}
).

Proof.

We could directly apply Proposition
B.1
, based on Gårding’s inequality, with
Q=-M_{H}
then
Q=-M_{E}.
However, our ellipticity assumption (Assumption
2.1
) bears on the real part of the spectrum of
iA,
by extension of
M_{E}
and
M_{H},
but condition (
B.2
) that is used in Proposition
B.1
, bears on the spectrum of
\Re e\,M_{\star},
which might happen to be far from the real part of the spectrum of
M_{\star}.
This is an issue in view of deriving an upper rate of growth
\gamma_{E}
that will be close to the lower rate of growth
\gamma_{E}^{-}
(see Lemma
3.7
).

We overcome this problem by approximately diagonalizing
M_{\star}.
This is reminiscent of the procedure carried out in Section 4 of
[
12
]
.

The goal is to derive an upper bound for
u_{\star}(t):=S_{\star}(t^{\prime};t)u_{0},
given
u_{0}\in L^{2}.
By definition,
u_{\star}
solves

\partial_{t}u_{\star}+{\rm op}_{j}(M_{\star})u_{\star}=0,\qquad u(t^{\prime})=u_{0}.

Let
K_{0}
be a matrix of a change of basis to upper triangular form for
M_{\star}(0,x^{0},\xi^{0}).
Let
K_{\delta}
be the invertible diagonal matrix with diagonal equal to
(1,\delta,\delta^{2},\dots,\delta^{N}).
Then,

\tilde{M}_{\star}(x^{0},\xi^{0}):=K_{\delta}^{-1}K_{0}^{-1}M_{\star}(0,x^{0},\xi^{0})K_{0}K_{\delta}

is upper triangular, with entries above the diagonal which are
O(\delta).
The support of the space-frequency
\psi^{\sharp}
which enters in
M_{\star}
has size
O(\delta).
Since
M_{\star}
has Hölder regularity in
(x,\xi),
the symbol

\tilde{M}_{\star}(x,\xi):=K_{\delta}^{-1}K_{0}^{-1}M_{\star}(0,x,\xi)K_{0}K_{\delta}

is at a distance
O(\delta^{\theta})
of
\tilde{M}_{\star}(x^{0},\xi^{0}).
We let

\tilde{u}_{\star}=K_{\delta}^{-1}K_{0}^{-1}u_{\star}.
Then
\tilde{u}_{\star}
solves

(3.67)

\partial_{t}\tilde{u}_{\star}+{\rm op}_{j}(\tilde{M}_{\star})\tilde{u}_{\star}=0.

By (
3.28
), the eigenvalues of
\Re e\,\tilde{M}_{\star}(x,\xi)
differ from the eigenvalues of
\Re e\,\tilde{M}_{\star}(x^{0},\xi^{0})
at most by
O(\delta^{1/N}).
Now the key is that, since
\tilde{M}_{\star}(x^{0},\xi^{0})
is upper triangular with entries above the diagonal that are
O(\delta),
the spectrum of
\Re e\,\tilde{M}_{\star}(x^{0},\xi^{0})
is differs from the real part of the spectrum of
M_{\star}(x^{0},\xi^{0})
by
O(\delta^{1/N})
at most.

Thus the spectrum of
\Re e\,\tilde{M}_{\star}(x,\xi)
differs from the real part of the spectrum of
M_{\star}(x^{0},\xi^{0})
by
O(\delta^{\min(\theta,1/N)})
at most. The spectra of
M_{E}(x^{0},\xi^{0})
and
M_{H}(x^{0},\xi^{0})
are described in Section
3.5
: the unique eigenvalue of
M_{E}(x^{0},\xi^{0})
is
\lambda_{0}
(see (
3.26
)), and the eigenvalues of
M_{H}(x^{0},\xi^{0})
have real part strictly smaller than
\Re e\,\lambda_{0}
(see (
3.27
)).

Thus

\Re e\,\tilde{M}_{\star}(x,\xi)\leq\gamma_{E},\qquad\mbox{for all $(x,\xi),$ with $\gamma_{E}\to\Re e\,\lambda_{0}$ as $\delta\to 0.$}

By Proposition
B.1
, this implies

\|\tilde{u}_{\star}(t)\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim e^{(t-t^{\prime})\gamma_{E}},

the small error in the growth rate from Proposition
B.1
being incorporated into the constant. A corresponding bound for
u_{\star}=S_{\star}(t^{\prime};t)u_{0}
follows.

3.13
Bounds for the iterated forward-in-time solution operators

The iterated solution operators
S_{\star k}
are defined in (
3.62
) in Section
3.10
. They involve the remainders
G_{\star},
for which standard pseudo-differential bounds, and regularity of the posited solution, give the following:

Lemma 3.5
.

On the time interval of existence of the posited solution
\tilde{u},
we have the bounds

\|G_{\star}z\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\|z\|_{L^{2}},\qquad\mbox{for all $z\in L^{2}$ and $\star\in\{H,E\},$}

provided that the small parameter
\varepsilon
involved in the regularization of the eigenprojectors
P_{\star}
in Section
3.6
satisfies
\varepsilon=2^{-j/(1+\theta)}.

Proof.

The remainder
G_{\star}
is defined in (
3.54
), which we reproduce here:

G_{\star}v:=-2^{j\theta^{\prime}}\Big({\rm op}_{j}(\psi^{\flat}P_{\star}^{\varepsilon})\big({\rm op}_{j}(\psi^{\sharp}\tilde{A})-{\rm op}_{j}(\tilde{A}){\rm op}_{j}(\psi^{\sharp})\big)+R_{\star}+{\rm op}_{j}(\psi^{\flat}\partial_{t}P_{\star}^{\varepsilon})\Big)v.

The projectors
P_{\star}^{\varepsilon}
are as regular as
M
in
\xi
and
(u,\partial_{x}u)
in
x.
In particular, using notation for norms of symbols introduced in (
A.9
),
\|\psi^{\flat}P_{\star}^{\varepsilon}\|_{0,0,1+[d/2]}<\infty,
since
(u,\partial_{x}u)\in H^{s-1}\hookrightarrow L^{\infty}.
Thus by (
A.17
), for all
z\in L^{2},

\|{\rm op}(\psi^{\flat}P_{\star}^{\varepsilon})z\|_{L^{2}\to L^{2}}\lesssim\|z\|_{L^{2}}.

By regularity of
u,
Lemma
A.1
and the composition result of Section
A.5
, we have, just like in the projection step of Section
3.6
,

\|{\rm op}_{j}(\psi^{\sharp}\tilde{A})-{\rm op}_{j}(\tilde{A}){\rm op}_{j}(\psi^{\sharp})\|_{L^{2}\to L^{2}}\lesssim 2^{-j\theta}.

The term
R_{\star}
contains terms of the form
R_{\theta}(\cdot,\cdot)
which are
L^{2}\to L^{2}
bounded, uniformly in
j,
by regularity of
u
and the composition result of Section
A.5
. The other terms in
R_{\star}
involve the difference
P_{\star}^{\varepsilon}-P_{\star},
where
P^{\varepsilon}_{\star}
was defined in Section
3.6
as a regularization of the eigenprojectors
P_{\star}.
We use the results of Section
A.3
:

\|{\rm op}_{j}(\psi^{\flat}(P_{H}-P_{H}^{\varepsilon}))\|_{L^{2}\to L^{2}}\lesssim\varepsilon^{\theta}.

Since
{\rm op}_{j}(M)
and
{\rm op}_{j}(M_{\star})
are
L^{2}\to L^{2}
bounded, uniformly in
j,
by (
A.17
), this implies

\|R_{\star}\|_{L^{2}\to L^{2}}\lesssim 2^{-j\theta}+\varepsilon^{\theta}.

The last term in
G_{\star}
implies
\partial_{t}P^{\varepsilon}_{\star}.
This is where the regularization was needed. We denote, as in the proof of Lemma
3.1
,

P_{\star}(x,\xi)=\underline{P}_{\star}\big(t,x,u(2^{-j}t,x),\partial_{x}u(2^{-j}t,x),\xi\big),

so that (see Section
A.3
),

P_{\star}(t,x,\xi)=\underline{P}_{\star}(2^{-j}t,x,k_{\varepsilon}\star\tilde{u}(t,x),k_{\varepsilon}\star\partial_{x}\tilde{u}(t,x),\xi).

Then, we have

\partial_{t}P_{\star}=2^{-j}\Big(\partial_{1}\underline{P}_{\star}+\partial_{3}\underline{P}_{\star}\cdot k_{\varepsilon}\star\partial_{t}u+\partial_{4}\underline{P}_{\star}\cdot k_{\varepsilon}\star\partial_{t}\partial_{x}u\Big).

The map
\underline{P}_{\star}
is smooth in its arguments, by spectral separation and smoothness of

In view of the original equation (

), we observe that

\partial_{t}k_{\varepsilon}\star u+k_{\varepsilon}\star F=0,\quad\mbox{so that}\quad k_{\varepsilon}\star\partial_{t}\partial_{x}u=-(\partial_{x}k_{\varepsilon})\star F,

where

In particular, by definition of the regularizing kernel

(or (
A.21
)),

\|k_{\varepsilon}\star\partial_{t}\partial_{x}u\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim\varepsilon^{-1},

so that (by (
A.17

\|{\rm op}(\psi^{\flat}\partial_{t}P_{\star}^{\varepsilon})\|_{L^{2}\to L^{2}}\lesssim 2^{-j}\varepsilon^{-1}.

It remains to choose
\varepsilon
so that the terms are the same order of magnitude:
\varepsilon^{\theta}=2^{-j}\varepsilon^{-1},
leading to
\varepsilon=2^{-j/(1+\theta)},
and the

norm of

finally appears to be bounded in

since
\theta^{\prime}=\theta/(1+\theta).

∎

O(\delta^{1/N}).
Thus

\Re e\,\tilde{M}_{E}(x,\xi)\geq\gamma_{E}^{-},\qquad\mbox{for all $(x,\xi)$ on the support of $\psi,$}

for some
\gamma_{E}^{-}
such that
\gamma_{E}^{-}\to\Re e\,\lambda_{0}
as
\delta\to 0.
Proposition
B.1
then provides a lower bound for the solution
\tilde{u}_{E}
to (
3.67
):

\|{\rm op}_{j}(\tilde{\psi})\tilde{u}_{E}(0)\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim e^{-t\gamma_{E}^{-}}\|\tilde{u}_{E}(t)\|_{L^{2}},

for
\tilde{u}_{\star}(0)
such that
{\rm op}_{j}(\tilde{\psi})\tilde{u}_{\star}(0)\neq 0,
and for
t\leq t_{\star}.

This translates into the announced bound for
S_{E},
up to
\delta
-dependent implicit constants.
∎

Corollary 3.8
.

We have, so long as
t\leq t_{\star},
with
t_{\star}
defined in (
B.6
), the bound

\|{\rm op}_{j}(\psi^{\flat}\big(v_{E}(0)-S_{E}(t;0)v^{f}_{Ek}(t)\big)\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim 2^{-j(\sigma+\theta^{\prime})}e^{t(\gamma_{E}-\gamma_{E}^{-})},

where
\theta^{\prime}=\theta/(1+\theta).

Proof.

Based on the definition of
S^{f}_{Ek}
in (
3.63
), we have

\displaystyle v_{E}(0)

\displaystyle-S_{E}(t;0)v^{f}_{Ek}(t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{1\leq k^{\prime}\leq k}2^{-jk^{\prime}\theta^{\prime}}S_{E}(t;0)\int_{D_{k^{\prime}}(t)}S_{Ek^{\prime}}(T_{k^{\prime}};t)\big(S_{H}(0;t_{k^{\prime}})v_{H}(0)+S_{E}(0;t_{k^{\prime}})v_{E}(0)\big)\,dT_{k^{\prime}}.

For a bound of
{\rm op}_{j}(\psi^{\flat})S_{E}(t;0)S_{Ek^{\prime}}(T_{k^{\prime}};t)S_{E}(0;t_{k^{\prime}}),
we use Lemma
3.4
, Corollary
3.6
and Lemma
3.7
:

\|{\rm op}_{j}(\psi^{\flat})S_{E}(t;0)S_{Ek^{\prime}}(T_{k^{\prime}};t)S_{E}(0;t_{k^{\prime}})\|_{L^{2}\to L^{2}}\lesssim e^{\gamma_{E}t_{k^{\prime}}}e^{\gamma_{E}(t-t_{k^{\prime}})}e^{-\gamma_{E}^{-}t}=e^{t(\gamma_{E}-\gamma_{E}^{-})}.

Since
v_{\star}(0)
is essentially the
j
th dyadic block of an
H^{\sigma}
datum (up to a projection), we have
\|v_{\star}(0)\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim 2^{-j\sigma}.
Integrating a constant over
D_{k}(t),
with

we find a prefactor that is bounded by a constant times

which can be made less than one by choosing

large enough.
∎

3.15
Bound for the poorly localized remainder terms

We turn to the “out” terms
G_{\star}^{out}
defined in (
3.55
), which we reproduce here:

\displaystyle G_{\star}^{out}

\displaystyle:=-{\rm op}_{j}(\psi^{\flat}P_{\star}^{\varepsilon})\Big(\tilde{R}^{para}_{j}+\Big({\rm op}_{j}(\tilde{A})\big({\rm op}_{j}(\psi^{\sharp}){\rm op}_{j}(\psi)-{\rm op}_{j}(\psi)\big)+\big[{\rm op}_{j}(\psi),{\rm op}_{j}(\tilde{A})\big]\Big)\tilde{u}.

Unlike the “in” remainder terms handled in Lemma
3.5
, the “out” terms above require sharp bounds. The third term in
G_{\star}^{out}
is bounded as follows:

Lemma 3.9
.

On a time interval of existence of the posited solution
\tilde{u},
we have the bound

\|{\rm op}_{j}(\psi^{\flat}P_{\star}^{\varepsilon})\big[{\rm op}_{j}(\psi),{\rm op}_{j}(\tilde{A})\big]\tilde{u}\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim 2^{-j(2s-1-d/2)}.

Note that by the composition result of Section
A.5
, and the fact that
\psi^{\flat}\prec\psi,
the
L^{2}\to L^{2}
norm of the operator
{\rm op}_{j}(\psi^{\flat}P_{\star}^{\varepsilon})\big[{\rm op}_{j}(\psi),{\rm op}_{j}(\tilde{A})\big]
is
O(2^{-j(s-1-d/2)}).
The issue here is that
\tilde{u}
is not localized in frequency.
The proof below gives a description of that operator which shows that, in effect, only frequencies
\sim 2^{j}
of
\tilde{u}
come into play.

Proof.

Recall that
\tilde{A}
is defined in (
3.12
). It has the form

\tilde{A}=A_{1}\cdot\xi+2^{-j}A_{0},\qquad A_{0},A_{1}\in H^{s-1}.

We may focus on the
A_{1}
term, since
A_{0}
has the same regularity and is smaller. By definition of para-differential operators in semiclassical quantization (
A.15
),

{\rm op}_{j}(A_{1}\cdot\xi)=H_{j}^{-1}{\rm op}(h_{j}A_{1}\cdot\xi)H_{j},

and
by definition of the admissible cut-off (
A.11
),

H_{j}^{-1}{\rm op}(h_{j}A_{1}\cdot\xi)H_{j}=\sum_{k\geq 0}H_{j}^{-1}\Big((\phi(2^{-k+N_{0}}D_{x})h_{j}A_{1})\cdot\bar{\phi}_{k}(D_{x})\Big)H_{j},\qquad\bar{\phi}_{k}(\xi):=\xi\phi_{k}(\langle\xi\rangle).

We observe the identity

(3.68)

\chi(D_{x})(h_{j}f)=h_{j}(\chi(2^{-j}D_{x})f),

for any Fourier multiplier
\chi(D_{x})
and any Sobolev map
f.

We have, for any maps
f,\chi,v:

H_{j}^{-1}(f\chi(D_{x})v)=(H_{j}^{-1}f)(h_{j}^{-1}\chi(D_{x})v),

since
f
acts on
\chi(D_{x})v
by multiplication. Thus

H_{j}^{-1}\Big((\phi(2^{-k+N_{0}}D_{x})h_{j}A_{1})\bar{\phi}_{k}(D_{x})\Big)H_{j}v=h_{j}^{-1}\Big(\phi(2^{-k+N_{0}}D_{x})h_{j}A_{1}\Big)h_{j}^{-1}\Big(\bar{\phi}_{k}(D_{x})(h_{j}v)\Big).

As a consequence, by (
3.68
),

H_{j}^{-1}\Big((\phi(2^{-k+N_{0}}D_{x})h_{j}A_{1})\bar{\phi}_{k}(D_{x})\Big)H_{j}v=(\phi(2^{-k-j+N_{0}}D_{x})A_{1})(\bar{\phi}_{k}(2^{-j}D_{x})v).

Recall (
3.15
) that
\psi(x,\xi)=\psi_{1}(x)\psi_{2}(\xi).
We use Proposition
A.3
:

(3.69)

\|\mathrm{pdo}_{j}(\psi)-{\rm op}_{j}(\psi)\|_{L^{2}\to L^{2}}\lesssim 2^{-j(s^{\prime\prime}-d)},

for any
s^{\prime\prime}>0,
since
\psi_{j}\in C^{\infty}_{c}.
Thus we may replace
{\rm op}_{j}(\psi)
by
\mathrm{pdo}_{j}(\psi)
in the commutator under scrutiny, and

\mathrm{pdo}_{j}(\psi)=H_{j}^{-1}h_{j}\psi_{1}\psi_{2}(D_{x})H_{j}.

We have, just like above,

H_{j}^{-1}h_{j}\psi_{1}\psi_{2}(D_{x})H_{j}=\psi_{1}\psi_{2}(2^{-j}D_{x}).

The focus is on

[{\rm op}_{j}(\psi),{\rm op}_{j}(A_{1}\cdot\xi)]=\sum_{k\geq 0}\Gamma_{k},

with notation

\Gamma_{k}:=\psi_{1}\psi_{2}(2^{-j}D_{x})A_{1}^{j+k}\bar{\phi}_{k}(2^{-j}D_{x})-A_{1}^{j+k}\bar{\phi}_{k}(2^{-j}D_{x})\psi_{1}\psi_{2}(2^{-j}D_{x}).

up to a very small term described in (
3.69
), with notation

(3.70)

A_{1}^{j+k}=\phi_{0}(2^{-k-j+N_{0}}D_{x})A_{1}.

We observe that

\Gamma_{k}=\psi_{1}[\psi_{2}(2^{-j}D_{x}),A_{1}^{j+k}]\bar{\phi}_{k}(2^{-j}D_{x})-A_{1}^{j+k}[\bar{\phi}_{k}(2^{-j}D_{x}),\psi_{1}]\psi_{2}(2^{-j}D_{x}).

We now take into account the truncation and projection operator
{\rm op}_{j}(\psi^{\flat}P_{\star}\varepsilon^{)}
to the left:

{\rm op}_{j}(\psi^{\flat}P_{\star}^{\varepsilon})=H_{j}^{-1}{\rm op}\big(h_{j}(\psi^{\flat}_{1}\psi^{\flat}_{2}P_{\star}^{\varepsilon})\big)H_{j}.

Since
\psi_{2}^{\flat}
depends only on
\xi,

{\rm op}\big(h_{j}(\psi^{\flat}_{1}\psi^{\flat}_{2}P_{\star}^{\varepsilon})\big)={\rm op}\big(h_{j}(\psi^{\flat}_{1}P_{\star}^{\varepsilon})\big)\psi_{2}^{\flat}(D_{x}).

Thus

\displaystyle{\rm op}_{j}(\psi^{\flat}P_{\star}^{\varepsilon})

\displaystyle=h_{j}^{-1}{\rm op}\big(h_{j}(\psi^{\flat}_{1}P_{\star}^{\varepsilon}))h_{j}\psi_{2}^{\flat}(2^{-j}D_{x})

\displaystyle=\sum_{k^{\prime}\geq 0}h_{j}^{-1}\mathrm{pdo}\Big(\phi(2^{-k^{\prime}+N_{0}}D_{x})(h_{j}(\psi_{1}^{\flat}P_{\star}^{\varepsilon}))\Big)\phi_{k}(\langle D_{x}\rangle)h_{j}\psi_{2}^{\flat}(2^{-j}D_{x})

\displaystyle=\sum_{k^{\prime}\geq 0}h_{j}^{-1}\mathrm{pdo}\Big(\phi(2^{-k^{\prime}+N_{0}}D_{x})(h_{j}(\psi_{1}^{\flat}P_{\star}\varepsilon^{)})\Big)h_{j}\phi_{k}(2^{-j}\langle D_{x}\rangle)\psi_{2}^{\flat}(2^{-j}D_{x}).

Now recall that in the Littlewood-Paley decomposition
(\phi_{k})_{k\geq 0},
only
\phi_{1}
is supported in a neighborhood of the unit sphere. Since
\psi_{2}^{\flat}
is supported near
\xi^{0}\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1},
this means that in the above sum, only
k^{\prime}=1
subsists, and, if the support of
\psi_{2}^{\flat}
is chosen small enough, then
\psi_{2}^{\flat}\phi_{1}\equiv\psi_{2}^{\flat}.
So

(3.71)

\displaystyle{\rm op}_{j}(\psi^{\flat}P_{\star}^{\varepsilon})

\displaystyle=h_{j}^{-1}\mathrm{pdo}\Big(\phi(2^{-1+N_{0}}D_{x})(h_{j}(\psi_{1}^{\flat}P_{\star}^{\varepsilon}))\Big)h_{j}\psi_{2}^{\flat}(2^{-j}D_{x})

\displaystyle=:Q_{\star}\psi_{2}^{\flat}(2^{-j}D_{x}).

The full operator under study now looks like

(3.72)

\sum_{k\geq 0}Q_{\star}\psi_{2}^{\flat}(2^{-j}D_{x})\Gamma_{k}.

Consider

\displaystyle\psi_{2}^{\flat}(2^{-j}D_{x})\Gamma_{k}

\displaystyle=:\sum_{1\leq\ell\leq 3}\Gamma_{k,\ell},

with notation

\displaystyle\Gamma_{k,1}

\displaystyle:=[\psi_{2}^{\flat}(2^{-j}D_{x}),\psi_{1}][\psi_{2}(2^{-j}D_{x}),A_{1}^{j+k}]\bar{\phi}_{k}(2^{-j}D_{x}),

\displaystyle\Gamma_{k,2}

\displaystyle:=\psi_{1}\psi_{2}^{\flat}(2^{-j}D_{x})[\psi_{2}(2^{-j}D_{x}),A_{1}^{j+k}]\bar{\phi}_{k}(2^{-j}D_{x})

\displaystyle\Gamma_{k,3}

\displaystyle:=\psi_{2}^{\flat}(2^{-j}D_{x})A_{1}^{j+k}[\bar{\phi}_{k}(2^{-j}D_{x}),\psi_{1}]\psi_{2}(2^{-j}D_{x}).

We apply the operator (
3.72
) to
\tilde{u}\in H^{s},
where
\tilde{u}(t,x)=u(2^{-j}t,x).
Once we verify the bound

(3.73)

\|Q_{\star}\|_{L^{2}\to L^{2}}\lesssim 1,

with
Q_{\star}
defined in (
3.71
),
it will remain to show

(3.74)

\sum_{k\geq 0}\|\psi_{2}^{\flat}(2^{-j}D_{x})\Gamma_{k,\ell}\tilde{u}\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim 2^{-j(2s-1-d/2)},\qquad\mbox{for all $\ell\in\{1,2,3\}.$}

We temporarily admit (
3.73
) and prove (
3.74
).

Consider the operator
\psi_{2}^{\flat}(2^{-j}D_{x})[\psi_{2}(2^{-j}D_{x}),A_{1}^{j+k}]
in
\Gamma_{k,2}.
We observe that

\psi_{2}^{\flat}\partial_{\xi}^{\alpha}\psi_{2}\equiv 0,\qquad\mbox{for all $|\alpha|>0,$}

since
\psi_{2}^{\flat}\prec\psi_{2}
(see (
3.20
)).
Thus, by Lemma
A.5
,

\psi_{2}^{\flat}(2^{-j}D_{x})[\psi_{2}(2^{-j}D_{x}),A_{1}^{j+k}]=\psi_{2}^{\flat}(2^{-j}D_{x})\tilde{R}_{s-1-d/2}(\psi_{2}(2^{-j}\cdot),A_{1}^{j+k}),

where we used notation
\tilde{R}
from the proof of Lemma
A.5
. Since
A_{1}\in H^{s-1},
we know from Lemma
A.5
that the
L^{2}\to L^{2}
operator norm of
\tilde{R}_{s-1-d/2}(\psi_{2}(2^{-j}\cdot),A_{1}^{j+k})
is
O(2^{-j(s-1-d/2)}).

Thus

\displaystyle\|\Gamma_{k,2}\tilde{u}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\|\psi_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\psi_{2}^{\flat}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\tilde{R}_{s-1-d/2}(\psi_{2}(2^{-j}\cdot),A_{1}^{j+k})\|_{L^{2}\to L^{2}}\|\bar{\phi}_{k}(2^{-j}D_{x})\tilde{u}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{-j(s-1-d/2)}\|\bar{\phi}_{k}(2^{-j}D_{x})\tilde{u}\|_{L^{2}}.

The support of
\bar{\phi}_{k}(2^{-j}\cdot)
is an annulus with frequencies
\sim 2^{j+k}.
Since
\tilde{u}\in H^{s},
this implies

\|\bar{\phi}_{k}(2^{-j}D_{x})\tilde{u}\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim 2^{k-(j+k)s},

the
2^{k}
factor coming from the
\xi
factor in
\bar{\phi}_{k},
itself a trace of the fact that the symbol
\tilde{A}
is order one. We can sum over
k
and find

\sum_{k\geq 0}\|\psi_{2}^{\flat}(2^{-j}D_{x})\Gamma_{k,2}\tilde{u}\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim 2^{-j(2s-1-d/2)}\sum_{k\geq 0}2^{(1-s)k}\lesssim 2^{-j(2s-1-d/2)},

which proves (
3.74
) in the case
\ell=2.

Consider now
\Gamma_{k,1}.
The commutator
[\psi_{2}^{\flat}(2^{-j}D_{x},\psi_{1}]
involves only smooth commutator functions. Thus we can push the expansion in Lemma
A.5
up to an arbitrarily high order. In particular, the
L^{2}\to L^{2}
norm of the remainder in Lemma
A.5
is smaller than
2^{-j(s-1-d/2)}
if the expansion is pushed to a sufficiently high order. Since
u\in H^{s},
the operator
[\psi_{2}(2^{-j}D_{x}),A_{1}^{j+k}],
where
A_{1}^{j+k}
depends on
(u,\partial_{x}u),
is
L^{2}\to L^{2}
bounded. Thus it remains to handle the first terms in the expansion of the commutator
[\psi_{2}^{\flat}(2^{-j}D_{x},\psi_{1}].
Those terms have the form
(\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\psi_{1})(\partial_{\xi}^{\alpha}\psi_{2}^{\flat})(2^{-j}D_{x}),
and we can now apply to the corresponding terms in
\Gamma_{k,1}
the arguments that we used for
\Gamma_{k,2}.

We turn to
\Gamma_{k,3}.
First we observe that the supports of
\bar{\phi}_{k}
and
\psi_{2}
are disjoint unless
k=1.
Thus only
\Gamma_{1,3}
remains. We have

\Gamma_{1,3}u=\psi_{2}^{\flat}(2^{-j}D_{x})(A_{1}^{j+1}f)=(\psi_{2}^{\flat}(2^{-j}D_{x})A_{1}^{j+1})f+\tilde{\Gamma}_{1,3},

with notation
f:=[\bar{\phi}_{1}(2^{-j}D_{x}),\psi_{1}]\psi_{2}(2^{-j}D_{x})\tilde{u},
and

\tilde{\Gamma}_{1,3}u=\psi_{2}^{\flat}(2^{-j}D_{x})(A_{1}^{j+1}f)-(\psi_{2}^{\flat}(2^{-j}D_{x})A_{1}^{j+1})f.

We bound

\|(\psi_{2}^{\flat}(2^{-j}D_{x})A_{1}^{j+1})f\|_{L^{2}}\leq\|\psi_{2}^{\flat}((2^{-j}D_{x})A_{1}^{j+1}\|_{L^{2}}\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}.

Since
\bar{\phi}_{1}(2^{-j}D_{x})
and
\psi_{1}
are
L^{\infty}\to L^{\infty}
bounded, uniformly in
j,
we have

\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim\|\psi_{2}(2^{-j}D_{x})\tilde{u}\|_{L^{\infty}},

and by Bernstein’s inequality (or the Sobolev embedding (
A.5
))

\|\psi_{2}(2^{-j}D_{x})u\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim 2^{jd/2}\|\psi_{2}(2^{-j}D_{x})\tilde{u}\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim 2^{-j(s-d/2)},

the last inequality by the posited regularity of
\tilde{u}.
Since
A_{1}\in H^{s-1},
we have

\|\psi_{2}^{\flat}(2^{-j}D_{x})A_{1}^{j+1}\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim 2^{-j(s-1)}.

Thus we proved

\|(\psi_{2}^{\flat}(2^{-j}D_{x})A_{1}^{j+1})f\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim 2^{-j(2s-1-d/2)},

and we turn to
\tilde{\Gamma}_{1,3}\tilde{u}:

\|\tilde{\Gamma}_{1,3}\tilde{u}\|_{L^{2}}=\left\|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(\psi_{2}^{\flat}(2^{-j}(\xi)-\psi_{2}^{\flat}(2^{-j}(\xi-\xi^{\prime}))){\mathcal{F}}(A_{1}^{j+1})(\xi-\xi^{\prime})\hat{f}(\xi^{\prime})\,d\xi^{\prime}\right\|_{L^{2}}.

We Taylor expand the frequency cut-off:

\displaystyle\psi_{2}^{\flat}(2^{-j}\xi)-\psi_{2}^{\flat}(2^{-j}(\xi-\xi^{\prime}))

\displaystyle=\sum_{1\leq|\alpha|\leq m}(\partial_{\xi}^{\alpha}\psi_{2}^{\flat})(2^{-j}(\xi-\xi^{\prime}))\cdot(-2^{-j}\xi^{\prime})^{\alpha}

\displaystyle+2^{-j(m+1)}R(\psi_{2}^{\flat}),

for any
m\in\mathbb{N},
where
R(\psi_{2}^{\flat})
is the remainder,
using the same notational convention as in the proof of Lemma
A.5
. The term of index
\alpha
(with
|\alpha|>0
) in the above expansion has a contribution to the
L^{2}
norm of
\tilde{\Gamma}_{1,3}u
that is controlled by

\|(\partial_{\xi}^{\alpha}\psi_{2}^{\flat})(2^{-j}D_{x})A_{1}^{1+j}\|_{L^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|2^{-j}\xi|^{|\alpha|}|\hat{f}(\xi)|\,d\xi.

Since
|\alpha|>0,
we have, by regularity of
A_{1},

\|(\partial_{\xi}^{\alpha}\psi_{2}^{\flat})(2^{-j}D_{x})A_{1}^{1+j}\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim 2^{-j(s-1)}.

Besides,

f=\bar{\phi}_{1}(2^{-j}D_{x})(\psi_{1}\psi_{2}(2^{-j}D_{x})\tilde{u})+\psi_{1}\bar{\phi}_{1}(2^{-j}D_{x})\psi_{2}(D_{x})\tilde{u})=:f_{1}+f_{2}.

Since
\xi\to|\xi|^{|\alpha|}\bar{\phi}_{1}(\xi)
is bounded,

\|(2^{-j}|\xi|)^{|\alpha|}\hat{f}_{1}\|_{L^{1}}\lesssim\|\hat{\psi}_{1}\star(\psi_{2}(2^{-j}\cdot){\mathcal{F}}(\tilde{u}))\|_{L^{1}}\lesssim 2^{-js},

since
\hat{\psi}_{1}
belongs to the Schwartz class and
\tilde{u}\in H^{s}.
Similarly,

\|(2^{-j}|\xi|)^{|\alpha|}\hat{f}_{2}\|_{L^{1}}\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\xi-\xi^{\prime}|^{|\alpha|}|\hat{\psi}_{1}(\xi-\xi^{\prime})||2^{-j}\xi^{\prime}|^{|\alpha|}|\bar{\phi}_{1}(2^{-j}\xi^{\prime})\psi_{2}(2^{-j}\xi^{\prime}){\mathcal{F}}(\tilde{u})(\xi^{\prime})|\,d\xi^{\prime},

so that

\|(2^{-j}|\xi|)^{|\alpha|}\hat{f}_{2}\|_{L^{1}}\lesssim 2^{-js}.

We finally bound the contribution to
\tilde{\Gamma}_{1,3}
of the remainder term in the Taylor expansion of
\psi_{2}^{\flat}.
This is a term of the form

\left\|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{0}^{1}\tilde{\psi}_{2}(\xi-\xi^{\prime},t\xi^{\prime}){\mathcal{F}}(A_{1}^{j+1})(\xi-\xi^{\prime})(2^{-j}t\xi^{\prime})^{\alpha}\hat{f}(\xi^{\prime})\,d\xi^{\prime}\,dt\right\|_{L^{2}},

up to a polynomial in
t,
with
\tilde{\psi}_{2}\in L^{\infty}.
This term has the same form as the previous terms in the expansion of
\psi_{2}^{\flat},
and we may conclude that the
L^{2}
norm of
\tilde{\Gamma}_{1,3}
is controlled by
2^{-j(2s-1)}.

In a last step, we prove (
3.73
), where
Q_{\star}=h_{j}^{-1}\mathrm{pdo}\Big(\phi_{0}(2^{-1+N_{0}}D_{x})(h_{j}(\psi_{1}^{\flat}P_{\star}^{\varepsilon}))\Big)h_{j}.
We use the first bound in (
A.17
):

\|Q_{\star}\|_{L^{2}\to L^{2}}\lesssim\sup_{|\alpha|,|\beta|\leq 1+[d/2]}\|\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\partial_{\xi}^{\beta}(\phi_{0}(2^{-1+N_{0}}D_{x})(h_{j}(\psi_{1}^{\flat}P_{\star}^{\varepsilon})\|_{L^{\infty}}.

Denote
\check{\phi}
the inverse Fourier transform of
\phi_{0}(2^{-1+N_{0}}\cdot),
so that
\check{\phi}
belongs to the Schwartz space. We have

\|\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\partial_{\xi}^{\beta}(\phi_{0}(2^{-1+N_{0}}D_{x})(h_{j}(\psi_{1}^{\flat}P_{\star}^{\varepsilon})\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim\|\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\check{\phi}_{0}\|_{L^{1}}\|\psi_{1}^{\flat}\partial_{\xi}^{\beta}P_{\star}^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}}.

By regularity of
P_{\star}^{\varepsilon},
we have
\|\partial_{\xi}^{\beta}P_{\star}^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim C(\|\tilde{u},\partial_{x}\tilde{u}\|_{L^{\infty}})<\infty,
since
(\tilde{u},\partial_{x}\tilde{u})\in H^{s-1}\hookrightarrow L^{\infty},
which concludes the proof.
∎

Corollary 3.10
.

On a time interval of existence of the posited solution
\tilde{u},
we have the bound

\displaystyle{\|G^{out}_{\star k}(t)\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim 2^{-j(2s-1-d/2)}e^{t\gamma_{E}}.}

Proof.

First we bound
G_{\star}^{out}.
We saw in the proof of Lemma
3.5
that
{\rm op}_{j}(\psi^{\flat}P_{\star}^{\varepsilon})
is
L^{2}\to L^{2}
bounded, uniformly in
j.
Thus with (
3.6
) (where we note that
2^{j}\partial_{x}u_{h}=\partial_{x}\tilde{u}
) and (
3.11
), we have

\|{\rm op}_{j}(\psi^{\flat}P_{\star}^{\varepsilon})\tilde{R}^{para}_{j}\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim 2^{-j(2s-1-d/2)}.

The term
{\rm op}_{j}(\tilde{A})\big({\rm op}_{j}(\psi^{\sharp}){\rm op}_{j}(\psi)-{\rm op}_{j}(\psi)\big)\tilde{u}
remains. Here we can use the composition result (
A.34
)-(
A.35
) up to an arbitrarily high order, since the cut-offs
\psi^{\sharp}
and
\psi
are smooth. In view of
\psi\prec\psi^{\sharp},
this gives

{\rm op}_{j}(\psi^{\sharp}){\rm op}_{j}(\psi)-{\rm op}_{j}(\psi)=2^{-jk}R_{k}(\psi^{\sharp},\psi),

where
R_{k}(\psi^{\sharp},\psi)
maps
L^{2}
to
H^{-k}_{j},
with a norm that does not depend on

Thus

\|{\rm op}_{j}(\psi^{\flat}P_{\star}^{\varepsilon}){\rm op}_{j}(\tilde{A})\big({\rm op}_{j}(\psi^{\sharp}){\rm op}_{j}(\psi)-{\rm op}_{j}(\psi)\big)\tilde{u}\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim 2^{-jk},

for any

With Proposition
3.9
, we conclude that

\|G_{\star}^{out}\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim 2^{-j(2s-1-d/2)}.

With Lemma
3.6
, this implies the bound for
G_{\star k}^{out}.

∎

3.16
Endgame

We use the backward-in-time representation (
3.66
), which we reproduce here:

(3.75)

\displaystyle v_{E}(0)

\displaystyle=\big(v_{E}(0)-S_{E}(t;0)v^{f}_{Ek}(t)\big)+S_{E}(t;0)v_{E}(t)-S_{E}(t;0)G^{out}_{Ek}(t)

\displaystyle-2^{-jk\theta}S_{E}(t;0)\int_{D_{k}(t)}S_{Ek}(T_{k};t)v(t_{k})dT_{k},\qquad\mbox{for any $k\geq 1.$}

On the one hand, we have a lower bound for
{\rm op}_{j}(\tilde{\psi})
applied to the left-hand side of (
3.75
): this is Corollary
3.3
. Recall that
\tilde{\psi}
is a smooth truncation near
(x^{0},\xi^{0})
with an appropriately small support. On the other hand, we have upper bounds for the operator
{\rm op}_{j}(\tilde{\psi})
applied to the right-hand side of (
3.75
). These show that the left-hand side of (
3.75
) (the datum) is much greater than the right-hand side, yielding a contradiction. Indeed:

\bullet
by Corollary
3.3
,

\|{\rm op}_{j}(\tilde{\psi})v_{E}(0)\|_{L^{2}}\geq C2^{-j\sigma}(1+j)^{-1},

for some constant
C>0;

\bullet
by Corollary

, for

As in the proof of Theorem

).
We identify the solution

with its extension to the whole space

For condition (iv) in Assumption

to make sense, we need the second time derivative of the characteristic polynomial

) that

4.1
The spectral picture

We describe here what Assumption
2.2
entails for the spectrum of the principal symbol
A
evaluated along the posited solution
u.
We denote

(4.1)

\underline{A}(t,x,\xi):=A(t,x,u(t,x),\partial_{x}u(t,x),\xi).

The map
\underline{A}
is
C^{1}
in
t
and
C^{1,1/2}
in
x
(by assumption on
s
in Theorem
2
, Sobolev embedding, and the original system (
1.1
)) and smooth in
\xi\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}.

By condition (i) in Assumption
2.2
, the spectrum of
\underline{A}
at
t=0
is real for all
(x,\xi)\in U.

Given
(x,\xi)\in U,
consider an eigenvalue
\mu
of
\underline{A}
such that
\partial_{\lambda}P(0,x,\xi,\mu(0,x,\xi))\neq 0:
the eigenvalue
\mu(0,x,\xi)
of
\underline{A}(0,x,\xi)
is simple. As a consequence, it is as smooth as
\underline{A},
pointwise in
(t,x,\xi),
and it stays real for
(t,x^{\prime},\xi^{\prime})
in a neighborhood of
(0,x,\xi).
Indeed, the coefficients of
\underline{A}
are real, implying that the spectrum is symmetric with respect to
\mathbb{R}:
eigenvalues away from
\mathbb{R}
occur in pairs, and by continuity for eigenvalues away from
\mathbb{R}
to be observed for
t>0,
we need eigenvalues to coalesce on the real axis at
t=0.

Consider now
(x^{0},\xi^{0})\in U
given by condition (iii) in Assumption
2.2
. For any
\lambda
such that
(0,x^{0},\xi^{0},\lambda)\in{\mathcal{S}}_{U},
either
\lambda
is a simple eigenvalue, or we have a neighborhood
\Omega_{\lambda}
of
\omega=(0,x^{0},\xi^{0},\lambda)
such that the conditions (iv) hold over
\Omega_{\lambda}.
Indeed, the conditions
\partial_{t}P=0,

\partial_{\lambda}P=0
are stated to hold locally, and the inequality in condition (iv) is an open condition. The projection of
\Omega_{\lambda}
over
\mathbb{R}^{2d}_{x,\xi}
is a neighborhood of
(x^{0},\xi^{0}),
which we denote
V_{\lambda}.
The finite intersection of the
V_{\lambda}
over all
\lambda
in the spectrum at
(0,x^{0},\xi^{0})
is a neighborhood of
(x^{0},\xi^{0})
which we denote
V.

Given
(x,\xi)\in V
and an eigenvalue
\mu(0,x,\xi)
of
\underline{A}(0,x,\xi),
let
\omega=(0,x,\xi,\mu(0,x,\xi)).
If we have
\partial_{\lambda}P(0,\omega)=0,
then the conditions (iv) in Assumption
2.2
hold at
\omega.
The multiplicity of
\mu
at
(0,x,\xi)
is exactly two. Thus
\mu
belongs to a branch (in
(x,\xi)
) of eigenvalues of
\underline{A}_{|t=0}
drawn over
V
which have constant multiplicity equal to two.

Now consider the time evolution of these branches of eigenvalues, for fixed
(x,\xi)\in V:

Lemma 4.1
.

Condition
(iv)
in Assumption
2.2
implies that two branches of simple eigenvalues
\mu^{\pm}
branch out of the eigenvalue
\mu
described just above, and out of the real axis at
t=0,
such that
\mu^{\pm}
are time-differentiable at
t=0,
for all
(x,\xi)\in V,
with

(4.2)

\mu^{\pm}(0,x,\xi)=\mu(0,x,\xi),\quad\partial_{t}\Im m\,\mu^{+}(0,x,\xi)=-\partial_{t}\Im m\,\mu^{-}(0,x,\xi)\neq 0.

Besides, under the conditions bearing on
s
and
\sigma
in Theorem
2
,
the functions
\mu^{\pm}
are as regular as
\underline{A},
and
\partial_{t}\mu^{\pm}
is continuous in
(t,x)
and smooth in
\xi.

Proof.

Given
(x,\xi)\in V
and an eigenvalue
\mu(0,x,\xi)
such that
\partial_{\lambda}P(0,\omega)=0,
the eigenvalue
\mu(0,x,\xi)
has multiplicity exactly two, by the inequality in condition (iv) from Assumption
2.2
, and is separated from the other eigenvalues. Thus for
t>0,
its multiplicity is at most two, and
\mu
is an eigenvalue for a size-two matrix
B,
with real coefficients just like
\underline{A},
the same regularity as
\underline{A},
and with associated characteristic polynomial
P_{0}.
The characteristic polynomial
P
of
\underline{A}
factorizes into
P=P_{0}P_{1},
with
P_{1}(0,\omega)\neq 0.
We have

2\mu(t,x,\xi)=\mbox{tr}\,B(t,x,\xi)\pm(\det B(t,x,\xi))^{1/2},

with
\det B(0,x,\xi)=0
for all
(x,\xi)\in V.

We compute, as in Proposition 1.4 and Appendix A of
[
12
]
:

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\det B(0,x,\xi)

\displaystyle=2\mbox{tr}\,B(0)\partial_{t}\mbox{tr}\,B(0)-4\partial_{t}\det B(0)=-4\partial_{t}P_{0}(0,x,\xi)=0,

and

\displaystyle\partial_{t}^{2}\det B(0,x,\xi)

\displaystyle=2(\partial_{t}\mbox{tr}\,B(0))^{2}+2\mbox{tr}\,B(0)\partial_{t}^{2}\mbox{tr}\,B(0)-4\partial_{t}^{2}\det B(0)

\displaystyle=2(\partial_{t}\partial_{\lambda}P_{0})^{2}-2\partial_{\lambda}^{2}P_{0}\partial_{t}^{2}P_{0}(0)<0

by the inequality in condition (iv) in Assumption
2.2
. We may shrink
V
if necessary so that
\partial_{t}^{2}B(0,x,\xi)
is bounded away from zero in

Thus, given
(x,\xi)\in V,

(4.3)

\mu(t,x,\xi)=\mbox{tr}\,B(t,x,\xi)\pm t\left(\int_{0}^{1}(1-s)\partial_{t}^{2}\det B(st,x,\xi)\,ds\right)^{1/2},

where
\partial_{t}^{2}B(t,x,\xi)<0
is bounded away from zero in

if

and
V
are small enough.

Thus
\mu
branches out of the imaginary axis, is differentiable in
t
at
(0,x,\xi),
and splits into two eigenvalues
\mu^{\pm},
with
\partial_{t}\Im m\,\mu^{\pm}(0,x,\xi)\neq 0.
The functions
(x,\xi)\to\partial_{t}\mu^{\pm}(0,x,\xi)
are continuous in
x
and smooth in

by condition on
\sigma:
since
\sigma>3+d/2,
the first and second time derivatives of

hence

are continuous in

the regularity in
\xi
comes from the smoothness in
\xi
of

and (
4.3
).

For

the eigenvalues
\mu^{\pm}(t,x,\xi)
are simple hence as regular as
\underline{A}.
By condition

the time-derivative
(\partial_{t}u,\partial_{t}\partial_{x}u)
is continuous in

hence for

\partial_{t}\mu^{\pm}
is continuous in

besides, it is also smooth in

just like

∎

Thus the spectral picture is as follows: for some
(x^{0},\xi^{0})
in
U,
the eigenvalues of
\underline{A}(t,x,\xi)
for
(t,x,\xi)
near
(0,x^{0},\xi^{0})
fall into two categories:

•

real eigenvalues with multiplicity one;

•

and eigenvalues which at

and in a neighborhood

of

are multiplicity-two and real, and which as

goes from

to

all simultaneously branch out of the real axis into simple eigenvalues with non-zero (and opposite) imaginary parts which are time-differentiable, hence
O(t).

The smooth diagonalizability of
A
at

is assumed: this is condition (ii) in Assumption
2.2
. The discussion above shows that condition (iv) in Assumption
2.2
implies furthermore the smooth diagonalizability of

(defined in (

)) for small positive times. Indeed, the bifurcating eigenvalues are semi-simple at

and split into distinct eigenvalues (see (

)) at

By continuity, these eigenvalues stay distinct for small times. The non-bifurcating eigenvalues are simple at

hence stay simple for small times, by continuity. The diagonalization matrix is also time-differentiable at
t=0:

Lemma 4.2
.

If the space-frequency domain
V
and
T>0
are small enough, then associated with the bifurcation eigenvalues
\mu^{\pm}
discussed in Lemma
4.1
we can find eigenvectors

which enjoy the same regularity property in

as

Proof.

The symbol
\underline{A}
can be smoothly block diagonalized, for all times. Consider
A_{0}
a block of size two associated with a pair of bifurcating eigenvalues as in Lemma
4.1
. At
t=0,
we have
A_{0}(0,x,\xi)=\mu(0,x,\xi){\rm Id},
since
\mu(0,x,\xi)
is a double and semi-simple eigenvalue of
A_{0}.
Thus the matrix
A_{0}-\mu{\rm Id}
vanishes at

and is time-differentiable at
t=0
(recall,
\underline{A}
is
C^{1}
in
t;
the time regularity of
\mu
was established in Lemma

), with a time derivative at
t=0
which is continuous in
(t,x),
and smooth in
\xi.
We may write
A_{0}-\mu{\rm Id}=t\tilde{A}_{0}(t,x,\xi)=:t(\tilde{a}_{ij})_{1\leq i,j\leq 2}.
If we had
\tilde{a}_{12}(0,x,\xi)=0,
then the eigenvalues of
\tilde{A}_{0}
would stay real. Indeed, those are

\mu_{0}^{\pm}=\frac{1}{2}\mbox{tr}\tilde{A}_{0}\pm\frac{1}{2}\big((\tilde{a}_{11}-\tilde{a}_{22})^{2}-4\tilde{a}_{12}\tilde{a}_{21}\big)^{1/2}.

Thus
\tilde{a}_{12}(0,x,\xi)\neq 0,
and we may assume that

and

are small enough so that

is bounded away from 0 over

Then, the entries

of an eigenvector of

take the form

∎

We now diagonalize

) hold, and, for all

\|\mathrm{pdo}(a)\|_{L^{2}\to H_{j}^{-m}}\lesssim\|a\|_{m,1+[d/2],1+[d/2]},\qquad\|{\rm op}_{j}(a)\|_{L^{2}\to H_{j}^{-m}}\lesssim\|a\|_{m,0,1+[d/2]},

the implicit constant depending only on dimensions. For a proof of the statement on pseudo-differential operators in (

[
10
]
; for a proof of the statement on para-differential operators in (

), see for instance Theorem 4.3.5 in
[
4
]
. The extension to semiclassical operators follow from the definitions (

) and (
A.15
), and the property

\|H_{j}u\|_{H^{s}}=\|u\|_{j,s}.

A.3
Regularized symbols

Let
a\in C^{0,\theta}S^{m},
with
m\in\mathbb{R}
and
0<\theta<1.
We regularize
a
into
a^{\varepsilon}\in C^{\infty}S^{m},
by spatial convolution with a regularizing kernel
k_{\varepsilon}(x)=\varepsilon^{-d}k(x/\varepsilon),
with
k\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),
such that
k\geq 0
and the integral of
k
over
\mathbb{R}^{d}
is equal to one. For all
f\in C^{0,\theta},
we have

\|f-k_{\varepsilon}\star f\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C_{\theta}\varepsilon^{\theta}\|f\|_{C^{0,\theta}},\qquad C_{\theta}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|y|^{\theta}k(y)\,dy.

We apply the above bound pointwise in
\xi
to
\partial_{\xi}^{\alpha}a,
for any
\alpha,
and obtain

(A.19)

\langle\xi\rangle^{m-|\alpha|}\|\partial_{\xi}^{\alpha}(a-a^{\varepsilon})(\cdot,\xi)\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C_{\theta}\varepsilon^{\theta}\|a\|_{m,\theta,|\alpha|},

where we used notation (
A.10
) for the symbolic norm. Applying the above to all
|\alpha|\leq 1+[d/2],
we deduce by (
A.17
) the bound

(A.20)

\|{\rm op}_{j}(a-a^{\varepsilon})\|_{H_{j}^{m}\to L^{2}}\lesssim\varepsilon^{\theta}.

Spatial derivatives of
k_{\varepsilon}
bring out powers of
\varepsilon^{-1}:

(A.21)

\|\partial_{x}^{\beta}(k_{\varepsilon}\star f)\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C_{\beta}\varepsilon^{-|\beta|}\|f\|_{L^{\infty}},\qquad\mbox{for some $C_{\beta}>0.$}

By (
A.21
), norms of
a^{\varepsilon}
are large in
\varepsilon:

(A.22)

\|a^{\varepsilon}\|_{m,k,k^{\prime}}\lesssim\varepsilon^{-k},\qquad k>0.

For
a
in the form (
A.4
), a variant on the above regularization procedure is given by

(A.23)

a^{\varepsilon}(x,\xi):=\sigma(k_{\varepsilon}\star u,\xi).

By regularity of
\sigma
in
u,
the above bounds (
A.20
) and (
A.22
) hold also in this case.

Given

Remark A.4
.

The proof of Proposition

tells us a bit more. For any symbol

of order

with an adequate degree of spatial regularity, the difference

A.4.1
Proof of Proposition
A.3

We prove here Proposition
A.3
, based on the results of
[
8
]
.

First we compute

(A.26)

\sum_{0\leq j^{\prime}\leq j_{0}}\phi_{j^{\prime}}=\phi_{0}+\phi_{1}+\dots+\phi_{j_{0}}=\phi_{0}(2^{-j_{0}}\xi),\qquad\mbox{for all $j_{0}\in\mathbb{N}.$}

where
(\phi_{j})_{j\geq 0}
is the Littlewood-Paley decomposition introduced in Section
3.4
.
Next we decompose any symbol
a
into

a(x,\xi)=\sum_{\begin{smallmatrix}p\geq 0\\
q\geq 0\end{smallmatrix}}\phi_{p}(D_{x})a(\cdot,\xi)\phi_{q}(\xi),

and

a(x,\xi)=\sum_{\begin{smallmatrix}|p-q|<N_{0}\end{smallmatrix}}\phi_{p}(D_{x})a(\cdot,\xi)\phi_{q}(\xi)+\sum_{\begin{smallmatrix}|p-q|\geq N_{0}\end{smallmatrix}}\phi_{p}(D_{x})a(\cdot,\xi)\phi_{q}(\xi).

The sum over
\{(p,q)\in\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{N},\quad|p-q|\geq N_{0}\}
decomposes into a sum over
p\geq N_{0}
with
q\leq p-N_{0}
and the symmetrical sum:

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{smallmatrix}|p-q|\geq N_{0}\end{smallmatrix}}\phi_{p}(D_{x})a(\cdot,\xi)\phi_{q}(\xi)=\sum_{\begin{smallmatrix}p\geq N_{0}\\
q\leq p-N_{0}\end{smallmatrix}}\phi_{p}(D_{x})a(\cdot,\xi)\phi_{q}(\xi)+\sum_{\begin{smallmatrix}q\geq N_{0}\\
p\leq q-N_{0}\end{smallmatrix}}\phi_{p}(D_{x})a(\cdot,\xi)\phi_{q}(\xi).

We use (
A.26
) in order to express the second term in the above right-hand side:

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{smallmatrix}q\geq N_{0}\\
p\leq q-N_{0}\end{smallmatrix}}\phi_{p}(D_{x})a(\cdot,\xi)\phi_{q}(\xi)

\displaystyle=\sum_{q\geq N_{0}}\Big(\sum_{0\leq p\leq q-N_{0}}\phi_{p}(D_{x})\Big)a(\cdot,\xi)\phi_{q}(\xi)

\displaystyle=\sum_{q\geq N_{0}}\phi_{0}(2^{-q+N_{0}}D_{x})a(\cdot,\xi)\phi_{q}(\xi).

Thus by definition of the admissible cut-off
\phi_{adm}:

\sum_{\begin{smallmatrix}q\geq N_{0}\\
p\leq q-N_{0}\end{smallmatrix}}\phi_{p}(D_{x})a(\cdot,\xi)\phi_{q}(\xi)=\phi_{adm}(D,\cdot)a(x,\xi)-\sum_{0\leq q\leq N_{0}-1}\phi_{0}(2^{-q+N_{0}}D_{x})a(\cdot,\xi)\phi_{q}(\xi).

We have symmetrically

\sum_{\begin{smallmatrix}p\geq N_{0}\\
q\leq p-N_{0}\end{smallmatrix}}\phi_{p}(D_{x})a(\cdot,\xi)\phi_{q}(\xi)=\sum_{p\geq 0}\phi_{p}(D_{x})a(\cdot,\xi)\phi_{0}(2^{-p+N_{0}}\xi)-\sum_{0\leq p\leq N_{0}-1}\phi_{p}(D_{x})a(\cdot,\xi)\phi_{0}(2^{-p+N_{0}}\xi).

In the end we obtain the decomposition

(A.27)

a(x,\xi)=\phi_{adm}(D_{x},\xi)a(\cdot,\xi)(x)+a_{R}(x,\xi)+a_{2}(x,\xi)+a_{LF}(x,\xi),

with notation

(A.28)

\left\{\begin{aligned} a_{R}(x,\xi)&:=\sum_{\begin{smallmatrix}|p-q|<N_{0}\end{smallmatrix}}\phi_{p}(D_{x})a(\cdot,\xi)\phi_{q}(\xi),\\
a_{2}(x,\xi)&:=\sum_{p\geq 0}\phi_{p}(D_{x})a(\cdot,\xi)\phi_{0}(2^{-p+N_{0}}\xi),\\
a_{LF}(x,\xi)&:=-\sum_{0\leq q\leq N_{0}-1}\phi_{0}(2^{-q+N_{0}}D_{x})a(\cdot,\xi)\phi_{q}(\xi)-\sum_{0\leq p\leq N_{0}-1}\phi_{p}(D_{x})a(\cdot,\xi)\phi_{0}(2^{-p+N_{0}}\xi).\end{aligned}\right.

A.5
Operator composition

Given
m_{1},m_{2}\in\mathbb{R},
given
r>0
, given
a_{1}\in C^{r}S^{m_{1}},

a_{2}\in C^{r}S^{m_{2}}
if

or given
a_{1}\in C^{[r],r-[r]}S^{m_{1}},

a_{2}\in C^{[r],r-[r]}S^{m_{2}}
if

we have

(A.34)

{\rm op}_{j}(a_{1}){\rm op}_{j}(a_{2})=\sum_{0\leq k<r}2^{-jk}\frac{(-i)^{|\alpha|}}{\alpha!}{\rm op}_{j}\left(\partial_{\xi}^{\alpha}a_{1}\partial_{x}^{\alpha}a_{2}\right)+2^{-jr}R_{r}(a_{1},a_{2}),

with a remainder
R_{r}(a_{1},a_{2})

which maps
H^{m_{1}+m_{2}-r}
to
L^{2},
with norm

(A.35)

\big\|R_{r}(a_{1},a_{2})v\big\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\Big(\|a_{1}\|_{m_{1},0,m(d,r)}\|a_{2}\|_{m_{2},r,d}+\|a_{1}\|_{m_{1},r,m(d,r)}\|a_{2}\|_{m_{2},0,d}\Big)\|v\|_{j,m_{1}+m_{2}-r},

for some
m(d,r)
depending on
d
and
r.

For a proof of (
A.34
)-(
A.35
), see for instance Theorem 6.1.4 of
[
4
]
.

The above fractional-order composition result has a simple proof in the particular case of a Fourier multiplier and a Sobolev map:

.

s>1+d/2,
given a Fourier multiplier
\chi(D_{x})
with
|\cdot|^{s-1-d/2}{\mathcal{F}}^{-1}\chi\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),

\displaystyle(\partial_{x}^{\alpha}f)(x)(\partial_{\xi}^{\alpha}\chi)(D_{x})v\Big\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f\|_{H^{s-1}}\big\||\cdot|^{s-1-d/2}{\mathcal{F}}^{-1}\chi\big\|_{L^{1}}\|v\|_{L^{2}},

In particular, under the above assumptions, we have

\displaystyle\Big\|[\chi(2^{-j}D_{x},f]v

\displaystyle-\sum_{1\leq\alpha\leq[\theta]-1}2^{-j|\alpha|}\frac{(-i)^{|\alpha|}}{|\alpha|!}(\partial_{x}^{\alpha}f)(x)(\partial_{\xi}^{\alpha}\chi)(2^{-j}D_{x})v\Big\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{-j\theta}\sup_{|\alpha|=[\theta]}\|\partial_{x}^{\alpha}f\|_{0,\theta-[\theta]}\big\||\cdot|^{\theta}{\mathcal{F}}^{-1}\chi\big\|_{L^{1}}\|v\|_{L^{2}}.

The Hölder norm in the upper bound is finite by the Sobolev embedding (
A.38
).

In the statement of Lemma
A.5

.

and with a Taylor expansion of

is bounded in both its arguments, with

\|\tilde{f}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\sup_{\begin{smallmatrix}|\alpha|=[\theta]\\
x\neq y\end{smallmatrix}}\frac{|\partial_{x}^{\alpha}f(x)-\partial_{x}^{\alpha}f(y)|}{|x-y|^{\theta-[\theta]}}=:\sup_{|\alpha|=[\theta]}\|\partial_{x}^{\alpha}f\|_{0,\theta-[\theta]}.

In particular, given
\chi
compactly supported and
f\in H^{s-1},
we find

\|\tilde{R}_{\theta}(\chi,f)v\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\||\cdot|^{\theta}{\mathcal{F}}^{-1}\chi\|_{L^{1}}\sup_{|\alpha|=[\theta]}\|\partial_{x}^{\alpha}f\|_{0,\theta-[\theta]}\|v\|_{L^{2}},

where the upper bound is finite by (
A.38
).

In semiclassical quantization, that is with
\chi(2^{-j}D_{x})
in place of
\chi(D_{x}),
it suffices to observe that

\big\||\cdot|^{\theta}{\mathcal{F}}^{-1}(\chi(2^{-j}\cdot))\big\|_{L^{1}}=2^{-j\theta}\big\||\cdot|^{\theta}{\mathcal{F}}^{-1}(\cdot))\big\|_{L^{1}}.

∎

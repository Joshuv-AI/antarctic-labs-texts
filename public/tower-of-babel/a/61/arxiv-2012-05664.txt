Construction of eigenfunctions for the elliptic Ruijsenaars difference operators
Edwin Langmann, Masatoshi Noumi and Junichi Shiraishi

Abstract
We present a perturbative construction of two kinds of eigenfunctions of the commuting family of difference operators defining the elliptic Ruijsenaars system. The first kind corresponds to elliptic deformations of the Macdonald polynomials, and the second kind generalizes asymptotically free eigenfunctions previously constructed in the trigonometric case. We obtain these eigenfunctions as infinite series which, as we show, converge in suitable domains of the variables and parameters. Our results imply that, for the domain where the elliptic Ruijsenaars operators define a relativistic quantum mechanical system, the elliptic deformations of the Macdonald polynomials provide a family of orthogonal functions with respect to the pertinent scalar product. Keywords : Ruijsenaars difference operators, perturbative eigenfunctions, elliptic deformation of Macdonald polynomials 2010 Mathematics Subject Classification : 81Q80; 33E30, 33D67

Note that the joint eigenfunction equations (

We also remark that

In the context of

We expect that the stationary and non-stationary Ruijsenaars functions should inherit various symmetry properties

We give below two results regarding symmetries of the Ruijsenaars function.

Theorem 2.8

The normalized Ruijsenaars function

(2.53)

The eigenvalues

\varepsilon^{(r)}(s_{2},\ldots,s_{n},s_{1};p)=\varepsilon^{(r)}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{n};p)\quad(r=1,\ldots,n).

(2.54)

Theorem 2.9

The normalized Ruijsenaars function

(2.56)

If we define

\psi(x;s;p|q,t)=\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq n}\frac{\Gamma(tx_{j}/x_{i};p,q)}{\Gamma(x_{j}/x_{i};p,q)}\cdot f(x;s;p|q,t),

(2.57)

Theorem

3
Integrable transform to the physical domain: a prototype

In this section, we deal with joint eigenfunctions of
the Macdonald-Ruijsenaars operators in the trigonometric case,
and introduce an integral transform that maps
holomorphic eigenfunctions in the asymptotic domain

|x_{1}|\gg|x_{2}|\gg\cdots\gg|x_{n}|

to symmetric eigenfunctions in the
physical domain

around the torus.
The method we explain below
will be extended to the elliptic case in Section
5

so as to establish the convergence of the
symmetric eigenfunctions
that deform Macdonald polynomials.

3.1
Integral transform

We first recall some basic results from the theory of Macdonald polynomials
[
16
]
.
The
Cauchy kernel

K(x,y)=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\prod_{j=1}^{n}\frac{(tx_{i}/y_{j};q)_{\infty}}{(x_{i}/y_{j};q)_{\infty}}

(3.1)

for the Macdonald polynomials in variables
x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
and
y=(y_{1},\ldots,y_{n})

is a meromorphic function on
(\mathbb{C}^{\ast})^{n}\times(\mathbb{C}^{\ast})^{n}
,
and it has an expansion

K(x,y)=\sum_{\ell(\lambda)\leq n}b_{\lambda}P_{\lambda}(x)P_{\lambda}(y^{-1}),\quad b_{\lambda}=\prod_{1\leq i\leq j\leq n}\frac{(t^{j-i+1}q^{\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j}};q)_{\lambda_{j}-\lambda_{j+1}}}{(t^{j-i}q^{\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j}+1};q)_{\lambda_{j}-\lambda_{j+1}}}

(3.2)

in the domain
|x_{i}/y_{j}|<1
(
i,j\in\left\{1,\ldots,n\right\}
),
where the sum is taken over
all partitions
\lambda
with
l(\lambda)\leq n
,
and
(a;q)_{k}=(1-a)(1-qa)\cdots(1-q^{k-1}a)
(
k=0,1,2,\ldots
).
We define the weight function
w(y)
by

w(y)=\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq n}(1-y_{j}/y_{i})\frac{(qy_{j}/ty_{i};q)_{\infty}}{(ty_{j}/y_{i};q)_{\infty}},

(3.3)

which is holomorphic in the domain
|y_{j}/y_{i}|<|t|^{-1}
(
1\leq i<j\leq n
).

For each
\theta>0
, we denote by
V_{\theta}
the domain in
(\mathbb{C}^{\ast})^{n}

specified by

V_{\theta}=\left\{{y=(y_{1},\ldots,y_{n})\in(\mathbb{C}^{\ast})^{n}}\big|{\ |y_{2}/y_{1}|<\theta,\ |y_{3}/y_{2}|<\theta,\ \ldots,\ |y_{n}/y_{n-1}|<\theta}\right\}.

(3.4)

Fixing positive constants
r,\sigma
such that
\sigma<\min\left\{1,\theta,|t|^{-1}\right\}
,
we define an
n
-cycle
C_{r,\sigma}
in
(\mathbb{C}^{\ast})^{n}

by

C_{r,\sigma}=\left\{{y=(y_{1},\ldots,y_{n})\in(\mathbb{C}^{\ast})^{n}}\big|{\ |y_{i}|=\sigma^{i-1}r\ \ (i=1,\ldots,n)}\right\}

(3.5)

with positive orientation.
Note that
w(y)
is holomorphic in an neighborhood of
C_{r,\sigma}
since

\sigma<\min\left\{1,|t|^{-1}\right\}
.
For each holomorphic function
\psi=\psi(y)
in
V_{\theta}
, we define
its integral transform
\mathcal{K}(\psi)
by

\mathcal{K}(\psi)(x)=\int_{C_{r,\sigma}}K(x,y)w(y)\psi(y)d\omega_{n}(y).

(3.6)

Lemma 3.1

The function
\varphi(x)=\mathcal{K}(\psi)(x)
defined as above is continued to an
entire symmetric holomorphic function in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
.

Proof
: The symmetry of
\varphi(x)
follows from that of
K(x,y)
in
x
.
Since
K(x,y)
is holomorphic in the domain
|x_{i}/y_{j}|<1
(
i,j=1,\ldots,n
), it is
is holomorphic in the domain
|x_{i}|<\sigma^{n-1}r

(i=1,\ldots,n)
when
y\in C_{r,\sigma}
.
This implies that
\varphi(x)
is holomorphic in the same domain.
Since the integral does not depend on
r\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
,

\varphi(x)
is continued to an entire function in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
.

\square

3.2
From eigenfunctions in the asymptotic domain
to Macdonald polynomials

In what follows, we use the generating function

D_{x}(u)=\sum_{r=0}^{n}(-u)^{r}D^{(r)}_{x}=\sum_{I\subseteq\left\{1,\ldots,n\right\}}(-u)^{I}A_{I}(x)T_{q,x}^{I}

(3.7)

of the Macdonald-Ruijsenaars operators
D^{(r)}_{x}
(
r=0,1,\ldots,n
),
so that

D_{x}(u)P_{\lambda}(x)=\varepsilon(u)P_{\lambda}(x),\quad\varepsilon(u)=\sum_{r=0}^{n}(-u)^{r}e_{r}(t^{\rho}q^{\lambda})=\prod_{j=1}^{n}(1-ut^{n-j}q^{\lambda_{j}}),

(3.8)

for each partition
\lambda
with
\ell(\lambda)\leq n
.
Also,
using the multi-index notation

T_{q,x}^{\mu}=T_{q,x_{1}}^{\mu_{1}}\cdots T_{q,x_{n}}^{\mu_{n}}
,
for a
q
-difference operator
L=\sum_{\mu\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}a_{\mu}(y)T_{q,y}^{\mu}

(finite sum) in
y
variables, we denote by

L^{\ast}=\sum_{\mu\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}T_{q,y}^{-\mu}a_{\mu}(y)

the formal adjoint of
L
with respect to
d\omega_{n}(y)
, noting that

(L_{1}L_{2})^{\ast}=L_{2}^{\ast}L_{1}^{\ast}
.

Recall that the Ruijsenaars-Macdonald operator
D_{x}(u)
is formally self-adjoint with respect
to the weight function

w^{\mathrm{sym}}(y)=\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq n}\frac{(y_{i}/y_{j};q)_{\infty}(y_{j}/y_{i};q)_{\infty}}{(ty_{i}/y_{j};q)_{\infty}(ty_{j}/y_{i};q)_{\infty}}

(3.9)

for the orthogonality of Macdonald polynomials;
the superscript “sym” is used to indicate that this weight function is symmetric with respect to

y=(y_{1},\ldots,y_{n})
.
Namely we have an identity

D_{y}(u)^{\ast}=w^{\mathrm{sym}}(y)D_{y^{-1}}(u)w^{\mathrm{sym}}(y)^{-1}

(3.10)

of
q
-difference operators. Also, the kernel function
K(x,y)
satisfies
the kernel function identity

D_{x}(u)K(x,y)=D_{y^{-1}}(u)K(x,y).

(3.11)

(See for instance,
[
19
]
and
[
11
]
.)
Hence we have

D_{x}(u)K(x,y)w^{\mathrm{sym}}(y)=w^{\mathrm{sym}}(y)D_{y^{-1}}K(x,y)=D_{y}(u)^{\ast}(K(x,y)w^{\mathrm{sym}}(y)).

(3.12)

We use the idea of integral representation of
[
19
]
for our weight
function
w(y)
defined above.
For this purpose we rewrite
w^{\mathrm{sym}}(y)
as follows:

\begin{split}w^{\mathrm{sym}}(y)&=\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq n}\frac{(y_{j}/y_{i};q)_{\infty}(qy_{j}/ty_{i};q)_{\infty}}{(ty_{j}/y_{i};q)_{\infty}(qy_{j}/y_{i};q)_{\infty}}\frac{\theta(y_{i}/y_{j};q)}{\theta(ty_{i}/y_{j};q)}\\
&=\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq n}(1-y_{j}/y_{i})\frac{(qy_{j}/ty_{i};q)_{\infty}}{(ty_{j}/y_{i};q)_{\infty}}\cdot\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq n}\frac{\theta(y_{i}/y_{j};q)}{\theta(ty_{i}/y_{j};q)}.\end{split}

(3.13)

This means that

w^{\mathrm{sym}}(y)=w(y)g(y),\quad g(y)=\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq n}\frac{\theta(y_{i}/y_{j};q)}{\theta(ty_{i}/y_{j};q)}.

(3.14)

Noting that

T_{q,y_{i}}g(y)=t^{n-2i+1}g(y)\qquad(i=1,\ldots,n),

(3.15)

we define the
n
-vector
\kappa=(\kappa_{1},\ldots,\kappa_{n})\in\mathbb{C}^{n}

by
\kappa_{i}=(n-2i+1)\log t/\log q

(i=1,\ldots,n)
.
Then we have

T_{q,y}^{\mu}g(y)=q^{\left\langle\mu,\kappa\right\rangle}g(y)\qquad(\mu\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}),

(3.16)

where
\left\langle\mu,\kappa\right\rangle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mu_{i}\kappa_{i}
.
This means that the ratio
g(y)/y^{\kappa}
is a
q
-periodic function in each
variable
y_{i}
(
i=1,\ldots,n
).

We introduce the generating function

E_{x,s}(u)=\sum_{I\subseteq\left\{1,\ldots,n\right\}}(-u)^{|I|}s^{\epsilon_{I}}B_{I}(x)T_{q,x}^{\epsilon_{I}}

(3.17)

for the
modified Ruijsenaars-Macdonald operators
E^{(r)}_{x,s}
of (
2.37
),
so that
D_{x}(u)=E_{x,t^{\rho}}(u)
.
Then the Macdonald function
f(x;s)
in the asymptotic domain
|x_{1}|\gg|x_{2}|\gg\cdots\gg|x_{n}|

satisfies

E_{x,s}(u)f(x;s)=\varepsilon(s;u)f(x;s),\quad\varepsilon(s;u)=\sum_{r=0}^{n}(-u)^{r}e_{r}(s)=\prod_{j=1}^{n}(1-us_{j}).

(3.18)

It is known by
[
20
]
that

f(x;s)
is holomorphic in the domain

|x_{j}/x_{i}|<|t/q|
(
1\leq i<j\leq n
) and
|s_{j}/s_{i}|<1/|q|
(
1\leq i<j\leq n
).

Returning to the kernel function,
by (
3.12
) we obtain

\begin{split}D_{x}(u)K(x,y)w(y)&=g(y)^{-1}D_{y}(u)^{\ast}\big(g(y)K(x,y)w(y)\big)\\
&=(g(y)D_{y}(u)g(y)^{-1})^{\ast}\big(K(x,y)w(y)\big)\\
&=(E_{y,t^{\rho}q^{-\kappa}})^{\ast}(K(x,y)w(y)).\end{split}

(3.19)

Note that

t^{\rho}q^{-\kappa}=(t^{n-1}\cdot t^{-n+1},t^{n-2}\cdot t^{-n+3},\ldots,1\cdot t^{n-1})=(1,t,\ldots,t^{n-1}).

(3.20)

Hereafter, for a vector
a=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})
, we denote by
a^{\vee}=(a_{n},\ldots,a_{1})

the
reversal
of
a
(the action of the longest element of
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
).
In this notation of reversals, we obtain the kernel function identity

D_{x}(u)K(x,y)w(y)=(E_{y,{t^{\rho^{\vee}}}})^{\ast}(K(x,y)w(y)).

(3.21)

Theorem 3.2

Let
\lambda
be a partition with
l(\lambda)\leq n
.
Setting
s=t^{\rho}q^{\lambda}
, let
f(y;s^{\vee})
be the Macdonald function in the
asymptotic domain
V_{\theta}

with parameters
s^{\vee}=t^{\rho^{\vee}}\!q^{\lambda^{\vee}}

with
\theta^{n-1}<|t/q|
.
Then we have

\int_{C_{r,\sigma}}K(x,y)w(y)\,y^{\lambda^{\vee}}\!f(y;s^{\vee})d\omega_{n}(y)=b_{\lambda}P_{\lambda}(x).

(3.22)

Proof
:
Taking
\sigma>0
such that
\sigma|q|<\min\left\{1,\theta,1/|t|\right\}
, we denote by

\varphi(x)=\mathcal{K}(f(y;s^{\vee}))
the left-hand side of (
3.22
).
In this setting, if
|x_{i}|<|q|\sigma^{n-1}r
(
i=1,\ldots,n
), then the
n
-cycle
C_{r,\sigma}

is
q
-shift invariant with respect to the variables
y_{i}
(
i=1,\ldots,n
) by Cauchy’s theorem.
Hence we obtain

\begin{split}D_{x}(u)\varphi(x)&=\int_{C_{r,\sigma}}D_{x}(u)K(x,y)w(y)\,y^{\lambda^{\vee}}\!f(y;s^{\vee})d\omega_{n}(y)\\
&=\int_{C_{r,\sigma}}(E_{y,t^{\rho^{\vee}}})^{\ast}\big(K(x,y)w(y))\,y^{\lambda^{\vee}}\!f(y;s^{\vee})d\omega_{n}(y)\\
&=\int_{C_{r,\sigma}}K(x,y)w(y)E_{y,t^{\rho^{\vee}}}\big(y^{\lambda^{\vee}}\!f(y;s^{\vee})\big)d\omega_{n}(y)\\
&=\int_{C_{r,\sigma}}K(x,y)w(y)\,y^{\lambda^{\vee}}E_{y,t^{\rho^{\vee}}q^{\lambda^{\vee}}}\big(f(y;s^{\vee})\big)d\omega_{n}(y)\\
\end{split}

(3.23)

Here we compute

E_{y,t^{\rho^{\vee}}}\big(y^{\lambda^{\vee}}\!f(y;s^{\vee})\big)=y^{\lambda^{\vee}}E_{y,t^{\rho^{\vee}}q^{\lambda^{\vee}}}f(y;t^{\rho^{\vee}}q^{\lambda^{\vee}})=\varepsilon(t^{\rho^{\vee}}q^{\lambda^{\vee}};u)y^{\lambda^{\vee}}f(y;t^{\rho^{\vee}}q^{\lambda^{\vee}}).

(3.24)

Since
\varepsilon(s^{\vee};u)=\varepsilon(s;u)=\prod_{i=1}^{n}(1-us_{i})
, we obtain

D_{x}(u)\varphi(x)=\varepsilon(t^{\rho}q^{\lambda};u)\varphi(x).

(3.25)

Note that the coefficient of
u^{n}
gives
t^{\binom{n}{2}}T_{q,x_{1}}\cdots T_{q,x_{n}}\varphi(x)=t^{\binom{n}{2}}q^{|\lambda|}\varphi(x)
, which implies
\varphi(x)
is homogenous of
degree
|\lambda|
in
x
.
Since
\varphi(x)
is a symmetric holomorphic function in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
, it is actually a
symmetric polynomial satisfying (
3.25
), and hence a constant multiple
of the Macdonald polynomial
P_{\lambda}(x)
.
On the other hand, by (

) we have

\varphi(x)=\sum_{\ell(\mu)\leq n}\ c_{\mu}b_{\mu}P_{\mu}(x),\qquad c_{\mu}=\int_{C_{r,\sigma}}P_{\mu}(y^{-1})\,y^{\lambda^{\vee}}\!f(y;s^{\vee})d\omega_{n}(y).

(3.26)

Since
\varphi(x)
is a constant multiple of
P_{\lambda}(x)
, we have

c_{\mu}=0
(
\mu\neq\lambda
) and
\varphi(x)=c_{\lambda}b_{\lambda}P_{\lambda}(x)
.
In fact we have
c_{\lambda}=1
, since

P_{\lambda}(y^{-1})
and
y^{\lambda^{\vee}}f(y;s^{\vee})
and
w(y)

have leading terms

y^{-\lambda^{\vee}}
,
y^{\lambda^{\vee}}
and
1
, respectively, with respect to the dominance order.
This implies
\varphi(x)=b_{\lambda}P_{\lambda}(x)
.

\square

This theorem explains how the Macdonald polynomials are reconstructed
by the integral transform from the

in the asymptotic domain

.

for proving

4
Existence of formal joint eigenfunctions

We begin this section by remarks on the condition on the parameter
t\in\mathbb{C}^{\ast}
that
we impose for the separation of eigenvalues.
After giving proofs to Propositions
2.1
and
2.5
,
we establish the existence theorems of formal joint eigenfunctions
for the two cases of elliptic deformations of Macdonald polynomials,
and asymptotically free solutions in the domain
|x_{1}|\gg\cdots\gg|x_{n}|\gg|px_{1}|
,
respectively.

In order to clarify how the Ruijsenaars operators are related to the root system
of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
, we recall here some standard notations and terminologies relevant to our arguments.
We denote by
\mathsf{P}=\mathbb{Z}\epsilon_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathbb{Z}\epsilon_{n}
the (integral)

weight lattice
of
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
; it is a free
\mathbb{Z}
-module with
canonical basis
\epsilon_{1},\ldots,\epsilon_{n}
, endowed with the
the scalar product (symmetric bilinear form)

\left\langle\ ,\,\right\rangle:\ \mathsf{P}\times\mathsf{P}\to\mathbb{Z}
such that
\left\langle\epsilon_{i},\epsilon_{j}\right\rangle=\delta_{i,j}

(
i,j\in\left\{1,\ldots,n\right\}
). We freely identify an integral weight

\mu=\mu_{1}\epsilon_{1}+\cdots+\mu_{n}\epsilon_{n}\in\mathsf{P}

with the
n
-vector of integers
\mu=(\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
,
and denote by
x^{\mu}=x_{1}^{\mu_{1}}\cdots x_{n}^{\mu_{n}}
the corresponding
monomial of the variables
x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
.
Fixing the
simple roots

\alpha_{i}=\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{i+1}\in\mathsf{P}
(
i=1,\ldots,n-1
),
we denote by

\mathsf{Q}=\mathbb{Z}\alpha_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathbb{Z}\alpha_{n-1}\subseteq\mathsf{P}

(4.1)

the
root lattice
of
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
, and by

\mathsf{P}_{+}=\left\{{\lambda\in\mathsf{P}}\big|{\ \left\langle\alpha_{i},\lambda\right\rangle\geq 0\ \ (i=1,\ldots,n-1)}\right\}\subseteq\mathsf{P},

(4.2)

the cone of
dominant integral weights
.
Note that an integral weight

\lambda=\lambda_{1}\epsilon_{1}+\cdots+\lambda_{n}\epsilon_{n}\in\mathsf{P}
,
or an integer vector
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
,
belongs
to
\mathsf{Q}
if and only if
|\lambda|=\lambda_{1}+\cdots+\lambda_{n}=0
,
and to
\mathsf{P}_{+}
if and only if
\lambda_{1}\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_{n}
.
In terms of the root system,
the dominance order
\mu\leq\nu
on
\mathsf{P}
is defined by the condition

\nu-\mu\in\mathsf{Q}_{+}=\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\alpha_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\alpha_{n-1}
.
We denote by

\Delta_{+}=\left\{{\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{j}}\big|{1\leq i<j\leq n}\right\}\subseteq\mathsf{Q}_{+}

the set of
positive roots
, so that

\Delta(x)=\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq n}(x_{i}-x_{j})=\prod_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}^{n-i}\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq n}(1-x_{j}/x_{i})=x^{\rho}\prod_{\alpha\in\Delta_{+}}(1-x^{-\alpha}),

(4.3)

where
\rho=\sum_{i=1}^{n}(n-i)\epsilon_{i}
.

4.1
Separation of eigenvalues

Introducing a parameter
u
, we define a generating function
D_{x}(u)

of the Macdonald-Ruijsenaars operators by

D_{x}(u)=\sum_{r=0}^{n}(-u)^{r}D_{x}^{(r)}=\sum_{I\subseteq\left\{1,\ldots,n\right\}}(-u)^{|I|}A_{I}(x)T_{q,x}^{\epsilon_{I}},\quad A_{I}(x)=\frac{T_{t,x}^{\epsilon_{I}}(\Delta(x))}{\Delta(x)}.

(4.4)

Here we
used the multi-index notation for

T_{q,x}^{\mu}=T_{q,x_{1}}^{\mu_{1}}\cdots T_{q,x_{n}}^{\mu}

for
q
-shift operators,
setting
\epsilon_{I}=\sum_{i\in I}\epsilon_{i}\in\mathsf{P}
for each subset
I\subseteq\left\{1,\ldots,n\right\}
.
Then, the joint eigenfunction equations (
2.15
) for the
Macdonald polynomial
P_{\lambda}(x)
(
\lambda\in\mathsf{P}_{+}
) can be written as a
single eigenfunction equation

D_{x}(u)P_{\lambda}(x)=d_{\lambda}(u)P_{\lambda}(x),\quad d_{\lambda}(u)=\sum_{r=0}^{n}(-u)^{r}e_{r}(t^{\rho}q^{\lambda})=\prod_{j=1}^{n}(1-ut^{n-j}q^{\lambda_{j}}),

(4.5)

containing a parameter
u
.
Note that, for a pair
\lambda,\mu\in\mathsf{P}
of integral weights,
d_{\lambda}(u)=d_{\mu}(u)
as polynomials in
u

if and only if there exists a permutation
\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}
such that

t^{n-j}q^{\mu_{j}}=t^{n-\sigma(j)}q^{\lambda_{\sigma(j)}}\quad(j=1,\ldots,n).

(4.6)

For a subset
L\subseteq\mathsf{P}
, we say that the polynomials

d_{\lambda}(u)

separate

L
if
d_{\lambda}(u)\neq d_{\mu}(u)

for any distinct pair
\lambda,\mu\in L
.
The condition
t^{k}\notin q^{\mathbb{Z}_{<0}}

(k=1,\ldots,n-1)
that we imposed for the existence
of Macdonald polynomials is equivalent to saying that
the polynomials
d_{\lambda}(u)
separate
\mathsf{P}_{+}
.
In fact, under our assumption
|q|<1
, we have

Lemma 4.1

(1)
The polynomials
d_{\lambda}(u)
separate
\mathsf{P}
if and only if
t^{k}\notin q^{\mathbb{Z}}

(k=1,\ldots,n-1)
.

(2)
The polynomials
d_{\lambda}(u)
separate
\mathsf{P}_{+}
if and only if
t^{k}\notin q^{\mathbb{Z}_{<0}}

(k=1,\ldots,n-1)
.

Proof
:
(1) If
d_{\lambda}(u)=d_{\mu}(u)
for a distinct pair
\lambda,\mu\in\mathsf{P}
,
we have
t^{n-j}q^{\mu_{j}}=t^{n-\sigma(j)}q^{\lambda_{\sigma(j)}}
(
j=1,\ldots,n
)
for a permutation
\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}
. Since
\lambda\neq\mu
, we have
\sigma\neq 1
,
and hence there exists an index
j\in\left\{1,\ldots,n\right\}
such that
\sigma(j)\neq j
and

t^{\sigma(j)-j}=q^{\lambda_{\sigma(j)}-\mu_{j}}\in q^{\mathbb{Z}}
,
which means
t^{k}\in q^{\mathbb{Z}}
for
k=|\sigma(j)-j|\in\left\{1,\ldots,n-1\right\}
.
Conversely,
suppose that
t^{n-i}=q^{-l}
for some
i\in\left\{1,\ldots,n-1\right\}
and
l\in\mathbb{Z}
, setting
i=n-k
.
Then we have

\begin{split}(t^{n-1}q^{\lambda_{1}},\ldots,t^{n-i}q^{\lambda_{i}},\ldots,q^{\lambda_{n}})&=(t^{n-1}q^{\lambda_{1}},\ldots,q^{\lambda_{i}-l},\ldots,t^{n-i}q^{\lambda_{n}+l})\\
&=(i,n).(t^{n-1}q^{\lambda_{1}},\ldots,t^{n-i}q^{\lambda_{n}+l},\ldots,q^{\lambda_{i}-l}),\end{split}

(4.7)

where
(i,n)
stands for the transposition of
i
and
n
.
This implies that
d_{\lambda}(u)=d_{\mu}(u)
for the pair

\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{i},\ldots,\lambda_{n}),\quad\mu=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n}+l,\ldots,\lambda_{i}-l)\in\mathsf{P}.

(4.8)

(2) We first show that, if
|t|\leq 1
, then the polynomials
d_{\lambda}(u)
separate

\lambda\in\mathsf{P}_{+}
.
Under the assumption
|t|\leq 1
,
the sequence
|t^{n-j}q^{\lambda_{j}}|
(
j=1,\ldots,n
) is non-decreasing
for any dominant
\lambda\in\mathsf{P}_{+}
.
If
d_{\lambda}(u)=d_{\mu}(u)
for a pair
\lambda,\mu\in\mathsf{P}_{+}
, then we have

|t^{n-j}q^{\lambda_{j}}|=|t^{n-j}q^{\mu_{j}}|

and hence
|q|^{\lambda_{j}}=|q|^{\mu_{j}}
for
j=1,\ldots,n
.
This implies
\lambda_{j}=\mu_{j}
(
j=1,\ldots,n
) (since
|q|<1
).
Suppose now that
d_{\lambda}(u)=d_{\mu}(u)
for a distinct pair
\lambda,\mu\in\mathsf{P}
.
Then we have
|t|>1
, and also
t^{k}\in q^{\mathbb{Z}}
for some
k\in\left\{1,\ldots,n-1\right\}
by (1).
Hence
t^{k}\in q^{\mathbb{Z}_{<0}}
for some
k\in\left\{1,\ldots,n-1\right\}
.
Conversely, suppose that
t^{n-i}=q^{-l}
for some
i\in\left\{1,\ldots,n-1\right\}
and
l\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
.
Then
d_{\lambda}(u)=d_{\mu}(u)
for

\lambda=(m,\ldots,m,0,0\ldots,0)
with
m
’s in the first
i-1
places,
and
\mu=(m,\ldots,m,l,0,\ldots,-l)
, both in
\mathsf{P}_{+}

if
m\geq l
.

\square

The following lemma is crucial in our proof of the existence theorem of formal joint eigenfunctions
for the elliptic Ruijsenaars operators.

Lemma 4.2

If the polynomials
d_{\lambda}(u)
separate a subset
L\subseteq\mathsf{P}
, then
there exists a constant
c\in\mathbb{C}^{\ast}
, such that
d_{\lambda}(c)\neq d_{\mu}(c)

for any distinct pair
\lambda,\mu\in L
.

Proof
:
The set

S=\left\{{a\in\mathbb{C}^{\ast}}\big|{\ d_{\lambda}(a)=d_{\mu}(a)\ \ \mbox{for some distinct pair $\lambda,\mu\in L$}}\right\}\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{\ast}

(4.9)

is countable, and hence the complement
\mathbb{C}^{\ast}\backslash S
is non-empty.
This implies that any element
c\in\mathbb{C}^{\ast}\backslash S
satisfies the condition

d_{\lambda}(c)\neq d_{\mu}(c)
for any distinct pair
\lambda,\mu\in\mathbb{C}^{\ast}
.

\square

4.2
Elliptic Ruijsenaars operators as power series of
p

In this subsection,
we clarify the structure of
p
-expansion of the elliptic Ruijsenaars operators,
thereby proving Propositions
2.1
and
2.5
.

Similarly to the trigonometric case, we introduce the generation function

\mathcal{D}_{x}(p;u)=\sum_{r=0}^{n}(-u)^{r}\mathcal{D}^{(r)}_{x}(p)=\sum_{I\subseteq\left\{1,\ldots,n\right\}}(-u)^{|I|}\mathcal{A}_{I}(x;p)T_{q,x}^{\epsilon_{I}},\quad\mathcal{A}_{I}(x;p)=\frac{T_{t,x}^{\epsilon_{I}}(\Delta(x;p))}{\Delta(x;p)},

(4.10)

for the elliptic Ruijsenaars operators,
where
\Delta(x;p)=x^{\rho}\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq n}\theta(x_{j}/x_{i};p)
.
We express the coefficients

\mathcal{A}_{I}(x;p)
in two ways:

\mathcal{A}_{I}(x;p)=t^{\left\langle\epsilon_{I},\rho\right\rangle}\mathcal{B}_{I}(x;p)=A_{I}(x)\mathcal{C}_{I}(x;p),

(4.11)

where
A_{I}(x)=\mathcal{A}_{I}(x;0)
are the coefficients of
D_{x}(u)

in (
2.32
) and

\begin{split}\mathcal{B}_{I}(x;p)&=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i<j\leq n\\
i\in I,\,j\notin I\end{subarray}}\frac{\theta(x_{j}/tx_{i};p)}{\theta(x_{j}/x_{i};p)}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i<j\leq n\\
i\notin I,\,j\in I\end{subarray}}\frac{\theta(tx_{j}/x_{i};p)}{\theta(x_{j}/x_{i};p)},\\
\mathcal{C}_{I}(x;p)&=\prod_{i\in I,\,j\notin I}\frac{(ptx_{i}/x_{j};p)(px_{j}/tx_{i};p)}{(px_{i}/x_{j};p)(px_{j}/x_{i};p)}.\end{split}

(4.12)

We remark here that, for each
I\subseteq\left\{1,\ldots,n\right\}
,

\mathcal{B}_{I}(x;p)
is a holomorphic function of the variables

z_{0}=px_{1}/x_{n},z_{1}=x_{2}/x_{1},\ldots,z_{n-1}=x_{n}/x_{n-1}

in the polydisc
|z_{0}|<1,|z_{1}|<1,\ldots,|z_{n-1}|<1
.
In fact, we have
z_{0}z_{1}\cdots z_{n-1}=p
and

x_{j}/x_{i}=z_{i}\cdots z_{j-1},\quad px_{i}/x_{j}=z_{j}\cdots z_{n-1}z_{0}\cdots z_{i-1}\quad(1\leq i<j\leq n).

(4.13)

This implies in particular that
\mathcal{B}_{I}(x;p)\in\mathbb{C}\left\{px_{1}/x_{n},x_{2}/x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}/x_{n-1}\right\}
, and hence,

\mathcal{B}_{I}(x;p)
is represented by a power series in
p
of the form

\mathcal{B}_{I}(x;p)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}p^{k}\,\mathcal{B}_{I,k}(x),\quad\mathcal{B}_{I,k}(x)\in(x_{1}/x_{n})^{k}\mathbb{C}\left\{x_{2}/x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}/x_{n-1}\right\}\quad(k=0,1,2,\ldots),

(4.14)

which proves Proposition
2.5
for
\mathcal{A}_{I}(x;p)=t^{\left\langle\epsilon_{I},\rho\right\rangle}\mathcal{B}_{I}(x;p)
.
On the other hand, from (
4.12
),
\mathcal{C}_{I}(x;p)
is
holomorphic in the domain
|p|<|x_{j}/x_{i}|<|p|^{-1}
, and
it can be represented there
by a power series of
p
whose coefficients are Laurent polynomials in
x
:

\mathcal{C}_{I}(x;p)=\sum_{k=0}^{p}p^{k}\mathcal{C}_{I,k}(x),\quad\mathcal{C}_{I,k}(x)\in\mathbb{C}[x^{\pm 1}]\quad(k=0,1,2,\ldots).

(4.15)

Hence,
each coefficients of
\mathcal{A}_{I}(x;p)
of the elliptic Ruijsenaars operators has a
p
-expansion of the form

\mathcal{A}_{I}(x;p)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}p^{k}\,\mathcal{A}_{I,k}(x),\quad\mathcal{A}_{I,k}(x)=t^{\left\langle\epsilon_{I},\rho\right\rangle}\mathcal{B}_{I,k}(x)=A_{I}(x)\mathcal{C}_{I,k}(x)\quad(k=0,1,2,\ldots).

(4.16)

Note that both expansions (
4.14
) and (
4.15
) make sense
in the domain
|p|^{\frac{1}{n-1}}<|x_{i+1}/x_{i}|<1
(
i=1,\ldots,n-1
).
Combining (
4.14
) and (
4.15
), we have

\mathcal{A}_{I,k}(x)\in\big(A_{I}(x)\mathbb{C}[x^{\pm 1}]\big)\cap\big((x_{1}/x_{n})^{k}\mathbb{C}\left\{x_{2}/x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}/x_{n-1}\right\}\big)

(4.17)

for
k=0,1,2,\ldots
.
For each
r=1,\ldots,n
,
the elliptic Ruijsenaars operator

\mathcal{D}_{x}^{(r)}(p)
is a
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
-invariant
q
-difference operator,
and hence
the coefficients
\mathcal{D}^{(r)}_{x,k}(u)

(k=0,1,2\ldots)

in the
p
-expansion

\mathcal{D}^{(r)}_{x}(p)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}p^{k}\,\mathcal{D}^{(r)}_{x,k},\quad\mathcal{D}^{(r)}_{x,k}=\sum_{I\subseteq\left\{1,\ldots,n\right\};\ |I|=r}\mathcal{A}_{I,k}(x)T_{q,x}^{\epsilon_{I}}

(4.18)

are also
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
-invariant.
Since
\Delta(x)\mathcal{D}^{(r)}_{x,k}

(k=0,1,2,\ldots)

have Laurent polynomial coefficients as well,
they stabilize the ring
\mathbb{C}[x^{\pm 1}]^{\mathfrak{S}_{n}}
of symmetric Laurent polynomials,
namely

\mathcal{D}^{(r)}_{x,k}\big(\mathbb{C}[x^{\pm 1}]^{\mathfrak{S}_{n}}\big)\subseteq\mathbb{C}[x^{\pm 1}]^{\mathfrak{S}_{n}}
.
(Note that
\mathcal{D}^{(r)}_{x,k}
do
not
stabilize
\mathbb{C}[x^{\pm 1}]
.)
Suppose that

\varphi(x)\in\mathbb{C}[x^{\pm 1}]^{\mathfrak{S}_{n}}_{\leq\mu}
for some
\mu\in\mathsf{P}_{+}
.
Since
\varphi(x)\in x^{\mu}\mathbb{C}\left\{x_{2}/x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}/x_{n-1}\right\}
,
by (
4.17
) we obtain

\psi(x)=\mathcal{D}^{(r)}_{x,k}\big(\varphi(x)\big)\in x^{\mu+k\phi}\mathbb{C}\left\{x_{2}/x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}/x_{n-1}\right\},

(4.19)

where
x^{\phi}=x_{1}/x_{n}
. Hence

\psi(x)\in\mathbb{C}[x^{\pm 1}]^{\mathfrak{S}_{n}}\cap\big(x^{\mu+k\phi}\mathbb{C}\left\{x_{2}/x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}/x_{n-1}\right\}\big)=\mathbb{C}[x^{\pm 1}]^{\mathfrak{S}_{n}}_{\leq\mu+k\phi}.

(4.20)

This means that

\mathcal{D}^{(r)}_{x,k}\big(\mathbb{C}[x^{\pm 1}]^{\mathfrak{S}_{n}}_{\leq\mu}\big)\subseteq\mathbb{C}[x^{\pm 1}]^{\mathfrak{S}_{n}}_{\leq\mu+k\phi}

for all
k=0,1,2,\ldots
and
\mu\in\mathsf{P}_{+}
,
which proves Proposition
2.1
.

4.3
Elliptic deformation of Macdonald polynomials

In this subsection, we complete our proof of Theorem
2.2
.

Denoting by
\mathcal{R}=\mathbb{C}[x^{\pm 1}]^{\mathfrak{S}_{n}}
the ring of symmetric
Laurent polynomials in
x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
,
we consider the commuting family of
\mathbb{C}[\hskip-1.0pt[p]\hskip-1.0pt]
-linear operators

\mathcal{D}^{(r)}_{x}(p):\ \ \mathcal{R}[\hskip-1.0pt[p]\hskip-1.0pt]\to\mathcal{R}[\hskip-1.0pt[p]\hskip-1.0pt]\qquad(r=1,\ldots,n)

(4.21)

defined by the elliptic Ruijsenaars operators, and investigate the joint eigenvalue problems

\mathcal{D}^{(r)}_{x}(p)\psi(x;p)=\varepsilon^{(r)}(p)\psi(x;p)\quad(r=1,\ldots,n)

(4.22)

where

\psi(x;p)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}p^{k}\psi_{k}(x)\in\mathcal{R}[\hskip-1.0pt[p]\hskip-1.0pt],\quad\varepsilon^{(r)}(p)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}p^{k}\varepsilon^{(r)}_{k}\in\mathbb{C}[\hskip-1.0pt[p]\hskip-1.0pt].

(4.23)

In terms of the generating function
\mathcal{D}_{x}(p;u)=\sum_{r=0}^{n}(-u)^{r}\mathcal{D}^{(r)}_{x}(p)
,
this joint eigenvalue problem is expressed as an equation

\mathcal{D}_{x}(u)\psi(x;p)=\varepsilon(p;u)\psi(x;p),\quad\varepsilon(p;u)=\sum_{r=0}^{n}(-u)^{r}\varepsilon^{(r)}(p)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}p^{k}\varepsilon_{k}(u)

(4.24)

with a parameter
u
.

We assume hereafter that the parameter
t
satisfies the condition

t^{k}\notin q^{\mathbb{Z}_{<0}}
(
k=1,\ldots,n-1
).
Then, by Lemma
4.1
, the polynomials

d_{\lambda}(u)=\prod_{j=1}^{n}(1-ut^{n-j}q^{\lambda_{j}})

in
u
separate the cone
\mathsf{P}_{+}
of dominant integral weights.
We also fix an arbitrary constant
c\in\mathbb{C}^{\ast}\backslash S
with
S

defined as in (
4.9
) of Lemma
4.2
,
so that
d_{\lambda}(c)\neq d_{\mu}(c)
for any distinct
\lambda,\mu\in\mathsf{P}_{+}
.
We first solve equation (
4.24
),
specializing the parameter
u
to
c
,
and then show the solution
\psi(x;p)
gives a joint eigenfunction for
all
\mathcal{D}^{(r)}_{x}(p)
(
r=1,\ldots,n
).

In terms of the expansion coefficients, the eigenfunction equation

\mathcal{D}_{x}(p;c)\psi(x;p)=\varepsilon(p;c)\psi(x;p),\quad\psi(x;p)\in\mathcal{R}[\hskip-1.0pt[p]\hskip-1.0pt]

(4.25)

is transferred to the recurrence relations

\sum_{i=0}^{k}\mathcal{D}_{x,i}(c)\psi_{k-i}(x)=\sum_{i=0}^{k}\varepsilon_{i}(c)\psi_{k-i}(x)\qquad(k=0,1,2,\ldots).

(4.26)

The conditions for
k=0
is the
eigenfunction equation

D_{x}(c)\psi_{0}(x)=\varepsilon_{0}(c)\psi_{0}(x)

(4.27)

for
\psi_{0}(x)\in\mathcal{R}=\mathbb{C}[x^{\pm 1}]^{\mathfrak{S}_{n}}
.
If
\psi_{0}(x)\neq 0
, it must be a constant multiple of
the Macdonald polynomial
P_{\lambda}(x)
for some
\lambda\in\mathsf{P}_{+}
.
Fixing a dominant integral weight
\lambda\in\mathsf{P}_{+}
, we take

\psi_{0}(x)=P_{\lambda}(x),\quad\varepsilon_{0}(c)=d_{\lambda}(c)

(4.28)

for the initial data.
We rewrite the recurrence relations
for
k>0
as

\mathcal{D}_{x,0}(c)\psi_{k}(x)+\sum_{i=1}^{k}\mathcal{D}_{x,i}(c)\psi_{k-i}(x)=\varepsilon_{0}(c)\psi_{k}(x)+\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}\varepsilon_{i}(c)\psi_{k-i}(x)+\varepsilon_{k}(c)\psi_{0}(x),

(4.29)

namely,

\big(D_{x}(c)-d_{\lambda}(c)\big)\psi_{k}(x)-\varepsilon_{k}(c)P_{\lambda}(x)=-\sum_{i=1}^{k}\mathcal{D}_{x,i}(c)\psi_{k-i}(x)+\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}\varepsilon_{i}(c)\psi_{k-i}(x).

(4.30)

At this step, regarding the right-hand side as known,
we need to solve this equation for
\psi_{k}(x)\in\mathcal{R}
and
\varepsilon_{k}(c)\in\mathbb{C}
.
Under the genericity assumption on
c
,
we verify that
\psi_{k}(x)
and
\varepsilon_{k}(c)
are determined inductively
from
\psi_{i}(x)
and
\varepsilon_{i}(c)
(
i=0,1,\ldots,k-1
).
For that purpose, we use the decomposition

\mathcal{R}=\mathbb{C}P_{\lambda}(x)\oplus\mathcal{S}_{\lambda},\quad\mathcal{S}_{\lambda}=\bigoplus_{\mu\in P_{+};\,\mu\neq\lambda}\mathbb{C}P_{\mu}(x).

(4.31)

of
\mathcal{R}
by means of the Macdonald basis.
Note that

\big(D_{x}(c)-d_{\lambda}(c)\big)P_{\mu}(x)=\big(d_{\mu}(c)-d_{\lambda}(c))P_{\mu}(x)

(4.32)

and
d_{\mu}(c)\neq d_{\lambda}(c)
for any
\mu\in P_{+}
with
\mu\neq\lambda
.
This implies that

\mathrm{Ker}(D_{x}(c)-d_{\lambda}(c):\mathcal{R}\to\mathcal{R})=\mathbb{C}P_{\lambda}(x)

and that
D_{x}(c)-d_{\lambda}(c)
induces a
\mathbb{C}
-isomorphism
\mathcal{S}_{\lambda}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\to}}\mathcal{S}_{\lambda}
.
In the recurrence relation (
4.30
), decompose the right-hand side into the
form
a_{k}P_{\lambda}(x)+\varphi_{k}(x)

with
a_{k}\in\mathbb{C}
and
\varphi_{k}(x)\in\mathcal{S}_{\lambda}
. Then

\psi_{k}(x)
and
\varepsilon_{k}(c)
are determined by

\varepsilon_{k}(c)=-a_{k},\quad(D_{x}(c)-d_{\lambda}(c))\psi_{k}(x)=\varphi_{k}(x).

(4.33)

If we assume
\psi_{k}(x)\in\mathcal{S}_{\lambda}
(
k>0
), then

\psi_{k}(x)
and
\varepsilon_{k}(c)
are determined uniquely by the recurrence.
Otherwise,
\psi_{k}(x)
is determined with freedom of
adding a multiple of
P_{\lambda}(x)
at each
k
th step.
(As we will see below, this ambiguity corresponds to the multiplication
by a power series
\gamma(p)\in\mathbb{C}[\hskip-1.0pt[p]\hskip-1.0pt]
with
\gamma(0)=1
.)
Also, it is verified inductively by the recurrence (
4.30
)
that
\psi_{k}(x)\in\mathcal{R}_{\leq\lambda+k\phi}
,
since
\psi_{\lambda,k-i}(x)\in\mathcal{R}_{\leq\lambda+(k-i)\phi}

and

\mathcal{D}_{x,i}(c)(\mathcal{R}_{\leq\lambda+(k-i)\phi})\subseteq\mathcal{R}_{\leq\lambda+k\phi}

by Proposition
2.1
.

From now on, we denote by

\varphi_{\lambda}(x;p)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}p^{k}\,\varphi_{\lambda;k}(x)\in\mathcal{R}[\hskip-1.0pt[p]\hskip-1.0pt]

(4.34)

the
unique
formal solution of the eigenfunction equation (
4.25
)
determined by the condition
that
\varphi_{\lambda;0}(x)=P_{\lambda}(x)
,
and
\varphi_{\lambda;k}(x)\in\mathcal{S}_{\lambda}
(
k>0
).
(This normalization of
\varphi_{\lambda}(x;p)

is different from the one we adopted below Theorem
2.2
.)
Also, we denote by
\varepsilon_{\lambda}(p;c)
the corresponding formal eigenvalue.
At this stage,
\varphi_{\lambda}(x;p)
may depend on the constant

c\in\mathbb{C}^{\ast}\backslash S

that we started with, but it is not the case as it will turn out later.
For this reason, we are suppressing the possible dependence on
c
in the notation
of
\varphi_{\lambda}(x;p)
.
We also remark that any formal solution of the eigenfunction equation
(
4.25
)
with initial condition
\psi(x;0)=P_{\lambda}(x)

is expressed as
\psi(x;p)=\gamma(p)\varphi_{\lambda}(x;p)
for some
\gamma(p)\in\mathbb{C}[\hskip-1.0pt[p]\hskip-1.0pt]

with
\gamma(0)=1
.
In fact, express
\psi(x;p)
as

\psi(x;p)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}p^{k}\psi_{k}(x),\quad\psi_{k}(x)=\sum_{\mu\in P_{+}}\psi_{k,\mu}P_{\mu}(x)\quad(k=0,1,2,\ldots)

(4.35)

with
\psi_{0}(x)=P_{\lambda}(x)
and
\psi_{0,\mu}=\delta_{\mu,\lambda}
.
If we set
\gamma(p)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}p^{k}\psi_{k,\lambda}
, it is easy to show
that
\gamma(p)^{-1}\psi(x;p)
satisfies the normalization condition
for
\varphi_{\lambda}(x;p)
.

We next show
that the formal solution
\varphi_{\lambda}(x;p)
is in fact a joint eigenfunction
for the elliptic Ruijsenaars operators
\mathcal{D}^{(r)}_{x}(p)
(
r=1,\ldots,n
) acting on

\mathcal{R}[\hskip-1.0pt[p]\hskip-1.0pt]
.
Since each
\mathcal{D}_{x}^{(r)}(p)
commute with
\mathcal{D}_{x}(p;c)
, we have

\mathcal{D}_{x}(p;c)\mathcal{D}_{x}^{(r)}(p)\varphi_{\lambda}(x;p)=\mathcal{D}_{x}^{(r)}(p)\mathcal{D}_{x}(p;c)\varphi_{\lambda}(x;p)=\varepsilon_{\lambda}(p;c)\mathcal{D}^{(r)}_{x}(p)\varphi_{\lambda}(x;p).

(4.36)

This means that
\mathcal{D}^{(r)}_{x}(p)\varphi_{\lambda}(x;p)
is a formal solution of the
eigenfunction equation (
4.25
) with initial condition

\mathcal{D}^{(r)}_{x}(p)\varphi_{\lambda}(x;p)\big|_{p=0}=D^{(r)}_{x}P_{\lambda}(x)=e_{r}(t^{\rho}q^{\lambda})P_{\lambda}(x).

(4.37)

Hence,
\mathcal{D}^{(r)}_{x}(p)\varphi_{\lambda}(x;p)
is expressed as

\varepsilon^{(r)}_{\lambda}(p)\varphi_{\lambda}(x;p)
with some
\varepsilon^{(r)}_{\lambda}(p)\in\mathbb{C}[\hskip-1.0pt[p]\hskip-1.0pt]

such that
\varepsilon^{(r)}_{\lambda}(0)=e_{r}(t^{\rho}q^{\lambda})
.
Namely, we have

\mathcal{D}^{(r)}_{x}(p)\varphi_{\lambda}(x;p)=\varepsilon^{(r)}_{\lambda}(p)\varphi_{\lambda}(x;p),\quad\varepsilon^{(r)}_{\lambda}(0)=e_{r}(t^{\rho}q^{\lambda})\quad(r=0,1,\ldots,n),

(4.38)

as desired. In particular, we have

\mathcal{D}_{x}(p;u)\varphi_{\lambda}(x;p)=\varepsilon_{\lambda}(p;u)\varphi_{\lambda}(x;p),\quad\varepsilon_{\lambda}(p;u)=\sum_{r=0}^{n}(-1)^{r}\varepsilon^{(r)}_{\lambda}(p)

(4.39)

for arbitrary
u\in\mathbb{C}
. This implies also that
\varphi_{\lambda}(x;p)
does
not
depend on
the constant
c\in\mathbb{C}^{\ast}\backslash S
that
we have chosen for the construction of
\varphi_{\lambda}(x;p)
.
This completes the proof of Theorem
2.2
.

and

4.4
Asymptotically free eigenfunctions in the domain
|x_{1}|\gg\cdots\gg|x_{n}|\gg|px_{1}|

In this subsection, we complete our proof of Theorem
2.6
.

As we remarked in Subsection
4.2
, the coefficients

\mathcal{A}_{I}(x;p)
of the elliptic Ruijsenaars operators
define formal power series in

\mathbb{C}[\hskip-1.0pt[px_{1}/x_{n},x_{2}/x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}/x_{n-1}]\hskip-1.0pt],

(4.40)

which is the set of all formal power series of the form

\varphi(x;p)=\sum_{l_{0},l_{1},\ldots,l_{n-1}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}}\varphi_{l_{0},l_{1},\dots,l_{n-1}}(px_{1}/x_{n})^{l_{0}}(x_{2}/x_{1})^{l_{1}}\cdots(x_{n}/x_{n-1})^{l_{n-1}}.

(4.41)

In order to simplify the presentation,
we use below the notation of monomials attached
to the root lattice
\mathsf{Q}^{\mathrm{af}}
of the affine Lie algebra
\widehat{\mathfrak{gl}}_{n}

[
9
]
.
Adding the
null root

\delta
to the basis, we extend the weight lattice
\mathsf{P}

of
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
to

\widetilde{\mathsf{P}}=\mathbb{Z}\delta\oplus\mathsf{P}=\mathbb{Z}\delta\oplus\mathbb{Z}\epsilon_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathbb{Z}\epsilon_{n},

(4.42)

together with the scalar product
\widetilde{\mathsf{P}}\times\widetilde{\mathsf{P}}\to\mathbb{Z}
extended by

\left\langle\mu,\delta\right\rangle=\left\langle\delta,\mu\right\rangle=0
(
\mu\in\mathsf{P}
) and
\left\langle\delta,\delta\right\rangle=0
.
For each element
\lambda=k\delta+\mu\in\widetilde{\mathsf{P}}

(k\in\mathbb{Z},\ \mu\in\mathsf{P})
,
we attach the monomial

x^{\lambda}=p^{-k}x^{\mu}=p^{-k}x_{1}^{\mu_{1}}\cdots x_{n}^{\mu_{n}}

(4.43)

with the convention
x^{\delta}=p^{-1}
. Introducing the 0th simple affine root

\alpha_{0}=\delta-\phi=\delta-\epsilon_{1}+\epsilon_{n}
, we denote by

\mathsf{Q}^{{\mathrm{af}}}=\mathbb{Z}\alpha_{0}\oplus\mathbb{Z}\alpha_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathbb{Z}\alpha_{n-1}=\mathbb{Z}\delta\oplus\mathsf{Q}\subseteq\widetilde{\mathsf{P}}

(4.44)

the root lattice of the affine Lie algebra
\widehat{\mathfrak{gl}}_{n}
.
Note here that
\alpha_{0}+\alpha_{1}+\cdots+\alpha_{n-1}=\delta
.
We also set
\mathsf{Q}^{\mathrm{af}}_{+}=\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\alpha_{0}\oplus\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\alpha_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\alpha_{n-1}.

Noting that

x^{-\alpha_{0}}=px_{1}/x_{n},\ \ x^{-\alpha_{1}}=x_{2}/x_{1},\ \ \ldots,\ \ x^{-\alpha_{n-1}}=x_{n}/x_{n-1},

(4.45)

we rewrite the monomials in
\mathbb{C}[\hskip-1.0pt[px_{1}/x_{n},x_{2}/x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}/x_{n-1}]\hskip-1.0pt]

as

(px_{1}/x_{n})^{l_{0}}(x_{2}/x_{1})^{l_{1}}\cdots(x_{n}/x_{n-1})^{l_{n-1}}=x^{-\beta},\quad\beta=l_{0}\alpha_{0}+l_{1}\alpha_{1}+\cdots+l_{n-1}\alpha_{n-1},

(4.46)

and call

\mathrm{ht}(\beta)=l_{0}+l_{1}+\cdots+l_{n-1}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}

the
height
of
\beta\in\mathsf{Q}^{\mathrm{af}}_{+}
.
In this notation, the formal power series in

\mathbb{C}[\hskip-1.0pt[px_{1}/x_{n},x_{2}/x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}/x_{n-1}]\hskip-1.0pt]

are simply expressed as

\varphi(x;p)=\sum_{\beta\in\mathsf{Q}^{\mathrm{af}}_{+}}\varphi_{\beta}x^{-\beta},\quad\varphi_{\beta}\in\mathbb{C}\ \ (\beta\in\mathsf{Q}^{\mathrm{af}}_{+}).

(4.48)

We recall the definition of the
modified elliptic Ruijsenaars operators

\mathcal{E}^{(r)}_{x,s}(p)

that we introduced in Subsection
2.3
:

\mathcal{E}^{(r)}_{x,s}(p)=\sum_{I\subseteq\left\{1,\ldots,n\right\};\ |I|=r}s^{\epsilon_{I}}\mathcal{B}_{I}(x;p)T_{q,x}^{\epsilon_{I}}\qquad(r=0,1,\ldots,n),

(4.49)

where

\mathcal{B}_{I}(x;p)=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i<j\leq n\\
i\in I;\,j\notin I\end{subarray}}\frac{\theta(x_{j}/tx_{i};p)}{\theta(x_{j}/x_{i};p)}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i<j\leq n\\
i\notin I;\,j\in I\end{subarray}}\frac{\theta(tx_{j}/x_{i};p)}{\theta(x_{j}/x_{i};p)}\qquad(I\subseteq\left\{1,\ldots,n\right\}).

(4.50)

Note that the coefficients
\mathcal{B}_{I}(x;p)
are expressed as

\mathcal{B}_{I}(x;p)=\sum_{\beta\in\mathsf{Q}^{{\mathrm{af}}}_{+}}x^{-\beta}\,b^{I}_{\beta}\in\mathbb{C}[\hskip-1.0pt[px_{1}/x_{n},x_{2}/x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}/x_{n-1}]\hskip-1.0pt],\quad b^{I}_{0}=1,

(4.51)

through the expansion in the domain

|px_{1}/x_{n}|<1
,
|x_{2}/x_{1}|<1
,
\ldots
,
|x_{n}/x_{n-1}|<1
.

In what follows, we regard
s=(s_{1},\ldots,s_{n})\in(\mathbb{C}^{\ast})^{n}
as fixed constants,
supposing that the genericity condition
s_{j}/s_{i}\notin q^{\mathbb{Z}}
(
1\leq i<j\leq n
) is satisfied.
In this setting, we investigate the joint eigenvalue problem

\mathcal{E}^{(r)}_{x,s}(p)\varphi(x;p)=\varepsilon^{(r)}(p)\varphi(x;p),\quad\varepsilon^{(r)}(0)=e_{r}(s)\qquad(r=1,\ldots,n)

(4.52)

for a formal power series

\varphi(x;p)=\sum_{\mu\in\beta\in\mathsf{Q}^{\mathrm{af}}_{+}}\varphi_{\beta}x^{-\beta}\in\mathbb{C}[\hskip-1.0pt[px_{1}/x_{n},x_{2}/x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}/x_{n-1}]\hskip-1.0pt]

(4.53)

with leading term
\varphi_{0}=1
.
Here we have suppressed the dependence on
s
in the notation of

\varphi(x;p)
and
\varepsilon^{(r)}(p)
.
We also use the generation functions

\mathcal{E}_{x,s}(p;u)=\sum_{r=0}^{n}(-u)^{r}\mathcal{E}^{(r)}_{x,s}(p),\quad\varepsilon(p;u)=\sum_{r=0}^{n}(-u)^{r}\varepsilon^{(r)}(p)

(4.54)

and consider the eigenfunction equation

\mathcal{E}_{x,s}(p;u)\varphi(x;p)=\varepsilon(p;u)\varphi(x;p),\quad\varepsilon(0;u)=\prod_{i=1}^{n}(1-s_{i}u),

(4.55)

with a parameter
u
.

In view of the expansion (
4.51
), we express the operator

\mathcal{E}_{x,s}(p;u)
as follows:

\begin{split}\mathcal{E}_{x,s}(p;u)&=\sum_{\beta\in\mathsf{Q}_{+}^{\mathrm{af}}}x^{-\beta}\,b_{\beta}(T_{q,x};u),\quad b_{\beta}(\xi;u)=\sum_{I\subseteq\left\{1,\ldots,n\right\}}(-u)^{|I|}b^{I}_{\beta}s^{\epsilon_{I}}\xi^{\epsilon_{I}},\end{split}

(4.56)

with symbols
\xi=(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n})
attached to
T_{q,x}=(T_{q,x_{1}},\ldots,T_{q,x_{n}})
.
Since
b^{I}_{0}=1
, we have

b_{0}(\xi;u)=\prod_{j=1}^{n}(1-us_{j}\xi_{j}).

(4.57)

Lemma 4.4

Suppose that
s_{j}/s_{i}\notin q^{\mathbb{Z}^{\ast}}

(1\leq i<j\leq n)
,
where
\mathbb{Z}^{\ast}=\mathbb{Z}\backslash\left\{0\right\}
.
Then, there exists a countable set
S\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{\ast}
such that, if
c\in\mathbb{C}^{\ast}\backslash S
,

\prod_{j=1}^{n}(1-cs_{j}q^{m_{j}})\neq\prod_{j=1}^{n}(1-cs_{j})

(4.58)

for any nonzero
m=(m_{1},\ldots,m_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
.

Proof
:
For each
m\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}\backslash\left\{0\right\}
,

g_{m}(u)=\prod_{j=1}^{m}(1-us_{j}q^{m_{j}})-\prod_{j=1}^{m}(1-us_{j})
is a nonzero
polynomial in
u
by the assumption
s_{j}/s_{i}\notin q^{\mathbb{Z}^{\ast}}
(
1\leq i<j\leq n)
.
(In fact,
suppose that there exists a nonzero
m\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
and
a permutation
\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}
such

s_{j}q^{m_{j}}=s_{\sigma(j)}
for
j=1,\ldots,m
.
Then, under our assumption that
|q|<1
,
for each index
i\in\left\{1,\ldots,n\right\}
such that
m_{i}\neq 0
, we have

\sigma(i)\neq i
and
s_{\sigma(i)}/s_{i}\in q^{\mathbb{Z}^{\ast}}
,
leading to a contradiction.)
Then, the set

S=\left\{{a\in\mathbb{C}^{\ast}}\big|{\ g_{m}(a)=0\ \mbox{for some}\ m\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}\backslash\left\{0\right\}}\right\}

(4.59)

is countable, and fulfills the condition of Lemma.

\square

Fixing a
generic
constant
c\in\mathbb{C}^{\ast}\backslash S
in this sense,
we investigate the eigenfunction equation

\mathcal{E}_{x,s}(p;c)f(x;p)=\varepsilon(p;c)f(x;p),\quad\varepsilon(p;c)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}p^{k}\varepsilon_{k}(c)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}x^{-k\delta}\varepsilon_{k}(c),

(4.60)

where

f(x;p)=\sum_{\mu\in\mathsf{Q}^{{\mathrm{af}}}_{+}}x^{-\mu}\,f_{\mu}\in\mathbb{C}[\hskip-1.0pt[px_{1}/x_{n},x_{2}/x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}/x_{n-1}]\hskip-1.0pt].

(4.61)

We have suppressed the dependence of
f(x;p)
and
\varepsilon(p;u)
on the
s
parameters in order to simplify the notation.
The eigenfunction equation (
4.60
)
is then equivalent to the recurrence relations

\sum_{\beta+\nu=\mu}b_{\beta}(q^{-\nu};c)f_{\nu}=\sum_{k\delta+\nu=\mu}\varepsilon_{k}(c)f_{\nu}\qquad(\mu\in\mathsf{Q}^{{\mathrm{af}}}_{+})

(4.62)

for the coefficients of
f(x;p)
.
In view of condition

b_{0}(1;c)f_{0}=\varepsilon_{0}(c)f_{0},

(4.63)

of the case
\mu=0
,
we start with

f_{0}=1,\quad\varepsilon_{0}(c)=b_{0}(1;c)=\prod_{j=1}^{n}(1-cs_{j}).

(4.64)

For
\mu>0
(i.e.
\mu\in\mathsf{Q}^{\mathrm{af}}_{+}
,
\mu\neq 0
), we have

(b_{0}(q^{-\mu};c)-\varepsilon_{0}(c))f_{\mu}+\sum_{\beta+\nu=\mu;\,\beta>0}b_{\beta}(q^{-\nu};c)f_{\nu}=\sum_{k\delta+\nu=\mu;\,k>0}\varepsilon_{k}(c)f_{\nu}.

(4.65)

Note here that, if
\mu=k_{0}\alpha_{0}+k_{1}\alpha_{1}+\cdots+k_{n-1}\alpha_{n-1}\in\mathsf{Q}^{\mathrm{af}}_{+}
,
we have

\left\langle\epsilon_{j},\mu\right\rangle=k_{j}-k_{j-1}\quad(j=1,\ldots,n),

(4.66)

with the convention
k_{n}=k_{0}
, and hence

b_{0}(q^{-\mu};c)=\prod_{j=1}^{n}(1-cs_{j}q^{-k_{j}+k_{j-1}}).

(4.67)

If
\mu\notin\mathbb{Z}_{>0}\delta
, there exists an index
i\in\left\{1,\ldots,n\right\}

such that
k_{i}-k_{i-1}\neq 0
,
and hence

\prod_{j=1}^{n}(1-cs_{j}q^{-k_{j}+k_{j-1}})\neq\prod_{j=1}^{n}(1-cs_{j})

(4.68)

by the genericity assumption of
c
.
Since
b_{0}(q^{-\mu};c)\neq\varepsilon_{0}(c)
,
by the recurrence relation (
4.65
)

f_{\mu}
is determined uniquely
from
f_{\nu}
(
\nu<\mu
) and
\varepsilon_{k}(c)
with
\mathrm{ht}(k\delta)=kn<\mathrm{ht}(\mu)
.

When
\mu=l\delta
(
l>0
), we have

b_{0}(q^{-l\delta};u)=b_{0}(1;c)=\varepsilon_{0}(c)
.
Hence the recurrence relation is written as

\sum_{\beta+\nu=l\delta;\,\beta>0}b_{\beta}(q^{-\nu};c)f_{\nu}=\varepsilon_{l}(c)+\sum_{k\delta+\nu=l\delta;\,0<k<l}\varepsilon_{k}(c)f_{\nu}.

(4.69)

This implies that

\varepsilon_{l}(c)
is determined uniquely from
f_{\nu}

(
\nu<l\delta
) and
\varepsilon_{k}(c)
(
k<l
), and
f_{l\delta}
can be fixed arbitrarily.
Since this ambiguity of
f_{k\delta}
(
k>0
) corresponds to the multiplication
by an element
\gamma(p)\in\mathbb{C}[\hskip-1.0pt[p]\hskip-1.0pt]
with
\gamma(0)=1
, we adopt here the
normalization

f_{0}=1,\quad f_{k\delta}=0\quad\mbox{for all}\quad k>0.

(4.70)

Under this normalization, the recurrence relation for
\varepsilon_{l}(u)

(l>0)
simplifies as

\varepsilon_{l}(c)=\sum_{\beta+\nu=l\delta;\,\beta>0}b_{\beta}(q^{-\nu};c)f_{\nu}.

(4.71)

This procedure implies the unique existence of a normalized formal solution

f(x;p)
. Also, one can verify that any eigenfunction is
expressed as
\gamma(p)f(x;p)
with some
\gamma(p)\in\mathbb{C}[\hskip-1.0pt[p]\hskip-1.0pt]
,
by a similar argument to the one we used in Subsection
4.3
.

We now prove that
f(x;p)
is a joint eigenfunction for all

\mathcal{E}_{x,s}^{(r)}(p)
(
r=0,1,\ldots,n
).
Since
\mathcal{E}_{x,s}^{(r)}(p)
commutes with
\mathcal{E}_{x,s}(p;c)
, we have

\mathcal{E}_{x,s}(p;c)\mathcal{E}_{x,s}^{(r)}(p)f(x;p)=\mathcal{E}_{x,s}^{(r)}(p)\mathcal{E}_{x,s}(p;c)f(x;p)=\varepsilon(p;c)\mathcal{E}_{x,s}^{(r)}(p)f(x;p).

(4.72)

This means that
g_{r}(x;p)=\mathcal{E}_{x,s}^{(r)}(p)f(x;p)
is a formal eigenfunction
of
\mathcal{E}_{x,s}(p;c)
, and hence

g_{r}(x;p)=\varepsilon^{(r)}(p)f(x;p)
for some
\varepsilon^{(r)}(p)\in\mathbb{C}[\hskip-1.0pt[p]\hskip-1.0pt]
,
namely,

\mathcal{E}_{x,s}^{(r)}(p)f(x;p)=\varepsilon^{(r)}(p)f(x;p)
for
r=0,1,\ldots,n
.
This also implies that the normalized formal solution
f(x;p)

satisfies

\mathcal{E}_{x,s}(p;u)f(x;p)=\varepsilon(p;u)f(x;p),\quad\varepsilon(p;u)=\sum_{r=0}^{n}(-u)^{r}\varepsilon^{(r)}(p)

(4.73)

for arbitrary
u\in\mathbb{C}
.
In particular,
f(x;p)
does not depend on the choice of
c\in\mathbb{C}^{\ast}\backslash S

that we started with.
This completes the proof of Theorem
2.6
.

If we regard as

and

(\beta\in\mathsf{Q}^{\mathrm{af}}_{+}
) of

and then apply an integral transform to them

(\lambda\in\mathsf{P}_{+})

We also include a proof of Theorem
2.4
on the orthogonality

\mathcal{P}_{\lambda}(x;p)

5.1
Ruijsenaars functions in the asymptotic domain

|x_{1}|\gg\cdots\gg|x_{n}|\gg|px_{1}|

In what follows, we fix the parameters
s=(s_{1},\ldots,s_{n})\in(\mathbb{C}^{\ast})^{n}

s_{j}/s_{i}\notin q^{\mathbb{Z}}

Keeping the notation of Subsection
4.4

we denote by

f(x;p)
of the normalized formal solution of the eigenfunction equation

\mathcal{E}_{x,s}(p;u)f(x;p)=\varepsilon(p;u)f(x;p),\quad f(x;p)\in\mathbb{C}[\hskip-1.0pt[px_{1}/x_{n},x_{2}/x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}/x_{n-1}]\hskip-1.0pt]

(5.1)

in the asymptotic domain
|x_{1}|\gg\cdots\gg|x_{n}|\gg|px_{1}|
.
(We write
f(x;p)=f(x;s;p)

\varepsilon(p;u)=\varepsilon(s;p;u)

As before, we expand the modified elliptic Ruijsenaars operator in the form

\mathcal{E}_{x,s}(p;u)=\sum_{I\subseteq\left\{1,\ldots,n\right\}}(-u)^{|I|}s^{\epsilon_{I}}\mathcal{B}_{I}(x;p)T_{q,x}^{\epsilon_{I}}=\sum_{\beta\in\mathsf{Q}^{\mathrm{af}}_{+}}x^{-\beta}b_{\beta}(T_{q,x};u),

Then the formal power series

and

(5.6)

Our goal is to establish estimates for the coefficients

and
\varepsilon_{k}(u)

satisfying these recurrence formulas, so as to guarantee the absolute convergence of

and
\varepsilon(p;u)

As far as the condition

(

will be uniform on every compact subset

Setting
U=\left\{{q\in\mathbb{C}^{\ast}}\big|{\ |q|<1}\right\}

of the space of parameters

Lemma 5.1

For any compact subset
K\subseteq D
, there exist
real constants
\tau_{0}\in(0,1]
and
\tau_{1}>0
such that

\tau_{0}\leq|s_{j}/s_{i}|\leq\tau_{0}^{-1},\quad|1-q^{l}s_{j}/s_{i}|\geq\tau_{1}\quad(l\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0})

(5.8)

for any distinct
i,j\in\left\{1,\ldots,n\right\}
.

Proof
:
We first take
\tau_{0}\in(0,1]
and
\rho\in(0,1)
so that

\tau_{0}\leq|s_{j}/s_{i}|\leq\tau_{0}^{-1}

(i,j\in\left\{1,\ldots,n\right\};\ i\neq j)

and
|q|\leq\rho
for all
(s;q)\in K
.
Let
l_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
be sufficient large so that
\rho^{l_{0}}\leq\tau_{0}
.
Then, for
l>l_{0}
, we have

|q^{l}s_{j}/s_{i}|\leq\rho^{l_{0}+1}\tau_{0}^{-1}\leq\rho

hence
|1-q^{l}s_{j}/s_{i}|\geq 1-\rho>0
on
K
,
for any distinct
i,j
.
The condition of Lemma is fulfilled if we let
\tau_{1}>0

sufficiently small so that
\tau_{1}\leq 1-\rho
and

|1-q^{l}s_{j}/s_{i}|\geq\tau_{1}

(0\leq l\leq l_{0})
on
K
,
for any distinct
i,j
.

\square

From now on, we assume that the parameters
(s;q,t)
belong
to an arbitrary compact subset of
\in D\times\mathbb{C}^{\ast}
,
and fix two real constants
\tau_{0}\in(0,1]
and
\tau_{1}>0

as in Lemma

.

As we already remarked in Subsection 2.2,
the coefficients
\mathcal{B}_{I}(x;p)
of
\mathcal{E}_{x,s}(p;u)
are holomorphic
functions in the variables
z=(z_{0},z_{1},\ldots,z_{n-1})
, defined by

z_{0}=px_{1}/x_{n},\ z_{1}=x_{2}/x_{1},\ \ldots,\ z_{n-1}=x_{n}/x_{n-1},

(5.9)

in the polydisc
|z_{0}|<1
,
|z_{1}|<1
,
\ldots
,
|z_{n-1}|<1

5.2
Elliptic deformation of Macdonald polynomials

We now proceed to the problem of
symmetric joint eigenfunctions around the torus
\mathbb{T}^{n}
.
We apply an idea of integral transforms, similar to that we already
presented in Section
3
,
to construct elliptic deformations of Macdonald polynomials from
Ruijsenaars functions in the asymptotic domain
|y_{1}|\gg\cdots\gg|y_{n}|\gg|py_{1}|
.

Recall that the
Ruijsenaars elliptic gamma function
is defined by

\Gamma(z;p,q)=\frac{(pq/z;p,q)_{\infty}}{(z;p,q)_{\infty}},\quad(z;p,q)_{\infty}=\prod_{i,j=0}^{\infty}(1-p^{i}q^{j}z).

(5.52)

Note that
\Gamma(z;p,q)
is a meromorphic function on
\mathbb{C}^{\ast}
, and satisfies the
functional equations

\Gamma(qz;p,q)=\theta(z;p)\Gamma(z;p,q),\ \ \Gamma(pz;p,q)=\theta(z;q)\Gamma(z;p,q),\ \ \Gamma(pq/z;p,q)=\Gamma(z;p,q)^{-1}.

(5.53)

In order to formulate an integral transform in the elliptic case, we use the following
elliptic deformation of
K(x,y)
and
w(y)
:

K(x,y;p)=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\prod_{j=1}^{n}\frac{\Gamma(x_{i}/y_{j};p,q)}{\Gamma(tx_{i}/y_{j};p,q)},\quad w(y;p)=\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq n}\theta(y_{j}/y_{i};p)\frac{\Gamma(ty_{j}/y_{i};p,q)}{\Gamma(qy_{j}/ty_{i};p,q)}.

(5.54)

The weight function
w(y;p)
is holomorphic in the domain

|pt|<|y_{j}/y_{i}|<|t|^{-1}\qquad(1\leq i<j\leq n).

(5.55)

Also, the kernel function
K(x,y;p)
is holomorphic in the domain

|pq/t|<|x_{i}/y_{j}|<1\qquad(i,j=1,\ldots,n).

(5.56)

Supposing that
0<\theta\leq\min\left\{1,1/|t|,|t/q|\right\}
and
|p|<\theta^{n}
, we define the domain
\mathcal{V}_{\theta}\subset(\mathbb{C}^{\ast})^{n}
by

\mathcal{V}_{\theta}=\left\{{y=(y_{1},\ldots,y_{n})\in(\mathbb{C}^{\ast})^{n}\,}\big|{\ |y_{2}/y_{1}|<\theta,\ \ldots,\ |y_{n}/y_{n-1}|<\theta,\ |py_{1}/y_{n}|<\theta}\right\}.

(5.57)

For any
r>0
and
\sigma>0
such that
|p|\leq\sigma^{n}<\theta^{n}
, we take the
n
-cycle

C_{r,\sigma}=\left\{{y=(y_{1},\ldots,y_{n})\in(\mathbb{C}^{\ast})^{n}}\big|{\ |y_{i}|=\sigma^{i-1}r\ \ (i=1,\ldots,n)}\right\}\subset(\mathbb{C}^{\ast})^{n}

(5.58)

as in the trigonometric case.
Then
C_{r,\sigma}
is contained in
\mathcal{V}_{\theta}
.
Also, since
|pt|\leq\sigma^{n}|t|<\sigma^{n-1}
,
w(y;p)
is holomorphic in an neighborhood
of
C_{r,\sigma}
.
On the other hand, if
y\in C_{r,\sigma}
,
K(x,y;p)
is holomorphic in the domain

\mathcal{U}_{r,\sigma}=\left\{{x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\in(\mathbb{C}^{\ast})^{n}}\big|{\ |pq/t|r<|x_{i}|<\sigma^{n-1}r\ \ (i=1,\ldots,n)}\right\}.

(5.59)

Since
|pq/t|<\sigma^{n-1}
,
\mathcal{U}_{r,\sigma}
is not empty.
Note also that, if
x\in\mathcal{U}_{r,\sigma}
, then
|pq/t|\sigma^{-n+1}<|x_{j}/x_{i}|<|t/pq|\sigma^{n-1}
.
In view of this fact, we set

\mathcal{U}_{\sigma}=\left\{{x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\in(\mathbb{C}^{\ast})^{n}}\big|{\ |pq/t|\sigma^{-n+1}<|x_{j}/x_{i}|<|t/pq|\sigma^{n-1}\ \ (1\leq i<j\leq n)}\right\},

(5.60)

so that
\mathcal{U}_{r,\sigma}\subseteq\mathcal{U}_{\sigma}
for any
r>0
.

Lemma 5.5

Let
\theta
be a positive constant such that
\theta\leq\min\left\{1,1/|t|,|t/q|\right\}

and suppose that

.
For each holomorphic function

in the domain

consider the holomorphic function

(5.61)

taking

and
\sigma>0
such that
\sigma<\theta

Then

\begin{split}&\left\langle\mathcal{D}_{x}(p;u)\varphi(x;p),\psi(x;p)\right\rangle\\
&=\int_{\mathbb{T}}(\mathcal{D}_{x^{-1}}(p;u)\varphi(x^{-1};p))\psi(x;p)d\omega_{n}(x)\\
&=\int_{\mathbb{T}}\mathcal{D}_{x}(p;u)^{\ast}(w^{\mathrm{sym}}(x;p)\varphi(x^{-1};p))\psi(x;p)d\omega_{n}(x)\\
&=\sum_{I\subseteq\left\{1,\ldots,n\right\}}(-u)^{|I|}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}\mathcal{A}_{I}(q^{-\epsilon_{I}}x;p)w^{\mathrm{sym}}(q^{-\epsilon_{I}}x;p)\varphi(q^{\epsilon_{I}}x^{-1};p)\psi(x;p)d\omega_{n}(x)\\
&=\sum_{I\subseteq\left\{1,\ldots,n\right\}}(-u)^{|I|}\int_{q^{-\epsilon_{I}}\mathbb{T}^{n}}\mathcal{A}_{I}(x;p)w^{\mathrm{sym}}(x;p)\varphi(x^{-1};p)\psi(q^{\epsilon_{I}}x;p)d\omega_{n}(x).\end{split}

(5.108)

Note here that, for each
I\subseteq\left\{1,\ldots,n\right\}
,
the poles of
\mathcal{A}_{I}(q^{-\epsilon_{I}})
are cancelled
by the zeros of
w^{\mathrm{sym}}(q^{-\epsilon_{I}};p)
.
Hence, under the condition
|t|<|q|<1
, the
n
-cycle
q^{-\epsilon_{I}}\mathbb{T}

is homologous to
\mathbb{T}
in the domain of holomorphy of the integrand.
Applying Cauchy’s theorem, we obtain

\begin{split}&\left\langle\mathcal{D}_{x}(p;u)\varphi(x;p),\psi(x;p)\right\rangle\\
&=\sum_{I\subseteq\left\{1,\ldots,n\right\}}(-u)^{|I|}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}\varphi(x^{-1};p)\mathcal{A}_{I}(x;p)\psi(q^{\epsilon_{I}}x;p)w^{\mathrm{sym}}(x;p)d\omega_{n}(x)\\
&=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}\varphi(x^{-1};p)\mathcal{D}_{x}(p;u)(\psi(q^{\epsilon_{I}}x;p))w^{\mathrm{sym}}(x;p)d\omega_{n}(x)\\
&=\left\langle\varphi(x;p),\mathcal{D}_{x}(p;u)\psi(x;p)\right\rangle.\end{split}

(5.109)

Let
\lambda,\mu\in\mathsf{P}_{+}
be two dominant integral weights,
and suppose that
\lambda\neq\mu
.
Then we have

\left\langle\mathcal{D}_{x}(p;u)\mathcal{P}_{\lambda}(x;p),\mathcal{P}_{\mu}(x;p)\right\rangle=\left\langle\mathcal{P}_{\lambda}(x;p),\mathcal{D}_{x}(p;u)\mathcal{P}_{\mu}(x;p)\right\rangle,

(5.110)

and hence

\varepsilon_{\lambda}(p;u)\left\langle\mathcal{P}_{\lambda}(x;p),\mathcal{P}_{\mu}(x;p)\right\rangle=\varepsilon_{\mu}(p;u)\left\langle\mathcal{P}_{\lambda}(x;p),\mathcal{P}_{\mu}(x;p)\right\rangle.

(5.111)

Since
\varepsilon_{\lambda}(p;u)\neq\varepsilon_{\mu}(p;u)
,
we obtain
\left\langle\mathcal{P}_{\lambda}(x;p),\mathcal{P}_{\mu}(x;p)\right\rangle=0
.
This completes the proof of Theorem
2.4
.

6
Symmetries of the Ruijsenaars function

In this section, we give proofs of
Theorems
2.8
and
2.9

on symmetries of the normalized Ruijsenaars function
f(x;s;p|q,t)

in the asymptotic domain
|x_{1}|\gg\cdots\gg|x_{n}|\gg|px_{1}|
.

6.1
Symmetry of
f(x;s;p|q,t)

with respect the rotation of variables

In this subsection, we give a proof of Theorem
2.8
.

Recall that the modified Ruijsenaars operator
\mathcal{E}_{x,s}(p;u)
is defined by

\mathcal{E}_{x,s}(p;u)=\sum_{I\subseteq\left\{1,\ldots,n\right\}}(-u)^{|I|}s^{\epsilon_{I}}\mathcal{B}_{I}(x;p)T_{q,x}^{\epsilon_{I}},

(6.1)

where

\mathcal{B}_{I}(x;p)=\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq n}\frac{\theta(t^{-\left\langle\epsilon_{I},\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{j}\right\rangle}x_{j}/x_{i};p)}{\theta(x_{j}/x_{i};p)}.

(6.2)

We denote by
\pi
the automorphism of the affine root lattice
\mathsf{Q}^{{\mathrm{af}}}
such that

\pi(\alpha_{i})=\alpha_{i+1}\quad(i=1,\ldots,n-1),\quad\pi(\alpha_{n-1})=\alpha_{0},

(6.3)

and by the same symbol the corresponding transformation of the
x
and
s
variables:

\begin{split}&\pi(x_{i})=x_{i+1}\quad(i=1,\ldots,n-1),\quad\pi(x_{n})=px_{1},\\
&\pi(s_{i})=s_{i+1}\quad(i=1,\ldots,n-1),\quad\pi(s_{n})=s_{1}.\end{split}

(6.4)

Then we have

\begin{split}\pi(\mathcal{B}_{I}(x;p))&=\prod_{2\leq i<j\leq n}\frac{\theta(t^{-\left\langle\epsilon_{I},\epsilon_{i-1}-\epsilon_{j-1}\right\rangle}x_{j}/x_{i};p)}{\theta(x_{j}/x_{i};p)}\cdot\prod_{j=2}^{n}\frac{\theta(t^{-\left\langle\epsilon_{I},\epsilon_{j-1}-\epsilon_{n}\right\rangle}px_{1}/x_{j};p)}{\theta(px_{1}/x_{j};p)}\\
&=\prod_{2\leq i<j\leq n}\frac{\theta(t^{-\left\langle\epsilon_{\pi(I)},\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{j}\right\rangle}x_{j}/x_{i};p)}{\theta(x_{j}/x_{i};p)}\cdot\prod_{j=2}^{n}\frac{\theta(t^{-\left\langle\epsilon_{\pi}(I),\epsilon_{1}-\epsilon_{j}\right\rangle}x_{j}/x_{1};p)}{\theta(x_{j}/x_{1};p)}\\
&=\mathcal{B}_{\pi(I)}(x;p)\end{split}

(6.5)

with the action of
\pi
on the indexing set
\left\{1,\ldots,n\right\}

specified by
\pi(i)=i+1
(
i=1,\ldots,n-1
) and
\pi(n)=1
.
Hence we obtain

\begin{split}\pi(\mathcal{E}_{x,s}(p;u))&=\sum_{I\subseteq\left\{1,\ldots,n\right\}}(-u)^{|I|}s^{\epsilon_{\pi(I)}}\pi(\mathcal{B}_{I}(x;p))T_{q,x}^{\epsilon_{\pi(I)}}\\
&=\sum_{I\subseteq\left\{1,\ldots,n\right\}}(-u)^{|I|}s^{\epsilon_{\pi(I)}}\mathcal{B}_{\pi(I)}(x;p)T_{q,x}^{\epsilon_{\pi(I)}}\\
&=\sum_{I\subseteq\left\{1,\ldots,n\right\}}(-u)^{|I|}s^{\epsilon_{I}}\mathcal{B}_{I}(x;p)T_{q,x}^{\epsilon_{I}}=\mathcal{E}_{x,s}(x;p).\end{split}

(6.6)

We apply
\pi
to the eigenfunction equation

\mathcal{E}_{x,s}(p;u)f(x;s;p)=\varepsilon(s;p;u)f(x;s;p)

(6.7)

to obtain

\pi(\mathcal{E}_{x,s}(p;u))\pi(f(x;s;p))=\pi(\varepsilon(s;p;u))\pi(f(x;s;p))

(6.8)

and hence

\mathcal{E}_{x,s}(p;u)\pi(f(x;s;p))=\pi(\varepsilon(s;p;u))\pi(f(x;s;p)).

(6.9)

Recall that the function
f(x;s;p)
is normalized so that the constant term with respect to
x
variables
should be 1.
Since
\pi
does not change the constant term of
f(x;s;p)
, it follows
that
\pi(f(x;s;p))=f(x;s;p)
and
\pi(\varepsilon(s;p;u))=\varepsilon(s;p;u)
.
This completes the proof of Theorem
2.8
.

6.2
Transformation of
f(x;s;p|q,t)

under the reflection
t\leftrightarrow q/t

In this subsection, we give a proof of Theorem
2.9
.

We modify
f(x;s;p)
by setting

\psi(x,s;p)=G(x;p)f(x;s;p),\quad G(x;p)=\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq n}\frac{\Gamma(tx_{j}/x_{i};p,q)}{\Gamma(x_{j}/x_{i};p,q)},

(6.10)

and denote by
{}^{G}\mathcal{E}_{x,s}(p;u)
the conjugation of
\mathcal{E}_{x,s}(p;u)
by
G(x;p)

:

{}^{G}\mathcal{E}_{x,s}(p;u)=G(x;p)\mathcal{E}_{x,s}(p;u)G(x;p)^{-1}.

(6.11)

For each subset
I\subseteq\left\{1,\ldots,n\right\}
, we have

\mathcal{B}_{I}(x;p)=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i<j\leq n\\
i\in I\,j\notin I\end{subarray}}\frac{\theta(x_{j}/tx_{i};p)}{\theta(x_{j}/x_{i};p)}\cdot\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i<j\leq n\\
i\notin I\,j\in I\end{subarray}}\frac{\theta(tx_{j}/x_{i};p)}{\theta(x_{j}/x_{i};p)}

(6.12)

and also,

\begin{split}\frac{T_{q,x}^{\epsilon_{I}}G(x;p)}{G(x;p)}&=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i<j\leq n\\
i\in I,\,j\notin I\end{subarray}}\frac{\Gamma(tx_{j}/qx_{i};p,q)}{\Gamma(x_{j}/qx_{i};p,q)}\frac{\Gamma(x_{j}/x_{i};p,q)}{\Gamma(tx_{j}/x_{i};p,q)}\\
&\quad\cdot\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i<j\leq n\\
i\notin I,\,j\in I\end{subarray}}\frac{\Gamma(qtx_{j}/x_{i};p,q)}{\Gamma(qx_{j}/x_{i};p,q)}\frac{\Gamma(x_{j}/x_{i};p,q)}{\Gamma(tx_{j}/x_{i};p,q)}\\
&=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i<j\leq n\\
i\in I,\,j\notin I\end{subarray}}\frac{\theta(x_{j}/qx_{i};p)}{\theta(tx_{j}/qx_{i};p)}\cdot\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i<j\leq n\\
i\notin I,\,j\in I\end{subarray}}\frac{\theta(tx_{j}/x_{i};p)}{\theta(x_{j}/x_{i};p)}.\end{split}

(6.13)

Hence we have

{}^{G}\mathcal{E}_{x,s}(p;u)=\sum_{I\subseteq\left\{1,\ldots,n\right\}}(-u)^{|I|}s^{\epsilon_{I}}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i<j\leq n\\
i\in I,\,j\notin I\end{subarray}}\frac{\theta(x_{j}/tx_{i};p)\theta(tx_{j}/qx_{i};p)}{\theta(x_{j}/x_{i};p)\theta(x_{j}/qx_{i};p)}T_{q,x}^{\epsilon_{I}},

(6.14)

which is manifestly invariant under the reflection
t\leftrightarrow q/t
of the parameter
t
.
Note that the eigenfunction equation

\mathcal{E}_{x,s}(p;u)f(x;s;p)=\varepsilon(s;p;u)f(x;s;p)

(6.15)

of
f(x;s;p)
implies

{}^{G}\mathcal{E}_{x,s}(p;u)\psi(x,s;p)=\varepsilon(s;p;u)\psi(x,s;p).

(6.16)

Denoting by
\tau
the reflection
t\to q/t
of the parameter
t
,
we apply
\tau
to this equation.
Since
{}^{G}\mathcal{E}_{x,s}(p;u)
is invariant under
\tau
, we have

{}^{G}\mathcal{E}_{x,s}(p;u)\tau(\psi(x,s;p))=\tau(\varepsilon(s;p;u))\tau(\psi(x,s;p))

(6.17)

and hence

\mathcal{E}_{x,s}(p;u)G(x;p)^{-1}\tau(\psi(x,s;p))=\tau(\varepsilon(s;p;u))G(x;p)^{-1}\tau(\psi(x,s;p)).

(6.18)

This means that

G(x;p)^{-1}\tau(\psi(x;p))=\frac{\tau(G(x;p))}{G(x;p)}\tau(f(x;s;p))

(6.19)

is an eigenfunction of
\mathcal{E}_{x,s}(p;u)
, and
it is also holomorphic in the domain
|x_{1}|\gg\cdots|x_{n}|\gg|px_{1}|

and has leading term 1.
Hence we see that

\frac{\tau(G(x;p))}{G(x;p)}\tau(f(x;s;p))=\gamma(s;p)f(x;s;p)

(6.20)

for some power series
\gamma(s;p)
of
p
, and that

\tau(\varepsilon(s;p,u))=\varepsilon(s;p;u)
.
This implies that

\begin{split}\tau(f(x;s;p))&=\gamma(s;p)\frac{G(x;p)}{\tau(G(x;p))}f(x;s;p)\\
&=\gamma(s;p)\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq n}\frac{\Gamma(tx_{j}/x_{i};p,q)}{\Gamma(qx_{j}/tx_{i};p,q)}\cdot f(x;s;p),\end{split}

(6.21)

namely

f(x;s;p|q,q/t)=\gamma(s;p|q,t)\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq n}\frac{\Gamma(tx_{j}/x_{i};p,q)}{\Gamma(qx_{j}/tx_{i};p,q)}\cdot f(x;s;p|q,t).

(6.22)

By the normalization condition of
f(x;s;p|q,t)
and
f(x;s;p|q,q/t)
,
we see that

\gamma(s,p|q,t)
is determined as the constant term of

f(x;s;p|q,q/t)\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq n}\frac{\Gamma(qx_{j}/tx_{i};p,q)}{\Gamma(tx_{j}/x_{i};p,q)}

(6.23)

with respect to
x
,
when regarded as a power series in
\mathbb{C}[\hskip-1.0pt[px_{1}/x_{n},x_{2}/x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}/x_{n-1}]\hskip-1.0pt]
.
Also, from the symmetry (
6.22
), it follows that
\gamma(s;p|q,t)
satisfies the functional equation

\gamma(s;p|q,t)\gamma(s;p|q,q/t)=1.

(6.24)

This completes the proof of Theorem
2.9
.

7
Concluding remarks

We constructed two kinds of eigenfunctions of the commuting family of difference operators defining the elliptic Ruijsenaars system.
The first kind of eigenfunction provide an elliptic deformation of the Macdonald polynomials
[
16
]
, and the second kind generalize the asymptotically free eigenfunctions in
[
20
]
.
We proved that these eigenfunctions define analytic functions in suitable domains of the variables and parameters.
By restricting parameters of variables to the physical domain, the first kind of eigenfunctions have a Hilbert space interpretation which allows to interpret them as quantum mechanical wave functions diagonalizing the Hamiltonian and momentum operator defining the elliptic Ruijsenaars system
[
21
]
.
In order to substantiate this interpretation, however,
it would be desirable
to refine our results to guarantee the existence of a common domain
of convergence which does not depend on
\lambda
.

We recall the hyperbolic Ruijsenaars model provides a quantum mechanical description of a relativistic quantum field theory known as sine-Gordon theory
[
21
]
and, motivated by this, the construction of eigenfunctions of the hyperbolic Ruijsenaars system was recently completed by Hallnäs and Ruijsenaars
[
8
]
; in this model, space is the real line
\mathbb{R}
and, for this reason, the spectrum of the Hamiltonian and momentum operator are purely continuous.
We regard the elliptic Ruijsenaars system as regularization of the hyperbolic one: space
\mathbb{R}
is replaced by a circle of circumference
L>0
and, by this, the continuous spectrum is changed to a pure point spectrum.
Such a regularization is standard in quantum field theory, and we therefore hope that our results on the elliptic system will be useful in future work to better understand quantum sine-Gordon theory.

In the present paper, we concentrated on the analytic properties of the eigenfunctions we considered, and we did not attempt to obtain explicit formulas for these functions.
The later certainly would be very interesting.
In fact, one of us (S) recently proposed a fully explicit formula for the functions

[

]
implies that a function

Two applications of Grunsky coefficients in the theory of univalent functions
Milutin Obradović, Nikola Tuneski

Abstract
. Let 𝒮 \mathcal{S} denote the class of functions f f which are analytic and univalent in the unit disk 𝔻 = { z : | z | < 1 } {\mathbb{D}}=\{z:|z|<1\} and normalized with f ⁡ ( z ) = z + ∑ n = 2 ∞ a n ​ z n f(z)=z+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n} . Using a method based on Grusky coefficients we study two problems over the class 𝒮 \mathcal{S} : estimate of the fourth logarithmic coefficient and upper bound of the coefficient difference | a 5 | − | a 4 | |a_{5}|-|a_{4}| .

1.
Introduction and definitions

Let
\mathcal{A}
be the class of functions
f
which are analytic in the open unit disc
{\mathbb{D}}=\{z:|z|<1\}
of the form

(1)

f(z)=z+a_{2}z^{2}+a_{3}z^{3}+\cdots,

and let
\mathcal{S}
be the subclass of
\mathcal{A}
consisting of functions that are univalent in
{\mathbb{D}}
.

For
f\in\mathcal{S}
the logarithmic coefficients,
\gamma_{n}
, are defined by

(2)

\log\frac{f(z)}{z}=2\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\gamma_{n}z^{n}.

Relatively little exact information is known about these coefficients. The natural conjecture
|\gamma_{n}|\leq 1/n
, inspired by the Koebe function (whose logarithmic coefficients are
1/n
) is false even in order of magnitude (see Duren
[
2
, Section 8.1]
).
For the class
\mathcal{S}
the sharp estimates of single logarithmic coefficients are known only for
\gamma_{1}
and
\gamma_{2}
, namely,

|\gamma_{1}|\leq 1\quad\mbox{and}\quad|\gamma_{2}|\leq\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{e}=0.635\ldots,

and are unknown for
n\geq 3
. In
[
7
]
the authors gave the estimate
|\gamma_{3}|\leq 0.5566178\ldots
for the class
\mathcal{S}
. In this paper for the same class we give the estimation
|\gamma_{4}|\leq 0.51059\ldots
.
For the subclasses of univalent functions the situation is not a great deal better. Only the estimates of the initial logarithmic coefficients are available. For details see
[
1
]
.

Another problem is finding sharp upper and lower bounds of the coefficient difference
|a_{n+1}|-|a_{n}|
over the class of univalent functions. Since the Keobe function has coefficients
a_{n}=n
, it is natural to conjecture that
||a_{n+1}|-|a_{n}||\leq 1
. But this is false even when
n=2,
due to Fekete and Szegö (
[
3
]
) who obtained the sharp bounds

-1\leq|a_{3}|-|a_{2}|\leq\frac{3}{4}+e^{-\lambda_{0}}(2e^{-\lambda_{0}}-1)=1.029\ldots,

where
\lambda_{0}
is the unique solution of the equation
4\lambda=e^{\lambda}
on the interval
(0,1)
.
Hayman in
[
4
]
showed that if
f\in{\mathcal{S}}
, then
||a_{n+1}|-|a_{n}||\leq C
, where
C
is an absolute constant
and the best estimate of
C
is
3.61\ldots
(
[
5
]
). In the case when
n=3
in
[
7
]
, the authors improved this to
1.751853\ldots
.
In this paper we also consider the difference
|a_{5}|-|a_{4}|
.

For the study of the problems defined above we will use method based on Grunsky coefficients.
In the proofs we will use mainly the notations and results given in the book of N. A. Lebedev (
[
6
]
).

Here are basic definitions and results.

Let
f\in\mathcal{S}
and let

\log\frac{f(t)-f(z)}{t-z}=\sum_{p,q=0}^{\infty}\omega_{p,q}t^{p}z^{q},

where
\omega_{p,q}
are so called Grunsky’s coefficients with property
\omega_{p,q}=\omega_{q,p}
.
For those coefficients we have the next Grunsky’s inequality (
[
2
,
6
]
):

(3)

\sum_{q=1}^{\infty}q\left|\sum_{p=1}^{\infty}\omega_{p,q}x_{p}\right|^{2}\leq\sum_{p=1}^{\infty}\frac{|x_{p}|^{2}}{p},

where
x_{p}
are arbitrary complex numbers such that last series converges.

Further, it is well-known that if
f
given by (
1
)
belongs to
\mathcal{S}
, then also

(4)

f_{2}(z)=\sqrt{f(z^{2})}=z+c_{3}z^{3}+c_{5}z^{5}+\cdots

belongs to the class
\mathcal{S}
. So, for the function
f_{2}
we have the appropriate Grunsky’s
coefficients of the form
\omega_{2p-1,2q-1}^{(2)}
and inequality (
3
) reaches the form:

(5)

\sum_{q=1}^{\infty}(2q-1)\left|\sum_{p=1}^{\infty}\omega_{2p-1,2q-1}x_{2p-1}\right|^{2}\leq\sum_{p=1}^{\infty}\frac{|x_{2p-1}|^{2}}{2p-1}.

Here, and further in the paper we omit the upper index ”(2)” in
\omega_{2p-1,2q-1}^{(2)}
if compared with Lebedev’s notation.

From inequality (
5
), when
x_{2p-1}=0
and
p=3,4,\ldots
, we have

(6)

\begin{split}&\quad|\omega_{11}x_{1}+\omega_{31}x_{3}|^{2}+3|\omega_{13}x_{1}+\omega_{33}x_{3}|^{2}+5|\omega_{15}x_{1}+\omega_{35}x_{3}|^{2}\\
&+7|\omega_{17}x_{1}+\omega_{37}x_{3}|^{2}\leq|x_{1}|^{2}+\frac{|x_{3}|^{2}}{3}.\end{split}

As it has been shown in
[
6
, p.57]
, if
f
is given by (
1
) then the coefficients
a_{2}
,
a_{3}
,
a_{4}
and
a_{5}
are expressed by Grunsky’s coefficients
\omega_{2p-1,2q-1}
of the function
f_{2}
given by
(
4
) in the following way:

(7)

\begin{split}a_{2}&=2\omega_{11},\\
a_{3}&=2\omega_{13}+3\omega_{11}^{2},\\
a_{4}&=2\omega_{33}+8\omega_{11}\omega_{13}+\frac{10}{3}\omega_{11}^{3},\\
a_{5}&=2\omega_{35}+8\omega_{11}\omega_{33}+5\omega_{13}^{2}+18\omega_{11}^{2}\omega_{13}+\frac{7}{3}\omega_{11}^{4},\\
0&=3\omega_{15}-3\omega_{11}\omega_{13}+\omega_{11}^{3}-3\omega_{33},\\
0&=\omega_{17}-\omega_{35}-\omega_{11}\omega_{33}-\omega_{13}^{2}+\frac{1}{3}\omega_{11}^{4}.\end{split}

We note that in the cited book of Lebedev there is a typing mistake for the coefficient
a_{5}
. Namely, instead of the term
5\omega_{13}^{2}
there stays
5\omega_{15}^{2}
.

We now give upper bound of the fourth logarithmic coefficient over the class
{\mathcal{S}}
.

Theorem 1
.

Let
f\in\mathcal{S}
and be given by (
1
). Then

|\gamma_{4}|\leq 0.51059\ldots.

Proof.

From (
1
) and (
2
), after differentiating and comparing coefficients, we receive

\gamma_{4}=\frac{1}{2}\left(a_{5}-a_{2}a_{4}-\frac{1}{2}a_{3}^{2}+a_{2}^{2}a_{3}-\frac{1}{4}a_{2}^{4}\right),

or by using the relation (
7
):

(8)

\gamma_{4}=\frac{1}{2}\left(2\omega_{35}+3\omega_{13}^{2}+4\omega_{11}\omega_{33}+4\omega_{11}^{2}\omega_{13}-\frac{5}{6}\omega_{11}^{4}\right).

If we combine the two last relations from (
7
), then we have

(9)

\omega_{33}=\omega_{15}-\omega_{11}\omega_{13}+\frac{1}{3}\omega_{11}^{3}

and

(10)

\omega_{35}=\omega_{17}-\omega_{11}\omega_{33}-\omega_{13}^{2}+\frac{1}{3}\omega_{11}^{4}=\omega_{17}-\omega_{11}\omega_{15}+\omega_{11}^{2}\omega_{13}-\omega_{13}^{2}.

Using the relations (
8
), (
9
)and (
10
), after some calculations, we get

\gamma_{4}=\omega_{17}+\omega_{11}\omega_{15}+\omega_{11}^{2}\omega_{13}+\frac{1}{2}\omega_{13}^{2}+\frac{1}{4}\omega_{11}^{4}.

Therefore,

(11)

\begin{split}|\gamma_{4}|&\leq|\omega_{17}|+|\omega_{11}||\omega_{15}|+|\omega_{11}|^{2}|\omega_{13}|+\frac{1}{2}|\omega_{13}|^{2}+\frac{1}{4}|\omega_{11}|^{4}\\
&:=\varphi(|\omega_{11}|,|\omega_{13}|,|\omega_{15}|,|\omega_{17}|).\end{split}

Now, choosing
x_{1}=1
and
x_{3}=0
in (
6
) we receive

|\omega_{11}|^{2}+3|\omega_{13}|^{2}+5|\omega_{15}|^{2}+7|\omega_{17}|^{2}\leq 1,

and also

|\omega_{11}|\leq 1,\quad|\omega_{11}|^{2}+3|\omega_{13}|^{2}\leq 1,\quad|\omega_{11}|^{2}+3|\omega_{13}|^{2}+5|\omega_{15}|^{2}\leq 1.

The above inequalities imply

(12)

\begin{split}|\omega_{13}|&\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}\sqrt{1-|\omega_{11}|^{2}}\,,\\
|\omega_{15}|&\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{5}}\sqrt{1-|\omega_{11}|^{2}-3|\omega_{13}|^{2}}\,,\\
|\omega_{17}|&\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{7}}\sqrt{1-|\omega_{11}|^{2}-3|\omega_{13}|^{2}-5|\omega_{15}|^{2}}\,.\end{split}

Using (
11
) and (
12
) we conclude that it remains to find
\max\varphi_{1}
, where

\varphi_{1}(x,y,z,t)=\frac{1}{4}x^{4}+\frac{1}{2}y^{2}+x^{2}y+xz+t,

where
(x,y,z,t)
is in the four dimensional hypercube
\Omega
described with

(13)

\begin{split}0&\leq x=|\omega_{11}|\leq 1,\\
0&\leq y=|\omega_{13}|\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}\sqrt{1-x^{2}},\\
0&\leq z=|\omega_{15}|\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{5}}\sqrt{1-x^{2}-3y^{2}},\\
0&\leq t=|\omega_{17}|\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{7}}\sqrt{1-x^{2}-3y^{2}-5z^{2}}.\end{split}

Since
\varphi_{1}
is an increasing function of
t
on the interval
(0,+\infty)
, we realize that it reaches its maximal value for
t=t_{0}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{7}}\sqrt{1-x^{2}-3y^{2}-5z^{2}}
, i.e.,

\max\{\varphi_{1}(x,y,z,t):(x,y,z,t)\in\Omega\}=\max\{\psi_{1}(x,y,z):(x,y,z)\in\Omega_{1}\},

where
\psi_{1}(x,y,z)\equiv\varphi_{1}(x,y,z,t_{0})
and

\Omega_{1}=\left\{(x,y,z):0\leq x\leq 1,\,0\leq y\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}\sqrt{1-x^{2}},\,0\leq z\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{5}}\sqrt{1-x^{2}-3y^{2}}\right\}.

The system of equations

\left\{\begin{split}\frac{\partial\psi_{1}}{\partial x}&=x^{3}+2xy+z-\frac{x/\sqrt{7}}{\sqrt{1-x^{2}-3y^{2}-5z^{2}}}=0\\
\frac{\partial\psi_{1}}{\partial y}&=x^{2}+y-\frac{3y/\sqrt{7}}{\sqrt{1-x^{2}-3y^{2}-5z^{2}}}=0\\
\frac{\partial\psi_{1}}{\partial z}&=x-\frac{5z/\sqrt{7}}{\sqrt{1-x^{2}-3y^{2}-5z^{2}}}=0\end{split}\right.

in the interior of
\Omega_{1}
, has a unique solution,

x_{0}=0.81907\ldots,\quad y_{0}=0.233235\ldots,\quad z_{0}=0.126778\ldots,

(obtained with Wolfram’s Mathematica) with
\psi_{1}(x_{0},y_{0},z_{0},t_{0})=\psi_{1}(x_{0},y_{0},z_{0})=0.51059\ldots
which will turn out to be the maximal value of
\varphi_{1}
on
\Omega
and an upper bound for
|\gamma_{4}|
.

Now we will study the behaviour of
\psi_{1}
on the boundaries of
\Omega_{1}
.

For
x=0
, we have that
\psi_{1}(0,y,z)=\frac{1}{2}y^{2}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{7}}\sqrt{1-3y^{2}-5z^{2}}
for
0\leq y\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}
and
0\leq z\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{5}}\sqrt{1-3y^{2}}
has maximal value
\frac{1}{\sqrt{7}}=0.37796\ldots
attained when
y=z=0
.

Next, for
x=1
, we have necessarily
y=z=0
, which leads to a maximal value
\psi_{1}(1,0,0)=1/4=0.25
.

For the case
y=0
, we have
\psi_{1}(x,0,z)=\frac{1}{4}x^{4}+xz+\frac{1}{\sqrt{7}}\sqrt{1-x^{2}-5z^{2}}
, with
0\leq x\leq 1
and
0\leq z\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{5}}\sqrt{1-x^{2}}
.
Further, for the solution
(x_{1},z_{1})
of the system of equations

\left\{\begin{split}\frac{\partial\psi_{1}(x_{1},0,z_{1})}{\partial x}&=x_{1}^{3}+z_{1}-\frac{x_{1}/\sqrt{7}}{\sqrt{-x_{1}^{2}-5z_{1}^{2}+1}}=0\\
\frac{\partial\psi_{1}(x_{1},0,z_{1})}{\partial z}&=x_{1}-\frac{5z_{1}/\sqrt{7}}{\sqrt{-x_{1}^{2}-5z_{1}^{2}+1}}=0\end{split}\right.,

we have

-5z_{1}^{2}-5x_{1}^{3}z_{1}+x_{1}^{2}=0,

leading further to

z_{1}=\frac{1}{10}\left(-5x_{1}^{3}+\sqrt{5}\sqrt{5x_{1}^{6}+4x_{1}^{2}}\right).

Finally,

\begin{split}&\quad\psi_{1}(x,0,z)\leq\psi(x_{1},0,z_{1})\\
&=-\frac{x_{1}^{4}}{4}+\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{x_{1}^{6}+\frac{4x_{1}^{2}}{5}}x_{1}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{14}}\left[\sqrt{\left(-5x_{1}^{4}+\sqrt{5}\sqrt{x_{1}^{2}\left(5x_{1}^{4}+4\right)}x_{1}-4\right)x_{1}^{2}+2}\right]\end{split}

By the means of calculus of real functions of one real variables, one can verify that the last function
attains its maximum for
x=0.80210\ldots
and
z=0.183847\ldots
, and that maximum is
0.414666\ldots
.

The case
y=\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}\sqrt{1-x^{2}}
, leads to
z=0
, and further to the function

\psi_{1}\left(x,\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}\sqrt{1-x^{2}},0\right)=\frac{x^{4}}{4}+\frac{x^{2}\sqrt{1-x^{2}}}{\sqrt{3}}+\frac{1}{6}\left(1-x^{2}\right)

with maximum
0.4000\ldots
for
x=0.8874\ldots
and
y=0.2661\ldots
.

For
z=0
, we have
\psi_{1}(x,y,0)=\frac{x^{4}}{4}+x^{2}y+\frac{y^{2}}{2}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{7}}\sqrt{-x^{2}-3y^{2}+1}
and working in the similar way as in the case
y=0
, we receive its maximum on
0\leq x\leq 1
and
0\leq y\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}\sqrt{1-x^{2}}
to be
0.4561\ldots
for
x=0.8358\ldots
and
y=0.2619\ldots
.

Finally, in a similar way as before, for the case
z=\frac{1}{\sqrt{5}}\sqrt{1-x^{2}-3y^{2}}
, by means of calculus, we can verify that the maximal value is
0.4570\ldots
obtained for
x=0.864969\ldots
and
y=0.239789\ldots
.
∎

We now give upper bound of
|a_{5}|-|a_{4}|
over the class
{\mathcal{S}}
.

Theorem 2
.

Let
f\in{\mathcal{S}}
and be given by (
1
). Then

|a_{5}|-|a_{4}|\leq 2.3297\ldots.

Proof.

Since

\begin{split}|a_{5}|-|a_{4}|&\leq|a_{5}|-|\omega_{11}||a_{4}|\leq|a_{5}-\omega_{11}a_{4}|\\
&=\Big|2\omega_{35}+6\omega_{11}\omega_{33}+10\omega_{11}^{2}\omega_{13}+5\omega_{13}^{2}-\omega_{11}^{4}\Big|,\end{split}

after applying (
9
) and (
10
), and some calculations, we have

\begin{split}|a_{5}|-|a_{4}|&\leq\Big|2\omega_{17}+4\omega_{11}\omega_{15}+6\omega_{11}^{2}\omega_{13}+3\omega_{13}^{2}+\omega_{11}^{4}\Big|\\
&\leq 2|\omega_{17}|+4|\omega_{11}||\omega_{15}|+6|\omega_{11}|^{2}|\omega_{13}|+3|\omega_{13}|^{2}+|\omega_{11}|^{4}\\
&:=\varphi_{2}(|\omega_{11}|,|\omega_{13}|,|\omega_{15}|,|\omega_{17}|),\end{split}

where

\varphi_{2}(x,y,z,t)=x^{4}+3y^{2}+6x^{2}y+4xz+2t,

with
x,y,z,t
, as well as their domain
\Omega
, are given in (
13
) from the previous theorem.

Now, in a similar way as in the proof of the previous theorem we will find the maximal value of the function
\psi_{2}
over the domain
\Omega
.

The function
\varphi_{2}
is an increasing one over the variable
t
, and therefore it reaches its maximal value for
t=t_{0}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{7}}\sqrt{1-x^{2}-3y^{2}-5z^{2}}
.
Using the notations

\psi_{2}(x,y,z)\equiv\varphi_{2}(x,y,z,t_{0})=x^{4}+6x^{2}y+4xz+3y^{2}+\frac{2\sqrt{-x^{2}-3y^{2}-5z^{2}+1}}{\sqrt{7}},

and
\Omega_{1}
as in the Theorem
1
,
again using Wolfram’s Mathematica we obtain that the system of equations

\left\{\begin{split}\frac{\partial\psi_{2}}{\partial x}&=4x^{3}+12xy+4z-\frac{2x}{\sqrt{7}\sqrt{1-x^{2}-3y^{2}-5z^{2}}}=0\\
\frac{\partial\psi_{2}}{\partial y}&=6x^{2}+y\left(6-\frac{6}{\sqrt{7}\sqrt{1-x^{2}-3y^{2}-5z^{2}}}\right)=0\\
\frac{\partial\psi_{2}}{\partial z}&=4x-\frac{10z}{\sqrt{7}\sqrt{1-x^{2}-3y^{2}-5z^{2}}}=0\end{split}\right.,

in the interior of
\Omega_{1}
has a unique solution,

x_{0}=0.82745\ldots,\quad y_{0}=0.29092\ldots,\quad z_{0}=0.098698\ldots\ldots,

such that
\varphi_{2}(x_{0},y_{0},z_{0},t_{0})=\psi_{2}(x_{0},y_{0},z_{0})=2.3297\ldots
. At the end, this will turn out to be the maximal value of
\psi_{2}
on
\Omega_{1}
and upper bound of
|a_{5}|-|a_{4}|
.

Now we will study the behaviour of
\psi_{2}
on the boundaries of
\Omega_{1}
.

For
x=0
, we receive
\psi_{2}(0,y,z)=3y^{2}+\frac{2\sqrt{-3y^{2}-5z^{2}+1}}{\sqrt{7}}
which is a decreasing function of
z
(since
z
is positive), thus with the same maximal value as
\psi_{2}(0,y,0)=3y^{2}+\frac{2\sqrt{1-3y^{2}}}{\sqrt{7}}
which turns out to be
1.142857\ldots
for
y=0.5345\ldots
.

If
x=1
, then necessarily
y=z=0
, and
\psi_{2}(1,0,0)=1
.

For
y=0
, we have
\psi_{2}(x,0,z)=x^{4}+4xz+\frac{2\sqrt{1-x^{2}-5z^{2}}}{\sqrt{7}}
which can be shown to have no critical points in the interior of

\left\{(x,z):0\leq x\leq 1,0\leq z\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{5}}\sqrt{1-x^{2}}\right\},

and a maximal value
1.3614\ldots
obtained for
x_{1}=0.9181\ldots
and
z_{1}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{5}}\sqrt{1-x_{1}^{2}}=0.1772\ldots
.

For the case
y^{*}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}\sqrt{1-x^{2}}
, we have
z=0
, and further, the function

\psi_{2}\left(x,y^{*},0\right)=x^{4}+\left(2\sqrt{3-3x^{2}}-1\right)x^{2}+1

has maximal value
2.118588\ldots
for
x=0.8427\ldots
.

Next, for
z=0
we receive

\psi_{2}(x,y,0)=x^{4}+6x^{2}y+3y^{2}+\frac{2\sqrt{-x^{2}-3y^{2}+1}}{\sqrt{7}},

with a critical point
(x_{2},y_{2})
(
x=0.83589\ldots
and
y=0.3097\ldots
) in the interior of

\left\{(x,y):0\leq x\leq 1,0\leq y\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}\sqrt{1-x^{2}}\right\},

such that
\psi_{2}(x_{2},y_{2},0)=2.162\ldots
. The boundaries of the above domain are already discussed above.

Finally, if
z^{*}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{5}}\sqrt{-x^{2}-3y^{2}+1}
, one can verify that the function

\psi_{2}(x,y,z)=x^{4}+6x^{2}y+3y^{2}+\frac{4x\sqrt{-x^{2}-3y^{2}+1}}{\sqrt{5}},

has critical point
(x_{3},y_{3})
with
x_{3}=0.8338\ldots
and
y_{3}=0.2921\ldots
, such that
\psi_{2}(x_{3},y_{3},z^{*})=2.287\ldots
.

All the above analysis brings us to the final conclusion that
\psi_{2}
on
\Omega_{1}
has a maximal value
2.3297\ldots
obtained for
x=x_{0}
,
y=y_{0}
and
z=z_{0}
.
∎

unique continuation problem on RCD Spaces. I
Qin Deng, Xinrui Zhao

Abstract
. In this note we establish the weak unique continuation theorem for caloric functions on compact R ​ C ​ D ​ ( K , 2 ) RCD(K,2) spaces and show that there exists an R ​ C ​ D ​ ( K , 4 ) RCD(K,4) space on which there exist non-trivial eigenfunctions of the Laplacian and non-stationary solutions of the heat equation which vanish up to infinite order at one point. We also establish frequency estimates for eigenfunctions and caloric functions on the metric horn. In particular, this gives a strong unique continuation type result on the metric horn for harmonic functions with a high rate of decay at the horn tip, where it is known that the standard strong unique continuation property fails.



0.
introduction

In this note we study the weak and strong unique continuation properties for the heat equation on
\RCD(K,N)
spaces. Consider the equation

(0.1)

\displaystyle\Delta u-\partial_{t}u=0.

Let
T>0
. On an
\RCD(K,N)
space
(X,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d},\mathfrak{m})
, a solution to (
0.1
) on
[0,T]
is defined as follows.

Definition
\the\fnum
.

A function
u:X\to\mathbb{R}
is a solution (resp. sub-solution, super-solution) of (
0.1
) if
u\in W^{1,2}_{loc}(X\times[0,T])
and that, for all
\phi\in{\text{Lip}}_{0}(X\times[0,T])
with
\phi\geq 0
, we have

(0.2)

\displaystyle\int_{X\times[0,T]}-\langle\nabla u,\nabla\phi\rangle-\phi\,\partial_{t}u\,\,d\mathfrak{m}dt=0\,(\text{resp. }\leq 0,\geq 0).

This paper is concerned with the validity of the strong and weak unique continuation property for the heat equation (
0.1
). To be precise, we say that

•

The heat equation satisfies the weak unique continuation property on an
\RCD(K,N)
space
(X,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d},\mathfrak{m})
if, for any solution
u\in W^{1,2}(X\times[0,T])
of (
0.1
), if
u
vanishes on any non-empty open subset of
X\times[0,T]
, then
u\equiv 0
.

•

A function
u\in L^{2}(X\times[0,T])
vanishes up to infinite order at some
(x_{0},t_{0})\in X\times[0,T]
if there exists some
R>0
so that for any integer
N>0
, there exists
C(N)>0
so that

\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})\times((t_{0}-r^{2},t_{0}+r^{2})\cap[0,T])}|u|^{2}\,\,d\mathfrak{m}dt\leq Cr^{N}.

•

The heat equation satisfies the strong unique continuation property on an
\RCD(K,N)
space
(X,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d},\mathfrak{m})
if, for any solution
u\in W^{1,2}(X\times[0,T])
of (
0.1
), if
u
vanishes up to infinite order at any
(x_{0},t_{0})\in X\times(0,T]
, then
u\equiv 0
.

Note that we restrict
t_{0}
to be away from
0
in the definition of strong unique continuation as it is always possible to solve the heat equation starting from some initial data
u(\cdot,0)
, which vanishes up to infinite order (spatially) at some

. This would easily imply that the heat flow also vanishes up to infinite order at
(x_{0},0)
in the smooth case. We mention that unique continuation type results are related to giving an upper bound for the measure of the nodal set of non-trivial solutions and that, recently, there has also been work to establish lower bounds in the nonsmooth setting, see
[
CF21
,
DPF21
]
.

Theorem
\the\fnum
.

(X,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d},\mathfrak{m})
be a compact
\RCD(K,2)

1.
weak unique continuation of caloric functions on
\RCD(K,2)
spaces

In this section we will consider the case of
\RCD(K,2)
spaces. In the smooth setting, the Ricci curvature in
2
dimensions completely determines sectional curvature and hence a Ricci lower bound translates to a sectional curvature lower bound. This is also true for any non-collapsed
\RCD(K,2)
space
(X,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d},\mathfrak{m})
.

Theorem
\the\fnum
.

(
[
LS18
]
)
If
\mathfrak{m}=c\mathcal{H}^{2}
with
c\geq 0
(i.e.
(X,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d},\mathfrak{m})
is non-collapsed), then
(X,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d})
is an Alexandrov space with curvature bounded below by
K
.

Therefore, it suffices to consider
2
-dimensional Alexandrov spaces of curvature at least
K
and collapsed
\RCD(K,2)
spaces. Alexandrov spaces are known to have a generalized Riemannian structure by
[
OS94
]
. We refer to
[
OS94
]
for relevant definitions.

Theorem
\the\fnum
.

(
[
OS94
]
)
Let
(X,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d})
be an
n
-dimensional Alexandrov space and denote
S_{X}
as the set of singular points. Then there exists a
C^{0}
-Riemannian structure on
X\setminus S_{X}\subset X
satisfying the following:

(1)

There exists an
X_{0}\subset X\setminus S_{X}
such that
X\setminus X_{0}
is of
n
-dimensional Hausdorff measure zero and that the Riemannian structure is
C^{\frac{1}{2}}
-continuous on
X_{0}\subset X
;

(2)

The metric structure on
X\setminus S_{X}
induced from the Riemannian structure coincides with the original metric of
X
.

In a coordinate neighborhood
U
given by the
C^{0}
-Riemannian structure of
X
, with corresponding metric
g_{ij}
, a solution
u
of (
0.1
) satisfies

(1.1)

\displaystyle\int_{U\times[0,T]}-g^{ij}\,u_{i}\,\phi_{j}-\phi\,\partial_{t}u\,\,d\mathfrak{m}\,dt=0

for all
\phi\in Lip_{0}(X\times[0,T])
supported in
U\times[0,T]
(Cf.
[
KMS01
]
). Note that since
g^{ij}
may not be Lipschitz, the results of
[
L90
]
do not apply. We will instead use techniques which are special in the 2-dimensional case. For more details, see for example
[
CH62
]
.

We proceed by taking local isothermal coordinates. To be precise, consider functions
\sigma,\rho:U\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
satisfying

(1.2)

\displaystyle\sigma_{x}=\frac{g^{xy}\rho_{x}+g^{yy}\rho_{y}}{\sqrt{g^{xx}g^{yy}-(g^{xy})^{2}}}

(1.3)

\displaystyle-\sigma_{y}=\frac{g^{xx}\rho_{x}+g^{xy}\rho_{y}}{\sqrt{g^{xx}g^{yy}-(g^{xy})^{2}}},

where derivatives are taken with respect to the coordinate chart for
U
. The existence of such functions is given by
[
M38
]
, see also
[
BN54
,
CH62
]
. This is equivalent to solving the complex equation

(1.4)

\displaystyle w_{\bar{z}}=\mu w_{z},

where
\mu=\frac{g^{yy}-g^{xx}-2ig^{xy}}{g^{xx}+g^{yy}+2\sqrt{g^{xx}g^{yy}-(g^{xy})^{2}}}
. Note that

(1.5)

\displaystyle|w_{z}|^{2}=\frac{(\sigma_{x}\rho_{y}-\sigma_{y}\rho_{x})}{4\sqrt{g^{xx}g^{yy}-(g^{xy})^{2}}}(g^{xx}+g^{yy}+2\sqrt{g^{xx}g^{yy}-(g^{xy})^{2}})

We have

(1.6)

\displaystyle u_{x}=u_{\rho}\rho_{x}+u_{\sigma}\sigma_{x},

(1.7)

\displaystyle u_{y}=u_{\rho}\rho_{y}+u_{\sigma}\sigma_{y},

and so

\displaystyle\int_{U\times[0,T]}

\displaystyle-(g^{xx}\,(u_{\rho}\rho_{x}+u_{\sigma}\sigma_{x})\,(\phi_{\rho}\rho_{x}+\phi_{\sigma}\sigma_{x}))-(g^{xy}\,(u_{\rho}\rho_{x}+u_{\sigma}\sigma_{x})\,(\phi_{\rho}\rho_{y}+\phi_{\sigma}\sigma_{y}))

(1.8)

\displaystyle-(g^{xy}\,(u_{\rho}\rho_{y}+u_{\sigma}\sigma_{y})\,(\phi_{\rho}\rho_{x}+\phi_{\sigma}\sigma_{x}))-(g^{yy}\,(u_{\rho}\rho_{y}+u_{\sigma}\sigma_{y})\,(\phi_{\rho}\rho_{y}+\phi_{\sigma}\sigma_{y}))-\phi\,\partial_{t}u\,\,d\mathfrak{m}\,dt=0.

After rearranging the terms,

(1.9)

\displaystyle\int_{U\times[0,T]}\sqrt{g^{xx}g^{yy}-(g^{xy})^{2}}(\sigma_{x}\rho_{y}-\sigma_{y}\rho_{x})(u_{\rho}\phi_{\rho}+u_{\sigma}\phi_{\sigma})-\phi\,\partial_{t}u\,\,d\mathfrak{m}\,dt=0.

and so in the new coordinates, we have

(1.10)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}\times[0,T]}(u_{\rho}\phi_{\rho}+u_{\sigma}\phi_{\sigma})-\frac{\phi\,\partial_{t}u}{\sqrt{g^{xx}g^{yy}-(g^{xy})^{2}}(\sigma_{x}\rho_{y}-\sigma_{y}\rho_{x})}\,\,dx\,dy\,dt=0.

Thus in the coordinate
(\rho,\sigma)
the equation becomes

(1.11)

\displaystyle\Delta u-a\partial_{t}u=0,

where
a
is measurable and Hölder on a full measure subset. Note that as now we have a possibly discontinuous coefficient in front of
\partial_{t}
, the result in
[
L90
]
cannot be used. We will instead use a geometrical argument to deal with this difficulty.

Now we are ready to state the main result of this section.

Theorem
\the\fnum
.

Let
u\in W^{1,2}(X\times[0,T])
be a solution of (
0.1
) on an
\RCD(K,2)
space. If
u
vanishes on a non-empty open set
\Gamma\subset X\times(0,T]
, then
u\equiv 0
on
\text{proj}_{X}(\Gamma)\times[0,T]
. Moreover if
X
is compact, then
u\equiv 0
on
X\times[0,T]
.

Proof.

By a similar discussion as in
[
DZ21
]
, we can only consider the non-collapsed case. We assume that u vanishes on a non-empty open set
V\times(t_{1},t_{2})
.

Since
u
satisfies the heat equation, we have the following a priori estimates (cf.
[
G20
, Remark 5.2.11]
):

(1.12)

\displaystyle\|u(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{2}}\leq\|u(\cdot,0)\|_{L^{2}},

(1.13)

\displaystyle\||\nabla u(\cdot,t)|\|_{L^{2}}\leq\frac{\|u(\cdot,0)\|_{L^{2}}}{\sqrt{t}},

(1.14)

\displaystyle\|\partial_{t}u(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{2}}=\|\Delta u(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{2}}\leq\frac{\|u(\cdot,0)\|_{L^{2}}}{t},

for any
t\in(0,T]
.

We first prove that
u
vanishes on
V\times[0,T]
by showing that
u(x,\cdot)
is analytic with respect to
t
. For the corresponding arguments on Riemannian manifolds see for example
[
Z21
]
. For the reader’s convenience we sketch the argument here.

As in
[
LZ19
]
, for any
(x_{0},t_{0})
with
0<t_{0}<T
,
k>0
and
j\leq k
, we consider

(1.15)

\displaystyle H_{j}^{1}

\displaystyle=B(x_{0},\frac{j\sqrt{t_{0}}}{\sqrt{2k}})\times[t_{0}-\frac{jt_{0}}{2k},t_{0}+\frac{jt_{0}}{2k}],

(1.16)

\displaystyle H_{j}^{2}

\displaystyle=B(x_{0},\frac{(j+0.5)\sqrt{t_{0}}}{\sqrt{2k}})\times[t_{0}-\frac{(j+0.5)t_{0}}{2k},t_{0}+\frac{(j+0.5)t_{0}}{2k}].

So that
u
is defined on these sets, we extend
u
to be a solution of (
0.1
) on
X\times[0,\infty)
.

By
[
MN19
]
, see also
[
CN12
]
, we may choose a cutoff function
\phi_{1}(x,t)
supported in
H^{2}_{j}
such that
\phi_{1}=1
on
H^{1}_{j}
, satisfying

(1.17)

\displaystyle|\nabla\phi_{1}(x,t)|^{2}+|\partial_{t}\phi_{1}(x,t)|\leq\frac{Ck}{t_{0}}.

Indeed,
\phi_{1}
may be chosen to be a product of two cutoff functions on the spatial and time coordinates respectively, so that the spatial and time derivatives are well-defined without further measure theoretic arguments.
It follows that

\displaystyle\int_{H^{2}_{j}}|\partial_{t}u|^{2}\phi_{1}^{2}\,d\mathfrak{m}\,dt

\displaystyle=-\int_{H_{j}^{2}}(\langle\nabla\partial_{t}u,\nabla u\rangle\phi_{1}^{2}+\partial_{t}u\langle\nabla u,\nabla\phi_{1}^{2}\rangle)\,d\mathfrak{m}\,dt

\displaystyle=-\int_{H_{j}^{2}}(\frac{1}{2}\partial_{t}|\nabla u|^{2}\phi_{1}^{2}+\partial_{t}u\langle\nabla u,\nabla\phi_{1}^{2}\rangle)\,d\mathfrak{m}\,dt

\displaystyle\leq\int_{H^{2}_{j}}(\frac{3}{4}|\partial_{t}u|^{2}\phi_{1}^{2}+\frac{Ck}{t_{0}}|\nabla u|^{2})\,d\mathfrak{m}\,dt,

where the last inequality follows from integration by parts, Young’s inequality and (
1.17
).
This gives that

(1.18)

\displaystyle\int_{H_{j}^{1}}|\partial_{t}u|^{2}\,d\mathfrak{m}\,dt\leq\frac{Ck}{t_{0}}\int_{H_{j}^{2}}|\nabla u|^{2}\,d\mathfrak{m}\,dt.

Similarly, we may choose a cutoff function
\phi_{2}(x,t)
supported in
H^{1}_{j+1}
such that
\phi_{2}=1
on
H^{2}_{j}
, satisfying

(1.19)

\displaystyle|\nabla\phi_{2}(x,t)|^{2}+|\partial_{t}\phi_{2}(x,t)|\leq\frac{Ck}{t_{0}}.

Arguing as before, we have

\displaystyle\int_{H_{j+1}^{1}}|\nabla u|^{2}\phi_{2}^{2}\,d\mathfrak{m}\,dt

\displaystyle=-\int_{H_{j+1}^{1}}u\,\partial_{t}u\,\phi_{2}^{2}+u\langle\nabla u,\nabla\phi_{2}^{2}\rangle\,d\mathfrak{m}\,dt

\displaystyle=-\int_{H_{j+1}^{1}}\frac{1}{2}\,\partial_{t}u^{2}\,\phi_{2}^{2}+u\langle\nabla u,\nabla\phi_{2}^{2}\rangle\,d\mathfrak{m}\,dt

(1.20)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{H_{j+1}^{1}}(\frac{1}{4}|\nabla u|^{2}\phi^{2}_{2}+\frac{Ck}{t_{0}}u^{2})\,d\mathfrak{m}\,dt,

and so

(1.21)

\displaystyle\int_{H_{j}^{2}}|\nabla u|^{2}\,dx\,dt\leq\frac{Ck}{t_{0}}\int_{H_{j+1}^{1}}u^{2}\,dx\,dt.

By using (
1.18
),(
1.21
) inductively on
j
, we obtain

(1.22)

\displaystyle\int_{H_{1}^{1}}(\partial_{t}^{k}u)^{2}\,dx\,dt\leq\frac{(Ck)^{2k}}{t_{0}^{2k}}\int_{H^{1}_{k+1}}u^{2}\,dx\,dt\leq\frac{(Ck)^{2k}}{t_{0}^{2k-1}},

where
C
at the end also depends on the
L^{2}
-norm of
u_{0}
.

From
[
HK00
,
BB11
,
R12
]
we know that the
(p,p)
-Poincaré inequality holds on
\RCD(K,N)
space for any
p\geq 1
(see, for example,
[
K15
, Section 4]
for a careful discussion of Poincaré inequalties in the
\RCD(K,N)
setting). Thus Moser iteration (
[
HK00
, Lemma 3.10]
) shows that for
R<1
there exist a mean value inequality

(1.23)

\displaystyle|\partial_{t}^{k}u(x_{0},t_{0})|^{2}\leq C(\frac{R^{\nu_{2}}}{|B(x_{0},R)|})\frac{1}{R^{\nu_{2}(1+\frac{2}{\nu_{2}})}}\int_{B(x,R)\times(t-R^{2},t)}|\partial_{t}^{k}u|^{2}\,d\mathfrak{m}\,dt.

Thus by taking
R=\frac{\sqrt{t_{0}}}{\sqrt{2k}}
and using (
1.22
), we obtain

(1.24)

\displaystyle|\partial_{t}^{k}u(x_{0},t_{0})|^{2}\leq\frac{C}{|B(x_{0},\frac{\sqrt{t_{0}}}{\sqrt{2k}})|(\frac{\sqrt{t_{0}}}{\sqrt{2k}})^{2}}\int_{H_{1}^{1}}|\partial_{t}^{k}u|^{2}\,d\mathfrak{m}\,dt\leq\frac{C}{|B(x_{0},\frac{\sqrt{t_{0}}}{\sqrt{2k}})|}\frac{(Ck)^{2k+1}}{t_{0}^{2k}},\,\,t_{0}<k.

Using volume comparison, this implies that
u(x_{0},\cdot)
is analytic with respect to
t
. In particular, since
u
vanishes on
V\times(t_{1},t_{2})
by assumption,
u
also vanishes on
V\times[0,T]
.

We now prove the second assertion of the theorem by contradiction. Assume
X
is compact and
u\not\equiv 0
.

Let
\phi_{k}
be eigenfunctions of
-\Delta
corresponding to eigenvalues
\lambda_{k}
with
\|\phi_{k}\|_{L^{2}}=1
and
0=\lambda_{0}<\lambda_{1}\leq\lambda_{2}\leq...\to\infty
, see
[
H18
]
for a discussion on this. It follows from the estimates obtained in the appendix of
[
AHTP18
]
that
u
admits the representation

(1.25)

\displaystyle u(x,t)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}a_{k}e^{-\lambda_{k}t}\phi_{k}(x).

Let
j
be the first index for which
a_{j}\neq 0
. As it does not affect the argument, we assume for simplicity that the dimension of the eigenspace corresponding to
\lambda_{j}
is
1
. This gives that for any
x_{0}\in V
,

(1.26)

\displaystyle 0=\mathop{lim}\limits_{t\to\infty}e^{\lambda_{j}t}u(x_{0},t)=a_{j}\phi_{j}(x_{0}),

where we have used
[
AHTP18
, Proposition 7.1]
to bound the values of
\phi_{k}(x_{0})
for
k>j
to obtain the second equality. As we assumed that
a_{j}\neq 0
, we conclude that
\phi_{j}(x_{0})=0
. This shows that
\phi_{j}(x_{0})=0
for all

and

. From
[
DZ21
]
, this implies that
\phi_{j}\equiv 0
, which is a contradiction. It follows that

. Note that similar arguments also work for Dirichlet problem on non-compact space.

There exists a smooth function

2.
Elliptic Frequency estimate on Metric horn

In this section we will give a frequency estimate on the metric horn, which allows us to prove a form of unique continuation. Recall from
[
DZ21
]
that the standard formulation of strong unique continuation does not hold at the horn tip. The form of unique continuation we will prove in this section will therefore assume a higher order of decay at the horn tip, see Remark
2
at the end of the section for more details.

On a weighted warped product
(X,dr^{2}+f^{2}(r)g_{S^{n-1}},e^{-\psi(r)}dvol)
, given function
\varphi
, we have that

(2.1)

\displaystyle\Delta\varphi=\partial_{r}^{2}\varphi+(n-1)\partial_{r}\varphi(log\,f)_{r}+f^{-2}\Delta_{S^{n-1}}\varphi-\partial_{r}\varphi\partial_{r}\psi,

away from
r=0
.
In the case of the standard metric horn,

(2.2)

\displaystyle f(r)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}r^{1+\epsilon},

(2.3)

\displaystyle\psi(r)

\displaystyle=-(N-n)(1-\eta)log(r),

and so,

(2.4)

\displaystyle\Delta\varphi=\partial_{r}^{2}\varphi+\partial_{r}\varphi(\frac{(n-1)(1+\epsilon)+(N-n)(1-\eta)}{r})+\frac{4}{r^{2+2\epsilon}}\Delta_{S^{n-1}}\varphi.

From the equation of Laplacian on metric horn, in particular, for
\varphi=r^{\alpha}
, we have that

(2.5)

\displaystyle\Delta\varphi=r^{\alpha-2}\alpha(\alpha+N-2+(n-1)\epsilon-(N-n)\eta)

and

(2.6)

\displaystyle{\text{Hess}}\,\varphi=\alpha(\alpha-1)r^{\alpha-2}dr\otimes dr+(\frac{r^{1+\epsilon}}{2})^{2}\alpha(1+\epsilon)r^{\alpha-2}g_{S^{n-1}}.

If we take
\alpha=2
, then

(2.7)

\displaystyle{\text{Hess}}\,\varphi=2(dr^{2}+(1+\epsilon)(\frac{r^{1+\epsilon}}{2})^{2}g_{S^{n-1}}).

Given an eigenfunction u with
\Delta u=\lambda u
, define scale-invariant quantities
I(r),E(r)
and the frequency function
U(r)
with respect to the level sets of the function
d_{p}
, where
p
is the horn tip. For
r>0
, we denote

(2.8)

\displaystyle B_{r}=\{x\,|\,d_{p}(x)<r\},

(2.9)

\displaystyle\partial B_{r}=\{x\,|\,d_{p}(x)=r\},

and define

(2.10)

\displaystyle I(r)=r^{1-n}\int_{\partial B_{r}}u^{2}d\mathfrak{m}_{r},

(2.11)

\displaystyle E(r)=r^{2-n}\int_{B_{r}}|\nabla u|^{2}+\lambda u^{2}d\mathfrak{m}=r^{2-n}\int_{\partial B_{r}}\frac{u}{|\nabla d_{p}|}\,\langle\nabla u,\nabla d_{p}\rangle d\mathfrak{m}_{r},

(2.12)

\displaystyle U(r)=\frac{E(r)}{I(r)},

where
\mathfrak{m}=e^{\psi}(r)dvol
is the weighted volume measure of the metric horn and
\mathfrak{m}_{r}=e^{\psi}(r)dvol_{r}
is the corresponding weighted area measure on
\partial B_{r}
.

We first compute the derivative of
I
. We remark that all computations are done away from the cone tip
p
, so no regularity issues will arise from
p
itself. Let
\phi
be any smooth function compactly supported on

\displaystyle-\int I(t)\phi^{\prime}(t)dt=-\int t^{1-n}\int_{\partial B_{t}}u^{2}\phi^{\prime}(t)dt=-\int u^{2}d^{1-n}_{p}\phi^{\prime}(d_{p})

\displaystyle=-\int u^{2}d^{1-n}_{p}\langle\nabla d_{p},\nabla\phi(d_{p})\rangle=\int{\text{div}}(u^{2}d^{1-n}_{p}\nabla d_{p})\phi(d_{p})

\displaystyle=\int\frac{1}{2-n}(2u\langle\nabla u,\nabla d_{p}^{2-n}\rangle+u^{2}\Delta d_{p}^{2-n})\phi(d_{p})

\displaystyle=\int_{t}\int_{\partial B_{t}}\frac{1}{(2-n)|\nabla d_{p}|}(2u\langle\nabla u,\nabla d^{2-n}_{p}\rangle+u^{2}\Delta d^{2-n}_{p})\phi(d_{p})dt.

\displaystyle I^{\prime}(r)

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial B_{r}}\frac{1}{(2-n)|\nabla d_{p}|}(2u\langle\nabla u,\nabla d^{2-n}_{p}\rangle+u^{2}\Delta d^{2-n}_{p})

\displaystyle=\frac{2E(r)}{r}+\int_{\partial B_{r}}\frac{1}{(2-n)|\nabla d_{p}|}u^{2}\Delta d^{2-n}_{p},\quad a.e.\,\,t\in(0,1),

and so by using (

\displaystyle(\log I)^{\prime}(r)-\frac{2U(r)}{r}=\frac{N-n+(n-1)\epsilon-(N-n)\eta}{r}.

For

) and the coarea formula, we have that

\displaystyle E^{\prime}(r)=(2-n)\frac{E(r)}{r}+r^{2-n}\int_{\partial B_{r}}({|\nabla u|^{2}}+\lambda u^{2}).

Now consider the vector field

, we have that

\displaystyle{\text{div}}(X)={{\text{Hess}}(d_{p}^{2})(\nabla u,\,\nabla u)-\frac{1}{2}|\nabla u|^{2}\Delta d_{p}^{2}}+\lambda u\langle\nabla(d_{p}^{2}),\,\nabla u\rangle

By Integrating both sides in

we have that

\displaystyle\int_{\partial B_{r}}\frac{2r}{|\nabla d_{p}|^{3}}|\langle\nabla d_{p},\,\nabla u\rangle|^{2}-r\frac{|\nabla u|^{2}}{|\nabla d_{p}|}

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial B_{r}}\langle X,\,\nabla d_{p}\rangle

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{r}}{{\text{Hess}}(d_{p}^{2})(\nabla u,\,\nabla u)-\frac{1}{2}|\nabla u|^{2}\Delta d_{p}^{2}}+\lambda u\langle\nabla(d_{p}^{2}),\,\nabla u\rangle.

Combining (
2.15
) and (

) we have that

(2.18)

\displaystyle E^{\prime}(r)=

\displaystyle(2-n)\frac{E(r)}{r}+r^{2-n}\int_{\partial B_{r}}\frac{2}{|\nabla d_{p}|^{3}}|\langle\nabla d_{p},\,\nabla u\rangle|^{2}+\lambda u^{2}

\displaystyle-r^{1-n}\int_{B_{r}}({{\text{Hess}}(d_{p}^{2})(\nabla u,\,\nabla u)-\frac{1}{2}|\nabla u|^{2}\Delta d_{p}^{2}}+\lambda u\langle\nabla(d_{p}^{2}),\,\nabla u\rangle).

. We have that

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dr}log\,U(r)=

\displaystyle\frac{E^{\prime}(r)}{E(r)}-\frac{I^{\prime}(r)}{I(r)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{E(r)}(-\frac{2E^{2}(r)}{rI(r)}+r^{2-n}\int_{\partial B_{r}}|2\langle\nabla d_{p},\,\nabla u\rangle|^{2}+\lambda u^{2})-\frac{1}{I(r)}\int_{\partial B_{r}}\frac{1}{2-n}u^{2}\Delta d^{2-n}_{p}+\frac{2-n}{r}

\displaystyle-\frac{2E^{2}(r)}{rI(r)}+2r^{2-n}\int_{\partial B_{r}}|\langle\nabla d_{p},\,\nabla u\rangle|^{2}\geq 0.

\displaystyle(r^{2\epsilon}U(r))^{\prime}\geq\lambda r^{1+2\epsilon},

\displaystyle r^{2\epsilon}U(r)-U(1)\leq\frac{\lambda}{2+2\epsilon}(r^{2+2\epsilon}-1),

\displaystyle\log I(r)\geq\log I(1)+C(1-r^{-2\epsilon}),

\displaystyle I(r)\geq Ce^{-Cr^{-2\epsilon}}.

Remark
\the\fnum

4.

In this section we give a parabolic frequency estimate and the corresponding unique continuation type result on the metric horn. For simplicity, we consider the metric horn which is not modified. For previous discussions on parabolic frequency see for example
[
P96
,
CM22
]

Consider the metric horn
(\mathbb{R}^{n},\,\,g_{\epsilon,n},\,\,e^{(N-n)(1-\eta)log(r)}dvol)
with

X
to denote the metric horn in the following. Let

be the heat kernel. Following
[
P96
]

and

0.1
) on

(4.1)

\displaystyle I(R)

\displaystyle=R^{2}\int_{t=-R^{2}}|\nabla u|^{2}G\,d\mathfrak{m},

(4.2)

\displaystyle D(R)

\displaystyle=\int_{t=-R^{2}}u^{2}G\,d\mathfrak{m},

(4.3)

\displaystyle N(R)

\displaystyle=\frac{I(R)}{D(R)}.

(4.4)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\log G}{\partial t}=-\Delta\log G-|\nabla\log G|^{2},

and so

(4.5)

\displaystyle\log G(x,t)=-\frac{(N+(n-1)\epsilon-(N-n)\eta)}{2}\log(-t)+\frac{|x|^{2}}{4t}.

Therefore,

(4.6)

\displaystyle I^{\prime}(R)=

\displaystyle 2R\int_{t=-R^{2}}|\nabla u|^{2}Gd\mathfrak{m}-2R^{3}\int_{t=-R^{2}}(2\langle\nabla u,\,\nabla u_{t}\rangle G+|\nabla u|^{2}\partial_{t}G)d\mathfrak{m}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2R\int_{t=-R^{2}}|\nabla u|^{2}Gd\mathfrak{m}+4R^{3}\int_{t=-R^{2}}u_{t}^{2}G+u_{t}\langle\nabla u,\,\nabla G\rangle d\mathfrak{m}

\displaystyle-2R^{3}\int_{t=-R^{2}}|\nabla u|^{2}\partial_{t}Gd\mathfrak{m}.

Consider the vector field
X=\langle\nabla G,\,\nabla u\rangle\nabla u
, then

(4.7)

\displaystyle div(X)={\text{Hess}}_{G}(\nabla u,\,\nabla u)+\frac{1}{2}\langle\nabla G,\,\nabla|\nabla u|^{2}\rangle+\langle\nabla G,\,\nabla u\rangle\Delta u.

\displaystyle\frac{{\text{Hess}}_{G}}{G}(x,t)=(\frac{|x|^{2}}{4t^{2}}+\frac{1}{2t})dr\otimes dr+\frac{(\frac{|x|^{1+\epsilon}}{2})^{2}}{2t}(1+\epsilon)g_{S^{n-1}},

\displaystyle\int{\text{Hess}}_{G}(\nabla u,\,\nabla u)+\langle\nabla G,\,\nabla u\rangle u_{t}d\mathfrak{m}=\int\frac{1}{2}\Delta G|\nabla u|^{2}d\mathfrak{m}.

\displaystyle I^{\prime}(R)

\displaystyle=4R^{3}\int_{t=-R^{2}}(\frac{x}{2t}\cdot\nabla u+u_{t})^{2}G(x,t)d\mathfrak{m}+4R^{3}\int_{t=-R^{2}}\frac{(\frac{|x|^{1+\epsilon}}{2})^{2}}{2t}\epsilon g_{S^{n-1}}(\nabla u,\,\nabla u)G(x,t)d\mathfrak{m}

\displaystyle\geq 4R^{3}\int_{t=-R^{2}}(\frac{x}{2t}\cdot\nabla u+u_{t})^{2}G(x,t)d\mathfrak{m}-\frac{2\epsilon}{R}I(R).

(4.11)

\displaystyle D^{\prime}(R)=-2R\int_{t=-R^{2}}(2uu_{t}G+u^{2}\partial_{t}G)d\mathfrak{m}=-4R\int_{t=-R^{2}}u(u_{t}+\frac{x}{2t}\cdot\nabla u)Gd\mathfrak{m},

so from integration by parts we have that

(4.12)

\displaystyle I(R)=-R^{2}\int_{t=-R^{2}}u(\partial_{t}u+\nabla u\cdot\frac{x}{2t})Gd\mathfrak{m}=\frac{R}{4}D^{\prime}(R).

(4.13)

\displaystyle(\int_{t=-R^{2}}(\frac{x}{2t}\cdot\nabla u+u_{t})^{2}G(x,t)d\mathfrak{m})(\int_{t=-R^{2}}u^{2}G(x,t)d\mathfrak{m})\geq(\int_{t=-R^{2}}u(\frac{x}{2t}\cdot\nabla u+u_{t})G(x,t)d\mathfrak{m})^{2},

So we have that

(4.14)

\displaystyle(\log N)^{\prime}(R)=\frac{I^{\prime}}{I}(R)-\frac{D^{\prime}}{D}(R)\geq-\frac{2\epsilon}{R},

and so

(4.15)

\displaystyle\log N(R)\leq\log N(1)-2\epsilon\log R,

which is equivalent to that

(4.16)

\displaystyle N(R)\leq CR^{-2\epsilon}.

(4.17)

\displaystyle(\log D)^{\prime}(R)=\frac{4I(R)}{RD(R)}=\frac{4}{R}N(R)\leq CR^{-1-2\epsilon},

(4.18)

\displaystyle\log D(R)\geq\log D(1)+C(1-R^{-2\epsilon}).

(4.19)

\displaystyle D(R)\geq Ce^{-CR^{-2\epsilon}}.

This gives a strong unique continuation type result for solution of heat equation on the metric horn.

1.
Introduction

Let
\Omega
be a bounded pseudoconvex domain in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
,
1\leq q\leq n
,
and let
Dom^{2}(\overline{\partial})
and
Dom^{2}(\overline{\partial}^{*})
denote the domains of the densely
defined operators
\overline{\partial}
and
\overline{\partial}^{*}
in
L^{2}_{(0,q)}(\Omega)
, respectively.
On bounded pseudoconvex domains, Hörmander in
[
Hör65
]

proved the following basic estimate,

\|f\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\|\overline{\partial}f\|_{L^{2}}+\|\overline{\partial}^{*}f\|_{L^{2}}

for all
(0,q)
-forms
f\in Dom^{2}(\overline{\partial})\cap Dom^{2}(\overline{\partial}^{*})\subset L^{2}_{(0,q)}(\Omega)
.
The sum on the right hand side is called the
L^{2}
-graph norm of the

(0,q)
-form
f
. In other words, the embedding operator

\mathcal{J}:Dom^{2}(\overline{\partial})\cap Dom^{2}(\overline{\partial}^{*})\to L^{2}_{(0,q)}(\Omega)

is bounded, where the space on the left hand side is endowed with the graph
norm.

Let
1<p,\widetilde{p}<\infty
such that
p^{-1}+\widetilde{p}^{-1}=1
. We define

Dom^{p}(\overline{\partial})=\{f\in L^{p}_{(0,q)}(\Omega):\overline{\partial}f\in L^{p}_{(0,q+1)}(\Omega)\}
.
We define
Dom^{p}(\overline{\partial}^{*})
as follows: we say

f\in Dom^{p}(\overline{\partial}^{*})
if
f\in L^{p}_{(0,q)}(\Omega)
and there exists
C>0
such that

|\langle f,\overline{\partial}g\rangle|\leq C\|g\|_{L^{\widetilde{p}}}

for all
g\in L^{\widetilde{p}}_{(0,q-1)}(\Omega)
with
\overline{\partial}g\in L^{\widetilde{p}}_{(0,q)}(\Omega)
.
Finally, we define the space

D^{p}_{(0,q)}(\Omega)=Dom^{p}(\overline{\partial})\cap Dom^{p}(\overline{\partial}^{*})\subset L^{p}_{(0,q)}(\Omega)

and endow it with the
L^{p}
-graph norm
\|.\|_{G^{p}}
defined as

\|f\|_{G^{p}}=\|\overline{\partial}f\|_{L^{p}}+\|\overline{\partial}^{*}f\|_{L^{p}}

for
f\in D^{p}_{(0,q)}(\Omega)
. We note that on bounded pseudoconvex
domains,
\|.\|_{G^{p}}
is a norm because
\overline{\partial}f=0
and
\overline{\partial}^{*}f=0

imply that
f=0
for
1\leq q\leq n
(see, for example,

[
CS01
, (4.4.2) in section 4.4]
).

Definition 1
.

We say that the operator
\mathcal{J}
is
L^{p}
-regular
on
D^{p}_{(0,q)}(\Omega)
if
there exists
C>0
such that

\|\mathcal{J}f\|_{L^{p}}=\|f\|_{L^{p}}\leq C\|f\|_{G^{p}}=C\left(\|\overline{\partial}f\|_{L^{p}}+\|\overline{\partial}^{*}f\|_{L^{p}}\right)

for all
f\in D^{p}_{(0,q)}(\Omega)
.

That is, whenever
\mathcal{J}:D^{p}_{(0,q)}(\Omega)\to L^{p}_{(0,q)}(\Omega)
is a bounded embedding
we say that it is
L^{p}
-regular. In particular, by Hörmander’s basic estimate above,

\mathcal{J}
is
L^{2}
-regular on bounded pseudoconvex domains. We note that
D^{p}_{(0,q)}(\Omega)

is a Banach space (for
1\leq q\leq n
with the graph norm
\|.\|_{G^{p}}
) when
\mathcal{J}
is

L^{p}
-regular.

The operator
\mathcal{J}
is related to the
\overline{\partial}
-Neumann operator
N
,
the bounded inverse of the complex Laplacian
\overline{\partial}^{*}\overline{\partial}+\overline{\partial}\overline{\partial}^{*}

on
L^{2}_{(0,q)}(\Omega)
, as
N=\mathcal{J}\mathcal{J}^{*}
(see, for example,

[
Str10
, Proof of Theorem 2.9]
).
Hence,
N
is compact if and only if
\mathcal{J}
is compact. In this note, we
show that compactness of
N
implies
L^{p}
-regularity of
\mathcal{J}
for

2\leq p<\infty
. We also note that it is not yet clear if
\mathcal{J}
is
L^{p}
-regular for

1<p<2
under the compactness assumption. We further note that the question of
whether the
\overline{\partial}
-Neumann operator or the Bergman projection are bounded
in
L^{p}
-norm whenever
\mathcal{J}
is compact is open as well.

Although the mapping properties of the canonical operators relate well in the

L^{2}
-Sobolev setting, similar equivalences in the
L^{p}
setting are less clear.
In
[
BS91
]
, Bonami and Sibony obtained
L^{p}
estimates for
the solutions of
\overline{\partial}
-problem under some Sobolev estimates.
In
[
HZ19
]
Harrington and Zeytuncu obtained some

L^{p}
estimates on the canonical operators under the assumption of the existence
of good weight functions. Both assumptions are more stringent than the compactness
of
N
and hence the
L^{p}
estimates are more general. Also, recently, Haslinger
in
[
Has16
, Theorem 2.2]
showed that if
\mathcal{J}
gains regularity in the

L^{p}
scale then
N
is compact. In this paper, we observe a property that is less
general than the ones in
[
BS91
,
HZ19
]
under a
weaker assumption, and that is in the converse direction of the result in
[
Has16
]
.
Namely, in Theorem
1
below, we show that compactness of
N_{1}

(at the
L^{2}
level) induces
L^{p}
-regularity of
\mathcal{J}
for
2\leq p<\infty
.

Theorem 1
.

Let
\Omega
be a
C^{4}
-smooth bounded pseudoconvex domain in
\mathbb{C}^{2}
.
Assume that
N_{1}
is compact on
L^{2}_{(0,1)}(\Omega)
(or, equivalently,
\mathcal{J}

is compact on
D^{2}_{(0,1)}(\Omega)
). Then
\mathcal{J}
is
L^{p}
-regular
on
D^{p}_{(0,1)}(\Omega)
for all
2\leq p<\infty
.

We note that the
L^{p}
boundedness is not an automatic consequence of
compactness on
L^{2}
; as we demonstrate with
Example
1
, in which we present an operator that is compact
on the
L^{2}
space but unbounded on any
L^{p}
spaces for
p\neq 2
.

In the rest of the paper, we use the symbol
x\lesssim y
to mean that there
exists
C>0
such that
x\leq Cy
. Furthermore, when we write a family of
inequalities depending on a parameter
\varepsilon

x\lesssim\varepsilon y,

we mean that there exists
C>0
that is independent of
\varepsilon
such
that
x\leq C\varepsilon y
.

Proof of Theorem
1

One can prove the following density lemma similarly as in

[
CS01
, Lemma 4.3.2]
(see also
[
Str10
, Proposition 2.3]
)
using an

version of Friedrichs Lemma
(see, for instance,
[

Lemma 1
.

Let
\Omega
be a
C^{k+1}
-smooth bounded domain in
\mathbb{C}^{n}

k,1\leq q\leq n
,
and
1<p<\infty
. Then
C^{k}_{(0,q)}(\overline{\Omega})\cap Dom(\overline{\partial}^{*})
is dense in

D^{p}_{(0,q)}(\Omega)
in the graph norm

f\to\|f\|_{L^{p}}+\|\overline{\partial}f\|_{L^{p}}+\|\overline{\partial}^{*}f\|_{L^{p}}
.
The statement also holds with

and
k+1
replaced with
\infty
.

We will need the following lemma which is a corollary of

[
JK95
, Theorem 1.1]
.

Lemma 2

(Jerison-Kenig)
.

Let
\Omega
be a
C^{1}
-smooth bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{n}

and
1<p<\infty
. Then there exists
C>0
such that

(1)

\displaystyle\|u\|_{W^{1,p}}\leq C\|\Delta u\|_{W^{-1,p}}

for all
u\in W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
.

Using the lemmas above together with the proof of
[
Str10
, Lemma 2.12]

one can prove the following estimate on the normal component of forms. We note
that, in the lemma below,
f_{norm}
denotes the normal component of
f

(see (2.86) in
[
Str10
]
).

Lemma 3
.

Let
\Omega
be a
C^{4}
-smooth bounded pseudoconvex domain in
\mathbb{C}^{n},1\leq q\leq n
,
and
1<p<\infty
. There exists
C>0
such that if
f\in D^{p}_{(0,q)}(\Omega)
then

f_{norm}\in W^{1,p}_{0,(0,q-1)}(\Omega)
and

\|f_{norm}\|_{W^{1,p}}\leq C\left(\|\overline{\partial}f\|_{L^{p}}+\|\overline{\partial}^{*}f\|_{L^{p}}+\|f\|_{L^{p}}\right).

We will use Lemma
2
to also prove the following
L^{p}
version of

[
CS01
, Proposition 5.1.1]
.

Proposition 1
.

Let
\Omega
be a
C^{2}
-smooth bounded domain in
\mathbb{C}^{n},1<p<\infty,1\leq q\leq n,

and
\phi\in C^{1}(\overline{\Omega})
such that
\phi=0
on
b\Omega
. Then there exists
C>0
such that

\displaystyle\|\phi f\|_{W^{1,p}}\leq C(\|\overline{\partial}f\|_{L^{p}}+\|\overline{\partial}^{*}f\|_{L^{p}}+\|f\|_{L^{p}})

for
f\in D^{p}_{(0,q)}(\Omega)
.

Proof.

First we assume that
g\in D^{p}_{(0,q)}(\Omega)
with coefficient functions in
W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
.
Then we have

\displaystyle\|g\|_{W^{1,p}}\lesssim\|\Delta g\|_{W^{-1,p}}\lesssim\|\overline{\partial}g\|_{L^{p}}+\|\overline{\partial}^{*}g\|_{L^{p}}.

Then we substitute
g=\phi f
for
f\in C^{1}_{(0,q)}(\overline{\Omega})\cap Dom(\overline{\partial}^{*})
in the
inequality above

\displaystyle\|\phi f\|_{W^{1,p}}\lesssim\|\overline{\partial}(\phi f)\|_{L^{p}}+\|\overline{\partial}^{*}(\phi f)\|_{L^{p}}\lesssim\|\overline{\partial}f\|_{L^{p}}+\|\overline{\partial}^{*}f\|_{L^{p}}+\|f\|_{L^{p}}.

Then we use Lemma
1
to conclude the proof.
∎

The interpolation inequality for Sobolev spaces together with
Proposition
1
imply the following corollary.

Corollary 1
.

Let
\Omega
be a
C^{2}
-smooth bounded domain in
\mathbb{C}^{n},1<p<\infty,1\leq q\leq n,

and
\phi\in C(\overline{\Omega})
such that
\phi=0
on
b\Omega
. Then the multiplication operator

M_{\phi}:D^{p}_{(0,q)}(\Omega)\to L^{p}_{(0,q)}(\Omega)
is compact.

In other words, in the terminology of
[
ÇS09
]
, continuous
functions on
\overline{\Omega}
that vanish on the boundary are compactness multipliers.

We note that even though
[
Str10
, Lemma 4.3]

is stated for Hilbert spaces the proof works for Banach spaces
as well. In the proof of i) of Lemma
4
below
one uses the facts that on reflective Banach spaces bounded
sequences have weakly convergent subsequences
(see
[
Yos95
, Theorem 1 on pg 126]
) as well as
compact operators map weakly convergent sequences to convergent
sequences. Therefore, proof of
[
Str10
, Lemma 4.3]

(see also exercise 6.13 in
[
Bre11
]
) implies the
following lemma.

Lemma 4
.

Let
T:X\to Y
be a bounded linear map where
X
is a normed linear space
and
Y
is a Banach space.

i.

Assume that for all
\varepsilon>0
there exist a Banach space

Z_{\varepsilon}
and a compact linear map
K_{\varepsilon}:X\to Z_{\varepsilon}
such that

\|Tx\|_{Y}\leq\varepsilon\|x\|_{X}+\|K_{\varepsilon}x\|_{Z_{\varepsilon}}

for all
x\in X
. Then
T
is compact.

ii.

Assume that
X
is reflexive Banach space,
T
is compact and

K:X\to Z
is an injective bounded linear map. Then for any
\varepsilon>0
there
exists
C_{\varepsilon}>0
such that

\|Tx\|_{Y}\leq\varepsilon\|x\|_{X}+C_{\varepsilon}\|Kx\|_{Z}

for all

.

Proof of Theorem
1
.

We define
K:D^{p}_{(0,1)}(\Omega)\to L^{p}_{(0,1)}(\Omega)
as
Kf=\rho f
where

\rho(z)=dist(z,b\Omega)
is the distance of
z
to the boundary of
\Omega
. Then
Corollary
1
implies that
K
is compact for all
1<p<\infty
.
We note that
K
is an injection as well.

Since
\Omega
is a bounded pseudoconvex domain,
D^{2}_{(0,1)}(\Omega)
is a Hilbert space.
Then we use ii. in Lemma
4
to get the following estimates:
for any
\varepsilon>0
there exists
C_{\varepsilon}>0
such that

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{2}}\leq\varepsilon(\|\overline{\partial}f\|_{L^{2}}+\|\overline{\partial}^{*}f\|_{L^{2}})+C_{\varepsilon}\|Kf\|_{L^{2}}

for all
f\in D^{2}_{(0,1)}(\Omega)
.

First we show how to get
L^{4}
-regularity. Let

F=f_{1}\overline{w}_{1}+f_{2}\overline{w}_{2}
be in
D^{4}_{(0,1)}(\Omega)\subset L^{4}_{(0,1)}(\Omega)

such that
f_{2}
is the normal component.
Because of Lemma
1
, without loss of generality,
we may assume that
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
are
C^{3}
-smooth on
\overline{\Omega}
.
We denote
F_{2}=f_{1}^{2}\overline{w}_{1}+f_{2}^{2}\overline{w}_{2}
. Since
f_{2}
vanishes
on the boundary we have
F_{2}\in D^{2}_{(0,1)}(\Omega)
. Then

\|F\|_{L^{4}}^{4}\approx\|F_{2}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}<\infty
and

\displaystyle\|F_{2}\|_{L^{2}}\leq

\displaystyle\varepsilon(\|\overline{\partial}F_{2}\|_{L^{2}}+\|\overline{\partial}^{*}F_{2}\|_{L^{2}})+C_{\varepsilon}\|KF_{2}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\varepsilon\left(\|f_{1}\overline{L}_{2}f_{1}-f_{2}\overline{L}_{1}f_{2}\|_{L^{2}}+\|f_{1}L_{1}f_{1}+f_{2}L_{2}f_{2}\|_{L^{2}}+\|F_{2}\|_{L^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle+C_{\varepsilon}\|KF_{2}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\varepsilon\left(\|f_{1}\overline{L}_{2}f_{1}-f_{1}\overline{L}_{1}f_{2}\|_{L^{2}}+\|f_{1}\overline{L}_{1}f_{2}-f_{2}\overline{L}_{1}f_{2}\|_{L^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle+\varepsilon\left(\|f_{1}L_{1}f_{1}+f_{1}L_{2}f_{2}\|_{L^{2}}+\|f_{1}L_{2}f_{2}-f_{2}L_{2}f_{2}\|_{L^{2}}+\|F_{2}\|_{L^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle+C_{\varepsilon}\|KF_{2}\|_{L^{2}}

By absorbing the terms that are multiple of
\|F_{2}\|_{L^{2}}

into the left hand side we get

(2)

\displaystyle\|F_{2}\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim

\displaystyle\varepsilon\left(\|f_{1}\overline{\partial}F\|_{L^{2}}+\|(f_{1}-f_{2})\overline{L}_{1}f_{2}\|_{L^{2}}+\|f_{1}\overline{\partial}^{*}F\|_{L^{2}}+\|(f_{1}-f_{2})L_{2}f_{2}\|_{L^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle+C_{\varepsilon}\|KF_{2}\|_{L^{2}}.

Using the facts that
\|F_{2}\|_{L^{2}}\approx\|F\|^{2}_{L^{4}}<\infty
,
KF_{2}=\rho F_{2}
,
and the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality we get

\displaystyle\|F\|^{2}_{L^{4}}\lesssim

\displaystyle\varepsilon\|F\|_{L^{4}}\left(\|\overline{\partial}F\|_{L^{4}}+\|\overline{\partial}^{*}F\|_{L^{4}}+\|f_{2}\|_{W^{1,4}}\right)+C_{\varepsilon}\|\rho^{1/2}F\|^{2}_{L^{4}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\varepsilon\|F\|_{L^{4}}\left(\|\overline{\partial}F\|_{L^{4}}+\|\overline{\partial}^{*}F\|_{L^{4}}+\|f_{2}\|_{W^{1,4}}\right)+C_{\varepsilon}\|F\|_{L^{4}}\|\rho F\|_{L^{4}}.

Using the inequality
2|xy|\leq|x|^{2}+|y|^{2}
on right hand
side we can absorb
\|F\|_{L^{4}}
into the left hand side and get
(
C_{\varepsilon}
below is different from its previous values, but it still depends on
\varepsilon
only)

(3)

\displaystyle\|F\|^{2}_{L^{4}}\lesssim\varepsilon\left(\|\overline{\partial}F\|^{2}_{L^{4}}+\|\overline{\partial}^{*}F\|^{2}_{L^{4}}+\|f_{2}\|^{2}_{W^{1,4}}\right)+C_{\varepsilon}\|\rho F\|^{2}_{L^{4}}.

Now we will concentrate on
\|f_{2}\|_{W^{1,4}}
. Using Lemma
2

and Lemma
3
we get

\displaystyle\|f_{2}\|^{2}_{W^{1,4}}\lesssim\|\overline{\partial}F\|^{2}_{L^{4}}+\|\overline{\partial}^{*}F\|^{2}_{L^{4}}+\|F\|^{2}_{L^{4}}.

Then the inequality (
3
) turns into

\displaystyle\|F\|^{2}_{L^{4}}\lesssim\varepsilon\left(\|\overline{\partial}F\|^{2}_{L^{4}}+\|\overline{\partial}^{*}F\|^{2}_{L^{4}}\right)+C_{\varepsilon}\|\rho F\|^{2}_{L^{4}}.

That is, we showed that for
\varepsilon>0
given there exists
C_{\varepsilon}>0
such that

(4)

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{J}F\|_{L^{4}}\leq\varepsilon\left(\|\overline{\partial}F\|_{L^{4}}+\|\overline{\partial}^{*}F\|_{L^{4}}\right)+C_{\varepsilon}\|KF\|_{L^{4}}

for
F\in D^{4}_{(0,1)}(\Omega)
. Therefore,
\mathcal{J}:D^{4}_{(0,1)}(\Omega)\to L^{4}_{(0,1)}(\Omega)

is a compact operator. Furthermore, since
\mathcal{J}
is
L^{4}
-regular,
one can show that
D^{4}_{(0,1)}(\Omega)
is a Banach space.

In a similar fashion, we use estimates (
4
) to show that for
every
\varepsilon>0
there exists
C_{\varepsilon}>0
such that

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{J}F\|_{L^{8}}\leq\varepsilon\left(\|\overline{\partial}F\|_{L^{8}}+\|\overline{\partial}^{*}F\|_{L^{8}}\right)+C_{\varepsilon}\|KF\|_{L^{8}}

for
F\in D^{8}_{(0,1)}(\Omega)
. That is,
\mathcal{J}:D^{8}_{(0,1)}(\Omega)\to L^{8}_{(0,1)}(\Omega)

is a compact linear map (by Lemma
4
) and
D^{8}_{(0,1)}(\Omega)

is a Banach space. Inductively, we show that

\mathcal{J}:D^{2^{p}}_{(0,1)}(\Omega)\to L^{2^{p}}_{(0,1)}(\Omega)
is a compact
linear map and
D^{2^{p}}_{(0,1)}(\Omega)
is a Banach space for
p\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
.

Note that for any
p\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
, we have

D^{2^{p}}_{(0,1)}(\Omega)\cap D^{2^{p+1}}_{(0,1)}(\Omega)=D^{2^{p+1}}_{(0,1)}(\Omega)

and
D^{2^{p}}_{(0,1)}(\Omega)+D^{2^{p+1}}_{(0,1)}(\Omega)\subset D^{2^{p}}_{(0,1)}(\Omega)
.
In other words, for
2^{p}<q<2^{p+1}
we get

D^{2^{p}}_{(0,1)}(\Omega)\subset D^{q}_{(0,1)}(\Omega)\subset D^{2^{p+1}}_{(0,1)}(\Omega)

and since the graph norm is the sum of
L^{p}
norms we conclude that

D^{q}_{(0,1)}(\Omega)
is an intermediate space (
[
BL76
, Definition 2.4.1]
)
for twoBanach spaces
D^{2^{p}}_{(0,1)}(\Omega)
and
D^{2^{p+1}}_{(0,1)}(\Omega)
.
Now, by the complex interpolation theorem (
[
BL76
, Chapter 4]
)
we conclude that
\mathcal{J}:D^{p}_{(0,1)}(\Omega)\to L^{p}_{(0,1)}(\Omega)
is

L^{p}
-regular and
D^{p}_{(0,1)}(\Omega)
is a Banach space for all
2\leq p<\infty
.
∎

Remark 1
.

The proof of Theorem
1
shows that we have the same result
for
(p,n-1)
-forms on
C^{4}
-smooth bounded pseudoconvex domains in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
.

We note that
Ker(\overline{\partial})
and
A^{2}(\Omega)^{\perp}
denote the set of

\overline{\partial}
-closed forms and the orthogonal complement of the
Bergman space
A^{2}(\Omega)\subset L^{2}(\Omega)
, respectively.

Proposition 2
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded pseudoconvex domain in
\mathbb{C}^{n},n\geq 2,
and
1<p\leq 2
.
Assume that
\mathcal{J}
is
L^{p}
-regular on
D^{p}_{(0,1)}(\Omega)
. Then the following operators
are bounded

i.

\overline{\partial}^{*}N_{2}:L^{p}_{(0,2)}(\Omega)\cap L^{2}_{(0,2)}(\Omega)\cap Ker(\overline{\partial})\to L^{p}_{(0,1)}(\Omega)
,

ii.

\overline{\partial}N_{0}:L^{p}(\Omega)\cap L^{2}(\Omega)\cap A^{2}(\Omega)^{\perp}\to L^{p}_{(0,1)}(\Omega)
.

Proof.

Since
\mathcal{J}
is
L^{p}
-regular and there exists
C>0
such that

(5)

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{p}}\leq C\left(\|\overline{\partial}f\|_{L^{p}}+\|\overline{\partial}^{*}f\|_{L^{p}}\right)

for
f\in D^{p}_{(0,1)}(\Omega)
. Note that
\overline{\partial}N_{0}g\in Dom^{2}(\overline{\partial}^{*})\subset Dom^{p}(\overline{\partial}^{*})

for
g\in L^{p}(\Omega)\cap L^{2}(\Omega)\cap A^{2}(\Omega)^{\perp}
and
p\leq 2
. Then applying
the estimate (
5
) to
\overline{\partial}N_{0}g
we get

\|\overline{\partial}N_{0}g\|_{L^{p}}\leq C\|\overline{\partial}^{*}\overline{\partial}N_{0}g\|_{L^{p}}=C\|g\|_{L^{p}}

for

.

Similarly, if we apply (
5
) to
\overline{\partial}^{*}N_{2}h
with

we get

\|\overline{\partial}^{*}N_{2}h\|_{L^{p}}\leq C\|\overline{\partial}\overline{\partial}^{*}N_{2}h\|_{L^{p}}=C\|h\|_{L^{p}}.

Hence the proof of the proposition is complete.
∎

The following example shows that the
L^{p}
boundedness of an operator
T

is not an automatic consequence of the compactness of
T
on
L^{2}
.

Example 1
.

Set

\phi(z)=\exp\left(\frac{-1}{1-|z|}\right)

and consider the weighted Bergman space
A^{2}(\mathbb{D},\phi)
on the unit disc.
The weighted Bergman projection
\mathbf{B}_{\phi}
is studied in

[
Dos04
,
Dos07
,
Zey13
]
, and it was noted that
\mathbf{B}_{\phi}

is unbounded on
L^{p}(\mathbb{D},\phi)
for any
p\not=2
.

We define an operator
T
on
L^{2}(\mathbb{D},\phi)
by

\displaystyle T

\displaystyle:L^{2}(\mathbb{D},\phi)\to L^{2}(\mathbb{D},\phi)

\displaystyle Tf(z)

\displaystyle=\mathbf{B}_{\phi}(f)(z)(1-|z|^{2})^{2}.

The operator
T
is bounded, linear and self-adjoint. Furthermore,
we show that
T
is compact on
L^{2}(\mathbb{D},\phi)
yet it is
unbounded on
L^{p}(\mathbb{D},\phi)
for any
p\not=2
.

First we show that
T
is compact. For
\varepsilon>0
there exists a compact set

K_{\varepsilon}\subset\mathbb{D}
such that
(1-|z|^{2})^{2}<\varepsilon
on
\mathbb{D}\setminus K
.
We define
S_{\varepsilon}f=\chi_{K_{\varepsilon}}Tf
where
\chi_{K_{\varepsilon}}
is the characteristic
function of
K_{\varepsilon}
. Montel’s theorem implies that
S_{\varepsilon}
is compact.

\|Tf\|^{2}=\|Tf\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{D}\setminus K,\phi)}+\|Tf\|^{2}_{L^{2}(K,\phi)}\leq\varepsilon\|\mathbf{B}_{\phi}f\|^{2}+\|S_{\varepsilon}f\|^{2}\leq\varepsilon\|f\|^{2}+\|S_{\varepsilon}f\|^{2}.

That is,
T
satisfies compactness estimates and by Lemma
4

it is a compact operator on
L^{2}(\mathbb{D},\phi)
(see also

[
D’A02
, Proposition V.2.3]
or
[
Str10
, Lemma 4.3]
).

Next we show that
T
is unbounded on
L^{p}(\mathbb{D},\phi)
for any

p\neq 2
. Let
0<p<2
and

f_{n}(z)=z^{kn}\overline{z}^{n}

where
k
is a positive integer to be determined later. Then one can compute that

Tf_{n}(z)=a_{n}z^{kn-n}(1-|z|^{2})^{2}

where

a_{n}=\frac{\int_{\mathbb{D}}|z|^{2kn}\phi(z)dA(z)}{\int_{\mathbb{D}}|z|^{2(k-1)n}\phi(z)dA(z)}.

Furthermore,

\displaystyle\frac{\|Tf_{n}\|_{p}^{p}}{\|f_{n}\|_{p}^{p}}=\left(\frac{\int_{\mathbb{D}}|z|^{2kn}\phi(z)dA(z)}{\int_{\mathbb{D}}|z|^{2(k-1)n}\phi(z)dA(z)}\right)^{p}\frac{\int_{\mathbb{D}}|z|^{pkn-pn}(1-|z|^{2})^{2p}\phi(z)dA(z)}{\int_{\mathbb{D}}|z|^{pkn+pn}\phi(z)dA(z)}.

We need the following asymptotic
[
Dos07
, Lemma 1]

\int_{\mathbb{D}}|z|^{t}(1-|z|^{2})^{2s}\phi(z)dA(z)\sim(t+1)^{\frac{-4s-3}{4}}\exp(-2\sqrt{t+1})

as
t\to\infty
.

We have the following asymptotic computations

\displaystyle\frac{\|Tf_{n}\|_{p}^{p}}{\|f_{n}\|_{p}^{p}}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{\int_{\mathbb{D}}|z|^{2kn}\phi(z)dA(z)}{\int_{\mathbb{D}}|z|^{2(k-1)n}\phi(z)dA(z)}\right)^{p}\frac{\int_{\mathbb{D}}|z|^{pkn-pn}(1-|z|^{2})^{2p}\phi(z)dA(z)}{\int_{\mathbb{D}}|z|^{pkn+pn}\phi(z)dA(z)}

\displaystyle\sim\frac{(2kn+1)^{-3p/4}\exp(-2p\sqrt{2kn+1})}{(2kn-2n+1)^{-3p/4}\exp(-2p\sqrt{2kn-2n+1})}

\displaystyle\times\frac{(pkn-pn+1)^{(-4p-3)/4}\exp(-2\sqrt{pkn-pn+1})}{(pkn+pn+1)^{-3/4}\exp(-2\sqrt{pkn+pn+1})}

\displaystyle\sim C_{k,p}n^{-p}\exp(2D_{k,p,n})

as
n\to\infty
where

C_{k,p}=\frac{(k+1)^{3/4}}{p^{p}k^{3p/4}(k-1)^{(3+p)/4}}

and

\displaystyle D_{k,p,n}=

\displaystyle-p\sqrt{2kn+1}+p\sqrt{2kn-2n+1}-\sqrt{pkn-pn+1}+\sqrt{pkn+pn+1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{-2pn}{\sqrt{2kn+1}+\sqrt{2kn-2n+1}}+\frac{2pn}{\sqrt{pkn-pn+1}+\sqrt{pkn+pn+1}}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{pn}{\sqrt{pkn+pn+1}}-\frac{pn}{\sqrt{2kn-2n+1}}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle p\sqrt{n}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{pk+p+1}}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2k-2}}\right).

Then one can show that for large
k
we have

\frac{1}{\sqrt{pk+p+1}}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2k-2}}\geq\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{p}}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\right)>0.

Therefore, for large
k
we have

D_{k,p,n}\geq\frac{\sqrt{n}p}{2}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{p}}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\right).

Therefore, for
k
large enough we have
C_{k,p}n^{-p}\exp(2D_{k,p,n})\to\infty
as
n\to\infty
.
Then we conclude that
\frac{\|Tf_{n}\|_{p}}{\|f_{n}\|_{p}}\to\infty
as
n\to\infty
.
Hence
T
is not bounded on
L^{p}(\mathbb{D},\phi)
for any
p<2
.
Furthermore, the fact that
T
is self-adjoint implies that
T
is unbounded
on
L^{p}(\mathbb{D},\phi)
for any
p\neq 2
.

Norm-Multiplicative Homomorphisms of Beurling Algebras
Matthew E. Kroeker, Alexander Stephens, Ross Stokke, Randy Yee

Abstract
We introduce and study “norm-multiplicative” homomorphisms φ : ℒ 1 ​ ( F ) → ℳ r ​ ( G ) \varphi:{\cal L}^{1}(F)\rightarrow{\cal M}_{r}(G) between group and measure algebras, and φ : ℒ 1 ​ ( ω F ) → ℳ ⁡ ( ω G ) \varphi:{\cal L}^{1}({\omega_{F}})\rightarrow{\cal M}({\omega_{G}}) between Beurling group and measure algebras, where F F and G G are locally compact groups with continuous weights ω F {\omega_{F}} and ω G {\omega_{G}} . Through a unified approach we recover, and sometimes strengthen, many of the main known results concerning homomorphisms and isomorphisms between these (Beurling) group and measure algebras. We provide a first description of all positive homomorphisms φ : ℒ 1 ​ ( F ) → ℳ r ​ ( G ) \varphi:{\cal L}^{1}(F)\rightarrow{\cal M}_{r}(G) . We state versions of our results that describe a variety of (possibly unbounded) homomorphisms φ : ℂ ​ F → ℂ ​ G \varphi:\mathbb{C}F\rightarrow\mathbb{C}G for (discrete) groups F F and G G . Primary MSC codes: 43A20, 43A10, 43A15, 43A22, 16S34 Key words and phrases: weighted locally compact group, Beurling algebra, homomorphisms of group algebras, group rings

Introduction

Throughout this article,
(F,{\omega_{F}})
and
(G,{\omega_{G}})
are weighted locally compact groups. The Beurling group and measure algebras over
(F,{\omega_{F}})
are denoted by
{\cal L}^{1}({\omega_{F}})
and
{\cal M}({\omega_{F}})
respectively; when
{\omega_{F}}
is the trivial weight, we obtain the usual group and measure algebras, denoted herein by
{\cal L}^{1}(F)
and
{\cal M}_{r}(F)
.

A natural endeavour in any area of mathematics is to describe the structure preserving maps between like objects. The existence of an algebra isomorphism between
\mathbb{C}\,\mathbb{Z}_{4}
and
\mathbb{C}(\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2})
shows that the algebraic structures of group and measure algebras do not alone determine the underlying group
[
15
]
. In abstract harmonic analysis, a well-studied question thus asks for a description of isomorphisms between various Banach algebras built over locally compact groups that take account of the additional structure of these algebras, and whether the existence of such isomorphisms determine the underlying locally compact groups. Work on this problem began with the study of isometric and bipositive isomorphisms between group algebras and measure algebras by Kawada, Wendel, Johnson and Strichartz in
[
16
,
29
,
30
,
13
,
27
]
(and elsewhere). These authors showed that the Banach algebra and order structures of group and measure algebras determine their underlying locally compact groups, i.e. that
{\cal L}^{1}(F)
and
{\cal M}_{r}(F)
are complete invariants for the locally compact group
F
. The literature concerned with determining whether other types of Banach algebras over
F
are complete invariants for
F
is quite extensive; a small sample of relevant papers is
[
7
,
8
,
9
,
17
,
28
]
.

More generally, the “homomorphism problem” in abstract harmonic analysis asks for a description of all homomorphisms
\varphi:{\cal L}^{1}(F)\rightarrow{\cal M}_{r}(G)
, (the dual problem asks for a description of all homomorphisms between Fourier and Fourier–Stieltjes algebras). This problem was completely solved in the abelian case by Paul Cohen in
[
1
]
, but remains open in general. Contractive homomorphisms
\varphi:{\cal L}^{1}(F)\rightarrow{\cal M}_{r}(G)
were first described by Greenleaf in
[
12
]
, and a characterization of such homomorphisms via a Cohen-type factorization is found in
[
25
]
; see
[
25
]
for more about the history of this old problem. There had been no known description of the positive (order-preserving) homomorphisms
\varphi:{\cal L}^{1}(F)\rightarrow{\cal M}_{r}(G)
.

Beurling group and measure algebras provide the setting for a rather active area of research, e.g., see
[
2
,
4
,
5
,
6
,
14
,
18
,
21
,
22
,
23
]
.
Zadeh
[
32
]
and Ghahramani-Zadeh
[
10
]
recently showed that the Beurling group algebra
{\cal L}^{1}({\omega_{F}})
and the Beurling measure algebra
{\cal M}({\omega_{F}})
are, up to an isometric/bipositive isomorphism, complete invariants for the underlying weighted locally compact group
(F,{\omega_{F}})
.

Extending work of Pym
[
20
]
and Greenleaf
[
12
]
to the setting of Beurling measure algebras, in Section 2 we describe all norm-one, and positive norm-one, idempotents in
{\cal M}({\omega_{G}})
. In Section 3 we introduce and completely describe the norm-multiplicative subgroups — or
\rm{NM}_{\omega_{G}}
-subgroups (
\rm{NM}
-subgroups in the case of the trivial weight) — of

of

-subgroups of
{\cal M}({\omega_{G}})

{\cal M}({\omega_{G}})

[

]
(and
[
25
]

[

,
25
]

\rm{NM}
-subgroup of

{\cal M}_{r}(G)

{\cal M}_{r}(G)

We call a bounded homomorphism
\varphi:{\cal L}^{1}({\omega_{F}})\rightarrow{\cal M}({\omega_{G}})
a
\rm{NM}_{\omega_{G}}

\rm{NM}
-)homomorphism if the image of
F
under the canonical strict-to-weak
∗
continuous extension of

to
{\cal M}({\omega_{F}})
is a
\rm{NM}_{\omega_{G}}
-subgroup of
{\cal M}({\omega_{G}})
(
\rm{NM}
-subgroup of
{\cal M}_{r}(G)
). We introduce some “basic homomorphisms” and characterize all
\rm{NM}_{\omega_{G}}
-homomorphisms (and
\rm{NM}
-homomorphisms for which

\rm{NM}_{\omega_{G}}

\rm{NM}
-homomorphisms

[

[

In Section 5, we state versions of some our main results describing various (not necessarily bounded) homomorphisms
\varphi:\mathbb{C}F\rightarrow\mathbb{C}G

Observe that
\nu\in{\cal M}_{r}(G)\cap{\cal M}(\omega)
, since it has compact support. Letting

,
\mu_{K}=\mu\large{|}_{\mathfrak{S}(K)}
, and
\omega_{K}=\omega\large{|}_{K}
, one can readily check that

, so by Remark

\|\nu_{K}\|_{\omega_{K}}=\|\mu_{K}\|=1
and

. The above claim applied to

and

shows that for any

for each

takes its maximum value at

\mu_{K}\cdot\phi
is constant on

is a translation-invariant positive norm-one measure on

is the normalized Haar measure on

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
\nu\in{\cal M}(\omega)
be a positive norm-one idempotent. Then there is a compact subgroup
K
of
G
such that
\nu=m_{K}
and
\omega\equiv 1
on
K
.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.1
,
K={\rm s}(\nu)
is a compact subgroup of
G
and
\omega\nu=m_{K}
or, equivalently,
\nu=(1/\omega)m_{K}
. We can assume that
G=K
; otherwise, as shown in the proof of Lemma
2.1
, we can work with
\nu_{K}=\nu\large{|}_{\mathfrak{S}(K)}
and
\omega_{K}=\omega\large{|}_{K}
. As
m_{K}
is also an idempotent in
{\cal M}_{r}(K)={\cal M}_{cr}(K)={\cal M}(\omega)
, for any
\phi\in C(K)^{+}
,

\displaystyle\langle m_{K},\phi/\omega\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\phi\,{1\over\omega}\,dm_{K}=\int\phi\,d\left({1\over\omega}m_{K}\right)=\langle\nu,\phi\rangle_{\omega}=\langle\nu*\nu,\phi\rangle_{\omega}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\iint\phi(st)\,d\nu(s)\,d\nu(t)=\iint\phi(st){1\over\omega(s)}\,dm_{K}(s)\,{1\over\omega(t)}\,dm_{K}(t)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\iint{\phi\over\omega}\left(st\right)\,dm_{K}(s)\,dm_{K}(t)=\langle m_{K}*m_{K},\phi/\omega\rangle=\langle m_{K},\phi/\omega\rangle.

Taking
\phi=\omega
(on
K
), we obtain

\iint{\omega(st)\over\omega(s)\omega(t)}\,dm_{K}(s)\,dm_{K}(t)=\int 1\,dm_{K}(s)\,dm_{K}(t).

Letting
\displaystyle g(s,t)=1-{\omega(st)\over\omega(s)\omega(t)}
,
g
is continuous and non-negative on
K\times K
, and

\displaystyle\int_{K\times K}g(s,t)\,d(m_{K}\times m_{K})(s,t)=0.

Hence,
g
vanishes on

K\times K
, meaning that
\omega(st)=\omega(s)\omega(t)
for
s,t\in K
. Thus,
\omega(K)
is a compact subgroup of
(0,\infty)
, and therefore
\omega\equiv 1
on
K
.
∎

Remark 2.3
.

If
\omega\equiv 1
on a subgroup

of

, then

is constant on the cosets of

, so

. In this case,

is hence a well-defined continuous function on

; moreover,
\omega_{G/K}
is a weight function on

when

.

In the case of the trivial weight, the following result contains, and gives independent proofs of, Theorems 2.1.1 and 2.1.2 of
[

]
. In the proof, we use the fact that

if and only if

for

.

Proposition 2.4
.

Suppose that
\mu,\nu\in{\cal M}(\omega)
and
\|\mu*\nu\|_{\omega}=\|\mu\|_{\omega}\|\nu\|_{\omega}
. Then
|\mu*\nu|=|\mu|*|\nu|
and
{\rm s}(\mu*\nu)=\overline{{\rm s}(\mu){\rm s}(\nu)}
.

Proof.

By
[
3
, III.10.3]
,
|\mu*\nu|\leq|\mu|*|\nu|
. To establish the reverse inequality, we show that
\omega|\mu*\nu|\leq\omega|\mu|*|\nu|
. To this end, suppose towards a contradiction that
\omega|\mu|*|\nu|(A)>\omega|\mu*\nu|(A)
for some

. Then

\displaystyle\|\mu*\nu\|_{\omega}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|(\omega|\mu*\nu|)_{e}\|=(\omega|\mu*\nu|)_{e}(G)=(\omega|\mu*\nu|)_{e}(A)+(\omega|\mu*\nu|)_{e}(G\backslash A)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\omega|\mu*\nu|(A)+(\omega|\mu|*|\nu|)_{e}(G\backslash A)

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle\omega|\mu|*|\nu|(A)+(\omega|\mu|*|\nu|)_{e}(G\backslash A)=(\omega|\mu|*|\nu|)_{e}(G)=\||\mu|*|\nu|\|_{\omega}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|\mu\|_{\omega}\|\nu\|_{\omega}=\|\mu*\nu\|_{\omega}.

By
[
3
, Proposition III.10.5]
, we now obtain

{\rm s}(\mu*\nu)={\rm s}(|\mu*\nu|)={\rm s}(|\mu|*|\nu|)=\overline{{\rm s}(|\mu|){\rm s}(|\nu|)}=\overline{{\rm s}(\mu){\rm s}(\nu)}.\qed

(i) Let

(ii) If

and part (i).
∎

{\cal M}(\omega)

-

\rm{NM}

Lemma 4.21

(i) The set of extreme points of the unit ball of

Let

Suppose that
\mu_{i}\rightarrow\mu

Definition 4.23

Proposition 4.29
.

Let
\mu\in{\cal M}(\omega)
. Then
\mu\in{\cal L}^{1}(\omega)
if and only if
x\mapsto\mu*\delta_{x}:G\rightarrow({\cal M}(\omega),\|\cdot\|_{\omega})
is continuous.

Proof.

Suppose that
x\mapsto\mu*\delta_{x}
is continuous. Let
(f_{i})
be a net of positive functions in
C_{00}(G)\subseteq{\cal L}^{1}(\omega)
such that
{\rm s}(f_{i})\rightarrow{e_{G}}
and
\int f_{i}\,dt=1
. Let
\varepsilon>0
and take
i_{0}
such that
\|\mu*\delta_{t}-\mu\|_{\omega}<\varepsilon
for

and

. Then, for
i\succeq i_{0}
and
\psi\in C_{00}(G)
with
\|\psi\|_{\infty,\omega^{-1}}\leq 1
,

\displaystyle|\langle\mu*f_{i}-\mu,\psi\rangle_{\omega}|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\iint f_{i}(s^{-1}t)\,d\mu(s)\,\psi(t)\,dt-\int\psi(s)\,d\mu(s)\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\iint f_{i}(t)\psi(st)\,dt\,d\mu(s)-\iint f_{i}(t)\psi(s)\,d\mu(s)\,dt\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\int f_{i}(t)\left(\int\psi(st)\,d\mu(s)-\int\psi(s)\,d\mu(s)\right)\,dt\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\int f_{i}(t)\langle\mu*\delta_{t}-\mu,\psi\rangle_{\omega}\,dt\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{{\rm s}(f_{i})}f_{i}(t)\|\mu*\delta_{t}-\mu\|_{\omega}\|\psi\|_{\infty,\omega^{-1}}\,dt\leq\varepsilon,

where we have used formula (10) in
[

and the Fubini Theorem. Hence,
\|\mu*f_{i}-\mu\|_{\omega}\rightarrow 0
; since

is a closed ideal in

[

. ∎

5
\rm{NM}
-homomorphisms
\mathbb{C}F\rightarrow\mathbb{C}G

In what follows,
F
and
G
are discrete groups. The purpose of this section is to observe that versions of all the previous results hold for the algebraists’ group algebras
\mathbb{C}F
and
\mathbb{C}G
. We believe these results are also new. To distinguish it from the pointwise-defined product, we continue to use the convolution notation
f*g
for the product of
f
and
g
in
\mathbb{C}G
and, when relevant, we use the
\|\cdot\|_{1}
-norm on these algebras. However, unless explicitly stated otherwise,
we no longer assume that linear maps on
\mathbb{C}F
are bounded/continuous with respect to any norm or topology.
We employ the obvious analogues of all definitions previously discussed, dropping all boundedness/continuity conditions (unless explicitly stated otherwise). In particular:

•

a subgroup
\Gamma
of
(\mathbb{C}G,*)
is a
\rm{NM}
-subgroup if
|f*g|=|f|*|g|
(equivalently,
\|f*g\|_{1}=\|f\|_{1}\|g\|_{1}
) for every
f,g\in\Gamma
;

•

a homomorphism — not necessarily bounded —
\varphi:\mathbb{C}F\rightarrow\mathbb{C}G
is a
\rm{NM}
-homomorphism if
\Gamma_{\varphi}=\varphi(F)
is a
\rm{NM}
-subgroup of
\mathbb{C}G
.

Remark 5.1
.

Suppose that
{\omega_{F}}
and
{\omega_{G}}
are weights on
F
and
G
. With respect to the norm
\|f\|_{\omega_{G}}=\|f{\omega_{G}}\|_{1}
,
\mathbb{C}G
is a normed algebra, denoted
\mathbb{C}(G,{\omega_{G}})
, with completion
\ell^{1}(G,{\omega_{G}})={\cal L}^{1}({\omega_{G}})={\cal M}({\omega_{G}})
in this, the discrete case. Although we have studied
\rm{NM}_{{\omega_{G}}}
-subgroups of
{\cal M}({\omega_{G}})
and
\rm{NM}_{{\omega_{G}}}
-homomorphisms
\varphi:{\cal L}^{1}({\omega_{F}})\rightarrow{\cal M}({\omega_{G}})
, our primary focus has been on
\rm{NM}
-subgroups and
\rm{NM}
-homomorphisms. As sets, and algebraically,
\mathbb{C}G=\mathbb{C}(G,{\omega_{G}})
, so
\varphi:\mathbb{C}(F,{\omega_{F}})\rightarrow\mathbb{C}(G,{\omega_{G}})
is a
\rm{NM}
-homomorphism if and only if
\varphi:\mathbb{C}F\rightarrow\mathbb{C}G
is a
\rm{NM}
-homomorphism (because we are no longer requiring continuity of
\varphi
, which would change things) if and only if
\Gamma_{\varphi}
is a
\rm{NM}
-subgroup of
\mathbb{C}G
. Thus, in this section in which we are no longer interested in, or required to consider, continuity, there is little purpose in considering non-trivial weights.

Let
K
be a finite subgroup of
G
,
\rho:K\rightarrow\mathbb{T}
a homomorphism. Then

m_{K}={1\over|K|}\sum_{k\in K}k\qquad\text{and}\qquad\rho m_{K}={1\over|K|}\sum_{k\in K}\rho(k)k

are (norm-one) idempotents in
\mathbb{C}G
; when
H
is a subgroup of
G
and
K\lhd H
,
m_{K}\in Z(\mathbb{C}H)
, and when it is also true that
\ker\rho\lhd H
and
K/\ker\rho\leq Z(H/\ker\rho)
, then
\rho m_{K}\in Z(\mathbb{C}H)
(and conversely).

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
\Gamma
be a
\rm{NM}
-subgroup of
\mathbb{C}G
with identity
\iota_{\Gamma}
. Then:

(i)

H={\rm s}(\Gamma)=\bigcup_{f\in\Gamma}{\rm s}(f)\leq G
,
\iota_{\Gamma}=\rho m_{K}
for some finite normal subgroup
K
of
H
and a homomorphism
\rho:K\rightarrow\mathbb{T}
such that
\ker\rho\lhd H
and
K/\ker\rho\leq Z(H/\ker\rho)
;

(ii)

\Omega_{\Gamma}=\{(\alpha,t)\in\mathbb{C}^{\times}\times H:\alpha t*\rho m_{K}\in\Gamma\}
is a subgroup of
\mathbb{C}^{\times}\times H
and

\Gamma_{\mathbb{C}^{\times}\times H}=\{\alpha t*\rho m_{K}:(\alpha,t)\in\mathbb{C}^{\times}\times H\}
is a
\rm{NM}
-subgroup of
\mathbb{C}G
containing
\Gamma
;

(iii)

\phi_{0}:(\alpha,t)\mapsto\alpha t*\rho m_{K}

defines a group homomorphism of
\Omega_{\Gamma}
onto
\Gamma
and of
\mathbb{C}^{\times}\times H
onto
\Gamma_{\mathbb{C}^{\times}\times H}
with kernel
\Omega_{\rho}=\{(\rho(k),k):k\in K\}
.

If
\Gamma
is a subgroup contained in the unit ball of
\mathbb{C}G
, then
\Gamma
is a
\rm{NM}
-subgroup of
\mathbb{C}G
, and
\mathbb{C}^{x}
can be replaced by
\mathbb{T}
in the above conditions; if
\Gamma
is a positive subgroup of
\mathbb{C}G
, i.e., if each
f\in\Gamma
is positive, then
\Gamma
is a
\rm{NM}
-subgroup of
\mathbb{C}G
,
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
can be replaced by
(0,\infty)
in the above conditions, and
\iota_{\Gamma}=m_{K}
.

Though Proposition
5.2
follows from the results in Section 3, it can be proved directly without considering the topological and measure-theoretic complications inherent in the general case.

Let
\gamma:F\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{\times}
,
\theta:F\rightarrow G
be homomorphisms,
K
a finite normal subgroup of
H
. The basic homomorphisms act on group elements by

\displaystyle M_{\gamma}^{*}:\mathbb{C}F\rightarrow\mathbb{C}G:s\mapsto\gamma(s)s,

\displaystyle j_{\theta}^{*}:\mathbb{C}F\rightarrow\mathbb{C}G:s\mapsto\theta(s),

\displaystyle j_{\gamma,\theta}^{*}=j_{\theta}^{*}\circ M_{\gamma}^{*}:\mathbb{C}F\rightarrow\mathbb{C}G:s\mapsto\gamma(s)\theta(s),

\displaystyle S_{K}^{*}:\mathbb{C}(H/K)\hookrightarrow\mathbb{C}H:sK\mapsto s*m_{K}.

If we assume that a
\rm{NM}
-homomorphism
\varphi:\mathbb{C}F\rightarrow\mathbb{C}G
is continuous, then it has a unique extension to a
\rm{NM}
-homomorphism
\varphi:\ell^{1}(F)={\cal M}_{r}(F)\rightarrow\ell^{1}(G)={\cal M}_{r}(G)
. Since the basic homomorphisms map
\mathbb{C}F
into
\mathbb{C}G
(etc.), the results in Section 4 immediately yield corresponding results for bounded
\rm{NM}
-homomorphisms
\varphi:\mathbb{C}F\rightarrow\mathbb{C}G
. However, versions of all statements from Section 4 hold without assuming
\varphi:\mathbb{C}F\rightarrow\mathbb{C}G
is bounded. The proofs from Section 4 can be used as a guide to establishing these results, however the proofs for homomorphisms
\varphi:\mathbb{C}F\rightarrow\mathbb{C}G
are usually much simpler. We conclude with a sample of some of these statements.

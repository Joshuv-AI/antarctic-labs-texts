A priori estimates for finite-energy sign-changing blowing-up solutions of critical elliptic equations
Bruno Premoselli

Abstract
. We prove sharp pointwise blow-up estimates for finite-energy sign-changing solutions of critical equations of Schrödinger-Yamabe type on a closed Riemannian manifold ( M , g ) (M,g) of dimension n ≥ 3 n\geq 3 . This is a generalisation of the so-called C 0 C^{0} -theory for positive solutions of Schrödinger-Yamabe type equations. To deal with the sign-changing case we develop a method of proof that combines an a priori bubble-tree analysis with a finite-dimensional reduction, and reduces the proof to obtaining sharp a priori blow-up estimates for a linear problem.



1.
Introduction and statement of the results

1.1.
Pointwise estimates for finite-energy blowing-up sequences of solutions

Let
(M,g)
be a smooth and closed Riemannian manifold of dimension
n\geq 3
, where closed means compact without boundary. Let
(h_{\alpha})_{\alpha}
be a sequence of bounded functions in
M
. We are interested in this work in the following Schrödinger-Yamabe type equation in
M
:

\triangle_{g}u+h_{\alpha}u=|u|^{2^{*}-2}u,

where
\triangle_{g}=-\textrm{div}_{g}(\nabla\cdot)
is the Laplace-Beltrami operator and
2^{*}=\frac{2n}{n-2}
is the critical exponent for the embedding of the Sobolev space
H^{1}(M)
into Lebesgue spaces. This equation originated a vast amount of work and has been the target of active investigation for decades.

Let
(u_{\alpha})_{\alpha}
, with
u_{\alpha}\in H^{1}(M)
, be a sequence of solutions of

\triangle_{g}u_{\alpha}+h_{\alpha}u_{\alpha}=|u_{\alpha}|^{2^{*}-2}u_{\alpha}

(1.1)

in
M
. Unless specified otherwise, the
u_{\alpha}
’s are allowed to change sign. Since
h_{\alpha}
is bounded the regularity result of Trudinger
[
56
]
ensures that
u_{\alpha}\in C^{1,\beta}(M)
for all
0<\beta<1
. Following standard terminology we say that
(u_{\alpha})_{\alpha}

blows-up with finite energy
if

\|u_{\alpha}\|_{H^{1}(M)}\leq C\quad\textrm{ and }\quad\lim_{\alpha\to+\infty}\|u_{\alpha}\|_{L^{\infty}(M)}=+\infty

(1.2)

for some
C>0
independent of
\alpha
. The asymptotic behavior of
(u_{\alpha})_{\alpha}
in the energy space
H^{1}(M)
has been known since Struwe’s seminal work
[
53
]
: assume e.g. that
h_{\alpha}
converges in
L^{2}(M)
towards
h
. Then, up to a subsequence,
(u_{\alpha})_{\alpha}
decomposes as

u_{\alpha}=u_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{k}V_{i,\alpha}+\phi_{\alpha},

(1.3)

where
\|\phi_{\alpha}\|_{H^{1}(M)}\to 0
as
\alpha\to+\infty
,
u_{0}
is a weak solution of the limiting equation

\triangle_{g}u_{0}+hu_{0}=|u_{0}|^{2^{*}-2}u_{0}

in
M
,
k\geq 1
is an integer and, for all
1\leq i\leq k
,
V_{i,\alpha}
is a
sign-changing bubble
. More precisely, there exists a nontrivial, possibly sign-changing finite-energy solution
V_{i}
of the euclidean Yamabe equation in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
:

\triangle_{\xi}V_{i}=|V_{i}|^{2^{*}-2}V_{i}

and there exist a sequence
(x_{i,\alpha})_{\alpha}
of points of
M
and a sequence
(\mu_{i,\alpha})_{\alpha}
of positive numbers with
\mu_{i,\alpha}\to 0
as
\alpha\to+\infty
such that

V_{i,\alpha}(x)\approx\mu_{i,\alpha}^{1-\frac{n}{2}}V_{i}\Big(\frac{1}{\mu_{i,\alpha}}\exp_{x_{i,\alpha}}^{-1}(x)\Big)

at first order, where
\exp_{x_{i,\alpha}}
is the exponential map for
g
at
x_{i,\alpha}
(for the precise expression of
V_{i,\alpha}
see (
2.14
) below). The sequences
(x_{i,\alpha})_{\alpha}
and
(\mu_{i,\alpha})_{\alpha}
,
1\leq i\leq k
, moreover satisfy (see e.g. Bahri-Coron
[
5
]
and Solimini
[
52
]
)

\mu_{i,\alpha}\to 0\quad\textrm{ and }\quad\frac{\mu_{i,\alpha}}{\mu_{j,\alpha}}+\frac{\mu_{j,\alpha}}{\mu_{i,\alpha}}+\frac{d_{g}(x_{i,\alpha},x_{j,\alpha})^{2}}{\mu_{i,\alpha}\mu_{j,\alpha}}\to+\infty\quad\textrm{ for all }i\neq j

(1.4)

as
\alpha\to+\infty
, we have
u_{\alpha}\rightharpoonup u_{0}
in
H^{1}(M)
and the energy of
u_{\alpha}
is quantified:

\|u_{\alpha}\|_{H^{1}(M)}^{2}=\|u_{0}\|_{H^{1}(M)}^{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{k}\|\nabla V_{i}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2}+o(1)

as
\alpha\to+\infty
. When all the functions
u_{\alpha}
are assumed to be
positive
the profiles
V_{i,\alpha}
are equal to the so-called positive standard bubbles

B_{i,\alpha}(x)=\frac{\mu_{i,\alpha}^{\frac{n-2}{2}}}{\big(\mu_{i,\alpha}^{2}+\frac{d_{g}(x_{i,\alpha},x)^{2}}{n(n-2)}\big)^{\frac{n-2}{2}}},\quad x\in M.

The
B_{i,\alpha}
’s carry the same energy up to an error term and, when
u_{\alpha}\geq 0
, the energy of
u_{\alpha}
is quantified as

\|u_{\alpha}\|_{H^{1}(M)}^{2}=\|u_{0}\|_{H^{1}(M)}^{2}+kE_{n}+o(1),

where
E_{n}
is a dimensional constant (see Section
2
below for more details). In the sign-changing regime the situation is more intricate as we do not have one sole energy level for the
V_{i}
’s (see e.g. Ding
[
17
]
). We let, for all
x\in M
,

B_{0,\alpha}(x)=\left\{\begin{aligned} &0&\textrm{ if }u_{0}\equiv 0\textrm{ and }\ker(\triangle_{g}+h)=\{0\}\\
&1&\textrm{ otherwise}\end{aligned}\right.,\\

where we keep the notations of (
1.3
).

An important issue is to know whether the description (
1.3
) holds true in the
C^{0}
space, and thus whether or not pointwise estimates on
(u_{\alpha})_{\alpha}
inherited from (
1.3
) hold. This has been known to hold true for positive solutions of (
1.1
) for almost two decades and has had spectacular applications, but no analogous results were obtained in the sign-changing case. In this work we answer this question positively when
u_{\alpha}
is allowed to change sign. Our main result is as follows :

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
(M,g)
be a closed Riemannian manifold of dimension
n\geq 3
, let
(h_{\alpha})_{\alpha}
be a sequence of functions converging in
L^{\infty}(M)
to some function
h
in
M
and let
(u_{\alpha})_{\alpha}
be a sequence of solutions of (
1.1
) that satisfies (
1.2
). Let
u_{0}
and
V_{1,\alpha},\dots,V_{k,\alpha}
be as in the Struwe decomposition (
1.3
) of
u_{\alpha}
, where the
V_{i,\alpha}
’s are given by (
2.14
) below. Then, up to passing to a subsequence for
(u_{\alpha})_{\alpha}
, we have

\frac{u_{\alpha}-u_{0}-\sum_{i=1}^{k}V_{i,\alpha}}{\sum_{i=0}^{k}B_{i,\alpha}}\to 0

in
L^{\infty}(M)
as
\alpha\to+\infty
.

The statement of Theorem
1.1
reformulates as follows: if
(u_{\alpha})_{\alpha}
is a sequence of solutions of (
1.1
) that blows-up with finite-energy then, up to passing to a subsequence for
(u_{\alpha})_{\alpha}
, for any sequence
(x_{\alpha})_{\alpha}
of points of
M

u_{\alpha}(x_{\alpha})=u_{0}(x_{\alpha})+\sum_{i=1}^{k}V_{i,\alpha}(x_{\alpha})+o\Big(\sum_{i=0}^{k}B_{i,\alpha}(x_{\alpha})\Big)

holds as
\alpha\to+\infty
. In other words, the difference between
u_{\alpha}
, the weak limit and the whole set of (possibly sign-changing) bubbles in Struwe’s decomposition is globally pointwise small with respect to the sum of the positive bubbles. In Theorem
1.1
no assumption on
h
is required: in particular one does not need to assume that
\triangle_{g}+h
has no kernel. No assumption on the limiting solutions
V_{i}
,
1\leq i\leq k
, of the Yamabe equation in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is made either.

Theorem
1.1
is the first comprehensive result of this type for finite-energy
sign-changing
solutions of (
1.1
) in dimensions
n\geq 3
. For
positive
solutions, however, the subject has a rich history, and pointwise blow-up estimates have been known for decades to be a fundamental technical tool to prove both existence results and
a priori
bounds.

If no bound on the energy is assumed, sharp blow-up estimates for positive solutions of (
1.1
) have been used to prove compactness and stability results. A remarkable success is the compactness of positive solutions of the Yamabe equation on manifolds which are not conformally diffeomorphic to the standard sphere, that corresponds to the case
h_{\alpha}\equiv\frac{n-2}{4(n-1)}S_{g}
, where
S_{g}
is the scalar curvature of
(M,g)
: see Li-Zhu
[
32
]
, Druet
[
18
]
, Marques
[
33
]
, Li-Zhang
[
30
]
, Khuri-Marques-Schoen
[
28
]
for compactness statements until dimension
24
and Brendle
[
7
]
and Marques-Brendle
[
8
]
for counter-examples when
n\geq 25
. We also mention the stability result for positive solutions of (
1.1
) of Druet
[
19
]
when
n\geq 3
and
h<\frac{n-2}{4(n-1)}S_{g}
in
M
(for the definition of what we mean by “stability” here see Hebey
[
27
]
). Global degree arguments based on a sharp pointwise blow-up description of positive solutions have also been used to prove involved existence and compactness results for the Nirenberg problem on the sphere, see e.g. the celebrated results of Chen-Lin
[
10
]
and Li
[
31
]
. It is worth mentioning that in most of these results one needs to prove, in the course of the analysis, that possible concentration points are isolated, thus reducing the problem to understanding isolated one-bubble blow-up.

This is however specific to the framework of the aforementioned results: even under the assumption (
1.2
) of finite-energy blow-up, and as the counter-examples of Robert-Vétois
[
50
]
show, concentration points for sequences
(u_{\alpha})_{\alpha}
of positive solutions of (
1.1
) need not be isolated. One then needs a pointwise description like that of Theorem
1.1
to describe the interactions between non-isolated bubbling profiles. Theorem
1.1
for
positive
solutions of (
1.1
) satisfying (
1.2
) was first proven in Druet-Hebey-Robert
[
21
]
, and the proof was subsequently shortened in Druet-Hebey
[
20
]
(see also Hebey
[
27
]
). It was then successfully applied to obtain
a priori
bounds for finite-energy solutions of (
1.1
). Druet
[
18
]
proved for instance that, if
n=4,5
or
n\geq 7
,
h_{\alpha}\to h
in
C^{1}(M)
and
h
satisfies
h(x)\neq\frac{n-2}{4(n-1)}S_{g}(x)
for all
x\in M
, every positive finite-energy sequence of solutions of (
1.1
) is uniformly bounded in
C^{2}(M)
(for details on the six-dimensional case see Druet-Hebey
[
20
]
).

The case
h_{\alpha}\equiv h>\frac{n-2}{4(n-1)}S_{g}
is worth mentioning: no finite-energy positive blowing-up solutions exist by Druet’s
[
18
]
result in this case but sequences of positive solutions of (
1.1
) whose energy goes to infinity have been constructed on
\mathbb{S}^{n}
by Chen-Wei-Yan
[
12
]
for
n\geq 5
(see also Vétois-Wang
[
58
]
for
n=4
). This intermediate case shows the relevance of developing analytical tools like Theorem
1.1
to specifically deal with the finite-energy case. Let us also mention that analogous results to Theorem
1.1
exist in dimension
2
for positive solutions of Liouville-type and Moser-Trudinger-type equations, and have been used to obtain existence and quantification results. See for instance Chen-Lin
[
11
]
, Li-Shafrir
[
29
]
, Druet-Thizy
[
22
]
and the references therein.

For
sign-changing
solutions of (
1.1
) the situation is much less understood. If no bound on the energy is assumed the situation is dimmer than for positive solutions. As shown by Premoselli-Vétois
[
45
]
, one can only expect compactness in dimensions
n\geq 7
, when
h<\frac{n-2}{4(n-1)}S_{g}
and when the negative part of the sequence
(u_{\alpha})_{\alpha}
is a priori bounded. The counterpart of Druet
[
19
]
fails for sign-changing solutions in all the other cases, as proven by Ding
[
17
]
for the Yamabe equation, Premoselli-Vétois
[
45
]
and Vétois
[
57
]
. The situation for sign-changing finite-energy blow-up is also considerably more involved than in the positive case: finite-energy sign-changing solutions of the Yamabe equation in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
are not classified, hence quantification of the energy is lost, and pointwise interactions between bubbles are weaker due to stronger decay at infinity. Few sign-changing finite-energy compactness results are known and this was mainly due, up to now, to the lack of precise pointwise blow-up estimates as those of Theorem
1.1
. We mention for instance the finite-energy compactness result of Vétois
[
57
]
when
n\geq 7
,
h<\frac{n-2}{4(n-1)}
and
(M,g)
is locally conformally flat, that relies on arguments from Devillanova-Solimini
[
16
]
in the Euclidean case. We also mention Ghoussoub-Mazumdar-Robert
[
26
]
, where the authors obtain sharp pointwise blow-up estimates for sign-changing solutions of Hardy-Schrödinger equations with boundary singularity, a case in which the bubbles are centered at the singularity.

Theorem
1.1
paves the way for more sophisticated sign-changing compactness results. Similar techniques have recently been applied to obtaining quantitative stability results for Struwe’s decomposition for positive Palais-Smale sequences, see Figalli-Glaudo
[
25
]
and Deng-Sun-Wei
[
15
]
in the positive case. It would be interesting to see if the approach of Theorem
1.1
can be applied to obtain analogous quantitative stability results in the sign-changing case.

1.2.
Strategy of proof and organisation of the paper

To prove Theorem
1.1
we develop a new strategy of proof, different from those in Druet-Hebey-Robert
[
21
]
, Druet-Hebey
[
20
]
and Ghoussoub-Mazumdar-Robert
[
26
]
, that allows us to overcome the technical challenges inherent to the sign-changing setting, such as the failure of Harnack inequalities and maximum principles or the presence of the kernel of the limiting operator
\triangle_{g}+h
. Our proof combines a bubble-tree analysis with a finite-dimensional reduction. The latter is in the spirit of Ambrosetti-Malchiodi
[
1
,
2
]
, Bahri
[
4
]
, Rey
[
46
]
, Rey-Wei
[
47
]
or Wei
[
59
]
.

The paper is organised as follows. In Section
2
we recall a few results on sign-changing solutions of the Yamabe equation in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and introduce the bubbling profiles we will consider. Section
3
is the core of the analysis of the paper. We linearize equation (
1.1
) at an approximate solution that is close to
u_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{k}V_{i,\alpha}
(more general configurations have to be taken into account) and we prove Theorem
3.2
below. Theorem
3.2
essentially says that solutions of the linearized equation inherit explicit pointwise estimates whose precision depends on the
pointwise
precision of the error of the approximate solution
u_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{k}V_{i,\alpha}
. It can be seen as a linearised version of Theorem
1.1
. The proof of Theorem
3.2
goes through an
a priori
bubble-tree analysis, where sharp estimates are obtained by an inductive bootstrap procedure in the bubble-tree. It requires a full
a priori
analysis and is not a simple invertibility result in weighted spaces. In Section
4
we first perform a nonlinear perturbative argument (Proposition
4.2
) and prove that (
1.1
) can be uniquely solved, up to kernel elements, by a function that satisfies explicit pointwise estimates; the analysis takes place in strong spaces and heavily relies on Theorem
3.2
. As a consequence we prove a slightly more general result than Theorem
1.1
, Theorem
4.3
below, that shows that approximate solutions of (
1.1
) obtained by Lyapunov-Schmidt-like techniques satisfy the same pointwise bounds as in Theorem
1.1
. This is a result which if of interest in itself. Finally the Appendix collects a few technical results used throughout the paper.

The method of proof that we develop in Sections
3
and
4
is particularly robust and applies to more general equations than (
1.1
). The invertibility result of Section
3
, Theorem
3.2
, allows us to bypass most of the technical difficulties that arise when proving blow-up pointwise estimates for critical nonlinear equations, since it only requires a representation formula for a linear problem. The approach developed in this paper adapts for instance to higher-order critical nonlinear equations involving linear operators that look like
(\triangle_{g})^{p}
for some integer
p\geq 1
at first-order. It also seems particularly suited to deal with highly nonlinear equations.

2.
Preliminary material

2.1.
Finite-energy nodal solutions of the Yamabe equation in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Let
n\geq 3
and denote by
D^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
the completion of
C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with respect to the norm
u\mapsto\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\nabla u|^{2}dx
, endowed with the associated scalar product. Let
V\in D^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be a solution of the nodal Yamabe equation in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
:

\triangle_{\xi}V=|V|^{2^{*}-2}V,

(2.1)

where
\xi
is the Euclidean metric and
\triangle_{\xi}=-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\partial_{i}^{2}
. Unless specified otherwise
V
is allowed to change sign. If
V
is positive it is equal by Obata
[
39
]
, up to translations and rescalings, to the so-called positive standard bubble

B_{0}(x)=\left(1+\frac{|x|^{2}}{n(n-2)}\right)^{-\frac{n-2}{2}},

(2.2)

and is in particular an extremal for the euclidean sharp Sobolev inequality. By Caffarelli-Gidas-Spruck
[
9
]
this result remains true for all positive solutions of (
2.1
), without the assumption that they belong to
D^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. The positive standard bubbles satisfy

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\nabla B_{0}|^{2}dx=K_{n}^{-n},

where
K_{n}
, an explicit dimensional constant, is the sharp constant for the embedding of
D^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
into
L^{2^{*}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. By contrast, any sign-changing solution
V
of (
2.1
) satisfies

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\nabla V|^{2}dx>2K_{n}^{-n}

as is easily seen by splitting
V
into its positive and negative part. A result of Weth
[
60
]
shows the existence of a dimensional constant
\varepsilon_{n}>0
so that every sign-changing solution of (
2.1
) satisfies
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\nabla V|^{2}dx\geq 2K_{n}^{-n}+\varepsilon_{n}
.

We describe in this subsection the behavior at infinity for solutions of (
2.1
) and of the linearized equation. We first recall the following simple result:

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
V\in D^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be a solution of (
2.1
).

•

There exists a constant
C=C(n,V)
such that, for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

|V(x)|+(1+|x|)|\nabla V(x)|\leq C(1+|x|)^{2-n}.

(2.3)

•

There exists
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
such that

V(x)=\frac{\lambda}{|x|^{n-2}}+O(|x|^{1-n})\quad\textrm{ as }|x|\to+\infty,

(2.4)

and it satisfies

(n-2)\omega_{n-1}\lambda=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|V|^{2^{*}-2}Vdx,

(2.5)

where
\omega_{n-1}
is the area of the standard sphere
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
.

If
V
has constant sign
\lambda=\pm(n(n-2))^{\frac{n-2}{2}}
by (
2.2
).

Proof.

First, by the regularity theory for critical elliptic equations (see e.g. Trudinger
[
56
]
or also Hebey
[
27
]
, Theorem
2.15
),
V
is smooth in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and (
2.3
) is true for
x\in\overline{B(0,1)}
. For
x\neq 0
let
V^{*}(x)=|x|^{2-n}V\big(\frac{x}{|x|^{2}}\big)
be the Kelvin transform of
V
. It is a well-known fact that
V^{*}
still satisfies (
2.1
) on
\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash\{0\}
. The Kelvin transform leaves the
L^{2^{*}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
norm invariant and hence
V^{*}\in L^{2^{*}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Standard removal of singularities results (see e.g. the arguments in Caffarelli-Gidas-Spruck
[
9
]
, Lemma
2.1
) then show that
V^{*}
is a distributional solution of (
2.1
) in the whole
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and hence belongs to
D^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Regularity theory shows again that
V^{*}
smoothly extends at the origin. Hence there exists a constant
C=C(n,V)>0
such that

\|V^{*}\|_{L^{\infty}(B(0,1))}+\|\nabla V^{*}\|_{L^{\infty}(B(0,1))}\leq C,

which, by definition of
V^{*}
, proves (
2.3
) for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash\overline{B(0,1)}
.

At the origin
V^{*}
moreover expands as
V^{*}(z)=V^{*}(0)+O(|z|)
. Letting
z=\frac{x}{|x|^{2}}
yields (
2.4
) with
\lambda=V^{*}(0)
when
|x|\to+\infty
. To prove (
2.5
) we write a representation formula for
V
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Let
R>0
be fixed and choose
|x|\geq 2R
. With (
2.3
) we can write that, for any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

\displaystyle V(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{1}{(n-2)\omega_{n-1}}|x-y|^{2-n}|V(y)|^{2^{*}-2}V(y)dy

\displaystyle=\int_{B(0,R)}\frac{1}{(n-2)\omega_{n-1}}|x-y|^{2-n}|V(y)|^{2^{*}-2}V(y)dy+O(\frac{1}{R^{2}}(1+|x|)^{2-n}).

Multiplying by
|x|^{n-2}
both sides of the equality and letting first
|x|\to+\infty
and then
R\to+\infty
proves (
2.5
).
∎

If
V\in D^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
solves (
2.1
) we let

K_{V}=\Big\{h\in D^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),\quad\triangle_{\xi}h=(2^{*}-1)|V|^{2^{*}-2}h\Big\}

(2.6)

be the set of solutions of the linearised equation at
V
. A simple integration by parts yields
V\in K_{V}^{\perp}
. The following result proves decay at infinity for the elements of
K_{V}
:

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
Z\in K_{V}
. There exists
C=C(n,V,Z)
such that

|Z(x)|+(1+|x|)|\nabla Z(x)|\leq C(1+|x|)^{2-n}

(2.7)

for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Proof.

Let, for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
U(x)^{\frac{4}{n-2}}=4(1+|x|^{2})^{-2}
. Let
\pi:\mathbb{S}^{n}\backslash\{N\}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
be the stereographic projection from the North pole and let, for
\varphi\in D^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and for
y\in\mathbb{S}^{n}
,
\tilde{\varphi}(y)=\frac{\varphi}{U}(\pi(y))
. The conformal invariance of the conformal laplacian shows that, for any
y\in\mathbb{S}^{n}
,
\tilde{\varphi}
satisfies

\Big(\triangle_{g_{0}}+\frac{n(n-2)}{4}\Big)\tilde{\varphi}(y)=U(\pi(y))^{1-2^{*}}\triangle_{\xi}\varphi(\pi(y))

(2.8)

where
g_{0}
is the round metric on
\mathbb{S}^{n}
. Recall that
g_{0}
satisfies
g_{0}=\pi^{*}\big(U^{\frac{4}{n-2}}\xi)
. Let
Z\in K_{V}
and consider
\tilde{Z}
. The assumption
Z\in D^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
translates, by (
2.8
), into

\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}|\nabla\tilde{Z}|_{g_{0}}^{2}+\frac{n(n-2)}{4}|\tilde{Z}|^{2}dv_{g_{0}}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\nabla Z|^{2}dx<+\infty,

so that
\tilde{Z}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{n})
. And by (
2.6
)
\tilde{Z}
satisfies

\triangle_{g_{0}}\tilde{Z}+b\tilde{Z}=0\quad\textrm{ in }\mathbb{S}^{n},

(2.9)

where we have let

If

r_{i,\alpha}=\left\{\begin{aligned} &\frac{i_{g}}{2}&\textrm{ if }u_{0}\equiv 0\textrm{ and }\ker(\triangle_{g}+h)=\{0\}\\
&\sqrt{\mu_{i,\alpha}}&\textrm{ otherwise }\\
\end{aligned}\right..

The radius

is defined so that

up to a subsequence. Assume that there exist

Let

\displaystyle\textrm{ for all }i\neq j\textrm{ such that }\mu_{i,\alpha}\asymp\mu_{j,\alpha},\quad\frac{2r_{i,\alpha}}{R_{0}}\leq\frac{1}{4}d_{g}(x_{i,\alpha},x_{j,\alpha}).

In particular

for all

.
The following lemma follows from the definitions of

and

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
i\in\{1,\dots,k\}
and
j\in\mathcal{A}_{i}
. Then

B_{j,\alpha}(x)\lesssim\frac{\mu_{i,\alpha}^{\frac{n-2}{2}}}{s_{i,j,\alpha}^{n-2}}\quad\textrm{ and }\quad|\nabla B_{j,\alpha}(x)|\lesssim\frac{\mu_{i,\alpha}^{\frac{n-2}{2}}}{s_{i,j,\alpha}^{n-1}}\quad\textrm{ for all }x\in B_{g}\Big(x_{i,\alpha},\frac{2r_{i,\alpha}}{R_{0}}\Big),

where
B_{j,\alpha}
is as in (
3.4
). In particular,

|V_{j,\alpha}|\lesssim B_{i,\alpha}\quad\textrm{ and }\quad|\nabla V_{j,\alpha}|\lesssim\theta_{i,\alpha}^{-1}B_{i,\alpha}

in
B_{g}\Big(x_{i,\alpha},\frac{2r_{i,\alpha}}{R_{0}}\Big)
, where
V_{j,\alpha}
is as in (
2.14
) and
\theta_{i,\alpha}
as in (
3.2
).

We still define here the radii of influence

by comparing the heights of the positive bubbles

, just as in the positive case of Druet
[
18
]
(see also Druet-Hebey
[

]
). This is not surprising since, although

might vanish, its value at a generic point is of the order of

by (

) and (

). We will refer to the ball

as

.

Any bubble in the bubble-tree also interacts pointwise with bubbles concentrating strictly faster – the so-called

only the higher bubbles interacting with

in its area of influence will have to be taken into account. Let

. We define:

\mathcal{B}_{i}=\Big\{j\in\{1,\dots,k\}\backslash\{i\}\textrm{ s. t. }\mu_{j,\alpha}=o(\mu_{i,\alpha})\textrm{ and }d_{g}(x_{i,\alpha},x_{j,\alpha})\leq\frac{2r_{i,\alpha}}{R_{0}}\Big\}.

(3.18)

We then have the following lemma:

Lemma 3.4
.

For any
x\in B_{g}(x_{i,\alpha},\frac{3r_{i,\alpha}}{2R_{0}})
we have

\displaystyle|\mathcal{W}_{\alpha}(x)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{j=0}^{k}B_{j,\alpha}(x)\lesssim B_{i,\alpha}(x)+\sum_{j\in\mathcal{B}_{i}}B_{j,\alpha}(x)\quad\textrm{ and }

(3.19)

\displaystyle|\nabla\mathcal{W}_{\alpha}(x)|

\displaystyle\lesssim B_{0,\alpha}(x)+\sum_{j=1}^{k}\theta_{j,\alpha}(x)^{-1}B_{j,\alpha}(x)

\displaystyle\lesssim\theta_{i,\alpha}(x)^{-1}B_{i,\alpha}(x)+\sum_{j\in\mathcal{B}_{i}}\theta_{j,\alpha}(x)^{-1}B_{j,\alpha}(x).

Proof.

By (
3.5
) and (

) we have, for any

By (

in

we always have

for

\displaystyle=f(\mathcal{W}_{\alpha}+\varphi)-f(\mathcal{W}_{\alpha})-f^{\prime}(\mathcal{W}_{\alpha})\varphi.

Step

\displaystyle\big|f(\mathcal{W}_{\alpha}+\varphi_{1})

\displaystyle-f(\mathcal{W}_{\alpha}+\varphi_{2})-f^{\prime}(\mathcal{W}_{\alpha})(\varphi_{1}-\varphi_{2})\big|

\displaystyle\big|f(\mathcal{W}_{\alpha}+\varphi_{1})

\displaystyle+O\Big((\varepsilon_{\alpha}+\sigma_{\alpha})\sum_{i=0}^{k}B_{i,\alpha}(x_{\alpha})\Big).

Let

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{i,\alpha}\cap B_{g}(x_{i,\alpha},R\mu_{i,\alpha})}G_{h}(x_{\alpha},\cdot)|u_{\alpha}|^{2^{*}-2}u_{\alpha}dv_{g}

\displaystyle=(1+o(1))G_{h}(x_{\alpha},x_{i,\alpha})\mu_{i,\alpha}^{\frac{n-2}{2}}\int_{B(0,R)}|V_{i}|^{2^{*}-2}V_{i}dx

\displaystyle=(1+o(1))G_{h}(x_{\alpha},x_{i,\alpha})(n-2)\omega_{n-1}\mu_{i,\alpha}^{\frac{n-2}{2}}\big(\lambda_{i}+O(R^{-2})\big)

\displaystyle=V_{i,\alpha}(x_{\alpha})+o(B_{i,\alpha}(x_{\alpha}))+O(R^{-2}B_{i,\alpha}(x_{\alpha}))

where the third line follows from (

Up to picking

Remark 4.5
.

The proof of Theorem
4.3
requires to prove the intermediate estimate (
4.11
), which is not as precise as (
4.10
) at finite distances from the points
(x_{i,\alpha})_{1\leq i\leq k}
. The additional terms
\Theta_{i,\alpha}B_{i,\alpha}
that have to be taken into account in Proposition
4.2
come from the error
O(B_{\mu_{\alpha},x_{\alpha}})
in the right-hand side of (
2.13
). If
V
is a sign-changing solution of (
2.1
), indeed, one only has in general

|(\triangle_{g}+h)V^{\mu_{\alpha},x_{\alpha}}-|V^{\mu_{\alpha},x_{\alpha}}|^{2^{*}-2}V^{\mu_{\alpha},x_{\alpha}}|\lesssim B_{\mu_{\alpha},x_{\alpha}}\quad\textrm{ in }M

where

is given by (

) and this error estimate does not seem to improve. This contrasts with what happens for positive solutions, where radiality ensures that

\triangle_{g}B_{\mu_{\alpha},x_{\alpha}}=B_{\mu_{\alpha},x_{\alpha}}^{2^{*}-1}

at any order in conformal normal coordinates.

Remark 4.6
.

Theorem
1.1
requires no assumption on
h
. If
\ker(\triangle_{g}+h)\neq\{0\}
and
u_{0}\equiv 0
one expects

to possess a non-zero projection on

. Under these assumptions we have

is estimated by

in this case.

Remark 4.7
.

When all the bubbles

appearing in the Struwe decomposition of

are

solutions of (

) it is likely that the estimates of Theorem

can be improved. Sharper estimates in dimensions

for solutions of (

(sections

). If one of the

Lemma A.2

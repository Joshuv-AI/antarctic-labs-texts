Mellin definition of the fractional Laplacian Journal: Fract. Calc. Appl. Anal.
Gianni Pagnini 1,2, Claudio Runfola 3

Abstract
It is known that at least ten equivalent definitions of the fractional Laplacian exist in an unbounded domain. Here we derive a further equivalent definition that is based on the Mellin transform and it can be used when the fractional Laplacian is applied to radial functions. The main finding is tested in the case of the space-fractional diffusion equation. The one-dimensional case is also considered, such that the Mellin transform of the Riesz (namely the symmetric Riesz–Feller) fractional derivative is established. This one-dimensional result corrects an existing formula in literature. Further results for the Riesz fractional derivative are obtained when it is applied to symmetric functions, in particular its relation with the Caputo and the Riemann–Liouville fractional derivatives.

Introduction

The fractional Laplacian
bucur_valdinoci-2016
;
lischke_etal-jcp-2020
,
or fractional Laplace operator, is a relevant example of
non-local pseudo-differential operator
within the theory of fractional calculus.
In an unbounded domain,
there exist at least ten equivalent definitions
of the fractional Laplacian

,
while this equivalence does not hold in bounded domains,
see, e.g.,

servadei_etal-prse-2014
;
cusimano_etal-siamjna-2018
;
duo_etal-dcdsb-2019
.
Here we show that a further equivalent definition in
unbounded domains can be
provided on the basis of the Mellin transform
when the fractional Laplacian is applied to radial functions.
Actually, the Mellin transform has a remarkable role
in fractional calculus, see, e.g.,

gorenflo_etal-fcaa-2000
;
mainardi_etal-fcaa-2001
;
mainardi_etal-fcaa-2003
;
luchko_etal-fcaa-2013
;
bardaro_etal-jfaa-2015
,
that is additionally supported by the present results.

More in detail, this equivalence holds when the fractional Laplacian
is an operator on Lebesgue spaces
\pazocal{L}^{p}
(with
p\in[1,\infty)
),
on the space of continuous functions vanishing at infinity and
on the space of bounded uniformly continuous functions

kwasnicki-fcaa-2017
.
For the present aims, and to lighten the text,
we assume sufficiently well-behaving functions
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
n\in\{1,2,\dots\}
such that
the considered formulae have sense.
In particular,
we consider radial functions in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
that are
radial Schwartz–Bruhat functions,

We denote the fractional Laplacian in
\mathbb{R}^{n}

The classical Laplacian

The reader interested in the technicalities concerning the

Before to start, we want to remind also that
the fractional Laplacian is a peculiar nonlocal operator
beside its role in fractional calculus.
In particular, it has been proved that
all functions are locally
s
-harmonic up to a small error

\alpha/2=s

Actually,

We begin by reminding the heat semi-group definition

;

;

\mathscr{F}\rho(\kappa)=e^{-|\kappa|^{2}t}\mathscr{F}u(\kappa)

and by remembering that

\displaystyle{\int_{0}^{\infty}(e^{-y}-1)\,y^{s-1}\,dy=\Gamma(s)}

-1<\Re(s)<0

whittaker_watson-1952

from the analytical continuation of the Gamma function

(

(

The fractional Laplacian is also related

;

Actually, integral (

In this paper we prove a further

When

, the left inversion of the Riesz potential (

Thus, in the one-dimensional case,

The Riesz fractional derivative can be written by highlighting its relation

-|\kappa|^{\alpha}=-\left(|\kappa|^{2}\right)^{\alpha/2}

and then, in analogy, the following notation can be formally adopted

\displaystyle{\mathscr{F}^{-1}(\mathscr{F}\mathscr{D}_{x}^{\alpha})=\mathscr{D}_{x}^{\alpha}:=-\left(-d^{2}/dx^{2}\right)^{\alpha/2}}

when
x\in\mathbb{R}

feller-1952
.
Moreover, we report here that W. Feller generalised the Riesz potential
(
1.4
) when
n=1
to any complex
\alpha
with

\Re(\alpha)>0

and included a further parameter
\theta
related with
operator asymmetry

.
In particular, by following the approach due to M. Riesz,
W. Feller introduced a family of one-dimensional pseudo-differential

;

.
Furthermore,

W. Feller provided such generalized operators,

Finally, the symbol of the Riesz–Feller fractional derivative
is
-|\kappa|^{\alpha}e^{i(\sgn\kappa)\theta\pi/2}

\alpha\in(0,2)
and

Thus, the symmetric Riesz–Feller fractional derivative

that is when the Riesz–Feller derivative is a symmetric operator,

Actually, the Riesz fractional derivative,

The Riesz fractional derivative

\mathscr{D}_{x}^{\alpha}f(x)=\frac{\Gamma(1+\alpha)}{\pi}\sin\frac{\pi\alpha}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{f(x+z)-2f(x)+f(x-z)}{z^{1+\alpha}}\,dz\,.

(1.5)

By exploiting the one-dimensional case of
the Mellin definition of the fractional Laplacian here derived,
we also establish the relations of

4
Application to space-fractional diffusion equation

The space-fractional diffusion equation is a diffusion equation
where the Laplacian is replaced by the fractional Laplacian,
see, e.g.,
hanyga-prsla-2001
.
The same holds in the one-dimensional case,
where the second derivative in
space is replaced by the Riesz (or Riesz–Feller) space-fractional
derivative with fractional order
\alpha\in(0,2)

gorenflo_etal-fcaa-2000
;
mainardi_etal-fcaa-2001
.

It is well-known that the solution of the Cauchy problem
for standard diffusion with initial datum
\delta(x)

is the Gaussian, or normal, distribution. In this case,
if
t>0
is the time variable, the solution is
self-similar with scaling law
t^{-1/2}
,
which is also the law ruling the linear growth in time of the
standard deviation of particle displacements.
In the case of space-fractional diffusion,
the solution in the same setting is a Lévy stable distribution
with scaling law
t^{-1/\alpha}
with
\alpha\in(0,2)

and infinite standard deviation.
As a matter of fact, the relation between space-fractional diffusion
and Lévy stable densities was indeed established by
W. Feller
feller-1952
as reported in Section
1
.

The diffusive processes underlying the space-fractional diffusion
are known as Lévy flights and they are Markovian processes
whose jump-size distribution has power-law decaying tails

chechkin_etal-anotrans-2008

and then the walkers’ distribution converges to a Lévy stable density,
see, e.g.,

shlesinger_etal-jsp-1982
;
gorenflo_etal-fcaa-1998
;
valdinoci-bsema-2009
.
Thus, the space-fractional diffusion equation is the governing equation
of Lévy flights.
These procesess have an important place in physics literature
for modelling anomalous diffusion in disordered media

shlesinger_etal-jsp-1982
;
ott_etal-prl-1990
;
bouchaud_etal-pr-1990
;
metzler_etal-pr-2000
;
metzler_etal-jpa-2004
;
zaburdaev_etal-rmp-2015
. However,
some issues rise when the experimental implications of
nonlocal models are considered
hilfer-fcaa-2015
.

In spite of the fact that the exact determination of
the jump-size distribution is not necessary for determining the convergence

shlesinger_etal-jsp-1982
;
klafter_sokolov-2011
;
meerschaert2012
,
the actual timescale for convergence can be indeed
dependent on the specific form of the jump-size distribution,
as it is the case with some bi-modal distributions

pagnini_etal-fcaa-2021
.
This has some implication on models
in optimal searching or foraging
that neglect the exact form of the jump distribution,
in particular with respect to transiency and recurrency

pagnini_etal-fcaa-2021
.

By assuming a unitary diffusion coefficient with
physical dimension
[L]^{2}[T]^{-\alpha}
,
the space-fractional diffusion equation is

\frac{\partial P}{\partial t}=\mathscr{L}P=-(-\Delta)^{\alpha/2}P\,,\quad P(x;0)=\delta(x)\,,\quad\text{in}\quad\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(0,\infty)\,.

(4.1)

We apply to both sides of (
4.1
)
the Mellin transform (
3.2
).
From Theorem
3.1
, see (
3.3
),
we have that the LHS reads

\frac{\partial\mathscr{M}P}{\partial t}=\pi^{n/2-s}\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{s}{2}\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{n-s}{2}\right)}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\mathscr{M}(\mathscr{F}^{\prime}f)(n-s)\,,

(4.2)

and from Theorem
2.1
, see (
2.1
),
we have that the RHS reads

\mathscr{M}(\mathscr{L}P)(s)=-2^{\alpha}\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{s}{2}\right)\Gamma\left[\frac{n-(s-\alpha)}{2}\right]}{\Gamma\left(\frac{n-s}{2}\right)\Gamma\left[\frac{s-\alpha}{2}\right]}\mathscr{M}P(s-\alpha)\,,

(4.3)

and, by applying formula (
3.3
) of Theorem
3.1

to the term
\mathscr{M}P(s-\alpha)
, we finally have

\mathscr{M}(\mathscr{L}P)(s)=-2^{\alpha}\pi^{n/2-(s-\alpha)}\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{s}{2}\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{n-s}{2}\right)}\mathscr{M}(\mathscr{F}^{\prime}P)(n-(s-\alpha))\,.

(4.4)

By comparing (
4.2
) and (
4.4
),
it holds

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\mathscr{M}(\mathscr{F}^{\prime}P)(n-s)=-(2\pi)^{\alpha}\mathscr{M}(\mathscr{F}^{\prime}P)(n-(s-\alpha))\,,

(4.5)

that can be explicitly written as

\!\!\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\partial\mathscr{F}^{\prime}P}{\partial t}|\kappa|^{(n-s)-1}\,d|\kappa|=-(2\pi)^{\alpha}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathscr{F}^{\prime}P(|\kappa|)\,|\kappa|^{(n-(s-\alpha))-1}\,d|\kappa|\,,

(4.6)

where on the LHS the Mellin transform and the derivation in time
are exchanged. Finally, by equalling the integrands and solving with
respect to
\mathscr{F}^{\prime}P
, it results

\mathscr{F}^{\prime}P(|\kappa|)=e^{-(2\pi|\kappa|)^{\alpha}t}\,,

(4.7)

that means
P(x;t)=t^{-1/\alpha}\mathscr{P}(|x|t^{-1/\alpha})

is a radial Lévy stable distribution
with stable parameter
\alpha\in(0,2)
.

The same holds for the one-dimensional case,
when the Riesz space-fractional derivative (
1.5
),
i.e., the symmetric Riesz–Feller fractional operator,
is used in the RHS of (
4.1
)
and Corollary
1
through formula (
2.2
)
is applied.

5
Conclusions

In this paper we have derived a further definition of the
fractional Laplacian on the basis of the Mellin transform.
In an unbounded domain, this definition is equivalent to
some other existing definitions whose equivalence has been
already discussed
kwasnicki-fcaa-2017
.
In general, this Mellin definition can be used for solving space-fractional
differential equation for radial functions.
In particular, we have applied this result in the case of
the space-fractional diffusion equation,
that is a remarkable example where the fractional Laplacian is involved.

If the one-dimensional case is considered,
then the fractional Laplacian reduces to the Riesz
(i.e., the symmetric Riesz–Feller) fractional derivative and the
radial property turns into the symmetric property.
In this setting, our results correct a previous
determination of the Mellin definition of the Riesz pseudo-differential operator

khan_etal-rp-2021
.
Beside this, we have also established the ralation of the Riesz
fractional derivative with the Caputo and with the Riemann–Liouville
fractional derivatives.

In the view of the recent efforts done for providing
a rigourous theory of a generalised fractional calculus

hanyga-fcaa-2020
;
diethelm_etal-fcaa-2020
;
luchko-s-2021
;
luchko-m-2021
;
luchko-fcaa-2021
;
tarasov-m-2023

and the corresponding desired future advancements

hilfer_etal-m-2019
;
diethelm_etal-nd-2022
,
the contribution of the present research to that program is
twofold.

In the case of multi-dimensional operators
tarasov-m-2023
,
we underline that settling down the fractional Laplacian,
which is the paradigmatic multi-dimensional nonlocal operator,
in the framework of the Mellin integral transform theory
(Theorem
2.1
) allows for the use of the related Mellin machinery.
This is a strong tool for deriving new results

marichev-1983
;
yakubovich_luchko-1994
;
paris_kaminski-2001
.
Actually, the success of the Mellin setting in relation with fractional calculus,
together with the Mellin–Barnes integrals,
is an established fact both for theoretical results, see,
e.g.,
mainardi_etal-fcaa-2003
;
luchko_etal-fcaa-2013
;
bardaro_etal-jfaa-2015
,
and for resolution techniques of fractional equations,
see, e.g.,
gorenflo_etal-fcaa-2000
;
mainardi_etal-fcaa-2001
.

Moreover, when the one-dimensional case is considered,
the Mellin definition of the fractional Laplacian allows for
the Mellin definition of two-sided fractional derivatives
(Corollary
1
).
This result is new and it joins with well-known results for one-sided derivatives

podlubny-1999
;
luchko_etal-fcaa-2013
.
By comparing the Mellin definitions for two-sided and one-sided
fractional derivatives their relationship emerges
(Theorems
2.2
and
2.3
).
This relationship is a key result that can lead to extend to two-sided operators
the present theory foundation that holds only for one-sided operators
because it is based on the Sonine equations

hanyga-fcaa-2020
;
luchko-fcaa-2021
.

Appendix:
Derivation of the kernel function
\mathscr{E}(\xi)
(
3.20
)

By using the rule

\sin(\theta+\omega)=\sin\theta\cos\omega+\cos\theta\sin\omega
,
formula (
3.19
) can be re-written as

\mathscr{M}\mathscr{E}(s)=\sin\left(\frac{\pi\alpha}{2}\right)\,\cot\left(\frac{\pi s}{2}\right)-\cos\left(\frac{\pi\alpha}{2}\right)\,,

and then we want two functions
\varphi_{1}(r)
and
\varphi_{2}(r)

such that
\mathscr{M}\varphi_{1}=\cot(\pi s/2)
and
\mathscr{M}\varphi_{2}=1
.

By noting from
(
marichev-1983
, formula (2.24))

that

\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{x^{s-1}}{1-x}\,dx=\pi\cot\pi s\,,\quad\Re(s)\in(0,1)\,,

we have, after replacing
x\to r^{2}
, the first pair

\mathscr{M}\varphi_{1}(s)=\frac{\pi}{2}\cot\frac{\pi s}{2}\,,\quad\text{with}\quad\varphi_{1}(r)=\frac{1}{1-r^{2}}\,.

Now, let
\mathscr{M}^{-1}
be the Mellin inverse transformation
then for a given function
\psi(r)
it holds
\psi=\mathscr{M}^{-1}\mathscr{M}\psi
.
Therefore, after the exchange of integration order we have

\psi(r)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\psi(y)\left\{\mathscr{M}^{-1}y^{s}\right\}\frac{dy}{y}\,.

This integral can be understood as a Mellin convolution integral where
by definition
\mathscr{M}^{-1}y^{s}=q(r/y)
and,
by using the properties of the delta-function, it holds

q(z)=z\delta(z-1)
with
z=r/y
.
Function
q(r/y)
is consistent with
\mathscr{M}q=y^{s}
.
Thus, by setting
y=1
, we have also the second pair

\mathscr{M}\varphi_{2}(s)=1\,,\quad\text{with}\quad\varphi_{2}(r)=r\delta(r-1)\,.

Finally it results

\mathscr{M}\mathscr{E}(s)=\frac{2}{\pi}\sin\left(\frac{\pi\alpha}{2}\right)\mathscr{M}\varphi_{1}(s)-\cos\left(\frac{\pi\alpha}{2}\right)\mathscr{M}\varphi_{2}(s)\,,\quad\Re(s)\in(0,1)\,,

from which formula (
3.20
) follows.

However,
also setting
\mathscr{M}^{-1}y^{s}=q^{*}(r/y)=\delta(r/y-1)
is appropriate
for solving the integral though properties of delta-function and
it is consistent with the Mellin convolution interpretation and
with formula
\mathscr{M}q^{*}=y^{s}
.
Thus, in our case, by setting
y=1
another suitable choice
for function
\varphi_{2}(r)
is
\varphi_{2}(r)=\delta(r-1)
.
Moreover, by disregarding the Mellin convolution interpretation,
also setting
\mathscr{M}^{-1}y^{s}=q^{\prime}(r,y)=y\delta(r-y)
or

\mathscr{M}^{-1}y^{s}=q{{}^{\prime\prime}}(r,y)=r\delta(r-y)
is appropriate
and consistent with the formula
\mathscr{M}q^{\prime}=\mathscr{M}q{{}^{\prime\prime}}=y^{s}
.
Actually, this proceedure does not solve the ambiguity emerging from
different rearrangements of the delta-function
like
a\delta(r-a)
and
r\delta(r-a)
, with
a>0
,
that provide the same Mellin transform.
More extensive analysis on the Mellin transform and delta-function
are available in literature

sudland_etal-fcaa-2004
;
sudland_etal-itsf-2019
.

Relaxation and domain wall structure of bilayer moiré systems Keywords: 2D materials, moiré, superlattice, domain walls, elasticity MSC codes: 74B99, 74G65, 74K99
Paul Cazeaux, Drake Clark, Rebecca Engelke, Philip Kim, Mitchell Luskin

Abstract
Moiré patterns result from setting a 2D material such as graphene on another 2D material with a small twist angle or from the lattice mismatch of 2D heterostructures. We present a continuum model for the elastic energy of these bilayer moiré structures that includes an intralayer elastic energy and an interlayer misfit energy that is minimized at two stackings (disregistries). We show by theory and computation that the displacement field that minimizes the global elastic energy subject to a global boundary constraint gives large alternating regions of one of the two energy-minimizing stackings separated by domain walls. We derive a model for the domain wall structure from the continuum bilayer energy and give a rigorous asymptotic estimate for the structure. We also give an improved estimate for the L 2 L^{2} -norm of the gradient on the moiré unit cell for twisted bilayers that scales at most inversely linearly with the twist angle, a result which is consistent with the formation of one-dimensional domain walls with a fixed width around triangular domains at very small twist angles.

2.3
Generalized stacking fault energy

The generalized stacking fault energy (GSFE) accounts for local contributions to the global
stacking energy and is assumed to depend only on the local configuration as measured by the disregistries.
The GFSE measures the energy cost of the layers not being uniformly in an optimal stacking configuration, but rather in a slowly varying disregistry induced by a global rotation or deformation of one of both layers.
As shown in Figure
4
for bilayer graphene, it is thus minimized at the AB and BA configurations and maximized at the AA configuration displayed in Figure
3
. The GSFE is computed for aligned layers and is a good approximation for small rotations
[
7
]
.

The generalized stacking fault energy for layer 1 with respect to layer 2 as a function of disregistry is given by
\Phi_{1}:\Gamma_{2}\to\mathbb{R}
and the GFSE for layer 2 with respect to layer 1 is given by

\Phi_{2}:\Gamma_{1}\to\mathbb{R}

[
7
]
. The GFSEs for unrelaxed bilayer structures at
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
are thus given by

\Phi_{1}\left(\mathbf{b}_{1\to 2}(\mathbf{x})\right)
and
\Phi_{2}\left(\mathbf{b}_{2\to 1}(\mathbf{x})\right)
.

2.4
Bilayer model in real space

Our starting point will be the model developed in
[
7
,
6
,
9
]
, where the displacement minimizes an elastic energy that decomposes as:

\mathcal{E}[\mathbf{u}_{1},\mathbf{u}_{2}]=\sum_{j=1}^{2}\mathcal{E}^{j}_{\mathrm{intra}}(\mathbf{u}_{j})+\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{inter}}(\mathbf{u}_{1}-\mathbf{u}_{2}),

(5)

where we assume that the displacements
\mathbf{u}_{1}(\mathbf{x})
and
\mathbf{u}_{2}(\mathbf{x})
are periodic functions with respect to the moiré
lattice (
4
). This can be motivated by equivalently assuming that the displacements
\mathbf{u}_{1}(\mathbf{x})
and
\mathbf{u}_{2}(\mathbf{x})
are functions of the disregistries

\mathbf{b}_{1\to 2}(\mathbf{x})
and
\mathbf{b}_{2\to 1}(\mathbf{x}),
respectively
[
7
]
.

The contribution
\mathcal{E}^{j}_{\mathrm{intra}}
corresponds to the elastic cost of straining the layers (which we model here by isotropic linear elasticity):

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{1}_{\mathrm{intra}}[\mathbf{u}_{1}]

\displaystyle=\int_{\Gamma_{\mathcal{M}}}\frac{\lambda_{1}}{2}\,(\mathrm{div}\ \mathbf{u}_{1})^{2}+\mu_{1}\,\varepsilon(\mathbf{u}_{1}):\varepsilon(\mathbf{u}_{1})d\mathbf{x},

(6)

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{2}_{\mathrm{intra}}[\mathbf{u}_{2}]

\displaystyle=\int_{\Gamma_{\mathcal{M}}}\frac{\lambda_{2}}{2}\,(\mathrm{div}\ \mathbf{u}_{2})^{2}+\mu_{2}\,\varepsilon(\mathbf{u}_{2}):\varepsilon(\mathbf{u}_{2})d\mathbf{x},

where
\lambda_{i}
and
\mu_{i}
are Lamé parameters and
\varepsilon(\mathbf{u}_{i})
are the linear strains for layer
i,
while the contribution
\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{inter}}
corresponds to the misfit energy due to lattice misalignment between the two layers:

\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{inter}}[\mathbf{v}]=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Gamma_{\mathcal{M}}}\Phi_{1}\left(\mathbf{b}_{1\to 2}(\mathbf{x})+\mathbf{v}(\mathbf{x})\right)+\Phi_{2}\left(\mathbf{b}_{2\to 1}(\mathbf{x})-\mathbf{v}(\mathbf{x})\right)d\mathbf{x},

(7)

where

\mathbf{v}=\mathbf{u}_{1}-\mathbf{u}_{2}.

We note that the arguments of the generalized stacking fault energies
\Phi_{1}:\Gamma_{2}\to\mathbb{R}
and
\Phi_{2}:\Gamma_{1}\to\mathbb{R}

in
\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{inter}}[\mathbf{v}]
are the modulated disregistries for the relative distortion of the lattice

\widetilde{\mathbf{b}}_{1\to 2}(\mathbf{x}):=\mathbf{b}_{1\to 2}(\mathbf{x})+\mathbf{v}(\mathbf{x})\quad\text{and}\quad\widetilde{\mathbf{b}}_{2\to 1}(\mathbf{x}):=\mathbf{b}_{2\to 1}(\mathbf{x})-\mathbf{v}(\mathbf{x}).

(8)

We look for a
local
minimum of the energy (note that the global energy minimum would have both layers aligned in the optimal stacking configuration, but we are enforcing a fixed global misalignment through the periodic boundary conditions on the moiré torus).

2.5
Interlayer symmetry

A common thread will be that for two vertically stacked, possibly strained but initially identical layers and neglecting non-linear elastic effects, we can model the intralayer
elastic energies by the same values of Lamé parameters
\lambda
,
\mu
between the two layers.
A natural symmetry then emerges: notice that
\mathcal{E}[\mathbf{u}_{1},\mathbf{u}_{2}]=\mathcal{E}[-\mathbf{u}_{2},-\mathbf{u}_{1}]
because the misfit energy depends only on the difference
\mathbf{v}=\mathbf{u}_{1}-\mathbf{u}_{2}=(-\mathbf{u}_{2})-(-\mathbf{u}_{1})
. In fact, the displacements are uniquely determined by the nonlinear forcing term in the linear elastic regime
[
7
]
as the Euler-Lagrange equations associated with the minimum read:

\left\{\begin{aligned} -\mathbf{div}\left(\lambda\,(\mathrm{div}\ \mathbf{u}_{1})I+2\mu\,\varepsilon(\mathbf{u}_{1})\right)&=\mathbf{F}_{\mathcal{M}}(\mathbf{v}),\\
-\mathbf{div}\left(\lambda\,(\mathrm{div}\ \mathbf{u}_{2})I+2\mu\,\varepsilon(\mathbf{u}_{2})\right)&=-\mathbf{F}_{\mathcal{M}}(\mathbf{v}),\end{aligned}\right.

where

\mathbf{F}_{\mathcal{M}}[\mathbf{v}](\mathbf{x}):=\frac{1}{2}\nabla\Phi_{1}\left(\mathbf{b}_{1\to 2}(\mathbf{x})+\mathbf{v}(\mathbf{x})\right)-\frac{1}{2}\nabla\Phi_{2}\left(\mathbf{b}_{2\to 1}(\mathbf{x})-\mathbf{v}(\mathbf{x})\right)
.

Uniqueness of the solution of the linear elasticity problem then implies:

\mathbf{u}:=\mathbf{u}_{1}=-\mathbf{u}_{2},

(9)

hence knowledge of the single planar displacement
\mathbf{u}
is sufficient to describe the displacement of both layers.
Furthermore, the total energy of the bilayer system thus simplifies to

\mathcal{E}[\mathbf{u}]=2\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{intra}}[\mathbf{u}]+\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{inter}}[2\mathbf{u}],

(10)

where
\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{intra}}[\mathbf{u}]:=\int_{\Gamma_{\mathcal{M}}}\frac{\lambda}{2}\,(\mathrm{div}\ \mathbf{u})^{2}+\mu\,\varepsilon(\mathbf{u}):\varepsilon(\mathbf{u})d\mathbf{x}
.

One expects the formation of a domain wall at such an interface between stable domains, modeled by an interlayer displacement which we may assume depends only on the transverse coordinate

Since the disregistry is

4.1
Twist

The layers are respectively twisted by an angle
\theta
:

A_{1}=R_{-\theta/2}A,\qquad A_{2}=R_{+\theta/2}A.

This case has been extensively studied both experimentally
[
19
]
as well as theoretically
[
9
,
6
,
7
]
.
The moiré pattern in this case is a triangular lattice with fundamental matrix (
4
)

A_{\mathcal{M}}=\frac{1}{2\sin(\theta/2)}J\,A\qquad\text{where}\quad J=R_{-\pi/2}=\begin{bmatrix}0&1\\
-1&0\end{bmatrix},

i.e., it is rotated by
-\pi/2
from the reference lattice, and scaled inversely proportionally to the twist angle as seen on Figure
8(a)
.
We can similarly calculate that

\displaystyle{\mathbf{b}}_{1\to 2}:\begin{cases}\Gamma_{\mathcal{M}}\to\Gamma_{2},\\
\mathbf{x}\mapsto 2\sin(\theta/2)JR_{\theta/2}\mathbf{x},\end{cases}\qquad{\mathbf{b}}_{2\to 1}:\begin{cases}\Gamma_{\mathcal{M}}\to\Gamma_{1},\\
\mathbf{x}\mapsto-2\sin(\theta/2)JR_{-\theta/2}\mathbf{x}.\end{cases}

By symmetry of the unrelaxed twisted bilayer structure, there exists a single GSFE functional

\Phi_{0}(\mathbf{x}):=\Phi_{1}({\mathbf{b}}_{1\to 2}(\mathbf{x}))=\Phi_{2}({\mathbf{b}}_{2\to 1}(\mathbf{x}))

where

\Phi_{0}:\Gamma_{\mathcal{M}}\to\mathbb{R}.

We can then derive the local interlayer energies with relative displacements
\mathbf{v}(\mathbf{x})
to be

\begin{split}\Phi_{1}({\mathbf{b}}_{1\to 2}(\mathbf{x})+\mathbf{v}(\mathbf{x}))&+\Phi_{2}({\mathbf{b}}_{2\to 1}(\mathbf{x})-\mathbf{v}(\mathbf{x}))\\
&=\Phi_{0}(\mathbf{x}-(2\sin(\theta/2))^{-1}JR_{-\theta/2}\mathbf{v})+\Phi_{0}(\mathbf{x}-(2\sin(\theta/2))^{-1}JR_{\theta/2}\mathbf{v})\end{split}

and the total interlayer energy to be

\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{inter}}[\mathbf{v}]=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Gamma_{\mathcal{M}}}\Phi_{0}(\mathbf{x}-(2\sin(\theta/2))^{-1}JR_{-\theta/2}\mathbf{v})+\Phi_{0}(\mathbf{x}-(2\sin(\theta/2))^{-1}JR_{\theta/2}\mathbf{v})\,d\mathbf{x}.

(14)

Following
[
7
]
, we can perform an asymptotic study by introducing a new angle-independent reference cell
\Gamma_{0}
such that

\Gamma_{\mathcal{M}}(\theta)=\frac{1}{2\sin(\theta/2)}\Gamma_{0},\quad\text{where}\ \begin{cases}\mathcal{R}_{0}:=A_{0}\mathbb{Z}^{2},\\
\Gamma_{0}:=\mathbb{R}^{2}/\mathcal{R}_{0},\end{cases}\quad\text{with }A_{0}:=JA.

(15)

We can then obtain by the scaling
\widehat{\mathbf{x}}=2\sin(\theta/2)\mathbf{x}
and
\Psi_{0}(\widehat{\mathbf{x}})=\Phi_{0}((2\sin(\theta/2))^{-1}\widehat{\mathbf{x}})=\Phi_{0}(\mathbf{x})

the relaxation problem (
10
) recast as a problem on the fixed reference cell
\Gamma_{0}
:

\text{Minimize}\quad\mathcal{E}_{0}^{\theta}[\mathbf{u}]:=2\mathcal{E}^{0}_{\mathrm{intra}}[\mathbf{u}]+\mathcal{E}^{\theta}_{\mathrm{inter}}[2\mathbf{u}],

(16)

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{0}_{\mathrm{intra}}[\mathbf{u}]

\displaystyle:=\int_{\Gamma_{0}}\frac{\lambda}{2}(\mathrm{div}\ \mathbf{u})^{2}+\mu\varepsilon(\mathbf{u}):\varepsilon(\mathbf{u}),

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{\theta}_{\textrm{inter}}[\mathbf{v}]

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{4\sin^{2}(\theta/2)}\int_{\Gamma_{0}}\frac{1}{2}\left(\Psi_{0}(\widehat{\mathbf{x}}-JR_{\theta/2}\mathbf{v})+\Psi_{0}(\widehat{\mathbf{x}}-JR_{-\theta/2}\mathbf{v})\right)d\widehat{\mathbf{x}}.

This explicit rescaling allows us to state the following result, improving on previous results in
[
7
]
by a simple variational argument:

Theorem 4.1
.

Assume that the functional
\Psi_{0}
is twice continuously differentiable. Then for any angle
\theta
which is not an integer multiple of
\pi
, there exists periodic minimizers
\mathbf{u}^{*}
to the relaxation problem (
16
) in the Sobolev space
W^{1,2}_{0}(\Gamma_{0}):=\{\mathbf{u}\in W^{1,2}_{0}(\Gamma_{0})\ |\ \int_{\Gamma_{0}}\mathbf{u}(\widehat{\mathbf{x}})d\widehat{\mathbf{x}}={\boldsymbol{0}}\}
, which satisfy

\|\mathbf{u}^{*}\|_{1,2}\leq\frac{1}{2\sin(\theta/2)}\left(\frac{1}{\mu C_{A}^{1}}\int_{\Gamma_{0}}[\Psi_{0}(\mathbf{x})-\Psi_{\mathrm{min}}]d\widehat{\mathbf{x}}\right)^{1/2},

where
C^{1}_{A}
is a fully identifiable constant depending on the lattice basis
A
only.

This new estimate shows in particular that the
L^{2}
-norm of the gradient on the moiré unit cell scales at most
inversely linearly
with the twist angle, i.e., linearly with the width of the moiré domains, a result consistent with the formation of one-dimensional domain walls with a fixed width around triangular domains at very small twist angles, as observed in simulations (see Figure
9
) and experiments
[
19
]
.

Proof.

As noted in Remark 4.5 and Theorem 4.10 in
[
7
]
, there exists global minimizers
\mathbf{u}^{*}
in
W^{1,2}_{0}(\Gamma_{0})
to problem (
16
) which solve the associated Euler-Lagrange equation and satisfy the following bound:

\|\mathbf{u}^{*}\|_{1,2}\leq\frac{1}{\mu C_{A}^{1}}\frac{\|\nabla\Psi_{0}\|_{\infty}}{4\sin^{2}(\theta/2)},

where
C_{A}^{1}
is a constant such that the following Korn-type inequality is satisfied for all functions
u\in W^{1,2}_{0}(\Gamma_{0})
:

\int_{\Gamma_{0}}\lambda(\divergence\mathbf{u})^{2}+2\mu\varepsilon(\mathbf{u}):\varepsilon(\mathbf{u})d\widehat{\mathbf{x}}\geq\mu C_{A}^{1}\|\mathbf{u}\|_{1,2}^{2}.

On the other hand, we note that the energy bound

\mathcal{E}_{0}^{\theta}[\mathbf{u}^{*}]\leq\mathcal{E}_{0}^{\theta}[\boldsymbol{0}]=\frac{1}{4\sin^{2}\theta/2}\int_{\Gamma_{0}}\Psi_{0}(\mathbf{x})d\widehat{\mathbf{x}}

directly implies

\begin{split}\mu C_{A}^{1}\|\mathbf{u}^{*}\|_{1,2}^{2}&\leq\int_{\Gamma_{0}}\lambda(\divergence\mathbf{u}^{*})^{2}+2\mu\varepsilon(\mathbf{u}^{*}):\varepsilon(\mathbf{u}^{*})d\widehat{\mathbf{x}}=\mathcal{E}_{0}^{\theta}[\mathbf{u}^{*}]-\mathcal{E}^{\theta}_{\textrm{inter}}[2\mathbf{u}^{*}]\\
&\leq\frac{1}{4\sin^{2}\theta/2}\int_{\Gamma_{0}}[\Psi_{0}(\widehat{\mathbf{x}})-\Psi_{\mathrm{min}}]d\widehat{\mathbf{x}}.\end{split}

∎

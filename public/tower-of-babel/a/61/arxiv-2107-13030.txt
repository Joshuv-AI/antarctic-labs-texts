One level summations for powers of Fibonacci and Lucas polynomials
Helmut Prodinger

Abstract
. Powers of Fibonacci polynomials are expressed as single sums, improving on a double sum recently seen in the literature.



2010 Mathematics Subject Classification
11B39

1.
Introduction

The generating function of Fibonacci numbers is

\sum_{n\geq 0}F_{n}z^{n}=\frac{z}{1-z-z^{2}}=z\sum_{k\geq 0}(z+z^{2})^{k}=\sum_{k\geq 0}z^{k+1}(1+z)^{k}.

Comparing coefficients on both sides,

F_{n+1}=\sum_{k\geq 0}[z^{n-k}](1+z)^{k}=\sum_{0\leq k\leq n}\binom{k}{n-k}=\sum_{0\leq i\leq n/2}\binom{n-i}{i}.

This is of course a very classical formula
[
3
]
.

Recently, the following formula was found
[
2
]
:

F_{n+1}^{2}=\sum_{0\leq j\leq i\leq 2n/3}\binom{i}{j}\binom{2n-2i-j}{i},

which contains already
two
levels of summation.

In
[
4
]
, the author derived, among many other things, the formula

\displaystyle F_{n}^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{5}F_{2(n+1)}-\frac{3}{5}F_{2n}-\frac{2}{5}(-1)^{n}=\frac{L_{2n}}{5}+\frac{2(-1)^{n-1}}{5},

with Lucas numbers, satisfying

L_{n}=\sum_{0\leq k\leq n/2}\frac{n}{n-k}\binom{n-k}{k},\quad n\geq 1.

Consequently, we find for
n\geq 1

\displaystyle F_{n}^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{5}\sum_{0\leq k\leq n}\frac{2n}{2n-k}\binom{2n-k}{k}+\frac{2(-1)^{n-1}}{5},

this result has only
one
level of summations. For fun, we go one step further. Since
[
4
]

F_{n}^{3}=\frac{1}{5}F_{3n}-\frac{3}{5}(-1)^{n}F_{n},

we find

F_{n}^{3}=\frac{1}{5}\sum_{0\leq i\leq(3n-1)/2}\binom{3n-1-i}{i}-\frac{3}{5}(-1)^{n}\sum_{0\leq i\leq(n-1)/2}\binom{n-1-i}{i},

again only one level of summation.

The rest of the paper is devoted to Fibonacci polynomials, defined by

F_{n+2}=xF_{n+1}+F_{n},\quad F_{0}=0,\ F_{1}=1,

and
arbitrary
powers
F_{n}^{d}
, for positive integers
d
, not just squares.
There are also Lucas polynomials

L_{n+2}=xL_{n+1}+L_{n},\quad L_{0}=2,\ L_{1}=x,

and if one understands these two, one understand arbitrary initial conditions, as well a the recursion
f_{n+2}=xf_{n+1}+yf_{n}
, since one parameter can be
eliminated by a simple substitution.

From now on
F
and
L
mean Fibonacci polynomials (or generalized Fibonacci numbers) and, respectively, Lucas polynomials (or generalized Lucas numbers).

2.
Linearizing powers of Fibonacci polynomials and Lucas polynomials

First, solving
\lambda^{2}=x\lambda+1
, with the two roots

\alpha,\beta=\frac{x\pm\sqrt{x^{2}+4}}{2},

we have the
Binet forms

F_{n}=\frac{\alpha^{n}-\beta^{n}}{\alpha-\beta}\quad\text{and}\quad L_{n}=\alpha^{n}+\beta^{n}.

Note that
\alpha-\beta=\sqrt{x^{2}+1}
and
\alpha\beta=-1
.
The expansions for
n\geq 1
are
[
3
]

\displaystyle F_{n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{0\leq k\leq(n-1)/2}\binom{n-1-k}{k}x^{n-1-2k},

\displaystyle L_{n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{0\leq k\leq n/2}\frac{n}{n-k}\binom{n-k}{k}x^{n-2k}.

It is to be noted that these expansion only contain one level of summation.

Doing a few experiments, we found two sets of formulae.

Theorem 1
.

For
n\geq 0
and
d\geq 0
,

F_{n}^{2d+1}=\frac{1}{(x^{2}+4)^{d}}\sum_{0\leq s\leq d}(-1)^{s(n-1)}\binom{2d+1}{s}F_{(2d+1-2s)n}.

For
n\geq 0
and
d\geq 1
,

F_{n}^{2d}=\frac{1}{(x^{2}+4)^{d}}\sum_{0\leq s\leq d-1}(-1)^{s(n-1)}\binom{2d}{s}L_{(2d-2s)n}+\frac{1}{(x^{2}+4)^{d}}\binom{2d}{d}(-1)^{d(n-1)}.

We will provide two sample computations that illustrate the general situation:

\displaystyle F_{n}^{7}

\displaystyle=\frac{(\alpha^{n}-\beta^{n})^{7}}{(\alpha-\beta)^{7}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha^{7n}-7\alpha^{6n}\beta^{n}+21\alpha^{5n}\beta^{2n}-35\alpha^{4n}\beta^{3n}+35\alpha^{3n}\beta^{4n}-21\alpha^{2n}\beta^{5n}+7\alpha^{n}\beta^{6n}-\beta^{7n}}{(\alpha-\beta)(x^{2}+4)^{3}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha^{7n}-\beta^{7n}-7(-1)^{n}\alpha^{5n}+7(-1)^{n}\beta^{5n}+21\alpha^{3n}-21\beta^{3n}-35(-1)^{n}\alpha+35(-1)^{n}\beta}{(\alpha-\beta)(x^{2}+4)^{3}}

\displaystyle=\frac{F_{7n}-7(-1)^{n}F_{5n}+21F_{3n}-35(-1)^{n}F_{n}}{(x^{2}+4)^{3}}.

The situation for even exponents is similar, but there is a middle term with no matched term:

\displaystyle F_{n}^{6}

\displaystyle=\frac{(\alpha^{n}-\beta^{n})^{6}}{(\alpha-\beta)^{6}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha^{6n}-6\alpha^{5n}\beta^{n}+15\alpha^{4n}\beta^{2n}-20\alpha^{3n}\beta^{3n}+15\alpha^{2n}\beta^{4n}-6\alpha^{n}\beta^{5n}+\beta^{6n}}{(x^{2}+4)^{3}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha^{6n}-6(-1)^{n}\alpha^{4n}+15\alpha^{2n}-20(-1)^{n}+15\beta^{2n}-6(-1)^{n}\beta^{4n}+\beta^{6n}}{(x^{2}+4)^{3}}

\displaystyle=\frac{L_{6n}-6(-1)^{n}L_{4n}+15L_{2n}-20(-1)^{n}}{(x^{2}+4)^{3}}.

Remark.
In
[
4
]
, a similar approach was used for Fibonacci resp. Lucas
numbers
(not polynomials). Since the notion of Lucas numbers was not
used in the even instance, the description was necessarily clumsier than here.

Now we move to powers of Lucas polynomials, which are a bit simpler to describe, although it is still better to distinguish odd resp. even exponents:

\displaystyle L_{n}^{2d+1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{0\leq s\leq 2d+1}\binom{2d+1}{s}\alpha^{(2d+1-s)n}\beta^{sn}

\displaystyle=\sum_{0\leq s\leq d}\binom{2d+1}{s}(-1)^{sn}\Big(\alpha^{(2d+1-2s)n}+\beta^{(2d+1-2s)n}\Big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{0\leq s\leq d}\binom{2d+1}{s}(-1)^{sn}L_{(2d+1-2s)n}.

\displaystyle L_{n}^{2d}

\displaystyle=\sum_{0\leq s\leq 2d}\binom{2d}{s}\alpha^{(2d-s)n}\beta^{sn}

\displaystyle=\sum_{0\leq s\leq d-1}\binom{2d}{s}(-1)^{sn}\Big(\alpha^{(2d-2s)n}+\beta^{(2d-2s)n}\Big)+\binom{2d}{d}(-1)^{dn}

\displaystyle=\sum_{0\leq s\leq d-1}\binom{2d}{s}(-1)^{sn}L_{(2d+1-2s)n}+\binom{2d}{d}(-1)^{dn}.

3.
Conclusion

The authors in
[
1
,
2
]
were also interested in writing
F_{n}F_{n+1}
as a sum. We can compute this more generally:

F_{n}F_{n+d}=\frac{1}{x^{2}+4}L_{2n+d}-\frac{L_{d}}{x^{2}+4}(-1)^{n},

and this is clearly a single sum (just one level of summations). The justification of the formula goes via the Binet forms of the product and expanding.

Abstract
In this work we establish the optimal Lipschitz regularity for non-negative almost minimizers of the one-phase Bernoulli-type functional 𝒥 G ( u , Ω ) : = ∫ Ω ( G ( | ∇ u | ) + χ { u > 0 } ) d x \mathcal{J}_{\mathrm{G}}(u,\Omega)\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\int_{\Omega}\left(\mathrm{G}(|\nabla u|)+\chi_{\{u>0\}}\right)\,dx where Ω ⊂ ℝ n \Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n} is a bounded domain and G : [ 0 , ∞ ) → [ 0 , ∞ ) \mathrm{G}:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty) is a Young function with G ′ = g \mathrm{G}^{\prime}=g satisfying the Lieberman’s classical conditions. Moreover, of independent mathematical interest, we also address a Höder regularity characterization via Campanato-type estimates in the context of Orlicz modulars, which is new for such a class of non-standard growth functionals. Keywords: Almost minimizers, Lipschitz regularity, Orlicz spaces MSC 2020: 35B65, 35J70, 35J87, 46E30.

Introduction and main result

In this manuscript we deal with one-phase problems driven by the
\mathrm{G}-
energy functionals, with
\mathrm{G}
a suitable function with non-polynomial growth. Specifically, we study nonnegative almost minimizers for one-phase Bernoulli-type functional as follows

\mathcal{J}_{\mathrm{G}}(u,\Omega)\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\int_{\Omega}\left(\mathrm{G}(|\nabla u|)+\chi_{\{u>0\}}\right)\,dx

where
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a bounded Lipschitz domain and
\mathrm{G}:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
is a Young function with
\mathrm{G}^{\prime}=g
satisfying

(1.1)

\delta\leq\frac{tg^{\prime}(t)}{g(t)}\leq g_{0}\qquad\text{for some constants}\qquad 1<\delta\leq g_{0}<\infty\quad(\text{Lieberman's condition}).

In our research, almost minimizers mean that we will consider functions
0\leq u\in W^{1,\mathrm{G}}(\Omega)
such that for some
\beta>0
and constant
\kappa\geq 0
it holds that

(1.2)

\mathcal{J}_{\mathrm{G}}(u,B_{r}(x))\leq(1+\kappa r^{\beta})\mathcal{J}_{\mathrm{G}}(v,B_{r}(x))

for any ball
B_{r}(x)
such that
\overline{B_{r}(x)}\subset\Omega
and any
v\in W^{1,\mathrm{G}}(B_{r}(x))
such that
v=u
on
\partial B_{r}(x)
in the sense of traces (see Definition
2.7
and Subsection
2.2
for more details).

Particularly, in this framework, we address optimal local Lipschitz regularity for such class of almost minimizers, where the corresponding estimate depends only on universal parameters

\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega^{\prime})}\leq\mathrm{C}(\delta,g_{0},\Omega^{\prime},\beta,\kappa)\mathrm{G}^{-1}\left(1+\int_{\Omega}\mathrm{G}\left(|\nabla u|\right)\>dx\right)\quad\text{for any}\quad\Omega^{\prime}\subset\subset\Omega.

We must highlight that the functional
\mathcal{J}_{\mathrm{G}}
is a natural generalization of the classical one-phase (Bernoulli type)
p-
energy functional, which corresponds to the choice of
\mathrm{G}(t)=\frac{1}{p}t^{p}
(for each
p\in\left(1,\infty\right)
fixed). We will denote by
\mathcal{J}_{p}
such functional.

Additionally, in the current literature, see
e.g.

[
36
]
, almost minimizers have been interpreted as a sort of minimizers of “perturbed functionals”. For instance, one can consider the functional with regional structure (which is quite similar to certain functionals arising in phase transitions for nonlinear nonlocal models - see

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{J}}_{\mathrm{G}}(u,\Omega)=\mathcal{J}_{\mathrm{G}}(u,\Omega)+\frac{1}{2}\iint_{\Omega\times\Omega}\Phi(y,z)\Psi(u(y)-u(z))dydz,

where
\Phi(y,z)=\rho(y)\rho(z)

\rho(x)=\Theta(u(x))

fulfils
\Theta\equiv 0

\Psi:\mathbb{R}\to[0,1]
satisfies
\Psi\equiv 0
for

Now, if we define the

\displaystyle(\Phi\ast\Psi_{u})(y,z)\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\iint_{\Omega\times\Omega}\Phi(y,z)\Psi(u(y)-u(z))dydz,

then (

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{J}}_{\mathrm{G}}(u,\Omega)=\mathcal{J}_{\mathrm{G}}(u,\Omega)+\frac{1}{2}\Phi(y,z)\ast\Psi_{u}(y,z).

Then, we verify that

\mathcal{J}_{\mathrm{G}}(u,\Omega)\leq\hat{\mathcal{J}}_{\mathrm{G}}(u,\Omega).

On the other hand, it is easy to see

\hat{\mathcal{J}}_{\mathrm{G}}(u,\Omega)\leq\left(1+\frac{1}{2}\omega_{n}r^{n}\right)\mathcal{J}_{\mathrm{G}}(u,\Omega)\leq\left(1+\frac{1}{2}\omega_{n}r^{n}\right)\mathcal{J}_{\mathrm{G}}(v,\Omega),

for any
v\in W^{1,\mathrm{G}}(B_{r}(x))
such that
v=u
on
\partial B_{r}(x)
. In short, a minimizer for the “perturbed functional”

be revealed to be an almost minimizer for the

The main regularity result of this proposal is the following (see Section
2
for details on the hypothesis):

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\mathrm{G}:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
be a Young function such that
g=\mathrm{G}^{\prime}
is convex and satisfies (
1.1
) with

\frac{n(\delta+1)}{n-(\delta+1)}>g_{0}+1.

Let
u\in W^{1,\mathrm{G}}(\Omega)
be an almost minimizer of
\mathcal{J}_{\mathrm{G}}
with exponent
\beta>0
and constant
\kappa\geq 0
. Then,
u
is Lipschitz continuous in the interior of
\Omega
. Moreover, for any
\Omega^{\prime}\subset\joinrel\subset\Omega
one has

\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega^{\prime})}\leq\mathrm{C}\mathrm{G}^{-1}\left(1+\int_{\Omega}\mathrm{G}\left(|\nabla u|\right)\>dx\right)\quad\text{for any}\quad\Omega^{\prime}\subset\subset\Omega.

where
\mathrm{C}=\mathrm{C}(\delta,g_{0},\Omega^{\prime},\beta,\kappa)>0
.

We stress that the nonlinear setting driven by
\mathcal{J}_{\mathrm{G}}(u,\Omega)
provides further technical obstacles, e.g. the sum of two weak solutions is no longer a weak solution. Therefore, it plays an essential role to develop a theory of
g-
harmonic replacements for our nonlinear scenario to overcome such complications.

Furthermore, we point out that the regularity results in this work do not follow from the nowadays classical literature from calculus of variations for operators with Orlicz-Sobolev structure: Indeed, the integrand of the
\mathrm{G}-
energy functional has a discontinuous signature, instead, the non-standard growth scenarios that dealt with continuous integrand or even Lipschitz continuous one, see e.g.
[
1
]
and
[
45
]
for such related topic.

Concerning the strategy of the proof of Theorem
1.1
: a dichotomy result is the main step to establishing the optimal Lipschitz continuity. In effect, the dichotomy property ensures that either the average of the
\mathrm{G}-
energy of an almost minimizer to
\mathcal{J}_{\mathrm{G}}
decreases in a suitable ball or the Luxemburg norm between its gradient and a fixed vector becomes sufficiently small in a proper sense.

Summarizing, either the average of the
\mathrm{G}-
energy of an almost minimizer decreases in a smaller domain, or the almost minimizer is close enough to a linear profile (with good a priori estimates). Once this dichotomy is proved, the next step is to show that one of such alternatives might be improved. Precisely, it will hold a kind of geometric improved of flatness, namely, if an almost minimizer is close to (in the sense of the average of the
\mathrm{G}-
energy) a suitable linear profile, in a ball, then it will be closer to a possibly other linear profile in a smaller ball. Therefore, by iterating such a reasoning, together with the dichotomy property, provides the desired Lipschitz regularity of almost minimizers to
\mathcal{J}_{\mathrm{G}}
,

Finally, in our scenario, we need to show that the sum of
\mathrm{G}-
harmonic replacements solve an equation for which
C^{1,\alpha}
estimates hold. In effect, this holds true using a linearization strategy, together with the assumptions of the dichotomy. The main difficulty appears in the proof of the improvement of dichotomy’s second alternative. The original strategy to solve such an obstacle strongly relies on De Silva-Savin’s work
[
33
]
, where the authors often use harmonic replacements as competitors. This property holds, for harmonic replacements allowing achieve estimates for the average of their energy, exploiting Schauder estimates instead of gradient estimates.

2
Preliminaries

In this section we present some preliminary definitions and results that will be used in the proofs of the main results of the manuscript. We start with some basic facts regarding Young functions and Orlicz-Sobolev spaces; these are rather well known but we include them for the sake of completeness.

2.1
Young functions

An application
\mathrm{G}\colon[0,\infty)\longrightarrow[0,\infty)
is said to be a
Young function
if it admits the integral representation
\displaystyle\mathrm{G}(t)=\int_{0}^{t}g(\tau)\,d\tau
, where
g
is a right continuous function defined on
[0,\infty)
and satisfying:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}g(0)=0,\quad g(t)>0\text{ for }t>0,\\
g\text{ is nondecreasing on }(0,\infty),\\
\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}g(t)=\infty.\end{array}\right.

From these properties it is easy to see that a Young function
\mathrm{G}
is continuous, nonnegative, strictly increasing and convex on
[0,\infty)
. Further, we recall that we may extend
g
to the whole
\mathbb{R}
in an odd fashion: for
t<0

g(t)=-g(-t)
. And we may (and will) assume without loss of generality that
\mathrm{G}(1)=\mathrm{G}^{-1}(1)=1
. Note that
\mathrm{G}
is strictly increasing and hence
\mathrm{G}^{-1}
is well defined.

In this work we will consider the class of Young functions such that
g=\mathrm{G}^{\prime}
is an absolutely continuous function that satisfies the condition

(2.1)

\delta\leq\frac{tg^{\prime}(t)}{g(t)}\leq g_{0}

for some constants
1<\delta\leq g_{0}
. This condition was first considered in the seminal work of G. Lieberman
[
45
]
and is the analogous to the ellipticity condition in the linear theory. For our purposes, we will make the (rather standard assumption) that

(2.2)

(\delta+1)^{\ast}>g_{0}+1

where
(\delta+1)^{\ast}
is the Sobolev conjugate of
(\delta+1)
, i.e.
(\delta+1)^{\ast}=\frac{n(\delta+1)}{n-(\delta+1)}
. We will also assume that

g\text{ is convex.}

This last assumption is analogous to the degenerate setting in the homogeneous problem, i.e. the case where
p\geq 2
.

Equation (
2.1
) implies that that
\mathrm{G}
satisfies

(2.3)

\delta+1\leq\frac{tg(t)}{\mathrm{G}(t)}\leq g_{0}+1;

this condition is equivalent to ask
\mathrm{G}
and
\tilde{\mathrm{G}}
to satisfy the
\Delta_{2}
condition or doubling condition
, i.e.,

(2.4)

\mathrm{G}(2t)\leq 2^{g_{0}+1}\mathrm{G}(t),\qquad\tilde{\mathrm{G}}(2t)\leq 2^{1+\frac{1}{\delta}}\tilde{\mathrm{G}}(t),

where the
complementary
function of a Young function
\mathrm{G}
is the Young function
\tilde{\mathrm{G}}
defined as

\tilde{\mathrm{G}}(t)=\sup\{ta-\mathrm{G}(a)\colon a>0\}.

The doubling condition allow us to split sums as

(2.5)

\mathrm{G}(a+b)\leq 2^{g_{0}}(\mathrm{G}(a)+\mathrm{G}(b)).

Moreover, integrating (
2.3
) we have that

(2.6)

\displaystyle t^{g_{0}+1}\leq\mathrm{G}(t)\leq t^{\delta+1},\quad\text{if }0<t\leq 1

\displaystyle t^{\delta+1}\leq\mathrm{G}(t)\leq t^{g_{0}+1},\quad\text{if }t>1.

As mentioned in the Introduction, the lack of homogeneity is a serious technical difficulty in our context. The following lemma gives the alternatives that hold in this scenario; its proof is elementary so we omit it.

Lemma 2.1
.

For
\theta\in[0,1]
and
t\geq 0

\mathrm{G}(\theta t)\leq\theta\mathrm{G}(t),

and for
\theta\geq 1
and
t\geq 0

\mathrm{G}(\theta t)\geq\theta\mathrm{G}(t).

More generally, for any,
\theta,t\geq 0

(2.7)

\displaystyle\mathrm{G}(t)\min\left\{\theta^{\delta+1},\theta^{g_{0}+1}\right\}\leq\mathrm{G}(\theta t)\leq\mathrm{G}(t)\max\left\{\theta^{\delta+1},\theta^{g_{0}+1}\right\},

(2.8)

\displaystyle\mathrm{G}^{-1}(t)\min\left\{\theta^{\frac{1}{\delta+1}},\theta^{\frac{1}{g_{0}+1}}\right\}\leq\mathrm{G}^{-1}(\theta t)\leq\mathrm{G}^{-1}(t)\max\left\{\theta^{\frac{1}{\delta+1}},\theta^{\frac{1}{g_{0}+1}}\right\}.

2.2
Orlicz-Sobolev spaces

Given a Young function
\mathrm{G}
and a bounded open set
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we consider the spaces
L^{\mathrm{G}}(\Omega)
and
W^{1,\mathrm{G}}(\Omega)
defined as follows:

\begin{array}[]{rcl}L^{\mathrm{G}}(\Omega)&=&\{u:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}\quad\text{measurable such that}\quad\Phi_{\mathrm{G},\Omega}(u)<\infty\}\\
W^{1,\mathrm{G}}(\Omega)&=&\{u\in L^{\mathrm{G}}(\Omega)\quad\text{such that}\quad\Phi_{\mathrm{G},\Omega}(|\nabla u|)<\infty\}\end{array}

where the modular
\Phi_{\mathrm{G},\Omega}(u)
stands for

\displaystyle\Phi_{\mathrm{G},\Omega}(u)=\int_{\Omega}\mathrm{G}(|u|)dx.

These spaces are endowed with the so-called Luxemburg norm defined as follows:

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{\mathrm{G}}(\Omega)}=\inf\left\{\lambda>0:\,\,\,\Phi_{\mathrm{G},\Omega}\left(\frac{u}{\lambda}\right)\leq 1\right\}

and

\|u\|_{W^{1,\mathrm{G}}(\Omega)}=\|u\|_{L^{\mathrm{G}}(\Omega)}+\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\mathrm{G}}(\Omega)}

In general, it is easy to see that

(2.9)

\|w\|_{L^{\mathrm{G}}(\Omega)}\leq\max\left\{\Phi_{\mathrm{G},\Omega}(w)^{\frac{1}{g_{0}+1}},\Phi_{\mathrm{G},\Omega}(w)^{\frac{1}{\delta+1}}\right\}.

2.3
Technical results concerning
g-
harmonic functions

The notion of
g-
harmonic function will of course be of great importance in our work; In effect,
g-
harmonic functions are weak solutions of the equation
\Delta_{g}v=0
where
\Delta_{g}
is the
g-
Laplacian operator given by

\Delta_{g}v=\mathop{\text{div}}\left(g(|\nabla v|)\frac{\nabla v}{|\nabla v|}\right).

More precisely, we will be interested in unique weak solutions of the Dirichlet problem

(2.10)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcrcl}\Delta_{g}v&=&0&\text{in}&\Omega\\
v&=&h&\text{on}&\partial\Omega\end{array}\right.

Such weak solutions can be constructed as minimizers of the energy functional
\displaystyle u\rightarrow\int_{\Omega}\mathrm{G}(|\nabla u|)\>dx
(with fixed Dirichlet boundary condition) via the Direct Method of the Calculus of Variations.

The following proposition will be used, its proof is contained in the Lieberman’s fundamental work
[
45
, Section 5]
. Before stating it, we recall that a constant will be called universal if it depends only on
n,\delta
and
g_{0}
(and eventually
\beta
or
\kappa
in Definition
2.7
); multiplicative constants will be denoted by
C
and in the proofs they may change from line to line.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
v\in W^{1,\mathrm{G}}(B_{R})
be a
g-
harmonic function. Then,

1.

there exists a universal constant
\mathrm{C}>0
such that for any
x\in B_{R/2}

(2.11)

\sup_{B_{R/4}(x)}\mathrm{G}\left(|\nabla v|\right)\leq\mathrm{C}\mathchoice{\mathop{\vrule width=6.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.5pt\kern-8.0pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-6.0ptB_{R/2}(x)}}{\mathop{\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.6pt\kern-6.0pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{R/2}(x)}}{\mathop{\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.6pt\kern-6.0pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{R/2}(x)}}{\mathop{\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.6pt\kern-6.0pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{R/2}(x)}}\mathrm{G}\left(|\nabla v|\right)\>dx.

2.

there exist universal constants
\mathrm{C}>0
and
\alpha\in(0,1)
such that

(2.12)

\mathop{\text{osc}}_{B_{\rho}}|\nabla v|\leq\mathrm{C}\sup_{B_{R}}|\nabla v|\left(\frac{\rho}{R}\right)^{\alpha},\quad 0<\rho<R.

Remark 2.3
.

The estimates in Proposition (
2.2
) still hold under the more general assumption that
v
satisfies
\mathop{\text{div}}(\mathbb{A}(x)\nabla v)=0
with
x\mapsto\mathbb{A}(x)
satisfying the structural condition:

\lambda\frac{g(|z|)}{|z|}|\xi|^{2}\leq\mathbb{A}(x)\xi\cdot\xi\leq\Lambda\frac{g(|z|)}{|z|}|\xi|^{2}.

See
[
45
]
for details.

Another important concept that will be used is that of harmonic replacement in a ball, which roughly speaking is the
g-
harmonic that coincides with a given function on the boundary of a ball:

Definition 2.4
.

The
g-
harmonic replacement of a function
u\in W^{1,G}(\Omega)
in
B_{r}(x)
is the unique
g-
harmonic function
v\in W^{1,G}(B_{r}(x))
that coincides with
u
on
\partial B_{r}(x)
in the sense of traces, i.e. the unique weak solution of (
2.10
) with
\Omega=B_{r}(x)
and
h=u
.

The following technical lemmas will be used later: the first one is a bound on the energy of difference between and function and its
g-
harmonic replacement and the other one is needed to ensure the desired ellipticity (in the sense of Remark
2.3
) so that interior estimates apply:

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
G
a Young function satisfying (
2.1
) and such that
\mathrm{G}^{\prime}=g
is a convex function,
u\in W^{1,G}(B_{r})
and
v
the
g-
harmonic replacement of
u
in
B_{r}
. Then the following inequality holds for a universal constant
\mathrm{C}>0

\int_{B_{r}}\mathrm{G}(|\nabla u-\nabla v|)\,dx\leq\mathrm{C}\int_{B_{r}}\mathrm{G}(|\nabla u|)-\mathrm{G}(|\nabla v|)\,dx.

Proof.

As
v
is a weak solution of
\Delta_{g}u=0
, we know that

(2.13)

\int_{B_{r}}g(|\nabla v|)\frac{\nabla v}{|\nabla v|}\nabla\varphi\,dx=0,

for all
\varphi\in W_{0}^{1,\mathrm{G}}(B_{r})
.

We consider the following convex combination

u^{s}(x)=su(x)+(1-s)v(x)\qquad 0\leq s\leq 1

Observe that
u^{0}=v
and
u^{1}=u
. Now using that
u-v\in W^{1,G}_{0}(\Omega)
and (
2.13
), we have

\displaystyle\int_{B_{r}}\mathrm{G}(|\nabla u|)-\mathrm{G}(|\nabla v|)\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{r}}\mathrm{G}(|\nabla u^{1}|)-\mathrm{G}(|\nabla u^{0}|)\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{r}}\left(\int_{0}^{1}\frac{d}{ds}\mathrm{G}(|\nabla u^{s}|)\,ds\right)\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{r}}\left(\int_{0}^{1}g(|\nabla u^{s}|)\frac{\nabla u^{s}}{|\nabla u^{s}|}\cdot\nabla(u-v)\,ds\right)\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{r}}\int_{0}^{1}\left(g(|\nabla u^{s}|)\frac{\nabla u^{s}}{|\nabla u^{s}|}-g(|\nabla v|)\frac{\nabla v}{|\nabla v|}\right)\cdot\nabla(u-v)\,ds\,dx.

Next, note that
u^{s}(x)-v(x)=su(x)-sv(x)=s(u(x)-v(x))
; then using that

\left(g(|a|)\frac{a}{|a|}-g(|b|)\frac{b}{|b|}\right)\cdot\nabla(a-b)\geq\mathrm{C}\mathrm{G}(|a-b|)

for a constant
\mathrm{C}=\mathrm{C}(\delta)>0
(see
[
16
, Lemma 3.1]
for a proof) and (
2.7
) the last above term is bounded in the following way

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\displaystyle\int_{B_{r}}\frac{1}{s}\int_{0}^{1}\left(g(|\nabla u^{s}|)\frac{\nabla u^{s}}{|\nabla u^{s}|}-g(|\nabla v|)\frac{\nabla v}{|\nabla v|}\right)\cdot\nabla(u^{s}-v)\,ds\,dx&\geq&\displaystyle\mathrm{C}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1}{s}\int_{B_{r}}\mathrm{G}(|\nabla u^{s}-\nabla v|)\,dx\,ds\\
&\geq&\displaystyle\mathrm{C}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{s^{g_{0}+1}}{s}\int_{B_{r}}\mathrm{G}(|\nabla u-\nabla v|)\,dx\,ds\\
&\geq&\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{C}}{g_{0}+1}\int_{B_{r}}\mathrm{G}(|\nabla u-\nabla v|)\,dx\end{array}

and the desired inequality follows.
∎

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
q\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
|h(x)|<\frac{|q|}{2}
. If
\overrightarrow{\mathbf{F}}(z)=g(|z|)\frac{z}{|z|}
for a Young function satisfying (
2.1
) and

\mathfrak{A}(x)\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\int_{0}^{1}D\overrightarrow{\mathbf{F}}\left(q+th(x)\right)\>dt

then

(2.14)

\lambda\frac{g(|q|)}{|q|}|\xi|^{2}\leq\mathfrak{A}(x)\xi\cdot\xi\leq\Lambda\frac{g(|q|)}{|q|}|\xi|^{2}

for any
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
and for some constants
0<\lambda\leq\Lambda
depending only on
\delta
and
g_{0}
.

Proof.

We start by computing
D\overrightarrow{\mathbf{F}}
: if
i\neq j

D_{i}\overrightarrow{\mathbf{F}}_{j}(z)=D_{i}\left(g(|z|)\frac{z_{j}}{|z|}\right)=g^{\prime}(|z|)\frac{z_{i}z_{j}}{|z|^{2}}-g(|z|)\frac{z_{i}z_{j}}{|z|^{3}}

while

D_{i}\overrightarrow{\mathbf{F}}_{i}(z)=D_{i}\left(g(|z|)\frac{z_{i}}{|z|}\right)=g^{\prime}(|z|)\frac{z_{i}^{2}}{|z|^{2}}+g(|z|)\left(\frac{1}{|z|}-\frac{z_{i}^{2}}{|z|^{3}}\right).

Then,

\displaystyle D\overrightarrow{\mathbf{F}}(z)\xi\cdot\xi

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\neq j}\left(g^{\prime}(|z|)\frac{z_{i}z_{j}}{|z|^{2}}-g(|z|)\frac{z_{i}z_{j}}{|z|^{3}}\right)\xi_{i}\xi_{j}+\sum_{i}\left(g^{\prime}(|z|)\frac{z_{i}^{2}}{|z|^{2}}+g(|z|)\left(\frac{1}{|z|}-\frac{z_{i}^{2}}{|z|^{3}}\right)\right)\xi_{i}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\neq j}\left(g^{\prime}(|z|)-\frac{g(|z|)}{|z|}\right)\frac{z_{i}z_{j}\xi_{i}\xi_{j}}{|z|^{2}}+g^{\prime}(|z|)|\xi|^{2}

\displaystyle=\left(g^{\prime}(|z|)-\frac{g(|z|)}{|z|}\right)\frac{1}{|z|^{2}}\left(z\cdot\xi\right)^{2}+\frac{g(|z|)}{|z|}|\xi|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\left(\frac{g(|z|)}{|z|}-\left(g^{\prime}(|z|)-\frac{g(|z|)}{|z|}\right)\right)|\xi|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq g_{0}\frac{g(|z|)}{|z|}|\xi|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq g^{\prime}(|z|)|\xi|^{2}.

This implies

\mathfrak{A}(x)\xi\cdot\xi\geq\int_{0}^{1}g^{\prime}(|q+th(x)|)\>dt|\xi|^{2}\geq g^{\prime}\left(\frac{|q|}{2}\right)|\xi|^{2}\geq\delta\frac{g\left(\frac{|q|}{2}\right)}{\frac{|q|}{2}}|\xi|^{2}\geq\lambda\frac{g\left(|q|\right)}{|q|}|\xi|^{2}

where
\lambda\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=2^{1-g_{0}}\delta
, and we have used that
|q+th(x)|\geq\frac{|q|}{2}
by hypothesis, that
g^{\prime}
is increasing and the doubling condition for
g
. This gives the lower bound on (
2.14
).

Similarly, we find that

D\overrightarrow{\mathbf{F}}(z)\xi\cdot\xi\leq\left(\frac{g(|z|)}{|z|}+\left(g^{\prime}(|z|)-\frac{g(|z|)}{|z|}\right)\right)|\xi|^{2}=g^{\prime}(|z|)|\xi|^{2}

and

\mathfrak{A}(x)\xi\cdot\xi\leq g^{\prime}\left(\frac{3|q|}{2}\right)|\xi|^{2}\leq g_{0}\frac{g\left(\frac{3|q|}{2}\right)}{\frac{3|q|}{2}}|\xi|^{2}\leq\Lambda\frac{g\left(|q|\right)}{|q|}|\xi|^{2}

now with
\Lambda\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\left(\frac{3}{2}\right)^{g_{0}-1}g_{0}
, this time using that
|q+th(x)|\leq\frac{3|q|}{2}
.
∎

2.4
Almost minimizers

In this section we give the rigorous definition of an almost minimizer and show that they satisfy a nice scaling property:

Definition 2.7
.

A function
u\in W^{1,\mathrm{G}}(\Omega)
is an almost minimizer of
\mathcal{J}_{G}
with exponent
\beta>0
and constant
\kappa\geq 0
if

1.

u\geq 0
a.e. in
\Omega
;

2.

for any ball
B_{r}(x)
such that
\overline{B_{r}(x)}\subset\Omega
and any
v\in W^{1,G}(B_{r}(x))
such that
v=u
on
\partial B_{r}(x)
in the sense of traces (i.e.
v-u\in W_{0}^{1,G}(B_{r}(x))
) it holds that

\mathcal{J}_{\mathrm{G}}(u,B_{r}(x))\leq(1+\kappa r^{\beta})\mathcal{J}_{\mathrm{G}}(v,B_{r}(x)).

Almost minimizers satisfy the following scaling property:

Lemma 2.8
.

If
u\in W^{1,G}(B_{1})
is an almost minimizer of
\mathcal{J}_{G}
in
B_{1}
with exponent
\beta>0
and constant
\kappa
and
0<r<1
then
u_{r}(x)\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\frac{u(rx)}{r}
is an almost minimizer of
\mathcal{J}_{G}
in
B_{1/r}
with exponent
\beta>0
and constant
r^{\beta}\kappa
.

Proof.

Assume
u
is an almost minimizer of
\mathcal{J}_{G}
in
B_{1}
with exponent
\beta>0
and constant
\kappa
. Let
\bar{x}\in B_{1/r}
and
\rho>0
be such that
\overline{B_{\rho}(\bar{x})}\subset B_{1/r}
and
v\in W^{1,G}(B_{\rho}(\bar{x}))
such that
v=u_{r}
on
\partial B_{\rho}(\bar{x})
in the sense of traces.

Denoting
\bar{y}=r\bar{x}
we have

\overline{B_{\rho}(\bar{x})}\subset B_{1/r}\>\Rightarrow\>\overline{B_{r\rho}(\bar{y})}\subset B_{1},\quad x\in\partial B_{r\rho}(\bar{y})\>\Rightarrow\>\frac{x}{r}\in\partial{B_{\rho}(\bar{x})}.

Then, by hypothesis

rv\left(\frac{x}{r}\right)=ru_{r}\left(\frac{x}{r}\right)=u(x)

when
x\in\partial B_{r\rho}(\bar{y})
and therefore

\mathcal{J}_{\mathrm{G}}(u,B_{r\rho}(\bar{y}))\leq(1+\kappa(r\rho)^{\beta})\mathcal{J}_{\mathrm{G}}\left(rv\left(\frac{\cdot}{r}\right),B_{r\rho}(\bar{y})\right).

But changing variables we have that

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{\mathrm{G}}(u,B_{r\rho}(\bar{y}))

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{r\rho}(\bar{y})}\mathrm{G}(|\nabla u(y)|)+\chi_{\{u>0\}}\,dy

\displaystyle=r^{n}\int_{B_{\rho}(\bar{x})}\mathrm{G}(|\nabla u(rx)|)+\chi_{\{u>0\}}(rx)\,dx

\displaystyle=r^{n}\int_{B_{\rho}(\bar{x})}\mathrm{G}(|\nabla u_{r}|)+\chi_{\{u_{r}>0\}}\,dx

\displaystyle=r^{n}\mathcal{J}_{\mathrm{G}}\left(u_{r},B_{\rho}(\bar{x})\right)

and a similar computation can be done to show that

\mathcal{J}_{\mathrm{G}}\left(rv\left(\frac{\cdot}{r}\right),B_{r\rho}(\bar{y})\right)=r^{n}\mathcal{J}_{\mathrm{G}}\left(v,B_{\rho}(\bar{x})\right)

and get the desired inequality.
∎

3.5
)

\mathchoice{\mathop{\vrule width=6.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.5pt\kern-8.0pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-6.0ptB_{\eta}}}{\mathop{\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.6pt\kern-6.0pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{\eta}}}{\mathop{\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.6pt\kern-6.0pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{\eta}}}{\mathop{\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.6pt\kern-6.0pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{\eta}}}\mathrm{G}(|\nabla v-q|)\,dx\leq\mathrm{C}\eta^{\alpha(\delta+1)}\mathrm{G}(a)\qquad\mbox{ for all }\eta\leq\frac{1}{2}.

Now, using the
\Delta_{2}
condition (see (
2.4
)),

\displaystyle\mathchoice{\mathop{\vrule width=6.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.5pt\kern-8.0pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-6.0ptB_{\eta}}}{\mathop{\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.6pt\kern-6.0pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{\eta}}}{\mathop{\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.6pt\kern-6.0pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{\eta}}}{\mathop{\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.6pt\kern-6.0pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{\eta}}}\mathrm{G}(|\nabla u-q|)\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\mathrm{C}\left(\mathchoice{\mathop{\vrule width=6.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.5pt\kern-8.0pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-6.0ptB_{\eta}}}{\mathop{\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.6pt\kern-6.0pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{\eta}}}{\mathop{\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.6pt\kern-6.0pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{\eta}}}{\mathop{\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.6pt\kern-6.0pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{\eta}}}\mathrm{G}(|\nabla u-\nabla v|)\,dx+\mathchoice{\mathop{\vrule width=6.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.5pt\kern-8.0pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-6.0ptB_{\eta}}}{\mathop{\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.6pt\kern-6.0pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{\eta}}}{\mathop{\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.6pt\kern-6.0pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{\eta}}}{\mathop{\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.6pt\kern-6.0pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{\eta}}}\mathrm{G}(|\nabla v-q|)\,dx\right)

\displaystyle=\mathfrak{I}+\mathfrak{II}

These two terms are bounded using the previous estimates:

\mathfrak{I}\leq\frac{1}{|B_{\eta}|}\int_{B_{1}}\mathrm{G}(|\nabla u-\nabla v|)\,dx\leq\frac{\mathrm{C}}{\eta^{n}}(\sigma\mathrm{G}(a)+1).

and

\mathfrak{II}\leq\mathrm{C}\eta^{\alpha(\delta+1)}\mathrm{G}(a).

So we get

(3.6)

\mathchoice{\mathop{\vrule width=6.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.5pt\kern-8.0pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-6.0ptB_{\eta}}}{\mathop{\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.6pt\kern-6.0pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{\eta}}}{\mathop{\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.6pt\kern-6.0pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{\eta}}}{\mathop{\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.6pt\kern-6.0pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{\eta}}}\mathrm{G}(|\nabla u-q|)\,dx\leq\mathrm{C}\eta^{-n}\sigma\mathrm{G}(a)+\mathrm{C}\eta^{-n}+\mathrm{C}\mathrm{G}(a)\eta^{\alpha(\delta+1)}

and

(3.7)

\mathchoice{\mathop{\vrule width=6.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.5pt\kern-8.0pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-6.0ptB_{\eta}}}{\mathop{\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.6pt\kern-6.0pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{\eta}}}{\mathop{\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.6pt\kern-6.0pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{\eta}}}{\mathop{\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.6pt\kern-6.0pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{\eta}}}\mathrm{G}(|\nabla u|)\,dx\leq\mathrm{C}\eta^{-n}\sigma\mathrm{G}(a)+\mathrm{C}\eta^{-n}+\mathrm{C}\mathrm{G}(a)\eta^{\alpha(\delta+1)}+\mathrm{C}\mathrm{G}(|q|).

Next, we choose
\sigma=\eta^{n+1}

\mathrm{C}\eta^{-n}\sigma\mathrm{G}(a)+\mathrm{C}\eta^{-n}+\mathrm{C}\mathrm{G}(a)\eta^{\alpha(\delta+1)}=\mathrm{C}\eta\mathrm{G}(a)+\mathrm{C}\eta^{-n}+\mathrm{C}\mathrm{G}(a)\eta^{\alpha(\delta+1)}

and
a
such that

(3.8)

\mathrm{C}\eta\mathrm{G}(a)+\mathrm{C}\eta^{-n}+\mathrm{C}\mathrm{G}(a)\eta^{\alpha(\delta+1)}\leq\mathrm{G}(\varepsilon a).

The inequality (
3.8
) holds if

a\geq\mathrm{G}^{-1}\left(\frac{\mathrm{C}\eta^{-n}}{\varepsilon^{g_{0}+1}-\mathrm{C}\eta-\mathrm{C}\eta^{\alpha(\delta+1)}}\right).

(Note that the denominator is positive if
\eta
is small enough.) Then,

\mathchoice{\mathop{\vrule width=6.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.5pt\kern-8.0pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-6.0ptB_{\eta}}}{\mathop{\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.6pt\kern-6.0pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{\eta}}}{\mathop{\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.6pt\kern-6.0pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{\eta}}}{\mathop{\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.6pt\kern-6.0pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{\eta}}}\mathrm{G}(|\nabla u|)\,dx\leq\mathrm{G}(\varepsilon a)+\mathrm{C}\mathrm{G}(|q|).

Next we split the two possible cases; if

|q|\leq\mathrm{G}^{-1}\left(\frac{1}{\mathrm{C}}\mathrm{G}\left(\frac{a}{4}\right)\right)

making
\varepsilon<1/4
we get

\mathchoice{\mathop{\vrule width=6.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.5pt\kern-8.0pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-6.0ptB_{\eta}}}{\mathop{\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.6pt\kern-6.0pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{\eta}}}{\mathop{\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.6pt\kern-6.0pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{\eta}}}{\mathop{\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.6pt\kern-6.0pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{\eta}}}\mathrm{G}(|\nabla u|)\,dx\leq 2\mathrm{G}\left(\frac{a}{4}\right)\leq\frac{2}{2^{\delta+1}}\mathrm{G}\left(\frac{a}{2}\right)\leq\mathrm{G}\left(\frac{a}{2}\right)

which is the first alternative in the Lemma.

On the other hand, if

|q|>\mathrm{G}^{-1}\left(\frac{1}{\mathrm{C}}\mathrm{G}\left(\frac{a}{4}\right)\right)

then by (
3.6
)

\mathchoice{\mathop{\vrule width=6.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.5pt\kern-8.0pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-6.0ptB_{\eta}}}{\mathop{\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.6pt\kern-6.0pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{\eta}}}{\mathop{\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.6pt\kern-6.0pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{\eta}}}{\mathop{\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.6pt\kern-6.0pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{\eta}}}\mathrm{G}(|\nabla u-q|)\,dx\leq\mathrm{G}(\varepsilon a).

Noting that (
3.5
) implies the bound on
|q|
we finish the proof.
∎

Remark 3.2
.

Using the
\Delta_{2}
condition (see in particular (
2.7
)) we can replace (
3.3
) by

(3.9)

\mathchoice{\mathop{\vrule width=6.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.5pt\kern-8.0pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-6.0ptB_{\eta}}}{\mathop{\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.6pt\kern-6.0pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{\eta}}}{\mathop{\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.6pt\kern-6.0pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{\eta}}}{\mathop{\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.6pt\kern-6.0pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{\eta}}}\mathrm{G}(|\nabla u-q|)\,dx\leq\varepsilon^{\delta+1}\mathrm{G}(a).

The following lemma can be seen as an improvement of the previous one in the case where
\varepsilon
and
\sigma
are (universally) small enough.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
0<a_{0}<a_{1}
and
\mathrm{G}
a Young function satisfying (
2.1
) such that
\mathrm{G}^{\prime}=g
is a convex function. Let
u\in W^{1,G}(B_{1})
with
u\geq 0
and:

\mathcal{J}_{\mathrm{G}}(u,B_{1})\leq(1+\sigma)\mathcal{J}_{\mathrm{G}}(v,B_{1})

for all
v\in W^{1,\mathrm{G}}(B_{1})
such that
v=u
on
\partial B_{1}
. Define
\mathrm{G}(a)
be as in (
3.1
) and assume

a_{0}\leq a\leq a_{1}.

Assume also that

\mathchoice{\mathop{\vrule width=6.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.5pt\kern-8.0pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-6.0ptB_{1}}}{\mathop{\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.6pt\kern-6.0pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{1}}}{\mathop{\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.6pt\kern-6.0pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{1}}}{\mathop{\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.6pt\kern-6.0pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{1}}}\mathrm{G}(|\nabla u-q|)\,dx\leq\mathrm{G}\left(a\right)\varepsilon^{\delta+1}

for
\varepsilon
small enough with
q\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
satisfying

(3.10)

\mathrm{G}^{-1}\left(\frac{1}{\mathrm{C}}\mathrm{G}\left(\frac{a}{8}\right)\right)\leq|q|\leq 2\mathrm{C}_{0}a

with
C_{0}
given in (
3.4
).

Then there exist universal constants
\varepsilon_{0},\alpha,r\in(0,1)
such that if
\varepsilon<\varepsilon_{0}
and
\sigma\leq c_{0}\mathrm{G}(\varepsilon)
then

\mathchoice{\mathop{\vrule width=6.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.5pt\kern-8.0pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-6.0ptB_{r}}}{\mathop{\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.6pt\kern-6.0pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{r}}}{\mathop{\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.6pt\kern-6.0pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{r}}}{\mathop{\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.6pt\kern-6.0pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{r}}}\mathrm{G}(|\nabla u-\tilde{q}|)\,dx\leq r^{\alpha(\delta+1)}\mathrm{G}(a)\varepsilon^{\delta+1}

where
\tilde{q}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
verifies

|q-\tilde{q}|\leq\tilde{C}\varepsilon^{\tau}a\qquad\mbox{ where }\qquad\tau=\frac{\delta+1}{g_{0}+1}

Proof.

Let
\hat{v}
be the
g-
harmonic replacement of
u
in
B_{1/2}
and define

\begin{cases}v=\hat{v}&\quad\text{ in }B_{1/2},\\
v=u&\quad\text{ in }B_{1}\setminus B_{1/2}.\end{cases}

Since
v\in W^{1,G}(B_{1})
and
v=u
on
\partial B_{1}
we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{\mathrm{G}}(u,B_{1/2})+\mathcal{J}_{\mathrm{G}}(u,B_{1}\setminus B_{1/2})

\displaystyle=\mathcal{J}_{\mathrm{G}}(u,B_{1})

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{J}_{\mathrm{G}}(v,B_{1})+\sigma\mathcal{J}_{\mathrm{G}}(v,B_{1})

\displaystyle=\mathcal{J}_{\mathrm{G}}(v,B_{1/2})+\mathcal{J}_{\mathrm{G}}(v,B_{1}\setminus B_{1/2})+\sigma\mathcal{J}_{\mathrm{G}}(v,B_{1}).

so

\mathcal{J}_{\mathrm{G}}(u,B_{1/2})\leq\mathcal{J}_{\mathrm{G}}(v,B_{1/2})+\sigma\mathcal{J}_{\mathrm{G}}(v,B_{1}).

Now using the definition of
\mathcal{J}_{G}
on the previous inequality we have

\int_{B_{1/2}}\mathrm{G}(|\nabla u|)\,dx+|\{u>0\}\cap B_{1/2}|\leq\int_{B_{1/2}}\mathrm{G}(|\nabla v|)\,dx+|B_{1/2}|+\sigma\mathcal{J}_{\mathrm{G}}(v,B_{1})

and hence

\int_{B_{1/2}}\mathrm{G}(|\nabla u|)-\mathrm{G}(|\nabla v|)\,dx\leq|B_{1/2}|-|\{u>0\}\cap B_{1/2}|+\sigma\mathcal{J}_{\mathrm{G}}(v,B_{1}).

Using Lemma
2.5
, the fact that

|B_{1/2}|-|\{u>0\}\cap B_{1/2}|=|\{u=0\}\cap B_{1/2}|

and that

\mathcal{J}_{\mathrm{G}}(v,B_{1})\leq\int_{B_{1}}\mathrm{G}(|\nabla u|)\,dx+|B_{1}|\leq|B_{1}|\left(\mathrm{G}(a)+1\right)

we arrive at the following inequality:

(3.11)

\int_{B_{1/2}}\mathrm{G}(|\nabla u-\nabla v|)\,dx\leq\mathrm{C}\sigma(\mathrm{G}(a)+1)+|\{u=0\}\cap B_{1/2}|.

Next we will prove that

(3.12)

|\{u=0\}\cap B_{1/2}|\leq\mathrm{C}\varepsilon^{\delta+1}\varepsilon^{\gamma}

for some
\gamma\in(0,1)
. We start out by defining

\mathfrak{l}(x)\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=q\cdot x+b

with

b\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\mathchoice{\mathop{\vrule width=6.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.5pt\kern-8.0pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-6.0ptB_{1}}}{\mathop{\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.6pt\kern-6.0pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{1}}}{\mathop{\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.6pt\kern-6.0pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{1}}}{\mathop{\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.6pt\kern-6.0pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{1}}}u(x)\>dx.

Notice that

\mathchoice{\mathop{\vrule width=6.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.5pt\kern-8.0pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-6.0ptB_{1}}}{\mathop{\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.6pt\kern-6.0pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{1}}}{\mathop{\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.6pt\kern-6.0pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{1}}}{\mathop{\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.6pt\kern-6.0pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{1}}}(u(x)-\mathfrak{l}(x))\>dx=-\mathchoice{\mathop{\vrule width=6.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.5pt\kern-8.0pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-6.0ptB_{1}}}{\mathop{\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.6pt\kern-6.0pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{1}}}{\mathop{\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.6pt\kern-6.0pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{1}}}{\mathop{\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.6pt\kern-6.0pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{1}}}q\cdot x\>dx=0.

Denote

\mathchoice{\mathop{\vrule width=6.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.5pt\kern-8.0pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-6.0ptB_{1}}}{\mathop{\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.6pt\kern-6.0pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{1}}}{\mathop{\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.6pt\kern-6.0pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{1}}}{\mathop{\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.6pt\kern-6.0pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{1}}}(u(x)-\mathfrak{l}(x))\>dx=(u-\mathfrak{l})_{B_{1}}

we have by Poincaré inequality

(3.13)

\|u-\mathfrak{l}-(u-\mathfrak{l})_{B_{1}}\|_{L^{\mathrm{G}}(B_{1})}=\|u-\mathfrak{l}\|_{L^{\mathrm{G}}(B_{1})}\leq\mathrm{C}\|\nabla(u-\mathfrak{l})\|_{L^{\mathrm{G}}(B_{1})}.

Now, we want to use the fact that

\mathchoice{\mathop{\vrule width=6.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.5pt\kern-8.0pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-6.0ptB_{1}}}{\mathop{\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.6pt\kern-6.0pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{1}}}{\mathop{\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.6pt\kern-6.0pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{1}}}{\mathop{\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.6pt\kern-6.0pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{1}}}\mathrm{G}(|\nabla u-q|)\,dx\leq\mathrm{C}\mathrm{G}\left(a\right)\varepsilon^{\delta+1}

so we need to compare modulars and norms. Applying (
2.9
) to
w=\nabla(u-l)
and using the previous inequality we have

(3.14)

\|\nabla(u-\mathfrak{l})\|_{L^{\mathrm{G}}(B_{1})}\leq\mathrm{C}\varepsilon^{\tau}\mathrm{G}(a)^{\frac{1}{g_{0}+1}}.

and by (
3.13
)

\|u-\mathfrak{l}\|_{L^{\mathrm{G}}(B_{1})}\leq\mathrm{C}\varepsilon^{\tau}\mathrm{G}(a)^{\frac{1}{g_{0}+1}}.

Next, we point out that if we denote
\mathfrak{l}^{-}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\min\{0,l\}
then
l^{-}\leq|u-l|
and hence

\mathrm{G}\left(\frac{\mathfrak{l}^{-}}{\lambda}\right)\leq\mathrm{G}\left(\frac{|u-\mathfrak{l}|}{\lambda}\right)

and we get

(3.15)

\|\mathfrak{l}^{-}\|_{L^{\mathrm{G}}(B_{1})}\leq\|u-\mathfrak{l}\|_{L^{\mathrm{G}}(B_{1})}\leq\mathrm{C}\varepsilon^{\tau}\mathrm{G}(a)^{\frac{1}{g_{0}+1}}.

Next, we claim that

(3.16)

\mathfrak{l}(x)\geq\tilde{\mathrm{c}}\mathrm{G}(a)^{\frac{1}{g_{0}+1}}

in
B_{1/2}
for sufficiently small
\varepsilon
and some constant
\tilde{c}
. Let us denote

\sigma\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\mathrm{G}(a)^{\frac{1}{g_{0}+1}}

to ease notation and suppose that (
3.16
) does not hold, i.e. for any
c
there exists
x_{0}\in B_{1/2}
such that

\mathfrak{l}(x_{0})<\mathrm{c}\sigma.

Since
\mathfrak{l}(x_{0})=q\cdot x_{0}+b
and hence

\mathfrak{l}(x)-b\in\left[\frac{-|q|}{2},\frac{|q|}{2}\right]

this yields

\mathrm{c}\sigma>b-\frac{|q|}{2}

or

(3.17)

\mathrm{c}\sigma+\frac{|q|}{2}>b.

Let

\mathcal{B}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\left\{x=-\frac{tq}{|q|}+\eta,t\in\left[\frac{6}{8},\frac{7}{8}\right],\eta\in B_{1/8}\right\}

so that, in particular,
\mathcal{B}\subset B_{1}
.

Now, for
x\in\mathcal{B}
and using (
3.17
)

\mathfrak{l}(x)=-t|q|+\eta\cdot q+b<\mathrm{c}\sigma+\frac{|q|}{2}-\frac{6}{8}|q|+\frac{1}{8}|q|=\mathrm{c}\sigma-\frac{1}{8}|q|

but then (
3.10
) gives

\mathfrak{l}(x)<\mathrm{c}\sigma-\frac{1}{8}\mathrm{G}^{-1}\left(\frac{1}{\mathrm{C}}\mathrm{G}\left(\frac{\sigma}{8}\right)\right)

from which we get the bound

\mathfrak{l}(x)<\mathrm{c}\sigma-\frac{\mathrm{C}}{64}\sigma.

which taking
\mathrm{c}>0
small enough gives
\mathfrak{l}(x)<-\bar{\mathrm{C}}\sigma
for a positive constant
\bar{\mathrm{C}}
. Inserting this into (
3.15
) gives

\varepsilon^{\tau}\sigma\geq\|\mathfrak{l}^{-}\|_{L^{\mathrm{G}}(B_{1})}>\|\bar{\mathrm{C}}\sigma\|_{L^{\mathrm{G}}(B_{1})}

which is a contradiction if
\varepsilon>0
is small enough. Therefore (
3.16
) must hold.

The next step of the proof is divided into two cases according to the embedding properties of Orlicz-Sobolev spaces (see
[
1
, Sections 8.27-8.35]
). First, if

\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{\mathrm{G}^{-1}(\tau)}{\tau^{1+\frac{1}{n}}}\>d\tau=\infty

we consider the the Sobolev conjugate of
\mathrm{G}

(\mathrm{G}^{\ast})^{-1}(t)\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\mathrm{G}^{-1}(\tau)}{\tau^{1+\frac{1}{n}}}\>d\tau.

Then, we have that

\|u-\mathfrak{l}\|_{L^{\mathrm{G}^{\ast}}(B_{1})}\leq\mathrm{C}\|\nabla(u-\mathfrak{l})\|_{L^{\mathrm{G}}(B_{1})}

(here we have also used (
3.13
)) which combined with (
3.14
) gives

\|u-\mathfrak{l}\|_{L^{\mathrm{G}^{\ast}}(B_{1})}\leq\mathrm{C}\varepsilon^{\tau}\mathrm{G}(a)^{\frac{1}{g_{0}+1}}.

On the other hand, (
3.16
) gives that

\|\mathfrak{l}^{-}\|_{L^{\mathrm{G}^{\ast}}(B_{1})}\geq\mathrm{c}\mathrm{G}(a)^{\frac{1}{g_{0}+1}}\|\chi_{\{u=0\}\cap B_{1/2}}\|_{\mathrm{G}^{\ast}}\geq\mathrm{c}\mathrm{G}(a)^{\frac{1}{g_{0}+1}}|\{u=0\}\cap B_{1/2}|^{1/(\delta+1)^{\ast}}

and putting both inequalities together we obtain

\mathrm{c}\mathrm{G}(a)^{\frac{1}{g_{0}+1}}|\{u=0\}\cap B_{1/2}|^{1/(\delta+1)^{\ast}}\leq\mathrm{C}\varepsilon^{\tau}\mathrm{G}(a)^{\frac{1}{g_{0}+1}}

or

|\{u=0\}\cap B_{1/2}|\leq\mathrm{C}\varepsilon^{\tau(\delta+1)^{\ast}}=\mathrm{C}\varepsilon^{\delta+1}\varepsilon^{\gamma}

which is (
3.12
) with
\gamma=\tau(\delta+1)^{\ast}-(\delta+1)>0
(being positive owing to (
2.2
)).

The second case is when

\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{\mathrm{G}^{-1}(\tau)}{\tau^{1+\frac{1}{n}}}\>d\tau<\infty,

in which the Morrey-type embedding holds and (again using Poincaré)

\sup_{B_{1}}|u-\mathfrak{l}|\leq\mathrm{C}\|\nabla(u-\mathfrak{l})\|_{L^{\mathrm{G}}(B_{1})}

so that

\sup_{B_{1}}|u-\mathfrak{l}|\leq\mathrm{C}\varepsilon^{\tau}\mathrm{G}(a)^{\frac{1}{g_{0}+1}}.

But then, using again (
3.16
), we have that in
B_{1/2}

\tilde{\mathrm{c}}\mathrm{G}(a)^{\frac{1}{g_{0}+1}}-u\leq\mathfrak{l}-u\leq\sup_{B_{1}}|u-\mathfrak{l}|\leq\mathrm{C}\varepsilon^{\tau}\mathrm{G}(a)^{\frac{1}{g_{0}+1}}

so that, for
x\in B_{1/2}

\mathrm{G}(a)^{\frac{1}{g_{0}+1}}\left(\tilde{\mathrm{c}}-\mathrm{C}\varepsilon^{\tau}\right)\leq u(x)

which means that
u(x)>0
if
\varepsilon>0
is small enough. In this case

|\{u=0\}\cap B_{1/2}|=0

and (
3.12
) is therefore proved.

With the aid of (
3.12
), (
3.11
) reads

(3.18)

\int_{B_{1/2}}\mathrm{G}(|\nabla u-\nabla v|)\,dx\leq\mathrm{C}\sigma(\mathrm{G}(a)+1)+\mathrm{C}_{0}\varepsilon^{\delta+1}\varepsilon^{\gamma}.

This readily implies, using (
2.5
) and the hypothesis, that

\displaystyle\int_{B_{1/2}}\mathrm{G}(|\nabla v-q|)\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\mathrm{C}\left(\int_{B_{1/2}}\mathrm{G}(|\nabla u-q|)\,dx+\int_{B_{1/2}}\mathrm{G}(|\nabla u-\nabla v|)\,dx\right)

\displaystyle\leq\mathrm{C}\varepsilon^{\delta+1}\mathrm{G}(a)+\mathrm{C}\sigma(\mathrm{G}(a)+1)+\mathrm{C}_{0}\varepsilon^{\delta+1}\varepsilon^{\gamma}.

Now using the fact that
\sigma\leq\mathrm{c}_{0}\varepsilon^{\delta+1}
(
\mathrm{c}_{0}>0
to be chosen later) and recalling that
\varepsilon^{\gamma}\leq\mathrm{G}(a)
and
a\in[a_{0},a_{1}]
and the we arrive at

(3.19)

\int_{B_{1/2}}\mathrm{G}(|\nabla v-q|)\,dx\leq\mathrm{C}\varepsilon^{\delta+1}\mathrm{G}(a).

The next step is to show that (
3.19
) implies

(3.20)

|\nabla v(x)-q|\,dx\leq\mathrm{C}\cdot(\varepsilon a)^{\nu},\>\forall x\in B_{1/2}

for some
\mathrm{C}>0,\nu\in(0,1)
and
\varepsilon
small enough.

Assume that (
3.20
) is false, then there exists
\hat{x}\in B_{1/2}
such that

|\nabla v(\hat{x})-q|\,dx>\mathrm{C}\cdot(\varepsilon a)^{\nu}.

By interior regularity, for any
x\in B_{1/8}(\hat{x})
we have

|\nabla v(x)-\nabla v(\hat{x})|\,dx\leq\mathrm{C}|x-\hat{x}|^{\alpha}

with a constant
\mathrm{C}>0
that, thanks to (

), depends only on
n,g_{0}
and
a_{1}
. Then,

\displaystyle|\nabla v(x)-q|

\displaystyle\geq|\nabla v(\hat{x})-q|-|\nabla v(x)-\nabla v(\hat{x})|

\displaystyle>\mathrm{C}\cdot(\varepsilon a)^{\nu}-\mathrm{C}|x-\hat{x}|^{\alpha}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\mathrm{C}\cdot(\varepsilon a)^{\nu}}{2}

as long as
x\in B_{\left(\frac{\mathrm{C}\cdot(\varepsilon a)^{\nu}}{2}\right)^{1/\alpha}}(\hat{x})
which is contained in
B_{1/2}
for
\varepsilon
small enough. Then

\displaystyle\int_{B_{1/2}}\mathrm{G}\left(|\nabla v(x)-q|\right)\>dx

\displaystyle\geq\int_{B_{\left(\frac{\mathrm{C}\cdot(\varepsilon a)^{\nu}}{2\mathrm{C}}\right)^{1/\alpha}}(\hat{x})}\mathrm{G}\left(|\nabla v(x)-q|\right)\,dx

\displaystyle\geq\mathrm{G}\left(\frac{\mathrm{C}\cdot(\varepsilon a)^{\nu}}{2\mathrm{C}}\right)\left|\frac{\mathrm{C}\cdot(\varepsilon a)^{\nu}}{2\mathrm{C}}\right|^{n/\alpha}|B_{1}|.

This, together with (
3.19
) gives

\mathrm{G}\left(\frac{\mathrm{C}\cdot(\varepsilon a)^{\nu}}{2}\right)\left(\frac{\mathrm{C}\cdot(\varepsilon a)^{\nu}}{2}\right)^{n/\alpha}|B_{1}|\leq\mathrm{C}\varepsilon^{\delta+1}\mathrm{G}(a)

or, using (

\left(\frac{\mathrm{C}\cdot(\varepsilon a)^{\nu}}{2}\right)^{g_{0}+1+\frac{n}{\alpha}}|B_{1}|\leq\varepsilon^{\delta+1}\mathrm{G}(a)

which gives a contradiction choosing
\nu
appropriately and for
\varepsilon
small enough thus proving (
3.20
).

Next we consider the vectorial function
\overrightarrow{\mathbf{F}}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
given by

\overrightarrow{\mathbf{F}}(z)\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=g(|z|)\frac{z}{|z|}

and compute

\displaystyle\overrightarrow{\mathbf{F}}(\nabla v)-\overrightarrow{\mathbf{F}}(q)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{d}{dt}\overrightarrow{\mathbf{F}}\left(q+t(\nabla v-q)\right)\>dt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}D\overrightarrow{\mathbf{F}}\left(q+t(\nabla v-q)\right)\cdot(\nabla v-q)\>dt

\displaystyle=\mathfrak{A}(x)(\nabla v-q)

with
\displaystyle\mathfrak{A}(x)\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\int_{0}^{1}D\overrightarrow{\mathbf{F}}\left(q+t(\nabla v-q)\right)\>dt
. Nonetheless, we have that

\mathop{\text{div}}\overrightarrow{\mathbf{F}}(\nabla v)=\mathop{\text{div}}\overrightarrow{\mathbf{F}}(q)=0

and hence

\mathop{\text{div}}\left(\mathfrak{A}(x)(\nabla v-q)\right)=0.

Applying interior estimates (i.e. using Proposition
2.2
with Remark
2.3
and Lemma
2.6
) and using (
3.19
) we have that

\mathrm{G}(|\nabla v(x)-q)|)\leq\sup_{B_{R/4}(x)}\mathrm{G}\left(|\nabla v-q|\right)\leq\mathrm{C}\mathchoice{\mathop{\vrule width=6.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.5pt\kern-8.0pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-6.0ptB_{R/2}(x)}}{\mathop{\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.6pt\kern-6.0pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{R/2}(x)}}{\mathop{\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.6pt\kern-6.0pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{R/2}(x)}}{\mathop{\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.6pt\kern-6.0pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{R/2}(x)}}\mathrm{G}\left(|\nabla v-q|\right)\>dx\leq\mathrm{C}\mathrm{G}(a)\varepsilon^{\delta+1}.

or, since
\mathrm{G}
is increasing,

|\nabla v(x)-q|\leq\mathrm{C}\mathrm{G}^{-1}\left(\mathrm{G}(a)\varepsilon^{\delta+1}\right).

In particular,

|\bar{q}|\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=|\nabla v(0)-q|\leq\mathrm{C}\mathrm{G}^{-1}\left(\mathrm{G}(a)\varepsilon^{\delta+1}\right)

so that

|\nabla v(x)-q-\bar{q}|\leq 2\mathrm{C}\mathrm{G}^{-1}\left(\mathrm{G}(a)\varepsilon^{\delta+1}\right),\>x\in B_{1/2}.

2.12
) in Proposition
2.2

\mathchoice{\mathop{\vrule width=6.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.5pt\kern-8.0pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-6.0ptB_{r}}}{\mathop{\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.6pt\kern-6.0pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{r}}}{\mathop{\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.6pt\kern-6.0pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{r}}}{\mathop{\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.6pt\kern-6.0pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{r}}}\mathrm{G}(|\nabla v-q-\bar{q}|)\,dx\leq\mathchoice{\mathop{\vrule width=6.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.5pt\kern-8.0pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-6.0ptB_{r}}}{\mathop{\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.6pt\kern-6.0pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{r}}}{\mathop{\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.6pt\kern-6.0pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{r}}}{\mathop{\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.6pt\kern-6.0pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{r}}}\mathrm{G}\left(\mathrm{C}\left(\frac{r}{\frac{1}{2}}\right)^{\mu}\sup_{B_{\frac{1}{2}}}|\nabla v-q-\bar{q}|\right)\leq\mathrm{C}r^{\mu(\delta+1)}\mathrm{G}(a)\varepsilon^{\delta+1}.

\displaystyle\mathchoice{\mathop{\vrule width=6.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.5pt\kern-8.0pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-6.0ptB_{r}}}{\mathop{\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.6pt\kern-6.0pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{r}}}{\mathop{\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.6pt\kern-6.0pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{r}}}{\mathop{\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.6pt\kern-6.0pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{r}}}\mathrm{G}(|\nabla u-q-\bar{q}|)\,dx

\alpha<\alpha_{0}

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathrm{C}}r^{\mu(\delta+1)}\mathrm{G}(a)\varepsilon^{\delta+1}=\tilde{\mathrm{C}}r^{(\alpha_{0}-\alpha)(\delta+1)}r^{\alpha(\delta+1)}\mathrm{G}(a)\varepsilon^{\delta+1}=\frac{1}{3}r^{\alpha(\delta+1)}\mathrm{G}(a)\varepsilon^{\delta+1}

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathrm{C}}r^{-n}\varepsilon^{\delta+1}\varepsilon^{\gamma}\leq\tilde{\mathrm{C}}r^{-n}\frac{r^{\alpha(\delta+1)+n}\mathrm{G}(a_{0})}{4\tilde{\mathrm{C}}}\varepsilon^{\delta+1}\leq\frac{1}{3}r^{\alpha(\delta+1)}\mathrm{G}(a)\varepsilon^{\delta+1}

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathrm{C}}r^{-n}\sigma(\mathrm{G}(a)+1)\leq\tilde{\mathrm{C}}r^{-n}\frac{r^{\alpha(\delta+1)+n}\mathrm{G}(a_{0})}{4\tilde{\mathrm{C}}(\mathrm{G}(a_{1})+1)}\varepsilon^{\delta+1}(\mathrm{G}(a)+1)\leq\frac{1}{3}r^{\alpha(\delta+1)}\mathrm{G}(a)\varepsilon^{\delta+1}.

\mathchoice{\mathop{\vrule width=6.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.5pt\kern-8.0pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-6.0ptB_{r}}}{\mathop{\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.6pt\kern-6.0pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{r}}}{\mathop{\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.6pt\kern-6.0pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{r}}}{\mathop{\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.6pt\kern-6.0pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{r}}}\mathrm{G}(|\nabla u-q-\bar{q}|)\,dx\leq r^{\alpha(\delta+1)}\mathrm{G}(a)\varepsilon^{\delta+1}

and hence the desired result with
\tilde{q}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=q+\bar{q}
.
∎

Appendix: a Campanato-type estimate

In this section we prove a Campanato-type result in the context of Orlicz modulars, i.e. that functions with appropriate average decay are Hölder continuous. As far as the authors’ are concerned, this result is new in the specialized literature and hence has independent interest. The proof follows Fernández Bonder’s personal notes
[
38
]
for the homogeneous setting.

Let us denote

\Omega_{x_{0},\varrho}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\Omega\cap B_{\varrho}(x_{0})\quad\text{and}\quad u_{x_{0},\varrho}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\frac{1}{|\Omega_{x_{0},\varrho}|}\int_{\Omega_{x_{0},\varrho}}u(x)\>dx=\mathchoice{\mathop{\vrule width=6.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.5pt\kern-8.0pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-6.0pt\Omega_{x_{0},\varrho}}}{\mathop{\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.6pt\kern-6.0pt\intop}\nolimits_{\Omega_{x_{0},\varrho}}}{\mathop{\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.6pt\kern-6.0pt\intop}\nolimits_{\Omega_{x_{0},\varrho}}}{\mathop{\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.6pt\kern-6.0pt\intop}\nolimits_{\Omega_{x_{0},\varrho}}}u(x)dx.

For our purposes, the analogous of the
L^{p}
norm and Campanato seminorm (see for instance
[
42
]
) will be given, for
\lambda\geq 0
, by

(4.7)

\Phi(u)\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\int_{\Omega}\mathrm{G}(|u|)\>dx,\quad\Phi_{\lambda}(u)\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}x_{0}\in\Omega\\

These quantities are not homogeneous and hence not a norm/seminorm, but they capture the essential properties that will be used to prove the desired continuity.

We will work with functions satisfying

\Phi(u)+\Phi_{\lambda}(u)\leq\mathrm{C}_{\Phi}

for some constant
\mathrm{C}_{\Phi}
. The first step is to prove that every point in
\Omega
is a Lebesgue point of

:

.

To prove the claim we note that for
z\in B_{r}(x)\cap B_{r}(y)
it holds that

\mathrm{G}(|u_{x,r}-u_{y,r}|)\leq 2^{g_{0}}\left(\mathrm{G}(|u_{x,r}-u(z)|)+\mathrm{G}(|u_{y,r}-u(z)|)\right)

and so integrating over
B_{r}(x)\cap B_{r}(y)
we get that

\displaystyle|B_{r}(x)\cap B_{r}(y)|\mathrm{G}(|u_{x,r}-u_{y,r}|)

\displaystyle\leq 2^{g_{0}}\left(\int_{B_{r}(x)}\mathrm{G}(|u_{x,r}-u(z)|)\>dz+\int_{B_{r}(y)}\mathrm{G}(|u_{y,r}-u(z)|)\>dz\right)

\displaystyle\leq 2^{g_{0}+1}r^{\lambda}\Phi_{\lambda}(u)

which leads to

|u_{x,r}-u_{y,r}|\leq\mathrm{G}^{-1}\left(\frac{1}{|B_{r}(x)\cap B_{r}(y)|}2^{g_{0}+1}r^{\lambda}\Phi_{\lambda}(u)\right).

The claim follows simply by noting that
B_{\frac{r}{2}}(x)\subset B_{r}(x)\cap B_{r}(y)
.

Since

|u(x)-u(y)|\leq|u(x)-u_{x,r}|+|u_{x,r}-u_{y,r}|+|u_{y,r}-u(y)|

the proposition follows using the claim and (
4.11
).
∎

We remark that
\Phi_{\lambda}(u)
is equivalent to

\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}x_{0}\in\Omega\\
\varrho>0\end{subarray}}\varrho^{-\lambda}\inf_{\xi\in\mathbb{R}}\int_{\Omega_{x_{0},\varrho}}\mathrm{G}(|u(x)-\xi|)\>dx;

indeed, one inequality being trivial, the other one follows by noting that Jensen’s inequality gives

\mathrm{G}(|u_{x_{0},\varrho}-\xi|)=\mathrm{G}\left(\left|\mathchoice{\mathop{\vrule width=6.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.5pt\kern-8.0pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-6.0pt\Omega_{x_{0},\varrho}}}{\mathop{\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.6pt\kern-6.0pt\intop}\nolimits_{\Omega_{x_{0},\varrho}}}{\mathop{\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.6pt\kern-6.0pt\intop}\nolimits_{\Omega_{x_{0},\varrho}}}{\mathop{\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.6pt\kern-6.0pt\intop}\nolimits_{\Omega_{x_{0},\varrho}}}(u(x)-\xi)\>dx\right|\right)\leq\mathchoice{\mathop{\vrule width=6.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.5pt\kern-8.0pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-6.0pt\Omega_{x_{0},\varrho}}}{\mathop{\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.6pt\kern-6.0pt\intop}\nolimits_{\Omega_{x_{0},\varrho}}}{\mathop{\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.6pt\kern-6.0pt\intop}\nolimits_{\Omega_{x_{0},\varrho}}}{\mathop{\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.6pt\kern-6.0pt\intop}\nolimits_{\Omega_{x_{0},\varrho}}}\mathrm{G}\left(\left|u(x)-\xi\right|\right)\>dx

from where

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{x_{0},\varrho}}\mathrm{G}\left(\left|u(x)-u_{x_{0},\varrho}\right|\right)\>dx

\displaystyle\leq 2^{g_{0}}\left(\int_{\Omega_{x_{0},\varrho}}\mathrm{G}\left(\left|u(x)-\xi\right|\right)\>dx+\int_{\Omega_{x_{0},\varrho}}\mathrm{G}\left(\left|\xi-u_{x_{0},\varrho}\right|\right)\>dx\right)

\displaystyle\leq 2^{g_{0}+1}\int_{\Omega_{x_{0},\varrho}}\mathrm{G}\left(\left|u(x)-\xi\right|\right)\>dx.

In summary, we have the following theorem:

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
u
be a measurable function satisfying

\int_{\Omega}\mathrm{G}(|u|)\>dx+\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}x_{0}\in\Omega\\
\varrho>0\end{subarray}}\varrho^{-\lambda}\inf_{\xi\in\mathbb{R}}\int_{\Omega_{x_{0},\varrho}}\mathrm{G}(|u(x)-\xi|)\>dx\leq\mathrm{C}_{0}

for some positive constant
\mathrm{C}_{0}
and
\lambda>n
. Then,
u\in C^{0,\gamma}(\Omega)
with
\gamma=\frac{\lambda-n}{g_{0}+1}
and

\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}x,y\in\Omega\\
x\neq y\end{subarray}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|}{|x-y|^{\gamma}}\leq\mathrm{C}\mathrm{G}^{-1}\left(\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}x_{0}\in\Omega\\
\varrho>0\end{subarray}}\varrho^{-\lambda}\inf_{\xi\in\mathbb{R}}\int_{\Omega_{x_{0},\varrho}}\mathrm{G}(|u(x)-\xi|)dx\right)

Remark 4.4
.

One could consider
\lambda=n+g_{0}+1
in the previous theorem and obtain
u\in C^{0,1}(\Omega)
, i.e.
u
is Lipschitz continuous in
\Omega
.

Definition 4.5

(
[
49
, Definition 1.2]
)
.

Let
p\in[1,\infty)
and a
\psi\colon(0,\infty)\to(0,\infty)
be a continuous function. We define the generalized Campanato space

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{p,\psi}(\Omega)\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\left\{f:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}:\,\,\|f\|_{\mathcal{L}_{p,\psi}(\Omega)}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\sup_{B_{r}(x_{0})\subset\Omega}\frac{1}{\psi(r)}\left(\mathchoice{\mathop{\vrule width=6.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.5pt\kern-8.0pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-6.0ptB_{r}(x_{0})}}{\mathop{\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.6pt\kern-6.0pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{r}(x_{0})}}{\mathop{\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.6pt\kern-6.0pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{r}(x_{0})}}{\mathop{\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.6pt\kern-6.0pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{r}(x_{0})}}|f(x)-f_{B_{r}(x_{0})}|^{p}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}<\infty\right\}.

Remark 4.6
.

Observe that
\|f\|_{\mathcal{L}_{p,\psi}(\Omega)}
is a norm (modulo constant functions), thus
\mathcal{L}_{p,\psi}(\Omega)
defines a Banach space. Moreover, if
p=1
and
\psi\equiv 1
, then
\mathcal{L}_{1,1}(\Omega)=\mathrm{BMO}(\Omega)
. Finally, if
p=1
and
\psi(r)=r^{\gamma}
(for some
0<\gamma\leq 1
), then
\mathcal{L}_{1,r^{\gamma}}(\Omega)
coincides with standard spaces
C^{0,\gamma}(\Omega)
.

We also consider now the following functional space

\text{BMO}^{\ast}(\Omega)\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\left\{u:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}:\,\,\,\displaystyle\|u\|_{\text{BMO}^{\ast}(\Omega)}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\sup_{x_{0}\in\Omega\atop{\rho>0}}\mathchoice{\mathop{\vrule width=6.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.5pt\kern-8.0pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-6.0pt\Omega_{x_{0},\rho}}}{\mathop{\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.6pt\kern-6.0pt\intop}\nolimits_{\Omega_{x_{0},\rho}}}{\mathop{\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.6pt\kern-6.0pt\intop}\nolimits_{\Omega_{x_{0},\rho}}}{\mathop{\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.6pt\kern-6.0pt\intop}\nolimits_{\Omega_{x_{0},\rho}}}|u-u_{x_{0},\rho}|dx<\infty\right\}.

Now, we will suppose that
\Omega
satisfies a
\hat{\mathrm{c}}_{0}-
uniform positive density (
\hat{\mathrm{c}}_{0}-
UPD for short), i.e., there exists a universal constant
\hat{\mathrm{c}}_{0}\in(0,1)
such that

\displaystyle\frac{|\Omega_{x_{0},\rho}|}{|B_{\rho}(x_{0})|}=\frac{|\Omega\cap B_{\rho}(x_{0})|}{|B_{\rho}(x_{0})|}\geq\hat{\mathrm{c}}_{0}\quad\forall\,\,x_{0}\in\overline{\Omega}\,\,\text{and}\,\,\rho>0.

Remember we are assuming that
\Omega
is a bounded Lipschitz domain, thus it fulfils
\hat{\mathrm{c}}_{0}-
UPD property for some
\hat{\mathrm{c}}_{0}\in(0,1)
.

Finally, taking into account the above definitions and using Jensen’s inequality for concave functions, we can conclude in the borderline case, i.e.,
\lambda=n
(see (
4.7
) and (
4.8
)) the following:

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\displaystyle\|u\|_{\text{BMO}^{\ast}(\Omega)}&\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=&\displaystyle\sup_{x_{0}\in\Omega\atop{\rho>0}}\mathchoice{\mathop{\vrule width=6.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.5pt\kern-8.0pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-6.0pt\Omega_{x_{0},\rho}}}{\mathop{\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.6pt\kern-6.0pt\intop}\nolimits_{\Omega_{x_{0},\rho}}}{\mathop{\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.6pt\kern-6.0pt\intop}\nolimits_{\Omega_{x_{0},\rho}}}{\mathop{\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.6pt\kern-6.0pt\intop}\nolimits_{\Omega_{x_{0},\rho}}}|u-u_{x_{0},\rho}|dx\\
&=&\displaystyle\sup_{x_{0}\in\Omega\atop{\rho>0}}\mathchoice{\mathop{\vrule width=6.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.5pt\kern-8.0pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-6.0pt\Omega_{x_{0},\rho}}}{\mathop{\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.6pt\kern-6.0pt\intop}\nolimits_{\Omega_{x_{0},\rho}}}{\mathop{\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.6pt\kern-6.0pt\intop}\nolimits_{\Omega_{x_{0},\rho}}}{\mathop{\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.6pt\kern-6.0pt\intop}\nolimits_{\Omega_{x_{0},\rho}}}\mathrm{G}^{-1}\left(\mathrm{G}(|u-u_{x_{0},\rho}|)\right)dx\\
&\leq&\displaystyle\sup_{x_{0}\in\Omega\atop{\rho>0}}\mathrm{G}^{-1}\left(\displaystyle\mathchoice{\mathop{\vrule width=6.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.5pt\kern-8.0pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-6.0pt\Omega_{x_{0},\rho}}}{\mathop{\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.6pt\kern-6.0pt\intop}\nolimits_{\Omega_{x_{0},\rho}}}{\mathop{\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.6pt\kern-6.0pt\intop}\nolimits_{\Omega_{x_{0},\rho}}}{\mathop{\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.6pt\kern-6.0pt\intop}\nolimits_{\Omega_{x_{0},\rho}}}\mathrm{G}(|u-u_{x_{0},\rho}|)dx\right)\\
&\leq&\mathrm{G}^{-1}\left(\displaystyle\sup_{x_{0}\in\Omega\atop{\rho>0}}\mathchoice{\mathop{\vrule width=6.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.5pt\kern-8.0pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-6.0pt\Omega_{x_{0},\rho}}}{\mathop{\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.6pt\kern-6.0pt\intop}\nolimits_{\Omega_{x_{0},\rho}}}{\mathop{\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.6pt\kern-6.0pt\intop}\nolimits_{\Omega_{x_{0},\rho}}}{\mathop{\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.6pt\kern-6.0pt\intop}\nolimits_{\Omega_{x_{0},\rho}}}\mathrm{G}(|u-u_{x_{0},\rho}|)dx\right)\\
&\leq&\mathrm{G}^{-1}\left(\hat{\mathrm{c}}_{0}^{-1}\omega_{n}^{-1}\displaystyle\sup_{x_{0}\in\Omega\atop{\rho>0}}\frac{1}{\rho^{n}}\int_{\Omega_{x_{0},\rho}}G(|u-u_{x_{0},\rho}|)dx\right)\\
&\leq&\mathrm{G}^{-1}\left(\hat{\mathrm{c}}_{0}^{-1}\omega_{n}^{-1}\mathrm{C}_{\Phi}\right).\end{array}

Therefore,
u\in\text{BMO}^{\ast}(\Omega)
provided
\lambda=n
and
\Omega
fulfils a
\hat{\mathrm{c}}_{0}-
UPD property.

Much efforts have been dedicated to finding the asymptotic converging

[

is called the persistence exponent. It often appears in
conjunction with Spitzer’s formula. The study of such asymptotic behavior has appealed attractions in the physics literature
as well, where the parameter

is often called the survival exponent. It also appears in reliability theory, where

is viewed
as a certain failure time whose typical upper tails are Pareto-like
[
5
]
. Other connections of the persistent exponents to
physics include regular points of inviscid Burgers equation with self-similar initial data
[
6
,
7
]
, positivity of random polynomials and diffusion equations
[

,
9
]

[

In this paper, we solve the problem that the summation of the sign of the partial sum of a zero-mean random walk is greater than
xs
for
a fixed
x\in[0,1)
and any positive integer
s
such that
s\leqslant t
. Let
S
be an one-dimeansional mean-zero random walk,
S_{n}
the partial sums and
S_{0}=0
. The sign of the partial sum

is
+1
if
S_{n}>0
, and
-1
if
S_{n}<0
. If
S_{n}=0
, then
\text{sgn}(S_{n})=\text{sgn}(S_{n-1})
for
n\geqslant 1
. In particular, we define
\text{sgn}(S_{0})=+1
. Then taking
0<x<1
, we define the following event:

\tilde{A}_{t,x}:=\{\sum\limits_{i=1}^{s}\text{sgn}(S_{i})>xs,\forall 1\leqslant s\leqslant t\}.

(1.1)

Our goal is to find the asymptotic probability of
\tilde{A}_{t,x}
. Note that if we want to know whether the summation of the sign of the partial sum of the random walk
S
is greater than
xs
for all
s\in[1,t]
, it is sufficient for us to check that at each time point
s^{\prime}
when the sign of partial sums changes from
-1
to
+1
, the summation of the sign is greater than
xs^{\prime}
. Now let’s define “crossing times”
t_{0},t_{1},...
in this way:

\begin{cases}t_{0}=0\\
t_{i}=\min\{s>t_{i-1}:\text{sgn}(S_{s})\times\text{sgn}(S_{s+1})=-1\}.\end{cases}

(1.2)

In other words, each
t_{i}
is a “crossing time” of the random walk
S
either from the positive axis to the negative axis or from the negative axis to the positive axis. Denote
t_{2i}
as the end point of the
i
th complete excursion, and
t_{2i-1}
as the end point of the
i
th half excursion. Let
\tau_{i}=t_{i}-t_{i-1}
for

be called the “inter-half-excursion time”. Then we are also interested in the event

A_{k,x}:=\{\sum\limits_{i=1}^{s}\text{sgn}(S_{i})>xs,\forall 1\leqslant s\leqslant t_{2k}\}.

(1.3)

Before we proceed further, let’s make clear some notations that we will use throughout this paper. Most of the random variables in this paper are from the class of zero-shift-stable random variable
\mathcal{C}(\alpha,\kappa,c)
, where
\alpha\in(0,2]
is the stability parameter,
\kappa\in[-1,1]
is the skewness parameter, and
c\in(0,\infty)
is the scale parameter. Let
\mathcal{Z}_{[\kappa,c]}=\mathcal{C}(1/2,\kappa,c)
, the subset of zero-shift-stable random variables with parameters
\alpha=1/2
. A distribution or a random variable is said to be stable if a linear combination of two independent copies of a random sample has the same distribution, up to location and scale parameters.

Godrèche et al
[
1
]
evaluated
\lim\limits_{k\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{P}(A_{k,x})
by approximating the inter-half-excursion time
\{\tau_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
of simple random walk with i.i.d. random variables in
\mathcal{Z}_{[1,1]}
. In particular, they give an explicit expression of the persistent power exponent:

\mathbb{P}(A_{k,x})=\frac{\Gamma(k+1-\phi(x,1))}{\Gamma(K+1)\Gamma(1-\phi(x,1))}=k^{-\phi(x,1)+o(1)},

(1.4)

where
\phi(x,1)=\mathbb{P}(Z<0)
, for
Z\in\mathcal{Z}_{[\frac{\sqrt{1-x}-\sqrt{1+x}}{\sqrt{1-x}+\sqrt{1+x}}]}
. For simple random walk,
b=1
. Note that
\mathbb{P}(Z_{i}<0)=\mathbb{P}(Z_{2}<0)
if

. In this paper, we make two generalizations of results (

). First, we do not restrict
S
to be simple random walk, but a larger set of random walk which gives inter-half-excursion time that has a property called “persistently power-skewness”, which we will define in a moment. Second, we move from the number of excursions up to a certain point to the time elapsed before a certain point and deduce that as time

, the persistent exponent should be
\phi(x,b)/2
.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
\{\xi_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
be a sequence of stationary random variables, and let

. Then
\{\xi_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
is called

if

\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\bigg|\frac{\log(\mathbb{P}(W_{1}\geqslant 0,...,W_{n}\geqslant 0))}{\log n}-\mathbb{P}(W_{n}<0)\bigg|=0.

(1.5)

Proposition 1.2
.

If
\mathbb{P}(W_{n}<0)
has a limit as
n\rightarrow\infty
, and
\xi_{i}
are i.i.d. random variables, then
\{\xi_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
are persistently power-skewed.

Remark 1.3
.

In our context, the role of
\xi_{i}
is played by
(1-x)\tau_{2i-1}-(1+x)\tau_{2i}
.

Now we are ready to state our theorem.

Theorem 1.4
.

If
S
is an one-dimensional random walk with increment mean 0, and if
\{(1-x)\tau_{2i-1}-(1+x)\tau_{2i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
is persistently power-skewed, then we have

(a)

\mathbb{P}(A_{k,x})=k^{-\phi(x,b)+o(1)}
as

, with
0<\phi(x,b)\leqslant 1
, where
\phi(x,b)=\mathbb{P}(Z<0)=\displaystyle\frac{1}{\pi}\arccos\bigg(\frac{\bar{\psi}-x}{1-\bar{\psi}x}\bigg)
, for
Z\in\mathcal{Z}_{[\kappa(x,b),1]},\kappa(x,b)=\displaystyle\frac{\sqrt{1-x}b-\sqrt{1+x}}{\sqrt{1-x}b+\sqrt{1+x}}
, and
b=b(S)
is a parameter that is a function of the random walk
S
, and
\psi=\displaystyle\frac{b^{2}-1}{b^{2}+1}
. We call

the relative asymmetry of the positive half excursion and the negative half excursion.

(b)

\mathbb{P}(\tilde{A}_{t,x})=t^{(-\phi(x,b)/2+o(1))}
as

, with
\phi(x,b)
being the same function as that in

.

Corollary 1.5
.

Simple random walk gives
\{(1-x)\tau_{2i-1}-(1+x)\tau_{2i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
that satisfies the persistently power-skewed property, because the inter-half-excursion times are i.i.d.. Thus, the conclusion of the above theorem holds. By symmetry,
b=1
, so
\kappa=\displaystyle\frac{\sqrt{1-x}-\sqrt{1+x}}{\sqrt{1-x}+\sqrt{1+x}}
.

Corollary 1.6
.

The case when we have only increment of 1 on the positive side and finitely many negative increments also gives persistently power-skewed
\{(1-x)\tau_{2i-1}-(1+x)\tau_{2i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
. Though
\tau_{i}
are not i.i.d.,
(1-x)\tau_{2i-1}-(1+x)\tau_{2i}
are i.i.d..

Corollary 1.7
.

The case when the positive increments follow a truncated geometric distribution gives persistently power-skewed
\{(1-x)\tau_{2i-1}-(1+x)\tau_{2i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
due to the memory-less property of geometric distribution.

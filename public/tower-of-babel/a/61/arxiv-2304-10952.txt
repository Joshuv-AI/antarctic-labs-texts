Simplest fidelity-estimation method for graph states with depolarizing noise
Tomonori Tanizawa, Yuki Takeuchi, Shion Yamashika, Ryosuke Yoshii, Shunji Tsuchiya

Abstract
Graph states are entangled states useful for several quantum information processing tasks such as measurement-based quantum computation and quantum metrology. As the size of graph states realized in experiments increases, it becomes more essential to devise efficient methods estimating the fidelity between the ideal graph state and an experimentally-realized actual state. Any efficient fidelity-estimation method, in general, must use multiple experimental settings, i.e., needs to switch between at least two measurements. Recently, it has been shown that a single measurement is sufficient if the noise can be modeled as the phase-flip error. Since the bit-flip error should also occur in several experiments, it is desired to extend this simplest method to noise models that include phase and bit-flip errors. However, it seems to be nontrivial because their result strongly depends on properties of the phase-flip error. In this paper, by analyzing effects of the bit-flip error on stabilizer operators of graph states, we achieve the extension to the depolarizing noise, which is a major noise model including phase and bit-flip errors. We also numerically evaluate our simplest method for noise models interpolating between the phase-flip and depolarizing noises.

II
Graph states in the depolarizing channel

In this section, we introduce graph states in the depolarizing channel.
To this end, we first define graph states
BR01
.
A graph

is a pair of the set

of
n

The
n
-qubit graph state
|G\rangle
that corresponds to the graph

is defined as

\displaystyle|G\rangle\equiv\left(\prod_{(i,j)\in E}CZ_{i,j}\right)|+\rangle^{\otimes n},

(1)

where
|+\rangle\equiv(|0\rangle+|1\rangle)/\sqrt{2}
with
|0\rangle
and
|1\rangle
being, respectively, eigenstates of the Pauli-
Z
operator with eigenvalues
+1
and
-1
, and

is the controlled-
Z
(

) gate applied on the

th and

th qubits.
The stabilizer generators

for

are defined as

\displaystyle g_{i}\equiv X_{i}\left(\prod_{j:\ (i,j)\in E}Z_{j}\right).

(2)

Here,
X_{i}
and
Z_{j}
are the Pauli-

and
Z
operators for the
i
th and
j
th qubits, respectively, and the product of

is taken over all vertices

such that

For any

[g_{i},g_{j}]=0

The graph state
|G\rangle
is the unique common eigenstate of

A stabilizer

is a product of stabilizer generators such that
S_{\ell}\equiv\prod_{i=1}^{n}g_{i}^{\ell_{i}}
, where
{\ell}\equiv\ell_{1}\ell_{2}\ldots\ell_{n}\in\{0,1\}^{n}

It is a tensor product of

are the two-dimensional identity operator and Pauli-

operator, respectively, it can be written as

S_{\ell}=(-1)^{s}\bigotimes_{i=1}^{n}\tau_{i}.

(3)

For any
\ell
, the equality
S_{\ell}|G\rangle=|G\rangle
can be easily checked from Eqs. (
1
) and (
2
).

The depolarizing channel is represented by the superoperator
NC00

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(\cdot)\equiv(1-p)I(\cdot)I+\frac{p}{3}\left[X(\cdot)X+Y(\cdot)Y+Z(\cdot)Z\right].

(4)

It operates independently on each qubit, where bit-flip (Pauli-
X
error), phase-flip (Pauli-
Z
error), and bit-phase-flip errors (Pauli-
Y

.
The depolarizing channel is a major noise model that is used in several analyses as explained in Sec.
I
.

Let
\ovalbox{$|\psi\rangle$}\equiv|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|
for any pure state

.
The density operator

\displaystyle\rho=

\displaystyle(1-p)^{n}\ovalbox{$|G\rangle$}+(1-p)^{n-1}\frac{p}{3}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{\mu=1}^{3}\ovalbox{$\sigma_{\mu i}|G\rangle$}+(1-p)^{n-2}\left(\frac{p}{3}\right)^{2}\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq\mu\leq 3\\
1\leq\nu\leq 3\end{subarray}}\ovalbox{$\sigma_{\mu i}\sigma_{\nu j}|G\rangle$}+\dots

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1-p)^{n}\ovalbox{$|G\rangle$}+\sum_{m=1}^{n}(1-p)^{n-m}\left(\frac{p}{3}\right)^{m}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i_{1}<i_{2}<\ldots<i_{m}\leq n\\
\mu_{1}\mu_{2}\ldots\mu_{m}\in\{1,2,3\}^{m}\end{subarray}}\ovalbox{$(\prod_{k=1}^{m}\sigma_{\mu_{k}i_{k}})|G\rangle$}~,

(5)

where
\sigma_{1i}\equiv X_{i}
,
\sigma_{2i}\equiv Y_{i}
, and
\sigma_{3i}\equiv Z_{i}
.

We define the fidelity between two states
\rho_{1}
and
\rho_{2}
as

\displaystyle F\equiv\left(\textrm{Tr}\sqrt{\sqrt{\rho_{1}}\rho_{2}\sqrt{\rho_{1}}}\right)^{2}.

(6)

Note that the fidelity is defined as

in Ref.

. For ease of our argument, we use the definition in Eq. (

Therefore, the fidelity between the graph state
\rho
in the depolarizing channel and the ideal state
|G\rangle\langle G|

can be written as

\displaystyle F=

\displaystyle\langle G|\rho|G\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1-p)^{n}+\sum_{m=1}^{n}(1-p)^{n-m}\left(\frac{p}{3}\right)^{m}

\displaystyle\times\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i_{1}<\ldots<i_{m}\\
\mu_{1}\ldots\mu_{m}\end{subarray}}\langle G|\left(\prod_{k=1}^{m}\sigma_{\mu_{k}i_{k}}\right)|G\rangle^{2}.

(7)

The following lemma is useful for evaluation of Eq. (
7
).

Lemma 1
.

Suppose
|G\rangle
is any
n
-qubit graph state, and
\sigma_{\mu i}
is the
\mu
component of the Pauli operator for the
i
th qubit. Then, for any natural number
m(\leq n)
,

\langle G|\left(\prod_{k=1}^{m}\sigma_{\mu_{k}i_{k}}\right)|G\rangle^{2}=1,

(8)

if and only if one of
\pm\prod_{k=1}^{m}\sigma_{\mu_{k}i_{k}}

coincides with a stabilizer of
|G\rangle
. Otherwise, it vanishes.

Although Lemma
1
can be straightforwardly obtained from basic properties of graph states (e.g., see Ref.
ACCDDA10
), we give a proof of Lemma
1
in Appendix A for the completeness of our paper.
From Lemma
1
,

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i_{1}<\ldots<i_{m}\\
\mu_{1}\ldots\mu_{m}\end{subarray}}\langle G|\left(\prod_{k=1}^{m}\sigma_{\mu_{k}i_{k}}\right)|G\rangle^{2}

(9)

is equal to the number of the stabilizers that are products of
m
Pauli operators.
Since the number of stabilizers increases exponentially with
n
, in general, it would be hard to derive the exact value of
F
for large

. However, in Sec.
IV.1
, we show that

can be represented by a simple formula (Eq. (
28
)) for fully-connected graph states.

We can assume, without loss of generality, that graph states we consider in this paper have no isolated single qubits, because verification of isolated single qubits can be performed independently of other connected qubits. Each stabilizer of a connected graph state is a product of at least two Pauli operators, so that the first-order error term (
m=1
) in Eq. (
7
) vanishes.
The second-order error term (
m=2
) also vanishes for any graph state that has no stabilizers consisting of two Pauli operators, such as a two-dimensional cluster state with

.

Using the identity

|G\rangle\langle G|=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{g_{i}+I_{i}}{2}=\frac{1}{2^{n}}\sum_{\ell}S_{\ell},

(10)

the fidelity can also be written as

\displaystyle F=\textrm{Tr}(\rho|G\rangle\langle G|)=\frac{1}{2^{n}}\sum_{\ell}\textrm{Tr}(\rho S_{\ell}).

(11)

Equation (

\{S_{\ell}\}_{\ell\in\{0,1\}^{n}}
. That is,

kinds of measurement settings are required.
Note that only a polynomial number of them are chosen uniformly at random and performed in actual experiments.
However, since chosen measurements vary in each experiment, the estimation of the fidelity requires the ability of performing any measurement in the
2^{n}
stabilizer measurements.

III
Fidelity estimation by measuring a single stabilizer

In this section, we discuss our idea used in constructing our simplest fidelity-estimation protocol for graph states in the depolarizing channel.
The average of the stabilizer is given as

\displaystyle{\rm Tr}(\rho S_{\ell})=(1-p)^{n}+\sum_{m=1}^{n}(1-p)^{n-m}\left(\frac{p}{3}\right)^{m}

\displaystyle\cdot\left[\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i_{1}<\ldots<i_{m}\\
\mu_{1}\ldots\mu_{m}\end{subarray}}\langle G|\left(\prod_{k=1}^{m}\sigma_{\mu_{k}i_{k}}\right)S_{\ell}\left(\prod_{k=1}^{m}\sigma_{\mu_{k}i_{k}}\right)|G\rangle\right].

(12)

Since the first-order error term vanishes in Eq. (
7
), the fidelity can be well approximated by the average of a stabilizer without the first-order error term when
p\ll 1
.
By comparing Eqs. (
7
) and (
12
), for
p\ll 1
, we expect that the fidelity can be accurately estimated by measuring a single stabilizer for which the first-order error term vanishes.

Let us investigate the condition on which the first-order error term in Eq. (
12
) vanishes.
Recall that
\tau_{i}
is a single-qubit operator for the
i
th qubit of
S_{\ell}
.
\tau_{i}
commutes with each of
X
,
Y
, and
Z
in the case of
\tau_{i}=I
, while it commutes with only one of them and anticommutes with the others in the case of
\tau_{i}\in\{X,Y,Z\}
. We thus obtain

\sum_{\mu=1}^{3}\langle G|\sigma_{\mu i}S_{\ell}\sigma_{\mu i}|G\rangle=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}3\quad(\tau_{i}=I)\\
-1\quad(\tau_{i}\in\{X,Y,Z\}).\end{array}\right.

(13)

Using this relation, we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{\mu=1}^{3}\langle G|\sigma_{\mu i}S_{\ell}\sigma_{\mu i}|G\rangle

\displaystyle=3n_{I}-(n-n_{I})

\displaystyle=4n_{I}-n,

(14)

where
n_{I}
denotes the number of
I
in
S_{\ell}
. Hence, the condition for the first-order error term to vanish is

n_{I}=\frac{n}{4}.

(15)

Generalizing the calculation of the first-order error term, the average of a stabilizer can be calculated in general as

\displaystyle{\rm Tr}(\rho S_{\ell})

\displaystyle=(1-p)^{n}

\displaystyle+\sum_{m=1}^{n}C(m)(1-p)^{n-m}\left(\frac{p}{3}\right)^{m},

(16)

\displaystyle C(m)

\displaystyle\equiv\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i_{1}<\ldots<i_{m}\\
\mu_{1}\ldots\mu_{m}\end{subarray}}\langle G|\left(\prod_{k=1}^{m}\sigma_{\mu_{k}i_{k}}\right)S_{\ell}\left(\prod_{k=1}^{m}\sigma_{\mu_{k}i_{k}}\right)|G\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=w(\ell,m,n)}^{f(\ell,m)}(-1)^{m-j}3^{j}\binom{n_{I}}{j}\binom{n-n_{I}}{m-j},

(17)

where
f(\ell,m)\equiv{\rm min}\{m,n_{I}\}
and
w(\ell,m,n)\equiv{\rm max}\{0,m+n_{I}-n\}
. We have assumed
\binom{0}{0}=1
.

C(m)
corresponds to the sum of the averages of
S_{\ell}
in the case of
m
errors.
The term
\binom{n_{I}}{j}\binom{n-n_{I}}{m-j}
expresses the number of cases in which

n_{I}
qubits for which
\tau_{i}=I
, while the other

errors occur on
(n-n_{I})
qubits for which
\tau_{i}\in\{X,Y,Z\}
.

From Eq. (
17
), the coefficient
C(2)
is given as

\displaystyle C(2)=8n_{I}^{2}-4(n+1)n_{I}+\frac{n(n-1)}{2}.

(18)

By comparing the second-order error terms (

) in Eqs. (
7
) and (

), it should be preferable to set

as a non-negative number for the verification with high accuracy.
Particularly in the case of cluster states,

However, in the case that the first-order error term vanishes, substituting

in Eq. (

.

From Eq. (
17
), one finds that

is equivalent to the coefficient of the term

. Then, substituting

and

in

, we finally obtain a simple analytical expression for the average of the stabilizer as follows:

\displaystyle{\rm Tr}(\rho S_{\ell})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[(1-p)+3\cdot\frac{p}{3}\right]^{n_{I}}\left[(1-p)-\frac{p}{3}\right]^{n-n_{I}}

(19)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(1-\frac{4}{3}p\right)^{n-n_{I}}.

It is clear from Eq. (

is determined solely by the number of

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(\cdot)=

generators, and arbitrary qubit unoccupied by a generator is connected to occupied qubits with an even number of edges. Another example of such stabilizers is given in Sec.
IV
for the fully-connected graph states.

Our main contribution is to derive Eq. (
20

1
can be immediately obtained.
A rigorous proof of Theorem
1
is given in Appendix C. Note that Theorem 1 holds for any graph state with
n=4k
under the condition that the graph state has no isolated single qubits, and the set
\mathcal{A}
is not empty.
Theorem

implies that the more the number

of qubits increases, the more the precision of the estimation for

by

improves.
When the second-order error term in Eq. (

, and so the estimation of
F
by

improves further. This is the case for two-dimensional (2D) cluster states, as we demonstrate it in the next section.

Based on Theorem
1
, our verification protocol runs as follows:

1.

A quantum computer generates
N
graph states
\rho^{\otimes N}
in the depolarizing channel and sends them to a verifier.

2.

The verifier measures
S_{\ell}
that satisfies the condition in Eq. (
15
) on each received state
\rho
.

3.

The verifier outputs

\displaystyle\tilde{F}_{\rm est}\equiv\cfrac{\sum_{i=1}^{N}o_{i}}{N}

(26)

as an estimated value of the fidelity, where
o_{i}\in\{+1,-1\}
denotes the

1\leq i\leq N

\tilde{F}_{\rm est}
converges to

in the limit of large

\displaystyle\left|F_{\rm est}-\tilde{F}_{\rm est}\right|\leq\epsilon

holds with probability at least

Here,

is the ceiling function.

The measurement of

in step 2 can be realized by single-qubit Pauli measurements because

is a tensor product of Pauli operators.
Furthermore, by sequentially sending qubits one by one in step 1, no quantum memory is required for the verifier.
To illustrate our protocol, we give concrete examples in Fig.

While the constraint in our protocol on the number of qubits, which must be a multiple of 4, may seem restrictive, we point out that it does not limit its practical applications.
The central goal of our protocol is to verify high fidelity for graph states.
Once high fidelity has been established for a graph state using our protocol, the number of qubits of the graph state can be changed by measurements in the

IV

In this section, we first discuss the estimation of the fidelity for the fully-connected graph states and the cluster states.
Particularly, the cluster states are important resource states for MBQC, which allows universal quantum computation.

VI
Conclusion and discussion

We have proposed a verification protocol for graph states assuming the depolarizing channel.
A remarkable feature of our verification protocol is that the fidelity of an
n
-qubit graph state can be estimated by just measuring a single stabilizer
S_{\ell}
that satisfies the condition
n_{I}=n/4
, where
n_{I}
denotes the number of identity operators in
S_{\ell}
, and so it requires only one measurement setting.
Furthermore, we have shown that the estimation improves as the number
n
of qubits increases.
We have also derived a simple analytic expression for the average of a stabilizer in the depolarizing channel.
We have applied our protocol to fully-connected graph states as well as cluster states and have demonstrated its usefulness.
Furthermore, we have evaluated our protocol for other noise models other than the depolarizing noise and have compared it with previous protocols.
Our protocol should be useful even when it is unknown whether the actual physical noise model is the phase-flip or depolarizing noise.

We note the scope of applications of our protocol in the presence of the depolarizing noise.
Our protocol, which estimates the fidelity of the graph state under the depolarizing noise, is specifically useful in the situation where we have almost no information about the actual physical noise channel for the following reason.
For a single-qubit state, the depolarizing noise introduces a probabilistic mixture of the ideal state and the maximally mixed state with probability
4p/3
(see Eq. (
4
)).
From Ref.
W11
, this noise model is valuable when knowledge about the underlying noise channel is quite limited.
In essence, the depolarizing noise is used as a representative model due to the lack of detailed information about the specific noise model.

Introducing the step of estimating the parameter
p
could indeed be an approach.
In the approach, to obtain an estimated value of the fidelity, Eq. (
7
) has to be calculated with the estimated value of
p
, which increases the required time for the estimation.
On the other hand, our protocol can directly estimate the fidelity without calculating Eq. (
7
).

As a future work, it would be interesting to extend our results to correlated noises such as the global depolarizing noise

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(\rho)=(1-p)\rho+p\cfrac{I^{\otimes n}}{2^{n}},

(46)

where
\rho
is any
n
-qubit state, and
p
is the error probability.
Since this error model is observed in actual experiments such as the quantum supremacy experiment in Ref.
google
, by doing so, we can make our results more practical.

APPENDIX B: Derivation of Eq. (

In this Appendix, we derive Eq. (
22
) and provide with an alternative derivation of Eq. (
20
).
For convenience, we express the general noise model Eq. (
21
) as

\mathcal{E}(\cdot)=\sum_{\mu=0,1,2,3}p_{\mu}\sigma_{\mu}(\cdot)\sigma_{\mu},

(50)

where
p_{1}=p_{x}
,
p_{2}=p_{y}
,
p_{3}=p_{z}
, and
p_{0}=1-p_{x}-p_{y}-p_{z}
,
\sigma_{0}=I
,
\sigma_{1}=X
,
\sigma_{2}=Y
, and
\sigma_{3}=Z
.

Using the cyclic property of trace and the anti-commuting property of the Pauli matrices, we can derive the following relation:

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tr}\left[\sigma_{\nu}\mathcal{E}(\cdot)\right]=\sum_{\mu=0,1,2,3}p_{\mu}\mathrm{Tr}\left[\sigma_{\mu}\sigma_{\nu}\sigma_{\mu}(\cdot)\right]

\displaystyle=f_{\nu}\mathrm{Tr}\left[\sigma_{\nu}(\cdot)\right],\quad(\nu=0,1,2,3).

(51)

Here,
f_{\nu}
is given as

\displaystyle f_{\nu}=p_{0}+\sum_{\mu=1,2,3}p_{\mu}(-1)^{1+\delta_{\mu,\nu}}

(52)

in the case of
\nu=1,2,3
, and
f_{0}=1
.
Using the above relation, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tr}\left[\left(\bigotimes_{i=1}^{n}\tau_{i}\right)\mathcal{E}^{\otimes n}(\cdot)\right]

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Tr}_{1}\cdots\mathrm{Tr}_{n}\left[\left(\bigotimes_{i=1}^{n}\tau_{i}\right)\mathcal{E}^{\otimes n}(\cdot)\right]

\displaystyle=f_{1}^{n_{X}}f_{2}^{n_{Y}}f_{3}^{n_{Z}}\mathrm{Tr}\left[\left(\bigotimes_{i=1}^{n}\tau_{i}\right)(\cdot)\right].

(53)

Thus, Eq. (
22
) can be obtained as

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tr}\left(S_{\ell}\rho\right)=

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tr}\left[\left((-1)^{s}\bigotimes_{i=1}^{n}\tau_{i}\right)\mathcal{E}^{\otimes n}(|G\rangle\langle G|)\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f_{1}^{n_{X}}f_{2}^{n_{Y}}f_{3}^{n_{Z}}

\displaystyle\times\mathrm{Tr}\left[\left((-1)^{s}\bigotimes_{i=1}^{n}\tau_{i}\right)(|G\rangle\langle G|)\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f_{1}^{n_{X}}f_{2}^{n_{Y}}f_{3}^{n_{Z}}\langle G|S_{\ell}|G\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(1-2p_{y}-2p_{z}\right)^{n_{X}}\left(1-2p_{z}-2p_{x}\right)^{n_{Y}}

\displaystyle\times\left(1-2p_{x}-2p_{y}\right)^{n_{Z}}.

(54)

For the depolarizing noise, setting
p_{x}=p_{y}=p_{z}=p/3
, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tr}\left(S_{\ell}\rho\right)=\left(1-\frac{4}{3}p\right)^{n-n_{I}},

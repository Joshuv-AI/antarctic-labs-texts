Affine Semigroup Algebras And Their Fibered Sums
C-Y. Jean Chan and I-Chiau Huang and Jung-Chen Liu

Abstract
. We study affine semigroup rings as algebras over subsemigroup rings. From this relative viewpoint with respect to a given subsemigroup ring, the fibered sum of two affine semigroup algebras is constructed. Such a construction is compared to the tensor product and to the classical gluings of affine semigroup rings as defined in Rosales [ 13 ] . While fibered sum can always be achieved, gluings of affine semigroup rings do not always exist. Therefore, we further investigate when the fibered sum of affine semigroup algebras gives rise to a gluing. A criterion is recovered in terms of the defining semigroups under which the gluing may take place.

1.
Introduction

The main aim of this paper is to construct fibered sums in the
category of affine semigroup rings as our perception regarding gluing of affine semigroup rings.
Our work is inspired by a question raised by Gimenez and Srinivasan
[
3
]
about when and how affine semigroup rings can be glued.
We recount the history of gluing in the literature below, following a brief introduction of affine semigroup rings.

In geometric perspective, an affine semigroup ring with the semigroup embedded in
{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
represents
regular functions on an affine variety parametrized by
d
variables.
For instance, numerical semigroup rings are associated with monomial curves.
Higher dimensional affine semigroup rings can be in some cases associated with affine toric varieties.
Due to the multiple layers of interpretations, affine semigroup rings have been of great interests to many disciplines in
mathematics.

An affine semigroup ring is usually regarded as a finitely generated algebra over a field
\kappa
.
Besides such an absolute viewpoint, an affine semigroup ring can also be regarded as an algebra over another ring
obtained from a subsemigroup.
The latter is what we call a relative viewpoint.
Works along this line among other topics have been done by the second author, Kim and
Jafari
[
8
,
9
,
10
]
in one-dimensional cases, that is,
for numerical semigroup rings.

In this paper, we implement our relative viewpoint on affine semigroup rings to the concept of gluing, which was introduced originally in absolute cases.
Gluing of numerical semigroup rings in a special case appeared in Watanabe
[
16
, Lemma 1]
following
the study of complete intersection numerical semigroup rings by Herzog
[
7
]
.
Delorme
[
1
]
treated gluing systematically for numerical semigroups and
obtained a thorough characterization for complete intersections.
Gluing was generalized to affine semigroup rings by Rosales
[
13
]
.
It was further studied in Gimenez and Srinivasan
[
3
,
4
]
.

In the approach just mentioned above, gluing is defined for arbitrary two numerical semigroup rings.
However, gluing is not a general operation in higher dimensions; it exists only between those pairs satisfying certain conditions.
More precisely, let
S_{1}
and
S_{2}
be two semigroups in
{\mathbb{N}}^{d}
for some positive
d
, and
consider their associated affine semigroup rings
\kappa[S_{1}]
and
\kappa[S_{2}]
over a field
\kappa
.
The gluing of
\kappa[S_{1}]
and
\kappa[S_{2}]
is defined if there exist two positive integers
a
and
b

such that
some specific identification holds between
\kappa[aS_{1}]
and
\kappa[bS_{2}]

(see (
5
) and (
8
) in Section
5
), where

aS_{1}
is the rescaling of
S_{1}
by the constant
a
, and similarly for
bS_{2}
.
In such a case, the gluing produces a larger affine semigroup ring
\kappa[aS_{1}+bS_{2}]
,
which is not necessarily isomorphic to
\kappa[S_{1}+S_{2}]
, even though
\kappa[aS_{1}]
is isomorphic to
\kappa[S_{1}]
and so is
\kappa[bS_{2}]
to
\kappa[S_{2}]
.
For example, the affine semigroup ring

A=\kappa[x^{3},x^{2}y,xy^{2},y^{3}]
can not be glued to itself as shown in

[
3
, Example 1]
.
On the other hand,

A
has another two isomorphic presentations

A_{1}=\kappa[x^{3}y^{3},x^{3}y^{2}z,x^{3}yz^{2},x^{3}z^{3}]
and
A_{2}=\kappa[x^{4},x^{3}y,x^{2}y^{2},xy^{3}]

(see Example
4.2
).
The latter two copies
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
can be glued
[
2
, Example 7]
.
It is crucial to observe that
A
is a subring of a polynomial ring in two variables while a polynomial ring of three variables is required to house both
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
simultaneously.
This example illustrates that the existence of gluing depends on the relative position of the two rings, or precisely, how the affine semigroup rings are embedded.

Through another set of rings in Example
5.1
and the statements following it,
we will provide detailed explanations about the contrast of the phenomenon presented by
A
versus
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
just described.
In particular, while an adjustment of rescaling by
a
and
b
is allowed for the purpose of possible gluing,
we see that if matrix multiplications are utilized instead of scalar multiplications,
then one can transform the affine semigroup rings into an appropriate ambient polynomial ring
where the gluing may take place. The resulting glued affine semigroup rings satisfy certain universal property. This leads to the fibered sum in our consideration.

In other words, the fibered sum of affine semigroup algebras constructed in this paper provides a more general perspective than gluing.
The consideration of algebras incorporates a coefficient ring which can be any affine subsemigroup ring in general.
Gluing is a special case of fibered sum where the coefficient ring is isomorphic to a polynomial ring in one variable.
We investigate also when the fibered sum of affine semigroup rings gives rise to a gluing.
An equivalent condition to the availability of gluing two affine semigroup rings with only scalar multiplications is worked out in Theorem
5.3
recovering a criterion in
[
13
, Theorem 1.4]
.

We see in Theorem
4.6
that, under certain conditions,
the fibered sum is the same as a tensor product.
Discussions in this regard can be found in Examples
4.10
and
4.11
.

Since affine semigroup rings can be viewed as
the exponential counterpart of affine semigroups,
our construction is built from the fibered sum of affine semigroups.
To make these statements precise, we begin with some definitions which will be used throughout the paper.

A
monoid
is a nonempty set with an associative binary operation and an identity element.
Since the inverse of any given element may not exist, a monoid is not necessarily a group.
The binary operator of a monoid under our consideration is always commutative.
An
affine semigroup
is a finitely generated monoid
S
that satisfies the following intrinsic properties:
for any
s,s_{1},s_{2}\in S
,

(i)

(
cancellative
) if
s+s_{1}=s+s_{2}
, then
s_{1}=s_{2}
;

(ii)

(
torsion free
) if
ns_{1}=ns_{2}
in
S
for some positive integer
n
, then
s_{1}=s_{2}
.

As seen in
[
14
, Corollary 3.4]
,
a finitely generated monoid is an affine semigroup if and only if it can be embedded in
{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}

(equivalently in
{\mathbb{Q}}^{d}
) for some positive integer
d
.
We will see in Section
2
that the cancellative and torsion free conditions are essential in many discussions.
As part of the definition, our affine semigroup always contains an identity element.
Following the modern convention, we use
{\mathbb{N}}
to denote the set of non-negative integers.

Let
\kappa
be a field. Associated to an affine semigroup
S
, an
affine semigroup ring
is a

\kappa
-algebra generated by monomials with exponents in
S
.
More precisely, by embedding
S
in
{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
, we may identify each element
s\in S
with a
d
-tuple
(s_{1},\dots,s_{d})\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}

and consider the
\kappa
-algebra

\kappa[\mathbf{u}^{S}]=\kappa[\{\mathbf{u}^{s}=u_{1}^{s_{1}}\cdots u_{d}^{s_{d}}\mid s=(s_{1},\dots,s_{d})\in S\}].

Obviously, the
\kappa
-algebra

\kappa[\mathbf{u}^{S}]
is finitely generated and since it is a subring of the Laurent polynomial ring

\kappa[u_{1}^{\pm 1},\dots,u_{d}^{\pm 1}]
, it is also an integral domain.
Henceforth, we will use
\kappa[\mathbf{u}^{S}]
to denote an affine semigroup ring instead of
\kappa[S]
.
It is essential to distinguish various embeddings of an affine semigroup ring.
The use of variables
\mathbf{u}
helps keep track of changes of embeddings.
Such necessity will become clear in Sections
4
and
5
, especially in the latter one.

The paper is organized as follows:
Section
2
first establishes fibered sums in the category of cancellative monoids.
A fibered sum of affine semigroups turns out to be a quotient of that of cancellative monoids.
Corollary
2.13
offers a glimpse of fibered sums in a special case: assuming that

S_{1}
,
S_{2}
and
S
are subsemigroups contained in an affine semigroup
T
with
S\subset S_{1}\cap S_{2}
,
then
S_{1}+S_{2}
in
T
is the fibered sum of
S_{1}
and
S_{2}
over
S
provided that
\gp(S_{1})\cap\gp(S_{2})=\gp(S)
, where

\gp(\cdot)
denotes the group generated by the argument in it.
The key fact that proves this statement is also used in proving Lemma
5.4
and Theorem
5.3
that provide a condition enabling the gluing to take place.

An affine semigroup
S
is
positive
if the only subgroup contained in
S
is the trivial group.
In particular, a numerical semigroup is positive.
In Section
3
, we extend the notion of Apéry elements from numerical semigroups to positive
affine semigroups.
The notion is relative in nature, with which we may talk about
representations of an element with respect to an affine
subsemigroup. This also leads to a criterion for flatness discussed in Section
4
.

Built from the fibered sums of affine semigroups,
Section
4
is dedicated to affine semigroup algebras and their fibered sums.
If
R^{\prime}
is a positive affine semigroup algebra over
R
, then, using Apéry elements,
Theorem
4.3
shows that
R^{\prime}
is free over
R
if and only if it is flat over
R
.
We also explore the comparison between fibered sums and the usual tensor products.
It is important to be reminded that the tensor product of two affine semigroup algebras is not necessarily an affine semigroup algebra.
Theorem
4.6
proves that under certain conditions, the fibered sum of affine semigroup algebras is isomorphic to the usual tensor product.

Section
5
offers an overall comparison between fibered sums and the gluing defined by Rosales
[
13
]
.
As mentioned previously, a fibered sum is a more general notion and can be done between arbitrary
two affine semigroup algebras with a prescribed common coefficient ring.
Our study of fibered sums is inspired by the works of Gimenez and Srinivasan
[
3
,
4
]
.
We discover that gluing, when available, satisfies certain universal property.
In fact, gluing is a special case of fibered sums when the coefficient ring is taken as a polynomial ring in one variable
(see Theorem
5.2
).
We also prove in Lemma
5.4
that whether or not a gluing
exists depends on the relationship among the groups generated by the semigroups under consideration.

Definition 2.6
.

In the category of cancellative monoids, let

and
S\rightarrow S_{2}
.
The cancellative monoid

is called the

S_{1}
and
S_{2}
over

.
Futhermore if
S
,
S_{1}
and
S_{2}
are affine semigroups, then

S_{1}\widetilde{\oplus}_{S}S_{2}
is called the
fibered sum of
S_{1}
and
S_{2}
over
S
in the category of
affine semigroups.

Properties of fibered sums depend on the underlying monoid
S
.

Example 2.7
.

It can be checked directly by the universal property that

2{\mathbb{N}}\oplus_{6{\mathbb{N}}}3{\mathbb{N}}\simeq\langle 2,3\rangle\subset{\mathbb{N}}
. Moreover,

2{\mathbb{N}}\widetilde{\oplus}_{6{\mathbb{N}}}3{\mathbb{N}}\simeq 2{\mathbb{N}}\oplus_{6{\mathbb{N}}}3{\mathbb{N}}

since
\langle 2,3\rangle
is already torsion free.
On the other hand in
2{\mathbb{N}}\oplus_{12{\mathbb{N}}}3{\mathbb{N}}
, we have

6\oplus 0\neq 0\oplus 6
while
2(6\oplus 0)=2(0\oplus 6)
, so

2{\mathbb{N}}\oplus_{12{\mathbb{N}}}3{\mathbb{N}}
is not torsion free. Hence,
2{\mathbb{N}}\widetilde{\oplus}_{12{\mathbb{N}}}3{\mathbb{N}}\not\simeq 2{\mathbb{N}}\oplus_{12{\mathbb{N}}}3{\mathbb{N}}
.

If
S\to S_{1}
and
S\to S_{2}
are embeddings of affine semigroups,
so are the canonical homomorphisms
S_{1}\to S_{1}\widetilde{\oplus}_{S}S_{2}
and

S_{2}\to S_{1}\widetilde{\oplus}_{S}S_{2}
.
It is always true that

is the sum of the images of

and
S_{2}
under the canonical homomorphisms.
In such a case,
S_{1}
and
S_{2}
can be considered as submonoids of
S_{1}\widetilde{\oplus}_{S}S_{2}
.
Furthermore,

into

via

for

agree as well, so

can be considered as a submonoid of
S_{1}\widetilde{\oplus}_{S}S_{2}
.
Through these embeddings, the intersection of

and
S_{2}
may take place
inside
S_{1}\widetilde{\oplus}_{S}S_{2}
. We may consider also the fibered sum of

and
S_{2}
over other affine semigroups such as

or any of its submonoids in
S_{1}\widetilde{\oplus}_{S}S_{2}
.

and
S_{2}
over any

S_{1}\cap S_{2}
containing

.

2{\mathbb{N}}\widetilde{\oplus}_{12{\mathbb{N}}}3{\mathbb{N}}\simeq 2{\mathbb{N}}\widetilde{\oplus}_{6{\mathbb{N}}}3{\mathbb{N}}
in Example
2.7

4.9

Proposition 2.8
.

Let
S\to S_{1}
and
S\to S_{2}
be embeddings of affine semigroups. Assume that

S_{1}
and
S_{2}
have the same rank as
S
. If
S_{1}
and
S_{2}
embed
to an affine semigroup
T
, whose compositions following the above embeddings agree on
S
, then
S_{1}+S_{2}
taken in

is isomorphic to
S_{1}\widetilde{\oplus}_{S}S_{2}

Proof.

The point is that the condition on the ranks is equivalent to

\gp(nS_{1})\subset\gp{S}
and
\gp{(nS_{2})}\subset\gp{S}
for some
n>0
.
We regard
S_{1}
,
S_{2}
and
S
as subsets of
T
. The universal property
of fibered sum gives a map
S_{1}\widetilde{\oplus}_{S}S_{2}\to S_{1}+S_{2}
with
s_{1}\widetilde{\oplus}s_{2}\mapsto s_{1}+s_{2}
, which
is clearly onto. To show that the map is one-to-one, we consider elements

s_{1}\widetilde{\oplus}s_{2}
and

in

such that
s_{1}+s_{2}=t_{1}+t_{2}
.
With the same

above,
ns_{1}-nt_{1}=nt_{2}-ns_{2}\in\gp{S}
. Then
ns_{1}\oplus ns_{2}=nt_{1}\oplus nt_{2}
and this implies
s_{1}\widetilde{\oplus}s_{2}=t_{1}\widetilde{\oplus}t_{2}
.
∎

Lemma 2.12
.

Let
S_{1}
,
S_{2}
and

be submonoids of a cancellative monoid

(i)

\gp(S_{1})\cap\gp(S_{2})=\gp(S)

(ii)

is one-to-one.

Proof.

Since the inclusion
\gp(S)\subset\gp(S_{1})\cap\gp(S_{2})
always holds, it suffices to verify

for condition (i). The latter inclusion means that
s_{1}-t_{1}=t_{2}-s_{2}
with

and
s_{2},t_{2}\in S_{2}
implies
s_{1}-t_{1}=t_{2}-s_{2}=b-a

for some

.
The last two equalities are equivalent to

in
S_{1}
and
s_{2}+b=t_{2}+a
in

, that is,

s_{1}\oplus s_{2}=t_{1}\oplus t_{2}
by definition as seen in (
2

In other words, this is the same as asking if
s_{1}+s_{2}=t_{1}+t_{2}
implies
s_{1}\oplus s_{2}=t_{1}\oplus t_{2}
.
Therefore
\gp(S_{1})\cap\gp(S_{2})\subset\gp(S)
is equivalent to that

given by

is a one-to-one homomorphism.
∎

The second condition in Lemma

implies that

are affine subsemigroups of

and

{\mathbb{Z}}
.

is a numerical semigroup in
{\mathbb{N}}
with the greatest common divisor

then

for all

\gp(S)=d{\mathbb{Z}}

Lemma 2.17
.

Let
S_{1}
and
S_{2}
be numerical semigroups in
{\mathbb{N}}
. Then

\gp(S_{1}\cap S_{2})=\gp(S_{1})\cap\gp(S_{2})
always holds.
Moreover, if
S
is a numerical semigroup contained in
S_{1}\cap S_{2}
,
then the following coditions are equivalent

(i)

\gp(S)=\gp(S_{1})\cap\gp(S_{2})
.

(ii)

\gcd S=\gcd(S_{1}\cap S_{2})
.

Proof.

For
i=1,2
, let
d_{i}=\gcd(S_{i})
, and let
d=\lcm(d_{1},d_{2})
. Then
\gp(S_{i})=d_{i}{\mathbb{Z}}
and
\gp(S_{1})\cap\gp(S_{2})=d{\mathbb{Z}}
.
Since
\gp(S_{1}\cap S_{2})\subseteq\gp(S_{1})\cap\gp(S_{2})
,
\gcd(S_{1}\cap S_{2})
is divisible by
d
. On the other hand,
nd\in S_{1}\cap S_{2}
for all
n\gg 0
since

nd_{1}\in S_{1}
and
nd_{2}\in S_{2}
for all
n\gg 0
. By choosing a large enough
n
, we have

d=(n+1)d-nd\in\gp(S_{1}\cap S_{2})
and so
d
is divisible by
\gcd(S_{1}\cap S_{2})
. Hence,

\gcd(S_{1}\cap S_{2})=d
, or equivalently,
\gp(S_{1})\cap\gp(S_{2})=\gp(S_{1}\cap S_{2})
.
The equivalent conditions follows directly from this.
∎

Corollary 2.18
.

Let
S_{1}
and

be numerical semigroups in
{\mathbb{N}}
.
Let

be a subsemigroup

. Then
\gcd S=\gcd(S_{1}\cap S_{2})

if and only if

.

Proof.

First note that, by Lemma

,
\gcd S=\gcd(S_{1}\cap S_{2})
is equivalent to
\gp(S)=\gp(S_{1})\cap\gp(S_{2})
. And by Proposition

, the latter is equivalent to
that the canonical homomorphism

is an isomorphism,
which means that

is torsion free and hence

S_{1}\oplus_{S}S_{2}=S_{1}\widetilde{\oplus}_{S}S_{2}
by the construction of

S_{1}\widetilde{\oplus}_{S}S_{2}
.
∎

3.
Apéry elements

We call an affine semigroup
positive
if
0
is the only invertible element.
Positive affine semigroups are exactly finitely generated monoids that
can be embedded into some
{\mathbb{N}}^{d}
(see
[
5
, Theorem 7.3]
).
Numerical semigroups are always positive.

In this section, we define Apéry elements extending the notion of Apéry numbers from numerical semigroups.
For an algebra defined by positive affine semigroups, we characterize if it is free or flat using Apéry elements
in Theorem
4.3
.
As seen in Example
2.15
, the fibered sum of positive affine semigroups may not be positive.
We end the section by presenting some conditions under which the positivity is preserved by taking fibered sums.

Let
S^{\prime}
be a monoid and
S
be its submonoid. An element of
S^{\prime}
is
called
Apéry
over
S
if it can not be written as
s+w
for any

w\in S^{\prime}
and
0\neq s\in S
. If
S^{\prime}
is a numerical semigroup and
0\neq n\in S^{\prime}
,
an Apéry element of
S^{\prime}
over
n{\mathbb{N}}
is called an Apéry number
over
n
in the literature. A
representation
of an element of
S^{\prime}

over
S
is an expression of the element of the form
s+w
, where

s\in S
and
w
is an Apéry element of
S^{\prime}
over
S
.
If
S^{\prime}
is positive, all of its elements have a representation. Without positivity,
Apéry elements may not exist.
We write
\Apr(S^{\prime}/S)
for the set of Apéry elements of
S^{\prime}
over
S
.

Example 3.1
.

The affine semigroup
{\mathbb{Z}}
does not have Apéry elements over
{\mathbb{N}}
.

Lemma 3.2
.

If
S\subset S^{\prime}
are positive affine semigroups and
S\simeq{\mathbb{N}}
, then
every element of
S^{\prime}
has a unique representation over
S
.

Proof.

The proof for the case of numerical semigroups
[
10
, Corollary 2.2]
carries over
to this lemma.
More precisely, let
S=\langle s\rangle
and let
w_{1},w_{2}\in\Apr(S^{\prime}/S)
. Suppose

\ell_{1}s+w_{1}=\ell_{2}s+w_{2}

in
S^{\prime}
with
\ell_{1},\ell_{2}\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Without loss of generality, we may assume

\ell_{1}\geq\ell_{2}
. Note that

(\ell_{1}-\ell_{2})s\in S
since
\ell_{1}-\ell_{2}\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Hence, we have

(\ell_{1}-\ell_{2})s+\ell_{2}s+w_{1}=\ell_{2}s+w_{2}

which implies

(\ell_{1}-\ell_{2})s+w_{1}=w_{2},

for
S^{\prime}
is a cancellative monoid.
However,
w_{2}\in\Apr(S^{\prime}/S)
, so
(\ell_{1}-\ell_{2})s=0
. This implies
w_{1}=w_{2}
and
\ell_{1}s=\ell_{2}s
.
∎

Unique representation is of special interest because we will see later that in the case of affine semigroup algebras, this is equivalent to flatness as well as to freeness.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
S\to S_{1}
and
S\to S_{2}
be embeddings of affine semigroups.
If
S_{2}
is positive and every element of
S_{2}
has a unique representation over
S
, then every
element of
S_{1}\oplus_{S}S_{2}
has a unique representation over
S_{1}
.

Proof.

Consider
s_{1}\oplus s_{2}\in S_{1}\oplus_{S}S_{2}
. If
s_{2}=a+w
for some

a\in S
and
w\in S_{2}
, then
s_{1}\oplus s_{2}=(s_{1}+a)\oplus w
.
Since
S_{2}
is positive, every element of
S_{1}\oplus_{S}S_{2}
can be
written as
s\oplus w
for some
s\in S_{1}
and
w\in\Apr(S_{2}/S)
.
We claim that
\Apr((S_{1}\oplus_{S}S_{2})/S_{1})
consists of elements in the form
of
0\oplus w
with
w\in\Apr(S_{2}/S)
.
If
t_{1}\oplus w_{1}=t_{1}^{\prime}\oplus w_{2}
with
t_{1},t_{1}^{\prime}\in S_{1}
and

w_{1},w_{2}\in\Apr(S_{2}/S)
, then
t_{1}^{\prime}+a=t_{1}+b
in
S_{1}
and
w_{2}+b=w_{1}+a
in
S_{2}
for some
a,b\in S
.
Since every element of
S_{2}
has a unique representation over
S
,
we must have
w_{1}=w_{2}
and
a=b
, which imply
t_{1}=t_{1}^{\prime}
. As a consequence,

0\oplus w\in\Apr((S_{1}\oplus_{S}S_{2})/S_{1})
if and only if
w\in\Apr(S_{2}/S)
.
Furthermore, the above argument also proves that
every element of
S_{1}\oplus_{S}S_{2}
has a unique
representation over
S_{1}
.
∎

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
S\to S_{1}
and
S\to S_{2}
be embeddings of positive cancellative monoids. Suppose
S
is numerical. If
s_{1}\oplus s_{2}=0
in
S_{1}\oplus_{S}S_{2}
, then
s_{i}=0
in
S_{i}
for each
i
.

Proof.

Since
s_{1}\oplus s_{2}=0\oplus 0
, there exist
a,b\in S
such that

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}s_{1}+a=0+b&\mbox{ in }S_{1}\\
s_{2}+b=0+a&\mbox{ in }S_{2}.\end{array}\right.

Let
\varphi:S\to{\mathbb{N}}
be an embedding and let
k=\varphi(a)
and
\ell=\varphi(b)
. Without loss of generality, we assume that
k\geq\ell
and let
m:=k-\ell
.
Note that
\ell a=kb
because
\varphi(\ell a)=\ell\varphi(a)=\ell k=k\ell=k\varphi(b)=\varphi(kb)
and
\varphi
is one-to-one. Moreover, in
S_{1}
,

ma+ks_{1}+\ell a=(m+\ell)a+ks_{1}=ka+ks_{1}=k(a+s_{1})=kb=\ell a.

Since
S_{1}
is cancellative,
ma+ks_{1}=0
. Since
S_{1}
is positive,
ma=ks_{1}=0
and so
k=0
or
s_{1}=0
.

If
k=0
, then
a=0
and so
s_{2}+b=0
. Since
S_{2}
is positive,
s_{2}=b=0
in
S_{2}
. This implies
s_{1}+a=0
in
S_{1}
. By the positivity of
S_{1}
,
s_{1}=0
.
If
s_{1}=0
, then
a=b
in
S_{1}
. Since
s_{2}+b=a
in
S_{2}
and
S_{2}
is cancellative,
s_{2}=0
.
∎

Proposition 3.5
.

For embeddings
S\to S_{1}
and
S\to S_{2}
of positive cancellative monoids,
the fibered sum
S_{1}\oplus_{S}S_{2}
is positive under either of
the following conditions.

(i)

Every element of
S_{2}
has a unique representation over
S
.

(ii)

S
is numerical.

Proof.

Suppose every
element of
S_{2}
has a unique representation over
S
.
Assume that

(s_{1}\oplus s_{2})+(t_{1}\oplus t_{2})=0
.
Then there exist
a,b\in S
such that
s_{1}+t_{1}+a=b
in
S_{1}
and
s_{2}+t_{2}+b=a
in
S_{2}
.
Since every element of
S_{2}
has a unique representation over
S
, the element

s_{2}+t_{2}
has to be in
S
. Hence, the equality
(s_{2}+t_{2})+b=a
holds in
S
and so also in
S_{1}
. Working on
S_{1}
, we have

(s_{1}+t_{1}+a)+(s_{2}+t_{2})=b+(s_{2}+t_{2})=a.

We may cancel out
a
and obtain

s_{1}+t_{1}+(s_{2}+t_{2})=0
. By positivity of
S_{1}
,
s_{1}=t_{1}=s_{2}+t_{2}=0
.
By positivity of
S_{2}
,
s_{2}=t_{2}=0
.
We conclude that
S_{1}\oplus_{S}S_{2}
is positive.

Next, assume that
S
is numerical. Suppose
s_{1}\oplus s_{2},t_{1}\oplus t_{2}\in S_{1}\oplus_{S}S_{2}
such that

(s_{1}\oplus s_{2})+(t_{1}\oplus t_{2})=0
in
S_{1}\oplus_{S}S_{2}
. By Lemma
3.4
, we have
s_{i}+t_{i}=0
in
S_{i}
for
i=1,\,2
. By the positivity of
S_{i}
, this implies that
s_{i}=t_{i}=0
. Therefore,
s_{1}\oplus s_{2}=t_{1}\oplus t_{2}=0
. This shows that
S_{1}\oplus_{S}S_{2}
is positive.
∎

The positivity of
S_{1}\oplus_{S}S_{2}
gives the positivity of
S_{1}\widetilde{\oplus}_{S}S_{2}
. More precisely,
suppose
s_{1}\widetilde{\oplus}s_{2},t_{1}\widetilde{\oplus}t_{2}\in S_{1}\widetilde{\oplus}_{S}S_{2}
such that

(s_{1}\widetilde{\oplus}s_{2})+(t_{1}\widetilde{\oplus}t_{2})=0
in
S_{1}\widetilde{\oplus}_{S}S_{2}
. Then there exists a positive integer
n
such that

n\big((s_{1}\oplus s_{2})+(t_{1}\oplus t_{2})\big)=0
and so
n(s_{1}\oplus s_{2})+n(t_{1}\oplus t_{2})=0
in
S_{1}\oplus_{S}S_{2}
. Since
S_{1}\oplus_{S}S_{2}
is positive, this implies that
n(s_{1}\oplus s_{2})=n(t_{1}\oplus t_{2})=0
, and so
s_{1}\widetilde{\oplus}s_{2}=t_{1}\widetilde{\oplus}t_{2}=0
and this shows that
S_{1}\widetilde{\oplus}_{S}S_{2}
is positive. With this observation and Proposition
3.5
, we have the following result.

Corollary 3.6
.

For embeddings
S\to S_{1}
and
S\to S_{2}
of positive affine semigroups,
the fibered sum
S_{1}\widetilde{\oplus}_{S}S_{2}
is positive under either of
the following
conditions.

(i)

Every element of
S_{2}
has a unique representation over
S
.

(ii)

S
is numerical.

4.
Algebras

For the remaining paper,
\kappa
is a field. Let
S
be a commutative monoid. The monoid ring

\kappa[\mathbf{u}^{S}]
in the symbol
\mathbf{u}
is the set of formal finite sums

\sum_{s\in S}a_{s}\mathbf{u}^{s}
,
where
a_{s}\in\kappa
, with term-wise addition and the multiplication given by

(a_{s}\mathbf{u}^{s})(a_{t}\mathbf{u}^{t})=a_{s}a_{t}\mathbf{u}^{s+t}
. If the commutative monoid is an affine
semigroup, the symbol
\mathbf{u}^{s}
can be interpreted as a monomial. By definition, an
affine semigroup
S
can be embedded into
{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
. With the embedding,
an element of
S
is realized as a
d
-tuple
(i_{1},\ldots,i_{d})
of integers. The

affine semigroup ring

R:=\kappa[\mathbf{u}^{S}]
is the
subring of the Laurent polynomial ring
\kappa[\mathbf{u},\mathbf{u}^{-1}]
in the variables
\mathbf{u}
generated by

u_{1}^{i_{1}}\cdots u_{d}^{i_{d}}
, where
(i_{1},\ldots,i_{d})\in S
. An affine semigroup
can also be embedded to
{\mathbb{Q}}^{d}
. Monomials with rational exponents naturally occur
in comparison of affine semigroup rings with various embeddings.

Example 4.1
.

The affine semigroup
\langle(2,0),(1,1),(0,2)\rangle\subset{\mathbb{N}}^{2}
also
embeds into
{\mathbb{Q}}^{2}
through
(1,0)\mapsto(1/2,0)
and
(0,1)\mapsto(1/2,1)
.
These two embeddings give rise to an isomorphism

\kappa[u_{1}^{2},u_{1}u_{2},u_{2}^{2}]\simeq\kappa[x,xy,xy^{2}]

of
\kappa
-algebras, where
u_{1}\mapsto x^{1/2}
and
u_{2}\mapsto x^{1/2}y
.
The affine semigroup also embeds into
{\mathbb{Q}}^{3}
through
(1,0)\mapsto(1,1/2,0)
and
(0,1)\mapsto(0,1/2,1)
.
These two embeddings give rise to an isomorphism

\kappa[u_{1}^{2},u_{1}u_{2},u_{2}^{2}]\simeq\kappa[x^{2}y,xyz,yz^{2}]

of
\kappa
-algebras, where
u_{1}\mapsto xy^{1/2}
and
u_{2}\mapsto y^{1/2}z
.
Another embedding into

{\mathbb{Q}}^{3}
through
(1,0)\mapsto(1/2,1,0)
and
(0,1)\mapsto(1/2,0,1)

gives rise to an isomorphism

\kappa[u_{1}^{2},u_{1}u_{2},u_{2}^{2}]\simeq\kappa[xy^{2},xyz,xz^{2}]

of
\kappa
-algebras, where
u_{1}\mapsto x^{1/2}y
and

.

The affine semigroup rings in the following example appeared in
[
2
, Example 7]
and
[
3
, Example 1]
.
In terms of rational exponents, we see that these rings are isomorphic.

Example 4.2
.

The affine semigroup
\langle(3,0),(2,1),(1,2),(0,3)\rangle\subset{\mathbb{N}}^{2}
embeds into

{\mathbb{N}}^{3}
through
(1,0)\mapsto(1,1,0)
and
(0,1)\mapsto(1,0,1)
. The embedding
gives rise to an isomorphism

\kappa[u_{1}^{3},u_{1}^{2}u_{2},u_{1}u_{2}^{2},u_{2}^{3}]\simeq\kappa[x^{3}y^{3},x^{3}y^{2}z,x^{3}yz^{2},x^{3}z^{3}]

of
\kappa
-algebras, where
u_{1}\mapsto xy
and
u_{2}\mapsto xz
.
The affine semigroup also embeds into
{\mathbb{Q}}^{2}
through
(1,0)\mapsto(4/3,0)
and
(0,1)\mapsto(1/3,1)
.
The latter embedding gives rise to an isomorphism

(5)

\kappa[u_{1}^{3},u_{1}^{2}u_{2},u_{1}u_{2}^{2},u_{2}^{3}]\simeq\kappa[x^{4},x^{3}y,x^{2}y^{2},xy^{3}]

of
\kappa
-algebras, where
u_{1}\mapsto x^{4/3}
and
u_{2}\mapsto x^{1/3}y
.

From the above perspective, an affine semigroup ring
R=\kappa[\mathbf{u}^{S}]
is not only a ring, but a ring together with
an embedding
R\to\kappa[\mathbf{u},\mathbf{u}^{-1}]
indicated by the variables
\mathbf{u}
.
We use the notation
S=\log_{\mathbf{u}}R
for the correspondence between the semigroup

S
and the commutative ring
R
. As seen in Examples
4.1
and
4.2
,
an affine semigroup ring may occur in different guises from various embeddings of the semigroup.
For an affine semigroup ring
R
in a set of variables

\mathbf{u}
and in another set of variables
\mathbf{v}
, there is an isomorphism

\log_{\mathbf{v}}\mathbf{u}\colon\log_{\mathbf{u}}R\to\log_{\mathbf{v}}R
, which can be
described by an invertible matrix. For instance,

\log_{\mathbf{v}}\mathbf{u}=\begin{pmatrix}4/3&1/3\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}

indicates the change of embeddings that gives rise to the isomorphism (
5
).
If
\log_{\mathbf{u}}R
and
\log_{\mathbf{v}}R
are subsets of
{\mathbb{Q}}
, the isomorphism

\log_{\mathbf{v}}\mathbf{u}
is simply the multiplication by the number
s\in{\mathbb{Q}}
given by

\mathbf{v}=\mathbf{u}^{s}
. If
s=\log_{\mathbf{u}}{\mathbf{v}}\in{\mathbb{N}}
, we say that the embedding of
R
in

\kappa[\mathbf{u}]
is
finer
than its embedding in
\kappa[\mathbf{v}]
.

If
S
is an affine subsemigroup of another affine semigroup
S^{\prime}
, we may consider them
as subsemigroups of some
{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
. The semigroup ring
R^{\prime}:=\kappa[\mathbf{u}^{S^{\prime}}]
is an
algebra over the coefficient ring
R:=\kappa[\mathbf{u}^{S}]
.
We use
R^{\prime}/R
to denote the
affine semigroup algebra

R^{\prime}
over
R
.
An affine semigroup ring
\kappa[\mathbf{u}^{S}]
is
positive
if
\mathbf{u}^{0}
is the only invertible monomial;
or equivalently, the affine semigroup
S
is positive.
We call an affine semigroup algebra
R^{\prime}/R
positive, if
R^{\prime}
is positive.
The coefficient ring of a positive affine semigroup algebra is also positive.

Let
R^{\prime}/R
be an affine semigroup algebra in the variables
\mathbf{u}
.
A monomial
\mathbf{u}^{w}\in R^{\prime}
is
Apéry
over
R
if
w
is Apéry over the semigroup

\log_{\mathbf{u}}R
. In other words,
\mathbf{u}^{w}
is
Apéry
over
R
if
it can not be written as
\mathbf{u}^{s}\mathbf{u}^{w_{0}}
for any
\mathbf{u}^{w_{0}}\in R^{\prime}
and

1\neq\mathbf{u}^{s}\in R
. The set of Apéry monomials of
R^{\prime}
over
R
is denoted by
\Apr(R^{\prime}/R)
.
The notion of representations in affine semigroups also has an exponential counterpart.
To be precise, a
representation
of a monomial
\mathbf{u}^{s^{\prime}}\in R^{\prime}

over
R
is an expression
\mathbf{u}^{s^{\prime}}=\mathbf{u}^{s}\mathbf{u}^{w}
, where
\mathbf{u}^{s}\in R
and
\mathbf{u}^{w}\in\Apr(R^{\prime}/R)
.
If
R^{\prime}/R
is positive, any monomial in
R^{\prime}
has a representation over
R
.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
R^{\prime}/R
be a positive affine semigroup algebra in the variables
\mathbf{u}
.
The following conditions are equivalent.

(i)

Every monomial in
R^{\prime}
has a unique representation over
R
.

(ii)

R^{\prime}
is free over
R
.

(iii)

R^{\prime}
is flat over
R
.

Proof.

First note that because
R
and
R^{\prime}
are both subrings of
\kappa[\mathbf{u}]
generated by monomials,
if
f
is in
R
(resp.
R^{\prime}
), then every monomial appearing in
f
is contained in
R
(resp.
R^{\prime}
). Hence, if every monomial in
R^{\prime}
has a unique representation, then
R^{\prime}
is a
free module over
R
with
\Apr(R^{\prime}/R)
as a basis. More precisely, suppose there exist distinct
\mathbf{u}^{w_{1}},\mathbf{u}^{w_{2}},\ldots,\mathbf{u}^{w_{n}}\in\Apr(R^{\prime}/R)
and nonzero
f_{1},f_{2},\ldots,f_{n}\in R
such that
f_{1}\mathbf{u}^{w_{1}}+f_{2}\mathbf{u}^{w_{2}}+\cdots+f_{n}\mathbf{u}^{w_{n}}=0
. Since
f_{1}
is nonzero, let
\mathbf{u}^{s_{1}}
be a monomial appearing in
f_{1}
. Then the monomial
\mathbf{u}^{s_{1}}\mathbf{u}^{w_{1}}
must appear in
f_{i}\mathbf{u}^{w_{i}}
for some
i
, and so
\mathbf{u}^{s_{i}}\mathbf{u}^{w_{i}}=\mathbf{u}^{s_{1}}\mathbf{u}^{w_{1}}
where
\mathbf{u}^{s_{i}}
is a monomial appearing in
f_{i}
. Since
\mathbf{u}^{w_{1}}
and
\mathbf{u}^{w_{i}}
are distinct,
the equality
\mathbf{u}^{s_{i}}\mathbf{u}^{w_{i}}=\mathbf{u}^{s_{1}}\mathbf{u}^{w_{1}}
contradicts the assumption that every monomial in
R^{\prime}
has a unique representation over
R
.
This proves that (i) implies (ii).

Since free modules are
flat, it remains to show that every monomial in
R^{\prime}
has a unique representation over
R
if
R^{\prime}
is flat over
R
.
Suppose there exist distinct
\mathbf{u}^{w_{1}},\mathbf{u}^{w_{2}}\in\Apr(R^{\prime}/R)
and
\mathbf{u}^{s_{1}},\mathbf{u}^{s_{2}}\in R
such that

\mathbf{u}^{s_{1}}\mathbf{u}^{w_{1}}=\mathbf{u}^{s_{2}}\mathbf{u}^{w_{2}},\quad\mbox{i.e., }\mathbf{u}^{s_{1}}\mathbf{u}^{w_{1}}-\mathbf{u}^{s_{2}}\mathbf{u}^{w_{2}}=0.

Since
R^{\prime}
is flat over
R
, by
[
11
, Theorem 7.6]
, there exist matrices

\mathcal{M}=\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0ptf_{11}&\cdots&f_{1\ell}\\
f_{21}&\cdots&f_{2\ell}\end{array}\right]\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\left[\begin{array}[]{c}g_{1}\\
\vdots\\
g_{\ell}\end{array}\right]

with
f_{ij}\in R
and
g_{i}\in R^{\prime}
such that

\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}\mathbf{u}^{s_{1}}&\mathbf{u}^{s_{2}}\end{array}\right]\mathcal{M}=0\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\mathcal{M}\left[\begin{array}[]{c}g_{1}\\
\vdots\\
g_{\ell}\end{array}\right]=\left[\begin{array}[]{c}\mathbf{u}^{w_{1}}\\
\mathbf{u}^{w_{2}}\end{array}\right].

Since
f_{11}g_{1}+\cdots+f_{1\ell}g_{\ell}=\mathbf{u}^{w_{1}}
, the monomial
\mathbf{u}^{w_{1}}
must appear in
f_{1i}g_{i}
for some
i
, and so we have
\mathbf{u}^{w_{1}}=\mathbf{u}^{t}\mathbf{u}^{t^{\prime}}
, where
\mathbf{u}^{t}
and
\mathbf{u}^{t^{\prime}}
are monomials appearing in
f_{1i}
and
g_{i}
, respectively.
That
f_{1i}\in R
and
g_{i}\in R^{\prime}
implies
\mathbf{u}^{t}\in R
and
\mathbf{u}^{t^{\prime}}\in R^{\prime}
. Since
\mathbf{u}^{w_{1}}\in\Apr(R^{\prime}/R)
,
\mathbf{u}^{t}=1
. Moreover, since
\mathbf{u}^{s_{1}}f_{1i}+\mathbf{u}^{s_{2}}f_{2i}=0
and
\mathbf{u}^{t}
is a monomial appearing in
f_{1i}
, the monomial
\mathbf{u}^{s_{1}}\mathbf{u}^{t}=\mathbf{u}^{s_{1}}
appears in
\mathbf{u}^{s_{1}}f_{1i}
and so
\mathbf{u}^{s_{1}}
appears in
\mathbf{u}^{s_{2}}f_{2i}
.
This implies
\mathbf{u}^{s_{1}}=\mathbf{u}^{s_{2}}\mathbf{u}^{r}
for some monomial
\mathbf{u}^{r}
appearing in
f_{2i}
.
Notice that
\mathbf{u}^{r}
must be in
R
because
f_{2i}\in R
. Therefore

\mathbf{u}^{s_{1}}\mathbf{u}^{w_{1}}=\mathbf{u}^{s_{2}}\mathbf{u}^{w_{2}}\implies\mathbf{u}^{s_{2}}\mathbf{u}^{r}\mathbf{u}^{w_{1}}=\mathbf{u}^{s_{2}}\mathbf{u}^{w_{2}}\implies\mathbf{u}^{r}\mathbf{u}^{w_{1}}=\mathbf{u}^{w_{2}}.

Since
\mathbf{u}^{w_{2}}\in\Apr(R^{\prime}/R)
and
\mathbf{u}^{r}\in R
,
\mathbf{u}^{r}=1
and so
\mathbf{u}^{w_{1}}=\mathbf{u}^{w_{2}}
,
which contradicts the assumption that
\mathbf{u}^{w_{1}}
and
\mathbf{u}^{w_{2}}
are distinct.
∎

The following corollary is an immediate consequence of Lemma

and Theorem
4.3
.

Corollary 4.4
.

Let
\kappa[\mathbf{u}^{S^{\prime}}]
be a positive affine semigroup ring and
s\in{S^{\prime}}
. Then
\kappa[\mathbf{u}^{S^{\prime}}]
is flat
over
\kappa[\mathbf{u}^{s}]
.

Consider affine semigroup algebras
R_{1}/R
in variables
\mathbf{u}
and
R_{2}/R

in variables
\mathbf{v}
. Denote
S_{1}=\log_{\mathbf{u}}R_{1}
,
S_{2}=\log_{\mathbf{v}}R_{2}
and

S=\log_{\mathbf{u}}R\simeq\log_{\mathbf{v}}R
. In previous sections, we construct two cancellative monoids
S_{1}\oplus_{S}S_{2}
and
S_{1}\widetilde{\oplus}_{S}S_{2}
. The former is finitely generated but not necessarily torsion free.
The latter is the quotient of the former by the equivalence relation
\sim_{\tor}
and thus is an affine semigroup.
In particular, using the fact that
S_{1}\widetilde{\oplus}_{S}S_{2}
is a fibered sum of
S_{1}
and
S_{2}
over
S
in the category of affine semigroups, it can be checked directly that

over
R
satisfies the universal property,
and thus is a fibered sum of
R_{1}/R
and
R_{2}/R
in the category of affine semigroup algebras.
We call the ring
\kappa[\mathbf{w}^{S_{1}\widetilde{\oplus}_{S}S_{2}}]
a fibered sum of the affine semigroup rings
R_{1}
and
R_{2}
over
R
.
In order to understand
\kappa[\mathbf{w}^{S_{1}\oplus_{S}S_{2}}]
and
\kappa[\mathbf{w}^{S_{1}\widetilde{\oplus}_{S}S_{2}}]
better,
it is desirable to compare them with other rings that naturally arise from
R_{1}
and
R_{2}
,
such as their tensor product over
R
.
The following lemma proves that if one of the algebras is positive and flat, then
\kappa[\mathbf{w}^{S_{1}\oplus_{S}S_{2}}]
is isomorphic to
R_{1}\otimes_{R}R_{2}
.

Lemma 4.5
.

Consider affine semigroup algebras
R_{1}/R
in variables
\mathbf{u}
and
R_{2}/R

in variables
\mathbf{v}
. Denote
S_{1}=\log_{\mathbf{u}}R_{1}
and
S_{2}=\log_{\mathbf{v}}R_{2}
. Let
S
be a semigroup isomorphic to both

\log_{\mathbf{u}}R
and
\log_{\mathbf{v}}R
. If

is positive and flat over

5.
Fibered Sum versus Gluing

Let
T_{1}
and
T_{2}
be numerical semigroups in
{\mathbb{N}}
, both with the greatest
common divisor
1
. Let
a\in T_{1}
and
b\in T_{2}
be relatively prime numbers
such that
a
(resp.
b
) is not a minimal generator of
T_{1}
(resp.
T_{2}
).
Under these conditions on
a
and
b
, the
numerical semigroup
bT_{1}+aT_{2}

is called a
gluing
of numerical semigroups (c.f.
[
15
, p. 130]
).

The gluing is in fact a fibered sum with respect to a third numerical semigroup to be unveiled here:
we look at this fact from the perspective of considering them as exponents in the semigroup rings.
Namely, we consider
R_{1}:=\kappa[u_{1}^{T_{1}}]
and
R_{2}:=\kappa[u_{2}^{T_{2}}]

as subrings of the polynomial rings
\kappa[u_{1}]
and
\kappa[u_{2}]
in variables
u_{1}
and
u_{2}
, respectively.
The aim of gluing is to equate the monomials
u_{1}^{a}
and
u_{2}^{b}
.
In fact, if we identify
u_{1}^{1/b}
and
u_{2}^{1/a}
and call it
u
,
then the new variable
u
works for this purpose.
Furthermore,
R_{1}=\kappa[u^{bT_{1}}]
and
R_{2}=\kappa[u^{aT_{2}}]
as subsets of
\kappa[u^{bT_{1}+aT_{2}}]

have an intersection
R:=\kappa[u^{ab}]
. In the way of

u_{1}^{a}=(u_{1}^{1/b})^{ba}=u^{ba}=u^{ab}=(u_{2}^{1/a})^{ab}=u_{2}^{b},

u_{1}^{a}
and
u_{2}^{b}
are “glued”.
Precisely, the process of gluing identifies the subring
\kappa[u_{1}^{a}]
of

\kappa[u_{1}^{T_{1}}]
and
\kappa[u_{2}^{b}]
of
\kappa[u_{2}^{T_{2}}]
.
If we change their embeddings all into
\kappa[u]
, then both
u_{1}^{a}
and
u_{2}^{b}
are mapped to
u^{ab}
.
Moreover,
R_{1}
and
R_{2}
now has a subring
\kappa[u^{ab}]
in common
over which the resulting gluing

\kappa[u^{bT_{1}+aT_{2}}]
appears as a universal object.
Returning to the semigroup perspective,
by Corollary
2.9
,
aT_{1}+bT_{2}
is exactly the fibered sum of
aT_{1}
and
bT_{2}
over

ab{\mathbb{N}}
as defined in Definition
2.6
.
We remark that, as far as fibered sums are concerned,
the condition stated in the above excluding
a
and
b
being minimal generators for gluing is not necessary.
The relatively prime condition on
a
and
b
assures that the gluing has greatest common divisor
1
.
This condition is neither essential.

Our interpretation of gluing imposes vital usage of variables
\mathbf{u}
that accommodate the
algebras
R_{1}/R
and
R_{2}/R
as well as the ring
R_{1}\widetilde{\otimes}_{R}R_{2}
.
In the classical treatment of affine semigroup rings, the role of variables is not brought
out into open. For an affine semigroup
S
, the associated affine semigroup ring is often
denoted by
\kappa[S]
, without referencing variables.
However, coming to actual operations of elements in the ring,
recognition of variables becomes essential in understanding the underline ring structures.

Gluing.

The gluing of numerical semigroup rings is generalized
to higher dimension by Rosales
[
13
]
.
Let
S
be an affine semigroup.
We consider the affine semigroup ring
\kappa[\mathbf{v}^{S}]
in multivariable
\mathbf{v}
.
For
a\in{\mathbb{N}}
and new multivariable
\mathbf{u}=\mathbf{v}^{1/a}

\kappa[\mathbf{u}^{aS}]=\kappa[\mathbf{v}^{S}]
has an embedding in
\kappa[\mathbf{u}]

finer
than its

\kappa[\mathbf{v}]
,
similarly to the numerical cases described after Example

Let

Rosales

defines that

and

can be glued
if
there exist

such that the
\kappa

\displaystyle\varphi_{1}

\displaystyle:

\displaystyle\kappa[X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}]\to\kappa[\mathbf{u}^{aS_{1}}],

\displaystyle\varphi_{2}

\displaystyle:

\displaystyle\kappa[Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{m}]\to\kappa[\mathbf{u}^{bS_{2}}],

Consider

On the other hand, one observes that

We propose a remedy to resolve such situation in the following.

In Example

and

are indeed the semigroups in

Instead of a scalar multiple as defined in the gluing, we suggest a matrix transformation on the generating set.
For instance,
let

A=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
1/2&1/2\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}\text{ and }B=\begin{pmatrix}1/2&1/2\\
1&0\\

Then

and

.
Let

for

We summarize the above comparison of the attempt of gluing
R^{\prime}
and itself to gluing

and
R_{2}
.
The semigroup
S^{\prime}
is of rank

.

By linear transformations, the semigroup

is transferred into two semigroups

and

in a free group of higher rank. In this way, not only

and
S_{2}
are degenerate, but they are also separate in a way that

Summary.

We conclude this paper by reiterating our “relative” point of view.
The idea of gluing is to construct a new affine semigroup ring from two given ones by identifying selected subrings.
We have seen two approaches in this section: from set-theoretic viewpoint or from categorical viewpoint.

The set-theoretic approach is the classical method initiated in Rosales
[
13
]
,
in which the part to be identified is a one-dimensional polynomial ring.
To assure that no extra identification occurs outside the polynomial ring,
this classical approach takes into account the defining relations of the given affine semigroup rings and those of the new one.
Precisely, the new affine semigroup ring, if constructible, is restricted to acquire only one extra relation among the generators
in addition to the existing relations in the given affine semigroup rings.

From another standpoint, our approach of taking fibered sum is functorial, where an affine semigroup ring is regarded as an object equipped with morphisms in the category of affine semigroup rings.
An affine semigroup ring containing the two given ones with identified parts fits in a commutative diagram. The desired construction is a diagram, smallest in the sense that all such diagrams emerge from it.
(This is as illustrated in the proof of Theorem
5.2
.)
So what we seek is a universal object among these diagrams, or in other words, a fibered sum in the category of affine semigroup rings.

Consideration of fibered sums yields the generality that the parts to be identified can be an arbitrary affine semigroup ring. This potentially widens the range of future studies in the field.
For example, in the numerical cases, singularities are stable under gluing and this fact can be explained by base changes
[
10
]
.
The fibered sum introduced in this paper, thus, calls for the investigation of singular properties of affine semigroup algebras under base changes, among many prospective directions.

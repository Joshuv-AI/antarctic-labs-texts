∗ * -Lie-type maps on C ∗ C^{*} -algebras


Abstract
Let 𝔄 {\mathfrak{A}}\, and 𝔄 ′ {\mathfrak{A}}\,^{\prime} be two C ∗ C^{*} -algebras with identities I 𝔄 I_{{\mathfrak{A}}\,} and I 𝔄 ′ I_{{\mathfrak{A}}\,^{\prime}} , respectively, and P 1 P_{1} and P 2 = I 𝔄 − P 1 P_{2}=I_{{\mathfrak{A}}\,}-P_{1} nontrivial symmetric projections in 𝔄 {\mathfrak{A}}\, . In this paper we study the characterization of multiplicative ∗ * -Lie-type maps. In particular, if ℳ \mathcal{M} is a factor von Neumann algebra then every complex scalar multiplication bijective unital multiplicative ∗ * -Lie-type map is ∗ * -isomorphism.

Introduction and Preliminaries

The study of additivity of maps strongly attracts attention of mathematicians, so that the first quite surprising result is due to Martindale who imposed conditions on a ring such that multiplicative bijective maps are all additive
[
17
]
. Thenceforth, diverse works have been published considering different types of associative and non-associative algebras. Among them we can mention
[

,
11
,
12
,
8
,
13
,
10
,
14
,
3

. In order to further develop the study of additivity of maps, the researches incorporated two new product into the theory, presented by Bre
\check{s}
ar and Fo
\check{s}
ner in
[
2
,
15
]
, where the definition is as follows: for
X,Y\in{\mathfrak{R}}\,
, where
{\mathfrak{R}}\,
is a
*-
ring, we denote by

and
[X,Y]_{*}=XY-YX^{*}
the
*
-Jordan product and the
*
-Lie product, respectively. In
[
4
]
, the authors proved that a map
\Phi
between two factor von Newmann algebras is a
*
-ring isomorphism if and only if
\Phi([X,Y]_{*})=[\Phi(X),\Phi(Y)]_{*}
. In
[
7
]
, Ferreira and Costa extended these new products and defined two other types of applications, named multiplicative
*
-Lie n-map and multiplicative
*
-Jordan n-map
and proved that, under certain conditions, an application between

-algebras that is multiplicative
*
-Lie n-map and multiplicative

*
-ring isomorphism.
In the second paper of this series
[

, Ferreira and Costa prove when a multiplicative

-Jordan n-map is a

they provide an application on von Neumann algebras, factor von Neumann algebras and prime algebras. Furthermore, they generalize the Main Theorem in
[

.
With this picture in mind, in this article we will discuss when a multiplicative
*
-Lie n-map is a
*
-isomorphism and, just as it was done in
[

]

we provide an application on von Neumann algebras, factor von Neumann algebras and prime algebras.

Let us define the following sequence of polynomials, as presented in

p_{1_{*}}(x)=x\,\,\text{and}\,\,p_{n_{*}}(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n})=\left[p_{(n-1)_{*}}(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n-1}),x_{n}\right]_{*},

Note that

By a

Main theorem

In the following we shall prove a part of the main result of this paper.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
{\mathfrak{A}}\,
and
{\mathfrak{A}}\,^{\prime}
be two
C^{*}
-algebras with identities
I_{{\mathfrak{A}}\,}
and
I_{{\mathfrak{A}}\,^{\prime}}

P_{2}=I_{{\mathfrak{A}}\,}-P_{1}
nontrivial symmetric projections in
{\mathfrak{A}}\,

{\mathfrak{A}}\,
satisfies

\displaystyle\left(\spadesuit\right)\ \ \ \ \ \ P_{j}{\mathfrak{A}}\,X=\left\{0\right\}\ \ \ \mbox{implies}\ \ \ X=0.

Even more, suppose that
\varphi:{\mathfrak{A}}\,\rightarrow{\mathfrak{A}}\,^{\prime}
is a bijective unital map which satisfies

\displaystyle\left(\bullet\right)\varphi(p_{n_{*}}(A,B,\Xi,...,\Xi))=p_{n_{*}}(\varphi(A),\varphi(B),\varphi(\Xi),...,\varphi(\Xi)),

for all
A,B\in{\mathfrak{A}}\,
and
\Xi\in\left\{P_{1},P_{2},I_{{\mathfrak{A}}\,}\right\}
. Then
\varphi
is
*
-additive.

The following claims and lemmas have the same hypotheses as the Theorem
2.1
and
we need them to prove the
*
-additivity of
\varphi

Claim 2.1
.

*({\mathfrak{A}}\,_{jk})\subset{\mathfrak{A}}\,_{kj}
, for

.

Proof.

It follows from the symmetry of
P_{1}
and
P_{2}
.
∎

Claim 2.2
.

Let
X,Y
and

be in
{\mathfrak{A}}\,
such that
\varphi(H)=\varphi(X)+\varphi(Y)
. Then,
given

,

\varphi(p_{n_{*}}(H,Z,\Xi,...,\Xi))=\varphi(p_{n_{*}}(X,Z,\Xi,...,\Xi))+\varphi(p_{n_{*}}(Y,Z,\Xi,...,\Xi))

and

\varphi(p_{n_{*}}(Z,H,\Xi,...,\Xi))=\varphi(p_{n_{*}}(Z,X,\Xi,...,\Xi))+\varphi(p_{n_{*}}(Z,Y,\Xi,...\Xi))

for
\Xi\in\left\{P_{1},P_{2},I_{{\mathfrak{A}}\,}\right\}
.

Proof.

Using the definition of
\varphi
and multilinearity of

we obtain

\displaystyle\varphi(p_{n_{*}}(H,Z,\Xi,...,\Xi))

\displaystyle=p_{n_{*}}(\varphi(H),\varphi(Z),\varphi(\Xi),...,\varphi(\Xi))

\displaystyle=p_{n_{*}}(\varphi(X)+\varphi(Y),\varphi(Z),\varphi(\Xi),...,\varphi(\Xi))

\displaystyle=p_{n_{*}}(\varphi(X),\varphi(Z),\varphi(\Xi),...,\varphi(\Xi))

\displaystyle+p_{n_{*}}(\varphi(Y),\varphi(Z),\varphi(\Xi),...,\varphi(\Xi))

\displaystyle=\varphi(p_{n_{*}}(X,Z,\Xi,...,\Xi))

\displaystyle+\varphi(p_{n_{*}}(X,Z,\Xi,...,\Xi)).

In a similar way we have

\varphi(p_{n_{*}}(Z,H,\Xi,...,\Xi))=\varphi(p_{n_{*}}(Z,X,\Xi,...,\Xi))+\varphi(p_{n_{*}}(Z,Y,\Xi,...,\Xi)).

∎

Claim 2.3
.

\varphi(0)=0

Proof.

Since
\varphi

Lemma 2.1

Then, by injectivity of
\varphi
,
-H_{21}+H_{21}^{*}=0
. Thus
H_{21}=0
. Moreover,

\varphi(p_{n_{*}}(P_{2},H,P_{2},...,P_{2}))=\varphi(p_{n_{*}}(P_{2},A_{11},P_{2},...,P_{2}))+\varphi(p_{n_{*}}(P_{2},B_{22},P_{2},...,P_{2})),

\varphi(-H_{12}+H_{12}^{*})=0.

Again, by injectivity of
\varphi
we conclude that
H_{12}=0

Furthermore, given

\displaystyle\varphi(p_{n_{*}}(D_{21},H,P_{1},...,P_{1}))

\displaystyle=\varphi(p_{n_{*}}(D_{21},A_{11},P_{1},...,P_{1}))

\displaystyle+\varphi(p_{n_{*}}(D_{21},B_{22},P_{1},...,P_{1})),

\displaystyle=\varphi(D_{21}A_{11}+D_{21}B_{11})

\displaystyle+\varphi(-(D_{21}A_{11})^{*}-(D_{21}B_{11})^{*})

\displaystyle=\varphi(D_{21}(A_{11}+B_{11})-(A_{11}^{*}+B_{11}^{*})D_{21}^{*}),

that is,
D_{21}H_{11}-(D_{21}H_{11})^{*}=D_{21}(A_{11}+B_{11})-(A_{11}^{*}+B_{11}^{*})D_{21}^{*}
, by injectivity of
\varphi
. Thus,

D_{21}(H_{11}-(A_{11}+B_{11}))=0
, which implies that
H_{11}=A_{11}+B_{11}
by
\left(\spadesuit\right)
.
∎

Now we are able to show that
\varphi
is
*
-additive. Using Lemmas
2.3
,
2.4
and
2.5
we have, for all
A,B\in{\mathfrak{A}}\,
,

\displaystyle\varphi(A+B)

\displaystyle=\varphi(A_{11}+A_{12}+A_{21}+A_{22}+B_{11}+B_{12}+B_{21}+B_{22})

\displaystyle=\varphi(A_{11}+B_{11})+\varphi(A_{12}+B_{12})+\varphi(A_{21}+B_{21})+\varphi(A_{22}+B_{22})

\displaystyle=\varphi(A_{11})+\varphi(B_{11})+\varphi(A_{12})+\varphi(B_{12})

\displaystyle+\varphi(A_{21})+\varphi(B_{21})+\varphi(A_{22})+\varphi(B_{22})

\displaystyle=\varphi(A_{11}+A_{12}+A_{21}+A_{22})

\displaystyle+\varphi(B_{11}+B_{12}+B_{21}+B_{22})=\varphi(A)+\varphi(B).

Besides, given
A\in{\mathfrak{A}}\,
, by additivity of
\varphi

\displaystyle 2^{n-2}(\varphi(A)-\varphi(A)^{*})

\displaystyle=p_{n_{*}}(\varphi(A),I_{{\mathfrak{A}}\,^{\prime}},...,I_{{\mathfrak{A}}\,^{\prime}})=\varphi(p_{n_{*}}(A,I_{{\mathfrak{A}}\,},...,I_{{\mathfrak{A}}\,}))

\displaystyle=\varphi(2^{n-2}(A-A^{*}))=2^{n-2}\varphi(A-A^{*})=2^{n-2}(\varphi(A)-\varphi(A^{*}))

and then we conclude that
\varphi(A^{*})=\varphi(A)^{*}
. This completes the proof of Theorem
2.1
.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
{\mathfrak{A}}\,
and
{\mathfrak{A}}\,^{\prime}
be two
C^{*}
-algebras with identities
I_{{\mathfrak{A}}\,}
and
I_{{\mathfrak{A}}\,^{\prime}}
, respectively, and
P_{1}
and
P_{2}=I_{{\mathfrak{A}}\,}-P_{1}
nontrivial symmetric projections in
{\mathfrak{A}}\,
.
Suppose that
{\mathfrak{A}}\,
satisfies

\displaystyle\left(\spadesuit\right)\ \ \ \ \ \ P_{j}{\mathfrak{A}}\,X=\left\{0\right\}\ \ \ \mbox{implies}\ \ \ X=0.

Even more, suppose that
\varphi:{\mathfrak{A}}\,\rightarrow{\mathfrak{A}}\,^{\prime}
is a complex scalar multiplication bijective unital map which satisfies

\displaystyle\left(\clubsuit\right)\ \ \ \ \ \ \varphi(P_{j}){\mathfrak{A}}\,^{\prime}Y=\left\{0\right\}\ \ \ \mbox{implies}\ \ \ Y=0

\displaystyle and

\displaystyle\left(\bullet\right)\varphi(p_{n_{*}}(A,B,\Xi,...,\Xi))=p_{n_{*}}(\varphi(A),\varphi(B),\varphi(\Xi),...,\varphi(\Xi)),

for all
A,B\in{\mathfrak{A}}\,
and
\Xi\in\left\{P_{1},P_{2},I_{{\mathfrak{A}}\,}\right\}
. Then
\varphi
is
*
-isomorphism.

With this hypothesis, we have already proved that
\varphi
is
*
-additive. It remains for us to show that
\varphi
is multiplicative.
In order to do that we will prove some more lemmas. Firstly, we observe that, for any
A\in{\mathfrak{A}}\,
,

Remark 2.1
.

Denoting by
\mathcal{R}(A)
and
\mathcal{I}(A)
the real part and imaginary part of
A\in{\mathfrak{A}}\,
, respectively, we have

\displaystyle\varphi(2^{n-1}\mathcal{R}(A))

\displaystyle=\varphi(2^{n-2}(A+A^{*}))=\varphi(p_{n_{*}}(iA,-iI_{{\mathfrak{A}}\,},I_{{\mathfrak{A}}\,},...,I_{{\mathfrak{A}}\,}))

\displaystyle=p_{n_{*}}(i\varphi(A),-i\varphi(I_{{\mathfrak{A}}\,}),I_{{\mathfrak{A}}\,^{\prime}},...,I_{{\mathfrak{A}}\,^{\prime}})=2^{n-2}(\varphi(A)+\varphi(A)^{*})

\displaystyle=2^{n-1}\mathcal{R}(\varphi(A))

and

\displaystyle\varphi(2^{n-1}i\,\mathcal{I}(A))

\displaystyle=\varphi(2^{n-2}(A-A^{*}))=\varphi(p_{n_{*}}(A,I_{{\mathfrak{A}}\,},...,I_{{\mathfrak{A}}\,}))

\displaystyle=p_{n_{*}}(\varphi(A),I_{{\mathfrak{A}}\,^{\prime}},...,I_{{\mathfrak{A}}\,^{\prime}})=2^{n-2}(\varphi(A)-\varphi(A)^{*})

\displaystyle=2^{n-1}i\,\mathcal{I}(\varphi(A)).

Even more,

Claim 2.4
.

Q_{j}=\varphi(P_{j})
is a symmetric projection in
{\mathfrak{A}}\,^{\prime}
, with
j\in\{1,2\}
.

Proof.

Since
\varphi
is a complex scalar multiplication, it follows that

\displaystyle 2^{n-1}i\,Q_{j}

\displaystyle=2^{n-1}i\varphi(P_{j})=\varphi(2^{n-1}iP_{j})=\varphi(p_{n_{*}}(iP_{j},P_{j},I_{{\mathfrak{A}}\,},...,I_{{\mathfrak{A}}\,}))

\displaystyle=p_{n_{*}}(i\varphi(P_{j}),\varphi(P_{j}),I_{{\mathfrak{A}}\,^{\prime}},...,I_{{\mathfrak{A}}\,^{\prime}}))=2^{n-1}i\varphi(P_{j})^{2}=2^{n-1}i\,{Q_{j}}^{2}.

Then we can conclude that
Q_{j}={Q_{j}}^{2}
. Moreover, since
P_{j}
is a symmetric projection in
{\mathfrak{A}}\,
we have that
p_{n_{*}}(P_{j},I_{{\mathfrak{A}}\,},...,I_{{\mathfrak{A}}\,})=0
. Besides,

0=\varphi(0)=\varphi(p_{n_{*}}(P_{j},I_{{\mathfrak{A}}\,},...,I_{{\mathfrak{A}}\,}))=p_{n_{*}}(Q_{j},I_{{\mathfrak{A}}\,^{\prime}},...,I_{{\mathfrak{A}}\,^{\prime}}).

Thus,
Q_{j}-{Q_{j}}^{*}=0
, that is,
Q_{j}={Q_{j}}^{*}
.
∎

Lemma 2.6
.

For all
A\in{\mathfrak{A}}\,
,
\varphi(P_{j}A)=\varphi(P_{j})\varphi(A)
and
\varphi(AP_{j})=\varphi(A)\varphi(P_{j})
.

Proof.

Firstly, observe that

p_{n_{*}}(iA,P_{j},I_{{\mathfrak{A}}\,},...,I_{{\mathfrak{A}}\,})=2^{n-2}i(AP_{j}+P_{j}A^{*})

and

p_{n_{*}}(A,P_{j},I_{{\mathfrak{A}}\,},...,I_{{\mathfrak{A}}\,})=2^{n-2}(AP_{j}-P_{j}A^{*}).

Still, by
(\bullet)
and
*
-additivity of
\varphi
,

\displaystyle\varphi(2^{n-2}i(AP_{j}+P_{j}A^{*}))

\displaystyle=\varphi(p_{n_{*}}(iA,P_{j},I_{{\mathfrak{A}}\,},...,I_{{\mathfrak{A}}\,}))

\displaystyle=p_{n_{*}}(\varphi(iA),\varphi(P_{j}),I_{{\mathfrak{A}}\,^{\prime}},...,I_{{\mathfrak{A}}\,^{\prime}})

\displaystyle=2^{n-2}i(\varphi(A)\varphi(P_{j})+\varphi(P_{j})\varphi(A)^{*})

and

\displaystyle\varphi(2^{n-2}(AP_{j}-P_{j}A^{*}))

\displaystyle=\varphi(p_{n_{*}}(A,P_{j},I_{{\mathfrak{A}}\,},...,I_{{\mathfrak{A}}\,}))

\displaystyle=p_{n_{*}}(\varphi(A),\varphi(P_{j}),I_{{\mathfrak{A}}\,^{\prime}},...,I_{{\mathfrak{A}}\,^{\prime}})

\displaystyle=2^{n-2}(\varphi(A)\varphi(P_{j})-\varphi(P_{j})\varphi(A)^{*}).

Now, since
\varphi
is
*
-additive, multiplying the second equality by
i
and adding these two equations we obtain
\varphi(AP_{j})=\varphi(A)\varphi(P_{j})
.
The second statement is obtained in a similar way.
∎

Lemma 2.7
.

\varphi({\mathfrak{A}}\,_{jk})\subset{\mathfrak{A}}\,_{jk}^{\prime}
.

Proof.

Given
X\in{\mathfrak{A}}\,_{jk}
, we have
X=P_{j}XP_{k}
and then, by Lemma
2.6
,
\varphi(X)=\varphi(P_{j})\varphi(XP_{k})=\varphi(P_{j})\varphi(X)\varphi(P_{k})\in{\mathfrak{A}}\,_{jk}^{\prime}
.
∎

Lemma 2.8
.

To
j\neq k:

•

If
A_{jk}\in{\mathfrak{A}}\,_{jk}
and
B_{kk}\in{\mathfrak{A}}\,_{kk}
then
\varphi(A_{jk}B_{kk})=\varphi(A_{jk})\varphi(B_{kk})
;

•

If
A_{jj}\in{\mathfrak{A}}\,_{jj}
and
B_{jk}\in{\mathfrak{A}}\,_{jk}
then
\varphi(A_{jj}B_{jk})=\varphi(A_{jj})\varphi(B_{jk})
;

•

If
A_{jk}\in{\mathfrak{A}}\,_{jk}
and
B_{kj}\in{\mathfrak{A}}\,_{kj}
then
\varphi(A_{jk}B_{kj})=\varphi(A_{jk})\varphi(B_{kj})
.

Proof.

In order to prove the first statement, on the one hand, by Lemma
2.7

\displaystyle\varphi(A_{jk}B_{kk})-\varphi(A_{jk}B_{kk})^{*}

\displaystyle=\varphi(A_{jk}B_{kk}-(A_{jk}B_{kk})^{*})

\displaystyle=\varphi(p_{n_{*}}(A_{jk},B_{kk},P_{k},...,P_{k}))

\displaystyle=p_{n_{*}}(\varphi(A_{jk}),\varphi(B_{kk}),Q_{k},...,Q_{k})

\displaystyle=\varphi(A_{jk})\varphi(B_{kk})-(\varphi(A_{jk})\varphi(B_{kk}))^{*}

and then
\mathcal{I}(\varphi(A_{jk}B_{kk}))=\mathcal{I}(\varphi(A_{jk})\varphi(B_{kk}))
. On the other hand, using
iA_{jk}
rather than
A_{jk}
we obtain

\mathcal{R}(\varphi(A_{jk}B_{kk}))=\mathcal{R}(\varphi(A_{jk})\varphi(B_{kk}))
. It concludes that
\varphi(A_{jk}B_{kk})=\varphi(A_{jk})\varphi(B_{kk})
.

The others statements are proved in a similar way.
∎

Lemma 2.9
.

If
A_{jj},B_{jj}\in{\mathfrak{A}}\,_{jj}
then
\varphi(A_{jj}B_{jj})=\varphi(A_{jj})\varphi(B_{jj})
.

Proof.

Let
X_{kj}
be an element of
{\mathfrak{A}}\,_{kj}
, with
j\neq k
. Using Lemma
2.8
we obtain

\varphi(X_{kj})\varphi(A_{jj}B_{jj})=\varphi(X_{kj}A_{jj}B_{jj})=\varphi(X_{kj}A_{jj})\varphi(B_{jj})=\varphi(X_{kj})\varphi(A_{jj})\varphi(B_{jj}),

that is,

\varphi(X_{kj})(\varphi(A_{jj}B_{jj})-\varphi(A_{jj})\varphi(B_{jj}))=0.

Now, by Lemma
2.7
,
\varphi(X_{kj})\in{\mathfrak{A}}\,^{\prime}_{kj}
as well as
\varphi(A_{jj}B_{jj})
and
\varphi(A_{jj})\varphi(B_{jj})\in{\mathfrak{A}}\,^{\prime}_{jj}
.
Then,
Q_{k}{\mathfrak{A}}\,^{\prime}(\varphi(A_{jj}B_{jj})-\varphi(A_{jj})\varphi(B_{jj}))=0
, which implies that
\varphi(A_{jj}B_{jj})=\varphi(A_{jj})\varphi(B_{jj})

.
∎

By additivity of
\varphi
and Lemmas
2.8
and

,

, that

.
It concludes the proof of Theorem

.

On Random Matrices Arising in Deep Neural Networks: General I.I.D. Case
L. Pastur, V. Slavin

Abstract
We study the eigenvalue distribution of random matrices pertinent to the analysis of deep neural networks. The matrices resemble the product of the sample covariance matrices, however, an important difference is that the analog of the population covariance matrix is now a function of random data matrices (synaptic weight matrices in the deep neural network terminology). The problem has been treated in recent work [ 1 ] by using the techniques of free probability theory. Since, however, free probability theory deals with population covariance matrices which are independent of the data matrices, its applicability in this case has to be justified. The justification has been given in [ 2 ] for Gaussian data matrices with independent entries, a standard analytical model of free probability, by using a version of the techniques of random matrix theory. In this paper we use another version of the techniques to extend the results of [ 2 ] to the case where the entries of the data matrices are just independent identically distributed random variables with zero mean and finite fourth moment. This, in particular, justifies the mean field approximation in the infinite width limit for the deep untrained neural networks and the property of the macroscopic universality of random matrix theory in this case.

Proof.
Note that in the course of proving Theorem

Let us prove assertion (i) of the lemma. It follows from (
2.17

h

\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{R}_{+}
that
k
is also analytic in
\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{R}_{+}

Next, for any solution of (
2.16

with

\Im k(z)=-\Im h(z)\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\lambda^{2}\nu_{K}(d\lambda)}{|h(z)\lambda+1|^{2}}

(3.92)

Let us prove now that the system (
2.16

analytic in
\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{R}_{+}
and satisfying (

The argument below is a version of that used in

Assume that there exist two different solutions
(h_{1},k_{1})
and
(h_{2},k_{2})
of (
2.16

2.17
), i.e., there exists
z_{0}\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{R}_{+}
, where at least one of two functions

\delta h=h_{1}-h_{2},\;\delta k=k_{1}-k_{2}
is not zero. Since
\delta h
and
\delta k
are
analytic in
\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{R}_{+}
, we can assume without loss
of generality that
\Im z_{0}\neq 0
. It follows then from (
2.16
) – (
2.17
) that

\delta h(z_{0})=-\delta k(z_{0})\;I_{k}(z_{0}),\;\delta k(z_{0})=-\delta h(z_{0})\;I_{h}(z_{0}),

(3.93)

where

\displaystyle I_{k}(z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\lambda^{2}\nu_{R}(d\lambda)}{(\lambda k_{1}(z)-z)(\lambda k_{2}(z)-z)},\;

\displaystyle I_{h}(z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\lambda^{2}\nu_{K}(d\lambda)}{(\lambda h_{1}(z)+1)(\lambda h_{2}(z)+1)}.

Viewing (
3.93

\delta h
and
\delta k
that has a non-trivial solution, we conclude that

1=I_{k}(z_{0})I_{h}(z_{0}),\;\Im z_{0}\neq 0.

(3.94)

On the other hand, we have by Schwarz inequality

|I_{k}(z)|\leq(A_{k_{1}}(z)A_{k_{2}}(z))^{1/2},\;|I_{h}(z)|\leq(A_{h_{1}}(z)A_{h_{2}}(z))^{1/2},

(3.95)

where

A_{k}(z)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\lambda^{2}\nu_{R}(d\lambda)}{|\lambda k(z)-z|^{2}},\;A_{h}(z)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\lambda^{2}\nu_{K}(d\lambda)}{|\lambda h(z)+1|^{2}}.

In addition, the imaginary parts of (
2.16

2.17

\displaystyle\Im k(z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\Im h(z)A_{h}(z),

\displaystyle\Im h(z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\Im k(z)A_{k}(z)+\Im z\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\lambda\nu_{R}(d\lambda)}{|\lambda k(z)-z|^{2}},

hence, by (

0<A_{k}(z)A_{h}(z)=1-\frac{\Im z}{\Im h(z)}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\lambda\nu_{R}(d\lambda)}{|\lambda k(z)-z|^{2}}<1,\;\Im z\neq 0.

This and (
3.95

|I_{k}(z)I_{h}(z)|^{2}\leq(A_{k_{1}}(z)A_{h_{1}}(z))(A_{k_{2}}(z)A_{h_{2}}(z))<1,\;\;\Im z\neq 0

which contradicts (
3.94

Let us prove assertion (ii) of the lemma. Since

\displaystyle k^{(p)}

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\lambda\nu_{K^{(p)}}(d\lambda)}{h^{(p)}\lambda+1}

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\lambda\nu_{K^{(p)}}(d\lambda)}{\widetilde{h}\lambda+1}+(\widetilde{h}-h^{(p)})\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\lambda^{2}\nu_{K^{(p)}}(d\lambda)}{(h^{(p)}\lambda+1)(\widetilde{h}\lambda+1)}.

Putting here

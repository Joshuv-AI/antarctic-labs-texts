Distribution of the Bad Part of Class Groups
Weitong Wang

Abstract
. Let G G be a finite group. The Cohen-Lenstra-Martinet Heuristics give a prediction of the distribution of Cl K ⁡ [ p ∞ ] \operatorname{Cl}_{K}[p^{\infty}] when K K runs over G G -fields and p ∤ | G | p\nmid\lvert G\rvert . In this paper, we prove several results on the distribution of ideal class groups for some p | | G | p\mid\lvert G\rvert , and show that the behaviour is qualitatively different than what is predicted by the heuristics when p ∤ | G | p\nmid\lvert G\rvert . We do this by using genus theory and the invariant part of the class group to investigate the algebraic structure of the class group. For general number fields, our result is conditional on a natural conjecture on counting fields. For abelian or D 4 D_{4} -fields, our result is unconditional.

be an integer, then

\displaystyle\#\{p\mid e_{K}(p)\equiv 0\bmod{q^{l}}\}-2(n-1)

\displaystyle\leq\operatorname{rk}_{q}q^{l-1}D_{K}^{G}\leq

\displaystyle\#\{p\mid e_{K}(p)\equiv 0\bmod{q^{l}}\}.

We prove the theorem by two lemmas.
The first lemma gives a detailed description of the elements in
D_{K}^{G}
in the sense of fractional ideals.
For each rational prime
p
, if
p\mathscr{O}_{K}=\mathfrak{p}_{1}^{e_{1}}\cdots\mathfrak{p}_{r}^{e_{r}}
is the factorization in
K
, then define

\mathfrak{a}(p):=\prod_{i=1}^{r}\mathfrak{p}_{i}^{e_{i}/e_{K}(p)}.

Lemma 3.6
.

[
18
, Equation (8)]

Let
K/\mathbb{Q}
be a number field whose Galois closure is a
G
-extension
L/\mathbb{Q}
.

(1)

The group
I_{K}\cap I_{L}^{G}
is free abelian generated by

\{\mathfrak{a}(p)\mid p\text{ is a rational prime}\}.

(2)

The group
\tilde{D}_{K}^{G}:=I_{K}\cap I_{L}^{G}/P_{\mathbb{Q}}
is isomorphic to
\prod_{p}\mathbb{Z}/e_{K}(p)
, where
P_{k}
means the group of principal ideals of a number field
k
.

The second lemma is to estimate the difference between principal ideals of
K
and
\mathbb{Q}
.

Lemma 3.7
.

[
18
, Equation(11)]

Let
K/\mathbb{Q}
be a number field whose Galois closure a
G
-extension
L/\mathbb{Q}
, and let
\mathscr{P}_{k}
be the group of principal ideals of a number field
k
.
Then

(3.1)

\operatorname{rk}_{q}{P}_{K}^{G}/P_{\mathbb{Q}}\leq 2(n-1),

where
P_{K}^{G}:=P_{K}\cap I_{L}^{G}
.

Now let’s prove the theorem.

Proof of Theorem
3.5
.

Lemma
3.6
gives the upper bound of
\operatorname{rk}_{q}q^{l-1}D_{K}^{G}
, because
P_{\mathbb{Q}}\subseteq P_{K}
.
According to the short exact sequence

(3.2)

0\to P_{K}^{G}/P_{\mathbb{Q}}\to I_{K}^{G}/P_{\mathbb{Q}}\to D_{K}^{G}\to 0,

the inequality (
3.1
) tells us the lower bound directly.
∎

We can apply the theorem to the class group
\operatorname{Cl}_{K}
, for
D_{K}^{G}\subseteq\operatorname{Cl}_{K}.

Corollary 3.8
.

Let
K
be a number field of degree
n
over
\mathbb{Q}
, and let
q
be a prime number, and let
l\geq 1
be an integer, then

\operatorname{rk}_{q}q^{l-1}\operatorname{Cl}_{K}\geq\#\{p\mid e_{K}(p)\equiv 0\bmod{q^{l}}\}-2(n-1).

One of the advantages of this theory, as mentioned above, is that
D_{K}^{G}
is a subgroup of
\operatorname{Cl}_{K}
.
So, we can even try to discuss the relative class group here, i.e., for a subfield
K^{\prime}\subseteq K
, we want to give a description for
D_{K}^{G}\cap\operatorname{Cl}(K/K^{\prime})
.

Theorem 3.9
.

Let
K/\mathbb{Q}
be a number field of degree
n
whose Galois closure is a
G
-extension
L/\mathbb{Q}
.
If
K^{\prime}\subseteq K
such that
q^{l}\mid[K:K^{\prime}]
where
q
is a rational prime, and
l\geq 1
, then

\operatorname{rk}_{q}q^{l-1}\operatorname{Cl}(K/K^{\prime})\geq\#\{p\mid e_{K}(p)\equiv 0\bmod{p^{l}}\}-2(n-1).

Proof.

For each prime
p
, recall that
\mathfrak{a}(p)
is the ideal of
K
such that
p\mathscr{O}_{K}=\mathfrak{a}(p)^{e_{K}(p)}
.
We’ve shown in Lemma
3.6
that
\mathfrak{a}(p)
is fixed by the action of
G
, viewed as an element of
I_{L}
.
So, we have the following computation

\operatorname{Nm}_{K/K^{\prime}}(\mathfrak{a}(p))=\mathfrak{a}(p)^{[K:K^{\prime}]},

where
\mathfrak{a}(p)^{[K:K^{\prime}]}
is treated as an ideal of
K^{\prime}
.
If
q^{l}\mid e_{K}(p)
, and
\mathfrak{b}(p):=(\mathfrak{a}(p))^{e_{K}(p)/q^{l}}
, then
\operatorname{Nm}_{K/K^{\prime}}(\mathfrak{b}(p))
becomes a power of
p\mathscr{O}_{K^{\prime}}
, hence a principal ideal of
K^{\prime}
.
So,
\mathfrak{b}(p)
represents an ideal class of
\operatorname{Cl}(K/K^{\prime})
.
Let’s define

I_{L/K}^{G}[q^{l}]:=\langle\mathfrak{b}(p)\mid e_{K}(p)\equiv 0\bmod{q^{l}}\rangle.

Note that
I_{L/K}^{G}[q^{l}]\subseteq I_{K}^{G}
, and we have

I_{L/K}^{G}[q^{l}]P_{\mathbb{Q}}/P_{\mathbb{Q}}\cong\prod_{i=1}^{s}\mathbb{Z}/q^{l}

where
s=\#\{p\mid e_{K}(p)\equiv 0\bmod{q^{l}}\}
.
By Lemma
3.6
, the group
D_{K}^{G}
is generated by ideals of the form
\mathfrak{a}(p)
, we therefore know that

D_{L/K}^{G}[q^{l}]:=I_{L/K}^{G}[q^{l}]P_{K}/P_{K}\subseteq D_{K}^{G}[q^{l}]\cap\operatorname{Cl}(K/K^{\prime}).

By Lemma
3.7
, we know that
\operatorname{rk}_{q}P^{G}_{K}/P_{\mathbb{Q}}
is bounded above by a constant
2(n-1)
.
Using the short exact sequence

1\to P_{K}^{G}/P_{\mathbb{Q}}\cap I_{L/K}^{G}[q^{l}]P_{\mathbb{Q}}/P_{\mathbb{Q}}\to I_{L/K}^{G}[q^{l}]P_{\mathbb{Q}}/P_{\mathbb{Q}}\to D_{L/K}^{G}[q^{l}]\to 1,

we obtain the desired result.
∎

4.
Non-random primes

Let
q
be a fixed rational prime.
In Theorem
1.5
and Theorem
1.6
, we see that
\operatorname{rk}_{q}\operatorname{Cl}_{K}
is related to the set

\{p\mid e_{K}(p)\equiv 0\bmod{q}\}.

The ramification of a prime
p
in the extension
K/\mathbb{Q}
could be recovered from the decomposition group
G_{p}=G_{p}(K/\mathbb{Q})
and the inertia subgroup
I_{p}
and so on.
So the condition
e_{K}(p)\equiv 0\bmod{q}
could be translated as a condition on the Galois group
G
.

Let
G
be a transitive permutation group in this section.
In a
G
-extension (see Definition
1.1
), let’s first give the translation by the following lemma.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
q
be a fixed prime,
and let
(K/\mathbb{Q},\psi)
be an extension of number fields such that its Galois closure
(\hat{K},\psi)
is a
G
-field and that
K=\hat{K}^{G_{1}}
, where
G_{1}
is the image of stabilizer of
1
.
For a fixed positive integer
l
, a rational prime
p\nmid\lvert G\rvert
satisfies the identity

e_{K}(p)\equiv 0\bmod{q^{l}},

if and only if
I_{p}\cap\Omega_{q^{l}}\neq\emptyset
where
I_{p}
is the inertia subgroup of
p
(up to conjugate), and
\Omega_{q^{l}}:=\{g\in G\mid e(g)\equiv 0\bmod{q^{l}}\}
.

Proof.

Let
\mathfrak{P}
be a prime of
\hat{K}
above
p
.
One can check that if
G_{\mathfrak{P}}
is the decomposition group of
\mathfrak{P}
, then the orbits of
G_{\mathfrak{P}}
on
G/G_{1}
correspond to the primes
\mathfrak{p}
of
K
above
p
, and the size of orbits of
I_{\mathfrak{P}}
corresponds to the ramification index respectively.

If
p
is a tamely ramified prime, then
I_{\mathfrak{P}}=\langle g\rangle
is just cyclic.
So,
e(p)\equiv 0\bmod{q^{l}}
if and only if the size of orbits of the inertia generator has common divisor
q^{l}
, i.e.,
q^{l}\mid e(g)
using the notations in Definition
1.4
and
1.7
, which means that
I_{\mathfrak{P}}\cap\Omega_{q^{l}}\neq\emptyset
.
Note that
I_{p}
is well-defined up to conjugation.
And the result just follows.
∎

So roughly speaking, if
q
is a prime such that the set

\Omega_{q}=\{g\in G\mid e(g)\equiv 0\bmod{q}\}

is nontrivial, then potentially results like Theorem
1.6
could give us some nontrivial estimate for
\operatorname{rk}_{q}\operatorname{Cl}_{K}
, when
K
runs over fields in
\mathcal{S}(G)
.
The discussion of this section is intended for such primes, and philosophically they should lead us to results like infinite
C_{q}
-moment introduced in Section
1
.
And this explains the title of the section.

Our most important goal in this section is to prove Theorem
1.11
.
We reach the theorem in several steps.
The first step is to show that the Hypothesis
1.10
implies zero-probability of
\operatorname{rk}_{q}\operatorname{Cl}_{K}\leq r
.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
\mathcal{S}:=\mathcal{S}(G)
ordered by a counting function
C
.
Let
G_{1}\subseteq H\subseteq G
be a tower of subgroups of
G
, where
G_{1}
is the stabilizer of
1
.
Let
q
be a rational prime such that
q^{l}\mid[H:G_{1}]
, where
l\geq 1
.
Let
\Omega
be a subset of
G
that is closed under invertible powering and conjugation such that for each
g\in\Omega
we have that
e(g)\equiv\bmod{q^{l}}
.
If the Hypothesis
1.10
hold for
(\mathcal{S},C,\Omega)
, i.e., for all non-negative integer
r
, we have

N_{\mathcal{S}_{\Omega}^{r},C}(X)=o\left(N_{\mathcal{S},C}(X)\right),

then for all
r
, we have

\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{S},C}(\operatorname{rk}_{q}q^{l-1}\operatorname{Cl}(K/\hat{K}^{H})\leq r)=0.

Proof.

Write
n:=[K:\mathbb{Q}]
.
According to Theorem
3.9
and Lemma
4.1
, for a fixed extension
K/\mathbb{Q}
in
\mathcal{S}
, we have that

[\#\{p\nmid\lvert G\rvert:I_{p}\cap\Omega\neq\emptyset\}\geq r+2n]\Rightarrow[\operatorname{rk}_{q}q^{l-1}\operatorname{Cl}(K/\hat{K}^{H})>r].

The condition says that for all
r
we have
N_{\mathcal{S}^{r}_{\Omega},C}(X)=o\left(N_{\mathcal{S},C}(X)\right)
.
This implies that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{S},C}(\operatorname{rk}_{q}q^{l-1}\operatorname{Cl}(K/\hat{K}^{H})\leq r)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{X\to\infty}\frac{\#\{K\in\mathcal{S}\mid C(K)<X\text{ and }\operatorname{rk}_{q}q^{l-1}\operatorname{Cl}(K/\hat{K}^{H})\leq r\}}{N_{\mathcal{S},C}(X)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\lim_{X\to\infty}\frac{\sum_{i=0}^{r+2n}N_{\mathcal{S}^{i}_{\Omega},C}(X)}{N_{\mathcal{S},C}(X)}=0.

∎

Then let’s prove that if the probability of
\operatorname{rk}_{q}\operatorname{Cl}_{K}\leq r
is bounded, then we have infinite moment.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
\mathcal{S}:=\mathcal{S}(G)
with a counting function
C
.
Let
G_{1}\subseteq H\subseteq G
be a tower of subgroups of
G
, where
G_{1}
is the image of stabilizer of
1
.
Let
q
be a rational prime and
l\geq 1
be an integer.
If there exists some constant
0\leq c<1
such that for all
r=0,1,2,\dots
, we have

\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{S},C}(\operatorname{rk}_{q}q^{l-1}\operatorname{Cl}(K/\hat{K}^{H})\leq r)\leq c,

then

\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{S},C}(\lvert\operatorname{Hom}(q^{l-1}\operatorname{Cl}(K/\hat{K}^{H}),C_{q})\rvert)=+\infty,

where
\hat{K}
is the Galois closure of
K
, and
C_{q}
is the cyclic group of order
q
.

Proof.

Write

H_{\mathcal{S},C}(X):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}K\in\mathcal{S}\\
C(K)<X\end{subarray}}\lvert\operatorname{Hom}(q^{l-1}\operatorname{Cl}(K/\hat{K}^{H}),C_{q}).

For each non-negative integer
r
, define

R^{r}_{\mathcal{S},C}(X):=\#\{K\in\mathcal{S}\mid C(K)<X\text{ and }\operatorname{rk}_{q}q^{l-1}\operatorname{Cl}(K/\hat{K}^{H})=r\}.

Let
d
be any fixed positive integer.
By definition,

\lim_{X\to\infty}\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{S},C}(\operatorname{rk}_{q}q^{l-1}\operatorname{Cl}(K/\hat{K}^{H})\leq d)=\lim_{X\to\infty}\sum_{i=0}^{d}\frac{R^{i}_{\mathcal{S},C}(X)}{N_{\mathcal{S},C}(X)}.

So, for each small number
\epsilon>0
, there exists some
N>0
such that for all
X>N
, we have that

\frac{\sum_{i=0}^{d}R^{l,i}_{\mathcal{S},C}(X)}{N_{\mathcal{S},C}(X)}\leq c+\epsilon.

Note that for a fixed
X
, the sum

\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\frac{R^{l,i}_{\mathcal{S},C}(X)}{N_{\mathcal{S},C}(X)}

is finite and equal to
1
.
So, for each
X>N
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{H_{\mathcal{S},C}(X)}{N_{\mathcal{S},C}(X)}=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}q^{i}\frac{R^{i}_{\mathcal{S},C}(X)}{N_{\mathcal{S},C}(X)}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle q^{d}(1-\sum_{i=0}^{d}\frac{R^{i}_{\mathcal{S},C}(X)}{N_{\mathcal{S},C}(X)})

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle q^{d}(1-c-\epsilon).

By letting
d
go to infinity, we see that for each positive number
M>0
, there exists some constant
N>0
such that for all
X>N
, the ratio

\frac{H_{\mathcal{S},C}(X)}{N_{\mathcal{S},C}(X)}>M,

hence the result.
∎

Before we move on to more concrete situations, let’s discuss an example where the fields are not ordered by product of ramified primes.

Example 4.4

(Discriminant)
.

Let’s consider the set
\mathcal{S}:=\mathcal{S}(S_{3})
of non-Galois cubic extensions.
Let
\Omega:=\{(123),(132)\}
.
In this case we want to show that the Hypothesis
1.10
is
false
for
(\mathcal{S},d,\Omega)
when the fields ordered by the
absolute discriminant

d
, despite the fact that
(123)
clearly satisfies the condition that
3\mid e((123))
.

According to the work of Bhargava, Shankar, and Tsimerman
[
4
, Theorem 8]
, there exists some positive constants
c_{1},c_{2}
such that

N_{\mathcal{S}_{\Omega}^{0},d}(X)\sim c_{1}X\quad\text{and}\quad N_{\mathcal{S},d}(X)\sim c_{2}X,

when the cubic fields are ordered by discriminant.
This already contradicts the Hypothesis
1.10
when ordering fields by discriminant.
On the other hand, Proposition
8.5
shows that, under some other assumptions deduced from Cohen-Lenstra Heuristics and Malle-Bhargava Heuristics, the Hypothesis
1.10
holds for
(\mathcal{S},P,\Omega)
, where
P
is the product of ramified primes.
This shows that the hypothesis itself is dependent on the ordering of number fields,
and we have to choose a suitable counting function.

5.
Dirichlet series and Tauberian Theorem

In this section, we discuss the analytic properties of some Dirichlet series, which will be useful tools in later sections.

5.1.
Tauberian Theorem

Let’s first present a Tauberian Theorem that is used in the paper repeatedly.
Recall that for the complex variable
s\in\mathbb{C}
, we write
s=\sigma+it
.

Theorem 5.1

(Delange-Ikehara)
.

[
15
, Appendix II Theorem I]

Assume that the coefficients of a Dirichlet series are real and non-negative, and that it converges in the half-plane
\sigma>1
, defining a holomorphic function
f(s)
.
Assume, moreover, that in the same half-plane one can write

f(s)=\sum_{j=0}^{q}g_{j}(s)\log^{b_{j}}\left(\frac{1}{s-1}\right)(s-1)^{-\alpha_{j}}+g(s),

where functions
g,g_{0},\dots,g_{q}
are holomorphic in the closed half plane
\sigma\geq 1
, the
b_{j}
-s are non-negative integers,
\alpha_{0}
is a positive real number,
\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{q}
are complex numbers with
\Re\alpha_{j}<\alpha_{0}
, and
g_{0}(1)\neq 0
.

Then for the summatory function
A(x)=\sum_{n<x}a_{n}
we have, for
x
tending infinity,

A(x)=\left(\frac{g_{0}(1)}{\Gamma(\alpha_{0})}+o(1)\right)x(\log x)^{\alpha_{0}-1}(\log\log x)^{b_{0}}.

If
f
satisfies the same assumptions, except that
\alpha_{0}=0
and
b_{0}\geq 1
, then

A(x)=(b_{0}g_{0}(1)+o(1))x\frac{(\log\log x)^{b_{0}-1}}{\log x}.

See the paper of Delange
[
8
]
for more results on Tauberian Theorems.

5.2.
Dirichlet L-series and some applications

Recall that if
m>1
is an integer and
\chi:(\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z})^{*}\to S^{1}
is a Dirichlet character mod
m
, then the
Dirichlet L-series
associated to
\chi
is the series

L(\chi,s):=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\chi(n)}{n^{s}}.

It admits many analytic properties similar to those of Riemann zeta function
\zeta(s)
.
Let’s summarize them in the following statement.

Proposition 5.2
.

(1)

The series
L(\chi,s)
converges absolutely and uniformly in the domain
\sigma\geq 1+\delta
, for any
\delta>0
.
It therefore represents a holomorphic function on the half-plane
\sigma>1
.

(2)

When
\sigma>1
, we have
Euler’s identity

L(\chi,s)=\prod_{p}\frac{1}{1-\chi(p)p^{-s}}.

(3)

If
\chi
is nontrivial, then it could be extended analytically to the whole complex plane.

(4)

For any Dirichlet character
\chi
, the series
L(\chi,s)
admits a zero-free region containing the closed half-plane
\sigma\geq 1
.

One can check its basic properties in some textbooks, say Neukirch
[
16
, VII.2]
.
The zero-free region could be viewed as a corollary of the zero-free region of Hecke
L
-series.
See Coleman
[
6
]
for example.
Let’s prove an analytic tool that will be used later.
Recall that
\phi
is the Euler’s phi function.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
m>1
be an integer.
Let
f:\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}\to\mathbb{N}
be a map such that
f(\alpha)\neq 0
for some
\alpha\in(\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z})^{*}
.
By composing
\mathbb{Z}\to\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}
, we also treat
f
as a map
\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
.
Define a Euler product

F(s):=\prod_{p\text{ rational prime}}(1+f(p)p^{-s}).

Then
F(s)
is absolutely and uniformly convergent in
\sigma>1+\delta
, for each
\delta>0
, and it represents a holomorphic function in the open half-plane
\sigma>1
.
If
f
is nonzero on
(\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z})^{*}
, then
F(s)
admits an analytic continuation to the line
\sigma=1
except at
s=1
.
In particular, there exists some holomorphic function
g(s)
at
s=1
with
g(1)\neq 0
such that

F(s)=g(s)(s-1)^{-\beta}

near
s=1
, where
\beta
is given by the formula

\beta=\frac{1}{\phi(m)}\sum_{\alpha\in(\mathbb{Z}/m)^{*}}f(\alpha).

Proof.

Clearly
f
is bounded above by some constant
N>0
, hence

\lvert F(s)\rvert\leq\prod_{p}(1+Np^{-\sigma}),

which implies that
F(s)
is absolutely and uniformly convergent in
\sigma>1+\delta
, for each
\delta>0
.
So it represents a holomorphic function in the half-plane
\sigma>1
.

Let’s write

F(s)=\prod_{\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}}\prod_{p\equiv\alpha\bmod{m}}(1+f(\alpha)p^{-s})=:\prod_{\alpha}F_{\alpha}(s).

It is clear that there are only finitely many primes
p\mid m
.
And for a fixed factor
(1+f(p)p^{-s})
, it is a nonzero holomorphic function in the closed half-plane
\sigma\geq 1
.
So it suffices to prove the statement for each
F_{\alpha}(s)
with
\alpha\in(\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z})^{*}
.

Let’s assume without loss of generality that
F(s)=F_{\alpha}(s)
for some
\alpha\in(\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z})^{*}
.
Since
F(s)
is nonzero in the open half-plane
\sigma>1
, we can take the logarithm.
For the primes
p>N
, we have that

\lvert\log\prod_{p>N}(1+f(\alpha)p^{-s})-\frac{f(\alpha)}{\phi(m)}\sum_{\chi}\chi(\alpha)\log(\bar{\chi},s)\rvert\leq\sum_{p\leq N}\frac{f(\alpha)}{p^{\sigma}}+\sum_{p>N}\frac{2f(\alpha)}{p^{2\sigma-\epsilon}},

where
\chi
runs over all characters modulo
m
, and
\epsilon>0
is some small real number.
The zero-free region of the Dirichlet
L
-series first shows that if
\chi
is nontrivial then
\log L(\chi,s)
is a holomorphic function in the closed half-plane
\sigma\geq 1
.
And the case when
\chi=\chi_{0}
is the trivial character reduces to the Riemann zeta function.
To be precise,
L(\chi_{0},s)
and
\zeta(s)
differs by finitely many Euler factors, so it is a holomorphic function in
\mathbb{C}\backslash\{0,1\}
with a simple pole at
s=1
.
In particular, it is nonzero in the closed half-plane
\sigma\geq 1
.
So
F(s)
could be extended analytically to the line
\sigma=1
except at
s=1
.
And the function defined by the formula

g(s):=(s-1)^{\beta}F(s)

is a nonzero holomorphic function at
s=1
, hence the result.
∎

From the above proof, we obtain the following.

Corollary 5.4
.

Let
m>1
be an integer.
For each
\alpha\in(\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z})^{*}
, define

l_{m}^{1}(\alpha,s):=\sum_{p\equiv\alpha\bmod{m}}p^{-s}.

Then
l_{m}^{1}(s)
is absolutely and uniformly convergent in the open half-plane
\sigma>1+\delta
for any
\delta>0
, and it represents a holomorphic function in the half-plane
\sigma>1
.
It could be extended analytically to the line
\sigma=1
except at
s=1
.
In particular, there exists some holomorphic function
g(x)
at
s=1
such that

l_{m}^{1}(\alpha,s)=\frac{1}{\phi(m)}\log\frac{1}{s-1}+g(s)

near
s=1
.

Proof.

Just compare
l_{m}^{1}(\alpha,1)
and

\frac{1}{\phi(m)}\sum_{\chi}\log L(\chi,s)

where
\chi
runs over all primitive characters mod
m
.
∎

As a generalization, we have the following.

Proposition 5.5
.

Let
m>1
be an integer.
Let
f:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
be a multiplicative map such that for all
p\equiv q\bmod{m}
we have that
f(p)=f(q)
and we treat it also as a map defined on
(\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z})
.
Define the series

l_{f}^{r}(s):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\text{ square-free}\\
\omega(n)=r\end{subarray}}f(n)n^{-s}.

Then
l_{f}^{r}(s)
defines a holomorphic function in the open half-plane
\sigma>1
.
If
f
is nonzero on
(\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z})^{*}
, then
l_{f}^{r}(s)
could be extended analytically to the line
\sigma=1
except at
s=1
.
In particular, there exists holomorphic functions
g_{0}(s),\dots,g_{r-1}(s)
and a nonzero constant function
g_{r}(s)
at
s=1
such that

l_{f}^{r}(s)=\sum_{j=0}^{r}g_{j}(s)\Bigl(\log\frac{1}{s-1}\Bigr)^{j}.

Proof.

First of all, if
f
is zero on
(\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z})^{*}
, then clearly
l_{f}^{r}(s)
is a holomorphic function in the half-plane
\sigma>0
, for there are only finitely many primes
p\mid m
.
Let’s start with the simplest case where
f(\alpha_{0})=1
for a fixed
\alpha_{0}\in(\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z})^{*}
and
f(\alpha)=0
for all
\alpha\neq\alpha_{0}
in
\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}
.
In this case, let’s just write

l_{m}^{r}(\alpha_{0},s):=l^{r}_{f}(s).

If
r=1
, then this is exactly the Corollary
5.4
.
Let’s prove the statement by induction on
r
.
Assume that
l_{m}^{j}(\alpha_{0},s)
satisfies the properties in the statement, for each
j=1,2,\dots,r-1
.
One can check that the following identity holds:

l_{m}^{r}(\alpha_{0},s)=\frac{1}{r}l_{m}^{1}(\alpha_{0},s)l_{m}^{r-1}(\alpha_{0},s)+\sum_{j=2}^{r}\frac{(-1)^{j}}{r}l_{m}^{1}(\alpha_{0},js)l_{m}^{r-j}(\alpha_{0},s).

Then the property of
l_{m}^{r}(\alpha_{0},s)
just follows from the induction assumption and the above identity.

Now assume that
f
is any function that satisfies the condition in the statement.
Let
(r_{\alpha})_{\alpha}
denote a point in
\mathbb{N}^{m}
, where the index
\alpha
is an element of
\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}
.
We see that

\displaystyle l_{f}^{r}(s)=

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\text{ square-free}\\
\omega(n)=r\end{subarray}}\prod_{p\mid n}f(p)p^{-s}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{(r_{\alpha})_{\alpha}}\prod_{\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}}f(\alpha)^{r_{\alpha}}l^{r_{\alpha}}_{m}(\alpha,s)

where
(r_{\alpha})_{\alpha}
runs over points in
\mathbb{N}^{m}
such that

\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}}r_{\alpha}=r.

This expression first holds formally.
However since the coefficients of
l_{f}^{r}(s)
are bounded, it is absolutely and uniformly convergent in the half-plane
\sigma>1+\delta
for any
\delta>0
.
So it holds as an equation of holomorphic functions in the half-plane
\sigma>1
.
And the properties of
l_{f}^{r}(s)
just follow from those of
l_{m}^{r}(\alpha,s)
.
∎

5.3.
Counting homomorphisms

In this section, let’s apply the analytic tools to prove a result that will be used later.

Let
G
be a finite abelian group and let
K
be a
G
-field, i.e.,
K/\mathbb{Q}
is a Galois extension such that
G(K/\mathbb{Q})\cong G
.
Let
A
be a finite
G
-module.
Denote the group of idèles of
K
by
J_{K}
, and let
S_{\infty}
be the set of primes of
K
at infinity.
Denote the set of
G
-morphisms
\operatorname{Hom}_{G}(J_{K}^{S_{\infty}},A)
by
\mathcal{T}_{K}
.
For each rational prime
p
, let
\mathscr{O}_{p}:=\mathscr{O}_{K}\otimes\mathbb{Z}_{p}=\prod_{\mathfrak{p}\mid p}\mathscr{O}_{\mathfrak{p}}
, where
\mathscr{O}_{K}
is the ring of integers of
K
.
When
p=\infty
, just let
\mathscr{O}_{\infty}:=K\otimes\mathbb{R}
.

Definition 5.6
.

Let
c:\operatorname{Hom}_{G}(\mathscr{O}_{p}^{*},A)\to\mathbb{N}
be a weight function for each prime
p
(including the infinite prime) defined as follows:

c(\rho_{p})=\left\{\begin{aligned} &1\quad\text{if }p\nmid P(K)\infty\text{ and }\operatorname{im}(\rho_{p})\neq\{0\}\\
&0\quad\text{otherwise.}\end{aligned}\right.

Let
P
be the counting function defined for
\mathcal{T}_{K}
via

P(\rho):=\prod_{p}p^{c(\rho_{p})},

where the value of
p=\infty
is taken to be the real constant
e
.
We here just call it the product of ramified primes of
\rho
for simplicity.

For each rational prime
p
, let
\mathcal{T}_{p}
be a nonempty subset of
\operatorname{Hom}_{G}(\mathscr{O}_{p}^{*},G)\backslash\{1\}
, where
1
means the trivial map.
In other words, this is a nontrivial local condition for each prime
p
.
Denote its complement
\operatorname{Hom}_{G}(\mathscr{O}_{p}^{*},G)-\mathcal{T}_{p}
as
\mathcal{T}_{p}^{c}
.
Let
m
be a fixed integer such that
\lvert A\rvert\mid m
.
For each non-negative integer
r
, define
\mathcal{T}_{K}^{r}
to be the subset of
\mathcal{T}_{K}
such that a
G
-morphisms
\rho
is contained in
\mathcal{T}_{K}^{r}
if and only if there are exact
r
primes
p\nmid m
such that
\rho_{p}\in\mathcal{T}_{p}
.
Now let’s first give an Euler product to estimate the number of homomorphisms in
\mathcal{T}_{K}^{r}
ordered by the product of ramified primes
P
.

Proposition 5.7
.

For each non-negative integer
r
, define the generating series

F_{\mathcal{T}_{K}^{r},P}(s):=\sum_{\rho\in\mathcal{T}_{K}^{r}}P(\rho)^{-s},

and let

(5.1)

E_{\mathcal{T}_{K}^{r},P}(s):=\prod_{p}\bigl(\sum_{\rho\in\mathcal{T}_{p}^{c}}p^{-c(\rho)s}\bigr)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\text{ square-free}\\
\gcd(m,n)=1,\omega(n)=r\end{subarray}}\prod_{p\mid n}\bigl(\sum_{\rho\in\mathcal{T}_{p}}p^{-c(\rho)s}\bigr).

Write
F_{\mathcal{T}_{K}^{r},P}(s)
and
E_{\mathcal{T}_{K}^{r},P}(s)
in the form of Dirichlet series:

F_{\mathcal{T}_{K}^{r},P}(s)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}n^{-s}\quad\text{and}\quad E_{\mathcal{T}_{K}^{r},P}(s)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}b_{n}n^{-s}.

For each positive integer
n
, we have that

a_{n}\leq b_{n}.

Proof.

For a fixed integer
n
, let
\mathcal{T}_{K}(n)
be the subset of
\mathcal{T}_{K}
such that a
G
-morphism
\rho
is contained in
\mathcal{T}_{K}(n)
if and only if the condition

\rho_{p}\in\mathcal{T}_{p}\iff p\mid n

holds.
Define the generating series for
\mathcal{T}_{K}(n)
as

F_{\mathcal{T}_{K}(n),P}(s):=\sum_{\rho\in\mathcal{T}_{K}(n)}P(\rho)^{-s}.

If we write every generating series in the form of a Dirichlet series, then we have the identity of series:

F_{\mathcal{T}_{K}^{r},P}(s)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\text{ square-free}\\
\gcd(m,n)=1,\omega(n)=r\end{subarray}}F_{\mathcal{T}_{K}(n),P}(s).

This is a corollary of the fact that

\mathcal{T}_{K}^{r}=\bigsqcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\text{ square-free}\\
\gcd(m,n)=1,\omega(n)=r\end{subarray}}\mathcal{T}_{K}(n).

Note that if
\gcd(m,n)=1
, then the series
F_{\mathcal{T}_{K}(n),P}(s)
admits a Euler product formally:

F_{\mathcal{T}_{K}(n),P}(s)=\prod_{p\nmid n}\bigl(\sum_{\rho\in\mathcal{T}^{c}_{p}}p^{-c(\rho)s}\bigr)\prod_{p\mid n}\bigl(\sum_{\rho\in\mathcal{T}_{p}}p^{-c(\rho)s}\bigr).

This is a corollary of the fact that

\operatorname{Hom}_{G}(J_{K}^{S_{\infty}},A)=\bigoplus_{p}\operatorname{Hom}_{G}(\mathscr{O}_{p}^{*},A).

And we can rewrite
F_{\mathcal{T}_{K}^{r},P}(s)
(formally) as follows:

(5.2)

F_{\mathcal{T}_{K}^{r},P}(s)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\text{ square-free}\\
\gcd(m,n)=1,\omega(n)=r\end{subarray}}\Bigg[\prod_{p\mid n}\bigl(\sum_{\rho\in\mathcal{T}_{p}}p^{-c(\rho)s}\bigr)\cdot\prod_{p\nmid n}\bigl(\sum_{\rho\in\mathcal{T}_{p}^{c}}p^{-c(\rho)s}\bigr)\Bigg].

Now let’s consider the following series

(5.3)

\displaystyle E_{\mathcal{T}_{K}^{r},P}(s)=

\displaystyle\prod_{p}\bigl(\sum_{\rho\in\mathcal{T}_{p}^{c}}p^{-c(\rho)s}\bigr)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\text{ square-free}\\
\gcd(m,n)=1,\omega(n)=r\end{subarray}}\prod_{p\mid n}\bigl(\sum_{\rho\in\mathcal{T}_{p}}p^{-c(\rho)s}\bigr)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\text{ square-free}\\
\gcd(m,n)=1,\omega(n)=r\end{subarray}}\prod_{p\mid n}\bigl(\sum_{\rho\in\mathcal{T}_{p}}p^{-c(\rho)s}\bigr)\cdot\prod_{p}\bigl(\sum_{\rho\in\mathcal{T}_{p}^{c}}p^{-c(\rho)s}\bigr),

defined as in the statement of the proposition.
Note that we can switch the order of the operations for the identities hold formally (omitting the issue of convergence).
Write
E_{\mathcal{T}_{K}^{r},P}(s)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}b_{n}n^{-s}
.
By the comparison between (
5.2
) and (
5.3
), we see that

a_{n}\leq b_{n}

for all
n\geq 1
. ∎

Then let’s give the asymptotic behaviour of
\sum_{n<X}b_{n}
under some assumptions on the local specifications, where
E_{\mathcal{T}_{K}^{r},P}(s)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}b_{n}n^{-s}
as in (
5.1
).

Proposition 5.8
.

Let
M
be a fixed nonzero integer, and let
f_{1},f_{2}:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
be two maps such that for almost all primes
p\equiv q\bmod{M}
, we have
f_{i}(p)=f_{i}(q)
with
i=1,2
, and
f_{1}
is nonzero for infinitely many primes.
We treat
f_{1}
also as a function defined on
(\mathbb{Z}/M\mathbb{Z})^{*}
.
Define

(5.4)

E_{P}(f_{1},f_{2},s):=\prod_{p}(1+f_{2}(p)p^{-s})\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\text{ square-free}\\
\gcd(M,n)=1,\omega(n)=r\end{subarray}}\prod_{p\mid n}(1+f_{2}(p)p^{-s})

and write it as a Dirichlet series
E_{P}(f_{1},f_{2},s)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}b_{n}n^{-s}
, then

\sum_{n<X}b_{n}\ll X(\log X)^{\beta-1}(\log\log X)^{r},

as
X\to\infty
, where
\beta
is given by the formula

\beta=\frac{1}{\phi(M)}\sum_{a\in(\mathbb{Z}/M\mathbb{Z})^{*}}f_{1}(a).

Proof.

Note that
f_{1}
is nonzero on
(\mathbb{Z}/M\mathbb{Z})^{*}
by our assumption.
And the asymptotic behaviour of
\sum_{n<X}b_{n}
is just a corollary of Lemma
5.3
and Proposition
5.5
and the Tauberian Theorem
5.1
.
∎

Roughly speaking, if the local conditions
\{\mathcal{T}_{p}\}_{p}
satisfy some arithmetic property, then we obtain the estimate of the summatory
N_{\mathcal{T}_{K}^{r},P}(X)
in a relatively simple way.

6.
Abelian extensions

In this section, let
G
be a finite abelian group.
The action of
G
on itself by (left) multiplication turns it into a transitive permutation group in
S_{m}
where
m=\lvert G\rvert
.
So
\mathcal{S}:=\mathcal{S}(G)
is just the set of abelian
G
-fields.
See also Definition
1.1
and
1.2
.
The main result of this section is the following.

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
1\notin\Gamma
be a non-empty subset of
G
that is closed under invertible powering.
Let

\beta:=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}g\in G\backslash\Gamma\\
g\neq 1\end{subarray}}[\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{r(g)}):\mathbb{Q}],

where
r(g)
is the order of
g
.
For each non-negative integer
r
, we have

N_{\mathcal{S}_{\Gamma}^{r},P}(X)\ll X(\log X)^{\beta-1}(\log\log X)^{r},

as
X\to\infty
.

See Definition
1.9
for the notation
\mathcal{S}_{\Gamma}^{r}
.
From the theorem, we immediately obtain the following.

Corollary 6.2
.

Let
1\notin\Gamma
be any nonempty subset of
G
that is closed under invertible powering.
The Hypothesis
1.10
is true for
(\mathcal{S},P,\Gamma)
.

Proof.

Counting abelian fields
N_{\mathcal{S},P}(X)
ordered by product of ramified primes is given by the formula

N_{\mathcal{S},P}(X)\sim cX(\log X)^{\alpha-1}

where
c>0
is a constant and

\alpha=\sum_{1\neq g\in G}[\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{r(g)}):\mathbb{Q}].

One can check Wood
[
21
]
for more details.
Since
\Gamma
is nonempty and contains elements other than
1
, it is clear that
\beta<\alpha
, where

\beta:=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}g\in G\backslash\Gamma\\
g\neq 1\end{subarray}}[\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{r(g)}):\mathbb{Q}]

as in Theorem
6.1
.
So the corollary follows immediately.
∎

Also we get the desired result on distribution of class groups.

Proof of Theorem
1.12
.

Recall that
H
is some subgroup of
G
, and
q
is a rational prime such that
q^{l}\mid\lvert G/H\rvert
with
l\geq 1
.
Apply the above corollary to

\Omega_{q^{l}}:=\{g\in G\mid e(g)\equiv 0\bmod{q^{l}}\},

and we see that the Hypothesis
1.10
holds for
(\mathcal{S},P,\Omega_{q^{l}})
, and Theorem
1.12
just follows Theorem
4.2
and
4.3
.
∎

In the rest of this section, let’s prove Theorem
6.1
.
Using Class Field Theory, we know that there exists a one-to-one correspondence

\operatorname{Sur}(\operatorname{C}_{\mathbb{Q}},G)\leftrightarrow\operatorname{Sur}(\prod_{p\nmid\infty}\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{*},G).

Here
\operatorname{C}_{K}
means the idèles class group of the number field
K
.
Recall from Definition
5.6
that we have a counting function
P
for
\rho:\prod_{p\nmid\infty}\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{*}\to G
.
In particular, if
\chi:\operatorname{C}_{\mathbb{Q}}\to G
is a continuous homomorphism, just define

P(\chi):=P(\prod_{p\nmid\infty}\chi_{p}).

Moreover, if
\chi
corresponds to a class field
K
, then

P(K)=P(\chi).

So in this case, the product of ramified primes of a homomorphism
\rho
is consistent with the one for fields.
Now that the homomorphisms are equipped with a counting function, we can proving the theorem of this section.

Proof of Theorem
6.1
.

Write
m:=\lvert G\rvert
.
If
p\nmid m
, then the weight function
c(\rho_{p})
(see Definition
5.6
) is totally determined by
\rho_{p}(\zeta_{p})
where
\zeta_{p}
here means the generator of the group of roots of unity in
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{*}
.
So for each rational prime
p
, we can give the following local condition in the form of a set

\mathcal{T}_{p}:=\{\rho:\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{*}\to G\mid\rho(\zeta_{p})\in\Gamma\}.

Let
\mathcal{T}_{p}^{c}
be the complement
\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{*},G)-\mathcal{T}_{p}
, and let

E_{\mathcal{T}_{\Gamma}^{r},P}(s):=\prod_{p}\bigl(\sum_{\rho\in\mathcal{T}_{p}^{c}}p^{-c(\rho)s}\bigr)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\text{ square-free}\\
\gcd(m,n)=1,\omega(n)=r\end{subarray}}\prod_{p\mid n}\bigl(\sum_{\rho\in\mathcal{T}_{p}}p^{-c(\rho)s}\bigr),

be the series defined as in (
5.1
).
If we write it as a Dirichlet series
E_{\mathcal{T}_{\Gamma}^{r},P}(s)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}b_{n}n^{-s}
, then by Proposition
5.7
, for all
X>0
, we have

N_{\mathcal{S}_{\Gamma}^{r},P}(X)\leq\sum_{n<X}b_{n}.

It is clear that the size of
\mathcal{T}_{p}
is totally determined by
p
mod
m
.
In other words, if we define

f_{1}(p):=\#\{\rho\in\mathcal{T}_{p}\mid c(\rho)=1\}\quad\text{and}\quad f_{2}(p):=\#\{\rho\notin\mathcal{T}_{p}\mid c(\rho)=1\}

then
f_{i}(p)=f_{i}(q)
whenever
p\equiv q\bmod{m}
, where
i=1,2
. And we see that

E_{\mathcal{T}_{\Gamma}^{r},P}(s)=E_{P}(f_{1},f_{2},s)

as defined in (
5.4
).
The conditions of Proposition
5.8
are all satisfied, and we have

N_{\mathcal{S}_{\Gamma}^{r},P}(X)\ll X(\log X)^{\beta^{\prime}-1}(\log\log X)^{r}.

where

\beta^{\prime}=\frac{1}{\phi(m)}\sum_{a\in(\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z})^{*}}f_{1}(a).

It suffices to show that
\beta^{\prime}=\beta
.
For each
n\mid m
, let
\pi_{n}:(\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z})^{*}\to(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})^{*}
be the surjective homomorphism between multiplicative groups.
Moreover we view
f_{1}
as a function defined on
(\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z})^{*}
.
Then we have the following computation:

\displaystyle\beta^{\prime}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\phi(m)}\sum_{a\in(\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z})^{*}}f_{1}(a)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\phi(m)}\sum_{a\in(\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z})^{*}}\#\{1\neq g\in G\backslash\Gamma\mid\pi_{r(g)}(a)=1\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\phi(m)}\sum_{1\neq g\in G\backslash\Gamma}\#\pi_{r(g)}^{-1}(1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\phi(m)}\sum_{1\neq g\in G\backslash\Gamma}\frac{\phi(m)}{\phi(r(g))}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{1\neq g\in G\backslash\Gamma}\phi(r(g))^{-1}=\beta.

And we are done for the proof.
∎

7.
D_{4}
extensions

In this section, let
D_{4}=\langle\sigma,\tau\mid\sigma^{4}=1=\tau^{2},\tau^{-1}\sigma\tau=\sigma^{3}\rangle
be the dihedral group of order
8
.
Define
\mathcal{S}:=\mathcal{S}(D_{4})
with
D_{4}
being a subgroup of
S_{4}
, i.e., we are focused on the quartic extensions whose Galois closure are
D_{4}
-fields (see also Definition
1.1
and

).

7.1.
Main results

We first introduce the definition of the
conductor
for a quadratic extension of a quadratic number field, which will be used here as the counting function of the number fields.

Definition 7.1

(Conductor)
.

If
K
is a quadratic number field and
L
is a quadratic extension of
K
,
define the
conductor
of the pair
(L,K)
as

C(L,K):=\frac{\operatorname{Disc}(L)}{\operatorname{Disc}(K)}.

If
L
is a
D_{4}
-field and
K
denotes its (unique) quadratic subfield, then
C(L,K)=C(L)
(the conductor of
L
).

Note that the notation given by the above definition agrees with the Artin conductor for the irreducible
2
-dimensional representation of
D_{4}
, if the quartic field has
D_{4}
-Galois closure.
See also
[
1
, Section 2.3]
for details.
We here follow this definition for the convenience of both computation and generalization to other quartic fields.
Define the following notation:

H_{\mathcal{S},C}(X):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}L\in\mathcal{S}\\
C(L)<X\end{subarray}}\lvert\operatorname{Hom}(\operatorname{Cl}_{L},C_{2})\rvert.

In this section we are going to prove the following main result.

Theorem 7.2
.

We have

\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{S},C}(\lvert\operatorname{Hom}(\operatorname{Cl}_{L},C_{2})\rvert)=\lim_{X\to\infty}\frac{H_{\mathcal{S},C}(X)}{N_{\mathcal{S},C}(X)}=+\infty,

where
C
is the conductor defined above.

7.2.
Preliminary tools

We want to study the statistical behaviour of
\operatorname{Cl}_{L}[2^{\infty}]
where
L\in\mathcal{S}
for the conductor.
Let’s first prove a lemma whose statement is similar to
[
1
, Lemma 5.1]
.
Recall that
\omega(n)
means the number of distinct prime factors dividing
n
, and
\mu(n)
is the Möbius function, and
\left(\dfrac{\cdot}{\cdot}\right)
is the Legendre symbol.

Lemma 7.3
.

For any
0<\epsilon<\frac{1}{2}
, we have

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0<D<X\\
D\text{ squarefree}\end{subarray}}\frac{2^{\omega(D)}}{D}\cdot\Biggl(\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n=1\\
mn\neq\square\end{subarray}}^{D^{\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon}}\frac{\mu(m)}{m^{2}n}\Bigl(\frac{D}{mn}\Bigr)\Biggr)=o(X),

as
X\to\infty
.

Proof.

The proof itself is straightforward.
First of all, by comparing with
\zeta(s)^{2}
, we see that the Euler product

\prod_{p\text{ rational prime}}(1+2p^{-s})

admits a meromorphic continuation to the closed half-plane
\Re(s)\geq 1
with a unique pole of order
2
at
s=1
.
Then, according to
[
14
, Theorem 5.11]
, we have

\sum_{n<X}\frac{1}{n}\sim c_{1}\log X\quad\text{and}\quad\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0<D<X\\
D\text{ squarefree}\end{subarray}}\frac{2^{\omega(D)}}{D}\sim c_{2}(\log X)^{2}

for some constant
c_{1},c_{2}>0
.
So, we know that

\displaystyle\Big\lvert\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0<D<X\\
D\text{ squarefree}\end{subarray}}\frac{2^{\omega(D)}}{D}\cdot\Bigl(\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\sum^{D^{\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon}}_{\begin{subarray}{c}n=1\\
mn\neq\square\end{subarray}}\frac{\mu(m)}{m^{2}n}\bigl(\frac{D}{mn}\bigr)\Bigr)\Big\rvert

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0<D<X\\
D\text{ squarefree}\end{subarray}}\frac{2^{\omega(D)}}{D}\cdot\Bigl(\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\sum_{n=1}^{X}\frac{1}{m^{2}n}\Bigr)

\displaystyle\ll

\displaystyle(\log X)^{3}=o(X),\text{ as }X\to\infty.

∎

Using the above lemma, we can prove the following proposition, which is similar to
[
1
, Proposition 5.2]
.

Proposition 7.4
.

We have:

(7.1)

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}[K:\mathbb{Q}]=2\\
0<\operatorname{Disc}(K)<X\end{subarray}}\frac{L(1,K/\mathbb{Q})}{L(2,K/\mathbb{Q})}\cdot\frac{2^{\omega(\operatorname{Disc}(K))}}{\operatorname{Disc}(K)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}[K:\mathbb{Q}]=2\\
0<\operatorname{Disc}(K)<X\end{subarray}}\frac{2^{\omega(\operatorname{Disc}(K))}}{\operatorname{Disc}(K)}\cdot\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0<a,b<\infty\\
(\operatorname{Disc}(K),ab)=1\end{subarray}}\frac{\mu(a)}{a^{3}b^{2}}+o(X),

\displaystyle\text{and }

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}[K:\mathbb{Q}]=2\\
-X<\operatorname{Disc}(K)<0\end{subarray}}\frac{L(1,K/\mathbb{Q})}{L(2,K/\mathbb{Q})}\cdot\frac{2^{\omega(\operatorname{Disc}(K))}}{\operatorname{Disc}(K)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}[K:\mathbb{Q}]=2\\
-X<\operatorname{Disc}(K)<0\end{subarray}}\frac{2^{\omega(\operatorname{Disc}(K))}}{\operatorname{Disc}(K)}\cdot\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0<a,b<\infty\\
(\operatorname{Disc}(K),ab)=1\end{subarray}}\frac{\mu(a)}{a^{3}b^{2}}+o(X),

as
X\to\infty
, where
L(s,K/\mathbb{Q})=\sum_{n}\frac{\chi_{K}(n)}{n^{s}}
and
\chi_{K}
is the quadratic character associated to
K
.

Proof.

The proof is similar to that of
[
1
, Proposition 5.2]
.
According to
[
1
, (17)]
, we have

\frac{L(1,K/\mathbb{Q})}{L(2,K/\mathbb{Q})}=\frac{1}{L(2,K/\mathbb{Q})}\cdot\sum_{n=1}^{\lvert\operatorname{Disc}(K)\rvert^{\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon}}\frac{\chi_{K}(n)}{n}+O_{\epsilon}\Biggl(\frac{\log(\lvert\operatorname{Disc}(K)\rvert)}{\lvert\operatorname{Disc}(K)\rvert^{\epsilon}}\Biggr).

Let’s rewrite the left-hand sides of (
7.1
) as

(7.2)

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}[K:\mathbb{Q}]=2\\

8.
Dihedral extensions in general

In this section, we focus on the case when the Galois group
G
is a Dihedral group.
We use the following notation to explain what we mean by dihedral extensions in this section.

Definition 8.1
.

Let
G
be a finite group.
We call
G
a dihedral group if there exists a split short exact sequence

1\to C_{m}\to G\to C_{2}\to 1,

of groups, where
C_{m}
is the cyclic group of order
m
, and
m\geq 3
is an integer, such that for the generator
g_{2}\in C_{2}
and for a generator
g_{m}\in C_{m}
we have
g_{2}g_{m}g_{2}^{-1}=g_{m}^{-1}
.
In this case we just write
G=D_{m}

In this section, let
m
be a fixed integer
\geq 3
.
Define

\mathcal{S}:=\{L\in\mathcal{S}(D_{m})\mid S^{1}\cap\hat{L}=\{\pm 1\}\},

where
\hat{L}
is the Galois closure of
L
.
See also Definition
1.1
and
1.2
.
Roughly speaking, we only consider the fields whose group of roots of unity is the same as the base field.
For example, the closure
\hat{L}
cannot contain
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-1})
or
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-3})
as a subfield.
We present our main result of this section as follows.
Recall that for an element
g\in G
, let
r(g)
be its order.

Theorem 8.2
.

Let
m=q^{l}
, where
q
is an odd prime and
l
is a positive integer.
Let
\Omega=C_{m}\backslash\{1\}
be the non-empty subset of
D_{m}
.
Assume that the Cohen-Lenstra Heuristics holds for quadratic number fields,
or to be precise, assume that as
X\to\infty
we have

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}K_{2}=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d})\\
P(K_{2})<X\end{subarray}}\lvert\operatorname{Hom}(\operatorname{Cl}_{K_{2}},C_{m})\rvert\ll X.

Then for each
r=1,2,\dots
, we have

N_{\mathcal{S}_{\Omega}^{r},P}(X)\ll X(\log\log X)^{r+1},

as
X\to\infty
.

When
m=4
, we are looking at
D_{4}
-fields, but this time they are ordered by the product of ramified primes.
And we can show the following on field-counting.

Theorem 8.3
.

Let
m=4
, and let
\Omega:=\{g_{4},g_{4}^{3}\}
where
g_{4}
is the generator of
C_{4}
.
Then for each non-negative integer
r
, we have that

N_{\mathcal{S}_{\Omega}^{r},P}(X)\ll X(\log X)^{2}(\log\log X)^{r+1}.

We’ll prove these two theorems later in this section.
Although in general, the moments of class groups are unknown even for quadratic fields, we still have some proven results.
And let’s see an example.

Example 8.4
.

Let
m=3
, the group
D_{3}
is just
S_{3}
.
And let
\Omega=\{(123),(132)\}
.
The set
\mathcal{S}_{\Omega}^{r}
means the set of non-Galois cubic fields such that its Galois closure contains only

as its group of roots of unity and such that it has exactly

and

By Davenport and Heilbronn

thereby the conditions of Theorem

So as

First we summarize the properties we need from Class Field Theory and group cohomology as the preliminaries.
For a number field

\mathscr{O}_{p}:=\prod_{\mathfrak{p}\mid p}\mathscr{O}_{\mathfrak{p}}=\mathscr{O}_{K}\otimes\mathbb{Z}_{p},

Clearly, since

Proposition 8.7
.

Let
G
be a finite group and let
K
be a
G
-field.

(1)

Let
A
be a finite

-module.
There is a one-to-one correspondence between

\operatorname{Sur}_{G}(\operatorname{C}_{K},A)\leftrightarrow\{L\in\mathcal{S}(A,K)\mid L\text{ is Galois over }\mathbb{Q}\text{ and }G(L/\mathbb{Q})\in\operatorname{EXT}(G,A)\}.

(2)

If
m
is odd, equip

with the

-action by
g_{2}\cdot h=h^{-1}
where
g_{2}
is the generator of
C_{2}
and
h\in C_{m}
.
Then

H^{2}(C_{2},C_{m})=0.

Sketch of the proof.

(1):
Note that Class Field Theory says that if
L/K
is an abelian
A
-extension, then

\operatorname{C}_{K}/\operatorname{Nm}_{L/K}\operatorname{C}_{L}\cong A.

In addition

is Galois if and only if
\operatorname{Nm}_{L/K}\operatorname{C}_{L}
is a well-defined

-module.
The “only if” part is easy to see.

Since the isomorphism defines the action of

(2):
This is a corollary of the standard result:

is finite and

See

(8.1)

\mathcal{T}_{K}:=\operatorname{Hom}_{C_{2}}(J_{K}^{S_{\infty}},C_{m}),

8.5
), we see that

N_{\mathcal{S}_{\Omega}^{r},P}(X)\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}[K:\mathbb{Q}]=2\\
P(K)<X\end{subarray}}\lvert\operatorname{Hom}(\operatorname{Cl}_{K},C_{m})\rvert\sum_{n<X/P(K)}a_{n}.

Note that
E_{\mathcal{T}_{\Omega}^{r},P}(s)
is independent of the choice of
K
.
Let

H(X):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}[K:\mathbb{Q}]=2\\
P(K)<X\end{subarray}}\lvert\operatorname{Hom}(\operatorname{Cl}_{K},C_{m})\rvert\quad\text{and}\quad A(X):=\sum_{n<X}a_{n}.

The asymptotic behaviour of
A(X)
could be deduced from Proposition
5.8
.
Recall that we have assumed that the Cohen-Lenstra Heuristics is true, or in this case, we take the following result as our condition:
as
X\to\infty
, we have

H(X)\ll X.

In terms of Riemann-Stieltjes integral (see Montgomery and Vaughan
[
14
, Appendix A]
for example), as
X\to\infty
, we have

\displaystyle N_{\mathcal{S}_{\Omega}^{r},P}(X)\ll

\displaystyle\sum_{n<X}a_{n}\frac{X}{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{1^{+}}^{X}\frac{X}{t}\operatorname{d}A(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{X}{t}A(t)\Big|_{1^{+}}^{X}+X\int_{1^{+}}^{X}\frac{A(t)}{t^{2}}\operatorname{d}t

\displaystyle\ll

\displaystyle X(\log\log X)^{r+1}.

And the result just follows.
∎

Then we deal with Theorem
8.3
.
We use the similar method as above to prove it.

Proof of Theorem
8.3
.

The proof is similar to that of Theorem
8.2
, except that in this case we have the following

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}K=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d})\\
P(K)<X\end{subarray}}\lvert\operatorname{Hom}(\operatorname{Cl}_{K},C_{4})\rvert\sim c_{2}X\log X,

as
X\to\infty
,
where
c_{2}
is a positive constant.
In other words, “most” homomorphisms are not surjective, and the main term is determined by the
2
-rank of
\operatorname{Cl}_{K}
.
See Alex Smith
[
19
]
for details on the distribution of
\operatorname{Cl}_{K}[2^{\infty}]
when
K
runs over quadratic number fields.
Again, using the notations of
\mathcal{T}_{K_{p},\Omega}
and
\mathcal{T}_{K_{p},\Omega}^{c}
and
\mathcal{T}_{K,\Omega}^{r}
from the proof of Theorem
8.2
, one can define the series
E_{\mathcal{T}_{K,\Omega}^{r},P}(s)
as in (
8.2
).
Write it as in the form of a Dirichlet series
E_{\mathcal{T}_{K,\Omega}^{r},P}(s)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}b_{K,n}n^{-s}
.
Note that the inequality

N_{\mathcal{S}_{\Omega}^{r},P}(X)\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}[K:\mathbb{Q}]=2\\
P(K)<X\end{subarray}}\lvert\operatorname{Hom}(\operatorname{Cl}_{K},C_{m})\rvert\sum_{n<X}b_{K,n}

is still true in this case by the proof of Theorem
8.2
.
Define in this case the series

E_{\mathcal{T}_{\Omega}^{r},P}(s)=\prod_{p\mid 2\infty}(1+Np^{-s})\prod_{p\nmid 2\infty}(1+p^{-s})\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\text{ square-free}\\
\omega(n)=r\end{subarray}}\prod_{p\mid n}(1+Np^{-s}),

where
N
is a large enough number such that

N\geq\max_{[K:\mathbb{Q}]=2}\max_{p}\{\#\operatorname{Hom}_{C_{2}}(\mathscr{O}_{p}^{*},C_{4})\}.

Write it also in the form of a Dirichlet series:
E_{\mathcal{T}_{\Omega}^{r},P}(s)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}n^{-s}
.
The comparison between
E_{\mathcal{T}_{K,\Omega}^{r},P}(s)
and
E_{\mathcal{T}_{\Omega}^{r},P}(s)
shows that for each quadratic number field and for each
n\geq 1
, we have that

a_{n}\geq b_{K,n}.

So we have that

N_{\mathcal{S}_{\Omega}^{r},P}(X)\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}[K:\mathbb{Q}]=2\\
P(K)<X\end{subarray}}\lvert\operatorname{Hom}(\operatorname{Cl}_{K},C_{m})\rvert\sum_{n<X/P(K)}a_{n}.

Note that
E_{\mathcal{T}_{\Omega}^{r},P}(s)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}n^{-s}
is independent of the choice of
K
.
And the asymptotic behaviour of
\sum_{n<X}a_{n}
could be deduced from Proposition
5.8
.
So it reduces to the following computation.
Write

A(X):=\sum_{n<X}a_{n}\quad\text{and}\quad H(X):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}[K:\mathbb{Q}]=2\\
P(K)<X\end{subarray}}\lvert\operatorname{Hom}(\operatorname{Cl}_{K},C_{4})\rvert.

Let
M
be a fixed constant such that for all
X>M
we have

H(X)\leq 2c_{2}X\log X\quad\text{and}\quad A(X)\leq d_{r}X(\log\log X)^{r},

where
d_{r}
is a positive constant.
The existence of such a number
M
follows from our assumption on
H(X)
and the asymptotic behaviour of
A(X)
.
Then for large
X
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}[K:\mathbb{Q}]\\
P(K)<X\end{subarray}}\lvert\operatorname{Hom}(\operatorname{Cl}_{K},C_{4})\rvert\sum_{n<X/P(K)}a_{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n<X}a_{n}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}[K:\mathbb{Q}]=2\\
P(K)<X/n\end{subarray}}\lvert\operatorname{Hom}(\operatorname{Cl}_{K},C_{4})\rvert

\displaystyle\ll

\displaystyle\sum_{X/M\leq n<X}a_{n}H(X/n)+\sum_{n<X/M}a_{n}\cdot\frac{X}{n}\log X

\displaystyle\ll

\displaystyle\sum_{n<X}a_{n}+\int_{1^{+}}^{X/M}\frac{X}{t}\log X\operatorname{d}A(t)

\displaystyle\ll

\displaystyle X(\log\log X)^{r}+\frac{X}{t}\log XA(t)\Big|_{1^{+}}^{X/M}+X\log X\int_{1^{+}}^{X/M}\frac{A(t)}{t^{2}}\operatorname{d}t

\displaystyle\ll

\displaystyle X\log X(\log\log X)^{r}+X\log X\cdot\Biggl[\Bigl(\int_{1^{+}}^{M}+\int_{M}^{X/M}\Bigr)\frac{A(t)}{t^{2}}\operatorname{d}t\Biggr]

\displaystyle\ll

\displaystyle X(\log X)^{2}(\log\log X)^{r+1}.

∎

Remark
.

Note that the computation under some heuristics including those of Cohen-Lenstra and Malle-Bhargava shows that when ordered by the conductor
C
introduced in Section
7
for
D_{4}
-fields, it is not likely that we can prove a result of zero-probability of
\operatorname{rk}_{2}2\operatorname{Cl}_{L}\leq r
.
Though even the heuristics hold in this case, the theories we’ve introduced here can only imply that

\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{S},C}(\lvert\operatorname{Hom}(2\operatorname{D}^{D_{4}}_{L},C_{2})\rvert)<\infty,

where
\operatorname{D}_{L}^{D_{4}}\subseteq\operatorname{Cl}_{L}
(see also Definition
3.4
).
Still results of this section indicates that ordering fields by the product of ramified primes keeps some possibility for us to prove the statistical results related to the infinite
C_{2}
-moments on the higher torsion part of the class group.

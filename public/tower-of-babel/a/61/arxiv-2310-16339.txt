Exponential relaxation to equilibrium for a kinetic Fokker-Planck-Alignment equation with force Thanks: Acknowledgment. The author would like to thank Professor Roman Shvydkoy for useful discussions and acknowledge partial support from NSF grant DMS-2107956 (PI: Roman Shvydkoy).
Vinh Nguyen

Abstract
. In this note, we consider a kinetic Fokker-Planck-Alignment equation with Rayleigh-type friction and self-propulsion force which is derived from general environmental averaging models. We show the exponential relaxation in time toward equilibrium of the solutions provided certain spectral gap conditions are satisfied. The result is proved by using Desvillettes-Villani’s method for collisional models to establish the global hypocoercivity.

35Q84, 35Q92, 92D25

1.
Introduction

In this note, we are interested in a kinetic Fokker-Plank-Alignment equation which is derived from general environmental averaging models. More specifically, let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a periodic domain. An agent is featured by its position
x\in\Omega
and its velocity
v\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. The density of agents who has position
x
and velocity
v
at time
t\geqslant 0
, denoted by
f=f(x,v,t)
, is governed by the following equation:

(1)

\partial_{t}f+v\cdot\nabla_{x}f=s_{\rho}\big[\Delta_{v}f+\nabla_{v}\cdot\big(\big[(v-[u]_{\rho})+F(v)\big]f\big)\big],

subject to the initial condition

f(x,v,0)=f_{0}(x,v).

Here
\rho
and
u
are macroscopic density and macroscopic velocity defined by

(2)

\rho(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x,v)\,\mbox{d}v,\quad u\rho(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}vf(x,v)\,\mbox{d}v.

The family of pairs
(\kappa_{\rho},[\cdot]_{\rho})
with
\,\mbox{d}\kappa_{\rho}:=s_{\rho}\,\mbox{d}\rho
satisfies the conditions for a material environmental averaging model introduced in
[
10
]
.
The Rayleigh-type friction and self-propulsion force
F
is given by

(3)

F(v)=\dfrac{\sigma(|v|^{p}-1)v}{\eta(|v|)},

where
\eta:\mathbb{R}_{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{+}
is a smooth, positive and increasing function satisfying

(4)

\eta(z)=1\text{ if }z\leqslant R\text{ for some }R>0;\text{ and }\eta(z)\sim z^{q}\text{ for some }q>p\text{ as }z\rightarrow\infty.

Our goal is to show that the solution of (
1
) relaxes exponentially fast toward its equilibrium. We utilize the Desvillettes-Villani’s method (see
[
5
,
13
]
) for collisional models to modify the entropy and establish a global hypocoercivity. Without additional force, Shvydkoy gave the first result on global hypocoercivity for this type of model in
[
9
]
. In that paper, the averaging operator is given by

[u]_{\rho}:=\phi*\big(\frac{\phi*(u\rho)}{\phi*\rho}\big),

where
\phi
is a radial non-negative non-increasing function satisfying

\int_{\Omega}\phi(x)\,\mbox{d}x=1,\quad\phi(x)\geqslant c_{0}{\mathds{1}}_{\{|x|<r_{0}\}}.

Then the result was extended to a class of kinetic equations in
[
10
]
. In this work, we show that if an extra force is added then we still have a global hypocoercivity and hence, an exponential relaxation to equilibrium provided that the force is small in the sense of assumption (iv) below.

Before stating our result, let us give some motivation for studying the equation (
1
). The study of collective behavior has attracted a lot of attention from the scientific community because it has diverse applications ranging from biology, physics, computer science, social science etc., see e.g.
[
2
,
11
,
12
,
14
]
and the references therein.

For microscopic descriptions, many models of collective behavior can be described as follows:

(5)

\begin{cases}\dot{x}_{i}=v_{i},\quad\quad(x_{i},v_{i})\in\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{n},\\
\dot{v}_{i}=s_{i}([v]_{i}-v_{i})+F_{i},\quad i=1,\ldots,N,\end{cases}

where
s_{i},F_{i}
are respectively the communication strength and the force corresponding to the
i
-th agent;
v=(v_{1},\ldots,v_{N})\mathbb{R}^{nN}
and
[v]_{i}
denotes the averaging operator acts on the
i
-th agent. The celebrated Cucker-Smale system
[
3
,
4
]
can be written in form (
5
) with

(6)

s_{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{N}m_{j}\phi(|x_{i}-x_{j}|),\qquad[v]_{i}=\dfrac{\sum_{j=1}^{N}m_{j}\phi(|x_{i}-x_{j}|)v_{j}}{\sum_{j=1}^{N}m_{j}\phi(|x_{i}-x_{j}|)},

where
\phi
is a smooth radial non-increasing function,
m_{i}
is the communication weight of the
i-
th agent. In this model
F_{i}=0
. For examples with nontrivial force
F_{i}
, the readers can see
[
7
,
8
,
11
]
. If we take
F_{i}
in (
5
) to be the combination of a deterministic force and a noise of the form

(7)

F_{i}=\dfrac{\sigma(1-|v_{i}|^{p})v_{i}}{\eta(|v_{i}|)}+\sqrt{2s_{i}(x)}\dot{B}_{i},\quad 0<\sigma<1\text{ and }p>0,

here
\eta
is given by (
4
) and
B_{i}^{\prime}s
are independent Brownian motions in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, then the stochastic mean-field limit of (
5
) formally leads to the kinetic equation (
1
).

In this short note, we will merely focus on the long-time behavior of the solution of (
1
) provided it exists. For a rigorous derivation of (
1
) via stochastic mean-field limit one can consult the scheme from
[
1
,
10
]
. For the existence of solution, we refer to
[
1
,
6
,
10
]
. We assume the solution
f
to (
1
) belongs to some weighted Sobolev space

H^{k}_{l}(\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{n}):=\left\{f:\sum_{k^{\prime}\leqslant k}\sum_{|\alpha|=k^{\prime}}\int_{\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left\langle v\right\rangle^{l+2(k-k^{\prime})}|\partial^{\alpha}_{x,v}f|^{2}\,\mbox{d}x\,\mbox{d}v<\infty\right\},

where
\left\langle v\right\rangle=\sqrt{1+|v|^{2}}
and
\alpha
denotes a multiindex.

Next let us introduce some more notations. Letting
G:\mathbb{R}_{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be the function defined by

G(z):=\int_{0}^{z}\dfrac{\sigma(y^{p+1}-y)}{\eta(y)}\mbox{ d}y,

and letting

(8)

V(v)=\dfrac{|v|^{2}}{2}+G(|v|).

Then the gradient and Hessian matrix of
V
can be computed explicitly,

(9)

\displaystyle\nabla V

\displaystyle=v+F(v),

(10)

\displaystyle\nabla^{2}V

\displaystyle=\left(1+\frac{\sigma(|v|^{p}-1)}{\eta(|v|)}\right)\mathbb{I}+\frac{\sigma|v|^{p}}{\eta(|v|)}\frac{v}{|v|}\otimes\frac{v}{|v|}-\frac{\sigma(|v|^{p}-1)|v|\eta^{\prime}(|v|)}{\eta^{2}(|v|)}\frac{v}{|v|}\otimes\frac{v}{|v|},

where
\mathbb{I}
is the identity matrix.

Remark 1.1
.

By the assumption (
4
) and the identity (
10
) we see that the Hessian matrix of
V
is bounded. Thus, there exists a positive constant
\Lambda
such that

(11)

|(\nabla^{2}V)(y)|\leqslant\Lambda|y|,\quad\forall y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

We also note that for
y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

(12)

y^{T}(\nabla^{2}V)y\geqslant\left(1-\frac{\sigma}{\eta(|v|)}-\frac{\sigma|v|^{p+1}\eta^{\prime}(|v|)}{\eta^{2}(|v|)}\right)|y|^{2}\geqslant\lambda|y|^{2},

where
\lambda>0
is a constant depending on
\sigma
.

We expect that the solution to (
1
) converges to

(13)

f_{\infty}:=\dfrac{1}{Z}e^{-V(v)}\quad\text{ with }Z=\int_{{\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}}e^{-V(v)}\,\mbox{d}v\,\mbox{d}x.

The macroscopic field
u_{F}
is defined by

\rho u_{F}(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}F(v)f(x,v)\,\mbox{d}v.

Denote
L^{2}(\kappa_{\rho}):=L^{2}(\mbox{d}\kappa_{\rho})
. The inner product in
L^{2}(\kappa_{\rho})
is denoted by
\left\langle\cdot,\cdot\right\rangle_{\kappa_{\rho}}
. Our main result is the following:

Theorem 1.2
.

Suppose that
f\in H^{k}_{l}({\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{n}})
is a solution to (
1
) such that
\rho(t)
satisfies the following assumptions for all
t\geqslant 0
:

(i)

c_{0}\leqslant s_{\rho}\leqslant c_{1}
and
\|\nabla s_{\rho}\|_{\infty}\leqslant c_{2}
, where
c_{0},c_{1},c_{2}
are positive constants,

(ii)

\nabla_{x}(s_{\rho}[\cdot]_{\rho}):L^{2}(\rho)\rightarrow L^{2}(\rho)
is uniformly bounded,

(iii)

there exists a constant
0<\varepsilon_{0}<1
such that

\sup\big\{\left\langle w,[w]_{\rho}\right\rangle_{\kappa_{\rho}}|\;w\in L^{2}(\kappa_{\rho}),\|w\|_{L^{2}(\kappa_{\rho})}=1\big\}\leqslant 1-\varepsilon_{0},

(iv)

there exists a constant
0<\varepsilon_{1}<1
such that

\|u_{F}\|_{L^{2}(\kappa_{\rho})}\leqslant\varepsilon_{1}\|u\|_{L^{2}(\kappa_{\rho})}.

Then
f
converges to
f_{\infty}
exponentially fast:

\|f(t)-f_{\infty}\|_{L^{1}({\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{n}})}\leqslant Ce^{-\delta t},

where
C>0
is a constant depending on initial data
f_{0}
and given parameters;
\delta>0
is a constant depending only on given parameters.

Remark 1.3
.

Observe that in the case of Cucker-Smale model, since
s_{\rho}=\phi*\rho
and
s_{\rho}[u]_{\rho}=\phi*(u\rho)
, condition (ii) holds automatically and condition (i) holds if
\phi*\rho\geqslant\underline{\rho}
for some
\underline{\rho}>0
.

2.
Proof of main result

In this section, we will prove Theorem
1.2
. Firstly, let us introduce some notations and definitions.

2.1.
Notations and preliminaries

The relative entropy is defined by

\mathcal{H}(f|f_{\infty})=\int_{\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}f\log\frac{f}{f_{\infty}}\,\mbox{d}v\,\mbox{d}x.

For our convenient computation, we will derive an equation for
h
satisfying
f=hf_{\infty}
. Plugging this
f
into equation (
1
), we have the following equation for
h
:

(14)

\partial_{t}h+v\cdot\nabla_{x}h=s_{\rho}\big(\Delta_{v}h-\nabla V\cdot\nabla_{v}h+h[u]_{\rho}\cdot\nabla V-[u]_{\rho}\cdot\nabla_{v}h\big).

Letting

A=\nabla_{v},\qquad B=v\cdot\nabla_{x},

and
A^{*}
be the adjoint of
A
with respect to the inner product in the weighted space
L^{2}(\mu)
:

\langle\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2}\rangle=\int_{\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}\varphi_{1}\varphi_{2}\,\mbox{d}\mu,\quad\,\mbox{d}\mu=f_{\infty}\,\mbox{d}v\,\mbox{d}x.

We can calculate
A^{*}
explicitly,

\quad A^{*}=(\nabla V-\nabla_{v})\cdot.

Then we can write (
14
) in the abstract form:

(15)

h_{t}=-s_{\rho}A^{*}Ah-Bh+s_{\rho}A^{*}(h[u]_{\rho}).

Following the notations from the paper
[
10
]
, let us define the partial Fisher information functionals as follows:

\mathcal{I}_{vv}(h)=\int_{\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}\dfrac{|\nabla_{v}h|^{2}}{h}\,\mbox{d}\mu,\quad\mathcal{I}_{xv}(h)=\int_{\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}\dfrac{\nabla_{x}h\cdot\nabla_{v}h}{h}\,\mbox{d}\mu,\quad\mathcal{I}_{xx}(h)=\int_{\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}\dfrac{|\nabla_{x}h|^{2}}{h}\,\mbox{d}\mu.

The full Fisher information is defined by

\mathcal{I}=\mathcal{I}_{vv}+\mathcal{I}_{xx}.

For our convenience we use the notation

(\varphi)_{\mu}:=\int_{\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}\varphi\,\mbox{d}\mu.

Denote
\bar{h}=\log h
and

\mathcal{D}_{vv}=(s_{\rho}h|\nabla^{2}_{v}\bar{h}|^{2})_{\mu},\quad\mathcal{D}_{xv}=(s_{\rho}h|\nabla_{v}\nabla_{x}\bar{h}|^{2})_{\mu},

where
\nabla_{v}^{2}\bar{h}
is the Hessian matrix with respect to
v
of
\bar{h}
. We will use the notations
J_{A},J_{B},J_{u}
to refer to the terms related to the operators
A,B
and related to
u
respectively. They are different in the proof of each lemma in the sequel. We denote by
C,c
positive constants which may vary from line to line.

2.2.
Proof of Theorem
1.2

By the Csiszár-Kullback inequality,

(16)

\|f-f_{\infty}\|^{2}_{L^{1}({\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{n}})}\leqslant c\mathcal{H}.

Therefore, it suffices to show that the entropy function
\mathcal{H}
decays exponentially fast in time.
Using (
1
) and integration by parts, we have

(17)

\frac{\mbox{d\,\,}}{\mbox{d}t}\mathcal{H}=-\int_{{\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}}s_{\rho}\dfrac{|\nabla_{v}f+\nabla Vf|^{2}}{f}\,\mbox{d}v\,\mbox{d}x+\left\langle u_{V},[u]_{\rho}\right\rangle_{\kappa_{\rho}},

where

(18)

u_{V}=u+u_{F}.

Define the partial Fisher information functional
\mathcal{I}_{vv}
by

\mathcal{I}_{vv}=\int_{{\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}}s_{\rho}\dfrac{|\nabla_{v}f+\nabla Vf|^{2}}{f}\,\mbox{d}v\,\mbox{d}x.

By the assumption (i) we have

(19)

\frac{\mbox{d\,\,}}{\mbox{d}t}\mathcal{H}\leqslant-c_{0}\mathcal{I}_{vv}+\left\langle u_{V},[u]_{\rho}\right\rangle_{\kappa_{\rho}}.

We can also rewrite (
17
) in the dissipative form:

(20)

\frac{\mbox{d\,\,}}{\mbox{d}t}\mathcal{H}=-\int_{{\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}}s_{\rho}\dfrac{|\nabla_{v}f+(\nabla V-u_{V})f|^{2}}{f}\,\mbox{d}v\,\mbox{d}x-\|u_{V}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\kappa_{\rho})}+\left\langle u_{V},[u]_{\rho}\right\rangle_{\kappa_{\rho}}.

By the triangle inequality and assumption (iv) we have

(21)

\|u\|_{L^{2}(\kappa_{\rho})}\leqslant\dfrac{1}{1-\varepsilon_{1}}\|u_{V}\|_{L^{2}(\kappa_{\rho})}.

Then by the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, assumptions (iii) and (iv) we have

\displaystyle\left\langle u_{V},[u]_{\rho}\right\rangle_{\kappa_{\rho}}=

\displaystyle\left\langle u_{V},[u_{V}]_{\rho}\right\rangle_{\kappa_{\rho}}-\left\langle u_{V},[u_{F}]_{\rho}\right\rangle_{\kappa_{\rho}}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle(1-\varepsilon_{0})\|u_{V}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\kappa_{\rho})}+\dfrac{\varepsilon_{1}}{1-\varepsilon_{1}}\|u_{V}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\kappa_{\rho})}

(22)

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle(1-c_{3})\|u_{V}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\kappa_{\rho})},

where
c_{3}>0
depending on
\varepsilon_{0},\varepsilon_{1}
. Plugging this inequality into (
20
)
we obtain

(23)

\frac{\mbox{d\,\,}}{\mbox{d}t}\mathcal{H}\leqslant-c_{3}\|u_{V}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\kappa_{\rho})}.

Combining (
19
), (
23
) and (
2.2
) we have

(24)

\frac{\mbox{d\,\,}}{\mbox{d}t}\mathcal{H}\leqslant-\dfrac{c_{0}c_{3}}{1+c_{3}}\mathcal{I}_{vv}-\dfrac{c^{2}_{3}}{1+c_{3}}\|u_{V}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\kappa_{\rho})}\leqslant-c\mathcal{I}_{vv}-c\|u_{V}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\kappa_{\rho})},

where
c>0
depending on
\varepsilon_{0},\varepsilon_{1},c_{0}
.

By (
12
),

f_{\infty}
satisfies a logarithmic Sobolev inequality, see
[
13
]
. Thus, we have

(25)

\mathcal{H}\leqslant c\mathcal{I}.

We have the following three estimates on the time derivative of partial Fisher information functionals. Their proofs will be presented in the next subsection.

Lemma 2.1
.

We have

(26)

\frac{\mbox{d\,\,}}{\mbox{d}t}\mathcal{I}_{vv}(h)\leqslant-2\mathcal{D}_{vv}-\lambda c_{0}\mathcal{I}_{vv}-2\mathcal{I}_{xv}+c\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\kappa_{\rho})},

where
c
is a positive constant depending on
c_{0},c_{1},\lambda,\Lambda
.

Lemma 2.2
.

We have

(27)

\frac{\mbox{d\,\,}}{\mbox{d}t}\mathcal{I}_{xv}\leqslant c\mathcal{I}_{vv}-\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{I}_{xx}+2\mathcal{D}_{vv}+\mathcal{D}_{xv}+c\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\kappa_{\rho})},

where
c
is dependent on
c_{0},c_{1},c_{2},\lambda,\Lambda
.

Lemma 2.3
.

We have

\frac{\mbox{d\,\,}}{\mbox{d}t}\mathcal{I}_{xx}(h)\leqslant c\mathcal{I}_{vv}-\mathcal{D}_{xv}+c\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\kappa_{\rho})},

where
c
is a constant depending on
\lambda,\Lambda
and the parameters in the assumption (i), (ii).

Choosing
\varepsilon>0
small so that if we define

(28)

\tilde{\mathcal{I}}=\mathcal{I}_{vv}+\varepsilon\mathcal{I}_{xv}+\frac{\lambda c_{0}}{c}\mathcal{I}_{xx},

then
\mathcal{I}\sim\tilde{\mathcal{I}}
. Combining three lemmas above and the assumption (iv) we have

(29)

\frac{\mbox{d\,\,}}{\mbox{d}t}\tilde{\mathcal{I}}\leqslant-\lambda c_{0}\mathcal{I}_{vv}-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\mathcal{I}_{xx}+C\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\kappa_{\rho})}\leqslant-\lambda c_{0}\mathcal{I}_{vv}-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\mathcal{I}_{xx}+C\|u_{V}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\kappa_{\rho})}.

From (
24
), (
29
) and (
25
) we can choose a constant
\gamma
such that

(30)

\displaystyle\frac{\mbox{d\,\,}}{\mbox{d}t}(\tilde{\mathcal{I}}+\gamma\mathcal{H})\lesssim-\mathcal{I}\leqslant-\delta(\tilde{\mathcal{I}}+\gamma\mathcal{H}).

Thus, by Grönwall’s inequality we obtain

(31)

\tilde{\mathcal{I}}+\gamma\mathcal{H}\leqslant(\tilde{\mathcal{I}}_{0}+\gamma\mathcal{H}_{0})e^{-\delta t}\leqslant c\mathcal{I}_{0}e^{-\delta t}.

Then we can conclude the theorem.

2.3.
Proof of three technical lemmas

In this subsection, we will give the proofs of three lemmas mentioned previously.

Proof of Lemma
2.1
.

Let us rewrite
\mathcal{I}_{vv}
in the form

\mathcal{I}_{vv}=(\nabla_{v}h\cdot\nabla_{v}\bar{h})_{\mu}.

By chain rule and equation (
15
) we get

\displaystyle\frac{\mbox{d\,\,}}{\mbox{d}t}\mathcal{I}_{vv}=

\displaystyle\,2(\nabla_{v}h_{t}\cdot\nabla_{v}\bar{h})_{\mu}-(|\nabla_{v}\bar{h}|^{2}h_{t})_{\mu}:=J_{A}+J_{B}+J_{u},

where

\displaystyle J_{A}

\displaystyle=-2(s_{\rho}\nabla_{v}A^{*}Ah\cdot\nabla_{v}\bar{h})_{\mu}+(s_{\rho}|\nabla_{v}\bar{h}|^{2}A^{*}Ah)_{\mu},

\displaystyle J_{B}

\displaystyle=-2(\nabla_{v}Bh\cdot\nabla_{v}\bar{h})_{\mu}+(|\nabla_{v}\bar{h}|^{2}Bh)_{\mu},

\displaystyle J_{u}

\displaystyle=2(s_{\rho}\nabla_{v}A^{*}([u]_{\rho}h)\cdot\nabla_{v}\bar{h})_{\mu}-(s_{\rho}|\nabla_{v}\bar{h}|^{2}A^{*}([u]_{\rho}h))_{\mu}.

For notational convenience we will use the Einstein summation convention in the sequel.

We firstly consider the term
J_{A}
. Using the identity

\partial_{v_{i}}(A^{*}Ah)=A^{*}Ah_{v_{i}}+\nabla V_{v_{i}}\cdot\nabla_{v}h,

J_{A}
equals to

\displaystyle-2(s_{\rho}A^{*}Ah_{v_{i}}\bar{h}_{v_{i}})_{\mu}-2(s_{\rho}(\nabla V_{v_{i}}\cdot\nabla_{v}h)\bar{h}_{v_{i}})_{\mu}+(s_{\rho}|\nabla_{v}\bar{h}|^{2}A^{*}Ah)_{\mu}=:J_{A}^{1}+J_{A}^{2}+J_{A}^{3}.

By (
12
) we have

J_{A}^{2}=-2(s_{\rho}h^{-1}(\nabla_{v}h)^{T}\nabla^{2}V\nabla_{v}h)_{\mu}\leqslant-2\lambda(s_{\rho}h^{-1}\nabla_{v}h\cdot\nabla_{v}h)_{\mu}.

Then the assumption (i) in Theorem
1.2
implies that

J_{A}^{2}\leqslant-2\lambda c_{0}\mathcal{I}_{vv}.

By switching
A^{*}
in
J_{A}^{1},J_{A}^{3}
we can write

\begin{split}J_{A}^{1}+J_{A}^{3}=&-2(s_{\rho}Ah_{v_{i}}\cdot A\bar{h}_{v_{i}})_{\mu}+(s_{\rho}A(|\nabla_{v}\bar{h}|^{2})\cdot Ah)_{\mu}\\
=&-2(s_{\rho}hA\bar{h}_{v_{i}}\cdot A\bar{h}_{v_{i}})_{\mu}-2(s_{\rho}\bar{h}_{v_{i}}Ah\cdot A\bar{h}_{v_{i}})_{\mu}+2(s_{\rho}\bar{h}_{v_{i}}A\bar{h}_{v_{i}}\cdot Ah)_{\mu}\\
=&-2(s_{\rho}hA\bar{h}_{v_{i}}\cdot A\bar{h}_{v_{i}})_{\mu}=-2\mathcal{D}_{vv}.\end{split}

Combining the above estimates we obtain

(32)

J_{A}\leqslant-2\mathcal{D}_{vv}-2\lambda c_{0}\mathcal{I}_{vv}.

For the term
J_{B}
, plugging
B=v\cdot\nabla_{x}
into
J_{B}
we have

J_{B}=-2(\nabla_{x}h\cdot\nabla_{v}\bar{h})_{\mu}-2((v\cdot\nabla_{x}h_{v_{i}})\bar{h}_{v_{i}})_{\mu}+(|\nabla_{v}\bar{h}|^{2}v\cdot\nabla_{x}h)_{\mu}.

Using the identity
\bar{h}_{v_{i}}=h_{v_{i}}h^{-1}
and integration by parts, we get

\begin{split}2((v\cdot\nabla_{x}h_{v_{i}})\bar{h}_{v_{i}})_{\mu}=\big(v\cdot\nabla_{x}|h_{v_{i}}|^{2}h^{-1}\big)_{\mu}=\big(|h_{v_{i}}|^{2}h^{-2}v\cdot\nabla_{x}h\big)_{\mu}=(|\nabla_{v}\bar{h}|^{2}v\cdot\nabla_{x}h)_{\mu}.\end{split}

Substituting this into
J_{B}
we yield

(33)

J_{B}=-2\mathcal{I}_{xv}.

For the last term
J_{u}
, we have

\begin{split}J_{u}=&\,2(s_{\rho}\nabla_{v}A^{*}([u]_{\rho}h)\cdot\nabla_{v}\bar{h})_{\mu}-(s_{\rho}|\nabla_{v}\bar{h}|^{2}A^{*}([u]_{\rho}h))_{\mu}\\
=&\,2(s_{\rho}\nabla_{v}(\nabla V\cdot[u]_{\rho}h-[u]_{\rho}\cdot\nabla_{v}h)\cdot\nabla_{v}\bar{h})_{\mu}-(s_{\rho}\nabla_{v}|\nabla_{v}\bar{h}|^{2}\cdot[u]_{\rho}h)_{\mu}\\
=&\,2(s_{\rho}\nabla^{2}V([u]_{\rho}h)\cdot\nabla_{v}\bar{h})_{\mu}+2(s_{\rho}(\nabla V\cdot[u]_{\rho})\nabla_{v}h\cdot\nabla_{v}\bar{h})_{\mu}-2(s_{\rho}\nabla^{2}_{v}h([u]_{\rho})\cdot\nabla_{v}\bar{h})_{\mu}\\
&\,-2(s_{\rho}\nabla_{v}^{2}\bar{h}(\nabla_{v}\bar{h})\cdot[u]_{\rho}h)_{\mu}\\
=&:J_{u}^{1}+J_{u}^{2}+J_{u}^{3}+J_{u}^{4}.\end{split}

Plugging

\bar{h}_{v_{i}v_{j}}=h^{-1}h_{v_{i}v_{j}}-h^{-2}h_{v_{i}}h_{v_{j}}

into
J_{u}^{4}
we get

\begin{split}J_{u}^{4}=&-2(s_{\rho}h^{-1}h_{v_{i}v_{j}}\bar{h}_{v_{j}}[u_{i}]_{\rho}h)_{\mu}+2(s_{\rho}h^{-2}h_{v_{i}}h_{v_{j}}\bar{h}_{v_{j}}[u_{i}]_{\rho}h)_{\mu}\\
=&-2(s_{\rho}\nabla^{2}_{v}h([u]_{\rho})\cdot\nabla_{v}\bar{h})_{\mu}+2(s_{\rho}|\nabla_{v}\bar{h}|^{2}\nabla_{v}h\cdot[u]_{\rho})_{\mu}\\
=&\,J_{u}^{3}+2(s_{\rho}|\nabla_{v}\bar{h}|^{2}\nabla_{v}h\cdot[u]_{\rho})_{\mu}.\end{split}

Therefore,

\begin{split}J_{u}^{2}+J_{u}^{3}+J_{u}^{4}=&\,2(s_{\rho}(\nabla V\cdot[u]_{\rho}h)\nabla_{v}\bar{h}\cdot\nabla_{v}\bar{h})_{\mu}-2(s_{\rho}|\nabla_{v}\bar{h}|^{2}\nabla_{v}h\cdot[u]_{\rho})_{\mu}+2J_{u}^{4}\\
=&\,2(s_{\rho}A^{*}([u]_{\rho}h)|\nabla_{v}\bar{h}|^{2})_{\mu}+2J^{4}_{u}\\
=&\,2(s_{\rho}h[u]_{\rho}\cdot A(|\nabla_{v}\bar{h}|^{2}))_{\mu}+2J^{4}_{u}\\
=&\,4(s_{\rho}h[u]_{\rho}\cdot\nabla_{v}^{2}\bar{h}(\nabla_{v}\bar{h}))_{\mu}+2J^{4}_{u}=0.\end{split}

Thus,

\displaystyle J_{u}=

\displaystyle\,2(s_{\rho}\nabla^{2}V([u]_{\rho}h)\cdot\nabla_{v}\bar{h})_{\mu}=2(s_{\rho}\nabla^{2}V([u]_{\rho})\cdot\nabla_{v}h)_{\mu}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\,2\Lambda c_{1}\|[u]_{\rho}\|_{L^{2}(\kappa_{\rho})}\sqrt{\mathcal{I}_{vv}}\qquad\text{(by (i), \eqref{HessV-bdd} and H\"{o}lder inequality)}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\,c\|u\|_{L^{2}(\kappa_{\rho})}\sqrt{\mathcal{I}_{vv}}

(34)

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\,c\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\kappa_{\rho})}+\lambda c_{0}\mathcal{I}_{vv}\quad(\text{ by Young's inequality)}.

Here the last constant
c
depends on
c_{0},c_{1},\lambda,\Lambda
.

Combining (
32
), (
33
) and (
34
) we have the conclusion of this lemma.
∎

Proof of Lemma
2.2
.

Computing the derivative of
\mathcal{I}_{xv}
with respect to
t
we get

\frac{\mbox{d\,\,}}{\mbox{d}t}\mathcal{I}_{xv}(h)=(\nabla_{x}h_{t}\cdot\nabla_{v}\bar{h})_{\mu}+(\nabla_{x}\bar{h}\cdot\nabla_{v}{h}_{t})_{\mu}-(h_{t}\nabla_{v}\bar{h}\cdot\nabla_{x}\bar{h})_{\mu}=:J_{A}+J_{B}+J_{u},

where

\displaystyle J_{A}=

\displaystyle-(\nabla_{x}(s_{\rho}A^{*}Ah)\cdot\nabla_{v}\bar{h})_{\mu}-(\nabla_{x}\bar{h}\cdot\nabla_{v}(s_{\rho}A^{*}Ah))_{\mu}+(s_{\rho}A^{*}Ah\nabla_{v}\bar{h}\cdot\nabla_{x}\bar{h})_{\mu}=:J_{A}^{1}+J_{A}^{2}+J_{A}^{3},

\displaystyle J_{B}=

\displaystyle-(\nabla_{x}(v\cdot\nabla_{x}h)\cdot\nabla_{v}\bar{h})_{\mu}-(\nabla_{x}\bar{h}\cdot\nabla_{v}(v\cdot\nabla_{x}h))_{\mu}+((v\cdot\nabla_{x}h)\nabla_{v}\bar{h}\cdot\nabla_{x}\bar{h})_{\mu}:=J_{B}^{1}+J_{B}^{2}+J_{B}^{3},

\displaystyle J_{u}=

\displaystyle(\nabla_{x}(s_{\rho}A^{*}([u]_{\rho}h))\cdot\nabla_{v}\bar{h})_{\mu}+(\nabla_{x}\bar{h}\cdot\nabla_{v}(s_{\rho}A^{*}([u]_{\rho}h)))_{\mu}-(s_{\rho}A^{*}([u]_{\rho}h)\nabla_{v}\bar{h}\cdot\nabla_{x}\bar{h})_{\mu}.

Let us firstly estimate
J_{A}
. Switching
A^{*}
and using the identity

\nabla_{v}h_{x_{i}}=\bar{h}_{x_{i}}\nabla_{v}h+h\nabla_{v}\bar{h}_{x_{i}}

we have

\begin{split}J_{A}^{1}&=-(s_{\rho}A^{*}Ah_{x_{i}}\bar{h}_{v_{i}})_{\mu}-((s_{\rho})_{x_{i}}A^{*}Ah\bar{h}_{v_{i}})_{\mu}=-(s_{\rho}\nabla_{v}h_{x_{i}}\cdot\nabla_{v}\bar{h}_{v_{i}})_{\mu}-((s_{\rho})_{x_{i}}\nabla_{v}h\cdot\nabla_{v}\bar{h}_{v_{i}})_{\mu}\\
&=-(s_{\rho}h\nabla_{v}\bar{h}_{x_{i}}\cdot\nabla_{v}\bar{h}_{v_{i}})_{\mu}-(s_{\rho}\bar{h}_{x_{i}}\nabla_{v}h\cdot\nabla_{v}\bar{h}_{v_{i}})_{\mu}-\left(\frac{(s_{\rho})_{x_{i}}}{s_{\rho}^{1/2}}\frac{\nabla_{v}h}{h^{1/2}}\cdot s_{\rho}^{1/2}h^{1/2}\nabla_{v}\bar{h}_{v_{i}}\right)_{\mu}.\end{split}

In view of assumption (i) in Theorem
1.2
,

J_{A}^{1}\leqslant-(s_{\rho}h\nabla_{v}\bar{h}_{x_{i}}\cdot\nabla_{v}\bar{h}_{v_{i}})_{\mu}-(s_{\rho}\bar{h}_{x_{i}}\nabla_{v}h\cdot\nabla_{v}\bar{h}_{v_{i}})_{\mu}+c\sqrt{\mathcal{I}_{vv}\mathcal{D}_{vv}},

where
c>0
is a constant depending on
c_{0},c_{2}
.
Next let us consider
J_{A}^{2}
. Since

\partial_{v_{i}}(A^{*}Ah)=A^{*}Ah_{v_{i}}+\nabla V_{v_{i}}\cdot\nabla_{v}h\text{ and }\nabla_{v}h_{v_{i}}=h\nabla_{v}\bar{h}_{v_{i}}+\bar{h}_{v_{i}}\nabla_{v}h,

we have

\begin{split}J_{A}^{2}=&-(s_{\rho}\bar{h}_{x_{i}}A^{*}A{h}_{v_{i}})_{\mu}-(s_{\rho}\bar{h}_{x_{i}}\nabla V_{v_{i}}\cdot\nabla_{v}h)_{\mu}\\
=&-(s_{\rho}\nabla_{v}\bar{h}_{x_{i}}\cdot\nabla_{v}{h}_{v_{i}})_{\mu}-(s_{\rho}\bar{h}_{x_{i}}\nabla V_{v_{i}}\cdot\nabla_{v}h)_{\mu}\\
=&-(s_{\rho}h\nabla_{v}\bar{h}_{x_{i}}\cdot\nabla_{v}\bar{h}_{v_{i}})_{\mu}-(s_{\rho}\bar{h}_{v_{i}}\nabla_{v}\bar{h}_{x_{i}}\cdot\nabla_{v}h)_{\mu}-(s_{\rho}\nabla_{x}\bar{h}\cdot(\nabla^{2}V)(\nabla_{v}h))_{\mu}.\end{split}

Then

\begin{split}J_{A}^{1}+J_{A}^{2}&\leqslant-(s_{\rho}\nabla_{x}\bar{h}\cdot(\nabla^{2}V)(\nabla_{v}h))_{\mu}-2(s_{\rho}h\nabla_{v}\bar{h}_{x_{i}}\cdot\nabla_{v}\bar{h}_{v_{i}})_{\mu}-(s_{\rho}Ah\cdot A(\nabla_{v}\bar{h}\cdot\nabla_{x}\bar{h}))_{\mu}+c\sqrt{\mathcal{I}_{vv}\mathcal{D}_{vv}}\\
&\leqslant-(s_{\rho}\nabla_{x}\bar{h}\cdot(\nabla^{2}V)(\nabla_{v}h))_{\mu}+2\sqrt{\mathcal{D}_{vv}\mathcal{D}_{xv}}+c\sqrt{\mathcal{I}_{vv}\mathcal{D}_{vv}}-J_{A}^{3}\\
&\leqslant c_{1}\Lambda\sqrt{\mathcal{I}_{vv}\mathcal{I}_{xx}}+2\sqrt{\mathcal{D}_{vv}\mathcal{D}_{xv}}+c\sqrt{\mathcal{I}_{vv}\mathcal{D}_{vv}}-J_{A}^{3}.\end{split}

Thus, combining all the terms of
J_{A}
and applying Young’s inequality we yield

\displaystyle J_{A}

\displaystyle\leqslant c_{1}\Lambda\sqrt{\mathcal{I}_{vv}\mathcal{I}_{xx}}+2\sqrt{\mathcal{D}_{vv}\mathcal{D}_{xv}}+c\sqrt{\mathcal{I}_{vv}\mathcal{D}_{vv}},

(35)

\displaystyle\leqslant c\mathcal{I}_{vv}+\frac{1}{4}\mathcal{I}_{xx}+\frac{3}{2}\mathcal{D}_{vv}+\mathcal{D}_{xv}.

Now we consider
J_{B}
. We have

\displaystyle J_{B}^{2}=

\displaystyle-(\nabla_{x}\bar{h}\cdot\nabla_{x}h)_{\mu}-(\bar{h}_{x_{i}}v_{j}h_{x_{j}v_{i}})_{\mu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\mathcal{I}_{xx}+(\bar{h}_{x_{i}x_{j}}v_{j}h_{v_{i}})_{\mu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\mathcal{I}_{xx}+({h}_{x_{i}x_{j}}v_{j}\bar{h}_{v_{i}})_{\mu}-(\bar{h}_{x_{i}}\bar{h}_{x_{j}}v_{j}h_{v_{i}})_{\mu}=-\mathcal{I}_{xx}-J_{B}^{1}-J_{B}^{3}.

In the last row we used the identity

\bar{h}_{x_{i}x_{j}}=h^{-1}{h}_{x_{i}x_{j}}-\bar{h}_{x_{i}}\bar{h}_{x_{j}}.

It follows that

(36)

J_{B}=-\mathcal{I}_{xx}.

Lastly let us examine
J_{u}
. We have

\begin{split}J_{u}=&\,((s_{\rho})_{x_{i}}A^{*}([u]_{\rho}h)\bar{h}_{v_{i}})_{\mu}+(s_{\rho}A^{*}(([u]_{\rho})_{x_{i}}h)\bar{h}_{v_{i}})_{\mu}+(s_{\rho}A^{*}([u]_{\rho}h_{x_{i}})\bar{h}_{v_{i}})_{\mu}\\
&+(s_{\rho}\bar{h}_{x_{i}}A^{*}([u]_{\rho}h_{v_{i}}))_{\mu}+(s_{\rho}\nabla_{x}\bar{h}\cdot(\nabla^{2}V)([u]_{\rho}h))_{\mu}-(s_{\rho}h[u]_{\rho}\cdot\nabla_{v}(\nabla_{v}\bar{h}\cdot\nabla_{x}\bar{h}))_{\mu}\\
=&\,(h(s_{\rho}[u]_{\rho})_{x_{i}}\cdot\nabla_{v}\bar{h}_{v_{i}})_{\mu}+(s_{\rho}h[u]_{\rho}\bar{h}_{x_{i}}\cdot\nabla_{v}\bar{h}_{v_{i}})_{\mu}+(s_{\rho}h\nabla_{v}\bar{h}_{x_{i}}\cdot[u]_{\rho}\bar{h}_{v_{i}})_{\mu}\\
&+(s_{\rho}\nabla_{x}\bar{h}\cdot(\nabla^{2}V)([u]_{\rho}h))_{\mu}-(s_{\rho}h[u]_{\rho}\cdot\nabla_{v}(\nabla_{v}\bar{h}\cdot\nabla_{x}\bar{h}))_{\mu}\\
=:&\,J_{u}^{1}+J_{u}^{2}+J_{u}^{3}+J_{u}^{4}+J_{u}^{5}.\end{split}

Since

J_{u}^{2}+J_{u}^{3}=(s_{\rho}h[u]_{\rho}\cdot\nabla_{v}(\nabla_{x}\bar{h}\cdot\nabla_{v}\bar{h}))_{\mu}=-J_{u}^{5},

we get

J_{u}=J_{u}^{1}+J_{u}^{4}.

By the assumption (ii) in Theorem
1.2
,

J_{u}^{1}=(h(s_{\rho}[u]_{\rho})_{x_{i}}\cdot\nabla_{v}\bar{h}_{v_{i}})_{\mu}\leqslant c\|u\|_{L^{2}(\kappa_{\rho})}\sqrt{\mathcal{D}_{vv}}.

For
J^{4}_{u}
we use the assumption (i) and (
11
) to get

\begin{split}J^{4}_{u}&=(s_{\rho}\nabla_{x}\bar{h}\cdot(\nabla^{2}V)([u]_{\rho}h))_{\mu}\\
&\leqslant c\|u\|_{L^{2}(\kappa_{\rho})}\sqrt{\mathcal{I}_{xx}},\end{split}

where
c
is a constant depending on
c_{1},\Lambda
. Hence, by Young’s inequality we obtain

(37)

J_{u}\leqslant\frac{1}{4}\mathcal{I}_{xx}+\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{D}_{vv}+c\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\kappa_{\rho})}.

Combining three estimates (
2.3
), (
36
) and (
37
) it implies the conclusion of this lemma.
∎

Proof of Lemma
2.3
.

Computing the derivative of
\mathcal{I}_{xx}(h)
with respect to
t
we get

\frac{\mbox{d\,\,}}{\mbox{d}t}\mathcal{I}_{xx}(h)=2(\nabla_{x}h_{t}\cdot\nabla_{x}\bar{h})_{\mu}-(|\nabla_{x}\bar{h}|^{2}h_{t})_{\mu}=:J_{A}+J_{B}+J_{u},

where

\displaystyle J_{A}=

\displaystyle-2(\nabla_{x}(s_{\rho}A^{*}Ah)\cdot\nabla_{x}\bar{h})_{\mu}+(s_{\rho}|\nabla_{x}\bar{h}|^{2}A^{*}Ah)_{\mu},

\displaystyle J_{B}=

\displaystyle-2(\nabla_{x}(v\cdot\nabla_{x}h)\cdot\nabla_{x}\bar{h})_{\mu}+(|\nabla_{x}\bar{h}|^{2}v\cdot\nabla_{x}h)_{\mu},

\displaystyle J_{u}=

\displaystyle 2(\nabla_{x}(s_{\rho}A^{*}([u]_{\rho}h))\cdot\nabla_{x}\bar{h})_{\mu}-(s_{\rho}|\nabla_{x}\bar{h}|^{2}A^{*}([u]_{\rho}h))_{\mu}.

For
J_{A}
we have

\begin{split}J_{A}&=-2((s_{\rho})_{x_{i}}Ah\cdot A\bar{h}_{x_{i}})_{\mu}-2(s_{\rho}Ah_{x_{i}}\cdot A\bar{h}_{x_{i}})_{\mu}+(s_{\rho}A|\nabla_{x}\bar{h}|^{2}\cdot Ah)_{\mu}=:J_{A}^{1}+J_{A}^{2}+J_{A}^{3}.\end{split}

By the assumption (i) in Theorem
1.2
,

J_{A}^{1}=-2\Big(\dfrac{(s_{\rho})_{x_{i}}}{s_{\rho}^{1/2}}\dfrac{\nabla_{v}h}{h^{1/2}}\cdot s_{\rho}^{1/2}h^{1/2}\nabla_{v}\bar{h}_{x_{i}}\Big)_{\mu}\leqslant c\sqrt{\mathcal{I}_{vv}\mathcal{D}_{xv}}.

Using the identity
\nabla_{v}h_{x_{i}}=h\nabla_{v}\bar{h}_{x_{i}}+\bar{h}_{x_{i}}\nabla_{v}h
, we have

J_{A}^{2}=-2(s_{\rho}h\nabla_{v}\bar{h}_{x_{i}}\cdot\nabla_{v}\bar{h}_{x_{i}})_{\mu}-2(s_{\rho}\bar{h}_{x_{i}}\nabla_{v}h\cdot\nabla_{v}\bar{h}_{x_{i}})_{\mu}=-2\mathcal{D}_{xv}-J_{A}^{3}.

Therefore,

(38)

J_{A}\leqslant c\sqrt{\mathcal{I}_{vv}\mathcal{D}_{xv}}-2\mathcal{D}_{xv}.

We have
J_{B}=0
because

\begin{split}-2(\nabla_{x}(v\cdot\nabla_{x}h)\cdot\nabla_{x}\bar{h})_{\mu}=-2((v\cdot\nabla_{x}h_{x_{i}})h_{x_{i}}h^{-1})_{\mu}=-((v\cdot\nabla_{x}|\nabla_{x}h|^{2}h^{-1})_{\mu}&=-(|\nabla_{x}\bar{h}|^{2}v\cdot\nabla_{x}h)_{\mu}.\end{split}

For
J_{u}
, we have

\begin{split}J_{u}=&\,2(h(s_{\rho}[u]_{\rho})_{x_{i}}\cdot\nabla_{v}\bar{h}_{x_{i}})_{\mu}+2(s_{\rho}h\bar{h}_{x_{i}}[u]_{\rho}\cdot\nabla_{v}\bar{h}_{x_{i}})_{\mu}-(s_{\rho}\nabla_{v}(|\nabla_{x}\bar{h}|^{2})\cdot[u]_{\rho}h)_{\mu}\\
=&\,2(h(s_{\rho}[u]_{\rho})_{x_{i}}\cdot\nabla_{v}\bar{h}_{x_{i}})_{\mu}\\
\leqslant&\,c\|u\|_{L^{2}(\kappa_{\rho})}\sqrt{\mathcal{D}_{xv}}\qquad\text{ (by the assumption (ii) in Theorem \ref{thm-main})}.\end{split}

Combining all the estimates for
J_{A},J_{B}
and
J_{u}
we get

\frac{\mbox{d\,\,}}{\mbox{d}t}\mathcal{I}_{xx}(h)\leqslant c\sqrt{\mathcal{D}_{xv}\mathcal{I}_{vv}}-2\mathcal{D}_{xv}+c\|u\|_{L^{2}(\kappa_{\rho})}\sqrt{\mathcal{D}_{xv}}.

Then by Young’s inequality, the lemma is derived.
∎

Abstract
We present the first analytic 𝒪 ⁡ ( α s ) {\cal O}(\alpha_{s}) -computation at twist- 1 1 , 2 2 of the B ¯ u , d , s → γ \bar{B}_{u,d,s}\to\gamma form factors within the framework of sum rules on the light-cone. These form factors describe the charged decay B ¯ u → γ ​ ℓ − ​ ν ¯ \bar{B}_{u}\to\gamma\ell^{-}\bar{\nu} , contribute to the flavour changing neutral currents B ¯ d , s → γ ​ ℓ + ​ ℓ − \bar{B}_{d,s}\to\gamma\ell^{+}\ell^{-} and serve as inputs to more complicated processes. We provide a fit in terms of a z z -expansion with correlation matrix and extrapolate the form factors to the kinematic endpoint by using the g B ​ B ∗ ​ γ g_{BB^{*}\gamma} couplings as a constraint. Analytic results are available in terms of multiple polylogarithms as ancillary files. We give binned predictions for the B ¯ u → γ ​ ℓ − ​ ν ¯ \bar{B}_{u}\to\gamma\ell^{-}\bar{\nu} branching ratio along with the associated correlation matrix. By comparing with three SCET-computations we extract the inverse moment B B -meson distribution amplitude parameter λ B = 360 ​ ( 110 ) ​ MeV \lambda_{B}=360(110)\textrm{\small MeV} . The uncertainty thereof could be improved by a more dedicated analysis. In passing, we extend the photon distribution amplitude to include quark mass corrections with a prescription for the magnetic vacuum susceptibility, χ q \chi_{q} , compatible with the twist-expansion. The values χ q = 3.21 ​ ( 15 ) ​ GeV − 2 \chi_{q}=3.21(15)\textrm{\small GeV}^{-2} and χ s = 3.79 ​ ( 17 ) ​ GeV − 2 \chi_{s}=3.79(17)\textrm{\small GeV}^{-2} are obtained.

The arguments below go beyond

In the charged case the photon couples to the charged

A_{5\mu}=\bar{q}\gamma_{\mu}\gamma_{5}b

\langle 0|B^{\dagger}(x)|\bar{B}\rangle=e^{-ip_{B}\cdot x}

One then easily finds

(10)

It seems worthwhile to point out that leptonic decays are free from hard-collinear logs of the form

Isidori:2020acz

Thus they can only arise from the point-like contribution which is itself free from

First we discuss

in (
16

As the handling of the

In phenomenology the light-cone OPE has its roots in deep inelastic scattering, which
is an inclusive measurement where the operators on the light-cone are the parton distribution functions

\langle P(p)|\bar{q}(z)\Gamma q(0)|P(p)\rangle

The Fourier transform of the light-cone direction
p^{-}z^{+}
describes the probability of finding a constituent parton of a certain momentum fraction.
The application of the light-cone OPE to hard exclusive processes

is a different matter, as the
light-cone operators correspond to DAs,
e.g.

by the variable

u

We refer the reader
to the technical review

where the connection to conformal symmetry is presented in depth and detail.

In a concrete setting an OPE is associated with factorisation. The light-cone OPE corresponds
to collinear factorisation and is often schematically presented (in momentum space) as

\Pi(q^{2},m_{b})=\sum_{i}T^{(i)}_{H}(q^{2},m_{b},\mu_{F})\otimes\phi_{i}(\mu_{F})\;,

where

a perturbatively calculable hard scattering kernel,

\phi_{i}
a DA,

is the factorisation scale and the sum runs over DAs of increasing twist.
A necessary condition for factorisation to hold is that the
\mu_{F}
-dependence cancels
to the given order in a computation. This corresponds to the absorption of
collinear IR-divergences of the hard kernel into the DAs. We will discuss this explicitly in Sec.

5.4
Extraction of the Inverse Moment
\lambda_{B}
of the
B
-meson DA

As previously mentioned, besides being a toy model for factorisation,
\bar{B}_{u}\to\gamma\ell^{-}\bar{\nu}

is of interest to extract the
B
-meson DA parameters from experiment.
Here, we replace experiment by our computation
which comes with the additional bonus that the
{\cal O}(8\%)
uncertainty of the
CKM matrix element
|V_{\textrm{ub}}|=3.82(24)\cdot 10^{-3}

PDG
is eliminated.
This is also useful as
|V_{\textrm{ub}}|
suffers from some tension between exclusive and inclusive
b\to u
transitions
PDG
.

Let us turn to some minimal definitions in order to clarify the context.
The
B
-meson DA of leading twist-
2
,
\phi^{B}_{+}
was originally
introduced in
Grozin:1996pq
.
Its inverse moment

\frac{1}{\lambda_{B}(\mu)}=\int_{0}^{\infty}d\omega\frac{\phi^{B}_{+}(\omega,\mu)}{\omega}\;,

(73)

is a genuinely unknown non-perturbative parameter
\lambda_{B}={\cal O}(\Lambda_{\textrm{QCD}})
. Above

\mu
is the renormalisation scale (or factorisation scale in processes), assumed to be

\mu=1\,\mbox{\small GeV}
if not stated otherwise.
Mainly due to its non-local character it has so far evaded a direct first principle determination.
In computations using the heavy quark limit it is often the leading uncertainty.
More precisely, at NLO there are two further non-perturbative parameters that appear,
the inverse logarithmic moments
\hat{\sigma}_{1,2}
e.g.

DescotesGenon:2002mw
;
Beneke:2011nf

\hat{\sigma}_{n}(\mu)=\lambda_{B}(\mu)\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{d\omega}{\omega}\ln^{n}\frac{\mu_{0}}{\omega}\phi_{+}(\omega,\mu)\;,\quad\mu_{0}=\lambda_{B}e^{-\gamma_{E}}\;,

(74)

and one should be aware of different conventions due to the choice
of
\mu_{0}
resulting in different values of
\sigma_{n}
.
Matters are further complicated by the fact that
\lambda_{B}
and
\hat{\sigma}_{1,2}
are
defined at
\mu=1\,\mbox{\small GeV}
but in computations they are evaluated at the hard-collinear scale
\mu={\cal O}(\sqrt{\Lambda_{\textrm{QCD}}m_{b}})\approx 2.2\,\mbox{\small GeV}
.
The evolution of the
B
-meson DA parameters involves solving an integro-differential equation
Lange:2003ff
;
Braun:2019wyx

f_{\hat{\mathcal{B}}}(M_{\hat{\mathcal{B}}}^{2})={\hat{\mathcal{B}}}f(Q^{2})=\lim_{Q^{2}\to\infty,n\to\infty}\dfrac{(Q^{2})^{n+1}}{n!}\left(-\dfrac{d}{dQ^{2}}\right)^{n+1}f(Q^{2})

\displaystyle{\hat{\mathcal{B}}}\dfrac{1}{(Q^{2}+m^{2})^{a}}

Appendix B
Gauge (In)variance and the
m_{\ell}=0
Amplitude

In this appendix we intend to clarify a few issues around gauge invariance.

B.1
Gauge-variant part of the Charged Form Factor

In Sec.
3.2
it was shown how the
Q_{\bar{B}}
-term in (
2.1
) appears
in the LCSR computation and below its consistency is illustrated on general grounds.
The
Q_{\bar{B}}
-term is present since

O_{\mu}
itself is not gauge invariant (charged) in the case where the weak current is charged.
Let us write the matrix element on the LHS of (
2.1
) as follows

\langle\gamma(k,\epsilon)|O^{V}_{\mu}|\bar{B}_{q}(p_{B})\rangle=-i\epsilon^{*}_{\rho}\int_{y}e^{ik\cdot y}\langle 0|Tj^{\rho}(y)O^{V}_{\mu}(0)|\bar{B}_{q}(p_{B})\rangle+\textrm{gauge-invariant}\;,

(B.1)

and then perform
a gauge tranformation
\epsilon\to\epsilon+k
,

\displaystyle-ik_{\rho}\int_{y}e^{ik\cdot y}\langle 0|Tj^{\rho}(y)O^{V}_{\mu}(0)|\bar{B}_{q}(p_{B})\rangle

\displaystyle\;=\;

\displaystyle e\langle 0|[Q,O^{V}_{\mu}(0)]|\bar{B}_{q}\rangle

(B.2)

\displaystyle\;=\;

\displaystyle Q_{\bar{B}}m_{B}\langle 0|\bar{q}\gamma_{\mu}(1-\gamma_{5})b|\bar{B}_{q}\rangle=-iQ_{\bar{B}}m_{B}(p_{B})_{\mu}f_{B}\;,

where we have used
\{q_{\alpha}^{\dagger}(\vec{x},0),q_{\beta}(0)\}=\delta^{(3)}(\vec{x})\delta_{\alpha\beta}
. It is readily seen
that this matches the gauge variation of the Low-term on the RHS in (
2.1
).

B.2
Gauge Invariance Restored, in the
\bar{B}_{u}\to\gamma\ell^{-}\bar{\nu}
-Amplitude

Here we illustrate that the total amplitude,
emission from the
B
-meson (i.e. FF) and the lepton leg,
is gauge invariant. The total amplitude
{\cal A}
is proportional to

{\cal A}[\bar{B}_{u}\to\ell^{-}\bar{\nu}\gamma(\epsilon)]\propto e\epsilon^{*}_{\rho}\int_{y}e^{iky}\left(\langle 0|Tj^{\rho}(y)O_{\mu}^{V}|\bar{B}_{u}\rangle\langle\ell^{-}\bar{\nu}|L^{\mu}|0\rangle+\langle 0|O_{\mu}^{V}|\bar{B}_{u}\rangle\langle\ell^{-}\bar{\nu}|Tj^{\rho}(y)L^{\mu}|0\rangle\right)\;,

where
H^{\textrm{eff}}\sim L^{\mu}O^{V}_{\mu}
and

L^{\mu}=\bar{\ell}\Gamma^{\mu}\nu
is the charged lepton current with some unspecified
Dirac matrix
\Gamma^{\mu}
. The total amplitude must be invariant under the residual gauge transformation

\epsilon\to\epsilon+k
,

\displaystyle\delta_{\textrm{gauge}}{\cal A}

\displaystyle\;\equiv\;

\displaystyle{\cal A}(\bar{B}_{u}\to\ell^{-}\bar{\nu}\gamma(\epsilon+k))-{\cal A}(\bar{B}_{u}\to\ell^{-}\bar{\nu}\gamma(\epsilon))

\displaystyle\;\propto\;

\displaystyle ie\underbrace{(Q_{b}-Q_{u}-Q_{\ell})}_{=0}\langle 0|O_{\mu}^{V}|\bar{B}_{u}\rangle\langle\ell^{-}\bar{\nu}|L^{\mu}|0\rangle=0\;,

(B.3)

which is the case if charge conservation is imposed (
Q_{b}=-1/3
,
Q_{u}=2/3
and
Q_{\ell}=-1
).
In conclusion, the total amplitude is indeed invariant under
\epsilon\to\epsilon+k
.

B.3
The
\bar{B}_{u}\to\gamma\ell^{-}\bar{\nu}
-Amplitude at
m_{\ell}\to 0

The
m_{\ell}
-case is special in that the emission term is local and allows a redefinition of the FF
to absorb all effects
Beneke:2011nf
. We follow this procedure in our notation as we consider
it worthwhile to make contact with the existing literature.

•

In the
m_{\ell}=0
case the emission from the lepton is proportional
to the tree level matrix element. Essentially

\epsilon^{*}_{\rho}\langle\ell(l_{1})\nu(l_{2})|\bar{\ell}\not{p}_{B}\frac{\not{k}+\not{l_{1}}}{(k+l_{1})^{2}}\gamma^{\rho}\nu|0\rangle=\epsilon^{*}_{\rho}\langle\ell(l_{1})\nu(l_{2})|\bar{\ell}\gamma^{\rho}\nu|0\rangle\;,

(B.4)

where
p_{B}=k+l_{1}+l_{2}
and the EOM was used for the
\nu
.
This allows to redefine the FF in such a way that the amplitude is proportional
to the FF only.

•

Focusing on the
\gamma_{5}
-part of the FFs (
2.1
)

\displaystyle\langle\gamma(k,\epsilon)|O^{V}_{\mu}|\bar{B}_{q}(p_{B})\rangle|_{\gamma_{5}-\textrm{part}}

\displaystyle\;=\;

D.3
Quark Propagator on the Light-cone in Fock-Schwinger Gauge

The Fock-Schwinger gauge is defined by
(x-x_{0})\cdot A^{a}(x)=0
.
The power of the technique is that one can replace usual by covariant derivatives

(x-x_{0})\cdot\partial\equiv(x-x_{0})\cdot D
.
For
x_{0}=0
a formal solution is given by

A^{a}_{\mu}(x)=\int_{0}^{1}d\alpha\,\alpha x^{\omega}G^{a}_{\omega\mu}(\alpha x)=\frac{1}{2}x^{\omega}G^{a}_{\omega\mu}(0)+\dots\,,

(D.26)

which is used to compute diagram
A_{D_{1}})
in Fig.
6
.

For the computation of the 3-particle DA contribution the propagator in the
gluon background field is of use and its light-cone version has been derived
in
Balitsky:1987bk
which we write for a massive propagator in the form

\langle 0|Tq(0)\bar{q}(x)|0\rangle_{A}
rather than
\langle 0|Tq(x)\bar{q}(0)|0\rangle_{A}

which amounts to a replacement of
v\to\bar{v}
in the gluon field. One expands

\displaystyle\langle 0|Tq(0)\bar{q}(x)|0\rangle_{A}=i\int\frac{d^{4}k}{(2\pi)^{4}}e^{ik\cdot x}S_{q}(k)\;,\quad S_{c}(k)=S_{c}^{(0)}(k)+S_{c}^{(2)}(k,x)+\dots\;,

(D.27)

where we only need the first correction
S_{q}^{(2)}
which can be written in a symmetric form,

\displaystyle S^{(0)}_{q}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\not{k}+m_{q}}{k^{2}-m_{q}^{2}}\;,

\displaystyle S^{(2)}_{q}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle s_{g}g_{s}\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{1}dv\Big(v\sigma\!\cdot\!G(\bar{v}x)\frac{\not{k}+m_{q}}{(k^{2}-m_{q}^{2})^{2}}+\bar{v}\frac{\not{k}+m_{q}}{(k^{2}-m_{q}^{2})^{2}}\sigma\!\cdot\!G(\bar{v}x)\big)\,.

(D.28)

The variable
s_{g}
is the sign of the covariant derivative (
A.1
) which we prefer to keep
explicit. Note further terms in the expansion can be read off from eq. A.16 in
Balitsky:1987bk
,
and even though given for a massless propagator the inclusion of quark mass would seem rather
straightforward.

Appendix F
Multiple-Polylogarithms

MPLs, or Goncharov functions, can be viewed as generalisations of the classical polylogarithms. MPLs arise naturally when using the iterative method of differential equations to compute Feynman integrals. The development of the method of differential equations and advances in the understanding of MPLs represent a major breakthrough for multi-loop calculations
Duhr:2014woa
.
The MPLs of weight
n
are defined iteratively by the integral,

G(w_{1},\dots w_{n};u)=\int_{0}^{u}\frac{dt}{t-w_{1}}G(w_{2},\dots,w_{n};t).

(F.1)

Some useful equivalences with the classical polylogarithms are given below.

\displaystyle G(\mathbf{0}_{n};u)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n!}\log^{n}(u)\;,

\displaystyle G(\mathbf{a}_{n};u)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n!}\log^{n}\left(1-\frac{u}{a}\right)\;,

\displaystyle G(\mathbf{0}_{n},a;u)

\displaystyle=-\mathrm{Li}_{n+1}\left(\frac{u}{a}\right)\;,

(F.2)

for
a\neq 0
. For the case where
w_{n}\neq 0
the following scaling property holds,

G(w_{1},\dots,w_{i},\dots,w_{n};u)=G\left(\frac{w_{1}}{w_{i}},\dots,1,\dots,\frac{w_{n}}{w_{i}};\frac{u}{w_{i}}\right)\;,

(F.3)

which proves useful in performing the photon on-shell limit
k^{2}\to 0
.

F.2
The MPL Basis

As discussed in section
4.1
it was necessary to compute additional master integrals to those of

DiVita:2017xlr
in order for large cancellations to become manifest. These additional integrals were related
to the ones of
DiVita:2017xlr
by the swapping of external legs (
p_{B}^{2}\leftrightarrow q^{2}
). Whilst both
the
Q_{q}
(A type) and
Q_{b}
(B type) computations required these additional integrals it was only necessary
to explicitly compute them for the B-type diagrams. For the A-type MI the relative simplicity of the alphabet
translates to there being just five unique MPL arguments,

\displaystyle w_{1}

\displaystyle=-\frac{k^{2}}{p_{B}^{2}}\;,

\displaystyle w_{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{2k^{2}}{k^{2}-q^{2}-p_{B}^{2}+\Lambda}\;,

\displaystyle w_{3}

\displaystyle=\frac{2k^{2}}{k^{2}-q^{2}-p_{B}^{2}-\Lambda}\;,

\displaystyle w_{4}

\displaystyle=-\frac{k^{2}}{q^{2}}\;,\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad w_{5}=-\frac{k^{2}}{m_{b}^{2}}\;,

(F.13)

where
\Lambda=\sqrt{(k^{2}-p_{B}^{2}-q^{2})^{2}-4p_{B}^{2}q^{2}}
. Under
p_{B}^{2}\leftrightarrow q^{2}
the arguments

w_{2,3,5}
map onto themselves and
w_{1}\leftrightarrow w_{4}
. Consequently the basis of MPLs for the A-
type MIs is closed under swapping of external legs. To generate the additional A-type MIs we can therefore
simply take
p_{B}^{2}\leftrightarrow q^{2}
in the appropriate MIs of
DiVita:2017xlr
and large cancellations of
MPLs will still be manifest. For the B-type MIs there are 19 possible arguments of the MPLs. These arguments
do not close under leg swapping so a similar procedure cannot be applied.

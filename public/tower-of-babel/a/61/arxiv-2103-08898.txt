EXISTENCE, UNIQUENESS AND STRICT COMPARISON THEOREMS FOR BACKWARD STOCHASTIC DIFFERENTIAL EQUATIONS DRIVEN BY RCLL MARTINGALES
Tianyang Nie, Marek Rutkowski

Abstract
Results on the existence, uniqueness and strict comparison for solutions to a BSDE driven by a multi-dimensional RCLL martingale are established. The goal is to develop a general multi-asset framework encompassing a wide spectrum of nonlinear financial models with jumps, including as particular cases the setups studied by Peng and Xu [ 27 , 28 ] and Dumitrescu et al. [ 7 ] who dealt with BSDEs driven by a one-dimensional Brownian motion and a purely discontinuous martingale with a single jump. Keywords: backward stochastic differential equation, RCLL martingale, comparison theorem AMS Subject Classification: 60H10, 60H30, 91G30, 91G40

2
BSDEs Driven by RCLL Martingales

We assume that we are given a filtered probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{F},\mathbb{P})
satisfying the usual conditions of right-continuity and completeness. Moreover, the initial
\sigma
-field
\mathcal{F}_{0}
is assumed to be trivial. For any two processes, say
X
and
Y
, the equality
X=Y
means that
\mathbb{P}(X_{t}=Y_{t},\,\forall\,t\in[0,T])=1
. By convention, we set
X_{0-}=0
for any stochastic process
X
and thus
\Delta X_{0}:=X_{0}-X_{0-}=X_{0}
is the jump of
X
at time 0. Finally, the integral
\int_{s}^{t}X_{u}\,dY_{u}
is interpreted as
\int_{]s,t]}X_{u}\,dY_{u}
where
]s,t]=\{u\in[0,T]:s<u\leq t\}
.

Let
M=((M^{1}_{t},M^{2}_{t},\ldots,M^{n}_{t})^{\ast},\,t\in[0,T])
be an
n
-dimensional, square-integrable martingale defined on a filtered probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{F},\mathbb{P})
. Since the filtration
\mathbb{F}
is assumed to be right-continuous, an RCLL modification of any
\mathbb{F}
-martingale is known to exist and thus it is assumed that
M
is an RCLL process.
We denote by
\langle M\rangle
(resp.
[M]
) the predictable covariation process (resp. covariation process) of
M
meaning that the process
\langle M\rangle
(resp.
[M]
) takes values in
\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
and the
(j,k)
th entry of the random matrix
\langle M\rangle_{t}
(resp.
[M]
) equals
\langle M^{j},M^{k}\rangle_{t}
(resp.
[M^{j},M^{k}]
). As in
[
4
,
10
,
24
]
, we henceforth work under the following standing assumption regarding the predictable covariation of
M
.

Assumption 2.1
.

There exists an
\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
-valued,
\mathbb{F}
-predictable process
m
and an
\mathbb{F}
-adapted, continuous, nondecreasing process
Q
with
Q_{0}=0
such that, for all
t\in[0,T]
,

\langle M\rangle_{t}=\int_{0}^{t}m_{u}m_{u}^{\ast}\,dQ_{u}.

(2.1)

In addition, the process
Q
is bounded: there exists a constant
C_{Q}
such that
Q_{t}\leq C_{Q}
for all
t\in[0,T]
.

The postulated continuity of
Q
entails that the process
\langle M\rangle
is continuous as well. This property is known to be satisfied when
M
is an
\mathbb{F}
-quasi-left-continuous process, meaning that it does not have discontinuities at
\mathbb{F}
-predictable stopping times (see Chapter VI in He et al.
[
14
]
or Chapter 4 in Protter
[
29
]
). In particular, it holds when the filtration
\mathbb{F}
is assumed to be quasi-left-continuous (as was postulated in Carbone et al.
[
4
]
), that is, if
\mathcal{F}_{\tau}=\mathcal{F}_{\tau-}
for every
\mathbb{F}
-predictable stopping time
\tau
. Indeed, the latter property is known to be valid if and only if any uniformly integrable
\mathbb{F}
-martingale is a quasi-left-continuous process. In fact, if the filtration
\mathbb{F}
is quasi-left-continuous, then
\mathbb{F}
-martingales jump only at
\mathbb{F}
-totally inaccessible stopping times.

The boundedness
Q
is easy to be satisfied, for more details, see e.g. Remark 2.1 in Nie and Rutkowski
[
24
]
or Section 2.1 in Morlais
[
23
]
. Let us also mention that it looks that the boundedness of
Q
is a useful and, in some sense, a necessary condition. Indeed, as we explained in the introduction, to show that the component
Y
of a solution to a BSDE is an RCLL process and ensure that the BSDE holds in the sense of indistinguishability of stochastic processes, the contraction mapping based on the weighted
L^{2}
-type norm for
Y
(see page 44 in
[
26
]
and proof of Proposition 3.1 in
[
4
]
) is not sufficient. Hence we construct the contraction mapping by using the supremum norm for
Y
and for that purpose the boundedness of
Q
is needed (see the proof of Theorem
4.1
).

For a positive semi-definite matrix
a
, we denote by
a^{\frac{1}{2}}
the unique square root of
a
, that is,
a^{\frac{1}{2}}a^{\frac{1}{2}}=a
. Recall that there exists an orthogonal matrix
O
and a diagonal matrix
b
with nonnegative diagonal entries such that
a=O^{\ast}bO
. The square root of
b
is also a diagonal matrix, denoted by
b^{\frac{1}{2}}
, with diagonal entries equal to square roots of diagonal entries of
b
. Then we set
a^{\frac{1}{2}}=O^{\ast}b^{\frac{1}{2}}O
. Moreover, if
a
is positive definite, then the inverse of
a^{\frac{1}{2}}
, denoted as
a^{-\frac{1}{2}}
, is well defined. It is known that
m_{u}m_{u}^{\ast}
is a square, positive semi-definite matrix, so that
(m_{u}m_{u}^{\ast})^{\frac{1}{2}}
is well defined. If
m_{u}m_{u}^{\ast}
is positive definite, then
m_{u}m_{u}^{\ast}
is invertible, and thus we can also define
n_{u}:=(m_{u}m_{u}^{\ast})^{-\frac{1}{2}}
.

Regarding the symmetry of the matrix-valued process
m
appearing in (
2.1
), observe that condition (
2.1
) with an
\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
-valued,
\mathbb{F}
-predictable process
m
is equivalent to
\langle M\rangle_{t}=\int_{0}^{t}\bar{m}_{u}\bar{m}_{u}^{\ast}\,dQ_{u}
with a symmetric
\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
-valued,
\mathbb{F}
-predictable process
\bar{m}
since it suffices to take
\bar{m}_{u}=(m_{u}m_{u}^{\ast})^{\frac{1}{2}}
. Hence we may and do assume, without loss of generality, that the process
m
takes values in the space of symmetric matrices so that
m_{u}=m^{\ast}_{u}=(m_{u}m_{u}^{\ast})^{\frac{1}{2}}
.

Let
\|\cdot\|
stand for the Euclidean norm in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. We denote by
{\mathcal{L}}^{2}(M)
the space of all
\mathbb{R}^{n}
-valued,
\mathbb{F}
-predictable processes
Z
with the pseudo-norm
\|\cdot\|_{{\mathcal{L}}^{2}(M)}
given by

\displaystyle\|Z\|_{{\mathcal{L}}^{2}(M)}^{2}:={\mathbb{E}}\bigg[\int_{0}^{T}\|m_{t}Z_{t}\|^{2}\,dQ_{t}\bigg]<\infty.

We also make the following standing assumption, which provides a natural underpinning for the existence of a solution the BSDE (
2.2
).

Assumption 2.2
.

The martingale
M
has the predictable representation property under
\mathbb{P}
with respect to the filtration
\mathbb{F}
, in the sense that for any real-valued, square-integrable
\mathbb{F}
-martingale
N
with
N_{0}=0
there exists a process
Z\in{\mathcal{L}}^{2}(M)
such that
N_{t}=\int_{0}^{t}Z^{*}_{u}\,dM_{u}
. Moreover, the uniqueness of
Z
in

\|\cdot\|_{{\mathcal{L}}^{2}(M)}
holds, that is, if
N_{t}=\int_{0}^{t}Z^{*}_{u}\,dM_{u}=\int_{0}^{t}\widetilde{Z}^{*}_{u}\,dM_{u}
, then
\|Z-\widetilde{Z}\|_{{\mathcal{L}}^{2}(M)}=0
.

Remark 2.1
.

Assumption
2.2
is known to be satisfied when
M
is either a Wiener process or a compensated Poisson process. Kusuoka
[
21
]
has shown that Assumption
2.2
is valid for some special choice of
\mathbb{F}
and
M
, which was made in
[
7
,
27
,
28
]
(see also Section 3.3 in Bielecki et al.
[
3
]
, Theorem 2.1 in Jeanblanc and Le Cam
[
16
]
or Assumption A.1 and Example 1 in Jeanblanc et al.
[
17
,
18
]
). If Assumption
2.2
is relaxed, then the results of this paper can be generalized by combining the methods used here with the arguments from
[
4
,
10
]
provided that we assume, in addition, that the filtration
\mathbb{F}
is quasi-left-continuous. Then the extended martingale representation property of
M
holds, in the sense that any real-valued, square-integrable
\mathbb{F}
-martingale
N
can be represented as
N_{t}=\int_{0}^{t}Z^{*}_{u}\,dM_{u}+L_{t}
where
L
is a square-integrable
\mathbb{F}
-martingale orthogonal to
M
.

For a fixed horizon date
T>0
, we consider the following
backward stochastic differential equation
(BSDE) on
[0,T]
with data
(g,\eta,D)

\left\{\begin{array}[c]{ll}dY_{t}=-g(t,Y_{t},Z_{t})\,dQ_{t}+Z_{t}^{\ast}\,dM_{t}+dD_{t},\\
Y_{T}=\eta,\end{array}\right.

(2.2)

where
D
is a given real-valued,
\mathbb{F}
-adapted process or, more explicitly, for every
t\in[0,T]
,

\displaystyle Y_{t}=\eta+\int_{t}^{T}g(t,Y_{u},Z_{u})\,dQ_{u}-\int_{t}^{T}Z_{u}^{\ast}\,dM_{u}-(D_{T}-D_{t})

(2.3)

where, as usual, equality (
2.3
) is assumed to be satisfied
\mathbb{P}
-a.s.. The next definition states the measurability properties of the
generator

g
.

Definition 2.1
.

A
generator

g:\Omega\times[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is a
\mathcal{F}\otimes\mathcal{B}([0,T])\otimes\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R})\otimes\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
-measurable function such that the process
g(\cdot,\cdot,y,z)
is
\mathbb{F}
-progressively measurable, for every
(y,z)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

We denote by
\mu_{Q}
the Doléans measure of the process
Q
on the space
\Omega\times[0,T]
endowed with the product
\sigma
-algebra

\mathcal{F}_{T}\otimes\mathcal{B}([0,T])
so that
\mu_{Q}(A)={\mathbb{E}}\big[\int_{0}^{T}\mathds{1}_{A}(\omega,t)\,dQ_{t}(\omega)\big]
for every
A\in\mathcal{F}_{T}\otimes\mathcal{B}([0,T])
.
We first recall the Lipschitz condition, which is frequently employed in the existing literature on BSDEs driven by a Brownian motion.

Definition 2.2
.

A generator
g
is
uniformly Lipschitz continuous
if there exists a constant
L
such that,
for
\mu_{Q}
-almost every
(\omega,t)
, for all
y_{1},y_{2}\in\mathbb{R},\,z_{1},z_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

|g(t,y_{1},z_{1})-g(t,y_{2},z_{2})|\leq L\big(|y_{1}-y_{2}|+\|z_{1}-z_{2}\|\big).

(2.4)

Let
{\mathcal{S}}^{2}
stand for the space of all real-valued, RCLL,
\mathbb{F}
-adapted processes endowed with the norm
\|\cdot\|_{{\mathcal{S}}^{2}}
given by

\displaystyle\|X\|_{{\mathcal{S}}^{2}}^{2}:={\mathbb{E}}\bigg[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}X^{2}_{t}\bigg]<\infty.

We denote by
{\mathcal{H}}^{2}(Q)
the space of equivalence classes of all real-valued,
\mathbb{F}
-progressively measurable processes
X
with the
pseudo-norm
\|\cdot\|_{{\mathcal{H}}^{2}(Q)}
given by

\displaystyle\|X\|_{{\mathcal{H}}^{2}(Q)}^{2}:={\mathbb{E}}\bigg[\int_{0}^{T}X^{2}_{t}\,dQ_{t}\bigg]<\infty.

We observe that
{\mathcal{S}}^{2}\subset{\mathcal{H}}^{2}(Q)
and, for brevity, we denote by
\mathbb{S}^{2}
the product space
{\mathcal{S}}^{2}\times{\mathcal{L}}^{2}(M)
with the following pseudo-norm,
for every
(X,Z)\in{\mathcal{S}}^{2}\times{\mathcal{L}}^{2}(M)
,

\displaystyle\|(X,Z)\|_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}=\|X\|_{{\mathcal{S}}^{2}}+\|Z\|_{{\mathcal{L}}^{2}(M)}.

By the same token, we introduce the space
\mathbb{H}^{2}:={\mathcal{H}}^{2}(Q)\times{\mathcal{L}}^{2}(M)
endowed with the natural pseudo-norm

\displaystyle\|(X,Z)\|_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}=\|X\|_{{\mathcal{H}}^{2}(Q)}+\|Z\|_{{\mathcal{L}}^{2}(M)}.

As usual,
L^{2}({\mathcal{F}}_{T})
stands for the space of all real-valued,
\mathcal{F}_{T}
-measurable random variables
\eta
such that
\|\eta\|_{L^{2}({\mathcal{F}}_{T})}^{2}={\mathbb{E}}(\eta^{2})<\infty
.

In the study of the classical BSDEs driven by a Brownian motion (also with jumps generated by a Poisson random measure), it suffices to take
Q_{t}=t
. Moreover, in order to ensure the existence and uniqueness of a solution
(Y,Z)
it is common to postulate that a generator
g
is uniformly Lipschitz continuous with respect to the variables
y
and
z
. However, it was observed that the uniform Lipschitz condition (
2.4
) is no longer suitable when dealing with BSDEs with a single jump, which was aimed to represent the occurrence of the default event, and thus condition (
2.4
) has been modified to explicitly account for the intensity process
\lambda
. For instance, Theorem 3.1 in Peng and Xu
[
27
,
28
]
and Proposition 2 in Dumitrescu et al.
[
7
]
(see also Proposition 2.3 in Dumitrescu et al.
[
8
]
) hinge on the postulate that a generator satisfies the following condition

|g(t,y_{1},z_{1},k_{1})-g(t,y_{2},z_{2},k_{2})|\leq L\big(|y_{1}-y_{2}|+\|z_{1}-z_{2}\|+\sqrt{\lambda_{t}}|k_{1}-k_{2}|\big)

(2.5)

where the variables
z_{1},z_{2}
(respectively,
k_{1},k_{2}
) correspond to the one-dimensional Brownian motion (respectively, the compensated martingale of the jump process). A generator
g(t,y,z,k)
satisfying condition (
2.5
) and such that the process
g_{t}:=g(t,0,0,0)
belongs to
{\mathcal{H}}^{2}(Q)
is called the
\lambda
-admissible driver
in
[
7
]

More generally, in existing papers concerned with BSDEs driven by a multi-dimensional martingale (see, e.g.,
[
4
,
10
,
24
]
) a generator is typically postulated to satisfy some kind of the
m
-Lipschitz condition where
m
is the process introduced in Assumption
2.1
. In particular, the BSDE driven by a multi-dimensional continuous martingale and with a uniformly
m
-Lipschitz continuous generator was studied in
[
24
]
where the authors introduced also certain alternative conditions, which are more adequate when the process
m
is unbounded. In the present work, we focus on BSDEs with generator satisfying the following regularity condition.

Definition 2.3
.

A generator
g
is
uniformly
m
-Lipschitz continuous
if there exists a constant
\widehat{L}>0
such that, for
\mu_{Q}
-almost every
(\omega,t)
,
and all
y_{1},y_{2}\in\mathbb{R},\,z_{1},z_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

|g(t,y_{1},z_{1})-g(t,y_{2},z_{2})|\leq\widehat{L}\big(|y_{1}-y_{2}|+\|m_{t}(z_{1}-z_{2})\|\big).

(2.6)

Remark 2.2
.

We note that condition (
2.5
), which is employed in Peng and Xu
[
27
,
28
]
and Dumitrescu et al.
[
7
]
, can be seen as a special case of (
2.6
).
Indeed, suppose that
M^{1}:=W
is a Brownian motion and
M^{2}_{t}:=\mathds{1}_{\{\tau\leq t\}}-\int_{0}^{t}\lambda_{u}\,du
is the compensated martingale of the indicator process of a strictly positive
\mathbb{F}
-stopping time
\tau
with the
\mathbb{F}
-intensity process
\lambda
(note that, as in
[
7
]
, we use here the convention that the intensity process
\lambda
is
\mathbb{F}
-progressively measurable and thus it necessarily vanishes after
\tau
). Then we have
\langle M^{1}\rangle_{t}=\langle W\rangle_{t}=t
and
\langle M^{2}\rangle_{t}=\int_{0}^{t}\lambda_{u}\,du
since
M^{2}
is a purely discontinuous martingale so that, from Theorem 4.52 in Jacod and Shiryaev
[
15
]
, we obtain
[M^{2}]_{t}=\sum_{u\leq t}(\Delta M^{2}_{u})^{2}=\mathds{1}_{\{\tau\leq t\}}
. Moreover,
M^{1}
is a continuous martingale and
M^{2}
is a purely discontinuous martingale and thus, by Corollary 4.55 in
[
15
]
, we have
[M^{1},M^{2}]_{t}=\langle M^{1},M^{2}\rangle_{t}=0
. Hence, if we set
Q_{t}=t
, then (
2.5
) can be identified with a particular instance of condition (
2.6
), which shows that a
\lambda
-admissible driver
satisfies Definition
2.3
.

We adopt the following definition of a solution to the BSDE (
2.2
).

Definition 2.4
.

A
solution
to the BSDE (
2.2
) with data
(g,\eta,D)
is a pair of stochastic processes
(Y,Z)\in\mathbb{H}^{2}
such that equality
(
2.2
) is satisfied pathwise, in the sense that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\bigg(Y_{t}=\eta+\int_{t}^{T}g(u,Y_{u},Z_{u})\,dQ_{u}-\int_{t}^{T}Z_{u}^{\ast}\,dM_{u}-(D_{T}-D_{t}),\,\forall\,t\in[0,T]\bigg)=1.

We say that the
uniqueness of a solution to
(
2.2
) holds if for any two solutions
(Y,Z)
and
(Y^{\prime},Z^{\prime})
to (
2.2
) we have

\|(Y,Z)-(Y^{\prime},Z^{\prime})\|_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}=0
.

We henceforth study BSDEs with generators satisfying Definition
2.3
and data
(g,\eta,D)
satisfying the following assumption.

Assumption 2.3
.

The triplet
(g,\eta,D)
is such that: (i) the generator
g
is uniformly
m
-Lipschitz continuous and the process
g(\cdot,0,0)
belongs to
{\mathcal{H}}^{2}(Q)
, (ii) the random variable
\eta
belongs to
L^{2}({\mathcal{F}}_{T})
, (iii) the process
D
belongs to
{\mathcal{H}}^{2}(Q)
with
D_{T}\in L^{2}({\mathcal{F}}_{T})
.

Remark 2.3
.

Under Assumption
2.3
, Definition
2.4
can be restated as follows: a pair
(Y,Z)\in\mathbb{H}^{2}
is a solution to the BSDE (
2.2
) if
the process
Y-D
is RCLL and, for every
t\in[0,T]
, equality (
2.3
) holds
\mathbb{P}
-a.s..
In fact, we prove in Theorem
4.1
that, under Assumptions
2.1
–
2.3
, there exists a solution
(Y,Z)
to the BSDE (
2.2
) such that
(Y-D,Z)
belongs to the space
\mathbb{S}^{2}
. To this end, we first establish some useful
a priori
estimates in Proposition
3.1
.

3
A Priori Estimates

We first derive some useful
a priori
estimates for solutions to BSDEs.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let Assumptions
2.1
–
2.3
be satisfied by the martingale
M
and the triplets
(g^{l},\eta^{l},D^{l})
for
l=1,2
.
For
l=1,2
, let
(Y^{l},Z^{l})
be a solution to the BSDE

\left\{\begin{array}[c]{ll}dY_{t}^{l}=-g^{l}(t,Y^{l}_{t},Z^{l}_{t})\,dQ_{t}+Z^{l,\ast}_{t}\,dM_{t}+dD^{l}_{t},\\
Y_{T}^{l}=\eta^{l},\end{array}\right.

(3.1)

and let
y:=Y^{1}-Y^{2},\,z:=Z^{1}-Z^{2},\,D:=D^{1}-D^{2},\,\eta:=\eta^{1}-\eta^{2}
and
g:=g^{1}-g^{2}
. Then there exists a constant
C\geq 0
such that

\|y-D\|_{{\mathcal{S}}^{2}}^{2}+\|y\|_{{\mathcal{H}}^{2}(Q)}^{2}+\|z\|_{{\mathcal{L}}^{2}(M)}^{2}\leq CJ

(3.2)

where

\displaystyle J:=\|\eta-D_{T}\|^{2}_{L^{2}({\mathcal{F}}_{T})}+\|D\|_{{\mathcal{H}}^{2}(Q)}^{2}+\|g(t,Y^{2},Z^{2})\|_{{\mathcal{H}}^{2}(Q)}^{2}<\infty.

Proof.
We write
\widetilde{Y}^{i}=Y^{i}-D^{i}
,
\widetilde{Z}^{i}=Z^{i}
,
\widetilde{\eta}^{i}=\eta^{i}-D_{T}^{i}
,
\widetilde{g}^{i}(t,y,z):=g^{i}(t,y+D_{t}^{i},z)
,
\widetilde{y}:=\widetilde{Y}^{1}-\widetilde{Y}^{2},\,\widetilde{z}:=\widetilde{Z}^{1}-\widetilde{Z}^{2},\,D:=D^{1}-D^{2}
and
\widetilde{\eta}:=\widetilde{\eta}^{1}-\widetilde{\eta}^{2}
.
Then we have

\left\{\begin{array}[c]{ll}d\widetilde{y}_{t}=-\delta_{t}\,dQ_{t}+\widetilde{z}^{*}_{t}\,dM_{t},\\
\widetilde{y}_{T}=\widetilde{\eta},\end{array}\right.

(3.3)

where
\delta_{t}:=\widetilde{g}^{1}(t,\widetilde{Y}^{1}_{t},\widetilde{Z}^{1}_{t})-\widetilde{g}^{2}(t,\widetilde{Y}^{2}_{t},\widetilde{Z}^{2}_{t})
.
Since
[e^{\beta Q},\widetilde{y}^{2}]=0
by virtue of Proposition 4.49 in Chapter I of
[
15
]
, the Itô integration by parts formula gives

\displaystyle d(e^{\beta Q_{t}}\widetilde{y}^{2}_{t})=\widetilde{y}^{2}_{t}\,de^{\beta Q_{t}}+e^{\beta Q_{t}}\,d\widetilde{y}^{2}_{t}=\widetilde{y}^{2}_{t}\beta e^{\beta Q_{t}}\,dQ_{t}+e^{\beta Q_{t}}\,d\widetilde{y}^{2}_{t}

where

\displaystyle d\widetilde{y}^{2}_{t}=2\widetilde{y}_{t-}\,d\widetilde{y}_{t}+d[\widetilde{y}]_{t}=2\widetilde{y}_{t-}\,d\widetilde{y}_{t}+dN_{t}+d\langle\widetilde{y}\rangle_{t}

and the process
N:=[\widetilde{y}]-\langle\widetilde{y}\rangle
is an
\mathbb{F}
-martingale. In view of (
3.3
), we have

\displaystyle\widetilde{y}_{t-}\,d\widetilde{y}_{t}=-\widetilde{y}_{t}\delta_{t}\,dQ_{t}+\widetilde{y}_{t-}z^{*}_{t}\,dM_{t}

and
d\langle\widetilde{y}\rangle_{t}=\|m_{t}\widetilde{z}_{t}\|^{2}\,dQ_{t}
. By integrating from
t
to
T
and taking the conditional expectation with respect to
\mathcal{F}_{t}
, we obtain

\begin{split}&e^{\beta Q_{t}}\widetilde{y}^{2}_{t}+\beta\,{\mathbb{E}}_{t}\bigg[\int_{t}^{T}e^{\beta Q_{u}}\widetilde{y}^{2}_{u}\,dQ_{u}\bigg]+{\mathbb{E}}_{t}\bigg[\int_{t}^{T}e^{\beta Q_{u}}\,\|m_{u}\widetilde{z}_{u}\|^{2}\,dQ_{u}\bigg]\\
&={\mathbb{E}}_{t}\Big[e^{\beta Q_{T}}\widetilde{\eta}^{2}\Big]+2\,{\mathbb{E}}_{t}\bigg[\int_{t}^{T}e^{\beta Q_{u}}\widetilde{y}_{u}\delta_{u}\,dQ_{u}\bigg].\end{split}

(3.4)

Let us denote

\displaystyle\widetilde{g}_{t}:=\widetilde{g}^{1}(t,\widetilde{Y}^{2}_{t},\widetilde{Z}^{2}_{t})-\widetilde{g}^{2}(t,\widetilde{Y}^{2}_{t},\widetilde{Z}^{2}_{t})=g^{1}(t,Y^{2}_{t}-D^{2}_{t}+D^{1}_{t},Z^{2}_{t})-g^{2}(t,Y^{2}_{t},Z^{2}_{t}).

Since
|\delta_{t}|\leq\widehat{L}_{1}(|\widetilde{y}_{t}|+\|m_{t}\widetilde{z}_{t}\|)+|\widetilde{g}_{t}|
, we deduce from (
3.4
) that

\displaystyle e^{\beta Q_{t}}\widetilde{y}^{2}_{t}

\displaystyle+\beta\,{\mathbb{E}}_{t}\bigg[\int_{t}^{T}e^{\beta Q_{u}}\widetilde{y}^{2}_{u}\,dQ_{u}\bigg]+{\mathbb{E}}_{t}\bigg[\int_{t}^{T}e^{\beta Q_{u}}\|m_{u}\widetilde{z}_{u}\|^{2}\,dQ_{u}\bigg]

\displaystyle\leq{\mathbb{E}}_{t}\Big[e^{\beta Q_{T}}\widetilde{\eta}^{2}\Big]+2\widehat{L}_{1}\,{\mathbb{E}}_{t}\bigg[\int_{t}^{T}e^{\beta Q_{u}}\widetilde{y}^{2}_{u}\,dQ_{u}\bigg]+2\,{\mathbb{E}}_{t}\bigg[\int_{t}^{T}e^{\beta Q_{u}}|\widetilde{y}_{u}|\big(\widehat{L}_{1}\|m_{u}\widetilde{z}_{u}\|+|\widetilde{g}_{u}|\big)\,dQ_{u}\bigg]

\displaystyle\leq{\mathbb{E}}_{t}\Big[e^{\beta Q_{T}}\widetilde{\eta}^{2}\Big]+(2\widehat{L}_{1}+2\widehat{L}_{1}^{2}+1)\,{\mathbb{E}}_{t}\bigg[\int_{t}^{T}e^{\beta Q_{u}}\widetilde{y}^{2}_{u}\,dQ_{u}\bigg]

\displaystyle+{\mathbb{E}}_{t}\bigg[\int_{t}^{T}e^{\beta Q_{u}}\Big(\frac{1}{2}\|m_{u}\widetilde{z}_{u}\|^{2}+|\widetilde{g}_{u}|^{2}\big)\Big)\,dQ_{u}\bigg].

We now take
\beta=2\widehat{L}_{1}+2\widehat{L}_{1}^{2}+2
and rearrange the above equation to obtain

\displaystyle e^{\beta Q_{t}}\widetilde{y}^{2}_{t}+\frac{1}{2}\,{\mathbb{E}}_{t}\bigg[\int_{t}^{T}e^{\beta Q_{u}}\|m_{u}\widetilde{z}_{u}\|^{2}\,dQ_{u}\bigg]\leq{\mathbb{E}}\big(e^{\beta Q_{T}}\widetilde{\eta}^{2}\big)+{\mathbb{E}}\bigg[\int_{t}^{T}e^{\beta Q_{t}}|\widetilde{g}_{t}|^{2}\,dQ_{t}\bigg].

Then, recalling also that
Q
is bounded and taking
t=0
, we obtain
the existence of a constant
C\geq 0
(note that the value of
C
may change from place to place) such that

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{y}\|^{2}_{{\mathcal{S}}^{2}}+\|\widetilde{y}\|_{{\mathcal{H}}^{2}(Q)}^{2}+\|\widetilde{z}\|_{{\mathcal{L}}^{2}(M)}^{2}\leq C\bigg[{\mathbb{E}}\big(\widetilde{\eta}^{2}\big)+{\mathbb{E}}\bigg(\int_{0}^{T}|\widetilde{g}_{t}|^{2}\,dQ_{t}\bigg)\bigg]

where we have also used the property that
\widetilde{y}
is an RCLL process.
Noticing that
|\widetilde{g}_{t}|^{2}\leq 2|g(t,Y_{t}^{2},Z_{t}^{2})|^{2}+2\widehat{L}_{1}^{2}|D_{t}|^{2}
, we get

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{y}\|^{2}_{{\mathcal{S}}^{2}}+\|\widetilde{y}\|_{{\mathcal{H}}^{2}(Q)}^{2}+\|\widetilde{z}\|_{{\mathcal{L}}^{2}(M)}^{2}\leq CJ,

which in turn implies that (recalling that
\widetilde{y}=y-D
and
\widetilde{z}=z
)

\displaystyle\|y-D\|^{2}_{{\mathcal{S}}^{2}}+\|y\|_{{\mathcal{H}}^{2}(Q)}^{2}+\|z\|_{{\mathcal{L}}^{2}(M)}^{2}\leq CJ.

Finally, it is easy to check that
J
is finite.

\Box

Remark 3.1
.

For later use, it is worth noting that (
3.2
) implies that if
|\eta^{1}-\eta^{2}|\leq\varepsilon
for some positive constant
\varepsilon
,
then the solutions
Y^{1}
and
Y^{2}
to the BSDE (
2.2
) with terminal conditions
\eta^{1}
and
\eta^{2}
, respectively,
satisfy
|Y^{1}_{0}-Y^{2}_{0}|\leq C\varepsilon
for some constant
C
.

4
Existence and Uniqueness Theorem for BSDEs

To establish the existence and uniqueness of solutions to the BSDE (
2.2
), it is useful to introduce several auxiliary normed spaces
of stochastic processes indexed by a parameter
\beta\geq 0
. First, for any fixed
\beta\geq 0
, we denote by
{\mathcal{L}}^{2}_{\beta}(M)
the class of all

\mathbb{R}^{n}
-valued,
\mathbb{F}
-predictable processes
Z
with the pseudo-norm
\|\cdot\|_{{\mathcal{L}}^{2}_{\beta}(M)}
given by

\displaystyle\|Z\|_{{\mathcal{L}}^{2}_{\beta}(M)}^{2}:={\mathbb{E}}\bigg[\int_{0}^{T}e^{\beta Q_{t}}\|m_{t}Z_{t}\|^{2}\,dQ_{t}\bigg]<\infty.

Hence
{\mathcal{L}}^{2}(M)=\mathcal{L}^{2}_{0}(M)
and the pseudo-norms
\|\cdot\|_{{\mathcal{L}}^{2}(M)}
and
\|\cdot\|_{{\mathcal{L}}^{2}_{\beta}(M)}
are equivalent for every
\beta>0
since the nondecreasing, nonnegative process
Q
is bounded.

Second, we denote by
{\mathcal{H}}^{2}_{\beta}(Q)
the space of all real-valued,
\mathbb{F}
-progressively measurable processes
X
with the pseudo-norm
\|\cdot\|_{{\mathcal{H}}^{2}_{\beta}(Q)}
given by

\displaystyle\|X\|_{{\mathcal{H}}^{2}_{\beta}(Q)}^{2}:={\mathbb{E}}\bigg[\int_{0}^{T}e^{\beta Q_{t}}X^{2}_{t}\,dQ_{t}\bigg]<\infty.

Third, we introduce the space
{\mathcal{S}}^{2}_{\beta}
of all real-valued, RCLL,
\mathbb{F}
-adapted processes
X
with the norm
\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{S}_{\beta}^{2}}
given by

\displaystyle\|X\|_{{\mathcal{S}}^{2}_{\beta}}^{2}:={\mathbb{E}}\bigg[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\big(e^{\beta Q_{t}}X^{2}_{t}\big)\bigg]<\infty.

It is clear that
{\mathcal{S}}^{2}=\mathcal{S}_{0}^{2}
and the norms
\|\cdot\|_{{\mathcal{S}}^{2}}
and
\|\cdot\|_{{\mathcal{S}}^{2}_{\beta}}
are equivalent for every
\beta>0
.

Finally, for a fixed
\beta\geq 0
, we denote by
\mathbb{S}^{2}_{\beta}
the Banach space
({\mathcal{S}}^{2}_{\beta}\times{\mathcal{L}}^{2}_{\beta}(M),\|\cdot\|_{\beta})
where the pseudo-norm
\|\cdot\|_{\beta}
is given by

\displaystyle\|(w,v)\|^{2}_{\beta}:=\|w\|^{2}_{{\mathcal{S}}^{2}_{\beta}}+\|v\|^{2}_{{\mathcal{L}}^{2}_{\beta}(M)}

and we note that
\mathbb{S}^{2}=\mathbb{S}^{2}_{0}
. We stress that, in view of Assumption
2.1
, the class
\mathbb{S}^{2}_{\beta}
of stochastic processes is independent of the choice of
\beta\geq 0
and thus the set
\mathbb{S}^{2}_{\beta}
can be formally identified with
\mathbb{S}^{2}
, although the pseudo-norm
\|\cdot\|_{\beta}
depends on
\beta
and thus it is advantageous to consider a whole family of pseudo-norms when searching for a solution to the BSDE (
2.2
) through a contraction mapping on
\mathbb{S}^{2}_{\beta}
for some
\beta>0
.

Notice that equation (
2.3
) can also be rewritten as follows

\displaystyle Y_{t}-D_{t}=\eta-D_{T}+\int_{t}^{T}\widetilde{g}(t,Y_{u}-D_{u},Z_{u})\,dQ_{u}-\int_{t}^{T}Z_{u}^{\ast}\,dM_{u}

where we set
\widetilde{g}(t,y,z):=g(t,y+D_{t},z)
for all
(\omega,t,y,z)\in\Omega\times[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
To simplify the presentation, we find it convenient to further transform the BSDE (
2.3
) by setting

\widetilde{Y}:=Y-D,\,\widetilde{\eta}:=\eta-D_{T}
and
\widetilde{Z}=Z
. Then we obtain the transformed BSDE with modified data
(\widetilde{g},\widetilde{\eta},0)

\displaystyle\widetilde{Y}_{t}=\widetilde{\eta}+\int_{t}^{T}\widetilde{g}(u,\widetilde{Y}_{u},\widetilde{Z}_{u})\,dQ_{u}-\int_{t}^{T}\widetilde{Z}_{u}^{\ast}\,dM_{u}.

(4.1)

It is easy to see that, under Assumption
2.3
, the random variable
\widetilde{\eta}
belongs to
L^{2}({\mathcal{F}}_{T})
, the modified generator

\widetilde{g}
is uniformly
m
-Lipschitz continuous with a constant
\widehat{L}
and the process
\widetilde{g}(\cdot,0,0)
belongs to
{\mathcal{H}}^{2}(Q)
.
Consistently with Definition
2.4
and Remark
2.3
, we now search for a solution
(\widetilde{Y},\widetilde{Z})\in\mathbb{H}^{2}
to the BSDE (
4.1
)
(in particular,
\widetilde{Y}
should be an RCLL process). It is readily seen that the following lemma is valid.

Lemma 4.1
.

Under Assumption
2.3
, the existence and uniqueness of a solution
(\widetilde{Y},\widetilde{Z})
to the transformed BSDE (
4.1
) is equivalent to the existence and uniqueness of a solution
(Y,Z)
to the BSDE (
2.2
) and the respective solutions are related to each other through the mapping
(Y,Z)=(\widetilde{Y}+D,\widetilde{Z})
.

We are now ready to prove the existence and uniqueness theorem for solutions to the BSDE (
2.2
) with generator satisfying the uniform
m
-Lipschitz condition.

Theorem 4.1
.

If Assumptions
2.1
–
2.3
are valid, then there exists a solution
(Y,Z)\in\mathbb{H}^{2}
to the BSDE (
2.2
) such that the process
Y-D
belongs to
{\mathcal{S}}^{2}
. Moreover, the uniqueness of a solution
(Y,Z)
holds in the space
\mathbb{H}^{2}
and the uniqueness of
(Y-D,Z)
holds in
\mathbb{S}^{2}
.

Proof.

It is clear that both statements about uniqueness are immediate consequences of inequality (
3.2
) in Proposition
3.1
since if
(Y,Z)
and
(Y^{\prime},Z^{\prime})
are two solutions to the BSDE (
2.2
), then we have
g^{1}=g^{2},\,D^{1}=D^{2}
and
\eta^{1}=\eta^{2}
so that (
3.2
) yields

\displaystyle\|Y-Y^{\prime}\|_{{\mathcal{S}}^{2}}^{2}+\|Y-Y^{\prime}\|_{{\mathcal{H}}^{2}(Q)}^{2}+\|Z-Z^{\prime}\|_{{\mathcal{L}}^{2}(M)}^{2}=0

where we observe that
Y-Y^{\prime}=(Y-D)-(Y^{\prime}-D)
. Hence it is enough to prove the first assertion.
In view of Lemma
4.1
and since
D
belongs to
{\mathcal{H}}^{2}(Q)
, it would be possible to assume, without loss of generality, that
D=0
.

In the first step, for a given pair
(w,v)\in\mathbb{S}^{2}_{\beta}
, we examine the following BSDE

\left\{\begin{array}[c]{ll}dY_{t}=-g_{t}\,dQ_{t}+Z_{t}^{\ast}\,dM_{t},\\
Y_{T}=\eta,\end{array}\right.

(4.2)

where
g_{t}:=g(t,w_{t},v_{t})
. To establish the existence of a unique solution to (
4.2
), it suffices to apply the postulated martingale representation property of
M
to the square-integrable
\mathbb{F}
-martingale
N_{t}:={\mathbb{E}}\left[\eta+\int_{0}^{T}g_{u}\,dQ_{u}\big|\mathcal{F}_{t}\right]
. Due to Assumption
2.2
, there exists a process
Z\in{\mathcal{L}}^{2}(M)
such that, for all
t\in[0,T]
,

\displaystyle N_{t}={\mathbb{E}}\bigg[\eta+\int_{0}^{T}g_{u}\,dQ_{u}\,\Big|\,\mathcal{F}_{t}\bigg]=\int_{0}^{t}Z^{*}_{u}\,dM_{u}

and the uniqueness of
Z
in
\|\cdot\|_{{\mathcal{L}}^{2}(M)}
holds. We define the RCLL,
\mathbb{F}
-adapted process
Y
by setting

\displaystyle Y_{t}:={\mathbb{E}}\bigg[\eta+\int_{t}^{T}g_{u}\,dQ_{u}\,\Big|\,\mathcal{F}_{t}\bigg]=N_{t}-\int_{0}^{t}g_{u}\,dQ_{u}=\int_{0}^{t}Z^{*}_{u}\,dM_{u}-\int_{0}^{t}g_{u}\,dQ_{u}.

Then we have, for all
t\in[0,T]
,

\displaystyle Y_{t}=\eta+\int_{t}^{T}g_{u}\,dQ_{u}-\int_{t}^{T}Z_{u}^{\ast}\,dM_{u}.

It is now easy to check that the pair
(Y,Z)
belongs to
\mathbb{S}^{2}_{\beta}
for any
\beta>0
and is a solution to the BSDE (
4.2
). Furthermore, the uniqueness of a solution to (
4.2
) in
\mathbb{H}^{2}_{\beta}
(and in
\mathbb{S}^{2}_{\beta}
) also follows from inequality (
3.2
) in Proposition
3.1
.

In the second step, for any
(w,v)\in\mathbb{S}^{2}_{\beta}
, we denote by
(Y^{w,v},Z^{w,v})\in\mathbb{S}^{2}_{\beta}
the unique solution to (
4.2
) with
g_{t}:=g(t,w_{t},v_{t})
and
we define the mapping
\Psi:\mathbb{S}^{2}_{\beta}\rightarrow\mathbb{S}^{2}_{\beta}
as follows: for any
(w,v)\in\mathbb{S}^{2}_{\beta}
, we set
\Psi(w,v)=(Y^{w,v},Z^{w,v})
. We claim that
\Psi
is a contraction mapping on the Banach space
\mathbb{S}^{2}_{\beta}
, provided that
\beta
is sufficiently large. To this end, we proceed as follows: for
l=1,2
, we assume that
(Y^{l},Z^{l})
is a solution to BSDE (
4.2
) with generator
g^{l}_{t}:=g(t,w^{l}_{t},v^{l}_{t})
where
(w^{l},v^{l})\in\mathbb{S}^{2}_{\beta}
, and we show that for an arbitrary
\beta>0

\|y\|^{2}_{{\mathcal{S}}^{2}_{\beta}}+\|z\|^{2}_{{\mathcal{L}}^{2}_{\beta}(M)}\leq 1158\beta^{-1}\widehat{L}^{2}(C_{Q}+1)\big(\|w\|_{{\mathcal{S}}^{2}_{\beta}}^{2}+\|v\|^{2}_{{\mathcal{L}}^{2}_{\beta}(M)}\big)

(4.3)

where
y:=Y^{1}-Y^{2},\,z:=Z^{1}-Z^{2},\,w:=w^{1}-w^{2}
and
v:=v^{1}-v^{2}
.

Assuming temporarily that (
4.3
) is valid, it is immediate from (
4.3
) that
\Psi:\mathbb{S}^{2}_{\beta}\rightarrow\mathbb{S}^{2}_{\beta}
is a contraction if we take
\beta
sufficiently large. Then, by the Banach theorem, there exists a unique fixed point
(Y,Z)\in\mathbb{S}^{2}_{\beta}
of
\Psi
. Furthermore, it is not hard to see that
Y\in{\mathcal{H}}^{2}(Q)
, due to the boundedness of
Q
(when
D\neq 0
, we also have that
Y\in{\mathcal{H}}^{2}(Q)
since
D\in{\mathcal{H}}^{2}(Q)
). Thus the above fixed point
(Y,Z)
is a solution to BSDE (
4.1
) in
\mathbb{S}^{2}
and thus also in
\mathbb{H}^{2}
.

It is now clear that to complete the proof, it suffices to establish (
4.3
). By taking the difference of solutions to BSDEs with generator
g^{l}_{t}:=g(t,w^{l}_{t},v^{l}_{t})
for
l=1,2
, we obtain

\left\{\begin{array}[c]{ll}dy_{t}=-\delta_{t}\,dQ_{t}+z^{*}_{t}\,dM_{t},\\
y_{T}=0,\end{array}\right.

where
\delta_{t}:=g(t,w^{1}_{t},v^{1}_{t})-g(t,w^{2}_{t},v^{2}_{t})
. From the first step, it is clear that for an arbitrary
\beta>0

{\mathbb{E}}\bigg[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\big(e^{\beta Q_{t}}y_{t}^{2}\big)\bigg]=\|y\|^{2}_{{\mathcal{S}}^{2}_{\beta}}\leq\|Y^{1}\|^{2}_{{\mathcal{S}}^{2}_{\beta}}+\|Y^{2}\|^{2}_{{\mathcal{S}}^{2}_{\beta}}<\infty

(4.4)

since
Y^{1}
and
Y^{2}
belong to
{\mathcal{S}}^{2}
and thus also to
{\mathcal{S}}^{2}_{\beta}
. The Itô integration by parts formula gives

\displaystyle d(e^{\beta Q_{t}}y^{2}_{t})=y^{2}_{t}\,de^{\beta Q_{t}}+e^{\beta Q_{t}}\,dy^{2}_{t}=y^{2}_{t}\beta e^{\beta Q_{t}}\,dQ_{t}+e^{\beta Q_{t}}\,dy^{2}_{t}

where

\displaystyle dy^{2}_{t}=2y_{t-}\,dy_{t}+d[y]_{t}=2y_{t-}\,dy_{t}+dN_{t}+d\langle y\rangle_{t}

and
N:=[y]-\langle y\rangle
. Consequently,

\begin{split}d(e^{\beta Q_{t}}y^{2}_{t})&=e^{\beta Q_{t}}\big(\beta y^{2}_{t}\,dQ_{t}-2y_{t-}\delta_{t}\,dQ_{t}+2y_{t-}z^{*}_{t}\,dM_{t}+d[y]_{t}\big)\\
&=e^{\beta Q_{t}}\big(\beta y^{2}_{t}\,dQ_{t}-2y_{t-}\delta_{t}\,dQ_{t}+2y_{t-}z^{*}_{t}\,dM_{t}+dN_{t}+d\langle y\rangle_{t}\big).\end{split}

(4.5)

To complete the derivation of (
4.3
), we first show that

\|z\|^{2}_{{\mathcal{L}}^{2}_{\beta}(M)}\leq 2\beta^{-1}\widehat{L}^{2}(C_{Q}+1)\big(\|w\|_{{\mathcal{S}}^{2}_{\beta}}^{2}+\|v\|^{2}_{{\mathcal{L}}^{2}_{\beta}(M)}\big).

(4.6)

After integrating (
4.5
) from
t
to
T
, taking the expectation and using the fact that
N
is
\mathbb{F}
-martingale
and
d\langle y\rangle_{t}=\|m_{t}z_{t}\|^{2}\,dQ_{t}
, we obtain

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\bigg[e^{\beta Q_{t}}y^{2}_{t}+\beta\,\int_{t}^{T}e^{\beta Q_{u}}y^{2}_{u}\,dQ_{u}+\int_{t}^{T}e^{\beta Q_{u}}\,\|m_{u}z_{u}\|^{2}\,dQ_{u}\bigg]\leq 2\,{\mathbb{E}}\bigg[\int_{t}^{T}e^{\beta Q_{u}}y_{u}\delta_{u}\,dQ_{u}\bigg]

\displaystyle\leq 2\widehat{L}\,{\mathbb{E}}\bigg[\int_{t}^{T}e^{\beta Q_{u}}|y_{u}|(|w_{u}|+\|m_{u}v_{u}\|)\,dQ_{u}\bigg]

\displaystyle\leq 2\lambda^{-1}\widehat{L}^{2}\,{\mathbb{E}}\bigg[\int_{t}^{T}e^{\beta Q_{u}}y^{2}_{u}\,dQ_{u}\bigg]+\lambda\,{\mathbb{E}}\bigg[\int_{t}^{T}e^{\beta Q_{u}}|w_{u}|^{2}\,dQ_{u}\bigg]+\lambda\,{\mathbb{E}}\bigg[\int_{t}^{T}e^{\beta Q_{u}}\|m_{u}v_{u}\|^{2}\,dQ_{u}\bigg].

Let us take
\lambda=2\beta^{-1}\widehat{L}^{2}
. Then we get

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\bigg[\int_{0}^{T}e^{\beta Q_{u}}\,\|m_{u}z_{u}\|^{2}\,dQ_{u}\bigg]\leq 2\beta^{-1}\widehat{L}^{2}\,{\mathbb{E}}\bigg[\int_{0}^{T}e^{\beta Q_{u}}|w_{u}|^{2}\,dQ_{u}\bigg]+2\beta^{-1}\widehat{L}^{2}\,{\mathbb{E}}\bigg[\int_{0}^{T}e^{\beta Q_{u}}\|m_{u}v_{u}\|^{2}\,dQ_{u}\bigg],

which gives (
4.6
) since
Q
is a nonnegative, nondecreasing process bounded by
C_{Q}
.

In the remainder of the proof, we analyze the norm
\|y\|_{{\mathcal{S}}^{2}_{\beta}}
and we show that

\|y\|^{2}_{{\mathcal{S}}^{2}_{\beta}}\leq 1156\beta^{-1}\widehat{L}^{2}(C_{Q}+1)\big(\|w\|_{{\mathcal{S}}^{2}_{\beta}}^{2}+\|v\|^{2}_{{\mathcal{L}}^{2}_{\beta}(M)}\big).

(4.7)

Using again (
4.5
) and noticing that the process
[y]
is nondecreasing, we get for any
\lambda>0
(recall that
\delta_{t}:=g(t,w^{1}_{t},v^{1}_{t})-g(t,w^{2}_{t},v^{2}_{t})

and
g
is uniformly
m
-Lipschitz continuous)

\displaystyle e^{\beta Q_{t}}y^{2}_{t}+\beta\,\int_{t}^{T}e^{\beta Q_{u}}y^{2}_{u}\,dQ_{u}\leq 2\,\int_{t}^{T}e^{\beta Q_{u}}y_{u}\delta_{u}\,dQ_{u}-2\int_{t}^{T}e^{\beta Q_{u}}y_{u-}z^{*}_{u}\,dM_{u}

\displaystyle\leq 2\lambda^{-1}\widehat{L}^{2}\int_{t}^{T}e^{\beta Q_{u}}y^{2}_{u}\,dQ_{u}+\lambda\int_{t}^{T}e^{\beta Q_{u}}|w_{u}|^{2}\,dQ_{u}+\lambda\int_{t}^{T}e^{\beta Q_{u}}\|m_{u}v_{u}\|^{2}\,dQ_{u}-2\int_{t}^{T}e^{\beta Q_{u}}y_{u-}z^{*}_{u}\,dM_{u},

which in turn yields, by taking
\lambda=2\beta^{-1}\widehat{L}^{2}

\begin{split}&e^{\beta Q_{t}}y^{2}_{t}\leq\lambda\int_{t}^{T}e^{\beta Q_{u}}|w_{u}|^{2}\,dQ_{u}+\lambda\int_{t}^{T}e^{\beta Q_{u}}\|m_{u}v_{u}\|^{2}\,dQ_{u}-2\int_{t}^{T}e^{\beta Q_{u}}y_{u-}z^{*}_{u}\,dM_{u}\\
&\leq\lambda(C_{Q}+1)\left(\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\big(e^{\beta Q_{t}}|w_{t}|^{2}\big)+\int_{t}^{T}e^{\beta Q_{u}}\|m_{u}v_{u}\|^{2}\,dQ_{u}\right)-2\int_{t}^{T}e^{\beta Q_{u}}y_{u-}z^{*}_{u}\,dM_{u}.\end{split}

(4.8)

Let us denote
\widetilde{y}_{u}=e^{\frac{\beta}{2}Q_{u}}y_{u}
and
\widetilde{M}_{t}:=\int_{0}^{t}e^{\frac{\beta}{2}Q_{u}}z^{\ast}_{u}\,dM_{u}
. The Davis inequality (see, e.g.,
Theorem 10.24 in He et al.
[
14
]
or Theorem 11.5.5 in Cohen and Elliott
[
5
]
with
p=1
) gives

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\left[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\Big|\int_{0}^{t}\widetilde{y}_{u-}\,d\widetilde{M}_{u}\Big|\right]\leq 2\sqrt{6}\,{\mathbb{E}}\left[\bigg(\int_{0}^{T}\widetilde{y}_{u-}^{2}\,d[\widetilde{M}]_{u}\bigg)^{1/2}\right]\leq 6\,{\mathbb{E}}\left[\bigg(\int_{0}^{T}\widetilde{y}_{u-}^{2}\,d[\widetilde{M}]_{u}\bigg)^{1/2}\right].

Consequently, using also the elementary inequalities

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\left[\bigg(\int_{0}^{T}\widetilde{y}_{u-}^{2}\,d[\widetilde{M}]_{u}\bigg)^{1/2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq{\mathbb{E}}\bigg[[\widetilde{M}]^{\frac{1}{2}}_{T}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|\widetilde{y}_{t}|\bigg]\leq\frac{1}{48}\,{\mathbb{E}}\bigg[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\widetilde{y}^{2}_{t}\bigg]+12\,{\mathbb{E}}\big([\widetilde{M}]_{T}\big)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{48}\,{\mathbb{E}}\bigg[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\widetilde{y}^{2}_{t}\bigg]+12\,{\mathbb{E}}\big(\langle\widetilde{M}\rangle_{T}\big),

we obtain

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\left[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\Big|\int_{0}^{t}\widetilde{y}_{u-}\,d\widetilde{M}_{u}\Big|\right]\leq\frac{1}{8}\,{\mathbb{E}}\bigg[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\widetilde{y}^{2}_{t}\bigg]+72\,{\mathbb{E}}\big(\langle\widetilde{M}\rangle_{T}\big),

which can be rewritten more explicitly as follows

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\left[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\Big|\int_{0}^{t}e^{\beta Q_{u}}y_{u-}z^{*}_{u}\,dM_{u}\Big|\right]\leq\frac{1}{8}\,{\mathbb{E}}\Big[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\big(e^{\beta Q_{t}}y^{2}_{t}\big)\Big]+72\,{\mathbb{E}}\bigg[\int_{0}^{T}e^{\beta Q_{u}}\,\|m_{u}z_{u}\|^{2}\,dQ_{u}\bigg].

Therefore, using also already proven inequality (
4.6
), we find that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\left[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\Big|\int_{t}^{T}e^{\beta Q_{u}}y_{u-}z^{*}_{u}\,dM_{u}\Big|\right]\leq\frac{1}{4}\,{\mathbb{E}}\bigg[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\big(e^{\beta Q_{t}}y^{2}_{t}\big)\bigg]+144\,{\mathbb{E}}\bigg[\int_{0}^{T}e^{\beta Q_{u}}\,\|m_{u}z_{u}\|^{2}\,dQ_{u}\bigg]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{4}\,{\mathbb{E}}\bigg[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\big(e^{\beta Q_{t}}y^{2}_{t}\big)\bigg]+144\lambda(C_{Q}+1)\big(\|w\|_{{\mathcal{S}}^{2}_{\beta}}^{2}+\|v\|^{2}_{{\mathcal{L}}^{2}_{\beta}(M)}\big).

By combining (
4.8
) with the inequality above, we deduce that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\Big[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\big(e^{\beta Q_{t}}y^{2}_{t}\big)\Big]

\displaystyle\leq\lambda(C_{Q}+1)\big(\|w\|_{{\mathcal{S}}^{2}_{\beta}}^{2}+\|v\|^{2}_{{\mathcal{L}}^{2}_{\beta}(M)}\big)+2\,{\mathbb{E}}\bigg[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\Big|\int_{t}^{T}e^{\beta Q_{u}}y_{u-}z^{*}_{u}\,dM_{u}\Big|\bigg]

\displaystyle\leq 289\lambda(C_{Q}+1)\big(\|w\|_{{\mathcal{S}}^{2}_{\beta}}^{2}+\|v\|^{2}_{{\mathcal{L}}^{2}_{\beta}(M)}\big)+\frac{1}{2}\,{\mathbb{E}}\bigg[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\big(e^{\beta Q_{t}}y^{2}_{t}\big)\bigg]

and thus, in view of the finiteness of the norm
\|y\|_{{\mathcal{S}}^{2}_{\beta}}
(see (
4.4
)), we obtain

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\bigg[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\big(e^{\beta Q_{t}}y_{t}^{2}\big)\bigg]\leq 578\lambda(C_{Q}+1)\big(\|w\|_{{\mathcal{S}}^{2}_{\beta}}^{2}+\|v\|^{2}_{{\mathcal{L}}^{2}_{\beta}(M)}\big),

which shows that (
4.7
) is satisfied (recall that
\lambda=2\beta^{-1}\widehat{L}^{2}
). Since inequalities (
4.6
) and (
4.7
) are now confirmed, we conclude that the desired inequality (
4.3
) is valid as well and thus the proof of the theorem is now complete.

\Box

5
Solution to the Linear BSDE

When examining the special case of the linear BSDE, we work under two additional postulates, which will allow us to find an explicit representation
for the component
Y
of a unique solution
(Y,Z)
. The first one concerns the structure of the predictable covariation process
\langle M\rangle
.

Assumption 5.1
.

The matrix
m_{t}
in representation (
2.1
) of the predictable covariation process
\langle M\rangle
is invertible for all
t\in[0,T]
.

Assumption
5.1
allows us to define the auxiliary function
\widehat{g}(t,y,z):=g(t,y,m^{-1}_{t}z)
. It is readily seen that if
g
is an arbitrary
uniformly
m
-Lipschitz continuous generator, then the function
\widehat{g}
is uniformly Lipschitz continuous since we have

|\widehat{g}(t,y_{1},z_{1})-\widehat{g}(t,y_{2},z_{2})|\leq\widehat{L}\big(|y_{1}-y_{2}|+\|z_{1}-z_{2}\|\big).

We also impose a specific structure on the
n
-dimensional martingale
M
.

Assumption 5.2
.

We assume that
M=(M^{1},\dots,M^{n})^{*}=(M^{c},M^{d})^{\ast}
where the process
M^{c}:=(M^{1},\dots,M^{k})^{*}
(respectively,
M^{d}=(M^{k+1},\dots,M^{n})^{*}
) is an
\mathbb{R}^{k}
-valued, continuous martingale (respectively, an
\mathbb{R}^{n-k}
-valued, purely discontinuous martingale) for some
k\in\{0,1,\dots,n\}
.

Remark 5.1
.

We stress that Assumption
5.2
is not directly concerned with the dynamics of asset prices, but rather with modeling of underlying sources for various kinds of financial risks. Therefore, it is not restrictive when dealing with practical applications of BSDEs driven by an RCLL martingale to problems arising in financial mathematics (see, in particular, Dumitrescu et al.
[
8
]
or Kim et al.
[
19
,
20
]
for recent studies of American and game options in nonlinear markets).

Let us denote
n_{t}:=m_{t}^{-1}
so that
n_{t}^{-1}=m_{t}=m_{t}^{\ast}
. Under Assumption
5.2
, we have that
\langle M^{i},M^{j}\rangle=0
for all
i,j
such that
i\leq k
and
j\geq k+1
. Therefore, we may and do assume that
m
is a symmetric block matrix and thus the notation
n^{c}_{t}=(m^{c}_{t})^{-1}
and
n^{d}_{t}=(m^{d}_{t})^{-1}
where
m^{c}_{t}
and
m^{d}_{t}
are symmetric random matrices of dimensions
k
and
n-k
, respectively, can be easily justified. By the same token, for any
z=(z_{1},\dots,z_{n})^{*}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
we write
z=(z^{c},z^{d})^{*}
where
z^{c}=(z_{1},\dots,z_{k})
and
z^{d}=(z_{k+1},\dots,z_{n})
. An analogous notational convention can be applied to the process
Z
, so that we may write
Z=(Z^{c},Z^{d})^{*}
and to other stochastic processes of interest.

We now examine the linear BSDE

\left\{\begin{array}[c]{ll}dY_{t}=-\big(a_{t}Y_{t}+b_{t}+c^{*}_{t}m^{c}_{t}Z^{c}_{t}+d^{*}_{t}m^{d}_{t}Z^{d}_{t}\big)\,dQ_{t}+Z_{t}^{\ast}\,dM_{t}+dD_{t},\\
Y_{T}=\eta,\end{array}\right.

(5.1)

and we work under the following variant of Assumption
2.3
.

Assumption 5.3
.

The triplet
(g,\eta,D)
is such that: (i) the generator
g
equals
g(t,y,z)=a_{t}y+b_{t}+c^{*}_{t}m^{c}_{t}z^{c}+d^{*}_{t}m^{d}_{t}z^{d}
where the real-valued processes
a,c
and
d
are bounded and

b
belongs to
{\mathcal{H}}^{2}(Q)
. (ii) the random variable
\eta
belongs to
L^{2}({\mathcal{F}}_{T})
, (iii)
D
is an RCLL process of finite variation and belongs to
{\mathcal{H}}^{2}(Q)
; furthermore,

|D|_{T}\in L^{2}({\mathcal{F}}_{T})
where
|D|=(|D|_{t},\,t\in[0,T])
is the total variation process of
D
.

Remark 5.2
.

Notice that the total variation of
D
satisfies

\displaystyle|D|_{T}\geq\sum_{u\leq t}|\Delta D_{u}|\geq\bigg(\sum_{u\leq t}(\Delta D_{u})^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}.

Hence we deduce from part (iii) in Assumption
5.3
that
{\mathbb{E}}\big(\sum_{u\leq t}(\Delta D_{u})^{2}\big)<\infty
.

Since the processes
a,c,d
are assumed to be bounded, the generator
g(t,y,z)=a_{t}y+b_{t}+c^{*}_{t}m^{c}_{t}z^{c}+d^{*}_{t}m^{d}_{t}z^{d}
is
uniformly
m
-Lipschitz continuous and the process
g(\cdot,0,0)=c
belongs to
{\mathcal{H}}^{2}(Q)
. Therefore, we may apply Theorem
4.1

to ensure that the BSDE (
5.1
) has a unique solution
(Y,Z)\in{\mathcal{S}}^{2}
provided that Assumptions
2.1
-
2.2
and
5.1
-
5.3
are satisfied. Our goal is to find an explicit representation for the process
Y
, which is convenient to establish the comparison property.

Before stating the next result, we need to introduce some notation. For an arbitrary
\mathbb{R}^{n-k}
-valued process
d
, which is assumed to be bounded and
\mathbb{F}
-predictable, we define the RCLL,
\mathbb{F}
-adapted process
q
by

q_{t}:=\mathcal{E}_{t}(\widehat{Q})\mathcal{E}_{t}(\widehat{M})=\mathcal{E}_{t}(\widehat{Q}+\widehat{M})

(5.2)

where
\widehat{Q}
is a continuous, bounded process of finite variation given by
\widehat{Q}_{t}:=\int_{0}^{t}a_{u}\,dQ_{u}
and
\widehat{M}

is an
\mathbb{F}
-martingale defined by

\widehat{M}_{t}:=\int_{0}^{t}c^{*}_{u}n^{c}_{u}\,dM^{c}_{u}+\int_{0}^{t}d^{\ast}_{u}n^{d}_{u}\,dM^{d}_{u}=\widehat{M}^{c}_{t}+\widehat{M}^{d}_{t}.

(5.3)

Here
\mathcal{E}(\widehat{M})
denotes the Doléans exponential given by the following expression

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{t}(\widehat{M})=\exp\Big(\widehat{M}_{t}-\frac{1}{2}\langle\widehat{M}^{c}\rangle_{t}\Big)\prod_{0<u\leq t}\big(1+\Delta\widehat{M}_{u}\big)e^{-\Delta\widehat{M}_{u}}.

For brevity, we denote

\Psi^{q,D}_{t}:=\int_{0}^{t}q_{u-}\,dD_{u}+\langle q,D\rangle_{t}.

(5.4)

We are ready to derive an explicit representation for the process
Y
in the unique solution to the linear BSDE.

Proposition 5.1
.

If Assumptions
2.1
-
2.2
and
5.1
-
5.3
hold,
then the linear BSDE (
5.1
) has a unique solution
(Y,Z)\in{\mathcal{S}}^{2}
and the process
Y
satisfies

q_{t}Y_{t}={\mathbb{E}}\bigg[q_{T}\eta+\int_{t}^{T}q_{u}b_{u}\,dQ_{u}-\big(\Psi^{q,D}_{T}-\Psi^{q,D}_{t}\big)\,\Big|\,\mathcal{F}_{t}\bigg]

(5.5)

where
q
and
\Psi^{q,D}
are given by (
5.2
) and (
5.4
), respectively.

Proof.
Since
\widehat{Q}
is an
\mathbb{F}
-adapted, bounded, continuous process of finite variation and
\widehat{M}
is an
\mathbb{F}
-martingale given by (
5.3
),
it is obvious that
[\widehat{Q},\widehat{M}]=0
. Recall that
\mathcal{E}(\widehat{M})
is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{t}(\widehat{M})=\exp\Big(\widehat{M}_{t}-\frac{1}{2}\langle\widehat{M}^{c}\rangle_{t}\Big)\prod_{0<u\leq t}\big(1+\Delta\widehat{M}_{u}\big)e^{-\Delta\widehat{M}_{u}}

where
\widehat{M}^{c}_{t}=\int_{0}^{t}c^{*}_{u}n^{c}_{u}\,dM^{c}_{u}
and
\Delta\widehat{M}_{u}:=\widehat{M}_{u}-\widehat{M}_{u-}=\widehat{M}^{d}_{u}-\widehat{M}^{d}_{u-}=d^{*}_{u}n^{d}_{u}\Delta M^{d}_{u}
.
Simple computations show that
q
can be represented as follows

\displaystyle q_{t}=\exp\left(\int_{0}^{t}a_{u}\,dQ_{u}\right)\exp\left(\widehat{M}^{c}_{t}-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\|c_{u}\|^{2}\,dQ_{u}\right)\exp\big(\widehat{M}^{d}_{t}\big)\prod_{0<u\leq t}\big(1+\Delta\widehat{M}^{d}_{u}\big)e^{-\Delta\widehat{M}^{d}_{u}}.

In view of (
5.2
) and (
5.3
), we have that

\displaystyle dq_{t}=q_{t-}\,d(\widehat{Q}_{t}+\widehat{M}_{t})=q_{t-}\,d\widehat{Q}_{t}+q_{t-}\,d\widehat{M}_{t}=q_{t-}\big(a_{t}\,dQ_{t}+c^{*}_{t}n^{c}_{t}\,dM^{c}_{t}+d^{\ast}_{t}n^{d}_{t}\,dM^{d}_{t}\big)

and thus, using (
5.1
), we obtain

d\langle q,Y\rangle_{t}=q_{t}\big(c^{*}_{t}m^{c}_{t}Z^{c}_{t}+d^{\ast}_{t}m^{d}_{t}Z^{d}_{t}\big)\,dQ_{t}+\langle q,D\rangle_{t}

where we denote
Z=(Z^{c},Z^{d})^{*}
. Since
D
is an RCLL process of finite variation, and we have

[q,D]_{t}=\sum_{u\leq t}\Delta q_{u}\Delta D_{u}

where
\Delta q_{t}=q_{t}-q_{t-}
and
\Delta D_{t}=D_{t}-D_{t-}
. Using the Itô integration by parts formula, we obtain

\displaystyle d(q_{t}Y_{t})

\displaystyle=q_{t-}\,dY_{t}+Y_{t-}\,dq_{t}+d[q,Y]_{t}

\displaystyle=-q_{t}\big(a_{t}Y_{t}+b_{t}+c^{*}_{t}m^{c}_{t}Z^{c}_{t}+d^{*}_{t}m^{d}_{t}Z^{d}_{t}\big)\,dQ_{t}+q_{t-}Z_{t}^{\ast}\,dM_{t}+q_{t-}\,dD_{t}

\displaystyle\quad\mbox{}+q_{t}Y_{t}a_{t}\,dQ_{t}+q_{t-}Y_{t-}\big(c^{*}_{t}n^{c}_{t}\,dM^{c}_{t}+d^{\ast}_{t}n^{d}_{t}\,dM^{d}_{t}\big)+d\langle q,Y\rangle_{t}+d[q,Y]_{t}-d\langle q,Y\rangle_{t}

\displaystyle=d\widetilde{M}_{t}-q_{t}\big(b_{t}+c^{*}_{t}m^{c}_{t}Z^{c}_{t}+d^{*}_{t}m^{d}_{t}Z^{d}_{t}\big)\,dQ_{t}+q_{t}\big(c^{*}_{t}m^{c}_{t}Z^{c}_{t}+d^{\ast}_{t}m^{d}_{t}Z^{d}_{t}\big)\,dQ_{t}+q_{t-}\,dD_{t}+d\langle q,D\rangle_{t}

\displaystyle=d\widetilde{M}_{t}-q_{t}b_{t}\,dQ_{t}+d\Psi^{q,D}_{t}

where the RCLL process
\widetilde{M}
is defined by

d\widetilde{M}_{t}=q_{t-}\big(Z_{t}^{\ast}\,dM_{t}+Y_{t-}c^{*}_{t}n^{c}_{t}\,dM^{c}_{t}+Y_{t-}d^{\ast}_{t}n^{d}_{t}\,dM^{d}_{t}\big)+d[q,Y]_{t}-d\langle q,Y\rangle_{t}

where
\widetilde{M}_{0}:=q_{0}Y_{0}=Y_{0}
and the process
N:=[q,Y]-\langle q,Y\rangle
is an
\mathbb{F}
-local martingale with
N_{0}=0
. We also have that

\displaystyle\widetilde{M}_{T}-\widetilde{M}_{t}=q_{T}\eta-q_{t}Y_{t}+\int_{t}^{T}q_{u}b_{u}\,dQ_{u}-(\Psi^{q,D}_{T}-\Psi^{q,D}_{t})

and thus, in view of Lemma
5.1
, the process
\widetilde{M}
is an
\mathbb{F}
-martingale. Hence, after taking the conditional expectation with respect
to
\mathcal{F}_{t}
, we obtain (
5.5
).

\Box

The following lemma is required for the proof of Proposition
5.1
and Theorem
6.1
.

Lemma 5.1
.

If Assumptions
2.1
-
2.2
and
5.1
-
5.3
are satisfied, then the process
\widetilde{M}
given by

\widetilde{M}_{t}=q_{t}Y_{t}-\Psi^{q,D}_{t}+\int_{0}^{t}q_{u}b_{u}\,dQ_{u},

(5.6)

is an
\mathbb{F}
-martingale.

Proof.
We claim that

{\mathbb{E}}\bigg[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|\widetilde{M}_{t}|\bigg]<\infty,

(5.7)

which is known to imply that
\widetilde{M}
is a uniformly integrable
\mathbb{F}
-martingale. Our goal is thus to establish (
5.7
). For this purpose,
we start by examining the process
q
. Since
c
and
d
are bounded, we have, for some constant
C
,

\langle\widehat{M}\rangle_{T}=\int_{0}^{T}\big(|c_{t}|^{2}+|d_{t}|^{2}\big)\,dQ_{t}\leq C<\infty.

(5.8)

We will deduce from (
5.2
) that

\|q\|^{2}_{{\mathcal{S}}^{2}}={\mathbb{E}}\bigg[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}q_{t}^{2}\bigg]<\infty.

(5.9)

To this end, we first observe that (
5.2
) gives
q_{t}=\mathcal{E}_{t}(\widehat{Q})\mathcal{E}_{t}(\widehat{M})
where the process
\mathcal{E}(\widehat{Q})
is bounded since
a

and
Q
are bounded. To establish (
5.9
), it thus suffices to show that

\|\mathcal{E}(\widehat{M})\|^{2}_{{\mathcal{S}}^{2}}={\mathbb{E}}\bigg[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\mathcal{E}_{t}^{2}(\widehat{M})\bigg]<\infty.

(5.10)

Observe that

\displaystyle d\mathcal{E}_{t}(\widehat{M})=\mathcal{E}_{t-}(\widehat{M})\,d\widehat{M}_{t}=\mathcal{E}_{t-}(\widehat{M})\,d\widehat{M}^{c}_{t}+\mathcal{E}_{t-}(\widehat{M})\,d\widehat{M}^{d}_{t}

and thus

\displaystyle d[\mathcal{E}(\widehat{M})]_{t}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{E}_{t-}^{2}(\widehat{M})\,d\langle\widehat{M}^{c}\rangle_{t}+\mathcal{E}_{t-}^{2}(\widehat{M})\,d[\widehat{M}^{d}]_{t}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{E}_{t-}^{2}(\widehat{M})\big(d\langle\widehat{M}^{c}\rangle_{t}+d\langle\widehat{M}^{d}\rangle_{t}\big)+\mathcal{E}_{t-}^{2}(\widehat{M})\big(d[\widehat{M}^{d}]_{t}-d\langle\widehat{M}^{d}\rangle_{t}\big),

which in turn yields

\displaystyle d\mathcal{E}_{t}^{2}(\widehat{M})

\displaystyle=2\mathcal{E}_{t-}(\widehat{M})\,d\mathcal{E}_{t}(\widehat{M})+d[\mathcal{E}(\widehat{M})]_{t}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{E}_{t-}^{2}(\widehat{M})\big(d\langle\widehat{M}^{c}\rangle_{t}+d\langle\widehat{M}^{d}\rangle_{t}\big)+\mathcal{E}_{t-}^{2}(\widehat{M})\big(2\,d\widehat{M}_{t}+d[\widehat{M}^{d}]_{t}-d\langle\widehat{M}^{d}\rangle_{t}\big).

Recalling that
\mathcal{E}_{0}(\widehat{M})=1
, we obtain
\mathcal{E}_{t}^{2}(\widehat{M})=\gamma_{t}\exp\big(\langle\widehat{M}^{c}\rangle_{t}+\langle\widehat{M}^{d}\rangle_{t}\big)

where
\gamma_{0}=1
and

\displaystyle d\gamma_{t}=\gamma_{t-}\Big(2\,d\widehat{M}_{t}+d[\widehat{M}^{d}]_{t}-d\langle\widehat{M}^{d}\rangle_{t}\Big).

From the last two equations, it follows that
\gamma
is a non-negative local martingale and hence a supermartingale,
so that
{\mathbb{E}}[\gamma_{t}]\leq{\mathbb{E}}[\gamma_{0}]=1
for all
t\in[0,T]
. It is clear from (
5.8
) that there exists a constant
C
such that, for all
t\in[0,T]

\displaystyle\exp\big(\langle\widehat{M}^{c}\rangle_{t}+\langle\widehat{M}^{d}\rangle_{t}\big)\leq C.

Consequently, it follows that
\mathcal{E}_{t}^{2}(\widehat{M})\leq C\gamma_{t}
for all
t\in[0,T]
. Since
\mathcal{E}^{2}(\widehat{M})
and
\gamma
are RCLL processes, we deduce that

\mathcal{E}_{\tau}^{2}(\widehat{M})\leq C\gamma_{\tau}
for all
\tau\in\mathcal{T}
where
\mathcal{T}
is the set of all stopping times taking values in
[0,T]
.
By applying the optional stopping theorem to
\gamma
, we get

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\Big[\mathcal{E}_{\tau}^{2}(\widehat{M})\Big]\leq C\,{\mathbb{E}}\big[\gamma_{\tau}\big]\leq C,\text{ for all }\tau\in\mathcal{T}.

Hence the family
\{\mathcal{E}_{\tau}(\widehat{M})\}_{\tau\in\mathcal{T}}
is uniformly integrable, which implies that
\mathcal{E}(\widehat{M})

is a nonnegative RCLL martingale. From Doob’s inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\bigg[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\mathcal{E}_{t}^{2}(\widehat{M})\bigg]\leq 4\,{\mathbb{E}}\big[\mathcal{E}_{T}^{2}(\widehat{M})\big]\leq 4C,

which shows that (
5.10
) is valid and thus (
5.9
) holds as well.

We are now ready to analyze the right-hand side of (
5.6
). It is clear that

\displaystyle\big|q_{t}Y_{t}-\Psi^{q,D}_{t}\big|\leq|q_{t}||Y_{t}-D_{t}|+|q_{t}||D_{t}|+(|D_{0}|+|D_{T}|)\sup_{t\in[0,T]}q_{t}+\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|\langle q,D\rangle_{t}|.

Since
D
is assumed to be a process of finite variation with the total variation process
|D|
such that
{\mathbb{E}}(|D|^{2}_{T})<\infty
, we have
\sup_{\,t\in[0,T]}D^{2}_{t}\leq 2(D^{2}_{0}+|D|^{2}_{T})<\infty
and thus
D
belongs to
{\mathcal{S}}^{2}
. Of course, the process

is in
{\mathcal{S}}^{2}
as well (see Theorem
4.1
). Using the Kunita-Watanabe inequality (see, for instance, Theorem 8.3 in He et al.
[
14
]
), we obtain, for all

,

\displaystyle|\langle q,D\rangle_{t}|\leq(\langle q\rangle_{t}\langle D\rangle_{t})^{1/2}\leq 2(\langle q\rangle_{t}+\langle D\rangle_{t})\leq 2(\langle q\rangle_{T}+\langle D\rangle_{T})

and thus

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\bigg[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|\langle q,D\rangle_{t}|\bigg]\leq 2\big({\mathbb{E}}(\langle q\rangle_{T})+{\mathbb{E}}(\langle D\rangle_{T})\big).

Since
D
is a process of finite variation, we have
[D]_{t}=\sum_{u\leq t}(\Delta D_{u})^{2}
and, from part (iii) in Assumption
5.3
,
we infer that
{\mathbb{E}}([D]_{T})<\infty
(see Remark
5.2
), which in turn implies that
{\mathbb{E}}(\langle D\rangle_{T})<\infty
. Similarly, since

\displaystyle dq_{t}=d(\mathcal{E}_{t}(\widehat{Q})\mathcal{E}_{t}(\widehat{M}))=\mathcal{E}_{t}(\widehat{Q})\,d\mathcal{E}_{t}(\widehat{M})+\mathcal{E}_{t-}(\widehat{M})\,d\mathcal{E}_{t}(\widehat{Q})

and
\langle\mathcal{E}(\widehat{Q})\rangle=\langle\mathcal{E}(\widehat{Q}),\mathcal{E}(\widehat{M})\rangle=0
, using the boundedness of the processes
\mathcal{E}(\widehat{Q})
and
\langle\widehat{M}\rangle

we obtain

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}(\langle q\rangle_{T})={\mathbb{E}}\bigg[\int_{0}^{T}\mathcal{E}^{2}_{t}(\widehat{Q})\mathcal{E}^{2}_{t-}(\widehat{M})\,d\langle\widehat{M}\rangle_{t}\bigg]\leq C\|\mathcal{E}(\widehat{M})\|^{2}_{{\mathcal{S}}^{2}}.

We conclude that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\bigg[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\big|q_{t}Y_{t}-\Psi^{q,D}_{t}\big|\bigg]\leq C\big[\|q\|_{{\mathcal{S}}^{2}}(\|y-D\|_{{\mathcal{S}}^{2}}+\|D\|_{{\mathcal{S}}^{2}})+{\mathbb{E}}(\langle D\rangle_{T})+\|\mathcal{E}(\widehat{M})\|^{2}_{{\mathcal{S}}^{2}}\big]<\infty

where we have also used (
5.9
) and (
5.10
).

Now let us study the integral
\int_{0}^{t}q_{u}b_{u}\,dQ_{u}
. Using the boundedness of

which may very line by line such that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\bigg[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\Big|\int_{0}^{t}q_{u}b_{u}\,dQ_{u}\Big|\bigg]

\displaystyle\leq{\mathbb{E}}\bigg[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\bigg(\int_{0}^{t}q^{2}_{u}b^{2}_{u}\,dQ_{u}\bigg)^{1/2}Q_{T}^{1/2}\bigg]\leq C\,{\mathbb{E}}\bigg[\bigg(\int_{0}^{T}q^{2}_{u}b^{2}_{u}\,dQ_{u}\bigg)^{1/2}\bigg]

\displaystyle\leq C\,{\mathbb{E}}\bigg[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}q_{t}\bigg(\int_{0}^{T}b^{2}_{u}\,dQ_{u}\bigg)^{1/2}\bigg]\leq 2C(\|q\|_{{\mathcal{S}}^{2}}+\|b\|_{{\mathcal{H}}^{2}(Q)})<\infty

where we have also used (
5.9
) in the last inequality. We have thus shown that (
5.7
) holds.

6
Strict Comparison Theorem

An important feature of solutions to a BSDE is the so-called (strict)

(see the statement of Theorem
6.1
), which underpins several important applications of BSDEs to arbitrage-free pricing. Therefore, our final goal is to establish the comparison theorem for the BSDE (
3.1
) within the setup given by Assumptions
2.1
,
2.2
,
5.1
and
5.2
. To prove the comparison property of solutions to the BSDE (
3.1
) by the well-known method of linearization, we define the following auxiliary processes

a_{t}:=\frac{g^{1}(t,Y^{1}_{t},Z^{1}_{t})-g^{1}(t,Y^{2}_{t},Z^{1}_{t})}{Y^{1}_{t}-Y^{2}_{t}}\,\mathds{1}_{\{Y^{1}_{t}\neq Y^{2}_{t}\}},

\displaystyle c^{1}_{t}:=\frac{1}{\widetilde{Z}^{1}_{t,1}-\widetilde{Z}^{2}_{t,1}}\Big[\widehat{g}^{1}(t,Y^{2}_{t},\widetilde{Z}^{1}_{t,1},\widetilde{Z}^{1}_{t,2}\ldots,\ldots,\widetilde{Z}^{1}_{t,d})-\widehat{g}^{1}(t,Y^{2}_{t},\widetilde{Z}^{2}_{t,1},\widetilde{Z}^{1}_{t,2}\ldots,\ldots,\widetilde{Z}^{1}_{t,d})\Big]\mathds{1}_{\widetilde{D}_{1}},

and, for every
j=2,3,\ldots,k
,

\displaystyle c^{j}_{t}

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{\widetilde{Z}^{1}_{t,j}-\widetilde{Z}^{2}_{t,j}}\Big[\widehat{g}^{1}(t,Y^{2}_{t},\widetilde{Z}^{2}_{t,1},\ldots,\widetilde{Z}^{2}_{t,j-1},\widetilde{Z}^{1}_{t,j},\widetilde{Z}^{1}_{t,j+1},\ldots,\widetilde{Z}^{1}_{t,d})

\displaystyle-\widehat{g}^{1}(t,Y^{2}_{t},\widetilde{Z}^{2}_{t,1},\ldots,\widetilde{Z}^{2}_{t,j-1},\widetilde{Z}^{2}_{t,j},\widetilde{Z}^{1}_{t,j+1},\ldots,\widetilde{Z}^{1}_{t,d})\Big]\mathds{1}_{\widetilde{D}_{j}}

where
\widetilde{Z}_{t,j}^{i}:=(m_{t}Z_{t}^{i})_{j}
for

and
\widetilde{D}_{j}:=\{\widetilde{Z}^{1}_{t,j}\neq\widetilde{Z}^{2}_{t,j}\}
for

.
For brevity, we denote by

the
\mathbb{R}^{k}
-valued process

. Recall that

and thus if

is uniformly

The following result extends the comparison theorem established in

\mu_{Q}(A)={\mathbb{E}}\big[\int_{0}^{T}\mathds{1}_{A}(\omega,t)\,dQ_{t}(\omega)\big]

.

Suppose that Assumptions

,
2.2

5.1

5.2

\mathbb{F}
-progressively measurable process
m
.
Let Assumption

where the process

Proof.

(6.2)

On a set of some recent contributions to energy equality for the Navier-Stokes equations.
Hugo Beirão da Veiga, Jiaqi Yang

Abstract
In these notes we want, in addition to presenting some recent results, to both clean up and refine some reflections on a couple of articles published on paper a few years ago, 2019-20. These papers concerned integral sufficient conditions on u , u\,, ∇ u , \nabla u\,, and mixed, to guarantee the equality of the energy, EE in the sequel, for solutions of the Navier-Stokes equations under the classical non-slip boundary condition. Concerning the ∇ u \nabla u case a crucial role was enjoyed by a previous well known Berselli and Chiodaroli’s pioneering 2019 work on the subject. The above three papers are the main sources of these notes. References will be mostly concentrated on their direct relation to the above papers at the time of pubblication. More recent results will be not stated throughout the article. However, in the last section, the reader will be suitably sent to the more recent bibliography. Below, we also turn back to the innovative interpretation of some main parameters which allowed to overcome their apparent incongruence. Non-Newtonian fluids were also considered in our 2019 paper, maybe for the first time in the above Berselli-Chiodaroli’s particular ∇ u \nabla u\, context. However we will stick mostly to the Newtonian case since in the end we come to the conclusion that there are not particular additional obstacles to extend the present results from Newtonian to non-Newtonian fluids. Hence we avoid to go further in this direction.

1
Introduction. The energy equality and inequality.

For a classical treatment of the energy equality problem for the Navier-Stokes equations we may recommend the fourth chapter of Galdi’s work
[
12
]
. This reference also provides an expert treatment of the most important classic topics on the Navier-Stokes equations. Another main reference on the subject is Temam’s famous treatise
[
20
]
.

In the following we are interested on integral assumptions which guarantees the energy equality (EE in the sequel) for weak (Leray-Hopf) solutions to Newtonian incompressible fluids

\begin{cases}{\partial}_{t}u+\,u\cdot\nabla\,u-\Delta u+\nabla\pi=0\,,\quad&\textrm{in $\Omega\times(0,T)$}\,,\\
\nabla\cdot\,u=0\,,\quad&\textrm{in $\Omega\times(0,T)$}\,,\\
u=0\,,\quad&\textrm{on ${\partial}\Omega\times(0,T)$}\,,\\
u(\cdot,\,0)=\,u_{0}\,,\quad&\textrm{in $\Omega$}\,,\end{cases}

(1.1)

where
\,{\Omega}\,
is a bounded, smooth domain in
\,\mathbb{R}^{3}\,.
The energy equality reads

\int_{\Omega}|u(t_{0})|^{2}dx+2\int_{0}^{t_{0}}\int_{\Omega}|{\nabla}u(\tau)|^{2}\,dxd\tau=\int_{\Omega}|u_{0}|^{2}dx\,,\quad\textrm{for any}\quad t_{0}\in[0,T).

(1.2)

Presently we have arrived at the conclusion that comparing the effectiveness of distinct integrability conditions yielding the energy equality is not suitable, or needs further ideas, when the number of space derivatives is different, or when the type of spaces to compare is too different (comparison between Sobolev and Hölder spaces, for example). This is now quite evident, and we have decided not to loose too much room for explanation in this regard. Since we have been interested on assumptions on the solution
u
itself and on its first derivatives
\nabla u\,,
we separate assumptions of
u-
type and assumptions of
\nabla u-
type, and appeal to two distinct SH numbers,
\theta
and
\theta^{*},
as useful tools. This useful device had not been used in reference
[
1
]
.

2
Assumptions of
\,u-
type. The Newtonian case.

In reference
[
18
]
M.Shinbrot shows that if a weak Leray-Hopf solution
\,u\,
to the Navier-Stokes equations (
1.1
) satisfies

u\in\,L^{p}(0,\,T;L^{s}(\Omega))\,,

(2.1)

where

\frac{2}{p}+\frac{2}{s}=\,1\,,\quad\textrm{and}\quad s\geq\,4,

(2.2)

then
\,u\,
satisfies the energy equality. This result is a generalization of previous results due to G.Prodi
[
17
]
and J.L.Lions
[
16
]
, where these authors proved the above result for
p=\,s=\,4\,.

For convenience we write the condition (
2.1
), (
2.2
) in the equivalent form

u\in\,L^{\frac{2s}{s-2}}(0,\,T;L^{s}(\Omega))\,,\quad s\geq 4\,.

(2.3)

Definition 2.1
.

For
u
satisfying (
2.1
) and (
2.2
), we call
Shinbrot number
(SH) the quantity

\theta(p,s)=:\frac{2}{p}+\frac{2}{s}\,.

(2.4)

For
u
satisfying

u\in\,L^{p}(0,\,T;W^{1,\,q}(\Omega))\,,

(2.5)

with
1<q<3\,,
we define the SH number

\theta^{*}(p,q)=:\frac{2}{p}+\frac{2}{q^{*}}\,,\quad q^{*}=\frac{3q}{3-q}\,.

(2.6)

The role in (
2.6
) of Sobolev’s embedding theorem
W^{1,q}\subset L^{q^{*}}
is clear.

In the following
H
denotes the completion of
\mathcal{V}=\{\phi\in C^{\infty}_{0}({\Omega}):\,\nabla\cdot\phi=\,0\,\}
in
L^{2}(\Omega)
.

In reference
[
2
]
Theorem 1.1 we have shown that Shinbrot’s criteria follows trivially, by interpolation, from the
\,L^{4}((0\,,T)\times{\Omega}))
particular case. Moreover we have extended Shinbrot’s result to the case (ii) below, as follows.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
u_{0}\in H
and let
u
be a Leray-Hopf weak solution of the Navier-Stokes equations (
1.1
). Assume that
u
satisfies one of the two conditions below:

\begin{cases}(i)\quad u\in\,L^{p}(0,\,T;L^{s}(\Omega))\,,\quad s\in[4,\,\infty]\,,\quad p=\frac{2s}{s-2}.\\
(ii)\quad u\in\,L^{p}(0,\,T;L^{s}({\Omega}))\,,\quad s\in[3,\,4]\,,\quad p=\frac{s}{s-3}.\end{cases}

(2.7)

Then
u
satisfies the energy equality

\|u(t_{0})\|_{2}^{2}\,+\,2\,\int_{0}^{t_{0}}\,\|\nabla u(\tau)\|_{2}^{2}\,d\tau=\,\|u_{0}\|_{2}^{2}\,,

(2.8)

for any
t_{0}\in[0,T)
, with
\theta=1
in case (i) and
\,\theta=2-\frac{4}{s}<1\,
in case (ii).

The results in item (i) and (ii) glue for the common value
r=4
. It looks significant that in correspondence to the extreme values
s=3
and
s=\infty
, and
only
for these two values, the assumptions imply the result simply by appealing to the classical Ladyzhenskaya-Prodi-Serrin (L-P-S) type conditions for regularity

u\in\,L^{p}(0,\,T;L^{q}(\Omega))\,,\quad\frac{2}{p}+\frac{3}{q}=\,1\,,\quad\textrm{for}\quad q\in[3,\,\infty].

(2.9)

In fact, our conditions become
u\in L^{\infty}(L^{3})
and
u\in L^{2}(L^{\infty})\,,
respectively.

The result in item (ii) succeed in proving, as a particular case, the result stated in item (j), Theorem 3.3 below, for
\,q\leq\frac{12}{7}\,
. In fact the following result holds.

Proposition 2.3
.

Assume that

{\nabla}u\in\,L^{\frac{q}{2q-3}}(0,\,T;L^{q}({\Omega}))\,,\quad\frac{3}{2}\leq q\leq\frac{12}{7}\,.

(2.10)

Then item (ii) in Theorem
2.2
, namely,

u\in\,L^{\frac{s}{s-3}}(0,\,T;L^{s}({\Omega}))\,,\quad 3\leq s\leq 4\,,

(2.11)

holds true.

Proof.

By a Sobolev’s embedding theorem it follows that (
2.10
) implies that

u\in\,L^{\frac{q}{2q-3}}(0,\,T;L^{\frac{3q}{3-q}}({\Omega}))\,,\quad\frac{3}{2}\leq q\leq\frac{12}{7}\,,

(2.12)

which is equivalent to (
2.11
), as shown by setting
\,s=\frac{3q}{3-q}
.
∎

Note that the full range in item (ii) was used in the proof. Moreover we have shown that (
2.12
) yields EE.

Remark 2.1
.

We have just shown two partial generalizations of the above referred item (j). Question: Which of the two extensions is more general? Actually, item (k) would be much stronger than (
2.12
) by assuming that it is equivalent to
\,u\in\,L^{\frac{6q}{5q-6}}(L^{\frac{3q}{3-q}})\,
. However it does not even imply the (weaker) assumption (
2.12
), which yields EE either. This remark has a general character, applicable each time we appeal to embedding theorems, which may lead to interesting, but rough, comparisons.

Is is worth noting that for
3\leq s\leq 4\,
the SH number
\theta^{*}
of assumption (
2.10
)
(write it in
s-
terms) is always equal to
1\,.
However the more general assumption (ii), which yields (
2.10
), has a ”worse” SH number
\theta=\,2-\frac{4}{s}<\,1\,.
This shows, once more, that comparison between
\theta
and
\theta^{*}
by means of Sobolev’s embedding Theorem is questionable.

\int_{\Omega}|u(t_{0})|^{2}dx+2\int_{0}^{t_{0}}\int_{\Omega}|D(u)(\tau)|^{r}\,dxd\tau=\int_{\Omega}|u_{0}|^{2}dx\,,

5
A new reading key of Shinbrot’s type numbers.

This chapter is of particular interest here. It concerns the comparison of the strength of distinct results by appealing to the SH-numbers. The classical SH number
\theta\,
looks significant in the
u-
case, but in
\nabla u-
case the SH-number
\theta^{*}\,
is totally inappropriate. In fact the SH number
\theta^{*}\,
obtained simply from
\theta\,
by appealing to Sobolev’s embedding, shows a dramatic dependence on
q
. However a new point of view gives meaning to this attempt, as shows in the Theorem below. As still claimed in reference
[
1
]
this result holds for
r\neq 2\,
, with essentially the same proof (which does not use the
r-
weak estimate).

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
u_{0}\in H\,,
and let
u
be a Leray-Hopf weak solution of (
1.1
) in a smooth bounded domain
\Omega
. Further assume that one of the two following assumptions hold.

(i)

u\in L^{p_{1}}(0,T;L^{q_{1}}(\Omega))\cap L^{\frac{9q}{8q-9}}(0,T;W^{1,q}(\Omega))\,,\quad

with
\quad\frac{2}{p_{1}}+\,\frac{2}{q_{1}}=\,\frac{10}{9}\,,\quad

\frac{9}{8}<q\,,\quad
and
\quad q_{1}\geq\,\frac{2q}{q-\,1}\,.

(ii)

u\in L^{\frac{5q}{5q-6}}(0,T;W^{1,q}(\Omega))\,,\quad
with
\quad\frac{9}{5}<\,q\,.

Then
u
satisfies the energy equality

\|u(t_{0})\|_{2}^{2}+2\int_{0}^{t_{0}}\|D(u)(\tau)\|^{2}_{2}d\tau=\|u_{0}\|_{2}^{2}\,,

for all
t_{0}\in\,[0,T).

Remark 5.1
.

In item (i) both spaces enjoy the same Shinbrot’s number
\,\theta=\theta^{*}=\frac{10}{9}\,,

for all
q<3\,.
Hence our main aim is fulfilled: Independently of the value of
q\,
all the assumptions (i) have the same SH number
\frac{10}{9}\,.
However, for
q>\,\frac{9}{5}\,,
item (i) holds but is overshadowed by the more general item (ii). This overlap does not invalidate its broad validity (note that item (ii) has SH-number
\theta^{*}>\frac{10}{9}\,
).

Remark 5.2
.

— For
q<\,\frac{9}{5}\,,
since
\frac{9q}{8q-9}<\frac{q}{2q-3}\,,
we have weakens the condition
\,\nabla u\in\,L^{\frac{q}{2q-\,3}}(0,\,T;L^{q}({\Omega}))\,
up to the desired level
\theta=\,\frac{10}{9}
enjoyed by
\,\nabla u\in L^{\frac{9q}{8q-9}}(0,T;L^{q}(\Omega))\,,
compensated by the addition of an assumption on
\,u
which enjoys just the same level
\theta=\,\frac{10}{9}
.

— For
q=\,\frac{9}{5}\,
the intersection of the two spaces in item (i) coincides with the second space, and the sufficient condition becomes simply
\,u\in L^{3}(0,\,T;W^{1,\,\frac{9}{5}}(\Omega))\,.
This is just the only case that, in reference
[
4
]
, yields the strongest result (by the
\theta^{*}
criterium), namely
\theta^{*}=\,\frac{10}{9}\,,
always reached up by the new criterium in (i) Theorem
5.1
.

— For
q>\frac{9}{5}\,
the assumption in item (i) is superseded by the broader assumption in item (ii)
1
of Theorem
3.1
since

L^{p_{1}}(0,T;L^{q_{1}}(\Omega))\cap L^{\frac{9q}{8q-9}}(0,T;W^{1,q}(\Omega))\subset L^{\frac{9q}{8q-9}}(0,T;W^{1,q}(\Omega))\subset L^{\frac{5q}{5q-6}}(0,T;W^{1,q}(\Omega))\,.

For this reason it seemed appropriate to have inserted this assumption in the above theorem as item (ii).

In reference
[
4
]
the authors warn against the risk of too simplified comparison between sufficient conditions fo EE in terms of distinct functional spaces, in particular between Sobolev and Hölder spaces. We strongly agree this warn. Actually, even comparison between conditions in terms of
u
and
\nabla u
via Sobolev’s embedding theorems could be misleading, as we still have seen.

]

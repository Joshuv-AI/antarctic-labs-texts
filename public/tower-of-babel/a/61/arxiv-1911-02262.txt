On the infinite loop spaces of algebraic cobordism and the motivic sphere
Tom Bachmann, Elden Elmanto, Marc Hoyois, Adeel A. Khan, Vladimir Sosnilo, Maria Yakerson

Abstract




Abstract.

We obtain geometric models for the infinite loop spaces of the motivic spectra
\mathrm{MGL}
,
\mathrm{MSL}
, and
\mathbf{1}
over a field. They are motivically equivalent to
\mathbf{Z}\times\mathrm{Hilb}_{\infty}^{\mathrm{lci}}(\mathbf{A}^{\infty})^{+}
,
\mathbf{Z}\times\mathrm{Hilb}_{\infty}^{\mathrm{or}}(\mathbf{A}^{\infty})^{+}
, and
\mathbf{Z}\times\mathrm{Hilb}_{\infty}^{\operatorname{fr}}(\mathbf{A}^{\infty})^{+}
, respectively, where
\mathrm{Hilb}_{d}^{\mathrm{lci}}(\mathbf{A}^{n})
(resp.
\mathrm{Hilb}_{d}^{\mathrm{or}}(\mathbf{A}^{n})
,
\mathrm{Hilb}_{d}^{\operatorname{fr}}(\mathbf{A}^{n})
) is the Hilbert scheme of lci points (resp. oriented points, framed points) of degree
d
in
\mathbf{A}^{n}
, and
+
is Quillen’s plus construction. Moreover, we show that the plus construction is redundant in positive characteristic.

Keywords.
Motivic homotopy theory; algebraic cobordism

1. Introduction

Let
k
be a field, let
\mathrm{MGL}
be Voevodsky’s algebraic cobordism spectrum over
k
, and let
\mathcal{FS}\mathrm{yn}
be the moduli stack of finite syntomic
k
-schemes. In
[
EHK+20b
, Corollary 3.4.2]
, we showed that there is an equivalence

{}^{\infty}_{\mathbf{T}}\mathrm{MGL}\simeq L_{\mathrm{mot}}(\mathcal{FS}\mathrm{yn}^{\mathrm{gp}}),

where
\mathcal{FS}\mathrm{yn}^{\mathrm{gp}}
is the group completion of
\mathcal{FS}\mathrm{yn}
under disjoint union and
L_{\mathrm{mot}}
is the motivic localization functor.
This can be regarded as an algebro-geometric analogue of the identification of
{}^{\infty}\mathrm{MO}
with the cobordism space of
0
-dimensional compact smooth manifolds. However, unlike in topology, the group completion is necessary in the above equivalence.
The goal of this paper is to obtain a group-completion-free description of
{}^{\infty}_{\mathbf{T}}\mathrm{MGL}
, which is more accessible for some purposes.
Let us write
\mathcal{FS}\mathrm{yn}_{\infty}
for the colimit of the sequence

\dotsb\to\mathcal{FS}\mathrm{yn}_{d}\xrightarrow{+1}\mathcal{FS}\mathrm{yn}_{d+1}\to\dotsb,

where
\mathcal{FS}\mathrm{yn}_{d}\subset\mathcal{FS}\mathrm{yn}
is the moduli stack of finite syntomic
k
-schemes of degree
d
.
Our main result is the following:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
k
be a field. Then there is an equivalence

{}^{\infty}_{\mathbf{T}}\mathrm{MGL}\simeq L_{\mathrm{mot}}(\mathbf{Z}\times\mathcal{FS}\mathrm{yn}_{\infty})^{+}

in
\mathbf{H}(k)
, where
+
denotes Quillen’s plus construction (in the
\infty
-topos of Nisnevich sheaves).
Moreover, there is an equivalence

({}^{\infty}_{\mathbf{T}}\mathrm{MGL})(k)\simeq\mathbf{Z}\times(L_{\mathbf{A}^{1}}\mathcal{FS}\mathrm{yn}_{\infty})(k)^{+}.

If
k
has positive characteristic, the same equivalences hold without the plus construction.

Here,
L_{\mathbf{A}^{1}}
is the “naive”
\mathbf{A}^{1}
-localization functor given by the formula

(L_{\mathbf{A}^{1}}\mathcal{F})(X)=\operatorname*{colim}_{n\in\Delta^{\mathrm{op}}}\mathcal{F}(X\times\mathbf{A}^{n}).

We recall that
\mathcal{FS}\mathrm{yn}_{d}
is motivically equivalent to the Hilbert scheme

\mathrm{Hilb}_{d}^{\mathrm{lci}}(\mathbf{A}^{\infty})=\operatorname*{colim}_{n\to\infty}\mathrm{Hilb}_{d}^{\mathrm{lci}}(\mathbf{A}^{n})

of finite local complete intersections of degree
d
in
\mathbf{A}^{\infty}
, which is a smooth ind-scheme
[
EHK+20b
, Lemma 3.5.1]
.
Taking the colimit along the obvious closed immersions

\mathrm{Hilb}_{d}^{\mathrm{lci}}(\mathbf{A}^{\infty})\to\mathrm{Hilb}_{d+1}^{\mathrm{lci}}(\mathbf{A}^{1}\times\mathbf{A}^{\infty})\simeq\mathrm{Hilb}_{d+1}^{\mathrm{lci}}(\mathbf{A}^{\infty}),

we obtain a smooth ind-scheme
\mathrm{Hilb}_{\infty}^{\mathrm{lci}}(\mathbf{A}^{\infty})
such that the canonical map (forgetting the embedding into
\mathbf{A}^{\infty}
)

\mathrm{Hilb}_{\infty}^{\mathrm{lci}}(\mathbf{A}^{\infty})\to\mathcal{FS}\mathrm{yn}_{\infty}

is a motivic equivalence, and in fact an
\mathbf{A}^{1}
-equivalence on affine schemes. Thus, we can rewrite Theorem
1.1
as follows:

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
k
be a field. Then there is an equivalence

{}^{\infty}_{\mathbf{T}}\mathrm{MGL}\simeq L_{\mathrm{mot}}(\mathbf{Z}\times\mathrm{Hilb}_{\infty}^{\mathrm{lci}}(\mathbf{A}^{\infty}))^{+}

in
\mathbf{H}(k)
. Moreover, there is an equivalence

({}^{\infty}_{\mathbf{T}}\mathrm{MGL})(k)\simeq\mathbf{Z}\times(L_{\mathbf{A}^{1}}\mathrm{Hilb}_{\infty}^{\mathrm{lci}}(\mathbf{A}^{\infty}))(k)^{+}.

If
k
has positive characteristic, the same equivalences hold without the plus construction.

Remark 1.3
.

Unfortunately, we do not know if the plus construction can be removed in characteristic zero. As we will see, this is the case if and only if the cyclic permutation of three points in
\mathcal{FS}\mathrm{yn}_{3}(k)
becomes homotopic to the identity in
(L_{\mathbf{A}^{1}}\mathcal{FS}\mathrm{yn}_{\infty})(k)
.

We have similar results for the motivic sphere spectrum
\mathbf{1}
and for the special linear algebraic cobordism spectrum
\mathrm{MSL}
. Recall that a
framing
of a finite syntomic morphism
f\colon Y\to X
is a trivialization of the cotangent complex
\mathcal{L}_{f}
in
K(Y)
, and an
orientation
of
f
is a trivialization of the dualizing sheaf
\omega_{f}=\det(\mathcal{L}_{f})
in
\mathrm{Pic}(Y)
. We denote by
\mathcal{FS}\mathrm{yn}^{\operatorname{fr}}
and
\mathcal{FS}\mathrm{yn}^{\mathrm{or}}
the moduli stacks of framed and oriented finite syntomic
k
-schemes, respectively. There are forgetful morphisms

\mathcal{FS}\mathrm{yn}^{\operatorname{fr}}\to\mathcal{FS}\mathrm{yn}^{\mathrm{or}}\to\mathcal{FS}\mathrm{yn}.

By
[
EHK+19
, Theorem 3.5.18]
and
[
EHK+20b
, Corollary 3.4.4]
, there are equivalences

{}^{\infty}_{\mathbf{T}}\mathbf{1}\simeq L_{\mathrm{mot}}(\mathcal{FS}\mathrm{yn}^{{\operatorname{fr}},\mathrm{gp}})\quad\text{and}\quad{}^{\infty}_{\mathbf{T}}\mathrm{MSL}\simeq L_{\mathrm{mot}}(\mathcal{FS}\mathrm{yn}^{\mathrm{or},\mathrm{gp}}).

Let
h\in\mathcal{FS}\mathrm{yn}^{\operatorname{fr}}(k)
be the framed finite syntomic
k
-scheme consisting of two
k
-points, one with trivial framing and the other with the opposite framing. We define

\displaystyle\mathcal{FS}\mathrm{yn}^{\operatorname{fr}}_{\infty}=\operatorname*{colim}(\dotsb\to\mathcal{FS}\mathrm{yn}_{2d}^{\operatorname{fr}}\xrightarrow{+h}\mathcal{FS}\mathrm{yn}_{2d+2}^{\operatorname{fr}}\to\dotsb),

\displaystyle\mathcal{FS}\mathrm{yn}^{\mathrm{or}}_{\infty}=\operatorname*{colim}(\dotsb\to\mathcal{FS}\mathrm{yn}_{2d}^{\mathrm{or}}\xrightarrow{+h}\mathcal{FS}\mathrm{yn}_{2d+2}^{\mathrm{or}}\to\dotsb).

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
k
be a field. Then there are equivalences

\displaystyle{}^{\infty}_{\mathbf{T}}\mathbf{1}\simeq L_{\mathrm{mot}}(\mathbf{Z}\times\mathcal{FS}\mathrm{yn}_{\infty}^{\operatorname{fr}})^{+},

\displaystyle{}^{\infty}_{\mathbf{T}}\mathrm{MSL}\simeq L_{\mathrm{mot}}(\mathbf{Z}\times\mathcal{FS}\mathrm{yn}_{\infty}^{\mathrm{or}})^{+}

in
\mathbf{H}(k)
. Moreover, there are equivalences

\displaystyle({}^{\infty}_{\mathbf{T}}\mathbf{1})(k)\simeq\mathbf{Z}\times(L_{\mathbf{A}^{1}}\mathcal{FS}\mathrm{yn}_{\infty}^{\operatorname{fr}})(k)^{+},

\displaystyle({}^{\infty}_{\mathbf{T}}\mathrm{MSL})(k)\simeq\mathbf{Z}\times(L_{\mathbf{A}^{1}}\mathcal{FS}\mathrm{yn}_{\infty}^{\mathrm{or}})(k)^{+}.

If
k
has positive characteristic, the same equivalences hold without the plus construction.

Finally, we also have a Hilbert scheme version of Theorem
1.4
: the moduli stacks
\mathcal{FS}\mathrm{yn}^{\operatorname{fr}}_{d}
and
\mathcal{FS}\mathrm{yn}^{\mathrm{or}}_{d}
are motivically equivalent to the smooth ind-schemes
\mathrm{Hilb}_{d}^{\operatorname{fr}}(\mathbf{A}^{\infty})
and
\mathrm{Hilb}_{d}^{\mathrm{or}}(\mathbf{A}^{\infty})
(see
[
EHK+19
, §5.1.6]
and
[
EHK+20b
, §3.5]
). We define
\mathrm{Hilb}_{\infty}^{\operatorname{fr}}(\mathbf{A}^{\infty})
and
\mathrm{Hilb}_{\infty}^{\mathrm{or}}(\mathbf{A}^{\infty})
in an obvious way so as to match the definitions of
\mathcal{FS}\mathrm{yn}^{\operatorname{fr}}_{\infty}
and
\mathcal{FS}\mathrm{yn}^{\mathrm{or}}_{\infty}
.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
k
be a field. Then there are equivalences

\displaystyle{}^{\infty}_{\mathbf{T}}\mathbf{1}\simeq L_{\mathrm{mot}}(\mathbf{Z}\times\mathrm{Hilb}_{\infty}^{\operatorname{fr}}(\mathbf{A}^{\infty}))^{+},

\displaystyle{}^{\infty}_{\mathbf{T}}\mathrm{MSL}\simeq L_{\mathrm{mot}}(\mathbf{Z}\times\mathrm{Hilb}_{\infty}^{\mathrm{or}}(\mathbf{A}^{\infty}))^{+}

in
\mathbf{H}(k)
. Moreover, there are equivalences

\displaystyle({}^{\infty}_{\mathbf{T}}\mathbf{1})(k)\simeq\mathbf{Z}\times(L_{\mathbf{A}^{1}}\mathrm{Hilb}_{\infty}^{\operatorname{fr}}(\mathbf{A}^{\infty}))(k)^{+},

\displaystyle({}^{\infty}_{\mathbf{T}}\mathrm{MSL})(k)\simeq\mathbf{Z}\times(L_{\mathbf{A}^{1}}\mathrm{Hilb}_{\infty}^{\mathrm{or}}(\mathbf{A}^{\infty}))(k)^{+}.

If
k
has positive characteristic, the same equivalences hold without the plus construction.

Our interest in these group-completion-free models is motivated in part by the following conjecture of Mike Hopkins, called the “Wilson space hypothesis”:

Conjecture 1.6

(M. Hopkins)
.

For every
n\in\mathbf{Z}
, the motive
M({}^{\infty-n}_{\mathbf{T}}\mathrm{MGL})\in\mathbf{DM}(k)
is pure Tate.

The analog of this conjecture for the complex cobordism spectrum
\mathrm{MU}
is a theorem of Wilson
[
Wil73
]
, and the analog in
C_{2}
-equivariant homotopy theory was recently proved by Hill and Hopkins
[
HH18
]
; these results are subsumed by Conjecture
1.6
for
k=\mathbf{C}
and
k=\mathbf{R}
, respectively. Since the plus construction is invisible to motives, Theorem
1.1
implies that

M({}^{\infty}_{\mathbf{T}}\mathrm{MGL})\simeq\bigoplusop\displaylimits_{d\in\mathbf{Z}}M(\mathrm{Hilb}^{\mathrm{lci}}_{\infty}(\mathbf{A}^{\infty})).

We thus obtain the following geometric reformulation of the case
n=0
of the above conjecture:

Corollary 1.7
.

Conjecture
1.6
holds for
n=0
if and only if
M(\mathrm{Hilb}^{\mathrm{lci}}_{\infty}(\mathbf{A}^{\infty}))
is a pure Tate motive.

Remark 1.8
.

The motive of
\mathrm{Hilb}^{\mathrm{lci}}(\mathbf{A}^{n})
is
not
pure Tate for
2\leqslant n<\infty
.
Indeed, one can show that
M(\mathrm{Hilb}_{3}^{\mathrm{lci}}(\mathbf{A}^{n}))
cannot be pure Tate by analyzing the restriction to the lci locus of the Białynicki-Birula stratification of the Hilbert scheme
\mathrm{Hilb}_{3}(\mathbf{A}^{n})
, which is smooth.
For example,

M(\mathrm{Hilb}_{3}^{\mathrm{lci}}(\mathbf{A}^{2}))\simeq\mathbf{Z}\oplus\mathbf{Z}(1)[2]\oplus\mathbf{Z}(2)[4]\oplus\mathbf{Z}(4)[7].

Nevertheless, the motive of
\mathrm{Hilb}_{3}^{\mathrm{lci}}(\mathbf{A}^{\infty})
turns out to be pure Tate, as shown in
[
HJN+20
, Theorem 7.1]
.

2. A moving lemma

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
k
be a field,
X
a smooth geometrically connected quasi-projective
k
-scheme of dimension
\geqslant 2
, and
A\subset X
a subscheme étale over
k
. Then there exists a smooth geometrically connected closed subscheme
H\subset X
of codimension
1
containing
A
.

Proof.

If
k
is infinite, a generic hypersurface section of
X
of large enough degree containing
A
is geometrically irreducible and smooth of codimension
1
, by
[
AK79
, Theorems 1 and 7]
.

If
k
is finite, choose an embedding
X\subset\mathbf{P}^{n}_{k}
and a first-order thickening
\tilde{A}
of
A
in
\mathbf{P}^{n}_{k}
that is transverse to
X
. Let
P_{d}=\Gamma(\mathbf{P}^{n}_{k},\mathcal{O}(d))
and let

\displaystyle Q_{d}

\displaystyle=\{f\in P_{d}\;|\;\text{$Z(f)\cap(X-A)$ is smooth of codimension $1$ in $X$ and $f|\tilde{A}=0$}\},

\displaystyle R_{d}

\displaystyle=\{f\in P_{d}\;|\;\text{$Z(f)\cap X$ is geometrically irreducible}\}.

Note that
Z(f)\cap X
is smooth for any
f\in Q_{d}
, so it suffices to show that
Q_{d}\cap R_{d}
is nonempty for some
d
.
We have

\operatorname*{lim}_{d\to\infty}\frac{\#Q_{d}}{\#P_{d}}>0\quad\text{and}\quad\operatorname*{lim}_{d\to\infty}\frac{\#R_{d}}{\#P_{d}}=1

by
[
Poo04
, Theorem 1.2]
and
[
CP16
, Theorem 1.1]
, respectively. Hence,
Q_{d}\cap R_{d}
is nonempty for all large enough
d
.
∎

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
k
be a field,
X\subset\mathbf{P}^{n}_{k}
a quasi-projective
k
-scheme, and
A,B\subset X
disjoint finite subschemes.
Suppose
A
étale over
k
and
X-B
smooth of dimension
m\geqslant 1
over
k
. Then, for every large enough
d
, there exist global sections
f,g\in\Gamma(\mathbf{P}^{n}_{k},\mathcal{O}(d))
such that:

(1)

f|B=g|B
;

(2)

f|A=0
;

(3)

g
does not vanish on
A\cup B
;

(4)

Z(f)\cap X
and
Z(g)\cap X
are smooth over
k
of dimension
m-1
.

Proof.

Suppose first that
k
is finite. Let
h\in\Gamma(A\cup B,\mathcal{O}(1))
be a nonvanishing section. Applying
[
Poo04
, Theorem 1.2]
with an auxiliary thickening of
A
as in the proof of Lemma
2.1
, we deduce that for every large enough
d
there exists a global section
f
of
\mathcal{O}(d)
satisfying conditions (2) and (4) such that
f|B=h^{\otimes d}|B
. By the same theorem, there also exists a global section
g
of
\mathcal{O}(d)
satisfying condition (4) such that
g|(A\cup B)=h^{\otimes d}
. Then
f
and
g
satisfy all conditions.

If
k
is infinite, take
d
such that
\mathcal{I}_{A}(d-1)
is generated by its global sections and such that the vanishing of
H^{1}(\mathbf{P}^{n}_{k},\mathcal{I}_{A\cup B}(d))
holds, where
\mathcal{I}_{Z}\subset\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{P}^{n}_{k}}
denotes the ideal defining a closed subscheme
Z
.
Let
f
and
h
be generic sections of
\mathcal{O}(d)
with
f|A=0
and
h|B=0
, and let
g=f+h
.
By choice of
d
, every global section of
\mathcal{O}(d)
is the sum of one vanishing on
A
and one vanishing on
B
. Hence,
g
is a generic section of
\mathcal{O}(d)
, and in particular does not vanish on
A\cup B
.
Moreover,
Z(f)\cap(X-B)
and
Z(g)\cap(X-B)
are smooth of dimension
m-1
by
[
AK79
, Theorem 7]
. The sections
f
and
g
thus satisfy conditions (1)–(4).
∎

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
k
be a field,
X
a smooth
k
-scheme, and
U\subset X
a dense open subscheme. For any
k
-point
x\colon\operatorname{Spec}k\to X
, there exist framed correspondences
\alpha,\beta\in\mathrm{Corr}^{\operatorname{fr}}_{k}(\operatorname{Spec}k,U)
with finite étale support and an
\mathbf{A}^{1}
-homotopy
x+\alpha\sim\beta
in
\mathrm{Corr}^{\operatorname{fr}}_{k}(\mathbf{A}^{1}_{k},X)
.

Proof.

Since the question is local around
x
, we can assume
X
quasi-projective and geometrically connected
[
Gro65
, corollaire 4.5.14]
.
By Lemma
2.1
, we can find a smooth connected subscheme
C\subset X
of dimension
1
such that
x\in C
and such that
C\cap U
is nonempty (hence cofinite in
C
).
Let
\overline{C}
be a projective closure of
C
and
\mathcal{L}
an ample invertible sheaf on
\overline{C}
. Shrinking
C
if necessary, we may assume that
C
is affine and that
C/k
and
\mathcal{L}|C
are trivial.
Let
A=\{x\}
, and let
B
be the complement of
C\cap U
in
\overline{C}
with
x
removed. Then
A
and
B
are disjoint finite subschemes of
\overline{C}
such that
A
is étale and
\overline{C}-B
is smooth.
By Lemma
2.2
, we can find an integer
d
and global sections
f
and
g
of
\mathcal{L}^{\otimes d}
with étale vanishing loci, such that
f(x)=0
,
f=g
on
B
, and
Z(f)-\{x\},Z(g)\subset C\cap U
. Let
h
be the section
(1-t)f+tg
over
\mathbf{A}^{1}\times\overline{C}
and let
H=Z(h)
. Then
H
is a proper local complete intersection of relative virtual dimension
0
over
\mathbf{A}^{1}_{k}
. Moreover, since
f
and
g
agree and do not vanish on
B
,
h
does not vanish on
\mathbf{A}^{1}\times B
, so
H\subset\mathbf{A}^{1}\times C
and
H
is finite over
\mathbf{A}^{1}_{k}
(hence syntomic by
[
EHK+19
, Proposition 2.1.16]
). Thus,
H
defines a finite syntomic correspondence from
\mathbf{A}^{1}_{k}
to
X
. Since
C/k
and
\mathcal{L}^{\otimes d}|C
are trivial, there exists a framing
\tau\colon\mathcal{L}_{H/\mathbf{A}^{1}_{k}}\simeq 0
in
K(H)
. Multiplying
\tau
by a unit of
k
, we can assume that
\tau
restricts to the trivial loop in
K(\{x\})
. Then
(H,\tau)
is a framed correspondence from
\mathbf{A}^{1}_{k}
to
X
with the desired properties.
∎

3. Quillen’s plus construction and group completion

We shall say that a morphism
f\colon X\to Y
in an
\infty
-topos
T
is
acyclic
if it is an epimorphism in the categorical sense,
i.e.
, if the square

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{id}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{id}}

is cocartesian.
Recall that a
modality
on
T
is a factorization system on
T
(in the sense of Joyal and as explained in
[
Lur17b
, §5.2.8]
) whose left class of morphisms is stable under base change
[
ABFJ20
, §2]
.

Lemma 3.1
.

In any
\infty
-topos
T
, acyclic morphisms form the left class of a modality.
In particular, acyclic morphisms are stable under composition, retracts, colimits, cobase change, base change, and finite products.

Proof.

Acyclic morphisms are stable under base change since cocartesian squares are (by universality of colimits).
By
[
Lur17b
, Proposition 5.5.5.7]
, it remains to show that the class of acyclic maps is of small generation as a saturated class.
For any
X\in T
, the full subcategory of
T_{/X}
spanned by the acyclic morphisms is accessible by
[
Lur17b
, Proposition 5.4.6.6]
, being the fiber of the suspension functor. It is thus generated under filtered colimits by a small subcategory. Let
C\subset\mathrm{Fun}(\Delta^{1},T)
be the union of these small subcategories of
T_{/X}
as
X
ranges over a small set of generators of
T
. Using that acyclic maps are stable under base change, we immediately deduce that
C
generates the class of acyclic maps under colimits.
∎

For
X\in T
, we denote by
X\to X^{+}
the final object in the
\infty
-category of acyclic maps out of
X
, which exists by Lemma
3.1
. The functor
X\mapsto X^{+}
is called the
plus construction
.
Note that acyclic morphisms are connected and become equivalences after a single suspension; it follows that the canonical map
X\to X^{+}
induces an isomorphism on
\pi_{0}
, and that it is an equivalence whenever
X
admits a structure of
\mathcal{E}_{1}
-group (
i.e.
, when
X
is a loop space
[
Lur17a
, Theorem 5.2.6.15]
).

In the
\infty
-topos
\mathrm{Spc}
of spaces, the above construction coincides with Quillen’s plus construction which was used to define algebraic
K
-theory
[
Qui72
, §12]
. More precisely,
X\to X^{+}
is the initial map that kills the maximal perfect subgroups of the fundamental groups of
X
, and hence it is an equivalence if and only if the fundamental groups of
X
are hypoabelian (
i.e.
, have no nontrivial perfect subgroups). We refer to
[
Rap19
]
for a discussion of acyclic morphisms in
\mathrm{Spc}
and for a proof of this fact.

Let
M
be a commutative monoid in
T
and let
m\colon*\to M
be a global section. We denote by
\mathbf{Mod}_{M}(T)
the
\infty
-category of
M
-modules,
i.e.
, objects of
T
with an action of
M
(which is again an
\infty
-topos). The full subcategory of
M
-modules on which

the associated localization functor. It is easy to check that this functor preserves finite products. In particular,

is again a commutative monoid and

is the initial morphism of commutative monoids sending

to an invertible global section. For

, we define the telescope
\mathrm{tel}{}_{m}(E)\in\mathbf{Mod}_{M}(T)
as the colimit of the sequence

\dotsb\to E\xrightarrow{\cdot m}E\to\dotsb.

There is then a canonical map of

-modules

, but unlike in the case of

-topoi, it is not invertible in general. For example, if
T=\mathrm{Spc}
and

is the free commutative monoid on a single element

, then

is the group completion of

does not admit a monoid structure since its fundamental groups are not abelian.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
T
be an
\infty
-topos, let
M
be a commutative monoid in
T
, and let
m\colon*\to M
be a global section.
For any
M
-module
E
, the canonical map
\mathrm{tel}{}_{m}(E)\to E[m^{-1}]
is acyclic.

Proof.

Since acyclic maps are closed under colimits and finite products, it suffices to prove the result for
E=M
.
The classifying
\infty
-topos for pointed commutative monoids is a presheaf
\infty
-topos (namely, presheaves on the pushout of
(\mathrm{Spc}_{*}^{\mathrm{fin}})^{\mathrm{op}}\leftarrow(\mathrm{Spc}^{\mathrm{fin}})^{\mathrm{op}}\to(\mathrm{CMon}{}(\mathrm{Spc})^{\mathrm{fin}})^{\mathrm{op}}
in the
\infty
-category of
\infty
-categories with finite limits), so we are immediately reduced to the case
T=\mathrm{Spc}
. Let
F
be the free commutative monoid on an element
a
. The element
m
induces a morphism of commutative monoids

sending
a
to
m
, and we have
\mathrm{tel}{}_{m}(M)=M\otimes_{F}\mathrm{tel}{}_{a}(F)
and

, where
\otimes_{F}
is the tensor product of spaces with
F
-action. Since acyclic maps are closed under colimits and finite products,

preserves acyclic maps. We can therefore replace
(M,m)
by
(F,a)
, and in particular we can assume that
\pi_{0}(M)[m^{-1}]
is a group. In this case we must show that the canonical map
\mathrm{tel}{}_{m}(M)^{+}\to M^{\mathrm{gp}}
is an equivalence, which is the classical McDuff–Segal group completion theorem
[
MS76
]
. We recall a proof due to Nikolaus
[
Nik17
]
.
Note that the plus construction preserves finite products and hence commutative monoids.
Consider the commutative square

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{tel}{}_{m}(M)^{+}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M^{\mathrm{gp}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{tel}{}_{m}(M^{+})^{+}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(M^{+})^{\mathrm{gp}}\hbox to0.0pt{.\hss}}

The left vertical is an equivalence since the plus construction is a left localization of
\mathrm{Spc}
. The lower horizontal map is an equivalence by Proposition

\Sigma_{5}

{\mathcal{E}_{\infty}}
-ring spectra. Hence the right vertical map is a stable equivalence. Since

5. Removing the plus construction in positive characteristic

The following proposition is an elaboration of
[
Rob15
, Proposition 2.19]
.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
C
be an
\mathcal{E}_{k}
-monoidal
\infty
-category with
2\leqslant k\leqslant\infty
and let
x\in C
.
Let
C[x^{-1}]
be the
\mathcal{E}_{k}
-monoidal
\infty
-category obtained from
C
by inverting
x
, and let
\mathrm{tel}{}_{x}(C)
be the
C
-module colimit of the sequence

\dotsb\to C\xrightarrow{\otimes x}C\to\dotsb.

Consider the following assertions:

(1)

The cyclic permutation of
x^{\otimes 3}
becomes homotopic to the identity in
\mathrm{tel}{}_{x}(C)
.

(2)

For some
n\geqslant 2
, the cyclic permutation of
x^{\otimes n}
becomes homotopic to the identity in
\mathrm{tel}{}_{x}(C)
.

(3)

The canonical map
\mathrm{tel}{}_{x}(C)\to C[x^{-1}]
is an equivalence.

Then
(1)\Rightarrow(2)\Rightarrow(3)
. If
k\geqslant 3
, then
(3)\Rightarrow(1)
.

Proof.

The implication
(1)\Rightarrow(2)
is trivial.
Note that assertion
(3)
is equivalent to the assertion that
x
acts invertibly on the telescope
\mathrm{tel}{}_{x}(C)
.
Consider the commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces C}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces C}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\dotsb}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces C}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces C}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\dotsb}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces x^{\otimes(n-1)}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces x}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces x^{\otimes(n-1)}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces x}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces x^{\otimes(n-1)}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces x^{\otimes(n-1)}}

where each square commutes via the cyclic permutation of
x^{\otimes n}
. Let
\mathrm{Seq}
be the
1
-skeleton of the nerve of the poset
\mathbf{N}
and let
\sigma\colon\mathrm{Seq}{}^{\triangleright}\to\infty\textnormal{-}\mathrm{Cat}
be the cone given by the first row mapping to the colimit of the second row. Then the action of
x
on
\mathrm{tel}{}_{x}(C)
is the induced map
\operatorname*{colim}(\sigma|\mathrm{Seq})\to\sigma(\infty)
.
Under assumption (2), we obtain an equivalent cone if we replace each cyclic permutation in the above diagram by the identity, which is trivially a colimiting cone. This proves
(2)\Rightarrow(3)
.

If
C
is
\mathcal{E}_{3}
-monoidal and
x\in C
is invertible, then the cyclic permutation of
x^{\otimes 3}
is homotopic to the identity. Indeed,
\pi_{0}\Aut(x^{\otimes 3})
is an abelian group and the cyclic permutation of order
3
becomes trivial in the abelianization of
\Sigma_{3}
. This proves
(3)\Rightarrow(1)
.
∎

Example 5.2
.

Let

be a derived commutative ring. The cyclic permutation of

is induced by a matrix in

and hence is
\mathbf{A}^{1}
-homotopic to the identity. Applying Proposition
5.1
to the
{\mathcal{E}_{\infty}}

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
p
be a prime number. Over
{\mathbf{F}}_{p}
, there exists a sequence of

-equivariant framed cobordisms between
p
with nontrivial
C_{p}
-action and
\langle-1\rangle p_{\epsilon}

Proof.

Let
f(t,x)=x(x-t)(x-2t)\dotsb(x-(p-1)t)

Consider the finite syntomic
{\mathbf{F}}_{p}[t]
-scheme

X=\operatorname{Spec}{\mathbf{F}}_{p}[t,x]/(f(t,x)),

with the framing

-f(t,x)

The group

Lemma 5.5
.

The presheaf

-contractible on affine

By the Artin–Schreier sequence

The following proposition completes the proofs of all the theorems in the introduction.

Proposition 5.6
.

Let
k
be a field of positive characteristic. Then the canonical maps

\displaystyle L_{\mathrm{mot}}(\mathcal{FS}\mathrm{yn}_{\infty})\to L_{\mathrm{mot}}(\mathcal{FS}\mathrm{yn}_{\infty})^{+}

\displaystyle(L_{\mathbf{A}^{1}}\mathcal{FS}\mathrm{yn}_{\infty})(k)\to(L_{\mathbf{A}^{1}}\mathcal{FS}\mathrm{yn}_{\infty})(k)^{+}

Remark 5.7
.

Let
C
be a smooth curve over a field
k
of characteristic zero. If
X
is a finite flat
C

-action. The group homomorphism

Global evolution in spherical symmetry for self-gravitating massive fields
Philippe G. LeFloch, Filipe C. Mena, The-Cang Nguyen

Abstract
We are interested in the global dynamics of a massive scalar field evolving under its own gravitational field and, in this paper, we study spherically symmetric solutions to Einstein’s field equations coupled with a Klein-Gordon equation with quadratic potential. For the initial value problem we establish a global existence theory when initial data are prescribed on a future light cone with vertex at the center of symmetry. A suitably generalized solution in Bondi coordinates is sought which has low regularity and possibly large but finite Bondi mass. A similar result was established first by Christodoulou for massless fields. In order to deal with massive fields, we must overcome several challenges and significantly modify Christodoulou’s original method. First of all, we formulate the Einstein-Klein-Gordon system in spherical symmetry as a non-local and nonlinear hyperbolic equation and, by carefully investigating the global dynamical behavior of the massive field, we establish various estimates concerning the Einstein operator, the Hawking mass, and the Bondi mass, including positivity and monotonicity properties. Importantly, in addition to a regularization at the center of symmetry we find it necessary to also introduce a regularization at null infinity. We also establish new energy and decay estimates for, both, regularized and generalized solutions.

Introduction

1.1
Global evolution of self-gravitating massive matter

Einstein-Klein-Gordon system.

We initiate the mathematical study of the global evolution problem for self-gravitating massive scalar fields in spherical symmetry and, specifically, we generalize here an existence result established by Christodoulou for massless fields within a pioneering work beginning with the papers
[
8

consisting of a four-dimensional manifold

\mathbf{g}
. In addition, the matter content is assumed here to be described by a massive scalar field

\Box_{\mathbf{g}}\phi-U^{\prime}(\phi)=0.

(1.1a)

Here,

denotes the wave operator associated with the unknown metric

and, for definiteness, we assume a quadratic potential (with

(1.1b)

Quadratic potentials are “critical” from the standpoint of global dynamics and decay, and commonly used in physical and numerical studies

Namely, we seek solutions

(1.2)

The stress-energy tensor

T[\phi,\mathbf{g}]=d\phi\otimes d\phi-{1\over 2}\bigl(\mathbf{g}(\nabla\phi,\nabla\phi)+c^{2}\phi^{2}\bigr)\,\mathbf{g}.

The Klein-Gordon equation coupled to the Einstein equations can be cast in the form of a nonlinear and coupled system of wave and Klein-Gordon equations. In comparison to earlier mathematical work, one of the major challenges that we need to overcome is that (

) is

Global evolution in spherical symmetry.

On the other hand, in order to investigate the global evolution problem for “arbitrary large” initial data, we find it necessary to focus on the class of spherically symmetric solutions and to introduce a notion of generalized solutions with low regularity.

The spacetimes
(M,\mathbf{g})
under consideration here are assume to enjoy spherical symmetry, in the sense that the rotation group
SO(3)
acts as an isometry group on the spacetime and that the set of fixed-points of this group is a timelike world-line while the orbits of all other spacetime points are spacelike topological two-spheres
\mathbb{S}^{2}
. In order to uncover the global geometry of such spacetimes, we formulate the initial value problem associated with the Einstein-Klein-Gordon system when data are imposed on a future light cone with vertex at the origin. We follow the strategy proposed in
[
8
,
9
]
and we thus introduce
generalized Bondi coordinates,
which are based on a null coordinate and a retarded time.
An initial data set is imposed on a complete future null geodesic cone extending to infinity, and is assumed to be asymptotically flat and have finite Bondi mass.
On the one hand, for
massless fields
enjoying suitable smallness conditions, Christodoulou
[
8
]
proved the global existence of
classical solutions
(enjoying sufficient regularity so that all of the terms in the Einstein equations make sense as continuous functions, at least) and established that such solutions disperse in the infinite future.
On the other hand, Christodoulou
[
9
]
considered “large” initial data and established the global existence of
generalized solutions,
defined in Bondi coordinates in a weak sense and covering a complete domain of outer communications.

Global evolution in a class of generalized solutions

Notion of generalized solutions.

The global evolution problem under consideration may leads to gravitational collapse and this motivates one to search for solutions with singularities.
We thus consider the initial value problem associated with the reduced equation (
1.8
) when an initial data

is prescribed on the null cone labelled

For each

It is also convenient to introduce the domain limited by this characteristic

Recall that from the single function

as well as

, and

.

A

(1.16a)

while the metric coefficients satisfy

(1.16b)

(which represents the variations of the matter along null directions)

(1.16d)

Main statement.

Our main result can be interpreted as a global nonlinear stability property of the exterior domain of communications of Schwarzschild spacetime, under under arbitrarily large perturbations by a massive matter field.

Theorem 1.2

(Global evolution of self-gravitating Klein-Gordon fields in spherical symmetry)
.

For every initial data
h_{0}\in C^{1}([0,+\infty))
with finite Bondi mass posed on an outgoing light cone with vertex at the center of symmetry,
there exists a global generalized solution
h
to the Cauchy problem (
1.8
) and (
1.12
)
describing the spherically symmetric evolution of a self-gravitating Klein-Gordon field with quadratic potential.

We emphasize that it is beyond the scope of the present paper to investigate qualitative properties of the generalized solutions beyond the ones stated in Definition
1.1
, and this issue is postponed for future work.

Related results.

Soon after Christodoulou’s work, Goldwirth and Piran
[
13
]
also considered massless scalar fields
for a class of Gaussian initial data, and numerically investigated the formation of black holes.
Choptuik
[
7
]
numerically discovered a (self-similar) critical phenomena in the collapse of massless scalar fields, while
Christodoulou
[
10
]
proved rigorously that
naked singularities
(with singularities “visible” from null infinity)
can form as result of the collapse of a scalar field, but however are not generic.

These theoretical and numerical advances on massless fields next inspired more numerical works, including a numerical investigations of
massive fields. Brady et al.
[
4
,
3
]
studied massive fields governed by a quadratic potential for a class of initial data sets with exponential decay. A class of quartic potentials was also studied numerically by Honda
[
14
]
, who studied the stability of the collapse of oscillating solutions.
Along similar lines, Zhang and Lu
[
23
]
considered the spherically-symmetric collapse of nonlinear massless scalar field with self-interactions
and first treated hyperbolic potentials, namely, exponentially decaying functions with powers of odd exponents. More recently, Michel and Moss
[
20
]
numerically studied trigonometric potentials, and emphasized the role of
the Bondi mass in defining the final black hole mass: its variation is a measure of the energy flux emanating from the collapsing star.

As far as the mathematical analysis is concerned, only few works took over Christodoulou’s framework and extended his results. Chae
[
5
]
proved the global existence of classical solutions with small data for the Einstein-massive field system with quartic or quintic potentials; he treated
initial data with polynomial decay and relied on a fixed-point method adapted to the potentials under consideration.
Extensions of this result to the Maxwell, Yang-Mills, and Higgs fields were given in
[
6
]
.
More recently, Costa et al.
[
11
,
12
]
considered the Einstein-massless field system with a positive cosmological
constant and established the global existence of classical solutions for a class of initial data sets.

2.3
Challenges overcome in this paper

Our main focus in this paper is on the definition and existence of generalized solutions to the Einstein equations in spherical symmetry.
For the large data problem under consideration, a singularity may arise at the center of symmetry and it is thus natural to introduce first
a regularization at the center of symmetry, based on replacing
r
by
r+\epsilon
in the equations and then letting the
parameter
\epsilon\in(0,1)
tend to zero eventually. A main part of the analysis will thus be devoted to establish the existence of regularized solutions and to prove that the enjoy the desired properties
uniformly
with respect to
\epsilon\to 0
.

Another main difficulty is proving that the generalized solutions satisfy not only the evolution equation,
but also the mass equation (
1.17b
), which is (equivalent to) one of the Einstein equations and, therefore, must be validated also at the level of generalized solutions. A related major difficulty comes from the fact that the mass does not control
the geometry in the interior of the spheres with radius
2m(r)
, so that
specific arguments must be developed in order to control the solution in the region near the center of symmetry.

As a matter of fact, several properties and arguments in Christodoulou’s proof
[
8
,
9
]
do not carry over from the massless to the massive matter problem.

•

Positivity and finiteness properties of the mass.
In the massless case
c=0
, the positivity of the function
\overline{g}
and the finiteness of the Bondi mass are immediate; moreover, they impose no further constraint on the sequence of
\epsilon
-regularized solutions and, in turn, the evolution equation for the Hawking mass is
not directly required
in order to deal with terms involving the metric coefficient
\overline{g}
. In contrast, for the massive fields having
c\neq 0
, the positivity property
\overline{g}>0

(as is also done for the finiteness of the Bondi mass) does not follow from the evolution equations but
must be propagated
from assumptions made on the initial hypersurface.

•

A consistent construction scheme.
For massless fields (
c=0
), the functions
Dh
and
D(\partial_{r}h)
have the same decay rate as the one of the functions
h
and
\partial_{r}h
, respectively. For these functions, the fall-off at null infinity is preserved during the evolution and this observation provides Christodoulou a basis for constructing a sequence of functions converging to the desired solution
h
and, in turn, these features made the construction relatively direct.
On the other hand, for massive fields (
c\neq 0
), the scaling invariance is no longer available and
the functions
Dh
and
D(\partial_{r}h)
might decay at slower rates than
h
and
\partial_{r}h
, respectively.
Hence, one of the challenges is to guarantee,
simultaneously
in the process of showing the existence of (regularized or exact) solutions,
(1) a consistent fall-off for the unknown function
h
,
(2) the positiveness of the coefficient
\overline{g}
, and (3) the finiteness of the Bondi mass.

•

Cut-off method to cope with null infinity.

For massless fields, Christodoulou proved that
h
and
Dh
are
C^{1}
-regular outside the central axis
r=0
. On the other hand,
for massive fields, the function
Dh
is not uniformly bounded in the
C^{1}
norm in the vicinity of null infinity.
This leads to an additional difficulty in the construction of a sequence of regularized solutions and the convergence analysis toward an actual solution. By taking advantage of the attractive nature of gravity and there a “favorable” sign on the characteristics of the reduced Einsteni equation, we introduce here a cut-off technique: we solve the equations first on a compact spacetime region and then rely on the finiteness of the Bondi mass in order to extend this solution. While Christodoulou deals with regularized solutions defined for all
r
and a compact interval in
u
, we construct regularized solutions defined on a
bounded
space interval
[\delta,b]
first, before letting
\delta\to 0
and
b\to+\infty
.

•

Fixed-point argument based on the Schauder theorem.

Our method of proof then requires the construction of a solution operator which is proven to be continuous and compact and, next, the application pf Schauder’s fixed-point theorem. A major advantage of the proposed strategy is that, throughout our analysis for, both, the approximate and the exact solutions, we may employ
properties derived from the evolution equations,
including the evolution equations for the Hawking mass. Throughout our analysis, the finiteness of the Bondi mass plays a crucial role.

•

Convergence toward generalized solutions.

Finally, for massless fields, Christodoulou established sup-norm estimates on the solution
h_{\epsilon}
to the regularized problem
before proving that it converges uniformly to a solution
h
of the original problem. On the other hand, for massive fields
such sup-norm estimates are not available and, in order to prove that
h_{\epsilon}\to h
, we develop here an alternative argument based on the weaker control provided by energy estimates.

3
Existence for the
(\epsilon,b)
-regularized problem

3.1
Main statement on the regularized problem

Functional setup.

Throughout this section, a small parameter
\epsilon\in(0,1)
is fixed and, for simplicity in the presentation, we suppress the subscript
\epsilon
from our notation. Our objective is to apply Schauder’s fixed-point theorem the regularized problem, namely, if
X
is a Banach space and
T:X\to X
is a continuous and compact operator whose image

is bounded, then
T
admits a fixed-point.
We focus on a spacetime slab limited by two light cones who proper distance is determined by the constant

c_{1}:=\min\Big(1,\frac{1}{20c^{2}}\Big),

(3.1)

In order to regularize our reduced equation at null infinity, we also fix a parameter

b\geq 1,

(3.2)

which will eventually be taken to tend to zero. We consider the Banach space
(X_{b},\,\|\cdot\|_{C^{1}})
of all continuously differentiable functions

and vanishing out side the interval
[0,b+2]

X_{b}:=\bigg\{h\in C^{1}\big([0,c_{1}]\times[0,+\infty)\big)\,\big/\,h(u,r)=0\hskip 10.00002pt\text{for all $r\geq b+2$}\bigg\}.

(3.3)

Regularization of the metric.

In order to guarantee that the expected expression of the metric coefficient
g=\big(1-4c^{2}\pi r^{2}\overline{h}^{2}\big)e^{\nu+\lambda}

Given a sufficiently large parameter

(3.4)

We also introduce a smooth function
\rho

\rho(x):=\begin{cases}x,&x\geq 0,\\
\in[-1,0]&x<0,\end{cases}

\displaystyle\partial_{u}\chi_{a,t,h}=-\frac{1}{2}\rho\big(\overline{g}_{a,t,h}\big),\hskip 20.00003pt\chi_{a,t,h}(u_{1};u_{1},r_{1})=r_{1},\hskip 20.00003ptu\in[0,u_{1}].

(3.6)

Regularization of the matter field.

For each
(t,h)\in[0,1]\times X_{b}

\displaystyle D_{a,t,h}k-\frac{t}{2(r+\epsilon)}\big(g_{a,t,h}-\overline{g}_{a,t,h}\big)k=\Omega_{a,t,h},\hskip 20.00003ptk\big|_{u=0}=h_{0},

\displaystyle D_{a,t,h}

\displaystyle:=\partial_{u}-\frac{1}{2}\rho(\overline{g}_{a,t,h})\partial_{r},

(3.8)

\displaystyle\Omega_{a,t,h}

\displaystyle:=-\frac{1}{2(r+\epsilon)}\big(g_{a,t,h}-\overline{g}_{a,t,h}\big)\,\overline{h}-\frac{c^{2}}{2}(r+\epsilon)\,\overline{h}\,e^{\nu_{h}+\lambda_{h}},

[0,b+2]
. So, we define the
solution mapping operator

by

where

\eta(r)\begin{cases}=1,\quad&r<b+1,\\
>0,&r\in(b+1,b+2),\\

(3.10)

Main statement for this section.

We are going to establish the following result.

(Existence theory for the
(\epsilon,b)
-regularized problem)

The solution mapping operator

). For every
s_{0}\in[0,+\infty)
, by the Sobolev inequality we get

\sup_{s\geq s_{0}}\xi^{*2}(s)\leq c_{*}\int_{s_{0}}^{+\infty}\Big(\xi^{*2}+\big(\partial_{s}\xi^{*}\big)^{2}\Big)\,ds,

(7.13)

where
c_{*}
is a constant, independent of
s_{0}
. Since
e^{\nu^{*}+\lambda^{*}}
is a non-increasing function of
s
, it follows that

\Big(\frac{\xi^{*2}}{e^{\nu^{*}+\lambda^{*}}}\Big)(s_{0})\leq\frac{c_{*}}{e^{(\nu^{*}+\lambda^{*})(s_{0})}}\int_{s_{0}}^{+\infty}\Big(\xi^{*2}+\big(\partial_{s}\xi^{*}\big)^{2}\Big)\,ds\leq c_{*}\int_{s_{0}}^{+\infty}\frac{1}{e^{\nu^{*}+\lambda^{*}}}\Big(\xi^{*2}+\big(\partial_{s}\xi^{*}\big)^{2}\Big)\,ds.

Therefore, we have

\sup_{s\geq 0}\Big(\frac{\xi^{*2}}{e^{\nu^{*}+\lambda^{*}}}\Big)(s)\leq c_{*}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\frac{1}{e^{\nu^{*}+\lambda^{*}}}\Big(\xi^{*2}+\big(\partial_{s}\xi^{*}\big)^{2}\Big)\,ds.

(7.14)

Taking into account (
7.10
) and the second inequality of (
7.11
), from (
7.14
)
we deduce that

\displaystyle\sup_{r\in[0,r_{0}]}\Big(\frac{\xi^{2}}{e^{\nu+\lambda}}\Big)(r)

\displaystyle\leq c_{*}\Big(\int_{0}^{r_{0}}\frac{\xi^{2}}{re^{\nu+\lambda}}\,dr+c^{4}r_{0}^{3}\int_{0}^{r_{0}}\overline{h}^{2}\,dr+\frac{1}{2\pi}\big(e^{(\nu+\lambda)(r_{0})}-e^{(\nu+\lambda)(0)}\big)\Big)

(7.15)

\displaystyle\leq c_{*}\Big(\int_{0}^{r_{0}}\frac{\xi^{2}}{re^{\nu+\lambda}}\,dr+c^{4}r_{0}^{3}F(u)+\frac{1}{2\pi}\big(e^{(\nu+\lambda)(r_{0})}-e^{(\nu+\lambda)(0)}\big)\Big),

where
F(u)
is a continuous function of
u
and we applied (
2.7
). Since
e^{\nu+\lambda}
is continuous on
[0,u_{0}]\times[0,+\infty)
, taking
r_{0}\to 0
in (
7.15
), we derive from (
7.8
) that, at almost all
u
,
\frac{\xi^{2}}{e^{\nu+\lambda}}\to 0
as
r\to 0
, as claimed.

Now, by integrating (
7.15
) with respect to
u
we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{u_{0}}\sup_{r\in[0,r_{0}]}\Big(\frac{\xi^{2}}{e^{\nu+\lambda}}\Big)(u,r)\,du

(7.16)

\displaystyle\leq c_{*}\Big(\iint_{\mathcal{O}(r_{0})}\frac{\xi^{2}}{re^{\nu+\lambda}}\,drdu+c^{4}r_{0}^{3}\int_{0}^{u_{0}}F(u)\,du+\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{u_{0}}\big(e^{(\nu+\lambda)(r_{0})}-e^{(\nu+\lambda)(0)}\big)\,du\Big).

It then follows from (
7.8
) that

\sup_{r\in[0,1]}\Big|\xi\,e^{-(\nu+\lambda)/2}\Big|\in L^{2}(0,u_{0}).

(7.17)

On the other hand, since

\xi(u,r)=\xi(u,1)+\int_{1}^{r}\Big(\overline{g}\frac{(h-\overline{h})}{s}-c^{2}s\overline{h}\,e^{\nu+\lambda}\Big)\,ds,

we have, for all
r>1
,

\displaystyle\Big(\frac{\xi^{2}}{r^{2}e^{\nu+\lambda}}\Big)(u,r)

\displaystyle\leq 2\Big(\frac{\xi^{2}}{e^{\nu+\lambda}}\Big)(u,1)+\frac{2}{r^{2}e^{\nu+\lambda}}\Big(\int_{1}^{r}\Big(\overline{g}\frac{(h-\overline{h})}{s}-c^{2}s\overline{h}\,e^{\nu+\lambda}\Big)\,ds\Big)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2\Big(\frac{\xi^{2}}{e^{\nu+\lambda}}\Big)(u,1)+\frac{2}{r}\int_{1}^{r}\Big((h-\overline{h})^{2}+c^{4}s^{2}\overline{h}^{2}\Big)\,ds\leq 2\Big(\frac{\xi^{2}}{e^{\nu+\lambda}}\Big)(u,1)+F_{1}(u),

where
F_{1}(u)
is a continuous function of
u
and we applied (
2.7
). Combining this result with (
7.17
), gives
the first statement in (
7.6
), as claimed.

Finally, since by dominated convergence,

\int_{0}^{u_{0}}\big(e^{(\nu+\lambda)(r_{0})}-e^{(\nu+\lambda)(0)}\big)\,du\to 0,\hskip 10.00002pt\text{as $r\to 0$},

taking
r_{0}\to 0
in (
7.16
), we complete the proof with

\int_{0}^{u_{0}}\Big(\frac{\xi^{2}}{e^{\nu+\lambda}}\Big)(u,r)\,du\to 0,\hskip 10.00002pt\text{as $r\to 0$}.

∎

7.2
Compactness arguments based on the mass equation

It is convenient to introduce the function

\gamma(u,r):=-\frac{\nu+\lambda}{4\pi}=\int_{r}^{+\infty}\frac{(h-\overline{h})^{2}}{s}\,ds.

Before we start studying in more detail the terms that appear in the evolution equation for the Hawking mass,
we recall that (cf. (
5.11
))

\partial_{u}\bigg(\int_{r}^{+\infty}\frac{(h_{\epsilon}-\overline{h}_{\epsilon})^{2}}{s+\epsilon}\,ds\bigg)=\int_{r}^{+\infty}\partial_{u}\bigg(\frac{(h_{\epsilon}-\overline{h}_{\epsilon})^{2}}{s+\epsilon}\bigg)\,ds,

so that, by straightforward calculations,

\displaystyle\partial_{u}\bigg(\int_{r}^{+\infty}\frac{(h_{\epsilon}-\overline{h}_{\epsilon})^{2}}{s+\epsilon}\,ds\bigg)=\int_{r}^{+\infty}\frac{2(h_{\epsilon}-\overline{h}_{\epsilon})}{s+\epsilon}\Big(Dh_{\epsilon}+\frac{1}{2}\overline{g}_{\epsilon}\partial_{r}h_{\epsilon}-D\overline{h}_{\epsilon}-\frac{1}{2}\overline{g}_{\epsilon}\partial_{r}\,\overline{h}_{\epsilon}\Big)\,ds

\displaystyle=\int_{r}^{+\infty}\frac{h_{\epsilon}-\overline{h}_{\epsilon}}{s+\epsilon}\Bigg(\frac{(g_{\epsilon}-\overline{g}_{\epsilon})(h_{\epsilon}-\overline{h}_{\epsilon})}{s+\epsilon}-c^{2}(s+\epsilon)\,\overline{h}_{\epsilon}e^{\nu_{\epsilon}+\lambda_{\epsilon}}+\overline{g}_{\epsilon}\partial_{r}h_{\epsilon}-\frac{\xi_{\epsilon}}{s+\epsilon}-\frac{\overline{g}_{\epsilon}}{s+\epsilon}(h_{\epsilon}-\overline{h}_{\epsilon})\Bigg)\,ds.

Combining this equation with

\frac{1}{r+\epsilon}\partial_{r}\overline{g}_{\epsilon}(h_{\epsilon}-\overline{h}_{\epsilon})^{2}+\frac{\overline{g}_{\epsilon}(h_{\epsilon}-\overline{h}_{\epsilon})}{r+\epsilon}\partial_{r}h_{\epsilon}-\frac{\overline{g}_{\epsilon}(h_{\epsilon}-\overline{h}_{\epsilon})^{2}}{(r+\epsilon)^{2}}=\frac{g_{\epsilon}(h_{\epsilon}-\overline{h}_{\epsilon})^{2}}{2(r+\epsilon)^{2}}+\partial_{r}\Big(\overline{g}_{\epsilon}\frac{(h_{\epsilon}-\overline{h}_{\epsilon})^{2}}{2(r+\epsilon)}\Big),

we obtain

\displaystyle\partial_{u}\bigg(\int_{r}^{+\infty}\frac{(h_{\epsilon}-\overline{h}_{\epsilon})^{2}}{s+\epsilon}\,ds\bigg)

(7.18)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2(r+\epsilon)}\overline{g}_{\epsilon}(h_{\epsilon}-\overline{h}_{\epsilon})^{2}+\int_{r}^{+\infty}\Big(\frac{1}{2}g_{\epsilon}(h_{\epsilon}-\overline{h}_{\epsilon})-\xi_{\epsilon}-c^{2}(s+\epsilon)^{2}\overline{h}_{\epsilon}e^{\nu_{\epsilon}+\lambda_{\epsilon}}\Big)\frac{h_{\epsilon}-\overline{h}_{\epsilon}}{(s+\epsilon)^{2}}\,ds.

So, for any smooth function
\varphi
whose support is compact and contained in the interior of
\mathcal{O}
, we have

\displaystyle\iint_{\mathcal{O}}\bigg(-\frac{\overline{g}_{\epsilon}(h_{\epsilon}-\overline{h}_{\epsilon})^{2}}{2(r+\epsilon)}+\int_{r}^{+\infty}\Big(\frac{g_{\epsilon}(h_{\epsilon}-\overline{h}_{\epsilon})}{2}-\xi_{\epsilon}-c^{2}(s+\epsilon)^{2}\overline{h}_{\epsilon}e^{\nu_{\epsilon}+\lambda_{\epsilon}}\Big)\frac{h_{\epsilon}-\overline{h}_{\epsilon}}{(s+\epsilon)^{2}}\,ds\bigg)\varphi\,dudr

(7.19)

\displaystyle=-\iint_{\mathcal{O}}\bigg(\int_{r}^{+\infty}\frac{(h_{\epsilon}-\overline{h}_{\epsilon})^{2}}{s+\epsilon}\,ds\bigg)(\partial_{u}\varphi)dudr,

and, by (
6.10
),

\iint_{\mathcal{O}}\bigg(\int_{r}^{+\infty}\frac{(h_{\epsilon}-\overline{h}_{\epsilon})^{2}}{s+\epsilon}\,ds\bigg)\partial_{u}\varphi\,dudr\to\iint_{\mathcal{O}}\gamma\partial_{u}\varphi\,dudr.

(7.20)

We now study the left-hand side of (
7.19
). First of all,
thanks to (2) in (
2.6
)
and Hölder’s inequality, we have

\displaystyle\bigg|\int_{r}^{+\infty}g_{\epsilon}\frac{(h_{\epsilon}-\overline{h}_{\epsilon})^{2}}{(s+\epsilon)^{2}}\,ds\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1+4\pi c^{2}\mathcal{A}^{2}_{\epsilon}(u,r)}{(r+\epsilon)^{2}}\int_{r}^{+\infty}(h_{\epsilon}-\overline{h}_{\epsilon})^{2}ds,

(7.21)

\displaystyle\bigg|\int_{r}^{+\infty}\overline{h}_{\epsilon}(h_{\epsilon}-\overline{h}_{\epsilon})e^{\nu_{\epsilon}+\lambda_{\epsilon}}\,ds\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{r+\epsilon}\bigg|\int_{r}^{+\infty}(r+\epsilon)\,\overline{h}_{\epsilon}(h_{\epsilon}-\overline{h}_{\epsilon})e^{\nu_{\epsilon}+\lambda_{\epsilon}}\,ds\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{r+\epsilon}\bigg(\int_{r}^{+\infty}\big(r+\epsilon)^{2}\overline{h}_{\epsilon}^{2}ds\bigg)^{1/2}\bigg(\int_{r}^{+\infty}(h_{\epsilon}-\overline{h}_{\epsilon})^{2}ds\bigg)^{1/2}.

By the fourth and fifth assertions of Theorem
2.1
,

\int_{r}^{+\infty}(h_{\epsilon}-\overline{h}_{\epsilon})^{2}ds
and
\int_{r}^{+\infty}\big(s+\epsilon)^{2}\overline{h}_{\epsilon}^{2}ds

are bounded by a continuous function of
u
and, consequently, (
7.21
) implies
(as
r\to+\infty
)

\int_{r}^{+\infty}g_{\epsilon}\frac{(h_{\epsilon}-\overline{h}_{\epsilon})^{2}}{(s+\epsilon)^{2}}\,ds\to 0,\hskip 10.00002pt\hskip 20.00003pt\int_{r}^{+\infty}\overline{h}_{\epsilon}(h_{\epsilon}-\overline{h}_{\epsilon})e^{\nu_{\epsilon}+\lambda_{\epsilon}}\,ds\to 0.

Combining this result with the property that (for any
r_{0}>\delta>0
)
h_{\epsilon},\,\overline{h}_{\epsilon},\,g_{\epsilon}
converge uniformly on
\mathcal{O}_{\delta,r_{0}}
to
h,\,\overline{h},\,g
, respectively, and proceeding similarly as in
(
6.10
) we deduce that

\displaystyle\int_{r}^{+\infty}g_{\epsilon}\frac{(h_{\epsilon}-\overline{h}_{\epsilon})^{2}}{(s+\epsilon)^{2}}\,ds

\displaystyle\to\int_{r}^{+\infty}g_{h}\frac{(h-\overline{h})^{2}}{s^{2}}\,ds\hskip 20.00003pt\text{uniformly on $\mathcal{O}_{\delta,r_{0}}$,}

(7.22)

\displaystyle\int_{r}^{+\infty}\overline{h}_{\epsilon}(h_{\epsilon}-\overline{h}_{\epsilon})e^{\nu_{\epsilon}+\lambda_{\epsilon}}\,ds

\displaystyle\to\int_{r}^{+\infty}\overline{h}(h-\overline{h})e^{\nu+\lambda}\,ds\hskip 20.00003pt\text{uniformly on $\mathcal{O}_{\delta,r_{0}}$. }

Next, by integration by parts we find

\displaystyle\int_{r}^{+\infty}\frac{h_{\epsilon}-\overline{h}_{\epsilon}}{(s+\epsilon)^{2}}\xi_{\epsilon}\,ds

(7.23)

\displaystyle=\xi_{\epsilon}\int_{r}^{+\infty}\frac{h_{\epsilon}-\overline{h}_{\epsilon}}{(s+\epsilon)^{2}}\,ds+\int_{r}^{+\infty}\bigg(\overline{g}_{\epsilon}\frac{(h_{\epsilon}-\overline{h}_{\epsilon})}{s+\epsilon}-c^{2}(s+\epsilon)\,\overline{h}_{\epsilon}e^{\nu_{\epsilon}+\lambda_{\epsilon}}\bigg)\bigg(\int_{s}^{+\infty}\frac{h_{\epsilon}-\overline{h}_{\epsilon}}{(s_{1}+\epsilon)^{2}}\,ds_{1}\bigg)\,ds.

Applying Hölder’s inequality we have

\displaystyle\int_{r}^{+\infty}\frac{h_{\epsilon}-\overline{h}_{\epsilon}}{(s+\epsilon)^{2}}\,ds

\displaystyle\leq\bigg(\frac{1}{3(r+\epsilon)^{3}}\bigg)^{1/2}\bigg(\int_{r}^{+\infty}(h_{\epsilon}-\overline{h}_{\epsilon})^{2}ds\bigg)^{1/2}\leq\frac{1}{(r+\epsilon)^{3/2}}F_{2}(u),

where
F_{2}(u)
is a continuous function of
u
, by Theorem
2.1
. Similarly to (
7.22
),
for any
r_{0}\geq\delta>0
the two expressions

\int_{r}^{+\infty}\frac{h_{\epsilon}-\overline{h}_{\epsilon}}{(s+\epsilon)^{2}}\,ds\hskip 10.00002pt\text{and}\hskip 10.00002pt\int_{r}^{+\infty}\bigg(\overline{g}_{\epsilon}\frac{h_{\epsilon}-\overline{h}_{\epsilon}}{s+\epsilon}-c^{2}(s+\epsilon)\,\overline{h}_{\epsilon}e^{\nu_{\epsilon}+\lambda_{\epsilon}}\bigg)\bigg(\int_{s}^{+\infty}\frac{h_{\epsilon}-\overline{h}_{\epsilon}}{(s_{1}+\epsilon)^{2}}\,ds_{1}\bigg)\,ds

converge uniformly on
\mathcal{O}_{\delta,r_{0}}
to, respectively,

\int_{r}^{+\infty}\frac{h-\overline{h}}{s^{2}}\,ds\hskip 10.00002pt\text{and}\hskip 10.00002pt\int_{r}^{+\infty}\bigg(\overline{g}\frac{h-\overline{h}}{s}-c^{2}s\overline{h}\,e^{\nu+\lambda}\bigg)\bigg(\int_{s}^{+\infty}\frac{h-\overline{h}}{s_{1}^{2}}\,ds_{1}\bigg)\,ds.

Taking these expressions as well as (
7.2
) into account in (
7.23
), we obtain

\iint_{\mathcal{O}}\bigg(\int_{r}^{+\infty}\frac{h_{\epsilon}-\overline{h}_{\epsilon}}{(s+\epsilon)^{2}}\xi_{\epsilon}\,ds\bigg)\varphi\,dudr\to\iint_{\mathcal{O}}\bigg(\int_{r}^{+\infty}\frac{h-\overline{h}}{s^{2}}\xi ds\bigg)\varphi\,dudr,

(7.24)

where, similarly to (
7.23
), we used integration by parts. Therefore, we conclude from (
7.22
) and (
7.24
) that the left-hand side of (
7.19
) converges to

\iint_{\mathcal{O}}\bigg(-\frac{1}{2r}\overline{g}(h-\overline{h})^{2}+\int_{r}^{+\infty}\Big(\frac{1}{2}g(h-\overline{h})-\xi-c^{2}s^{2}\overline{h}\,e^{\nu+\lambda}\Big)\frac{(h-\overline{h})}{s^{2}}\,ds\bigg)\varphi\,dudr.

In view of this result together with (
7.21
), we infer from (
7.19
) that
\gamma
is weakly differentiable with respect to
u
in
\mathcal{O}
and

\displaystyle D\gamma=\int_{r}^{+\infty}\Big(\frac{1}{2}g(h-\overline{h})-\xi-c^{2}s^{2}\overline{h}\,e^{\nu+\lambda}\Big)\frac{(h-\overline{h})}{s^{2}}\,ds.

(7.25)

Consequently,
e^{\nu+\lambda}
is weakly differentiable with respect to
u
in
\mathcal{O}
and

D\big(e^{\nu+\lambda}\big)=-4\pi e^{\nu+\lambda}D\gamma.

We next compute the evolution equation for
\frac{1}{r}\int_{\delta}^{r}g\,ds
, with
\delta>0
. We compute

\displaystyle

\displaystyle D\Big(\frac{1}{r}\int_{\delta}^{r}g\,ds\Big)=\frac{\overline{g}}{2r}\Big(\frac{1}{r}\int_{\delta}^{r}(1-4\pi c^{2}s^{2}\overline{h}^{2})e^{\nu+\lambda}\,ds-(1-4\pi c^{2}r^{2}\overline{h}^{2})e^{\nu+\lambda}\Big)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{r}\int_{0}^{r}\Big(D\big(e^{\nu+\lambda}\big)+\frac{1}{2}\overline{g}\partial_{r}\big(e^{\nu+\lambda}\big)-8\pi c^{2}s^{2}e^{\nu+\lambda}\overline{h}\partial_{u}\overline{h}-4\pi c^{2}s^{2}\overline{h}^{2}\partial_{u}\big(e^{\nu+\lambda}\big)\Big)\,ds

\displaystyle=\frac{\overline{g}}{2r^{2}}(r\overline{g}-\delta\overline{g}(\delta))-\frac{1}{2r}\overline{g}g-\frac{4\pi}{r}\int_{\delta}^{r}\Big(gD\gamma-\frac{1}{2}g\overline{g}\frac{(h-\overline{h})^{2}}{s}+c^{2}s\overline{h}(h-\overline{h})\overline{g}e^{\nu+\lambda}+2\,c^{2}s^{2}\overline{h}D\overline{h}\,e^{\nu+\lambda}\Big)\,ds

and, from (
7.25
),

\displaystyle\int_{\delta}^{r}gD\gamma ds=r\overline{g}D\gamma-\delta\overline{g}(\delta)D\gamma(\delta)-\int_{\delta}^{r}s\overline{g}\partial_{r}D\gamma ds,

\displaystyle\int_{\delta}^{r}\overline{g}\Big(s\partial_{r}D\gamma+\frac{g}{2}\frac{(h-\overline{h})^{2}}{s}-c^{2}s\overline{h}(h-\overline{h})e^{\nu+\lambda}\Big)\,ds=\int_{\delta}^{r}\frac{(h-\overline{h})}{s}\overline{g}\xi ds,

so that, in view of (
7.4
) and (
7.7
) we arrive at the identity

\displaystyle D\Big(\frac{1}{r}\int_{\delta}^{r}g\,ds\Big)

\displaystyle=\frac{\overline{g}}{2r^{2}}(r\overline{g}-\delta\overline{g}(\delta))-\frac{1}{2r}\overline{g}g-4\pi\overline{g}D\gamma+\frac{4\pi\,\delta}{r}\overline{g}(\delta)D\gamma(\delta)+\frac{2\,c^{2}\pi}{r}\int_{\delta}^{r}\xi\partial_{r}\xi ds

(7.26)

\displaystyle=f_{\delta}-\frac{1}{2r}\overline{g}(g-\overline{g})-4\pi\overline{g}D\gamma+\frac{2\pi}{r}\xi^{2},

where

f_{\delta}(u,\delta):=-\frac{\overline{g}}{2r^{2}}\delta\overline{g}(u,\delta)-\frac{c^{2}\pi}{r}\xi^{2}(u,\delta)+\frac{4\pi\,\delta}{r}\overline{g}(u,\delta)D\gamma(u,\delta).

The first term of
f_{\delta}
converges to
0
, as
\delta\to 0
, and by Proposition
7.2
, so does the second term for almost all
u
.
Therefore, in order to conclude that
f_{\delta}\to 0
, at almost all
u
, as
\delta\to 0
, it suffices to check the lemma stated next.

7.3
Final argument

Lemma 7.3
.

For almost all
u
, one has
\delta D\gamma(u,\delta)\to 0
as
\delta\to 0
.

Proof.

In view of (
7.25
) we have

\displaystyle|D\gamma|

\displaystyle=\Big|\int_{\delta}^{+\infty}\Big(\frac{1}{2}g(h-\overline{h})-\xi-c^{2}r^{2}\overline{h}\,e^{\nu+\lambda}\Big)\frac{(h-\overline{h})}{r^{2}}\,dr\Big|

(7.27)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{\delta}^{+\infty}e^{\nu+\lambda}\frac{(h-\overline{h})^{2}}{r^{2}}\,dr+c^{4}\pi\,\int_{\delta}^{+\infty}\overline{h}^{2}(h-\overline{h})^{2}e^{\nu+\lambda}\,dr

\displaystyle+c^{2}\int_{\delta}^{+\infty}|\overline{h}(h-\overline{h})|e^{\nu+\lambda}\,dr+\int_{\delta}^{+\infty}|\xi|\frac{|h-\overline{h}|}{r^{2}}\,dr.

By a direct computation, we have

\displaystyle\delta\int_{\delta}^{+\infty}e^{\nu+\lambda}\frac{(h-\overline{h})^{2}}{r^{2}}\,dr=\delta\int_{\delta}^{\sqrt{\delta}}e^{\nu+\lambda}\frac{(h-\overline{h})^{2}}{r^{2}}\,dr+\delta\int_{\sqrt{\delta}}^{+\infty}e^{\nu+\lambda}\frac{(h-\overline{h})^{2}}{r^{2}}\,dr

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\delta}^{\sqrt{\delta}}e^{\nu+\lambda}\frac{(h-\overline{h})^{2}}{r}\,dr+\sqrt{\delta}\int_{\sqrt{\delta}}^{+\infty}e^{\nu+\lambda}\frac{(h-\overline{h})^{2}}{r}\,dr\leq\frac{1}{4\pi}\Big(e^{(\nu+\lambda)(\sqrt{\delta})}-e^{(\nu+\lambda)(\delta)}+\sqrt{\delta}(1-e^{(\nu+\lambda)(\sqrt{\delta})})\Big)

and, since the right-hand side of the previous inequality converges to
0
, as
\delta\to 0
, we obtain

\delta\int_{\delta}^{+\infty}e^{\nu+\lambda}\frac{(h-\overline{h})^{2}}{r^{2}}\,dr\to 0\hskip 10.00002pt\text{as $\delta\to 0$}.

Combining this result with the fact that
r\,\overline{h}
is bounded by a continuous function of
u
(cf. (
2.6
)), we get

\delta\int_{\delta}^{+\infty}e^{\nu+\lambda}\overline{h}^{2}(h-\overline{h})^{2}\,dr=\delta\int_{\delta}^{+\infty}\big(r\,\overline{h}\big)^{2}\Big(e^{\nu+\lambda}\frac{(h-\overline{h})^{2}}{r^{2}}\Big)\,dr\to 0\hskip 10.00002pt\text{as $\delta\to 0$}.

Next, by Hölder’s inequality and (iii) in Section
6.2
we deduce that

\delta\int_{\delta}^{+\infty}|\overline{h}(h-\overline{h})|e^{\nu+\lambda}\,dr\leq\delta\Big(\int_{\delta}^{+\infty}|\overline{h}|^{2}\,dr\Big)^{1/2}\Big(\int_{\delta}^{+\infty}(h-\overline{h})^{2}\,dr\Big)^{1/2}\to 0\hskip 10.00002pt\text{as $\delta\to 0$,}

and the last integral in (
7.27
) can be rewritten as

\int_{\delta}^{+\infty}|\xi|\frac{|h-\overline{h}|}{r^{2}}\,dr=\int_{\delta}^{\sqrt{\delta}}|\xi|\frac{|h-\overline{h}|}{r^{2}}\,dr+\int_{\sqrt{\delta}}^{1}|\xi|\frac{|h-\overline{h}|}{r^{2}}\,dr+\int_{1}^{+\infty}|\xi|\frac{|h-\overline{h}|}{r^{2}}\,dr.

By Hölder’s inequality, we also obtain

\displaystyle\delta\int_{\delta}^{\sqrt{\delta}}|\xi|\frac{|h-\overline{h}|}{r^{2}}\,dr\leq\int_{\delta}^{\sqrt{\delta}}|\xi|\frac{|h-\overline{h}|}{r}\,dr

\displaystyle\leq\Big(\int_{\delta}^{\sqrt{\delta}}\frac{\xi^{2}}{re^{\nu+\lambda}}\,dr\Big)^{1/2}\Big(\int_{\delta}^{\sqrt{\delta}}e^{\nu+\lambda}\frac{(h-\overline{h})^{2}}{r}\,dr\Big)^{1/2}

\displaystyle=\Big(\int_{\delta}^{\sqrt{\delta}}\frac{\xi^{2}}{re^{\nu+\lambda}}\,dr\Big)^{1/2}\Big(\frac{1}{4\pi}(e^{(\nu+\lambda)(\sqrt{\delta})}-e^{(\nu+\lambda)(\delta)})\Big)^{1/2}.

Since, at almost all
u
,
\frac{\xi}{\sqrt{re^{\nu+\lambda}}}\in L^{2}(0,r_{0})
for all
r_{0}>0
, then
\int_{\delta}^{\sqrt{\delta}}\frac{\xi^{2}}{re^{\nu+\lambda}}\,dr\to 0
at almost all
u
as
\delta\to 0
. Hence, by the previous inequality

\delta\int_{\delta}^{\sqrt{\delta}}|\xi|\frac{|h-\overline{h}|}{r^{2}}\,dr\to 0\hskip 10.00002pt\text{as $\delta\to 0$}.

Similarly, we obtain

\displaystyle\delta\int_{\sqrt{\delta}}^{1}|\xi|\frac{|h-\overline{h}|}{r^{2}}\,dr\leq\sqrt{\delta}\int_{0}^{1}|\xi|\frac{|h-\overline{h}|}{r}\,dr

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{\delta}\Big(\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\xi^{2}}{re^{\nu+\lambda}}\,dr\Big)^{1/2}\Big(\int_{0}^{1}e^{\nu+\lambda}\frac{(h-\overline{h})^{2}}{r}\,dr\Big)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\Big(\frac{\delta}{4\pi}\Big)^{1/2}\Big(\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\xi^{2}}{re^{\nu+\lambda}}\,dr\Big)^{1/2}\to 0\hskip 10.00002pt\text{as $\delta\to 0$}.

To complete the proof of the lemma, it only remains to check that at almost all
u

\delta\int_{1}^{+\infty}|\xi|\frac{|h-\overline{h}|}{r^{2}}\,dr\to 0\hskip 10.00002pt\text{as $\delta\to 0$}.

(7.28)

In fact, by Hölder’s inequality

\displaystyle\int_{1}^{+\infty}|\xi|\frac{|h-\overline{h}|}{r^{2}}\,dr

\displaystyle\leq\Big(\int_{1}^{+\infty}\frac{\xi^{2}}{r^{4}}\,dr\Big)^{1/2}\Big(\int_{1}^{+\infty}(h-\overline{h})^{2}\,dr\Big)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{r\geq 1}\Big(\frac{|\xi|}{r}\Big)\Big(\int_{1}^{+\infty}\frac{1}{r^{2}}\,dr\Big)^{1/2}\Big(\int_{1}^{+\infty}(h-\overline{h})^{2}\,dr\Big)^{1/2}.

Observe that, thanks to (
2.7
),

\int_{1}^{+\infty}(h-\overline{h})^{2}\,dr

is bounded by a continuous function of
u
. Furthermore, by Proposition
7.2
,
\sup_{r\geq 1}\frac{|\xi|}{r}
is finite for almost all
u
. Therefore,
\int_{1}^{+\infty}|\xi|\frac{|h-\overline{h}|}{r^{2}}\,dr
is finite for almost all
u
, hence (
7.28
) holds for almost all
u
, as desired.
∎

We have established that, for almost all
u
and all
r>0
,
f_{\delta}\to 0
as
\delta\to 0
. It then follows from (
7.26
) that

\lim_{\delta\to 0}D\Big(\frac{1}{r}\int_{\delta}^{r}g\,ds\Big)=-\frac{1}{2r}\overline{g}(g-\overline{g})-4\pi\overline{g}D\gamma+\frac{2\pi}{r}\xi^{2}.

(7.29)

Furthermore, for any smooth function
\varphi
whose support is a compact set contained in the interior of
\mathcal{O}
, we have

-\iint_{\mathcal{O}}\Big(\frac{1}{r}\int_{\delta}^{r}g\,ds\Big)D\varphi\,dudr=\iint_{\mathcal{O}}D\Big(\frac{1}{r}\int_{\delta}^{r}g\,ds\Big)\varphi\,dudr.

Since
\frac{1}{r}\int_{\delta}^{r}g\,ds\to\overline{g}
for
\delta\to 0
uniformly in the support of
\varphi
, it follows that

-\iint_{\mathcal{O}}\overline{g}D\varphi\,dudr=\lim_{\delta\to 0}\iint_{\mathcal{O}}D\Big(\frac{1}{r}\int_{\delta}^{r}g\,ds\Big)\varphi\,dudr.

(7.30)

On the other hand,
from Proposition
7.2
and the proof of Lemma
7.3

we deduce
that
\varphi f_{\delta}
is dominated by an integrable function and converges to
0
almost everywhere as
\delta\to 0
. Therefore, by the dominated convergence theorem we have

\lim_{\delta\to 0}\iint_{\mathcal{O}}D\Big(\frac{1}{r}\int_{\delta}^{r}g\,ds\Big)\varphi\,dudr=\iint_{\mathcal{O}}\lim_{\delta\to 0}D\Big(\frac{1}{r}\int_{\delta}^{r}g\,ds\Big)\varphi\,dudr.

(7.31)

Hence, we conclude from (
7.26
), Lemma
7.3
and (
7.29
) –(
7.31
) that
\overline{g}
is weakly differentiable in
\mathcal{O}
and

D\overline{g}=\lim_{\delta\to 0}D\Big(\frac{1}{r}\int_{\delta}^{r}g\,ds\Big)=-\frac{1}{2r}\overline{g}(g-\overline{g})-4\pi\overline{g}D\gamma+\frac{2\pi}{r}\xi^{2}.

This means that the Hawking mass
m
is weakly differentiable in
\mathcal{O}
and

\displaystyle Dm

\displaystyle=-\frac{r}{2e^{\nu+\lambda}}\Big(D\overline{g}-\frac{\overline{g}}{e^{\nu+\lambda}}D\big(e^{\nu+\lambda}\big)\Big)-\frac{\overline{g}(g-\overline{g})}{4e^{\nu+\lambda}}-c^{2}\pi r^{2}\overline{h}^{2}\overline{g}=-\frac{\pi}{re^{\nu+\lambda}}\xi^{2}-c^{2}\pi r^{2}\overline{h}^{2}\overline{g}.

Finally, we also check similarly
that
\int_{0}^{r}\Big(\frac{e^{\nu+\lambda}}{\overline{g}}\,ds\Big)
is weakly differentiable in
\mathcal{O}
and

D\Big(\int_{0}^{r}\frac{e^{\nu+\lambda}}{\overline{g}}\,dr\Big)=-2\pi\,\int_{0}^{r}\Big(\frac{e^{\nu+\lambda}}{\overline{g}^{2}}\frac{\xi^{2}}{r}\Big)\,dr-\frac{1}{2}e^{(\nu+\lambda)(u,0)}.

Integrating this equation along the characteristics
\chi(u;u_{1},r_{1})
, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{r_{1}}\Big(\frac{e^{\nu+\lambda}}{\overline{g}}\Big)(u_{1},r)\,dr+2\pi\,\iint_{\mathcal{O}(u_{1},r_{1})}\Big(\frac{e^{\nu+\lambda}}{\overline{g}^{2}}\frac{\xi^{2}}{(r+\epsilon)}\Big)\,drdu+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{u_{1}}e^{(\nu+\lambda)(u,0)}\,du=\int_{0}^{r_{0}}\Big(\frac{e^{\nu+\lambda}}{\overline{g}}\Big)(0,r)\,dr,

with
\mathcal{O}(u_{1},r_{1})=\big\{(u,r)\,/\,0<u<u_{1},\,0<r<\chi(u;u_{1},r_{1})\big\}.

Consequently, the generalized solution exists globally and this concludes the proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Continuity and compactness of the solution mapping

Continuity of the mapping
\Psi_{b}
.

Assume that
(t_{i},h_{i})\to(t,h)
in
[0,1]\times X_{b}
(that is, in the
C^{1}

k
and
k_{i}

(t,h)
and
(t_{i},h_{i})

k_{i}
converges to

in the
C^{1}
norm.
Since the set of all smooth functions in
X_{b}
is dense, we can pick up a sequence of smooth functions
\{z_{i}\}\subset X_{b}
such that
\|h_{i}-z_{i}\|_{C^{1}}\to 0
. We then define
w_{i}
to be the unique solution of (

) associated with
(t_{i},\,z_{i})
. Obviously, we have

\|k_{i}-k\|_{C^{1}}\leq\|k_{i}-w_{i}\|_{C^{1}}+\|w_{i}-k\|_{C^{1}}

(A.1)

and we analyze first the term

\displaystyle D_{a,\,t_{i},z_{i}}w_{i}-\frac{t_{i}}{2(r+\epsilon)}(g_{a,\,t_{i},z_{i}}-\overline{g}_{a,\,t_{i},z_{i}})w_{i}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2(r+\epsilon)}(g_{a,\,t_{i},z_{i}}-\overline{g}_{a,\,t_{i},z_{i}})\overline{w}_{i}-\frac{c^{2}(r+\epsilon)}{2}e^{\nu_{z_{i}}+\lambda_{z_{i}}}\overline{w}_{i},

\displaystyle D_{a,t,h}k-\frac{t}{2(r+\epsilon)}(g_{a,t,h}-\overline{g}_{a,t,h})k

D_{a,t,h}(w_{i}-k)-\frac{t}{2r}(g_{a,t,h}-\overline{g}_{a,t,h})(w_{i}-k)=P(t,h,\,t_{i},\,z_{i}),

(A.3)

\displaystyle P(t,h,\,t_{i},\,z_{i})

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{2(r+\epsilon)}(g_{a,t,h}-\overline{g}_{a,t,h})\,\overline{h}-\frac{1}{2(r+\epsilon)}(g_{a,\,t_{i},z_{i}}-\overline{g}_{a,\,t_{i},z_{i}})\overline{w}_{i}

\displaystyle+\frac{c^{2}(r+\epsilon)}{2}e^{\nu_{h}+\lambda_{h}}\overline{h}-\frac{c^{2}(r+\epsilon)}{2}e^{\nu_{z_{i}}+\lambda_{z_{i}}}\overline{w}_{i}+\frac{1}{2}\big(\rho(\overline{g}_{a,\,t_{i},z_{i}})-\rho(\overline{g}_{a,t,h})\big)\partial_{r}w_{i}

\|h-z_{i}\|_{C^{1}}
and
|t_{i}-t|

0
, it follows from the continuity of
\Psi_{b}
and the
C^{2}
bound on

that

P(t,h,\,t_{i},\,z_{i})\to 0\hskip 18.49988pt\mbox{in $C^{0}$}.

(A.5)

On the other hand, since
w_{i}(0,r)=k(0,r)=h_{0}(r)

(A.6)

Hence, from (
A.5
) and (
A.6

w_{i}\to k
in
C^{0}
.

(A.7)

Differentiating now the equations (
A.2

\displaystyle D_{a,t,h}(\partial_{r}k)-\frac{1}{2(r+\epsilon)}\Big((g_{a,t,h}-\overline{g}_{a,t,h})\rho^{\prime}(\overline{g}_{a,t,h})+t(g_{a,t,h}-\overline{g}_{a,t,h})\Big)\partial_{r}k=f_{1}(h,k,t)

\displaystyle D_{a,\,t_{i},z_{i}}(\partial_{r}w_{i})-\frac{1}{2(r+\epsilon)}\Big((g_{a,\,t_{i},z_{i}}-\overline{g}_{a,\,t_{i},z_{i}})\rho^{\prime}(\overline{g}_{a,\,t_{i},z_{i}})+t_{i}(g_{a,\,t_{i},z_{i}}-\overline{g}_{a,\,t_{i},z_{i}})\Big)\partial_{r}w_{i}=f_{1}(z_{i},w_{i},t_{i}),

where

is given as in (

). Comparing the previous two equations we find

\displaystyle D_{a,t,h}(\partial_{r}w_{i}-\partial_{r}k)-

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2(r+\epsilon)}\Big((g_{a,t,h}-\overline{g}_{a,t,h})\rho^{\prime}(\overline{g}_{a,t,h})+t(g_{a,t,h}-\overline{g}_{a,t,h})\Big)\,(\partial_{r}w_{i}-\partial_{r}k)=\widetilde{P}_{i}(t,h,\,t_{i},\,z_{i}),

with

\displaystyle\widetilde{P}(t,h,\,t_{i},\,z_{i})

\displaystyle:=f_{1}(h,k,t)-f_{1}(z_{i},w_{i},t_{i})+\frac{1}{2}(\rho(\overline{g}_{a,\,t_{i},z_{i}})-\rho(\overline{g}_{a,t,h}))\partial_{rr}w_{i}

\displaystyle

\displaystyle\partial_{r}(w_{i}-k)(u_{1},r(u_{1}))=\int_{0}^{u_{1}}\widetilde{P}(t,h,\,t_{i},\,z_{i})\big|_{\chi_{h,u_{1}}}e^{\int_{u_{2}}^{u_{1}}\frac{1}{2(r_{3}+\epsilon)}\big((g_{a,t,h}-\overline{g}_{a,t,h})\rho^{\prime}(\overline{g}_{a,t,h})+t(g_{a,t,h}-\overline{g}_{a,t,h})\big)\big|_{\chi_{h,u_{1}}}\,du_{3}}\,du_{2}.

From the basic continuity properties in Section

. Combining this result with (

), we find that

and, similarly,
\|w_{i}-k_{i}\|_{C^{1}}\to 0
. Therefore, returning to (

converges to

in the

Compactness of the mapping
\Psi_{b}
.

Given any bounded sequence
(t_{i},h_{i})
in
[0,1]\times X_{b}
, let us denote by
k_{i}
the unique solution of the transport equation (
3.7
) associated with
(t_{i},h_{i})
. We need to prove that there exists a subsequence (still denoted by
\{k_{i}\}
) such that
\{k_{i}\}
converges in the space
X_{b}
.
Without loss of generality we assume that
h_{i}
is smooth. Since
\{h_{i}\}
is bounded in
C^{1}
, a standard compact embedding theorem tells us that there exists a subsequence (still denoted by
\{h_{i}\}
) such that
\{h_{i}\}
converges in the
C^{0}
norm. In particular, given any
\alpha>0
we can guarantee that

|h_{i}-h_{j}|\leq\alpha\hskip 9.24994pt\text{ for all sufficiently large }i,j.

(A.10)

After passing to a subsequence, we may assume that
|t_{i}-t_{j}|\leq\alpha
.
An analysis similar to the one used to prove the continuity of
\Psi_{b}
shows that, along the characteristics
\chi_{h_{j},u_{1}}=\chi_{a,t_{j},h_{j}}(\cdot;u_{1},r_{1})
,

\displaystyle(k_{i}-k_{j})(u_{1},r(u_{1}))

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{u_{1}}P(t_{i},h_{i},\,t_{j},h_{j})\big|_{\chi_{h_{j},u_{1}}}e^{\int_{u_{2}}^{u_{1}}\frac{t_{j}}{2(r_{3}+\epsilon)}(g_{a,\,t_{j},h_{j}}-\rho(\overline{g}_{a,\,t_{j},h_{j}}))\big|_{\chi_{h_{j},u_{1}}}\,du_{3}}\,du_{2},

\displaystyle\partial_{r}(k_{i}-k_{j})(u_{1},r(u_{1}))

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{u_{1}}\hskip-5.69046pt\widetilde{P}(t_{i},h_{i},\,t_{j},h_{j})\big|_{\chi_{h_{j},u_{1}}}e^{\int_{u_{2}}^{u_{1}}\frac{1}{2(r_{3}+\epsilon)}\big((g_{a,\,t_{j},h_{j}}-\overline{g}_{a,\,t_{j},h_{j}})\rho^{\prime}(\overline{g}_{a,\,t_{j},h_{j}})+t_{j}(g_{a,\,t_{j},h_{j}}-\overline{g}_{a,\,t_{j},h_{j}})\big)\big|_{\chi_{h_{j},u_{1}}}du_{3}}\,du_{2},

where

and
\widetilde{P}
are defined as before.
Thanks to the continuity of
\Psi_{b}
and the

bound available on
k
, this implies
that there exists a constant

depending on
\|h_{i}\|_{C^{1}}
and
(h_{0},a,b,\epsilon)
such that

\|k_{i}-k_{j}\|_{C^{1}}\leq a_{7}\alpha
.
We conclude that
\{k_{i}\}
converges in
C^{1}
, and this completes the proof of Proposition
3.1
.

Appendix B
Derivation of energy estimates

We give here a proof of Proposition
4.5
.
We start from the identity

\partial_{r}(D\overline{h}_{\epsilon,b})+\frac{1}{r}D\overline{h}_{\epsilon,b}=\frac{1}{2r^{2}}e^{\nu_{\epsilon,b}-\lambda_{\epsilon,b}}(h_{\epsilon,b}-\overline{h}_{\epsilon,b})-\frac{c^{2}}{2}e^{\nu_{\epsilon,b}+\lambda_{\epsilon,b}}\overline{h}_{\epsilon,b},

4.2

\displaystyle-4\pi\,\int_{0}^{b}\partial_{r}\,\overline{h}_{\epsilon,b}\bigg(\int_{0}^{r}\overline{g}_{\epsilon,b}\partial_{r}\,\overline{h}_{\epsilon,b}\,ds-c^{2}\int_{0}^{r}(s+\epsilon)\,\overline{h}_{\epsilon,b}e^{\nu_{\epsilon,b}+\lambda_{\epsilon,b}}\,ds\bigg)dr.

(B.3)

) for estimating

Therefore, by Lemma

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{+\infty}(h_{\epsilon,b}-\overline{h}_{\epsilon,b})^{2}\,dr

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{b}(h_{0}-\overline{h}_{0})^{2}\,dr+\frac{u}{4\pi}+\frac{17}{\pi}\int_{0}^{u}\bigg(\max\big\{c^{2}e^{-2},\,m_{\epsilon,b}(u_{1},b)\big\}\bigg)\,du_{1}

Proof of Lemma
6.1
.

By the Hölder inequality we have

|\overline{h}_{\epsilon}|\leq\frac{1}{r}\int_{0}^{r}|h_{\epsilon}(u,s)|\,ds\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{r}}\left(\int_{0}^{r}h^{2}_{\epsilon}(u,s)\,ds\right)^{1/2},

hence

\sup_{r\geq\delta}|\overline{h}_{\epsilon}(u,r)|\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{\delta}}\left(\int_{0}^{+\infty}h^{2}_{\epsilon}(u,s)\,ds\right)^{1/2},

and the result then follows from the inequality (3) in (

).
∎

Proof of Lemma

From the proof of Theorem

so it only remains to check that

\lim_{r\to+\infty}\big(\sup_{u\in[0,u_{0}]}|(r+\epsilon)\,\overline{h}_{\epsilon}(u,r)|\big)=0.

(C.1)

We argue by contradiction. Suppose that there exists a sequence
(u_{i},r_{i})
in

(u_{\infty},+\infty)
such that

For any
\zeta>0
, let

|u_{\zeta}-u_{\infty}|\leq\zeta

2.7
)

Next, similarly to (
4.6

which, in combination with (

\alpha=\lim\big(|(r_{i}+\epsilon)\,\overline{h}_{\epsilon}|(u_{i},r_{i})\big)\leq\sqrt{\frac{\zeta M_{\epsilon}(0)}{2\pi}},

which is a contradiction if
\zeta\leq\frac{\alpha^{2}\pi}{M_{\epsilon}(0)}

∎

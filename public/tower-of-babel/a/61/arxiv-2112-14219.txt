On the collapse of the local Rayleigh condition for the Hydrostatic Euler equations and the finite time blow-up for the Semi-Lagrangian equations
Victor Cañulef-Aguilar

Abstract
. In this paper we study the propagation of the local Rayleigh condition for the two-dimensional hydrostatic Euler equation in the framework of the local well-posedness result by Masmoudi and Wong [ MW12 ] . We show under certain assumptions that such solutions will develop singularities or collapse the local Rayleigh condition. In addition, we find necessary conditions for the global solvability. Finally, we establish the finite time blow-up of solutions to the semi-lagrangian equations introduced by Brenier in [ Bre99 ] for certain class of initial data.



1.
Introduction

We consider the two-dimensional hydrostatic Euler equation in a periodic channel
\Omega=\mathbb{T}\times(0,1)
:

(1.1)

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} u_{t}+uu_{x}+vu_{y}+P_{x}&=0,&&\\
u_{x}+v_{y}&=0,&&\\
P_{y}&=0,&&\\
v\rvert_{y=0,1}&=0,&&\\
u\rvert_{t=0}&=u_{0},&&\end{aligned}\right.

where
(u,v)
is the velocity field,
P
is the scalar pressure and
u
satisfies the local Rayleigh condition (i.e.
\partial_{y}^{2}u>0
). Equations (
1.1
) are derived from the two-dimensional incompressible Euler equation by means of the hydrostatic approximation. The mathematical justification of the formal limit has been established in several articles under the local Rayleigh condition (see for instance
[
Bre03
]
,
[
Gre99
]
and
[
MW12
]
). On the other hand, if the initial condition does not satisfies the local Rayleigh condition, the convergence may not hold, as shown in
[
Gre00
]
,
[
Gre99
]
. Furthermore, the ill-posedness of the linearization of (
1.1
) around certain shear flows was proved in
[
Ren09
]
, as well as the ill-posedness of (
1.1
) around certain shear flows, which was proved in
[
HKN16
]
.

The local existence of solutions to (
1.1
) has been proven in
[
KTVZ10
]
and
[
KTVZ11
]
in the analytic setting. Nevertheless, the finite time blow-up of solutions to (
1.1
) was established in
[
KW12
]
(under the assumption
u_{0}(x_{0},y,t)\equiv C
, for
y\in[0,1]
, which is incompatible with the local Rayleigh condition) and
[
CINT12
]
(under the assumption of parity, which is again incompatible with the local Rayleigh condition. See also
[
EE97
]
, where the finite time blow-up of certain solutions to the unsteady Prandtl equations is proved by similar methods).

Equivalently, we may consider the vorticity formulation of the hydrostatic Euler equations (
1.1
)

(1.2)

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} \omega_{t}+u\omega_{x}+v\omega_{y}&=0,&&\\
(u,v)&=\nabla^{\perp}\mathcal{A}\omega,&&\\
\omega\rvert_{t=0}&=\omega_{0},&&\\
\end{aligned}\right.

where the vorticity
\omega
is defined by

(1.3)

\omega=u_{y}.

The local well-posedness of (
1.2
) was proved in
[
MW12
]
in Sobolev spaces
H^{s}
, with
s\geq 4
, for initial vorticity profiles that satisfy the local Rayleigh condition (i.e.
\partial_{y}\omega_{0}>0
). In this article we address the propagation in time of the local Rayleigh condition; we get lower bounds for certain functionals that quantify in some sense the validity of the local Rayleigh condition (see (
1.13
) and (
1.14
)). Under certain assumptions, we will prove that the above functionals cannot remain bounded, which implies the collapse of the local Rayleigh condition or the formation of singularities (see Theorem
1
, which is proved in Section
4
). One of the main achievements of this work, is the derivation of certain identities which are satisfied by every solution to (
1.2
), as long as the solution exists (see Proposition
4
in Section
3
). In addition, we derive some necessary conditions for the global solvability in the framework of the local well-posedness result by Masmoudi and Wong
[
MW12
]
(see Theorem
2
and Section
5
). Despite Theorem
1
does not guarantees that the solution remains in
H^{1}(\mathbb{T}\times(0,1))
as long as the local Rayleigh condition holds, we can control the
H^{1}
norm and the validity of the local Rayleigh condition under additional assumptions, which is presented in Section
6
.

The second main result of this article deals with the finite time blow-up of smooth solutions to the semilagrangian equations, which are derived from the hydrostatic Euler equations (
1.2
) under certain assumptions. More precisely, if
\omega_{0}
satisfies the local Rayleigh condition and

(1.4)

\displaystyle\omega_{0}(x,0)

\displaystyle\equiv k,

(1.5)

\displaystyle\omega_{0}(x,1)

\displaystyle\equiv k+1,

for certain constant
k
, then problem (
1.2
) is equivalent to the following problem in
\Omega=\mathbb{T}\times(0,1)
:

(1.6)

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} \varv_{t}+\partial_{x}\left(\frac{\varv^{2}}{2}+P\right)&=0,&&\\
\partial_{t}h_{a}+\partial_{x}\left(\varv h_{a}\right)&=0,&&\\
\int_{0}^{1}h_{a}da&=1,&&\\
\partial_{a}P&=0,&&\\
(\nu,h_{a})\rvert_{t=0}&=(\nu_{0},\partial_{a}h_{0}),&&\\
\end{aligned}\right.

where
\varv
and
h_{a}
are defined by

(1.7)

\displaystyle\omega(x,h(x,t,a),t)

\displaystyle\equiv k+a,\text{\hskip 2.84544pt for $a\in[0,1]$,}

(1.8)

\displaystyle\varv(x,t,a)

\displaystyle=u(x,h(x,t,a),t),\text{\hskip 2.84544pt for $a\in[0,1]$,}

in this case we assume that
\partial_{a}h_{0}
is positive, which is equivalent to the validity of the local Rayleigh condition. Equations (
1.6
) are known as the semilagrangian equations, which were introduced by Brenier in
[
Bre99
]
. In the same article, the local existence of solutions to (
1.2
) was proved in the class
C
(which is defined as the set of
C^{1}
-functions in
\mathbb{T}\times(0,1)
that satisfy the local Rayleigh condition, (
1.4
) and (
1.5
)). The semilagrangian equations (
1.6
) have a natural extension to higher dimensions, namely

(1.9)

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}\mathbf{v}+\mathbf{v}\cdot\nabla\mathbf{v}+\nabla P&=0,&&\\
\partial_{t}h_{a}+\nabla\cdot\left(\mathbf{v}h_{a}\right)&=0,&&\\
\int_{0}^{1}h_{a}da&=1,&&\\
\partial_{a}P&=0,&&\\
\partial_{i}\varv_{j}-\partial_{j}\varv_{i}&=0,&&\text{for $i,j\leq d$,}\\
(\mathbf{v},h_{a})\rvert_{t=0}&=(\mathbf{v}_{0},\partial_{a}h_{0}),&&\end{aligned}\right.

where
\mathbf{v}:\mathbb{T}^{d}\times[0,1]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
is the semilagrangian velocity,
P
is the scalar pressure and
h_{a}
is a positive function in
\mathbb{T}^{d}\times[0,1]
. Let us point out that the semilagrangian equations (
1.6
) establish a vertical change of coordinates by means of the level curves of the vorticity
\omega
, where the injectivity follows by the local Rayleigh condition. Hence, the extension of the semilagrangian equations to higher dimensions have no clear physical meaning.

A necessary condition for the global solvability of (
1.9
) was obtained in
[
Bre99
]
, which is given by:

(1.10)

\int_{0}^{1}\left\lvert\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\mathbf{v}(x^{\prime},t,a)dx^{\prime}\right\rvert^{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}}h_{a}(x,t,a)dxda=\int_{\mathbb{T}\times(0,1)}\lvert\mathbf{v}\rvert^{2}(x,t,a)h_{a}(x,t,a)dxda,

where both sides are time independent. Theorem
3
establishes the finite time blow-up of solutions to the semilagrangian equations (
1.9
) for certain class of initial data, which is proved in Section
7
. In the following subsections, we will state the main results of this article.

1.1.
Collapse of the local Rayleigh condition or singularity formation

Theorem 1
.

Assume that the initial vorticity
\omega_{0}\in H^{4}(\mathbb{T}\times(0,1))
satisfies the local Rayleigh condition (i.e.
\partial_{y}\omega_{0}>0
) and
\partial_{x}\omega_{0}
is not identically
0
. Let
\omega\in C([0,T];H^{4}(\mathbb{T}\times(0,1)))
be the solution to the problem (
1.2
) that satisfies the local Rayleigh condition in
[0,T]
. Set

(1.11)

E_{1}(t)=\int_{\mathbb{T}\times(0,1)}\frac{\omega\omega_{x}}{\omega_{y}}dxdy=\int_{\mathbb{T}\times(0,1)}\left(\frac{\omega\omega_{x}}{\omega_{y}}-u_{x}\right)dxdy

and

(1.12)

E_{2}(t)=\int_{\mathbb{T}\times(0,1)}u^{2}\frac{\omega\omega_{x}}{\omega_{y}}dxdy=\int_{\mathbb{T}\times(0,1)}u^{2}\left(\frac{\omega\omega_{x}}{\omega_{y}}-u_{x}\right)-uP_{x}dxdy.

Assume that
E_{1}(0)\geq 0
or
E_{2}(0)\geq 0
. Then there exists a positive time
T^{*}
such that at least one of the following properties holds:

•

\displaystyle\limsup_{t\rightarrow T^{*}}\|\omega(t)\|_{H^{4}(\mathbb{T}\times(0,1))}=\infty,

•

\displaystyle\limsup_{t\rightarrow T^{*}}\left\|\frac{1}{\omega_{y}(t)}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}\times(0,1))}=\infty.

Furthermore, as long as
\|\omega(t)\|_{H^{4}(\mathbb{T}\times(0,1))}
and
\left\|\frac{1}{\omega_{y}(t)}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}\times(0,1))}
remain bounded, we have the following estimates:

(1.13)

\log\left(\frac{1}{E_{1}(0)}\frac{1}{\frac{1}{E_{1}(0)}-t}\right)\leq\int_{\mathbb{T}\times(0,1)}\log\left(\frac{\partial_{y}\omega_{0}}{\omega_{y}(t)}\right)dxdy,\text{\hskip 48.36958ptif $E_{1}(0)>0,$}

(1.14)

\log\left(\frac{\|u\|_{2}^{2}}{E_{2}(0)}\frac{1}{\frac{\|u\|_{2}^{2}}{E_{2}(0)}-t}\right)\leq tC_{1}(\omega_{0})+C_{2}(\omega_{0})\int_{\mathbb{T}\times(0,1)}\log\left(\frac{\partial_{y}\omega_{0}}{\omega_{y}(t)}\right)dxdy,\text{\hskip 2.84544ptif $E_{2}(0)>0,$}

(1.15)

\frac{1}{\frac{1}{E_{1}(0)}-t}\leq E_{1}(t)\leq E_{1}(0)+C_{3}(\omega_{0})\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}\times(0,1)}\omega_{x}^{2}\left(1+\frac{1}{\omega_{y}^{2}}\right)dxdyd\tau,\text{\hskip 2.84544ptif $E_{1}(0)>0,$}

(1.16)

\frac{\|u\|_{2}^{2}}{\frac{\|u\|_{2}^{2}}{E_{2}(0)}-t}\leq E_{2}(t)\leq E_{2}(0)+C_{4}(\omega_{0})\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}\times(0,1)}\omega_{x}^{2}\left(1+\frac{1}{\omega_{y}^{2}}\right)dxdyd\tau,\text{\hskip 1.42271ptif $E_{2}(0)>0,$}

where
C_{1}(\omega_{0})=\left(\frac{3}{2}\frac{\|\omega_{0}\|_{\infty}}{\|u\|_{2}}\right)^{4}\lvert E_{1}(0)\rvert+\frac{E_{2}(0)}{\|u\|_{2}^{2}}
,
C_{2}(\omega_{0})=\left(\frac{3}{2}\frac{\|\omega_{0}\|_{\infty}}{\|u\|_{2}}\right)^{4},

C_{3}(\omega_{0})=2\|\omega_{0}\|_{\infty}^{2}+\frac{2}{\pi^{2}}
and
C_{4}(\omega_{0})=2\left(\frac{3}{\pi}\|\omega_{0}\|_{\infty}\right)^{2}+2\left(\frac{3}{2}\|\omega_{0}\|_{\infty}\right)^{2}C_{3}(\omega_{0})
.

Remark 1
.

If
\omega_{0}\in H^{4}(\mathbb{T}\times(0,1))
satisfies the local Rayleigh condition and
\partial_{x}\omega_{0}\equiv 0
, then the solution to (
1.2
) is stationary. Conversely, the stationary solutions in
H^{4}(\mathbb{T}\times(0,1))
that satisfy the local Rayleigh condition are
x
-independent (see Corollary
2
).

Remark 2
.

Suppose that
\omega_{0}\in H^{4}(\mathbb{T}\times(0,1))
is even in
x
, satisfies the local Rayleigh condition and
\partial_{x}\omega_{0}
is not identically
0
, then
E_{1}(0)=0
, which implies the collapse in finite time (more precisely, the local Rayleigh condition will collapse or
\omega
will develop singularities) of the solution to (
1.2
). Moreover, since
\omega(x,y,t)
and
\omega(-x,y,-t)
solve (
1.2
) with the same initial condition,
\omega(x,y,t)\equiv\omega(-x,y,-t)
(thanks to the uniqueness). In that case, the solution will collapse going forward and backward in time.

Remark 3
.

It is worth mentioning that the set of initial conditions that satisfy the assumptions of Theorem
1
is nonempty. Indeed, if we choose
\omega_{0}(x,y)=2y-sin(2\pi x-y)
, then
\partial_{x}\omega_{0}=-2\pi cos(2\pi x-y)
and
\partial_{y}\omega_{0}=2+cos(2\pi x-y)
, which yields

\displaystyle E_{1}(0)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{T}\times(0,1)}\frac{(2y-sin(2\pi x-y))\cdot-2\pi cos(2\pi x-y)}{2+cos(2\pi x-y)}dxdy

\displaystyle=-2\pi\int_{\mathbb{T}\times(0,1)}\left(2y-sin(2\pi x-y)\right)dxdy+4\pi\int_{\mathbb{T}\times(0,1)}\frac{(2y-sin(2\pi x-y))}{2+cos(2\pi x-y)}dxdy

\displaystyle=-2\pi+\int_{\mathbb{T}\times(0,1)}\frac{4\pi\cdot 2y}{2+cos(2\pi x-y)}dxdy

\displaystyle=-2\pi+\frac{4\pi}{\sqrt{3}}\int_{0}^{1}2ydy

\displaystyle=2\pi\left(\frac{2}{\sqrt{3}}-1\right)

\displaystyle>0.

Remark 4
.

Suppose that
\omega_{0}(x,y)\in H^{4}(\mathbb{T}\times(0,1))
satisfies the local Rayleigh condition,
\partial_{x}\omega_{0}
is not identically
0
but
E_{1}(0)<0
, then
\tilde{\omega}_{0}(x,y)=\omega_{0}(-x,y)
satisfies
E_{1}(0)>0
(the same applies for
E_{2}
). Hence, the assumptions of Theorem
1
are not so restrictive.

1.2.
Necessary conditions for global solvability

Theorem 2
.

Assume that the initial vorticity
\omega_{0}\in H^{4}(\mathbb{T}\times(0,1))
satisfies the local Rayleigh condition (i.e.
\partial_{y}\omega_{0}>0
). Let
\omega\in C([0,T];H^{4}(\mathbb{T}\times(0,1)))
be the solution to the problem (
1.2
) that satisfies the local Rayleigh condition in
[0,T]
. Let
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
be defined by (
1.11
) and (
1.12
) respectively. Assume further that
T
can be chosen arbitrarily large, then we have:

(1.17)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{T}\times(0,1)}\left\lvert P_{x}+u\left(u_{x}-\frac{\omega\omega_{x}}{\omega_{y}}\right)\right\rvert^{2}dxdydt=-E_{2}(0),

(1.18)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{T}\times(0,1)}\left\lvert u_{x}-\frac{\omega\omega_{x}}{\omega_{y}}\right\rvert^{2}dxdydt=-E_{1}(0),

(1.19)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{1}}P_{x}^{2}dxdt\leq-2E_{2}(0)-\frac{9}{2}\|\omega_{0}\|_{\infty}^{2}E_{1}(0),

(1.20)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\lvert E_{1}(t)\rvert dt\leq\int_{\mathbb{T}\times(0,1)}\log\left(\frac{2\|\omega_{0}\|_{\infty}}{\partial_{y}\omega_{0}}\right)dxdy,

where
E_{1}(0),E_{2}(0)<0
.

Remark 5
.

Let us observe that in the semilagrangian formulation (
1.6
), the above energies are equal to (see Proposition
2
):

\displaystyle E_{1}(T)

\displaystyle=-\int_{\mathbb{T}\times(0,1)}\varv_{x}h_{a}dxda=E_{1}(0)+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}\times(0,1)}\varv_{x}^{2}h_{a}dxdadt,

\displaystyle E_{2}(T)

\displaystyle=-\int_{\mathbb{T}\times(0,1)}\varv^{2}\varv_{x}h_{a}dxda=E_{2}(0)+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}\times(0,1)}\varv_{t}^{2}h_{a}dxdadt,

thanks to the change of coordinates
y=h(x,t,a)
. The above identities are a particular case of (
7.2
) and (
7.3
), when
d=1
.

1.3.
Finite time blow-up of the Semi-lagrangian equations

Theorem 3
.

Let
(\mathbf{v},h_{a})
be a smooth solution to (
1.9
). Set

(1.21)

E_{1}(t)=-\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}\times(0,1)}\left(\nabla\cdot\mathbf{v}\right)h_{a}dxda

and

(1.22)

E_{2}(t)=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}\times(0,1)}\left(\mathbf{v}\cdot\mathbf{v}_{t}\right)h_{a}dxda.

Assume that
E_{1}(0)>0
or
E_{2}>0
, then the solution blows up in finite time.
Moreover, as long as the solution exists, we have the following estimates:

(1.23)

\displaystyle d\log\left(\frac{d}{E_{1}(0)}\frac{1}{\frac{d}{E_{1}(0)}-t}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}\times(0,1)}\log(h_a(\tau))h_{a}(\tau)dxda\rvert_{\tau=0}^{\tau=t},\text{\hskip 2.84544ptif $E_{1}(0)>0,$}

(1.24)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{\frac{d}{E_{1}(0)}-t}\leq E_{1}(t)

\displaystyle=E_{1}(0)+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}\times(0,1)}\lvert\nabla\mathbf{v}\rvert^{2}h_{a}dxdadt,\text{\hskip 2.84544ptif $E_{1}(0)>0,$}

(1.25)

\displaystyle\frac{\|\mathbf{v}\|^{2}}{\frac{\|\mathbf{v}\|^{2}}{E_{2}(0)}-t}\leq E_{2}(t)

\displaystyle=E_{2}(0)+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}\times(0,1)}\lvert\partial_{t}\mathbf{v}\rvert^{2}h_{a}dxdadt,\text{\hskip 1.42271ptif $E_{2}(0)>0,$}

where
\|\mathbf{v}\|^{2}=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}\times(0,1)}\lvert\mathbf{v}\rvert^{2}h_{a}dxda
.

The following corollary is a direct consequence of Theorem
3
, Jensen’s inequality and Lemma
7
(applied to the probability measure
\mu=h_{a}dxda
).

Corollary 1
.

Let
(\mathbf{v},h_{a})
be a smooth solution to (
1.9
) in
[0,T]
. Let
E_{1}
be defined by (
1.21
). Suppose that
E_{1}(0)>0
, then

\displaystyle\exp\left(\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}\times(0,1)}\partial_{a}h_{0}\log\left(\partial_{a}h_{0}\right)dxda\right)\left(\frac{d}{E_{1}(0)}\frac{1}{\frac{d}{E_{1}(0)}-t}\right)^{d}

\displaystyle\leq\exp\left(\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}\times(0,1)}h_{a}(T)\log\left(h_{a}(T)\right)dxda\right)

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}\times(0,1)}h_{a}(T)^{1+p}dxda\right)^{1/p},

for every
p\in(0,\infty)
. Moreover

(1.26)

\exp\left(\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}\times(0,1)}h_{a}\log(h_a)dxda\right)=\lim_{p\rightarrow 0}\left(\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}\times(0,1)}h_{a}^{1+p}dxda\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Remark 6
.

Corollary
1
gives a lower bound for the
L^{p}(\mathbb{T}^{d}\times(0,1))
norm of
h_{a}
for
p>1
, which cannot be extended to
p=1
, because
\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}\times(0,1)}h_{a}dxda=1
.

Remark 7
.

The first equation in (
1.9
), is equivalent to:

(1.27)

\partial_{t}\mathbf{v}+\nabla\left(\frac{\lvert\mathbf{v}\rvert^{2}}{2}+P\right)=0,

thanks to the curl free condition
\partial_{i}\varv_{j}=\partial_{j}\varv_{i}
.

Remark 8
.

In particular, smooth solutions to (
1.9
) satisfy the following equations in
\mathbb{T}^{d}
:

(1.28)

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}\int_{0}^{1}\mathbf{v}h_{a}da+\nabla\cdot\int_{0}^{1}\mathbf{v}\otimes\mathbf{v}h_{a}da+\nabla P&=0,&&\\
\int_{0}^{1}h_{a}da&=0,&&\\
\nabla\cdot\int_{0}^{1}\mathbf{v}h_{a}da&=0,&&\\
\partial_{i}\varv_{j}-\partial_{j}\varv_{i}&=0,&&\text{for $i,j\leq d$},&&\end{aligned}\right.

which can be seen as an averaged version of the incompressible Euler equations in
\mathbb{T}^{d}

(1.29)

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}\mathbf{v}+\nabla\cdot\left(\mathbf{v}\otimes\mathbf{v}\right)+\nabla P&=0,&&\\
\nabla\cdot\mathbf{v}&=0,&&\\
\mathbf{v}\rvert_{t=0}&=\mathbf{v}_{0}.&&\end{aligned}\right.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section we will present some elementary results concerning equations (
1.1
), (
1.2
) and (
1.9
) that will be used throughout this article.

2.1.
Hydrostatic Euler equations

First, let us point out that any solution to (
1.1
) satisfies
\int_{0}^{1}u(x,y,t)dy\equiv k
(see Proposition
1
). Moreover,
\tilde{u}(x,y,t)=u(x+kt,y,t)-k
solves (
1.1
) with a slightly different initial data and
\int_{0}^{1}\tilde{u}(x,y,t)dy\equiv 0
. Hence, without loss of generality, we may assume that the solution to (
1.1
) satisfies

(2.1)

\int_{0}^{1}u(x,y,t)dy\equiv 0.

The following proposition summarizes some properties that will be used in the rest of the article.

Proposition 1
.

Assume that the initial vorticity
\omega_{0}\in H^{4}(\mathbb{T}\times(0,1))
satisfies the local Rayleigh condition (i.e.
\partial_{y}\omega_{0}>0
). Let
\omega\in C([0,T];H^{4}(\mathbb{T}\times(0,1)))
be the solution to the problem (
1.2
) that satisfies the local Rayleigh condition in
[0,T]
. Then we have

(2.2)

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}(\omega)=(1-y)\int_{0}^{y}z\omega(x,z,t)dz+y\int_{y}^{1}(1-z)\omega(x,z,t)dz,

(2.3)

\displaystyle u(x,y,t)=\int_{0}^{1}z\omega(x,z,t)dz-\int_{y}^{1}\omega(x,z,t)dz,

(2.4)

\displaystyle v(x,y,t)=(1-y)\int_{0}^{y}z\omega_{x}(x,z,t)dz+y\int_{y}^{1}(1-z)\omega_{x}(x,z,t)dz,

(2.5)

\displaystyle\partial_{x}\int_{0}^{1}udy=0,

(2.6)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\int_{0}^{1}udy=0,

(2.7)

\displaystyle P_{x}(x,t)=-\partial_{x}\left(\int_{0}^{1}u^{2}(x,y,t)dy\right),

(2.8)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\|u\|_{2}^{2}=0,

(2.9)

\displaystyle\|u\|_{\infty}\leq\frac{3}{2}\|\omega_{0}\|_{\infty}.

Proof.

By (
2.1
), we may assume that the stream function
\mathcal{A}(\omega)
solves

(2.10)

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} -\partial_{y}^{2}\mathcal{A}(\omega)&=\omega,&&\\
\mathcal{A}(\omega)\rvert_{y=0,1}&=0,&&\end{aligned}\right.

which implies

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}(\omega)

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{y}\int_{0}^{z}\omega(x,s,t)dsdz+y\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{z}\omega(x,s,t)dsdz

\displaystyle=(1-y)\int_{0}^{y}z\omega(x,z,t)dz+y\int_{y}^{1}(1-z)\omega(x,z,t)dz,

from which we get (
2.2
). Next, (
2.3
) and (
2.4
) follow directly from (
2.2
). The incompressibility condition and the boundary value in (
1.1
) implies (
2.5
). Next, by (
2.5
), (
1.1
), the
x
-periodicity and integration by parts,

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\int_{0}^{1}udy

\displaystyle=\partial_{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}\times(0,1)}udxdy

\displaystyle=-\int_{\mathbb{T}\times(0,1)}\partial_{x}\left(\frac{u^{2}}{2}+P\right)+v\omega dxdy

\displaystyle=-\int_{\mathbb{T}\times(0,1)}uu_{x}dxdy

\displaystyle=-\int_{\mathbb{T}\times(0,1)}\partial_{x}\left(\frac{u^{2}}{2}\right)dxdy

\displaystyle=0,

which proves (

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\left(u_{t}+uu_{x}+vu_{y}+P_{x}\right)dy

\displaystyle=2\int_{0}^{1}uu_{x}dy+P_{x},

-periodicity and integration by parts

3.

and

Throughout this section we will work in the framework of the local well-posedness result by Masmoudi and Wong
[

]
, namely, we will assume that

The following two lemmas are elementary, so the proof will be omitted.

Lemma 1
.

Assume that
\omega_{0}\in H^{4}(\mathbb{T}\times(0,1))
satisfies the local Rayleigh condition. Let
\omega\in C([0,T];H^{4}(\mathbb{T}\times(0,1)))

. Denote by

the material derivative, then:

(3.1)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}\times(0,1)}f(x,y,t)dxdy

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{T}\times(0,1)}D_{t}f(x,y,t)dxdy,

(3.2)

\displaystyle D_{t}(fg)

\displaystyle=fD_{t}g+gD_{t}f,

(3.3)

\displaystyle D_{t}\left(\frac{f}{g}\right)

\displaystyle D_{t}\left(\log(g)\right)

.

Lemma 2
.

Assume that
\omega_{0}\in H^{4}(\mathbb{T}\times(0,1))
satisfies the local Rayleigh condition. Let

(3.5)

\displaystyle D_{t}(u)

\displaystyle=-P_{x},

(3.6)

\displaystyle D_{t}(\omega)

\displaystyle=0,

(3.7)

\displaystyle D_{t}(u_{x})

\displaystyle=-u_{x}^{2}-v_{x}\omega-P_{xx},

(3.8)

\displaystyle D_{t}(P_{x})

\displaystyle=P_{xt}+uP_{xx},

(3.9)

\displaystyle D_{t}(\omega_{x})

\displaystyle=-u_{x}\omega_{x}-v_{x}\omega_{y},

(3.10)

\displaystyle D_{t}(\omega_{y})

\displaystyle=u_{x}\omega_{y}-\omega\omega_{x},

where
0\leq t\leq T

Lemma 3
.

Assume that
\omega_{0}\in H^{4}(\mathbb{T}\times(0,1))

Lemma 4
.

Assume that
\omega_{0}\in H^{4}(\mathbb{T}\times(0,1))
satisfies the local Rayleigh condition. Let
\omega\in C([0,T];H^{4}(\mathbb{T}\times(0,1)))

) that satisfies the local Rayleigh condition in

\omega
does not vanishes. By (
2.5
), there exists

such that

),

at time

. Thus,

is

-independent at

satisfy (

(see
[

]

Conversely, assume that
\omega
vanishes in some points. Applying (

), we have

(3.22)

0,&if\hskip 8.5359pt\omega=0.\\
\end{cases}

. Moreover,

Finally, if

).
Thus,
f\equiv u_{t}\equiv 0
at time

. Furthermore,

) with the same initial data. Then, by uniqueness

(see
[

]
). Moreover, since

is

Lemma 6
.

Let

. Assume that

and

. Then there exists a positive time

as

for

From which we obtain:

Proof.

Let

\mathbb{T}

\displaystyle\frac{u_{x}}{\omega}(x,\tilde{y},t)

\displaystyle=\int_{y_{0}(x)}^{\tilde{y}}\frac{\omega\omega_{x}-u_{x}\omega_{y}}{\omega^{2}}dy

\displaystyle=\int_{y_{0}(x)}^{\tilde{y}}\frac{\omega\omega_{x}-u_{x}\omega_{y}}{\omega_{y}}\frac{\omega_{y}}{\omega^{2}}dy,

from which we get:

\displaystyle\lvert u_{x}\rvert(x,\tilde{y},t)

\displaystyle\leq\lvert\omega\rvert(x,\tilde{y},t)\left\lvert\frac{\omega\omega_{x}-u_{x}\omega_{y}}{\omega_{y}}\right\rvert_{\infty}\int_{y_{0}(x)}^{\tilde{y}}\frac{\omega_{y}}{\omega^{2}}dy

\displaystyle=\left\lvert\frac{\omega\omega_{x}-u_{x}\omega_{y}}{\omega_{y}}\right\rvert_{\infty}\left\lvert\frac{\omega(x,\tilde{y},t)}{\omega(x,y_{0}(x),t)}-1\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{\|\omega_{0}\|_{\infty}}{\min\lvert\omega_{0}\rvert}+1\right)\left\lvert\frac{\omega\omega_{x}-u_{x}\omega_{y}}{\omega_{y}}\right\rvert_{\infty},

for every

On the other hand, by Lemmas

and

\displaystyle D_{t}\left(\frac{\omega_{x}^{2}}{\omega_{y}}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{-2u_{x}\omega_{x}^{2}\omega_{y}-2\omega_{x}v_{x}\omega_{y}^{2}-u_{x}\omega_{x}^{2}\omega_{y}+\omega\omega_{x}^{3}}{\omega_{y}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{\omega\omega_{x}}{\omega_{y}}-3u_{x}\right)\frac{\omega_{x}^{2}}{\omega_{y}}-2\omega_{x}v_{x},

integrating in
\mathbb{T}\times(0,1)
, we get

(6.4)

\partial_{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}\times(0,1)}\frac{\omega_{x}^{2}}{\omega_{y}}dxdy=\int_{\mathbb{T}\times(0,1)}\frac{\omega_{x}^{2}}{\omega_{y}}\left(\frac{\omega\omega_{x}}{\omega_{y}}-3u_{x}\right)dxdy,

where we have used that

\int_{\mathbb{T}\times(0,1)}\omega_{x}v_{x}dxdy=\int_{\mathbb{T}\times(0,1)}u_{x}u_{xx}dxdy=0,

thanks to integration by parts and the
x
-periodicity. Thus, by (
6.4
) and (
6.2
)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}\times(0,1)}\frac{\omega_{x}^{2}}{\omega_{y}}dxdy

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{T}\times(0,1)}\frac{\omega_{x}^{2}}{\omega_{y}}\left(\frac{\omega\omega_{x}}{\omega_{y}}-3u_{x}\right)dxdy

\displaystyle\leq\left(\left\lvert\frac{\omega\omega_{x}-u_{x}\omega_{y}}{\omega_{y}}\right\rvert_{\infty}+2\left\lvert u_{x}\right\rvert_{\infty}\right)\int_{\mathbb{T}\times(0,1)}\frac{\omega_{x}^{2}}{\omega_{y}}dxdy

\displaystyle\leq\left(3+\frac{2\|\omega_{0}\|_{\infty}}{\min\lvert\omega_{0}\rvert}\right)\left\lvert\frac{\omega\omega_{x}-u_{x}\omega_{y}}{\omega_{y}}\right\rvert_{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{T}\times(0,1)}\frac{\omega_{x}^{2}}{\omega_{y}}dxdy,

By applying Grönwall’s inequality, we get (
6.3
), which completes the proof of Proposition
8
.

∎

The aim of this section is to prove Theorem

Proposition 9
.

Let
(\mathbf{v},h_{a})
be a smooth solution to (
1.9
). Let
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
be defined by (
1.21
) and (
1.22

(7.1)

\displaystyle E_{1}

\displaystyle=\partial_{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}\times(0,1)}\log(h_a)h_{a}dxda,

(7.2)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}E_{1}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}\times(0,1)}\left\lvert\nabla\mathbf{v}\right\rvert^{2}h_{a}dxda,

(7.3)

which proves (
7.1
). Next, let us compute the time derivative of

\displaystyle\partial_{t}E_{1}(t)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}\times(0,1)}\nabla\cdot\nabla\left(\frac{\lvert\mathbf{v}\rvert^{2}}{2}+P\right)h_{a}+\nabla\cdot\mathbf{v}\nabla\cdot(\mathbf{v}h_{a})dxda

8.
Appendix. Integral lemma

The aim of this appendix is to prove the integral identity conerning the integral of the logarithm of a function.

Lemma 7
.

Let
(\Omega,\mu)
be a measure space and let
f\in L^{1}(\Omega)
be a positive function in
\Omega
. Assume that
\mu(\Omega)=1
, then

(8.1)

\exp\left(\int_{\Omega}\log(f)d\mu\right)=\lim_{p\rightarrow 0}\left(\int_{\Omega}f^{p}d\mu\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Proof.

Let us proceed as in
[
Gra14
]
, let
p_{n}
be any positive sequence converging to
0
.
By Jensen’s inequality,

\exp\left(\int_{\Omega}\log(f)d\mu\right)\leq\left(\int_{\Omega}f^{p}d\mu\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},\text{\hskip 2.84544pt $\forall p>0$},

which implies

(8.2)

\exp\left(\int_{\Omega}\log(f)d\mu\right)\leq\liminf\left(\int_{\Omega}f^{p_{n}}d\mu\right)^{\frac{1}{p_{n}}}.

Since
\log t\leq t-1
, for
t>0
, we conclude that

(8.3)

\log\left(\int_{\Omega}f^{p_{n}}d\mu\right)\leq\int_{\Omega}(f^{p_{n}}-1)d\mu.

Multiplying (
8.3
) by
\frac{1}{p_{n}}
and applying exponential, we get

\left(\int_{\Omega}f^{p_{n}}d\mu\right)^{\frac{1}{p_{n}}}\leq\exp\left(\frac{1}{p_{n}}\int_{\Omega}\left(f^{p_{n}}-1\right)d\mu\right),

which implies

(8.4)

\limsup\left(\int_{\Omega}f^{p_{n}}d\mu\right)^{\frac{1}{p_{n}}}\leq\limsup\exp\left(\frac{1}{p_{n}}\int_{\Omega}\left(f^{p_{n}}-1\right)d\mu\right).

Now, let us compute
\limsup\exp\left(\frac{1}{p_{n}}\int_{\Omega}\left(f^{p_{n}}-1\right)d\mu\right)
. Assume that
q_{n}\searrow 0
, then

(8.5)

\frac{1}{q_{n}}\left(t^{q_{n}}-1\right)\searrow\log(t),\text{\hskip 2.84544pt $\forall t>0$ }.

Let
h_{n}:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a sequence of positive functions defined by

h_{n}(x)=\frac{1}{q_{0}}\left(f^{q_{0}}-1\right)-\frac{1}{q_{n}}\left(f^{q_{n}}-1\right).

By (
8.5
),

h_{n}(x)\nearrow\frac{1}{q_{0}}\left(f^{q_{0}}-1\right)-\log(f).

Applying Lebesgue’s monotone convergence theorem, we get

\int_{\Omega}h_{n}d\mu\nearrow\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{q_{0}}\left(f^{q_{0}}-1\right)-\log(f)d\mu.

Thus,

\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{q_{n}}\left(f^{q_{n}}-1\right)d\mu\searrow\int_{\Omega}\log(f)d\mu.

Therefore,

(8.6)

\limsup\exp\left(\frac{1}{p_{n}}\int_{\Omega}\left(f^{p_{n}}-1\right)d\mu\right)=\exp\left(\int_{\Omega}\log(f)d\mu\right).

Finally, by (
8.2
), (
8.4
) and (
8.6
)

\limsup\left(\int_{\Omega}f^{p_{n}}d\mu\right)^{\frac{1}{p_{n}}}\leq\exp\left(\int_{\Omega}\log(f)d\mu\right)\leq\liminf\left(\int_{\Omega}f^{p_{n}}d\mu\right)^{\frac{1}{p_{n}}}.

This concludes the proof of Lemma
7
.

∎

Normal approximation for subgraph count in random hypergraphs 0 0 footnotetext: 2020 MS Classification: 05C65, 05C80, 60F05. Key words and phrases : random hypergraph, subgraph count, asymptotic normality, Kolmogorov distance, Wasserstein distance.
Wojciech Michalczuk, Mikołaj Nieradko, Grzegorz Serafin

Abstract
A non-uniform and inhomogeneous random hypergraph model is considered, which is a straightforward extension of the celebrated binomial random graph model 𝔾 ⁡ ( n , p ) \mathbb{G}(n\mathchar 59\relax p) . We establish necessary and sufficient conditions for small hypergraph count to be asymptotically normal, and complement them with convergence rate in both the Wasserstein and Kolmogorov distances. Next we narrow our attention to the homogeneous model and relate the obtained results to the fourth moment phenomenon. Additionally, a short proof of necessity of aforementioned conditions is presented, which seems to be absent in the literature even in the context of the model 𝔾 ⁡ ( n , p ) \mathbb{G}(n\mathchar 59\relax p) .

Introduction

Let
\mathbb{H}(n\mathchar 59\relax\boldsymbol{p})
,
\boldsymbol{p}=(p_{1}\mathchar 59\relax p_{2}\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}\ldots\mathchar 59\relax p_{n})
, be a random hypergraph model on vertices from the set
[n]:=\{1\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}2\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}\ldots\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}n\}
, in which every non-empty edge
e
exists independently with probability
p_{|e|}\in(0\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}1)
depending on its size
|e|
. In particular, loops are included. When restricted to edges of size
2
only, we obtain the celebrated binomial (Gilbert-Erdős-Rényi) random graph model
\mathbb{G}(n\mathchar 59\relax p)
. In order to understand better the context of this article, let us briefly introduce the model
\mathbb{G}(n\mathchar 59\relax p)
, and recall its properties that are relevant from our point of view.

In the random graph model
\mathbb{G}(n\mathchar 59\relax p)
any two of
n
vertices are independently connected with probability
p=p(n)
. When
n\rightarrow\infty
and
p
depends on
n
in a suitable manner, one can observe many interesting phenomena, that have been subject of research for last decades (see
[
17
,
20
]
and references therein). One of the studied quantities is so-called small subgraph count. Namely, for a fixed graph
G
we denote by
Z_{G}^{n}
the random variable that counts the number of subgraphs of
\mathbb{G}(n\mathchar 59\relax p)
that are isomorphic to
G
. It is shown in
[
30
]
that the standardized variable
\widetilde{Z}_{G}^{n}=(Z_{G}^{n}-\mathbf{E}[Z_{G}^{n}])/\sqrt{\text{Var}[Z_{G}^{n}]}
converges in distribution to a r.v.
\mathcal{N}
with the standard normal distribution
\mathcal{N}(0\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}1)
if and only if

\displaystyle\min_{F\subseteq G:\,e_{F}\geq 1}\{n^{v_{F}}p^{e_{F}}\}\rightarrow\infty\ \ \ \ \text{ and }\ \ \ \ (1-p)n^{2}\rightarrow\infty\mathchar 59\relax

(1.1)

where
v_{F}
and
e_{F}
stand for the numbers of vertices and edges of
F
, respectively. Later on, this result has been complemented with the convergence rate of the form
[
1
]

\displaystyle d_{1}(\widetilde{Z}_{G}^{n}\mathchar 59\relax\mathcal{N}):=\sup_{h\in\mathcal{L}}\big|\mathbf{E}[h(\widetilde{Z}_{G}^{n})]-\mathbf{E}[h(\mathcal{N})]\big|\leq C\,\big((1-p)\min_{F\subseteq G:\,e_{F}\geq 1}\{n^{v_{F}}p^{e_{F}}\}\big)^{-1/2}\mathchar 59\relax

(1.2)

where
C
is a constant depending on
G
and
\mathcal{L}
is the class of functions
h:\mathbf{R}\rightarrow\mathbf{R}
satisfying
\|h\|_{\infty}+\|h^{\prime}\|_{\infty}\leq 1
. The conjecture that the same rate holds for more desired Kolmogorov distance (cf. (
3.6
)) remained unsolved for the next three decades, and eventually it was confirmed in
[
26
]
, see also
[
29
,
32
,
16
]
.

There exists a vast literature on random hypergraphs, however, mostly uniform (every edge is of the same size) and homogeneous (edge existence probabilities are the same) models are considered, see e.g.,
[
22
,
4
,
5
,
24
]
, and also
[
2
,
6
,
7
,
8
,
18
]
for some other models.
In
[
11
]
, necessary and sufficient conditions for asymptotic normality of
\widetilde{Z}_{H}^{n}
have been obtained for
k
-uniform hypergraphs
H
.
Comparing to (
1.1
), the only change is that
n^{2}
is replaced with
n^{k}
.
From a technical point of view, deriving convergence rates of
\widetilde{Z}_{H}^{n}
to the standard normal distribution seems very similar in random graphs and uniform random hypergraphs. This might explain, why there is no separate result of this kind for the latter ones. As mentioned in the last paragraph, even for random graphs estimates of the Kolmogorov distance are very recent
[
26
]
. In inhomogeneous random (hyper)graphs some novel phenomena occur, as for instance some of the edge probabilities may be close to zero, while other ones are close to one. A uniform random hypergraph with strong inhomogeneity has been studied in
[
7
]
in the context of large deviations. On the other hand, non-uniformity creates another kind of obstacles. For example, when all of the edge probabilities are close to one, the subgraph count might be approximated by a sum of independent random variables, nonetheless the Berry-Esseen theorem still does not provide us with bounds recovering conditions for asymptotic normality. The level of generality presented in this article has been recently addressed
in
[
12
]
, where some kind of a counterpart of a threshold for the property
\{Z_{H}^{n}>0\}
is derived. Also, a bound on the Wasserstein distance between
\widetilde{Z}_{H}^{n}
and
\mathcal{N}
in a similar model is established in
[
31
]
.

Following the history of research on
Z_{G}^{n}
in the binomial model
\mathbb{G}(n\mathchar 59\relax p)
, we devote this article to examining the asymptotic normality of
\widetilde{Z}_{H}^{n}
, where
Z_{H}^{n}
stands for the variable counting copies of a hypergraph
H
in
\mathbb{H}(n\mathchar 59\relax\boldsymbol{p})
.
Precisely, we derive necessary and sufficient conditions for
\widetilde{Z}_{H}^{n}
to be asymptotically normal, and complement them with convergence rates in both: Wasserstein and Kolmogorov distances. The obtained results extend the ones known for
\mathbb{G}(n\mathchar 59\relax p)
. Next, we narrow our attention to the homogeneous case, where
\boldsymbol{p}=(p\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}p\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}\ldots\mathchar 59\relax p)
for some
p\in(0\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}1)
, and express the aforementioned results by means of the excess kurtosis
\mathbf{E}[(\widetilde{Z}^{n}_{H})^{4}]-3
. This refers to the so-called
fourth moment phenomenon
, which has been initially studied by Peter de Jong
[
9
,
10
]
for general
U
-statistics and recently gained a lot of attention in the field of stochastic analysis, where its quantitative versions in various settings are studied
[
25
,
15
,
14
,
13
,
23
]
. Additionally, we provide a very simple proof of the necessary conditions for the asymptotic normality of
\widetilde{Z}_{H}^{n}
, which seems to be new even for the model
\mathbb{G}(n\mathchar 59\relax p)
.

The complex structure of the model
\mathbb{H}(n\mathchar 59\relax\boldsymbol{p})
makes it resistant to many methods applied to the
\mathbb{G}(n\mathchar 59\relax p)
model, including the classical ones from
[
1
,
20
,
11
,
30
]
. In order to obtain the convergence rates we employ the tools developed in
[
27
,
26
,
28
]
, as they seem more flexible. Nevertheless, direct application does not lead to satisfactory results, therefore much more subtle strategy is implemented. The starting point of most of the proofs is the Hoeffding decomposition of
\widetilde{Z}_{H}^{n}
, which is not only needed for the aforementioned approach, but also allows us to determine the core terms responsible for the asymptotic behaviour. To obtain the necessary condition the Feller-Lévy condition is verified, and then Lindeberg condition is presented in the simplest possible form. Let us note here that stronger Lyapunov condition fails in the case of inhomogeneous model.
Finally, the derivation of the results related to the fourth moment phenomenon relies mainly on combinatorial arguments.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section 2 we describe the model
\mathbb{H}(n\mathchar 59\relax\boldsymbol{p})
in detail, establish and discuss the Hoeffding decomposition of the variable
\widetilde{Z}_{H}^{n}
, and also gather essential notation used in the article. Section 3 is devoted to the main results of the paper, while the remaining sections contain their proofs (for
H
without isolated vertices, see Remark

2
Preliminaries

2.1
Basic properties of the model
\mathbb{H}(n\mathchar 59\relax\boldsymbol{p})

As mentioned in Introduction, the random hypergraph model
\mathbb{H}(n\mathchar 59\relax\boldsymbol{p})
is built on
n
vertices, which we take from the set
[n]=\{1\mathchar 59\relax 2\mathchar 59\relax 3\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}\ldots\mathchar 59\relax n\}
. We impose no restrictions on sizes of edges (or hyperedges), thus any of
2^{n}-1
edges from the set
2^{[n]}\backslash\{\emptyset\}
may occur. The complete hypergraph
([n]\mathchar 59\relax 2^{[n]}\backslash\{\emptyset\})
will be denoted by
K_{n}^{*}
.
The vector
\boldsymbol{p}=\boldsymbol{p}(n)
is the vector of edge probabilities, i.e. every edge
e
of size
|e|
exists, independently of the other edges, with the probability
p_{|e|}\in(0\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}1)
.

Our interest is focused on the random variable counting isomorphic copies of a given hypergraph
H
in the random hypergraph
\mathbb{H}(n\mathchar 59\relax\boldsymbol{p})
. Precisely, for a fixed
H
we define

Z_{H}^{n}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}H^{\prime}\subseteq K^{*}_{n}\\
H^{\prime}\simeq H\end{subarray}}\mathds{1}_{\left\{H^{\prime}\subseteq\mathbb{H}(n\mathchar 59\relax\boldsymbol{p})\right\}}.

(2.1)

Here, the relation
\simeq
denotes standard isomorphism between two hypergraphs. Furthermore,
\subseteq
stands for strong (and not necessarily induced) inclusion of hypergraphs, i.e. for two hypergraphs
F
and
H
we write
F\subseteq H
whenever
V(F)\subseteq V(H)
and
E(F)\subseteq E(H)
, where
V(H)
and
E(H)
are the number of vertices and edges, respectively, of the hypergraph
H
. Let us note that in the literature one often writes
\left\{H\subseteq\mathbb{H}(n\mathchar 59\relax\boldsymbol{p})\right\}
in the sense of
\left\{Z_{H}^{n}\geq 1\right\}
. For clarity, we use only the latter notation of this property.

By
\widetilde{Z}_{H}^{n}
we denote the standardization of the random variable
Z_{H}^{n}
, i.e.

\widetilde{Z}_{H}^{n}=\frac{Z_{H}^{n}-\mathbf{E}[Z_{H}^{n}]}{\sqrt{\text{Var}{Z_{H}^{n}}}}

Expected value of
Z_{H}^{n}
is easy to calculate and is given by
\mathbf{E}[Z_{H}^{n}]=N_{H}^{n}\prod_{e\in E(H)}p_{|e|}
, where

N_{H}^{n}:=|\{H^{\prime}\subseteq K^{*}_{n}:H^{\prime}\simeq H\}|
is the total number of copies of
H
in the complete hypergraph
K_{n}^{*}
. In the case of the variance, the formula is more complicated. Nevertheless, from our point of view estimates are sufficient and they are given by (see
[
12
, proof of Theorem 3.2]
, and also formula (
2.7
) below for the variance)

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}[Z_{H}^{n}]

\displaystyle\approx n^{v_{H}}P_{H}\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}

(2.2)

\displaystyle\text{Var}[Z_{H}^{n}]

\displaystyle\approx P_{H}^{2}\max_{F\subseteq H\mathchar 59\relax e_{F}\geq 1}n^{2v_{H}-v_{F}}\frac{1-P_{F}}{P_{F}}\approx P_{H}^{2}\max_{F\subseteq H\mathchar 59\relax e_{F}\geq 1}n^{2v_{H}-v_{F}}\frac{Q_{F}}{P_{F}}\mathchar 59\relax

(2.3)

where
v_{H}=|V(H)|
and

\displaystyle P_{H}=\prod_{e\in E(H)}p_{|e|}\mathchar 59\relax\hskip 30.0ptQ_{H}=\prod_{e\in E(H)}(1-p_{|e|}).

The notation
\approx
is understood as follows: for two sequences of non-negative functions
f_{n}
and
g_{n}
we write
f_{n}\approx g_{n}
whenever

C_{1}f_{n}\leq g_{n}\leq C_{2}f_{2}

holds on the indicated domain for almost all
n\in\mathbf{N}
. Here,
C_{1}\mathchar 59\relax C_{2}>0
are constant possibly dependent on the fixed hypergraph
H
. If they also depend on some other parameter
a
, we indicate it by
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle a}}{{\approx}}
. Additionally, if we are interested only in one of inequalities above, we write
f_{n}\lesssim g_{n}
or
g_{n}\lesssim f_{n}
.

2.2
Hoeffding decomposition

The Hoeffding decomposition is a very useful tool originally introduced in
[
19
]
for symmetric
U
-statistics. Below, we present it in the general setup. It has been already exploited in the context of subgraph count in random graphs in
[
3
,
21
,
11
,
26
,
28
]
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
(X_{1}\mathchar 59\relax\ldots\mathchar 59\relax X_{n})
be a family of independent random variables, and
(\mathcal{F}_{J})_{J\subseteq[n]}
be a family of
\sigma
-fields defined by

\mathcal{F}_{J}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\sigma(X_{j}:j\in J)\mathchar 59\relax~~~~J\subseteq[n].

A centered
\mathcal{F}_{[n]}
-measurable random variable admits the Hoeffding decomposition whenever it might be expressed as

W_{n}=\sum_{J\subseteq[n]}W_{J}\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}

where every
W_{J}
,
J\subseteq[n]
is a
\mathcal{F}_{J}
-measurable random variable and

\mathbb{E}[W_{J}\mid\mathcal{F}_{K}]=0\mathchar 59\relax~~~~\text{if}~~~~J\nsubseteq K\subseteq[n].

In particular, if for some fixed
d\in[n]
it holds that
W_{J}=0
a.s. for
|J|>d
and there exists
J\subseteq[n]
such that
|J|=d
and
\text{Var}[W_{J}]>0
, we call
W_{n}
a generalized
U
-statistic of order
d
.

Let us denote by
e_{1}\mathchar 59\relax e_{2}\mathchar 59\relax\ldots\mathchar 59\relax e_{2^{n}-1}
all the edges of the complete hypergraph
K^{*}_{n}
. Then, let
X_{1}\mathchar 59\relax X_{2}\mathchar 59\relax\ldots\mathchar 59\relax X_{2^{n}-1}
be independent random variables representing the existence of these edges, such that for any
i\in[2^{n}-1]

X_{i}
is a random variable with the Bernoulli distribution
\mathcal{B}(1\mathchar 59\relax p_{|e_{i}|})
.
Notice that hypergraphs without isolated vertices are uniquely determined by their edges. Thus, for such a hypergraph
H
we have (cf. Remark
3.8
)

Z_{H}^{n}=\sum_{A\subseteq[2^{n}-1]}\mathds{1}_{A\sim H}\prod_{a\in A}X_{a}\mathchar 59\relax

(2.4)

where
{A\sim H}
means that the edges
\{e_{a}:a\in A\}
constitute a hypergraph which is isomorphic to
H
. Clearly, only the terms corresponding to the sets
A
satisfying
|A|=e_{H}
do not vanish.

Theorem 2.2
.

For a hypergraph
H
without isolated vertices the random variable
\widetilde{Z}_{H}^{n}
admits the Hoeffding decomposition

\displaystyle\widetilde{Z}_{H}^{n}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}B\subseteq[2^{n}-1]\\
1\leq|B|\leq e_{H}\end{subarray}}W_{B}\mathchar 59\relax

where

\displaystyle W_{B}=\frac{P_{H}}{\sqrt{\mathrm{Var}[Z_{H}^{n}]}}\left[\prod_{b\in B}\left(\widetilde{X}_{b}\sqrt{\frac{1-p_{|e_{b}|}}{p_{|e_{b}|}}}\right)\right]\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}A\subseteq[2^{n}-1]\\
A\cap B=\emptyset\end{subarray}}\mathds{1}_{A\cup B\sim H}.

(2.5)

Proof.

The uncorrelatedness of
W_{B}
and
W_{B^{\prime}}
for
B\neq B^{\prime}
is clear, since every
\widetilde{X}_{b}
is centered.
Rewriting (
2.4
) we get

\displaystyle Z_{H}^{n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{A\subseteq[2^{n}-1]}\mathds{1}_{A\sim H}\prod_{a\in A}X_{a}=\sum_{A\subseteq[2^{n}-1]}\mathds{1}_{A\sim H}\prod_{a\in A}\big[(X_{a}-p_{|e_{a}|})+p_{|e_{a}|}\big]

\displaystyle=\sum_{A\subseteq[2^{n}-1]}\mathds{1}_{A\sim H}\sum_{B\subseteq A}\left(\prod_{b\in B}(X_{b}-p_{|e_{b}|})\right)\left(\prod_{a\in A\backslash B}p_{|e_{a}|}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{A\subseteq[2^{n}-1]}\mathds{1}_{A\sim H}\sum_{B\subseteq A}\left(\prod_{b\in B}\frac{X_{b}-p_{|e_{b}|}}{p_{|e_{b}|}}\right)\left(\prod_{a\in A}p_{|e_{a}|}\right)

\displaystyle=P_{H}\sum_{A\subseteq[2^{n}-1]}\mathds{1}_{A\sim H}\sum_{B\subseteq A}\prod_{b\in B}\left(\widetilde{X}_{b}\sqrt{\frac{1-p_{|e_{b}|}}{p_{|e_{b}|}}}\right)

\displaystyle=P_{H}\sum_{B\subseteq[2^{n}-1]}\prod_{b\in B}\left(\widetilde{X}_{b}\sqrt{\frac{1-p_{|e_{b}|}}{p_{|e_{b}|}}}\right)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}A\subseteq[2^{n}-1]\\
A\cap B=\emptyset\end{subarray}}\mathds{1}_{A\cup B\sim H}.

Observe now that the term of the sum corresponding to
B=\emptyset
is

P_{H}\sum_{A}\mathds{1}_{A\sim H}=\mathbf{E}\left[Z_{H}^{n}\right]
. Furthermore, due to the last indicator
\mathds{1}_{A\cup B\sim H}
, we can restrict the size of
B
to be less or equal
e_{H}
.
Thus, we arrive at

\widetilde{Z}_{H}^{n}=\frac{Z_{H}^{n}-\mathbf{E}\left[Z_{H}^{n}\right]}{\sqrt{\mathrm{Var}[Z_{H}^{n}]}}=\frac{P_{H}}{\sqrt{\mathrm{Var}[Z_{H}^{n}]}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}B\subseteq[2^{n}-1]\\
1\leq|B|\leq e_{H}\end{subarray}}\prod_{b\in B}\left(\widetilde{X}_{b}\sqrt{\frac{1-p_{|e_{b}|}}{p_{|e_{b}|}}}\right)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}A\subseteq[2^{n}-1]\\
A\cap B=\emptyset\end{subarray}}\mathds{1}_{A\cup B\sim H}

as required.
∎

Using the notation from Theorem
2.2
, we define for
H
and
F
without isolated vertices

\displaystyle I_{F}

\displaystyle:=\sum_{B\sim F}W_{B}\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}\ \ \ \ F\subseteq H\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}

\displaystyle I_{m}

\displaystyle:=\sum_{|B|=m}W_{B}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}F\subseteq H\\
e_{F}=m\end{subarray}}I_{F}\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}\ \ \ \ \ 1\leq m\leq e_{H}.

This lets us write

\displaystyle\widetilde{Z}_{H}^{n}=\sum_{F\subseteq H}I_{F}=\sum_{m=1}^{e_{H}}I_{m}.

(2.6)

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
H
be a hypergraph without isolated vertices. Then, for any
F\subseteq H
with at least one edge and without isolated vertices we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{Var}[I_{F}]\approx\frac{P_{H}^{2}}{\mathrm{Var}[Z_{H}^{n}]}n^{2v_{H}-v_{F}}\frac{Q_{F}}{P_{F}}.

Furthermore, for any hypergraph
H
it holds that

\displaystyle\mathrm{Var}[Z_{H}^{n}]

\displaystyle\approx P_{H}^{2}n^{2v_{H}}\max_{F\subseteq H\mathchar 59\relax e_{F}\geq 1}n^{-v_{F}}\frac{Q_{F}}{P_{F}}={P_{H}^{2}n^{2v_{H}}}\left({\min_{F\subseteq H\mathchar 59\relax e_{F}\geq 1}n^{v_{F}}P_{F}/{Q_{F}}}\right)^{-1}.

(2.7)

Proof.

From (
2.5
) we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{Var}[I_{F}]=\sum_{B\sim F}\frac{P^{2}_{H}}{{\mathrm{Var}[Z_{H}^{n}]}}\prod_{b\in B}\left({\frac{1-p_{|e_{b}|}}{p_{|e_{b}|}}}\right)\bigg(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}A\subseteq[2^{n}-1]\\
A\cap B=\emptyset\end{subarray}}\mathds{1}_{A\cup B\sim H}\bigg)^{2}.

Since
\sum_{A}\mathds{1}_{A\cup B\sim H}\approx n^{v_{H}-v_{F}}
when
B\sim F
, and
\sum_{B\sim F}1\approx n^{v_{F}}
, we estimate

\displaystyle\mathrm{Var}[I_{F}]

\displaystyle\approx n^{v_{F}}\frac{P^{2}_{H}}{{\mathrm{Var}[Z_{H}^{n}]}}\frac{Q_{F}}{P_{F}}\left(n^{v_{H}-v_{F}}\right)^{2}=\frac{P_{H}^{2}}{\mathrm{Var}[Z_{H}^{n}]}n^{2v_{H}-v_{F}}\frac{Q_{F}}{P_{F}}\mathchar 59\relax

as required.

Let us turn our attention to the latter part of the assertion. For hypergraphs without isolated vertices, it follows from orthogonality of the variables
I_{F}
and the equality

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}F\subseteq H\\
e_{F}\geq 1\end{subarray}}\mathrm{Var}[I_{F}]=\mathrm{Var}[\widetilde{Z}_{H}^{n}]=1.

It remains to prove the assertion for
H
with isolated vertices. Creating such a hypergraph
H
by adding
k
vertices to
H^{\prime}
without isolated vertices, we simply have
Z_{H}^{n}={n-v_{H^{\prime}}\choose k}Z_{H^{\prime}}^{n}
and hence

\displaystyle\text{Var}[Z_{H}^{n}]

\displaystyle={n-v_{H^{\prime}}\choose k}^{2}\text{Var}[Z_{H^{\prime}}^{n}]\approx n^{2(v_{H}-v_{H^{\prime}})}P_{H^{\prime}}^{2}\max_{F\subseteq H^{\prime}:e_{F}\geq 1}n^{2v_{H^{\prime}}-v_{F}}\frac{Q_{F}}{P_{F}}

\displaystyle\approx P_{H}^{2}\max_{F\subseteq H\mathchar 59\relax e_{F}\geq 1}n^{2v_{H}-v_{F}}\frac{Q_{F}}{P_{F}}\mathchar 59\relax

where we used the fact that the last maximum is realised by a subhypergraph
F
without isolated vertices.
∎

Remark 2.4
.

Due to inhomogeneity of the model
\mathbb{H}(n\mathchar 59\relax\boldsymbol{p})
, the maximum in (
2.7
) is rather involved. One may understand it better by observing that if we add to a subhypergraph
F
an edge whose probability is greater than
1/2
, then the expression
n^{-v_{F}}\frac{Q_{F}}{P_{F}}
decreases. Thus, it is sufficient to consider subhypergraphs with minimal number of such edges, which leads to

\displaystyle\mathrm{Var}[Z_{H}^{n}]

\displaystyle\approx P_{H}^{2}n^{2v_{H}}\left(\max\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}F\subseteq H\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}\,e_{F}\geq 1\\
p_{|e|}\leq 1/2\text{ for }e\in E(F)\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{n^{v_{F}}P_{F}}+\max_{e\in E(H):\,p_{|e|}>1/2}\frac{1-p_{|e|}}{n^{|e|}}\right)

(2.8)

\displaystyle\approx P_{H}^{2}n^{2v_{H}}\left(\max\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}F\subseteq H\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}\,e_{F}\geq 1\\
p_{|e|}\leq 1/2\text{ for }e\in E(F)\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{n^{v_{F}}P_{F}}+\max_{e\in E(H)}\frac{1-p_{|e|}}{n^{|e|}}\right)\mathchar 59\relax

(2.9)

where in the last maximum one can include terms corresponding to
p_{|e|}\leq 1/2
, since they are dominated by the terms from the previous maximum.

Let us note that in
[
12

one can find an estimate of the variance of a somewhat different form

\mathrm{Var}[Z_{H}^{n}]\approx P_{H}^{2}\max_{F\subseteq H\mathchar 59\relax e_{F}\geq 1}n^{2v_{H}-v_{F}}\frac{1-P_{F}}{P_{F}}.

Nevertheless, both of them are equivalent, even though
1-P_{F}\approx\max_{e\in E(F)}\{1-p_{|e|}\}
is generally not comparable with
Q_{F}
.

We end this subsection with an observation that if the first sum in (
2.6
) is restricted to subhypergraphs of some

, then we obtain the variable
\widetilde{Z}^{n}_{H^{\prime}}

Proposition 2.5
.

For the decomposition (

) and for any

without isolated vertices we have

\sum_{F\subseteq H^{\prime}}I_{F}=\alpha_{n}(H^{\prime}\mathchar 59\relax H)\widetilde{Z}_{H^{\prime}}^{n}

with

\displaystyle\alpha_{n}(H^{\prime}\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}H)=C_{H^{\prime}}{n-v_{H^{\prime}}\choose v_{H}-v_{H^{\prime}}}\frac{P_{H}\sqrt{\mathrm{Var}[Z_{H^{\prime}}^{n}]}}{P_{H^{\prime}}\sqrt{\mathrm{Var}[Z_{H}^{n}]}}\mathchar 59\relax

where
C_{H^{\prime}}
denotes the number of hypergraphs isomorphic to
H
that might be built on
H^{\prime}
and some given
v_{H}-v_{H^{\prime}}
other vertices. Furthermore, the following estimate holds

\displaystyle\alpha_{n}(H^{\prime}\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}H)\approx\sqrt{\frac{\max\limits_{F\subseteq H^{\prime}\mathchar 59\relax e_{F}\geq 1}n^{-v_{F}}{Q_{F}}/{P_{F}}}{\max\limits_{F\subseteq H\mathchar 59\relax e_{F}\geq 1}n^{-v_{F}}{Q_{F}}/{P_{F}}}}.

Proof.

Let

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}A\subseteq[2^{n}-1]\\

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}A_{1}\subseteq[2^{n}-1]\\

\displaystyle\sum_{F\subseteq H^{\prime}}I_{F}

\displaystyle=\frac{P_{H}\sqrt{\mathrm{Var}[Z_{H^{\prime}}^{n}]}}{P_{H^{\prime}}\sqrt{\mathrm{Var}[Z_{H}^{n}]}}\sum_{F\subseteq H^{\prime}}\sum_{B\sim F}\frac{P_{H^{\prime}}}{\sqrt{\mathrm{Var}[Z_{H^{\prime}}^{n}]}}\prod_{b\in B}\left(\widetilde{X}_{b}\sqrt{\frac{1-p_{|e_{b}|}}{p_{|e_{b}|}}}\right)C_{H^{\prime}}{n-v_{H^{\prime}}\choose v_{H}-v_{H^{\prime}}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}A\subseteq[2^{n}-1]\\

\displaystyle=C_{H^{\prime}}{n-v_{H^{\prime}}\choose v_{H}-v_{H^{\prime}}}\frac{P_{H}\sqrt{\mathrm{Var}[Z_{H^{\prime}}^{n}]}}{P_{H^{\prime}}\sqrt{\mathrm{Var}[Z_{H}^{n}]}}\widetilde{Z}_{H^{\prime}}^{n}\mathchar 59\relax

as required. Next, by (

\displaystyle\alpha_{n}(H\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}H^{\prime})

\displaystyle\approx n^{v_{H}-v_{H^{\prime}}}\frac{P_{H}}{P_{H^{\prime}}}\sqrt{\frac{P_{H^{\prime}}^{2}\max\limits_{F\subseteq H^{\prime}\mathchar 59\relax e_{F}\geq 1}n^{2v_{H^{\prime}}-v_{F}}{Q_{F}}/{P_{F}}}{P_{H}^{2}\max\limits_{F\subseteq H\mathchar 59\relax e_{F}\geq 1}n^{2v_{H}-v_{F}}{Q_{F}}/{P_{F}}}}=\sqrt{\frac{\max\limits_{F\subseteq H^{\prime}\mathchar 59\relax e_{F}\geq 1}n^{-v_{F}}{Q_{F}}/{P_{F}}}{\max\limits_{F\subseteq H\mathchar 59\relax e_{F}\geq 1}n^{-v_{F}}{Q_{F}}/{P_{F}}}}\mathchar 59\relax

which ends the proof.

Main results

3.1
General case

A typical starting point in research on properties of random graphs is the threshold function. In our setting, the edge existence probability is a vector, and therefore we provide a threshold-like result in the following, a bit implicit, but very simple manner.

Proposition 3.1
.

For a fixed hypergraph
H
we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(Z_{H}^{n}>0)\overset{n\rightarrow\infty}{\longrightarrow}\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}1\mathchar 59\relax&\text{when }&\min\limits_{F\subseteq H:\,e_{F}\geq 1}\{n^{v_{F}}\hskip-5.0pt\prod\limits_{e\in E(F)}p_{|e|}\}\rightarrow\infty\mathchar 59\relax\\[10.0pt]
0\mathchar 59\relax&\text{when}&\min\limits_{F\subseteq H:\,e_{F}\geq 1}\{n^{v_{F}}\hskip-5.0pt\prod\limits_{e\in E(F)}p_{|e|}\}\rightarrow 0.\end{array}\right.

The above proposition is a slightly improved version of Theorem 3.2 in
[
12
]
. The first condition is unchanged and the latter one is obtained by Markov’s inequality applied as follows

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\left(Z_{H}^{n}>0\right)\leq\min\limits_{F\subseteq H:\,e_{F}\geq 1}\mathbf{P}\left(X_{F}>0\right){\leq}\min\limits_{F\subseteq H:\,e_{F}\geq 1}\mathbf{E}\left[X_{F}\right]\approx\min\limits_{F\subseteq H:\,e_{F}\geq 1}\{n^{v_{F}}\hskip-5.0pt\prod\limits_{e\in E(F)}p_{|e|}\}\mathchar 59\relax

(3.3)

where minima were simply added in comparison to the original proof in
[
12
]
.

Next, we turn our attention to the conditions of the asymptotic normality. Theorem
3.2
generalizes (
1.1
) as well as the result for uniform hypergraphs from
[
11
]
.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
H
be any hypergraph.
Then
\widetilde{Z}_{H}^{n}\overset{d}{\rightarrow}\mathcal{N}

holds if and only if

\min\limits_{F\subseteq H:\,e_{F}\geq 1}P_{F}\,n^{v_{F}}\rightarrow\infty

and

\forall_{e\in E(H)}\,\left({n^{|e|}(1-p_{|e|})}+\frac{\max_{F\subseteq H:\,e_{F}\geq 1}n^{-v_{F}}Q_{F}/P_{F}}{n^{-{|e|}}(1-p_{{|e|}})}\right){\rightarrow}\,\infty.

(3.4)

The last quotient of (
3.4
) looks a bit mysterious, so let us make some comments on it. First of all, it matters only for
p_{|e|}
close to
1
, since else
n^{|e|}(1-p_{|e|})\rightarrow\infty
and the whole expression in parentheses tends to
\infty
anyway. Thus, for
p_{|e|}>1/2
, we may estimate the said quotient by
\text{Var}[Z_{H}^{n}]/\text{Var}[X_{\{e\}}]
(see Proposition
2.3
), where
\{e\}
stands for the hypergraph consisting of the edge
e
and its vertices only. Additionally, since
\mathbf{E}[Z^{n}_{F}]{\approx}P_{F}n^{v_{F}}
for

, we may rewrite the conditions from Theorem
3.2
probabilistically as follows

\min\limits_{F\subseteq H:\,e_{F}\geq 1}\mathbf{E}[Z_{F}^{n}]\rightarrow\infty\ \ \text{ and }\ \ \forall_{e\in E(H)}\left[{n\choose|e|}-\mathbf{E}[Z_{\{e\}}^{n}]\right]+\frac{\text{Var}[Z_{H}^{n}]}{\text{Var}[Z_{\{e\}}^{n}]}\ {\longrightarrow}\ \infty.

This gives us some ideas of interpretation. By Proposition
3.1
, the first condition ensures at least one copy of
H
with high probability. The limit
\left[{n\choose|e|}-\mathbf{E}[Z_{\{e\}}^{n}]\right]\rightarrow\infty
means that the number of edges of size
|e|
that do not exist tends to infinity, so that the subhypergraph of
\mathbb{H}(n\mathchar 59\relax\boldsymbol{p})
consisting of the edges of that size is not to close to the deterministic complete
|e|
-uniform hypergraph. However, even in the case it is too close, we can hope for randomness coming from other edges, which may happen if
\text{Var}[Z_{\{e\}}^{n}]
has asymptotically no impact on
\text{Var}[Z_{H}^{n}]
.

Next, we establish bounds on the Wasserstein distance

\displaystyle d_{W}(\widetilde{Z}_{H}^{n}\mathchar 59\relax\mathcal{N}):=\sup_{\text{Lip}(h)\leq 1}\big|\mathbf{E}[h(\widetilde{Z}_{H}^{n})-h(\mathcal{N})]\big|\mathchar 59\relax

where
\text{Lip}(h)
denotes the Lipschitz constant of a function

, and the Kolmogorov distance

\displaystyle d_{K}(\widetilde{Z}_{H}^{n}\mathchar 59\relax\mathcal{N}):=\sup_{t\in\mathbf{R}}\big|\mathbf{P}\left(\widetilde{Z}_{H}^{n}\leq t\right)-\mathbf{P}(\mathcal{N}\leq t)]\big|

between
\widetilde{Z}_{H}^{n}
and a random variable
\mathcal{N}
with the standard normal distribution. In the sequel, we additionally use the notation

Theorem 3.3

For any hypergraph

d_{W}(\widetilde{Z}_{H}^{n}\mathchar 59\relax\mathcal{N})\lesssim\frac{1}{\left({{\min\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}F\subseteq H:\,e_{F}\geq 1\end{subarray}}{P_{F}}\,n^{v_{F}}}}\right)^{1/4}}+\sum_{e\in E(H)}\left(\frac{n^{-|e|}(1-p_{|e|})}{\max\limits_{F\subseteq H:\,e_{F}\geq 1}n^{-v_{F}}Q_{F}/P_{F}}\wedge\frac{1}{(1-p_{|e|})n^{|e|}}\right)^{1/2}

Additionally, if

and all

\displaystyle d_{W/K}(\widetilde{Z}_{H}^{n}\mathchar 59\relax\mathcal{N})\lesssim\frac{1}{\left({{\min\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}F\subseteq H:\,e_{F}\geq 1\end{subarray}}{P_{F}}\,n^{v_{F}}}}\right)^{1/2}}.

(3.7)

Remark 3.4
.

The first two bounds correspond to convergence conditions and have no limitations on probabilities

. On the other hand, we pay a price for this universality and for some ranges of parameters the rates are not optimal, which might be verified for instance in the case of
2

(cf. Theorem

3.2
Homogeneous case

In the case when

. First, to identify properly the quantities appearing below, let us observe that estimates (

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}[{Z}_{H}^{n}]

\displaystyle\approx p^{e_{H}}n^{v_{H}}\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}

The conditions for asymptotic normality of

Although the proofs are moved to subsequent sections, we would like to present here the proof of the implication

Proof of 1)

The first condition in

\displaystyle\widetilde{Z}_{H}^{n}=\frac{{Z}_{H}^{n}-\mathbf{E}[{Z}_{H}^{n}]}{\sqrt{\mathrm{Var}[{Z}_{H}^{n}]}}\geq-\frac{\mathbf{E}[{Z}_{H}^{n}]}{\sqrt{\mathrm{Var}[{Z}_{H}^{n}]}}.

Since

Similarly, in the case of the latter condition in

it suffices to focus on

. Let us denote by

the number of all hypergraphs isomorphic to

that do not occur in

where

that are isomorphic to

. Since

\displaystyle\frac{\mathbf{E}[{V}_{H}^{n}]}{\sqrt{\mathrm{Var}[{V}_{H}^{n}]}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathbf{E}\left[\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}H^{\prime}\subseteq K^{*}_{n}\\

\displaystyle\approx n^{v_{H}}(1-p)\sqrt{\frac{\min\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}F\subseteq H:\,e_{F}\geq 1\end{subarray}}n^{v_{F}}}{{{(1-p)n^{2v_{H}}}}}}=\sqrt{(1-p)n^{\min\{|e|:e\in E(H)\}}}\mathchar 59\relax

\displaystyle d_{W/K}(W\mathchar 59\relax\mathcal{N}(0\mathchar 59\relax 1))\leq C_{d}\sqrt{S_{1}+S_{2}+S_{3}}\mathchar 59\relax

\displaystyle=\sum_{0\leq l<i\leq d}\sum_{|A|=i-l}\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big(\sum_{|B|=l\mathchar 59\relax B\cap A=\emptyset}\mathbb{E}[W_{A\cup B}^{2}|\mathcal{F}_{A}]\Big)^{2}\Big]\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}

\displaystyle S_{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{1\leq l<i\leq d}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|A_{1}|=|A_{2}|=i-l\\

\displaystyle d_{W/K}(\widetilde{Z}_{H}^{n}\mathchar 59\relax\mathcal{N})

\displaystyle\lesssim\bigg({\min\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}F\subseteq H\\

\displaystyle a_{i}\approx\sqrt{\frac{1-p_{|e_{i}|}}{{\mathrm{Var}[Z_{H}^{n}]}}}\,n^{v_{H}-|e_{i}|}\mathchar 59\relax

\displaystyle\approx\frac{n_{k}^{-|e|}(1-p_{|e|})}{\max_{e^{\prime}\in E(\bar{H}^{1})}n_{k}^{-|e^{\prime}|}(1-p_{|e^{\prime}|})}(\alpha_{n_{k}}^{1})^{2}\longrightarrow\infty.

This ends the proof.
∎

7
Appendix

Surprisingly, the literature on bounds on distances between sums of random variables seems to be very limited. In this section we derive some inequalities that are crucial for the proofs of the main results of the article.

Proposition 7.1
.

For any random variables
X
and
Y
such that
\mathbf{E}[Y]=0
and
\mathbf{E}[Y^{2}]<\infty
, we have

\displaystyle d_{W}(X+Y\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}\,\mathcal{N})

\displaystyle\leq d_{W}\left(X\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}\,\mathcal{N}\right)+(\mathrm{Var}[Y])^{1/2}\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}

(7.1)

\displaystyle d_{K}(X+Y\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}\,\mathcal{N})

\displaystyle\leq d_{K}\left(X\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}\,\mathcal{N}\right)+\tfrac{4}{3}(\mathrm{Var}[Y])^{1/3}.

(7.2)

Proof.

Regarding the Wasserstein distance, we have

\displaystyle d_{W}(X+Y\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}\,\mathcal{N})

\displaystyle\leq d_{W}(X+Y\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}X)+d_{W}(X\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}\,\mathcal{N})

(7.3)

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{\text{Lip}(h)\leq 1}\mathbf{E}\left[\left|h(X+Y)-h(X)\right|\right]+d_{W}(X\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}\,\mathcal{N})

(7.4)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbf{E}\left[\left|Y\right|\right]+d_{W}(X\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}\,\mathcal{N})\leq(\mathrm{Var}[Y])^{1/2}+d_{W}(X\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}\,\mathcal{N})\mathchar 59\relax

(7.5)

as required. Let us turn our attention to the bound for the Kolmogorov distance.
For any
\varepsilon>0
we have

\displaystyle\big|\mathbf{P}(X+Y\leq t)-\mathbf{P}(\mathcal{N}\leq t)\big|

\displaystyle\leq\big|\mathbf{P}(X+Y\leq t\mathchar 59\relax|Y|\leq\varepsilon)-\mathbf{P}(\mathcal{N}\leq t)\big|+\mathbf{P}(|Y|>\varepsilon).

Since

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}(X+Y\leq t\mathchar 59\relax|Y|\leq\varepsilon)-\mathbf{P}(\mathcal{N}\leq t)

(7.6)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbf{P}(X-\varepsilon\leq t\mathchar 59\relax|Y|\leq\varepsilon)-\mathbf{P}(\mathcal{N}\leq t)

(7.7)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbf{P}(X-\varepsilon\leq t)-\mathbf{P}(\mathcal{N}\leq t)

(7.8)

\displaystyle=\big(\mathbf{P}(X\leq t+\varepsilon)-\mathbf{P}(\mathcal{N}\leq t+\varepsilon)\big)+\big(\mathbf{P}(\mathcal{N}\leq t+\varepsilon)-\mathbf{P}(\mathcal{N}\leq t)\big)

(7.9)

\displaystyle\leq d_{K}\left(X\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}\,\mathcal{N}\right)+\frac{\varepsilon}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\mathchar 59\relax

(7.10)

and, similarly,

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}(X+Y\leq t\mathchar 59\relax|Y|\leq\varepsilon)-\mathbf{P}(\mathcal{N}\leq t)

(7.11)

\displaystyle\geq\mathbf{P}(X+\varepsilon\leq t\mathchar 59\relax|Y|\leq\varepsilon)-\mathbf{P}(\mathcal{N}\leq t)

(7.12)

\displaystyle\geq\mathbf{P}(X+\varepsilon\leq t)-\mathbf{P}\left(|Y|>\varepsilon\right)-\mathbf{P}(\mathcal{N}\leq t)

(7.13)

\displaystyle=\big(\mathbf{P}(X\leq t-\varepsilon)-\mathbf{P}(\mathcal{N}\leq t-\varepsilon)\big)-\mathbf{P}\left(|Y|>\varepsilon\right)-\big(\mathbf{P}(\mathcal{N}\leq t)-\mathbf{P}(\mathcal{N}\leq t-\varepsilon)\big)

(7.14)

\displaystyle\geq-d_{K}\left(X\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}\,\mathcal{N}\right)-\mathbf{P}\left(|Y|>\varepsilon\right)-\frac{\varepsilon}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\mathchar 59\relax

(7.15)

we have

\displaystyle d_{K}(X+Y\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}\,\mathcal{N})

\displaystyle\leq d_{K}(X\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}\,\mathcal{N})+2\mathbf{P}\left(|Y|>\varepsilon\right)+\frac{\varepsilon}{\sqrt{2\pi}}

\displaystyle\leq d_{K}(X\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}\,\mathcal{N})+2\frac{\mathrm{Var}[Y]}{\varepsilon^{2}}+\frac{\varepsilon}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\mathchar 59\relax

were we applied Chebyshev’s inequality in the last inequality. Due to arbitrariness of
\varepsilon
and the fact
\min\{\frac{a}{\varepsilon^{2}}+\varepsilon:\varepsilon>0\}=3(a/4)^{1/3}
,
a>0
, we get

\displaystyle d_{K}(X+Y\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}\,\mathcal{N})

\displaystyle\leq d_{K}(X\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}\,\mathcal{N})+\frac{3\left(\mathrm{Var}[Y]\right)^{1/3}}{\left(4\pi\right)^{1/3}}\leq d_{K}(X\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}\,\mathcal{N})+\tfrac{4}{3}\left(\mathrm{Var}[Y]\right)^{1/3}\mathchar 59\relax

which ends the proof.
∎

Remark 7.2
.

Let us show that the power
\tfrac{1}{3}
in (
7.2
) is optimal. Namely, for

X\sim\mathcal{N}(0\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}1)
and
\varepsilon>0
define

Y=-\varepsilon\mathbf{1}_{\{X\in(0\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}\varepsilon)\}}+\varepsilon\mathbf{1}_{\{X\in(-\varepsilon-\delta\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}-\varepsilon)\}}\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}

where
\delta>0
is chosen such that
\mathbf{P}\left(X\in(0\mathchar 59\relax\varepsilon)\right)=\mathbf{P}\left(X\in(-\varepsilon-\delta\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}-\varepsilon)\right)
.
Then
\mathbf{E}\left[Y\right]=0
and

\text{Var}[Y]=2\varepsilon^{2}\mathbf{P}\left(X\in(0\mathchar 59\relax\varepsilon)\right)\leq\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\varepsilon^{3}.

Noting additionally that
d_{K}(X\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}\,\mathcal{N})=0
, we conclude

\displaystyle d_{K}\left(X+Y\mathchar 59\relax\mathcal{N}\right)

\displaystyle\geq|\mathbf{P}\left(X+Y\leq 0\right)-\mathbf{P}\left(\mathcal{N}\leq 0\right)|=\mathbf{P}\left(X]leq\varepsilon\right)-\mathbf{P}\left(\mathcal{N}\leq 0\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbf{P}\left(X\in(0\mathchar 59\relax\varepsilon)\right)\geq\varepsilon\frac{e^{-\varepsilon^{2}/2}}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\geq\frac{e^{-\varepsilon^{2}/2}}{{(4\pi)^{1/3}}}\left(\text{Var}[Y]\right)^{1/3}.

Since additionally
d_{K}(X\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}\,\mathcal{N})=0
, one can see that
\left(\text{Var}[Y]\right)^{1/3}
is of optimal order for
\text{Var}[Y]
close to zero.

Corollary 7.3
.

For uncorrelated random variables
X
and
Y
such that
\mathbf{E}[X]=\mathbf{E}[Y]=0
and
\mathrm{Var}[X]+\mathrm{Var}[Y]=1
, we have

\displaystyle d_{W}(X+Y\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}\,\mathcal{N})

\displaystyle\leq d_{W}\left(\frac{X}{\sqrt{\mathrm{Var}[X]}}\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}\,\mathcal{N}\right)+2(\mathrm{Var}[Y])^{1/2}\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}

(7.16)

\displaystyle d_{K}(X+Y\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}\,\mathcal{N})

\displaystyle\leq d_{K}\left(\frac{X}{\sqrt{\mathrm{Var}[X]}}\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}\,\mathcal{N}\right)+3(\mathrm{Var}[Y])^{1/3}.

(7.17)

Proof.

We present only the proof for Kolmogorov distance, as the one for the Wasserstein distance is analogous and even slightly simpler. From the previous proposition we get

\displaystyle d_{K}(X\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}\,\mathcal{N})

\displaystyle=d_{K}\left(\frac{X}{\sqrt{\mathrm{Var}[X]}}-\left(1-\sqrt{\mathrm{Var}[X]}\right)\frac{X}{\sqrt{\mathrm{Var}[X]}}\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}\,\mathcal{N}\right)

(7.18)

\displaystyle\leq d_{K}\left(\frac{X}{\sqrt{\mathrm{Var}[X]}}\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}\,\mathcal{N}\right)+\frac{4}{3}\left(1-\sqrt{\mathrm{Var}[X]}\right)^{2/3}

(7.19)

\displaystyle=d_{K}\left(\frac{X}{\sqrt{\mathrm{Var}[X]}}\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}\,\mathcal{N}\right)+\frac{4}{3}\left(\frac{{\mathrm{Var}[Y]}}{1+\sqrt{\mathrm{Var}[X]}}\right)^{2/3}

(7.20)

\displaystyle\leq d_{K}\left(\frac{X}{\sqrt{\mathrm{Var}[X]}}\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}\,\mathcal{N}\right)+\frac{4}{3}\left({{\mathrm{Var}[Y]}}\right)^{1/3}\mathchar 59\relax

(7.21)

where the last inequality follows from the assumption
\mathrm{Var}[Y]=1-\mathrm{Var}[X]\leq 1
. Applying this to (
7.2
) we obtain the assertion.
∎

Proposition 7.4
.

Let
(X_{i})_{i=1}^{n}
be a collection of centred independent random variables satisfying
\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathrm{Var}[X_{i}]=1
. Then

\displaystyle d_{W}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}\,\mathcal{N}\right)

\displaystyle\leq 2\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left[\left({\mathrm{Var}[X_{i}]}\right)^{1/2}\wedge d_{W}\left(\frac{X_{i}}{\sqrt{\mathrm{Var}[X_{i}]}}\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}\,\mathcal{N}\right)\right]\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}

\displaystyle d_{K}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}\,\mathcal{N}\right)

\displaystyle\leq 3\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left[\left({\mathrm{Var}[X_{i}]}\right)^{1/3}\wedge d_{K}\left(\frac{X_{i}}{\sqrt{\mathrm{Var}[X_{i}]}}\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}\,\mathcal{N}\right)\right].

Proof.

First, let us observe that it suffices to assume that all
X_{i}
’s are non-degenerate.

Consider the case
n=2
.
For a random variable
X
, let us denote its distribution by
\mu_{X}
. Furthermore, let
\mathcal{A}
be a family of Borel functions
h:\mathbf{R}\rightarrow\mathbf{R}
that is closed for operations
h(\cdot)\rightarrow h(a\,(\cdot))
and
h(\cdot)\rightarrow h((\cdot))+b
for any
a\in(0\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}1)\mathchar 59\relax b\in\mathbf{R}
. Then, for
\mathcal{N}_{1}\sim\mathcal{N}(0\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}{\text{Var}[X_{i}]})
,
i=1\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}2
, independent of each other and of
X_{1}\mathchar 59\relax X_{2}
, we have

\displaystyle\sup_{h\in\mathcal{A}}\left|\mathbf{E}\left[h(X_{1}+X_{2})-h(\mathcal{N})\right]\right|=\sup_{h\in\mathcal{A}}\left|\mathbf{E}\left[h(X_{1}+X_{2})-h(\mathcal{N}_{1}+\mathcal{N}_{2})\right]\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{h\in\mathcal{A}}\left|\mathbf{E}\left[h(X_{1}+X_{2})-h(X_{1}+\mathcal{N}_{2})\right]\right|+\sup_{h\in\mathcal{A}}\left|\mathbf{E}\left[h(X_{1}+\mathcal{N}_{2})-h(\mathcal{N}_{1}+\mathcal{N}_{2})\right]\right|

\displaystyle\leq\mathbf{E}\bigg[\sup_{h\in\mathcal{A}}\left|\mathbf{E}\Big[h(X_{1}+X_{2})-h(X_{1}+\mathcal{N}_{2})\big|X_{1}\Big]\right|\bigg]

\displaystyle\ \ \ +\mathbf{E}\bigg[\sup_{h\in\mathcal{A}}\left|\mathbf{E}\Big[h(X_{1}+\mathcal{N}_{2})-h(N_{1}+\mathcal{N}_{2})\big|\mathcal{N}_{2}\Big]\right|\bigg]

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{2}\sup_{h\in\mathcal{A}}\left|\mathbf{E}\left[h\left({X_{i}}\right)-h\left(\mathcal{N}_{i}\right)\right]\right|

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{2}\sup_{h\in\mathcal{A}}\left|\mathbf{E}\left[h\left(\sqrt{\mathrm{Var}[{X_{i}}]}\frac{X_{i}}{\sqrt{\mathrm{Var}[{X_{i}}]}}\right)-h\left(\sqrt{\mathrm{Var}[{X_{i}}]}\,\mathcal{N}\right)\right]\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{2}\sup_{h\in\mathcal{A}}\left|\mathbf{E}\left[h\left(\frac{X_{i}}{\sqrt{\mathrm{Var}[{X_{i}}]}}\right)-h\left(\mathcal{N}\right)\right]\right|.

Taking
\{h\in\mathbf{R}^{\mathbf{R}}:\text{Lip(1)}\leq 1\}
or
\{(-\infty\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}t):t\in\mathbf{R}\}
as
\mathcal{A}
, we arrive at

\displaystyle d_{W}(X_{1}+X_{2}\mathchar 59\relax\mathcal{N})

\displaystyle\leq d_{W}\left(\frac{X_{1}}{\sqrt{\mathrm{Var}[{X_{1}}]}}\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}\,\mathcal{N}\right)+d_{W}\left(\frac{X_{2}}{\sqrt{\mathrm{Var}[{X_{2}}]}}\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}\,\mathcal{N}\right)\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}

(7.22)

\displaystyle d_{K}(X_{1}+X_{2}\mathchar 59\relax\mathcal{N})

\displaystyle\leq d_{K}\left(\frac{X_{1}}{\sqrt{\mathrm{Var}[{X_{1}}]}}\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}\,\mathcal{N}\right)+d_{K}\left(\frac{X_{2}}{\sqrt{\mathrm{Var}[{X_{2}}]}}\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}\,\mathcal{N}\right).

(7.23)

From this point the proofs for both of the distances are analogous, we therefore focus only on the Kolmogorov distance. Combining (
7.23
) with (
7.17
) we get

\displaystyle d_{K}(X_{1}+X_{2}\mathchar 59\relax\mathcal{N})

\displaystyle\leq d_{K}\left(\frac{X_{1}}{\sqrt{\mathrm{Var}[{X_{1}}]}}\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}\,\mathcal{N}\right)+3\left[\left({\mathrm{Var}[X_{2}]}\right)^{1/3}\wedge d_{K}\left(\frac{X_{2}}{\sqrt{\mathrm{Var}[{X_{2}}]}}\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}\,\mathcal{N}\right)\right].

(7.24)

Since we can clearly exchange
X_{1}
with
X_{2}
, we complete the proof in this case (
n=2
) with

d_{K}(X_{1}+X_{2}\mathchar 59\relax\mathcal{N})\leq 1=\mathrm{Var}[{X_{1}}]+\mathrm{Var}[{X_{2}}]\leq 3\left[\left(\mathrm{Var}[{X_{1}}]\right)^{1/3}+\left(\mathrm{Var}[{X_{2}}]\right)^{1/3}\right].

In the case

it is enough to iterate the bound (
7.24
) until obtaining

\displaystyle d_{K}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}\mathchar 59\relax\mathcal{N}\right)

\displaystyle\leq d_{K}\left(\frac{X_{1}+X_{2}}{\sqrt{\mathrm{Var}[{X_{1}+X_{2}}]}}\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}\,\mathcal{N}\right)+3\sum_{i=3}^{n}\left[\left({\mathrm{Var}[X_{i}]}\right)^{1/3}\wedge d_{K}\left(\frac{X_{i}}{\sqrt{\mathrm{Var}[{X_{i}}]}}\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}\,\mathcal{N}\right)\right]\mathchar 59\relax

and then apply the already proven bound for

Spin Statistics and Surgeries of Topological Solitons in QCD Matter in Magnetic Field
Yuki Amari, Muneto Nitta, and Ryo Yokokura

Abstract
The ground state of QCD with two flavors (up and down quarks) at finite baryon density in sufficiently strong magnetic field is in a form of either a chiral soliton lattice(CSL), an array of solitons stacked along the magnetic field, or a domain-wall Skyrmion phase in which Skyrmions are spontaneously created on top of the CSL. In the latter, one 2D (baby) Skyrmion in the chiral soliton corresponds to two 3D Skyrmions (baryons) in the bulk. In this paper, we study spin statistics of topological solitons by using the following two methods: the conventional Witten’s method by embedding the pion fields of two flavors into those of three flavors with the Wess-Zumino-Witten (WZW) term, and a more direct method by using the two-flavor WZW term written in terms of a spin structure. We find that a chiral soliton of finite quantized size called a pancake soliton and a hole on a chiral soliton are fermions or bosons depending on odd or even quantizations of their surface areas, respectively, and a domain-wall Skyrmion is a boson. We also propose surgeries of topological solitons: a domain-wall Skyrmion (boson) can be cut into a pancake soliton (fermion) and a hole (fermion), and a chiral soliton without Skyrmions can be cut into a pancake soliton (fermion) and a hole (fermion).

Introduction

Spin statistics says that a wave function
or a Hilbert space
is invariant under exchanges of two bosons
while it acquires a minus sign under exchange of two fermions.
Bosons and fermions obey Bose-Einstein and Fermi-Dirac statistics, respectively.
Anyons can exist in lower dimensions.
In the case of elementary particles or their composites,
their statistics are a priori given

However, once one considers solitons, the problem becomes quite subtle.

5
Spin statistics of topological solitons

In this section, we discuss the
spin statistics of the pancake solitons, holes
and domain-wall Skyrmions.
The statistics can be found by the responses
of the effective action under a

2\pi
rotation of the solitons.
We will show that
the pancake solitons and holes
with the minimal winding numbers
are fermions,
while the domain-wall Skyrmions
are bosons.

There are at least two ways to see the statistics of the solitons.
One is the method developed by Witten
[
4
]

(see also Ref.
[
12
]
).
Another is to see the
coupling of the baryon current and
the spin structure, which
was recently discussed by Lee, Ohmori, and Tachikawa
[
13
]
.
Here, we will discuss both methods.

5.1
Witten’s method

In this method, we embed the configuration of
\text{SU}(2)
-valued
pion field to
\text{SU}(3)
-valued one,
and evaluate the WZW term of the fifth order of the pion field.
In this procedure, we can relate the
spatial rotation with the rotation in
the internal
\text{SU}(2)
flavor rotation.
The
2\pi
rotation of the solitons in the spacetime corresponds to the rotation by

-\bm{1}_{2}
of the flavor
\text{SU}(2)
.
Since
\pi_{4}(\text{SU}(2))=\mathbb{Z}_{2}
,
the
\text{SU}(2)
transformation can have the
winding in the spacetime where
the transformation cannot be homotopically
equivalent to the identity.
We can efficiently calculate the
response of the effective action under
the homotopically non-trivial
\text{SU}(2)

transformation using the embedding
of
\text{SU}(2)
to
\text{SU}(3)
,
where the homotopically non-trivial
\text{SU}(2)

transformation can be expressed by
homotopically trivial transformation in
\text{SU}(3)
.
It has been found that
the fifth order of the pion field
in the WZW term is relevant to the response
of the effective action.

In the following, we will calculate
the response
in the case of the pancake solitons.
We first embed the
\text{SU}(2)
-valued
pion field
\Sigma\in\text{SU}(2)
to
U\in\text{SU}(3)
,

U(\bm{x})=\left(\begin{matrix}\Sigma(\bm{x})&\\
&1\end{matrix}\right),

(61)

where
\Sigma(\bm{x})
is given by
eqs. (
51

54

Since the spin statistics can be regarded as a response under

2\pi
rotation in the space, we consider the spatial rotation
along
\varphi
direction with an angle
\alpha

as
\varphi\to\varphi-\alpha
.
The spatial rotation can be expressed
in terms of the flavor
\text{SU}(2)

U(\bm{x})\to\left(\begin{matrix}e^{i\alpha/2}&0&0\\
0&e^{-i\alpha/2}&0\\
0&0&1\end{matrix}\right)U(\bm{x})\left(\begin{matrix}e^{-i\alpha/2}&0&0\\
0&e^{i\alpha/2}&0\\
0&0&1\end{matrix}\right),

(62)

where we have used
\phi=\nu\varphi

We can immediately see that

the transformation by
-\bm{1}_{2}
in
\text{SU}(2)

Therefore, the
\text{SU}(2)

We can resolve the problem

satisfying

.
Now, we rotate the soliton with the time coordinate

\alpha=t
,
and discuss the response of the effective action
with the pancake soliton.
The non-trivial response comes from the 5-dimensional term in the WZW

\Gamma_{5}=\frac{iN_{\rm C}}{240\pi^{2}}\int d^{5}x\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma\tau}\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(\tilde{L}_{\mu}\tilde{L}_{\nu}\tilde{L}_{\rho}\tilde{L}_{\sigma}\tilde{L}_{\tau}).

To construct

We regard the time direction and the fifth direction as a disc,
where the radial and

\xi

Therefore, our four-dimensional spacetime

\xi=1

We take the configuration of

Hole soliton.

5.2
Lee-Ohmori-Tachikawa’s method

Another method to find the spin statistics
is to see the coupling of the soliton charge
and the spin structure.
6
6

6

The authors thank Kantaro Ohmori for discussions on this method.

Here, the spin structure
is roughly the

\mathbb{Z}_{2}
center sector of the
{\rm Spin}(3,1)
spin connection 1-form for the spacetime rotational symmetry.
In quantum field theories, we can understand the spin structure as
an assignment of the periodic or anti-periodic boundary conditions for
the fermions when we compactify one of the spacetime directions to
S^{1}
.
Under the
2\pi
rotation along the
S^{1}
,
the fermions can receive the
\pm 1
phase,
and the spin structure specifies the
phase.
Since the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
sector only changes
the phase of fermions but does not change
that of bosons, we can distinguish the spin statistics using the spin structure.

In practice,
we can distinguish
the spin statistics of the solitons by the response under the change of the spin structure,
i.e., the change of the boundary conditions along
S^{1}
direction.
The change of the boundary condition can be described by
a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
1-form gauge field
\eta
,

\displaystyle\oint_{S^{1}}\eta\in\pi{\mathbb{Z}},

(73)

which can be understood as an Aharonov-Bohm phase associated with the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
sector of

{\rm Spin}(3,1)
connection.

It has been recently found that
there should be a coupling between
the spin structure and the Skyrmion current
in the the WZW term for two flavors
N_{\rm F}=2
in order to match the non-perturbative anomaly of the global
\text{SU}(2)_{L}
symmetry
[
13
]
.
The explicit form of the
effective action is

e^{iS^{(2)}_{\rm WZW}[\mathfrak{s}]}=(-1)^{N_{\rm C}[\Sigma:M_{4}\to{\rm SU}(2)]},

(74)

where
[\Sigma:M_{4}\to{\rm SU}(2)]
is an equivalence class
specified by the reduced bordism group
\tilde{\Omega}^{\rm spin}_{4}({\rm SU}(2))=\mathbb{Z}_{2}
.
While the effective action may be abstract,
we can express the effective action in the point particle limit of the Skyrmion using the holonomy associated with the spin connection of the spacetime.
We introduce a holonomy of
{\rm Spin}(3,1)
gauge field,

{\rm hol}(\mathfrak{s},{\cal C}):={\rm P}\exp\left(i\int_{\cal C}\frac{1}{2!}\omega_{\mu}{}^{ab}\Sigma_{ab}dx^{\mu}\right).

(75)

Here,

\mathfrak{s}
in the left-hand side specifies the
configuration of the link variable given by the spin connection,

\omega_{\mu}{}^{ab}

is the spin connection of the spacetime given by
the vielbein
e_{\mu}{}^{a}

with the local Lorentz indices
a,b,...=0,1,2,3
,
the symbol
\Sigma_{ab}
is the generator of
{\rm Spin}(3,1)
,

{\cal C}
denotes a closed worldline of the Skyrmion,
and
“
{\rm P}
” represents the path ordered product.
In the flat spacetime, the holonomy can be reduced to

\mathbb{Z}_{2}\subset{\rm Spin}(3,1)
rather than the identity,

{\rm hol}(\mathfrak{s},{\cal C}):={\rm P}\exp\left(i\int_{\cal C}\frac{1}{2!}\omega_{\mu}{}^{ab}\Sigma_{ab}dx^{\mu}\right)=(-1)^{\epsilon({\cal C})}\bm{1}_{{\rm Spin}(3,1)},

(76)

with
\epsilon({\cal C})=0,1
.
Now, we can have a WZW
action for the Skyrmion
using the holonomy on
{\cal C}

associated with
{\rm Spin}(3,1)
as

e^{iS^{(2)}_{\rm WZW}[\mathfrak{s}]}=(-1)^{N_{\rm B}N_{\rm C}\epsilon({\cal C})}.

(77)

The multiplication by
N_{\rm B}

comes from the baryon number specified by
{\rm SU}(2)
,
and that by
N_{\rm C}
comes from the
number of quarks, which is the same as the conventional WZW term.
This effective action means that the Skyrmion
has a spin, and its statistics can be classified by the color
N_{\rm C}
as well as the baryon number.

We can see the spin statistics of the Skyrmion
by the change of the configuration of the
link variable
\mathfrak{s}
.
By changing
\epsilon({\cal C})

by
Aharonov-Bohm phase of
{\rm Spin}(3,1)
,
the effective action is changed as

e^{iS^{(2)}_{\rm WZW}[\mathfrak{s}]}\to e^{iN_{\rm B}N_{\rm C}\int_{\cal C}\eta}e^{iS^{(2)}_{\rm WZW}[\mathfrak{s}]}.

(78)

Since the response
e^{iN_{\rm B}N_{\rm C}\int_{\cal C}\eta}

is Abelian, we can now recover the
configuration of the Skyrmion from
the point-like one to a general one as

e^{iS^{(2)}_{\rm WZW}[\mathfrak{s}]}\to\exp\left(iN_{\rm C}\int_{M_{4}}\eta\wedge\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}\cdot 3!}\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}[(\Sigma d\Sigma^{\dagger})^{3}]\right)e^{iS^{(2)}_{\rm WZW}[\mathfrak{s}]}.

(79)

This response clearly shows that
the Skyrmions with the odd Skyrmion number
are fermions when
N_{\rm C}
is odd.
To see this,
we compactify the time direction to

S^{1}
, i.e.,

M_{4}=S^{1}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}
,
take
\eta
as

\int_{S^{1}}\eta=\pi,

(80)

and consider the response of the effective action,

\begin{split}&\exp\left(iN_{\rm C}\int_{M_{4}}\eta\wedge\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}\cdot 3!}\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}[(\Sigma d\Sigma^{\dagger})^{3}]\right)\\
&=\exp\left(iN_{\rm C}\int_{S^{1}}\eta\cdot\int_{M_{3}}{\rm tr}[(\Sigma d\Sigma^{\dagger})^{3}]\right)\\

(81)

By substituting the
configuration of
the pancake soliton,
hole soliton, and
domain-wall Skyrmion,
we find that
the pancake soliton
and the hole soliton with
odd winding numbers are
fermions,
and the others are bosons.

One comment is in order.
From the effective action
with the spin structure,
we have the coupling between
the spin structure and

Summary and Discussion

In this paper, we have clarified the spin statistics of

\text{SU}(3)

Then, we have clarified spin statistics
of topological solitons:

are fermions
with baryon number
N_{\rm B}=1

We also have constructed the low-energy effective theory

obtained

to confirm that
domain-wall Skyrmions are bosons.
We also have proposed surgeries of topological solitons.

Appendix A
Calculation of the spin statics of pancake soliton

In this appendix, we examine the statistics of pancake soliton by evaluating rather the WZW action that Witten used for the discussion of the statistics of Skyrmions
[
4
,
18
]
than the
N_{\mathrm{F}}=2
WZW term (
79
).
For this purpose, we apply the method described in Ref.
[
98
]
.
As Witten did, we extend the space-time
M_{4}=S^{1}\times M_{3}
to
M_{5}=D_{2}\times M_{3}
, where
M_{3}
is the physical space and
D_{2}
stands for a two-dimensional disc parameterized by (normalized) time
t\in[0,2\pi]
as an axis variable and
\xi\in[0,1]
as a radial variable.
We consider an
\text{SU}(3)
chiral field on the five dimensional space-time
M_{5}
defined in eq. (
66
), i.e.,
\tilde{U}(\bm{x},t,\xi)=V(t,\xi)U(\bm{x})V^{\dagger}(t,\xi)
,
where
U(\bm{x})
stands for a static Skyrmion field and
V
is a collective coordinate defined on
D_{2}
. The variable
V
is assumed to satisfy the boundary condition
V(0,\xi)=V(2\pi,\xi)
.
The left invariant Maurer-Cartan form of
\tilde{U}
can be written as

\displaystyle\tilde{L}

\displaystyle=\tilde{U}d\tilde{U}^{\dagger}=V\left(U\Omega U^{\dagger}+L-\Omega\right)V^{\dagger}

(86)

where
L=UdU^{\dagger},
and
\Omega=V^{\dagger}dV
.
Using this, we obtain

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tr}\left(\tilde{L}^{5}\right)=

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tr}\left(L^{5}\right)

(87)

\displaystyle+5d\operatorname{Tr}\left[L^{3}\Omega-\frac{1}{2}(L\Omega)^{2}+L\Omega^{3}\right.

\displaystyle+U(L-\Omega)^{3}U^{\dagger}\Omega\left.+\frac{1}{2}\left(U(L-\Omega)U^{\dagger}\Omega\right)^{2}+U(L-\Omega)U^{\dagger}\Omega^{3}\right]

where we also employed

\displaystyle dL=-L^{2},\qquad d\Omega=-\Omega^{2},\qquad dU=-LU,\qquad dU^{\dagger}=U^{\dagger}L.

(88)

It implies that the WZW term can be written as

\displaystyle\Gamma_{5}\equiv\frac{iN_{\rm C}}{240\pi^{2}}\int_{M_{5}}\operatorname{Tr}\left(\tilde{L}^{5}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{iN_{\rm C}}{240\pi^{2}}\int_{M_{5}}\operatorname{Tr}\left(L^{5}\right)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{iN_{\rm C}}{48\pi^{2}}\int_{M_{4}}\operatorname{Tr}\left[L^{3}\Omega-\frac{1}{2}(L\Omega)^{2}+L\Omega^{3}\right.

(89)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\left.+U^{\dagger}(L-\Omega)^{3}U\Omega\left.+\frac{1}{2}\left(U^{\dagger}(L-\Omega)U\Omega\right)^{2}+U^{\dagger}(L-\Omega)U\Omega^{3}\right]\right|_{\xi=1}.

Taking space-time dependency of
L
and
\Omega
into account, i.e.,
L^{4}=\Omega^{2}|_{\xi=1}=0
, this can be simplified as

\displaystyle\Gamma_{5}

\displaystyle=\frac{iN_{\rm C}}{48\pi^{2}}\int_{M_{4}}\operatorname{Tr}\left(L^{3}\Omega+U^{\dagger}L^{3}U\Omega\right)=\frac{iN_{\rm C}}{48\pi^{2}}\int_{M_{4}}\operatorname{Tr}\left[(L^{3}+R^{3})\Omega\right]

(90)

where
R=dU^{\dagger}U
.
Expanding
\Omega_{0}
in terms of the Gell-Mann matrices
\lambda_{\alpha}
(
\alpha=1,2,...,8
) as

\Omega_{0}=V^{\dagger}\partial_{0}V=\frac{\lambda_{\alpha}}{2}\operatorname{Tr}\left(\lambda_{\alpha}V^{\dagger}\partial_{0}V\right)=\frac{i}{2}\lambda_{\alpha}\omega^{\alpha}\ ,

(91)

we obtain

\Gamma_{5}=+\frac{N_{\rm C}}{96\pi^{2}}\int d^{4}x~\varepsilon^{ijk}\operatorname{Tr}\left[\left(L_{i}L_{j}L_{k}+R_{i}R_{j}R_{k}\right)\lambda_{\alpha}\right]\omega^{\alpha}

where we used
dx^{\mu}\wedge dx^{\nu}\wedge dx^{\alpha}\wedge dx^{\beta}=dx^{4}\varepsilon^{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}
.
In order to evaluate the WZW term for
\text{SU}(2)
solitons, we consider the
\text{SU}(3)
chiral field into which the
\text{SU}(2)
chiral field
\Sigma
is trivially embedded as defined in eq. (
61
).
Then, the Maurer-Cartan forms can simply be written as

L=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\Sigma d\Sigma^{\dagger}&0\\
0&0\end{array}\right),\qquad R=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}d\Sigma^{\dagger}\Sigma&0\\
0&0\end{array}\right)\ .

We decompose their elements in terms of the Pauli matrices
\tau_{a}
as

\displaystyle\Sigma\partial_{i}\Sigma^{\dagger}=\frac{\tau_{a}}{2}\operatorname{Tr}\left(\tau_{a}\Sigma\partial_{i}\Sigma^{\dagger}\right)\equiv\frac{i}{2}\tau_{a}\xi_{i}^{a}\ ,

(92)

\displaystyle\partial_{i}\Sigma^{\dagger}\Sigma=\frac{\tau_{a}}{2}\operatorname{Tr}\left(\tau_{a}\partial_{i}\Sigma^{\dagger}\Sigma\right)\equiv\frac{i}{2}\tau_{a}\zeta_{i}^{a}\ .

Using the decomposition, one obtains after simple algebra that

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{ijk}\Sigma\partial_{i}\Sigma^{\dagger}\Sigma\partial_{j}\Sigma^{\dagger}\Sigma\partial_{k}\Sigma^{\dagger}=\frac{1}{8}\varepsilon^{ijk}\varepsilon_{abc}\xi_{i}^{a}\xi_{j}^{b}\xi_{k}^{c}\mathbf{1}_{2}\ ,

(93)

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{ijk}\partial_{i}\Sigma^{\dagger}\Sigma\partial_{i}\Sigma^{\dagger}\Sigma\partial_{k}\Sigma^{\dagger}\Sigma=\frac{1}{8}\varepsilon^{ijk}\varepsilon_{abc}\zeta_{i}^{a}\zeta_{j}^{b}\zeta_{k}^{c}\mathbf{1}_{2}\ ,

where
\mathbf{1}_{2}
is the
2\times 2
unit matrix.
It indicates that the baryon number
N_{\rm B}\in\pi_{3}[\text{SU}(2)]
defined in eq. (
14
) can be cast into the form

\displaystyle N_{\rm B}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{96\pi^{2}}\int d^{3}x~\varepsilon^{ijk}\varepsilon_{abc}\xi_{i}^{a}\xi_{j}^{b}\xi_{k}^{c}=\frac{1}{96\pi^{2}}\int d^{3}x~\varepsilon^{ijk}\varepsilon_{abc}\zeta_{i}^{a}\zeta_{j}^{b}\zeta_{k}^{c}.

(94)

Consequently, we find that the WZW term can be written as

\displaystyle\Gamma_{5}

\displaystyle=\frac{N_{\rm C}}{96\pi^{2}}\int d^{4}x~\frac{1}{8}\varepsilon^{ijk}\varepsilon_{abc}\left(\xi_{i}^{a}\xi_{j}^{b}\xi_{k}^{c}+\zeta_{i}^{a}\zeta_{j}^{b}\zeta_{k}^{c}\right)\operatorname{Tr}\left(h\lambda_{\alpha}\right)\omega^{\alpha}

\displaystyle=\frac{N_{\rm C}N_{\rm B}}{4}\int dx^{0}\operatorname{Tr}\left(h\lambda_{\alpha}\right)\omega^{\alpha}

\displaystyle=\frac{N_{\rm C}N_{\rm B}}{2\sqrt{3}}\int dx^{0}\omega^{8}

(95)

where
h=\operatorname{diag}(1,1,0)
.

Let us evaluate the WZW term using the explicit form of
V
given in eq. (
67
).
Since
\Omega_{0}
is given by

\Omega_{0}|_{\xi=1}=V^{\dagger}\partial_{t}V|_{\xi=1}=\operatorname{diag}(0,-i,i)\ ,

(96)

we obtain

\omega^{8}=-i\operatorname{Tr}(\lambda_{8}\Omega_{0})|_{\xi=1}=-\sqrt{3}.

(97)

Therefore, we find that the WZW term takes

S_{\text{WZW}}=-\pi N_{\rm C}N_{\rm B}\ .

(98)

Let us explicitly calculate the WZW term of the pancake solitons. With the parameterization of the
\text{SU}(2)
chiral field (
55
), the
\text{SU}(3)
chiral field for pancake solitons is given by

U(\bm{x})=\left(\begin{matrix}\sigma+i\pi^{0}&i(\pi^{1}-i\pi^{2})&0\\
i(\pi^{1}+i\pi^{2})&\sigma-i\pi^{0}&0\\
0&0&1\end{matrix}\right)=\left(\begin{matrix}e^{i\theta}\cos\chi&ie^{-i\phi}\sin\chi&0\\
ie^{i\phi}\sin\chi&e^{-i\theta}\cos\chi&0\\
0&0&1\end{matrix}\right)

(99)

Here,
\chi=\chi(\rho)
,
\theta=\theta(z)
,
and
\phi=\phi(\varphi)=\nu\varphi
.
By explicit calculation, one gets

\begin{split}U\partial_{\rho}U^{\dagger}&=\left(\begin{matrix}e^{i\theta}\cos\chi&ie^{-i\phi}\sin\chi&0\\
ie^{i\phi}\sin\chi&e^{-i\theta}\cos\chi&0\\
0&0&1\end{matrix}\right)\partial_{\rho}\left(\begin{matrix}e^{-i\theta}\cos\chi&-ie^{-i\phi}\sin\chi&0\\
-ie^{i\phi}\sin\chi&e^{i\theta}\cos\chi&0\\
0&0&1\end{matrix}\right)\\
&=\left(\begin{matrix}e^{i\theta}\cos\chi&ie^{-i\phi}\sin\chi&0\\
ie^{i\phi}\sin\chi&e^{-i\theta}\cos\chi&0\\
0&0&1\end{matrix}\right)\left(\begin{matrix}-e^{-i\theta}\sin\chi&-ie^{-i\phi}\cos\chi&0\\
-ie^{i\phi}\cos\chi&-e^{i\theta}\sin\chi&0\\
0&0&0\end{matrix}\right)\partial_{\rho}\chi\\
&=\left(\begin{matrix}0&-ie^{i(\theta-\phi)}&0\\
-ie^{-i(\theta-\phi)}&0&0\\
0&0&0\end{matrix}\right)\partial_{\rho}\chi\,,\end{split}

(100)

\begin{split}U\partial_{\varphi}U^{\dagger}&=\left(\begin{matrix}e^{i\theta}\cos\chi&ie^{-i\phi}\sin\chi&0\\
ie^{i\phi}\sin\chi&e^{-i\theta}\cos\chi&0\\
0&0&1\end{matrix}\right)\partial_{\varphi}\left(\begin{matrix}e^{-i\theta}\cos\chi&-ie^{-i\phi}\sin\chi&0\\
-ie^{i\phi}\sin\chi&e^{i\theta}\cos\chi&0\\
0&0&1\end{matrix}\right)\\
&=\left(\begin{matrix}e^{i\theta}\cos\chi&ie^{-i\phi}\sin\chi&0\\
ie^{i\phi}\sin\chi&e^{-i\theta}\cos\chi&0\\
0&0&1\end{matrix}\right)\left(\begin{matrix}0&-e^{-i\phi}\sin\chi&0\\
e^{i\phi}\sin\chi&0&0\\
0&0&0\end{matrix}\right)\partial_{\varphi}\phi\\
&=\left(\begin{matrix}i\sin^{2}\chi&-e^{i(\theta-\phi)}\sin\chi\cos\chi&0\\
e^{-i(\theta-\phi)}\sin\chi\cos\chi&-i\sin^{2}\chi&0\\
0&0&0\end{matrix}\right)\partial_{\varphi}\phi\,,\end{split}

(101)

\begin{split}U\partial_{z}U^{\dagger}&=\left(\begin{matrix}e^{i\theta}\cos\chi&ie^{-i\phi}\sin\chi&0\\
ie^{i\phi}\sin\chi&e^{-i\theta}\cos\chi&0\\
0&0&1\end{matrix}\right)\partial_{z}\left(\begin{matrix}e^{-i\theta}\cos\chi&-ie^{-i\phi}\sin\chi&0\\
-ie^{i\phi}\sin\chi&e^{i\theta}\cos\chi&0\\
0&0&1\end{matrix}\right)\\
&=\left(\begin{matrix}e^{i\theta}\cos\chi&ie^{-i\phi}\sin\chi&0\\
ie^{i\phi}\sin\chi&e^{-i\theta}\cos\chi&0\\
0&0&1\end{matrix}\right)\left(\begin{matrix}-ie^{-i\theta}\cos\chi&0&0\\
0&ie^{i\theta}\cos\chi&0\\
0&0&0\end{matrix}\right)\partial_{z}\theta\\
&=\left(\begin{matrix}-i\cos^{2}\chi&-e^{i(\theta-\phi)}\sin\chi\cos\chi&0\\
e^{-i(\theta-\phi)}\sin\chi\cos\chi&i\cos^{2}\chi&0\\
0&0&0\end{matrix}\right)\partial_{z}\theta\,,\end{split}

(102)

and

\begin{split}&U\partial_{\rho}U^{\dagger}U\partial_{\varphi}U^{\dagger}U\partial_{z}U^{\dagger}\\
&=\left(\begin{matrix}0&-ie^{i(\theta-\phi)}&0\\
-ie^{-i(\theta-\phi)}&0&0\\
0&0&0\end{matrix}\right)\left(\begin{matrix}i\sin^{2}\chi&-e^{i(\theta-\phi)}\sin\chi\cos\chi&0\\
e^{-i(\theta-\phi)}\sin\chi\cos\chi&-i\sin^{2}\chi&0\\
0&0&0\end{matrix}\right)\\
&\quad\times\left(\begin{matrix}-i\cos^{2}\chi&-e^{i(\theta-\phi)}\sin\chi\cos\chi&0\\
e^{-i(\theta-\phi)}\sin\chi\cos\chi&i\cos^{2}\chi&0\\
0&0&0\end{matrix}\right)\partial_{\rho}\chi\partial_{\varphi}\phi\partial_{z}\theta\\
&=\left(\begin{matrix}-i\sin\chi\cos\chi&-e^{i(\theta-\phi)}\sin^{2}\chi&0\\
e^{-i(\theta-\phi)}\sin^{2}\chi&i\sin\chi\cos\chi&0\\
0&0&0\end{matrix}\right)\\
&\quad\times\left(\begin{matrix}-i\cos^{2}\chi&-e^{i(\theta-\phi)}\sin\chi\cos\chi&0\\
e^{-i(\theta-\phi)}\sin\chi\cos\chi&i\cos^{2}\chi&0\\
0&0&0\end{matrix}\right)\partial_{\rho}\chi\partial_{\varphi}\phi\partial_{z}\theta\\
&=-\sin\chi\cos\chi\partial_{\rho}\chi\partial_{\varphi}\phi\partial_{z}\theta\left(\begin{matrix}1&&\\
&1&\\
&&0\end{matrix}\right),\end{split}

(103)

\begin{split}&U\partial_{\varphi}U^{\dagger}U\partial_{\rho}U^{\dagger}U\partial_{z}U^{\dagger}\\
&=\left(\begin{matrix}i\sin^{2}\chi&-e^{i(\theta-\phi)}\sin\chi\cos\chi&0\\
e^{-i(\theta-\phi)}\sin\chi\cos\chi&-i\sin^{2}\chi&0\\
0&0&0\end{matrix}\right)\left(\begin{matrix}0&-ie^{i(\theta-\phi)}&0\\
-ie^{-i(\theta-\phi)}&0&0\\
0&0&0\end{matrix}\right)\\
&\quad\times\left(\begin{matrix}-i\cos^{2}\chi&-e^{i(\theta-\phi)}\sin\chi\cos\chi&0\\
e^{-i(\theta-\phi)}\sin\chi\cos\chi&i\cos^{2}\chi&0\\
0&0&0\end{matrix}\right)\partial_{\rho}\chi\partial_{\varphi}\phi\partial_{z}\theta\\
&=\left(\begin{matrix}i\sin\chi\cos\chi&e^{i(\theta-\phi)}\sin^{2}\chi&0\\
-e^{-i(\theta-\phi)}\sin^{2}\chi&-i\sin\chi\cos\chi&0\\
0&0&0\end{matrix}\right)\\
&\quad\times\left(\begin{matrix}-i\cos^{2}\chi&-e^{i(\theta-\phi)}\sin\chi\cos\chi&0\\
e^{-i(\theta-\phi)}\sin\chi\cos\chi&i\cos^{2}\chi&0\\
0&0&0\end{matrix}\right)\partial_{\rho}\chi\partial_{\varphi}\phi\partial_{z}\theta\\
&=+\sin\chi\cos\chi\partial_{\rho}\chi\partial_{\varphi}\phi\partial_{z}\theta\left(\begin{matrix}1&&\\
&1&\\
&&0\end{matrix}\right),\end{split}

(104)

and so on.
Therefore, we obtain

\begin{split}\Gamma_{5}&=\frac{iN_{\rm C}}{48\pi^{2}}\int d^{4}x\varepsilon^{ijk}\operatorname{Tr}\left[\left(L_{i}L_{j}L_{k}+R_{i}R_{j}R_{k}\right)\Omega_{0}\right]|_{\xi=1}\\
&=-\frac{2\cdot 3!\cdot N_{\rm C}}{48\pi^{2}}\int d^{3}\bm{x}\sin\chi\cos\chi\partial_{\rho}\chi\partial_{\varphi}\phi\partial_{z}\theta\int_{0}^{2\pi}dt(-1)\,.\end{split}

(105)

The spatial integral is evaluated as

\begin{split}&\int_{0}^{\infty}d\rho\int_{0}^{2\pi}d\varphi\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dz\sin\chi\cos\chi\partial_{\rho}\chi\partial_{\varphi}\phi\partial_{z}\theta\\
&=2\pi\nu\left(\int_{0}^{R}d\rho\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dz\partial_{z}\theta+\int_{R}^{\infty}d\rho\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dz\partial_{z}\theta\right)\sin\chi\cos\chi\partial_{\rho}\chi\\
&=2\pi\nu\int_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}d\chi\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dz\partial_{z}\theta\sin\chi\cos\chi\\
&=2\pi^{2}\nu.\end{split}

(106)

Therefore, we find the WZW term for the pancake solutions is given by

\begin{split}\Gamma_{5}&=\frac{iN_{\rm C}}{48\pi^{2}}\int d^{4}x\varepsilon^{ijk}\operatorname{Tr}\left[\left(L_{i}L_{j}L_{k}+R_{i}R_{j}R_{k}\right)\Omega_{0}\right]|_{\xi=1}\\
&=-\frac{2\cdot 3!\cdot N_{\rm C}}{48\pi^{2}}(2\pi^{2}\nu)\cdot(-2\pi)=N_{\rm C}\pi\nu.\end{split}

(107)

It implies that the pancake solitons are fermions for
N_{\rm C}=3
.

Note that for the domain wall Skyrmion, the second term in the second line of eq. (
106
) does not vanish, and one can evaluate it as

\int_{R}^{\infty}d\rho\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dz\partial_{z}\theta\partial_{\rho}\chi\sin\chi\cos\chi=-2\pi\int_{\pi/2}^{0}d\chi\sin\chi\cos\chi=\pi.

(108)

It implies that the WZW term for the domain wall Skyrmion is
2\pi
, and thereby they are bosons.

Since the baryon number
can be similarly calculated
as

\begin{split}N_{\text{B}}&=\frac{1}{24\pi^{2}}\int d^{3}\bm{x}\epsilon^{ijk}\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(L_{i}L_{j}L_{k})\\
&=-\frac{1}{2\pi^{2}}\int d^{3}\bm{x}\sin\chi\cos\chi\partial_{\rho}\chi\partial_{\varphi}\phi\partial_{z}\theta\\
&=-\frac{1}{2\pi^{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}d\rho\partial_{\rho}\chi\sin\chi\cos\chi\int_{0}^{2\pi}d\varphi\partial_{\varphi}\phi\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dz\partial_{z}\theta\\
&=\begin{cases}-2\nu\displaystyle{\int_{0}^{\pi/2}}d\chi\sin\chi\cos\chi=-\nu,&\text{for pancake solitons [Figure 3 (c)]}\\
+2\nu\displaystyle{\int_{\pi/2}^{0}}d\chi\sin\chi\cos\chi=-\nu,&\text{for hole solitons [Figure 5 (b)]},\end{cases}\end{split}

(109)

we can relate the WZW term and
the baryon number as

\Gamma_{5}=-\pi N_{\rm C}N_{\rm B}.

(110)

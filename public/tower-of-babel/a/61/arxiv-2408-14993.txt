Conditioning the Logistic continuous-state branching process on non-extinction via its total progeny
Clément Foucart, Víctor Rivero, Anita Winter

Abstract
. The problem of conditioning a continuous-state branching process with quadratic competition (logistic CB process) on non-extinction is investigated. We first establish that non-extinction is equivalent to the total progeny of the population being infinite. The conditioning we propose is then designed by requiring the total progeny to exceed arbitrarily large exponential random variables. This is related to a Doob’s h h -transform with an explicit excessive function h h . The h h -transformed process, i.e. the conditioned process, is shown to have a finite lifetime almost surely (it is either killed or it explodes continuously). When starting from positive values, the conditioned process is furthermore characterized, up to its lifetime, as the solution to a certain stochastic equation with jumps. The latter superposes the dynamics of the initial logistic CB process with an additional density-dependent immigration term. Last, it is established that the conditioned process can be starting from zero. Key tools employed are a representation of the logistic CB process through a time-changed generalized Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process, as well as Laplace and Siegmund duality relationships with auxiliary diffusion processes.



Keywords
: Continuous-state branching process, competition, logistic growth, non-extinction, total progeny, conditioning, Doob’s
h
-transform, strict local martingale, explosion, Laplace duality, Siegmund duality.

1.
Introduction

Branching processes conditioned on never becoming extinct are a cornerstone in the theory of branching processes. It is well known that they have the same law as certain branching processes with immigration, which is derived by size-biasing the offspring distribution. Immigration is interpreted as an immortal genealogical line, or spine, on which copies of the initial branching process are grafted, see Lyons et al.
[
LPP95
]
, Duquesne
[
Duq09
]
and Chen and Delmas
[
CD12
]
for some applications.

There is considerable interest in exploring such conditionings for more general stochastic population models that account for interactions between individuals. Notably, these interactions typically disrupt the branching property, rendering the analysis of the process more challenging.

The objective of this article is to study a conditioning for the class of one-dimensional continuous-state branching processes (CB processes) with
quadratic competition
. These processes, referred to as logistic branching processes, were defined by Lambert in
[
Lam05
]
and are of particular interest in the theory of stochastic population models with self-regulation properties.

They can be seen as random analogues of Verhulst’s logistic function, see
[
Ver38
]
, initially designed to model populations with finite carrying capacity, thereby preventing Malthusian growth. Heuristically, in addition to the classical reproduction dynamics, continuously in time and at constant rate, a pair of individuals is picked, and the first kills the second. The process, which tracks the total size of the continuous population, is the so-called logistic continuous-state branching (LCB) process.

An LCB process
Z
, starting from
z\in(0,\infty)
, can be seen as the unique weak solution, up to the first explosion time, of the following stochastic equation with jumps:

\displaystyle Z_{t}=z+\sigma\int_{0}^{t}\sqrt{Z_{s}}\mathrm{d}B_{s}+\gamma\int_{0}^{t}Z_{s}\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{Z_{s-}}\!\!\int_{0}^{1}y\bar{\mathcal{M}}(\mathrm{d}s,\mathrm{d}u,\mathrm{d}y)

(1)

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{Z_{s-}}\!\!\int_{1}^{\infty}y\mathcal{M}(\mathrm{d}s,\mathrm{d}u,\mathrm{d}y)-\frac{c}{2}\int_{0}^{t}Z^{2}_{s}\mathrm{d}s,\ t\in[0,\infty),

where
c,\sigma\geq 0
,
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
,
B
is a Brownian motion and
\mathcal{M}
is an independent Poisson random measure (PRM) with intensity
\mathrm{d}s\mathrm{d}u\pi(\mathrm{d}y)
with
\pi
a Lévy measure such that
\int_{0}^{\infty}1\wedge y^{2}\pi(\mathrm{d}y)<\infty
.

When
c>0
, the negative quadratic drift in (
1
) represents the competition. Heuristically continuously in time at constant rate
c/2
, a pair of individuals is picked and the first kills the second. When
c=0
, there is no competition, and the process
Z
is a classical CB(
\Psi
). We refer the reader to Dawson and Li
[
DL12
]
and
[
Li22
, Chapter 10]
for the setting of CB processes. The stochastic equation (
1
) with competition has been studied in several works, including Berestycki et al.
[
BFF18
, Proposition 1.1]
and Palau and Pardo
[
PP18
, Theorem 1]
.

The law of an LCB process is characterized by the competition parameter
c/2\geq 0
and the branching mechanism
\Psi
, which takes the following Lévy-Khintchine form:

(2)

\Psi(x):=\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}x^{2}-\gamma x+\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(e^{-xy}-1+xy\mathbbm{1}_{\{y\leq 1\}}\right)\pi(\mathrm{d}y).

In this article, we are interested in the regime where the process
Z
becomes extinct almost surely, i.e.,
Z_{t}\underset{t\rightarrow\infty}{\longrightarrow}0
,
\mathbb{P}_{z}
-a.s. for all
z\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
. We emphasize that the framework of a continuous-state space enables sample paths to be attracted towards
0
, without necessarily reaching it. The population is sometimes said to be
extinguishing
, see Kyprianou’s book
[
Kyp14
, Chapter 12, page 345]
.

Our aim is to construct, using a Doob
h
-transform, a conditioning of the process
(Z,\mathbb{P}_{z})
on the singular event of non-extinction

\mathscr{S}:=\{Z_{t}\underset{t\rightarrow\infty}{\longrightarrow}0\}^{c}.

Aside from the pure diffusive case, where general methods can be applied, this question, to the best of our knowledge, has not been addressed in the literature.
Before discussing logistic CB processes, we will briefly review what is known about classical CB processes.

Without competition

First of all, in the framework of classical CB processes, the event of extinction
\{Z_{t}\underset{t\rightarrow\infty}{\longrightarrow}0\}
happens almost surely if and only if the process is critical or subcritical, that is to say
\Psi^{\prime}(0+)=0
or
\Psi^{\prime}(0+)>0
. We refer for instance to
[
Kyp14
, Chapter 12]
. Furthermore, a necessary and sufficient condition for the process to hit
0
is the so-called Grey’s condition,
\int^{\infty}\frac{\mathrm{d}u}{\Psi(u)}<\infty
. Under this condition, the extinction time
\zeta_{0}:=\inf\{t>0:Z_{t}=0\}
, of a (sub)-critical CB process
Z
, is finite almost surely and one has
\mathscr{S}=\{\exists t>0:Z_{t}=0\}^{c}
a.s..

In the setting of finite-time extinction, the conditioning of the process to survive forever has been studied in Li
[
Li00
]
and Lambert
[
Lam01
,
Lam07
]
. The conditioned process is built by forcing the extinction time to be larger than any deterministic time.
This is the notion of
Q
-process. Heuristically, the null-set
\mathscr{S}
is “approached” by
\bigcap_{s>0}\{\zeta_{0}>s\}
and
the law of the
Q
-process arises as the following limit:

\mathbb{Q}_{z}(\Lambda):=\underset{s\rightarrow\infty}{\lim}\mathbb{P}_{z}(\Lambda\,|\zeta_{0}>t+s),\quad\forall\,t\geq 0,\,\forall\,\Lambda\in\mathcal{F}_{t}.

The law
\mathbb{Q}_{z}
is furthermore related to
\mathbb{P}_{z}
by the change of measure with respect to the martingale
(e^{\varrho t}Z_{t},t\geq 0)
, where
\varrho:=\Psi^{\prime}(0+)\geq 0
, that is to say:

\mathrm{d}\mathbb{Q}_{z}=e^{\varrho t}\frac{Z_{t}}{z}\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}_{z}\text{ on }\mathcal{F}_{t},\ \forall\,t\geq 0.

As far as we know, the issue of conditioning a (sub)critical continuous-state space branching process to survive when the process is only extinguishing (and hence Grey’s condition does not hold), has not been investigated yet. We briefly explain how such a conditioning can be achieved by choosing another way to “approximate” the null-set
\mathscr{S}
. The approach we are going to design for LCB processes will follow the same methodology.

The total progeny of the population is the random variable

(3)

J:=\int_{0}^{\infty}\!Z_{s}\mathrm{d}s.

As noticed by Bingham
[
Bin76
, Proposition 2.3 and Remark 2.4]
, see also
[
Kyp14
, Corollary 12.10]
, for any CB process
Z
, the following events are identical up to a null set:

(4)

\{Z_{t}\underset{t\rightarrow\infty}{\longrightarrow}0\}=\{J<\infty\}.

This entails that it is equivalent to condition the process not to get extinct or condition it to have an infinite progeny. We will define the conditioned process by forcing the total progeny to be infinite by exceeding arbitrarily large exponential random variables. This type of conditioning, defined in relation with exponential random variables, is well-known in the theory of Lévy processes. Notable references are the works by Chaumont and Doney
[
CD05
]
, and Kyprianou et al.
[
KRŞ17
]
. This approach relies solely on knowledge of Laplace transforms and proves to be an effective method in many cases.

Let
\mathbbm{e}
be an independent standard exponential random variable (i.e. with unit mean). The event of survival is “approached” by
\bigcap_{\theta>0}\{J\geq\mathbbm{e}/\theta\}
. We will see that the following limit exists:

(5)

\mathbb{P}_{z}^{\uparrow}(\Lambda,t<\zeta)=\underset{\theta\rightarrow 0}{\lim}\,\mathbb{P}_{z}(\Lambda,t\leq\mathbbm{e}/\theta\,|J>\mathbbm{e}/\theta),\ \forall\Lambda\in\mathcal{F}_{t},\forall t\geq 0,

with
\zeta
the lifetime of the process under
\mathbb{P}_{z}^{\uparrow}
. Moreover, the measure
\mathbb{P}_{z}^{\uparrow}
is the Doob’s transform of
\mathbb{P}_{z}
, defined as follows

\mathbbm{1}_{\{t<\zeta\}}\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}^{\uparrow}_{z}=\frac{Z_{t}}{z}\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}_{z}\text{ on }\mathcal{F}_{t},\ \forall\,t\geq 0,

where
\zeta:=\inf\{t>0:\,Z_{t}\notin[0,\infty)\}
is the lifetime of
(Z,\mathbb{P}_{z}^{\uparrow})
and
\infty
is chosen as a
cemetery state
. Background on Doob’s transforms is given in the forthcoming Section
3.1
. It is worth noticing that
\mathbb{P}_{z}^{\uparrow}
and
\mathbb{Q}_{z}
only differ on
\mathcal{F}_{t}
by the mass
e^{\rho t}
. Aside from the killing term, they thus both coincide.

With competition

The longterm behaviors of the LCB process have been investigated in
[
Fou19
]
. We gather here the results we need for our purpose. We start by explaining under which conditions, extinction occurs almost-surely.

In contrast with the previous setting, the quadratic competition term “
-\frac{c}{2}Z_{t}^{2}\mathrm{d}t
”, in general may push the population with supercritical branching dynamics towards extinction. We will therefore also consider branching mechanisms
\Psi
such that
\Psi^{\prime}(0+)\in[-\infty,0)
.

According to
[
Fou19
, Theorem 3.9-(1)]
, the extinction of the LCB
(\Psi,c/2)
process happens
\mathbb{P}_{z}
-almost surely, meaning the event of survival
\mathscr{S}
is a
\mathbb{P}_{z}
-null set, if and only if the following condition holds

\mathbb{H}

\qquad\qquad\mathcal{E}:=\int_{0}^{x_{0}}\!\tfrac{1}{u}e^{\int_{u}^{x_{0}}\frac{2\Psi(v)}{cv}\mathrm{d}v}\mathrm{d}u=\infty\text{, for some}\,x_{0}>0,\text{ and }\Psi(\infty):=\underset{x\rightarrow\infty}{\lim}\Psi(x)=\infty.

The first integral test,
\mathcal{E}=\infty
, guarantees that the LCB process
Z
cannot explode, i.e. it does not hit
\infty
in finite time, see
[
Fou19
, Theorem 3.1]
. The value
x_{0}>0
does not play any role in the finiteness of the integral. We shall therefore fix it in
(0,\infty)
. The condition
\mathcal{E}=\infty
implies moreover that the LCB process has its boundary
\infty
as an instantaneous entrance, see
[
Fou19
, Theorem 3.3]
. Namely, besides that the process cannot explode, it can start from
\infty
and will leave it instantaneously.
A sufficient condition for
\mathcal{E}=\infty
, is that
\int_{0+}\frac{|\Psi(v)|}{v}\mathrm{d}v<\infty
. The latter integral condition is equivalent to the following moment condition
\int^{\infty}\log(y)\pi(\mathrm{d}y)<\infty
, see e.g.

[
Fou19
, Proposition 3.13]
.

The second condition,
\Psi(\infty)=\infty
, is equivalent to
\Psi(x)\geq 0
for sufficiently large

. This condition ensures that the pure CB process with mechanism
\Psi
is not immortal, meaning it is not going to
\infty
almost surely. This prevents a potential phenomenon of stationarity from occurring in the LCB process, as detailed in
[
Fou19
, Theorem 3.7]
. To say it in another way, without competition, under the condition
\Psi(\infty)=\infty
, the CB
(\Psi)
will tend to
0
with positive probability, see
[
Kyp14
, Theorem 12.5]
. One says that the boundary
0
is
attracting
. Heuristically, with the additional negative competition drift, this probability becomes one, when the process cannot explode.

As a matter of fact, Grey’s condition is also necessary and sufficient for the boundary
0
of an LCB process to be accessible; see
[
Fou19
, Theorem 3.9]
and
[
Lam05
, Theorem 3.5]
(with the finite log-moment assumption). In other words, the competition solely does not cause the population to die out
in finite time
. It prevents however the supercritical growth when
\mathcal{E}=\infty
.

An inherent difficulty in the setting with competition is that many arguments known for the branching world do not apply. We will see however that the identity (

The role of duality

The key tool in order to find the excessive function
h
lies in some duality relationships satisfied by the LCB process. We briefly explain them. More details are given in Section
3.3.1
.

Any LCB process
Z
admits a
Laplace dual
process
U:=(U_{t},t\geq 0)
. Specifically, for all
x,z\in(0,\infty)
and
t\geq 0
,

(7)

\mathbb{E}_{z}(e^{-xZ_{t}})=\mathbb{E}_{x}(e^{-zU_{t}}),

where
U
is the diffusion, weak solution to

\mathrm{d}U_{t}=\sqrt{cU_{t}}\mathrm{d}B_{t}-\Psi(U_{t}),U_{0}=x,

for some Brownian motion
B
. The relationship (
7
) has been established in several works, including Hutzenthaler and Wakolbinger
[
HW07
]
, Hutzenthaler and Alkemper
[
AH07
]
and Greven et al.
[
GSWZ15
]
for the setting of
spatial
logistic Feller diffusions, whose branching mechanism is
\Psi(x):=\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}x^{2}-\gamma x
. The setting without spatial component and with a general branching mechanism
\Psi
, i.e. of the form (
2
), is studied in
[
Fou19
]
.

The diffusion
U
, which is stochastically monotone, meaning that for any
y>0
, the map
x\mapsto\mathbb{P}_{x}(U_{t}\geq y)
is non-decreasing, also gives rise to a certain dual process
V
. For a general statement, refer to Siegmund
[
Sie76
]
, and for the framework under consideration, see Foucart
[
Fou23
, Section 6]
. Specifically, for all
x,y\in(0,\infty)
and
t\geq 0
, there is a process
V
, called
Siegmund dual
of
U
, such that

\mathbb{P}_{x}(U_{t}\geq y)=\mathbb{P}_{y}(x\geq V_{t}),

where, to alleviate the notation, we keep denoting by
\mathbb{P}_{\mathrm{a}}
the law of each process starting at
\mathrm{a}
. The process
(V,\mathbb{P}_{y})
is the diffusion, weak solution to

\mathrm{d}V_{t}=\sqrt{cV_{t}}\mathrm{d}B_{t}+\big(c/2+\Psi(V_{t})\big)\mathrm{d}t,\ V_{0}=y,

with
B
some Brownian motion.

By combining the two duality relationships, we will see in the forthcoming Proposition
4
, that for all
t\geq 0
,
x,z\in(0,\infty)
,

\mathbb{E}_{z}(e^{-xZ_{t}})=\int_{0}^{\infty}ze^{-zy}\mathbb{P}_{y}(V_{t}>x)\mathrm{d}y.

We call
V
the
bidual
process of
Z
. This relationship will enable us to define the excessive function
h
for the LCB process
Z
, in an explicit manner, by utilizing the scale function
S
of
V
. The diffusion setting provides many tools and will facilitate a deep study of the excessive function of
Z
. Instead of studying the LCB process
Z
directly, we will first analyze
V
and its Doob’s transform associated to
S
. The information obtained will then be transferred to the Doob’s
h
-transform of
Z
.

We will then study the process under the new measure
\mathbb{P}^{\uparrow}_{z}
, establish the identity in (
6
), and investigate, among other things, the stochastic equation solved by it, if any. In a reminiscent way as in the CB case, where a dichotomy occurs depending on whether the mechanism is critical or subcritical, two different settings will arise according to whether the branching Lévy measure has a finite log-moment or not.

2.
Main results

We start by a statement for the pure CB process. This can be seen as a benchmark result in order to understand the next to come, in which competition is considered. Recall that we denote the total progeny by
J=\int_{0}^{\infty}Z_{s}\mathrm{d}s
.

As explained in the introduction, extinction occurs if and only if the total progeny is finite, see (
4
), and we will condition the CB process to never converge towards
0
by forcing
J
to be greater than arbitrarily large exponential random variables, see (
5
). By doing so, we recover,
aside from the killing
, the classical
Q
-process of the CB process, as studied in
[
Li00
]
and
[
Lam07
]
. It is however important to note that, unlike in those works, Grey’s condition is not assumed here. The process may thus only be extinguishing.

Theorem 1

(No competition)
.

For all
z\in(0,\infty)
, let
\mathbb{P}_{z}
be the law of the CB process
Z
starting from

. Assume the branching mechanism
\Psi
(sub)-critical, i.e.
\varrho=\Psi^{\prime}(0+)\geq 0
(equivalently
J<\infty
,
\mathbb{P}_{z}
-a.s.).

i)

The process
(Z_{t},t\geq 0)
is a
\mathbb{P}_{z}
-martingale in the critical case, i.e.
\varrho=0
. It is a
\mathbb{P}_{z}
-supermartingale in the subcritical case, i.e.
\varrho>0
.

Define the (sub)-probability measure
\mathbb{P}^{\uparrow}_{z}
as follows. Set

\mathbb{P}^{\uparrow}_{z}(\Lambda,t<\zeta):=\mathbb{E}_{z}\left(\frac{Z_{t}}{z}\mathbbm{1}_{\Lambda}\right),\quad\forall\,\Lambda\in\mathcal{F}_{t},\ \forall\,t\geq 0,

with
\zeta:=\inf\{t>0:Z_{t}\notin[0,\infty)\}
the lifetime of

.

Two possible scenarios arise.

•

In the critical case,

Remark 1
.

At the level of the progeny, the distinction between the critical and subcritical cases can be understood through the expected value of the progeny
\mathbb{E}_{z}(J)
. In the critical case
\rho=0
,
\mathbb{E}_{z}(J)=\infty
. Conversely,
\mathbb{E}_{z}(J)<\infty
when
\rho>0
. In the subcritical case, forcing the progeny to be infinite imposes such a significant distortion on the CB process that roughly, by pulling upwards the path, so to avoid it heading down to zero, we end up “breaking” it. This is formalized by killing the conditioned process.
In the critical case, the mean of the progeny being infinite, the conditioning is less stringent.

Remark 2
.

We recognize in (
8
) the stochastic equation solved by a continuous-state branching process with immigration (CBI) with branching mechanism
\Psi
and immigration
\Psi^{\prime}
, see
[
Li22
, Chapter 10]
. Specifically, the last line in (
8
) is a subordinator with Laplace exponent
\Psi^{\prime}
, representing the immigration term (with a killing term when
\varrho=\Psi^{\prime}(0+)>0

Theorem
1

\Psi^{\prime}(\infty)<\infty
, equivalently there is no diffusive part, namely
\sigma=0
in (

\int_{0}^{\infty}1\wedge y\,\pi(\mathrm{d}y)<\infty
. The (sub)critical mechanism, in this setting, takes the form

(9)

\Psi(x)=bx-\int_{0}^{\infty}(1-e^{-xy})\pi(\mathrm{d}y),

with

and
\Psi^{\prime}(0+)=b-\int_{0}^{\infty}y\pi(\mathrm{d}y)\geq 0
.
Pakes and Trajtsman
[

Proposition 1
.

Assume

Remark 3

Denote by

3.1.
Doob’s

-transforms via supermartingales and local martingales

We provide here background on the construction of (sub)Markovian probability laws with the help of supermartingales. We refer the reader e.g. to Chung and Walsh’s book
[

for background on Doob’s

Let
(P_{t})_{t\geq 0}

, there exists a right-continuous Markov process

with state space
[0,\infty]
and transition semigroup
(P_{t})_{t\geq 0}
, satisfying

For all

,
\mathbb{P}_{z}(Z_{0}=z)=1
,

ii)

is a strong Markov process,

iii)

Z
has an infinite lifetime,
\zeta=\infty
,
\mathbb{P}_{z}\text{-a.s. for all }z\in[0,\infty]
.

Recall that a positive function

is said to be excessive for

(h(Z_{t}),t\geq 0)
is a positive supermartingale, i.e.

Theorem A

(Change of measure with a supermartingale)
.

Assume

is an excessive function,
0<h(z)<\infty
for all
z\in(0,\infty)
and
h(0)=0
,

. Then,

i)

The family of operators defined on the

-valued bounded Borel functions by:

P^{\uparrow}_{t}f(z):=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{h(z)}P_{t}(hf)(z),&\text{ if }z\in(0,\infty),\\
0,&\text{ if }z\in\{0,\infty\},\end{cases}

is a semigroup on
[0,\infty)
, possibly sub-Markovian, i.e. one has
P_{t}^{\uparrow}1\leq 1
for all

ii)

The process with semigroup
(P^{\uparrow}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
admits a right-continuous version and we denote by
\mathbb{P}^{\uparrow}_{z}
its law on
(\Omega,\mathcal{F}^{0})
, when issued from

(Z,\mathbb{P}^{\uparrow}_{z})
is strongly Markovian with respect to
(\mathcal{F}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
and if

is an
(\mathcal{F}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
-stopping time, then for any

and

,

(15)

\mathbb{P}_{z}^{\uparrow}(A,T<\zeta)=\frac{1}{h(z)}\mathbbm{E}_{z}\left(h(Z_{T})\mathbbm{1}_{A}\right),\text{ with }\zeta\text{ the lifetime of }(Z,\mathbb{P}_{z}^{\uparrow}).

The
h
-transform covers three different settings:

i)

If
(h(Z_{t}),t\geq 0)
is a
\mathbb{P}_{z}
-martingale, then the process
(Z,\mathbb{P}_{z}^{\uparrow})
has an infinite lifetime (it is not killed and does not explode).

ii)

If
(h(Z_{t}),t\geq 0)
is a
\mathbb{P}_{z}
-supermartingale but not a local martingale, we say that it is a
strict
supermartingale. In this case, the process
(Z,\mathbb{P}_{z}^{\uparrow})
has a finite lifetime, it is moreover killed, meaning the process is sent at
\infty
by a single jump with positive probability. Provided that there exists
1
1

1

this will be the case in our setting
a.s. a left limit at

iii)

If
(h(Z_{t}),t\geq 0)
is a local martingale but not a martingale, we say that it is a
strict
local martingale. In this case, the process has a finite lifetime, but is not killed. Explosion occurs here “continuously”, that is to say
Z_{\zeta-}=\infty
,
\mathbb{P}_{z}^{\uparrow}
-a.s..

We now provide further details on the second and third cases, as both will occur in our framework.

Lemma A

(Strict local martingale and continuous explosion)
.

Under the same assumptions as Theorem
A
. If
(h(Z_{t}),t\geq 0)
is a supermartingale and not a martingale then
\mathbb{P}^{\uparrow}_{z}(\zeta<\infty)>0
. For all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, set
\zeta_{n}^{+}:=\inf\{t>0:Z_{t}>n\}\in[0,\infty]
.

•

If
(h(Z_{t}),t\geq 0)
is a strict local martingale, then on
\{\zeta<\infty\}
,
\zeta=\underset{n\rightarrow\infty}{\lim}\zeta_{n}^{+}
,
\mathbb{P}^{\uparrow}_{z}
-a.s..

•

If
(h(Z_{t}),t\geq 0)
is a strict supermartingale, then, one has for some
n\geq 1
,
\mathbb{P}_{z}^{\uparrow}(\zeta_{n}^{+}\geq\zeta)>0
. Thus, there exists an
n
such that with positive probability, the process is killed before getting above level
n
.

Proof.

First, by taking
A=\Omega
and
T=t
a deterministic time, in (
15
), we get

\mathbb{P}_{z}^{\uparrow}(t<\zeta)=\mathbb{E}_{z}\left(\frac{h(Z_{t})}{h(z)}\right)<1
,
hence,
\mathbb{P}_{z}^{\uparrow}(\zeta<\infty)>0
.

Suppose that
(h(Z_{t}),t\geq 0)
is a strict local martingale. We explain why the explosion is continuous on the event
\{\zeta<\infty\}
. We start by checking that the sequence of stopping times,
(\tau_{n}):=(\zeta_{n}^{+}\wedge n)
, localizes
(h(Z_{t}),t\geq 0)
. First, by assumption,
\zeta_{n}^{+}\rightarrow\infty
,
\mathbb{P}_{z}
-a.s. as
n
goes to
\infty
, and the same holds for
(\tau_{n})
. Observe next that since
(h(Z_{t}),t\geq 0)
is a supermartingale and
\tau_{n}
is a bounded stopping time, one has by the optimal stopping theorem
\mathbb{E}_{z}\big(h(Z_{\tau_{n}})\big)\leq h(z)<\infty
. This entails that
h(Z_{\tau_{n}})\vee h(n)
is integrable. Finally, notice that

\sup_{t\geq 0}h(Z_{t\wedge\tau_{n}})\leq h(Z_{\tau_{n}})\vee h(n)\text{ a.s..}

Since the stopped process
(h(Z_{t\wedge\tau_{n}}),t\geq 0)
is a local martingale, bounded by an integrable random variable, it is actually a true martingale. By taking now
A=\Omega
and
T=\tau_{n}
in (
15
), we get for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

\mathbb{P}_{z}^{\uparrow}(\zeta_{n}^{+}\wedge n<\zeta)=\frac{1}{h(z)}\mathbbm{E}_{z}\left(h(Z_{\zeta_{n}^{+}\wedge n})\right)=1.

Hence,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{z}^{\uparrow}(\zeta<\infty)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}_{z}^{\uparrow}(\zeta_{n}^{+}\wedge n<\zeta<\infty)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}_{z}^{\uparrow}(\zeta_{n}^{+}<n,\zeta_{n}^{+}<\zeta<\infty)+\mathbb{P}_{z}^{\uparrow}(\zeta_{n}^{+}\geq n,n<\zeta<\infty)\underset{n\rightarrow\infty}{\longrightarrow}\mathbb{P}_{z}^{\uparrow}(\zeta_{m}^{+}<\zeta<\infty,\forall m\geq 1).

Therefore, denoting by
\zeta_{\infty}:=\underset{m\rightarrow\infty}{\lim}\!\uparrow\zeta_{m}^{+}=\inf\{t>0:Z_{t}=\infty\}
, we have, conditionally on the event
\{\zeta<\infty\}
,
\zeta_{\infty}\leq\zeta
,
\mathbb{P}_{z}^{\uparrow}
-almost surely, hence
\zeta_{\infty}=\zeta
,
\zeta
is thus predictable, and
\mathbb{P}_{z}^{\uparrow}(Z_{\zeta-}=\infty)=1
.

Suppose now that
(h(Z_{t}),t\geq 0)
is a strict supermartingale. Then, necessarily
(\zeta_{n}^{+}\wedge n)
is not a localizing sequence of stopping times (otherwise the process would be a local martingale). Therefore, for some

,

\mathbb{P}_{z}^{\uparrow}(s\wedge\zeta_{n}^{+}\wedge n<\zeta)=\frac{1}{h(z)}\mathbbm{E}_{z}\left(h(Z_{s\wedge\zeta_{n}^{+}\wedge n})\right)<1,

and we finally see that
\mathbb{P}_{z}^{\uparrow}(\zeta_{n}^{+}\geq\zeta)>0
.
\Box

Remark 9

(Almost sure killing)
.

In the case ii) of a strict supermartingale, we have seen that the process
(Z,\mathbb{P}_{z}^{\uparrow})
is killed before explosion with positive probability. This occurs almost surely if and only if the supermartingale
\big(h(Z_{\zeta_{n}^{+}\wedge n}),n\geq 0\big)
is
\mathbb{P}_{z}
-uniformly integrable. Indeed, in this scenario, recalling

, and that
\mathbb{P}_{z}
-almost surely,
\zeta_{n}^{+}\wedge n\rightarrow\infty

goes to

, we see that
(h(Z_{\zeta_{n}^{+}\wedge n}),n\geq 0)
converges towards

. Therefore, we have

\underset{n\rightarrow\infty}{\lim}\mathbb{P}_{z}^{\uparrow}(\zeta_{n}^{+}\wedge n<\zeta)=\frac{1}{h(z)}\underset{n\rightarrow\infty}{\lim}\mathbbm{E}_{z}\left(h(Z_{\zeta_{n}^{+}\wedge n})\right)=\frac{1}{h(z)}\mathbbm{E}_{z}\left(\underset{n\rightarrow\infty}{\lim}h(Z_{\zeta_{n}^{+}\wedge n})\right)=0,

implying that
\mathbb{P}_{z}^{\uparrow}(\exists n\geq 1:\zeta_{n}^{+}\geq\zeta)=1
.

JS87
, Page 7]
, and since
\{\tau^{r}<t\}
is a
\mathbb{P}_{z}
-null set, one has

\mathbb{P}_{z}^{\uparrow}(\tau^{r}<t,t<\zeta)=\frac{1}{h(z)}\mathbb{E}_{z}\big(h(Z_{t})\mathbbm{1}_{\{\tau^{r}<t\}}\big)=0
.

Hence, the event
\{\exists t>0:\Delta Z_{t}<0\}=\cup_{r\in\mathbb{Q}_{+}^{\star}}\{\tau^{r}<\infty\}
is a
\mathbb{P}_{z}^{\uparrow}
-null set.

ii)

Notice that
\mathbb{P}^{\uparrow}_{z}
may charge
\mathbb{P}_{z}
-null sets. For instance, by assumption here,
\mathbb{P}_{z}(\zeta<\infty)=0
, and according to Lemma
A
,
\mathbb{P}^{\uparrow}_{z}(\zeta<\infty)>0
when
(h(Z_{t}),t\geq 0)
is not a true martingale.

3.2.
Background on LCB processes

We gather here some fundamental properties of LCB processes. Most of those results can be found in
[
Fou19
]
. Proposition
2
is established in forthcoming Section
3.2.2
. We also state some basic facts on the martingale problem solved by the LCB. Those are mostly direct consequences of the stochastic equation (
1
) solved by the LCB process. They will play an important role in several places in the proofs.

3.2.1.
Martingale problem and Lamperti’s time change for the LCB process

Denote by
C^{2}([0,\infty))
the space of functions that are twice differentiable on
[0,\infty)
. Let
\hat{C}
be the space of continuous functions on
[0,\infty]
, hence with a finite limit at
\infty
. Set

(16)

\mathcal{D}_{Z}:=\left\{f\in C^{2}([0,\infty))\cap\hat{C}:\underset{z\rightarrow\infty}{\lim}\big(z|f(z)-f(\infty)|+z|f^{\prime\prime}(z)|+(z+cz^{2})|f^{\prime}(z)|\big)=0\right\}.

Denote by
C_{c}^{2}(0,\infty)
the space of
C^{2}
functions vanishing outside a compact set included in
(0,\infty)
. Note that
C_{c}^{2}(0,\infty)\subset\mathcal{D}_{Z}
.

Martingale problem
. Let
Z
be the solution to the stochastic equation (
1
). For any
f\in C^{2}([0,\infty))
and
z\in[0,\infty)
, set

(17)

\mathscr{L}f(z):=z\mathrm{L}^{\Psi}f(z)-\frac{c}{2}z^{2}f^{\prime}(z),

with
\mathrm{L}^{\Psi}
the generator of a spectrally positive Lévy process with Laplace exponent
\Psi
, viz.

\mathrm{L}^{\Psi}f(z):=\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(f(z+y)-f(z)-yf^{\prime}(z)\mathbbm{1}_{\{y<1\}}\right)\pi(\mathrm{d}y)-\gamma f^{\prime}(z)+\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}f^{\prime\prime}(z).

Lemma 1
.

The LCB process
Z
, stopped at its first explosion time, is the unique solution to the following martingale problem:

\mathrm{(MP)}_{Z}
: for any
z>0
, and any
f\in C_{c}^{2}([0,\infty))
, the process

M:=(M_{t},t\geq 0):=\left(f(Z_{t})-\int_{0}^{t}\mathscr{L}f(Z_{s})\mathrm{d}s,t\geq 0\right)\text{ is a }\mathbb{P}_{z}\text{-martingale}.

Moreover,
M
is a true martingale for any
f\in\mathcal{D}_{Z}
and is a local one for any
f\in C^{2}([0,\infty))
.

Proof.

Recall that the LCB process
Z
is solution of the stochastic equation (
1
). The claim that
M
is a local martingale for any
f\in C^{2}([0,\infty))
is a consequence of the application of Itô’s lemma on
f(Z_{t})
, see e.g. Meyer
[
Mey76
, Theorem 3, Chapter III and Theorem 21, Chapter IV]
. When
f\in\mathcal{D}_{Z}
, we have
\sup|\mathscr{L}f(z)|<\infty
. The local martingale
M
, being bounded on any interval
[0,t]
, is a true martingale. In particular, this is the case when
f\in C^{2}_{c}(0,\infty)
and
Z
satisfies the martingale problem
\mathrm{(MP)}_{Z}
. The fact that there is a unique solution of the latter under the condition of non-explosion
\mathcal{E}=\infty
has been shown in
[
Fou19
]
.
\Box

Lamperti’s time change
. Following an idea already present in
[
Lam05
]
, and further detailed in
[
Fou19
, Section 4]
, the LCB process
Z
, starting from
z>0
, can be constructed as follows. Let
Y
be a spectrally positive Lévy process with Laplace exponent
\Psi
, starting from
z>0
. Set

R_{t}:=Y_{t}-\frac{c}{2}\int_{0}^{t}R_{s}\mathrm{d}s,\text{ for any }t\geq 0.

Set
\sigma_{0}:=\inf\{s>0:R_{s}\leq 0\}
and define

[0,\infty]\ni u\mapsto\theta_{u}:=\int_{0}^{u\wedge\sigma_{0}}\frac{\mathrm{d}s}{R_{s}}\in[0,\infty].

The process
R
is a generalized Ornstein-Uhlenbeck (GOU) process, see e.g. Sato’s book
[
Sat13
, Chapter 3.17]
. Notice that its generator is of the form
\mathscr{L}^{R}f(z):=\mathrm{L}^{\Psi}f(z)-\frac{c}{2}zf^{\prime}(z)
, where
\mathrm{L}^{\Psi}
is the generator of
Y
. The identity
z\mathscr{L}^{R}f(z)=\mathscr{L}f(z)
, together with Volkonski’s theorem on time-changes of Markov processes, see e.g. Ethier-Kurtz’s book
[
EK05
, Theorem 1.4 page 309]
, ensures that the process
(Z_{t},t\geq 0)
, defined by

(18)

\displaystyle Z_{t}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}R_{C_{t}}&0\leq t<\theta_{\infty},\\
0&t\geq\theta_{\infty}\text{ and }\sigma_{0}<\infty,\\
\infty&t\geq\theta_{\infty}\text{ and }\sigma_{0}=\infty,\end{cases}

with
C_{t}:=\inf\{u>0:\theta_{u}>t\}=\int_{0}^{t}Z_{s}\mathrm{d}s
, is a solution to
(\mathrm{MP})_{Z}
, and thus a weak solution to (
1
), see e.g. Kurtz
[
Kur11
, Theorem 2.3]
. In the case without competition, we recover Lamperti’s time-change of CBs, see e.g.
[
Kyp14
, Theorem 12.2]
.

3.2.2.
Extinction and total progeny: proof of Proposition
2

The possible behaviors of the LCB process at the boundaries and in its long-term have been classified in
[
Fou19
]
, see the Introduction.

Fix
x_{0}
in
(0,\infty)
and recall

(19)

\mathcal{E}=\int_{0}^{x_{0}}\!\tfrac{1}{u}e^{\int_{u}^{x_{0}}\frac{2\Psi(v)}{cv}\mathrm{d}v}\mathrm{d}u.

The condition
\mathcal{E}=\infty
is known to be necessary and sufficient for the GOU process
R
to be recurrent, see Shiga
[
Shi90
]
. The latter is furthermore positive recurrent if and only if
\int^{\infty}\log(y)\pi(\mathrm{d}y)<\infty
.

When
R
is recurrent, the random variable
\theta_{\infty}
is necessarily infinite on the event
\{\sigma_{0}=\infty\}
. This explains that the LCB process does not explode. On the other hand, the assumption
\Psi(\infty)=\infty
entails that
R
can hit any point in
\mathbb{R}
, so that
\sigma_{0}
is finite with positive probability. We can now proceed to show Proposition
2
. This follows the same argument as Bingham
[
Bin76
]
who dealt with the pure CB case in which
c=0
.

Proof of Proposition
2
. Recall that the condition
\mathbb{H}
encompasses
\mathcal{E}=\infty
and
\Psi(\infty)=\infty
. It ensures therefore that
\{\sigma_{0}<\infty\}
is almost sure. By the time-change relationship (
18
),
Z
goes to
0
a.s. on the event
\{\sigma_{0}<\infty\}
. Moreover
\sigma_{0}
is nothing but the limit
C_{\infty}=\underset{t\rightarrow\infty}{\lim}C_{t}
. Therefore, up to a null set

\{Z_{t}\underset{t\rightarrow\infty}{\longrightarrow}0\}=\{\sigma_{0}<\infty\}=\Big\{C_{\infty}=\int_{0}^{\infty}\!Z_{s}\mathrm{d}s<\infty\Big\},

and we have
J=C_{\infty}
. One also sees that
\mathbb{E}_{z}(\sigma_{0})=\mathbb{E}_{z}(J)<\infty
if and only if
\int^{\infty}\log(y)\pi(\mathrm{d}y)<\infty
, since the log-moment is necessary and sufficient for
R
to be positive recurrent, see
[
Sat13
, Chapter 3.17]
.
\Box

Study of auxiliary diffusions

A striking feature of the LCBs is that they satisfy some duality relationships with certain

[

[

[

We will briefly reexplain them in the setting of a non-exploding LCB which gets extinct a.s., see the forthcoming section

3.3.1.
Laplace and Siegmund dualities

Define

for any

.

Lemma 2

(Lemma 5.1 in
[
Fou19
]
)
.

One has

(20)

\mathscr{L}e_{x}(z)=\mathscr{A}e_{z}(x),

with

and

\mathscr{A}g(x):=\frac{c}{2}xg^{\prime\prime}(x)-\Psi(x)g^{\prime}(x).

Moreover, under

\mathrm{d}U_{t}=\sqrt{cU_{t}}\mathrm{d}B_{t}-\Psi(U_{t})\mathrm{d}t,\ U_{0}=x.

Lemma 3

(Lemma 5.2 in
[
Fou19
]
)
.

Assume

. Then, the boundary
0
of

is an exit (i.e.

hits

with positive probability and stays there).

The process

. We shall refer to it as the

.

Lemma 4

(Proposition 2.1 in
[

]
)
.

Assume

. The Siegmund dual process

of

\mathrm{d}V_{t}=\sqrt{cV_{t}}\mathrm{d}B_{t}+\big(c/2+\Psi(V_{t})\big)\mathrm{d}t,\quad V_{0}=y.

acts on

\mathscr{G}f(x):=\frac{c}{2}xf^{\prime\prime}(x)+(c/2+\Psi(x))f^{\prime}(x).

The classification of the boundaries of

]

3.3.2.
Study of the scale function and speed measure of
V

The speed measure of
V
is given by

(27)

\displaystyle m(\mathrm{d}y)

\displaystyle=m(y)\mathrm{d}y:=e^{\int_{x_{0}}^{y}\frac{2\Psi(u)}{cu}\mathrm{d}u}\mathrm{d}y.

Since under the assumption
\mathbb{H}
,
\infty
is an attracting boundary for
V
, any scale function of
V
is finite at
\infty
. Denote by
S
the scale function of
V
such that
S(\infty)=0
. Recall that this is the unique positive solution to
\mathscr{G}S=0
, vanishing at
\infty
, up to a multiplicative constant.

One has

(28)

S(x):=\int_{x}^{\infty}\frac{1}{y}e^{-\int_{x_{0}}^{y}\frac{2\Psi(u)}{cu}\mathrm{d}u}\mathrm{d}y=\int_{x}^{\infty}\frac{1}{ym(y)}\mathrm{d}y,\quad\forall x>0.

We stress that
\mathcal{E}=\infty
is equivalent to
S(0)=\infty
. Recall the measure
s
defined in (
10
). We have
s(\mathrm{d}x)=-S^{\prime}(x)\mathrm{d}x=\frac{1}{x}e^{-\int_{x_{0}}^{x}\frac{2\Psi(u)}{cu}\mathrm{d}u}\mathrm{d}x
. Moreover, the tail of
s
is
S
, i.e.
\int_{x}^{\infty}s(\mathrm{d}y)=S(x)
,
\forall x\geq 0
.

We gather in the next technical lemma some properties of the scale function and speed measure that will be useful for our study.

Lemma 5
.

Assume
\mathbb{H}
. The following holds:

i)

(29)

\displaystyle\int_{0+}S(x)m(x)\mathrm{d}x<\infty,

ii)

(30)

x(-S)^{\prime}(x)\underset{x\rightarrow 0}{\longrightarrow}\ell:=\begin{cases}e^{\int_{0}^{x_{0}}\frac{2\Psi(u)}{cu}\mathrm{d}u}\in(0,\infty)&\text{ if }\int^{\infty}\log y\,\pi(\mathrm{d}y)<\infty,\\
0\,&\text{ if }\int^{\infty}\log y\,\pi(\mathrm{d}y)=\infty,\end{cases}

iii)

(31)

\int_{0}^{\infty}x(-S)^{\prime}(x)\mathrm{d}x<\infty.

Proof.

i)

Notice that
\Psi(x)\longrightarrow\Psi(0)=0
, as
x\rightarrow 0
, by continuity of
\Psi
. Hence, by Karamata’s representation theorem, see
[
BGT89
, Theorem 1.3.1, page 13]
, both functions
m:y\mapsto e^{-\int_{y}^{x_{0}}\frac{2}{c}\frac{\Psi(u)}{u}\mathrm{d}u}
and
1/m
are slowly varying at
0
. By
[
BGT89
, Proposition 1.5.9a, page 26]
, this is also the case for
S:x\mapsto\int_{x}^{x_{0}}\frac{\mathrm{d}y}{y}\frac{1}{m(y)}
. The slow variation property of
S
ensures that for any
\eta>0
,
x^{\eta}S(x)\underset{x\rightarrow 0}{\rightarrow}0
, see
[
BGT89
, Proposition 1.3.6, page 16]
. Let
\delta\in(0,1)
. By choosing
\eta>0
such that
\eta+\delta<1
, we see that
\int_{0+}x^{-\delta}S(x)\mathrm{d}x=\int_{0+}x^{-(\delta+\eta)}x^{\eta}S(x)\mathrm{d}x<\infty
. Similarly, the slow variation of
m
ensures that
x^{\delta}m(x)\underset{x\rightarrow 0}{\longrightarrow}0
and we get

\int_{0+}S(x)m(x)\mathrm{d}x=\int_{0+}S(x)x^{-\delta}x^{\delta}m(x)\mathrm{d}x<\infty
,

which establishes (
29
).
Notice that the assumption
\mathcal{E}=\infty
was not used here, only the fact that
\Psi(0)=0
.

ii)

We now establish (
30
). One has
-S^{\prime}(x)=\frac{1}{x}\exp\left(-\int_{x_{0}}^{x}\frac{2\Psi(u)}{cu}\mathrm{d}u\right)
, thus

(32)

x(-S)^{\prime}(x)=\exp\left(\int_{x}^{x_{0}}\frac{2\Psi(u)}{cu}\mathrm{d}u\right)=1/m(x).

Since, as
x
goes to
0
,
\int_{x}^{x_{0}}\frac{\Psi(u)}{u}\mathrm{d}u
either tends to
-\infty
or has a finite limit, the function
x\mapsto x(-S)^{\prime}(x)
converges as
x
goes to
0
towards the finite nonnegative limit
\exp\left(\int_{0}^{x_{0}}\frac{2\Psi(u)}{cu}\mathrm{d}u\right)
, the latter is
0
if and only if
\int_{0}^{x_{0}}\frac{\Psi(u)}{u}\mathrm{d}u=-\infty
which is equivalent to
\int^{\infty}\log y\,\pi(\mathrm{d}y)=\infty
.

iii)

The fact that
x(-S)^{\prime}(x)
admits a finite limit at
0
ensures that
\int_{0+}x(-S)^{\prime}(x)\mathrm{d}x<\infty
. We now check that
\int^{\infty}x(-S)^{\prime}(x)\mathrm{d}x<\infty
. Recall that by assumption
\Psi(\infty)=\infty
. Without loss of generality, assume that
x_{0}
is large enough so that
\Psi(x_{0})>0
. The Lévy-Khintchine form of
\Psi
, see (
2
), and the convexity it induces, ensures that
\Psi(u)/u
is non-decreasing and positive on
[x_{0},\infty)
. Hence
0<b:=\Psi(x_{0})/x_{0}\leq\Psi(u)/u
, for all
u\geq x_{0}
, and by (
32
), one has, for some constant
C>0
, the bound
x(-S)^{\prime}(x)\leq Ce^{-\frac{2b}{c}x}
, which is integrable near
\infty
.

\Box

3.3.3.
The diffusion

We now introduce a certain Doob’s transform of the process

. We refer the reader to Borodin and Salminen’s book
[

, see also Salminen
[

Recall

-local martingale, see e.g.

\mathbb{P}_{x}^{\downarrow}

,

, by Assumption
\mathbb{H}

\mathbb{P}_{x}^{\downarrow}\big(A,T<\sigma\big)=\frac{1}{S(x)}\mathbb{E}_{x}\big(S(V_{T})\mathbbm{1}_{A}\big).

Lemma B
.

For all

,
\sigma=T_{0}:=\inf\{t>0:V_{t}=0\}
,
\mathbb{P}_{x}^{\downarrow}
-almost surely.

Proof.

, see also Remark
10
. Define the sequence of stopping times

and

\mathbb{P}_{x}
-a.s. one has
\sigma_{n}\underset{n\rightarrow\infty}{\longrightarrow}\infty

\mathbb{P}_{x}
-a.s. Furthermore, the map

By the same arguments as in Lemma

.
\Box

The next result, which holds for general one-dimensional diffusions, see for instance
[

, characterizes the law of the process

Proposition A
.

Assume

\displaystyle m^{\downarrow}(\mathrm{d}y)

\displaystyle=S(y)^{2}m(\mathrm{d}y),

(35)

Proof.

The speed measure and the scale function of the process

Remark 13
.

The process
(V,\mathbb{P}_{y}^{\downarrow})
can be seen as the diffusion

[

The following lemma will be crucial in the study of the LCB process.

Lemma 6
.

Assume
\mathbb{H}
. For all

, the process
(V,\mathbb{P}_{x}^{\downarrow})
has almost surely a finite lifetime, namely it hits

,
\mathbb{P}_{x}^{\downarrow}
-a.s.. Moreover,
\mathbb{P}^{\downarrow}_{x}(T_{0}>t)=\mathbb{E}_{x}\left(\frac{S(V_{t})}{S(x)}\right)

Proof.

Since

[
BSW14
, Theorem 2.21]
, namely:

\mathscr{L}f(z)=\mathscr{A}f(z)+\mathscr{J}f(z),

with
\mathscr{A}
the local part and
\mathscr{J}
the jump part, that is to say

\mathscr{A}f(z):=a(z)f^{\prime\prime}(z)+b(z)f^{\prime}(z)\text{ and }\mathscr{J}f(z):=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(f(z+y)-f(z)-yf^{\prime}(z)\mathbbm{1}_{\{y<1\}}\right)\nu(z,\mathrm{d}y),

for
a,b
two functions (
a
is nonnegative) and some Lévy kernel
(\nu(z,\mathrm{d}y),z\in\mathbb{R})
such that for any
z\in\mathbb{R}
,
\nu(z,\{0\})=0
and
\int_{\mathbb{R}}(1\wedge y^{2})\nu(z,\mathrm{d}y)<\infty
.

The statement below is not new, see for instance Kunita
[
Kun69
]
. We provide a quick proof.

Lemma 24
.

Let
h
and
f
be such that
hf\in C^{2}([0,\infty))
and
\int^{\infty}|f(y)h(y)|\pi(\mathrm{d}y)<\infty
. For any
z>0
,

\mathscr{L}^{\uparrow}f(z)=\mathscr{L}f(z)+\mathscr{B}f(z),

with

\displaystyle\mathscr{B}f(z)

\displaystyle:=\frac{\mathscr{L}(hf)(z)-h(z)\mathscr{L}f(z)}{h(z)}

(72)

\displaystyle=a(z)\frac{h^{\prime}(z)}{h(z)}f^{\prime}(z)+\int_{\mathbb{R}}\big(f(z+y)-f(z)\big)\frac{h(z+y)-h(z)}{h(z)}\nu(z,\mathrm{d}y)+\frac{\mathscr{L}h(z)}{h(z)}f(z).

Proof.

We compute
\mathscr{L}fh(z)
. We first consider the local part. One has

\displaystyle\mathscr{A}fh(z)

\displaystyle=b(z)\big(h^{\prime}(z)f(z)+h(z)f^{\prime}(z)\big)+\frac{a(z)}{2}\big(h^{\prime\prime}(z)f(z)+2h^{\prime}(z)f^{\prime}(z)+h(z)f^{\prime\prime}(z)\big)

(73)

\displaystyle=h(z)\mathscr{A}f(z)+f(z)\mathscr{A}h(z)+a(z)h^{\prime}(z)f^{\prime}(z).

For the jump part, one can plainly check the following identity:

\displaystyle f(z+y)h(z+y)-f(z)h(z)

\displaystyle-y(fh)^{\prime}(y)\mathbbm{1}_{\{y<1\}}

\displaystyle=h(z)\big(f(z+y)-f(z)-yf^{\prime}(z)\mathbbm{1}_{\{y<1\}}\big)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\big(h(z+y)-h(z)\big)\big(f(z+y)-f(z)\big)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad+f(z)\big(h(z+y)-h(z)-yh^{\prime}(z)\mathbbm{1}_{\{y<1\}}\big).

By integrating with respect to
\nu(z,\mathrm{d}y)
both sides of the equality above, we get

(74)

\mathscr{J}fh(z)=h(z)\mathscr{J}f(z)+\int_{0}^{\infty}\big(h(z+y)-h(z)\big)\big(f(z+y)-f(z)\big)\nu(z,\mathrm{d}y)+f(z)\mathscr{J}h(z).

Finally, we obtain by adding (
73
) and (
74
),

\frac{\mathscr{L}fh(z)}{h(z)}=\frac{1}{h(z)}\left(\mathscr{A}fh(z)+\mathscr{J}fh(z)\right)=\mathscr{A}f(z)+\mathscr{J}f(z)+\mathscr{B}f(z)=\mathscr{L}f(z)+\mathscr{B}f(z).

\Box

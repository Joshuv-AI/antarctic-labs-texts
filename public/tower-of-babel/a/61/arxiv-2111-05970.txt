Core size of a random partition for the Plancherel measure
Salim Rostam

Abstract
We prove that the size of the e e -core of a partition chosen under the Poissonised Plancherel measure converges in distribution to, as the Poisson parameter goes to + ∞ +\infty and after a suitable renormalisation, a sum of e − 1 e-1 mutually independent Gamma distributions with explicit parameters. Such a result already exists for the uniform measure on the set of partitions of n n as n n goes to + ∞ +\infty , the parameters of the Gamma distributions being all equal. We rely on the fact that the descent set of a partition is a determinantal point process under the Poissonised Plancherel measure and on a central limit theorem for such processes.

Introduction

The conjugacy classes of the symmetric group
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
on
n
letters are parametrised by the set
\mathcal{P}_{n}
of
partitions
of the integer
n
. These are the sequences
\lambda=(\lambda_{1}\geq\lambda_{2}\geq\dots)
of positive integers with sum
|\lambda|\coloneqq n
.
In particular, the irreducible complex representations
S^{\lambda}
of
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
can be parametrised by
\lambda\in\mathcal{P}_{n}
. It turns out that one can do this parametrisation such that the dimension of
S^{\lambda}
as a
\mathbb{C}
-vector space is the number
\#\mathrm{Std}(\lambda)
of
standard Young tableaux
of shape
\lambda
, so that one has the formula

n!=\sum_{\lambda\in\mathcal{P}_{n}}\#\mathrm{Std}(\lambda)^{2}

(1.1)

(see, for instance,
[
Sa
]
).
Quotienting by
n!
, the above equality exhibits a particular probability measure
\mathrm{Pl}_{n}
on
\mathcal{P}_{n}
, known as the
Plancherel measure
. The equality (
1.1
) can also be seen as a consequence of the Robinson–Schensted correspondence between permutations in
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
and pairs of standard Young tableaux of the same shape with
n
boxes, for which the Plancherel measure
\mathrm{Pl}_{n}
is then the image of the uniform measure of
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
. This correspondence has many other additional properties, namely: if
\lambda=(\lambda_{1}\geq\dots)\in\mathcal{P}_{n}
corresponds to
\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}
, then the first part
\lambda_{1}
is exactly the length of a longest increasing subsequence in
\sigma
(see, for instance,
[
Rom
,
Sa
]
for more details).

A natural question is the following: what does the probability measure
\mathrm{Pl}_{n}
on
\mathcal{P}_{n}
look like? The question for an arbitrary
n
is quite complicated; however there is a very nice description as
n\to+\infty
. Namely, Logan–Shepp
[
LoSh
]
and independently Kerov–Vershik
[
KeVe
]
proved that there exists a universal limit shape
\Omega
for the partitions chosen under
\mathrm{Pl}_{n}
. More precisely, the rim of a partition
\lambda\in\mathcal{P}_{n}
converges uniformly in probability under
\mathrm{Pl}_{n}
to the limit shape
\Omega
as
n\to+\infty
. We represent in Figure
1
such a large partition, drawn with the Russian convention for the Young diagram, together with the limit shape

. Note that there exist limit shapes for other probability measures on

We now focus on the Plancherel measure
\mathcal{P}_{n}
. The derivative of the limit shape
\Omega
represents the proportion of “up” and “down” steps made by the Young diagram at a given position. The “down” steps correspond to the elements of the
descent set

\mathcal{D}(\lambda)\coloneqq\{\lambda_{a}-a:a\geq 1\}\subseteq\mathbb{Z}.

Now if we look at several positions at the same time, for a given (finite) set
X\subseteq\mathbb{Z}
we are interested in
\mathrm{Pl}_{n}\bigl(X\subseteq\mathcal{D}(\lambda)\bigr)
. These quantities as functions of
X
are known as
correlation functions
. It turns out that these correlation functions are well-understood for the
Poissonised
Plancherel measures on
\mathcal{P}=\amalg_{n\geq 0}\mathcal{P}_{n}
. Given
t>0
, the Poissonised Plancherel measure
\mathrm{pl}_{t}
is the Plancherel measure
\mathrm{Pl}_{n}
where
n
is a Poisson random variable of parameter
t
. In other words, setting
\mathrm{Pl}_{n}(\lambda)=0
if
|\lambda|\neq n
, for any
\lambda\in\mathcal{P}
we have

\mathrm{pl}_{t}(\lambda)=\exp(-t)\sum_{n=0}^{+\infty}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}\mathrm{Pl}_{n}(\lambda).

(We denote by
\exp
the exponential function, to avoid any ambiguity with an integer
e\geq 2
that we will intensively use in the sequel.) Borodin, Okounkov and Olshanski
[
BOO
]
proved that for any fixed
s\geq 1
and
x_{1},\dots,x_{s}\in\mathbb{Z}
pairwise distinct, we have

\mathrm{pl}_{t}\bigl(x_{1},\dots,x_{s}\in\mathcal{D}(\lambda)\bigr)=\det\bigl[\mathsf{J}^{t}(x_{a},x_{b})\bigr]_{1\leq a,b\leq s},

(1.2)

where
\mathsf{J}^{t}
is the
discrete Bessel kernel
, built with Bessel functions of the first kind. This means that the discrete point process
\mathcal{D}(\lambda)
is
determinantal
, with kernel
\mathsf{J}^{t}
. Under some conditions on
x_{1},\dots,x_{s}
, they were able to
de-Poissonise
the result, proving that the limit of
\mathrm{Pl}_{n}\bigl(x_{1},\dots,x_{s}\in\mathcal{D}(\lambda)\bigr)
as
n\to+\infty
satisfies an equality like (
1.2
), where the discrete Bessel kernel
\mathsf{J}^{t}
is replaced by the
discrete sine kernel
.

Determinantal point processes are well-studied and appear for instance in the theory of random matrices and in mathematical physics (see, for instance,
[
So00-a
, §2]
). Given a determinantal point process, for each
t>0
let
I_{t}\subseteq\mathbb{R}
be an interval and
\#_{t}
the number of points of the determinantal point process inside
I_{t}
. Then under some conditions we have the following central limit theorem:

\frac{\#_{t}-\mathbb{E}\#_{t}}{\sqrt{\mathrm{Var}\#_{t}}}\xrightarrow[t\to+\infty]{d}\mathcal{N}(0,1),

where
\mathcal{N}(0,1)
is the standard Gaussian distribution. This convergence in distribution was first established by Costin–Lebowitz
[
CoLe
]
for the sine kernel, and was then generalised for more general kernels by Widom and Soshnikov
[
So00-b
,
So00-a
]
. In the context of the determinantal point process
\mathcal{D}(\lambda)
, this central limit theorem was namely used by Bogachev and Su
[
BoSu-a
,
BoSu-b
]
to prove a pointwise analogue of Kerov’s central limit theorem
[
Ke
]
.

Let
\lambda=(\lambda_{1}\geq\dots\geq\lambda_{N}>0)
be a partition.
We will now recall a particularly nice link between the descent set
\mathcal{D}(\lambda)
and the “(rim) hook removal operation” in the Young diagram of
\lambda
(we refer, for instance, to
[
JaKe
, §2.7]
for more details).
We identify the Young diagram
\mathcal{Y}(\lambda)
with the corresponding part of
\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}^{2}
, more precisely:

\mathcal{Y}(\lambda)\coloneqq\bigl\{(a,b)\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}^{2}:1\leq a\leq N\text{ and }1\leq b\leq\lambda_{a}\bigr\}.

Given a node
(a,b)\in\mathcal{Y}(\lambda)
of the Young diagram, let us define two subsets of
\mathcal{Y}(\lambda)
:

—

the
hook

h_{(a,b)}
is given by:

h_{(a,b)}\coloneqq\bigl\{(a^{\prime},b^{\prime})\in\mathcal{Y}(\lambda):(a^{\prime}=a\text{ and }b^{\prime}\geq b)\text{ or }(a^{\prime}\geq a\text{ and }b^{\prime}=b)\bigr\},

—

the
rim hook

r_{(a,b)}
is the subset of the rim of
\mathcal{Y}(\lambda)
given by the nodes that are between the southmost and eastmost nodes of
h_{(a,b)}
, that is:

r_{(a,b)}\coloneqq\bigl\{(a^{\prime},b^{\prime})\in\mathcal{Y}(\lambda):a^{\prime}\geq a,\,b^{\prime}\geq b\text{ and }(a^{\prime}+1,b^{\prime}+1)\notin\mathcal{Y}(\lambda)\bigr\}.

An example of a hook and its associated rim hook is given in Figure
2
. Note that we always have
\#h_{(a,b)}=\#r_{(a,b)}
.
Removing the hook
h_{(a,b)}
from
\mathcal{Y}(\lambda)
and then patching the two (possibly one) disconnect parts together gives another Young diagram, of a partition
\mu
. Note that
\mathcal{Y}(\mu)
is also obtained from
\mathcal{Y}(\lambda)
by removing the rim hook
r_{(a,b)}
. By definition of the hook removal operation, the following property holds:

if
h\coloneqq\#r_{(a,b)}
then there exists
\beta\in\mathcal{D}(\lambda)
with
\beta-h\notin\mathcal{D}(\lambda)

such that
\mathcal{D}(\mu)=\bigl(\mathcal{D}(\lambda)\setminus\{\beta\}\bigr)\sqcup\{\beta-h\}
.

A rim hook with
\#r_{(a,b)}=h
is called a
h
-rim hook
.

Now fix
e\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 2}
. We are ready to define the notion of
e
-core
of a partition (again, we refer to
[
JaKe
, §2.7]
for more details). Starting from (the Young diagram of)
\lambda
we can repeatedly remove
e
-rim hooks until there are no more
e
-rim hooks. By the above property, the following two quantities are uniquely determined:

—

the number
\mathrm{w}_{e}(\lambda)
of
e
-rim hooks that we have removed,

—

the final partition
\overline{\lambda}
.

The integer
\mathrm{w}_{e}(\lambda)
is the
e
-weight
of
\lambda
and
\overline{\lambda}
is the
e
-core
of
\lambda
. We show in Figure
3
an example of an
8
-core calculation. Saying that two partitions
\lambda,\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{n}
have the same
e
-core is equivalent to saying that the Young diagrams of
\lambda
and
\mu
have the same number
c_{i}(\lambda)
of
i
-nodes
for all
i\in\mathbb{Z}/e\mathbb{Z}
. It is also equivalent to
\lambda
and
\mu
belonging to the same block of a certain Iwahori–Hecke algebra (see, for instance,
[
Ma
, Chapter 5]
).

If a limit shape exists for a given probability measure on
\mathcal{P}_{n}
for
n\to+\infty
, it is thus natural to ask what is the behaviour of
\overline{\lambda}
as
n\to+\infty
. When
\mathcal{P}_{n}
is endowed with the uniform measure, Lulov–Pittel
[
LuPi
]
and later Ayyer–Sinha
[
AySi
]
proved that
\frac{\pi}{\sqrt{n}}|\overline{\lambda}|
converges in distribution to the Gamma distribution
\Gamma\bigl(\frac{e-1}{2},\sqrt{6}\bigr)
with shape
\frac{e-1}{2}
and scale
\sqrt{6}
.
In this paper, we study the behaviour of
|\overline{\lambda}|
as
t\to+\infty
when
\lambda
is chosen under the Poissonised Plancherel measure
\mathrm{pl}_{t}
. Note that the study of
|\overline{\lambda}|
is a first step toward a better understanding of
\overline{\lambda}
, namely, the behaviour of its parts (see Remark
5.7
for the size of the first part) or the behaviour of its rim (as in
[
LuPi
]
). As in
[
LuPi
,
AySi
]
we rely on the following identity:

|\overline{\lambda}|=\frac{e}{2}\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}/e\mathbb{Z}}x_{i}(\lambda)^{2}+\sum_{i=0}^{e-1}ix_{i}(\lambda),

where
x_{i}(\lambda)\coloneqq c_{i}(\lambda)-c_{i+1}(\lambda)
.
Now the works of
[
LuPi
,
AySi
]
both rely on the asymptotics of Hardy–Ramanujan for the partition function
\#\mathcal{P}_{n}
. For our means, as in
[
BoSu-a
,
BoSu-b
]
we heavily rely on the fact (
1.2
) that
\mathcal{D}(\lambda)
is determinantal
[
BOO
]
and we then exploit the first order asymptotics of Bessel functions. Our first result is the following (see Proposition
4.13
and Theorem
4.53
), where
\mathbb{E}_{t}
,
\mathrm{Var}_{t}
and
\mathrm{Cov}_{t}
respectively denote the expectation, variance and covariance with respect to
\mathrm{pl}_{t}
.

Theorem A
.

Under the Poissonised Plancherel measure
\mathrm{pl}_{t}
, as
t\to+\infty
we have:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{t}x_{i}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=O(1),

\displaystyle\mathrm{Cov}_{t}\bigl(x_{i}(\lambda),x_{j}(\lambda)\bigr)

\displaystyle\sim\frac{2\sqrt{t}}{\pi e^{2}}\left[\cot(j-i+{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}})\tfrac{\pi}{e}-\cot(j-i-{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}})\tfrac{\pi}{e}\right],

for all
i,j\in\mathbb{Z}/e\mathbb{Z}
. In particular, we have
\mathrm{Var}_{t}x_{i}(\lambda)\sim\frac{4\sqrt{t}}{\pi e^{2}}\cot\frac{\pi}{2e}
.

We can already deduce the following asymptotics (Proposition
5.1
):

\mathbb{E}_{t}|\overline{\lambda}|\sim\frac{2\sqrt{t}}{\pi}\cot\frac{\pi}{2e},

as
t\to+\infty
. To compare with
[
LuPi
,
AySi
]
, when
\lambda
in chosen under the uniform measure on
\mathcal{P}_{n}
then
\mathbb{E}|\overline{\lambda}|\sim\frac{e-1}{2\pi}\sqrt{6n}
as
n\to+\infty
.
We then show that we can apply the central limit theorem
[
So00-b
]
with the variables
x_{i}(\lambda)
for
i\in\mathbb{Z}/e\mathbb{Z}
(Theorem
5.5
).

Theorem B
.

The random vector

e\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2}}\left(\frac{x_{i}(\lambda)}{t^{1/4}}\right)_{i\in\mathbb{Z}/e\mathbb{Z}},

converges in distribution as
t\to+\infty
to the centred normal distribution with covariance matrix
\bigl(\cot(j-i+{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}})\tfrac{\pi}{e}-\cot(j-i-{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}})\tfrac{\pi}{e}\bigr)_{i,j\in\mathbb{Z}/e\mathbb{Z}}
.

In particular, as
t\to+\infty
the variables
t^{-1/4}x_{i}(\lambda)
and
t^{-1/4}x_{j}(\lambda)
for
i\neq j
are not independent. We show in fact that the variables
\sum_{i=0}^{e-1}\zeta^{i}t^{-1/4}x_{i}(\lambda)
for
\zeta\neq 1
a complex
e
-th root of unity are (Gaussian and) mutually independent as
t\to+\infty
.
We then deduce our main result, Theorem
5.10
.

Theorem C
.

Under the Poissonised Plancherel measure
\mathrm{pl}_{t}
, as
t\to+\infty
the rescaled size
\frac{\pi}{4\sqrt{t}}|\overline{\lambda}|
of the
e
-core of
\lambda
converges in distribution to a sum of mutually independent Gamma distributions
\Gamma\bigl(\frac{1}{2},\sin\frac{k\pi}{e}\bigr)
for
k\in\{1,\dots,e-1\}
.

The difference between our main theorem and the result of
[
LuPi
,
AySi
]
is twofold:

—

our convergence is for the Poissonised Plancherel measure
\mathrm{pl}_{t}
on
\mathcal{P}
as
t\to+\infty
, and the not for the uniform measure on
\mathcal{P}_{n}
as
n\to+\infty
,

—

the Gamma distribution
\Gamma\bigl(\frac{e-1}{2},\sqrt{6}\bigr)=\sum_{k=1}^{e-1}\Gamma\bigl(\frac{1}{2},\sqrt{6}\bigr)
is for us split into
e-1
summands with different scale parameters.

Note that the order
\sqrt{t}
of
|\overline{\lambda}|
corresponds to the order
\sqrt{n}
of
[
LuPi
,
AySi
]
, at it was already the case for the expectation. As in
[
BOO
,
Jo
]
it should also be possible to state Theorem
C
for the Plancherel measure
\mathrm{Pl}_{n}
for
n\to+\infty
. This process, the so-called
de-Poissonisation
, will not be considered here. Note that, as it is mentioned in
[
CoLe
,
So00-b
]
, the convergence in the central limit theorem holds in fact in moments, so that we deduce

\mathrm{Var}_{t}|\overline{\lambda}|\sim\frac{4et}{\pi^{2}},

as
t\to+\infty
(Corollary
5.13
).

Let us conclude with two variations on our problem. First, as we already mentioned, the Plancherel measure
on partitions is connected with other probabilistic objects such as longest subsequences or
random matrices. To the author’s knowledge, it is not known whether the
e
-core of partitions under
the Plancherel measure behaves well with these connections.
Second, besides the asymptotic behaviour of the
e
-core under the (Poissonised) Plancherel measure, one can also study the
e
-quotient
(see, for instance,
[
JaKe
, §2.7]
). The
e
-quotient of a partition is an
e
-tuple of partitions, whose construction is complementary to the
e
-core of the partition; namely, the data of the
e
-core and the
e
-quotient allows to recover the (unique) underlying partition. The same questions as for the
e
-core can be studied (
e.g.
size and size of the parts) but it should also be interesting to study the behaviour of the descent sets, namely, whether they are determinantal.

We now give the outline of the paper. In Section
2
we recall the result from Borodin-Okounkov-Olshanski saying that
\mathcal{D}(\lambda)
is a determinantal point process under
\mathrm{pl}_{t}
.
Section
3
is devoted to the combinatorics of
e
-cores. In Definition
3.6
we define the variables
x_{i}(\lambda)\coloneqq c_{i}(\lambda)-c_{i+1}(\lambda)
, which we relate in (
3.12
) to
|\overline{\lambda}|
by a quadratic polynomial and in Lemma
3.13
to
\mathcal{D}(\lambda)
in a view to applying the central limit theorem of Costin–Lebowitz and Soshnikov for determinantal point process.
Section
4
is devoted to expectation and covariance calculations. In §
4.1
we show how we will apply the central limit theorem to
x_{i}(\lambda)
.
In §
4.2
we prove that the expectation
\mathbb{E}_{t}x_{i}(\lambda)
of
x_{i}(\lambda)
under the Poissonised Plancherel measure
\mathrm{pl}_{t}
is bounded (Proposition
4.13
).
Subsection §
4.4
is the technical heart of the paper. We compute the first order asymptotics of the covariance
\mathrm{Cov}_{t}\bigl(x_{i}(\lambda),x_{j}(\lambda)\bigr)
under
\mathrm{pl}_{t}
for
i\neq j\in\mathbb{Z}/e\mathbb{Z}
(Theorem
4.53
). We deduce in §
4.5
the first order asymptotics of the variance
\mathrm{Var}_{t}x_{i}(\lambda)
under
\mathrm{pl}_{t}
(Corollary
4.55
). In Section
5
we apply the preceding results to study the asymptotics of
|\overline{\lambda}|
when
\lambda
is chosen under the Poissonised Plancherel measure
\mathrm{pl}_{t}
as
t\to+\infty
. We first obtain in Proposition
5.1
the asymptotics of the expectation
\mathbb{E}_{t}|\overline{\lambda}|
. Using the central limit theorem of Costin–Lebowitz and Soshnikov, we obtain in Theorem
5.5
that the random vector
\bigl(x_{i}(\lambda)\bigr)_{i\in\mathbb{Z}/e\mathbb{Z}}
converges in distribution to a certain centred normal vector. Then using a contour integration to compute the eigenvalues of the covariance matrix, we deduce our main result, Theorem

, which says that
|\overline{\lambda}|/\sqrt{t}
converges in distribution under
\mathrm{pl}_{t}
as

to a sum of squares of mutually independent centred Gaussian variables, that is, a sum of mutually independent Gamma variables of shape
\frac{1}{2}
. Since the convergence in the central limit theorem holds in moments, we deduce in Corollary

\mathrm{Var}_{t}|\overline{\lambda}|
.

6

(Proposition

Setting

Let
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
.

2.1
Partitions

Partitions

A
partition
of
n
is a non-increasing sequence
\lambda=(\lambda_{1}\geq\dots\geq\lambda_{h(\lambda)}>0)
of positive integers with sum
|\lambda|\coloneqq\sum_{a=1}^{h(\lambda)}\lambda_{a}=n
, where
h(\lambda)\geq 0
. We denote by
\mathcal{P}_{n}
the set of partitions of
n
and
\mathcal{P}\coloneqq\sqcup_{n\geq 0}\mathcal{P}_{n}
. The
conjugate
of
\lambda
, denoted by
\lambda^{\prime}
, is the partition given by
\lambda^{\prime}_{a}=\#\{b\geq 1:\lambda_{b}\geq a\}
for all
a\in\{1,\dots,\lambda_{1}\}
.

Young diagrams

The
Young diagram
of
\lambda\in\mathcal{P}_{n}
is the subset
\mathcal{Y}(\lambda)
of
\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}^{2}
given by

\mathcal{Y}(\lambda)\coloneqq\left\{(a,b)\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}^{2}:1\leq a\leq h(\lambda)\text{ and }1\leq b\leq\lambda_{a}\right\}.

.

(4,3,2,2)

.

The Young diagram of
\lambda^{\prime}
is obtained by flipping
\mathcal{Y}(\lambda)
with respect to the diagonal, in other words, we have
(a,b)\in\mathcal{Y}(\lambda^{\prime})\iff(b,a)\in\mathcal{Y}(\lambda)
.

A
standard tableau of shape
\lambda
is a bijection
\mathfrak{t}:\mathcal{Y}(\lambda)\to\{1,\dots,n\}
such that
\mathfrak{t}
increases along the rows and down the columns, in other words for
(a,b)\in\mathcal{Y}(\lambda)
we have
\mathfrak{t}(a,b)<\mathfrak{t}(a+1,b)
if
(a+1,b)\in\mathcal{Y}(\lambda)
and
\mathfrak{t}(a,b)<\mathfrak{t}(a,b+1)
if
(a,b+1)\in\mathcal{Y}(\lambda)
. We denote by
\mathrm{Std}(\lambda)
the set of standard Young tableaux of shape
\lambda
. Note that
\mathrm{Std}(\lambda)
and
\mathrm{Std}(\lambda^{\prime})
are naturally in bijection.

Russian convention

Rotating the Young diagram of
\lambda^{\prime}
by an angle of
\frac{3\pi}{4}
and applying a linear homothety of ratio
\sqrt{2}
gives the
Russian convention
for the Young diagram of
\lambda
. Note that the node
(a,b)\in\mathcal{Y}(\lambda)
corresponds to the (square) box with opposite vertices
(a-1,b-1)
and
(a,b)
.
We denote by
\omega_{\lambda}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
the upper rim of the resulting diagram, extending
\omega_{\lambda}
by
\omega_{\lambda}(x)\coloneqq|x|
outside the diagram.
Then
\omega_{\lambda}
is a continuous piecewise linear function such that:

—

for each
k\in\mathbb{Z}
we have

\omega^{\prime}_{\lambda}|_{(k,k+1)}=\pm 1,

(2.2)

—

we have
\omega_{\lambda}(x)=|x|
for
|x|\gg 0
(more precisely, for
x\leq-\lambda^{\prime}_{1}
or
x\geq\lambda_{1}
),

—

we have
\int_{\mathbb{R}}\bigl[\omega_{\lambda}(x)-|x|\bigr]dx=2n
, in particular each box in the Young diagram of
\lambda
in the Russian convention has area
2
and semi-diagonal length
1

Plancherel measure

Recall from the introduction the following identity:

n!=\sum_{\lambda\in\mathcal{P}_{n}}\#\mathrm{Std}(\lambda)^{2}.

(2.6)

Definition 2.7
.

The
Plancherel measure
on the set
\mathcal{P}_{n}
of the partitions of
n
is given by

\mathrm{Pl}_{n}(\lambda)\coloneqq\frac{\#\mathrm{Std}(\lambda)^{2}}{n!},

for all
\lambda\in\mathcal{P}_{n}
.

Recalling that
\lambda^{\prime}
denotes the conjugate partition of
\lambda
, we have
\#\mathrm{Std}(\lambda)=\#\mathrm{Std}(\lambda^{\prime})
thus

\mathrm{Pl}_{n}(\lambda)=\mathrm{Pl}_{n}(\lambda^{\prime}).

(2.8)

Definition 2.9
.

Let
t>0
. The
Poissonised
Plancherel measure
\mathrm{pl}_{t}
on the set
\mathcal{P}
of all partitions is given for
\lambda\in\mathcal{P}
by

\mathrm{pl}_{t}(\lambda)\coloneqq\exp(-t)t^{\lvert\lambda\rvert}\left(\frac{\#\mathrm{Std}(\lambda)}{\lvert\lambda\rvert!}\right)^{2}.

In other words, setting
\mathrm{Pl}_{n}(\lambda)\coloneqq 0
if
|\lambda|\neq n
, we have

\mathrm{pl}_{t}=\exp(-t)\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}\mathrm{Pl}_{n}.

We use the notation
\exp
for the exponential function since the notation
e
will be used for an integer later (see §
3
).
It follows from (
2.8
) that if
\Lambda
is any set of partitions and
\Lambda^{\prime}\coloneqq\{\lambda^{\prime}:\lambda\in\Lambda\}
then

\mathrm{pl}_{t}(\Lambda)=\mathrm{pl}_{t}(\Lambda^{\prime}).

(2.10)

Correlation function

Definition 2.11
.

The
correlation function

\rho^{t}
is defined for any finite subset
X\subseteq\mathbb{Z}
by

\rho^{t}(X)\coloneqq\mathrm{pl}_{t}\bigl\{\lambda\in\mathcal{P}:X\subseteq\mathcal{D}(\lambda)\bigr\}.

In the sequel, when we write
X=\{x_{1},\dots,x_{s}\}\subseteq\mathbb{Z}
then it will always be understood that
x_{a}\neq x_{b}
if
a\neq b
. In particular, we write

\rho^{t}(x_{1},\dots,x_{s})\coloneqq\rho^{t}(X).

Note that (
2.10
) gives that we also have

\rho^{t}(X)=\mathrm{pl}_{t}\left\{\lambda\in\mathcal{P}:X\subseteq\mathcal{D}(\lambda^{\prime})\right\}.

(2.12)

Lemma 2.13
.

For any
k\in\mathbb{Z}
we have

1-\rho^{t}(k)=\rho^{t}(-k-1).

Proof.

By (
2.5
) we have

\displaystyle 1-\rho^{t}(k)

\displaystyle=1-\mathrm{pl}_{t}\left(k\in\mathcal{D}(\lambda)\right)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{pl}_{t}\left(k\in\mathcal{D}(\lambda)^{c}\right)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{pl}_{t}\left(-k-1\in-\mathcal{D}(\lambda)^{c}-1\right)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{pl}_{t}\left(-k-1\in\mathcal{D}(\lambda^{\prime})\right),

and we conclude by (
2.12
).
∎

Discrete Bessel kernel

For any

, we denote by

the Bessel function of order

Definition 2.14

(
[

)
.

The

\mathsf{J}^{t}(x,y)\coloneqq\sqrt{t}\frac{{J}^{t}_{x}{J}^{t}_{y+1}-{J}^{t}_{x+1}{J}^{t}_{y}}{x-y}.

Note that the diagonal values

are well-defined since

is an entire function of

\mathsf{J}^{t}(x,x)=\sqrt{t}\left[{L}^{t}_{x}{J}^{t}_{x+1}-{J}^{t}_{x}{L}^{t}_{x+1}\right],

where

(see
[

, (3.27)]
). The next result shows that, under

is determinantal.

Theorem 2.15

(
[

, Theorem 2]
)
.

For any

we have

\rho^{t}(x_{1},\dots,x_{s})=\det\bigl[\mathsf{J}^{t}(x_{a},x_{b})\bigr]_{1\leq a,b\leq s}.

Remark 2.16
.

Under
\mathrm{Pl}_{n}
, the process

is a priori not determinantal; however
[

]
proved namely that, under some assumptions,

, where

.

3
Cores

Let
e\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 2}
. We will identify
\mathbb{Z}/e\mathbb{Z}
and
\{0,\dots,e-1\}
in the natural way. Let
\lambda\in\mathcal{P}_{n}
be a partition of
n
.

Definition 3.1
.

For any
i\in\mathbb{Z}/e\mathbb{Z}
, we define
c_{i}(\lambda)\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
to be the number of nodes
(a,b)\in\mathcal{Y}(\lambda)
of the Young diagram of
\lambda
such that
b-a\equiv i\pmod{e}
.

Such a node
(a,b)\in\mathcal{Y}(\lambda)
with
b-a\equiv i\pmod{e}
is an
i
-node
of
\mathcal{Y}(\lambda)
. Note that

\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}/e\mathbb{Z}}c_{i}(\lambda)=|\lambda|.

(3.2)

The next result is immediate from the definition of
\omega_{\lambda}
.

Lemma 3.3
.

For any
i\in\mathbb{Z}/e\mathbb{Z}
we have

c_{i}(\lambda)=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\in\mathbb{Z}\\
m=i\bmod e\end{subarray}}\omega_{\lambda}(m)-|m|=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\omega_{\lambda}(i+ke)-\lvert i+ke\rvert.

Proof.

First note that the sum is finite. Recall that, in the Russian convention, each box has diagonal
2
. Then for any
m\in\mathbb{Z}
, the number
\frac{1}{2}\left[\omega_{\lambda}(m)-|m|\right]
is (an integer and is) the number of nodes
(a,b)\in\mathcal{Y}(\lambda)
such that
b-a=m
.
∎

If
\lambda,\mu\in\mathcal{P}
are such that
c_{i}(\lambda)=c_{i}(\mu)
for all
i\in\mathbb{Z}/e\mathbb{Z}
then we do not necessarily have
\lambda=\mu
(unless if we are in the, excluded, case
e=0
). However, as we mentioned in the introduction we know that
\lambda
and
\mu
share the same
e
-core
(see Remark
3.11
for a slight generalisation). As a quick reminder, the
e
-core of
\lambda
, denoted by
\overline{\lambda}
, is constructed from
\mathcal{Y}(\lambda)
by successively removing as many
e
-rim hooks as possible, where
e
-rim hooks are certain subsets of
e
consecutive nodes of the rim of the Young diagram. The
e
-weight

\mathrm{w}_{e}(\lambda)
of
\lambda
is the number of
e
-rim hooks that we remove to reach the
e
-core
\overline{\lambda}
, in particular, we have the relation:

|\lambda|=|\overline{\lambda}|+e\mathrm{w}_{e}(\lambda).

(3.4)

More precisely, since any
e
-rim hook has exactly one
i
-node for any
i\in\mathbb{Z}/e\mathbb{Z}
(this follows from the hook removal operation; see also, for instance,
[
Ros
, Remark 2.3]
) we deduce that

c_{i}(\lambda)=c_{i}(\overline{\lambda})+\mathrm{w}_{e}(\lambda),

(3.5)

recovering (
3.4
) by (
3.2
).

Definition 3.6
.

For any
i\in\mathbb{Z}/e\mathbb{Z}
we define
x_{i}(\lambda)\coloneqq c_{i}(\lambda)-c_{i+1}(\lambda)\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Note that

\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}/e\mathbb{Z}}x_{i}(\lambda)=0.

(3.7)

Remark 3.8
.

The quantities
x_{0}(\lambda),\dots,x_{e-1}(\lambda)
can be read on the
e
-abacus
of
\overline{\lambda}
, see, for instance,
[
Ros
, Lemma 2.11]
.

The analogue of (
3.2
) is the following (non-trivial) equality, where
\lVert\cdot\rVert
denotes the Euclidean norm:

c_{0}(\overline{\lambda})=\frac{1}{2}\lVert x(\lambda)\rVert^{2}.

(3.9)

Indeed, by
[
Fa
, Proposition 2.1]
(see also
[
Ros
, Proposition 2.14]
or also
[
GKS
, Bijection 2]
) we have
c_{0}(\overline{\lambda})=\frac{1}{2}\lVert x(\overline{\lambda})\rVert^{2}
, but
x(\overline{\lambda})=x(\lambda)
by (
3.5
). Hence, for any
i\in\{1,\dots,e-1\}
we have

c_{i}(\overline{\lambda})=\frac{1}{2}\lVert x(\lambda)\rVert^{2}-x_{0}(\lambda)-\dots-x_{i-1}(\lambda).

(3.10)

Remark 3.11
.

It follows that if
\lambda,\mu\in\mathcal{P}
satisfy
x_{i}(\lambda)=x_{i}(\mu)
for all
i\in\mathbb{Z}/e\mathbb{Z}
then
c_{i}(\overline{\lambda})=c_{i}(\overline{\mu})
for all
i\in\mathbb{Z}/e\mathbb{Z}
thus
\overline{\lambda}=\overline{\mu}
.

Note the following consequence of (
3.2
), (
3.7
), (
3.9
) and (
3.10
):

\lvert\overline{\lambda}\rvert=\frac{e}{2}\lVert x(\lambda)\rVert^{2}+\sum_{i=0}^{e-1}ix_{i}(\lambda).

(3.12)

One of the aim of this paper is to study the asymptotic behaviour of
\lvert\overline{\lambda}\rvert
under the Plancherel measure. By (
3.12
), for any
i\in\mathbb{Z}/e\mathbb{Z}
we have

x_{i}(\lambda)=O\left(\sqrt{|\lambda|}\right).

We will see how to refine this asymptotics when
\lambda
is chosen under the (Poissonised) Plancherel measure, by using Theorem
2.15
(see Theorem
5.10
).

Lemma 3.13
.

For any
i\in\{0,\dots,e-1\}
we have

\displaystyle x_{i}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=\#(e\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}+i)\cap\mathcal{D}(\lambda)-\#(e\mathbb{Z}_{<0}+i)\cap\mathcal{D}(\lambda)^{c}

\displaystyle=\#(e\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}+i)\cap\mathcal{D}(\lambda)-\#(e\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}-i-1)\cap\mathcal{D}(\lambda^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\#(e\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}+i)\cap\mathcal{D}(\lambda)-\#(e\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}+i^{*})\cap\mathcal{D}(\lambda^{\prime}),

with
i^{*}\coloneqq e-1-i\in\{0,\dots,e-1\}
.

Proof.

Write
\delta_{k}\coloneqq\omega_{\lambda}(ke+i)-\omega_{\lambda}(ke+i+1)
. Recalling (
2.2
) and (
2.4
), we have:

\delta_{k}=\begin{cases}1,&\text{if }ke+i\in\mathcal{D}(\lambda),\\
-1,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

(3.14)

We have, using Lemma
3.3
and the fact that
i\in\{0,\dots,e-1\}
,

\displaystyle 2x_{i}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=2\bigl(c_{i}(\lambda)-c_{i+1}(\lambda)\bigr)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\Bigl(\omega_{\lambda}(ke+i)-\lvert ke+i\rvert\Bigr)-\Bigl(\omega_{\lambda}(ke+i+1)-\lvert ke+i+1\rvert\Bigr)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k<0}\delta_{k}-\lvert ke+i\rvert+\lvert ke+i+1\rvert+\sum_{k\geq 0}\delta_{k}-\lvert ke+i\rvert+\lvert ke+i+1\rvert

\displaystyle=\sum_{k<0}\delta_{k}+(ke+i)-(ke+i+1)+\sum_{k\geq 0}\delta_{k}-(ke+i)+(ke+i+1)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k<0}\bigl(\delta_{k}-1\bigr)+\sum_{k\geq 0}\bigl(\delta_{k}+1\bigr).

By (
3.14
), we have

\delta_{k}+1=\begin{cases}2,&\text{if }ke+i\in\mathcal{D}(\lambda),\\
0,&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

thus
\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k\geq 0}(\delta_{k}+1)=\#(e\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}+i)\cap\mathcal{D}(\lambda)
. Similarly, we have

\delta_{k}-1=\begin{cases}-2,&\text{if }ke+i\in\mathcal{D}(\lambda)^{c},\\
0,&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

thus
\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k<0}(\delta_{k}-1)=-\#(e\mathbb{Z}_{<0}+i)\cap\mathcal{D}(\lambda)^{c}
. We deduce the first equality of the lemma. By (
2.5
) we have
\#(e\mathbb{Z}_{<0}+i)\cap\mathcal{D}(\lambda)^{c}=\#(e\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}-i-1)\cap\mathcal{D}(\lambda^{\prime})
thus we deduce the last two announced equalities.
∎

4
Covariance calculations

Our aim is to evaluate
x_{i}(\lambda)
as
\lvert\lambda\rvert\to\infty
under the Poissonised Plancherel measure.

4.1
Central limit theorem

With a view to Lemma
3.13
, we will use the following central limit theorem.

Theorem 4.1

(Costin–Lebowitz
[
CoLe
]
, Soshnikov
[
So00-b
]
)
.

Let
(\mathcal{D}_{t})_{t}
be a sequence of determinantal point processes on a locally compact Polish space. Assume that each
\mathcal{D}_{t}
is associated with a Hermitian non-negative locally trace class operator in
L^{2}
. Let
\bigl\{I^{(0)}_{t},\dots,I^{(e-1)}_{t}\bigr\}_{t}
be a sequence of measurable sets, disjoints for any fixed
t
. We denote by
\mathbb{E}_{t}
,
\mathrm{Var}_{t}
and
\mathrm{Cov}_{t}
the expectation, variance and covariance with respect to the probability distribution of the random process
\mathcal{D}_{t}
. Let
\#_{t}^{(i)}
be the random variable given by the number of points of
\mathcal{D}_{t}
inside
I_{t}^{(i)}
. If
\mathrm{Var}_{t}\#_{t}^{(i)}\to+\infty
as
t\to+\infty
for all
i
and if for all
i,j

\frac{\mathrm{Cov}_{t}\left(\#_{t}^{(i)},\#_{t}^{(j)}\right)}{\sqrt{\mathrm{Var}_{t}\#_{t}^{(i)}\mathrm{Var}_{t}\#_{t}^{(j)}}}\xrightarrow{t\to+\infty}b_{ij},

for some
b_{ij}\in\mathbb{R}
then the vector

\left(\frac{\#_{t}^{(i)}-\mathbb{E}_{t}\#_{t}^{(i)}}{\sqrt{\mathrm{Var}_{t}\#_{t}^{(i)}}}\right)_{0\leq i<e}

converges in distribution to the
k
-dimensional centred normal vector with covariance matrix
(b_{ij})_{0\leq i,j<e}
.

Remark 4.2
.

Theorem
4.1
in
[
So00-b
]
has only been explicitly stated for some particular determinantal processes. However, the proof is valid in the general case (see
[
So02
, Remark 2]
and
[
So00-a
, §4]
). See also
[
BKPV
]
.

Remark 4.3
.

The discrete Bessel kernel
\mathsf{J}^{t}
satisfies the assumptions of Theorem
4.1
, see
[
BOO
, Corollary 2.10]
. In particular, we will be able to use Theorem
4.1
in our setting.

Let
i\in\{0,\dots,e-1\}
. Recall from Lemma
3.13
that
x_{i}(\lambda)=\#(e\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}+i)\cap\mathcal{D}(\lambda)-\#(e\mathbb{Z}_{<0}+i)\cap\mathcal{D}(\lambda)^{c}
. We define

\hat{x}_{i}(\lambda)\coloneqq\#(e\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}+i)\cap\mathcal{D}(\lambda)-\#(e\llbracket-t^{2},-1\rrbracket+i)\cap\mathcal{D}(\lambda)^{c}.

We have

\hat{x}_{i}(\lambda)-x_{i}(\lambda)=\#(e\mathbb{Z}_{<-t^{2}}+i)\cap\mathcal{D}(\lambda)^{c},

(4.4)

and

\displaystyle\hat{x}_{i}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=\#(e\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}+i)\cap\mathcal{D}(\lambda)-\Bigl[\#\bigl(e\llbracket-t^{2},-1\rrbracket+i\bigr)-\#\bigl(e\llbracket-t^{2},-1\rrbracket+i\bigr)\cap\mathcal{D}(\lambda)\Bigr]

\displaystyle=\#(e\mathbb{Z}_{\geq-t^{2}}+i)\cap\mathcal{D}(\lambda)-t^{2}.

(4.5)

Lemma 4.6
.

For any
0\leq i<e
and
r\geq 1
we have

\mathbb{E}_{t}\bigl|\hat{x}_{i}(\lambda)-x_{i}(\lambda)\bigr|^{r}=o\bigl(t^{-t}),

as
t\to+\infty
. In particular, the variable
\hat{x}_{i}(\lambda)-x_{i}(\lambda)
converges to
0
in distribution under
\mathrm{pl}_{t}
as
t\to+\infty
.

Proof.

Recall from (
2.5
) that
\mathcal{D}(\lambda)^{c}=-\mathcal{D}(\lambda^{\prime})-1
, so that

\displaystyle\hat{x}_{i}(\lambda)-x_{i}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=\#(e\mathbb{Z}_{<-t^{2}}+i)\cap\mathcal{D}(\lambda)^{c}

\displaystyle=\#(e\mathbb{Z}_{<-t^{2}}+i)\cap\bigl(-\mathcal{D}(\lambda^{\prime})-1\bigr)

\displaystyle=\#(e\mathbb{Z}_{>t^{2}}-i-1)\cap\mathcal{D}(\lambda^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\#(e\mathbb{Z}_{\geq t^{2}}+i^{*})\cap\mathcal{D}(\lambda^{\prime}),

where
i^{*}=e-1-i\in\{0,\dots,e-1\}
.
Hence, by (
2.10
) it suffices to prove the result for the random variable
\#(e\mathbb{Z}_{\geq t^{2}}+i^{*})\cap\mathcal{D}(\lambda)
.

We first prove that

\#(e\mathbb{Z}_{\geq t^{2}}+i^{*})\cap\mathcal{D}(\lambda)\leq\max(T-t^{2},0),

(4.7)

where
T\coloneqq\lvert\lambda\rvert
is (by definition) a Poisson random variable with parameter
t
. If
\#\bigl(e\mathbb{Z}_{\geq t^{2}}+i^{*}\bigr)\cap\mathcal{D}(\lambda)=n
with
n\geq 1
then
\mathcal{D}(\lambda)
contains an element of the form
ek+i^{*}
for
k\geq t^{2}+n-1
. Hence, we have, recalling that
i^{*}\geq 0
,
n\geq 1
and
e\geq 2
,

\lambda_{1}-1=\max\mathcal{D}(\lambda)\geq e(t^{2}+n-1)+i^{*}\geq t^{2}+n-1.

Thus, we obtain that

T\geq\lambda_{1}\geq t^{2}+n,

and thus
T-t^{2}\geq n
thus
n\leq\max\bigl(T-t^{2},0\bigr)
as desired.
This inequality is satisfied when
n=0
as well, proving (
4.7
).

We deduce from (
4.7
) that, using Cauchy–Schwarz inequality,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{t}\bigl|\#(e\mathbb{Z}_{\geq t^{2}}+i^{*})\cap\mathcal{D}(\lambda)\bigr|^{r}

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}_{t}\left[\max(T-t^{2},0)^{r}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}_{t}\bigl[\mathbf{1}_{T\geq t^{2}}(T-t^{2})^{r}\bigr]

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{\mathbb{E}_{t}\mathbf{1}_{T\geq t^{2}}}\sqrt{\mathbb{E}_{t}(T-t^{2})^{2r}}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\mathbb{P}_{t}(T\geq t^{2})}\sqrt{\mathbb{E}_{t}(T-t^{2})^{2r}}.

(4.8)

The second term in the right-hand side is the square root of a polynomial
P_{r}(t)
in
t
since
\mathbb{E}_{t}T^{k}
is a polynomial in
t
for any
k\geq 0
(we have
\mathbb{E}_{t}T^{k}=\sum_{a=0}^{k}t^{a}S_{k,a}
where
S_{k,a}
are the Stirling numbers of the second kind, see for instance
[
Ri
]
). For the first one, we use the following Chernoff-type bound, valid for
x>t
(see
[
MiUp
, Theorem 5.4]
):

\mathbb{P}_{t}(T\geq x)\leq\frac{\exp(-t)\exp(x)t^{x}}{x^{x}}=\exp\bigl(x(1+\ln t)-x\ln x-t\bigr).

For
t>1
and
x=t^{2}
we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{t}\bigl(T\geq t^{2}\bigr)

\displaystyle\leq\exp\left(t^{2}(1+\ln t)-2t^{2}\ln t-t\right)

\displaystyle\leq\exp\left(-t^{2}\ln t+t^{2}-t\right).

Hence, by (
4.8
) we deduce that

t^{t}\mathbb{E}_{t}\bigl|\#(e\mathbb{Z}_{\geq t^{2}}+i^{*})\cap\mathcal{D}(\lambda)\bigr|^{r}\leq\sqrt{P_{r}(t)}\exp\left(\frac{1}{2}\left(-t^{2}\ln t+t^{2}-t\right)+t\ln t\right)\xrightarrow{t\to+\infty}0,

whence the result.
∎

We now want to apply Theorem
4.1
with
\hat{x}_{i}(\lambda)
. We thus have to compute the asymptotics of the covariances. We will make the calculations for
x_{i}(\lambda)
and then see that it gives the desired result.

4.2
Expectation

Lemma 4.9
.

We have
\sum_{m=0}^{+\infty}\rho^{t}(m)\leq t
.

Proof.

Let
m\geq 0
. If
m\in\mathcal{D}(\lambda)
for
\lambda\in\mathcal{P}
then
m=\lambda_{a}-a
for some
a\geq 1
thus
|\lambda|\geq\lambda_{a}=m+a>m
. We deduce that

\displaystyle\sum_{m=0}^{+\infty}\rho^{t}(m)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{m=0}^{+\infty}\mathrm{pl}_{t}\bigl(m\in\mathcal{D}(\lambda)\bigr)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{m=0}^{+\infty}\mathrm{pl}_{t}\bigl(|\lambda|>m\bigr)=\mathbb{E}_{t}|\lambda|,

since
|\lambda|\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
. We thus obtain the desired inequality since
|\lambda|
is a Poisson variable with parameter
t
under
\mathrm{pl}_{t}
.
∎

Recall that
{J}^{t}_{n}=J_{n}(2\sqrt{t})
.

Proposition 4.10
.

Under

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\geq 0}\mathrm{pl}_{t}(ek+i\in\mathcal{D}(\lambda))-\sum_{k\geq 1}\mathrm{pl}_{t}(ek-i-1\in\mathcal{D}(\lambda^{\prime}))

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\geq 0}\rho^{t}(ek+i)-\sum_{k\geq 1}\rho^{t}(ek-i-1).

Note that each sum above is finite by Lemma

Using Lemma

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{t}x_{i}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\geq 0}\sum_{s\geq 1}J_{ek+i+s}^{t,2}-\sum_{k\geq 1}\sum_{s\geq 1}J_{ek-i-1+s}^{t,2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{s\geq 1}J_{i+s}^{t,2}+\sum_{k\geq 1}\sum_{s\geq 1}\left(J_{ek+i+s}^{t,2}-J_{ek-i-1+s}^{t,2}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{s\geq 1}J_{i+s}^{t,2}-\sum_{k\geq 1}\sum_{s=-i}^{i}J_{ek+s}^{t,2}.

∎

Lemma 4.16
.

For any
y\neq x
we have

\mathrm{Cov}_{t}\bigl(\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{D}(\lambda)}(x),\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{D}(\lambda)}(y)\bigr)=-\mathsf{J}^{t}(x,y)^{2}.

Proof.

Using Theorem
2.15
, a direct computation gives

\displaystyle\mathrm{Cov}_{t}\left(\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{D}(\lambda)}(x),\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{D}(\lambda)}(y)\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{t}\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{D}(\lambda)}(x,y)-\mathbb{E}_{t}\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{D}(\lambda)}(x)\mathbb{E}_{t}\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{D}(\lambda)}(y)

\displaystyle=\rho^{t}(x,y)-\rho^{t}(x)\rho^{t}(y)

\displaystyle=\left(\rho^{t}(x)\rho^{t}(y)-\mathsf{J}^{t}(x,y)^{2}\right)-\rho^{t}(x)\rho^{t}(y)

\displaystyle=-\mathsf{J}^{t}(x,y)^{2}.

∎

Proposition 4.17
.

Let
i,j\in\{0,\dots,e-1\}
with
i\neq j
. We have, under

,

\mathrm{Cov}_{t}\bigl(x_{i}(\lambda),x_{j}(\lambda)\bigr)=-\sum_{m\in e\mathbb{Z}+i}\sum_{n\in e\mathbb{Z}+j}\mathsf{J}^{t}(m,n)^{2}.

\displaystyle x_{i}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=\sum_{m\in e\mathbb{Z}+i+1}J_{2m}(y)\exp 2\mathbf{i}(m-1)\theta-\exp 2\mathbf{i}\theta\sum_{m\in e\mathbb{Z}+i}J_{2m}(y)\exp 2\mathbf{i}m\theta

\displaystyle=\begin{multlined}\exp(-2\mathbf{i}\theta)\sum_{m\in 2e\mathbb{Z}+2(i+1)}J_{m}(y)\exp\mathbf{i}m\theta\\
-\exp 2\mathbf{i}\theta\sum_{m\in 2e\mathbb{Z}+2i}J_{m}(y)\exp\mathbf{i}m\theta\end{multlined}

\displaystyle=\begin{multlined}\frac{1}{2e}\sum_{\omega\in\Omega_{2e}}\exp(-2\mathbf{i}\theta)\exp\mathbf{i}\bigl[-2(i+1)\omega+y\sin(\omega+\theta)\bigr]\\
-\exp(2\mathbf{i}\theta)\exp\mathbf{i}\bigl[-2i\omega+y\sin(\omega+\theta)\bigr]\end{multlined}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2e}\sum_{\omega\in\Omega_{2e}}\bigl[\exp\bigl(-2\mathbf{i}(\theta+\omega)\bigr)-\exp(2\mathbf{i}\theta)\bigr]\exp\mathbf{i}\bigl[-2i\omega+y\sin(\theta+\omega)\bigr].

Defining, for any
u,\theta\in\mathbb{R}
and
\omega\in\Omega_{2e}
, the quantities

\displaystyle\mathsf{F}(u)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\frac{2}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\pi/2}\exp\mathbf{i}\bigl[u\cos\eta\bigr]d\eta,

(4.32)

\displaystyle f_{\omega}(\theta)

\displaystyle\coloneqq E^{[e]}_{j-i}(\theta)\Bigl[\exp\bigl(-2\mathbf{i}(\theta+\omega)\bigr)-\exp(2\mathbf{i}\theta)\Bigr]\exp(-2\mathbf{i}i\omega),

(4.33)

we thus obtain, recalling (
4.31
),

D(x)=\frac{2x}{e\pi}\sum_{\omega\in\Omega_{2e}}\int_{0}^{\pi/2}J_{0}(2x\sin\theta)f_{\omega}(\theta)\mathsf{F}\bigl(2x\sin(\theta+\omega)\bigr)d\theta.

We now fix
\omega\in\Omega_{2e}
and we study the asymptotics as
x\to+\infty
of

D_{\omega}(x)=\frac{2x}{e\pi}\int_{0}^{\pi/2}J_{0}(2x\sin\theta)f_{\omega}(\theta)\mathsf{F}\bigl(2x\sin(\theta+\omega)\bigr)d\theta,

(4.34)

so that

D=\sum_{\omega\in\Omega_{2e}}D_{\omega}.

(4.35)

4.4.3
Negligible terms

By
[
OLBC
, 10.9.1]
, for
y\in\mathbb{R}
we have

J_{0}(y)=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\pi}\cos\bigl(y\cos(\eta)\bigr)d\eta,

thus
J_{0}(y)=\frac{2}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\pi/2}\cos\bigl(y\cos(\eta)\bigr)d\eta=\Re\mathsf{F}(y)
. By
[
OLBC
, 10.17.3]
we have, for
x>0
,

J_{0}(x)=\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi x}}\left(\cos\left(x-\frac{\pi}{4}\right)+M(x)\right),\qquad M(x)=_{x\to+\infty}O\left(\frac{1}{x}\right).

(4.36)

Since
M(x)=\sqrt{\frac{\pi x}{2}}J_{0}(x)-\cos\left(x-\frac{\pi}{4}\right)
for all
x\geq 0
, the function
M
is continuous on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
and thus there exists a constant
C\geq 0
such that

\lvert M(x)\rvert\leq\frac{C}{1+x},

for all
x\geq 0
.

Now,
\Im\mathsf{F}(y)=\frac{2}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\pi/2}\sin\bigl(y\cos\eta\bigr)d\eta
is the
Struve function of zero order
, thus by
[
OLBC
, 11.2.5, 11.6.1, 10.17.4]
and (
4.36
) we have, for
x>0
,

\mathsf{F}(x)=\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi x}}\left(\exp\mathbf{i}\left(x-\frac{\pi}{4}\right)+N(x)\right),\qquad N(x)=_{x\to+\infty}O\left(\frac{1}{x}\right).

Using the equality
\mathsf{F}(-x)=\overline{\mathsf{F}(x)}
, we deduce that we have, for
x<0
,

\mathsf{F}(x)=\sqrt{\frac{2}{-\pi x}}\left(\exp\mathbf{i}\left(x+\frac{\pi}{4}\right)+\overline{N(-x)}\right),\qquad\overline{N(x)}=_{x\to+\infty}O\left(\frac{1}{x}\right).

so that for all
x\neq 0
,

\mathsf{F}(x)=\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi\lvert x\rvert}}\left(\exp\mathbf{i}\left(x-\mathrm{sgn}(x)\frac{\pi}{4}\right)+N(x)\right),\qquad N(x)=_{\lvert x\rvert\to+\infty}O\left(\frac{1}{x}\right).

(4.37)

Similarly to
M
, we can take
C
large enough so that for any
x\in\mathbb{R}
we have

\lvert N(x)\rvert\leq\frac{C}{1+\lvert x\rvert}.

(4.38)

Let us now recall the following version of the Riemann–Lebesgue lemma (we provide a proof in §
A.2
).

Lemma 4.39

(Riemann–Lebesgue)
.

Let
f
be integrable on
(a,b)
and
\phi
be continuously differentiable on
[a,b]
. If
\phi
vanishes at a finite number of points then

\int_{a}^{b}f(t)\exp\mathbf{i}\bigl(x\phi(t)\bigr)dt\xrightarrow{x\to+\infty}0.

Proposition 4.40
.

If
\omega\notin\{0,\pi\}
then

\lim_{x\to+\infty}D_{\omega}(x)=0.

Proof.

Recalling (
4.34
), write

D_{\omega}(x)=\frac{1}{e\pi}\int_{0}^{\pi/2}\sqrt{2x\sin\theta}J_{0}(2x\sin\theta)\sqrt{2x\lvert\sin(\theta+\omega)\rvert}\mathsf{F}\bigl(2x\sin(\theta+\omega)\bigr)g_{\omega}(\theta)d\theta,

where
g_{\omega}(\theta)\coloneqq\frac{f_{\omega}(\theta)}{\sqrt{\sin(\theta)\lvert\sin(\theta+\omega)\rvert}}
. Note that
f_{\omega}
is piecewise continuous, moreover by assumption if
\sin(\theta)=0
then
\sin(\theta+\omega)\neq 0
(since
\omega\not\equiv 0\pmod{\pi}
) thus
\theta\mapsto\frac{1}{\sqrt{\sin(\theta)\lvert\sin(\theta+\omega)\rvert}}
and thus
g_{\omega}
is integrable on
(0,\pi/2)
.

Using (
4.36
) and (
4.37
) we obtain for
D_{\omega}(x)
a sum of these following four integrals (with some multiplicative coefficients that do not depend on
x
):

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\pi/2}\cos\left(2x\sin\theta-\frac{\pi}{4}\right)\exp\mathbf{i}\left(2x\sin(\theta+\omega)-\mathrm{sgn}\bigl(\sin(\theta+\omega)\bigr)\frac{\pi}{4}\right)g_{\omega}(\theta)d\theta,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\pi/2}\cos\left(2x\sin\theta-\frac{\pi}{4}\right)N\bigl(2x\sin(\theta+\omega)\bigr)g_{\omega}(\theta)d\theta,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\pi/2}\exp\mathbf{i}\left(2x\sin(\theta+\omega)-\mathrm{sgn}\bigl(\sin(\theta+\omega)\bigr)\frac{\pi}{4}\right)M(2x\sin\theta)g_{\omega}(\theta)d\theta,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\pi/2}M(2x\sin\theta)N\bigl(2x\sin(\theta+\omega)\bigr)g_{\omega}(\theta)d\theta.

We can easily see that the last three integrals go to zero as
x
grows to infinity. For instance, we have, using (
4.38
),

\left\lvert\int_{0}^{\pi/2}\cos\left(2x\sin\theta-\frac{\pi}{4}\right)N\bigl(2x\sin(\theta+\omega)\bigr)g_{\omega}(\theta)d\theta\right\rvert\leq C\int_{0}^{\pi/2}\frac{\lvert g_{\omega}(\theta)\rvert}{1+2x\bigl\lvert\sin(\theta+\omega)\bigr\rvert}d\theta,

and since

\frac{\lvert g_{\omega}(\theta)\rvert}{1+2x\bigl\lvert\sin(\theta+\omega)\bigr\rvert}\leq\bigl\lvert g_{\omega}(\theta)\rvert,

is integrable on
(0,\pi/2)
and

\frac{\lvert g_{\omega}(\theta)\rvert}{1+2x\bigl\lvert\sin(\theta+\omega)\bigr\rvert}\xrightarrow{x\to+\infty}0,

for almost all
\theta\in(0,\pi/2)
(all except maybe the only
\theta
for which
\sin(\theta+\omega)=0
)
we obtain the result by dominated convergence.

For the first integral, we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\pi/2}g_{\omega}(\theta)\cos

\displaystyle\left(2x\sin\theta-\frac{\pi}{4}\right)\exp\mathbf{i}\left(2x\sin(\theta+\omega)-\mathrm{sgn}\bigl(\sin(\theta+\omega)\bigr)\frac{\pi}{4}\right)d\theta

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\epsilon\in\{\pm 1\}}\int_{0}^{\pi/2}g_{\omega}(\theta)\exp\epsilon\mathbf{i}\left(2x\sin\theta-\frac{\pi}{4}\right)\exp\mathbf{i}\left(2x\sin(\theta+\omega)-\mathrm{sgn}\bigl(\sin(\theta+\omega)\bigr)\frac{\pi}{4}\right)d\theta

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\epsilon\in\{\pm 1\}}\int_{0}^{\pi/2}h_{\omega,\epsilon}(\theta)\exp\bigl(2\mathbf{i}x\phi_{\epsilon}(t)\bigr)d\theta,

(4.41)

with

\phi_{\epsilon}(t)=\epsilon\sin\theta+\sin(\theta+\omega),

and

h_{\omega,\epsilon}(\theta)=g_{\omega}(\theta)\exp\left[\frac{-\mathbf{i}\pi}{4}\bigl(\epsilon+\mathrm{sgn}\bigl(\sin(\theta+\omega)\bigr)\bigr)\right].

The function
h_{\omega,\epsilon}
is integrable on
(0,\pi/2)
, and we will now see that
\phi^{\prime}_{\epsilon}
vanishes at at most one point. We have
\phi^{\prime}_{\epsilon}(\theta)=\epsilon\cos\theta+\cos(\theta+\omega)
. For
\epsilon=1
we have

\displaystyle\phi^{\prime}_{1}(\theta)=0

\displaystyle\iff\cos(\theta+\omega)=-\cos\theta

\displaystyle\iff\theta+\omega\equiv\pi\pm\theta\pmod{2\pi},

thus since
\omega\neq\pi
we obtain

\displaystyle\phi^{\prime}_{1}(\theta)=0

\displaystyle\iff\theta+\omega\equiv\pi-\theta\pmod{2\pi}

\displaystyle\iff\theta\equiv\frac{\pi-\omega}{2}\pmod{2\pi}.

Similarly, for
\epsilon=-1
we have

\displaystyle\phi^{\prime}_{-1}(\theta)=0

\displaystyle\iff\cos(\theta+\omega)=\cos\theta

\displaystyle\iff\theta+\omega\equiv\pm\theta\pmod{2\pi}

thus since
\omega\neq 0
we obtain

\displaystyle\phi^{\prime}_{-1}(\theta)=0

\displaystyle\iff\theta+\omega\equiv-\theta\pmod{2\pi}

\displaystyle\iff\theta=\pi-\frac{\omega}{2}.

We thus conclude, applying the Riemann–Lebesgue lemma (Lemma
4.39
), that the quantity in (
4.41
) goes to zero as
x\to+\infty
and this finishes the proof of the proposition.
∎

4.4.4
Contributing terms

If
\omega\in\{0,\pi\}
then
2\omega\equiv 0\pmod{2\pi}
and thus, recalling (
4.33
),

f_{\omega}(\theta)=-2\mathbf{i}\sin(2\theta)E^{[e]}_{j-i}(\theta).

(4.42)

We deduce from (
4.34
) and (
4.42
) that if
\omega\in\{0,\pi\}
then

D_{\omega}(x)=-\frac{4\mathbf{i}x}{e\pi}\int_{0}^{\pi/2}E^{[e]}_{j-i}(\theta)J_{0}(2x\sin\theta)\sin(2\theta)\mathsf{F}\bigl(2x\sin(\theta+\omega)\bigr)d\theta.

In particular, noting from (
4.32
) that
\mathsf{F}(-y)=\overline{\mathsf{F}(y)}
we have

\displaystyle D_{0}(x)

\displaystyle=-\frac{4\mathbf{i}x}{e\pi}\int_{0}^{\pi/2}E^{[e]}_{j-i}(\theta)J_{0}(2x\sin\theta)\sin(2\theta)\mathsf{F}(2x\sin\theta)d\theta,

(4.43a)

\displaystyle D_{\pi}(x)

\displaystyle=-\frac{4\mathbf{i}x}{e\pi}\int_{0}^{\pi/2}E^{[e]}_{j-i}(\theta)J_{0}(2x\sin\theta)\sin(2\theta)\overline{\mathsf{F}(2x\sin\theta)}d\theta.

(4.43b)

Recall that we have assumed at the beginning of §
4.4
that
i\neq j
. Recall also the notation
\omega_{\ell}=\frac{\ell\pi}{e}
for
-e\leq\ell\leq e
.

Proposition 4.44
.

We have

\lim_{x\to+\infty}D_{0}(x)=\lim_{x\to+\infty}D_{\pi}(x)=d_{e,j-i},

where

d_{e,k}\coloneqq\left(\frac{2}{e\pi}\right)^{2}\left(\sum_{\omega\in\Omega_{2e}}\omega\exp 2\mathbf{i}k\omega+2\pi\sum_{n=1}^{e^{\prime}-1}(1-\sin\omega_{n})\exp 2\mathbf{i}k\omega_{n}\right),

for
k\notin e\mathbb{Z}
and
e^{\prime}\coloneqq\bigl\lceil\frac{e}{2}\bigr\rceil
.

Proof.

By (
4.36
) and (
4.37
), for
t\geq 0
we have

\displaystyle tJ_{0}(t)\mathsf{F}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{\pi}\cos\left(t-\frac{\pi}{4}\right)\exp\mathbf{i}\left(t-\frac{\pi}{4}\right)+R(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\pi}\left[2\cos^{2}\left(t-\frac{\pi}{4}\right)+\mathbf{i}\sin\left(2t-\frac{\pi}{2}\right)\right]+R(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\pi}\left[\cos\left(2t-\frac{\pi}{2}\right)+1+\mathbf{i}\sin\left(2t-\frac{\pi}{2}\right)\right]+R(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\pi}+\frac{1}{\pi}\bigl(\sin(2t)-\mathbf{i}\cos(2t)\bigr)+R(t),

(4.45)

where
R
is continuous on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
and satisfies
R(t)=O\bigl(\frac{1}{t}\bigr)
when
t\to+\infty
. In particular, there exists
C\geq 0
such that
\lvert R(t)\rvert\leq\frac{C}{1+t}
for all
t\geq 0
.

Recall from Lemma
4.28
that
E^{[e]}_{j-i}
is constant on each interval
\bigl(\omega_{\ell},\omega_{\ell+1}\bigr)
for each
-e\leq\ell<e
. From (
4.43a
) and (
4.45
) we have, for any
0\leq a<b\leq\frac{\pi}{2}
,

\displaystyle\frac{4x}{e\pi}\int_{a}^{b}\sin(2\theta)J_{0}(2x\sin\theta)

\displaystyle\mathsf{F}(2x\sin\theta)d\theta

\displaystyle=\frac{4}{e\pi}\int_{a}^{b}2x\sin(\theta)J_{0}(2x\sin\theta)\mathsf{F}(2x\sin\theta)\cos(\theta)d\theta

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{e\pi}\int_{2\sin a}^{2\sin b}xuJ_{0}(xu)\mathsf{F}(xu)du

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{e\pi^{2}}\int_{2\sin a}^{2\sin b}1+\sin(2xu)-\mathbf{i}\cos(2xu)+\pi R(xu)du,

(4.46)

where we did the variable change
u=2\sin\theta
. Moreover, as
x\to+\infty
we have

\int_{2\sin a}^{2\sin b}\sin(2xu)-\mathbf{i}\cos(2xu)du=o(1),

by the Riemann–Lebesgue lemma, and

\displaystyle\left\lvert\int_{2\sin a}^{2\sin b}R(xu)du\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\int_{2\sin a}^{2\sin b}\frac{C}{1+xu}du

\displaystyle=\frac{C}{x}\left[\ln(1+xu)\right]_{2\sin a}^{2\sin b}

\displaystyle=o(1),

so that (
4.46
) gives

\frac{4x}{e\pi}\int_{a}^{b}\sin(2\theta)J_{0}(2x\sin\theta)\mathsf{F}(2x\sin\theta)d\theta=\frac{4}{e\pi^{2}}(\sin a-\sin b)+o(1),

(4.47)

as
x\to+\infty
.

Now define
e^{\prime}\coloneqq\bigl\lceil\frac{e}{2}\bigr\rceil
so that
\omega_{e^{\prime}}\geq\pi/2>\omega_{e^{\prime}-1}
.
Define
\hat{\omega}_{\ell}
for
\ell\in\{0,\dots,e^{\prime}\}
by

\hat{\omega}_{\ell}\coloneqq\min\left(\omega_{\ell},\frac{\pi}{2}\right)=\begin{cases}\omega_{\ell},&\text{if }\ell<e^{\prime},\\
\frac{\pi}{2},&\text{if }\ell=e^{\prime}.\end{cases}

We deduce from (
4.12a
), (
4.43
) and (
4.47
) that, denoting by
\mathfrak{e}_{\ell}
the value of
E^{[e]}_{j-i}
on the interval
(\omega_{\ell},\omega_{\ell+1})
,

D_{0}(x)=\frac{-4\mathbf{i}}{e\pi^{2}}\sum_{\ell=0}^{e^{\prime}-1}\mathfrak{e}_{\ell}\left(\sin\hat{\omega}_{\ell+1}-\sin\hat{\omega}_{\ell}\right)+o(1)=D_{\pi}(x),

(4.48)

as
x\to+\infty
. Now we have, noting that
\sin\hat{\omega}_{e^{\prime}}=1
and
\sin\hat{\omega}_{0}=0
,

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell=0}^{e^{\prime}-1}\mathfrak{e}_{\ell}\bigl(\sin\hat{\omega}_{\ell+1}-\sin\hat{\omega}_{\ell}\bigr)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=1}^{e^{\prime}}\mathfrak{e}_{\ell-1}\sin\hat{\omega}_{\ell}-\sum_{\ell=0}^{e^{\prime}-1}\mathfrak{e}_{\ell}\sin\hat{\omega}_{\ell}

\displaystyle=\mathfrak{e}_{e^{\prime}-1}\sin\hat{\omega}_{e^{\prime}}-\mathfrak{e}_{0}\sin\hat{\omega}_{0}+\sum_{\ell=1}^{e^{\prime}-1}(\mathfrak{e}_{\ell-1}-\mathfrak{e}_{\ell})\sin\hat{\omega}_{\ell}

\displaystyle=\mathfrak{e}_{e^{\prime}-1}+\sum_{\ell=1}^{e^{\prime}-1}(\mathfrak{e}_{\ell-1}-\mathfrak{e}_{\ell})\sin\omega_{\ell}.

(4.49)

By Lemma
4.28
we have, for any
x\in(\omega_{\ell},\omega_{\ell+1})
and with
k\coloneqq j-i\pmod{e}
(thus
k\notin e\mathbb{Z}
),

\displaystyle\mathfrak{e}_{\ell}

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathbf{i}}{e}\sum_{\omega\in\Omega_{2e}}\exp(2\mathbf{i}k\omega)\bigl(\omega-\mathrm{sgn}(\omega-x)\pi\bigr)

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathbf{i}}{e}\sum_{\omega\in\Omega_{2e}}\omega\exp 2\mathbf{i}k\omega+\frac{\mathbf{i}\pi}{e}\sum_{n=-e+1}^{\ell}\exp 2\mathbf{i}k\omega_{n}-\frac{\mathbf{i}\pi}{e}\sum_{n=\ell+1}^{e}\exp 2\mathbf{i}k\omega_{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathbf{i}}{e}\sum_{\omega\in\Omega_{2e}}\omega\exp 2\mathbf{i}k\omega-\frac{2\mathbf{i}\pi}{e}\sum_{n=\ell+1}^{e}\exp 2\mathbf{i}k\omega_{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathbf{i}}{e}\sum_{\omega\in\Omega_{2e}}\omega\exp 2\mathbf{i}k\omega+\frac{2\mathbf{i}\pi}{e}\sum_{n=1}^{\ell}\exp 2\mathbf{i}k\omega_{n}

(4.50)

since
\sum_{\omega\in\Omega_{2e}}\exp 2\mathbf{i}k\omega=0=\sum_{n=1}^{e}\exp 2\mathbf{i}k\omega_{n}
since
2k\notin 2e\mathbb{Z}
, thus

\displaystyle\mathfrak{e}_{\ell-1}-\mathfrak{e}_{\ell}

\displaystyle=\frac{2\mathbf{i}\pi}{e}\sum_{n=1}^{\ell-1}\exp 2\mathbf{i}k\omega_{n}-\frac{2\mathbf{i}\pi}{e}\sum_{n=1}^{\ell}\exp 2\mathbf{i}k\omega_{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{-2\mathbf{i}\pi}{e}\exp(2\mathbf{i}k\omega_{\ell}).

(4.51)

Gathering (
4.48
), (
4.49
), (
4.50
) and (
4.51
) gives the desired result.
∎

4.4.5
Making the limit explicit

We are now interested in a closed form for the elements
\Re d_{e,k}
, where

d_{e,k}=\left(\frac{2}{e\pi}\right)^{2}\left(\sum_{\omega\in\Omega_{2e}}\omega\exp 2\mathbf{i}k\omega+2\pi\sum_{n=1}^{e^{\prime}-1}(1-\sin\omega_{n})\exp 2\mathbf{i}k\omega_{n}\right),

are the elements of Proposition
4.44
, where
k\notin e\mathbb{Z}
and
e^{\prime}=\bigl\lceil\frac{e}{2}\bigr\rceil
.

Lemma 4.52
.

For any
k\notin e\mathbb{Z}
we have

\Re d_{e,k}=\frac{2}{\pi e^{2}}\left[\cot\bigl(k-{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}\bigr)\tfrac{\pi}{e}-\cot\bigl(k+{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}\bigr)\tfrac{\pi}{e}\right].

In particular, we have
\Re d_{e,k}\neq 0
.

Proof.

First, we have

\displaystyle\Re\sum_{\omega\in\Omega_{2e}}\omega\exp(2\mathbf{i}k\omega)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\omega\in\Omega_{2e}}\omega\cos 2k\omega

\displaystyle=\pi,

noticing that if
\omega\in\Omega_{2e}\setminus\{\pi\}
then
-\omega\in\Omega_{2e}
. We now have to deal with the sum

A\coloneqq\Re\left(2\sum_{n=1}^{e^{\prime}-1}(1-\sin\omega_{n})\exp 2\mathbf{i}k\omega_{n}\right)=2\sum_{n=1}^{e^{\prime}-1}(1-\sin\omega_{n})\cos 2k\omega_{n}.

Recall that
\omega_{n}=\frac{\pi n}{e}
. For any
n\in\{1,\dots,e-1\}
we thus have
\omega_{e-n}=\pi-\omega_{n}
, hence
\sin\omega_{e-n}=\sin\omega_{n}
and
\cos 2k\omega_{e-n}=\cos(2k\pi-2k\omega_{n})=\cos 2k\omega_{n}
. Hence, we have

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{e-1}(1-\sin\omega_{n})\cos 2k\omega_{n}-\begin{cases}\bigl(1-\sin\omega_{e/2}\bigr)\cos 2k\omega_{e/2},&\text{if }n\text{ is even},\\
0,&\text{if }n\text{ is odd},\end{cases}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{e-1}(1-\sin\omega_{n})\cos 2k\omega_{n},

since
\sin\omega_{e/2}=\sin\frac{\pi}{2}=1
if
e
is even. Since
\sum_{n=1}^{e-1}\cos 2k\omega_{n}=\sum_{n=1}^{e-1}\cos\bigl(\frac{2k\pi}{e}n\bigr)=-1
since
2k\notin 2e\mathbb{Z}
, we obtain that, using the identity
\sin(x)\cos(y)=\frac{1}{2}\bigl[\sin(x+y)+\sin(x-y)\bigr]
,

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle=-1-\sum_{n=1}^{e-1}\sin\omega_{n}\cos 2k\omega_{n}

\displaystyle=-1+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{n=1}^{e-1}\bigl[\sin(2k-1)\omega_{n}-\sin(2k+1)\omega_{n}\bigr].

We now recall the following standard formula (see, for instance,
[
GrRy
, 1.342.1]
):

\sum_{n=1}^{e-1}\sin nx=\sin\frac{ex}{2}\sin\frac{(e-1)x}{2}\cosec\frac{x}{2},

valid for any
x\notin 2\pi\mathbb{Z}
. We obtain, using the identity
\sin\left(a\pm\frac{\pi}{2}\right)=\pm\cos a
,

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{e-1}\sin(2k\pm 1)\omega_{n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{e-1}\sin\frac{(2k\pm 1)\pi n}{e}

\displaystyle=\sin\left[\left(k\pm{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}\right)\pi\right]\sin\left[(1-e^{-1})\left(k\pm{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}\right)\pi\right]\cosec\left[e^{-1}\left(k\pm{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}\right)\pi\right]

\displaystyle=\pm(-1)^{k}\sin\left[(1-e^{-1})\left(k\pm{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}\right)\pi\right]\cosec\left[e^{-1}\left(k\pm{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}\right)\pi\right]

\displaystyle=\pm(-1)^{k}\sin\left[\left((1-e^{-1})k\mp{\textstyle\frac{e^{-1}}{2}}\pm{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}\right)\pi\right]\cosec\left[e^{-1}\left(k\pm{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}\right)\pi\right]

\displaystyle=(-1)^{k}\cos\left[\left((1-e^{-1})k\mp{\textstyle\frac{e^{-1}}{2}}\right)\pi\right]\cosec\left[e^{-1}\left(k\pm{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}\right)\pi\right]

\displaystyle=\cos\left[\left(-e^{-1}k\mp{\textstyle\frac{e^{-1}}{2}}\right)\pi\right]\cosec\left[e^{-1}\left(k\pm{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}\right)\pi\right]

\displaystyle=\cos\left[e^{-1}\left(k\pm{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}\right)\pi\right]\cosec\left[e^{-1}\left(k\pm{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}\right)\pi\right]

\displaystyle=\cot\left[e^{-1}\left(k\pm{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}\right)\pi\right].

and thus

A=-1+\frac{1}{2}\left(\cot\left[e^{-1}\left(k-{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}\right)\pi\right]-\cot\left[e^{-1}\left(k+{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}\right)\pi\right]\right).

Finally, we obtain

\displaystyle\Re d_{e,k}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{2}{e\pi}\right)^{2}\bigl(\pi+\pi A\bigr)

\displaystyle=\frac{4}{\pi e^{2}}(1+A),

as desired. Note that
\Re d_{e,k}\neq 0
since the cotangent function is bijective on
(0,\pi)
(taking
1\leq k\leq e-1
).
∎

We conclude this part by putting (
4.26
), (
4.35
), Proposition
4.40
, Proposition
4.44
and Lemma
4.52
to find that

\lim_{x\to+\infty}C^{\prime}_{i,j}(x)=2\Re d_{e,j-i}=\frac{4}{\pi e^{2}}\left[\cot(j-i-{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}})\tfrac{\pi}{e}-\cot(j-i+{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}})\tfrac{\pi}{e}\right],

in particular, the limit depends only on
j-i
. Since
\Re d_{e,j-i}\neq 0
we find that

C_{i,j}(x)\sim 2\Re d_{e,j-i}x,

as
x\to+\infty
. By (
4.19
), (
4.22
) and Proposition
4.17
, we have, with
i\neq j
,

\mathrm{Cov}_{t}\bigl(x_{i}(\lambda),x_{j}(\lambda)\bigr)=\frac{-1}{4}C_{i,j}(2\sqrt{t}),

thus
\mathrm{Cov}_{t}\bigl(x_{i}(\lambda),x_{j}(\lambda)\bigr)\sim-\Re d_{e,j-i}\sqrt{t}
. We thus deduce from Lemma
4.52
the final result of this part.

Theorem 4.53
.

If
i\neq j
then as
t\to+\infty
we have, under the Poissonised Plancherel measure
\mathrm{pl}_{t}
,

\mathrm{Cov}_{t}\bigl(x_{i}(\lambda),x_{j}(\lambda)\bigr)\sim\frac{2\sqrt{t}}{\pi e^{2}}\left[\cot\bigl(j-i+{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}\bigr)\tfrac{\pi}{e}-\cot\bigl(j-i-{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}\bigr)\tfrac{\pi}{e}\right].

4.5
Asymptotics of the variance

Recall from (
3.7
) that
\sum_{i}x_{i}(\lambda)=0
. Hence, we directly obtain that

\mathrm{Var}_{t}x_{i}(\lambda)=\mathrm{Cov}_{t}\bigl(x_{i}(\lambda),x_{i}(\lambda)\bigr)=-\sum_{j\neq i}\mathrm{Cov}_{t}\bigl(x_{i}(\lambda),x_{j}(\lambda)\bigr).

(4.54)

Corollary 4.55
.

As
t\to+\infty
we have, under
\mathrm{pl}_{t}
,

\mathrm{Var}_{t}x_{i}(\lambda)\sim\frac{4\sqrt{t}}{\pi e^{2}}\cot\frac{\pi}{2e}.

Proof.

By Theorem
4.53
and (
4.54
), it suffices to prove that
\sum_{k=1}^{e-1}\Re d_{e,k}=\frac{4}{\pi e^{2}}\cot\frac{\pi}{2e}
. Recalling from Lemma
4.52
that

d_{e,k}=\frac{2}{\pi e^{2}}\left[\cot\bigl(k-{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}\bigr)\tfrac{\pi}{e}-\cot\bigl(k+{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}\bigr)\tfrac{\pi}{e}\right],

we obtain, using the fact that
\cot(e-{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}})\frac{\pi}{e}=\cot(\pi-\frac{\pi}{2e})=-\cot\frac{\pi}{2e}
,

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{e-1}d_{e,k}

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{\pi e^{2}}\left[\cot\frac{\pi}{2e}-\cot(e-{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}})\frac{\pi}{e}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{4}{\pi e}\cot\frac{\pi}{2e},

as desired. Note that
\cot\frac{\pi}{2e}\neq 0
indeed since
e\geq 2
.
∎

Remark 4.56
.

In particular, Theorem
4.53
remains valid in the case
i=j
, and the limit of
t^{-1/2}\mathrm{Var}_{t}x_{i}(\lambda)
as
t\to+\infty
does not depends on
i
.

With Proposition
4.13
and Remark
4.56
, we thus have proved Theorem
A
of the introduction.

We will now use the results of the previous sections to determine the limit law of

5.1
Limit expectation

Write
v_{t}\coloneqq\frac{4\sqrt{t}}{\pi e^{2}}\cot\frac{\pi}{2e}
. From (

) we have

\frac{|\overline{\lambda}|}{v_{t}}=\frac{e}{2}\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}/e\mathbb{Z}}\frac{x_{i}(\lambda)^{2}}{v_{t}}+\sum_{i=0}^{e-1}i\frac{x_{i}(\lambda)}{v_{t}}.

From Proposition
4.13
and Corollary

, we obtain that

\lim_{t\to+\infty}\mathbb{E}_{t}\frac{|\overline{\lambda}|}{v_{t}}=\frac{e^{2}}{2},

and thus the following result.

Proposition 5.1
.

Under the Poissonised Plancherel measure
\mathrm{pl}_{t}
, as
t\to+\infty
we have

\mathbb{E}_{t}|\overline{\lambda}|\sim\frac{2\sqrt{t}}{\pi}\cot\frac{\pi}{2e}.

Remark 5.2
.

(See
[
LuPi
,
AySi
]
.)
Under the uniform measure on partitions of
n
, the expectation of

is
\frac{(e-1)\sqrt{6n}}{2\pi}
. The order of the asymptotics is the same but the constant is quite different.

5.2
Limit law for
x_{i}(\lambda)

Recall from (
4.5
) that for
i\in\{0,\dots,e-1\}
we have

\hat{x}_{i}(\lambda)=\#_{t}^{(i)}-t^{2},

(5.3)

where
\#_{t}^{(i)}=\#(e\mathbb{Z}_{\geq-t^{2}}+i)\cap\mathcal{D}(\lambda)
. In particular, we have
\mathrm{Cov}_{t}\bigl(\hat{x}_{i}(\lambda),\hat{x}_{j}(\lambda)\bigr)=\mathrm{Cov}_{t}\bigl(\#_{t}^{(i)},\#_{t}^{(j)}\bigr)
.
Now define

b_{ij}\coloneqq\begin{cases}2\cot\frac{\pi}{2e},&\text{if }i=j,\\
\cot\bigl(j-i+{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}\bigr)\tfrac{\pi}{e}-\cot\bigl(j-i-{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}\bigr)\tfrac{\pi}{e},&\text{if }i\neq j,\end{cases}

(5.4)

for
0\leq i,j<e
(the formula for
i\neq j
being in fact valid for
i=j
), set
c_{ij}\coloneqq\frac{2}{\pi e^{2}}b_{ij}
and let
N
be an
e
-dimensional centred normal random vector with covariance matrix
B=(b_{ij})
. By Theorem
4.53
and Remark
4.56
, we know that
\mathrm{Cov}_{t}\bigl(x_{i}(\lambda),x_{j}(\lambda)\bigr)\sim c_{ij}\sqrt{t}
as
t\to+\infty
(recalling that
c_{ij}>0
).
We have

\displaystyle t^{-1/2}\mathrm{Cov}_{t}\bigl(\#_{t}^{(i)},\#_{t}^{(j)}\bigr)

\displaystyle=t^{-1/2}\mathrm{Cov}_{t}\bigl(\hat{x}_{i}(\lambda),\hat{x}_{j}(\lambda)\bigr)

\displaystyle=t^{-1/2}\mathrm{Cov}_{t}\Bigl(x_{i}(\lambda)+\bigl(\hat{x}_{i}(\lambda)-x_{i}(\lambda)\bigr),x_{j}(\lambda)+\bigl(\hat{x}_{i}(\lambda)-x_{i}(\lambda)\bigr)\Bigr)

\displaystyle=t^{-1/2}\mathrm{Cov}_{t}\bigl(x_{i}(\lambda),x_{j}(\lambda)\bigr)+t^{-1/2}o(1).

Indeed, by Lemma
4.6
and Cauchy-Schwarz inequality we have that, as
t\to+\infty
,

\mathrm{Cov}_{t}\bigl(\hat{x}_{i}(\lambda)-x_{i}(\lambda),\hat{x}_{j}(\lambda)-x_{j}(\lambda)\bigr)=o\bigl(t^{-t}\bigr)

and

\mathrm{Cov}_{t}\bigl(x_{i}(\lambda),\hat{x}_{j}(\lambda)-x_{j}(\lambda)\bigr)=o\bigl(t^{\frac{1}{4}-\frac{t}{2}}\bigr),

(and the same quantity with
i
and
j
permuted) thus both are
o(1)
. We deduce that
\mathrm{Cov}_{t}\bigl(\#_{t}^{(i)},\#_{t}^{(j)}\bigr)\sim_{t}c_{ij}\sqrt{t}
and thus

\frac{\mathrm{Cov}_{t}\bigl(\#_{t}^{(i)},\#_{t}^{(j)}\bigr)}{\sqrt{\mathrm{Var}_{t}\#_{t}^{(i)}}\sqrt{\mathrm{Var}_{t}\#_{t}^{(j)}}}\xrightarrow{t\to+\infty}\frac{b_{ij}}{b_{ii}}.

We can thus apply Theorem
4.1
to find that the vector

\left(\frac{\#_{t}^{(i)}-\mathbb{E}_{t}\#_{t}^{(i)}}{t^{1/4}\sqrt{c_{ii}}}\right)_{i\in\mathbb{Z}/e\mathbb{Z}},

converges in distribution to the
e
-dimensional centred normal vector with covariance matrix
\bigl(\frac{b_{ij}}{b_{ii}}\bigr)
, thus the vector

e\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2}}\left(\frac{\#_{t}^{(i)}-\mathbb{E}_{t}\#_{t}^{(i)}}{t^{1/4}}\right)_{i\in\mathbb{Z}/e\mathbb{Z}},

converges in distribution to
N
.
Now by (
5.3
) we have
\mathbb{E}_{t}\hat{x}_{i}(\lambda)=\mathbb{E}_{t}\#_{t}^{(i)}-t^{2}
and thus

e\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2}}\left(\frac{\hat{x}_{i}(\lambda)-\mathbb{E}_{t}\hat{x}_{i}(\lambda)}{t^{1/4}}\right)_{i\in\mathbb{Z}/e\mathbb{Z}},

converges in distribution to
N
as well. By Lemma
4.6
and Proposition
4.13
, we know that
\mathbb{E}_{t}\hat{x}_{i}(\lambda)
is bounded as
t\to+\infty
thus the vector

e\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2}}\left(\frac{\hat{x}_{i}(\lambda)}{t^{1/4}}\right)_{i\in\mathbb{Z}/e\mathbb{Z}},

again converges in distribution to
N
. Finally, by Slutsky’s theorem and Lemma
4.6
, we obtain the following final result (which is Theorem
B
of the introduction).

Theorem 5.5
.

Under the Poissonised Plancherel measure
\mathrm{pl}_{t}
, as
t\to+\infty
the random vector

e\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2}}\left(\frac{x_{i}(\lambda)}{t^{1/4}}\right)_{i\in\mathbb{Z}/e\mathbb{Z}},

converges in distribution to the
e
-dimensional centred normal vector with covariance matrix
B=(b_{ij})_{i,j\in\mathbb{Z}/e\mathbb{Z}}
given by
b_{ij}=\cot\bigl(j-i+{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}\bigr)\tfrac{\pi}{e}-\cot\bigl(j-i-{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}\bigr)\tfrac{\pi}{e}
.

Remark 5.6
.

In many cases of determinantal point processes (as in

).

Remark 5.7
.

By Remark

5.3
Limit law for
|\overline{\lambda}|

Note that the covariance matrix
B=(b_{ij})
of Theorem
5.5
is (symmetric, positive semi-definite and) circulant. Recall from (

) that

\lvert\overline{\lambda}\rvert=\frac{e}{2}\sum_{i=0}^{e-1}x_{i}(\lambda)^{2}+\sum_{i=0}^{e-1}ix_{i}(\lambda).

Hence, by Theorem
5.5
and Slutsky’s theorem we know that
t^{-1/2}|\overline{\lambda}|
has asymptotically the same law as
t^{-1/2}\frac{e}{2}\sum_{i=0}^{e-1}x_{i}(\lambda)^{2}
. To explicit this law, it suffices to compute the eigenvalues of
B
. Note that since each line of
B
sums to
0
(by (
3.7
)), we already know that
0
is an eigenvalue of
B
.

Lemma 5.8
.

The eigenvalues of
B
are

\lambda_{k}=2e\sin\frac{k\pi}{e},

for
k\in\{0,\dots,e-1\}
. In particular, the only zero eigenvalue is
\lambda_{0}
.

Proof.

For any
0\leq j<e
, we write
b_{j}\coloneqq b_{0j}
the entries of the first row of
B
. Note that since
\cot(\pi+x)=\cot(x)
, we deduce that
b_{j}
is defined for
j\in\mathbb{Z}/e\mathbb{Z}
.
Since
B
is circulant, with the permutation matrix

P\coloneqq\begin{pmatrix}&1\\
&&\ddots\\
&&&1\\
1\end{pmatrix},

we have

B=\sum_{j=0}^{e-1}b_{j}P^{j}.

Now
P
has each element of
\mu_{e}(\mathbb{C})
for eigenvalue, an eigenvector associated with
\zeta\in\mu_{e}(\mathbb{C})
being
\bigl(\zeta^{j}\bigr)_{0\leq j<e}
. We deduce that the eigenvalues of
B
are the elements, with
\zeta_{k}\coloneqq\exp(2\mathbf{i}k\pi/e)
for
k\in\mathbb{Z}/e\mathbb{Z}
,

\lambda_{k}=\sum_{j=0}^{e-1}b_{j}\zeta_{k}^{j}=\sum_{j=0}^{e-1}b_{j}\cos\frac{2kj\pi}{e},

(5.9)

recalling that the eigenvalues of
B
are real since
B
is real symmetric.
Recalling that
b_{j}=\cot(j+{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}})\frac{\pi}{e}-\cot(j-{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}})\frac{\pi}{e}
, we thus have to compute the sum

\sum_{j=0}^{e-1}\left[\cot(j+{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}})\frac{\pi}{e}-\cot(j-{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}})\frac{\pi}{e}\right]\cos\frac{2kj\pi}{e},

for
0\leq k<e
. First, if
k=0
then
\lambda_{0}=0
since
\cot
is
\pi
-periodic. We thus now assume
k\neq 0
.

We mimic the calculation in
[
BeYe
, §4]
of the following trigonometric sum (the case
n=1
having been first studied by Eisenstein):

\sum_{j=1}^{e-1}\sin\frac{2\pi kj}{e}\cot^{n}\left(\frac{\pi j}{e}\right).

Let us consider the following meromorphic function over
\mathbb{C}
:

g_{1}:z\mapsto\frac{\exp 2\mathbf{i}\pi kz}{\exp(2\mathbf{i}\pi ez)-1}-\frac{\exp(-2\mathbf{i}\pi kz)}{\exp(-2\mathbf{i}\pi ez)-1}.

The function
g_{1}
has simple poles at each
z=\frac{j}{e}
for
j\in\mathbb{Z}
, with residue

\displaystyle\frac{\exp\frac{2\mathbf{i}\pi kj}{e}}{2\mathbf{i}\pi e\exp(2i\pi j)}-\frac{\exp\frac{-2\mathbf{i}\pi kj}{e}}{-2\mathbf{i}\pi e\exp(-2\mathbf{i}\pi j)}

\displaystyle=\frac{\exp\frac{2\mathbf{i}\pi kj}{e}}{2\mathbf{i}\pi e}-\frac{\exp\frac{-2\mathbf{i}\pi kj}{e}}{-2\mathbf{i}\pi e}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\mathbf{i}\pi e}\cos\frac{2\pi kj}{e}.

Now the following meromorphic function over
\mathbb{C}
:

g_{2}:z\mapsto\cot\pi\left(z+\frac{1}{2e}\right)-\cot\pi\left(z-\frac{1}{2e}\right),

has simple poles at each
\pm\frac{1}{2e}+\mathbb{Z}
. Note that since
e\geq 2
then each pole is simple indeed. Recalling that
z\cot z\sim 1
as
z\to 0
, we find that the residue at
\pm\frac{1}{2e}+\mathbb{Z}
is
\pm\frac{1}{\pi}
. We thus find that, with
g\coloneqq g_{1}g_{2}
,

\displaystyle\mathrm{Res}_{\frac{j}{e}}g

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Res}_{\frac{j}{e}}(g_{1})g_{2}\left(\frac{j}{e}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\mathbf{i}\pi e}\cos\frac{2\pi kj}{e}\left[\cot\pi\left(\frac{j}{e}+\frac{1}{2e}\right)-\cot\pi\left(\frac{j}{e}-\frac{1}{2e}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{b_{j}}{\mathbf{i}\pi e}\cos\frac{2\pi kj}{e},

for all
j\in\mathbb{Z}
, and

\displaystyle\mathrm{Res}_{\pm\frac{1}{2e}}g

\displaystyle=g_{1}\left(\frac{\pm 1}{2e}\right)\mathrm{Res}_{\pm\frac{1}{2e}}g_{2}

\displaystyle=\pm\frac{1}{\pi}\left(\frac{\exp\frac{\pm\mathbf{i}\pi k}{e}}{-2}-\frac{\exp\frac{\mp\mathbf{i}\pi k}{e}}{-2}\right)

\displaystyle=\mp\frac{\mathbf{i}}{\pi}\sin\frac{\pm\pi k}{e}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\mathbf{i}}{\pi}\sin\frac{\pi k}{e}.

Theorem 5.10
.

The rescaled size
\frac{\pi}{4\sqrt{t}}|\overline{\lambda}|
of the

has, under the Poissonised Plancherel measure
\mathrm{pl}_{t}
, asymptotically as

e-1

\Gamma\bigl({\textstyle\frac{1}{2}},\sin\frac{k\pi}{e}\bigr)
for
k\in\{1,\dots,e-1\}
.

Proof.

We saw at the beginning of §

t^{-1/2}|\overline{\lambda}|
has asymptotically the same law as
t^{-1/2}\frac{e}{2}\sum_{i=0}^{e-1}x_{i}(\lambda)^{2}
. By Theorem
5.5
and the preceding discussion, since the vector
t^{-1/4}e\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2}}\bigl(x_{i}(\lambda)\bigr)_{i}
converges in distribution to
N
, we deduce that
t^{-1/2}e^{2}\frac{\pi}{2}\sum_{i=0}^{e-1}x_{i}(\lambda)^{2}

G

\Gamma({\textstyle\frac{1}{2}},2\lambda_{i})

. We deduce that

\frac{1}{e\pi}G

\lambda_{i}=2e\sin\frac{\pi i}{e}

\frac{1}{4e}G

\Gamma\bigl(\frac{1}{2},\sin\frac{\pi i}{e}\bigr)

\frac{\pi}{4\sqrt{t}}|\overline{\lambda}|
converges in distribution to
\frac{1}{4e}G
.

\mathbb{E}\Gamma(k,\theta)=k\theta
and
\sum_{k=1}^{e-1}\sin\frac{k\pi}{e}=\cot\frac{\pi}{2e}
(see, for instance,
[
GrRy
, 1.344.1]
). We also obtain the following result.

Corollary 5.13
.

Under the Poissonised Plancherel measure
\mathrm{pl}_{t}
, as
t\to+\infty
we have

\mathrm{Var}_{t}|\overline{\lambda}|\sim\frac{4et}{\pi^{2}}.

Proof.

Recall that
\mathrm{Var}\,\Gamma(k,\theta)=k\theta^{2}
, so that by Theorem
5.10
and the preceding discussion we have

\lim_{t\to+\infty}\frac{\pi^{2}}{16t}\mathrm{Var}_{t}|\overline{\lambda}|=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=1}^{e-1}\sin^{2}\frac{k\pi}{e}.

Now recalling the formula

\sum_{k=1}^{e-1}\sin^{2}kx=\frac{e-1}{2}-\frac{\cos ex\sin(e-1)x}{2\sin x},

for
x\notin\pi\mathbb{Z}
(see, for instance,
[
GrRy
, 1.351.1]
), we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{e-1}\sin^{2}\frac{k\pi}{e}

\displaystyle=\frac{e-1}{2}-\frac{\cos\pi\sin(e-1)\frac{\pi}{e}}{2\sin\frac{\pi}{e}}

\displaystyle=\frac{e-1}{2}+\frac{1}{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{e}{2},

and thus the desired result.
∎

6
Number of
i
-nodes

For
i\in\mathbb{Z}/e\mathbb{Z}
, we now study the number
c_{i}(\lambda)
of
i
-nodes of
\lambda
.

6.1
Poissonised version

Recall from (
3.4
) that the
e
-weight of
\lambda
is the integer
\mathrm{w}_{e}(\lambda)
such that

|\lambda|=|\overline{\lambda}|+e\mathrm{w}_{e}(\lambda),

more precisely by (
3.5
) we have

c_{i}(\lambda)=c_{i}(\overline{\lambda})+\mathrm{w}_{e}(\lambda),

for all
i\in\mathbb{Z}/e\mathbb{Z}
. If
\lambda
is chosen under
\mathrm{pl}_{t}
and
t\to+\infty
, we know that
|\lambda|
follows a Poisson distribution with parameter
t
thus by the law of large numbers we have that
\frac{|\lambda|}{t}
converges in distribution to
1
(recalling that
|\lambda|
is then a sum of
t
independent Poisson variables of parameter
1
). By Theorem
5.10
, we know that
\frac{|\overline{\lambda}|}{t}
converges in distribution to
0
, thus by Slutsky’s theorem we deduce that
\frac{\mathrm{w}_{e}(\lambda)}{t}
converges in distribution to
\frac{1}{e}
.

By (
3.2
) and Theorem
5.10
, we know that
\frac{c_{i}(\overline{\lambda})}{t}
converges to
0
in distribution. We have just seen that
\frac{\mathrm{w}_{e}(\lambda)}{t}
converges to
\frac{1}{e}
in distribution, thus we have proven the following result.

Proposition 6.1
.

Let
i\in\mathbb{Z}/e\mathbb{Z}
. Under the Poissonised Plancherel measure
\mathrm{pl}_{t}
, the random variable
\frac{c_{i}(\lambda)}{t}
converges in distribution (and thus in probability) to
\frac{1}{e}
as
t\to+\infty
.

We will now prove the de-Poissonised version of the above result, not by using a de-Poissonisation technique but instead by using the fact that
\omega_{\lambda}
converges to a limit shape.

6.2
Law of large numbers

Let
n\geq 1
,
\lambda\in\mathcal{P}_{n}
and
i\in\mathbb{Z}/e\mathbb{Z}
. Recall from §
2.1
the definition of the function
\omega_{\lambda}
. We denote by
\widetilde{\omega}_{\lambda}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
the function defined by
\widetilde{\omega}_{\lambda}(s)\coloneqq\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\omega_{\lambda}\bigl(s\sqrt{n}\bigr)
. Note that the area between the graphs of
\widetilde{\omega}_{\lambda}
and
|\cdot|
is
2
. It follows from Lemma
3.3
that

c_{i}(\lambda)=\frac{\sqrt{n}}{2}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\widetilde{\omega}_{\lambda}\left(\frac{i+ke}{\sqrt{n}}\right)-\left\lvert\frac{i+ke}{\sqrt{n}}\right\rvert.

(6.2)

Theorem 6.3

(
[
LoSh
]
,
[
KeVe
]
,
[
Rom
, Theorem 1.26]
)
.

Let
\Omega:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
be defined by

\Omega(s)\coloneqq\begin{cases}\frac{2}{\pi}\left(s\arcsin(\frac{s}{2})+\sqrt{4-s^{2}}\right),&\text{if }\lvert s\rvert\leq 2,\\
\lvert s\rvert,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Then, under the Plancherel measure
\mathrm{Pl}_{n}
, the function
\widetilde{\omega}_{\lambda}
converges uniformly in probability to
\Omega
as
n\to+\infty
. In other words, for any
\delta>0
we have

\mathrm{Pl}_{n}\left(\sup_{\mathbb{R}}\bigl|\widetilde{\omega}_{\lambda}-\Omega\bigr|>\delta\right)\xrightarrow{n\to+\infty}0.

Moreover, we also have convergence of the supports, that is:

\inf\bigl\{s\in\mathbb{R}:\widetilde{\omega}_{\lambda}(s)\neq|s|\bigr\}\longrightarrow-2,

and

\sup\bigl\{s\in\mathbb{R}:\widetilde{\omega}_{\lambda}(s)\neq|s|\bigr\}\longrightarrow 2,

in probability under
\mathrm{Pl}_{n}
as
n\to+\infty
.

We can now directly deduce the de-Poissonised version of Proposition
6.1
.

Proposition 6.4
.

For any
i\in\mathbb{Z}/e\mathbb{Z}
, the random variable
\frac{c_{i}(\lambda)}{n}
converges in probability to
\frac{1}{e}
under the Plancherel measure
\mathrm{Pl}_{n}
as
n\to+\infty
.

Proof.

Take
i\in\{0,\dots,e-1\}
and write
\frac{c_{i}(\lambda)}{n}=S^{i}_{n}+R_{i}(\lambda)
, where

\displaystyle S_{n}^{i}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\frac{1}{2\sqrt{n}}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\Omega\left(\frac{i+ke}{\sqrt{n}}\right)-\left\lvert\frac{i+ke}{\sqrt{n}}\right\rvert,

\displaystyle R_{i}(\lambda)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\frac{1}{2\sqrt{n}}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\widetilde{\omega}_{\lambda}\left(\frac{i+ke}{\sqrt{n}}\right)-\Omega\left(\frac{i+ke}{\sqrt{n}}\right)

(note that both sums are in fact finite). We have

S_{n}^{i}=\frac{1}{2e}\frac{e}{\sqrt{n}}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}g\left(\frac{i+ke}{\sqrt{n}}\right),

where
g\coloneqq\Omega-|\cdot|
is continuous on
\mathbb{R}
. Since
g
has compact support, we deduce that
S_{n}^{i}
is a mere Riemann sum thus
(S_{n}^{i})_{n}
converges as
n\to+\infty
and

\lim_{n\to+\infty}S_{n}^{i}=\frac{1}{2e}\int_{\mathbb{R}}g(t)dt=\frac{1}{2e}2=\frac{1}{e}

(to obtain that
\int_{\mathbb{R}}g(t)dt=2
we can just make the explicit calculation, or note from Theorem
6.3
that
g
is a uniform limit of continuous functions of integral
2
).

Thus, it now suffices to prove that
R_{i}(\lambda)
converges to
0
in probability.
Let
\delta>0
. By Theorem
6.3
, the probability for
\lambda\in\mathcal{P}_{n}
to satisfy

\displaystyle\lVert\widetilde{\omega}_{\lambda}-\Omega\rVert_{\infty}\leq\delta,

(6.5a)

\displaystyle\mathrm{supp}(\widetilde{\omega}_{\lambda}-\lvert\cdot\rvert)\subseteq[-2-\delta,2+\delta],

(6.5b)

goes to
1
as
n\to\infty
. Now if
|k|\geq\frac{(2+\delta)\sqrt{n}}{e}+1
then

\displaystyle\left|\frac{i+ke}{\sqrt{n}}\right|

\displaystyle=\frac{e}{\sqrt{n}}\left|k+\frac{i}{e}\right|

\displaystyle\geq\frac{e}{\sqrt{n}}\left(|k|-\left|\frac{i}{e}\right|\right)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{e}{\sqrt{n}}\bigl(|k|-1\bigr)

\displaystyle\geq 2+\delta,

thus for such a
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
we have
\bigl(\widetilde{\omega}_{\lambda}-|\cdot|\bigr)\bigl(\frac{i+ke}{\sqrt{n}}\bigr)=0
.
We deduce that for
\lambda
satisfying (
6.5
) we have

\displaystyle\bigl\lvert R_{i}(\lambda)\bigr\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2\sqrt{n}}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\left\lvert\widetilde{\omega}_{\lambda}\left(\frac{i+ke}{\sqrt{n}}\right)-\Omega\left(\frac{i+ke}{\sqrt{n}}\right)\right\rvert

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{n}}\sum_{\lvert k\rvert<\frac{(2+\delta)\sqrt{n}}{e}+1}\left\lvert\widetilde{\omega}_{\lambda}\left(\frac{i+ke}{\sqrt{n}}\right)-\Omega\left(\frac{i+ke}{\sqrt{n}}\right)\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2\sqrt{n}}\left(\frac{2(2+\delta)\sqrt{n}}{e}+3\right)\delta

\displaystyle\leq\frac{(2+\delta)\delta}{e}+\frac{3\delta}{2\sqrt{n}}.

\displaystyle\leq\frac{(2+\delta)\delta}{e}+\frac{3\delta}{2}.

We thus have proved that
\mathrm{Pl}_{n}(\left\lvert R_{i}(\widetilde{\omega}_{\lambda})\right\rvert>\frac{2(2+\delta)\delta}{e}+\frac{3\delta}{2})\xrightarrow{n\to\infty}0
and this concludes the proof since
\frac{2(2+\delta)\delta}{e}+\frac{3\delta}{2}\xrightarrow{\delta\to 0}0
.
∎

Appendix A
Intermediate proofs

We provide here the proofs of Lemma
4.28
and Lemma
4.39
.

A.1
A conditionally convergent series

We prove here Lemma
4.28
.
Let
k\in\mathbb{Z}
with
k\notin e\mathbb{Z}
. With
E^{[e]}_{k}(x)\coloneqq\sum_{n\in e\mathbb{Z}+k}\frac{\exp 2\mathbf{i}nx}{n}=2\sum_{n\in 2e\mathbb{Z}+2k}\frac{\exp\mathbf{i}nx}{n}
, we want to prove that

E^{[e]}_{k}(x)=\frac{\mathbf{i}}{e}\sum_{\omega\in\Omega_{2e}}\exp(2\mathbf{i}k\omega)(\omega-\epsilon_{x,\omega}\pi),

for any
x\in(-\pi,\pi)\setminus\Omega_{2e}
, where
\epsilon_{x,\omega}=\mathrm{sgn}(\omega-x)
and
\Omega_{2e}=\bigl\{\frac{\ell\pi}{e}:-e<\ell\leq e\bigr\}\subseteq(-\pi,\pi]
. In particular, with
\omega_{\ell}=\frac{\ell\pi}{e}
we have
\Omega_{2e}=\{\omega_{\ell}\}_{-e<\ell\leq e}
and the above equality shows that
E^{[e]}_{k}
is piecewise constant on each interval
\bigl(\omega_{\ell},\omega_{\ell+1}\bigr)
for
-e\leq\ell<e
.

We denote by
\ln:\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{R}_{-}\to\{z\in\mathbb{C}:-\pi<\arg z<\pi\}
the principal value of the logarithm. Recall that for any
z\in\mathbb{C}
with
|z|\leq 1
and
z\neq 1
we have

-\ln(1-z)=\sum_{n=1}^{+\infty}\frac{z^{n}}{n}.

As in the proof of Lemma
4.14
, we deduce that if moreover
z\notin\mu_{2e}
then, recalling (
4.15
),

\displaystyle-\sum_{\zeta\in\mu_{2e}}\zeta^{-2k}\ln(1-\zeta z)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{+\infty}\frac{z^{n}}{n}\sum_{\zeta\in\mu_{2e}}\zeta^{n-2k}

\displaystyle=2e\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n=1\\
n\in 2e\mathbb{Z}+2k\end{subarray}}^{+\infty}\frac{z^{n}}{n}.

We deduce that

\displaystyle\sum_{n\in 2e\mathbb{Z}+2k}\frac{z^{n}}{n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n=1\\
n\in 2e\mathbb{Z}+2k\end{subarray}}^{+\infty}\frac{z^{n}}{n}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n=-\infty\\
n\in 2e\mathbb{Z}+2k\end{subarray}}^{-1}\frac{z^{n}}{n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n=1\\
n\in 2e\mathbb{Z}+2k\end{subarray}}^{+\infty}\frac{z^{n}}{n}-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n=1\\
n\in 2e\mathbb{Z}-2k\end{subarray}}^{+\infty}\frac{z^{-n}}{n}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2e}\sum_{\zeta\in\mu_{2e}}\zeta^{-2k}\ln(1-\zeta z)+\frac{1}{2e}\sum_{\zeta\in\mu_{2e}}\zeta^{2k}\ln\bigl(1-\zeta z^{-1}\bigr)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2e}\sum_{\zeta\in\mu_{2e}}\zeta^{2k}\left[\ln\bigl(1-\zeta z^{-1}\bigr)-\ln\bigl(1-\zeta^{-1}z\bigr)\right].

Now if
|z|=1
, we have
\arg\bigl(1-\zeta z^{-1}\bigr),\arg\bigl(1-\zeta^{-1}z\bigr)\in(-\pi/2,\pi/2)
thus

-\pi<\arg\bigl(1-\zeta z^{-1}\bigr)-\arg\bigl(1-\zeta^{-1}z\bigr)<\pi.

We deduce that

\ln\bigl(1-\zeta z^{-1}\bigr)-\ln\bigl(1-\zeta^{-1}z\bigr)=\ln\frac{1-\zeta z^{-1}}{1-\zeta^{-1}z}.

We have
\frac{1-\zeta z^{-1}}{1-\zeta^{-1}z}=-\zeta z^{-1}
thus we obtain

\sum_{n\in 2e\mathbb{Z}+2k}\frac{z^{n}}{n}=\frac{1}{2e}\sum_{\zeta\in\mu_{2e}}\zeta^{2k}\ln\left(-\zeta z^{-1}\right),

for
|z|=1
and
z\notin\mu_{2e}
.
With
z=\exp\mathbf{i}x
with
x\in(-\pi,\pi)\setminus\Omega_{2e}
, we obtain that, with
\epsilon_{x,\omega}\coloneqq\mathrm{sgn}(\omega-x)
,

\displaystyle E^{[e]}_{k}(x)

\displaystyle=2\sum_{n\in 2e\mathbb{Z}+2k}\frac{\exp\mathbf{i}nx}{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{e}\sum_{\omega\in\Omega_{2e}}\exp\bigl(2\mathbf{i}k\omega\bigr)\ln\left(-\exp\mathbf{i}(\omega-x)\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{e}\sum_{\omega\in\Omega_{2e}}\exp\bigl(2\mathbf{i}k\omega\bigr)\ln\Bigl[\exp\mathbf{i}(\omega-x-\epsilon_{x,\omega}\pi)\Bigr].

We have
x\in(-\pi,\pi)
and
\omega\in(-\pi,\pi]
thus
\omega-x-\epsilon_{x,\omega}\pi\in(-\pi,\pi)
thus

\ln\Bigl[\exp\mathbf{i}(\omega-x-\epsilon_{x,\omega}\pi)\Bigr]=\mathbf{i}(\omega-x-\epsilon_{x,\omega}\pi).

We deduce that

\displaystyle E^{[e]}_{k}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathbf{i}}{e}\sum_{\omega\in\Omega_{2e}}\exp(2\mathbf{i}k\omega)(\omega-x-\epsilon_{x,\omega}\pi)

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathbf{i}}{e}\sum_{\omega\in\Omega_{2e}}\exp(2\mathbf{i}k\omega)(\omega-\epsilon_{x,\omega}\pi)-\frac{\mathbf{i}x}{e}\sum_{\omega\in\Omega_{2e}}\exp(2\mathbf{i}k\omega),

which gives the desired result since

\sum_{\omega\in\Omega_{2e}}\exp(2\mathbf{i}k\omega)=\sum_{\zeta\in\mu_{2e}}\zeta^{2k}=0,

since
e\nmid k
.

Remark A.1
.

If
k\in e\mathbb{Z}
, then the sum
E^{[e]}(x)\coloneqq\sum_{n\in e\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\exp 2\mathbf{i}nx}{n}
is not piecewise constant. Indeed, we have

E^{[e]}(x)+\sum_{k=1}^{e-1}E^{[e]}_{k}(x)=\sum_{n\neq 0}\frac{\exp 2\mathbf{i}nx}{n}=2\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{\sin 2nx}{n}=2S(2x),

where
S(y)\coloneqq\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{\sin ny}{n}
. By a standard equality we have
S(y)=\frac{\pi-y}{2}
for all
y\in(0,2\pi)
(see, for instance,
[
GrRy
, 1.441.1]
), thus by Lemma
4.28
we know that
E^{[e]}
is not piecewise constant (but piecewise affine). We can also directly compute
E^{[e]}
since

E^{[e]}(x)=\frac{1}{e}\sum_{n\neq 0}\frac{\sin 2enx}{n}=\frac{2}{e}S(2ex).

A.2
Riemann–Lebesgue lemma

We prove here Lemma
4.39
.
Let
f
be integrable on
(a,b)
and
\phi
be continuously differentiable on
[a,b]
. We want to prove that if
\phi
vanishes at a finite number of points then

\int_{a}^{b}f(t)\exp\mathbf{i}\bigl(x\phi(t)\bigr)dt\xrightarrow{x\to+\infty}0.

If
\phi^{\prime}
does not vanish in
[a,b]
the result reduces to the classical Riemann–Lebesgue lemma after the variable change
u=\phi(t)
. Thus, by additivity it suffices to consider the case where
\phi
vanishes only at
a
. Let
0<\epsilon\ll 1
and write

\int_{a}^{b}f(t)\exp\mathbf{i}\bigl(x\phi(t)\bigr)dt=\int_{a}^{a+\epsilon}f(t)\exp\mathbf{i}\bigl(x\phi(t)\bigr)dt+\int_{a+\epsilon}^{b}f(t)\exp\mathbf{i}\bigl(x\phi(t)\bigr)dt.

The first integral is bounded in module by the integral of
|f|
over
(a,a+\epsilon)
, and the second one goes to zero as
x\to+\infty
by the first case since
\phi^{\prime}
does not vanish in
[a+\epsilon,b]
. We conclude that for any
\epsilon>0
we have

\limsup_{x\to+\infty}\left\lvert\int_{a}^{b}f(t)\exp\mathbf{i}\bigl(x\phi(t)\bigr)dt\right\rvert\leq\int_{a}^{a+\epsilon}|f(t)|dt,

and now the quantity in the right-hand side goes to zero as
\epsilon\to 0
since
f
is integrable.

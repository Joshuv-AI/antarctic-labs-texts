1.
Introduction

In their study of the space
\mathcal{DH}_{n}
of diagonal harmonics
[
2
]
, Garsia and Haiman introduced a
q,t
-analogue of the Catalan numbers,
which they called the
q,t
-Catalan sequence. There are several equivalent characterizations of the (original)
q,t
-Catalan sequence, which includes two combinatorial
formulas:
One is Haiman’s dinv-area
q,t
-Catalan sequence; the other is Haglund’s area-bounce
q,t
-Catalan sequence. See
[
6
,
8
]
.
Further information about
q,t
-Catalan sequence and related results can be found in
[
3
,
5
,
7
]
.
In
[
12
]
, we introduced
q,t
-Catalan numbers
C_{\lambda}(q,t)
of type (a partition)
\lambda
as an extension of Haglund and Haiman’s combinatorial
formula for ordinary
q,t
-Catalan numbers.
We also investigated the
q,t
-symmetry of
C_{\lambda}(q,t)
:
The symmetry is easily proved when the length of
\lambda
is
\ell(\lambda)=2
; The symmetry may be proved by using MacMahon’s partition analysis technique when
\ell(\lambda)=3
;
No symmetry holds in general when
\ell(\lambda)\geq 4
, but we conjecture the
q,t
-symmetry of
C_{\lambda}(q,t)
when

\lambda=((a+1)^{s},a^{n-s})
.

We find it better to define
C_{\vec{k}}(q,t)
for any ordered partition, i.e., vector
\vec{k}=(k_{1},\dots,k_{n})
of positive integers, by

\displaystyle C_{\vec{k}}(q,t)=\sum_{D\in{\mathcal{D}}_{\vec{k}}}q^{\operatorname{\texttt{area}}(D)}t^{\operatorname{\texttt{bounce}}(D)},

where the sum ranges over all
\vec{k}
-Dyck paths
D
, and
\operatorname{\texttt{area}}(D)
and
\operatorname{\texttt{bounce}}(D)
are two statistics of
D
.

In the special case when
k_{i}=k
for all
i
, denote by
k^{n}=(k,\dots,k)\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
and

\displaystyle C_{k^{n}}(q,t)=\sum_{D\in{\mathcal{D}}_{k^{n}}}q^{\operatorname{\texttt{area}}(D)}t^{\operatorname{\texttt{bounce}}(D)},

where the sum ranges over all
k^{n}
-Dyck paths
D
.

Our main result is the following.

Theorem 1
.

For
\vec{k}=(k_{1},k_{2},k_{3})
, the
q,t
-Catalan number
C_{\vec{k}}(q,t)
is
q,t
-symmetric, i.e.,
C_{\vec{k}}(q,t)=C_{\vec{k}}(t,q)
.

A direct corollary is the following, since

C_{\lambda}(q,t)=\sum_{\lambda(\vec{k})=\lambda}C_{\vec{k}}(q,t),

where
\lambda(\vec{k})
is the partition obtained by arranging the entries of
\vec{k}
decreasingly.

Corollary 2
.

For any partition
\lambda
of length
3
, the
q,t
-Catalan number
C_{\lambda}(q,t)
is
q,t
-symmetric, i.e.,
C_{\lambda}(q,t)=C_{\lambda}(t,q)
.

Note that one can define

H_{\vec{k}}(q,t)=\sum_{D\in{\mathcal{D}}_{\vec{k}}}q^{\operatorname{\texttt{dinv}}(D)}t^{\operatorname{\texttt{area}}(D)},\quad\text{ and }H_{\lambda}=\sum_{\lambda(\vec{k})=\lambda}H_{\vec{k}}(q,t).

We have
C_{\lambda}(q,t)=H_{\lambda}(q,t)
by the fact that the sweep map takes dinv to area, and area to bounce, but

C_{\vec{k}}(q,t)\neq H_{\vec{k}}(q,t)
because the sweep map takes a path in
{\mathcal{D}}_{\vec{k}}
to a path in
{\mathcal{D}}_{\vec{k}^{\prime}}
for some
k^{\prime}
with
\lambda(\vec{k})=\lambda(\vec{k}^{\prime})
.
Indeed,
H_{\vec{k}}(q,t)
is not symmetric even when
\vec{k}
is of length
3
. In this sense, the bounce statistic is nicer than the dinv statistic.

We give two proofs of Theorem
1
. One is by MacMahon’s partition analysis as we proposed; the other is by
a direct bijection. For
\vec{k}=(k_{1},k_{2},k_{3})
, the statistics area and bounce have a simple description.
Dyck paths
D\in{\mathcal{D}}_{\vec{k}}
are uniquely determined by their red ranks
(r_{1}=0,r_{2},r_{3})
, which satisfy the conditions
0\leq r_{2}\leq k_{1}
and
0\leq r_{3}\leq r_{2}+k_{2}
.
Let
\lceil\alpha\rceil
denote the least integer greater than or equal to
\alpha
.
The area of
D
is simply
\operatorname{\texttt{area}}(D)=r_{2}+r_{3}
, and the bounce of
D
is given by

\displaystyle\operatorname{\texttt{bounce}}(D)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}2(k_{1}-r_{2})+r_{2}+k_{2}-r_{3}-\min(r_{2},k_{2}),&\text{ if }r_{2}+k_{2}-r_{3}\geq 2\min(r_{2},k_{2});\\
2(k_{1}-r_{2})+\lceil{\frac{r_{2}+k_{2}-r_{3}}{2}}\rceil,&\text{otherwise.}\end{array}\right.

The theorem can be proved by MacMahon’s partition analysis, which was developed by MacMahon for solving problems
in partition theory. The fundamental ingredient is MacMahon’s Omega operator on the
\lambda
variables defined by

\displaystyle\mathop{\Omega}_{\geq}\sum_{i_{1},i_{2},\dots i_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}}A_{i_{1},\dots,i_{n}}\lambda_{1}^{i_{1}}\lambda_{2}^{i_{2}}\cdots\lambda_{n}^{i_{n}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i_{1},i_{2},\dots i_{n}\geq 0}A_{i_{1},\dots,i_{n}},

\displaystyle\mathop{\Omega}_{\mu=}\sum_{i_{1},i_{2},\dots i_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}}A_{i_{1},\dots,i_{n}}\mu_{1}^{i_{1}}\mu_{2}^{i_{2}}\cdots\mu_{n}^{i_{n}}

\displaystyle=A_{0,\dots,0}.

In other words, the
\mathop{\Omega}_{\geq}
operator extracts all terms with nonnegative power in
\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n}
and then set them to be equal to
1
,
and the
\mathop{\Omega}_{\mu=}
operator extracts the term independent of the
\mu
variables. The to-be-eliminated variables
\lambda_{i}
or
\mu_{j}
are
usually clear from the context.
Andrews et. al. developed the Mathematica package Omega
[
1
]
to eliminate the
\lambda
variables. We use Xin’s Ell2 Maple package
[
10
]
in our computation.

The paper is organized as follows. In this introduction, we have introduced the basic concepts.
In Section
2
we prove Theorem
1
by MacMahon’s partition analysis.
In Section
3
, we give a bijective proof of Theorem
1
.
In Section
4
we prove
C_{k^{4}}(q,t)=C_{k^{4}}(t,q)
by MacMahon’s partition analysis.

It’s trivial.

3.
A Bijective Proof of Theorem

In this section, we use the following notation to describe a Dyck path

to obtain a simpler formula of

For

A
\vec{k}
-Dyck path

can be uniquely determined by the parameters

as follows. It starts with
a red arrow
S^{a}
(i.e. up step
(1,a)
) followed by
b
blue arrows
W
(down step
(1,-1)
),
then a red arrow
S^{c}
followed by

blue arrows

Clearly, the red ranks of

.
See

The formula of
\operatorname{\texttt{area}}(D)
is as follows

\operatorname{\texttt{area}}(D)=r_{2}+r_{3}=2a-2b+c-d.

We recall that the formula of
\operatorname{\texttt{bounce}}(D)=b(r_{2},r_{3})
, i.e.,

b(r_{2},r_{3})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}2(k_{1}-r_{2})+r_{2}+k_{2}-r_{3}-\min(r_{2},k_{2}),&\text{ if }r_{2}+k_{2}-r_{3}\geq 2\min(r_{2},k_{2});\\

Then, we have a simpler formula of

B(b,d)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}2b+d-\min(a-b,c),&\text{ if }d\geq 2\min(a-b,c);\\

We will give an involution (a bijection whose square is the identity map) on
{\mathcal{D}}_{\vec{k}}
for
\vec{k}=(a,c,e)
which interchanges
\operatorname{\texttt{area}}
and
\operatorname{\texttt{bounce}}

If

, we have

Now we need to give the following Lemma.

Lemma 4
.

Giving two non-negative integers
c
and

. If the following three conditions hold

(1)

Y=\chi((c+\lceil{\frac{d}{2}}\rceil)~is~odd)
;

(2)

d^{\prime}=2\lfloor{\frac{d}{2}}\rfloor+Y
;

(3)

Y^{\prime}=\chi((c+\lceil{\frac{d^{\prime}}{2}}\rceil)~is~odd)
.

Then
Y^{\prime}=\chi(d~is~odd)
.

Proof.

We have

c+\lceil{\frac{d^{\prime}}{2}}\rceil=c+\lfloor{\frac{d}{2}}\rfloor+Y

and

c+\lfloor{\frac{d}{2}}\rfloor+Y=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}c+\lfloor{\frac{d}{2}}\rfloor+1=c+\lceil{\frac{d}{2}}\rceil,&\hbox{ if }c+\lceil{\frac{d}{2}}\rceil\text{is odd and }d\text{ is odd};\\
c+\lfloor{\frac{d}{2}}\rfloor+1=c+\lceil{\frac{d}{2}}\rceil+1,&\hbox{ if }c+\lceil{\frac{d}{2}}\rceil\text{is odd and }d\text{ is even};\\
c+\lfloor{\frac{d}{2}}\rfloor=c+\lceil{\frac{d}{2}}\rceil-1,&\hbox{ if }c+\lceil{\frac{d}{2}}\rceil\text{is even and }d\text{ is odd};\\
c+\lfloor{\frac{d}{2}}\rfloor=c+\lceil{\frac{d}{2}}\rceil,&\hbox{ if }c+\lceil{\frac{d}{2}}\rceil\text{is even and }d\text{ is even}.\end{array}\right.

So,

c+\lceil{\frac{d^{\prime}}{2}}\rceil\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}is~odd,&\hbox{ if }d\text{ is odd};\\
is~even,&\hbox{ if }d\text{ is even}.\end{array}\right.

We get

Y^{\prime}=\chi(d~is~odd).

3.1.
\vec{k}=(a,c,e)
with

The formula of

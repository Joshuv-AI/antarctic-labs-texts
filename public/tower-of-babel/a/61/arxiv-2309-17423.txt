Long time dynamics of a fractional dissipative model of electroconvection in bounded domains
Elie Abdo, Mihaela Ignatova

Abstract
. We consider a nonlocal nonlinear model with fractional diffusion motivated by studies of electroconvection phenomena in incompressible viscous fluids. We address the global well-posedness, global regularity and long time dynamics of the model in bounded smooth domains with Dirichlet boundary conditions. We prove the existence and uniqueness of exponentially decaying in time solutions for H 1 H^{1} initial data regardless of the fractional dissipative regularity. In the presence of time independent body forces in the fluid, we prove the existence of a compact finite dimensional global attractor. In the case of periodic boundary conditions, we prove that the unique smooth solution is globally analytic in time, and belongs to a Gevrey class of functions that depends on the dissipative regularity of the model.



1.
Introduction

Electroconvection is a term associated to the nonlinear dynamics created by the interaction of fluid flow, ionic transport and electrostatic forces. In certain controlled experimental situations the dynamics are chaotic, similar to classical hydrodynamic transition to turbulence. The analogy to Rayleigh-Bénard convection
[
19
,
36
]
, is motivated not only by qualitative observations, but also by the fact that in both systems the fluid is driven by body forces which are the product of a transported scalar and a vector field. In thermal convection the scalar is the temperature and the vector is the gravitational field, while in electroconvection, the scalar is the charge density and the vector is the electric field. Numerical simulations and physical experiments have been used to study electroconvection in thin liquid crystals
[
2
,
19
,
44
]
. Electroconvection is of broad interest in electrochemistry, material science and applied physics (see for instance
[
45
,
35
,
41
]
), but our motivation and focus is on mathematical challenges of long time behavior, in the important case when physical boundaries are present.

In
[
6
]
, the authors considered an electroconvection model describing the nonlinear time evolution of a surface charge density
q
in an incompressible viscous fluid, confined to a two dimensional bounded domain
\Omega
, with velocity
u
and pressure
p
. The model is described by the system

(1)

\partial_{t}q+u\cdot\nabla q+\Lambda q=\Delta\Phi,

(2)

\partial_{t}u+u\cdot\nabla u-\Delta u+\nabla p=-qRq-q\nabla\Phi+f,

(3)

\nabla\cdot u=0,

(4)

q|_{\partial\Omega}=u|_{\partial\Omega}=0,

(5)

u(x,0)=u_{0}(x),q(x,0)=q_{0}(x),

where
\Lambda
is the square root of the Dirichlet Laplacian,
R:=\nabla\Lambda^{-1}
is the Riesz transform,
f
is a time independent body force in the fluid, and
\Phi
is a time independent potential resulting from a boundary applied voltage.

The global regularity of a unique solution to the initial boundary value problem (
1
)–(
5
) was obtained in
[
6
]
for Sobolev
H^{2}
initial data based on a two-tier Galerkin approximation. On the torus
{\mathbb{T}}^{2}
with periodic boundary conditions, we showed in
[
1
]
that the system (
1
)–(
5
) has a unique strong solution provided that the initial charge density belongs to the Lebesgue space
L^{4}
and the initial velocity belongs to the Sobolev space
H^{1}
. Moreover, we obtained the existence of a finite dimensional global attractor which reduces to a single point in the absence of body forces in the fluid.

In this paper, we are interested in the long time dynamics of (
1
)–(
5
) on bounded domains with smooth boundaries. We fix
\alpha\in(0,1]
and consider the generalized electroconvection model in
\Omega
described by the system

(6)

\partial_{t}q+u\cdot\nabla q+\Lambda^{\alpha}q=\Delta\Phi,

(7)

\partial_{t}u+u\cdot\nabla u-\Delta u+\nabla p=-qRq-q\nabla\Phi+f,

(8)

\nabla\cdot u=0,

(9)

q|_{\partial\Omega}=u|_{\partial\Omega}=0,

(10)

u(x,0)=u_{0}(x),q(x,0)=q_{0}(x),

with a fractional diffusion driven by the operator
\Lambda^{\alpha}
. We address the following three main problems:

(i)

Global existence and uniqueness of solutions for Sobolev
H^{1}
initial data;

(ii)

Long time dynamics in the absence and in the presence of body forces and of voltage applied at the boundary;

(ii)

Global Gevrey regularity of solutions in the case of periodic boundary conditions.

The system (
6
)–(
10
) is reminiscent of the dissipative surface quasi-geostrophic (SQG) equation, proposed in
[
10
]
as a model of hydrodynamic creation of small scales. In SQG the fluid velocity
u
depends on the scalar
q
via the relation
u=\nabla^{\perp}(-\Delta)^{-\frac{1}{2}}q
. In
[
16
]
, the existence and uniqueness of global smooth solutions were obtained in the subcritical
(\alpha>1)
case, whereas the existence of global decaying weak solutions was obtained in the supercritical
(\alpha<1)
and critical
(\alpha=1)
cases. Global regularity of solutions to the critical SQG equation on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
was established in
[
34
]
based on modulus of continuity techniques, in
[
5
]
based on De Giorgi techniques, and in
[
15
]
based on nonlinear maximum principles. In
[
14
]
, the authors addressed the long time dynamics of the forced critical SQG in the spatially periodic case and proved the existence of a finite dimensional global attractor. The global well-posedness of the supercritical SQG equation on
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
was obtained in
[
30
]
for small initial data in Besov spaces, and the supercritical regularity was studied in
[
17
]
where the authors proved that Hölder continuous solutions of subcritical type are actually
C^{\infty}
classical solutions of the equation. In
[
18
]
, it was shown that the solution of the supercritical equation with periodic boundary conditions does not blow up in finite time for all fractional powers
\gamma\geq\gamma_{1}
where
\gamma_{1}
is a constant depending on the size of the initial data. Recently, the critical SQG equation in bounded smooth domains was addressed in
[
8
]
,
[
9
]
, and
[
31
]
where global interior Lipschitz solutions were constructed, and in
[
40
]
where global Hölder regularity up to the boundary was obtained. In
[
13
]
, the local well-posedness for the inviscid case and the global existence of strong solutions for small initial data in the supercritical and critical cases are established in bounded smooth domains. The global regularity and time asymptotic behavior of solutions to the critical and supercritical SQG equations on bounded domains are open problems.

The system (
6
)–(
10
) has a different smoothness balance than the supercritical SQG equation due to the coupling to the Navier-Stokes equations which results in a higher spatial regularity for the fluid velocity. However, many challenges arise, not only from the nonlocality and the nonlinearity of the electric forces driving the fluid velocity, but also and chiefly from the presence of boundaries.

The existence and uniqueness of solutions of (
6
)–(
10
) relies on control of the spatial
L^{p}
norms of the charge density
q
, which evolve via regular nonlinear advection
u\cdot\nabla q
. The need for cancellation of advective terms in
L^{p}
is crucial, and a direct Galerkin approximation procedure does not work. We consider instead a spectral regularization of
(-\Delta)^{-\frac{1}{2}}
, denoted by
(\Lambda^{-1})_{\epsilon}
, that depends on a small positive parameter
\epsilon>0
, and we define the corresponding truncated Riesz transform
R_{\epsilon}=\nabla(\Lambda^{-1})_{\epsilon}
. Then we take a regularized version of (
6
)–(
10
) in which the nonlinear nonlocal electric forces
qRq
are replaced by
q^{\epsilon}R_{\epsilon}q^{\epsilon}
, and we use Galerkin approximations and compactness arguments to prove that each
\epsilon
-approximate system has global in time regular up to the boundary solutions that may depend badly on
\epsilon
. By making use of convex damping inequalities (Proposition
3
), we manage to derive
L^{p}
bounds for the family of viscous charge densities
\left\{q^{\epsilon}\right\}_{\epsilon>0}
, uniform in
\epsilon
. This allows us to obtain good control of
q^{\epsilon}R_{\epsilon}q^{\epsilon}
, uniform in
\epsilon
, due to the boundedness of the Dirichlet Riesz transform on
L^{p}
spaces, generating consequently a spatial Sobolev
H^{1}
regularity, global in time, for both the charge density and velocity solving (
6
)–(
10
).

The long time dynamics of the forced electroconvection system (
1
)–(
5
) in periodic domains was addressed in
[
1
]
on the basis of Fourier series techniques employed in the study of commutators

[\Lambda^{s},u\cdot\nabla]q:=\Lambda^{s}(u\cdot\nabla q)-u\cdot\nabla\Lambda^{s}q

for positive and negative powers
s
, and of interpolation inequalities for fractional powers of the Laplacian. The facts that the fractional Laplacians have explicit representation formulas and
that the periodic operators
\Lambda^{s}
, defined as Fourier multipliers, commute with differential operators were essentially used in that work. These properties and techniques break down on bounded domains where the nonlocal operators
\Lambda^{s}
are defined via eigenfunction expansions, in terms of the eigenfunctions of the homogeneous Dirichlet Laplace operator, are not translation invariant, and don’t have integral representations with explicit kernels. This gives rise to many technical mathematical challenges and the need for new ideas.

At each positive time
t
, the forced initial boundary value problem (
6
)–(
10
) has a well defined solution map
\mathcal{S}(t)
on

\mathcal{V}=\left\{(q,u)\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\times(H_{0}^{1}(\Omega))^{2}:\nabla\cdot u=0\right\},

which is the largest space in which the model (
6
)–(
10
) has unique solutions. We prove the existence of an absorbing ball

\mathcal{B}=\left\{(q,u)\in\mathcal{V}:\|\nabla q\|_{L^{2}}+\|\Delta u\|_{L^{2}}\leq R\right\}

with a radius
R
depending only on the forcing terms
f
and
\Phi
, whose image under
\mathcal{S}(t)
is a subset of
\mathcal{B}
itself starting at a time
T:=T(R)
depending only on
R
(Proposition
13
). This requires uniform control of
q
in
H^{1}
and
u
in
H^{2}
starting with Sobolev
H^{1}
initial data. The first main challenging elements of the proof are
L_{t}^{\infty}
and
L_{t}^{2}
uniform boundedness of the velocity in
H^{1+\epsilon}
and
H^{2+\epsilon}
respectively. Due to the presence of electric forces
qRq
driven by the charge density, fractional product inequalities for small powers of the Stokes operator
A
are needed to estimate
A^{\epsilon}(\mathbb{P}(qRq))
and are established in Proposition
10
below. The second major element of the proof is
L_{t}^{\infty}
boundedness of the
L^{p}
norm of the velocity gradients. These are obtained via a mild formulation of the Navier-Stokes equations and use of Stokes semi-group estimates. The third challenging element of the proof is
L_{t}^{\infty}
uniform control of the density gradient in
L^{2}
. For that purpose, we track the time evolution of
\|\nabla q\|_{L^{2}}
via energy estimates and handle the nonlinearity by making use of velocity gradient bounds in
L^{p}
and fractional interpolation inequalities that hold on bounded domains. Consequently, the desired property
\mathcal{S}(t)\mathcal{B}\subset\mathcal{B}
follows after waiting some time. The compactness of the ball
\mathcal{B}
in the weaker topology of

\mathcal{H}=\left\{(q,u)\in L^{2}(\Omega)\times(L^{2}(\Omega))^{2}:\nabla\cdot u=0,q|_{\partial\Omega}=u|_{\partial\Omega}=0\right\},

together with the continuity and injectivity properties of
\mathcal{S}(t)
, yield the existence of a global attractor, compact in the norm of
\mathcal{H}
.

The passage from the
H^{1}
to the
H^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}
regularity of the charge density is intriguing because the nonlinear transport and dissipation governing the evolution of the
H^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}
norm of
q
do not balance each other. Nonetheless, the case
\alpha=1
provides a stronger dissipative structure that is exploited to improve the smoothness of the global attractor. In this latter situation, we control the nonlinearity of (
1
) by establishing pointwise commutator estimates that are inversely proportional to powers of the distance to the boundary function

d(x):=d(x,\partial\Omega),

and we control the weighted vector field
u(x)/d(x)
and scalar function
q(x)/d(x)
in
L^{p}
via use of Rellich inequalities. This yields good control of the fractional energy norms
\|\Lambda^{\frac{s}{2}}q\|_{L^{2}}
for any integer
s\geq 1
, and upgrade of the attractor’s regularity via bootstrapping arguments.

Posed on the two dimensional torus
{\mathbb{T}}^{2}
with periodic boundary conditions, the system (
6
)–(
10
) has a unique Gevrey regular solution. In spite of the fractional diffusion governing the system, we show that the time evolution of the Gevrey norm depends on the dissipative structure at hand using Fourier series techniques, Gevrey commutator estimates, and Gevrey cancellation laws. We obtain a local in time control of the Gevrey norm by the Sobolev regularity of the solution and, consequently a global extension in the spirit of the global in time boundedness of solutions in fractional Sobolev spaces.

2.
Main Results

2.1.
Functional Setting.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
be a bounded domain with smooth boundary. For
1\leq p\leq\infty
, we denote by
L^{p}(\Omega)
the Lebesgue spaces of measurable functions
f
from
\Omega
to
\mathbb{R}
(or
{\mathbb{R}}^{2})
such that

(11)

\|f\|_{L^{p}}=\left(\int_{\Omega}\|f\|^{p}dx\right)^{1/p}<\infty

if
p\in[1,\infty)
and

(12)

\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}={\mathrm{esssup}}_{\Omega}|f|<\infty

if
p=\infty
. The
L^{2}
inner product is denoted by
(\cdot,\cdot)_{L^{2}}
.

For
k\in\mathbb{N}
, we denote by
H^{k}(\Omega)
the Sobolev spaces of measurable functions
f
from
\Omega
to
\mathbb{R}
(or
{\mathbb{R}}^{2})
with weak derivatives of order
k
such that

(13)

\|f\|_{H^{k}}^{2}=\sum\limits_{|\alpha|\leq k}\|D^{\alpha}f\|_{L^{2}}^{2}<\infty,

and by
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
the closure of
C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
in
H^{1}(\Omega)
.

For a Banach space
(X,\|\cdot\|_{X})
and
p\in[1,\infty]
, we consider the Lebesgue spaces
L^{p}(0,T;X)
of functions
f
from
X
to
\mathbb{R}
(or
{\mathbb{R}}^{2})
satisfying

(14)

\int_{0}^{T}\|f\|_{X}^{p}dt<\infty

with the usual convention when
p=\infty
.

Fractional Powers of the Laplacian
. We denote by
\Delta
the Laplacian operator with homogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions. We note that
-\Delta
is defined on
\mathcal{D}(-\Delta)=H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
, and is positive and self-adjoint in
L^{2}(\Omega)
. We consider an orthonormal basis of
L^{2}(\Omega)
consisting of eigenfunctions
\left\{w_{j}\right\}_{j=1}^{\infty}\subset H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
of
-\Delta
satisfying

(15)

-\Delta w_{j}=\lambda_{j}w_{j}

where the eigenvalues
\lambda_{j}
obey
0<\lambda_{1}\leq...\leq\lambda_{j}\leq...\rightarrow\infty
.
For
s\in\mathbb{R}
, we define the fractional Laplacian operator of order
s
, denoted by
\Lambda^{s}
, by

(16)

\Lambda^{s}f=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{j}^{\frac{s}{2}}(f,w_{j})_{L^{2}}w_{j}

with domain

(17)

\mathcal{D}(\Lambda^{s})=\left\{f\in L^{2}(\Omega):\|\Lambda^{s}f\|_{L^{2}}^{2}:=\sum\limits_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\lambda_{j}^{s}(f,w_{j})_{L^{2}}^{2}<\infty\right\}.

For
s\in[0,1]
, we identify the domains
\mathcal{D}(\Lambda^{s})
with the usual Sobolev spaces as follows,

(18)

\mathcal{D}(\Lambda^{s})=\begin{cases}H^{s}(\Omega)\hskip 227.62204pt\mathrm{if}\;s\in[0,\frac{1}{2})\\
H_{00}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\Omega)=\left\{f\in H_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\Omega):f/\sqrt{d(x)}\in L^{2}(\Omega)\right\}\hskip 56.9055pt\mathrm{if}\;s=\frac{1}{2}\\
H_{0}^{s}(\Omega)\hskip 227.62204pt\mathrm{if}\;s\in(\frac{1}{2},1]\end{cases}

where
H_{0}^{s}(\Omega)
is the Hilbert subspace of
H^{s}(\Omega)
with vanishing boundary trace elements.

Stokes Operator.
We recall some basic notions of the Stokes operator
[
7
]
. We denote by
H
and
V
the spaces

(19)

H=\left\{v\in(L^{2}(\Omega))^{2}:\nabla\cdot v=0,v\cdot n|_{\partial\Omega}=0\right\}

where
n
is the outward unit normal to
\partial\Omega
, and

(20)

V=\left\{v\in(H_{0}^{1}(\Omega))^{2}:\nabla\cdot v=0\right\}.

Let
\mathbb{P}:(L^{2}(\Omega))^{2}\rightarrow H
be the Leray Hodge projection. We define the Stokes operator, denoted by
A
, as

(21)

A:=-\mathbb{P}\Delta

with domain
\mathcal{D}(A)=V\cap(H^{2}(\Omega))^{2}
.
A
is positive, self-adjoint, and injective, and its inverse
A^{-1}
is compact. We denote the eigenvalues of
A
by
\mu_{j},j=1,2,...
, and the corresponding eigenfunctions by
\phi_{j},j=1,2...
, and we note that
0<\mu_{1}\leq...\leq\mu_{j}\leq...\rightarrow\infty
. We define the fractional powers of the Stokes operator, denoted by
A^{s}
, as

(22)

A^{s}v=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\mu_{j}^{s}(v,\phi_{j})_{L^{2}}\phi_{j}

with domain

(23)

\mathcal{D}(A^{s})=\left\{v\in H:\|A^{s}v\|_{L^{2}}^{2}:=\sum\limits_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\mu_{j}^{2s}(v,\phi_{j})_{L^{2}}^{2}<\infty\right\}.

Periodic Gevrey classes.
Let
{\mathbb{T}}^{2}=[0,2\pi]^{2}
be the two dimensional torus.

For
s\in\mathbb{R}
, the periodic fractional Laplacian
\Lambda^{s}
applied to a mean zero function
f
is a Fourier multiplier with symbol
|k|^{s}
, that is, for
f
given by

(24)

f=\sum\limits_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\setminus\left\{0\right\}}f_{k}e^{ik\cdot x},

and obeying

(25)

\sum\limits_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\setminus\left\{0\right\}}|k|^{2s}|f_{k}|^{2}<\infty,

we have

(26)

\Lambda^{s}f=\sum\limits_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\setminus\left\{0\right\}}|k|^{s}f_{k}e^{ik\cdot x}.

For
\tau>0
,
s>0
, we define

(27)

e^{\tau\Lambda^{s}}f=\sum\limits_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\setminus\left\{0\right\}}e^{\tau|k|^{s}}f_{k}e^{ik\cdot x}

on

(28)

\mathcal{D}(e^{\tau\Lambda^{s}})=\left\{f\in L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{2}):\sum\limits_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\setminus\left\{0\right\}}e^{2\tau|k|^{s}}|f_{k}|^{2}<\infty\right\}.

3.1.
Properties of the Fractional Powers of the Laplacian.

We recall the identity

(50)

\lambda^{\frac{s}{2}}=c_{s}\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{-1-\frac{s}{2}}(1-e^{-t\lambda})dt

that holds for
s\in(0,2)
and

(51)

1=c_{s}\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{-1-\frac{s}{2}}(1-e^{-t})dt,

from which we obtain the integral representation

(52)

(\Lambda^{s}f)(x)=c_{s}\int_{0}^{\infty}[f(x)-e^{t\Delta}f(x)]t^{-1-\frac{s}{2}}dt

for
f\in\mathcal{D}(\Lambda^{s})
and
s\in(0,2)
, where the heat operator
e^{t\Delta}
is defined as

(53)

(e^{t\Delta}f)(x)=\int_{\Omega}H_{D}(x,y,t)f(y)dy

with kernel
H_{D}(x,y,t)
given by

(54)

H_{D}(x,y,t)=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}e^{-t\lambda_{j}}w_{j}(x)w_{j}(y).

In 2D, the heat kernel
H_{D}(x,y,t)
obeys

(55)

|H_{D}(x,y,t)|\leq Ct^{-1}e^{\frac{-|x-y|^{2}}{kt}},

(56)

|\nabla_{y}H_{D}(x,y,t)|\leq Ct^{-\frac{3}{2}}e^{\frac{-|x-y|^{2}}{kt}},

and

(57)

|\nabla_{x}H_{D}(x,y,t)|\leq Ct^{-\frac{3}{2}}e^{\frac{-|x-y|^{2}}{kt}}

for all
(x,y)\in\Omega\times\Omega
and
t>0
. Moreover, the following estimates

(58)

\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{-1-\frac{s}{2}}\int_{\Omega}|x-y|^{q}|(\nabla_{x}+\nabla_{y})H_{D}(x,y,t)|dydt\leq Cd(x)^{-s-1+q},

(59)

\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{-1-\frac{s}{2}}\int_{\Omega}|x-y|^{q}|\nabla_{x}(\nabla_{x}+\nabla_{y})H_{D}(x,y,t)|dydt\leq Cd(x)^{-s-2+q},

and

(60)

\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{-1-\frac{s}{2}}\int_{\Omega}|x-y|^{q}|\nabla_{y}(\nabla_{x}+\nabla_{y})H_{D}(x,y,t)|dydt\leq Cd(x)^{-s-2+q}

hold for any
q\geq 0
,
s\in(0,2)
and
x\in\Omega
. We refer the reader to
[
8
,
12
]
for detailed proofs of analogous estimates.

3.2.
Nonlinear Poincaré inequality.

We recall the following pointwise inequality in bounded domains
[
8
]
:

Proposition 3
.

Let
0\leq s<2
. There exists a constant
c>0
depending only on the domain
\Omega
and on
s
, such that, for any
\Phi
, a
C^{2}
convex function satisfying
\Phi(0)=0
, and any function
f\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
, the inequality

(73)

\Phi^{\prime}(f)\Lambda^{s}f-\Lambda^{s}(\Phi(f))\geq\frac{c}{d(x)^{s}}(f\Phi^{\prime}(f)-\Phi(f))

holds pointwise in
\Omega
.

For an even integer
p\geq 2
, we let
\Phi(x)=\frac{1}{p}x^{p}
, and we apply Proposition
3
to infer that

(74)

f^{p-1}\Lambda^{s}f\geq\frac{1}{p}\Lambda^{s}(f^{p})+\frac{c}{d(x)^{s}}\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)f^{p}

for any
f\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
. Integrating over
\Omega
, we have

(75)

\int_{\Omega}f^{p-1}\Lambda^{s}fdx\geq\frac{1}{p}\int_{\Omega}\Lambda^{s}(f^{p})dx+C_{\Omega,s}\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)\|f\|_{L^{p}}^{p}

for some positive constant
C_{\Omega,s}
depending only on the size of
\Omega
and
s
. In view of the integral representation formula (
52
), the maximum principle, and the positivity of
f^{p}
, we deduce that

(76)

\int_{\Omega}\Lambda^{s}(f^{p})dx\geq 0.

This yields the
L^{p}
nonlinear Poincaré inequality

(77)

\int_{\Omega}f^{p-1}\Lambda^{s}fdx\geq C_{\Omega,s}\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)\|f\|_{L^{p}}^{p}.

3.3.
Brezis-Mironescu interpolation inequality.

We define the fractional spaces
W^{s,p}(\Omega)
as

(78)

W^{s,p}(\Omega)=\left\{v\in L^{p}(\Omega):\|v\|_{W^{s,p}}=\left(\|v\|_{L^{p}}^{p}+\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}\frac{|v(x)-v(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{2+sp}}dxdy\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}<\infty\right\}.

Let
1\leq p,p_{1},p_{2}\leq\infty
with
p_{2}\neq 1
. Let
s,s_{1},s_{2}
be nonnegative real numbers such that
s_{1}\leq s_{2}
. Let
\theta\in(0,1)
such that
s=\theta s_{1}+(1-\theta)s_{2}
and
\frac{1}{p}=\frac{\theta}{p_{1}}+\frac{1-\theta}{p_{2}}
. Then there exists a positive universal constant
C
such that the following interpolation inequality

(79)

\|f\|_{W^{s,p}}\leq C\|f\|_{W^{s_{1},p_{1}}}^{\theta}\|f\|_{W^{s_{2},p_{2}}}^{1-\theta}

holds for any
f\in W^{s_{1},p_{1}}(\Omega)\cap W^{s_{2},p_{2}}(\Omega)
. We refer the reader to
[
3
]
for a detailed proof.

3.4.
Rellich’s inequality

We denote by
W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
the closure of the space of smooth compactly supported functions
C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
under the norm of
W^{1,p}
.
For
1\leq p<\infty
and
f\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
, the following Rellich inequality holds:

(80)

\int_{\Omega}\frac{|f(x)|^{p}}{d(x)^{p}}dx\leq\int_{\Omega}|\nabla f(x)|^{p}dx.

(see
[
22
]
and references therein).

For

Proposition 4

Let

,

, and

can be uniquely extended from

to

\|[\Lambda^{s},\tilde{u}]\tilde{q}\|_{L^{2}}\leq C[\tilde{u}]_{C^{0,\gamma}}\|\tilde{q}\|_{L^{2}}

holds for any
\tilde{q}\in L^{2}
.

Proof.
The estimate (
83
) is a particular case of Theorem 2.6 in
[
11
]
.

Proposition 5
.

Let
s\in(0,2)
and
p\in(2,\infty]
. Let
\tilde{q}\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
. Then the estimate

(84)

|[\nabla\nabla,\Lambda^{s}]\tilde{q}(x)|\leq C\left(\|\tilde{q}\|_{W^{1,p}}d(x)^{-s-1-\frac{2}{p}}+|\tilde{q}(x)|d(x)^{-s-2}\right)

holds for all

.

Proof.
Using the integral representation formula (
52
) and integrating by parts, we have

(85)

|[\nabla\nabla,\Lambda^{s}]\tilde{q}(x)|=c_{s}\left|\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{-1-\frac{s}{2}}\int_{\Omega}(\nabla_{x}\nabla_{x}-\nabla_{y}\nabla_{y})H_{D}(x,y,t)\tilde{q}(y)dydt\right|,

which can be bounded as

\displaystyle|[\nabla\nabla,\Lambda^{s}]\tilde{q}(x)|

\displaystyle\leq c_{s}\left|\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{-1-\frac{s}{2}}\int_{\Omega}(\nabla_{x}\nabla_{x}+\nabla_{x}\nabla_{y})H_{D}(x,y,t)\tilde{q}(y)dydt\right|

\displaystyle\quad\quad+c_{s}\left|\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{-1-\frac{s}{2}}\int_{\Omega}(\nabla_{y}\nabla_{x}+\nabla_{y}\nabla_{y})H_{D}(x,y,t)\tilde{q}(y)dydt\right|

\tilde{q}(x)
, the latter inequality yields

\displaystyle|[\nabla\nabla,\Lambda^{s}]\tilde{q}(x)|

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{-1-\frac{s}{2}}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla_{x}(\nabla_{x}+\nabla_{y})H_{D}(x,y,t)||\tilde{q}(y)-\tilde{q}(x)|dydt

\displaystyle\quad\quad+C|\tilde{q}(x)|\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{-1-\frac{s}{2}}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla_{x}(\nabla_{x}+\nabla_{y})H_{D}(x,y,t)|dydt

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad+C\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{-1-\frac{s}{2}}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla_{y}(\nabla_{x}+\nabla_{y})H_{D}(x,y,t)||\tilde{q}(y)-\tilde{q}(x)|dydt

\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{-1-\frac{s}{2}}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla_{x}(\nabla_{x}+\nabla_{y})H_{D}(x,y,t)|dydt\leq Cd(x)^{-s-2}.

By making use of the heat kernel estimate (

\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{-1-\frac{s}{2}}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla_{x}(\nabla_{x}+\nabla_{y})H_{D}(x,y,t)||\tilde{q}(y)-\tilde{q}(x)|dydt\leq C[q]_{C^{0,1-\frac{2}{p}}}d(x)^{-s-1-\frac{2}{p}}

\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{-1-\frac{s}{2}}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla_{y}(\nabla_{x}+\nabla_{y})H_{D}(x,y,t)|dydt\leq Cd(x)^{-s-2}.

Proposition 8
.

Let

be an integer. Let
\tilde{u}=(\tilde{u}_{1},\tilde{u}_{2})\in H^{m+1}

\tilde{q}\in\mathcal{D}(\Lambda^{m+\frac{\alpha}{2}})

is odd and

and
\alpha

We present a proof by induction. Suppose

. Let
\tilde{u}=(\tilde{u}_{1},\tilde{u}_{2})\in H^{3}
and

hence its

norm can be bounded as

(123)

\|(-\Delta)(\tilde{u}\cdot\nabla\tilde{q})-\tilde{u}\cdot\nabla(-\Delta)\tilde{q}\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\left(\|\Delta\tilde{u}\|_{L^{q}}\|\nabla\tilde{q}\|_{L^{p}}+\|\nabla\tilde{u}\|_{L^{q}}\|\nabla\nabla\tilde{q}\|_{L^{p}}\right)

, any field
\tilde{u}\in H^{m+1}

\tilde{q}\in\mathcal{D}(\Lambda^{m+\frac{\alpha}{2}})

with the property

. Indeed, the commutator in (

\displaystyle(-\Delta)^{\frac{m+2}{2}}(\tilde{u}\cdot\nabla\tilde{q})-\tilde{u}\cdot\nabla(-\Delta)^{\frac{m+2}{2}}\tilde{q}

\displaystyle=(-\Delta)^{\frac{m}{2}}\left((-\Delta)\tilde{u}\cdot\nabla\tilde{q}+\tilde{u}\cdot\nabla(-\Delta)\tilde{q}-2\nabla\tilde{u}\cdot\nabla\nabla\tilde{q}\right)-\tilde{u}\cdot\nabla(-\Delta)^{\frac{m+2}{2}}\tilde{q}

\displaystyle\quad+\left[(-\Delta)^{\frac{m}{2}}\left(\tilde{u}\cdot\nabla(-\Delta)\tilde{q}\right)-\tilde{u}\cdot\nabla(-\Delta)^{\frac{m}{2}}(-\Delta)\tilde{q}\right]-2(-\Delta)^{\frac{m}{2}}(\nabla\tilde{u}\cdot\nabla\nabla\tilde{q})-\Delta\tilde{u}\cdot\nabla(-\Delta)^{\frac{m}{2}}\tilde{q},

\displaystyle\quad+C\|(-\Delta)^{\frac{m}{2}}(\nabla\tilde{u}\cdot\nabla\nabla\tilde{q})\|_{L^{2}}+C\|\Delta\tilde{u}\cdot\nabla(-\Delta)^{\frac{m}{2}}\tilde{q}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\tilde{u}\|_{H^{m+1}}\|\tilde{q}\|_{H^{m+2}}\leq C\|\tilde{u}\|_{H^{m+1}}\|\Lambda^{m+2}\tilde{q}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\quad\leq C_{\Omega}\|R_{i}\rho\|_{L^{2p_{1}}}^{2}\left(\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}\frac{|\rho(x)-\rho(y)|^{2p_{2}}}{|x-y|^{2+2p_{2}(1+s-\delta-1/p_{2})}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p_{2}}}

for any

slightly bigger than 1 so that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}R_{i}\rho(x)^{2}\int_{\Omega}\frac{|\rho(x)-\rho(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{2+2s-2\delta}}dxdy

\displaystyle\leq C_{\Omega}\|\rho\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\left(\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}\frac{|\rho(x)-\rho(y)|^{\frac{4}{2-\delta}}}{|x-y|^{2+\left(s-\frac{\delta}{2}\right)\left(\frac{4}{2-\delta}\right)}}\right)^{1-\frac{\delta}{2}}

Due to the continuous embeddings of

into

Putting together (

Lemma 1

Proof.
The proof of (

(197)

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|q^{\epsilon}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\Lambda^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}q^{\epsilon}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\epsilon\|\Lambda q^{\epsilon}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=0,

from which we obtain the differential inequality

\frac{d}{dt}\|q^{\epsilon}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+c_{1}\|q^{\epsilon}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq 0

in view of the Poincaré inequality. Multiplying both sides by

, and integrating in time from

to

, we infer that

(199)

\|q^{\epsilon}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\|q_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}e^{-c_{1}t}

for all

. Integrating (

0
to

, we also have the bound

for all

.

Step 2. Uniform

bounds for

.

with

. We estimate the nonlinear term

Step 4. Uniform

The

\displaystyle\leq C\|q^{\epsilon}\|_{L^{q}}\|\Lambda^{1+\frac{\alpha}{2}}(\Lambda^{-1})_{\epsilon}q^{\epsilon}\|_{L^{2}}\|Au^{\epsilon}\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\|q^{\epsilon}\|_{L^{q}}\|\Lambda^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}q^{\epsilon}\|_{L^{2}}\|Au^{\epsilon}\|_{L^{2}}

Putting (

6.
Higher Regularity: Proof of Theorem
2

In this section, we bootstrap the regularity of solutions and show that the charge density
q
and velocity
u
satisfying (
147
)–(
150
) decay exponentially in time in all Sobolev spaces and, consequently, in all Hölder spaces.

Theorem
2
is a direct consequence of the following proposition:

Proposition 12
.

Let
\epsilon>0
. Fix an integer
k\geq 2
. Suppose that
q_{0}\in\mathcal{D}(\Lambda^{k})
and
u_{0}\in\mathcal{D}(A^{\frac{k}{2}})
. Assume there is a positive constant
\Gamma_{k}
depending only on the
H^{k-1}
norms of the initial data and
k
such that the bounds

(222)

\|\Lambda^{{k-1}}q^{\epsilon}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\Gamma_{k}e^{-c_{1}t}

and

(223)

\int_{0}^{t}\|A^{\frac{k}{2}}u^{\epsilon}(s)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}ds\leq\Gamma_{k}

hold for any
t\geq 0
. Then there is a positive constant
\Gamma_{k}^{\prime}
depending only on the
H^{k}
norms of the initial data and
k
and a constant
c>0
depending only on the size of
\Omega
and
\alpha
such that the bounds

(224)

\|\Lambda^{k}q^{\epsilon}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|A^{\frac{k}{2}}q^{\epsilon}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\Gamma_{k}^{\prime}e^{-ct}

and

(225)

\int_{0}^{t}\|A^{\frac{k+1}{2}}u^{\epsilon}(s)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}ds\leq\Gamma_{k}^{\prime}

hold for any
t\geq 0
.

Proof.
For any arbitrary positive time
T
, the charge density and velocity approximants
q^{\epsilon}
and
u^{\epsilon}
belong to the spaces
L^{\infty}(0,T;\mathcal{D}(\Lambda^{k}))\cap L^{2}(0,T;\mathcal{D}(\Lambda^{k+1}))
and
L^{\infty}(0,T;\mathcal{D}(A^{\frac{k}{2}}))\cap L^{2}(0,T;\mathcal{D}(A^{\frac{k+1}{2}}))
respectively, a fact that can be shown by performing energy estimates on the Galerkin regularized system (
167
)–(
168
) and passage in the weak limit by use of the Banach Alaoglu theorem. We establish decaying in time bounds which do not depend on
\epsilon
.

We start by showing that the spatial
H^{k+1}
norm of
u^{\epsilon}
is finite in time over
[0,\infty)
and obeys

(226)

\int_{0}^{\infty}\|u^{\epsilon}(t)\|_{H^{k+1}}^{2}dt\leq\tilde{\Gamma}_{k}

for some positive constant
\tilde{\Gamma}_{k}
depending only on
k
and the initial data. Indeed, the
L^{2}
norm of
A^{\frac{k}{2}}u^{\epsilon}
evolves according to the energy equality

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|A^{\frac{k}{2}}u^{\epsilon}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|A^{\frac{k}{2}+\frac{1}{2}}u^{\epsilon}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

(227)

\displaystyle\quad\quad=-\int_{\Omega}A^{\frac{k}{2}-\frac{1}{2}}B(u^{\epsilon},u^{\epsilon})\cdot A^{\frac{k}{2}+\frac{1}{2}}u^{\epsilon}dx-\int_{\Omega}A^{\frac{k}{2}-\frac{1}{2}}\mathbb{P}(q^{\epsilon}\nabla(\Lambda^{-1})_{\epsilon}q^{\epsilon})\cdot A^{\frac{k}{2}+\frac{1}{2}}u^{\epsilon}dx.

We estimate the nonlinear term in
u^{\epsilon}
as follows,

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\Omega}A^{\frac{k}{2}-\frac{1}{2}}B(u^{\epsilon},u^{\epsilon})\cdot A^{\frac{k}{2}+\frac{1}{2}}u^{\epsilon}dx\right|\leq\frac{1}{4}\|A^{\frac{k}{2}+\frac{1}{2}}u^{\epsilon}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\|A^{\frac{k}{2}-\frac{1}{2}}B(u^{\epsilon},u^{\epsilon})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

(228)

\displaystyle\quad\quad\leq\frac{1}{4}\|A^{\frac{k}{2}+\frac{1}{2}}u^{\epsilon}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\|u^{\epsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\|A^{\frac{k}{2}}u^{\epsilon}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\frac{1}{4}\|A^{\frac{k}{2}+\frac{1}{2}}u^{\epsilon}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\|A^{\frac{k}{2}}u^{\epsilon}\|_{L^{2}}^{4}

where the second inequality holds due to the fractional product estimate (
131
) and the last inequality follows from the continuous embedding of
\mathcal{D}(A^{\frac{k}{2}})
in
L^{\infty}
when
k
is strictly greater than 1. As for the nonlinear term in
q^{\epsilon}
, we have

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\Omega}A^{\frac{k}{2}-\frac{1}{2}}\mathbb{P}(q^{\epsilon}\nabla(\Lambda^{-1})_{\epsilon}q^{\epsilon})\cdot A^{\frac{k}{2}+\frac{1}{2}}u^{\epsilon}dx\right|\leq\frac{1}{4}\|A^{\frac{k}{2}+\frac{1}{2}}u^{\epsilon}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\|A^{\frac{k}{2}-\frac{1}{2}}\mathbb{P}(q^{\epsilon}\nabla(\Lambda^{-1})_{\epsilon}q^{\epsilon})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\leq\frac{1}{4}\|A^{\frac{k}{2}+\frac{1}{2}}u^{\epsilon}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\left(\|\Lambda^{k-1}q^{\epsilon}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\|\nabla(\Lambda^{-1})_{\epsilon}q^{\epsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}+\|q^{\epsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\|\nabla(\Lambda^{-1})_{\epsilon}q^{\epsilon}\|_{H^{k-1}}^{2}\right)

(229)

\displaystyle\quad\quad\leq\frac{1}{4}\|A^{\frac{k}{2}+\frac{1}{2}}u^{\epsilon}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\|\Lambda^{1+\frac{\alpha}{2}}q^{\epsilon}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\|\Lambda^{k-1}q^{\epsilon}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

where the second inequality follows from Proposition
9
, and the last inequality uses the continuous embedding of
\mathcal{D}(\Lambda^{1+\frac{\alpha}{2}})
in
L^{\infty}
and the uniform boundedness of
\nabla(\Lambda)^{-1}_{\epsilon}
in Sobolev spaces (see Lemma
1
). Collecting the bounds (
6
) and (
6
), and inserting them in (
6

(230)

\frac{d}{dt}\|A^{\frac{k}{2}}u^{\epsilon}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|A^{\frac{k}{2}+\frac{1}{2}}u^{\epsilon}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq C\|\Lambda^{1+\frac{\alpha}{2}}q^{\epsilon}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\|\Lambda^{k-1}q^{\epsilon}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\|A^{\frac{k}{2}}u^{\epsilon}\|_{L^{2}}^{4},

which reduces to

(231)

\frac{d}{dt}\|A^{\frac{k}{2}}u^{\epsilon}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+(c_{2}-C\|A^{\frac{k}{2}}u^{\epsilon}\|_{L^{2}}^{2})\|A^{\frac{k}{2}}u^{\epsilon}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq C\|\Lambda^{1+\frac{\alpha}{2}}q^{\epsilon}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\|\Lambda^{k-1}q^{\epsilon}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

by making use of the Poincaré inequality. Multiplying both sides by the integrating factor

(232)

e^{\min\left\{c_{1},c_{2}\right\}t-C\int_{0}^{t}\|A^{\frac{k}{2}}u^{\epsilon}(s)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}ds},

integrating in time from
0
to
t
, using the hypotheses (
222
) and (
223
), and exploiting the square integrability in time of
\|\Lambda^{1+\frac{\alpha}{2}}q^{\epsilon}\|_{L^{2}}
obtained in (

(233)

\|A^{\frac{k}{2}}u^{\epsilon}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\Gamma_{k,1}e^{-\min\left\{c_{1},c_{2}\right\}t}

for any
t\geq 0
. Integrating (
230

0
to
t
, we also conclude that

(234)

\int_{0}^{t}\|A^{\frac{k}{2}+\frac{1}{2}}u^{\epsilon}(s)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}ds\leq\Gamma_{k,2}

for any
t\geq 0
. Here
\Gamma_{k,1}
and
\Gamma_{k,2}
are positive constants which do not depend on
\epsilon
nor on the time

but only on the initial data and the order of regularity

. Since

The evolution of the spatial
L^{2}
norm of
\Lambda^{k}q^{\epsilon}
is described by the ODE

(235)

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|\Lambda^{k}q^{\epsilon}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\Lambda^{k+\frac{\alpha}{2}}q^{\epsilon}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\epsilon\|\Lambda^{k+1}q^{\epsilon}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=\int_{\Omega}\Lambda^{k-1}(u^{\epsilon}\cdot\nabla q^{\epsilon})\Lambda^{k+1}q^{\epsilon}dx.

Suppose

\displaystyle\quad\quad\leq C\|\Lambda^{k}q^{\epsilon}\|_{L^{2}}\|u^{\epsilon}\|_{H^{k+1}}\|\Lambda^{k+\frac{\alpha}{2}}q^{\epsilon}\|_{L^{2}}

(236)

\displaystyle\quad\quad\leq\frac{1}{2}\|\Lambda^{k+\frac{\alpha}{2}}q^{\epsilon}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\|u^{\epsilon}\|_{H^{k+1}}^{2}\|\Lambda^{k}q^{\epsilon}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

\displaystyle\quad\quad=\left|\int_{\Omega}\left[\nabla\Lambda^{k-1}(u^{\epsilon}\cdot\nabla q^{\epsilon})-u^{\epsilon}\cdot\nabla\nabla\Lambda^{k-1}q^{\epsilon}\right]\cdot\nabla\Lambda^{k-1}q^{\epsilon}dx\right|

\displaystyle\quad\quad\leq C\|\Lambda^{k}q^{\epsilon}\|_{L^{2}}\|u^{\epsilon}\|_{H^{k+1}}\|\Lambda^{k+\frac{\alpha}{2}}q^{\epsilon}\|_{L^{2}}

(237)

\displaystyle\quad\quad\leq\frac{1}{2}\|\Lambda^{k+\frac{\alpha}{2}}q^{\epsilon}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\|u^{\epsilon}\|_{H^{k+1}}^{2}\|\Lambda^{k}q^{\epsilon}\|_{L^{2}}^{2},

in view of the estimate (
121
). This yields the differential inequality

(238)

\frac{d}{dt}\|\Lambda^{k}q^{\epsilon}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\Lambda^{k+\frac{\alpha}{2}}q^{\epsilon}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq C\|u^{\epsilon}\|_{H^{k+1}}^{2}\|\Lambda^{k}q^{\epsilon}\|_{L^{2}}^{2},

which implies that

e^{c_{1}t-C\int_{0}^{t}\|u^{\epsilon}\|_{H^{k+1}}^{2}ds}
, integrate in time from
0
to
t
, make use of (
234
), and conclude that

(240)

\|\Lambda^{k}q^{\epsilon}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\Gamma_{k,3}e^{-c_{1}t}

for any
t\geq 0
. Also here
\Gamma_{k,3}
is a positive constant depending only on the initial data and
k
. We have thus completed the proof of Theorem

.

7.

In this section, we address the long time dynamics of the forced model (

)–(

\Phi

For

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}q+u\cdot\nabla q+\Lambda^{\alpha}q=0\\
\partial_{t}u+u\cdot\nabla u+\nabla p-\Delta u=-qRq+f,\\

in the presence of smooth time independent divergence-free body forces

in the fluid. The system (
241

\Omega\times[0,\infty)
, with homogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions and initial data
q_{0}
and

. We address the long time behavior of solutions.

We recall the spaces
\mathcal{H}
and

defined respectively in (

(242)

where

is the unique solution of (

\omega_{0}:=(q_{0},u_{0})
at time

7.1.
Existence of an Absorbing Ball.

We start by proving the existence of a ball
B_{\rho}
, compact in
\mathcal{H}
, such that the image of
B_{\rho}
under
\mathcal{S}(t)
lies in
B_{\rho}
itself for all large times.

Proposition 13

Suppose
\alpha\in\left(0,1\right]
and

. Then there exists a radius
\rho>0
depending only on the

and some universal constants such that for each
\omega_{0}=(q_{0},u_{0})\in\mathcal{V}

\|\nabla q_{0}\|_{L^{2}}
and
\|\nabla u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}
and universal constants such that

(244)

\mathcal{S}(t)\omega_{0}\in\mathcal{B}_{\rho}:=\left\{(q,u)\in\mathcal{V}:\|\nabla q\|_{L^{2}}+\|\Delta u\|_{L^{2}}\leq\rho\right\}

for all

and

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\left(\|\nabla q\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)+\|\Lambda^{1+\frac{\alpha}{2}}q\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|Au\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad\leq\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{1}||\nabla q|^{2}dx+\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u||\nabla q_{2}||\nabla q|dx+\int_{\Omega}|u_{1}||\nabla u||Au|dx

(293)

\displaystyle\quad\quad+\int_{\Omega}|u||\nabla u_{2}||Au|dx+\int_{\Omega}|q_{1}||Rq||Au|dx+\int_{\Omega}|q||Rq_{2}||Au|dx

after integration by parts. By making use of Hölder’s inequality, continuous Sobolev embeddings, the boundedness of the Riesz transform on

) reduces to

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\left(\|\nabla q\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)+\|\Lambda^{1+\frac{\alpha}{2}}q\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|Au\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad\leq C\|\Delta u_{1}\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla q\|_{L^{2}}\|\Lambda^{1+\frac{\alpha}{2}}q\|_{L^{2}}+C\|Au\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla q\|_{L^{2}}\|\Lambda^{1+\frac{\alpha}{2}}q_{2}\|_{L^{2}}+C\|u_{1}\|_{L^{4}}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|Au\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{3}{2}}

(294)

\displaystyle\quad\quad+C\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla u_{2}\|_{L^{4}}\|Au\|_{L^{2}}+C\|q_{1}\|_{L^{4}}\|q\|_{L^{4}}\|Au\|_{L^{2}}+C\|q\|_{L^{4}}\|q_{2}\|_{L^{4}}\|Au\|_{L^{2}}.

An application of Young’s inequality yields

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\left(\|\nabla q\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)+\|\Lambda^{1+\frac{\alpha}{2}}q\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\Delta u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

(295)

\displaystyle\quad\leq C\left(\|\Delta u_{1}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\Delta u_{2}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|u_{1}\|_{L^{4}}^{4}+\|q_{1}\|_{L^{4}}^{2}+\|\Lambda^{1+\frac{\alpha}{2}}q_{2}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)\left(\|\nabla q\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right),

which gives the desired
\mathcal{V}
-Lipschitz continuity property (
288
), with

(296)

K_{2}(t)=\exp\left\{C\int_{0}^{t}\left(\|\Delta u_{1}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\Delta u_{2}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|u_{1}\|_{L^{4}}^{4}+\|q_{1}\|_{L^{4}}^{2}+\|\Lambda^{1+\frac{\alpha}{2}}q_{2}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)ds\right\}.

Finally, we prove the Lipschitzianity property (
289
). We seek a differential inequality of the form

(297)

\frac{d}{dt}\left(t^{\delta}\|\omega\|_{\mathcal{V}}^{2}\right)\leq Ct^{-\beta}\|\omega\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}+C\delta t^{\delta-1}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+Z(t)\left(t^{\delta}\|\omega\|_{\mathcal{V}}^{2}\right)

for some
\beta<1
and a locally integrable function in time
Z(t)
. Solving (
297
), integrating (
7.2
) in time from
0
to
t
, and using (
287
), we obtain

(298)

t^{\delta}\|\omega\|_{\mathcal{V}}^{2}\leq K_{3}(t)\|\omega_{0}\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}

where

(299)

K_{3}(t)=C\left[\frac{t^{1-\beta}}{1-\beta}K_{1}(t)+t^{\delta-1}\ln K_{1}(t)\int_{0}^{t}K_{1}(s)ds+1\right]\exp\left\{\int_{0}^{t}Z(s)ds\right\}.

Indeed, the
L^{2}
norm of
t^{\delta}\|\omega\|_{\mathcal{V}}^{2}
obeys

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\left(t^{\delta}\|\omega\|_{\mathcal{V}}^{2}\right)+t^{\delta}\left(\|\Lambda^{1+\frac{\alpha}{2}}q\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\Delta u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\quad\leq\delta t^{\delta-1}\|\omega\|_{\mathcal{V}}^{2}+C\left(\|\Delta u_{1}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\Delta u_{2}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|u_{1}\|_{L^{4}}^{4}+\|q_{1}\|_{L^{4}}^{2}+\|\Lambda^{1+\frac{\alpha}{2}}q_{2}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)\left(t^{\delta}\|\omega\|_{\mathcal{V}}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\quad\leq\delta t^{\delta-1}\|\nabla q\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\delta t^{\delta-1}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

(300)

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad+C\left(\|\Delta u_{1}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\Delta u_{2}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|u_{1}\|_{L^{4}}^{4}+\|q_{1}\|_{L^{4}}^{2}+\|\Lambda^{1+\frac{\alpha}{2}}q_{2}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)\left(t^{\delta}\|\omega\|_{\mathcal{V}}^{2}\right)

due to (
7.2
). This latter energy inequality is of type (
297
) provided that we have good control of the term
\delta t^{\delta-1}\|\nabla q\|_{L^{2}}^{2}
. By the Brezis-Mironescu inequality, we have

(301)

\|\nabla q\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq C\|q\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{2\alpha}{2+\alpha}}\|\Lambda^{1+\frac{\alpha}{2}}q\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{4}{2+\alpha}},

hence

(302)

\delta t^{\delta-1}\|\nabla q\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq C\delta t^{\delta-1-\frac{2\delta}{2+\alpha}}\|q\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{2\alpha}{2+\alpha}}\left(t^{\frac{2\delta}{2+\alpha}}\|\Lambda^{1+\frac{\alpha}{2}}q\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{4}{2+\alpha}}\right)\leq\frac{1}{2}t^{\delta}\|\Lambda^{1+\frac{\alpha}{2}}q\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C_{\alpha}\delta^{\frac{2+\alpha}{\alpha}}t^{\delta-1-\frac{2}{\alpha}}\|q\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

via use of Young’s inequality for sums with exponents
\frac{2+\alpha}{2}
and
\frac{2+\alpha}{\alpha}
. Since
\delta
is greater than
\frac{2}{\alpha}
, (
297
) holds with
\beta=1+\frac{2}{\alpha}-\delta
and

(303)

Z(t)=C\left(\|\Delta u_{1}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\Delta u_{2}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|u_{1}\|_{L^{4}}^{4}+\|q_{1}\|_{L^{4}}^{2}+\|\Lambda^{1+\frac{\alpha}{2}}q_{2}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right),

completing the proof of Proposition
14
.

Remark 7
.

The choice of
\delta
in (
289
) results from the need to control the local in time integrals of
\|\nabla q\|_{L^{2}}^{2}
by constant multiples of the difference of the initial data in
\mathcal{H}
. However, the energy equality (
7.2
) gives such a boundedness only for
\int_{0}^{t}\|\Lambda^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}q\|_{L^{2}}^{2}ds
. The remedy is interpolation and control by the dissipation of the energy inequality in hand (
7.2
), which imposes restrictions on the power
\delta
of the time singularity.

7.3.
Injectivity of the Solution Map.

We obtain injectivity of the solution map
\mathcal{S}(t)
by adapting the approach of
[
7
]
to the system (
241
).

Proposition 15
.

Let
\omega_{1}^{0}=(q_{1}(0),u_{1}(0)),\omega_{2}^{0}=(q_{2}(0),u_{2}(0))\in\mathcal{V}
. Suppose there exists a time
T>0
such that
\mathcal{S}(T)\omega_{1}^{0}=\mathcal{S}(T)\omega_{2}^{0}
. Then
\omega_{1}^{0}=\omega_{2}^{0}
.

Proof.
The proof is divided into main steps.

Step 1. Time analyticity of solutions.
Suppose
(q_{0},u_{0})\in\mathcal{V}
, and denote the solution of (
241
) at time
t
by
(q(t),u(t))
. We complexify all functional spaces and operators, fix an angle
\theta\in(-\frac{\pi}{2},\frac{\pi}{2})
, and take
t=se^{i\theta}
for
s>0
. We have

\displaystyle\frac{d}{ds}\left[\|\nabla q(se^{i\theta})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|A^{\frac{1}{2}}u(se^{i\theta})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{d}{ds}\left[(q(se^{i\theta}),-\Delta q(se^{i\theta}))_{L^{2}}+(u(se^{i\theta}),Au(se^{i\theta}))_{L^{2}}\right]

\displaystyle=\left(e^{i\theta}\frac{dq}{dt}(se^{i\theta}),-\Delta q(se^{i\theta})\right)_{L^{2}}+\left(q(se^{i\theta}),-e^{i\theta}\Delta\frac{dq}{dt}(se^{i\theta})\right)_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\quad\quad+\left(e^{i\theta}\frac{du}{dt}(se^{i\theta}),Au(se^{i\theta})\right)_{L^{2}}+\left(u(se^{i\theta}),e^{i\theta}A\frac{du}{dt}(se^{i\theta})\right)_{L^{2}}

(304)

\displaystyle=2Re\left[e^{i\theta}\left(\frac{dq}{dt}(se^{i\theta}),-\Delta q(se^{i\theta})\right)_{L^{2}}+e^{i\theta}\left(\frac{du}{dt}(se^{i\theta}),Au(se^{i\theta})\right)_{L^{2}}\right]

where
Re(z)
denotes the real part of a complex number
z\in\mathbb{C}
, and
(\cdot,\cdot)_{L^{2}}
is the complexified
L^{2}
inner product. Thus, the evolution of the norm
\|\nabla q(se^{i\theta})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|A^{\frac{1}{2}}u(se^{i\theta})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}
is described by

(305)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{ds}\left[\|\nabla q(se^{i\theta})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|A^{\frac{1}{2}}u(se^{i\theta})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right]+\cos\theta\left[\|\Lambda^{1+\frac{\alpha}{2}}q(se^{i\theta})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|Au(se^{i\theta})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right]

(306)

\displaystyle=Re\left[e^{i\theta}(u\cdot\nabla q,-\Delta q)_{L^{2}}-e^{i\theta}(B(u,u),Au)_{L^{2}}-e^{i\theta}(\mathbb{P}(qRq),Au)_{L^{2}}+e^{i\theta}(f,Au)_{L^{2}}\right].

We estimate

\displaystyle\left|(u\cdot\nabla q,-\Delta q)_{L^{2}}\right|\leq C\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\frac{4}{\alpha}}}\|\nabla q\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla q\|_{L^{\frac{4}{2-\alpha}}}\leq C\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\|Au\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{2-\alpha}{2}}\|\nabla q\|_{L^{2}}\|\Lambda^{1+\frac{\alpha}{2}}q\|_{L^{2}}

(307)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\cos\theta}{8}\left[\|\Lambda^{1+\frac{\alpha}{2}}q(se^{i\theta})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|Au(se^{i\theta})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right]+\frac{C}{(\cos\theta)^{\frac{4-\alpha}{\alpha}}}\|\nabla u(se^{i\theta})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\|\nabla q(se^{i\theta})\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{4}{\alpha}},

(308)

\left|(B(u,u),Au)_{L^{2}}\right|\leq C\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{3}{2}}\|Au\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{3}{2}}\leq\frac{\cos\theta}{8}\|Au(se^{i\theta})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{C}{(\cos\theta)^{3}}\|\nabla u(se^{i\theta})\|_{L^{2}}^{6},

(309)

\left|(\mathbb{P}(qRq),Au)_{L^{2}}\right|\leq\frac{\cos\theta}{8}\|Au(se^{i\theta})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{C}{\cos\theta}\|\nabla q(se^{i\theta})\|_{L^{2}}^{4},

and

(310)

\left|(f,Au)_{L^{2}}\right|\leq\frac{\cos\theta}{8}\|Au(se^{i\theta})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{C}{\cos\theta}\|f\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

using the Hölder, Gagliardo-Nirenberg, and Young inequalities, and continuous Sobolev embeddings. Combining (
305
)–(
310
), we obtain the differential inequality

\displaystyle\frac{d}{ds}\left[\|\nabla q(se^{i\theta})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|A^{\frac{1}{2}}u(se^{i\theta})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right]\leq\frac{C}{(\cos\theta)^{\frac{4-\alpha}{\alpha}}}\|\nabla u(se^{i\theta})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\|\nabla q(se^{i\theta})\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{4}{\alpha}}

(311)

\displaystyle\quad\quad+\frac{C}{(\cos\theta)^{3}}\|\nabla u(se^{i\theta})\|_{L^{2}}^{6}+\frac{C}{\cos\theta}\|\nabla q(se^{i\theta})\|_{L^{2}}^{4}+\frac{C}{\cos\theta}\|f\|_{L^{2}}^{2},

from which we conclude that

(312)

\|\nabla q(se^{i\theta})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|A^{\frac{1}{2}}u(se^{i\theta})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq 2\left[\|\nabla q_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+1\right]

provided that

(313)

s\left(\frac{C}{\cos\theta}\|f\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{C}{\cos\theta}+\frac{C}{(\cos\theta)^{3}}+\frac{C}{(\cos\theta)^{\frac{4-\alpha}{\alpha}}}\right)\leq\Gamma_{0}

Here
C
is a positive universal constant, and
\Gamma_{0}
is a positive constant depending only on
\|\omega_{0}\|_{\mathcal{V}}
. Therefore
(q,u)
is locally time analytic on the region
\mathcal{R}
consisting of complex times
t=se^{i\theta}
obeying (
313
). Due to the uniform-in-time boundedness of
(q,u)
in the norm of
\mathcal{V}
, the time analyticity becomes global.

Step 2. Backward uniqueness.
Since
\mathcal{S}(T)w_{1}^{0}=\mathcal{S}(T)w_{2}^{0}
, then
\mathcal{S}(t)w_{1}^{0}=\mathcal{S}(t)w_{2}^{0}
for all times
t\geq T
due to the uniqueness of solutions in
\mathcal{V}
. From the time analyticity property derived in Step 1, we conclude that
\mathcal{S}(T)w_{1}^{0}
and
\mathcal{S}(T)w_{2}^{0}
coincides for all positive times. Consequently
w_{1}^{0}=w_{2}^{0}
, ending the proof of Proposition
15
.

7.4.
Decay of Volume Elements.

Let
\phi
be a smooth function defined on open set
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
,
N\geq 1
, and taking values in
\mathcal{V}
. Let
\Sigma_{t}=S(t)\phi(\Omega)
. The volume element in
\Sigma_{t}
is given by

\left|\frac{\partial}{\partial\alpha_{1}}(S(t)\phi(\alpha))\wedge\dots\wedge\frac{\partial}{\partial\alpha_{N}}(S(t)\phi(\alpha))\right|d\alpha_{1}\dots d\alpha_{N},

where
d\alpha_{1}\dots d\alpha_{N}
is the volume element in
{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
. The functions

(314)

\omega_{i}=\frac{\partial}{\partial\alpha_{i}}S(t)\phi(\alpha),\hskip 28.45274pti=1,\dots,N

solve the linearized system

(315)

\partial_{t}(q,u)+\mathcal{A}(q,u)+L(\bar{\omega})(q,u)=0

along
(\bar{q}(t),\bar{u}(t)):=\bar{\omega}(t)=\mathcal{S}(t)\phi(\alpha)
, where

(316)

\mathcal{A}(q,u)=(\Lambda^{\alpha}q,Au)

and

(317)

L(\bar{\omega})(q,u)=\left(\bar{u}\cdot\nabla q+u\cdot\nabla\bar{q},B(u,\bar{u})+B(\bar{u},u)+\mathbb{P}(qR\bar{q}+\bar{q}Rq)\right).

We address the time evolution of the volume element of the
N
-dimensional surface
\phi(\Omega)
transported by
\mathcal{S}(t)
. For that purpose, we consider the norm

(318)

V_{N}(t)=\|\omega_{1}(t)\wedge\dots\wedge\omega_{N}(t)\|_{\Lambda^{N}\mathcal{H}}

where
\omega_{1},\dots,\omega_{N}
solves (
315
) along some
\bar{\omega}(t)=\mathcal{S}(t)\bar{\omega}_{0}
, and
\Lambda^{N}\mathcal{H}
is the
N
-th exterior product of
\mathcal{H}
with the following scalar product

(\omega_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge\omega_{N};y_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge y_{N})_{\Lambda^{N}\mathcal{H}}=det(w_{i},y_{j})_{\mathcal{H}}.

Proposition 16
.

There exists a positive time
t_{0}
depending only on
\|\bar{\omega}_{0}\|_{\mathcal{V}}
, a positive integer
N_{0}
depending only on
\|f\|_{L^{2}}
, and a positive constant
c
depending on
\alpha
, such that the following decaying estimate

(319)

V_{N}(t)\leq V_{N}(0)e^{-cN^{1+\frac{\alpha}{2}}t}

holds for all
t\geq t_{0}
and
N\geq N_{0}
.

Proof of Proposition
16
.
We have

(320)

\partial_{t}(\omega_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge\omega_{N})+(\mathcal{A}+L(\bar{\omega}))_{N}(\omega_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge\omega_{N})=0,

where

(321)

(\mathcal{A}+L(\bar{\omega}))_{N}=(\mathcal{A}+L(\bar{\omega}))\wedge\mathcal{I}\wedge\dots\wedge\mathcal{I}+\dots+\mathcal{I}\wedge\dots\wedge\mathcal{I}\wedge(\mathcal{A}+L(\bar{\omega})),

\mathcal{I}
being the identity operator. Consequently, we obtain the evolution equation

(322)

\frac{d}{dt}V_{N}+\mathrm{Trace}((\mathcal{A}+L(\bar{\omega}))Q_{N})V_{N}=0

where
Q_{N}
is the orthogonal projection in
\mathcal{H}
onto the space spanned by
\omega_{1},\dots,\omega_{N}
. By Gronwall’s inequality, we obtain

(323)

V_{N}(t)\leq V_{N}(0)\exp\left\{-\int_{0}^{t}\mathrm{Trace}((\mathcal{A}+L(\bar{\omega}))Q_{N})ds\right\}.

For each time
t>0
, we let
\phi_{i}=(r_{i},v_{i})
,
i=1,\dots,N
, be an orthonormal family of functions in
\mathcal{H}
spanning the linear span of
\omega_{1},\dots,\omega_{N}
. Then, we have

(324)

\mathrm{Trace}((\mathcal{A}+L(\bar{\omega}))Q_{N})=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}(\mathcal{A}\phi_{i},\phi_{i})_{L^{2}}+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}(L(\bar{\omega})\phi_{i},\phi_{i})_{L^{2}}.

In view of Lemma
2
, we obtain the lower estimate

(325)

\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}(\mathcal{A}\phi_{i},\phi_{i})_{L^{2}}=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\left[(\Lambda^{\alpha}r_{i},r_{i})_{L^{2}}+(Av_{i},v_{i})_{L^{2}}\right]\geq\mu_{1}+\dots+\mu_{N}\geq CN^{1+\frac{\alpha}{2}}

where
\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{N}
are the first
N
eigenvalues of
\mathcal{A}
. Now we show that

(326)

|\mathrm{Trace}(L(\bar{\omega})Q_{N})|\leq C\left(\|\bar{\omega}\|_{{\mathcal{V}}}^{2}+\|\bar{\omega}\|_{\mathcal{V}}^{\frac{4}{\alpha}}+1\right)N+\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{Trace}(\mathcal{A}Q_{N}).

Indeed, the trace of
L(\bar{\omega})Q_{N}
can be estimated as follows,

\displaystyle|\mathrm{Trace}(L(\bar{\omega})Q_{N})|=\left|\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}(L(\bar{\omega})\phi_{i},\phi_{i})_{L^{2}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\left|(\bar{u}\cdot\nabla r_{i}+v_{i}\cdot\nabla\bar{q},r_{i})_{L^{2}}\right|+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\left|(B(v_{i},\bar{u})+B(\bar{u},v_{i})+\mathbb{P}(r_{i}R\bar{q}+\bar{q}Rr_{i}),v_{i})_{L^{2}}\right|

(327)

\displaystyle\leq C\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\left[\|v_{i}\|_{L^{\frac{4}{\alpha}}}\|\nabla\bar{q}\|_{L^{2}}\|r_{i}\|_{L^{\frac{4}{2-\alpha}}}+\|v_{i}\|_{L^{4}}\|\nabla\bar{u}\|_{L^{2}}\|v_{i}\|_{L^{4}}+\|r_{i}\|_{L^{2}}\|\bar{q}\|_{L^{4}}\|v_{i}\|_{L^{4}}\right].

Here the boundedness of the Riesz transform on
L^{p}
spaces is exploited. In view of the continuous embedding of
\mathcal{D}(\Lambda^{\frac{\alpha}{2}})
into
L^{\frac{4}{2-\alpha}}
and the Gagliardo-Nirenberg interpolation inequalities, we bound

\displaystyle\|v_{i}\|_{L^{\frac{4}{\alpha}}}\|\nabla\bar{q}\|_{L^{2}}\|r_{i}\|_{L^{\frac{4}{2-\alpha}}}\leq C\|v_{i}\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\|\nabla v_{i}\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{2-\alpha}{2}}\|\nabla\bar{q}\|_{L^{2}}\|\Lambda^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}r_{i}\|_{L^{2}}

(328)

\displaystyle\quad\quad\leq\frac{1}{4}\left[\|\Lambda^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}r_{i}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|A^{\frac{1}{2}}v_{i}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right]+C\|\nabla\bar{q}\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{4}{\alpha}}\|v_{i}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Applications of Ladyzhenskaya’s interpolation inequality and Young’s inequality give

\displaystyle\|v_{i}\|_{L^{4}}\|\nabla\bar{u}\|_{L^{2}}\|v_{i}\|_{L^{4}}+\|r_{i}\|_{L^{2}}\|\bar{q}\|_{L^{4}}\|v_{i}\|_{L^{4}}

(329)

\displaystyle\quad\quad\leq\frac{1}{4}\|A^{\frac{1}{2}}v_{i}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\left(\|\nabla\bar{u}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\bar{q}\|_{L^{4}}^{2}+1\right)\left(\|v_{i}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|r_{i}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right).

Putting (
7.4
)–(
7.4
) together, and using the normalization
\|\phi_{i}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=\|r_{i}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|v_{i}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=1
, we obtain the desired estimate (
326
), from which we infer that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\mathrm{Trace}((\mathcal{A}+L(\bar{\omega}))Q_{N})(s)ds

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\mathrm{Trace}(\mathcal{A}Q_{N})ds-CN\int_{0}^{t}\left(\|\bar{\omega}\|_{\mathcal{V}}^{2}+\|\bar{\omega}\|_{\mathcal{V}}^{\frac{4}{\alpha}}+1\right)ds

(330)

\displaystyle\geq CNt\left(N^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}-\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\left(\|\bar{\omega}\|_{\mathcal{V}}^{2}+\|\bar{\omega}\|_{\mathcal{V}}^{\frac{4}{\alpha}}+1\right)ds\right).

We apply Proposition
13
and obtain the existence of a time
t_{0}
depending only on
\|\bar{\omega}_{0}\|_{\mathcal{V}}
and a radius
\rho_{f}
depending only on
\|f\|_{L^{2}}
such that

(331)

\|\bar{\omega}\|_{\mathcal{V}}^{2}+\|\bar{\omega}\|_{\mathcal{V}}^{\frac{4}{\alpha}}+1\leq\rho_{f}

for all
t\geq t_{0}
. Consequently, it holds that

(332)

\int_{0}^{t}\mathrm{Trace}((\mathcal{A}+L(\bar{\omega}))Q_{N})(s)ds\geq CN^{1+\frac{\alpha}{2}}t

for all
t\geq t_{0}
, provided that
N^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\geq 2\rho_{f}
. Putting (
323
) and (
332
) together, we obtain (
319
), completing the proof of Proposition
16
.

7.5.
Existence of a Finite Dimensional Global Attractor.

As a consequence of the connectedness and compactness of the absorbing ball
\mathcal{B}_{\rho}
in
\mathcal{H}
, the continuity and injectivity of the solution map
\mathcal{S}(t)
, and the exponential time decay of volume elements, we conclude that the model (
241
) has a finite-dimensional global attractor. We refer the reader to
[
7
, Chapter 14]
for a detailed proof of the analogous result for the two-dimensional forced Navier-Stokes equations.

Remark 8
.

We note that Propositions
13
,
14
,
15
, and
16
hold in the presence of a time independent potential
\Phi
. In this latter case, the radius of the absorbing ball depends on the size of the body forces
f
in the fluid and the potential
\Phi
. This gives Theorem
3
.

8.
Regularity of the Global Attractor for
\alpha=1
: Proof of Theorem
4

In this section, we address the forced electroconvection model (
241
), where
\alpha
is taken to be 1. We prove the existence of an absorbing ball, compact in the strong norm of
\mathcal{V}
.

Proposition 17
.

Suppose
\alpha=1
and
f\in\mathcal{D}(A^{\frac{1}{2}})
. Then there exists a radius
\tilde{\rho}>0
depending only on
f
and some universal constants such that for each
\omega_{0}=(q_{0},u_{0})\in\mathcal{V}
, there exists a time
\tilde{T}_{0}
depending only on
\|\nabla q_{0}\|_{L^{2}}
and
\|\nabla u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}
and universal constants such that

(333)

\mathcal{S}(t)\omega_{0}\in\mathcal{B}_{\tilde{\rho}}:=\left\{(q,u)\in\mathcal{V}:\|\Lambda^{\frac{3}{2}}q\|_{L^{2}}+\|\Delta u\|_{L^{2}}\leq\tilde{\rho}\right\}

for all
t\geq\tilde{T}_{0}
.

Proof.
There exists a radius
R>0
depending only on the body forces
f
, such that for any
\omega_{0}\in\mathcal{V}
, there is a positive time
t_{0}
depending only on the
\mathcal{V}
-norm of
\omega_{0}=(q_{0},u_{0})
such that the solution
(q,u)
of (
241
), with initial datum
\omega_{0}
, obeys

(334)

\|q(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\nabla q(t)\|_{L^{2}}+\|\Delta u(t)\|_{L^{2}}+\int_{t}^{t+1}\|\nabla\Delta u(s)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}ds\leq R

at any time
t\geq t_{0}
, a fact that follows from the proof of Proposition
13
. Moreover, there exists a positive time
t_{1}\geq t_{0}
depending only on
\|\omega_{0}\|_{\mathcal{V}}
such that for
q(t_{1})\in\mathcal{D}(\Lambda^{\frac{3}{2}})
with a size dependency only on the forces
f
, and such that
q\in L^{\infty}(t_{1},T;\mathcal{D}(\Lambda^{\frac{3}{2}})\cap L^{2}(t_{1},T;\mathcal{D}(\Lambda^{2}))
for any
T\geq t_{1}
. We seek bounds for the charge density in those latter Lebesgue spaces, independent of the initial datum but depending only on the size of the body forces.

The
L^{2}
norm of
\Lambda^{\frac{3}{2}}q

) together, we obtain the energy inequality

(425)

\frac{d}{dt}\left[\|\Lambda^{\frac{m}{2}}q\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\Lambda^{\frac{m}{2}+1}u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right]\leq C\left[\|\nabla\Delta u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\Lambda^{1+\frac{\alpha}{2}}q\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right]\left[\|\Lambda^{\frac{m}{2}}q\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\Lambda^{\frac{m}{2}+1}u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right].

Since

(426)

\int_{0}^{\infty}\left[\|\Lambda^{1+\frac{\alpha}{2}}q(s)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla\Delta u(s)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right]ds\leq C(\|\Lambda q_{0}\|_{L^{2}},\|\Lambda^{2}u_{0}\|_{L^{2}})

holds for all
t\geq 0
(see Theorems
1
and
2
), we conclude that
(q,u)
satisfies (
420
). We have thus finished the proof of Step 2, completing the proof of Theorem
5
.

Appendix A
Spectral Lemma

We present a lemma describing the asymptotic behavior of eigenvalues associated with a vector-valued operator:

Lemma 2
.

Let
\tilde{H}
be a Hilbert space. Suppose
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
are operators defined on
\mathcal{D}(A_{1})\subset\tilde{H}
and
\mathcal{D}(A_{2})\subset\tilde{H}
respectively

(427)

A_{1}:\mathcal{D}(A_{1})\subset\tilde{H}\mapsto\tilde{H},

(428)

A_{2}:\mathcal{D}(A_{2})\subset\tilde{H}\mapsto\tilde{H},

such that
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
are strictly positive and injective, with compact inverses,
A_{1}^{-1}
and
A_{2}^{-1}
, in
\tilde{H}
. Let
\tilde{A}
be the operator defined on
\mathcal{D}(A_{1})\times\mathcal{D}(A_{2})
by

(429)

\tilde{A}(a_{1},a_{2})=(A_{1}a_{1},A_{2}a_{2}).

Then
\tilde{A},A_{1}
and
A_{2}
have unbounded increasing sequences of eigenvalues,
\left\{\mu_{j}\right\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
,
\left\{\lambda_{j}^{1}\right\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
and
\left\{\lambda_{j}^{2}\right\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
respectively, such that

(430)

\left\{\mu_{j}\right\}_{j=1}^{\infty}=\left\{\lambda_{j}^{1}\right\}_{j=1}^{\infty}\cup\left\{\lambda_{j}^{2}\right\}_{j=1}^{\infty}.

If
\lambda_{j}^{1}\geq c_{1}j^{\beta_{1}}
and
\lambda_{j}^{2}\geq c_{1}j^{\beta_{2}}
for all nonnegative integers
j
, then

(431)

\mu_{j}\geq\frac{\min\left\{c_{1},c_{2}\right\}}{2^{1+\min\left\{\beta_{1},\beta_{2}\right\}}}j^{\min\left\{\beta_{1},\beta_{2}\right\}}

for all integers
j\geq 0
. Consequently, the sum of the first
N
eigenvalues of
\tilde{A}
obeys

(432)

\mu_{1}+\dots+\mu_{N}\geq C_{\beta_{1},\beta_{2}}\min\left\{c_{1},c_{2}\right\}N^{1+\min\left\{\beta_{1},\beta_{2}\right\}}

for some positive constants
C_{\beta_{1},\beta_{2}}
depending only
\beta_{1}
and
\beta_{2}
.

Proof.
The operators
A_{1}^{-1}
and
A_{2}^{-1}
are self-adjoint, injective, and compact, with ranges
\mathcal{D}(A_{1})
and
\mathcal{D}(A_{2})
respectively. By the spectral theory for Hilbert spaces, there are orthonormal bases of
\tilde{H}
,
\left\{\xi_{j}^{1}\right\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
and
\left\{\xi_{j}^{2}\right\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
, consisting of eigenfunctions of the operators
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
respectively, such that

(433)

A_{1}\xi_{j}^{1}=\lambda_{j}^{1}\xi_{j}^{1},

(434)

A_{2}\xi_{j}^{2}=\lambda_{j}^{2}\xi_{j}^{2},

with
0<\lambda_{1}^{1}\leq\lambda_{2}^{1}\leq\dots\leq\lambda_{j}^{1}\leq\lambda_{j+1}^{1}\leq\dots\rightarrow\infty
and
0<\lambda_{1}^{2}\leq\lambda_{2}^{2}\leq\dots\leq\lambda_{j}^{2}\leq\lambda_{j+1}^{2}\leq\dots\rightarrow\infty
.
The operator
\tilde{A}^{-1}
is also self-adjoint, injective, and compact in
\tilde{H}\times\tilde{H}
, so there is an orthonormal basis of
\tilde{H}\times\tilde{H}
consisting of eigenvectors
\left\{\xi_{j}\right\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
of
\tilde{A}
, such that

(435)

\tilde{A}\xi_{j}=\mu_{j}\xi_{j},

with
0<\mu_{1}\leq\mu_{2}\leq\dots\leq\mu_{j}\leq\mu_{j+1}\leq\dots\rightarrow\infty
. The eigenvalues of
\tilde{A}
are precisely the collection of eigenvalues of
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
, counted with multiplicity. For
N\geq 1
, we have

(436)

\left\{\mu_{i}:i=1,\dots,N\right\}=\left\{\lambda_{i}^{1}:i=1,\dots,j\right\}\cup\left\{\lambda_{i}^{2}:i=1,\dots,k\right\}

for some nonnegative integers
j
and
k
obeying
N=j+k
. If
\mu_{N}=\lambda_{j}^{1}
, then
\mu_{N}\geq c_{1}j^{\beta_{1}}
and
\mu_{N}\geq\lambda_{k}^{2}\geq c_{2}k^{\beta_{2}}
. If
\mu_{N}=\lambda_{k}^{2}
, then
\mu_{N}\geq c_{2}k^{\beta_{2}}
and
\mu_{N}\geq\lambda_{j}^{1}\geq c_{1}j^{\beta_{1}}
. Thus, we infer that

\displaystyle\mu_{N}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\mu_{N}+\frac{1}{2}\mu_{N}\geq\frac{c_{1}}{2}j^{\beta_{1}}+\frac{c_{2}}{2}k^{\beta_{2}}\geq\frac{1}{2}\min\left\{c_{1},c_{2}\right\}\left[j^{\min\left\{\beta_{1},\beta_{2}\right\}}+k^{\min\left\{\beta_{1},\beta_{2}\right\}}\right]

(437)

\displaystyle\quad\quad\geq\frac{\min\left\{c_{1},c_{2}\right\}}{2^{1+\min\left\{\beta_{1},\beta_{2}\right\}}}(j+k)^{\min\left\{\beta_{1},\beta_{2}\right\}}=\frac{\min\left\{c_{1},c_{2}\right\}}{2^{1+\min\left\{\beta_{1},\beta_{2}\right\}}}N^{\min\left\{\beta_{1},\beta_{2}\right\}}.

As a consequence of these latter lower bounds, we obtain (
432
). This ends the proof of Lemma
2
.

Appendix B
Uniform Gronwall Lemma

We present a Gronwall Lemma that will be used to study the time asymptotic behavior of solutions.

Lemma 3
.

Let
y(t)
be a nonnegative function of time
t
that solves the differential inequality

(438)

\frac{d}{dt}y+cy\leq C_{1}+C_{2}F_{1}+C_{3}F_{2}y^{n},

where
c>0
is a positive real number,
C_{1},C_{2}
and
C_{3}
are nonnegative real numbers,
n
is a nonnegative integer, and
F_{1}
and
F_{2}
are nonegative functions of time
t
. Suppose there exists a time
t_{0}
and a positive number
R
such that
y(t_{0})<\infty
and, for any
t\geq t_{0}
, it holds that

(439)

\int_{t}^{t+1}F_{1}(s)ds\leq R

if
C_{3}=0
, and

(440)

\int_{t}^{t+1}\left[F_{1}(s)+F_{2}(s)y^{n-1}(s)+y(s)\right]ds\leq R

if
C_{3}\neq 0
and
n\geq 1
. Then

(441)

y(t)\leq\left(c^{-1}C_{1}+2C_{2}R+2R\right)e^{2C_{3}R}

for all times
t\geq t_{0}+1
.

Proof.
We distinguish two cases:
C_{3}\neq 0
,
n\geq 1
and
C_{3}=0
. In the first case, we fix two times
s
and
t
such that
t_{0}\leq s\leq t
. We multiply both sides of the inequality (
438
) by
e^{ct-C_{3}\int_{s}^{t}F_{2}y^{n-1}(\tau)d\tau}
and integrate in time from
s
to
t
. We obtain the bound

(442)

y(t)\leq\left(y(s)+\frac{C_{1}}{c}+C_{2}\int_{s}^{t}F_{1}(\tau)d\tau\right)\exp\left\{C_{3}\int_{s}^{t}F_{2}(\tau)y^{n-1}(\tau)d\tau\right\}.

In view of (
440
), we have

(443)

\int_{t_{0}+k}^{t_{0}+k+1}y(\tau)d\tau\leq R

for any nonnegative integer
k\geq 0
. Thus, for each integer
k\geq 0
, there exists a time
\tilde{t}_{k}\in[t_{0}+k,t_{0}+k+1]
such that

(444)

y(\tilde{t}_{k})\leq 2R.

We note that the distance between two consecutive times
\tilde{t}_{k}
and
\tilde{t}_{k+1}
does not exceed two. By making use of (
440
), we infer that

\displaystyle y(t)

\displaystyle\leq\left(y(\tilde{t}_{k})+\frac{C_{1}}{c}+C_{2}\int_{\tilde{t}_{k}}^{\tilde{t}_{k+1}}F_{1}(\tau)d\tau\right)\exp\left\{C_{3}\int_{\tilde{t}_{k}}^{\tilde{t}_{k+1}}F_{2}(\tau)y^{n-1}(\tau)d\tau\right\}

(445)

\displaystyle\leq\left(2R+\frac{C_{1}}{c}+2C_{2}R\right)e^{2C_{3}R}

for any
t\in[\tilde{t}_{k},\tilde{t}_{k+1}]
. Therefore, (
B
) holds on the time interval
[\tilde{t}_{0},\infty)
, yielding the desired bound (
441
). In the case where
C_{3}
vanishes, the estimate (
441
) holds as a consequence of
[
1
, Lemma 1]
.

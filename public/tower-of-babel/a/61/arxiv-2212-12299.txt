Rota-Baxter family Ω \Omega -associative conformal algebras and their cohomology theory
Yuanyuan Zhang, Jun Zhao, Genqiang Liu

Abstract
. In this paper, we first propose the concept of Rota-Baxter family Ω \Omega -associative conformal algebras, then we study the cohomology theory of Rota-Baxter family Ω \Omega -associative conformal algebras of any weight and justify it by interpreting the lower degree cohomology groups as formal deformations.



2010 Mathematics Subject Classification

16W99, 16E40, 16S80, 16S70

1.
Introduction

Rota-Baxter algebra was introduced by Baxter
[
4
]
. Later some combinatoric properties of Rota-Baxter algebras were studied by Rota
[
32
]
and Cartier
[
8
]
.
Let
\mathbb{F}
be any field and
\lambda\in\mathbb{F}
. A Rota-Baxter algebra of weight
\lambda
is an associative
\mathbb{F}
-algebra with a Rota-Baxter operator
P:A\rightarrow A
satisfying

P(a)P(b)=P(aP(b)+P(a)b+\lambda ab),\,\text{ for }\,a,b\in A.

The concept of algebras with multiple linear operators (also called
\Omega
-algebra) was first introduced by A. G. Kurosch in
[
28
]
. The concept of Rota-Baxter family algebra is a generalization of Rota-Baxter algebras
[
21
]
, which was proposed by Guo. It arises naturally in renormalization of quantum field theory (
[
13
, Proposition 9.1]
and
[
30
, Theorem 3.7.2]
). Recently, many scholars begin to pay attention to families algebraic structures, such as Foissy
[
15
]
, Zhang and Gao
[
37
,
19
]
, Aguiar
[
2
]
, Das
[
9
]
and so on.

The notion of a conformal algebra encodes an axiomatic description of the operator product expansion (OPE) of chiral fields in conformal field theory and it is an adequate tool for the study of infinite-dimensional Lie algebras satisfying the locality property
[
3
,
26
,
27
]
and Hamiltonian formalism in the theory of nonlinear evolution equations
[
12
]
. Lie conformal algebra appears as a useful tool in studying vertex algebra.
An algebraic formalization of the properties of the OPE in 2-dimensional conformal field theory
[
6
]
gives rise to a new class of algebraic systems, vertex operator algebras
[
7
,
14
]
. The singular part of the OPE describes the commutator of two fields, and the corresponding algebraic structures are called conformal (Lie) algebras
[
26
]
or vertex Lie algebras
[
16
]
.
Lie conformal algebras correspond to vertex algebras by the same way as Lie algebras correspond to their associative enveloping algebras. In particular, associative conformal algebras naturally appear in the representation theory of Lie conformal algebras. In
[
23
]
, Hong and Bai developed a bialgebra theory for associative conformal algebras, which is not only a conformal analogue of associative bialgebras, but also an associative analogue of conformal bialgebras. They also introduced the notions of
\cal{O}
-operators of associative conformal algebras and dendriform conformal algebras to construct solutions of associative conformal Yang-Baxter equation. In
[
25
]
, the authors give a study of the
\mathbb{F}[\partial]
-split extending structures problem for associative conformal algebras.

The formal deformation theory of algebraic structures was first developed for associative algebras in the classical work of Gerstenhaber
[
17
,
18
]
, which is closely related to the cohomology theory of Hochschild cohomology on associative algebras
[
22
]
.
Then Nijenhuis and Richard
[
31
]
extended it to Lie algebras. Recently, Tang, Bai, Guo and Sheng
[
33
]
developed deformation theory and cohomology theory of
\cal{O}
-operators on Lie algebras. Das
[
9
]
developed the corresponding weight zero cohomology theory for Rota-Baxter associative algebra. Wang and Zhou
[
34
,
35
]
defined cohomology theory for Rota-Baxter associative algebras of any weight, determined the underlying
L_{\infty}
-algebra and also showed that the dg operad of homotopy Rota-Baxter associative algebras is the minimal model of that of Rota-Baxter associative algebras. In
[
5
]
, the authors develop a cohomology theory of conformal algebras with coefficients in an arbitrary module and it possess standards properties of cohomology theories. In
[
36
]
, the authors study
\cal{O}
-operator of associative conformal algebras with respect to conformal bimodules and construct cohomology of
\cal{O}
-operators.

In the present paper, we introduce the Rota-Baxter family version of associative conformal algebras, and their cohomology theory. This paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we mainly introduce some basic concepts of conformal algebras, Rota-Baxter family conformal algebras,
\Omega
-associative conformal algebras and construct a new bimodule structure on
\Omega
-associative conformal algebras. In Section
3
, we define a cohomology theory for Rota-Baxter family
\Omega
-associative conformal algebras of any weight and built a chain map between the cohomology of Rota-Baxter family operator and the cohomology of Rota-Baxter family
\Omega
-associative conformal algebras. In Section
4
, we study formal deformations of Rota-Baxter family on
\Omega
-associative conformal algebras and interpret them via lower degree cohomology groups.
In Section
5
, we study abelian extensions of Rota-Baxter family
\Omega
-associative conformal algebras and show that they are classified by the second cohomology, as one would expect of a good cohomology theory.

Notations

Throught this paper, we denote by
\mathbb{F}
any field and
\mathbb{R}
the field of real numbers. All tensors over
\mathbb{F}
are denoted by
\otimes.
If
A
is a vector space, then the space of polynomials of
\lambda
with coefficients in
A
is denoted by
A[\lambda].

Rota-Baxter family conformal algebras

In this subsection, we first recall the concept of Rota-Baxter family algebras,
which arises naturally in renormalization of quantum field theory

, some related results can be found in
[
2
,

,
38
,

,

]
. Then we recall some definitions, notations and results about conformal algebras and Rota-Baxter conformal algebras

.

Definition 2.1
.

(
[
13
,
20
]
)
Let
\Omega
be a semigroup and
q\in\mathbb{F}
be given.
A
Rota-Baxter family
of weight
q
on an algebra
A
is a collection of linear operators
(P_{\omega})_{\omega\in\Omega}:A\rightarrow A
such that

P_{\alpha}(a)P_{\beta}(b)=P_{\alpha\beta}\left(P_{\alpha}(a)b+aP_{\beta}(b)+qab\right),\,\text{ for }\,a,b\in A\,\text{ and }\,\alpha,\,\beta\in\Omega.

Then the pair
(A,\,(P_{\omega})_{\omega\in\Omega})
is called a
Rota-Baxter family algebra
of weight
q
.

Example 2.2
.

[
40
]

Let
\Omega=(\mathbb{R},+)
be a semigroup, and
R
is the
\mathbb{R}
-algebra consisting of all continous functions from
\mathbb{R}
to
\mathbb{R}
, the multiplication is defined by

(fg)(x):=f(x)g(x),\text{ for }\,f,g\in R.

And for any
\alpha\in\Omega
, define a family linear operator
P_{\alpha}:R\rightarrow R
as follows

P_{\alpha}(f)(x)=e^{-\alpha A(x)}\int_{0}^{x}e^{\alpha A(t)}f(t)\,dt,\,\text{ for }\,\alpha\in\Omega,

where
A
is a fixed nonzero element of
R
. Then
(P_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in\Omega}
is a Rota-Baxter family of weight
0
. In fact, for any
f,g\in R
, we have

\displaystyle P_{\alpha+\beta}\Big(P_{\alpha}(f)g+fP_{\beta}(g)\Big)(x)=e^{-(\alpha+\beta)A(x)}\int_{0}^{x}e^{(\alpha+\beta)A(t)}\Big(P_{\alpha}(f)(t)g(t)+f(t)P_{\beta}(g)(t)\Big)\,dt

\displaystyle=e^{-(\alpha+\beta)A(x)}\int_{0}^{x}e^{(\alpha+\beta)A(t)}\Biggl(g(t)e^{-\alpha A(t)}\int_{0}^{t}e^{\alpha A(s)}f(s)\,ds+f(t)e^{-\beta A(t)}\int_{0}^{t}e^{\beta A(s)}g(s)ds\Biggl)\,dt

\displaystyle=e^{-(\alpha+\beta)A(x)}\Biggl(\int_{0}^{x}g(t)e^{\beta A(t)}\Big(\int_{0}^{t}e^{\alpha A(s)}f(s)\,ds\Big)\,dt+\int_{0}^{x}e^{\alpha A(t)}f(t)\Big(\int_{0}^{t}e^{\beta A(s)}g(s)\,ds\Big)\,dt\Biggl)

\displaystyle=e^{-(\alpha+\beta)A(x)}\left(\iint_{0\leq s\leq t\leq x}e^{\beta A(t)}g(t)e^{\alpha A(s)}f(s)\,dsdt+\iint_{0\leq t\leq s\leq x}e^{\beta A(t)}g(t)e^{\alpha A(s)}f(s)\,dsdt\right)

\displaystyle=e^{-(\alpha+\beta)A(x)}\int_{0}^{x}e^{\alpha A(t)}f(t)\,dt\int_{0}^{x}e^{\beta A(t)}g(t)\,dt=P_{\alpha}f(x)P_{\beta}g(x).

Hence
P_{\alpha+\beta}\Big(P_{\alpha}(f)g+fP_{\beta}(g)\Big)=P_{\alpha}(f)P_{\beta}(g).

Definition 2.3
.

[
3
]
An associative conformal algebra is a
\mathbb{F}[\partial]
-module
A
endowed with a
\lambda
-multiplication
a_{\lambda}b
which defines a
\mathbb{F}
-linear map

\mu_{\lambda}:A\otimes A\rightarrow A[\lambda],\quad(a,b)\mapsto a\cdot_{\lambda}b:=a_{\lambda}b,\,\text{ for }\,a,b\in A,

satisfying the following axiom

\displaystyle(\partial a)_{\lambda}b=

\displaystyle-\lambda a_{\lambda}b,\quad a_{\lambda}\partial b=(\partial+\lambda)a_{\lambda}b,\quad\text{(conformal sesquilinearity-I)}

\displaystyle(a_{\lambda}b)_{\lambda+\mu}c=a_{\lambda}(b_{\mu}c).\quad\text{(associativity)}

A conformal algebra is called
finite
if it is finitely generated as
\mathbb{F}[\partial]
-module. The
rank
of a conformal algebra
A
is its rank as a
\mathbb{F}[\partial]
-module.

Remark 2.4
.

[
3
]

The following properties always hold in an associative conformal algebra:

\displaystyle a_{\lambda}\big(b_{-\partial-\mu}c\big)

\displaystyle=\big(a_{\lambda}b\big)_{-\partial-\mu}c,

\displaystyle a_{-\partial-\lambda}\big(b_{\mu}c\big)

\displaystyle=\big(a_{-\partial-\mu}b\big)_{-\partial+\mu-\lambda}c,

\displaystyle a_{-\partial-\lambda}\big(b_{-\partial-\mu}c\big)

\displaystyle=\big(a_{-\partial+\mu-\lambda}b\big)_{-\partial-\mu}c.

Example 2.5
.

[
23
]

Let
(A,\cdot)
be an associative algebra. Then
\operatorname{Cur}(A)=\mathbb{F}[\partial]\otimes A
is an associative conformal algebra with the following
\lambda
-product:

(p(\partial)a)_{\lambda}(q(\partial)b)=p(-\lambda)q(\lambda+\partial)(a\cdot b),\,\text{ for }\,p(\partial),q(\partial)\in\mathbb{F}[\partial]\,\text{ and }\,a,b\in A.

The author in
[
23
]
introduced the associative conformal Yang-Baxter equation as a conformal analogue of the associative equation and interpret it in terms of its operator forms by introducing the notion of
\mathcal{O}
-operators (generalized Rota-Baxter operator) of associative conformal algebras. They also proved an antisymmetric solution of the associative conformal Yang-Baxter equation corresponds to the skew-symmetric part of a conformal linear map
P
.

Definition 2.6
.

[
23
]

Let
A
be an associative conformal algebra. If
P:A\rightarrow A
is a
\mathbb{F}[\partial]
-module homomorphism satisfying

P(a)_{\lambda}P(b)=P(P(a)_{\lambda}b)+P(a_{\lambda}P(b))+qP(a_{\lambda}b),\,\text{ for }\,a,b\in A,

then
P
is called a Rota-Baxter operator of weight
q
on
A
, where
q\in\mathbb{F}.

Example 2.7
.

[
23
]

Let
A
be a finite associative conformal algebra which is free as a
\mathbb{F}[\partial]
-module and
r\in A\otimes A
be a symmetric Frobenius conformal algebra, that is,
A
has a non-degenerate symmetric invariant conformal bilinear form, then
r
is a solution of associative conformal Yang-Baxter equation if and only if
P_{0}^{r}
is a Rota-Baxter operator of weight zero on
A
.

Rota-Baxter family algebra is a generalization of Rota-Baxter algebra, which arises naturally in renormalization of quantum field theory
[
13
]
. Recently, some scholars begin to pay attention to the study of family algebra structures, such as Aguiar
[
2
]
, Foissy
[
15
]
and so on. Now we propose the concept of Rota-Baxter family conformal algebras of weight
q
.

Definition 2.8
.

Let
\Omega
be a semigroup and
A
an associative conformal algebra. If
(P_{\omega})_{\omega\in\Omega}:A\rightarrow A
is a family of
\mathbb{F}[\partial]
-module homomorphisms satisfying

P_{\alpha}(a)_{\lambda}P_{\beta}(b)=P_{\alpha\beta}(P_{\alpha}(a)_{\lambda}b+a_{\lambda}P_{\beta}(b)+qa_{\lambda}b),\,\text{ for }\,a,b\in A,

then
(P_{\omega})_{\omega\in\Omega}
is called
a Rota-Baxter family operator of weight
q
on
A
, where
q\in\mathbb{F}.

Example 2.9
.

Let
(R,(P_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in\Omega})
be a Rota-Baxter family algebra defined just as Example
2.2
. Define

f\cdot_{\lambda}g:=fg,\,\text{ for }\,f,g\in R\text{ and satisfy}\,P_{\alpha}\partial=\partial P_{\alpha}.

Then
\operatorname{Cur}(R)=\mathbb{R}[\partial]\otimes R
endowed with a natural Rota-Baxter family conformal associative algebra and
(P_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in\Omega}
is a Rota-Baxter family of weight
0
.

The following Propositions provide the way to construct Rota-Baxter family conformal algebras.

Proposition 2.10
.

Let
\Omega
be a semigroup and
\mathbb{F}\Omega
a semigroup algebra.
Then
(A,\mu_{\lambda},(P_{\omega})_{\omega\in\Omega})
is a Rota-Baxter family conformal algebra of weight
q
, if and only if,

(A\otimes\mathbb{F}\Omega,P)
is a Rota-Baxter conformal algebra of weight
q
, where
P:A\otimes\mathbb{F}\Omega\rightarrow A\otimes\mathbb{F}\Omega,a\otimes\omega\mapsto P_{\omega}(a)\otimes\omega.

Proof.

For
x,y\in A
and
\alpha,\beta\in\Omega,
we have

\displaystyle P(x\otimes\alpha)_{\lambda}P(y\otimes\beta)

\displaystyle=\bigl(P_{\alpha}(x)\otimes\alpha\bigr)_{\lambda}(P_{\beta}(y)\otimes\beta)=\bigl(P_{\alpha}(x)_{\lambda}P_{\beta}(y)\bigr)\otimes\alpha\beta

\displaystyle=P_{\alpha\beta}\bigl({P_{\alpha}(x)}_{\lambda}y+x_{\lambda}P_{\beta}(y)+qx_{\lambda}y\bigr)\otimes\alpha\beta

\displaystyle=P\Bigl(\bigl({P_{\alpha}(x)}_{\lambda}y+x_{\lambda}P_{\beta}(y)+qx_{\lambda}y\bigr)\otimes\alpha\beta\Bigr)

\displaystyle=P\bigl({P_{\alpha}(x)}_{\lambda}y\otimes\alpha\beta+x_{\lambda}P_{\beta}(y)\otimes\alpha\beta+qx_{\lambda}y\otimes\alpha\beta\bigr)

\displaystyle=P\Bigl(\bigl(P_{\alpha}(x)\otimes\alpha\bigr)_{\lambda}(y\otimes\beta)+(x\otimes\alpha)_{\lambda}\bigl(P_{\beta}(y)\otimes\beta\bigr)+q(x\otimes\alpha)_{\lambda}(y\otimes\beta)\Bigr)

\displaystyle=P\bigl(P(x\otimes\alpha)_{\lambda}(y\otimes\beta)+(x\otimes\alpha)_{\lambda}P(y\otimes\beta)+qa(x\otimes\alpha)_{\lambda}(y\otimes\beta)\bigr),

as required.
∎

Proposition 2.12
.

Let
(A,\,\mu_{A,\,\lambda},(P_{\omega})_{\omega\in\Omega})
and
(B,\mu_{B,\,\lambda},(P_{\omega})_{\omega\in\Omega})
be two Rota-Baxter family conformal algebras. The tensor product
(A\otimes B,\mu_{A\otimes B,\lambda},(P_{\omega})_{\omega\in\Omega})
is still a Rota-Baxter family conformal algebra with the multiplication

\mu_{A\otimes B,\lambda}:(A\otimes B)\otimes(A\otimes B)\xrightarrow{\mathrm{id}_{A}\otimes\tau\otimes\mathrm{id}_{B}}A\otimes A\otimes B\otimes B\xrightarrow{\mu_{A,\,\lambda}\otimes\mu_{B,\,\lambda}}A\otimes B[\lambda],

\left(a_{1}\otimes b_{1}\right)\otimes\left(a_{2}\otimes b_{2}\right)\mapsto\left(a_{1}\cdot_{\lambda}a_{2}\right)\otimes\left(b_{1}\cdot_{\lambda}b_{2}\right),a_{1},a_{2}\in A,b_{1},b_{2}\in B\text{, }

with
\tau:B\otimes A\rightarrow A\otimes B,b\otimes a\mapsto a\otimes b
being the switch map,
and define

P_{\omega}(a_{1}\otimes b_{1}):=P_{\omega}(a_{1})\otimes b_{1}.

Proof.

We only need to prove the Rota-Baxter family conformal relation, that is

P_{\alpha}(a_{1}\otimes b_{1})\cdot_{\lambda}P_{\beta}(a_{2}\otimes b_{2})=P_{\alpha\beta}\Big(P_{\alpha}(a_{1}\otimes b_{1})\cdot_{\lambda}(a_{2}\otimes b_{2})+(a_{1}\otimes b_{1})\cdot_{\lambda}P_{\beta}(a_{2}\otimes b_{2})+q(a_{1}\otimes b_{1})\cdot_{\lambda}(a_{2}\otimes b_{2})\Big).

We have

\displaystyle P_{\alpha}(a_{1}\otimes b_{1})\cdot_{\lambda}P_{\beta}(a_{2}\otimes b_{2})

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle(P_{\alpha}(a_{1})\otimes b_{1})\cdot_{\lambda}(P_{\beta}(a_{2})\otimes b_{2})=\Big(P_{\alpha}(a_{1})\cdot_{\lambda}P_{\beta}(a_{2})\Big)\otimes(b_{1}\cdot_{\lambda}b_{2})

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle P_{\alpha\beta}\Big((P_{\alpha}(a_{1})\cdot_{\lambda}a_{2})+(a_{1}\cdot_{\lambda}P_{\beta}(a_{2}))+q(a_{1}\cdot_{\lambda}a_{2})\Big)\otimes(b_{1}\cdot_{\lambda}b_{2})

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle P_{\alpha\beta}\Big(P_{\alpha}(a_{1}\otimes b_{1})\cdot_{\lambda}(a_{2}\otimes b_{2})+(a_{1}\otimes b_{1})\cdot_{\lambda}P_{\beta}(a_{2}\otimes b_{2})+q(a_{1}\otimes b_{1})\cdot_{\lambda}(a_{2}\otimes b_{2})\Big).

∎

.

(1)

Proposition 2.15
.

Let
\Omega
be a semigroup and
(d_{\omega})_{\omega\in\Omega}
a family of invertible maps. Let
(A,(d_{\omega})_{\omega\in\Omega})
be a differential family conformal algebra of weight
q
. Then the collection
(d^{-1}_{\omega})_{\omega\in\Omega}
is a Rota-Baxter family operator of weight
q
.

Proof.

Since
(d_{\omega})_{\omega\in\Omega}
is an invertible family, suppose that
a=d^{-1}_{\alpha}(u)
and
b=d^{-1}_{\beta}(v)
in Eq. (
1
). Then we have

d_{\alpha\beta}(d^{-1}_{\alpha}(u)_{\lambda}d^{-1}_{\beta}(v))=u_{\lambda}d^{-1}_{\beta}(v)+d^{-1}_{\alpha}(u)_{\lambda}v+qu_{\lambda}v,

This shows that the collection

∎

2.2.
Rota-Baxter family conformal modules

In this subsection, we first recall conformal module from
[

,

,
24
]
, then we give the definition of Rota-Baxter family conformal bimodule and construct a new Rota-Baxter family conformal algebra via semi-direct product.

Definition 2.16
.

[
3
]

Let
A
be an associative conformal algebra and
M
a
\mathbb{F}[\partial]
-module.

(1)

A left module
M
over an associative conformal algebra
A
is a
\mathbb{F}[\partial]
-module endowed with a
\mathbb{F}
-bilinear map
A\times M\rightarrow M[\lambda]
,
(a,m)\mapsto a\cdot_{\lambda}m
, and for all
a,b\in A
and
m\in M
satisfying

\displaystyle(\partial a)\cdot_{\lambda}m

\displaystyle=-\lambda a\cdot_{\lambda}m,

\displaystyle a\cdot_{\lambda}(\partial m)

\displaystyle=(\partial+\lambda)(a\cdot_{\lambda}m),

\displaystyle(a\cdot_{\lambda}b)\cdot_{\lambda+\mu}m

\displaystyle=a\cdot_{\lambda}(b\cdot_{\mu}m).

(2)

A right module
M
over an associative conformal algebra
A
is a
\mathbb{F}[\partial]
-module endowed with a
\mathbb{F}
-bilinear map
M\times A\rightarrow M[\lambda]
,
(m,a)\mapsto m\cdot_{\lambda}a
, and for all
a,b\in A
and
m\in M
satisfying

\displaystyle(\partial m)\cdot_{\lambda}a

\displaystyle=-\lambda m\cdot_{\lambda}a,

\displaystyle m\cdot_{\lambda}(\partial a)

\displaystyle=(\partial+\lambda)(m\cdot_{\lambda}a),

\displaystyle(m\cdot_{\lambda}a)\cdot_{\lambda+\mu}b

\displaystyle=m\cdot_{\lambda}(a\cdot_{\mu}b).

(3)

M
is called a conformal bimodule of
A
(or conformal
A
-bimodule) if it is both a left conformal module and a right conformal module, and for all
a,b\in A
and
m\in M
satisfying the following compatible condition

\displaystyle(a\cdot_{\lambda}m)\cdot_{\lambda+\mu}b=a\cdot_{\lambda}(m\cdot_{\mu}b).

Definition 2.17
.

[
3
]

Let
M,N
be two
\mathbb{F}[\partial]
-modules.
A conformal linear map
from
M
to
N
is a
\mathbb{F}
-linear map
f:M\rightarrow N[\lambda]
, denoted
f_{\lambda}:M\rightarrow N
, such that
f_{\lambda}\partial=(\partial+\lambda)f_{\lambda}
. The space of such maps is denoted by
\operatorname{Chom}(M,N)
. It has canonical structures of a
\mathbb{F}[\partial]
-module

\displaystyle(\partial f)_{\lambda}

\displaystyle=-\lambda f_{\lambda},\,\text{ for }\,f\in\operatorname{Chom}(M,N).

In the special case
M
, let
\operatorname{Cend}(M)=\operatorname{Chom}(M,M)
denote the space of conformal linear endomorphisms of
M
. Then
\operatorname{Cend}(M)
carries the natural structure of an associative conformal algebra, defined by

\left(f_{\lambda}g\right)_{\mu}m:=f_{\lambda}\big(g_{\mu-\lambda}m\big),\quad f,g\in\operatorname{Cend}(M),m\in M.

Remark 2.18
.

[
23
]

Set
a\cdot_{\lambda}v=l_{A}(a)_{\lambda}v
and
v\cdot_{\lambda}a=r_{A}(a)_{-\lambda-\partial}v
. Then a structure of a bimodule
M
over an associative conformal algebra
A
is the same as two
\mathbb{F}[\partial]
-module homomorphisms
l_{A}
and
r_{A}
from
A
to
\operatorname{Cend}(M)
such that the following conditions hold:

\displaystyle l_{A}\left(a_{\lambda}b\right)_{\lambda+\mu}v

\displaystyle=l_{A}(a)_{\lambda}(l_{A}(b)_{\mu}v),

\displaystyle r_{A}(b)_{-\lambda-\mu-\partial}(r_{A}(a)_{-\lambda-\partial}v)

\displaystyle=r_{A}(a_{\mu}b)_{-\lambda-\partial}v,

\displaystyle l_{A}(a)_{\lambda}(r_{A}(b)_{-\mu-\partial}v)

\displaystyle=r_{A}(b)_{-\lambda-\mu-\partial}(l_{A}(a)_{\lambda}v),

for all
a,b\in A
and
v\in M
. Denoted by this bimodule by
(M,l_{A},r_{A}).

Definition 2.19
.

Let
(A,\mu_{\lambda},(P_{\omega})_{\omega\in\Omega})
be a Rota-Baxter family conformal algebras of weight
q
and
M
be a bimodule
over associative conformal algebra
(A,\mu_{\lambda})
. We say that

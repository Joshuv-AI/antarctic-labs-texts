Bifurcation results for nonlinear eigenvalue problems involving the ( p , q ) (p,q) -Laplace operator.
Emmanuel Wend-Benedo Zongo and Bernhard Ruf

Abstract
. In this paper, we analyze an eigenvalue problem for nonlinear elliptic operators involving homogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions in a open smooth bounded domain. We prove bifurcation results from trivial solutions and from infinity for the considered nonlinear eigenvalue problem. We also show the existence of multiple solutions of the nonlinear problem using variational methods. Keywords: nonlinear operators, variational eigenvalues, bifurcation from trivial solution, bifurcation from infinity, multiple solutions. 2010 Mathematics Subject Classification: 35J20, 35J92, 35J25.

Introduction

Assume
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
(
N\geq 2
) is an open bounded domain with smooth boundary
\partial\Omega.

In
[
8
]
, the authors investigated the asymptotic behavior of the spectrum and the existence of multiple solutions of the following nonlinear eigenvalue problem

(1.1)

\begin{cases}-\Delta_{p}u-\Delta u=\lambda u\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \text{on}\penalty\ \penalty\ \Omega,\\
u=0\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \text{on}\penalty\ \penalty\ \partial\Omega,\end{cases}

where
-\Delta_{p}
denotes the
p
-Laplace operator. In
[
8
]
it was shown that for
p>2
there exist eigenvalue branches emanating from
(\lambda_{k},0),
and for
1<p<2
there exist eigenvalue branches emanating from
(\lambda_{k},\infty)
, where
\lambda_{k}
stands as the
k
-th Dirichlet eigenvalue of the Laplacian.
In this paper, we consider the following q-homogenous eigenvalue problem with a perturbation by a p-Laplace term:

(1.2)

\begin{cases}-\Delta_{p}u-\Delta_{q}u=\lambda|u|^{q-2}u\penalty\ \penalty\ \text{in}\penalty\ \penalty\ \Omega\\
u=0\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \text{on}\penalty\ \penalty\ \partial\Omega.\end{cases}

The operator
\Delta_{s},
formally defined as
\Delta_{s}u:=\text{div}(|\nabla u|^{s-2}\nabla u)
for
s=p,q\in(1,\infty)
is the
s
-Laplacian,
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
is a parameter. The
(p,q)
-Laplace operator given by
-\Delta_{p}-\Delta_{q}
appears in a wide range of applications that include biophysics
[
10
]
, plasma physics
[
15
]
and reaction-diffusion equations
[
2
,
9
]
. The
(p,q)
-Laplace operator has been widely studied; for some results related to our studies, see e.g.,
[
5
,
6
,
4
,
14
,
12
]
. We say that
\lambda
is a “first eigenvalue”, if the corresponding eigenfunction
u
is positive or negative.

Note that by taking
q=2
in equation (
1.2
), we recover the case of equation (
1.1
). We remark however that for
q=2
equation (
1.2
) describes bifurcation (caused by a
p
-Laplace operator) from the
linear
equation
-\Delta u-\lambda u
, while for
q\not=2
we prove for equation (
1.2
) the existence of bifurcation branches (again forced by a
p
-Laplace operator) from the eigenvalues of a
nonlinear, but
q
-homogenous
equation. Indeed, it was shown in
[
11

that there exists a nondecreasing sequence of

positive eigenvalues
\{\lambda_{k}^{D}(q)\}_{k}
tending to

as

for the following nonlinear and

-homogenous eigenvalue problem

(1.3)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}-\Delta_{q}u=\displaystyle\lambda|u|^{q-2}u\penalty\ \penalty\ \text{in $\Omega$},\\
u=\displaystyle 0\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \text{on $\partial\Omega$}.\end{array}\right.

Moreover, it is known that the first eigenvalue of problem (
1.3
) is characterized in the variational point of view by,

\lambda^{D}_{1}(q):=\inf_{u\in W^{1,q}_{0}(\Omega)\backslash\{0\}}\left\{\frac{\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{q}\penalty\ dx}{\int_{\Omega}|u|^{q}\penalty\ dx}\right\}.

We consider the sets

D_{1}(q)=\{u\in W^{1,q}_{0}(\Omega)\backslash\{0\}\penalty\ :\penalty\ \int_{\Omega}|u|^{q}dx=1\},

and
\Sigma,
the class of closed symmetric (with respect to the origin) subsets of
W^{1,q}_{0}(\Omega)\backslash\{0\},
i.e,

\Sigma=\{A\subset W^{1,q}_{0}(\Omega)\backslash\{0\}:\penalty\ \penalty\ A\penalty\ \penalty\ \text{closed},\penalty\ \penalty\ A=-A\}.

For

we define

\gamma(A)=\inf\{k\in\mathbb{N}\penalty\ :\penalty\ \exists\varphi\in C(A,\mathbb{R}^{k}\backslash\{0\}),\penalty\ \varphi(-x)=-\varphi(x)\}.

If such

does not exist, we then define

The number

Let us consider the family of sets

\Sigma_{k}=\{A\subset\Sigma\cap D_{1}(q):\penalty\ \gamma(A)\geq k\}.

Following the proof in

\lambda^{D}_{k}(q)=\displaystyle{\inf_{A\in\Sigma_{k}}\sup_{u\in A}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{q}dx}.

In this paper, we discuss the nonlinear variational eigenvalues of equation (

Our main results are:

1.2

In this section we show that equation (

Definition 2.1
.

We say that
u\in W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)

(
if
p>q

)
or
u\in W^{1,q}_{0}(\Omega)

(

)
is a weak solution of problem (

) if the following integral equality holds:

(2.1)

\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u\cdot\nabla v\penalty\ dx+\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{q-2}\nabla u\cdot\nabla v\penalty\ dx=\lambda\int_{\Omega}|u|^{q-2}u\,v\penalty\ dx,

for all
v\in W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)\cap W^{1,q}_{0}(\Omega)
.

We say that
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
is an eigenvalue of problem (
1.2
) if there exists an eigenfunction
u_{\lambda}\in(W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)\cap W^{1,q}_{0}(\Omega))\backslash\{0\}
associated to
\lambda
such that relation (
2.1
) holds.

We say that
\lambda^{D}_{1}(p,q,\rho)
is a first eigenvalue of equation (
1.2
) if the corresponding eigenfunction
u_{1}(p,q;\rho)
is a minimizer of the following expression, for some
\rho>0
,

(2.2)

c_{1}(p,q;\rho):=\inf_{\{u\in W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)\cap W^{1,q}_{0}(\Omega),\int_{\Omega}|u|^{q}=\rho\}}\Big(\frac{1}{p}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{p}dx+\frac{1}{q}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{q}dx\Big).

Note that
\lambda^{D}_{1}(p,q;\rho)
satisfies

\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{1}|^{p}dx+\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{1}|^{q}dx=\lambda^{D}_{1}(p,q;\rho)\int_{\Omega}|u_{1}|^{q}dx=\lambda^{D}_{1}(p,q;\rho)\rho.

Proposition 2.2
.

If it holds
\lambda\leq\lambda^{D}_{1}(q)
then problem (
2.1
) has no nontrivial solutions.

Proof.

Suppose by contradiction that there exists
\lambda<\lambda^{D}_{1}(q)
which is an eigenvalue of problem (
1.2
) with
u_{\lambda}\in(W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)\cap W^{1,q}_{0}(\Omega))\backslash\{0\}
the corresponding eigenfunction. Let
v=u_{\lambda}
in relation (
2.1
), we then have

\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{\lambda}|^{p}dx+\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{\lambda}|^{q}dx=\lambda\int_{\Omega}|u_{\lambda}|^{q}dx.

On the other hand, we have

(2.3)

\lambda^{D}_{1}(q)\int_{\Omega}|u_{\lambda}|^{q}dx\leq\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{\lambda}|^{q}dx,

and subtracting by
\lambda\displaystyle{\int_{\Omega}}|u_{\lambda}|^{q}dx
from both sides of (
2.3
), it follows that

(\lambda^{D}_{1}(q)-\lambda)\int_{\Omega}|u_{\lambda}|^{q}dx\leq\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{\lambda}|^{q}dx-\lambda\int_{\Omega}|u_{\lambda}|^{q}dx.

This implies that

0<(\lambda^{D}_{1}(q)-\lambda)\int_{\Omega}|u_{\lambda}|^{q}dx\leq\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{\lambda}|^{q}dx+\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{\lambda}|^{p}dx-\lambda\int_{\Omega}|u_{\lambda}|^{q}dx=0.

Hence
\lambda<\lambda^{D}_{1}(q)
is not an eigenvalue of problem (
1.2
) with
u_{\lambda}\neq 0.

Now, assume that
\lambda=\lambda^{D}_{1}(q)
is an eigenvalue of equation (
1.2
), thus there exists an eigenfunction
u_{\lambda^{D}_{1}(q)}\in(W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)\cap W^{1,q}_{0}(\Omega))\backslash\{0\}
associated to
\lambda^{D}_{1}(q)
such that relation (
2.1
) holds. Letting
v=u_{\lambda^{D}_{1}(q)}
in (
2.1
), we obtain

\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{\lambda^{D}_{1}(q)}|^{p}dx+\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{\lambda^{D}_{1}(q)}|^{q}dx=\lambda^{D}_{1}(q)\int_{\Omega}|u_{\lambda^{D}_{1}(q)}|^{q}dx.

Since

\lambda^{D}_{1}(q)\int_{\Omega}|u_{\lambda^{D}_{1}(q)}|^{q}dx\leq\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{\lambda^{D}_{1}(q)}|^{q}dx,

it follows that

\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{\lambda^{D}_{1}(q)}|^{p}dx+\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{\lambda^{D}_{1}(q)}|^{q}dx\leq\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{\lambda^{D}_{1}(q)}|^{q}dx

and then
u_{\lambda^{D}_{1}(q)}=0
by the Poincaré inequality. This concludes the proof.
∎

Proposition 2.3
.

The first eigenfunctions
u_{1}^{\lambda}
associated to some
\lambda\in(\lambda^{D}_{1}(q),\infty)
are positive or negative in
\Omega.

Proof.

Let
u^{\lambda}_{1}\in(W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)\cap W^{1,1}_{0}(\Omega))\setminus\{0\}
be a first eigenfunction associated to
\lambda\in(\lambda^{D}_{1}(q),\infty),
then

\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u^{\lambda}_{1}|^{p}dx+\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u^{\lambda}_{1}|^{q}dx=\lambda\int_{\Omega}|u^{\lambda}_{1}|^{q}dx,

which means that
u^{\lambda}_{1}
achieves the infimum in the definition of
\mu_{1}(p,q;\rho)
, with
\rho=\frac{1}{q}\int_{\Omega}|u|^{q}
. On the other hand, we have
\big\|\nabla|u^{\lambda}_{1}|\big\|_{1,s}=\|\nabla u^{\lambda}_{1}\|_{1,s}
for
s=p,q
and
\big\||u^{\lambda}_{1}|\big\|_{q}=\|u^{\lambda}_{1}\|_{q}
since
\big|\nabla|u^{\lambda}_{1}|\big|=|\nabla u^{\lambda}_{1}|
and
\big||u^{\lambda}_{1}|\big|=|u^{\lambda}_{1}|
almost everywhere. Then, it follows that
|u^{\lambda}_{1}|
achieves also the infimum in the definition of
\mu_{1}(p,q;\rho).
Therefore by the Harnack inequality, we have
|u^{\lambda}_{1}|>0
for all
x\in\Omega
and consequently
u^{\lambda}_{1}
is either positive or negative in
\Omega.

∎

The Palais-Smale condition plays an important role in the minimax argument, and we recall here its definition.

Definition 2.4
.

A
C^{1}
functional
I
defined on a smooth submanifold
M
of a Banach space
X
is said to satisfy the Palais-Smale condition on
M
if any sequence
\{u_{n}\}\subset M
satisfying that
\{I(u_{n})\}_{n}
is bounded and
\big(I\big|_{M}\big)^{\prime}(u_{n})\rightarrow 0
as
n\rightarrow+\infty
has a convergent subsequence.

c_{k}(p,q;\rho)=\inf_{A\in\Sigma_{k}(p,q)}\sup_{u\in A}\left(\frac{1}{p}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{p}dx+\frac{1}{q}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{q}dx\right)>0

\displaystyle o(1)=\langle I^{\prime}(u_{n})-I^{\prime}(u),u_{n}-u\rangle

Remark 3.1
.

Let
p>q
. We recall that the nonlinear operator
\Theta:W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)\rightarrow W^{-1,q^{\prime}}(\Omega)\subset W^{-1,p^{\prime}}(\Omega)
defined by

\langle\Theta u,v\rangle=\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u\cdot\nabla v\penalty\ dx+\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{q-2}\nabla u\cdot\nabla v\penalty\ dx

is continuous and so it is demi-continuous. The operator
\Theta
is said to be demi-continuous if
\Theta
satisfies that whenever
u_{n}\in W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
converges to some
u\in W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
then
\Theta u_{n}\rightharpoonup\Theta u
as
n\rightarrow\infty.

In addition, we claim that the operator
\Theta
satisfies the following condition: for any
u_{n}\in W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
satisfying
u_{n}\rightharpoonup u
in
W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
and
\limsup\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\langle\Theta u_{n},u_{n}-u\rangle\leq 0,
then
u_{n}\rightarrow u
in
W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
as
n\rightarrow\infty.
The same result hold in the case where
p<q.

Indeed, assume that
u_{n}\rightharpoonup u
in
W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
and
\limsup\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\langle\Theta u_{n},u_{n}-u\rangle\leq 0.
Hence
u_{n}
converges strongly to
u
in
L^{p}(\Omega)
and one has

\begin{split}0&\geq\limsup\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\langle\Theta u_{n}-\Theta u,u_{n}-u\rangle\\
&=\limsup\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\int_{\Omega}\left[|\nabla u_{n}|^{p-2}\nabla u_{n}-|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u+|\nabla u_{n}|^{q-2}\nabla u_{n}-|\nabla u|^{q-2}\nabla u)\right]\cdot\nabla(u_{n}-u)dx.\end{split}

On the other hand, for any
\nabla u_{n},\nabla u\in(L^{p}(\Omega))^{N}
, one has,

\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}&\displaystyle(|\nabla u_{n}|^{p-2}\nabla u_{n}-|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u)\cdot\nabla(u_{n}-u)dx\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}(|\nabla u_{n}|^{p}+|\nabla u|^{p}-|\nabla u_{n}|^{p-2}\nabla u_{n}\cdot\nabla u\\
&\displaystyle-|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u\cdot\nabla u_{n})dx\\

We then deduce from this inequality that

as

as

Consequently

converges strongly to

in

4.
First eigenvalues

In this section we prove that every
\lambda>\lambda_{1}^{D}(q)
is a first eigenvalue of problem (

We define the energy functional
E_{\lambda}:W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)\cap W^{1,q}_{0}(\Omega)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
associated to relation (

(4.1)

E_{\lambda}(u)=\frac{1}{p}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{p}dx+\frac{1}{q}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{q}dx-\frac{\lambda}{q}\int_{\Omega}|u|^{q}dx.

Lemma 4.1
.

Suppose that
p>q.
Then for each
\lambda>0,
the functional
E_{\lambda}
defined in (

Proof.

If
p>q,
We have that

and the following inequalities hold true

(i)

\frac{1}{p}\displaystyle{\int_{\Omega}}|\nabla u|^{p}dx+\frac{1}{q}\displaystyle{\int_{\Omega}}|\nabla u|^{q}dx\geq\frac{1}{p}\displaystyle{\int_{\Omega}}|\nabla u|^{p}dx,

(ii)

\displaystyle{\int_{\Omega}}|\nabla u|^{q}dx\leq C\|u\|^{q}_{1,p}
(using the Hölder inequality).

With items (i) and (ii) we obtain
E_{\lambda}(u)\geq\frac{1}{p}\|u\|^{p}_{1,p}-\tilde{C}\|u\|^{q}_{1,p}
and consequently
E_{\lambda}(u)\rightarrow+\infty
as
\|u\|_{1,p}\rightarrow+\infty.

∎

Remark 4.2
.

We notice that

is not bounded below if

and
\lambda>\lambda^{D}_{1}(q)
since for every

the first eigenfunction of (
1.3
) with

we have

as

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
p>q
. Then every
\lambda\in(\lambda^{D}_{1}(q),\infty)
is a first eigenvalue of problem (
1.2
).

Proof.

Standard arguments show that

with its derivative given by

\langle E^{\prime}_{\lambda}(u),v\rangle=\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u\cdot\nabla v\penalty\ dx+\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{q-2}\nabla u\cdot\nabla v\penalty\ dx-\lambda\int_{\Omega}|u|^{q-2}u\penalty\ v\penalty\ dx,

for all

On the other hand

is weakly lower semi-continuous on

since

is a continuous convex functional. This fact and Lemma
4.1
allow one to apply a direct calculus of variations result in order to obtain the existence of global minimum point of

. We denote by
u_{0}
such a global minimum point, i.e,

We observe that for

(where

But
E_{\lambda}(u_{0})\leq E_{\lambda}(u_{\lambda})<0,

We also have that

∎

To treat the case where

we constrain

on the Nehari set

\begin{split}\mathcal{N}_{\lambda}&=\{u\in W^{1,q}_{0}(\Omega)/\penalty\ u\neq 0,\penalty\ \langle E^{\prime}_{\lambda}(u),u\rangle=0\}\\
&=\{u\in W^{1,q}_{0}(\Omega)/\penalty\ u\neq 0,\penalty\ \int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{p}dx+\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{q}dx=\lambda\int_{\Omega}|u|^{q}dx\}.\end{split}

On

the functional

reads as

This shows at once that

is coercive in the sense that if

satisfies

We define

and we show through a series of propositions that

is attained by some

which is a critical point of

considered on the whole space

and therefore a solution to equation (

).

Proposition 4.4
.

The set
\mathcal{N}_{\lambda}
is not empty for
\lambda>\lambda^{D}_{1}(q).

Proof.

Since

there exists

not identically zero such that

We then see that

which is solved by

∎

Proposition 4.5

Proof.

Let

(4.2)

Suppose on the contrary that

is not bounded i.e.
\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{n}|^{q}\penalty\ dx\rightarrow\infty
as

\displaystyle{\int_{\Omega}}|u_{n}|^{q}\penalty\ dx\rightarrow\infty
as

4.2

Since

∎

Theorem 4.8

Let

Then every

We recall that for

For every

certainly the function

does not vanish identically. Let

The map

5.
Bifurcation

In this section we discuss bifurcation phenomena for problem (

). We begin with the following.

Definition 5.1

A real number
\mu
is called a bifurcation point of (
1.2
) if and only if there is a sequence
(u_{n},\mu_{n})
of solutions of (

Observations:

Define

by

F(u)=\frac{\frac{1}{p}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{p}dx+\frac{1}{q}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{q}dx}{\frac{1}{q}\int_{\Omega}|u|^{q}dx},\penalty\ \penalty\ \text{for all}\penalty\ \penalty\ u\in W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)\cap W^{1,q}_{0}(\Omega).

By setting

, where

stands as the normalized eigenfunction associated to the eigenvalue

of the
q
-homogenous equation (

We distinguish two cases:

(i)

Assume that

Thus we find that
F(re_{1})\rightarrow\lambda^{D}_{1}(q)
as

which indicates bifurcation in
0
from
\lambda^{D}_{1}(q).

(ii)

Assume that
p<q.
We find that
F(re_{1})\rightarrow\infty
as

which indicates there is no bifurcation in
0
from
\lambda^{D}_{1}(q).
One is lead to look for bifurcation at infinity.

Our aim is to show that the variational
q
-homogenous eigenvalues
\lambda^{D}_{k}(q)
of equation (

\lambda^{D}_{k}(p,q;\rho)
of equation (

As in section

\int_{\Omega}|\nabla w_{k}|^{p}\penalty\ dx\leq C_{1}\|w_{k}\|^{p}_{1,q}
, and so also
\|w_{k}\|_{1,p}\to 0
. This completes the proof.
∎

6.
Multiplicity results

In this section we prove a multiplicity result: we show that for fixed
\lambda\in(\lambda_{k}^{D}(q),\lambda_{k+1}^{D}(q))
there exist at least
k
pairs of eigenfunctions
\pm u_{i}^{\lambda}(p,q),i=1,\dots,k,
such that
(\lambda,\pm u_{i}^{\lambda}(p,q))
solve equation (
2.1
), i.e.

\lambda=\lambda^{D}_{1}(p,q,\rho_{1})=\dots=\lambda^{D}_{k}(p,q;\rho_{k})\ ,\ \hbox{ with }\ \rho_{i}=\int_{\Omega}|u_{i}^{\lambda}(p,q)|^{q}dx.

We distinguish again the two cases
p<q
and
p>q
. The proofs rely on variational methods.

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
1<q<p<\infty
or
1<p<q<\infty
, and suppose that
\lambda\in(\lambda^{D}_{k}(q),\lambda^{D}_{k+1}(q))
. Then equation (
1.2
) has at least
k
pairs of nontrivial solutions.

Proof.

We split the proof into two parts.

Part 1:

p<q.

In this case we will make use of
[
1
, Proposition 10.8]
. We consider the functional
J_{\lambda}:W^{1,q}_{0}(\Omega)\backslash\{0\}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
associated to the problem (
1.2
) defined by

J_{\lambda}(u)=\frac{q}{p}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{p}\penalty\ dx+\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{q}\penalty\ dx-\lambda\int_{\Omega}|u|^{q}\penalty\ dx.

The functional
J_{\lambda}
is not bounded from below on
W^{1,q}_{0}(\Omega)
, so we consider again the constraint set
\mathcal{N}_{\lambda}
, on which we minimize the functional
J_{\lambda}.
We recall that the constraint set is given by

\mathcal{N}_{\lambda}:=\{u\in W^{1,q}_{0}(\Omega)\backslash\{0\}:\penalty\ \langle J^{\prime}_{\lambda}(u),u\rangle=0\}.

On
\mathcal{N}_{\lambda},
we have
J_{\lambda}(u)=(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q})\displaystyle{\int_{\Omega}}|\nabla u|^{p}\penalty\ dx>0.
We clearly have that

J_{\lambda}
is even and bounded from below on
\mathcal{N}_{\lambda}.

Next we show that every Palais-Smale (PS) sequence for
J_{\lambda}
has a converging subsequence on
\mathcal{N}_{\lambda}.
Let
(u_{n})_{n\geq 0}
be a (PS) sequence, i.e,
|J_{\lambda}(u_{n})|\leq C
, for all
n
, for some
C>0
and
J^{\prime}_{\lambda}(u_{n})\rightarrow 0
in
W^{-1,q^{\prime}}(\Omega)
as
n\rightarrow+\infty,
with
\frac{1}{q}+\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}=1.

We first show that the sequence
(u_{n})_{n\geq 0}
is bounded on
\mathcal{N}_{\lambda}
.
Suppose that
(u_{n})_{n\geq 0}
is not bounded, so
\displaystyle{\int_{\Omega}}|\nabla u_{n}|^{q}\penalty\ dx\rightarrow+\infty
as
n\rightarrow+\infty.
Since
J_{\lambda}(u_{n})=(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q})\displaystyle{\int}_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{n}|^{p}\penalty\ dx,
we have
\displaystyle{\int}_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{n}|^{p}\penalty\ dx\leq c.
On
\mathcal{N}_{\lambda}
, we have

(6.1)

0<\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{n}|^{p}\penalty\ dx=\lambda\int_{\Omega}|u_{n}|^{q}\penalty\ dx-\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{n}|^{q}\penalty\ dx,

and hence
\displaystyle{\int_{\Omega}}|u_{n}|^{q}\penalty\ dx\rightarrow+\infty.
Let
v_{n}=\frac{u_{n}}{\|u_{n}\|_{q}}
then
\displaystyle{\int_{\Omega}}|\nabla v_{n}|^{q}\penalty\ dx<\lambda
(using (
6.1
)) and hence
v_{n}
is bounded in
W^{1,q}_{0}(\Omega).
Therefore there exists
v_{0}\in W^{1,q}_{0}(\Omega)
such that
v_{n}\rightharpoonup v_{0}
in
W^{1,q}_{0}(\Omega)
and
v_{n}\rightarrow v_{0}
in
L^{q}(\Omega).
Dividing (
6.1
) by
\|u_{n}\|^{p}_{q},
we have

\frac{\lambda\displaystyle{\int_{\Omega}}|u_{n}|^{q}\penalty\ dx-\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{n}|^{q}\penalty\ dx}{\|u_{n}\|^{p}_{q}}=\int_{\Omega}|\nabla v_{n}|^{p}\penalty\ dx\rightarrow 0,

since
\lambda\displaystyle{\int_{\Omega}}|u_{n}|^{q}\penalty\ dx-\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{n}|^{q}\penalty\ dx=(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q})^{-1}J_{\lambda}(u_{n})
,
|J_{\lambda}(u_{n})|\leq C
and
\|u_{n}\|^{p}_{q}\rightarrow+\infty.
Now, since
v_{n}\rightharpoonup v_{0}
in
W^{1,q}_{0}(\Omega)\subset W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega),
we infer that

\int_{\Omega}|\nabla v_{0}|^{p}\penalty\ dx\leq\liminf_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla v_{n}|^{p}\penalty\ dx=0,

and consequently
v_{0}=0.
So
v_{n}\rightarrow 0
in
L^{q}(\Omega)\subset L^{p}(\Omega)
and this is a contradiction since
\|v_{n}\|_{q}=1.
Thus
(u_{n})_{n\geq 0}
is bounded on
\mathcal{N}_{\lambda}.
Now, we show that
u_{n}
converges strongly to
u
in
W^{1,q}_{0}(\Omega).

We have
\displaystyle{\int_{\Omega}}|u_{n}|^{q-2}u_{n}\penalty\ dx\rightarrow\displaystyle{\int_{\Omega}}|u|^{q-2}u\penalty\ dx
as
n\rightarrow\infty
and since
J_{\lambda}^{\prime}(u_{n})\rightarrow 0
in
W^{-1,q^{\prime}}(\Omega),

u_{n}\rightharpoonup u
in
W^{1,q}_{0}(\Omega),
we also have
J_{\lambda}^{\prime}(u_{n})(u_{n}-u)\rightarrow 0
and
J_{\lambda}^{\prime}(u)(u_{n}-u)\rightarrow 0
as
n\rightarrow+\infty.
We recall that with the computations made in Remark
3.1
, we have for
1<p<\infty

\int_{\Omega}\left(|\nabla u_{n}|^{p-2}\nabla u_{n}-|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u\right)\cdot\nabla(u_{n}-u)\penalty\ dx\geq(\|u_{n}\|^{p-1}_{1,p}-\|u\|^{p-1}_{1,p})(\|u_{n}\|^{p}_{1,p}-\|u\|^{p}_{1,p})\geq 0

Then,

\displaystyle\langle J^{\prime}_{\lambda}(u_{n})-J^{\prime}_{\lambda}(u),u_{n}-u\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle q\left[\int_{\Omega}\left(|\nabla u_{n}|^{p-2}\nabla u_{n}-|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u\right)\cdot\nabla(u_{n}-u)\penalty\ dx\right]

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle q\left[\int_{\Omega}\left(|\nabla u_{n}|^{q-2}\nabla u_{n}-|\nabla u|^{q-2}\nabla u\right)\cdot\nabla(u_{n}-u)\penalty\ dx\right]

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\lambda q\left[\int_{\Omega}\left(|u_{n}|^{q-2}u_{n}-|u|^{q-2}u\right)\cdot(u_{n}-u)\penalty\ dx\right]

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle q\left[\int_{\Omega}\left(|\nabla u_{n}|^{q-2}\nabla u_{n}-|\nabla u|^{q-2}\nabla u\right)\cdot\nabla(u_{n}-u)\penalty\ dx\right]

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\lambda q\left[\int_{\Omega}\left(|u_{n}|^{q-2}u_{n}-|u|^{q-2}u\right)\cdot(u_{n}-u)\penalty\ dx\right].

Using Lemma
3.3
, it follows that

\begin{split}\langle J^{\prime}_{\lambda}(u_{n})-J^{\prime}_{\lambda}(u),u_{n}-u\rangle&\geq C\|u_{n}-u\|^{q}_{1,q}-\lambda q\left[\int_{\Omega}\left(|u_{n}|^{q-2}u_{n}-|u|^{q-2}u\right)\cdot(u_{n}-u)\penalty\ dx\right].\end{split}

Therefore
\|u_{n}-u\|_{1,q}\rightarrow 0
as
n\rightarrow+\infty
and
u_{n}
converges strongly to
u
in
W^{1,q}_{0}(\Omega).

Let
\Sigma=\{A\subset\mathcal{N}_{\lambda}:\penalty\ A\penalty\ \text{closed}\penalty\ \text{and}\penalty\ -A=A\}
and
\Gamma_{j}=\{A\in\Sigma:\penalty\ \gamma(A)\geq j\},
where
\gamma(A)
denotes the Krasnoselski’s genus. We show that
\Gamma_{j}\neq\emptyset,
for
j\in\{1,\dots,k\}
.

Let
\lambda\in(\lambda^{D}_{j}(q),\lambda^{D}_{j+1}(q))
and choose
S^{\varepsilon}_{j}\in\Sigma\cap\{\int_{\Omega}|u|^{q}\penalty\ dx=1\}
such that

\sup_{v\in S^{\varepsilon}_{j}}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla v|^{q}dx\leq\lambda^{D}_{j}(q)+\varepsilon,\penalty\ \penalty\ \varepsilon:=\frac{\lambda-\lambda^{D}_{j}(q)}{2}.

Then, for
v\in S^{\varepsilon}_{j}

we set

\rho(v)=\left[\frac{\int_{\Omega}|\nabla v|^{p}\penalty\ dx}{\lambda\int_{\Omega}|v|^{q}\penalty\ dx-\int_{\Omega}|\nabla v|^{q}\penalty\ dx}\right]^{\frac{1}{q-p}},

with

\displaystyle\lambda\int_{\Omega}|v|^{q}\penalty\ dx-\int_{\Omega}|\nabla v|^{q}\penalty\ dx

\displaystyle\geq\lambda\int_{\Omega}|v|^{q}\penalty\ dx-(\lambda^{D}_{j}(q)+\varepsilon)\int_{\Omega}|v|^{q}\penalty\ dx

\displaystyle=(\lambda-\lambda^{D}_{j}(q)-\varepsilon)\int_{\Omega}|v|^{q}\penalty\ dx

\displaystyle=[\lambda-\lambda^{D}_{j}(q)-(\frac{\lambda-\lambda^{D}_{j}(q)}{2})]\int_{\Omega}|v|^{q}\penalty\ dx

\displaystyle=\frac{\lambda-\lambda^{D}_{j}(q)}{2}\int_{\Omega}|v|^{q}\penalty\ dx>0,\penalty\ \penalty\ \text{for all}\penalty\ \penalty\ v\in S^{\varepsilon}_{j}.

Hence,
\rho(v)v\in\mathcal{N}_{\lambda},
and then
\rho(S^{\varepsilon}_{j})\in\Sigma,
and
\gamma(\rho(S^{\varepsilon}_{j}))=\gamma(S^{\varepsilon}_{j})=j
for
1\leq j\leq k.

It is then standard
[
1
, Proposition 10.8]
to conclude that

\sigma_{\lambda,j}=\inf_{A\in\Gamma_{j}}\sup_{u\in A}J_{\lambda}(u),\penalty\ \penalty\ 1\leq j\leq k,\penalty\ \penalty\ \text{for any }\penalty\ k\in\mathbb{N}^{*}

yields
k
pairs of nontrivial critical points for
J_{\lambda},
which gives rise to
k
nontrivial solutions of problem (
1.2
).

Part 2:

p>q.

In this case, we will rely on the following theorem.

Theorem
(Clark,
[
7
]
) .

Let
X
be a Banach space and
G\in C^{1}(X,\mathbb{R})
satisfying the Palais-Smale condition with
G(0)=0.
Let
\Gamma_{k}=\{\penalty\ A\in\Sigma\penalty\ :\penalty\ \gamma(A)\geq k\penalty\ \}
with
\Sigma=\{\penalty\ A\subset X\penalty\ ;\penalty\ A=-A\penalty\ \text{and}\penalty\ A\penalty\ \text{closed}\penalty\ \}.
If
c_{k}=\inf\limits_{A\in\Gamma_{k}}\sup\limits_{u\in A}G(u)\in(-\infty,0),
then
c_{k}
is a critical value.

We consider the
C^{1}
functional
J_{\lambda}:W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)\subset W^{1,q}_{0}(\Omega)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}

J_{\lambda}(u)=\frac{q}{p}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{p}\penalty\ dx+\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{q}\penalty\ dx-\lambda\int_{\Omega}|u|^{q}\penalty\ dx.

Let
\Gamma_{k}=\{A\subset W^{1,q}_{0}(\Omega)\backslash\{0\},\penalty\ \penalty\ A\penalty\ \penalty\ \text{compact},\penalty\ \penalty\ A=-A,\penalty\ \penalty\ \gamma(A)\geq k\},
and for
\varepsilon>0
small let
A_{\varepsilon}\in\Gamma_{k}
such that

\sup_{\{u\in A_{\varepsilon},\penalty\ \int_{\Omega}|u|^{q}dx=1\}}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{q}dx\leq\lambda^{D}_{k}(q)+\varepsilon.

We would like to show that

(6.2)

-\infty<\alpha_{\lambda,k}=\inf_{A\in\Gamma_{k}}\sup_{u\in A}J_{\lambda}(u)

are critical values for
J_{\lambda}.

We clearly have that
J_{\lambda}(u)
is an even functional for all
u\in W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
, and also
J_{\lambda}
is bounded from below on
W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
since
J_{\lambda}
is coercive on
W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
.

We show that
J_{\lambda}(u)
satisfies the (PS) condition. Let
\{u_{n}\}
be a Palais-Smale sequence, i.e.,
|J_{\lambda}(u_{n})|\leq M
for all
n,

M>0
and
J_{\lambda}^{\prime}(u_{n})\rightarrow 0
in
W^{-1,p^{\prime}}(\Omega)
as
n\rightarrow\infty.
We first show that
\{u_{n}\}
is bounded in
W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega).
We have

\displaystyle M

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle|C\|u_{n}\|_{1,p}^{p}-C^{\prime}\|u_{n}\|^{q}_{1,p}|\geq|C\|u_{n}\|_{1,p}^{p-q}-C^{\prime}|\|u_{n}\|_{1,p}^{q},

and so
\{u_{n}\}
is bounded in
W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega).

Therefore,
u\in W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
exists such that, up to subsequences that we will denote by
(u_{n})_{n}
we have
u_{n}\rightharpoonup u
in
W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
and
u_{n}\rightarrow u
in
L^{q}(\Omega).
Arguing as in Part 1, we obtain that
\|u_{n}-u\|_{1,p}\rightarrow 0
as
n\rightarrow+\infty
, and so
u_{n}
converges to
u
in
W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)\subset W^{1,q}_{0}(\Omega).

As in section
5
, we approximate
A_{\varepsilon}
by a finite-dimensional set.
Next, we show that there exists sets
D^{\varepsilon}
of genus greater of equal to
k
such that
\sup\limits_{u\in D^{\varepsilon}}J_{\lambda}(u)<0.

For any
s\in(0,1)
, we define the set
D^{\varepsilon}(s):=s\cdot(P_{n}A_{\varepsilon})
and so
\gamma(D^{\varepsilon}(s))=\gamma(P_{n}A_{\varepsilon})\geq k.
We have, for any
s\in(0,1)

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{u\in D^{\varepsilon}}J_{\lambda}(u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{u\in P_{n}A_{\varepsilon}}J_{\lambda}(su)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{u\in P_{n}A_{\varepsilon}}\left\{\frac{qs^{p}}{p}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{p}dx+s^{q}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{q}dx-\lambda s^{q}\int_{\Omega}|u|^{q}dx\right\}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{u\in P_{n}A_{\varepsilon}}\left\{\frac{qs^{p}}{p}c(n)^{p}\|u\|^{p}_{1,q}+s^{q}(\lambda^{D}_{k}(q)+\varepsilon-\lambda)\right\}<0

for
s>0
sufficiently small.

Finally, we conclude that
\alpha_{\lambda,k}
are critical values for
J_{\lambda}
thanks to Clark’s Theorem.
∎

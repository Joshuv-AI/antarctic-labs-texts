Kodaira-Saito vanishing for the irregular Hodge filtration
Claude Sabbah

Abstract
. After making correct, and then improving, our definition of the category of irregular mixed Hodge modules thanks to Mochizuki’s recent results, we show how these results allow us to obtain Kodaira-Saito-type vanishing theorems for the irregular Hodge filtration of irregular mixed Hodge modules.

In the memoir

[

1.c.
An irregular Kodaira-Saito vanishing theorem

Let
X
be a smooth projective variety of dimension
n
. Let
\mathscr{T}
be an object of
\IrrMHM(X)
(e.g. with respect to the improved definition of Section
3.d
) and let
\EuScript{M}
be the underlying holonomic (left)
\EuScript{D}_{X}
-module. Then
\EuScript{M}
can be equipped in a canonical way with a coherent filtration indexed by
A+\mathbb{Z}
for some finite set
A\subset[0,1)
, called the
irregular Hodge filtration
and denoted by
F^{\mathrm{irr}}_{\scriptscriptstyle\bullet}\EuScript{M}
. Given
\alpha\in A
, we denote by
F^{\mathrm{irr}}_{\alpha+\mathbb{Z}}\EuScript{M}
the corresponding
\mathbb{Z}
-indexed filtration. The shifted holomorphic de Rham complex
\pDR\EuScript{M}
is thus filtered by setting

F^{\mathrm{irr}}_{\alpha+p}\pDR\EuScript{M}=\{0\rightarrow F^{\mathrm{irr}}_{\alpha+p}\EuScript{M}\rightarrow\Omega^{1}_{X}\otimes F^{\mathrm{irr}}_{\alpha+p+1}\EuScript{M}\rightarrow\cdots\rightarrow\underset{{\raisebox{1.0pt}{${\scriptscriptstyle\bullet}$}}}{\Omega^{n}_{X}\otimes F^{\mathrm{irr}}_{\alpha+p+n}\EuScript{M}}\rightarrow 0\},

where
{\scriptscriptstyle\bullet}
indicates the term in degree zero. We will show how the results of T. Mochizuki
[
Moc21
]
, combined with the original proof of M. Saito
[
Sai90
]
,

lead to the following vanishing result and its corollaries:

Theorem 1.1

(Kodaira-Saito vanishing)
\pointrait

Let

be an ample line bundle on
X
and let
\mathscr{T}
,
\EuScript{M}
and
A
be as above. Then, for each
\alpha\in A
, we have

\displaystyle H^{i}(X,\gr^{F^{\mathrm{irr}}_{\alpha}}\pDR(\EuScript{M})\otimes L)

\displaystyle=0\quad\text{for }i>0,

\displaystyle H^{i}(X,\gr^{F^{\mathrm{irr}}_{\alpha}}\pDR(\EuScript{M})\otimes L^{-1})

\displaystyle=0\quad\text{for }i<0.

Corollary 1.2
\pointrait

For
\mathscr{T}
,
\EuScript{M}
and
A
as above, let
a_{o}\in A+\mathbb{Z}
be such that
F^{\mathrm{irr}}_{<a_{o}}\EuScript{M}=0
, and let us set
\omega_{X}=\Omega^{n}_{X}
. Then we have the vanishing

H^{k}(X,\omega_{X}\otimes F^{\mathrm{irr}}_{a}(\EuScript{M})\otimes L)=0\quad\forall\,k>0,\text{ and }\forall\,a\in[a_{o},a_{o}+1).

Note that we can replace
F^{\mathrm{irr}}_{a}(\EuScript{M})
by
\gr^{F^{\mathrm{irr}}}_{a}(\EuScript{M})
in this corollary. We also have the analogue of Kollár’s vanishing theorem:

Corollary 1.3

(Kollár vanishing for the irregular Hodge filtration)
\pointrait

Let
\mathscr{T}
,
\EuScript{M}
,
A
and
a_{o}
be as in Corollary
1.2
. Let
f:X\mathchoice{\longrightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}Y
be a projective morphism to a smooth projective variety
Y
and let
L
be an ample line bundle on

H^{k}(Y,R^{j}f_{*}(\omega_{X}\otimes F^{\mathrm{irr}}_{a}\EuScript{M})\otimes L)=0\quad\forall\,j,\;\forall\,k>0,\text{ and }\forall\,a\in[a_{o},a_{o}+1).

1.d.
Geometric consequences

Let us emphasize an example where Theorem
1.1
applies. Let
L
be an ample line bundle on
X
and let
D\subset X
be a divisor with normal crossings. Recall that the Kodaira-Norimatsu vanishing theorem (
[
Nor78
]
): for each integer
p\geqslant 0
,

\displaystyle H^{q}(X,\Omega_{X}^{p}(\log D)\otimes L)

\displaystyle=0\quad\text{for }p+q>n,

(1.5)

\displaystyle H^{q}(X,\Omega_{X}^{p}(\log D)\otimes L^{-1})

\displaystyle=0\quad\text{for }p+q<n.

Theorem
1.1
enables us to extend this vanishing result when
D
contains the support of the pole divisor
P
of a morphism
\varphi:X\mathchoice{\longrightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}\mathbb{P}^{1}
. The sheaf
\Omega^{p}(\log D,\varphi)
is defined as the subsheaf of
\Omega_{X}^{p}(\log D)
consisting of logarithmic forms
\omega
such that
{\mathrm{d}}\varphi\wedge\omega
remains logarithmic. More generally, for each
\alpha\in[0,1)\cap\mathbb{Q}
and each
p\geqslant 0
, we define
\Omega^{p}(\log D,\varphi,\alpha)
as the subsheaf of
\EuScript{O}_{X}({\lfloor\alpha P\rfloor})\otimes_{\EuScript{O}_{X}}\Omega_{X}^{p}(\log D)
consisting of germs of meromorphic
p
-forms
\omega
such that
{\mathrm{d}}\varphi\wedge\omega
is a local section of
\EuScript{O}_{X}({\lfloor\alpha P\rfloor})\otimes_{\EuScript{O}_{X}}\Omega_{X}^{p+1}(\log D)
.

Let us decompose the reduced divisor
P_{\mathrm{red}}
as
\mathop{\textstyle\bigcup}\displaylimits_{i\in I}P_{i}
and let
m_{i}
be the multiplicity of
P_{i}
in
P
. For each
\alpha\in[0,1)\cap\mathbb{Q}
, let us set
I_{\alpha}=\{i\in I\mid\alpha m_{i}\in\mathbb{N}\}
and
P_{\alpha}=\mathop{\textstyle\bigcup}\displaylimits_{i\in I_{\alpha}}P_{i}
. In what follows, it is enough to consider that
\alpha
belongs to the finite subset
A\subset[0,1)\cap\mathbb{Q}
consisting of those
a
such that
am_{i}\in\mathbb{N}
for some
i\in I
. If
\alpha>0
, we denote by
{<}\alpha
the predecessor of
\alpha
in
A
. For
\alpha\in A\cap(0,1)
and
p\geqslant 0
, the quotient sheaf
\Omega^{p}(\log D,\varphi,\alpha)/\Omega^{p}(\log D,\varphi,{<}\alpha)
is supported on the divisor
P_{\alpha}
.

Corollary 1.6

(of Theorem
1.1
)
\pointrait

With the above assumptions and notations, for each
p\geqslant 0
, the sheaves
\Omega^{p}(\log D,\varphi,\alpha)
(
\alpha\in A
) satisfy the Kodaira-Saito vanishing property analogous to (
1.5
).

In particular, we obtain the vanishing
H^{k}(X,\omega_{X}(D+\lfloor\alpha P\rfloor)\otimes L)
for
k>n
.

Remark 1.7
.

By using Remark
1.4
(
2
), one also obtains that the property of Corollary
1.6
holds for the sheaves
\Omega^{p}(\log D,\varphi,\alpha)/\Omega^{p}(\log D,\varphi,{<}\alpha)
(
\alpha\in A\cap(0,1)
).

Corollary 1.8

(of Corollary
1.3
)
\pointrait

Let
\varphi:X\mathchoice{\longrightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}\mathbb{P}^{1}
be a projective morphism and set
P=\varphi^{*}(\infty)
. Assume that the support of
P
is contained in a (reduced) divisor with normal crossings
D
in
X
. Let
f:X\mathchoice{\longrightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}Y
be a projective morphism to a smooth projective variety
Y
and let
L
be an ample line bundle on
Y
. Then for each
\alpha\in A
we have the vanishing property

H^{k}(Y,R^{j}f_{*}\omega_{X}(D+\lfloor\alpha P\rfloor)\otimes L)=0\quad\text{for all $k>0$ and all $j$}.

Appendix B

Non-integral gradings

In this section, we indicate the arguments for obtaining the assertion in Remark
1.4
(
2
).

We extend the Rees construction to filtrations indexed by
A+\mathbb{Z}
, where
A
is a finite subset of
[0,1)
with
\#A=r
. We assume that
A
contains
0
, so that we have a (unique) increasing bijection
A\simeq\{0,1/r,\dots,(r-1)/r\}
. In the following, we identify these two sets.

We consider the ring
\mathbb{C}[u]
with the subring
\mathbb{C}[z]\mathrel{\lhook\joinrel\mathchoice{\longrightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}}\mathbb{C}[u]
so that
z
is mapped to
u^{r}
. The variable
u
is given the degree
1/r
. Recall that
R_{X}
denotes the graded ring
R_{F}\EuScript{D}_{X}
. We set
R_{X}^{(r)}=\mathbb{C}[u]\otimes_{\mathbb{C}[z]}R_{X}
. This is a
\frac{1}{r}\mathbb{Z}
-graded ring containing
R_{X}
as a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded subring, with term of degree
p/r
given by

(R_{X}^{(r)})_{p/r}=(R_{X})_{\lfloor p/r\rfloor}.

In other words,
R_{X}^{(r)}
is the Rees ring of
\EuScript{D}_{X}
with respect to the filtration

F_{p}^{(r)}\EuScript{D}_{X}=F_{\lfloor p/r\rfloor}\EuScript{D}_{X}.

Proposition B.1
\pointrait

Giving a
\frac{1}{r}\mathbb{Z}
-graded
R_{X}^{(r)}
-module
M^{(r)}
is equivalent to giving a
finite
family
M_{i/r}
(
i=0,\dots,r-1
) in
\mathsf{Modgr}(R_{X})
together with morphisms
M_{(i-1)/r}\mathchoice{\longrightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}M_{i/r}
(
i=1,\dots,r-1
) and
M_{(r-1)/r}\mathchoice{\longrightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}M_{1}(1)
such that, for each
i=0,\dots,r
, their composition
M_{i/r}\mathchoice{\longrightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}M_{1+i/r}
is equal to the multiplication by
z
. As an
R_{X}
-module,
M^{(r)}
decomposes as
\mathop{\textstyle\bigoplus}\displaylimits_{i=0}^{r-1}M_{i/r}\otimes u^{i}
.

Furthermore,
M^{(r)}
is strict (i.e.,
\mathbb{C}[u]
-flat) if and only if each
M_{i/r}
is strict (i.e.,
\mathbb{C}[z]
-flat). Lastly,
M^{(r)}
is
R_{X}^{(r)}
-coherent if and only if each
M_{i/r}
is
R_{X}
-coherent.

For
i=0,\dots,r-1
, we consider the
\mathbb{Z}
-graded objects
M^{(r)}_{i+r\mathbb{Z}}
. These are
\mathbb{Z}
-graded
R_{X}
-modules, that we denote by
M_{i/r}
. The morphism
u:M^{(r)}_{j}\mathchoice{\longrightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}M^{(r)}_{j+1}
induces the desired family of morphisms.

Conversely, from the family
M_{i/r}
and the morphisms, we set, for
p=qr+i
with
i\in\{0,\dots,r-1\}
,
M^{(r)}_{p/r}:=(M_{i/r})_{q}
and the morphisms
M_{(i-1)/r}\mathchoice{\longrightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}M_{i/r}
(
i=1,\dots,r-1
) and
M_{(r-1)/r}\mathchoice{\longrightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}M_{1}(1)
are interpreted as the multiplication by
u
.

The flatness statement is then clear since the set of elements of
\mathbb{C}[u]
-torsion is equal to that of
\mathbb{C}[z]
-torsion in
M^{(r)}
, and the last statement follows e.g. from
[
Kas03
, Prop. A.10]
.

\MakeQed

If
M^{(r)}
is
\mathbb{C}[u]
-flat, it is equal to the Rees module of some
\EuScript{D}_{X}
-module
\EuScript{M}
with respect to a
\frac{1}{r}\mathbb{Z}
-indexed
F^{(r)}
-filtration
F^{(r)}_{\scriptscriptstyle\bullet}\EuScript{M}
, that we can consider as a family of nested
\mathbb{Z}
-indexed
F
-filtrations
F_{i/r+{\scriptscriptstyle\bullet}}\EuScript{M}
with Rees module
M_{i/r}
, i.e., satisfying

i/r+p\leqslant j/r+q\implies F_{i/r+p}\EuScript{M}\subset F_{j/r+q}\EuScript{M}\quad\forall\,p,q\in\mathbb{Z},\;\forall\,i,j\in\{0,\dots,r-1\}.

We then have

\gr^{F^{(r)}}\EuScript{M}=\mathop{\textstyle\bigoplus}\displaylimits_{a\in\frac{1}{r}\mathbb{Z}}F^{(r)}_{a}\EuScript{M}/F^{(r)}_{<a}\EuScript{M}=\mathop{\textstyle\bigoplus}\displaylimits_{a\in\frac{1}{r}\mathbb{Z}}\gr^{F^{(r)}}_{a}(\EuScript{M}).

We say that
F^{(r)}_{\scriptscriptstyle\bullet}(\EuScript{M})
is a coherent
F^{(r)}
-filtration if
M^{(r)}
is
R_{X}^{(r)}
-coherent, and this property is equivalent to each
F_{i/r+{\scriptscriptstyle\bullet}}(\EuScript{M})
being a coherent
F
-filtration of
\EuScript{M}
.

Conversely, given a family of nested
\mathbb{Z}
-indexed
F
-filtrations
F_{i/r+{\scriptscriptstyle\bullet}}(\EuScript{M})
, we claim that the Rees module
R_{F^{(r)}}(\EuScript{M})=\mathop{\textstyle\bigoplus}\displaylimits_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}F^{(r)}_{k/r}(\EuScript{M})\cdot u^{k}
is an
R_{X}^{(r)}
-module. Indeed, we need to prove that
F_{\lfloor k/r\rfloor}(\EuScript{D}_{X})\cdot F^{(r)}_{\ell/r}(\EuScript{M})\subset F^{(r)}_{(k+\ell)/r}(\EuScript{M})
: this follows from the inequality
k+\ell\geqslant r\lfloor k/r\rfloor+\ell
.

Lemma B.2
\pointrait

For any
(\EuScript{M},F^{\mathrm{irr}}_{\alpha+\mathbb{Z}}\EuScript{M})
as in Theorem
1.1
, there exists an isomorphism in
\mathsf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}_{\mathrm{coh}}(\EuScript{O}_{X})
:

\boldsymbol{D}(\gr^{F^{\mathrm{irr}}}\pDR(\EuScript{M}))\simeq\gr^{F^{\mathrm{irr}}}\pDR(\boldsymbol{D}\EuScript{M}).

We can adapt the proof for
R_{F}\EuScript{D}_{X}
-modules as given e.g. in
[
MHMP
, Prop. 8.8.31]
, by replacing the ring
R_{F}\EuScript{D}_{X}
(denoted there by
\widetilde{\EuScript}{D}_{X}
) with
R_{X}^{(r)}
. We only need to check that
M^{(r)}
is strictly holonomic as an
R_{X}^{(r)}
-module, knowing that each
M_{i/r}
is strictly holonomic as an
R_{X}
-module (because of Proposition
3.12
).

We can regard
M^{(r)}
as an
R_{X}
-module, and it decomposes as such as the direct sum of the
R_{X}
-modules
M_{i/r}
(
i=0,\dots,r-1
), and so
\mathop{\boldsymbol{R}\mathop{\EuScript{H}\mkern-4.0mu\mathit{om}}\nolimits}\nolimits_{R_{X}}(M^{(r)},R_{X})
has cohomology in degree
n
at most. The same property holds then for

\displaystyle\mathop{\boldsymbol{R}\mathop{\EuScript{H}\mkern-4.0mu\mathit{om}}\nolimits}\nolimits_{R_{X}}(M^{(r)},R_{X})\otimes^{\boldsymbol{L}}_{\mathbb{C}[z]}\mathbb{C}[u]

\displaystyle\simeq\mathop{\boldsymbol{R}\mathop{\EuScript{H}\mkern-4.0mu\mathit{om}}\nolimits}\nolimits_{R_{X}}(M^{(r)},R_{X}^{(r)})

\displaystyle\simeq\mathop{\boldsymbol{R}\mathop{\EuScript{H}\mkern-4.0mu\mathit{om}}\nolimits}\nolimits_{R_{X}^{(r)}}(\mathbb{C}[u]\otimes_{\mathbb{C}[z]}M^{(r)},R_{X}^{(r)}).

It is thus enough to check that
M^{(r)}
is a direct summand of
\mathbb{C}[u]\otimes_{\mathbb{C}[z]}M^{(r)}
, i.e., to find a section
M^{(r)}\mathchoice{\longrightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}\mathbb{C}[u]\otimes_{\mathbb{C}[z]}M^{(r)}
of the natural surjective morphism
\mathbb{C}[u]\otimes_{\mathbb{C}[z]}M^{(r)}\mathchoice{\longrightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}M^{(r)}
.

We write

\mathbb{C}[u]\otimes_{\mathbb{C}[z]}M^{(r)}=\mathop{\textstyle\bigoplus}\displaylimits_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\Bigl(\mathop{\textstyle\bigoplus}\displaylimits_{\alpha}F^{\mathrm{irr}}_{\alpha+\lfloor k/r-\alpha\rfloor}(\EuScript{M})\Bigr)\cdot u^{k},

where
\alpha
runs in
\{0,1/r,\dots,(r-1)/r\}
, and the surjective morphism to
M^{(r)}
is induced by

\mathop{\textstyle\bigoplus}\displaylimits_{\alpha}F^{\mathrm{irr}}_{\alpha+\lfloor k/r-\alpha\rfloor}(\EuScript{M})\mathchoice{\longrightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}\sum_{\alpha}F^{\mathrm{irr}}_{\alpha+\lfloor k/r-\alpha\rfloor}(\EuScript{M})\subset\EuScript{M}.

We have
\alpha+\lfloor k/r-\alpha\rfloor\leqslant k/r
with equality if and only if
\alpha=\{k/r\}
(fractional part), so that the right-hand side above is equal to
F^{\mathrm{irr}}_{k/r}(\EuScript{M})
. Since
F^{\mathrm{irr}}_{k/r}(\EuScript{M})
is a summand in the left-hand side, we obtain the desired section.

\MakeQed

That the statement of Theorem
4.1
holds when we replace coherent
F_{\scriptscriptstyle\bullet}\EuScript{D}_{X}
-filtrations with coherent
F^{(r)}_{\scriptscriptstyle\bullet}\EuScript{D}_{X}
-filtrations is obtained by applying the same method as in
[
MHMP
,
§
11.9.d]
, since Lemma
B.2
reduces the proof to the vanishing of
H^{i}(X,\gr^{F^{(r)}}\pDR(\EuScript{N})\otimes L)
for
i>0
and
\EuScript{N}=\EuScript{M}
or
\boldsymbol{D}\EuScript{M}
.∎

We first note that Assumption (
2
) of Lemma
4.2
also implies that (
4.3
) also holds with
\gr^{F}
instead of
F
. The irregular Hodge filtration indexed by
A+\mathbb{Z}
satisfies Assumption (
2
) of Lemma
4.2
when the latter is extended to such kinds of filtrations by means of the equivalence of Proposition
B.1
. The previous remark applies then to the graded objects
\gr^{F^{\mathrm{irr}}}_{a}
for
a\in A+\mathbb{Z}
.

Then for
a\in[a_{o},a_{o}+1)
, we find

\omega_{Y}\otimes\gr^{F^{\mathrm{irr}}}_{a+m-n}({}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{D}}f^{(j)}_{*}\EuScript{M})\simeq R^{j}f_{*}(\omega_{X}\otimes\gr^{F^{\mathrm{irr}}}_{a}\EuScript{M}).

We conclude by applying Corollary
1.2
, as we already noticed that one can replace
F^{\mathrm{irr}}_{a}
with
\gr^{F^{\mathrm{irr}}}_{a}
in this corollary.

\MakeQed

Weights for π \pi -partial characters of π \pi -separable groups
Xuewu Chang and Ping Jin

Abstract
. The aim of this paper is to confirm an inequality predicted by Isaacs and Navarro in 1995, which asserts that for any π ′ \pi^{\prime} -subgroup Q Q of a π \pi -separable group G G , the number of π ′ \pi^{\prime} -weights of G G with Q Q as the first component always exceeds that of irreducible π \pi -partial characters of G G with Q Q as their vertex. We also give some sufficient condition to guarantee that these two numbers are equal, and thereby strengthen their main theorem on the π \pi -version of the Alperin weight conjecture.



1.
Introduction

Let
p
be a prime, and let
G
be a finite group.
Recall that a
p
-weight
of
G
is a pair
(Q,\tau)
, where
Q
is a
p
-subgroup of
G
and

\tau\in\Irr(\NN_{G}(Q)/Q)
has
p
-defect zero, i.e., whose degree has the same
p
-part as
|\NN_{G}(Q):Q|
.
The Alperin weight conjecture (often abbreviated AWC) asserts that
the number of
G
-conjugacy classes of
p
-weights coincides with the number of conjugacy classes of
p^{\prime}
-elements of
G
.

The first complete published proof of the AWC for
p
-solvable groups was given by
Isaacs and Navarro
[
4
]
in 1995 by introducing many new techniques to the
\pi
-theory of characters of
\pi
-separable groups for a set
\pi
of primes, and in fact, they established a
\pi
-version of this conjecture.
Specifically, they defined a
\pi^{\prime}
-weight
of a
\pi
-separable group
G
to be an ordered pair
(Q,\tau)
,
where
Q
is a
\pi^{\prime}
-subgroup of
G
and
\tau\in\Irr(\NN_{G}(Q)/Q)
has
p
-defect zero for every prime
p\in\pi^{\prime}
,
and then proved the following result, which appears in
[
4
]
as Theorem A
and reduces to the
p
-solvable case of AWC by taking
\pi=p^{\prime}
, the complement of the prime
p
.

Theorem 1.1

(Isaacs-Navarro)
.

Let
G
be a
\pi
-separable group, and assume that the Hall
\pi
-complements of
G
are nilpotent.
Then the number of
G
-conjugacy classes of
\pi^{\prime}
-weights is equal to the number of conjugacy classes of
\pi
-elements of
G
.

Furthermore, they defined the
vertices
for every irreducible
\pi
-partial character
of a
\pi
-separable group
G
,
and showed that the set of vertices for an irreducible
\pi
-partial character of
G
forms a single conjugacy class of
\pi^{\prime}
-subgroups of
G
. We will review the relevant definitions and properties in the next section.
Now let
Q
be a
\pi^{\prime}
-subgroup of
G
, and write
\I(G|Q)
for the set of irreducible
\pi
-partial characters of
G

with vertex
Q
. It is easy to show that
|\I(\NN_{G}(Q)|Q)|
is exactly the number of
\pi^{\prime}
-weights of
G
with first component
Q
; see Lemma 6.28 of
[
3
]
or Lemma
2.4
below.
Using the notion of vertices, Isaacs and Navarro obtained a strengthened version of Theorem
1.1
, and they also weakened the nilpotency condition on
Q
; see Theorem C and Theorem 6.3 of
[
4
]
.

Theorem 1.2

(Isaacs-Navarro)
.

Let
G
be
\pi
-separable, and let
Q\leq G
be a solvable
\pi^{\prime}
-subgroup with the property that
whenever
Q\leq X<Y\leq G
, where
Y
is a
\pi^{\prime}
-subgroup, we have
X<\NN_{Y}(X)
.
Then
|\I(G|Q)|=|\I(\NN_{G}(Q)|Q)|
.

The main purpose of this paper is to confirm an inequality predicted by Isaacs and Navarro
at the end of
[
4
]
, which asserts that for any
\pi^{\prime}
-subgroup
Q
of a
\pi
-separable group
G
,
the number of
\pi^{\prime}
-weights of
G
with
Q
as the first component always exceeds that of irreducible
\pi
-partial characters of
G
with
Q
as their vertex or, equivalently, that
|\I(\NN_{G}(Q)|Q)|\geq|\I(G|Q)|
.

Theorem A
.

Let
G
be a
\pi
-separable group, and let
Q
be a
\pi^{\prime}
-subgroup of
G
. Then

|\I(G|Q)|\leq|\I(\NN_{G}(Q)|Q)|.

Our proof of Theorem
A
was inspired by the techniques developed by Navarro and Sambale in
[
7
]
.
Actually, they explored nilpotent
\pi^{\prime}
-weights in
\pi
-separable groups
and gave a generalization of Theorem
1.1
.

Using the same argument as in the proof of Theorem
A
,
we obtain a strong form of Theorem
1.2
.

Theorem B
.

Let
G
be a
\pi
-separable group, and suppose that
Q\leq G
is a
\pi^{\prime}
-subgroup
with the property that
Q<\NN_{X}(Q)
for every
\pi^{\prime}
-subgroup
X
of
G
that properly contains
Q
.
Then

|\I(G|Q)|=|\I(\NN_{G}(Q)|Q)|.

The following is an immediate consequence of Theorem
B

in which
\XX_{\pi}(G)
denotes the set of
\pi
-special
characters of
G

(for the definition, see Section 2 below).

Corollary C
.

Let
G
be a
\pi
-separable group and let
Q
be a Hall
\pi
-complement of
G
. Then

(a)

|\I(G|Q)|=|\I(\NN_{G}(Q)|Q)|
, and

(b)

|\XX_{\pi}(G)|=|\Irr(\NN_{G}(Q)/Q)|
.

We mention that part (b) in the above corollary is a deep result due to Wolf,
which appears in
[
9
]
as Corollary 1.16.

All groups considered in this paper are finite, and the notation and terminology are mostly taken from Isaacs’ books

[
1
]
and
[

]
.

2.1.
\pi
-Partial characters and vertices

Let
G
be a
\pi
-separable group for a set
\pi
of primes, and let
G^{0}
be the set of
\pi
-elements of
G
.
For any complex character
\chi
of
G
, we say that the restriction
\chi^{0}
of
\chi
on
G^{0}
is a
\pi
-partial character
of
G
. If a
\pi
-partial character
\varphi
of
G
cannot be written as a sum of two
\pi
-partial characters of
G
, then we say that
\varphi
is an
irreducible
\pi
-partial character
of
G
.
We use
\I(G)
to denote the set of irreducible
\pi
-partial characters of
G
.
Note that if
\pi=p^{\prime}
, the complement of a single prime
p
, then
\I(G)=\IBr_{p}(G)
by the Fong-Swan theorem for
p
-solvable groups, and if
\pi
is the set of all primes, then
\I(G)=\Irr(G)
.

Recall that an irreducible complex character
\chi\in\Irr(G)
is
\pi
-special
if
\chi(1)
is a
\pi
-number and the determinantal order
o(\theta)
is a
\pi
-number, where
\theta
is any irreducible constituent of
\chi_{S}
for every subnormal subgroup
S
of
G
. Denote by
\XX_{\pi}(G)
for the set of all
\pi
-special characters of
G
.

The following is essential in the study of
\pi
-partial characters.

Lemma 2.1

(
[
3
, Theorem 3.14]
)
.

Let
G
be
\pi
-separable. Then the map
\chi\mapsto\chi^{0}

defines a bijection from
\XX_{\pi}(G)
onto the set of those irreducible
\pi
-partial characters of
G
whose degree is a
\pi
-number.

Now let
Q
be a
\pi^{\prime}
-subgroup of
G
.
We say that
Q
is a
vertex
for an irreducible
\pi
-partial character
\varphi\in\I(G)
if there exists a subgroup
U
of
G
and
\theta\in\I(U)
, such that
\varphi=\theta^{G}
,
\theta(1)
is a
\pi
-number and
Q
is a Hall
\pi^{\prime}
-subgroup of
U
. As mentioned in the introduction, we write
\I(G|Q)
for the set of irreducible
\pi
-partial characters of
G
with vertex
Q
.

Lemma 2.2

(
[
3
, Corollary 5.18]
)
.

Let
G
be
\pi
-separable and let
\varphi\in\I(G)
.
If
\varphi\in\I(G|Q)
, then
\varphi(1)_{\pi^{\prime}}|Q|=|G|_{\pi^{\prime}}
.
In particular, if
Q
is a Hall
\pi^{\prime}
-subgroup of
G
, then

\varphi\in\I(G|Q)
if and only if
\varphi(1)
is a
\pi
-number.

Also, we say that
\chi\in\Irr(G)
has
\pi^{\prime}
-defect zero
if
\chi(1)_{\pi^{\prime}}=|G|_{\pi^{\prime}}

or equivalently, if
\chi
has
p
-defect zero for every
p\in\pi^{\prime}
.
Write
\dz(G)
for the set of irreducible complex characters of
G
having
\pi^{\prime}
-defect zero,
so a pair
(Q,\tau)
is a
\pi^{\prime}
-weight of
G
if
Q
is a
\pi^{\prime}
-subgroup of
G
and
\tau\in\dz(\NN_{G}(Q)/Q)
.

We need two basic facts on vertices and weights.

Lemma 2.3

(
[
3
, Theorem 5.17]
)
.

Let
G
be a
\pi
-separable group and let
\varphi\in\I(G)
. Then all vertices for
\varphi
is a single conjugacy class of
\pi^{\prime}
-subgroups of
G
.

Lemma 2.4

(
[
3
, Lemma 6.28]
)
.

Let
G
be a
\pi
-separable group and let
Q
be a
\pi^{\prime}
-subgroup of
G
. Then the number of
\pi^{\prime}
-weights of
G
with first component
Q
is equal to
|\I(\NN_{G}(Q)|Q)|
.
Actually,
\tau\mapsto\tau^{0}
defines a canonical bijection

\dz(\NN_{G}(Q)/Q)\to\I(\NN_{G}(Q)|Q)
.

2.2.
The Clifford correspondence for
\pi
-partial characters

Let
K\leq G
and
\theta\in\I(K)
. We say that
\varphi\in\I(G)

lies over

\theta

or equivalently, that
\theta

lies under

\varphi
if
\theta
is an irreducible constituent of
\varphi_{K}
.
We use
\I(G|\theta)
to denote the set of all irreducible
\pi
-partial characters of
G
lying over
\theta
.
It is natural to define
\theta^{G}
by the usual formula for induced characters but applied only to
\pi
-elements of
G
,
so that
\theta^{G}
is also a
\pi
-partial character of
G
.

Furthermore, if
K\vartriangleleft G
, then
\theta^{g}\in\I(K)
for all
g\in G
,
where
\theta^{g}
is defined by
\theta^{g}(x^{g})=\theta(x)
for
x\in K^{0}
.
Then
G
acts on the set
\I(K)
via conjugation, and we write
G_{\theta}
for the stabilizer of
\theta
in
G
.

Now we can state the Clifford correspondence for
\pi
-partial characters.

Lemma 2.5

(
[
3
, Theorem 5.11]
)
.

Suppose that
\theta\in\I(N)
, where
N\vartriangleleft G
and
G
is
\pi
-separable.
Then induction
\alpha\mapsto\alpha^{G}
defines a bijection
\I(G_{\theta}|\theta)\to\I(G|\theta)
.
Also, if
\varphi=\alpha^{G}
, then
\alpha
is the unique irreducible
\pi
-partial character of
G_{\theta}

that lies under
\varphi
and over
\theta
, and in this case, we often write
\alpha=\varphi_{\theta}

and call it the Clifford correspondent of
\varphi
over
\theta
.

Following Navarro-Sambale
[
7
]
, we write
\I(G|Q,\tau)=\I(G|Q)\cap\I(G|\tau)
,
where
Q
is a
\pi^{\prime}
-subgroup of a
\pi
-separable group
G
and
\tau\in\I(N)
for some
N\vartriangleleft G
.

Lemma 2.6

(
[
3
, Lemma 6.33]
)
.

Let
G
be a
\pi
-separable group and let
\varphi\in\I(G|Q)
, where
Q
is a
\pi^{\prime}
-subgroup of
G
,
and let
K\vartriangleleft G
.

(a)

There exists a unique
\mathbf{N}_{G}(Q)
-orbit of some
\theta\in\I(K)
such that
\varphi_{\theta}\in\I(G_{\theta}|Q)
.

(b)

If
\tau\in\I(K)
is
Q
-invariant and
G=G_{\tau}\NN_{G}(Q)
,
then induction of
\pi
-partial characters defines a bijection

\I(G_{\tau}|Q,\tau)\to\I(G|Q,\tau)
.

The following corollary tells us how to count the quantity
|\I(G|Q)|
“over a normal subgroup”.

Corollary 2.7
.

Let
G
be a
\pi
-separable group with
K\vartriangleleft G
, and suppose that
Q
is a
\pi^{\prime}
-subgroup of
G
.
Let
\mathscr{A}
be a set of representatives for the
\NN_{G}(Q)
-orbits
of
Q
-invariant members of
\I(K)
. Then

|\I(G|Q)|=\sum_{\tau\in\mathscr{A}}|\I(G_{\tau}|Q,\tau)|.

Sometimes we need to work in quotient groups modulo normal
\pi^{\prime}
-subgroups when studying
\pi
-partial characters and vertices.

Lemma 2.8

(
[
3
, Theorem 3.10 and Lemma 5.31]
)
.

Let
G
be a
\pi
-separable group and let
N\vartriangleleft G
be a
\pi^{\prime}
-subgroup.
Write
\bar{G}=G/N
. Then

(a)

There is a bijection
\varphi\mapsto\bar{\varphi}
from
\I(G)
onto
\I(\bar{G})
, where
\bar{\varphi}(\bar{x})=\varphi(x)
for all
x\in G^{0}
.

(b)

If
\varphi\in\I(G|Q)
, where
Q
is a
\pi^{\prime}
-subgroup of
G
, then
N\leq Q
and
\bar{\varphi}\in\I(\bar{G}|\bar{Q})
.
In particular, we have
{\bf O}_{\pi^{\prime}}(G)\leq Q
.

Lemma 2.9

(
[
3
, Theorem 6.31]
)
.

Let
G
be a
\pi
-separable group and let
K\vartriangleleft G
be a
\pi
-subgroup.
Also, let
Q\leq G
be a
\pi^{\prime}
-subgroup, and let
L\vartriangleleft G
, where
L\leq Q
.
Let
\theta\in\Irr(K)
be
G
-invariant, and write
\bar{G}=G/L
.
Then
|\I(G|Q,\theta)|=|\I(\bar{G}|\bar{Q},\bar{\theta})|
,
where
\bar{\theta}\in\Irr(\bar{K})
corresponds to
\theta
via the natural isomorphism
K\to KL/L
.

2.3.
Vertices and character-triple isomorphisms

By a
character triple

(G,N,\theta)
, we mean that
G
is a group,
N\vartriangleleft G
and
\theta\in\Irr(N)
is
G
-invariant. For the definition and properties of character-triple isomorphisms, see Chapter 1 of
[
3
]
or Chapter 11 of
[
1
]
.

Lemma 2.10

(
[
3
, Lemma 3.11]
)
.

Let
(G,N,\theta)
be a character triple, where
G
is a group and
N
is a
\pi
-subgroup.
Then there exists a character triple
(G^{*},N^{*},\theta^{*})
such that
N^{*}\leq\mathbf{Z}(G^{*})
is also a
\pi
-subgroup and
(G^{*},N^{*},\theta^{*})
is isomorphic to
(G,N,\theta)
.

The irreducible
\pi
-partial characters behave very well under the isomorphism of character triples.

Lemma 2.11

(
[
3
, Lemmas 6.21 and 6.32]
)
.

Let
(G,N,\theta)
and
(G^{*},N^{*},\theta^{*})
be isomorphic character triples, where
G
and
G^{*}
are
\pi
-separable and
N
and
N^{*}
are
\pi
-groups. Then

(a)

There exists a unique bijection
\varphi\mapsto\varphi^{*}
from
\I(G|\theta)
onto
\I(G^{*}|\theta^{*})
such that if
\chi
is a lift of
\varphi
in
\Irr(G|\theta)
and
\chi^{*}\in\Irr(G^{*}|\theta^{*})
corresponds to
\chi
under the character-triple isomorphism, then
\chi^{*}
is a lift of
\varphi^{*}
. In particular, we have
\varphi(1)_{\pi^{\prime}}=\varphi^{*}(1)_{\pi^{\prime}}
.

(b)

Let
Q\leq G
and
Q^{*}\leq G^{*}
be
\pi^{\prime}
-groups, and assume that the intermediate subgroups
NQ
and
N^{*}Q^{*}
correspond via the character-triple isomorphism.
Then
\varphi\in\I(G|Q,\theta)
if and only if
\varphi^{*}\in\I(G^{*}|Q^{*},\theta^{*})
.
In particular, we have
|\I(G|Q,\theta)|=|\I(G^{*}|Q^{*},\theta^{*})|
.

2.4.
Above the Glauberman-Isaacs correspondence

The following deep theorem was proved by Dade and Puig in the case where
Q
is solvable,
which is thoroughly explained in
[
8
]
as mentioned at the end of page 2532 of
[
7
]
.
If
Q
is not solvable, then it must have even order by the Feit-Thompson theorem,
and thus
K
has odd order. In this case, Lewis
[
5
]
showed that the result also holds.

Lemma 2.12

(Dade-Puig-Lewis)
.

Let
G
be an arbitrary group with
K\vartriangleleft G
and
Q\leq G
, and assume that
KQ\vartriangleleft G
and that
(|K|,|Q|)=1
.
Let
\theta\in\Irr(K)
be
Q
-invariant, and write
\theta^{*}\in\Irr(\CC_{K}(Q))

for its Glauberman-Isaacs correspondent.
Then
(G_{\theta},K,\theta)
and
(\NN_{G}(Q)_{\theta^{*}},\CC_{K}(Q),\theta^{*})
are isomorphic character triples.

2.5.
Weights and vertices

One of essential ingredients in our proofs of Theorems
A
and
B
is Theorem
2.14
below,
whose proof relies on the following lemma.

Lemma 2.13

(
[
3
, Lemma 6.30]
)
.

Let
K\vartriangleleft G
, where
G
is
\pi
-separable and
K
is a
\pi
-subgroup,
and suppose
Q\leq G
is a
\pi^{\prime}
-subgroup such that
KQ\vartriangleleft G
.
Let
\theta\in\Irr(K)
be
Q
-invariant and
\varphi\in\I(G|\theta)
.
Then the following hold.

(a)

There exists a vertex
P
of
\varphi
such that
Q\leq P\leq\NN_{G}(Q)
.
In particular,
Q
is a vertex for
\varphi
if and only if
\varphi(1)_{\pi^{\prime}}=|G:Q|_{\pi^{\prime}}
.

(b)

Furthermore, any two of such vertices
P
for
\varphi
are
\NN_{G}(Q)
-conjugate.

Theorem 2.14
.

Let
K\vartriangleleft G
, where
G
is
\pi
-separable and
K
is a
\pi
-subgroup,
and suppose
Q\leq G
is a
\pi^{\prime}
-subgroup such that
KQ\vartriangleleft G
.
For any
Q
-invariant character
\tau\in\Irr(K)
, we have

|\I(G|Q,\tau)|=|\I({\bf N}_{G}(Q)|Q,\tau^{*})|

where
\tau^{*}
is the image of
\tau
under the Glauberman-Isaacs correspondence

\Irr_{Q}(K)\to\Irr({\bf C}_{K}(Q))
.

Proof..

By the Frattini argument, we have
G=K{\bf N}_{G}(Q)=G_{\tau}{\bf N}_{G}(Q)
,
and by Lemma
2.6
(b), we see that
|\I(G|Q,\tau)|=|\I(G_{\tau}|Q,\tau)|
.
Also, since
{\bf N}_{G}(Q)_{\tau^{*}}={\bf N}_{G}(Q)_{\tau}
, the same reason yields

|\I({\bf N}_{G}(Q)|Q,\tau^{*})|=|\I({\bf N}_{G}(Q)_{\tau}|Q,\tau^{*})|=|\I({\bf N}_{G_{\tau}}(Q)|Q,\tau^{*})|.

To complete the proof, therefore, we can assume without loss that
\tau
is
G
-invariant.
Then, by Lemma
2.12
, the character triples
(G,K,\tau)
and
({\bf N}_{G}(Q),{\bf C}_{K}(Q),\tau^{*})
are isomorphic,
and by Lemma
2.11
(a), the map
\varphi\mapsto\varphi^{*}
defines a bijection
\I(G|\tau)\to\I({\bf N}_{G}(Q)|\tau^{*})
such that
\varphi(1)_{\pi^{\prime}}=\varphi^{*}(1)_{\pi^{\prime}}
.
In this case, Lemma
2.13
(a) tells us that
Q
is a vertex for
\varphi
if and only if

\varphi(1)_{\pi^{\prime}}=|G:Q|_{\pi^{\prime}}=|{\bf N}_{G}(Q):Q|_{\pi^{\prime}},

and this is equivalent to saying that
\varphi^{*}(1)_{\pi^{\prime}}=|{\bf N}_{G}(Q):Q|_{\pi^{\prime}}
.
Thus
\varphi\in\I(G|Q,\tau)
if and only if
\varphi^{*}\in\I({\bf N}_{G}(Q)|Q,\tau^{*})
,
which implies that the above map
\varphi\mapsto\varphi^{*}
is also a bijection
from
\I(G|Q,\tau)
onto
\I({\bf N}_{G}(Q)|Q,\tau^{*})
, and the result follows.

3.
Proof of Theorems A and B

In this section, we will establish Theorems
A
and
B
simultaneously from the introduction.
As usual, we need to prove the following “relative version”,
which clearly includes Theorems
A
and
B
(by taking
Z=1
).

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
G
be a
\pi
-separable group, and let
Q
be a
\pi^{\prime}
-subgroup of
G
.
Suppose that
Z
is a normal
\pi
-subgroup of
G
and that
\lambda\in\Irr(Z)
is invariant in
G
.
Then the following hold.

(a)

|\I(G|Q,\lambda)|\leq|\I(\NN_{G}(QZ)|Q,\lambda)|
.

(b)

Equality holds in (a) if
Q
has the property that
whenever
Q<X
, where
X
is a
\pi^{\prime}
-subgroup of
G
,
then
Q<\NN_{X}(Q)
.

Proof..

We proceed by induction on
|G:Z|
.

Step 1. We may assume that
Z\leq{\bf Z}(G)
, so that
\NN_{G}(QZ)=\NN_{G}(Q)
.

By Lemma
2.10
, we can choose a character triple
(G^{*},Z^{*},\lambda^{*})

isomorphic to
(G,Z,\lambda)
, where
Z^{*}\leq{\bf Z}(G^{*})
is a
\pi
-subgroup. Since
G^{*}/Z^{*}\cong G/Z
and
G
is
\pi
-separable, we see that
G^{*}
is also
\pi
-separable.

For each
\pi^{\prime}
-subgroup
X
of
G
,
we know that the intermediate subgroup
XZ
corresponds to
(XZ)^{*}

via the isomorphism
XZ/Z\cong(XZ)^{*}/Z^{*}
,
and since
XZ/Z\cong X
is a
\pi^{\prime}
-group and
Z^{*}
is central in
(XZ)^{*}
,
there exists a unique
\pi^{\prime}
-subgroup
X^{*}
of
G^{*}
such that
(XZ)^{*}=X^{*}\times Z^{*}
.
It follows that
X\mapsto X^{*}
defines a surjective map from the set of
\pi^{\prime}
-subgroups of
G

onto that of
G^{*}
.

For (a), we conclude by Lemma
2.11
(b) that
|\I(G|Q,\lambda)|=|\I(G^{*}|Q^{*},\lambda^{*})|
and

|\I(\NN_{G}(QZ)|Q,\lambda)|=|\I(\NN_{G^{*}}(Q^{*}Z^{*})|Q^{*},\lambda^{*})|.

So, it suffices to prove (a) in
G^{*}
.

For (b), if
Q<X
, then
QZ<XZ
, which implies that
Q^{*}Z^{*}=(QZ)^{*}<(XZ)^{*}=X^{*}Z^{*}
and hence
Q^{*}<X^{*}
.
Using the natural isomorphism
X\cong XZ/Z
, it is easy to see that
Q<\NN_{X}(Q)
if and only if
QZ/Z<\NN_{XZ/Z}(QZ/Z)
,
and this is equivalent to saying that

(QZ)^{*}/Z^{*}<\NN_{(XZ)^{*}/Z^{*}}((QZ)^{*}/Z^{*})=\NN_{X^{*}Z^{*}/Z^{*}}(Q^{*}Z^{*}/Z^{*}),

which again happens if and only if
Q^{*}<\NN_{X^{*}}(Q^{*})
.
So, we may also work in
G^{*}
to establish (b).
To complete the proof, therefore, we may assume without loss that
Z\leq{\bf Z}(G)
.

Step 2. We may assume that
{\bf O}_{\pi^{\prime}}(G)=1
.

Let
L={\bf O}_{\pi^{\prime}}(G)
and write
\bar{G}=G/L
. Suppose first that
L\not\leq Q
.
Then Lemma
2.8
(b) tells us that
\I(G|Q,\lambda)
is empty, and (a) follows.
For (b), we need to prove that
\I(N|Q,\lambda)
is also empty.
To do this, write
N=\NN_{G}(Q)
, and since
L\cap N\leq{\bf O}_{\pi^{\prime}}(N)
, it suffices to show that
L\cap N\not\leq Q
.
Note that
Q<LQ
, and by the assumption on
Q
, we have

Q<\NN_{LQ}(Q)=\NN_{L}(Q)Q=(L\cap N)Q

and thus
L\cap N\not\leq Q
.
This proves the theorem in the case where
L\not\leq Q
.

Now suppose that
L\leq Q
. By Lemma
2.9
, we can deduce that

|\I(G|Q,\lambda)|=|\I(\bar{G}|\bar{Q},\bar{\lambda})|\quad\text{and}\quad|\I(\NN_{G}(Q)|Q,\lambda)|=|\I(\NN_{\bar{G}}(\bar{Q})|\bar{Q},\bar{\lambda})|.

So, it is no loss to work in
\bar{G}=G/L
, and thus we may assume that
L=1

(note that
\OO_{\pi^{\prime}}(G/L)=1
).

Step 3. We may assume that
|G:K|<|G:Z|
, where
K=\OO_{\pi}(G)
.

Note that
Z\leq K
, so we need to show that
Z<K
.
Suppose that
Z=K
, so that
K
is central in
G
by Step 1.
Since
\OO_{\pi^{\prime}}(G)=1
by Step 2, the Hall-Higman Lemma 1.2.3 (see Theorem 3.21 of
[
2
]
for example)
implies that
G=\CC_{G}(K)\leq K
. It follows that
G
is a
\pi
-group, and
in this case, the result is trivial. Thus we may assume that
Z<K
.

Step 4. Complete the proof.

Let
\mathscr{A}
be a set of representatives of
\NN_{G}(Q)
-orbits in the set of
Q
-invariant characters in
\Irr(K|\lambda)
.
Since
\lambda
is
G
-invariant, it follows by Corollary
2.7
that

|\I(G|Q,\lambda)|=\sum_{\tau\in\mathscr{A}}|\I(G_{\tau}|Q,\tau)|.

Furthermore, let
\mathscr{A}^{*}
be the image of
\mathscr{A}

under the Glauberman-Isaacs correspondence from
\Irr_{Q}(K)
to
\Irr({\bf C}_{K}(Q))
.
Then
\mathscr{A}^{*}
is clearly a set of representatives of
\NN_{G}(Q)
-orbits in

\Irr({\bf C}_{K}(Q)|\lambda)
.
As before, we have

|\I(\NN_{G}(Q)|Q,\lambda)|=\sum_{\tau\in\mathscr{A}}|\I(\NN_{G}(Q)_{\tau}|Q,\tau^{*})|.

Note that
K\NN_{G}(Q)_{\tau}=\NN_{G_{\tau}}(KQ)
,
and by Theorem
2.14
, we have

|\I(\NN_{G_{\tau}}(KQ)|Q,\tau)|=|\I(\NN_{G}(Q)_{\tau}|Q,\tau^{*})|,

for all
\tau\in\mathscr{A}
. To complete the proof, therefore, it suffices to show that

|\I(G_{\tau}|Q,\tau)|\leq|\I(\NN_{G_{\tau}}(KQ)|Q,\tau)|

with equality if
Q
has the property described in (b).
This follows by the inductive hypothesis applied in
G_{\tau}
since then
|G_{\tau}:K|<|G:Z|
by Step 4,
and the proof is complete.
∎

The following is Corollary
C
in the introduction, which we restate here for convenience.

Corollary 3.2
.

Let
G
be a
\pi
-separable group, and let
Q
be a Hall
\pi
-complement of
G
. Then

(a)

|\I(G|Q)|=|\I(\NN_{G}(Q)|Q)|
and

(b)

|\XX_{\pi}(G)|=|\Irr(\NN_{G}(Q)/Q)|
.

Proof..

(a) is clear by Theorem
B
.
To prove (b), we use Lemmas
2.1
and
2.2
to deduce that

|\XX_{\pi}(G)|=|\I(G|Q)|\;\text{and}\;|\XX_{\pi}(\NN_{G}(Q))|=|\I(\NN_{G}(Q)|Q)|,

and by (a), we obtain
|\XX_{\pi}(G)|=|\XX_{\pi}(\NN_{G}(Q))|
.
By definition, it is easy to see that all normal
\pi^{\prime}
-subgroups of a
\pi
-separable group are contained
in the kernel of each
\pi
-special character,
and thus we may identify
\XX_{\pi}(\NN_{G}(Q))
with
\XX_{\pi}(\NN_{G}(Q)/Q)
.
But
\NN_{G}(Q)/Q
is a
\pi
-group, so all irreducible characters of
\NN_{G}(Q)/Q
are
\pi
-special,
that is,
\Irr(\NN_{G}(Q)/Q)=\XX_{\pi}(\NN_{G}(Q)/Q)
.
It follows that
|\XX_{\pi}(G)|=|\Irr(\NN_{G}(Q)/Q)|
, as desired.
∎

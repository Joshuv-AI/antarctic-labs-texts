A new proof of the geometric Sobolev embedding for generalised Kolmogorov operators
Nicola Garofalo, Giulio Tralli

Abstract
. In this note we revisit a result in [ 9 ] , where we established nonlocal isoperimetric inequalities and the related embeddings for Besov spaces adapted to a class of Hörmander operators of Kolmogorov-type. We provide here a new proof which exploits a weak-type Sobolev embedding established in [ 11 ] .



1.
Introduction

Consider the Kolmogorov-Fokker-Planck operators in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
N\geq 2
, defined as follows

(1.1)

\mathscr{A}u=\operatorname{tr}(Q\nabla^{2}u)+\left\langle BX,\nabla u\right\rangle,

where the
N\times N
matrices
Q
and
B
have real, constant coefficients,
Q=Q^{\star}\geq 0
, and
X
stands for the generic point in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
. The operators
\mathscr{A}
in (
1.1
) were introduced in
[
13
]
, where Hörmander showed that they are hypoelliptic if and only if the covariance matrix

(1.2)

K(t)=\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}e^{sB}Qe^{sB^{\star}}ds\quad\mbox{ is positive definite for every $t>0$.}

This condition will be henceforth assumed throughout the whole paper. Equations such as (
1.1
) are of considerable interest in physics, probability and finance, and have been the subject of intense study during the past three decades (see the recent survey article
[
2
]
, and the references therein). Besides the classical Laplace equation (which corresponds to the non-degenerate model
Q=I_{N}
,
B=O_{N}
), they encompass the Ornstein-Uhlenbeck operator (which is obtained by taking
Q=I_{N}
and
B=-I_{N}
), as well as the degenerate operator of Kolmogorov in
\mathbb{R}^{2n}

(1.3)

\mathscr{A}_{0}u=\Delta_{v}u+<v,\nabla_{x}u>,

corresponding to the choice
N=2n
,
Q=\begin{pmatrix}I_{n}&0_{n}\\
0_{n}&0_{n}\end{pmatrix}
, and
B=\begin{pmatrix}0_{n}&0_{n}\\
I_{n}&0_{n}\end{pmatrix}
. Such operator arises in the kinetic theory of gases and was first introduced in the seminal note
[
14
]
on Brownian motion. One should note that
\mathscr{A}_{0}
fails to be elliptic since it is missing the diffusive term
\Delta_{x}u
. However, it does satisfy Hörmander’s hypoellipticity condition since one easily checks that
K(t)=\begin{pmatrix}I_{n}&t/2\ I_{n}\\
t/2\ I_{n}&t^{2}/3\ I_{n}\end{pmatrix}>0
for every
t>0
. In this respect, it should be noted that Kolmogorov himself had already shown the hypoellipticity of his operator since in
[
14
]
he constructed an explicit heat kernel for
\mathscr{A}_{0}
which is
C^{\infty}
outside the diagonal. Kolmogorov’s construction was generalised in
[
13
]
, where it was shown that the heat kernel for
\mathscr{A}
can be explicitly written as

(1.4)

p(X,Y,t)=\frac{\omega_{N}(4\pi)^{-\frac{N}{2}}}{V(t)}\exp\left(-\frac{m_{t}(X,Y)^{2}}{4t}\right).

In (
1.4
), for
X,Y\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
we have let, for
t>0
,

\displaystyle m_{t}(X,Y)

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\left\langle K(t)^{-1}(Y-e^{tB}X),Y-e^{tB}X\right\rangle},

\displaystyle B_{t}(X,r)

\displaystyle=\{Y\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\mid m_{t}(X,Y)<r\}

(1.5)

\displaystyle V(t)

\displaystyle=\left|B_{t}(X,\sqrt{t})\right|=\omega_{N}(\det(tK(t)))^{1/2},

where
\omega_{N}
indicates the Lebesgue measure
\left|\hphantom{l}\cdot\hphantom{l}\right|
of the unit ball in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
. If we indicate with

P_{t}f(X)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}p(X,Y,t)f(Y)dY

the Hörmander semigroup, then it is well-known that

P_{t}1\equiv 1\qquad\mbox{ and }\qquad P^{*}_{t}1\equiv e^{-t\operatorname{tr}B}.

As a consequence, under the assumption that the matrix
B
of the drift satisfies

(1.6)

\operatorname{tr}B\geq 0,

we obtain a non-symmetric semigroup which is contractive in
L^{p}:=L^{p}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N},dX\right)
,
1\leq p\leq\infty
. Under the condition (
1.6
), in a series of papers
[
10
,
11
,
8
,
9
,
3
]
we have developed some basic functional analytic aspects of the class (
1.1
). In particular, since the operators
\mathscr{A}
do not possess a variational structure, one focus of our investigation has been a notion of
gradient
. Guiding by the idea that the heat kernel (
1.4
) should encapsulate the geometry underlying
\mathscr{A}
, we introduced the following class of Besov spaces naturally associated with the semigroup
P_{t}
.

Definition 1.1
.

For any
0<s<1
and
1\leq p<\infty
, the Besov space
\mathfrak{B}^{\mathscr{A}}_{s,p}
is the collection of all functions
f\in L^{p}
such that

(1.7)

\mathscr{N}^{\mathscr{A}}_{s,p}(f)=\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{t^{\frac{sp}{2}+1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}P_{t}\left(|f-f(X)|^{p}\right)(X)dXdt\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}<\infty.

Under condition (
1.6
), we know that smooth functions with compact support are contained and dense in
\mathfrak{B}^{\mathscr{A}}_{s,p}
(see Proposition
2.5
below). Denoting by
\mathbf{1}_{E}
the indicator function of a set
E
, we have introduced the following.

Definition 1.2
.

Fix
0<s<\frac{1}{2}
. We say that a measurable set
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
has finite
s
-
perimeter
if
\mathbf{1}_{E}\in\mathfrak{B}^{\mathscr{A}}_{2s,1}
and we define the
s
-perimeter associated to
\mathscr{A}
as

\mathfrak{P}_{\mathscr{A},s}(E)=\mathscr{N}^{\mathscr{A}}_{2s,1}(\mathbf{1}_{E}).

The reader should keep in mind that

(1.8)

\mathscr{N}^{\mathscr{A}}_{2s,1}(\mathbf{1}_{E})=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{t^{1+s}}\|P_{t}\mathbf{1}_{E}-\mathbf{1}_{E}\|_{1}dt,

see in this respect
[
8
, Corollary 3.6]
and
[
9
, Section 3]
. Formula (
1.8
) underscores the role of the
1
-parameter family of pseudo-distances
m_{t}(\cdot,\cdot)
in (
1.4
) via the
s
-dependent average of
\|P_{t}\mathbf{1}_{E}-\mathbf{1}_{E}\|_{1}
.

When specialized to
\mathscr{A}=\Delta
, up to a renormalizing factor the seminorm
\mathscr{N}^{\mathscr{A}}_{s,p}(\cdot)
coincides with the classical Aronszajn-Gagliardo-Slobedetzky seminorm: in such framework it is nowadays a common practice to call
nonlocal perimeter
the Aronszajn-Gagliardo-Slobedetzky seminorm of the indicator function, and we refer the reader to the influential work
[
4
]
where the structure of the critical points of nonlocal perimeters was first analyzed. The notion of nonlocal perimeter in the Euclidean setting was implicitly present in
[
1
,
17
]
, and the nonlocal counterpart of the classical De Giorgi’s isoperimetric inequality
[
5
]
was established in
[
1
,
7
]
with respect to such notion.

Concerning the general class of operators
\mathscr{A}
in (
1.1
), we established in
[
9
, Theorem 1.1 and Theorem 1.3]
the following nonlocal isoperimetric inequalities and the related embeddings of the Besov spaces
\mathfrak{B}^{\mathscr{A}}_{2s,1}
in the relevant
L^{q}
-space of functions with higher integrability properties.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
0<s<\frac{1}{2}
, and assume (
1.6
). Suppose there exist
D,\gamma_{D}>0
such that

(1.9)

V(t)\geq\gamma_{D}\ t^{D/2}\quad\mbox{ for all }t>0.

Then

\mathfrak{B}^{\mathscr{A}}_{2s,1}\hookrightarrow L^{\frac{D}{D-2s}}.

More precisely, there exists a positive constant
c
(depending on
N,D,s,\gamma_{D}
) such that for every
f\in\mathfrak{B}^{\mathscr{A}}_{2s,1}
one has

(1.10)

||f||_{\frac{D}{D-2s}}\leq\frac{1}{c}\mathscr{N}^{\mathscr{A}}_{2s,1}(f).

In particular, for any measurable set
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
with
|E|<\infty
, one has

\mathfrak{P}_{\mathscr{A},s}(E)\geq\ c\ |E|^{(D-2s)/D}.

In Theorem
1.3
the assumption (
1.9
) on the growth of the volume function
V(t)
is crucial as it allows to detect the dimensional parameter
q=\frac{D}{D-2s}
, and it deserves a detailed explanation. If we first take a look at the Kolmogorov example
\mathscr{A}_{0}
in
\mathbb{R}^{2n}
recalled in (
1.3
), the constant
D=4n
is determined by the direct computation
V(t)=\omega_{2n}12^{-\frac{n}{2}}t^{2n}
. More generally, the fact that one can uniquely identify the constant
D
in (
1.9
) is shared by the subclass of (
1.1
) which possesses invariances with respect to a family of non-isotropic dilations since in this case
V(t)\equiv V(1)t^{\frac{D}{2}}
(to fix the ideas, for
\mathscr{A}_{0}
the dilations are defined by
\delta_{\lambda}(v,x)=(\lambda v,\lambda^{3}x)
): such homogeneous class was introduced in
[
15
]
where Lanconelli and Polidoro provided an explicit characterization in terms of the matrices
Q
and
B
. Besides the homogeneous case, for the general class (
1.1
) one can always say

(1.11)

\exists D_{0}\geq N\,\,\mbox{ such that }\,\,V(t)\ \cong\ t^{D_{0}/2}\,\,\mbox{ as }t\to 0^{+},

see
[
11
, Section 2.4]
. We refer to the number
D_{0}
as the intrinsic dimension of the semigroup
\{P_{t}\}_{t>0}
at zero. With this perspective in mind, the validity of the assumption (
1.9
) readily implies that
D\geq D_{0}
(which in particular ensures
D>2s
and
\frac{D}{D-2s}>1
) and it underscores the importance of the behaviour of
V(t)
for large
t
. For example, the operator

\Delta_{v}+\left\langle v,\nabla_{v}\right\rangle+\left\langle v,\nabla_{x}\right\rangle\qquad\mbox{ in }\mathbb{R}^{2n}

fits the assumptions of Theorem
1.3
for every
D\geq 4n
: as a matter of fact one can check that in this case
V(t)=\omega_{2n}\left(2e^{t}-\frac{t}{2}-1+\frac{t}{2}e^{2t}-e^{2t}\right)^{n}
which tells that
D_{0}=4n
and
V(t)
grows exponentially fast at
t\to\infty
. In
[
11
, Definition 3.4]
we introduced the notion of intrinsic dimension at infinity of the semigroup
\{P_{t}\}_{t>0}
in order to handle the behaviour for large
t
of the volume function. We are going to recall such notion in Definition
2.1
below, and we denote by
D_{\infty}
such a constant. It turns out that
D_{\infty}\in[2,\infty]
under assumption (
1.6
), and the validity of (
1.9
) forces
D_{\infty}\geq D_{0}
and
D\in[D_{0},D_{\infty}]
. This gives a way to understand the operators
\mathscr{A}
which do not satisfy the volume growth condition (
1.9
). In fact, we can consider the following operator

\partial^{2}_{v}+v\partial_{x}-x\partial_{v}\qquad\mbox{ in }\mathbb{R}^{2}

for which
V(t)=\pi\left(\frac{t^{2}}{4}+\frac{1}{8}\left(\cos(2t)-1\right)\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}
: in this situation we thus have
D_{0}=4>D_{\infty}=2
and therefore Theorem
1.3
cannot apply to such case. In
[
9
, Theorem 1.2 and Theorem 7.6]
we established the following substitute result to treat the operators (
1.1
) with
D_{0}>D_{\infty}
.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
0<s<\frac{1}{2}
. Assume (
1.6
), and
D_{0}>D_{\infty}
. Suppose there exists
\gamma>0
such that

(1.12)

V(t)\geq\gamma\min\{t^{D_{0}/2},t^{D_{\infty}/2}\}.\quad\mbox{ for all }t>0.

Then

\mathfrak{B}^{\mathscr{A}}_{2s,1}\hookrightarrow L^{\frac{D_{0}}{D_{0}-2s}}+L^{\frac{D_{\infty}}{D_{\infty}-2s}}.

More precisely, there exists a positive constant
c
(depending on
N,D_{0},D_{\infty},s,\gamma
) such that for every
f\in\mathfrak{B}^{\mathscr{A}}_{2s,1}
one has

(1.13)

||f||_{L^{\frac{D_{0}}{D_{0}-2s}}+L^{\frac{D_{\infty}}{D_{\infty}-2s}}}\leq\frac{1}{c}\mathscr{N}^{\mathscr{A}}_{2s,1}(f).

Moreover, there exists a positive constant
\tilde{c}
(depending on
N,D_{0},D_{\infty},s,\gamma
) such that for any measurable set
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
with
|E|<\infty
, one has

\mathfrak{P}_{\mathscr{A},s}(E)\geq\ \tilde{c}\min\left\{|E|^{\frac{D_{0}-2s}{D_{0}}},|E|^{\frac{D_{\infty}-2s}{D_{\infty}}}\right\}.

We notice that, under the assumptions of Theorem
1.4
, we have
D_{0}>D_{\infty}>2s
and then
\frac{D_{\infty}}{D_{\infty}-2s}>\frac{D_{0}}{D_{0}-2s}>1
. The proofs of Theorem
1.3
and Theorem
1.4
which can be found in
[
9
]
were inspired by the powerful and flexible semigroup approach to isoperimetric inequalities which is due to Ledoux
[
16
]
in the local case. We also refer the interested reader to
[
21
,
20
]
for more insights on such a heat-kernel approach to perimeters and isoperimetric properties. The purpose of the present note is to provide a different proof of Theorems
1.3
and
1.4
which instead relies on an embedding in a weak
L^{q}
-space
of a fractional
(p=1)
-Sobolev space: these Sobolev spaces are tailored on the fractional powers of
\mathscr{A}
and their relevant embeddings were established in
[
11
]
. For a proper historical perspective concerning the classical Sobolev spaces, we recall that from the representation formula
|f(X)|\leq C(N)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|\nabla f(Y)|}{|X-Y|^{N-1}}dY
, and the
L^{1}
-mapping properties of the Riesz potentials, one knows that
W^{1,1}\hookrightarrow L^{N/(N-1),\infty}
. A remarkable aspect of the end-point case
p=1
is that such weak Sobolev embedding in fact implies the classical isoperimetric inequality
P(E)\geq C_{N}|E|^{\frac{N-1}{N}}
. The latter, in turn, combined with the coarea formula, is equivalent to the strong embedding
W^{1,1}\hookrightarrow L^{N/(N-1)}
. This establishes the beautiful fact that, in the geometric case
p=1
, the weak Sobolev embedding is equivalent to the strong one, and they are both equivalent to the isoperimetric inequality, see
[
18
]
. The main focus of this paper is a semigroup generalisation of this circle of ideas to the nonlocal degenerate setting of the operators
\mathscr{A}
.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section we recall the main ingredients we shall need in the proofs of Theorems
1.3
and
1.4
that we present in Section
3
. The main character in our analysis is the kernel in (
1.4
). As we mentioned it is well-known that
p(X,Y,t)
is the heat kernel, i.e. that
p(\cdot,Y,\cdot)
is a solution of the heat equation
\mathscr{A}u=\partial_{t}u
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}\times(0,\infty)
for any
Y\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
and
p(X,\cdot,t)
tends to the Dirac delta
\delta_{X}
in the distributional sense as
t\to 0^{+}
for any
X\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
. In particular we are going to exploit the following property concerning such an approximation of the identity

(2.1)

P_{t}\varphi(X)\underset{t\to 0^{+}}{\longrightarrow}\varphi(X)\qquad\mbox{ for every }\varphi\in L^{\infty}\cap\,C\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)\mbox{ and }X\in\mathbb{R}^{N}.

The limit in (
2.1
) can be verified directly using the Markovian condition
P_{t}1\equiv 1
and the properties of the positive definite matrices
K(t)
in (
1.2
) (see, e.g.,
[
12
, Proposition 2.1]
; see also the analytic and probabilistic tools in
[
6
,
19
]
for a treatment of a more general class of operators with varying coefficients).

It is very convenient for us to exploit the notations we adopted with the explicit expression (
1.4
), as in this way we can put the
1
-parameter family of pseudoballs
B_{t}(X,\sqrt{t})
at the center stage together with their volume function
V(t)
in (
1
). We already stressed that, as a by product of the analysis in
[
15
]
, the small-time behaviour of
V(t)
is governed by a suitable infinitesimal homogeneous structure which we encode in the number
D_{0}
defined via (
1.11
) (i.e. the intrinsic dimension of the semigroup
\{P_{t}\}_{t>0}
at zero). On the other hand, the following definition allows us to handle the large-time behaviour of
V(t)
.

Definition 2.1
.

Consider the set

\Sigma_{\infty}=\left\{\alpha>0\,\big|\,\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{t^{\frac{\alpha}{2}-1}}{V(t)}dt<\infty\right\}.

We call the number

D_{\infty}=\sup\Sigma_{\infty}

the
intrinsic dimension at infinity
of the semigroup
\{P_{t}\}_{t>0}
.

Thanks to the study of the large-time behaviour of the eigenvalues of
tK(t)
performed in
[
11
, Section 3]
(see also
[
3
, Proposition 2.3]
), we know that
2\leq D_{\infty}\leq\infty
under the assumption (
1.6
).

2.1.
Fractional powers of
\mathscr{A}
, and their Sobolev embeddings

In
[
10
]
we developed a fractional calculus for
\mathscr{A}
. On functions belonging to the Schwartz class
\mathscr{S}
the nonlocal operator
(-\mathscr{A})^{s}
, for
0<s<1
, is defined through the following pointwise formula

(2.2)

(-\mathscr{A})^{s}f(X)=-\frac{s}{\Gamma(1-s)}\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{-(1+s)}\left[P_{t}f(X)-f(X)\right]dt,\qquad X\in\mathbb{R}^{N}.

If
\operatorname{tr}B\geq 0
, the formula in (
2.2
) defines in fact an
L^{p}
-function for any
1\leq p<\infty
. We can then extend the operator
(-\mathscr{A})^{s}
to a closed operator on its domain in
L^{p}
endowed with the graph norm: this is precisely what we are doing with the following definition.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
,
0<s<1
, and assume (
1.6
). We define the Sobolev space
\mathscr{L}^{2s,p}
as

\mathscr{L}^{2s,p}=\left\{f\in L^{p}\mid(-\mathscr{A})^{s}f\in L^{p}\right\}

with

\|f\|_{\mathscr{L}^{2s,p}}=\|f\|_{p}+\|(-\mathscr{A})^{s}f\|_{p}.

Thanks to a density result the Banach space
\mathscr{L}^{2s,p}
coincides with the completion of
\mathscr{S}
with respect to
\|\cdot\|_{\mathscr{L}^{2s,p}}
, see in this respect
[
11
, Definition 4.4 and Proposition 4.6]
as well as
[
9
, Proposition 2.13]
.

In
[
11
, Theorem 7.5 and Theorem 7.7]
we proved the following

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
0<s<1
, and assume (
1.6
).

(a)

If (
1.9
) hold, then we have
\mathscr{L}^{2s,1}\ \hookrightarrow\ L^{\frac{D}{D-2s},\infty}
. More precisely, there exists a constant
S_{1,s}>0
, depending on
N,D,s,\gamma_{D}
, such that for any
f\in\mathscr{L}^{2s,1}
one has

(2.3)

\underset{\lambda>0}{\sup}\ \lambda|\{X\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\mid|f(X)|>\lambda\}|^{\frac{D-2s}{D}}\leq S_{1,s}\|(-\mathscr{A})^{s}f\|_{1}.

(b)

If instead
D_{0}>D_{\infty}
and (
1.12
) hold, we have
\mathscr{L}^{2s,1}\ \hookrightarrow\ L^{\frac{D_{0}}{D_{0}-2s},\infty}+L^{\frac{D_{\infty}}{D_{\infty}-2s},\infty}
. More precisely, there exists a constant
S_{1,s}>0
, depending on
N,D_{\infty},D_{0},s,\gamma
, such that for any
f\in\mathscr{L}^{2s,1}
one has

\displaystyle\min\{\underset{\lambda>0}{\sup}\ \lambda\ |\{X\mid|f(X)|>\lambda\}|^{\frac{D_{0}-2s}{D_{0}}},\underset{\lambda>0}{\sup}\ \lambda\ |\{X\mid|f(X)|>\lambda\}|^{\frac{D_{\infty}-2s}{D_{\infty}}}\}

(2.4)

\displaystyle\leq S_{1,s}\|(-\mathscr{A})^{s}f\|_{1}.

We refer the reader to
[
11
]
for the case
p>1
, where we established the strong embeddings

(2.5)

\mathscr{L}^{2s,p}\hookrightarrow\ L^{\frac{pD}{D-2sp}}\qquad\mbox{ and }\qquad\mathscr{L}^{2s,p}\hookrightarrow L^{\frac{pD_{0}}{D_{0}-2sp}}+L^{\frac{pD_{\infty}}{D_{\infty}-2sp}}

under the respective assumptions (
1.9
) and (
1.12
) (with
p<\frac{D}{2s}
and
p<\frac{D_{\infty}}{2s}
).

Let us spend some words on the proofs provided in
[
11
]
of Theorem
2.3
and (
2.5
), which were inspired by the works by Varopoulos in the framework of positive symmetric semigroups (see, e.g.,
[
23
]
). With the aid of a crucial inversion formula for the fractional powers of
\mathscr{A}
in terms of suitable Riesz-type potentials having the following semigroup representation

f\mapsto\frac{1}{\Gamma(s)}\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{s-1}P_{t}fdt,

we were able to deduce the proof of our Sobolev-type embeddings from the
L^{p}-L^{q}
mapping properties of these Riesz potentials. The identification of such
q
is the point in the proof where the volume growth conditions (
1.9
) and (
1.12
) come into play. The key technical tool to show these mapping properties is the introduction of a maximal function related to
\mathscr{A}
, which we believe has interest in its own. Influenced by the powerful ideas by Stein in
[
22
]
, we exploited the Poisson semigroup
e^{z\sqrt{-\mathscr{A}}}
to define the maximal function

\mathscr{M}^{\star}f(X)=\underset{z>0}{\sup}\ \left|\frac{1}{\sqrt{4\pi}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{z}{t^{3/2}}e^{-\frac{z^{2}}{4t}}P_{t}f(X)dt\right|,\ \ \ \ X\in\mathbb{R}^{N}.

The operator
\mathscr{M}^{\star}
maps in fact continuously
L^{1}
in
L^{1,\infty}
and any
L^{p}
in itself for
p>1
(see
[
11
, Theorem 5.5]
), i.e. the following maximal theorem for the class (
1.1
) holds true.

Theorem 2.4
.

Assume (
1.6
).

(i)

There exists a universal constant
A_{1}>0
such that, given
f\in L^{1}
, one has

\underset{\lambda>0}{\sup}\ \lambda|\{X\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\mid\mathscr{M}^{\star}f(X)>\lambda\}|\leq A_{1}\|f\|_{1};

(ii)

If
1<p\leq\infty
, there exists a universal constant
A_{p}>0
such that for any
f\in L^{p}
one has

\|\mathscr{M}^{\star}f\|_{p}\leq A_{p}\|f\|_{p}.

2.2.
Nonlocal perimeters and coarea formulas

As we want to go back to the study of the Besov spaces
\mathfrak{B}^{\mathscr{A}}_{s,p}
and their seminorm
\mathscr{N}^{\mathscr{A}}_{s,p}
defined in (
1.7
), we start by recalling the relationship between the
s
-perimeter associated to
\mathscr{A}
and the fractional power
(-\mathscr{A})^{s}
. Keeping in mind Definition
1.2
and (
2.2
), we have in fact

(2.6)

\mathfrak{P}_{\mathscr{A},s}(E)=\frac{\Gamma(1-s)}{s}\|(-\mathscr{A})^{s}\mathbf{1}_{E}\|_{1}\qquad\mbox{ if }\mathbf{1}_{E}\in\mathfrak{B}^{\mathscr{A}}_{2s,1}.

We refer to
[
8
, Corollary 3.6]
and
[
9
, Lemma 3.3]
for a proof of (
2.6
). Moreover, in
[
8
, Proposition 3.3]
we showed the boundedness of the map
(-\mathscr{A})^{s}:\mathfrak{B}^{\mathscr{A}}_{2s,1}\longrightarrow L^{1}
. This says that

(2.7)

\mathfrak{B}^{\mathscr{A}}_{2s,1}\hookrightarrow\mathscr{L}^{2s,1}.

Like the Sobolev-type spaces
\mathscr{L}^{2s,p}
, also the Besov spaces
\mathfrak{B}^{\mathscr{A}}_{s,p}
enjoy useful density properties. In the next section it will play a role the following density result which was established in
[
3
, Proposition 3.2]
(see also
[
9
, Lemma 7.3]
).

Proposition 2.5
.

Remark 2.7

If one compares Definition

{\inf}\left\{\underset{k\to\infty}{\liminf}\ \|(-\mathscr{A})^{s}f_{k}\|_{1}\,\mid\,\{f_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\in\mathscr{S}\mbox{ such that }f_{k}\to\mathbf{1}_{E}\mbox{ in }L^{1}\right\}

In

The proof of Theorem
1.3
and Theorem
1.4
will result as a combination of Theorem
2.3
with the following lemma.

Lemma 3.1
.

Consider a measurable set
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
with
|E|<\infty
. Then we have

\underset{t\to 0^{+}}{\liminf}\left|\left\{X\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\mid P_{t}\mathbf{1}_{E}(X)>\frac{1}{2}\right\}\right|\geq\left|{\rm{int}}E\right|

Proof.

We start by noticing that

(3.1)

\mbox{ for every }X\in{\rm{int}}E\mbox{ we have }\underset{t\to 0^{+}}{\lim}P_{t}\mathbf{1}_{E}(X)=1.

The statement in (
3.1
) is a consequence of (
2.1
). As a matter of fact, for any
x\in{\rm{int}}E
, we can pick
\rho_{X}>0
such that an open neighborhood of size
\rho_{X}
is contained in
E
. Thanks to this fact, it is easy to construct a continuous function
\varphi_{X}
such that

0\leq\varphi_{X}\leq 1,\quad\varphi_{X}(X)=1,\quad\varphi_{X}\equiv 0\mbox{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}\smallsetminus E.

Hence we have

1\geq P_{t}\mathbf{1}_{E}(X)\geq P_{t}\varphi_{X}(X)\underset{t\to 0^{+}}{\longrightarrow}\varphi_{X}(X)=1,

which ensures the validity of (
3.1
). Therefore

\mbox{ for every }X\in{\rm{int}}E\,\,\mbox{ there exists }t_{X}>0\,\,\mbox{ such that }P_{t}\mathbf{1}_{E}(X)>\frac{1}{2}\,\,\mbox{ for }0<t<t_{X}.

This fact implies that

\underset{t\to 0^{+}}{\lim}\mathbf{1}_{E_{t}}(X)=1\quad\mbox{ for every }x\in{\rm{int}}E

once we denote

E_{t}=\left\{X\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\mid P_{t}\mathbf{1}_{E}(X)>\frac{1}{2}\right\}.

By Fatou’s Lemma we then obtain

\underset{t\to 0^{+}}{\liminf}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\mathbf{1}_{E_{t}}(X)dX\geq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left(\underset{t\to 0^{+}}{\liminf}\mathbf{1}_{E_{t}}(X)\right)dX\geq\int_{{\rm{int}}E}1dX,

which completes the proof of the desired statement.
∎

We are thus ready to provide the proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

For any
f\in\mathfrak{B}^{\mathscr{A}}_{2s,1}
, we denote

E_{\sigma}=\{X\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\mid|f(X)|>\sigma\},\qquad\mbox{for }\sigma>0.

Since by Proposition
2.6
we have

\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathfrak{P}_{\mathscr{A},s}\left(E_{\sigma}\right)d\sigma=\mathscr{N}^{\mathscr{A}}_{2s,1}(|f|)\leq\mathscr{N}^{\mathscr{A}}_{2s,1}(f)<\infty,

it is clear that for almost any
\sigma
one has
1_{E_{\sigma}}\in\mathfrak{B}^{\mathscr{A}}_{2s,1}
. As a consequence, for such
\sigma
, we obtain from
[
9
, Corollary 3.5]
and (
2.6
) that

(3.2)

\underset{t\to 0^{+}}{\lim}\|(-\mathscr{A})^{s}P_{t}1_{E_{\sigma}}\|_{1}=\|(-\mathscr{A})^{s}1_{E_{\sigma}}\|_{1}=\frac{s}{\Gamma(1-s)}\mathfrak{P}_{\mathscr{A},s}(E_{\sigma}).

The aim of the proof is to establish the following bound

(3.3)

||f||_{\frac{D}{D-2s}}\leq\frac{2sS_{1,s}}{\Gamma(1-s)}\mathscr{N}^{\mathscr{A}}_{2s,1}(f)\quad\mbox{ for every }f\in C^{\infty}\cap\mathfrak{B}^{\mathscr{A}}_{2s,1},

where
S_{1,s}
is the positive constant appearing in (
2.3
). Thus, let us fix
f\in C^{\infty}\cap\mathfrak{B}^{\mathscr{A}}_{2s,1}
and denote
G(\sigma)=|E_{\sigma}|
. Since
E_{\sigma}
has finite measure (by Chebyshev’s inequality) and it is open (by the continuity of
f
), we are entitled to apply Lemma
3.1
and we obtain

(3.4)

G(\sigma)\leq\underset{t\to 0^{+}}{\liminf}\left|\left\{X\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\mid P_{t}\mathbf{1}_{E_{\sigma}}(X)>\frac{1}{2}\right\}\right|.

On the other hand, since for
t>0
the positive function
P_{t}1_{E_{\sigma}}
belongs to
\mathscr{L}^{2s,1}
(this holds true for any
\sigma
such that
1_{E_{\sigma}}\in\mathfrak{B}^{\mathscr{A}}_{2s,1}
, see in this respect
[
9
, Lemma 3.4]
), Theorem
2.3
yields

(3.5)

\frac{1}{2}\left|\left\{X\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\mid P_{t}\mathbf{1}_{E_{\sigma}}(X)>\frac{1}{2}\right\}\right|^{\frac{D-2s}{D}}\leq S_{1,s}||(-\mathscr{A})^{s}P_{t}\mathbf{1}_{E_{\sigma}}||_{1}.

Therefore, from the combination of (
3.4
), (
3.5
), and (
3.2
), we obtain

G(\sigma)^{\frac{D-2s}{D}}\leq\frac{2sS_{1,s}}{\Gamma(1-s)}\mathfrak{P}_{\mathscr{A},s}(E_{\sigma})

Since
G
is non-increasing and
D\geq D_{0}\geq 2>2s
, we then have

\displaystyle||f||_{\frac{D}{D-2s}}

\displaystyle=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|f|^{\frac{D}{D-2s}}(X)dX\right)^{\frac{D-2s}{D}}=\left(\frac{D}{D-2s}\int_{0}^{\infty}\sigma^{\frac{2s}{D-2s}}G(\sigma)d\sigma\right)^{\frac{D-2s}{D}}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}G(\sigma)^{\frac{D-2s}{D}}d\sigma\leq\frac{2sS_{1,s}}{\Gamma(1-s)}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathfrak{P}_{\mathscr{A},s}\left(E_{\sigma}\right)d\sigma=\frac{2sS_{1,s}}{\Gamma(1-s)}\mathscr{N}^{\mathscr{A}}_{2s,1}(|f|)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2sS_{1,s}}{\Gamma(1-s)}\mathscr{N}^{\mathscr{A}}_{2s,1}(f).

The previous inequality proves the desired (
3.3
). Hence, the density of
C^{\infty}
in
\mathfrak{B}^{\mathscr{A}}_{2s,1}
(which is a consequence of Proposition
2.5
) implies the validity of (
1.10
) with the choice

c=\frac{\Gamma(1-s)}{2sS_{1,s}}.

In particular, if
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
has finite
s
-perimeter, we can plug
f=\mathbf{1}_{E}
in (
1.10
) and we deduce

\mathfrak{P}_{\mathscr{A},s}(E)=\mathscr{N}^{\mathscr{A}}_{2s,1}(\mathbf{1}_{E})\geq\frac{\Gamma(1-s)}{2sS_{1,s}}\|\mathbf{1}_{E}\|_{\frac{D}{D-2s}}=\frac{\Gamma(1-s)}{2sS_{1,s}}|E|^{\frac{D-2s}{D}},

which completes the proof of the theorem.
∎

We conclude the paper with the proof of Theorem
1.4
. To this aim we fix some notation. We set

q_{0}=\frac{D_{0}}{D_{0}-2s}\quad\mbox{ and }\quad q_{\infty}=\frac{D_{\infty}}{D_{\infty}-2s}.

We recall that, in the assumptions of the theorem, we have
D_{0}>D_{\infty}\geq 2>2s
. This says in particular that

(3.6)

q_{\infty}>q_{0}>1.

Let us also recall that, when we write
L^{q_{0}}+L^{q_{\infty}}
, we mean the Banach space of functions
f
which can be written as
f=f_{1}+f_{2}
with
f_{1}\in L^{q_{0}}
and
f_{2}\in L^{q_{\infty}}
, endowed with the norm

||f||_{L^{q_{0}}+L^{q_{\infty}}}=\inf_{f=f_{1}+f_{2},\\
f_{1}\in L^{q_{0}},\,f_{2}\in L^{q_{\infty}}}{||f_{1}||_{q_{0}}+||f_{2}||_{q_{\infty}}}.

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

We want to argue as similar as possible to the proof of Theorem
1.3
, from which we also borrow the notations for the sets
E_{\sigma}
and the non-increasing function
G(\sigma)
. We then fix an arbitrary function
f\in C^{\infty}\cap\mathfrak{B}^{\mathscr{A}}_{2s,1}
. From the combination of Lemma
3.1
, Theorem
2.3
, and (
3.2
), we obtain

\min\left\{G(\sigma)^{\frac{1}{q_{0}}},G(\sigma)^{\frac{1}{q_{\infty}}}\right\}\leq\frac{2sS_{1,s}}{\Gamma(1-s)}\mathfrak{P}_{\mathscr{A},s}(E_{\sigma}),

where
S_{1,s}
is the positive constant appearing in (
(b)
). If we then exploit the coarea formula (
2.8
), we deduce that

(3.7)

\int_{0}^{\infty}\min\left\{G(\sigma)^{\frac{1}{q_{0}}},G(\sigma)^{\frac{1}{q_{\infty}}}\right\}d\sigma\leq\frac{2sS_{1,s}}{\Gamma(1-s)}\mathscr{N}^{\mathscr{A}}_{2s,1}(|f|)\leq\frac{2sS_{1,s}}{\Gamma(1-s)}\mathscr{N}^{\mathscr{A}}_{2s,1}(f).

We now want to provide a lower bound for the left-hand side of (
3.7
) in terms of
||f||_{L^{q_{0}}+L^{q_{\infty}}}
. To this aim, we denote

\sigma_{f}=\sup\{\sigma>0\,:\,G(\sigma)>1\}.

If
|E_{\sigma}|\leq 1
for all
\sigma
, we agree to let
\sigma_{f}=0
. Since
f\in L^{1}
we have that
\sigma_{f}\in[0,\infty)
. We consider

(3.8)

f_{1}(X)=f(X)\mathbf{1}_{E_{\sigma_{f}}}(X)\quad\mbox{and}\quad f_{2}(X)=f(X)(1-\mathbf{1}_{E_{\sigma_{f}}}(X)).

We notice that
f_{1}(X)
and
f_{2}(X)
cannot be both non-null for the same
X
, and in particular the following holds true

\left|f(X)\right|=\left|f_{1}(X)+f_{2}(X)\right|=\left|f_{1}(X)\right|+\left|f_{2}(X)\right|.

We also make use of the notation
E^{i}_{\sigma}=\{X\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\,:\,\left|f_{i}(X)\right|>\sigma\}
for
i\in\{1,2\}
. One can check the following relations (see also
[
9
, pg. 38]
)

E^{1}_{\sigma}=\begin{cases}E_{\sigma}\qquad\,\,\mbox{if }\sigma>\sigma_{f},\\
E_{\sigma_{f}}\qquad\mbox{if }\sigma\leq\sigma_{f}\end{cases}\quad\mbox{ and }\quad E^{2}_{\sigma}=\begin{cases}\varnothing\,\,\,\quad\quad\quad\qquad\mbox{if }\sigma>\sigma_{f},\\
E_{\sigma}\smallsetminus E_{\sigma_{f}}\qquad\mbox{if }\sigma\leq\sigma_{f}.\end{cases}

Since
|E^{1}_{\sigma}|\leq 1
and
|E^{1}_{\sigma}|\leq G(\sigma)
for all
\sigma
, by (
3.6
) we obtain

(3.9)

\displaystyle||f_{1}||_{q_{0}}

\displaystyle=\left(q_{0}\int_{0}^{\infty}\sigma^{q_{0}-1}|E^{1}_{\sigma}|d\sigma\right)^{\frac{1}{q_{0}}}\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}|E^{1}_{\sigma}|^{\frac{1}{q_{0}}}d\sigma=\int_{0}^{\infty}\min\left\{|E^{1}_{\sigma}|^{\frac{1}{q_{0}}},|E^{1}_{\sigma}|^{\frac{1}{q_{\infty}}}\right\}d\sigma

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}\min\left\{G(\sigma)^{\frac{1}{q_{0}}},G(\sigma)^{\frac{1}{q_{\infty}}}\right\}d\sigma.

On the other hand, since
G(\sigma)
is bigger than
1
on the interval
(0,\sigma_{f})
, by (
3.6
) we also have

(3.10)

\displaystyle||f_{2}||_{q_{\infty}}

\displaystyle=\left(q_{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}\sigma^{q_{\infty}-1}|E^{2}_{\sigma}|d\sigma\right)^{\frac{1}{q_{\infty}}}\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}|E^{2}_{\sigma}|^{\frac{1}{q_{\infty}}}d\sigma=\int_{0}^{\sigma_{f}}|E^{2}_{\sigma}|^{\frac{D_{\infty}-2s}{D_{\infty}}}d\sigma

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{\sigma_{f}}G(\sigma)^{\frac{1}{q_{\infty}}}d\sigma=\int_{0}^{\sigma_{f}}\min\left\{G(\sigma)^{\frac{1}{q_{0}}},G(\sigma)^{\frac{1}{q_{\infty}}}\right\}d\sigma.

The combination of (
3.9
) and (
3.10
) yields

||f_{1}||_{q_{0}}+||f_{2}||_{q_{\infty}}\leq 2\int_{0}^{\infty}\min\left\{G(\sigma)^{\frac{1}{q_{0}}},G(\sigma)^{\frac{1}{q_{\infty}}}\right\}d\sigma,

Keeping in mind (
3.7
), we have just proved that

(3.11)

||f||_{L^{q_{0}}+L^{q_{\infty}}}\leq||f_{1}||_{q_{0}}+||f_{2}||_{q_{\infty}}\leq\frac{4sS_{1,s}}{\Gamma(1-s)}\mathscr{N}^{\mathscr{A}}_{2s,1}(f)\quad\mbox{ for every }f\in C^{\infty}\cap\mathfrak{B}^{\mathscr{A}}_{2s,1}.

The density of

in

implies then the validity of (
1.13
) with the choice

c=\frac{\Gamma(1-s)}{4sS_{1,s}}.

Finally, since it is not completely obvious to deduce from (
1.13
) the nonlocal isoperimetric inequality stated in Theorem
1.4
, we provide here the details. Let us thus take a set
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
with finite
s
-perimeter. We can consider a sequence
\{f_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
of Friedrichs’ mollifiers for the function

f_{k}\in C^{\infty}\cap\mathfrak{B}^{\mathscr{A}}_{2s,1},\qquad\qquad 0\leq f_{k}\leq 1.

If we let
(f_{k})_{1}
and
(f_{k})_{2}
the splitting of the function
f_{k}
according to (
3.8

(3.12)

\displaystyle\min\left\{\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|(f_{k})_{1}(X)|^{q_{0}}dX+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|(f_{k})_{2}(X)|^{q_{\infty}}dX\right)^{\frac{1}{q_{0}}},\right.

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt\left.\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|(f_{k})_{1}(X)|^{q_{0}}dX+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|(f_{k})_{2}(X)|^{q_{\infty}}dX\right)^{\frac{1}{q_{\infty}}}\right\}

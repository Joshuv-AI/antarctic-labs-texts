The positive energy theorem for weighted asymptotically anti-de Sitter spacetimes
Yaohua Wang †, Xiao Zhang ♭

Abstract
. The positive energy theorem for weighted asymptotically flat spin manifolds was proved by Baldauf and Ozuch [ 3 ] , and for non-spin case by Chu and Zhu [ 11 ] . In this paper, we generalize the positive energy theorem for 3-dimensional asymptotically anti-de Sitter initial data sets to weighted asymptotically anti-de Sitter initial data sets assuming that the weighted dominant energy condition holds.



1.
Introduction

The positive energy theorem plays a fundamental role in general relativity. In the case of zero cosmological constant, the positive energy theorem was first proved by Schoen and Yau
[
24
,
25
]
, then by Witten
[
29
,
23
]
. For time-symmetric initial data sets, the dominant energy condition is equivalent to

\displaystyle R\geq 0,

(1.1)

where
R
is the scalar curvature of the initial data set. In this case, Schoen and Yau used minimal surfaces to prove that total energy is nonnegative, and zero if and only if the manifold is the Euclidean space
[
24
]
. For non time-symmetric initial data sets, they solved the Jang equation and reduced the dominant energy condition to

\displaystyle R-2|X|^{2}+2\mbox{div}X\geq 0

(1.2)

for some falling off vector
X
, and proved that the total energy is nonnegative, and zero if and only if the manifold can be isometrically embedded into the Minkowski spacetime as a spacelike hypersurface equipped with the prescribed metric and the second fundamental form
[
25
]
. Such strategy was also used to prove the positivity of Liu-Yau’s quasi-local mass by Liu and Yau
[
20
]
. The condition (
1.2
) indicates that the positive energy theorem could hold true even if the scalar curvature is negative somewhere. Inspired by this observation, it was shown that the first Neumann eigenvalue is nonnegative can imply the total energy is nonnegative
[
30
]
. Furthermore, the second-named author derived the Schrödinger-Lichnerowicz formula for the Dirac-Witten operator in terms of the intrinsic spin connection
[
31
]
and used it to prove the positive total energy under the condition

\displaystyle R+H^{2}-2|\nabla H|\geq 0

(1.3)

for some falling off function
H

[
33
]
. (The theorem was proved for 3-dimensional spacelike hypersurfaces, but the argument can be extended to higher dimensional spin manifolds.) Recently, the condition (
1.3
) was applied to prove the quantitative shielding theorem via stable
\mu
-bubbles duo to Lesourd, Unger and Yau
[
19
]
, which was revisited via the Dirac operator by Cecchini and Zeidler
[
10
]
(see also
[
9
]
). By using the Penrose operator
[
10
]
, or choosing the second fundamental forms in initial data sets to be proportional to the metrics with proportional factor
-\frac{H}{n-1}
in the dominant energy condition
[
18
]
, Cecchini and Zeidler, as well as Lee, Lesourd and Unger showed that the quantitative shielding theorem also holds under the condition

\displaystyle R+\frac{n}{n-1}H^{2}-2|\nabla H|\geq 0.

(1.4)

In
[
3
]
, Baldauf and Ozuch defined the weighted total energy for weighted asymptotically flat manifolds

\displaystyle m_{f}(g)=m(g)+2\lim_{R\longrightarrow\infty}\int_{S_{R}}\langle\nabla f,\nu\rangle

for some real function. In the case of spin manifolds, they perturbed the Dirac operator and the Laplacian operator

\displaystyle D_{f}=D-\frac{1}{2}\nabla f,\qquad\Delta_{f}=\Delta-\nabla_{\nabla f}

and derived the Schrödinger-Lichnerowicz formula

\displaystyle D_{f}^{2}=-\Delta_{f}+\frac{1}{4}\big(R+2\Delta f-|\nabla f|^{2}\big).

Baldauf and Ozuch proved the positive energy theorem for
m_{f}(g)
by using Witten’s argument under the condition
[
3
]

R+2\Delta f-|\nabla f|^{2}\geq 0.

(1.5)

A non-spin version was proved by Chu and Zhu
[
11
]
.

It is clearly that (
1.2
) is stronger than (
1.5
) by setting
X=\nabla f
and (
1.3
) is stronger than (
1.4
). They yield slightly different conclusions. The positivity of
m_{f}(g)
does not conclude the positivity of
m(g)
. Under condition (
1.3
), that the total energy is equal to the total momentum implies that the manifold is the Euclidean space and
H
is zero
[
33
]
. But, under condition (
1.4
), it needs further assumptions on the metrics as well as the function
H
to make it true. We refer
[
5
,
6
,
7
,
14
,
15
,
13
,
16
]
for the general discussing on the rigidity when
E=|P|
.

When the cosmological constant is negative, the positive energy theorem was first proved by Wang
[
26
]
, and then by Chruściel and Herzlich
[
4
]
for asymptotically anti-de Sitter initial data sets with the trivial second fundamental form. For the nontrivial second fundamental forms,
Chruściel, Maerten and Tod
[
8
]
proved it by assuming the existence of center of AdS mass’ coordinate systems
[
21
,
8
]
, and the authors and Xie finally proved it for general case
[

]

[

.

In this paper, we generalize the positive energy theorem in
[
27
]
to that of weighted asymptotically anti-de Sitter initial data. Let

E_{0}
be the total energy,
c_{i}
,
{c^{\prime}}_{i}
and
J_{i}
be the total momenta provided in
[
27
]
. Let

be certain suitable

function and
E_{0}^{f}
be the weighted total energy,

,

and

be the weighted total momenta with respect to

\displaystyle E_{0}^{f}\geq\sqrt{L_{f}^{2}-2V_{f}^{2}+2\big(\max\{A_{f}^{4}-L_{f}^{2}V_{f}^{2},0\}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}},

The paper is organized as follows:

In Section 3, we provide the definition of the energy-momentum endomorphism.
In Section 4, we prove the positive energy theorem for the weighted asymptotically anti-de Sitter initial data sets satisfying the weighted dominant energy conditions. In appendix, we provide the restriction of the ten Killing vectors

2.
Weighted total energy-momenta

Let
(N,\widetilde{g})
be a 4-dimensional spacetime where
\widetilde{g}
satisfies the Einstein field equations

\displaystyle\widetilde{Ric}-\frac{\widetilde{R}}{2}\tilde{g}+\Lambda\tilde{g}=T

with negative cosmological constant

\displaystyle\Lambda=-3\kappa^{2}

for some positive constant
\kappa
. Let
M
be a spacelike hypersurface of
N
with the induced metric
g
and the second fundamental form
h

An weighted initial data set is a quadruple
(M,g,h,e^{-f}dv)
where
f
is a real function. Denote

\displaystyle R_{f}=R+2\Delta f-|\nabla f|^{2}

where
R

if the following inequality holds

\displaystyle\mu^{f}\geq|\nu^{f}|+\kappa|\nabla f|,

where

\displaystyle\mu^{f}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\Big(R_{f}+6\kappa^{2}+\big(\sum h_{ii}\big)^{2}-\sum_{i,j}h_{ij}^{2}\Big),

\displaystyle\nu^{f}_{i}

\displaystyle=\nabla_{j}h_{ij}-\nabla_{i}h_{jj}-f_{j}h_{ij}.

Definition 2.1
.

Weighted initial data set
(M,g,h,e^{-f}dv)
is weighted asymptotically anti-de Sitter of order
\tau>0
if

(1)
There is a compact set
K\subset M
such that

is diffeomorphic to the disjoint union of a finite number of subsets (ends)

and each
M_{i}

is diffeomorphic to
\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus B_{r_{i}}
with
B_{r_{i}}
the closed ball of radius
r_{i}
;

(2)
Under this diffeomorphism, on each end the metric

g_{ij}=g(\breve{e}_{i},\breve{e}_{j})

is of the form
g_{ij}=\delta_{ij}+a_{ij}
where
a_{ij}
satisfies

\displaystyle a_{ij}=O(e^{-\tau\kappa r}),\quad\breve{{\nabla}}_{k}a_{ij}=O(e^{-\tau\kappa r}),\quad\breve{\nabla}_{l}\breve{{\nabla}}_{k}a_{ij}=O(e^{-\tau\kappa r}),

the second fundamental form
h_{ij}=h(\breve{e}_{i},\breve{e}_{j})
satisfies

\displaystyle h_{ij}=O(e^{-\tau\kappa r}),\quad\breve{{\nabla}}_{k}h_{ij}=O(e^{-\tau\kappa r}),

and
f
satisfies

\displaystyle f=O(e^{-\tau\kappa r}),\quad\breve{{\nabla}}_{k}f=O(e^{-\tau\kappa r}),\quad\breve{\nabla}_{l}\breve{{\nabla}}_{k}f=O(e^{-\tau\kappa r});

(3)
There exists a distance function
\rho_{z}
such that

\displaystyle\mu e^{\kappa\rho_{z}},\,\,\nu_{i}e^{\kappa\rho_{z}},\,\,\nabla fe^{\kappa\rho_{z}}\in L^{1}(M)

where
\breve{{\nabla}}
and
\{\breve{e}_{i}\}
are the Levi-Civita connection and frame of the hyperbolic metric

\displaystyle\breve{g}=dr^{2}+\frac{\sinh^{2}(\kappa r)}{\kappa^{2}}\big(d\theta^{2}+\sin^{2}\theta d\psi^{2}\big).

When
f=0
, the above definition reduces to the asymptotically anti-de Sitter initial set
[
27
]
. The condition
(3)
in Definition
2.1
is used to ensure that the weighted total energy-momenta are well-defined.

Throughout the paper, we always assume
\tau>\frac{3}{2}
. Denote

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{i}=\breve{\nabla}^{j}g_{ij}-\breve{{\nabla}}_{i}tr_{\breve{g}}(g)-\kappa(a_{1i}-g_{1i}tr_{\breve{g}}(a)),\quad\mathcal{P}_{ki}=h_{ki}-g_{ki}tr_{\breve{g}}(h).

Recall the following total energy-momenta provided in

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} E_{0}=&\frac{\kappa}{16\pi}\lim_{r\rightarrow\infty}\int_{S_{r}}\mathcal{E}_{1}U_{40}^{(0)}\breve{\omega},\\

(2.2)

,

\varepsilon_{ijl}V_{i}=U_{jl}
.

Denote

\displaystyle\widetilde{\nabla}_{e_{0}}e_{j}(x)=0,\quad\nabla_{e_{i}}e_{j}(x)=0

\displaystyle h_{ij}=\widetilde{g}(\widetilde{\nabla}_{e_{i}}e_{0},e_{j}),\quad H=g^{ij}h_{ij}

\displaystyle\widetilde{\nabla}_{e_{i}}e_{0}(x)=h_{ij}e_{j},\quad\widetilde{\nabla}_{e_{i}}e_{j}(x)=h_{ij}e_{0},

\widehat{D}_{f}^{*}\widehat{D}_{f}=\widehat{D}_{f}\widehat{D}_{f}^{*}=\widehat{\nabla}^{*}\widehat{\nabla}+\widehat{\mathcal{R}}_{f},

\displaystyle\widehat{D}_{f}^{*}\widehat{D}_{f}=

\displaystyle\widehat{D}_{f}\widehat{D}_{f}^{*}=\nabla^{*}\nabla+\frac{1}{4}\Big(R+H^{2}+9\kappa^{2}\Big)

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\nabla_{i}h_{jj}e_{i}\cdot e_{0}\cdot+\frac{1}{2}(\Delta f)-\frac{1}{4}|\nabla f|^{2}+f_{i}\nabla_{i}.

Note that

Energy-momentum endomorphism

The spinor bundle is
\hbox{\bb C}^{4}
over the anti-de Sitter spacetime and the anti-de Sitter spacetime is characterized by imaginary Killing spinors satisfying the following equations

\displaystyle\widetilde{\nabla}^{AdS}_{X}\Phi_{0}+\frac{\kappa\sqrt{-1}}{2}X\cdot\Phi_{0}=0.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
(M,g,h)
be a weighted
3

for all

Lemma 4.2
.

For 3-dimensional weighted asymptotically AdS initial data set, there is a constant

such that

for all

Proof:
The proof is similar to the corresponding Poincaré inequalities in
[

,
28

. As
C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{S})
is dense in

Separate
M
into two parts
K

K
is a compact set and

is constitute of ends. For any

\displaystyle C_{1}\leq e^{-f}\leq C_{2}

for some positive constants

\int_{K}|\phi|^{2}e^{-f}dV_{g}\leq C\bigg(\int_{K}|\widehat{\nabla}\phi|^{2}e^{-f}dV_{g}+\int_{\partial(M\setminus K)}|\phi|^{2}d\sigma_{g}\bigg).

(4.5)

On each end, the volume element

\displaystyle e^{-f}dV_{g}\approx d\mu=e^{2\kappa r}drdS_{0},

where
dS_{0}
is the area element of unit sphere

. Thus we just need to prove the corresponding inequality for the volume element

\displaystyle\int_{M_{\infty}}|\phi|^{2}e^{2\kappa r}drdS_{0}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\kappa}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus B(0.R_{0})}|\phi|^{2}d(e^{2\kappa r})dS_{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2\kappa}\int_{\partial B(0,R_{0})}|\phi|^{2}d\sigma-\frac{1}{2\kappa}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus B(0.R_{0})}\partial_{r}|\phi|^{2}d\mu.

Note that

\displaystyle\bigg|\frac{1}{2\kappa}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus B(0,R_{0})}\partial_{r}|\phi|^{2}d\mu\bigg|\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\kappa}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus B(0,R_{0})}\left(2|\langle\widehat{\nabla}\phi,\phi\rangle|+|h||\phi|^{2}+\kappa|\phi|^{2}\right)d\mu

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus B(0,R_{0})}|\phi|^{2}d\mu+\frac{1}{\kappa}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus B(0,R_{0})}|\langle\widehat{\nabla}\phi,\phi\rangle|d\mu

\displaystyle+\frac{C}{2\kappa}e^{-\tau\kappa R_{0}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus B(0,R_{0})}|\phi|^{2}d\mu

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Big(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\epsilon}{\kappa}+\frac{C}{2\kappa}e^{-\tau\kappa R_{0}}\Big)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus B(0,R_{0})}|\phi|^{2}d\mu

\displaystyle+\frac{4}{\epsilon\kappa}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus B(0,R_{0})}|\widehat{\nabla}\phi|^{2}d\mu,

we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus B(0,R_{0})}|\phi|^{2}d\mu\leq

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2\kappa}\int_{\partial B(0,R_{0})}|\phi|^{2}d\sigma+\frac{4}{\epsilon\kappa}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus B(0,R_{0})}|\widehat{\nabla}\phi|^{2}d\mu

\displaystyle+\Big(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\epsilon}{\kappa}+\frac{C}{2\kappa}e^{-\tau\kappa R_{0}}\Big)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus B(0,R_{0})}|\phi|^{2}d\mu.

Choosing

sufficiently small and
R_{0}
sufficiently large, we get

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus B(0,R_{0})}|\phi|^{2}d\mu\leq-A_{1}\int_{\partial B(0,R_{0})}|\phi|^{2}d\sigma+A_{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus B(0,R_{0})}|\widehat{\nabla}\phi|^{2},

(4.6)

for some positive constants
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
. This implies

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus B(0,R_{0})}e^{-f}|\phi|^{2}dV_{g}\leq

\displaystyle-B_{1}\int_{\partial B(0,R_{0})}|\phi|^{2}d\sigma_{g}

(4.7)

\displaystyle+B_{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus B(0,R_{0})}e^{-f}|\widehat{\nabla}\phi|^{2}dV_{g}

for some positive constants
B_{1}
and
B_{2}
. Combining (

4.7
), we get the desired Poincaré inequality.

Proposition 4.1

5.
Appendix: Killing vectors for the anti-de Sitter spacetime

The anti-de Sitter spacetime can be viewed as the hyperboloid

\displaystyle\eta_{\alpha\beta}y^{\alpha}y^{\beta}=-\frac{1}{\kappa^{2}}

in
\hbox{\bb R}^{3,2}
equipped with the metric

\displaystyle\eta_{\alpha\beta}dy^{\alpha}dy^{\beta}=-(dy^{0})^{2}+\sum^{3}_{i=1}(dy^{i})^{2}-(dy^{4})^{2}.

Under coordinate transformations

\displaystyle y^{0}=\frac{\cos(\kappa t)}{\kappa}\cosh(\kappa r),\quad y^{i}=\frac{1}{\kappa}\sinh(\kappa r)n^{i},\quad y^{4}=\frac{\sin(\kappa t)}{\kappa}\cosh(\kappa r),

where

\displaystyle n^{1}=\sin\theta\cos\psi,\quad n^{2}=\sin\theta\sin\psi,\quad n^{3}=\cos\theta,

the induced anti-de Sitter metric is

\displaystyle\widetilde{g}_{AdS}=-\cosh^{2}(\kappa r)dt^{2}+dr^{2}+\frac{\sinh^{2}(\kappa r)}{\kappa^{2}}\big(d\theta^{2}+\sin^{2}\theta d\psi^{2}\big).

Restricted on the hyperboloid, the rotation vectors for
\hbox{\bb R}^{3,2}

\displaystyle U_{\alpha\beta}=y_{\alpha}\frac{\partial}{\partial y^{\beta}}-y_{\beta}\frac{\partial}{\partial y^{\alpha}}

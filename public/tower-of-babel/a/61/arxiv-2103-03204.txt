On the empirical spectral distribution for certain models related to sample covariance matrices with different correlations
Alicja Dembczak-Kołodziejczyk, Anna Lytova

Abstract
Given n , m ∈ ℕ n,m\in\mathbb{N} , we study two classes of large random matrices of the form ℒ n = ∑ α = 1 m ξ α ​ 𝐲 α ​ 𝐲 α T and 𝒜 n = ∑ α = 1 m ξ α ​ ( 𝐲 α ​ 𝐱 α T + 𝐱 α ​ 𝐲 α T ) , \mathcal{L}_{n}=\sum_{\alpha=1}^{m}\xi_{\alpha}\mathbf{y}_{\alpha}\mathbf{y}_{\alpha}^{T}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathcal{A}_{n}=\sum_{\alpha=1}^{m}\xi_{\alpha}(\mathbf{y}_{\alpha}\mathbf{x}_{\alpha}^{T}+\mathbf{x}_{\alpha}\mathbf{y}_{\alpha}^{T}), where for every n n , ( ξ α ) α ⊂ ℝ (\xi_{\alpha})_{\alpha}\subset\mathbb{R} are iid random variables independent of ( 𝐱 α , 𝐲 α ) α (\mathbf{x}_{\alpha},\mathbf{y}_{\alpha})_{\alpha} , and ( 𝐱 α ) α (\mathbf{x}_{\alpha})_{\alpha} , ( 𝐲 α ) α ⊂ ℝ n (\mathbf{y}_{\alpha})_{\alpha}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n} are two (not necessarily independent) sets of independent random vectors having different covariance matrices and generating well concentrated bilinear forms. We consider two main asymptotic regimes as n , m ⁡ ( n ) → ∞ n,m(n)\to\infty : a standard one, where m / n → c m/n\to c , and a slightly modified one, where m / n → ∞ m/n\to\infty and 𝐄 ​ ξ → 0 \mathbf{E}\xi\to 0 while m ​ 𝐄 ​ ξ / n → c m\mathbf{E}\xi/n\to c for some c ≥ 0 c\geq 0 . Assuming that vectors ( 𝐱 α ) α (\mathbf{x}_{\alpha})_{\alpha} and ( 𝐲 α ) α (\mathbf{y}_{\alpha})_{\alpha} are normalized and isotropic “in average”, we prove the convergence in probability of the empirical spectral distributions of ℒ n \mathcal{L}_{n} and 𝒜 n \mathcal{A}_{n} to a version of the Marchenko-Pastur law and so called effective medium spectral distribution, correspondingly. In particular, choosing normalized Rademacher random variables as ( ξ α ) α (\xi_{\alpha})_{\alpha} , in the modified regime one can get a shifted semicircle and semicircle laws. We also apply our results to the certain classes of matrices having block structures, which were studied in [ 9 , 21 ] .

Introduction

In
[
9
,
21
]
, the authors studied the empirical spectral distributions of the following two related models of sparse block matrices.
Given
r,d\in\mathbb{N}
, let
(\mathbf{v}^{kl})_{1\leq k<l\leq r}
be independent copies of a random vector
\mathbf{v}
uniformly distributed on the unit sphere in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and let
(\xi_{kl})_{1\leq k<l\leq r}
be independent copies of a
0/1
random variable
\xi=\xi_{r}
such that
\mathbf{E}\xi=p_{r}
for some
p_{r}\in(0,1]
. Define
\mathcal{A}_{rd}
and
\mathcal{L}_{rd}
as
rd\times rd
block matrices of the form

\mathcal{A}_{rd}=\Big((1-\delta_{k\ell})B_{k\ell}\Big)_{k,\ell=1}^{r},\quad\mathcal{L}_{rd}=\Big(\delta_{k\ell}\sum_{j\neq k}B_{j\ell}\Big)_{k,\ell=1}^{r}-\mathcal{A}_{rd},

(1.1)

where for
1\leq k<l\leq r
blocks

B_{k\ell}=\xi_{k\ell}\mathbf{v}^{k\ell}\mathbf{v}^{k\ell\,T}

(1.2)

are
d\times d
rank-one matrices with probability
p_{r}
(and 0 otherwise).
These models were introduced in
[
9
]
while studying the elastic
vibrational modes of amorphous solids. Roughly speaking they describe a system of
r

d
-dimensional points connected
by springs (see also
[
15
,
27
]
and references therein for the application of these models in
the study of certain disordered systems). Evidently, for
d=1
matrices
\mathcal{A}_{rd}
and
\mathcal{L}_{rd}
reduce to the adjacency matrix and Laplacian of the Erdős–Rényi graph.

Recall that given a Hermitian or symmetric
n\times n
matrix
\mathcal{M}_{n}
with eigenvalues
(\lambda_{i})_{i}
, the empirical spectral distribution
N_{\mathcal{M}_{n}}
of
\mathcal{M}_{n}
and its Stieltjes transform
s_{\mathcal{M}_{n}}
are defined by the formulas

N_{\mathcal{M}_{n}}(\Delta)=|\{i:\,\lambda_{i}\in\Delta\}|/n,\,\,\forall\Delta\subset\mathbb{R},\quad\text{and}

s_{\mathcal{M}_{n}}(z)=\int\frac{N_{\mathcal{M}_{n}}(d\lambda)}{\lambda-z}=\frac{1}{n}\Tr(\mathcal{M}_{n}-z)^{-1},\,\,\Im z\neq 0.

In
[
9
,
21
]
, the authors studied the empirical spectral distributions
N_{\mathcal{A}_{rd}}
and
N_{\mathcal{L}_{rd}}
as
r\rightarrow\infty
in different asymptotic regimes, depending on
d
and
p_{r}
. In the case when
d
and
rp_{r}
are some fixed numbers (sparse matrices), the first several moments of the limiting distributions were computed. In the “dense” regime when

d\rightarrow\infty,\quad p_{r}=O(1),\quad\text{and}\quad p_{r}r/d\rightarrow c>0,\quad\text{as}\quad r\rightarrow\infty,

the
convergence in mean
of
N_{\mathcal{L}_{rd}}
and
N_{\mathcal{A}_{rd}}
to the Marchenko-Pastur law and to the so called effective medium spectral distribution, correspondingly, was proved. (Here we follow terminology from
[
9
]
, see also
[
23
]
.) It was shown that the limits of the corresponding Stieltjes transforms,
f_{\mathcal{L}}:=\lim_{r\rightarrow\infty}\mathbf{E}s_{\mathcal{L}_{rd}}
and

f_{\mathcal{A}}:=\lim_{r\rightarrow\infty}\mathbf{E}s_{\mathcal{A}_{rd}}
, satisfy equations

\displaystyle 2zf_{\mathcal{L}}^{2}+(z+2-c)f_{\mathcal{L}}+1=0\quad\text{and}\quad zf_{\mathcal{A}}^{3}+(1-c)f_{\mathcal{A}}^{2}-zf_{\mathcal{A}}-1=0.

(1.3)

Also the first several moments of
N_{\mathcal{A}_{rd}}
and
N_{\mathcal{L}_{rd}}
were calculated in the so called “dilute” regime when
d
is fixed and
p_{r}=O(r^{-\delta})
,
\delta\in(0,1)
, as
r\rightarrow\infty
, and it was claimed that for big enough
r
these moments coincide with the moments of the semicircle and shifted semicircle distributions and, in particular,

\displaystyle 2f_{\mathcal{L}_{rd}}^{2}+(z-c_{r})f_{\mathcal{L}_{rd}}+1=0\quad\text{and}\quad c_{r}f_{\mathcal{A}_{rd}}^{2}+zf_{\mathcal{A}_{rd}}+1=0,

(1.4)

where
c_{r}:=rp_{r}/d\to\infty
, as
r\to\infty
.

In our work we suppose that
d
grows to infinity with
r
. We modify the dilute regime as follows:

p_{r}\to 0
and
r/d\to\infty
while
c_{r}=rp_{r}/d\to c\geq 0
as
r,d\to\infty
. This guarantees that the corresponding sequences of empirical spectral distributions
N_{\mathcal{L}_{rd}}
and
N_{\mathcal{A}_{rd}}
are tight and also allows to unify two regimes as follows:

d\to\infty\quad\text{and}\quad rp_{r}/d\to c\geq 0\quad\text{as}\quad r\to\infty.

(1.5)

We consider models (
1.1
) corresponding to normalized isotropic vectors
(\mathbf{v}^{k\ell})_{k<\ell}
which generate well-concentrated bilinear forms (see Assumption 1), and applying the Stieltjes transform method give a straightforward proof of the convergence in probability of
N_{\mathcal{L}_{rd}}
and
N_{\mathcal{A}_{rd}}
to the Marchenko-Pastur law and the effective medium spectral distribution. We show that the limits are the same for both regimes (in contrast to (
1.3
) and (
1.4
)), and that to get (
1.4
) with
c_{r}=c
in the new dilute regime,
(\xi^{k\ell})_{k<\ell}
needs
to take both negative and positive values with non-zero probability. In particular, one can get (
1.4
) if
(\xi^{k\ell})_{k<\ell}
are properly normalized Rademacher random variables (see Example 3 and Remark
1.6
).

Note that we can rewrite matrices
\mathcal{A}_{rd}
and
\mathcal{L}_{rd}
in the form

\mathcal{L}_{rd}=\sum_{1\leq k<\ell\leq r}\xi_{k\ell}Y^{k\ell}Y^{k\ell\,T}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathcal{A}_{rd}=\sum_{1\leq k\neq\ell\leq r}\xi_{k\ell}X^{k\ell}X^{\ell k\,T},

(1.6)

where
\xi_{k\ell}=\xi_{\ell k}
,
(X^{k\ell})_{k\neq\ell}
and
(Y^{k\ell})_{k<\ell}
are sparse block vectors in

given by

Y^{k\ell}=((\delta_{jk}-\delta_{j\ell})\mathbf{v}^{kl})_{j=1}^{r}\quad\text{and}\quad X^{k\ell}=(\delta_{jk}\mathbf{v}^{kl})_{j=1}^{r}.

This naturally leads to the study of more general classes of random matrices of the form

\mathcal{L}_{n}=\sum_{\alpha}\xi_{\alpha}\mathbf{y}_{\alpha}\mathbf{y}_{\alpha}^{T}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathcal{A}_{n}=\sum_{\alpha}\xi_{\alpha}(\mathbf{y}_{\alpha}\mathbf{x}_{\alpha}^{T}+\mathbf{x}_{\alpha}\mathbf{y}_{\alpha}^{T}),

(1.7)

where
\xi_{\alpha}\in\mathbb{R}
and
\mathbf{y}_{\alpha},\mathbf{x}_{\alpha}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
\alpha\leq m
, are some random variables and vectors.
We mainly concentrate on model
\mathcal{L}_{n}
, which is closely related to the sample covariance matrices, only that here we allow vectors
(\mathbf{y}_{\alpha})_{\alpha}
to have
different covariance matrices

Q_{\alpha}:=\mathbf{E}\mathbf{y}_{\alpha}\mathbf{y}_{\alpha}^{T}
,
\alpha\leq m
(note that here

m^{-1}\sum_{\alpha}Q_{\alpha}\approx n^{-1}I_{n}
, which allows to show that the empirical spectral distributions still converge to the Marchenko-Pastur law.

Certain closely related models were also studied in

Assumption 1.

We suppose that for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
\mathbf{y}_{\alpha}=\mathbf{y}_{\alpha,n}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
\alpha\leq m,
are mutually independent random vectors such that for all deterministic matrices
D=D_{n}
with

\sup_{\alpha}\mathbf{Var}(D\,\mathbf{y}_{\alpha},\mathbf{y}_{\alpha})={o}(1),\quad n\rightarrow\infty.

(1.8)

Here and in what follows, given a matrix
D

\|D\|_{op}
and
\|D\|_{HS}

Assumption 2.

For every
n\in\mathbb{N}
, let
\xi_{\alpha}=\xi_{\alpha,n}\in\mathbb{R}
,
\alpha\leq m,
be mutually independent copies of a random variable
\xi_{n}

m/n=O(1)

\frac{m}{n}\mathbf{E}\xi_{n}=O(1)

We suppose that as

,
\widetilde{\sigma}_{n}
converges weakly to a signed measure
\widetilde{\sigma}
such that
|\widetilde{\sigma}(\mathbb{R})|<\infty
, and

\displaystyle\sup_{n}\int|\xi^{p}d\widetilde{\sigma}_{n}(\xi)|=\sup_{n}\frac{m}{n}\mathbf{E}|\xi_{n}|^{p+1}<\infty,\quad p=1,2,3.

We use notation
c_{1}:=\widetilde{\sigma}(\mathbb{R})\in\mathbb{R}

|c_{1}|<\infty
.

Remark 1.1
.

Note that if
m/n\to c>0
, then
\widetilde{\sigma}_{n}=c\sigma_{n}+o(1)
as
n\to\infty
. Also, a bit more delicate but quite standard nowadays argument based on a truncation procedure for

,

.

Our main result concerns convergence of the empirical spectral distributions of

Theorem 1.2
.

Given
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
, consider
n\times n
matrices

\mathcal{L}_{n}=\sum_{\alpha}\xi_{\alpha}\mathbf{y}_{\alpha}\mathbf{y}_{\alpha}^{T},

where
\xi_{\alpha}
and
\mathbf{y}_{\alpha}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
\alpha\leq m
, are mutually independent random variables satisfying Assumptions 1, 2. Let

Q_{\alpha}:=\mathbf{E}\mathbf{y}_{\alpha}\mathbf{y}_{\alpha}^{T}

be such that

\displaystyle\sup_{\alpha}\|Q_{\alpha}\|_{op}=O(n^{-1}),\quad\sup_{\alpha}|\Tr Q_{\alpha}-1|=o(1),\,\,\text{and}

(1.9)

\displaystyle Q_{(n)}:=\frac{1}{m}\sum_{\alpha}Q_{\alpha}=\frac{1}{n}I_{n}+B_{n},\,\,\text{where}\,\,\|B_{n}\|_{HS}=o(n^{-1/2}),\,\,n\to\infty.

(1.10)

Then as
n\to\infty
the empirical spectral distributions
N_{\mathcal{L}_{n}}
converge in probability to a non-random probability measure
N_{\mathcal{L}}
which Stieltjes transform
f
is uniquely determined by the equation

zf(z)=-1+f(z)\int\frac{d\widetilde{\sigma}(\xi)}{1+\xi f(z)}

(1.11)

in the class of Stieltjes transforms of non-negative measures.

Remark 1.3
.

A simple renormalization allows to show that if in (
1.9
) and (
1.10
) we have

\sup_{\alpha}|\Tr Q_{\alpha}-a|=o(1)\quad\text{and}\quad Q_{(n)}=\frac{a}{n}I_{n}+B_{n}\quad\text{for some}\,\,a>0,

then

zf=-1+af\int(1+a\xi f)^{-1}d\widetilde{\sigma}(\xi).

Also in the case
d\widetilde{\sigma}(\xi)=c\xi d\sigma(\xi)
we restore the Marchenko-Pastur distribution.

Proof of Theorem
1.2

The proof is based on the standard nowadays method of Stieltjes transform which goes back to
[
18
]
(see
[
2
,
3
,
5
,
20
]
for the details of the method and main properties of the Stieltjes transform), and which is used in a huge number of results on convergence of empirical spectral distributions of random matrices. This method is based on the fact that there is a one-to-one continuous correspondence between non-negative measures and their Stieltjes transforms, so that to find a weak limit in probability of random probability measures
N_{\mathcal{L}_{n}}
it is enough to show that for every
z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{R}
the Stieltjes transforms
s_{n}:=s_{\mathcal{L}_{n}}
of
N_{\mathcal{L}_{n}}

converge in probability to a deterministic limit
f
satisfying
\lim_{\eta\to\infty}\eta|f(i\eta)|=1
. Then
f
is the Stieltjes transform of a probability measure
N
such that
N_{\mathcal{L}_{n}}
converge weakly in probability to
N
and for every
\Delta\subset\mathbb{R}

N(\Delta)=\frac{1}{\pi}\lim_{\eta\to+0}\int_{\Delta}f(\lambda+i\eta)d\lambda.

Our scheme of the proof is as follows: in Lemma
2.2
we show that
\mathbf{Var}s_{n}(z)=o(1)
as
n\to\infty
, that reduces the problem to finding the limit of the expectations
\mathbf{E}s_{n}:=f_{n}
, then in the main body of the proof (Lemma
2.3
) we show that for every convergent subsequence of
(f_{n})_{n}
, its limit satisfies (
1.11
), and finally, the unique solvability of (
1.11
) in the class of the Stieltjes transforms of probability measures follows from Lemma (
2.1
) below.

Lemma 2.1
.

(Solvability and uniqueness).
Let
\widetilde{\sigma}
be a signed measure defined in Assumption 1.
Then there is a unique solution
f
of (
1.11
) in the class of Stieltjes transforms of the non-negative measures. Moreover,

\lim_{\eta\to\infty}\eta|f(i\eta)|=1
, so that the corresponding to
f
measure
N
is a probability measure,
N(\mathbb{R})=1
.

Proof.

We show first that if
f
is the Stieltjes transform of a non-negative measure
N
then for any

n\times n
matrix

\mathcal{A}_{n}=\sum_{\alpha}\xi_{\alpha}(\mathbf{x}_{\alpha}\mathbf{y}_{\alpha}^{T}+\mathbf{y}_{\alpha}\mathbf{x}_{\alpha}^{T}),

where

(i)
{(\xi_{\alpha})}_{\alpha}
are iid copies of a 0/1 random variable
\xi=\xi_{n}
with
\mathbf{P}(\xi=1)=p_{n}
,

(ii)
\frac{m}{n}p_{n}\to c_{1}>0
as
n\to\infty
(without loss of generality we assume that
\frac{m}{n}p_{n}\equiv c_{1}
),

(iii)
(\mathbf{x}_{\alpha})_{\alpha},\,(\mathbf{y}_{\alpha})_{\alpha}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
are two sets of mutually independent random vectors such that

\|\mathbf{x}_{\alpha}\|_{2}^{2}\leq C_{0}
,
\|\mathbf{y}_{\alpha}\|^{2}_{2}\leq C_{0}

for some
C_{0}>0
and for all deterministic matrices
D=D_{n}
with
\|D\|_{op}=1
we have (cf (
1.8
))

\sup_{\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}\in(\mathbf{x}_{\alpha},\mathbf{y}_{\alpha})_{\alpha}}\mathbf{Var}(D\,\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v})={o}(1),\quad n\rightarrow\infty.

(iv) matrices
Q^{x\alpha}:=E\mathbf{x}_{\alpha}\mathbf{x}_{\alpha}^{T}
,
Q^{y\alpha}:=E\mathbf{y}_{\alpha}\mathbf{y}_{\alpha}^{T}
, and
Q^{xy\alpha}:=E\mathbf{x}_{\alpha}\mathbf{y}_{\alpha}^{T}=Q^{yx\alpha T}
have the operator norms of order
O(n^{-1})
and

\sup_{Q_{\alpha}\in(Q^{x\alpha},Q^{y\alpha})_{\alpha}}|\Tr Q_{\alpha}-1|=o(1),

(v) for every
n\times n
matrices
K_{1}
,
K_{2}
we have

\frac{1}{m}\sum_{\alpha}\Tr Q^{x\alpha}K_{1}\Tr Q^{y\alpha}K_{2}=\frac{1}{n}\Tr K_{1}\frac{1}{n}\Tr K_{2},

(vi) matrix
Q^{xy}:=(\frac{1}{m}\sum_{\alpha}|Q_{ij}^{xy\alpha}|)_{i,j}
satisfies
\|Q^{xy}\|_{HS}=o(n^{-1/2})
.

Then as
n\to\infty
the empirical spectral distributions
N_{\mathcal{A}_{n}}
converge in probability to a non-random probability measure
N_{\mathcal{A}}
which Stieltjes transform
f
is uniquely determined by the equation

zf^{3}+(1-2c_{1})f^{2}-zf-1=0

(4.1)

in the class of Stieltjes transforms of non-negative measures.

Remark 4.2
.

A more general case corresponding to
\xi_{\alpha}
satisfying Assumption 2 contains more pure technical details and we do not treat it here, but we strongly believe that following essentially the same scheme one can prove that in this case
f
solves the equation

zf=-1-2f^{2}\int\frac{\xi d\widetilde{\sigma}(\xi)}{1-\xi^{2}f^{2}}.

Proof.

Following the scheme of the proof of Theorem
1.2
note first that the proof of the analog of Lemma
2.1
is trivial in this case and the proof of the analog of Lemma
2.2
is essentially the same. Thus we only need to prove the convergence in mean (cf Lemma
2.3
). To this end introduce

\mathcal{A}_{n}^{\alpha}:=\mathcal{A}_{n}-\xi_{\alpha}(\mathbf{x}_{\alpha}\mathbf{y}_{\alpha}^{T}+\mathbf{y}_{\alpha}\mathbf{x}_{\alpha}^{T})\quad\text{and}\quad G^{\alpha}(z):=(\mathcal{A}_{n}^{\alpha}-zI_{n})^{-1},

where

z\in\mathbb{C}_{\eta_{0}}
for a big enough
\eta_{0}
.
Given an
n\times n
symmetric matrix
K
, applying twice (
2.7
) we get

(KG\mathbf{x}_{\alpha},\mathbf{y}_{\alpha})=\frac{(KG^{\alpha}\mathbf{x}_{\alpha},\mathbf{y}_{\alpha})(1+\xi_{\alpha}(G^{\alpha}\mathbf{x}_{\alpha},\mathbf{y}_{\alpha}))-\xi_{\alpha}^{2}(KG^{\alpha}\mathbf{y}_{\alpha},\mathbf{y}_{\alpha})(G^{\alpha}\mathbf{x}_{\alpha},\mathbf{x}_{\alpha})}{(1+\xi_{\alpha}(G^{\alpha}\mathbf{x}_{\alpha},\mathbf{y}_{\alpha}))^{2}-\xi_{\alpha}^{2}(G^{\alpha}\mathbf{y}_{\alpha},\mathbf{y}_{\alpha})(G^{\alpha}\mathbf{x}_{\alpha},\mathbf{x}_{\alpha})}.

(4.2)

It follows from the resolvent identity, (ii) and (
4.2
) with
K=I
, that

\displaystyle zf_{n}(z)+1

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{n}\sum_{\alpha}\mathbf{E}\xi_{\alpha}(G\mathbf{x}_{\alpha},\mathbf{y}_{\alpha})

\displaystyle=\frac{2c_{1}}{m}\sum_{\alpha}\mathbf{E}\frac{(G^{\alpha}\mathbf{x}_{\alpha},\mathbf{y}_{\alpha})(1+(G^{\alpha}\mathbf{x}_{\alpha},\mathbf{y}_{\alpha}))-(G^{\alpha}\mathbf{y}_{\alpha},\mathbf{y}_{\alpha})(G^{\alpha}\mathbf{x}_{\alpha},\mathbf{x}_{\alpha})}{(1+(G^{\alpha}\mathbf{x}_{\alpha},\mathbf{y}_{\alpha}))^{2}-(G^{\alpha}\mathbf{y}_{\alpha},\mathbf{y}_{\alpha})(G^{\alpha}\mathbf{x}_{\alpha},\mathbf{x}_{\alpha})}

\displaystyle=2c_{1}-\frac{2c_{1}}{m}\sum_{\alpha}\mathbf{E}\frac{1+(G^{\alpha}\mathbf{x}_{\alpha},\mathbf{y}_{\alpha})}{A_{\alpha}}

\displaystyle=2c_{1}-\frac{2c_{1}}{m}\sum_{\alpha}\frac{1+\mathbf{E}\Tr Q^{xy\alpha}G^{\alpha}}{\mathbf{E}A_{\alpha}}+R_{n},

(4.3)

where

\displaystyle A_{\alpha}

\displaystyle=(1+(G^{\alpha}\mathbf{x}_{\alpha},\mathbf{y}_{\alpha}))^{2}-(G^{\alpha}\mathbf{y}_{\alpha},\mathbf{y}_{\alpha})(G^{\alpha}\mathbf{x}_{\alpha},\mathbf{x}_{\alpha}),

\displaystyle R_{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{2c_{1}}{m}\sum_{\alpha}\frac{1}{\mathbf{E}A_{\alpha}}\mathbf{E}\frac{(1+(G^{\alpha}\mathbf{x}_{\alpha},\mathbf{y}_{\alpha}))A_{\alpha}^{\circ}}{A_{\alpha}}.

Applying (iii) and an analog of Lemma
3.1
(ii), one can show that

\displaystyle|A_{\alpha}|\geq\frac{1}{2}-3C_{0}/\eta_{0}^{2}>0,

\displaystyle\mathbf{Var}A_{\alpha}={o}(1),\ \text{(see also (\ref{VGyy})) and}

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}A_{\alpha}=(1+\mathbf{E}\Tr Q^{xy\alpha}G^{\alpha})^{2}-\mathbf{E}\Tr Q^{x\alpha}G^{\alpha}\mathbf{E}\Tr Q^{y\alpha}G^{\alpha}+o(1),

where with the help of (iv)
G^{\alpha}
can be replaced with
G
with an error term of order
O(n^{-1})
(cf (
2.10
)) so that

\mathbf{E}A_{\alpha}=(1+\mathbf{E}\Tr Q^{xy\alpha}G)^{2}-f_{n}^{x\alpha}f_{n}^{y\alpha}+o(1),

and we use notations

f_{n}^{x\alpha}=\mathbf{E}\Tr Q^{x\alpha}G,\quad f_{n}^{y\alpha}=\mathbf{E}\Tr Q^{y\alpha}G.

By (iv) and (vi),
|\Tr Q^{xy\alpha}G|=O(1)
and

\frac{1}{m}\sum_{\alpha}|\Tr Q^{xy\alpha}G|\leq\sum_{i,j}Q_{ij}^{xy}|G_{ij}|\leq\|Q^{xy}\|_{HS}\|G\|_{HS}=o(1).

(4.4)

Hence
R_{n}={o}(1)
and

\displaystyle zf_{n}+1

\displaystyle=2c_{1}-\frac{2c_{1}}{m}\sum_{\alpha}\frac{1}{1-f_{n}^{x\alpha}f_{n}^{y\alpha}}+o(1).

It follows from (v) that
{m}^{-1}\sum_{\alpha}f_{n}^{x\alpha}f_{n}^{y\alpha}=f_{n}^{2}
, hence,

\displaystyle zf_{n}+1=2c_{1}-\frac{2c_{1}}{1-f_{n}^{2}}+R^{\prime}_{n}+o(1),

(4.5)

where

\displaystyle R^{\prime}_{n}=-\frac{1}{1-f_{n}^{2}}\frac{2c_{1}}{m}\sum_{\alpha}\frac{f_{n}^{x\alpha}f_{n}^{y\alpha}-f_{n}^{2}}{1-f_{n}^{x\alpha}f_{n}^{y\alpha}},

(4.6)

\displaystyle|R^{\prime}_{n}|\leq\frac{2c_{1}}{\eta_{0}(1-\eta_{0}^{-2})^{2}}\Delta_{n},\quad\Delta_{n}:=\max_{\alpha}(|f_{n}^{x\alpha}-f_{n}|+|f_{n}^{y\alpha}-f_{n}|).

It remains to show that
\Delta_{n}=o(1)
. To this end we treat similarly
f_{n}^{x\alpha}
(and
f_{n}^{y\alpha}
) and applying (
4.2
) with
K=Q^{x\alpha}
we get

\displaystyle zf_{n}^{x\alpha}+\Tr Q^{x\alpha}

\displaystyle=\frac{2c_{1}}{m}\sum_{\beta}\mathbf{E}\frac{(nQ^{x\alpha}G^{\beta}\mathbf{x}_{\beta},\mathbf{y}_{\beta})(1+(G^{\beta}\mathbf{x}_{\beta},\mathbf{y}_{\beta}))-(nQ^{x\alpha}G^{\beta}\mathbf{y}_{\beta},\mathbf{y}_{\beta})(G^{\beta}\mathbf{x}_{\beta},\mathbf{x}_{\beta})}{A_{\beta}}.

Note that
\|nQ^{x\alpha}\|_{op}=O(1)
, hence repeating steps leading to (
4.5
) – (
4.6
) and applying (v), one can get

\displaystyle zf_{n}^{x\alpha}+\Tr Q^{x\alpha}

\displaystyle=-\frac{2c_{1}}{m}\sum_{\beta}\frac{\mathbf{E}n\Tr Q^{x\alpha}Q^{y\beta}G\mathbf{E}\Tr Q^{x\beta}G}{1-f_{n}^{x\beta}f_{n}^{y\beta}}+o(1)

\displaystyle=-2c_{1}\frac{f_{n}^{x\alpha}f_{n}}{1-f_{n}^{2}}+R^{\prime}_{n,\alpha}+o(1),

(4.7)

where

\displaystyle R^{\prime}_{n,\alpha}=-\frac{1}{1-f_{n}^{2}}\frac{2c_{1}}{m}\sum_{\beta}\frac{f_{n}^{x\beta}\mathbf{E}n\Tr Q^{x\alpha}Q^{y\beta}G(f_{n}^{x\alpha}f_{n}^{y\alpha}-f_{n}^{2})}{1-f_{n}^{x\beta}f_{n}^{y\beta}},

\displaystyle|R^{\prime}_{n,\alpha}|\leq\frac{2c_{1}C_{0}^{2}}{(1-\eta_{0}^{-2})^{2}}\Delta_{n}.

Now subtracting (
4.7
) from (
4.5
) and using (iv) one can show that
\Delta_{n}=o(1)
(cf(
2.17
)). Thus

zf_{n}+1=-\frac{2c_{1}f_{n}^{2}}{1-f_{n}^{2}}+o(1),

which leads to (
4.1
) and finishes the proof of Theorem
4.1
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.5
.
Now we have

\mathcal{A}_{rd}=\sum_{1\leq k\neq\ell\leq r}\xi_{k\ell}X^{k\ell}X^{\ell k\,T},\quad X^{k\ell}=(\delta_{jk}\mathbf{v}^{k\ell})_{j=1}^{n},

(4.8)

so that in terms of Theorem
1.4

\sum_{\alpha}=\sum_{1\leq k<\ell\leq r},\quad m=r(r-1)/2,\,\,n=rd,\,\,c_{1}=c/2,\quad\mathbf{x}_{\alpha}=X^{k\ell},\,\mathbf{y}_{\alpha}=X^{\ell k},\,k<\ell,

and the analogs of
Q^{x\alpha}
and
Q^{xy\alpha}
are given by

\displaystyle Q^{kk}:=\mathbf{E}X^{k\ell}X^{k\ell T}=d^{-1}(\delta_{ik}\delta_{jk}I_{d})_{i_{j}=1}^{r},\,\,\Tr Q^{kk}=1,\,\,\text{and}

\displaystyle Q^{k\ell}:=\mathbf{E}X^{k\ell}X^{\ell kT}=d^{-1}(\delta_{ik}\delta_{j\ell}I_{d})_{i_{j}=1}^{r}.

We suppose that
(\mathbf{v}^{k\ell})_{k<\ell}
have uniformly bounded norms, so let
C_{0}>0
be such that
\|\mathbf{v}^{kl}\|^{2}_{2}\leq C_{0}
for every
k<\ell
.

Checking the conditions of Theorem
4.1
, note first that (iii) follows from the definition of
X^{k\ell}
and conditions for
\mathbf{v}^{k\ell}
. As to (v-vi), these conditions are not fulfilled with
\sum_{\alpha}=\sum_{k<\ell}
, but since in (
4.8
) we have
\sum_{k\neq\ell}
, by the definitions of
Q^{k\ell}
we get the following analogs of (v-vi):

\displaystyle\text{(v')}\quad\frac{1}{r}\sum_{k}\Tr Q^{kk}K=\frac{1}{rd}\Tr K\quad\text{and}

\displaystyle\text{(vi')}\quad\|\widetilde{Q}^{xy}\|_{HS}=O\big((r\sqrt{d})^{-1}\big),\quad\text{where}\quad\widetilde{Q}^{xy}_{i\gamma,j\beta}:=\frac{1}{r^{2}}\sum_{k,\ell}|Q^{k\ell}_{i\gamma,j\beta}|=\frac{1}{dr^{2}}\delta_{\gamma\beta}.

Thus again the only condition which is not fulfilled is the first part of (iv), because now
\|Q^{kk}\|_{op}
,
\|Q^{k\ell}\|_{op}=O(d^{-1})
(instead of
O((rd)^{-1})
). On the other hand, matrices
(Q^{kk})_{k}
are “orthogonal” up to normalisation:

Q^{kk}Q^{p\ell}=\frac{1}{d}\delta_{kp}Q^{k\ell},\quad\text{and also}\quad Q^{kk}X^{p\ell}=\frac{1}{d}\delta_{kp}X^{k\ell},

(4.9)

thus in the corresponding places of the proof we have single sums instead of double sums. This allows to repeat the proof of Theorem
4.1
with slight modifications and to get first

\displaystyle zf_{r}+1=\frac{1}{rd}\sum_{k\neq\ell}\mathbf{E}\xi_{k\ell}(GX^{k\ell},X^{\ell k})=c-\frac{c}{r^{2}}\sum_{k\neq\ell}\mathbf{E}\frac{1+(G^{k\ell}X^{k\ell},X^{\ell k})}{A_{k\ell}},

(4.10)

where

A_{k\ell}=(1+(G^{k\ell}X^{k\ell},X^{\ell k}))^{2}-(G^{k\ell}X^{k\ell},X^{k\ell})(G^{k\ell}X^{\ell k},X^{\ell k}).

Since for any matrix
B
,
|(B\mathbf{v}^{k\ell},\mathbf{v}^{k\ell})|\leq C_{0}\|B\|_{op}
, we have

\mathbf{Var}A_{k\ell}\leq C(\eta_{0})\max_{k,\ell}\{\mathbf{Var}(G^{k\ell}X^{k\ell},X^{\ell k}),\mathbf{Var}(G^{k\ell}X^{k\ell},X^{k\ell})\},

(4.11)

where we use notation
C(\eta_{0})
for every positive function uniformly bounded in
\eta_{0}\to\infty
,

C(\eta_{0})=O(1),\quad\eta_{0}\to\infty.

It follows from Assumption 1 and Lemma
4.3
below that

\displaystyle\mathbf{Var}(G^{k\ell}X^{k\ell},X^{\ell k})=\mathbf{E}\mathbf{Var}_{k\ell}(G^{k\ell}X^{k\ell},X^{\ell k})+\mathbf{Var}\Tr Q^{k\ell}G^{k\ell}=o(1),\quad\text{and}

\displaystyle\mathbf{Var}(G^{k\ell}X^{k\ell},X^{k\ell})=\mathbf{E}\mathbf{Var}_{k\ell}(G^{k\ell}X^{k\ell},X^{k\ell})+\mathbf{Var}\Tr Q^{kk}G^{k\ell}=o(1),

(4.12)

(cf (
4.11
)), hence,
\mathbf{Var}A_{k\ell}=o(1)
,
r\to\infty
.
Also similar to (
4.4
) one can show that the terms containing
\mathbf{E}(G^{k\ell}X^{k\ell},X^{\ell k})=\Tr Q^{k\ell}G^{k\ell}
do not contribute to the limit. It follows from above that

\displaystyle zf_{r}+1

\displaystyle=c-\frac{c}{r^{2}}\sum_{k,\ell}\frac{1}{1-f_{r}^{kk}f_{r}^{\ell\ell}}+o(1)

\displaystyle=c-\frac{c}{1-f_{r}^{2}}+R^{\prime}_{r}+o(1),

(4.13)

where

f_{r}^{kk}=\mathbf{E}\Tr Q^{kk}G=d^{-1}\sum_{\gamma}\mathbf{E}G_{k\gamma,k\gamma},\quad\frac{1}{r}\sum_{k}f_{r}^{kk}=f_{r},

and

\displaystyle R^{\prime}_{r}=-\frac{c}{1-f_{r}^{2}}\frac{1}{r^{2}}\sum_{k,\ell}\frac{f_{r}^{kk}f_{r}^{\ell\ell}-f_{r}^{2}}{1-f_{r}^{kk}f_{r}^{\ell\ell}},\quad|R^{\prime}_{r}|\leq C(\eta_{0})\Delta_{r},\quad\Delta_{r}=\max_{\ell}|f_{r}-f_{r}^{\ell\ell}|.

Using (
4.9
) – (
4.10
), similar to (
4.7
) one can get for every
q\leq r

\displaystyle zf^{qq}_{r}+1

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\neq\ell}\mathbf{E}\xi_{k\ell}(Q^{qq}GX^{k\ell},X^{\ell k})=\frac{1}{d}\sum_{k\neq q}\mathbf{E}\xi_{kq}(GX^{kq},X^{qk})

(4.14)

\displaystyle=c-\frac{c}{r}\sum_{k\neq q}\frac{1}{1-f_{r}^{kk}f_{r}^{qq}}+o(1)=c-\frac{c}{1-f_{r}^{qq}f_{r}}+R^{q}_{r}+o(1),

where
|R^{q}_{r}|\leq C(\eta_{0})\Delta_{r}
. Hence,

\displaystyle z(f_{r}-f^{qq}_{r})=\frac{cf_{r}(f^{qq}_{r}-f_{r})}{(1-f_{r}^{qq}f_{r})(1-f_{r}^{2})}+R^{\prime}_{r}-R^{q}_{r}+o(1),

and choosing
\eta_{0}
big enough we get similar to (
2.17
)
\Delta^{\prime}_{r}=o(1)
, which leads to (
2.11
). To finish the proof of Theorem
1.4
, it remains to prove the following statement, which is an analog of Lemma
3.1
(see also Lemma
2.2
).

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
V_{r}:=\max_{1\leq k,\ell\leq r}\mathbf{Var}\Tr Q^{k\ell}G
. Then
V_{r}=o(1)
as
r\to\infty
.

Proof.

Note that the simple trick based on the resolvent identity, which we have used in the last line of the proof of Lemma
3.1
to get reed of the double sum over
i,j
, does not work here. So we will go another way.

For every
q\leq r
, it follows from (
4.14
) that

\displaystyle z\mathbf{Var}\Tr Q^{qq}G=

\displaystyle z\mathbf{E}\Tr Q^{qq}G(\Tr Q^{qq}\overline{G})^{\circ}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{d}\sum_{k\neq q}\mathbf{E}\xi_{kq}(GX^{kq},X^{qk})(\Tr Q^{qq}\overline{G}^{kq})^{\circ}+R_{r},

(4.15)

where
x^{\circ}=x-\mathbf{E}x
and

R_{r}=\frac{1}{d}\sum_{k\neq q}\mathbf{E}\xi_{kq}(GX^{kq},X^{qk})(\Tr Q^{qq}(\overline{G}-\overline{G}^{kq}))^{\circ}.

We have

|\Tr Q^{qq}(\overline{G}-\overline{G}^{kq})|=\xi_{kq}|(Q^{qq}G^{kq}X^{kq},\overline{G}X^{qk})+(Q^{qq}G^{kq}X^{qk},\overline{G}X^{kq})|\leq 2\|Q^{qq}\|_{op}\|\mathbf{v}^{qk}\|_{2}^{2}/\eta_{0}^{2}=O(d^{-1}),

hence,
R_{r}=O(d^{-1})
. Also, we have

\mathbf{Var}\Tr Q^{qq}G=\mathbf{Var}\Tr Q^{qq}G^{kq}+O(d^{-1}).

Applying (
4.2
), one can continue (
4.15
) and get similar to (
4.10
)

\displaystyle z\mathbf{Var}\Tr Q^{qq}G

\displaystyle=-\frac{c}{r}\sum_{k\neq q}\mathbf{E}\frac{(1+(G^{kq}X^{kq},X^{qk}))(\Tr Q^{qq}\overline{G}^{kq})^{\circ}}{A_{kq}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{c}{r}\sum_{k\neq q}\frac{\mathbf{E}(G^{kq}X^{kq},X^{qk})(\Tr Q^{qq}\overline{G}^{kq})^{\circ}}{\mathbf{E}A_{kq}}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{c}{r}\sum_{k\neq q}\frac{1}{\mathbf{E}A_{kq}}\mathbf{E}\frac{(1+(G^{kq}X^{kq},X^{qk}))(\Tr Q^{qq}\overline{G}^{kq})^{\circ}A_{kq}^{\circ}}{A_{kq}}=:T^{(1)}_{r}+T^{(2)}_{r}.

Since by the Schwartz inequality

\displaystyle|\mathbf{E}(G^{kq}X^{kq},X^{qk})(\Tr Q^{qq}\overline{G}^{kq})^{\circ}|

\displaystyle=|\mathbf{E}\Tr Q^{kq}{G}^{kq}(\Tr Q^{qq}\overline{G}^{kq})^{\circ}|

\displaystyle\leq(\mathbf{Var}\Tr Q^{kq}{G}^{kq})^{1/2}(\mathbf{Var}\Tr Q^{qq}{G}^{kq})^{1/2}\leq V_{r}+O(d^{-1}),

we have
|T^{(1)}_{r}|\leq C(\eta_{0})V_{r}+O(d^{-1})
. It follows from (
4.11
) – (
4
) that

\mathbf{Var}A_{kq}\leq V_{r}+o(1)
. This and the Schwartz inequality allows to get

|T^{(2)}_{r}|\leq C(\eta_{0})V_{r}^{1/2}(\max_{k,q}\mathbf{Var}A_{kq})^{1/2}\leq C(\eta_{0})V_{r}+o(1).

Summarising we get from for every
q\leq r

\eta_{0}\mathbf{Var}\Tr Q^{qq}G\leq C(\eta_{0})V_{r}+o(1).

Similar, one can show that
\eta_{0}\mathbf{Var}\Tr Q^{qk}G\leq C(\eta_{0})V_{r}+o(1)
for every
k,q
. Hence, taking maximum over
k,q
, we get

\eta_{0}V_{r}\leq C(\eta_{0})V_{r}+o(1)
, where
C(\eta_{0})
remains bounded as
\eta_{0}\to\infty
. Thus choosing
\eta_{0}
big enough we get

V_{r}=o(1)
as
r\to\infty
. This finishes the proof of the lemma and the proof of Theorem
1.5
.
∎

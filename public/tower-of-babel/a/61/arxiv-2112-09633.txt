A super Littlewood–Richardson type rule
Nohra Hage

Abstract




1. Introduction

The super algebraic structures have been used
as combinatorial tools in the study of the invariant theory and the representation
theory of superalgebras, and algebras satisfying identities, and have played an important role in various areas of theoretical and mathematical physics,
[
2
,
3
,
4
,
9
]
.

We are interested on the combinatorial study of super Young tableaux over signed alphabets of any order. These objects form a set of representatives of elements of the super plactic monoid, which is defined independently in
[
1
,
12
,
14
]
as a generalization of the classical plactic monoid over signed alphabets.
The plactic monoid has been originally introduced in
[
16
,
11
,
13
]
, and has found several applications in representation theory, algebraic and probabilistic combinatorics, and rewriting theory,
[
13
,
5
,
8
]
.
Super versions of the Robinson–Schensted–Knuth correspondence and the Littlewood–Richardson rule have been also introduced over signed alphabets,
[
2
,
3
,
10
,
12
,
15
,
7
,
6
]
.
We introduce in this paper a super Robinson–Schensted correspondence over signed alphabets and we present a super version of the Littlewood–Richardson rule in terms of the classical Littlewood–Richardson tableaux.

2. A super Robinson–Schensted type correspondence

We recall the notion of super tableaux from
[
12
,
7
,
6
]
, and we introduce a super Robinson–Schensted correspondence over signed alphabets.

Denote
[n]:=\{1<\ldots<n\}
, for
n>0
.
A
signed alphabet
is a pair
(\Si,||.||)
where
\Si
is a finite ordered set and
||.||
is any map from
\Si
to the additive cyclic group
\mathbb{Z}_{2}=\{0,1\}
of order
2
. Denote
\Si_{0}
(resp.
\Si_{1}
) the set of
x
in
\Si
such that
||x||=0
(resp.
||x||=1
).
A monoid
\mathbf{M}
is a
supermonoid
if a map
||.||:\mathbf{M}\to\mathbb{Z}_{2}
is given such that
||uv||=||u||+||v||
, for all
u,v
in
\mathbf{M}
. Denote
\Si^{\ast}
the free monoid of words over
\Si
.
A
partition
of
n>0
of
height

k
is a weakly decreasing
sequence
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{k})\in\mathbb{N}^{k}
such that
\sum\lambda_{i}=n
.
Denote
\mathcal{P}_{n}
the set of partitions of
n
and set
P=\bigcup\mathcal{P}_{n}
.
The
Young diagram

of a partition
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{k})
, denoted by
\mathcal{Y}(\lambda)
, is the set of pairs
(i,j)
such that
1\leqslant i\leqslant k
and
1\leqslant j\leqslant\lambda_{i}
, which is represented by a diagram by drawing a box for each of its pairs.
A
super tableau of shape

\lambda
over
\Si
, is a
pair
t:=\penalty\ (\lambda,\mathcal{T})
, where
\mathcal{T}:\mathcal{Y}(\lambda)\to\Si
is a map satisfying:

C1)

\mathcal{T}(i,j)\leqslant\penalty\ \mathcal{T}(i,j+1)
with
\mathcal{T}(i,j)=\penalty\ \mathcal{T}(i,j+1)
only if
\mathcal{T}(i,j)\in\Si_{0}
,

C2)

\mathcal{T}(i,j)\leqslant\mathcal{T}(i+1,j)
with
\mathcal{T}(i,j)=\mathcal{T}(i+1,j)
only if
\mathcal{T}(i,j)\in\Si_{1}
.

The set
\mathcal{Y}(\lambda)
(resp. map
\mathcal{T}
) is called the
frame
(resp.
filling
) of
t
.
When
\mathcal{T}
is injective,
t
is called a
standard tableau
over
\Si
.
Denote by
\YoungST(\Si)
the set of all standard tableaux over
\Si
,
and by
\YoungT(\Si)
(resp.
\YoungT(\Si,\lambda)
) the set of all super tableaux (resp. of shape
\lambda
) over
\Si
.
An
empty tableau of shape

\lambda
is a super tableau of shape
\lambda
where all its boxes are empty. If there is no confusion, suppose that all empty tableaux are equal and denote by
\emptyset
the corresponding empty tableau.
Let
\lambda,\mu\in\mathcal{P}
of heights
k
and
l
, respectively, such that
l\leqslant k
. Denote by
\mu\subseteq\lambda
if
\mu_{i}\leqslant\lambda_{i}
, for any
i
, and by
\mathcal{Y}(\lambda/\mu)
the set of pairs
(i,j)
such that
1\leqslant i\leqslant k
and
\mu_{i}<j\leqslant\penalty\ \lambda_{i}
.
A
super skew
tableau
of
shape

\lambda/\mu
over
\Si
, is a pair
S:=(\lambda/\mu,\U)
where
\U:\mathcal{Y}(\lambda/\mu)\to\Si
is a map satisfying
{\bf C1)}
and
{\bf C2)}
.
The set
\mathcal{Y}(\lambda/\mu)
(resp. map
\U
) is called the
frame
(resp.
filling
) of
S
.
We call
\lambda/\mu
a
skew shape
and set
\lambda/0:=\lambda
.
Denote by
\SkewT(\Si)
(resp.
\SkewT(\Si,\lambda/\mu)
) the set of all super skew tableaux (resp. of shape
\lambda/\mu
) over
\Si
.
An
inner corner
of
S\in\SkewT(\Si,\lambda/\mu)
is a box in
\mathcal{Y}(\mu)
such that the
boxes below and to the right are not in
\mathcal{Y}(\mu)
, and
an
outer corner
is a box such that neither box
below or to the right is in
\mathcal{Y}(\lambda/\mu)
.
Denote by

\mathbf{r}:\SkewT(\Si)\to\Si^{\ast}\quad\big(\text{resp}.\penalty\ \mathbf{c}:\SkewT(\Si)\to\Si^{\ast}\big)

the
row
(resp.
column
)
reading
that reads a tableau row (resp. column)-wise from bottom to top and from left to right.

The
column insertion
, denoted by
\rightsquigarrow
, inserts an element
x
in
\Si
into a super tableau
t
of
\YoungT(\Si)
as follows,
[
12
]
.
If
t
is empty, create a box and label it
x
.
Suppose
t
is non-empty. If
x\in\Si_{0}
(resp.
x\in\Si_{1}
) is larger than (resp. at least as large as) the bottom element of the leftmost column of
t
, then put
x
in a box to the bottom of this column; Otherwise, let
y
be the smallest element of this column such that
y\geqslant x
(resp.
y>x
). Then replace
y
by
x
and recursively insert
y
into the super tableau formed by the columns of
t
to the right of the leftmost.
Note that this recursion may end with an insertion into an empty column to the right of the
existing columns of
t
. Output the resulting super tableau as
(x\rightsquigarrow t)
.
One defines in a similar way the
row insertion
, denoted by

\rightsquigarrow

, that inserts an element in
\Si
into the rows of a super tableau starting from its topmost one,
[
12
]
.
Define

\mathbf{T}(w):=(x_{1}\rightsquigarrow(\ldots\rightsquigarrow(x_{k}\rightsquigarrow\emptyset)\ldots)),

for any
w=x_{1}\ldots x_{k}
in
\Si^{\ast}
.

The
super plactic monoid
over
\Si
, denoted by
\mathbf{P}(\Si)
, is the quotient of
\Si^{\ast}

by the family of
super Knuth relations
,
[
1
,
12
,
14
]
:

\begin{array}[]{rl}xzy=zxy,&\text{ with }x=y\text{ only if }||y||=0\;\text{ and }y=z\text{ only if }||y||=1,\\
yxz=yzx,&\text{ with }x=y\text{ only if }||y||=1\;\text{ and }y=z\text{ only if }||y||=0,\end{array}

for any
x\leqslant y\leqslant z\in\Si
.
Denote by
\sim_{\mathbf{P}(\Si)}
the super plactic congruence.
The set
\YoungT(\Si)
satisfies the
cross-section property
for
\sim_{\mathbf{P}(\Si)}
:
w\sim_{\mathbf{P}(\Si)}w^{\prime}
if and only if
\mathbf{T}(w)=\mathbf{T}(w^{\prime})
, for all
w,w^{\prime}
in
\Si^{\ast}
,
[
12
]
.
In particular, we have

(y\rightsquigarrow(t\;\raisebox{1.00006pt}{{\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{$\rightsquigarrow$}}}\;x))=((y\rightsquigarrow t)\;\raisebox{1.00006pt}{{\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{$\rightsquigarrow$}}}\;x),

for all
t
in
\YoungT(\Si)
and
x,y
in
\Si
. Moreover, we have
\mathbf{T}(w)=((\ldots(\emptyset\;\raisebox{1.00006pt}{{\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{$\rightsquigarrow$}}}\;x_{1})\ldots)\;\raisebox{1.00006pt}{{\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{$\rightsquigarrow$}}}\;x_{k})
, for any
w=x_{1}\ldots x_{k}
in
\Si^{\ast}
.

Define the following products on
\YoungT(\Si)
:

\begin{array}[]{rl}(t\;\raisebox{1.00006pt}{{\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{$\rightsquigarrow$}}}_{r}\;t^{\prime})=(t\;\raisebox{1.00006pt}{{\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{$\rightsquigarrow$}}}\;\mathbf{r}(t^{\prime}))&\quad\big(\text{resp. }(t^{\prime}\rightsquigarrow_{r}t)=(\mathbf{r}(t^{\prime})\rightsquigarrow t)\big),\\
(t\;\raisebox{1.00006pt}{{\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{$\rightsquigarrow$}}}_{c}\;t^{\prime})=(t\;\raisebox{1.00006pt}{{\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{$\rightsquigarrow$}}}\;\mathbf{c}(t^{\prime}))&\quad\big(\text{resp. }(t^{\prime}\rightsquigarrow_{c}t)=(\mathbf{c}(t^{\prime})\rightsquigarrow t)\big),\end{array}

for all
t,t^{\prime}
in
\YoungT(\Si)
.
By the cross-section property, we have

(t\;\raisebox{1.00006pt}{{\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{$\rightsquigarrow$}}}_{r}\;t^{\prime})=(t\rightsquigarrow_{r}t^{\prime})=(t\;\raisebox{1.00006pt}{{\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{$\rightsquigarrow$}}}_{c}\;t^{\prime})=(t\rightsquigarrow_{c}t^{\prime}),

((t\;\raisebox{1.00006pt}{{\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{$\rightsquigarrow$}}}_{r}\;t^{\prime})\;\raisebox{1.00006pt}{{\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{$\rightsquigarrow$}}}_{r}\;t^{\prime\prime})=(t\;\raisebox{1.00006pt}{{\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{$\rightsquigarrow$}}}_{r}\;(t^{\prime}\rightsquigarrow_{r}t^{\prime\prime}))=((t\rightsquigarrow_{r}t^{\prime})\rightsquigarrow_{r}t^{\prime\prime}),

and

((t\;\raisebox{1.00006pt}{{\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{$\rightsquigarrow$}}}_{c}\;t^{\prime})\;\raisebox{1.00006pt}{{\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{$\rightsquigarrow$}}}_{c}\;t^{\prime\prime})=(t\;\raisebox{1.00006pt}{{\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{$\rightsquigarrow$}}}_{c}\;(t^{\prime}\;\raisebox{1.00006pt}{{\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{$\rightsquigarrow$}}}_{c}\;t^{\prime\prime}))=((t\rightsquigarrow_{c}t^{\prime})\rightsquigarrow_{c}t^{\prime\prime}),

for all
t,t^{\prime},t^{\prime\prime}
in
\YoungT(\Si)
.

Starting from a word
w=x_{1}\ldots x_{k}
over
\Si
, we compute a pair of same shape tableaux
(\mathbf{T}(w),\mathbf{Q}(w))\in\YoungT(\Si)\times\YoungST([n])
, as follows.
Start with empty super tableaux
\mathbf{T}_{k+1}
and
\mathbf{Q}_{k+1}
. For each
i=k,\ldots,1
, compute
x_{i}\rightsquigarrow\mathbf{T}_{i+1}
; let
\mathbf{T}_{i}
be the resulting super tableau. Add a
box filled with (
k-i+1
) to
\mathbf{Q}_{i+1}
in the same place as the box that belongs to
\mathbf{T}_{i}
but not
to
\mathbf{T}_{i+1}
; let
\mathbf{Q}_{i}
be the resulting standard tableau. Output
\mathbf{T}_{1}
for
\mathbf{T}(w)
and
\mathbf{Q}_{1}
as
\mathbf{Q}(w)
.

Let
t
be in
\YoungT(\Si)
and
x
in
\Si
.
Starting from
(x\rightsquigarrow t)
, together with the outer corner that has been added to the frame of
t
after insertion, we recover
t
and
x
as follows.
Suppose that
y\in\Si_{1}
(resp.
y\in\Si_{0}
) is the entry in the outer corner of
t
. Find in the column to the right of this outer corner the largest entry which is strictly smaller (resp. smaller)
than
y
. Then this entry is replaced by
y
and it is bumped right to the next column where the process is repeated until an entry is bumped out of the leftmost column. Output the resulting super tableau for
t
and the last element which is bumped out for
x
.

Starting from a pair of same shape tableaux
(\mathbf{T},\mathbf{Q})\in\YoungT(\Si)\times\YoungST([n])
consiting of
k
boxes, we compute a word
w\in\Si^{\ast}
as follows.
Start with
\mathbf{T}_{k}=\mathbf{T}
and
\mathbf{Q}_{k}=\mathbf{Q}
. For each
i=k-1,\ldots,1
, take the box filled with
i+1
in
\mathbf{Q}_{i+1}
, and apply the reverse insertion algorithm to
\mathbf{T}_{i+1}
with the outer corner corresponding to that box. The
resulting tableau is
\mathbf{T}_{i}
, and the element that is bumped out of the leftmost column of
\mathbf{T}_{i+1}
is denoted by
x_{i+1}
.
Remove the box containing
i+1
from
\mathbf{Q}_{i+1}
and denote the resulting tableau by
\mathbf{Q}_{i}
. Output the word
x_{1}\ldots x_{k}
as
w
.

As a consequence, we deduce the following result:

Theorem 2.1
.

The map
w\mapsto(\mathbf{T}(w),\mathbf{Q}(w))
defines a bijection between words over
\Si
and
pairs of same-shape tableaux in
\YoungT(\Si)\times\YoungST([n])
.

3. A super Littlewood–Richardson type rule

Following the approach developed in
[
17
]
for the non-signed case, we introduce a super version of the Littlewood–Richardson rule over signed alphabets.

Consider
\lambda,\mu,\nu\in\mathcal{P}
such that the partition
\lambda\subseteq\nu
is of height
h
. Consider
t\in\YoungT(\Si,\lambda)
and
t^{\prime}\in\YoungT(\Si,\mu)
such that
(t\rightsquigarrow_{r}t^{\prime})\in\YoungT(\Si,\nu)
, with
\mathbf{r}(t)=x_{1}\ldots x_{k}
and
\mathbf{c}(t)=y_{1}\ldots y_{k}
.
When computing
(t\rightsquigarrow_{r}t^{\prime})
(resp.
(t\rightsquigarrow_{c}t^{\prime})
), one constructs a standard skew tableau
\mathbf{Q}(t\rightsquigarrow_{r}\penalty\ t^{\prime})
(resp.
\mathbf{Q}(t\rightsquigarrow_{c}t^{\prime})
) of shape
\nu/\mu
over
[n]
.
Start with
\mathbf{T}^{k+1}=t^{\prime}
and an empty tableau
\mathbf{Q}_{r}^{k+1}
(resp.
\mathbf{Q}_{c}^{k+1}
) of shape
\mu
. For each
i=k,\ldots,1
, compute
x_{i}\rightsquigarrow\mathbf{T}^{i+1}
(resp.
y_{i}\rightsquigarrow\mathbf{T}^{i+1}
); let
\mathbf{T}^{i}
be the resulting super tableau. Add a
box filled with
(k-i+1)
to
\mathbf{Q}_{r}^{i+1}
(resp.
\mathbf{Q}_{c}^{i+1}
) in the same place as the box that belongs to
\mathbf{T}^{i}
but not
to
\mathbf{T}^{i+1}
; let
\mathbf{Q}_{r}^{i}
(resp.
\mathbf{Q}_{c}^{i}
) be the resulting tableau. Output
\mathbf{Q}_{r}^{1}
(resp.
\mathbf{Q}_{c}^{1}
) as
\mathbf{Q}(t\rightsquigarrow_{r}t^{\prime})
(resp.
\mathbf{Q}(t\rightsquigarrow_{c}t^{\prime})
).
Note that
\mathbf{Q}(t\rightsquigarrow_{r}t^{\prime})\neq\mathbf{Q}(t\rightsquigarrow_{c}t^{\prime})
, for some
t,t^{\prime}
in
\YoungT(\Si)
.

When computing
(t\rightsquigarrow_{r}t^{\prime})
(resp.
(t\rightsquigarrow_{c}t^{\prime})
), one constructs a skew tableau
\mathbf{R}(t\rightsquigarrow_{r}t^{\prime})
(resp.
\mathbf{R}(t\rightsquigarrow_{c}t^{\prime})

over
[n]

Start with
\mathbf{T}^{k+1}=\penalty\ t^{\prime}
and an empty tableau

(resp.
\mathbf{R}_{c}^{k+1}
) of shape
\mu
. For each

, suppose that

is the position of
x_{l}
in

(resp.
y_{l}\rightsquigarrow\mathbf{T}^{l+1}
); let
\mathbf{T}^{l}
be the resulting super tableau. Add a

i_{l}
to
\mathbf{R}_{r}^{l+1}
(resp.
\mathbf{R}_{c}^{l+1}
) in the same place as the box that belongs to
\mathbf{T}^{l}
but not
to
\mathbf{T}^{l+1}
; let
\mathbf{R}_{r}^{l}
(resp.
\mathbf{R}_{c}^{l}
) be the resulting super skew tableau. Output
\mathbf{R}_{r}^{1}
(resp.
\mathbf{R}_{c}^{1}

\mathbf{Q}(t\rightsquigarrow_{c}t^{\prime})

.

As a consequence, we obtain the following result:

Theorem 3.2
.

There is a one-to-one correspondence between the set of skew tableaux
\mathbf{R}(t\rightsquigarrow_{r}t^{\prime})
and the set of standard skew tableaux
\mathbf{Q}(t\rightsquigarrow_{r}t^{\prime})
(resp.
\mathbf{Q}(t\rightsquigarrow_{c}t^{\prime})
), for all
t,t^{\prime}
in
\YoungT(\Si)
.

Denote by
R_{\Si}
the
\mathbb{Z}
-algebra whose linear generators are the monomials in
\mathbf{P}(\Si)
.
A generic element in
R_{\Si}
is realized by a formal sum of super plactic classes with coefficients from
\mathbb{Z}
, and also by a formal sum of super tableaux.
Construct a canonical homomorphism
R_{\Si}\to\mathbb{Z}[X]
sending each super tableau
t
to its monomial
x^{t}
formed by the product of
x_{i}
, each occurring as many times in
x^{t}
as
i
occurs in
t
.
Define
S_{\lambda}
(resp.
S_{\lambda/\mu}
) in

to be the sum of all super tableaux of shape
\lambda
(resp.
\lambda/\mu
) over
\Si
.
Consider
\lambda,\mu,\nu
in
\mathcal{P}
such that
\mu,\lambda\subseteq\nu
. For any
t^{\prime\prime}\in\YoungT(\Si,\nu)
, the
super Littlewood–Richardson coefficient
, denoted by
c_{\lambda,\mu}^{\nu}
, is the number of pairs
(t,t^{\prime})\in\YoungT(\Si,\lambda)\times\YoungT(\Si,\mu)
such that
(t\rightsquigarrow_{r}t^{\prime})=t^{\prime\prime}
. The following identities hold in
R_{\Si}
,
[
7
,
6
]
:

S_{\lambda}S_{\mu}=\underset{\nu}{\sum}c_{\lambda,\mu}^{\nu}S_{\nu}\quad\text{ and }\quad S_{\nu/\lambda}=\underset{\mu}{\sum}c_{\lambda,\mu}^{\nu}S_{\mu}.

A word
x_{1}\ldots x_{k}
over
[n]
is

if every left subword
x_{k}\ldots x_{l}
contains at least as many
1^{\prime}

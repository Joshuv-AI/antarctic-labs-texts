Convergence Rate of Projected Subgradient Method with Time-varying Step-sizes
Zhihan Zhu, Yanhao Zhang, Yong Xia

Abstract
We establish the optimal ergodic convergence rate for the classical projected subgradient method with time-varying step-sizes. This convergence rate remains the same even if we slightly increase the weight of the most recent points, thereby relaxing the ergodic sense.

1
Introduction

Consider the nonsmooth convex optimization problem

x^{*}\in{\rm argmin}_{x\in\mathcal{X}}f(x),

where
\mathcal{X}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
is a compact convex set that is contained within the Euclidean ball
B(x^{*},R)
, and
f
is (possibly nonsmooth) convex and Lipschitz on
\mathcal{X}
, i.e., there is an
L>0
such that
\|g\|\leq L
for any
g\in\partial f(x)\neq\emptyset
and
x\in\mathcal{X}
.

The classical projected subgradient method (PSG) iterates the following equations for
t\geq 1
:

\left\{\begin{aligned} y_{t+1}&=x_{t}-\eta_{t}g_{t},~\text{where }g_{t}\in\partial f(x_{t}),\\
x_{t+1}&={\rm argmin}_{x\in\mathcal{X}}\|x-y_{t+1}\|.\end{aligned}\right.

Utilizing the following constant step size

\eta_{s}\equiv\frac{R}{L\sqrt{t}},~s=1,\cdots,t,

(1)

PSG achieves an optimal ergodic convergence rate (see, for example,
nesterov2018lectures
;
bubeck2015convex
;
MITlecture
) expressed as
1
1

1

f((\sum_{s=1}^{t}x_{s})/t)
can be replaced with
\min_{s=1,\cdots,t}f(x_{s})
based on similar analysis.

f\left(\frac{\sum_{s=1}^{t}x_{s}}{t}\right)-f(x^{*})\leq\frac{RL}{\sqrt{t}}.

(2)

A more practical approach is to use a step-size that monotonically decreases towards
0
.
One such time-varying step-size, motivated by (
1
) and suggested in
nesterov2018lectures
;
bubeck2015convex
, is expressed as follows:

\eta_{s}=\frac{R}{L\sqrt{s}},~s=1,\cdots,t.

(3)

However, using this step size results in sub-optimal ergodic convergence rate
nesterov2018lectures
;
bubeck2015convex
, as it adds an additional
\log(t)
factor compared to the right-hand side of (
2
).

The contribution of this note is to demonstrate that PSG with the time-varying step-size (
3
) indeed achieves the following optimal convergence rate.

Theorem 1.1

PSG with the time-varying step-size (
3
) satisfies

f\left(\frac{\sum_{s=1}^{t}x_{s}}{t}\right)-f(x^{*})\leq\frac{3RL}{2\sqrt{t}}.

In Section 2, we present a more generalized convergence analysis, allowing us to provide some insightful observations.

2
Analysis

Consider PSG with a general step-size
\eta_{s}
, which is
assumed to be positive and non-increasing. We can establish the following convergence rate.

Theorem 2.1

For any fixed
k\geq-1
,
PSG with a positive and non-increasing step-size sequence
\{\eta_{s}\}
satisfies

f\left(\frac{\sum_{s=1}^{t}\frac{1}{\eta_{s}^{k}}x_{s}}{\sum_{s=1}^{t}\frac{1}{\eta_{s}^{k}}}\right)-f(x^{*})\leq\frac{\frac{R^{2}}{\eta_{t}^{k+1}}+L^{2}\sum_{s=1}^{t}\frac{1}{\eta_{s}^{k-1}}}{2\sum_{s=1}^{t}\frac{1}{\eta_{s}^{k}}}.

(4)

Proof

According to the definition of subgradient, we have

\displaystyle f(x_{s})-f(x^{*})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle g_{s}^{T}(x_{s}-x^{*})

(5)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\eta_{s}}(x_{s}-y_{s+1})^{T}(x_{s}-x^{*})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\eta_{s}}(\|x_{s}-y_{s+1}\|^{2}+\|x_{s}-x^{*}\|^{2}-\|y_{s+1}-x^{*}\|^{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\eta_{s}}(\|x_{s}-x^{*}\|^{2}-\|y_{s+1}-x^{*}\|^{2})+\frac{\eta_{s}}{2}\|g_{s}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\eta_{s}}(\|x_{s}-x^{*}\|^{2}-\|x_{s+1}-x^{*}\|^{2})+\frac{\eta_{s}}{2}L^{2},

(6)

where (
5
) follows from the elementary identity
2a^{T}b=\|a\|^{2}+\|b\|^{2}-\|a-b\|^{2}
, and (
6
) holds since
\|g_{s}\|\leq L
and

\|y_{s+1}-x^{*}\|^{2}\geq\|x_{s+1}-x^{*}\|^{2},

which is implied by the projection theorem.

Consequently, we have

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{s=1}^{t}\frac{1}{\eta_{s}^{k}}\right)\left(f\left(\frac{\sum_{s=1}^{t}\frac{1}{\eta_{s}^{k}}x_{s}}{\sum_{s=1}^{t}\frac{1}{\eta_{s}^{k}}}\right)-f(x^{*})\right)

(7)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{s=1}^{t}\frac{1}{\eta_{s}^{k}}(f(x_{s})-f(x^{*}))~~~~({\rm since~}f{\rm~is~convex})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{s=1}^{t}\frac{1}{2\eta_{s}^{k+1}}(\|x_{s}-x^{*}\|^{2}-\|x_{s+1}-x^{*}\|^{2})+\sum_{s=1}^{t}\frac{1}{2\eta_{s}^{k-1}}L^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\eta_{1}^{k+1}}\|x_{1}-x^{*}\|^{2}+\sum_{s=2}^{t}(\frac{1}{2\eta_{s}^{k+1}}-\frac{1}{2\eta_{s-1}^{k+1}})\|x_{s}-x^{*}\|^{2}

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2\eta_{t}^{k+1}}\|x_{t+1}-x^{*}\|^{2}+\sum_{s=1}^{t}\frac{1}{2\eta_{s}^{k-1}}L^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{R^{2}}{2\eta_{1}^{k+1}}+\frac{R^{2}}{2}\sum_{s=2}^{t}(\frac{1}{\eta_{s}^{k+1}}-\frac{1}{\eta_{s-1}^{k+1}})+\sum_{s=1}^{t}\frac{1}{2\eta_{s}^{k-1}}L^{2}

(8)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{R^{2}}{2\eta_{t}^{k+1}}+\sum_{s=1}^{t}\frac{1}{2\eta_{s}^{k-1}}L^{2},

where (
7
) follows from (
6
), and (
8
) holds since
1/\eta_{s}^{k+1}-1/\eta_{s-1}^{k+1}\geq 0
when
k\geq-1
. The proof is complete. ∎

Remark 1

By setting
k=-1
in Theorem
2.1
, the upper bound on the right-hand side (
4
) simplifies to

\frac{R^{2}+L^{2}\sum_{s=1}^{t}\eta_{s}^{2}}{2\sum_{s=1}^{t}\eta_{s}},

which is exactly the same as the result presented in
nesterov2018lectures
. Then PSG with the time-varying step-size (
3
) satisfies

f\left(\frac{\sum_{s=1}^{t}\frac{1}{\sqrt{s}}x_{s}}{\sum_{s=1}^{t}\frac{1}{\sqrt{s}}}\right)-f(x^{*})\leq\frac{2RL+RL\log t}{4(\sqrt{t+1}-1)},

(9)

which is sub-optimal. Note that computing the weighted average of the iterates in the second half of the sequence yields the optimal convergence rate
(
Lan
, Corollary 3.2)
.

Remark 2

By setting
k=0
in Theorem
2.1
, we can immediately obtain the optimal convergence rate, as presented in Theorem
1.1
.

Remark 3

By setting any
k
such that
k>-1
in Theorem
2.1
, the convergence rate of
f((\sum_{s=1}^{t}x_{s}/\eta_{s}^{k})/(\sum_{s=1}^{t}1/\eta_{s}^{k}))-f(x^{*})
will be
\mathcal{O}(1/\sqrt{t})
, without the presence of a
\log t
factor. In comparison with the sub-optimal case (
9
), when
k>0
, the
weighting scheme
(\sum_{s=1}^{t}x_{s}/\eta_{s}^{k})/(\sum_{s=1}^{t}1/\eta_{s}^{k})
assigns smaller weights to the initial points and larger weights to the most recent points. This new result can be referred to as the “weak” ergodic convergence rate.

Remark 4

We can apply the same proof techniques to extend the conclusion of weak ergodic convergence to mirror descent, Nesterov’s dual averaging, and other schemes with time-varying step sizes for solving nonsmooth convex optimization, see
nesterov2018lectures
;
bubeck2015convex
.

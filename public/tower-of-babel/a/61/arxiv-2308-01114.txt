Strict Wick–type deformation quantization on Riemann surfaces: Rigidity and Obstructions


Abstract
Let X X be a hyperbolic Riemann surface. We study a convergent Wick–type star product ⋆ X \star_{X} on X X which is induced by the canonical convergent star product ⋆ 𝔻 \star_{{\mathbb{D}}} on the unit disk 𝔻 {\mathbb{D}} via Uniformization Theory. While by construction, the resulting Fréchet algebras ( 𝒜 ( X ) , ⋆ X ) (\mathcal{A}(X),\star_{X}) are strongly isomorphic for conformally equivalent Riemann surfaces, our work exhibits additional severe topological obstructions. In particular, we show that the Fréchet algebra ( 𝒜 ( X ) , ⋆ X ) (\mathcal{A}(X),\star_{X}) degenerates if and only if the connectivity of X X is at least 3 3 , and ( 𝒜 ( X ) , ⋆ X ) (\mathcal{A}(X),\star_{X}) is noncommutative if and only if X X is simply connected. We also explicitly determine the algebra 𝒜 X \mathcal{A}_{X} and the star product ⋆ X \star_{X} for the intermediate case of doubly connected Riemann surfaces X X . As a perhaps surprinsing result, we deduce that two such Fréchet algebras are strongly isomorphic if and only if either both Riemann surfaces are conformally equivalent to an (not neccesarily the same) annulus or both are conformally equivalent to a punctured disk.

Definition

(Convergent Wick–star product on hyperbolic Riemann surfaces)

Let
X
be a hyperbolic Riemann surface and
\pi:{\mathbb{D}}\to X

\mathcal{A}(X):=\left\{f:X\to{\mathbb{C}}\,|\,f\circ\pi\in\mathcal{A}({\mathbb{D}})\right\}\,

\left(f\star_{X}g\right)\circ\pi:=(f\circ\pi)\star_{{\mathbb{D}}}(g\circ\pi)\,.

Moreover, we equip

(\mathcal{A}({\mathbb{D}}),\star_{{\mathbb{D}}})

Proposition 1.4

(Conformal invariance of the Fréchet algebra
(\mathcal{A}(X),\star_{X})
)

Let

\Psi:Y\to X

\Psi^{*}:(\mathcal{A}(X),\star_{X,\hbar})\to(\mathcal{A}(Y),\star_{Y,\hbar})

\Psi^{*}(f)(z)=f(\Psi(z))\,,\qquad f\in\mathcal{A}(X),\,z\in Y\,.

The isomorphism

\hbar

\mathcal{A}(Y)

Definition

(Strong isomorphy)

Let
X
and
Y

\mathcal{A}(X)

(\mathcal{A}(X),\star_{X,\hbar})

(\mathcal{A}(Y),\star_{Y,\hbar})

In this case, we write

We can now state the main result of this paper.

Theorem 1.5

Let

(a)

\mathcal{A}(X)\mathop{{\rm\simeq}}\mathcal{A}({\mathbb{D}})\,.

(b)

\mathcal{A}(X)\mathop{{\rm\simeq}}\mathcal{A}(A_{R})

\mathcal{A}(X)\mathop{{\rm\simeq}}\mathcal{A}(A_{R})\quad\text{ for every }R>1\,.

(c)

is conformally equivalent to the punctured disk

\mathcal{A}(X)\mathop{{\rm\simeq}}\mathcal{A}({\mathbb{D}}^{*})\,.

(d)

In all other cases,
\mathcal{A}(X)
consists only of constant functions.

4
Möbius–invariant holomorphic functions on the second configuration space of the sphere

In order to describe the algebras
\mathcal{A}(X)
for hyperbolic Riemann
surfaces
X
which are not simply connected, we are going to use some few basic facts about
Fuchsian groups, see e.g.
[
13
,
30
,
17
]
.
Recall that a
Fuchsian group
is a discrete Möbius group
\Gamma
acting on the upper half–plane
{\mathbb{H}}
. In particular,
every Fuchsian group is a subgroup of the group
\mathop{{\rm Aut}}({\mathbb{H}})
of all conformal
automorphisms of
{\mathbb{H}}
and
the quotient
{\mathbb{H}}/\Gamma
is a hyperbolic Riemann surface.
In fact, every hyperbolic Riemann surface arises in this way.
If
X
is a hyperbolic Riemann surface and
\pi:{\mathbb{H}}\to X
is a universal
covering map, then its group
\Gamma
of covering transformations is a

fixed point free
Fuchsian group, that it, every
\gamma\in\Gamma\setminus\{id\}
has no fixed points
in

{\mathbb{H}}
. In this case,
X
is
conformally equivalent to
{\mathbb{H}}/\Gamma
, and every function
g:X\to{\mathbb{C}}
can be identified with the

\Gamma
–invariant function
f:=g\circ\pi:{\mathbb{H}}\to{\mathbb{C}}
and vice versa.

We conclude that in order to find
\mathcal{A}({\mathbb{H}}/\Gamma)
we only need to identify the
\Gamma
–invariant functions in
\mathcal{A}({\mathbb{H}})
.
Now every
\gamma\in\mathop{{\rm Aut}}({\mathbb{H}})
induces in a natural way an element
\hat{\gamma}
of the group
\mathop{{\rm Aut}}(G)
of biholomorphic self–maps of
G
via

\hat{\gamma}(z,w)=(\gamma(z),\gamma(w))\,.

Lemma 4.1

Let
f\in\mathcal{A}({\mathbb{H}})
and
F\in\mathcal{H}(G)
such that
f(z)=F(z,\overline{z})
. Suppose that
\gamma\in\mathop{{\rm Aut}}({\mathbb{H}})
. Then

f
is
\gamma
–invariant if and only if

F
is
\hat{\gamma}
–invariant.

Proof.

This is immediate from

(f\circ\gamma)(z)=F\left(\gamma(z),\overline{\gamma(z)}\right)=F(\gamma(z),\gamma(\overline{z}))=(F\circ\hat{\gamma})(z,\overline{z})

which is based on the crucial property that every

\gamma\in\mathop{{\rm Aut}}({\mathbb{H}})
not only maps
{\mathbb{H}}
onto
{\mathbb{H}}
, but is symmetric w.r.t. the real axis, that is,
\overline{\gamma(z)}=\gamma(\overline{z})
.
∎

We are therefore led to the following problem.

Problem 4.2

Let
\Gamma
be a Fuchsian group acting on the upper half–plane
{\mathbb{H}}
.
Find the set of all
F\in\mathcal{H}(G)
which are invariant under the subgroup

\hat{\Gamma}:=\{\hat{\gamma}\,:\,\gamma\in\Gamma\}

of
\mathop{{\rm Aut}}(G)
.

It turns out that the so–called limit set of the Fuchsian group
\Gamma
plays
the crucial role here.
The limit set of
\Gamma
, denoted by
\Lambda(\Gamma)
, is the set of
accumulation points of the
\Gamma
–orbits of any point
z\in{\mathbb{H}}
. Since the
action of
\Gamma
is properly discontinuous,
\Lambda(\Gamma)\subseteq\partial{\mathbb{H}}:={\mathbb{R}}\cup\{\infty\}
.
A Fuchsian group
G
is said to be elementary if
\Lambda(\Gamma)
consists of exactly zero, one or two elements.
Otherwise, it is called non–elementary. It is well–known (see
e.g.
[
13
, Corollary 3.4.6]
) that the limit set of any non–elementary
Fuchsian group is either
\partial{\mathbb{H}}
or a Cantor set.

Theorem 4.3

Let
\Gamma
be a non–elementary Fuchsian group. Then
every
\hat{\Gamma}
–invariant holomorphic function on
G
is constant.

Corollary 4.4

Suppose that
X
is a hyperbolic Riemann surface
with a non–elementary covering group. Then
\mathcal{A}(X)
consists only of constant functions.

Corollary
4.4
applies in particular to all compact Riemann surfaces of higher genus.
We wish to point out that this strong
rigidity result
only depends on the
function–theoretic description
of
\mathcal{A}({\mathbb{D}})
in terms of
\mathcal{H}(\Omega)
and conformal invariance.

We now turn to the remaining cases, when
X
is not simply connected and the Fuchsian group
\Gamma
is
elementary. Since we are only interested in hyperbolic Riemann surfaces,
we may restrict ourselves to the case that
\Gamma
is a covering group, so it acts freely on

{\mathbb{H}}
. Recalling that two fixed–point free Fuchsian
groups
\Gamma
and
\Gamma^{\prime}
are conjugated if and only if the
corresponding Riemann surfaces
{\mathbb{D}}/\Gamma
and
{\mathbb{D}}/\Gamma^{\prime}
are conformally
equivalent, the well–known classification of elementary
Fuchsian groups (see
[
13
, Proposition 3.1.2]
) implies that there are only two cases to be considered. Either
\Gamma
is conjugate to a group generated by exactly one parabolic element (one fixed point on
\partial{\mathbb{H}}
) or
by exactly one hyperbolic element (two fixed points on
\partial{\mathbb{H}}
), that is,
{\mathbb{D}}/\Gamma
is either the punctured unit disk or a proper annulus.

We first consider the case of a proper annulus
A_{R}=\{z\in{\mathbb{C}}\,:\,1/R<|z|<R\}
for some
R>1
.
Here
A_{R}={\mathbb{H}}/\Gamma
, where
\Gamma
is the cyclic group generated by
z\mapsto c\cdot z
with

\log c=\pi^{2}/\log R>0
.

Theorem 4.5

(Annulus)

Let
\Gamma=\{z\mapsto c^{n}z\,:\,n\in\mathbb{Z}\}
for some

c>1
.
Then
F\in\mathcal{H}(G)
is
\hat{\Gamma}
–invariant if and only if

F(z,w)=g\left(\frac{w}{z-w}\right)

for some
g\in\mathcal{H}({\mathbb{C}})
.

On the Danielewski surface
D
(see (
3.1
)) the functions described in Theorem

are exactly all holomorphic functions
(a,b,c)\mapsto F(a,b,c)
which depend only on

.

We finally consider the case of the punctured unit disk
{\mathbb{D}}^{*}:={\mathbb{D}}\setminus\{0\}={\mathbb{H}}/\Gamma
, where

\Gamma
is the cyclic group generated by right–translation
z\mapsto z+1
and associated universal covering

z\mapsto e^{2\pi iz}
.

Theorem 4.6

Let
\Gamma:=\{z\mapsto z+n\,:\,n\in\mathbb{Z}\}
. Then

F\in\mathcal{H}(G)
is
\hat{\Gamma}
–invariant if and only if

F(z,w)=g\left(\frac{1}{z-w}\right)

for some
g\in\mathcal{H}({\mathbb{C}})
.

On the Danielewski surface

5
Möbius–invariant holomorphic functions on the second configuration space of the sphere – Proof of Theorem
4.3
and Theorem
4.5

For the proof of Theorem
4.3
we recall that
\gamma\in\mathop{{\rm Aut}}({\mathbb{H}})
is said to be hyperbolic if it has two distinct fixed–points on

\partial{\mathbb{H}}
, one repulsive and one attractive. If
\Gamma
is a
non–elementary Fuchsian group, then its limit set
\Lambda(\Gamma)
has the
following two well–known properties

(P1)

\Lambda(\Gamma)

is the
closure of the set of fixed points of the hyperbolic elements in
\Gamma

(
[
17
, Thm. 3.4.4]
);

(P2)

\Lambda(\Gamma)
has no isolated points (
[
17
, Thm. 3.4.6]
).

The idea of the proof of Theorem
4.3
is as follows. In a first
step, one proves that for each fixed point
w_{0}
of any hyperbolic element in

\Gamma
the holomorphic functions
F(w_{0},\cdot)
and
F(\cdot,w_{0})
are constant.
In a second step, which is based on (P1), it is then shown that
F
is constant on

(\Lambda(\Gamma)\times\Lambda(\Gamma))\cap G
. From this and using (P2), one
finally can prove that
F
is constant on
G
.

Proof of Theorem
4.3
.

Let
F\in\mathcal{H}(G)
be
\hat{\Gamma}
-invariant.

(i) Let
\gamma
be a hyperbolic automorphism in
\Gamma
with fixed point
w_{0}\in\partial{\mathbb{H}}={\mathbb{R}}\cup\{\infty\}
. We show that

F(\cdot,w_{0})\in{\mathbb{H}}(\hat{{\mathbb{C}}}\setminus\{w_{0}\})
and
F(w_{0},\cdot)\in{\mathbb{H}}(\hat{{\mathbb{C}}}\setminus\{w_{0}\})
are constant.

Since
\gamma
is hyperbolic, it has a second fixed point
z_{0}\in\partial{\mathbb{H}}\setminus\{w_{0}\}
. We may assume that
z_{0}\in{\mathbb{R}}
, so

\gamma^{\prime}(z_{0})\not\in\partial{\mathbb{D}}
. The function

g:=F(\cdot,w_{0})\in\mathcal{H}(\hat{{\mathbb{C}}}\setminus\{w_{0}\})
is

\gamma
–invariant, since

(g\circ\gamma)(z)=F(\gamma(z),w_{0})=F(\gamma(z),\gamma(w_{0}))=F(z,w_{0})=g(z)\,.

(5.1)

In particular,
g
has a fixed point at
z_{0}
, and taking the derivative in
(
5.1
) at

leads to
g^{\prime}(z_{0})\cdot\gamma^{\prime}(z_{0})=g^{\prime}(z_{0})
. Since

\gamma^{\prime}(z_{0})\not=1

g^{\prime}(z_{0})=0
. Taking the second derivative
in (
5.1
) at

leads to
g^{\prime\prime}(z_{0})=0
and continuing this way, we
easily see that
g
is constant. In a similar way, one can show that

F(w_{0},\cdot)\in\mathcal{H}(\hat{{\mathbb{C}}}\setminus\{w_{0}\})
is constant.

(ii) Fix two distinct
fixed points

of some hyperbolic automorphism in
\Gamma
and let

C:=F(p,q)
. Now, let

be two distinct
fixed points of another hyperbolic automorphism in
\Gamma
.
By (i),
F(p,\hat{{\mathbb{C}}}\setminus\{p\})=\{C\}
, so
F(p,s)=C
. Again by (i), we get
F(\hat{{\mathbb{C}}}\setminus\{s\},s)=C
, so
F(r,s)=C
. Since
\Gamma
is non–elementary, the fixed points of all hyperbolic
elements in
\Gamma
are dense in
\Lambda(\Gamma)
. This implies that
F
is constant
=C
on

(\Lambda(\Gamma)\times\Lambda(\Gamma))\cap G
as follows. Let
(p,q)\in\left(\Lambda(\Gamma)\times\Lambda(\Gamma)\right)\cap\Omega
. In particular,
p\not=q
. Then there are sequences
(p_{n})
,
(q_{n})\subseteq\partial{\mathbb{H}}
,
(T_{n})\subseteq\Gamma
such that each
T_{n}
is hyperbolic and
T_{n}(p_{n})=p_{n}\to p
as well as
T_{n}(q_{n})=q_{n}\to q
as

. Hence

q_{n}\not=p_{n}
for

sufficiently large, so
F(p_{n},\cdot)\equiv C
on
\hat{{\mathbb{C}}}\setminus\{p_{n}\}
and thus
F(p,q)=\lim_{n\to\infty}F(p_{n},q_{n})=C
.

(iii) Since
\Lambda(\Gamma)
contains no isolated points, it is clear that any

which is constant on

{\partial h/\partial z}={\partial h/\partial w}=0

(\Lambda(\Gamma)\times\Lambda(\Gamma))\cap G
.
In particular, this implies that for any
(p,q)\in(\Lambda(\Gamma)\times\Lambda(\Gamma))\cap G

F

at the point
(p,q)
vanish. Hence
F
is constant.
∎

Proof of Theorem

.

Since

\hat{\Gamma}=\{(z,w)\mapsto(c^{n}z,c^{n}w)\,:\,n\in\mathbb{Z}\}\,,

it is clear that for every entire function

is
\hat{\Gamma}
–invariant. In order to prove the only–if part of Theorem

\hat{\Gamma}

Proof of Theorem
4.6
(a).

Since

\hat{\Gamma}=\{(z,w)\mapsto(z+n,w+n)\,:\,n\in\mathbb{Z}\}\,,

it is clear that for every entire function

\hat{\Gamma}

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{k}F}{\partial w^{k}}(z,\infty)

6
The convergent Wick–star product on doubly connected Riemann surfaces

In this section we prove Theorem
1.7
and Corollary
1.8
as well as Theorem
1.9
and Theorem
1.11
. The main tools will be (i) an explicit formula for the star product
\star_{\hbar,{\mathbb{D}}}
on the unit disk
{\mathbb{D}}
and (ii) an asymptotic expansion for it as
\hbar\to 0
. Both results have recently been established in
[
10
]
and rely essentially on formula (5.24) in
[
26
]
. The explicit formula for
\star_{{\mathbb{D}}}
is based
on invariant derivatives of Peschl–Minda type, which are defined as follows. For
z\in{\mathbb{D}}
denote

T_{z}(u):=\frac{z+u}{1+\overline{z}u}\in\mathop{{\rm Aut}}({\mathbb{D}})\,,

and by

\partial:=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial x}-i\frac{\partial}{\partial y}\right)

the standard (Wirtinger) differential operator.
Then for every
C^{\infty}
–function
f:{\mathbb{D}}\to{\mathbb{C}}
the Peschl–Minda derivatives of
f
of order
n
at the point
z\in D
are defined by

D^{n}f(z):=\partial^{n}(f\circ T_{z})(0)\,,\qquad\overline{D}^{n}f(z):=\overline{D^{n}(\overline{f})(z)}\,,

see e.g.
[
10
,
18
]
. Corollary 5.1 in
[
10
]
provides the following explicit formula for
D^{n}f
in terms of the Wirtinger derivative
\partial
:

D^{n+1}f(z)=\left(1-|z|^{2}\right)\partial^{n+1}\left[\left(1-|z|^{2}\right)^{n}f(z)\right]

(6.1)

Unlike
\partial^{n+1}=\partial^{n}\partial
one has
D^{n+1}\not=D^{n}D^{1}
in general. The basic result we need is now

(f\star_{{\mathbb{D}},\hbar}\tilde{f})(z)=\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{c_{n}(\hbar)}{n!}\,D^{n}\tilde{f}(z)\cdot\overline{D}^{n}f(z)\,

(6.2)

for every
f,\tilde{f}\in\mathcal{A}({\mathbb{D}})
and every
\hbar\in\mathscr{D}
, and with
c_{n}(\hbar)
defined as in (
1.2
).
In fact, for each fixed
z\in{\mathbb{D}}
the series (
6.2
) converges absolutely and locally uniformly w.r.t.
\hbar\in\mathscr{D}
, so
\hbar\mapsto(f\star_{{\mathbb{D}},\hbar}\tilde{f})(z)
is holomorphic on
\mathscr{D}
. Moreover, for fixed
\hbar\in\mathscr{D}
, the series (
6.1
) converges in the topology of
\mathcal{A}({\mathbb{D}})
. See
[
10
, Lemma 6.5]
.

.

(i)

and

\displaystyle\Psi_{R^{\prime},R}(g\circ f_{R^{\prime}})\star_{A_{R}}\Psi_{R^{\prime},R}(\tilde{g}\circ f_{R^{\prime}})

\displaystyle\overset{(\ref{eq:Psi2})}{=}

\displaystyle(g\circ f_{R})\star_{A_{R}}(\tilde{g}\circ f_{R})

\displaystyle\overset{(\ref{eq:FormulaStarAnnulus})}{=}

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}c_{n}(\hbar)\left(f_{R}^{2}-1\right)^{n}\left(g^{(n)}\circ f_{R}\right)\left(\tilde{g}^{(n)}\circ f_{R}\right)

\displaystyle\overset{(\ref{eq:Psi2})}{=}

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}c_{n}(\hbar)\Psi_{R^{\prime},R}\left[\left(f_{R^{\prime}}^{2}-1\right)^{n}\left(g^{(n)}\circ f_{R^{\prime}}\right)\left(\tilde{g}^{(n)}\circ f_{R^{\prime}}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Psi_{R^{\prime},R}\left[\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}c_{n}(\hbar)\left(f_{R^{\prime}}^{2}-1\right)^{n}\left(g^{(n)}\circ f_{R^{\prime}}\right)\left(\tilde{g}^{(n)}\circ f_{R^{\prime}}\right)\right]

\displaystyle\overset{(\ref{eq:FormulaStarAnnulus})}{=}

\displaystyle\Psi_{R^{\prime},R}\left(f\star_{A_{R}^{\prime}}\tilde{f}\right)\,.

∎

Proof of Theorem
1.9
.

The proof is very similar to the proof of Theorem
1.7
, so we only indicate the main steps.
We first prove (
1.7
). To this end, we note that

\pi:{\mathbb{H}}\to{\mathbb{D}}^{*}
,
\pi(z)=e^{2\pi iz}
, is a universal covering of
{\mathbb{D}}^{*}
and
\Gamma=\{z\mapsto z+n\,:\,n\in\mathbb{Z}\}
is the associated group of deck transformations.
Hence a function
f:{\mathbb{D}}^{*}\to{\mathbb{C}}
belongs to
\mathcal{A}({\mathbb{D}}^{*})
if and only
if the
\Gamma
–invariant function
f\circ\pi
belongs to
\mathcal{A}({\mathbb{H}})
. By
Lemma
4.1
this is equivalent to
(f\circ\pi)(z)=F(z,\overline{z})
for some
\hat{\Gamma}
–invariant function
F\in\mathcal{H}(G)
. Theorem
4.6
therefore shows that
f\in\mathcal{A}({\mathbb{D}}^{*})
if and only if

f(e^{2\pi iz})=(f\circ\pi)(z)=\tilde{g}\left(\frac{1}{z-\overline{z}}\right)\,,\qquad z\in{\mathbb{H}}\,,

for some entire function
\tilde{g}:{\mathbb{C}}\to{\mathbb{C}}
or

f(w)=g\left(\frac{1}{\log|w|}\right)=g(f_{0}(w))\,,\qquad w\in{\mathbb{D}}^{*}\,,

for the entire function
g(z):=\tilde{g}(-\pi z/i)
. Conversely,
f:=g\circ f_{0}
belongs to
\mathcal{A}({\mathbb{D}}^{*})
for every
g\in\mathcal{H}({\mathbb{C}})
, proving (
1.7
).

Finally, we prove the explicit formula (
1.8
) for the punctured disk
{\mathbb{D}}^{*}
, and for this purpose we work on the unit disk
{\mathbb{D}}
and employ the universal covering map
\pi_{0}:{\mathbb{D}}\to{\mathbb{D}}^{*}
given by

\pi_{0}(z):=\exp\left(-\frac{1+z}{1-z}\right)\,.

Consider the auxiliary function

q:{\mathbb{D}}\to{\mathbb{C}}\,,\qquad q(z):=\frac{|1-z|^{2}}{1-|z|^{2}}

and the function
f_{0}:{\mathbb{D}}^{*}\to{\mathbb{C}}
defined in (
1.6
). Then

\pi_{0}\circ f_{0}=q\,,

and it easily follows inductively from (
6.1
) that

\displaystyle D^{n}(g\circ q)(z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-1)^{n}\frac{(1-\overline{z})^{2n}}{(1-|z|^{2})^{n}}\,\left(g^{(n)}\circ q\right)(z)

\displaystyle\overline{D}^{n}(g\circ q)(z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-1)^{n}\frac{(1-z)^{2n}}{(1-|z|^{2})^{n}}\,\left(g^{(n)}\circ q\right)(z)\,

and hence

\overline{D}^{n}(g\circ q)D^{n}(\tilde{g}\circ q)=q^{2n}\left(g^{(n)}\circ q\right)\left(\tilde{g}^{(n)}\circ q\right)

(6.5)

for all
g,\tilde{g}\in\mathcal{H}({\mathbb{C}})
. Plugging this expression into (
6.2
) results in
(
1.8
)
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.11
.

The proof is by contradiction.
Assume there is a strong Fréchet algebra isomorphism
\Psi:(\mathcal{A}(A_{R}),\star_{A_{R}})\to(\mathcal{A}({\mathbb{D}}^{*}),\star_{{\mathbb{D}}^{*}})
.

(i) Consider
f_{R}\in\mathcal{A}(A_{R})
as defined by (
1.1
). Then (
1.5
) for
g(z)=\tilde{g}(z)=z

implies

f_{R}\star_{A_{R}}f_{R}=f_{R}^{2}+\left(f_{R}^{2}-1\right)\hbar\,

and hence

\Psi(f_{R}\star_{A_{R}}f_{R})=\Psi\left(f_{R}^{2}+\hbar\left(f_{R}^{2}-1\right)\right)=\Psi(f_{R}^{2})+\left(\Psi(f_{R}^{2})-1\right)\hbar\,.

Here, we have used that
\Psi
is a linear map and
\Psi(1)=1
, with
1
denoting the function with constant value
1
.

(ii) By Theorem
1.9
, there is a unique
g\in\mathcal{H}({\mathbb{C}})
such that

\pi_{0}\circ\Psi(f_{R})=g\circ q\,.

Here,
\pi_{0}
and
q
have the same meaning as in the proof of Theorem
1.9
.
It follows from the definition of the star product
\star_{\hbar,{\mathbb{D}}^{*}}
that

\left(\Psi(f_{R})\star_{\hbar,{\mathbb{D}}^{*}}\Psi(f_{R})\right)\circ\pi_{0}=(g\circ q)\star_{\hbar,{\mathbb{D}}}(g\circ q)\,.

We now make use of the asymptotic expansion for
\star_{\hbar,{\mathbb{D}}}
as
\hbar\to 0
established in
[
10
, Theorem 6.9]
, which asserts

\displaystyle(g\circ q)\star_{\hbar,{\mathbb{D}}}(g\circ q)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(g\circ q)^{2}+\hbar\overline{D}^{1}(g\circ q)D^{1}(g\circ q)+\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2}\overline{D}^{2}(g\circ q)D^{2}(g\circ q)+O(\hbar^{3})

\displaystyle\overset{(\ref{eq:Bi})}{=}

\displaystyle\Psi(f_{R})^{2}\circ\pi_{0}+\hbar q^{2}(g^{\prime}\circ q)^{2}+\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2}q^{4}(g^{\prime\prime}\circ q)^{2}+O(\hbar^{3})\,.

(iii) Combing (i) and (ii), we see that under the assumption that
\Psi(f_{R}\star_{\hbar,A_{R}}f_{R})=\Psi(f_{R})\star_{\hbar,{\mathbb{D}}^{*}}\Psi(f_{R})
for all
\hbar\in\mathscr{D}
,

\Psi(f_{R}^{2})\circ\pi_{0}+\left(\Psi(f_{R}^{2})\circ\pi_{0}-1\right)\hbar=\Psi(f_{R})^{2}\circ\pi_{0}+\hbar q^{2}(g^{\prime}\circ q)^{2}+\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2}q^{4}(g^{\prime\prime}\circ q)^{2}+O(\hbar^{3})\,.

Comparing equal powers of
\hbar
,
we get

(I)

\hbar^{2}
:
\frac{1}{2}q^{4}(g^{\prime\prime}\circ q)^{2}=0
, so
g^{\prime\prime}=0
, and hence
g(z)=\alpha z+\beta
for some
\alpha,\beta\in{\mathbb{C}}
.

(II)

\hbar^{1}
:
\Psi(f_{R}^{2})\circ\pi_{0}-1=q^{2}\alpha^{2}
.

(III)

\hbar^{0}
:
\Psi(f_{R}^{2})\circ\pi_{0}=\Psi(f_{R})^{2}\circ\pi_{0}
and hence
\Psi(f_{R}^{2})\circ\pi_{0}=(g\circ q)^{2}=(\alpha^{2}q^{2}+2\alpha\beta q+\beta^{2})
.

Combining (II) and (III) gives
\alpha=0
and
\beta=\pm 1
. Therefore,
\Psi(f_{R})=\pm 1=\pm\Psi(1)=\Psi(\pm 1)
, and thus
f_{R}=\pm 1
, a contradiction.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.5
.

The “only if” parts of (a), (b) and (c) follow from Proposition
1.4
.
In order to prove the “if part” of (a) let
\mathcal{A}(X)\mathop{{\rm\simeq}}\mathcal{A}({\mathbb{D}})
. In particular,
\mathcal{A}(X)
contains non–constant functions and
\star_{X}
is noncommutative. Therefore Corollary
4.4
implies that the covering group of
X
is elementary, and Theorem
1.7
and Theorem
1.9
imply that
X
is not conformally equivalent to a proper annulus or the punctured disk. Hence
X
is simply connected.
Now assume
\mathcal{A}(X)\mathop{{\rm\simeq}}\mathcal{A}(A_{R})
for some
R>1
. Then, by Corollary
1.8
,
\mathcal{A}(X)\mathop{{\rm\simeq}}\mathcal{A}(A_{R^{\prime}})
for every
R^{\prime}>1
. Moreover,
(\mathcal{A}(X),\star_{X})
is not strongly isomorphic to
(\mathcal{A}({\mathbb{D}}^{*}),\star_{{\mathbb{D}}^{*}})
by Theorem
1.11
and hence
X
is not conformally equivalent to
{\mathbb{D}}^{*}
by Proposition
1.4
.
X
cannot be conformally equivalent to
{\mathbb{D}}
since otherwise
(\mathcal{A}(X),\star_{X})
would be noncommutative, contradicting Theorem
1.7
. We conclude that
X
has to be conformally equivalent to some proper annulus
A_{\rho}
,
\rho>1
. This proves the “if part” of (b). The “if part” of (c) follows in a similar way. To prove (d) note that if
X
is neither conformally equivalent to
{\mathbb{D}}
nor to some annulus or punctured disk, then the covering group of
X
is non–elementary, so
\mathcal{A}(X)
consist only of constant functions by Corollary
4.4
.
∎

Proof of Corollary
1.13
.

Note that
\mathcal{A}(A_{R})
and
\mathcal{A}({\mathbb{D}}^{*})
do not separate points since they consists only of radially symmetric functions by Theorem
1.7
and Theorem
1.9
. On the other hand,

\mathcal{A}({\mathbb{D}})
separates points, since
\mathcal{H}(\Omega)
separates points as
\Omega
is a Stein manifold, see Theorem
3.1
. This proves that (a) and (b) are equivalent. The equivalence of (a) and (c) follows directly from Theorem
1.5
, Theorem
1.7
and Theorem
1.9
.
∎

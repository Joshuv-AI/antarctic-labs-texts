Infinitely many Brake orbits of Tonelli Hamiltonian systems on the cotangent bundle
Duanzhi Zhang, Zhihao Zhao

Abstract
We prove that on the twisted cotangent bundle of a closed manifold with an exact magnetic form, a Hamiltonian system of a time-dependent Tonelli Hamiltonian possesses infinitely many brake orbits. More precisely, by applying Legendre transform we show that there are infinitely many symmetric orbits of the dual Euler-Lagrange system on the configuration space. This result contains an assertion for the existence of infinitely many symmetric orbits of Tonelli Euler-Lagrange systems given by G. Lu at the end of [ Lu09a , Remark 6.1] . In this paper, we will present a complete proof of this assertion.



Introduction

Let
M
be a closed
N
-dimensional smooth manifold.
We denote by the triple
(T^{*}M,\omega,R)

the symplectic manifold
(T^{*}M,\omega)
associated with an anti-symplectic involution
R
, i.e.
R^{*}\omega=-\omega
and
R^{2}=id
.
Let
H:S_{\tau}\times T^{*}M\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a smooth
\tau
-periodic Hamiltonian on symplectic manifold

(T^{*}M,\omega)
. In this paper, we focus on
H
with the following conditions:

(H1) the fiberwise Hessian of
H
is positive definite, i.e.

\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}\frac{\partial^{2}H}{\partial p_{i}\partial p_{j}}(t,q,p)w_{i}w_{j}>0

for all
(t,q,p)\in S_{\tau}\times T^{*}M
and nonzero
w=\sum_{i=1}^{N}w_{i}\tfrac{\partial}{\partial p_{i}}\in T_{p}T_{q}^{*}M.

(H2)
H
is fiberwise superlinear, i.e.

\lim_{|p|_{q}\rightarrow\infty}\frac{H(t,q,p)}{|p|_{q}}=\infty,

for all
(t,q)\in S_{\tau}\times M.

(H3)
R^{*}H(-t,\cdot)=H(t,\cdot).

(H1)(H2) are the so-called Tonelli conditions, and we say
H
a Tonelli Hamiltonian.
Besides, if
H
satisfies (H3), we say
H
a Hamiltonian defined on
(T^{*}M,\omega,R)
.
A
\tau
-periodic brake orbit
is defined as the solution of the following Hamilition system

\displaystyle\dot{x}(t)

\displaystyle=X_{H}(t,x(t)),

(1.1)

\displaystyle Rx(t)

\displaystyle=x(-t),

\displaystyle x(t)

\displaystyle=x(t+\tau),

where
X_{H}
is a Hamiltonian vector field defined by
i_{X_{H}}\omega=-\text{d}H
.

If
H=K+V
is the sum of kinetic energy and the potential energy, which is symmetric in the momenta, then
R_{0}^{*}H=H
and

H
defines the classical mechanical
system on
(T^{*}M,\omega_{0},R_{0})
,
where
\omega_{0}=\text{d}q\wedge\text{d}p,\ p\in T_{q}^{*}M
and
R_{0}:(q,p)\rightarrow(q,-p)
is the standard anti-symplectic involution on
T^{*}M
with zero
section
M_{0}
as the fixed point set.
The existence of brake orbits in this case goes back to
[
Sei48
,
Bol78
]
.
Analogous to the conjecture of the existence of infinitely many periodic orbits for
Hamiltonian system on
(T^{*}M,\omega_{0})
(see e.g.
[
Lon00
,
Lu09b
,
Maz11
]
),
it is natural to consider the problem of the existence of infinitely many brake orbits
for Hamilition system on
(T^{*}M,\omega_{0})
. This problem has been studied in
[
LW07
,
LW09
]

for some special cases of
H
and
M
.
The latest
result in
[
Lu09b
]
gives the positive answer when
H
is a
\tau
-periodic convex quadratic-growth Hamiltonian. More precisely,
if there exist constants
0<h_{1}<h_{2}
, depending on the local coordiantes of
M
, such that

\displaystyle h_{1}|w|_{q}^{2}\leq\sum_{ij}\tfrac{\partial^{2}H}{\partial p_{i}\partial p_{j}}(t,q,p)w_{i}w_{j}\leq h_{2}|w|_{q}^{2},\ \forall 0\neq w=\sum_{i=1}^{N}w_{i}\tfrac{\partial}{\partial p_{i}}\in T_{q}^{*}M,

(1.2)

\displaystyle\left\lvert\tfrac{\partial^{2}H}{\partial q_{i}\partial p_{j}}(t,q,p)\right\rvert\leq h_{2}(1+|p|_{q}),\ \left\lvert\tfrac{\partial^{2}H}{\partial q_{i}\partial q_{j}}(t,q,p)\right\rvert\leq h_{2}(1+|p|_{q}^{2}),

then the time-
\tau
map of the Hamiltonian flow
\Psi_{H}^{t}
(the Poincar
\acute{\text{e}}
map) of
H
has infinitely many periodic points sitting in
the zero section
M_{0}
of
T^{*}M.
Note that each
\tau
-periodic point of
\Psi_{H}^{t}
sitting in
M_{0}
corresponds to a

\tau
-periodic brake orbit in
T^{*}M
.

Let
\sigma
be a closed 2-form (magnetic form) on the closed connected Riemannian manifold
M
. Equip
T^{*}M
with the twisted symplectic
form
\omega=\omega_{0}-\pi^{*}\sigma
, where
\pi:T^{*}M\rightarrow M
is the natural projection.
When
\sigma=\text{d}\theta
is exact, there exists an anti-symplectic involution

R:T^{*}M\rightarrow T^{*}M
defined by

R_{1}:(q,p)\mapsto(q,-p-2\theta(q))

(see in Section
3
).
We prove that
there are infinitely many brake orbits of the Hamiltonian system on
(T^{*}M,\omega)
provided that

H
is a time-dependent Tonelli Hamilition and
R_{1}^{*}H(-t,\cdot)=H(t,\cdot)
.
We always assume that
H
defines
a global Hamiltonian flow on
T^{*}M.

The main result is the following.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
M
be a smooth closed manifold,
\sigma
an exact magnetic form on
M
.

H:S_{1}\times T^{*}M\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is a smooth 1-periodic Tonelli Hamilition on
(T^{*}M,\omega_{0}-\pi^{*}\sigma,R_{1})

with global flow.
Then the Hamilition system of
H
on
(T^{*}M,\omega_{0}-\pi^{*}\sigma)
admits infinitely
many brake orbits with period that is a power of
2
.

In particular, for the standard symplectic form
\omega_{0}
on
T^{*}M
and a Tonelli Hamiltonian
H
on

(T^{*}M,\omega_{0},R_{0})
, we have the following result which has been satated informally in
[
Lu09a
, Remark 6.1]
.

Corollary 1.2
.

Let
M
be a smooth closed manifold.

H:S_{1}\times T^{*}M\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is a smooth 1-periodic Tonelli Hamilition on
(T^{*}M,\omega_{0},R_{0})

with global flow.
Then the Hamilition system of
H
on
(T^{*}M,\omega_{0})
admits infinitely
many brake orbits with period that is a power of
2
.

Let
L:S_{1}\times TM\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be the dual Lagrangian of
H
. It is well known that the
Legendre transform sets up a one-to-one correspondence between the integer-periodic (brake) solutions of the Hamilition system
of
H
on
(T^{*}M,\omega_{0}-\pi^{*}\sigma)
and integer-periodic (symmetric) solutions of the Euler-Lagrange system of
L+\theta
on
M
, where
\theta

is a primitive of
\sigma
(see in Section
3
).
Therefore Theorem
1.1
can be equivalently stated in the Lagrangian formulation as follows.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
M
be a smooth closed manifold with a smooth exact magnetic form
\sigma=\text{d}\theta
,

L:S_{1}\times TM\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
a smooth 1-periodic Tonelli Lagrangian with global flow and

(L+\theta)(-t,q,-v)=(L+\theta)(t,q,v)
. Then the Euler-Lagrangian system of
L+\theta

admits infinitely many symmetric periodic solutions with period that is a power of
2
.

The proof of Theorem 1.3 is based on the method of
[
Lon00
]
. More precisely, let
a
be some
large number in
\mathbb{R}
, we assume by a
contradiction that there are only finite symmetric periodic solutions with mean Lagrangian action less than
a
,
then we deduce that there exists a symmetric periodic solution with the local Morse homology non-zero
under infinitly many iterations. This will contradict the homological
vanishing result based on a time-reversible Bangert homotopy defined in Section
6
.
As far as we know, the Bangert homotopy defined in
[
BK83
]
(see also

[
Lu09b
]
) may not be
a time-reversible one under any reparametrization. In Lemma
6.1
, we give a construction of the time-reversible
Bangert homotopy, which is used to the proof of the homological vanishing theorem.

Preliminaries

The symmetric loop space

We define
S_{\tau}:=\mathbb{R}/\tau\mathbb{Z},\,\forall\tau\in\mathbb{N}.
Let
M
be an
N
-dimensional
smooth manifold with Riemannian metric
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
.
We denote by
H(\tau):=W^{1,2}(S_{\tau},M)

with square-integrable weak
derivative.

Let
\gamma:S_{\tau}\rightarrow M

C^{\infty}

\tau
-periodic loop in

, and

\gamma^{*}TM\rightarrow S_{\tau}
be the pull-back of the tangent bundle

We denote by
W^{1,2}(\gamma^{*}TM)

\langle\!\langle\xi,\zeta\rangle\!\rangle_{\gamma}:=\int_{0}^{\tau}[\langle\xi(t),\zeta(t)\rangle_{\gamma(t)}+\langle\nabla_{t}\xi(t),\nabla_{t}\zeta(t)\rangle_{\gamma(t)}]\text{d}t,\ \xi,\zeta\in W^{1,2}(\gamma^{*}TM),

(2.1)

where

denotes the covariant derivative with respect to the Levi-Cicita connection on the Riemannian manifold
(M,\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle)
.
It is well known that
H(\tau)
is a Hilbert manifold and the tangent space
at
\gamma
is
W^{1,2}(\gamma^{*}TM)
, and (
2.1
) defines a Hilbert-Riemannian metric on
H(\tau)
.
In this case,
H(\tau)
turns out to be a complete Hilbert-Riemannian manifold (see
[
Kli78
]
).

If
\gamma\in H(\tau)
is time-reversible, i.e.,
\gamma(-t)=\gamma(t),\forall t\in\mathbb{R},

we call
\gamma
a symmetric loop. We follow the notation in
[
Lu09b
]
and denote by
EH(\tau)
the symmetric (even)
loop space consisting of time-reversible loops in
H(\tau)
.

Now we assume
\gamma\in EH(\tau)
is smooth. Let
EW^{1,2}(\gamma^{*}TM)
be the subspace of
W^{1,2}(\gamma^{*}TM)

consisting of time-reversible
W^{1,2}
-sections. Then
EW^{1,2}(\gamma^{*}TM)
is a

W^{1,2}(\gamma^{*}TM)
with the Hilbert structure defined in (

).
Let
\epsilon>0
be a constant smaller than

(M,\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle)
. We define
N_{\epsilon}:=\{v\in T_{q}M\,|\,q\in M,|v|_{q}<\epsilon\}

and
\gamma^{*}N_{\epsilon}:=\gamma^{*}TM\cap N_{\epsilon}
. Then
EW^{1,2}(\gamma^{*}N_{\epsilon})\subset EW^{1,2}(\gamma^{*}TM)
is an open set of sections along
\gamma
that
take values inside
N_{\epsilon}
.
we define a injective map

\exp_{\gamma}:EW^{1,2}(\gamma^{*}N_{\epsilon})\rightarrow H(\tau)

as

(\exp_{\gamma}\xi)(t):=\exp_{\gamma(t)}(\xi(t)),\quad\forall\xi\in EW^{1,2}(\gamma^{*}N_{\epsilon}).

It is easy to know that
(\exp_{\gamma}\xi)(-t)=(\exp_{\gamma}\xi)(t).
Let
\mathcal{U}_{\gamma}
be the image of
\exp_{\gamma}
,
the following natural atlas

\left\{(\exp_{\gamma}^{-1},\mathcal{U}_{\gamma}),\quad\text{for time-reversible}\ \gamma\in C^{\infty}(S_{\tau},M)\right\}

define the differentiable structure of
EH(\tau)
. If we restrict
\langle\!\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle\!\rangle

on
EH(\tau)
, (
2.1
) also defines a Hilbert-Riemannian metric on
EH(\tau)

and makes the latter into a complete Hilbert-Riemannian manifold.

For each
\gamma\in EH(\tau)
, we define a homotopy

\gamma_{s}
connecting
\gamma_{0}:=\gamma(0)
and
\gamma_{1}:=\gamma
as

\gamma_{s}(t):=\begin{cases}\gamma(st),&t\in[0,\tfrac{\tau}{2})\\
\gamma(s(\tau-t)),&t\in[\tfrac{\tau}{2},\tau].\end{cases}

Therefore
\gamma
is contractible and
EH(\tau)
is a subspace of the contractible loop space of

Let
S_{n\tau}:=\mathbb{R}/n\tau\mathbb{Z}
be the
n
-fold covering of
S_{\tau}.
We define the iteration map

\psi^{[n]}:H(\tau)\hookrightarrow H(n\tau)

such that
\gamma^{[n]}:=\psi^{[n]}(\gamma)
is defined as the composition of
\gamma
with the
n
-fold covering map of
S_{\tau}.

For each
\gamma\in H(\tau)
, it is easy to know that the differential

of

defines an iteration map

\text{d}\psi^{[n]}:W^{1,2}(\gamma^{*}TM)\hookrightarrow W^{1,2}(\gamma^{[n]*}TM),

, and

We notice that both the maps
\psi^{[n]}
and
\text{d}\psi^{[n]}
restrict as iteration maps
on
EH(\tau)
and

respectively.

Hamiltonian settings and Legendre transform

Let
H
be a smooth
\tau
-periodic Tonelli Hamiltonian on symplectic manifold

(T^{*}M,\omega_{0},R_{0})
.
The Tonelli conditions (H1)(H2) admits the global existence of the inverse Legendre teansform, given by

\mathcal{L}_{H}:S_{\tau}\times T^{*}M\rightarrow S_{\tau}\times TM,\;(t,q,p)\rightarrow(t,q,\partial_{p}H(t,q,p)).

\mathcal{L}_{H}
defines a fiber-preserving
C^{\infty}
-diffeomorphism.

In this case, the Fenchel transform of the Tonelli Hamiltonian
H:S_{1}\times T^{*}M\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, defined by

L(t,q,v):=\max\{p[v]-H(t,q,p)\,|\,p\in T_{q}^{*}M\},\quad\forall(t,q,v)\in S_{1}\times TM,

will be a
C^{\infty}
Lagrangian function and satisfies

L(t,q,v)=p(t,q,v)[v]-H(t,q,p(t,q,v)),

where
p=p(t,q,v)
is the unique point determined by the equality
v=\partial_{p}H(t,q,p)
. We refer the reader to

[
Fat08
]
for more details about Tonelli functions and the
properties of
Legendre transform.
It is easy to know that the dual Lagrangian
L
satisfies the following properties.

(L1) the fiberwise Hessian of
L
is positive definite, i.e.

\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}\frac{\partial^{2}L}{\partial v_{i}\partial v_{j}}(t,q,v)u_{i}u_{j}>0

for all
(t,q,v)\in S_{\tau}\times TM
and nonzero
u=\sum_{i=1}^{N}u_{i}\tfrac{\partial}{\partial v_{i}}\in T_{q}M.

(L2)
L
is fiberwise superlinear, i.e.

\lim_{|v|_{q}\rightarrow\infty}\frac{L(t,q,v)}{|v|_{q}}=\infty,

for all
(t,q)\in S_{\tau}\times M.

(L3)
L(-t,q,-v)=L(t,q,v),\,\forall(t,q,v)\in S_{\tau}\times TM.

(L1)(L2) are the Tonelli conditions of
L
and we say
L
the Tonelli Lagrangian.
We define the Euler-Lagrange system of
L
on the configuration space
M
by

\frac{\text{d}}{\text{d}t}\frac{\partial L}{\partial v_{j}}(t,\gamma(t),\dot{\gamma}(t))=\frac{\partial L}{\partial q_{j}}(t,\gamma(t),\dot{\gamma}(t)),\;j=1,\dots,N.

(2.2)

in any local coordinates
(q_{1},\dots,q_{N}).
It is well known that
the Euler-Lagrange flow
\Psi_{L}^{t}
is conjugated by
\mathcal{L}_{H}
to the Hamiltonian flow
\Psi_{H}^{t}
of
H
(see
[
Fat08
, Theorem 3.4.2]
).
Therefore, there is a one-to-one correspondence between the periodic solutions of the Euler-Lagrange system of

and the periodic solutions of the Hamiltonian system of

. In particular,
\gamma(t)
is a
\tau
-periodic symmetric solution of (

)
if and only if
x(t)=(\gamma(t),\partial_{v}L(t,\gamma(t),\dot{\gamma}(t)))
is a
\tau
-periodic brake orbit of
the Hamilition system (
1.1
)
with
H
satisfying (H1)(H2).

For each
\tau\in\mathbb{N}
, we define the mean action functional with respect to
L

H(\tau)
by

A^{[\tau]}(\gamma)=\frac{1}{\tau}\int_{0}^{\tau}L(t,\gamma(t),\dot{\gamma}(t))\text{d}t.

We denote by
EA^{[\tau]}:=A^{[\tau]}|_{EH(\tau)}.

A^{[\tau]}
and
EA^{[\tau]}

and

Each
\tau
-periodic symmetric solution

Let

-extremal of

we have

We rewrite
\xi=\tfrac{1}{2}(\xi+\Xi(\xi))+\tfrac{1}{2}(\xi-\Xi(\xi)):=\xi_{0}+\xi_{1}.
Since

\displaystyle dA^{[\tau]}(\gamma)[\xi]

\displaystyle=dA^{[\tau]}(\gamma)[\xi_{1}]

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{2}dA^{[\tau]}(\gamma)[\xi]-\tfrac{1}{2}dA^{[\tau]}(\gamma)[\Xi(\xi)]

. However,

It is noted that each Tonelli Lagrangian can be modified to be quadratically growth in

is the so-called convex quadratic-growth Lagrangian.

Morse index and Maslov-type index

We shall construct a

\displaystyle 0=\frac{\text{d}}{\text{d}t}(\phi^{t})^{*}\omega_{t}=(\phi^{t})^{*}\text{d}\left(i_{X_{t}}\omega_{t}-\pi^{*}\theta\right).

\displaystyle:=\max\{H(t,q,p)\ |\ (t,q,p)\in S_{1}\times T^{*}M,\,|p|_{q}\leq K+1\}

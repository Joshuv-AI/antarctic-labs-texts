On Ricci flows with closed and smooth tangent flows
Pak-Yeung Chan, Zilu Ma, Yongjia Zhang

Abstract
In this paper, we consider Ricci flows admitting closed and smooth tangent flows in the sense of Bamler [ Bam20c ] . The tangent flow in question can be either a tangent flow at infinity for an ancient Ricci flow, or a tangent flow at a singular point for a Ricci flow developing a finite-time singularity. Among other things, we prove: (1) that in these cases the tangent flow must be unique, (2) that if a Ricci flow with finite-time singularity has a closed singularity model, then the singularity is of Type I and the singularity model is the tangent flow at the singular point; this answers a question proposed in [ CCGGIIKLLN10 ] , (3) a dichotomy theorem that characterizes ancient Ricci flows admitting a closed and smooth backward sequential limit.

Introduction

In
[
Bam20a
]
—
[
Bam20c
]
, Bamler has established a theory for the weak limits (
\mathbb{F}
-limits) of Ricci flows on closed manifolds. These limit spaces are metric flows called
singular spaces
. The theory of singular spaces bears many similarities with several other geometric analysis theories such as the minimal surface, the Brakke flow, and the Cheeger-Colding theory. Among many other things, one important result proved in
[
Bam20c
]
is the codimension-
4
theorem for the singular space. Similar to the theorem of Cheeger-Naber
[
CN15
]
, the singularity of a singular space also has parabolic (space-time) Minkowski codimension no smaller than
4

[
Bam20c
, Theorem 2.3]
.

Another important theme in the study of Bamler’s singular space is the uniqueness of
tangent flow
, as in the cases of the mean curvature flow, the minimal surface, and the Cheeger-Colding theory (in the latter two cases, the counterpart of tangent flow is called
tangent cone
). In the field of minimal surface, White
[
W83
]
proved that a
2
-dimensional minimizing current has unique tangent cone everywhere, Allard-Almgren
[
AA81
]
proved the uniqueness of tangent cone under some integrability condition for the cross section, Simon
[
S83
]
proved that if a tangent cone at a point on a minimal surface is smooth, then it is the unique tangent cone at that point. In Cheeger-Colding theory, Colding-Minicozzi
[
CM14
]
proved that a tangent cone at a point on a
noncollapsed
Ricci limit space is the unique tangent cone at that point if the cone has
a smooth
cross section. In the mean curvature flow, Colding-Minicozzi
[
CM15
]
proved that if a tangent flow at a point is a cylinder, then it is the unique tangent flow at that point. We would like to draw the reader’s attention to a recent post of Colding-Minicozzi
[
CM21
]
. Their method, as is claimed in that paper, can be applied to show that cylindrical singularities in the Ricci flow are isolated singularities, and they will prove that this type of blow-up limits must be unique in a forthcoming paper.

In all these fields, it is also interesting to consider the tangent cone/flow at infinity of a noncompact minimal surface/Ricci limit space or an ancient mean curvature flow/singular space. For instance, Cheeger-Tian
[
CT94
]
proved the uniqueness of tangent cone at infinity for Ricci flat manifolds with Euclidean volume growth under certain curvature
and integrability assumptions. This result was later strengthened by
Colding-Minicozzi
[
CM14
]
. For an ancient singular space, Bamler
[
Bam20c
]
has considered the tangent flow at infinity and has shown that it is, as in the case of the tangent flow at one point, a shrinking metric soliton. The authors
[
CMZ21
]
proved that if Bamler’s tangent flow at infinity is smooth, then it is not different from Perelman’s asymptotic shrinking gradient Ricci soliton.

In this article, we shall study both the tangent flow at infinity for an ancient Ricci flow and the tangent flow at a singular point for a Ricci flow developing a finite-time singularity. One of our main results is that, in either of the two cases above, if the tangent flow is smooth and closed, then this tangent flow is the unique one, and hence independent of the choice of the scaling factors or the base point (in the case of a Ricci flow forming a finite-time singularity, the base point should really be understood as the choice of the
singular conjugate heat kernel
; see Section 2 for the definition).

Our first main results are:

Theorem 1.1

(Uniqueness of smooth and closed tangent flow at infinity)
.

Let
(M^{n},g_{t})_{t\in(-\infty,0]}
be an ancient solution satisfying (
1.2
), where
M
is a closed manifold. Assume that there is a sequence of scaling factors
\{\tau_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}

\tau_{i}\nearrow\infty

\left((M,\tau_{i}^{\prime-1}g_{\tau_{i}^{\prime}t})_{t\in-(\infty,0]},(\nu_{p_{1},t_{1}|\tau_{i}^{\prime}t})_{t\in-(\infty,0]}\right)\xrightarrow{\makebox[28.45274pt]{$\mathbb{F}$}}\left(\mathcal{X}^{\infty},(\mu^{\infty}_{t})_{t\in-(\infty,0)}\right).

In other words, the tangent flow at infinity is independent of the sequence of scaling factors and the base point.

Theorem 1.2

(Uniqueness of smooth and closed tangent flow at singular point)
.

Let
(M^{n},g_{t})_{t\in[-T,0)}
be a Ricci flow on a closed manifold
M
which develops a finite-time singularity at
t=0
. Assume that there exist a sequence
\{\tau_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
with
\tau_{i}\searrow 0
such that the limit flow
\mathcal{X}^{\infty}
in (
1.1
) is a smooth Ricci flow on a closed manifold. Then for any sequence
\{\tau_{i}^{\prime}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
with
\tau_{i}^{\prime}\searrow 0

\left((M,\tau_{i}^{\prime-1}g_{\tau_{i}^{\prime}t})_{t\in[-T\tau_{i}^{\prime-1},0)},(\mu^{\prime}_{\tau_{i}^{\prime}t})_{t\in[-T\tau_{i}^{\prime-1},0)}\right)\xrightarrow{\makebox[28.45274pt]{$\mathbb{F}$}}\left(\mathcal{X}^{\infty},(\mu^{\infty}_{t})_{t\in-(\infty,0)}\right).

In regard of the ancient Ricci flow in Theorem
1.1
, one may rightly ask whether the ancient solution in question is the same as the asymptotic soliton itself. In fact, this question is answered by Kröncke
[
Kr15
]
: it depends on the stability of the asymptotic soliton. If the closed asymptotic soliton is stable, then the ancient Ricci flow must be the same as this soliton. However, there exists nontrivial (that is, non-self-similar) ancient solution flowing out of an unstable closed soliton. For other results related to Theorem
1.2
, refer to
[
Ac12
]
and
[
Se06
]
.

Our major technique of proving the above two results is a Łojaciewicz inequality proved by Sun-Wang
[
SW15
]
. This Łojaciewicz inequality shows that, once the Riemannian metric evolving by a
modified
(forward or backward) Ricci flow becomes too close to a shrinking gradient Ricci soliton, then it will stay close to this soliton for all the time; if this soliton is also a sequential limit, then the modified flow converges to the soliton at polynomial rate. The following corollaries follow immediately from this technique.

Corollary 1.3
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
1.1
, the limit flow
\left(\mathcal{X}^{\infty},(\mu^{\infty}_{t})_{t\in-(\infty,0)}\right)
is the canonical form of a shrinking gradient Ricci soliton
(M^{n},g_{o},f_{o})
, where
\mu_{t}^{\infty}
is the conjugate heat flow constructed using
f_{o}
. Furthermore, the modified backward Ricci flow converges to
g_{o}
at polynomial rate. More precisely, for all
t\ll-1
, there is a self-diffmorphism
\phi_{t}:M^{n}\to M^{n}
, evolving smoothly in
t
, such that

\left\|\,\tfrac{1}{|t|}\phi_{t}^{*}g_{t}-g_{o}\right\|_{C^{k,\gamma}_{g_{o}}}\leq C(\log(-t))^{-\beta},

where
k\gg 1
,
\gamma,\beta\in(0,1)
, and
C<\infty
.

Corollary 1.4
.

Under assumptions of Theorem
1.2
, the limit flow
\left(\mathcal{X}^{\infty},(\mu^{\infty}_{t})_{t\in-(\infty,0)}\right)
is the canonical form of a shrinking gradient Ricci soliton
(M^{n},g_{o},f_{o})
, where
\mu^{\infty}_{t}
is the conjugate heat flow constructed using
f_{o}
. Furthermore, the modified Ricci flow converges to
g_{o}
at a polynomial rate. More precisely, for all
t
close enough to
0
, there is a self-diffmorphism
\phi_{t}:M^{n}\to M^{n}
, evolving smoothly in
t
, such that

\left\|\,\tfrac{1}{|t|}\phi_{t}^{*}g_{t}-g_{o}\right\|_{C^{k,\gamma}_{g_{o}}}\leq C(-\log(-t))^{-\beta},

where
k\gg 1
,
\gamma,\beta\in(0,1)
, and
C<\infty
.

A more general and difficult question is whether one can prove the uniqueness of the tangent flow (either at a point or at infinity) without the assumptions in Theorem
1.1
or Theorem
1.2
. In fact, by definition, it is easy to observe that the
uniqueness of tangent flow at infinity
consists of two statements: (1) that the tangent flow is independent of the base point, (2) that the tangent flow is independent of the sequence of scaling factors. As mentioned earlier, Bamler’s tangent flow at infinity resembles the asymptotic cone of some static spaces. It is well-known that the asymptotic cone of a boundedly compact metric space does not depend on the base point; see, e.g.,
[
BBI01
, Proposition 8.2.8]
.
Here we present a parallel result, which also generalizes the authors’ previous result
[
CMZ21
, Theorem 8.1]
. Due to the technicality of the
\mathbb{F}
-convergence, the proof of the this theorem is significantly more complicated than the parallel case of asymptotic cone, although they are in the same spirit. We are not yet able to show that the tangent flow at infinity is independent of the scaling factors.

Theorem 1.5
.

The tangent flow at infinity of an ancient
H
-concentrated (c.f.
[
Bam20b
, Definition 3.30]
) metric flow does not depend on the base point.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
1.1
or Theorem
1.2
, the limit in (
1.1
) is smooth, and hence the
\mathbb{F}
-convergence is automatically smooth convergence according to
[
Bam20c
, Theorem 2.5]
. Note that the scaling in (
1.1
) is a Type I scaling. We are indeed able to further weaken this condition by a noncollapsing argument of Bamler
[
Bam20a
, Theorem 6.2]
. More precisely, we show that any Ricci flow admitting a closed forward/backward limit must be a forward/backward Type I Ricci flow, and hence satisfies all the results established thus far. In particular, we
do not
assume that the scaled limit is obtained from a Type I scaling process. The following two theorems are stronger versions of all the results stated above, hence we will be focusing on them in most part of this paper.

The technique implemented in proving Theorem

A Riemannian manifold

{\rm Vol}(B(x,r))\geq\kappa r^{n},\quad\text{ whenever }\sup_{B(x,r)}R\leq r^{-2}.

A Ricci flow

such that each time slice

(A dichotomy for ancient Ricci flows with closed backward limits)
.

Let
(M^{n},g_{t})_{t\in(-\infty,0]}
be an ancient Ricci flow, where
M

\displaystyle\left(M^{n},Q_{i}g_{t_{i}}\right)\longrightarrow(M^{n},g_{o}),

(1)

Preliminaries

2.1
Perelman’s entropy and Nash entropy

Let
(M^{n},g)
be a closed Riemannian manifold, Perelman
[
Per02
]
defined the following functionals:

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}(g,f,\tau)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\int_{M}\big(\tau(|\nabla f|^{2}+R)+f-n\big)(4\pi\tau)^{-\frac{n}{2}}e^{-f}dg,\quad\tau>0,

\displaystyle\mu(g,\tau)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\inf\left\{\mathcal{W}(g,f,\tau)\,\bigg|\,\int_{M}(4\pi\tau)^{-\frac{n}{2}}e^{-f}dg=1\right\},

\displaystyle\nu(g,\tau)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\inf_{0<s\leq\tau}\mu(g,\tau),\quad\nu(g)\ :=\ \inf_{\tau>0}\mu(g,\tau).

It is well-known that
\mu(g,\tau)
is the logarithmic Sobolev constant at scale
\tau
and
\nu(g)
is the Sobolev constant.

Let
(M^{n},g_{t})_{t\in[-T,0)}
be a Ricci flow, where
M
is a closed manifold. Let
(p_{0},t_{0})\in M^{n}\times(-T,0)
be a fixed point in the space-time and let

v(\cdot,t):=(4\pi(t_{0}-t))^{-\frac{n}{2}}e^{-f(\cdot,t)}

be the conjugate heat kernel based at
(p_{0},t_{0})
. We shall follow Bamler
[
Bam20a
]
and call the evolving probability measure

\nu_{p_{0},t_{0}\,|\,t}(A):=\int_{A}v(\cdot,t)dg_{t}\quad\text{ for all measurable }\quad A\subset M

the
conjugate heat kernel
based at
(p_{0},t_{0})
as well. More generally, one may also use an arbitrary positive solution to the conjugate heat equation with unit integral to construct an evolving probability measure, and we shall call such an evolving probability measure a
conjugate heat flow
. Then Perelman’s entropy and the Nash entropy are respectively defined as

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}_{p_{0},t_{0}}(\tau)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}(g_{t_{0}-\tau},f(\cdot,t_{0}-\tau),\tau),

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{p_{0},t_{0}}(\tau)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\int_{M}f(\cdot,t_{0}-\tau)\,d\nu_{p_{0},t_{0}\,|\,t_{0}-\tau}-\frac{n}{2}.

Perelman’s monotonicity formula implies that both
\mathcal{W}_{p_{0},t_{0}}(\tau)
and
\mathcal{N}_{p_{0},t_{0}}(\tau)
are decreasing in
\tau
. Indeed, we have

\displaystyle\frac{d}{d\tau}\mathcal{W}_{p_{0},t_{0}}(\tau)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2\int_{M}\tau\left|\Ric+\nabla^{2}f-\frac{1}{2\tau}g\right|^{2}(\cdot,t_{0}-\tau)\,d\nu_{p_{0},t_{0}\,|\,t_{0}-\tau},

(2.1)

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}_{p_{0},t_{0}}(\tau)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{p_{0},t_{0}}(\tau)\leq 0\quad\text{ for all }\quad\tau>0.

If
t=0
is a singular time of the Ricci flow, by
[
MM15
]
, we may let
t_{i}\nearrow 0
and consider a sequence of conjugate heat kernels based at
(p_{0},t_{i})
. If
M^{n}
is closed, then, after passing to a subsequence, we obtain a “singular” conjugate heat kernel

v(\cdot,t):=(4\pi|t|)^{-\frac{n}{2}}e^{-f(\cdot,t)},\quad d\nu_{p_{0},0\,|\,t}:=v(\cdot,t)dg_{t}.

Obviously,
\nu_{p_{0},0\,|\,t}
is still a probability measure for each
t\in[-T,0)
. Hence, we may use this singular conjugate heat kernel to define
\mathcal{W}_{p_{0},0}(\tau)
and
\mathcal{N}_{p_{0},0}(\tau)
as well, and one may easily verify that (
2.1
) still holds in this case. Note that the singular conjugate heat kernel based at a fixed point is not necessarily unique.

2.2
Forward and backward modified Ricci flow

For any closed Riemannian manifold
(M^{n},g)
, we define

\displaystyle\mu_{g}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\mu(g,1)=\inf\left\{\mathcal{W}(g,f,1)\,\bigg|\,\int_{M}(4\pi)^{-\frac{n}{2}}e^{-f}dg=1\right\}.

Although the minimizer of
\mathcal{W}(g,\cdot,1)
always exists, it is not necessarily unique (see
[
CCGGIIKLLN10
, Lemma 17.22]
). If the minimizer is unique, we shall denote it by
f_{g}
. By a straightforward computation, the
L^{2}
gradient of the
\mu_{(\cdot)}
functional is

\nabla\mu_{g}=-2\left(\Ric_{g}+\nabla^{2}f_{g}-\frac{1}{2}g\right).

The gradient flow of
\mu_{g}

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}g_{t}=-2\left(\Ric_{g_{t}}+\nabla^{2}f_{g_{t}}-\frac{1}{2}g_{t}\right)

is called the
modified Ricci flow
. We shall also define the
backward modified Ricci flow
as

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}g_{t}=2\left(\Ric_{g_{t}}+\nabla^{2}f_{g_{t}}-\frac{1}{2}g_{t}\right)

In fact, given arbitrary initial data, the modified Ricci flow does not always exist, but when it exists, it is the composite of a Ricci flow with a one-parameter family of diffeomorphism and a time-scaling. Obviously, along the forward/backward modified Ricci flow,
\mu_{g(t)}
is monotonically increasing/decreasing.

2.3
Regular neighborhood and Łojaciewicz inequality

In this subsection we introduce some important techniques introduced by Sun-Wang
[
SW15
]
, especially the Łojaciewicz inequality for the Ricci flow. Let
(M^{n},g_{o},f_{o})
be a compact Ricci shrinker, normalized in the way that

\displaystyle\Ric_{g_{o}}+\nabla^{2}f_{o}=\frac{1}{2}g_{o},

(2.2)

\displaystyle\int_{M}(4\pi)^{-\frac{n}{2}}e^{-f_{o}}dg_{o}=1.

Then
f_{o}\equiv f_{g_{o}}
is the unique minimizer of
\mathcal{W}(g_{o},\cdot\,,1)
. Letting
\mathfrak{R}(M)
be the space of Riemannian metrics on
M
,
k\,\in\mathbb{N}
,
\gamma\in(0,1)
, the
C^{k,\gamma}
neighborhood of
g_{o}
is defined to be

\mathcal{V}^{k,\gamma}_{\delta}:=\left\{g\in\mathfrak{R}(M)\,\Big|\,\|g-g_{o}\|_{C^{k,\gamma}_{g_{o}}}\leq\delta\right\}.

Sung-Wang
[
SW15
]
proved the following Łojaciewicz inequality:

Theorem 2.1

(Lemma 3.1 in
[
SW15
]
)
.

There is a
C^{k,\gamma}
(
k\gg 1
) neighborhood
\mathcal{U}
of
g_{o}
, called a
regular neighborhood
, such that for all
g\in\mathcal{U}
, there is a unique
f_{g}
, and the map
P:f\mapsto f_{g}
is analytic on
\mathcal{U}
. Furthermore, there are constants
C>0
and
\alpha\in[\frac{1}{2},1)
, such that for any
g\in\mathcal{U}
, we have

\|\nabla\mu_{g}\|_{L^{2}_{g}}\geq C|\mu_{g_{o}}-\mu_{g}|^{\alpha},

where
\|\,\cdot\,\|_{L^{2}_{g}}
is the
L^{2}
norm taken with respect to
g
.

The following theorem is a consequence of the above Łojaciewic inequality.

Theorem 2.2

(Lemma 3.2 in
[
SW15
]
)
.

Suppose
(M^{n},g_{o},f_{o})
is a closed Ricci shrinker normalized as in (
2.2
).
Let
(M^{n},g_{t})_{t\in[0,T)}
be a modified Ricci flow

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}g_{t}=-2\left(\Ric_{g_{t}}+\nabla^{2}f_{g_{t}}-\tfrac{1}{2}g_{t}\right),

where
T\in(0,\infty]
and
[0,T)
is the maximum interval of existence for
g_{t}
. Furthermore, assume
\mu_{g_{t}}\leq\mu_{g_{o}}
for all
t\in[0,T)
. Then for all
\varepsilon>0
small enough, there is
\delta\in(0,\varepsilon)
, such that if
g_{0}\in\mathcal{V}^{k+10,\gamma}_{\delta}\subset\mathcal{U}
, then

g_{t}\in\mathcal{V}^{k,\gamma}_{\varepsilon}\subset\mathcal{U}
for all
t\in[0,T)
. If this ever happens, and if, in addition,
T=\infty
, then we also have that
g_{t}
converges in the
C^{k,\gamma}_{g_{o}}
norm to a limit
g_{\infty}
, which is also a shrinker metric in
\mathcal{U}
with
\mu_{g_{o}}=\mu_{g_{\infty}}
. The convergence is in a polynomial rate, that is

\left\|g_{\infty}-g_{t}\right\|_{C^{k,\gamma}_{g_{o}}}\leq Ct^{-\beta}\quad\text{ for all }\quad t\geq 1,

where
\beta
is a constant depending only on
\alpha
in the statement of Theorem
2.1
.

Note that the above theorem is slightly weaker than the original statement of
[
SW15
, Theorem 3.2, Theorem 3.3]
, since we have stated it in the way corresponding to the backward version below, which can be proved by using the same technique, i.e. the Łojasiewicz argument. The proof of Theorem
2.3
is not essentially different from that of Theorem
2.2
. Nevertheless, for the convenience of the reader, we have included its proof in the appendix. Note that in Theorem
2.2
, it is necessary to assume
g_{0}\in\mathcal{V}^{k+10,\gamma}_{\delta}
in stead of
g_{0}\in\mathcal{V}^{k,\gamma}_{\delta}
, since the forward short-time stability (i.e.

for a short time) requires higher regularity of

.

Theorem 2.3

(Backward version)
.

Suppose that
(M^{n},g_{o},f_{o})
is a closed Ricci shrinker normalized as in (

).
Let

where

is the maximum interval of existence for

. Furthermore, assume

for all

The convergence is in a polynomial rate, that is

\left\|g_{\infty}-g_{t}\right\|_{C^{k,\gamma}_{g_{o}}}\leq Ct^{-\beta}\quad\text{ for all }\quad t\geq 1,

2.4
A noncollapsing estimate of Bamler

The following theorem of Bamler shows that for a Ricci flow with bounded Nash entropy, the volume of a disk around an
H_{n}
-center cannot become too collapsed. Let
(M^{n},g_{t})_{t\in I}
be a Ricci flow, where
M
is a closed manifold. Let
(\nu_{x_{0},t_{0}\,|\,t})_{t<t_{0}}
be the conjugate heat kernel based at
(x_{0},t_{0})
.
An
H_{n}
-center
(z,t)
of
(x_{0},t_{0})
, where
t<t_{0}
, can be understood as the point around which
\nu_{x_{0},t_{0}\,|\,t}
accumulates its mass. An important fact we shall used concerning an
H_{n}
-center is that
it always exists
; see
[
Bam20a
]
for more details.

Theorem 2.4

(Theorem 6.2 in
[
Bam20a
]
)
.

Let
(M^{n},g_{t})_{t\in I}
be a Ricci flow on a closed manifold. Let
t\in I
and
r>0
such that
[t-r^{2},t]\subset I
. Assume that
(z,t-r^{2})\in M\times I
is an
H_{n}
-center of some
(x,t)\in M\times I
. If
R_{g_{t-r^{2}}}\geq R_{\operatorname{min}}
, then we have

\operatorname{Vol}_{g_{t-r^{2}}}\left(B_{g_{t-r^{2}}}\left(z,\sqrt{2H_{n}}r\right)\right)\geq c(R_{\operatorname{min}}r^{2})\exp(\mathcal{N}_{x,t}(r^{2}))r^{n},

where
c(R_{\operatorname{min}}r^{2})=c(n)\exp(-2(n-2R_{\operatorname{min}}r^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}})
and

.

When introducing the notion of tangent flow, we implement the same notations as in

for any

. We can then define

\mathcal{T}_{x_{0}}^{\infty}:=\{\text{ tangent flows at infinity based at }x_{0}\},

more precisely as follows. Note that in the statement of Theorem

, the base points

and

.

Suppose that

Type I bounds of the scaling factors

In this section, we shall show that under the conditions of Theorem
1.6
or Theorem
1.7
, the scaling factors must have the following Type I bounds.

Proposition 3.1
.

Under the assumptions of either Theorem
1.6
or Theorem
1.7
, we have

c\leq|t_{i}|Q_{i}\leq C\quad\text{ for all }\quad i\in\mathbb{N},

(3.1)

where

are constants independent of

Proof.

Let us first of all consider the case of Theorem
1.7
, and the case of Theorem
1.6
is similar.

The lower bound is straightforward. By the

convergence (
1.4

Q_{i}^{-1}\max_{M}\left|{\Rm_{g_{t_{i}}}}\right|=\max_{M}\left|\Rm_{Q_{i}g_{t_{i}}}\right|\longrightarrow\max_{M}|{\Rm_{g_{o}}}|.

On the other hand, by applying the maximum principle to the inequality

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}|{\Rm}|-\Delta|{\Rm}|\leq 8|{\Rm}|^{2},

we have

\displaystyle\left(\max_{M}|{\Rm_{g_{t}}}|\right)^{-1}\leq c_{0}^{-1}+8|t|\quad\text{ for all }\quad t<0,

(3.2)

where
\displaystyle c_{0}:=\limsup_{t\to 0}\max_{M}|\Rm_{g_{t}}|\in(0,\infty]
.
Hence, in the case of Theorem

|t|\max_{M}|{\Rm_{g_{t}}}|\geq\frac{1}{8}
for all

We shall then prove the upper bound in (
3.1
) by contradiction. By possibly passing to a subsequence, we assume that

\lim_{i\to\infty}Q_{i}|t_{i}|=\infty.

(3.3)

By the

convergence (
1.4
) again, we have

\lim_{i\to\infty}\operatorname{Vol}(M,Q_{i}g_{t_{i}})=\operatorname{Vol}(M,g_{o})\in(0,\infty).

(3.4)

Hence we have

\displaystyle\lim_{i\to\infty}\operatorname{Vol}(M,|t_{i}|^{-1}g_{t_{i}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{i\to\infty}\operatorname{Vol}\Big(M,(Q_{i}|t_{i}|)^{-1}Q_{i}g_{t_{i}}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{i\to\infty}\frac{\operatorname{Vol}(M,Q_{i}g_{t_{i}})}{(Q_{i}|t_{i}|)^{\frac{n}{2}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0,

where we have applied both (
3.3
) and (
3.4
). This is equavalent to

\lim_{i\to\infty}\frac{\operatorname{Vol}(M,g_{t_{i}})}{|t_{i}|^{\frac{n}{2}}}=0.

(3.5)

Next, we fix a point
p_{0}\in M
. For any
i\in\mathbb{N}
, let
(p_{i},t_{i})\in M\times[-T,0)
be an

-center of

. By Perelman’s monotonicity formula, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{p_{0},\frac{t_{i}}{2}}(r^{2})\geq\mathcal{W}_{p_{0},\frac{t_{i}}{2}}(r^{2})\geq\mu(g_{t_{i}},r^{2})\geq\mu\big(g_{-T},r^{2}+t_{i}+T\big)\geq\nu(g_{-T},2T)>-\infty.

Note that
\nu(g_{-T},2T)
is a constant depending only on the geometry of

. Since

, we may, without loss of generality, assume that

for all

. The maximum principle implies that

R_{g_{t_{i}}}\geq\inf_{M}R_{g_{-T/2}}\geq-\tfrac{n}{T}.

We may then apply Theorem
2.4
with
(x,t)=(p_{0},\frac{t_{i}}{2})

r=\sqrt{\frac{|t_{i}|}{2}}\leq\sqrt{\frac{T}{2}}
,

\displaystyle\operatorname{Vol}_{g_{t_{i}}}\left(B_{g_{t_{i}}}(p_{i},\sqrt{2H_{n}}r)\right)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle c(n)\exp\left(-2(n-2R_{\operatorname{min}}r^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)\cdot\exp\left(\mathcal{N}_{p_{0},\frac{t_{i}}{2}}(r^{2})\right)\cdot r^{n}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle c(n)\exp\left(-2\left(n+\tfrac{n}{T}\cdot\left(\sqrt{\tfrac{T}{2}}\right)^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)\cdot\exp\Big(\nu(g(-T),2T)\Big)\cdot r^{n}

\displaystyle c_{0}|t_{i}|^{\frac{n}{2}},

where

is a constant independent of

Let us now consider the case of Theorem

4

In this section we study the geometric properties of the limit metric

Lemma 4.1

Under the assumption of Theorem
1.6
, Theorem
1.7
, and Theorem
1.8

\displaystyle\lim_{i\to\infty}\max_{M}\left|\Rm_{g_{i,0}}\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\max_{M}\left|\Rm_{g_{o}}\right|<\infty

\displaystyle\lim_{i\to\infty}\Vol(M,g_{i,0})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Vol(M,g_{o})>0,

\displaystyle\lim_{i\to\infty}\operatorname{diam}(M,g_{i,0})

\displaystyle\operatorname{diam}(M,g_{o})<\infty.

Proposition 4.2

1.6

\displaystyle g_{i,t}

\displaystyle|t_{i}|^{-1}g_{|t_{i}|t},

\displaystyle v_{i}(\cdot,t)

\displaystyle(4\pi|t|)^{-\frac{n}{2}}e^{-f_{i}(\cdot,t)}=|t_{i}|^{\frac{n}{2}}v(\cdot,|t_{i}|t).

\displaystyle-2\int_{-(1+\epsilon)}^{-(1-\epsilon)}\int_{M}|t|\left|\Ric_{g_{i,t}}+\nabla^{2}_{g_{i,t}}f_{i}(\cdot,t)-\frac{1}{2|t|}g_{i,t}\right|^{2}v_{i}\,dg_{i,t}dt,

Lemma A.1

Proof.

Since the backward modified Ricci flow and the backward Ricci flow differ only by a one-parameter family of diffeomorphisms and a time scaling, we can consider

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}\tilde{g}_{t}&=\ \psi_{t}^{*}g_{t},\\
\partial_{t}\psi_{t}&=\ -\nabla_{g_{t}}f_{g_{t}}\circ\psi_{t},\\

Note that the existence of

\tilde{g}
is a normalized backward Ricci flow satisfying

Applying Bamler’s backward pseudolocality at

to

, we have that, there is an

, such that

. Therefore, taking
\eta_{1}\leq\overline{\eta}_{1}(\eta_{0},\delta)

\left\|\Ric_{g_{t}}+\nabla^{2}_{g_{t}}f_{g_{t}}-\frac{1}{2}g_{t}\right\|_{C^{l,\gamma}_{g_{t}}}=\left\|\Ric_{\tilde{g}_{t}}+\nabla^{2}_{\tilde{g}_{t}}f_{\tilde{g}_{t}}-\frac{1}{2}\tilde{g}_{t}\right\|_{C^{l,\gamma}_{\tilde{g}_{t}}}\leq C(l),

for all

Appendix B
\nu
-functional of closed manifolds with positive scalar curvature

The following proposition is a straightforward and well-understood consequence of the Sobolev inequality. Since we have not found it in the literature, we shall include the proof for the convenience of the reader.

Proposition B.1
.

Let
(M^{n},g)
be a closed Riemannian manifold satisfying the following Sobolev inequality

\displaystyle\left(\int_{M}|u|^{\frac{2n}{n-2}}dg\right)^{\frac{n-2}{n}}\leq A\int_{M}|\nabla u|^{2}dg+B\int_{M}u^{2}dg\quad\text{ for all }\quad u\in W^{1,2}(M).

Furthermore, assume
R\geq c_{0}>0
everywhere on
M
. Then we have

\nu(g)\geq-C,

where
C
is a constant depending only on
n
,
A
,
B
, and
c_{0}
.

Proof.

It is well-known that a Sobolev inequality is equivalent to a family of logarithmic Sobolev inequalities; see, for instance,
[
Zhq11
, Theorem 4.2.1]
. Hence, for all
\epsilon>0
and for all
v\in W^{1,2}(M)
with
\int_{M}v^{2}dg=1
we have

\displaystyle\int_{M}v^{2}\log v^{2}dg\leq\epsilon^{2}\int_{M}|\nabla v|^{2}dg-\frac{n}{2}\log\epsilon^{2}+BA^{-1}\epsilon^{2}+\frac{n}{2}\log\frac{nA}{2e}.

By the lower bound of
R
, we have
\displaystyle\int_{M}Rv^{2}dg\geq c_{0}
. Furthermore, we can always take
A
to be larger. Hence we may assume that
4B(Ac_{0})^{-1}\leq 1
. Then, for all
v\in W^{1,2}(M)
with
\int_{M}v^{2}dg=1
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{M}v^{2}\log v^{2}dg

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{4}\left(\int_{M}4|\nabla v^{2}|dg+4BA^{-1}\right)-\frac{n}{2}\log\epsilon^{2}+\frac{n}{2}\log\frac{nA}{2e}.

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{4}\left(\int_{M}4|\nabla v^{2}|dg+4B(Ac_{0})^{-1}\int_{M}Rv^{2}dg\right)-\frac{n}{2}\log\left(4\pi\cdot\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{4}\right)+\frac{n}{2}\log\frac{nA\pi}{2e}.

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\tau\int_{M}\left(4|\nabla v|^{2}+Rv^{2}\right)dg-\frac{n}{2}\log(4\pi\tau)-n+C(n,A,B,c_{0}),

where we have let
\tau:=\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{4}
. This obviously is equivalent to

\mu(g,\tau)\geq-C(n,A,B,c_{0})

and the conclusion follows.

Flexibility and rigidity of frameworks consisting of triangles and parallelograms
Georg Grasegger, Jan Legerský

Abstract
A framework, which is a (possibly infinite) graph with a realization of its vertices in the plane, is called flexible if it can be continuously deformed while preserving the edge lengths. We focus on flexibility of frameworks in which 4-cycles form parallelograms. For the class of frameworks considered in this paper (allowing triangles), we prove that the following are equivalent: flexibility, infinitesimal flexibility, the existence of at least two classes of an equivalence relation based on 3- and 4-cycles and being a non-trivial subgraph of the Cartesian product of graphs. We study the algorithmic aspects and the rotationally symmetric version of the problem. The results are illustrated on frameworks obtained from tessellations by regular polygons.

When we take a look at a scaffold from the front we see a rectangular grid with a certain amount of diagonal bars. Scaffolds are intended to be rigid. From the mathematical point of view such a construction can be considered as a graph where the bars of the scaffold form the edges and their joints are the vertices.

Such a graph together with a placement of the vertices in the plane or in space gives a framework.
A framework is called flexible if there is a non-trivial flex (a deformation of the placement preserving the distances between adjacent vertices that is not induced by a rigid motion). Otherwise it is called rigid.
In this paper, all placements are in the plane.

There is an amount of papers, see below, dealing with the question
where to put diagonal bars in a grid construction in order to make it rigid.
In this paper we go a step further and allow the grid to be more general.
In particular we allow non-rectangular parallelograms and triangles in the the underlying graph
that do not come from inserting a diagonal. We show when such a framework is flexible
and give an algorithm for finding a parametrization of a flex.
We apply the theory to rotationally symmetric frameworks and illustrate it on periodic tilings by regular polygons.

\minisec

Our contribution
In this paper we define a class of frameworks that contains all frameworks mentioned above
as well as others (see
Figure

2
), called
walk-independent frameworks
.
We consider both infinitesimal and continuous flexibility.

Figure 2
:
A framework whose flexibility can be described by the presented results.

Instead of constructing an auxiliary graph whose connectivity would characterize flexibility of a given framework,
we partition the edges into equivalent classes where two
edges are in the same class whenever they are opposite in a parallelogram or
when they are part of the same triangle.
The classes have the property that any two edges in the same class keep their mutual angle during any flex,
hence, we call them
angle-preserving classes
.
Comparing to the previous cases, each row/column/ribbon is a subset of an angle-preserving class
and two of them are connected by a path in the corresponding auxiliary graphs
if and only if they belong to the same angle-preserving class.
Namely, the disconnectedness of an auxiliary graph is equivalent to the number of angle-preserving classes being at least two.

Our main result is that a walk-independent framework is flexible if and
only if it has at least two angle-preserving classes.
We show how to construct a flex using angle-preserving classes, give an algorithm to find the classes and analyze the algorithm’s complexity.
The classes can be used also for determining which braces could be added to enforce rigidity:
two classes are merged when a diagonal of a 4-cycle intersecting both classes is added.
Similarly to the literature a relation to NAC-colorings can be established but
this paper can be read without knowing them.
The flexibility is also related to non-trivial subgraphs of the Cartesian product of graphs.
We define a class of graphs which can be seen simply connected
when considering 3- and 4-cycles as faces.
We show that every injective placement of such a graph with all 4-cycles being parallelograms is walk-independent,
hence our result applies.

The result is further extended to rotationally symmetric flexes which are related to respective symmetric versions of classes and colorings.
Finally we apply the theory to periodic tilings of the plane.

1.2
Relation to NAC-colorings

NAC-colorings have been defined in
[
12
]
as a combinatorial property of graphs that have flexible frameworks.
We show here how angle-preserving classes can be connected to NAC-colorings.

Definition 1.9
.

Let
G
be a graph, possibly countably infinite.
A coloring of edges
\delta\colon E_{G}\rightarrow\{\text{\text{blue}{}, \text{red}{}}\}

is called a
NAC-coloring
,
if it is surjective and for every cycle in
G
,
either all edges have the same color, or
there are at least two edges in each color (see
Figure

7
).

The main theorem for NAC-colorings shows their relation to flexes of a graph.

Theorem 1.10

(
[
7
,
12
]
)
.

A connected non-trivial graph allows a flexible framework if and only if it has a NAC-coloring.

This result was generalized to periodic
[
5
]
and
rotational symmetry preserving flexes
[
6
,
7
]
.
Checking whether a general finite graph has a NAC-coloring is NP-complete
[
9
]
.
Similarly to
[
11
]
we need a specific type of NAC-colorings in this paper.

Definition 1.11
.

A NAC-coloring
\delta
of a graph
G
is called
Cartesian

if no two distinct vertices are connected by a red and blue path simultaneously.

Figure 7
:
A coloring that is not a NAC-coloring (left), a Cartesian NAC-coloring (middle)
and a NAC-coloring that is not Cartesian (right).

The name
Cartesian
is motivated by the relation to the Cartesian product of graphs,
see the discussion in
[
11
, Appendix A]
.
Prior to the references mentioned in the discussion,
finite graphs that are non-trivial subgraphs of Cartesian products
were characterized in
[
16
]

using edge colorings (called
2-labelings with Condition A
in the paper) by two colors satisfying
the condition stated in the following lemma, which proves that these are exactly Cartesian NAC-colorings.

Lemma 1.12
.

Let
G
be a connected graph.
A surjective edge coloring of
G
by red and blue is a Cartesian NAC-coloring
if and only if for each induced non-monochromatic cycle the colors change
at least three times while passing the cycle.

Proof.

It is trivial that every Cartesian NAC-coloring satisfies the condition above:
if the colors in a non-monochromatic induced cycle would change only twice,
then the two vertices where the changes occur were connected by a red and a blue path simultaneously.

In order to show that an edge coloring
\delta
of
G
satisfying the given condition is a Cartesian NAC-coloring,
we follow the idea of the injectivity part of the proof of
[
16
, Theorem 2.2]
.
We show that all non-monochromatic cycles have at least three changes of colors, not only induced ones.
Suppose for contradiction that there is a cycle with only two color changes.
Let
C
be such a cycle of the minimal length and
P_{\text{red}}
and
P_{\text{blue}}
be the red and blue paths it consists of.
By assumption,
C
is not induced, namely, there is an edge
uv\in E_{G}
such that
u,v
are in
C
but
uv
is not in
C
.
Suppose
u,v
are both in
P_{\text{red}}
, then
\delta(uv)
cannot be red since this would contradict the minimality of
C
.
But
\delta(uv)
cannot be blue either since then the part of
P_{\text{red}}
from
u
to
v
together with the edge
uv

would create a cycle with two color changes shorter than
C
.
Similarly,
u,v
cannot be both in
P_{\text{blue}}
.
Notice that we have also covered the case where
u
or
v
is a vertex where a color change occurs.
The only case left is when
u
is a vertex with two incident blue edges of
C
and
v
with two such red edges.
But this also gives a contradiction as independently of
\delta(uv)
being red or blue,
there would be a non-monochromatic cycle with two color changes shorter than
C
.
Therefore, all non-monochromatic cycles have at least three color changes,
hence, no two distinct vertices are connected by a red and blue path simultaneously.
This also implies that there are at least two red and two blue edges in every non-monochromatic cycle.
Hence,
\delta
is a Cartesian NAC-coloring.
∎

After this detour to definitions from the literature we now relate Cartesian NAC-colorings to angle-preserving classes.

Lemma 1.13
.

Let
(G,\rho)
be walk-independent.
A surjective edge coloring
\delta
of
G
by red and blue is a Cartesian NAC-coloring
if and only if every angle-preserving class is monochromatic.

Proof.

\implies:
since
\delta
is a NAC-coloring, 3-cycles are monochromatic.
The opposite edges of a 4-cycle in a Cartesian NAC-coloring have the same color as well.
Hence, edges in relation
\triangle
or
\square
have the same color,
which gives that angle-preserving classes are monochromatic.

\impliedby:

First, we show that
\delta
is a NAC-coloring.
Consider a cycle
C
.
If
uv
is an edge in
C
which belongs to an angle-preserving class
r
,
there is another edge

in
C
belonging to
r

since
r
separates

and
v
by
Lemma

1.5
.
Therefore, if a color occurs in
C
, it occurs at least twice as
\delta(uv)=\delta(e)

by the assumption that angle-preserving classes are monochromatic.

It cannot happen that two vertices are connected by a red and a blue path simultaneously,
since they are separated by an angle-preserving class by
Lemma

1.5
.
Hence,
\delta
is a Cartesian NAC-coloring.
∎

Finally Cartesian NAC-colorings form a correspondence to flexible walk-independent frameworks.

Corollary 1.14
.

For a walk-independent framework
(G,\rho)
, the following are equivalent:

(i)

(G,\rho)
is flexible,

(ii)

G
has a Cartesian NAC-coloring,

(iii)

is a non-trivial subgraph of the Cartesian product of graphs.

Proof.

By
Proposition

, if
(G,\rho)
is flexible,
then

has at least two angle-preserving classes.
Coloring the edges of some of the angle-preserving classes by red and the rest by blue
gives a Cartesian NAC-coloring by
Lemma

1.13
.
On the other hand, if
G
has a Cartesian NAC-coloring,
there must be at least two angle-preserving classes by
Lemma

,
hence
(G,\rho)
is flexible by

.

The existence of a Cartesian NAC-coloring is equivalent to being
a non-trivial subgraph of the Cartesian product of graphs
by

and
[

.
∎

We remark that another characterization of a graph being a non-trivial subgraph
of the Cartesian product of graphs is given in

This characterization was used to prove that determining whether

[

which is apparently not the case for graphs that admit a walk-independent framework by our results.

1.4

We define a class of graphs which “consist of 3- and 4-cycles” and whose parallelogram placements are walk-independent.
The corresponding frameworks are called TP-frameworks. For their definition we first need the notion of a simplicial complex.

Definition 1.19

An

Each element

of

The

of

The

is the abstract simplicial complex formed by all faces of

of dimension at most

In the following, we abuse the notation and identify singletons

and we write

instead of

for 1-dimensional faces of

or edges of a graph.

Definition 1.20
.

Let

A

or the inverse operation.
Two closed walks based at the same point are

if one can be obtained from the other by a sequence of moves or inverse moves.

2
Rotationally symmetric TP-frameworks

This section is devoted to results on symmetric graphs, frameworks and flexes.
Symmetric flexibility of graphs has been considered in
[
6
]
.
We again relate the NAC-colorings (in this case symmetric ones) to symmetric angle-preserving classes.

We start with the definition of the symmetric version from the notions of the previous sections.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
G
be a connected graph and
n\in\mathbb{N},n\geq 2
.
Let the group
\mathcal{C}_{n}
be a cyclic subgroup of order
n
(generated by
\omega
) of the automorphism group of
G
.
For
\gamma\in\mathcal{C}_{n}
, we define
\gamma v:=\gamma(v)
for
v\in V_{G}
and

\gamma e:=\gamma u\gamma v
for
e=uv\in E_{G}
.
We call a subset
S\subset V_{G}

invariant
if
\gamma S=S
for all
\gamma\in\mathcal{C}_{n}
,
resp.
partially invariant
if
\gamma S=S
for some
\gamma\in\mathcal{C}_{n},\gamma\neq 1
.
A vertex
v\in V_{G}
is called
(partially) invariant

if
\{v\}
is (partially) invariant.
The graph
G
is called
\mathcal{C}_{n}
-symmetric

if the set of invariant vertices is an independent set in
G

and we have that if
v\in V_{G}
is partially invariant,
then
v
is invariant.

Definition 2.2
.

A framework
(G,\rho)
with
G
being
\mathcal{C}_{n}
-symmetric
is called
\mathcal{C}_{n}
-symmetric

if
\rho(\omega v)=\Theta_{\omega}\rho(v)
for each
v\in V_{G}
,
where
\Theta_{\omega}:=\Theta\left(\frac{2\pi}{n}\right)
is the
\frac{2\pi}{n}
rotation matrix.
If there is a non-trivial flex
\rho_{t}
of
(G,\rho)

such that each
(G,\rho_{t})
is
\mathcal{C}_{n}
-symmetric,
then
(G,\rho)
is
\mathcal{C}_{n}
-symmetric flexible
,
and
\mathcal{C}_{n}
-symmetric rigid
otherwise.

Definition 2.3
.

An infinitesimal flex
\varphi
of a
\mathcal{C}_{n}
-symmetric framework
(G,\rho)

is
\mathcal{C}_{n}
-symmetric
if for all
v\in V_{G}
:

\varphi(\omega v)=\Theta_{\omega}\varphi(v)\,.

The framework
(G,\rho)
is called
\mathcal{C}_{n}
-symmetric infinitesimally flexible
if it has a non-trivial
\mathcal{C}_{n}
-symmetric infinitesimal flex.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
G
be a
\mathcal{C}_{n}
-symmetric graph.
Consider the following relation on the edge set
E_{G}
:

e_{1},e_{2}
are in relation if and only if

e_{1}(\triangle\cup\square)e_{2}
(see
Definition

1.1
)
or
e_{1}=\omega^{k}e_{2}
for some
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
.
An equivalence class of the transitive closure of the relation
is called a
\mathcal{C}_{n}
-angle-preserving class
.

Again we can say something on the number of
\mathcal{C}_{n}
-angle-preserving classes of a flexible framework.

Proposition 2.5
.

If a
(G,\rho)
is a

-symmetric flexible framework

has at least two

-angle-preserving classes.

We proceed analogously to the proof of

1.2

with the following observation:
the angle between the lines
given by

and

in any

-symmetric realization, namely, it is constant along any
\mathcal{C}_{n}
-symmetric flex.

Similarly we can show this for infinitesimal flexibility.

Proposition 2.6
.

If a
(G,\rho)
is a
\mathcal{C}_{n}
-symmetric infinitesimally flexible framework such that all induced 4-cycles are

has at least two
\mathcal{C}_{n}
-angle-preserving classes.

We proceed as in the proof of

1.3
,
we only need to prove extra that

for every

Recall that

\displaystyle uv\measuredangle_{\text{inf}}\,\,\omega u\omega v\iff

\displaystyle\left(\rho_{u,v},\rho_{\omega u,\omega v}\text{ are LI}\land\exists\alpha\in\mathbb{R}:\varphi_{u,v}=\alpha R\rho_{u,v}\land\varphi_{\omega u,\omega v}=\alpha R\rho_{\omega u,\omega v}\right)

By the definition of a

-symmetric realization/infinitesimal flex,
we have

\displaystyle\rho_{\omega u,\omega v}=\Theta_{\omega}\rho_{u,v}\quad\text{ and }\quad\varphi_{\omega u,\omega v}=\Theta_{\omega}\varphi_{u,v}\,.

If

are linearly independent,

Otherwise, we have necessarily

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{j-1}\sum_{(w_{1},w_{2})\in W\cap r_{i}}\left(\rho(\omega^{k}w_{2})-\rho(\omega^{k}w_{1})\right)\,.

Appendix A
Verification of walk-independence

In this section, we show how the walk-independence of a finite framework
(G,\rho)

can be checked.
Let
T
be a spanning tree of
G
.
For
e\in E_{G}\setminus E_{T}
, we define
C_{e}^{T}
to be the unique cycle in
T+e
.
There are exactly
|E_{G}|-|V_{G}|+1
such cycles.

Proposition A.1
.

Let
(G,\rho)
be a framework such that
\rho
is a parallelogram placement.
Let
T
be a spanning tree of
G
.
The framework is walk-independent if and only if
Equation

2

holds for all cycles
C_{e}^{T}
.

In order to prove the proposition, we exploit the idea of a
cycle basis
.
We recall the necessary notions as simple as possible.
See for instance
[
14
]
for more details on the topic.

Definition A.2
.

Let
\vec{G}=(V_{G},A)
be an orientation of a finite graph
G
.
A vector
\vec{C}=(\vec{C}(u,v))_{(u,v)\in A}\in\mathbb{Q}^{A}
is called a
circulation

if for every
v\in V_{G}
the
conservation law
holds:

\sum_{(u,v)\in A}\vec{C}(u,v)=\sum_{(v,u)\in A}\vec{C}(v,u)\,.

For a closed walk
C=(v_{1},\ldots,v_{\ell})
in
G
, we define a circulation

\vec{C}\in\mathbb{Q}^{A}
by

\vec{C}(w,w^{\prime})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(v_{i},v_{i+1})\in C\\
(v_{i},v_{i+1})=(w,w^{\prime})\end{subarray}}\!\!1\ -\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(v_{i},v_{i+1})\in C\\
(v_{i},v_{i+1})=(w^{\prime},w)\end{subarray}}\!\!1\,.

The vector
\vec{C}
is indeed a circulation:
the conservation law at vertex
w
is equivalent to

\displaystyle\sum_{(u,w)\in A}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(v_{i},v_{i+1})\in C\\
(v_{i},v_{i+1})=(u,w)\end{subarray}}\!\!1\ +\sum_{(w,u)\in A}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(v_{i},v_{i+1})\in C\\
(v_{i},v_{i+1})=(u,w)\end{subarray}}\!\!1

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{(u,w)\in A}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(v_{i},v_{i+1})\in C\\
(v_{i},v_{i+1})=(w,u)\end{subarray}}\!\!1\ +\sum_{(w,u)\in A}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(v_{i},v_{i+1})\in C\\
(v_{i},v_{i+1})=(w,u)\end{subarray}}\!\!1\,,

where the left, resp. right, hand side gives the count on how many times the walk
C

enters, resp. leaves, the vertex
w
.
Obviously, these two numbers are equal as
C
is closed.

Proof of
Proposition

A.1
.

The “only if” part of the statement is trivial.

Suppose that
Equation

2
holds for all cycles
C_{e}^{T}
,
we denote them by
C_{1},\ldots,C_{k}
, where
k=|E_{G}|-|V_{G}|+1
.
We fix an orientation
\vec{G}=(V_{G},A)
of
G
.
The circulations
\vec{C}_{1},\ldots,\vec{C}_{k}

form a so called
fundamental cycle basis
of the
vector space of all circulations of
\vec{G}
with element-wise addition
and scalar multiplication over
\mathbb{Q}

[
14
]
.
Hence, for a closed walk
C
in
G
,
we have
\vec{C}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\alpha_{i}\vec{C}_{i}

for some
\alpha_{i}\in\mathbb{Q}
.
Now, for an angle-preserving class
r
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{(u,v)\in r\cap C}(\rho(v)-\rho(u))

\displaystyle=\sum_{(u,v)\in r\cap A}\vec{C}(u,v)(\rho(v)-\rho(u))

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\alpha_{i}\sum_{(u,v)\in r\cap A}\vec{C}_{i}(u,v)(\rho(v)-\rho(u))

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\alpha_{i}\sum_{(u,v)\in r\cap C_{i}}(\rho(v)-\rho(u))=(0,0)\,.\qed

Regarding the complexity of checking the walk-independence,
suppose we have
(G,\rho)
with
n=|V_{G}|
,
m=|E_{G}|
, and with edges labeled by angle-preserving classes they belong to.
Let
a
denote the number of angle-preserving classes.
A spanning tree
T
can be found in
{O(m+n)=O(m)}
.
We fix a vertex
w
.
For every angle-preserving class
r
and vertex

,
we compute the vector

\displaystyle z_{r}(w^{\prime})=\sum_{(u,v)\in r\cap W}(\rho(v)-\rho(u))\,,

where
W
is the walk from
w
to
w^{\prime}
in
T
.
These values can be obtained by traversing
T
,
namely, in
O(an)
operations.
Now, the condition of the walk-independence holds for cycle
C_{e}^{T}
,

e=uv
belonging to the angle-preserving class
r
,
if and only if

.
There are
O(m)

Hence, in total the complexity is

.
Notice that

if the walk-independence holds:
if there was an edge in

But

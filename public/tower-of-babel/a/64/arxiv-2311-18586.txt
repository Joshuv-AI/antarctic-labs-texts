Local Minimizers of Nonconvex Functions in Banach Spaces via Moreau Envelopes
Pham Duy Khanh, Vu V.H. Khoa, Boris S. Mordukhovich, Vo Thanh Phat

Abstract




To Tamás Terlaky in honor of his 70th birthday

with friendship and great respect

Abstract.

This paper investigates the preservation of local minimizers and strong minimizers of extended-real-valued lower semicontinuous functions under taking their Moreau envelopes. We address a general setting of Banach spaces, while all the obtained results are new even for functions in finite dimensions. Our main motivation came from applications to numerical methods in nonsmooth optimization dealing with broad classes of nondifferentiable and nonconvex functions. The paper also formulates and discusses some open questions stemming from this study.

Keywords.
Local minimizers and strong local minimizers, Moreau envelopes, proximal mappings, convex and variational analysis, generalized differentiation, Banach and Hilbert spaces.

1
Introduction

The concept of Moreau envelopes, introduced by Jean-Jacques Moreau in
[
17
]
, has been recognized as a fundamental concept in convex and variational analysis, optimization, and their numerous applications. This concept has been well understood as an efficient tool to study both theoretical and numerical aspects of analysis and its applications.

Moreau envelopes were first defined and explored for extended-real-valued convex functions and then were expanded to a general class of lower semicontinuous (l.s.c.) functions in finite-dimensional and infinite-dimensional spaces. It has been confirmed that this concept provides an effective
regularization
of the given function to get certain desired properties that include continuity, differentiability, full domain, etc. For further explorations of Moreau envelopes in these and other contexts of convex and variational analysis, we refer the reader to the books
[
1
,
2
,
20
,
21
]
and their extended bibliographies.

From the viewpoint of optimization, a crucial property of Moreau envelopes is their ability to preserve the global minimum value and the set of
global minimizers
; see
[
20
, Example 1.46]
with the proof and references therein. In the local setting, an optimization relationship between a given function and its Moreau envelope was established for the particular type of solutions known as
tilt-stable local minimizers
. This remarkable notion was introduced by Poliquin and Rockafellar
[
19
]
for extended-real-valued functions on finite-dimensional spaces and then developed by Mordukhovich and Nghia
[
15
]
in the Hilbert space setting. By now, tilt-stable minimizers have been largely investigated and characterized for various classes of constrained optimization problems in both finite and infinite dimensions. We refer the reader to the book by the third author
[
14
]
for a full account on tilt stability and its applications.

The aforementioned relationship for tilt-stable minimizers was established by Mordukhovich and Sarabi in the proof of
[
16
, Theorem 6.2]
for the special class of
prox-regular
and
subdifferentially continuous
functions on
{\rm I\!R}^{n}
, which play a pivoting role in second-order variational analysis. This result says that if
\bar{x}
is a tilt-stable local minimizer for such a function, then it is a tilt-stable minimizer for its Moreau envelope. The obtained result reduces finding tilt-stable minimizers of extended-real-valued prox-regular functions to determining such minimizers of their Moreau envelopes, which are continuously differentiable with Lipschitz continuous gradients. This scheme has been implemented in
[
6
,
8
,
9
,
14
,
16
]
for developing generalized Newton algorithms to find tilt-stable local minimizers as well as global solutions to various classes of optimization and related problems in finite-dimensional spaces.

Apart of this, we are not familiar with any relationships between usual (not tilt-stable)
local minimizers
of a given function and its Moreau envelope, even in finite dimensions. Establishing such relationships is a challenging issue being instrumental to conduct
local convergence analysis
of
nonconvex
optimization problems by employing the aforementioned scheme for
generalized Newtonian algorithms
. Furthermore, the
inexact proximal point method
for a given function is essentially the
inexact gradient descent method
for its Moreau envelope; see
[
10
,
11
,
12
]
. Therefore, exploring the preservation of local minimizers for Moreau envelopes provides insights into conducting local convergence analysis of the inexact proximal point method and its variants from the perspectives of the inexact gradient descent method and its modifications.

The
primary goal
of this paper is to establish the
equivalence
between
arbitrary local minimizers
of a given l.s.c. prox-bounded function and its Moreau envelope under general unrestrictive assumptions in
reflexive Banach spaces
. Moreover, such an equivalence will also be established for
strong local minimizers
with some quantitative modulus relationships in the setting of
Hilbert spaces
. To achieve these aims, we use powerful tools of variational analysis married to geometric theory of Banach spaces.

The subsequent content is organized as follows. Section
2
presents some preliminary material from variational analysis and optimization needed for the formulations and proofs of the main results. Section
3
revolves around the relationships between local minimizers of functions and their Moreau envelopes. Section
4
is devoted to the study of such relationships for strong local minimizers. The final Section
5
formulates some open questions for the future research.

2
Preliminaries

In this section, we define and discuss some notions from variational analysis and generalized differentiation that are frequently used in what follows. More details and references can be found in the books
[
14
,
20
,
21
]
in finite and infinite dimensions. We also recall the notions of local and strong local minimizers that are investigated in this paper. For the notions and results from geometry of Banach spaces, we refer the reader to the book
[
3
]
.

Unless otherwise stated, the spaces under consideration are assumed to be
Banach
with the generic symbols
\|\cdot\|
for norms and
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
for the canonical pairing between a space
X
and its topological dual
X^{*}
. We use ‘
\rightarrow
’ and ‘
\overset{w}{\rightarrow}
’ to indicate the strong/norm and weak convergence, respectively. The symbols
\mathbb{B}_{r}(x)
and
B_{r}(x)
signify the closed and open balls centered at
x
with radius
r>0
, respectively. Given
C\subset X
and
x\in X
, the
distance function to

C
is defined as

d(x;C):=\inf_{y\in C}\|x-y\|\;\mbox{ for all }\;x\in X.

(2.1)

Given an l.s.c. function
\varphi\colon X\to\overline{{\rm I\!R}}
on a Banach space
X
,
the
Moreau envelope
of
\varphi
is

e_{\lambda}\varphi(x):=\inf_{w\in X}\left\{\varphi(w)+\frac{1}{2\lambda}\|w-x\|^{2}\right\},\hskip 10.00002ptx\in X,

(2.2)

where
\lambda>0
is a parameter. The corresponding
proximal mapping
is

P_{\lambda}\varphi(x):=\text{\rm argmin}_{w\in X}\left\{\varphi(w)+\frac{1}{2\lambda}\|w-x\|^{2}\right\},\hskip 10.00002ptx\in X.

(2.3)

As in
[
21
]
, we say that
\varphi\colon X\to\overline{{\rm I\!R}}
is
prox-bounded
if

\varphi(x)\geq\alpha\|x-\bar{x}\|^{2}+\beta\;\mbox{ for some }\;\alpha,\beta\in{\rm I\!R}\;\mbox{ and }\;\bar{x}\in X.

(2.4)

Recall further that
\bar{x}^{*}\in X^{*}
is a
proximal subgradient
of
\varphi
at
\bar{x}\in\mbox{\rm dom}\,\varphi
if there exist positive numbers
r
and
\varepsilon
for which

\varphi(x)\geq\varphi(\bar{x})+\langle\bar{x}^{*},x-\bar{x}\rangle-\frac{r}{2}\|x-\bar{x}\|^{2}\;\mbox{ whenever }\;x\in B_{\varepsilon}(\bar{x}).

(2.5)

The collection of all proximal subgradients of
\varphi
at
\bar{x}
is called the
proximal subdifferential
of the function
at this point and is denoted by
\partial_{P}\varphi(\bar{x})
.

We say that a function
\varphi:X\rightarrow\overline{{\rm I\!R}}
is
weakly sequentially lower semicontinuous
(weakly sequentially l.s.c.) at
\bar{x}\in\mbox{\rm dom}\,\varphi
if for any sequence
\{x_{k}\}
which weakly converges to
\bar{x}
, it holds that
\liminf_{k\rightarrow\infty}\varphi(x_{k})\geq\varphi(\bar{x})
. This function is called
weakly sequentially l.s.c.
if the preceding property holds for all
\bar{x}\in\mbox{\rm dom}\,\varphi
. It easily follows from the definition that
\varphi
is weakly sequentially l.s.c. if and only if the level sets
\{x\in X\mid\varphi(x)\leq\alpha\}
are weakly sequentially closed in
X
for all
\alpha\in{\rm I\!R}
. We clearly have that the weak sequential lower semicontinuity of a function implies its lower semicontinuity, while not vice versa. The weak sequential l.s.c. of an extended-real-valued function
\varphi:X\rightarrow\overline{{\rm I\!R}}
together with its
coercivity

|\varphi(x)|\rightarrow\infty\ \text{ whenever }\ \|x\|\rightarrow\infty

(2.6)

guarantees the existence of its
global minimizer
according to the following version of the Weierstrass existence theorem taken from
[
13
, Theorem 7.3.7]
.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
X
be a reflexive Banach space, and let
\varphi:X\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a coercive and weakly sequentially lower semicontinuous function. Then there exists
x_{0}\in X
such that

f\left(x_{0}\right)=\inf_{x\in X}f(x).

We conclude this section with recalling the notions of local minimizers and strong local minimizers considered in what follows.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
\varphi:X\to\overline{{\rm I\!R}}
, and let
\bar{x}\in\mbox{\rm dom}\,\varphi
. We say that:

(i)

\bar{x}
is a
local minimizer
of
\varphi
if there exists
\varepsilon>0
such that

\varphi(x)\geq\varphi(\bar{x})\hskip 10.00002pt\text{for all }\;x\in B_{\varepsilon}(\bar{x}).

(ii)

\bar{x}
is a
strong local minimizer
of
\varphi
with modulus
\sigma>0
if there exists
\varepsilon>0
such that

\varphi(x)\geq\varphi(\bar{x})+\frac{\sigma}{2}\|x-\bar{x}\|^{2}\hskip 10.00002pt\text{for all }\;x\in B_{\varepsilon}(\bar{x}).

3
Relationships for Local Minimizers

In this section, we establish relationships between local minimizers of a function
\varphi\colon X\to\overline{{\rm I\!R}}
and of its Moreau envelope
e_{\lambda}\varphi
in Banach spaces. To proceed, we first need to recall some known results used below. Two following lemmas are taken from
[
5
, Propositions 6.1 and 6.2]
.

Lemma 3.1
.

\varphi\colon X\to\overline{{\rm I\!R}}
be an l.s.c. function defined on a Banach space
X
. Then we have the three equivalent assertions:

(i)

\varphi
is prox-bounded in the sense of (
2.4
).

(ii)
For some
\lambda>0
and
x\in X
, it holds that
e_{\lambda}\varphi(x)>-\infty
.

(iii)
There is
\lambda_{0}>0
such that
e_{\lambda}\varphi(x)>-\infty
for any
0<\lambda<\lambda_{0}
and any
x\in X
.

The positive constant
\lambda_{0}
in (iii) is usually called the
threshold of prox-boundedness
of
\varphi
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\varphi:X\to\overline{{\rm I\!R}}
be an l.s.c. function on a Banach space
X
, and let
\bar{x}\in\mbox{\rm dom}\,\varphi
. Then we have the three equivalent assertions:

(i)

\varphi
is prox-bounded, and
0\in X^{*}
is a proximal subgradient of
\varphi
at
\bar{x}
.

(ii)

P_{\lambda}\varphi(\bar{x})=\{\bar{x}\}
for some
\lambda>0
.

(iii)

P_{\lambda}\varphi(\bar{x})=\{\bar{x}\}
for all
\lambda>0
sufficiently small.

To establish the main result of this section, we need the following proposition of its own interest about a nice behavior of proximal mappings in reflexive Banach spaces.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
\varphi:X\rightarrow\overline{\mathbb{R}}
be a proper, prox-bounded, and weakly sequentially l.s.c. function defined on a reflexive Banach space
X
. Fixing any
0<\lambda<\lambda_{\varphi}
, we have the assertions:

(i)

P_{\lambda}\varphi(x)\neq\emptyset
for any
x\in X
.

(ii)
If
w^{k}\in P_{\lambda}\varphi(x^{k})
for
k\in{\rm I\!N}
such that
x^{k}\rightarrow\bar{x}
as
k\to\infty
, then the sequence
\left\{w^{k}\right\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
is bounded and all its weak accumulation points lie in
P_{\lambda}\varphi(\bar{x})
.

[
Proof.]
First we verify (i). Fix any
x\in X
and claim that
\varphi(\cdot)+\frac{1}{2\lambda}\|x-\cdot\|^{2}
is coercive in the sense of (
2.6
), which then opens the door to utilizing Proposition
2.1
. To proceed, choose any
\lambda_{1}\in(\lambda,\lambda_{\varphi})
and deduce from Lemma
3.1
(iii) that
\beta:=e_{\lambda_{1}}\varphi(0)>-\infty
. The definition of
\beta
implies that
\varphi(w)+\frac{1}{2\lambda_{1}}\|w\|^{2}\geq\beta
for all
w\in X
. Therefore, we get the estimates

\displaystyle\varphi(w)+\dfrac{1}{2\lambda}\|w-x\|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle-\dfrac{1}{2\lambda_{1}}\|w\|^{2}+\dfrac{1}{2\lambda}\|w-x\|^{2}+\beta

(3.1)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\left(\dfrac{1}{2\lambda}-\dfrac{1}{2\lambda_{1}}\right)\|w\|^{2}-\dfrac{1}{\lambda}\|w\|\cdot\|x\|+\dfrac{1}{2\lambda}\|x\|^{2}+\beta

for all
w\in X
, where the triangle inequality is used in (
3.1
). As the leading term
\frac{1}{2\lambda}-\frac{1}{2\lambda_{1}}
is positive whenever
\lambda_{1}>\lambda
, the right-hand side of (
3.1
) tends to
\infty
as
\|w\|\rightarrow\infty
which verifies the coercivity of
\varphi(\cdot)+\frac{1}{2\lambda}\|x-\cdot\|^{2}
on
X
. Since both
\varphi
and the norm-square function
\|x-\cdot\|^{2}
are weakly sequentially l.s.c., their sum is weakly sequentially l.s.c. as well. Thus it follows from Proposition
2.1
that
P_{\lambda}\varphi(x)\neq\emptyset
, which justifies assertion (i).

To verify (ii), suppose that there exist sequences
x^{k}\rightarrow\bar{x}
and
\{w_{k}\}\subset X
for which
w^{k}\in P_{\lambda}\varphi(x^{k})
as
k\in{\rm I\!N}
. Pick any
\alpha>e_{\lambda}\varphi(\bar{x})
. By the global continuity of
e_{\lambda}\varphi
on
X
proved in
[
4
, Proposition 3.3]
and the fact that
x^{k}\rightarrow\bar{x}
as
k\to\infty
, it follows from
\alpha>e_{\lambda}\varphi(\bar{x})
that
\alpha>e_{\lambda}\varphi(x^{k})
for sufficiently large
k\in{\rm I\!N}
. The latter can be rewritten by the fact that
w^{k}\in P_{\lambda}\varphi(x^{k})
for all
k\in{\rm I\!N}
as follows:

{\alpha>e_{\lambda}\varphi(x^{k})=\varphi(w^{k})+\frac{1}{2\lambda}\|w^{k}-x^{k}\|^{2}\hskip 10.00002pt\text{for large }\;k.}

(3.2)

Combining (
3.1
) and (
3.2
) for
w:=w^{k}
whenever
k\in{\rm I\!N}
tells us that

\left(\dfrac{1}{2\lambda}-\dfrac{1}{2\lambda_{1}}\right)\|w^{k}\|^{2}-\dfrac{1}{\lambda}\|w^{k}\|\cdot\|x^{k}\|+\dfrac{1}{2\lambda}\|x^{k}\|^{2}+\beta-\alpha<0\hskip 10.00002pt\text{ for large }k\in{\rm I\!N}.

(3.3)

Since
\lambda_{1}>\lambda
and the sequence
\{x^{k}\}
converges (and hence is bounded), the inequality in (
3.3
) implies that the sequence
\{w^{k}\}
is bounded as well.

Remembering that the space
X
is reflexive, we suppose without loss of generality the weak convergence of
(w^{k},x^{k})
to
(\hat{w},\bar{x})
for some
\hat{w}\in X
. Observe further that the inequality
\varphi(\hat{w})+\frac{1}{2\lambda}\|\hat{w}-\bar{x}\|^{2}\leq\alpha
holds. Indeed, the weak sequential l.s.c. of
\varphi
and
\|\cdot\|^{2}
yields the weak sequential l.s.c. of
\varphi(\cdot)+\frac{1}{2\lambda}\|\cdot-\cdot\|^{2}
with respect to
(w,x)
. The latter implies that
\varphi(\hat{w})+\frac{1}{2\lambda}\|\hat{w}-\bar{x}\|^{2}\leq\alpha
by letting
k\rightarrow\infty
in (
3.2
). Finally, letting
\alpha\searrow e_{\lambda}\varphi(\bar{x})
brings us to

\varphi(\hat{w})+\dfrac{1}{2\lambda}\|\hat{w}-\bar{x}\|^{2}\leq e_{\lambda}\varphi(\bar{x}),

i.e.,
\hat{w}\in P_{\lambda}\varphi(\bar{x})
, which justifies (ii) and completes the proof of the proposition.

Remark 3.4
.

The nonemptiness of the proximal values
P_{\lambda}\varphi(x)
for
x\in X
established in Proposition
3.3
(i) plays an important role in the proof of our main results presented below. In our proof, this crucial property is obtained by using merely the prox-boundedness and weak sequential l.s.c. of the function defined on a reflexive Banach space. Observe to this end that the paper
[
18
]
contains various sufficient conditions on the function and the space in question to ensure that
P_{\lambda}\varphi(x)\neq\emptyset
. Namely,
[
18
, Corollary 3.7]
requires
X
to be
M
-reflexive and Fréchet smooth in order to ensure the density of the set
\{x\in X\mid P_{\lambda}\varphi(x)\neq\emptyset\}
. Comparing with our requirements, the result of
[
18
]
relaxes the weak sequential l.s.c. of
\varphi
, while demands on the other hand the
M
-reflexivity and Fréchet smoothness of
X
. It is worth noting that since the definition of Moreau envelopes and proximal mappings depend heavily on the norm under consideration, renorming procedures may not be useful.

The next theorem is the main outcome in this paper establishing close relationships between local minimizers of the function in question and of its Moreau envelope in Banach spaces.

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
\varphi:X\to\overline{{\rm I\!R}}
be an l.s.c. function defined on a Banach space
X
, and let
\bar{x}\in\mbox{\rm dom}\,\varphi
. Assume that
\varphi
is prox-bounded with threshold
\lambda_{\varphi}>0
, and let
0<\lambda<\lambda_{\varphi}
. Consider the following assertions:

(i)

\bar{x}
is a local minimizer of
\varphi
.

(ii)

\bar{x}
is a local minimizer of
e_{\lambda}\varphi
.

Then we have the implication
(i)
\Longrightarrow
(ii)
provided that
X
is reflexive and that
\varphi
is weakly sequentially lower semicontinuous. Moreover, in this case there exists a neighborhood
U_{\lambda}
of
\bar{x}
such that the lower error bound for the Moreau envelope

e_{\lambda}\varphi(x)-e_{\lambda}\varphi(\bar{x})\geq\frac{1}{2\lambda}d^{2}\big(x;P_{\lambda}\varphi(x)\big)\hskip 10.00002pt\text{for all}\hskip 10.00002ptx\in U_{\lambda}

(3.4)

holds via the distance function (
2.1
). The inverse implication
(ii)
\Longrightarrow
(i)
is fulfilled if
P_{\lambda}\varphi(\bar{x})
is nonempty, which is surely the case when
X
is reflexive and
\varphi
is weakly sequentially l.s.c.

[
Proof.]
First we verify implication (i)
\Longrightarrow
(ii) and estimate (
3.4
) under the weak sequential l.s.c. of the function
\varphi
and the reflexivity of the Banach space
X
. Suppose that
\bar{x}
is a local minimizer of
\varphi
and find a neighborhood
U
of
\bar{x}
such that

\varphi(x)\geq\varphi(\bar{x})\hskip 10.00002pt\text{for all}\hskip 10.00002ptx\in U.

(3.5)

As
\bar{x}
is a local minimizer of
\varphi
, the origin
0\in X^{*}
is a proximal subgradient of
\varphi
at
\bar{x}
. Lemma
3.2
tells us that whenever
0<\lambda<\lambda_{\varphi}
we have
P_{\lambda}\varphi(\bar{x})=\{\bar{x}\}
. Supposing, contrary to the existence of some
U_{\lambda}
guaranteeing (
3.4
), that inside any neighborhood of
\bar{x}
there exists a point
x
for which
e_{\lambda}\varphi(x)-e_{\lambda}\varphi(\bar{x})<\frac{1}{2\lambda}d^{2}\big(x;P_{\lambda}\varphi(x)\big)
. This allows us to construct a sequence
\{x_{k}\}
that converges to
\bar{x}
and satisfies the inequality

e_{\lambda}\varphi(x_{k})-e_{\lambda}\varphi(\bar{x})<\dfrac{1}{2\lambda}d^{2}\big(x_{k};P_{\lambda}\varphi(x_{k})\big)\;\mbox{ for all }\;k\in{\rm I\!N}.

(3.6)

Take any
k\in{\rm I\!N}
and deduce from Proposition
3.3
(i) that
P_{\lambda}\varphi(x_{k})\neq\emptyset
. The latter allows to pick some
w_{k}\in P_{\lambda}\varphi(x_{k})
for all
k\in{\rm I\!N}
. Combining this with
x_{k}\rightarrow\bar{x}
as
k\to\infty
, Proposition
3.3
(ii) tells us there is a subsequence
\{w_{k_{m}}\}_{m\in{\rm I\!N}}
of
\{w_{k}\}_{k\in{\rm I\!N}}
ensuring the weak convergence
w_{k_{m}}\xrightarrow{w}\bar{x}
by taking into account that
P_{\lambda}\varphi(\bar{x})=\{\bar{x}\}
. Since
\varphi
is weakly sequentially l.s.c. at
\bar{x}
and
e_{\lambda}\varphi
is continuous at
\bar{x}=\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}x_{k_{m}}
, we have the relationships

\displaystyle e_{\lambda}\varphi(\bar{x})\leq\varphi(\bar{x})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\liminf_{m\rightarrow\infty}\varphi(w_{k_{m}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\liminf_{m\rightarrow\infty}\left(e_{\lambda}\varphi(x_{k_{m}})-\frac{1}{2\lambda}\|x_{k_{m}}-w_{k_{m}}\|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e_{\lambda}\varphi(\bar{x})-\dfrac{1}{2\lambda}\limsup_{m\rightarrow\infty}\|x_{k_{m}}-w_{k_{m}}\|^{2},

which imply the limiting conditions

\limsup_{m\to\infty}\|x_{k_{m}}-w_{k_{m}}\|^{2}=0\;\mbox{ and hence }\;\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}\|x_{k_{m}}-w_{k_{m}}\|^{2}=0.

Since
x_{k_{m}}\rightarrow\bar{x}
as
m\to\infty
, it turns out that
w_{k_{m}}\rightarrow\bar{x}
as well. Therefore, for sufficiently large
m\in{\rm I\!N}
we have
w_{k_{m}}\in U
and thus get by (
3.5
) the estimates

\displaystyle e_{\lambda}\varphi(\bar{x})\leq\varphi(\bar{x})\leq\varphi(w_{k_{m}})=e_{\lambda}\varphi(x_{k_{m}})-\frac{1}{2\lambda}\|x_{k_{m}}-w_{k_{m}}\|^{2}\leq e_{\lambda}\varphi(x_{k_{m}})-\frac{1}{2\lambda}d^{2}\big(x_{k_{m}};P_{\lambda}\varphi(x_{k_{m}})\big),

i.e.,
e_{\lambda}\varphi(x_{k_{m}})-e_{\lambda}\varphi(\bar{x})\geq\frac{1}{2\lambda}d^{2}\big(x_{k_{m}};P_{\lambda}\varphi(x_{k_{m}})\big)
for large
m
. This contradicts (
3.6
), and so (
3.4
) holds. Finally,
\bar{x}
is a local minimizer, which justifies the first part of the theorem.

To verify the reverse implication (ii)
\Longrightarrow
(i), suppose that
P_{\lambda}\varphi(\bar{x})\neq\emptyset
and pick
\bar{y}\in P_{\lambda}\varphi(\bar{x})
. Since
\bar{x}
is a local minimizer of
e_{\lambda}\varphi
, there exists a neighborhood
U
of
\bar{x}
on which
e_{\lambda}\varphi(x)\geq e_{\lambda}\varphi(\bar{x})
. Due to the definition of
e_{\lambda}\varphi
, this amounts to saying that

\varphi(y)+\dfrac{1}{2\lambda}\|x-y\|^{2}\geq\varphi(\bar{y})+\dfrac{1}{2\lambda}\|\bar{x}-\bar{y}\|^{2}\;\mbox{ for all }\;x\in U\;\text{ and }\;y\in X.

(3.7)

In (
3.7
), by choosing
y:=\bar{y}
and
x:=\bar{x}+\varepsilon(\bar{y}-\bar{x})
with
\varepsilon>0
small enough to ensure that
x\in U
, we arrive at the estimate

\|\bar{x}+\varepsilon(\bar{y}-\bar{x})-\bar{y}\|^{2}\geq\|\bar{x}-\bar{y}\|^{2},

which can be equivalently rewritten as

(1-\varepsilon)^{2}\|\bar{x}-\bar{y}\|^{2}\geq\|\bar{x}-\bar{y}\|^{2},\;\mbox{ i.e., }\;(\varepsilon-2)\|\bar{x}-\bar{y}\|^{2}\geq 0.

This obviously implies that
\bar{x}=\bar{y}
, and hence

P_{\lambda}\varphi(\bar{x})=\{\bar{x}\}
. Then

\varphi(x)\geq e_{\lambda}\varphi(x)\geq e_{\lambda}\varphi(\bar{x})=\varphi(\bar{x})+\frac{1}{2\lambda}\|\bar{x}-\bar{x}\|^{2}=\varphi(\bar{x})\hskip 10.00002pt\text{for all }\;x\in U,

which justifies (i) and thus completes the proof of the theorem.

4
Relationships for Strong Local Minimizers

Now we proceed with establishing
qualitative and quantitative
relationships between
strong local minimizers
of extended-real-valued functions and their Moreau envelopes in
Hilbert spaces
. Given an l.s.c. function
\varphi:X\to\overline{{\rm I\!R}}
with
\bar{x}\in\mbox{\rm dom}\,\varphi
and given a modulus
\sigma\neq 0
, consider the
quadratically
\sigma
-shifted function
associated with
\varphi
by

\psi(x):=\varphi(x)-\frac{\sigma}{2}\|x-\bar{x}\|^{2}\ \text{ for all }\ x\in X.

(4.1)

Recall first the following lemma taken from
[
5
, Proposition 6.8]

Theorem 4.3

(

Let
\varphi:X\to\overline{{\rm I\!R}}

Then we have the implication
(i)
\Longrightarrow
(ii)

(ii)
\Longrightarrow

We start with verifying implication (i)
\Longrightarrow

\varphi

(4.6)

(4.7)

5
Open Questions

The paper establishes close relationships and equivalences between arbitrary local minimizers of general extended-real-valued functions and their Moreau envelopes in reflexive Banach spaces. Moreover, such relationships and equivalences are obtained for strong local minimizers of functions and their Moreau envelopes in Hilbert spaces. This line of research should be continued, since many important unsolved questions remain. We now list just few of them.

\bullet
Is it true that Moreau envelopes preserve the other types of local minimizers such as weak-sharp minimizers, tilt-stable minimizers in infinite-dimensions, etc.?

\bullet
How to apply the obtained results to construct second-order necessary and sufficient conditions for nonsmooth optimization problems?

\bullet
How to apply the obtained results to conduct local convergence analysis of first-order and second-order numerical optimization algorithms in finite- and infinite-dimensional spaces?

We plan to investigate these and related topics in our future research.

Finiteness properties of the category of mod p p representations of GL 2 ⁡ ( ℚ p ) \GL_{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
Vytautas Paškūnas, Shen-Ning Tung

Abstract
. We establish Bernstein-centre type of results for the category of mod p p representations of GL 2 ⁡ ( ℚ p ) \GL_{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}) . We treat all the remaining open cases, which occur when p p is 2 2 or 3 3 . Our arguments carry over for all primes p p . This allows us to remove the restrictions on the residual representation at p p in Lue Pan’s recent proof of the Fontaine–Mazur conjecture for Hodge–Tate representations of Gal ⁡ ( ℚ ¯ / ℚ ) \Gal(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q}) with equal Hodge–Tate weights.

1.1.
A sketch of the proof

We will now explain the construction of the map (

\bar{\rho}=\mathbf{1}\oplus\omega

\bar{\rho}=\mathbf{1}\oplus\mathbf{1}

2

Let

2.
Endomorphism rings

Let
E
be a pseudo-compact
\mathcal{O}
-algebra and let
\mathrm{PC}(E)
be the category of left pseudo-compact
E
-modules, see
[
12
]
,
[
31
, §IV.3]
. Let
\Irr(E)
be the set of equivalence classes of irreducible objects in
\mathrm{PC}(E)
.

Let
M
be in
\mathrm{PC}(E)
. We assume that we are given a continuous
E
-linear action of a profinite group
\mathcal{G}
on
M
, which makes
M
into a pseudo-compact module over the completed group algebra
\mathcal{O}\llbracket\mathcal{G}\rrbracket
. The action induces a homomorphism of
\mathcal{O}
-algebras
\mathcal{O}\llbracket\mathcal{G}\rrbracket\rightarrow\End_{E}^{\mathrm{cont}}(M)
. In this section we will study, when this map is surjective and its kernel.

If
N
is a pseudo-compact
E
-module, which is finitely generated as an
E
-module, then we may present
N
as

\prod_{i\in I}E\rightarrow E^{\oplus n}\rightarrow N\rightarrow 0.

By applying
\Hom^{\mathrm{cont}}_{E}(\ast,M)
we obtain an exact sequence

0\rightarrow\Hom^{\mathrm{cont}}_{E}(N,M)\rightarrow M^{\oplus n}\rightarrow\oplus_{i\in I}M.

We thus may identify
\Hom^{\mathrm{cont}}_{E}(N,M)
with a closed submodule of
M^{\oplus n}
, which makes
\Hom^{\mathrm{cont}}_{E}(N,M)

into a pseudo-compact left
\mathcal{O}\llbracket\mathcal{G}\rrbracket
-module.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
N
be a finitely generated projective
E
-module and let
\mathrm{m}
be a right pseudo-compact
\mathcal{O}\llbracket\mathcal{G}\rrbracket
-module. Then the natural
map

(4)

\mathrm{m}\wtimes_{\mathcal{O}\llbracket\mathcal{G}\rrbracket}\Hom^{\mathrm{cont}}_{E}(N,M)\rightarrow\Hom^{\mathrm{cont}}_{E}(N,\mathrm{m}\wtimes_{\mathcal{O}\llbracket\mathcal{G}\rrbracket}M)

is an isomorphism.

Proof.

Since
N
is finitely generated and projective, we may present it as

F\overset{e}{\longrightarrow}F\rightarrow N\rightarrow 0,

where
F\cong E^{\oplus n}
and
e
is an idempotent. In particular,
N
is a pseudo-compact
E
-module. The map in (
4
) is induced by a continuous bilinear map

(v,\phi)\mapsto[w\mapsto v\wtimes\phi(w)].

It is an isomorphism if
N=F
. The general case follows by applying the idempotent
e
to the isomorphism obtained for
N=F
.
∎

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\{\rho_{i}\}_{i\in I}
be a family of pairwise distinct absolutely irreducible right pseudo-compact
\mathcal{O}\llbracket\mathcal{G}\rrbracket
-modules. Then the map

(5)

\mathcal{O}\llbracket\mathcal{G}\rrbracket\rightarrow\prod_{i\in I}\End_{k}(\rho_{i})^{\mathrm{op}}

is surjective.

Proof.

Since
\mathcal{G}
is profinite each
\rho_{i}
is a finite dimensional
k
-vector space. Thus
\varphi_{i}:\mathcal{O}\llbracket\mathcal{G}\rrbracket\rightarrow\End_{k}(\rho_{i})^{\mathrm{op}}
, given by the action, is continuous for the discrete topology on the target. Since
\rho_{i}
is absolutely irreducible
\varphi_{i}
is surjective. Moreover,
\Ker\varphi_{i}
is an open maximal two-sided ideal of
\mathcal{O}\llbracket\mathcal{G}\rrbracket
. If
i\neq j
then
\rho_{i}\not\cong\rho_{j}
and thus
\Ker\varphi_{i}+\Ker\varphi_{j}=\mathcal{O}\llbracket\mathcal{G}\rrbracket
. This implies that for every finite subset
F
of
I
the map
\mathcal{O}\llbracket\mathcal{G}\rrbracket\rightarrow\prod_{i\in F}\End_{k}(\rho_{i})^{\mathrm{op}}
is surjective. Thus the image of (
5
) is dense for the product topology on the target. On the other hand (
5
) is a continuous map between pseudo-compact
\mathcal{O}
-modules and thus its image is closed, which implies surjectivity.
∎

If
M
is in
\mathrm{PC}(E)
then we let
\mathfrak{r}(M)
be the intersection of open
maximal submodules of
M
. Then
\mathfrak{r}(E)
is a closed two-sided ideal of
E
and

\mathfrak{r}(M)
is the closure of
\mathfrak{r}(E)M
inside
M
.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let us assume that the following hold

(1)

M
is a finitely generated projective
E
-module;

(2)

M/\mathfrak{r}(M)=\cosoc_{\mathcal{G}}M
;

(3)

for all
S\in\Irr(E)
, such that

\rho_{S}:=\Hom_{E}^{\mathrm{cont}}(M,S)\neq 0,

\dim_{k}\rho_{S}
is finite and
\rho_{S}
is an absolutely irreducible representation of
\mathcal{G}
;

(4)

if
S,S^{\prime}\in\Irr(E)
and
S\not\cong S^{\prime}
then
\Hom_{\mathcal{G}}(\rho_{S},\rho_{S^{\prime}})=0
.

Then the map
\mathcal{O}\llbracket\mathcal{G}\rrbracket\rightarrow\End_{E}^{\mathrm{cont}}(M)
is surjective.

Proof.

For
S\in\Irr(E)
we let
\overline{M}_{S}
be the smallest quotient of
M
such that

\rho_{S}=\Hom_{E}^{\mathrm{cont}}(M,S)=\Hom_{E}^{\mathrm{cont}}(\overline{M}_{S},S).

It follows from (3) that the above subspaces are finite dimensional. Thus
\overline{M}_{S}\cong S^{\oplus d}
such that
d\cdot\dim_{k}\End_{E}^{\mathrm{cont}}(S)=\dim_{k}\rho_{S}
. Since
S
is irreducible
\End_{E}^{\mathrm{cont}}(S)
is a skew field. It acts on
\rho_{S}
and this action commutes with the action of
\mathcal{G}
. Since
\rho_{S}
is absolutely irreducible we conclude that
\End_{E}^{\mathrm{cont}}(S)=k
and
\dim_{k}\rho_{S}=d
. Thus
\End^{\mathrm{cont}}_{E}(\overline{M}_{S})\cong M_{d}(k)
and thus does not have non-trivial two-sided ideals. Hence, the natural right action of
\End_{E}(\overline{M}_{S})
on
\rho_{S}
induces an injective ring homomorphism

(6)

\End_{E}^{\mathrm{cont}}(\overline{M}_{S})\rightarrow\End_{k}(\rho_{S})^{\mathrm{op}},

which has to be surjective as both
k
-vector spaces have dimension equal to
d^{2}
.

The isomorphism
M/\mathfrak{r}(M)\cong\prod_{S\in\Irr(E)}\overline{M}_{S}
induces an isomorphism

\End_{E}^{\mathrm{cont}}(M/\mathfrak{r}(M))\cong\prod_{S\in\Irr(E)}\End_{E}^{\mathrm{cont}}(\overline{M}_{S}).

Since
\rho_{S}\not\cong\rho_{S^{\prime}}
if
S\not\cong S^{\prime}
, it follows from Lemma
2.2
together with the isomorphism (
6
) that the action of
\mathcal{O}\llbracket\mathcal{G}\rrbracket
on
M/\mathfrak{r}(M)
induces a
surjection

(7)

\mathcal{O}\llbracket\mathcal{G}\rrbracket\twoheadrightarrow\End_{E}^{\mathrm{cont}}(M/\mathfrak{r}(M)).

Since
M
is projective and
M/\mathfrak{r}(M)
is pro-semisimple we have

(8)

\End^{\mathrm{cont}}_{E}(M)\twoheadrightarrow\Hom^{\mathrm{cont}}_{E}(M,M/\mathfrak{r}(M))\cong\End_{E}^{\mathrm{cont}}(M/\mathfrak{r}(M)).

If
\mathrm{m}
is an irreducible right pseudo-compact
\mathcal{O}\llbracket\mathcal{G}\rrbracket
-module and
\mathfrak{a}
is its annihilator then
\mathcal{O}\llbracket\mathcal{G}\rrbracket/\mathfrak{a}
is a finite dimensional simple
k
-algebra, and thus
\mathcal{O}\llbracket\mathcal{G}\rrbracket/\mathfrak{a}
is semi-simple as a left
\mathcal{O}\llbracket\mathcal{G}\rrbracket
-module, and thus
(\mathcal{O}\llbracket\mathcal{G}\rrbracket/\mathfrak{a})\wtimes_{\mathcal{O}\llbracket\mathcal{G}\rrbracket}M
is semi-simple as a left
\mathcal{O}\llbracket\mathcal{G}\rrbracket
-module. Hence, the surjection
M\twoheadrightarrow(\mathcal{O}\llbracket\mathcal{G}\rrbracket/\mathfrak{a})\wtimes_{\mathcal{O}\llbracket\mathcal{G}\rrbracket}M
factors through as

M\twoheadrightarrow\cosoc_{\mathcal{G}}M\twoheadrightarrow(\mathcal{O}\llbracket\mathcal{G}\rrbracket/\mathfrak{a})\wtimes_{\mathcal{O}\llbracket\mathcal{G}\rrbracket}M.

Moreover, the maps become isomorphisms after applying
\mathrm{m}\wtimes_{\mathcal{O}\llbracket\mathcal{G}\rrbracket}
. Thus

(9)

\mathrm{m}\wtimes_{\mathcal{O}\llbracket\mathcal{G}\rrbracket}M\cong\mathrm{m}\wtimes_{\mathcal{O}\llbracket\mathcal{G}\rrbracket}\cosoc_{\mathcal{G}}M\cong\mathrm{m}\wtimes_{\mathcal{O}\llbracket\mathcal{G}\rrbracket}M/\mathfrak{r}(M),

as

by assumption. Lemma
2.1
together with (

\mathrm{m}\wtimes_{\mathcal{O}\llbracket\mathcal{G}\rrbracket}
to (

(10)

\begin{split}\mathrm{m}\wtimes_{\mathcal{O}\llbracket\mathcal{G}\rrbracket}\End^{\mathrm{cont}}_{E}(M)&\overset{\cong}{\longrightarrow}\Hom^{\mathrm{cont}}_{E}(M,\mathrm{m}\wtimes_{\mathcal{O}\llbracket\mathcal{G}\rrbracket}M)\\
&\overset{\cong}{\longrightarrow}\Hom^{\mathrm{cont}}_{E}(M,\mathrm{m}\wtimes_{\mathcal{O}\llbracket\mathcal{G}\rrbracket}M/\mathfrak{r}(M))\\
&\overset{\cong}{\longrightarrow}\mathrm{m}\wtimes_{\mathcal{O}\llbracket\mathcal{G}\rrbracket}\Hom^{\mathrm{cont}}_{E}(M,M/\mathfrak{r}(M))\\
&\overset{\cong}{\longrightarrow}\mathrm{m}\wtimes_{\mathcal{O}\llbracket\mathcal{G}\rrbracket}\End^{\mathrm{cont}}_{E}(M/\mathfrak{r}(M)),\end{split}

where the
\mathcal{G}
-action on
\End^{\mathrm{cont}}_{E}(M)
and
\End^{\mathrm{cont}}_{E}(M/\mathfrak{r}(M))
is given by
(g.\varphi)(v):=g(\varphi(v))
.

If the map
\mathcal{O}\llbracket\mathcal{G}\rrbracket\rightarrow\End^{\mathrm{cont}}_{E}(M)
is not surjective then its cokernel is a non-zero left pseudo-compact

-module and thus

. If we let

3.
Pseudorepresentations

Let
\mathcal{G}
be a profinite group and let
\bar{\rho}
be a continuous semi-simple representation of
\mathcal{G}
on a
2
-dimensional
k
-vector space.
We fix a continuous group homomorphism
\psi:\mathcal{G}\rightarrow\mathcal{O}^{\times}
lifting
\det\bar{\rho}
. Let
D^{\mathrm{ps},\psi}
be the functor from the category of augmented artinian
\mathcal{O}
-algebras with residue field
k
to the category of sets, which maps
(A,\mathfrak{m}_{A})
to the set of continuous functions
t:\mathcal{G}\rightarrow A
satisfying the following conditions:

•

t(1)=2
;

•

t(g)\equiv\operatorname{tr}\bar{\rho}(g)\pmod{\mathfrak{m}_{A}},\quad\forall g\in\mathcal{G}
;

•

t(gh)=t(hg),\quad\forall g,h\in\mathcal{G}
;

•

\psi(g)t(g^{-1}h)-t(g)t(h)+t(gh)=0,\quad\forall g,h\in\mathcal{G}
.

The data
(t,\psi)
determines a continuous polynomial law of homogeneous degree
2
on
\mathcal{G}
, see
[
18
, Lemma 1.9]
. This deformation problem is pro-representable by a local
\mathcal{O}
-algebra
R^{\mathrm{ps},\psi}
with residue field
k
, complete with respect to profinite topology. We denote by
T:\mathcal{G}\rightarrow R^{\mathrm{ps},\psi}
the universal deformation. We extend it
R^{\mathrm{ps},\psi}
-linearly to a continuous function
T:R^{\mathrm{ps},\psi}\llbracket\mathcal{G}\rrbracket\rightarrow R^{\mathrm{ps},\psi}
. The homomorphism
\psi:\mathcal{G}\rightarrow\mathcal{O}^{\times}
induces a continuous
\mathcal{O}
-algebra homomorphism
\psi:\mathcal{O}\llbracket\mathcal{G}\rrbracket\rightarrow\mathcal{O}
, which we extend
R^{\mathrm{ps},\psi}
-linearly to a continuous
R^{\mathrm{ps},\psi}
-algebra homomorphism

\psi:R^{\mathrm{ps},\psi}\llbracket\mathcal{G}\rrbracket\rightarrow R^{\mathrm{ps},\psi}
. Let
J
be the closed two-sided ideal of
R^{\mathrm{ps},\psi}\llbracket\mathcal{G}\rrbracket
generated by
a^{2}-T(a)a+\psi(a)
for all
a\in R^{\mathrm{ps},\psi}\llbracket\mathcal{G}\rrbracket
.

Proposition 3.1
.

The ring homomorphism
\mathcal{O}\llbracket\mathcal{G}\rrbracket\rightarrow R^{\mathrm{ps},\psi}\llbracket\mathcal{G}\rrbracket/J
is surjective.

Proof.

Let
\overline{R}
and
C
be the images of
R^{\mathrm{ps},\psi}
and
\mathcal{O}\llbracket\mathcal{G}\rrbracket
in
R^{\mathrm{ps},\psi}\llbracket\mathcal{G}\rrbracket/J
, respectively. Since
R^{\mathrm{ps},\psi}
,
\mathcal{O}\llbracket\mathcal{G}\rrbracket
and
R^{\mathrm{ps},\psi}\llbracket\mathcal{G}\rrbracket/J
are pseudo-compact
\mathcal{O}
-modules
\overline{R}
and
C
are closed subrings of
R^{\mathrm{ps},\psi}\llbracket\mathcal{G}\rrbracket/J
.
It is enough to show that
C
contains
\overline{R}
, since in this case we deduce that
C
contains the image of
R^{\mathrm{ps},\psi}\llbracket\mathcal{G}\rrbracket
, which is equal to
R^{\mathrm{ps},\psi}\llbracket\mathcal{G}\rrbracket/J
.

Let
B=\overline{R}\cap C
and let
\mathfrak{m}_{B}
be the intersection of
B
with the maximal ideal of
\overline{R}
. Then
B
is a closed subring of
\overline{R}
. This implies that
B
is complete for the profinite topology. If
x\in\mathfrak{m}_{B}
then
1+x
has an inverse in
B
given by the geometric series. Since
B
is an
\mathcal{O}
-algebra and the residue field of
\overline{R}
is
k
, we conclude that
(B,\mathfrak{m}_{B})
is a local ring with residue field
k
.

Let
\overline{T}
be the specialisation of
T
along
R^{\mathrm{ps},\psi}\twoheadrightarrow\overline{R}
. The relation
g^{2}-\overline{T}(g)g+\psi(g)=0
in
R^{\mathrm{ps},\psi}\llbracket\mathcal{G}\rrbracket/J
implies that
\overline{T}(g)=g+g^{-1}\psi(g)
. Thus
\overline{T}
takes values in
B
. The universal property of
R^{\mathrm{ps},\psi}
implies that there is a continuous homomorphism of
\mathcal{O}
-algebras
\varphi:R^{\mathrm{ps},\psi}\rightarrow B
, such that
\varphi(T(g))=\overline{T}(g)
for all
g\in\mathcal{G}
. Using the universal property of
R^{\mathrm{ps},\psi}
again, we conclude that if we compose
\varphi
with the inclusion
B\subset\overline{R}
we get back the surjection
R^{\mathrm{ps},\psi}\twoheadrightarrow\overline{R}
that we have started with. Thus
B=\overline{R}
.
∎

4.1.
Blocks

From now on, we will assume

and hence is an object of

Let

be the set of irreducible representations in

Barthel-Livné

and Breuil

admitting a central character. The blocks containing an absolutely irreducible representation have been determined in

(i)

(ii)

(iii)

Lemma 4.1

If

Let
\mathcal{G}_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}

\mathcal{G}_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}

Colmez

\mathcal{O}

6.2.

Lemma A.6
.

Let
A
be a normal noetherian ring and let
G
be a group acting on

by ring automorphisms. Then the subring of
G
-invariants

is normal.

Proof.

If
A
is a domain then the assertion is proved in
[
13
, Proposition 6.4.1]
. The same proof works in our setting, as we will explain for the lack of a reference. Since
A
is noetherian and normal, it is a finite product of normal domains. Let
\Frac(A)
denote its total ring of fractions. Then
\Frac(A)
is a finite product of fields. The group
G
acts on
\Frac(A)
and we have

A^{G}=A\cap\Frac(A)^{G}.

We claim that
\Frac(A)^{G}
is a finite product of fields. The claim implies that
\Frac(A)^{G}
is its own ring of fractions. Since
A
is normal, (
26
) implies that

is reduced, integrally closed in its ring of fractions and has only finitely many minimal prime ideals, and hence is normal by Lemma 10.37.16 in
[
45
,
Tag 037B
]
.

To prove the claim, we note that
\Spec\Frac(A)
consists of finitely many primes and is in bijection with the set
\mathcal{E}
of idempotents

, such that

is a field. We have

and

if

. If

is a

-invariant idempotent then

on

is transitive. If

and

for some

then using the transitivity of the action we obtain that

for all

and so

. Hence, if

is non-zero then

is non-zero and we denote its inverse in the field

by

. If we let

then

. Thus

is a unit in

and its inverse

is unique. Uniqueness implies that

is

-invariant. Thus if

-acts transitively on

then

is a field.

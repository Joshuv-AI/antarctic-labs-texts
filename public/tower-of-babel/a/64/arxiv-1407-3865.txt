Inequalities for Euler-Mascheroni constant
Hongmin Xu, Xu You

Abstract
The aim of this paper is to establish new inequalities for the Euler-Mascheroni by the continued fraction method.

1
Introduction

Euler’s Constant was first introduced by Leonhard Euler (1707-1783) in 1734 as the limit of the sequence

(1.1)

\displaystyle\gamma(n):=\sum_{m=1}^{n}\frac{1}{m}-\ln n.

It is also known as the Euler-Mascheroni constant. There are many famous unsolved problems about the nature of this constant(See e.g. the survey papers or books of R.P. Brent and P. Zimmermann
[
1
]
, Dence and Dence
[
3
]
, Havil
[
5
]
and Lagarias
[
8
]
). For example, it is a long-standing open problem if it is a rational number. A good part of its mystery comes from the fact that the known algorithms converging to
\gamma
are not very fast, at least, when they are compared to similar algorithms for
\pi
and
e
.

The sequence
\left(r(n)\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
converges very slowly toward
\gamma
, like
(2n)^{-1}
. Up to now, many authors are
preoccupied to improve its rate of convergence, see e.g.
[
2
,
3
,
4
,
6
,
7
,
9
,
10
,
11
,
14
]
and references therein. We list some main results as follows:

\displaystyle\sum_{m=1}^{n}\frac{1}{m}-\ln\left(n+\frac{1}{2}\right)=\gamma+O(n^{-2}),\quad\mbox{(DeTemple, \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{De}{}{}]})}

\displaystyle\sum_{m=1}^{n}\frac{1}{m}-\ln\frac{n^{3}+\frac{3}{2}n^{2}+\frac{227}{240}+\frac{107}{480}}{n^{2}+n+\frac{97}{240}}=\gamma+O(n^{-6}),\quad\mbox{(Mortici, \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{Mor1}{}{}]})}

\displaystyle\sum_{m=1}^{n}\frac{1}{m}-\ln\left(1+\frac{1}{2n}+\frac{1}{24n^{2}}-\frac{1}{48n^{3}}+\frac{23}{5760n^{4}}\right)=\gamma+O(n^{-5}),\quad\mbox{(Chen and Mortici, \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{CM}{}{}]})}

Recently, Mortici and Chen
[
14
]
provided a very interesting sequence

\displaystyle\nu(n)=

\displaystyle\sum_{m=1}^{n}\frac{1}{m}-\frac{1}{2}\ln\left(n^{2}+n+\frac{1}{3}\right)

\displaystyle-\left(\frac{-\frac{1}{180}}{\left(n^{2}+n+\frac{1}{3}\right)^{2}}+\frac{\frac{8}{2835}}{\left(n^{2}+n+\frac{1}{3}\right)^{3}}++\frac{\frac{5}{1512}}{\left(n^{2}+n+\frac{1}{3}\right)^{4}}+\frac{\frac{592}{93555}}{\left(n^{2}+n+\frac{1}{3}\right)^{5}}\right),

and proved

(1.2)

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}n^{12}\left(\nu(n)-\gamma\right)=-\frac{796801}{43783740}.

Hence the rate of the convergence of the sequence
\left(\nu(n)\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is
n^{-12}
.

Very recently, by inserting the continued fraction term in (1.1), Lu
[
9
]
introduced a class of sequences
\left(r_{k}(n)\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
(see Theorem 1 below), and showed

(1.3)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{72(n+1)^{3}}<\gamma-r_{2}(n)<\frac{1}{72n^{3}},

(1.4)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{120(n+1)^{4}}<r_{3}(n)-\gamma<\frac{1}{120(n-1)^{4}}.

In fact, Lu
[
9
]
also found
a_{4}
without proof. In general, the continued fraction method could provide a better approximation
than others, and has less numerical computations.

First, we will prove

Theorem 1
.

For Euler-Mascheroni constant, we have the following convergent sequence

\displaystyle r(n)=1+\frac{1}{2}+\cdots+\frac{1}{n}-\ln n-\frac{a_{1}}{n+\frac{a_{2}n}{n+\frac{a_{3}n}{n+\ddots}}},

where

(a_{1},a_{2},a_{4},a_{6},a_{8},a_{10},a_{12})=\left(\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{6},\frac{3}{5},\frac{79}{126},\frac{7230}{6241},\frac{4146631}{3833346},\frac{306232774533}{179081182865}\right)
,
and
a_{2k+1}=-a_{2k}
for
1\leq k\leq 6
.

Let

\displaystyle R_{k}(n):=\frac{a_{1}}{n+\frac{a_{2}n}{n+\frac{a_{3}n}{n+\frac{a_{4}n}{\frac{\ddots}{n+a_{k}}}}}},

(See Appendix for their simple expressions) and

\displaystyle r_{k}(n):=\sum_{m=1}^{n}\frac{1}{m}-\ln n-R_{k}(n).

For
1\leq k\leq 13
, we have

(1.5)

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}n^{k+1}\left(r_{k}(n)-\gamma\right)=C_{k},

where

(C_{1},\cdots,C_{13})=\left(-\frac{1}{12},-\frac{1}{72},\frac{1}{120},\frac{1}{200},-\frac{79}{25200},-\frac{6241}{3175200},\frac{241}{105840},\frac{58081}{22018248},-\frac{262445}{91974960},\right.

\left.-\frac{2755095121}{892586949408},\frac{20169451}{3821257440},\frac{406806753641401}{45071152103463200},-\frac{71521421431}{5152068292800}\right)
.

Open problem
For every
k\geq 1
, we have
a_{2k+1}=-a_{2k}
.

The main aim of this paper is to improve (1.3) and (1.4). We establish the following more precise inequalities.

Theorem 2
.

Let
r_{10}(n),r_{11}(n)
,
C_{10}
and
C_{11}
be defined in Theorem 1, then

(1.6)

\displaystyle C_{10}\frac{1}{(n+1)^{11}}<\gamma-r_{10}(n)<C_{10}\frac{1}{n^{11}},

(1.7)

\displaystyle C_{11}\frac{1}{(n+1)^{12}}<r_{11}(n)-\gamma<C_{11}\frac{1}{n^{12}}.

Remark 1
.

In fact, Theorem 2 implies that

r_{10}(n)
is a strictly increasing function of
n
, whereas

r_{11}(n)
is a strictly decreasing function of
n
. Certainly, it has the similar inequalities for
r_{k}(n)(1\leq k\leq 9)
, we leave these for readers to verify. It is also should be noted that (1.4) cannot deduce the monotony of
r_{3}(n)
.

Remark 2
.

It is
worth to pointing out that Theorem 2 provides sharp bounds for harmonic sequence, which are superior to Theorem 3 and 4 of Mortici and Chen
[
14
]
.

2
The Proof of Theorem 1

The following lemma gives a method for measuring the rate of convergence, This Lemma was first used by Mortici
[
12
,
13
]
for constructing asymptotic expansions, or to accelerate some convergences. For proof and other details, see, e.g.,
[
13
]
.

Lemma 1
.

If the sequence
(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is convergent to zero and there exists the limit

(2.1)

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}n^{s}(x_{n}-x_{n+1})=l\in[-\infty,+\infty]

with
s>1
, then there exists the limit:

(2.2)

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}n^{s-1}x_{n}=\frac{l}{s-1}.

In the sequel, we always assume
n\geq 2
.

We need to find the value
a_{1}\in\mathbb{R}
which produces the most accurate approximation of the form

(2.3)

\displaystyle r_{1}(n)=\sum_{m=1}^{n}\frac{1}{m}-\ln n-\frac{a_{1}}{n},

here we note
R_{1}(n)=\frac{a_{1}}{n}
. To measure the accuracy of this approximation, we usually say that an approximation (2.3) is better as
r_{1}(n)-\gamma
faster converges to zero. Clearly

(2.4)

\displaystyle r_{1}(n)-r_{1}(n+1)=\ln\left(1+\frac{1}{n}\right)-\frac{1}{n+1}+\frac{a_{1}}{n+1}-\frac{a_{1}}{n}.

It is well-known that for
|x|<1
,

\displaystyle\ln(1+x)=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{m-1}\frac{x^{m}}{m}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\frac{1}{1-x}=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}x^{m}.

Developing the expression (2.4) into power series expansion in
\frac{1}{n}
, we easily obtain

(2.5)

\displaystyle r_{1}(n)-r_{1}(n+1)=\left(\frac{1}{2}-a_{1}\right)\frac{1}{n^{2}}+\left(a_{1}-\frac{2}{3}\right)\frac{1}{n^{3}}+\left(\frac{3}{4}-a_{1}\right)\frac{1}{n^{4}}+O\left(\frac{1}{n^{5}}\right).

From Lemma 1, we see that the rate of convergence of the sequence
\left(r_{1}(n)-\gamma\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is even higher as the value
s
satisfying (2.1). By lemma 1, we have

(i) If
a_{1}\neq\frac{1}{2}
, then the rate of convergence of the

\left(r_{1}(n)-\gamma\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is
n^{-1}
, since

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}n\left(r_{1}(n)-\gamma\right)=\frac{1}{2}-a_{1}\neq 0.

(ii) If
a_{1}=\frac{1}{2}
, from (2.5) we have

\displaystyle r_{1}(n)-r_{1}(n+1)=-\frac{1}{6}\frac{1}{n^{3}}+O\left(\frac{1}{n^{4}}\right).

Hence the rate of convergence of the

\left(r_{1}(n)-\gamma\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is
n^{-2}
, since

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}n^{2}\left(r_{1}(n)-\gamma\right)=-\frac{1}{12}.

We also observe that the fastest possible sequence
\left(r_{1}(n)\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is obtained only for
a_{1}=\frac{1}{2}
.

Just as Lu
[
9
]
did, we may repeat the above approach to determine
a_{1}
to
a_{4}
step by step. However, the computations
become very difficult when
k\geq 5
. In this paper we will use the
Mathematica
software to manipulate symbolic computations.

Let

(2.6)

\displaystyle r_{k}(n)=\sum_{m=1}^{n}\frac{1}{m}-\ln n-R_{k}(n),

then

(2.7)

\displaystyle r_{k}(n)-r_{k}(n+1)=\ln\left(1+\frac{1}{n}\right)-\frac{1}{n+1}+R_{k}(n+1)-R_{k}(n).

It is easy to get the following power series

(2.8)

\displaystyle\ln\left(1+\frac{1}{n}\right)-\frac{1}{n+1}=\sum_{m=2}^{\infty}(-1)^{m}\frac{m-1}{m}\frac{1}{n^{m}}.

Hence the key step is to expand
R_{k}(n+1)-R_{k}(n)
into power series in
\frac{1}{n}
. Here we use some examples to explain our method.

Step 1:
. For example, given
a_{1}
to
a_{7}
, find
a_{8}
. Define

(2.9)

\displaystyle R_{8}(n)=

\displaystyle\frac{\frac{1}{2}}{n+\frac{\frac{n}{6}}{n+\frac{-\frac{n}{6}}{n+\frac{\frac{3}{5}*n}{n+\frac{\frac{-3}{5}*n}{n+\frac{\frac{79}{126}*n}{n+\frac{\frac{-79}{126}*n}{n+a_{8}}}}}}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{-237+1405a_{8}+1800n+1740a_{8}n-630n^{2}+3780a_{8}n^{2}+3780n^{3}}{6(79a_{8}+600a_{8}n+600n^{2}+790a_{8}n^{2}+1260a_{8}n^{3}+1260n^{4})}.

By using the
Mathematica
software(The
Mathematica Program
is very similar to one given in Remark 3 below, however it has a parameter
a_{8}
), we obtain

(2.10)

\displaystyle R_{8}(n+1)-R_{8}(n)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2n^{2}}+\frac{2}{3n^{3}}-\frac{3}{4n^{4}}+\frac{4}{5n^{5}}-\frac{5}{6n^{6}}+\frac{6}{7n^{7}}-\frac{7}{8n^{8}}

\displaystyle+\frac{360030-6241a_{8}}{396900n^{9}}+\frac{-346440+24964a_{8}+6241a_{8}^{2}}{352800n^{10}}+O\left(\frac{1}{n^{11}}\right).

Substituting (2.8) and (2.10) into (2.7), we get

(2.11)

\displaystyle r_{8}(n)-r_{8}(n+1)=

\displaystyle\left(-\frac{8}{9}+\frac{360030-6241a_{8}}{396900}\right)\frac{1}{n^{9}}

\displaystyle+\left(\frac{9}{10}+\frac{-346440+24964a_{8}+6241a_{8}^{2}}{352800}\right)\frac{1}{n^{10}}+O\left(\frac{1}{n^{11}}\right).

The fastest possible sequence
\left(r_{8}(n)\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is obtained only for
a_{8}=\frac{7230}{6241}
. At the same time, it follows from (2.11),

(2.12)

\displaystyle r_{8}(n)-r_{8}(n+1)=\frac{58081}{2446472}\frac{1}{n^{10}}+O\left(\frac{1}{n^{11}}\right),

the rate of convergence of the

\left(r_{8}(n)-\gamma\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is
n^{-9}
, since

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}n^{9}\left(r_{8}(n)-\gamma\right)=-\frac{58081}{22018248}.

We can use the above approach to find
a_{k}(3\leq k\leq 8)
. Unfortunately, it does not work well for
a_{9}
. Since
a_{3}=-a_{2}
,
a_{5}=-a_{4}
and
a_{7}=-a_{6}
. So we may conjecture
a_{9}=-a_{8}
. Now let’s check it carefully.

Step 2:
Check
a_{9}=-\frac{7230}{6241}
to
a_{13}=-\frac{306232774533}{179081182865}
.

Let
a_{1},\cdots,a_{9}
, and
R_{9}(n)
be defined in Theorem 1. Applying the
Mathematica
software, we obtain

(2.13)

\displaystyle R_{9}(n+1)-R_{9}(n)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2n^{2}}+\frac{2}{3n^{3}}-\frac{3}{4n^{4}}+\frac{4}{5n^{5}}-\frac{5}{6n^{6}}+\frac{6}{7n^{7}}-\frac{7}{8n^{8}}+\frac{8}{9}\frac{1}{n^{9}}

\displaystyle-\frac{9}{10}\frac{1}{n^{10}}+\frac{736265}{836136}\frac{1}{n^{11}}+O\left(\frac{1}{n^{12}}\right),

which is the desired result. Substituting (2.8) and (2.13) into (2.7), we get

(2.14)

\displaystyle r_{9}(n)-r_{9}(n+1)=-\frac{262445}{9197496}\frac{1}{n^{11}}+O\left(\frac{1}{n^{12}}\right),

the rate of convergence of the

\left(r_{9}(n)-\gamma\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is
n^{-10}
, since

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}n^{10}\left(r_{9}(n)-\gamma\right)=-\frac{262445}{91974960}.

Next, we can use the
Step 1
to find
a_{10}
, and the
Step
2 to check
a_{11}
and
a_{12}
. It should be noted that
Theorem 2 will provide their another proofs for
a_{10}
and
a_{11}
. So we omit the details here.

Finally, we check
a_{13}=-\frac{306232774533}{179081182865}
.

(2.15)

\displaystyle R_{13}(n+1)-R_{13}(n)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2n^{2}}+\frac{2}{3n^{3}}-\frac{3}{4n^{4}}+\frac{4}{5n^{5}}-\frac{5}{6n^{6}}+\frac{6}{7n^{7}}-\frac{7}{8n^{8}}+\frac{8}{9}\frac{1}{n^{9}}

\displaystyle-\frac{9}{10}\frac{1}{n^{10}}+\frac{10}{11}\frac{1}{n^{11}}-\frac{11}{12}\frac{1}{n^{12}}+\frac{12}{13}\frac{1}{n^{13}}-\frac{13}{14}\frac{1}{n^{14}}

\displaystyle+\frac{1903648586623}{2576034146400}\frac{1}{n^{15}}+O\left(\frac{1}{n^{16}}\right).

Substituting (2.8) and (2.15) into (2.7), one has

(2.16)

\displaystyle r_{13}(n)-r_{13}(n+1)=-\frac{500649950017}{2576034146400}\frac{1}{n^{15}}+O\left(\frac{1}{n^{16}}\right).

Since

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}n^{14}\left(r_{13}(n)-\gamma\right)=-\frac{71521421431}{5152068292800},

thus the rate of convergence of the

\left(r_{13}(n)-\gamma\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is
n^{-14}
.

This completes the proof of Theorem 1.∎

Remark 3
.

In fact, if the assertion
a_{13}=-\frac{306232774533}{179081182865}
holds, then the other values
a_{j}(1\leq j\leq 12)
must be true. The following

Mathematica program
will generate
R_{13}(n+1)-R_{13}(n)
into power series in
\frac{1}{n}
with order 16:

Normal[Series
[(R_{13}[n+1]-R_{13}[n])/.\quad n\rightarrow 1/x,\{x,0,16\}]]/.\quad x\rightarrow 1/n

Remark 4
.

It is a very interesting question to find
a_{k}
for
k\geq 14
. However, it seems impossible by the above method.

3
The Proof of Theorem 2

Before we prove the Theorem 2, let us give a simple inequality, which plays an important role of the proof.

Lemma 2
.

Let
f^{\prime\prime}(x)
be a continuous function. If
f^{\prime\prime}(x)>0
, then

(3.1)

\displaystyle\int_{a}^{a+1}f(x)dx>f(a+1/2).

Proof.

Let
x_{0}=a+1/2
. By Taylor’s formula, we have

\displaystyle\int_{a}^{a+1}f(x)dx=

\displaystyle\int_{a}^{a+1}\left(f(x_{0})+f^{\prime}(x_{0})(x-x_{0})+\frac{1}{2}f^{\prime\prime}(\theta_{x})(x-x_{0})^{2}\right)dx

\displaystyle>

\displaystyle\int_{a}^{a+1}\left(f(x_{0})+f^{\prime}(x_{0})(x-x_{0})\right)dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f(a+1/2).

This completes the proof of Lemma 2.∎

In the sequel, the notation
P_{k}(x)
means a polynomial of degree
k
in
x
with all of its non-zero coefficients positive, which may be different at each occurrence.

Let’s begin to prove Theorem 2. Note
r_{10}(\infty)=0
, it is easy to see

(3.2)

\displaystyle\gamma-r_{10}(n)=\sum_{m=n}^{\infty}\left(r_{10}(m+1)-r_{10}(m)\right)=\sum_{m=n}^{\infty}f(m),

where

f(m)=\frac{1}{m+1}-\ln\left(1+\frac{1}{m}\right)-R_{10}(m+1)+R_{10}(m).

Let
D_{1}=\frac{2755095121}{6762022344}
. By using the
Mathematica
software, we have

\displaystyle f^{\prime}(x)+D_{1}\frac{1}{(x+1)^{13}}=-\frac{P_{19}(x)(x-1)+1619906998377\cdots 5270931}{33810111720x(1+x)^{13}P_{10}^{(1)}(x)P_{10}^{(2)}(x)}<0,

and

\displaystyle f^{\prime}(x)+D_{1}\frac{1}{\left(x+\frac{1}{2}\right)^{13}}=\frac{P_{22}(x)}{4226263965x(1+x)^{2}(1+2x)^{13}P_{10}^{(3)}(x)P_{10}^{(4)}(x)}>0.

Hence, we get the following inequalities for
x\geq 1
,

(3.3)

\displaystyle D_{1}\frac{1}{(x+1)^{13}}<-f^{\prime}(x)<D_{1}\frac{1}{(x+\frac{1}{2})^{13}}

Applying
f(\infty)=0
, (3.3) and Lemma 2, we get

(3.4)

\displaystyle f(m)=

\displaystyle-\int_{m}^{\infty}f^{\prime}(x)dx\leq D_{1}\int_{m}^{\infty}\left(x+\frac{1}{2}\right)^{-13}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{D_{1}}{12}\left(m+\frac{1}{2}\right)^{-12}\leq\frac{D_{1}}{12}\int_{m}^{m+1}x^{-12}dx.

From (3.1) and (3.4) we obtain

(3.5)

\displaystyle\gamma-r_{10}(n)\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{m=n}^{\infty}\frac{D_{1}}{12}\int_{m}^{m+1}x^{-12}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{D_{1}}{12}\int_{n}^{\infty}x^{-12}dx=\frac{D_{1}}{132}\frac{1}{n^{11}}.

Similarly, we also have

\displaystyle f(m)=

\displaystyle-\int_{m}^{\infty}f^{\prime}(x)dx\geq D_{1}\int_{m}^{\infty}\left(x+1\right)^{-13}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{D_{1}}{12}(m+1)^{-12}\geq\frac{D_{1}}{12}\int_{m+1}^{m+2}x^{-12}dx,

and

(3.6)

\displaystyle\gamma-r_{10}(n)\geq

\displaystyle\sum_{m=n}^{\infty}\frac{D_{1}}{12}\int_{m+1}^{m+2}x^{-12}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{D_{1}}{12}\int_{n+1}^{\infty}x^{-12}dx=\frac{D_{1}}{132}\frac{1}{(n+1)^{11}}.

Combining (3.5) and (3.6) completes the proof of (1.6).

Note
r_{11}(\infty)=0
, it is easy to deduce

(3.7)

\displaystyle r_{11}(n)-\gamma=\sum_{m=n}^{\infty}\left(r_{11}(m)-r_{11}(m+1)\right)=\sum_{m=n}^{\infty}g(m),

where

g(m)=\ln\left(1+\frac{1}{m}\right)-\frac{1}{m+1}-R_{11}(m)+R_{11}(m+1).

We write
D_{2}=\frac{20169451}{24495240}
. By using the
Mathematica
software, we have

\displaystyle-g^{\prime}(x)-D_{2}\frac{1}{(x+1)^{14}}=\frac{P_{18}(x)}{24495240x^{3}(1+x)^{14}P_{8}^{(1)}(x)P_{8}^{(2)}(x)}>0

and

\displaystyle-g^{\prime}(x)-D_{2}\frac{1}{\left(x+\frac{1}{2}\right)^{14}}=-\frac{P_{19}(x)(x-1)+4622005677839353997724676307741}{6123810x^{3}(1+x)^{3}(1+2x)^{14}P_{8}^{(3)}(x)P_{8}^{(4)}(x)}<0.

Hence for
x\geq 1
,

(3.8)

\displaystyle D_{2}\frac{1}{(x+1)^{14}}<-g^{\prime}(x)<D_{2}\frac{1}{\left(x+\frac{1}{2}\right)^{14}}.

Applying
g(\infty)=0
, (3.8) and (3.1), we get

(3.9)

\displaystyle g(m)=

\displaystyle-\int_{m}^{\infty}g^{\prime}(x)dx\leq D_{2}\int_{m}^{\infty}\left(x+\frac{1}{2}\right)^{-14}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{D_{2}}{13}\left(m+\frac{1}{2}\right)^{-13}\leq\frac{D_{2}}{13}\int_{m}^{m+1}x^{-13}dx.

It follows from (3.7) and (3.9)

(3.10)

\displaystyle r_{11}(n)-\gamma\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{m=n}^{\infty}\frac{D_{2}}{13}\int_{m}^{m+1}x^{-13}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{D_{2}}{13}\int_{n}^{\infty}x^{-13}dx=\frac{D_{2}}{156}\frac{1}{n^{12}}.

Finally,

\displaystyle g(m)=

\displaystyle-\int_{m}^{\infty}g^{\prime}(x)dx\geq D_{2}\int_{m}^{\infty}(x+1)^{-14}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{D_{2}}{13}(m+1)^{-13}\geq\frac{D_{2}}{13}\int_{m+1}^{m+2}x^{-13}dx.

and

(3.11)

\displaystyle r_{11}(n)-\gamma\geq

\displaystyle\sum_{m=n}^{\infty}\frac{D_{2}}{13}\int_{m+1}^{m+2}x^{-13}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{D_{2}}{13}\int_{n+1}^{\infty}x^{-13}dx=\frac{D_{2}}{156}\frac{1}{(n+1)^{12}}.

Combining (3.10) and (3.11) completes the proof of (1.7).∎

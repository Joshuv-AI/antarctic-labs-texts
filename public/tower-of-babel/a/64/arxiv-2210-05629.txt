A lower-tail limit in the weak noise theory
Yier Lin and Li-Cheng Tsai

Abstract
. We consider the variational problem associated with the Freidlin–Wentzell Large Deviation Principle of the Stochastic Heat Equation (SHE). The logarithm of the minimizer of the variational problem gives the most probable shape of the solution of the Kardar–Parisi–Zhang equation conditioned on achieving certain unlikely values. Taking the SHE with the delta initial condition and conditioning the value of its solution at the origin at a later time, under suitable scaling, we prove that the logarithm of the minimizer converges to an explicit function as we tune the value of the conditioning to 0 0 . Our result confirms the physics prediction [ KK09 , MKV16 , KMS16 ] .



1.
Introduction

In this paper we study the following variational problem.
For a given
\rho=\rho(t,x)\in L^{2}([0,2]\times\mathbb{R})
, consider the heat equation driven by the potential
\rho
, with the delta initial condition:

(1.1)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\mathsf{Z}=\tfrac{1}{2}\partial_{xx}\mathsf{Z}+\rho\mathsf{Z},\ (t,x)\in(0,2)\times\mathbb{R},

\displaystyle\mathsf{Z}(0,{\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{\scalebox{1.8}{$\cdot$}}})=\delta({\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{\scalebox{1.8}{$\cdot$}}}).

We write
\mathsf{Z}=\mathsf{Z}[\rho]=\mathsf{Z}[\rho](t,x)
for the solution of this equation.
The variational problem of interest is

(1.2)

\displaystyle\inf\big\{\tfrac{1}{2}\|\rho\|^{2}_{2}\,:\,\mathsf{Z}[\rho](2,0)=e^{-\lambda}\big\},

where
\lambda>0
is a parameter, and
\|{\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{\scalebox{1.8}{$\cdot$}}}\|_{2}
denotes the
L^{2}
norm.
It was proven in
[
Tsa22b
, Corollary 2.5(b)]
that, for all large enough
\lambda
, the variational problem (
1.2
) has a unique minimizer
\rho^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{m}}=\rho^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{m}}(\lambda;t,x)
.

This variational problem describes the Freidlin–Wentzell Large Deviation Principle (LDP) of the Stochastic Heat Equation (SHE) and the Kardar–Parisi–Zhang (KPZ) equation.
Consider the SHE
\partial_{t}Z_{\varepsilon}=\frac{1}{2}\partial_{xx}Z_{\varepsilon}+\sqrt{\varepsilon}\xi Z_{\varepsilon}
with
Z_{\varepsilon}(0,{\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{\scalebox{1.8}{$\cdot$}}})=\delta({\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{\scalebox{1.8}{$\cdot$}}})
, where
\xi
denotes the spacetime white noise.
It was proven in
[
LT21
]
that
Z_{\varepsilon}
enjoys the LDP with speed
\varepsilon^{-1}
and the rate function

I_{\mathrm{SHE}}(f):=\inf\{\frac{1}{2}\|\rho\|^{2}_{2}:\mathsf{Z}[\rho]=f\}.

Given the LDP, we see that the variational problem (
1.2
) corresponds to conditioning the value of
Z_{\varepsilon}(2,0)
around
e^{-\lambda}
.
Accordingly, the function
\mathsf{Z}[\rho^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{m}}](t,x)
is the most probable path of
Z_{\varepsilon}
under the conditioning.
The solution of the SHE produces the solution of the KPZ equation through taking logarithm.
Namely,
\log Z_{\varepsilon}=h_{\varepsilon}
, and
h_{\varepsilon}
solves the KPZ equation.
Hence
h:=\log\mathsf{Z}[\rho^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{m}}]
gives the most probable path of the solution of the KPZ equation, which we refer to as the
most probable shape
.

Studying the Freidlin–Wentzell LDPs for the SHE and the KPZ equation through the variational problem goes under the name of the
weak noise theory
.
There has been much development around the weak noise theory in the physics and mathematics literature

[
KK07
,
KK08
,
KK09
,
JKM16
,
KMS16
,
MS17
,
HLDM+18
,
MV18
,

,
SMS18
,
ALM19
,
HMS19
,
SMV19
,
HKLD20
,
HMS21
,
GLLT21
,
LT21
]
, and more recently around the connection of the weak noise theory to integrable PDEs
[
Kra20
,
KLD21
,
KLD22b
,
BSM22
,
KLD22a
,
MMS22
,
Tsa22b
]
.
Among the many questions of interest in the weak noise theory are the behaviors of the most probable shape under certain limits.
Particular limits of interest are sending the conditioned value
\log\mathsf{Z}[\rho](2,0)
to
+\infty
or
-\infty
.
We refer to them as the (one-point) upper- and lower-tail limits, respectively.
For the delta initial condition considered here, the upper- and lower-tail limits of the most probability shape (under suitable scaling) have been predicted in the physics works
[
KK09
,
MKV16
,
KMS16
]
.
Later, the work
[
GLLT21
]
gave a rigorous proof of the upper-tail limit.
The behaviors of the upper- and lower-tail limits are very different, and a rigorous proof of the lower-tail limit remained open.

In this paper, we prove the lower-tail limit of the most probable shape.
Let us introduce the scaling.
Set
\mathsf{Z}_{\lambda}[\rho](t,x):=\mathsf{Z}[\lambda\rho({\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{\scalebox{1.8}{$\cdot$}}},\lambda^{-1/2}{\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{\scalebox{1.8}{$\cdot$}}})](t,x\lambda^{1/2})
.
Under this scaling, the variational problem becomes

(1.3)

\displaystyle\eqref{e.variationalproblem}=\lambda^{5/2}\,\inf\big\{\tfrac{1}{2}\|\rho\|^{2}_{2}\,:\,\mathsf{Z}_{\lambda}[\rho](2,0)=e^{-\lambda}\big\},

and its minimizer is
\rho^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{m}}_{\lambda}(t,x):=\lambda^{-1}\rho^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{m}}(\lambda;t,\lambda^{1/2}x)
.
Let
\mathsf{Z}_{\lambda}[\rho^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{m}}_{\lambda}](t,x):=\mathsf{Z}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{m}}_{\lambda}(t,x)
and
h_{\lambda}(t,x):=\frac{1}{\lambda}\log\mathsf{Z}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{m}}_{\lambda}(t,x)
.
The main result is as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
h_{*}
be given by (
2.7
) and (
2.10
) and depicted in Figure
2
in Section
2
.
For any
\delta>0
,

\displaystyle\lim_{\lambda\to\infty}\,\sup\big\{|h_{\lambda}(t,x)-h_{*}(t,x)|\,:\,(t,x)\in(\delta,2]\times[-\delta^{-1},\delta^{-1}]\big\}=0.

Let us provide a context of Theorem
1.1
in terms of Hamilton–Jacobi-Fokker–Planck (HJ-FP) equations.
To begin, one can derive the Euler–Lagrangian equation for the variational problem (
1.3
) and turn the equation into a system of Hamilton equations.
This derivation is done in the physics literature of the weak noise theory (see
[
KMS16
, Appendix]
,
[
MKV16
, Supp. Mat. A]
,
[
KLD21
, Supp. Mat. A]
) and has recently been proven in
[
Tsa22b
, Theorem 2.1]
(at the level of
\mathsf{Z}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{m}}_{\lambda}
).
At the level of
h_{\lambda}
, the Hamilton equations are

(1.4a)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}h_{\lambda}

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{2\lambda}\partial_{xx}h_{\lambda}+\tfrac{1}{2}(\partial_{x}h_{\lambda})^{2}+\rho^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{m}}_{\lambda},

(1.4b)

\displaystyle-\partial_{t}\rho^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{m}}_{\lambda}

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{2\lambda}\partial_{xx}\rho^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{m}}_{\lambda}-\partial_{x}(\rho^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{m}}_{\lambda}\partial_{x}h_{\lambda}),

where the second equation is solved backward in time with a suitable terminal condition at
t=2
.
This system can be viewed as an instance of the HJ-FP equations studied in mean field games
[
Lio07
,
Car10
,
GLL11
]
.
The lower-tail limit
\lambda\to\infty
here corresponds to the inviscid limit in the language of mean field games and PDE.
We expect that, for
\rho^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{m}}_{\lambda}\leq 0
, the solution of (
1.4
) converges to the entropy solution of

(1.5a)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}h_{*}

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{2}(\partial_{x}h_{*})^{2}+\rho_{*},

(1.5b)

\displaystyle-\partial_{t}\rho_{*}

\displaystyle=-\partial_{x}(\rho_{*}\partial_{x}h_{*}).

The physic works
[
KK09
,
MKV16
,
KMS16
]
solved (
1.5
) for the entropy solution and found an explicit expression for
\rho_{*}
.
Accordingly, the function
h_{*}
can be expressed in terms of
\rho_{*}
; see Section
2.2
.

From the PDE perspective, the challenge of proving Theorem
1.1
lies in controlling
\rho^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{m}}_{\lambda}
.
We will show in Appendix
A.2
that
\rho^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{m}}_{\lambda}\leq 0
for all
\lambda
large enough, and we will explain in Section
2.2
that the results from
[
LT21
]
gives
\rho^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{m}}_{\lambda}\to\rho_{*}
in
L^{2}
.
These properties alone, however, do
not
suffice for
h_{\lambda}\to h_{*}
.
For
b>0
, set
w_{\lambda}(t,x):=\rho_{*}(t,x)\mathbf{1}_{\{|x|>\lambda^{-b}\}}
, which is non-positive and converges to
\rho_{*}
in
L^{2}
.
It is possible to show that, there exist a small enough
b
and a neighborhood
\Omega
around
(2,0)
such that
\lim_{\lambda\to\infty}\inf_{\Omega}|\frac{1}{\lambda}\log\mathsf{Z}_{\lambda}[w_{\lambda}]-h_{*}|>0
.
In many settings of mean field games, the term
\rho^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{m}}_{\lambda}
in (
1.4a
) is replaced by a better-behaved term, and phenomena like the one just shown does not occur.

Our proof proceeds through the Feynman–Kac formula and bypasses the need to control
\rho^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{m}}_{\lambda}
.
The first key observation is that the property
\rho^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{m}}_{\lambda}\to\rho_{*}
in
L^{2}
alone
does
suffice for proving the lower bound
(\liminf_{\lambda\to\infty}h_{\lambda})\geq h_{*}
and in fact a stronger version of it: Proposition
3.1
.
Roughly speaking, Proposition
3.1
states that the change of
h_{\lambda}
along a geodesic (defined in Section
2.2
) is bounded from below by the change of
h_{*}
along the same geodesic.
The second key observation is that the upper bound, which is the more subtle bound, follows by combining Proposition
3.1
and the property
\mathsf{Z}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{m}}_{\lambda}(2,0)=e^{\lambda h_{\lambda}(2,0)}=e^{-\lambda}
, as well as a Hölder continuity estimate.
This observation is manifested in the proof in Section
5.1
.

Let us mention that
\mathsf{Z}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{m}}_{\lambda}
permits an explicit formula.
The expression was derived by
[
KLD21
]
based on
[
Kra20
]
at a physics level of rigor, and later proven in
[
Tsa22b
, Theorem 2.3, Corollary 2.5]
.
Extracting the limit of
h_{\lambda}:=\frac{1}{\lambda}\log\mathsf{Z}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{m}}_{\lambda}
from this explicit formula is an interesting open problem and will provide another proof of Theorem
1.1
.

The analysis has been carried out in the physics literature for various initial and boundary conditions

KLD18a
]
.
While the explicit formulas does not give direct access to the most probable shape, they allow for studying LDPs for the KPZ equation in the long time regime

2.
Notation and tools

2.1.
The Feynman–Kac formula

Let us introduce the Feynman–Kac formula for
\mathsf{Z}_{\lambda}[\rho]
.
For
s\leq t\in[0,2]
, let
\mathrm{BB}_{\lambda}((t,x)\to(s,y))
denote the law of the following Brownian bridge, which goes
backward
in time:

(2.1)

\displaystyle\lambda^{-1/2}\big(B(t-u)-\tfrac{u-s}{t-s}B(t-s)\big)+\tfrac{(u-s)x+(t-u)y}{t-s},\qquad u\in[s,t],

where
B
denotes a standard Brownian motion.
For any
\rho\in L^{2}([0,2]\times\mathbb{R})
, define the Feynman–Kac expectation

(2.2)

\displaystyle\mathsf{FK}_{\lambda}[\rho](s,y;t,x):=\mathbb{E}\Big[\exp\Big(\int_{s}^{t}\lambda\rho(u,W_{\lambda}(u))\,\mathrm{d}u\Big)\Big],

\displaystyle W_{\lambda}\sim\mathrm{BB}_{\lambda}((t,x)\to(s,y)).

Let
p_{\lambda}(t,x):=\exp(-\lambda\frac{x^{2}}{2t})\sqrt{\lambda/(2\pi t)}
denote the scaled heat kernel.
We have the Feynman–Kac formula

(2.3)

\displaystyle\mathsf{Z}_{\lambda}[\rho](t,x)

\displaystyle=p_{\lambda}(t,x)\,\mathsf{FK}_{\lambda}[\rho](0,0;t,x),

(2.4)

\displaystyle\mathsf{Z}_{\lambda}[\rho](t,x)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}p_{\lambda}(t-s,x-y)\,\mathsf{FK}_{\lambda}[\rho](s,y;t,x)\,\mathsf{Z}_{\lambda}[\rho](s,y)\,\mathrm{d}y,\qquad s<t\in(0,2].

For the sake of completeness, we provide a proof of (
2.3
) in Appendix
A.3
.
The formula (
2.4
) follows from (
2.3
) via conditioning
W_{\lambda}
at time
s
.

2.2.
The limits
\rho_{*}
and
h_{*}

We begin by introducing
\rho_{*}
, which is the limit of
\rho^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{m}}_{\lambda}
as
\lambda\to\infty
.
Let
r
be the unique
C^{1}[1,2)
-valued solution with
r|_{(1,2)}>\frac{\pi}{2}
of the differential equation

(2.5)

\displaystyle\dot{r}=2^{1/2}\pi^{-1/2}r^{2}(r-\tfrac{\pi}{2})^{1/2},\qquad r(1)=\tfrac{\pi}{2},

and symmetrically extend
r
to
C^{1}(0,2)
by setting
r(t):=r(2-t)
for
t\in(0,1)
.
Set
\ell(t):=1/r(t)
.
Integrating the differential equation by separation of variables and analyzing the result near
t=2
give

(2.6)

\displaystyle r(t)=r(2-t)

\displaystyle=(\tfrac{2}{3})^{2/3}(\tfrac{\pi}{2})^{1/3}t^{-2/3}+o(t^{-2/3})\text{ when }t\to 0.

In particular,
r\to+\infty
as
t\to 0
or
2
.
We hence set
\ell(0)=\ell(2):=0
to make
\ell\in C[0,2]
.
The limit
\rho_{*}
is given by

(2.7)

\displaystyle\rho_{*}(t,x):=-\tfrac{1}{2\pi}r(t)\big(1-\tfrac{x^{2}}{\ell(t)^{2}}\big)_{+}.

This expression was derived in the physics works
[
KK09
,
MKV16
,
KMS16
]
by solving (
1.5
) for the entropy solution.
In the mathematics literature,
[
LT21
]
gives the
L^{2}
convergence

(2.8)

\displaystyle\lim_{\lambda\to\infty}\|\rho^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{m}}_{\lambda}-\rho_{*}\|_{2}=0.

More precisely, this follows by combining
\|\rho^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{m}}_{\lambda}-\rho_{*}\|^{2}_{2}=\|\rho^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{m}}_{\lambda}\|^{2}_{2}-\|\rho_{*}\|^{2}_{2}+2\langle\rho_{*},\rho_{*}-\rho^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{m}}_{\lambda}\rangle
and Equations (4.28) and (4.32) in
[
LT21
]
.
Note that the expression within the limsup in
[
LT21
, Equation (4.28)]
is
\frac{1}{2}\|\rho^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{m}}_{\lambda}\|^{2}_{2}
.

We now turn to
h_{*}
. The function is defined by

(2.9)

\displaystyle h_{*}(t,x):=-\inf\Big\{\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1}{2}\dot{\gamma}^{2}(s)-\rho_{*}(s,\gamma(s))\,\mathrm{d}s\,:\,\gamma\in H^{1}((0,0)\to(t,x))\Big\},

with the convention
\inf\emptyset:=\infty
.
Hereafter
H^{1}((s,y)\to(t,x))
denotes the space of paths
\gamma:[s,t]\to\mathbb{R}
such that
(\gamma(s),\gamma(t))=(y,x)
and
\int_{s}^{t}\dot{\gamma}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}u<\infty
.
To understand the motivation of this definition, recall that
h_{\lambda}:=\frac{1}{\lambda}\log\mathsf{Z}_{\lambda}[\rho^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{m}}_{\lambda}]
.
In view of (
2.8
), it is natural to expect (but non-trivial to prove) that this function approximates
\frac{1}{\lambda}\log\mathsf{Z}[\rho_{*}]
as
\lambda\to\infty
.
Since
\rho_{*}
is uniformly continuous except near
(0,0)
and
(2,0)
, through the Feynman–Kac formula (
2.3
), the last expression can be analyzed by Varadhan’s lemma, which yields (
2.9
) as
\lambda\to\infty
.

Crucial to our analysis is the notion of geodesics.
Proposition 4.3 in
[
LT21
]
shows that the infimum is achieved in
H^{1}((0,0)\to(t,x))
for all
(t,x)\in(0,2]\times\mathbb{R}
.
We call a path realizing the infimum a
geodesic
.
Proposition 4.3 in
[
LT21
]
characterizes all geodesics, and they are depicted in Figure
2
; see the caption there.
We write
(s,y)\xrightarrow{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{geod.}}(t,x)
for “
(s,y)
and
(t,x)
are connected by a geodesic with
s\leq t\in[0,2]
”, and write
(s,y)\xrightarrow{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{geod.}\theta}(t,x)
to signify that the geodesic is
\theta
.
We can rewrite (
2.9
) more explicitly as

(2.10)

\displaystyle h_{*}(t,x)=\int_{0}^{t}\Big(-\frac{1}{2}\dot{\theta}^{2}(u)+\rho_{*}(u,\theta(u))\Big)\,\mathrm{d}u,

\displaystyle(0,0)\xrightarrow{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{geod.}\theta}(t,x).

Note that, for any
(t,x)\neq(2,0)
with
t>0
, there exists a unique geodesic that connects
(0,0)
and
(t,x)
; when
(t,x)=(2,0)
, the expression (
2.10
) holds for
\theta=a\ell
, for all
a\in[-1,1]
; when
t=0
, by definition
h_{*}(0,x)=0\mathbf{1}_{\{x=0\}}-\infty\mathbf{1}_{\{x\neq 0\}}
; a plot of
h_{*}
is shown in Figure
2
.
Further, for any given
(s,y)\xrightarrow{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{geod.}\theta}(t,x)
, the geodesic
\theta
can always be extended backward in time to
(0,0)
.
This together with (
2.10
) gives

(2.11)

\displaystyle h_{*}(t,x)-h_{*}(s,y)=\int_{s}^{t}\Big(-\frac{1}{2}\dot{\theta}^{2}(u)+\rho_{*}(u,\theta(u))\Big)\,\mathrm{d}u,

\displaystyle(s,y)\xrightarrow{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{geod.}\theta}(t,x).

Figure 1.
The geodesics. Note that the
t
axis goes top-down. The thick lines are
\pm\ell
. We refer to the region bounded by
\pm\ell
the lens region. The geodesics within the lens region are
a\ell
, for
a\in[-1,1]
. The geodesics outside of the lens region are straight lines that touch
\pm\ell
at tangent.

h_{*}
. The curves on
h_{*}
are traced out by the geodesics. For better visualization, we did not show some part of
h_{*}
outside of the lens region.

3.
Lower bound

In this section we prove the following proposition.

Proposition 3.1
.

For any
\{\rho^{(\lambda)}\}_{\lambda}\subset L^{2}([0,2]\times\mathbb{R})
with
\rho^{(\lambda)}\to\rho_{*}
in
L^{2}
,

\displaystyle\liminf_{\lambda\to\infty}\,\inf\Big\{\frac{1}{\lambda}\log\mathsf{FK}_{\lambda}[\rho^{(\lambda)}](s,y;t,x)-\Big(h_{*}(t,x)-h_{*}(s,y)+\frac{(y-x)^{2}}{2(t-s)}\Big)\Big\}\geq 0,

where the infimum goes over all
(s,y)\xrightarrow{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{geod.}}(t,x)
.

Proposition
3.1
already gives the lower half of Theorem
1.1
.
To see how, set
(s,y)=(0,0)
and
\rho^{(\lambda)}=\rho^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{m}}_{\lambda}
in Proposition
3.1
, note that
h_{*}(0,0):=0
and that any
(t,x)\in(0,2]\times\mathbb{R}
is connected to
(0,0)
through a geodesic, and combine the result with (
2.3
) for
\rho=\rho^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{m}}_{\lambda}
(note that
\mathsf{Z}_{\lambda}[\rho^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{m}}_{\lambda}]=:\exp(\lambda h_{\lambda})
).
Doing so gives the following.

Corollary 3.2

(The lower half of Theorem
1.1
)
.

For any
\delta>0
,

\displaystyle\liminf_{\lambda\to\infty}\ \inf\Big\{h_{\lambda}(t,x)-h_{*}(t,x)\,:(t,x)\in[\delta,2]\times[-\delta^{-1},\delta^{-1}]\Big\}\geq 0.

Proof of Proposition
3.1
.

We start by deriving an inequality:
For any
\rho\in L^{2}([0,2]\times\mathbb{R})
and
\gamma\in H^{1}((s,y)\to(t,x))
,

(3.1)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\lambda}\log\mathsf{FK}_{\lambda}[\rho](s,y;t,x)\geq\int_{s}^{t}\mathbb{E}\big[\rho(t-u,(W^{0}_{\lambda}-\gamma)(u))\big]\,\mathrm{d}u-\frac{1}{2}\int_{s}^{t}\dot{\gamma}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}u+\frac{(x-y)^{2}}{2(t-s)},

where
W^{0}_{\lambda}=\mathrm{BB}_{\lambda}((t,0)\to(s,0))
, which goes
backward
in time; see (
2.1
).
To derive this inequality, take (
2.2
) and express the Brownian bridge as in (
2.1
).
Let
\gamma_{\text{tilted}}(u):=\gamma(u)-\frac{1}{t-s}((u-s)x+(t-u)y)
.
Apply the Cameron–Martin–Girsanov theorem to transform the measure of the standard Brownian motion
B
to that of
B+\lambda^{1/2}\gamma_{\text{tilted}}
:

\displaystyle\mathsf{FK}_{\lambda}[\rho](s,y;t,x)=\mathbb{E}\Big[\exp\Big(\int_{s}^{t}\lambda\rho(u,(W^{0}_{\lambda}-\gamma)(u))\,\mathrm{d}u+\lambda^{1/2}\int_{s}^{t}\dot{\gamma}_{\text{tilted}}\,\mathrm{d}B-\frac{\lambda}{2}\int_{s}^{t}\dot{\gamma}_{\text{tilted}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}u\Big)\Big].

Next, apply Jensen’s inequality
\frac{1}{\lambda}\log\mathbb{E}[\exp(\ldots)]\geq\frac{1}{\lambda}\mathbb{E}[(\ldots)]
to the right side.
Note that
\mathbb{E}[\int_{s}^{t}\dot{\gamma}_{\text{tilted}}\,\mathrm{d}B]=0
and that
\frac{1}{2}\int_{s}^{t}\dot{\gamma}^{2}_{\text{tilted}}\,\mathrm{d}u=\frac{1}{2}\int_{s}^{t}\dot{\gamma}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}u-(x-y)^{2}/(2(t-s))
.
We see that (
3.1
) follows.

Let us explain the idea of the rest of the proof.
Fix
(s,y)\xrightarrow{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{geod.}\theta}(t,x)
.
In (
3.1
), set
\gamma\mapsto-\theta
and
\rho\mapsto\rho^{(\lambda)}
:

(3.2)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\lambda}\log\mathsf{FK}_{\lambda}[\rho^{(\lambda)}](s,y;t,x)\geq\int_{s}^{t}\mathbb{E}\big[\rho^{(\lambda)}(u,(\theta+W^{0}_{\lambda})(u))\big]\,\mathrm{d}u-\frac{1}{2}\int_{s}^{t}\dot{\theta}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}u+\frac{(x-y)^{2}}{2(t-s)}.

On the right side, only the first term depends on
\lambda
.
To guess what the limit of it could be, recall that
\rho^{(\lambda)}\to\rho_{*}
in
L^{2}
, and note that
W^{0}_{\lambda}
converges in law to the constant-zero path as
\lambda\to\infty
.
We hence expect the limit to be
\int_{s}^{t}\rho_{*}(u,\theta(u))\,\mathrm{d}u
.
Once the first term on the right side of (
3.2
) is replaced by
\int_{s}^{t}\rho_{*}(u,\theta(u))\,\mathrm{d}u
,
by (
2.11
), the entire right side becomes
h_{*}(t,x)-h_{*}(s,y)+(x-y)^{2}/(2(t-s))
, which is the desired lower bound.
Hence it suffices to pass the first term on the right hand of (
3.2
) to the limit.

However
, taking such a limit seems challenging, because we only have
\rho^{(\lambda)}\to\rho_{*}
in
L^{2}
, not in
L^{\infty}
.
To circumvent this challenge, we perturb
\theta
and average over the perturbation:

(3.3)

\displaystyle\theta_{a}(u):=\theta(u)+a\eta(u),

\displaystyle\eta(u):=(u-s)^{3/4}\mathbf{1}_{[s,(t+s)/2]}(u)+(t-u)^{3/4}\mathbf{1}_{((t+s)/2,t]}(u).

In (
3.1
), substitute in
\gamma\mapsto-\theta_{a}
and
\rho\mapsto\rho^{(\lambda)}
.
For small
a_{0}>0
, apply
\frac{1}{a_{0}}\int_{0}^{a_{0}}({\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{\scalebox{1.8}{$\cdot$}}})\,\mathrm{d}a
to the result.
We have

(3.4)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\lambda}\log\mathsf{FK}_{\lambda}[\rho^{(\lambda)}](s,y;t,x)\geq\frac{1}{a_{0}}\int_{0}^{a_{0}}\hskip-6.0pt\int_{s}^{t}\mathbb{E}\big[\rho^{(\lambda)}(u,(\theta_{a}+W^{0}_{\lambda})(u))\big]\mathrm{d}u\mathrm{d}a-\frac{1}{2a_{0}}\int_{0}^{a_{0}}\hskip-6.0pt\int_{s}^{t}\dot{\theta}_{a}^{2}\mathrm{d}u\mathrm{d}a+\frac{(x-y)^{2}}{2(t-s)}.

Compared with (
3.2
), the right side of (
3.4
) is better behaved.
We will take
\lambda\to\infty
first and
a_{0}\to 0
later.

Below, we will show that, uniformly over
(s,y)\xrightarrow{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{geod.}}(t,x)
,

(3.5)

\displaystyle\lim_{\lambda\to\infty}\frac{1}{a_{0}}

\displaystyle\int_{s}^{t}\int_{0}^{a_{0}}\mathbb{E}\big[\big|\rho^{(\lambda)}(u,(\theta_{a}+W^{0}_{\lambda})(u))-\rho_{*}(u,(\theta_{a}+W^{0}_{\lambda})(u))\big|\big]\,\mathrm{d}a\mathrm{d}u=0,

(3.6)

\displaystyle\lim_{(a_{0},\lambda)\to(0,\infty)}\frac{1}{a_{0}}

\displaystyle\int_{s}^{t}\int_{0}^{a_{0}}\mathbb{E}\big[\big|\rho_{*}(u,(\theta_{a}+W^{0}_{\lambda})(u))-\rho_{*}(u,\theta(u))\big|\big]\,\mathrm{d}a\mathrm{d}u=0,

(3.7)

\displaystyle\lim_{a_{0}\to 0}\Big(\frac{1}{2a_{0}}

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{a_{0}}\int_{s}^{t}\dot{\theta}_{a}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}u\mathrm{d}a-\frac{1}{2}\int_{s}^{t}\dot{\theta}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}u\Big)=0.

Once these limits are obtained, taking the iterated limit
\lim_{a_{0}\to 0}\lim_{\lambda\to\infty}
on both sides of (
3.4
) and combining the result with (
2.11
) completes the proof.

To prove (
3.5
), consider the expression within the limit, swap the integrals and the expectation, and perform a change of variables
a\mapsto z:=\theta(u)+W^{0}_{\lambda}(u)+a\eta(u)
.
Doing so gives

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\frac{1}{a_{0}}\int_{s}^{t}\frac{1}{\eta(u)}\int_{(\theta+W^{0}_{\lambda})(u)}^{{(\theta+W^{0}_{\lambda}+a_{0}\eta)(u)}}|\rho^{(\lambda)}-\rho_{*}|(u,z)\,\mathrm{d}z\mathrm{d}u\Big].

Apply the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality over the double integral to bound the expression by

\mathbb{E}[\|\rho^{(\lambda)}-\rho_{*}\|_{2}(\frac{1}{a_{0}}\int_{s}^{t}\frac{1}{\eta}\,\mathrm{d}u)^{1/2}].

The expression within the last expectation is deterministic; the last integral is uniformly bounded over
s<t\in[0,2]
and does not depend on
x,y
.
Sending
\lambda\to\infty
using
\|\rho^{(\lambda)}-\rho_{*}\|_{2}\to 0
gives the desired result (
3.5
).

To prove (
3.6
), fix a small
\delta>0
and divide the
u
integral into
[s,t]\cap([0,\delta)\cup(2-\delta,2])
and
[s,t]\cap[\delta,2-\delta]
.
For the former, by (
A.1
), the contribution is bounced by
c\,\delta^{1/3}
.
For the latter, note that the function
\rho_{*}
is uniformly Lipschitz on
[\delta,2-\delta]\times\mathbb{R}
.
The contribution of the latter is hence bounded by
\int_{s}^{t}c(\delta)\,(|a_{0}|+\mathbb{E}|W^{0}_{\lambda}(u)|)\,\mathrm{d}u
, where
c(\delta)<\infty
depends only on
\delta
.
For any fixed
\delta>0
, the last integral converges to zero as
{(a_{0},\lambda)\to(0,\infty)}
uniformly over
s\leq t\in[0,2]
, and does not depend on
x,y
.
The desired result (
3.6
) follows.

Move onto (
3.7
).
Let
t_{\text{exit}}:=\inf\{u:|\theta(u)|>|\ell(u)|\}
be the time that
\theta
exits the lens region, with the convention
\inf\emptyset=+\infty
; see Figure
2
.
Expand the expression within the limit in (
3.7
) and evaluate the integrals over
a
to get

\displaystyle\frac{a_{0}^{2}}{6}\int_{s}^{t}\dot{\eta}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}u+\frac{a_{0}}{2}\int_{[s,t]\cap[0,t_{\text{exit}}]}\dot{\eta}\,\dot{\theta}\,\mathrm{d}u+\frac{a_{0}}{2}\int_{[s,t]\cap[t_{\text{exit}},2]}\dot{\eta}\,\dot{\theta}\,\mathrm{d}u.

Given the prefactors, which converge to
0
as
a_{0}\to 0
, it suffices to argue that the integrals are uniformly (in
\theta
) bounded.
This holds for the first integral since it is independent of
\theta
.
For the second integral, we have
|\dot{\theta}(u)|\leq|\dot{\ell}(u)|
.
This and (
A.2
) shows the uniform boundedness.
For the third integral, the derivative
\dot{\theta}
is a constant
\pm\dot{\ell}(t_{\text{exit}})
; see Figure
2
.
The third integral is hence
\pm\dot{\ell}(t_{\text{exit}})(\eta|_{\partial([s,t]\cap[t_{\text{exit}},2])})
.
Using (
A.2
) and (
3.3
), it is not hard to show the uniform boundedness.
The desired result (
3.7
) follows.
∎

4.
Spatial Hölder continuity

Proposition 4.1
.

For any
\delta>0
, there exists a constant
c(\delta)\in(0,\infty)
such that, for all
(t,x),(t,x^{\prime})\in[\delta,2]\times[-\delta^{-1},\delta^{-1}]
and for all
\lambda\geq\frac{1}{c(\delta)}
, we have

|h_{\lambda}(t,x)-h_{\lambda}(t,x^{\prime})|\leq c(\delta)|x-x^{\prime}|^{\frac{2}{13}-\delta}.

Proof.

We begin by describing the structure of the proof.
By the Feynman–Kac formula (
2.3
), it suffices to show that, for all
(t,x),(t,x^{\prime})\in[\delta,2]\times[-\delta^{-1},\delta^{-1}]
,

(4.1)

\displaystyle\mathsf{FK}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{m}}_{\lambda}(0,0;t,x):=\mathbb{E}\Big[\exp\Big(\int_{0}^{t}\lambda\rho^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{m}}_{\lambda}(u,W^{x}_{\lambda}(u))\,\mathrm{d}u\Big)\Big]\geq\mathsf{FK}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{m}}_{\lambda}(0,0;t,x^{\prime})\frac{p_{\lambda}(t,x^{\prime})}{p_{\lambda}(t,x)}\cdot e^{-\lambda c(\delta)|x-x^{\prime}|^{\frac{2}{13}-\delta}},

where
W^{x}_{\lambda}\sim\mathrm{BB}((t,x)\to(0,0))
and
W^{x^{\prime}}_{\lambda}\sim\mathrm{BB}((t,x^{\prime})\to(0,0))
.
Once this is done, taking the logarithm on both sides and swapping
x
and
x^{\prime}
give the desired result.
To prove (
4.1
), we will start from the left side, and perform a sequence of “surgeries”.
The goal is to convert the expectation on the left side to the expectation on the right side.
Each surgery will reduce the expression by a factor, and we will need to control the factor.

The first surgery is a truncation in time.
Let
s\in(0,t)
be close to
t
, which will be fixed later, and consider

\mathcal{A}_{1}:=\{\int_{s}^{t}|\rho^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{m}}_{\lambda}(u,W^{x}_{\lambda}(u))|\,\mathrm{d}u\leq(t-s)^{b_{1}}\}.

Hereafter, our analysis will involve powers of
(t-s)
and
|x-x^{\prime}|
.
The exponents
b_{1},b_{2},\ldots
will all be
\in(0,1)
and be fixed later.
Within the expectation on the left side of (
4.1
), introduce the indicator
\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{A}}
.
Doing so makes the expectation smaller.
Given the indicator, the integral from
s
to
t
is at least
-(t-s)^{b_{1}}
.
Hence

(Surgery I)

\displaystyle\text{left side of \eqref{e.p.holder.goal}}\geq e^{-\lambda(t-s)^{b_{1}}}I_{1},

\displaystyle I_{1}:=\mathbb{E}\Big[\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{A}}\exp\Big(\int_{0}^{s}\lambda\rho^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{m}}_{\lambda}(u,W^{x}_{\lambda}(u))\,\mathrm{d}u\Big)\Big].

We proceed to the second surgery, which is applied to
I_{1}
.
Within the expectation in (
Surgery I
), condition on
W^{x}_{\lambda}(s)=y
.
Under such a conditioning, the two segments of the bridge
\{W^{x}_{\lambda}(u)\}_{u\in[s,t]}
and
\{W^{x}_{\lambda}(u)\}_{u\in[0,s]}
are independent, and their laws are respectively
\mathrm{BB}((t,x)\to(s,y))
and
\mathrm{BB}((s,y)\to(0,0))
.
Let

(4.2)

\displaystyle f(\lambda,y):=\mathbb{P}\Big[\int_{s}^{t}\big|\rho^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{m}}_{\lambda}(u,\overline{W}_{\lambda}(u)\big)\big|\,\mathrm{d}u\leq(t-s)^{b_{2}}\,\Big],

\displaystyle\overline{W}_{\lambda}\sim\mathrm{BB}((t,x)\to(s,y)).

We have

I_{1}=\mathbb{E}[f(\lambda,W^{x}_{\lambda}(s))\,\mathsf{FK}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{m}}_{\lambda}(0,0;s,W^{x}_{\lambda}(s))].

To complete the second surgery, we need a lower bound on
f(\lambda,z)
that is uniform in
z\in\mathbb{R}
.
In (
4.2
), express the bridge as a Brownian motion (similar to (
2.1
)) and apply the Cameron–Martin–Girsanov theorem.
We have, for any
\gamma\in H^{1}((t,x)\to(s,y))
,

(4.3)

\displaystyle f(\lambda,y)=\mathbb{E}\Big[e^{-\frac{\lambda}{2}\int_{s}^{t}\dot{\gamma}^{2}\mathrm{d}u}e^{\lambda^{1/2}\int_{s}^{t}\dot{\gamma}\mathrm{d}B}\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{A}_{2}}\Big],

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{2}:=\Big\{\int_{s}^{t}|\rho^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{m}}_{\lambda}(u,\overline{W}_{\lambda}-\gamma)|\mathrm{d}u\leq(t-s)^{b_{1}}\Big\}.

Let
\eta(u):=(u-s)^{b_{2}}\mathbf{1}_{[s,(t+s)/2]}(u)+(t-u)^{b_{2}}\mathbf{1}_{((t+s)/2,t]}(u)
and set
\gamma=a\eta
, for
a\in\mathbb{R}
.
The first exponential factor in (
4.3
) is at least
\exp(-c\lambda\,(t-s)^{2b_{2}-1})
.
To control the second exponential factor, introduce the indicator
\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{A}_{3}}
, where

\mathcal{A}_{3}:=\{|\int_{s}^{t}\dot{\gamma}\mathrm{d}B|\leq\lambda^{1/2}(t-s)^{b_{3}}\}.

We have

f(\lambda,y)\geq\exp(-\lambda\,c\,((t-s)^{2b_{2}-1}+(t-s)^{b_{3}}))\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{A}_{2}\cap\mathcal{A}_{3}].

For the last factor, use
\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{A}_{2}\cap\mathcal{A}_{3}]\geq 1-\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{A}_{2}^{\mathrm{c}}]-\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{A}_{3}^{\mathrm{c}}]
and use Markov’s inequality to bound

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{A}_{2}^{\mathrm{c}}]

\displaystyle\leq(t-s)^{-b_{1}}\int_{s}^{t}\mathbb{E}\big[\big|\rho^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{m}}_{\lambda}(u,\overline{W}_{\lambda}-a\eta)\big|\big]\mathrm{d}u,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{A}_{3}^{\mathrm{c}}]

\displaystyle\leq\big(\lambda^{1/2}(t-s)^{b_{3}}\big)^{-2}\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big|\int_{s}^{t}\dot{\gamma}\mathrm{d}B\Big|^{2}\Big]\leq\frac{c}{\lambda}(t-s)^{2b_{2}-2b_{3}-1}.

Putting these inequalities together and average the result over
a\in[0,1]
.
We have

\displaystyle f(\lambda,y)\geq e^{-\lambda c\,((t-s)^{2b_{2}-1}+(t-s)^{b_{3}})}\Big(1-\frac{c}{\lambda}(t-s)^{2b_{2}-2b_{3}-1}-(t-s)^{-b_{1}}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{s}^{t}\mathbb{E}\big[\big|\rho^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{m}}_{\lambda}(u,\overline{W}_{\lambda}-a\eta)\big|\big]\mathrm{d}u\mathrm{d}a\Big).

For the last term, apply the same argument in the proof of (
3.5
):
Swap the integrals with the expectation, perform a change of variables
a\mapsto y:=\overline{W}_{\lambda}+a\eta
, and apply the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality over the integrals.
We bound the term by
c\,\|\rho^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{m}}_{\lambda}\|_{2}(t-s)^{(1-b_{2})/2}\leq c\,(t-s)^{(1-b_{2})/2}
.
This gives a lower bound of
f(\lambda,y)
.
Recall the expression of
I_{1}
from after (
4.2
).
We have now completed the second surgery

(Surgery II)

\displaystyle I_{1}\geq e^{-\lambda c\,((t-s)^{2b_{2}-1}+(t-s)^{b_{3}})}\big(1-\big(\tfrac{c}{\lambda}(t-s)^{2b_{2}-2b_{3}-1}+c\,(t-s)^{\frac{1}{2}-b_{1}-\frac{b_{2}}{2}}\big)\big)_{+}\,I_{2},

where
I_{2}:=\mathbb{E}[\mathsf{FK}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{m}}_{\lambda}(0,0;s,W^{x}_{\lambda}(s))]
.

Before moving on to the third surgery, let us fix the exponents and combine (
Surgery I
) and (
Surgery II
).
Set
b_{1}=\frac{1}{11}
,
b_{2}=\frac{7}{11}
, and
b_{3}=\frac{1}{11}
.
Assume
|t-s|
is small enough so that in (
Surgery II
) we have
(1-(\ldots))_{+}\geq\exp(-c\,(\ldots))
.
Using this in (
Surgery II
) and combining the result with (
Surgery I
) give

(Surgery I+II)

\displaystyle\text{(left side of \eqref{e.p.holder.goal})}\geq e^{-\lambda c\,(t-s)^{\frac{1}{11}}}I_{2},

\displaystyle I_{2}:=\mathbb{E}[\mathsf{FK}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{m}}_{\lambda}(0,0;s,W^{x}_{\lambda}(s))].

In the third and last surgery, we will convert
I_{2}
to the right side of (
4.1
).
The first step is to convert the
W^{x}_{\lambda}(s)
in
I_{2}
into
W^{x^{\prime}}_{\lambda}(s)
.
Let
q^{x}(y)
and
q^{x^{\prime}}(y)
denote their respective probability density functions.
Write
I_{2}
as the integral
\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathsf{FK}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{m}}_{\lambda}(0,0;s,y)q^{x}(y)\,\mathrm{d}y
.
Note that
q^{x}(y)
and
q^{x^{\prime}}(y)
can be expressed in terms of the heat kernel.
Doing so gives
q^{x}(y)=\frac{p_{\lambda}(x^{\prime})}{p_{\lambda}(x)}\exp(-\lambda\frac{|x-x^{\prime}|^{2}}{2(t-s)})\exp(-\lambda\frac{(x^{\prime}-y)(x-x^{\prime}))}{(t-s)})q^{x^{\prime}}(y)
.
To control the last exponential factor, we restrict the integral to
|y-x^{\prime}|\leq\frac{1}{2}|x-x^{\prime}|^{b_{4}}
.
Doing so makes the resulting integral smaller.
We have

\displaystyle I_{2}\geq\frac{p_{\lambda}(x^{\prime})}{p_{\lambda}(x)}e^{-\lambda\frac{|x-x^{\prime}|^{2}+|x-x^{\prime}|^{1+b_{4}}}{2(t-s)}}\mathbb{E}\big[\mathsf{FK}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{m}}_{\lambda}(0,0;s,W^{x^{\prime}}_{\lambda}(s))\mathbf{1}\{|W^{x^{\prime}}_{\lambda}(s)-x^{\prime}|\leq\tfrac{1}{2}|x-x^{\prime}|^{b_{4}}\}\big].

Note that the ratio of heat kernels is expected since our goal is (
4.1
).
In the last expectation, write
\mathbf{1}\{\ldots\}=1-\mathbf{1}\{\ldots\}
and decompose the expectation into two accordingly.
The first expectation is equal to
\mathbb{E}[\exp(\int_{0}^{s}\lambda\rho^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{m}}_{\lambda}(u,W^{x^{\prime}}_{\lambda}(u))\,\mathrm{d}u)]
.
Granted that
\rho^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{m}}_{\lambda}\leq 0
(Appendix
A.2
), we extend the integral from
[0,s]
to
[0,t]
, which only makes the expectation smaller.
The result is exactly
\mathsf{FK}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{m}}_{\lambda}(0,0;t,x^{\prime})
.
For the latter expectation, use
\mathsf{FK}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{m}}_{\lambda}\leq 1
, which follows from (
2.2
) and
\rho^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{m}}_{\lambda}\leq 0
, and use
\mathbb{P}[|W^{x^{\prime}}_{\lambda}(s)-x^{\prime}|>|x-x^{\prime}|^{+b_{4}}]\leq\exp(-\lambda\frac{|x-x^{\prime}|^{2b_{4}}}{c(\delta)(t-s)})
, for all
x,x^{\prime}\in[-\delta^{-1},\delta^{-1}]
.
We have

\displaystyle I_{2}\geq\frac{p_{\lambda}(x^{\prime})}{p_{\lambda}(x)}e^{-\lambda\frac{|x-x^{\prime}|^{2}+|x-x^{\prime}|^{1+b_{4}}}{2(t-s)}}\big(\mathsf{FK}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{m}}_{\lambda}(0,0;t,x^{\prime})-e^{-\lambda\frac{|x-x^{\prime}|^{2b_{4}}}{c(\delta)(t-s)}}\big)_{+}.

Combining Proposition
3.1
and (
A.3
) shows that
\mathsf{FK}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{m}}_{\lambda}(0,0;s,y)\geq e^{-\lambda c}
.
Equipped with this bound, we factor out the first term within the last
(\ldots)_{+}
.
What remains is bounded below by
(1-\exp(-\lambda\frac{|x-x^{\prime}|^{2b_{4}}}{c(\delta)(t-s)}+\lambda c))_{+}
.
This concludes the third surgery

(Surgery III)

\displaystyle I_{2}\geq e^{-\lambda\frac{|x-x^{\prime}|^{2}+|x-x^{\prime}|^{1+b_{4}}}{2(t-s)}}\big(1-e^{-\lambda\frac{|x-x^{\prime}|^{2b_{4}}}{c(\delta)(t-s)}+\lambda c}\big)_{+}\,\frac{p_{\lambda}(x^{\prime})}{p_{\lambda}(x)}\mathsf{FK}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{m}}_{\lambda}(0,0;t,x^{\prime}).

It remains only to fix
s
and
b_{4}
.
Set
(t-s)=|x-x^{\prime}|^{\frac{22}{13}}
and
b_{4}=\frac{11}{13}-\delta
.
We assume that
|x-x^{\prime}|
is small enough so that the
+\lambda c
term in (
Surgery III
) is subdominant to its preceding term.
Note that this assumption does not lose any generality because the interval
[-\delta^{-1},\delta^{-1}]
is compact.
Also, this assumption is consistent with the previous assumption that
(t-s)
is small enough.
The prefactors in (
Surgery I+II
) and (
Surgery III
) are
\geq\exp(-c(\delta)|x-x^{\prime}|^{\frac{2}{13}-\delta})
.
This completes the proof.
∎

5.
Upper bound

Our goal here is to prove the following proposition, which together with Corollary
3.2
gives Theorem
1.1
.

Proposition 5.1

(Upper half of Theorem
1.1
)
.

For any
\delta>0
,

\displaystyle\limsup_{\lambda\to\infty}\ \sup\Big\{h_{\lambda}(t,x)-h_{*}(t,x)\,:(t,x)\in[\delta,2]\times[-\delta^{-1},\delta^{-1}]\Big\}\leq 0.

We will prove this proposition separately within the lens region and outside of the lens region.
More precisely, the lens region is
\mathsf{Lens}:=\{(t,x):|x|\leq\ell(t)\}
.
We will prove the pointwise version of Proposition
5.1
and argue in Section
5.2
how to upgrade the pointwise result to a uniform result.

5.1.
Pointwise bound within
\mathsf{Lens}

Fixing any
(t_{0},x_{0})\in\mathsf{Lens}
, we seek to prove

(5.1)

\displaystyle\limsup_{\lambda\to\infty}h_{\lambda}(t_{0},x_{0})\leq h_{*}(t_{0},x_{0}).

Given the Hölder continuity from Proposition
4.1
, it suffices to consider
(t_{0},x_{0})\in\mathsf{Lens}^{\circ}
, which we assume hereafter.
To begin the proof, in (
2.4
), set
\rho=\rho^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{m}}_{\lambda}
,
(t,x)=(2,0)
, and
s=t_{0}
and write
\mathsf{Z}_{\lambda}[\rho^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{m}}_{\lambda}]:=e^{\lambda h_{\lambda}}
.
Restrict the integral to a small interval
[-v+x_{0},x_{0}+v]
.
Doing so makes the integral smaller, whereby

\displaystyle e^{\lambda h_{\lambda}(2,0)}\geq\int_{[-v+x_{0},x_{0}+v]}p_{\lambda}(2-t_{0},y)\mathsf{FK}_{\lambda}[\rho](t_{0},y;2,0)\,e^{\lambda h_{\lambda}(t_{0},y)}\,\mathrm{d}y.

Given that
(t_{0},x_{0})\in\mathsf{Lens}^{\circ}
, we assume
v
is small enough so that each
y
in this integral lies in
\mathsf{Lens}
.
For such a
y
, there exists a geodesic that connects
(t_{0},y)
to
(2,0)
, so by Proposition
3.1
,
\mathsf{FK}_{\lambda}[\rho](t_{0},y;2,0)\geq
\exp(\lambda h_{*}(2,0))

\exp(-\lambda h_{*}(t_{0},y))

\exp(\frac{\lambda y^{2}}{2(2-t_{0})})

\exp(-o(\lambda))
.
Note that
h_{*}(2,0)
and
h_{\lambda}(2,0)
are both equal to
-1
, so we can cancel these two factors from both sides.
The factor
\exp(\frac{\lambda y^{2}}{2(2-t_{0})})
can be combined with
p_{\lambda}(2-t_{0},y)
.
Altogether we have

\displaystyle 1\geq\sqrt{\lambda/(2\pi(2-t_{0}))}\int_{[-v+x_{0},x_{0}+v]}e^{\lambda(h_{\lambda}(t_{0},y)-h_{*}(t_{0},y)-o(1))}\,\mathrm{d}y.

By Proposition
4.1
the function
h_{\lambda}(t_{0},y)
is Hölder in
y
, and it is not hard to verify from (
2.9
) that
h_{*}(t,x)
is Lipschitz except near
t=0
.
Hence the integral is bounded below by
2v\exp(\lambda(h_{\lambda}(t_{0},x_{0})-h_{*}(t_{0},x_{0})-o(1)))\exp(-\lambda cv^{1/13})
.
Apply
\frac{1}{\lambda}\log({\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{\scalebox{1.8}{$\cdot$}}})
to both sides, take
\lambda\to\infty
, and take
v\to 0
later.
Doing so gives the desired result (
5.1
).

5.2.
From a pointwise bound to a uniform bound

Here we explain how to upgrade a pointwise lower bound into a uniform one.
First, thanks to the Hölder continuity from Proposition
4.1
, we can upgrade (
5.1
) into

\displaystyle\limsup_{\lambda\to\infty}\{h_{\lambda}(t_{0},x)-h_{*}(t_{0},x):|x|\leq\ell(t_{0})\}\leq 0,

\displaystyle\text{for any fixed }t_{0}\in(0,2].

In fact, the Hölder continuity allows us to go slightly beyond the lens:

(5.2)

\displaystyle\limsup_{\lambda\to\infty}\big\{h_{\lambda}(t_{0},x)-h_{*}(t_{0},x)\,:\,|x|\leq\ell(t_{0})+v\big\}\leq cv^{1/13},

\displaystyle\text{for any fixed }t_{0}\in(0,2]\text{ and }v>0.

To further upgrade (
5.2
) into a spacetime-uniform bound, we will develop a one-sided time continuity estimate in Lemma
5.2
.
Combining Lemma
5.2
and (
5.2
) gives the desired uniform bound:

(5.3)

\displaystyle\limsup_{\lambda\to\infty}\big\{h_{\lambda}(t,x)-h_{*}(t,x)\,:\,(t,x)\in\mathsf{Lens}\cap([\delta,2]\times[-\delta^{-1},\delta^{-1}])\big\}\leq 0.

Lemma 5.2
.

For any
\delta>0
,

\displaystyle\liminf_{(\lambda,u)\to(\infty,0)}\,\inf\big\{h_{\lambda}(s,x)-h_{\lambda}(t,x):s<t\in[\delta,2],\,|t-s|\leq u,\,|x|\leq\delta^{-1}\big\}\geq 0.

Proof.

In (
2.4
), set
\rho=\rho^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{m}}_{\lambda}
and bound
\mathsf{FK}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{m}}_{\lambda}(s,y;t,x)\leq 1
, which holds because
\rho^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{m}}_{\lambda}\leq 0
(Appendix
A.2
).
Recognize the resulting integral as
\mathbb{E}[\mathsf{Z}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{m}}_{\lambda}(s,x+Y)]
, where
Y
is a zero-mean Gaussian with variance
(t-s)/\lambda
.
Divide this expectation into
\{|Y|\leq v\}
and
\{|Y|>v\}
.
For the former, use Proposition
4.1
to bound expectation from above by
\exp(\lambda(h_{\lambda}(s,t)+cv^{1/13}))
.
For the latter, use
\mathsf{Z}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{m}}_{\lambda}(s,y)=p_{\lambda}(s,y)\mathsf{FK}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{m}}_{\lambda}(s,y;t,x)\leq p_{\lambda}(s,y)\leq c(\delta)\sqrt{\lambda}
to bound the expectation from above by
c(\delta)\sqrt{\lambda}\exp(-\lambda v^{2}/(3(t-s)))
.
Altogether, we have

(5.4)

\displaystyle e^{\lambda h_{\lambda}(t,x)}\leq e^{\lambda(h_{\lambda}(s,x)+cv^{1/13})}+c(\delta)\sqrt{\lambda}e^{-\lambda\frac{v^{2}}{3(t-s)}}.

Recall the lower bound of
h_{\lambda}
from Corollary
3.2
, and note that
h_{*}
is bounded on
[\delta,2]\times[-\delta^{-1},\delta^{-1}]
; see (
A.3
).
Given this bound, we see that for fixed
\delta,v>0
, the first term on the right side of (
5.4
) dominate the entire right side as
(\lambda,u)\to(\infty,0)
, uniformly over
s<t\in[\delta,2]
and
|x|\leq\delta^{-1}
with
|t-s|\leq u
.
This being the case, apply
\log({\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{\scalebox{1.8}{$\cdot$}}})
to both sides, take the relevant infimum, take
\liminf_{(\lambda,u)\to(\infty,0)}
, and finally take
v\to 0
.
This completes the proof.
∎

5.3.
Bound within
\mathsf{Lens}^{\mathrm{c}}

To complete the proof of Proposition
3.1
, we establish the bound in
\mathsf{Lens}^{\mathrm{c}}
.
We will only carry out the proof of the pointwise bound: The same argument in Section
5.2
applies to upgrade the pointwise bound to the uniform bound.
Fix
(t_{0},x_{0})\in\mathsf{Lens}^{\mathrm{c}}
with
t_{0}\in(0,2]
.
Assume
x_{0}>\ell(t_{0})
to simplify notation.

The first step is to decompose the Feynman–Kac formula according to the first hitting time at the boundary of
\mathsf{Lens}
.
Express
\mathsf{Z}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{m}}_{\lambda}(t_{0},x_{0})
by (
2.3
).
The relevant Brownian bridge
W_{\lambda}\sim\mathrm{BB}((t_{0},x_{0})\to(0,0))
goes backward in time.
Let
\tau:=\sup\{u\in[0,t_{0}]:W_{\lambda}(u)\leq\ell(u)\}
be the first (going backward in time) hitting time at the boundary of
\mathsf{Lens}
.
In the Feynman–Kac expectation (namely (
2.2
) with
\rho=\rho^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{m}}_{\lambda}
and
(s,y;t,x)=(0,0;t_{0},x_{0})
), decompose the integral into
\int_{0}^{\tau}
and
\int_{\tau}^{t_{0}}
and bound the contribution of the latter using
\rho^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{m}}_{\lambda}\leq 0
.
For the former, note that the law of
\{W_{\lambda}(u)\}_{u\in[0,\tau]}
conditioned on
\tau=s
is
\mathrm{BB}_{\lambda}((s,\ell(s))\to(0,0))
.
We recognize the resulting expectation as
\mathbb{E}[(\mathsf{Z}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{m}}_{\lambda}/p_{\lambda})(\tau,\ell(\tau))]
.
Hence,

(5.5)

\displaystyle e^{\lambda h_{\lambda}(t_{0},x_{0})}=:\mathsf{Z}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{m}}_{\lambda}(t_{0},x_{0})\leq p_{\lambda}(t_{0},x_{0})\,\mathbb{E}\big[\big(\mathsf{Z}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{m}}_{\lambda}/p_{\lambda}\big)(\tau,\ell(\tau))\big].

To control the right side of (
5.5
), we discretize in time.
Let
s_{i}:=\frac{i}{n}t_{0}
, and decompose the expectation in (
5.5
) into
n
expectations according to
\tau\in[0,s_{1})
,
\tau\in[s_{1},s_{2})
,
\ldots
.
For the first one, we use
(\mathsf{Z}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{m}}_{\lambda}/p_{\lambda})(t,x)=\mathsf{FK}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{m}}_{\lambda}(0,0;t,x)\leq 1
.
For the remaining
(n-1)
ones, use (
5.3
) to bound
\mathsf{Z}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{m}}_{\lambda}(u,\ell(u))
by
\exp(-\lambda(h_{*}(u,\ell(u))+o(1)))
, where the
o(1)
depends only on
t_{0}/n
.
Given this bound, we further bound
(\mathsf{Z}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{m}}_{\lambda}/p_{\lambda})(\tau,\ell(\tau))
by
c(n,t_{0})\max_{u\in[s_{i},s_{i+1})}\exp(\lambda(h_{*}(u,\ell(u))+\frac{\ell(u)^{2}}{2u}+o(1)))
.
Altogether, we have

(5.6)

\displaystyle e^{\lambda h_{\lambda}(t_{0},x_{0})}\leq c(n,t_{0})e^{-\frac{\lambda x_{0}^{2}}{2t_{0}}}\Big(\mathbb{P}\big[\tau\in[0,s_{1})\big]+\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\max_{u\in[s_{i},s_{i+1})}\Big\{e^{\lambda(h_{*}(u,\ell(u))+\frac{\ell(u)^{2}}{2u}+o(1))}\Big\}\mathbb{P}\big[\tau\in[s_{i},s_{i+1})\big]\Big).

We next involve the LDP of
W_{\lambda}
to control
\mathbb{P}[\tau\in[s_{i},s_{i+1})]
.
The Brownian bridge enjoys an LDP on
C((t_{0},x_{0})\to(0,0))
, the space of continuous paths connecting
(t_{0},x_{0})
and
(0,0)
.
The rate function is
I_{\mathrm{BB}}(\gamma):=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t_{0}}\dot{\gamma}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}u-x_{0}^{2}/(2t_{0})
, where the integral is interpreted as
+\infty
when
\gamma\notin H^{1}((t_{0},x_{0})\to(0,0))
.
Now, consider the set
\mathcal{T}_{i}:=\{\gamma:\gamma[s_{i},s_{i+1}]\cap(\partial\mathsf{Lens})\neq\emptyset,\,\gamma[s_{i+1},t_{0}]\cap\mathsf{Lens}^{\circ}=\emptyset\}
.
Indeed,
\mathcal{T}_{i}
is a closed set in
C((t_{0},x_{0})\to(0,0))
and
\{\tau\in[s_{i},s_{i+1})\}\subset\{W_{\lambda}\in\mathcal{T}_{i}\}
.
By the LDP of
W_{\lambda}
,

(5.7)

\displaystyle\limsup_{\lambda\to\infty}\frac{1}{\lambda}\log\mathbb{P}\big[\tau\in[s_{i},s_{i+1})\big]\leq-\inf_{\gamma\in\mathcal{T}_{i}}\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t_{0}}\dot{\gamma}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}u+\frac{x_{0}^{2}}{2t_{0}}.

We would like to modify the right side of (
5.7
).
Let
t_{\gamma}:=\sup\{u:\gamma(u)\leq\ell(u)\}
be the first (going backward in time) hitting time of
\gamma\in H^{1}((t_{0},x_{0})\to(0,0))
and consider the set
\mathcal{S}_{i}:=\{\gamma:t_{\gamma}\in[s_{i},s_{i+1}]\}
.
Even though
\mathcal{S}_{i}\subsetneq\mathcal{T}_{i}
, it is not hard to verify that the infimum in (
5.7
) does not change if we replace
\mathcal{T}_{i}
with
\mathcal{S}_{i}
.
Hence

(5.8)

\displaystyle\limsup_{\lambda\to\infty}\frac{1}{\lambda}\log\mathbb{P}\big[\tau\in[s_{i},s_{i+1})\big]\leq-\inf\Big\{\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t_{0}}\dot{\gamma}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}u\,:\gamma\in H^{1}((t_{0},x_{0})\to(0,0)),t_{\gamma}\in[s_{i},s_{i+1}]\Big\}+\frac{x_{0}^{2}}{2t_{0}}.

We now combine (
5.6
) and (
5.8
) to complete the proof.
Apply
\limsup_{\lambda\to\infty}\frac{1}{\lambda}\log({\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{\scalebox{1.8}{$\cdot$}}})
to both sides of (
5.6
) with the aid of (
5.8
), and take
n\to\infty
with the aid of the continuity of
h_{*}(u,\ell(u))|_{u\in[0,t_{0}]}
.
We arrive at

(5.9)

\displaystyle\limsup_{\lambda\to\infty}h_{\lambda}(t_{0},x_{0})\leq\sup\Big\{-\int_{0}^{t_{0}}\frac{1}{2}\dot{\gamma}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}u+\frac{\ell(t_{\gamma})^{2}}{2t_{\gamma}}+h_{*}(t_{\gamma},\ell(t_{\gamma}))\,:\,\gamma\in H^{1}((t_{0},x_{0})\to(0,0))\Big\}.

Bound from above the first two terms within the supremum together by
-\int_{t_{\gamma}}^{t_{0}}\frac{1}{2}\dot{\gamma}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}u
.
To see why such a bound holds, write the first term as
-\int_{t_{\gamma}}^{t_{0}}-\int_{0}^{t_{\gamma}}
, and note that the second term is
\int_{0}^{t_{\gamma}}\frac{1}{2}\dot{\gamma}_{\mathrm{line}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}u
, where
{\gamma}_{\mathrm{line}}
is the linear path that connects
(t_{\gamma},\ell(\gamma))
and
(0,0)
.
A linear path minimizes such an integral among all paths connecting the same points, so the bound follows.
Next, use (
2.11
) to express
h_{*}(t_{\gamma},\ell(t_{\gamma}))
as
\int_{0}^{t_{\gamma}}(-\frac{1}{2}\dot{\ell}(u)^{2}+\rho_{*}(u,\ell(u)))\,\mathrm{d}u
.
At this stage, the relevant paths are
\ell(u)|_{u\in[0,t_{\gamma}]}
and
\gamma(u)|_{u\in[t_{\gamma},t_{0}]}
.
Concatenating these paths gives a path
\eta\in H^{1}((t_{0},x_{0})\to(0,0))
.
Hence,

\displaystyle\limsup_{\lambda\to\infty}h_{\lambda}(t_{0},x_{0})\leq\sup\Big\{\int_{0}^{t_{0}}\Big(-\frac{1}{2}\dot{\eta}^{2}+\rho_{*}(u,\eta(u))\Big)\,\mathrm{d}u\,:\,\eta\in H^{1}((t_{0},x_{0})\to(0,0))\Big\}.

The right side is exactly
h_{*}(t_{0},x_{0})
; see (
2.9
).
This completes the proof.

Appendix A
Technical tools

A.1.
Some bounds

Here are some useful bounds.
It is not hard to derive them from (
2.5
), (
2.6
), (
2.7
), and (
2.10
).

(A.1)

\displaystyle\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}|\rho_{*}(t,x)|

\displaystyle\leq c\,(t^{-2/3}+(2-t)^{-2/3}),

(A.2)

\displaystyle|\dot{\ell}(t)|

\displaystyle\leq c\,(t^{-1/3}+(2-t)^{-1/3}),

(A.3)

\displaystyle\sup_{(t,x)\in(0,2]\times\mathbb{R}}\big|h_{*}(t,x)+\tfrac{x^{2}}{2t}\big|

\displaystyle<\infty,

where
c\in(0,\infty)
denotes a universal constant.

A.2.
The sign of
\rho^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{m}}_{\lambda}

Next, we prove that
\rho^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{m}}_{\lambda}\leq 0
a.e. on
[0,2]\times\mathbb{R}
, for all
e^{-\lambda}<p_{\lambda}(2,0)
.
Let us argue by contradiction: Consider
(\rho^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{m}}_{\lambda})_{+}:=\min\{\rho^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{m}}_{\lambda},0\}
and assume the function is not 0 a.e.
Set
\rho^{1}:=\rho^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{m}}_{\lambda}-(\rho^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{m}}_{\lambda})_{+}
.
By the monotonicity of
\mathsf{FK}_{\lambda}[\rho]
in
\rho
, we have
e^{-\lambda}=\mathsf{FK}_{\lambda}[\rho^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{m}}_{\lambda}](2,0)\geq\mathsf{FK}_{\lambda}[\rho^{1}](2,0)
.
Also, we have
\mathsf{FK}_{\lambda}[0](2,0)=p_{\lambda}(2,0)>e^{-\lambda}
.
It is not hard to verify that
\mathsf{FK}_{\lambda}[a\rho^{1}](2,0)
varies continuously in
a
.
Hence there exists
a_{1}\in(0,1]
such that
\mathsf{FK}_{\lambda}[a_{1}\rho^{1}](2,0)=e^{-\lambda}
.
However, we have
\|\rho^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{m}}_{\lambda}\|_{2}>\|\rho^{1}\|_{2}\geq\|a_{1}\rho^{1}\|_{2}
, which contradicts with the assumption that
\rho^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{m}}_{\lambda}
is the minimizer.

A.3.
The Feynman–Kac formula

Finally, let us provide a proof of the Feynman–Kac formula (
2.3
).
We consider
\lambda=1
only, and the case for
\lambda>0
follows by scaling.
Let
p(t,x)
denote the standard heat kernel.
First, by writing (
1.1
) in Duhamel’s form and iterating the result, we have

(A.4)

\displaystyle\mathsf{Z}[\rho](t,x)=p(t,x)+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}I_{n}(t,x),

where

(A.5)

\displaystyle I_{n}(t,x):=\int_{0<u_{1}<\ldots<u_{n}<t}\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}p(u_{1},z_{1})\prod_{i=1}^{n}\Big(p(u_{i+1}-u_{i},z_{i+1}-z_{i})\rho(u_{i},z_{i})\mathrm{d}z_{i}\Big)\Big)\mathrm{d}u_{1}\cdots\mathrm{d}u_{n},

with the convention
u_{n+1}:=t
and
z_{n+1}:=x
.
To ensure that (
A.4
) gives a convergence series, we derive a bound on
I_{n}(t,x)
.
Apply the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality in (
A.5
) over the integrals to “separate the
p
and
\rho
” to get

(A.6)

\displaystyle|I_{n}(t,x)|^{2}\leq

\displaystyle\int_{0<s_{1}<\ldots<s_{n}<t}\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\Big(\rho(s_{i},y_{i})^{2}\mathrm{d}y_{i}\Big)\Big)\mathrm{d}s_{1}\cdots\mathrm{d}s_{n}

(A.7)

\displaystyle\cdot\int_{0<s_{1}<\ldots<s_{n}<t}\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}p^{2}(s_{1},y_{1})\prod_{i=1}^{n}\Big(p^{2}(s_{i+1}-s_{i},y_{i+1}-y_{i})\mathrm{d}y_{i}\Big)\Big)\mathrm{d}s_{1}\cdots\mathrm{d}s_{n}.

The integral in (
A.6
) is equal to
\frac{1}{n!}\|\rho\|^{2}_{L^{2}([0,t]\times\mathbb{R})}
; the integral in (
A.7
) is

2^{-n}t^{n/2-1}\pi^{-1/2}e^{-x^{2}/t}/\Gamma(\frac{n+1}{2}).

Therefore,

(A.8)

\displaystyle|I_{n}(t,x)|\leq\pi^{1/4}\,(n!\,\Gamma(\tfrac{n+1}{2}))^{-1/2}\,\big(2^{-1/2}t^{1/4}\|\rho\|_{L^{2}([0,t]\times\mathbb{R})}\big)^{n}\cdot p(t,x).

Next, recall that the Brownian Bridge
W\sim\mathrm{BB}_{1}((t,x)\to(0,0))
has the following finite dimensional distributions; see
[
KS12
, 6.11 Problem]
for example.

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\Big[W(u_{1})\in A_{1},W(u_{2})\in A_{2},\ldots,W(u_{n})\in A_{n}\Big]=\frac{1}{p(t,x)}\displaystyle\int_{\prod_{i=1}^{n}A_{i}}p(u_{1},z_{1})\prod_{i=1}^{n}\Big(p(u_{i+1}-u_{i},z_{i+1}-z_{i})\mathrm{d}z_{i}\Big),

with the convention
u_{0}:=0
,
u_{n+1}:=t
,
z_{0}:=0
, and
z_{n+1}:=x
.
We hence recognize
I_{n}(t,x)
as an expectation over the Brownian bridge:

(A.9)

\displaystyle I_{n}(t,x)

\displaystyle=\int_{0<u_{1}<\ldots<u_{n}<t}\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big(\prod_{i=1}^{n}\rho(u_{i},W(u_{i}))\Big)\Big]\cdot p(t,x)\,\mathrm{d}u_{1}\cdots\mathrm{d}u_{n}

(A.10)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{0<u_{1}<\ldots<u_{n}<t}\Big(\prod_{i=1}^{n}\rho(u_{i},W(u_{i}))\Big)\,\mathrm{d}u_{1}\cdots\mathrm{d}u_{n}\Big]\cdot p(t,x).

The exchange of the sum and integral in (
A.9
)–(
A.10
) is justified: If we replace
\rho
with
|\rho|
in (
A.9
)–(
A.10
), the result is bounded by the right hand side of (
A.8
); hence Fubini’s theorem applies.
Next, the integral in (
A.10
) can be written as

(A.11)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n!}\int_{0}^{t}\cdots\int_{0}^{t}\Big(\prod_{i=1}^{n}\rho(u^{\prime}_{i},W(u^{\prime}_{i}))\Big)\,\mathrm{d}u^{\prime}_{1}\cdots\mathrm{d}u^{\prime}_{n}=\frac{1}{n!}\Big(\int_{0}^{t}\rho(u^{\prime},W(u^{\prime}))\,\mathrm{d}u^{\prime}\Big)^{n}.

This is because
\prod_{i=1}^{n}\rho(u^{\prime}_{i},W(u^{\prime}_{i}))
is invariant under reordering of the
u^{\prime}_{i}
.
Now, combine (
A.9
), (
A.10
), and (
A.11
); sum the result over
n\geq 1
; add
p(t,x)
to the result; combine the result with (
A.4
).
We have

\displaystyle\mathsf{Z}[\rho](t,x)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}\Big[\frac{1}{n!}\Big(\int_{0}^{t}\rho(u,W(u))\,\mathrm{d}u\Big)^{n}\Big]\cdot p(t,x)=\mathbb{E}\Big[\exp\Big(\int_{0}^{t}\rho(u,W(u))\,\mathrm{d}u\Big)\Big]\cdot p(t,x).

The right side is exactly
\mathsf{FK}_{1}[\rho](t,x)
.

1.
Introduction

Let
G
be an abelian group, written multiplicatively, and let
A,B\subseteq G
be finite subsets. The sumset
AB
is
\{ab\;\mid\;a\in A,b\in B\}
. A fundamental problem in additive combinatorics is to find a lower bound for the cardinality of
AB
in terms of the cardinalities of
A
and
B
. For a finite set
S
, let
\lvert S\rvert
denote its cardinality. Let
\Stab(AB)=\{g\in G\mid gAB=AB\}
. In 1961, Kneser obtained the following lower bound.

Theorem 1.1

(Kneser
[
2
]
)
.

For all abelian groups
G
and finite subsets
A,B\subseteq G
, we have

\lvert AB\rvert\geq\lvert A\rvert+\lvert B\rvert-\lvert\Stab(AB)\rvert.

If
G
is a cyclic group of prime order
p
, then
Theorem

1.1
specializes to the Cauchy–Davenport theorem, which states that every pair of nonempty subsets
A,B\subseteq G
satisfies
\lvert AB\rvert\geq\min\{p,\lvert A\rvert+\lvert B\rvert-1\}
. Bachoc, Serra, and Zémor generalized
Theorem

1.1
to field extensions.

Theorem 1.2

(Bachoc, Serra, Zémor
[
1
]
, Theorem 2)
.

For all field extensions
L/K
and nonempty
K
-subvector spaces
A,B\subseteq L
of positive finite dimension, we have

\dim_{K}AB\geq\dim_{K}A+\dim_{K}B-\dim_{K}\Stab(AB).

In this article, we instead study the sumsets of sequences in abelian groups and flags in field extensions.

1.1.
Setup

For a positive integer
n
, let
[n]
denote the set
\{0,\dots,n-1\}
.

1.1.1.
Flags of abelian groups

Let
G
be any finite abelian group, written multiplicatively, of cardinality
n
. A
flag of
G
is an indexed set
\mathcal{F}=\{F_{i}\}_{i\in[n]}
of subsets of
G
such that

\{1\}=F_{0}\subset F_{1}\subset\dots\subset F_{n-1}=G

and
\lvert F_{i}\rvert=i+1
for all
i\in[n]
.

1.1.2.
Flags of field extensions

Let
L/K
be any degree
n
field extension. A
flag of
L
over
K
is an indexed set
\mathcal{F}=\{F_{i}\}_{i\in[n]}
of
K
-subvector spaces of
L
such that

K=F_{0}\subset F_{1}\subset\dots\subset F_{n-1}=L

and
\dim_{K}F_{i}=i+1
for all
i\in[n]
.

1.1.3.
The function
T_{\mathcal{F}}

Let
\mathcal{F}=\{F_{i}\}_{i\in[n]}
be a flag of an abelian group or a field extension.
Associate to
\mathcal{F}
the function

\displaystyle T_{\mathcal{F}}\colon[n]\times[n]

\displaystyle\longrightarrow[n]

\displaystyle(i\;\;,\;\;j)\;

\displaystyle\longmapsto\min\{k\in[n]\mid F_{i}F_{j}\subseteq F_{k}\}.

We call the associated function
T_{\mathcal{F}}
the
flag type
of
\mathcal{F}
. Which functions
T_{\mathcal{F}}
arise from groups and field extensions?

The function
T_{\mathcal{F}}
satisfies the following conditions: for all
i,j\in[n]
, we have

(1)

T_{\mathcal{F}}(i,j)=T(j,i)
;

(2)

T_{\mathcal{F}}(i,0)=i
;

(3)

and if
i<n-1
, we have
T_{\mathcal{F}}(i,j)\leq T_{\mathcal{F}}(i+1,j)
.

Motivated by the functions
T_{\mathcal{F}}
, we make the following definition.

Definition 1.3
.

A
flag type of degree
n
is a function
T\colon[n]\times[n]\rightarrow[n]
such that for any
i,j\in[n]
, we have:

(1)

T(i,j)=T(j,i)
;

(2)

T(i,0)=i
;

(3)

and
T(i,j)\leq T(i+1,j)
if
i<n-1
.

Definition 1.4
.

We say a flag type is
realizable
if it arises from a flag of an abelian group or field extension.

In this article, let
T
denote a flag type. Is every flag type realizable? The answer is no: for any integers
i\geq 0
and
j>0
, let
(i\perc j)
denote the remainder when dividing
i
by
j
.
Lemma

4.2
implies that if
T(i,j)<i+j
, then there exists an integer
1<k<n
such that
k\mid n
and

(i\perc k)+(j\perc k)\neq(i+j)\perc k.

There are also other constraints on flag types; see
Proposition

4.3
for more information.

Morever, say
T
is
realizable for groups
if there exists a finite abelian group with a flag realizing
T
. Similarly, say
T
is
realizable for field extensions
if there exists a field extension with a flag realizing
T
. Every flag type that is realizable for groups is realizable for fields (
Lemma

4.4
).

In this article, we aim to describe the set of realizable flag types.

1.2.
Some fundamental flag types

We now construct some flag types that play a fundamental role in what follows.

Definition 1.5
.

A

is a
t

\mathfrak{T}=(n_{1},\dots,n_{t})\in\mathbb{Z}_{>1}^{t}
for some

of the tower type and

In this article,
\mathfrak{T}
will denote a tower type of length

Definition 1.6
.

Choose a tower type
\mathfrak{T}=(n_{1},\dots,n_{t})
and

in
mixed radix notation with respect to
\mathfrak{T}

i=i_{1}+i_{2}n_{1}+i_{3}(n_{1}n_{2})+\dots+i_{t}(n_{1}\dots n_{t-1})

where

is an integer such that
0\leq i_{s}<n_{s}
for
1\leq s\leq t
. Note that the integers

are uniquely determined.

Fix a tower type
\mathfrak{T}=(n_{1},\dots,n_{t})
. For any positive integer

denote the cyclic group of order

For

The sequence

\bigg\{1,e_{1},e_{1}^{2},\dots,e_{1}^{n_{1}-1},e_{2},e_{1}e_{2},e_{1}^{2}e_{2},\dots,\prod_{s=1}^{t}e_{s}^{n_{s}-1}\bigg\}.

Define the flag

by

.

Definition 1.7
.

Let

be the flag type of

.

We may describe the flag types

even more explicitly; see
Corollary

1.23

arise from flags of abelian groups. We will show that, in an appropriate sense, they are often
minimal
among various subsets of realizable flag types.

Lemma 6.4
.

Let
q
be an odd prime. For

, we have

a\bigg\{\frac{q+1}{2},\dots,q-1\bigg\}=\bigg\{\frac{q+1}{2},\dots,q-1\bigg\}\ (\mathrm{mod}\ q)

if and only if
a\equiv 1\ (\mathrm{mod}\ q)
.

Proof.

The statement

a\bigg\{\frac{q+1}{2},\dots,q-1\bigg\}=\bigg\{\frac{q+1}{2},\dots,q-1\bigg\}\ (\mathrm{mod}\ q)

is equivalent to the statement

a\bigg\{1,\dots,\frac{q-1}{2}\bigg\}=\bigg\{1,\dots,\frac{q-1}{2}\bigg\}\ (\mathrm{mod}\ q).

If
q=3
, it is clear that
a\equiv 1\ (\mathrm{mod}\ q)
; assume
q\neq 3
, and hence
q\geq 5
. Then as
a(q-1)\equiv-a\in\{\frac{q+1}{2},\dots,q-1\}\ (\mathrm{mod}\ q)
, we must have
a\in\{1,\dots,\frac{q-1}{2}\}\ (\mathrm{mod}\ q)
. If
a\not\equiv 1\ (\mathrm{mod}\ q)
, then there exists
b\in\{1,\dots,\frac{q-1}{2}\}\ (\mathrm{mod}\ q)
such that

, which is a contradiction.
∎

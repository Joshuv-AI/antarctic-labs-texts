Stability of a class of action functionals depending on convex functions
Luigi Ambrosio, Camillo Brena

Abstract
. We study the stability of a class of action functionals induced by gradients of convex functions with respect to Mosco convergence, under mild assumptions on the underlying space.



Introduction

The aim of this short note is to investigate the stability of action functionals of the form

\gamma\mapsto\int_{0}^{1}\absolutevalue{\dot{\gamma}}^{2}(t)+\absolutevalue{\partial f}^{2}(\gamma(t))\differential{t},

with respect to a suitable notion of convergence of the functions
f
.
Namely, we consider functionals defined on
{\mathrm{C}}([0,1],{\mathsf{X}})
as

\Theta^{f}_{x_{0},x_{1}}(\gamma)\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\absolutevalue{\dot{\gamma}}^{2}(t)+\absolutevalue{\partial f}^{2}(\gamma(t))\differential{t}\quad&\text{if }\gamma(0)=x_{0},\gamma(1)=x_{1};\\
+\infty&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

and we study the
\Gamma
–convergence of
\Theta^{f^{h}}_{x^{h}_{0},x^{h}_{1}}
to
\Theta^{f}_{x_{0},x_{1}}
whenever
x_{0}^{h}\rightarrow x_{0}
,
x_{1}^{h}\rightarrow x_{1}
and
f^{h}
Mosco converges to
f
.

In the recent
[
ABB21
]
, it has been proved that, in the Hilbertian setting, Mosco convergence of uniformly
\lambda
–convex functions
f^{h}
to
f
together with an uniform bound on the slopes
\absolutevalue{\partial f^h}(x_{0}^{h})
and
\absolutevalue{\partial f^h}(x_{1}^{h})
is enough to guarantee
\Gamma
–convergence of the respective functionals. In the cited paper, the authors built recovery sequences relying on an interpolation lemma, which is in turn related to Minty’s trick and the structure of monotone operators. To follow such procedure a linear structure of the underlying space seems necessary, so that a generalization of their result to a wider class of spaces requires some work. Also, in the cited paper, the authors suggested that their result could be generalized to non-Hilbertian spaces, and that a natural instance would be the Wasserstein space
\mathcal{P}_{2}({\mathsf{X}})
.

The last observation of the paragraph above motivates then our note. We first generalize the
\Gamma
–convergence result to the case of complete
{\mathrm{CAT}}(0)
spaces, in Theorem
12
, and this is the content of Section
2
. We then generalize the
\Gamma
–convergence result to the locally compact and geodesic metric space case, in Theorem
17
, with the uniform
\lambda
–convexity assumption taking the form of Assumption
2
, and this is the content of Section
3
. We point out that Assumption
2
is already extensively used in the monograph
[
AGS08
]
as it is a crucial hypothesis to guarantee the existence of
\mathrm{EVI}_{\lambda}
gradient flows in metric spaces.
Moreover, if
{\mathsf{X}}
is an Hilbert space, functionals defined on the Wasserstein space
\mathcal{P}_{2}({\mathsf{X}})
that are
\lambda
–convex along generalized geodesics (
[
AGS08
, Definition 9.2.4]
) satisfy Assumption
2
, so we see that our result gives a positive answer to the problem suggested in
[
ABB21
]
, at least for Wasserstein spaces built on compact Hilbert spaces.
We see also with Theorem
18
that on any geodesic metric space, if
f^{h}=\varepsilon_{h}f
, with
\varepsilon_{h}\searrow 0
, where
f
is regular enough and is continuous along the sequences
\{x_{0}^{h}\}_{h}
and
\{x_{1}^{h}\}_{h}
, then the equiboundedness assumption on the slopes is not even needed to gain the
\Gamma
–convergence result, obtaining a different proof of a result contained in
[
MTV20
]
.

The proofs of our main results, Theorem
12
and Theorem
17
are similar in structure but use different tools, and are inspired by the techniques employed in
[
ABB21
]
and
[
MTV20
]
respectively. Now we briefly sketch the proofs of our main results, both to give an overview of the similarities of the procedure we use to what is already present in the literature and to highlight where are contained the main new ideas of this note. To keep this introduction short, we assume that the endpoints
x_{0}
and
x_{1}
are kept fixed. Of course, the only problem lies in building recovery sequences for a curve
\gamma
.

In Section
2
, we start following
[
ABB21
]
transforming
\gamma
through the resolvent operator with respect to
f^{h}
,
J^{f^{h}}_{\tau_{h}}
, considering the curve
t\mapsto J^{f^{h}}_{\tau_{h}}\gamma(t)
, for a suitable sequence
\tau_{h}\searrow 0
. Then we have to correct
\gamma^{h}
at the endpoints. In
[
ABB21
]
this is done using the interpolation lemma we have already mentioned above, here we use instead a variational interpolation applicable thanks to the crucial estimate contained in Proposition
8
.

For what concerns Section
3
, we argue similarly as before, but replacing the resolvent operators with their “continuous version” given by the gradient flow trajectories
G^{f^{h}}_{\,\cdot\,}
. Also in
[
MTV20
]
gradient flows were employed in a similar way, with the difference that they modified the curve in one step as
t\mapsto G^{f^{h}}_{g(t)}\gamma(t)
for some continuous
g:[0,1]\rightarrow[0,1]
, whereas we first transform the whole curve into
t\mapsto G^{f^{h}}_{\tau_{h}}\gamma(t)
, for a suitable sequence
\tau_{h}\searrow 0
, and then we use the gradient flow again to recover the endpoints condition. The latter approach seems more flexible and allows us to treat other cases than the simpler one in which
f^{h}=\varepsilon_{h}f
for a sequence
\varepsilon_{h}\searrow 0
.

1.
Preliminaries

Fix a metric space
({\mathsf{X}},{\mathsf{d}})
and
f:{\mathsf{X}}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
. We define the effective domain of
f
as

D(f)\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\left\{x\in{\mathsf{X}}:f(x)<+\infty\right\}.

We define also the descending slope,

\absolutevalue{\partial f}(x)\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\begin{cases}+\infty&\text{if $x\in{\mathsf{X}}\setminus D(f)$}\\
0&\text{if $x\in D(f)$ is isolated}\\
\limsup_{y\rightarrow x}\frac{(f(y)-f(x))^{-}}{{\mathsf{d}}(y,x)}\quad&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

We will often require one of the following assumptions to hold.

Assumption 1
.

[
AGS08
, Assumption 2.4.5]

Let
({\mathsf{X}},{\mathsf{d}})
be a metric space and
f:{\mathsf{X}}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
. For
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
assume that for any
x_{0},x_{1}\in D(f)
there exists a curve
\gamma:[0,1]\rightarrow{\mathsf{X}}
joining
x_{0}
to
x_{1}
such that the map

x\mapsto f(x)+\frac{{\mathsf{d}}(x,x_{0})^{2}}{2\tau}

is
(\lambda+\tau^{-1})
convex along the curve
\gamma
for every
\tau\in\left(0,\frac{1}{\lambda^{-}}\right)
.

Assumption 2
.

[
AGS08
, Assumption 4.0.1]

Let
({\mathsf{X}},{\mathsf{d}})
be a metric space and
f:{\mathsf{X}}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
. For
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
assume that for any
y,x_{0},x_{1}\in D(f)
there exists a curve
\gamma:[0,1]\rightarrow{\mathsf{X}}
joining
x_{0}
to
x_{1}
such that the map

x\mapsto f(x)+\frac{{\mathsf{d}}(x,y)^{2}}{2\tau}

is
(\lambda+\tau^{-1})
convex along the curve
\gamma
for every
\tau\in\left(0,\frac{1}{\lambda^{-}}\right)
.

It is clear that Assumption
2
is stronger than Assumption
1
.

Proposition 3
.

[
AGS08
, Theorem 2.4.9]

Let
({\mathsf{X}},{\mathsf{d}})
be a metric space and let
f:{\mathsf{X}}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
satisfy Assumption
1
for some
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
. Then, for every
x\in D(f)
,

\absolutevalue{\partial f}(x)=\sup_{y\neq x}\frac{\left(f(y)-f(x)-\frac{\lambda}{2}{\mathsf{d}}(y,x)^{2}\right)^{-}}{{\mathsf{d}}(y,x)}.

Proposition 4
.

Let
({\mathsf{X}},{\mathsf{d}})
be a metric space and let
f:{\mathsf{X}}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
satisfy Assumption
1
for some
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
. Assume moreover that there exists
\bar{x}\in D(f)
such that

m\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\inf_{x\in\bar{B}_{1}(\bar{x})}f>-\infty.

Then

(1)

f(y)\geq\frac{\lambda}{2}{\mathsf{d}}(y,\bar{x})^{2}+\left(m-f(\bar{x})-\frac{\lambda^{+}}{2}\right){\mathsf{d}}(y,\bar{x})+m.

Proof.

If
{\mathsf{d}}(y,\bar{x})\leq 1
, the claim follows by a direct computation. If instead
{\mathsf{d}}(y,\bar{x})>1
, we can conclude following similar computations to the ones used in
[
AGS08
]
.
Indeed, if
0<\tau<\frac{1}{\lambda^{-}}
and
y
is such that
{\mathsf{d}}(y,\bar{x})>1
, we can take a curve (independent of
\tau
)
\gamma:[0,1]\rightarrow{\mathsf{X}}
joining
\bar{x}
to y such that

\begin{split}f(\gamma(t))+\frac{{\mathsf{d}}(\gamma(t),\bar{x})^{2}}{2\tau}&\leq(1-t)f(\bar{x})+tf(y)+t\frac{{\mathsf{d}}(y,\bar{x})^{2}}{2\tau}-\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{1}{\tau}+\lambda\right)t(1-t){\mathsf{d}}(y,\bar{x})^{2}\\
&=(1-t)f(\bar{x})+tf(y)-\frac{\lambda}{2}t(1-t){\mathsf{d}}(y,\bar{x})^{2}+\frac{1}{2\tau}t^{2}{\mathsf{d}}(y,\bar{x})^{2}.\end{split}

Notice that, as
\gamma
is independent of
\tau
, we can multiply the inequality above by
\tau
and let
\tau\searrow 0
to obtain (here we assume
f(y)<+\infty
, otherwise there is nothing to show)

{\mathsf{d}}(\gamma(t),\bar{x})\leq t{\mathsf{d}}(y,\bar{x}).

Therefore, if
\bar{t}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}={\mathsf{d}}(y,\bar{x})^{-1}
, we have that
f(\gamma(t))\geq m
, so that if we neglect
\frac{{\mathsf{d}}(\gamma(t),\bar{x})^{2}}{2\tau}
and let
\tau\nearrow\frac{1}{\lambda^{-}}
,

\begin{split}m&\leq(1-\bar{t})f(\bar{x})+\bar{t}f(y)-\frac{\lambda}{2}(1-\bar{t}){\mathsf{d}}(y,\bar{x})+\frac{\lambda^{-}}{2}\\
&=(1-\bar{t})f(\bar{x})+\bar{t}f(y)-\frac{\lambda}{2}{\mathsf{d}}(y,\bar{x})+\frac{\lambda^{+}}{2}\end{split}

so that the claim follows rearranging and noticing that

f(\bar{x})\geq m
.
∎

Here we introduce our main object of investigation.

Definition 5
.

Let
({\mathsf{X}},{\mathsf{d}})
be a metric space,
x_{0},x_{1}\in{\mathsf{X}}
and
f:{\mathsf{X}}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
. We define

\Theta^{f}_{x_{0},x_{1}}:{\mathrm{C}}([0,1],{\mathsf{X}})\rightarrow\mathbb{R}

\Theta^{f}_{x_{0},x_{1}}(\gamma)\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\absolutevalue{\dot{\gamma}}^{2}(t)+\absolutevalue{\partial f}^{2}(\gamma(t))\differential{t}\quad&\text{if }\gamma\in{\mathrm{AC}}([0,1],{\mathsf{X}}),\gamma(0)=x_{0},\gamma(1)=x_{1};\\
+\infty&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

If
({\mathsf{X}},{\mathsf{d}})
is a metric space and
f:{\mathsf{X}}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
, given
\tau\in(0,+\infty)
, we define the resolvent map

J^{f}_{\tau}x\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\argmin\left\{f(\,\cdot\,)+\frac{{\mathsf{d}}(\,\cdot\,,x)^{2}}{2\tau}\right\}.

Clearly,
J^{f}_{\tau}x
can be empty as well as it can contain more than one element.

For what concerns the following result, the first inequality is
[
AGS08
, Lemma 3.1.3]
, whereas the second can be showed following the proof of
[
ABB21
, Theorem 2.1]
.

Proposition 6
.

Let
({\mathsf{X}},{\mathsf{d}})
be a metric space and
f:{\mathsf{X}}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
be satisfying Assumption
1
for some
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
.
If
\tau\in\left(0,\frac{1}{\lambda^{-}}\right)
. Then, for every
u\in J_{\tau}^{f}x
,

(2)

\absolutevalue{\partial f}(u)\leq\frac{{\mathsf{d}}(u,x)}{\tau}\leq\frac{1}{1+\lambda\tau}\absolutevalue{\partial f}(x).

2.
CAT
(0)
setting

A
{\mathrm{CAT}}(0)
space is a geodesic metric space
({\mathsf{X}},{\mathsf{d}})
such that, for every triple of points
y,x_{0},x_{1}\in{\mathsf{X}}
and constant speed geodesic
x:[0,1]\rightarrow{\mathsf{X}}
joining
x_{0}
to
x_{1}
, it holds

{\mathsf{d}}(y,x_{t})^{2}\leq(1-t){\mathsf{d}}^{2}(y,x_{0})+t{\mathsf{d}}^{2}(y,x_{1})-t(1-t){\mathsf{d}}^{2}(x_{0},x_{1})\quad\text{for every }t\in[0,1].

It easily follows that in
{\mathrm{CAT}}(0)
spaces geodesics are unique, up to reparametrization: if
x,y:[0,1]\rightarrow{\mathsf{X}}
are constant speed geodesics, then

(3)

\begin{split}{\mathsf{d}}(y_{t},x_{t})^{2}&\leq(1-t){\mathsf{d}}^{2}(y_{t},x_{0})+t{\mathsf{d}}^{2}(y_{t},x_{1})-t(1-t){\mathsf{d}}^{2}(x_{0},x_{1})\\
&=\left((1-t)t^{2}+t(1-t)^{2}-t(1-t)\right){\mathsf{d}}(y_{0},y_{1})^{2}=0.\end{split}

A complete
{\mathrm{CAT}}(0)
space is usually called an Hadamard space.
From now on
({\mathsf{X}},{\mathsf{d}})
will denote an Hadamard space. General references for this topic are
[
Bac14
,
BH99
]
. It may be useful to know that the completion of a
{\mathrm{CAT}}(0)
is still a
{\mathrm{CAT}}(0)
space.

We can define a notion of weak convergence on Hadamard spaces (
[
Bac14
, P. 58]
) as follows.
First, if
\{x^{h}\}_{h}\subseteq{\mathsf{X}}
is any bounded sequence, one may prove that the map

y\mapsto\limsup_{h}{\mathsf{d}}(y,x^{h})^{2}

has a unique minimizer, that will be called the asymptotic centre of the sequence
\{x^{h}\}_{h}
.
We say then that
x^{h}
weakly converges to
x
, and we write
x^{h}\rightharpoonup x
, if
x
is the asymptotic centre of each subsequence
\{x^{h_{k}}\}_{k}
.
We remark that it is not known if the weak convergence is induced by a topology.
We state for future reference the following properties of weak convergence in Hadamard spaces.

Proposition 7
.

Let
({\mathsf{X}},{\mathsf{d}})
be an Hadamard space. Then

i)

if
\{x^{h}\}_{h}\subseteq{\mathsf{X}}
is a bounded sequence, then it has a weakly convergent subsequence;

ii)

if
x^{h}\rightharpoonup x
and
{\mathsf{d}}(x^{h},y)\rightarrow{\mathsf{d}}(x,w)
for some
w\in{\mathsf{X}}
, then
x^{h}\rightarrow x
;

iii)

for every
w\in{\mathsf{X}}
,
{\mathsf{d}}(w,\,\cdot\,)
is lower semicontinuous with respect to the weak convergence.

Proof.

Items
i)
,
ii)
and
iii)
are taken respectively from
[
Bac14
, Proposition 3.1.2, Proposition 3.1.6 and Corollary 3.2.4]
.

We give a shorter and self contained proof of item
iii)
for the reader’s convenience.
We argue by contradiction assuming that for some
w\in{\mathsf{X}}
it holds that

\liminf_{h}{\mathsf{d}}(x^{h},w)<{\mathsf{d}}(x,w).

Possibly extracting subsequences and rescaling the metric, we can assume that also
{\mathsf{d}}(x,w)>1
but
{\mathsf{d}}(x^{h},w)\leq 1
for every
h
. Let
\gamma:[0,1]\rightarrow{\mathsf{X}}
be the (unique) constant speed geodesic joining
w
to
x
and let also
s\in[0,1]
be the (unique) point for which
{\mathsf{d}}(w,\gamma(s))=1
, i.e.
s={\mathsf{d}}(w,x)^{-1}
.

By the
{\mathrm{CAT}}(0)
hypothesis, it holds that, for every
h
,

\begin{split}{\mathsf{d}}(x^{h},\gamma(s))^{2}&\leq(1-s){\mathsf{d}}(x^{h},w)^{2}+s{\mathsf{d}}(x^{h},x)^{2}-s(1-s){\mathsf{d}}(w,x)^{2}\\
&\leq 1-s+s{\mathsf{d}}(x^{h},x)^{2}-(1-s){\mathsf{d}}(w,x)\leq s{\mathsf{d}}(x^{h},x)^{2}\end{split}

that is a contradiction to the fact
x^{h}\rightharpoonup x
as
s<1
and
\gamma(s)\neq x
.
∎

We can see that this notion of weak topology coincides with the usual weak topology in Hilbert spaces. In order to do so, recall that if
x^{h}
weakly converges to
x
with respect to the weak topology induced by the Hilbert structure, then

{\mathsf{d}}(y,x)^{2}+\limsup_{h}{\mathsf{d}}(x,x^{h})^{2}\leq\limsup_{h}{\mathsf{d}}(y,x^{h})^{2}

for any
y\in{\mathsf{X}}
.
Then, to conclude, just recall that bounded sets are relatively compact with respect to both notions of weak convergence and that the two notions of weak convergence are stable with respect to the extraction of subsequences.

If
f:{\mathsf{X}}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
, we say that
f
is
\lambda
–convex if for every constant speed geodesic
x:[0,1]\rightarrow{\mathsf{X}}
, it holds

f(x_{t})\leq(1-t)f(x_{0})+tf(x_{1})-\frac{\lambda}{2}t(1-t){\mathsf{d}}(x_{0},x_{1})^{2}\quad\text{for every }t\in[0,1].

If
\lambda=0
, we just say that
f
is convex.
In the sequel, we will often use the following fact, without notice: if
f
is
\lambda
–convex, then
f
satisfies Assumption
2
(
a fortiori
, Assumption
1
) for that value of
\lambda
.

Proposition 8
.

Let
({\mathsf{X}},{\mathsf{d}})
be an Hadamard space and
f:{\mathsf{X}}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
be proper,
\lambda
–convex and lower semicontinuous.

i)

If
\tau\in\left(0,\frac{1}{2\lambda^{-}}\right)
, then

(4)

If

i)
is proved in
[
May98
,
Lemma 1.12]
, whereas item

follows combining

) and the so called resolvent identity (

,

(6)

\gamma

.

Proposition 9

(
[
Bac14
, Proposition 2.2.17]
)
.

Let
({\mathsf{X}},{\mathsf{d}})
be an Hadamard space and

Definition 10
.

Let
({\mathsf{X}},{\mathsf{d}})
be an Hadamard space. Let
\{f^{h}\}_{h}
be functions from
{\mathsf{X}}
to
\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
and let
f:{\mathsf{X}}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
. We say that
f_{h}
Mosco converge to
f
if the following two conditions hold:

i)

\Gamma
–
\liminf
inequality: if
\{x^{h}\}_{h}\subseteq{\mathsf{X}}
is such that
x^{h}\rightharpoonup x
, then

f(x)\leq\liminf_{h}f^{h}(x^{h});

ii)

\Gamma
–
\limsup
inequality: for every
x\in{\mathsf{X}}
, there exists
\{x^{h}\}_{h}\subseteq{\mathsf{X}}
with
x^{h}\rightarrow x

Proposition 11
.

Let
({\mathsf{X}},{\mathsf{d}})
be an Hadamard space and
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
.
Let
\{f^{h}\}_{h}
,
f
be proper
\lambda
–convex and lower semicontinuous functions from
{\mathsf{X}}
to
\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
such that
f^{h}
Mosco converge to
f
. Then, for any
x\in{\mathsf{X}}
and
\tau\in\left(0,\frac{1}{2\lambda^{-}}\right)
, it holds

(7)

J^{f^{h}}_{\tau}x\rightarrow J^{f}_{\tau}x.

Proof.

Pick
z\in{\mathsf{X}}
with
f(z)<+\infty
and, thanks to the
\Gamma
–
\limsup
inequality, take a sequence
\{z^{h}\}_{h}\subseteq{\mathsf{X}}
such that
z^{h}\rightarrow z
and
f^{h}(z^{h})\rightarrow f(z)
. In particular, we can assume
f(z^{h})<+\infty
for every

. We write

G^{h}_{\varepsilon}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=f^{h}(\,\cdot\,)+\left(\frac{1}{2\tau}-\varepsilon\right){\mathsf{d}}(\,\cdot\,,x)^{2}

where
\varepsilon>0
is small enough so that

, notice that

f^{h}(y)+\frac{1}{2\tau}{\mathsf{d}}(y,x)^{2}=G^{h}_{\varepsilon}(y)+\varepsilon{\mathsf{d}}(y,x)^{2}.

Now,
G^{h}_{\varepsilon}
is
\lambda^{\prime}
convex and lower semicontinuous, with
\lambda^{\prime}>0
, so that, as a consequence of Proposition
4
, setting
m^{h}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\inf_{\bar{B}_{1}(z^{h})}G^{h}_{\varepsilon}
, we have that

G^{h}_{\varepsilon}(y)\geq(m^{h}-G^{h}_{\varepsilon}(z^{h})){\mathsf{d}}(y,z^{h})+m^{h}.

Notice that thanks to items
ii)
and
iii)
of Proposition
7
and the
\Gamma
–
\liminf
inequality, it holds, with the obvious definition for

\liminf_{h}\inf_{\bar{B}_{1}(z^{h})}G_{\varepsilon}^{h}\geq\inf_{\bar{B}_{1}(z)}G_{\varepsilon}\geq\inf_{{\mathsf{X}}}G_{\varepsilon}>-\infty

where the last inequality follows from Proposition
9
.
Therefore, recalling (
8
), we easily obtain that

(9)

f^{h}(y)+\frac{1}{2\tau}{\mathsf{d}}(y,x)^{2}\geq C_{1}+C_{2}{\mathsf{d}}(y,x)+\varepsilon{\mathsf{d}}(y,x)^{2}\quad\text{for every }h

for some constants
C_{1},C_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
independent of

.
As the
\Gamma
–
\limsup
inequality easily implies that

(10)

\limsup_{h}\inf_{y}f^{h}(y)+\frac{1}{2\tau}{\mathsf{d}}(y,x)^{2}\leq\inf_{y}f(y)+\frac{1}{2\tau}{\mathsf{d}}(y,x)^{2},

we easily obtain from (

J_{\tau}^{f^{h}}x\rightharpoonup w

\liminf

\liminf_{h}f^{h}(J_{\tau}^{f^{h}}x)+\frac{1}{2\tau}{\mathsf{d}}(J_{\tau}^{f^{h}}x,x)^{2}\geq f(w)+\frac{1}{2\tau}{\mathsf{d}}(w,x)^{2}.

\lim_{h}f^{h}(J_{\tau}^{f^{h}}x)+\frac{1}{2\tau}{\mathsf{d}}(J_{\tau}^{f^{h}}x,x)^{2}=f(J_{\tau}^{f}x)+\frac{1}{2\tau}{\mathsf{d}}(J_{\tau}^{f}x,x)^{2},

ii)
of Proposition

.

Theorem 12
.

Let
({\mathsf{X}},{\mathsf{d}})
be an Hadamard space and
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
.
Let
\{f^{h}\}_{h}
,
f
be proper
\lambda
–convex and lower semicontinuous functions from
{\mathsf{X}}

3.
Gradient Flows Case

Recall that if
({\mathsf{X}},{\mathsf{d}})
is a metric space,
f:{\mathsf{X}}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
and
x\in{\mathsf{X}}
, an
\mathrm{EVI}_{\lambda}
gradient flow trajectory for
f
starting at
x
is a map
G^{f}_{\cdot}x:[0,+\infty)\rightarrow D(f)
such that the curve
x_{t}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=G^{f}_{t}x
is locally absolutely continuous in
(0,+\infty)
with

\lim_{t\searrow 0}x_{t}=x_{0}=x

and satisfies, for every
v\in D(f)
,

\frac{1}{2}\derivative{t}{\mathsf{d}}(x_{t},v)^{2}+\frac{\lambda}{2}{\mathsf{d}}(x_{t},v)^{2}\leq f(v)-f(x_{t})\quad\text{for a.e.\ }t\in(0,+\infty).

We remark (
[
AGS08
, Theorem 4.0.4]
) that if
({\mathsf{X}},{\mathsf{d}})
is complete and
f:{\mathsf{X}}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
is proper, lower semicontinuous, bounded from below on some ball intersecting
D(f)
and satisfies Assumption
2
for some
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
, then, given any
x\in\overline{D(f)}
, there exists an
\mathrm{EVI}_{\lambda}
gradient flow trajectory for
f
starting at
x
.

In the following proposition we recall a few properties of
\mathrm{EVI}_{\lambda}
gradient flows for future reference. We refer to
[
AGS08
,
MS20
]
.

Proposition 13
.

Let
({\mathsf{X}},{\mathsf{d}})
be a metric space,
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
,
f:{\mathsf{X}}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
be a proper and lower semicontinuous function and
x_{0},x_{1}\in{\mathsf{X}}
. Assume that there exist two
\mathrm{EVI}_{\lambda}
gradient flows trajectories
G^{f}_{\,\cdot\,}x_{0},G^{f}_{\,\cdot\,}x_{1}
starting respectively from
x_{0},x_{1}
,
Then

i)

It holds

(21)

{\mathsf{d}}(G_{t}^{f}x_{0},G_{t}^{f}x_{1})\leq e^{-\lambda t}{\mathsf{d}}(x_{0},x_{1})\quad\text{for every $t\in[0,+\infty)$}.

ii)

It holds

(22)

|(G_{t}^{f}x_{0})^{\prime}|^{2}(t)=\absolutevalue{\partial f}^{2}(G_{t}^{f}x_{0})=-\derivative{t}(f\circ G_t^f x_0)\quad\text{for a.e.\ }t\in(0,+\infty).

iii)

It holds

(23)

\frac{e^{\lambda t}-1}{\lambda t}\absolutevalue{\partial f}(G^{f}_{t}x_{0})\leq\frac{{\mathsf{d}}(G^{f}_{t}x_{0},x_{0})}{t}\leq e^{\lambda^{-}t}\absolutevalue{\partial f}(x_{0})\quad\text{for every }t\in(0,+\infty),

where
\frac{e^{\lambda t}-1}{\lambda t}
has to be understood to be
1
if
\lambda=0
.

We remark that the second inequality of (

), after taking the square root of both sides, and taking into account that

is nonincreasing and right continuous and the first is precisely
[
MS20
, Equation (3.13)]
with

in place of

.

Definition 14
.

Let
({\mathsf{X}},{\mathsf{d}})
be a metric space. Let

be functions from
{\mathsf{X}}
to
\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}

Let
({\mathsf{X}},{\mathsf{d}})\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=([0,\infty),{\mathsf{d}}_{e})

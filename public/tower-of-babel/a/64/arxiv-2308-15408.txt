Illposedness for dispersive equations: Degenerate dispersion and Takeuchi–Mizohata condition
In-Jee Jeong, Sung-Jin Oh

Abstract
We provide a unified viewpoint on two illposedness mechanisms for dispersive equations in one spatial dimension, namely degenerate dispersion and (the failure of) the Takeuchi–Mizohata condition. Our approach is based on a robust energy- and duality-based method introduced in an earlier work of the authors in the setting of Hall-magnetohydynamics. Concretely, the main results in this paper concern strong illposedness of the Cauchy problem (e.g., non-existence and unboundedness of the solution map) in high-regularity Sobolev spaces for various quasilinear degenerate Schrödinger- and KdV-type equations, including the Hunter–Smothers equation, K ⁡ ( m , n ) K(m,n) models of Rosenau–Hyman, and the inviscid surface growth model. The mechanism behind these results may be understood in terms of combination of two effects: degenerate dispersion – which is a property of the principal term in the presence of degenerating coefficients – and the evolution of the amplitude governed by the Takeuchi–Mizohata condition – which concerns the subprincipal term. We also demonstrate how the same techniques yield a more quantitative version of the classical L 2 L^{2} -illposedness result by Mizohata for linear variable-coefficient Schrödinger equations with failed Takeuchi–Mizohata condition.

Quasilinear degenerate dispersive equations

In this paper, we study the issue of illposedness of the Cauchy problem for various quasilinear dispersive equations in one spatial dimension in the presence of

\partial_{t}u+(\tfrac{1}{m}u^{m})_{x}+(\tfrac{1}{2}u^{2})_{xxx}=0\quad(m\hbox{ is a nonnegative integer}),

\partial_{t}h+((h_{x})^{2})_{xx}=0,

In each of these equations, observe that the the highest order term is nonlinear – more specifically, quadratic or cubic – in the solution. Vanishing of the solution, therefore, leads to some kind of “degeneracy” of the highest order term, which in turn gives rise to delicate issues in the (local) well-posedness of the associated Cauchy problem.

Indeed, for initial data that are uniformly bounded away from
0
(a property henceforth referred to as
nondegeneracy
), one expects local well-posedness in high-regularity
L^{2}
-based Sobolev spaces
H^{s}(\mathbb{T})
. For example, in the case of (
1.1
), for a sufficiently regular solution
\phi
, one has the conservation of the
L^{2}
-norm:

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\left(\int_{\mathbb{T}}|\phi|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\right)=0.

Obtaining higher regularity apriori estimates is a much more nontrivial task. One can observe the following bound for
n\geq 1
(in operator notation) at each
t
:

\begin{split}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\left(\int_{\mathbb{T}}|(\partial_{x}|\phi|^{2}\partial_{x})^{n}\phi|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\right)\lesssim_{n}\|{\phi}\|_{H^{2n}}^{4n+2}.\end{split}

Furthermore, as long as the solution stays nondegenerate at
t
, in the sense that
\inf_{x}|\phi(x,t)|^{2}>c
for some
c>0
, a standard argument involving the ellipticity of
(\partial_{x}|\phi(x,t)|^{2}\partial_{x})^{n}
allows us to bound
\|{\phi(\cdot,t)}\|_{H^{2n}}^{2}
by the integral on the left-hand side up to errors of the form
O(\|{\phi(\cdot,t)}\|_{H^{2n}}^{4n+2})
. Putting these together, one can establish a short-time
H^{2n}
apriori estimate for the solution
\phi
with nondegenerate initial data. However, in the case of
degenerate
initial data (i.e., those without a uniform bound away from
0
), the above scheme for a short-time
H^{2n}
apriori estimate with
n\geq 1
clearly breaks down.

In this paper, we show that this failure of proof of higher derivative apriori estimates is, in fact, a manifestation of genuine illposedness in standard function spaces. Despite the formal conservation of the
L^{2}
-norm, we demonstrate that all of the equations above are rather strongly ill-posed – in the sense of non-existence of solutions and unboundedness of the data-to-solution map in suitable set-ups – in a neighborhood of degenerate initial data (e.g., zero data) in high-regularity spaces (
C^{k-1,1}
, Sobolev or Hölder spaces).

1.3
Key mechanism: degenerate dispersion and Takeuchi–Mizohata condition

The nonlinear illposedness results in this paper are firmly based on a detailed and quantitative understanding of illposedness for the linearized equation around a background solution
f
whose initial data contains a degeneracy. For simplicity, in this subsection we shall assume that the linearization takes the form

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u-i\partial_{x}(a\partial_{x}u)-ib\partial_{x}u=(\hbox{lower order})&\hbox{ in the Schr\"{o}dinger case},\\
\partial_{t}u+\tfrac{1}{2}(\partial_{x}^{3}(au)+a\partial_{x}^{3}u)+b\partial_{x}^{2}u=(\hbox{lower order})&\hbox{ in the KdV case},\end{cases}

(1.9)

where
a=a(x)
is real-valued in both cases, and
b=b(x)
is also real-valued in the KdV case.

For Schrödinger-type problems, we regard first-order terms of the form
\widetilde{b}(x)\partial_{x}\overline{u}
as
(lower order)

\psi

Remark 1.9

(On time independence of the coefficients in (
1.9
))
.

While we assumed that
a(x)
and
b(x)
are time independent, the actual linearization of (
1.5
) and (
1.8
) on a dynamic background solution

would, of course, have time-dependent coefficients. Nevertheless, the time scale of the illposedness mechanism is arbitrarily short, and hence we may effectively approximate these coefficients by the initial values for the purpose of our discussion.

It is conceptually useful to distinguish two intertwined mechanisms for illposedness,
degenerate dispersion
and
Takeuchi–Mizohata instability
, which can be seen from the principal and sub-principal terms, respectively. Both phenomena must be taken into account to obtain a comprehensive picture of the illposedness of (
1.5
) and (
1.8
) in the presence of a degeneracy in the initial data (and, more concretely, to explain the relevance of the exponents
\sigma_{c}
and

).

1. Principal term: dynamics of bicharacteristics
. The illposedness of (
1.9
) from degenerate dispersion can be most easily described at the level of the
bicharacteristic ODE system
associated with the principal symbol
p
of the spatial part of (
1.9
), which is given by

\left\{\begin{aligned} \dot{X}&=\partial_{\xi}p(X,\Xi),\\
\dot{\Xi}&=-\partial_{x}p(X,\Xi),\end{aligned}\right.

(1.10)

where
p(x,\xi)=-a(x)\xi^{2}
in the Schrödinger case, and
-a(x)\xi^{3}
in the KdV case. By geometric optics, the trajectory
(X(t),\Xi(t))
describes (at least on sufficiently short timescales) wave packets concentrated near
X(t)
in the physical space and
\Xi(t)
in the frequency space; see (
1.3
) and (
1.3
) below for further discussion. If (
1.10
) admits the growth of
|\Xi|
by a definite factor (e.g.,
2
) in arbitrarily short timescales, we would have a strong indication of illposedness of (
1.9
) in high-regularity Sobolev spaces. In turn, such a growth may come from some degeneracy of
p
in
X
– this phenomenon is what we shall refer to as
degenerate dispersion
.

To be concrete, let us assume that dynamics is given in
(x,\xi)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}
and the coefficient
a
in
p
is of the degenerate form
a(x)\approx Ax^{n}

(n>0)
for
|{x}|
small, so that

\begin{split}p(x,\xi)\approx-Ax^{n}\xi^{m}\quad\hbox{ for $|x|$ small and $|\xi|$ large}.\end{split}

(1.11)

Note that
m=2
in the Schrödinger case and
3
in the KdV case. The associated bicharacteristic ODE system is

\left\{\begin{aligned} \dot{X}&\approx-AmX^{n}\Xi^{m-1}\\
\dot{\Xi}&\approx AnX^{n-1}\Xi^{m},\end{aligned}\right.

(1.12)

In view of the fact that the group velocity
\dot{X}
vanishes at the point
x=0
(since
n>0
), we shall say that
p
is
degenerate
at
x=0
.

We shall now describe the illposedness mechanism of degenerate dispersion in this concrete case. (This analysis can be found in the introduction of
[
10
]
as well.) With a change of the time variable, we may take
A=1
. Assume, for the sake of this heuristic discussion, that
\approx
’s above are exact equalities. Consider the solution to (
1.12
) with initial conditions
(X_{0},\Xi_{0})
, where
0<X_{0}\ll 1
and
\Xi_{0}\gg 1
. Then, appealing to the fact that
X^{n}\Xi^{m}
is conserved in time, we have

\begin{split}\Xi(t)=\Xi_{0}\left(1+(n-m)X_{0}^{n-1}\Xi_{0}^{m-1}t\right)^{\frac{m}{m-n}}\end{split}

for
m\neq n
. When
m=n
, which will be referred to as the
critical
case, we have instead

\begin{split}\Xi(t)=\Xi_{0}\exp\left(mX_{0}^{m-1}\Xi_{0}^{m-1}t\right).\end{split}

(1.13)

In all cases, the frequency magnitude doubles (i.e.,
|{\Xi(\tau_{2})}|=2|{\Xi_{0}}|
) at time
\tau_{2}\simeq|{\Xi_{0}}|^{1-m}|{X_{0}}|^{1-n}
. If the order of

\tau_{2}
may be taken to be arbitrarily small by choosing
|{\Xi_{0}}|

Remark 1.10

1.4
Discussion of the proof

Our discussion so far has been rather formal; deriving actual nonlinear illposedness in standard function spaces requires more ideas. Our main technical contribution in this work is developing a robust scheme for establishing quantitative illposedness, which is not only able to deduce strong illposedness in quasilinear cases but also yields much stronger statements for linear equations. The scheme largely consists of three parts: 1. construction of degenerating wave packets for the linearized equation, 2. duality testing argument and 3. incorporation of the nonlinearity.

1. Degenerating wave packets
.
We first describe the ideas for construction of a degenerating wave packet. Compared to the heuristic discussion above, the actual construction of such an approximate solution
\widetilde{u}
to (
1.9
) has to (1) allow for time-dependent coefficients
a=a(t,x)
and
b=b(t,x)
(as the background solution may depend on time, see Part 3 below), and (2) solve the linearized equation up to an equation error
\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{\widetilde{u}}
of size
\mathcal{O}(\Xi_{0}^{m-1-\delta})
(in a suitable norm) for some
\delta>0
(here,
m=2
for Schrödinger and
3
for KdV). Property (2) is necessary to justify the approximation on a longer time scale than
\Xi_{0}^{1-m}
, which is the instability time scale; see (
1.13
).

Our idea is to make appropriate changes of the independent and dependent variables from
(x,u)
to
(y,v)
to reduce the problem to the constant coefficient case, for which the construction is standard. For time-dependent coefficients
a=a(t,x)
and
b=b(t,x)
, the transformation
(x,u)\mapsto(y,\check{v})
is of the form

\mathrm{d}x=(a(t,x))^{\frac{1}{m}}\mathrm{d}y,\quad u=(w\check{w})^{-1}\check{v},\quad\hbox{ where }w^{-1}\partial_{x}w=\frac{\Re b}{ma},\,\check{w}^{-1}\partial_{x}\check{w}=\frac{\partial_{x}a}{2ma}.

Roughly speaking, Takeuchi–Mizohata instability is renormalized by the conjugation of the dependent variable by the weight
w
, in the sense that
v(t,x):=wu(t,x)
solves (
1.9
) with
b=0
(with possibly different lower order terms). Similarly, degenerate dispersion is renormalized by the change of variables
x\mapsto y
accompanied with the conjugation of the dependent variable by the weight
\check{w}
, in the sense that the
\check{v}(t,y)
solves the constant coefficient problem
(\partial_{t}+i(i\partial_{y})^{m})\check{v}=(\hbox{lower order terms})
. Now, starting from a standard wave packet for the constant coefficient problem traveling towards the degeneracy and returning to original variables, we obtain a degenerating wave packet
\widetilde{u}
with the desired properties.

In order to make the above heuristic discussion precise, there are several more factors to consider. For instance, we need to make sure that the contribution of
\partial_{t}a
,
\partial_{t}b
are indeed acceptable, which ultimately relies on the estimates we have on the time derivative of the background solution
f(t,x)
in applications; see 3. below. In the Schrödinger case, we need the following two additional ideas: (i) an extra change of dependent variables to treat terms of the form
\widetilde{b}\partial_{x}\overline{u}
, and (ii) an extra phase rotation
e^{i\lambda S}
for the wave packet
\check{v}
to treat terms of the form
-(\Im b+\partial_{t}y)\partial_{y}\check{v}
, both of which are potentially problematic for achieving Property (2). For more details, see the proofs of Propositions
2.7
and
3.3
for details.

Remark 1.14

(Numerologies in the Schrödinger vs. KdV cases)

In order to justify the properties of

3. Incorporation of the nonlinearity
.
The ideas discussed so far explain how to prove the illposedness of (

) and (
1.8
) around a regular solution

whose initial data has a critical degeneracy at

[

and

for details. A key new feature of the present paper compared to
[

need not be stationary solutions, and are given as a part of the contradiction hypothesis in the proof of the non-existence theorems.

1.5

-illposedness à la Takeuchi–Mizohata

In view of the extensive appearance of the Takeuchi–Mizohata instability in this paper, it is perhaps not surprising that our techniques also apply to the original setting considered by Takeuchi and Mizohata of

-illposedness of linear nondegenerate Schrödinger-type equations. In Appendix

Proposition 1.15
.

Consider the following linear first-order perturbation of the Schrödinger equation on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
:

i\partial_{t}u+\Delta u+b^{j}(x)\partial_{j}u=0

(1.24)

where
b\in C^{1,1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Suppose that the Takeuchi–Mizohata condition for (
1.24
) fails, i.e.,

\sup\left\{\int_{0}^{T}\Re b^{j}(x-2s\omega)\omega_{j}\,\mathrm{d}s:x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\,\omega\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1},\,T>0\right\}=+\infty.

(1.25)

Then for any
\delta>0
, every solution map
L^{2}\to L^{\infty}_{t}([0,\delta];L^{2})
for (
1.24
), if it exists, is unbounded.

Note that Proposition
1.15
clearly implies the result proved in
[
25
, §VII.2]
, namely, the impossibility of having a solution map for the inhomogeneous equation

i\partial_{t}u+\Delta u+b^{j}(x)\partial_{j}u=f

(1.26)

satisfying

\|{u}\|_{L^{\infty}([0,\delta];L^{2})}\leq C_{0}\left(\|{u_{0}}\|_{L^{2}}+\|{f}\|_{L^{1}([0,\delta];L^{2})}\right)

for some

. In fact, via Duhamel’s principle, this result is equivalent to Proposition
1.15
. Nonetheless, our techniques generalize easily to other situations when such an equivalence is not obvious, e.g., when

depends on time.

More interestingly, we also provide some new unconditional quantitative lower bounds for (
1.24

O(\tfrac{1}{\lambda})

2.1
Degenerating wave packets for model linear equation

To motivate what is to follow, consider the case (

i\partial_{t}\phi+\partial_{x}(|\phi|^{2}\partial_{x}\phi)=0.

(
1.1
)

We note that when the domain is taken to be
\mathbb{R}
,
f(t,x)=xe^{2it}
is formally a solution to (
1.1
).
4
4

4

In this section, we take the domain to be
\mathbb{R}
rather than
\mathbb{T}
.
Indeed, for initial data

which is

should approximate

uniformly for small

.
The goal of this section is to sketch a proof of the fact that the linearized equation around the explicit solution

is

. To derive the equation, we write

\begin{split}\phi(t,x)=xe^{2it}+\tilde{\phi}(t,x).\end{split}

(2.1)

Plugging in (

) into (

) and dropping quadratic or higher terms in

, we obtain that

\begin{split}i\partial_{t}\tilde{\phi}+\mathcal{L}[\tilde{\phi}]=0,\quad\mathcal{L}[\cdot]=\partial_{x}(x^{2}\partial_{x}(\cdot))+2x\partial_{x}\Re(\cdot).\end{split}

(2.2)

An important observation regarding the operator

is that for any sufficiently regular

\begin{split}\left|\langle{|x|^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathcal{L}[v],|x|^{\frac{1}{2}}v}\rangle\right|\leq C\|{|x|^{\frac{1}{2}}v}\|_{L^{2}}^{2},\end{split}

(2.3)

which suggests that the correct way to measure regularity for solutions of (

--weighted spaces
5
5

5

As we shall see below, the exponents

. To this end, we set
\|{v}\|_{L^{2}_{w}}=\|{|x|^{\frac{1}{2}}v}\|_{L^{2}}
.
The main result in this section is the following.

Proposition 2.1
.

The equation (
2.2
) is ill-posed in

. More specifically, for any profile
g_{0}\in C^{\infty}(\frac{1}{2},1)
,
any

L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}_{w}
-solution
\tilde{\phi}_{(\lambda)}
to (
2.2
) with initial data

\begin{split}\tilde{\phi}_{(\lambda),0}(x)=g_{0}(x)\exp(i\lambda\ln|x|),\quad\lambda<0\end{split}

satisfies the growth

\begin{split}\|{\tilde{\phi}_{(\lambda)}}\|_{L^{2}}(t)\geq c_{0}\exp(|\lambda|t),\quad\mbox{for any }0<t<T,\end{split}

with constants

depending only on
g_{0}
.

Remark 2.2
.

By an
L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}_{w}
solution to (
2.2
), we mean a weak solution
\tilde{\phi}
which satisfies the bound

\begin{split}\|{\tilde{\phi}}\|_{L^{2}_{w}}(t)\leq\exp(Ct)\|{\tilde{\phi}_{0}}\|_{L^{2}_{w}}\end{split}

where

is the constant from (
2.3
) and attains the initial data in the weak sense. Existence of an
L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}_{w}
solution given an
L^{2}_{w}
initial data follows from a standard argument involving Aubin–Lions lemma (see
[
16
, Appendix A]
for instance). Note that
\|{\tilde{\phi}_{(\lambda),0}}\|_{L^{2}},\|{\tilde{\phi}_{(\lambda),0}}\|_{L^{2}_{w}}\lesssim 1
uniformly in
\lambda
. While we cannot rule out the possibility of non-uniqueness, the above result applies to
all

L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}_{w}
solutions.

In following the proof, the reader may already find Items 1 (Degenerating wave packets) and 2 (Modified energy estimate and duality testing argument) in Section
1.4
expanded in detail. See also the remarks following the proof, which discuss additional ideas that go into the proof of Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
.

Proof.

We demonstrate how to construct approximate solutions to (
2.2
), from which Proposition
2.1
naturally follows. To begin with, we make a change of variables
y=\ln x
for
x\geq 0
. Then using
x\partial_{x}=\partial_{y}
, (
2.2
) transforms into

\begin{split}i\partial_{t}\tilde{\phi}+\partial_{yy}\tilde{\phi}+\partial_{y}\tilde{\phi}+2\partial_{y}\Re(\tilde{\phi})=0.\end{split}

Defining
\varphi=e^{y}\tilde{\phi}
,

i\partial_{t}\varphi+\partial_{yy}\varphi+\partial_{y}\overline{\varphi}-2\varphi-\overline{\varphi}=0.

We then introduce

\psi=\varphi+A\overline{\varphi},

(2.4)

where
A=\frac{1}{2}\partial_{y}^{-1}
is (formally) an operator of order
-1
. Then

\displaystyle i\partial_{t}\psi

\displaystyle=-\partial_{yy}\varphi+A\partial_{yy}\overline{\varphi}-\partial_{y}\overline{\varphi}+A\partial_{y}\varphi

\displaystyle\mathrel{\phantom{=}}-2\varphi-\overline{\varphi}+2A\overline{\varphi}+A\varphi

\displaystyle=-\partial_{yy}\psi+A\partial_{yy}\overline{\varphi}+\partial_{yy}A\overline{\varphi}-\partial_{y}\overline{\varphi}

\displaystyle\mathrel{\phantom{=}}+A\partial_{y}\varphi-2\varphi-\overline{\varphi}+2A\overline{\varphi}+A\varphi.

Then we see that

i\partial_{t}\psi+\partial_{yy}\psi=A\partial_{y}\varphi-2\varphi-\overline{\varphi}+2A\overline{\varphi}+A\varphi.

Since the right hand side is of order zero, it suggests that a degenerating wave packet
\varphi
may be constructed by taking
\psi
to be an approximate wave packet solution to the one-dimensional Schrödinger equation, and then going back to
\varphi
. More precisely, take

\psi^{app}_{(\lambda)}(t,y)=e^{i\lambda y-i\lambda^{2}t}a_{0}(y-2\lambda t),

(2.5)

where we fix
a_{0}
to be
C^{\infty}
–smooth and supported in
\{-2<y<-1\}
. We need to take
\lambda<0
, so that the support of
\phi^{app}(t,\cdot)
is confined to
\{y<-1\}
for all
t\geq 0
.
To invert (
2.4
), we wish to take
\varphi\approx\psi-A\overline{\psi}
. Since
A=\frac{1}{2}\partial_{y}^{-1}
acts like
-\frac{1}{2i\lambda}
on
\overline{\psi}
, we are motivated to take

\displaystyle\varphi^{app}(t,y)

\displaystyle=\psi^{app}_{(\lambda)}(t,y)+\frac{1}{2i\lambda}\overline{\psi^{app}_{(\lambda)}}(t,y)

(2.6)

and then set

\begin{split}\tilde{\phi}^{app}=e^{-y}\varphi^{app}=e^{-y}\left(e^{i\lambda(y-\lambda t)}a_{0}(y-2\lambda t)+\frac{1}{2i\lambda}e^{-i\lambda(y-\lambda t)}\overline{a_{0}}(y-2\lambda t)\right).\end{split}

Returning to the
x
-coordinates and defining the error by
\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{\tilde{\phi}}=[i\partial_{t}+\mathcal{L}]\tilde{\phi}^{app}
, we have

\begin{split}\|{\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{\tilde{\phi}}(t)}\|_{L^{2}_{w}}\lesssim\|{a_{0}}\|_{H^{2}_{x}},\qquad t\geq 0,\end{split}

(2.7)

uniformly in
\lambda
. In this sense,
\tilde{\phi}^{app}
is an approximate solution of (
2.2
). Moreover,
\tilde{\phi}^{app}
itself satisfies the bound
\|{{\tilde{\phi}^{app}}(t)}\|_{L^{2}_{w}}\lesssim\|{a_{0}}\|_{L^{2}_{x}}
. The last key property is degeneration: with a weight higher than
|x|^{\frac{1}{2}}
,
\tilde{\phi}^{app}(t)
decays in the
O(|\lambda|^{-1})
–timescale: for example, with the weight
|x|
, we have

\begin{split}\|{|x|\tilde{\phi}^{app}(t,x)}\|_{L^{2}_{x}}\lesssim e^{-|\lambda|t}\|{a_{0}}\|_{L^{2}_{x}}.\end{split}

(2.8)

Interpolating (
2.8
) with
L^{2}_{w}
–estimate shows that
\|{\tilde{\phi}^{app}(t,\cdot)}\|_{L^{2}}\gtrsim e^{|\lambda|t}
. Now, let
\tilde{\phi}
be an
L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}_{w}
solution of (
2.2
). Then, with a direct computation, we have the
generalized energy estimate
for the weighted
L^{2}
–estimate (see §
2.4.2
):

\begin{split}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\langle{|x|^{\frac{1}{2}}\tilde{\phi},|x|^{\frac{1}{2}}\tilde{\phi}^{app}}\rangle=\langle{|x|^{\frac{1}{2}}\tilde{\phi},|x|^{\frac{1}{2}}\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{\tilde{\phi}}}\rangle,\end{split}

which gives, together with (
2.7
),

\begin{split}\Re\,\langle{|x|^{\frac{1}{2}}\tilde{\phi},|x|^{\frac{1}{2}}\tilde{\phi}^{app}}\rangle(t)\geq\Re\,\langle{|x|^{\frac{1}{2}}\tilde{\phi},|x|^{\frac{1}{2}}\tilde{\phi}^{app}}\rangle(t=0)-Ct\|{\tilde{\phi}}\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}_{w}}\|{a_{0}}\|_{H^{2}_{x}}.\end{split}

At the initial time, by choosing
a_{0}
in a way depending only on
g_{0}
, we can guarantee that
\Re\,\langle{|x|^{\frac{1}{2}}\tilde{\phi},|x|^{\frac{1}{2}}\tilde{\phi}^{app}}\rangle(t=0)\geq\frac{1}{2}\|{\tilde{\phi}_{0}}\|_{L^{2}_{w}}\|{\tilde{\phi}^{app}_{0}}\|_{L^{2}_{w}}
. Then, for
0<t<C\|{a_{0}}\|_{H^{2}_{x}}/(4\|{\tilde{\phi}_{0}}\|_{L^{2}_{w}})

\begin{split}\frac{1}{C}e^{-|\lambda|t}\|{a_{0}}\|_{L^{2}}\|{\tilde{\phi}(t)}\|_{L^{2}}\geq\Re\,\langle{\tilde{\phi},|x|\tilde{\phi}^{app}}\rangle(t)=\Re\,\langle{|x|^{\frac{1}{2}}\tilde{\phi},|x|^{\frac{1}{2}}\tilde{\phi}^{app}}\rangle(t)\geq\frac{1}{4}\|{\tilde{\phi}_{0}}\|_{L^{2}_{w}}\|{\tilde{\phi}^{app}_{0}}\|_{L^{2}_{w}},\end{split}

which gives the claimed exponential growth of
\|{\tilde{\phi}(t)}\|_{L^{2}}
.
∎

Remark 2.3

(Illposedness in
H^{m}
for
m>0
)
.

In fact, a small modification of the above proof shows that (
2.2
) is ill-posed in
H^{m}
for
m>0
. More precisely, the following growth occurs:

\|{{\partial_{x}^{m}}\tilde{\phi}_{(\lambda)}}\|_{L^{2}}(t)\geq c_{0}\exp({(1+2m)}|\lambda|t),\quad\mbox{for any }m\geq 0\mbox{ and }0<t<T,

with
c_{0},T>0
depending only on
g_{0}
and
m
.

We now sketch the needed modification; see Section
2.5
for the complete proof. We would like to modify the last part of the proof of Proposition
2.1
using “differentiation by parts”: under the assumption that
\partial_{x}^{-m}(|x|\tilde{\phi}^{app})\in L^{2}
,

\begin{split}\|{\partial_{x}^{m}\tilde{\phi}}\|_{L^{2}}\|{\partial_{x}^{-m}(|x|\tilde{\phi}^{app})}\|_{L^{2}}\geq\Re\,\langle{\partial_{x}^{m}\tilde{\phi},\partial_{x}^{-m}(|x|\tilde{\phi}^{app})}\rangle(t)=\Re\,\langle{|x|^{\frac{1}{2}}\tilde{\phi},|x|^{\frac{1}{2}}\tilde{\phi}^{app}}\rangle(t).\end{split}

Now the point is that
\partial_{x}^{-1}=x\partial_{y}^{-1}=e^{y}\partial_{y}^{-1}
and
y\simeq-2|\lambda|t
on the support of
\tilde{\phi}^{app}(t)
, which gives a faster rate of degeneration
\|{\partial_{x}^{-m}(|x|\tilde{\phi}^{app})}\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim e^{-(2m+1)|\lambda|t}
. This gives the claimed lower bound for
\|{\partial_{x}^{m}\tilde{\phi}}\|_{L^{2}}
. In general, there could be some low frequency part of
|x|\tilde{\phi}^{app}
which does not degenerate, and for this reason we introduce a decomposition of
|x|\tilde{\phi}^{app}
into high and low frequency parts in the actual proof in Section
2.5
.

Remark 2.4

(Additional ideas in the proof of Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
)
.

For a general equation of the form (

), we do not have access to a stationary solution with a linear degeneracy in general (furthermore, we shall also require that

be compactly supported, which rules out

, too). Hence, we shall carry out the above analysis (degenerating wave packet construction, modified energy estimate and duality) where the background solution

is merely a regular (most likely)

solution to (

), which has compactly supported initial data
f_{0}
with a linear degeneracy, in place of

.

Theorem

is proved by considering a perturbation

of such an

, and arguing that if

exists as a regular solution (i.e., if we are in the second case in Theorem

), then the above growth mechanism for
\widetilde{\phi}
can be justified. To prove Theorem
1.2
, we consider initial data
\widetilde{\phi}_{0}
consisting of a superposition of an infinitude of configurations as above (i.e.,
\sum_{k}(f_{k,0}+\widetilde{\phi}_{k,0})

has a linear degeneracy and
\widetilde{\phi}_{k,0}
is a degenerating wave packet adapted to

\{{\mathrm{supp}}\,f_{k,0}\cup{\mathrm{supp}}\,\phi_{k,0}\}_{k}
is pairwise disjoint), yet with an
\epsilon

-norm. Then we perform a contradiction argument: if a regular solution
\phi
to such initial data exists, then we may justify the growth mechanism (as in Proposition
2.1
), which is absurd. For details, see Sections
2.5
and
2.6
below.

2.2
Properties of a regular linearly degenerate solution

We shall assume that there exists a smooth solution to (
1.5
) which is linearly degenerate, and analyze its properties. To be precise, let
f:[0,\delta]\times[-x_{1},x_{1}]\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
be a
L^{\infty}([0,\delta];C^{3,1}([{x_{0}}-x_{1},{x_{0}+}x_{1}]))
solution to (
1.5
) with some
x_{1},\delta>0
satisfying

\begin{split}f_{0}\in C^{3,1}([{x_{0}}-x_{1},{x_{0}}+x_{1}]),\quad f_{0}(x_{0})=0,\quad|f^{\prime}_{0}(x_{0})|>0\end{split}

at
t=0
for some
x_{0}\in\mathbb{T}
.

Owing to the symmetries of (
1.5
) (translation and phase rotation), as well as its behavior under the transformation
\phi\mapsto c\phi
, we may assume without loss of generality that
x_{0}=0
and
f^{\prime}_{0}(0)=1
. Then, from the equation it is easy to see that, on the time interval
[0,\delta]
,

\begin{split}f(t,0)=0\end{split}

and

\begin{split}i\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}f^{\prime}(t,0)=-(\alpha_{1}+\beta_{1})|f^{\prime}(t,0)|^{2}f^{\prime}(t,0),\end{split}

which implies in particular that

|f^{\prime}(t,0)|=1,\quad\hbox{ and }|f(t,x)|=x+O(|x|^{2})\hbox{ uniformly in $t$.}

More generally, we have the following lemma.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
s\geq 2
be an integer and let
f\in C_{t}([0,\delta];C^{s-1,1}(\mathbb{T}))
be a solution to (
1.5
). Then

1.

The zero set of
f(t,x)
is preserved in time, i.e.,
a\in\mathbb{T}
is a zero of
f(0,x)
if and only if it is a zero for
f(t,x)
for all
t\in[0,\delta]
.

2.

Let
a\in\mathbb{T}
be a zero of
f(0,x)
. Then
\{\partial_{x}^{k}f(t,a)\}_{k=0}^{s-1}
is determined by the initial data at
x=a
, i.e.,
\{\partial_{x}^{k}f(0,a)\}_{k=0}^{s-1}
.

Here, the important point is that, thanks to the regularity assumption,
f(0,x)
vanishes at least linearly at each zero
x=a
, which is
critical
for (
1.5
) in the senses discussed in §
1.3
.

Proof.

By the regularity assumption (in particular, that
s\geq 2
), it follows from (
1.5
) that
|{\partial_{t}f(t,x)}|\leq C|{f(t,x)}|
; hence the first statement follows. To prove the second statement, consider a zero
a
of
f(0,x)
. Without any loss of generality, we may assume that
a=0
. By the assumption and Taylor expansion, we have

\displaystyle f(t,x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{s-1}\frac{1}{k!}\partial_{x}^{k}f(t,0)x^{k}+O(|{x}|^{s}),

\displaystyle f_{x}(t,x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{s-2}\frac{1}{k!}\partial_{x}^{k+1}f(t,0)x^{k}+O(|{x}|^{s-1}),

\displaystyle f_{xx}(t,x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{s-3}\frac{1}{k!}\partial_{x}^{k+2}f(t,0)x^{k}+O(|{x}|^{s-2}),

where the implicit constants depend only on
\|{f}\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}C^{s-1,1}_{x}}
(and in particular are independent of
(t,x)
). Plugging this into (
1.5
) and matching the coefficients of
x^{k}
for
k=1,\ldots,s-1
, we formally obtain a determined system of first-order ODE’s for
\{\partial_{x}^{k}f(t,0)\}_{k=1}^{s-1}
; here, the fact that
f(t,x)
vanishes at least linearly is crucially used to ensure that no
\partial_{x}^{k}f(t,0)
with
k>s-1
arises. Indeed, these ODEs may be justified in the sense of distributions by testing (
1.5
) against a test function of the form
\eta(t)(-1)^{k}\partial_{x}^{k}\chi_{\epsilon}(x)
, where
\eta\in C^{\infty}_{c}(0,\delta)
,
\chi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2})
with
\int\chi=1
and
\chi_{\epsilon}(x)=\epsilon^{-1}\chi(\epsilon^{-1}x)
. By the uniqueness of this ODE system, the desired statement follows.
∎

From now on, given
f_{0}
which are linearly degenerate at
x=0
and
f_{x}(0,0)
positive real, we are going to take
0<x_{1}<1
smaller if necessary, so that

\begin{split}\left(\sup_{x\in[-x_{1},x_{1}]}|f_{xx}(0,x)|\right)x_{1}<\frac{f_{x}(0,0)}{2}\end{split}

(2.9)

holds. In particular, we have that

\begin{split}\frac{1}{2}f_{x}(0,0)<|f_{x}(0,x)|<2f_{x}(0,0),\quad\mbox{for all}\quad x\in[-x_{1},x_{1}].\end{split}

Proposition 2.6
.

Let
f\in L^{\infty}_{t}([0,\delta];C^{3,1}([-x_{1},x_{1}])
be a solution to (
1.5
), and set
M=\|{f}\|_{L^{\infty}([0,\delta];C^{3,1})}
. Then, we have the pointwise bounds

\begin{split}|f(t,x)|\leq|f_{0}(x)|\exp(CM^{2}t)\end{split}

(2.10)

and

\begin{split}\left|\partial_{t}(|f(t,x)|^{2})\right|\leq CM\exp(CM^{2}\delta)\left(1+(f_{x}(0,0))^{-1}M\right)^{3}\left(|f_{0}(x)|^{3}+tM^{3}|f_{0}(x)|^{2}\right)\end{split}

(2.11)

for all
t\in[0,\delta]
and
|x|\leq x_{1}
.

Proof.

We first note directly from (
1.5
) that
\left|\partial_{t}|f(t,x)|\right|\leq C\|{f}\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}_{t}C^{1,1}_{x}}|f(t,x)|
holds, which gives (
2.10
). Now note that
f\in L^{\infty}([0,\delta];C^{3,1}([-x_{1},x_{1}]))
implies, via (
1.5
), that

\begin{split}|\partial_{t}f(t,x)|\leq C|f(t,x)|\|{f}\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}_{t}C^{1,1}_{x}},\qquad|\partial_{tt}f(t,x)|&\leq C|f(t,x)|^{2}\|{f}\|^{3}_{L^{\infty}_{t}C^{3,1}_{x}}.\end{split}

(2.12)

Then, the Taylor expansion in time of
f(t,x)
gives

\begin{split}|f(t,x)|^{2}&=|f_{0}(x)|^{2}+2\Re\left(\overline{f_{0}(x)}\int_{0}^{t}(\partial_{t}f)(t^{\prime},x)\,\mathrm{d}t^{\prime}\right)+\left|\int_{0}^{t}(\partial_{t}f)(t^{\prime},x)\,\mathrm{d}t^{\prime}\right|^{2}.\end{split}

Taking the time derivative,

\begin{split}\partial_{t}(|f(t,x)|^{2})=2\Re\left(\overline{f_{0}(x)}(\partial_{t}f)(t,x)\right)+2\Re\left(\overline{(\partial_{t}f)(t,x)}\int_{0}^{t}(\partial_{t}f)(t^{\prime},x)\,\mathrm{d}t^{\prime}\right).\end{split}

(2.13)

Using (
2.10
), the last term in (
2.13
) is bounded by

\begin{split}C\|{f}\|^{4}_{L^{\infty}_{t}C^{3,1}_{x}}|f(t,x)|\int_{0}^{t}|f(t^{\prime},x)|\,\mathrm{d}t^{\prime}\leq CM^{4}t\exp(CM^{2}\delta)|f_{0}(x)|^{2}.\end{split}

For the other term in the right hand side of (
2.13
), we further rewrite as

\begin{split}2\Re\left(\overline{f_{0}(x)}(\partial_{t}f)(t,x)\right)=2\Re\left(\overline{f_{0}(x)}(\partial_{t}f)(0,x)\right)+2\Re\left(\overline{f_{0}(x)}\int_{0}^{t}(\partial_{tt}f)(t^{\prime},x)\,\mathrm{d}t^{\prime}\right),\end{split}

and note that the last term is bounded using (
2.12
) by
C\exp(CM^{2}\delta)M^{3}t|f_{0}(x)|^{3}.

On the other hand, the first term on the right hand side equals

\begin{split}&\Im\left(\overline{f_{0}(x)}\left(|{f_{0}(x)}|^{2}\partial_{xx}f_{0}(x)+\alpha_{1}f_{0}(x)|{\partial_{x}f_{0}(x)}|^{2}+\beta_{1}\overline{f_{0}(x)}(\partial_{x}f_{0}(x))^{2}+\mu_{1}|{f_{0}(x)}|^{2}f_{0}(x)\right)\right)\\
&\qquad=\beta_{1}\Im\left(\overline{f_{0}(x)}^{2}(\partial_{x}f_{0}(x))^{2}\right)+O(\|{f_{0}}\|_{C^{1,1}})|f_{0}(x)|^{3},\end{split}

and we see that the leading term in the Taylor expansion of
\overline{f_{0}(x)}^{2}(\partial_{x}f_{0}(x))^{2}
is purely real, with remainder bounded by

\begin{split}C(f_{x}(0,0)^{3}|x|^{3}\|{f_{0}}\|_{\dot{C}^{1,1}}+f_{x}(0,0)^{2}|x|^{4}\|{f_{0}}\|_{\dot{C}^{1,1}}^{2}+f_{x}(0,0)^{3}|x|^{5}\|{f_{0}}\|_{\dot{C}^{1,1}}^{2})\leq C(1+f_{x}(0,0)^{-1}M)^{3}|f_{0}(x)|^{3},\end{split}

where we have used
|x|<x_{1}
and the smallness of
x_{1}
from (
2.9
). Collecting the bounds, we obtain the proposition. ∎

2.3
Degenerating wave packets for the linearized equation

In this subsection, our goal is to construct approximate solutions, called degenerating wave packets, for the linearization of (
1.5
) around a (possibly hypothetical) regular linearly degenerate solution, which possess the desired degeneration property that is responsible for the illposedness of (
1.5
); see Proposition
2.7
below.

2.3.1
Properties of degenerating wave packets

Given a smooth solution
f
to (
1.5
), let us write
\phi=f+\tilde{\phi}
where
\phi
is another smooth solution to (
1.5
). The equation for
\tilde{\phi}
is given by

\begin{split}&i\partial_{t}\tilde{\phi}+|{f}|^{2}\partial_{xx}\tilde{\phi}+\alpha_{1}f(\overline{\partial_{x}f}\partial_{x}\tilde{\phi}+\partial_{x}f\partial_{x}\overline{\tilde{\phi}})+2\beta_{1}\overline{f}\partial_{x}f\partial_{x}\tilde{\phi}+V_{f}\tilde{\phi}+W_{f}\overline{\tilde{\phi}}=Q_{f}[\tilde{\phi}],\end{split}

3.4
Modified energy estimate for the perturbation

Recall the equation satisfied by
\phi
:

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\phi+f\phi_{xxx}+\alpha_{1}f_{x}\phi_{xx}+(\alpha_{1}f_{xx}+\mu_{1}f^{m-1})\phi_{x}+(f_{xxx}+(m-1)\mu_{1}f^{m-2}f_{x})\phi

(3.37)

\displaystyle=-\phi\phi_{xxx}-\alpha_{1}\phi_{x}\phi_{xx}-\mu_{1}\left((f+\phi)^{m}-f^{m}-mf^{m-1}\phi\right)_{x}.

Regarding a solution
\phi
of the above, we have the following modified energy estimate.

Proposition 3.4
.

Assume that
f
is a solution to (
1.8
) satisfying
f\in L^{\infty}([0,\delta];\widetilde{C}^{3,\alpha}(I))
with initial data
f_{0}
that is positive on
I\setminus\partial I
and vanishes to order at least
3
on each point in
\partial I
. Moreover, assume that
\phi\in L^{\infty}([0,\delta];C^{3,\alpha}(I))
is a solution to (
3.37
) satisfying

\begin{split}f+\phi\in L^{\infty}([0,\delta];\widetilde{C}^{3,\alpha}(I)),\qquad f^{-1}(f+\phi)\in L^{\infty}([0,\delta];L^{\infty}(I)).\end{split}

Then we have the estimate

\begin{split}\int_{{I}}\phi^{2}(t,x){f(t,x)^{-\frac{2}{3}\sigma_{c}}}\,\mathrm{d}x\leq{\exp\left(C_{f,f+\phi}t\right)}\int_{{I}}\phi_{0}^{2}(x){f(t,x)^{-\frac{2}{3}\sigma_{c}}}\,\mathrm{d}x\end{split}

for
t\in[0,\delta]
, where
\phi_{0}(x)=\phi(0,x)
and

C_{f,f+\phi}\leq C\sup_{t\in[0,\delta]}(\|{f}\|_{Y}+(1+\|{f^{-1}(f+\phi)}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{\frac{1}{2}})\|{f+\phi}\|_{Y}+(\|{f}\|_{C^{0,1}}+\|{f+\phi}\|_{C^{0,1}})^{m-1}),

(3.38)

with
C>0
an absolute constant.

Proof.

In what follows, we shall simply present a formal computation without worrying about the validity of the expressions and manipulations. Also, all integrals are taken over
I
. As in §
2.4.1
, the assumption
|{\phi_{0}(x)}|\leq C|{f_{0}(x)}|
and the finiteness of the right-hand side of
\int\phi^{2}_{0}(x){f(t,x)^{-\frac{2}{3}\sigma_{c}}}\,\mathrm{d}x<+\infty
would imply, via Lemma
3.1
, the vanishing property of
\phi(t,\cdot)
on
\partial I
that is necessary to justify the computation; we shall leave the routine details to the reader.

To prove the proposition, we compute

\begin{split}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\int\phi^{2}{f(t,x)^{-\frac{2}{3}\sigma_{c}}}\,\mathrm{d}x&=\int 2\phi\partial_{t}\phi{f(t,x)^{-\frac{2}{3}\sigma_{c}}}\,\mathrm{d}x+\int\phi^{2}\partial_{t}f(t,x)^{-\frac{2}{3}\sigma_{c}}\,\mathrm{d}x,\end{split}

and the last term can be bounded as in the proof of (
3.4
); we have

\begin{split}\left|\int\phi^{2}\partial_{t}{f(t,x)^{-\frac{2}{3}\sigma_{c}}}\,\mathrm{d}x\right|\leq C\|{f}\|_{Y}\int\phi^{2}{f(t,x)^{-\frac{2}{3}\sigma_{c}}}\,\mathrm{d}x.\end{split}

We decompose the other term in the right hand side as follows, up to a factor of 2:

\begin{split}I&=-\int\phi\left(f\phi_{xxx}+\phi f_{xxx}+{\alpha_{1}}f_{x}\phi_{xx}+{\alpha_{1}}f_{xx}\phi_{x}\right){f^{-\frac{2}{3}\sigma_{c}}}\,\mathrm{d}x,\\
II&=-\mu_{1}\int\phi\left((m-1)\phi f^{m-2}f_{x}+f^{m-1}\phi_{x}\right){f^{-\frac{2}{3}\sigma_{c}}}\,\mathrm{d}x,\\
III&=-\int\phi\,{Q[\phi]}{f^{-\frac{2}{3}\sigma_{c}}}\,\mathrm{d}x.\end{split}

To estimate
I
, we observe the following chain of inequalities:

\begin{split}\left|\int\phi^{2}f_{xxx}{f^{-\frac{2}{3}\sigma_{c}}}\,\mathrm{d}x\right|\leq C\|{f}\|_{Y}\int\phi^{2}{f^{-\frac{2}{3}\sigma_{c}}}\,\mathrm{d}x,\end{split}

\begin{split}\left|\int{\alpha_{1}}\phi\phi_{x}f_{xx}{f^{-\frac{2}{3}\sigma_{c}}}\,\mathrm{d}x\right|=\left|\frac{{\alpha_{1}}}{2}\int\phi^{2}\partial_{x}(f_{xx}{f^{-\frac{2}{3}\sigma_{c}}})\,\mathrm{d}x\right|,\end{split}

\begin{split}\int{\alpha_{1}}\phi\phi_{xx}f_{x}{f^{-\frac{2}{3}\sigma_{c}}}\,\mathrm{d}x=-{\alpha_{1}}\int(\phi_{x})^{2}f_{x}{f^{-\frac{2}{3}\sigma_{c}}}\,\mathrm{d}x+\frac{{\alpha_{1}}}{2}\int\phi^{2}\partial_{xx}(f_{x}{f^{-\frac{2}{3}\sigma_{c}}})\,\mathrm{d}x,\end{split}

and lastly

\begin{split}\int\phi\phi_{xxx}f{f^{-\frac{2}{3}\sigma_{c}}}\,\mathrm{d}x&=-\int\phi_{xx}\phi_{x}f{f^{-\frac{2}{3}\sigma_{c}}}-\phi_{xx}\phi\partial_{x}(f{f^{-\frac{2}{3}\sigma_{c}}})\,\mathrm{d}x\\
&=\frac{3}{2}\int(\phi_{x})^{2}\partial_{x}(f{f^{-\frac{2}{3}\sigma_{c}}})\,\mathrm{d}x-\frac{1}{2}\int\phi^{2}\partial_{xxx}(f{f^{-\frac{2}{3}\sigma_{c}}})\,\mathrm{d}x.\end{split}

From

\begin{split}\partial_{x}(f{f^{-\frac{2}{3}\sigma_{c}}})=\left(1-\frac{2}{3}\sigma_{c}\right){f^{-\frac{2}{3}\sigma_{c}}}f_{x}{=\frac{2}{3}\alpha_{1}f^{-\frac{2}{3}\sigma_{c}}f_{x}},\end{split}

we obtain a cancellation of terms involving
(\phi_{x})^{2}
and then we observe

\begin{split}|\partial_{x}(f_{xx}{f^{-\frac{2}{3}\sigma_{c}}})|+|\partial_{xx}(f_{x}{f^{-\frac{2}{3}\sigma_{c}}})|+|\partial_{xxx}(f{f^{-\frac{2}{3}\sigma_{c}}})|\leq C\|{f}\|_{Y}{f^{-\frac{2}{3}\sigma_{c}}}\end{split}

to conclude the estimate

\begin{split}|I|\leq C\|{f}\|_{Y}\left|\int\phi^{2}{f^{-\frac{2}{3}\sigma_{c}}}\,\mathrm{d}x\right|.\end{split}

Next, to treat
II
we simply integrate by parts:

\begin{split}\left|II\right|&={\left|\mu_{1}\int\phi^{2}\left((m-1)f^{m-2}f_{x}-\frac{-\frac{2}{3}\sigma_{c}+m-1}{2}f^{m-2}f_{x}\right)f^{-\frac{2}{3}\sigma_{c}}\,\mathrm{d}x\right|}\leq C{\|{f}\|_{C^{0,1}}^{m-1}}\int\phi^{2}{f^{-\frac{2}{3}\sigma_{c}}}\,\mathrm{d}x.\end{split}

Finally, we turn to
III
. Observe that we are free to use
\|{\phi}\|_{C^{3,\alpha}}
, since it is controlled by
\|{f}\|_{C^{3,\alpha}}+\|{f+\phi}\|_{C^{3,\alpha}}
; similarly for
\|{\phi}\|_{C^{0,1}}
. Recall the expression for
Q[\phi]
given in (
3.15
). We first estimate

\begin{split}\left|\int\phi(-\phi\phi_{xxx})f^{-\frac{2}{3}\sigma_{c}}\,\mathrm{d}x\right|\leq C\|{\phi}\|_{C^{2,1}}\int\phi^{2}f^{-\frac{2}{3}\sigma_{c}}\,\mathrm{d}x,\end{split}

and

\displaystyle\left|\int\phi(-\alpha_{1}\phi_{x}\phi_{xx})f^{-\frac{2}{3}\sigma_{c}}\,\mathrm{d}x\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\int\frac{\alpha_{1}}{2}\phi^{2}\phi_{xxx}f^{-\frac{2}{3}\sigma_{c}}\,\mathrm{d}x\right|+\left|\int\frac{\alpha_{1}\sigma_{c}}{3}\phi^{2}(\phi+f)_{xx}f^{\frac{-2\sigma_{c}-3}{3}}f_{x}\,\mathrm{d}x\right|+\left|\int\frac{\alpha_{1}\sigma_{c}}{3}\phi^{2}f_{xx}f^{\frac{-2\sigma_{c}-3}{3}}f_{x}\,\mathrm{d}x\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\int\frac{\alpha_{1}}{2}\phi^{2}\phi_{xxx}f^{-\frac{2}{3}\sigma_{c}}\,\mathrm{d}x\right|+\left|\int\frac{\alpha_{1}\sigma_{c}}{3}\phi^{2}(\phi+f)_{xx}f^{\frac{-2\sigma_{c}-3}{3}}f_{x}\,\mathrm{d}x\right|+\left|\int\frac{\alpha_{1}\sigma_{c}}{3}\phi^{2}f_{xx}f^{\frac{-2\sigma_{c}-3}{3}}f_{x}\,\mathrm{d}x\right|

\displaystyle\leq C(\|{\phi}\|_{C^{2,1}}+\|{f^{-1}(f+\phi)}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{\frac{1}{3}}\|{f+\phi}\|_{Y}^{\frac{2}{3}}\|{f}\|_{Y}^{\frac{1}{3}}+\|{f}\|_{Y})\int\phi^{2}f^{0\frac{2}{3}\sigma_{c}}\,\mathrm{d}x.

The remaining terms in

are easier to treat, and collecting the estimates, we conclude

\begin{split}\left|\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\int\phi^{2}f^{-\frac{2}{3}\sigma_{c}}\,\mathrm{d}x\right|\leq C_{f,f+\phi}\int\phi^{2}f^{-\frac{2}{3}\sigma_{c}}\,\mathrm{d}x\end{split}

for some

satisfying (
3.38
). The proposition follows by integrating in time. ∎

&\mathrel{\phantom{=}}-\langle{\mathcal{L}_{f}[\phi],\phi^{app}_{(\lambda)}}\rangle_{f}+\langle{Q_{f}[\phi],\phi^{app}_{(\lambda)}}\rangle_{f}\\
&\mathrel{\phantom{=}}-\frac{2}{3}\sigma_{c}\langle{f^{-1}\partial_{t}f\phi,\phi^{app}_{(\lambda)}}\rangle_{f}.\end{split}

We first uncover some cancellations between the two terms involving the linearized operator

\displaystyle\mathrel{\phantom{=}}-\mu_{1}\int\phi\left(f^{m-1}\phi^{app}_{(\lambda)x}+(m-1)f^{m-2}f_{x}\phi^{app}_{(\lambda)}\right)f^{-\frac{2}{3}\sigma_{c}}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\mathrel{\phantom{=}}-\int\left(f\phi_{xxx}+\alpha_{1}f_{x}\phi_{xx}+\alpha_{1}f_{xx}\phi_{x}+f_{xxx}\phi\right)\phi^{app}_{(\lambda)}f^{-\frac{2}{3}\sigma_{c}}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\mathrel{\phantom{=}}-\mu_{1}\int\left(f^{m-1}\phi_{x}+(m-1)f^{m-2}f_{x}\phi\right)\phi^{app}_{(\lambda)}f^{-\frac{2}{3}\sigma_{c}}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=:I+II+III+IV.

and

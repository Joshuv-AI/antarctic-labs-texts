Theorem 1.1

Let

(

In the classical Nash-Kuiper approach, a normal vector field is used to deform the initial embedding and increase the metric. In the setting of Gromov’s theorem, we have both timelike and spacelike normal directions, which allows deforming the embedding in both directions and hence, increasing and decreasing the metric following which direction is used. In the following, we will be interested in the codimension
1

, and
r_{+}=q_{+}
:

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
(V,g)
be a compact Riemannian manifold,
(W,h)
a pseudo-Riemannian manifold and
f:V\rightarrow(W,h)
a long spacelike embedding i.e,
g\leq f^{*}h
. Then, the embedding
f
can be
C^{0}
-approximated by a
C^{1}
isometric embedding
F:(V,g)\rightarrow(W,h)
.

The main difference here with the classical Nash-Kuiper theorem and Gromov’s generalization is that the normal at any point of
V
(seen as an embedded hypersurface in

f
following the Nash-Kuiper process. However, we need to ensure that the tangent space is far from the light cone at each step of the process to guarantee the
C^{1}
regularity at the end (see lemma
3.7

As in the classical Nash-Kuiper theorem, Theorem
1.2
reflects high flexibility when considering
C^{1}
-isometric embeddings, which is not the case for higher regularity. Indeed, for any surface
\Sigma
of genus
\geq 2
and any cocompact lattice

isomorphic to

\Sigma
that admits a smooth isometric embedding in the quotient of the
(2+1)
-dimensional solid timelike cone by
\Gamma
. On the other hand, Theorem
1.2
implies:

Corollary 1.3
.

Let
\Sigma
and
\Gamma
as above. Then, any hyperbolic metric on
\Sigma
admits a
C^{1}
-isometric embedding in the quotient of the
(2+1)
-dimensional solid timelike cone by
\Gamma
.

2.
The Nash-Kuiper process

We will give the proof of Theorem
1.2
in the case where
(V,g)
is a compact Riemannian surface and
(W,h)
is a
3
-dimensional Lorentzian manifold. The methods used here can be applied in higher dimensions without employing any additional ideas.

In order to apply the Nash-Kuiper process in this setting, we need to start with a long embedding, that is an embedding
f:V\rightarrow W
, such that the metric
\Delta:=f^{*}h-g
is positive semi-definite. We call
\Delta
the isometric default.

The Nash-Kuiper process consists of reducing the isometric default gradually. More precisely, we will consider a sequence
(g_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of Riemannian metrics defined by
g_{n}=g+\delta_{n}\Delta
, where
(\delta_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a decreasing sequence of positive numbers converging to
0
. Using a variant of the convex integration formula called the corrugation process
[
10
]
, we build a sequence
(f_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of embeddings from
V
to
W
such that:

(1)

\parallel f_{n}^{*}h-g_{n}\parallel<\parallel g_{n}-g_{n+1}\parallel
,

(2)

f_{n}
is
C^{0}
close to
f_{n-1}
,

(3)

\parallel f_{n}-f_{n+1}\parallel_{g,\widetilde{h}}
is under control,

where
\parallel.\parallel_{g,\widetilde{h}}
is the operator norm with respect to the metric
g
on
V
and any fixed Riemannian metric on
W
, we denote it by
\widetilde{h}
. Note that the first condition implies that
f_{n}
is long for the metric
g_{n+1}
since
g_{n}\geq g_{n+1}
. Therefore, we need to be able, starting from a long embedding, to construct an embedding which is
\varepsilon
-isometric, i.e, with small isometric default. To build
f_{n+1}
from
f_{n}
, we will use Theillière’s corrugation process formula:

Definition 2.1

(
[
10
]
)
.

Let
f:U\rightarrow(W,h)
be a map from an open set
U\subset V
,
\pi:U\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
a submersion,
N\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
and
\gamma:U\times\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}\rightarrow f^{*}TW
be a smooth loop family such that
\gamma(p,.):\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}\rightarrow f^{*}TW_{p}
for every
p\in U
. The map defined by Corrugation Process is given by:

F:p\mapsto exp_{f(p)}(\frac{1}{N}\Gamma(p,N\pi(p)))

where
\Gamma(p,s)=\int_{0}^{s}(\gamma(p,t)-\bar{\gamma}(p)dt
and
\bar{\gamma}(p)=\int_{0}^{1}\gamma(p,t)dt
. We call
N
the corrugation number.

Remark 2.2
.

Unlike the convex integration formula, the corrugation process formula is defined directly on the manifold (coordinate free). Moreover,
F(p)
is determined by the value of
f,\,\gamma,\,\pi
at
p
.

In addition, since we are using a Riemannian metric
\widetilde{h}
on
W
to compute the
C^{1}
distance, it will be important to control the norm with respect to
\widetilde{h}
of the timelike normal to
f_{n}
for each step

. We will prove that this is possible and that the evolution of this norm at each

can be absorbed by a good choice of the sequence
\delta_{n}
, which will ensure the
C^{1}
convergence (see section
4
).

d\ell_{1},...,d\ell_{k}
,
M(\alpha_{max})
depends on the maximal value
\alpha_{max}
of the amplitude factors
\alpha^{j}
of
F_{j}=CP(F_{j-1},\mu_{j},N_{j})
over
C
for all
j\in[\![1,k]\!]
and
\widetilde{K}(\alpha_{max})=2^{k}(2\cosh(\alpha_{max})+1)^{k}
.

Proof.

We have by lemma
3.6
:

\displaystyle\parallel dF-df\parallel_{g,\widetilde{h}}\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\parallel dF_{j+1}-dF_{j}\parallel_{g,\widetilde{h}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\left[O(1/N_{j})+M(\alpha^{j}_{max})\eta_{j}^{\frac{1}{2}}\parallel d\ell_{j}\parallel_{g,\widetilde{h}}(\parallel{\mathbf{n}}_{j}\parallel_{\widetilde{h}}+\parallel dF_{j}\parallel_{g,\widetilde{h}})\right]

Here,
{\mathbf{n}}_{j}
is a normal to
TF_{j}
,
\alpha^{j}_{max}
is the maximum value of the amplitude factor for
F_{j}=CP(F_{j-1},\mu_{j},N_{j})
over
C
and
N_{j}
is taken large enough as in lemma
3.7
. Take now
\alpha_{max}=\max\alpha^{j}_{max}
and let
K=2\cosh(\alpha_{max})+1
. Note that
K
satisfy

K\geq K_{j}(\alpha^{j}_{max})

where
K_{j}(\alpha^{j}_{max})=2\cosh(\alpha^{j}_{max})+1
as in lemma
3.7
. We have

\parallel dF-df\parallel_{g,\widetilde{h}}\leq\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\left[O(1/N_{j})+M(\alpha_{max})\eta_{j}^{\frac{1}{2}}\parallel d\ell_{j}\parallel_{g,\widetilde{h}}(2K)^{j}(\parallel df\parallel_{g,\widetilde{h}}+\parallel{\mathbf{n}}_{0}\parallel_{\widetilde{h}})\right]

Recall that we have
\Delta=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\eta_{j}d\ell_{j}^{2}
, then there exists a constant
c>0
that depends only on
d\ell_{1},...,d\ell_{k}
and by taking each
N_{j}
large enough we get

\displaystyle\parallel dF-df\parallel_{g,\widetilde{h}}\leq\varepsilon+M(\alpha_{max})c\parallel\Delta\parallel^{\frac{1}{2}}_{g,\widetilde{h}}(\parallel df\parallel_{g,\widetilde{h}}+\parallel{\mathbf{n}}_{0}\parallel_{\widetilde{h}})\widetilde{K}(\alpha_{max})

where
\widetilde{K}(\alpha_{max})=2^{k}(2\cosh(\alpha_{max})+1)^{k}
.
∎

4.
The
C^{1}
embedding

We are now ready to apply the Nash-Kuiper process, recall that
f:(C,g)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{2,1}
is a long spacelike embedding, meaning that the isometric default
\Delta=f^{*}h-g=:\sum_{1}^{k}\eta_{j}d\ell_{j}^{2}
is positive semi-definite (we recall that
h
is the Lorentzian metric on the Minkowski space). The goal is to construct a
C^{1}
isometric embedding
f_{\infty}:(C,g)\rightarrow(\mathbb{R}^{2,1},h)
. The construction goes as follows:

•

First, notice that by lemma
3.5
, if the isometric default converges to
0
, then the amplitude factor
\alpha
converges to
0
too. In the Nash-Kuiper process (see section
2
), the isometric default is getting smaller in each step. Hence, we can choose
\alpha_{max}
from the beginning such that the amplitude factor is bounded above by
\alpha_{max}
for any
n
.

•

Up to taking large corrugation numbers at each step and since
C
is compact, we can choose the
1
-forms
d\ell_{1},...,d\ell_{k}
once for all (see lemma
29.3.1
in
[
2
]
).

•

We consider a sequence
(g_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of Riemannian metrics defined by
g_{n}=g+\delta_{n}\Delta
, where
(\delta_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a decreasing sequence of positive numbers converging to
0
such that:

\sum\sqrt{\delta_{n-1}-\delta_{n}}(2\widetilde{K})^{n}<\infty

where
\widetilde{K}=2^{k}(2\cosh(\alpha_{max})+1)^{k}
, where
k
is the number of
1
-forms
d\ell_{1},...,d\ell_{k}
in the previous point.

•

For any fixed
\varepsilon>0
, we introduce a sequence
a_{n}
of positive numbers such that

\sum a_{n}<\varepsilon

Starting from
f_{0}
, we build by corrugation process a sequence
(f_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of embeddings such that:

(1)

\parallel f_{n}^{*}h-g_{n}\parallel_{g,\widetilde{h}}\leq\parallel g_{n+1}-g_{n}\parallel_{g,\widetilde{h}}
,

(2)

\parallel f_{n}-f_{n-1}\parallel_{\widetilde{h}}\leq a_{n}
,

(3)

\parallel df_{n}-df_{n-1}\parallel_{\widetilde{h}}\leq a_{n}+T\parallel g_{n}-g_{n-1}\parallel^{\frac{1}{2}}_{g,\widetilde{h}}(2\widetilde{K})^{n}\;\,
,

where
T
is a constant that does not depend on
n
. By the construction
3.2
, we only need to take large corrugation numbers at each step to satisfy the first two conditions. Regarding the third condition, notice that condition one ensures that
f_{n-1}
is long for the metric
g_{n}
, applying
3.2
on
f_{n-1}
we can build an almost isometric embedding
f_{n}
for the metric
g_{n}
. We have by proposition
3.8
:

\displaystyle\parallel df_{n}-df_{n-1}\parallel_{\widetilde{h}}\leq a_{n}+M(\alpha_{max})c\parallel g_{n}-f_{n-1}^{*}h\parallel^{\frac{1}{2}}_{g,\widetilde{h}}\widetilde{K}(\parallel df_{n-1}\parallel_{g,\widetilde{h}}+\parallel{\mathbf{n}}_{f_{n-1}}\parallel_{\widetilde{h}})

where by
{\mathbf{n}}_{f_{n-1}}
we mean the normal to
f_{n-1}(C)
with respect to the Lorentzian metric
h
. Using lemma
3.7
successively, we get

\displaystyle\parallel df_{n}-df_{n-1}\parallel_{\widetilde{h}}\leq

\displaystyle a_{n}+M(\alpha_{max})c\parallel g_{n}-f_{n-1}^{*}h\parallel^{\frac{1}{2}}_{g,\widetilde{h}}(2\widetilde{K})^{n}(\parallel df_{0}\parallel_{g,\widetilde{h}}+\parallel{\mathbf{n}}_{f_{0}}\parallel_{\widetilde{h}})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle a_{n}+2M(\alpha_{max})c\parallel g_{n}-g_{n-1}\parallel^{\frac{1}{2}}_{g,\widetilde{h}}(2\widetilde{K})^{n}(\parallel df_{0}\parallel_{g,\widetilde{h}}+\parallel{\mathbf{n}}_{f_{0}}\parallel_{\widetilde{h}})

the last inequality comes from combining the first condition with the triangle inequality:

\displaystyle\parallel f_{n-1}^{*}h-g_{n}\parallel^{\frac{1}{2}}_{g,\widetilde{h}}\leq

\displaystyle\parallel f_{n-1}^{*}h-g_{n-1}\parallel^{\frac{1}{2}}_{g,\widetilde{h}}+\parallel g_{n-1}-g_{n}\parallel^{\frac{1}{2}}_{g,\widetilde{h}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\parallel g_{n}-g_{n-1}\parallel_{g,\widetilde{h}}^{\frac{1}{2}}

We take the constant
T:=2M(\alpha_{max})c(\parallel df_{0}\parallel_{g,\widetilde{h}}+\parallel{\mathbf{n}}_{f_{0}}\parallel_{\widetilde{h}})
.

By construction, the sequence
(f_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
will converge to a
C^{1}
embedding
f_{\infty}
which is isometric between
(C,g)
and
(W,h)
. Indeed, condition
(2)
implies that the sequence
(f_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
converges to a continuous map
f_{\infty}
and such that

\parallel f_{0}-f_{\infty}\parallel_{\widetilde{h}}\leq\varepsilon

Moreover, combining condition
(3)
and the fact that

\sum\parallel g_{n}-g_{n-1}\parallel^{\frac{1}{2}}_{g,\widetilde{h}}(2\widetilde{K})^{n}=\parallel\Delta\parallel_{g,\widetilde{h}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\sum\sqrt{\delta_{n-1}-\delta_{n}}(2\widetilde{K})^{n}<\infty

ensures that
f_{\infty}
is
C^{1}
. We conclude by condition
(1)
that
f_{\infty}^{*}h=g
.

5.
The Global construction

The constructions presented in section
3
are defined locally and can be extended globally by the relative property of the corrugation process. We give here the global construction for completeness.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
(V,g)
be a closed Riemannian surface and
(W,h)
a Lorentzian manifold. For any long spacelike embedding
f:V\rightarrow W
(i.e,
\Delta=f^{*}h-g
is semi-definite positive), and any
\varepsilon>0
, there exists a spacelike embedding
F:V\rightarrow W
such that:

\left\|F^{*}h-g\right\|_{C^{0}}\leq\varepsilon

Proof.

The construction follows the approach of Nash
[
9
]
and relies on the relative property of Theilllière’s corrugation process
[
10
]
:

•

We choose a finite set
\phi_{i}:U_{i}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}\rightarrow V_{i}\subset V
of local parametrizations of
V
and a partition of unity
\{\rho_{i}\}
subordinated to it (
supp(\rho_{i})\subset U_{i}
) for all
i\in[\![1,m]\!]
.

•

We identify the space of inner products of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
(given by symmetric positive definite matrices
\begin{bmatrix}E&F\\
F&G\\
\end{bmatrix}
) with the interior of the cone
S^{+}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
defined by the conditions:

\begin{cases}EG-F^{2}>0,\\
E,G>0\end{cases}

•

We note
\Delta_{i}=:\phi_{i}^{*}\Delta_{|\phi_{i}(supp(\rho_{i}))}
so that we have

\Delta=\sum_{i}\rho_{i}\Delta_{i}.

Since
supp(\rho_{i})
is compact and
\Delta_{i}:supp(\rho_{i})\rightarrow S^{+}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
is continuous, we can find
p_{i}
squares of linear forms
\ell_{i,1}\otimes\ell_{i,1},\ell_{i,2}\otimes\ell_{i,2},...,\ell_{i,p_{i}}\otimes\ell_{i,p_{i}}
such that

\Delta_{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{{p_{i}}}\eta_{i,j}\ell_{i,j}\otimes\ell_{i,j}

where each
\eta_{i,j}:supp(\rho_{i})\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{+}
is a smooth function. For a proof of this fact, check lemma
29.3.1
in
[
2
]
.

•

Working chart by chart, we will build a sequence of intermediary maps
f=F_{0},F_{1},...,F_{m}
defined on
V
such that

\left\|F_{i}^{*}h-(f^{*}h-\sum_{k=1}^{i}\rho_{j}\Delta_{j})\right\|_{C^{0}}\leq\varepsilon

The map
F_{m}
will be
\varepsilon
-isometric for the metric
g
provided the corrugation numbers are large enough in each step.

•

Each map
F_{i}
is built by applying
p_{i}
successive corrugation process on
F_{i-1}
. We will use proposition
3.1
in the chart
U_{i}
to build a sequence of maps
F_{i-1}=F_{i}^{0},F_{i}^{1},...,F_{i}^{p_{i}}=F_{i}
, where

F_{i}^{j}=CP(F_{i}^{j-1},\mu_{i}^{j},N_{i,j})

and
\mu_{i}^{j}=F_{i}^{j-1*}h-\rho_{i}\eta_{i,j}\ell_{i,j}\otimes\ell_{i,j}
. By remark
3.4
, the maps are well defined on
\Sigma
, in fact, they all coincide outside
\phi_{i}(supp(\rho_{i}))
since the metric
\mu_{i}^{j}
is equal to
F_{i-1}^{*}h
there.

Even though we introduced the construction when
W
is the Minkowski space, the construction is still well defined for general
W
and the conclusion remains the same. Indeed, in the coordinate free definition
2.1
of the corrugation process, If we take
U
to be compact and such that
f^{*}TW
is trivial, then we can find a smaller neighborhood of the zero section of
f^{*}TW\rightarrow U
where the exponential map is well defined. Since the loop family in definition
2.1

\Gamma(x,s)
is bounded, we can choose
N
large enough so that
\frac{1}{N}\Gamma(x,N\pi(x))
lies inside this neighborhood. Using the same loop family
1
as in proposition
3.1
, we get an
\varepsilon
-isometric embedding.

Using standard arguments of Nash-Kuiper, the construction above can be adapted to prevent self intersections provided, we start with an embedding. Hence, it produces embeddings and not only immersions. Moreover, if the Euler characteristic of
V
is different than
0
, the immersions constructed are embeddings. Indeed, we can choose a tubular neighborhood of
f(V)
which is globally hyperbolic, of the form
V\times]-\varepsilon,\varepsilon[
, where
f(V)
is identified
with level
0
. By
C^{0}
-closeness, we can construct the immersion
F
to lie inside
V\times]-\varepsilon,\varepsilon[
. Now, notice that
F
composed with the projection on the first factor is a covering map (since
F
is spacelike), and since
V
is compact and its Euler characteristic is different from
0
, we conclude that it is a diffeomorphism. Therefore,
F
is an embedding.
∎

6.
Appendix

In this appendix, we prove that the function
\psi
appearing in the proof of lemma
3.6
and defined by:

\psi:\alpha\in[0,+\infty[\mapsto\frac{\frac{\sqrt{2\cosh(\alpha)^{2}-2\varphi(\alpha)}+\sinh(\alpha)}{\varphi(\alpha)}}{\sqrt{1-\frac{1}{\varphi(\alpha)^{2}}}}

is well defined and continuous at zero.

First, notice that:

\displaystyle\psi(\alpha)=

\displaystyle\frac{\sqrt{2\cosh(\alpha)^{2}-2\varphi(\alpha)}+\sinh(\alpha)}{\sqrt{\varphi(\alpha)^{2}-1}}

We note

\psi_{1}(\alpha):=\frac{\cosh(\alpha)^{2}-\varphi(\alpha)}{\varphi(\alpha)^{2}-1}\;\;\;\text{and}\;\;\psi_{2}(\alpha):=\frac{\sinh(\alpha)^{2}}{\varphi(\alpha)^{2}-1}

In the following, we will use L’Hôpital’s rule to prove that
\psi_{1}
and
\psi_{2}
are well defined at
0
. Noting that
\psi=\sqrt{2\psi_{1}}+\sqrt{\psi_{2}}
, we can conclude.

By
5
, we have:

\varphi^{\prime}(\alpha)=\int_{0}^{1}\cos(2\pi s)\sinh(\alpha\cos(2\pi s))\mathrm{d}s

\varphi^{\prime\prime}(\alpha)=\int_{0}^{1}\cos(2\pi s)^{2}\cosh(\alpha\cos(2\pi s))\mathrm{d}s

Since
\cosh(0)^{2}-\varphi(0)=0=\varphi(0)^{2}-1
and both
\cosh(\alpha)^{2}-\varphi(\alpha)
and
\varphi(\alpha)^{2}-1
are differentiable on the right, and
(\varphi(\alpha)^{2}-1)^{\prime}=2\varphi^{\prime}(\alpha)\varphi(\alpha)
is strictly positive near
0
, we can apply L’Hôpital’s rule:

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{\alpha\to 0}\psi_{1}(\alpha)=

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{\alpha\to 0}\frac{(\cosh(\alpha)^{2}-\varphi(\alpha))^{\prime}}{2\varphi^{\prime}(\alpha)\varphi(\alpha)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{\alpha\to 0}\frac{\sinh(2\alpha)-\varphi^{\prime}(\alpha)}{2\varphi^{\prime}(\alpha)\varphi(\alpha)}

Since
\sinh(0)-\varphi^{\prime}(0))=0=2\varphi^{\prime}(0)\varphi(0)
, we need to apply L’Hôpital’s rule a second time, we get:

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{\alpha\to 0}\psi_{1}(\alpha)=

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{\alpha\to 0}\frac{(\sinh(2\alpha)-\varphi^{\prime}(\alpha))^{\prime}}{(2\varphi^{\prime}(\alpha)\varphi(\alpha))^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{\alpha\to 0}\frac{2\cosh(2\alpha)-\varphi^{\prime\prime}(\alpha)}{2\varphi^{\prime\prime}(\alpha)\varphi(\alpha)+2\varphi^{\prime}(\alpha)^{2}}

We have

2\varphi^{\prime\prime}(0)\varphi(0)+2\varphi^{\prime}(0)^{2}=2\int_{0}^{1}\cos(2\pi s)^{2}\mathrm{d}s=1

Hence
\psi_{1}(0)
is well defined.

By the same arguments, we have:

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{\alpha\to 0}\psi_{2}(\alpha)=

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{\alpha\to 0}\frac{\sinh(\alpha)^{2}}{2\varphi^{\prime}(\alpha)\varphi(\alpha)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{\alpha\to 0}\frac{\sinh(2\alpha)}{2\varphi^{\prime}(\alpha)\varphi(\alpha)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{\alpha\to 0}\frac{\sinh^{\prime}(2\alpha)}{(2\varphi^{\prime}(\alpha)\varphi(\alpha))^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{\alpha\to 0}\frac{2\cosh(2\alpha)}{2\varphi^{\prime\prime}(\alpha)\varphi(\alpha)+2\varphi^{\prime}(\alpha)^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2}{2\int_{0}^{1}\cos(2\pi s)^{2}\mathrm{d}s}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2

Therefore, the function
\psi
is well defined and continuous at
0
.

Convergence analysis of online algorithms for vector-valued kernel regression
Michael Griebel, Peter Oswald

Abstract
We consider the problem of approximating the regression function from noisy vector-valued data by an online learning algorithm using an appropriate reproducing kernel Hilbert space (RKHS) as prior. In an online algorithm, i.i.d. samples become available one by one by a random process and are successively processed to build approximations to the regression function. We are interested in the asymptotic performance of such online approximation algorithms and show that the expected squared error in the RKHS norm can be bounded by C 2 ( m + 1 ) − s / ( 2 + s ) C^{2}(m+1)^{-s/(2+s)} , where m m is the current number of processed data, the parameter 0 < s ≤ 1 0<s\leq 1 expresses an additional smoothness assumption on the regression function, and the constant C C depends on the variance of the input noise, the smoothness of the regression function, and other parameters of the algorithm.

\mathbb{E}(\|f(\omega)-y\|_{Y}^{2})=\int_{\Omega\times Y}\|f(\omega)-y\|_{Y}^{2}\,d\mu(\omega,y)\longmapsto\min

(\omega_{m},y_{m}),m=0,\ldots,N-1

The symbol

For the minimization problem (

Since solving the discretized least-squares problem

\frac{1}{N}\sum_{m=0}^{N-1}\|f(\omega_{m})-y_{m}\|^{2}_{Y}\longmapsto\min

H=\{f_{v}(\omega)=R^{\ast}_{\omega}v:\;\|f_{v}\|_{H}:=\|v\|_{V},\;v\in V\}

Detailed definitions and necessary assumptions on the feature map are given in the next section. Obviously, the RKHS

The online algorithms considered in this paper build, starting from an initial guess

By setting

f^{(m+1)}(\omega)=\alpha_{m}(f^{(m)}(\omega)+\mu_{m}K(\omega,\omega_{m})(y_{m}-f^{(m)}(\omega_{m}))),\qquad m=0,1,\ldots,N-1,

(5)

where

\alpha_{m}=\frac{m+1}{m+2},\qquad\mu_{m}=\frac{A}{(m+1)^{t}},\qquad m=0,1,\ldots,

(6)

(7)

2
Setting and main result

Let us first introduce our approach to vector-valued RKHS
H\subset L^{2}_{\rho}(\Omega,Y)
, where
\Omega
is a compact metric space with Borel probability measure
\rho
and
Y
is a separable Hilbert space. Note that such a
RKHS
H
can be implicitly introduced by a family
\mathbf{R}=\{R_{\omega}\}_{\omega\in\Omega}
of bounded linear operators
R_{\omega}:\,Y\to V
, where
V
is another separable Hilbert space (we will tactitly assume that
V
is infinite-dimensional). More precisely, we introduce the notation

f_{v}(\omega):=R_{\omega}^{\ast}v,\quad\omega\in\Omega,\quad v\in V,

and set

H:=\{f_{v}:\;v\in V,\quad\|f_{v}\|_{H}:=\|v\|_{V}\}.

Under the simplifying conditions

\|R^{\ast}_{\omega}v\|_{Y}=0\quad\rho-\mbox{a.e.}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad v=0\qquad\forall\;v\in V,

(8)

and

f_{v}(\omega)\in C(\Omega,Y)\qquad\forall\,v\in V,

(9)

we then have
H\subset C(\Omega,Y)\subset L^{2}_{\rho}(\Omega,Y)
and
f_{v}=0
in

L^{2}_{\rho}(\Omega,Y)
implies
v=0
.
By definition,
H
and
V
are isometric, which allows us to easily switch between
H
and
V
in the sequel.

The condition (
9
) is the continuity of the operator family
\mathbf{R}
in the strong operator topology and ensures Bochner integrability of functions from
\Omega
into
Y
and
V
, respectively, which appear in the formulas
below. Because of the assumed compactness of
\Omega
, it also implies

\|R_{\omega}\|_{Y\to V}^{2}\leq\Lambda<\infty,\qquad\omega\in\Omega,

(10)

with some
\Lambda<\infty
. Another consequence is that the operator kernel

K(\omega,\theta):=R_{\omega}^{\ast}R_{\theta}:\;Y\to Y,\qquad\omega,\theta\in\Omega,

associated with the vector-valued RKHS
H
is a Mercer kernel. The condition
(
10
) is equivalent to the uniform boundedness

\|K(\omega,\theta)\|_{Y}\leq\Lambda,\qquad\omega,\theta\in\Omega,

(11)

of the operator kernel
K
. Furthermore, (
10
) is equivalent to the uniform boundedness
of the operator family

P_{\omega}:=R_{\omega}R_{\omega}^{\ast}:\,V\to V,\qquad\omega\in\Omega,

in
V
,
i.e.,

\|P_{\omega}\|_{V}\leq\Lambda,\qquad\omega\in\Omega.

(12)

For fixed
V
and
\mathbf{R}
satisfying the above properties, instead of solving the minimization problem (
1
) on
L^{2}_{\rho}(\Omega,Y)
,
one now searches for the minimizer
u\in V
of (
7
).
The solution
u
of this quadratic minimization problem on
V
, if it exists, must satisfy the
necessary condition

\mathbb{E}((R^{\ast}_{\omega}u-y,R^{\ast}_{\omega}w)_{Y})=\mathbb{E}((P_{\omega}u-R_{\omega}y,w)_{V})=0\qquad\forall\;w\in V.

This condition corresponds to the linear operator equation

P_{\rho}u=\mathbb{E}(R_{\omega}y),\qquad P_{\rho}:=\mathbb{E}(P_{\omega})=\mathbb{E}(R_{\omega}R^{\ast}_{\omega}),

(13)

in
V
. Note that in general we cannot expect that
f_{u}=f_{\mu}
, since the closure
W

of
H
in
L^{2}_{\rho}(\Omega,Y)
does not necessarily coincide with
L^{2}(\Omega,Y)
.

The operator
P_{\rho}:\,V\to V
defined in (
13
), which plays the role of a covariance operator, is bounded and symmetric positive definite.
Indeed,

\displaystyle(P_{\rho}v,w)_{V}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega\times Y}(P_{\omega}v,w)_{V}\,d\mu(\omega,y)=\int_{\Omega}(P_{\omega}v,w)_{V}\,d\rho(\omega)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}(R^{\ast}_{\omega}v,R^{\ast}_{\omega}w)_{Y}\,d\rho(\omega)=(f_{v},f_{w})_{L^{2}(\Omega,Y)}=(v,P_{\rho}w)_{V},\quad v,w\in V,

and setting
w=v
we also have
\mathrm{ker}(P_{\rho})=\{0\}
since

(P_{\rho}v,v)=\|f_{v}\|_{L^{2}_{\rho}(\Omega,V)}>0,\qquad 0\neq v\in V,

by (
8
).
The boundedness of
P_{\rho}:\,V\to V
, together with the estimate

\|P_{\rho}\|_{V}\leq\Lambda,

follows from (
10
). The spectrum of
P_{\rho}
is thus contained in
[0,\Lambda]
. We also assume that
P_{\rho}
is compact. A sufficient condition, which is often satisfied in applications, is the trace class property for
P_{\rho}
which holds in particular if the operators
R_{\omega}
,
\omega\in\Omega
, have uniformly bounded finite rank.

Note that the assumptions on
\Omega
and
\mathbf{R}
can be weakened, see for example
CVT2006
, and that the compactness of
P_{\rho}
is used only as a technical simplification. In particular, the compactness assumption allows us to define the scale of smoothness spaces
V_{P_{\rho}}^{s}
,
s\in\mathbb{R}
, generated by
P_{\rho}
using the complete orthonormal system (CONS)
\Psi:=\{\psi_{k}\}
of eigenvectors of
P_{\rho}
and associated eigenvalues
\lambda_{1}\geq\lambda_{2}\geq\ldots>0
with
limit
0
in
V
as follows:
V_{P_{\rho}}^{s}
is the completion of
\mathrm{span}(\Psi)

with respect to the norm

\|\sum_{k}c_{k}\psi_{k}\|_{V_{P_{\rho}}^{s}}=\left(\sum_{k}\lambda_{k}^{-s}c_{k}^{2}\right)^{1/2},

which is well defined on
\mathrm{span}(\Psi)
for any
s
. These spaces will appear in the investigation below.

For our convergence analysis in
V
, we now assume that

\mathbb{E}(R_{\omega}y)\in\mathrm{ran}(P_{\rho}).

(14)

In other words, we assume that (
7
) has a unique solution
u\in V
for which (
13
) holds. For general
y
with
\mathbb{E}(\|y\|_{Y}^{2})<\infty
, only
\mathbb{E}(R_{\omega}y)\in\mathrm{ran}(P^{1/2}_{\rho})
can be established. It can also be proved that the functional
J(v)
in (
7
)
does not have a global minimum if (
14
) is violated (the infimum of
J(v)
is then only approached if

in
V
in a certain way).
It will turn out that the condition (

V

4
)-(

),
see Subsection

For a given prior RKHS
H
induced by the operator family
\mathbf{R}
with associated space

J_{N}(v):=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{m=0}^{N-1}\|f_{v}(\omega_{m})-y_{m}\|_{Y}^{2}+\kappa_{N}\|v\|_{V}^{2}\longmapsto\min

We focus here on the online learning algorithm (
4
)-(
6
) for finding approximations to the minimizer

of (

For this purpose, we define the noise term

\epsilon_{\omega}:=y-f_{u}(\omega)=y-R_{\omega}^{\ast}u,\qquad\omega\in\Omega,

which is a
Y

\Omega\times Y
(to keep the notation short, the dependence of
\epsilon_{\omega}
on

is not explicitly shown).
By (

) we have
\mathbb{E}(\epsilon_{\omega}|\omega)=f_{\mu}(\omega)-f_{u}(\omega)
for any
\omega\in\Omega
. Also, the noise variance

\sigma^{2}_{H}:=\mathbb{E}(\|\epsilon_{\omega}\|_{Y}^{2})=\mathbb{E}(\|y-f_{\mu}\|_{Y}^{2})+\|f_{\mu}-f_{u}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega,Y)}^{2}

(16)

with respect to
f_{u}\in H
is finite, since
\mathbb{E}(\|y\|_{Y}^{2})<\infty

was assumed in the first place. The value of
\sigma_{H}
depends on both the average size of the noise

on
\Omega

Y

L_{\rho}^{2}(\Omega,Y)

from

The online algorithm (
4
)-(
6
) is a particular instance of a randomized Schwarz approximation method associated with
\mathbf{R}
. Its noiseless version, where
y_{m}=R_{\omega_{m}}^{\ast}u
, was studied in
GrOs2018
under the assumption
u\in V^{s}_{P_{\rho}}
,
0\leq s\leq 1
, where
\alpha_{m}
was as in (
6
) but
\mu_{m}
was determined by a steepest descent rule. Our goal in this paper is to derive convergence results for the expected squared error
\mathbb{E}(\|u-u^{(m)}\|^{2}_{V})
,
m=1,2,\ldots
.
As expected, such estimates again require additional smoothness assumptions on
u
in the form
u\in V_{P_{\rho}}^{s}
with
0<s\leq 1
. However, in contrast to the noiseless case
GrOs2018
, they include a dependence on the noise variance
\sigma_{H}^{2}
in addition to the dependence on the initial error and the smoothness of
u
. The prize of convergence
is a certain decay of the step-sizes
\mu_{m}\to 0
, as assumed in (
6
), which is typical for
stochastic approximation algorithms. Our main result is as follows:

Theorem 2.1

Let
Y,V
be separable Hilbert spaces,
\Omega
be a compact metric space,
\mu
be a Borel probability measure on
\Omega\times Y
, and
\rho
be the marginal Borel probability measure on
\Omega
induced by
\mu
.
Assume that

\mathbb{E}(\|y\|_{Y}^{2})=\int_{\Omega\times Y}\|y\|_{Y}^{2}\,d\mu<\infty.

For the operator family
\mathbf{R}=\{R_{\omega}\}_{\omega\in\Omega}
, we need the conditions
(
8
) and (
10
). We also assume that the operator

P_{\rho}=\mathbb{E}(R_{\omega}R_{\omega}^{\ast})
is compact.
Finally, we assume (
14
) and that
u\in V_{P_{\rho}}^{s}
for some
0<s\leq 1
.

Consider the online learning algorithm (
4
), where
u^{(0)}\in V
is arbitrary, the parameters
\alpha_{m},\mu_{m}
are given by (
6
) with
t=t_{s}:=(1+s)/(2+s)
and
A=1/(2\Lambda)
, and
the random samples
(\omega_{m},y_{m})
,
m=0,1,\ldots,N\leq\infty
,
are i.i.d. with respect to
\mu
. Then the expected squared error in
V

satisfies

\mathbb{E}(\|u-u^{(m)}\|^{2}_{V})=\mathbb{E}(\|f_{u}-f^{(m)}\|_{H}^{2})\leq C^{2}(m+1)^{-s/(2+s)},\qquad m=1,2,\ldots,N+1,

(17)

where
f^{(m)}=f_{u^{(m)}}
, the noise variance
\sigma_{H}^{2}
is defined in (
16
), and

C^{2}=2\|u-u^{(0)}\|^{2}_{V}+2\|u\|^{2}_{V}+8\Lambda^{s}\|u\|_{V_{P_{\rho}}^{s}}^{2}+\sigma_{H}^{2}/\Lambda.

In this generality, Theorem
2.1
has not yet appeared in the literature, at least to our knowledge. Its proof is given in Section
4
. For the parameter range
0<s\leq 1
, the exponent
-s/(2+s)
in the right-hand side of (
17
) is best possible under the general conditions
stated in Theorem
2.1
.
Estimates of the form (
17
) also hold for arbitrary values

1/2<t<1
and
0<A\leq 1/(2\Lambda)
in (
6
), albeit with non-optimal exponents depending on
t
and different constants
C
varying with
t
and
A
. Without the condition (
14
), which ensures the existence of the minimizer
u\in V
in (
7
), the online method (
4
)-(
6
) diverges in expectation.
Note that convergence estimates for
with respect to the weaker
L^{2}_{\rho}(\Omega,Y)
norm are also of great interest, but cannot be obtained within our framework. We will comment on these issues in the concluding Section
5
.

There is a huge amount of literature devoted to the convergence theory of various versions of
the algorithm (
4
)-(
5
), especially for the
scalar-valued case
Y=\mathbb{R}
.
In particular, it is often considered in the so-called finite horizon case, where

is fixed and the step-sizes
\mu_{m}
are chosen in dependence on

so that expectations such as
\mathbb{E}(\|u-u^{(N)}\|_{V}^{2})
or
\mathbb{E}(\|f_{u}-f_{u^{(N)}}\|_{L^{2}_{\rho}(\Omega,Y)}^{2})

3
Results related to Theorem
2.1

Given the large number of publications on convergence rates for learning algorithms,
we will present only a selection of results focusing on the RKHS setting and online algorithms similar to (
4
)-(
5
). The results we cite are often stated and proved for the scalar-valued case
Y=\mathbb{R}
, although some authors claim that their methods extend to the case of an arbitrary separable Hilbert space
Y
with minor modifications. One of the first papers on the vector-valued case is
CaVi2007
, where the authors provide upper bounds in probability for the

L^{2}_{\rho}(\Omega,Y)
error of
f_{u_{N}}

if
N\to\infty
and
\kappa_{N}\to 0
, where
u_{N}
is the solution of (
15
).
These bounds depend in a specific way on the smoothness of
u\in V_{P_{\rho}}^{s}
,
0\leq s\leq 1
, and on the spectral properties of
P_{\rho}
. Note that in
CaVi2007
and in many other papers stronger assumptions on the compactness of
P_{\rho}
are made than we do, and that bounds in probability do not automatically imply bounds in expectation. Moreover, the error measured in the
L^{2}_{\rho}(\Omega,Y)
norm is with respect to
f_{u_{N}}

and not with respect to approximations such as
f_{u^{(m)}}
,
m\leq N
, which are produced by a special algorithm comparable to (
5
).

In
TaYa2014
, the authors provide estimates in probability for an
algorithm similar to (
4
)-(
5
) for the scalar-valued case
Y=\mathbb{R}
. They cover both, convergence in
L^{2}_{\rho}(\Omega,\mathbb{R})
and
H
norms. There, the main additional assumption needed for the application of certain results from martingale theory is that, for some constant
M_{\rho}<\infty
, the random variable
y
satisfies

|y|\leq M_{\rho}

a.e. on the support of
\rho
. If
u^{(0)}=0
(as assumed in
TaYa2014
), then
this assumption implies bounds for
\|u-u^{(0)}\|_{V}=\|u\|_{V}

and
\sigma
with constants that depend on

M_{\rho}
. Up to the specification of constants and using the notation of this paper, the convergence result for the
H
norm stated in
(
TaYa2014
, Theorem B)
is as follows: Consider the online algorithm (
4
) with starting value
u^{(0)}=0
and parameters

\alpha_{m}=\frac{m+m_{0}-1}{m+m_{0}},\qquad\alpha_{m}\mu_{m}=\frac{A}{(m+m_{0})^{(s+1)/(s+2)}},\qquad m=0,1,\ldots,

for some (sufficiently large)
m_{0}
and suitable
A
. Then, if
u\in V_{P_{\rho}}^{s}
for some

0<s\leq 2
, we have

\mathbb{P}\left(\|u-u^{(m)}\|_{V}^{2}\leq\frac{C}{(m+m_{0})^{s/(s+2)}}\right)\geq 1-\delta,\qquad 0<\delta<1,\quad m=0,1,\ldots,

for some constant

C=C(M_{\rho},\|u\|_{V_{P_{\rho}}^{s}},m_{0},s,\Lambda,\log(2/\delta))<\infty
.
Here
V=H
is an RKHS of functions
u:\,\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
generated by some scalar-valued Mercer kernel
K:\,\Omega\times\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
and
\Lambda=\max_{\omega\in\Omega}K(\omega,\omega)
. The associated maps
R_{\omega}
are given by
R_{\omega}y=yK(\omega,\cdot)
,
y\in\mathbb{R}
.
Consequently,
R_{\omega}^{\ast}u=u(\omega)
,
\omega\in\Omega
, corresponds to function evaluation.
So, for
0<s\leq 1
, we get the same rate as in our Theorem
2.1

which was, however, based on the expectation of the
squared error in
V=H
in the more general vector-valued case. What our rather elementary method does not provide is a result for the case
1<s\leq 2
and for

L^{2}_{\rho}(\Omega,Y)
convergence. For the latter situation,
(
TaYa2014
, Theorem C)
gives the better estimate

\mathbb{P}\left(\|u-u^{(m)}\|_{L^{2}_{\rho}(\Omega,\mathbb{R})}^{2}\leq\frac{\bar{C}}{(m+m_{0})^{(s+1)/(s+2)}}\right)\geq 1-\delta,\quad 0<\delta<1,\quad m=0,1,\ldots,

under the same assumptions, but with a different constant

\bar{C}=\bar{C}(M_{\rho},\|u\|_{V_{P_{\rho}}^{s}},m_{0},s,\Lambda,\log(2/\delta))<\infty.

This is almost matching the lower estimates for kernel learning derived in
CaVi2007
for classes of instances where the spectrum of
P_{\rho}
has a
prescribed decay of the form
\lambda_{k}\asymp k^{-b}
for some
b>1
.
Note that for Mercer kernels, the operator

P_{\rho}:\;u\in H\quad\longmapsto\quad(P_{\rho}u)(\cdot)=\int_{\Omega}K(\cdot,\theta)u(\theta)\,d\rho(\theta)

is trace class, while in our Theorem
2.1
no stronger decay of eigenvalues is assumed.

Estimates in expectation close to our result have also been obtained for slightly different settings. For example, in
YiPo2008
both, the so-called
regularized
(
\alpha_{m}<1
) and the
unregularized
online algorithm
(
\alpha_{m}=1
) were analyzed in the scalar-valued case
Y=\mathbb{R}
under assumptions similar to ours for
L^{2}_{\rho}(\Omega,\mathbb{R})
and
V=H
convergence. We quote only the result for convergence in the RKHS
V=H
. It concerns the so-called
finite horizon
case of the unregularized online algorithm (
4
) with
\alpha_{m}=1
, where one fixes
N<\infty
, chooses a constant step-size
\mu_{m}=\mu
,

m=0,\ldots,N-1
, which depends on
N
, stops the iteration at
m=N
and asks
for a good estimate of the expectation of
\mathbb{E}(\|u-u^{(N)}\|^{2}_{V})
for the final iterate only.
Up to the specification of constants, Theorem 6 in
YiPo2008
states that, under the condition
u\in V^{s}_{P_{\rho}}
,
s>0
, one can obtain the bound

\mathbb{E}(\|u-u^{(N)}\|^{2}_{V})=\mathrm{O}(N^{-s/(s+2)}),\qquad N\to\infty,

if one sets
\mu_{N}=cN^{-(s+1)/(s+2)}
with a properly adjusted value of
c
. Note that
s>0
is arbitrary with the exponent approaching
-1
if the smoothness parameter
s
tends to
\infty
, while our result gives no improvement for
s>1
. The drawback of the finite horizon case is that the estimate concerns only a fixed iterate
u^{(N)}
with an
N
to be decided beforehand. In a sense, this can be seen as building an approximation to the solution
u_{N}
of (
15
) with
\kappa_{N}=\mu_{N}
from a single pass over the
N
i.i.d. samples
(\omega_{m},y_{m})
,
m=0,\ldots,N-1
.

In recent years, attention has shifted to obtaining refined rates when
P_{\rho}
possesses faster eigenvalue decay, usually expressed by the property that
P_{\rho}^{\beta}
is trace class for some
\beta<1
or by the slightly weaker assumption

\lambda_{k}=\mathrm{O}(k^{-1/\beta}),\qquad k\to\infty,

(18)

on the eigenvalues of the covariance operator
P_{\rho}
.
Bounds that require knowledge of
\beta<1
are sometimes called capacity dependent, so our bounds in Theorem
2.1
as well as the cited results from
TaYa2014
;
YiPo2008
are capacity independent. Capacity dependent convergence rates for the expected squared error for the online algorithm
(
4
)-(
5
) have been obtained, among others, in
DiBa2016
;
DFB2017
;
GuSh2019
;
GLZ2022
, again in the scalar-valued case
Y=\mathbb{R}
and with various parameter settings, including unregularized and finite horizon versions. In
DiBa2016
, rates for

\mathbb{E}(\|u-\bar{u}^{(m)}\|_{L^{2}_{\rho}(\Omega,\mathbb{R})}^{2})
have been established,
where

\bar{u}^{(m)}=\frac{1}{m+1}\sum_{k=0}^{m}u^{(k)},\qquad m=0,1,\ldots,

(19)

is the sequence of averages associated with the sequence
u^{(m)}
,
m=0,1,\ldots
, obtained by the unregularized iteration (
4
) with
\alpha_{m}=1
and
u^{(0)}=0
.
That averaging has a similar effect as
regularization with
\alpha_{m}=(m+1)/(m+2)
in (
4
) considered in Theorem
2.1
can be guessed if
one observes that

\bar{u}^{(m+1)}=\frac{m+1}{m+2}\bar{u}^{(m)}+\frac{1}{m+2}{u}^{(m+1)},

where
u^{(m+1)}=u^{(m)}+\mu_{m}(y_{m}-R_{\omega_{m}}u^{(m)})
, and compares with
the regularized iteration (
4
) with
\alpha_{m}
given in (
6
).
To illustrate the influence of
\beta
, we formulate the following bound, which is a
consequence of
(
DiBa2016
, Corollary 3)
: Under an additional technical assumption on the noise term
\epsilon_{\omega}
, if the condition (
18
) holds for some
0<\beta<1
and

u\in V_{P_{\rho}}^{s}
,
s>-1
, then for suitable choices for the learning rates
\mu_{m}
, we have

\mathbb{E}(\|u-\bar{u}^{(m)}\|_{L^{2}_{\rho}(\Omega,\mathbb{R})}^{2})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\mathrm{O}((m+1)^{-(s+1)}),&-1<s<-\beta,\\
\mathrm{O}((m+1)^{-(s+1)/(s+1+\beta)}),&-\beta<s<1-\beta,\\
\mathrm{O}((m+1)^{-(1-\beta/2)}),&1-\beta<s.\end{array}\right.

Thus, stronger eigenvalue decay generally implies stronger asymptotic error decay in the
L^{2}_{\rho}(\Omega,\mathbb{R})
norm. In
(
DFB2017
, Section 6)
,
similar rates are obtained in the finite horizon setting for both, the above averaged iterates

\bar{u}^{(N)}
and for
u^{(N)}
produced by a two-step extension
of the one-step iteration (
4
).

In addition to
L^{2}_{\rho}(\Omega,\mathbb{R})
convergence results, the paper
GuSh2019
also provides a capacity dependent convergence estimate in the RKHS norm for the unregularized algorithm (
4
)-(
5
) with parameters
\alpha_{m}=1
and

\mu_{m}=c(m+1)^{-1/2}
. Under the boundedness assumption
|y|\leq M_{\rho}
, Theorem 2 in
GuSh2019
implies
that

\mathbb{E}(\|u-{u}^{(m)}\|^{2}_{V})=\mathrm{O}((m+1)^{-\min(s,1-\beta)/2)}\log^{2}(m+1)),\qquad m=1,2,\ldots,

if
u\in V^{s}_{P_{\rho}}
for some
s>0
,
P_{\rho}^{\beta}
is trace class for some

0<\beta<1
, and
c
is properly adjusted.

Finally, the scalar-valued least-squares regression problem with
Y=\mathbb{R}
and RKHS prior space
H
can also be cast as linear regression
problem in
V=H
. This was done in
DFB2017
;
GLZ2022
. More abstractly,
given a
\mu
-distributed random variable
(\xi_{\omega},y)\in V\times\mathbb{R}
on
\Omega\times\mathbb{R}
, the task is to find approximations to the minimizer
u\in V
of the problem

\mathbb{E}(|(\xi_{\omega},v)-y|^{2})\longmapsto\min,\qquad v\in V,

(20)

from i.i.d. samples
(\xi_{\omega_{i}},y_{i})
. If
V=H
is the RKHS that regularizes the scalar-valued least-squares regression problem on
\Omega\times\mathbb{R}
, then
the canonical choice is
\xi_{\omega}=K(\omega,\cdot)
. In
GLZ2022
, for the
iteration

u^{(m+1)}=u^{(m)}+\mu_{m}(y_{m}-(\xi_{\omega_{m}},u^{(m)}))\xi_{\omega_{m}},\qquad m=0,1,\ldots,

weak convergence in
V
is studied by deriving estimates for quantities such as
\mathbb{E}((v,u-u^{(m)})^{2})
and
\mathbb{E}((\xi_{\omega^{\prime}},u-u^{(m)})^{2})

under some simplifying assumptions on the noise and the normalization
\|\xi_{\omega}\|=1
.
Note that this iteration is nothing but the unregularized iteration (
4
) with
\alpha_{m}=1
since
(\xi_{\omega_{m}},u^{(m)})=u^{(m)}(\omega_{m})
in this case.
In the learning application, the assumption
\|\xi_{\omega}\|=1
means
K(\omega,\omega)=1
.
Moreover, in this case

\mathbb{E}((\xi_{\omega^{\prime}},u-u^{(m)})^{2})=\mathbb{E}(\|u-u^{(m)}\|_{L^{2}_{\rho}(\Omega,\mathbb{R})}),

since the expectation on the left, in addition to the i.i.d. samples
(\xi_{\omega_{k}},y_{k})
,
k=0,\ldots,m-1
, is also taken with respect to the independently
\rho
-distributed random variable
\xi_{\omega^{\prime}}
. This is related to learning rates
in the
L^{2}_{\rho}(\Omega,\mathbb{R})
norm. The estimates for
\mathbb{E}((\xi_{\omega^{\prime}},u-u^{(m)})^{2})
given in
GLZ2022
concern both, the
finite horizon and the online setting and again depend on the parameters

s\geq 0
(smoothness of
u
) and
0<\beta\leq 1
(capacity assumption on
P_{\rho}
). For the estimates of
\mathbb{E}((v,u-u^{(m)})^{2})
, the smoothness
s^{\prime}\geq 0
of the fixed element
v\in V^{s^{\prime}}_{P_{\rho}}
is traded against the smoothness
s\geq 0
of
u\in V^{s}_{P_{\rho}}
. See
GLZ2022
for details.

4
Proof of Theorem
2.1

In this subsection we will use the notation and assumptions outlined above, with the only change that the scalar product in
V
is simply denoted by
(\cdot,\cdot)
and the associated norm
\|\cdot\|_{V}
is accordingly denoted by
\|\cdot\|
. We also set
e^{(m)}:=u-u^{(m)}
.
We will prove an estimate of the form

\mathbb{E}(\|e^{(m)}\|^{2})=\mathrm{O}((m+1)^{-s/(2+s)}),\qquad m\to\infty,

(21)

under the assumption
u\in V_{P_{\rho}}^{s}
,
0<s\leq 1
, if the parameters
A
and
t

in (
6
) are chosen accordingly. The precise statement and the dependence
of the constant in (
21
) on the initial error, the noise variance and the smoothness assumption
are given in the formulation of Theorem
2.1
.

From (
4
) and
y_{m}=R_{\omega_{m}}^{\ast}u+\epsilon_{\omega_{m}}
we derive the error representation

e^{(m+1)}=\underbrace{\alpha_{m}(e^{(m)}-\mu_{m}P_{\omega_{m}}e^{(m)})+\bar{\alpha}_{m}u}_{\bar{e}^{(m+1)}:=}-\alpha_{m}\mu_{m}R_{\omega_{m}}\epsilon_{\omega_{m}},

where
\bar{\alpha}_{m}:=1-\alpha_{m}=(m+2)^{-1}
, compare also
6
. The first term
\bar{e}^{(m+1)}
corresponds to the noiseless case considered in
GrOs2018
, while the remainder term is the noise contribution.
Thus,

\|e^{(m+1)}\|^{2}=\|\bar{e}^{(m+1)}\|^{2}-2\alpha_{m}\mu_{m}(R_{\omega_{m}}\epsilon_{\omega_{m}},\bar{e}^{(m+1)})+\alpha_{m}^{2}\mu_{m}^{2}\|R_{\omega_{m}}\epsilon_{\omega_{m}}\|^{2}.

(22)

We now estimate the conditional expectation with respect to the given
u^{(m)}
, separately for the three terms in (
22
). Here and in the following we denote this conditional expectation by
\mathbb{E}^{\prime}
.
For the third term, by (
10
) and the definition (
16
) of the noise variance
\sigma_{H}^{2}
, we have

\mathbb{E}^{\prime}(\|R_{\omega_{m}}\epsilon_{\omega_{m}}\|^{2})\leq\Lambda\mathbb{E}(\|\epsilon_{\omega}\|_{Y}^{2})=\Lambda\sigma_{H}^{2}.

(23)

For the second term, we need

\mathbb{E}((R_{\omega}\epsilon_{\omega},w))=\mathbb{E}((y-R_{\omega}^{\ast}u,R_{\omega}^{\ast}w)_{Y})=0\qquad\forall\;w\in V.

This follows directly from the fact that
u\in V
is the minimizer of the problem (
7
). Thus, by setting
w=\alpha_{m}e^{(m)}+\bar{\alpha}_{m}u
, we get

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{\prime}(-2\alpha_{m}\mu_{m}(R_{\omega_{m}}\epsilon_{\omega_{m}},\bar{e}^{(m+1)}))

\displaystyle\qquad\quad=2\alpha_{m}\mu_{m}(\alpha_{m}\mu_{m}\mathbb{E}^{\prime}((R_{\omega_{m}}\epsilon_{\omega_{m}},P_{\omega_{m}}e^{(m)}))-\mathbb{E}^{\prime}((R_{\omega_{m}}\epsilon_{\omega_{m}},w)))

\displaystyle\qquad\quad=2\alpha_{m}^{2}\mu_{m}^{2}\mathbb{E}^{\prime}((R_{\omega_{m}}\epsilon_{\omega_{m}},P_{\omega_{m}}e^{(m)}))

\displaystyle\qquad\quad\leq\alpha_{m}^{2}\mu_{m}^{2}(\mathbb{E}^{\prime}(\|R_{\omega_{m}}\epsilon_{\omega_{m}}\|^{2})+\mathbb{E}^{\prime}(\|P_{\omega_{m}}e^{(m)}\|^{2})).

Here, the first term is estimated by (
23
). For the second term, we substitute
the upper bound

\mathbb{E}^{\prime}(\|P_{\omega_{m}}e^{(m)}\|^{2})\leq\Lambda\mathbb{E}^{\prime}((P_{\omega_{m}}e^{(m)},e^{(m)}))=\Lambda(P_{\rho}e^{(m)},e^{(m)}),

(24)

which follows from (
10
) and the definition of
P_{\rho}
. Together this gives

\mathbb{E}^{\prime}(-2\alpha_{m}\mu_{m}(R_{\omega_{m}}\epsilon_{\omega_{m}},\bar{e}^{(m+1)}))\leq\Lambda\alpha_{m}^{2}\mu_{m}^{2}(\sigma_{H}^{2}+(P_{\rho}e^{(m)},e^{(m)}))

(25)

for the second term in (
22
).

To estimate the first term
\mathbb{E}^{\prime}(\|\bar{e}^{(m+1)}\|^{2})
, we modify the arguments from
GrOs2018
, where the case
\epsilon_{m}=0
was treated. We use the error decomposition

\displaystyle\|\bar{e}^{(m+1)}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bar{\alpha}_{m}^{2}\|u\|^{2}+2\alpha_{m}\bar{\alpha}_{m}(u,e^{(m)}-\mu_{m}P_{\omega_{m}}e^{(m)}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad+\,\alpha_{m}^{2}(\|e^{(m)}\|^{2}-2\mu_{m}(e^{(m)},P_{\omega_{m}}e^{(m)}))+\mu_{m}^{2}\|P_{\omega_{m}}e^{(m)}\|^{2}).

After taking conditional expectations, we arrive with the definition of
P_{\rho}

and (
24
) at

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{\prime}(\|\bar{e}^{(m+1)}\|^{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bar{\alpha}_{m}^{2}\|u\|^{2}+2\alpha_{m}\bar{\alpha}_{m}(u,e^{(m)}-\mu_{m}P_{\rho}e^{(m)})

\displaystyle\quad+\,\alpha_{m}^{2}(\|e^{(m)}\|^{2}-2\mu_{m}(e^{(m)},P_{\rho}e^{(m)})+\mu_{m}^{2}\mathbb{E}^{\prime}(\|P_{\omega_{m}}e^{(m)}\|^{2}))

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\bar{\alpha}_{m}^{2}\|u\|^{2}+2\alpha_{m}\bar{\alpha}_{m}(u,e^{(m)}-\mu_{m}P_{\rho}e^{(m)})

\displaystyle\quad+\,\alpha_{m}^{2}(\|e^{(m)}\|^{2}-\mu_{m}(2-\Lambda\mu_{m})(e^{(m)},P_{\rho}e^{(m)})).

Next, to estimate the term

(u,e^{(m)}-\mu_{m}P_{\rho}e^{(m)})
,
we take an arbitrary

h=P^{1/2}_{\rho}v\in V_{P_{\rho}}^{1}
, where
v\in V=V_{P_{\rho}}^{0}
and

\|h\|_{V_{P_{\rho}}^{1}}=\|v\|
. This gives us

\displaystyle 2\alpha_{m}\bar{\alpha}_{m}(u,e^{(m)}-\mu_{m}P_{\rho}e^{(m)})

\displaystyle\quad=2\alpha_{m}\bar{\alpha}_{m}((u-h,(I-\mu_{m}P_{\rho})e^{(m)})+(h,(I-\mu_{m}P_{\rho})e^{(m)}))

\displaystyle\quad\leq 2\alpha_{m}\bar{\alpha}_{m}\|u-h\|\|(I-\mu_{m}P_{\rho})e^{(m)}\|+2(\bar{\alpha}_{m}\mu_{m}^{-1/2}(I-\mu_{m}P_{\rho})v,\alpha_{m}\mu_{m}^{1/2}e^{(m)})

\displaystyle\quad\leq 2\alpha_{m}\bar{\alpha}_{m}\|u-h\|\|e^{(m)}\|+\bar{\alpha}_{m}^{2}\mu_{m}^{-1}\|(I-\mu_{m}P_{\rho})v\|^{2}+\alpha_{m}^{2}\mu_{m}\|P_{\rho}^{1/2}e^{(m)}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad\leq 2\alpha_{m}\bar{\alpha}_{m}\|u-h\|\|e^{(m)}\|+\bar{\alpha}_{m}^{2}\mu_{m}^{-1}\|h\|_{V_{P_{\rho}}^{1}}^{2}+\alpha_{m}^{2}\mu_{m}(P_{\rho}e^{(m)},e^{(m)}).

Here we have silently used that
\|(I-\mu_{m}P_{\rho})e^{(m)}\|\leq\|e^{(m)}\|

and similarly

\|(I-\mu_{m}P_{\rho})v\|\leq\|v\|=\|h\|_{V_{P_{\rho}}^{1}},

which holds since
0<\mu_{m}\leq A\leq(2\Lambda)^{-1}
according to (
6
) and the restriction on
A
.
Substitution into the previous inequality yields

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{\prime}(\|\bar{e}^{(m+1)}\|^{2})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\bar{\alpha}_{m}^{2}(\|u\|^{2}+\mu_{m}^{-1}\|h\|_{V_{P_{\rho}}^{1}}^{2})+2\alpha_{m}\bar{\alpha}_{m}\|u-h\|\|e^{(m)}\|

\displaystyle\quad+\,\alpha_{m}^{2}(\|e^{(m)}\|^{2}-\mu_{m}(1-\Lambda\mu_{m})(e^{(m)},P_{\rho}e^{(m)})).

Now, combining this estimate for the conditional expectation of the first term in (
22
) with the bounds (
23
) and (
25
) for the third and second terms, respectively, we get

\mathbb{E}^{\prime}(\|\bar{e}^{(m+1)}\|^{2})\leq\alpha_{m}^{2}(\|e^{(m)}\|^{2}+2\Lambda\sigma_{H}^{2}\mu_{m}^{2})\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad

(26)

\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+\,2\alpha_{m}\bar{\alpha}_{m}\|u-h\|\|e^{(m)}\|+\bar{\alpha}_{m}^{2}(\|u\|^{2}+\mu_{m}^{-1}\|h\|_{V_{P_{\rho}}^{1}}^{2}).

Here the term involving
(e^{(m)},P_{\rho}e^{(m)})\geq 0
has been omitted, since
its forefactor
-\mu_{m}(1-2\Lambda\mu_{m})
is non-positive due to the restriction on

A
in (
6
).

For given

u=\sum_{k}c_{k}\psi_{k}\in V_{P_{\rho}}^{s},\qquad 0<s\leq 1,

in (
26
) we choose

h=\sum_{k:\,\lambda_{k}(m+1)^{b}\geq B}c_{k}\psi_{k}

with some fixed constants
b,B>0
specified below. This gives

\|h\|_{V^{1}_{P_{\rho}}}^{2}=\sum_{k:\,\lambda_{k}(m+1)^{b}\geq B}\lambda_{k}^{-(1-s)}(\lambda_{k}^{-s}c_{k}^{2})\leq B^{-(1-s)}(m+1)^{(1-s)b}\|u\|_{V_{P_{\rho}}^{s}}^{2}

and

\|u-h\|^{2}=\sum_{k:\,\lambda_{k}(m+1)^{b}<B}\lambda_{k}^{s}(\lambda_{k}^{-s}c_{k}^{2})\leq B^{s}(m+1)^{-bs}\|u\|_{V_{P_{\rho}}^{s}}^{2}.

After substitution into (
26
), we get

\mathbb{E}^{\prime}(\|\bar{e}^{(m+1)}\|^{2})\leq\alpha_{m}^{2}(\|e^{(m)}\|^{2}+2\Lambda\sigma_{H}^{2}\mu_{m}^{2})+2\alpha_{m}\bar{\alpha}_{m}B^{s/2}(m+1)^{-bs/2}\|u\|_{V_{P_{\rho}}^{s}}\|e^{(m)}\|

(27)

\qquad\qquad+\,\bar{\alpha}_{m}^{2}(\|u\|^{2}+\mu_{m}^{-1}B^{-(1-s)}(m+1)^{(1-s)b}\|u\|_{V_{P_{\rho}}^{s}}^{2}).

Obviously, if
s=1
, we can set
h=u
which greatly simplifies the
considerations below and leads to a more accurate final estimate, see Section
5.1
.

Next, we switch to full expectations in (
27
) by using the independence assumption for the sampling process and taking into account that

\varepsilon_{m}:=\mathbb{E}(\|e^{(m)}\|^{2})^{1/2}\geq\mathbb{E}(\|e^{(m)}\|).

Together with (
6
) and
\alpha_{m}=(m+1)\bar{\alpha}_{m}
,
this gives

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{m+1}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\alpha_{m}^{2}(\varepsilon_{m}^{2}+2A^{2}\Lambda\sigma_{H}^{2}(m+1)^{-2t})+2\alpha_{m}\bar{\alpha}_{m}B^{s/2}(m+1)^{-bs/2}\|u\|_{V_{P_{\rho}}^{s}}\varepsilon_{m}

\displaystyle\qquad+\,\bar{\alpha}_{m}^{2}(\|u\|^{2}+A^{-1}B^{-(1-s)}(m+1)^{(1-s)b+t}\|u\|_{V_{P_{\rho}}^{s}}^{2})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\alpha_{m}^{2}\varepsilon_{m}^{2}+\bar{\alpha}_{m}^{2}(2A^{2}\Lambda\sigma_{H}^{2}(m+1)^{2-2t}+2B^{s/2}(m+1)^{-bs/2+1}\|u\|_{V_{P_{\rho}}^{s}}\varepsilon_{m}\|e^{(m)}\|

\displaystyle\qquad+\,\|u\|^{2}+A^{-1}B^{-(1-s)}(m+1)^{(1-s)b+t}\|u\|_{V_{P_{\rho}}^{s}}^{2}).

In a final step, we assume for a moment that

\varepsilon_{k}\leq C(k+1)^{-r},\qquad k=0,\ldots,m,

(28)

holds for some constants
C,r>0
. Next, we set

a:=\max(2-2t,-bs/2+1-r,(1-s)b+t)

and

D:=2A^{2}\Lambda\sigma_{H}^{2}+2CB^{s/2}\|u\|_{V_{P_{\rho}}^{s}}+\|u\|^{2}+A^{-1}B^{-(1-s)}\|u\|_{V_{P_{\rho}}^{s}}^{2}.

Since
1/2<t<1
is assumed in (
6
), we have
a>0
.
Then, for
k=0,1,\ldots,m
, the estimate for
\varepsilon_{k+1}
simplifies to

\varepsilon_{k+1}^{2}\leq\alpha_{k}^{2}\varepsilon_{k}^{2}+D\bar{\alpha}_{k}^{2}(k+1)^{a}

or, since
\alpha_{k}^{2}\bar{\alpha}_{k-1}^{2}=\bar{\alpha}_{k}^{2}
, to

d_{k+1}:=\bar{\alpha}_{k}^{-2}\varepsilon_{k+1}^{2}\leq\alpha_{k}^{2}\bar{\alpha}_{k}^{-2}\varepsilon_{k}^{2}+D(k+1)^{a}=d_{k}+D(k+1)^{a}.

By recursion we get

d_{m+1}\leq d_{0}+D\sum_{k=0}^{m}(k+1)^{a}=\varepsilon_{0}^{2}+D\sum_{k=0}^{m}(k+1)^{a}

and finally

\varepsilon_{m+1}^{2}\leq(m+2)^{-2}(\|e^{(0)}\|^{2}+D(m+2)^{a+1})<(\|e^{(0)}\|^{2}+D)(m+2)^{a-1},

since we have
a>0
and

\sum_{k=0}^{m}(k+1)^{a}\leq\int_{1}^{m+2}x^{a}\,dx<(m+2)^{a+1}.

(29)

So (
28
) holds by induction for all
m
if we ensure that

1-a\geq 2r,\qquad\|e^{(0)}\|^{2}+D\leq C^{2}.

(30)

To complete the proof of Theorem
2.1
, it remains to maximize
r
for given
0<s\leq 1
. For this purpose, it is intuitively clear to require

a=1-2r=2-2t=-bs/2+1-r=(1-s)b+t.

This system of equations has the unique solution

t=\frac{1+s}{2+s},\quad b=\frac{1}{2+s},\quad 2r=\frac{s}{2+s},\quad a=\frac{2}{2+s}.

Furthermore, the appropriate value for
C
in (
28
) must satisfy

\|e^{(0)}\|^{2}+\|u\|^{2}+2A^{2}\Lambda\sigma_{H}^{2}+2CB^{s/2}\|u\|_{V_{P_{\rho}}^{s}}+A^{-1}B^{-(1-s)}\|u\|_{V_{P_{\rho}}^{s}}^{2}\leq C^{2}.

With such choices for
t
and
C
, the condition (
30
) is guaranteed, and (
28
) gives the desired bound

\varepsilon_{m}^{2}\leq C^{2}(m+1)^{-s/(s+2)},\qquad m=1,2,\ldots,N-1.

By choosing concrete values for
0<A\leq(2\Lambda)^{-1}
and
B>0
, the constant
C^{2}
can be made more explicit. For example, substituting the upper bound

2CB^{s/2}\|u\|_{V_{P_{\rho}}^{s}}\leq\frac{C^{2}}{2}+2B^{s}\|u\|_{V_{P_{\rho}}^{s}}^{2}

and rearranging the terms shows that

C^{2}=2\left(\|e^{(0)}\|^{2}+\|u\|^{2}+B^{s}(2+(AB)^{-1})\|u\|_{V_{P_{\rho}}^{s}}^{2}+2A^{2}\Lambda\sigma_{H}^{2}\right)

is appropriate. In particular, setting for simplicity
A
to its maximal value
A=(2\Lambda)^{-1}
and
taking
B=\Lambda
gives a more explicit dependence of
C^{2}
on
the assumptions on
\|e^{(0)}\|^{2}
, the noise variance
\sigma_{H}^{2}
, and the smoothness of
u
, namely

C^{2}=2\|e^{(0)}\|^{2}+2\|u\|^{2}+8\Lambda^{s}\|u\|_{V_{P_{\rho}}^{s}}+\sigma_{H}^{2}/\Lambda.

(31)

This is the constant shown in the formulation of Theorem
2.1
. Obviously, varying
A
and
B
changes the trade-off between initial error, noise variance, and smoothness assumptions in
the convergence estimate (
28
). Note also that
B
is not part of the algorithm and can be adjusted to any value.
This concludes the proof of Theorem
2.1
.

Comments on Theorem

In the special case

is simplified as follows: In (

\mathbb{E}(\|e^{(m)}\|^{2})\leq\frac{\|e^{(0)}\|^{2}}{(m+1)^{2}}+\frac{2A^{2}\Lambda\sigma_{H}^{2}}{(m+1)^{2t-1}}+\frac{\|u\|^{2}}{m+1}+\frac{A^{-1}\|u\|_{V_{P_{\rho}}^{1}}^{2}}{(m+1)^{1-t}},

(33)

m=1,2,\ldots
. This estimate shows more clearly the guaranteed error decay with respect to the initial error
\|e^{(0)}\|^{2}
, the noise variance
\sigma_{H}^{2}
, and the norms
\|u\|^{2}
and
\|u\|_{V_{P_{\rho}}^{1}}^{2}
of the solution
u
, respectively, in dependence on
t
. The asymptotically
dominant term is of the form

\mathrm{O}((m+1)^{-\min(2t-1,1-t)})

and is minimized when
t=2/3
. For this value of
t
and with
A=(2\Lambda)^{-1}
we get

\mathbb{E}(\|e^{(m)}\|^{2})\leq\frac{\|e^{(0)}\|^{2}}{(m+1)^{2}}+\frac{\|u\|^{2}}{m+1}+\frac{2\Lambda\|u\|_{V_{P_{\rho}}^{1}}^{2}+(2\Lambda)^{-1}\sigma_{H}^{2}}{(m+1)^{1/3}}.

(34)

Without further assumptions one cannot expect a better error decay rate, see Section
3
and Subsection
5.4
.

Another comment concerns the finite horizon setting, which is often treated instead of a true online method. Here one fixes a finite

N
, chooses a constant learning rate
\mu_{m}=\mu
for
m=0,\ldots,N-1
in dependence on
N
, and only asks for a best possible bound for
\mathbb{E}(\|e^{(N)}\|^{2})
. Our approach easily provides
results for this case as well. We demonstrate this only for
s=1
.
For fixed
\mu_{m}=\mu
, the error recursion for the quantities
d_{m}
now takes the form

d_{m+1}\leq d_{m}+(2A^{2}\Lambda\sigma_{H}^{2}(m+1)^{2}\mu^{2}+\|u\|^{2}+\mu^{-1}\|u\|_{V_{P_{\rho}}^{1}}^{2}),\quad m=0,\ldots,N-1,

and gives

\mathbb{E}(\|e^{(N)}\|^{2})\leq\frac{\|e^{(0)}\|^{2}}{(N+1)^{2}}+2\Lambda\sigma_{H}^{2}\mu^{2}(N+1)+\frac{\|u\|^{2}+\mu^{-1}\|u\|_{V_{P_{\rho}}^{1}}^{2}}{N+1}.

Setting
\mu=(2\Lambda)^{-1}(N+1)^{-2/3}
results in a final estimate for the finite horizon case similar to
(
34
), but only for
m=N
.

There are obvious drawbacks of the whole setting in which Theorem
2.1
is formulated.
First, the assumptions are at most qualitative: Since
\mu
, and thus
\rho
, is usually not at our disposal, we cannot verify the assumption
u\in V_{P_{\rho}}^{s}
, nor assess the value of
\sigma_{H}^{2}
.
Moreover, although the restriction to learning rates
\mu_{m}
of the form (
6
) may not cause problems in view of the results obtained, the choice of optimal values for
t
and
A
is by no means obvious.
A rule for the adaptive choice of
\mu_{m}
that does not require knowledge of the values of
s
and the size of the norms of
u
, but leads to the same quantitative error decay as guaranteed by Theorem
2.1
, would be desirable.

5.2
Difficulties with convergence in
L^{2}_{\rho}(\Omega,Y)

Our result for the vector-valued case concerned convergence in

V
, which is isometric to the RKHS
H
generated by
\mathbf{R}
. What we did not succeed in is extending our methods to obtain better asymptotic convergence rates of
f_{u^{(m)}}\to f_{u}
in the
L^{2}_{\rho}(\Omega,Y)
norm. Under
the assumption (
14
) about the existence of the minimizer
u
in (
7
), error estimates in the
L^{2}_{\rho}(\Omega,Y)
norm require the study of
\mathbb{E}(\|P_{\rho}^{1/2}e^{(m)}\|^{2})=\mathbb{E}((P_{\rho}e^{(m)},e^{(m)}))
instead of
\mathbb{E}(\|e^{(m)}\|^{2})
.
If, in analogy to (
22
), one examines the error decomposition

\displaystyle\|P_{\rho}^{1/2}e^{(m+1)}\|^{2}\leq\|P_{\rho}^{1/2}\bar{e}^{(m+1)}\|^{2}-2\alpha_{m}\mu_{m}(P_{\rho}R_{\omega_{m}}\epsilon_{\omega_{m}},\bar{e}^{(m+1)})+\alpha_{m}^{2}\mu_{m}^{2}\|P_{\rho}^{1/2}R_{\omega_{m}}\epsilon_{\omega_{m}}\|^{2},

then difficulties mostly arise from the first term in the right-hand side. In fact, we have

\displaystyle\|P_{\rho}^{1/2}\bar{e}^{(m+1)}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bar{\alpha}_{m}^{2}\|P_{\rho}^{1/2}u\|^{2}+2\alpha_{m}\bar{\alpha}_{m}(P_{\rho}u,e^{(m)}-\mu_{m}P_{\omega_{m}}e^{(m)})

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\alpha_{m}^{2}(\|P_{\rho}^{1/2}e^{(m)}\|^{2}-2\mu_{m}(P_{\rho}e^{(m)},P_{\omega_{m}}e^{(m)}))+\mu_{m}^{2}\|P_{\rho}^{1/2}P_{\omega_{m}}e^{(m)}\|^{2}).

After taking conditional expectations
\mathbb{E}^{\prime}(\|P_{\rho}^{1/2}\bar{e}^{(m+1)}\|^{2})
, we get a negative term

-2\alpha_{m}^{2}\mu_{m}\|P_{\rho}e^{(m)}\|^{2}

on the right-hand side which must compensate for positive contributions from terms such as

\mathbb{E}^{\prime}(\|P_{\rho}^{1/2}P_{\omega_{m}}e^{(m)}\|^{2}).

Since in general
P_{\rho}
does not commute with the operators
P_{\omega}
, this strategy does not work without additional assumptions.

5.3
A divergence result

If the crucial condition (
14
) in Theorem
2.1
does not hold, i.e., if

g:=\mathbb{E}(R_{\omega}y)=\mathbb{E}(R_{\omega}f_{\mu}(\omega))\not\in\mathrm{ran}(P_{\rho}).

(35)

then the sequence
u^{(m)}
obtained from the online algorithm
(
4
)-(
6
) diverges in expectation to
\infty
in
V
for any choice of the parameters
1/2<t<1
and
0<A\leq(2\Lambda)^{-1}
. This negative result is equivalent to proving

\|\mathbb{E}(u^{(m)})\|^{2}\to\infty,\qquad m\to\infty,

(36)

and shows that (
14
) is essential in Theorem
2.1
. As before, norm and scalar product in
V
are denoted by
\|\cdot\|
and
(\cdot,\cdot)
, respectively.

To establish (
36
), we expand
g
and
u^{(0)}
with respect to the CONS
\Psi
, and derive an inhomogeneous linear recursion for the coefficients of the expected error trajectory
U^{(m)}:=\mathbb{E}(u^{(m)})
. To do this, set

u^{(0)}=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{N}}c_{i}\psi_{i},\qquad g=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{N}}g_{i}\psi_{i},

where
c_{i}=(u^{(0)},\psi_{i})
,
g_{i}=(g,\psi_{i})
, and denote
c_{i}^{(m)}:=(U^{(m)},\psi_{i})
,
i\in\mathbb{N}
. Obviously,
u^{(0)}=U^{(0)}
and thus
c_{i}=c_{i}^{(0)}
. From (
4
) we have

U^{(m+1)}=\alpha_{m}(U^{(m)}+\mu_{m}(g-P_{\rho}U^{(m)}))=\alpha_{m}(I-\mu_{m}P_{\rho})U^{(m)}+\alpha_{m}\mu_{m}g,\quad m\geq 0

which, by iteration using the properties of the regularization parameters
\alpha_{m}=(m+1)/(m+2)
, immediately yields

U^{(m)}=\frac{1}{(m+1)}\left(\prod_{l=0}^{m-1}(I-\mu_{l}P_{\rho})u^{(0)}+\sum_{k=1}^{m}k\mu_{k-1}\prod_{l=k}^{m-1}(I-\mu_{l}P_{\rho})g\right),

m=1,2,…. By linearity of the expectation operator and the fact that
\Psi
consists of the eigenvectors of
P_{\rho}
, we get

c_{i}^{(m)}=\frac{1}{(m+1)}\left(\underbrace{\prod_{l=0}^{m-1}(1-\mu_{l}\lambda_{i})}_{\epsilon_{m}(\lambda_{i})}c_{i}+\underbrace{(\sum_{k=1}^{m}k\mu_{k-1}\prod_{l=k}^{m-1}(1-\mu_{l}\lambda_{i}))}_{d_{m}(\lambda_{i}):=}g_{i}\right),

(37)

m=1,2,\ldots
, separately for each
i\in\mathbb{N}
.
Under the restrictions on the parameters
\mu_{m}
from
(
6
), we have
1/2\leq 1-\mu_{l}\lambda_{i}\leq 1
. This shows that we have

0<\epsilon_{m}(\lambda_{i})\leq 1
for the factor in front of
c_{i}
, which implies that the contribution
of the initial guess
u^{(0)}
can be neglected for
m\to\infty
.

Next, we focus on lower bounds for the factor
d_{m}(\lambda_{i})
in front of
g_{i}

in (
37
). For our purposes it is sufficient to show that, for some

m_{0}\geq 1
depending on
t
,

d_{m}(\lambda)\geq c_{0}(m+1)\lambda^{-1},\qquad c_{1}(m+1)^{t-1}\leq\lambda\leq\Lambda,\qquad m\geq m_{0},

(38)

with constants
c_{0}
,
c_{1}
independent of
\lambda
and
m
. In fact, with (
38
) at hand, we have

\displaystyle\|U^{(m)}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(m+1)^{-2}\sum_{i\in\mathbb{N}}(\epsilon_{m}(\lambda_{i})c_{i}+d_{m}(\lambda_{i})g_{i})^{2}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle(m+1)^{-2}\left(\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i\in\mathbb{N}}d_{m}(\lambda_{i})^{2}g_{i}^{2}-\sum_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\epsilon_{m}(\lambda_{i})^{2}c_{i}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{c_{0}^{2}}{2}\left(\sum_{\lambda_{i}\geq c_{0}(m+1)^{t-1}}\lambda_{i}^{-2}g_{i}^{2}\right)-\frac{\|u^{(0)}\|^{2}}{(m+1)^{2}},

where the elementary inequality
(a+b)^{2}\geq b^{2}/2-a^{2}
,
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
, was used in the first step.
But if
g\not\in\mathrm{ran}(P_{\rho})
, then
\sum_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\lambda_{i}^{-2}g_{i}^{2}=\infty
and the above lower bound also tends to infinity. This proves (
36
).

It remains to show (
38
). Set

\Pi_{k}^{m-1}(\lambda):=\prod_{l=k}^{m-1}(1-\mu_{k}\lambda),\quad k=0,\ldots,m-1,\qquad\Pi_{m}^{m-1}(\lambda)=1.

Since
1/2\leq 1-\mu_{m}\lambda\leq 1
, we have
\log(1-\mu_{m}\lambda)\geq-\log(2)\mu_{m}\lambda

for all
m\geq 0
. This gives

\Pi_{k}^{m-1}(\lambda)\geq 2^{-\lambda\sum_{l=k}^{m-1}\mu_{l}},\qquad k=1,\ldots,m-1.

But for
0<t<1
we have

\displaystyle\sum_{l=k}^{m-1}\mu_{l}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle A\sum_{l=k+1}^{m}l^{-t}\leq\frac{1}{2\Lambda}\int_{k+1/2}^{m+1/2}x^{-t}\,dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(m+\frac{1}{2})^{1-t}-(k+\frac{1}{2})^{1-t}}{2\Lambda(1-t)}\leq C(m+1)^{1-t}-(k+1)^{1-t}),\quad k=1,\ldots,m-1,

where
C
depends on
t
and
\Lambda
. So,

\Pi_{k}^{m-1}(\lambda)\geq 2^{-C\lambda((m+1)^{1-t}-(k+1)^{1-t})},\qquad k=1,\ldots,m,

and, consequently,

d_{m}(\lambda)\geq\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k\in I_{m}(\lambda)}k\mu_{k-1},\qquad I_{m}(\lambda)=\{k\leq m:\;C\lambda((m+1)^{1-t}-(k+1)^{1-t})\leq 1\}.

(39)

Obviously, the cardinality of
I_{m}(\lambda)
is equal to
|I_{m}(\lambda)|=m-k_{0}
, where

k_{0}
is the largest
k\geq 0
not in
I_{m}(\lambda)
, i.e.,

(k_{0}+1)^{1-t}<(m+1)^{1-t}-(C\lambda)^{-1}\leq(k_{0}+2)^{1-t}.

If

(2/C)(m+1)^{t-1}\leq\lambda\leq\Lambda,

then
(k_{0}+2)^{1-t}\geq(m+1)^{1-t}/2
, which implies that, for sufficiently large
m\geq m_{0}
, there exists such a
k_{0}
that satisfies
k_{0}+1\geq c_{2}(m+1)
with some constant
c_{2}>0
, where
m_{0}
and
c_{2}
depend on
t
. Furthermore, by definition of
k_{0}
, we have

\frac{1}{C\lambda}<(m+1)^{1-t}-(k_{0}+1)^{1-t}\leq(1-t)\frac{m-k_{0}}{(k_{0}+1)^{t}}.

Substituting this,
k\geq k_{0}+1\geq c_{2}(m+1)
, and
\mu_{k-1}=Ak^{-t}
into (
39
) we get

\displaystyle d_{m}(\lambda)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{A}{2}(k_{0}+1)^{1-t}(m-k_{0})\geq\frac{A(k_{0}+1)}{2(1-t)C\lambda}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{Ac_{2}}{2(1-t)C}\frac{m+1}{\lambda},\qquad(2/C)(m+1)^{t-1}\leq\lambda\leq\Lambda,\quad m\geq m_{0}.

This proves (
38
) if we set

c_{0}=\frac{Ac_{2}}{2(1-t)C},\qquad c_{1}=\frac{2}{C},

and finishes the argument for (
36
).

5.4
A special case

Now consider the special ”learning” problem of recovering an unknown element

u\in V
from noisy measurements of its coefficients with respect to a CONS
\Psi=\{\psi_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
in
V
by the online method considered in this paper. To do this, we assume that
we are given
\mu
-distributed random samples
(i_{m},y_{m})
, where
i_{m}\in\mathbb{N}
and

y_{m}=(u,\psi_{i_{m}})+\epsilon_{m},\qquad m=0,1,\ldots

(40)

are the noisy samples of the coefficients
(u,\psi_{i})
. Starting from
u^{(0)}=0
,
we want to approximate
u
by the iterates
u^{(m)}
obtained from the online algorithm

u^{(m+1)}=\alpha_{m}u^{(m)}+\alpha_{m}\mu_{m}(y_{m}-(u^{(m)},\psi_{i_{m}}))\psi_{i_{m}},\qquad m=0,1,\ldots,

(41)

where the coefficients
\alpha_{m}
and
\mu_{m}
are given by (
6
) with
\Lambda=1
. This is a special instance of (
4
) if we set
\Omega=\mathbb{N}
,
Y=\mathbb{R}
and
define
R_{i}:\,\mathbb{R}\to V
and
R^{\ast}_{i}:\,V\to\mathbb{R}
by

R_{i}y=y\psi_{i}
and
R_{i}^{\ast}v=(v,\psi_{i})
, respectively. To simplify things further, let
i_{m}
be i.i.d.
samples from
\mathbb{N}
with respect to a discrete probability measure
\rho
on
\mathbb{N}
,
and let
\epsilon_{m}
be i.i.d. random noise with zero mean and finite variance
\sigma^{2}<\infty
that is independent of
i_{m}
.
The corresponding operator
P_{\rho}
is given by

P_{\rho}v=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\rho_{i}(v,\psi_{i})\psi_{i},

its eigenvalues
\lambda_{i}=\rho_{i}
are given by
\rho
, and it is trace class
(w.l.o.g., we assume
\rho_{1}\geq\rho_{2}\geq\ldots
). The spaces
V_{P_{\rho}}^{s}
,
-\infty<s<\infty
, can now be identified as sets of formal orthogonal series

V_{P_{\rho}}^{s}:=\left\{u\sim\sum_{i\in\mathbb{N}}c_{i}\psi_{i}\;:\quad\|u\|_{V_{P_{\rho}}^{s}}^{2}=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\rho_{i}^{-s}c_{i}^{2}\right\}.

Obviously,
V_{P_{\rho}}^{s}\subset V=V_{P_{\rho}}^{0}
for
s>0
. Since functions
f:\,\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{R}
can be identified with formal series with respect to
\Psi
by

u\sim\sum_{i\in\mathbb{N}}c_{i}\psi_{i}\quad\leftrightarrow\quad f_{u}:\;f_{u}(i)=c_{i},

we have
\|f_{u}\|_{L^{2}_{\rho}(\mathbb{N},\mathbb{R})}=\|u\|_{V_{P_{\rho}}^{-1}}
and we can
silently identify
L^{2}_{\rho}(\mathbb{N},\mathbb{R})
with
V_{P_{\rho}}^{-1}
. Under the assumptions made, the underlying minimization problem (
7
) on
V
is

\mathbb{E}(|f_{v}-y|^{2})=\|f_{v}-f_{u}\|_{L^{2}_{\rho}(\mathbb{N},\mathbb{R})}^{2}+\sigma^{2}\quad\longmapsto\quad\min,

and, as expected, has
u
as its unique solution. This example also shows that sometimes it is more important to consider convergence in
V
than convergence in the sense of
L_{\rho}^{2}(\Omega,Y)
.

The simplicity of this example allows a rather comprehensive convergence theory with respect to the scale of
V_{P_{\rho}}^{s}
spaces. We state the following results without detailed proof.

Theorem 5.1

Let
\,-1\leq\bar{s}\leq 0\leq s
, and
\bar{s}<s\leq\bar{s}+2
. Then, for the sampling process described above, the online algorithm (
41
) converges for
u\in V_{P_{\rho}}^{s}
in the
V_{P_{\rho}}^{\bar{s}}
norm with the bound

\mathbb{E}(\|e^{(m)}\|^{2}_{V_{P_{\rho}}^{\bar{s}}})\leq C(m+1)^{-\min(\frac{s-\bar{s}}{s+2},\frac{2}{\bar{s}+4})}(A^{\bar{s}-s}\|u\|^{2}_{V_{P_{\rho}}^{s}}+A^{2+\bar{s}}\sigma^{2}),\quad m=1,2,\ldots,

(42)

if the parameters
t
and
A
in (
6
) satisfy

t=t_{s,\bar{s}}:=\max((s+1)/(s+2),(\bar{s}+3)/(\bar{s}+4)),\qquad 0<A\leq 1/2.

Setting
\bar{s}=0
, one concludes from (
42
) that the
convergence estimate for the online algorithm (
4
), which holds
by Theorem
2.1
for
0<s\leq 1
in the general case, is indeed matched.
For
\bar{s}=-1
, which corresponds to
L^{2}_{\rho}(\mathbb{N},\mathbb{R})
convergence, the rate is better and in line with known lower bounds.

The estimate (
42
) for the online algorithm (
41
) is best possible in the sense that, under the conditions of Theorem
5.1
, the exponent in (
42
) cannot be increased without additional assumptions on
\rho
. In particular, there is no further improvement for
s>\bar{s}+2
, i.e., the estimate actually saturates at
s=\bar{s}+2
. This can be seen from the following result.

Theorem 5.2

Let
-1\leq\bar{s}\leq 0\leq s
,
\bar{s}<s
and
\sigma>0
. For the online algorithm (
41
) we have

\sup_{\rho}\sup_{u:\,\|u\|_{V_{P_{\rho}}^{s}}=1}(m+1)^{\min((s-\bar{s})/(2+s),2/(\bar{s}+4))}\mathbb{E}(\|e^{(m)}\|^{2}_{V_{P_{\rho}}^{\bar{s}}})\geq c>0,

(43)

m=1,2,\ldots,
, where
c
depends on
\bar{s}
,
s
,
\sigma
, and the parameters
t
and
A
in (
6
), but is independent of
m
.

The proofs of these statements are elementary but rather tedious and will be given elsewhere.
Let us just note that the simplicity of this example allows us to reduce the considerations
to explicit linear recursions for expectations associated with the decomposition coefficients
c_{i}^{(m)}:=(e^{(m)},\psi_{i})
of the errors
e^{(m)}=u-u^{(m)}
with respect to
\Psi
for each
i\in\mathbb{N}
separately. This is because

\mathbb{E}(\|e^{(m)}\|^{2}_{V_{P_{\rho}}^{\bar{s}}})=\sum_{i}\rho_{i}^{-\bar{s}}\mathbb{E}((c_{i}^{(m)})^{2}),\qquad\|u\|^{2}_{V_{P_{\rho}}^{s}}=\|e^{(0)}\|^{2}_{V_{P_{\rho}}^{s}}=\sum_{i}\rho_{i}^{-s}c_{i}^{2}

(44)

and

\displaystyle c_{i}^{(m+1)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bar{\alpha}_{m}c_{i}+\alpha_{m}c_{i}^{(m)}+\alpha_{m}\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\mu_{m}(y_{i_{m}}-(u^{(m)},\psi_{i_{m}})),&\;i_{m}=i\\
0,&\;i_{m}\neq i\end{array}\right.

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bar{\alpha}_{m}c_{i}+\alpha_{m}(c_{i}^{(m)}-\delta_{i,i_{m}}\mu_{m}(c_{i}^{(m)}+\epsilon_{m}))

for
m=0,1,\ldots
, where
\delta_{i,i_{m}}=1
with probability
\rho_{i}
, and
\delta_{i,i_{m}}=0
with probability
1-\rho_{i}
. So if we denote
\varepsilon_{m,i}:=\mathbb{E}((c_{i}^{(m)})^{2})
and
\bar{\varepsilon}_{m,i}:=\mathbb{E}(c_{i}^{(m)})
and use the independence assumption, we get a system of linear recursions

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{m+1,i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\alpha_{m}^{2}(1-\rho_{i}\mu_{m}(2-\mu_{m}))\varepsilon_{m,i}+2\alpha_{m}\bar{\alpha}_{m}(1-\rho_{i}\mu_{m})c_{i}\bar{\varepsilon}_{m,i}+\,\bar{\alpha}_{m}^{2}c_{i}^{2}+\rho_{i}\alpha_{m}^{2}\mu_{m}^{2}\sigma^{2},

\displaystyle\bar{\varepsilon}_{m+1,i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\alpha_{m}(1-\rho_{i}\mu_{m})\bar{\varepsilon}_{m,i}+\bar{\alpha}_{m}c_{i},

m=0,1,\ldots
, with starting values
\varepsilon_{0,i}=c_{i}^{2}
and
\bar{\varepsilon}_{0,i}=c_{i}
. In principle, this system can be solved explicitly. For example, we have

\bar{\varepsilon}_{m,i}=\frac{1}{m+1}c_{i}S_{m},\qquad S_{m}:=\sum_{k=0}^{m}\Pi_{k}^{m-1},

where the notation

\Pi_{k}^{m-1}=(1-\frac{a}{m^{t}})\cdot\ldots(1-\frac{a}{(k+1)^{t}}),\quad 0\leq k\leq m-1,\quad\Pi_{m}^{m-1}=1,

(46)

is used with
a=A\rho_{i}
. Similarly, we get

\varepsilon_{m,i}\leq\frac{1}{(m+1)^{2}}\left(2c_{i}^{2}\sum_{k=0}^{m}\Pi_{k}^{m-1}S_{k}+\rho_{i}\sigma^{2}\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}(k+1)^{2}\mu_{k}^{2}\Pi_{k+1}^{m-1}\right).

A matching lower bound for
\varepsilon_{m,i}
can be obtained by using a slightly different value of
a
in the definition of
the products
\Pi_{k}^{m-1}
. The remainder of the argument for Theorem
5.1
first requires the substitution of tight upper
bounds for
\Pi_{k}^{m-1}
and
S_{k}
in dependence on
t
and
a
into the bounds for
\varepsilon_{m,i}
.
Next, after substituting the estimates for
\varepsilon_{m,i}
into (
44
), the resulting
series has to be estimated separately for the index sets
I_{1}:=\{i:\,A\rho_{i}\leq(m+1)^{t-1}\}
and
I_{2}:=\mathbb{N}\backslash I_{1}
followed by choosing the indicated optimal value of
t=t_{s,\bar{s}}
.
This leads to the bound (
42
) in Theorem
5.1
. For the proof of Theorem
5.2
, lower bounds for

\Pi_{k}^{m-1}
,
S_{k}
and consequently for
\varepsilon_{m}
are needed, combined with choosing
suitable discrete probability distributions
\rho
. Regarding lower bounds for

\Pi_{k}^{m-1}
, see the considerations in Subsection
5.3
.

Abstract.

We prove Bloch’s formula for the Chow group of 0-cycles with modulus
on a smooth quasi-projective
surface over a field. We use this formula to give a simple
proof of the rank one case of a conjecture of Deligne and Drinfeld
on lisse
\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}_{\ell}
-sheaves. This was
originally solved by Kerz and Saito in characteristic
\neq 2
.

1.
Introduction

Let
X
be a normal and complete variety over a finite field

k
. Let
U\subset X
be a quasi-projective
open subvariety which is smooth and
whose complement is the support of an effective Cartier divisor on
X
.
Let
(V_{Z})_{Z}
be a family of semisimple lisse
\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}_{\ell}
-sheaves on
the normalizations
Z^{N}
, where
Z
runs through all closed integral curves
on
U
. The family
(V_{Z})_{Z}
is said to be a 2-skeleton sheaf if
for two curves
Z_{1}\neq Z_{2}
, the sheaves
V_{Z_{1}}
and
V_{Z_{2}}

become isomorphic up to semi-simplification after pull-back to
the support of
Z^{N}_{1}\times_{U}Z^{N}_{2}
.

Let
\psi_{Z}\colon\overline{Z}^{N}\to\overline{Z}
be the normalization of the closure

\overline{Z}
of an integral curve
Z\subset U
in
X

and let
Z_{\infty}=\psi^{-1}_{Z}(\overline{Z}\setminus U)
. The family
(V_{Z})_{Z}
is said to have
bounded ramification if there exists an effective Cartier divisor

D\subset X
supported on
X\setminus U
such that for
all integral curves
Z\subset U
, one has the inequality of
Cartier divisors

{\underset{y\in Z_{\infty}}{\sum}}{\rm ar}_{y}(V_{Z})[y]\leq\psi^{*}_{Z}(D)
on
\overline{Z}^{N}
,
where
{\rm ar}_{y}(V_{Z})
is the local Artin conductor of
V_{Z}
at
y

(see
[
Ser68
]
).

Motivated by the work of Drinfeld
[
Dri12
]
, a conjecture of
Deligne
[
EK12
]
states that given a 2-skeleton sheaf

(V_{Z})_{Z}
with bounded ramification, there exists a lisse

\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}_{\ell}
-sheaf
V
on
U
such that
V|_{Z^{N}}\cong V_{Z}
after
semi-simplification for all integral curves
Z\subset U
.

Let
{\rm CH}_{0}(X|D)
denote the Chow group of 0-cycles on
X
with modulus
(see
[
BS19
]
or
[
KS16
]
) for any effective Cartier divisor
D\subset X
.
Let
C(U)={\underset{D\subset X\setminus U}{\varprojlim}}{\rm CH}_{0}(X|D)
,
where
D
runs through all effective Cartier divisors supported on

X\setminus U
. Let
\pi^{{\rm ab}}_{1}(U)
denote the abelianized étale
fundamental group of
U
.

Kerz and Saito
[
KS16
]
showed that
C(U)
is independent of the
choice of the compactification
X
and that there is a reciprocity map
\rho_{U}\colon C(U)\to\pi^{{\rm ab}}_{1}(U)
. This induces a map
\rho^{0}_{U}\colon C(U)^{0}\to\pi^{{\rm ab}}_{1}(U)^{0}
on the degree zero parts (see
8.3
).
The rank one case of the above conjecture of Deligne
is then a direct consequence of another conjecture, namely, the map

\rho^{0}_{U}
is an isomorphism of topological pro-finite abelian groups.
Kerz and Saito proved this latter conjecture when
{\rm char}(k)\neq 2

through a series of several highly non-trivial reductions.

In this paper, we give a simple and independent proof of
the theorem of Kerz and Saito, including the
missing characteristic
2
case. As a byproduct, we obtain a simple proof of
the rank one case of Deligne’s conjecture in all positive characteristics.
We deduce these results by proving
Bloch’s formula for
{\rm CH}_{0}(X|D)
when
X
is a smooth quasi-projective surface over an arbitrary base field
k
.

1.1.
The main results

We now state our precise results.
For a local ring
A
and an ideal
I\subset A
, let
K^{M}_{*}(A,I)

denote the relative Milnor
K
-theory (e.g., see
[
KS86
, § 1.3]
).
Let
A^{h}
denote the Henselization of
A
with respect to its maximal ideal.
For a closed immersion of Noetherian schemes
D\subset X

defined by the sheaf of ideals
{\mathcal{I}}_{D}
,
we let
{\mathcal{K}}^{M}_{n,X}
denote the Zariski (resp. Nisnevich) sheaf on

X
whose stalk at a point
x\in X
is the relative Milnor
K
-group

K^{M}_{n}({\mathcal{O}}_{X,x},{\mathcal{I}}_{D,x})
(resp.
K^{M}_{n}({\mathcal{O}}^{h}_{X,x},{\mathcal{I}}^{h}_{D,x})
)

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
X
be a smooth quasi-projective surface over a field
k
and let

D\subset X
be an effective Cartier divisor. Then there are canonical
isomorphisms

(1.1)

\rho_{X|D}\colon{\rm CH}_{0}(X|D)\xrightarrow{\cong}H^{2}_{{\rm zar}}(X,{\mathcal{K}}^{M}_{2,(X,D)})\xrightarrow{\cong}H^{2}_{{\rm nis}}(X,{\mathcal{K}}^{M}_{2,(X,D)}).

When
D=0
, Theorem
1.1
is classical and was first
proven by Bloch
[
Blo74
]
for Quillen
K
-theory and by
Kato
[
Kat86
]
for Milnor
K
-theory.
For general
D
, Theorem
1.1
was proven earlier
by Binda and Krishna
[
BK18
, Theorem 1.8]
when
k
is algebraically closed.
When
X
is affine and
k
is perfect,
this theorem was proven by Gupta and Krishna
[
GK20b
, Theorem 1.4]
.
We shall refer to this theorem as the Bloch-Kato formula.

Combining Theorem
1.1

with an induction argument due to Kerz and Saito
[
KS16
]
and
the Kato-Saito class field theory
[
KS86
]
, we obtain the
following.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
U
be a smooth quasi-projective variety of dimension
d\geq 1
over
a finite field
k
. Suppose there is an open immersion
U\subset X

such that
X
is normal and proper over
k
and
(X\setminus U)_{{\rm red}}
is
the support of an effective Cartier divisor on
X
. Then the
reciprocity map

\rho^{0}_{U}\colon C(U)^{0}\to\pi^{{\rm ab}}_{1}(U)^{0}

is an isomorphism of pro-finite topological abelian groups.

Since the rank one lisse

\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}_{\ell}
-sheaves on
U
are same as the characters of
the pro-finite group
\pi^{{\rm ab}}_{1}(U)
, the following is
an immediate consequence of Theorem
1.2
and the
Pontryagin duality theorem for pro-finite groups.

Corollary 1.3
.

The rank one case of Deligne’s conjecture is true for any smooth
quasi-projective variety
U
over a finite field.

We list some more applications of Theorem
1.1
.
Apart from its application to higher dimensional class field theory
and solution of the rank one case of Deligne’s conjecture,
Theorem
1.1
has further applications. It is used in

[
BK21b
]
to prove a restriction isomorphism for the
relative 0-cycle group on a regular and flat projective scheme over
a henselian discrete valuation ring with arbitrary reduction.
Theorem
1.1
is used in
[
BK21a
]
to
give an explicit comparison between
the motivic cohomology (in the sense of Suslin-Voevodsky)
and the Levine-Weibel Chow group of a normal crossing scheme.
Finally, it forms a crucial step in the recent proof of Bloch’s
formula for the Chow group of 0-cycles with modulus in higher dimensions
in
[
GK20c
]
.

1.2.
Outline of proofs

Let
U
be as in Theorem
1.2
.
Kerz and Saito
[
KS16
]
defined a reciprocity map

\rho_{U}\colon C(U)\to\pi^{{\rm ab}}_{1}(U)
, and the goal is to show that this is an
isomorphism on the degree zero parts. As a first step, it is possible to use an alteration (in the sense of de Jong) to assume that the compactification
X
of
U
is smooth and projective, with complement
(X\setminus U)_{{\rm red}}
the support of a strict normal crossing divisor.
Next, using a generalized version of
the Bertini theorem of Poonen
[
Poo04
]
and the
Lefschetz hyperplane theorem of Kerz and Saito
[
KS14
]
, one
reduces to the case when
d\leq 2
.
This is the main point, and it is the case for which we provide a new and
simple proof in this paper.

The
d=1
case is classical.
Our strategy for proving Theorem
1.2
in
d=2
case
is the following. We define a cycle class map

\rho_{X|D}\colon{\rm CH}_{0}(X|D)\to H^{2}_{{\rm nis}}(X,{\mathcal{K}}^{M}_{2,(X,D)})

for any effective Cartier divisor
D\subset X
supported on
X\setminus U
.
Theorem
1.1
says that this map is an isomorphism.
Taking the limit over all such
D\subset X
, we get an
isomorphism

\widetilde{\rho}_{U}\colon C(U)\to{\underset{D}{\varprojlim}}\ H^{2}_{{\rm nis}}(X,{\mathcal{K}}^{M}_{2,(X,D)})
.
Let us denote the limit on the right side as
C^{\rm KS}(U)
.

Kato and Saito
[
KS86
]
constructed a reciprocity isomorphism

\widehat{\rho}^{0}\colon C^{\rm KS}(U)^{0}\xrightarrow{\cong}\pi^{{\rm ab}}_{1}(U)^{0}
.
We thus have maps

C(U)^{0}\xrightarrow{\widetilde{\rho}_{U}^{0}}C^{\rm KS}(U)^{0}\xrightarrow{\widehat{\rho}^{0}}\pi^{{\rm ab}}_{1}(U)^{0}.

It is easy to check that the composite map is the reciprocity map

\rho^{0}_{U}
of Kerz and Saito. The map

\widehat{\rho}^{0}
was shown to be an isomorphism by Kato and Saito

[
KS86
]
. This provides a proof of Theorem
1.2
.

The heart of this paper is the proof of Theorem
1.1
.
This proof has two main ingredients: (1) the fundamental exact sequence
of
[
BK18
]
which relates the 0-cycles group with modulus on smooth
varieties with a modified version of the Levine-Weibel 0-cycle group
(referred to as the lci Chow group) on a
singular variety, and (2) a proof of Bloch’s formula for the modified version
of the Levine-Weibel 0-cycle group.
For (2), we use the existence of a good theory of pull-back and
push-forward for the modified Levine-Weibel Chow group and a
pro-
\ell
extension trick to reduce it
to the case when the ground field is infinite.

When the ground field is algebraically closed,
Levine
[
Lev85b
]
constructed
a geometric theory of Chow ring on singular varieties.
As a consequence, he showed that for a reduced quasi-projective
surface
X
over an algebraically closed field,
the cycle class map from the Levine-Weibel Chow group to
K_{0}(X)

is injective. Even as Levine’s complete proof is yet unpublished,
a published account of the dimension two case of his proof
is available in
[
BS98
]
.
A major part of this paper is devoted to showing that
Levine’s program can be carried out for surfaces over an arbitrary infinite field.
In particular, the injectivity of the cycle class map (for surfaces) holds over
any infinite field. A known relation between the Levine-Weibel
and the lci Chow groups (shown in
[
BK18
]
) then shows that this holds
for the latter group too. The injectivity of the cycle class map
implies Bloch’s formula.

In §
2
, we recall definitions of all cycle groups
and recollect some results from
[
BK18
]
that play key roles
in our proofs.
In §
3
, we construct the cycle class map and the
Bloch-Kato map which are used in the proof of Bloch’s formula.
We prove some results regarding these maps which are later used in the
main proofs. The key results in this section are
Theorems
3.6
and
3.7
.
The next four sections form the heart of the proofs, where
the goal is to carry out Levine’s strategy over an arbitrary infinite fields.
Here, the main idea is solely due to Levine. However, due to the
complications arising from the arbitrariness
of the ground field, we need to include new arguments at several places
to ensure that Levine’s results remain valid over such fields.
In §
8
, we finish the proofs of our main results.

2.
The Chow group of 0-cycles

In this section, we fix our notations and recall the definitions of various
0-cycle groups that we use in our proofs. We also prove some
results relating these groups.

2.1.
Notations

For a field
k
, we shall let
{\operatorname{\mathbf{Sch}}}_{k}
denote the category of
quasi-projective schemes over
k
. We let
{\mathbf{Sm}}_{k}
denote the
subcategory of
{\operatorname{\mathbf{Sch}}}_{k}
consisting of smooth schemes over
k
.
For
X\in{\operatorname{\mathbf{Sch}}}_{k}
, we let
X_{\rm reg}
denote the largest open
subscheme of
X
which is regular. We let
X_{\rm sing}
denote
the complement of
X_{\rm reg}
in
X
with its reduced induced closed
subscheme structure. Recall here the convention that if
\text{\rm dim}(X)=d
, then

X_{\rm sing}
contains every point of
X
which lies on an
irreducible component of
X
of dimension less than
d
.
We shall let
X_{{\operatorname{sm}}}
denote the set of points in
X
where
the map
X\to{\rm Spec\,}(k)
is smooth. It is clear by the definition
of smooth morphisms that
X_{{\operatorname{sm}}}\subset X
is open.
We let
X_{{\rm nsm}}=X\setminus X_{\operatorname{sm}}
with the reduced induced
closed subscheme structure. Note that

X_{\operatorname{sm}}\subset X_{\rm reg}
and hence
X_{\rm sing}\subset X_{\rm nsm}
.

For
X,Y\in{\operatorname{\mathbf{Sch}}}_{k}
, we shall write the
product
X\times_{k}Y
as
X\times Y
.
If
X_{1}\times\cdots\times X_{n}
is a product of schemes, then

p_{i}
will denote the projection from this product to its
i
-th
factor (unless we use a specific notation in some
context).
We shall often write
X\times{\mathbb{P}}^{n}_{k}
as
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}_{X}
.
If
z\in Z:=X\times Y
is a closed point, then it is not true in general
that
z
is uniquely determined by its projections

p_{1}(z)\in X
and
p_{2}(z)\in Y
. However, this is indeed true if

z
happens to be a
k
-rational point of
X\times Y
.
In this case, we can uniquely write
z=(p_{1}(z),p_{2}(z))\in X\times Y
.
More generally, if
S
is a scheme and
X,Y\in{\operatorname{\mathbf{Sch}}}_{S}
,
then the canonical map
(X\times_{S}Y)(S)\to X(S)\times Y(S)
of sets is bijective.
This fact will be used frequently in this paper.
If
f\colon X^{\prime}\to X
is a morphism in
{\operatorname{\mathbf{Sch}}}_{k}
, then a rational fiber
of
f
will mean the scheme-theoretic fiber over a
k
-rational point of
X
.

We shall let
{\mathcal{Z}}(X)
denote the free abelian group
generated by integral closed subschemes of
X
.
If
f\colon X^{\prime}\to X

is a proper map and
V\subset X^{\prime}
is an integral closed subscheme,
then we shall let
f_{*}(V)\in{\mathcal{Z}}(X)

denote the push-forward on
X
of the cycle

[V]
in the sense of
[
Ful98
]
.

If
X\in{\operatorname{\mathbf{Sch}}}_{k}
and
V_{1},V_{2}\subset X
are two irreducible closed
subschemes of
X
, then we
shall say that they intersect properly if

{\rm codim}_{X}(V_{1}\cap V_{2})\geq{\rm codim}_{X}(V_{1})+{\rm codim}_{X}(V_{2})
.
If
V_{1}
or
V_{2}
is not necessarily irreducible, then
we shall say that they intersect properly if every
irreducible component of
V_{1}
intersects every irreducible component of

V_{2}
properly. If
V_{1},V_{2}
are two closed subschemes intersecting
properly, we shall let
V_{1}\cdot V_{2}
be their intersection product
as a cycle on
X
in the sense of
[
Ful98
, Chapter 6]
.
We shall use this intersection product only in the case
when
V_{1}\cap V_{2}\subset X_{\rm reg}
.
We shall let

{\rm codim}_{X}(\emptyset)=\infty
. If
X
is integral and
f\in k(X)^{\times}

is a rational function, we shall let
{\rm div}(f)
denote the cycle
associated to
f
in
{\mathcal{Z}}(X)
as in
[
Ful98
]
.

2.2.
The Levine-Weibel Chow group of a surface

Since we use the Levine-Weibel Chow group
[
LW85
]
only for surfaces, we
do not recall its definition in full generality. In the case of
surfaces, we shall use the following variant of the Levine-Weibel Chow group
which was defined by Levine
[
Lev85b
]
. It was shown
in
ibid.
that this variant is actually isomorphic to the
Levine-Weibel Chow group, but we shall have no occasion to use this
comparison. We fix an arbitrary field
k
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
X
be a reduced quasi-projective surface over
k
and let

Y\subset X
be a nowhere dense closed subscheme containing
X_{{\rm sing}}
.
Let
{\mathcal{Z}}_{0}(X,Y)
be the free abelian group on the set of closed points
lying in
X\setminus Y
. Let
{\mathcal{R}}^{L}_{0}(X,Y)
denote the subgroup of

{\mathcal{Z}}_{0}(X,Y)
generated by cycles
C_{0}-C_{\infty}\in{\mathcal{Z}}_{0}(X,Y)

such that the following hold:

There exists a closed subscheme
C\subset X\times{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{k}

of pure dimension one such that

(1)

C\cap{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{Y}
is finite and
C\cap(Y\times\{0,\infty\})=\emptyset
.

(2)

C\cap(X\times\{0,\infty\})
is finite.

(3)

The projection map
C\to{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{k}
is flat over a neighborhood of

\{0,\infty\}
.

(4)

If
p_{1}\colon{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{X}\to X
is the projection, then

(p_{1})_{*}(C\cdot(X\times\{0\}))=C_{0}
and

(p_{1})_{*}(C\cdot(X\times\{\infty\}))=C_{\infty}
.

(5)

The ideal of
C
in
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{X}
is a complete intersection
in the local ring of every point
x\in C\cap{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{Y}
.

We define
{\rm CH}^{L}_{0}(X,Y)
to be the quotient

\frac{{\mathcal{Z}}_{0}(X,Y)}{{\mathcal{R}}^{L}_{0}(X,Y)}
. When
Y=X_{\rm sing}
, we write

{\rm CH}^{L}_{0}(X,Y)
simply as
{\rm CH}^{L}_{0}(X)
.
In this paper, we shall refer to
{\rm CH}^{L}_{0}(X)
as the
‘Levine-Weibel Chow group’ of
X
.
We shall call a subscheme
C
as above a
Cartier curve

relative to
Y
. A Cartier curve in general will mean the one
relative to
X_{\rm sing}
.

In the sequel, we shall use the notation
{\rm CH}^{{\rm LW}}_{0}(X,Y)
for the original
definition of the Chow group of 0-cycles given by Levine and Weibel
[
LW85
]
.
The notion of “Cartier curves” was introduced in
[
LW85
, Definition 1.2]
:
we shall reserve the term ‘Levine-Weibel Cartier curves’ for those
defined in
[
LW85
]
.

The following lemma allows us to assume
C
to be reduced in the
definition of the Levine-Weibel Chow group if
k
is infinite.

Lemma 2.2
.

Assume that
k
is infinite.
Let the pair
(X,Y)
be as in Definition
2.1
.
Then
{\mathcal{R}}^{L}_{0}(X,Y)
is the subgroup of
{\mathcal{Z}}_{0}(X,Y)
generated by cycles

C_{0}-C_{\infty}\in{\mathcal{Z}}_{0}(X,Y)
, where
C
is a reduced Cartier curve
in
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{X}
. If
X
is integral, we can assume
C
to be integral.

Proof.

Let
C\subset{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{X}
be a Cartier curve. Then it is also a Levine-Weibel
Cartier curve on
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{X}
. In the latter case, it
was shown in
[
Lev87
, Lemma 1.4]
that we can find
reduced Levine-Weibel Cartier curves
C_{1},\ldots,C_{r}
on
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{X}

which miss
Y\times\{0,\infty\}
(since its codimension
in
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{X}
is at least two, see
[
ESV99
, Lemma 1.3]
)
such that we have

C\cdot(X\times\{0\})-C\cdot(X\times\{\infty\})=\stackrel{{\scriptstyle r}}{{\underset{i=1}{\sum}}}\left[C_{i}\cdot(X\times\{0\})-C_{i}\cdot(X\times\{\infty\})\right]

in
{\mathcal{Z}}_{0}({\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{X},{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{Y})
.
In particular, we get

C_{0}-C_{\infty}=\stackrel{{\scriptstyle r}}{{\underset{i=1}{\sum}}}\left[(p_{1})_{*}(C_{i}\cdot(X\times\{0\}))-(p_{1})_{*}(C_{i}\cdot(X\times\{\infty\}))\right]=\stackrel{{\scriptstyle r}}{{\underset{i=1}{\sum}}}\left[(C_{i})_{0}-(C_{i})_{\infty}\right].

Since each
C_{i}
is a Cartier curve in our sense and is reduced,
we are done. If
X
was integral, it was shown in

[
Lev87
, Lemma 1.4]
that we could have chosen each

C_{i}
to be integral. This finishes the proof.
∎

Lemma 2.3
.

Assume that
C\subset{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{X}
is a reduced Cartier curve.
We can then find another reduced Cartier curve
C^{\prime}\subset{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{X}

such that the projection
C^{\prime}\to X
is finite and

C_{0}-C_{\infty}=C^{\prime}_{0}-C^{\prime}_{\infty}
.

Proof.

Write
C=C_{1}\cup\ldots\cup C_{r}
as union of irreducible components.
Suppose that all the components
C_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq s
are such that
the composition
C_{i}\to X
is finite and
p_{1}(C_{i})=x_{i}
is a closed
point of
X
for
s<i\leq r
. Note that
p_{1}
is projective.
Let
C_{i}
be one of these non-finite components.
Then condition (1) in Definition
2.1
implies that

x_{i}\in X_{\rm reg}
.
Moreover, we have

(p_{1})_{*}(C_{i}\cdot(X\times\{0\}))-(p_{1})_{*}(C_{i}\cdot(X\times\{\infty\}))=(p_{1})_{*}(C_{i}\times_{{\mathbb{P}}^{1}}\{0\}-C_{i}\times_{{\mathbb{P}}^{1}}\{\infty\})=0

as
0
-cycles on
X_{\rm reg}
. Note that the above intersection makes sense,
thanks to conditions (1) and (2) in Definition
2.1
.

We let
C^{\prime}:=\overline{C\setminus(C_{s+1}\cup\cdots\cup C{r})}

(where the closure is taken in
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{X}
)
with the reduced induced closed subscheme structure.
Since
x_{i}
is a closed point of
X_{\rm reg}
for every
s<i\leq r

and
C
is reduced, it follows that the schemes
C
and
C^{\prime}

agree in an open neighborhood of
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{X_{\rm sing}}
.
We conclude that
C^{\prime}
is a reduced Cartier curve on
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{X}
,
the projection
p_{1}\colon C^{\prime}\to X
is finite and

C_{0}-C_{\infty}=C^{\prime}_{0}-C^{\prime}_{\infty}
.
∎

Note that the above proof shows that
an integral Cartier curve
C
is either finite over
X

or
C_{0}-C_{\infty}=0
in
{\mathcal{Z}}_{0}(X,Y)
.
Combining Lemmas
2.2
and
2.3
, we
therefore get the following.

Corollary 2.4
.

Assume that
k
is infinite.
Let the pair
(X,Y)
be as in Definition
2.1
.
Then
{\mathcal{R}}^{L}_{0}(X,Y)
is the subgroup of
{\mathcal{Z}}_{0}(X,Y)
generated by cycles

C_{0}-C_{\infty}\in{\mathcal{Z}}_{0}(X,Y)
, where
C
is a reduced Cartier curve
in
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{X}
which is finite over
X
.
If
X
is integral, we can assume
C
to be integral.

2.3.
The lci Chow group of a singular scheme

Let
k
be any field. The lci Chow group of a singular scheme was
introduced in
[
BK18
]
. We recall it here.
Let
C
be a reduced equidimensional curve over
k
and let

k(C)
denote its ring of total quotients. Let

C_{1},\ldots,C_{r}
be the irreducible components of
C
.
Then
k(C)
is the product of the quotient fields of all

C_{i}
’s. For
f\in k(C)^{\times}
, we can therefore write

f=(f_{i})\in\stackrel{{\scriptstyle r}}{{\underset{i=1}{\prod}}}k(C_{i})^{\times}
.
We let
{\rm div}(f)
be the sum
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle r}}{{\underset{i=1}{\sum}}}{\rm div}(f_{i})\in{\mathcal{Z}}(C)
. If
Z\subset C
is a finite closed subset, we
let
{\mathcal{O}}_{C,Z}
denote the semi-local ring of
C
at
Z
.
Since
{\mathcal{O}}^{\times}_{C,Z}\subset k(C)^{\times}
, the
cycle
{\rm div}(f)\in{\mathcal{Z}}(C)
makes sense for any

f\in{\mathcal{O}}^{\times}_{C,Z}
. For such finite set
Z
, we let
{\mathcal{Z}}_{0}(C,Z)

denote the subgroup of
{\mathcal{Z}}(C)
generated by closed points away from

Z
.

Let
X
be a reduced quasi-projective scheme
over
k
and let
Y\subset X
be a nowhere dense closed subscheme
containing
X_{{\rm sing}}
.

Definition 2.5
.

Let
C
be a reduced curve in
{\operatorname{\mathbf{Sch}}}_{k}
and let
\nu\colon C\to X
be a
finite morphism. We say that
\nu\colon(C,Z)\to(X,Y)
is

a good curve relative to
Y
if
Z
is a closed
subscheme of
C
such that the following hold.

(1)

No component of
C
is contained in
Z
.

(2)

\nu^{-1}(Y)\subseteq Z
.

(3)

\nu
is a local complete intersection morphism at every
point
x\in C
such that
\nu(x)\in Y
.

Given any good curve
(C,Z)
relative to
Y
, we can consider the
push-forward (see §
2.1
)

{\mathcal{Z}}_{0}(C,Z)\xrightarrow{\nu_{*}}{\mathcal{Z}}_{0}(X,Y)
.
We let
{\mathcal{R}}_{0}(C,Z,X)
be the subgroup
of
{\mathcal{Z}}_{0}(X,Y)
generated by the set

\{\nu_{*}({{\rm div}}(f))|f\in\mathcal{O}^{\times}_{C,Z}\}
.
We write
{\mathcal{R}}_{0}(X,Y)
for the subgroup of
{\mathcal{Z}}_{0}(X,Y)
defined as the
image of the map

(2.1)

{\underset{\nu\colon(C,Z)\to(X,Y)\ {\rm good}}{\bigoplus}}{\mathcal{R}}_{0}(C,Z,X)\to{\mathcal{Z}}_{0}(X,Y),

where the index set runs over the set of good curves relative to
(X,Y)
.
We define the
Chow group of 0-cycles on
X
(relative to
Y
) to be the
quotient

(2.2)

{\rm CH}_{0}(X,Y)=\frac{{\mathcal{Z}}_{0}(X,Y)}{{\mathcal{R}}_{0}(X,Y)}.

If
Y=X_{\rm sing}
, we shall write
{\rm CH}_{0}(X,Y)
simply as
{\rm CH}_{0}(X)
.
To avoid any conflict of notations, we shall denote the Chow group
of
n
-dimensional cycles on
X
modulo rational equivalence
in the sense of
[
Ful98
, Chapter 1]
by
{\rm CH}^{F}_{n}(X)
.
To distinguish
{\rm CH}_{0}(X,Y)
from
{\rm CH}^{L}_{0}(X,Y)
, we shall often
refer to it as the ‘lci Chow group’ of
(X,Y)
.
The relation between
{\rm CH}^{L}_{0}(X,Y)
and
{\rm CH}_{0}(X,Y)
is given by the
following lemma.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
k
be an infinite field. Let
X
be a reduced quasi-projective surface
over
k
and let
Y\subset X
be a nowhere dense closed subscheme
containing
X_{{\rm sing}}
. Then the identity map of
{\mathcal{Z}}_{0}(X,Y)
induces
a canonical surjection

\phi_{(X,Y)}\colon{\rm CH}^{L}_{0}(X,Y)\twoheadrightarrow{\rm CH}_{0}(X,Y).

Proof.

Let
C\subset{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{X}
be a Cartier curve relative to
Y

and let
Z={\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{Y}\cap C
. We can assume
that
C
is reduced and finite over
X

by Corollary
2.4
. It is then easy to see that

(C,Z)
is a good curve relative to
Y
via the composite map

\nu\colon C\hookrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{X}\xrightarrow{p_{X}}X
.
Furthermore, if we let
f\colon C\to{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{k}

be the projection map, then it defines an element of
k(C)
.
The conditions (1) and (2) of Definition
2.1
imply in fact that

f\in{\mathcal{O}}^{\times}_{C,Z}
.
It is clear that
\nu_{*}({\rm div}(f))=C_{0}-C_{\infty}
.
Hence,
C_{0}-C_{\infty}\in{\mathcal{R}}_{0}(X,Y)
. This finishes the proof.
∎

The lci Chow group
{\rm CH}_{0}(X)
possesses a reasonable theory of
pull-back and push-forward maps.
This property will play a very important role in the proofs of our
main theorems. In contrast, the Levine-Weibel Chow
group
{\rm CH}^{L}_{0}(X)
is not known to have any good theory of push-forward maps
induced by finite maps between two varieties.
A particular result that we shall use in the main proofs is the
following.
We let
(X,Y)
be as in §
2.3
.
For any field extension
k\hookrightarrow k^{\prime}
, we let

X^{\prime}=X_{k^{\prime}}:=X\otimes_{k}k^{\prime}
and
Y^{\prime}=Y_{k^{\prime}}:=Y\otimes_{k}k^{\prime}
.
We let
{\rm pr}_{{k^{\prime}}/{k}}:X^{\prime}\to X
denote the
projection map.

Proposition 2.7
.

Let
k\hookrightarrow k^{\prime}
be a separable algebraic (possibly infinite)
extension of fields. Then the following hold.

(1)

There exists a pull-back map

{\rm pr}^{*}_{{k^{\prime}}/{k}}\colon{\rm CH}_{0}(X,Y)\to{\rm CH}_{0}(X^{\prime},Y^{\prime})
.

(2)

If there exists a sequence of separable
algebraic field extensions
k=k_{0}\subset k_{1}\subset\cdots\subset k^{\prime}
with
k^{\prime}=\cup_{i}k_{i}
such that
X_{i}:=X_{k_{i}}

and
Y_{i}:=Y_{k_{i}}
for each
i\geq 1
, then
we have
{\underset{i}{\varinjlim}}\ {\rm CH}_{0}(X_{i},Y_{i})\xrightarrow{\simeq}{\rm CH}_{0}(X^{\prime},Y^{\prime})
.

(3)

If
k\hookrightarrow k^{\prime}
is finite, then there exists a push-forward

({\rm pr}_{{k^{\prime}}/{k}})_{*}\colon{\rm CH}_{0}(X^{\prime},Y^{\prime})\to{\rm CH}_{0}(X,Y)
such that

({\rm pr}_{{k^{\prime}}/{k}})_{*}\circ{\rm pr}^{*}_{{k^{\prime}}/{k}}
is multiplication by

[k^{\prime}:k]
.

Proof.

See
[
BK18
, Proposition 6.1]
.
∎

2.4.
Zero cycles with modulus

Let
k
be any field.
Given an integral normal curve
{C}
over
k
and an effective divisor

E\subset{C}
, we say that a rational function
f
on
{C}
has modulus

E
if
f\in{\rm Ker}({\mathcal{O}}^{\times}_{C,E}\to{\mathcal{O}}^{\times}_{E})
.
Here,
{\mathcal{O}}_{C,E}
is the semi-local ring of
C
at the union of

E
and the generic point of
C
.
In particular,
{\rm Ker}({\mathcal{O}}^{\times}_{C,E}\to{\mathcal{O}}^{\times}_{E})
is just

k(C)^{\times}
if
|E|=\emptyset
.
Let
G({C},E)
denote the group of such rational functions.

Let
{X}
be a reduced quasi-projective scheme over
k
and let
D
be an
effective Cartier divisor on
X
(we allow
D
to be empty).
Let
Z_{0}(X,D)
be the free abelian group on
the set of closed points of
X\setminus D
. Let
{C}
be an integral normal
curve over
k
and
let
\varphi_{{C}}\colon{C}\to{X}
be a finite morphism such that

\varphi_{{C}}({C})\not\subset D
.
The push forward of cycles along
\varphi_{{C}}

gives a well defined group homomorphism

(\varphi_{C})_{*}\circ{\rm div}\colon G({C},\varphi_{{C}}^{*}(D))\to Z_{0}(X,D).

Definition 2.8

(Kerz-Saito)
.

We define the Chow group
{\rm CH}_{0}({X}|D)
of 0-cycles of
{X}
with
modulus
D
as the cokernel of the homomorphism

(2.3)

\bigoplus_{\varphi_{{C}}\colon{C}\to{X}}G({C},\varphi_{{C}}^{*}(D))\to Z_{0}(X,D),

where the sum is taken over the set of finite morphisms

\varphi_{{C}}\colon{C}\to{X}
from an integral normal curve such that

\varphi_{{C}}({C})\not\subset D
.

A general theory of higher Chow groups with modulus
{\rm CH}_{p}(X|D,q)

was introduced in
[
BS19
]
, where it was shown that
{\rm CH}_{0}(X|D,0)\cong{\rm CH}_{0}({X}|D)
. It is clear from the definition that
the inclusion
Z_{0}(X|D)\subset Z_{0}(X)
defines a canonical
‘forget modulus’ map
{\rm CH}_{0}({X}|D)\to{\rm CH}_{0}^{F}(X)
(the latter being Fulton’s Chow group of zero cycles).
It is known that the Chow group of 0-cycles with modulus is covariantly
functorial for the proper maps: if
f\colon{X^{\prime}}\to{X}
is a proper map,

D
and
D^{\prime}
are effective Cartier divisors on
{X}
and
{X^{\prime}}

such that
f^{*}(D)=D^{\prime}
, then there is a push-forward
map
f_{*}\colon{\rm CH}_{0}({X^{\prime}}|D^{\prime})\to{\rm CH}_{0}({X}|D)
.
If
f
is flat (but not necessarily proper) of relative dimension zero,
then there is a
pull-back map
f^{*}\colon{\rm CH}_{0}({X}|D)\to{\rm CH}_{0}({X^{\prime}}|D^{\prime})

(see
[
BS19
, Lemma 2.7]
or
[
KP17
, Propositions 2.10, 2.12]
).

Analogous to the case of the lci Chow group, the
Chow group of 0-cycles with modulus has the following nice
behavior with respect to the change of the base field.

Proposition 2.9
.

Let
k\hookrightarrow k^{\prime}
be a separable algebraic (possibly infinite)
extension of fields. Let

X
be a non-singular quasi-projective scheme
over
k
with an effective Cartier divisor
D
.
Let
X^{\prime}=X_{k^{\prime}}
and
D^{\prime}=D_{k^{\prime}}
denote the base change of
X
and

D
, respectively. Let
{\rm pr}_{{k^{\prime}}/{k}}:X^{\prime}\to X
be the
projection map. Then the following hold.

(1)

There exists a pull-back

{\rm pr}^{*}_{{k^{\prime}}/{k}}:{\rm CH}_{0}(X|D)\to{\rm CH}_{0}(X^{\prime}|D^{\prime})
.

(2)

If there exists a sequence of separable algebraic
field extensions
k=k_{0}\subset k_{1}\subset\cdots\subset k^{\prime}
with
k^{\prime}=\cup_{i}k_{i}
, then
we have
{\underset{i}{\varinjlim}}\ {\rm CH}_{0}(X_{k_{i}}|D_{k_{i}})\xrightarrow{\simeq}{\rm CH}_{0}(X^{\prime}|D^{\prime})
.

(3)

If
k\hookrightarrow k^{\prime}
is finite, then there exists a push-forward

{\rm pr}_{{k^{\prime}}/{k}\ *}:{\rm CH}_{0}(X^{\prime}|D^{\prime})\to{\rm CH}_{0}(X|D)
such that

({\rm pr}_{{k^{\prime}}/{k}})_{*}\circ{\rm pr}^{*}_{{k^{\prime}}/{k}}
is multiplication by

[k^{\prime}:k]
.

Proof.

See
[
BK18
, Proposition 6.2]
.
∎

Remark 2.10
.

The reader should be aware of the fact that in the literature there exists another notion of Chow group of zero cycles with modulus, introduced by H. Russell

Rus13

is different from the one used here (and in
[

3.
The cycle class and Bloch-Kato maps

In this section, we shall recall the connection between
various Chow groups defined in §
2
and the
algebraic
K
-groups. We shall construct the Bloch-Kato map
which will be used in the proof of the Bloch-Kato formula.
We fix a field
k
.
For a
k
-scheme
X
, we let
K_{0}(X)
denote
\pi_{0}(K(X))
, where

K(X)
is the Bass-Thomason-Trobaugh
K
-theory spectrum of
X
.
If
X
is quasi-projective over
k
, then
K_{0}(X)
coincides with
the Grothendieck group of locally free sheaves on
X
.
For a closed immersion of
k
-schemes
D\hookrightarrow X
,
let
K(X,D)
denote the homotopy fiber of the restriction map
of Bass-Thomason-Trobaugh
K
-theory spectra
K(X)\to K(D)
.
We let
K_{i}(X,D)
denote the
i
-th stable homotopy group of
K(X,D)
.

3.1.
The cycle class maps

Let us assume that
X
is a reduced quasi-projective scheme
over
k
and let
Y\subset X
be any closed
subscheme containing
X_{\rm sing}
(but not necessarily nowhere dense).
If
x\in X\setminus Y
is a closed point, then it lies in the regular
locus of
X
. In particular, the inclusion map

{\rm Spec\,}(k(x))\hookrightarrow X
has finite tor-dimension. This yields the maps of
spectra
K({\rm Spec\,}(k(x)))\xrightarrow{(\iota_{x})_{*}}K(X)\xrightarrow{\iota^{*}_{Y}}K(Y)
.
Since the composite map is canonically null-homotopic,
(\iota_{x})_{*}

has a canonical factorization
(\iota_{x})_{*}\colon K({\rm Spec\,}(k(x)))\to K(X,D)
.
In particular, we have a map
(\iota_{x})_{*}\colon{\mathbb{Z}}=K_{0}({\rm Spec\,}(k(x)))\to K_{0}(X,D)
. Letting
cyc_{(X,Y)}([x])=(\iota_{x})_{*}(1)
, and extending it linearly on
{\mathcal{Z}}_{0}(X,D)
, we
get a cycle class map

(3.1)

cyc_{(X,Y)}\colon{\mathcal{Z}}_{0}(X,Y)\to K_{0}(X,Y).

Composing
cyc_{(X,Y)}
with the canonical map

K_{0}(X,Y)\to K_{0}(X)
, we obtain the cycle class map

cyc_{(X,Y)}\colon{\mathcal{Z}}_{0}(X,Y)\to K_{0}(X)
.
It is shown in
[
BK18
, Lemma 3.13]
that if
Y
is nowhere dense in
X
,
this map kills
{\mathcal{R}}_{0}(X,Y)
and hence defines a cycle class map

(3.2)

cyc_{(X,Y)}\colon{\rm CH}_{0}(X,Y)\to K_{0}(X).

We shall write
cyc_{(X,X_{\rm sing})}
in short as
cyc_{X}
.
We shall denote the image of
cyc_{X}
by
F^{2}K_{0}(X)
.
Lemma
2.6
implies the following.

Corollary 3.1
.

Assume that
k
is infinite,
\text{\rm dim}(X)=2
and
Y
is
nowhere dense in
X
. Then the cycle class map
{\mathcal{Z}}_{0}(X,Y)\to K_{0}(X)
induces the commutative diagram

(3.3)

\textstyle{{\rm CH}^{L}_{0}(X,Y)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\phi_{(X,Y)}}
\scriptstyle{cyc^{L}_{(X,Y)}}
\textstyle{{\rm CH}_{0}(X,Y)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{cyc_{(X,Y)}}
\textstyle{K_{0}(X),}

where the map
cyc^{L}_{(X,Y)}
is the cycle class map for the Levine Chow group defined in
[
LW85
]
(see
[
BK18
, Section 3]
).

We next recall the cycle class map for the 0-cycle group with modulus.
Let
X
be a non-singular quasi-projective surface over
k

and let
D\subset X
be an effective Cartier divisor.
By (
3.1
), there is a cycle class map

cyc_{(X,D)}\colon{\mathcal{Z}}_{0}(X,D)\to K_{0}(X,D)
.
It follows from
[
Kri15
, Theorem 1.2]
that this map factors through

{\rm CH}_{0}(X|D)
so that there is a cycle class map

(3.4)

cyc_{X|D}\colon{\rm CH}_{0}(X|D)\to K_{0}(X,D).

When
X
is non-singular, it is clear from the above construction that the
composition

{\rm CH}_{0}(X|D)

\xrightarrow{cyc_{X|D}}K_{0}(X,D)\to K_{0}(X)

coincides with the composition
{\rm CH}_{0}(X|D)\to{\rm CH}^{F}_{0}(X)\xrightarrow{cyc_{X}}K_{0}(X)
,
where
cyc_{X}
is as in (
3.2
).

3.2.
The Cohomology of
K
-theory sheaves

We shall now establish a key step in the proof of Bloch-Kato formula
for the Chow group of 0-cycles.
We first recall the definition of relative Milnor
K
-theory.

Let
A
be a Noetherian local ring and let
I\subset A
be an
ideal. We let
K^{M}_{n}(A)
denote the Milnor

K
-group of
A
as defined by Kato-Saito
[
KS86
, § 1.3]
.
We let
K^{M}_{n}(A,I)={\rm Ker}(K^{M}_{n}(A)\twoheadrightarrow K^{M}_{n}(A/I))
.
The Milnor
K
-theory of local rings was also defined by Kerz in

[
Ker09
]
. The two definitions agree for
n\leq 2
.
However, this may not be the case when
n\geq 3
and

A
has finite residue field.
Since we are interested only in
n\leq 2
case in this paper,
we shall ignore this subtlety.

We let

Proof.

Suppose first that

is infinite.
Let

Since

to find a complete intersection reduced curve

3.5.
Some functoriality properties

We shall use the following functoriality properties of the
Chow group of 0-cycles and their cycle classes. For any
Y\in{\operatorname{\mathbf{Sch}}}_{k}
,
recall our notation that
{\rm CH}_{*}^{F}(Y)
is the direct sum of all
Chow groups of
Y
in the sense of
[
Ful75
, Chapter 1]
.

Proposition 3.8
.

Let
k
be any field and let
X
be a reduced quasi-projective surface over

k
. Let
j\colon U\hookrightarrow X
be an open immersion such that

X_{\rm sing}\subset U
. Then we have an exact sequence
of abelian groups

{\rm CH}^{F}_{0}(X\setminus U)\xrightarrow{i_{*}}{\rm CH}^{L}_{0}(X)\xrightarrow{j^{*}}{\rm CH}^{L}_{0}(U)\to 0.

The same holds for the lci Chow group.

Proof.

The proofs of the proposition for the lci and the Levine-Weibel Chow groups
are completely identical. We give the proof for the latter case.
We have to first explain the maps
i_{*}
and
j^{*}
.
It is clear that the restriction of cycles with respect to the open
immersion
j
indeed gives the map
j^{*}
. It is clearly surjective
because
X_{\rm sing}\subset U
.

The map
i_{*}
is the push-forward map
with respect to the closed immersion
i\colon Z:=X\setminus U\hookrightarrow X
.
It is clearly well-defined at the level of cycles. If

C\subset Z\times{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{k}
is an irreducible 1-cycle dominant over

{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{k}
, then it is also clear (note that
X_{\rm sing}\subset U
)
that
i_{*}(C)
is a Cartier curve on

{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{X}
which defines the rational equivalence between

i_{*}(C_{0}-C_{\infty})
. Hence, the maps in the above sequence are
defined.
We only need to see that this sequence is exact in the middle.

So suppose that
\alpha=\stackrel{{\scriptstyle r}}{{\underset{i=1}{\sum}}}n_{i}[x_{i}]

is a 0-cycle on
X
such that
j^{*}(\alpha)=0
.
We can assume without loss of generality that
x_{i}\in U
for
each
i
. We let
C^{1},\ldots,C^{s}
be Cartier curves on

{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{U}
such that

j^{*}(\alpha)=\stackrel{{\scriptstyle s}}{{\underset{i=1}{\sum}}}(C^{i}_{0}-C^{i}_{\infty})
.
We let
\overline{C}^{i}
be the scheme-theoretic closure of
C^{i}
is
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{X}
.
Since
X_{\rm sing}\subset U
, it follows that
\overline{C}^{i}
is
a Cartier curve on
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{X}
and
\overline{C}^{i}_{0}-\overline{C}^{i}_{\infty}=(C^{i}_{0}-C^{i}_{\infty})+\beta_{i}
, where
\beta_{i}
is a 0-cycle supported on

Z
. Letting
\beta=\stackrel{{\scriptstyle s}}{{\underset{i=1}{\sum}}}\beta_{i}
,
we get
\alpha-\beta=\stackrel{{\scriptstyle s}}{{\underset{i=1}{\sum}}}(\overline{C}^{i}_{0}-\overline{C}^{i}_{\infty})
. This finishes the proof.
∎

4.
The family of good cycles

Our next goal is to show that the cycle class
map
{\rm CH}^{L}_{0}(X,Y)\to K_{0}(X)
is injective
if
X
is a reduced quasi-projective surface over an infinite field.
As we stated in §
1
, this was proven by
Levine in his yet unpublished manuscript
[
Lev85b
]

when the base field is algebraically closed.
A published account of Levine’s proof
is available in
[
BS98
]
.
In the next few sections, our goal is to revisit Levine’s proof
and show that it can be carried over to all infinite fields
with the help of new arguments at every step of the proof.

In this section, we shall construct families
\phi\colon\Gamma_{U}\to U

of cycles on
X
. We shall state and prove an upgraded version of
Kleiman’s transversality theorem
[
Kle74
, Theorem 2]
and
its generalization by Levine
[
Lev85b
, Lemma 1.2]

(see also
[
Blo86
, Lemma 1.1]
) which works over an
arbitrary infinite field. This theorem will be used to ensure that
every member of a family
\phi
as above gives rise to a
well defined 0-cycle on
X
.
The proof closely follows the one
in
[
Lev85b
, Lemma 1.2]
. Unless stated otherwise,
k
will always
denote an infinite ground field in this section.

4.1.
Some recollection

We begin with some standard algebraic geometry results that
we shall use repeatedly. We collect them here for

See
[

4.2.
Morphisms to homogeneous spaces

We recall homogeneous spaces and describe some properties of
morphisms from schemes to these spaces. The set-up of
this section will be used throughout the next few sections of this
paper.

Let
G
be a connected reductive algebraic group over
k
and let

H=G/P
be a projective homogeneous space for
G
.
Recall that a smooth closed subgroup
P^{\prime}\subset G
is parabolic
if and only if the variety
G/{P^{\prime}}
is complete (equivalently, projective).
It follows that
P
is a parabolic subgroup of
G
.
In particular, it is connected (e.g., see
[
Bor91
, Theorem 11.16]
).
We shall let
d_{P}=\text{\rm dim}(P)
.

Let
\pi\colon G\to H
be the resulting
P
-torsor.
Note that since
P
is affine,
\pi
is an fppf locally trivial

P
-torsor, it follows that
\pi
is fppf locally an affine morphism.
But this implies that
\pi
is an affine morphism.
Since
G
is reductive, it
is smooth over
k
and an fppf descent argument shows that

H
must also be smooth over
k
. Furthermore, as
G
is connected,
it must be geometrically connected (any
X\in{\operatorname{\mathbf{Sch}}}_{k}
which is
connected and
X(k)\neq\emptyset
is geometrically connected).
It follows that
G
is geometrically integral.
In particular,
H=G/P
is also geometrically integral.

Another property we shall use frequently is that, being reductive,
G

is a uni-rational variety over
k
(i.e., admits a dominant
k
-morphism
from a dense open subset of an affine space over
k
)
by
[
Bor91
, Theorem 18.2]
.
It follows that
H
is also uni-rational. Since
k
is infinite,
this implies that for any dense open
U\subset H
(or in
G
),
the set of
k
-rational points
U(k)
is Zariski
dense in
H
(or in
G
).

Let
G
act on a reduced quasi-projective scheme
X
over
k
and let

\mu\colon G\times X\to X
be the action map. Let

\Phi=(\mu,{\operatorname{id}}_{X})\colon G\times X\to X\times X
denote the map

\Phi(g,x)=(\mu(g,x),x)
.
Let
\widetilde{\mu}=({\operatorname{id}}_{G},\mu)\colon G\times X\to G\times X
denote the map

\Phi(g,x)=(g,\mu(g,x))
.
Since
\widetilde{\mu}
is an isomorphism and
G
is smooth, it follows that

\mu
is a smooth surjective morphism.

For a closed point
g\in G
, the composite morphism

{\lambda}_{g}\colon{\rm Spec\,}(k(g))\times X=X_{k(g)}\hookrightarrow G\times X\xrightarrow{\mu}X
can be
easily seen to be closed. In particular,

is closed
in

for any closed subscheme

.
If

, we can identify

with

and then

defines an automorphism of

.
It is easy to see that

a group homomorphism

.

6.2.
The pull-back map on the Chow groups

We let
G,H,X
and

be as we described in the beginning of
§
6
. We have shown in
§
6.1
that

induces a pull-back map

f^{*}\colon{\mathcal{Z}}^{2}(H)\to{\rm CH}^{L}_{0}(X)
. We shall now show that this map
factors through the rational equivalence in the classical Chow group of

.

Proposition 6.7
.

The morphism
f^{*}\colon{\mathcal{Z}}^{2}(H)\to{\rm CH}^{L}_{0}(X)
descends to a homomorphism

f^{*}\colon{\rm CH}^{2}(H)\to{\rm CH}^{L}_{0}(X)

satisfying the following property:
for every
Y\subset H
integral subvariety of codimension
2
,
the equality
f^{*}[Y]=[f^{-1}(gY)]
holds in
{\rm CH}^{L}_{0}(X)
for every

k
-point
g\in V(Y,f)
, where
V(Y,g)
is the open dense subset of
G
of
Lemma
6.6
.

Proof.

We only need to show that
f^{*}
respect the rational equivalence, since it is
already clear by construction that on generators the pullback can be
computed by first translating
Y
by a rational point of
V(Y,f)

followed by the naive pullback.

It is well-known that the rational equivalence in
{\rm CH}^{2}(H)
can be presented
in terms of subvarieties of
H
of codimension
1
with rational functions on
them, or in terms of subvarieties of codimension
2
of
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{H}
.
The latter presentation is easier to handle in our context.
Following Levine
[
Lev85b
]
, let
W\subset{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{H}
be an integral
subscheme of codimension
2
, flat over
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{k}
, and let

p^{\prime}_{1}\colon{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{H}\to H
be the projection.

Let
W_{0}=p^{\prime}_{1}(W\cdot(H\times\{0\}))
and
W_{\infty}=p^{\prime}_{1}(W\cdot(H\times\{\infty\}))
. Note that the intersection product is
well defined (this is simply the pull-back to
W
of the Cartier divisors

0
and
\infty
of
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{k}
, which is well defined since the map is flat).
We need to show that the difference
f^{*}[W_{0}]-f^{*}[W_{\infty}]
is an element of

{\mathcal{R}}^{L}_{0}(X,X_{\rm sing})
.
Let
V_{0}=V(f,W_{0})
and let
V_{\infty}=V(f,W_{\infty})
. If we take

g\in(V_{0}\cap V_{\infty})(k)
, we have that
f^{*}[W_{0}]=[f^{-1}{(gW_{0})}]
and

f^{*}[W_{\infty}]=[f^{-1}(gW_{\infty})]
(for the same
k
-point
g
of
G
).

By Proposition
4.9
, there exists an open dense subset

U=U(f\times{\operatorname{id}}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{1}},W)
of
G
such that for every
g\in U(k)
,
the inverse image
(f\times{\operatorname{id}}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{1}})^{-1}(gW)
defines a purely

1
-dimensional closed subscheme
\Gamma
of
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{X}
satisfying the following
properties.

(1)

The inclusion
\Gamma\subset{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{X}
is a local complete intersection
morphism at every point of
\Gamma\cap{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{X_{\rm sing}}
.

(2)

\Gamma\cap{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{X_{\rm sing}}
is a finite set and

\Gamma\cap(X_{\rm sing}\times\{0,\infty\})=\emptyset
.

(3)

The induced map
\Gamma\to{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{k}
is flat over an open neighborhood of

.

In particular,

(p_{1})_{*}(\Gamma\cdot(X\times\{0\}))-(p_{1})_{*}(\Gamma\cdot(X\times\{\infty\}))\in{\mathcal{R}}^{L}_{0}(X,X_{\rm sing})

If we choose

, we conclude that

\begin{array}[]{lll}f^{*}[W_{0}]=[f^{-1}(gW_{0})]&=&(p_{1})_{*}(\Gamma\cdot(X\times\{0\}))\sim(p_{1})_{*}(\Gamma\cdot(X\times\{\infty\}))\\
&=&[f^{-1}(gW_{\infty})]=f^{*}[W_{\infty}],\end{array}

as required.

6.3.
Functoriality of
f^{*}
in
G
and
H

We now show that the pull-back on the Chow groups behaves well with
respect to the change in the group
G
and the homogeneous space
H
.
Let
(G,H)
and
(G^{\prime},H^{\prime})
be two pairs of groups and their
homogeneous spaces of the types described in the beginning of
§
6
.
A morphism
\Psi\colon(G,H)\to(G^{\prime},H^{\prime})
is a pair of maps

\rho\colon G\to G^{\prime}
and
h\colon H\to H^{\prime}
such that
\rho
is
a group homomorphism and
h
is
G
-equivariant, where the latter acts
on
H^{\prime}
via
\rho
.

Let
\Psi\colon(G,H)\to(G^{\prime}H^{\prime})
be as above.
Let

be an equidimensional reduced quasi-projective surface
and let there be a commutative diagram

(6.6)

\textstyle{X\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{f}
\scriptstyle{f^{\prime}}
\textstyle{H\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{h}
\textstyle{H^{\prime}.}

We now prove the following functoriality property with respect to the change
of homogeneous spaces.

Lemma 6.8
.

There is a commutative diagram

(6.7)

\textstyle{{\rm CH}^{2}(H^{\prime})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{h^{*}}
\scriptstyle{f^{\prime*}}
\textstyle{{\rm CH}^{2}(H)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{f^{*}}
\textstyle{{\rm CH}^{L}_{0}(X).}

Proof.

Let
Y
be an integral subscheme of
H^{\prime}
of codimension
2
.
Using Lemma
6.6
(applied simultaneously to

h
and
f^{\prime}
), we let
U^{\prime}\subset G^{\prime}
be a dense open such that

\alpha^{*}([Y])=[\alpha^{-1}(gY)]
and
[\alpha^{-1}(gY)]\sim[\alpha^{-1}(g^{\prime}Y)]
for

and

We fix

and

Then
U^{\prime}_{0}

6.6
(applied to

(6.8)

f^{*}(h^{*}([Y]))=f^{*}([h^{-1}(Y_{0})])=[f^{-1}(gh^{-1}(Y_{0}))]\ \ \mbox{and}

Lemma 6.9
.

Let

7.
The Chern classes for singular surfaces

We fix an infinite field
k
and a connected equidimensional reduced
quasi-projective surface
X
over
k
. In this section, we shall
review the ring structure on
{\rm CH}^{L}_{0}(X)
due to Levine
[
Lev85b
]

and define Chern classes of vector bundles in this ring.

7.1.
Intersection product of Cartier divisors

For a closed subscheme
D\subset X
, we let
|D|
denote the support of

D
. We let
{\mathcal{K}}_{X}
denote the Zariski sheaf of total quotient rings on
X
.
We let
{\mathcal{Z}}^{1}(X,X_{\rm sing})
denote the subgroup of Cartier divisors

D\in H^{0}_{\rm zar}(X,{{\mathcal{K}}^{\times}_{X}}/{{\mathcal{O}}^{\times}_{X})}
such that

|D|\cap X_{\rm sing}
is finite.
We let
{\mathcal{R}}^{1}(X,X_{\rm sing})
be the subgroup of
{\mathcal{Z}}^{1}(X,X_{\rm sing})
generated by
principal Cartier divisors
{\rm div}(f)
, where
f\in H^{0}_{\rm zar}(X,{\mathcal{K}}^{\times}_{X})

is invertible at all generic points of
X_{\rm sing}
.
We let
{\rm CH}^{1}(X)={{\mathcal{Z}}^{1}(X,X_{\rm sing})}/{{\mathcal{R}}^{1}(X,X_{\rm sing})}
.
It is easy to check that
there is a canonical injective map
{\rm CH}^{1}(X)\hookrightarrow{\rm Pic}(X)

which takes a Cartier divisor
D
to the associated line bundle
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(D)
.
We let
{\rm CH}^{0}(X)
denote the free abelian group on the cycle

[X]
so that there is a canonical isomorphism

{\mathbb{Z}}\xrightarrow{\cong}{\rm CH}^{0}(X)
.
We let
{\rm CH}^{*}(X)={\rm CH}^{0}(X)\oplus{\rm CH}^{1}(X)\oplus{\rm CH}^{L}_{0}(X)
.
Then a ring structure on
{\rm CH}^{*}(X)
is completely determined by
defining the intersection product

(7.1)

{\rm CH}^{1}(X)\otimes{\rm CH}^{1}(X)\to{\rm CH}^{L}_{0}(X).

This construction is identical to the case of
smooth surfaces. We recall it here.
Let
D,E
be two effective Cartier divisors on
X
with no common
components. Let
x\in X_{\rm reg}
be a closed point. Recall that
the intersection multiplicity of
D
and
E
at
x
is defined as

i_{x}(D,E)=\ell(\mathcal{O}_{X,x}/\mathcal{O}_{X}(-D-E)_{x}).

The number
i_{x}(D,E)
is non-zero only if
x\in{\operatorname{Supp}}(D)\cap{\operatorname{Supp}}(E)
,
and satisfies the standard properties of symmetry and additivity.
In particular, if
|D|\cap|E|\subset X_{{\rm reg}}
, the product

(D\cdot E)=\sum_{x\in|D|\cap|E|}i_{x}(D,E)[x]

is a well-defined element of the Chow group
{\rm CH}^{L}_{0}(X)
.

Note that since
X
is quasi-projective, the map
{\rm CH}^{1}(X)\hookrightarrow{\rm Pic}(X)
is in fact an isomorphism. In fact, we can write up to linear
equivalence
D=D_{1}-D_{2}
as difference of effective and very ample Cartier
divisors without common components (e.g., see
[
Har77
, II Ex.7.5]
).
Since
k
is infinite, we can use the classical Bertini theorem
(e.g., see
[
Jou83
, 6.3 b)]
) to ensure
that
|D_{1}|\cap|D_{2}|\cap X_{\rm sing}=\emptyset
and

|D_{i}|\cap X_{\rm sing}
finite for
i=1,2
.
We do the same for
E
, which we write (up to linear equivalence) as

E_{1}-E_{2}
with the additional property that
D_{i}\cap E_{j}
is finite and
disjoint from
X_{{\rm sing}}
.
At this point, we can define

(D\cdot E)=(D_{1}-D_{2})\cdot(E_{1}-E_{2})=D_{1}\cdot E_{1}-D_{1}\cdot E_{2}-D_{2}\cdot E_{1}+D_{2}\cdot E_{2}.

It is straightforward to check that the product is
well-defined (i.e., it does not depend on the presentation of

D
and
E
). We leave out the details.

7.2.
Compatibility of product with pull-backs

Let
G
and
H
be as in §
6
. We let

{\rm CH}^{*}(H)=\oplus_{i\geq 0}{\rm CH}^{i}(H)
denote the classical Chow ring of
H
.
We assume that
\text{\rm dim}(H)\geq 2
.
Let
f\colon X\to H
be a morphism. We have a well-defined pull-back map

f^{*}\colon{\rm Pic}(H)\to{\rm Pic}(X)
. Equivalently, a pull-back map

f^{*}\colon{\rm CH}^{1}(H)\to{\rm CH}^{1}(X)
.
To give a more explicit description of this, note that
for any integral divisor

, we can apply
Theorem
4.8
(to

and

)
to find a dense open

such that
for every

, the scheme-theoretic pull-back

satisfies conditions (1)
\sim
(3) of Theorem

.
But this precisely means that

is an effective Cartier divisor
on

whose no irreducible component is contained in

.
It is then clear from the definition of
{\rm CH}^{1}(X)
in the beginning of

that

.

We have also defined the pull-back

Since

is identity as

we have a well-defined codimension preserving (we define

to be
zero on
{\rm CH}^{\geq 3}(H)
) group homomorphism
f^{*}\colon{\rm CH}^{*}(H)\to{\rm CH}^{*}(X)
.

Proposition 7.1
.

The map
f^{*}\colon{\rm CH}^{*}(H)\to{\rm CH}^{*}(X)
is a ring homomorphism.
If
\Psi=(\rho,h)\colon

(G,H)\to(G^{\prime},H^{\prime})
is a morphism as
in §
6.3
, then
(h\circ f)^{*}=f^{*}\circ h^{*}

as ring homomorphisms.

Proof.

We only need to show this for the product of cycles lying in

{\rm CH}^{1}(H)
. For this, we note that
{\rm CH}^{1}(H)\cong{\rm Pic}(H)

and we know that every line bundle on
H
is a difference of
two very ample line bundles. Furthermore, if

D
is a Cartier divisor on
H
such that
{\mathcal{O}}_{H}(D)
is very ample,
then the classical Bertini smoothness theorem over infinite fields
tells us that
D\sim D^{\prime}
, where
D^{\prime}
is a smooth effective Cartier
divisor on
H
. It follows therefore that
{\rm CH}^{1}(H)
is generated
by smooth very ample divisors.

Now, given two smooth very ample divisors
D_{1}
and
D_{2}
on
H
,
we can use the Bertini theorem of Jouanolou (see also

[
KA79
, Theorem 1]
) to find elements

D^{\prime}_{1}
and
D^{\prime}_{2}
in the linear systems
|H^{0}(H,{\mathcal{O}}_{H}(D_{1}))|

and
|H^{0}(H,{\mathcal{O}}_{H}(D_{2}))|
, respectively such that
D^{\prime}_{1}
and
D^{\prime}_{2}

are geometrically integral smooth schemes which
intersect transversely in a smooth and
(geometrically) integral codimension two subscheme
Y\subset H
.
We remind here that
H
is smooth and geometrically integral.
We therefore need to show that if
D_{1}
and
D_{2}
are two smooth
connected very ample effective Cartier divisors on
H
whose scheme-theoretic
intersection
Y
is smooth and integral codimension two cycle on

H
, then
[f^{*}(D_{1})]\cdot[f^{*}(D_{2})]=[f^{*}(Y)]
in
{\rm CH}^{L}_{0}(X)
.

We now apply Theorem
4.8
again.
We can then find an open dense subset
U_{i}
of
G
, for
i=1,2
such that,
for every
g\in U_{i}(k)
, the scheme-theoretic pull-back
f^{-1}(gD_{i})
is
an effective Cartier divisor on
X
having finite intersection with
X_{\rm sing}
.
By replacing
U_{1}
and
U_{2}
by their intersection,
we can assume that
U_{1}=U_{2}=U
and that, the same
g
works for both

D_{1}
and
D_{2}
.

Let
V(f,Y)
be the open subset of
Lemma
6.6
.
Then for every
g\in V(f,Y)(k)
, we have
f^{*}(D_{1}\cdot D_{2})=f^{*}[Y]=[f^{-1}(gY)]
, with
f^{-1}(gY)\cap X_{\rm sing}=\emptyset
.
Choosing
g\in(V(f,Y)\cap U)(k)
, we obtain

\begin{array}[]{lll}f^{*}([D_{1}]\cdot[D_{2}])=[f^{-1}(gY)]&=&[f^{-1}([gD_{1}]\cdot[gD_{2}])]\\
&=&[f^{-1}(gD_{1})]\cdot[f^{-1}(gD_{2})]\\
&=&f^{*}[D_{1}]\cdot f^{*}[D_{2}],\end{array}

as required (the second equality follows directly from the local definition
of the intersection product in §
7.1
).
The second assertion of the proposition follows directly from
Lemma
6.8
.
∎

7.3.
The Chern classes

Let
X
be as in the beginning of §
7
.
We now review the construction of Chern classes in
{\rm CH}^{*}(X)
of vector bundles
on
X
. This construction depends solely on the results of the previous
sections of this manuscript and does not use
any further information about the nature of the field
k
.
Hence, all the proofs in the construction of the Chern classes given
in
[
Lev85b
]
and
[
BS98
]
verbatim remain valid and we
have nothing extra to add.
We shall therefore only recall it very briefly and refer to
[
BS98
, § 5]

for details.

For any vector bundle
{\mathcal{E}}
of rank
n
on
X
, we let
c_{0}({\mathcal{E}})=1

and
c_{1}({\mathcal{E}})=c_{1}(\bigwedge^{n}({\mathcal{E}}))=[\bigwedge^{n}({\mathcal{E}})]\in{\rm Pic}(X)={\rm CH}^{1}(X)
. It is easy to check that

c_{1}({\mathcal{E}})=c_{1}({\mathcal{E}}^{\prime})+c_{1}({\mathcal{E}}^{\prime\prime})
if

0\to{\mathcal{E}}^{\prime}\to{\mathcal{E}}\to{\mathcal{E}}^{\prime\prime}\to 0

is an exact sequence of vector bundles.
These definitions are identical to the classical case.

We now recall the construction of
c_{2}({\mathcal{E}})
.
We first recall that for integers
0\leq n\leq r
,
the Grassmanian variety
{\text{\rm Gr}}_{k}(n,r)
is a representable functor
on
{\operatorname{\mathbf{Sch}}}_{k}
which to any
Y\in{\operatorname{\mathbf{Sch}}}_{k}
associates the set of
quotients
{\mathcal{O}}^{r}_{Y}\twoheadrightarrow{\mathcal{F}}
, where
{\mathcal{F}}
is locally free of
rank
n
on
Y
. Note that this construction holds over any base scheme
S
.
It is classically known that

{\operatorname{\rm GL}}_{r,k}
acts transitively on
{\text{\rm Gr}}_{k}(n,r)
and the latter
is projective. Hence, we have that
{\text{\rm Gr}}_{k}(n,r)\cong{{\operatorname{\rm GL}}_{r,k}}/P
with

P
parabolic.

Let us now assume that
{\mathcal{E}}
is globally generated by the sections

s_{1},\ldots,s_{r}\in H^{0}(X,{\mathcal{E}})
. Then it follows from the
above definition of
{\text{\rm Gr}}_{k}(n,r)
that there is a unique

k
-morphism
f\colon X\to{\text{\rm Gr}}_{k}(n,r)
such that
{\mathcal{E}}\cong f^{*}({\mathcal{Q}}_{n,r})
, where
{\mathcal{Q}}_{n,r}
is the universal quotient vector
bundle on
{\text{\rm Gr}}_{k}(n,r)
.
We let
c_{2}({\mathcal{E}}):=f^{*}(c_{2}({\mathcal{Q}}_{n,r}))
. Note that this makes sense
since we have a well-defined theory of Chern classes of vector bundles
on smooth schemes. The fact that this definition does not depend on the
choice of the chosen sections follows from the following.

Lemma 7.2
.

Let
\{t_{1},\ldots,t_{r}\}
and
\{g_{1},\ldots,g_{s}\}

be two sets of global sections
generating
\mathcal{E}
. Let
f_{1}\colon X\to{\text{\rm Gr}}_{k}(n,r)
and

f_{2}\colon X\to{\text{\rm Gr}}_{k}(n,s)
be the classifying morphisms.
Then
f_{1}^{*}(c_{2}(\mathcal{Q}_{n,r}))=f_{2}^{*}(c_{2}(\mathcal{Q}_{n,s}))
in

{\rm CH}^{L}_{0}(X)
.

Proof.

This is straightforward using
Lemmas
6.8
and
6.10

(e.g., see
[
BS98
, Lemma 10]
).
∎

Lemma 7.3
.

Suppose that
{\mathcal{E}}
is globally generated by
\{t_{1},\ldots,t_{r}\}
and
let
f\colon X\to{\text{\rm Gr}}_{k}(n,r)
be the associated classifying morphism.
Let
{\mathcal{L}}
be a line bundle on
X
globally generated by

\{g_{1},\ldots,g_{s}\}
. Let
h\colon X\to{\text{\rm Gr}}_{k}(n,rs)

be the classifying morphism for the vector bundle
{\mathcal{E}}\otimes_{{\mathcal{O}}_{X}}{\mathcal{L}}

and sections
\{t_{i}\otimes g_{j}|1\leq i\leq s,1\leq j\leq s\}
.
Then

(7.2)

h^{*}(c_{2}({\mathcal{Q}}_{n,rs}))=f^{*}(c_{2}(\mathcal{Q}_{n,r}))+(n-1)c_{1}(\mathcal{E})\cdot c_{1}(\mathcal{L})+\binom{n}{2}c_{1}(\mathcal{L})^{2}.

Proof.

This is straightforward using Lemma
6.8

and the theory of Chern classes on smooth schemes
(e.g., see
[
BS98
, Lemma 11]
).
∎

Let
\mathcal{E}
be a rank
n
vector bundle on
X
.
Let
{\mathcal{L}}
be a globally generated line bundle on
X
such that

\mathcal{E}\otimes_{{\mathcal{O}}_{X}}\mathcal{L}
is generated by global sections

t_{1},\ldots,t_{r}
. Let
f\colon X\to{\text{\rm Gr}}_{k}(n,r)
be the classifying
morphism given by the
t_{i}
’s. In view of
Lemmas
7.2
and
7.3
,
the following definition is meaningful.

Definition 7.4
.

We let

c_{2}({\mathcal{E}})=f^{*}(c_{2}({\mathcal{Q}}_{n,r}))-(n-1)c_{1}(\mathcal{E})\cdot c_{1}(\mathcal{L})-\binom{n}{2}c_{1}(\mathcal{L})^{2}.

We define the total Chern class of
{\mathcal{E}}
to be

c({\mathcal{E}})=1+c_{1}({\mathcal{E}})+c_{2}({\mathcal{E}}).

Note that the above definitions coincide with the classical definitions
of Chern classes if
X
happened to be non-singular.

Lemma 7.5
.

The following hold for vector bundles on
X
and their Chern
classes.

(1)

Suppose
H=G/P
is as in §
5.1
and
f\colon X\to H

is a morphism. If
{\mathcal{E}}
is a
G
-equivariant vector bundle on
H
, then

f^{*}(c({\mathcal{E}}))=c(f^{*}({\mathcal{E}}))
.

(2)

If
{\mathcal{E}}
is a vector bundle on
X
, then there exists

H=G/P
as in §
5.1
and a
G
-equivariant vector bundle

{\mathcal{E}}^{\prime}
on
H
such that
{\mathcal{E}}\cong f^{*}({\mathcal{E}}^{\prime})
.

(3)

If

0\to{\mathcal{E}}^{\prime}\to{\mathcal{E}}\to{\mathcal{E}}^{\prime\prime}\to 0

is an exact sequence of vector bundles on
X
, then

c({\mathcal{E}})=c({\mathcal{E}}^{\prime})\cdot c({\mathcal{E}}^{\prime\prime})
.

(4)

If
\pi\colon\widetilde{X}\to X
is a resolution of singularities of
X
,
then
c(\pi^{*}({\mathcal{E}}))=\pi^{*}(c({\mathcal{E}}))
.

Proof.

We first prove (1).
It suffices to prove the lemma for
c_{2}({\mathcal{E}})
as it is obvious for
other Chern classes by their definitions.
We now note that since
G
and
H
are smooth and connected,
it follows from
[
Tho87
, Lemma 2.10]
that
H
admits

G
-equivariant very ample line bundles. Hence, we can find a
very ample
G
-equivariant line bundle
{\mathcal{L}}
generated by its global sections
such that
{\mathcal{E}}^{\prime}:={\mathcal{E}}\otimes_{{\mathcal{O}}_{H}}{\mathcal{L}}

is also generated by its global sections.

Let
\{s_{1},\ldots,s_{r}\}
be a
k
-basis of

V:=H^{0}(H,{\mathcal{E}}^{\prime})
. Then
V
becomes a rational
representation of
G
, giving a group homomorphism

\rho\colon G\to{\operatorname{\rm GL}}_{k}(V)
. Furthermore, the classifying morphism

h\colon H\to{\text{\rm Gr}}_{k}(n,V)
given by the above basis of
V

is
G
-equivariant. It follows that
h\circ f:X\to{\text{\rm Gr}}_{k}(n,V)
is the classifying morphism for the vector bundle

f^{*}({\mathcal{E}}^{\prime})
with sections
\{f^{*}(s_{1}),\ldots,f^{*}(s_{r})\}
.
The item (1) of the lemma is clear for
h\circ f
by the construction of
the Chern classes on
X
. Since

{\mathcal{E}}^{\prime}=h^{*}({\mathcal{Q}}_{n,r})
, it follows from the theory
of Chern classes on smooth schemes that

c_{2}({\mathcal{E}}^{\prime})=h^{*}(c_{2}({\mathcal{Q}}_{n,r}))
.
We now conclude the proof of (1) by using
Lemmas
6.8
,

7.2
and
7.3
.

For (2), we choose a globally generated line bundle
{\mathcal{L}}

such that
{\mathcal{E}}\otimes_{{\mathcal{O}}_{X}}{\mathcal{L}}
is also globally generated.
This gives rise to the classifying morphisms

f_{1}\colon X\to{\text{\rm Gr}}_{k}(n,r)
and
f_{2}\colon X\to{\mathbb{P}}^{s}_{k}
for
some
r,s\geq 1
, where
n={\rm rank}({\mathcal{E}})
. We thus get a map

f\colon X\to{\text{\rm Gr}}_{k}(n,r)\times{\mathbb{P}}^{s}_{k}
, and it is clear from
various universal properties that
{\mathcal{E}}\cong f^{*}({\mathcal{Q}}_{n,r}\boxtimes{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{s}_{k}}(-1))
.

The item (3) is obtained exactly as (2).
Let
{\rm Fl}_{k}(n,n^{\prime\prime},r)
be the (partial) flag variety of
quotients
k^{r}\twoheadrightarrow V\twoheadrightarrow W
, where
V
and
W
are
k
-vector
spaces of ranks
n={\rm rank}({\mathcal{E}})
and
n^{\prime\prime}={\rm rank}({\mathcal{E}}^{\prime\prime})
,
respectively. Then
{\rm Fl}_{k}(n,n^{\prime\prime},r)
is the projective homogeneous
space for
{\operatorname{\rm GL}}_{r,k}
. This is a closed subvariety of

{\text{\rm Gr}}_{k}(n,r)\times{\text{\rm Gr}}_{k}(n^{\prime\prime},r)
and the projections to these
Grassmanian varieties define a universal exact sequence of

{\operatorname{\rm GL}}_{r,k}
-equivariant vector bundles

0\to{\mathcal{S}}\to p^{*}_{1}({\mathcal{Q}}_{n,r})\to p^{*}_{2}({\mathcal{Q}}_{n^{\prime\prime},r})\to 0.

We now choose a globally generated line bundle
{\mathcal{L}}

such that
{\mathcal{E}}\otimes_{{\mathcal{O}}_{X}}{\mathcal{L}}
is also globally generated.
Then
{\mathcal{E}}^{\prime\prime}\otimes{\mathcal{L}}
is globally generated too. As in (2),
this gives rise to the classifying morphism

f\colon{\rm Fl}_{k}(n,n^{\prime\prime},r)\times{\mathbb{P}}^{s}_{k}

such that

0\to{\mathcal{S}}\boxtimes{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{s}_{k}}(-1)\to p^{*}_{1}({\mathcal{Q}}_{n,r})\boxtimes{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{s}_{k}}(-1)\to p^{*}_{2}({\mathcal{Q}}_{n^{\prime\prime},r})\boxtimes{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{s}_{k}}(-1)\to 0

pulls back to the exact sequence of (3) on
X
via
f
.
We are now done by (1) and Proposition
7.1

because (3) is well-known for exact sequences of vector bundles on
smooth schemes.

The item (4) is again clear for
c_{0}
and
c_{1}
. For
c_{2}
, we let

f\colon X\to{\text{\rm Gr}}_{k}(n,r)
be the morphism obtained just above
Definition
7.4
. We let
\widetilde{f}\colon\widetilde{X}\xrightarrow{\pi}X\to{\text{\rm Gr}}_{k}(n,r)
be the composite map.
We then get

\begin{array}[]{lll}c_{2}\circ\pi^{*}({\mathcal{E}})=c_{2}\circ\pi^{*}\circ f^{*}({\mathcal{Q}}_{n,r})&=&c_{2}\circ\widetilde{f}^{*}({\mathcal{Q}}_{n,r})\ \ {=}^{1}\ \ \widetilde{f}^{*}\circ c_{2}({\mathcal{Q}}_{n,r})\\
&{=}^{2}&\pi^{*}\circ f^{*}\circ c_{2}({\mathcal{Q}}_{n,r})\ \ {=}^{3}\ \ \pi^{*}\circ c_{2}\circ f^{*}({\mathcal{Q}}_{n,r})\\
&=&\pi^{*}\circ c_{2}({\mathcal{E}}).\end{array}

In the above, the equality
{=}^{1}
follows from the known functoriality
of Chern classes on smooth schemes,
{=}^{2}
follows from
Lemma
6.9
and
{=}^{3}
follows from the item (1) of the
lemma. This finishes the proof of (4).

7.4.
The Chern classes on
K_{0}(X)

Let
X
be as in the beginning of §
7
. We let

{\rm CH}^{*}(X)^{\times}
denote the multiplicative group of units in
{\rm CH}^{*}(X)

whose codimension zero part is equal to 1 (see
[
Ful75
, § 15.3]
).
An immediate consequence of Lemma
7.5
(3) is that the
total Chern class defines a group homomorphism

(7.3)

c_{X}\colon K_{0}(X)\to{\rm CH}^{*}(X)^{\times},

where the left hand side uses the additive group structure of the ring

K_{0}(X)
.

Let
\widetilde{K}_{0}(X)
be the kernel of the the rank map

{\rm rk}\colon K_{0}(X)\twoheadrightarrow{\mathbb{Z}}
. There is
a canonical map
\det\colon\widetilde{K}_{0}(X)\to{\rm Pic}(X)
, induced by
taking a vector bundle to its determinant. This map is split
by the natural map
{\rm Pic}(X)\to\widetilde{K}_{0}(X)
that sends a line bundle

{\mathcal{L}}
to
[{\mathcal{L}}]-[{\mathcal{O}}_{X}]
. Let
SK_{0}(X)
denote the kernel of
\det
.
It follows that there is a natural decomposition

(7.4)

K_{0}(X)={\mathbb{Z}}\oplus{\rm Pic}(X)\oplus SK_{0}(X).

We let
x\in X_{\rm reg}
be a closed point and let
U=X\setminus\{x\}
.
Let
j\colon U\hookrightarrow X
be the inclusion and let
j^{*}\colon K_{0}(X)\to K_{0}(U)
be the induced map.
It is then clear that the rank map of
K_{0}(X)
factors through

K_{0}(U)
. Furthermore, as
x
is a regular closed point of
X
and

\text{\rm dim}(X)\geq 2
,
one knows that the map
{\rm Pic}(X)\to{\rm Pic}(U)
is an isomorphism.
Since
j^{*}([{\mathcal{O}}_{\{x\}}])=0
, it follows that the first and the
second components of
[{\mathcal{O}}_{\{x\}}]\in K_{0}(X)
under the decomposition
(
7.4
) are zero. We conclude that
the cycle class map of (
3.3
) canonically
factors through

(7.5)

cyc^{L}_{X}\colon{\rm CH}^{L}_{0}(X)\to SK_{0}(X).

We shall denote the image of the cycle class map
cyc^{L}_{X}
by
F^{2}K_{0}(X)
.
It also follows from the above discussion and the definitions
of Chern classes that
c_{i}(cyc^{L}_{X}([x]))=0

for
i=0,1
. In particular, we get
c_{X}\circ cyc^{L}_{X}([x])=c_{2}\circ cyc^{L}_{X}([x])
.
This also implies that
c_{2}\colon F^{2}K_{0}(X)\to{\rm CH}^{L}_{0}(X)
is
a group homomorphism.

Lemma 7.6
.

The composite map
{\rm CH}^{L}_{0}(X)\xrightarrow{cyc^{L}_{X}}F^{2}K_{0}(X)\xrightarrow{-c_{2}}{\rm CH}^{L}_{0}(X)
is identity.

Proof.

This is proven in
[
Lev85b
]
and
[
BS98
, Proposition 2]
.
We reproduce the latter proof for sake of completeness.
It is enough to check that
c_{2}\circ cyc^{L}_{X}([x])+[x]=0
if

x\in X_{\rm reg}
is a closed point.
Fix such a point and let
j\colon U=X\setminus\{x\}\hookrightarrow X
be as
above. Let
j^{*}\colon{\rm CH}^{L}_{0}(X)\to{\rm CH}^{L}_{0}(U)
be the
restriction map of Proposition
3.8
. Then it is clear from
the construction of the Chern classes that
j^{*}\circ c_{X}=c_{U}\circ j^{*}
.
It follows therefore from the above argument that
j^{*}\circ c_{2}\circ cyc^{L}_{X}([x])=0
. We conclude from Proposition
3.8
that

c_{2}\circ cyc^{L}_{X}([x])=m[x]
for some
m\in{\mathbb{Z}}
.
It remains to show that
m=-1
.

We let
\pi\colon\widetilde{X}\to X
denote a resolution of singularities
of
X
. This exists over all base fields since
\text{\rm dim}(X)=2
.
Since
x\in X_{\rm reg}\subset\widetilde{X}
, we see that

\pi^{*}([x])=[x]
under the pull-back map
\pi^{*}\colon{\rm CH}^{L}_{0}(X)\to{\rm CH}^{2}(\widetilde{X})
.
Lemma
7.5
(4) easily implies that

\pi^{*}\circ c_{2}\circ cyc^{L}_{X}([x])=c_{2}\circ\pi^{*}\circ cyc^{L}_{X}([x])
.
This yields

\begin{array}[]{lll}c_{2}\circ cyc_{\widetilde{X}}([x])&=&c_{2}\circ cyc_{\widetilde{X}}\circ\pi^{*}([x])\ {=}^{\dagger}\ c_{2}\circ\pi^{*}\circ cyc^{L}_{X}([x])\\
&=&\pi^{*}\circ c_{2}\circ cyc^{L}_{X}([x])=\pi^{*}(m[x])\\
&=&m\pi^{*}([x])=m[x],\end{array}

where
{=}^{\dagger}
follows from Proposition
3.9
.
We thus get
c_{2}\circ cyc_{\widetilde{X}}([x])=m[x]
on
\widetilde{X}
.
Since the lemma is well known for non-singular surfaces, this
forces
m=-1
.
∎

For a connected projective variety

.

Corollary 8.3
.

Let

be a smooth and connected projective surface over a finite

8.3.
The theorem of Kerz-Saito

Let
k
be a finite field of characteristic
p>0
.
Let
U
be a smooth and
connected quasi-projective scheme over
k
of dimension

d\geq 1
. Choose a compactification
U\subset X
with

X
normal and proper over
k
such that
C=(X\setminus U)_{\rm red}
is the
support of an effective Cartier divisor on
X
.
We let

(8.4)

C(U)={\underset{D}{\varprojlim}}\ {\rm CH}_{0}(X|D),

where the limit is taken over all effective Cartier divisors on
X

supported on
C
. We endow each
{\rm CH}_{0}(X|D)
with the discrete topology
and
C(U)
with the inverse limit topology.
It is known (e.g., see
[
KS16
, Lemma 3.1]
) that
C(U)
is independent of
the choice of the compactification
X
.
We have the degree map
\deg\colon C(U)\to{\mathbb{Z}}
which takes any
0-cycle to its degree. We let
C(U)^{0}={\rm Ker}(\deg)
.

Let
\pi^{{\rm ab}}_{1}(U)
denote the abelianized étale fundamental group
of
U
. We let
\pi^{{\rm ab}}_{1}(U)^{0}={\rm ker}(\pi^{{\rm ab}}_{1}(U)\to{\rm Gal}({\overline{k}}/{k}))
.
If
x\in U
is a closed point, then the inclusion

\iota_{x}\colon{\rm Spec\,}(k(x))\hookrightarrow U
defines the natural map

(\iota_{x})_{*}\colon\pi_{1}({\rm Spec\,}(k(x)))\to\pi^{{\rm ab}}_{1}(U)
.
Letting
\rho_{U}(x)=(\iota_{x})_{*}(F_{x})
(with
F_{x}
being the Frobenius
element of the Galois group of
k(x)
) and extending linearly,
we get a group homomorphism
\rho_{U}\colon{\mathcal{Z}}_{0}(U)\to\pi^{{\rm ab}}_{1}(U)
.
It follows from
[
KS16
, Proposition 3.2]
that this map induces a
reciprocity map
\rho_{U}\colon C(U)\to\pi^{{\rm ab}}_{1}(U)
.

If
D\subset X
is an effective Cartier divisor supported on
C
, we let

(8.5)

\pi^{{\rm ab}}_{1}(X,D)={\rm Hom}_{\rm cont}({\rm fil}_{D}H^{1}(U,{{\mathbb{Q}}}/{{\mathbb{Z}}}),{{\mathbb{Q}}}/{{\mathbb{Z}}}),

where
{\rm fil}_{D}H^{1}(U,{{\mathbb{Q}}}/{{\mathbb{Z}}})
is the group of continuous
characters
\chi\colon\pi^{{\rm ab}}_{1}(U)\to{{\mathbb{Q}}}/{{\mathbb{Z}}}
such that for any
integral curve
Z\subset U
, the restriction

\chi|_{Z}\colon\pi^{{\rm ab}}_{1}(Z)\to{{\mathbb{Q}}}/{{\mathbb{Z}}}
satisfies
the following inequality of Cartier divisors on
\overline{Z}^{N}
:

{\underset{y\in\psi_{Z}^{-1}(C)}{\sum}}{\rm art}_{y}(\chi|_{Z})[y]\leq\psi^{*}_{Z}(D).

Here,
\psi_{Z}\colon\overline{Z}^{N}\to X
is the projection map
from the normalization of the closure of
Z
in
X
and

{\rm art}_{y}(\chi|_{Z})
is the Artin conductor of the
restriction of
\chi
to
{\rm Gal}(k(Z)_{y})
, where
k(Z)_{y}

is the completion of
k(Z)
at
y
(see
[
Ser68
]
).

It is easy to check that there is an exact sequence

(8.6)

{\underset{Z\subset U}{\bigoplus}}\big({\underset{y\in|\psi^{-1}_{Z}(D)|}{\bigoplus}}G^{n_{y}}_{k(Z)_{y}}\big)\to\pi^{{\rm ab}}_{1}(U)\to\pi^{{\rm ab}}_{1}(X,D)\to 0,

where the sum on the left runs over all integral curves

Z\subset U
. Here,
\psi_{Z}^{*}(D)=\sum_{y\in|\psi^{-1}_{Z}(D)|}n_{y}[y]

and
G^{n_{y}}_{k(Z)_{y}}
is the higher ramification subgroup (for the upper
numbering) of

{\rm Gal}(k(Z)_{y})
.
The first map in (
8.6
) is the composite

G^{n_{y}}_{k(Z)_{y}}\to{\rm Gal}(k(Z^{N}))\to\pi_{1}(Z^{N})\xrightarrow{(\psi_{Z})_{*}}\pi^{{\rm ab}}_{1}(U).

It follows from (
8.5
) that

\pi^{{\rm ab}}_{1}({X},D)
is the unique quotient of

\pi^{{\rm ab}}_{1}(U)
which classifies all finite abelian Galois covers
of
U
whose ramification away from
U
is bounded by the divisor

D
.
It follows
[
EK12
, Proposition 3.9]
(see also
[
KS16
, Proposition 2.10]
)
that the limit of these quotient maps induces an
isomorphism

(8.7)

\pi^{{\rm ab}}_{1}(U)\xrightarrow{\cong}{\underset{D}{\varprojlim}}\ \pi^{{\rm ab}}_{1}(X,D).

We let
\rho_{(X,D)}
denote the composition

{\mathcal{Z}}_{0}(U)\xrightarrow{\rho_{U}}\pi^{{\rm ab}}_{1}(U)\twoheadrightarrow\pi^{{\rm ab}}_{1}(X,D)
.
It follows from the classical ramified class field theory for curves
that this descends to a map

\rho_{(X,D)}\colon{\rm CH}_{0}(X|D)\to\pi^{{\rm ab}}_{1}(X,D)
, compatible with

\rho_{U}
. Furthermore,

\rho_{U}={\underset{D}{\varprojlim}}\ \rho_{(X,D)}
.

We let
\pi^{{\rm ab}}_{1}(X,D)^{0}
denote the
kernel of the map
\pi^{{\rm ab}}_{1}(X,D)\to{\rm Gal}({\overline{k}}/{k}))\cong\widehat{{\mathbb{Z}}}
.
It follows from
[
KS14
, Corollary 1.2]

that
\pi^{{\rm ab}}_{1}(X,D)^{0}
is a finite abelian group.
The reciprocity map
\rho_{(X,D)}
restricts to a continuous homomorphism
of discrete abelian groups

\rho^{0}_{(X,D)}\colon{\rm CH}_{0}(X|D)^{0}\to\pi^{{\rm ab}}_{1}(X,D)^{0}
.
Moreover,
\rho^{0}_{U}={\underset{D}{\varprojlim}}\ \rho^{0}_{(X,D)}
.

Using (
8.7
) and the finiteness of

\pi^{{\rm ab}}_{1}(X,D)^{0}
, we get a commutative diagram
of exact sequences of
topological abelian groups and continuous group homomorphisms

(8.8)

\textstyle{0\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{C(U)^{0}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\rho^{0}_{U}}
\textstyle{C(U)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\rm deg}
\scriptstyle{\rho_{U}}
\textstyle{{\mathbb{Z}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{0\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\pi^{{\rm ab}}_{1}(U)^{0}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\pi^{{\rm ab}}_{1}(U)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\widehat{{\mathbb{Z}}},}

where the groups on the bottom have their canonical pro-finite topology
and the right-most vertical arrow is the pro-finite completion morphism.
The horizontal arrows on the right are surjective if
U
is
geometrically connected over
k
.

An easy consequence of (
8.4
),
(
8.6
) and
(
8.7
) is the following
(see
[
KS16
, Corollary 3.4]
).

Lemma 8.4
.

The map
\rho^{0}_{U}
is an isomorphism of topological abelian
groups if and only if

\rho^{0}_{(X,D)}\colon{\rm CH}_{0}(X|D)^{0}\to\pi^{{\rm ab}}_{1}(X,D)^{0}

is an isomorphism of finite abelian groups for all effective Cartier
divisors
D\subset X
which are supported on
C
.

The following result is due to Kerz and Saito
[
KS16
]

when
p\neq 2
.
We shall prove this using Theorem
8.2
and the Kato-Saito
class field theory.

Theorem 8.5
.

\rho^{0}_{U}
is an isomorphism of topological abelian groups.

Proof.

By using Wiesend’s trick (see
[
KS16
, Lemma 3.6]
), we can replace
the chosen compactification
X
by any of its alterations in the sense of
de Jong. We can therefore assume that
X
is smooth projective
and
C=X\setminus U
is a simple normal crossing divisor on
X
.

Suppose now that
d\geq 3
. By Lemma
8.4
,
it suffices to show that
\rho^{0}_{(X,D)}
is an isomorphism for
all
D
. Since
\pi^{{\rm ab}}_{1}(X,D)^{0}
is finite by

[
KS14
, Corollary 1.2]
, it follows immediately from
the Chebotarev-Lang
density theorem (e.g., see
[
Ser68
, Theorem 7]

or
[
Sza09
, Theorem 5.8.16]
)
that
\rho^{0}_{(X,D)}
is surjective. The heart of the
proof therefore is to show that

\rho_{(X,D)}\colon{\rm CH}_{0}(X|D)\to\pi^{{\rm ab}}_{1}(X,D)
is injective.

Let
\alpha\in{\rm CH}_{0}(X|D)
be
a 0-cycle such that
\rho_{(X,D)}(\alpha)=0
.
By a generalized version of Poonen’s Bertini theorem over
finite fields (see
[
GK20a
, Corollary 5.4]
),
we can find a very ample line bundle
{\mathcal{L}}
on

X
and a section
s\in H^{0}(X,{\mathcal{L}})
such that its zero locus

Y=Z(s)\subset X
is smooth,
Y\times_{X}C
is a simple
normal crossing divisor on
Y
and
|\alpha|\subset Y
.
Let
\iota\colon Y\hookrightarrow X
be the inclusion
and let
E=Y\times_{X}D
.
By the choice of
Y
, there exists
\alpha^{\prime}\in{\rm CH}_{0}(Y|E)
such that

\alpha=\iota_{*}(\alpha^{\prime})
.

We now have the diagram:

(8.9)

\textstyle{{\rm CH}_{0}(Y|E)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\rho_{(Y,E)}}
\scriptstyle{\iota_{*}}
\textstyle{\pi^{{\rm ab}}_{1}(Y,E)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\iota_{*}}
\textstyle{{\rm CH}_{0}(X|D)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\rho_{(X,D)}}

whose commutativity is immediate from
the definition of the reciprocity maps.

By
[
KS14
, Theorem 1.1]
, we can choose
{\mathcal{L}}
ample enough
(depending on
D
) so that the right vertical arrow
in (

) is an isomorphism.
It follows that
\rho_{(Y,E)}(\alpha^{\prime})=0
.
We have therefore inductively reduced the proof of the theorem
to the case when
d\leq 2
.

We shall now show that
\rho^{0}_{U}
is an isomorphism when
d\leq 2
.
Since we have already seen that the map
\rho^{0}_{(X,D)}\colon{\rm CH}_{0}(X|D)^{0}\to\pi^{{\rm ab}}_{1}(X,D)^{0}
is surjective
for all
D
supported on
C
and since
{\rm CH}_{0}(X|D)^{0}
is finite
by Corollary
8.3
(note that the
d=1
case of this finiteness
is classical), it follows that the
map
\rho_{U}^{0}\colon C(U)^{0}\to\pi^{{\rm ab}}_{1}(U)^{0}
is surjective.
It suffices therefore to show that the map

\rho_{U}\colon C(U)\to\pi^{{\rm ab}}_{1}(U)
is injective.

Let
\rho^{{\rm nis}}_{X|D}=\lambda_{(X,D)}\circ\rho_{X|D}\colon{\rm CH}_{0}(X|D)\to H^{d}_{{\rm nis}}(X,{\mathcal{K}}^{M}_{d,(X,D)})

be the cycle class map from (
8.2
) for any
D
.
Let
H^{d}_{{\rm nis}}(X,{\mathcal{K}}^{M}_{d,(X,D)})^{0}
be the image of

{\rm CH}_{0}(X|D)^{0}
under this map (see the proof of Corollary
8.3
).
It is clear from the definition of
\rho^{\rm nis}_{X|D}
(see §
3.4
)
that it is compatible
with the inclusions of effective Cartier divisors
D\subset D^{\prime}
.
We let
\widetilde{\rho}_{U}:={\underset{D}{\varprojlim}}\ \rho^{{\rm nis}}_{X|D}
.

We now consider our key diagram

(8.10)

\textstyle{C(U)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\rho_{U}}
\scriptstyle{\widetilde{\rho}_{U}}

\textstyle{\pi^{{\rm ab}}_{1}(U),}

where
\widehat{\rho}_{U}
is the reciprocity map of Kato-Saito

[
KS86
, § 3]
. The map
\widetilde{\rho}_{U}
is the inverse limit of
the cycle class maps
\rho^{{\rm nis}}_{X|D}
from
(

), taken
over effective Cartier divisors

.
It is immediate from the construction of
the three maps in (

that this diagram is commutative.

Since

is finite for all effective Cartier divisors

,
it follows that the canonical map

On the other hand, the reciprocity map

we conclude that

is injective.
This finishes the proof.
∎

The reciprocity maps

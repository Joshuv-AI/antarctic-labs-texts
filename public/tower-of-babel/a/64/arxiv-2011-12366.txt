1.
Introduction

One of the interesting problems in character theory of finite groups is determine the structure a group using information from its character table. Below are some results to this effect. Let
G
be a finite group and
p
be a prime. Let
{\mathrm{Irr}}(G)
denote the set of complex irreducible characters of
G
,
{\mathrm{cd}}(G)
the set of character degrees of
G
,
{\mathrm{cd}}_{p}(G)
the subset of
{\mathrm{cd}}(G)
consisting of character degrees divisible by
p
and
{\mathrm{cd}}_{p^{\prime}}(G)
the subset of
{\mathrm{cd}}(G)
consisting of those character degrees not divisible by
p
. A classical theorem of Thompson
[
17
, Corollary 12.2]
states that if
|{\mathrm{cd}}_{p^{\prime}}(G)|=1
, then
\textbf{O}^{pp^{\prime}}(G)=1
, where
\textbf{O}^{pp^{\prime}}(G)=\textbf{O}^{p^{\prime}}(\textbf{O}^{p}(G))
. Let
\textbf{O}^{pp^{\prime}pp^{\prime}}(G)=\textbf{O}^{pp^{\prime}}(\textbf{O}^{pp^{\prime}}(G))
. Recently, Giannelli, Rizo and Schaeffer Fry
[
13
]
proved a variation Thompson’s theorem by showing the following result:

Theorem 1.1
.

[
13
, Theorem A]

Let G be a finite group and let
p>3
be a prime. Suppose that
|{\mathrm{cd}}_{p^{\prime}}(G)|=2
. Then G is solvable and
\textbf{O}^{pp^{\prime}pp^{\prime}}(G)=1
.

We are interested in studying sets of vanishing elements with some restrictions corresponding to these results. An element
g\in G
is a vanishing element of
G
if there exists
\chi\in{\mathrm{Irr}}(G)
such that
\chi(g)=0
. The set of all vanishing elements of G is denoted by Van(G). A classical theorem of Burnside
[
17
, Theorem 3.15]
states that
{\mathrm{Van}}(G)
is non-empty if there is a non-linear
\chi\in{\mathrm{Irr}}(G)
. Let
{\mathrm{Vo}}(G)
denote the set of the orders of elements in
{\mathrm{Van}}(G)
,
{\mathrm{Vo}}_{p}(G)
be the subset of
{\mathrm{Vo}}(G)
consisting of orders of vanishing elements divisible by
p
and
{\mathrm{Vo}}_{p^{\prime}}(G)
be the subset of
{\mathrm{Vo}}(G)
consisting of those orders of vanishing elements not divisible by
p
.

Dolfi Pacifi, Sanus and Spiga proved the following result analogous to Thompson’s Theorem:

Theorem 1.2
.

[
8
, Corollary B]
Let
G
be a finite group and let
p
be a prime. Suppose that
{\mathrm{Vo}}_{p^{\prime}}(G)=\emptyset
. Then
\textbf{O}^{pp^{\prime}}(G)=1
.

As a variation of Theorem
1.2
, we study certain finite groups
G
such that
|{\mathrm{Vo}}_{p^{\prime}}(G)|=1
with an extra condition and obtain our first result.

Theorem A
.

Let
G
be a finite group and let
p
and
q
be distinct primes. Suppose that
|{\mathrm{Vo}}_{p^{\prime}}(G)|=1
. Then G is solvable. Moreover, suppose that one of the following holds:

(a)

{\mathrm{Vo}}_{p^{\prime}}(G)=\{b\}
such that
b
is divisible by at least two primes;

(b)

{\mathrm{Vo}}_{p^{\prime}}(G)=\{q^{n}\}
for some positive integer
n
and
Q^{\prime}
is subnormal, where
Q
is a Sylow
q
-subgroup of
G
.

Then
\textbf{O}^{pp^{\prime}pp^{\prime}}(G)=1
.

We remark that it is not clear if the hypothesis that
Q^{\prime}
is subnormal in
G
is necessary. In our proof of Theorem A, we use a theorem of Franciosi, de Giovanni, Heineken and Newell (see Lemma
2.9
) which states that if
G=AB
is a product of an abelian group
A
and a nilpotent group
B
, then
A\mathbf{F}(G)
is normal in
G
and
G
has Fitting height at most
3
. In general, it is well known that for any positive integer
n
, there is group of order
p^{a}q^{b}
and Fitting height
n
. Hence a Fitting height of a product of two Sylow subgroups can be arbitrarily large.

The opposite of Thompson’s theorem is the famous Ito-Michler theorem. It states that if
{\mathrm{cd}}_{p}(G)=\emptyset
, then
G
has a normal and abelian Sylow
p
-subgroup. As a generalization of this, Isaacs, Moréto, Navarro and Tiep
[
18
, Theorem A]
proved that if
|{\mathrm{cd}}_{p}(G)|=1
, then

is abelian and subnormal in
G
. In particular,
P/\textbf{O}_{p}(G)
is cyclic when

is solvable. Another generalization of the Ito-Michler theorem in terms of vanishing elements was proved by Dolfi, Pacifi, Sanus and Spiga
[
8
]
. They showed that if all
p
-elements are non-vanishing elements of
G
, then
G
has a normal Sylow
p
-subgroup and that the Sylow
p
-subgroup need not be abelian. Their result implies that if
{\mathrm{Vo}}_{p}(G)=\emptyset
, then
G
has a normal Sylow
p
-subgroup. As an analogue of the theorem of Isaacs, Moréto, Navarro and Tiep
[
18
, Theorem A(b)]
for solvable groups, we consider the following for solvable groups.

Theorem B
.

Let
G
be a finite solvable group. If
|{\mathrm{Vo}}_{p}(G)|=1
, then
P^{\prime}
is subnormal in
G
. In particular,
P/\textbf{O}_{p}(G)
is cyclic.

We now discuss our next problem. Bubboloni, Dolfi and Spiga studied finite groups such that every vanishing element is of order

for some integer

Theorem 1.3
.

[
5

Let

be a finite group and

for some integer

(a)

is a

(b)

is a Frobenius group with a Frobenius complement of

-power order and
\mathbf{Z}(G)=\textbf{O}_{p}(G)

Observe that groups described in Theorem

are such that
\textbf{O}^{pp^{\prime}}(G)=1
. Consider finite groups

\gcd(a,b)=p^{m}

Property (

Using the classification of finite simple groups, we show that if

:

Theorem C
.

Let

be a finite group and

be a prime. If

satisfies property (

Zhang, Li and Shao
[
26
]
studied finite non-solvable groups whose irreducible characters vanish only on elements of prime power order. We investigate finite solvable groups whose irreducible characters vanish only on elements of prime power order. This is another generalization of Theorem
1.3
. We show that either these groups have vanishing elements which are of
p
-power order or they are groups of order

for some primes

Theorem D
.

Let
G
be a finite non-abelian solvable group and

be distinct primes. If every irreducible character of

(i)

(ii)

is a Frobenius group with a Frobenius complement of

\textbf{Z}(G)=\textbf{O}_{p}(G)

(iii)

(iv)

Higman
[
16
]
described finite groups in which every element is of prime power order. In particular, he proved that the solvable groups with this property have orders that are divisible by at most two distinct primes. Hence Theorem D generalizes Higman’s result for solvable groups. Note that groups in Theorem D(ii) have orders that may be divisible by more than two distinct primes (see
[
5
, Proposition 5.4]
).

Preliminaries

In this section we shall list some properties of vanishing elements.

Lemma 2.1
.

[
22
, Lemma 2]

Let

(b)

If

The existence of

[

Let

be a prime. If

is a finite group of Lie type, or if

of

-defect zero.

Lemma 2.3

[
3
, Lemma 2.2]
Let

be a finite group,

and

Lemma 2.4

[
2
, Lemma 5]
Let

be a finite group, and

a minimal normal subgroup of

is isomorphic to a non-abelian simple group

Lemma 2.5
.

[
20
, Theorem 1.1]

Suppose that

is a minimal normal non-abelian subgroup of a finite group

. Then there exists an irreducible character
\theta
of

such that

is extendible to

\theta(1)\geqslant 5

Given a finite set of positive integers

the prime graph
\Pi(Y)
is defined as the undirected graph whose vertices are the primes

divisible by

are adjacent if and only if there exists an element of

divisible by

, is the prime graph
\Pi(\mathrm{Vo}(G))
. For a group

, let
\omega(G)
be the set of orders of elements of
G
. We shall state a result on non-solvable groups with disconnected vanishing prime graphs. Let

be the number of connected components of the graph

.

Theorem 2.6
.

[
9
, Theorem B]

Let
G
be a finite non-solvable group. If
\Gamma(G)
is disconnected, then
G
has a unique non-abelian composition factor
S
, and
n(\Gamma(G))\leqslant n(\Pi(\omega(S)))
unless

is isomorphic to
\mathrm{A}_{7}
.

We shall also state a result on solvable groups with disconnected vanishing prime graphs. We first recall two definitions:

A group

is said to be a
2
-Frobenius group
if there exists two normal subgroups

and
L
of

such that
G/F
is a Frobenius group with kernel
L/F
and
L
is a Frobenius group with kernel

.

A group

is said to be a

-Frobenius group
if there exist two normal subgroups

and

of

with the following properties:
F=F_{1}\times F_{2}
is nilpotent, where

and

are normal subgroups of

is a Frobenius group with kernel

,
G/F_{1}
is a Frobenius group with kernel

, and

is a
2
-Frobenius group.

Theorem 2.7
.

[
10
, Theorem A]

Let
G
be a finite solvable group. Then
\Gamma(G)
has at most two connected components. Moreover, if
\Gamma(G)
is disconnected, then

is either a Frobenius group or a nearly
2

The following is a classification of Frobenius complements.

Theorem 2.8
.

[
4
, Theorem 1.4]

Let

be a Frobenius group with Frobenius complement

. Then

has a normal subgroup

(a)

(b)

(e)

Lemma 2.9
.

[
12
, Lemma 3]

Let the finite group

be the product of an abelian subgroup

Abstract
. We investigate the behaviour of radial solutions to the Lin-Ni-Takagi problem in the ball B R ⊂ ℝ N B_{R}\subset\mathbb{R}^{N} for N ≥ 3 N\geq 3 : { − △ ​ u p + u p = | u p | p − 2 ​ u p in ​ B R , ∂ ν u p = 0 on ​ ∂ B R , \left\{\begin{aligned} -\triangle u_{p}+u_{p}&=|u_{p}|^{p-2}u_{p}&\textrm{ in }B_{R},\\ \partial_{\nu}u_{p}&=0&\textrm{ on }\partial B_{R},\end{aligned}\right. when p p is close to the first critical Sobolev exponent 2 ∗ = 2 ​ N N − 2 2^{*}=\frac{2N}{N-2} . We obtain a complete classification of finite energy radial smooth blowing up solutions to this problem. We describe the conditions preventing blow-up as p → 2 ∗ p\to 2^{*} , we give the necessary conditions in order for blow-up to occur and we establish their sharpness by constructing examples of blowing up sequences. Our approach allows for asymptotically supercritical values of p p . We show in particular that, if p ≥ 2 ∗ p\geq 2^{\ast} , finite-energy radial solutions are precompact in C 2 ​ ( B R ¯ ) C^{2}(\overline{B_{R}}) provided that N ≥ 7 N\geq 7 . Sufficient conditions are also given in smaller dimensions if p = 2 ∗ p=2^{\ast} . Finally we compare and interpret our results to the bifurcation analysis of Bonheure, Grumiau and Troestler in Nonlinear Anal. 147 (2016).



1.
Introduction

1.1.
Statement of the results

Let
R>0
and
B_{R}=B(0,R)
be the ball in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
N\geq 3
. We investigate the behaviour of families
(u_{p})_{p>2}
of radial solutions to the so-called Lin-Ni-Takagi problem in
B_{R}
:

(1.1)

\left\{\begin{aligned} -\triangle u_{p}+u_{p}&=|u_{p}|^{p-2}u_{p}&\textrm{ in }B_{R}\\
\partial_{\nu}u_{p}&=0&\textrm{ in }\partial B_{R}.\end{aligned}\right.

This equation can be derived from the Patlak-Keller-Segel system

(1.2)

\begin{cases}\dfrac{\partial u}{\partial t}=\Delta u-D_{1}\nabla\cdot(u\nabla\phi(v))\quad&\text{in }B_{R},\\[11.38109pt]
\dfrac{\partial v}{\partial t}=D_{2}\Delta v-D_{3}v+D_{4}u\quad&\text{in }B_{R},\\[5.69054pt]
\partial_{\nu}u=\partial_{\nu}v=0\quad&\text{on }\partial B_{R},\\
u,v>0&\text{in }B_{R},\end{cases}

where
D_{i}
,
i=1,\ldots,4
are positive constants and
\phi
is a smooth strictly increasing function. This system has been introduced in
[
KS70
]
to model chemotaxis, a situation where organisms move towards areas of high concentration of the chemicals they secrete. In this model
u(x,t)
represents the concentration of the considered organisms and
v(x,t)
the one of the chemical released. A huge literature exists on the dynamics of this system, concerning several fondamental questions such as the Cauchy theory, blow-up in finite or infinite time and stability to cite a few of them. Since it is not our purpose to analyse the time-depending solutions, we just refer to some recent papers
[
SW19
,
DdPD+19
,
CGMN19
,
Win20
]
and the many citations therein to trace the most important results in this direction. A large variety of highly nontrivial equilibria have been detected since the seminal work
[
LNT88
]
, see for instance

[
MM02
,
MNW05
,
MM07
,
DRW12
,
AWZ13
,
dPMM14
,
PV15
,
BGT16
,
dPPV16
,
BGNT16
,
BCN17a
,
BCN17b
,
BCR21
]
. This list of references is far from being exhaustive.
One can prove that stationary positive solutions satisfy

\nabla\cdot(u\nabla(\log u-D_{1}\phi(v))=0,

which, together with the boundary conditions, implies that
u=Ce^{D_{1}\phi(v)}
for some positive constant
C
. Taking
\phi(v)=\ln v
one recovers (
1.1
) for a suitable choice of parameters
D_{i}
and
C
. The Lin-Ni-Takagi problem has been intensively studied these last decades. We refer to
[
LNT88
,
LN88
,
AY91
,
BKP91
,
AY97
,
RW04
,
dPMP05
,
WWY10
,
WWY11
,
DRW12
]
for a non-exhaustive list of existence and non-existence results for (
1.1
), related to the so-called Lin-Ni conjecture, and mostly concerning the close-to-critical case. As mentioned by several experts of the community, the role of the many highlighted equilibria, as actually the role of any stationary solution, in the dynamics of the parabolic problem (
1.2
), seems unclear up to now. However, it is to expect that loss of compactness by bubbling in the stationary frame plays a role in the dynamics of (
1.2
). This issue has been investigated in other time-depending equations, as for instance in
[
LW08
,
KST09
,
DdPW20
,
CdPM20
,
JM20
,
dPMW21a
,
dPMW21b
]
and the included citations.

We will be interested in this work in the blow-up behaviour of radial solutions of (
1.1
) at the first critical exponent
p=2^{*}
, where we have let
2^{*}=\frac{2N}{N-2}
. Our main goal in this paper is to provide a complete description of the radial blow-up behaviour of (
1.1
) when
p\to 2^{*}
. This means giving sufficient conditions preventing the blow-up; describing
a priori
necessary condition in order for blow-up to occur; and constructing, when the necessary conditions are satisfied, radial blowing up sequences of solutions of (
1.1
). Our investigations are motivated, among other things, by the bifurcation analysis in
[
BGT16
]
, for which we refer to Section
1.2
below.

For simplicity we will work, in the following, up to a subsequence: we let
(p_{k})_{k\geq 0}
be a sequence of positive real numbers converging towards
2^{*}
as
k\to+\infty
and we let
(u_{k})_{k\geq 0}
be a sequence of
possibly sign-changing
radial solutions of the following equation:

(1.3)

\left\{\begin{aligned} -\triangle u_{k}+u_{k}&=|u_{k}|^{p_{k}-2}u_{k}&\textrm{ in }B_{R},\\
\partial_{\nu}u_{k}&=0&\textrm{ in }\partial B_{R},\end{aligned}\right.

which is just (
1.1
) with
p=p_{k}
.
We will always work with solutions that are
energy-bounded
in the following sense: we assume that there exists
C>0
such that

(1.4)

\|u_{k}\|_{L^{\alpha_{k}}(B_{R})}\leq C,

for all
k\geq 0
, where

\alpha_{k}=\max\big(2^{*},\frac{N}{2}p_{k}-N\big).

If
p_{k}\leq 2^{*}
, then
\alpha_{k}=2^{*}
and (
1.4
) implies that each
u_{k}
is smooth by the classical argument of
[
Tru68
]
(see also
[
BK79
]
).
Observe that in this case (
1.4
) is equivalent to a
H^{1}(B_{R})
bound on the sequence
(u_{k})_{k\geq 0}
. When
p_{k}>2^{*}
we have
\alpha_{k}>p_{k}
and thus assumption (
1.4
) is stronger than a uniform bound on the
H^{1}(B_{R})
norm. The exponent
\alpha_{k}
appears however naturally since it defines the precise norm which is preserved by the natural scaling that is formally associated to (
1.1
) : given
p>2
, the
L^{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
norm of
\lambda^{\frac{2}{p-2}}u(\lambda x)
is preserved if and only if
\alpha=\frac{N}{2}(p-2)
. Such an
\alpha
is also the exact exponent for which a natural bootstrap procedure in (
1.1
) does not improve the integrability of a solution. Observe also that when
p_{k}>2^{*}
in (
1.3
), the smoothness of the solutions is not guaranteed solely by (
1.4
) anymore. We thus assume in addition, if
p_{k}>2^{*}
, that
u_{k}
is smooth, by which we mean
C^{2}(\overline{B_{R}})
, for any
k\geq 0
. Smooth radial solutions of (
1.1
) for all
p
have for instance been constructed in
[
MNW05
,
ST11
,
BNW12
,
BST13
,
BGT16
]
. Singular solutions have been constructed in
[
Miy15
,
CF20
]
in the supercritical case.

We say that a sequence of smooth, possibly sign-changing, radial solutions
(u_{k})_{\geq 0}
of (
1.3
) blows up if

\lim_{k\to+\infty}\|u_{k}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{R})}=+\infty.

Since we deal with energy bounded sequences, Strauss lemma
[
Str77
]
equivalently implies

\lim_{k\to+\infty}|u_{k}(0)|=+\infty.

Standard elliptic theory and (
1.3
) show that if
(u_{k})_{k\geq 0}
does not blow up, then it is precompact in the
C^{2}(\overline{B_{R}})
topology. Our first result is a precompactness theorem that states that if the
p_{k}
’s approach the critical exponent
2^{*}
from above, then an energy-bounded sequence of solutions
(u_{k})_{k}
of (
1.3
) never blows up in dimensions
N\geq 7
.

Theorem 1.1

(Critical or supercritical case in dimensions
N\geq 7
)
.

Assume
N\geq 7
and let
(p_{k})_{k\geq 0}
be a sequence of positive real numbers such that
p_{k}\geq 2^{*}
for all
k\geq 0
and
\lim_{k\to+\infty}p_{k}=2^{*}
.
Any sequence
(u_{k})_{k\geq 0}
of
C^{2}(\overline{B_{R}})
radial solutions of (
1.3
) satisfying (
1.4
) is precompact in
C^{2}(\overline{B_{R}})
.

By standard elliptic theory, Theorem
1.1
implies that
(u_{k})_{k\geq 0}
strongly converges, up to a subsequence, in
C^{2}(\overline{B_{R}})
to a solution of (
1.1
) with
p=2^{*}
.
By the result of
[
Ni83
]
, radial solutions of (
2.2
) when
p_{k}\geq 2^{*}
have fixed sign (otherwise there would be a Dirichlet solution on some smaller ball and this is impossible in a star-shaped domain). In lower dimensions, we can cover the exactly critical case
p_{k}=2^{*}
for all
k\geq 0
.

Theorem 1.2

(Exactly critical case)
.

Let

p_{k}=2^{*}
for all
k\geq 0
.
Assume that
(u_{k})_{k\geq 0}
is a sequence of radial solutions of (
1.3
) that is uniformly bounded in
L^{2^{*}}(B_{R})
.

•

If
N\not\in\{3,6\}
, then
(u_{k})_{k\geq 0}
is precompact in
C^{2}(\overline{B_{R}})
.

•

If
N=3
, there exists an exceptional radius
R^{*}>0
such that if
R\neq R^{*}
, then
(u_{k})_{k\geq 0}
is precompact in
C^{2}(\overline{B_{R}})
.

•

If
N=6
there exists as increasing sequence of exceptional radii
(R_{\ell})_{\ell\geq 1}
, with
R_{\ell}\to+\infty
and
\liminf_{\ell\to+\infty}(R_{\ell}-R_{\ell-1})>0
, such that if
R\not\in\{R_{\ell}\}_{\ell\geq 1}
then
(u_{k})_{k\geq 0}
is precompact in
C^{2}(\overline{B_{R}})
.

The value of the exceptional radius
R^{*}
in dimension
N=3
is described in Section
3.1
. We believe that precompactness also holds for
R=R^{*}
but it would require an additional and tedious analysis to prove it, see Remark
3.2
.
In dimension
N=6
, the values of
(R_{\ell})_{\ell\geq 1}
are also univocally determined. We refer again to the proof of Theorem
1.2
in Section
3.1
.
Under an additional assumption which seems numerically true, we are able to fully prove precompactness, see Lemma
3.5
below and Remark
3.6
that follows.
This almost completely settles the exactly critical case
p_{k}=2^{*}
in (
1.3
) and leads to the following conjecture :

Conjecture 1.3

(Exactly critical case)
.

Any uniformly (energy) bounded sequence
(u_{k})_{k\geq 0}
of radial solutions of (
1.3
) with
p_{k}=2^{*}
for all
k\geq 0
, is precompact in
C^{2}(\overline{B_{R}})
.

The missing cases have just been mentioned :
N=3
and
R=R^{*}
in which case the conjecture is supported by Remark

and

and

Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
are the main precompactness results of this paper. They do not just follow from classical precompactness results for (possibly sign-changing) solutions of equations similar to (
1.1
), such as
[
DS02
]
.
There are two main novelties in our approach here: first, we include
sign-changing
solutions in our analysis, and second, we allow for asymptotically
supercritical
perturbations in (
1.3
). To our knowledge this seems to be the first
a priori
blow-up analysis for asymptotically supercritical solutions of equations like (
1.1
). We prove Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
by contradiction. Assuming that blow-up occurs we prove a sharp pointwise asymptotic description of blowing up sequences of solutions (see Proposition
2.1
below). The
radial symmetry
forces blow-up to occur in the form of finite sums of positive bubbles at the origin, with possibly alternating signs (Proposition
2.1
deals also with the subcritical case and sign-changing solutions exist in that case). This pointwise description is then used to rule out blow-up by means of Pohozaev identities. The ideas that we use in the proofs have been recently developed to prove compactness results for finite-energy sign-changing solutions of critical elliptic equations: we refer for instance to
[
GMR
,
PV22a
,
PV22b
]
, and to
[
PV19
]
for compactness results without bound on the energy.

The techniques that we use to prove Theorems
1.1
and

.

for all

be a sequence of

) satisfying (

•

N=6
.

When

, the blow-up occurs with a single positive bubble at

.

The value of the radius
R^{*}
in dimension

is as in Theorem

. We refer to Theorem

1.2.
Radial bifurcation analysis of (
1.1
)

We conclude this introduction by describing how Theorems
1.1
to
1.8
complement the radial bifurcation analysis of
[
BGT16
]
around the critical exponent
p=2^{*}
. In
[
BGT16
]
the authors have studied radial positive solutions of problem (
1.1
) bifurcating from the trivial solution
1
as the parameter
p
varies. Let
\lambda_{i}^{rad}
be the
i
-th eigenvalue of the operator
-\Delta+1
in
B_{R}
with Neumann boundary condition on radial solutions. Among other things, the authors have shown in
[
BGT16
]
that for every
i\geq 2
,
(2+\lambda_{i}^{rad},1)
is a bifurcation point for problem (
1.1
). If
\mathcal{B}_{i}
denotes the continuum of solutions that branches out of
(2+\lambda_{i}^{rad},1)
they also proved that

(i)

the branches
\mathcal{B}_{i}
are unbounded and do not intersect; close to
(2+\lambda_{i}^{rad},1)
,
\mathcal{B}_{i}
is a
C^{1}
-curve;

(ii)

if
u\in\mathcal{B}_{i}
then
u>0
;

(iii)

each branch consists of two connected components: the lower branch
\mathcal{B}_{i}^{-}
, along which
u(0)<1
, and the upper branch
\mathcal{B}_{i}^{+}
, along which
u(0)>1
;

(iv)

if
u\in\mathcal{B}_{i}
then
u-1
has exactly
i-1
zeros,
u^{\prime}
has exactly
i-2
zeros and each zero of
u^{\prime}
lies between two zeros of
u-1
;

(v)

the functions satisfying
u(0)<1
are uniformly bounded in the
C^{1}
-norm.

Figure 2.
Bifurcation diagram in dimension
N=4
. This figure is taken from
[
BGT16
]
with the courtesy of the authors.

The numerical simulations of
[
BGT16
]
seem to indicate that the upper branches
\mathcal{B}_{i}^{+}
can be split into two categories: those having a vertical asymptote at
p=2^{*}
, that we shall call “critical”, and those that have vertical asymptotes at supercritical exponents
p_{i}>2^{*}
, that we shall call “supercritical” : see e.g. Figure
1
of this paper and Figure
13
and Figure
16
of
[
BGT16
]
.

Theorems
1.6
to
1.8
provide explicit examples of solutions that, we believe, describe the critical upper branches constructed in
[
BGT16
]
. For instance, as Theorems
1.6
shows, positive radial solutions of type
B
exist for all dimensions
N\geq 4
in the subcritical case. These solutions take the value
1
only once and satisfy
u(0)>1
when
p
is close to
2^{*}
. They are good candidates to belong to the second upper branch
\mathcal{B}^{+}_{2}
but a proof of this fact seems quite difficult. Similarly, the branches
\mathcal{B}^{+}_{i}
, for
i\geq 3
, are made of functions that take
i+1
times the value
1
so it is natural to believe that they correspond to the solutions of the form
u_{0}^{i}+B
constructed in Theorems
1.7
and
1.8
, where
u_{0}^{i}
is a solution of (
1.6
) which takes
i
(if
u^{i}(0)<1
) or
i+1
(if
u^{i}(0)\geq 1
) times the value
1
. A weak limit
u_{0}^{i}
that satisfies
u_{0}^{i}(0)<1
and that takes
i
times the value
1
is for instance obtained by taking the intersection of the lower branch
\mathcal{B}^{-}_{i+1}
with the vertical line
p=2^{\ast}
. This always occurs for the
i\geq 1
satisfying
2+\lambda^{i}_{rad}<2^{\ast}
. Note that, for fixed
i\geq 1
,
\lambda^{i}_{rad}
goes to
0
as
R\to+\infty
. Up to assuming that
R
is big enough we can thus generically find weak solutions
u_{0}^{i}
that oscillate around
1
a fixed arbitrary number of times.

Thorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.2
also provide informations on the behaviour of the critical branches
\mathcal{B}^{+}_{i}
. We now explain how this sheds new light on the numerical simulations of
[
BGT16
]
. Consider Figure
1
of this paper, which covers dimension
N=4
. We consider the two branches
\mathcal{B}_{3}^{+}
and
\mathcal{B}_{4}^{+}
(those starting from
2+\lambda_{rad}^{3}
and
2+\lambda_{rad}^{4}
). These branches seem to blow up from the left of the vertical line
p=2^{*}
.
We assume that this is the case.
If we moreover assume that they have bounded
H^{1}(B_{R})
energy contradicts Theorem
1.4
: this Theorem indeed claims, since
N=4
, that solutions along
\mathcal{B}_{3}^{+}
and
\mathcal{B}_{4}^{+}
blow up with a single bubble at
0
. But then Proposition
2.1
below would show that these solutions only take the value
1
once, which is not the case. There are two possible explanations for this phenomenon. The first is that the solutions on the branches
\mathcal{B}_{3}^{+}
and
\mathcal{B}_{4}^{+}
in the figure all have finite energy and actually cross the line
p=2^{\ast}
for very large values of
u(0)
that are not in the figure; and they then blow up from the right of the vertical line
p=2^{*}
according to Theorem
1.8
, or may even blow up at a larger supercritical exponent
p_{*}>2^{*}
. The second is that the solutions along
\mathcal{B}_{3}^{+}
and
\mathcal{B}_{4}^{+}
do blow up from the left of
p=2^{*}
but have infinite energy as
u(0)\to+\infty
along the branch. The latter situation could correspond, for instance, to an infinite tower of bubbles centered at
0
glued to a non-zero weak limit
u_{0}
as
p\to 2^{*}
,
p\leq 2^{*}
. Note however that infinite tower of bubbles as
p\to 2^{*}
are not known to exist in the context of (
1.1
) yet. In the same vein, Theorem
1.2
implies that the finite-energy (in the sense of (
1.4
)) branches that blow up along the vertical line
p=2^{*}
can only oscillate around it a finite number of times (dimension
3
and
6
are not fully covered by Theorem
1.2
). If Conjecture
1.3
holds, it completely rules out a snaking behaviour of branches of finite-energy solutions around
p=2^{*}
.

We also expect the supercritical branches to correspond to the solutions that have been constructed in
[
CF20
]
when
p>2^{\ast}
. These are positive radial solutions blowing up at the origin and intersecting an arbitrary number of time the value
1
. These solutions were proved to have infinite Morse index if their vertical asymptote
p
satisfies
2^{\ast}-1<p<p_{JL}
, where
p_{JL}
is the Joseph and Lundgren exponent (see
[
JL73
]
) and have finite Morse index if
p>p_{JL}
. This suggests that the branches with vertical asymptote between
2^{\ast}-1
and
p_{JL}
oscillates infinitely many times around the vertical line
p
, a phenomenon which is also observed numerically see Figure
1
. We refer to
[
BCF20
]
for similar results related to the Keller-Segel equation. In this case, the Joseph and Lundgren exponent is replaced by a condition on the dimension of
B_{R}
.

The previous considerations describe the behaviour of the branches
\mathcal{B}_{i}
in the direction where
u(0)
blows up. It is also natural to investigate what happens on the other end of
\mathcal{B}_{i}
, i.e. when
p\rightarrow\infty
. We believe that the solutions corresponding to the lower branches
\mathcal{B}_{i}^{-}
are the solutions that have been constructed in
[
BGNT16
]
. There the authors prove, when
p\rightarrow\infty
, the existence of radial positive solutions concentrating on an arbitrary number of spheres and thus intersecting an arbitrary number of times the value
1
and whose value at the origin is strictly less than
1
as
p\rightarrow\infty
. Let us finally mention that analogous results have also been obtained for the Keller-Segel equation in
[
BCN17b
]
, where the polynomial nonlinearity is replaced by
e^{\mu(u-1)}
and the bifurcation analysis is done with respect to the parameter
\mu>0
.

1.3.
Organization of the paper

In Section
2
we prove a sharp pointwise asymptotic description of finite-energy solutions of (
1.3
), namely Proposition
2.1
. This is, to our knowledge, the first sharp a priori asymptotic pointwise description of smooth solutions of (
1.3
) when
p_{k}>2^{*}
,
p_{k}\to 2^{*}
.
We apply it to obtain necessary condition for the existence of blowing up solutions of (
1.3
) in Section
3
in a general setting (Theorem
3.1
). We prove Theorems
1.1
to
1.5
as a consequence. We finally prove Theorems
1.6
to
1.8
in Section
4
.

2.
A priori analysis of finite-energy solutions of (
1.3
)

Let
N\geq 3
and let
(p_{k})_{k\geq 0}
be a sequence of positive numbers,
p_{k}\geq 2
, converging to
2^{*}=\frac{2N}{N-2}
as
k\to+\infty
. For
R>0
we will denote by
B_{R}\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
the euclidean ball centered at
0
and of radius
R
. Throughout this section we let
(u_{k})_{k\geq 0}
be a sequence of possibly sign-changing radial solutions of (
1.3
) in
C^{2}(\overline{B_{R}})
satisfying (
1.4
). A consequence of (
1.3
) and (
1.4
) is that
u_{k}
weakly converges in
H^{1}(B_{R})
and
L^{2^{*}}(B_{R})
towards some weak limit
u_{0}
, that solves (
1.1
) with
p=2^{*}
and is hence at least in
C^{2}(\overline{B_{R}})
.

We assume throughout this section that the sequence
(u_{k})_{k\geq 0}
blows up, that is

(2.1)

\lim_{k\to+\infty}\|u_{k}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{R})}=+\infty

up to a subsequence. For any
k\geq 0
, since
u_{k}
is radial, it satisfies the ODE

(2.2)

\left\{\begin{aligned} -u_{k}^{\prime\prime}(r)-\frac{N-1}{r}u_{k}^{\prime}(r)+u_{k}(r)&=|u_{k}(r)|^{p_{k}-2}u_{k}(r)&\textrm{ in }[0,R]\\
u_{k}^{\prime}(0)=u_{k}^{\prime}(R)&=0.&\end{aligned}\right.

Since the energy

(2.3)

r\mapsto E_{k}(r):=\frac{|u_{k}^{\prime}(r)|^{2}}{2}+\frac{|u_{k}(r)|^{p_{k}}}{p_{k}}-\frac{|u_{k}(r)|^{2}}{2}

is nonincreasing in
[0,R]
,

\max_{B_{R}}|u_{k}|=|u_{k}(0)|
, hence
|u_{k}(0)|\to+\infty
as
k\to+\infty
by (
2.1
). Let
1\leq\ell\in\mathbb{N}
and
(\mu_{1,k})_{k\geq 0}
,
\dots
,
(\mu_{\ell,k})_{k\geq 0}
be
\ell
sequences of positive numbers converging to
0
as
k\to+\infty
. For
1\leq i\leq\ell
, we adopt the notations

(2.4)

B_{i,k}(r)=\big(N(N-2)\big)^{\frac{N-2}{4}}\left(\frac{\mu_{i,k}}{\mu_{i,k}^{2}+r^{2}}\right)^{\frac{N-2}{2}}\quad\textrm{ and }\quad\tilde{B}_{i,k}(r)=\mu_{i,k}^{\frac{N-2}{2}-\frac{2}{p_{k}-2}}B_{i,k}(r),

(2.5)

B_{0}(r)=\big(N(N-2)\big)^{\frac{N-2}{4}}\Big(1+r^{2}\Big)^{1-\frac{N}{2}},

where
r\geq 0
.
It is well-known that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}B_{0}^{2^{*}}dx=K_{N}^{-N},

where
K_{N}
is the sharp constant for the embedding of
\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
(the homogeneous Sobolev space) into
L^{2^{\ast}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
given by

(2.6)

K_{N}=\sqrt{\frac{4}{N(N-2)\omega_{N}^{\frac{2}{N}}}}.

Since
p_{k}\to 2^{*}
, observe that
\frac{N-2}{2}-\frac{2}{p_{k}-2}\to 0
as
k\to+\infty
, and
\tilde{B}_{i,k}
has to be understood as a perturbation of
B_{i,k}
that takes into account the asymptotic criticality of (
2.2
). The main result of this section is the following, sharp pointwise asymptotic description of
u_{k}
:

Proposition 2.1
.

Assume that
(u_{k})_{k\geq 0}
satisfies (
2.1
) (
2.2
) and (
1.4
) and let
u_{0}
be its weak limit in
H^{1}(B_{R})
. There exist a sequence
(\varepsilon_{k})_{k\geq 0}
converging to
0
,
C>0
, an integer
L\geq 1
,
L
sequences
(\mu_{1,k})_{k\geq 0},\dots,(\mu_{L,k})_{k\geq 0}
of positive numbers satisfying
\mu_{i,k}=o(\mu_{i+1,k})
for any
1\leq i\leq L-1
and a family
(\kappa_{i})_{1\leq i\leq L}\in\{\pm 1\}^{L}
such that

\left|u_{k}(r)-u_{0}(r)-\sum_{i=1}^{L}\kappa_{i}\tilde{B}_{i,k}(r)\right|\leq\varepsilon_{k}\Big(\|u_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{R})}+\sum_{i=1}^{L}\tilde{B}_{i,k}(r)\Big)+C\mu_{L,k}^{N-2-\frac{2}{p_{k}-2}}

for any
k\geq 0
and every
r\in[0,R]
.

Proposition
2.1
implies that for any sequence
(r_{k})_{k\geq 0}
of points of
[0,R]
, we have

u_{k}(r_{k})=u_{0}(r_{k})+\sum_{i=1}^{L}\kappa_{i}\tilde{B}_{i,k}(r_{k})+o\Big(\|u_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{R})}+\sum_{i=1}^{L}\tilde{B}_{i,k}(r_{k})\Big)+O(\mu_{L,k}^{N-2-\frac{2}{p_{k}-2}})

as
k\to+\infty
. In other words,
u_{k}
looks like, at a pointwise level and in strong spaces, the sum of alternating positive bubbles centred at
0
and concentrating at different rates.

We prove Proposition
2.1
in the general context of possibly
sign-changing
solutions, and not just positive ones. Pointwise descriptions of finite-energy blowing up
positive
sequences of solutions of equations like (
1.3
) have been known to hold for a while. A general result is available in
[
DHR04
]
, and other possible references are
[
LZ99
,
Dru03
,
Heb14
]
. Specific reference for the Lin-Ni-Takagi problem are
[
DRW12
,
Thi16
]
. Very recently, in
[
Pre21
]
, this type of results has been extended to the general setting of possibly
sign-changing
solutions, and applications to the precompactness of sign-changing solutions of Schrödinger-Yamabe type equation on manifolds have been given in
[
PV22b
,
PV22a
]
. An analogous result for sign-changing solutions of Hardy-Schrödinger type equations is in
[
GMR
]
.

The proof of Proposition
2.1
goes through an iterative construction of bubbling scales. The radiality of
u_{k}
rules out non-trivial sign-changing bubbles and forces
u_{k}
to blow up, at the origin, as a sum of positive bubbles with possibly alternating signs. This observation allows us to adapt in the context of (
1.3
) the proof of
[
GMR
]
, which is our main inspiration. Note that our proof does not assume the existence of a Struwe-type decomposition
[
Str84
]
for solutions of (
1.3
): for this reason it also works in the asymptotically supercritical case
p_{k}\geq 2^{*}
(when (
1.4
) is satisfied).

The remaining part of the section is dedicated to the proof of Proposition
2.1
. The proof goes through several steps. The arguments in what follows are written using the notations from (
1.3
) since many of them would adapt to the non-radial case. We however explicitly mention when radiality is crucially used.

Step
1
: A global scale-invariant estimate.
We claim that there exists
C>0
such that, for all
k\geq 0
and
r\in[0,R]
,

(2.7)

r^{\frac{2}{p_{k}-2}}|u_{k}(r)|\leq C.

In the case where
p_{k}\leq 2^{*}
for all
k\geq 0
, the estimate (
2.7
) follows directly from Strauss’s lemma
[
Str77
]
in
H^{1}(B_{R})
, i.e.

r^{\frac{2}{p_{k}-2}}|u_{k}(r)|\leq R^{\frac{2p_{k}-N(p_{k}-2)}{2(p_{k}-2)}}r^{\frac{N-2}{2}}|u_{k}(r)|\leq C.

When
p_{k}<2^{*}
, the power
\frac{2}{p_{k}-2}
is clearly not sharp.
In the supercritical case
p_{k}>2^{*}
, we need a preliminary step. The assumption (
1.4
) implies
(u_{k})_{k}
is bounded in
W^{2,s}(B_{R})
with
s=\frac{N(p_{k}-2)}{2p_{k}-2}
(observe this exponent is strictly bigger than
1
). By Sobolev embeddings,
(u_{k})_{k}
is therefore bounded in
W^{1,t}(B_{R})
with
t=\frac{Ns}{N-s}=\frac{N(p_{k}-2)}{p_{k}}
. Now, Strauss’s radial lemma in
W^{1,t}(B_{R})
, see for instance
[
Lio82
, Corollaire II.1]
, implies

r^{\frac{N-t}{t}}|u_{k}(r)|=r^{\frac{2}{p_{k}-2}}|u_{k}(r)|\leq C.

In the case
p_{k}>2^{*}
, the power
\frac{2}{p_{k}-2}
is probably sharp.

A first consequence of (
1.3
), (
2.7
) and standard elliptic theory is that

(2.8)

u_{k}\to u_{0}\quad\text{ in }C^{2}([\delta,R]),\text{ for any fixed }\delta>0

as
k\to+\infty
. Hence blow-up only occurs at
0
. We now iteratively construct the blow-up scales of
(u_{k})_{k\geq 0}
. If
\ell\geq 1
is an integer we consider the following property that we denote by
(H_{\ell})
:

(
H_{\ell}
)

\begin{array}[]{l}\textrm{there exist }\ell\textrm{ sequences }(\mu_{1,k})_{k\geq 0},\dots,(\mu_{\ell,k})_{k\geq 0}\textrm{ of positive numbers }\textrm{ satisfying: }\\[5.69054pt]
\quad\bullet\ \mu_{i,k}=o(\mu_{i+1,k})\text{ as }k\to+\infty\text{ for any }1\leq i\leq\ell-1\text{ and }\lim_{k\to+\infty}\mu_{\ell,k}=0\\[5.69054pt]
\quad\bullet\ \mu_{i,k}^{\frac{2}{p_{k}-2}}u_{k}\big(\mu_{i,k}\,\cdot)\to\kappa_{i}B_{0}\text{ in }C^{2}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash\{0\})\text{ for some }\kappa_{i}\in\{-1,1\}\\[5.69054pt]
\text{ as }k\to+\infty\text{ for any }1\leq i\leq\ell.\\
\end{array}

Step
2
:
(H_{1})
holds true.
By (
2.1
) and (
2.3
),
\max_{B_{R}}|u_{k}|=|u_{k}(0)|\to+\infty
as
k\to+\infty
. We can thus let

(2.9)

\mu_{1,k}=\big(N(N-2)\big)^{\frac{(N-2)(p_{k}-2)}{8}}|u_{k}(0)|^{-\frac{p_{k}-2}{2}},

and
\mu_{1,k}\to 0
as
k\to+\infty
. The sequence
v_{1,k}(x):=\mu_{1,k}^{\frac{2}{p_{k}-2}}u_{k}\big(\mu_{1,k}x\big)
, defined for
x\in B_{\frac{R}{\mu_{1,k}}}
then satisfies
|v_{1,k}(0)|=\big(N(N-2)\big)^{\frac{N-2}{4}}
,
\|v_{1,k}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{\frac{R}{\mu_{1,k}}})}\leq\big(N(N-2)\big)^{\frac{N-2}{4}}
and, by (
1.3
),

-\triangle v_{1,k}+\mu_{1,k}^{2}v_{1,k}=v_{1,k}^{p_{k}-1}\quad\text{ in }B_{\frac{R}{\mu_{1,k}}}.

By standard elliptic theory, and since
u_{k}
is radial,
v_{1,k}
converges in
C^{2}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, up to a subsequence, towards a radial solution
v_{1}
of
-\triangle v_{1}=|v_{1}|^{2^{*}-2}v_{1}
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
with
|v_{1}(0)|=\big(N(N-2)\big)^{\frac{N-2}{4}}
and
\|v_{1}\|_{{}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq\big(N(N-2)\big)^{\frac{N-2}{4}}
. By the Pohozaev identity
[
Poh65
]
there do not exist radial sign-changing solutions of this equation in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
. Hence
v_{1}
is of constant sign, and by the classification result of
[
CGS89
]
there exists
\kappa_{1}\in\{\pm 1\}
such that
v_{1}(r)=\kappa_{1}B_{0}(r)
, where
B_{0}
is as in (
2.5
). Since
v_{1,k}\to B_{0}
in
C^{2}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
this proves
(H_{1})
.

Step
3
: the induction property.

Step
3.1
.
We claim that the following holds true: assume that
(H_{\ell})
is true for some
\ell\geq 1
. Then either

(2.10)

\lim_{M\to+\infty}\limsup_{k\to+\infty}\max_{[M\mu_{\ell,k},R]}r^{\frac{2}{p_{k}-2}}|u_{k}(r)-u_{0}(r)|=0

or
(H_{\ell+1})
is true.

Proof of (
2.10
).

We proceed by contradiction and, up to passing to a subsequence, assume that there exists
s_{k}\in(0,R]
with
s_{k}>>\mu_{\ell,k}
and

(2.11)

s_{k}^{\frac{2}{p_{k}-2}}|u_{k}(s_{k})-u_{0}(s_{k})|\to a>0

as
k\to+\infty
. Remark that the right-hand side of (
2.11
) is always finite by (
2.7
). By (
2.8
)
s_{k}\to 0
, thus
s_{k}^{\frac{2}{p_{k}-2}}|u_{k}(s_{k})|\to a
and
|u_{k}(s_{k})|\to+\infty
. For all
r\in[0,\frac{R}{s_{k}}]
, we let

w_{k}(r)=s_{k}^{\frac{2}{p_{k}-2}}u_{k}\big(s_{k}r\big).

By (
1.3
) and (
2.7
)
w_{k}
satisfies
-\triangle w_{k}+s_{k}^{2}w_{k}=|w_{k}|^{p_{k}-2}w_{k}
in
B_{\frac{R}{s_{k}}}
and
|w_{k}(r)|\leq Cr^{-\frac{2}{p_{k}-2}}
for any
0<r\leq\frac{R}{s_{k}}
. By standard elliptic theory
w_{k}
thus converges, in
C^{2}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash\{0\})
, towards a function
w_{0}
satisfying
|w_{0}(1)|=a>0
and

-\triangle w_{0}=|w_{0}|^{2^{*}-2}w_{0}\quad\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash\{0\}.

Using (
1.4
) we have, for
k\geq 0
and for all
0<\delta<1
fixed,

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\frac{1}{\delta}}\backslash B_{\delta}}|w_{0}|^{2^{*}}dx

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{\frac{1}{\delta}}\backslash B_{\delta}}|w_{k}|^{\alpha_{k}}dx+o(1)

\displaystyle=s_{k}^{\frac{2\alpha_{k}}{p_{k}-2}-N}\int_{B_{\frac{\nu_{\ell+1,k}}{\delta}}\backslash B_{\delta\nu_{\ell+1,k}}}|u_{k}|^{\alpha_{k}}dx+o(1)

\displaystyle\leq Cs_{k}^{\frac{2\alpha_{k}}{p_{k}-2}-N}+o(1).

As explained in the proof of Step
1
we have
s_{k}^{\frac{2\alpha_{k}}{p_{k}-2}-N}\leq 1
so that, after passing to the limit as
k\to+\infty
and then as
\delta\to 0
,
w_{0}\in L^{2^{*}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. Classical removable singularity results (see e.g. Lemma
2.1
in
[
CGS89
]
) show that
w_{0}
satisfies
-\triangle w_{0}=|w_{0}|^{2^{*}-2}w_{0}
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
and is thus at least of class
C^{2}
. As in the proof of Step
2
, since
u_{k}
is radial
w_{0}
is also radial and in particular does not change sign. Since
|w_{0}(1)|=a>0
the classification result of
[
CGS89
]
again shows that

w_{0}(r)=\kappa_{\ell+1}\frac{\lambda^{\frac{N-2}{2}}}{\big(\lambda^{2}+\frac{r^{2}}{N(N-2)}\big)^{\frac{N-2}{2}}}

for some
\lambda>0
depending on
a
and some
\kappa_{\ell+1}\in\{\pm 1\}
. It remains to let

\mu_{\ell+1,k}=\big(N(N-2)\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\lambda s_{k}.

Since
s_{k}>>\mu_{\ell,k}
and
s_{k}=o(1)
this proves that
(H_{\ell+1})
is satisfied for the sequences
(\mu_{1,k})_{k\geq 0},\dots,(\mu_{\ell,k})_{k\geq 0}
and
(s_{k})_{k\geq 0}
.
∎

Step
3.2
.
We claim that the following holds true: assume that
(H_{\ell})
is true for some
\ell\geq 1
and let
\delta>0
. Then either

(2.12)

\lim_{M\to+\infty}\limsup_{k\to+\infty}\max_{[M\mu_{i,k},\delta\mu_{i+1,k}]}r^{\frac{2}{p_{k}-2}}|u_{k}(r)-\tilde{B}_{i+1,k}(r)|=0

for all
1\leq i\leq\ell-1
or
(H_{\ell+1})
is true.

Proof of (
2.12
).

We proceed again by contradiction and assume that there exists
i\in\{1,\dots,\ell-1\}
and a sequence
(s_{k})_{k\geq 0}
with
\frac{s_{k}}{\mu_{i,k}}\to+\infty
and
s_{k}\leq\delta\mu_{i+1,k}
such that, up to a subsequence,

(2.13)

\lim_{k\to+\infty}s_{k}^{\frac{2}{p_{k}-2}}|u_{k}(s_{k})-\tilde{B}_{i+1,k}(s_{k})|=a>0.

Note that the limit is finite by (
2.4
) and (
2.7
). By the second bullet in
(H_{\ell})
we also have
s_{k}=o(\mu_{i+1,k})
, and hence (
2.13
) becomes, with (
2.4
),

\lim_{k\to+\infty}s_{k}^{\frac{2}{p_{k}-2}}|u_{k}(s_{k})|=a>0.

For all
r\in[0,\frac{R}{s_{k}}]
, we let

w_{k}(r)=s_{k}^{\frac{2}{p_{k}-2}}u_{k}\big(s_{k}r\big).

Mimicking the proof of (
2.10
) shows that
(H_{\ell+1})
is then satisfied for the sequences
(\mu_{1,k})_{k\geq 0},\dots,(\mu_{i,k})_{k\geq 0}
,
\big(\big(N(N-2)\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\lambda s_{k}\big)_{k\geq 0}
,
(\mu_{i+1,k})_{k\geq 0},\dots,(\mu_{\ell,k})_{k\geq 0}
, where
\lambda
is some positive number that depends on
a
.
∎

Step
4
: the number of concentration points is finite.
We let

L=\max\{\ell\geq 1\text{ such that }(H_{\ell})\text{ holds true}\}.

We claim that
L<+\infty
and that, for any fixed
\delta>0
,

(2.14)

\displaystyle\lim_{M\to+\infty}\limsup_{k\to+\infty}\max_{[M\mu_{i,k},\delta\mu_{i+1,k}]}r^{\frac{2}{p_{k}-2}}|u_{k}(r)-\tilde{B}_{i+1,k}(r)|=0,\quad 1\leq i\leq L-1

\displaystyle\lim_{M\to+\infty}\limsup_{k\to+\infty}\max_{[M\mu_{L,k},R]}r^{\frac{2}{p_{k}-2}}|u_{k}(r)-u_{0}(r)|=0.

Proof of the fact that
L<+\infty
and of (
2.14
).

Let
\ell\geq 1
for which
(H_{\ell})
is true and let
0<\delta<1
. Then by
(H_{\ell})
we can write that

\displaystyle\int_{B_{R}}|u_{k}|^{\alpha_{k}}dx

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\int_{B_{\frac{\mu_{i,k}}{\delta}}\backslash B_{\delta\mu_{i,k}}}|u_{k}|^{\alpha_{k}}dx

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\mu_{i,k}^{N-\frac{2\alpha_{k}}{p_{k}-2}}\Big(\int_{B_{\frac{1}{\delta}}\backslash B_{\delta}}B_{0}^{2^{*}}dx+o(1)\Big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\mu_{i,k}^{N-\frac{2\alpha_{k}}{p_{k}-2}}\Big(K_{N}^{-N}+\varepsilon_{\delta}+o(1)\Big),

where
\varepsilon_{\delta}\to 0
as
\delta\to 0
. As before,
N-\frac{2\alpha_{k}}{p_{k}-2}\leq 0
, so that
\mu_{i,k}^{N-\frac{2\alpha_{k}}{p_{k}-2}}\geq 1
for all
1\leq i\leq\ell
. Passing to the limit as
k\to+\infty
and as
\delta\to 0
we thus obtain

\ell\leq CK_{N}^{N}

where
C
is the bound on the energy in (
1.4
). This shows that
L<\infty
. Equation (
2.14
) then follows from the maximality of
L
, (
2.10
) and (
2.12
).
∎

In what follows, for any
0<\delta<R
fixed, we let

\eta_{k}(\delta)=\max_{r\in[\delta,R]}|u_{k}(r)|.

Step
5
: Interpolation inequalities.

In this step we obtain a first set of pointwise estimates on
|u_{k}|
that interpolate between (
2.10
) and (
2.12
).

Step
5.1
: interpolation between
\tilde{B}_{L,k}
and
u_{0}
.
Let
L
be as in Step
4
. We claim that for any
0<\varepsilon<\frac{1}{2}
fixed there exists
\delta_{\varepsilon}
>0 and
C_{\varepsilon}>0
such that, for any
r\in[\mu_{L,k},R]
,

(2.15)

|u_{k}(r)|\leq C_{\varepsilon}\Big(\mu_{L,k}^{\frac{2}{p_{k}-2}(1-2\varepsilon)}r^{-\frac{4(1-\varepsilon)}{p_{k}-2}}+\eta_{k}(\delta_{\varepsilon})r^{-\frac{4\varepsilon}{p_{k}-2}}\Big).

Proof of (
2.15
).

Let
0<\varepsilon<\frac{1}{2}
and
0<\delta<R
be fixed. For
r\in[\mu_{L,k},R]
we let

\Phi_{k}^{\varepsilon}(r)=\mu_{L,k}^{\frac{2}{p_{k}-2}(1-2\varepsilon)}G(r)^{\frac{4(1-\varepsilon)}{(N-2)(p_{k}-2)}}+\eta_{k}(\delta)G(r)^{\frac{4\varepsilon}{(N-2)(p_{k}-2)}},

where, for
r=|x|
we have let
G(r)=\frac{1}{(N-2)\omega_{N-1}}|x|^{2-N}
, where
\omega_{N-1}
is the area of
\mathbb{S}^{N-1}\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
.
We let
s_{k}\in[\mu_{L,k},R]
be such that

\frac{u_{k}(s_{k})}{\Phi_{k}^{\varepsilon}(s_{k})}=\max_{s\in[\mu_{L,k},R]}\frac{u_{k}(s)}{\Phi_{k}^{\varepsilon}(s)}.

We first assume that, up to a subsequence,

(2.16)

\mu_{L,k}=o(s_{k})\quad\text{ and }\quad s_{k}\leq\delta

as
k\to+\infty
. Let
x_{k}\in B_{R}\backslash B_{\mu_{L,k}}
be such that
s_{k}=|x_{k}|
. By definition of
s_{k}
we have

(2.17)

\frac{-\triangle u_{k}(x_{k})}{u_{k}(x_{k})}\geq\frac{-\triangle\Phi_{k}^{\varepsilon}(x_{k})}{\Phi_{k}^{\varepsilon}(x_{k})}.

On the one side, straightforward computations show that

s_{k}^{2}\frac{-\triangle\Phi_{k}^{\varepsilon}(x_{k})}{\Phi_{k}^{\varepsilon}(x_{k})}\to(n-2)^{2}\varepsilon(1-\varepsilon)>0

as
k\to+\infty
. On the other side, (
1.3
), (
2.14
) and (
2.16
) show, since
s_{k}\to 0
, that

s_{k}^{2}\frac{-\triangle u_{k}(x_{k})}{u_{k}(x_{k})}\leq\delta^{2}\|u_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{R})}^{\frac{4}{N-2}}+o(1)

as
k\to+\infty
. If
u_{0}\equiv 0
we have a contradiction. If
u_{0}\not\equiv 0
we can now choose
\delta=\delta_{\varepsilon}
so that
\delta_{\varepsilon}^{2}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{R})}^{\frac{4}{N-2}}\leq\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon(1-\varepsilon)
. We then get a contradiction with (
2.17
), which shows that (
2.16
) cannot happen.

Thus, we either have
\frac{s_{k}}{\mu_{L,k}}\to C\geq 1
or
s_{k}\geq\delta_{\varepsilon}
, for some
\delta_{\varepsilon}>0
, up to a subsequence. In the first case (
2.15
) follows from the second bullet in
(H_{L})
, while in the second case (
2.15
) follows from the definition of
\eta_{k}(\delta_{\varepsilon})
. This shows that

u_{k}(r)\leq C_{\varepsilon}\Big(\mu_{L,k}^{\frac{2}{p_{k}-2}(1-2\varepsilon)}r^{-\frac{4(1-\varepsilon)}{p_{k}-2}}+\eta_{k}(\delta_{\varepsilon})r^{-\frac{4\varepsilon}{p_{k}-2}}\Big)

for any
r\in[\mu_{L,k},R]
. Since
-u_{k}
also satisfies (
1.4
), (
2.1
) and (
2.2
) the same inequality remains true also for
-u_{k}
. Hence (
2.15
) holds true.
∎

Step
5.2
: interpolation between
\tilde{B}_{i,k}
and
\tilde{B}_{i+1,k}
.
Let
L
be as in Step
4
and
i\in\{1,\dots,L-1\}
. We claim that for any
0<\varepsilon<\frac{1}{2}
there exists a constant
C_{\varepsilon}>0
such that, for any
r\in[\mu_{i,k},\mu_{i+1,k}]
,

(2.18)

u_{k}(r)\leq C_{\varepsilon}\Big(\mu_{i,k}^{\frac{2}{p_{k}-2}(1-2\varepsilon)}r^{-(1-\varepsilon)\frac{4}{p_{k}-2}}+\mu_{i+1,k}^{-\frac{2}{p_{k}-2}}\left(\frac{\mu_{i+1,k}}{r}\right)^{\frac{4\varepsilon}{p_{k}-2}}\Big).

Remark that, when
r=\mu_{i+1,k}
,
u_{k}
is of order
\mu_{i+1,k}^{-\frac{2}{p_{k}-2}}
by the second bullet in
(H_{L})
. Estimate (
2.18
) is therefore the direct analogue of (
2.15
) where
\eta_{k}(\delta)
has been formally replaced by
\mu_{i+1,k}^{-\frac{2}{p_{k}-2}}
(and
r^{-\frac{4\varepsilon}{p_{k}-2}}
has been replaced by
\mu_{i+1,k}^{\frac{4\varepsilon}{p_{k}-2}}r^{-\frac{4\varepsilon}{p_{k}-2}}
to attain the value
\mu_{i+1,k}^{-\frac{2}{p_{k}-2}}
at the correct scale).

Proof of (
2.18
).

The proof is very similar to the proof of (
2.15
). Let
i\in\{1,\dots,L-1\}
and
0<\varepsilon<\frac{1}{2}
be fixed. For
r\in[\mu_{i,k},\mu_{i+1,k}]
we let

\Phi_{i,k}^{\varepsilon}(r)=\mu_{i,k}^{\frac{2}{p_{k}-2}(1-2\varepsilon)}G(r)^{\frac{4(1-\varepsilon)}{(N-2)(p_{k}-2)}}+\mu_{i+1,k}^{-\frac{2(1-2\varepsilon)}{p_{k}-2}}G(r)^{\frac{4\varepsilon}{(N-2)(p_{k}-2)}},

where
G(r)
is as in the proof of (
2.15
). We let
s_{k}\in[\mu_{L,k},R]
be such that

\frac{u_{k}(s_{k})}{\Phi_{i,k}^{\varepsilon}(s_{k})}=\max_{s\in[\mu_{L,k},R]}\frac{u_{k}(s)}{\Phi_{i,k}^{\varepsilon}(s)}.

We first assume that, up to a subsequence,
\mu_{i,k}=o(s_{k})
and
s_{k}=o(\mu_{i+1,k})
as
k\to+\infty
. Let
x_{k}
be such that
s_{k}=|x_{k}|
. By definition of
s_{k}
we have

\frac{-\triangle u_{k}(x_{k})}{u_{k}(x_{k})}\geq\frac{-\triangle\Phi_{i,k}^{\varepsilon}(x_{k})}{\Phi_{i,k}^{\varepsilon}(x_{k})}.

Straightforward computations show again that

s_{k}^{2}\frac{-\triangle\Phi_{i,k}^{\varepsilon}(x_{k})}{\Phi_{i,k}^{\varepsilon}(x_{k})}\to(n-2)^{2}\varepsilon(1-\varepsilon)>0

as
k\to+\infty
, while (
1.3
) and (
2.14
) again show that

s_{k}^{2}\frac{-\triangle u_{k}(x_{k})}{u_{k}(x_{k})}=o(1)

as
k\to+\infty
, a contradiction. Thus, we either have
\frac{s_{k}}{\mu_{i,k}}\to C\geq 1
or
\frac{s_{k}}{\mu_{i+1,k}}\to C\leq 1
up to a subsequence. In both cases we obtain that

\frac{u_{k}(s_{k})}{\Phi_{i,k}^{\varepsilon}(s_{k})}\leq C_{\varepsilon}

for some positive constant
C_{\varepsilon}
. The inequality on
-u_{k}
is obtained as in the proof of (
2.15
) and (
2.18
) follows.
∎

Step
6
: improved pointwise estimates.
Let
0<\delta<\frac{R}{2}
be fixed. We prove that there exists
C_{\delta}>0
such that, for any
k\geq 0
and
r\in[0,R]
,

(2.19)

\big|u_{k}(r)\big|\leq C_{\delta}\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{L}\tilde{B}_{i,k}(r)+\eta_{k}(\delta)\Big)

holds, where
\tilde{B}_{i,k}
is as in (
2.4
).

Proof of (
2.19
).

Let
G_{1}
be the Green’s function of
-\triangle+1
in
B_{R}
with Neumann boundary condition. Recall that there exists
C>0
such that
\big|G_{1}(x,y)\big|\leq C|x-y|^{2-n}
for any
x,y\in B_{R},x\neq y
(see the arguments in
[
Roba
]
). Let
(s_{k})_{k\geq 0},s_{k}=|x_{k}|
, be any sequence of points in
[0,R]
. First, if
s_{k}\in[R/2,R]
, (
2.19
) follows from the definition of
\eta_{k}(\delta)
. We can thus assume that
s_{k}\in[0,R/2]
for all
k\geq 0
. A representation formula for
u_{k}
at
s_{k}
(or

) then gives, thanks to (
1.3
) and
(H_{L})

(2.20)

\displaystyle u_{k}(r_{k})

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{\mu_{1,k}}}G_{1}(x_{k},y)|u_{k}(|y|)|^{p_{k}-2}u_{k}(|y|)dy

\displaystyle+\sum_{i=2}^{L}\int_{B_{\mu_{i,k}}\backslash B_{\mu_{i-1,k}}}G_{1}(x_{k},y)|u_{k}(|y|)|^{p_{k}-2}u_{k}(|y|)dy

(2.21)

\left|\int_{B_{\mu_{1,k}}}G_{1}(x_{k},y)|u_{k}(|y|)|^{p_{k}-2}u_{k}(y)dy\right|\leq C\tilde{B}_{1,k}(s_{k})

(2.22)

\left|\int_{B_{\mu_{i,k}}\backslash B_{\mu_{i-1,k}}}G_{1}(x_{k},y)|u_{k}(|y|)|^{p_{k}-2}u_{k}(y)dy\right|\leq C\Big(\tilde{B}_{i-1,k}(s_{k})+\tilde{B}_{i,k}(s_{k})\Big)

for some
C>0
independent of

. Similarly (

(2.23)

\left|\int_{B_{R}\backslash B_{\mu_{L,k}}}G_{1}(x_{k},y)|u_{k}(|y|)|^{p_{k}-2}u_{k}(y)dy\right|\leq C\Big(\tilde{B}_{L,k}(s_{k})+\eta_{k}(\delta)^{p_{k}-1}\Big)

We are now in position to conclude the proof of Proposition
2.1
.

End of the proof of Proposition
2.1
.

Let
0<\delta<\frac{R}{2}
be fixed. We first prove that, if
u_{0}\equiv 0
, we have

(2.24)

\eta_{k}(\delta)\leq C_{\delta}^{\prime}\mu_{L,k}^{N-2-\frac{2}{p_{k}-2}}

for some
C_{\delta}^{\prime}>0
. We proceed by contradiction and assume that, up to a subsequence,

\eta_{k}(\delta)>>\mu_{L,k}^{N-2-\frac{2}{p_{k}-2}}

as
k\to+\infty
, and let
\tilde{u}_{k}=\frac{u_{k}}{\eta_{k}(\delta)}
. We then have
\eta_{k}(\delta)>>\mu_{i,k}^{N-2-\frac{2}{p_{k}-2}}
for any
1\leq i\leq L
and, by (
2.19
) and the expression of
\tilde{B}_{i,k}
in (
2.4
), we have

(2.25)

\|\tilde{u}_{k}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{R}\backslash B_{\sigma})}\leq C_{\delta}+o(1)

for any fixed
0<\sigma<R
. Since
u_{0}\equiv 0
we have, by (
2.8
),
\eta_{k}(\delta)=o(1)
as
k\to+\infty
. Therefore, by (
1.3
) and standard elliptic theory,
\tilde{u}_{k}\to\tilde{u}_{0}
in
C^{2}_{loc}(B_{R}\backslash\{0\})
where
\tilde{u}_{0}
satisfies

\left\{\begin{aligned} -\triangle\tilde{u}_{0}+\tilde{u}_{0}&=0&\textrm{ in }B_{R}\backslash\{0\}\\
\partial_{\nu}\tilde{u}_{0}&=0&\textrm{ in }\partial B_{R}\end{aligned}\right.

Passing (
2.25
) to the limit shows that
|\tilde{u}_{0}|\leq C_{\delta}
in
B_{R}\backslash\{0\}
, so the singularity at
0
is removable. Hence
\tilde{u}_{0}
is smooth in
B_{R}
and satisfies

\left\{\begin{aligned} -\triangle\tilde{u}_{0}+\tilde{u}_{0}&=0&\textrm{ in }B_{R}\\
\partial_{\nu}\tilde{u}_{0}&=0&\textrm{ in }\partial B_{R}\end{aligned}\right..

Integrating by parts yields
\tilde{u}_{0}\equiv 0
, which is a contradiction since, by definition,
|\tilde{u}_{0}(s_{0})|=1
for some
s_{0}\in[\delta,R]
. This proves (
2.24
)

If now
u_{0}\not\equiv 0
in
B_{R}
, we have
\eta_{k}(\delta)\leq\|u_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{R})}+o(1)
by (
2.8
). Coming back to (
2.19
) with (
2.24
) we have thus shown that, whatever
u_{0}
is, there exists
C>0
such that, for any
k\geq 0
,

(2.26)

\big|u_{k}(r)\big|\leq C\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{L}\tilde{B}_{i,k}(r)+\|u_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{R})}+\mu_{L,k}^{N-2-\frac{2}{p_{k}-2}}\Big)

holds for any
r\in[0,R]
. We now improve this estimate into an optimal expansion of
u_{k}
. We let again
s_{k}=|x_{k}|
be a sequence in
[0,R]
. If
s_{k}\not\to 0
, then

(2.27)

u_{k}(s_{k})=u_{0}(s_{k})+o\big(\|u_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{R})}\big)+O(\mu_{L,k}^{N-2-\frac{2}{p_{k}-2}})

by (
2.8
) and (
2.26
), which proves Proposition
2.1
when
s_{k}\not\to 0
. We thus assume that
s_{k}=o(1)
as
k\to+\infty
. Let
0<\sigma<1
. Note first that

(2.28)

\int_{B_{\frac{\mu_{L,k}}{\sigma}}}G_{1}(x_{k},y)|u_{0}|^{2^{*}-2}(y)u_{0}(|y|)dy=o\big(\|u_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{R})}\big)

as
k\to+\infty
. This is obvious if
u_{0}\equiv 0
and, if
u_{0}\not\equiv 0
, it follows since
\mu_{L,k}=o(1)
. Taking the difference of two representation formulae, one for
u_{k}
and one for
u_{0}
, we then obtain with (
2.28
) that

(2.29)

\displaystyle u_{k}(s_{k})-u_{0}(s_{k})

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{\frac{\mu_{1,k}}{\sigma}}}G_{1}(x_{k},y)|u_{k}(|y|)|^{p_{k}-2}u_{k}(|y|)dy+o\big(\|u_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{R})}\big)

\displaystyle+\sum_{i=2}^{L}\int_{B_{\frac{\mu_{i,k}}{\sigma}}\backslash B_{\frac{\mu_{i-1,k}}{\sigma}}}G_{1}(x_{k},y)|u_{k}(|y|)|^{p_{k}-2}u_{k}(|y|)dy

\displaystyle+\int_{B_{R}\backslash B_{\frac{\mu_{L,k}}{\sigma}}}G_{1}(x_{k},y)\Big(|u_{k}(|y|)|^{p_{k}-2}u_{k}(|y|)-|u_{0}(|y|)|^{2^{*}-2}u_{0}(|y|)\Big)dy.

Using (
2.26
) and the dominated convergence theorem, straightforward computations show that

(2.30)

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\frac{\mu_{1,k}}{\sigma}}}G_{1}(x_{k},y)|u_{k}(|y|)|^{p_{k}-2}u_{k}(|y|)dy

and that, for any

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{\sigma\mu_{i,k}}\backslash B_{\frac{\mu_{i-1,k}}{\sigma}}}G_{1}(x_{k},y)|u_{k}(|y|)|^{p_{k}-2}u_{k}(|y|)dy

\displaystyle+\int_{B_{\frac{\mu_{i,k}}{\sigma}}\backslash B_{\sigma\mu_{i,k}}}G_{1}(x_{k},y)|u_{k}(|y|)|^{p_{k}-2}u_{k}(|y|)dy

\displaystyle=\big(1+O(\varepsilon_{\sigma})\big)\tilde{B}_{i,k}(s_{k})+O\big(\varepsilon_{\sigma}\tilde{B}_{i-1,k}(s_{k})\big)+O\big(\mu_{L,k}^{N-2-\frac{2}{p_{k}-2}}\delta_{iL}\big)

hold as

as

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\delta_{k}}}u_{k}^{2}dx

\displaystyle+\frac{(N-2)^{2}}{4N}(p_{k}-2^{*})\int_{B_{\delta_{k}}}|u_{k}|^{p_{k}}dx

Assume by contradiction that

(3.8)

Using Proposition

\displaystyle\omega_{N-1}\delta_{k}^{N-1}\Bigg(-\frac{\delta_{k}}{2}(u_{k}^{\prime}(\delta_{k}))^{2}-\frac{N-2}{2}u_{k}(\delta_{k})u_{k}^{\prime}(\delta_{k})+\frac{\delta_{k}}{2}(u_{k}(\delta_{k}))^{2}-\frac{\delta_{k}}{p_{k}}|u_{k}(\delta_{k})|^{p_{k}}\Bigg)

\displaystyle=O\Big(\mu_{1,k}^{N-2-\frac{4}{p_{k}-2}}\left(\frac{\mu_{1,k}}{\mu_{2,k}}\right)^{\frac{N-2}{2}}\Big),

that

Lemma 3.4
.

Assume that
p_{k}\geq 2^{*}
for all

,

and that

blows up with at least two bubbles, that is

such that

\left(\frac{\mu_{i,k}}{\mu_{i+1,k}}\right)^{\frac{N-2}{2}}=\big(C_{i}+o(1)\big)(p_{k}-2^{*}).

We first prove that there exists a constant
C_{1}>0
such that

(3.14)

p_{k}-2^{*}=\big(C_{1}+o(1)\big)\left(\frac{\mu_{1,k}}{\mu_{2,k}}\right)^{\frac{N-2}{2}}.

p_{k}-2^{*}=o\big((\mu_{1,k}\mu_{2,k}^{-1})^{\frac{N-2}{2}}\big)
. This implies in particular that

\left(\frac{\mu_{1,k}}{\mu_{2,k}}\right)^{\frac{N-2}{2}-\frac{2}{p_{k}-2}}=1+o(1)

as

. With the latter estimates (
3.9
), (
3.11
) and (
3.12
) obtained in the proof of Theorem

are still true (since they did not use the assumption

). Then plugging (
3.12
) and (

) gives

\big(1+o(1)\big)\left(\frac{\mu_{1,k}}{\mu_{2,k}}\right)^{\frac{N-2}{2}}=\left\{\begin{aligned} &O\big(\mu_{1,k}\big)&\textrm{ if }N=3\\

&\big(C_{1}(N)+o(1)\big)\mu_{1,k}^{2}&\textrm{ if }N=5,6.\\
\end{aligned}\right.

Since
\mu_{2,k}\to 0
as

this is a contradiction. This proves that

. The other inequality is given by (
3.11
), and this proves (
3.14
) up to passing to a subsequence. If now
i\in\{1,\dots,L-1\}
, we apply a Pohozaev identity on

, where

. Estimates (
3.9
), (
3.11
) and (
3.12
) remain true with
\mu_{1,k}
and
\mu_{2,k}
replaced by
\mu_{i,k}
and
\mu_{i+1,k}
respectively. The proof then follows the same lines as the proof of (

∎

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

We assume that
N\geq 7
,
p_{k}\geq 2^{*}
for all

and
\lim_{k\to+\infty}p_{k}=2^{*}

(u_{k})_{k\geq 0}

and (

be the integer given by Proposition
2.1
and let
\delta_{k}=\sqrt{\mu_{L,k}}

Lemma 3.5
.

Assume that
N=6
and
p_{k}=3
for all
k\geq 0
. Let
(u_{k})_{k\geq 0}
be a sequence of radial solutions of (

) that satisfies (

be its weak limit in

is non-degenerate in the sense of (

(u_{k})_{k\geq 0}
is bounded in

(and hence precompact in

Proof.

By Theorem
3.1
we know that
u_{0}
is positive, satisfies
u_{0}(0)=\frac{1}{2}
and the blow-up arises with a single bubble at the origin. For any
k\geq 0
, we let

. By (
1.3
)

satisfies

(3.15)

-\triangle v_{k}+h_{0}v_{k}=v_{k}^{2}\quad\text{ in }B_{R},

where we have let

. As a consequence of the non-degeneracy of

(see (

)) it is easily seen that

has no kernel in the sense of (

). Hence

, the Green’s function of

with Neumann boundary condition, is uniquely defined. Since
h_{0}(0)=0
and
h_{0}^{\prime}(0)=0
,

satisfies (
3.3
).

We will need a refinement of the Pohozaev identity for (
3.15
). Let
0<\delta<R
be fixed. Integrating (
3.15
) against
rv_{k}^{\prime}(r)
gives, after a few integration by parts (see again
[
Heb14
]
, Proposition
6.2
), that for any
k\geq 0

(3.16)

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\delta}}\big(h_{0}+\frac{1}{2}rh_{0}^{\prime}(r)\big)v_{k}^{2}dx

\displaystyle=\omega_{5}\delta^{5}\Bigg(-\frac{\delta}{2}(v_{k}^{\prime}(\delta))^{2}-2v_{k}(\delta)v_{k}^{\prime}(\delta)+\delta\frac{h_{0}(\delta)}{2}(v_{k}(\delta))^{2}-\frac{\delta}{3}v_{k}(\delta)^{3}\Bigg)

holds. By Proposition
2.1
applied to
u_{k}
and by definition of
v_{k}
we have

(3.17)

v_{k}(r)=\big(1+o(1)\big)B_{1,k}(r)+O(\mu_{1,k}^{2})+o(\|u_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{R})})

as
k\to+\infty
, for any
r\in[0,R]
, where
B_{1,k}
is as in (
2.4
). We claim that we actually have

(3.18)

v_{k}(r)=\big(1+o(1)\big)B_{1,k}(r)+O(\mu_{1,k}^{2})

for any
r\in[0,R]
. This follows from an adaptation of the arguments in the proof of Proposition
2.1
. More precisely, it is easily seen with (
3.17
) that (
2.15
) still holds for
v_{k}
with
L=1
and
\eta_{k}(\delta_{\varepsilon})
now defined as
\eta_{k}(\delta_{\varepsilon})=\max_{[\delta_{\varepsilon},R]}|v_{k}|
. As a consequence, (
2.19
) remains true and is proven in the same way. Since

has no kernel the same arguments as in the proof of (
2.24
) then show that
\eta_{k}(\delta_{\varepsilon})=O(\mu_{1,k}^{2})
, from which (
3.18
) follows.

With (
3.15
) and (
3.18
) we then get that
\mu_{1,k}^{-2}v_{k}(r)\to G_{h_{0}}(r)
in

as

) we then have

\displaystyle\mu_{1,k}^{-4}\omega_{5}\delta^{5}\Bigg(-\frac{\delta}{2}(v_{k}^{\prime}(\delta))^{2}-2v_{k}(\delta)v_{k}^{\prime}(\delta)+\delta\frac{h_{0}}{2}(v_{k}(\delta))^{2}-\frac{\delta}{3}v_{k}(\delta)^{3}\Bigg)\to 2C_{0}\ln\delta+O(1)\quad\text{ as }k\to+\infty,

where

denotes a quantity that remains bounded as
\delta\to 0
. (Note however that

is fixed in this proof). Independently, and by Proposition

as

Remark 3.6

4.1.
Preliminaries and notation

In all the following, we will denote by
\left\langle.,.\right\rangle
the scalar product given, for

, by

\left\langle u,v\right\rangle=\int_{B_{R}}(\nabla u\cdot\nabla v+uv)dx,

where
\cdot
denotes the scalar product in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and by
\|\cdot\|
the associated norm in

.
We denote by

the adjoint operator of the embedding

,
i.e. for all

, the function
u=i^{\ast}(\varphi)\in H^{1}(B_{R})
is the unique solution
of

\left\{\begin{aligned} -\Delta u+u&=\varphi\ \text{in}\ B_{R}\subset\mathbb{R}^{N},\\
\partial_{\nu}u&=0,\ \text{on}\ \partial B_{R}.\end{aligned}\right.

In particular,
\|u\|\leq C\|\varphi\|_{L^{\frac{2N}{N+2}}(B_{R})}
for some
C=C(N,R)>0
. In view of equation (
4.1
), we consider the following problem:
find a positive
u\in H^{1}(B_{R})
such that

u=i^{\ast}(f_{\varepsilon}(u)),

where we have let, for
u\in\mathbb{R}
,

f_{\varepsilon}(u)=(u)_{+}^{2^{\ast}-1-\varepsilon}

and
u_{+}=\max(u,0)
. For
s>\frac{2N}{N-2}
and by definition of
i
we have, by standard elliptic regularity results,

(4.2)

\|i^{\ast}(w)\|+\|i^{\ast}(w)\|_{L^{s}(B_{R})}\leq C\|w\|_{L^{\frac{Ns}{N+2s}}(B_{R})}

for some
C=C(N,R,s)>0
. For
\varepsilon
small enough, we define

\alpha_{\varepsilon}=\begin{cases}2^{\ast}-\frac{N}{2}\varepsilon,&\text{if}\ \varepsilon<0,\\
2^{\ast},&\text{if}\ \varepsilon>0.\end{cases}

This exponent coincides with the exponent
\alpha_{k}
defined by (
1.4
) since
p_{k}=2^{*}-\varepsilon
in this section. We let
\mathcal{H}_{\varepsilon}=H^{1}(B_{R})\cap L^{\alpha_{\varepsilon}}(B_{R})
endowed with the norm

\|w\|_{\alpha_{\varepsilon}}=\|w\|+\|w\|_{L^{\alpha_{\varepsilon}}(B_{R})},

which is a Banach space. It is known (see
[
CGS89
]
,
[
Oba72
]
) that all the positive solutions
u
of the equation

-\Delta u=u^{2^{\ast}-1}\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}

are given by

B_{\delta,y}(x)=\delta^{\frac{2-N}{2}}B_{0}\Big(\frac{x-y}{\delta}\Big),\ \delta>0,\ y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}

where

is as in (
2.5
). It is also well known (see
[
BE91
]
) that all

solving

are linear combinations of

V_{0}(x)=\dfrac{|x|^{2}-1}{(1+|x|^{2})^{\frac{N}{2}}}

and

V_{i}(x)=\dfrac{x_{i}}{(1+|x|^{2})^{\frac{N}{2}}},\ i=1,\ldots,N.

If

is in addition assumed to be radial it is then a multiple of

.

From now on we will only consider

solutions of (

\chi:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a smooth cutoff function such that

,

if

and

if

B_{\lambda}(x)=\chi(|x|)\lambda^{\frac{2-N}{2}}B_{0}(\lambda^{-1}|x|)

and

Z_{\lambda,0}(x)=\chi(|x|)\lambda^{\frac{2-N}{2}}V_{0}(\lambda^{-1}|x|).

These are radial functions in
B_{R}
and are compactly supported in
B_{R}
, thus have zero Neumann derivative. We denote by
\Pi_{\lambda}
, respectively

, the orthogonal projection of

onto

K_{\lambda}=\mathrm{span}\left\{Z_{\lambda,0}\right\},

respectively onto

K_{\lambda}^{\bot}=\left\{\phi\in H^{1}(B_{R})/\penalty\left\langle\phi,Z_{\lambda,0}\right\rangle=0\right\}.

We recall that a solution
u_{0}
of (

1.7

Remark 4.1

(Encoding the radius of the ball with a parameter)
.

If
\mu>0
is fixed it is easily seen that
u
solves
-\Delta u+\mu u=u^{2^{*}-1}
in
B_{R}
, with
\partial_{\nu}u=0
on
\partial B_{R}
, if and only if
v(x):=\mu^{-\frac{N-2}{4}}u(\frac{x}{\sqrt{\mu}})
solves
-\Delta v+v=v^{2^{*}-1}
in
B_{R\sqrt{\mu}}
with
\partial_{\nu}v=0
on
\partial B_{R\sqrt{\mu}}
(no radiality is assumed here). Clearly,
v
is non-degenerate if and only if
u
is non-degenerate.

With Remark
4.1
in mind, the following result shows that the existence of non-degenerate solutions of (
4.1
) when
\varepsilon=0
is a generic property in the choice of the radius
R
:

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
u_{0}\in H^{1}(B_{R})
be a positive solution to

\left\{\begin{aligned} -\Delta u_{0}+\mu u_{0}&=u_{0}^{2^{\ast}-1},&\text{in}\ B_{R},\\
\partial_{\nu}u_{0}&=0,&\text{on}\ \partial B_{R}\end{aligned}\right.

for some
\mu>0
. Fix
\nu>0
. Then, there exist
\mu_{\nu}>0
and
u_{0,\nu}>0
such that
|\mu_{\nu}-\mu|+\|u_{0}-u_{0,\nu}\|_{C^{2,\theta}(\overline{B_{R}})}<\nu
,
\theta\in(0,1)
, and
u_{0,\nu}>0
is a non-degenerate solution of

\left\{\begin{aligned} -\Delta u_{0,\nu}+\mu_{\nu}u_{0,\nu}&=u_{0,\nu}^{2^{\ast}-1},\text{ in }\ B_{R},\\
\partial_{\nu}u_{0,\nu}&=0\text{ on }\ \partial B_{R}.\end{aligned}\right.

Proof.

We proceed as in
[
RV13
]
. We set, for
\eta\geq 0
,

A_{\eta}=\inf_{u\in H^{1}(B_{R})\backslash\{0\}}\dfrac{\int_{B_{R}}\Big(|\nabla u|^{2}+(\mu-(2^{\ast}-1)u_{0}^{2^{\ast}-2}-\eta)u^{2}\Big)dx}{(\int_{B_{R}}|u|^{2^{\ast}}dx)^{\frac{2}{2^{\ast}}}}.

It is easy to see that
A_{\eta}<0
and
\lim_{\eta\rightarrow 0}A_{\eta}=A_{0}<0
. Direct minimisation yields, for all
\eta\geq 0
, a minimizer
w_{\eta}\in C^{2,\theta}(\overline{B_{R}})
,
w_{\eta}>0
, such that

(4.4)

\left\{\begin{aligned} -\Delta w_{\eta}+(\mu-(2^{\ast}-1)u_{0}^{2^{\ast}-2}-\eta)w_{\eta}&=A_{\eta}w_{\eta}^{2^{\ast}-1}\text{ in }B_{R}\\
\partial_{\nu}w_{\eta}&=0\text{ on }\partial B_{R}\end{aligned}\right..

With (
4.4
), and since
A_{\eta}<0
for all
\eta\geq 0
, it is easily seen that the family
(w_{\eta})_{\eta\geq 0}
is relatively compact in
C^{2}(\overline{B_{R}})
.

We now claim that, when
\eta=0
,
u_{0}
is the only solution of (
4.4
) that is positive in
\overline{B_{R}}
. We assume that
u
and
v
are two such solutions and we let
x_{0}\in\overline{B_{R}}
be the maximum point of
\frac{u}{v}
in
\overline{B_{R}}
. Straightforward computations show that
w:=\frac{u}{v}
satisfies

-\triangle w-A_{0}w^{2^{*}-1}\Big(1-\frac{1}{w^{2^{*}-2}}\Big)=2\langle\nabla w,\nabla\ln v\rangle\quad\text{ in }\overline{B_{R}}.

By definition of
x_{0}
, and since
\partial_{\nu}w=0
in
\partial B_{R}
, we always have
\nabla w(x_{0})=0
, regardless of whether
x_{0}
is an interior or boundary point. If we write
w(x)=w(r\omega)
in spherical coordinates for
x=r\omega\in\overline{B_{R}}
, the condition that
x_{0}
is the global maximum of
w
in
\overline{B_{R}}
implies both
\triangle_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}w(R\cdot)\leq 0
and, since
\partial_{\nu}w(x_{0})=0
,
\partial_{\nu}^{2}w(x_{0})\leq 0
. Therefore
-\triangle w(x_{0})\geq 0
, so that applying the latter equation at
x_{0}
and since
A_{0}<0
yields
w(x_{0})\leq 1
. This implies that
u\leq v
in
\overline{B_{R}}
. The same argument with the roles of
u
and
v
reversed also shows that
v\leq u
in
\overline{B_{R}}
, and thus
u\equiv v
in
\overline{B_{R}}
.

A first consequence of this uniqueness result is that
A_{0}=-(2^{*}-2)
, which is easily seen integrating (
4.4
) by parts against
u_{0}
when
\eta=0
. As a second consequence, we get that
\|w_{\eta}-u_{0}\|_{C^{2,\theta}(\overline{B_{R}})}\rightarrow 0
as
\eta\rightarrow 0
. Let now, for
\eta>0
,
\mu_{\eta}=\mu-(2^{\ast}-1)(u_{0}^{2^{\ast}-2}-w_{\eta}^{2^{\ast}-2})-\eta
. Clearly
\lim_{\eta\to 0}\mu_{\eta}=\mu>0
. By (
4.4
)
w_{\eta}
satisfies

-\triangle w_{\eta}+\mu_{\eta}w_{\eta}=\big(2^{*}-1+A_{\eta}\big)w_{\eta}^{2^{*}-1}\quad\text{ in }\overline{B_{R}}.

We let
u_{0,\eta}=\big(2^{*}-1+A_{\eta}\big)^{\frac{N-2}{4}}w_{\eta}
. Since
2^{*}-1+A_{\eta}\to 1
as
\eta\to 0
we have
u_{0,\eta}\to u_{0}
in
C^{2}(\overline{B_{R}})
as
\eta\to 0
. Let
\nu>0
be fixed. It remains to choose
\eta>0
small enough so that
-\triangle+\big(1-(2^{*}-1)u_{0}^{2^{*}-2}-\eta\big)
has no kernel in the sense of (
2.35
) and
|\mu_{\eta}-\mu|+\|u_{0}-u_{0,\eta}\|_{C^{2,\theta}(\overline{B_{R}})}<\nu
. This choice of
\eta
implies that
u_{0,\eta}
is still non-degenerate and concludes the proof.
∎

\Pi_{\lambda(t)}^{\bot}(u_{0}+B_{\lambda(t)}+\phi(t)-i^{\ast}(f_{\varepsilon}(u_{0}+B_{\lambda(t)}+\phi(t))))=0.

,

also satisfies the Neumann boundary condition in the two previous equations. We begin by solving (

u_{0}+B_{\lambda(t)}

From now on, if

and

denote two positive families of real numbers, we will write

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
0<a<b
be real numbers. There exists a positive constant
C_{a,b}
such that for

small we have

\displaystyle\left\|i^{\ast}(f_{\varepsilon}(u_{0}+B_{\lambda(t)}))-u_{0}-B_{\lambda(t)}\right\|_{\alpha_{\varepsilon}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{a,b}\left\{\begin{aligned} &\Big(\frac{|\varepsilon|}{\ln\frac{1}{|\varepsilon|}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\text{ if }N=4\ \text{ and }u_{0}=0,\\
&|\varepsilon|^{\frac{3}{4}}\text{ if }N=5\text{ and }u_{0}=0,\\
&|\varepsilon|\ln\frac{1}{|\varepsilon|}\text{ otherwise }.\end{aligned}\right.

uniformly for
t\in[a,b]
.

Proof.

Let
0<a<b
be real numbers. By (
4.5
), unless
N=4
and
u_{0}\equiv 0
,
\lambda_{\varepsilon}(t)
converges uniformly to zero for
t\in[a,b]
as
\varepsilon\to 0
. If
N=4
and
u_{0}\equiv 0
, again by (
4.5
),
\lambda_{\varepsilon}(t)
converges uniformly to zero for
t\in[a,+\infty)
as
\varepsilon\to 0
.
For simplicity we set
\alpha_{\varepsilon}=\alpha
in this proof. We have, by (

)

\displaystyle\left\|i^{\ast}(f_{\varepsilon}(u_{0}+B_{\lambda(t)}))-u_{0}-B_{\lambda(t)}\right\|_{\alpha}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|(f_{\varepsilon}(u_{0}+B_{\lambda(t)}))-(-\Delta+Id)(u_{0}+B_{\lambda(t)})\right\|_{L^{\frac{N\alpha}{N+2\alpha}}(B_{R}).}

The triangle inequality yields

\displaystyle\left\|i^{\ast}(f_{\varepsilon}(u_{0}+B_{\lambda(t)}))-u_{0}-B_{\lambda(t)}\right\|_{\alpha}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|f_{\varepsilon}(u_{0}+B_{\lambda(t)})-f_{\varepsilon}(u_{0})-f_{\varepsilon}(B_{\lambda(t)})\right\|_{L^{\frac{N\alpha}{N+2\alpha}}(B_{R})}

\displaystyle\quad+\left\|f_{\varepsilon}(u_{0})+\Delta u_{0}-u_{0}\right\|_{L^{\frac{N\alpha}{N+2\alpha}}(B_{R})}

\displaystyle\quad+\left\|f_{\varepsilon}(B_{\lambda(t)})+(\Delta-Id)(B_{\lambda(t)})\right\|_{L^{\frac{N\alpha}{N+2\alpha}}(B_{R})}

\displaystyle\lesssim(I_{1}+I_{2}+I_{3}).

We begin by estimating

. Using once more the triangle inequality, we get

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|(f_{\varepsilon}(u_{0}+B_{\lambda(t)})-f_{\varepsilon}(B_{\lambda(t)}))1_{B_{\sqrt{\lambda(t)}}}\right\|_{L^{\frac{N\alpha}{N+2\alpha}}(B_{R})}

\displaystyle\quad+\left\|(f_{\varepsilon}(u_{0}+B_{\lambda(t)})-f_{\varepsilon}(u_{0}))1_{B_{R}\backslash B_{\sqrt{\lambda(t)}}}\right\|_{L^{\frac{N\alpha}{N+2\alpha}}(B_{R})}

\displaystyle\quad+\left\|f_{\varepsilon}(B_{\lambda(t)})1_{B_{R}\backslash B_{\sqrt{\lambda(t)}}}\right\|_{L^{\frac{N\alpha}{N+2\alpha}}}+\left\|f_{\varepsilon}(u_{0})1_{B_{\sqrt{\lambda(t)}}}\right\|_{L^{\frac{N\alpha}{N+2\alpha}}(B_{R})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|u_{0}B_{\lambda(t)}^{2^{\ast}-2-\varepsilon}1_{B_{\sqrt{\lambda(t)}}}\right\|_{L^{\frac{N\alpha}{N+2\alpha}}(B_{R})}+\left\|u_{0}^{2^{\ast}-2-\varepsilon}B_{\lambda(t)}1_{B_{R}\backslash B_{\sqrt{\lambda(t)}}}\right\|_{L^{\frac{N\alpha}{N+2\alpha}}(B_{R})}

(4.8)

\displaystyle\quad+\left\|f_{\varepsilon}(B_{\lambda(t)})1_{B_{R}\backslash B_{\sqrt{\lambda(t)}}}\right\|_{L^{\frac{N\alpha}{N+2\alpha}}(B_{R})}+\left\|f_{\varepsilon}(u_{0})1_{B_{\sqrt{\lambda(t)}}}\right\|_{L^{\frac{N\alpha}{N+2\alpha}}(B_{R})}.

A first remark is that, by definition of
\lambda(t)=\lambda_{\varepsilon}(t)
in (
4.5
), we have

(4.9)

\lambda_{\varepsilon}(t)^{\varepsilon}=1+o(1)\quad\text{ uniformly for }t\in[a,b]

and

\varepsilon|\ln\lambda(t)|\lesssim|\varepsilon|\ln\frac{1}{|\varepsilon|}\quad\text{ uniformly for }t\in[a,b].

By definition of
\alpha=\alpha_{\varepsilon}
, we have
\frac{N\alpha}{N+2\alpha}=\frac{2N}{N+2}+O(|\varepsilon|)
as

B_{\lambda_{\varepsilon}(t)}\lesssim\lambda(t)^{-\frac{N-2}{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|u_{0}B_{\lambda(t)}^{2^{\ast}-2}1_{B_{\sqrt{\lambda(t)}}}\right\|_{L^{\frac{2N}{N+2}}(B_{R})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\{\begin{aligned} &\lambda(t)^{\frac{N-2}{2}}&\text{ if }N=3,4,5,\\
&\lambda(t)^{2}|\ln\lambda(t)|^{\frac{2}{3}}&\text{ if }N=6,\\
&\lambda(t)^{2}&\text{ if }N\geq 7.\end{aligned}\right.

Similar computations using (
4.9
) and (

\displaystyle I_{1}\lesssim\lambda(t)^{\frac{N+2}{4}}+\left\{\begin{aligned} &\lambda(t)^{\frac{N-2}{2}}&\text{ if }N=3,4,5,\\
&\lambda(t)^{2}|\ln\lambda(t)|^{\frac{2}{3}}&\text{ if }N=6,\\
&\lambda(t)^{2}&\text{ if }N\geq 7.\end{aligned}\right.

Let us turn to

\displaystyle\quad+\left\|(\chi^{2^{\ast}-1-\varepsilon}-\chi^{2^{\ast}-1})B^{2^{\ast}-1}_{\lambda(t)}\right\|_{L^{\frac{N\alpha}{N+2\alpha}}(B_{R})}

\displaystyle\quad+\left\|f_{0}(B_{\lambda(t)})-(-\Delta+Id)(B_{\lambda(t)})\right\|_{L^{\frac{N\alpha}{N+2\alpha}}(B_{R})}.

Using Taylor’s expansions, (

and

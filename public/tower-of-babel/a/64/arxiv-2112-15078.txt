μ \mu -norm and regularity
D.Treschev

Abstract
In [ 22 ] we introduce the concept of a μ \mu -norm for a bounded operator in a Hilbert space. The main motivation is the extension of the measure entropy to the case of quantum systems. In this paper we recall the basic results from [ 22 ] and present further results on the μ \mu -norm. More precisely, we specify three classes of unitary operators for which the μ \mu -norm generates a bistochastic operator. We plan to use the latter in the construction of quantum entropy.

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\#I}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z},\,l\in I,\,|q|\leq Q(\sigma)}W_{l+m,k}\overline{W}_{l,k-q+n}{\bf g}_{q},

\displaystyle\Omega_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\#I}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z},\,l\in I,\,|q|>Q(\sigma)}W_{l+m,k}\overline{W}_{l,k-q+n}{\bf g}_{q}.

In the sum
\Omega_{1}
the index
q
contains only a finite number of values. Hence for all sufficiently big
\#I
we have:

\Omega_{1}=\sum_{|q|\leq Q(\sigma)}\omega_{m,n-q}{\bf g}_{q}+\Delta,\qquad|\Delta|\leq\sum_{|q|\leq Q(\sigma)}\sigma|{\bf g}_{q}|\leq\sigma{\bf c}_{g}.

Now we estimate
\Omega_{2}
. Let
c_{k}
be the majorating sequence for
W
with

\sum c_{k}={\bf c}
. Then

\displaystyle|\Omega_{2}|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\#I}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z},\,l\in I\,|q|>Q(\sigma)}c_{l+m-k}c_{l-k+q-n}|{\bf g}_{q}|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z},\,|q|>Q(\sigma)}c_{m+j}c_{q-n+j}|{\bf g}_{q}|=\sum_{|q|>Q(\sigma)}\widetilde{c}_{m+n-q}\sigma|{\bf g}_{q}|,

where
\widetilde{c}_{s}=\sum_{j}c_{s+j}c_{j}
,
\sum_{s}\widetilde{c}_{s}={\bf c}^{2}
. Hence

|\Omega_{2}|\leq{\bf c}^{2}\sum_{|q|>Q(\sigma)}|{\bf g}_{q}|\leq{\bf c}^{2}\sigma.

By (
10.2
)

\bigg|\sum_{|q|>Q(\sigma)}\omega_{m,n-q}{\bf g}_{q}\bigg|\leq{\bf c}^{2}\sum_{|q|>Q(\sigma)}|{\bf g}_{q}|\leq{\bf c}^{2}\sigma.

Therefore

\Big|\widetilde{\omega}_{I,m,n}-\sum_{q\in\mathbb{Z}}\omega_{m,n+q}{\bf g}_{q}\Big|\leq\sigma({\bf c}_{g}+2{\bf c}^{2}).

Finally note that

\displaystyle\nu(x,a)|g(a)|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{q,p\in\mathbb{Z}}g_{p}\overline{g}_{p-q}e^{iqa}\sum_{m,k\in\mathbb{Z}}\omega_{m,k}e^{imx+ika}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{m,n,q,p\in\mathbb{Z}}\omega_{m,n-q}g_{p}\overline{g}_{p-q}e^{imx+ina}.

These computations imply that for all sufficiently big
\#I
the quantity
\widetilde{\omega}_{I,m,n}
differs arbitrarily small from

\sum_{p,q\in\mathbb{Z}}\omega_{m,n+q}g_{p}\overline{g}_{p-q}=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}e^{-imx-ina}\nu(x,a)|g(a)|^{2}\,dxda.

8.2
The operator
\widehat{g}W

Lemma 8.2

Suppose
W\in{\cal R}(\mathbb{T})
and
g\in{\cal ACF}(\mathbb{T})
. Then
\widetilde{W}=\widehat{g}W
is also regular and the corresponding coefficients
\widetilde{\omega}_{m,n}
satisfy

\sum_{m,n\in\mathbb{Z}}\widetilde{\omega}_{m,n}e^{imx+ina}=|g(x)|^{2}\nu(x,a).

(8.3)

Corollary 8.2

Suppose
W\in{\cal R}(\mathbb{T})
and
g\in{\cal ACF}(\mathbb{T})
. Then by (
4.2
) and (
4.9
)

\|\widehat{g}W\|_{\mu}^{2}=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}|g(x)|^{2}\nu(x,a)\,dxda.

(8.4)

Proof of Proposition
8.2
. We denote by
\widetilde{\omega}_{I,m,n}
the quantities (
4.1
), corresponding to the operator

\widetilde{W}=\widehat{g}W
. Then

\displaystyle\widetilde{\omega}_{I,m,n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\#I}\sum_{k,s,j\in\mathbb{Z},\,l\in I}g_{l+m-k}W_{k,j}\overline{g}_{l-s}\overline{W}_{s,j+n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\#I}\sum_{k,s,j\in\mathbb{Z},\,l\in I}g_{m-k}\overline{g}_{-s}W_{k+l,j}\overline{W}_{s+l,j+n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\#I}\sum_{k,s,j\in\mathbb{Z},\,l\in I+s}g_{m-k}\overline{g}_{-s}W_{k-s+l,j}\overline{W}_{l,j+n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k,s\in\mathbb{Z}}g_{m-k}\overline{g}_{-s}\omega_{I+s,k-s,n}.

We define
{\bf c}_{g}=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}|g_{j}|
.
Given
\sigma>0
we take
M=M(\sigma)>|m|
such that

\sum_{|j|\geq(M-|m|)/2}|g_{j}|<\sigma.

(8.5)

We take
N=N(\sigma,M)
such that for any
j
,
|j|\leq M
and any interval
J\subset\mathbb{Z}
,
\#J>N

|\omega_{J,j,n}-\omega_{j,n}|<\sigma.

(8.6)

Then
\widetilde{\omega}_{I,m,n}=\Sigma_{0}+\Sigma_{1}+\Sigma_{2}+\Sigma_{3}
, where

\displaystyle\Sigma_{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k,s\in\mathbb{Z}}g_{m-k}\overline{g}_{-s}\omega_{k-s,n},

\displaystyle\Sigma_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{|k-s|\geq M}g_{m-k}\overline{g}_{-s}\omega_{k-s,n},

\displaystyle\Sigma_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\sum_{|k-s|<M}g_{m-k}\overline{g}_{-s}(\omega_{I+s,k-s,n}-\omega_{k-s,n}),

\displaystyle\Sigma_{3}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{|k-s|\geq M}g_{m-k}\overline{g}_{-s}\omega_{I,k-s,n}.

First, we transform the sum
\Sigma_{0}
:

\displaystyle\Sigma_{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}e^{-imx-ina}\sum_{k,s\in\mathbb{Z}}g_{m-k}\overline{g}_{-s}e^{i(m-k+s)x}\omega_{k-s,n}e^{i(k-s)x+ina}\,dxda

(8.7)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}e^{-imx-ina}|g(x)|^{2}\nu(x,a)\,dxda.

We estimate
\Sigma_{1}
by using (
10.1
) and (
8.5
):

|\Sigma_{1}|\leq{\bf c}^{2}\sum_{|k-s|\geq M}|g_{m-k}||g_{-s}|\leq 2{\bf c}^{2}\sum_{\beta\in\mathbb{Z},|\alpha|\geq(M-|m|)/2}|g_{\alpha}||g_{\beta}|\leq 2{\bf c}^{2}{\bf c}_{g}\sigma.

(8.8)

We have by (

|\Sigma_{2}|\leq\sigma\sum_{k,s\in\mathbb{Z}}|g_{m-k}||g_{-s}|=\sigma{\bf c}_{g}^{2}.

(8.9)

Combining (

).

9

Unfortunately we do not know if equation (
6.35
) remains valid for
W\in{\cal R}(\mathbb{T})
and arbitrary

g_{1},g_{2}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})
.
In this section we present two partial results in this direction. More precisely, we show that equations (
8.2
) and (
8.4
) hold for
g
lying in sets which are larger than declared in Corollaries
8.1
and
8.2

9.1
Computation of
\|W\widehat{g}\|_{\mu}

Lemma 9.1

Suppose
W\in{\cal R}(\mathbb{T})
and
g\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})
. Then equation (
8.2
) remains valid.

Proof
. For any
\sigma>0
there exists
g_{*}\in{\cal ACF}(\mathbb{T})
such that

\|g_{*}\|_{\infty}\leq\|g\|_{\infty}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\|g-g_{*}\|<\sigma.

Indeed, it is sufficient to take
\widetilde{g}
equal to a finite, but sufficiently long part of the Fourier series of
g
, so that

\big|\|g\|^{2}-\|\widetilde{g}\|^{2}\big|<\sigma
. Then we define
g_{*}
as a cut off of
\widetilde{g}
:

g_{*}(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccc}\widetilde{g}(x)&\mbox{ if }&|\widetilde{g}(x)|\leq\|g\|_{\infty},\\
\|g\|_{\infty}&\mbox{ if }&\widetilde{g}(x)>\|g\|_{\infty},\\
-\|g\|_{\infty}&\mbox{ if }&\widetilde{g}(x)<-\|g\|_{\infty}.\\
\end{array}\right.

The function
g_{*}(x)
is Lipschitz. Hence, it lies in
{\cal ACF}(\mathbb{T})
.

By using the triangle inequality, (
1.5
) and Assertion
(7)
, we have:

\Big|\|W\widehat{g}\|_{\mu}-\|W\widehat{g}_{*}\|_{\mu}\Big|\leq\|W(\widehat{g}-\widehat{g}_{*})\|_{\mu}\leq\|W\|\,\|(\widehat{g}-\widehat{g}_{*})\|_{\mu}\leq\sigma\|W\|.

(9.1)

On the other hand, by (
8.2
)

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\Big|\|W\widehat{g}_{*}\|_{\mu}^{2}-\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}\nu(x,a)|g(a)|^{2}\,dadx\Big|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\Big|\int_{\mathbb{T}}\varphi(a)(|g_{*}(a)|^{2}-|g(a)|^{2})\,da\Big|,

\displaystyle\varphi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\nu(x,\cdot)\,dx.

By using the estimate
\|\varphi\|_{\infty}\leq\|\varphi\|_{\cal DT}\leq\|W\|_{\cal DT}^{2}

(Lemma
6.9
), we obtain:

\Big|\|W\widehat{g}_{*}\|_{\mu}^{2}-\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}\nu(x,a)|g(a)|^{2}\,dadx\Big|\leq\|W\|_{\cal DT}^{2}\sigma

(9.2)

Hence, Lemma
9.1
is a combination of (
9.1
) and (
9.2
).

9.2
Computation of
\|\widehat{g}W\|_{\mu}

Definition 9.1

We say that
g\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})
is almost
{\cal ACF}(\mathbb{T})
(the notation is
g\in{\cal AACF}(\mathbb{T})
) if there exists a continuous in

\varepsilon\in(-\varepsilon_{0},\varepsilon_{0})
family of functions

g_{\varepsilon}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})
such that the following conditions hold:

A1
.
g_{0}=g
,

A2
. if
\varepsilon\neq 0
then
g_{\varepsilon}\in{\cal ACF}(\mathbb{T})
,

A3
.
|g_{\varepsilon}-g|\leq|g_{\varepsilon}-g_{-\varepsilon}|
if
0\leq\varepsilon<\varepsilon_{0}
,

A4
.
\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\|g_{\varepsilon}-g_{-\varepsilon}\|=0
.

A typical example of an
{\cal AACF}(\mathbb{T})
function is the indicator of an interval (see Lemma
9.3
below).

Lemma 9.2

Suppose
W\in{\cal R}(\mathbb{T})
and
g\in{\cal AACF}(\mathbb{T})
. Then equation (
8.4
) remains valid.

Proof
. Let
g_{\varepsilon}
be the family from Definition
9.1
.
By
A3
and
A1
for any
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0})
and any
X\in{\cal B}

0\leq\|(\widehat{g}_{\varepsilon}-\widehat{g})W\pi_{X}\|^{2}\leq\|(\widehat{g}_{\varepsilon}-\widehat{g}_{-\varepsilon})W\pi_{X}\|^{2}.

This inequality implies

0\leq\|(\widehat{g}_{\varepsilon}-\widehat{g})W\|_{\mu}^{2}\leq\|(\widehat{g}_{\varepsilon}-\widehat{g}_{-\varepsilon})W\|_{\mu}^{2},\qquad\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0}).

By
A2
and (
8.4
)

\|(\widehat{g}_{\varepsilon}-\widehat{g})W\|_{\mu}^{2}\leq\Delta_{\varepsilon}
,

\Delta_{\varepsilon}=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}|g_{\varepsilon}(x)-g_{-\varepsilon}(x)|^{2}\nu(x,a)\,dxda\leq\|g_{\varepsilon}-g_{-\varepsilon}\|^{2}\|\psi\|_{\infty},\quad\psi=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\nu(\cdot,a)\,da

(see also (
6.33
)). Note that
\|\psi\|_{\infty}\leq\|\psi\|_{\cal DT}
. Hence by

A4
and Lemma
6.9

\Delta_{\varepsilon}\to 0
as
\varepsilon\to 0
.

By triangle inequality

\Big|\|\widehat{g}_{\varepsilon}W\|_{\mu}^{2}-\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}|g(x)|^{2}\nu(x,a)\,dadx\Big|=\big|\|\widehat{g}W\|_{\mu}^{2}-\|\widehat{g}_{\varepsilon}W\|_{\mu}^{2}\leq\Delta_{\varepsilon}.

Hence equation (
8.4
) follows.

10

\omega_{m,n}

In this section
W\in{\cal DT}(\mathbb{T})
,
\{c_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
is its majorating sequence, and

.

Lemma 10.1

For any

and any

\omega_{m,n}=\overline{\omega}_{-m,-n}.

Proof
. This equation follows from the identity

\omega_{I,m,n}=\overline{\omega}_{I+m,-m,-n}
for any interval
I\subset\mathbb{Z}
.

We have the estimates

\begin{array}[]{ccc}\displaystyle\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}|\omega_{I,m,n}|&\leq&\displaystyle\frac{1}{\#I}\sum_{j,n\in\mathbb{Z},\,l\in I}c_{l+m-j}c_{l-j-n}={\bf c}^{2},\\

(10.1)

These estimates imply the following

Corollary 10.1

If
W
is regular then (compare with (
6.17
))

\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}|\omega_{m,n}|\leq{\bf c}^{2},\quad\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}|\omega_{n,m}|\leq{\bf c}^{2}\quad\mbox{ for any $m\in\mathbb{Z}$}.

(10.2)

We put

P_{M}(m)=\{n\in\mathbb{Z}:|n+m|\geq 2M\}.

Lemma 10.2

For any
m\in\mathbb{Z}

\sum_{n\in P_{M}(m)}|\omega_{m,n}|\leq 2{\bf c}\sum_{|k|\geq M}c_{k}.

(10.3)

Proof
. For any integer interval
I

\displaystyle\sum_{n\in P_{M}(m)}|\omega_{I,m,n}|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\#I}\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z},\,n\in P_{M}(m),\,l\in I}c_{l-j+m}c_{l-j-n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z},\,n\in P_{M}(m)}c_{m+n-k}c_{k}\;=\;\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z},\,s\in P_{M}(0)}c_{s-k}c_{k}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z},\,|m|\geq M}c_{m}c_{k}+\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z},\,|k|\geq M}c_{m}c_{k}\;=\;2{\bf c}\sum_{|k|\geq M}c_{k}.

In the limit

we obtain (

).

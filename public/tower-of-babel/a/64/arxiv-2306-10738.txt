Abstract
. Let A = ( a 1 , a 2 , … , a n ) A=(a_{1},a_{2},...,a_{n}) be relative prime positive integers with a i ≥ 2 a_{i}\geq 2 . The Frobenius number F ⁡ ( A ) F(A) is the largest integer not belonging to the numerical semigroup ⟨ A ⟩ \langle A\rangle generated by A A . The genus g ⁡ ( A ) g(A) is the number of positive integer elements that are not in ⟨ A ⟩ \langle A\rangle . The Frobenius problem is to find F ⁡ ( A ) F(A) and g ⁡ ( A ) g(A) for a given sequence A A . In this note, we study the Frobenius problem of A = ( a , b ​ a + d , b 2 ​ a + b 2 − 1 b − 1 ​ d , … , b k ​ a + b k − 1 b − 1 ​ d ) A=\left(a,ba+d,b^{2}a+\frac{b^{2}-1}{b-1}d,...,b^{k}a+\frac{b^{k}-1}{b-1}d\right) and obtain formulas for F ⁡ ( A ) F(A) and g ⁡ ( A ) g(A) when a ≥ k − 1 a\geq k-1 . Our formulas simplifies further for some special cases, such as repunit, Mersenne and Thabit numerical semigroups. The idea is similar to that in [ [ 13 ] ,arXiv:2306.03459].

2.
Preliminary

It is convenient to use the short hand notation

A:=(a,B)=(a,b_{1},b_{2},...,b_{k})

\gcd(A)=1
,
a,b_{i}\in\mathbb{P}

\langle A\rangle=\left\{ax+\sum_{i=1}^{k}b_{i}x_{i}\ \mid x,x_{i}\in\mathbb{N}\right\}

. The

of

. In
[

]

Ape(A,w)=\{N_{0},N_{1},N_{2},...,N_{w-1}\},

where
N_{r}:=\min\{a_{0}\mid a_{0}\equiv r\mod w,\ a_{0}\in\langle A\rangle\}
,

. We usually take

.

A. Brauer and J. E. Shockley
[
4
]
, E. S. Selmer
[

]
gave the following results respectively.

Lemma 2.1

(
[
4
]
,
[
26
]
)
.

Suppsoe

. The
Apéry set
of
a
in
\langle A\rangle
is

Ape(A,a)=\{N_{0},N_{1},N_{2},...,N_{a-1}\}
. Then the Frobenius number and genus of

are respectively:

\displaystyle F(A)

\displaystyle=F(a,B)=\max_{r\in\{0,1,...,a-1\}}N_{r}-a,

\displaystyle g(A)

\displaystyle=g(a,B)=\frac{1}{a}\sum_{r=1}^{a-1}N_{r}-\frac{a-1}{2}.

Now we define the following order relation in

:

. It is proved that this relation

(Proposition 2.20,
[

Let

Note that

.

Let

,
\gcd(a,d)=1,\ d\in\mathbb{P}
. Then we have

\{N_{0},N_{1},N_{2},...,N_{a-1}\}=\{N_{d\cdot 0},N_{d\cdot 1},N_{d\cdot 2},...,N_{d\cdot(a-1)}\}.

(1)

Lemma 2.4

(
[
13
]
)
.

Let

\gcd(A)=1
,

By Lemma

, we can define an intermediate function with respect to

N_{dr}(m):=O_{B}^{H}(ma+r)\cdot a+(ma+r)d.

We also need the following definition.
For a given positive integers sequence

,

(3)

The problem

opt_{B}(M)\leq grd_{B}(M)

(One-Point Theorem,

,

,

Suppose

. Let

. Then the sequence
B=(1,b_{1},...,b_{k},b_{k+1})
is orderly if and only if

.

3.
The Frobenius Problem for

Now we consider the case
A=(a,Ha+dB)=\left(a,ba+d,b^{2}a+\frac{b^{2}-1}{b-1}d,...,b^{k}a+\frac{b^{k}-1}{b-1}d\right)
,
b\geq 2
, i.e.,
H=(b,b^{2},...,b^{k})
and
B=\left(1,\frac{b^{2}-1}{b-1},...,\frac{b^{k}-1}{b-1}\right)
. We first consider

opt_{B}(M)=\min\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{k}x_{i}\mid\sum_{i=1}^{k}\frac{b^{i}-1}{b-1}x_{i}=M,\ \ M,x_{i}\in\mathbb{N},1\leq i\leq k\right\}.

By the
One-Point Theorem
, we can obtain the following result.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
k\in\mathbb{P}
and
b\geq 2
. Then the sequence
B=\left(1,\frac{b^{2}-1}{b-1},...,\frac{b^{k}-1}{b-1}\right)
is orderly, i.e.,

for all

.

Proof.

By induction, we first know that the sequence
(1)
and
\left(1,\frac{b^{2}-1}{b-1}\right)
are orderly sequences. Suppose
\left(1,\frac{b^{2}-1}{b-1},...,\frac{b^{k-1}-1}{b-1}\right)
is orderly. We have
s=\left\lceil\frac{b^{k}-1}{b^{k-1}-1}\right\rceil=\left\lceil\frac{b(b^{k-1}-1)+b-1}{b^{k-1}-1}\right\rceil=b+1
. By
(b+1)\cdot\frac{b^{k-1}-1}{b-1}=\frac{b^{k}-1}{b-1}+b\cdot\frac{b^{k-2}-1}{b-1}
, we have
opt_{B}(s(\frac{b^{k-1}-1}{b-1}))=grd_{B}(s(\frac{b^{k-1}-1}{b-1}))=b+1
. By Lemma
2.5
, the sequence
B
is orderly. This completes the proof.
∎

For a given
M\in\mathbb{P}
and
B=\left(1,\frac{b^{2}-1}{b-1},...,\frac{b^{k}-1}{b-1}\right)

, we have the following properties for

(1)

x_{k}=\left\lfloor\frac{(b-1)M}{b^{k}-1}\right\rfloor

(2)

(3)

if

, then
x_{1}=\cdots=x_{i-1}=0
.

If a solution
X=(x_{1},x_{2},...,x_{k})

satisfies the above conditions (1), (2) and (3), we call

of

.
We define

to be the set of all greedy presentations of

. We define a colexicographic order on

as follows:

\displaystyle(x_{1}^{\prime},x_{2}^{\prime},...,x_{k}^{\prime})\preceq(x_{1},x_{2},...,x_{k})

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow

\displaystyle x_{i}^{\prime}=x_{i}\ \ \text{for any}\ \ i>0\ \ \text{or}

\displaystyle x_{j}^{\prime}<x_{j},x_{i}^{\prime}=x_{i}\ \ \text{for a certain}\ \ j>0\ \ \text{and any}\ \ i>j.

Obviously, the order relation

.

Remark 3.2
.

Lemma
3.1
essentially provides the construction process of

\displaystyle=\min\left\{(b-1)M+\sum_{i=1}^{k}x_{i}\mid\sum_{i=1}^{k}\frac{b^{i}-1}{b-1}x_{i}=M,\ x_{i}\in\mathbb{N},1\leq i\leq k\right\}.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
A=\left(a,ba+d,b^{2}a+\frac{b^{2}-1}{b-1}d,...,b^{k}a+\frac{b^{k}-1}{b-1}d\right)
,

and
\gcd(a,d)=1

is increasing with respect to

. More precisely, if
X=(x_{1},x_{2},...,x_{k})
is a greedy presentation of

N_{dr}=\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}x_{i}\right)a+r((b-1)a+d)=\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}b^{i}x_{i}\right)a+rd.

(4)

Proof.

Given a
m\in\mathbb{N}
. Assuming
X^{\prime}=(x_{1}^{\prime},...,x_{k}^{\prime})
and
X=(x_{1},...,x_{k})
are greedy presentations of
(m+1)a+r

. Furthermore

N_{dr}(m)=\left((b-1)(ma+r)+\sum_{i=1}^{k}x_{i}\right)a+(ma+r)d.

Therefore we have

\displaystyle N_{dr}(m+1)-N_{dr}(m)

\displaystyle=(b-1)a^{2}+ad+\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}x_{i}^{\prime}-\sum_{i=1}^{k}x_{i}\right)a

\displaystyle\geq\left((b-1)a+d+\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}x_{i}^{\prime}-\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}x_{i}\right)a

\displaystyle\geq((b-1)a+d-b-(k-2)(b-1))a

\displaystyle=((b-1)(a-k+1)+d-1)a\geq 0.

Then

is increasing with respect to

. Moreover
N_{dr}=N_{dr}(0)
. This completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 3.4
.

Suppose
X^{\prime}=(x_{1}^{\prime},...,x_{k}^{\prime})\in R(M)

are greedy presentations of
r_{1}
and

, respectively. If
(x_{1}^{\prime},...,x_{k}^{\prime})\preceq(x_{1},...,x_{k})

Proof.

For
X=(x_{1},x_{2},...,x_{k})\in R(M)
, it is easy to obtain that the maximum of
\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}b^{i}x_{i}
is
b^{k}
. At this point, the possible values for
(x_{1},...,x_{k-1})

(b,b-1,b-1,...,b-1),(0,b,b-1,...,b-1),...,(0,0,0,...,b).

We consider the following three cases.

If
x_{i}^{\prime}=x_{i}
for any
1\leq i\leq k
, then
w(r_{1})=w(r_{2})
.

If
x_{k}^{\prime}<x_{k}
, then

\displaystyle w(r_{1})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{k}b^{i}x_{i}^{\prime}=\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}b^{i}x_{i}^{\prime}+b^{k}x_{k}^{\prime}\leq b^{k}+b^{k}x_{k}^{\prime}=b^{k}(1+x_{k}^{\prime})\leq b^{k}x_{k}\leq w(r_{2}).

If there is a certain

, so that
x_{u}^{\prime}<x_{u}
and
x_{u+1}^{\prime}=x_{u+1},...,x_{k}^{\prime}=x_{k}
. Similarly we have

\displaystyle w(r_{1})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{k}b^{i}x_{i}^{\prime}=\sum_{i=1}^{u}b^{i}x_{i}^{\prime}+\sum_{j=u+1}^{k}b^{j}x_{j}^{\prime}

\displaystyle\leq b^{u}(1+x_{u}^{\prime})+\sum_{j=u+1}^{k}b^{j}x_{j}^{\prime}=b^{u}(1+x_{u}^{\prime})+\sum_{j=u+1}^{k}b^{j}x_{j}

\displaystyle\leq b^{u}x_{u}+\sum_{j=u+1}^{k}b^{j}x_{j}\leq w(r_{2}).

This completes the proof.
∎

Now we can obtain the main result of this section.

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
A=\left(a,ba+d,b^{2}a+\frac{b^{2}-1}{b-1}d,...,b^{k}a+\frac{b^{k}-1}{b-1}d\right)
,
a,d,b,k\in\mathbb{P}
,
\gcd(a,d)=1
,
b\geq 2
and
a\geq k-1
. We have

\displaystyle F(A)=\left((b-1)a-b+d+\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}x_{i}\right)_{a-1}\right)a-d,

\displaystyle g(A)=\sum_{r=1}^{a-1}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}x_{i}\right)_{r}+\frac{(a-1)((b-1)a+d-1)}{2},

where
(\sum_{i=1}^{k}x_{i})_{r}
is the sum of elements in the greedy presentation of
r
. Obviously
x_{k}=\left\lfloor\frac{(b-1)r}{b^{k}-1}\right\rfloor
.

Proof.

By Lemma
3.4
and Equation (
4
), we have

\max N_{dr}=N_{d(a-1)}=\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}x_{i}\right)_{a-1}\cdot a+(a-1)((b-1)a+d).

Further from Lemma
2.1
, we can obtain the Frobenius number formula
F(A)
. From Equation (
4
) and Lemma
2.1
again, we can obtain the genus
g(A)
. This completes the proof.
∎

4.
A Generalization of Repunit Numerical Semigroup

In this section we mainly focus on the generalization of repunit numerical semigroup.
For
A=\left(a,ba+d,b^{2}a+\frac{b^{2}-1}{b-1}d,...,b^{k}a+\frac{b^{k}-1}{b-1}d\right)
, suppose
k:=n-1
,
a:=\frac{b^{n}-1}{b-1}
,
b\geq 2
. Therefore we have

A=\Bigg(\frac{b^{n}-1}{b-1},b\cdot\frac{b^{n}-1}{b-1}+d,b^{2}\cdot\frac{b^{n}-1}{b-1}+\frac{b^{2}-1}{b-1}d,...,b^{n-1}\cdot\frac{b^{n}-1}{b-1}+\frac{b^{n-1}-1}{b-1}d\Bigg).

We consider the numerical semigroup
\langle A\rangle
generated by
A
.

By
a-1=\frac{b^{n}-1}{b-1}-1=b\cdot\frac{b^{n-1}-1}{b-1}
,
we have

\displaystyle R(a-1)

\displaystyle=\Big\{(x_{1},x_{2},...,x_{n-1})\mid\ 0\leq x_{i}\leq b\ \ \text{for}\ \ 1\leq i\leq n-1;

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \ \text{if}\ \ x_{i}=b,\ \ \text{then}\ \ x_{j}=0\ \ \text{for}\ \ j\leq i-1\Big\}.

(5)

Theorem 4.1
.

For
A=\left(a,ba+d,b^{2}a+\frac{b^{2}-1}{b-1}d,...,b^{k}a+\frac{b^{k}-1}{b-1}d\right)
, suppose
k:=n-1
,
a:=\frac{b^{n}-1}{b-1}
,
b,n\geq 2
and
\gcd(a,d)=1
. We have

\displaystyle F(A)=(b^{n}+d-1)\cdot\frac{b^{n}-1}{b-1}-d,

\displaystyle g(A)=\frac{(b^{n}-b)(b^{n}+d-1)}{2(b-1)}+\frac{b^{n}(n-1)}{2}.

Proof.

By Theorem
3.5
and
a-1=b\cdot\frac{b^{n-1}-1}{b-1}
, we have

\displaystyle F(A)

\displaystyle=\left((b-1)\cdot\frac{b^{n}-1}{b-1}-b+d+b\right)\cdot\frac{b^{n}-1}{b-1}-d

\displaystyle=(b^{n}+d-1)\cdot\frac{b^{n}-1}{b-1}-d.

Now we consider the genus
g(A)
. Based on the composition of
R(a-1)
mentioned above, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{r=1}^{a-1}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}x_{i}\right)_{r}=

\displaystyle(n-1)b^{n-2}\cdot\sum_{i=0}^{b-1}i+\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\left(b\cdot b^{n-1-j}+(n-1-j)\cdot b^{n-2-j}\cdot\sum_{i=0}^{b-1}i\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(n-1)b^{n-1}(b-1)}{2}+\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\left(b^{n-j}+\frac{b^{n-1-j}(b-1)(n-j-1)}{2}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{b^{n}-b}{2(b-1)}+\frac{b^{n}(n-1)}{2}.

By Theorem
3.5
, we have

\displaystyle g(A)

\displaystyle=\frac{(b^{n}-b)(b^{n}+d-1)}{2(b-1)}+\frac{b^{n}(n-1)}{2}.

This completes the proof.
∎

Let
d=1
in Theorem
4.1
, the
\langle A\rangle
becomes the repunit numerical semigroup (
[
23
]
). Clearly, we have the following corollary.

Corollary 4.2

(Theorem 20, Theorem 25,
[
23
]
)
.

Let
n,b\geq 2
,
d=1
in Theorem
4.1
. Then
S(b,n)=\langle A\rangle
is the repunit numerical semigroup in
[
23
]
. Furthermore, we have

\displaystyle F(S(b,n))=\frac{b^{n}-1}{b-1}b^{n}-1,

\displaystyle g(S(b,n))=\frac{b^{n}}{2}\left(\frac{b^{n}-1}{b-1}+n-1\right).

Theorem 4.3
.

For
A=\left(a,ba+d,b^{2}a+\frac{b^{2}-1}{b-1}d,...,b^{k}a+\frac{b^{k}-1}{b-1}d\right)
, suppose
k:=n-1
,
a:=\frac{b^{n}-1}{b-1}
,
b,n\geq 2
and
\gcd(a,d)=1
. Then
t(A)=n-1
. Furthermore

PF(A)=\{F(A),F(A)-d,...,F(A)-(n-2)d\}.

Proof.

Let
X=(x_{1},x_{2},...,x_{n-1})
be a greedy presentation of
r
,
0\leq r\leq a-1
.
By Equation (
4
), we have

\displaystyle N_{dr}

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}b^{i}x_{i}\right)a+\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\frac{b^{i}-1}{b-1}x_{i}d

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}b^{i}x_{i}\right)a+\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\frac{b^{i}x_{i}}{b-1}d-\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\frac{x_{i}}{b-1}d.

For a given
0\leq r\leq a-1
, we have

r=s\cdot\frac{b^{n-1}-1}{b-1}+r^{\prime}
,
0\leq s\leq b
and
0\leq r^{\prime}\leq\frac{b^{n-1}-b}{b-1}
. Therefore, under the order relation
\preceq_{\langle A\rangle}
, the greedy presentations of the maximal elements in
R(a-1)
are contained in the following elements:

(b,b-1,b-1,...,b-1,b-1),\ (0,b,b-1,...,b-1,b-1),\ (0,0,b,...,b-1,b-1),\ ...,\ (0,0,0,...,0,b).

For the above greedy presentations, we always have

\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}b^{i}x_{i}=b^{n}.

For the above maximal elements, let
X^{\prime}=(x_{1}^{\prime},...,x_{n-1}^{\prime})\in R(a-1)
and
X=(x_{1},...,x_{n-1})\in R(a-1)
be greedy presentations of
r_{1}
and
r_{2}
, respectively.
We need to consider the following case

N_{dr_{1}}-N_{dr_{2}}=\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\frac{x_{i}}{b-1}d-\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\frac{x_{i}^{\prime}}{b-1}d=td,\ \ t=1,2,...,n-2.

Now we need to determine whether
td
belongs to
\langle A\rangle
. If
td\in\langle A\rangle
, there would exist
(y_{0},y_{1},...,y_{n-1})
such that

td=y_{0}a+y_{1}(ba+d)+\cdots+y_{n-1}\left(b^{n-1}a+\frac{b^{n-1}-1}{b-1}d\right).

These
y_{0},...,y_{n-1}\in\mathbb{N}
are not all
0
.
Then we have

(n-2)d\geq\left(t-y_{1}-y_{2}\frac{b^{2}-1}{b-1}-\cdots-y_{n-1}\frac{b^{n-1}-1}{b-1}\right)d=(y_{0}+by_{1}+\cdots+b^{n-1}y_{n-1})a>0.

By
\gcd(a,d)=1
, we have
a|\left(t-y_{1}-y_{2}\frac{b^{2}-1}{b-1}-\cdots-y_{n-1}\frac{b^{n-1}-1}{b-1}\right)
.
However
a>n-2\geq t
, we have
t=y_{1}+y_{2}\frac{b^{2}-1}{b-1}+\cdots+y_{n-1}\frac{b^{n-1}-1}{b-1}
.
So
(y_{0}+by_{1}+\cdots+b^{n-1}y_{n-1})a=0
, a contradiction.

Therefore we have

\max\nolimits_{\preceq_{\langle A\rangle}}Ape(A,a)=\{N_{dr}\mid r=a-\{1,2,...,n-1\}\}.

By Lemma
2.2
, we have

\displaystyle PF(A)

\displaystyle=\{N_{dr}-a\mid r=a-\{1,2,...,n-1\}\}

\displaystyle=\left\{(b^{n}-1)a+\frac{b^{n}d}{b-1}-\left\{\frac{bd}{b-1},\frac{(2b-1)d}{b-1},\frac{(3b-2)d}{b-1},...,\frac{((n-1)b-(n-2))d}{b-1}\right\}\right\}

\displaystyle=\{F(A),F(A)-d,...,F(A)-(n-2)d\}.

This completes the proof.
∎

AdaGrad under Anisotropic Smoothness
Yuxing Liu, Rui Pan, Tong Zhang

Abstract
Adaptive gradient methods have been widely adopted in training large-scale deep neural networks, especially large foundation models. Despite the huge success in practice, their theoretical advantages over classical gradient methods with uniform step sizes across all coordinates (e.g. SGD) have not been fully understood, especially in the large batch-size setting commonly used in practice. This is because the only theoretical result that can demonstrate this benefit was obtained in the original paper of Adagrad for convex nonsmooth objective functions, which is insufficient for large batch algorithms. In this work, we attempt to resolve this gap between theory and practice by proposing a novel anisotropic generalized smoothness assumption and providing corresponding analyses of Adagrad. It is shown that under anisotropic smoothness and noise conditions, AdaGrad can achieve faster convergence guarantees in terms of better dimensional dependence than algorithms with uniform step sizes across all coordinates. Experiments in logistic regression and instruction following fine-tuning tasks provide strong evidence to support our novel assumption and theoretical analysis.

Introduction

To solve the stochastic optimization problem

\displaystyle\min_{\mathbf{w}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(\mathbf{w})\triangleq\mathbb{E}_{\xi}[f(\mathbf{w};\xi)],

(1)

adaptive gradient methods
[
Duchi et al., 2011
,
Zeiler, 2012
,
Tieleman et al., 2012
,
Kingma & Ba, 2014
,
Loshchilov & Hutter, 2017
]
are among the most popular methods. These methods have gained incredible importance from their superior efficiency, especially in training large foundation models, where large batch sizes are commonly employed.
One of the most important features of adaptive gradient methods is the coordinate-wise anisotropic step size.
Take Adagrad
[
Duchi et al., 2011
,
Streeter & McMahan, 2010
]
, the first adaptive gradient method as an example, which writes

\displaystyle\mathbf{w}_{t+1}=\mathbf{w}_{t}-\eta_{t}\mathbf{\Lambda}_{t}^{-1}\mathbf{g}_{t},

where
\mathbf{g}_{t}
notes the stochastic gradient estimation obtained at
\mathbf{w}_{t}
and
\mathbf{\Lambda}_{t}\neq\mathbf{I}_{d}
is a diagonal matrix, representing the square root of a coordinate-wise summation of former gradient estimations.
Despite the huge success in practice, as also noted in
Li et al. [2021]
,
Kunstner et al. [2023]
,
Li et al. [2024]
, the theoretical understanding of when and why adaptive gradient methods enjoy acceleration over classical gradient algorithms with uniform step size across all coordinates such as SGD, is still limited.
On the theory side, the original Adagrad paper
Duchi et al. [2011]
,
Streeter & McMahan [2010]
shows the superiority of Adagrad over SGD in the convex non-smooth scheme, suggesting that if the gradients are sparse and the predictor is limited in an appropriate convex set, Adagrad can converge faster in terms of better dimensional dependence.
However, as the convergence rates in nonsmooth settings rely on the scale of stochastic gradients, their results can be insufficient for the smooth and large-batch training scheme, which is a realistic setting gaining extensive focus. This is because the scale of stochastic gradients does not decrease linearly as the batch size
M
increases. Therefore, if we fix the stochastic gradient computation number
N=MT
, where
M
is the batch size and
T
is the total iteration number, such that increasing
M
leads to a linear decrease in
T
, the original convergence rate
\mathcal{O}(1/\sqrt{T})
may be unsatisfying.
Fine analysis in smooth settings can solve this problem as the obtained convergence results in this case depend on the gradient variance, which decreases linearly as the batch size
M
increases.
Many theoretical papers have also conducted analysis of popular adaptive gradient methods (e.g. Adagrad, Adam) in smooth settings under smoothness and noise assumptions with respect to
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{2}
. However, to the best of our knowledge, their proven results have no better or even worse dimensional dependence than the standard convergence results of SGD in the same settings. The existence of this gap between theory and practice makes us wonder:
Can we obtain better theoretical guarantees of adaptive gradient methods to explain their practical success, especially in large batch settings?

To answer this question, it would be a good starting point to revisit the well-known insight of adaptive gradient methods.
Intuitively, Adagrad shines when the problem is highly imbalanced, i.e., coordinates of the gradients have very different magnitudes. It schedules a larger learning rate (compared to SGD) for coordinates with small gradients and thus converges faster.
This implies that the performance of adaptive gradient methods relies largely on the anisotropic structure of the problem. However, existing standard assumptions fail to describe this property. Take the standard smoothness
[
Nesterov et al., 2018
]
as an example, which assumes a constant
L>0
such that
-L\mathbf{I}_{d}\preceq\nabla^{2}f(\mathbf{w})\preceq L\mathbf{I}_{d}
for all
\mathbf{w}
. It is evident that this assumption is coordinate-wise equivalent and cannot reflect the imbalance between coordinates, thus the benefits of adaptive gradient methods are hidden, as in the results of
Vaswani et al. [2020]
,
Défossez et al. [2020]
,
Wang et al. [2023]
.

To better explore the provable benefits of adaptive gradient methods, it is necessary to employ appropriate assumptions that can better describe the structure of models.
In this paper, we consider the anisotropic smoothness and noise assumptions. Based on these assumptions, we give novel convergence analysis of Adagrad and show that Adagrad can adapt well to the problem’s anisotropic nature.
To extend our results to a more general setting, we additionally introduce a novel generalized anisotropic smoothness assumption and provide corresponding analysis of AdaGrad.
By comparisons between the convergence results of Adagrad and gradient methods with step sizes across all coordinates (SGD and AdaGrad-Norm
[
Streeter & McMahan, 2010
]
as two representatives), we justify the power of adaptive gradient methods when the problem has highly anisotropic nature.

Our contributions are summarized as follows:

1.

We present a fine-grained analysis of Adagrad under anisotropic smoothness and noise assumptions, leading to novel theoretical convergence guarantees for Adagrad. We further introduce a generalized form of anisotropic smoothness, extending these results to more practical settings.

2.

3.

Experiments on logistic regressions on real-world datasets and instruction-following fine-tuning with GPT-2 provide concrete empirical evidence to support our claims.

Theoretical understanding of adaptive gradient methods:

Surprisingly, though the community tends to have a common sense that adaptive gradient methods converge much faster than algorithms with uniform step sizes in specific tasks, theoretical explanations on when and why this happens are relatively rare compared to extensive empirical studies. It is worth pointing out that among all the above-mentioned works on theoretical analysis of adaptive gradient methods, only the initial Adagrad analysis
[
Duchi et al., 2011
]
clearly shows this acceleration in terms of possibly lower dimensional dependence in the online convex programming setting.
How adaptive gradient methods can help accelerate convergence in smooth or large batch stochastic optimization still remains unclear.
We also notice that
Cesa-Bianchi et al. [2007]
,
Orabona & Pál [2015]
,
Orabona & Pál [2018]
,
Zhuang et al. [2022]
mentioned the intuition of scale-free algorithms and
Zhuang et al. [2022]
demonstrates the connection between scale-freeness of adaptive gradient methods and better condition number dependence. More recently,
Zhang et al. [2024]
,
Das et al. [2024]
also investigated why Adam is effective in certain tasks compared to SGD and gives theoretical results in quadratics settings.
However, their results are more intuitive with very restrictive analysis that are insufficient to explain real-world tasks.
In another line of work,
Bernstein et al. [2018a]
,
Wu et al. [2020]
,
Kunstner et al. [2023]
,
Liu et al. [2023a]
suggests a relation between the benefits of adaptive gradient methods and their sign-based nature. These intuitions may shed light on the theoretical understanding of adaptive gradient methods. In parallel with our work, a concurrent study
[

investigates a similar approach, compared to which we further generalize the assumptions to obtain a more broadly applicable analysis.

Preliminaries

3.1
Notations

We use
\odot
to denote the coordinate-wise product of vectors and without leading to confusion,
\sqrt{\cdot}
is sometimes used to denote the coordinate-wise square root of a vector or diagonal matrix.
Let
\mathbf{H}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
be a symmetric positive definite matrix, we denote the vector norm induced by
\mathbf{H}
that

\left\|\mathbf{w}\right\|_{\mathbf{H}}^{2}\triangleq\mathbf{w}^{\top}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{w}.

With a slightly abuse of notation, for a vector
\mathbf{h}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}
, we denote
\left\|\mathbf{w}\right\|_{\mathbf{h}}^{2}=\sum_{j=1}^{d}\mathbf{h}_{j}\mathbf{w}_{j}^{2}
. For a symmetric positive definite matrix
\mathbf{H}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
and convex set
\mathcal{\mathcal{W}}
we introduce the
\mathbf{H}
-based projection operator
\Pi^{\mathbf{H}}_{\mathcal{W}}(\cdot)
such that

\Pi^{\mathbf{H}}_{\mathcal{W}}(\mathbf{w})={\text{argmin}}_{\mathbf{z}\in\mathcal{W}}\left\|\mathbf{z}-\mathbf{w}\right\|_{\mathbf{H}}^{2}.

As discussed in
Hazan et al. [2007]
, the projection is a convex program and can be solved efficiently.

Let us denote
\nabla_{\mathbf{w}}f(\mathbf{w};\xi)
the stochastic gradient oracle at
\mathbf{w}
and
\mathbf{g}_{t}
the gradient estimation employed at
\mathbf{w}_{t}
.
\mathcal{F}_{t}\triangleq\sigma(\mathbf{g}_{0},\cdots,\mathbf{g}_{t-1})
stands for the sigma field of the gradient estimators from the first iteration to the
t-1
iteration. We use
\mathbb{E}[\cdot]
to denote total expectation over
\mathcal{F}_{T}
where
T
is the maximum iteration number and
\mathbb{E}_{t}[\cdot]
as an abbreviation of the conditional expectation
\mathbb{E}[\cdot|\mathcal{F}_{t}]
.

3.2
Problem Settings and Assumptions

We study the stochastic optimization problem (
1
),
where we can only access the stochastic gradient oracle
\nabla f(\mathbf{w};\xi)
at
\mathbf{w}
.
Throughout this paper, we consider the following assumptions.

Assumption 3.1

(Convexity)
.

f(\cdot)
is convex. For convex cases, we search solution in a closed convex set
\mathcal{W}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that there exists at least one optimal solution
\mathbf{w}_{*}\in\mathcal{W}
and

\displaystyle\max_{\mathbf{w},\mathbf{w}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{W}}\left\|\mathbf{w}-\mathbf{w}^{\prime}\right\|_{\infty}\leq D_{\infty}\quad\text{and}\quad\max_{\mathbf{w},\mathbf{w}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{W}}\left\|\mathbf{w}-\mathbf{w}^{\prime}\right\|_{2}\leq D_{2}.

(2)

Assumption 3.2

(Lower bounded)
.

There exists constant
f^{*}
such that for
\mathbf{w}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
f(\mathbf{w})\geq f^{*}
.

Assumption 3.3

(Anisotropic
\mathbf{L}
-smoothness)
.

There exists a positive vector
\mathbf{L}=[L_{1},\dots,L_{d}]\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d}
such that
f(\cdot)
is
\mathbf{L}
-smooth, namely, for
\mathbf{w},\mathbf{w}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\displaystyle\left\|\nabla f(\mathbf{w})-\nabla f(\mathbf{w}^{\prime})\right\|_{\mathbf{L}^{-1}}\leq\left\|\mathbf{w}-\mathbf{w}^{\prime}\right\|_{\mathbf{L}}.

(3)

Assumption
3.1
and
3.2
are standard for convex and nonconvex problems, respectively.
Assumption
3.3
is an anisotropic generalization of the smoothness condition, which has also been employed in a line of work on SignSGD
[
Bernstein et al., 2018a
,
Bernstein et al., 2018b
]
. Its intuition can be understood in the following manner. When the loss function
f(\cdot)
is twice-differentiable, Assumption
3.3
is equivalent to
\nabla^{2}f\preceq\text{diag}(\mathbf{L})
that implies the standard smoothness assumption by
L=\left\|\mathbf{L}\right\|_{\infty}
.
When the Hessian of
f(\cdot)
is imbalanced, namely, coordinates have very different scales,
\mathbf{L}
can be adapted to this imbalanced distribution and can describe a tighter upper bound of the Hessian of
f(\cdot)
, resulting in
\left\|\mathbf{L}\right\|_{1}\ll Ld
. This benefit is realistic as the highly imbalanced spectrum distribution of the Hessian has been widely observed in multiple circumstances
[
Sagun et al., 2016
,
Arjevani & Field, 2020
,
Pan et al., 2021
]
.
The power of this adaptation shines when adaptive gradient methods are employed.

We consider the standard stochastic approximation framework
[
Kushner & Clark, 2012
]
and denote the gradient noise at
\mathbf{w}
to be

\mathbf{n}(\mathbf{w};\xi)\triangleq\nabla f(\mathbf{w})-\nabla_{\mathbf{w}}f(\mathbf{w};\xi).

We assume the following assumptions on gradient noise throughout this paper.

Assumption 3.4

(Unbiased Independent gradient)
.

Each
\nabla f(\mathbf{w};\xi)
is independently drawn and

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbf{n}(\mathbf{w};\xi)\right]=0.

(4)

Assumption 3.5

(Anisotropic noise)
.

There exists positive vector
\bm{\sigma}=[\bm{\sigma}_{1},\dots,\bm{\sigma}_{d}]\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d}
such that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbf{n}_{j}(\mathbf{w};\xi)^{2}\right]\leq\bm{\sigma}_{j}^{2}\quad\text{for all}\quad j\in[d].

(5)

Note that Assumption
3.5
implies the standard bounded noise assumption by
\mathbb{E}[\left\|\mathbf{n}(\mathbf{w};\xi)\right\|_{2}^{2}]\leq\left\|\bm{\sigma}\right\|_{2}^{2}
. Intuitively, it upper bounds all the coordinates of
\mathbf{n}(\mathbf{w};\xi)
instead of only the norm and gives more detailed information on the scale of noise. Generally, Combining Assumption
3.3
and
3.5
allows more fine-grained analysis, which can take the sparsity of the model into account.
Note that the anisotropic noise assumption has also been explored in other lines of studies on sign-based methods
[
Bernstein et al., 2018a
,
Bernstein et al., 2018b
,
Crawshaw et al., 2022
]
and quadratics
[
Dieuleveut et al., 2017
,
Ge et al., 2019
,
Pan et al., 2021
,
Pan et al., 2023
]
. It is also closely related to Assumption 2 in
Zhang et al. [2020b]
, where the authors attempt to model the heavy-tailedness of neural networks.

AdaGrad with Anisotropic Assumptions

How to accelerate the convergence of SGD has been a fundamental problem in training machine learning models. One possible approach is to lower the implicit dependence of dimension
d
.
As discussed in
Nguyen et al. [2019]
, in smooth strongly convex settings, the lower bound of SGD can be
d
times larger than a wider class of algorithms including adaptive gradient methods.
The original Adagrad paper
[
Duchi et al., 2011
]
showed that Adagrad has better dimension dependence than SGD when the stochastic gradients are generally sparse in the non-smooth convex scheme.
However, in the stochastic smooth optimization scheme, to the best of our knowledge, existing theoretical results are insufficient to account for the benefit of adaptive gradient methods.
We attempt to fill the gap, equipped with the anisotropic Assumptions
3.3
and
3.5
.

4.1
Convex Cases

For a warmup, we present results in the convex case to first gain some intuition on how the anisotropic assumptions can better describe the convergence of AdaGrad in the large batch setting.

Similar to the convex case, the introduction of the anisotropic assumptions
3.3
and
3.5
removes the explicit dependence on dimension
d
compared to existing results on adaptive gradient methods like
Défossez et al. [2020]
,
Liu et al. [2023b]
,
Zhang et al. [2022]
,
Wang et al. [2022]
, rendering AdaGrad at least comparable to algorithms with coordinate-wisely uniform step sizes like SGD, which has the following results:

\displaystyle\text{SGD:}\quad\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\nabla f(\mathbf{w})\right\|_{2}^{2}\right]\leq\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{\sqrt{\left\|\mathbf{L}\right\|_{\infty}\Delta}\left\|\bm{\sigma}\right\|_{2}}{\sqrt{MT}}+\frac{\left\|\mathbf{L}\right\|_{\infty}\Delta}{T}\right)

(7)

We can find that when the batch size is large enough such that
M\geq\left\|\bm{\sigma}\right\|_{1}^{2}/(\left\|\mathbf{L}\right\|_{1}\Delta)
, the comparison between Theorem
4.3
and (
7
) is generally consistent with the comparison between SGD and SignSGD
[
Bernstein et al., 2018a
]
. It mainly relies on the sparsity of
\nabla f(\mathbf{w}_{t})
,
\mathbf{L}
, and
\bm{\sigma}
, which can determine the ratio between the upper bounds. Generally speaking, when
\mathbf{L}
and
\bm{\sigma}
are sparse and the gradients
\nabla f(\mathbf{w}_{t})
are relatively dense, Adagrad shines compared to SGD in terms of having a tighter convergence guarantee. We note that this can be a realistic case, as
\mathbf{L}
can be extremely sparse based on many observations on multiple scenarios
[
Sagun et al., 2016
,
Arjevani & Field, 2020
,
Pan et al., 2021
]
, and on the other side,
\frac{\left\|\bm{\sigma}\right\|_{1}}{\left\|\bm{\sigma}\right\|_{2}}
and
\frac{\left\|\nabla f(\mathbf{w}_{t})\right\|_{1}}{\left\|\nabla f(\mathbf{w}_{t})\right\|_{2}}
can be mild constants as examined by
Bernstein et al. [2018a]
. Therefore, with all these conditions satisfied, we show the potentially faster convergence of AdaGrad compared to SGD.
Also, the consistency between the convergence results of AdaGrad and SignSGD provides theoretical insights into the close relation between adaptive gradient methods and sign-based methods.

5
AdaGrad with Generalized Anisotropic Smoothness

In previous sections, we have discussed how AdaGrad can potentially outperform algorithms with uniform step sizes under the anisotropic smoothness settings. However, the loss function in practice can commonly dissatisfy the smoothness assumption, with local smoothness potentially unbounded.
To better describe the complicated real cases,
Zhang et al. [2019]
introduce the
(L_{0},L_{1})
-smoothness as a generalization of the standard
L
-smoothness, which can be written as

\displaystyle\left\|\nabla f(\mathbf{w})-\nabla f(\mathbf{w}^{\prime})\right\|_{2}\leq\left(L_{0}+L_{1}\left\|\nabla f(\mathbf{w})\right\|_{2}\right)\left\|\mathbf{w}-\mathbf{w}^{\prime}\right\|_{2}

(8)

for all
\left\|\mathbf{w}-\mathbf{w}^{\prime}\right\|\leq 1/L_{1}

[
Zhang et al., 2020a
]
. By involving the gradient term to describe the local smoothness, this assumption can even be applicable to describe neural networks
[
Zhang et al., 2019
,
Crawshaw et al., 2022
]
.
In this section, we discuss an extension of both the anisotropic
\mathbf{L}
-smoothness and the
(L_{0},L_{1})
-smoothness and the corresponding convergence results of AdaGrad.

Assumption 5.1

(Anisotropic
(\mathbf{L}_{0},\mathbf{L}_{1})
-smoothness)
.

There exists positive vectors
\mathbf{L}_{0}=[\mathbf{L}_{0,1},\dots,\mathbf{L}_{0,d}]\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d}
and
\mathbf{L}_{1}=[\mathbf{L}_{1,1},\dots,\mathbf{L}_{1,d}]\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d}
such that
f(\cdot)
is (
\mathbf{L}_{0}
,
\mathbf{L}_{1}
)-smooth, namely, for
\mathbf{w},\mathbf{w}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{W}
such that
\left\|\mathbf{w}-\mathbf{w}^{\prime}\right\|_{\mathbf{L}_{1}}\leq\sqrt{d}
, it holds that

\displaystyle\left\|\nabla f(\mathbf{w})-\nabla f(\mathbf{w}^{\prime})\right\|_{(\mathbf{L}(\mathbf{w}))^{-1}}\leq\left\|\mathbf{w}-\mathbf{w}^{\prime}\right\|_{\mathbf{L}(\mathbf{w})},

(9)

where
\mathbf{L}(\mathbf{w})=[[\mathbf{L}(\mathbf{w})]_{1},\dots,[\mathbf{L}(\mathbf{w})]_{d}]\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
[\mathbf{L}(\mathbf{w})]_{j}=\mathbf{L}_{0,j}+\mathbf{L}_{1,j}\left|\partial_{j}f(\mathbf{w})\right|
for all
j\in[d]
.

For one thing, Assumption
5.1
is a natural generalization of Assumption
3.3
and can directly imply it by setting
\mathbf{L}_{1}=0
, hence also enjoys the nice properties we have discussed for Assumption
3.3
.
Also, in the spirits of (
8
), we include the gradient magnitudes to describe the local smoothness. Instead of the gradient 2-norm, we use the absolute value of each coordinate of the gradient, which is intuitively more relevant to the anisotropic nature of curvature. We also conducted numerical experiments to verify the validity of the assumption, as shown in Figure
1
.

Remark 5.2
.

It is also worth noticing that a similar but different coordinate-wise generalized smoothness assumption has been considered in
Crawshaw et al. [2022]
:

\displaystyle\left|\partial_{j}f(\mathbf{w})-\partial_{j}f(\mathbf{w}^{\prime})\right|\leq\left(\mathbf{L}_{0,j}+\mathbf{L}_{1,j}\left|\partial_{j}f(\mathbf{w})\right|\right)\left\|\mathbf{w}-\mathbf{w}^{\prime}\right\|_{2}

(10)

for all
\left\|\mathbf{w}-\mathbf{w}^{\prime}\right\|_{2}\leq 1/\left\|\mathbf{L}_{1}\right\|_{\infty}
and all coordinates
j\in[d]
.
Compared to this assumption, Assumption
5.1
offers several evident advantages: (1) Assumption
5.1
does not require conditions for each coordinate; (2) Experiments show that Assumption
5.1
can well describe the real anisotropic curvature; (3) Technically, it is difficult to avoid explicit existence of
d
in the final convergence bound of AdaGrad if Eqn. (
10
) is employed, for instance, even the convergence of the Generalized SignSGD obtained in
Crawshaw et al. [2022]
has explicit dependence on
d
.

Theorem 5.3

(Convergence of Adagrad with generalized smoothness)
.

Under Assumptions
3.2
,
3.4
,
3.5
,
5.1
, for the sequence
\{\mathbf{w}_{t}\}_{t=1}^{T-1}
generated by Adagrad (Algorithm
1
with option II) with constant step size
\eta_{t}\equiv\eta=\min\left\{\frac{1}{4\left\|\mathbf{L}_{1}\right\|_{\infty}},\sqrt{\frac{\left\|\mathbf{L}_{0}\right\|_{1}}{\Delta}}\right\}
, it holds that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{T}\left(\mathbb{E}\left[\sqrt{\sum_{t=0}^{t-1}\left\|\nabla f(\mathbf{w}_{t})\right\|_{1}^{2}}\right]\right)^{2}=

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathcal{O}}\left(\frac{\sqrt{\left\|\mathbf{L}_{0}\right\|_{1}\Delta}\left\|\bm{\sigma}\right\|_{1}}{\sqrt{MT}}+\frac{\left\|\bm{\sigma}\right\|_{1}^{2}}{M\sqrt{T}}+\frac{\left\|\mathbf{L}_{0}\right\|_{1}\Delta}{T}\right)

\displaystyle+\tilde{\mathcal{O}}\left(\frac{\left\|\mathbf{L}_{1}\right\|_{\infty}\Delta\left\|\bm{\sigma}\right\|_{1}}{\sqrt{MT}}+\frac{\left\|\mathbf{L}_{1}\right\|_{\infty}^{2}\Delta^{2}}{T}\right)

\displaystyle+\tilde{\mathcal{O}}\left(\frac{d\epsilon\left(\sqrt{\left\|\mathbf{L}_{0}\right\|_{1}\Delta}+\left\|\mathbf{L}_{1}\right\|_{\infty}\Delta\right)}{T}+\frac{d\epsilon\left\|\bm{\sigma}\right\|_{1}}{\sqrt{M}T}\right),

where
\Delta=f(\mathbf{w}_{0})-f^{*}
and we use
\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(\cdot)
to hide logarithmic factors.

Note that Theorem
5.3
is a generalization of Theorem
4.3
based on the relation between Assumptions
5.1
and
3.3
, which can directly imply Theorem
4.3
by simply setting
\mathbf{L}_{1}=0
. Intuitively, the interpretation of the theorem leads to the following implications.

Comparison with SGD.

Zhang et al. [2019]
show that SGD generally requires an additional assumption on universally bounded gradients to obtain convergence under the generalized
(L_{0},L_{1})
-smoothness, and thus clipping should be introduced. The theoretical properties of clipped SGD has been extensively studied after that. However, to the best of our knowledge, existing results on clipped SGD either require restrictive noise assumptions
[
Zhang et al., 2019
,
Zhang et al., 2020a
,
Chen et al., 2020
,
Qian et al., 2021
]
or can only shows worse rate on
T
and
M

[
Koloskova et al., 2023
,
Gorbunov et al., 2020
]
. These results are generally incomparable with Theorem
4.3
, showing the superiority of adaptive gradient methods, as also found in existing results
[
Wang et al., 2023
,
Li et al., 2024
]
.

Comparison with AdaGrad-Norm.

One can also look at the convergence results of AdaGrad-Norm
[
Streeter & McMahan, 2010
]
assuming (
8
), a scalar step size variant of AdaGrad:

AdaGrad-Norm:

\displaystyle\quad\left(\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\nabla f(\mathbf{w})\right\|_{2}\right]\right)^{2}\leq\tilde{\mathcal{O}}\left(\frac{\sqrt{L_{0}\Delta}\left\|\bm{\sigma}\right\|_{2}}{\sqrt{MT}}+\frac{L_{0}\Delta}{T}+\frac{L_{1}\Delta\left\|\bm{\sigma}\right\|_{2}}{\sqrt{MT}}+\frac{L_{1}^{2}\Delta^{2}}{T}\right),

(11)

which is obtained by taking
\eta
to optimize the upper bound in Theorem 3 in
Faw et al. [2023]
. It is worth noticing that in the case
\mathbf{L}_{1}=0

4

On the other hand, when it comes to the general
(\mathbf{L}_{0},\mathbf{L}_{1})
-smoothness assumption, things become more complicated given the indeterminate relationship between the anisotropic
\mathbf{L}_{1}
and
L_{1}
in (

).
This relation is mainly about whether Assumption
5.1
or the initial
(L_{0},L_{1})
-smoothness assumption can better fit the common training settings, such as large-scale language model pre-training, which is an intriguing topic worth exploring in the future.

Remark 5.4
.

(Technical Contribution)
From the technical perspective, our proof generally considers a similar main line to
Défossez et al. [2020]
but involves multiple novel proof techniques to remove the restrictive assumption on globally bounded
\left\|\nabla f(\mathbf{w})\right\|
and enable the presence of generalized smoothness. Also, Theorem
4.3
can directly imply a comparable convergence result of AdaGrad-Norm with
Faw et al. [2023]
,
Wang et al. [2023]
while it avoids the complicated proof in
Faw et al. [2023]
and the heavy dependence on
1/\epsilon
in
Wang et al. [2023]
. On the other hand, however,
Faw et al. [2023]
,
Wang et al. [2023]
allows relaxed noise assumption, which may be of interest and we leave it for possible future work.

7
Conclusion

In this paper, we present the theoretical convergence results of Adagrad under anisotropic smoothness and noise assumptions. We further introduce a novel anisotropic generalized smoothness assumption to extend the aforementioned results in a more fine-grained analysis.
Based on the theorems, we conduct comparisons between the convergence rates of AdaGrad and SGD and AdaGrad-Norm in the large batch settings, which provides a deeper theoretical understanding of when and why adaptive gradient methods can outperform classical gradient algorithms with coordinate-wise uniform step sizes.
Empirical studies offer strong evidence to support our proposed assumptions and theory.

Example B.1
.

Consider a finite-sum optimization problem in a hypercube
\mathcal{W}
:

\displaystyle f(\mathbf{w})=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\phi_{i}(\mathbf{x}_{i}^{\top}\mathbf{w}),

where
\phi_{i}:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
are convex and twice-differentiable functions and
\mathbf{x}_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbf{w}\in\mathcal{W}
. We assume that the first and second order derivatives are bounded such that
\left|\phi_{i}^{\prime}(\mathbf{w})\right|\leq G_{1}
and
\phi_{i}^{\prime\prime}(\mathbf{w})\leq G_{2}
for all
i
and
\mathbf{w}\in\mathcal{W}
.
We also assume that the data points
\{\mathbf{x}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
yield that

\displaystyle\left[\sum_{j=1}^{d}\left[{\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{x}_{i,j}^{2}}\right]^{\frac{p}{2}}\right]^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq M_{p},

for
p\geq 1
and denote
\mathbf{H}=1/n\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{x}_{i}\mathbf{x}_{i}^{\top}
.
In this case, stochastic gradient yields that

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mathbf{w}}f(\mathbf{w};\xi)=\mathbf{x}_{\xi}\cdot\phi_{\xi}^{\prime}(\mathbf{x}_{\xi}^{\top}\mathbf{w}),

where
\xi
is uniformly sampled from
\{1,\cdots,n\}
.

Note that Example
B.1
includes a wide range of problems such as linear and logistic regression.
We can check that the problem in Example
B.1
yields Assumptions of Theorem
D.1
and
4.1
. As the stochastic gradients are sampled uniformly with replacement, the variance of gradient noise is reduced by factor
M
, where
M
is the batch size, as proven in Lemma
C.2
.
We also assume an exponential tail to quantify the imbalance of Hessian in our following example.

Assumption B.2
.

We say the problem has an exponential tail when there exists some constants
\tau>1/d
such that

\displaystyle\mathbf{H}_{j,j}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{x}_{i,j}^{2}\propto\exp\left(-\tau j\right)

for all
j=1,\cdots,d
,
where without loss of generality, we assume that
\mathbf{H}_{j,j}\geq\mathbf{H}_{k,k}
for all
j\leq k
.

Note that the exponential tail is a typical example of highly imbalanced distributions of data, which is common in natural language modeling
Zipf [2016]
, social networks
Muchnik et al. [2013]
, and recommender systems
Yin et al. [2012]
.
Then recall the comparison between AdaGrad and SGD in the convex case:

AdaGrad:

\displaystyle\quad\mathbb{E}\left[f(\bar{\mathbf{w}}_{T})-f(\mathbf{w}_{*})\right]=\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{D_{\infty}\left\|\bm{\sigma}\right\|_{1}}{\sqrt{MT}}+\frac{\left\|\mathbf{L}\right\|_{1}D_{\infty}^{2}}{T}\right)

SGD & AdaGrad-Norm:

\displaystyle\quad\mathbb{E}\left[f(\bar{\mathbf{w}}_{T})-f(\mathbf{w}_{*})\right]=\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{D_{2}\left\|\bm{\sigma}\right\|_{2}}{\sqrt{MT}}+\frac{\left\|\mathbf{L}\right\|_{\infty}D_{2}^{2}}{T}\right)

and we take the following ratios to denote the difference in bias and variance terms separately.

\displaystyle R_{1}\triangleq\frac{D_{\infty}\left\|\bm{\sigma}\right\|_{1}}{D_{2}\left\|\bm{\sigma}\right\|_{2}},\quad R_{2}\triangleq\frac{\left\|\mathbf{L}\right\|_{1}D_{\infty}^{2}}{\left\|\mathbf{L}\right\|_{\infty}D_{2}^{2}}.

As we take
\mathcal{W}
to be a hypercube, it holds that
D_{2}^{2}=dD_{\infty}^{2}
.
Concerning
R_{1}
, we have

\displaystyle\left\|\bm{\sigma}\right\|_{1}\leq\max_{\mathbf{w}\in\mathcal{W}}\sum_{j=1}^{d}\sqrt{\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left[\phi_{i}^{\prime}(\mathbf{x}_{i}^{\top}\mathbf{w})\right]^{2}\mathbf{x}_{i,j}^{2}}\leq G_{1}M_{1}

and similarly,

\displaystyle\left\|\bm{\sigma}\right\|_{2}\leq\max_{\mathbf{w}\in\mathcal{W}}\sqrt{\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{d}\left[\phi_{i}^{\prime}(\mathbf{x}_{i}^{\top}\mathbf{w})\right]^{2}\mathbf{x}_{i,j}^{2}}\leq G_{1}M_{2}.

Therefore, based on the heavy-tailed assumption (Assumption
B.2
), we can obtain that

\displaystyle R_{1}=\sqrt{\frac{\phi(\bm{\sigma})}{\phi(D)}}=\frac{M_{1}D_{\infty}}{M_{2}D_{2}}=\frac{M_{1}}{\sqrt{d}M_{2}}\propto\frac{\sum_{j=1}^{d}\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}\tau j\right)}{\sqrt{d\sum_{j=1}^{d}\exp\left(-\tau j\right)}}\leq\frac{\sqrt{1-\exp\left(-\tau\right)}}{1-\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}\tau\right)}\cdot\frac{1}{\sqrt{d}}.

If we consider
\tau
to be a mild constant, it should be clear that
R_{1}
can be close to
1/\sqrt{d}
and
R_{1}\ll 1
.
On the other side, we have for all
\mathbf{w}\in\mathcal{W}
,

\displaystyle\mathbf{0}\preceq\nabla^{2}f(\mathbf{w})=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\phi_{i}^{\prime\prime}(\mathbf{w})\cdot\mathbf{x}_{i}\mathbf{x}_{i}^{\top}\preceq G_{2}\mathbf{H}.

Moreover, that the diagonal matrix
\mathbf{L}_{m}=\text{diagonal}(\mathbf{L})
satisfies
\mathbf{L}_{m}\succeq G_{2}\mathbf{H}
is a sufficient condition for the smoothness assumption (Assumption
3.3
). This is equivalent to that
\left\|\mathbf{L}_{m}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{L}_{m}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\right\|_{2}\leq 1/G_{2}
. Thus actually
\mathbf{L}_{m}
can be taken as the optimal solution to an optimization problem:

\displaystyle\min_{\mathbf{L}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d}}\left\|\mathbf{L}\right\|_{1}\quad\text{s.t.}\quad\left\|\mathbf{L}_{m}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{L}_{m}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\right\|_{2}\leq\frac{1}{G_{2}},

which shares a similar form with the optimal diagonal preconditioner problem in solving linear systems, where an optimal preconditioner for a fixed matrix
\mathbf{H}
can result in much faster convergence as pointed out in
Qu et al. [2022]
. As an example, when
\mathbf{H}
is diagonally dominant, we can choose
\mathbf{L}_{m}=\text{diagonal}({2G_{2}\mathbf{H}})
which takes the diagonal entries. Then we have
\left\|\mathbf{L}\right\|_{\infty}=2G_{2}\mathbf{H}_{1,1}
and
\left\|\mathbf{L}\right\|_{1}=2G_{2}{\rm tr}\left(\mathbf{H}\right)
. In this case, based on the heavy-tailed assumption (Assumption
B.2
), we have

\displaystyle R_{2}=\frac{\phi(\mathbf{L})}{\phi(D)}=\frac{{\rm tr}\left(\mathbf{H}\right)D_{\infty}^{2}}{\mathbf{H}_{1,1}D_{2}^{2}}=\frac{{\rm tr}\left(\mathbf{H}\right)}{d\mathbf{H}_{1,1}}\propto\frac{\sum_{j=1}^{d}\exp(-\tau j)}{d\exp(-\frac{1}{2}\tau)}\leq\frac{1}{1-\exp\left(-\tau\right)}\cdot\frac{1}{d}.

When
\tau
is a mild constant, it should be clear that
R_{2}
can be close to
1/d
and
R_{2}\ll 1
.

To conclude, in this concrete example, we address that
R_{1}
can be close to
1/\sqrt{d}
,
R_{2}
can be close to
1/d
and both
R_{1},R_{2}\ll 1
, showing that AdaGrad has better dimensional dependence than SGD and AdaGrad and the larger gap between bias terms than that of variance terms.

Lemma C.1

(Projection)
.

Suppose
\mathcal{W}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a closed convex set and
\mathbf{\Lambda}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
is symmetric and positive definite. Then for
\mathbf{w}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
\bar{\mathbf{w}}\in\Pi_{C}^{\mathbf{\Lambda}}[\mathbf{w}]
if and only if for all
\mathbf{z}\in\mathcal{W}
,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left\|\mathbf{w}-\mathbf{z}\right\|_{\mathbf{\Lambda}}^{2}\geq\frac{1}{2}\left\|\mathbf{w}-\bar{\mathbf{w}}\right\|_{\mathbf{\Lambda}}^{2}

(13)

or equivalently, for all
\mathbf{z}\in\mathcal{W}
,

\displaystyle\left\langle\mathbf{z}-\bar{\mathbf{w}},\mathbf{\Lambda}(\mathbf{w}-\bar{\mathbf{w}})\right\rangle\leq 0.

(14)

Proof.

Equation (
13
) simply follows the definition. To prove (
14
), take any
\mathbf{z}\in\mathcal{W}
and
\alpha\in(0,1)
we know that
\alpha\mathbf{z}+(1-\alpha)\bar{\mathbf{w}}\in\mathcal{W}
as
\mathcal{W}
is a convex set. Hence by (
13
), we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left\|\mathbf{w}-\bar{\mathbf{w}}\right\|_{\mathbf{\Lambda}}^{2}\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left\|\mathbf{w}-[\alpha\mathbf{z}+(1-\alpha)\bar{\mathbf{w}}]\right\|_{\mathbf{\Lambda}}^{2}

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow\quad\frac{1}{2}\left\|\mathbf{w}-\bar{\mathbf{w}}\right\|_{\mathbf{\Lambda}}^{2}\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left\|(\mathbf{w}-\bar{\mathbf{w}})-\alpha(\mathbf{z}-\bar{\mathbf{w}})\right\|_{\mathbf{\Lambda}}^{2}

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow\quad 0\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha^{2}}{2}\left\|\mathbf{z}-\bar{\mathbf{w}}\right\|_{\mathbf{\Lambda}}^{2}-\alpha\left\langle\mathbf{z}-\bar{\mathbf{w}},\mathbf{\Lambda}(\mathbf{w}-\bar{\mathbf{w}})\right\rangle

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow\quad\left\langle\mathbf{z}-\bar{\mathbf{w}},\mathbf{\Lambda}(\mathbf{w}-\bar{\mathbf{w}})\right\rangle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha}{2}\left\|\mathbf{z}-\bar{\mathbf{w}}\right\|_{\mathbf{\Lambda}}^{2}.

As
\alpha
can be arbitrarily close to
0
, we have that (
14
) holds.
∎

Lemma C.3

(Descent lemma)
.

Under Assumption
5.1
, for all
\mathbf{w},\mathbf{w}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{W}
such that
\left\|\mathbf{w}-\mathbf{w}^{\prime}\right\|_{\mathbf{L}_{1}}\leq\sqrt{d}
, it holds that

\displaystyle f(\mathbf{w}^{\prime})\leq f(\mathbf{w})+\left\langle\nabla f(\mathbf{w}),\mathbf{w}^{\prime}-\mathbf{w}\right\rangle+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{d}\left(\mathbf{L}_{0,j}+\mathbf{L}_{1,j}\partial_{j}f(\mathbf{w})\right)\left|\mathbf{w}^{\prime}-\mathbf{w}\right|_{j}^{2},

(16)

where
\left|\mathbf{w}^{\prime}-\mathbf{w}\right|_{j}
denotes the absolute value of the
j
-th coordinate of
\mathbf{w}^{\prime}-\mathbf{w}
.

Proof.

Denote
\mathbf{w}_{u}=u\mathbf{w}^{\prime}+(1-u)\mathbf{w}
with
u\in[0,1]
. Based on the Taylor expansion, we have

\displaystyle f(\mathbf{w}^{\prime})=

\displaystyle f(\mathbf{w})+\int_{0}^{1}\left\langle\nabla f(\mathbf{w}_{u}),\mathbf{w}^{\prime}-\mathbf{w}\right\rangle{\rm d}u

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f(\mathbf{w})+\left\langle\nabla f(\mathbf{w}),\mathbf{w}^{\prime}-\mathbf{w}\right\rangle+\int_{0}^{1}\left\langle\nabla f(\mathbf{w}_{u})-\nabla f(\mathbf{w}),\mathbf{w}^{\prime}-\mathbf{w}\right\rangle{\rm d}u

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f(\mathbf{w})+\left\langle\nabla f(\mathbf{w}),\mathbf{w}^{\prime}-\mathbf{w}\right\rangle+\int_{0}^{1}\sum_{j=1}^{d}{(\partial_{j}f(\mathbf{w}_{u})-\partial_{j}f(\mathbf{w}))(\mathbf{w}^{\prime}-\mathbf{w})_{j}}{\rm d}u

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle f(\mathbf{w})+\left\langle\nabla f(\mathbf{w}),\mathbf{w}^{\prime}-\mathbf{w}\right\rangle

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{1}\sqrt{\sum_{j=1}^{d}\frac{\left|\partial_{j}f(\mathbf{w}_{u})-\partial_{j}f(\mathbf{w})\right|^{2}}{\left(\mathbf{L}_{0,j}+\mathbf{L}_{1,j}\left|\partial_{j}f(\mathbf{w})\right|\right)}}\cdot\sqrt{\sum_{j=1}^{d}\left(\mathbf{L}_{0,j}+\mathbf{L}_{1,j}\left|\partial_{j}f(\mathbf{w})\right|\right)\left|\mathbf{w}_{u}-\mathbf{w}\right|_{j}^{2}}{\rm d}u

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq:asm_smooth}}{\leq}

\displaystyle f(\mathbf{w})+\left\langle\nabla f(\mathbf{w}),\mathbf{w}^{\prime}-\mathbf{w}\right\rangle+\int_{0}^{1}\sum_{j=1}^{d}\left(\mathbf{L}_{0,j}+\mathbf{L}_{1,j}\left|\partial_{j}f(\mathbf{w})\right|\right)\left|\mathbf{w}_{u}-\mathbf{w}\right|_{j}^{2}{\rm d}u

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f(\mathbf{w})+\left\langle\nabla f(\mathbf{w}),\mathbf{w}^{\prime}-\mathbf{w}\right\rangle+\sum_{j=1}^{d}\left(\mathbf{L}_{0,j}+\mathbf{L}_{1,j}\left|\partial_{j}f(\mathbf{w})\right|\right)\left|\mathbf{w}^{\prime}-\mathbf{w}\right|_{j}^{2}\cdot\int_{0}^{1}u{\rm d}u

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f(\mathbf{w})+\left\langle\nabla f(\mathbf{w}),\mathbf{w}^{\prime}-\mathbf{w}\right\rangle+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{d}\left(\mathbf{L}_{0,j}+\mathbf{L}_{1,j}\left|\partial_{j}f(\mathbf{w})\right|\right)\left|\mathbf{w}^{\prime}-\mathbf{w}\right|_{j}^{2},

where we use Cauchy-Schwarz inequality in the first inequality.
∎

Proof of Theorem
D.1
.

For
\mathbf{w}_{*}\in\mathcal{W}
, it holds that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{t}\left[\left\|\mathbf{w}_{t+1}-\mathbf{w}_{*}\right\|_{2}^{2}\right]\overset{\eqref{eq:lem_projection_1}}{\leq}

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{t}\left[\left\|\mathbf{w}_{t}-\eta_{t}\mathbf{g}_{t}-\mathbf{w}_{*}\right\|_{2}^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\|\mathbf{w}_{t}-\mathbf{w}_{*}\right\|_{2}^{2}-2\eta_{t}\mathbb{E}_{t}\left[\left\langle\mathbf{g}_{t},\mathbf{w}_{t}-\mathbf{w}_{*}\right\rangle\right]+\eta_{t}^{2}\mathbb{E}_{t}\left[\left\|\mathbf{g}_{t}\right\|_{2}^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\|\mathbf{w}_{t}-\mathbf{w}_{*}\right\|_{2}^{2}-2\eta_{t}\left\langle\nabla f(\mathbf{w}_{t}),\mathbf{w}_{t}-\mathbf{w}_{*}\right\rangle+\eta_{t}^{2}\left\|\nabla f(\mathbf{w}_{t})\right\|_{2}^{2}+\eta_{t}^{2}\mathbb{E}_{t}\left[\left\|\mathbf{n}_{t}\right\|_{2}^{2}\right],

where
\mathbf{n}_{t}=\mathbf{g}_{t}-\nabla f(\mathbf{w}_{t})
.
With convexity and standard smoothness of
f(\cdot)
, we have

\displaystyle\left\langle\nabla f(\mathbf{w}_{t}),\mathbf{w}_{t}-\mathbf{w}_{*}\right\rangle\overset{}{\geq}f(\mathbf{w}_{t})-f(\mathbf{w}_{*})\quad\text{and}\quad\left\langle\nabla f(\mathbf{w}_{t}),\mathbf{w}_{t}-\mathbf{w}_{*}\right\rangle\overset{}{\geq}\frac{1}{\left\|\mathbf{L}\right\|_{\infty}}\left\|\nabla f(\mathbf{w}_{t})\right\|_{2}^{2}.

Then after rearranging, it holds that

\displaystyle(2-\eta_{t}\left\|\mathbf{L}\right\|_{\infty})(f(\mathbf{w}_{t})-f(\mathbf{w}_{*}))\leq\frac{1}{\eta_{t}}\left\|\mathbf{w}_{t}-\mathbf{w}_{*}\right\|_{2}^{2}-\frac{1}{\eta_{t}}\mathbb{E}_{t}\left[\left\|\mathbf{w}_{t+1}-\mathbf{w}_{*}\right\|_{2}^{2}\right]+\eta_{t}\mathbb{E}_{t}\left[\left\|\mathbf{n}_{t}\right\|_{2}^{2}\right].

If we take summation and full expectation with
\eta_{t}\leq 1/L
and
\eta_{t}\leq\eta_{t+1}
for
t=1,\cdots,T
, it holds that

\displaystyle\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\mathbb{E}\left[f(\mathbf{w}_{t})-f(\mathbf{w}_{*})\right]\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{1}{\eta_{t}}\left\|\mathbf{w}_{t}-\mathbf{w}_{*}\right\|_{2}^{2}-\frac{1}{\eta_{t}}\left\|\mathbf{w}_{t+1}-\mathbf{w}_{*}\right\|_{2}^{2}\right]+\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\eta_{t}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\mathbf{n}_{t}\right\|_{2}^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq:lem_variance_batch}}{\leq}

\displaystyle\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{1}{\eta_{t}}\left\|\mathbf{w}_{t}-\mathbf{w}_{*}\right\|_{2}^{2}-\frac{1}{\eta_{t}}\left\|\mathbf{w}_{t+1}-\mathbf{w}_{*}\right\|_{2}^{2}\right]+\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\frac{\eta_{t}\left\|\bm{\sigma}\right\|_{2}^{2}}{M}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\eta_{0}}\left\|\mathbf{w}_{0}-\mathbf{w}_{*}\right\|_{2}^{2}-\frac{1}{\eta_{T-1}}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\mathbf{w}_{T}-\mathbf{w}_{*}\right\|_{2}^{2}\right]+\sum_{t=1}^{T-1}\left(\frac{1}{\eta_{t}}-\frac{1}{\eta_{t-1}}\right)\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\mathbf{w}_{t}-\mathbf{w}_{*}\right\|_{2}^{2}\right]

\displaystyle+\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\frac{\eta_{t}\left\|\bm{\sigma}\right\|_{2}^{2}}{M}

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq:asm_convex_set}}{\leq}

\displaystyle\frac{D_{2}^{2}}{\eta_{0}}+\sum_{t=1}^{T-1}\left(\frac{1}{\eta_{t}}-\frac{1}{\eta_{t-1}}\right)D_{2}^{2}+\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\frac{\eta_{t}\left\|\bm{\sigma}\right\|_{2}^{2}}{M}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{D_{2}^{2}}{\eta_{T-1}}+\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\frac{\eta_{t}\left\|\bm{\sigma}\right\|_{2}^{2}}{M}.

Therefore, by having both sides of the inequality divided by
T
and making use of the convexity, we conclude the proof that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[f(\bar{\mathbf{w}}_{T})-f(\mathbf{w}_{*})\right]\overset{\text{Asm.}~\ref{asm:convex_set}}{\leq}\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\mathbb{E}\left[f(\mathbf{w}_{t})-f(\mathbf{w}_{*})\right]\leq\frac{D_{2}^{2}}{\eta_{T-1}T}+\frac{\left\|\bm{\sigma}\right\|_{2}^{2}}{MT}\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\eta_{t},

where
\bar{\mathbf{w}}_{T}=1/T\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\mathbf{w}_{t}
.
Then by taking

\displaystyle\eta_{t}=\min\left\{\frac{1}{\left\|\mathbf{L}\right\|_{\infty}},\sqrt{p\frac{D^{2}_{2}M}{\sigma^{2}_{2}(t+1)}}\right\},

where
p
is a constant,
we can obtain that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[f(\bar{\mathbf{w}}_{T})-f(\mathbf{w}_{*})\right]\leq

\displaystyle\frac{D_{2}^{2}}{\eta_{T-1}T}+\frac{\left\|\bm{\sigma}\right\|_{2}^{2}}{MT}\sum_{t=0}^{t-1}\eta_{t}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\left\|\mathbf{L}\right\|_{\infty}D_{2}^{2}}{T}+\sqrt{\frac{D_{2}^{2}\left\|\bm{\sigma}\right\|_{2}^{2}}{MT}}\cdot\sqrt{\frac{1}{p}}+\sqrt{\frac{D_{2}^{2}\left\|\bm{\sigma}\right\|_{2}^{2}}{MT}}\cdot 2\sqrt{p},

where the second inequality holds as

\displaystyle\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\frac{1}{\sqrt{t+1}}\leq 2\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\frac{1}{\sqrt{t+1}+\sqrt{t}}=2\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\frac{\sqrt{t+1}-\sqrt{t}}{t+1-t}=2\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\sqrt{t+1}-\sqrt{t}=2\sqrt{T}.

Thus by taking
p=\frac{1}{2}
, we finish the proof.
∎

Proof of Theorem
D.2
.

Based on the smoothness condition, it holds that

\displaystyle f(\mathbf{w}_{t+1})\leq

\displaystyle f(\mathbf{w}_{t})-\left\langle\nabla f(\mathbf{w}_{t}),\mathbf{w}_{t+1}-\mathbf{w}_{t}\right\rangle+\frac{\left\|\mathbf{L}\right\|_{\infty}}{2}\left\|\mathbf{w}_{t+1}-\mathbf{w}_{t}\right\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle f(\mathbf{w}_{t})-\eta_{t}\left\langle\nabla f(\mathbf{w}_{t}),\mathbf{g}_{t}\right\rangle+\frac{\left\|\mathbf{L}\right\|_{\infty}\eta_{t}^{2}}{2}\left\|\mathbf{g}_{t}\right\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle f(\mathbf{w}_{t})-\eta_{t}\left\langle\nabla f(\mathbf{w}_{t}),\mathbf{g}_{t}\right\rangle+\left\|\mathbf{L}\right\|_{\infty}\eta_{t}^{2}\left\|\nabla f(\mathbf{w}_{t})\right\|_{2}^{2}+\left\|\mathbf{L}\right\|_{\infty}\eta_{t}^{2}\left\|\mathbf{n}_{t}\right\|_{2}^{2}.

By taking expectation, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[f(\mathbf{w}_{t+1})\right]\leq

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[f(\mathbf{w}_{t})\right]-\eta_{t}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\langle\nabla f(\mathbf{w}_{t}),\mathbf{g}_{t}\right\rangle\right]+\frac{\left\|\mathbf{L}\right\|_{\infty}\eta_{t}^{2}}{2}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\mathbf{g}_{t}\right\|_{2}^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[f(\mathbf{w}_{t})\right]-\eta_{t}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\nabla f(\mathbf{w}_{t})\right\|_{2}^{2}\right]+\frac{\left\|\mathbf{L}\right\|_{\infty}\eta_{t}^{2}}{2}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\nabla f(\mathbf{w}_{t})\right\|_{2}^{2}\right]+\frac{\left\|\mathbf{L}\right\|_{\infty}\eta_{t}^{2}}{2}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\mathbf{n}_{t}\right\|_{2}^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq:lem_variance_batch}}{\leq}

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[f(\mathbf{w}_{t})\right]-\eta_{t}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\nabla f(\mathbf{w}_{t})\right\|_{2}^{2}\right]+\frac{\left\|\mathbf{L}\right\|_{\infty}\eta_{t}^{2}}{2}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\nabla f(\mathbf{w}_{t})\right\|_{2}^{2}\right]+\frac{\left\|\mathbf{L}\right\|_{\infty}\eta_{t}^{2}\left\|\bm{\sigma}\right\|_{2}^{2}}{2M}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[f(\mathbf{w}_{t})\right]-\frac{\eta_{t}}{2}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\nabla f(\mathbf{w}_{t})\right\|_{2}^{2}\right]+\frac{\left\|\mathbf{L}\right\|_{\infty}\eta_{t}^{2}\left\|\bm{\sigma}\right\|_{2}^{2}}{2M},

where
\mathbf{n}_{t}=\nabla f(\mathbf{w}_{t})-\mathbf{g}_{t}
and the last inequality holds as we take
\eta_{t}\leq 1/\left\|\mathbf{L}\right\|_{\infty}
. As
\eta_{t}\equiv\eta
, we have after rearranging that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\nabla f(\mathbf{w}_{t})\right\|_{2}^{2}\right]\leq

\displaystyle\frac{2\mathbb{E}[f(\mathbf{w}_{0})-f(\mathbf{w}_{T})]}{\eta T}+\frac{\left\|\mathbf{L}\right\|_{\infty}\eta\left\|\bm{\sigma}\right\|_{2}^{2}}{M}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{2(f(\mathbf{w}_{0})-f^{*})}{\eta T}+\frac{\left\|\mathbf{L}\right\|_{\infty}\eta\left\|\bm{\sigma}\right\|_{2}^{2}}{M}.

Then by taking
\eta=\min\left\{\frac{1}{\left\|\mathbf{L}\right\|_{\infty}},\sqrt{\frac{(f(\mathbf{w}_{0})-f^{*})M}{\left\|\mathbf{L}\right\|_{\infty}\left\|\bm{\sigma}\right\|_{2}^{2}T}}\right\}
, we finish the proof.
∎

Proof of Theorem

To prove Theorem

, we first look at the standard AdaGrad convergence under the non-smooth convex stochastic optimization setting.

Theorem E.1

(Convergence for convex nonsmooth AdaGrad)
.

Under Assumption
3.1
,
3.4
, for the sequence
\{\mathbf{w}_{t}\}_{t=1}^{T}
generated by Algorithm

\eta_{t}\equiv\eta

\displaystyle\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\mathbb{E}[f(\mathbf{w}_{t})]-f(\mathbf{w}_{*})\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(\eta+\frac{D_{\infty}^{2}}{\eta}\right)\sum_{j=1}^{d}\mathbb{E}\left[\sqrt{\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\nabla f_{t,j}^{2}}+\sqrt{\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\mathbf{n}_{t,j}^{2}}\right]+\frac{\epsilon D_{2}^{2}}{2\eta}

(17)

Proof.

This proof is a stochastic optimization version of the proof of AdaGrad in the online convex learning scheme
Duchi et al. [2011]
,
Streeter & McMahan [2010]

. We also include the proof here for completeness.

First, we give an important result that the gradient norm can be expressed as
\mathbf{\Lambda}_{t}-\mathbf{\Lambda}_{t-1}
.

\displaystyle\left\|\mathbf{g}_{t}\right\|_{\mathbf{\Lambda}_{t}^{-1}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{d}\frac{\mathbf{g}_{t,j}^{2}}{\mathbf{\lambda}_{t,j}}\leq\sum_{j=1}^{d}\frac{\mathbf{g}_{t,j}^{2}}{\mathbf{\lambda}_{t,j}+\mathbf{\lambda}_{t-1,j}}=\sum_{j=1}^{d}\frac{\mathbf{\lambda}_{t,j}^{2}-\mathbf{\lambda}_{t-1,j}^{2}}{\mathbf{\lambda}_{t,j}+\mathbf{\lambda}_{t-1,j}}={\rm tr}\left(\mathbf{\Lambda}_{t}-\mathbf{\Lambda}_{t-1}\right),

(18)

where we note
\mathbf{\lambda}_{t,j}
is the
j

\mathbf{\Lambda}_{t}
. Then we start the proof.

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{t}\left[\left\|\mathbf{w}_{t+1}-\mathbf{w}_{*}\right\|^{2}_{\mathbf{\Lambda}_{t}}\right]\overset{\eqref{eq:lem_projection_1}}{\leq}

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\mathbf{w}_{t}-\mathbf{w}_{*}-\eta_{t}\mathbf{\Lambda}_{t}^{-1}\mathbf{g}_{t}\right\|_{\mathbf{\Lambda}_{t}}^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{t}\left[\left\|\mathbf{w}_{t}-\mathbf{w}_{*}\right\|_{\mathbf{\Lambda}_{t}}^{2}-2\eta_{t}\left\langle\mathbf{g}_{t},\mathbf{w}_{t}-\mathbf{w}_{*}\right\rangle+\eta_{t}^{2}\left\|\mathbf{g}_{t}\right\|_{\mathbf{\Lambda}_{t}^{-1}}^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{t}\left[\left\|\mathbf{w}_{t}-\mathbf{w}_{*}\right\|_{\mathbf{\Lambda}_{t}}^{2}\right]-2\eta_{t}\left\langle\nabla f(\mathbf{w}_{t}),\mathbf{w}_{t}-\mathbf{w}_{*}\right\rangle+\mathbb{E}_{t}\left[\eta_{t}^{2}\left\|\mathbf{g}_{t}\right\|_{\mathbf{\Lambda}_{t}^{-1}}^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq:proof_thm_convex_adagrad_norm_trace}}{\leq}

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{t}\left[\left\|\mathbf{w}_{t}-\mathbf{w}_{*}\right\|_{\mathbf{\Lambda}_{t}}^{2}\right]-2\eta_{t}\left\langle\nabla f(\mathbf{w}_{t}),\mathbf{w}_{t}-\mathbf{w}_{*}\right\rangle+\mathbb{E}_{t}\left[\eta_{t}^{2}{\rm tr}\left(\mathbf{\Lambda}_{t}-\mathbf{\Lambda}_{t-1}\right)\right]

\displaystyle\overset{}{\leq}

\displaystyle\left\|\mathbf{w}_{t}-\mathbf{w}_{*}\right\|_{\mathbf{\Lambda}_{t-1}}^{2}-2\eta_{t}\left(f(\mathbf{w}_{t})-f(\mathbf{w}_{*})\right)+\mathbb{E}_{t}\left[\eta_{t}^{2}{\rm tr}\left(\mathbf{\Lambda}_{t}-\mathbf{\Lambda}_{t-1}\right)\right]

\displaystyle+\mathbb{E}_{t}\left[\left\|\mathbf{w}_{t}-\mathbf{w}_{*}\right\|_{\mathbf{\Lambda}_{t}-\mathbf{\Lambda}_{t-1}}^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq:asm_convex_set}}{\leq}

\displaystyle\left\|\mathbf{w}_{t}-\mathbf{w}_{*}\right\|_{\mathbf{\Lambda}_{t-1}}^{2}-2\eta_{t}\left(f(\mathbf{w}_{t})-f(\mathbf{w}_{*})\right)+\mathbb{E}_{t}\left[\eta_{t}^{2}{\rm tr}\left(\mathbf{\Lambda}_{t}-\mathbf{\Lambda}_{t-1}\right)\right]

\displaystyle+\mathbb{E}_{t}\left[D_{\infty}^{2}{\rm tr}\left(\mathbf{\Lambda}_{t}-\mathbf{\Lambda}_{t-1}\right)\right],

where the third inequality holds as
f(\cdot)
is convex.
After taking
\eta_{t}=\eta
, summing up, taking full expectation and rearrangement, we can obtain that

\displaystyle\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\mathbb{E}\left[f(\mathbf{w}_{t})-f(\mathbf{w}_{*})\right]\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\eta}\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\mathbf{w}_{t}-\mathbf{w}_{*}\right\|_{\mathbf{\Lambda}_{t-1}}^{2}-\left\|\mathbf{w}_{t+1}-\mathbf{w}_{*}\right\|^{2}_{\mathbf{\Lambda}_{t}}\right]

\displaystyle+\left(\frac{\eta}{2}+\frac{D_{\infty}^{2}}{2\eta}\right)\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\mathbb{E}\left[{\rm tr}\left(\mathbf{\Lambda}_{t}-\mathbf{\Lambda}_{t-1}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\epsilon\left\|\mathbf{w}_{0}-\mathbf{w}_{*}\right\|_{2}^{2}}{2\eta}-\frac{\left\|\mathbf{w}_{T}-\mathbf{w}_{*}\right\|^{2}_{\mathbf{\Lambda}_{T-1}}}{2\eta}+\left(\frac{\eta}{2}+\frac{D_{\infty}^{2}}{2\eta}\right)\mathbb{E}\left[{\rm tr}\left(\mathbf{\Lambda}_{T-1}-\epsilon\mathbf{I}_{d}\right)\right]

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq:asm_convex_set}}{\leq}

\displaystyle\frac{\epsilon D_{2}^{2}}{2\eta}+\left(\frac{\eta}{2}+\frac{D_{\infty}^{2}}{2\eta}\right)\sum_{j=1}^{d}\mathbb{E}\left[\sqrt{\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\mathbf{g}_{t,j}^{2}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\epsilon D_{2}^{2}}{2\eta}+\sqrt{2}\left(\frac{\eta}{2}+\frac{D_{\infty}^{2}}{2\eta}\right)\sum_{j=1}^{d}\mathbb{E}\left[\sqrt{\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\nabla f_{t,j}^{2}}+\sqrt{\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\mathbf{n}_{t,j}^{2}}\right],

where we take
\mathbf{\Lambda}_{-1}=\epsilon\mathbf{I}_{d}
and the last inequality holds as
\sqrt{x+y}\leq\sqrt{x}+\sqrt{y}
for all
x,y\geq 0
.
∎

Then we consider giving a bound on the noise summation term.

Lemma E.2

(variance bound)
.

Under Assumption
3.4
and
3.5
, it holds that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{T}\sum_{j=1}^{d}\mathbb{E}\left[\sqrt{\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\mathbf{n}_{t,j}^{2}}\right]\leq\frac{\left\|\bm{\sigma}\right\|_{1}}{\sqrt{MT}}.

Proof.

It holds that

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{d}\mathbb{E}\left[\sqrt{\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\mathbf{n}_{t,j}^{2}}\right]\leq\sum_{j=1}^{d}\mathbb{E}\left[\sqrt{\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\mathbb{E}_{t}\left[\mathbf{n}_{t,j}^{2}\right]}\right]\overset{\eqref{eq:lem_variance_batch}}{\leq}\sum_{j=1}^{d}\sqrt{\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\frac{\bm{\sigma}_{j}^{2}}{M}}=\frac{\sqrt{T}\left\|\bm{\sigma}\right\|_{1}}{\sqrt{M}},

where the first inequality holds as Jensen’s inequality.
∎

Then we are ready to prove Theorem
4.1
.

Proof of Theorem
4.1
.

From Theorem
E.1
, we can obtain that

\displaystyle\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\mathbb{E}[f(\mathbf{w}_{t})]-f(\mathbf{w}_{*})\overset{\eqref{eq:thm_convex_adagrad}}{\leq}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(\eta+\frac{D_{\infty}^{2}}{\eta}\right)\sum_{j=1}^{d}\mathbb{E}\left[\sqrt{\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\nabla f_{t,j}^{2}}+\sqrt{\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\mathbf{n}_{t,j}^{2}}\right]+\frac{\epsilon D_{2}^{2}}{2\eta},

and in Lemma

It holds that

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{d}\mathbb{E}\left[\sqrt{\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\nabla f_{t,j}^{2}}\right]\overset{\eqref{eq:lem_useful_inequality}}{\leq}\mathbb{E}\left[\sqrt{\left\|\mathbf{L}\right\|_{1}\sum_{j=1}^{d}\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\frac{\nabla f_{t,j}^{2}}{L_{j}}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{\left\|\mathbf{L}\right\|_{1}\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{j=1}^{d}\frac{\nabla f_{t,j}^{2}}{L_{j}}\right]}=\sqrt{\left\|\mathbf{L}\right\|_{1}\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\nabla f(\mathbf{w}_{t})\right\|_{\mathbf{L}^{-1}}^{2}\right]},

Moreover, if we make use of Assumption

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2T}{\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\nabla f(\mathbf{w}_{t})\right\|_{\mathbf{L}^{-1}}^{2}\right]}\leq\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\mathbb{E}\left[f(\mathbf{w}_{t})-f(\mathbf{w}_{*})\right].

\displaystyle\sqrt{A}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\left[CC_{1}+\sqrt{C^{2}C_{1}^{2}+4CC_{0}}\right]

\displaystyle\Longrightarrow A

Proof of Theorem

(19)

Lemma F.1

(Bound of

Under the same setting as Theorem

(20)

Proof.

It holds that

\displaystyle\left\langle\nabla f(\mathbf{w}_{t}),\left(\mathbf{\tilde{\Lambda}}_{t}^{-1}-\mathbf{\Lambda}_{t}^{-1}\right)\mathbf{g}_{t}\right\rangle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{d}\nabla f_{t,j}\mathbf{g}_{t,j}\left(\frac{1}{\mathbf{\tilde{\lambda}}_{t,j}}-\frac{1}{\mathbf{\lambda}_{t,j}}\right)

(21)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{d}\frac{\nabla f_{t,j}\mathbf{g}_{t,j}\left(\mathbf{g}_{t,j}^{2}-\mathbb{E}_{t}\left[\mathbf{g}_{t,j}^{2}\right]\right)}{\mathbf{\tilde{\lambda}}_{t,j}\mathbf{\lambda}_{t,j}(\mathbf{\lambda}_{t,j}+\mathbf{\tilde{\lambda}}_{t,j})}

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{d}\frac{\left|\nabla f_{t,j}\right|\left|\mathbf{g}_{t,j}\right|\left|\mathbf{g}_{t,j}^{2}-\mathbb{E}_{t}\left[\mathbf{g}_{t,j}^{2}\right]\right|}{\mathbf{\tilde{\lambda}}_{t,j}\mathbf{\lambda}_{t,j}(\mathbf{\lambda}_{t,j}+\mathbf{\tilde{\lambda}}_{t,j})}

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{d}\frac{\left|\nabla f_{t,j}\right|\left|\mathbf{g}_{t,j}\right|\left|\left|\mathbf{g}_{t,j}\right|-\sqrt{\mathbb{E}_{t}\left[\mathbf{g}_{t,j}^{2}\right]}\right|}{\mathbf{\tilde{\lambda}}_{t,j}\mathbf{\lambda}_{t,j}},

\displaystyle\mathbf{\tilde{\lambda}}_{t,j}^{2}=\mathbf{\lambda}_{t-1,j}^{2}+\mathbb{E}_{t}\left[\mathbf{g}_{t,j}^{2}\right]\geq\mathbb{E}_{t}\left[\mathbf{g}_{t,j}^{2}\right]\quad\text{and}\quad\mathbf{\lambda}_{t,j}^{2}=\mathbf{\lambda}_{t-1,j}^{2}+\mathbf{g}_{t,j}^{2}\geq\mathbf{g}_{t,j}^{2}.

j\in[d]
.
By applying inequality (
30

\displaystyle c=\frac{\mathbb{E}_{t}\left[\left(\left|\mathbf{g}_{t,j}\right|-\sqrt{\mathbb{E}_{t}\left[\mathbf{g}_{t,j}^{2}\right]}\right)^{2}\right]}{\mathbf{\tilde{\lambda}}_{t,j}},\quad x=\frac{\left|\mathbf{g}_{t,j}\right|}{\mathbf{\lambda}_{t,j}},\quad y=\frac{\left|\left|\mathbf{g}_{t,j}\right|-\sqrt{\mathbb{E}_{t}\left[\mathbf{g}_{t,j}^{2}\right]}\right|\left|\nabla f_{t,j}\right|}{\mathbf{\tilde{\lambda}}_{t,j}},

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{t}\left[\frac{\left|\nabla f_{t,j}\right|\left|\mathbf{g}_{t,j}\right|\left|\left|\mathbf{g}_{t,j}\right|-\sqrt{\mathbb{E}_{t}\left[\mathbf{g}_{t,j}^{2}\right]}\right|}{\mathbf{\tilde{\lambda}}_{t,j}\mathbf{\lambda}_{t,j}}\right]\overset{\eqref{eq:lem_basic_inequality}}{\leq}

\displaystyle\frac{\mathbb{E}_{t}\left[\left(\left|\mathbf{g}_{t,j}\right|-\sqrt{\mathbb{E}_{t}\left[\mathbf{g}_{t,j}^{2}\right]}\right)^{2}\right]}{2\mathbf{\tilde{\lambda}}_{t,j}}\mathbb{E}_{t}\left[\frac{\mathbf{g}_{t,j}^{2}}{\mathbf{\lambda}_{t,j}^{2}}\right]+\frac{\mathbf{\tilde{\lambda}}_{t,j}}{2}\frac{\nabla f_{t,j}^{2}}{\mathbf{\tilde{\lambda}}_{t,j}^{2}}

\displaystyle\sqrt{\mathbb{E}_{t}\left[\left(\left|\mathbf{g}_{t,j}\right|-\sqrt{\mathbb{E}_{t}\left[\mathbf{g}_{t,j}^{2}\right]}\right)^{2}\right]}\mathbb{E}_{t}\left[\frac{\mathbf{g}_{t,j}^{2}}{\mathbf{\lambda}_{t,j}^{2}}\right]+\frac{\nabla f_{t,j}^{2}}{2\mathbf{\tilde{\lambda}}_{t,j}}

\displaystyle 2\mathbb{E}_{t}\left[\mathbf{g}_{t,j}^{2}\right]-2\mathbb{E}_{t}\left[\left|\mathbf{g}_{t,j}\right|\right]\sqrt{\mathbb{E}_{t}\left[\mathbf{g}_{t,j}^{2}\right]}\leq 2\mathbb{E}_{t}\left[\mathbf{g}_{t,j}^{2}\right]

\displaystyle 2\mathbf{\tilde{\lambda}}_{t,j}\sqrt{\mathbb{E}_{t}\left[\mathbf{g}_{t,j}^{2}\right]}

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{t}\left[\left(\left|\mathbf{g}_{t,j}\right|-\sqrt{\mathbb{E}_{t}\left[\mathbf{g}_{t,j}^{2}\right]}\right)^{2}\right]=

\displaystyle 2\mathbb{E}_{t}\left[\mathbf{g}_{t,j}^{2}\right]-2\mathbb{E}_{t}\left[\left|\mathbf{g}_{t,j}\right|\right]\sqrt{\mathbb{E}_{t}\left[\mathbf{g}_{t,j}^{2}\right]}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\sqrt{\mathbb{E}_{t}\left[\mathbf{g}_{t,j}^{2}\right]}\left(\sqrt{\mathbb{E}_{t}\left[\mathbf{g}_{t,j}^{2}\right]}-\mathbb{E}_{t}\left[\left|\mathbf{g}_{t,j}\right|\right]\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\sqrt{\mathbb{E}_{t}\left[\mathbf{g}_{t,j}^{2}\right]}\frac{\mathbb{E}_{t}\left[\mathbf{g}_{t,j}^{2}\right]-\mathbb{E}_{t}\left[\left|\mathbf{g}_{t,j}\right|\right]^{2}}{\sqrt{\mathbb{E}_{t}\left[\mathbf{g}_{t,j}^{2}\right]}+\mathbb{E}_{t}\left[\left|\mathbf{g}_{t,j}\right|\right]}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\sqrt{\mathbb{E}_{t}\left[\mathbf{g}_{t,j}^{2}\right]}\frac{\mathbb{E}_{t}\left[\mathbf{g}_{t,j}^{2}\right]-\mathbb{E}_{t}\left[{\mathbf{g}_{t,j}}\right]^{2}}{\sqrt{\mathbb{E}_{t}\left[\mathbf{g}_{t,j}^{2}\right]}+\mathbb{E}_{t}\left[\left|\mathbf{g}_{t,j}\right|\right]}

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq:asm_anisotropic_noise}}{\leq}

\displaystyle\frac{2\sqrt{\mathbb{E}_{t}\left[\mathbf{g}_{t,j}^{2}\right]}\bm{\sigma}_{j}^{2}}{\sqrt{\mathbb{E}_{t}\left[\mathbf{g}_{t,j}^{2}\right]}+\mathbb{E}_{t}\left[\left|\mathbf{g}_{t,j}\right|\right]}\leq 2\bm{\sigma}_{j}^{2}.

Thus by substituting into (
21
), we can obtain that

Lemma F.2

(Bound of
\mathbf{L}_{1,j}\left|\nabla f_{t,j}\right|\frac{\mathbf{g}_{t,j}^{2}}{\mathbf{\lambda}_{t,j}^{2}}

(22)

Proof.

Let us first consider an arbitrary coordinate
j\in[d]
and two cases regarding
\left|\nabla f_{t,j}\right|
.

(1)
If
\left|\nabla f_{t,j}\right|\leq\bm{\sigma}_{j}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{t}\left[\mathbf{L}_{1,j}\left|\nabla f_{t,j}\right|\frac{\mathbf{g}_{t,j}^{2}}{\mathbf{\lambda}_{t,j}^{2}}\right]=\mathbf{L}_{1,j}\left|\nabla f_{t,j}\right|\mathbb{E}_{t}\left[\frac{\mathbf{g}_{t,j}^{2}}{\mathbf{\lambda}_{t,j}^{2}}\right]\leq\mathbf{L}_{1,j}\bm{\sigma}_{j}\mathbb{E}_{t}\left[\frac{\mathbf{g}_{t,j}^{2}}{\mathbf{\lambda}_{t,j}^{2}}\right].

(2)
Else, we have
\left|\nabla f_{t,j}\right|\geq\bm{\sigma}_{j}
. In this case, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{t}\left[\frac{\mathbf{g}_{t,j}^{2}}{\mathbf{\lambda}_{t,j}^{2}}\right]=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{t}\left[\frac{\mathbf{g}_{t,j}^{2}}{\mathbf{\lambda}_{t-1,j}^{2}+\mathbf{g}_{t,j}^{2}}\right]\leq\frac{\mathbb{E}_{t}\left[\mathbf{g}_{t,j}^{2}\right]}{\mathbf{\lambda}_{t-1,j}^{2}+\mathbb{E}_{t}\left[\mathbf{g}_{t,j}^{2}\right]}\overset{\eqref{eq:asm_anisotropic_noise}}{\leq}\frac{\nabla f_{t,j}^{2}+\bm{\sigma}_{j}^{2}}{\mathbf{\lambda}_{t-1,j}^{2}+\mathbb{E}_{t}\left[\mathbf{g}_{t,j}^{2}\right]}\leq\frac{2\nabla f_{t,j}^{2}}{\mathbf{\tilde{\lambda}}_{t,j}^{2}},

where we use the Jensen inequality of convex function
h(x)=\frac{-x}{a^{2}+x}
in the first inequality. Therefore,

\displaystyle\mathbf{L}_{1,j}\left|\nabla f_{t,j}\right|\mathbb{E}_{t}\left[\frac{\mathbf{g}_{t,j}^{2}}{\mathbf{\lambda}_{t,j}^{2}}\right]\leq 2\mathbf{L}_{1,j}\left|\nabla f_{t,j}\right|\frac{\nabla f_{t,j}^{2}}{\mathbf{\tilde{\lambda}}_{t,j}^{2}}\leq 2\mathbf{L}_{1,j}\frac{\nabla f_{t,j}^{2}}{\mathbf{\tilde{\lambda}}_{t,j}},

where in the last inequality we use the fact that
\left|\nabla f_{t,j}\right|=\left|\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{g}_{t,j}]\right|\leq\sqrt{\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbf{g}_{t,j}^{2}\right]}\leq\mathbf{\tilde{\lambda}}_{t,j}
.
By combining the two cases, we have

\displaystyle\mathbf{L}_{1,j}\left|\nabla f_{t,j}\right|\mathbb{E}_{t}\left[\frac{\mathbf{g}_{t,j}^{2}}{\mathbf{\lambda}_{t,j}^{2}}\right]\leq 2\mathbf{L}_{1,j}\frac{\nabla f_{t,j}^{2}}{\mathbf{\tilde{\lambda}}_{t,j}}+\mathbf{L}_{1,j}\bm{\sigma}_{j}\mathbb{E}_{t}\left[\frac{\mathbf{g}_{t,j}^{2}}{\mathbf{\lambda}_{t,j}^{2}}\right]

and by summing up, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{t}\left[\sum_{j=1}^{d}\mathbf{L}_{1,j}\left|\nabla f_{t,j}\right|\frac{\mathbf{g}_{t,j}^{2}}{\mathbf{\lambda}_{t,j}^{2}}\right]\leq 2\left\|\mathbf{L}_{1}\right\|_{\infty}\left\langle\nabla f(\mathbf{w}_{t}),\mathbf{\tilde{\Lambda}}_{t}^{-1}\nabla f(\mathbf{w}_{t})\right\rangle+\sum_{j=1}^{d}\mathbf{L}_{1,j}\bm{\sigma}_{j}\mathbb{E}_{t}\left[\frac{\mathbf{g}_{t,j}^{2}}{\mathbf{\lambda}_{t,j}^{2}}\right].

=&\sum_{j=1}^{d}\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\left|\nabla f_{t,j}\right|^{2}}{\sqrt{\mathbf{\lambda}_{T-1,j}^{2}+\sum_{s=0}^{T-1}\nabla f_{s,j}^{2}+\bm{\sigma}_{j}^{2}}}\right]\\
\geq&\sum_{j=1}^{d}\frac{\mathbb{E}\left[\sqrt{\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\left|\nabla f_{t,j}\right|^{2}}\right]^{2}}{\mathbb{E}\left[\sqrt{\mathbf{\lambda}_{T-1,j}^{2}+\sum_{s=0}^{T-1}\nabla f_{s,j}^{2}+\bm{\sigma}_{j}^{2}}\right]}\\
\geq&\frac{\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{j=1}^{d}\sqrt{\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\left|\nabla f_{t,j}\right|^{2}}\right]^{2}}{\sum_{j=1}^{d}\mathbb{E}\left[\sqrt{\mathbf{\lambda}_{T-1,j}^{2}+\sum_{s=0}^{T-1}\nabla f_{s,j}^{2}+\bm{\sigma}_{j}^{2}}\right]},\end{split}

(27)

where in the second last and last inequality we apply Cauchy-Schwarz inequality

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\left|XY\right|]\leq\mathbb{E}\left[\left|X\right|^{2}\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}\cdot\mathbb{E}\left[\left|Y\right|^{2}\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}\quad\text{and}\quad\sum_{j=1}^{d}\left|X_{j}Y_{j}\right|\leq\left(\sum_{j=1}^{d}X_{j}^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{d}Y_{j}^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

We can further deal with the denominator such that for all
j\in[d]
,

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathbb{E}\left[\sqrt{\mathbf{\lambda}_{T-1,j}^{2}+\sum_{s=0}^{T-1}\nabla f_{s,j}^{2}+\bm{\sigma}_{j}^{2}}\right]\leq&\mathbb{E}\left[\sqrt{\sum_{s=0}^{T-1}\mathbf{g}_{s,j}^{2}}\right]+\mathbb{E}\left[\sqrt{\sum_{s=0}^{T-1}\nabla f_{s,j}^{2}+\bm{\sigma}_{j}^{2}}\right]\\
=&\mathbb{E}\left[\sqrt{\sum_{s=0}^{T-1}\mathbf{g}_{s,j}^{2}}\right]+\mathbb{E}\left[\sqrt{\sum_{s=0}^{T-1}\left(\mathbf{g}_{s,j}-\mathbf{n}_{s,j}\right)^{2}+\bm{\sigma}_{j}^{2}}\right]\\
\leq&\mathbb{E}\left[\sqrt{\sum_{s=0}^{T-1}\mathbf{g}_{s,j}^{2}}\right]+\mathbb{E}\left[\sqrt{\sum_{s=0}^{T-1}2\mathbf{g}_{s,j}^{2}+2\mathbf{n}_{s,j}^{2}+\bm{\sigma}_{j}^{2}}\right]\\
\leq&3\mathbb{E}\left[\sqrt{\sum_{s=0}^{T-1}\mathbf{g}_{s,j}^{2}}\right]+\mathbb{E}\left[\sqrt{\sum_{s=0}^{T-1}2\mathbf{n}_{s,j}^{2}+\bm{\sigma}_{j}^{2}}\right]\\
\overset{\eqref{eq:asm_anisotropic_noise}}{\leq}&3\mathbb{E}\left[\sqrt{\sum_{s=0}^{T-1}\mathbf{g}_{s,j}^{2}}\right]+\sqrt{T+1}\bm{\sigma}_{j}=3\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbf{\lambda}_{t,j}\right]+\sqrt{T+1}\bm{\sigma}_{j}\end{split}

(28)

where the first and the third inequality holds as
\sqrt{a+b}\leq\sqrt{a}+\sqrt{b}
for
a,b>0
and the second inequality holds as
(a+b)^{2}\leq 2a^{2}+2b^{2}
for
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
.
Therefore, with Lemma
F.1
bounding
{\rm tr}\left(\mathbf{\Lambda}_{t}\right)
, combining (
26
) with (
27
) and (
28
) we can obtain that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{j=1}^{d}\sqrt{\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\left|\nabla f_{t,j}\right|^{2}}\right]^{2}

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq:proof_thm_nonconvex_main},\eqref{eq:proof_thm_nonconvex_frac_left}}{\leq}

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{d}\mathbb{E}\left[\sqrt{\mathbf{\lambda}_{T-1,j}^{2}+\sum_{s=0}^{T-1}\nabla f_{s,j}^{2}}+\bm{\sigma}_{j}\right]

\displaystyle\cdot\left(\frac{4}{\eta}\mathbb{E}[f(\mathbf{w}_{0})-f(\mathbf{w}_{T})]+\left(12\left\|\bm{\sigma}\right\|_{1}+2\eta\left\|\mathbf{L}_{0}\right\|_{1}\right)\ln\left(\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{({\rm tr}\left(\mathbf{\Lambda}_{T-1})^{2}\right)}{\epsilon}\right]\right)\right)

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq:proof_thm_nonconvex_denominator}}{\leq}

\displaystyle\left(3\mathbb{E}\left[{\rm tr}\left(\mathbf{\Lambda}_{T-1}\right)\right]+\sqrt{T+1}\left\|\bm{\sigma}\right\|_{1}\right)

\displaystyle\cdot\left(\frac{4}{\eta}\mathbb{E}[f(\mathbf{w}_{0})-f(\mathbf{w}_{T})]+\left(12\left\|\bm{\sigma}\right\|_{1}+2\eta\left\|\mathbf{L}_{0}\right\|_{1}\right)\ln\left(\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{({\rm tr}\left(\mathbf{\Lambda}_{T-1})^{2}\right)}{\epsilon}\right]\right)\right)

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq:lem_bound_bla}}{\leq}

where we denote

and

\displaystyle C_{\log}=

\displaystyle+5\left(2\eta\left\|\mathbf{L}_{0}\right\|_{1}+5\sqrt{T}\left\|\bm{\sigma}\right\|_{1}\right)\ln\left(\frac{2\eta\left\|\mathbf{L}_{0}\right\|_{1}+5\sqrt{T}\left\|\bm{\sigma}\right\|_{1}}{\epsilon}+{\rm e}\right)\Bigg)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}\left(\log T\right)

based on Lemma
F.4
.
By taking

, where
\Delta\triangleq f(\mathbf{w}_{0})-f^{*}
, we can obtain that

\displaystyle B(\eta)=\tilde{\mathcal{O}}\left(\sqrt{\left\|\mathbf{L}_{0}\right\|_{1}\Delta}+\left\|\mathbf{L}_{1}\right\|_{\infty}\Delta\right).

Then combining the fact proven in Lemma

that

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{d}\sqrt{\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\left|\nabla f_{t,j}\right|^{2}}\geq\sqrt{\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\left\|\nabla f(\mathbf{w}_{t})\right\|_{1}^{2}}

and dividing

\displaystyle\frac{1}{T}\left(\mathbb{E}\left[\sqrt{\sum_{t=0}^{t-1}\left\|\nabla f(\mathbf{w}_{t})\right\|_{1}^{2}}\right]\right)^{2}=

which concludes the proof.

Appendix G
Useful Lemmas

Lemma G.1

(A useful inequality)
.

Assume a non-negative sequence
\{x_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n}
and a positive sequence
\{s_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n}
with
S=\sum_{i=1}^{n}s_{j}
, it holds that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{S}\sum_{j=1}^{n}x_{j}\leq\sqrt{\frac{1}{S}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{x_{j}^{2}}{s_{j}}}.

(29)

The inequality holds as an equality if and only if for all
i=1,\cdots,n
and
j=1,\cdots,n
,

\frac{x_{i}}{s_{i}}=\frac{x_{j}}{s_{j}}.

Proof.

We first multiply
S
by both sides and take the square, then the right-hand side minus the left-hand side will be

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{S}{s_{j}}x_{j}^{2}-\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}x_{j}\right)^{2}=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{s_{i}}{s_{j}}x_{j}^{2}-\sum_{j=1}^{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}x_{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j\neq i}\frac{s_{i}}{s_{j}}x_{j}^{2}-\sum_{j\neq i}x_{i}x_{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j\neq i}\left(\sqrt{\frac{s_{i}}{s_{j}}}x_{j}-\sqrt{\frac{s_{j}}{s_{i}}}x_{i}\right)^{2}\geq 0,

which concludes the proof. Note that this result is an application of the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality.
∎

Lemma G.2

(A basic inequality)
.

For
c\geq 0
and
x,y\in\mathbb{R}
, it holds that

\displaystyle\left|xy\right|\leq\frac{c}{2}x^{2}+\frac{1}{2c}y^{2}.

(30)

Lemma G.3

(sum of ratios with the denominator being the sum of past numerators)
.

Assume a non-negative sequence
(a_{n})
and
\epsilon>0
. We define
b_{n}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}
. Then it holds that

\displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{N}\frac{a_{t}}{b_{t}+\epsilon}\leq\ln\left(\frac{b_{N}+\epsilon}{\epsilon}\right).

(31)

Proof.

It holds that

\displaystyle\frac{a_{t}}{b_{t}+\epsilon}\leq

\displaystyle-\ln\left(1-\frac{a_{t}}{\epsilon+b_{t}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ln(b_{t}+\epsilon)-\ln(b_{t}-a_{t}+\epsilon)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ln(b_{t}+\epsilon)-\ln(b_{t-1}+\epsilon),

where the inequality is based on the fact that
x\leq\ln(1+x)
for all
x>-1
and we set
b_{0}=0
. Then by summing up for
1
to
N
we finish the proof.
∎

Lemma G.4

(Solving inequality
x\leq C_{0}+C_{1}\ln x
)
.

Assume
C_{1}\geq C_{0}\geq 0
and
x>0
. If
x\leq C_{0}+C_{1}\ln x
(where
\ln
denotes the natural logarithm), it holds that for
\zeta\geq 5
,

\displaystyle x\leq 2C_{0}+\zeta C_{1}\ln(C_{1}+e).

(32)

Proof.

Denote
g(x)=x-C_{1}\ln x-C_{0}
. Then we have
g
is a convex function and attains uniform lower bound at
C_{1}
. For
x\geq C_{1}
,
g
is a monotonically increasing function. Therefore, to verify (
32
), it is equivalent to verify that if
x=2C_{0}+\zeta C_{1}\ln(C_{1}+e)
, where
\zeta=5
, then
x\geq C_{0}+C_{1}\ln x
. We begin the verification then.

Denote
z=2C_{0}+\zeta C_{1}\ln(C_{1}+e)
. Then we have

\displaystyle z-(C_{0}+C_{1}\ln z)=

\displaystyle z-\left(C_{0}+C_{1}\ln\left(2C_{0}+\zeta C_{1}\ln(C_{1}+e)\right)\right)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle z-\left(C_{0}+\frac{1}{2}\cdot 2C_{0}+C_{1}\ln\left(\zeta C_{1}\ln(C_{1}+e)\right)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C_{1}\left(\zeta\ln(C_{1}+e)-\ln\left(\zeta C_{1}\ln(C_{1}+e)\right)\right),

where the second inequality is based on the fact that

C_{1}\ln\left(2C_{0}+\zeta C_{1}\ln(C_{1}+e)\right)\leq\frac{1}{2}C_{0}+C_{1}\ln\left(\zeta C_{1}\ln(C_{1}+e)\right)

for
C_{0}\geq 0
and
\zeta\geq 2
.
Then let us consider function
h(y)=\zeta\ln(y+e)-\ln\left(\zeta y\ln(y+e)\right)
for
y\geq 0
and we have
h(1)\geq 0
. We also have

\displaystyle h^{\prime}(y)=\frac{\zeta}{y+e}-\frac{1}{\zeta y\ln(y+e)}\left(\zeta\ln(y+e)+\frac{\zeta y}{y+e}\right)=\frac{\zeta}{y+e}-\frac{1}{y}-\frac{1}{(y+e)\ln(y+e)},

which implies that if
y\geq 1
, we have
h^{\prime}(y)\geq 0
and thus
h(y)\geq 0
for
y\geq 1
. For
y\in[0,1)
, it is also straightforward to obtain that
h(y)\geq\zeta-\ln(\zeta\ln(1+e))\geq 0
. Therefore, we conclude that
h(y)\geq 0
. By substituting
y=C_{1}
, we have

\displaystyle z-(C_{0}+C_{1}\ln z)\geq 0

and thus
x\leq C_{0}+C_{1}\ln x
implies
x\leq z
, which finishes the proof.
∎

Lemma G.5

(comparison of measures)
.

For a sequence
\{a_{t,j}\}
with
t=1,...,T
and
j=1,...,d
, it holds that

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{j=1}^{d}\sqrt{\sum_{t=1}^{T}a_{t,j}^{2}}\right)^{2}\geq\sum_{t=1}^{T}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{d}\left|a_{t,j}\right|\right)^{2}.

(33)

Proof.

It holds that

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{j=1}^{d}\sqrt{\sum_{t=1}^{T}a_{t,j}^{2}}\right)^{2}\geq

\displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{T}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{d}\left|a_{t,j}\right|\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow\quad\sum_{j_{1}\neq j_{2}}^{d}\sqrt{\sum_{t=1}^{T}a_{t,j_{1}}^{2}}\cdot\sqrt{\sum_{t=1}^{T}a_{t,j_{2}}^{2}}\geq

\displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{j_{1}\neq j_{2}}^{d}\left|a_{t,j_{1}}a_{t,j_{2}}\right|

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow\quad\sum_{t=1}^{T}a_{t,j_{1}}^{2}\cdot\sum_{t=1}^{T}a_{t,j_{2}}^{2}\geq

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{t=1}^{T}\left|a_{t,j_{1}}a_{t,j_{2}}\right|\right)^{2},\quad\forall j_{1}\neq j_{2}.

The last inequality follows from the Cauchy-Schwarz Inequality. Thus we finish the proof.
∎

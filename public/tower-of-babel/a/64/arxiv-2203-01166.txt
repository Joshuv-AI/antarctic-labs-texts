Hecke algebras and the Schlichting completion for discrete quantum groups
Adam Skalski, Roland Vergnioux, Christian Voigt

Abstract
. We introduce Hecke algebras associated to discrete quantum groups with commensurated quantum subgroups. We study their modular properties and the associated Hecke operators. In order to investigate their analytic properties we adapt the construction of the Schlichting completion to the quantum setting, thus obtaining locally compact quantum groups with compact open quantum subgroups. We study in detail a class of examples arising from quantum HNN extensions.

Primary 46L67; Secondary 16T20, 20C08, 20G42, 46L05, 46L65

1.
Introduction

Hecke algebras, originally studied in the analysis of Hecke operators for
elliptic modular forms, play a prominent rôle in representation theory and
harmonic analysis. In applications to number theory one is typically interested
in Hecke operators associated with arithmetic groups. Abstractly, the relevant operators can be
described starting from a discrete group
\Gamma
together with a commensurated
subgroup, i.e. a subgroup
\Lambda\subset\Gamma
such that

\Lambda\cap\Lambda^{g}
has finite index in
\Lambda
for all
g\in\Gamma
,
where
\Lambda^{g}=g\Lambda g^{-1}
. At this level of generality, Hecke
operators can be viewed as
\Gamma
-equivariant bounded operators on

\ell^{2}(\Gamma/\Lambda)
and can be described using the Hecke algebra

\mathcal{H}(\Gamma,\Lambda)
, which is nothing but the space of functions on double
cosets
c_{c}(\Lambda\backslash\Gamma/\Lambda)
equipped with a suitable convolution
product.

In their seminal paper
[
BC95
]
, Bost and Connes exhibited an
intriguing connection between Hecke algebras, number theory and noncommutative
geometry. The Hecke algebra underlying the Bost-Connes system
is part of a quantum statistical mechanical system whose equilibrium states are
intimately related to class field theory, and the time evolution of the system can
be explicitly described by means of the modular function

\nabla:g\mapsto[\Lambda:\Lambda\cap\Lambda^{g}]/[\Lambda^{g}:\Lambda\cap\Lambda^{g}],

comparing the number of left and right cosets in a double coset.

Hecke operators and Hecke algebras can also be defined for locally compact
groups
G
together with compact open subgroups
H\subset G
. Moreover, both
situations are related by the Schlichting completion construction which
associates to each discrete Hecke pair
(\Gamma,\Lambda)
in a canonical way a pair
(G,H)

consisting of a totally disconnected locally compact group
G
and a compact open subgroup
H\subset G

such that
\mathcal{H}(\Gamma,\Lambda)\cong\mathcal{H}(G,H)

[
Sch80
]
.
Analytical properties of the algebra of Hecke operators are often easier to analyze at
the level of the Schlichting completion, see
[
Tza03
]
,
[
AD14
]
and

[
KLQ08
]
.

The aim of this article is to extend some of this theory to the case of locally
compact quantum groups, both in the discrete and compact open settings. This
exhibits new combinatorial behavior which is invisible in the classical case, related
to the “relative dimension” constants naturally associated with quantum subgroups of
discrete quantum groups. A major motivation for the passage to the quantum
framework, apart from producing interesting examples of von Neumann algebras, is
to create new locally compact quantum groups out of known discrete quantum
groups. For this purpose we develop a generalization of the Schlichting
completion procedure, which provides a slightly new perspective
even for classical groups, and leads to a deeper understanding of the quantum
quotient spaces
\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Lambda}
for discrete quantum groups. The Schlichting
completion yields algebraic quantum groups in the sense of Van Daele

[
VD98
]
, and we obtain concrete examples by considering pairs

(\mathbbold{\Gamma},\mathbbold{\Lambda})
of discrete quantum groups arising from HNN extensions.

Let us describe the main results obtained in the article. After collecting some
preliminaries in Section
2
, we begin our analysis in the
setting of a subgroup
\mathbbold{\Lambda}
in a discrete quantum group
\mathbbold{\Gamma}
in
Section
3
. We give a detailed description of the
noncommutative quotient space
\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Lambda}
and of the associated module
category, see Proposition
3.5
and
Theorem
3.10
. This allows us to obtain an explicit formula for the
quantum analogue
\mu
of the counting measure on
\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Lambda}
, in terms
of the equivalence relation induced by
\mathbbold{\Lambda}
on irreducible
corepresentations of
\mathbbold{\Gamma}
, see Definition
3.13
. We also give in
Proposition
3.17
a categorical interpretation of the constants

\kappa
that appear in this formula. These constants are trivial in the
classical case.

With this in place it is easy to write down the definition of the
convolution product of the Hecke algebra
\mathcal{H}(\mathbbold{\Gamma},\mathbbold{\Lambda})
, see
Definitions
3.19
and
3.24
. We prove in
Theorem
3.29
that this algebra is canonically isomorphic to the
algebra of Hecke operators, i.e.
\mathbbold{\Gamma}
-equivariant linear maps on

c_{c}(\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Lambda})
. Moreover, in Theorem
3.32
we give a
combinatorial characterization of the boundedness of the Hecke operators on

\ell^{2}(\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Lambda})
, in terms of the constants
\kappa
.

Next we investigate the modular properties of the canonical state on

\mathcal{H}(\mathbbold{\Gamma},\mathbbold{\Lambda})
and give an explicit formula for the corresponding
modular operator in Proposition
3.42
and
Theorem
3.36
. This formula involves the number of left
and right cosets in double cosets, as in the classical case, but in general also the
modular structure of the discrete quantum group
\mathbbold{\Gamma}
, as well as the constants
\kappa
.
In Paragraph
3.3
we consider an explicit class of examples arising from HNN extensions.

In Section
4
we switch to the setting of a locally
compact quantum group
\mathbb{G}
with a compact open quantum subgroup
\mathbb{H}
, working in the
framework of algebraic quantum groups. In this case it is easier to define the
Hecke algebra
\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{G},\mathbb{H})
since compactly supported functions on

\mathbb{H}\backslash\mathbb{G}/\mathbb{H}
are also compactly supported on
\mathbb{G}
, and one can directly use
the convolution product of the quantum algebra of functions
\mathcal{O}_{c}(\mathbb{G})
. Again,
we establish a canonical isomorphism of
\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{G},\mathbb{H})
with the algebra of

\mathbb{G}
-equivariant maps on
c_{c}(\mathbb{G}/\mathbb{H})
in Proposition
4.3
. As
an example, we discuss the case of the quantum doubles
\mathbb{G}=\mathbb{H}\bowtie\hat{\mathbb{H}}
of
a compact quantum group
\mathbb{H}
: in this case
\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{G},\mathbb{H})
identifies with the
algebra of characters of
\mathbb{H}
.

In Paragraph
4.3
we associate a pair
(\mathbb{G},\mathbb{H})
to each
discrete Hecke pair
(\mathbbold{\Gamma},\mathbbold{\Lambda})
by means of a quantum analogue of the
Schlichting completion. More precisely, we construct
\mathcal{O}_{c}(\mathbb{G})
directly as a
subalgebra of
\ell^{\infty}(\mathbbold{\Gamma})
using the Hecke convolution product between

c_{c}(\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Lambda})
and
c_{c}(\mathbbold{\Lambda}\backslash\mathbbold{\Gamma})
, see
Definition
4.5
. This seems to be a new point of view even in
the classical case. Moreover we establish in Proposition
4.14
a
canonical identification between
\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{G},\mathbb{H})
and
\mathcal{H}(\mathbbold{\Gamma},\mathbbold{\Lambda})
. It
follows in particular that Hecke operators are bounded on
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{G}/\mathbb{H})
as
well as on
\ell^{2}(\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Lambda})
, and this yields an analytic proof of
the combinatorial property of the constants
\kappa
mentioned above, see
Definition
3.30
and Theorem
3.32
.

Finally, in Paragraph
4.4
we study the notion of reduced pair
both in the discrete setting and in the compact open one, with the property
of being reduced corresponding to faithfulness of the
\mathbbold{\Gamma}
-action on
\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Lambda}
,
resp. of the
\mathbb{G}
-action on
\mathbb{G}/\mathbb{H}
. We construct a reduced pair associated to an
arbitrary Hecke pair in Propositions
4.19

and
4.23
. Moreover we prove that the Schlichting completion

\mathbb{G}
is non-discrete whenever the Hecke pair
(\mathbbold{\Gamma},\mathbbold{\Lambda})
is reduced
and
\mathbbold{\Lambda}
is infinite, see Lemma
4.21
. It follows
that the Schlichting completions of the Hecke pairs constructed in Section
3.3

via HNN extensions yield non-discrete locally compact quantum groups, whose modular
automorphisms can be computed by the explicit formulas of Paragraph
3.2
.

[

]
,
[
Wor98
]
,
[
VD98
]
,

[
KV00
]
,
[
NT13
]
,
[
VY20
]
.

Tensor products of algebras, minimal/spatial tensor products of
C^{*}
-algebras
and Hilbert space tensor product of spaces/operators will be usually denoted by

\otimes
; if we want to stress that we are dealing with the algebraic tensor
product we will use the symbol
\odot
. If
\varphi
is a linear form on an

A
and
a\in A

a\varphi

\varphi a

a\varphi(b)=\varphi(ba)

2.1.
Algebraic quantum groups

By definition, an algebraic quantum group
\mathbb{G}
is given by a multiplier Hopf

*
-algebra
\mathcal{O}_{c}(\mathbb{G})
together with positive invariant functionals

[
VD98
]
. Recall from
[
KVD97
]

that one can associate to
\mathbb{G}
in a canonical way a locally compact quantum
group, i.e. a (reduced) Hopf
C^{*}
-algebra
C_{0}(\mathbb{G})
satisfying the axioms of
Kustermans and Vaes
[
KV00
]
and containing
\mathcal{O}_{c}(\mathbb{G})
as a dense

*
-subalgebra. We denote by
\varphi
,
\psi
the left and right Haar weights
of
\mathbb{G}
, which are defined on
\mathcal{O}_{c}(\mathbb{G})
. We denote the dual multiplier Hopf
algebra by
\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{G})
.

Not all locally compact quantum groups arise in this way. In particular classical locally compact groups which fit into the algebraic quantum group framework
are precisely the ones admitting a compact open subgroup
[
LVD08
, Section 3]
, and this is precisely the class of groups which
naturally appears in the study of Hecke algebras.

More specifically, if
G
is a locally compact group with a compact open
subgroup
H\subset G
, then we get an algebraic quantum group by considering

\mathcal{O}_{c}(G)=\operatorname{Span}G\cdot\mathcal{O}(H)\subset C_{0}(G),

where
\mathcal{O}(H)
is the usual space of representative functions on
H
, which
embeds canonically in
C_{0}(G)
via extension by
0
, and
(g\cdot f)(x)=f(xg)

for
g\in G
and
f\in C_{0}(G)
is the action by right translation.
The resulting multiplier Hopf
*
-algebra is independent of the choice of
H
,
and in fact uniquely determined
[
LVD08
]
.

A morphism between algebraic quantum groups
from
\mathbb{G}_{1}
to
\mathbb{G}_{2}
is given by a nondegenerate
*
-homomorphism

\pi:\mathcal{O}_{c}(\mathbb{G}_{2})\rightarrow\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{O}_{c}(\mathbb{G}_{1}))
, compatible with the
comultiplications. Observe that the algebraic multiplier algebra

\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{O}_{c}(\mathbb{G}_{1}))
typically contains operators which are unbounded at the
Hilbert space level. However, if
W_{\mathbb{G}_{2}}
denotes the multiplicative unitary associated with
\mathbb{G}_{2}
, then
(\pi\otimes\mathord{\operatorname{id}})(W_{\mathbb{G}_{2}})
is a unitary
element of
\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{O}_{c}(\mathbb{G}_{1})\odot\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{G}_{2}))
, and hence it induces a bounded
(unitary) operator on
L^{2}(\mathbb{G}_{1})\otimes L^{2}(\mathbb{G}_{2})
.
From the properties of the multiplicative unitary at the algebraic level it
follows that this yields in fact a bicharacter at the
C^{*}
-level, and from

[
MRW12
]
we conclude that
\pi
extends to a
nondegenerate
*
-homomorphism
C^{u}_{0}(\mathbb{G}_{2})\rightarrow M(C_{0}^{u}(\mathbb{G}_{1}))

between the universal completions constructed in

[
Kus03
]
. That is,
\pi
determines a morphism between
the associated locally compact quantum groups. For simplicity we will
abbreviate
C_{b}(\mathbb{G}_{1})=M(C_{0}^{u}(\mathbb{G}_{1}))
.

If
\mathbb{G}
is an algebraic quantum group, then an algebraic compact open quantum
subgroup
\mathbb{H}\subset\mathbb{G}
is given by a non-zero central projection

p_{\mathbb{H}}\in\mathcal{O}_{c}(\mathbb{G})
such that

\Delta(p_{\mathbb{H}}){(1\otimes p_{\mathbb{H}})}={p_{\mathbb{H}}\otimes p_{\mathbb{H}}}
, compare
[
KKS16
, Theorem
4.3, Proposition 4.4, Corollary 3.8]
. Then

C(\mathbb{H})=p_{\mathbb{H}}C_{0}(\mathbb{G})
is a Woronowicz-
C^{*}
-algebra and the canonical
morphism from
\mathbb{H}
to
\mathbb{G}
corresponds to the Hopf
*
-homomorphism

\pi_{\mathbb{H}}:C_{0}(\mathbb{G})\to C(\mathbb{H})
,

f\mapsto p_{\mathbb{H}}f
. We note that it seems unclear whether central projections of

C_{0}(\mathbb{G})
satisfying the above condition (which a priori describe all open
compact quantum subgroups of
\mathbb{G}
) automatically lie in
\mathcal{O}_{c}(\mathbb{G})
.

2.2.
Discrete quantum groups

An algebraic quantum group
\mathbbold{\Gamma}
is called discrete if the corresponding
algebra
\mathcal{O}_{c}(\mathbbold{\Gamma})
is a direct sum of matrix algebras. The existence of
invariant functionals is then automatic and we have

C_{0}(\mathbbold{\Gamma})=C_{0}^{u}(\mathbbold{\Gamma})
. In this case we use a different notation to
emphasize the analogy with the classical situation: we put

\mathcal{O}_{c}(\mathbbold{\Gamma})=c_{c}(\mathbbold{\Gamma})
,
C_{0}(\mathbbold{\Gamma})=c_{0}(\mathbbold{\Gamma})
,

C_{b}(\mathbbold{\Gamma})=\ell^{\infty}(\mathbbold{\Gamma})
,
\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{O}_{c}(\mathbbold{\Gamma}))=c(\mathbbold{\Gamma})
,

\mathcal{D}(\mathbbold{\Gamma})=\mathbb{C}[\mathbbold{\Gamma}]
. The left, resp. right Haar weights of
\mathbbold{\Gamma}

are denoted
h_{L}
,
h_{R}
and their modular groups
\sigma^{L}
,
\sigma^{R}
; the
scaling automorphism group will be denoted
\tau
. We will also use the antipode

S
, the unitary antipode
R
and the co-unit
\epsilon
.

We denote by
\operatorname{Corep}(\mathbbold{\Gamma})
the category of finite dimensional normal unital

*
-representations
\alpha:\ell^{\infty}(\mathbbold{\Gamma})\to B(H_{\alpha})
, equipped
with the tensor structure
\tilde{\otimes}
coming from the coproduct:

\alpha\tilde{\otimes}\beta:=(\alpha\otimes\beta)\Delta
; for simplicity we will
simply write
\alpha\otimes\beta
in what follows. We denote
\operatorname{dim}(\alpha)
,
resp.
\operatorname{dim_{\text{$q$}}}(\alpha)
the classical, resp. quantum dimension of a
corepresentation
\alpha
. The trivial corepresentation is denoted

1\in I(\mathbbold{\Gamma})
. We denote
\bar{\alpha}
the conjugate corepresentation of

\alpha
, which is unique up to equivalence; by definition we have non-zero
morphisms
t_{\alpha}\in\operatorname{Hom}(1,\bar{\alpha}\otimes\alpha)
which are uniquely
determined up to a scalar when
\alpha
is irreducible and satisfy the conjugate
equation.

We choose a set
I(\mathbbold{\Gamma})
of representatives of irreducible objects up to
equivalence. We can then identify

\ell^{\infty}(\mathbbold{\Gamma})=\ell^{\infty}\text{-}\bigoplus_{\alpha\in I(\mathbbold{\Gamma})}B(H_{\alpha})

and we have
c_{c}(\mathbbold{\Gamma})=\bigoplus B(H_{\alpha})
and

c(\mathbbold{\Gamma})=\prod B(H_{\alpha})
. We denote by
p_{\alpha}\in c_{c}(\mathbbold{\Gamma})
,

\alpha\in I(\mathbbold{\Gamma})
the minimal central projections and write

a_{\alpha}=p_{\alpha}a
for
a\in c(\mathbbold{\Gamma})
. The coproduct is determined by
the formula
(p_{\beta}\otimes p_{\gamma})\Delta(a)v=va_{\alpha}
for any

v\in\operatorname{Hom}(\alpha,\beta\otimes\gamma)
and
a\in c_{c}(\mathbbold{\Gamma})
. Let us record the
following fact which is certainly well-known to experts.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\alpha,\beta\in I(\mathbbold{\Gamma})
.
The tensor product corepresentation
\alpha\otimes\beta
contains at most

\operatorname{dim}(\beta)^{2}
irreducible subobjects (counted with multiplicities).

Proof.

Decompose
\alpha\otimes\beta=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{p}\gamma_{i}
with

\gamma_{i}\in I(\mathbbold{\Gamma})
. By Frobenius reciprocity we have

\alpha\subset\gamma_{i}\otimes\bar{\beta}
, hence

\operatorname{dim}(\gamma_{i})\geq\operatorname{dim}(\alpha)/\operatorname{dim}(\beta)
. Then we can write

\displaystyle\operatorname{dim}(\alpha)\operatorname{dim}(\beta)={\textstyle\sum}\operatorname{dim}(\gamma_{i})\geq p~\frac{\operatorname{dim}(\alpha)}{\operatorname{dim}(\beta)},

which yields the result.
∎

A quantum subgroup
\mathbbold{\Lambda}
of
\mathbbold{\Gamma}
is given by a surjective

*
-homomorphism
\pi:\ell^{\infty}(\mathbbold{\Gamma})\to\ell^{\infty}(\mathbbold{\Lambda})
such
that
(\pi\otimes\pi)\Delta=\Delta\pi
. Due to the very special structure of

\ell^{\infty}(\mathbbold{\Gamma})
, one can identify

\ell^{\infty}(\mathbbold{\Lambda})
with
p{\Lambda}\ell^{\infty}(\mathbbold{\Gamma})
for a uniquely
determined central projection
p{\Lambda}\in\ell^{\infty}(\mathbbold{\Gamma})
, in such a way
that
\pi(a)=p{\Lambda}a
. The coproduct of
\ell^{\infty}(\mathbbold{\Lambda})
is then

\Delta{\Lambda}(a)=(p{\Lambda}\otimes p{\Lambda})\Delta(a)={(p{\Lambda}\otimes\mathord{\operatorname{id}})}\Delta(a)=(\mathord{\operatorname{id}}\otimes p{\Lambda})\Delta(a)
for

a\in\ell^{\infty}(\mathbbold{\Lambda})
. Note that we have in the same way

c_{c}(\mathbbold{\Lambda})=p{\Lambda}c_{c}(\mathbbold{\Gamma})
,

c(\mathbbold{\Lambda})=p{\Lambda}c(\mathbbold{\Gamma})
. Using precomposition with
\pi
the
category
\operatorname{Corep}(\mathbbold{\Lambda})
is fully and faithfully embedded in

\operatorname{Corep}(\mathbbold{\Gamma})
. We take for
I(\mathbbold{\Lambda})
the subset of
I(\mathbbold{\Gamma})
such
that
\alpha\in I(\mathbbold{\Lambda})
iff
p{\Lambda}p_{\alpha}\neq 0
. We have

1\in I(\mathbbold{\Lambda})
and if
\alpha
,
\beta\in I(\mathbbold{\Lambda})
,

\gamma\in I(\mathbbold{\Gamma})
and
\gamma\subset\alpha\otimes\beta
then

\gamma\in I(\mathbbold{\Lambda})
and
\bar{\alpha}
,
\bar{\beta}\in I(\mathbbold{\Lambda})
.

3.
Discrete quantum Hecke pairs

In this section we define the Hecke algebra associated to a discrete quantum
group
\mathbbold{\Gamma}
and a commensurated quantum subgroup
\mathbbold{\Lambda}
. We start by
studying the structure of the quotient spaces
\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Lambda}
,

\mathbbold{\Lambda}\backslash\mathbbold{\Gamma}
, first at the level of the set of irreducible
corepresentations
I(\mathbbold{\Gamma})
, and then at the finer level of the quantum
algebras of functions
\ell^{\infty}(\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Lambda})
,

\ell^{\infty}(\mathbbold{\Lambda}\backslash\mathbbold{\Gamma})
. We obtain in particular in
Theorem
3.10
a description of
\ell^{\infty}(\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Lambda})

using the classical quotient space
I(\mathbbold{\Gamma})/\mathbbold{\Lambda}
.

We can then define the Hecke algebra
\mathcal{H}(\mathbbold{\Gamma},\mathbbold{\Lambda})
and its convolution
product, and prove that it is represented by Hecke operators on

c_{c}(\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Lambda})
. We give a combinatorial characterization of the

\ell^{2}
-boundedness of Hecke operators and describe the modular properties of
the canonical state of
\mathcal{H}(\mathbbold{\Gamma},\mathbbold{\Lambda})
. Finally we investigate examples
arising from quantum HNN extensions.

3.1.
Quotient spaces

3.1.1.
Classical cosets

Suppose that
\mathbbold{\Gamma}
is a discrete quantum group with a quantum subgroup

\mathbbold{\Lambda}
. Associated to
\mathbbold{\Lambda}
is the equivalence relation
\sim
on

I(\mathbbold{\Gamma})
such that
\alpha\sim\beta
iff

\alpha\subset\beta\otimes\gamma
for some
\gamma\in I(\mathbbold{\Lambda})
iff

\Delta(p_{\alpha})(p_{\beta}\otimes p{\Lambda})\neq 0
, see

[
Ver04
, Lemma 2.3]
. There is corresponding left version:

\alpha\backsim\beta
iff
\alpha\subset\gamma\otimes\beta
for some

\gamma\in I(\mathbbold{\Lambda})
iff

\Delta(p_{\alpha})(p{\Lambda}\otimes p_{\beta})\neq 0
. We denote by

I(\mathbbold{\Gamma})/\mathbbold{\Lambda}
,
\mathbbold{\Lambda}\backslash I(\mathbbold{\Gamma})
the corresponding quotient
spaces and by
[\alpha]
the class of
\alpha\in I(\mathbbold{\Gamma})
in

I(\mathbbold{\Gamma})/\mathbbold{\Lambda}
or in
\mathbbold{\Lambda}\backslash I(\mathbbold{\Gamma})
, with the context
determining the choice of left or right cosets. For

\sigma\in I(\mathbbold{\Gamma})/\mathbbold{\Lambda}
or
\mathbbold{\Lambda}\backslash I(\mathbbold{\Gamma})
we write

p_{\sigma}=\sum_{\alpha\in\sigma}p_{\alpha}\in\ell^{\infty}(\mathbbold{\Gamma})
. Note that
in both cases
p_{[1]}=p{\Lambda}
, and the projections
p_{\sigma}
are
pairwise orthogonal, central and sum up to
1
. Note that they need not be
minimal central projections in
\ell^{\infty}(\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Lambda})
, but are finite
sums of such, see
[
DCKSS18
, Section 5]
and also the
discussion below.

Recall that the quantum quotient spaces are given by the algebras

\displaystyle\ell^{\infty}(\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Lambda})=\{a\in\ell^{\infty}(\mathbbold{\Gamma})\mid(1\otimes p{\Lambda})\Delta(a)=a\otimes p{\Lambda}\},

\displaystyle\ell^{\infty}(\mathbbold{\Lambda}\backslash\mathbbold{\Gamma})=\{a\in\ell^{\infty}(\mathbbold{\Gamma})\mid(p{\Lambda}\otimes 1)\Delta(a)=p{\Lambda}\otimes a\}.

One can use the same conditions to define
c(\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Lambda})
,

c(\mathbbold{\Lambda}\backslash\mathbbold{\Gamma})
but one has to be a bit more careful for the spaces of
finitely supported functions. One can check that

p_{\sigma}\in c(\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Lambda})
for any
\sigma\in I(\mathbbold{\Gamma})/\mathbbold{\Lambda}
and
one defines

c_{c}(\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Lambda})=\operatorname{Span}\{p_{\sigma}c(\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Lambda}),\sigma\in I(\mathbbold{\Gamma})/\mathbbold{\Lambda}\}
(and similarly for the left versions). One can show that

p_{\sigma}c(\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Lambda})=p_{\sigma}\ell^{\infty}(\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Lambda})
for any

\sigma\in I(\mathbbold{\Gamma})/\mathbbold{\Lambda}

[
VV13
, Lemma 3.3]
. It is easy to
check that the coproduct
\Delta
restricts to von Neumann, resp. algebraic left
coactions

\Delta:\ell^{\infty}(\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Lambda})\to\ell^{\infty}(\mathbbold{\Gamma})\bar{\otimes}\ell^{\infty}(\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Lambda})
, resp.
\Delta:c_{c}(\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Lambda})\to\mathcal{M}(c_{c}(\mathbbold{\Gamma})\otimes c_{c}(\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Lambda}))
, and similarly to right coactions

\Delta:\ell^{\infty}(\mathbbold{\Lambda}\backslash\mathbbold{\Gamma})\to\ell^{\infty}(\mathbbold{\Lambda}\backslash\mathbbold{\Gamma})\bar{\otimes}\ell^{\infty}(\mathbbold{\Gamma})
resp.

\Delta:c_{c}(\mathbbold{\Lambda}\backslash\mathbbold{\Gamma})\to\mathcal{M}(c_{c}(\mathbbold{\Lambda}\backslash\mathbbold{\Gamma})\otimes c_{c}(\mathbbold{\Gamma}))
. We have e.g.

(c_{c}(\mathbbold{\Gamma})\otimes 1)\Delta(c_{c}(\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Lambda}))=c_{c}(\mathbbold{\Gamma})\otimes c_{c}(\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Lambda})
.

The next Lemma works in general for open quantum subgroups of locally compact
quantum groups, see
[
KKS16
, Lemma 3.1, Corollary
3.9]
. The fact that the unitary antipode exchanges
the left and right von Neumann algebraic homogeneous spaces for any closed
quantum subgroup can be found for example in

[
KS20
]
. We include a simple proof for the discrete
case.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\mathbbold{\Lambda}
be a quantum
subgroup of
\mathbbold{\Gamma}
. We have

R(c(\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Lambda}))=c(\mathbbold{\Lambda}\backslash\mathbbold{\Gamma})
and

S(c(\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Lambda}))=c(\mathbbold{\Lambda}\backslash\mathbbold{\Gamma})
. The groups
\tau
,

\sigma^{L}
,
\sigma^{R}
stabilize
c(\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Lambda})
and

c(\mathbbold{\Lambda}\backslash\mathbbold{\Gamma})
.

Proof.

For any
\alpha\in I(\mathbbold{\Gamma})
and
t\in\mathbb{R}
we have

S(p_{\alpha})=R(p_{\alpha})=p_{\bar{\alpha}}
and

\tau_{t}(p_{\alpha})=\sigma_{t}^{R}(p_{\alpha})=\sigma_{t}^{L}(p_{\alpha})=p_{\alpha}

hence

S(p{\Lambda})=R(p{\Lambda})=\tau_{t}(p{\Lambda})=\sigma_{t}^{R}(p{\Lambda})=\sigma_{t}^{R}(p{\Lambda})
. For

we have

(p{\Lambda}\otimes 1)\Delta(S(a))=\sigma(S\otimes S)[\Delta(a)(1\otimes S^{-1}(p{\Lambda}))]=\sigma(S\otimes S)[\Delta(a)(1\otimes p{\Lambda})]=\sigma(S\otimes S)(a\otimes p{\Lambda})=p{\Lambda}\otimes S(a)

since we also have

\Delta(R(a))=\sigma(R\otimes R)\Delta(a)

, and similarly on the left. Finally, note
that in the discrete case we have
\tau_{t}=\sigma^{L}_{t}=\sigma^{R}_{-t}
.
∎

\operatorname{Corep}(\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Lambda})

\operatorname{Corep}(\mathbbold{\Gamma})

i.e. the spaces

Remark 3.6
.

In particular
p_{[\alpha]}\ell^{\infty}(\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Lambda})
is a finite
dimensional
C^{*}
-algebra for all
[\alpha]\in I(\mathbbold{\Gamma})/\mathbbold{\Lambda}
, hence

\ell^{\infty}(\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Lambda})
is a von Neumann direct product of matrix
algebras. The inclusion

p_{\alpha}\ell^{\infty}(\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Lambda})\subset B(H_{\alpha})
can be strict,
even for all elements
\alpha
in a given class

\sigma\in I(\mathbbold{\Gamma})/\mathbbold{\Lambda}
— e.g. we always have

p_{[1]}\ell^{\infty}(\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Lambda})=\mathbb{C}
, and for the dual of
SO(3)
seen
as a subgroup of the dual of
SU(2)
we have

p_{\sigma}\ell^{\infty}(\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Lambda})=\mathbb{C}
for both classes

\sigma\in I(\mathbbold{\Gamma})/\mathbbold{\Lambda}
. It can also happen, e.g. for

\mathbbold{\Gamma}_{1}\subset\mathbbold{\Gamma}_{1}\times\mathbbold{\Gamma}_{2}
, that

p_{\alpha}\ell^{\infty}(\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Lambda})
is a proper, non-trivial sub-
C^{*}
-algebra of
B(H_{\alpha})
.

Further, recall that
[
DCKSS18
, Theorem 5.2, Theorem 5.6]

show that each
[\alpha]
corresponds to a certain equivalence class of
minimal central projections in
\ell^{\infty}(\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Lambda})
, determined by
the left adjoint action of
\widehat{\mathbbold{\Gamma}}
, and
p_{[\alpha]}
is equal to
the sum of the projections in the aforementioned equivalence class. Thus the
fact that each
p_{\alpha}\ell^{\infty}(\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Lambda})
is simple is equivalent
to the equivalence relation above being trivial. This need not be the case, as
already classical Clifford theory shows.

One can reformulate the Hecke condition of Definition
3.3

using the left action of
\mathbbold{\Lambda}
on
\ell^{\infty}(\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Lambda})
given by

\alpha(a)=(p{\Lambda}\otimes 1)\Delta(a)\in\ell^{\infty}(\mathbbold{\Lambda})\bar{\otimes}\ell^{\infty}(\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Lambda})
for
a\in\ell^{\infty}(\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Lambda})
. Following
[
DKSS12
, Section
4]
, define an equivalence relation on

by putting

if

\Delta(q_{i})(p{\Lambda}\otimes q_{j})\neq 0
, where
q_{i}
,
q_{j}
are the minimal
central projections in
\ell^{\infty}(\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Lambda})
corresponding to
i
and

j
respectively. Equivalently,
i\equiv{\Lambda}j
iff

j\subset\lambda\otimes i
for some
\lambda\in I(\mathbbold{\Lambda})
with respect to
the module-category structure mentioned previously. Then the Hecke condition is
satisfied iff the action of
\mathbbold{\Lambda}
on
\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Lambda}
“has finite
orbits”, as stated precisely in the next proposition.

Proposition 3.7
.

The pair
(\mathbbold{\Gamma},\mathbbold{\Lambda})
is a
Hecke pair iff the equivalence relation
\equiv{\Lambda}
on

I(\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Lambda})
has finite classes.

Proof.

Denote by
q_{i}\in\ell^{\infty}(\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Lambda})
the minimal central projection
associated with
i\in I(\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Lambda})
. For any

[\alpha]\in I(\mathbbold{\Gamma})/\mathbbold{\Lambda}
we have a finite set

I([\alpha])\subset I(\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Lambda})
such that

p_{[\alpha]}=\sum_{i\in I([\alpha])}q_{i}
. This implies that the action of

\mathbbold{\Lambda}
on
\ell^{\infty}(\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Lambda})
has finite orbits if and only
if for any
[\alpha]\in I(\mathbbold{\Gamma})/\mathbbold{\Lambda}
there exist only finitely many

[\beta]\in I(\mathbbold{\Gamma})/\mathbbold{\Lambda}
such that

\Delta(p_{[\alpha]})(p{\Lambda}\otimes p_{[\beta]})\neq 0
. This means that
we can find
\alpha^{\prime}\in[\alpha]
,
\beta^{\prime}\in[\beta]
such that

\alpha^{\prime}\backsim\beta^{\prime}
, in other words
\llbracket\alpha\rrbracket=\llbracket\beta\rrbracket
. This ends the
proof.
∎

. Then, if
(v_{i})_{i}
is an ONB of

\operatorname{Hom}(\alpha,\lambda\otimes\beta)
and

P_{\alpha}^{\lambda,\beta}=\sum v_{i}v_{i}^{*}\in B(H_{\lambda}\otimes H_{\beta})

denotes the orthogonal projection onto the
\alpha
-isotypic component of

\lambda\otimes\beta
, we have

\displaystyle(h_{R}p_{\lambda}\otimes p_{\beta})\Delta(p_{\alpha})

\displaystyle=\operatorname{dim_{\text{$q$}}}(\lambda)~{\textstyle\sum_{i}}(t_{\lambda}^{*}\otimes\mathord{\operatorname{id}})(\mathord{\operatorname{id}}\otimes v_{i}p_{\alpha}v_{i}^{*})(t_{\lambda}\otimes\mathord{\operatorname{id}})

\displaystyle=\operatorname{dim_{\text{$q$}}}(\lambda)~(t_{\lambda}^{*}\otimes\mathord{\operatorname{id}})(\mathord{\operatorname{id}}\otimes P_{\alpha}^{\lambda,\beta})(t_{\lambda}\otimes\mathord{\operatorname{id}})\in\operatorname{Hom}(\beta,\beta).

Hence there is a scalar
\kappa_{\alpha,\bar{\beta}}^{\lambda}\geq 0
such that

(h_{R}p_{\lambda}\otimes p_{\beta})\Delta(p_{\alpha})=\kappa_{\alpha,\bar{\beta}}^{\lambda}p_{\beta}
. This scalar can be computed by
evaluating both sides against
h_{R}
. We obtain, after dividing both sides
by
\operatorname{dim_{\text{$q$}}}(\beta)
:

\displaystyle\operatorname{dim_{\text{$q$}}}(\beta)\times\kappa_{\alpha,\bar{\beta}}^{\lambda}

\displaystyle=\operatorname{dim_{\text{$q$}}}(\lambda)~t_{\beta}^{*}(\mathord{\operatorname{id}}\otimes t_{\lambda}^{*}\otimes\mathord{\operatorname{id}})(\mathord{\operatorname{id}}\otimes\mathord{\operatorname{id}}\otimes P_{\alpha}^{\lambda,\beta})(\mathord{\operatorname{id}}\otimes t_{\lambda}\otimes\mathord{\operatorname{id}})t_{\beta}

\displaystyle=\operatorname{dim_{\text{$q$}}}(\lambda)~t_{\lambda\otimes\beta}^{*}(\mathord{\operatorname{id}}_{\bar{\beta}\otimes\bar{\lambda}}\otimes P_{\alpha}^{\lambda,\beta})t_{\lambda\otimes\beta}=\operatorname{dim_{\text{$q$}}}(\lambda)~{\textstyle\sum_{i}}t_{\lambda\otimes\beta}^{*}(\mathord{\operatorname{id}}_{\bar{\beta}\otimes\bar{\lambda}}\otimes v_{i}v_{i}^{*})t_{\lambda\otimes\beta}

\displaystyle=\operatorname{dim_{\text{$q$}}}(\lambda)c_{\alpha}^{\lambda,\beta}t_{\alpha}^{*}t_{\alpha}=c_{\alpha}^{\lambda,\beta}\operatorname{dim_{\text{$q$}}}(\lambda)\operatorname{dim_{\text{$q$}}}(\alpha),

where
c_{\alpha}^{\lambda,\beta}=\operatorname{dim}(\operatorname{Hom}(\alpha,\lambda\otimes\beta))
. Note that an analogous formula appears already in work of Izumi, see the remark before
Corollary 3.7 in
[
Izu02
]
.

By Frobenius reciprocity we also have

c_{\alpha}^{\lambda,\beta}=c_{\lambda}^{\alpha,\bar{\beta}}
. Summing over

\lambda\in I(\mathbbold{\Lambda})
we get

\displaystyle\operatorname{dim_{\text{$q$}}}(\beta)\times(h_{R}p{\Lambda}\otimes p_{\beta})\Delta(p_{\alpha})

\displaystyle=\operatorname{dim_{\text{$q$}}}(\alpha)\left(\textstyle{\sum_{\lambda\in I(\mathbbold{\Lambda})}}c_{\lambda}^{\alpha,\bar{\beta}}\operatorname{dim_{\text{$q$}}}(\lambda)\right)p_{\beta}

\displaystyle=\operatorname{dim_{\text{$q$}}}(\alpha)\operatorname{dim_{\text{$q$}}}(\alpha\otimes\bar{\beta}){\Lambda}p_{\beta},

which yields the claim.
∎

Theorem

below is a very useful tool for the study of Hecke

of Proposition

from

, and it is a simple
consequence of the so-called strong invariance properties of the Haar
weights. It is well-known in the case when
\mathbbold{\Lambda}=\{e\}
— then

is the support of the co-unit and
\kappa_{\alpha}=1
for every

\alpha

Theorem 3.10
.

For any
a\in c_{c}(\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Lambda})
,

,
\beta\in[\beta]

(3.1)

\displaystyle a

\displaystyle=\sum_{[\alpha]\in I(\mathbbold{\Gamma})/\mathbbold{\Lambda}}\kappa_{\alpha}^{-1}(S^{-1}(a_{\alpha})h_{R}\otimes\mathord{\operatorname{id}})\Delta(p{\Lambda})~=\sum_{[\alpha]\in I(\mathbbold{\Gamma})/\mathbbold{\Lambda}}\kappa_{\alpha}^{-1}(h_{R}S(a_{\alpha})\otimes\mathord{\operatorname{id}})\Delta(p{\Lambda})

(3.2)

\displaystyle b

\displaystyle=\sum_{[\beta]\in\mathbbold{\Lambda}\backslash I(\mathbbold{\Gamma})}\kappa_{\bar{\beta}}^{-1}(\mathord{\operatorname{id}}\otimes h_{L}S^{-1}(b_{\beta}))\Delta(p{\Lambda})~=\sum_{[\beta]\in\mathbbold{\Lambda}\backslash I(\mathbbold{\Gamma})}\kappa_{\bar{\beta}}^{-1}(\mathord{\operatorname{id}}\otimes S(b_{\beta})h_{L})\Delta(p{\Lambda})

Proof.

\alpha\in I(\mathbbold{\Gamma})
and

and applying Lemma

p_{[\alpha]}a=\kappa_{\alpha}^{-1}(S^{-1}(a_{\alpha})h_{R}\otimes p_{[\alpha]})\Delta(p{\Lambda})

3.1

Equation (
3.2
) follows by
applying (
3.1
) to
a=S^{-1}(b)

{(h_{R}b_{\bar{\alpha}}\otimes S)}\Delta(p{\Lambda})

p{\Lambda}=S^{-1}(p{\Lambda})
and

\Delta S^{-1}=(S^{-1}\otimes S^{-1})\sigma\Delta
we also have

b=\sum_{[\alpha]\in I(\mathbbold{\Gamma})/\mathbbold{\Lambda}}\kappa_{\alpha}^{-1}(\mathord{\operatorname{id}}\otimes(h_{R}b_{\bar{\alpha}})S^{-1})\Delta(p{\Lambda})

(h_{R}b_{\bar{\alpha}})S^{-1}=S(b_{\bar{\alpha}})h_{L}

[\beta]=[\bar{\alpha}]
runs through

runs through
I(\mathbbold{\Gamma})/\mathbbold{\Lambda}

The missing identities in (

∎

Corollary 3.11
.

Let
a\in c(\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Lambda})
,

b\in c(\mathbbold{\Lambda}\backslash\mathbbold{\Gamma})
. Then
\kappa_{\alpha}^{-1}h_{L}(a_{\alpha})
only
depends on the class
[\alpha]\in I(\mathbbold{\Gamma})/\mathbbold{\Lambda}
, and

\kappa_{\bar{\beta}}^{-1}h_{R}(b_{\beta})
only depends on the class

[\beta]\in\mathbbold{\Lambda}\backslash I(\mathbbold{\Gamma})
.

Proof.

One can assume that
a\in p_{[\alpha]}c(\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Lambda})
and

b=S(a)\in p_{[\beta]}c(\mathbbold{\Lambda}\backslash\mathbbold{\Gamma})
with
\beta=\bar{\alpha}
.
We have

a=\kappa_{\alpha}^{-1}(S^{-1}(a_{\alpha})h_{R}\otimes\mathord{\operatorname{id}})\Delta(p{\Lambda})

by (
3.1
) and

by (

). We
compute then

\displaystyle\kappa_{\bar{\beta}}^{-1}h_{R}(b_{\beta})

\displaystyle=\kappa_{\bar{\beta}}^{-1}h_{L}(S^{-1}(p_{\beta})a)=\kappa_{\bar{\beta}}^{-1}\kappa_{\alpha}^{-1}(S^{-1}(a_{\alpha})h_{R}\otimes h_{L}S^{-1}(p_{\beta}))\Delta(p{\Lambda})

\displaystyle=\kappa_{\alpha}^{-1}h_{R}(p_{\beta}S^{-1}(a_{\alpha}))=\kappa_{\alpha}^{-1}h_{L}(a_{\alpha}S(p_{\beta}))=\kappa_{\alpha}^{-1}h_{L}(a_{\alpha}).

[\alpha]
(resp.

3.1.3.
The constants
\kappa

In contrast to the classical case, where they trivialize, the constants
\kappa

of Definition
3.8
play an important role in the theory of quantum
group Hecke algebras (or in the quantum version of the Clifford theory). In this
subsection we give more details about them, which will however not be used in
the rest of the article (except in Proposition
3.22
).

Let us first note that related constants already appeared in Lemma 3.5 of

[
KKSV
]
, which states the existence, for every

\alpha\in I(\mathbbold{\Gamma})
, of a constant
\eta_{\alpha}>0
such that

(\mathord{\operatorname{id}}\otimes\operatorname{qTr}_{\bar{\alpha}})\Delta(p{\Lambda})=\eta_{\alpha}p_{[\alpha]},

where
[\alpha]\in\mathbbold{\Lambda}\backslash I(\mathbbold{\Gamma})
. As we have

h_{L}p_{\bar{\alpha}}=\operatorname{dim_{\text{$q$}}}(\alpha)\operatorname{qTr}_{\bar{\alpha}}
, comparing the
formulas shows that we have in fact

\eta_{\alpha}=\kappa_{\bar{\alpha}}/\operatorname{dim_{\text{$q$}}}(\alpha)
.

The next example shows that we do not have
\kappa_{\bar{\alpha}}=\kappa_{\alpha}

in general.

Example 3.15
.

We consider the quantum subgroup

\mathbbold{\Lambda}\subset\mathbbold{\Gamma}=\mathbb{Z}*\mathbbold{\Lambda}
, where
\mathbbold{\Lambda}
is any discrete
quantum group. Denote by
a
the generating corepresentation of
\mathbb{Z}
and take

v\in I(\mathbbold{\Lambda})
. Consider
\alpha=v\otimes a
, which is an irreducible
corepresentation of
\mathbbold{\Gamma}
. We have

\bar{\alpha}\otimes\alpha\simeq\bigoplus_{w\subset\bar{v}\otimes v}a^{-1}\otimes w\otimes a
, hence
\kappa_{\alpha}=1
, and

\alpha\otimes\bar{\alpha}=v\otimes\bar{v}
hence

\kappa_{\bar{\alpha}}=\operatorname{dim_{\text{$q$}}}(v)^{2}
.

If
\mathbbold{\Lambda}
is non-classical one can choose
v
such that

\kappa_{\bar{\alpha}}=\operatorname{dim_{\text{$q$}}}(v)^{2}>1=\kappa_{\alpha}
, and if
\mathbbold{\Lambda}
is
finite, we are in the setting of Hecke pairs. For
\mathbbold{\Lambda}
finite and non
classical we can further consider
\mathbbold{\Lambda}^{\infty}=\varinjlim\mathbbold{\Lambda}^{n}

which is still commensurated inside
\mathbbold{\Gamma}^{\infty}
. For

w\in\operatorname{Corep}(\mathbbold{\Gamma})
we denote
w^{(k)}\in\operatorname{Corep}(\mathbbold{\Gamma}^{\infty})
the
corepresentation corresponding to
w
and the
k
th copy of
\mathbbold{\Gamma}
in

\mathbbold{\Gamma}^{\infty}
. Then for any
v\in I(\mathbbold{\Lambda})
such that
\operatorname{dim_{\text{$q$}}}(v)>1

and
\alpha=\bigotimes_{k=1}^{n}(v\otimes a)^{(k)}\in I(\mathbbold{\Gamma}^{\infty})
we
have
\kappa_{\bar{\alpha}}/\kappa_{\alpha}=\operatorname{dim_{\text{$q$}}}(v)^{2n}
which is not bounded.

If one allows non commensurated quantum subgroups, one can take for
\mathbbold{\Lambda}
the
dual of
SU(2)
and we see again that the ratios

\kappa_{\bar{\alpha}}/\kappa_{\alpha}
are not bounded when
\alpha

varies. Notice also that
v=\alpha\otimes\bar{a}
,

\kappa_{v}=\operatorname{dim_{\text{$q$}}}(v)^{2}
and
\kappa_{\alpha}=1
. This shows that there is
no control on the ratios
\kappa_{\gamma}/\kappa_{\alpha}
either when

\gamma\subset\alpha\otimes\beta
with
\beta\in I(\mathbbold{\Gamma})
fixed.

We shall now give another interpretation of the constants

\kappa_{\alpha,\beta}
in terms of the
\operatorname{Corep}(\mathbbold{\Gamma})
-module category

\operatorname{Corep}(\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Lambda})
. Let us first introduce some more relevant notation. For
\pi\in\operatorname{Corep}(\mathbbold{\Gamma})
and

\alpha\in I(\mathbbold{\Gamma})
we denote
M_{\alpha,\pi}=\operatorname{Hom}(\alpha,\pi)
, so that

H_{\pi}\simeq\bigoplus_{\alpha\in I(\mathbbold{\Gamma})}H_{\alpha}\otimes M_{\alpha,\pi}

canonically. When
\pi=\beta\otimes\gamma
we denote

C_{\alpha}^{\beta,\gamma}=M_{\alpha,\pi}
, so that the dimensions

c_{\alpha}^{\beta,\gamma}=\operatorname{dim}(C_{\alpha}^{\beta,\gamma})
are the
structure constants of the based Grothendieck ring of
\operatorname{Corep}(\mathbbold{\Gamma})
.

We can extend this notation to objects of
\operatorname{Corep}(\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Lambda})
. For

i\in I(\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Lambda})
,
\pi\in\operatorname{Corep}(\mathbbold{\Gamma})
we thus denote

M_{i,\pi}=\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Lambda}}(i,\pi)
where
\pi
is identified to an
object in
\operatorname{Corep}(\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Lambda})
by restriction. Similarly we denote

C_{i}^{\beta,j}=\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Lambda}}(i,\beta\otimes j)
for
i
,

j\in I(\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Lambda})
and
\beta\in\operatorname{Corep}(\mathbbold{\Gamma})
.

Recall that the modular group of the left Haar weight is implemented by the
(possibly unbounded) modular element
F\in c(\mathbbold{\Gamma})
via the formula

\sigma_{t}^{L}(f)=F^{it}fF^{-it}
. For
\pi\in\operatorname{Corep}(\mathbbold{\Gamma})
we denote

F_{\pi}=\pi(F)\in B(H_{\pi})
and we recall the definition of the quantum
dimension
\operatorname{dim_{\text{$q$}}}(\pi)=\operatorname{Tr}(F_{\pi})=\operatorname{Tr}(F_{\pi}^{-1})
. Decomposing

H_{\pi}\simeq\bigoplus_{\alpha}H_{\alpha}\otimes M_{\alpha,\pi}
we have

F_{\pi}=\sum F_{\alpha}\otimes\mathord{\operatorname{id}}
.

In general
F
does not belong to
c_{c}(\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Lambda})
but we still have an
induced modular structure on this subalgebra. More precisely, take

\alpha\in I(\mathbbold{\Gamma})
and decompose

H_{\alpha}\simeq\bigoplus_{i\in I(\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Lambda})}H_{i}\otimes M_{i,\alpha}
in

\operatorname{Corep}(\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Lambda})
. Since
\sigma_{t}^{L}
stabilizes

\ell^{\infty}(\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Lambda})
by Lemma
3.1
,

\mathbbold{\Gamma}
, with restriction morphism

\rho:C_{u}(\mathbb{G})\to C_{u}(\mathbb{H})
, such that given
\alpha\in\operatorname{Corep}(\mathbbold{\Gamma})
we
have
\alpha\in\operatorname{Corep}(\mathbbold{\Lambda})
iff
(\mathord{\operatorname{id}}\otimes\rho)(\alpha)
is
trivial — or, equivalently, contains the
1
-dimensional
corepresentation. It is then easy to check that
\alpha\sim\beta
iff

1\!\!1\subset(\mathord{\operatorname{id}}\otimes\rho)(\bar{\alpha}\otimes\beta)
iff

1\!\!1\subset(\mathord{\operatorname{id}}\otimes\rho)(\beta\otimes\bar{\alpha})
iff

\alpha\backsim\beta
. In particular

I(\mathbbold{\Gamma})/\mathbbold{\Lambda}=\mathbbold{\Lambda}\backslash I(\mathbbold{\Gamma})/\mathbbold{\Lambda}=\mathbbold{\Lambda}\backslash I(\mathbbold{\Gamma})
and
L(\llbracket\alpha\rrbracket)=R(\llbracket\alpha\rrbracket)=1
for all

\alpha\in I(\mathbbold{\Gamma})
, so that
(\mathbbold{\Gamma},\mathbbold{\Lambda})
is a Hecke pair.

By uniqueness and the invariance result of
Proposition
3.21
, the functional
\mu
,
\nu
of
Definition
3.13
are the left, resp. right Haar weights
of
\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Lambda}
, normalized by the condition

\mu(p{\Lambda})=\nu(p{\Lambda})=1
. Moreover, from the formula

a*b=(\mathord{\operatorname{id}}\otimes S(b)\mu)\Delta(a)
we recognize the usual convolution
product of the discrete quantum group algebra
\ell^{\infty}(\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Lambda})

(or its opposite, depending on conventions), transported from the product of

\mathbb{C}[\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Lambda}]
via the Fourier transform.

3.2.2.
Hecke operators

Now we want to identify the Hecke algebra with
an algebra of equivariant endomorphisms of
c_{c}(\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Lambda})
. We turn

c_{c}(\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Lambda})
into a right
\mathbb{C}[\mathbbold{\Gamma}]
-module via the formula

a\cdot x=(x\otimes\mathord{\operatorname{id}})\Delta(a)
, where
a\in c_{c}(\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Lambda})
,

x\in\mathbb{C}[\mathbbold{\Gamma}]
and we view
\mathbb{C}[\mathbbold{\Gamma}]
as linear subspace of

c_{c}(\mathbbold{\Gamma})^{*}
via evaluation.
By definition, a linear map
F:c_{c}(\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Lambda})\to c_{c}(\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Lambda})

is
\mathbbold{\Gamma}
-equivariant iff it is
\mathbb{C}[\mathbbold{\Gamma}]
-linear, and we write

\operatorname{End}{\Gamma}(c_{c}(\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Lambda}))
for the space of
\mathbbold{\Gamma}
-equivariant
linear endomorphisms of
c_{c}(\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Lambda})
.

Restricting the action of
\mathbb{C}[\mathbbold{\Gamma}]
to
\mathbb{C}[\mathbbold{\Lambda}]
we obtain the
associated space of fixed points

c_{c}(\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Lambda}){\Lambda}=\{a\in c_{c}(\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Lambda})\mid(p{\Lambda}\otimes 1)\Delta(a)=p{\Lambda}\otimes a\}=\{a\in c_{c}(\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Lambda})\mid a\cdot x=\hat{\epsilon}(x)a\}=c_{c}(\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Lambda})\cap c(\mathbbold{\Lambda}\backslash\mathbbold{\Gamma})
.

Proposition 3.27
.

Let
\mathbbold{\Lambda}
be a quantum subgroup of

\mathbbold{\Gamma}
. The map
{\operatorname{ev}}{\Lambda}:F\mapsto f:=F(p{\Lambda})

defines an antimultiplicative isomorphism from

\operatorname{End}{\Gamma}(c_{c}(\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Lambda}))
to
c_{c}(\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Lambda}){\Lambda}
,
with inverse bijection
T
given by
T(f)(a)=a*f
,

f\in c_{c}(\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Lambda}){\Lambda}
,
a\in c_{c}(\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Lambda})
.

Proof.

Let
F\in\operatorname{End}{\Gamma}(c_{c}(\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Lambda}))
. For all

g\in c_{c}(\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Lambda})
and
\alpha\in I(\mathbbold{\Gamma})
we have

{(p_{\alpha}\otimes\mathord{\operatorname{id}})}\Delta(g)\in c_{c}(\mathbbold{\Gamma})\otimes c_{c}(\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Lambda})
and equivariance of
F
means that we have

(\mathord{\operatorname{id}}\otimes F)((p_{\alpha}\otimes\mathord{\operatorname{id}})\Delta(g))={(p_{\alpha}\otimes\mathord{\operatorname{id}})}\Delta(F(g))
. Applying this to
g=p{\Lambda}
we
obtain

(\mathord{\operatorname{id}}\otimes F)((p_{\alpha}\otimes\mathord{\operatorname{id}})\Delta(p{\Lambda}))={(p_{\alpha}\otimes\mathord{\operatorname{id}})}

\Delta(f)
. Since
p{\Lambda}
is also left
invariant under
\mathbbold{\Lambda}
, for
\alpha=\lambda\in I(\mathbbold{\Lambda})
we obtain

(p_{\lambda}\otimes\mathord{\operatorname{id}})\Delta(f)=(\mathord{\operatorname{id}}\otimes F)(p_{\lambda}\otimes p{\Lambda})=p_{\lambda}\otimes f
and we have indeed

f\in c_{c}(\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Lambda}){\Lambda}
. The same equivariance formula can
also be written

F((a_{\alpha}h_{R}\otimes\mathord{\operatorname{id}})\Delta(p{\Lambda}))=(a_{\alpha}h_{R}\otimes\mathord{\operatorname{id}})\Delta(f)
for any
a\in c(\mathbbold{\Gamma})
and
\alpha\in I(\mathbbold{\Gamma})
.

Moreover, take
a\in p_{[\alpha]}c_{c}(\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Lambda})
. Using
Theorem
3.10
and the previous equivariance formula we can
write

\displaystyle F(a)

\displaystyle=\kappa_{\alpha}^{-1}F((S^{-1}(a_{\alpha})h_{R}\otimes\mathord{\operatorname{id}})\Delta(p{\Lambda}))

\displaystyle=\kappa_{\alpha}^{-1}(S^{-1}(a_{\alpha})h_{R}\otimes\mathord{\operatorname{id}})\Delta(f)=a*f=T(f)(a).

This shows that
T
is a left inverse of
{\operatorname{ev}}{\Lambda}
.

Conversely starting from
f\in c_{c}(\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Lambda}){\Lambda}
we can
consider
T(f):a\mapsto a*f
. We already noticed at
Proposition
3.23
that
T(f)(a)\in c_{c}(\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Lambda})
, and
after Definition
3.19
that
T(f)
is equivariant with respect
to the left
\mathbbold{\Gamma}
-action induced by
\Delta
. Since
p{\Lambda}
is the
unit of the convolution product,
T
is a right inverse of

{\operatorname{ev}}{\Lambda}
. Finally
T
is antimultiplicative by associativity
of the convolution product.
∎

We now use the prehilbertian structure on
c_{c}(\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Lambda})
obtained from
the functional
\mu
and consider the corresponding subspace of adjointable
operators in
\operatorname{End}{\Gamma}(c_{c}(\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Lambda}))
. Note that the formula
(
3.3
) for
(a\mid b)
given in
Definition
3.13
also makes sense for

a\in c_{c}(\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Lambda})
,
b\in c(\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Lambda})
, or for

a\in c(\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Lambda})
,
b\in c_{c}(\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Lambda})
. We use this in the
following proposition. Note also that
\|p{\Lambda}\|_{\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Lambda}}=1
.

Proposition 3.28
.

For
a
,
b\in c_{c}(\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Lambda})

and
c\in c(\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Lambda})\cap c(\mathbbold{\Lambda}\backslash\mathbbold{\Gamma})
we have

(a\mid b*c)=(a*c^{\sharp}\mid b)
.

Proof.

We compute, using (
3.3
) and (
3.5
):

\displaystyle(a*c^{\sharp}\mid b)

\displaystyle=\sum_{[\beta]}\kappa_{\beta}^{-1}(b_{\beta}h_{L})((a*c^{\sharp})^{*})=\sum_{[\alpha],[\beta]}\kappa_{\alpha}^{-1}\kappa_{\beta}^{-1}(\overline{S^{-1}(a_{\alpha})h_{R}}\otimes b_{\beta}h_{L})\Delta(c^{\sharp*})

\displaystyle=\sum_{[\alpha],[\beta]}\kappa_{\alpha}^{-1}\kappa_{\beta}^{-1}(b_{\beta}h_{L}\otimes\overline{S^{-1}(a_{\alpha})h_{R}})(S^{-1}\otimes S^{-1})\Delta(c)

\displaystyle=\sum_{[\alpha],[\beta]}\kappa_{\alpha}^{-1}\kappa_{\beta}^{-1}(h_{R}S(b_{\beta})\otimes S^{2}(a_{\alpha}^{*})h_{L})\Delta(c)=\sum_{[\alpha]}\kappa_{\alpha}^{-1}(h_{L}a_{\alpha}^{*})(b*c)=(a\mid b*c).

We also used the identities

\overline{S^{-1}(a_{\alpha})h_{R}}\circ S^{-1}=S^{2}(a_{\alpha}^{*})h_{L}=h_{L}a_{\alpha}^{*}
which are easy to check.
∎

In the next theorem, which offers an alternative description of the Hecke
algebra, we write

\operatorname{End}^{\prime}(c_{c}(\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Lambda}))\subset\operatorname{End}(c_{c}(\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Lambda}))
for the
subspace of adjointable maps, i.e. of maps
T
for which there exists

S\in\operatorname{End}(c_{c}(\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Lambda}))
satisfying
(a\mid Tb)=(Sa\mid b)
for
all
a
,
b\in c_{c}(\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Lambda})
.

Theorem 3.29
.

Let
f\in c_{c}(\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Lambda}){\Lambda}
. Then we have

T(f)\in\operatorname{End}^{\prime}(c_{c}(\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Lambda}))
iff

f\in c_{c}(\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Lambda})\cap c_{c}(\mathbbold{\Lambda}\backslash\mathbbold{\Gamma})
. As a result,

T
implements an antimultiplicative
*
-isomorphism between

\mathcal{H}(\mathbbold{\Gamma},\mathbbold{\Lambda})
and
\operatorname{End}^{\prime}{\Gamma}(c_{c}(\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Lambda}))
. If

(\mathbbold{\Gamma},\mathbbold{\Lambda})
is a Hecke pair, all maps in

\operatorname{End}{\Gamma}(c_{c}(\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Lambda}))
are adjointable.

Proof.

In view of Proposition
3.27
it essentially suffices to understand
the relevant
*
-structures. By Proposition
3.28
, if

T(f)
is adjointable then its adjoint is
S:a\mapsto a*f^{\sharp}
. Taking
a=p{\Lambda}
we obtain

f^{\sharp}\in c_{c}(\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Lambda})
, hence
f\in c_{c}(\mathbbold{\Lambda}\backslash\mathbbold{\Gamma})
.
The converse implication and the statement about
T
are then clear. If

(\mathbbold{\Gamma},\mathbbold{\Lambda})
is a Hecke pair, we have

c_{c}(\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Lambda}){\Lambda}=c_{c}(\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Lambda})\cap c_{c}(\mathbbold{\Lambda}\backslash\mathbbold{\Gamma})
.
∎

Now we investigate the question whether Hecke operators extend to bounded
operators on
\ell^{2}(\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Lambda})
, the completion of

c_{c}(\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Lambda})
with respect to the norm
\|\cdot\|_{\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Lambda}}

arising from Definition
3.13
. In the setting of discrete quantum
groups we prove in Theorem
3.32
that this is equivalent to a
combinatorial property of the constants
\kappa_{\alpha}
introduced in
Definition
3.30
below. Surprisingly we could not prove directly that
this property always hold for Hecke pairs. However we will show later in
Section
4.3
, using the Schlichting completion, that this is indeed the case, by showing that Hecke
operators are always bounded.

Definition 3.30
.

Given the inclusion
\mathbbold{\Lambda}\subset\mathbbold{\Gamma}
and
\beta\in I(\mathbbold{\Gamma})
we say that
\beta
satisfies property (RT) if there exists a constant
C_{\beta}
such
that
\kappa_{\gamma}\leq C_{\beta}\kappa_{\alpha}
for all
\alpha
,
\gamma\in I(\mathbbold{\Gamma})
such that
\gamma\subset\alpha\otimes\beta
. We say that the
inclusion
\mathbbold{\Lambda}\subset\mathbbold{\Gamma}
satisfies property (RT) if the property (RT) holds for each
\beta\in I(\mathbbold{\Gamma}^{\prime})
, where
\mathbbold{\Gamma}^{\prime}
denotes the
commensurator of
\mathbbold{\Lambda}
in
\mathbbold{\Gamma}
.

Property (RT) is of course always verified in the classical case since then

\kappa_{\alpha}=1
for all
\alpha\in I(\mathbbold{\Gamma})
. In general it does not hold for all corepresentations, as shown by the (non-commensurated)
Example
3.15
.

Lemma 3.31
.

For
[\alpha]\in I(\mathbbold{\Gamma})/\mathbbold{\Lambda}
,
[\beta]\in\mathbbold{\Lambda}\backslash I(\mathbbold{\Gamma})
we
have

(3.9)

p_{[\alpha]}*p_{[\beta]}=\frac{\operatorname{dim_{\text{$q$}}}(\beta)}{\kappa_{\bar{\beta}}}{\sum_{\delta\in I(\mathbbold{\Gamma})}}\frac{\operatorname{dim_{\text{$q$}}}(\delta\otimes\bar{\beta})_{[\alpha]}}{\operatorname{dim_{\text{$q$}}}(\delta)}~p_{\delta},

where
(\delta\otimes\bar{\beta})_{[\alpha]}
denotes the span of the
subobjects of
\delta\otimes\bar{\beta}
isomorphic to elements of
[\alpha]
.

Proof.

Note first that in the proof of Lemma
3.9
we showed that for each

\gamma,\delta,\mu\in I(\mathbbold{\Gamma})
we have

(h_{R}p_{\gamma}\otimes p_{\delta})\Delta(p_{\mu})=\operatorname{dim_{\text{$q$}}}(\gamma)\operatorname{dim_{\text{$q$}}}(\mu)\operatorname{dim_{\text{$q$}}}(\delta)^{-1}c_{\mu}^{\gamma,\delta}p_{\delta},

where
c_{\mu}^{\gamma,\delta}=\operatorname{dim}(\operatorname{Hom}(\mu,\gamma\otimes\delta))
.
Applying the antipode to this formula yields

(p_{\delta}\otimes h_{L}p_{\gamma})\Delta(p_{\mu})=\operatorname{dim_{\text{$q$}}}(\gamma)\operatorname{dim_{\text{$q$}}}(\mu)\operatorname{dim_{\text{$q$}}}(\delta)^{-1}c_{\bar{\mu}}^{\bar{\gamma},\bar{\delta}}p_{\delta}.

Thus for each
[\alpha]\in I(\mathbbold{\Gamma})/\mathbbold{\Lambda}
,

[\beta]\in\mathbbold{\Lambda}\backslash I(\mathbbold{\Gamma})
we obtain

\displaystyle p_{[\alpha]}*p_{[\beta]}

\displaystyle=\kappa_{\alpha}^{-1}\kappa_{\bar{\beta}}^{-1}(h_{R}p_{\bar{\alpha}}\otimes\mathord{\operatorname{id}}\otimes h_{L}p_{\bar{\beta}})\Delta^{2}(p{\Lambda})

\displaystyle=\kappa_{\alpha}^{-1}\kappa_{\bar{\beta}}^{-1}\textstyle\sum_{\delta\in I(\mathbbold{\Gamma}),\lambda\in I(\mathbbold{\Lambda})}(h_{R}p_{\bar{\alpha}}\otimes p_{\delta}\otimes h_{L}p_{\bar{\beta}})\Delta^{2}(p_{\lambda})

\displaystyle=\kappa_{\alpha}^{-1}\kappa_{\bar{\beta}}^{-1}\textstyle\sum_{\delta\in I(\mathbbold{\Gamma}),\lambda\in I(\mathbbold{\Lambda}),\gamma\in I(\mathbbold{\Gamma})}(h_{R}p_{\bar{\alpha}}\otimes p_{\delta})\circ\Delta\circ(p_{\gamma}\otimes h_{L}{p_{\bar{\beta}}})(\Delta(p_{\lambda}))

\displaystyle=\kappa_{\alpha}^{-1}\kappa_{\bar{\beta}}^{-1}\textstyle\sum_{\delta,\lambda,\gamma}\operatorname{dim_{\text{$q$}}}(\bar{\alpha})\operatorname{dim_{\text{$q$}}}(\delta)^{-1}c_{\gamma}^{\bar{\alpha},\delta}\operatorname{dim_{\text{$q$}}}(\bar{\beta})\operatorname{dim_{\text{$q$}}}(\lambda)c_{\bar{\lambda}}^{\beta,\bar{\gamma}}p_{\delta}

\displaystyle=\frac{\operatorname{dim_{\text{$q$}}}(\alpha)}{\kappa_{\alpha}}\frac{\operatorname{dim_{\text{$q$}}}(\beta)}{\kappa_{\bar{\beta}}}\sum_{\delta\in I(\mathbbold{\Gamma})}\frac{\operatorname{dim_{\text{$q$}}}(\bar{\alpha}\otimes\delta\otimes\bar{\beta}){\Lambda}}{\operatorname{dim_{\text{$q$}}}(\delta)}~p_{\delta}.

We used the identity

\sum_{\gamma\in I(\mathbbold{\Gamma})}c_{\gamma}^{\bar{\alpha},\delta}c_{\bar{\lambda}}^{\beta,\bar{\gamma}}=\sum_{\gamma\in I(\mathbbold{\Gamma})}c_{\gamma}^{\bar{\alpha},\delta}c_{\lambda}^{\gamma,\bar{\beta}}=\operatorname{dim}\operatorname{Hom}(\lambda,\bar{\alpha}\otimes\delta\otimes\bar{\beta})
, so that adding the
quantum dimensions
\operatorname{dim_{\text{$q$}}}(\lambda)
over
\lambda\in I(\mathbbold{\Lambda})
with these multiplicities yields

\operatorname{dim_{\text{$q$}}}(\bar{\alpha}\otimes\delta\otimes\bar{\beta}){\Lambda}
.

Notice that the term corresponding to
\delta
vanishes unless

\delta\subset\alpha\otimes\lambda\otimes\beta
for some

\lambda\in I(\mathbbold{\Lambda})
. Moreover, decomposing into irreducibles

\delta\otimes\bar{\beta}=\bigoplus c_{\alpha^{\prime}}^{\delta,\bar{\beta}}\alpha^{\prime}

we can write, using Corollary
3.12
:

\displaystyle\operatorname{dim_{\text{$q$}}}(\bar{\alpha}\otimes\delta\otimes\bar{\beta}){\Lambda}

\displaystyle={\textstyle\sum_{\alpha^{\prime}}}c_{\alpha^{\prime}}^{\delta,\bar{\beta}}\operatorname{dim_{\text{$q$}}}(\bar{\alpha}\otimes\alpha^{\prime}){\Lambda}

\displaystyle={\textstyle\sum_{\alpha^{\prime}\in[\alpha]}}c_{\alpha^{\prime}}^{\delta,\bar{\beta}}\kappa_{\alpha}\frac{\operatorname{dim_{\text{$q$}}}(\alpha^{\prime})}{\operatorname{dim_{\text{$q$}}}(\alpha)}=\frac{\kappa_{\alpha}}{\operatorname{dim_{\text{$q$}}}(\alpha)}\operatorname{dim_{\text{$q$}}}(\delta\otimes\bar{\beta})_{[\alpha]}

and the formula follows.
∎

Theorem 3.32
.

Let
\mathbbold{\Lambda}\subset\mathbbold{\Gamma}
be an inclusion of discrete quantum groups. The operator
T(b)
is bounded with respect
to
\|\cdot\|_{\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Lambda}}
for every
b\in\mathcal{H}(\mathbbold{\Gamma},\mathbbold{\Lambda})
iff the inclusion
\mathbbold{\Lambda}\subset\mathbbold{\Gamma}
satisfies property (RT).

Proof.

Assume first that
T(b)
is bounded with respect to
\|\cdot\|_{\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Lambda}}
for all
b\in\mathcal{H}(\mathbbold{\Gamma},\mathbbold{\Lambda})
. In particular
T(p_{\tau})

is bounded for every
\tau\in\mathbbold{\Lambda}\backslash I(\mathbbold{\Gamma}^{\prime})/\mathbbold{\Lambda}
. Consider
\xi_{[\alpha]}=\kappa_{\alpha}^{1/2}p_{[\alpha]}/\operatorname{dim_{\text{$q$}}}(\alpha)
, which has
norm
1
with respect to
\|\cdot\|_{\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Lambda}}
. By definition of the scalar product, and writing
\tau
as a disjoint union of finitely many left
classes
[\beta]
we have

\displaystyle(\xi_{[\gamma]}\mid\xi_{[\alpha]}*p_{\tau})

\displaystyle=\frac{\kappa_{\alpha}^{1/2}\kappa_{\gamma}^{1/2}}{\operatorname{dim_{\text{$q$}}}(\alpha)\operatorname{dim_{\text{$q$}}}(\gamma)}(p_{[\gamma]}|p_{[\alpha]}*p_{\tau})=\frac{\kappa_{\alpha}^{1/2}\kappa_{\gamma}^{1/2}}{\operatorname{dim_{\text{$q$}}}(\alpha)\operatorname{dim_{\text{$q$}}}(\gamma)}\kappa_{\gamma}^{-1}h_{L}(p_{\gamma}(p_{[\alpha]}*p_{\tau}))

\displaystyle=\frac{\kappa_{\alpha}^{1/2}\kappa_{\gamma}^{1/2}}{\operatorname{dim_{\text{$q$}}}(\alpha)\operatorname{dim_{\text{$q$}}}(\gamma)}\kappa_{\gamma}^{-1}\sum_{[\beta]\subset\tau}h_{L}(p_{\gamma}(p_{[\alpha]}*p_{[\beta]})).

Using (
3.9
) in the preceding lemma we can further compute:

\displaystyle(\xi_{[\gamma]}\mid\xi_{[\alpha]}*p_{\tau})

\displaystyle=\sum_{[\beta]\subset\tau}\frac{\kappa_{\alpha}^{1/2}\kappa_{\gamma}^{1/2}}{\operatorname{dim_{\text{$q$}}}(\alpha)\operatorname{dim_{\text{$q$}}}(\gamma)}\kappa_{\gamma}^{-1}\frac{\operatorname{dim_{\text{$q$}}}(\beta)}{\kappa_{\bar{\beta}}}\frac{\operatorname{dim_{\text{$q$}}}(\gamma\otimes\bar{\beta})_{[\alpha]}}{\operatorname{dim_{\text{$q$}}}(\gamma)}h_{L}(p_{\gamma})

\displaystyle=\sum_{[\beta]\subset\tau}\frac{\operatorname{dim_{\text{$q$}}}(\beta)}{\kappa_{\bar{\beta}}}\frac{\kappa_{\alpha}^{1/2}}{\operatorname{dim_{\text{$q$}}}(\alpha)}\frac{\operatorname{dim_{\text{$q$}}}(\gamma\otimes\bar{\beta})_{[\alpha]}}{\kappa_{\gamma}^{1/2}}.

Finally we decompose
(\gamma\otimes\bar{\beta})
into irreducible subobjects

\alpha^{\prime}
and select the ones in
[\alpha]
. Using
Corollary
3.12
this yields

\displaystyle(\xi_{[\gamma]}\mid\xi_{[\alpha]}*p_{\tau})

\displaystyle=\sum_{[\beta]\subset\tau}\frac{\operatorname{dim_{\text{$q$}}}(\beta)}{\kappa_{\bar{\beta}}}\sum_{\alpha^{\prime}\in[\alpha]}c_{\alpha^{\prime}}^{\gamma,\bar{\beta}}\frac{\kappa_{\alpha}^{1/2}}{\operatorname{dim_{\text{$q$}}}(\alpha)}\frac{\operatorname{dim_{\text{$q$}}}(\alpha^{\prime})}{\kappa_{\gamma}^{1/2}}=\sum_{[\beta]\subset\tau}\frac{\operatorname{dim_{\text{$q$}}}(\beta)}{\kappa_{\bar{\beta}}}\sum_{\alpha^{\prime}\in[\alpha]}c_{\alpha^{\prime}}^{\gamma,\bar{\beta}}\frac{\kappa_{\alpha^{\prime}}^{1/2}}{\kappa_{\gamma}^{1/2}}.

As a result we have, for any
\alpha^{\prime}
,
\gamma\in I(\mathbbold{\Gamma})
and
\beta\in I(\mathbbold{\Gamma}^{\prime})
such
that
\alpha^{\prime}\subset\gamma\otimes\bar{\beta}
:

\frac{\kappa_{\alpha^{\prime}}}{\kappa_{\gamma}}\leq\frac{\kappa_{\bar{\beta}}^{2}~\|T(p_{\llbracket\beta\rrbracket})\|^{2}}{\operatorname{dim_{\text{$q$}}}(\beta)^{2}}.

This shows the existence of the constants
C_{\beta}
and the direct
implication.

Conversely, assume that (RT) holds. Taking

b\in c_{c}(\mathbbold{\Lambda}\backslash\mathbbold{\Gamma}^{\prime}/\mathbbold{\Lambda})
, we can assume that

b\in p_{\tau}c_{c}(\mathbbold{\Lambda}\backslash\mathbbold{\Gamma}^{\prime}/\mathbbold{\Lambda})
with

\tau\in\mathbbold{\Lambda}\backslash I(\mathbbold{\Gamma}^{\prime})/\mathbbold{\Lambda}
, and we then have a decomposition

b=\sum_{[\beta]\subset\tau}p_{[\beta]}b
with
L(\tau)
terms, where

[\beta]\in\mathbbold{\Lambda}\backslash I(\mathbbold{\Gamma})
. We first fix classes
[\alpha]
,

[\gamma]\in I(\mathbbold{\Gamma})/\mathbbold{\Lambda}
. For

a\in p_{[\alpha]}c_{c}(\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Lambda})
,

c\in p_{[\gamma]}c_{c}(\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Lambda})
we have

\displaystyle(c\mid a*b)

\displaystyle=\sum_{[\beta]\subset\tau}\kappa_{\bar{\beta}}^{-1}(c\mid(\mathord{\operatorname{id}}\otimes S(b_{\beta})h_{L})\Delta(a))

\displaystyle=\sum_{[\beta]\subset\tau,\alpha^{\prime}\in[\alpha]}\kappa_{\bar{\beta}}^{-1}\kappa_{\gamma}^{-1}(h_{L}c_{\gamma}^{*}\otimes S(b_{\beta})h_{L})\Delta(a_{\alpha^{\prime}}).

The sum is in fact finite since its terms vanish unless

\alpha^{\prime}\subset\gamma\otimes\bar{\beta}
.

Now we use the case when
\mathbbold{\Lambda}
is the trivial subgroup, denoting

\|\cdot\|{\Gamma}
the hermitian norm on
c_{c}(\mathbbold{\Gamma})
and
T{\Gamma}

the homomorphism from
c_{c}(\mathbbold{\Gamma})
to
\operatorname{End}(c_{c}(\mathbbold{\Gamma}))
. We know that

T{\Gamma}(d)
extends to a bounded operator on
\ell^{2}(\mathbbold{\Gamma})
for any

d\in c_{c}(\mathbbold{\Gamma})
, because it is an operator from the right regular
representation of
\mathbbold{\Gamma}
. Fix a choice of representatives
\beta
for the
classes
[\beta]
and put

M=\max_{[\beta]\subset\tau}\|T{\Gamma}(b_{\beta})\|
(so that
M

might depend on the choice made). We then have

|(h_{L}c_{\gamma}^{*}\otimes S(b_{\beta})h_{L})\Delta(a_{\alpha^{\prime}})|=|(c_{\gamma}\mid T{\Gamma}(b_{\beta})(a_{\alpha^{\prime}})){\Gamma}|\leq M\|c_{\gamma}\|{\Gamma}\|a_{\alpha^{\prime}}\|{\Gamma}.

We have moreover

\|c_{\gamma}\|{\Gamma}=\sqrt{\kappa}_{\gamma}\|c\|_{\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Lambda}}
,

\|a_{\alpha^{\prime}}\|{\Gamma}=\sqrt{\kappa}_{\alpha^{\prime}}\|a\|_{\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Lambda}}
by
definition.

As a result we can write:

\displaystyle|(c\mid a*b)|

\displaystyle\leq M\sum_{[\beta]\subset\tau}\sum_{\alpha^{\prime}\in[\alpha],\alpha^{\prime}\subset\gamma\otimes\bar{\beta}}\kappa_{\bar{\beta}}^{-1}\kappa_{\gamma}^{-1/2}\kappa_{\alpha^{\prime}}^{1/2}\|c\|_{\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Lambda}}\|a\|_{\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Lambda}}\leq K\|c\|_{\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Lambda}}\|a\|_{\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Lambda}},

where

only depends on
b
(and not on
[\alpha]
,
[\gamma]
,
a
,

c
). Here we used property (RT) and the fact from
Lemma
2.1
that
\gamma\otimes\bar{\beta}
has at most

\operatorname{dim}(\beta)^{2}
irreducible subobjects.

Let use write
[\gamma]\#[\alpha]
if there exist

c\in p_{[\gamma]}c_{c}(\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Lambda})
,

a\in p_{[\alpha]}c_{c}(\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Lambda})
such that
(c\mid a*b)\neq 0
. The
arguments above also show that
[\gamma]\#[\alpha]
implies

\gamma\subset\alpha\otimes\lambda\otimes\beta
for some

\lambda\in I(\mathbbold{\Lambda})
and
\beta\in\tau
. Writing
\tau
as a disjoint
union of
R(\tau)
classes

, this inclusion
implies

for some
i
and

\mu\in I(\mathbbold{\Lambda})
. Since

contains at most

\operatorname{dim}(\beta_{i})^{2}
irreducibles, this shows that, once

is fixed, we
can have

for at most
\sum\operatorname{dim}(\beta_{i})^{2}
classes

. Similarly, since

, if
[\gamma]

is fixed, we can have
[\gamma]\#[\alpha]
for at most
\sum\operatorname{dim}(\beta^{\prime}_{j})^{2}

classes
[\alpha]
, where we have now written
\tau
as a disjoint union of

L(\tau)
classes
[\beta^{\prime}_{j}]\in\mathbbold{\Lambda}\backslash I(\mathbbold{\Gamma})
. Let us denote by

Finally we can use Cauchy-Schwarz to write, for any

\displaystyle|(c\mid a*b)|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{[\gamma]\#[\alpha]}|(p_{[\gamma]}c\mid(p_{[\alpha]}a)*b)|\leq K\sum_{[\gamma]\#[\alpha]}\|p_{[\gamma]}c\|_{\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Lambda}}\|p_{[\alpha]}a\|_{\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Lambda}}

\displaystyle\leq K\Big(\sum_{[\gamma]\#[\alpha]}\|p_{[\gamma]}c\|_{\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Lambda}}^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\Big(\sum_{[\gamma]\#[\alpha]}\|p_{[\alpha]}a\|_{\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Lambda}}^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\leq KN\|c\|_{\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Lambda}}\|a\|_{\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Lambda}}.

This shows that

is bounded with

.
∎

3.2.3.
Modular structure

Definition 3.33
.

The

on

\mu

\nu

Definition 3.34

The

associated with

More concretely, fix

\displaystyle\mu_{\alpha}(a_{\alpha})

\displaystyle=\sum\{\kappa_{\delta}^{-1}h_{L}(a_{\delta})\mid[\delta]\subset\llbracket\alpha\rrbracket,[\delta]\in I(\mathbbold{\Gamma})/\mathbbold{\Lambda}\},

\displaystyle\nu_{\alpha}(a_{\alpha})

Lemma 3.35

We have

\sigma_{t}^{R}(\nabla)=\nabla

S(\nabla)=\nabla^{-1}=S^{-1}(\nabla)

Since

The next result shows that the operator

Theorem 3.36

For every

Lemma 3.39

We have

Proof.

Recall that

Definition 3.40

3.3.

Let
\mathbbold{\Gamma}_{0}

\mathbbold{\Lambda}_{\epsilon}\subset\mathbbold{\Gamma}_{0}

\mathbbold{\Gamma}=HNN(\mathbbold{\Gamma}_{0},\theta)

[
Fim13
]
.
It follows in particular that

is partitioned into the subsets

\displaystyle I(\mathbbold{\Gamma})_{n}

\displaystyle=\{\alpha\subset\alpha_{0}\otimes w^{\epsilon_{1}}\otimes\alpha_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes w^{\epsilon_{n}}\otimes\alpha_{n}\mid

\displaystyle\hskip 85.35826pt\alpha_{i}\in I(\mathbbold{\Gamma}_{0}),\epsilon_{i}=\pm 1,\alpha_{i}\notin I(\mathbbold{\Lambda}_{\epsilon_{i}})~\text{whenever}~\epsilon_{i+1}\neq\epsilon_{i}\}.

Proposition 3.43
.

Assume that the quantum subgroups
\mathbbold{\Lambda}_{\epsilon}
have finite index in

\mathbbold{\Gamma}_{0}
and at least one of them is distinct from

\mathbbold{\Gamma}_{0}

Write

\gamma_{\epsilon,0}=1

\gamma_{-\epsilon_{1},k_{1}}\otimes w^{\epsilon_{1}}\otimes\gamma_{-\epsilon_{2},k_{2}}\otimes w^{\epsilon_{2}}\otimes\cdots\otimes w^{\epsilon_{n}}\otimes\alpha_{n}

\alpha\subset\alpha_{0}\otimes w^{\epsilon_{1}}\otimes\alpha_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes w^{\epsilon_{n}}\otimes\alpha_{n}

with

and decompose

\alpha
is irreducible it appears as a subobject of one of

In particular it follows that
I(\mathbbold{\Gamma})_{n}/\mathbbold{\Gamma}_{0}

\backsim

\mathbbold{\Gamma}_{0}

\mathbbold{\Gamma}_{0}

\mathbbold{\Gamma}

since the subsets

, are non empty. Finally, assuming e.g.

belongs to

would

with

, but then
w\otimes\alpha\otimes w^{*}\simeq\beta

would belong to
I(\mathbbold{\Gamma})_{0}
. Hence
\mathbbold{\Gamma}_{0}
is not normal in
\mathbbold{\Gamma}
.
∎

Denote by
N(\mathbbold{\Gamma}\curvearrowright\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Lambda})
the weak closure of

\{(\mathord{\operatorname{id}}\otimes\varphi)\Delta(a)\mid a\in c_{0}(\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Lambda}),\varphi\in c_{0}(\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Lambda})^{*}\}
in
\ell^{\infty}(\mathbbold{\Gamma})
. Recall from

[
KKSV
]
that the action of
\mathbbold{\Gamma}
on
\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Lambda}
is
called faithful if we have
N(\mathbbold{\Gamma}\curvearrowright\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Lambda})=\ell^{\infty}(\mathbbold{\Gamma})
.

Lemma 3.44
.

Let
\mathbbold{\Lambda}\subset\mathbbold{\Gamma}
be a quantum subgroup. Assume that for every
non-trivial
\alpha\in I(\mathbbold{\Gamma})
we can find
\gamma_{\alpha}\in I(\mathbbold{\Gamma})

such that no subobjects of
\alpha\otimes\gamma_{\alpha}
belong to

[\gamma_{\alpha}]\in I(\mathbbold{\Gamma})/\mathbbold{\Lambda}
. Then the action of
\mathbbold{\Gamma}
on

\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Lambda}
is faithful.

Proof.

We denote
p_{0}
the minimal central projection
p_{\alpha}
corresponding to the
trivial corepresentation
\alpha=1
. It is also the central support of the
counit
\epsilon
of
c_{c}(\mathbbold{\Gamma})
. The condition on
\alpha
,

\gamma_{\alpha}
can also be written

(p_{\alpha}\otimes p_{\gamma_{\alpha}})\Delta(p_{[\gamma_{\alpha}]})=0
. On the
other hand we have

(p_{0}\otimes p_{\gamma_{\alpha}})\Delta(p_{[\gamma_{\alpha}]})=(p_{0}\epsilon\otimes p_{\gamma_{\alpha}})\Delta(p_{[\gamma_{\alpha}]})=p_{0}\otimes p_{\gamma_{\alpha}}p_{[\gamma_{\alpha}]}=p_{0}\otimes p_{\gamma_{\alpha}}
. For

\alpha\neq 1
we denote

x_{\alpha}=\operatorname{dim_{\text{$q$}}}(\gamma_{\alpha})^{-2}(\mathord{\operatorname{id}}\otimes h_{L}p_{\gamma_{\alpha}})\Delta(p_{[\gamma_{\alpha}]})
. We have

p_{\alpha}x_{\alpha}=0
,
p_{0}x_{\alpha}=p_{0}
and
\|x_{\alpha}\|\leq 1
since

\operatorname{dim_{\text{$q$}}}(\gamma)^{-2}h_{L}p_{\gamma}
is a state. Introduce a total order on

I(\mathbbold{\Gamma})
and put
y_{F}=\prod_{\alpha\in F}x_{\alpha}
for

F\subset I(\mathbbold{\Gamma})
finite,
1\notin F
. Then the net
(y_{F})_{F}
converges to

p_{0}
in the weak topology. Indeed the elements of the net are uniformly
bounded in norm, and for
F\subset I(\mathbbold{\Gamma})
finite,
1\notin F
, we have

p_{0}y_{F}=p_{0}
and
p_{\beta}y_{F}=0
as soon as
\beta\in F
. Since

x_{\alpha}\in N(\mathbbold{\Gamma}\curvearrowright\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Lambda})
for all
\alpha\in I(\mathbbold{\Gamma})
,

\alpha\neq 1
, it follows that
p_{0}\in N(\mathbbold{\Gamma}\curvearrowright\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Lambda})
, which implies

N(\mathbbold{\Gamma}\curvearrowright\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Lambda})=\ell^{\infty}(\mathbbold{\Gamma})
by

[
KKSV
, Prop. 2.10]
.
∎

We still denote by
\theta:I(\mathbbold{\Lambda}_{1})\to I(\mathbbold{\Lambda}_{-1})
the map induced
by
\theta
on irreducible representations. We define by induction

\operatorname{Dom}\theta^{k}\subset I(\mathbbold{\Gamma}_{0})
, for
k\in\mathbb{Z}
, by putting

\operatorname{Dom}\theta^{0}=I(\mathbbold{\Gamma}_{0})
and

\operatorname{Dom}\theta^{(n+1)\epsilon}=\{\alpha\in\operatorname{Dom}\theta^{n\epsilon}\mid\theta^{n\epsilon}(\alpha)\in I(\mathbbold{\Lambda}_{\epsilon})\}
for
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

\epsilon=\pm 1
.

Proposition 3.45
.

Assume that
\bigcap_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\operatorname{Dom}\theta^{k}=\{1\}
. Then the action of

\mathbbold{\Gamma}
on
\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Gamma}_{0}
is faithful.

Proof.

We apply Lemma
3.44
. Take
\alpha\in I(\mathbbold{\Gamma})

non-trivial. If
\alpha\notin I(\mathbbold{\Gamma}_{0})
, we can just take

\gamma=1
. Hence we can assume
\alpha\in\mathbbold{\Gamma}_{0}
,
\alpha\neq 1
. By
assumption there exists
\epsilon\in\{\pm 1\}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
such that

\alpha\in\operatorname{Dom}\theta^{(n-1)\epsilon}
but

\alpha\notin\operatorname{Dom}\theta^{n\epsilon}
. We take

\gamma_{\alpha}=w^{-n\epsilon}
. We have then

\alpha\otimes w^{-n\epsilon}=w^{-(n-1)\epsilon}\otimes\theta^{(n-1)\epsilon}(\alpha)\otimes w^{-\epsilon}
, which is irreducible and
not equivalent to
w^{-n\epsilon}\otimes\beta
with
\beta\in I(\mathbbold{\Gamma}_{0})
—
indeed
\beta
is in
I(\mathbbold{\Gamma})_{0}
but

w^{\epsilon}\otimes\theta^{(n-1)\epsilon}(\alpha)\otimes w^{-\epsilon}
is in

I(\mathbbold{\Gamma})_{2}
since

\theta^{(n-1)\epsilon}(\alpha)\notin I(\mathbbold{\Lambda}_{\epsilon})
.
∎

Note that, in the classical case, if
K
is a central subgroup of
\Gamma_{0}

contained in
\bigcap_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\operatorname{Dom}\theta^{k}
, then it acts trivially on

\Gamma/\Gamma_{0}
.

Now we compute the modular function
\nabla
on generators, using
Proposition
3.42
. Clearly
\nabla_{\alpha}=p_{\alpha}
for

\alpha\in I(\mathbbold{\Gamma}_{0})\subset I(\mathbbold{\Gamma})
. The value at
w
is given as
follows in terms of
\mathbbold{\Gamma}_{0}
and
\mathbbold{\Lambda}_{\epsilon}
:

Proposition 3.46
.

Assume that the quantum subgroups
\mathbbold{\Lambda}_{\epsilon}
have finite index in

\mathbbold{\Gamma}_{0}
and denote

I(\mathbbold{\Gamma}_{0})/\mathbbold{\Lambda}_{-1}=\{[\epsilon_{0}],\ldots,[\epsilon_{p}]\}
,

\mathbbold{\Lambda}_{1}\backslash I(\mathbbold{\Gamma})=\{[\delta_{0}],\ldots,[\delta_{q}]\}
. Then we have

\nabla_{w}=p_{w}\tilde{R}_{w}^{-1}\tilde{L}_{w}
with

\tilde{L}_{w}=\sum_{i=1}^{q}\frac{\operatorname{dim_{\text{$q$}}}(\delta_{i}\otimes\bar{\delta}_{i})}{\operatorname{dim_{\text{$q$}}}(\delta_{i}\otimes\bar{\delta}_{i})_{\mathbbold{\Lambda}_{1}}}p_{\llbracket w\rrbracket}\quad\text{and}\quad\tilde{R}_{w}=\sum_{j=1}^{p}\frac{\operatorname{dim_{\text{$q$}}}(\bar{\epsilon}_{j}\otimes\epsilon_{j})}{\operatorname{dim_{\text{$q$}}}(\bar{\epsilon}_{j}\otimes\epsilon_{j})_{\mathbbold{\Lambda}_{-1}}}p_{\llbracket w\rrbracket}.

Proof.

We have
\llbracket w\rrbracket=\bigcup_{j}[\epsilon_{j}\otimes w]
and

\epsilon_{j}\otimes w\backsim w
, see the proof of
Proposition
3.43
. Note that
\epsilon_{j}\otimes w
is
irreducible (whereas
\epsilon_{j}\otimes w\otimes\alpha
needs not to be):
indeed

\epsilon_{j}\otimes w\otimes w^{*}\otimes\bar{\epsilon}_{j}=\epsilon_{j}\otimes\bar{\epsilon}_{j}
contains the trivial representation only once. Moreover we
claim that if
k\neq l
then the classes
[\epsilon_{k}\otimes w]
,

[\epsilon_{l}\otimes w]
are distinct, i.e.
w^{*}\otimes\bar{\epsilon}_{l}\otimes\epsilon_{k}\otimes w
has no subobject in

I(\mathbbold{\Gamma}_{0})
. Indeed by definition
\bar{\epsilon}_{l}\otimes\epsilon_{k}
has no
subobject in
\mathbbold{\Lambda}_{-1}
, hence the irreducible subobjects of

w^{*}\otimes\bar{\epsilon}_{l}\otimes\epsilon_{k}\otimes w
belong to

I(\mathbbold{\Gamma})_{2}
.

Then we can apply the definition of
\tilde{L}_{w}
,
\tilde{R}_{w}
and
Proposition
3.42
. Note that we are in the case when

p_{\llbracket w\rrbracket}c(\mathbbold{\Gamma}_{0}\backslash\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Gamma}_{0})=\mathbb{C}p_{\llbracket w\rrbracket}
since

\operatorname{dim}(w)=1
, see Remark
3.41
. For
\alpha=\epsilon\otimes w
,

\epsilon\in I(\mathbbold{\Gamma}_{0})
as above, we have

\kappa_{\alpha}=\operatorname{dim_{\text{$q$}}}(w^{*}\otimes\bar{\epsilon}\otimes\epsilon\otimes w)_{\mathbbold{\Gamma}_{0}}=\operatorname{dim_{\text{$q$}}}(\bar{\epsilon}\otimes\epsilon)_{\mathbbold{\Lambda}_{-1}}
—
whereas

\kappa_{\bar{\alpha}}=\operatorname{dim_{\text{$q$}}}(\epsilon\otimes\bar{\epsilon})=\operatorname{dim_{\text{$q$}}}(\bar{\epsilon}\otimes\epsilon)
. This gives the formula for
\tilde{R}_{w}
,
and the one for
\tilde{L}_{w}
follows by symmetry.
∎

Example 3.47
.

One can construct quantum examples
as follows. Take two finite quantum groups
\Sigma_{\pm 1}
, for instance
duals of classical finite groups. Form the restricted product

\mathbbold{\Gamma}_{0}=\prod^{\prime}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{*}}\Sigma_{\operatorname{sgn}(k)}
, which is the dual of a
profinite group if
\Sigma_{\pm 1}
is the dual of a finite classical
group. If one group
\Sigma_{\epsilon}
is not classical,
\mathbbold{\Gamma}_{0}
is a
unimodular non-classical discrete quantum group. Consider the finite index
subgroups

\mathbbold{\Lambda}_{\epsilon}=\prod^{\prime}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{*},k\neq\epsilon}\Sigma_{\operatorname{sgn}(k)}
. We
have evident isomorphisms
\mathbbold{\Lambda}_{\epsilon}\simeq\mathbbold{\Gamma}
obtained by
shifting the copies of
\Sigma_{\epsilon}
towards
k=0
in the restricted
product. We denote by
\theta:\mathbb{C}[\mathbbold{\Lambda}_{1}]\ \to\mathbb{C}[\mathbbold{\Lambda}_{-1}]
the
corresponding isomorphism.

Denoting
I_{\epsilon}=\operatorname{Corep}(\Sigma_{\epsilon})
, we have natural
identifications
I(\mathbbold{\Gamma}_{0})\simeq\prod^{\prime}_{k}I_{\operatorname{sgn}(k)}
,

\mathbbold{\Lambda}_{\epsilon}\backslash I(\mathbbold{\Gamma}_{0})=I(\mathbbold{\Gamma}_{0})/\mathbbold{\Lambda}_{\epsilon}\simeq I_{\epsilon}
. For
\gamma\in I_{-1}
we have

(\bar{\gamma}\otimes\gamma)_{\mathbbold{\Lambda}_{-1}}=\{1\}
hence

\tilde{R}_{w}=\sum_{\gamma\in I_{-1}}\operatorname{dim}(\gamma)^{2}=\#\Sigma_{-1}
and
similarly
\tilde{L}_{w}=\#\Sigma_{1}
, where we denote

. As a result the modular function
\nabla
of the

(\mathbbold{\Gamma},\mathbbold{\Gamma}_{0})
is non trivial as soon as
\Sigma_{1}
,

\Sigma_{-1}
have different dimensions/cardinals. If one of
\Sigma_{\pm 1}

is non classical (e.g. the dual of a non abelian finite group), the HNN
extension
\mathbbold{\Gamma}
is neither classical, nor co-classical (but it is
unimodular).

An element
\alpha=(\alpha_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{*}}
of
I(\mathbbold{\Gamma}_{0})
is in

\operatorname{Dom}\theta^{n\epsilon}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
, iff we have

\alpha_{\epsilon}=\cdots=\alpha_{n\epsilon}=1
. Hence

\bigcap_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\operatorname{Dom}\theta^{k}=\{1\}
and the action of
\mathbbold{\Gamma}
on

is faithful. Observe also that
\mathbbold{\Gamma}
is finitely
generated although
\mathbbold{\Gamma}_{0}
is not: indeed, denoting
\Sigma^{(k)}
the

is generated by
\Sigma^{(1)}
,
\Sigma^{(-1)}
and
w
.

Example 3.48
.

One can also construct quantum examples by
taking for
\mathbbold{\Gamma}_{0}
the dual of a compact group
G
, and using quantum subgroups

\mathbbold{\Lambda}_{\epsilon}
associated with quotients
H_{\epsilon}=G/K_{\epsilon}
. The
index of
\mathbbold{\Lambda}_{\epsilon}
in
\mathbbold{\Gamma}
is finite iff
K_{\epsilon}
is
finite. If
G
is connected, the subgroups
K_{\epsilon}
must then be central,
and we have
\#I(\mathbbold{\Gamma}_{0})/\mathbbold{\Lambda}_{\epsilon}=\#K_{\epsilon}
.

Assume that
G
is a connected compact Lie group. Then the fundamental group
of
H_{\epsilon}
remembers the cardinality of the kernel
K_{\epsilon}
,
and since we assume
H_{1}
and
H_{-1}
to be isomorphic we will always have

L(\llbracket w\rrbracket)=\#K_{1}=\#K_{-1}=R(\llbracket w\rrbracket)
in this case. Similarly, subobjects of

\bar{\gamma}\otimes\gamma
factor through the center
Z(G)
for any

\gamma\in I(\mathbbold{\Gamma}_{0})\subset\operatorname{Rep}(G)
, hence always belong to

I(\mathbbold{\Lambda}_{\epsilon})
so that we have

\operatorname{dim_{\text{$q$}}}(\bar{\gamma}\otimes\gamma)_{\mathbbold{\Lambda}_{\epsilon}}=\operatorname{dim_{\text{$q$}}}(\bar{\gamma}\otimes\gamma)
and

\tilde{L}_{w}=\tilde{R}_{w}=(\#K_{1})p_{w}
. Moreover, in most
simple Lie groups the center is cyclic so that
\#K_{\epsilon}
determines

K_{\epsilon}
and
\mathbbold{\Lambda}_{1}\simeq\mathbbold{\Lambda}_{-1}
implies in fact

\mathbbold{\Lambda}_{1}=\mathbbold{\Lambda}_{-1}
. This does not mean that the Hecke algebra will
be completely trivial. One can also take for
\theta
a non-inner automorphism
of
H
to make the construction more interesting, so that the resulting

\mathbbold{\Gamma}
looks like a variant of the partial crossed-product construction.

A typical case is given by

G=SU(n)\twoheadrightarrow H_{1}=H_{-1}=PSU(n)
, to be compared with the
“classical case” of the Baumslag-Solitar group

BS(n,n)=HNN(\mathbb{Z},\mathord{\operatorname{id}}:n\mathbb{Z}\to n\mathbb{Z})
. On the other hand

Z(\mathrm{Spin}(4k))=(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})^{2}
, so that the dual of
SO(4k)
can be
realized in two different ways as a subgroup of the dual of

\mathrm{Spin}(4k)
. Of course one can also look at
SO(3)\times SU(2)
which
is a quotient of
SU(2)\times SU(2)
in two different ways. In all these
cases we have
\nabla=1
because
\tilde{L}_{w}=\tilde{R}_{w}
.

Note that since
K_{\epsilon}
is contained in any maximal torus of
G
, the
above construction is compatible with

-deformations –
K_{\epsilon}
remains
a quantum subgroup of the compact quantum group
\mathbb{G}_{q}
corresponding to
G
.
However, we still get
\nabla=1
, by essentially the same reasoning since the
fusion ring of
\mathbb{G}_{q}
is the same as the one of
G
.

4.
Compact open quantum Hecke pairs

In this section we introduce Hecke algebras in the setting of locally compact
(algebraic) quantum groups with compact open quantum subgroups. We then describe
a generalized Schlichting completion, which allows us to subsume the Hecke
algebras from section
3
in this setting, and deduce some
analytic consequences. Finally, we describe how to pass from arbitrary Hecke
pairs to their reduced versions, and exhibit some new examples of algebraic
quantum groups.

4.1.
Compact open Hecke algebras

Let us fix an algebraic quantum group
\mathbb{G}
together with an algebraic quantum
subgroup
\mathbb{H}\subset\mathbb{G}
. Recall that this is determined by a non-zero central
projection
p_{\mathbb{H}}\in\mathcal{O}_{c}(\mathbb{G})
such that

\Delta(p_{\mathbb{H}})(1\otimes p_{\mathbb{H}})=p_{\mathbb{H}}\otimes p_{\mathbb{H}}
. Throughout this section we
normalize the Haar functionals of
\mathcal{O}_{c}(\mathbb{G})
in such a way that

\varphi(p_{\mathbb{H}})=\psi(p_{\mathbb{H}})=1
.

The definition of compactly supported functions on the quantum homogeneous space

\mathbb{G}/\mathbb{H}
is easier than in the discrete case, since the relevant invariant
functions on
\mathbb{G}
are also compactly supported. Namely, we define

c_{c}(\mathbb{G}/\mathbb{H})=\mathcal{O}_{c}(\mathbb{G})^{\mathbb{H}}=\{f\in\mathcal{O}_{c}(\mathbb{G})\mid\Delta(f){(1\otimes p_{\mathbb{H}})}=f\otimes p_{\mathbb{H}}\}
. It is shown in

[
LVD
]
that this algebra is a direct sum of
matrix algebras, i.e. it corresponds to a “discrete” quantum space. We denote
by
c_{0}(\mathbb{G}/\mathbb{H})
the closure of
c_{c}(\mathbb{G}/\mathbb{H})
in
C_{0}(\mathbb{G})
and

\ell^{2}(\mathbb{G}/\mathbb{H})
its closure in
L^{2}(\mathbb{G})
. It can be shown that
c_{0}(\mathbb{G}/\mathbb{H})

is a quantum homogeneous space in the sense of
[
Vae05
]
, cf

[
KKS16
, Proposition 6.2, Theorem 6.4]
. One can
define
c_{c}(\mathbb{H}\backslash\mathbb{G})
,
c_{c}(\mathbb{H}\backslash\mathbb{G}/\mathbb{H})
exactly in the same way, as
well as the corresponding
c_{0}
and
\ell^{2}
spaces.

To define the Hecke algebra
\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{G},\mathbb{H})
it suffices to restrict the natural
convolution product of
\mathcal{O}_{c}(\mathbb{G})
to the space of
\mathbb{H}
-biinvariant functions.
This product is transported from the dual multiplier Hopf algebra
\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{G})
via
the Fourier transform
\mathcal{F}:\mathcal{O}_{c}(\mathbb{G})\rightarrow\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{G})
determined by

(\mathcal{F}(f),h)=\varphi(hf)
. Explicitly we have, for
f
,
g\in\mathcal{O}_{c}(\mathbb{G})
:

f*g=(f\varphi\otimes\mathord{\operatorname{id}})(S^{-1}\otimes\mathord{\operatorname{id}})\Delta(g)=(\mathord{\operatorname{id}}\otimes\varphi S^{-1}(g))\Delta(f).

Together with the
*
-structure
f^{\sharp}:=\mathcal{F}^{-1}(\mathcal{F}(f)^{*})
, not to be
confused with the given
*
-structure on
\mathcal{O}_{c}(\mathbb{G})
, this turns
\mathcal{O}_{c}(\mathbb{G})

into a
*
-algebra. Explicitly we have
f^{\sharp}=S(f)^{*}\delta
, where

\delta\in\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{O}_{c}(\mathbb{G}))
is the modular element of
\mathbb{G}
.

The left regular representation of the dual algebra

\lambda:\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{G})\rightarrow B(L^{2}(\mathbb{G}))
is then given by

\lambda(\mathcal{F}(f))(\Lambda(g))=\Lambda(f*g)
, see

[
VY20
, Section 4.2.2]
. We also have the right regular
representation
\rho:\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{G})\rightarrow B(L^{2}(\mathbb{G}))
given by

\rho(\mathcal{F}(f))=\hat{J}\lambda(\mathcal{F}(f))^{*}\hat{J}
. Here
\hat{J}
is the
modular conjugation operator for
\hat{\varphi}
, the dual left Haar weight also
given by the formula
\hat{\varphi}(\mathcal{F}(f))=\epsilon(f)
.

Explicitly we have

\rho(\mathcal{F}(f))(\Lambda(g))=\Lambda(g*\hat{\sigma}_{-i/2}(f))
for all

f,g\in\mathcal{O}_{c}(\mathbb{G})
. Here, by slight abuse of notation, we write

\hat{\sigma}_{-i/2}(f)
instead of
\mathcal{F}^{-1}(\hat{\sigma}_{-i/2}(\mathcal{F}(f))
,
where
(\hat{\sigma}_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
is the modular group of

\hat{\varphi}
. Note that the map
f\mapsto\hat{\sigma}_{-i/2}(f)
is an
algebra isomorphism from
(\mathcal{O}_{c}(\mathbb{G}),*)
to
\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{G})
. We have

\delta p_{\mathbb{H}}=p_{\mathbb{H}}
because
\mathbb{H}
is compact and

\hat{\sigma}_{t}(p_{\mathbb{H}})=p_{\mathbb{H}}
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
because the
restriction of
\hat{\varphi}:\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{G})\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
to
\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{H})
is the
left Haar weight of
\hat{\mathbb{H}}
.

Lemma 4.1
.

We have
c_{c}(\mathbb{H}\backslash\mathbb{G}/\mathbb{H})=p_{\mathbb{H}}*\mathcal{O}_{c}(\mathbb{G})*p_{\mathbb{H}}
. In particular

c_{c}(\mathbb{H}\backslash\mathbb{G}/\mathbb{H})
is closed under the convolution product
*
and the
involution
\sharp
.

Proof.

Let
f\in\mathcal{O}_{c}(\mathbb{G})
. Using the definition of the convolution product we
calculate

p_{\mathbb{H}}*f=(p_{\mathbb{H}}\varphi\otimes\mathord{\operatorname{id}})(S^{-1}\otimes\mathord{\operatorname{id}})\Delta(f)=(h\otimes\mathord{\operatorname{id}})(\pi_{\mathbb{H}}S^{-1}\otimes\mathord{\operatorname{id}})\Delta(f)=(h\pi_{\mathbb{H}}\otimes\mathord{\operatorname{id}})\Delta(f)
, where
h
is the Haar
functional of
\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{H})
and
\pi_{\mathbb{H}}:\mathcal{O}_{c}(\mathbb{G})\rightarrow\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{H})
the
restriction map. It follows that
p_{\mathbb{H}}*f=f
iff
f\in c_{c}(\mathbb{H}\backslash\mathbb{G})
. Similarly one checks
f\in c_{c}(\mathbb{G}/\mathbb{H})
iff

f*p_{\mathbb{H}}=f
. Since
p_{\mathbb{H}}
is a projection in the convolution algebra
this yields the claim.
∎

This allows us to give the following definition.

Definition 4.2
.

The Hecke algebra of
(\mathbb{G},\mathbb{H})
is the
*
-algebra

\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{G},\mathbb{H})=c_{c}(\mathbb{H}\backslash\mathbb{G}/\mathbb{H})
with the convolution product and

*
-structure
\sharp
as above.

We obtain a nondegenerate
*
-representation of
\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{G},\mathbb{H})
on

\ell^{2}(\mathbb{G}/\mathbb{H})
by considering the restriction of the right regular
representation. Here we use that for
f\in c_{c}(\mathbb{H}\backslash\mathbb{G}/\mathbb{H})
and

g\in c_{c}(\mathbb{G}/\mathbb{H})
the element
g*f
is again contained in

c_{c}(\mathbb{G}/\mathbb{H})
, so that
\rho(\mathcal{F}(f))
indeed maps
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{G}/\mathbb{H})
to
itself. We also observe that
\rho\circ\mathcal{F}:\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{G},\mathbb{H})\rightarrow B(\ell^{2}(\mathbb{G}/\mathbb{H}))

is anti-multiplicative for the convolution product, that is,

\rho(\mathcal{F}(f*g))=\rho(\mathcal{F}(g))\rho(\mathcal{F}(f))
for all
f,g\in\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{G},\mathbb{H})
.

In the same way as in the discrete case we view
c_{c}(\mathbb{G}/\mathbb{H})
as a

\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{G})
-module, see the discussion before Proposition
3.27
, and
obtain the space
\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb{G}}(c_{c}(\mathbb{G}/\mathbb{H}))
of
\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{G})
-module maps.

Proposition 4.3
.

We have (mutually inverse)
anti-multiplicative algebra isomorphisms

\displaystyle T:\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{G},\mathbb{H})\rightarrow\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb{G}}(c_{c}(\mathbb{G}/\mathbb{H})),\quad T(f)(h)=h*f\qquad\text{and}

\displaystyle T^{-1}:\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb{G}}(c_{c}(\mathbb{G}/\mathbb{H}))\rightarrow\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{G},\mathbb{H})\cong c_{c}(\mathbb{G}/\mathbb{H})^{\mathbb{H}},\quad T^{-1}(F)=F(p_{\mathbb{H}}).

Proof.

Since
T(f)
for
f\in\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{G},\mathbb{H})
commutes with the left convolution
action of
\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{G})
it is clear that
T
is well-defined, and it is obvious
from the definition that
T
is anti-multiplicative.

Let us verify that
T
is an isomorphism by verifying that the above formula
for
T^{-1}
yields indeed its inverse. Well-definedness is again easy to
check, and using left
\mathbb{H}
-invariance of

f\in\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{G},\mathbb{H})=c_{c}(\mathbb{H}\backslash\mathbb{G}/\mathbb{H})
we get

(T^{-1}T)(f)=T(f)(p_{\mathbb{H}})=p_{\mathbb{H}}*f=f.

Conversely, given
F\in\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{G}}(c_{c}(\mathbb{G}/\mathbb{H}),c_{c}(\mathbb{G}/\mathbb{H}))
we compute

\displaystyle(TT^{-1})(F)(h)

\displaystyle=(TT^{-1})(F)(h*p_{\mathbb{H}})=h*(TT^{-1})(F)(p_{\mathbb{H}})

\displaystyle=h*p_{\mathbb{H}}*T^{-1}(F)=h*F(p_{\mathbb{H}})=F(h*p_{\mathbb{H}})=F(h)

for all
h\in c_{c}(\mathbb{G}/\mathbb{H})
, using that
c_{c}(\mathbb{G}/\mathbb{H})\subset\mathcal{O}_{c}(\mathbb{G})

because
\mathbb{H}\subset\mathbb{G}
is compact open.
∎

4.2.
Example: quantum doubles

Let us consider the situation where
\mathbb{H}
is a compact quantum group and

\mathbb{G}=\mathbb{H}\bowtie\hat{\mathbb{H}}
the quantum double of
\mathbb{H}
. Recall that
\mathbb{G}
is
the locally compact quantum group given by the von Neumann algebra

coproduct

\Delta_{\mathbb{G}}=(\mathord{\operatorname{id}}\otimes\sigma\otimes\mathord{\operatorname{id}})(\mathord{\operatorname{id}}\otimes\mathrm{ad}(W)\otimes\mathord{\operatorname{id}})(\Delta\otimes\hat{\Delta}),

In fact, the quantum double of a compact quantum group is naturally an algebraic
quantum group in the sense of Van Daele, which allows us to give algebraic
descriptions of almost all the data involved

]
,

[
VY20

\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{H})
for the

\mathcal{O}_{c}(\mathbb{G})=\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{H})\odot\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{H})

naturally as an algebraic compact open quantum subgroup, and the corresponding
group-like projection is
p_{\mathbb{H}}=1\otimes p_{0}
, where

is the
central support of the counit. One checks that

\displaystyle c_{c}(\mathbb{H}\backslash\mathbb{G})=1\otimes\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{H})\qquad c_{c}(\mathbb{G}/\mathbb{H})

\displaystyle=W^{*}(1\otimes\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{H}))W

inside
\mathcal{O}_{c}(\mathbb{G})
, and the space of
\mathbb{H}
-biinvariant functions on
\mathbb{G}

c_{c}(\mathbb{H}\backslash\mathbb{G}/\mathbb{H})\cong\{x\in\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{H})\mid W^{*}(1\otimes x)W=1\otimes x\}=Z(\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{H})),

i.e.
c_{c}(\mathbb{H}\backslash\mathbb{G}/\mathbb{H})
identifies with the center of the algebraic
convolution algebra of
\mathbb{H}
with respect to its ordinary product.

By definition, the Hecke algebra
\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{G},\mathbb{H})
is
c_{c}(\mathbb{H}\backslash\mathbb{G}/\mathbb{H})

equipped with the restriction of the convolution product on
\mathcal{O}_{c}(\mathbb{G})
. In the
present situation, it is more convenient to describe the
*
-subalgebra

\mathcal{F}(c_{c}(\mathbb{H}\backslash\mathbb{G}/\mathbb{H}))\subset\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{G})
obtained via the Fourier transform

\mathcal{F}:\mathcal{O}_{c}(\mathbb{G})\rightarrow\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{G}),\mathcal{F}(f)(h)=\varphi(hf)
. As discussed in

[
VY20
, Chapter 4]
, we can identify

, which is the algebraic tensor product

\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{H})\otimes\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{H})
equipped with the twisted multiplication

(x\bowtie f)(y\bowtie g):=xy_{(2)}(y_{(1)},f_{(1)})\bowtie(\hat{S}(y_{(3)}),f_{(3)})f_{(2)}g,

for
x,y\in\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{H})
,
f,g\in\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{H})
. The
*
-structure on
\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{G})
is
defined in such a way that both
\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{H})\bowtie 1
and
1\bowtie\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{H})

are
*
-subalgebras of
\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{G}))
, and the natural skew-pairing between

\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{G})
and
\mathcal{O}_{c}(\mathbb{G})
is

(y\bowtie g,f\otimes x)=(y,f)(g,x),\quad x,y\in\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{H}),f,g\in\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{H}),

again following the conventions in
[
VY20
]
. The left and right
invariant Haar functional on
\mathcal{O}_{c}(\mathbb{G})
is given by

\varphi=\hat{h}\otimes h_{R}
, where
\hat{h}
is the Haar state of

\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{H})
and

the right Haar functional on
\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{H})
, compare

[
VY20
, Proposition 4.19]
. As is well-known, using the Fourier transform

can identify

with the

*
-subalgebra

{(p_{0}\bowtie 1)}{(1\bowtie\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{H}))}

{(p_{0}\bowtie 1)}

=p_{0}\bowtie\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{H})^{\mathrm{ad}}\subset\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{G})

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{H})^{\mathrm{ad}}

\displaystyle=\{f\in\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{H})\mid f_{(2)}\otimes\hat{h}(f_{(1)}S^{-1}(f_{(3)}))=f\otimes 1\}

\displaystyle=\{f\in\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{H})\mid\Delta^{\mathrm{cop}}(f)=\Delta(f)\},

with the product and

This is precisely the algebra of characters inside

We note that, with suitable adjustments, similar computations go through for

4.3.
The Schlichting completion

Let
\mathbbold{\Gamma}
be a discrete quantum group and
\mathbbold{\Lambda}\subset\mathbbold{\Gamma}
be a
quantum subgroup. If
\mathbbold{\Lambda}\subset\mathbbold{\Gamma}
is almost normal
(see Definition

consisting of an algebraic quantum group

and a compact open quantum

[

More precisely, our strategy is as follows. We first define the algebra

\mathcal{O}_{c}(\mathbb{G})
as a subalgebra of
\ell^{\infty}(\mathbbold{\Gamma})
using the “discrete”
Hecke convolution product, see Definition
4.5
. The key point
of the construction consists then in proving that this algebra is a multiplier
Hopf
*
-algebra, and more specifically, that the coproduct takes its values in
the appropriate subspace of
\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{O}_{c}(\mathbb{G})\odot\mathcal{O}_{c}(\mathbb{G}))
, see
Proposition
4.6
.

It is then easy to see that the
projection
p{\Lambda}
corresponds to a CQG algebra

\mathcal{O}_{c}(\mathbb{H})\subset\mathcal{O}_{c}(\mathbb{G})
, and that
c_{c}(\mathbb{G}/\mathbb{H})=c_{c}(\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Lambda})

as subspaces of
\ell^{\infty}(\mathbbold{\Gamma})
, see Propositions
4.10

and
4.11
. Using the Haar functional of
\mathcal{O}_{c}(\mathbb{H})

and the
\mathbbold{\Gamma}
-invariant functional

on

one can
then construct the integrals of
\mathcal{O}_{c}(\mathbb{G})
, so that
\mathbb{G}
is in fact a locally
compact quantum group by
[
KVD97
]
.
We end the section by making the connection between the “discrete” and
“compact open” Hecke algebras
\mathcal{H}(\mathbbold{\Gamma},\mathbbold{\Lambda})
and
\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{G},\mathbb{H})
.

Lemma 4.4
.

We say that
\gamma\in I(\mathbbold{\Gamma})
is in the support of

x\in\ell^{\infty}(\mathbbold{\Gamma})
if
p_{\gamma}x\neq 0
. Then given

\gamma\in I(\mathbbold{\Gamma})
,
a\in c_{c}(\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Lambda})
and

b\in c_{c}(\mathbbold{\Lambda}\backslash\mathbbold{\Gamma})
such that
\gamma\in\operatorname{Supp}(a*b)
, there exist

\alpha\in\operatorname{Supp}(a)
,
\beta\in\operatorname{Supp}(b)
and
\lambda\in I(\mathbbold{\Lambda})
such that

\gamma\subset\alpha\otimes\lambda\otimes\beta
.

Proof.

If
\gamma\in\operatorname{Supp}a*b
, at least one term of the sum (
3.6
)
has
\gamma
in its support. Hence there exist
\alpha\in\operatorname{Supp}(a)
,

\beta\in\operatorname{Supp}(b)
such the corepresentation

\bar{\alpha}\otimes\gamma\otimes\bar{\beta}
contains an element

\lambda\in I(\mathbbold{\Lambda})
. By Frobenius reciprocity a non-zero morphism

\lambda\to\bar{\alpha}\otimes\gamma\otimes\bar{\beta}
induces a non-zero
morphism
\alpha\otimes\lambda\otimes\beta\to\gamma
and we are done.
∎

Similarly one can define the support
\operatorname{Supp}(\varphi)
of a linear functional

\varphi\in c_{c}(\mathbbold{\Gamma})^{*}
as the set of elements
\gamma\in I(\mathbbold{\Gamma})
such
that
p_{\gamma}\varphi\neq 0
. If
\varphi
has finite support, it extends
uniquely to a normal functional
\varphi\in\ell^{\infty}(\mathbbold{\Gamma})_{*}
.

Definition 4.5
.

Given a Hecke pair
(\mathbbold{\Gamma},\mathbbold{\Lambda})
we denote by
\mathcal{O}_{c}(\mathbb{G})

(resp.
C_{0}(\mathbb{G})
) the subalgebra (resp. the

-subalgebra) of

\ell^{\infty}(\mathbbold{\Gamma})
generated by the elements

with

,

In the following lemmas we will always assume that
\mathcal{O}_{c}(\mathbb{G})
and
C_{0}(\mathbb{G})

arise in the above way from a Hecke pair. Note that by definition of the

is a finite sum of

so that it is indeed in

\ell^{\infty}(\mathbbold{\Gamma})

fact a
*

\ell^{\infty}(\mathbbold{\Gamma})
and

In view of the definition of

\mathcal{O}_{c}(\mathbb{G})

(\mathord{\operatorname{id}}\otimes\varphi)\Delta(a)

,

,

for all but a
finite number of right classes

. We denote

the space of these finitely supported functionals. It follows from this

\mathcal{O}_{c}(\mathbb{G})
in

is
the so-called cokernel of the
\mathbbold{\Gamma}
-action on
\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Lambda}
, which is
known to be a Baaj-Vaes subalgebra

, Definition 2.8,

. Below we prove a more precise,

For
x\in C_{0}(\mathbb{G})
we can consider
\Delta(x)
which is a priori an element of

\ell^{\infty}(\mathbbold{\Gamma})\bar{\otimes}\ell^{\infty}(\mathbbold{\Gamma})
.

Proposition 4.6
.

We have
\Delta(\mathcal{O}_{c}(\mathbb{G}))(1\otimes\mathcal{O}_{c}(\mathbb{G}))
,

\Delta(\mathcal{O}_{c}(\mathbb{G}))(\mathcal{O}_{c}(\mathbb{G})\otimes 1)

\subset

{\mathcal{O}_{c}(\mathbb{G})\odot\mathcal{O}_{c}(\mathbb{G})}
and
\Delta(C_{0}(\mathbb{G}))(1\otimes C_{0}(\mathbb{G}))

\Delta(C_{0}(\mathbb{G}))(C_{0}(\mathbb{G})\otimes 1)

\subset C_{0}(\mathbb{G})\otimes C_{0}(\mathbb{G})
.

Proof.

The assertions about
C_{0}(\mathbb{G})
follow by density from the ones for

\mathcal{O}_{c}(\mathbb{G})
, since
\Delta
is continuous. Let us prove that

\Delta(\mathcal{O}_{c}(\mathbb{G}))(\mathcal{O}_{c}(\mathbb{G})\otimes 1)\subset{\mathcal{O}_{c}(\mathbb{G})\odot\mathcal{O}_{c}(\mathbb{G})}

form
\Delta(x)(y\otimes 1)
with

,
y=c*d
,

a,c\in c_{c}(\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Lambda})
,
b,d\in c_{c}(\mathbbold{\Lambda}\backslash\mathbbold{\Gamma})
, and by
linearity we can assume

,
b=p_{[\beta]}b
,

c=p_{[\gamma]}c
,

using left or right classes as
appropriate.

As a first step, consider a linear functional
\varphi\in c_{c}(\mathbbold{\Gamma})^{*}
with
finite support and compute, using (
3.5
) and coassociativity:

(\mathord{\operatorname{id}}\otimes\varphi)\Delta(x)=\kappa_{\alpha}^{-1}(h_{R}S(a_{\alpha})\otimes\mathord{\operatorname{id}}\otimes\varphi)\Delta^{2}(b)=a*((\mathord{\operatorname{id}}\otimes\varphi)\Delta(b)).

Since

\Delta(\ell^{\infty}(\mathbbold{\Lambda}\backslash\mathbbold{\Gamma}))\subset\ell^{\infty}(\mathbbold{\Lambda}\backslash\mathbbold{\Gamma})\bar{\otimes}\ell^{\infty}(\mathbbold{\Gamma})
we have

(\mathord{\operatorname{id}}\otimes\varphi)\Delta(b)\subset\ell^{\infty}(\mathbbold{\Lambda}\backslash\mathbbold{\Gamma})
. Take
moreover
\tau\in\mathbbold{\Lambda}\backslash I(\mathbbold{\Gamma})
such that

(p_{\tau}\otimes\varphi)\Delta(b)\neq 0

\nu\in\tau
,

\mu\in\operatorname{Supp}(\varphi)
and
\lambda\in I(\mathbbold{\Lambda})
such that

\lambda\otimes\beta
and
\nu\otimes\mu
have a common irreducible
subobject. By Frobenius reciprocity this implies

\nu\subset\lambda\otimes\beta\otimes\bar{\mu}

\operatorname{Supp}(\varphi)
is
finite, this shows that
\tau
belongs to a finite subset of

\mathbbold{\Lambda}\backslash I(\mathbbold{\Gamma})
(depending on
\operatorname{Supp}(\varphi)
and
\beta

(\mathord{\operatorname{id}}\otimes\varphi)\Delta(b)\in c_{c}(\mathbbold{\Lambda}\backslash\mathbbold{\Gamma})
and

(\mathord{\operatorname{id}}\otimes\varphi)\Delta(x)\in\mathcal{O}_{c}(\mathbb{G})
.

Second step. Observe that we have by (
3.7
) and
coassociativity:

\Delta(a*b)(y\otimes 1)=\kappa_{\bar{\beta}}^{-1}(\mathord{\operatorname{id}}\otimes\mathord{\operatorname{id}}\otimes h_{L}S^{-1}(b_{\beta}))(\mathord{\operatorname{id}}\otimes\Delta)[\Delta(a)(y\otimes 1)].

Recall that we have

\Delta(a)\in\ell^{\infty}(\mathbbold{\Gamma})\bar{\otimes}\ell^{\infty}(\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Lambda})
. Moreover, take
\tau\in I(\mathbbold{\Gamma})/\mathbbold{\Lambda}

such that
\Delta(a)(y\otimes p_{\tau})\neq 0
. Then there exist

\mu\in\operatorname{Supp}(y)
,
\nu\in\tau
,
\lambda\in I(\mathbbold{\Lambda})
such that

\alpha\otimes\lambda
and
\mu\otimes\nu
have a common irreducible
subobject. By Frobenius reciprocity this implies

\nu\subset\bar{\mu}\otimes\alpha\otimes\lambda

Lemma
4.4
there exists
\lambda^{\prime}\in I(\mathbbold{\Lambda})
such
that
\mu\subset\gamma\otimes\lambda^{\prime}\otimes\delta
. Since double classes in

\tau

Note that

\ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{P}):=\sum_{\tau\in P}p_{\tau}\ell^{\infty}(\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Lambda})

. It follows that there

and elements

\Delta(a*b)(y\otimes 1)=\sum s_{i}\otimes(t_{i}*b)\in\ell^{\infty}(\mathbbold{\Gamma})\odot(\ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{P})*b)

Choose now a finite, linearly independent family of vectors

the finite dimensional subspace
\ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{P})*b\subset\mathcal{O}_{c}(\mathbb{G})
, and

\varphi_{k}\in c_{c}(\mathbbold{\Gamma})^{*}
such that

\varphi_{l}(x_{k})=\delta_{k,l}
for all

; we have then

z_{k}=((\mathord{\operatorname{id}}\otimes\varphi_{k})\Delta(x))y
.
Applying the first step to
\varphi_{k}
we get

, hence

.
∎

Recall that the antipode of
\mathbbold{\Gamma}
is well-defined as a map

Proposition 4.7
.

We have
S(\mathcal{O}_{c}(\mathbb{G}))\subset\mathcal{O}_{c}(\mathbb{G})
. Equipped with the restriction of

\Delta

(resp.

) is a multiplier Hopf-

-algebra
(resp. a bicancellative Hopf-

For any

,

S(b^{*})*S(a^{*})=S((a*b)^{*})=S(a^{*}*b^{*})

Note that the central projection

Definition 4.8
.

We denote
p_{\mathbb{H}}=p{\Lambda}\in\mathcal{O}_{c}(\mathbb{G})
and

We now describe the connection of the construction developed above to the

.

Proposition 4.9
.

Let
(\mathbbold{\Gamma},\mathbbold{\Lambda})=(\Gamma,\Lambda)
be a classical discrete Hecke
pair, with Schlichting completion
(G,H)
. The canonical map
\Gamma\to G

with dense image induces an embedding

C_{0}(G)=C_{0}(\mathbb{G})

and
\mathcal{O}_{c}(G)=\mathcal{O}_{c}(\mathbb{G})
, and similarly
C(H)=C(\mathbb{H})
and

\mathcal{O}(H)=\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{H})

Proof.

Observe that
a*b
for
a\in c_{c}(\Gamma/\Lambda)
,

b\in c_{c}(\Lambda\backslash\Gamma)
is right invariant under the action of the
intersection of the point stabilizers in
\Gamma
of all points in the
finite set
\operatorname{Supp}(b)\subset\Lambda\backslash\Gamma
. Since this group contains the
intersection of finitely many point stabilizers of the action of
\Gamma
on

\Gamma/\Lambda
we see that

, compare the description of
the latter in
[

]
. Hence we get

\mathcal{O}_{c}(\mathbb{G})\subset\mathcal{O}_{c}(G)
. Conversely, an element

of
\mathcal{O}(G)
can be
written as finite sum of characteristic functions on
\Gamma/\Gamma_{F}
for
finite sets

. Modulo left translation by
\Gamma
we
can assume that

is the characteristic function of the point
\Gamma_{F}
in

\Gamma/\Gamma_{F}
. We can write this as product of all

,
where

a_{\gamma}=\delta_{\gamma\Lambda}\in c_{c}(\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Lambda}),f_{\gamma}={\operatorname{ev}}_{\gamma\Lambda}\in c_{c}(\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Lambda})^{*}
for

. This yields the equality

\mathcal{O}_{c}(G)=\mathcal{O}_{c}(\mathbb{G})

From the fact that both

and

are completions of

. Finally, the claim about the canonical subgroups follows
from
\mathcal{O}(H)=p_{\Lambda}\mathcal{O}_{c}(G)=p_{\Lambda}\mathcal{O}_{c}(\mathbb{G})=\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{H})
.
∎

We now return to the general setup of quantum Hecke pairs.

Proposition 4.10

Equipped with the restriction
\Delta_{\mathbb{H}}
of
(p_{\mathbb{H}}\otimes p_{\mathbb{H}})\Delta

\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{H})
(resp.

Recall that the comultiplication on
\mathcal{O}_{c}(\mathbb{G})

\mathbbold{\Gamma}

in

extends continuously to a unital

This implies that there is a Haar functional on

∎

So the corresponding compact quantum group

.

We have

-invariant functional on

{(\mathord{\operatorname{id}}\otimes\mu)}

{((p_{\alpha}\otimes 1)\Delta(x))}=\mu(x)p_{\alpha}

Note that

.

For any

(\mathord{\operatorname{id}}\otimes hp_{\mathbb{H}})\Delta(x)\in c_{c}(\mathbb{G}/\mathbb{H})
. If
\mu

\mathbb{G}
-invariant functional on

Proof.

Define a map

,

4.4.
Reduction procedure

Starting from a discrete Hecke pair
(\mathbbold{\Gamma},\mathbbold{\Lambda})
, it can well happen
that the Schlichting completion
\mathbb{G}
is in fact discrete or even trivial. This
is connected to the faithfulness of the action of
\mathbbold{\Gamma}
on

\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Lambda}
and to the reduction procedure that we describe now.

Suppose that
\mathbbold{\Gamma}
is a discrete quantum group with a quantum subgroup

\mathbbold{\Lambda}
and the corresponding projection

p_{\mathbbold{\Lambda}}\in\ell^{\infty}(\mathbbold{\Gamma})
. By
c_{c}(\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Lambda})^{\vee}
we
denote finitely supported functionals on
\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Lambda}
as in the previous
subsection.

Recall from
[
KKSV
, Definition 2.8]
that the cokernel of an action
of a discrete quantum group
\mathbbold{\Gamma}
on a
C^{*}
-algebra
A=C_{0}(\mathbb{X})
, given by a

*
-homomorphism
\alpha:C_{0}(\mathbb{X})\to M(c_{0}(\mathbbold{\Gamma})\otimes C_{0}(\mathbb{X}))
, is
defined as the following weak closure in
\ell^{\infty}(\mathbbold{\Gamma})
:

(4.1)

N(\mathbbold{\Gamma}\curvearrowright\mathbb{X})=\{(\mathord{\operatorname{id}}\otimes\mu)\alpha(a);a\in A,\mu\in A^{*}\}^{\prime\prime}.

Here we are concerned with the case
C_{0}(\mathbb{X})=c_{0}(\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Lambda})
, with
\alpha

being the appropriate restriction of
\Delta
.

Definition 4.18
.

We say that the pair
(\mathbbold{\Gamma},\mathbbold{\Lambda})
is reduced if the canonical action
of
\mathbbold{\Gamma}
on
\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Lambda}
is faithful, i.e. the
cokernel
N(\mathbbold{\Gamma}\curvearrowright\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Lambda})
coincides with
\ell^{\infty}(\mathbbold{\Gamma})
.

Note that in the definition (
4.1
) of the cokernel, when

\mathbb{X}=\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Lambda}
, we can also work with

a\in\ell^{\infty}(\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Lambda})
and
\mu\in\ell^{\infty}(\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Lambda})_{*}
,
or with
a\in c_{c}(\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Lambda})
and
\mu\in c_{c}(\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Lambda})^{\vee}
.
In particular we see, following the discussion after
Definition
4.5
, that a Hecke pair
(\mathbbold{\Gamma},\mathbbold{\Lambda})
is
reduced if and only if
\mathbbold{\Gamma}
embeds into its Schlichting completion
(\mathbb{G},\mathbb{H})
, i.e. the canonical map
\iota:\mathcal{O}_{c}(\mathbb{G})\to\ell^{\infty}(\mathbbold{\Gamma})
has strictly
dense image.

It is shown in
[
KKSV
, Proposition 2.9]
that the cokernel

N(\mathbbold{\Gamma}\curvearrowright\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Lambda})
is a Baaj-Vaes subalgebra of
\ell^{\infty}(\mathbbold{\Gamma})
, so
that there exists a discrete quantum group
\tilde{\mathbbold{\Gamma}}
such that

\ell^{\infty}(\tilde{\mathbbold{\Gamma}})=N(\mathbbold{\Gamma}\curvearrowright\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Lambda})
(with the
comultiplication given simply by the restriction). Putting
a=p{\Lambda}
and
taking for
\mu
the restriction of the counit
\epsilon
, we see that

p{\Lambda}
belongs also to
N(\mathbbold{\Gamma}\curvearrowright\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Lambda})
and thus defines a quantum
subgroup
\tilde{\mathbbold{\Lambda}}
of
\tilde{\mathbbold}{\Gamma}
such that

\ell^{\infty}(\tilde{\mathbbold{\Lambda}})=p{\Lambda}\ell^{\infty}(\tilde{\mathbbold{\Gamma}})
.

When
\mathbbold{\Lambda}
is normal in
\mathbbold{\Gamma}
, it follows for example from the proof of

[
VV03
, Theorem 2.11]
(or from results of

[
KKS16
]
) that

N(\mathbbold{\Gamma}\curvearrowright\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Lambda})=\ell^{\infty}(\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Lambda})
. In this case it is
easy to see that
\tilde{\mathbbold{\Lambda}}=\{e\}
: as

\ell^{\infty}(\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Lambda})
is the space of left slices of

\Delta(p{\Lambda})
by
[
KKS16
, Theorem 3.3]
, we have that

\displaystyle p{\Lambda}\ell^{\infty}(\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Lambda})

\displaystyle=p{\Lambda}\{(\omega\otimes\mathord{\operatorname{id}})\Delta(p{\Lambda})\mid\omega\in\ell^{1}(\mathbbold{\Gamma})\}^{\prime\prime}

\displaystyle=\{(\omega\otimes\mathord{\operatorname{id}})((1\otimes p{\Lambda})\Delta(p{\Lambda}))\mid\omega\in\ell^{1}(\mathbbold{\Gamma})\}^{\prime\prime}=\mathbb{C}p{\Lambda}.

Proposition 4.19
.

Let
(\mathbbold{\Gamma},\mathbbold{\Lambda})
and
(\tilde{\mathbbold}{\Gamma},\tilde{\mathbbold}{\Lambda})
be as above.
Then
(\tilde{\mathbbold}{\Gamma},\tilde{\mathbbold}{\Lambda})
(called further the
reduction
of

(\mathbbold{\Gamma},\mathbbold{\Lambda})
) is reduced,

\ell^{\infty}(\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Lambda})=\ell^{\infty}(\tilde{\mathbbold}{\Gamma}/\tilde{\mathbbold}{\Lambda})
,

\ell^{\infty}(\mathbbold{\Lambda}\backslash\mathbbold{\Gamma})=\ell^{\infty}(\tilde{\mathbbold}{\Lambda}\backslash\tilde{\mathbbold}{\Gamma})
. Moreover if

\theta:\ell^{\infty}(\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Lambda})_{+}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
is an nsf weight, then
it is
\mathbbold{\Gamma}
-invariant iff it is
\tilde{\mathbbold}{\Gamma}
-invariant.

Proof.

Denote by
\tilde{\alpha}
the action of
\tilde{\mathbbold{\Gamma}}
on

\ell^{\infty}(\tilde{\mathbbold}{\Gamma}/\tilde{\mathbbold}{\Lambda})
. Note that this is again given by
the (suitable restriction of) the coproduct of
\ell^{\infty}(\mathbbold{\Gamma})
. Thus to
see that
(\tilde{\mathbbold}{\Gamma},\tilde{\mathbbold}{\Lambda})
is reduced it suffices to show that

N(\mathbbold{\Gamma}\curvearrowright\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Lambda})=N(\tilde{\mathbbold}{\Gamma}\curvearrowright\tilde{\mathbbold}{\Gamma}/\tilde{\mathbbold}{\Lambda})
; this in turn will
follow once we establish the latter part of the proposition.

Note that

\ell^{\infty}(\tilde{\mathbbold}{\Gamma}/\tilde{\mathbbold}{\Lambda})=\{a\in N(\mathbbold{\Gamma}\curvearrowright\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Lambda})\mid(1\otimes p{\Lambda})\Delta(a)=p{\Lambda}\otimes a\}.

Thus it suffices to show that we have

\ell^{\infty}(\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Lambda})\subset N(\mathbbold{\Gamma}\curvearrowright\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Lambda})
. That however
follows immediately as we can simply put
\mu=\epsilon
in the definition of

N(\mathbbold{\Gamma}\curvearrowright\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Lambda})
.

The equality of the left coset spaces would follow in a similar manner once we
observe that

\ell^{\infty}(\mathbbold{\Lambda}\backslash\mathbbold{\Gamma})\subset N(\mathbbold{\Gamma}\curvearrowright\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Lambda})
. This is true as
N(\mathbbold{\Gamma}\curvearrowright\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Lambda})
is

\ell^{\infty}(\mathbbold{\Lambda}\backslash\mathbbold{\Gamma})=R\big(\ell^{\infty}(\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Lambda})\big)

The last statement is then obvious (the invariance condition is literally the
same).
∎

Note that the inclusion

\ell^{\infty}(\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Lambda})\subset N(\mathbbold{\Gamma}\curvearrowright\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Lambda})
can be informally
understood as the classically obvious fact that the kernel of the action of

\mathbbold{\Gamma}
on
\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Lambda}
is contained in
\mathbbold{\Lambda}
.

Proposition 4.20
.

Let
(\mathbbold{\Gamma},\mathbbold{\Lambda})
be as above and let

(\tilde{\mathbbold}{\Gamma},\tilde{\mathbbold}{\Lambda})
be its reduction. Then
(\mathbbold{\Gamma},\mathbbold{\Lambda})

satisfies the Hecke condition if and only if
(\tilde{\mathbbold}{\Gamma},\tilde{\mathbbold}{\Lambda})

does, and if this is the case, the corresponding Hecke algebras are

Proof.

The first statement follows from Proposition
3.7
, as the
proposition above implies that the actions of
\mathbbold{\Lambda}
on

\ell^{\infty}(\mathbbold{\Gamma}/\mathbbold{\Lambda})
and of
\tilde{\mathbbold}{\Lambda}
on

\ell^{\infty}(\tilde{\mathbbold}{\Gamma}/\tilde{\mathbbold}{\Lambda})
are given by the same von
Neumann algebraic morphism and the notion of finite orbits does not formally
involve any quantum group structure. The second statement follows now from the
identification of Hecke algebras as certain commutants with respect to these
actions.
∎

We can now characterize the Hecke pairs that give rise to non-discrete
Schlichting completions as follows.

Lemma 4.21
.

Let
(\mathbbold{\Gamma},\mathbbold{\Lambda})
be a Hecke pair,
(\tilde{\mathbbold}{\Gamma},\tilde{\mathbbold}{\Lambda})

its reduction and
(\mathbb{G},\mathbb{H})
its Schlichting completion. Then
\mathbb{G}
is
discrete if and only if
\tilde{\mathbbold}{\Lambda}
is finite.

Proof.

Recall that we have by construction the strictly dense (equivalently,
so-dense) inclusion
C_{0}(\mathbb{G})\subset\ell^{\infty}(\tilde{\mathbbold}{\Gamma})
. Multiplying
by
p{\Lambda}=p_{\tilde{\mathbbold}{\Lambda}}=p_{\mathbb{H}}
we see that
C(\mathbb{H})
is strictly
dense (equivalently, so-dense) in
\ell^{\infty}(\tilde{\mathbbold}{\Lambda})
, so that

\tilde{\mathbbold}{\Lambda}
is finite if and only if
\mathbb{H}
is finite.

Now if
\mathbb{H}
is finite,
[
KKS16
, Proposition 4.5]

implies that
\mathbb{G}
is discrete. On the other hand if
\mathbb{G}
is discrete,
\mathbb{H}
,
being an open (hence also closed by
[
KKS16
, Theorem
3.6]
) quantum subgroup of
\mathbb{G}
, must be discrete by

[
DKSS12
, Theorem 6.2]
. Finally a discrete and compact
quantum group must be finite.
∎

In particular if
(\mathbbold{\Gamma},\mathbbold{\Lambda})
is a reduced Hecke pair, the associated
Schlichting completion is discrete if and only if
\mathbbold{\Lambda}
is finite. This
shows, with the help of Lemma

quantum Hecke pairs discussed in the previous section lead to non-discrete
Schlichting completions.

.

The Schlichting completions associated with the HNN Hecke pairs of

groups, with non trivial modular group as soon as

, since they

\psi b|_{c_{c}(\mathbb{G}/\mathbb{H})}
for
b\in c_{c}(\mathbb{G}/\mathbb{H}))
,
where
\psi
is the right Haar weight of
\mathbb{G}
.

Proposition 4.23
.

The algebra
\mathcal{A}(\mathbb{G}\curvearrowright\mathbb{G}/\mathbb{H})
defines
an algebraic quantum group (with the structure inherited from
\mathcal{O}_{c}(\mathbb{G})
). Moreover

p_{\mathbb{H}}\in\mathcal{A}(\mathbb{G}\curvearrowright\mathbb{G}/\mathbb{H})
and
c_{c}(\mathbb{G}/\mathbb{H})\subset{\mathcal{A}(\mathbb{G}\curvearrowright\mathbb{G}/\mathbb{H})}
.

Proof.

We will use the map
T:\mathcal{O}_{c}(\mathbb{G})\to c_{c}(\mathbb{G}/\mathbb{H})
given by the formula

T(a)=(\mathord{\operatorname{id}}\otimes h_{\mathbb{H}})(\Delta(a)(1\otimes p_{\mathbb{H}})),\qquad a\in\mathcal{O}_{c}(\mathbb{G}).

Note that this map extends to a
C^{*}
-algebraic conditional expectation
(preserving the right invariant Haar weight) from
C_{0}(\mathbb{G})
onto

c_{0}(\mathbb{G}/\mathbb{H})
, with the property
T(\mathcal{O}_{c}(\mathbb{G}))=c_{c}(\mathbb{G}/\mathbb{H})
. This,
together with
[
VD98
, Remarks, p. 342]
shows the following
facts:

\displaystyle c_{c}(\mathbb{G}/\mathbb{H})^{\vee}

\displaystyle=\{\omega|_{c_{c}(\mathbb{G}/\mathbb{H})}\mid\omega\in\widehat{\mathcal{O}_{c}(\mathbb{G})}\},\quad\text{and}

\displaystyle c_{c}(\mathbb{G}/\mathbb{H})^{\vee}

\displaystyle=\{b\psi|_{c_{c}(\mathbb{G}/\mathbb{H})}\mid b\in c_{c}(\mathbb{G}/\mathbb{H})\}=\{b\varphi|_{c_{c}(\mathbb{G}/\mathbb{H})}\mid b\in c_{c}(\mathbb{G}/\mathbb{H})\}

\displaystyle=\{\varphi b|_{c_{c}(\mathbb{G}/\mathbb{H})}\mid b\in c_{c}(\mathbb{G}/\mathbb{H})\},

where
\varphi
is the left-invariant weight.

Using the properties of
T
and the fact that
\mathcal{O}_{c}(\mathbb{G})
is a multiplier
Hopf algebra one can show the following fact:

\mathcal{O}_{c}(\mathbb{G})\odot c_{c}(\mathbb{G}/\mathbb{H})=\Delta(c_{c}(\mathbb{G}/\mathbb{H}))(\mathcal{O}_{c}(\mathbb{G})\otimes 1)
. This implies (via the arguments of
[
VD98
, Proposition
4.2]
) that convolving a functional

\omega\in\widehat{\mathcal{O}_{c}(\mathbb{G})}
and a functional

\mu\in c_{c}(\mathbb{G}/\mathbb{H})^{\vee}
yields

\omega\star\mu\in c_{c}(\mathbb{G}/\mathbb{H})^{\vee}
.

We need to show that for every
a,b\in\mathcal{A}(\mathbb{G}\curvearrowright\mathbb{G}/\mathbb{H})
we have (for
example)

\Delta(a)(b\otimes 1)\in\mathcal{A}(\mathbb{G}\curvearrowright\mathbb{G}/\mathbb{H})\otimes\mathcal{A}(\mathbb{G}\curvearrowright\mathbb{G}/\mathbb{H})
. To this
end it suffices to prove that for all
\omega\in\widehat{\mathcal{O}_{c}(\mathbb{G})},

a\in c_{c}(\mathbb{G}/\mathbb{H})
and
\mu\in c_{c}(\mathbb{G}/\mathbb{H})^{\vee}
the elements

(\omega\otimes\mathord{\operatorname{id}})\big(\Delta((\mathord{\operatorname{id}}\otimes\mu)(\Delta(a)))(b\otimes 1)\big)
and

(\mathord{\operatorname{id}}\otimes\omega)\big(\Delta((\mathord{\operatorname{id}}\otimes\mu)(\Delta(a))(b\otimes 1)\big)
belong to
\mathcal{A}(\mathbb{G}\curvearrowright\mathbb{G}/\mathbb{H})
;
and the latter amount to noting that
c_{c}(\mathbb{G}/\mathbb{H})
is right-invariant (for
the first expression) and exploiting the convolution statement of the previous
paragraph (for the second expression).

Furthermore,
\mathcal{A}(\mathbb{G}\curvearrowright\mathbb{G}/\mathbb{H})
is
S
-invariant. This is an easy consequence
of the strong invariance of the left Haar weight, which says that for all

a,b\in\mathcal{O}_{c}(\mathbb{G})
we have

S\big((\mathord{\operatorname{id}}\otimes\phi)(\Delta(a^{*})(1\otimes b)\big)=(\mathord{\operatorname{id}}\otimes\phi)((1\otimes a^{*})(\Delta(b)),

combined with the statements in the beginning of the proof. This suffices to
complete the proof that
\mathcal{A}(\mathbb{G}\curvearrowright\mathbb{G}/\mathbb{H})
is a multiplier Hopf
*
-algebra,
and in fact an algebraic quantum group, as we can just use the invariant
weights of
\mathcal{O}_{c}(\mathbb{G})
.

Then it suffices to show that as we have
\epsilon(p_{\mathbb{H}})=1
we also have

\epsilon=\epsilon p_{\mathbb{H}}
, so that
\epsilon|_{c_{c}(\mathbb{G}/\mathbb{H})}
is finitely
supported. This implies that
c_{c}(\mathbb{G}/\mathbb{H})\subset\mathcal{A}(\mathbb{G}\curvearrowright\mathbb{G}/\mathbb{H})
.
∎

Proposition 4.26
.

Let
(\mathbbold{\Gamma},\mathbbold{\Lambda})
be a Hecke pair and
(\mathbb{G},\mathbb{H})
its Schlichting
completion, with the canonical embedding

\iota:\mathcal{O}_{c}(\mathbb{G})\to\ell^{\infty}(\mathbbold{\Gamma})
defining the morphism from

\mathbbold{\Gamma}
to
\mathbb{G}
. Then for any other Schlichting pair
(\mathbb{G}^{\prime},\mathbb{H}^{\prime})
and any
morphism from
\mathbbold{\Gamma}
to
\mathbb{G}^{\prime}
mapping
\mathbbold{\Lambda}
to
\mathbb{H}^{\prime}
and given by
an injective map
\iota^{\prime}:\mathcal{O}_{c}(\mathbb{G}^{\prime})\to\ell^{\infty}(\mathbbold{\Gamma})
, there
exists a unique morphism from
\mathbb{G}
to
\mathbb{G}^{\prime}
, described by a map

\sigma:\mathcal{O}_{c}(\mathbb{G}^{\prime})\to\mathcal{O}_{c}(\mathbb{G})
, such that

\iota\circ\sigma=\iota^{\prime}
. If in addition we assume that

\iota^{\prime}(p_{\mathbb{H}^{\prime}})=p{\Lambda}
then the morphism from
\mathbb{G}
to
\mathbb{G}^{\prime}
is an
isomorphism.

Proof.

A moment of thought shows that it suffices to show that

\iota^{\prime}(\mathcal{O}_{c}(\mathbb{G}^{\prime}))\subset\iota(\mathcal{O}_{c}(\mathbb{G}))
. Ignoring the injective
embedding maps, and using the fact that both
(\mathbb{G},\mathbb{H})

(\mathbb{G}^{\prime},\mathbb{H}^{\prime})

c_{c}(\mathbb{G}^{\prime}/\mathbb{H}^{\prime})\subset c_{c}(\mathbb{G}/\mathbb{H})
. But this follows as we have

P\leq P^{\prime}
(again viewing both as projections in
\ell^{\infty}(\mathbbold{\Gamma}))
.
The second part follows similarly.

On the Existence of 4-regular Matchstick Graphs
Mike Winkler, Peter Dinkelacker, Stefan Vogel

Abstract
A matchstick graph is a planar unit-distance graph. That is a graph drawn with straight edges in the plane such that the edges have unit length, and non-adjacent edges do not intersect. We call a matchstick graph 4-regular if every vertex has only degree 4. Examples of 4-regular matchstick graphs with less than 63 vertices are only known for 52, 54, 57, and 60 vertices. It is shown that for all number of vertices ≥ 63 \geq 63 at least one example of a 4-regular matchstick graph exists.

Introduction

A matchstick graph is a planar unit-distance graph. That is a graph drawn with straight edges in the plane such that the edges have unit length, and non-adjacent edges do not intersect. We call a matchstick graph 4-regular if every vertex has only degree 4, and

We also show the four known examples with less than 63 vertices.

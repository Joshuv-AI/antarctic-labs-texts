Deformation quantization with separation of variables of G 2 , 4 ​ ( ℂ ) G_{2,4}\left(\mathbb{C}\right)
Taika Okuda, Akifumi Sako

Abstract
We construct a deformation quantization with separation of variables of the Grassmannian G 2 , 4 ​ ( ℂ ) G_{2,4}\left(\mathbb{C}\right) . A star product on G 2 , 4 ​ ( ℂ ) G_{2,4}\left(\mathbb{C}\right) can be explicitly determined as the solution of the recurrence relations for G 2 , 4 ​ ( ℂ ) G_{2,4}\left(\mathbb{C}\right) given by Hara and one of the authors (A. S.). To provide the solution to the recurrence relations, it is necessary to solve a system of linear equations in each order. However, to give a concrete expression of the general term is not simple because the variables increase with the order of the differentiation of the star product. For this reason, there has been no formula to express the general term of the recurrence relations. In this paper, we overcome this problem by transforming the recurrence relations into simpler ones. We solve the recurrence relations using creation and annihilation operators on a Fock space. From this solution, we obtain an explicit formula of a star product with separation of variables on G 2 , 4 ​ ( ℂ ) G_{2,4}\left(\mathbb{C}\right) .

In this section, we review the construction method of a deformation quantization with separation of variables for locally symmetric Kähler manifolds proposed by Hara and A. S.
[
20
,
21
]
. A Kähler manifold
M
is “locally symmetric” if its Riemann curvature tensor satisfies

\nabla_{\partial_{E}}{R_{ABC}}^{D}=0

for

A,B,C,D,E\in\left\{1,\cdots,N,\overline{1},\cdots,\overline{N}\right\}
.
In this paper, we define the Riemann curvature tensor

R^{\nabla}:\Gamma(TM)\times\Gamma(TM)\times\Gamma(TM)\to\Gamma(TM)

on
M
for vector fields

X,Y\in\Gamma(TM)

by

\displaystyle R^{\nabla}\left(X,Y\right):=\nabla_{X}\nabla_{Y}-\nabla_{Y}\nabla_{X}-\nabla_{\left[X,Y\right]}.

We denote the component of
R^{\nabla}
by

\displaystyle R^{\nabla}\left(\partial_{A},\partial_{B}\right)\partial_{C}:={R_{ABC}}^{D}\partial_{D}

(7)

as a local expression. Here, we note that the notation
{R_{ABC}}^{D}
used in this paper can be expressed by the relation

\displaystyle{R_{ABC}}^{D}={\mathfrak{R}^{D}}_{CAB}

using the notation

\displaystyle R^{\nabla}\left(\partial_{A},\partial_{B}\right)\partial_{C}:={\mathfrak{R}^{D}}_{CAB}\partial_{D}

(8)

by Kobayashi-Nomizu
[
26
]
. Note that

{R_{ABC}}^{D}=-{\mathfrak{R}_{ABC}}^{D}
.

Let
M
be an
N
-dimensional locally symmetric Kähler manifold, and
(U,\phi)
be a chart of
M
. We now assume the following form for a star product with separation of variables on
M
for any

f,g\in C^{\infty}\left(U\right)
:

\displaystyle f\ast g=L_{f}g:=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{\overrightarrow{\alpha_{n}},\overrightarrow{\beta_{n}^{\ast}}}T_{\overrightarrow{\alpha_{n}},\overrightarrow{\beta_{n}^{\ast}}}^{n}\left(D^{\overrightarrow{\alpha_{n}}}f\right)\left(D^{\overrightarrow{\beta_{n}^{\ast}}}g\right).

(9)

Here, recalling that

D^{\overrightarrow{\alpha_{n}}},D^{\overrightarrow{\beta_{n}^{\ast}}}

are differential operators defined by

\displaystyle D^{\overrightarrow{\alpha_{n}}}:=D^{\alpha_{1}^{n}}\cdots D^{\alpha_{N}^{n}},\quad D^{\alpha_{k}^{n}}:=\left(D^{k}\right)^{\alpha_{k}^{n}},

\displaystyle D^{\overrightarrow{\beta_{n}^{\ast}}}:=\overline{D^{\overrightarrow{\beta_{n}}}}=\overline{D^{\beta_{1}^{n}}}\cdots\overline{D^{\beta_{N}^{n}}},\quad\overline{D^{\beta_{k}^{n}}}:=\left(D^{\overline{k}}\right)^{\beta_{k}^{n}},

\displaystyle\overrightarrow{\alpha_{n}},\overrightarrow{\beta_{n}}\in\left\{\left(\gamma_{1}^{n},\cdots,\gamma_{N}^{n}\right)\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}\middle|\sum_{k=1}^{N}\gamma_{k}^{n}=n\right\},

respectively.
Note that
\hbar
is included in the coefficient
T_{\overrightarrow{\alpha_{n}},\overrightarrow{\beta_{n}^{\ast}}}^{n}
for
n\geq 1
. The coefficients

T_{\overrightarrow{\alpha_{n}},\overrightarrow{\beta_{n}^{\ast}}}^{n}

can be assumed to be covariantly constants by using the fact that
M
is a locally symmetric space. Here, we define

T_{\overrightarrow{\alpha_{n}},\overrightarrow{\beta_{n}^{\ast}}}^{n}:=0

when

\overrightarrow{\alpha_{n}}\notin\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{N}

or

\overrightarrow{\beta_{n}}\notin\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{N}
.
For
n=0,1
, it is known that the coefficient

T_{\overrightarrow{\alpha_{n}},\overrightarrow{\beta_{n}^{\ast}}}^{n}

are explicitly given as follows.

Proposition 2.1

(
[
20
,
21
]
)
.

For a star product with separation of variables
\ast
on
U,

\displaystyle T_{\overrightarrow{0},\overrightarrow{0^{\ast}}}^{0}=1,\qquad T_{\overrightarrow{e_{i}},\overrightarrow{e_{j}^{\ast}}}^{1}=\hbar g_{i\overline{j}},

(10)

where

\overrightarrow{e_{i}}=\left(\delta_{1i},\cdots,\delta_{Ni}\right).

Proposition
2.1
states that the coefficients for

are completely determined for any
N
-dimensional locally symmetric Kähler manifold.
In particular, the fact “
T_{\overrightarrow{0},\overrightarrow{0^{\ast}}}^{0}=1
” plays the role of the initial condition for the recurrence relations which gives the star product to be shown later.
The following theorem proposed by
[

,
21
]
describes the existence of a star product with separation of variables such that it is given by (

Theorem 2.2

(
[

,

]
)
.

Let

be an
N
-dimensional locally symmetric Kähler manifold. For

,
there exists locally a star product with separation of variables
\ast
such that

\displaystyle f\ast g=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{\overrightarrow{\alpha_{n}},\overrightarrow{\beta_{n}^{\ast}}}T_{\overrightarrow{\alpha_{n}},\overrightarrow{\beta_{n}^{\ast}}}^{n}\left(D^{\overrightarrow{\alpha_{n}}}f\right)\left(D^{\overrightarrow{\beta_{n}^{\ast}}}g\right),

(11)

where the coefficient

\displaystyle\sum_{d=1}^{N}\hbar g_{\overline{i}d}T_{\overrightarrow{\alpha_{n}}-\overrightarrow{e_{d}},\overrightarrow{\beta_{n}^{\ast}}-\overrightarrow{e_{i}^{\ast}}}^{n-1}

\displaystyle=\beta_{i}^{n}T_{\overrightarrow{\alpha_{n}},\overrightarrow{\beta_{n}^{\ast}}}^{n}+\sum_{k=1}^{N}\sum_{\rho=1}^{N}\hbar\binom{\beta_{k}^{n}-\delta_{k\rho}-\delta_{ik}+2}{2}R_{\overline{\rho}\ \ \ \,\overline{i}}^{\ \,\overline{k}\,\overline{k}}T_{\overrightarrow{\alpha_{n}},\overrightarrow{\beta_{n}^{\ast}}-\overrightarrow{e_{\rho}^{\ast}}+2\overrightarrow{e_{k}^{\ast}}-\overrightarrow{e_{i}^{\ast}}}^{n}

\displaystyle\mbox{}\quad+\sum_{k=1}^{N-1}\sum_{l=1}^{N-k}\sum_{\rho=1}^{N}\hbar\left(\beta_{k}^{n}-\delta_{k\rho}-\delta_{ik}+1\right)\left(\beta_{k+l}^{n}-\delta_{k+l,\rho}-\delta_{i,k+l}+1\right)R_{\overline{\rho}\hskip 8.19447pt\ \ \ \,\overline{i}}^{\ \,\overline{k+l}\,\overline{k}}T_{\overrightarrow{\alpha_{n}},\overrightarrow{\beta_{n}^{\ast}}-\overrightarrow{e_{\rho}^{\ast}}+\overrightarrow{e_{k}^{\ast}}+\overrightarrow{e_{k+l}^{\ast}}-\overrightarrow{e_{i}^{\ast}}}^{n}.

Here

is a binomial coefficient.

Smoothed Online Learning for Prediction in Piecewise Affine Systems
Adam Block, Max Simchowitz, Russ Tedrake

Abstract
The problem of piecewise affine (PWA) regression and planning is of foundational importance to the study of online learning, control, and robotics, where it provides a theoretically and empirically tractable setting to study systems undergoing sharp changes in the dynamics. Unfortunately, due to the discontinuities that arise when crossing into different “pieces,” learning in general sequential settings is impossible and practical algorithms are forced to resort to heuristic approaches. This paper builds on the recently developed smoothed online learning framework and provides the first algorithms for prediction and simulation in PWA systems whose regret is polynomial in all relevant problem parameters under a weak smoothness assumption; moreover, our algorithms are efficient in the number of calls to an optimization oracle. We further apply our results to the problems of one-step prediction and multi-step simulation regret in piecewise affine dynamical systems, where the learner is tasked with simulating trajectories and regret is measured in terms of the Wasserstein distance between simulated and true data. Along the way, we develop several technical tools of more general interest.



1
Introduction

A central problem in the fields of online learning, control, and robotics is how to efficiently plan through piecewise-affine (PWA) systems. Such systems are described by a finite set of disjoint regions in state-space, within which the dynamics are affine. In this paper, we consider the related problem of learning to make predictions in PWA systems when the dynamics are unknown. While recent years have seen considerable progress around our understanding of linear control systems, the vast majority of dynamics encountered in practical settings involve nonlinearities. Learning and predicting in nonlinear systems is typically far more challenging because, unlike their linear counterparts, the dynamics in a local region of a nonlinear system do not determine its global dynamics.

PWA systems allow for discontinuities across the separate regions (“pieces”), and are thus a simplified way of modeling rich phenomena that arise in numerous robotic manipulation and locomotion tasks
[
Mason 2001
,
Van Der Schaft and Schumacher 2007
,
Marcucci and Tedrake 2019
]
, such as modeling dynamics involving contact. In addition, deep neural networks with ReLU activation
[
Goodfellow et al. 2016
]
are PWA systems, providing further motiviation for their study. Already, there is a computational challenge simply in optimizing an objective over these systems, which is the subject of much of the previous literature. Here, we take a statistical perspective, assuming that we have access to effective heuristic algorithms for this optimization task, as is common in online learning
[
Hazan and Koren 2016
,
Block et al. 2022
,
Haghtalab et al. 2022
]
. Uniformly accurate learning of the dynamics across all pieces is typically impossible because some regions of space require onerous exploration to locate; thus, we instead consider an iterative prediction setting, where at each time
t
, the learner predicts the subsequent system state. From this, we extend to prediction of entire trajectories: over episodes
t=1,\dots,T
, a learner suggests a policy
\pi_{t}
, and the learner attempts to learn the dynamics so as to minimize prediction error along the trajectory
\pi_{\tau}
induces. This is motivated by iterative learning control, where our notion of error is an upper bound on the discrepancy between the planner’s estimate of policy performance and the ground truth.

Our iterative formulation of the learning problem is equivalent to the regret metric favored by the online learning community, and which has seen application to online/adaptive control and filtering problems in recent work. Critically, regret does not require uniform identification of system parameters, which is typically impossible. The key pathology is that policies can oscillate across the boundary of two regions, accruing significant prediction error due to sharp discontinuities of the dynamics between said regions, a problem well-known to the online learning community
[
Littlestone 1988
]
. Moreover, this pathology is not merely a theoretical artifact: discontinuities and non-smoothness pose significant challenges to planning and modeling contact-rich dynamics such as those encountered in robotic manipulation
[
Suh et al. 2022a
,
Suh et al. 2022b
,
Posa et al. 2014
]
.

Our solution takes inspiration from, and establishes a connection between, two rather different fields. Recent work in the robotics and control communities has relied on
randomized smoothing
to improve planning across discontinuous and non-smooth dynamics
[
Suh et al. 2022a
,
Suh et al. 2022b
,
Lidec et al. 2022
,
Pang et al. 2022
]
. Additionally, the online learning community has studied
smoothed online learning

[
Rakhlin et al. 2011
,
Haghtalab et al. 2021
,
Haghtalab et al. 2022
,
Block et al. 2022
,
Block and Simchowitz 2022
,
Block and Polyanskiy 2023
]
, which circumvents the threshold-effect pathologies described above.

We show that, if the dynamics and the control inputs are subject to randomized smoothing, low regret becomes achievable. We note that the randomized smoothing approach is in some sense canonical in mitigating the aforementioned pathology of policies that oscillate across the boundaries of two regions; smoothing prohibits this pathology by ensuring that the system is generally far from these boundaries. More importantly, our proposed no-regret algorithm is
efficient
in terms of the number of calls to an optimization oracle, a popular notion of computational efficiency in the online learning community
[
Hazan and Koren 2016
,
Kalai and Vempala 2005
,
Block et al. 2022
,
Haghtalab et al. 2022
]
. In our setting, the optimization oracle required finds the
best-fit
PWA system to a batch of given data. Though this problem is intractable
[
Amaldi and Mattavelli 2002
]
, there is a rich literature of popular heuristics
[
Garulli et al. 2012
,
Paoletti et al. 2007
,
Ferrari-Trecate et al. 2003
]
. Unlike those works, we examine the
statistical challenge
of generalization to novel data given such an oracle. We remark that in many practical cases, our smoothness assumption comes for free, in the sense that the Gaussian noise used to smooth gradients in
Suh et al. 2022a
is already sufficient for our results to hold.

We stress that we consider
online learning
of PWA systems, so as to accomodate for distribution shift that arises as a controlling agent interacts with a dynamical system and updates its own behavior. In the
PAC learning
setting, it is possible to derive on-distribution generalization bounds by bounding the pseudo-dimension of piecewise-affine functions
[
Balcan et al. 2021
]
. Moreover, our class of functions do not satisfy the “dispersion condition”
[
Balcan et al. 2018
]
, which instead would render the class online-learnable without smoothing; further still, it remains an open problem as to how to develop
oracle-efficient
online learning algorithms based on dispersion.

Contributions.

A formal description of our setting is deferred to
Section

2
. Informally, we consider the problem of prediction in a PWA system over a horizon of
T
steps, where the regions are determined by intersections of halfspaces; we obtain prediction in PWA dynamical systems as a special case. We aim to achieve sublinear-in-
T
excess square-loss error (
regret
) of
O\left(T^{1-\alpha}\right)
with respect to the optimal predictor which knows the system dynamics, where
\alpha>0
is constant and the prefactor on the regret is at most polynomial in all of the problem parameters. Our result is derived from a general guarantee for online piecewise affine regression, which subsumes the online PWA identification setting. We show that, when the dynamics and the control inputs are subject to randomized smoothing satisfying the
\sigma_{\mathrm{dir}}
-directional smoothness condition introduced by
Block and Simchowitz 2022
, our regret bound is polynomial in
\sigma_{\mathrm{dir}}
, dimension, and other natural problem parameters. While the exact dependence on horizon may be far from optimal, we emphasize that our algorithm is the first to achieve regret even polynomial in all parameters in the difficult setting of PWA systems. Moreover, our work provides the first regret bound with polynomial dependence in dimension for oracle-efficient smoothed online learning in the sense of
Hazan and Koren 2016
of a noncontinuous function class without a realizability assumption and allowing for process noise.

As a further application, we adapt our algorithm to make predictions, at each time
t
, of the trajectory comprising the next
H
steps in the evolution of the dynamical system. We bound the
simulation regret
, the cumulative error in Wasserstein distance between the distribution of our proposed trajectory and that of the actual trajectory. Assuming a Lyapunov condition, we demonstrate that our modified algorithm achieves simulation regret
O\left(\mathsf{poly}(H)\cdot T^{1-\alpha}\right)
, which allows for efficient simulation.

Key challenges.

As noted above, vanishing regret in our setting is impossible without directional smoothness due to discontinuity in the regressors between different regions. The key challenge is to leverage smoothness to mitigate the effect of these discontinuties. Note that, if the learner were to observe the region in addition to the state, regression would be easy by decomposing the problem into
K
separate affine regression instances; the difficulty is in minimicking this approach
without explicit labels indicating which points are in which regions.
We remark that were we to care only about low regret without regard to efficiency, a simple discretization scheme of size exponential in dimension coupled with a standard learning from experts algorithm
[
Cesa-Bianchi and Lugosi 2006
]
can achieve low regret; realizing an oracle-efficient algorithm is significantly more challenging.

We adopt a natural, epoch-based approach by calling the optimization oracle between epochs, estimating the underlying system, and using this estimate to assign points to regions in the subsequent epoch. This introduces three new challenges, each of which requires substantial technical effort to overcome. First, we need to control the performance of our oracle on the regression task, which we term
parameter recovery
below. Second, we must enforce consistency between regions learned in different epochs, so that we can predict the correct regions for subsequent covariates; doing this necessitates an intricate analysis of how the estimated parameters evolve between epochs. Finally, we need to modify the output of our oracle to enforce low regret with respect to the classification problem of correctly predicting which mode we are in on as yet unseen data.

Our techniques.

Our algorithm proceeds in epochs, at the end of each of which we compute a best-fitting piecewise affine approximation to available data by calling the Empirical Risk Minimization (ERM) oracle. The analysis of this best-fit shows that we recover the parameters and decision boundaries of the associated regions (“pieces”) frequently visited during that epoch. By the pigeonhole principle, this ultimately covers all regions visited a non-neglible number of times
t
over the horizon
T
. Here, we leverage a careful analysis based on two modern martingale-least squares techniques: the self-normalized concentration bound
[
Abbasi-Yadkori et al. 2011
]
for establishing recovery in a norm induced by within-region empirical covariance matrices, and the block-martingale small-ball method
[
Simchowitz et al. 2018
]
to lower-bound the smallest eigenvalues of empirical covariances with high probability. In addition, we introduce and bound a new complexity parameter, the
disagreement cover
, in order to make our statements uniform over the set of possible decision boundaries.

After the estimation phase, each epoch uses this estimate to make predictions on new data as it arrives. The key challenge is classifying the region to which each newly observed data point belongs. Without smoothing, this can be particularly treacherous, as points on or near to the boundary of regions can easily be misclassified and the correct predictions are discontinous. To leverage smoothing, we propose a reduction to online convex optimization on the hinge loss with lazy updates and show, through a careful decomposition of the loss, that our classification error can be controlled in terms of the assumed directional smoothness. Combining this bound on the classification error with the parameter recovery described in the preceding paragraph yields our sublinear regret guarantee.

We instantiate one-step prediction as a special case of the more general online linear regression problem, showing that the approach described above immediately applies to the first problem under consideration. Finally, in order to simulate trajectories, we use the learned parameters in the epoch to predict the next
H
steps of the evolution. We then use the fact that our main algorithm recovers the parameters defining the PWA system to show that simulation regret is bounded, which solves the second main problem and allows for simulation in such systems.

Setting

In this section, we formally describe the problem setting of online PWA regression. We suppose there is a ground truth classifier
g_{\star}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to[K]
and matrices
\left\{\mathbf{\Theta}^{\star}_{i}\mid i\in[K]\text{ and }\mathbf{\Theta}^{\star}_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times(d+1)}\right\}
such that

\displaystyle\mathbf{y}_{t}=\mathbf{\Theta}^{\star}_{i_{t}}\cdot\bar{\mathbf{x}}_{t}+\mathbf{e}_{t}+\bm{\delta}_{t},\quad i_{t}=g^{\star}(\bar{\mathbf{x}}_{t}),\quad\bar{\mathbf{x}}_{t}=[\mathbf{x}_{t}^{\top}|1]^{\top},\quad\left|\left|\bm{\delta}_{t}\right|\right|\leq\varepsilon_{\mathrm{crp}},

(2.1)

where
\mathbf{x}_{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
are covariates,
\mathbf{y}_{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
are responses,
\mathbf{e}_{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
are zero-mean noise vectors in
\mathbb{R}^{m}
,
\bm{\delta}_{t}
are (small) non-stochastic corruptions with norm at most
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{crp}}\ll 1
, and
i_{t}\in[K]
are the regression modes, which depend on the covariates
\mathbf{x}_{t}
. The extension to
\bar{\mathbf{x}}_{t}
accomodates affine regressors. We suppose that the learner has access to pairs
(\mathbf{x}_{t},\mathbf{y}_{t})
but, critically, does not know the regression modes
i_{t}
. We do however suppose that
g_{\star}
is an
affine classifer
; that is

g^{\star}\in\mathcal{G}=\left\{\mathbf{x}\mapsto\argmax_{1\leq i\leq K}\left\langle\mathbf{w}_{i},\mathbf{x}\right\rangle+b_{i}|\mathbf{w}_{i}\in S^{d-1},b_{i}\in[-C,C]\right\},

(2.2)

where
S^{d-1}
is the unit sphere.

It can be shown that many natural physical systems are indeed modeled as PWA with affine boundaries
[
Marcucci and Tedrake 2019
]
, and the closed loop dynamics for model-predictive control (MPC) in these cases is also a PWA system
[
Borrelli et al. 2017
]
. We will assume throughout access to an empirical risk minimization oracle,
ErmOracle
, which satisfies the following guarantee:

Assumption 1

(
ErmOracle
guarantee)
.

Given data
(\bar{\mathbf{x}}_{1:s},\mathbf{y}_{1:s})
,
ErmOracle
returns
\left\{\widehat{\mathbf{\Theta}}_{i}|i\in[K]\right\},\widehat{g}
satisfying

\sum_{t=1}^{s}\left|\left|\widehat{\mathbf{\Theta}}_{\widehat{g}(\mathbf{x}_{t})}\bar{\mathbf{x}}_{t}-\mathbf{y}_{t}\right|\right|^{2}-\inf_{g\in\mathcal{G},\,\left\{\mathbf{\Theta}_{i}|i\in[K]\right\}}\sum_{t=1}^{s}\left|\left|\mathbf{\Theta}_{g(\mathbf{x}_{t})}\bar{\mathbf{x}}_{t}-\mathbf{y}_{t}\right|\right|^{2}\leq\varepsilon_{\mathrm{orac}}.

(2.3)

While such an oracle is not computationally efficient in the worst case
[
Amaldi and Mattavelli 2002
]
, there are many popular heuristics used in practical situations
[
Ferrari-Trecate et al. 2003
,
Garulli et al. 2012
]
. The learner aims to construct an algorithm, efficient with respect to the above oracle, that at each time step
t
produces
\mathbf{\widehat{y}}_{t}
such that

\reg_{T}:=\sum_{t=1}^{T}\left\|\mathbf{\widehat{y}}_{t}-\mathbf{\Theta}^{\star}_{g_{\star}(\bar{\mathbf{x}}_{t})}\bar{\mathbf{x}}_{t}\right\|^{2}=o(T).

(2.4)

This notion of regret is standard in the online learning community and, as we shall see, immediately leads to bounds on prediction error.

Directional Smoothness.

As previously mentioned, without further restriction, sublinear regret is infeasible, as formalized in the following proposition:

Proposition 1
.

In the above setting, there exists an adversary with
m=d=1
, K=2, that chooses
\mathbf{\Theta}^{\star}
and
g_{\star}
, as well as
\mathbf{x}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{x}_{T}
such that any learner experiences

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\reg_{T}\right]\geq\frac{T}{2}.

Proposition

1
follows from a construction of a system where there is a discontinuity across a linear boundary, across which the states
\mathbf{x}_{t}
oscillate. The bound is then a consequence of the classical fact that online classification with linear thresholds suffers linear regret; see
Section

B.1
for a formal proof, included for the sake of completeness. Crucially, a discontinuity in the dynamics necessitates an
\Omega(1)
contribution to regret each time the decision boundary is incorrectly determined. To avoid this pathology, we suppose that the contexts are smooth; however, because standard smoothness
[
Haghtalab et al. 2020
]
leads to regret that is exponential in dimension
[
Block and Simchowitz 2022
]
, we instead consider a related condition, ‘‘directional smoothness.’’

3.1
Regret for One-Step Prediction in PWA Systems

A direct application of our main result is online prediction in piecewise affine systems. Consider the following dynamical system with state
\mathbf{z}_{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{z}}

(3.2)

Substitute
\mathbf{\Theta}^{\star}_{i}:=[\mathbf{A}_{i}\mid\mathbf{B}_{i}\mid\mathbf{m}_{i}]

\bar{\mathbf{x}}_{t}=[\mathbf{x}_{t}\mid 1]

The primary challenge is that directional smoothness, as opposed to independence, is insufficiently strong an assumption to guarantee that a learned classifier
\widehat{g}_{\tau}

There are two challenges in ensuring good performance of our online classifier. First, the dynamics described in (

\displaystyle\pi_{\tau}(i)=\argmin_{1\leq j\leq K}\|\widehat{\mathbf{\Theta}}_{\tau,i}-\mathbf{\Theta}^{\star}_{j}\|_{\mathrm{F}}.

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{t}^{\mathrm{mode}}\subset\left\{\pi_{\tau+1}(\widetilde{g}_{\tau}(\bar{\mathbf{x}}_{t}))\neq g_{\star}(\bar{\mathbf{x}}_{t})\right\}\cup\left\{\pi_{\tau}(\widetilde{g}_{\tau}(\bar{\mathbf{x}}_{t}))\neq\pi_{\tau+1}(\widetilde{g}_{\tau}(\bar{\mathbf{x}}_{t}))\right\}.

\displaystyle\left\{\pi_{\tau+1}(\widetilde{g}_{\tau}(\bar{\mathbf{x}}_{t}))\neq g_{\star}(\bar{\mathbf{x}}_{t})\right\}\subset\left\{\pi_{\tau+1}(\widehat{g}_{\tau+1}(\bar{\mathbf{x}}_{t}))\neq g_{\star}(\bar{\mathbf{x}}_{t})\right\}\cup\left\{\widetilde{g}_{\tau}(\bar{\mathbf{x}}_{t})\neq\widehat{g}_{\tau+1}(\bar{\mathbf{x}}_{t})\right\}.

5
Discussion

We have given an efficient online learning algorithm for prediction in piecewise affine (PWA) systems. Our results are the first foray into the study of such systems, and a number of outstanding questions remain:

•

Does directional smoothness facilliate low regret for planning and control, in addition to for simulation and prediction?

•

If the systems are piecewise-affine but forced to be continuous on their boundaries, is there an oracle efficient algorithm which suffers low regret without assuming directional smoothness? Note that exponential weights on a suitable discretization suffices for an information-theoretic bound.

•

Can learning in piecewise affine systems be obtained under
partial observation
of the system state?

•

What guarantees are possible if the underlying system can be approximated by a PWA system with many pieces, even if it is not exactly described by one? Note that this may require algorithms which overcome the separation condition,
5
.

•

What other class of systems, like Linear Complementarity systems, studied in
[
Jin et al. 2022
]
, are amenable to oracle-efficient online learning?

We hope that our work initiates investigation of the above as the field continues to expand its understanding beyond the linear regime.

C.1
Directional Smoothness of Gaussians and Uniform Distributions

Lemma C.1
.

Let
\mathbf{w}
be distributed as a centred Gaussian with covariance
\sigma^{2}\mathbf{I}
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Then for any
\mathbf{z}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, if
\mathbf{w}
is independent of
\mathbf{z}
, it holds that
\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{z}+\mathbf{w}
is
\sigma_{\mathrm{dir}}
-directionally smooth, with
\sigma_{\mathrm{dir}}=\sqrt{2\pi}\sigma
.

Proof.

Fix some
\mathbf{u}\in\mathcal{S}^{d-1}
and
c\in\mathbb{R}
. Then note that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\left|\left\langle\mathbf{x},\mathbf{u}\right\rangle-c\right|<\delta\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}\left(\left|\left\langle\mathbf{w},\mathbf{u}\right\rangle-\left(-\left\langle\mathbf{z},\mathbf{u}\right\rangle+c\right)\right|<\delta\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}\left(\left|\left\langle\mathbf{w},\mathbf{u}\right\rangle-c^{\prime}\right|<\delta\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\delta}{\sqrt{2\pi}\sigma},

where the last inequality follows by the fact that
\left\langle\mathbf{w},\mathbf{u}\right\rangle
is distributed as a centred univarate Gaussian with variance
\sigma^{2}
and the fact that such a distribution has density upper bounded by

. The result follows.

Lemma C.2
.

Let
\mathbf{w}
be uniform on a centred Euclidean ball of radius
\sigma
. Then for any
\mathbf{z}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, if
\mathbf{w}
is independent of
\mathbf{z}

\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{z}+\mathbf{w}

\sigma_{\mathrm{dir}}
-directionally smooth, with

Proof.

Let
\mathbf{v}
denote a point sampled uniformly from the unit Euclidean ball and note that
\mathbf{w}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}\sigma\mathbf{v}
. We then have for any
\mathbf{u}\in\mathcal{S}^{d-1}

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\left|\left\langle\mathbf{w},\mathbf{u}\right\rangle-c^{\prime}\right|<\delta\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}\left(\left|\left\langle\mathbf{v},\mathbf{u}\right\rangle-c\right|<\frac{\delta}{\sigma}\right).

Let

uniformly. Using the co-area formula

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\left|\left\langle\mathbf{v},\mathbf{u}\right\rangle-c\right|<\frac{\delta}{\sigma}\right)

\displaystyle=\int_{\phi^{-1}(A)}d\vol_{d}(\mathbf{v})

\displaystyle=\int_{\phi^{-1}(A)}\sqrt{\det\left(D\phi(\mathbf{v})D\phi(\mathbf{v})^{T}\right)}d\vol_{d}(\mathbf{v})

\displaystyle=\int_{A}\vol_{d-1}\left(\phi^{-1}(y)\right)dy

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sup_{y\in A}\vol_{d-1}\left(\phi^{-1}(y)\right)\right)\int_{c-\frac{\delta}{\sigma}}^{c+\frac{\delta}{\sigma}}dy

C.2
Directional Smoothness Equivalent to Lebesgue Density

Lemma C.3
.

Let
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a (Borel-measurable) random vector. Then,
\mathbf{x}
is
\sigma_{\mathrm{dir}}
-smooth if and only if, for any
\mathbf{w}\in\mathcal{S}^{d-1}
,
\langle\mathbf{w},\mathbf{x}\rangle
admits a density
p(\cdot)
with respect to the Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{R}
with
\esssup_{v\in\mathbb{R}}p(v)\leq 2/\sigma_{\mathrm{dir}}
.

Proof.

The “if” direction is immediate. For the converse, let
\mu(B)
denote the Lebesgue measure of a Borel set
B\subset\mathbb{R}
. We observe that
\sigma_{\mathrm{dir}}
-smoothness proves that for any interval
I=(a,b])\subset\mathbb{R}

\mathbb{P}[\langle\mathbf{w},\mathbf{x}\rangle\in I]\leq\sigma_{\mathrm{dir}}\mu(I)/2
(consider
c=(a+b)/2
and
\delta=|b-a|/2
). Since the Borel sigma-algebra is the generated by open intervals, this implies that for any Borel subset
B
of
\mathbb{R}
,
\mathbb{P}[\langle\mathbf{w},\mathbf{x}\rangle\in B]\leq 2\mu(B)/\sigma_{\mathrm{dir}}
, where
\mu(\cdot)
denotes the Lebesgue measure. Thus we see that the law of
\left\langle\mathbf{w},\mathbf{x}\right\rangle
is absolutely continuous with respect to the Lebesgue measure and, by definition of the Radon-Nikodym derivative, there exists some
p
such that for all Borel
B
, it holds that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\left\langle\mathbf{w},\mathbf{x}\right\rangle\in B\right)=\int_{B}p(a)da.

Now, let

\displaystyle B=\left\{a|p(a)>\frac{2}{\sigma_{\mathrm{dir}}}\right\}

and note that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\left\langle\mathbf{w},\mathbf{x}\right\rangle\in B\right)

\displaystyle=\int_{B}p(a)da>\frac{2}{\sigma_{\mathrm{dir}}}\cdot\mu(B).

Combining this with the fact that
\mathbb{P}\left(\left\langle\mathbf{w},\mathbf{x}\right\rangle\in B\right)\leq\frac{2\mu(B)}{\sigma_{\mathrm{dir}}}
, we see that
\mu(B)=0
and the result holds.
∎

-disagrement cover
if the following two properties hold:

1.

For every
g\in\mathcal{G}
, there exists some
i
such that
(g_{i},\mathscr{D}_{i})\in\mathscr{D}
and
\left\{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}|g_{i}(\mathbf{x})\neq g(\mathbf{x})\right\}\subset\mathscr{D}_{i}
.

2.

For all
i
and
t
, it holds that
\mathbb{P}\left(\mathbf{x}_{t}\in\mathscr{D}_{i}\mid\mathscr{F}_{t-1}\right)\leq\varepsilon

We will denote by
\textsc{DN}(\mathcal{G},\varepsilon)
(or
\textsc{DN}(\varepsilon)
when
\mathcal{G}
is clear from context) the minimal size of an
\varepsilon
-disagreement cover of
\mathcal{G}
.

We remark that a disagreement cover is stronger than the more classical notion of an
\varepsilon
-net because the sets of points where multiple different functions
g
disagree with a single element of the cover
g_{i}
has to be contained in a single set. With an
\varepsilon
-net, there is nothing stopping the existence of an
i
such that the set of points on which at least one
g
satisfying
\mathbb{P}(g_{i}\neq g)\leq\varepsilon
is the entire space. The reason that this uniformity is necessary is to provide the following bound:

Lemma D.1
.

Let
\mathscr{D}
be an
\varepsilon
-disagreement cover for
\mathcal{G}
. Then, with probability at least
1-\delta
, it holds that

\displaystyle\sup_{g\in\mathcal{G}}\min_{(g_{i},\mathscr{D}_{i})\in\mathscr{D}}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbb{I}\left[g(\mathbf{x}_{t})\neq g_{i}(\mathbf{x}_{t})\right]\leq 2T\varepsilon+6\log\left(\frac{\left|\mathscr{D}\right|}{\delta}\right).

Proof.

Note that by the definition of a disagreement cover, it holds that

\displaystyle\sup_{g\in\mathcal{G}}\min_{(g_{i},\mathscr{D}_{i})\in\mathscr{D}}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbb{I}\left[g(\mathbf{x}_{t})\neq g_{i}(\mathbf{x}_{t})\right]\leq\max_{(g_{i},\mathscr{D}_{i})\in\mathscr{D}}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbb{I}\left[\mathbf{x}_{t}\in\mathscr{D}_{i}\right].

Note that for any fixed
i
, it holds that
\mathbb{P}\left(\mathbf{x}_{t}\in\mathscr{D}_{i}|\mathscr{F}_{t-1}\right)\leq\varepsilon
, also by construction. Applying a Chernoff bound (
Lemma

D.2
), we see that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbb{I}\left[\mathbf{x}_{t}\in\mathscr{D}_{i}\right]\geq 2T\varepsilon+6\log\left(\frac{1}{\delta}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\leq\delta.

Taking a union bound over
\mathscr{D}_{i}\in\mathscr{D}
concludes the proof.
∎

For the sake of completeness, we state and prove the standard Chernoff Bound with dependent data used in the previous argument:

Lemma D.2

(Chernoff Bound)
.

Suppose that
X_{1},\dots,X_{T}
are random variables such that
X_{t}\in\left\{0,1\right\}
for all
1\leq t\leq T
. Suppose that there exist
p_{t}
such that
\mathbb{P}\left(X_{t}=1|\mathscr{F}_{t-1}\right)\leq p_{t}
almost surely, where
\mathscr{F}_{t-1}
is the
\sigma
-algebra generated by
X_{1},\dots,X_{t-1}
. Then

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\sum_{t=1}^{T}X_{t}>2\sum_{t=1}^{T}p_{t}+\frac{1}{2}\log\left(\frac{1}{\delta}\right)\right)\leq\delta

Proof.

We use the standard Laplace transform trick:

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\sum_{t=1}^{T}X_{t}>2\sum_{t=1}^{T}p_{t}+u\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}\left(e^{\lambda\sum_{t=1}^{T}X_{t}}>e^{2\lambda u+2\lambda\sum_{t=1}^{T}p_{t}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq e^{-2\lambda u-2\lambda\sum_{t=1}^{T}p_{t}}\cdot\mathbb{E}\left[\prod_{t=1}^{T}e^{\lambda X_{t}}\right]

\displaystyle=e^{-2\lambda u-2\lambda\sum_{t=1}^{T}p_{t}}\cdot\mathbb{E}\left[\prod_{t=1}^{T}\mathbb{E}\left[e^{\lambda X_{t}}|\mathscr{F}_{t-1}\right]\right]

\displaystyle\leq e^{-2\lambda u-2\lambda\sum_{t=1}^{T}p_{t}}\cdot\mathbb{E}\left[\prod_{t=1}^{T}e^{\lambda}\mathbb{P}\left(X_{t}=1|\mathscr{F}_{t-1}\right)+(1-\mathbb{P}\left(X_{t}=1|\mathscr{F}_{t-1}\right))\right]

\displaystyle\leq e^{-2\lambda u-2\lambda\sum_{t=1}^{T}p_{t}}\cdot\prod_{t=1}^{T}e^{\left(e^{\lambda}-1\right)p_{t}}.

Thus we see that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\sum_{t=1}^{T}X_{t}>2\sum_{t=1}^{T}p_{t}+u\right)

\displaystyle\leq e^{-2\lambda u+\left(e^{\lambda}-1-2\lambda\right)\sum_{t=1}^{T}p_{t}}.

Setting
\lambda=1
and noting that
\sum_{t=1}^{T}p_{t}>0
tells us that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\sum_{t=1}^{T}X_{t}>2\sum_{t=1}^{T}p_{t}+u\right)\leq e^{-2u}

and the result follows.
∎

Returning to the main thread, we see that
Lemma

D.1
allows us to uniformly bound the approximation error of considering a disagreement cover. Before we can apply the result, however, we need to show that this complexity notion is small for the relevant class,
\mathcal{G}
. We have the following result:

Lemma D.3
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be the set of classifiers considered above. Then it holds that

\displaystyle\log\left(\textsc{DN}(\mathcal{G},\varepsilon)\right)\leq K(d+1)\log\left(\frac{3BK}{\varepsilon}\right)

We prove the result in two parts. For the first part, we show that any function class that is constructed by aggregating
K
simpler classes has a disagreement cover whose size is controlled by that of the
K
classes:

Lemma D.4
.

Let
\mathcal{G}_{1},\dots,\mathcal{G}_{K}
be function classes mapping
\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{Y}
. Let
h:\mathcal{Y}^{\times K}\to\mathbb{R}
be some aggregating function and define

\mathcal{G}=\left\{\mathbf{x}\mapsto h(g_{1}(\mathbf{x}),\dots,g_{K}(\mathbf{x}))\mid g_{1}\in\mathcal{G}_{1},\dots,g_{K}\in\mathcal{G}_{K}\right\}

Let
\textsc{DN}(\mathcal{G}_{i},\varepsilon)
be the minimal size of an
\varepsilon
-disagreement cover of
\mathcal{G}_{i}
. Then

\textsc{DN}(\mathcal{G},\varepsilon)\leq\prod_{i=1}^{K}\textsc{DN}\left(\mathcal{G}_{i},\frac{\varepsilon}{K}\right)

Proof.

Suppose that
\mathscr{D}_{i}
are
\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{K}\right)
-disagreement covers for
\mathcal{G}_{i}
and for any
g_{i}\in\mathcal{G}_{i}
denote by
(\pi(g_{i}),\mathscr{D}_{i}(g_{i}))
the pair of functions and disagreement sets satisfying the definition of a disagreement cover. Then we claim that

\mathscr{D}=\left\{\left(h(g_{1},\dots,g_{K}),\bigcup_{i}\mathscr{D}_{i}\right)|(g_{i},\mathscr{D}_{i})\in\mathscr{D}_{i}\right\}

is an
\varepsilon
-disagreement cover for
\mathcal{G}
. Note that
\left|\mathscr{D}\right|
is clearly bounded by the desired quantity so this claim suffices to prove the result.

To prove the claim, we first note that a union bound ensures that
\mathbb{P}(\mathbf{x}_{t}\in\bigcup_{i}\mathscr{D}_{i}\mid\mathscr{F}_{t-1})\leq\varepsilon
. We further note that if
g=h(g_{1},\dots,g_{K})
then

\displaystyle\left\{\mathbf{x}\mid g(\mathbf{x})\neq h(\pi(g_{1}(\mathbf{x})),\dots,\pi(g_{K}(\mathbf{x})))\right\}

\displaystyle\subset\bigcup_{1\leq i\leq K}\left\{\mathbf{x}\mid g_{i}(\mathbf{x})\neq\pi(g_{i}(\mathbf{x}))\right\}\subset\bigcup_{1\leq i\leq K}\mathscr{D}_{i}(g_{i}).

The result follows.
∎

We now prove that the class of linear threshold functions has bounded disagreement cover:

Lemma D.5
.

Let
\mathcal{H}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\left\{\pm 1\right\}
be the class of affine thresholds given by
\mathbf{x}\mapsto\sign(\left\langle\mathbf{w},\mathbf{x}\right\rangle+b)
for some
\mathbf{w}\in B^{d}
and some
b\in[-B,B]
. Then

\displaystyle\log\textsc{DN}(\mathcal{H},\varepsilon)\leq(d+1)\log\left(\frac{3B}{\varepsilon}\right).

Proof.

We first let
\mathcal{N}:=\{(\mathbf{w}_{i},b_{i})\}
denote an
\varepsilon
-net on
\mathcal{B}^{d}\times[-B,B]
. Associate each
(\mathbf{w}_{i},b_{i})
to its corresponding classifier
h_{i}(\mathbf{x})\sign(\left\langle\mathbf{w}_{i},\mathbf{x}\right\rangle+b_{i})
. We claim that for each
h_{i}
, there is a region
\mathscr{D}_{i}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that
\{(h_{i},\mathscr{D}_{i})\}
is an
\varepsilon
-disagreement cover. To see this, consider some
h
with parameters
(\mathbf{w},b)
, and let
h_{i}
with parameters
(\mathbf{w}_{i},b_{i})
ensure
\left|\left|\mathbf{w}_{i}-\mathbf{w}\right|\right|+\left|b_{i}-b\right|\leq\sigma_{\mathrm{dir}}\varepsilon/B
. Consider any
\mathbf{x}
such that

\displaystyle\sign\left(\left\langle\mathbf{w}_{i},\mathbf{x}\right\rangle+b_{i}\right)\neq\sign\left(\left\langle\mathbf{w},\mathbf{x}\right\rangle+b\right)

By the continuity of affine functions, there is some
\lambda\in(0,1)
such that if
\mathbf{w}_{\lambda}=(1-\lambda)\mathbf{w}_{i}+\lambda\mathbf{w}
and
b_{\lambda}=(1-\lambda)b_{i}+\lambda b
then

\displaystyle\left\langle\mathbf{w}_{\lambda},\mathbf{x}\right\rangle+b_{\lambda}=0

Note however that

\displaystyle\left|\left\langle\mathbf{w}_{\lambda},\mathbf{x}\right\rangle+b_{\lambda}-\left\langle\mathbf{w}_{i},\mathbf{x}\right\rangle-b_{i}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\lambda\left|\left\langle\mathbf{w}-\mathbf{w}_{i},\mathbf{x}\right\rangle\right|+\lambda\left|b_{i}-b\right|\leq B\left(\frac{\sigma_{\mathrm{dir}}\varepsilon}{B}\right)\leq\sigma_{\mathrm{dir}}\varepsilon

by the definition of our
\varepsilon
-net. Thus, let

\mathscr{D}_{i}=\left\{\mathbf{x}\mid\left|\left\langle\mathbf{w}_{i},\mathbf{x}\right\rangle+b\right|\leq\frac{\sigma_{\mathrm{dir}}\varepsilon}{B}\right\}

and note that the above computation tells us that if
\mathbf{x}\not\in\mathscr{D}_{i}
then
h_{i}
must agree with
h=(\mathbf{w},b)
for all
h
that are mapped to
h_{i}
by the projection onto the
\varepsilon
-net. Thus for all such
h
, it holds that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(h(\mathbf{x}_{t})\neq h_{i}(\mathbf{x}_{t})|\mathscr{F}_{t-1}\right)\leq\mathbb{P}\left(\left|\left\langle\mathbf{w}_{i},\mathbf{x}_{t}\right\rangle+b\right|\leq\frac{\sigma_{\mathrm{dir}}\varepsilon}{B}|\mathscr{F}_{t-1}\right)\leq\varepsilon.

Thus the claim holds and we have constructed an
\varepsilon
-disagreement cover. By noting that there are at most
\left(\frac{3}{\varepsilon}\right)^{d}\cdot\frac{B}{\varepsilon}
members of this cover by a volume argument we conclude the proof.
∎

D.2
Lower Bounding the Covariance

We continue by lower bounding
\Sigma_{ij}(\widehat{g})
. Before we begin, we introduce some notation. For any

, we will let

\displaystyle Z_{t;ij}(g)=\mathbb{I}\left[g(\bar{\mathbf{x}}_{t})=i\text{ and }g_{\star}(\bar{\mathbf{x}}_{t})=j\right]

\displaystyle\bar{Z}_{t;ij}(g)=\mathbb{E}\left[Z_{t;ij}(g)|\mathscr{F}_{t-1}\right]

or, in words,
Z_{t;ij}(g)
is the indicator of the event that a classifier predicts label
i
when
g_{\star}
predicts
j
and
\bar{Z}_{t;ij}(g)
is probability of this event conditioned on the
\mathbf{x}
-history. We will work under the small-ball assumption that there exist constants
c_{0},c_{1}
as well as
\zeta_{t},\rho_{t}
such that for any
\mathbf{w}\in\mathcal{S}^{d}
, it holds for all

that

\mathbb{P}\left(\left\langle\bar{\mathbf{x}}_{t},\mathbf{w}\right\rangle^{2}\geq c_{0}\zeta_{t}^{2}\mid\mathscr{F}_{t},Z_{t;ij}(g)=1\right)\geq c_{1}\rho_{t}

(D.2)

We will show that (
D.2
) holds and defer control of the values of
\zeta_{t},\rho_{t}
to
Section

D.3
, but, for the sake of clarity, we take these constants as given for now. We proceed to show that for a single function
g
, that for all
i,j
such that
I_{ij}(g)
is big, it holds that
\Sigma_{ij}(g)
is also large. We will then apply our results in the previous section to lift this statement to one uniform in
\mathcal{G}
. We have the following result:

Lemma D.6
.

Let
\rho=\min_{t}\rho_{t}
and let
\zeta=\min_{t}\zeta_{t}
, where
\rho_{t},\zeta_{t}
are from (
D.2
). Then with probability at least
1-\delta
, for all
1\leq i,j\leq K
and all
g\in\mathcal{G}
such that

\displaystyle\left|I_{ij}(g)\right|\geq\max\left(\frac{2}{\rho^{2}}\left(\log\left(\frac{2T}{\delta}\right)+\frac{d}{2}\log\left(C\frac{B\zeta^{2}}{\rho}\right)+\log\left(K^{2}\textsc{DN}(\varepsilon)\right)\right),C\frac{B^{2}}{\zeta^{2}}\left(2T\varepsilon+6\log\left(\frac{\textsc{DN}(\varepsilon)}{\delta}\right)\right)\right)

it holds that

\Sigma_{ij}(g)\succeq\frac{c_{0}\zeta^{2}}{8}\left|I_{ij}(g)\right|

We will prove
Lemma

D.6
by first fixing
g\in\mathcal{G}
and showing the statement for the fixed
g
and then using the results of
Section

D.3
to make the statements uniform in
\mathcal{G}
. To prove the statement for a fixed
g
, we require the following self-normalized martingale inequality:

Lemma D.7
.

Let
\mathscr{F}_{t}
be a filtration with
A_{t}\in\mathscr{F}_{t}
and
B_{t}\in\mathscr{F}_{t-1}
for all
t
. Let

\displaystyle p_{A_{t}}=\mathbb{P}\left(A_{t}\mid\mathscr{F}_{t-1},\,B_{t}\right)

\displaystyle p_{B_{t}}=\mathbb{P}\left(B_{t}\mid\mathscr{F}_{t-1}\right)

and suppose that
p_{A_{t}}\geq\rho
for all
t
. Then, with probability at least
1-\delta
,

\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbb{I}[A_{t}]\mathbb{I}[B_{t}]\geq\rho\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbb{I}[B_{t}]-\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{2\left(\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbb{I}[B_{t}]\right)\log\left(\frac{2T}{\delta}\right)}

In particular with probability at least
1-\delta
, if

\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbb{I}[B_{t}]\geq\frac{2}{\rho^{2}}\log\left(\frac{2T}{\delta}\right)

then

\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbb{I}[A_{t}]\mathbb{I}[B_{t}]\geq\frac{\rho}{2}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbb{I}[B_{t}]

In order to prove
Lemma

D.7
, we will require the following general result:

Lemma D.8

(Theorem 1 from
Abbasi-Yadkori et al. 2011
)
.

Let
(u_{t})
be predictable with respect to a filtration
(\mathscr{G}_{t})
, and let
(e_{t})
be such that
e_{t}\mid\mathscr{G}_{t}
is
\nu^{2}
-subGaussian. Then, for any fixed parameter
\lambda>0
, with probability
1-\delta
,

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{t=1}^{T}u_{t}e_{t}\right)^{2}\leq 2\nu^{2}\left(\lambda+\sum_{t=1}^{T}u_{t}^{2}\right)\log\frac{(1+\lambda^{-1}\sum_{t=1}^{T}u_{t}^{2})^{1/2}}{\delta}

We now present the proof of
Lemma

D.7
:

Proof of Lemma
D.7
.

We apply
Lemma

D.8
with
u_{t}=\mathbb{I}[B_{t}]
,
e_{t}=\mathbb{I}[A_{t}]-p_{A_{t}}
, and
\mathscr{G}_{t}=\mathscr{F}_{t}
. Noting that the latter is
\frac{1}{8}
-subGaussian because it is conditionally mean zero and bounded in absolute value by
1
, we have with probability at least
1-\delta
,

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{t=1}^{T}(\mathbb{I}[A_{t}]-p_{A_{t}})\mathbb{I}[B_{t}]\right|\leq\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\left(\lambda+\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbb{I}[B_{t}]\right)\log\left(\frac{\sqrt{1+\frac{\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbb{I}[B_{t}]}{\lambda}}}{\delta}\right)}

Taking a union bound over
\lambda\in[T]
and noting that
\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbb{I}[B_{t}]\in[T]
almost surely, we recover the result.
∎

We now proceed to apply

.

D.3
Small Ball Inequality

Introduce the shorthand
\mathbb{P}_{t}\left(\cdot\right):=\mathbb{P}\left(\cdot\mid\mathscr{F}_{t-1}\right)
and
\E_{t}\left[\cdot\right]:=\E\left[\cdot\mid\mathscr{F}_{t-1}\right]
. Recall from the previous section that we assumed that there are constants
c_{0},c_{1}
and
\zeta_{t},\rho_{t}
such that for any
\bar{\mathbf{w}}\in\mathcal{S}^{d}
, and any
g\in\mathcal{G}
, it holds that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{t}\left(\left\langle\bar{\mathbf{x}}_{t},\bar{\mathbf{w}}\right\rangle^{2}\geq c_{0}\zeta_{t}^{2}\mid Z_{t;ij}(g)=1\right)\geq c_{1}\rho_{t}.

(D.4)

In this section, we will show that smoothness and an assumption on
Z_{t;ij}(g)
suffice to guarantee that this holds. We will show the following result:

Lemma D.10
.

Suppose that Assumptions
4
and
2
hold. Fix a
g\in\mathcal{G}
and
1\leq i,j\leq K
and suppose that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{t}\left(Z_{t;ij}(g)\right)\geq\xi.

For all
\bar{\mathbf{w}}\in\mathcal{S}^{d}
, it holds that, for universal constants
c_{0}
and
c_{1}
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{t}\left(\left\langle\bar{\mathbf{x}}_{t},\bar{\mathbf{w}}\right\rangle^{2}\geq c_{0}\frac{\sigma_{\mathrm{dir}}^{2}\xi^{2}}{B^{2}}\mid Z_{t;ij}(g)=1\right)\geq c_{1}\frac{\sigma_{\mathrm{dir}}^{4}\xi^{4}}{B^{4}}.

We begin by defining some notation. First, we let
\mathbb{V}_{\mathbf{x}_{t}}
denote variance with respect to
\mathbf{x}_{t}
, conditioned on the
\mathbf{x}
-history and
Z_{t;ij}(g)
. That is, for some function
f
of
\mathbf{x}_{t}
, we let

\displaystyle\mathbb{V}_{\mathbf{x}_{t}}\left[f\right]=\mathbb{E}_{t}\left[f(\mathbf{x}_{t})^{2}\mid Z_{t;ij}(g)\right]-\mathbb{E}_{t}\left[f(\mathbf{x}_{t})\mid Z_{t;ij}(g)\right]^{2}.

(D.5)

For a fixed
\bar{\mathbf{w}}\in\mathcal{S}^{d}
, denote by
\mathbf{w}
the first
d
coordinates. For fixed
g\in\mathcal{G}
, and
1\leq i,j\leq K
, we will denote:

\displaystyle\rho_{t}(g,\bar{\mathbf{w}})

denote functions satisfying the following properties:

1.

The function
f^{\star}
can be decomposed as
f^{\star}(\mathbf{x})=f^{\star}_{g_{\star}(\mathbf{x})}(\mathbf{x})
and similarly for
\widehat{f}
it holds that
\widehat{f}(\mathbf{x})=\widehat{f}_{\widehat{g}(\mathbf{x})}(\mathbf{x})
for some
g_{\star},\widehat{g}\in\mathcal{G}
.

2.

For all
i\in[K]
, it holds that both
\widehat{f}_{i}
and
f^{\star}_{i}
are contractions, i.e.,
\left|\left|f^{\star}_{i}(\mathbf{x})-f^{\star}_{i}(\mathbf{x}^{\prime})\right|\right|\leq\left|\left|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{x}^{\prime}\right|\right|
.

Suppose that for
0\leq h\leq H-1
, we let

\displaystyle\mathbf{x}_{h+1}=f^{\star}(\mathbf{x}_{h})+\mathbf{e}_{h}

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathbf{x}}_{h+1}=\widehat{f}(\widehat{\mathbf{x}}_{h})+\mathbf{e}_{h}^{\prime}

for
\mathbf{e}_{h},\mathbf{e}_{h}^{\prime}
identically distributed satisfying
10
. Suppose that
\mathbf{x}_{0}
is
\sigma_{\mathrm{dir}}
-smooth and that, almost surely,
\mathbf{x}_{h},\widehat{\mathbf{x}}_{h}
have norms bounded uniformly by
B
. If
\max_{1\leq h<H}\left|\left|\widehat{f}(\mathbf{x}_{h})-f^{\star}(\mathbf{x}_{h})\right|\right|\leq\varepsilon
then it holds that

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}_{2}^{2}\left(\mathbf{x}_{H},\widehat{\mathbf{x}}_{H}\right)\leq\frac{9B^{2}H^{6}}{\sigma_{\mathrm{dir}}^{2}}\varepsilon^{2}\varepsilon.

Proof.

We begin by introducing intermediate random variables
\mathbf{x}_{H}^{(\ell)}
for
1\leq\ell\leq H
. We let
\mathbf{x}_{h}^{(\ell)}=\mathbf{x}_{h}
for
h\leq\ell
and let

\displaystyle\mathbf{x}_{h+1}^{(\ell)}=\widehat{f}\left(\mathbf{x}_{h}^{(\ell)}\right)+\mathbf{e}_{t}^{\prime}

otherwise. Note that
\mathbf{x}_{H}^{(1)}=\widehat{\mathbf{x}}_{H}
and that
\mathbf{x}_{H}^{(H)}=\mathbf{x}_{H}
. By the triangle inequality applied to Wasserstein distance, we observe that

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}_{2}(\mathbf{x}_{H},\widehat{\mathbf{x}}_{H})\leq\sum_{\ell=1}^{H}\mathcal{W}_{2}\left(\mathbf{x}_{H}^{(\ell)},\mathbf{x}_{H}^{(\ell+1)}\right).

Consider a coupling where
\mathbf{e}_{h}=\mathbf{e}_{h}^{\prime}
for all
h
. Note that under this coupling, for any
h>\ell
, it holds that

\displaystyle\left|\left|\mathbf{x}_{h}^{(\ell)}-\mathbf{x}_{h}^{(\ell+1)}\right|\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\left|\widehat{f}(\mathbf{x}_{h-1}^{(\ell)})+\mathbf{e}_{t}-\widehat{f}(\mathbf{x}_{h-1}^{(\ell+1)})-\mathbf{e}_{t}^{\prime}\right|\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\left|\widehat{f}(\mathbf{x}_{h-1}^{(\ell)})-\widehat{f}(\mathbf{x}_{h-1}^{(\ell+1)})\right|\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\left|\mathbf{x}_{h-1}^{(\ell)}-\mathbf{x}_{h-1}^{(\ell+1)}\right|\right|+B\cdot\mathbb{I}\left[\widehat{g}(\mathbf{x}_{h-1}^{(\ell)})\neq\widehat{g}(\mathbf{x}_{h-1}^{(\ell+1)})\right].

Thus, in particular,

\displaystyle\left|\left|\mathbf{x}_{H}^{(\ell)}-\mathbf{x}_{H}^{(\ell+1)}\right|\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\left|\mathbf{x}_{\ell+1}^{(\ell)}-\mathbf{x}_{\ell+1}^{(\ell+1)}\right|\right|+B\sum_{h=\ell}^{H-1}\mathbb{I}\left[\widehat{g}(\mathbf{x}_{h-1}^{(\ell)})\neq\widehat{g}(\mathbf{x}_{h-1}^{(\ell+1)})\right]

\displaystyle\leq\left|\left|\widehat{f}(\mathbf{x}_{\ell})-f^{\star}(\mathbf{x}_{\ell})\right|\right|+B\sum_{h=\ell}^{H-1}\mathbb{I}\left[\widehat{g}(\mathbf{x}_{h-1}^{(\ell)})\neq\widehat{g}(\mathbf{x}_{h-1}^{(\ell+1)})\right]

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon+B\sum_{h=\ell}^{H-1}\mathbb{I}\left[\widehat{g}(\mathbf{x}_{h-1}^{(\ell)})\neq\widehat{g}(\mathbf{x}_{h-1}^{(\ell+1)})\right]

by the assumption that
\left|\left|\widehat{f}(\mathbf{x}_{h})-f^{\star}(\mathbf{x}_{h})\right|\right|\leq\varepsilon
. We now note that a union bound implies:

\displaystyle\sum_{h=\ell}^{H-1}\mathbb{I}\left[\widehat{g}(\mathbf{x}_{h-1}^{(\ell)})\neq\widehat{g}(\mathbf{x}_{h-1}^{(\ell+1)})\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{h=\ell}^{H-1}\mathbb{I}\left[\widehat{g}(\mathbf{x}_{h-1}^{(\ell)})\neq\widehat{g}(\mathbf{x}_{h-1}^{(\ell+1)})\text{ and }\widehat{g}(\mathbf{x}_{h^{\prime}}^{(\ell)})=g_{\star}(\mathbf{x}_{h^{\prime}}^{(\ell+1)})\text{ for }h^{\prime}\leq h\right]

\displaystyle+\sum_{h=\ell}^{H-1}\mathbb{I}\left[\widehat{g}(\mathbf{x}_{h-1}^{(\ell)})\neq\widehat{g}(\mathbf{x}_{h-1}^{(\ell+1)})\text{ and there exists }h^{\prime}\leq h\text{ such that }\widehat{g}(\mathbf{x}_{h^{\prime}}^{(\ell)})\neq g_{\star}(\mathbf{x}_{h^{\prime}}^{(\ell+1)})\right]

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{h=\ell}^{H-1}\mathbb{I}\left[\widehat{g}(\mathbf{x}_{h-1}^{(\ell)})\neq\widehat{g}(\mathbf{x}_{h-1}^{(\ell+1)})\text{ and }\widehat{g}(\mathbf{x}_{h^{\prime}}^{(\ell)})=g_{\star}(\mathbf{x}_{h^{\prime}}^{(\ell+1)})\text{ for }h^{\prime}\leq h\right]

\displaystyle+\sum_{h=\ell}^{H-1}(H-1-\ell)\mathbb{I}\left[\widehat{g}(\mathbf{x}_{h-1}^{(\ell)})\neq\widehat{g}(\mathbf{x}_{h-1}^{(\ell+1)})\text{ and }\widehat{g}(\mathbf{x}_{h^{\prime}}^{(\ell)})=g_{\star}(\mathbf{x}_{h^{\prime}}^{(\ell+1)})\text{ for }h^{\prime}\leq h\right]

\displaystyle\leq H\sum_{h=\ell}^{H-1}\mathbb{I}\left[\widehat{g}(\mathbf{x}_{h-1}^{(\ell)})\neq\widehat{g}(\mathbf{x}_{h-1}^{(\ell+1)})\text{ and }\widehat{g}(\mathbf{x}_{h^{\prime}}^{(\ell)})=g_{\star}(\mathbf{x}_{h^{\prime}}^{(\ell+1)})\text{ for }h^{\prime}\leq h\right].

We now observe that

\displaystyle\mathbb{I}\left[\widehat{g}(\mathbf{x}_{h-1}^{(\ell)})\neq\widehat{g}(\mathbf{x}_{h-1}^{(\ell+1)})\text{ and }\widehat{g}(\mathbf{x}_{h^{\prime}}^{(\ell)})=g_{\star}(\mathbf{x}_{h^{\prime}}^{(\ell+1)})\text{ for }h^{\prime}\leq h\right]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{I}\left[\widehat{g}(\mathbf{x}_{h-1}^{(\ell)})\neq\widehat{g}(\mathbf{x}_{h-1}^{(\ell+1)})\text{ and }\left|\left|\mathbf{x}_{h-1}^{(\ell)}-\mathbf{x}_{h-1}^{(\ell+1)}\right|\right|\leq\varepsilon\right]

By the fact that
\mathcal{G}
is given by multi-class affine thresholds, we see that

\displaystyle\mathbb{I}\left[\widehat{g}(\mathbf{x}_{h-1}^{(\ell)})\neq\widehat{g}(\mathbf{x}_{h-1}^{(\ell+1)})\text{ and }\left|\left|\mathbf{x}_{h-1}^{(\ell)}-\mathbf{x}_{h-1}^{(\ell+1)}\right|\right|\leq\varepsilon\right]

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{1\leq i,j\leq K}\mathbb{I}\left[\left|\left\langle\mathbf{w}_{ij},\mathbf{x}_{h-1}\right\rangle\right|\leq 2\varepsilon\right]

where the
\mathbf{w}_{ij}
are the unit vectors determining the decision boundaries induced by
\widehat{g}
. By smoothness, then, we see that

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}(\mathbf{x}_{H}^{(\ell)},\mathbf{x}_{H}^{(\ell+1)})

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon+BH\cdot\mathbb{P}\left(\sum_{h=\ell}^{H-1}\mathbb{I}\left[\widehat{g}(\mathbf{x}_{h-1}^{(\ell)})\neq\widehat{g}(\mathbf{x}_{h-1}^{(\ell+1)})\text{ and }\widehat{g}(\mathbf{x}_{h^{\prime}}^{(\ell)})=g_{\star}(\mathbf{x}_{h^{\prime}}^{(\ell+1)})\text{ for }h^{\prime}\leq h\right]>1\right)

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon+BH\sum_{h=1}^{H}\mathbb{P}\left(\widehat{g}(\mathbf{x}_{h-1}^{(\ell)})\neq\widehat{g}(\mathbf{x}_{h-1}^{(\ell+1)})\text{ and }\widehat{g}(\mathbf{x}_{h^{\prime}}^{(\ell)})=g_{\star}(\mathbf{x}_{h^{\prime}}^{(\ell+1)})\text{ for }h^{\prime}\leq h\right)

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon+BH\sum_{h=1}^{H}\sum_{1\leq i,j\leq K}\mathbb{P}\left(\left|\left\langle\mathbf{w}_{ij},\mathbf{x}_{h}\right\rangle\right|\leq 2\varepsilon\right)

\displaystyle\leq\left(1+\frac{2BH^{2}}{\sigma_{\mathrm{dir}}}\right)\varepsilon.

Plugging this into our bound at the beginning and noting that
1\leq\frac{BH^{2}}{\sigma_{\mathrm{dir}}}
concludes the proof.
∎

We are now ready to prove
Theorem

18
. In particular, we use
Lemma

G.1
to reduce a bound on simulation regret to one of the distance between
\widehat{f}
and
f^{\star}
evaluated on the data sequence. We then apply
Theorems

12
and
14
to control this distance. The details are below:

Proof of
Theorem

18
.

We begin by applying
Lemma

G.1
. Observe that in our setting,
\mathcal{G}
is the class of multi-class affine classifiers and
f^{\star},\widehat{f}_{t}
can be decomposed as required by the theorem, where we let
\tau
denote the epoch in which
t
is placed and let

\displaystyle\widehat{f}_{t}(\mathbf{x})=\widetilde{\mathbf{\Theta}}_{\tau,\widetilde{g}_{\tau}(\mathbf{x})}\cdot\mathbf{x}.

By the fact that
\widetilde{\mathbf{\Theta}}_{\tau,i},\mathbf{\Theta}^{\star}_{i}\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathbf{P}}
, which follows from the construction of
\widetilde{\mathbf{\Theta}}_{\tau,i}
and
8
, the contractivity assumption required by
Lemma

G.1
holds. Furthermore, note that because
\mathcal{C}_{\mathbf{P}}
is convex and
\mathbf{\Theta}^{\star}_{i}\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathbf{P}}
, it holds that

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{\mathbf{\Theta}}_{\tau,i}-\mathbf{\Theta}^{\star}_{\pi_{\tau(i)}}\|_{\mathrm{F}}\leq\|\widehat{\mathbf{\Theta}}_{\tau,i}-\mathbf{\Theta}^{\star}_{\pi_{\tau(i)}}\|_{\mathrm{F}}.

(G.2)

Now, note that
Algorithm

1
does not update the predicting functions within the epoch and thus for all
t
such that
\left\{t,t+1,\dots,t+H\right\}
does not contain an integral multiple of
E
, it holds that our prediction function
\widehat{f}_{t}
is constant on the interval. Let
\mathcal{I}
denote the set of times
t
such that the previous condition fails, i.e.,

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}=\left\{t\leq T|\tau E-t>H\text{ for all }\tau\in\mathbb{N}\right\}.

Then, we may apply
Lemma

G.1
to get

\displaystyle\mathrm{SimReg}_{T,H}

\displaystyle=\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{h=1}^{H}\mathcal{W}_{2}^{2}(\mathbf{x}_{t+h},\widehat{\mathbf{x}}_{t+h}|\mathscr{F}_{t-1})

\displaystyle=\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbb{I}\left[t\in\mathcal{I}\right]\sum_{h=1}^{H}\mathcal{W}_{2}^{2}(\mathbf{x}_{t+h},\widehat{\mathbf{x}}_{t+h}|\mathscr{F}_{t-1})+\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbb{I}\left[t\not\in\mathcal{I}\right]\sum_{h=1}^{H}\mathcal{W}_{2}^{2}(\mathbf{x}_{t+h},\widehat{\mathbf{x}}_{t+h}|\mathscr{F}_{t-1})

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbb{I}\left[t\in\mathcal{I}\right]\sum_{h=1}^{H}\frac{9B^{2}H^{6}}{\sigma_{\mathrm{dir}}^{2}}\max_{1\leq h\leq H}\left|\left|\widehat{f}(\mathbf{x}_{t+h})-f^{\star}(\mathbf{x}_{t+h})\right|\right|^{2}+4HB^{2}R^{2}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbb{I}\left[t\notin\mathcal{I}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{9B^{2}H^{7}}{\sigma_{\mathrm{dir}}^{2}}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbb{I}\left[t\in\mathcal{I}\right]\sum_{h=1}^{H}\left|\left|\widehat{f}(\mathbf{x}_{t+h})-f^{\star}(\mathbf{x}_{t+h})\right|\right|^{2}+4B^{2}R^{2}H\cdot\frac{HT}{E}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{9B^{2}H^{8}}{\sigma_{\mathrm{dir}}^{2}}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbb{I}\left[t\in\mathcal{I}\right]\left|\left|\widehat{f}(\mathbf{x}_{t})-f^{\star}(\mathbf{x}_{t})\right|\right|^{2}+4B^{2}R^{2}H^{2}\cdot\frac{T}{E}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{9B^{2}H^{8}}{\sigma_{\mathrm{dir}}^{2}}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\left|\left|\mathbf{\widehat{y}}_{t}-f^{\star}(\mathbf{x}_{t})\right|\right|^{2}+4B^{2}R^{2}H^{2}\cdot\frac{T}{E},

where
\mathbf{\widehat{y}}_{t}
is the prediction of
Algorithm

1
. We may now apply
Theorem

16
to bound this last quantity by applying (
G.2
). The result follows.
∎

G.5
Extensions

For simplicity, we considered a setting where all linear dynamics were stable with a common Lyapunov function. Our results can be extended to the case where there are mode-dependent gains
(\mathbf{K}_{i})_{i\in K}
, where
\mathbf{K}_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{z}\times d_{u}}
, as well, because our proof demonstrates that as we achieve low-regret, our simulation accurately recovers the correct mode sequence. Thus, if we know gains which ensure mutual stability, we can apply these gains as well. More general closed-loop policies can also be accomodated, provided we maintain the requisite smoothness (as, for example, ensured by
Lemma

3.1
). Lastly, as our regret-guarantees ensure parameter recovery, we can envision settings where the gains are constructed, for example, by certainty-equivalent control synthesis, and analyze their suboptimality via perturbation bounds such as those in
Simchowitz and Foster 2020
.

Abstract
. On a smooth, closed Riemannian manifold, we study the question of proportionality of components, also called synchronization, of vector-valued solutions to nonlinear elliptic Schrödinger systems with constant coefficients. In particular, we obtain bifurcation results showing the existence of branches of non-synchronized solutions emanating from the constant solutions.



1.
Introduction

On a smooth, closed Riemannian manifold
\left(M,g\right)
of dimension
n
, we consider vector-valued solutions
\left(u_{1},u_{2}\right)\in C^{2}\left(M\right)^{2}
to elliptic systems of the form

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\Delta_{g}u_{1}=F_{1}\left(u_{1},u_{2}\right)&&\text{in }M\\
&\Delta_{g}u_{2}=F_{2}\left(u_{1},u_{2}\right),&&\text{in }M\end{aligned}\right.

(1.1)

where
F_{1}
and
F_{2}
are
C^{1}
functions and
\Delta_{g}:=-\divergence\nabla
is the Laplace–Beltrami operator. In particular, we are interested in the stationary nonlinear Schrödinger system

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\Delta_{g}u_{1}+\lambda_{1}u_{1}=a_{11}u_{1}^{q-1}+a_{12}u_{2}^{q-2}u_{1}&&\text{in }M\\
&\Delta_{g}u_{2}+\lambda_{2}u_{2}=a_{21}u_{1}^{q-2}u_{2}+a_{22}u_{2}^{q-1}&&\text{in }M\\
&u_{1},u_{2}>0&&\text{in }M,\end{aligned}\right.

(1.2)

where
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},a_{11},a_{12},a_{21},a_{22}\in\mathbb{R}
and
q\in\left(2,\infty\right)
. In the cubic case
q=4
, the system (
1.2
) arises in particular in nonlinear optics (see for instance Akhmediev and Ankiewicz
[
AA
]
and Kanna and Lakshmanan
[
KL
]
) and the Hartree–Fock theory for Bose–Einstein condensates (see Esry, Greene, Burke and Bohn
[
EGBB
]
and Timmermans
[
T
]
). Such systems have received considerable attention from mathematicians in recent years. Among many others, let us refer for instance to the work of Bartsch, Dancer and Wang
[
BDW
]
, Clapp and Pistoia
[
CP1
,
CP2
]
, Gladiali, Grossi and Troestler
[
GGT2
,
GGT1
]
, Guo, Li and Wei
[
GLW
]
, Guo and Liu
[
GL
]
, Lin and Wei
[
LW1
]
, Liu and Wang
[
LW2
]
, Peng, Peng and Wang
[
PPW
]
, Sirakov
[
S
]
, Soave and Zilio
[
SZ
]
, Terracini and Verzini
[
TV
]
and Wei and Wu
[
WW
]
in the case where
M=\mathbb{R}^{n}
(note that when
\left(M,g\right)
is the standard round sphere,
n\geq 3
,
\lambda_{1}=\lambda_{2}=n\left(n-2\right)/4
and
q=2n/\left(n-2\right)
, we can use stereographic projection to write (
1.2
) as a system in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
) and Chen and Zou
[
CZ
]
, Clapp, Pistoia and Tavares
[
CPT
]
, Druet and Hebey
[
DH
]
and Druet, Hebey and Vétois
[
DHV
]
in the case of a more general manifold.

In this paper, we are interested in the question of proportionality of components, also called synchronization, of solutions to the system (
1.2
). A solution
\left(u_{1},u_{2}\right)
of (
1.2
) is said to be
synchronized
if there exists a constant
\Lambda>0
such that
u_{2}\equiv\Lambda u_{1}
in
M
. This question has been studied for instance by Montaru, Sirakov and Souplet
[
MSS
]
and Quittner and Souplet
[
QS
]
in the case of systems in domains of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. It also naturally arises in the case of a closed manifold.

It is easy to see that every synchronized solution
\left(u_{1},u_{2}\right)=\left(u_{1},\Lambda u_{1}\right)
of the system (
1.2
) is such that
u_{1}
is constant in the case where
\lambda_{1}\neq\lambda_{2}
and
u_{1}
is a solution of the equation

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\Delta_{g}u_{1}+\lambda_{1}u_{1}=\mu_{1}u_{1}^{q-2}&&\text{ in }M\\
&u_{1}>0&&\text{ in }M,\end{aligned}\right.

(1.3)

where
\mu_{1}:=a_{11}+a_{12}\Lambda^{q-2}=a_{21}+a_{22}\Lambda^{q-2}
, in the case where
\lambda_{1}=\lambda_{2}
. We know from a result of Bidaut-Véron and Véron
[
BV
]
that the equation (
1.3
) does not have any non-constant solutions when

2<q\leq 2^{*}\quad\text{and}\quad\left\{\begin{aligned} &\frac{n-1}{n}\left(q-2\right)\lambda_{1}g\leq\Ricci_{g}&&\text{if }q<2^{*}\\
&\frac{n-1}{n}\left(q-2\right)\lambda_{1}g<\Ricci_{g}&&\text{if }q=2^{*},\end{aligned}\right.

(1.4)

where
2^{*}:=\infty
if
n\leq 2
,
2^{*}:=2n/\left(n-2\right)
if
n\geq 3
,
\Ricci_{g}
is the Ricci curvature of the manifold and the latter inequalities are in the sense of bilinear forms. On the other hand, existence results of non-constant solutions to equation (
1.3
) abound in the case where (
1.4
) is not satisfied (see for instance Chen, Wei and Yan
[
CWY
]
, Hebey and Vaugon
[
HV
]
, Hebey and Wei
[
HW
]
, Micheletti, Pistoia and Vétois
[
MPV
]
and Vétois and Wang
[
VW
]
.)

For simplicity, in this introduction, we state our results in the case of the sphere
\left(\mathbb{S}^{n},g_{0}\right)
, where
g_{0}
is the standard round metric. Furthermore, we assume that
\lambda_{1}=\lambda_{2}
and
a_{12}=a_{21}
, namely we consider the system

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\Delta_{g_{0}}u_{1}+\lambda u_{1}=au_{1}^{q-1}+bu_{2}^{q-2}u_{1}&&\text{in }\mathbb{S}^{n}\\
&\Delta_{g_{0}}u_{2}+\lambda u_{2}=bu_{1}^{q-2}u_{2}+cu_{2}^{q-1}&&\text{in }\mathbb{S}^{n}\\
&u_{1},u_{2}>0&&\text{in }\mathbb{S}^{n},\end{aligned}\right.

(1.5)

where
\lambda,a,b,c\in\mathbb{R}
and
q\in\left(2,\infty\right)
. In the Euclidean space, this case has been studied for instance by Clapp and Pistoia
[
CP1
]
(via stereographic projection, the system studied in
[
CP1
]
matches with (
1.5
) when
n=4
,
\lambda=2
and the parameters
\alpha
and
\beta
in
[
CP1
]
are equal to 2). We refer to Sections
2
and
3
for results applying to more general systems and more general manifolds. For the system (
1.5
), we obtain the following:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\lambda,a,b,c\in\mathbb{R}
and
q\in\left(2,\infty\right)
.

(i)

If either
c\leq b\leq a
or
a\leq b\leq c
and at least one of the two inequalities is strict, then the system (
1.5
) has no solutions.

(ii)

If either [
a<b
and
c<b
] or
a=b=c
, then every solution of (
1.5
) is synchronized.

(iii)

There exist real numbers
\lambda
,
a
,
b
and
c
such that
\lambda>0
,
a=c>b>0
and (
1.5
) has non-synchronized solutions. More precisely, we have the following result: for every
\lambda,a,b\in C^{1}\left(\left[-\delta,\delta\right]\right)
,
\delta>0
, if the following conditions hold:

(A1)

\lambda\left(0\right)\left(a\left(0\right)+b\left(0\right)\right)>0
,

(A2)

\lambda\left(0\right)\not\in\big\{\frac{2j\left(2j+n-1\right)}{q-2}:\ j\in\mathbb{N}\big\}
, where
\mathbb{N}:=\left\{1,2,\dotsc\right\}
,

(A3)

\beta\left(0\right):=\lambda\left(0\right)\frac{a\left(0\right)-b\left(0\right)}{a\left(0\right)+b\left(0\right)}\in\big\{\frac{j\left(j+n-1\right)}{q-2}:\ j\in\mathbb{N}\big\}
and
\beta^{\prime}\left(0\right)\neq 0
,

then there exists a
C^{1}
branch (see Definition
2.1
) of non-synchronized solutions to (
1.5
) with
\lambda=\lambda\left(\alpha\right)
,
c=a=a\left(\alpha\right)
and
b=b\left(\alpha\right)
emanating from the constant solution at
\alpha=0
.

Theorem
1.1
(iii) extends a previous result obtained by Gladiali, Grossi and Troestler
[
GGT1
]
for systems with Sobolev critical growth in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, which, via stereographic projection, corresponds to the case where
n\geq 3
,
\lambda=n\left(n-2\right)/4
and
q=2n/\left(n-2\right)
. Like in
[
GGT1
]
, our approach is based on the bifurcation theory at eigenvalues of odd multiplicity. Unlike in
[
GGT1
]
, by taking advantage of our closed manifold setting, we perform our constructions in
C^{1,\theta}\left(M\right)
,
\theta\in\left(0,1\right)
, instead of Sobolev spaces, which allows us to treat the case of systems with supercritical growth.

Theorem
1.1
(iii) is proven in Section
2
(as a particular case of Theorem
2.3
) and Theorem
1.1
(i) and (ii) are proven in Section
3
(as a particular cases of Theorem
3.1
(i) and (ii)).

2.
Bifurcation results

This section is devoted to bifurcation results showing the existence of branches of non-synchronized solutions for systems like (
1.2
).

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\left(M,g\right)
be a smooth, closed Riemannian manifold,
\Omega
be an open set in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
,
I:=\left[-\delta,\delta\right]
,
\delta>0
, and
F_{1},F_{2}\in C^{1}\left(I\times\Omega\right)
. Consider the system

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\Delta_{g}u_{1}=F_{1}\left(\alpha,u_{1},u_{2}\right)&&\text{in }M\\
&\Delta_{g}u_{2}=F_{2}\left(\alpha,u_{1},u_{2}\right)&&\text{in }M,\end{aligned}\right.

(2.1)

where
\alpha\in I
. Assume that for every
\alpha\in I
, there exists a solution
\left(\overline{u}_{1}\left(\alpha\right),\overline{u}_{2}\left(\alpha\right)\right)\in C^{2}\left(M\right)^{2}
of (
2.1
) such that
\left(\overline{u}_{1}\left(\alpha\right),\overline{u}_{2}\left(\alpha\right)\right)\to\left(\overline{u}_{1}\left(0\right),\overline{u}_{2}\left(0\right)\right)
in
C^{2}\left(M\right)^{2}
as
\alpha\to 0
. Let
\mathcal{S}
be the set of all solutions
\left(\alpha,u_{1},u_{2}\right)\in I\times C^{2}\left(M\right)^{2}
to (
2.1
) such that
\left(u_{1},u_{2}\right)\neq\left(\overline{u}_{1}\left(\alpha\right),\overline{u}_{2}\left(\alpha\right)\right)
. We say that the solution
\left(0,\overline{u}_{1}\left(0\right),\overline{u}_{2}\left(0\right)\right)
is a
bifurcation point
of (
2.1
) if
\left(0,\overline{u}_{1}\left(0\right),\overline{u}_{2}\left(0\right)\right)\in\overline{\mathcal{S}}
, where
\overline{\mathcal{S}}
stands for the closure of
\mathcal{S}
in
I\times C^{2}\left(M\right)^{2}
. Furthermore, we say that a subset
\mathcal{B}\subseteq\mathcal{S}
is a
C^{1}

branch
of solutions to (
2.1
) emanating from
\left(0,\overline{u}_{1}\left(0\right),\overline{u}_{2}\left(0\right)\right)
if
\mathcal{B}\neq\emptyset
and
\mathcal{B}\cup\left\{\left(0,\overline{u}_{1}\left(0\right),\overline{u}_{2}\left(0\right)\right)\right\}
is a
C^{1}
curve in
I\times C^{2}\left(M\right)^{2}
.

In the case of the sphere, we obtain Theorem
1.1
(iii). In the case of a more general manifold, we obtain the following:

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
\left(M,g\right)
be a smooth, closed Riemannian manifold,
I:=\left[-\delta,\delta\right]
,
\delta>0
,
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},a_{11},a_{12},a_{21},a_{22}\in C^{1}\left(I\right)
and
q\in\left(2,\infty\right)
. Consider the system

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\Delta_{g}u_{1}+\lambda_{1}\left(\alpha\right)u_{1}=a_{11}\left(\alpha\right)u_{1}^{q-1}+a_{12}\left(\alpha\right)u_{2}^{q-2}u_{1}&&\text{in }M\\
&\Delta_{g}u_{2}+\lambda_{2}\left(\alpha\right)u_{2}=a_{21}\left(\alpha\right)u_{1}^{q-2}u_{2}+a_{22}\left(\alpha\right)u_{2}^{q-1}&&\text{in }M\\
&u_{1},u_{2}>0&&\text{in }M,\end{aligned}\right.

(2.2)

where
\alpha\in I
. Assume that the following conditions hold:

(B1)

For every
\alpha\in I
, the system (
1.2
) has a unique constant solution
\left(\overline{u}_{1}\left(\alpha\right),\overline{u}_{2}\left(\alpha\right)\right)
.

(B2)

For every
\alpha\in I
, the matrix

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}\left(\alpha\right):=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}a_{11}\left(\alpha\right)\overline{u}_{1}\left(\alpha\right)^{q-2}&a_{12}\left(\alpha\right)\overline{u}_{2}\left(\alpha\right)^{q-3}\overline{u}_{1}\left(\alpha\right)\\
a_{21}\left(\alpha\right)\overline{u}_{1}\left(\alpha\right)^{q-3}\overline{u}_{2}\left(\alpha\right)&a_{22}\left(\alpha\right)\overline{u}_{2}\left(\alpha\right)^{q-2}\end{array}\right)

has two distinct, non-zero, real eigenvalues
\beta_{1}\left(\alpha\right)
and
\beta_{2}\left(\alpha\right)
.

(B3)

\mathcal{H}^{*}_{1}\times\mathcal{H}^{*}_{2}
has odd dimension, where

\mathcal{H}^{*}_{i}:=\left\{\varphi\in C^{2}\left(M\right):\Delta_{g}\varphi=\left(q-2\right)\beta_{i}\left(0\right)\varphi\hbox{ in }M\right\}\quad\forall i\in\left\{1,2\right\}.

(B4)

For every
i\in\left\{1,2\right\}
, if
\mathcal{H}^{*}_{i}\neq\left\{0\right\}
, then
\beta^{\prime}_{i}\left(0\right)\neq 0
and either
\lambda_{1}\left(0\right)\neq\lambda_{2}\left(0\right)
or
\beta_{i}\left(0\right)\neq\lambda_{1}\left(0\right)=\lambda_{2}\left(0\right)
.

Then the solution
\left(0,\overline{u}_{1}\left(0\right),\overline{u}_{2}\left(0\right)\right)
is a bifurcating point of the system (
2.2
). Furthermore, there exists a neighborhood
\mathcal{N}
of
\left(0,\overline{u}_{1}\left(0\right),\overline{u}_{2}\left(0\right)\right)
in
I\times C^{2}\left(M\right)^{2}
such that for every solution
\left(\alpha,u_{1},u_{2}\right)\in\mathcal{N}
of (
2.2
), if
\left(u_{1},u_{2}\right)\neq\left(\overline{u}_{1}\left(\alpha\right),\overline{u}_{2}\left(\alpha\right)\right)
, then
\left(u_{1},u_{2}\right)
is non-synchronized. If moreover
\mathcal{H}^{*}_{1}\times\mathcal{H}^{*}_{2}
has dimension one, then there exists a
C^{1}
branch of non-synchronized solutions to (
2.2
) emanating from
\left(0,\overline{u}_{1}\left(0\right),\overline{u}_{2}\left(0\right)\right)
.

Both Theorem
1.1
(iii) and Theorem
2.2
follow from the following general bifurcation result for systems of the form (
2.1
):

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
\left(M,g\right)
be a smooth, closed Riemannian manifold,
\Omega
be an open set in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
such that
\left(0,0\right)\in\Omega
,
I:=\left[-\delta,\delta\right]
,
\delta>0
, and
F_{1},F_{2}\in C^{1}\left(I\times\Omega\right)
such that
\partial_{\alpha}\partial_{u_{i}}F_{j}
exists and is continuous in
I\times\Omega
for all
i,j\in\left\{1,2\right\}
, where we denote by
\left(\alpha,u_{1},u_{2}\right)
a point in
I\times\Omega
. Assume that the following conditions hold:

(C1)

F_{1}\left(\alpha,0,0\right)=F_{2}\left(\alpha,0,0\right)=0
for all
\alpha\in I
.

(C2)

\partial_{u_{2}}F_{1}\left(\alpha,0,0\right)=\partial_{u_{1}}F_{2}\left(\alpha,0,0\right)=0
for all
\alpha\in I
.

(C3)

There exist two closed subspaces
\mathcal{H}_{1}
and
\mathcal{H}_{2}
of
C^{1,\theta}\left(M\right)
,
\theta\in\left(0,1\right)
, and two open subsets
\mathcal{U}_{1}\subseteq\mathcal{H}_{1}
and
\mathcal{U}_{2}\subseteq\mathcal{H}_{2}
which contain 0 and satisfy the following conditions:

–

\left(u_{1}\left(x\right),u_{2}\left(x\right)\right)\in\Omega
for all
x\in M
and
\left(u_{1},u_{2}\right)\in{\mathcal{U}_{1}}\times{\mathcal{U}_{2}}
.

–

\left(\Delta_{g}+1\right)^{-1}u_{i}\in\mathcal{H}_{i}
for all
u_{i}\in{{\mathcal{U}}_{i}}
and
i\in\left\{1,2\right\}
.

–

\left(\Delta_{g}+1\right)^{-1}F_{i}\left(\alpha,u_{1},u_{2}\right)\in\mathcal{H}_{i}
for all
\left(\alpha,u_{1},u_{2}\right)\in I\times{{\mathcal{U}}_{1}}\times{{\mathcal{U}}_{2}}
and
i\in\left\{1,2\right\}
.

(C4)

\mathcal{H}^{*}_{1}\times\mathcal{H}^{*}_{2}
has odd dimension, where

\mathcal{H}^{*}_{i}:=\left\{\varphi\in\mathcal{H}_{i}:\ \Delta_{g}\varphi=\partial_{u_{i}}F_{i}\left(0,0,0\right)\varphi\text{ in }M\right\}\quad\forall i\in\left\{1,2\right\}.

(C5)

For every
i\in\left\{1,2\right\}
, if
\mathcal{H}^{*}_{i}\neq\left\{0\right\}
, then
\partial_{\alpha}\partial_{u_{i}}F_{i}\left(0,0,0\right)\neq 0
.

Then there exists a sequence of solutions
\left(\left(\alpha_{m},u_{1,m},u_{2,m}\right)\right)_{m\in\mathbb{N}}
to the system (
2.1
) such that
\left(\alpha_{m},u_{1,m},u_{2,m}\right)\in I\times\left(\left(\mathcal{U}_{1}\times\mathcal{U}_{2}\right)\backslash\left\{\left(0,0\right)\right\}\right)
and
\left(\alpha_{m},u_{1,m},u_{2,m}\right)\to\left(0,0,0\right)
in
I\times C^{2}\left(M\right)^{2}
as
m\to\infty
. Furthermore, every such sequence
\left(\left(\alpha_{m},u_{1,m},u_{2,m}\right)\right)_{m\in\mathbb{N}}
is such that up to a subsequence,

u_{i,m}=\varepsilon_{m}\varphi_{i}+\smallo\left(\varepsilon_{m}\right)\quad\hbox{in }\mathcal{H}_{i}\quad\forall i\in\left\{1,2\right\}

(2.3)

as
m\to\infty
for some
\left(\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2}\right)\in\left(\mathcal{H}^{*}_{1}\times\mathcal{H}^{*}_{2}\right)\backslash\left\{\left(0,0\right)\right\}
and
\varepsilon_{m}>0
such that
\varepsilon_{m}\to 0
. If moreover
\mathcal{H}^{*}_{1}\times\mathcal{H}^{*}_{2}
has dimension one and
\partial_{u_{i}}\partial_{u_{j}}F_{k}
exists and is continuous in
I\times\Omega
for all
i,j,k\in\left\{1,2\right\}
, then there exists a neighborhood
\mathcal{N}
of
\left(0,0,0\right)
in
I\times\mathcal{U}_{1}\times\mathcal{U}_{2}
such that the set of solutions
\left(\alpha,u_{1},u_{2}\right)\in\mathcal{N}\backslash\left(I\times\left\{\left(0,0\right)\right\}\right)
to (
2.1
) is a
C^{1}
branch emanating from
\left(0,0,0\right)
whose tangent line at
\left(0,0,0\right)
is directed by some vector in
\mathbb{R}\times\left(\left(\mathcal{H}^{*}_{1}\times\mathcal{H}^{*}_{2}\right)\backslash\left\{\left(0,0\right)\right\}\right)
.

Proof of Theorem
2.3
.

Let

\mathcal{H}:=\mathcal{H}_{1}\times\mathcal{H}_{2},\quad\mathcal{H}^{*}:=\mathcal{H}_{1}^{*}\times\mathcal{H}_{2}^{*}\quad\hbox{and}\quad\mathcal{U}:=\mathcal{U}_{1}\times\mathcal{U}_{2}.

By replacing
\mathcal{U}_{1}
and
\mathcal{U}_{2}
by smaller sets if necessary, we may assume that there exists a compact set
K\subset\Omega
such that
\left(u_{1}\left(x\right),u_{2}\left(x\right)\right)\in K
for all
x\in M
and
\left(u_{1},u_{2}\right)\in\mathcal{U}
. The solutions
\left(u_{1},u_{2}\right)\in\mathcal{U}
to (
2.1
) are given by the zeros of the function
\mathcal{T}:I\times{{\mathcal{U}}}\to\mathcal{H}
defined by

\mathcal{T}\left(\alpha,u_{1},u_{2}\right):=\mathcal{I}\left(u_{1},u_{2}\right)-\mathcal{K}_{\alpha}\left(u_{1},u_{2}\right),\quad\text{where}\quad\mathcal{I}\left(u_{1},u_{2}\right):=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}u_{1}\\
u_{2}\end{array}\right)

and

\mathcal{K}_{\alpha}\left(u_{1},u_{2}\right):=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\left(\Delta_{g}+1\right)^{-1}\left[F_{1}\left(\alpha,u_{1},u_{2}\right)+u_{1}\right]\\
\left(\Delta_{g}+1\right)^{-1}\left[F_{2}\left(\alpha,u_{1},u_{2}\right)+u_{2}\right]\end{array}\right)

for all
\left(\alpha,u_{1},u_{2}\right)\in I\times{{\mathcal{U}}}
. By the assumption (C3), we have that the functions
\mathcal{K}_{\alpha}
and
\mathcal{T}
are well-defined. In what follows, we write a point in
I\times{{\mathcal{U}}}
as
\left(\alpha,U\right)
.

Step 1.

We begin with proving that
\mathcal{K}_{\alpha}
is compact,
\mathcal{T}\in C^{1}\left(I\times\mathcal{U}\right)
and
D_{U}\,\partial_{\alpha}\mathcal{T}
exists and is continuous in
I\times\mathcal{U}
.

Suppose that
\left(\left(u_{1,m},u_{2,m}\right)\right)_{m\in\mathbb{N}}
is a bounded sequence in
{{\mathcal{U}}}
. Then the sequences
\left(u_{1,m}\right)_{m}
and
\left(u_{2,m}\right)_{m}
are bounded in
C^{1,\theta}\left(M,K\right)
and up to a subsequence,
\left(u_{1,m},u_{2,m}\right)\to\left(u_{1,0},u_{2,0}\right)
in
C^{1}\left(M,K\right)^{2}
as
m\to\infty
. Let
\alpha\in I
and

\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\tilde{u}_{1,m}\\
\tilde{u}_{2,m}\end{array}\right):=\mathcal{K}_{\alpha}\left(u_{1,m},u_{2,m}\right)

so that

\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\Delta_{g}\tilde{u}_{1,m}+\tilde{u}_{1,m}\\

a\left(\alpha\right)>b\left(\alpha\right)
and
\lambda\left(\alpha\right)\left(a\left(\alpha\right)+b\left(\alpha\right)\right)>0
for all
\alpha\in I
. Then the unique constant solution for the system (
1.5
) is given by

\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\overline{u}_{1}\left(\alpha\right)\\
\overline{u}_{2}\left(\alpha\right)\end{array}\right):=\overline{u}\left(\alpha\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{c}1\\
1\end{array}\right),\quad\text{where }\overline{u}\left(\alpha\right):=\left(\frac{\lambda\left(\alpha\right)}{a\left(\alpha\right)+b\left(\alpha\right)}\right)^{{1}/{\left(q-2\right)}}

and it satisfies

\left\{\begin{aligned} &a\left(\alpha\right)\overline{u}_{1}\left(\alpha\right)^{q-2}+b\left(\alpha\right)\overline{u}_{2}\left(\alpha\right)^{q-2}=\lambda\left(\alpha\right)\\
&b\left(\alpha\right)\overline{u}_{1}\left(\alpha\right)^{q-2}+a\left(\alpha\right)\overline{u}_{2}\left(\alpha\right)^{q-2}=\lambda\left(\alpha\right).\end{aligned}\right.

We look for solutions of (
1.5
) bifurcating from
\left(\overline{u}_{1}\left(\alpha\right),\overline{u}_{2}\left(\alpha\right)\right)
. For (
1.5
), the eigenvalues
\beta_{1}\left(\alpha\right)
and
\beta_{2}\left(\alpha\right)
of
\mathcal{A}\left(\alpha\right)
are given by

\beta_{1}\left(\alpha\right)=\lambda\left(\alpha\right)\quad\text{and}\quad\beta_{2}\left(\alpha\right)=\lambda\left(\alpha\right)\frac{a\left(\alpha\right)-b\left(\alpha\right)}{a\left(\alpha\right)+b\left(\alpha\right)}\,.

We let
F
and
\Omega=\left(-\delta_{0},\delta_{0}\right)^{2}
be defined similarly as in the proof of Theorem
2.2
, so that in particular, the conditions (C1) and (C2) of Theorem
2.3
are satisfied. In this case, we find

\mathcal{P}\left(\alpha\right)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&1\\
1&-1\end{array}\right).

Next, we choose the appropriate subspaces
\mathcal{H}_{1}
and
\mathcal{H}_{2}
and open subsets
\mathcal{U}_{1}\subseteq\mathcal{H}_{1}
and
\mathcal{U}_{2}\subseteq\mathcal{H}_{2}
. For this, we use an idea from Gladiali, Grossi and Troestler
[
GGT1
]
. Consider the reflexion
\hat{v}
across the equator
\left\{x_{n}=0\right\}
of the sphere
\mathbb{S}^{n}
defined by

\hat{v}\left(x\right):=v\left(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n},-x_{n+1}\right)\quad\forall x=\left(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n},x_{n+1}\right)\in\mathbb{S}^{n}

for all functions
v:\mathbb{S}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
. By stereographic projection along with a conformal change of metric, this corresponds to the Kelvin transform in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. We let
N_{0}:=\left(0,\ldots,0,1\right)
and
j_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
be such that

\left(q-2\right)\beta_{2}\left(0\right)=\lambda_{j_{0}}:=j_{0}\left(j_{0}+n-1\right)

i.e. the
j_{0}
-th eigenvalue of
\Delta_{g_{0}}
on
\mathbb{S}^{n}
(the existence of
j_{0}
is given by the assumption (A3)). We then define
\mathcal{H}_{1}
and
\mathcal{H}_{2}
as

\mathcal{H}_{1}:=\left\{v\in C^{1,\theta}\left(\mathbb{S}^{n}\right):\hbox{$v$ is radial with respect to $N_{0}$ and $\hat{v}=v$}\right\}

and

\mathcal{H}_{2}:=\left\{\hskip-4.0pt\begin{aligned} &\left\{v\in C^{1,\theta}\left(\mathbb{S}^{n}\right):\hbox{$v$ is radial w.r.t.~$N_{0}$ and $\hat{v}=v$}\right\}\,\hbox{if $j_{0}$ is even}\\
&\left\{v\in C^{1,\theta}\left(\mathbb{S}^{n}\right):\hbox{$v$ is radial w.r.t.~$N_{0}$ and $\hat{v}=-v$}\right\}\,\hbox{if $j_{0}$ is odd}.\end{aligned}\right.

For
i=1,2
, we take
\mathcal{U}_{i}:=C^{1,\theta}\left(\mathbb{S}^{n},\left(-\delta_{0},\delta_{0}\right)\right)\cap\mathcal{H}_{i}
. For every
\left(v_{1},v_{2}\right)\in\mathcal{U}
, letting
\left(u_{1},u_{2}\right)
be as in (
2.7
), we then obtain

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\hat{u}_{1}\\
\hat{u}_{2}\end{array}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\overline{u}_{1}\left(\alpha\right)\\
\overline{u}_{2}\left(\alpha\right)\end{array}\right)+\frac{1}{2}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\hat{v}_{1}+\hat{v}_{2}\\
\hat{v}_{1}-\hat{v}_{2}\end{array}\right)

\displaystyle=\left\{\begin{aligned} &\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\overline{u}_{1}\left(\alpha\right)\\
\overline{u}_{2}\left(\alpha\right)\end{array}\right)+\frac{1}{2}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}v_{1}+v_{2}\\
v_{1}-v_{2}\end{array}\right)=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}u_{1}\\
u_{2}\end{array}\right)&&\text{if }j_{0}\text{ is even}\\
&\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\overline{u}_{1}\left(\alpha\right)\\
\overline{u}_{2}\left(\alpha\right)\end{array}\right)+\frac{1}{2}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}v_{1}-v_{2}\\
v_{1}+v_{2}\end{array}\right)=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}u_{2}\\
u_{1}\end{array}\right)&&\text{if }j_{0}\text{ is odd}.\end{aligned}\right.

It follows that

\displaystyle F\left(\alpha,\hat{v}_{1},\hat{v}_{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}F_{1}\left(\alpha,\hat{v}_{1},\hat{v}_{2}\right)\\
F_{2}\left(\alpha,\hat{v}_{1},\hat{v}_{2}\right)\end{array}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&1\\
1&-1\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{c}a\left(\alpha\right)\hat{u}_{1}^{q-1}+b\left(\alpha\right)\hat{u}_{2}^{q-2}\hat{u}_{1}-\lambda\left(\alpha\right)\hat{u}_{1}\\
b\left(\alpha\right)\hat{u}_{1}^{q-2}\hat{u}_{2}+a\left(\alpha\right)\hat{u}_{2}^{q-1}-\lambda\left(\alpha\right)\hat{u}_{2}\end{array}\right)

\displaystyle=\left\{\begin{aligned} &F\left(\alpha,v_{1},v_{2}\right)&&\text{if }j_{0}\text{ is even}\\
&\left(\begin{array}[]{c}F_{1}\left(\alpha,v_{1},v_{2}\right)\\
-F_{2}\left(\alpha,v_{1},v_{2}\right)\end{array}\right)&&\text{if }j_{0}\text{ is odd}.\end{aligned}\right.

This along with standard elliptic regularity and symmetry arguments gives that the condition (C3) is satisfied.

Recall that the spherical harmonics
\varphi
satisfying
\Delta_{g_{0}}\varphi=\lambda_{j_{0}}\varphi
are given by the restriction to
\mathbb{S}^{n}
of the harmonic polynomials of degree
j_{0}
in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
. In particular, up to a constant factor, the unique such function
\varphi_{N_{0},j_{0}}
that is radial with respect to
N_{0}
is given by the Jacobi polynomial

\varphi_{N_{0},j_{0}}\left(x_{1},\dotsc,x_{n+1}\right):=\sum_{j=0}^{j_{0}}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}j_{0}+\left(n-2\right)/2\\
j\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{c}j_{0}+\left(n-2\right)/2\\
j_{0}-j\end{array}\right)\\
\times\left(\frac{x_{n+1}-1}{2}\right)^{j_{0}-j}\left(\frac{x_{n+1}+1}{2}\right)^{j}\quad\forall x\in\mathbb{S}^{n}

(see Gladiali
[
G
]
). So then the assumptions (A2) and (A3) give that
\mathcal{H}^{*}_{1}=\left\{0\right\}
and
\mathcal{H}^{*}_{2}
has dimension one. In particular, we obtain that the condition (C4) is satisfied. Furthermore, the condition (C5) follows from (A3).

By applying Theorem
2.3
, we then obtain that there exists a
C^{1}
branch of solutions to (
2.2
) emanating from
\left(0,\overline{u}\left(0\right),\overline{u}\left(0\right)\right)
whose tangent line at
\left(0,\overline{u}\left(0\right),\overline{u}\left(0\right)\right)
is directed by some vector of the form
\left(\mu,\varphi,-\varphi\right)
, where
\mu\in\mathbb{R}
and
\varphi\in\mathcal{H}_{2}^{*}\backslash\left\{0\right\}
. In particular, we obtain that near
\left(0,\overline{u}\left(0\right),\overline{u}\left(0\right)\right)
, the solutions on this branch are non-synchronized, which completes the proof of Theorem
1.1
(iii).
∎

3.

In this section, we prove the following results, which extend Theorem
1.1
to more general systems and manifolds:

Theorem 3.1
.

(Case
\lambda_{1}=\lambda_{2}
)
Let
\lambda,a_{11},a_{12},a_{21},a_{22}\in\mathbb{R}
,
q\in\left(2,\infty\right)
and
\left(M,g\right)
be a smooth, closed Riemannian manifold. Consider the system (
1.2
) with
\lambda_{1}=\lambda_{2}=\lambda
.

(i)

If either [

and
a_{22}\leq a_{12}
] or [
a_{11}\leq a_{21}
and
a_{12}\leq a_{22}
] and at least one of the two inequalities is strict, then (

) has no solutions.

(ii)

If either [

and
a_{22}<a_{12}
] or [
a_{11}=a_{21}
and
a_{22}=a_{12}
], then every solution of (

(iii)

Assuming that there exists at least one non-zero eigenvalue of

with odd multiplicity, Theorem
2.2
provides examples of real numbers

such that

,

and (

Theorem 3.2
.

(Case

(ii)

Proof of Theorem
3.1
(i) and Theorem
3.2
(i).

Suppose that there exists a solution
\left(u_{1},u_{2}\right)
of (
1.2
). We define

v\left(x\right):=\frac{u_{1}\left(x\right)}{u_{2}\left(x\right)}\qquad\forall x\in M.

We then obtain

\displaystyle\Delta_{g}v=\frac{\Delta_{g}u_{1}}{u_{2}}+2\frac{\left<\nabla_{g}u_{1},\nabla_{g}u_{2}\right>_{g}}{u_{2}^{2}}-\frac{u_{1}\Delta_{g}u_{2}}{u_{2}^{2}}-2\frac{u_{1}\left|\nabla_{g}u_{2}\right|^{2}_{g}}{u_{2}^{3}}

\displaystyle=\left(a_{11}-a_{21}\right)u_{1}^{q-2}v+\left(a_{12}-a_{22}\right)u_{2}^{q-2}v+\left(\lambda_{2}-\lambda_{1}\right)v+2\frac{\left<\nabla_{g}v,\nabla_{g}u_{2}\right>_{g}}{u_{2}}

in
M
. In the case where
a_{21}\leq a_{11}
,
a_{22}\leq a_{12}
,
\lambda_{1}\leq\lambda_{2}
and one of these inequalities is strict, we then obtain

\Delta_{g}v>2\left<\nabla_{g}v,\nabla_{g}\left[\ln u_{2}\right]\right>_{g}\quad\text{in }M,

which is in contradiction with the minimum principle. Similarly, in the case where
a_{11}\leq a_{21}
,
a_{12}\leq a_{22}
,
\lambda_{2}\leq\lambda_{1}
and one of these inequalities is strict, we obtain

\Delta_{g}v<2\left<\nabla_{g}v,\nabla_{g}\left[\ln u_{2}\right]\right>_{g}\quad\text{in }M,

which is in contradiction with the maximum principle. This proves Theorem
3.1
(i) and Theorem
3.2
(i).
∎

Proof of Theorem
3.1
(ii).

Suppose first that
a_{11}<a_{21}
,
a_{22}<a_{12}
and
\lambda_{1}=\lambda_{2}
. Let
v
be as in the previous proof and

v\left(x_{1}\right)=\min\left\{v\left(x\right):\ x\in M\right\}\quad\text{and}\quad v\left(x_{2}\right)=\max\left\{v\left(x\right):\ x\in M\right\}.

We then obtain

0\geq\Delta_{g}v\left(x_{1}\right)=\left[\left(a_{12}-a_{22}\right)u_{2}\left(x_{1}\right)^{q-2}-\left(a_{21}-a_{11}\right)u_{1}\left(x_{1}\right)^{q-2}\right]v\left(x_{1}\right)

and

which imply

v\left(x_{2}\right)\leq\left(\frac{a_{12}-a_{22}}{a_{21}-a_{11}}\right)^{1/\left(q-2\right)}\leq v\left(x_{1}\right).

It follows that

Now suppose that

3.1

∎

Nuclear incompressibility and sound speed in uniform matter and finite nuclei
G. Grams, R. Somasundaram, J. Margueron, E. Khan

Abstract
We have extended the compressible liquid-drop model (CLDM) with a density-dependent surface term (eCLDM), which allows for a unified description of both the nuclear ground state energies and the incompressibility modulus in finite nuclei K A K_{A} . We analyse the role of the nuclear empirical parameters, e.g., K sat K_{\mathrm{sat}} , Q sat Q_{\mathrm{sat}} , L sym L_{\mathrm{sym}} and K sym K_{\mathrm{sym}} , which contribute to the bulk properties, as well as the role of the finite size contributions. For the bulk properties, the density and isospin dependencies of the nuclear incompressibility in infinite matter are characterized by introducing new empirical parameters, and two new constraints for the value of K sym K_{\mathrm{sym}} are suggested. For finite nuclei, we employ a Bayesian approach coupled to a Markov-Chain Monte-Carlo (MCMC) exploration of the parameter space to confront the model predictions of K A K_{A} in Zr, Sn and Pb isotopes to the experimental data. We show that Q sat ≈ − 950 ± 200 Q_{\mathrm{sat}}\approx-950\pm 200 MeV describes the experimental measurements of K A K_{A} in these isotopes. This value is different from the ones deduced from phenomenological nuclear energy density functionals, suggesting a possible explanation of their difficulty to accurately describe Zr, Sn and Pb data all together. In addition we explore the impact of a fictitious measurement of the Giant Monopole Resonance energy in 132 Sn. We show that this measurement, provided it is accurate enough, will allow to better determine K sym K_{\mathrm{sym}} and K τ K_{\tau} . Finally we explore the properties of the sound speed around saturation density and show the important role of finite size terms in finite nuclei since they reduce the sound speed to approximately half compared to nuclear matter.



I
Introduction

The response of nuclear matter to compression and expansion plays a very important role in many phenomena in nature, from finite nuclei
van der Woude and Harakeh 2001
, which can be viewed as non-uniform pieces of nuclear matter squeezed by the effects of the surface terms, up to astrophysical nuclear systems such as neutron stars, supernovae or kilonovae
Rezzolla et al. 2018
, where nuclear matter explores densities and isospin asymmetries in extreme regimes. In finite nuclei, the repulsive surface tension and the Coulomb interaction counter balance the attractive bulk nuclear force and allow the exploration of densities close to the saturation density of nuclear matter (
n_{\mathrm{sat}}\approx 0.155
fm
-3

Margueron et al. 2018a
), while in compact stars, the bulk nuclear force resists gravity for densities corresponding to several times saturation density. In these examples, the equilibrium states of these systems represent a balance between the bulk properties and the action of external forces (finite size terms or gravitational force). It is then important to quantify precisely the response of bulk nuclear matter (incompressibility) from analyses of finite nuclei properties (giant monopole resonances), which is the scope of the present study.

At first order, the energy required to compress matter from its equilibrium state, is given by the incompressibility modulus
K_{\mathrm{sat}}
with an isospin asymmetry dependence driven by the parameter
K_{\tau}

Blaizot 1980
:
K_{\mathrm{sat}}+\delta^{2}K_{\tau}
. These nuclear empirical parameters could be extracted from the analysis of the isoscalar giant monopole resonance (ISGMR), excited by the scattering of alpha particles, see for instance Ref.
Garg and Coló 2018
and references therein. The relation between the energy of the ISGMR,
E_{\mathrm{ISGMR}}
, and the incompressibility in finite nuclei
K_{A}
is
Garg and Coló 2018
,

E_{\mathrm{ISGMR}}=\hbar\sqrt{\frac{K_{A}}{m_{N}\langle r^{2}\rangle}}\simeq\hbar\sqrt{\frac{5K_{A}}{3m_{N}R_{A}}}\,,

(1)

where
m_{N}
is the nucleon mass and
\langle r^{2}\rangle
is the mean square radius of the density distribution in finite nuclei. The last expression is obtained assuming a flat density distribution up to
R_{A}
, as in the compressible liquid-drop model (CLDM). Considering a leptodermous expansion as in the liquid-drop model, the incompressibility modulus in finite nuclei
K_{A}
can be expressed as
Blaizot 1980

K_{A}=K_{\mathrm{sat}}+K_{\tau}\delta^{2}+K_{\mathrm{Coul}}\frac{Z^{2}}{A^{4/3}}+K_{\mathrm{surf}}A^{-1/3}+\dots

(2)

where
K_{\mathrm{sat}}
and
K_{\tau}
are the bulk contributions which we aim to extract from experimental data,
K_{\mathrm{Coul}}
is the Coulomb repulsive contribution and
K_{\mathrm{surf}}
the surface attractive contribution. This leptodermous expansion is however difficult to employ for the determination of
K_{\mathrm{sat}}
and
K_{\tau}
from experimental measurements of
E_{\mathrm{ISGMR}}
, as Eqs. (
1
) and (
2
) may suggest, especially since the term
K_{\mathrm{surf}}
is difficult to fix from the few existing experimental data. The situation is different in the case of the leptodermous expansion of the nuclear mass, since more than 2000 nuclei have been measured
Huang et al. 2021
. In a recent analysis
Li and Xie 2021
, precise values for
K_{\mathrm{sat}}
and
K_{\tau}
have been obtained from Eq. (
2
), fixing
K_{\mathrm{surf}}=cK_{\mathrm{sat}}
(with
c\approx-1.2\pm 0.12

Patra et al. 2002
) and
K_{\mathrm{Coul}}\approx-5.2\pm 0.7
MeV
Sagawa et al. 2007
. This indicates that the key quantity which would allow the use of such an empirical relation is the surface term. In this paper, we investigate the impact of the surface term in the CLDM framework and its role to reproduce experimental data.

By using the energy density functional approach, the first precise extraction of
K_{\mathrm{sat}}
gave
K_{\mathrm{sat}}=210\pm 30
MeV
Blaizot 1980
, corrected to
240\pm 20
MeV later on as a good compromise between data in
208
Pb and
90
Zr
Garg and Coló 2018
. The isospin dependence of the incompressibility
K_{\tau}
is more difficult to determine from experimental data. It has recently been extracted from systematical exploration of Sn isotopic chain, giving
K_{\tau}\approx-550\pm 100
MeV
Garg and Coló 2018
. However, there are several unsettled questions: by using non-magic nuclei, the analysis of the data requires the understanding of many-body correlations (pairing, deformation, etc.) on the incompressibility of finite nuclei. The question of the isoscalar and isovector properties of the incompressibility is also important since the density and the isospin asymmetry distributions in finite nuclei are different from one to another. In addition, a systematic difference between the incompressibility extracted from
208
Pb and from
120
Sn (which tends toward
K_{\mathrm{sat}}\simeq 205
MeV
Avogadro and Bertulani 2013
) remains which origin is still not well understood. This issue could be of similar origin as the systematical dispersion for
K_{\mathrm{sat}}
obtained by using different models, where
K_{\mathrm{sat}}\approx 220
MeV is preferred by Gogny forces
Goriely et al. 2009
while non-linear relativistic mean field models favor
K_{\mathrm{sat}}\approx 250
MeV
Lalazissis et al. 2005
. It was suggested that these systematical differences could be related to the different density dependence of the models, encoded in the nuclear empirical parameter (NEP)
Q_{\mathrm{sat}}

Khan et al. 2012
;
Khan and Margueron 2013
. It is indeed a general result that a large uncertainty on a high order NEP impacts the precise determination of lower order ones
Margueron and Gulminelli 2019
.

In uniform matter (UM), the incompressibility
K_{\mathrm{UM}}
is defined as the second derivative of the energy density
\epsilon_{\mathrm{UM}}=E_{\mathrm{UM}}/V
as

\displaystyle K_{\mathrm{UM}}(n,\delta)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 9n\frac{\partial^{2}\epsilon_{\mathrm{UM}}(n,\delta)}{\partial n^{2}}\,,

(3)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{18}{n}P_{\mathrm{UM}}(n,\delta)+9n^{2}\frac{\partial^{2}e_{\mathrm{UM}}(n,\delta)}{\partial n^{2}}\,,

(4)

where
n
is the isoscalar density
n=n_{n}+n_{p}
and
\delta
the isovector parameter
\delta=(n_{n}-n_{p})/n
, the energy per particle is
e_{\mathrm{UM}}=\epsilon_{\mathrm{UM}}/n
, and the pressure
P_{\mathrm{UM}}
is defined as

P_{\mathrm{UM}}=n^{2}\frac{\partial e_{\mathrm{UM}}}{\partial n}\,.

(5)

Note that
K_{\mathrm{UM}}=K_{\mathrm{sat}}
if
n=n_{\mathrm{sat}}
and
\delta=0
. In finite nuclei, the isovector parameter is noted
\delta_{A}=(N-Z)/A
.

In the absence of external forces, such as gravity for instance, matter minimizes its energy (mechanical equilibrium) by imposing
P_{\mathrm{UM}}=0
. We note
n_{\mathrm{eq}}^{\mathrm{UM}}
the equilibrium density in symmetric (SM) and isospin asymmetric (AM) matter. The latter always deals with small isospin asymmetries
|\delta_{A}|\lesssim 0.3
as expected in finite nuclei. In nuclear matter and at equilibrium, the first term in Eq. (
4
) vanishes but in finite nuclei however the equilibrium density
n_{\mathrm{eq}}^{A}
is slightly different from the one in uniform matter
n_{\mathrm{eq}}^{\mathrm{UM}}
, due to the presence of finite size terms which contribute to the pressure. This effect shifts
n_{\mathrm{eq}}^{\mathrm{UM}}
by about 10% at maximum and impacts the value of the bulk incompressibility in finite nuclei. One could then view the finite size terms as an ”external” force probing the response of the bulk. Consequently, there is a contribution of the finite size terms to the incompressibility in finite nuclei, in addition to the density and isospin asymmetry dependence of the bulk term
Blaizot 1980
. In addition, the equilibrium density
n_{\mathrm{eq}}^{A}
in finite nuclei varies around
n_{\mathrm{eq}}^{\mathrm{UM}}
through the nuclear chart, modifying the value of the energy in the bulk. Since this value is controlled at first order by the incompressibility modulus, the energy of finite nuclei in their ground state also contains a contribution originating from the incompressibility of nuclear matter, in addition to the symmetry energy and to the finite size terms. This contribution is difficult to extract from microscopic approaches, e.g., energy density functional, shell model approaches, as well as ab-initio ones, but it could be more visible in macroscopic models such the CLDM that we employ in this study. The fact that the fluctuations in
n_{\mathrm{eq}}^{A}
impact both the energy
e_{A}
and the incompressibility
K_{A}
requires to employ a model which could describe these two quantities in a unique framework. This is the motivation for the development of the eCLDM that we present in this paper.

The CLDM has been shown relevant to describe nuclear masses
Myers and Swiatecki 1969
;
Weiss and Cameron 1969
and was also employed to study the clusterized matter present on neutron star crusts
Baym et al. 1971
;
Steiner 2008
;
Carreau et al. 2019
;
Grams et al. 2022a
;
Grams et al. 2022b
. Many variations of the model can be found in the literature, however, it has been argued by Blaizot
Blaizot 1980
that the CLDM is not appropriate to accurately extract the incompressibility modulus
K_{\mathrm{sat}}
from finite nuclei. The reason lies in the contribution of the density dependent surface term to the incompressibility, which is absent in most of the macroscopic models. In the present work, we however construct an extended CLDM (eCLDM) with a density-dependent surface tension allowing to describe both nuclear masses and incompressibilities. Furthermore, the bulk term of the present model is described with the meta-model
Margueron et al. 2018a
, an energy density functional in which the parameters of the model are the empirical parameters of nuclear matter. The meta-model has the advantage of being flexible enough to allow an independent variation of the NEP and can thus be used to easily perform a sensitivity analysis of the individual impact of the NEP on the incompressibility
K_{A}
, as well as extensive searches of the best parameter sets reproducing experimental data.

The paper is organized as follow: In Sec. II we explore the incompressibility modulus in nuclear matter in terms of the NEP, or equivalently as a function of the density and the isospin asymmetry. A new constraint on
K_{\mathrm{sym}}
is derived and compared to other existing ones. Following the line suggested by Blaizot
Blaizot 1980
, we then address finite nuclei in Sec. III as described by our eCLDM model (with a density dependent surface tension), which allows to reproduce both finite nuclei and incompressibility modulus from the same approach. In Sect. IV we compare the predictions of the eCLDM to experimental data and analyse the role of the NEP
K_{\mathrm{sat}}
,
Q_{\mathrm{sat}}
,
L_{\mathrm{sym}}
and
K_{\mathrm{sym}}
in a Bayesian framework. Finally, in Sec. V we discuss the sound speed in both uniform matter and finite nuclei.

\delta

II.2
An alternative representation of the nuclear matter energy and incompressibility modulus

In this section, we explore an alternative representation of the uniform matter properties, where the equilibrium density
n_{\mathrm{eq}}
is taken, in place of the saturation density
n_{\mathrm{sat}}
. This alternative representation is equivalent to the existing one up to
\delta^{2}
, but generates non-quadratic terms. In the view of constraining uniform matter parameters from measurements of incompressibilities in nuclei, it may be more relevant to consider such a representation: the equilibrium density in uniform matter shall be closer – than the saturation density – to the average one of the nucleus
Khan et al. 2012
;
Khan and Margueron 2013
.

In this alternative approach, the associated density parameter is set to be
\tilde{x}=(n-n_{\mathrm{eq}}^{\mathrm{UM}})/(3n_{\mathrm{eq}}^{\mathrm{UM}})

n/n_{\mathrm{eq}}^{\mathrm{UM}}=1+3\tilde{x}

The alternative density parameter

\displaystyle\tilde{x}=\frac{x+(L_{\mathrm{sym}}/K_{\mathrm{sat}})\delta^{2}}{1-3(L_{\mathrm{sym}}/K_{\mathrm{sat}})\delta^{2}}\,.

\displaystyle e(\tilde{x}=0,\delta)=E_{\mathrm{sat}}+E_{\tau}\delta^{2}\,,

\displaystyle\tilde{Q}(\delta)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{3}e(\tilde{x},\delta)}{\partial\tilde{x}^{3}}\Bigr\rvert_{\tilde{x}=0}=Q_{\mathrm{sat}}+Q_{\tau}\delta^{2}\,,

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{4}e(\tilde{x},\delta)}{\partial\tilde{x}^{4}}\Bigr\rvert_{\tilde{x}=0}=Z_{\mathrm{sat}}+Z_{\tau}\delta^{2}\,.

II.3
Constraints on
K_{\mathrm{sym}}

From the existence of a lower bound on the energy of NM, on the basis of unitary-gas considerations, the following constraint on
K_{\mathrm{sym}}
was obtained
Tews et al. 2017
,

K_{\mathrm{sym}}\approx-306.0+3.41L_{\mathrm{sym}}\pm 28.3\hbox{ MeV }\,,

(47)

when models with
K_{\mathrm{sat}}>275
MeV are excluded. Considering
L_{\mathrm{sym}}\approx 50
MeV for instance, this constraint imposes
K_{\mathrm{sym}}\geqslant\simeq-150
MeV (see Table
1
).

In Eq. (
47
), the coefficients of the correlation are obtained from a fit to a given set of model realizations. In the following, we will demonstrate the existence of a lower limit from purely theoretical considerations.

It is possible to express the equilibrium density from
K_{\mathrm{sym}}
, by solving the mechanical stability condition
\partial e_{\mathrm{UM}}(x,\delta)/\partial x=0
, with an expansion of the energy to
x^{3}
and beyond
\delta^{2}
approximation. The physical solution of this second order equation is

\displaystyle x_{\mathrm{eq},2}^{\mathrm{UM}}(\delta)=\frac{K(\delta)}{Q(\delta)}\left[-1+\sqrt{1-2\frac{L_{\mathrm{sym}}Q(\delta)}{K(\delta)^{2}}\delta^{2}}\right]\,,

(48)

satisfying the limit
n_{\mathrm{eq},2}^{\mathrm{UM}}\rightarrow n_{\mathrm{sat}}
as
\delta\rightarrow 0
. Eq. (
48
) is well defined if
K(\delta)^{2}\geq 2L_{\mathrm{sym}}Q(\delta)\delta^{2}
for all values of
\delta
for which equilibrium density is defined, which is ranging from SM to very asymmetric matter. There is no equilibrium density in NM, but there is still an equilibrium very close to NM. Since Eq. (
48
) weakly depends on
\delta
for isospin asymmetries close to NM, we fix
\delta=1
in Eq. (
48
) for simplicity. We then obtain:
K_{\mathrm{sym}}\geq-K_{\mathrm{sat}}+\sqrt{2L_{\mathrm{sym}}Q(\delta=1)}
or
K_{\mathrm{sym}}\leq-K_{\mathrm{sat}}-\sqrt{2L_{\mathrm{sym}}Q(\delta=1)}
. Considering typical values for the NEPs extracted from Table
1
,
\sqrt{2L_{\mathrm{sym}}Q(\delta=1)}\sim 70-100
MeV, so the previous condition gives
K_{\mathrm{sym}}\gtrsim-150
MeV or
K_{\mathrm{sym}}\lesssim-350
MeV. Since the second case is excluded by the constraint on
K_{\mathrm{sym}}
given by considerations based on the unitary-gas
Tews et al. 2017
, we are then left with the first condition alone:

K_{\mathrm{sym}}\geq-K_{\mathrm{sat}}+\sqrt{2L_{\mathrm{sym}}Q(\delta=1)}\sim-150\hbox{ MeV}\,.

(49)

Note that using the model averaged values of
Q_{\mathrm{sat}}
and

, we have the following condition:
Q(\delta)>0
for all
\delta
, constraining

and

, as
Q_{\mathrm{sym}}>-Q_{\mathrm{sat}}
. However, this relation is not always satisfied, as shown in table

.

Other estimates of

III
The compressible liquid drop model with a density dependence of the surface tension

The CLDM has been originally developed on top of the liquid drop model, where the bulk term is a constant
Myers and Swiatecki 1969
. In the CLDM
Myers and Swiatecki 1969
;
Weiss and Cameron 1969
, the bulk term is density-dependent and the density is fixed variationally by the mechanical stability condition. In the present approach, we suggest an extension of the CLDM by introducing a density-dependent surface term. We show that the present eCLDM could describe accurately both the energy of finite nuclei in their ground state as well as the ISGMR energy.

III.1
Density dependent surface tension

The novelty of the present work is the introduction of a density dependent surface tension, which is expressed as,

\displaystyle\sigma_{\mathrm{surf}}(n_{\mathrm{cl}},I_{\mathrm{cl}})=\sigma_{\mathrm{surf}}(I_{\mathrm{cl}})\left[1+a_{\mathrm{surf}}f(A_{\mathrm{cl}})x_{\mathrm{cl}}^{2}\right]\,,

(50)

where
x_{\mathrm{cl}}=(n_{\mathrm{cl}}-n_{\mathrm{sat}})/3n_{\mathrm{sat}}
and the parameter
a_{\mathrm{surf}}
controls the density dependence of the surface energy. In practice, it encodes the deviation from
n_{\mathrm{sat}}
. It is then larger for nuclei for which
n_{\mathrm{cl}}
is farther from
n_{\mathrm{sat}}
, i.e. for light and intermediate mass nuclei as well as for exotic nuclei. In Sec.
A.3
we suggest a way to estimate
a_{\mathrm{surf}}
by using a single microscopic calculation of
K_{A}
in
100
Sn.

In Eq. (
50
), the function
f(A_{\mathrm{cl}})
is defined as,

f(A_{\mathrm{cl}})=\frac{1}{1+\exp[-(A_{\mathrm{cl}}-A_{0})/A_{w}]},

(51)

where
A_{\mathrm{cl}}
is the mass number of the considered nucleus. This function has been introduced to suppress the density dependence of the surface tension in light nuclei, where it appears to be unrealistically too large. From a qualitative study, we suggest the following values for the parameters of the function
f
:
A_{0}=70
and
A_{w}=10
.

It should be noted that we have chosen the exponent of the density-dependent term in Eq. (

Blaizot 1980

The sound speed is an important property in transport models
Danielewicz et al. 2002
. It is interesting to address the effects of the nuclear properties, e.g., NEPs, on the sound speed in uniform matter and in finite nuclei. Moreover, the sound speed is an important ingredient in the calculation of the tidal deformation in binary neutron stars
Hinderer 2008
;
Flanagan and Hinderer 2008
;
Somasundaram et al. 2021b
;
Han and Steiner 2019
. Therefore a connection between the sound speeds in finite nuclei and infinite matter could help in constraining NS observables from nuclear experiments.

The sound speed

in a nuclear fluid is largely determined from the nuclear incompressibility in asymmetric matter. It is defined as
Blaizot 1980

c_{s}^{2}=\frac{K(n,\delta)}{9h(n,\delta)}\,,

(56)

where
h
is the enthalpy per particle
h=mc^{2}+e+P/n
. In uniform matter, we determine the sound speed from the following quantities
e_{\mathrm{UM}}
(
9
),
P_{\mathrm{UM}}
(
5
) and
K_{\mathrm{UM}}
(
4
), while in finite nuclei, we use
e_{A}
(
57
),
P_{A}
(
65
) and
K_{A}
(
71
). All these quantities have been defined in previous sections and in the appendix A.

We show in Fig.
8
the correlation between the speed-of-sound in NM at
n_{\mathrm{sat}}

. We have used the posterior distributions corresponding to the three cases: dist1f, dist2f and dist3f for all the NEP (here

,

and
L_{\mathrm{sym}}
). The findings of Fig.

K_{\mathrm{sym}}
. With the advent of ab-initio calculations such as

EFT
Drischler et al. 2020

It is also relevant to explore the correlation between the sound-speed and

VI
Conclusions

In this work we have explored various ways to encode the density and isospin asymmetry dependence of the incompressibility in nuclear matter. We have discussed the dominant contribution of
L_{\mathrm{sym}}
in the determination of the isospin dependence of the incompressibility modulus. A better knowledge of the incompressibility modulus in AM requires therefore an accurate knowledge of
L_{\mathrm{sym}}
. In finite nuclei, by introducing an extended CLDM (eCLDM) adding a density dependence to the surface tension proportional to
x_{\mathrm{cl}}^{2}
, where
x_{\mathrm{cl}}=(n_{\mathrm{cl}}-n_{\mathrm{sat}})/3n_{\mathrm{sat}}
, we were able to provide a unified macroscopic model for nuclear masses and incompressibility modulus. We have then rederived
K_{A}
from the eCLDM framework along the lines originally suggested by Blaizot
Blaizot 1980
. In this way, the contribution of the new density-dependent term, in the surface tension to
K_{A}
, is explicitly shown in the equations.

We have compared the predictions of the eCLDM for the nucleus incompressibility
K_{A}
with microscopic calculations and experimental data. Thanks to the flexibility of the meta-model, a sensitivity analysis on the impact of individual nuclear empirical parameter is made. As expected, the isoscalar channel influences the absolute values of the energies while the isoscalar one impact the slope of the
K_{A}
as function of the isospin asymmetry. A full exploration in the parameter space formed by
K_{\mathrm{sat}}
,
Q_{\mathrm{sat}}
,
L_{\mathrm{sym}}
and
K_{\mathrm{sym}}
is also performed, showing that the parameter
Q_{\mathrm{sat}}
must be approximately
Q_{\mathrm{sat}}\approx 950\pm 200
MeV to reconcile the experimental GMR energies measured in Zr, SN and Pb isotopes. Since this suggested value is different from the ones of phenomenological forces, we then suggest a possible explanation of the origin of the difficulties these forces faces in reproducing the experimental data on
K_{A}
, on both Sn and Pb nuclei.

In addition, we explore the impact of a fictitious accurate measurement for the GMR energy in
132
Sn. We show that with such a measurement, the value of
K_{\mathrm{sym}}
and
K_{\tau}
would be much better determined than they are with the present data.

We have also derived two new constraints on
K_{\mathrm{sym}}
:

•

From the equilibrium density:
K_{\mathrm{sym}}\geq-K_{\mathrm{sat}}+\sqrt{2L_{\mathrm{sym}}Q(\delta=1)}\sim-150
MeV and
Q_{\mathrm{sym}}>-Q_{\mathrm{sat}}
.

•

From the confrontation of our prediction for the sound speed in NM with the
\chi
EFT, we found
-200\lesssim K_{\mathrm{sym}}\lesssim 50
MeV. This constraint could be more accurate if a measurement of the GMR energy in
132
Sn is known.

Let us remark that the constraint on
Q_{\mathrm{sym}}
combined with the MCMC exploration for
Q_{\mathrm{sat}}
leads to the following consequence:
Q_{\mathrm{sym}}\gtrsim 950\pm 200
MeV.

In conclusion, the present work suggests a new way to analyze the experimental
K_{A}
and to extract the values of the NEP
K_{\mathrm{sat}}
,
Q_{\mathrm{sat}}
,
L_{\mathrm{sym}}
and
K_{\mathrm{sym}}
, which are the most influential ones. This method is comparable to the microscopic Hartree-Fock one, except that it does not describe shell effects. These shell effects are however reduced by the treatment of the pairing, as shown in the microscopic Hartree-Fock Bogoliubov calculations
Garg and Coló 2018
. The advantage of our method is that we use the flexible nuclear meta-model to simulate the role of the nuclear interaction. At variance with phenomenological forces, the nuclear meta-model is able to freely choose the best NEP which describe the experimental data. We found that the data favors a large and negative value for
Q_{\mathrm{sat}}
which is not possible with phenomenological forces. We then suggest a possible origin for the observed limitations of these forces.

We note that our ability to extract information on
Q_{\mathrm{sat}}
and
K_{\mathrm{sym}}
from finite nuclei, is based on the variation in density and isospin asymmetry explored by the isotopes defining the loss function
\chi_{E}
. For simplicity we have based our analysis on the results of an eCLDM where the densities and isospin asymmetries are taken flat in finite nuclei. This is clearly an important feature which has to be improved in the future. One may think for instance in implementing the meta-model in a modeling of finite nuclei with better density profiles compared to the eCLDM. Further works in this direction are therefore envisioned.

A.2
Pressure in finite nuclei:
P_{A}

The pressure
P_{A}
in finite nuclei is defined as,

P_{A}\equiv n_{\mathrm{cl}}^{2}\frac{\partial e_{A}}{\partial n_{\mathrm{cl}}}\Bigr\rvert_{A},

(65)

which can be decomposed into a bulk term, originating from uniform matter and a finite size contribution:
P_{A}=P_{A,\mathrm{UM}}+P_{A,\mathrm{FS}}
. The bulk term is decomposed into SM and isospin asymmetry contributions, as in Eq. (
5
):
P_{A,\mathrm{UM}}=P_{A,\mathrm{SM}}+P_{A,\mathrm{sym}}\delta^{2}
, taking
P_{A,\mathrm{SM}}=P_{\mathrm{SM}}(n_{\mathrm{cl}})
and
P_{A,\mathrm{sym}}=P_{\mathrm{sym}}(n_{\mathrm{cl}})
.

Note that the functions of
R_{A}
in the binding energy also contribute to the pressure as,

P_{A}=-\frac{R_{A}n_{\mathrm{cl}}}{3}\frac{\partial e_{A}}{\partial R_{A}}\Bigr|_{A}\,,

(66)

since the partial derivative w.r.t.
n_{\mathrm{cl}}
at fixed
A
is equivalent to a partial derivative w.r.t.
R_{A}
, with appropriate factor, see appendix
D
.

The finite size pressure is
P_{A,\mathrm{FS}}=P_{A,\mathrm{surf}}+P_{A,\mathrm{Coul}}
, with the Coulomb pressure term derived as

P_{A,\mathrm{Coul}}=\frac{\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{Coul}}}{5}\frac{Z^{2}e^{2}n_{\mathrm{cl}}}{R_{A}}\frac{1}{A}.

(67)

The surface term is decomposed into two contributions

P_{A,\mathrm{surf}}=P_{A,\mathrm{surf}}^{\mathrm{CLDM}}+P_{A,\mathrm{surf}}^{\mathrm{DD}}

(68)

where the first term is the usual CLDM contribution, while the second term originates from the new density-dependent (DD) term. They are defined as,

\displaystyle P_{A,\mathrm{surf}}^{\mathrm{CLDM}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{surf}}}{3}\,8\pi\sigma_{\mathrm{surf}}R_{A}^{2}n_{\mathrm{cl}}\frac{1}{A}\,,

(69)

\displaystyle P_{A,\mathrm{surf}}^{\mathrm{DD}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{surf}}\,4\pi R_{A}^{2}n_{\mathrm{cl}}^{2}\frac{\partial\sigma_{\mathrm{surf}}}{\partial n_{\mathrm{cl}}}\frac{1}{A}\,.

(70)

Note that since
\partial\sigma_{\mathrm{surf}}/\partial n_{\mathrm{cl}}\propto x_{\mathrm{cl}}\approx 0
, the contribution of the new DD term to the pressure is small.

Numerically, the cluster density
n_{\mathrm{cl}}
is obtained from the mechanical stability condition
P_{A}=0
, using the Newton-Raphson algorithm with
n_{\mathrm{eq}}^{\mathrm{UM}}
as starting solution.

A.3
Incompressibility in finite nuclei:
K_{A}

The incompressibility
K_{A}
in finite nuclei is defined as,

\displaystyle K_{A}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle 9n_{\mathrm{cl}}\frac{\partial^{2}\epsilon_{A}}{\partial n_{\mathrm{cl}}^{2}}\Bigr|_{A},

(71)

with the energy density given by
\epsilon_{A}=e_{A}n_{\mathrm{cl}}
. Similarly to the energy and the pressure, the linearity of the derivative operator allows to decompose the incompressibility
K_{A}
in finite nuclei as bulk and FS terms,

\displaystyle K_{A}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle K_{A,\mathrm{UM}}+K_{A,\mathrm{FS}}\,,

(72)

where
K_{A,\mathrm{UM}}=K_{A,\mathrm{SM}}+K_{A,\mathrm{sym}}\delta^{2}
,
K_{A,\mathrm{SM}}=K_{\mathrm{SM}}(n_{\mathrm{cl}})
and
K_{A,\mathrm{sym}}=K_{\mathrm{sym}}(n_{\mathrm{cl}})
.

The finite size contribution to the incompressibility are given as
K_{A,\mathrm{FS}}=K_{A,\mathrm{surf}}+K_{A,\mathrm{Coul}}
, where the Coulomb term reads,

\displaystyle K_{A,\mathrm{Coul}}=\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{Coul}}\frac{12}{5}\frac{Z^{2}e^{2}}{R_{A}}\frac{1}{A}\,,

(73)

and the surface term is expressed as

\displaystyle K_{A,\mathrm{surf}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{surf}}\Big[-8\pi R_{A}^{2}\sigma_{\mathrm{surf}}+24\pi n_{\mathrm{cl}}R_{A}^{2}\frac{\partial\sigma_{\mathrm{surf}}}{\partial n_{\mathrm{cl}}}

(74)

\displaystyle+36\pi n_{\mathrm{cl}}^{2}R_{A}^{2}\frac{\partial^{2}\sigma_{\mathrm{surf}}}{\partial n_{\mathrm{cl}}^{2}}\Big]\frac{1}{A}\,.

A.4
Re-expression of
K_{A}

The first time a CLDM model was used to compute the incompressibility of nuclei goes back to the seminal work of Blaizot
Blaizot 1980
.
In order to compare our expression for

with his work, we dedicate this section to re-write our equations and obtain the equivalent of Eq. (6.3) of Ref.
Blaizot 1980
.

In finite nuclei, the density is different from the saturation density due to the contribution of FS and isospin asymmetry terms. If the density parameter
x_{\mathrm{cl}}
remains small, Blaizot suggested to express
K_{A}
as
Blaizot 1980
,

K_{A}=K_{\mathrm{sat}}+\delta^{2}\bar{K}_{A,\mathrm{sym}}+\bar{K}_{A,\mathrm{FS}}\,.

(75)

The new terms
\bar{K}_{A,\mathrm{sym}}
and
\bar{K}_{A,\mathrm{FS}}
incorporate, in addition to the contribution
K_{A,\mathrm{sym}}
and
K_{A,FS}
, the shift in density between
n_{\mathrm{cl}}
and
n_{\mathrm{sat}}
, see Appendix
B
, and more specifically Eq. (
91
). To do so, we consider the expression for
K_{A,\mathrm{SM}}
up to the linear order in
x_{\mathrm{cl}}
from Eq. (
30
), where the expression for
x_{\mathrm{cl}}
in terms of
P_{A,\mathrm{sym}}
and
P_{A,\mathrm{FS}}
from Eq. (

) is injected:

\displaystyle K_{A,\mathrm{SM}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle K_{\mathrm{sat}}-\frac{3P_{A,\mathrm{sym}}}{n_{\mathrm{cl}}K_{\mathrm{sat}}}\delta^{2}\left(12K_{\mathrm{sat}}+Q_{\mathrm{sat}}\right)

(76)

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt-\frac{3P_{A,\mathrm{FS}}}{n_{\mathrm{cl}}K_{\mathrm{sat}}}\left(12K_{\mathrm{sat}}+Q_{\mathrm{sat}}\right)\,.

Re-ordering the different terms into
K_{A}
gives Eq. (

) where

\displaystyle\bar{K}_{A,\mathrm{sym}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle K_{A,\mathrm{sym}}-\frac{3P_{A,\mathrm{sym}}}{n_{\mathrm{cl}}K_{\mathrm{sat}}}\left(12K_{\mathrm{sat}}+Q_{\mathrm{sat}}\right)\,,

(77)

\displaystyle\bar{K}_{A,\mathrm{FS}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle K_{A,\mathrm{FS}}-\frac{3P_{A,\mathrm{FS}}}{n_{\mathrm{cl}}K_{\mathrm{sat}}}\left(12K_{\mathrm{sat}}+Q_{\mathrm{sat}}\right)\,.

(78)

At order

, the term

(79)

The FS terms could be decomposed into the Coulomb and surface contributions. The Coulomb term reads,

\bar{K}_{A,\mathrm{Coul}}=-\frac{3\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{Coul}}}{5}\frac{Z^{2}e^{2}}{R_{A}}\frac{1}{A}\left(8+\frac{Q_{\mathrm{sat}}}{K_{\mathrm{sat}}}\right)=\bar{K}_{\mathrm{Coul}}Z^{2}A^{-4/3}\,,

(80)

with

\displaystyle\bar{K}_{\mathrm{Coul}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{3\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{Coul}}}{5}\frac{e^{2}}{r_{0}}\left(8+\frac{Q_{\mathrm{sat}}}{K_{\mathrm{sat}}}\right)\,.

(81)

The surface term reads,

\displaystyle\bar{K}_{A,\mathrm{surf}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big(\bar{K}_{\mathrm{surf}}^{\mathrm{CLDM}}+\bar{K}_{\mathrm{surf}}^{\mathrm{DD},\dot{\sigma}}+\bar{K}_{\mathrm{surf}}^{\mathrm{DD},\ddot{\sigma}}\Big)A^{-1/3}\,.

(82)

with

\displaystyle\bar{K}_{\mathrm{surf}}^{\mathrm{CLDM}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{surf}}8\pi r^{2}_{\mathrm{cl}}\sigma_{\mathrm{surf}}\left(11+\frac{Q_{\mathrm{sat}}}{K_{\mathrm{sat}}}\right)\,,

(83)

\displaystyle\bar{K}_{\mathrm{surf}}^{\mathrm{DD},\dot{\sigma}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{surf}}12\pi n_{\mathrm{cl}}r_{\mathrm{cl}}^{2}\frac{\partial\sigma_{\mathrm{surf}}}{\partial n_{\mathrm{cl}}}\left(10+\frac{Q_{\mathrm{sat}}}{K_{\mathrm{sat}}}\right)

(84)

\displaystyle\bar{K}_{\mathrm{surf}}^{\mathrm{DD},\ddot{\sigma}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{surf}}36\pi n_{\mathrm{cl}}^{2}r_{\mathrm{cl}}^{2}\frac{\partial^{2}\sigma_{\mathrm{surf}}}{\partial n_{\mathrm{cl}}^{2}}\,.

(85)

Note that the first derivative term,
\bar{K}_{\mathrm{surf}}^{\mathrm{DD},\dot{\sigma}}
, is expected to be small since
\partial\sigma_{\mathrm{surf}}/\partial n_{\mathrm{cl}}\propto x_{\mathrm{cl}}\approx 0
. In order to compare the above finite size contributions for Eq. (

) with Eq. (6.3) of Blaizot

how to write
\bar{K}_{A,\mathrm{FS}}
in Blaizot notation.

Appendix B
Expression for
x_{\mathrm{cl}}
in finite nuclei

We follow the approach of Blaizot
Blaizot 1980
and rewrite
x_{\mathrm{cl}}
as follows. From the definition of the compressibility
\chi(n)
,

\chi=\frac{1}{n}\left(\frac{dP}{dn}\right)^{-1}\,,\;\;\;\hbox{ we have }\;\;\;\frac{dP}{dn}=\frac{1}{n\chi}.

(86)

In
N=Z
nuclei,
P=P_{A,SM}
, and by integrating (
86
) from saturation (
n_{\mathrm{sat}}
) to equilibrium (
n_{\mathrm{cl}}
),

P_{A,SM}(n_{\mathrm{cl}})-P_{A,SM}(n_{\mathrm{sat}})=\int^{n_{\mathrm{cl}}}_{n_{\mathrm{sat}}}\frac{1}{n\chi}dn\,.

(87)

By definition
P_{A,SM}(n_{\mathrm{sat}})=0
, and for
x_{\mathrm{cl}}
is close to
n_{\mathrm{sat}}
we approximate
\chi(n)\approx\chi(n_{0})
with
n_{0}\in[n_{\mathrm{sat}},n_{\mathrm{cl}}]
, leading to

P_{A,SM}(n_{\mathrm{cl}})\approx\frac{1}{\chi(n_{0})}\log\frac{n_{\mathrm{cl}}}{n_{\mathrm{sat}}}\approx\frac{1}{\chi(n_{0})}\frac{n_{\mathrm{cl}}-n_{\mathrm{sat}}}{n_{\mathrm{sat}}}\,.

(88)

Since
K=9/(n\chi)
, we have

\frac{1}{\chi(n_{0})}=\frac{n_{0}K_{A,SM}(n_{0})}{9}\approx\frac{n_{\mathrm{cl}}K_{\mathrm{sat}}}{9},,

(89)

since
K_{A,\mathrm{SM}}
is an increasing function of the density.
Finally, we obtain

x_{\mathrm{cl}}=\frac{n_{\mathrm{cl}}-n_{\mathrm{sat}}}{3n_{\mathrm{sat}}}=\frac{3}{n_{\mathrm{cl}}K_{\mathrm{sat}}}P_{A,SM}(n_{\mathrm{cl}}).

(90)

Eq. (
90
) could be interpreted as the following: there is an equivalence between the density shift
x_{\mathrm{cl}}
which is different from zero for densities different from
n_{\mathrm{sat}}
, as an effect of an external pressure
P_{A,SM}
, shifting the equilibrium density to a slightly different one. In finite nuclei, this extra-pressure is originating from the FS and isospin asymmetry terms, since
P_{A}(n_{\mathrm{cl}})=0
. We therefore deduce
P_{A,SM}(n_{\mathrm{cl}})=-P_{A,\mathrm{FS}}(n_{\mathrm{cl}})-\delta^{2}P_{A,\mathrm{sym}}(n_{\mathrm{cl}})
, and we can rewrite Eq. (
90
) as

x_{\mathrm{cl}}=-\frac{3}{n_{\mathrm{cl}}K_{\mathrm{sat}}}\left(P_{A,\mathrm{FS}}(n_{\mathrm{cl}})+\delta^{2}P_{A,\mathrm{sym}}(n_{\mathrm{cl}})\right).

(91)

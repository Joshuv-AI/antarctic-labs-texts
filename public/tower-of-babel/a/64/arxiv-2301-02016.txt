Comment on “A comment on metric vs metric-affine gravity”
Gonzalo J. Olmo, P. J. Porfírio

Abstract
It has been recently claimed in Lind that an action of the Einstein-Palatini form plus a torsionless Pontryagin term (multiplied by a constant) represents a counterexample to the conclusions of Exirifard:2007da , namely, that Lovelock gravity is the only case in which the metric and metric-affine formulations of gravity are equivalent. However, given that the Pontryagin term (multiplied by a constant) can be written as a total D-divergence, it is a textbook matter to realise that the addition of such (or any other) D-divergence only affects at the boundary, leaving invariant the field equations and its solutions, which are those of GR à la Palatini. We thus conclude that the example provided in Lind is not a valid counterexample of Exirifard:2007da .



The torsionless metric-affine action in
D=2n
dimensions considered in
Lind
,

S=\int d^{D}x\left(\sqrt{-g}g^{ab}R_{ab}(\Gamma)+\frac{1}{n}\theta\epsilon^{a_{1}a_{2}...a_{D}}R^{i_{1}}_{\,\,\,i_{2}a_{1}a_{2}}(\Gamma)R^{i_{2}}_{\,\,\,i_{3}a_{3}a_{4}}(\Gamma)...R^{i_{n}}_{\,\,\,i_{1}a_{D-1}a_{D}}(\Gamma)\right)\ ,

(1)

can be written when
\theta
is constant as

S=\int d^{D}x\left(\sqrt{-g}g^{ab}R_{ab}(\Gamma)+\theta\partial_{a}K^{a}\right)\ ,

(2)

with
K^{a}
as in Eq.(2) of
Lind
. The addition of this fancy total derivative (or of any other) does not have any effect on the field equations
Landau
. Since the scalar
g^{ab}R_{ab}(\Gamma)
is just the linear in curvature term of Lovelock gravity, the action (
1
) can still be regarded as included in the equivalence class of Lovelock theories, up to the addition of irrelevant total divergences. Thus, the example presented in
Lind
cannot be regarded as a counterexample of the conclusions of
Exirifard:2007da
.

If the parameter
\theta
in (
1
) is promoted to the status of field, one obtains that the connection field equations are given by

-\nabla_{c}\left(\sqrt{-g}g^{i_{2}b}\right)+\nabla_{d}\left(\sqrt{-g}g^{i_{2}d}\right)\delta^{b}_{c}-2\left(\nabla_{d}\theta\right)\epsilon^{dba_{3}...a_{D-1}a_{D}}R^{i_{2}}_{\,\,\,i_{3}a_{3}a_{4}}(\Gamma)...R^{i_{n}}_{\,\,\,ca_{D-1}a_{D}}(\Gamma)=0\ ,

(3)

which naturally boil down to those of GR when
\theta
is a constant. In the non-constant case, taking
b=c
in Eq.(
3
), one finds
\nabla_{d}\left(\sqrt{-g}g^{i_{2}d}\right)=0
. Plugging this result back into Eq.(
3
), one arrives at

\nabla_{c}\left(\sqrt{-g}g^{i_{2}b}\right)+2\left(\nabla_{d}\theta\right)\epsilon^{dba_{3}...a_{D-1}a_{D}}R^{i_{2}}_{\,\,\,i_{3}a_{3}a_{4}}(\Gamma)...R^{i_{n}}_{\,\,\,ca_{D-1}a_{D}}(\Gamma)=0.

(4)

In particular, when
D=4
, Eq.(
3
) boils down to

\nabla_{c}\left(\sqrt{-g}g^{ab}\right)=-2\left(\nabla_{d}\theta\right)\epsilon^{dba_{3}a_{4}}R^{a}_{\,\,\,ca_{3}a_{4}}(\Gamma),

(5)

which is similar to the connection equation obtained either in
Sul
or in
Bo
for the torsionless case and by disregarding the homothetic curvature terms. The resulting equations are obviously different from those found in the metric formulation and lead to different phenomenology
Bo2
;
Bombacigno:2022naf
. Thus, when
\theta\neq
constant, the conclusions of
Exirifard:2007da
for the action (
1
) hold as well.

Before concluding, we note that
Exirifard:2007da
neither provided an algorithm to find all the solutions for the connection in the metric-affine formulation of Lovelock theories nor ruled out the possible existence of solutions different from the Levi-Civita one
Janssen:2019doc
.

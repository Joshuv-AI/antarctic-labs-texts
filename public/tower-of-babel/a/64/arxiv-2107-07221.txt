Abstract
. We consider a family of random normal matrix models whose eigenvalues tend to occupy lemniscate type droplets as the size of the matrix increases. Under the insertion of a point charge, we derive the scaling limit at the singular boundary point, which is expressed in terms of the solution to the model Painlevé IV Riemann-Hilbert problem. For this, we introduce a version of the Christoffel-Darboux identity and combine it with the strong asymptotics of the associated orthogonal polynomials due to Bertola, Elias Rebelo and Grava.

1.
Introduction and main results

In the theory of random normal matrices
[
51
,
31
]
, one usually starts with a suitable real-valued function
\mathcal{W}
called the
external potential
and consider a normal matrix of size
N
picked randomly with respect to the measure proportional to

e^{-N\,\textup{Tr}\,\mathcal{W}(M)}\,dM
.
Here
dM
is the induced surface measure on the space of normal matrices
\{M\in\mathbb{C}^{N^{2}}:MM^{*}=M^{*}M\}
.
Then its eigenvalues
\{\lambda_{j}\}_{1}^{N}
behave like equally charged Coulomb particles
[
33
,
50
,
45
]
in the external field
N\mathcal{W}
at specific inverse temperature
\beta=2
, namely, the joint probability distribution of the system is proportional to

(1.1)

\prod_{j<k}|\lambda_{j}-\lambda_{k}|^{2}\prod_{j=1}^{N}e^{-N\mathcal{W}(\lambda_{j})}\,dA(\lambda_{j}),\qquad(dA(\lambda):=d^{2}\lambda/\pi).

We refer to
[
23
, Section 5]
for a recent review.

As the size of the matrix increases, the eigenvalue ensemble tends to minimise the weighted logarithmic energy functional
[
48
]
, which can be recognised as the continuum limit of its discrete Hamiltonian, see e.g.
[
26
,
8
]
.
In particular the support of the limiting empirical distribution is given by a certain compact set called the
droplet
.
Due to Sakai’s regularity theory
[
49
]
, it is well known that for a real analytic potential
\mathcal{W}
, all but finitely many boundary points of the droplet are “regular” in a proper sense.
Furthermore in the case that there exists a local Schwarz function near the prescribed boundary point, the possible types of singularities are classified.
On the other hand, the construction of a droplet containing singular boundary points requires a separate analysis, see
[
2
,
15
,
13
,
20
,
40
,
21
,
29
]
and references therein for recent works in this direction.

The detailed statistical information about the joint intensity functions of the eigenvalue system can be effectively analysed by the
correlation (reproducing) kernel
of the orthogonal polynomials with respect to the weighted Lebesgue measure
e^{-N\mathcal{W}}\,dA
.
Recently, for a quite general class of the potentials, the asymptotic behaviours of the associated planar orthogonal polynomials were obtained by Hedenmalm and Wennman
[
35
]
.
As a consequence, they derived the boundary scaling limit of the correlation kernel, which leads to the local universality at regular boundary points of the droplet. (We also refer to
[
10
]
for an earlier work on the local universality at regular bulk points.)

On the other hand, it is intuitively clear that different kinds of scaling limits should appear at singular boundary points. However the description of such scaling limits remains open in general and we aim to contribute to this problem.
In particular we shall consider two types of singularities; one is the
lemniscate
type singularity arising from the local geometric structure of the droplet (see Figure
1
) and the other is the
spectral
singularity arising from an insertion of a point charge.

1.1.
Setup

Let us be more precise now in introducing our model that we call the
lemniscate ensemble
following
[
11
]
.
First we consider the (shifted) Gaussian potential
Q
of the form

(1.2)

Q(\zeta):=|\zeta-a|^{2},\qquad a\geq 0.

This is a building block to define

(1.3)

V(\zeta):=\frac{1}{d}Q(\zeta^{d})=\frac{1}{d}\,|\zeta^{d}-a|^{2},

where
d>1
is a fixed integer.
We remark that even though
Q
can be realised as a special case of
V
with
d=1
, we intentionally distinguish this case for our purpose described below.
For a given point charge
c>-1
, let

(1.4)

Q_{c}(\zeta):=Q(\zeta)-\frac{2c}{N}\log|\zeta|,\qquad V_{c}(\zeta):=V(\zeta)-\frac{2c}{N}\log|\zeta|.

Such an extra logarithmic factor is often referred to as a spectral singularity, see e.g.
[
1
, Chapter 6]
.
Here the condition
c>-1
is required to guarantee that the partition functions
Z_{N},\widehat{Z}_{N}
below are finite.

We shall study random normal matrix ensembles
\{\zeta_{j}\}_{1}^{N}
,
\{\widehat{\zeta}_{j}\}_{1}^{N}
associated with the potentials
V_{c}
,
Q_{c}
respectively. By definition, their joint probability distributions
\mathbf{P}_{N},\widehat{\mathbf{P}}_{N}
are given by

(1.5)

\displaystyle d\mathbf{P}_{N}(\zeta_{1},\ldots,\zeta_{N})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{Z_{N}}\prod_{j<k}|\zeta_{j}-\zeta_{k}|^{2}\prod_{j=1}^{N}|\zeta_{j}|^{2c}e^{-NV(\zeta_{j})}\,dA(\zeta_{j}),

(1.6)

\displaystyle d\widehat{\mathbf{P}}_{N}(\widehat{\zeta}_{1},\ldots,\widehat{\zeta}_{N})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\widehat{Z}_{N}}\prod_{j<k}|\widehat{\zeta}_{j}-\widehat{\zeta}_{k}|^{2}\prod_{j=1}^{N}|\widehat{\zeta}_{j}|^{2c}e^{-NQ(\widehat{\zeta}_{j})}\,dA(\widehat{\zeta}_{j}),

where
Z_{N},\widehat{Z}_{N}
are normalisation constants which turn
\mathbf{P}_{N},\widehat{\mathbf{P}}_{N}
into probability measures, see
[
30
,
53
]
for asymptotics of the partition function
\widehat{Z}_{N}
.
We also mention that for an integer-valued
c
, the system
\{\widehat{\zeta}_{j}\}_{1}^{N}
has an alternative realisation as eigenvalues of the
induced Ginibre ensemble

[
32
]
, an extension of the Ginibre ensemble to include zero eigenvalues.

The well-known circular law
[
34
]
asserts that as
N
increases, the eigenvalues
\{\widehat{\zeta}_{j}\}_{1}^{N}
tend to be uniformly distributed on the disc
\widehat{S}:=\{\zeta\in\mathbb{C}:|\zeta-a|^{2}\leq 1\}
.
As a consequence, it is easy to observe that
\{\zeta_{j}\}_{1}^{N}
tend to occupy the droplet

(1.7)

S:=\{\zeta\in\mathbb{C}:|\zeta^{d}-a|^{2}\leq 1\}

and that the limiting density on
S
with respect to the area measure
dA
is given by

(1.8)

\Delta V(\zeta)=d\,|\zeta|^{2d-2},\qquad(\Delta:=\partial{\bar{\partial}}),

see
[
15
, Lemma 1]
. Note that the topology of
S
reveals a phase transition at the value

, where the droplet

is of lemniscate type having

We denote by

,

(1.9)

Here

is the Kronecker delta.
The strong asymptotics of

were extensively studied in

Let us write
\mathbf{K}_{N}^{c}
,
\widehat{\mathbf{K}}_{N}^{c}
for the correlation kernels of the point processes
\{\zeta_{j}\}_{1}^{N}
,
\{\widehat{\zeta}_{j}\}_{1}^{N}
, respectively. Due to Dyson’s determinantal formula, we have the canonical expressions

(1.10)

\displaystyle\mathbf{K}_{N}^{c}(\zeta,\eta)

\displaystyle=(\zeta\bar{\eta})^{c}e^{-\frac{N}{2}(V(\zeta)+V(\eta))}\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}q_{j,N}^{c}(\zeta)\overline{q_{j,N}^{c}(\eta)},

(1.11)

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathbf{K}}_{N}^{c}(\zeta,\eta)

\displaystyle=(\zeta\bar{\eta})^{c}e^{-\frac{N}{2}(Q(\zeta)+Q(\eta))}\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}p_{j,N}^{c}(\zeta)\overline{p_{j,N}^{c}(\eta)}.

The joint intensity (correlation) functions are then given in terms of the determinant of such correlation kernels, see e.g.
[
33
,
23
]
.

To describe the local statistics of
\{\zeta_{j}\}_{1}^{N}
,
\{\widehat{\zeta}_{j}\}_{1}^{N}
at the origin, it is convenient to define the rescaled point processes
\{z_{j}\}_{1}^{N}
,
\{\widehat{z}_{j}\}_{1}^{N}
as

(1.12)

z_{j}:=(N/d)^{\frac{1}{2d}}\cdot\zeta_{j},\qquad\widehat{z}_{j}:=N^{\frac{1}{2}}\cdot\widehat{\zeta}_{j},

see Figure
1
. Here the rescaling order
N^{\frac{1}{2d}}
is chosen according to the mean eigenvalue density (
1.8
) at the origin.
By definition, the correlation kernels
K_{N}^{c},\widehat{K}_{N}^{c}
associated with the point processes
\{z_{j}\}_{1}^{N},\{\widehat{z}_{j}\}_{1}^{N}
are given by

(1.13)

\displaystyle K_{N}^{c}(z,w):=\frac{1}{(N/d)^{\frac{1}{d}}}\mathbf{K}_{N}^{c}\Big(\frac{z}{(N/d)^{\frac{1}{2d}}},\frac{w}{(N/d)^{\frac{1}{2d}}}\Big),\qquad\widehat{K}_{N}^{c}(z,w):=\frac{1}{N}\widehat{\mathbf{K}}_{N}^{c}\Big(\frac{z}{\sqrt{N}},\frac{w}{\sqrt{N}}\Big).

We aim to derive the large-
N
limits

(1.14)

K^{c}:=\lim\limits_{N\to\infty}K_{N}^{c},\qquad\widehat{K}^{c}:=\lim\limits_{N\to\infty}\widehat{K}_{N}^{c}

of the correlation kernels, where the convergence is uniform on compact subsets of
\mathbb{C}
. The existence of the large-
N
limits can be found in
[
12
, Theorem 1.1]
and
[
11
, Lemma 3]
. Let us also stress here that by
[
11
, Lemma 1]
, the limiting point processes are indeed determined by their
1
-point densities

(1.15)

R^{c}(z):=K^{c}(z,z),\qquad\widehat{R}^{c}(z):=\widehat{K}^{c}(z,z).

1.2.
Finite-
N
analysis

The main ingredient to analyse the correlation kernel is a version of the
Christoffel-Darboux identity
.
This can be applied to various situations for instance to the case studied in
[
13
]
; cf. see
[
25
]
for a recent implementation.

To describe the Christoffel-Darboux formula, let
P_{j}
be the
monic
orthogonal polynomial of degree
j
satisfying

(1.16)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{C}}P_{j}(\zeta)\overline{P_{k}(\zeta)}|\zeta-a|^{2c}e^{-N|\zeta|^{2}}\,dA(\zeta)=h_{j}\,\delta_{jk},

where
h_{j}
is the orthogonal norm.
We denote

(1.17)

\psi_{j}(\zeta):=(\zeta-a)^{c}P_{j}(\zeta),\qquad\phi_{j}(\zeta):=(\zeta-a)^{c}\frac{P_{j}(\zeta)}{h_{j}}.

and define

(1.18)

\widetilde{\mathbf{K}}_{N}^{c}(\zeta,\eta):=e^{-N\zeta\bar{\eta}}\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\overline{\phi_{j}(\eta)}\psi_{j}(\zeta)=|\zeta-a|^{2c}e^{-N\zeta\bar{\eta}}\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}P_{j}(\zeta)\overline{P_{j}(\eta)}.

Note that it is related to
\widehat{\mathbf{K}}_{N}^{c}
in (
1.11
) as

(1.19)

\widehat{\mathbf{K}}_{N}^{c}(\zeta,\zeta)=\widetilde{\mathbf{K}}_{N}^{c}(a-\zeta,a-\zeta).

We obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 1.1

(
Christoffel-Darboux formula
)
.

Suppose that
a\not=0
.
Then we have

(1.20)

\displaystyle\begin{split}{\bar{\partial}}_{\eta}\widetilde{\mathbf{K}}_{n}^{c}(\zeta,\eta)&=e^{-N\zeta\bar{\eta}}\frac{1}{\tfrac{n+c}{N}h_{n-1}-h_{n}}{\bar{\partial}}_{\eta}\overline{\psi_{n}(\eta)}\Big(\psi_{n}(\zeta)-\zeta\psi_{n-1}(\zeta)\Big)\\
&\quad-e^{-N\zeta\bar{\eta}}\frac{P_{n+1}(a)}{P_{n}(a)}\frac{N\,h_{N}/h_{N-1}}{\tfrac{n+c+1}{N}h_{n}-h_{n+1}}\overline{\psi_{n-1}(\eta)}\Big(\psi_{n+1}(\zeta)-\zeta\psi_{n}(\zeta)\Big).\end{split}

Contrary to the classical Christoffel-Darboux formula for the orthogonal polynomial kernel on the real axis, the identity (
1.20
) shows that the summation in (
1.18
) can be expressed in terms of the three last orthogonal polynomials.

Remark 1.2
.

For the radially symmetric case when
a=0
, we have

(1.21)

P_{j}(\zeta)=\zeta^{j},\qquad h_{j}=\frac{\Gamma(j+c+1)}{N^{j+c+1}}.

Thus Theorem
1.1
cannot be directly applied to this case since

(1.22)

\tfrac{N+c}{N}h_{N-1}-h_{N}=\psi_{N}(\zeta)-\zeta\psi_{N-1}(\zeta)=0.

On the one hand, for
a\not=0
, it was shown in
[
13
, Appendix D]
that
\tfrac{N+c}{N}h_{N-1}-h_{N}
does not vanish.

Remark 1.3

(Three-term recurrence relation)
.

In Subsection
3.2
, we also show that the orthogonal polynomial
P_{k}
satisfies the (non-standard) three-term recurrence relation of the form

(1.23)

z\,P_{k}(z)=P_{k+1}(z)+b_{k}\,P_{k}(z)+c_{k}\,z\,P_{k-1}(z),

where

(1.24)

b_{k}:=-\frac{P_{k+1}(0)}{P_{k}(0)},\qquad c_{k}:=\frac{P_{k}(0)-P_{k+1}^{\prime}(0)-b_{k}P_{k}^{\prime}(0)}{P_{k-1}(0)}.

This relation (
1.23
) plays an important role in the proof of Theorem
1.1
.
We mention that it is shown in
[
6
, Corollary 5.3]
that
P_{k}
does not satisfy the standard three-term recurrence relation, (i.e. the relation of the form (
1.23
) with
c_{k}z
replaced by
c_{k}
).

Example 1.4
.

(Exactly solvable case:
c=1
)

For an integer-valued point charge
c
, one can explicitly express the associated orthogonal polynomials using the well-known special functions. For instance, when
c=1
, we have

(1.25)

\displaystyle\begin{split}P_{k}(\zeta)&=\sum_{j=0}^{k}a^{k-j}\frac{k!}{j!}\frac{\Gamma(j+1,Na^{2})}{\Gamma(k+1,Na^{2})}\zeta^{j}=\frac{1}{\zeta-a}\Big(\zeta^{k+1}-e^{aN(\zeta-a)}\frac{Q(k+1,Na\,\zeta)}{Q(k+1,Na^{2})}a^{k+1}\Big)\end{split}

and

(1.26)

h_{k}=\frac{(k+1)!}{N^{k+2}}\frac{Q(k+2,Na^{2})}{Q(k+1,Na^{2})},

see
[
7
, Section 3]
.
Here
Q
is the regularised incomplete Gamma function.
Then by using some basic properties of the incomplete Gamma function, one can directly check the Christoffel-Darboux formula (
1.20
) as well as the three-term recurrence relation (
1.23
).

Due to the relation (
1.19
), one can notice that the use of Theorem
1.1
can be made to derive the asymptotic behaviours of
\widehat{K}_{N}^{c}
in (
1.13
).
Furthermore, the behaviours of
K_{N}^{c}
follows from the following proposition.

Proposition 1.5

(
Multi-fold transform
)
.

For each
c>-1
and
d\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

(1.27)

\displaystyle\mathbf{K}_{dN}^{c}(\zeta,\eta)

\displaystyle=d(\zeta\bar{\eta})^{d-1}\sum_{l=0}^{d-1}\widehat{\mathbf{K}}_{N}^{\frac{c+l+1}{d}-1}(\zeta^{d},\eta^{d}),

(1.28)

\displaystyle K_{dN}^{c}(z,w)

\displaystyle=d(z\bar{w})^{d-1}\sum_{l=0}^{d-1}\widehat{K}_{N}^{\frac{c+l+1}{d}-1}(z^{d},w^{d}).

We refer to
[
27
, Proposition 2.1]
and
[
5
, Appendix B]
for related statements on Hermitian matrix models.
This proposition follows from a simple relation (
2.17
) between the orthogonal polynomials.

2.
Recursive formula and multi-fold transformation

In this section, we present the
recursive formula
and
multi-fold transformation
of correlation kernels.
First let us recall some well-known facts.

Note that
E_{1,c}(z)=z^{-c}e^{z}P(c,z)
, where
P(c,z):=\frac{1}{\Gamma(c)}\gamma(c,z)
is the regularised incomplete Gamma function.
For
a\in[0,1)
fixed, we write
\widehat{K}^{c}_{\textup{bulk}}
,
K^{c}_{\textup{bulk}}
for the corresponding correlation kernels.
It then follows from (
1.46
) that

(2.1)

\widehat{R}^{c}_{\textup{bulk}}(z)=P(c,|z|^{2}),\qquad\widehat{K}^{c}_{\textup{bulk}}(z,w)=G(z,w)P(c,z\bar{w}),

where

(2.2)

G(z,w):=e^{z\bar{w}-|z|^{2}/2-|w|^{2}/2}

is the bulk Ginibre kernel.
On the other hand when
c=0
, we have the boundary Ginibre kernel

(2.3)

\widehat{R}^{0}_{\textup{edge}}(z)=\tfrac{1}{2}\operatorname{erfc}(-\tfrac{z+\bar{z}-\mathcal{S}}{\sqrt{2}}),\qquad\widehat{K}^{0}_{\textup{edge}}(z,w)=G(z,w)\tfrac{1}{2}\operatorname{erfc}(-\tfrac{z+\bar{w}-\mathcal{S}}{\sqrt{2}}).

2.1.
Recursive formula

Let us define the
Berezin kernel

(2.4)

\widehat{B}^{c}_{N}(z,w):=\frac{|\widehat{K}_{N}^{c}(z,w)|^{2}}{\widehat{R}_{N}^{c}(z)}.

We now derive the following recursive formula for
\widehat{R}^{c}_{N}
, see
[
10
, Lemma 7.6.2]
for a similar statement.

Lemma 2.1
.

For any
a\geq 0
and
c>-1,
we have

(2.5)

\widehat{R}_{N}^{c+1}(z)=\widehat{R}_{N+1}^{c}(z)-\widehat{B}_{N+1}^{c}(0,z).

As an immediate consequence, by letting
N\to\infty
, we obtain (
1.44
).
Before the proof, let us present some examples.

Example 2.2
.

(Bulk case) By (
2.1
) and
[
47
, Eq.(8.7.1)]
, we have

(2.6)

\frac{|\widehat{K}^{c}_{\textup{bulk}}(0,z)|^{2}}{\widehat{R}^{c}_{\textup{bulk}}(z)}=e^{-|z|^{2}}\frac{|z|^{2c}}{\Gamma(c+1)}.

Then it follows from the recurrence relation of the regularised Gamma function (see
[
47
, Eq.(8.8.5)]
) that

(2.7)

\widehat{R}^{c+1}_{\textup{bulk}}(z)=\widehat{R}^{c}_{\textup{bulk}}(z)-\frac{|\widehat{K}^{c}_{\textup{bulk}}(0,z)|^{2}}{\widehat{R}^{c}_{\textup{bulk}}(z)}=P(c+1,|z|^{2}).

Example 2.3
.

(Edge case)
For
a=1
, by (
2.3
), we have

(2.8)

\widehat{R}^{1}_{\textup{edge}}(z)=\tfrac{1}{2}\operatorname{erfc}(-\tfrac{z+\bar{z}}{\sqrt{2}})-\tfrac{1}{2}e^{-|z|^{2}}|\operatorname{erfc}(-\tfrac{z}{\sqrt{2}})|^{2}.

Similarly, we have

(2.9)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\widehat{R}^{2}_{\textup{edge}}(z)&=\tfrac{1}{2}\operatorname{erfc}(-\tfrac{z+\bar{z}}{\sqrt{2}})-\tfrac{1}{2}e^{-|z|^{2}}|\operatorname{erfc}(-\tfrac{z}{\sqrt{2}})|^{2}-\tfrac{1}{\pi-2}e^{-|z|^{2}}\Big|(\sqrt{\tfrac{\pi}{2}}z-1)\operatorname{erfc}(-\tfrac{z}{\sqrt{2}})+e^{-z^{2}/2}\Big|^{2}.\end{split}

2.1
.

Proof of Lemma
2.1
.

Let us write

(2.10)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\widehat{\mathbf{R}}_{N,k}^{c}(\widehat{\zeta}_{1},\ldots,\widehat{\zeta}_{k})&:=\tfrac{1}{\widehat{Z}_{N}}\tfrac{N!}{(N-k)!}\int_{\mathbb{C}^{N-k}}\prod_{j<k}|\widehat{\zeta}_{j}-\widehat{\zeta}_{k}|^{2}e^{-N\sum_{j}Q_{c}(\widehat{\zeta}_{j})}\prod_{j=k+1}^{N}dA(\widehat{\zeta}_{j})\end{split}

for the
k
-point correlation (joint intensity) function. Recall that we have

(2.11)

\widehat{\mathbf{R}}_{N,k}^{c}(\widehat{\zeta}_{1},\ldots,\widehat{\zeta}_{k})=\det\Big[\widehat{\mathbf{K}}_{N}(\widehat{\zeta}_{j},\widehat{\zeta}_{l})\Big]_{j,l=1}^{k},

see e.g.
[
33
]
.

The main idea of the proof is the following simple observation

\displaystyle|\Delta(\widehat{\zeta}_{1},\cdots,\widehat{\zeta}_{N})|^{2}\prod_{j=1}^{N}|\widehat{\zeta}_{j}|^{2}=|\Delta(\widehat{\zeta}_{1},\cdots,\widehat{\zeta}_{N},0)|^{2},

where
\Delta
is the Vandermonde determinant.
Using this, we have

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathbf{R}}_{N,1}^{c+1}(\widehat{\zeta}_{1})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\widehat{Z}_{N}}\frac{N!}{(N-1)!}\int_{\mathbb{C}^{N-1}}|\Delta(\widehat{\zeta}_{1},\cdots,\widehat{\zeta}_{N})|^{2}e^{-N\sum_{j}Q_{c+1}(\widehat{\zeta}_{j})}\prod_{j=2}^{N}dA(\widehat{\zeta}_{j})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\widehat{Z}_{N}}\frac{1}{N+1}\frac{(N+1)!}{(N-1)!}\int_{\mathbb{C}^{N-1}}|\Delta(\widehat{\zeta}_{1},\cdots,\widehat{\zeta}_{N},0)|^{2}e^{-N\sum_{j}Q_{c}(\widehat{\zeta}_{j})}\prod_{j=2}^{N}dA(\widehat{\zeta}_{j})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N+1}\widehat{\mathbf{R}}_{N+1,2}^{c}(\widehat{\zeta}_{1},0),

which leads to

\widehat{\mathbf{R}}_{N,1}^{c+1}(\zeta)=\frac{N}{N+1}\frac{\widehat{\mathbf{R}}_{N+1,2}^{c}(\zeta,0)}{\widehat{\mathbf{R}}_{N+1,1}^{c}(0)}.

Therefore we obtain

\displaystyle\widehat{R}_{N,1}^{c+1}(z)=\frac{1}{N}\widehat{\mathbf{R}}_{N,1}^{c+1}(\tfrac{z}{\sqrt{N}})=\frac{1}{N+1}\frac{\widehat{\mathbf{R}}_{N+1,2}^{c}(\frac{z}{\sqrt{N}},0)}{\widehat{\mathbf{R}}_{N+1,1}^{c}(0)}.

Since

\widehat{R}_{N+1,1}^{c}(0)=\frac{1}{N+1}\widehat{\mathbf{R}}_{N+1,1}^{c}(0),\qquad\widehat{R}_{N+1,2}^{c}(0,z)=\frac{1}{(N+1)^{2}}\widehat{\mathbf{R}}_{N+1,2}^{c}(\tfrac{z}{\sqrt{N}},0),

we conclude

\widehat{R}_{N,1}^{c+1}(z)=\frac{\widehat{R}_{N+1,2}^{c}(0,z)}{\widehat{R}_{N+1,1}^{c}(0)}=\widehat{R}_{N+1,1}^{c}(z)-\widehat{B}_{N+1}^{c}(0,z).

This completes the proof.
∎

2.2.
Multi-fold transformations

In this subsection, we show Proposition
1.5
.

We first note that by taking
N\to\infty
of (
1.28
), we have

(2.12)

K^{c}(z,w)=d(z\bar{w})^{d-1}\sum_{l=0}^{d-1}\widehat{K}^{\frac{c+l+1}{d}-1}(z^{d},w^{d}).

Remark 2.4
.

In the opposite direction, one can also express
\widehat{K}^{c}
in terms of
K^{c}
. For instance when
d=2
, we have the relations

(2.13)

\displaystyle K^{c}(z,w)+K^{c}(z,-w)

\displaystyle=4z\bar{w}\,\widehat{K}^{\frac{c-1}{2}}(z^{2},w^{2}),

(2.14)

\displaystyle K^{c}(z,w)-K^{c}(z,-w)

\displaystyle=4z\bar{w}\,\widehat{K}^{\frac{c}{2}}(z^{2},w^{2}).

Summing these two equations, we obtain (
2.12
) with
d=2
:

(2.15)

K^{c}(z,w)=2z\bar{w}\Big(\widehat{K}^{\frac{c-1}{2}}(z^{2},w^{2})+\widehat{K}^{\frac{c}{2}}(z^{2},w^{2})\Big).

We remark that when
c\in\{0,1\}
, the term

2z\bar{w}\,\widehat{K}^{0}(z^{2},w^{2})
in the right-hand side of the above equation corresponds to the kernel appearing in the context of chiral Ginibre ensembles, see
[
2
, Theorem 3]
.

Before the proof, we interpret
K^{c}_{\textup{bulk}}
for general
d>1
from the viewpoint of Proposition
1.5
.

Example 2.5
.

(Bulk case)
It follows from the definition (
1.47
) that

\displaystyle\sum_{l=0}^{d-1}x^{l}\,E_{1,\frac{c+l+1}{d}}(x^{d})

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=0}^{d-1}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{x^{dk+l}}{\Gamma(k+\frac{c+l+1}{d})}=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{x^{j}}{\Gamma(\frac{j}{d}+\frac{1+c}{d})}=E_{\frac{1}{d},\frac{1+c}{d}}(x).

Then by (
2.12
), one can obtain
K^{c}_{\textup{bulk}}
from
\widehat{K}^{c}_{\textup{bulk}}
as

(2.16)

\displaystyle\begin{split}K^{c}_{\textup{bulk}}(z,w)&=d(z\bar{w})^{d-1}\sum_{l=0}^{d-1}\widehat{K}_{\textup{bulk}}^{\frac{c+l+1}{d}-1}(z^{d},w^{d})\\
&=d(z\bar{w})^{c}e^{-|z|^{2d}/2-|w|^{2d}/2}\sum_{l=0}^{d-1}(z\bar{w})^{l}E_{1,\frac{c+l+1}{d}}((z\bar{w})^{d})\\
&=d(z\bar{w})^{c}e^{-|z|^{2d}/2-|w|^{2d}/2}E_{\frac{1}{d},\frac{1+c}{d}}(z\bar{w}).\end{split}

Proof of Proposition
1.5
.

By the change of variable
\zeta\mapsto\zeta^{d}
, it is easy to observe that
p_{j,N}^{c}
and
q_{j,N}^{c}
enjoy the intimate relation

(2.17)

q_{dj+l,dN}^{c}(\zeta)=\sqrt{d}\,\zeta^{l}p_{j,N}^{\frac{c+l+1}{d}-1}(\zeta^{d}).

This property is also discussed in
[
17
, Section 3]
but it is easy enough to recall a proof.
By definition, we have

\displaystyle\delta_{jk}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{C}}q^{c}_{j,dN}(\zeta)\overline{q^{c}_{k,dN}(\zeta)}|\zeta|^{2c}e^{-dNV(\zeta)}\,dA(\zeta)=\int_{\mathbb{C}}p_{j,N}^{c}(\zeta)\overline{p_{k,N}^{c}(\zeta)}|\zeta|^{2c}e^{-NQ(\zeta)}\,dA(\zeta).

Since
V_{c}(\zeta)
is invariant under the discrete rotation
\zeta\mapsto e^{2\pi i/d}\cdot\zeta
, there exists a polynomial
p_{j}
such that
q_{dj+l,dN}^{c}(\zeta)=\zeta^{l}p_{j}(\zeta^{d}).

By the change of variable
\eta=\zeta^{d}
, we have

\displaystyle\delta_{jk}

\displaystyle=d\int_{0<\arg\zeta<\frac{2\pi}{d}}p_{j}(\zeta^{d})\overline{p_{k}(\zeta^{d})}|\zeta|^{2c+2l}e^{-NQ(\zeta^{d})}\,dA(\zeta)=\frac{1}{d}\int_{\mathbb{C}}p_{j}(\eta)\overline{p_{k}(\eta)}|\eta|^{\frac{2c+2l+2}{d}-2}e^{-NQ(\eta)}\,dA(\eta).

Thus we obtain

p_{j}(\zeta)=\sqrt{d}\,p_{j,N}^{\frac{c+l+1}{d}-1}(\zeta),

which leads to (
2.17
).

By (
1.10
) and (
1.11
) we have

(2.18)

\displaystyle\mathbf{K}_{dN}^{c}(\zeta,\eta)

\displaystyle=(\zeta\bar{\eta})^{c}e^{-\frac{dN}{2}V(\zeta)-\frac{dN}{2}V(\eta)}\sum_{j=0}^{dN-1}q_{j,dN}^{c}(\zeta)\overline{q_{j,dN}^{c}(\eta)},

(2.19)

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathbf{K}}_{N}^{c}(\zeta,\eta)

\displaystyle=(\zeta\bar{\eta})^{c}e^{-\frac{N}{2}Q(\zeta)-\frac{N}{2}Q(\eta)}\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}p_{j,N}^{c}(\zeta)\overline{p_{j,N}^{c}(\eta)}.

Observe here that by (
2.17
),

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{dN-1}q_{j,dN}^{c}(\zeta)\overline{q_{j,dN}^{c}(\eta)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=0}^{d-1}\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}q_{dj+l,dN}^{c}(\zeta)\overline{q_{dj+l,dN}^{c}(\eta)}=d\sum_{l=0}^{d-1}(\zeta\bar{\eta})^{l}\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}p_{j,N}^{\frac{c+l+1}{d}-1}(\zeta^{d})\overline{p_{j,N}^{\frac{c+l+1}{d}-1}(\eta^{d})}.

Thus we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbf{K}_{dN}^{c}(\zeta,\eta)

\displaystyle=d\sum_{j=0}^{d-1}(\zeta\bar{\eta})^{c+l}e^{-\frac{N}{2}Q(\zeta^{d})-\frac{N}{2}Q(\eta^{d})}\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}p_{j,N}^{\frac{c+l+1}{d}-1}(\zeta^{d})\overline{p_{j,N}^{\frac{c+l+1}{d}-1}(\eta^{d})}

\displaystyle=d(\zeta\bar{\eta})^{d-1}\sum_{l=0}^{d-1}\widehat{\mathbf{K}}_{N}^{\frac{c+l+1}{d}-1}(\zeta^{d},\eta^{d}).

Therefore we conclude

\displaystyle K_{dN}^{c}(z,w)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N^{1/d}}\mathbf{K}_{N}^{c}\Big(\frac{z}{N^{\frac{1}{2d}}},\frac{w}{N^{\frac{1}{2d}}}\Big)

\displaystyle=d(z\bar{w})^{d-1}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{l=0}^{d-1}\widehat{\mathbf{K}}_{N}^{\frac{c+l+1}{d}-1}\Big(\frac{z^{d}}{\sqrt{N}},\frac{w^{d}}{\sqrt{N}}\Big)=d(z\bar{w})^{d-1}\sum_{l=0}^{d-1}\widehat{K}_{N}^{\frac{c+l+1}{d}-1}(z^{d},w^{d}),

which completes the proof. ∎

3.
Christoffel-Darboux identity for planar orthogonal polynomials

This section is devoted to proving the Christoffel-Darboux identity, Theorem
1.1
, see
[
18
]
for a similar method of deriving such identity in the context of bi-orthogonal polynomials.
We also refer to
[
3
, Subsection 4.1]
for a version of the Christoffel-Darboux which involves differential operators.

3.1.
Elliptic potential revisited

To better introduce the general strategy of deriving the Christoffel-Darboux identity for planar orthogonal polynomials, let us first consider the elliptic potential

(3.1)

Q(\zeta):=\tfrac{1}{1-\tau^{2}}(|\zeta|^{2}-\tau\,\operatorname{Re}\zeta^{2}),\qquad\tau\in[0,1).

The random normal matrix ensemble associated with such a potential is equivalent to the
elliptic Ginibre ensemble
.
It is well known that the orthogonal polynomial with respect to the measure
e^{-NQ}\,dA
can be expressed in terms of the Hermite polynomial
H_{j}(x)=(-1)^{j}e^{x^{2}}\frac{d^{j}}{dx^{j}}e^{-x^{2}}
.
More precisely, the monic orthogonal polynomial

(3.2)

P_{j}(\zeta)=(\tfrac{\tau}{2N})^{\frac{j}{2}}H_{j}(\sqrt{\tfrac{N}{2\tau}}\zeta)

satisfies the orthogonality relation

(3.3)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{C}}P_{j}(\zeta)\overline{P_{k}(\zeta)}e^{-NQ(\zeta)}\,dA(\zeta)=h_{j}\,\delta_{jk},\qquad h_{j}=\sqrt{1-\tau^{2}}\tfrac{j!}{N^{j+1}},

see e.g.
[
52
]
or
[
4
, Lemma 7]
.

Let us write

(3.4)

W(\zeta):=e^{-\frac{\tau N}{2(1-\tau^{2})}\zeta^{2}},\qquad\psi_{j}(\zeta):=W(\zeta)P_{j}(\zeta),\qquad\phi_{j}(\zeta):=W(\zeta)\,\frac{P_{j}(\zeta)}{h_{j}}.

Then the associated correlation kernel
\widetilde{\mathbf{K}}_{N}
is written as

(3.5)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\widetilde{\mathbf{K}}_{N}(\zeta,\eta)&=e^{-\frac{N}{1-\tau^{2}}\zeta\bar{\eta}}\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}\overline{\phi_{j}(\eta)}\psi_{j}(\zeta).\end{split}

We have the following form of the Christoffel-Darboux identity.

Proposition 3.1
.

We have

(3.6)

{\bar{\partial}}_{\eta}\widetilde{\mathbf{K}}_{N}(\zeta,\eta)=\frac{N}{1-\tau^{2}}e^{-\frac{N}{1-\tau^{2}}\zeta\bar{\eta}}\Big(\tau\,\overline{\phi_{N}(\eta)}\psi_{N-1}(\zeta)-\overline{\phi_{N-1}(\eta)}\psi_{N}(\zeta)\Big).

Remark 3.2
.

Note in particular that the
1
-point function
\widetilde{\mathbf{R}}_{N}(\zeta)=\widetilde{\mathbf{K}}_{N}(\zeta,\zeta)
satisfies

(3.7)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\partial_{x}\widetilde{\mathbf{R}}_{N}(x+iy)=-2\frac{N^{N+1}}{(N-1)!}\frac{e^{-NQ(x+iy)}}{(1-\tau^{2})(1+\tau)}\mathrm{Re}\Big[P_{N}(x-iy)P_{N-1}(x+iy)\Big].\end{split}

We refer to
[
41
, Proposition 2.3]
for a direct proof of (
3.6
) using the three-term recurrence relation and differentiation rule of Hermite polynomials. (See also
[
9
, Lemma 4.1]
for a related statement.)
Together with Plancherel-Rotach type strong asymptotics of Hermite polynomials, the identities (
3.6
), (
3.7
) were used in
[
41
,
9
]
to derive the associated limiting local kernels in various situations. Beyond the study of determinantal point processes, the identity (
3.7
) was also utilized to analyse real eigenvalue distributions of real elliptic random matrices, see
[
24
]
.

Proof of Proposition
3.1
.

Let us define semi-infinite dimensional vectors

\Psi:=[\psi_{0},\psi_{1},\cdots]^{t},\qquad\Phi:=[\phi_{0},\phi_{1},\cdots]^{t},

where
t
is the transpose of a matrix and write

(3.8)

\Pi_{N}:=\textup{diag}(\underbrace{1,\cdots,1}_{N},0,\cdots)

for the projection (truncation) operator.
Then the kernel
\widetilde{\mathbf{K}}_{N}
can be rewritten as

(3.9)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\widetilde{\mathbf{K}}_{N}(\zeta,\eta)=e^{-\frac{N}{1-\tau^{2}}\zeta\bar{\eta}}\Phi^{*}(\eta)\Pi_{N}\Psi(\zeta),\end{split}

where
*
denotes the Hermitian transpose of the matrix.
We also define

\langle T|S\rangle:=\int\overline{T}\,S\,e^{-\frac{N}{1-\tau^{2}}|\zeta|^{2}}\,dA(\zeta)=\overline{\langle S|T\rangle}.

By definition, there exist semi-infinite dimensional matrices
A,B
such that

(3.10)

\displaystyle\partial\phi_{j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k}A_{jk}\phi_{k},\qquad\qquad\partial\Phi=A\Phi,

(3.11)

\displaystyle\zeta\psi_{j}(\zeta)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k}B_{jk}\psi_{k}(\zeta),\qquad\zeta\Psi(\zeta)=B\Psi.

Notice here that integration by parts gives rise to

\displaystyle B_{jk}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{C}}\overline{\phi_{k}(\zeta)}e^{-\frac{N}{1-\tau^{2}}\zeta\bar{\zeta}}\zeta\psi_{j}(\zeta)\,dA(\zeta)=-\tfrac{1-\tau^{2}}{N}\int_{\mathbb{C}}\overline{\phi_{k}(\zeta)}{\bar{\partial}}\Big(e^{-\frac{N}{1-\tau^{2}}\zeta\bar{\zeta}}\Big)\psi_{j}(\zeta)\,dA(\zeta)

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1-\tau^{2}}{N}\int_{\mathbb{C}}{\bar{\partial}}\overline{\phi_{k}(\zeta)}\psi_{j}(\zeta)\,e^{-\frac{N}{1-\tau^{2}}\zeta\bar{\zeta}}\,dA(\zeta)=\tfrac{1-\tau^{2}}{N}\overline{A_{kj}}.

In other words, we have

(3.12)

B=\frac{1-\tau^{2}}{N}A^{*}.

Using this, we obtain

(3.13)

\displaystyle\begin{split}(\zeta-\tfrac{1-\tau^{2}}{N}{\bar{\partial}}_{\eta})\Phi^{*}(\eta)\Pi_{N}\Psi(\zeta)&=\Phi^{*}(\eta)\Pi_{N}B\Psi(\zeta)-\tfrac{1-\tau^{2}}{N}\Phi^{*}(\eta)A^{*}\Pi_{N}\Psi(\zeta)\\
&=\Phi^{*}(\eta)(\Pi_{N}B-B\Pi_{N})\Psi(\zeta).\end{split}

Thus we have

(3.14)

\displaystyle\begin{split}{\bar{\partial}}_{\eta}\mathbf{K}_{N}(\zeta,\eta)&={\bar{\partial}}_{\eta}\Big[e^{-\frac{N}{1-\tau^{2}}\zeta\bar{\eta}}\Phi^{*}(\eta)\Pi_{N}\Psi(\zeta)\Big]\\
&=-\tfrac{N}{1-\tau^{2}}e^{-\frac{N}{1-\tau^{2}}\zeta\bar{\eta}}(\zeta-\tfrac{1-\tau^{2}}{N}{\bar{\partial}}_{\eta})\Phi^{*}(\eta)\Pi_{N}\Psi(\zeta)\\
&=-\tfrac{N}{1-\tau^{2}}e^{-\frac{N}{1-\tau^{2}}\zeta\bar{\eta}}\Phi^{*}(\eta)(\Pi_{N}B-B\Pi_{N})\Psi(\zeta).\end{split}

Now let us determine the matrix
B
. It follows from the three-term recurrence relation of Hermite polynomials that

(3.15)

\zeta P_{j}(\zeta)=P_{j+1}(\zeta)+\tfrac{j\tau}{N}P_{j-1}(\zeta).

Thus we obtain

(3.16)

B_{j,k}=\begin{cases}1&\text{if }k=j+1,\\
\frac{j\tau}{N}&\text{if }k=j-1,\\
0&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Using this, we conclude

\displaystyle\Phi^{*}(\eta)(\Pi_{N}B-B\Pi_{N})\Psi(\zeta)

\displaystyle=\overline{\phi_{N-1}(\eta)}B_{N-1,N}\psi_{N}(\zeta)-\overline{\phi_{N}(\eta)}B_{N,N-1}\psi_{N-1}(\zeta)

\displaystyle=\overline{\phi_{N-1}(\eta)}\psi_{N}(\zeta)-\tau\,\overline{\phi_{N}(\eta)}\psi_{N-1}(\zeta).

This completes the proof.
∎

3.2.
Gaussian potential with an insertion of a point charge

In this subsection, we derive the Chritoffel-Darboux identity (Theorem
1.1
) for the orthogonal polynomials associated with the potential of the form (
1.4
).
The overall strategy is similar to the one presented in the previous subsection. However, it requires some modifications due to the lack of standard three-term recurrence relation.
We now prove Theorem
1.1
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

As in Subsection
3.1
, we write
\Psi:=[\psi_{0},\psi_{1},\cdots]^{t}
,
\Phi:=[\phi_{0},\phi_{1},\cdots]^{t}
.

Using the projection operator
\Pi_{N}
in (
3.8
), we write

(3.17)

\widetilde{\mathbf{K}}_{N}^{c}(\zeta,\eta)=e^{-N\zeta\bar{\eta}}\Phi^{*}(\eta)\Pi_{n}\Psi(\zeta).

Then we have

(3.18)

{\bar{\partial}}_{\eta}\widetilde{\mathbf{K}}_{N}^{c}(\zeta,\eta)=-Ne^{-N\zeta\bar{\eta}}(\zeta-\tfrac{1}{N}{\bar{\partial}}_{\eta})\Phi^{*}(\eta)\Pi_{n}\Psi(\zeta).

For each
j
, let

(3.19)

L_{j,j-1}=-\frac{\int\overline{\zeta\psi_{j}(\zeta)}\,\phi_{0}(\zeta)\,e^{-N|\zeta|^{2}}\,dA(\zeta)}{\int\overline{\zeta\psi_{j-1}(\zeta)}\,\phi_{0}(\zeta)\,e^{-N|\zeta|^{2}}\,dA(\zeta)}.

Notice that the denominator does not vanish.
Note also that

\zeta(\psi_{j}(\zeta)+L_{j,j-1}\psi_{j-1}(\zeta))\,\bot\,\phi_{0}

with respect to the inner product

(3.20)

\langle T|S\rangle:=\int\overline{T}\,S\,e^{-N|\zeta|^{2}}\,dA(\zeta)=\overline{\langle S|T\rangle}

The numbers
L_{j,j-1}
are building blocks to define the lower diagonal matrix

L:=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&0&0&\dots\\
L_{2,1}&0&0&0&\dots\\
0&L_{3,2}&0&0&\dots\\
0&0&L_{4,3}&0&\dots\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\end{pmatrix}.

Write

(3.21)

\widetilde{\psi}_{j}:=\psi_{j}+L_{j,j-1}\psi_{j-1}.

Then if

\phi(\zeta)=(\text{polynomials of deg}\leq j-2)\cdot(\zeta-a)\cdot(\zeta-a)^{c},

we have

(3.22)

\langle\phi\,|\,\zeta\widetilde{\psi}_{j}\rangle=\langle\partial\phi\,|\,\widetilde{\psi}_{j}\rangle=0.

In other words, we have

\mathrm{span}\{\phi_{0},\phi_{1},\cdots,\phi_{j-1}\}\,\bot\,\zeta\widetilde{\psi}_{j},

which leads to

(3.23)

\zeta\widetilde{\psi}_{j}(\zeta)=\psi_{j+1}(\zeta)+B_{j,j}\,\psi_{j}(\zeta)

for some
B_{j,j}
.
Thus we obtain

(3.24)

\zeta(I+L)\Psi=B\,\Psi,\qquad B:=\begin{pmatrix}B_{1,1}&1&0&0&\dots\\
0&B_{2,2}&1&0&\dots\\
0&0&B_{3,3}&1&\dots\\
0&0&0&B_{4,4}&\ddots\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\ddots\end{pmatrix}.

Let us also write

(3.25)

U_{j,j+1}=-\frac{h_{j+1}}{h_{j}}\frac{P_{j}(a)}{P_{j+1}(a)},

and define the upper diagonal matrix

U:=\begin{pmatrix}0&U_{1,2}&0&0&\dots\\
0&0&U_{2,3}&0&\dots\\
0&0&0&U_{3,4}&\dots\\
0&0&0&0&\ddots\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots\end{pmatrix}.

Then the function

(3.26)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\widehat{\phi}_{j}(\zeta)&:=\phi_{j}(\zeta)+U_{j,j+1}\phi_{j+1}(\zeta)=(\text{polynomials of deg}\leq j)\cdot(\zeta-a)\cdot(\zeta-a)^{c}\end{split}

satisfies

(3.27)

\langle\partial\widehat{\phi}_{j}\,|\,\psi_{k}\rangle=\langle\widehat{\phi}_{j}\,|\,\zeta\,\psi_{k}\rangle=0\quad\text{if }k\leq j-2.

Thus we have

(3.28)

\partial\widehat{\phi}_{j}=A_{j,j}\phi_{j}+A_{j,j-1}\phi_{j-1}

for some
A_{j,k}
, equivalently,

(3.29)

\partial(I+U)\Phi=A\,\Phi,\qquad A:=\begin{pmatrix}A_{1,1}&0&0&0&\dots\\
A_{2,1}&A_{2,2}&0&0&\dots\\
0&A_{3,2}&A_{3,3}&0&\dots\\
0&0&A_{4,3}&A_{4,4}&\dots\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\ddots\end{pmatrix}.

We now determine
A_{j,j-1}
and
A_{j,j}
. Note that integration by parts gives

(3.30)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\overline{B}(I+U)^{t}&=\overline{B}\langle\Psi\,|\,\Phi^{t}\rangle(I+U)^{t}=\langle B\Psi\,|\,\Phi^{t}(I+U)^{t}\rangle\\
&=\langle\zeta(I+L)\Psi\,|\,\Phi^{t}(I+U)^{t}\rangle=\tfrac{1}{N}\langle(I+L)\Psi\,|\,\partial\Phi^{t}(I+U)^{t}\rangle\\
&=\tfrac{1}{N}\langle(I+L)\Psi\,|\,\Phi^{t}A^{t}\rangle=\tfrac{1}{N}(I+\overline{L})A^{t}.\end{split}

Thus we obtain the relation

(3.31)

\tfrac{1}{N}A(I+L^{*})=(I+U)B^{*},\qquad B=\tfrac{1}{N}(I+L)A^{*}(I+U^{*})^{-1}.

Comparing the terms involving
A_{j,j-1}
, one can observe that

(3.32)

A_{j,j-1}=N.

To determine
A_{j,j}
, note that

\displaystyle\partial\widehat{\phi}_{j}(\zeta)

\displaystyle=\partial\Big(\phi_{j}+U_{j,j+1}\phi_{j+1}\Big)=\frac{1}{h_{j}}\partial\Big((\zeta-a)^{c}P_{j}\Big)+\frac{U_{j,j+1}}{h_{j+1}}\partial\Big((\zeta-a)^{c}P_{j+1}\Big)

\displaystyle=(\zeta-a)^{c-1}\frac{1}{h_{j}}\Big[\Big(cP_{j}+(\zeta-a)P_{j}^{\prime}\Big)-\frac{P_{j}(a)}{P_{j+1}(a)}\Big(cP_{j+1}+(\zeta-a)P_{j+1}^{\prime}\Big)\Big]

\displaystyle=(\zeta-a)^{c}\frac{1}{h_{j}}\Big[\frac{c}{\zeta-a}\Big(P_{j}-\frac{P_{j}(a)}{P_{j+1}(a)}P_{j+1}\Big)+P_{j}^{\prime}-\frac{P_{j}(a)}{P_{j+1}(a)}P_{j+1}^{\prime}\Big].

This gives

A_{j,j}P_{j}+N\frac{h_{j}}{h_{j-1}}P_{j-1}=\frac{c}{\zeta-a}\Big(P_{j}-\frac{P_{j}(a)}{P_{j+1}(a)}P_{j+1}\Big)+P_{j}^{\prime}-\frac{P_{j}(a)}{P_{j+1}(a)}P_{j+1}^{\prime}.

Comparing the coefficient of
\zeta^{j}
term of this identity, we obtain

(3.33)

A_{j,j}=-\frac{P_{j}(a)}{P_{j+1}(a)}(c+j+1).

Notice in particular that
A_{j,k}
’s are real.

Now let us consider the decomposition

(3.34)

A=N\,T_{-}+A_{0},

where

T_{-}:=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&0&0&\dots\\
1&0&0&0&\dots\\
0&1&0&0&\dots\\
0&0&1&0&\dots\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\end{pmatrix},\qquad A_{0}:=\textup{diag}(A_{1,1},A_{2,2},\dots)

are the translation and the diagonal part respectively. Write

(3.35)

A^{*}=NT_{+}+A_{0}^{*},\qquad T_{+}:=T_{-}^{*}.

Note also that we have

\displaystyle\quad(T_{+}-\zeta)\Psi=(T_{+}-(I+L)^{-1}B)\Psi=(T_{+}-(I+L)^{-1}\tfrac{1}{N}(I+L)A^{*}(I+U^{*})^{-1})\Psi

\displaystyle=(T_{+}-\tfrac{1}{N}A^{*}(I+U^{*})^{-1})\Psi=(T_{+}(I+U^{*})-\tfrac{1}{N}A^{*})(I+U^{*})^{-1}\Psi=(T_{+}U^{*}-\tfrac{1}{N}A_{0}^{*})(I+U^{*})^{-1}\Psi,

where the second and the fourth identity follow from (
3.31
) and (
3.34
) respectively.

We pause here to observe that
(T_{+}U^{*}-\tfrac{1}{N}A_{0}^{*})
is invertible.
Suppose that this is not the case. Then there exists some
k
such that

U^{*}_{k-1,k}-\tfrac{1}{N}A_{k-1,k-1}=0
. Consequently, we have
\psi_{k}(\zeta)=\zeta\psi_{k-1}(\zeta)
. This is a contradiction due to the assumption
a\not=0
, see Remark
1.2
.
Therefore we have shown that
(T_{+}U^{*}-\tfrac{1}{N}A_{0}^{*})
is invertible.

By letting

(3.36)

\widehat{\Psi}:=(T_{+}U^{*}-\tfrac{1}{N}A_{0}^{*})^{-1}(T_{+}-\zeta)\Psi,

we have

(3.37)

(I+U^{*})\widehat{\Psi}=\Psi.

Note that

\displaystyle\begin{split}\widehat{\Psi}&:=[\widehat{\psi}_{0},\widehat{\psi}_{1},\cdots]^{t}=(T_{+}U^{*}-\tfrac{1}{N}A_{0}^{*})^{-1}(T_{+}-\zeta)[\psi_{0},\psi_{1},\cdots]^{t}\\
&=\textup{diag}(U^{*}_{1,2}-\tfrac{1}{N}A_{1,1},U^{*}_{2,3}-\tfrac{1}{N}A_{2,2},\cdots)^{-1}[\psi_{1}-\zeta\psi_{0},\psi_{2}-\zeta\psi_{1},\cdots]^{t}.\end{split}

Thus we have

(3.38)

\widehat{\psi}_{j}=\frac{\psi_{j+1}-\zeta\psi_{j}}{U_{j,j+1}^{*}-\tfrac{1}{N}A_{j,j}}.

Here the denominator again does not vanish due to (
3.25
) and (
3.33
).
Then by (
3.24
) and (
3.31
), we have

\displaystyle(I+L)\zeta(I+U^{*})\widehat{\Psi}

\displaystyle=(I+L)\zeta\Psi=B\Psi=B(I+U^{*})\widehat{\Psi}=\tfrac{1}{N}(I+L)A^{*}\widehat{\Psi},

which leads to

(3.39)

\zeta(I+U^{*})\widehat{\Psi}=\tfrac{1}{N}A^{*}\widehat{\Psi}.

Combining (
3.39
), (
3.29
) and (
3.31
), we obtain

\displaystyle(\zeta-\tfrac{1}{N}{\bar{\partial}}_{\eta})\Phi^{*}(\eta)\Pi_{n}\Psi(\zeta)

\displaystyle=(\zeta-\tfrac{1}{N}{\bar{\partial}}_{\eta})\Phi^{*}(\eta)\Pi_{n}(I+U^{*})\widehat{\Psi}(\zeta)

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{N}\Phi^{*}(\eta)[\Pi_{n},A^{*}]\widehat{\Psi}(\zeta)-\tfrac{1}{N}{\bar{\partial}}_{\eta}\Phi^{*}(\eta)[\Pi_{n},I+U^{*}]\widehat{\Psi}(\zeta).

Moreover by (
3.32
) and (
3.38
), we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}\Phi^{*}(\eta)[\Pi_{n},A^{*}]\widehat{\Psi}(\zeta)&=\overline{\phi_{n-1}(\eta)}A_{n,n-1}\widehat{\psi}_{n}(\zeta)-\overline{\phi_{n}(\eta)}A_{n-1,n}\widehat{\psi}_{n-1}(\zeta)\\
&=\frac{N}{U_{n,n+1}^{*}-\tfrac{1}{N}A_{n,n}}\overline{\phi_{n-1}(\eta)}(\psi_{n+1}(\zeta)-\zeta\psi_{n}(\zeta)).\end{split}

Similarly, we obtain

\displaystyle\begin{split}{\bar{\partial}}_{\eta}\Phi^{*}(\eta)[\Pi_{n},I+U^{*}]\widehat{\Psi}(\zeta)&={\bar{\partial}}_{\eta}\overline{\phi_{n-1}(\eta)}U_{n,n-1}^{*}\widehat{\psi}_{n}(\zeta)-{\bar{\partial}}_{\eta}\overline{\phi_{n}(\eta)}U_{n-1,n}^{*}\widehat{\psi}_{n-1}(\zeta)\\
&=-\frac{U_{n-1,n}^{*}}{U_{n-1,n}^{*}-\tfrac{1}{N}A_{n-1,n-1}}{\bar{\partial}}_{\eta}\overline{\phi_{n}(\eta)}(\psi_{n}(\zeta)-\zeta\psi_{n-1}(\zeta)).\end{split}

Combining all of the above identities with (
3.18
), the proof is complete.
∎

, we have

(D.8)

\widetilde{R}_{\textup{bulk}}^{c}(z)=P(c,|z|^{2}).

Proof.

By (
D.4
), we have

(D.9)

\psi_{N}(\zeta)=\zeta^{N+c}\Big[1-(-N\phi_{A}(\zeta))^{c}e^{N\phi_{A}(\zeta)}\Big(\hat{f}(-N\phi_{A}(\zeta))+O(\tfrac{1}{N})\Big)+O(\tfrac{1}{N^{\infty}})\Big].

Differentiating (
D.9
), we have

\displaystyle\psi_{N}^{\prime}(\zeta)

\displaystyle=(N+c)\zeta^{N+c-1}(1+O(\tfrac{1}{N^{\infty}}))-(N+c)\zeta^{N+c-1}(-N\phi_{A}(\zeta))^{c}e^{N\phi_{A}(\zeta)}\Big(\hat{f}(-N\phi_{A}(\zeta))+O(\tfrac{1}{N})\Big)

\displaystyle+N\zeta^{N+c-1}(a\zeta-1)(-N\phi_{A}(\zeta))^{c}e^{N\phi_{A}(\zeta)}\Big(\big(\tfrac{-c}{N\phi_{A}(\zeta)}-1\big)\big(\hat{f}(-N\phi_{A}(\zeta))+O(\tfrac{1}{N})\big)+\hat{f}^{\prime}(-N\phi_{A}(\zeta))\Big),

where we have used
\phi_{A}^{\prime}(\zeta)=(a\zeta-1)/\zeta.

Rearranging the terms using (
D.2
) and (
D.7
), we have

\displaystyle\psi_{N}^{\prime}(\zeta)

\displaystyle=(N+c)\zeta^{N+c-1}(1+O(\tfrac{1}{N^{\infty}}))-Na\zeta^{N+c}(-N\phi_{A}(\zeta))^{c}e^{N\phi_{A}(\zeta)}\big(\hat{f}(-N\phi_{A}(\zeta))+O(\tfrac{1}{N})\big).

Then it follows from

(D.10)

\zeta^{N+c}=a^{N+c}e^{\frac{1}{a}\sqrt{N}z-\frac{z^{2}}{2a^{2}}}\cdot(1+o(1))

and (
D.2
) that

\psi_{N}^{\prime}(\zeta)=a^{N+c-1}N\Big[e^{\frac{1}{a}\sqrt{N}z-\frac{z^{2}}{2a^{2}}}\cdot(1+o(1))-\Big(\hat{f}(-N\phi_{A}(\zeta))+O(\tfrac{1}{N})\Big)a^{2}(\tfrac{1-a^{2}}{a}\sqrt{N}z)^{c}e^{a\sqrt{N}z}\cdot(1+o(1))\Big].

Now let us compute the asymptotic of
\psi_{N}(\zeta)-\zeta\psi_{N-1}(\zeta).

By (
D.9
) and (
D.5
),

\displaystyle\psi_{N}(\zeta)-\zeta\psi_{N-1}(\zeta)

\displaystyle=\zeta^{N+c}(-N{\phi}_{A}(\zeta))^{c}e^{N{\phi}_{A}(\zeta)}\Big(\hat{f}(-N{\phi}_{A}(\zeta))+O(\tfrac{1}{N})\Big)

\displaystyle-\zeta^{N+c}(-(N-1)\widehat{\phi}_{A}(\zeta))^{c}e^{(N-1)\widehat{\phi}_{A}(\zeta)}\Big(\hat{f}(-(N-1)\widehat{\phi}_{A}(\zeta))+O(\tfrac{1}{N})\Big).

Note that by (
D.3
),

\displaystyle(N-1)\widehat{\phi}_{A}(\zeta)-N\phi_{A}(\zeta)

\displaystyle=a(\zeta-a)-\phi_{A}(\zeta)=\frac{z}{a}\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}+O(\frac{1}{N}).

Using this, we have

\displaystyle\Big(\frac{N-1}{N}\frac{\hat{\phi}_{A}(\zeta)}{\phi_{A}(\zeta)}\Big)^{c}e^{(N-1)\widehat{\phi}_{A}(\zeta)-N\phi_{A}(\zeta)}\frac{\hat{f}(-(N-1)\hat{\phi}_{A}(\zeta))}{\hat{f}(-N{\phi}_{A}(\zeta))}=1+\frac{z}{a}\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}+O(\frac{1}{N}).

This gives that

\displaystyle\psi_{N}(\zeta)-\zeta\psi_{N-1}(\zeta)

\displaystyle=\zeta^{N+c}(-N\phi_{A}(\zeta))^{c}e^{N\phi_{A}(\zeta)}\hat{f}(-N\phi_{A}(\zeta))\frac{1}{a}\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\cdot(z+O(\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{N}})).

Then it again follows from (
D.10
) and (
D.2
) that

(D.11)

\psi_{N}(\zeta)-\zeta\psi_{N-1}(\zeta)=a^{N+c-1}e^{a\sqrt{N}z}(\tfrac{1-a^{2}}{a}\sqrt{N}z)^{c}\hat{f}(-N\phi_{A}(\zeta))\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\cdot(z+o(1)).

Note that by (
D.4
), we have

(D.12)

\frac{P_{N+1}(a)}{P_{N}(a)}=a+o(1).

Using the above asymptotic behaviours, we obtain

(D.13)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\textup{I}_{N}^{c}(z)&=\frac{e^{-Na^{2}}}{N}\frac{a^{2N+2c-2}\,(\tfrac{1-a^{2}}{a}\sqrt{N})^{c-1}}{\tfrac{N+c}{N}h_{N-1}-h_{N}}\\
&\times\Big(e^{(\frac{1}{a}-a)\sqrt{N}\bar{z}-\frac{\bar{z}^{2}}{2a^{2}}}-\hat{f}(-N\phi_{A}(\overline{\zeta}))a^{2}(\tfrac{1-a^{2}}{a}\sqrt{N}\bar{z})^{c}\Big)\cdot(z^{c}+o(1))\end{split}

and

(D.14)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\textup{II}^{c}_{N}(z)&=\frac{e^{-Na^{2}}}{N}\frac{h_{N}/h_{N-1}\,a^{2N+2c}(\tfrac{1-a^{2}}{a}\sqrt{N})^{c-1}}{\tfrac{N+c+1}{N}h_{N}-h_{N+1}}\\
&\times\Big(e^{(\frac{1}{a}-a)\sqrt{N}\bar{z}-\frac{\bar{z}^{2}}{2a^{2}}}-\hat{f}(-N\phi_{A}(\overline{\zeta}))(\tfrac{1-a^{2}}{a}\sqrt{N}\bar{z})^{c}\Big)\cdot(z^{c}+o(1)).\end{split}

Combining above equations with

(D.15)

\hat{f}(-N\phi_{A}(\zeta))=\frac{1}{\Gamma(c)}\Big(\frac{1}{\tfrac{1-a^{2}}{a}\sqrt{N}z}+o(1)\Big),

we obtain that for
\operatorname{Re}z<0,

(D.16)

{\bar{\partial}}_{z}\widetilde{R}_{N}(z)=C_{N}(a)\,\Big(\frac{z\,|z|^{2c-2}e^{-|z|^{2}}}{\Gamma(c)}+o(1)\Big),

where

C_{N}(a):=\frac{e^{-Na^{2}}}{N}a^{2N+2c}(\tfrac{1-a^{2}}{a}\sqrt{N})^{2c-2}\Big(\frac{h_{N}/h_{N-1}}{\tfrac{N+c+1}{N}h_{N}-h_{N+1}}-\frac{1}{\tfrac{N+c}{N}h_{N-1}-h_{N}}\Big).

We remark here that the case
\operatorname{Re}z>0
follows from the case
\operatorname{Re}z<0
since in the end, the limiting point process has the rotation invariance, see
[
12
, Section 5]
.

Since
\widetilde{R}_{N}
has a non-trivial limit
\widetilde{R}
, the existence of the limit

(D.17)

C(a):=\lim_{N\to\infty}C_{N}(a),\qquad(C(a)\not=0)

follows. Therefore we obtain that

(D.18)

\widetilde{R}_{\textup{bulk}}^{c}(z)=C(a)\,P(c,|z|^{2})+\widehat{C}(a),

where
\widehat{C}(a)\in\mathbb{R}
is some constant.

We now recall from the general theory on determinantal point process that

(D.19)

\displaystyle\widetilde{R}_{\textup{bulk}}^{c}(z)

\displaystyle\to 1,\qquad\qquad(z\to\infty),

(D.20)

\displaystyle\widetilde{R}_{\textup{bulk}}^{c}(z)

\displaystyle=O(|z|^{2c}),\qquad(z\to 0),

see
[
12
, Theorem 1.4]
. This behaviour implies that
C(a)=1
and
\widehat{C}(a)=0,
which completes the proof.
∎

We recall some basic facts and definition on modular forms. For more details, one can see
[
7
]
,
[

]
. We start with some matrix groups. We define

\displaystyle\Gamma:=\mathrm{SL_{2}}(\mathbb{Z})=

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{bmatrix}a&&b\\
c&&d\end{bmatrix}:a,b,c,d\in\mathbb{Z},ad-bc=1\right\},

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\infty}:=

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{bmatrix}1&n\\

For a positive integer

\displaystyle\Gamma_{0}(N):=

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{bmatrix}a&&b\\
c&&d\end{bmatrix}\in\mathrm{SL_{2}}(\mathbb{Z}):c\equiv 0\pmod{N}\right\},

\displaystyle\Gamma_{1}(N):=

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{bmatrix}a&&b\\
c&&d\end{bmatrix}\in\Gamma_{0}(N):a\equiv d\equiv 1\pmod{N}\right\}

and

\displaystyle\Gamma(N):=\left\{\begin{bmatrix}a&&b\\
c&&d\end{bmatrix}\in\mathrm{SL_{2}}(\mathbb{Z}):a\equiv d\equiv 1\pmod{N},b\equiv c\equiv 0\pmod{N}\right\}.

A subgroup of
\Gamma=\mathrm{SL_{2}}(\mathbb{Z})
is called a congruence subgroup if it contains
\Gamma(N)
for some
N
and the smallest
N
with this property is called its level. Note that
\Gamma_{0}(N)
and
\Gamma_{1}(N)
are congruence subgroup of level
N,
whereas
\mathrm{SL_{2}}(\mathbb{Z})
and
\Gamma_{\infty}
are congruence subgroups of level
1.
The index of
\Gamma_{0}(N)
in
\Gamma
is

\displaystyle[\Gamma:\Gamma_{0}(N)]=N\prod\limits_{p|N}\left(1+\frac{1}{p}\right)

where

.

Let
\mathbb{H}
denote the upper half of the complex plane

. The group

\displaystyle\mathrm{GL_{2}^{+}}(\mathbb{R}):=\left\{\begin{bmatrix}a&&b\\
c&&d\end{bmatrix}:a,b,c,d\in\mathbb{R},ad-bc>0\right\},

acts on

by
\begin{bmatrix}a&&b\\
c&&d\end{bmatrix}z=\frac{az+b}{cz+d}.
We identify

and define
\begin{bmatrix}a&&b\\
c&&d\end{bmatrix}\frac{r}{s}=\frac{ar+bs}{cr+ds},
where

. This gives an action of

on the extended half plane

. Suppose that
\Gamma

The group

c&&d\end{bmatrix}\in\mathrm{GL_{2}^{+}}(\mathbb{R})

\displaystyle(g|_{k}\gamma)(z):=(\det\gamma)^{k/2}(cz+d)^{-k}g(\gamma z).

Definition 2.1

Let

\displaystyle g\left(\frac{az+b}{cz+d}\right)=(cz+d)^{k}g(z)

If

\displaystyle(g|_{k}\gamma)(z):=\sum\limits_{n\geq 0}a_{\gamma}(n)q_{N}^{n}

.

For a positive integer

.

Lemma 6.5
.

Let
p\geq 3
be a prime number. For a positive integer
m
,
M=p
,

and
\gamma=\begin{bmatrix}a&b\\

, we have

\displaystyle p_{m,r}(\gamma)=\min\limits_{\lambda\in\{0,1,\dots,m-1\}}\frac{1}{24}\sum\limits_{\delta|M}r_{\delta}\frac{\gcd^{2}(\delta a+\delta\kappa\lambda c,mc)}{\delta m}\geqslant 0.

Proof.

We note that

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\delta|M}r_{\delta}\frac{\gcd^{2}(\delta a+\delta\kappa\lambda c,mc)}{\delta m}=p\frac{\gcd^{2}(pa+p\kappa\lambda c,mc)}{pm}-\frac{\gcd^{2}(a+\kappa\lambda c,mc)}{m}

Since
\gamma\in\Gamma=SL_{2}(\mbox{$\mathbb{Z}$})
, we have
ad-bc=1
which implies that
\gcd(a,c)=1
. Thus it follows that
\gcd(a+\kappa\lambda c,c)=1
. Therefore it is enough to prove that

\displaystyle G:={\gcd}^{2}(pa+p\kappa\lambda c,mc)-{\gcd}^{2}(a+\kappa\lambda c,m)\geq 0

for each
\lambda\in\{0,1,\dots,m-1\}
.

Let
\lambda\in\{0,1,\dots,m-1\}
be fixed. We consider the two cases
p|c
and
p\nmid c
separately.

Case 1:
p|c
.
We set
c_{p}=\frac{c}{p}
. We observe that
\gcd(a,c)=1
implies that
\gcd(a,c_{p})=1
which in turn implies
\gcd(a+\kappa\lambda c,c_{p})=\gcd(a+\kappa\lambda pc_{p},c_{p})=1
. Hence we have

\displaystyle G

\displaystyle=p^{2}{\gcd}^{2}(a+\kappa\lambda c,mc_{p})-{\gcd}^{2}(a+\kappa\lambda c,m)

\displaystyle=p^{2}{\gcd}^{2}(a+\kappa\lambda c,m)-{\gcd}^{2}(a+\kappa\lambda c,m)>0.

Case 2:

.
In this case, we have

\displaystyle G

\displaystyle={\gcd}^{2}(p(a+\kappa\lambda c),m)-{\gcd}^{2}(a+\kappa\lambda c,m).

If
p\nmid m
, then
\gcd(p(a+\kappa\lambda c),m)=\gcd(a+\kappa\lambda c,m)
and thus
G=0
. We now assume that
p|m
. We set

\displaystyle G

\displaystyle=p^{2}{\gcd}^{2}(a+\kappa\lambda c,m_{p})-{\gcd}^{2}(a+\kappa\lambda c,m).

Let

By Radu and Seller’s method, for a prime

in Theorems

,

and

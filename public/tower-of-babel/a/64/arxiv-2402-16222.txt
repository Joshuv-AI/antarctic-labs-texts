1.
Introduction

In the present paper, we investigate the
L^{2}
-orbital stability of the 1-soliton solution for the derivative nonlinear Schrödinger (DNLS) equation

\displaystyle iq_{t}+q_{xx}+i(|q|^{2}q)_{x}=0,

(1.1)

where
q(t,x)
is the function in dimensionless space-time variables
(t,x)
. The equation (
1.1
) first appeared in the literature as a model for the propagation of large-wavelength Alfvén waves in plasma. It governs the evolution of small but finite amplitude nonlinear Alfvén waves that propagate quasi-parallel to the magnetic field in space plasma physics
[
28
,
29
]
. The DNLS equation (
1.1
) is a canonical dispersive equation derived from the Magneto-Hydrodynamic equations in the presence of the Hall effect
[
26
]
. Additionally, it is used to describe sub-picosecond pulses in single-mode optical fibers
[
1
,
3
,
36
]
.

Global existence and main result.

One crucial element required for our development is a theory of existence for the initial-value problem. Our stability result is primarily related to global existence for arbitrary data in the
L^{2}
space.

The local well-posedness of the Cauchy problem for the DNLS equation in the space
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}),s>3/2
was established by Tsutsumi and Fukuda using a parabolic regularization in
[
35
]
. Later, Takaoka further generalized the local well-posedness in spaces of lower regularity, specifically
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}),s\geq 1
, employing the Fourier transform restriction method
[
34
]
. With the aid of Strichartz estimates, Hayashi in
[
15
]
proved the global existence of solutions to the DNLS equation in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
, provided that the initial data are small in the
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
norm. Moreover, Wu in
[
38
]
removed the necessity of Hayashi’s requirement and established that for
|q_{0}|_{L^{2}}\leq 4\pi
, the DNLS equation (
1.1
) remains globally well-posed in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
and extends to
H^{1/2}(\mathbb{R})
as demonstrated in
[
12
]
.

It has been shown that the DNLS equation (
1.1
) is completely integrable and admits a Lax pair
[
19
]
. Integrability is also utilized to study global well-posedness of the DNLS equation (
1.1
)
[
18
,
25
,
31
,
32
]
. Recently, based on its complete integrability, the well-posedness for the DNLS equation (
1.1
) in low regularity cases has been intensively studied, for example, in
[
4
,
13
,
20
]
. The latest result in
[
14
]
provides a unique global solution
q\in C([0,\infty),H^{s}(\mathbb{R}))
with any
s\geq 0
, for the Cauchy problem associated with the DNLS equation (
1.1
).

Proposition 1.1
.

[
14
]

The DNLS equation (
1.1
) is globally well-posed in
H^{s}(\mathbb{R})
, for every
s\geq 0
. More precisely, given any
T>0
, an initial data
q(0)\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
and a sequence of Schwartz-class initial data
q_{n}(0)
converging to
q(0)
in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
, then the sequence of corresponding solutions
q_{n}(t)
with initial data
q_{n}(0)
converges to
q(t)
in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
uniformly when
0\leq t\leq T
.

The
L^{2}
space considered here seems natural to establish our stability:
the DNLS equation (
1.1
) is invariant under the scaling transformation

\displaystyle q(t,x)\to q_{\lambda}(t,x)=\sqrt{\lambda}q(\lambda^{2}t,\lambda x),\qquad\lambda>0,

which implies that
\|q(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}=\|q_{\lambda}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}
.
On the other hand, it is known that the infinitely many conserved quantities of the DNLS equation (
1.1
) play an important role in the well-posedness theory. The first three are the mass, energy and momentum conservation laws
respectively given by

\displaystyle M(q):=\int_{\mathbb{R}}|q|^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x,

(1.2)

\displaystyle E(q):=-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}i(\bar{q}_{x}q-q_{x}\bar{q})+|q|^{4}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x,

(1.3)

\displaystyle P(q):=\int_{\mathbb{R}}|q_{x}|^{2}+\frac{3i}{4}|q|^{2}(\bar{q}_{x}q-q_{x}\bar{q})+\frac{1}{2}|q|^{6}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x,

(1.4)

where the mass conservation quantity
M
in
L^{2}
is only necessary for the development of stability.

The exact expression of the 1-soliton solution for the DNLS equation (
1.1
) under the spectrum
z_{0}
with Im

, is provided in
[

,
31
,
39
]
.

\displaystyle\psi^{z_{0}}(t,x)=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{z_{0}e^{2(\im z_{0}^{2}x+2\im z_{0}^{4}t)}+\bar{z}_{0}e^{-2(\im z_{0}^{2}x+2\im z_{0}^{4}t)}}{z_{0}e^{-2(\im z_{0}^{2}x+2\im z_{0}^{4}t)}+\bar{z}_{0}e^{2(\im z_{0}^{2}x+2\im z_{0}^{4}t)}}\right)^{2}\times

\displaystyle\frac{2i(z_{0}^{2}-\bar{z}_{0}^{2})e^{-2i(\re z_{0}^{2}x+2\re z_{0}^{4}t)}}{z_{0}e^{-2(\im z_{0}^{2}x+2\im z_{0}^{4}t)}+\bar{z}_{0}e^{2(\im z_{0}^{2}x+2\im z_{0}^{4}t)}}.

(1.5)

Remark 1.1
.

Although there are different explicit forms for the 1-soliton of the DNLS equation (

The aim of this paper is to demonstrate the
L^{2}
-orbital stability of the 1-soliton for the DNLS equation (
1.1
). The main result is stated as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
z_{0}
be a complex number with
\im z_{0}^{2}\neq 0
, and let the associated 1-soliton
\psi^{z_{0}}(t,x)
be given by (

There exist a real small positive constant

and a constant

such that for
any

\displaystyle\|q_{0}(\cdot)-\psi^{z_{0}}(0,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}<\varepsilon,

then there exists a unique global solution

and there exists a constant

satisfying

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}^{+}}\inf_{a,b\in\mathbb{R}}\|q(t,\cdot)-e^{bi}\psi^{z_{1}}(t,\cdot+a)\|_{L^{2}}+|z_{1}-z_{0}|<C\varepsilon.

Prior work and motivation.
There are two typical approaches for studying stability issues of nonlinear dispersive equations. One method is the variational approach, which constructs solitary waves as energy minimizers under appropriate constraints
[
5
,
37
]
. Another approach to studying stability is to linearize the equation around the solitary waves; it is expected that nonlinear stability is governed by the linearized equation
[
9
]
.
In recent years, many other tools have emerged for studying low-regularity orbital stability based on complete integrability. For instance, Merle and Vega in
[
30
]
demonstrated
L^{2}
-stability and asymptotic stability of solitons for the Korteweg-de Vries (KdV) equation by invoking the Miura transformation. This special Bäcklund transformation transforms solutions of the KdV equation into solutions of the modified KdV equation.
On another front, Hoffman and Wayne in
[
16
]
formulated an abstract orbital stability result for soliton solutions of integrable equations through the Bäcklund transformations. They extended their analysis to include the orbital stability of the sine-Gordon equation and the Toda lattice. Similarly, they in
[
2
]
established that modified KdV breathers are
H^{1}
-stable, and negative energy breathers are asymptotically stable in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}),
viewing the problem from the perspective of dynamical systems. Additionally, Mizumachi and Pelinovsky in
[
27
]
proved
L^{2}
-orbital stability of the nonlinear Schrödinger (NLS) 1-soliton using the Bäcklund transformation. (This transformation is also referred to as the Darboux transformation, because it can not only transform one solution to another but also transform the eigenvector to eigenvector of the corresponding Lax pair associated with these two solutions.) They further in
[
8
]
applied this method to the Massive Thirring Model. Koch and Tataru in
[
22
]
proved low-regularity orbital stability of NLS multi-solitons. Utilizing the complete integrability of the KdV equation, Killip and Vişan in
[
21
]
employed the (doubly) renormalized perturbation to establish the determinant of orbital stability of KdV multi-solitons in
H^{-1}(\mathbb{R})
. Contreras and Pelinovsky in
[
7
]
established
L^{2}
-orbital stability of multi-solitons of the NLS equation through a dressing transformation. To the best of our knowledge, these ideas have not yet been applied to the DNLS equation (
1.1
).

For the DNLS equation (
1.1
), Guo et al., via constructing three appropriate invariants of motion, obtained the stability of solitary waves in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
for a class of solitons that admit certain limits of the spectrum parameter
[
11
]
. Their result is further supplemented in
[
6
,
23
]
. Colin and Ohta in
[
6
]
relaxed the limiting conditions on the spectrum parameter in
[
11
]
and established the stability of solitary waves in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}).
Kwon and Wu in
[
23
]
studied the zero-mass case, which was not covered by the aforementioned papers. They provided orbital stability in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
and presented a self-similar type blow-up criterion for solutions with critical mass
4\pi
. Regarding multi-solitons, with appropriate assumptions on the speeds and frequencies of the composing solitons, Le Coz and Wu established the orbital stability of multi-solitons in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
in
[
24
]
.

We study the orbital stability of the 1-soliton for the DNLS equation motivated by the following problems:

•

Until recently, Harrop-Griffiths et al. obtained the global well-posedness of the DNLS equation (
1.1
) in
L^{2}
space
[
14
]
. Therefore, it is natural to consider the
L^{2}
-orbital stability of the DNLS solitons.

•

Comparing with the classical NLS equation
[
27
]
, the Lax pair of the DNLS equation (
1.1
) possesses more symmetries. This results in its Bäcklund transformation being more complicated than that of the NLS equation, which, in turn, causes additional difficulties when using perturbation theory to analyze the properties of the solution of the Lax pair of the DNLS equation (
1.1
).

Our approach to the stability of solitons is inspired by the work of Mizumachi and Pelinovsky in
[

]
.

In the first step, at time

The rest of this paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we review the process of constructing the Bäcklund transformation.
Section
3
explains how the Bäcklund transformation turns the area around a 1-soliton into an area around the zero solution in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
at time
t=0
.
In Section
4
, assuming the initial data are smooth enough, the Bäcklund transformation creates a smooth connection between the neighborhood of the zero solution and the neighborhood of a 1-soliton, linking solutions around 1-soliton and solutions around the zero solution at all times.
The proof of Theorem
1.1
is presented in Section

.

Preliminaries

The DNLS equitation (

\displaystyle\Phi_{x}=X(q,z)\Phi,

(2.1)

\displaystyle\Phi_{t}=T(q,z)\Phi,

(2.2)

(2.3)

\displaystyle U(q,z)=z\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&q\\

\displaystyle q^{(1)}=\left(\frac{z|\Phi_{2}|^{2}+\bar{z}|\Phi_{1}|^{2}}{z|\Phi_{1}|^{2}+\bar{z}|\Phi_{2}|^{2}}\right)^{2}\left[-q+\frac{2i(z^{2}-\bar{z}^{2})\Phi_{1}\bar{\Phi}_{2}}{z|\Phi_{2}|^{2}+\bar{z}|\Phi_{1}|^{2}}\right],

(2.12)

\displaystyle\Phi^{(1)}_{1}=\frac{\bar{\Phi}_{2}}{z|\Phi_{1}|^{2}+\bar{z}|\Phi_{2}|^{2}},\qquad\Phi^{(1)}_{2}=\frac{\bar{\Phi}_{1}}{\bar{z}|\Phi_{1}|^{2}+z|\Phi_{2}|^{2}}.

Lemma 3.1

\displaystyle|z_{1}-z_{0}|+\|\tilde{\Phi}-\vec{\Phi}^{z_{0}}_{1}\|_{H^{1}}\mathchar 13358\relax\|q_{0}-\psi^{z_{0}}_{0}\|_{2}.

Proof.

We rewrite the spectral problem (

\displaystyle\left(\sigma_{3}\partial_{x}+iz^{2}-z\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&q_{0}\\

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(z)=\sigma_{3}\partial_{x}+iz^{2}-z\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&\psi^{z}_{0}\\

Then

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(z_{0})\tilde{\Phi}=\tilde{\Delta}\tilde{\Phi},

\displaystyle\tilde{\Delta}=-i(z_{0}-z)(z_{0}+z)+z_{0}\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&\psi^{z_{0}}_{0}\\

To solve the perturbation equation (

\{\vec{\Phi}^{z_{0}}_{1}\}

to

\displaystyle(\sigma_{3}\partial_{x}+iz_{0}^{2})^{-1}\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}f_{1}\\
f_{2}\end{array}\right)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\int^{x}_{+\infty}e^{-iz_{0}^{2}(x-s)}f_{1}(s)ds\\
-\int^{x}_{+\infty}e^{iz_{0}^{2}(x-s)}f_{2}(s)ds\end{array}\right).

A simple calculation leads to

\displaystyle z_{0}\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&\psi^{z_{0}}_{0}\\

\bar{\psi}^{z_{0}}_{0}(x)\int^{x}_{+\infty}e^{-iz_{0}^{2}(x-s)}f_{1}(s)ds\end{array}\right).

Then,

\bar{\psi}^{z_{0}}_{0}&0\end{array}\right)(\sigma_{3}\partial_{x}+iz_{0}^{2})^{-1}

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(z_{0})(\sigma_{3}\partial_{x}+iz_{0}^{2})^{-1}=I-z_{0}\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&\psi^{z_{0}}_{0}\\
\bar{\psi}^{z_{0}}_{0}&0\end{array}\right)(\sigma_{3}\partial_{x}+iz_{0}^{2})^{-1}

\{\vec{\Phi}^{z_{0}}_{1}\}

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(z_{0})\Phi_{s}=(I-Q)\tilde{\Delta}(\Phi_{s}+\vec{\Phi}^{z_{0}}_{1}).

\displaystyle-\langle z\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&q_{0}-\psi^{z_{0}}_{0}\\
\bar{q}_{0}-\bar{\psi}^{z_{0}}_{0}&0\end{array}\right)(\Phi_{s}+\vec{\Phi}^{z_{0}}_{1}),\sigma_{1}\overline{\vec{\Phi}^{z_{0}}_{1}}\rangle

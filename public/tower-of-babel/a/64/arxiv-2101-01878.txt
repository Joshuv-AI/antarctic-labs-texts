A curl-free improvement of the Rellich-Hardy inequality with weight
Naoki Hamamoto and Futoshi Takahashi

Abstract
. We consider the best constant in the Rellich-Hardy inequality (with a radial power weight) for curl-free vector fields on ℝ N \mathbb{R}^{N} , originally found by Tertikas-Zographopoulos [ 12 ] for unconstrained fields. This inequality is considered as an intermediate between Hardy-Leray and Rellich-Leray inequalities. Under the curl-free condition, we compute the new explicit best constant in the inequality and prove the non-attainability of the constant. This paper is a sequel to [ 6 , 7 ] .



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
Primary 26D10; Secondary 35A23.

1.
Introduction

Let
N\in\mathbb{N}
be an integer with
N\geq 2
, let
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
and put
{\bm{x}}=(x_{1},\cdots,x_{N})\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
.
In the following, the notation
\mathcal{D}_{\gamma}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
denotes the set of compact supported, smooth vector fields on
\mathbb{R}^{N}

{\bm{u}}=(u_{1},u_{2},\cdots,u_{N}):\mathbb{R}^{N}\ni{\bm{x}}\mapsto{\bm{u}}({\bm{x}})\in\mathbb{R}^{N}

with the integrability condition

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|{\bm{u}}|^{2}|{\bm{x}}|^{2\gamma-2}dx<\infty.

1.1.
Preceding results and motivation

It is well known that the
Hardy-Leray inequality

\left(\gamma+\tfrac{N}{2}-1\right)^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|{\bm{u}}|^{2}}{|{\bm{x}}|^{2}}|{\bm{x}}|^{2\gamma}d{x}\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla{\bm{u}}|^{2}|{\bm{x}}|^{2\gamma}d{x}

(1)

holds for any vector field
{\bm{u}}\in\mathcal{D}_{\gamma}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, with the best constant on the left-hand side.
This was first proved by J. Leray
[
10
]
for
(N,\gamma)=(3,0)
, as a higher-dimensional extension of Hardy’s inequality in one dimension
[
8
]
, see also the book by Ladyzhenskaya
[
9
]
.
Costin and Maz’ya
[
4
]
improved the best value of the constant by assuming
{\bm{u}}
to be divergence-free (under the additional assumption of axisymmetry for
N\geq 3
): for the case
N=2
, it was shown that the inequality

C_{\gamma}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{|{\bm{u}}|^{2}}{|{\bm{x}}|^{2}}|{\bm{x}}|^{2\gamma}dx\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|\nabla{\bm{u}}|^{2}|{\bm{x}}|^{2\gamma}dx

holds with the best constant
C_{\gamma}=\begin{cases}\frac{3+(\gamma-1)^{2}}{1+(\gamma-1)^{2}}\gamma^{2}&\left(|\gamma+1|\leq\sqrt{3}\right)\\
\gamma^{2}+1&\left(|\gamma+1|>\sqrt{3}\right)\end{cases}
for divergence-free vector fields
{\bm{u}}\in\mathcal{D}_{\gamma}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
.
Since there is an isometry on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
between divergence-free fields and curl-free fields, the same conclusion also holds for the two-dimensional curl-free fields.
As a generalization of this result, we have derived in recent papers
[
6
,
7
]
the Hardy-Leray inequality

H_{N,\gamma}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|{\bm{u}}|^{2}}{|{\bm{x}}|^{2}}|{\bm{x}}|^{2\gamma}dx\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla{\bm{u}}|^{2}|{\bm{x}}|^{2\gamma}dx

(2)

for curl-free fields
{\bm{u}}\in\mathcal{D}_{\gamma}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
with the best constant

H_{N,\gamma}=\begin{cases}\left(\gamma+\frac{N}{2}-1\right)^{2}\frac{3(N-1)+\left(\gamma+\frac{N}{2}-2\right)^{2}}{N-1+\left(\gamma+\frac{N}{2}-2\right)^{2}}&\text{if}\quad\big|\gamma+\frac{N}{2}\big|\leq\sqrt{N+1},\\
\left(\gamma+\frac{N}{2}-1\right)^{2}+N-1&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

(3)

Since
C_{\gamma}=H_{2,\gamma}
, this result recovers Costin-Maz’ya’s one for
N=2
.

The
Rellich-Leray inequality
is given by

B_{N,\gamma}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|{\bm{u}}|^{2}}{|{\bm{x}}|^{4}}|{\bm{x}}|^{2\gamma}dx\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\triangle{\bm{u}}|^{2}|{\bm{x}}|^{2\gamma}dx

(4)

for unconstrained fields
{\bm{u}}\in\mathcal{D}_{\gamma-1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, where the constant
B_{N,\gamma}
is sharp when

B_{N,\gamma}=\min_{\nu\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}}\left(\left(\gamma-1\right)^{2}-\left(\nu+\tfrac{N}{2}-1\right)^{2}\right)^{2}.

(5)

This was found by Rellich
[
11
]
for
\gamma=0
and Caldiroli-Musina
[
2
]
for
\gamma\neq 0
.
In recent papers
[
6
,
7
]
, we additionally considered the curl-free improvement of the Rellich-Leray inequality: if
{\bm{u}}\in\mathcal{D}_{\gamma-1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
is assumed to be curl-free, then the same inequality (
4
) holds with the best constant
B_{N,\gamma}
:

B_{N,\gamma}=\min\left\{\left((\gamma-1)^{2}-\tfrac{N^{2}}{4}\right)^{2},\ \min_{\nu\in\mathbb{N}}\tfrac{\left(\gamma+\frac{N}{2}-1\right)^{2}+\alpha_{\nu}}{\left(\gamma+\frac{N}{2}-3\right)^{2}+\alpha_{\nu}}\left((\gamma-2)^{2}-\left(\nu+\tfrac{N}{2}-1\right)^{2}\right)^{2}\right\};

(6)

here and hereafter we use the notation

\alpha_{s}={s}({s}+N-2)

(7)

for any
{s}\in\mathbb{R}
.

In this paper, we are interested in another version of Rellich-Leray inequality
[
1
,
5
,
12
]
:

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\triangle{\bm{u}}|^{2}|{\bm{x}}|^{2\gamma}dx\geq A_{N,\gamma}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|\nabla{\bm{u}}|^{2}}{|{\bm{x}}|^{2}}|{\bm{x}}|^{2\gamma}dx,\qquad\forall{\bm{u}}\in\mathcal{D}_{\gamma-1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})

(8)

holds with the best constant
\displaystyle A_{N,\gamma}=\min_{\nu\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}}A_{N,\gamma,\nu}
, where

A_{N,\gamma,\nu}:=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}\left(\gamma-\frac{N}{2}\right)^{2}&\text{for }\nu=0,\\
\frac{\left(\left(\gamma-1\right)^{2}-\left(\nu+\frac{N}{2}-1\right)^{2}\right)^{2}}{\left(\gamma+\frac{N}{2}-2\right)^{2}+\alpha_{\nu}}&\text{for }\nu\in\mathbb{N}.\end{array}\right.

(9)

We call (
8
) the
Rellich-Hardy inequality
.
This inequality was first found for
N\geq 5
by Tertikas-Zographopoulos
[
12
, Theorem 1.7]
.
Subsequently, Beckner
[
1
]
and Ghoussoub-Moradifam
[
5
]
established the same inequality when
N\in\{3,4\}
and
\gamma=0
,
with the best constants
A_{3,0}=\frac{25}{36}
and
A_{4,0}=3
.
See also Cazacu
[
3
]
for the unified proof of the inequality when
\gamma=0
.

Now, let us assume that
\gamma
satisfies

A_{N,\gamma}=\min_{\nu\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}}A_{N,\gamma,\nu}=A_{N,\gamma,0}=\left(\gamma-\tfrac{N}{2}\right)^{2}.

Then we see that a successive application of Rellich-Hardy and Hardy-Leray inequalities reproduces Rellich-Leray inequality (
4
): we have

\begin{split}\mathop{\int}_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\triangle{\bm{u}}|^{2}|{\bm{x}}|^{2\gamma}dx&\geq A_{N,\gamma,0}\mathop{\int}_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla{\bm{u}}|^{2}|{\bm{x}}|^{2(\gamma-1)}dx\qquad\qquad\ \ \qquad\left(\text{from \eqref{RH}}\right)\\
&\geq A_{N,\gamma,0}\left(\gamma+\tfrac{N}{2}-2\right)^{2}\mathop{\int}_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|{\bm{u}}|^{2}}{|{\bm{x}}|^{2}}|{\bm{x}}|^{2(\gamma-1)}dx\quad\left(\begin{array}[]{ll}\small\text{from \eqref{Hardy}}\\
\small\text{with $\gamma$ replaced by $\gamma-1$}\end{array}\right)\\
&=\left(\left(\gamma-1\right)^{2}-\left(\tfrac{N}{2}-1\right)^{2}\right)^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|{\bm{u}}|^{2}}{|{\bm{x}}|^{4}}|{\bm{x}}|^{2\gamma}dx\\
&\geq B_{N,\gamma}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|{\bm{u}}|^{2}}{|{\bm{x}}|^{4}}|{\bm{x}}|^{2\gamma}dx\end{split}

with
B_{N,\gamma}
given by (
5
).
Hence, Rellich-Hardy inequality can be considered as a stronger version of Rellich-Leray inequality
and plays a role as an intermediate between Rellich-Leray and Hardy-Leray inequalities.

In the context of the curl-free improvement, it seems also natural to ask whether the same phenomenon will happen in (
8
); in particular, the main interest of our problem is how the best constant in (
8
) will change when
{\bm{u}}
is assumed to be curl-free.

1.2.
Results

Motivated by the observation above, we aim to derive the best constant in Rellich-Hardy inequality for curl-free fields.
Now, our main result reads as follows:

Theorem 1
.

Let
N\geq 2
.
Let
{\bm{u}}\in\mathcal{D}_{\gamma-1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
be a curl-free vector field. Then the inequality

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\triangle{\bm{u}}|^{2}|{\bm{x}}|^{2\gamma}dx\geq C_{N,\gamma}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|\nabla{\bm{u}}|^{2}}{|{\bm{x}}|^{2}}|{\bm{x}}|^{2\gamma}dx

(10)

holds with the best constant
C_{N,\gamma}
expressed as

C_{N,\gamma}=\min_{\nu\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}}C_{N,\gamma,\nu},

where

\displaystyle C_{N,\gamma,0}=\frac{\left((\gamma-1)^{2}-\frac{N^{2}}{4}\right)^{2}}{\left(\gamma+\frac{N}{2}-2\right)^{2}+N-1}=A_{N,\gamma,1},

(11)

\displaystyle C_{N,\gamma,1}=\frac{\left(\gamma-\frac{N}{2}-2\right)^{2}\left(\left(\gamma+\frac{N}{2}-1\right)^{2}+N-1\right)}{\left(\gamma+\frac{N}{2}-3\right)^{2}+3(N-1)}

and

C_{N,\gamma,\nu}=\tfrac{\left((\gamma-2)^{2}-\left(\nu+\frac{N}{2}-1\right)^{2}\right)^{2}\left(\left(\gamma+\frac{N}{2}-1\right)^{2}+\alpha_{\nu}\right)}{\left((\gamma-2)^{2}-\left(\nu+\frac{N}{2}-1\right)^{2}\right)^{2}+2(\gamma-1)\left(\left(2\gamma+N-5\right)\alpha_{\nu}+(N-1)\left(\gamma+\frac{N}{2}-3\right)^{2}\right)}\quad\text{ for }\nu\geq 2.

Moreover, we obtain a stronger inequality by adding a remainder term to the right-hand side of (
10
).

Theorem 2
.

Let
C_{N,\gamma}
be the same constant as in Theorem
1
. Then there exists an absolute constant
c>0
such that the inequality

\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\triangle{\bm{u}}|^{2}|{\bm{x}}|^{2\gamma}dx&-C_{N,\gamma}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|\nabla{\bm{u}}|^{2}}{|{\bm{x}}|^{2}}|{\bm{x}}|^{2\gamma}dx\\
&\geq{c}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left|\nabla\left(|{\bm{x}}|^{2-\frac{N}{2}-\gamma}({\bm{x}}\cdot\nabla)\big(|{\bm{x}}|^{\gamma+\frac{N}{2}-2}{\bm{u}}\big)\right)\right|^{2}|{\bm{x}}|^{2\gamma-2}dx\end{split}

(12)

holds for all curl-free fields
{\bm{u}}\in\mathcal{D}_{\gamma-1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
.

Remark 3
.

From the proof below, we see that the constant
c
on the right-hand side of (
12
) can be estimated by

\displaystyle c\geq 1

\displaystyle\text{\rm when}\quad\gamma\leq 1,

\displaystyle c\geq 1/2

\displaystyle\text{\rm when}\quad N\geq 3\quad\text{\rm and}\quad\gamma>1,

\displaystyle c\geq 1/3\quad

\displaystyle\text{\rm when}\quad N=2\quad\text{\rm and}\quad\gamma>1.

However, the best possible (the largest) value of
c
is unknown.

As a direct consequence of Theorem
2
, we can conclude that the best constant
C_{N,\gamma}
of the inequality (
10
) is never attained in
\mathcal{D}_{\gamma-1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\setminus\{{\bm{0}}\}
:

Corollary 4
.

If the equation

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\triangle{\bm{u}}|^{2}|{\bm{x}}|^{2\gamma}dx=C_{N,\gamma}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|\nabla{\bm{u}}|^{2}}{|{\bm{x}}|^{2}}|{\bm{x}}|^{2\gamma}dx

holds for a curl-free field
{\bm{u}}\in\mathcal{D}_{\gamma-1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, then
{\bm{u}}\equiv{\bm{0}}
.

Proof.

Let (
10
) holds true. Then the right-hand side of (
12
) must vanish.
Thus

{r}\partial_{r}\left({r}^{\gamma+\frac{N}{2}-2}{\bm{u}}({r}{\bm{\sigma}})\right)={r}^{\gamma+\frac{N}{2}-2}{\bm{x}}_{0}

holds for some constant vector filed
{\bm{x}_{0}}
, where
({r},{\bm{\sigma}})=(|{\bm{x}}|,{\bm{x}}/|{\bm{x}}|)
.
Integrating both sides on any interval
[{s},{r}]\subset\mathbb{R}_{+}
with respect to the measure
\frac{1}{{r}}d{r}
,
we have

{\bm{u}}({r}{\bm{\sigma}})=\begin{cases}\left(\frac{{s}}{{r}}\right)^{\gamma+\frac{N}{2}-2}{\bm{u}}({s}{\bm{\sigma}})+\frac{\left(\frac{{s}}{{r}}\right)^{\gamma+\frac{N}{2}-2}-1}{\gamma+\frac{N}{2}-2}{\bm{x}}_{0}&(\gamma\neq 2-\tfrac{N}{2})\\
{\bm{u}}({s}{\bm{\sigma}})+{\bm{x}}_{0}\log\frac{{s}}{{r}}&(\gamma=2-\tfrac{N}{2})\end{cases}.

In the case
\gamma\neq 2-\frac{N}{2}
, take the limit
s\to\infty
(resp.
s\to+0
) when
\gamma<2-\frac{N}{2}
(resp.
\gamma>2-\frac{N}{2}
),
then we obtain

{\bm{u}}({r}{\bm{\sigma}})\equiv\tfrac{-1}{\gamma+\frac{N}{2}-2}{\bm{x}}_{0}\quad\text{ and hence }\quad{\bm{u}}({\bm{x}})\equiv{\bm{u}}({\bm{0}}).

This fact together with the integrability condition
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|{\bm{u}}|^{2}|{\bm{x}}|^{2\gamma-4}dx<\infty
says that

{\bm{u}}({\bm{0}})
must vanish, whence
{\bm{u}}\equiv{\bm{0}}
.
In the case
\gamma=2-\frac{N}{2}
, taking
{s}\to 0
leads to

{\bm{u}}({r}{\bm{\sigma}})={\bm{u}}({\bm{0}})+{\bm{x}}_{0}\lim_{{s}\to 0}\log\frac{{s}}{{r}}

and the finiteness of the right-hand side yields
{\bm{x}}_{0}={\bm{0}}
.
Therefore, we see again that
{\bm{u}}\equiv{\bm{u}}({\bm{0}})
and hence
{\bm{u}}\equiv{\bm{0}}
. ∎

As another direct consequence of Theorem
2
, we have the following fact:

Corollary 5
.

Let
C_{N,\gamma}
be the same constant as in Theorem
1
. Then there exists an absolute constant
c>0
such that the inequality

\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla\triangle\phi|^{2}|{\bm{x}}|^{2\gamma}dx&-C_{N,\gamma}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|D^{2}\phi|^{2}}{|{\bm{x}}|^{2}}|{\bm{x}}|^{2\gamma}dx\\
&\geq{c}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left|\nabla\left(|{\bm{x}}|^{2-\frac{N}{2}-\gamma}({\bm{x}}\cdot\nabla)\big(|{\bm{x}}|^{\gamma+\frac{N}{2}-2}\nabla\phi\big)\right)\right|^{2}|{\bm{x}}|^{2\gamma-2}dx\end{split}

holds for all scalar field
\phi
such that
\nabla\phi\in\mathcal{D}_{\gamma-1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
.
Here
D^{2}\phi
denotes a Hessian matrix of
\phi
.

2.
Preliminary for the proof of main theorem

2.1.
Notations and definitions in vector calculus on
\dot{\mathbb{R}}^{N}

Here we summarize the minimum required notations and definitions for the proof of our main theorems.
We basically use the notation

\dot{\mathbb{R}}^{N}=\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{{\bm{0}}\}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mathbb{S}^{N-1}=\left\{{\bm{x}}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}:\ |{\bm{x}}|=1\right\}.

For every vector
{\bm{x}}\in\dot{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
, the notation

{r}=|{\bm{x}}|>0,\quad{\bm{\sigma}}={\bm{x}}/|{\bm{x}}|\in\mathbb{S}^{N-1}

denotes the radius of
{\bm{x}}
and its unit-vector part, which defines the smooth transformation

\dot{\mathbb{R}}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{S}^{N-1},\quad{\bm{x}}\mapsto({r},{\bm{\sigma}})

together with its inverse

\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{S}^{N-1}\to\dot{\mathbb{R}}^{N},\quad({r},{\bm{\sigma}})\mapsto{r}{\bm{\sigma}}.

Every vector field
{\bm{u}}=(u_{1},u_{2},\cdots,u_{N}):\dot{\mathbb{R}}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}^{N}
has its radial scalar component
u_{R}=u_{R}({\bm{x}})
and spherical vector part
{\bm{u}}_{S}={\bm{u}}_{S}({\bm{x}})
given by the formulae

{\bm{u}}={\bm{\sigma}}u_{R}+{\bm{u}}_{S},\quad\ {\bm{\sigma}}\cdot{\bm{u}}_{S}=0

for all
{\bm{x}}\in\dot{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
; the two fields are explicitly given by

u_{R}={\bm{\sigma}}\cdot{\bm{u}}\quad\text{ and }\quad{\bm{u}}_{S}={\bm{u}}-{\bm{\sigma}}u_{R}.

In a similar way, the gradient operator
\nabla=\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{1}},\cdots,\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{N}}\right)
can be decomposed into the radial derivative
\partial_{r}
and the spherical gradient
\nabla_{\!\sigma}
as

\nabla={\bm{\sigma}}\partial_{r}+\frac{1}{{r}}\nabla_{\!\sigma},

in order that
\partial_{r}f=(\nabla f)_{R}={\bm{\sigma}}\cdot\nabla f
and
\frac{1}{{r}}\nabla_{\!\sigma}f=(\nabla f)_{S}
for all
f\in C^{\infty}(\dot{\mathbb{R}}^{N})
.
The notation

\partial:={r}\partial_{r}={\bm{x}}\cdot\nabla

(13)

denotes an alternative radial derivative, in order that the above decomposition formula of
\nabla
can be rewritten as

{r}\nabla={\bm{\sigma}}\partial+\nabla_{\!\sigma}.

The Laplace operator
\triangle=\sum_{k=1}^{N}\partial^{2}/\partial x_{k}^{2}
is known to be expressed in terms of
({r},{\bm{\sigma}})
by the formula

\triangle=\frac{1}{{r}^{N-1}}\partial_{r}\left({r}^{N-1}\partial_{r}\right)+\frac{1}{{r}^{2}}\triangle_{\sigma}=\frac{1}{r^{2}}\left(\partial^{2}+(N-2)\partial+\triangle_{\sigma}\right),

where
\triangle_{\sigma}
denotes the Laplace-Beltrami operator on
\mathbb{S}^{N-1}
.
We understand that the action of the operator
\partial_{r}
or
\partial
on a vector field
{\bm{u}}
is associated with the function
{r}\mapsto{\bm{u}}({r}{\bm{\sigma}})
for
{\bm{\sigma}}\in\mathbb{S}^{N-1}
fixed,
whereas
\nabla_{\!\sigma}
or
\triangle_{\sigma}
is associated with the function
{\bm{\sigma}}\mapsto{\bm{u}}({r}{\bm{\sigma}})
for
{r}=|{\bm{x}}|
fixed.
As a simple example, the operation of
\nabla
and
\triangle
on the scalar field
{r}=|{\bm{x}}|
or its powers gives

\nabla{r}={\bm{\sigma}}
and
\triangle{r}^{s}=\alpha_{s}{r}^{s-2}
for all
s\in\mathbb{R}
, where
\alpha_{s}
is the same as in (
7
).

2.2.
Radial-spherical-scalar representation of curl-free fields

Every vector field
{\bm{u}}\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})^{N}
is said to be curl-free if

\frac{\partial u_{k}}{\partial x_{j}}=\frac{\partial u_{j}}{\partial x_{k}}\quad\text{ on }\mathbb{R}^{N}\qquad\forall j,k\in\{1,\cdots,N\},

or equivalently if there exists a scalar field
\phi\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
satisfying

{\bm{u}}=\nabla\phi\quad\text{ on }\mathbb{R}^{N}.

(14)

In view of this equation, we say that
{\bm{u}}
has a scalar potential
\phi
. As another representation of curl-free fields, let us recall the following fact:

Proposition 6

(
[
7
]
)
.

Let
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
.
Then a vector field
{\bm{u}}\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})^{N}
is curl-free
if and only if there exist two scalar fields
f,\varphi\in C^{\infty}(\dot{\mathbb{R}}^{N})
satisfying

\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}f\text{ is radially symmetric and }\ \int_{\mathbb{S}^{N-1}}\varphi(r{\bm{\sigma}})\mathrm{d}\sigma=0&\forall\,r>0,\\
{r}^{1-\lambda}{\bm{u}}={\bm{\sigma}}\big(f+(\lambda+\partial_{t})\varphi\big)+\nabla_{\!\sigma}\varphi&{\rm on}\ \mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{{\bm{0}}\}.\end{array}\right.

Moreover, such
f
and
\varphi
are uniquely determined, and they are explicitly given by the equations

f={r}^{-\lambda}\partial\overline{\phi}\quad\text{ and }\quad\varphi={r}^{-\lambda}\left(\phi-\overline{\phi}\right),

where we set
\overline{\phi}({\bm{x}})=\frac{1}{|\mathbb{S}^{N-1}|}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{N-1}}\phi(|{\bm{x}}|{\bm{\sigma}})\mathrm{d}\sigma
as the spherical mean of the scalar potential
\phi
given by (
14
). In particular, if
{\bm{u}}
has a compact support on
\dot{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
, then so do
f
and
\varphi
.

Later, we will use Proposition
6
by choosing
\lambda=2-\frac{N}{2}-\gamma
. (See (
20
)).

3.
Proof of Theorem
1

In this section, we prove Theorem
1
.
Roughly speaking, the proof consists of theoretical part (§
3.1
and §
3.2
) and computational part (from §
3.3
to §
3.7
).
Since the theoretical part is already well established in our previous work, we will only state its minimum required content: we exploit some
L^{2}
formulae of curl-free fields given in
[
7
]
.
Instead, emphasis is placed on the computational part.

3.1.
Reduction to the case of compact support on
\dot{\mathbb{R}}^{N}

Let
\phi
be the scalar potential of the curl-free field
{\bm{u}}
satisfying
\phi({\bm{0}})=0
. The integrability condition
{\bm{u}}\in\mathcal{D}_{\gamma-1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})

(
namely
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|{\bm{u}}|^{2}|{\bm{x}}|^{2\gamma-4}dx<\infty
)

together with the smoothness of
{\bm{u}}
on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
implies that there exists an integer
m>\frac{3}{2}-\gamma
satisfying

{\bm{u}}({\bm{x}})=\nabla\phi({\bm{x}})=O(|{\bm{x}}|^{m}),\quad\text{ and hence }\quad\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\phi({\bm{x}})=O(|{\bm{x}}|^{m+1}),\\
\nabla{\bm{u}}({\bm{x}})=O(|{\bm{x}}|^{m-1}),\\
\triangle{\bm{u}}({\bm{x}})=O(|{\bm{x}}|^{m-2})\end{array}\right.

as
{\bm{x}}\to{\bm{0}}
.
Then it additionally follows that the integrals

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\phi^{2}|{\bm{x}}|^{2\gamma-6}dx,\quad\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla{\bm{u}}|^{2}|{\bm{x}}|^{2\gamma-2}dx\quad\text{ and }\quad\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\triangle{\bm{u}}|^{2}|{\bm{x}}|^{2\gamma}dx

(15)

are all finite.

For the purpose of deriving the best constant
C_{N,\gamma}
in inequality (
10
), it is enough to consider the case where the curl-free field
{\bm{u}}=\nabla\phi
is compactly supported on
\dot{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
.
Here let us verify this fact. First of all let us define
\{{\bm{u}}_{n}\}\subset C_{c}^{\infty}(\dot{\mathbb{R}}^{N})^{N}
as a sequence of curl-free fields by

{\bm{u}}_{n}({\bm{x}}):=\nabla\left({\zeta}\left(\frac{1}{n}\log|{\bm{x}}|\right)\phi({\bm{x}})\right)\quad\text{for every }\ n\in\mathbb{N},

where
{\zeta}\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
such that

{\zeta}({t})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}0&\text{ for }\ {t}\leq-1\\
1&\text{ for }\ 1\leq{t}\end{array}\right.
.
We use the abbreviations such as
\zeta_{n}=\zeta\left(\frac{1}{n}\log|{\bm{x}}|\right)
,
\zeta_{n}^{\prime}=\zeta^{\prime}\left(\frac{1}{n}\log|{\bm{x}}|\right)
, and
\zeta_{n}^{\prime\prime}=\zeta^{\prime\prime}\left(\frac{1}{n}\log|{\bm{x}}|\right)
.
Noticing the asymptotic formulae

\displaystyle\partial\zeta_{n}=\frac{1}{n}\zeta_{n}^{\prime}=O(n^{-1}),\quad\nabla\zeta_{n}=\frac{{\bm{\sigma}}}{n{r}}\zeta_{n}^{\prime}=\frac{{\bm{\sigma}}}{{r}}O(n^{-1}),\quad\nabla\zeta_{n}^{\prime}=\frac{{\bm{\sigma}}}{n{r}}\zeta_{n}^{\prime\prime}=\frac{{\bm{\sigma}}}{{r}}O(n^{-1})

as
n\to\infty
,
we have the following calculations:

\displaystyle{\bm{u}}_{n}

\displaystyle=\nabla\left(\zeta_{n}\phi\right)=(\nabla\zeta_{n})\phi+\zeta_{n}\nabla\phi=\frac{{\bm{\sigma}}\phi}{{r}}O(1/n)+\zeta_{n}{\bm{u}},

\displaystyle\nabla{\bm{u}}_{n}

\displaystyle=\nabla\left(\frac{{\bm{\sigma}}}{n\hskip 1.00006pt{r}}{\zeta}^{\prime}_{n}\phi+{\zeta}_{n}{\bm{u}}\right)=\frac{{\bm{\sigma}}}{n{r}}(\nabla\zeta_{n}^{\prime})\phi+\frac{\zeta_{n}^{\prime}}{n}\nabla\frac{{\bm{\sigma}}\phi}{{r}}+(\nabla\zeta_{n}){\bm{u}}+\zeta_{n}\nabla{\bm{u}}

\displaystyle=\frac{{\bm{\sigma}}{\bm{\sigma}}\phi}{{r}^{2}}O(n^{-2})+\frac{\zeta_{n}^{\prime}}{n}\left(\frac{{\bm{\sigma}}}{{r}}\nabla\phi+\left(\nabla\frac{{\bm{\sigma}}}{{r}}\right)\phi\right)+\left(\frac{{\bm{\sigma}}}{{r}}{\bm{u}}\right)O(n^{-1})+\zeta_{n}\nabla{\bm{u}}

\displaystyle=\frac{{\bm{\sigma}}{\bm{\sigma}}\phi}{{r}^{2}}O(n^{-1})+{\bm{\sigma}}\frac{{\bm{u}}}{{r}}O(n^{-1})+\zeta_{n}\nabla{\bm{u}}

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{l}\text{where we abbreviate as }{\bm{v}}{\bm{w}}:={\bm{v}}\otimes{\bm{w}}=\left(v_{i}w_{j}\right)_{i,j\in\{1,\cdots,N\}^{2}}\\
\text{the tensor product of two vector fields}\end{array}\right),

\displaystyle\triangle{\bm{u}}_{n}

\displaystyle=\triangle\nabla\left(\zeta_{n}\phi\right)=\nabla\big((\triangle\zeta_{n})\phi+2(\partial_{r}\zeta_{n})\partial_{r}\phi+\zeta_{n}\triangle\phi\big)

\displaystyle=\nabla\left(\left(\partial^{2}\zeta_{n}+(N-2)\partial\zeta_{n}\right){r}^{-2}\phi+\frac{2\partial\zeta_{n}}{{r}}\partial_{r}\phi\right)+\nabla\left(\zeta_{n}\triangle\phi\right)

\displaystyle=\nabla\left(\left(n^{-2}\zeta_{n}^{\prime\prime}+(N-2)n^{-1}\zeta_{n}^{\prime}\right)\frac{\phi}{{r}^{2}}+\frac{2\zeta_{n}^{\prime}}{n}\frac{\partial_{r}\phi}{{r}}\right)+\nabla\left(\zeta_{n}\triangle\phi\right)

\displaystyle=\Big(\underbrace{n^{-2}\nabla\zeta_{n}^{\prime\prime}+(N-2)n^{-1}\nabla\zeta_{n}^{\prime}}_{\frac{{\bm{\sigma}}}{{r}}O(n^{-1})}\Big)\frac{\phi}{{r}^{2}}+\Big(\underbrace{n^{-2}\zeta_{n}^{\prime\prime}+(N-2)n^{-1}\zeta_{n}^{\prime}}_{O(n^{-1})}\Big)\nabla\frac{\phi}{{r}^{2}}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{2\nabla\zeta_{n}^{\prime}}{n}\frac{\partial_{r}\phi}{{r}}+\frac{2\zeta_{n}^{\prime}}{n}\nabla\frac{\partial_{r}\phi}{{r}}+(\nabla\zeta_{n})\triangle\phi+\zeta_{n}\triangle\nabla\phi

\displaystyle=\frac{{\bm{\sigma}}}{{r}}O(n^{-1})\frac{\phi}{{r}^{2}}+O(n^{-1})\nabla\frac{\phi}{{r}^{2}}+\frac{{\bm{\sigma}}\partial_{r}\phi}{{r}^{2}}O(n^{-2})+O(n^{-1})\nabla\frac{\partial_{r}\phi}{{r}}

\displaystyle\quad+{\bm{\sigma}}O(n^{-1})\frac{\triangle\phi}{{r}}+\zeta_{n}\triangle\nabla\phi

\displaystyle=\frac{{\bm{\sigma}}\phi}{{r}^{3}}O(n^{-1})+\frac{{\bm{u}}}{{r}^{2}}O(n^{-1})+\frac{{\bm{\sigma}}u_{R}}{{r}^{2}}O(n^{-1})+\frac{\partial_{r}{\bm{u}}}{{r}}O(n^{-1})

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{{\bm{\sigma}}{\rm div}\hskip 1.00006pt{\bm{u}}}{{r}}O(n^{-1})+\zeta_{n}\triangle{\bm{u}}

hold as
n\to\infty
. Therefore, taking the
L^{2}(|{\bm{x}}|^{2\gamma}dx)
integration yields

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|{\bm{u}}_{n}|^{2}}{|{\bm{x}}|^{4}}|{\bm{x}}|^{2\gamma}dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|\zeta_{n}{\bm{u}}|^{2}}{|{\bm{x}}|^{4}}|{\bm{x}}|^{2\gamma}dx+O(n^{-1})\to\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|{\bm{u}}|^{2}}{|{\bm{x}}|^{4}}|{\bm{x}}|^{2\gamma}dx,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|\nabla{\bm{u}}_{n}|^{2}}{|{\bm{x}}|^{2}}|{\bm{x}}|^{2\gamma}dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|\zeta_{n}\nabla{\bm{u}}|^{2}}{|{\bm{x}}|^{2}}|{\bm{x}}|^{2\gamma}dx+O(n^{-1})\to\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|\nabla{\bm{u}}|^{2}}{|{\bm{x}}|^{2}}|{\bm{x}}|^{2\gamma}dx,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\triangle{\bm{u}}_{n}|^{2}|{\bm{x}}|^{2\gamma}dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\zeta_{n}\triangle{\bm{u}}|^{2}|{\bm{x}}|^{2\gamma}dx+O(n^{-1})\to\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\triangle{\bm{u}}|^{2}|{\bm{x}}|^{2\gamma}dx

with the aid of the integrability conditions (
15
). This fact shows that the two integrals in (
10
) can be approximated by curl-free fields with compact support on
\dot{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
, as desired.

3.2.
Radial- and spherical-scalar expression of the integrals

In the rest of the present section, we use the notation

{t}=\log|{\bm{x}}|=\log{r}

for an alternative radial coordinate obeying the differential rules

\partial_{t}={r}\partial_{r}=\partial={\bm{x}}\cdot\nabla\quad\text{ and }\quad d{t}=d{r}/{r},

(16)

which reproduces the same notation
\partial
given in (
13
).
For any parameter
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
, let
f
and
\varphi
be the scalar fields determined by the curl-free field
{\bm{u}}
, as given in Proposition
6
, and we set

{\bm{v}}({\bm{x}})=|{\bm{x}}|^{1-\lambda}{\bm{u}}({\bm{x}})

(17)

as a new vector field in
C_{c}^{\infty}(\dot{\mathbb{R}}^{N})^{N}
.
Then the equation

{\bm{v}}={\bm{\sigma}}\big(f+(\lambda+\partial)\varphi\big)+\nabla_{\!\sigma}\varphi

(18)

holds on
\dot{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
. Here we keep in mind that the equations (
17
) and (
18
) are invariant under the following replacement of the quadruple:

(f,\varphi,{\bm{v}},{\bm{u}})\longmapsto\left(\partial f,\partial\varphi,\partial{\bm{v}},{r}^{\lambda-1}\partial({r}^{1-\lambda}{\bm{u}})\right).

(19)

Now, we choose

\lambda=2-\frac{N}{2}-\gamma,

(20)

and let us recall from
[
7
, §3.2]
that the integral on the right-hand side of the Hardy-Leray inequality (
2
) can be expressed in terms of
({\bm{v}},f,\varphi)
as follows:

\displaystyle\mathop{\int}_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla{\bm{u}}|^{2}|{\bm{x}}|^{2\gamma}dx

\displaystyle=\mathop{\iint}_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{S}^{N-1}}\left((\lambda-1)^{2}|{\bm{v}}|^{2}+|\partial{\bm{v}}|^{2}+|\nabla_{\!\sigma}{\bm{v}}|^{2}\right)dt\hskip 1.00006pt\mathrm{d}\sigma,

(21)

\displaystyle\mathop{\iint}_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{S}^{N-1}}|\nabla_{\!\sigma}{\bm{v}}|^{2}dt\hskip 1.00006pt\mathrm{d}\sigma

\displaystyle=\mathop{\iint}_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{S}^{N-1}}\Big((\triangle_{\sigma}\varphi)^{2}+\left((\lambda-2)^{2}-2N\right)|\nabla_{\!\sigma}\varphi|^{2}\Big)dt\hskip 1.00006pt\mathrm{d}\sigma

\displaystyle\quad+\iint_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{S}^{N-1}}\left(|\partial\nabla_{\!\sigma}\varphi|^{2}+(N-1)|{\bm{v}}|^{2}\right)dt\hskip 1.00006pt\mathrm{d}\sigma,

(22)

\displaystyle\mathop{\iint}_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{S}^{N-1}}|{\bm{v}}|^{2}dt\hskip 1.00006pt\mathrm{d}\sigma

\displaystyle=\iint_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{S}^{N-1}}\left(f^{2}+(\partial\varphi)^{2}+\lambda^{2}\varphi^{2}+|\nabla_{\!\sigma}\varphi|^{2}\right)dt\hskip 1.00006pt\mathrm{d}\sigma

\displaystyle=\iint_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{S}^{N-1}}\left(f^{2}+\varphi\left(\lambda^{2}-\partial^{2}-\triangle_{\sigma}\right)\varphi\right)d{t}\mathrm{d}\sigma.

(23)

Here the last equality follows from integration by parts together with the support compactness of
{\bm{v}}
or
f,\varphi
.
Applying (
23
) to (
19
), we also obtain

\displaystyle\mathop{\iint}_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{S}^{N-1}}|\partial{\bm{v}}|^{2}d{t}\hskip 1.00006pt\mathrm{d}\sigma

\displaystyle=\mathop{\iint}_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{S}^{N-1}}\Big((\partial f)^{2}+(\partial^{2}\varphi)^{2}+\lambda^{2}(\partial\varphi)^{2}+|\partial\nabla_{\!\sigma}\varphi|^{2}\Big)dt\hskip 1.00006pt\mathrm{d}\sigma

\displaystyle=\mathop{\iint}_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{S}^{N-1}}\Big(f(-\partial^{2})f+\varphi\left(\lambda^{2}-\partial^{2}-\triangle_{\sigma}\right)(-\partial^{2}\varphi)\Big)dt\hskip 1.00006pt\mathrm{d}\sigma

(24)

by integration by parts.
After plugging (
22
) into (
21
), substitute (
23
) and (
24
) into the
L^{2}
terms of
{\bm{v}}
and
\partial{\bm{v}}
; then we get

\displaystyle\mathop{\int}_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla{\bm{u}}|^{2}|{\bm{x}}|^{2\gamma}dx

\displaystyle=\left((\lambda-1)^{2}+N-1\right)\mathop{\iint}_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{S}^{N-1}}|{\bm{v}}|^{2}d{t}\mathrm{d}\sigma+\mathop{\iint}_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{S}^{N-1}}|\partial{\bm{v}}|^{2}d{t}\mathrm{d}\sigma

\displaystyle\quad+\mathop{\iint}_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{S}^{N-1}}\Big((\triangle_{\sigma}\varphi)^{2}+\left((\lambda-2)^{2}-2N\right)|\nabla_{\!\sigma}\varphi|^{2}+|\partial\nabla_{\!\sigma}\varphi|^{2}\Big)d{t}\mathrm{d}\sigma

\displaystyle=\left((\lambda-1)^{2}+N-1\right)\mathop{\iint}_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{S}^{N-1}}\left(f^{2}+\varphi\left(\lambda^{2}-\partial^{2}-\triangle_{\sigma}\right)\varphi\right)d{t}\mathrm{d}\sigma

\displaystyle\quad+\iint_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{S}^{N-1}}\Big(f(-\partial^{2})f+\varphi\left(\lambda^{2}-\partial^{2}-\triangle_{\sigma}\right)(-\partial^{2}\varphi)\Big)d{t}\mathrm{d}\sigma

\displaystyle\quad+\iint_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{S}^{N-1}}\varphi\Big(\triangle_{\sigma}^{2}-\left((\lambda-2)^{2}-2N\right)\triangle_{\sigma}+\partial^{2}\triangle_{\sigma}\Big)\varphi d{t}\mathrm{d}\sigma

\displaystyle=\iint_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{S}^{N-1}}\left(\varphi\mathcal{P}_{1}(-\partial^{2},-\triangle_{\sigma},\lambda)\varphi+f\mathcal{P}_{0}(-\partial^{2},\lambda)f\right)d{t}\mathrm{d}\sigma

by integration by parts, where we have defined two polynomials
\mathcal{P}_{1}
and
\mathcal{P}_{0}
by

\left\{\begin{split}\mathcal{P}_{1}(\tau,{a},\lambda)&=\left((\lambda-1)^{2}+N-1\right)\left(\lambda^{2}+\tau+{a}\right)\\
&\quad+\left(\lambda^{2}+\tau+a\right)\tau+a^{2}+\left((\lambda-2)^{2}-2N\right){a}+{a}\tau\\
&={a}^{2}+\left(2\lambda^{2}-6\lambda+4-N+2\tau\right){a}\\
&\quad+\left(\lambda^{2}+\tau\right)\left(\left(\lambda-1\right)^{2}+N-1+\tau\right),\\
\mathcal{P}_{0}(\tau,\lambda)&=(\lambda-1)^{2}+N-1+\tau.\end{split}\right.

Now let us replace
\gamma
by
\gamma-1
; in view of (
20
), this manipulation is equivalent to replacing
\lambda
by
\lambda+1
. Then the result of the above integral computation changes into

\left.\begin{aligned} \mathop{\int}_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|\nabla{\bm{u}}|^{2}}{|{\bm{x}}|^{2}}|{\bm{x}}|^{2\gamma}dx&=\mathop{\iint}_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{S}^{N-1}}\left(\varphi P_{1}(-\partial^{2},-\triangle_{\sigma})\varphi+fP_{0}(-\partial^{2})f\right)d{t}\mathrm{d}\sigma,\\
\text{where and hereafter}&\text{ we abbreviate as}\\
P_{1}(\tau,{a}):\hskip-2.5pt&=\mathcal{P}_{1}(\tau,{a},\lambda+1)\\
&={a}^{2}+\Big(2\left(\lambda^{2}-\lambda+\tau\right)-N\Big){a}\\
&\quad+\left((\lambda+1)^{2}+\tau\right)\left(\lambda^{2}+N-1+\tau\right),\\
P_{0}(\tau):\hskip-2.5pt&=\mathcal{P}_{0}(\tau,\lambda+1)=\lambda^{2}+N-1+\tau,\end{aligned}\right\}

(25)

as the expression in terms of
f,\varphi
for the integral on the right-hand side of the Rellich-Hardy inequality (
10
).
To express the left-hand side,
we exploit the result of
[
7
, Eq.(30),(31) with
\lambda
replaced by
\lambda+1
]
: it holds that

\hskip-7.5pt\left.\begin{aligned} \mathop{\int}_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\triangle{\bm{u}}|^{2}|{\bm{x}}|^{2\gamma}&dx=\mathop{\iint}_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{S}^{N-1}}\Big(\varphi\,Q_{1}(-\partial^{2},-\triangle_{\sigma})\varphi+fQ_{0}(-\partial^{2})f\Big)dt\hskip 1.00006pt{\mathrm{d}}\sigma,\\
\text{where $Q_{1}$ and}&\text{ $Q_{0}$ are the polynomials given by}\\
Q_{1}(\tau,{a})&=\scalebox{0.89}[1]{$\left(\tau+{a}+(\lambda-1)^{2}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{l}\left(\tau+{a}+(\lambda+1)^{2}\right)\left(\tau+{a}+(\lambda+N-1)^{2}\right)\\
-(2\lambda+N)^{2}{a}\end{array}\right)$}\\
&=\scalebox{0.85}[1]{$\big(\tau+{a}+(\lambda-1)^{2}\big)\Big(\tau^{2}+\left(2\left({a}+\alpha_{\lambda+1}\right)+(N-2)^{2}\right)\tau+\left({a}-\alpha_{\lambda+1}\right)^{2}\Big)$},\\
Q_{0}(\tau)&=\left(\tau+(\lambda-1)^{2}\right)\left(\tau+(\lambda+N-1)^{2}\right).\end{aligned}\right\}\hskip-7.5pt

(26)

To proceed further, let us apply to
\varphi
and
f
the one-dimensional Fourier transformation with respect to
t
:
we set

\widehat{\varphi}(\tau,{\bm{\sigma}})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-i\tau t}\varphi(e^{t}{\bm{\sigma}})dt,\quad\ \widehat{f}(\tau)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-i\tau t}f(e^{t}{\bm{\sigma}})dt

for
(\tau,{\bm{\sigma}})\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{S}^{N-1}
, where
i=\sqrt{-1}
.
Also we apply to
\widehat{\varphi}
the spherical harmonics decomposition:

\widehat{\varphi}=\sum_{\nu\in\mathbb{N}}\widehat{\varphi}_{\nu},\qquad\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}-\triangle_{\sigma}\widehat{\varphi}_{\nu}=\alpha_{\nu}\widehat{\varphi}_{\nu},\\
\alpha_{\nu}=\nu(\nu+N-2)\quad\forall\nu\in\mathbb{N}.\end{array}\right.

Now, we are in a position to evaluate the quantity

\frac{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\triangle{\bm{u}}|^{2}|{\bm{x}}|^{2\gamma}dx}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla{\bm{u}}|^{2}|{\bm{x}}|^{2\gamma-2}dx},

(27)

which we simply call the
R-H quotient
.
To this end, by using (
25
), (
26
) and the
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
isometry of the Fourier transformation, we have

\displaystyle\frac{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\triangle{\bm{u}}|^{2}|{\bm{x}}|^{2\gamma}dx}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla{\bm{u}}|^{2}|{\bm{x}}|^{2\gamma-2}dx}

\displaystyle=\frac{\displaystyle\iint_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{S}^{N-1}}\left(\sum_{\nu\in\mathbb{N}}Q_{1}(\tau^{2},\alpha_{\nu})|\widehat{\varphi_{\nu}}|^{2}+Q_{0}(\tau^{2})|\widehat{f}|^{2}\right)d\tau\hskip 1.00006pt\mathrm{d}\sigma}{\displaystyle\iint_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{S}^{N-1}}\left(\sum_{\nu\in\mathbb{N}}P_{1}(\tau^{2},\alpha_{\nu})|\widehat{\varphi_{\nu}}|^{2}+P_{0}(\tau^{2})|\widehat{f}|^{2}\right)d\tau\hskip 1.00006pt\mathrm{d}\sigma}

\displaystyle\geq\min\left\{\inf_{\tau\in\mathbb{R}\backslash\{0\}}\frac{Q_{0}(\tau^{2})}{P_{0}(\tau^{2})},\ \inf_{\nu\in\mathbb{N}}\inf_{\tau\in\mathbb{R}\backslash\{0\}}\frac{Q_{1}(\tau^{2},\alpha_{\nu})}{P_{1}(\tau^{2},\alpha_{\nu})}\right\}

\displaystyle=\min\left\{\inf_{\tau>0}\frac{Q_{0}(\tau)}{P_{0}(\tau)},\ \inf_{\nu\in\mathbb{N}}\inf_{\tau>0}\frac{Q_{1}(\tau,\alpha_{\nu})}{P_{1}(\tau,\alpha_{\nu})}\right\}.

(28)

Hence, our goal is reduced to evaluate the fractions
Q_{0}/P_{0}
and
Q_{1}/P_{1}
.
In the following subsections, we will show that the infimum values of these fractions are achieved at
\tau=0
.

3.3.
Evaluation of
Q_{0}/P_{0}

A direct calculation yields

\displaystyle\frac{Q_{0}(\tau)}{P_{0}(\tau)}

\displaystyle=\frac{\left(\tau+(\lambda-1)^{2}\right)\left(\tau+(\lambda+N-1)^{2}\right)}{\lambda^{2}+N-1+\tau}

\displaystyle=\tau+\left(\lambda+N-2\right)^{2}+(N-1)\left(1-\frac{\left(2\lambda+N-2\right)^{2}}{\tau+\lambda^{2}+N-1}\right)

for all
\tau\geq 0
.
The last expression is of the form
g(\tau)=\tau+a-\frac{b}{\tau+c}
for some constants
a,b\geq 0
and
c>0
, which leads to
g(\tau)-g(0)=\tau+\frac{b\tau}{c(\tau+c)}\geq\tau
. Thus we have

\frac{1}{\tau}\left(\frac{Q_{0}(\tau)}{P_{0}(\tau)}-\frac{Q_{0}(0)}{P_{0}(0)}\right)\geq 1\qquad\forall\tau>0,

(29)

whence in particular we obtain

\quad\displaystyle\inf_{\tau>0}\frac{Q_{0}(\tau)}{P_{0}(\tau)}=\frac{Q_{0}(0)}{P_{0}(0)}=\frac{(\lambda-1)^{2}\left(\lambda+N-1\right)^{2}}{\lambda^{2}+N-1}
.

3.4.
The case when
P_{1}
has zeros

Here we specify when
P_{1}(\tau,\alpha_{\nu})=0
happens.
Notice from (
25
) that
P_{1}(\tau,{a})
is strictly monotone increasing in
\tau\geq 0
for any
{a}>0
, and hence it holds that

\begin{split}P_{1}(\tau,{a})&>P_{1}(0,{a})\\
&={a}^{2}+\left(2(\lambda-1/2)^{2}-\tfrac{1}{2}-N\right){a}+(\lambda+1)^{2}\left(\lambda^{2}+N-1\right)\end{split}

for all
\tau>0
, as well as that

P_{1}(\tau,\alpha_{1})>P_{1}(0,\alpha_{1})=P_{1}(0,N-1)=\lambda^{2}\left((\lambda+1)^{2}+3(N-1)\right)

for all
\tau>0
. Notice on the right-hand side of the (three lines) above inequality that the center of the graph of the quadratic function
{a}\mapsto P_{1}(0,{a})
is located at
{a}=-\left(\lambda-\frac{1}{2}\right)^{2}+\frac{1}{4}+\frac{1}{2}N\leq 2N=\alpha_{2}
. Then we see that for all
\tau>0
and
\nu\geq 2
,

\begin{split}P_{1}(\tau,\alpha_{\nu})&>P_{1}(0,\alpha_{\nu})\\
&\geq P_{1}(0,\alpha_{2})\\
&=\lambda^{4}+2\lambda^{3}+5N\lambda^{2}-2(N+1)\lambda+(N+1)(2N-1)\\
&=\lambda^{2}(\lambda+1)^{2}+2(2N-1)\lambda^{2}+(N+1)(\lambda-1)^{2}+2(N^{2}-1)\\
&\geq 2(N^{2}-1)>0.\end{split}

In view of the above discussion, we see that

P_{1}(\tau,\alpha_{\nu})=0\quad\text{ holds if and only if }\ (\tau,\nu,\lambda)=(0,1,0)

Hence, every time we treat the rational polynomial
Q_{1}/P_{1}
, we have to deal with the case
\lambda=0

(
or equivalently
\gamma=2-\frac{N}{2}
)
as a special one.
For this reason, in the rest of this paper we always assume
\lambda\neq 0

(
\Leftrightarrow\gamma\neq 2-\frac{N}{2}
)
unless others are specified.

3.5.
Evaluation of
Q_{1}/P_{1}

Let us check that

\inf_{\tau>0}\frac{Q_{1}(\tau,{a})}{P_{1}(\tau,{a})}=\frac{Q_{1}(0,{a})}{P_{1}(0,{a})}\qquad\forall{a}\in\{\alpha_{\nu}\}_{\nu\in\mathbb{N}}

in order to evaluate (
28
) from below.
This equation is equivalent to the inequality
\frac{Q_{1}(\tau,{a})}{P_{1}(\tau,{a})}\geq\frac{Q_{1}(0,{a})}{P_{1}(0,{a})}

(\forall\tau>0)
,
which can be verified by directly evaluating
Q_{1}(\tau,{a})P_{1}(0,{a})-P_{1}(\tau,{a})Q_{1}(0,{a})
to be nonnegative.
However, we further show the following stronger fact, which serves as a key tool for the proof of Theorem
2
:

Lemma 7
.

There exists a constant number
{c_{0}}>0
such that the inequality

\frac{1}{\tau}\left(\frac{Q_{1}(\tau,{a})}{P_{1}(\tau,{a})}-\frac{Q_{1}(0,{a})}{P_{1}(0,{a})}\right)\geq{c_{0}}

holds for all
\tau>0
and
a\in\{\alpha_{\nu}\}_{\nu\in\mathbb{N}}
.

Here we give the proof of the lemma only for the case
\gamma\leq 1
. Since the proof for
\gamma>1
follows by a similar technique, we postpone it in later section (see §
6
).
The proof of Lemma
7
consists of tedious computations, and we used
Maxima
in the course of the proof. However, we need many computational techniques to simplify the calculations and ideas to make the proof understandable, even with the use of Maxima.

Proof of Lemma
7
for
\gamma\leq 1

(
or equivalently
\lambda\geq 1-\frac{N}{2}
)
.
It suffices to check the inequality for
c_{0}=1
:

\frac{1}{\tau}\left(\frac{Q_{1}(\tau,{a})}{P_{1}(\tau,{a})}-\frac{Q_{1}(0,{a})}{P_{1}(0,{a})}\right)\geq 1\qquad\forall\tau>0,\quad\forall{a}\in\left\{\alpha_{\nu}\right\}_{\nu\in\mathbb{N}}.

To this end, we directly compute the left-hand side minus right-hand side: by using (
25
) and (
26
) we get

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\tau}

\displaystyle\left(\frac{Q_{1}(\tau,{a})}{P_{1}(\tau,{a})}-\frac{Q_{1}(0,{a})}{P_{1}(0,{a})}\right)-1

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\tau}\left(\frac{\left(\tau+{a}+(\lambda-1)^{2}\right)\Big(\tau^{2}+\left(2\left({a}+\alpha_{\lambda+1}\right)+(N-2)^{2}\right)\tau+\left({a}-\alpha_{\lambda+1}\right)^{2}\Big)}{{a}^{2}+\big(2\left(\lambda^{2}-\lambda+\tau\right)-N\big){a}+\left((\lambda+1)^{2}+\tau\right)\left(\lambda^{2}+N-1+\tau\right)}\right.\hskip-15.00002pt

\displaystyle\qquad\quad\ \left.-\ \frac{\left({a}+(\lambda-1)^{2}\right)\left({a}-\alpha_{\lambda+1}\right)^{2}}{{a}^{2}+\big(2(\lambda^{2}-\lambda)-N\big){a}+(\lambda+1)^{2}\left(\lambda^{2}+N-1\right)}\right)-1

\displaystyle=\ \underbrace{\!\!(2\lambda+N-2)_{\vphantom{{\begin{subarray}{c}A\\
Aa\end{subarray}}}}\hskip-5.0pt}_{\hskip 8.19447pt\geq\,0}\ \frac{\ G_{0}({a})+G_{1}({a})\tau}{P_{1}(0,{a})P_{1}(\tau,{a})},

(30)

where we have defined

\left.\begin{split}G_{0}({a})&:=(2\lambda+N){a}^{3}+\Big(\left(2\lambda^{2}-N+5\right)(2\lambda+N)-2(N-1)\Big){a}^{2}\\
&\quad\ +\left(\begin{array}[]{l}2\lambda^{5}+(N-8)\lambda^{4}-8N\lambda^{3}-2(N^{2}+2N-2)\lambda^{2}\\
-\,2(6N-7)\lambda-2N^{2}-N+4\end{array}\right){a}\\
&\quad\ +(N-1)(2\lambda+N-2)(\lambda+1)^{4},\\
G_{1}({a})&:=(2\lambda+N){a}^{2}+\Big((2\lambda+N)\left((\lambda-1)^{2}-N+1\right)-2(N-1)\Big){a}\\
&\quad\ +(N-1)(2\lambda+N-2)(\lambda+1)^{2}\end{split}\right\}

(31)

as cubic and quadratic polynomials in
{a}
. Then the necessary and sufficient condition for the nonnegativity of (
30
)
(\forall\tau\geq 0)
is given by the inequalities

whence our goal is reduced to showing them. The first inequality is easier to prove, by considering the Taylor series of
G_{1}({a})
at
{a}=\alpha_{1}
: a straightforward calculation yields

\displaystyle G_{1}(\alpha_{1}+{s})

\displaystyle=s^{2}(2\lambda+N)+s\Big((N-1)(N+2\lambda-2)+(\lambda-1)^{2}(2\lambda+N)\Big)

\displaystyle\quad+2(N-1)\lambda^{2}(2\lambda+N-1)

. This fact directly implies

for all

, as desired.

Now, all we have to do is to show
G_{0}({a})\geq 0
for
{a}\in\{\alpha_{\nu}\}_{\nu\in\mathbb{N}}
. To do so, let us consider the Taylor series of
G_{0}({a})
at
{a}=\alpha_{1}

\displaystyle G_{0}(\alpha_{1}+{s})

\displaystyle=s^{3}(2\lambda+N)+s^{2}\mathcal{G}_{2}(\lambda)+s\mathcal{G}_{1}(\lambda)+2(N-1)\lambda^{4}(2\lambda+N-1)

by a straightforward calculation, where

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{2}(\lambda)

\displaystyle:=(2\lambda+N-2)\Big((\lambda+1)^{2}+\lambda^{2}+N+3\Big)+(N-1)^{2}+9,

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{1}(\lambda)

\displaystyle:=\lambda^{4}(2\lambda+N-8)+2\lambda^{2}\left(2-4\lambda-4N+N^{2}\right)+N^{2}(2\lambda+N).

Noticing that

Then we directly compute

\begin{split}\mathcal{G}_{1}(\lambda)&=\left(1-\frac{N{s}}{2}\right)^{4}\left(N-N{s}-6\right)+2\left(1-\frac{N{s}}{2}\right)^{2}\left(2N{s}-2-4N+N^{2}\right)\\

&\quad+(N-2)\Big((1-s)^{2}s(17-s-5s^{2})+21-10s-3s^{2}\Big)\\
&\quad+2s\Big((1-s)(3+s)\left(5-4s+s^{2}\right)+4\Big)\end{split}

are all nonnegative since

. Therefore, we get

Now, all that is left is to show (

\begin{split}\frac{1}{{s}}\,G_{0}(\alpha_{1}+{s})&\geq{s}\hskip 1.00006pt\mathcal{G}_{2}(\lambda)+\mathcal{G}_{1}(\lambda)\\
&\geq{s}\hskip 1.00006pt(2\lambda+N-2)\Big((\lambda+1)^{2}+\lambda^{2}\Big)\\
&\quad+\lambda^{4}(2\lambda+N-8)+2\lambda^{2}\left(2-4\lambda-4N+N^{2}\right)\end{split}

holds for all

Replacing

\begin{split}\frac{G_{0}(\alpha_{2}+{s})}{N+1+{s}}&=\frac{1}{N+1+{s}}\hskip 1.00006ptG_{0}(\alpha_{1}+N+1+{s})\\

for all

. Notice here that the coefficients of the powers of

3.6.
A lower bound for the R-H quotient

In view of the estimate (
28
) for the R-H quotient (

, §
3.4
and §

Notice from (

\begin{split}\frac{Q_{1}(0,\alpha_{\nu})}{P_{1}(0,\alpha_{\nu})}&=\frac{\left(\alpha_{\nu}+(\lambda-1)^{2}\right)(\alpha_{\lambda+1}-\alpha_{\nu})^{2}}{\left(\alpha_{\lambda+1}-\alpha_{\nu}\right)^{2}+\left(2\lambda+N-2\right)\big((2\lambda+1)\alpha_{\nu}-(N-1)(\lambda+1)^{2}\big)}\\

\frac{Q_{0}(0)}{P_{0}(0)}&=\frac{(\lambda-1)^{2}(\lambda+N-1)^{2}}{\lambda^{2}+N-1}=\frac{\left((\gamma-1)^{2}-\frac{1}{4}N^{2}\right)^{2}}{\left(\gamma+\frac{N}{2}-2\right)^{2}+N-1},\end{split}

in other words, we have

in terms of the same notation in Theorem

3.7.
Sharpness of
C_{N,\gamma}

We show here the optimality for the constant
C_{N,\gamma}
in the inequality (

). To this end, we construct a sequence of curl-free fields minimizing the value of the R-H quotient (
27
).
First of all, choose
\nu_{0}\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
to be such that

\min_{\nu\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}}C_{N,\gamma,\nu}=C_{N,\gamma,\nu_{0}}.

If

5.2
) below (§
5

Hence it follows from §

Define a sequence of curl-free fields
\{{\bm{u}}_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset C_{c}^{\infty}(\dot{\mathbb{R}}^{N})^{N}

{\bm{u}}_{n}({\bm{x}}):=\begin{cases}\ {\bm{x}}|{\bm{x}}|^{\lambda-1}h\left(\frac{1}{n}\log|{\bm{x}}|\right)&\text{if }\nu_{0}=0\\
\ \nabla\left(|{\bm{x}}|^{\lambda+1}\varphi_{n}({\bm{x}})\right)&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

-\triangle_{\sigma}Y=\alpha_{\nu_{0}}Y\qquad\text{on }\mathbb{S}^{N-1}.

Also define
\{{\bm{v}}_{n}\}\subset C_{c}^{\infty}(\dot{\mathbb{R}}^{N})^{N}
as a sequence of vector fields by the formula

{\bm{u}}_{n}({\bm{x}})=|{\bm{x}}|^{\lambda}{\bm{v}}_{n}({\bm{x}})

in the same way as (
17

Then we have

{\bm{v}}_{n}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}{\bm{\sigma}}f_{n}&\text{ if }\ \nu_{0}=0\\
{\bm{\sigma}}(\partial+\lambda+1)\varphi_{n}+\nabla_{\!\sigma}\varphi_{n}&\text{ otherwise}\end{array}\right.,

where
f_{n}
is given by
f_{n}({\bm{x}})=h(\frac{1}{n}\log|{\bm{x}}|)
.
In this setting, let us now apply the formulae (
25
) and (
26

({\bm{u}},f,\varphi)=({\bm{u}}_{n},f_{n},0)

({\bm{u}},f,\varphi)=({\bm{u}}_{n},0,\varphi_{n})

\displaystyle\qquad\frac{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\triangle{\bm{u}}_{n}|^{2}|{\bm{x}}|^{2\gamma}dx}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla{\bm{u}}_{n}|^{2}|{\bm{x}}|^{2\gamma-2}dx}

\displaystyle=\left\{\begin{aligned} &\frac{\int_{\mathbb{R}}h({t})\hskip 1.00006ptQ_{0}\left(-n^{-2}\partial_{t}^{2}\right)h({t})dt}{\int_{\mathbb{R}}h({t})\hskip 1.00006ptP_{0}\left(-n^{-2}\partial_{t}^{2}\right)h({t})dt}&\text{ if }\ \nu_{0}=0,\\
&\frac{\int_{\mathbb{R}}h({t})\hskip 1.00006ptQ_{1}(-n^{-2}\partial_{t}^{2},\alpha_{\nu_{0}})h\left({t}\right)\hskip 1.00006ptdt}{\int_{\mathbb{R}}h({t})\hskip 1.00006ptP_{1}(-n^{-2}\partial_{t}^{2},\alpha_{\nu_{0}})h\left({t}\right)\hskip 1.00006ptdt}&\text{ otherwise}.\end{aligned}\right.

Notice on the right-hand side that the denominator always exceeds a fixed positive number, since

and

Therefore, passing to

\displaystyle\frac{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\triangle{\bm{u}}_{n}|^{2}|{\bm{x}}|^{2\gamma}dx}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla{\bm{u}}_{n}|^{2}|{\bm{x}}|^{2\gamma-2}dx}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow C_{N,\gamma,\nu_{0}}=C_{N,\gamma},

which gives the desired sharpness of

.

Now, the proof of Theorem

has been completed.
∎

Let

5.1.

In view of the original best constant (

5.2.

\displaystyle A_{N,2-\frac{N}{2}}

\displaystyle=\min\left\{\left(2-N\right)^{2},\ \min_{\nu\in\mathbb{N}}\alpha_{\nu}\right\}=\min\Big\{(N-2)^{2},\ \underbrace{N-1}_{\hskip 8.19447pt=\,\alpha_{1}}\Big\}

N-1&(N\geq 3)\\
\end{cases},

Here the third equality from the last in (
5.2
) follows with the aid of computing

C_{N,2-\frac{N}{2},\nu}=(\alpha_{\nu}+1)\left(1-\tfrac{N-2}{\alpha_{\nu}-1}\right)

which is monotone increasing in
\nu\geq 2
. Summarizing the results above, we have obtained

\begin{split}&C_{2,1}=C_{2,1,0}=1>0=A_{2,1,0}=A_{2,1},\\
&C_{N,2-\frac{N}{2}}=C_{N,2-\frac{N}{2},0}=N-1=A_{N,2-\frac{N}{2},1}=A_{N,2-\frac{N}{2}}\quad(N\geq 3).\end{split}

In particular, we see that the best constant in the two-dimensional Rellich-Hardy inequality (with
\gamma=1
) can be really improved by the curl-free condition on the test vector fields.

5.3.
The case
\gamma\neq 2-\frac{N}{2}
(or equivalently
\lambda\neq 0
)

Recall from §
3.6
and §
3.4
that

\begin{cases}C_{N,\gamma,\nu}=\dfrac{\left(\alpha_{\nu}+(\lambda-1)^{2}\right)(\alpha_{\lambda+1}-\alpha_{\nu})^{2}}{P_{1}(0,\alpha_{\nu})},\vskip 5.0pt\\
P_{1}(0,\alpha_{\nu})=\left(\alpha_{\lambda+1}-\alpha_{\nu}\right)^{2}+\left(2\lambda+N-2\right)\big((2\lambda+1)\alpha_{\nu}-(N-1)(\lambda+1)^{2}\big)>0\end{cases}

for
\nu\in\mathbb{N}
. By using this expression together with (
40
), a direct computation yields

\begin{cases}\displaystyle\ C_{N,\gamma,\nu}-A_{N,\gamma,\nu-1}=-\frac{2(\nu-\lambda-1)^{2}W(\alpha_{\nu})(\nu+N-2)}{(\alpha_{\nu-1}+\lambda^{2})P_{1}(0,\alpha_{\nu})},\vskip 5.0pt\\
\displaystyle\ C_{N,\gamma,\nu}-A_{N,\gamma,\nu+1}=\frac{2\nu W(\alpha_{\nu})(1-\nu-N-\lambda)^{2}}{(\alpha_{\nu+1}+\lambda^{2})P_{1}(0,\alpha_{\nu})}\end{cases}

for all
\nu\in\mathbb{N}
,
where

W(\alpha_{\nu})=\lambda^{2}(2\lambda+N-4)(2\lambda+N)+(N-2)^{2}-\left(\alpha_{\nu}+\lambda^{2}-1\right)^{2}.

Then we have

\left(C_{N,\gamma,\nu}-A_{N,\gamma,\nu-1}\right)\left(C_{N,\gamma,\nu}-A_{N,\gamma,\nu+1}\right)\leq 0

or equivalently

\min\{A_{N,\gamma,\nu-1},A_{N,\gamma,\nu+1}\}\leq C_{N,\gamma,\nu}\leq\max\{A_{N,\gamma,\nu-1},A_{N,\gamma,\nu+1}\}

(45)

for all
\nu\in\mathbb{N}
. Based on this fact, let us consider the following two simplest cases:

The case
A_{N,\gamma}=A_{N,\gamma,1}

It holds from (
11
) that

A_{N,\gamma}=A_{N,\gamma,1}=C_{N,\gamma,0}\geq C_{N,\gamma},

and hence that
C_{N,\gamma}=A_{N,\gamma}
from (
39
). This fact says that the curl-free restriction causes no effect on the improvement of the best constant.
Now, we seek for when the equation
A_{N,\gamma}=A_{N,\gamma,1}
will happen.
In view of the inequalities (
43
) with
k=1
, this equation is equivalent to that both the inequalities

A_{N,\gamma,1}\leq A_{N,\gamma,0}\quad\text{ and }\quad A_{N,\gamma,1}\leq A_{N,\gamma,2}

hold true;
in other words,
A_{N,\gamma}=A_{N,\gamma,1}
holds if and only if both the numerators of the right-hand sides of (
41
) and (
42
)
ν=1
evaluate to

\begin{split}&3\lambda^{2}+4(N-2)\lambda+N^{2}-5N+5\geq 0,\qquad\text{and}\\
&\alpha_{2}\alpha_{1}+\lambda^{2}\left(2N+\big(1+\tfrac{1}{N-1}\lambda^{2}\big)\left(A_{N,\gamma,1}-A_{N,\gamma,0}\right)\right)\\
&\quad=-3\lambda^{4}-4(N-2)\lambda^{3}-(N^{2}-7N+5)\lambda^{2}+2N(N-1)\\
&\quad\geq 0.\end{split}

For example, if
\gamma=0

(
or equivalently
\lambda=2-\frac{N}{2}
)
, then the two inequalities become

\begin{split}&N^{2}-4N-4\leq 0,\quad\text{ and}\\
&N^{4}-4N^{3}+12N^{2}+64N-64\geq 0\quad(\text{which is always true}).\end{split}

This is the case when
N\leq 4
, and hence we have

\begin{cases}C_{2,0}=C_{2,0,0}=A_{2,0,1}=A_{2,0}=0,\\
C_{3,0}=C_{3,0,0}=A_{3,0,1}=A_{3,0}=\frac{25}{36},\\
C_{4,0}=C_{4,0,0}=A_{4,0,1}=A_{4,0}=3,\end{cases}

which says that no curl-free improvement occurs when
N\in\{2,3,4\}
and
\gamma=0
.

We may understand the above result in the following way:
we can choose a sequence
\{{\bm{u}}_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of vector fields by the formula

{\bm{w}}_{n}({\bm{x}})={\bm{x}}f_{n}(|{\bm{x}}|)\qquad\left(n=1,2,\cdots\right)

in order that

\begin{split}&\frac{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\triangle{\bm{u}_{n}}|^{2}|{\bm{x}}|^{2\gamma}dx}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla{\bm{w}}_{n}|^{2}|{\bm{x}}|^{2\gamma-2}dx}\to A_{N,\gamma,1}\qquad(n\to\infty),\end{split}

where
\{f_{n}({r})\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
are a smooth functions vanishing near
{r}=0
. We may see this fact by noticing that
each coordinate function
x_{k}

(k=1,\cdots,N)
satisfies the eigenequation
-\triangle_{\sigma}x_{k}=\alpha_{1}x_{k}
.
On the other hand, it is easy to check that
\{{\bm{w}}_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
are always curl-free, whence
C_{N,\gamma}\leq A_{N,\gamma}
. Therefore, we get
C_{N,\gamma}=A_{N,\gamma}
from
A_{N,\gamma}=A_{N,\gamma,1}
.

The case
A_{N,\gamma}=A_{N,\gamma,0}

Since
A_{N,\gamma,1}\geq A_{N,\gamma,0}=A_{N,\gamma}
, we see from (
43
) and (
45
) that

A_{N,\gamma,\nu-1}\leq C_{N,\gamma,\nu}\leq A_{N,\gamma,\nu+1}

holds for all
\nu\in\mathbb{N}
.
This fact implies

\min_{k\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}}C_{N,\gamma,2k}=C_{N,\gamma,0}\quad\text{ and }\quad\min_{k\in\mathbb{N}}C_{N,\gamma,2k-1}=C_{N,\gamma,1},

whence

C_{N,\gamma}=\min\left\{C_{N,\gamma,0},\ C_{N,\gamma,1}\right\}.

On the other hand, a direct computation yields

\begin{split}C_{N,\gamma,1}-C_{N,\gamma,0}&=\frac{(\lambda-1)^{2}\left(A_{N,\gamma,1}-A_{N,\gamma,0}\right)+\frac{(N^{2}-1)}{5}\frac{(5\lambda+2N-4)^{2}+N^{2}+N-1}{\lambda^{2}+N-1}}{(\lambda+1)^{2}+3(N-1)}\\
&>0\end{split}

from
A_{N,\gamma,1}\geq A_{N,\gamma,0}
. Therefore, it holds from (
11
) that

C_{N,\gamma}=C_{N,\gamma,0}=A_{N,\gamma,1},

or equivalently that

C_{N,\gamma}-A_{N,\gamma}=A_{N,\gamma,1}-A_{N,\gamma,0}.

This fact together with (
41
) shows that the inequality
C_{N,\gamma}>A_{N,\gamma}
(namely the effect of the curl-free-improvement) holds
as far as the right-hand side of (
41
) is strictly negative,
or equivalently

\left|\gamma-\tfrac{N+4}{6}\right|<\tfrac{1}{3}\sqrt{N^{2}-N+1}.

For example, this is the case if
\gamma=0
and
N\geq 5
, whence we have

C_{N,0}=C_{N,0,0}=A_{N,0,1}=\tfrac{(\frac{N^{2}}{4}-1)^{2}}{\frac{N^{2}}{4}-N+3}>\tfrac{N^{2}}{4}=A_{N,0,0}=A_{N,0}

for all
N\geq 5
.

We may understand the above phenomenon in the following way: we can choose a sequence
\{{\bm{w}}_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of vector fields by the formula

{\bm{w}}_{n}({\bm{x}})=\left(f_{n,1}(|{\bm{x}}|),\cdots,f_{n,N}(|{\bm{x}}|)\right)\qquad(n=1,2,\cdots)

in order that

\frac{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\triangle{\bm{w}_{n}}|^{2}|{\bm{x}}|^{2\gamma}dx}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla{\bm{w}}_{n}|^{2}|{\bm{x}}|^{2\gamma-2}dx}\to A_{N,\gamma,0}\qquad(n\to\infty).

(46)

In order for
{\bm{w}}_{n}
to be curl-free, it must hold that

\frac{\partial f_{n,j}}{\partial x_{k}}-\frac{\partial f_{n,k}}{\partial x_{j}}=\frac{\partial{r}}{\partial x_{k}}f_{n,j}^{\prime}-\frac{\partial{r}}{\partial x_{j}}f_{n,k}^{\prime}=\frac{x_{k}}{{r}}f_{n,j}^{\prime}-\frac{x_{j}}{{r}}f_{n,k}^{\prime}=0,\quad\forall j,k\in\{1,\cdots,N\},

where the notation
(\cdots)^{\prime}
denotes the derivative of a one-dimensional function.
This fact implies that
f_{n,k}^{\prime}=(x_{k}/x_{j})f_{n,j}^{\prime}
for all
j\neq k
, and hence that

f_{n,j}={\rm const}\qquad\forall j\in\{1,\cdots,N\}

from the radial symmetry of the function
{\bm{x}}\mapsto f_{n,j}(|{\bm{x}}|)
. Consequently, we have
|\triangle{\bm{w}}_{n}|=|\nabla{\bm{w}}_{n}|\equiv 0
.
This phenomenon indicates that there may be no curl-free sequence
\{{\bm{w}}_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
satisfying (
46
), which we can naturally interpret as a result of the inequality
C_{N,\gamma}>A_{N,\gamma}
.

Incidentally, let us further consider the case

\begin{cases}1-\frac{N}{2}+\sqrt{N+1}\leq\gamma\leq\frac{N+4}{6}+\frac{1}{3}\sqrt{N^{2}-N+1}&\text{for }\ 3\leq N\leq 11\\
\left|\gamma-\frac{N+4}{6}\right|\leq\frac{1}{3}\sqrt{N^{2}-N+1}&\text{for }\ N\geq 12\end{cases}

which ensures both
C_{N,\gamma}=C_{N,\gamma,0}
and

H_{N,\gamma-1}=\left(\gamma+\tfrac{N}{2}-2\right)^{2}+N-1

from (
3
).
(For
\gamma=0
, this is the case for all
N\geq 8
.)
Then a successive application of Rellich-Hardy inequality and Hardy-Leray inequality reproduces Rellich-Leray inequality: for all curl-free fields
{\bm{u}}\in\mathcal{D}_{\gamma-1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, it holds that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\triangle{\bm{u}}|^{2}|{\bm{x}}|^{2\gamma}dx

\displaystyle\geq C_{N,\gamma}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla{\bm{u}}|^{2}|{\bm{x}}|^{2\gamma-2}dx\qquad\qquad(\text{from \eqref{RH_cf}})

\displaystyle\geq C_{N,\gamma}H_{N,\gamma-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|{\bm{u}}|^{2}}{|{\bm{x}}|^{2}}|{\bm{x}}|^{2\gamma-2}dx\quad\left(\begin{array}[]{ll}\small\text{from \eqref{HL}}\\
\small\text{with $\gamma$ replaced by $\gamma-1$}\end{array}\right)

\displaystyle=C_{N,\gamma,0}\left(\left(\gamma+\tfrac{N}{2}-2\right)^{2}+N-1\right)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|{\bm{u}}|^{2}}{|{\bm{x}}|^{2}}|{\bm{x}}|^{2\gamma-2}dx

\displaystyle=\left((\gamma-1)^{2}-\tfrac{N^{2}}{4}\right)^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|{\bm{u}}|^{2}}{|{\bm{x}}|^{2}}|{\bm{x}}|^{2\gamma-2}dx

\displaystyle\geq B_{N,\gamma}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|{\bm{u}}|^{2}}{|{\bm{x}}|^{4}}|{\bm{x}}|^{2\gamma}dx

with
B_{N,\gamma}
given by (
6
). Therefore, even under the curl-free constraint, Rellich-Hardy inequality bridges between Hardy-Leray and Rellich-Leray inequalities, and so serves as a stronger version of the Rellich-Leray inequality.

6.2.

, we have the identity

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\tau}\left(\frac{Q_{1}(\tau,{a})}{P_{1}(\tau,{a})}-\frac{Q_{1}(0,{a})}{P_{1}(0,{a})}\right)-1=\,\frac{2\lambda\left(G_{0}({a})+G_{1}({a})\tau\right)}{P_{1}(0,{a})P_{1}(\tau,{a})},

where

P_{1}(\tau,{a})=\tau^{2}+2\left({a}+\lambda^{2}+\lambda+1\right)\tau+\left({a}+\lambda^{2}-\lambda-1\right)^{2}+(4\lambda+3)\lambda^{2}

is the same as in (
25
) with
N=2
,
and where
G_{0}({a})
and
G_{0}({a})
are the same as in (
31
) with
N=2
.
Adding
2/3
to both sides of the above identity, we then get

\begin{split}\frac{1}{\tau}&\left(\frac{Q_{1}(\tau,{a})}{P_{1}(\tau,{a})}-\frac{Q_{1}(0,{a})}{P_{1}(0,{a})}\right)-\frac{1}{3}\\
&=\frac{2}{3}\left(1+\frac{3\lambda\left(G_{0}({a})+G_{1}({a})\tau\right)}{\Big(\tau^{2}+2\big({a}+\lambda^{2}+\lambda+1\big)\tau\Big)P_{1}(0,{a})+\left(P_{1}\left(0,{a}\right)\right)^{2}}\right)\\
&=\frac{2}{3}\cdot\frac{\tau^{2}P_{1}(0,{a})+2\tau F_{1}({a})+F_{0}({a})}{P_{1}(0,{a})P_{1}(\tau,{a})},\end{split}

where

\displaystyle F_{1}({a})

\displaystyle:=\left({a}+\lambda^{2}+\lambda+1\right)P_{1}(0,{a})+\tfrac{3}{2}\lambda G_{1}({a}),

(56)

\displaystyle F_{0}({a})

\displaystyle:=\left(P_{1}(0,{a})\right)^{2}+3\lambda G_{0}({a}).

(57)

Thus, it is enough to show that

F_{1}({a})\geq 0\quad\text{ and }\quad F_{0}({a})\geq 0\qquad\forall{a}\in\{\alpha_{\nu}=\nu(\nu+1)\}_{\nu\in\mathbb{N}}

under the assumption
\lambda<1-\frac{N}{2}=0
.

Proof of
F_{1}({a})\geq 0
for
{a}\in\{\alpha_{\nu}\}_{\nu\in\mathbb{N}}

It suffices to check that
F_{1}(\alpha_{1}+{s})\geq 0
, i.e.,

F_{1}(1+{s})\geq 0\qquad\forall{s}\geq 0.

In order to compute the left-hand side, apply
N=2
to (
51
) and (
32
); then we get

\displaystyle P_{1}(0,1+{s})

\displaystyle={s}^{2}+2{s}\lambda(\lambda-1)+\lambda^{2}\left((\lambda+1)^{2}+3\right),

(58)

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{2}G_{1}(1+{s})

\displaystyle=(\lambda+1){s}^{2}+{s}\left(\lambda+(\lambda-1)^{2}(\lambda+1)\right)+\lambda^{2}(2\lambda+1).

Substitute them into (
56
) and (
57
) with
{a}=1+{s}
, then we have

\begin{split}F_{1}(1+{s})&=\left({s}+\lambda^{2}+\lambda+2\right)P_{1}(0,1+{s})+\tfrac{3}{2}\lambda G_{1}(1+{s})\\
&=\left({s}+\lambda^{2}+\lambda+2\right)\Big({s}^{2}+2{s}\lambda(\lambda-1)+\lambda^{2}\left((\lambda+1)^{2}+3\right)\Big)\\
&\quad+3\lambda\Big((\lambda+1){s}^{2}+{s}\left(\lambda+(\lambda-1)^{2}(\lambda+1)\right)+\lambda^{2}(2\lambda+1)\Big)\\
&={s}^{3}+2\left(3\lambda^{2}+\lambda+1\right){s}^{2}+\lambda(6\lambda-1)\left(\lambda^{2}+1\right){s}\\
&\quad+\lambda^{2}\left(\lambda^{4}+3\lambda^{3}+14\lambda^{2}+11\lambda+8\right)\\
&={s}^{3}+\left(6\left(\lambda+\tfrac{1}{6}\right)^{2}+\tfrac{11}{6}\right){s}^{2}-\lambda(1-6\lambda)\left(\lambda^{2}+1\right){s}\\
&\quad+\lambda^{2}\left((\lambda+1)^{2}\left(\lambda+\tfrac{1}{2}\right)^{2}+\tfrac{27}{4}(\lambda+1)^{2}+(2\lambda-1)^{2}\right)\end{split}

In view of the right-hand side, the coefficients of the powers of
{s}
are all positive since
\lambda<0
, which implies that
F_{1}({a})\geq 0
for all
{a}\geq\alpha_{1}
, as desired.

Proof of
F_{0}({a})\geq 0
for
{a}\in\{\alpha_{\nu}\}_{\nu\in\mathbb{N}}

First of all, let us compute
F_{0}(1+{s})
. To this end, apply
N=2
to (
33
), (
34
) and (
35
); then we get

\begin{split}\tfrac{1}{2}G_{0}(1+{s})&=(\lambda+1){s}^{3}+\tfrac{1}{2}{s}^{2}\mathcal{G}_{2}(\lambda)+\tfrac{1}{2}{s}\mathcal{G}_{1}(\lambda)+\lambda^{4}(2\lambda+1)\\
&=(\lambda+1){s}^{3}+{s}^{2}\left(2\lambda^{3}+2\lambda^{2}+6\lambda+5\right)\\
&\quad+{s}(\lambda-1)\left(\lambda^{4}-2\lambda^{3}+6\lambda^{2}-8\lambda-4\right)+\lambda^{4}(2\lambda+1).\end{split}

Substitute this expression and (
58
) into (
57
) with
{a}=1+{s}
, then we have

\displaystyle F_{0}(1+{s})

\displaystyle=\left(P_{1}(0,1+{s})\right)^{2}+3\lambda G_{0}(1+{s})

\displaystyle=\Big({s}^{2}+2{s}\lambda(\lambda-1)+\lambda^{2}\left((\lambda+1)^{2}+3\right)\Big)^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+3\lambda\Big(2(\lambda+1){s}^{3}+{s}^{2}\mathcal{G}_{2}(\lambda)+{s}\mathcal{G}_{1}(\lambda)+2\lambda^{4}(2\lambda+1)\Big)

\displaystyle=\Big({s}^{2}+2{s}\lambda(\lambda-1)+\lambda^{2}\left((\lambda+1)^{2}+3\right)\Big)^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+6\lambda\left(\begin{array}[]{l}(\lambda+1){s}^{3}+{s}^{2}\left(2\lambda^{3}+2\lambda^{2}+6\lambda+5\right)+\lambda^{4}(2\lambda+1)\\
+\,{s}(\lambda-1)\left(\lambda^{4}-2\lambda^{3}-6\lambda^{2}-8\lambda-4\right)\end{array}\right)

\displaystyle={s}^{4}+2\lambda(5\lambda+1){s}^{3}+2\lambda(9\lambda^{3}+4\lambda^{2}+24\lambda+15){s}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+2\lambda(\lambda-1)\left(5\lambda^{4}-2\lambda^{3}-10\lambda^{2}-24\lambda-12\right){s}

\displaystyle\quad+\lambda^{4}\left((\lambda+1)^{4}+9(\lambda+1)^{2}+9\lambda^{2}+6\right)

(59)

as a Taylor series of
F_{0}({a})
at
{a}=\alpha_{1}=1
. Then it is clear that
F_{0}(\alpha_{1})=F_{0}(1)\geq 0
, and hence our goal is reduced to showing
F_{0}({a})\geq 0
for
{a}\geq\alpha_{2}
. For this purpose, it suffices to check that

F_{0}(\alpha_{2}+{s})=F_{0}(4+{s})\geq 0\qquad\forall{s}\geq 0.

To this end, notice from (
59
) that

\begin{split}\frac{F_{0}(1+{s})}{{s}}&\geq\frac{F_{0}(1+{s})-F_{0}(1)}{{s}}\\
&={s}^{3}+2\lambda\left(\begin{array}[]{l}(5\lambda+1){s}^{2}+\left(9\lambda^{3}+4\lambda^{2}+24\lambda+15\right){s}\\
+\,(\lambda-1)\left(5\lambda^{4}-2\lambda^{3}-10\lambda^{2}-24\lambda-12\right)\end{array}\right)\end{split}

holds for all
{s}>0
. Replacing
{s}
by
3+{s}
on both sides, we get

\begin{split}\frac{F_{0}(4+{s})}{3+{s}}&\geq({s}+3)^{3}+2\lambda\left(\begin{array}[]{l}(5\lambda+1)({s}+3)^{2}\\
+\,\left(9\lambda^{3}+4\lambda^{2}+24\lambda+15\right)({s}+3)\\
+\,(\lambda-1)\left(5\lambda^{4}-2\lambda^{3}-10\lambda^{2}-24\lambda-12\right)\end{array}\right)\\
&=s^{3}+\left((\lambda+1)^{2}+9\lambda^{2}+8\right)s^{2}\\
&\quad+\Big(14\lambda^{4}+\lambda^{2}\left(4(\lambda+1)^{2}+83\right)+21(\lambda+1)^{2}+6\Big)s\\
&\quad+3\lambda^{6}+\lambda^{4}\left(7(\lambda-1)^{2}+27\right)+\lambda^{2}(2\lambda-1)^{2}\\
&\quad+16(4\lambda+1)^{2}+(\lambda+2)^{2}+7\\
&\geq 0,\end{split}

and therefore
F_{0}(4+{s})\geq 0
for all
{s}\geq 0
, as desired.

Appendix

Here we give another proof of the second inequality
E_{01}(\lambda)\geq 0
of (
55
), by the method of completing the square.

Substitute (
35
) into (
54
), then we have

\begin{split}E_{01}(\lambda)&=(2\lambda+N-2)\left(\begin{array}[]{l}\lambda^{4}(2\lambda+N-8)+\,N^{2}(2\lambda+N)\\
+\,2\lambda^{2}\left(2-4\lambda-4N+N^{2}\right)\end{array}\right)\\
&\quad+\lambda^{2}\left((\lambda+1)^{2}+3N-3\right)\left(2\lambda^{2}-2\lambda+N-2\right)\\
&=2\lambda^{6}+\tfrac{1}{4}\Big((4\lambda+2N-9)^{2}+8N-25\Big)\lambda^{4}\\
&\quad+\Big(N(4N-1)+2\Big)\lambda^{2}+N^{2}(N-1)(\lambda+2)^{2}+4N^{2}\\
&\quad+(N-6)\Big((2\lambda+N-1)^{2}\lambda^{2}+N^{3}\Big)\end{split}

by a straightforward computation, whence we get

E_{01}(\lambda)\geq 0\quad\text{ for all }N\geq 6.

Moreover, the same also applies to the case
N\in\{3,4,5\}
, as can be directly verified by the following calculation:

\begin{split}\text{when }N=3,\quad E_{01}(\lambda)&=6\lambda^{6}-6\lambda^{5}+10\lambda^{4}-24\lambda^{3}+41\lambda^{2}+72\lambda+27\\
&=\tfrac{1}{63}\left(\lambda+\tfrac{2}{3}\right)^{2}\left(40+\left(28\lambda-\tfrac{145}{3}\right)^{2}\right)\\
&\quad+\left(\lambda+\tfrac{2}{3}\right)^{4}\left(\tfrac{1}{18}+\tfrac{1}{6}(6\lambda-11)^{2}\right)+\tfrac{26}{9}\left(3\lambda+\tfrac{11}{9}\right)^{2}+\tfrac{1405}{729};\\
\text{when }N=4,\quad E_{01}(\lambda)&=6\lambda^{6}-2\lambda^{5}-6\lambda^{4}-24\lambda^{3}+92\lambda^{2}+192\lambda+128\\
&=\left(2\lambda^{3}-\tfrac{1}{2}\lambda^{2}-8\lambda-8\right)^{2}+2\lambda^{6}+\tfrac{103}{4}\lambda^{4}\\
&\quad+4\lambda^{2}+16(\lambda+2)^{2};\\
\text{when }N=5,\quad E_{01}(\lambda)&=6\lambda^{6}+2\lambda^{5}-16\lambda^{3}+181\lambda^{2}+400\lambda+375\\
&=5\left(\lambda^{3}-\tfrac{3}{2}\right)^{2}+\lambda^{2}\left(\lambda^{2}+\lambda-\tfrac{1}{2}\right)^{2}\\
&\quad+(\tfrac{25}{2}\lambda+16)^{2}+\tfrac{49}{2}\lambda^{2}+\tfrac{431}{4}.\end{split}

Therefore, we have obtained
E_{01}(\lambda)\geq 0
for all
N\geq 3
, as desired.

A Dynamical Systems Framework for Generating the Riemann Zeta-Function and Dirichlet L-functions
\name Shantanu Chakrabartty \email shantanu@wustl.edu

Abstract
Using an extension of the Price’s theorem we show how to construct a dynamical systems model which in its steady-state serves as an analytic continuation of the completed Riemann zeta function and Dirichlet L-functions over the entire critical strip. The resulting mathematical construct involves a linear interpolation of two symmetric generator functions and is used to infer the global properties of the non-trivial zeros of the Dirichlet L-functions using concentration bounds. The proposed dynamical systems framework thus provides an alternative method to investigate the celebrated Generalized Riemann Hypothesis (G.R.H) which is shown in this paper to hold almost surely.



Keywords:

Riemann Hypothesis, Riemann zeta function, Dirichlet L-functions Dynamical Systems, Generator functions,
Generalized Riemann Hypothesis

1
Introduction

For the sake of exposition and clarity, in this paper we introduce different steps of the derivation at a time. Starting from a basic introduction to the statement of the Riemann Hypothesis (R.H), in section
2
we first introduce the mathematical constructs used in the derivation. Then, in sections
3
and
4
we derive the dynamical systems model whose steady-state serves as the analytical continuation of the completed Riemann zeta function to the critical strip. There we show that within our dynamical systems framework the R.H. is almost surely true. In the subsequent section
5
we show that the approach could be generalized to understand the non-trivial zeros of the Dirichlet L-functions. There we show that under specific conditions the Riemann Hypothesis (R.H.) and the generalized Riemann Hypothesis (G.R.H) is almost surely true.
Please note that since the prior work addressing these topics is extensive, for the sake of brevity, we have included only the key references that support the constructs used in this derivation.

The completed Riemann zeta function
\phi:\mathbb{C}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}

(
Riemann, 1859
)
, is defined over the complex domain
s\in\mathbb{C}
and
\mathfrak{Re}(s)>1
as

\phi(s)=\pi^{-\frac{s}{2}}\Gamma(\frac{s}{2})\zeta(s)

(1)

where
\zeta(s)
is the Riemann zeta function
(
Titchmarsh, 1986
)
and

\Gamma(s)=\int_{0}^{\infty}y^{s-1}\exp\left(-y\right)dy

(2)

is the Gamma function. The function
\phi
satisfies the symmetry condition
\phi(s)=\phi(1-s)
and the zeros of this function
correspond to the non-trivial zeros of the Riemann zeta function
\zeta(s)
. Thus, the celebrated Riemann’s hypothesis (R.H.)
(
Riemann, 1859
;
Titchmarsh, 1986
)
which states that
all non-trivial zeros of
\zeta(s)
lie on the axis
\mathfrak{Re}(s)=\frac{1}{2}
is equivalent to the statement
that
all zeros of
\phi(s)
lie on the axis
\mathfrak{Re}(s)=\frac{1}{2}
. In his 1859 paper,
Riemann also expressed the function
\phi(.)
in an integral form as

\phi(s)=\int_{0}^{\infty}y^{\frac{s}{2}-1}\Psi(y)dy

(3)

where the function
\Psi(y)
is given by

\Psi(y)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\exp\left(-n^{2}\pi y\right).

(4)

with the domain still restricted to
\mathfrak{Re}(s)>1
.
Riemann then used equation
3
and the Poission summation formula to derive

\phi(s)=-\frac{1}{s}-\frac{1}{1-s}+\int_{1}^{\infty}\left(y^{\frac{s}{2}-1}+y^{\frac{1-s}{2}-1}\right)\Psi(y)dy

(5)

which is convergent over the entire s-plane and hence serves as an analytic continuation of the
\phi(s)

(
Riemann, 1859
)
. Note that the completed zeta function
\phi(s)
has two simple poles at
s=0
and
s=1
. Riemann also introduced the Xi function
\xi(s)
defined as

\xi(s)=s(s-1)\phi(s).

(6)

which eliminated the poles at
s=0
and
s=1
in equation
6
and has been extensively used
(
Bruijn, 1950
;
Newman, 1976
;
Rogers, 2020
)
used to understand the properties of the non-trivial zeros of
\zeta(s)
. The relation
5
will be used in the subsequent sections to find an alternate
analytic continuation of
\phi(s)
but for the critical strip
\mathfrak{Re}(s)\in(0,1)
.

2
Lemmas based on Gaussian Distribution Function

We define a two dimensional Gaussian distribution function
p:\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
over variables
x_{1}\in\mathbb{R}
and
x_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
as

p\left[x_{1},x_{2};(m_{1},m_{2},\rho)\right]=\frac{1}{2\pi\sqrt{1-\rho^{2}}}\exp\left[-\frac{\left(x_{1}-m_{1}\right)^{2}+\left(x_{2}-m_{2}\right)^{2}-2\rho\left(x_{1}-m_{1}\right)\left(x_{2}-m_{2}\right)}{2\left(1-\rho^{2}\right)}\right]

(7)

with
\rho\in\mathbb{R}
is a covariance parameter
|\rho|<1
and
m_{1}:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
,
m_{2}:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
are analytic functions with respect to the parameter
\rho
. Note that
A
is normalized as

\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}p\left[x_{1},x_{2};(m_{1},m_{2},\rho)\right]dx_{1}dx_{2}=1

(8)

and its Fourier transform of
M:\mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{C}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
is well defined as

\displaystyle M(\omega_{1},\omega_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}p\left[x_{1},x_{2};(m_{1},m_{2},\rho)\right]{\bf e}^{j\omega_{1}x_{1}+j\omega_{2}x_{2}}

(9)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\bf e}^{-\frac{1}{2}\omega_{1}^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\omega_{2}^{2}-\rho\omega_{1}\omega_{2}}\ {\bf e}^{jm_{1}\omega_{1}+jm_{2}\omega_{2}}.

(10)

Then, we can derive an extension of the Price’s theorem
(
Price, 1958
;
McMahon, 1964
;
Papoulis, 1965
)
as stated in the following Lemmas.

Lemma 1

Let
f:\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be an arbitrary memory-less, non-linear function that admits a Fourier transform, then

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial\rho}\mathcal{E}\left[f;(m_{1},m_{2},\rho)\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}\left[\frac{\partial^{2}f}{\partial x_{1}\partial x_{2}};(m_{1},m_{2},\rho)\right]

(11)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{dm_{1}}{d\rho}\mathcal{E}\left[\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{1}};(m_{1},m_{2},\rho)\right]+\frac{dm_{2}}{d\rho}\mathcal{E}\left[\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{2}};(m_{1},m_{2},\rho)\right]

(12)

where the operator
\mathcal{E}
is defined as

\mathcal{E}\left[f;(m_{1},m_{2},\rho)\right]=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}f(x_{1},x_{2})p\left[x_{1},x_{2};(m_{1},m_{2},\rho)\right]dx_{1}dx_{2}.

(13)

Proof

See Appendix I for proof.

Before we use Lemma
1
for the next result, we summarize the following useful relationships:

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}p\left[x_{1},x_{1};(0,\alpha,\rho)\right]dx_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\sqrt{\pi}\sqrt{1-\rho}}\exp\left[-\frac{\alpha^{2}}{4\left(1-\rho\right)}\right]

(14)

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}p\left[x_{1},-x_{1};(0,\alpha,\rho)\right]dx_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\sqrt{\pi}\sqrt{1+\rho}}\exp\left[-\frac{\alpha^{2}}{4\left(1+\rho\right)}\right]

(15)

To simplify the notations, we also define

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}\left[f;(\alpha,0,\rho)\pm(-\alpha,0,\rho)\pm(0,\alpha,\rho)\pm(0,-\alpha,\rho)\right]

(16)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}f(x_{1},x_{2})p\left[x_{1},x_{2};(\alpha,0,\rho)\right]dx_{1}dx_{2}

\displaystyle\pm

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}f(x_{1},x_{2})p\left[x_{1},x_{2};(-\alpha,0,\rho)\right]dx_{1}dx_{2}

\displaystyle\pm

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}f(x_{1},x_{2})p\left[x_{1},x_{2};(0,\alpha,\rho)\right]dx_{1}dx_{2}

\displaystyle\pm

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}f(x_{1},x_{2})p\left[x_{1},x_{2};(0,-\alpha,\rho)\right]dx_{1}dx_{2}

using which we can state the following lemma.

Lemma 2

Let
p\left[x_{1},x_{2};(0,0,\rho)\right]
be defined over the variables
x_{1}\in\mathbb{R}
and
x_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
as equation
7
and let
\alpha:\mathbb{C}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a complex function with respect to
\rho
. Denoting
\left|.\right|
as an absolute-value function

\left|x\right|=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcc}x&;&x\geq 0\\
-x&;&x<0\end{array}\right.

(17)

then

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial\rho}\mathcal{E}\left[\left|x_{1}-x_{2}\right|;(\alpha,0,\rho)+(-\alpha,0,\rho)+(0,\alpha,\rho)+(0,-\alpha,\rho)\right]

(18)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{4}{\sqrt{\pi}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-\rho}}\exp\left[-\frac{{\alpha}^{2}}{4\left(1-\rho\right)}\right].

Proof

First note that

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\left|x_{1}-x_{2}\right|}{\partial x_{1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\rm sgn}\left(x_{1}-x_{2}\right)

(19)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\left|x_{1}-x_{2}\right|}{\partial x_{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-{\rm sgn}\left(x_{1}-x_{2}\right)

(20)

where
{\rm sgn}(.)
is a sign function

{\rm sgn}\left(x\right)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcc}1&;&x\geq 0\\
-1&;&x<0\end{array}\right.

(21)

Also,

\frac{\partial^{2}\left|x_{1}-x_{2}\right|}{\partial x_{1}\partial x_{2}}=-2\delta\left(x_{1}-x_{2}\right)

(22)

where
\delta(.)
denotes the Dirac-delta function.

Then applying Lemma
1
to the LHS of equation
18
leads to

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial\rho}\mathcal{E}\left[\left|x_{1}-x_{2}\right|;(\alpha,0,\rho)+(-\alpha,0,\rho)+(0,\alpha,\rho)+(0,-\alpha,\rho)\right]

(23)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\left(p\left[x_{1},x_{1};(\alpha,0,\rho)\right]+p\left[x_{1},x_{1};(-\alpha,0,\rho)\right]\right)dx_{1}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle 2\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\left(p\left[x_{1},x_{1};(0,\alpha,\rho)\right]+p\left[x_{1},x_{1};(0,-\alpha,\rho)\right]\right)dx_{1}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial\rho}\mathcal{E}\left[{\rm sgn}\left(x_{1}-x_{2}\right);(\alpha,0,\rho)-(-\alpha,0,\rho)+(0,-\alpha,\rho)-(0,\alpha,\rho)\right].

Using the property
F(x_{1},x_{2})=-F(x_{2},x_{1})\implies\mathcal{E}\left[F(x_{1},x_{2})\right]=0
, the last term can be shown to be zero, and hence

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial\rho}\mathcal{E}\left[\left|x_{1}-x_{2}\right|;(\alpha,0,\rho)+(-\alpha,0,\rho)+(0,\alpha,\rho)+(0,-\alpha,\rho)\right]

(24)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{4}{\sqrt{\pi}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-\rho}}\exp\left[-\frac{{\alpha}^{2}}{4\left(1-\rho\right)}\right].

3
Complementary Generator Functions and Riemann Zeta Function

We will now apply Lemma’s
1
and
2
to the following function

\displaystyle z_{N}(\rho):

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\mathcal{E}\left[\left|x_{1}-x_{2}\right|;(0,0,\rho)\right]

(25)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{N}\left(\mathcal{E}\left[\left|x_{1}-x_{2}\right|;(\alpha_{n},0,\rho)+(-\alpha_{n},0,\rho)+(0,\alpha_{n},\rho)+(0,-\alpha_{n},\rho)\right]\right)

where
\alpha_{n}\in\mathbb{C},n=1,2,..,N
in equation
25
are a countable set of functions with respect to the parameter
\rho
. Note that
|\rho|<1
for Lemmas
1
and
2
to be valid. Applying Lemma
2
to the function
25
and using equation
22
leads to

\frac{\partial}{\partial\rho}z_{N}(\rho)=\frac{4}{\sqrt{\pi}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-\rho}}\left[\frac{1}{2}+\sum_{n=1}^{N}\exp\left[-\frac{{\alpha_{n}}^{2}}{4\left(1-\rho\right)}\right]\right].

(26)

Equation
26
will be the fundamental generator equation which will be used to produce different functions based on the choice of
\{\alpha_{n}\}
as a function of
\rho
.

We first define a generator function based on the following choice of
\{\alpha_{n}\}
as

\alpha_{n}(s,\rho)=n\sqrt{2\pi}\left(1-\rho\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(1-\rho\right)^{\frac{1}{2s}}

(27)

where
s\in\mathbb{C}
is a complex variable that is restricted to the domain
\mathfrak{Re}{s}\in(0,1)
.

Inserting equation
27
in
26
leads to a complex function
z_{N}(s)
as

z_{N}(s)=\frac{4}{\sqrt{\pi}}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-\rho}}\left[\frac{1}{2}+\sum_{n=1}^{N}\exp\left[-n^{2}\pi\left(1-\rho\right)^{\frac{1}{s}}\right]\right]d\rho.

(28)

Since
\alpha_{n}(s)
includes a multi-valued exponential function, we define a complex variable

y=\exp\left(\frac{1}{s}\log\left(1-\rho\right)\right)

(29)

along the principal branch and substitute
y
in the equation
28
which leads to

\displaystyle z_{N}(s)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\sqrt{\frac{16}{\pi}}s\int_{0}^{1}y^{\frac{s}{2}-1}\left[\frac{1}{2}+\sum_{n=1}^{N}\exp\left[-n^{2}\pi y\right]\right]dy

(30)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\sqrt{\frac{16}{\pi}}s\left(\int_{0}^{1}y^{\frac{s}{2}-1}\left[\sum_{n=1}^{N}\exp\left[-n^{2}\pi y\right]\right]dy+\frac{1}{s}\right).

(31)

Note that the path of integration is along a contour path that lies within the domain
|y^{s}|<1
. Since the function
\sum_{n=1}^{N}\exp\left[-n^{2}\pi y\right]
does not have any singularity in this domain for
s\in(0,1)
, in the limit
N\rightarrow\infty

z(s)=\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}z_{N}(s)=-\sqrt{\frac{16}{\pi}}s\left[\int_{0}^{1}y^{\frac{s}{2}-1}\Psi(y)dy+\frac{1}{s}\right].

(32)

We now construct another generator function
z_{N}(1-s,\rho)
as

\displaystyle z_{N}(1-s,\rho)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\mathcal{E}\left[\left|\tilde{x}_{1}-\tilde{x}_{2}\right|;(0,0,\rho)\right]

(33)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{N}\left(\mathcal{E}\left[\left|\tilde{x}_{1}-\tilde{x}_{2}\right|;(\beta_{n},0,\rho)+(-\beta_{n},0,\rho)+(0,\beta_{n},\rho)+(0,-\beta_{n},\rho)\right]\right)

using a countable set of complex functions
\{\beta_{n}\}:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
as

\beta_{n}(s,\rho)=n\sqrt{2\pi}\left(1-\rho\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(1-\rho\right)^{\frac{1}{2(1-s)}}

(34)

The complex variable
s
is still constrained
\mathfrak{Re}(s)\in(0,1)
.
Applying a similar procedure as equations
28
-
32
leads to

z_{N}(1-s)=\sqrt{\frac{16}{\pi}}(1-s)\left(\int_{0}^{1}y^{\frac{s}{2}-1}\left[\sum_{n=1}^{N}\exp\left[-n^{2}\pi y\right]\right]dy+\frac{1}{1-s}\right)

and after applying the limit
N\rightarrow\infty

z(1-s)=\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}z_{N}(1-s)=\sqrt{\frac{16}{\pi}}(1-s)\left[\int_{0}^{1}y^{\frac{1-s}{2}-1}\Psi(y)dy+\frac{1}{1-s}\right].

(35)

Combining equations
32
and
35
leads to

\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{16}}\left[\frac{z(s)}{s}+\frac{z(1-s)}{1-s}\right]=\frac{1}{s}+\frac{1}{1-s}+\int_{0}^{1}y^{\frac{s}{2}-1}\Psi(y)dy+\int_{0}^{1}y^{\frac{1-s}{2}-1}\Psi(y)dy

(36)

defined over the domain
\mathfrak{Re}(s)\in(0,1)
.

Note that the form of equation
36
is similar to that of the completed Riemann zeta function
\phi(s)
in
5
. Since
5
is valid over
the entire s-plane, we can combine equations
36
and
5
over the restricted domain
\mathfrak{Re}(s)\in(0,1)
. This leads to

\displaystyle\phi(s)+\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{16}}\left[\frac{z(s)}{s}+\frac{z(1-s)}{1-s}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}y^{\frac{s}{2}-1}\Psi(y)dy+\int_{0}^{\infty}y^{\frac{1-s}{2}-1}\Psi(y)dy

(37)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\phi(s).

(38)

The RHS is based on the integral representation of
\phi(.)
in equation
3
and based on the symmetry
\phi(s)=\phi(1-s)
, but analytically continued to the critical strip using the generator functions. Thus,

\phi(s)=\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{16}}\left[\frac{z(s)}{s}+\frac{z(1-s)}{1-s}\right];\quad\mathfrak{Re}(s)\in(0,1)

(39)

The key advantage of the formulation
39
is that it is a linear interpolation of two symmetrical constructs
z(s)
and
z(1-s)
. Thus, for
\phi(s)=0
, the relative ratio between
z(s)
and
z(1-s)
could be investigated to understand global property of the solutions that satisfy
\phi(s)=0
.

4
Dynamical Systems Model and R.H.

Before we construct a dynamical systems model based on the equation
39

we first apply a variable transformation
s=\frac{1}{2}+j\left(\omega+j\sigma\right),\omega,\sigma\in\mathbb{R}
which is similar to the centering procedure used by Riemann
(
Riemann, 1859
)
. Using
39
, the completed Riemann zeta function
\phi(\frac{1}{2}+j\left(\omega+j\sigma\right))
can be written as

\sqrt{\frac{16}{\pi}}\phi\left(\left[\frac{1}{2}-\sigma\right]+j\omega\right)=\frac{z\left(\left[\frac{1}{2}-\sigma\right]+j\omega\right)}{\left(\left[\frac{1}{2}-\sigma\right]+j\omega\right)}+\frac{z\left(\left[\frac{1}{2}+\sigma\right]-j\omega\right)}{\left(\left[\frac{1}{2}+\sigma\right]-j\omega\right)}.

(40)

Since
\mathfrak{Re}(s)\in(0,1)
for our formulation,
\sigma\in(-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2})
.
The function
\alpha_{n}
in equation
27
can be expressed as

\alpha_{n}\left(\frac{1}{2}+j\left(\omega+j\sigma\right),\rho\right)=n\sqrt{2\pi}\exp\left(\frac{1}{2}\left[1+\frac{1}{\left(\frac{1}{2}-\sigma\right)+j\omega}\right]\log\left(1-\rho\right)\right)

(41)

We define the following parameters

\displaystyle\gamma_{\alpha}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\left(\frac{1}{2}-\sigma\right)}{\left(\frac{1}{2}-\sigma\right)^{2}+\omega^{2}}

(42)

\displaystyle\omega_{\alpha}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\omega}{\left(\frac{1}{2}-\sigma\right)^{2}+\omega^{2}}.

(43)

and choose
\rho
to be a function of time
t\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
as

\rho(t)=1-e^{-t}.

(44)

Then as
t:0\rightarrow\infty
,
\rho:0\rightarrow 1
. It is easy to verify that
|\rho|<1,\forall t

which is required for the Gaussian probability density functions to be integrable. Combining
41
and
44
leads to

\alpha_{n}(t)=\alpha_{n}\left(\frac{1}{2}+j\left(\omega+j\sigma\right),\rho(t)\right)=n\sqrt{2\pi}\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}\left[1+\gamma_{\alpha}\right]t\right)\exp\left(\frac{j}{2}\omega_{\alpha}t\right).

(45)

Similarly, we define another set of parameters for
the other generator function
z(1-s)

\displaystyle\gamma_{\beta}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\left(\frac{1}{2}+\sigma\right)}{\left(\frac{1}{2}+\sigma\right)^{2}+\omega^{2}}

(46)

\displaystyle\omega_{\beta}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\omega}{\left(\frac{1}{2}+\sigma\right)^{2}+\omega^{2}}

(47)

which from equation
34
leads to

\beta_{n}(t)=\beta_{n}\left(\frac{1}{2}-j\left(\omega+j\sigma\right),\rho(t)\right)=n\sqrt{2\pi}\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}\left[1+\gamma_{\beta}\right]t\right)\exp\left(\frac{j}{2}\omega_{\beta}t\right)

(48)

We can now describe the time evolution of the dynamical system according to the following set of equations

\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\rho(t)=1;\quad\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\alpha_{n}(t)=0;\quad\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\beta_{n}(t)=0

(49)

\displaystyle\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{16}}\left[\frac{z_{N}\left(\left(\frac{1}{2}-\sigma\right)+j\omega,\rho(t)\right)}{\left(\frac{1}{2}-\sigma\right)+j\omega}+\frac{z_{N}\left(\left(\frac{1}{2}+\sigma\right)-j\omega,\rho(t)\right)}{\left(\frac{1}{2}+\sigma\right)-j\omega}\right]=\phi(\frac{1}{2}+j\left(\omega+j\sigma\right))

which will be used to investigate the Riemann Hypothesis and is stated in the following lemma:

Lemma 3

If the completed Riemann zeta function
\phi(\frac{1}{2}+j\left(\omega+j\sigma\right))=0
, then almost surely
\sigma=0
.

Proof

We will use the notation

\displaystyle z(-\sigma+j\omega,t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}z_{N}(-\sigma+j\omega,t)=\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}z_{N}\left(\left(\frac{1}{2}-\sigma\right)+j\omega,\rho(t)\right)

\displaystyle z(\sigma-j\omega,t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}z_{N}(\sigma-j\omega,t)=\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}z_{N}\left(\left(\frac{1}{2}+\sigma\right)-j\omega,\rho(t)\right).

(50)

Then, setting
\phi\left(\frac{1}{2}+j\left(\omega+j\sigma\right)\right)=0
in equation
49
, we obtain from equation
40
the following condition:

\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\left|z_{N}(-\sigma+j\omega,t)\right|}{\left|z_{N}(\sigma-j\omega,t)\right|}=\frac{\left|\left(\frac{1}{2}-\sigma\right)+j\omega\right|}{\left|\left(\frac{1}{2}+\sigma\right)-j\omega\right|}

(51)

where
\left|.\right|
denotes the magnitude of the complex variable. The RHS of
51
is bounded since
-\frac{1}{2}<\sigma<\frac{1}{2}
. Therefore, the asymptotic behavior of the LHS in equation
51
should also be bounded for all non-trivial zeros.

Using the definition of
z_{N}(-\sigma+j\omega,t)
and
z_{N}(\sigma-j\omega,t)
according to equations
25
and
33
as

\displaystyle z_{N}(-\sigma+j\omega,t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}\left[\left|x_{1}-x_{2}\right|;(0,0,e^{-t})\right]

(52)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{N}\mathcal{E}\left[\left|x_{1}-x_{2}\right|;(0,\alpha_{n}(t),e^{-t})+(0,-\alpha_{n}(t),e^{-t})+(\alpha_{n}(t),0,e^{-t})+(-\alpha_{n}(t),0,e^{-t})\right]

\displaystyle z_{N}(\sigma-j\omega,t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}\left[\left|x_{1}-x_{2}\right|;(0,0,e^{-t})\right]

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{N}\mathcal{E}\left[\left|x_{1}-x_{2}\right|;(0,\beta_{n}(t),e^{-t})+(0,-\beta_{n}(t),e^{-t})+(\beta_{n}(t),0,e^{-t})+(-\beta_{n}(t),0,e^{-t})\right]

where
\alpha_{n}(t)
and
\beta_{n}(t)
are defined in equations
45
and
48
.

Substituting
\Delta x=x_{1}-x_{2}
leads to

\displaystyle z_{N}(-\sigma+j\omega,t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{e^{t/2}}{\sqrt{\pi}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|\Delta x|\exp\left[-\frac{1}{4}e^{t}\left(\Delta x\right)^{2}\right]d\Delta x

(53)

\displaystyle+

We now show that the dynamical systems framework in section

can also be extended to study the non-trivial zeros of the Dirichlet L-functions. The Dirichlet L-function
(

is defined as an analytic continuation of the Dirichlet series

represents a Dirichlet character modulo

with the following properties

1.

\chi(m\times n)=\chi(m)\chi(n)
for every

.

2.

\chi(n+P)=\chi(n)
for all
n
.

3.

\chi(n)=0
if gcd
(n,q)>1
.

Note that for a trivial primitive Dirichlet character
\chi(n)=1
modulo
P
, the Dirichlet L-function reduces to the Riemann zeta-function.
Even though the series
L(s,\chi)
is defined over the domain
\mathfrak{Re}(s)>1
it can be extended over the entire s-domain provided
\chi
is primitive modulo
P

(
Dirichlet, 1969
)
. Like in sections
3
and
4
, generating the Dirichlet L-function will exploit the analytic continuation procedure, but we will restrict the domain only to the critical-strip. We will use this procedure to investigate the Generalized Riemann hypothesis (G.R.H.)
(
Dirichlet, 1969
;
Titchmarsh, 1986
)
which states that
all non-trivial zeros of
L(s,\chi)
lie on the axis
\mathfrak{Re}(s)=\frac{1}{2}
. However, we will consider two cases
L(s,\chi)
of depending on whether
\chi
is even or odd.

5.1

\chi

For an even parity
\chi
function satisfying

and then working backwards, like in section

6
Discussions and Conclusions

In this paper we used an extension of the Price’s theorem to construct a novel dynamical systems model whose steady-state evolves towards the completed Riemann zeta function and in a more general case towards a Dirichlet L-function.

The model describes a dynamical system that combines both stochastic and deterministic processes where the two random sources become more correlated and asymptotically cancel each other out resulting in an evolution towards a deterministic steady-state function. In this paper we chose a specific form of the dynamical system that resulted in the completed Riemann zeta function and the Dirichlet L-function as the steady-state. However, choosing different forms of non-linear function
z_{N}
and the variables
\alpha_{n},\beta_{n},n=1,2,..
, should result in other dynamical systems formulation that evolve towards other functions. We believe that any Dirichlet series function that admits analytic continuation similar to the steps in
78
and
80
, can be analyzed using this model.

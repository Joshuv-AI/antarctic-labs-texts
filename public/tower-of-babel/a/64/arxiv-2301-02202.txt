Abstract
. We provide a natural criterion that implies equality of the test ideal and big test ideal in local rings of prime characteristic. Most notably, we show that the criterion is met by every local weakly F F -regular ring whose anti-canonical algebra is Noetherian on the punctured spectrum.

Remark 1.5

Annihilators of Local Cohomology

This section is devoted to proving Theorem
B
. Let
(R,\mathfrak{m},k)
be an excellent local normal domain of Krull dimension
d\geq 3
and
I\subseteq R
an ideal of pure height
1
. Let
W=R\setminus\bigcup_{P\in\min(I)}P

Let

0\to\frac{\overline{J^{n-1}}}{\overline{J^{n}}}\to\frac{R}{\overline{J^{n}}}\to\frac{R}{\overline{J^{n-1}}}\to 0,

and so there are exact sequences of local cohomology modules

H^{i}_{\mathfrak{m}}\left(\frac{\overline{J^{n-1}}}{\overline{J^{n}}}\right)\to H^{i}_{\mathfrak{m}}\left(\frac{R}{\overline{J^{n}}}\right)\to H^{i}_{\mathfrak{m}}\left(\frac{R}{\overline{J^{n-1}}}\right).

Our aim is to establish uniform annihilators of the local cohomology modules

•

R[Jt]=\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}J^{n}t^{n}
is the Rees algebra of

The Faltings Annihilator Theorem, later generalized by Brodmann, provides a criterion to establish such annihilation properties.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m},k)
be an excellent local normal domain of Krull dimension
d

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we may pass to the completion of

If

Then, because

\displaystyle 0pt(G_{P})+0pt\left(\frac{\mathfrak{m}S+P}{P}\right)

\displaystyle=0pt_{S}(P)-1+\dim(S/P)-\dim(S/\mathfrak{m}S+P)

\displaystyle=0pt_{S}(P)-1+d+1-0pt_{S}(P)-\dim(S/\mathfrak{m}S+P)

\displaystyle=d-\dim(S/\mathfrak{m}S+P).

Recall that

3.
Tight closure, local cohomology, and local cohomology bounds

3.1.
Tight closure

Let
R
be a ring of prime characteristic
p>0
and let
R^{\circ}
be the complement of the union of the minimal primes of
R
. The
e
th Frobenius functor, or the
e
th Peskine-Szpiro functor, is the functor
F^{e}:\operatorname{Mod}(R)\to\operatorname{Mod}(R)
obtained by extending scalars along the
e
th iterate of the Frobenius endomorphism. If
N\subseteq M
are
R
-modules and
m\in M
, then
m
is in the tight closure of
N
relative to
M
if there exists a
c\in R^{\circ}
such that for all
e\gg 0
the element
m
is in the kernel of the following composition of maps:

M\to M/N\to F^{e}(M/N)\xrightarrow{\cdot c}F^{e}(M/N).

In particular, if we consider an inclusion of
R
-modules of the form
I\subseteq R
then
F^{e}(R/I)\cong R/I^{[p^{e}]}
where
I^{[p^{e}]}=(r^{p^{e}}\mid r\in I)
, and an element
r\in R
is in the tight closure of
I
relative to
R
if there exists a
c\in R^{\circ}
such that
cr^{p^{e}}\in I^{[p^{e}]}
for all
e\gg 0
. The tight closure of the module
N
relative to the module
M
is denoted
N^{*}_{M}
. In the case that
M=R
and
N=I
is an ideal then we denote the tight closure of
I
relative to
R
as
I^{*}
. We say that
N
is tightly closed in
M
if
N=N^{*}_{M}
. If an ideal is tightly closed in
R
then we simply say that the ideal is tightly closed. The finitistic tight closure of
N\subseteq M
is denoted
N^{*fg}_{M}
and is the union of
(N\cap M^{\prime})^{*}_{M^{\prime}}
where
M^{\prime}
runs over all finitely generated submodules of
M
.

The notions of weak
F
-regularity and strong
F
-regularity can be compared by studying the finitistic tight closure and tight closure of the zero submodule of the injective hull of a local ring by
[
HH90
, Proposition 8.23]
and
[
Smi93
, Proposition 7.1.2]
. Suppose that
(R,\mathfrak{m},k)
is complete local and
E_{R}(k)
is the injective hull of the residue field. The finitistic test ideal of
R
is
\tau_{fg}(R)=\bigcap_{I\subseteq R}\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(I^{*}/I)
and agrees with
\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(0^{*fg}_{E_{R}(k)})
. The (big) test ideal of
R
is
\tau(R)=\bigcap_{N\subseteq M\in\operatorname{Mod}(R)}\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(N^{*}_{M}/N)
and agrees with
\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(0^{*}_{E_{R}(k)})
. The ring
R
is weakly
F
-regular if and only if
\tau_{fg}(R)=R
and
R
is strongly
F
-regular if and only if
\tau(R)=R
. Thus to prove the conjectured equivalence of weak and strong
F
-regularity it is enough to show
0^{*}_{E_{R}(k)}=0^{*fg}_{E_{R}(k)}
under hypotheses satisfied by rings which are weakly
F
-regular.

To explore the tight closure of the zero submodule of
E_{R}(k)
we exploit the structure of

as a direct limit of
0
-dimensional Gorenstein quotients of

described in
[
Hoc77
]
. Suppose
(R,\mathfrak{m},k)
is a complete local Cohen-Macaulay domain of Krull dimension

and

a canonical ideal. Let

,
x_{2},\ldots,x_{d}\in R
a parameter sequence, and for each

let

. The sequences of ideals

form a decreasing sequence of irreducible
\mathfrak{m}
-primary ideals cofinal with

. Moreover, the direct limit system
\varinjlim R/I_{t}\xrightarrow{\cdot x_{1}\cdots x_{d}}R/I_{t+1}
is isomorphic to

. The following lemma uses this description of the injective hull of the residue field to describe any potential difference between the modules

and

. We refer the reader to the discussion at the beginning of
[

for details.

Let

Our study of local cohomology bounds begins with two useful observations.

Lemma 3.3

Let

R
-module, and

\operatorname{lcb}_{i}(\underline{x}^{j};M)\leq\operatorname{lcb}_{i}(\underline{x};M)

where

Proof.

One only has to observe that
\alpha^{i}_{M;\underline{x}^{j};k,k+m}=\alpha^{i}_{M;\underline{x};jk,jk+jm}

∎

If
x_{1},\ldots,x_{d}
is a sequence of elements in a ring

and if

for some

then the short exact sequence of Koszul cocomplexes

Lemma 3.4

an

a sequence of elements such that

Let

0pt(\operatorname{Coker}(\partial_{h}^{*}))=d-h
. Moreover, there is a short exact sequence

0\to\operatorname{Ext}^{h}_{S}(M,S)\to\operatorname{Coker}(\partial_{h}^{*})\to\operatorname{Im}(\partial_{h+1}^{*})\to 0.

The module
\operatorname{Im}(\partial_{h+1}^{*})
is torsion-free and therefore has depth at least
1
. If
d-h\geq 2
then
\operatorname{Ext}^{h}_{S}(M,S)
has depth at least
2
. If
d-h=1
then the depth of
\operatorname{Ext}^{h}_{S}(M,S)
is
1
. If
d-h=0
then
M
is
0
-dimensional. Therefore if
\operatorname{ht}(\operatorname{Ann}_{S}(M))=h
then
\operatorname{Ext}^{h}_{S}(M,S)
is an
(S_{2})
-module over its support.
∎

Continue to consider the ring
S
, the module
M
, and the resolution
(F_{\bullet},\partial_{\bullet})
as above. Suppose further

is a regular local ring and hence every finitely generated
S
-module has a finite free resolution. Consider the minimal free resolution
(G_{\bullet},\delta_{\bullet})
of
\operatorname{Ext}^{h}_{S}(M,S)
. If

is maximal, then
\operatorname{Ext}^{h}_{S}(M,S)=\operatorname{Coker}(\partial_{h}^{*})
and therefore
(G_{\bullet},\delta_{\bullet})
is the complex

In particular, if
0pt(M)=d-h
then
\operatorname{Ext}^{h}_{S}(\operatorname{Ext}^{h}_{S}(M,S),S)\cong M
. Suppose

and let
(F_{\bullet}^{*},\partial_{\bullet}^{*})_{tr}
be the complex obtained by truncating

at the

th spot. That is
(F_{\bullet}^{*},\partial_{\bullet}^{*})_{tr}
is the minimal free resolution of
\operatorname{Coker}(\partial_{h}^{*})
. Then the natural inclusion
\operatorname{Ext}^{h}_{S}(M,S)\subseteq\operatorname{Coker}(\partial_{h}^{*})
lifts to a map of complexes
(G_{\bullet},\delta_{\bullet})\to(F_{\bullet}^{*},\partial_{\bullet}^{*})_{tr}
and therefore there is an induced natural map

. The map

is an isomorphism whenever

is a (maximal) Cohen-Macaulay module over its support.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m},k)
be a complete local normal domain of Krull dimension
d\geq 1
and
J\subseteq R
a pure height
1
ideal. Suppose
(S,\mathfrak{n},k)
is a regular local ring mapping onto
R
,
R\cong S/P
, and
\operatorname{ht}(P)=h
. Then for every integer
i
the kernel of the natural map
R/J^{i}\to\operatorname{Ext}^{h+1}_{S}(\operatorname{Ext}^{h+1}_{S}(R/J^{i},S),S)
is
J^{(i)}/J^{i}
. In particular, for every integer
i
there is a natural inclusion
R/J^{(i)}\subseteq\operatorname{Ext}^{h+1}_{S}(\operatorname{Ext}^{h+1}_{S}(R/J^{i},S),S)
. Moreover, the natural inclusion
R/J^{(i)}\subseteq\operatorname{Ext}^{h+1}_{S}(\operatorname{Ext}^{h+1}_{S}(R/J^{i},S),S)
is an isomorphism whenever localized at prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}\in V(J)
such that
(R/J^{(i)})_{\mathfrak{p}}
is Cohen-Macaulay.

Proof.

Let
L_{i}\subseteq R
be the ideal of
R
, containing
J^{i}
, so that
L_{i}/J^{i}
is the kernel of
R/J^{i}\to\operatorname{Ext}^{h+1}_{S}(\operatorname{Ext}^{h+1}_{S}(R/J^{i},S),S)
. Then
R/L_{i}\subseteq\operatorname{Ext}^{h+1}_{S}(\operatorname{Ext}^{h+1}_{S}(R/J^{i},S),S)
. If
P
is a prime component of
J
then
R_{P}/J^{i}R_{P}
is
0
-dimensional and therefore Cohen-Macaulay. By the above the discussion, the map under analysis is an isomorphism in the Cohen-Macaulay locus, and therefore

R_{P}/J^{i}R_{P}=R_{P}/J^{(i)}R_{P}=R_{P}/{L_{i}}_{P}=\operatorname{Ext}_{S_{P}}^{h+1}(\operatorname{Ext}_{S_{P}}^{h+1}(R_{P}/J^{(i)}R_{P},S_{P}),S_{P})

at prime
P
which are minimal components of
J
.

If
P
is a prime of
R
of height
1
which is not a component of
J
, then
R_{P}/J^{i}R_{P}=0
and the identifications above remain true. Therefore the height
1
components of the ideal of
L_{i}
are precisely the height
1
components of
J^{i}
, i.e.
J^{(i)}
. To conclude that

it remains to show that the ideal
L_{i}
does not have embedded components. The module
\operatorname{Ext}^{h+1}_{S}(\operatorname{Ext}^{h+1}_{S}(R/J^{i},S),S)
is an
(S_{2})

R/J^{i}
-module and
R/L_{i}
is a submodule. Therefore
R/L_{i}
is an
(S_{1})
-module and hence
L_{i}
cannot have an embedded component.

We have proven the first claim of the lemma that for each
i\in\mathbb{N}
there is a natural inclusion
R/J^{(i)}\subseteq\operatorname{Ext}^{h+1}_{S}(\operatorname{Ext}^{h+1}_{S}(R/J^{i},S),S)
. It remains to check that this inclusion is an isomorphism whenever localized at prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}\in V(J)
such that
(R/J^{(i)})_{\mathfrak{p}}
is Cohen-Macaulay. Indeed,
R/J^{i}\to R/J^{(i)}
induces an isomorphism

\operatorname{Ext}^{h+1}_{S}(R/J^{(i)},S)\xrightarrow{\cong}\operatorname{Ext}^{h+1}_{S}(R/J^{i},S)

as
J^{(i)}/J^{i}
is not supported at any height
h+1
component of
S
. Therefore the inclusion
R/J^{(i)}\subseteq\operatorname{Ext}^{h+1}_{S}(\operatorname{Ext}^{h+1}_{S}(R/J^{i},S),S)
is the same as

R/J^{(i)}\to\operatorname{Ext}^{h+1}_{S}(\operatorname{Ext}^{h+1}_{S}(R/J^{(i)},S),S)

and this map is an isomorphism in the Cohen-Macaulay locus by the discussion preceding the lemma.
∎

Lemma 4.7

(4.2)

\displaystyle\operatorname{Ext}^{h+1}_{S}\left(\frac{R}{J_{1}^{(mi+1)}},S\right)\cong\omega_{R/J_{1}^{(mi+1)}}\cong\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{1}\left(\frac{R}{J_{1}^{(mi+1)}},J_{1}\right).

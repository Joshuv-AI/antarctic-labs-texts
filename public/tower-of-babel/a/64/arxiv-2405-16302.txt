Fernando C. Marques and André Neves

Abstract
. In this paper, we describe the intersection between geodesic and conformal currents on closed hyperbolic three-manifolds. We use this to prove some sharp bounds which involve the Liouville entropy of a negatively curved metric, the minimal surface entropy, and the area ratio. Using these ideas we also give a new proof of the Mostow Rigidity Theorem in the three-dimensional case.



1.
Introduction

Let
\Gamma
be a group of isometries which acts properly discontinuously on the hyperbolic space
\mathbb{H}^{3}
, so that
M=\mathbb{H}^{3}/\Gamma
is a closed manifold.

In
[
6
]
, Bonahon defined
geodesic currents as
\Gamma
-invariant Radon measures on the space of hyperbolic geodesics and, in the two-dimensional case, he defined the intersection number of geodesic currents. These concepts are essential objects in the study of geodesics in
surfaces of negative curvature. In
[
25
]
, Labourie defined conformal currents as
\Gamma
-invariant Radon measures on the space of quasi-circles of
S^{2}
.

In this paper, we will use the intersection number between conformal and geodesic currents and derive some applications.

We denote the space of geodesic currents by
\mathcal{C}(\Gamma)
and that of conformal currents by
\mathcal{C}_{2}(\Gamma)
. The hyperbolic metric on
M
is denoted by
\overline{g}
and
g
denotes a metric of negative sectional curvature on
M
.

A nontrivial closed curve
\gamma
in
M
induces a homotopy class
[\gamma]
and
l_{g}([\gamma])
denotes the least length of curves in
[\gamma]
. If
\Sigma
is an essential (or incompressible)
surface in
M
then it induces a homotopy class
\Pi
. The surface
\Sigma
is said to be quasi-Fuchsian if the asymptotic boundary of its lift to
\mathbb{H}^{3}
is a quasi-circle. The group
\Gamma
acts on
S^{2}=\partial\mathbb{H}^{3}
by conformal transformations, and hence preserve the space of quasi-circles. We will denote by
{\rm area}_{g}(\Pi)
the least area of surfaces in
\Pi
.

A map
\Pi\mapsto\delta_{\Pi}\in{\mathcal{C}_{2}}(\Gamma)
was defined in
[
25
, Section 5.2]
for homotopy classes
\Pi
of quasi-Fuchsian surfaces which generalizes the map
[\gamma]\mapsto\delta_{[\gamma]}\in\mathcal{C}(\Gamma)
of
[
6
]
. There is a Radon measure
\nu
, with support the space of round circles
of
S^{2}
, that is invariant by the group
\text{\rm M\"{o}b}(S^{2})
of
Möbius
transformations. These are the maps induced
on
S^{2}
by the hyperbolic isometries and hence
\nu\in{\mathcal{C}_{2}}(\Gamma)
. The negatively curved metric
g
induces a Liouville current
\lambda_{g}\in\mathcal{C}(\Gamma)
. These examples of geodesic currents and conformal currents are described in detail in Section
2.3
and Section
3.2
.

The intersection between geodesic and conformal currents can be defined following
[
6
]
, and it is a bilinear map

I=I_{\Gamma}:{\mathcal{C}_{2}}(\Gamma)\times\mathcal{C}(\Gamma)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{+}.

This map was also used in
[
8
]
to approximate by geometric actions of
\Gamma
on cube complexes the action of
\Gamma
on the hyperbolic space.

The intersection form
I
is related to the geometric intersection number. For instance,
I(\delta_{\Pi},\delta_{[\gamma]})\leq\#(\Sigma\cap\alpha),

where
\Sigma
is any immersed surface in
\Pi
and
\alpha
is any closed curve in
[\gamma]
which intersect transversely. If
\Sigma\in\Pi
is a totally geodesic surface and
\alpha\in[\gamma]
is a closed geodesic for the hyperbolic metric which intersect transversely, then
I(\delta_{\Pi},\delta_{[\gamma]})=\#(\Sigma\cap\alpha)
. If
\Sigma
and
\alpha
are induced by the immersions
i_{\Sigma}:\Sigma\rightarrow M
,
i_{\alpha}:S^{1}\rightarrow M
, then

\#(\Sigma\cap\alpha)
is the number of
(x,y)\in\Sigma\times S^{1}
such that
i_{\Sigma}(x)=i_{\alpha}(y)
.

We prove:

Theorem 1.1
.

For a negatively curved metric
(M,g)
:

(i)

I(\delta_{\Pi},\lambda_{g})\leq\pi\,{\rm area}_{g}(\Pi),
and if equality holds then
\Pi
contains a totally geodesic surface in the metric
g
;

(ii)

I(\nu,\lambda_{g})=2\pi^{2}{\rm vol}_{g}(M)i(g,\overline{g})
, where
i(g,\overline{g})
denotes the geodesic stretch of
\overline{g}
with respect to
g
and its Liouville measure.

The geodesic stretch was introduced and called intersection by Croke and Fathi in
[
11
]
.

1.1.
Applications

We describe some consequences of Theorem
1.1
. We say
\Pi\in S_{\eta}(M)
if the asymptotic boundary of the lift to
\mathbb{H}^{3}
of a surface in
\Pi
is a
(1+\eta)
-quasi-circle.

Minimal surface entropy.
In
[
9
]
, Calegari and the authors defined the
minimal surface entropy

E(g)
of
(M,g)
by

(1)

E(g)=\lim_{\eta\to 0}\liminf_{L\to\infty}4\pi\frac{\ln\#\{\Pi\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\eta}(M):\text{area}_{g}(\Pi)\leq L\}}{L\ln L}.

In Proposition
2.1
it is proved that formula (
1
) is also true by taking the limsup. It is shown in
[
9
]
that
E(\overline{g})=2
and that, for a metric
g
with sectional curvature less than or equal to
-1
,
E(g)\geq E(\overline{g})=2
and equality implies
g
is hyperbolic. In
[
26
]
, it is shown that
E(g)\leq 2
for any metric
g
with scalar curvature greater than or equal to
-6
, and equality implies
g
is a hyperbolic metric.

Let
\mu
be a unit Radon measure on the Grassmannian
Gr_{2}(M)
of
M
that is invariant by the homogeneous action of
{\rm PSL}(2,\mathbb{R})
. One such example is the normalized Liouville measure of the metric
\overline{g}
on
Gr_{2}(M)
which we denote by
\overline{\nu}
. The theory of Ratner-Shah
[
30
,
36
]
classifies those measures as linear combinations of
\overline{\nu}
and measures supported on closed totally geodesic surfaces.

Let
\rho
be a metric which topologizes the space of unit Radon measures on
Gr_{2}(M)
. There is
\tilde{\eta}>0
such that if
\Pi\in S_{\eta}(M)
,
0<\eta<\tilde{\eta}
, then there is a unique
\Sigma\in\Pi
such that
{\rm area}_{\overline{g}}(\Sigma)={\rm area}_{\overline{g}}(\Pi)
(combining
[
35
]
and
[
39
, Theorem 3.3]
). We set
\Sigma=\Sigma(\Pi)
and define a unit measure on
Gr_{2}(M)

\nu_{\Pi}(\psi)=\frac{1}{\text{area}_{\overline{g}}(\Sigma(\Pi))}\int_{\Sigma(\Pi)}\psi(x,T_{x}\Sigma(\Pi))\,d\Sigma(\Pi)_{\overline{g}}(x)

where
\psi:Gr_{2}(M)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is a continuous function and
T_{x}\Sigma
denotes the tangent space to
\Sigma
at
x
.

We define the
minimal surface entropy
E_{\mu}(g)
for
\mu
of
(M,g)
as being

(2)

E_{\mu}(g)=\lim_{\eta\to 0}\liminf_{L\to\infty}4\pi\frac{\ln\#\{\Pi\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\eta,\mu}(M):\text{area}_{g}(\Pi)\leq L\}}{L\ln L},

where
\mathcal{S}_{\eta,\mu}(M)=\{\Pi\in S_{\eta}(M):\rho(\nu_{\Pi},\mu)<\eta\}
. Since the space of unit Radon measures on
Gr_{2}(M)
is compact, the definition of
E_{\mu}(g)
does not depend on the metric
\rho
. Notice that
E_{\mu}(g)\leq E(g)
for any
\mu
.

We use Theorem
1.1
to prove a sharp bound between
E_{\overline{\nu}}
and
h_{L}(g)
, the Liouville entropy of the geodesic flow of
g
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Suppose that
g
is a metric of negative sectional curvature on
M
. Then

E_{\overline{\nu}}(g)h_{L}(g)\text{\rm vol}_{g}(M)\leq 4\text{\rm vol}_{\overline{g}}(M),

and if equality holds the metric
g
has constant sectional curvature.

We briefly sketch the proof of the inequality in Theorem
1.2
. We first show the existence of
\Pi_{i}\in\mathcal{S}_{1/i}(M)
so that

E_{\overline{\nu}}(g)=2\lim_{i\to\infty}\frac{{\rm area}_{\overline{g}}(\Pi_{i})}{{\rm area}_{g}(\Pi_{i})}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\lim_{i\to\infty}\frac{1}{{\rm area}_{\overline{g}}(\Pi_{i})}\delta_{\Pi_{i}}=\frac{1}{2\pi{\rm vol}_{\bar{g}}(M)}\,{\nu}.

Knieper
[
23
, Theorem 1.1]
showed that
i(g,\overline{g})\geq\frac{h_{L}(g)}{2}
. The form
I
is continuous at
(\nu,\lambda)
for any
\lambda\in\mathcal{C}(\Gamma)
by Proposition
3.4
(also
[
8
]
). Hence, by Theorem
1.1
,

(3)

\text{\rm vol}_{g}(M)h_{L}(g)\leq 2\text{\rm vol}_{g}(M)i(g,\overline{g})=\frac{I(\nu,\lambda_{g})}{\pi^{2}}\\
=2\frac{{\rm vol}_{\bar{g}}(M)}{\pi}\lim_{i\to\infty}I(\frac{\delta_{\Pi_{i}}}{{\rm area}_{\overline{g}}(\Pi_{i})},\lambda_{g})\leq 2{\rm vol}_{\bar{g}}(M)\lim_{i\to\infty}\frac{{\rm area}_{{g}}(\Pi_{i})}{{\rm area}_{\overline{g}}(\Pi_{i})}\\
=4\frac{{\rm vol}_{\bar{g}}(M)}{E_{\overline{\nu}}(g)}.

The entropy
E_{\overline{\nu}}(g)
counts only the homotopy classes for which the area-minimizing surfaces in the hyperbolic metric equidistribute in the Grassmannian
Gr_{2}(M)
of
M
. Such sequences of homotopy classes are constructed in
[
26
, Proposition 6.1]
using the work of Kahn-Markovic
[
17
]
(see also
[
25
, Theorem 5.7]
). In
[
19
]
there is a description of general limiting measures.

If the sectional curvatures of
g
are less than or equal to
-1
, the same proof as in
[
9
]
implies that
E_{\overline{\nu}}(g)\geq 2

and equality holds if and only if
g
is hyperbolic.
Since in this case, by Pesin’s formula (
[
29
]
),
h_{L}(g)\geq 2
it follows from Theorem
1.2
that

{\rm sec}_{g}\leq-1
implies
{\rm vol}_{g}(M)\leq{\rm vol}_{\overline{g}}(M)
. This is known and was proven in the general
n
-dimensional case in
[
5
]
.

The previous theorem implies the following corollary for the minimal surface entropy.

Corollary 1.1
.

We have

E(g)h_{L}(g)\text{\rm vol}_{g}(M)\leq 4\text{\rm vol}_{\overline{g}}(M)

for any metric of negative sectional curvature
g
on
M

if and only if
(M,\overline{g})
has no closed totally geodesic surfaces. In this case, if equality holds then the metric
g
has constant sectional curvature.

Indeed, if
(M,\overline{g})
does not have compact totally geodesic surfaces, then
E_{\overline{\nu}}(g)=E(g)
(Proposition
5.1
) and so Theorem
1.2
applies. If
(M,\overline{g})
admits a closed totally geodesic surface we prove in Proposition
5.2
that the inequality in Corollary
1.1
fails for some perturbation of
\bar{g}
.

There are closed hyperbolic three-manifolds which do not have compact totally geodesic surfaces (
[
27
]
).

Area ratio.

In
[
14
, Section 2.4.C]
, Gromov defined

A(g/\bar{g})=\frac{1}{2\pi\,{\rm vol}_{\overline{g}}(M)}\int_{Gr_{2}(M)}\frac{{\rm area}_{g}}{{\rm area}_{\overline{g}}}(x,P)dV_{\overline{g}},

where for
(x,P)\in Gr_{2}(M)
,

\frac{{\rm area}_{g}}{{\rm area}_{\overline{g}}}(x,P)=\lim_{\delta\to 0}\frac{{\rm area}_{g}(P_{\delta})}{\delta},

with
P_{\delta}\subset M
a surface tangent to
P
at
x\in M
and
\bar{g}
-area
\delta
,
dV_{\overline{g}}
the Liouville measure.

For surfaces the analogue quantity to the area ratio was introduced by Katok
[
21
]
. In
[
26
, Corollary 1.3]
it is shown that for any metric
g
with scalar curvature greater than or equal to
-6
we have
{\rm Area}(g/\overline{g})\geq 1
and equality occurs if and only if
g=\bar{g}
.

In Theorem 1.2 of
[
26
]
it was shown
A(g/\bar{g})E(g)\geq 2
for every metric
g
and that equality implies that
g
is a multiple of
\bar{g}
. In Section
5
we show that
A(g/\bar{g})E_{\overline{\nu}}(g)\geq 2
for any metric
g
. Combining with Theorem
1.2
we deduce:

Corollary 1.2
.

Let
g
be a metric of negative sectional curvature on
M
. Then

h_{L}(g){\rm vol}_{g}(M)\leq 2A(g/\bar{g})\text{\rm vol}_{\overline{g}}(M),

and if equality holds the metric
g
is a constant multiple of
\bar{g}
.

Mostow Rigidity Theorem.
The Mostow Rigidity Theorem states that closed hyperbolic manifolds of dimension greater than or equal to three that are homotopically equivalent are isometric. There are different proofs for this theorem (e.g.
[
4
]
,
[
13
]
,
[
28
]
). In Section
6
we give a new proof for the three-dimensional case.

Our proof combines some generalizations of Theorem
1.1
for maps and minimal surface theory. We sketch the argument in the following particular case. Suppose that
N=\mathbb{H}^{3}/\Gamma_{N}
is diffeomorphic to
M=\mathbb{H}^{3}/\Gamma_{M}
by a map
E:M\rightarrow N
. We want to show that
E
is homotopic to an isometry.

The map
E
induces a homeomorphism on the boundary at infinity
\tilde{E}:S^{2}\rightarrow S^{2}
. The strategy consists to show that
\tilde{E}
maps round circles to round circles. If that is the case, a theorem of Carathéodory
[
10
]
implies the map
\tilde{E}
is a Möbius transformation and so there is a unique
I\in\text{Isom}(\mathbb{H}^{3})
so that
I_{|S^{2}}=\tilde{E}
. Then standard arguments show that
I
induces an isometry between
M
and
N
.

Set
g=E^{*}(\bar{g}_{N})
and without loss of generality assume that
\text{\rm vol}_{\overline{g}}(M)\leq\text{\rm vol}_{g}(M)
. Consider the sequence
\Pi_{i}\in S_{1/i}(M)
used in deducing (
3
) and let
S_{i}\in\Pi_{i}
denote the minimal surface with respect to
g
for which
{\rm area}_{{g}}(\Pi_{i})={\rm area}_{{g}}(S_{i})
. From the volume inequality and
h_{L}(g)=2
, we see from (
3
) that
1\leq\lim_{i\to\infty}\frac{{\rm area}_{{g}}(\Pi_{i})}{{\rm area}_{\overline{g}}(\Pi_{i})}
. We then use the Gauss-Bonnet Theorem to show that
\frac{1}{\text{area}_{g}(S_{i})}\int_{S_{i}}{|A_{S_{i}}|^{2}}dA_{g}\to 0
, where
A_{S_{i}}
denotes the second fundamental form of
S_{i}
with respect to
g
.

In summary, we know that the area minimizer in
\Pi_{i}
for the metric
\bar{g}
is becoming totally geodesic as
i\to\infty
(from
[
35
,
39
]
) and we have argued that the area minimizer in
\Pi_{i}
for the metric
E^{*}(\bar{g}_{N})
is also becoming totally geodesic as
i\to\infty
. In Section
6
we show that suffices to see that
\tilde{E}
maps round circles to round circles.

2.
Preliminaries

Let
\mathbb{H}^{3}
be the three-dimensional hyperbolic space. We use the Poincaré unit ball model
B^{3}\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
. The compactification
\overline{\mathbb{H}}^{3}
of
\mathbb{H}^{3}
becomes identified with
\overline{B}^{3}
, and the boundary at infinity
\partial_{\infty}\mathbb{H}^{3}
with
\partial\overline{B}^{3}=S^{2}
. If
X\subset\mathbb{H}^{3}
, we denote by
\overline{X}\subset\overline{\mathbb{H}}^{3}
its closure in the cone topology (the Euclidean topology on
\overline{B}^{3}
) and set

\partial_{\infty}X=\overline{X}\cap\partial_{\infty}\mathbb{H}^{3}=\overline{X}\cap S^{2}.
The group
\text{Isom}_{+}(\mathbb{H}^{3})
of orientation-preserving isometries of the three-dimensional hyperbolic space can be identified by restriction to the boundary with the group of orientation-preserving conformal automorphisms of
S^{2}
, denoted by
\text{M\"{o}b}(S^{2})
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be
(S^{2}\times S^{2}-\{(x,x):x\in S^{2}\})/\sim
, where
(x,y)\sim(y,x)
, with the quotient topology. The group
\text{M\"{o}b}(S^{2})
acts diagonally on
\mathcal{G}
. We denote by
G(\mathbb{H}^{3})
the space of unoriented complete geodesics in
\mathbb{H}^{3}
with the compact-open topology. If
\gamma\in G(\mathbb{H}^{3})
, then
\partial_{\infty}\gamma
consists of two points of
S^{2}
.
The map
\partial_{\infty}:G(\mathbb{H}^{3})\rightarrow\mathcal{G}
is a homeomorphism.

Consider
\Gamma\subset\text{Isom}_{+}(\mathbb{H}^{3})
a discrete group of isometries such that
M=\mathbb{H}^{3}/\Gamma
is a closed manifold. Let
\pi:\mathbb{H}^{3}\rightarrow\mathbb{H}^{3}/\Gamma
be the canonical projection.
A Riemannian metric
g
on
M
can be pulled back to a metric on
\mathbb{H}^{3}
denoted also by
g
. We will assume that
g
has negative sectional curvature and we denote by
\overline{g}
the hyperbolic metric.

A geodesic
\gamma
in the metric
g
in
\mathbb{H}^{3}
defines a unique hyperbolic geodesic
\tilde{\gamma}
such that the Hausdorff distance between
\gamma
and
\tilde{\gamma}
is bounded. The geodesic
\gamma
is determined by
\partial_{\infty}\gamma
. Hence the boundary at infinity of
(\mathbb{H}^{3},g)
is canonically identified with
\partial_{\infty}{\mathbb{H}^{3}}=S^{2}
. With
G(\mathbb{H}^{3},g)
denoting the set of all unoriented complete geodesics in
(\mathbb{H}^{3},g)
with the compact-open topology, the map
\partial_{\infty}:G(\mathbb{H}^{3},g)\rightarrow\mathcal{G}
is a homeomorphism and we denote the inverse map by
u\in\mathcal{G}\mapsto\gamma_{g}(u)\in G(\mathbb{H}^{3},g)
.

The conjugacy class of
\alpha\in\Gamma
and the free homotopy class of of a curve
\sigma
are denoted by
[\alpha]
and
[\sigma]
respectively. These classes are in one-to-one correspondence with each other. Every homotopy class
[\sigma]
contains a unique closed geodesic
\gamma
with respect to
g
which is also the curve of least length in
[\sigma]
. We use
l_{g}([\gamma])
or
l_{g}(\gamma)
denote the length of
\gamma
with respect to
g
.

Let
T^{1}_{g}\mathbb{H}^{3}
and
T^{1}_{g}M
denote the unit tangent bundles of
(\mathbb{H}^{3},g)
and
(M,g)
. The Liouville measure on either
T^{1}_{g}\mathbb{H}^{3}
or
T^{1}_{g}M
is denoted by
dV_{g}
and
dV_{g}(T^{1}_{g}M)=4\pi\text{\rm vol}_{g}(M)
. If we do not specify the Riemannian metric, it is because we are referring to the hyperbolic metric
\overline{g}
on either
M
or
\mathbb{H}^{3}
.

2.1.
Quasi-circles

A simple closed curve
\sigma\subset S^{2}
is called a
(1+\eta)
-quasi-circle
if there is a
(1+\eta)
-quasi-conformal homeomorphism
f:S^{2}\rightarrow S^{2}

such that
f(\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|=1\})=\sigma
. Let
{QC}_{\eta}
be the space of
(1+\eta)
-quasi-circles of
S^{2}
with the topology induced by the Hausdorff distance
d_{\mathcal{H},S^{2}}
. Hence
{QC}_{0}
is the space of geodesic circles in
S^{2}
, which is homeomorphic to a punctured
\mathbb{RP}^{3}
.
The group
\text{M\"{o}b}(S^{2})
acts continuously on
{QC}_{\eta}
by taking the image
\sigma\mapsto\phi(\sigma)
,
\phi\in\text{M\"{o}b}(S^{2})
.

If a sequence of
(1+\eta)
-quasi-circles
\{\sigma_{n}\}
is such that for different points
x,y,z\in S^{2}
, there exists
\{x_{n},y_{n},z_{n}\}\subset\sigma_{n}
with
x_{n}\rightarrow x
,
y_{n}\rightarrow y
,
z_{n}\rightarrow z
, then there is a subsequence
\{\sigma_{j}\}\subset\{\sigma_{n}\}
that converges to a
(1+\eta)
-quasi-circle
\sigma
containing
x,y,z
. Hence, for any
r>0
the set
K_{r}\subset{QC}_{\eta}
of quasi-circles which are not contained in some geodesic ball
B_{r}(x)\subset S^{2}
is compact. The space
QC_{\eta}
is then locally compact and Hausdorff. If
\eta<\eta^{\prime}
, then
QC_{\eta}
is a closed subset of
QC_{\eta^{\prime}}
. Also
QC_{\eta}
is a
\sigma
-compact topological space, since
QC_{\eta}=\cup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}K_{1/n}
.

2.2.
Convex hull

A set
C\subset\overline{\mathbb{H}}^{3}
is convex if the geodesic connecting any pair of points in
C
is contained in
C
. For a closed set
X\subset\partial_{\infty}\mathbb{H}^{3}
, its
convex hull

\text{conv}(X)\subset\overline{\mathbb{H}}^{3}
is the intersection of all convex subsets of
\overline{\mathbb{H}}^{3}
which contain
X
. If
\sigma\in QC_{0}
, then
\text{conv}(\sigma)
is a totally geodesic disk
D(\sigma)
with
\partial_{\infty}D(\sigma)=\sigma
.

The convex hull satisfies some continuity properties. Consider a sequence
(\sigma_{i},\partial_{\infty}\gamma_{i})
in
QC_{\eta}\times\mathcal{G}
converging to
(\sigma,\partial_{\infty}\gamma)
, where
\gamma_{i},\gamma\in G(\mathbb{H}^{3})
,
i\in\mathbb{N}
. Then
[
7
, Theorem 1.4]
implies that
\text{conv}(\sigma_{i})
and
\bar{\gamma}_{i}
converge, respectively, in the Hausdorff distance of the Euclidean metric on
\overline{B}^{3}
, to
\text{conv}(\sigma)
and
\bar{\gamma}
.

2.3.
Integral geometry

The Grassmannian of unoriented
2
-planes in
\mathbb{H}^{3}
is denoted by
Gr_{2}(\mathbb{H}^{3})
. Given
(x,P)\in Gr_{2}(\mathbb{H}^{3})
, there is a unique totally geodesic disk
D(x,P)\subset\mathbb{H}^{3}
with
x\in D(x,P)
and
T_{x}D(x,P)=P
. Thus we get a continuous map
\theta:Gr_{2}(\mathbb{H}^{3})\rightarrow QC_{0}
defined by

(4)

\theta(x,P)=\partial_{\infty}D(x,P).

Let
dV
be the natural volume measure on
Gr_{2}(\mathbb{H}^{3})
. Santaló’s formula (see Chapter 14 of
[
31
]
) gives a Radon measure
\nu
on
QC_{0}
that is invariant under
\text{M\"{o}b}(S^{2})
, and such that for every region
\Omega\subset\mathbb{H}^{3}
, every surface
S\subset\mathbb{H}^{3}
, and every curve
c\subset\mathbb{H}^{3}
we have, respectively,

(5)

\int_{QC_{0}}\text{area}(D(\sigma)\cap\Omega)\,d\nu(\sigma)=2\pi\,\text{\rm vol}(\Omega),

(6)

\int_{QC_{0}}\text{length}(D(\sigma)\cap S)\,d\nu(\sigma)=\frac{\pi^{2}}{2}\,\text{area}(S),

and

(7)

\int_{QC_{0}}\#(D(\sigma)\cap c)\,d\nu(\sigma)=\pi\,\text{length}(c),

If
\psi\in C_{c}(Gr_{2}(\mathbb{H}^{3}))
, then

(8)

\int_{Gr_{2}(\mathbb{H}^{3})}\psi\,dV=\int_{QC_{0}}\int_{D(\sigma)}\psi(x,T_{x}D(\sigma))\,dA(x)\,d\nu(\sigma).

The measure
dV
also induces a volume measure
dV
on
Gr_{2}(M)
, the Grassmanian of unoriented
2
-planes of
M
, whose total volume is
2\pi\text{\rm vol}(M)
.

For a negatively curved metric
g
on
M
, we consider the maps

(9)

p_{1}:T^{1}_{g}(\mathbb{H}^{3})\rightarrow\mathbb{H}^{3},\quad p_{1}(x,v)=x

and

(10)

p_{2}:T^{1}_{g}(\mathbb{H}^{3})\rightarrow\mathcal{G},\quad p_{2}(x,v)=\partial_{\infty}\gamma_{g}(x,v),

where
\gamma_{g}(x,v)
is the unique geodesic in
(\mathbb{H}^{3},g)
determined by
(x,v)\in T^{1}_{g}(\mathbb{H}^{3})
. For each
x\in\mathbb{H}^{3}
, the metric
g
induces a natural area measure
\omega_{x}
on the fiber
p_{1}^{-1}(x)
with area
4\pi
.

In
[
31
, IV.19.4]
, a measure
\lambda_{g}
on
\mathcal{G}
(called the
Liouville measure on
\mathcal{G}
) is defined so that for every surface
\Sigma\subset\mathbb{H}^{3}
and every nonnegative measurable function
F
in
\mathcal{G}
we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{G}}\#(\gamma_{g}(u)\cap\Sigma)F(u)d\lambda_{g}(u)

(11)

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Sigma}\int_{p_{1}^{-1}(x)}F\circ p_{2}(x,v)|\langle v,N_{\Sigma}(x)\rangle|d\omega_{x}(v)dA_{g}(x),

where
N_{\Sigma}
is a unit normal to
\Sigma
for the metric
g
and
\gamma_{g}(u)\in G(\mathbb{H}^{3},g)
is the unique geodesic such that
\partial_{\infty}\gamma_{g}(u)=u
. In particular,

(12)

\int_{\mathcal{G}}\#(\gamma_{g}(u)\cap\Sigma)d\lambda_{g}(u)=\pi\text{area}_{g}(\Sigma).

Also, if
\psi\in C_{c}(Gr_{1}(\mathbb{H}^{3}))
then

(13)

\int_{Gr_{1}(\mathbb{H}^{3})}\psi\,dV_{g}=\int_{\mathcal{G}}\int_{\gamma_{g}(\sigma)}\psi(x,T_{x}\gamma_{g}(\sigma))\,ds_{g}(x)\,d\lambda_{g}(\sigma),

where
ds_{g}
is arc length in the metric
g
and
dV_{g}
is the natural volume measure on
Gr_{1}(\mathbb{H}^{3})
induced by
g
.

2.4.
Geodesic stretch

Let
(N,g_{1})
,
(M,g_{2})
be compact manifolds with negative sectional curvature and
F:N\rightarrow M
be a continuous map. We denote by
\pi(v)
the basepoint of a vector
v
. The geodesic flow with respect to the metric
g_{1}
is denoted by

g_{1}^{t}:T^{1}_{g_{1}}(N)\rightarrow T^{1}_{g_{1}}(N)
, or
g_{1}^{t}:T^{1}_{g_{1}}(\mathbb{H}^{3})\rightarrow T^{1}_{g_{1}}(\mathbb{H}^{3})
,
t\in\mathbb{R}
.
For
v\in T^{1}_{g_{1}}(N)
, denote by
a(t,v)
the minimum length in the
g_{2}
-metric of paths in
M
homotopic with fixed endpoints to

s\mapsto F(\pi(g_{1}^{s}(v))),\quad 0\leq s\leq t.

If
\gamma
is a unit speed geodesic in
(\mathbb{H}^{3},g_{1})
so that
(\gamma(0),\gamma^{\prime}(0))
is mapped to
v\in T^{1}_{g_{1}}(N)
, and if
F:\mathbb{H}^{3}\rightarrow\mathbb{H}^{3}
denotes a lift of
F:N\rightarrow M
,
then
a(v,t)=\text{d}_{g_{2}}(F(\gamma(0)),F(\gamma(t)))
.

In
[
11
]
, Croke and Fathi defined the
geodesic stretch
of
g_{2}
with respect to
g_{1}
under the map
F
as

(14)

i_{F}(g_{1},g_{2})=\frac{1}{\text{\rm vol}_{g_{1}}(T^{1}_{g_{1}}N)}\lim_{t\to\infty}\int_{T^{1}_{g_{1}}(N)}\frac{a(t,v)}{t}dV_{g_{1}}(v).

If
F
is the identity map, we denote
i_{id}(g_{1},g_{2})
by
i(g_{1},g_{2})
. The geodesic stretch is referred to as the intersection of
g_{1},g_{2}
in
[
11
]
.

2.5.
Essential surfaces

A
surface group
is a subgroup
G<\Gamma
such that there is a closed surface
\Sigma
and an immersion
i:\Sigma\rightarrow M
that is
\pi_{1}
-injective with
i_{*}(\pi_{1}(\Sigma))=G
. Such immersions are called
essential surfaces
. We denote by
\Pi
the conjugacy class of
G
in
\Gamma
. The set of conjugacy classes of surface groups is in one-to-one correspondence with the set of free homotopy classes of essential surfaces.
We denote
\Sigma\in\Pi
if the associated immersion
i:\Sigma\rightarrow M
satisfies
i_{*}(\pi_{1}(\Sigma))\in\Pi
. Hence we refer to
\Pi
as a
homotopy class of essential surfaces.

The immersion
i:\Sigma\rightarrow M
lifts to a
G
-invariant immersion
\tilde{i}:D\rightarrow\mathbb{H}^{3}
, where
D
is the universal cover of
\Sigma
. Hence

\tilde{i}(\phi(x))=i_{*}(\phi)(\tilde{i}(x))
for every
x\in D
and
\phi\in\pi_{1}(\Sigma)
.
We will identify
\Sigma
and
D
with their images in
M
and
\mathbb{H}^{3}
under the immersions
i
and
\tilde{i}
, respectively, depending on the context.
If
\Lambda(G)\subset S^{2}
denotes the limit set of
G
, defined as the set of accumulation points in
\overline{\mathbb{H}}^{3}
of an orbit
Gx
, for
x\in\mathbb{H}^{3}
, then
\partial_{\infty}D=\Lambda(G)
. The group
G
preserves
\Lambda(G)
, and if
H\in\Pi
is conjugate to
G
there is
\phi\in\Gamma
so that
\phi(\Lambda(H))=\Lambda(G)
. The group
G
is called quasi-Fuchsian if the limit set
\Lambda(G)
is a Jordan curve, in which case it is a quasi-circle (see
[
38
, Prop 8.7.2]
). We define
\mathcal{S}_{\eta}(M)
to be the set of all conjugacy classes of quasi-Fuchsian surface groups with limit set in
QC_{\eta}
.

If
g
is a Riemannian metric on
M
, we denote the area of
\Sigma\in\Pi
with respect to
i^{*}g
by
\text{area}_{g}(\Sigma)
. For a homotopy class
\Pi
, we define

(15)

\text{area}_{g}(\Pi)={\text{inf}}\{\text{area}_{g}(\Sigma):\Sigma\in\Pi\}.

There exists an immersed minimal surface (with respect to
g
)
\tilde{\Sigma}\in\Pi
with

\text{area}_{g}(\tilde{\Sigma})=\text{area}_{g}(\Pi)
(e.g.
[
34
]
). We set
\text{area}(\Pi)=\text{area}_{\overline{g}}(\Pi)
.

Each
\sigma\in QC_{0}
has a totally geodesic disk as the unique solution to the asymptotic Plateau problem. A standard compactness argument shows the existence of
\tilde{\eta}>0
such that for any
\sigma\in QC_{\tilde{\eta}}
the area minimizing surface
D\subset\mathbb{H}^{3}
with
\partial_{\infty}D=\sigma
produced in
[
2
, Theorem 2.1]
has principal curvatures bounded from above by a constant
\lambda<1
. Uhlenbeck
[
39
]
uniqueness argument can be applied in this setting (
[
16
, Lemma 2.2]
) to show that
D
is a disk, unique, and we set
D(\sigma)=D
(see also
[
15
]
for other properties). Hence any
\Pi\in\mathcal{S}_{\tilde{\eta}}(M)
has a unique minimal surface
\Sigma\in\Pi
which we denote by
\Sigma(\Pi)
(
[
39
]
).

2.6.
Minimal surface entropy

In
[
9
]
, Calegari and the authors defined the
minimal surface entropy

E(g)
of
(M,g)
. This is the invariant

(16)

E(g)=\lim_{\eta\to 0}\liminf_{L\to\infty}4\pi\frac{\ln\#\{\Pi\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\eta}(M):\text{area}_{g}(\Pi)\leq L\}}{L\ln L}.

Let

(17)

A(g,\eta)=\inf\{\text{area}_{g}(\Pi)/\text{area}(\Pi):\Pi\in\mathcal{S}_{\eta}(M)\},

and

A(g)=\lim_{\eta\to 0}A(g,\eta).

Proposition 2.1
.

The minimal surface entropy satisfies

\displaystyle E(g)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{\eta\to 0}\limsup_{L\to\infty}4\pi\frac{\ln\#\{\Pi\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\eta}(M):\text{\rm area}_{g}(\Pi)\leq L\}}{L\ln L}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2}{A(g)}.

Proof.

Let
\Pi\in S_{\eta}(M)
. If
\Sigma\in\Pi
is a minimal surface in the hyperbolic metric
\overline{g}
, then by the Gauss-Bonnet theorem

\text{area}(\Sigma)=4\pi(g(\Pi)-1)-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Sigma}|A_{\Sigma}|^{2}dA_{\overline{g}},

where
g(\Pi)
is the genus of
\Sigma
and
A_{\Sigma}
is its second fundamental form. By
[
35
]
,
|A_{\Sigma}|\leq C\ln(1+\eta)
. Hence

\lim_{\eta\rightarrow 0}\frac{\text{area}(\Pi)}{4\pi(g(\Pi)-1)}=1.

If
\text{area}_{g}(\Pi)\leq L
, then we have from (
17
) that
\text{area}(\Pi)A(g,\eta)\leq L
. Hence, for any
\alpha>0
, there exists
\eta^{\prime}>0
such that if
0<\eta<\eta^{\prime}
then

4\pi(g(\Pi)-1)\leq(1+\alpha)\text{area}(\Pi)\leq(1+\alpha)A(g,\eta)^{-1}L.

In particular,
g(\Pi)\leq h=(4\pi)^{-1}(1+\alpha)A(g,\eta)^{-1}L+1
. Therefore

\displaystyle\#\{\Pi\in\mathcal{S}_{\eta}(M):\text{area}_{g}(\Pi)\leq L\}\leq\#\{\Pi\in\mathcal{S}_{\eta}(M):g(\Pi)\leq h\}\leq(c_{2}h)^{2h},

where we are using the work of Kahn and Markovic
[
18
]
in the last inequality. Since

4\pi\frac{\ln\#\{\Pi\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\eta}(M):\text{area}_{g}(\Pi)\leq L\}}{L\ln L}\leq 4\pi\frac{2h\ln(h)+2h\ln c_{2}}{L\ln L}\\
\leq 4\pi\frac{2(L^{-1}h)L\ln(L)+2(L^{-1}h)\ln(L^{-1}h)L+(2L^{-1}h\ln c_{2})L}{L\ln L},

it follows that

\limsup_{L\to\infty}4\pi\frac{\ln\#\{\Pi\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\eta}(M):\text{area}_{g}(\Pi)\leq L\}}{L\ln L}\leq 8\pi(4\pi)^{-1}(1+\alpha)A(g,\eta)^{-1}.

Therefore, since
\alpha>0
is arbitrary,

(18)

\lim_{\eta\to 0}\limsup_{L\to\infty}4\pi\frac{\ln\#\{\Pi\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\eta}(M):\text{area}_{g}(\Pi)\leq L\}}{L\ln L}\leq\frac{2}{A(g)}.

Let
\Pi_{i}\in\mathcal{S}_{1/i}(M)
be a sequence so that

A(g)=\lim_{i\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\text{area}_{g}(\Pi_{i})}{\text{area}(\Pi_{i})}.

Denote by
C(i,k)
the set of all index
k
subgroups of
\Pi_{i}
. The elements of
C(i,k)
correspond to surface subgroups obtained by taking a
k
-degree cover of an essential surface in
\Pi_{i}
. The essential surfaces corresponding to an element of
C(i,k)
have genus
g(i,k)=k(g(\Pi_{i})-1)+1
.

If
\Lambda\in C(i,k)
, then
\Lambda\in\mathcal{S}_{1/i}(M)
and
\text{area}_{g}(\Lambda)\leq k\,\text{area}_{g}(\Pi_{i})
. Hence

\#\{\Pi\in\mathcal{S}_{1/i}(M):\text{area}_{g}(\Pi)\leq k\,\text{area}_{g}(\Pi_{i})\}\geq\#C(i,k).

We use the Müller-Puchta’s formula as in the proof of
[
9
, Theorem 4.2]
to deduce the existence of a constant
c_{i}>0
so that
\#C(i,k)\geq(c_{i}g(i,k))^{2g(i,k)}
.

If
L\in[k\,\text{area}_{g}(\Pi_{i}),(k+1)\,\text{area}_{g}(\Pi_{i}))
, then

\displaystyle\frac{\ln\#\{\Pi\in{\mathcal{S}}_{1/i}(M):\text{area}_{g}(\Pi)\leq L\}}{L\ln L}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\ln\#\{\Pi\in{\mathcal{S}}_{1/i}(M):\text{area}_{g}(\Pi)\leq k\,\text{area}_{g}(\Pi_{i})\}}{(k+1)\,\text{area}_{g}(\Pi_{i})\ln((k+1)\,\text{area}_{g}(\Pi_{i}))}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\ln\#C(i,k)}{(k+1)\,\text{area}_{g}(\Pi_{i})\ln((k+1)\,\text{area}_{g}(\Pi_{i}))}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{2g(i,k)\ln(c_{i}g(i,k))}{(k+1)\,\text{area}_{g}(\Pi_{i})\ln((k+1)\,\text{area}_{g}(\Pi_{i}))}

\displaystyle=\frac{\{2k(g(\Pi_{i})-1)+2\}(\ln(k(g(\Pi_{i})-1)+1)+\ln(c_{i}))}{(k+1)\,\text{area}_{g}(\Pi_{i})(\ln(k+1)+\ln\text{area}_{g}(\Pi_{i}))}.

This implies

\displaystyle 4\pi\liminf_{L\to\infty}\frac{\ln\#\{\Pi\in{\mathcal{S}}_{1/i}(M):\text{area}_{g}(\Pi)\leq L\}}{L\ln L}\geq 4\pi\frac{2(g(\Pi_{i})-1)}{\text{area}_{g}(\Pi_{i})}.

Therefore, letting
i\rightarrow\infty
,

(19)

\lim_{\eta\to 0}\liminf_{L\to\infty}4\pi\frac{\ln\#\{\Pi\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\eta}(M):\text{area}_{g}(\Pi)\leq L\}}{L\ln L}\geq\frac{2}{A(g)}.

The proposition follows by combining (
18
) and (
19
).

∎

If
\Lambda
is a subgroup of
\Pi
of finite index, with
\Pi\in S_{\eta}(M)
,
\eta<\tilde{\eta}
, where
\tilde{\eta}
is as in the introduction, by uniqueness
\Sigma(\Lambda)
is a degree
k
cover of
\Sigma(\Pi)
and hence

\nu_{\Lambda}=\nu_{\Pi}
. This allows to generalize Proposition
2.1
to the entropies
E_{\mu}(g)
with the same proof. It follows that

(20)

E_{\mu}(g)=\lim_{\eta\to 0}\limsup_{L\to\infty}4\pi\frac{\ln\#\{\Pi\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\eta,\mu}(M):\text{\rm area}_{g}(\Pi)\leq L\}}{L\ln L}=\frac{2}{A_{\mu}(g)},

where
A_{\mu}(g)=\lim_{\eta\to 0}\inf\{\text{area}_{g}(\Pi)/\text{area}(\Pi):\Pi\in\mathcal{S}_{\eta,\mu}(M)\}
. As a result,
E_{\mu}(\overline{g})=2
.

3.
Conformal currents and the intersection form

Recall that
\Gamma\subset\text{Isom}_{+}(\mathbb{H}^{3})
is a discrete group of isometries such that
M=\mathbb{H}^{3}/\Gamma
is a closed manifold.
In
[
6
]
, Bonahon defined a
geodesic current
to be a
\Gamma
-invariant Radon measure on
\mathcal{G}
. The space of geodesic currents is denoted by
\mathcal{C}(\Gamma)
. Labourie
[
25
, Section 5.2]
defined a
conformal current
to be a
\Gamma
-invariant Radon measure on some
QC_{\eta}
. Let us denote the space of conformal currents by
{\mathcal{C}_{2}}(\Gamma)

(see
[
20
]
for a definition based on closed sets).

The results of this section overlap with those of Brody and Reyes
[
8
]
, which introduced the notion of currents on systems of spheres generalizing conformal currents. The statements of this section and their proofs were developed independently.

3.1.
Intersection number

In
[
6
]
, Bonahon defined the intersection number between geodesic currents. We will use an intersection number between geodesic currents and conformal currents.

Given
(\sigma,\{x,y\})\in QC_{\eta}\times\mathcal{G}
, its (unoriented) linking number
\text{lk}(\sigma,\{x,y\})
is
1
if
x,y
belong to separate connected components of
S^{2}-\sigma
and
0
otherwise. In particular, if
\text{lk}(\sigma,\{x,y\})=1
then
\sigma\cap\{x,y\}=\emptyset
.

Consider the open set

(21)

X=\{(\sigma,u)\in QC_{\eta}\times\mathcal{G}:\text{lk}(\sigma,u)=1\}

in
QC_{\eta}\times\mathcal{G}
.
The space
X
is a locally compact Hausdorff space. It is also
\sigma
-compact, since the set

\{(\sigma,\{x,y\})\in X:\sigma\in K_{r},d_{S^{2}}(x,y)\geq r,d_{S^{2}}(x,\sigma)\geq r,d_{S^{2}}(y,\sigma)\geq r\}

is compact, where
K_{r}
is as in Section
2.1
.

The group
\Gamma
acts on
X
diagonally. In what follows we refer to the notation introduced in Section
2.2
. For every
(\sigma,\partial_{\infty}\gamma)\in X
, with
\gamma\in G(\mathbb{H}^{3})
, the compact set
\text{conv}(\sigma)\cap\gamma
is non-empty. This is because in
[
2
, Theorem 4.1]
it is produced a minimal disk
D\subset\mathbb{H}^{3}
with
\partial_{\infty}D=\sigma
, which intersects
\gamma
, and is contained in
\text{conv}(\sigma)
.

Lemma 3.1
.

The group
\Gamma
acts freely and properly discontinuously on
X
. Hence
X/\Gamma
is a locally compact Hausdorff space.

Proof.

Suppose
\phi\in\Gamma
and
(\sigma,\{x,y\})\in X
are such that
\phi(\sigma,\{x,y\})=(\sigma,\{x,y\})
, with
\phi\neq{\rm id}
. Then
\phi
leaves
\{x,y\}
invariant. If
\phi(x)=y
then
\phi
sends the geodesic connecting
x
and
y
to itself with the reverse orientation. This implies that
\phi
has a fixed point in
\mathbb{H}^{3}
which is a contradiction. Hence
\phi(x)=x
and
\phi(y)=y
. Since
\sigma
does not intersect
\{x,y\}
, it follows that
\phi^{n}(\sigma)
converges to either
x
or
y
as
n\to\infty
. This is impossible because
\phi
fixes
\sigma
. This proves
\Gamma
acts freely.

To show that the action is properly discontinuous, we prove that for every compact set
\mathcal{K}\subset X
, the set
\{\phi\in\Gamma:\phi(\mathcal{K})\cap\mathcal{K}\neq\emptyset\}
is finite.
Define
K=\cup_{\{(\sigma,\partial_{\infty}\gamma)\in\mathcal{K}\}}\{\gamma\cap\text{conv}(\sigma)\}
.

Claim:

K\subset\mathbb{H}^{3}
is compact.

Let
\{x_{i}\}\subset K
be a sequence, with
x_{i}\in\gamma_{i}\cap\text{conv}(\sigma_{i})
for some
(\sigma_{i},\partial_{\infty}\gamma_{i})\in\mathcal{K}
. By compactness there is
\{x_{j}\}\subset\{x_{i}\}
with
(\sigma_{j},\partial_{\infty}\gamma_{j})\to(\sigma,\partial_{\infty}\gamma)\in\mathcal{K}
. Theorem 1.4 in
[
7
]
implies that
\text{conv}(\sigma_{j})\rightarrow{\rm conv}(\sigma)
and

\overline{\gamma}_{j}\rightarrow\overline{\gamma}
, both in the Hausdorff topology for the Euclidean metric. Hence
there exists
\{x_{k}\}\subset\{x_{j}\}
that converges in the Euclidean metric to a point
p\in\overline{\gamma}\cap\text{conv}(\sigma)
.
Since
(\sigma,\partial_{\infty}\gamma)\in\mathcal{K}
, the set
\overline{\gamma}\cap\text{conv}(\sigma)
is compact in

\mathbb{H}^{3}
. Hence
x_{k}\rightarrow x\in K
in the hyperbolic metric. This proves the claim.

If
\phi\in\Gamma
and
(\sigma,\partial_{\infty}\gamma)\in\mathcal{K}
are such that

(\phi(\sigma),\phi(\partial_{\infty}\gamma))\in\mathcal{K}
, then
\phi(K)\cap K\neq\emptyset
. This is because
\phi({\rm conv}(Y))={\rm conv}(\phi(Y))
for any
Y
. Since the group
\Gamma
acts
properly discontinuously on
\mathbb{H}^{3}
, it follows that the set
\{\phi\in\Gamma:\phi(K)\cap K\neq\emptyset\}
is finite which finishes the proof.

∎

Let
P:X\rightarrow X/\Gamma
denote the quotient map. Given
f\in C_{c}(X)
, set

f^{\Gamma}(x)=\sum_{\phi\in\Gamma}f(\phi(x))
. The function
f^{\Gamma}
is well defined due to Lemma
3.1
. Since
f^{\Gamma}
is
\Gamma
-invariant, there is
\tilde{f}\in C_{c}(X/\Gamma)
so that
f^{\Gamma}=\tilde{f}\circ P
.

For
(\mu,\lambda)\in{\mathcal{C}}_{2}(\Gamma)\times\mathcal{C}(\Gamma)
, the
\Gamma
-invariant measure
\mu\times\lambda
induces a unique Radon measure
[\mu\times\lambda]
on the quotient space
X/\Gamma
so that

[\mu\times\lambda](\tilde{f})=(\mu\times\lambda)(f),\quad f\in C_{c}(X).

Lemma 3.2
.

If
(\mu,\lambda)\in{\mathcal{C}}_{2}(\Gamma)\times\mathcal{C}(\Gamma)
, then
[\mu\times\lambda](X/\Gamma)<\infty
.

Proof.

For each compact set
K\subset X
, it holds that

[\mu\times\lambda](P(K))\leq(\mu\times\lambda)(K)
. This is because

(\mu\times\lambda)(K)=\inf_{f\in C_{K}}(\mu\times\lambda)(f),

where
C_{K}
is the set of functions
f\in C_{c}(X)
satisfying
0\leq f\leq 1
on
X
and
f=1
on
K
. We are using that
\tilde{f}=1
on
P(K)
for each
f\in C_{K}
.

Let
\Delta\subset\mathbb{H}^{3}
be a Dirichlet fundamental domain of
M
with closure
\bar{\Delta}
and set

(22)

Y=\{(\sigma,\partial_{\infty}\gamma)\in QC_{\eta}\times\mathcal{G}:\text{conv}(\sigma)\cap\gamma\cap\bar{\Delta}\neq\emptyset\}.

It follows from Sections
2.1
and
2.2
that
Y
is compact.
For every
z\in X
, there is
\phi\in\Gamma
so that
\phi(z)\in Y
. Thus
P(Y\cap X)=X/\Gamma
.

We write
X=\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}K_{n}
, where
K_{n}\subset K_{n+1}
and each
K_{n}
is a compact set. Hence

Y\cap K_{n}\subset X
is a compact set. Therefore

[\mu\times\lambda](P(Y\cap K_{n}))\leq(\mu\times\lambda)(Y\cap K_{n})\leq(\mu\times\lambda)(Y)<\infty.

Since
X/\Gamma=P(Y\cap X)=\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}P(Y\cap K_{n})
, we get that
[\mu\times\lambda](X/\Gamma)\leq(\mu\times\lambda)(Y)
. This finishes the proof of the lemma.
∎

The
intersection form
between conformal currents and geodesic currents is the bilinear map

I=I_{\Gamma}:{\mathcal{C}}_{2}(\Gamma)\times\mathcal{C}(\Gamma)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}

defined by

I(\mu,\lambda)=[\mu\times\lambda](X/\Gamma).

3.2.
Examples of conformal currents and geodesic currents

Consider a homotopy class of quasi-Fuchsian surfaces
\Pi
and
G
a surface group in
\Pi
. Let
\Gamma/G=\{\phi G:\phi\in\Gamma\}
be the set of cosets and choose a representative
\tilde{\phi}
in each coset
\phi G
. Choose
\eta>0
so that
\Lambda(G)\in QC_{\eta}
. We consider the
\Gamma
-invariant measure (see
[
25
, Proposition 5.5.(ii)]
)

\delta_{\Pi}=\sum_{\tilde{\phi}}\delta_{\tilde{\phi}(\Lambda(G))}.

That
\delta_{\Pi}
is a locally finite measure was proven in
[
25
]
under the condition that
\eta<\eta_{1}
for some
\eta_{1}>0
. To see that this holds for any
\eta
, and thus that
\delta_{\Pi}\in{\mathcal{C}}_{2}(\Gamma)
, we proceed as follows.

Let
\delta_{\Lambda(G)}
be the Dirac delta supported on the set
\{{\phi(\Lambda(G))}:\phi\in\Gamma\}
and
H=\{\phi\in\Gamma:\phi(\Lambda(G))=\Lambda(G)\}
, so
G<H
. Since the quotient of
{\rm conv}(\Lambda(G))
by the action
of
G
is compact, and
H
preserves
{\rm conv}(\Lambda(G))
, it follows that
|H:G|<\infty
. Then
\delta_{\Pi}=|H:G|\cdot\delta_{\Lambda(G)}
. Suppose by contradiction that
\delta_{\Lambda(G)}
is not locally finite.
Then one can find
\{\phi_{i}\}_{i}\subset\Gamma
so that
\phi_{i}(\Lambda(G))\rightarrow C
in the Hausdorff distance with
\phi_{i}(\Lambda(G))\neq\phi_{j}(\Lambda(G))
for every
i\neq j
. Let
p,q
be in different connected components of
S^{2}\setminus C
, and
\gamma
be the hyperbolic geodesic connecting
p
and
q
. Since we can find a converging sequence
\{f_{i}\}_{i}
of
(1+\eta)
-quasiconformal homeomorphisms of
S^{2}
with

In this section we prove:

Theorem 6.1

(Mostow Rigidity)
.

Consider two closed hyperbolic manifolds
M=\mathbb{H}^{3}/\Gamma_{M}
and
N=\mathbb{H}^{3}/\Gamma_{N}
for which there is a homotopy equivalence
F:N\rightarrow M
. Then
F
is homotopic to an isometry
G:N\rightarrow M
.

Let
E:M\rightarrow N
denote the homotopic inverse of
F
. The lifts
F:\mathbb{H}^{3}\rightarrow\mathbb{H}^{3}
,
E:\mathbb{H}^{3}\rightarrow\mathbb{H}^{3}
can be assumed to be
C^{1}
and both maps are quasi-isometries (see
[
3
, Chapter C]
for instance). They induce homeomorphisms on the boundary at infinity
\tilde{F}:S^{2}\rightarrow S^{2}
,
\tilde{E}:S^{2}\rightarrow S^{2}
, with
\tilde{E}\circ\tilde{F}=\tilde{F}\circ\tilde{E}=id_{S^{2}}.
The maps
\tilde{E},\tilde{F}
are quasi-conformal and thus map quasi-circles to quasi-circles. The maps
F_{*}:\Gamma_{N}\rightarrow\Gamma_{M}
and
E_{*}:\Gamma_{M}\rightarrow\Gamma_{N}
are inverses of each other. For
\phi\in\Gamma_{N}
, we have
F_{*}(\phi)\circ F=F\circ\phi
and
F_{*}(\phi)\circ\tilde{F}=\tilde{F}\circ\phi
. For
\psi\in\Gamma_{M}
,
E_{*}(\psi)\circ E=E\circ\psi
and
E_{*}(\psi)\circ\tilde{E}=\tilde{E}\circ\psi
.

If
\Pi<\Gamma_{M}
is a homotopy class of essential surfaces and
G\in\Pi
, then
E_{*}(G)
is a surface group of
\Gamma_{N}
whose conjugacy class coincides with
E_{*}(\Pi)=\{E_{*}(G):G\in\Pi\}
. If
\Pi
is quasi-Fuchsian, then
E_{*}(\Pi)
is also quasi-Fuchsian since
\tilde{E}
is quasi-conformal. The map
\tilde{F}
induces a homeomorphism on the space of all geodesics of
\mathbb{H}^{3}
. Hence if
\lambda\in\mathcal{C}(\Gamma_{N})
is a geodesic current, then
(\tilde{F})_{*}\lambda\in\mathcal{C}(\Gamma_{M})
. Notice that
if
\Pi_{N}<\Gamma_{N}
is a homotopy class of quasi-Fuchsian surfaces, then
(\tilde{F})_{*}\delta_{\Pi_{N}}=\delta_{F_{*}(\Pi_{N})}.

We claim that the proof of Theorem
1.1
(i) can be extended to show that for any negatively curved metric
g
on
N
and every quasi-Fuchsian homotopy class
\Pi<\Gamma_{M}
, we have

(33)

I_{\Gamma_{M}}(\delta_{\Pi},(\tilde{F})_{*}\lambda_{g})\leq\pi\text{area}_{g}(E_{*}(\Pi)).

Consider
\{f_{i}\}_{i}
as the partition of unity of the proof of Proposition
3.5
.
Since
\delta_{\Pi}=(\tilde{F})_{*}\delta_{E_{*}(\Pi)}
, it follows as in the proof of Theorem
1.1
(i),

\displaystyle[\delta_{\Pi}\times(\tilde{F})_{*}\lambda_{g}](X/\Gamma_{M})=\sum_{i}[\delta_{\Pi}\times(\tilde{F})_{*}\lambda_{g}](f_{i})=\sum_{i}(\delta_{\Pi}\times(\tilde{F})_{*}\lambda_{g})(\tilde{f}_{i})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}((\tilde{F})_{*}\delta_{E_{*}(\Pi)}\times(\tilde{F})_{*}\lambda_{g})(\tilde{f}_{i})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}(\delta_{E_{*}(\Pi)}\times\lambda_{g})(\tilde{f}_{i}\circ\tilde{F})=[\delta_{E_{*}(\Pi)}\times\lambda_{g}](X/\Gamma_{N}).

Hence
I_{\Gamma_{M}}(\delta_{\Pi},(\tilde{F})_{*}\lambda_{g})=I_{\Gamma_{N}}(\delta_{E_{*}(\Pi)},\lambda_{g})
. Therefore (
33
) follows from Theorem
1.1
(i).

Abstract
. We study the dynamics of the one-dimensional ε \varepsilon -dependent Cahn-Hilliard / Allen-Cahn equation within a neighborhood of an equilibrium of N N transition layers, that in general does not conserve mass. Two different settings are considered which differ in that, for the second, we impose a mass-conservation constraint in place of one of the zero-mass flux boundary conditions at x = 1 x=1 . Motivated by the study of Carr and Pego on the layered metastable patterns of Allen-Cahn in [ 10 ] , and by this of Bates and Xun in [ 5 ] for the Cahn-Hilliard equation, we implement an N N -dimensional, and a mass-conservative N − 1 N-1 -dimensional manifold respectively; therein, a metastable state with N N transition layers is approximated. We then determine, for both cases, the essential dynamics of the layers (ode systems with the equations of motion), expressed in terms of local coordinates relative to the manifold used. In particular, we estimate the spectrum of the linearized Cahn-Hilliard / Allen-Cahn operator, and specify wide families of ε \varepsilon -dependent weights δ ⁡ ( ε ) \delta(\varepsilon) , μ ⁡ ( ε ) \mu(\varepsilon) , acting at each part of the operator, for which the dynamics are stable and rest exponentially small in ε \varepsilon . Our analysis enlightens the role of mass conservation in the classification of the general mixed problem into two main categories where the solution has a profile close to Allen-Cahn, or, when the mass is conserved, close to the Cahn-Hilliard solution.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
The equation

In this paper, we examine the dynamics of the Cahn-Hilliard /
Allen-Cahn equation

(1.1)

u_{t}=-\delta(\varepsilon)\,\big(\varepsilon^{2}u_{xx}-f(u)\big)_{xx}\;+\;\mu(\varepsilon)(\varepsilon^{2}u_{xx}-f(u)),\quad x\in(0,1),\quad t>0,

in a neighborhood of a layered equilibrium parameterized by a
small positive constant
\varepsilon
.

The nonlinearity
f(u)=F^{\prime}(u)
is the derivative of a double
equal-well potential
F
taking a non-degenerate global minimum
value zero at
u=\pm 1,
in particular

(1.2)

\displaystyle F(\pm 1)=f(\pm 1)=0,

(1.3)

\displaystyle f^{\prime}(\pm 1)>0,

(1.4)

\displaystyle F(u)>0\quad\mbox{for}\quad u\in(-1,1).

We define, for simplicity,

f(u):=u^{3}-u,

which is a typical example for a potential

F(u):=\tfrac{1}{4}(u^{2}-1)^{2}
. However, many of the results are
valid for more general nonlinearities.

The initial condition

u_{0}(x;\varepsilon)=:u(x,0),\;x\in(0,1),

is assumed layered with respect to

\varepsilon
which stands as a measure of the layers width
corresponding to a time scale proportional to
e^{C\varepsilon^{-1}}

for
C>0
, and therefore, to very long times as
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
where the solution is expected to change very slowly; see in

[
10
]
for the analogous considerations on the Allen-Cahn
equation.

We introduce the positive constant
\delta(\varepsilon)>0
and the
non-negative one
\mu(\varepsilon)\geq 0
in order to control the
coexistence of the
2
operators in terms of
\varepsilon
. Moreover, we
impose the presence of the Cahn-Hilliard part in the combined
model as
\delta(\varepsilon)\neq 0
, while for
\delta(\varepsilon):=1
and

\mu(\varepsilon):=0
the Cahn-Hilliard equation stands as a special
case.

An equation of the form (
1.1
) has been first
introduced in
[
18
]
as a mean field model of the microscopic
dynamics associated with adsorption and desorption mechanisms in
the context of surface processes; see also in
[
20
,
4
]
for
more details on the physical motivation. The combined
Cahn-Hilliard / Allen-Cahn model in study describes surface
diffusion including particle/particle interactions and adsorption
and desorption from the surface. It is noticeable that the
mobility is completely different from this of Allen-Cahn
equation, which implies that the diffusion speeds up the mean
curvature flow, (
[
17
,
22
]
).

E
quation (
1.1
) is a gradient flow for the
above energy with respect to an
\varepsilon
-weighted metric. In
particular, the standard Allen-Cahn equation is written as

u_{t}=(\Delta-\varepsilon^{-2}I)u=:\mathcal{A}^{\varepsilon}(u),

the Cahn-Hilliard equation, after rescaling, as

u_{t}=(-\varepsilon\Delta)(\Delta-\varepsilon^{-2}I)u=(-\varepsilon\Delta)(\mathcal{A}^{\varepsilon}(u)),

while the mixed model (
1.1
), as

u_{t}=(\varepsilon^{2}(-\delta(\varepsilon)\Delta+\mu(\varepsilon)I))(\Delta-\varepsilon^{-2}I)u=(\varepsilon^{2}(-\delta(\varepsilon)\Delta+\mu(\varepsilon)I))(\mathcal{A}^{\varepsilon}(u)),

and the
\varepsilon
-weighted metric is given by

<f,g>_{\varepsilon}:=(f,(\varepsilon^{2}(-\delta(\varepsilon)\Delta+\mu(\varepsilon)I))^{-1}g),

for
(\cdot,\cdot)
the
L^{2}((0,1))
inner product; see also the
discussion in
[
18
]
for the case
\delta(\varepsilon):=1
,

\mu(\varepsilon)=c_{0}\varepsilon^{-2}
.

The deterministic Allen-Cahn equation was proposed in
[
2
]

as a model for the dynamics of interfaces of crystal structures
in alloys. The same equation also appears as a model for various
other problems, including population genetics and nerve
conduction. As far as the one-dimensional case is concerned, the
limiting behaviour was analyzed in
[
10
,
13
]
. After a
very short time, generation of many very steep transition layers
is observed. These well developed transition layers then start to
move very slowly, and each time a pair of transition layers meet,
the two layers annihilate each other, and thus the number of
layers decrease gradually. Although those collision-annihilation
process takes place rather quickly, the motion of layers between
the collisions is extremely slow, and the profile of the layers
look nearly unchanged during those slow motion periods; in other
words, the solution exhibits a metastable pattern. The situation
is quite different in the multi-dimensional case, where such
metastable patterns hardly appear because of the curvature effect
on the motion of the interface as
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
; for
rigorous justification of singular limits, see for example in

[
8
]
,
[
11
,
12
]
,
[
21
]
.

The deterministic Cahn-Hilliard equation, proposed by

[
9
]
, describes the evolution of transitions (mass
transfer) during the phase separation of alloys.
In the case of only two layers, the exponentially slow dynamics
have been studied in
[
1
]
, where a one-dimensional invariant
manifold of slowly moving states was constructed. More details of
the phenomenon and the motion towards the boundary can be found in

[
16
]
,
[
15
]
.

Considering the CH/AC equation (
1.1
), in higher
dimensions, and for a specific choice of the coefficients

\mu(\varepsilon):=\varepsilon^{-2},\;\;\delta(\varepsilon):=\mathcal{O}(1)
, motion
by mean curvature was derived on the sharp interface limit

\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
, in
[
18
]
, as in the Allen-Cahn equation
limiting dynamics. A main result of our work is the analysis of
the spectrum of the linearized operator, where a crucial spectral
condition

\varepsilon^{2}\mu(\varepsilon)\geq\mathcal{O}(\delta(\varepsilon)),

is determined.
For the existence and the regularity properties of
(
1.1
), we refer to
[
19
]
, while the
stochastic version thereof was investigated in
[
4
]
. As it
has been observed in
[
4
]
, the operator at the right-hand
side of the mixed equation (
1.1
) is strongly
parabolic in the sense of Petrovskǐi and the bi-Laplacian
since existing (
\delta(\varepsilon)>0
) dominates, resulting to
regularity properties identical to the Cahn-Hilliard equation (at
least in the stochastic setting). However, the sharp interface
limit of the deterministic equation may exhibit a different
profile closer to this of the Allen-Cahn,
[
18
]
. The above,
ignites a special interest on the scaling of the chosen parameters

\delta(\varepsilon),\;\mu(\varepsilon)
, and the motivation of a further
investigation of their influence on the dynamics of the layers.

It is well known that the Cahn-Hilliard equation with the standard
Neumann boundary conditions for
u
and its Laplacian is mass
conservative in the sense that the integral of the solution in
space is time independent. In contrast, the Allen-Cahn equation
with Neumann or Dirichlet boundary is not satisfying such a
property unless a non-local integral term is added, which is the
case for the mixed equation as well; this is not considered in
this work, however it consists a future plan in progress the
detailed investigation of the dynamics for such a version, i.e.,
(
1.1
) with the extra integral term.

Our main aim is to obtain the equations of motion and estimate
the dynamics of a fixed number of layers, when
\varepsilon
is
sufficiently small, for the combined model (
1.1
),
and in dimension one. For this, when the initial and boundary
value problem involves the Neumann conditions, and so mass
conservation is not holding true, the solution will be
approximated into the manifold constructed and effectively used
for the Allen-Cahn equation in the classical result of Carr and
Pego,
[
10
]
. Then, by imposing mass conservation, not
through the pde but replacing one only of the b.c. with an
integral one, we will apply the mass conserving manifold of Bates
and Xun,
[
5
,
6
]
, which has been proposed for
the integrated Cahn-Hillliard equation. There, the derived initial
and boundary value problem for the integrated equation is
identical to this of
[
5
,
6
]
, when

\mu(\varepsilon):=0
. We note that the problem is of fourth order,
since
\delta(\varepsilon)
is not vanishing, while in dimension one the
boundary consists of only two well separated points where four
boundary conditions are applied on, and could be therefore of
different type. In both cases we will determine the ode systems of
the dynamics, and investigate the main order terms with respect to
the order in
\varepsilon
of
\delta(\varepsilon)
and
\mu(\varepsilon)
, and
stability.

The general approach for deriving the equations of motion, as a
system of odes, consists of specifying the approximate solution
into a proper approximate manifold with a residual orthogonal to
a set of approximate tangent vectors to the manifold.
Differentiating in time the orthogonality condition then yields
the system describing the dynamics of the layers.

1.2.
Main results

Let
u
be the solution of (
1.1
), with the
standard Neumann conditions,
u_{x}=u_{xxx}=0
at
x=0,1
, (non

of exactly

More precisely, the function

\Omega_{\rho}=\bigg\{h=(h_{1},\ldots,h_{\scriptscriptstyle N}):\;\frac{\varepsilon}{2\rho}<h_{1}<\cdots<h_{N}<1-\frac{\varepsilon}{2\rho},\quad\mbox{and}\quad\min\limits_{\scriptscriptstyle 2\leq j\leq N}(h_{j}-h_{j-1})>\frac{\varepsilon}{\rho}\bigg\},

\varepsilon
, which

The residual

at

2.
Non mass conserving layer dynamics

We supplement (
1.1
) with the standard Neumann
b.c. on
u
and
u_{xx}
, and consider the following initial and
boundary value problem

(2.1)

\begin{split}&u_{t}=-\delta(\varepsilon)\,\big(\varepsilon^{2}u_{xx}-f(u)\big)_{xx}\;+\;\mu(\varepsilon)(\varepsilon^{2}u_{xx}-f(u)),\quad x\in(0,1),\quad t>0,\\
&u_{x}=u_{xxx}=0\quad\mbox{at}\quad x=0,1,\quad t>0,\\
&u(x,0)=u_{0}(x,\varepsilon),\quad x\in(0,1).\end{split}

Let us point out that (
2.1
) does not conserve mass for any

\mu(\varepsilon)\neq 0
since in general

\partial_{t}\int_{0}^{1}u(x,t)dx=\int_{0}^{1}u_{t}(x,t)dx=\int_{0}^{1}\mu(\varepsilon)(\varepsilon^{2}u_{xx}(x,t)-f(u(x,t)))dx=-\mu(\varepsilon)\int_{0}^{1}f(u(x,t))dx,

is not the zero function, while
the solutions of the boundary problem

(2.2)

\begin{split}&\varepsilon^{2}u_{xx}-f(u)=0,\quad 0<x<1,\\
&u_{x}=0\quad\mbox{at}\quad x=0,1,\end{split}

are obviously steady states of (
2.1
); (
2.2
) follows by setting
u_{t}=0
at the equation of
(
2.1
) and integrating in space twice using the boundary
conditions.

We assume, therefore, in the context of Section
2
, that

\mu(\varepsilon)>0
for all
\varepsilon>0
.

2.1.
Free energy decreasing

The relevant to the scaling of the standard Allen-Cahn operator

\varepsilon^{2}\Delta u-f(u),

free energy functional is defined as
follows

(2.3)

E(u):=\int_{0}^{1}\Big(\frac{\varepsilon^{2}|\nabla u|^{2}}{2}+F(u)\Big)dx.

Multiplying both sides of the equation of the i.b.v.p
(
1.1
) with
\varepsilon^{2}\Delta u-f(u)
, integrating in
space, and using the boundary conditions, we derive

(2.4)

\begin{split}(u_{t},\varepsilon^{2}\Delta u-f(u))=&-\delta(\varepsilon)(\Delta\big(\varepsilon^{2}\Delta u-f(u)\big),\varepsilon^{2}\Delta u-f(u))+\mu(\varepsilon)\|\varepsilon^{2}\Delta u-f(u)\|^{2}\\
=&\delta(\varepsilon)\|\nabla\big(\varepsilon^{2}\Delta u-f(u)\big)\|^{2}+\mu(\varepsilon)\|\varepsilon^{2}\Delta u-f(u)\|^{2},\end{split}

where here and for the rest of the manuscript,
(\cdot,\cdot)

denotes the
L^{2}((0,1))
inner product, and
\|\cdot\|
the
induced
L^{2}((0,1))
norm.

Differentiating in time, (
2.3
) yields

(2.5)

\begin{split}\frac{\partial E(u)}{\partial t}=&\int_{0}^{1}\Big(\varepsilon^{2}\nabla u\nabla u_{t}+F^{\prime}(u)u_{t}\Big)dx\\
=&\int_{0}^{1}\Big(\varepsilon^{2}\nabla u\nabla u_{t}+f(u)u_{t}\Big)dx\\
=&\int_{0}^{1}\Big(-\varepsilon^{2}\Delta uu_{t}+f(u)u_{t}\Big)dx\\
=&-(u_{t},\varepsilon^{2}\Delta u-f(u)).\end{split}

So, by (
2.4
) and (
2.5
), we obtain the free
energy decreasing property for the combined model

(2.6)

\begin{split}\frac{\partial E(u)}{\partial t}=-\delta(\varepsilon)\|\nabla\big(\varepsilon^{2}\Delta u-f(u)\big)\|^{2}-\mu(\varepsilon)\|\varepsilon^{2}\Delta u-f(u)\|^{2}\leq 0,\end{split}

since
\delta(\varepsilon)>0
and
\mu(\varepsilon)\geq 0
.

Besides the three homogeneous equilibria
u=\pm 1
and
u=u_{0}
for the zero
u_{0}
of
f
in
(-1,1)
(
u=0
for odd
f
as in our special case), problem (
2.2
) has non-constant solutions for all sufficiently small
\varepsilon;
see e.g.
[
16
]
. More precisely, if
\varepsilon_{n+1}\leq\varepsilon<\varepsilon_{n}
with
\varepsilon_{i}:=(-f^{\prime}(0))^{1/2}/2\pi i,\,i=1,2,\ldots,
then problem (
2.2
) has exactly
n
pairs of non-constant solutions
u^{\pm}_{\varepsilon_{i}},\,1\leq i\leq n
(
u^{-}_{\varepsilon_{i}}=-u^{+}_{\varepsilon_{i}}
if
f
is odd). For each
i,
the equilibria
u^{-}_{\varepsilon_{i}},u^{+}_{\varepsilon_{i}}
have exactly
i
zeros at
x=1/2i,3/2i,\ldots,1-1/2i.
The two solutions for
i=1
are monotone, and the other solutions for
i\geq 2
are oscillating taken as rescaled reflections and periodic extensions of monotone solutions, they correspond to periodic orbits around the origin on the phase plane and we speak of solutions with
i
internal
transition
layers.

Since the internal transition layers of stationary solutions must have periodic spacing, the solutions of (
1.1
) which reach patterns that are nearly piecewise constant, say with
N
transition layers, but not periodic, they are close to a stationary state but they are
not solutions of the steady state problem and we do not expect them to remain at these patterns. We
will concern with these solutions yet not with their end-state but rather with their dynamics as long as
they remain at these “metastable”
N
-layered patterns.

of
N
layer positions, we construct
u^{h}
by piecing together
steady state solutions of (
2.7
). We set

u^{h}=(1-\chi^{j})\phi^{j}+\mathcal{\chi}^{j}\phi^{j+1}
on
[h_{j}-\varepsilon,h_{j}+\varepsilon]
(shaded areas). Note that
h_{0}=-h_{1},\,h_{N+1}=2-h_{N},
and
m_{1}=0,m_{N+1}=1.

2.4.
Properties of
u^{h}

Note that
u^{h}
is a smooth function of
x
and
h.
In
particular, for
x\in[m_{j},\,m_{j+1}],
we have

(2.13)

u_{x}^{h}=\begin{cases}\;\phi_{x}^{j},&\quad m_{j}\leq x\leq h_{j}-\varepsilon,\\
\chi_{x}^{j}\,\big(\phi^{j+1}-\phi^{j}\big)+\big(1-\chi^{j}\big)\,\phi_{x}^{j}+\chi^{j}\,\phi_{x}^{j+1},&\quad|x-h_{j}|<\varepsilon,\\
\;\phi_{x}^{j+1},&\quad h_{j}+\varepsilon\leq x\leq m_{j+1},\end{cases}

and so

(2.14)

u_{xx}^{h}=\begin{cases}\;\phi_{xx}^{j},&\quad m_{j}\leq x\leq h_{j}-\varepsilon,\\
\chi_{xx}^{j}\,\big(\phi^{j+1}-\phi^{j}\big)+2\chi_{x}^{j}\,\big(\phi_{x}^{j+1}-\phi_{x}^{j}\big)+(1-\chi^{j})\phi^{j}_{xx}+\chi^{j}\phi^{j+1}_{xx},&\quad|x-h_{j}|<\varepsilon,\\
\;\phi_{xx}^{j+1},&\quad h_{j}+\varepsilon\leq x\leq m_{j+1},\end{cases}

where, from the definition of
\phi^{j}
(see (
2.7
)),

(2.15)

\varepsilon^{2}\,\phi_{xx}^{j}\,=\,f(\phi^{j}),\qquad\mbox{on}\quad[h_{j-1}-\varepsilon,\,h_{j}+\varepsilon].

It is then straightforward to see that
u^{h}
satisfy the bistable equation

(2.16)

L^{b}(u^{h})=0,\qquad\mbox{for}\quad|x-h_{j}|\geq\varepsilon,\;\;j=1,2,\ldots,N.

where
L^{b}
is the bistable operator

(2.17)

L^{b}(u):=A_{{}_{2,\varepsilon}}(u)=\varepsilon^{2}u_{xx}-f(u).

Notice also that by reflecting the solutions
\phi(\cdot,\ell,\pm 1)
of (
2.7
) about the origin we can show
that they are even in
x,
and thus

(2.18)

\phi_{x}(0,\ell,\pm 1)=0,

which together with (
2.10
) and (
2.8
) yields

u^{h}_{x}(m_{j})=0,\;j=1,\ldots,N+1.

So, we obtain

(2.19)

u^{h}_{x}(0)=u^{h}_{x}(1)=0,

and, therefore,

(2.20)

\frac{\partial}{\partial x}f(u^{h})=0\qquad\mbox{at \, $x=0,1$}.

Moreover, we note that

u_{j}^{h}\sim-u_{x}^{h},\qquad\mbox{as}\quad r\to 0,\qquad\mbox{uniformly on}\quad I_{j}:=[m_{j},\,m_{j+1}],

see Remark
4.9
for more details thereof.

2.5.
The coordinate system

Following
[
10
]
, we introduce a local coordinate system

u\mapsto(h,\upsilon),

in a tubular neighborhood of the
approximate manifold
\mathcal{M},
defined by the decomposition

(2.21)

u(x,t)=u^{h(t)}(x)+\upsilon(x,t),

with
u^{h}\in\mathcal{M}
, and
\upsilon
satisfying the
orthogonality condition

(2.22)

\langle\upsilon,\tau_{j}^{h}\rangle:=\int_{0}^{1}\upsilon\,\tau_{j}^{h}\,dx=0,\qquad j=1,\ldots,N,

where
\tau_{j}^{h}
are approximate tangent vectors to
\mathcal{M}\,
at
u^{h}
.

More precisely, let

(2.23)

\gamma^{j}(x):=\chi\big(\tfrac{x-m_{j}-\varepsilon}{\varepsilon}\big)\Big[1-\chi\big(\tfrac{x-m_{j+1}+\varepsilon}{\varepsilon}\big)\Big],

which yields that

\gamma^{j}(x)=\begin{cases}0,&x\notin(m_{j},\,m_{j+1}),\\
1,&x\in[m_{j}+2\varepsilon,\,m_{j+1}-2\varepsilon].\end{cases}

The approximate tangent vectors are then defined through

\gamma^{j}(x)
by

(2.24)

\tau_{j}^{h}(x)\,=\,\gamma^{j}(x)\,u^{h}_{x}(x)

which are smooth functions of
x
and
h
.

Considering differentiation, we introduce the notation

\tau_{j,k}^{h}:=\frac{\partial\tau_{j}^{h}}{\partial h_{k}}\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\tau_{j,x}^{h}:=\frac{\partial\tau_{j}^{h}}{\partial x},

and observe that
\tau_{j,x}^{h}=0
at
x=0,1,
for
j=1,\ldots,N.

2.6.
Equations of motion

For a classical solution
u=u(x,t)
of (
1.1
),
we will establish the ODEs system for the dynamics of
(h,\upsilon),
which is defined by (
2.21
)-(
2.22
).

This first order ODE system with unknowns the positions
coordinates
h_{k}(t)
,
k=1,\cdots,N
for each one of the
N

layers (fronts) will be derived by differentiating in time the
orthogonality condition (
2.22
). We will
insert the Cahn-Hilliard / Allen-Cahn equation into the
differentiated condition and then we shall use linearization which
will be given by the linear combination through the weights

\delta(\varepsilon)
,
\mu(\varepsilon)
of the C-H and A-C linearized
operators respectively.

We differentiate (
2.22
), with respect to
t,
to get

\big\langle\partial_{t}\upsilon\;,\;\tau_{j}^{h}\big\rangle\,+\,\big\langle\upsilon\;,\;\partial_{t}\tau_{j}^{h}\big\rangle=0,\qquad j=1,\ldots,N.

Using
(
2.21
), and the substituting
u_{t}
by the
equation (
1.1
), together with the definition
(
1.5
) of the operator
A_{\varepsilon}
, we
obtain

\begin{split}\partial_{t}\upsilon=&\partial_{t}(u-u^{h})\\
=&-{\delta(\varepsilon)}\Big(\varepsilon^{2}u_{xx}-f(u)\Big)_{xx}+\mu(\varepsilon)(\varepsilon^{2}u_{xx}-f(u))-\partial_{t}u^{h})-\partial_{t}u^{h}\\
=&A_{\varepsilon}u-\partial_{t}u^{h}=A_{\varepsilon}u-\sum_{k=1}^{N}(\partial_{\!{}_{\scriptscriptstyle h_{k}}}\!\!u^{h})\dot{h}_{k},\end{split}

to arrive at the system

(2.25)

\sum\limits_{k=1}^{N}a_{jk}\,\dot{h}_{k}\;=\;\big\langle A_{\varepsilon}(u^{h}+\upsilon)\;,\;\tau_{j}^{h}\;\big\rangle,\qquad j=1,2,\ldots,N,

where

(2.26)

a_{jk}:=\big\langle u^{h}_{k}\;,\;\tau_{j}^{h}\big\rangle\;-\;\big\langle\upsilon\;,\;\tau^{h}_{j,k}\,\big\rangle,\qquad j,k=1,2,\ldots,N.

In the above, the subscripts
k
indicate differentiation
w.r.t.
h_{k},
i.e.,

u^{h}_{k}:=\frac{\partial u^{h}}{\partial h_{k}}\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\tau^{h}_{j,k}:=\frac{\partial\tau^{h}_{j}}{\partial h_{k}}.

We expand

(2.27)

\displaystyle A_{\varepsilon}(u^{h}+\upsilon)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle A_{\varepsilon}(u^{h})\,+\,L_{\varepsilon}^{h}(\upsilon)\,-\,{\delta(\varepsilon)}\,\big(f^{h}\,\upsilon^{2}\big)_{xx}\,+\,\mu(\varepsilon)f^{h}\,\upsilon^{2},

where
L_{\varepsilon}^{h}(\upsilon)
is the linearization of

A_{\varepsilon}
at
u^{h}
, i.e.,

(2.28)

\begin{split}L_{\varepsilon}^{h}(\upsilon):=&-\delta(\varepsilon)\Big(\varepsilon^{2}\upsilon_{xx}-f^{\prime}(u^{h})\upsilon\Big)_{xx}+\mu(\varepsilon)(\varepsilon^{2}\upsilon_{xx}-f^{\prime}(u^{h})\upsilon)\\
=:&-\delta(\varepsilon)L_{1,\varepsilon}^{h}(\upsilon)+\mu(\varepsilon)L_{2,\varepsilon}^{h}(\upsilon),\end{split}

and

(2.29)

f^{h}(x):=\int_{0}^{1}(\tau-1)\;f^{\prime\prime}(u^{h}+\tau\upsilon)\;d\tau.

Using (
2.27
), then the system (
2.25
) is written as

(2.30)

\begin{split}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{N}a_{jk}\,\dot{h}_{k}\;=&\;\big\langle A_{\varepsilon}(u^{h})\;,\;\tau_{j}^{h}\;\big\rangle\;+\;\big\langle L_{\varepsilon}^{h}(\upsilon)\;,\;\tau_{j}^{h}\;\big\rangle\;\\
&-\;{\delta(\varepsilon)}\,\big\langle\big(f^{h}\,\upsilon^{2}\big)_{xx}\;,\;\tau_{j}^{h}\;\big\rangle\;+\;\mu(\varepsilon)\big\langle f^{h}\,\upsilon^{2}\;,\;\tau_{j}^{h}\;\big\rangle\\
=&\,-{\delta(\varepsilon)}\,\big\langle A_{1,\varepsilon}(u^{h})+L_{1,\varepsilon}^{h}(\upsilon)+\big(f^{h}\,\upsilon^{2}\big)_{xx}\;,\;\tau_{j}^{h}\big\rangle\;\\
&+\mu(\varepsilon)\big\langle A_{2,\varepsilon}(u^{h})+L_{2,\varepsilon}^{h}(\upsilon)+f^{h}\,\upsilon^{2}\;,\;\tau_{j}^{h}\big\rangle\\
=&\,-{\delta(\varepsilon)}\,\big\langle A_{1,\varepsilon}(u^{h}),\tau_{j}^{h}\big\rangle+\mu(\varepsilon)\big\langle A_{2,\varepsilon}(u^{h}),\tau_{j}^{h}\big\rangle\\
&-{\delta(\varepsilon)}\,\big\langle L_{1,\varepsilon}^{h}(\upsilon)+\big(f^{h}\,\upsilon^{2}\big)_{xx}\;,\;\tau_{j}^{h}\big\rangle\;\\
&+\mu(\varepsilon)\big\langle L_{2,\varepsilon}^{h}(\upsilon)+f^{h}\,\upsilon^{2}\;,\;\tau_{j}^{h}\big\rangle,\end{split}

for
j=1,2,\ldots,N
.

Remark 2.2
.

2.7.
The spectrum of the linearized operator and transverse coercivity

A key point in the analysis of the equations of motion (
2.30
),(
2.33
) is the spectral analysis of the linear operator
L^{h}_{\varepsilon}
on
\upsilon=u-u^{h},
the part of the solution transverse to the manifold
\mathcal{M}.

We
consider the eigenvalue problem

(EVP)

\begin{cases}L_{\varepsilon}^{h}(\phi):=-\delta(\varepsilon)\big(\varepsilon^{2}\phi^{\prime\prime}-f^{\prime}(u^{h})\phi\big)^{\prime\prime}+\mu(\varepsilon)\big(\varepsilon^{2}\phi^{\prime\prime}-f^{\prime}(u^{h})\phi\big)=\lambda(\varepsilon)\phi,\quad 0<x<1,\\
\phi^{\prime}(0)=\phi^{\prime}(1)=0,\\
\phi^{\prime\prime\prime}(0)=\phi^{\prime\prime\prime}(1)=0,\end{cases}

and the associated quadratic form

(2.39)

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{\varepsilon}[\phi]

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle-\langle L_{\varepsilon}^{h}(\phi)\,,\,\phi\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\left[{\delta(\varepsilon)}\left(\varepsilon^{2}\phi_{xx}^{2}\,+\,f^{\prime}(u^{h})\,\phi_{x}^{2}\,-\,\tfrac{1}{2}\,\big(f^{{}^{\prime}}(u^{h})\big)_{xx}\phi^{2}\right)\,+\mu(\varepsilon)\left(\varepsilon^{2}\phi_{x}^{2}\,+\,f^{\prime}(u^{h})\phi^{2}\right)\right]\,dx,

where we have applied integration by parts. We note that the operator
L_{\varepsilon}^{h}:\mathcal{D}(L_{\varepsilon}^{h})\to L^{2}(0,1)
, with domain

\mathcal{D}(L_{\varepsilon}^{h}):=\Big\{\phi\in H^{4}(0,1):\;\phi^{\prime}(0)=\phi^{\prime}(1)=0=\phi^{\prime\prime\prime}(0)=\phi^{\prime\prime\prime}(1)\Big\},

is not symmetric in
L^{2}(0,1).
However (
EVP
) may be recast into a self-adjoint form, as the form
\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{\varepsilon}
it will be seen in
the sequel to be lower semibounded and it is also closable since it is associated with the symmetric operator
S_{\varepsilon}^{h}:\mathcal{D}(S_{\varepsilon}^{h})\to L^{2}(0,1)
,
defined by

(2.40)

S_{\varepsilon}^{h}(\phi):=-\delta(\varepsilon)\Big(\varepsilon^{2}\phi^{\prime\prime\prime\prime}\;-\;\big(f^{\prime}(u^{h})\phi^{\prime}\big)^{\prime}-\frac{1}{2}\big(f^{{}^{\prime}}(u^{h})\big)_{xx}\phi\Big)+\mu(\varepsilon)\Big(\varepsilon^{2}\phi^{\prime\prime}-f^{\prime}(u^{h})\phi\Big),

with domain
\mathcal{D}(S_{\varepsilon}^{h})\equiv\mathcal{D}(L_{\varepsilon}^{h}),
in the sense that

(2.41)

\langle L_{\varepsilon}^{h}(\phi)\,,\,\phi\rangle=-\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{\varepsilon}[\phi]=\langle S_{\varepsilon}^{h}(\phi)\,,\,\phi\rangle,\qquad\mbox{for any}\quad\phi\in\mathcal{D}(L_{\varepsilon}^{h}).

We can then consider the self-adjoint extension (Friedrichs extension) of
S_{\varepsilon}^{h}
associated with the closure of
\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{\varepsilon}
which we still denote by
S_{\varepsilon}^{h}.
The spectrum of
S_{\varepsilon}^{h}
turns out to consist of a sequence of real eigenvalues

\cdots\leq\lambda_{N+1}\leq\lambda_{N}\leq\dots\leq\lambda_{1},

satisfying, when

\varepsilon^{2}\mu(\varepsilon)\geq\mathcal{O}(\delta(\varepsilon)),

\lambda_{k}\leq C\mu(\varepsilon),

and in particular for
k=N+1,N+2,\cdots

\lambda_{k}\leq-C=-C(\varepsilon)<0,

for some
C=C(\varepsilon)
which will be specified. To this aim, we will use the variational characterization of the eigenvalues for
S_{\varepsilon}^{h},

(2.42)

-\lambda_{N+1}:=\max\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}V\\
\scriptscriptstyle\operatorname{dim}\,V=N\end{subarray}}\min\limits_{\phi\in V^{\bot}}\frac{\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{\varepsilon}[\phi]}{\|\phi\|^{2}},

where the maximum is taken over the linear
N
-dimensional
subspaces
V
of

(2.43)

\big\{\phi\in H^{2}(0,1):\,\phi^{\prime}(0)=\phi^{\prime}(1)=0\big\}.

Let us decompose the Rayleigh quotient in (
2.42
) into the Cahn-Hilliard and the Allen-Cahn part, i.e.

(2.44)

R(\phi):=\frac{\langle-S_{\varepsilon}^{h}(\phi),\phi\rangle}{\|\phi\|^{2}}={\delta(\varepsilon)}R_{1}(\phi)\,+\mu(\varepsilon)R_{2}(\phi),

for

(2.45)

\begin{split}&R_{1}(\phi):=\frac{\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}(\varepsilon^{2}\phi_{xx}^{2}\,+\,f^{\prime}(u^{h})\,\phi_{x}^{2}\,-\,\tfrac{1}{2}\,\big(f^{{}^{\prime}}(u^{h})\big)_{xx}\phi^{2})\,dx}{\|\phi\|^{2}},\\
&R_{2}(\phi):=\frac{\overbrace{\int_{0}^{1}(\varepsilon^{2}\phi_{x}^{2}\,+\,f^{\prime}(u^{h})\phi^{2})\,dx}^{\scriptscriptstyle\langle L_{2,\varepsilon}^{h}\phi,\phi\rangle}}{\|\phi\|^{2}}.\quad\end{split}

In the sequel we will assume that
\delta(\varepsilon)>0
and
\mu(\varepsilon)>0
satisfy the condition

(2.46)

\varepsilon^{2}\mu(\varepsilon)\geq C_{0}\delta(\varepsilon)\qquad\;\forall\varepsilon>0,

for some
C_{0}\geq C_{\min}>0
, where
C_{\min}
depends on the
supremum of
|\varepsilon^{2}(f^{\prime}(u^{h}))_{xx}|=\mathcal{O}(1)
in
(0,1)
and can be determined implicitly in the context of the proof of following theorem.

We shall show that when
\delta(\varepsilon),\mu(\varepsilon)
satisfy (
2.46
), then

-\lambda_{N+1}\geq C(\varepsilon)=\mathcal{O}(\mu(\varepsilon)-C_{\min}\delta(\varepsilon)\varepsilon^{-2})>0.

For our purpose, we state the result in the variational form. The main implication for us is the transverse coercivity (cf. Lemma
2.5
), the coercivity of
L_{\varepsilon}^{h}
on
\upsilon=u-u^{h}
which recall that is approximately orthogonal to the tangent space (see (
2.22
)).

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
\delta(\varepsilon)>0
and
\mu(\varepsilon)>0
satisfying
(
2.46
). Then there exist
\Lambda,\rho_{2}>0
such that for
h\in\Omega_{\rho}
with
\rho\leq\rho_{2}
it holds that

\min\limits_{\phi\in V^{\bot}}\frac{-\langle S_{\varepsilon}^{h}(\phi)\,,\,\phi\rangle}{\|\phi\|^{2}}\geq\eta(\varepsilon)>0,

for
V=\operatorname{span}\{\tau_{i}^{h},\;i=1,2,\ldots,N\},
with
\tau_{i}^{h}
are the approximate tangent
vectors defined in (
2.24
), and

(2.47)

\eta(\varepsilon):=\Lambda(\mu(\varepsilon)-C_{\min}\delta(\varepsilon)\varepsilon^{-2}).

Proof.

Remind that
f(u)=u^{3}-u
, and so
f^{\prime}(u)=3u^{2}-1\geq-1
.

Moreover, it holds that

|(f^{{}^{\prime}}(u^{h}))_{xx}|\leq C\varepsilon^{-2}\qquad\mbox{in}\quad[0,1],

since there

|u^{h}_{x}|\leq C\varepsilon^{-1},\qquad|u^{h}_{xx}|\leq C\varepsilon^{-2},

see
in Appendix relations (
4.34
), and (
4.35
), while
u^{h}
is bounded.

Using that
\delta(\varepsilon)>0
,
\mu(\varepsilon)>0
, and the above, we
get for any
\phi\in V^{\bot}

(2.48)

\begin{split}R(\phi):=&\frac{\langle-S_{\varepsilon}^{h}(\phi),\phi\rangle}{\|\phi\|^{2}}\\
=&{\delta(\varepsilon)}\frac{\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}(\varepsilon^{2}\phi_{xx}^{2}+f^{\prime}(u^{h})\phi_{x}^{2}-\tfrac{1}{2}\big(f^{{}^{\prime}}(u^{h})\big)_{xx}\phi^{2})dx}{\|\phi\|^{2}}\\
&+\mu(\varepsilon)\frac{\int_{0}^{1}(\varepsilon^{2}\phi_{x}^{2}+f^{\prime}(u^{h})\phi^{2})dx}{\|\phi\|^{2}}\\
\geq&-{\delta(\varepsilon)}\frac{\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\phi_{x}^{2}dx}{\|\phi\|^{2}}+\mu(\varepsilon)\frac{\int_{0}^{1}(\varepsilon^{2}\phi_{x}^{2}+f^{\prime}(u^{h})\phi^{2})dx}{\|\phi\|^{2}}\\
&-{\delta(\varepsilon)}\frac{\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\tfrac{1}{2}\big(f^{{}^{\prime}}(u^{h})\big)_{xx}\phi^{2}dx}{\|\phi\|^{2}}\\
\geq&-{\delta(\varepsilon)}\frac{\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\phi_{x}^{2}dx}{\|\phi\|^{2}}+\mu(\varepsilon)\Lambda\frac{\int_{0}^{1}(\varepsilon^{2}\phi_{x}^{2}+\phi^{2})dx}{\|\phi\|^{2}}\\
&-\frac{{\delta(\varepsilon)}}{2}\displaystyle{\sup_{(0,1)}}|(f^{{}^{\prime}}(u^{h}))_{xx}|\\
\geq&\frac{\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}[-{\delta(\varepsilon)}+\mu(\varepsilon)\Lambda\varepsilon^{2}]\phi_{x}^{2}dx}{\|\phi\|^{2}}+\mu(\varepsilon)\Lambda-\displaystyle{\sup_{(0,1)}}|\varepsilon^{2}(f^{{}^{\prime}}(u^{h}))_{xx}|\frac{\delta(\varepsilon)}{2}\varepsilon^{-2}\\
=&\Lambda\Big(\mu(\varepsilon)\varepsilon^{2}-\frac{1}{\Lambda}{\delta(\varepsilon)}\Big)\frac{\|\phi_{x}\|^{2}}{\|\phi\|^{2}}+\Lambda\varepsilon^{-2}\Big(\mu(\varepsilon)\varepsilon^{2}-\frac{C_{1}}{2\Lambda}{\delta(\varepsilon)}\Big)\end{split}

where we applied the Main Theorem 4.2 (i) of
[
10
]
, at
pg. 538, and defined

C_{1}:=\displaystyle{\sup_{(0,1)}}|\varepsilon^{2}(f^{{}^{\prime}}(u^{h}))_{xx}|=\mathcal{O}(1).

More specifically, we used that for some
\Lambda>0
and
\rho_{2}>0

\int_{0}^{1}(\varepsilon^{2}\phi_{x}^{2}+f^{\prime}(u^{h})\phi^{2})dx\geq\Lambda\int_{0}^{1}(\varepsilon^{2}\phi_{x}^{2}+\phi^{2})dx

for
h\in\Omega_{\rho}
with
\rho\leq\rho_{2}.
Here,
\Lambda
satisfies

\Lambda\leq\frac{\Lambda_{*}}{\Lambda_{*}+2},

(see
[
10
]
, at pg. 541,
and use the specific
f
), with
\Lambda_{*}
arbitrary in

(0,\lambda_{*})
for some
\lambda_{*}>0
such that

\lambda_{*}\leq\min f^{\prime}(\pm 1)=2,

see
[
10
]
, at pg. 536, for the
detailed definition.

Therefore, in (
2.48
), if we choose
\mu(\varepsilon),\delta(\varepsilon)

such that

\varepsilon^{2}\mu(\varepsilon)\geq C_{0}\delta(\varepsilon),

for

C_{0}\geq C_{\min}:=\max\Big\{\frac{1}{\Lambda},\;\frac{C_{1}}{2\Lambda}\Big\}+\beta^{2},

for
\beta=\mathcal{O}(1)\neq 0
as small, we obtain

\frac{\langle-S_{\varepsilon}^{h}(\phi),\phi\rangle}{\|\phi\|^{2}}\geq C(\varepsilon)=\Lambda(\mu(\varepsilon)-C_{\min}\delta(\varepsilon)\varepsilon^{-2})>0,

for any
\phi\in V^{\bot}
, and thus the result.
∎

The next theorem, gives an upper bound for the spectrum.

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
\delta(\varepsilon)>0
and
\mu(\varepsilon)>0
satisfying (
2.46
),

\varepsilon^{2}\mu(\varepsilon)\geq C_{0}\delta(\varepsilon)\qquad\;\forall\varepsilon>0,

for
C_{0}\geq C_{\min}>0

as defined in Theorem

. Then, for any
k
it holds that

\lambda_{k}\leq C\mu(\varepsilon),

for some
C>0
.

Proof.

Using that
\delta(\varepsilon)>0
,
\mu(\varepsilon)>0
, and
f(u)=u^{3}-u
,

f^{\prime}(u)=3u^{2}-1\geq-1
, we get for any
\phi
, and since
C_{0}\geq\frac{1}{\Lambda}>1

(2.49)

\begin{split}R(\phi):=&\frac{\langle-S_{\varepsilon}^{h}(\phi),\phi\rangle}{\|\phi\|^{2}}\\
=&{\delta(\varepsilon)}\frac{\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}(\varepsilon^{2}\phi_{xx}^{2}+f^{\prime}(u^{h})\phi_{x}^{2}-\tfrac{1}{2}\big(f^{{}^{\prime}}(u^{h})\big)_{xx}\phi^{2})dx}{\|\phi\|^{2}}\\
&+\mu(\varepsilon)\frac{\int_{0}^{1}(\varepsilon^{2}\phi_{x}^{2}+f^{\prime}(u^{h})\phi^{2})dx}{\|\phi\|^{2}}\\

Uniqueness:
Let
u,v
be solutions of the problem (
ACH
)-(
BC1
)-(
BC2
)-(
MC
) and consider the difference
\mathrm{v}=u-v.
In view of (
ACH
), we have

(4.75)

\mathrm{v}_{t}=-\delta(\varepsilon)\,\varepsilon^{2}\mathrm{v}_{xxxx}\,+\,\delta(\varepsilon)\,\big(W^{\prime}(u)\,-\,W^{\prime}(v)\big)_{xx}\;+\;\mu(\varepsilon)\,\varepsilon^{2}\mathrm{v}_{xx}\,-\,\mu(\varepsilon)\,\big(W^{\prime}(u)\,-\,W^{\prime}(v)\big),

the (
BC1
)-(
BC2
) yield the boundary conditions

(4.76)

\displaystyle\mathrm{v}_{x}(0,\,t)=\mathrm{v}_{x}(1,\,t)

\displaystyle\;=0,

(4.77)

\displaystyle\mathrm{v}_{xxx}(0,\,t)

\displaystyle\;=0,

and (
MC
) implies

(4.78)

\int_{0}^{1}\mathrm{v}(x,t)\,dx=0,\qquad t>0.

Multiply the pde (
4.75
) by
\mathrm{v},
then integrate with respect to
x
and apply (
4.76
)-(
4.77
) to get

(4.79)

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|\mathrm{v}\|^{2}\;+\;\delta(\varepsilon)\,\varepsilon^{2}\,\|\mathrm{v}_{xx}\|^{2}\;+\;\mu(\varepsilon)\,\varepsilon^{2}\,\|\mathrm{v}_{x}\|^{2}\;=\;-\delta(\varepsilon)\,\varepsilon^{2}\,\mathrm{v}_{xxx}(1,t)\,\mathrm{v}(1,t)\\[5.0pt]
+\;\delta(\varepsilon)\int_{0}^{1}\big(W^{\prime}(u)\,-\,W^{\prime}(v)\big)\,\mathrm{v}_{xx}\,dx\;-\;\mu(\varepsilon)\int_{0}^{1}\big(W^{\prime}(u)\,-\,W^{\prime}(v)\big)\,\mathrm{v}\,dx.

Let us next estimate the terms in the rhs of (
4.79
). To this aim, we set
K_{\scriptscriptstyle T}:=\sup\big\{\|u(\cdot,t)\|_{\infty},\|\upsilon(\cdot,t)\|_{\infty}:\,0\leq t\leq T\big\}
and
L=\max\big\{|W^{\prime\prime}(w)|:\,|w|\leq K_{\scriptscriptstyle T}\big\}.
In view of (
4.72
) we have
K_{\scriptscriptstyle T}<\infty,
and
L
depends on
u,\upsilon,W,T,
but it is independent of
t.

We then have that

(4.80)

\left|W^{\prime}\big(\upsilon(x,t)\big)\,-\,W^{\prime}\big(u(x,t)\big)\right|\leq L\,|\upsilon(x,t)-u(x,t)|,\qquad 0\leq t\leq T.

Regarding the first term in the rhs of (
4.79
), we clearly have

\mathrm{v}(1,t)=\int_{y}^{1}\mathrm{v}_{x}\,dx\,+\,\mathrm{v}(y,t)\leq\int_{0}^{1}|\mathrm{v}_{x}|\,dx\,+\,\mathrm{v}(y,t)

and integrate this inequality with respect to
y,
to get, by virtue of (
4.78
),

(4.81)

\mathrm{v}(1,t)\leq\int_{0}^{1}|\mathrm{v}_{x}|\,dx\,+\,\int_{0}^{1}\mathrm{v}(x,t)\,dx\;=\;\left\|\mathrm{v}_{x}(\cdot,t)\right\|_{1}.

Moreover, by (
4.54
) and (
4.80
) we get

(4.82)

\delta(\varepsilon)\,\varepsilon^{2}\,\mathrm{v}_{xxx}(1,t)=\mu(\varepsilon)\int_{0}^{1}\big(W^{\prime}(\upsilon)\,-\,W^{\prime}(u)\big)\,dx\;\leq\;L\,\mu(\varepsilon)\int_{0}^{1}|\upsilon-u|\,dx=L\,\mu(\varepsilon)\,\left\|\mathrm{v}\right\|_{1}.

Consequently, by (
4.81
)-(
4.82
) we obtain that

(4.83)

\displaystyle\delta(\varepsilon)\,\varepsilon^{2}\,\mathrm{v}_{xxx}(1,t)\,\mathrm{v}(1,t)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{L^{2}\,\mu(\varepsilon)}{4\epsilon}\,\left\|\mathrm{v}\right\|_{1}^{2}\,+\,\epsilon\,\mu(\varepsilon)\,\left\|\mathrm{v}_{x}\right\|_{1}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{L^{2}\,\mu(\varepsilon)}{4\epsilon}\,\left\|\mathrm{v}\right\|^{2}\,+\,\epsilon\,\mu(\varepsilon)\,\left\|\mathrm{v}_{x}\right\|^{2}

for an arbitrary positive
\epsilon<1.

As for the second term in the rhs of (
4.79
), again we use (
4.80
) to see that

(4.84)

\displaystyle\delta(\varepsilon)\int_{0}^{1}\big(W^{\prime}(u)\,-\,W^{\prime}(v)\big)\,\mathrm{v}_{xx}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\delta(\varepsilon)\,L\,\int_{0}^{1}\big|u-v\big|\,|\mathrm{v}_{xx}|\,dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{L^{2}\,\delta(\varepsilon)}{4\epsilon}\,\left\|\mathrm{v}\right\|^{2}\,+\,\epsilon\,\delta(\varepsilon)\,\left\|\mathrm{v}_{xx}\right\|^{2},

and for the last term in (
4.79
), estimate (
4.80
) yield the bound

(4.85)

\mu(\varepsilon)\int_{0}^{1}\big(W^{\prime}(u)\,-\,W^{\prime}(v)\big)\,\mathrm{v}\,dx\;\leq\;\mu(\varepsilon)\,L\,\int_{0}^{1}\big|u-v\big|\,|\mathrm{v}|\,dx\;=\;\mu(\varepsilon)\,L\,\left\|\mathrm{v}\right\|^{2}.

We apply (
4.83
), (
4.84
), (
4.85
) into (
4.79
) to obtain

(4.86)

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|\mathrm{v}\|^{2}\;+\;\delta(\varepsilon)\,(\varepsilon^{2}-\epsilon)\,\|\mathrm{v}_{xx}\|^{2}\;+\;\mu(\varepsilon)\,(\varepsilon^{2}-\epsilon)\,\|\mathrm{v}_{x}\|^{2}\leq\left(\frac{L^{2}\,\mu(\varepsilon)}{4\epsilon}+\frac{L^{2}\,\delta(\varepsilon)}{4\epsilon}+\mu(\varepsilon)\,L\right)\,\left\|\mathrm{v}\right\|^{2}.

Therefore

(4.87)

\frac{d}{dt}\|\mathrm{v}\|^{2}\leq c\,\left\|\mathrm{v}\right\|^{2},\qquad 0\leq t\leq T,

for some constant
c
depending on
\delta(\varepsilon),\mu(\varepsilon),T,
but independent of
t
and integrating with respect to

we obtain

\left\|\mathrm{v}(\cdot,t)\right\|^{2}\leq e^{ct}\,\left\|\mathrm{v}(\cdot,0)\right\|^{2}\;=\;0,\qquad 0\leq t\leq T,

that is

so the solution of (

)-(
BC1
)-(
BC2
)-(
MC
) is unique.

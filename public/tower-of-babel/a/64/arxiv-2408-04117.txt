The dynamics of the Hesse derivative on the j j -invariant
Jake Kettinger

Abstract
In this paper, we study the Hesse derivative of a cubic curve on the set of j j -invariants, which can be viewed as a rational function on the Riemann sphere. We then analyze the dynamics of this rational function, including counting the number of orbits of a given size. We proceed to investigate when a cubic curve is isomorphic to its n n -fold Hesse derivative, showing that when an elliptic curve has a j j -invariant that is periodic under this rational function, the curve itself must be periodic under the Hesse derivative. We finish with some data collected comparing the sizes of the orbits of elliptic curves to those of their j j -invariants, and some further questions about this dynamical system.

Lemma 2

Proposition 4
.

Let

be a PCF rational function and

Proof.

First we will demonstrate that if the multiplicity of

is greater than 1, then
\left.\displaystyle\frac{\mathsf{d}}{\mathsf{d}z}F^{n}(z)\right|_{z=\phi}=1

and
\left.\displaystyle\frac{\mathsf{d}}{\mathsf{d}z}[F^{n}(z)-z]\right|_{z=\phi}=0
(by definition of multiplicity), then
\left.\displaystyle\frac{\mathsf{d}}{\mathsf{d}z}F^{n}(z)\right|_{z=\phi}-\left.\displaystyle\frac{\mathsf{d}}{\mathsf{d}z}z\right|_{z=\phi}=0
, so
\left.\displaystyle\frac{\mathsf{d}}{\mathsf{d}z}F^{n}(z)\right|_{z=\phi}=1
.

Now note that
\displaystyle\frac{\mathsf{d}}{\mathsf{d}z}F^{n}(z)=\displaystyle\prod_{i=0}^{n-1}F^{\prime}(F^{i}(z))
by the chain rule. So in the case of the fixed point

\left.\frac{\mathsf{d}}{\mathsf{d}z}F^{n}(z)\right|_{z=\phi}=\prod_{i=0}^{n-1}F^{\prime}(F^{i}(\phi)).

Because

, the set

. Therefore the product above is the multiplier for the cycle of

This contradicts Corollary 14.5 of

Let

Definition 9

The

Theorem 1

Definition 11

Let
C=V(f)\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{2}
be a curve of degree

(or

\mathfrak{P}_{Q}(C)=V(Q_{0}\cdot f_{x}+Q_{1}\cdot f_{y}+Q_{2}\cdot f_{z}).

Lemma 5

Given a curve

of degree
d
and any
Q\in\mathbb{P}^{2}
and any

, one has

A(\mathfrak{P}_{Q}(C))=\mathfrak{P}_{AQ}(AC).

Proof.

Note again that
\mathfrak{P}_{Q}(C)
is the unique degree
d-1
curve whose intersection with
C
is exactly the points of
C
whose tangent lines contain
Q
, counting with multiplicity. In other words,

\mathfrak{P}_{Q}(C).C=P_{1}+P_{2}+\cdots+P_{d(d-1)}

where

\{Q\}=\bigcap_{i=1}^{d(d-1)}T_{P_{i}}(C).

Note that in some cases, like if
Q\in C
, we can have
P_{i}=P_{j}
for
i\neq j
.

Since automorphisms preserve incidence and linearity, we have

A(\mathfrak{P}_{Q}(C)).AC=AP_{1}+AP_{2}+\cdots+AP_{d(d-1)}

where

\{AQ\}=\bigcap_{i=1}^{d(d-1)}T_{AP_{i}}(AC).

Thus
A(\mathfrak{P}_{Q}(C))
is a degree
d-1
curve whose intersection with
AC
is exactly the points whose tangent lines contain
AQ
. Since
\mathfrak{P}_{AQ}(AC)
is the unique curve with such properties, we must have
A(\mathfrak{P}_{Q}(C))=\mathfrak{P}_{AQ}(AC)
.
∎

Lemma 6
.

Given a curve
C\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{2}
and an automorphism

\text{\bngx H}AC=A\text{\bngx H}C

Proof.

Note that
\text{\bngx H}C
satisfies

\text{\bngx H}C=\{Q\in\mathbb{P}^{2}:\mathfrak{P}^{(2)}_{Q}(C)\text{ is reducible}\},

see Section 1.1.4 of
[
7
]
.
By the previous lemma,
A(\mathfrak{P}_{Q}(C))=\mathfrak{P}_{AQ}(AC)
, so automorphisms commute with finding the polar curve. Thus we have
A(\mathfrak{P}^{(2)}_{Q}(C))=\mathfrak{P}^{(2)}_{AQ}(AC)
. So if
Q\in\text{\bngx H}C
, then
\mathfrak{P}^{(2)}_{Q}(C)
is reducible and so
\mathfrak{P}^{(2)}_{AQ}(AC)=A(\mathfrak{P}^{(2)}_{Q}(C))
is reducible. Thus

, so

. A similar argument works for the reverse containment. Thus we have equality.
∎

Further Work and Questions

There is a known connection between the
j
-invariant of an elliptic curve
E
and its endomorphism ring
\text{End}(E)
. For example, it is shown in Theorem 4.3 of Chapter II of
[
14
]
that for an elliptic curve
E
with
j
-invariant
j(E)\in\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
,
[\mathbb{Q}(j(E)):\mathbb{Q}]=h_{K}
, where
h_{K}
is the class number of the field
K=\text{frac}(\text{End}(E))
. This brings us to the following question.

Question 2
.

Can we classify all elliptic curves
E
for which
\emph{End}(E)=\emph{End}(\text{\bngx H}E)
?

It is worth noting that an elliptic curve
E
is not necessarily isogenous to its Hesse derivative over
\mathbb{Q}
, even when
j(E)
has finite orbit over
H
. For example, the curve
E=V(y^{2}z-x^{3}-9xz^{2})
has
j
-invariant
1728
and has Hesse derivative
\text{\bngx H}E=V(24xy^{2}+216x^{2}z-648z^{3})=V(xy^{2}+9x^{2}z-27z^{3})
, which is
\mathbb{Q}
-isomorphic to
V(y^{2}z-x^{3}+3xz^{2})
under the map
x\mapsto z
,
y\mapsto y
,
z\mapsto x/3
. We can see then that
E
has isogeny class
576.c
and
\text{\bngx H}E
has isogeny class
576.i
in the L-functions and modular forms database
[
10
]
. We do however have
\text{End}(E)=\text{End}(\text{\bngx H}E)=\mathbb{Z}
, and
\text{End}(E_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}})=\text{End}(\text{\bngx H}E_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}})=\mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{-1}]
.

It is also interesting to further investigate the dynamics of the function
H

Question 3

For which
j\in\mathbb{Q}
and
q

q

Experimental evidence computed in Maple appears to corroborate the following conjecture, which comes from a collaboration between the author and Chris Peterson, but their proofs (if they exist) remain elusive.

Conjecture 1
.

Let
j\in\mathbb{R}
be generic and consider the sequence
\{H^{n}(j)\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
. Define

\displaystyle L_{n}

\displaystyle=\#\{H^{i}(j):i\leq n,H^{i}(j)<0\},

\displaystyle M_{n}

\displaystyle=\#\{H^{i}(j):i\leq n,0<H^{i}(j)<6912\},

\displaystyle R_{n}

\displaystyle=\#\{H^{i}(j):i\leq n,6912<H^{i}(j)\}.

Then

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{L_{n}}{n}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{M_{n}}{n}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{R_{n}}{n}=\frac{1}{3}.

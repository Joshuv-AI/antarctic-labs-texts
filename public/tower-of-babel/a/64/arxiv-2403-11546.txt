Norm-induced Cuts: Optimization with Lipschitzian Black-box Functions
Adrian Gö β ∗ \beta^{\ast} , Alexander Martin , Sebastian Pokutta , Kartikey Sharma

Abstract
. In this paper, we consider a finite dimensional optimization problem minimizing a continuous objective on a compact domain subject to a multi-dimensional constraint function. For the latter, we only assume the availability of a Lipschitz property. In recent literature methods based on non-convex outer approximation are proposed for tackling one dimensional equality constraints on bounded polyhedral domains, which are Lipschitz with respect to the maximum norm. To the best of our knowledge, however, there exists no non-convex outer approximation method for a general problem class. We introduce a meta-level solution framework to solve such problems and tackle the underlying theoretical foundations. Considering the feasible domain without the constraint function as manageable, our method relaxes the multidimensional constraint and iteratively refines the feasible region by means of norm-induced cuts, relying on an oracle for the resulting sub-problems. We show the method’s correctness and investigate the problem complexity. In order to account for discussions about functionality, limits, and extensions, we present computational examples including illustrations.

In terms of applications, our approach in this paper is particularly well suited for non-linear optimization problems which use surrogate models as constraints along with simpler, e.g., convex, domains.

To the best of our knowledge, there exists no extended approach to handle the posed general problem classes,

We are also not restricted to a particular norm, as long as the respective Lipschitz property is available and the norm is monotonous.

the function in question is defined on a lower dimensional subspace of the feasible set.
The combination of these two extension is also treatable.
A formalization of this is given below.
\par\begin{remark}If $r=(r_{p})_{p=1,\dots,R}$ for $R\in andLipschitzconstants$L_p > 0$areavailableforthefunctions$r_p : \Omega\rightarrow{m_p}$,$m_p \in, we can re-write \lx@cref{refnum}{original} as
\begin{equation}\begin{aligned} \min_{x}\ &f(x)&\\
\text{s.t.}\ &r_{p}(x)\leq\textup{{0}},&\text{for }p=1,\dots,R,\\
&x\in\Omega.\end{aligned}\end{equation}Then, we can perform \lx@cref{refnum}{alg:nic} analogously by checking for each $r_{p}(x^{k})\leq\textup{{0}}$ before step\penalty\ \lx@cref{refnum}{step:nic_ret_sol}.

\begin{equation*}\lVert x-x^{k}\rVert\geq\lVert r_{p}(x^{k})_{+}\rVert/L_{p}.\end{equation*}One can see that the proofs above hold equally for such a procedure.
We visualize this case on a specific instance in \lx@cref{creftypecap\lx@tilde refnum}{ex:berthold}.
\par Apart from that, if $r$ in \lx@cref{refnum}{original} maps from a lower dimensional subset of $\Omega$ into the $m$,
we can re-write $x=(x^{\prime},x^{\prime\prime})\in\Omega=\Omega^{\prime}\times\Omega^{\prime\prime}$ with $\dim(\Omega^{\prime}),\,\dim(\Omega^{\prime\prime})\geq 1$, $\dim(\Omega^{\prime})+\dim(\Omega^{\prime\prime})=n$, and $r:\Omega^{\prime}\rightarrow m$.

\begin{equation*}\lVert x^{\prime}-(x^{k})^{\prime}\rVert\geq\lVert r((x^{k})^{\prime})_{+}\rVert/L,\end{equation*}with an appropriate norm for $\Omega^{\prime}$.
This can lead to faster convergence, as cuts are added in a possible smaller subspace $\Omega^{\prime}$.
\par In conclusion, one can even combine several inequalities depending on different subvectors of $x$.
The only necessary condition is the availability of respective Lipschitz constants and norms.
\end{remark}\par We showed that \lx@cref{refnum}{alg:nic} works correctly when we have a global solution method for\penalty\ \lx@cref{refnum}{kth-relaxed-prob}.
But the question arises whether the algorithm still fulfills the desired behavior when only a local solution method is available.
In the following, we verify that if only local solutions can be found in step\penalty\ \lx@cref{refnum}{step:nic_solve}, a point returned in step\penalty\ \lx@cref{refnum}{step:nic_ret_sol} is indeed a locally optimal point.
\par\begin{theorem}Let \lx@cref{refnum}{original} be feasible, i.e., $Q\neq\emptyset$, and let step\penalty\ \lx@cref{refnum}{step:nic_solve} produce a locally optimal point $x^{k}$ for \lx@cref{refnum}{kth-relaxed-prob}, $k\in 0$.

\end{theorem}\par\@proof Assume that \lx@cref{refnum}{alg:nic} stops in step\penalty\ \lx@cref{refnum}{step:nic_ret_sol} in iteration $k\in 0$.

\begin{equation*}f(x^{k})=\min_{x\in B_{\varepsilon}(x^{k})\cap Q_{k}}f(x).\end{equation*}By \lx@cref{creftypecap\lx@tilde refnum}{thm:feas} $x^{k}\in Q$ and, thus, $x^{k}\in Q\cap B_{\varepsilon}(x^{k})$.
We infer
\begin{equation*}\min_{x\in Q\cap B_{\varepsilon}(x^{k})}f(x)\leq f(x^{k}).\end{equation*}Further, the result from \lx@cref{creftypecap\lx@tilde refnum}{lem:relax} implies $Q\cap B_{\varepsilon}(x^{k})\subseteq Q_{k}\cap B_{\varepsilon}(x^{k})$.

\begin{equation*}f(x^{k})=\min_{x\in Q_{k}\cap B_{\varepsilon}(x^{k})}f(x)\leq\min_{x\in Q\cap B_{\varepsilon}(x^{k})}f(x),\end{equation*}and, thus, conclude that
\begin{equation*}\min_{x\in Q\cap B_{\varepsilon}(x^{k})}f(x)=f(x^{k}).\end{equation*}Therefore, $x^{k}$ is a local optimum for \lx@cref{refnum}{original} and the proof is complete.
\qed\end@proof

\par We have introduced the \lx@cref{refnum}{alg:nic} method in order to tackle the abstract problem class\penalty\ \lx@cref{refnum}{original}.
Besides a continuous objective on a compact domain, the method only requires a Lipschitz property of the constraint functions.
Leveraging an oracle for the global solution of \lx@cref{refnum}{kth-relaxed-prob},
we have proven the correctness of \lx@cref{refnum}{alg:nic} in terms of returning an optimal solution when terminating or,
if not, creating a sequence whose accumulation points are all optimal.
This has been complemented by discussions about the termination bound in the infeasible case and the problem complexity, showing asymptotic convergence of the bounds in the latter.
The theoretical content has been supplemented by examples, illustrating the method, visualizing the possible effects of a local oracle, and demonstrating the benefit of knowing Lipschitz constants for the components of the constraint function.
\par While there exist optimization software like Gurobi\penalty\ \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{Gurobi801}{}{}]}
which successfully tackle quadratic constraint problems,
until recently they struggled when facing general non-linear constraints.
Our approach enables such software to solve even more general problems by executing \lx@cref{refnum}{alg:nic} using them as an oracle.
\par As observed especially in the examples, the magnitude of the radius of the norm-induced cuts depends on the Lipschitz constant $L$ in the denominator and is crucial.
If an evaluation of the constraint function is cheap, one can compute an approximation of the point-dependent Lipschitz constant and, thus, reduce its value.
Apart from that, applications with small $L$ seem reasonable.
For example, the field of optimal control of power networks
include sine or cosine constraints
with small $L$ and, thus, could be tackled by \lx@cref{refnum}{alg:nic}.
This gives rise to another interesting topic of research.

\par If the norm-induced cuts \lx@cref{refnum}{eq:def-nic} are defined with respect to the 1-norm or the infinity norm (over $or$), then we can formulate the relaxed problem as a mixed-integer linear problem using binary variables.
For simplicity, consider a cut of the form
$$\|x-a\|_{p}\geq b,$$where $p=1$ or $\infty$.\\
{Case $p=1$:} The cut then is equivalent to
$$\sum_{i=1}^{n}|x_{i}-a_{i}|\geq b.$$By introduction of continuous variables $y_{i}$ and binary variables $z_{i}$ this constraint can be equivalently expressed as
$$\begin{aligned} &\sum_{i=1}^{n}y_{i}\geq b,\\
&y_{i}\geq(x_{i}-a_{i})-Mz_{i},\\
&y_{i}\leq(x_{i}-a_{i})+Mz_{i},\\
&y_{i}\geq-(x_{i}-a_{i})-M(1-z_{i}),\\
&y_{i}\leq-(x_{i}-a_{i})+M(1-z_{i}),\\
&z_{i}\in\{0,1\},\\
&y_{i}\geq 0,\end{aligned}$$where all except for the first inequality are given for all $i\in[n]$ and $M>0$ large.
In order to show this equivalence of both models, on the one hand, observe that if $z_{i}=0$ the respective constraints simplify to
$$\begin{aligned} &y_{i}\geq(x_{i}-a_{i}),\\
&y_{i}\leq(x_{i}-a_{i}),\\
&y_{i}\geq-(x_{i}-a_{i})-M,\\
&y_{i}\leq-(x_{i}-a_{i})+M,\end{aligned}$$for all $i\in[n]$, where the first two constraints ensure that $y_{i}=x_{i}-a_{i}$ and the second two constraints impose no restriction on $y_{i}$ for $M>0$ sufficiently large.
On the other hand, if $z_{i}=1$, then we have
$$\begin{aligned} &y_{i}\geq(x_{i}-a_{i})-M,\\
&y_{i}\leq(x_{i}-a_{i})+M,\\
&y_{i}\geq-(x_{i}-a_{i}),\\
&y_{i}\leq-(x_{i}-a_{i}),\end{aligned}$$for all $i\in[n]$, so $y_{i}=-(x_{i}-a_{i})$ is enforced.
The last set of constraints $y_{i}\geq 0$ then ensures that variable $y_{i}$ is assigned with the positive value, resulting in $y_{i}=\lvert x_{i}-a_{i}\rvert$.
In conclusion, $\sum_{j=1}^{n}y_{i}\geq b$ is equivalent to the cut for the 1-norm.\\
{Case $p=\infty$:} The cut then is equivalent to
$$\max_{i=1,\dots,n}|x_{i}-a_{i}|\geq b.$$In contrast to the first case, we have to introduce continuous variables $w_{i}$ and binary variables $u_{i}$ {additionally} in order to cope with the maximum formulation.
Then the cut can be equivalently expressed as
$$\begin{aligned} &\sum_{i=1}^{n}w_{i}\geq b,\\
&w_{i}\leq y_{i}+M(1-u_{i}),\\
&0\leq w_{i}\leq Mu_{i},\\
&\sum_{i=1}^{n}u_{i}=1,\\
&\sum_{j=1}^{n}w_{j}\geq y_{i}\\
&y_{i}\geq(x_{i}-a_{i})-Mz_{i},\\
&y_{i}\leq(x_{i}-a_{i})+Mz_{i},\\
&y_{i}\geq-(x_{i}-a_{i})-M(1-z_{i}),\\
&y_{i}\leq-(x_{i}-a_{i})+M(1-z_{i}),\\
&z_{i}\in\{0,1\},\\
&u_{i}\in\{0,1\},\\
&y_{i}\geq 0,\end{aligned}$$for all $i\in[n]$.
As in the case before, the variables $y_{i}$, $z_{i}$ and respective constraints ensure that $y_{i}=\lvert x_{i}-a_{i}\rvert$.
For the maximum, observe that $u_{i^{*}}$ is enforced to be nonzero, i.e., 1, for exactly one $i^{*}\in[n]$.
This also implies that $u_{i}$ and due to the big-$M$ formulation $w_{i}$ are zero for $i\in[n]\setminus\{i^{*}\}$.
Regarding $w_{i^{*}}$ we note that the upper bound supposed to be taking effect is by the second inequality, reducing to $w_{i^{*}}\leq y_{i^{*}}$.
That is, exactly one $w_{i}$ is nonzero and upper bounded by $y_{i}$.
In addition with $\sum_{j=1}^{n}w_{j}\geq y_{i}$ for $i\in[n]$ we conclude that $w_{i^{*}}$ takes exactly the maximum of all $y_{i}$, all other $w_{i}$ being zero.
This shows the equivalence of modeling.
\par Note that the value of $M>0$ can always be bounded by a diameter of the feasible region or an upper bound to this value respectively.

Mittag-Leffler Operator Connected with Certain Subclasses of Bazilevic̆ functions
Om Ahuja, Asena Çetinkaya, Naveen Kumar Jain

Abstract
. In this paper, we introduce a new generalized class of analytic functions involving the Mittag-Leffler operator and Bazilevic̆ functions. We examine inclusion properties, radius problems and an application of the generalized Bernardi-Libera-Livingston integral operator for this function class.

Introduction

Let
\mathcal{A}
be the family of all functions of the form

f(z)=z+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n},

(1.1)

that are analytic in the open unit disc
\mathbb{D}:=\{z:|z|<1\}
. Denote by
\mathcal{S}
the subfamily of
\mathcal{A}
consisting of functions that are univalent in
{\mathbb{D}}
. Let
\mathcal{K}
,
\mathcal{S}^{\ast},
and
\mathcal{C}
be the well-known subclasses of
\mathcal{S}
consisting of functions that are, respectively, convex, starlike (w.r.t. the origin) and close-to-convex in
{\mathbb{D}}
. The class
{\mathcal{P}}
of the functions
p
that are analytic in
{\mathbb{D}}
and satisfy the conditions
p(0)=1
and
\RE p(z)>0
in
{\mathbb{D}}
is also well-known in the theory of the univalent functions. For definitions, properties and history of these classes, one may refer to a survey article by the first author
[
3
]
and
[
12
]
. Recently, Ali
et al.

[
5
]
and Anand
et al.

[
7
]
studied these classes to find various radius problems.

The Mittag-Leffler function
E_{\alpha}

E_{\alpha}(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{z^{n}}{\Gamma(\alpha n+1)},\quad(\alpha\in{\mathbb{C}};\RE(\alpha)>0;z\in{\mathbb{C}})

(1.2)

was introduced in 1903 by G.M. Mittag-Leffler
[
18
,
19
]
in connection with his method of summation of some divergent series. A general form of this special function (
1.2
) given by

E_{\alpha,\beta}(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{z^{n}}{\Gamma(\alpha n+\beta)},\quad(\alpha,\beta\in{\mathbb{C}};\RE(\alpha)>0;\RE(\beta)>0;z\in{\mathbb{C}})

(1.3)

was studied by Wiman
[
30
]
in 1905. During this last twenty-five years, interest in Mittag-Leffler type functions (
1.2
) and (
1.3
) have significantly increased among engineers and scientists due to their applications in numerous applied problems, such as fluid flows, diffusive transport skin to diffusion, electric networks, probability, and statistically distribution theory. For detailed account of various properties and references related to applications one may refer to

,

,

recently studied certain applications of generalized Mittag-Leffler operator involving differential subordination. Moreover, Jain
et al.

[

]
studied certain unified integrals involving a multivariate Mittag-Leffler function.

Corresponding to the function
E_{\alpha,\beta}
, Elhaddad
et al.

[
11
]
introduced the Mittag-Leffler linear operator
\mathcal{E}^{m}_{\lambda,\alpha,\beta}f:\mathcal{A}\rightarrow\mathcal{A}
given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{m}_{\lambda,\alpha,\beta}f(z)

\displaystyle=z+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{\Gamma(\beta)(1+(n-1)\lambda)^{m}}{\Gamma(\alpha(n-1)+\beta)}a_{n}z^{n},

(1.4)

where
m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}=\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
,
\lambda\geq 0
,
\alpha,\beta\in{\mathbb{C}}
,
\RE(\alpha)>0
,
\RE(\beta)>0
, and when
\RE(\alpha)=0
, then
\beta\neq 0
. From (
1.4
), the following recurrence formula can be easily obtained:

\mathcal{E}^{m+1}_{\lambda,\alpha,\beta}f(z)=(1-\lambda)\mathcal{E}^{m}_{\lambda,\alpha,\beta}f(z)+\lambda z(\mathcal{E}^{m}_{\lambda,\alpha,\beta}f(z))^{\prime},

(1.5)

where
m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,
\lambda\geq 0
. For suitable values of the parameters
m,\alpha,\beta
, and
\lambda
, we may get several linear operators; for example

1.

For
\alpha=0
and
\beta=1
, we get Al-Oboudi operator
[
6
]
.

2.

For
\alpha=0
,
\beta=1
and
\lambda=1
, we get Sălăgean operator
[
27
]
.

3.

For
m=0
and
\lambda=1
, we get the operator

\mathbb{E}_{\alpha,\beta}(z)=z\Gamma(\beta)E_{\alpha,\beta}(z)=z+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{\Gamma(\beta)}{\Gamma(\alpha(n-1)+\beta)}z^{n}.

Let us denote by
\mathcal{B}(\vartheta,\tau,g,p)
or briefly denote by
\mathcal{B}
, a class of functions
f\in\mathcal{A}
for which
p\in{\mathcal{P}}
,
g\in\mathcal{S}^{\ast}
and real numbers
\vartheta,\tau
with
\vartheta>0
such that

f(z)=\bigg[(\vartheta+i\tau)\int_{0}^{z}p(t)g(t)^{\vartheta}t^{i\tau-1}dt\bigg]^{1/(\vartheta+i\tau)},

(1.6)

where powers are taken as principal values. Bazilevic̆
[
9
]
proved that
\mathcal{B}\subset\mathcal{S}
. In fact, it is known that
\mathcal{K}\subset\mathcal{S}^{\ast}\subset\mathcal{C}\subset\mathcal{B}\subset\mathcal{S}
. For
\vartheta>0
and
\rho<1
, we define

\mathcal{B}_{1}(\vartheta,\rho)=\bigg\{f\in\mathcal{A}:\RE\bigg(\frac{zf^{\prime}(z)}{f^{1-\vartheta}(z)g^{\vartheta}(z)}\bigg)>\rho,z\in{\mathbb{D}}\bigg\}.

For
g(z)\equiv z
, we obtain

\mathcal{B}_{2}(\vartheta,\rho)=\bigg\{f\in\mathcal{A}:\RE\bigg(\frac{zf^{\prime}(z)}{f(z)}\bigg(\frac{f(z)}{z}\bigg)^{\vartheta}\bigg)>\rho,z\in{\mathbb{D}}\bigg\},

\mathcal{B}_{3}(\rho):=\mathcal{B}_{2}(0,\rho)=\bigg\{f\in\mathcal{A}:\RE\bigg(\frac{zf^{\prime}(z)}{f(z)}\bigg)>\rho,z\in{\mathbb{D}}\bigg\}=\mathcal{S}^{\ast}(\rho),

\mathcal{B}_{4}(\rho):=\mathcal{B}_{2}(1,\rho)=\bigg\{f\in\mathcal{A}:\RE\big(f^{\prime}(z)\big)>\rho,z\in{\mathbb{D}}\bigg\}={\mathcal{P}}^{\prime}(\rho).

In view of (
1.6
), Singh
[
28
]
observed that
\mathcal{B}_{1}(\vartheta,0)
,
\mathcal{B}_{2}(\vartheta,0)
,
\mathcal{B}_{3}(0)=\mathcal{S}^{\ast}
, and
\mathcal{B}_{4}(0)={\mathcal{P}}^{\prime}
are subclasses of
\mathcal{B}
. For further details, one may refer to
[

,
25
]
.

{\mathcal{P}}_{k}(\rho)

\int^{2\pi}_{0}\bigg|\frac{\RE p(z)-\rho}{1-\rho}\bigg|d\theta\leq k\pi,

(1.7)

and
0\leq\rho<1

Lemma 1.1
.

[
22
]
If
p\in{\mathcal{P}}_{k}(\rho)
, then

p(z)=\frac{1}{2}\int^{2\pi}_{0}\frac{1+(1-2\rho)ze^{-i\theta}}{1-ze^{-i\theta}}d\mu(\theta),

where
\mu(\theta)
is a function with bounded variation on
[0,2\pi]
such that

\int^{2\pi}_{0}d\mu(\theta)=2\pi\quad\text{and}\quad\int^{2\pi}_{0}|d\mu(\theta)|\leq k\pi.

From (
1.7
), it is observed that
p\in{\mathcal{P}}_{k}(\rho)
if and only if there exists
p_{1},p_{2}\in{\mathcal{P}}(\rho)
such that (see
[
20
]
)

p(z)=\left(\frac{k}{4}+\frac{1}{2}\right)p_{1}(z)-\left(\frac{k}{4}-\frac{1}{2}\right)p_{2}(z),\quad(z\in{\mathbb{D}}).

(1.8)

We note that for
\rho=0
, we obtain the class
{\mathcal{P}}_{k}(0):={\mathcal{P}}_{k}
defined by Pinchuk
[
23
]
. For
k=2
, we get
{\mathcal{P}}_{2}(\rho):={\mathcal{P}}(\rho)
the class of analytic functions with positive real part greater than
\rho
, and for
k=2
,
\rho=0
we have the class
{\mathcal{P}}_{2}(0):={\mathcal{P}}
of functions with positive real part.

(1.9)

where

.

In the present investigation, using the Mittag-Leffler operator and Bazilevi˘c functions we introduce a new generalized class of analytic functions. We investigate inclusion properties, radius problems and an application of the generalized Bernardi-Libera-Livingston
integral operator for this function class. We shall also make connections with some earlier works.

We list some preliminary lemmas required for proving our main results.

Lemma 1.7
.

[
17
]
Let
u=u_{1}+iu_{2}
and
v=v_{1}+iv_{2}
, and suppose
\Psi(u,v)
is a complex function satisfying the conditions

i)

\Psi(u,v)
is continuous in a domain
D\subset{\mathbb{C}}^{2}
,

ii)

(1,0)\in D
and
\RE\Psi(1,0)>0
,

iii)

\RE\Psi(iu_{2},v_{1})\leq 0
, whenever
(iu_{2},v_{1})\in D
and
v_{1}\leq-\frac{1+u_{2}^{2}}{2}
.

If
p(z)=1+c_{1}z+c_{2}z^{2}+...
is an analytic function in
{\mathbb{D}}
such that
(p(z),zp^{\prime}(z))\in D
and
\RE\Psi(p(z),zp^{\prime}(z))>0
for
z\in{\mathbb{D}}
, then
\RE p(z)>0
in
{\mathbb{D}}
.

Lemma 1.8
.

[
24
]
If
p
is in
{\mathcal{P}}
, and if
\zeta
is a complex number satisfying
\RE(\zeta)\geq 0,\ \zeta\neq 0
, then
\RE\{p(z)+\zeta zp^{\prime}(z)\}>\rho\ \ (0\leq\rho<1)
implies that

\RE p(z)>\rho+(1-\rho)(2\iota_{1}-1),

where

\iota_{1}=\int_{0}^{1}\big(1+t^{\RE(\zeta)}\big)^{-1}dt,

and where
\iota_{1}
is an increasing function of
\RE(\zeta)
and
1/2\leq\iota_{1}<1
. This estimate cannot be improved in general.

2.
Inclusion Properties

In this section, we examine some inclusion properties for the class
\mathcal{M}^{m,\gamma}_{\lambda,\alpha,\beta}(k,\vartheta,\rho)
.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
\gamma>0
and
f\in\mathcal{M}^{m,\gamma}_{\lambda,\alpha,\beta}(k,\vartheta,\rho)
. Then

\bigg(\frac{\mathcal{E}^{m}_{\lambda,\alpha,\beta}f(z)}{z}\bigg)^{\vartheta}\in{\mathcal{P}}_{k}(\rho_{1}),

(2.1)

where
\rho_{1}
is given by

\rho_{1}=\frac{2\vartheta\rho+\lambda\gamma}{2\vartheta+\lambda\gamma}.

(2.2)

Proof.

Let

\bigg(\frac{\mathcal{E}^{m}_{\lambda,\alpha,\beta}f(z)}{z}\bigg)^{\vartheta}=(1-\rho_{1})p(z)+\rho_{1},

(2.3)

where
p(0)=1
and

p(z)=\left(\frac{k}{4}+\frac{1}{2}\right)p_{1}(z)-\left(\frac{k}{4}-\frac{1}{2}\right)p_{2}(z).

Thus, we obtain

\displaystyle\bigg\{(1-\gamma)\bigg(\frac{\mathcal{E}^{m}_{\lambda,\alpha,\beta}f(z)}{z}\bigg)^{\vartheta}+\gamma\bigg(\frac{\mathcal{E}^{m+1}_{\lambda,\alpha,\beta}f(z)}{z}\bigg)\bigg(\frac{\mathcal{E}^{m}_{\lambda,\alpha,\beta}f(z)}{z}\bigg)^{\vartheta-1}\bigg\}

\displaystyle=(1-\gamma)\big((1-\rho_{1})p(z)+\rho_{1}\big)+\gamma\bigg[\frac{\mathcal{E}^{m+1}_{\lambda,\alpha,\beta}f(z)}{\mathcal{E}^{m}_{\lambda,\alpha,\beta}f(z)}\big((1-\rho_{1})p(z)+\rho_{1}\big)\bigg].

(2.4)

Taking logarithmic differentiation of (
2.3
), we get

\vartheta\bigg[\frac{z(\mathcal{E}^{m}_{\lambda,\alpha,\beta}f(z))^{\prime}}{\mathcal{E}^{m}_{\lambda,\alpha,\beta}f(z)}-1\bigg]=\frac{(1-\rho_{1})zp^{\prime}(z)}{(1-\rho_{1})p(z)+\rho_{1}}.

By using the identity (
1.5
) in the last expression, we obtain

\frac{\mathcal{E}^{m+1}_{\lambda,\alpha,\beta}f(z)}{\mathcal{E}^{m}_{\lambda,\alpha,\beta}f(z)}=\frac{\lambda(1-\rho_{1})zp^{\prime}(z)}{\vartheta\big((1-\rho_{1})p(z)+\rho_{1}\big)}+1.

(2.5)

Substituting (
2.5
) into (
2
) and using (
1.8
), we arrive at

\displaystyle(1-\rho_{1})p(z)+\rho_{1}+\frac{\lambda\gamma(1-\rho_{1})zp^{\prime}(z)}{\vartheta}

\displaystyle\ \ =\left(\frac{k}{4}+\frac{1}{2}\right)\bigg\{(1-\rho_{1})p_{1}(z)+\rho_{1}+\frac{\lambda\gamma(1-\rho_{1})zp_{1}^{\prime}(z)}{\vartheta}\bigg\}

\displaystyle\ \ -\left(\frac{k}{4}-\frac{1}{2}\right)\bigg\{(1-\rho_{1})p_{2}(z)+\rho_{1}+\frac{\lambda\gamma(1-\rho_{1})zp_{2}^{\prime}(z)}{\vartheta}\bigg\}.

Since
f\in\mathcal{M}^{m,\gamma}_{\lambda,\alpha,\beta}(k,\vartheta,\rho)
, it follows that

\bigg\{(1-\rho_{1})p_{i}(z)+\rho_{1}+\frac{\lambda\gamma(1-\rho_{1})zp_{i}^{\prime}(z)}{\vartheta}\bigg\}\in{\mathcal{P}}(\rho),\ \ (0\leq\rho<1;i=1,2).

That is

\frac{1}{1-\rho}\bigg\{(1-\rho_{1})p_{i}(z)+\rho_{1}+\frac{\lambda\gamma(1-\rho_{1})zp_{i}^{\prime}(z)}{\vartheta}-\rho\bigg\}\in{\mathcal{P}}.

(2.6)

To prove the theorem, we will show that
p_{i}\in{\mathcal{P}},\ (i=1,2)
. We form the functional
\Psi(u,v)
by taking
u=u_{1}+iu_{2}
and
v=v_{1}+iv_{2}
such that

\Psi(u,v)=(1-\rho_{1})u+\rho_{1}-\rho+\frac{\lambda\gamma(1-\rho_{1})v}{\vartheta}.

Using (
2.6
), it is easy to see that the first two conditions of Lemma
1.7
are satisfied. To verify the condition (iii), we get

\displaystyle\RE\Psi(iu_{2},v_{1})

\displaystyle=\rho_{1}-\rho+\RE\bigg\{\frac{\lambda\gamma(1-\rho_{1})v_{1}}{\vartheta}\bigg\}

\displaystyle\leq\rho_{1}-\rho-\frac{\lambda\gamma(1-\rho_{1})(1+u_{2}^{2})}{2\vartheta}

\displaystyle=\frac{2\vartheta\rho_{1}-2\vartheta\rho-\lambda\gamma(1-\rho_{1})(1+u_{2}^{2})}{2\vartheta}

\displaystyle=\frac{2\vartheta(\rho_{1}-\rho)-\lambda\gamma(1-\rho_{1})-\lambda\gamma(1-\rho_{1})u^{2}_{2}}{2\vartheta}:=\frac{A+Bu^{2}_{2}}{2C},

where
v_{1}\leq-\frac{1+u_{2}^{2}}{2}
. Now,
\RE\Psi(iu_{2},v_{1})\leq 0
if

\displaystyle A=2\vartheta(\rho_{1}-\rho)-\lambda\gamma(1-\rho_{1})\leq 0,

\displaystyle B=-\lambda\gamma(1-\rho_{1})\leq 0,

\displaystyle C=\vartheta>0.

From
A\leq 0
, we obtain
\rho_{1}
as given by (
2.2
) and
B\leq 0
ensures that
0\leq\rho_{1}<1
. In view of Lemma
1.7
, for
p(z)=p_{i}(z)
,
p_{i}\in{\mathcal{P}}
, where
i=1,2
. Consequently,
p\in{\mathcal{P}}_{k}(\rho_{1})
.
∎

For
m=0
,
\lambda=1
,
\alpha=0
and
\beta=1
, Theorem
2.1
reduces to the following new result.

Corollary 2.2
.

Let
\gamma>0
and
f\in\mathcal{M}^{\gamma}(k,\vartheta,\rho)
. Then

\big(\frac{f(z)}{z}\big)^{\vartheta}\in{\mathcal{P}}_{k}(\rho_{1})
, where
\rho_{1}
is given by

\rho_{1}=\frac{2\vartheta\rho+\gamma}{2\vartheta+\gamma}.

By using the inclusion relation given in Theorem
2.1
, we prove the following theorem.

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
\vartheta>0
and
0\leq\gamma_{1}<\gamma_{2}
. Then
\mathcal{M}^{m,\gamma_{2}}_{\lambda,\alpha,\beta}(k,\vartheta,\rho)\subset\mathcal{M}^{m,\gamma_{1}}_{\lambda,\alpha,\beta}(k,\vartheta,\rho).

Proof.

Let
f\in\mathcal{M}^{m,\gamma_{2}}_{\lambda,\alpha,\beta}(k,\vartheta,\rho)
. Then, we have

H_{2}(z)=\bigg\{(1-\gamma_{2})\bigg(\frac{\mathcal{E}^{m}_{\lambda,\alpha,\beta}f(z)}{z}\bigg)^{\vartheta}+\gamma_{2}\bigg(\frac{\mathcal{E}^{m+1}_{\lambda,\alpha,\beta}f(z)}{z}\bigg)\bigg(\frac{\mathcal{E}^{m}_{\lambda,\alpha,\beta}f(z)}{z}\bigg)^{\vartheta-1}\bigg\}\in{\mathcal{P}}_{k}(\rho).

In view of Theorem
2.1
, we conclude that

\bigg(\frac{\mathcal{E}^{m}_{\lambda,\alpha,\beta}f(z)}{z}\bigg)^{\vartheta}:=H_{1}(z)\in{\mathcal{P}}_{k}(\rho_{1})\subset{\mathcal{P}}_{k}(\rho).

Thus, for
\gamma_{1}\geq 0
,

\begin{split}\bigg\{(1-\gamma_{1})\bigg(\frac{\mathcal{E}^{m}_{\lambda,\alpha,\beta}f(z)}{z}\bigg)^{\vartheta}+\gamma_{1}\bigg(\frac{\mathcal{E}^{m+1}_{\lambda,\alpha,\beta}f(z)}{z}\bigg)\bigg(\frac{\mathcal{E}^{m}_{\lambda,\alpha,\beta}f(z)}{z}\bigg)^{\vartheta-1}\bigg\}\\
=\bigg(1-\frac{\gamma_{1}}{\gamma_{2}}\bigg)H_{1}(z)+\frac{\gamma_{1}}{\gamma_{2}}H_{2}(z).\end{split}

(2.7)

Because the class
{\mathcal{P}}_{k}(\rho)
is a convex set (see
[
20
]
), it follows that the right sides of (
2.7
) belongs to
{\mathcal{P}}_{k}(\rho)
, therefore
f\in\mathcal{M}^{m,\gamma_{1}}_{\lambda,\alpha,\beta}(k,\vartheta,\rho)
.
∎

For
m=0
,
\lambda=1
,
\alpha=0
, and
\beta=1
, Theorem
2.3
reduces to the following inclusion result.

Remark 2.4
.

[
20
]
Let
\vartheta>0
and
0\leq\gamma_{1}<\gamma_{2}
. Then
\mathcal{M}^{\gamma_{2}}(k,\vartheta,\rho)\subset\mathcal{M}^{\gamma_{1}}(k,\vartheta,\rho).

3.
Radius Problem

In this section, we examine certain radius problems.

Theorem 3.1
.

If a function
f\in\mathcal{A}
satisfies

\bigg(\frac{\mathcal{E}^{m}_{\lambda,\alpha,\beta}f(z)}{z}\bigg)^{\vartheta}\in{\mathcal{P}}_{k}(\rho),

(3.1)

then
f\in\mathcal{M}^{m,\gamma}_{\lambda,\alpha,\beta}(k,\vartheta,\rho)
for
|z|<r_{1}
, where

r_{1}=\frac{\lambda\gamma+\vartheta-\sqrt{\lambda^{2}\gamma^{2}+2\lambda\gamma\vartheta}}{\vartheta}.

(3.2)

Proof.

In view of (
3.1
), we have

\bigg(\frac{\mathcal{E}^{m}_{\lambda,\alpha,\beta}f(z)}{z}\bigg)^{\vartheta}=(1-\rho)p(z)+\rho,

(3.3)

where
p\in{\mathcal{P}}_{k}
.

Hence, by using (
1.5
) and (
3.3
), we easily get

\displaystyle\frac{1}{1-\rho}\bigg\{(1-\gamma)\bigg(\frac{\mathcal{E}^{m}_{\lambda,\alpha,\beta}f(z)}{z}\bigg)^{\vartheta}+\gamma\bigg(\frac{\mathcal{E}^{m+1}_{\lambda,\alpha,\beta}f(z)}{z}\bigg)\bigg(\frac{\mathcal{E}^{m}_{\lambda,\alpha,\beta}f(z)}{z}\bigg)^{\vartheta-1}-\rho\bigg\}

\displaystyle=p(z)+\frac{\lambda\gamma zp^{\prime}(z)}{\vartheta}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{k}{4}+\frac{1}{2}\right)\bigg\{p_{1}(z)+\frac{\lambda\gamma zp_{1}^{\prime}(z)}{\vartheta}\bigg\}-\left(\frac{k}{4}-\frac{1}{2}\right)\bigg\{p_{2}(z)+\frac{\lambda\gamma zp_{2}^{\prime}(z)}{\vartheta}\bigg\},

(3.4)

where
p_{1},p_{2}\in{\mathcal{P}}
and
z\in{\mathbb{D}}
.

Now, by using well-known estimates (see
[
12
]
) for the class
{\mathcal{P}}
,

|zp_{i}^{\prime}(z)|\leq\frac{2r\RE p_{i}(z)}{1-r^{2}},\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad

\RE p_{i}(z)\geq\frac{1-r}{1+r},\ \ (|z|<r<1;i=1,2;z\in{\mathbb{D}}),\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad

we have

\displaystyle\RE\bigg\{p_{i}(z)+\frac{\lambda\gamma zp^{\prime}_{i}(z)}{\vartheta}\bigg\}

\displaystyle\geq\RE\bigg\{p_{i}(z)-\frac{\lambda\gamma|zp^{\prime}_{i}(z)|}{\vartheta}\bigg\}

\displaystyle\geq\RE p_{i}(z)\bigg\{1-\frac{2\lambda\gamma r}{\vartheta(1-r^{2})}\bigg\}

\displaystyle=\RE p_{i}(z)\bigg\{\frac{\vartheta(1-r)^{2}-2\lambda\gamma r}{\vartheta(1-r)^{2}}\bigg\}.

The right hand side of the last inequality is positive if
r<r_{1}
, where
r_{1}
is given by (
3.2
). The result is sharp, therefore from (
3
) we prove that
f\in\mathcal{M}^{m,\gamma}_{\lambda,\alpha,\beta}(k,\vartheta,\rho)
for
|z|<r_{1}
.

The sharp function is proven in the following. From (
3.1
), we have

\bigg(\frac{\mathcal{E}^{m}_{\lambda,\alpha,\beta}f(z)}{z}\bigg)^{\vartheta}=p(z)=\left(\frac{k}{4}+\frac{1}{2}\right)p_{1}(z)-\left(\frac{k}{4}-\frac{1}{2}\right)p_{2}(z)\in{\mathcal{P}}_{k}(\rho),

where
p_{i}(z)=\frac{1+(1-2\rho)z}{1-z}\ (i=1,2)
, then clearly we write

(\mathcal{E}^{m}_{\lambda,\alpha,\beta}f(z))^{\vartheta}=z^{\vartheta}p(z).

(3.5)

Differentiating on both sides of the equation (
3.5
), and using (
1.5
) together with routine calculations, we easily get

(1-\gamma)\bigg(\frac{\mathcal{E}^{m}_{\lambda,\alpha,\beta}f(z)}{z}\bigg)^{\vartheta}+\gamma\bigg(\frac{\mathcal{E}^{m+1}_{\lambda,\alpha,\beta}f(z)}{z}\bigg)\bigg(\frac{\mathcal{E}^{m}_{\lambda,\alpha,\beta}f(z)}{z}\bigg)^{\vartheta-1}=p(z)+\frac{\lambda\gamma zp^{\prime}(z)}{\vartheta}\in{\mathcal{P}}_{k}(\rho).

Thus, by applying Hallenbeck and Ruscheweyh
[
14
]
, we observe that

\bigg(\frac{\mathcal{E}^{m}_{\lambda,\alpha,\beta}f(z)}{z}\bigg)^{\vartheta}=\frac{\vartheta}{\lambda\gamma}\int_{0}^{1}\bigg[\left(\frac{k}{4}+\frac{1}{2}\right)u^{\frac{\vartheta}{\lambda\gamma}-1}\frac{1+(1-2\rho)uz}{1-uz}\\
-\left(\frac{k}{4}-\frac{1}{2}\right)u^{\frac{\vartheta}{\lambda\gamma}-1}\frac{1-(1-2\rho)uz}{1+uz}\bigg]du

(3.6)

is the sharp function for
f\in\mathcal{M}^{m,\gamma}_{\lambda,\alpha,\beta}(k,\vartheta,\rho)
. We note that for
m=0
,
\lambda=1
,
\alpha=0
and
\beta=1
, the sharp function given by (
3.6
) reduces to the sharp function given in
[
20
]
.
∎

Putting
m=0
,
\lambda=1
,
\alpha=0
and
\beta=1
, Theorem
3.1
reduces to the following new result.

Corollary 3.2
.

If a function
f\in\mathcal{A}
satisfies
\big(\frac{f(z)}{z}\big)^{\vartheta}\in{\mathcal{P}}_{k}(\rho)
, then
f\in\mathcal{M}^{\gamma}(k,\vartheta,\rho)
for
|z|<r_{2}
, where

r_{2}=\frac{\gamma+\vartheta-\sqrt{\gamma^{2}+2\gamma\vartheta}}{\vartheta}.

Remark 3.3
.

Setting
m=\alpha=0
,
\gamma=\beta=\lambda=\vartheta=1
, and
k=2
in Theorem
3.1
, then
f
is in
\mathcal{M}^{0,1}_{1,0,1}(2,1,\rho)=:\mathcal{B}_{2}^{1}(1,1,1,\rho,1,0)
for
|z|<2-\sqrt{3}\approx 0.2679
, which was given in
[
21
, Theorem 3.4]
.

4.
Application of An Integral Operator

In this section, we consider an application of the generalized Mittag-Leffler operator given by (
1.4
) involving the generalized Bernardi-Libera-Livingston integral operator
\mathcal{L}_{\sigma}:\mathcal{A}\rightarrow\mathcal{A}
given by

\mathcal{L}_{\sigma}f(z)=\frac{\sigma+1}{z^{\sigma}}\int_{0}^{z}t^{\sigma-1}f(t)dt\ \ \ \ (\sigma>-1).

(4.1)

From this operator, we easily get

z(\mathcal{E}^{m}_{\lambda,\alpha,\beta}\mathcal{L}_{\sigma}f(z))^{\prime}=(\sigma+1)\mathcal{E}^{m}_{\lambda,\alpha,\beta}f(z)-\sigma\mathcal{E}^{m}_{\lambda,\alpha,\beta}\mathcal{L}_{\sigma}f(z).

(4.2)

For several special cases of this operator and related operators, one may refer to a survey article by the first two authors
[
4
]
and related references therein.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
f\in\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{L}_{\sigma}f
be given by (
4.1
). If

\bigg\{(1-\gamma)\frac{\mathcal{E}^{m}_{\lambda,\alpha,\beta}\mathcal{L}_{\sigma}f(z)}{z}+\gamma\frac{\mathcal{E}^{m}_{\lambda,\alpha,\beta}f(z)}{z}\bigg\}\in{\mathcal{P}}_{k}(\rho),

(4.3)

then

\frac{\mathcal{E}^{m}_{\lambda,\alpha,\beta}\mathcal{L}_{\sigma}f(z)}{z}\in{\mathcal{P}}_{k}(\iota),\ \ (z\in{\mathbb{D}}),

where
\iota
is given by

\iota=\rho+(1-\rho)(2\iota_{1}-1),\ \ \iota_{1}=\int_{0}^{1}(1+t^{\frac{\gamma}{\sigma+1}})^{-1}dt.

(4.4)

Proof.

Consider the function

\frac{\mathcal{E}^{m}_{\lambda,\alpha,\beta}\mathcal{L}_{\sigma}f(z)}{z}=p(z)=\bigg\{\left(\frac{k}{4}+\frac{1}{2}\right)p_{1}(z)-\left(\frac{k}{4}-\frac{1}{2}\right)p_{2}(z)\bigg\}.

Differentiating both sides, and using (
4.2
) we get

\frac{\mathcal{E}^{m}_{\lambda,\alpha,\beta}f(z)}{z}=p(z)+\frac{zp^{\prime}(z)}{\sigma+1}.

If we use the identity given by (
4.3
), we obtain

(1-\gamma)\frac{\mathcal{E}^{m}_{\lambda,\alpha,\beta}\mathcal{L}_{\sigma}f(z)}{z}+\gamma\frac{\mathcal{E}^{m}_{\lambda,\alpha,\beta}f(z)}{z}=p(z)+\frac{\gamma zp^{\prime}(z)}{\sigma+1}\in{\mathcal{P}}_{k}(\rho).

This implies that

\RE\bigg\{p_{i}(z)+\frac{\gamma zp^{\prime}_{i}(z)}{\sigma+1}\bigg\}>\rho,\ \ (i=1,2).

By using Lemma
1.8
, we see that
\RE\{p_{i}(z)\}>\iota
, where
\iota
is given by (
4.4
). Thus, we arrive at
p\in{\mathcal{P}}_{k}(\iota)
. This completes the proof.
∎

Setting
m=0
,
\lambda=1
,
\alpha=0
, and
\beta=1
in operator
\mathcal{E}^{m}_{\lambda,\alpha,\beta}f
, Theorem
4.1
gives the following result.

Corollary 4.2
.

Let
f\in\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{L}_{\sigma}f
be given by (
4.1
). If

\bigg\{(1-\gamma)\frac{\mathcal{L}_{\sigma}f(z)}{z}+\gamma\frac{f(z)}{z}\bigg\}\in{\mathcal{P}}_{k}(\rho),

then

\frac{\mathcal{L}_{\sigma}f(z)}{z}\in{\mathcal{P}}_{k}(\iota)
, where
\iota
is given by

\iota=\rho+(1-\rho)(2\iota_{1}-1),\ \ \iota_{1}=\int_{0}^{1}(1+t^{\frac{\gamma}{\sigma+1}})^{-1}dt.

Conclusion

We conclude our investigation by remarking that the defined new generalized class of analytic functions involving the Mittag-Leffler operator and Bazilevic̆ functions gives various well known subclasses of Bazilevic̆ functions as particular cases which in turn yields many proved results as corollary. We are investigating the main results to find potentially useful applications in a variety of areas.

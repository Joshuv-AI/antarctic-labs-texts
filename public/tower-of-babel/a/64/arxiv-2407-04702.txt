On the Nonexistence of Centered Co-Circular Central Configurations With Three Unequal masses


Abstract
This paper examines the existence of centered co-circular central configurations in the general power-law potential n n -body problem. We prove the nonexistence of such configurations when the system consists of n − 3 n-3 equal masses and three arbitrary masses, under the condition that the three special masses are distinct or, if two of them are equal, not arranged in a specific manner.

1
Introductions

Central configurations are crucial solutions in the Newtonian
n
-body problem. They naturally arise when searching for self-similar solutions and play a key role in identifying bifurcations in the topology of integral manifolds
[
12
,
11
,
8
,
1
,
2
]
. These configurations also have important applications in astronomy and spacecraft mission design
[
9
]
.

In this work, we focus on central configurations where all bodies lie on a common circle and the center of mass coincides with the center of the circle. These are referred to as
centered co-circular central configurations
.

Any regular polygon with equal masses forms a centered co-circular central configuration
[
10
,
14
]
. When investigating the question:
Do planar choreography solutions exist when the masses are not all equal?
, Chenciner posed a related problem
[
3
]
:
Is the regular polygon with equal masses the unique centered co-circular central configuration?
This question was later included in a well-known list of open problems on the classical
n
-body problem, compiled by Albouy, Cabral, and Santos
[
1
]
. Hampton confirmed the uniqueness for
n=4

[
6
]
, and Wang’s recent research extended the result to
n=5
and
n=6

[
15
]
.

This question is also considered in the general power-law potential
n
-body problem, where the potential is given by
U_{\alpha}=\sum_{i<j}\frac{m_{i}m_{j}}{|q_{i}-q_{j}|^{\alpha}}
.
When
\alpha=1
, the problem corresponds to the Newtonian
n
-body problem, while the limiting case
\alpha=0
corresponds to the
n
-vortex problem. For the limiting case
\alpha=0
, Cors, Hall, and Roberts confirmed Chenciner’s conjecture for any
n

[
5
]
. For
\alpha>0
, Wang provided a positive answer for
n=3
and
n=4

[
15
]
.

Another interesting approach to Chenciner’s question was initiated by Hampton
[
7
]
, where he demonstrated that no centered co-circular central configuration exists if the masses are sufficiently close to the equal mass case, although the exact size of this neighborhood remains unknown. Subsequent efforts have sought to determine this size. Corbera and Valls
[
4
]
showed the nonexistence of centered co-circular central configurations for
n
equal masses plus one arbitrary mass. Our previous result
[
13
]
extended this by proving nonexistence for
n
equal masses plus two arbitrary masses, for two groups of equal masses, and for a group of equal masses combined with a group of heavier (lighter) masses, provided the two groups are arranged in an interlaced manner.

The aim of this paper is to establish the nonexistence of centered co-circular central configurations for
n
equal masses and three arbitrary masses. This nonexistence result holds under the condition that the three special masses are distinct or,
if two are equal, they are not arranged in a specific way (see Theorem
3
).

After the introduction, the paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we briefly present the definitions and necessary lemmas. In Section
3
, we state and prove our main result.

2
Basic settings and lemmas

In this section, we briefly introduction the central
configurations and list some necessary results.

Let
q_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
and
m_{i}
be the position
and mass, respectively, of the
i
-th body. Let
r_{ij}=\left\|q_{i}-q_{j}\right\|
be
the distance between the
i
-th and
j
-th bodies.
The center of mass is given by
c=\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n}m_{i}q_{i}}{\sum_{i=1}^{n}m_{i}}
.

Consider the power-law potentials
U_{\alpha}
of the form

U_{\alpha}=\sum_{i<j}\frac{m_{i}m_{j}}{r_{ij}^{\alpha}},\alpha>0.

A planar
central configuration
is a special set of distinct positions
q_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}

satisfying

\sum_{j\neq i}^{n}\frac{m_{i}m_{j}\left(q_{j}-q_{i}\right)}{r_{ij}^{\alpha+2}}+\frac{\lambda}{\alpha}m_{i}q_{i}=0\text{ for each }i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}

and for some scalar
\lambda
independent of
i
.
Summing up all the equations in the above system yields

\sum_{i}m_{i}q_{i}=0
, i.e., the center of mass of the
central configuration is at the origin.

The term
centered co-circular central configurations
refers to
central configurations that all bodies lie on a common
circle and with
center of mass coincides with the center of
the circle. Let
{\bf{m}}=\left(m_{1},m_{2},\ldots,m_{n}\right)
.
Without lose of generality,
let
q_{i}=\left(\cos\theta_{i},\sin\theta_{i}\right)
, and

0<\theta_{1}<\theta_{2}<\ldots<\theta_{n}\leq 2\pi.

In this way, the mass vector determines the order of the masses on the circle.
We also write the configuration by
\theta=(\theta_{1},\ldots,\theta_{n}).

Note that
r_{jk}=\left|2\sin\frac{\theta_{j}-\theta_{k}}{2}\right|.

Obviously, the set of centered central configurations are invariant under rotations of the circle.
To remove the symmetry, we specify that
\theta_{n}=2\pi
. Let

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{0}=\left\{\mathbf{\theta}:0<\theta_{1}<\theta_{2}<\ldots<\theta_{n}=2\pi\right\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{CC}_{0}=\left\{(\mathbf{\bf m},\theta)\ \mbox{ is a centered co-circular central configuration},\,\theta\in\mathcal{K}_{0}\right\}.

Cors et al. found (
[
5
]
) that a configuration is a centered co-circular central configuration if

\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta_{k}}U_{\alpha}=0,\ \ \sum_{j\neq k}\frac{m_{j}}{r_{jk}^{\alpha}}=\frac{2\lambda}{\alpha},\ k=1,\ldots,n.

Lemma 1

(
[
5
]
)
.

For any
\mathbf{\bf m},
there is a unique point
in
\mathcal{K}_{0}
satisfying
\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta_{k}}U_{\alpha}=0,k=1,\ldots,n.

Moreover, the critical point is a minimum, denoted by
\theta_{\bf m}
.

The dihedral group,
D_{n}
, acts on the set
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}\times\mathcal{K}_{0}

as followes. Denote

P=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccccc}0&1&0&\ldots&0&0\\
0&0&1&\ldots&0&0\\
.&.&.&\ldots&.&.\\
0&0&0&\ldots&0&1\\
1&0&0&\ldots&0&0\end{array}\right),\ \ S=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccccc}0&0&\ldots&0&1&0\\
0&0&\ldots&1&0&0\\
.&.&\ldots&.&.&.\\
1&0&\ldots&0&0&0\\
0&0&\ldots&0&0&1\end{array}\right),

\mathcal{P}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccccc}-1&1&0&\ldots&0&0\\
-1&0&1&\ldots&0&0\\
.&.&.&\ldots&.&.\\
-1&0&0&\ldots&0&1\\
0&0&0&\ldots&0&1\end{array}\right),\ \ \mathcal{S}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccccc}0&0&\ldots&0&-1&1\\
0&0&\ldots&-1&0&1\\
.&.&\ldots&.&.&.\\
-1&0&\ldots&0&0&1\\
0&0&\ldots&0&0&1\end{array}\right).

The action of
D_{n}
on
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}
is by the matrix group
generated by
P,S
, and the action of
D_{n}
on
\mathcal{K}_{0}

is by the matrix group generated by
\mathcal{P},\mathcal{S}
. For
any
g=P^{h}S^{l}\in D_{n},
letting
\hat{g}=\mathcal{P}^{h}\mathcal{S}^{l},
define
the action of
D_{n}
on
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}\times\mathcal{K}_{0}

by

g\cdot(\mathbf{m,}\theta)=(g\mathbf{\bf m},\hat{g}\theta).

Lemma 2
.

[
13
]

Assume that
(\mathbf{m,}\theta_{\mathbf{\bf m}})\in\mathcal{CC}_{0}

is a centered co-circular central configuration, then

1.

For any
g\in D_{n}
,
g\cdot(\mathbf{m,}\theta_{\mathbf{\bf m}})\in\mathcal{CC}_{0}
.

2.

U_{\alpha}(\mathbf{m,}\theta_{\mathbf{\bf m}})=U_{\alpha}(g\mathbf{m,}\hat{g}\theta_{\mathbf{\bf m}})\leq U_{\alpha}(g\mathbf{m,}\theta_{\mathbf{\bf m}})

and
\hat{g}\theta_{\mathbf{\bf m}}=\theta_{g\mathbf{\bf m}}.

3.

\mathbf{m=}g\mathbf{\bf m}
implies
\hat{g}\theta_{\mathbf{\bf m}}=\theta_{\mathbf{\bf m}}
.

Consider the symmetric matrix
H_{\mathbf{{\bf m}}}
, which is determined
by
\mathbf{m}
and the corresponding
\theta_{{\bf m}}
, by
(
H_{{\bf m}})_{ij}
=1/
r_{ij}^{\alpha}
when

i\neq j
, and
(H_{{\bf m}})_{ii}=0.
A direct
consequence of Lemma
2
is the following.

Lemma 3
.

[
13
]

Given
\mathbf{m}
and the corresponding
\theta_{\bf m}
,
if there is some
g\in D_{n}
such that
H_{\mathbf{\bf m}}(g\mathbf{m}-\mathbf{m})<0
,
then
(\mathbf{m},\theta_{\bf m})\notin\mathcal{CC}_{0}
.

When investigating the sign of
H_{\mathbf{\bf m}}(g\text{{m}}-\mathbf{m})
,
the following fact is useful.

Lemma 4
.

[

]

Suppose that four points
A, B, C, D, are ordered counterclockwise on a circle. See Figure

. Then for
any
\alpha>0,
it holds that

\frac{1}{AC^{\alpha}}+\frac{1}{BD^{\alpha}}-(\frac{1}{AD^{\alpha}}+\frac{1}{BC^{\alpha}})<0.

The following two results will be essential for our discussion:

Theorem 1
.

[
13
]

In the general power
law potential n-body problem, no centered co-circular central configurations exist when precisely
n-2
of the masses are equal.

Theorem 2
.

[

]

In the general power-law potential n-body problem, when masses can be grouped into two sets of equal masses, no centered co-circular central configurations exist unless all masses are equal.

3
Nonexistence of centered co-circular central configurations

In this section, we will establish the following result:

Theorem 3
.

Consider a co-circular configuration
(\mathbf{m},\theta_{\bf m})
of
the general power-law potential
n
-body problem. Assume all but three masses are equal and that the three special masses are not ordered symmetrically. Then this configuration cannot be a centered co-circular central configuration.

The term
special mass
refers to one of the three unequal masses. We say that three masses are
ordered symmetrically
in a co-circular configuration if two of the masses are equal, and the number of masses between each of the two equal masses and the third mass (counted along the circle) is the same. See Figure
2
.

Figure 2:
A five-body co-circular configuration with
m_{2}=m_{3}
.
We say that the three masses
m_{2},m_{3},m_{5}
are ordered symmetrically.

The idea of the proof is the following:
Consider the corresponding
quadratic form
H_{\bf m}
. If there is a
g\in D_{n}
so that
H_{\bf m}(g\text{{m}}-\mathbf{m})
is negative, then Lemma
3
yields the desired result.

Without lose of generality, assume that the equal masses are 1. Assume
the three special masses are positioned at the
l
-th,
s
-th, and
n
-th locations (
1\leq l<s<n
) respectively, their masses are
m_{l},m_{s},m_{n}
, and these masses are not equal to
1
. Thus, the mass vector is:

\mathbf{m}=(1,\ldots,1,m_{l},1,\ldots,1,m_{s},1,\ldots,1,m_{n})

We start by considering the following specific case:

Proposition 1
.

Consider a co-circular configuration
(\mathbf{m},\theta_{\bf m})
of the general power-law potential
n
-body problem. Suppose that
n
is even, all but three masses are equal, and two special masses
are separated by exactly
\frac{n}{2}-1
masses (counted on the circle). Then
the configuration is not a centered co-circular central configuration.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, assume that
m_{s}
and
m_{n}
are separated by
\frac{n}{2}-1
masses. Then
s=\frac{n}{2}
and
the mass distribution

\mathbf{m}=(1,\ldots,1,m_{l},1,\ldots,1,m_{\frac{n}{2}},1,\ldots,1,m_{n})

Let
g=S
. Then

g\mathbf{m}-\mathbf{m}=(1-m_{l})(0,\ldots,1,0,\ldots,0,-1,0,\ldots,0),

where the nonzero terms are at the
l
-th and
(n-l)
-th positions.
So,

H_{\mathbf{\bf m}}(g\text{{m}}-\mathbf{m})=-2(1-m_{l})^{2}\frac{1}{r^{\alpha}_{l,n-l}}<0,

By Lemma
3
,
(\mathbf{m},\theta_{\bf m})
is not
a centered co-circular central configuration.
∎

Hence, in the following, we assume that there are not two special masses separated by
\frac{n}{2}-1
masses, or equivaently, the following holds:

s-l-\frac{n}{2}\neq 0,\,n-s-\frac{n}{2}\neq 0,\,n-l-\frac{n}{2}\neq 0.

We now establish two critical lemmas to handle the general cases.

Lemma 5
.

Suppose that
(\mathbf{m},\theta_{\bf m})
is a centered co-circular central configuration
of the general power-law potential
n
-body problem.
Assume that all masses equal except three.
If
(m_{l}-1)(m_{s}-1)<0
, then

(s-\frac{n}{2})(l-\frac{n}{2})<0
.

Proof.

Suppose for contradiction that
(s-\frac{n}{2})(l-\frac{n}{2})\geq 0
. Since the masses are not separated by
\frac{n}{2}-1
masses, we assume
s<\frac{n}{2}
. See Figure
3

The mass distribution is

\mathbf{m}=(1,\ldots,1,m_{l},1,\ldots,1,m_{s},1,\ldots,1,m_{n})

Let
g=S

g\mathbf{m}-\mathbf{m}=(0,\ldots,0,1-m_{l},0,\ldots,0,1-m_{s},0,\ldots,0,m_{s}-1,0,\ldots,0,m_{l}-1,0,\ldots,0),

where the nonzero terms are at the
l
-th,
s
-th,
(n-s)
-th, and
(n-l)
-th positions.
So,

\displaystyle H_{\mathbf{\bf m}}(g\text{{m}}-\mathbf{m})

\displaystyle=-\frac{(1-m_{l})^{2}}{r_{l,n-l}^{\alpha}}-\frac{(1-m_{s})^{2}}{r_{s,n-s}^{\alpha}}+

\displaystyle 2(1-m_{l})(m_{s}-1)\left(-\frac{1}{r_{ls}^{\alpha}}+\frac{1}{r_{l,n-s}^{\alpha}}+\frac{1}{r_{s,n-l}^{\alpha}}-\frac{1}{r_{n-s,n-l}^{\alpha}}\right)

\displaystyle<0,

.

(s-\frac{n}{2})(l-\frac{n}{2})<0

.

Lemma 6
.

Suppose that
(\mathbf{m},\theta_{\bf m})
is a centered co-circular central configuration
of the general power-law potential
n
-body problem.

If
(m_{l}-1)(m_{s}-1)>0,

(l-\frac{n}{2})(s-\frac{n}{2})>0

Proof.

Suppose by contradiction that
(s-\frac{n}{2})(l-\frac{n}{2})<0

and
s>\frac{n}{2}
.
There are two cases:

1.
l+s=n

g\mathbf{m}-\mathbf{m}=(0,\ldots,0,m_{s}-m_{l},0,\ldots,0,m_{l}-m_{s},0,\ldots,0),

where the nonzero terms are at the

H_{\mathbf{m}}(g\mathbf{m}-\mathbf{m})=-(m_{l}-m_{s})^{2}\frac{1}{r_{l,s}}<0.

2.
l+s\neq n
. Without lose of generality, assume
\frac{n}{2}-l>s-\frac{n}{2}
. See Figure
3
, right.
Let
g=S
. Then

g\mathbf{m}-\mathbf{m}=(0,\ldots,0,1-m_{l},0,\ldots,0,m_{s}-1,0,\ldots,0,1-m_{s},0,\ldots,0,m_{l}-1,0,\ldots,0),

-th,
(n-s)
-th ,

-th, and
(n-l)
-th positions. Thus,

\displaystyle H_{\mathbf{\bf m}}(g\text{{m}}-\mathbf{m})

\displaystyle=-\frac{(1-m_{l})^{2}}{r_{l,n-l}^{\alpha}}-\frac{(1-m_{s})^{2}}{r_{s,n-s}^{\alpha}}+

.

(s-\frac{n}{2})(l-\frac{n}{2})>0
.
∎

Proof of Theorem

By Theorem

, we assume that the three special
masses are not all equal.
Therefore, in the following, withou any lose of generality,

and Remark

after Lemma

Obviously, no solutions exist because these inequalities are mutually incompatible.

m_{l}<m_{s}<1<m_{n}

(\mathbf{m},\theta_{\mathbf{m}})\in\mathcal{CC}_{0}

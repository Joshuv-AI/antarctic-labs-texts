Introduction

One of the most significant operators in harmonic analysis is the Riesz potential operator
I_{\alpha}
of order
\alpha
, also known as the fractional integral operator. Let
\mathbb{R}^{n}
be the
n
-dimensional Euclidean space endowed with the Lebesgue measure
dx
and the Euclidean norm
|\cdot|
. Given
\alpha\in(0,n)
, the Riesz potential operator
I_{\alpha}
of order
\alpha
is defined by

I_{\alpha}f(x):=\frac{1}{\gamma(\alpha)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{f(y)}{|x-y|^{n-\alpha}}dy,\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

where
\gamma(\alpha)=\frac{2^{\alpha}\pi^{n/2}\Gamma(\alpha/2)}{\Gamma({(n-\alpha)}/2)}
and
\Gamma(\cdot)
being the usual gamma function. Let
(-\Delta)^{\alpha/2}
(under
0<\alpha<n
) denote the
\alpha/2
-th order Laplacian. Then
u=I_{\alpha}f
is viewed as a solution of the
\alpha/2
-th order Laplace equation

(-\Delta)^{\alpha/2}u=f

in the sense of the Fourier transform; i.e.,
(-\Delta)^{\alpha/2}
exists as the inverse of
I_{\alpha}
. It is well known that the Riesz potential operator
I_{\alpha}
plays an important role in harmonic analysis, PDE and potential theory. The classical Hardy–Littlewood–Sobolev theorem states that
I_{\alpha}
is bounded from
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
to
L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
when
1<p<q<\infty
and
1/q=1/p-\alpha/n
. For
p=1
, we know that
I_{\alpha}
is bounded from
L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
to
WL^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
when
0<\alpha<n
and
q=n/{(n-\alpha)}
. Moreover, for the critical index
p=n/{\alpha}
, it is easy to verify that
I_{\alpha}
is not bounded from
L^{n/{\alpha}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
to
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
(see
[
14
, p.119]
). In this case, we also know that as a substitute the Riesz potential operator
I_{\alpha}
is bounded from
L^{n/{\alpha}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
to
\mathrm{BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
(see
[
7
, p.130]
). For each locally integrable function
f
, closely related to the Riesz potential operator is the fractional maximal operator
M_{\alpha}
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, which is defined by

M_{\alpha}f(x):=\sup_{r>0}\frac{1}{|B(x,r)|^{1-\alpha/n}}\int_{|x-y|<r}|f(y)|\,dy,\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

where the supremum is taken over all
r>0
. It can be shown that
M_{\alpha}
satisfies the same norm inequalities as
I_{\alpha}
for
0<\alpha<n
,
1<p<n/{\alpha}
and
1/q=1/p-\alpha/n
. The weak type
(1,q)
inequality can be proved by using covering lemma arguments when
q=n/{(n-\alpha)}
(see, for example,
[
13
, Theorem 2]
), and
M_{\alpha}
is clearly of strong type
(p,\infty)
when
p=n/{\alpha}
. Actually, by Hölder’s inequality

\frac{1}{|B(x,r)|^{1-\alpha/n}}\int_{|x-y|<r}|f(y)|\,dy\leq\bigg(\int_{|x-y|<r}|f(y)|^{p}\,dy\bigg)^{1/p}\leq\|f\|_{L^{p}}.

The strong type
(p,q)
inequalities then follow by Marcinkiewicz interpolation theorem.

In 1974, Muckenhoupt and Wheeden studied the weighted boundedness of
I_{\alpha}
when
0<\alpha<n
. A weight
w
is a nonnegative, locally integrable function on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
that take values in
(0,\infty)
almost everywhere. Given a weight
w
and a measurable set
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we use the notation

w(E):=\int_{E}w(x)\,dx

to denote the
w
-measure of the set
E
. The complement of the set
E
is denoted by
E^{\complement}
, and the characteristic function of the set
E
is denoted by
\chi_{E}
:
\chi_{E}(x)=1
if
x\in E
and 0 if
x\notin E
. For given
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
r>0
,
B(x_{0},r)
denotes the open ball centered at
x_{0}
with radius
r
. Given a ball
B
and
\lambda>0
,
\lambda B
denotes the ball with the same center as
B
whose radius is
\lambda
times that of
B
. Following
[
13
]
, let us give the definitions of some weight classes.

Definition 1.1

(
[
13
]
)
.

A weight function
w
is said to belong to the Muckenhoupt–Wheeden class
A(p,q)
for
1<p<q<\infty
, if there exists a constant
C>0
such that

\bigg(\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}w(x)^{q}\,dx\bigg)^{1/q}\bigg(\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}w(x)^{-p^{\prime}}\,dx\bigg)^{1/{p^{\prime}}}\leq C<\infty,

for every ball
B
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, where
p^{\prime}
denotes the conjugate exponent of
p>1
such that
1/{p^{\prime}}+1/p=1
, and
p^{\prime}=1
when
p=\infty
. When
p=1
, a weight function
w
is in the Muckenhoupt–Wheeden class
A(1,q)
with
1<q<\infty
, if there exists a constant
C>0
such that

\bigg(\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}w(x)^{q}\,dx\bigg)^{1/q}\bigg(\underset{x\in B}{\mathrm{ess\,sup}}\;\frac{1}{w(x)}\bigg)\leq C<\infty,

for every ball
B
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. A weight function
w
is said to belong to the Muckenhoupt–Wheeden class
A(p,\infty)
with
1<p<\infty
, if there exists a constant
C>0
such that

\bigg(\underset{x\in B}{\mathrm{ess\,sup}}\;w(x)\bigg)\bigg(\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}w(x)^{-p^{\prime}}\,dx\bigg)^{1/{p^{\prime}}}\leq C<\infty,

for every ball
B
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

The weighted Lebesgue spaces and weak spaces with respect to the measure
w(x)\,dx
are denoted by
L^{p}(w)
and
WL^{p}(w)
with
1\leq p<\infty
, respectively. In 1974, Muckenhoupt and Wheeden proved the following strong type and weak type estimates of
M_{\alpha}
and
I_{\alpha}
(see
[
13
, Theorems 2 through 5]
).

Theorem A

(
[
13
]
)
.

Let
0<\alpha<n
,
1<p<n/{\alpha}
and
1/q=1/p-{\alpha}/n
. If
w\in A(p,q)
, then the operators
M_{\alpha}
and
I_{\alpha}
are bounded from
L^{p}(w^{p})
to
L^{q}(w^{q})
. If
p=1
and
w\in A(1,q)
with
q=n/{(n-\alpha)}
, then the operators
M_{\alpha}
and
I_{\alpha}
are bounded from
L^{1}(w)
to
WL^{q}(w^{q})
.

Definition 1.2

(
[
8
,
13
]
)
.

A locally integrable function
\hbar
is said to be in the bounded mean oscillation space
\mathrm{BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, if

\|\hbar\|_{*}:=\sup_{B}\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}|\hbar(x)-\hbar_{B}|\,dx<\infty,

where
\hbar_{B}
stands for the average of
\hbar
on
B
; i.e.,
\hbar_{B}:=\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}\hbar(y)\,dy
and the supremum is taken over all balls
B
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. For a weight function
w
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the weighted version of
\mathrm{BMO}
is denoted by
\mathrm{BMO}(w)
. We say that
\hbar
is in the weighted space
\mathrm{BMO}(w)
, if there is a constant
C>0
such that for any ball
B
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

\bigg(\underset{x\in B}{\mathrm{ess\,sup}}\;w(x)\bigg)\bigg(\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}|\hbar(x)-\hbar_{B}|\,dx\bigg)\leq C<\infty.

Moreover, for the critical index
p=n/{\alpha}
, Muckenhoupt and Wheeden proved the following result (see
[
13
, Theorem 7]
).

Theorem B

(
[
13
]
)
.

Let
0<\alpha<n
and
p=n/{\alpha}
. If
w\in A(p,\infty)
, then the Riesz potential operator
I_{\alpha}
is bounded from
L^{p}(w^{p})
to
\mathrm{BMO}(w)
.

We now introduce the function space
L^{\infty}(w)
, the weighted version of
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. We say that a measurable function
\hslash
belongs to the weighted space
L^{\infty}(w)
, if there is a constant
C>0
such that for any ball
B
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

\bigg(\underset{x\in B}{\mathrm{ess\,sup}}\;w(x)\bigg)\Big(\big\|\hslash\big\|_{L^{\infty}(B)}\Big)\leq C<\infty,

where

\big\|\hslash\big\|_{L^{\infty}(B)}:=\underset{x\in B}{\mbox{ess\,sup}}\;|\hslash(x)|=\inf\big\{M>0:\big|\{x\in E:|\hslash(x)|>M\}\big|=0\big\}.

We prove in this paper that the strong type
(p,\infty)
result remains valid for
M_{\alpha}
in our weighted spaces, under the assumptions that
w\in A(p,\infty)
and
p=n/{\alpha}
. Actually, by Hölder’s inequality and the condition
w\in A(p,\infty)
, we get

\begin{split}&\bigg(\underset{y\in B(x,r)}{\mathrm{ess\,sup}}\;w(y)\bigg)\cdot\frac{1}{|B(x,r)|^{1-\alpha/n}}\int_{|x-y|<r}|f(y)|\,dy\\
&\leq\bigg(\int_{B(x,r)}|f(y)|^{p}w(y)^{p}\,dy\bigg)^{1/p}\bigg(\underset{y\in B(x,r)}{\mathrm{ess\,sup}}\;w(y)\bigg)\bigg(\frac{1}{|B(x,r)|}\int_{B(x,r)}w(y)^{-p^{\prime}}\,dy\bigg)^{1/{p^{\prime}}}\\
&\leq C\|f\|_{L^{p}(w^{p})}.\end{split}

Thus, the fractional maximal operator
M_{\alpha}
is bounded from
L^{p}(w^{p})
to
L^{\infty}(w)
.

\mathbf{S}^{n-1}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:|x|=1\}

T_{\Omega,\alpha}f(x):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{\Omega(x-y)}{|x-y|^{n-\alpha}}f(y)\,dy,\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

(1.1)

Given any
\alpha\in(0,n)
and
f\in L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, the fractional maximal function of

with homogeneous kernel is defined by

M_{\Omega,\alpha}f(x):=\sup_{r>0}\frac{1}{|B(x,r)|^{1-\alpha/n}}\int_{|x-y|<r}|\Omega(x-y)||f(y)|\,dy,\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

(1.2)

where the supremum on the right-hand side is taken over all

.

In 2009, Komori and Shirai
[
9
]
defined and investigated the weighted Morrey spaces
L^{p,\kappa}(\mu,\nu)
which could be viewed as an extension of weighted Lebesgue spaces, and obtained the boundedness of some classical integral operators on these weighted spaces. Let us now give the definition of weighted Morrey spaces.

Definition 1.4

(
[
9
]
)
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
and
0<\kappa<1
. For two weights
\mu
and
\nu
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the weighted Morrey space

is defined by

L^{p,\kappa}(\mu,\nu):=\Big\{f\in L^{p}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mu):\|f\|_{L^{p,\kappa}(\mu,\nu)}<\infty\Big\},

where

\|f\|_{L^{p,\kappa}(\mu,\nu)}:=\sup_{B}\bigg(\frac{1}{\nu(B)^{\kappa}}\int_{B}|f(x)|^{p}\mu(x)\,dx\bigg)^{1/p}

and the supremum is taken over all balls
B
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. If
\mu=\nu
, then
L^{p,\kappa}(\mu,\nu)
is simply denoted by
L^{p,\kappa}(\mu)
.

It is known that
L^{p,\kappa}
is an extension of

in the sense that
L^{p,0}=L^{p}
. Komori and Shirai obtained the strong type estimates for
M_{\alpha}
and

in weighted Morrey spaces based on Theorem A (see
[
9

(
[

]
)

and the Riesz potential operator

are bounded from

.

and

(

Suppose that

In the present work, we study the homogeneous fractional integral operators and fractional maximal operators on the weighted Morrey spaces

. Motivated by Theorems D and F, it is natural to ask whether there is a corresponding estimate for the critical case

is bounded from

Throughout this paper,

3
Proofs of Theorems
2.5
and
2.6

In this section, we will prove the conclusions of Theorems
2.5
and
2.6
.

Proof of Theorem
2.5
.

Given a ball
B(x,r)
with center
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and radius
r\in(0,\infty)
, by using Hölder’s inequality twice (since
p>s^{\prime}
), we get

\begin{split}&\bigg(\underset{y\in B(x,r)}{\mathrm{ess\,sup}}\;w(y)\bigg)\cdot\frac{1}{|B(x,r)|^{1-\alpha/n}}\int_{|x-y|<r}|\Omega(x-y)||f(y)|\,dy\\
&\leq\bigg(\underset{y\in B(x,r)}{\mathrm{ess\,sup}}\;w(y)\bigg)\cdot\frac{1}{|B(x,r)|^{1/{p^{\prime}}}}\bigg(\int_{|x-y|<r}|\Omega(x-y)|^{s}dy\bigg)^{1/s}\bigg(\int_{|x-y|<r}|f(y)|^{s^{\prime}}dy\bigg)^{1/{s^{\prime}}}\\
&\leq\bigg(\underset{y\in B(x,r)}{\mathrm{ess\,sup}}\;w(y)\bigg)\cdot\frac{1}{|B(x,r)|^{1/{p^{\prime}}}}\bigg(\int_{|x-y|<r}|\Omega(x-y)|^{s}dy\bigg)^{1/s}\\
&\times\bigg(\int_{B(x,r)}|f(y)|^{p}w(y)^{p}dy\bigg)^{1/p}\bigg(\int_{B(x,r)}w^{s^{\prime}}(y)^{-\big(\frac{p}{s^{\prime}}\big)^{\prime}}dy\bigg)^{\frac{1}{s^{\prime}\big(\frac{p}{s^{\prime}}\big)^{\prime}}}.\end{split}

Note that
\Omega\in L^{s}(\mathbf{S}^{n-1})
. Using polar coordinates, we obtain

\begin{split}&\bigg(\int_{|x-y|<r}|\Omega(x-y)|^{s}dy\bigg)^{1/s}\\
&=\bigg(\int_{0}^{r}\int_{\mathbf{S}^{n-1}}|\Omega(x^{\prime})|^{s}\varrho^{n-1}d\sigma(x^{\prime})d\varrho\bigg)^{1/s}\\
&=C\cdot r^{n/s}\|\Omega\|_{L^{s}(\mathbf{S}^{n-1})}=C|B(x,r)|^{1/s}\|\Omega\|_{L^{s}(\mathbf{S}^{n-1})}.\end{split}

(3.1)

On the other hand, a direct computation shows that

\begin{split}\frac{1}{s^{\prime}\big(\frac{p}{s^{\prime}}\big)^{\prime}}=\frac{1}{s^{\prime}}\cdot\frac{p-s^{\prime}}{p}=\frac{1}{s^{\prime}}-\frac{1}{p}=\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}-\frac{1}{s}.\end{split}

This, combined with (
3.1
), yields

\begin{split}&\bigg(\underset{y\in B(x,r)}{\mathrm{ess\,sup}}\;w(y)\bigg)\cdot\frac{1}{|B(x,r)|^{1-\alpha/n}}\int_{|x-y|<r}|\Omega(x-y)||f(y)|\,dy\\
&\leq C\|\Omega\|_{L^{s}(\mathbf{S}^{n-1})}\bigg(\int_{B(x,r)}|f(y)|^{p}w(y)^{p}\,dy\bigg)^{1/p}\\
&\times\bigg(\underset{y\in B(x,r)}{\mathrm{ess\,sup}}\;w(y)\bigg)\bigg(\frac{1}{|B(x,r)|}\int_{B(x,r)}w^{s^{\prime}}(y)^{-\big(\frac{p}{s^{\prime}}\big)^{\prime}}dy\bigg)^{\frac{1}{s^{\prime}\big(\frac{p}{s^{\prime}}\big)^{\prime}}}\\
&=C\|\Omega\|_{L^{s}(\mathbf{S}^{n-1})}\bigg(\int_{B(x,r)}|f(y)|^{p}w(y)^{p}\,dy\bigg)^{1/p}\\
&\times\bigg[\bigg(\underset{y\in B(x,r)}{\mathrm{ess\,sup}}\;w^{s^{\prime}}(y)\bigg)\bigg(\frac{1}{|B(x,r)|}\int_{B(x,r)}w^{s^{\prime}}(y)^{-\big(\frac{p}{s^{\prime}}\big)^{\prime}}dy\bigg)^{\frac{1}{\big(\frac{p}{s^{\prime}}\big)^{\prime}}}\bigg]^{\frac{1}{s^{\prime}}}\\
&\leq C\|f\|_{L^{p}(w^{p})},\end{split}

where in the last step we have used the fact that
w^{s^{\prime}}\in A(p/{s^{\prime}},\infty)
. This ends the proof of Theorem
2.5
by the definition of
L^{\infty}(w)
.
∎

Before we proceed to prove our next theorem, we need the following estimate which can be found in
[
5
, Lemma 1]
.

Lemma 3.9

(
[
5
]
)
.

Suppose that
0<\alpha<n
and
\Omega
satisfies the
L^{s}
-Dini condition
\mathfrak{D}_{s}
with
1<s\leq\infty
. If there exists a constant
0<\tau<1/2
such that if
|x|<\tau R
, then we have

\bigg(\int_{R\leq|z|<2R}\bigg|\frac{\Omega(z-x)}{|z-x|^{n-\alpha}}-\frac{\Omega(z)}{|z|^{n-\alpha}}\bigg|^{s}dz\bigg)^{1/s}\leq C\cdot R^{n/s-(n-\alpha)}\bigg(\frac{|x|}{R}+\int_{\frac{|x|}{2R}}^{\frac{|x|}{R}}\omega_{s}(\delta)\frac{d\delta}{\delta}\bigg),

where the constant
C>0
is independent of
R
and
x
.

Remark 3.10
.

Following the same arguments as in the proof of Lemma 5 in
[
10
]
, we can also establish the above lemma on the homogeneous kernel
\Omega(z)
.

Proof of Theorem
2.6
.

For any ball
B=B(x_{0},r)
with
(x_{0},r)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(0,\infty)
and
f\in L^{p,\kappa}(w^{p},w^{q})
, we are going to give the estimation of the following expression

\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}\big|T_{\Omega,\alpha}f(x)-(T_{\Omega,\alpha}f)_{B}\big|\,dx.

(3.2)

For this purpose, we decompose
f
as
f=f_{1}+f_{2}
, where
f_{1}=f\cdot\chi_{4B}
,
f_{2}=f\cdot\chi_{(4B)^{\complement}}
,
4B=B(x_{0},4r)
. By the linearity of the fractional integral operator
T_{\Omega,\alpha}
, the expression (
3.2
) will be divided into two parts. That is,

\begin{split}&\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}\big|T_{\Omega,\alpha}f(x)-(T_{\Omega,\alpha}f)_{B}\big|\,dx\\
&\leq\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}\big|T_{\Omega,\alpha}f_{1}(x)-(T_{\Omega,\alpha}f_{1})_{B}\big|\,dx+\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}\big|T_{\Omega,\alpha}f_{2}(x)-(T_{\Omega,\alpha}f_{2})_{B}\big|\,dx\\
&:=\mathrm{I+II}.\end{split}

Let us first consider the term
\mathrm{I}
. When
x\in B
and
y\in 4B
, we have
|x-y|<5r
. An application of Fubini’s theorem yields

\begin{split}\mathrm{I}&\leq\frac{2}{|B|}\int_{B}|T_{\Omega,\alpha}f_{1}(x)|\,dx\\
&\leq\frac{2}{|B|}\int_{B}\bigg(\int_{4B}\frac{|\Omega(x-y)|}{|x-y|^{n-\alpha}}|f(y)|\,dy\bigg)dx\\
&\leq\frac{2}{|B|}\int_{4B}|f(y)|\bigg(\int_{|x-y|<5r}\frac{|\Omega(x-y)|}{|x-y|^{n-\alpha}}\,dx\bigg)dy.\end{split}

Since
\Omega\in L^{s}(\mathbf{S}^{n-1})
, we have
\Omega\in L^{1}(\mathbf{S}^{n-1})
, and

\|\Omega\|_{L^{1}(\mathbf{S}^{n-1})}\leq C\|\Omega\|_{L^{s}(\mathbf{S}^{n-1})}.

Note that
\alpha>0
. Using polar coordinates, we can deduce that

\begin{split}\int_{|x-y|<5r}\frac{|\Omega(x-y)|}{|x-y|^{n-\alpha}}\,dx&=\int_{0}^{5r}\int_{\mathbf{S}^{n-1}}\frac{|\Omega(x^{\prime})|}{\varrho^{n-\alpha}}\varrho^{n-1}d\sigma(x^{\prime})d\varrho\\
&=C\cdot r^{\alpha}\|\Omega\|_{L^{1}(\mathbf{S}^{n-1})}\leq C|B|^{\alpha/n}\|\Omega\|_{L^{s}(\mathbf{S}^{n-1})}.\end{split}

(3.3)

Applying the Hölder inequality, we obtain

\begin{split}\int_{4B}|f(y)|\,dy&\leq\bigg(\int_{4B}|f(y)|^{p}w(y)^{p}\,dy\bigg)^{1/p}\bigg(\int_{4B}w(y)^{-p^{\prime}}dy\bigg)^{1/{p^{\prime}}}.\end{split}

(3.4)

Notice that

s^{\prime}\Big(\frac{p}{s^{\prime}}\Big)^{\prime}=\frac{s^{\prime}}{1-\frac{s^{\prime}}{p}}=\frac{ps^{\prime}}{p-s^{\prime}}.

(3.5)

By a simple calculation, we can see that when
s^{\prime}\geq 1
,

\frac{ps^{\prime}}{p-s^{\prime}}\geq\frac{p}{p-1}=p^{\prime}.

Using Hölder’s inequality and the condition
w^{s^{\prime}}\in A(p/{s^{\prime}},q/{s^{\prime}})
, we get

\begin{split}\bigg(\frac{1}{|4B|}\int_{4B}w(y)^{-p^{\prime}}dy\bigg)^{1/{p^{\prime}}}&\leq\bigg(\frac{1}{|4B|}\int_{4B}w(y)^{-s^{\prime}\big(\frac{p}{s^{\prime}}\big)^{\prime}}dy\bigg)^{\frac{1}{s^{\prime}\big(\frac{p}{s^{\prime}}\big)^{\prime}}}\\
&=\bigg(\frac{1}{|4B|}\int_{4B}w^{s^{\prime}}(y)^{-\big(\frac{p}{s^{\prime}}\big)^{\prime}}dy\bigg)^{\frac{1}{s^{\prime}\big(\frac{p}{s^{\prime}}\big)^{\prime}}}\\
&\leq C\bigg(\frac{1}{|4B|}\int_{4B}w^{s^{\prime}}(y)^{\frac{q}{s^{\prime}}}dy\bigg)^{-\frac{s^{\prime}}{q}\cdot\frac{1}{s^{\prime}}}\\
&=C\bigg(\frac{1}{|4B|}\int_{4B}w(y)^{q}dy\bigg)^{-1/{q}}.\end{split}

(3.6)

A direct calculation shows that

\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}+\frac{1}{q}=1-\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=1-\frac{\alpha}{n}.

(3.7)

Hence, from (
3.3
), (
3.4
) and (
3.6
), it follows that

\begin{split}\mathrm{I}&\leq C\cdot\frac{1}{|B|^{1-\alpha/n}}\int_{4B}|f(y)|\,dy\\
&\leq C\cdot\frac{1}{|B|^{1-\alpha/n}}\cdot|4B|^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}+\frac{1}{q}}\bigg(\int_{4B}|f(y)|^{p}w(y)^{p}dy\bigg)^{1/p}\bigg(\int_{4B}w(y)^{q}dy\bigg)^{-1/{q}}\\
&\leq C\cdot\frac{w^{q}(4B)^{\kappa/p}}{w^{q}(4B)^{1/q}}\|f\|_{L^{p,\kappa}(w^{p},w^{q})}=C\|f\|_{L^{p,\kappa}(w^{p},w^{q})},\end{split}

where the last equality holds because
\kappa=p/q
. Now we turn our attention to the second term
\mathrm{II}
. It is easy to see that

\begin{split}\mathrm{II}&=\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}\big|T_{\Omega,\alpha}f_{2}(x)-(T_{\Omega,\alpha}f_{2})_{B}\big|\,dx\\
&\leq\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}\bigg\{\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}\big|T_{\Omega,\alpha}f_{2}(x)-T_{\Omega,\alpha}f_{2}(y)\big|\,dy\bigg\}dx\\
&\leq\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}\bigg\{\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}\bigg(\sum_{j=2}^{\infty}\int_{2^{j+1}B\backslash 2^{j}B}\Big|\frac{\Omega(x-z)}{|x-z|^{n-\alpha}}-\frac{\Omega(y-z)}{|y-z|^{n-\alpha}}\Big||f(z)|dz\bigg)dy\bigg\}dx.\end{split}

Since

\begin{split}\Big|\frac{\Omega(x-z)}{|x-z|^{n-\alpha}}-\frac{\Omega(y-z)}{|y-z|^{n-\alpha}}\Big|&\leq\Big|\frac{\Omega(x-z)}{|x-z|^{n-\alpha}}-\frac{\Omega(x_{0}-z)}{|x_{0}-z|^{n-\alpha}}\Big|\\
&+\Big|\frac{\Omega(x_{0}-z)}{|x_{0}-z|^{n-\alpha}}-\frac{\Omega(y-z)}{|y-z|^{n-\alpha}}\Big|,\end{split}

then by using Hölder’s inequality and Minkowski’s inequality, we have

\begin{split}&\int_{2^{j+1}B\backslash 2^{j}B}\Big|\frac{\Omega(x-z)}{|x-z|^{n-\alpha}}-\frac{\Omega(y-z)}{|y-z|^{n-\alpha}}\Big||f(z)|dz\\
&\leq\bigg(\int_{2^{j+1}B\backslash 2^{j}B}\Big|\frac{\Omega(x-z)}{|x-z|^{n-\alpha}}-\frac{\Omega(y-z)}{|y-z|^{n-\alpha}}\Big|^{s}dz\bigg)^{1/s}\bigg(\int_{2^{j+1}B\backslash 2^{j}B}|f(z)|^{s^{\prime}}dz\bigg)^{1/{s^{\prime}}}\\
&\leq\bigg(\int_{2^{j+1}B\backslash 2^{j}B}\Big|\frac{\Omega(x-z)}{|x-z|^{n-\alpha}}-\frac{\Omega(x_{0}-z)}{|x_{0}-z|^{n-\alpha}}\Big|^{s}dz\bigg)^{1/s}\bigg(\int_{2^{j+1}B\backslash 2^{j}B}|f(z)|^{s^{\prime}}dz\bigg)^{1/{s^{\prime}}}\\
&+\bigg(\int_{2^{j+1}B\backslash 2^{j}B}\Big|\frac{\Omega(x_{0}-z)}{|x_{0}-z|^{n-\alpha}}-\frac{\Omega(y-z)}{|y-z|^{n-\alpha}}\Big|^{s}dz\bigg)^{1/s}\bigg(\int_{2^{j+1}B\backslash 2^{j}B}|f(z)|^{s^{\prime}}dz\bigg)^{1/{s^{\prime}}}.\end{split}

For any given
x\in B
and
z\in 2^{j+1}B\backslash 2^{j}B
with
j\geq 2
, a trivial computation shows that

|x-x_{0}|<\frac{\,1\,}{4}|z-x_{0}|.

According to Lemma
3.9
(if we take
R=2^{j}r
), we get

\begin{split}&\bigg(\int_{2^{j+1}B\backslash 2^{j}B}\Big|\frac{\Omega(x-z)}{|x-z|^{n-\alpha}}-\frac{\Omega(x_{0}-z)}{|x_{0}-z|^{n-\alpha}}\Big|^{s}dz\bigg)^{1/s}\\
&=\bigg(\int_{2^{j}r\leq|z-x_{0}|<2^{j+1}r}\Big|\frac{\Omega(z-x_{0}-(x-x_{0}))}{|z-x_{0}-(x-x_{0})|^{n-\alpha}}-\frac{\Omega(z-x_{0})}{|z-x_{0}|^{n-\alpha}}\Big|^{s}dz\bigg)^{1/s}\\
&\leq C(2^{j}r)^{n/s-(n-\alpha)}\bigg[\frac{|x-x_{0}|}{2^{j}r}+\int_{\frac{|x-x_{0}|}{2^{j+1}r}}^{\frac{|x-x_{0}|}{2^{j}r}}\omega_{s}(\delta)\frac{d\delta}{\delta}\bigg]\\
&\leq C|2^{j+1}B|^{1/s-(1-\alpha/n)}\bigg[\frac{1}{2^{j}}+\int_{\frac{|x-x_{0}|}{2^{j+1}r}}^{\frac{|x-x_{0}|}{2^{j}r}}\omega_{s}(\delta)\frac{d\delta}{\delta}\bigg].\end{split}

Similarly, for any given
y\in B
, we can also obtain

\begin{split}&\bigg(\int_{2^{j+1}B\backslash 2^{j}B}\Big|\frac{\Omega(x_{0}-z)}{|x_{0}-z|^{n-\alpha}}-\frac{\Omega(y-z)}{|y-z|^{n-\alpha}}\Big|^{s}dz\bigg)^{1/s}\\
&\leq C(2^{j}r)^{n/s-(n-\alpha)}\bigg[\frac{|y-x_{0}|}{2^{j}r}+\int_{\frac{|y-x_{0}|}{2^{j+1}r}}^{\frac{|y-x_{0}|}{2^{j}r}}\omega_{s}(\delta)\frac{d\delta}{\delta}\bigg]\\
&\leq C|2^{j+1}B|^{1/s-(1-\alpha/n)}\bigg[\frac{1}{2^{j}}+\int_{\frac{|y-x_{0}|}{2^{j+1}r}}^{\frac{|y-x_{0}|}{2^{j}r}}\omega_{s}(\delta)\frac{d\delta}{\delta}\bigg].\end{split}

On the other hand, we now claim that the following inequality holds:

\displaystyle\bigg(\int_{2^{j+1}B\backslash 2^{j}B}|f(z)|^{s^{\prime}}dz\bigg)^{1/{s^{\prime}}}

(3.8)

\displaystyle\leq C\bigg(\int_{2^{j+1}B}|f(z)|^{p}w(z)^{p}dz\bigg)^{1/p}\bigg(\int_{2^{j+1}B}w(z)^{q}dz\bigg)^{-1/{q}}\times|2^{j+1}B|^{(1-\alpha/n)-1/s}.

To prove (
3.8
), we consider the following two cases:
p=s^{\prime}
and
p>s^{\prime}
.

Case 1:

p=s^{\prime}
. In this case, it follows from the condition
w^{p}\in A(1,q/{p})
and (
3.7
) that

\begin{split}&\bigg(\int_{2^{j+1}B\backslash 2^{j}B}|f(z)|^{s^{\prime}}dz\bigg)^{1/{s^{\prime}}}\\
&\leq\bigg(\int_{2^{j+1}B}|f(z)|^{p}w(z)^{p}w(z)^{-p}dz\bigg)^{1/{p}}\\
&\leq\bigg(\int_{2^{j+1}B}|f(z)|^{p}w(z)^{p}dz\bigg)^{1/p}\bigg(\underset{z\in 2^{j+1}B}{\mbox{ess\,sup}}\;\frac{1}{w^{p}(z)}\bigg)^{1/{p}}\\
&\leq C\bigg(\int_{2^{j+1}B}|f(z)|^{p}w(z)^{p}dz\bigg)^{1/p}\bigg(\int_{2^{j+1}B}w(z)^{q}dz\bigg)^{-1/{q}}\times|2^{j+1}B|^{1/q}\\
&=C\bigg(\int_{2^{j+1}B}|f(z)|^{p}w(z)^{p}dz\bigg)^{1/p}\bigg(\int_{2^{j+1}B}w(z)^{q}dz\bigg)^{-1/{q}}\times|2^{j+1}B|^{(1-\alpha/n)-1/s}.\end{split}

Case 2:

p>s^{\prime}
. Applying Hölder’s inequality with exponent
t=p/{s^{\prime}}>1
and the condition
w^{s^{\prime}}\in A(p/{s^{\prime}},q/{s^{\prime}})
, we get

\begin{split}&\bigg(\int_{2^{j+1}B\backslash 2^{j}B}|f(z)|^{s^{\prime}}dz\bigg)^{1/{s^{\prime}}}\leq\bigg(\int_{2^{j+1}B}|f(z)|^{s^{\prime}}dz\bigg)^{1/{s^{\prime}}}\\
&\leq\bigg(\int_{2^{j+1}B}|f(z)|^{p}w(z)^{p}dz\bigg)^{\frac{1}{s^{\prime}}\cdot\frac{s^{\prime}}{p}}\bigg(\int_{2^{j+1}B}w^{s^{\prime}}(z)^{-\big(\frac{p}{s^{\prime}}\big)^{\prime}}dz\bigg)^{\frac{1}{s^{\prime}\big(\frac{p}{s^{\prime}}\big)^{\prime}}}\\
&\leq C\bigg(\int_{2^{j+1}B}|f(z)|^{p}w(z)^{p}dz\bigg)^{1/p}\bigg(\int_{2^{j+1}B}w(z)^{q}dz\bigg)^{-1/{q}}\times\big|2^{j+1}B\big|^{\frac{1}{s^{\prime}\big(\frac{p}{s^{\prime}}\big)^{\prime}}+\frac{1}{q}}.\end{split}

It follows directly from (
3.5
) that

\frac{1}{s^{\prime}\big(\frac{p}{s^{\prime}}\big)^{\prime}}+\frac{\,1\,}{q}=\frac{p-s^{\prime}}{ps^{\prime}}+\frac{\,1\,}{q}=\Big(1-\frac{\alpha}{n}\Big)-\frac{\,1\,}{s}.

Thus, in both cases (
3.8
) holds. Consequently, for any
x\in B
and
y\in B
, we can deduce that

\begin{split}&\sum_{j=2}^{\infty}\int_{2^{j+1}B\backslash 2^{j}B}\Big|\frac{\Omega(x-z)}{|x-z|^{n-\alpha}}-\frac{\Omega(y-z)}{|y-z|^{n-\alpha}}\Big||f(z)|\,dz\\
&\leq C\sum_{j=2}^{\infty}\bigg[\frac{1}{2^{j-1}}+\int_{\frac{|x-x_{0}|}{2^{j+1}r}}^{\frac{|x-x_{0}|}{2^{j}r}}\omega_{s}(\delta)\frac{d\delta}{\delta}+\int_{\frac{|y-x_{0}|}{2^{j+1}r}}^{\frac{|y-x_{0}|}{2^{j}r}}\omega_{s}(\delta)\frac{d\delta}{\delta}\bigg]\\
&\times\bigg(\int_{2^{j+1}B}|f(z)|^{p}w(z)^{p}dz\bigg)^{1/p}\bigg(\int_{2^{j+1}B}w(z)^{q}dz\bigg)^{-1/{q}}\\
&\leq C\sum_{j=2}^{\infty}\bigg[\frac{1}{2^{j-1}}+\int_{\frac{|x-x_{0}|}{2^{j+1}r}}^{\frac{|x-x_{0}|}{2^{j}r}}\omega_{s}(\delta)\frac{d\delta}{\delta}+\int_{\frac{|y-x_{0}|}{2^{j+1}r}}^{\frac{|y-x_{0}|}{2^{j}r}}\omega_{s}(\delta)\frac{d\delta}{\delta}\bigg]\\
&\times\frac{w^{q}(2^{j+1}B)^{\kappa/p}}{w^{q}(2^{j+1}B)^{1/q}}\|f\|_{L^{p,\kappa}(w^{p},w^{q})}\\
&=C\bigg[1+2\int_{0}^{1}\omega_{s}(\delta)\frac{d\delta}{\delta}\bigg]\|f\|_{L^{p,\kappa}(w^{p},w^{q})},\end{split}

where the last equality follows from the assumption that
\kappa=p/q
. Therefore, we obtain

\begin{split}\mathrm{II}&\leq\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}\bigg\{\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}\bigg(\sum_{j=2}^{\infty}\int_{2^{j+1}B\backslash 2^{j}B}\Big|\frac{\Omega(x-z)}{|x-z|^{n-\alpha}}-\frac{\Omega(y-z)}{|y-z|^{n-\alpha}}\Big||f(z)|\,dz\bigg)dy\bigg\}dx\\
&\leq C\bigg[1+2\int_{0}^{1}\omega_{s}(\delta)\frac{d\delta}{\delta}\bigg]\|f\|_{L^{p,\kappa}(w^{p},w^{q})}.\end{split}

Combining the above estimates for I and II and taking the supremum over all balls
B
, we conclude the proof of Theorem
2.6
.
∎

By using the same arguments as in the proof of Theorems
2.5
and
2.6
, we can also show the following result. The proof needs appropriate but only minor modifications and we leave this to the interested reader.

Theorem 3.11
.

Suppose that
\Omega\in L^{s}(\mathbf{S}^{n-1})
with
1<s\leq\infty
. If
0<\alpha<n
,
s^{\prime}\leq p<n/{\alpha}
,
1/q=1/p-{\alpha}/n
,
\kappa=p/q=1-{(\alpha p)}/n
and
w^{s^{\prime}}\in A(p/{s^{\prime}},q/{s^{\prime}})
, then the fractional maximal operator
M_{\Omega,\alpha}
with homogeneous kernel is bounded from
L^{p,\kappa}(w^{p},w^{q})
to
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

In particular, when
\Omega\equiv 1
and
s=\infty
, as a direct consequence of Theorem
3.11
, we have

Corollary 3.12
.

If
0<\alpha<n
,
1\leq p<n/{\alpha}
,
1/q=1/p-{\alpha}/n
,
\kappa=p/q=1-{(\alpha p)}/n
and
w\in A(p,q)
, then the fractional maximal operator
M_{\alpha}
is bounded from
L^{p,\kappa}(w^{p},w^{q})
to
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

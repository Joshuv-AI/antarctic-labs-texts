Abstract
In this work we present a new framework of the gravity sector by considering the extension F ⁡ ( R , w ) F(R,w) , in which R R is the Ricci scalar and w w is the equation of state. Three different choices of function F ⁡ ( R , w ) F(R,w) are investigated under the Palatini formalism. The models appear equivalent to F ⁡ ( R ) F(R) models of gravity with effective momentum-energy tensors. For linear dependence of Ricci scalar in which F ⁡ ( R , w ) = k ⁡ ( w ) ​ R F(R,w)=k(w)R , the model appears equivalent to Einstein-Hilbert action with effective momentum-energy tensor. Recovering the minimal coupling case of the last choice does not face Jordan-Einstein frame ambiguities and exhibits natural alignments with general relativity results in the matter / radiation dominated eras. We discuss some astrophysical implications of the model by considering scalar fields as dominant matter forms. We show that the Higgs inflation could be saved within the F ⁡ ( R , w ) F(R,w) model. We suggest some future investigations exemplified by constant-roll inflation and universe evolution for F ⁡ ( R ) = f ⁡ ( R ) ​ k ​ ( w ) F(R)=f(R)k(w) where f ⁡ ( R ) f(R) represents the Starobinsky gravitational form. Using the model and comparing it with pure F ⁡ ( R ) F(R) gravity, we provide preliminary indications of F ⁡ ( R , w ) F(R,w) ’s impact. As a final note, we suggest using the Polytropic equation of state in future works to investigate F ⁡ ( R , w ) F(R,w) .

Introduction:

The theory of general relativity GR
Einstein:1916vd
has made significant contributions to cosmology, which cannot be ignored. The theory of general relativity alters our understanding of space-time and its relationship to energy in some way that we cannot avoid in any future developments of our knowledge of the universe.

In spite of the previous notes, GR, under its original minimal metric formalism, failed to explain some recent cosmological observations, such as, the late expansion of the universe measured from the light-curves of several hundred type Ia supernovae

In the current work, we introduce a novel gravity framework by considering an extended form of gravity denoted
F(R,w)
, where
w
represents the equation of state. The model exhibits a notable characteristics:
The model coincides naturally with General Relativity during epochs in which matter and radiation are dominant.
A closer look at specific forms of
F(R,w)
shows changes in the scales of some cosmological quantities such as the Hubble parameter and the cosmological acceleration of the universe, but the functions of these quantities remain unchanged. This statement, however, needs further investigation before it can be generalized.
The model also has a significant impact when scalar fields become dominant during early stages of the evolution of the universe (inflaton fields) or during the later expansion phase (quintessence).
Unlike common models of non-minimal coupling, for
F(R,w)=R+\alpha Rw
under Palatini formalism, the model appears to be free of Jordan-Einstein frame ambiguity since it is reduced to the minimal case without the need for conformal transformation.

The paper is organized as follows. In section. 2,

Section. 3 discusses specific cases of model

Finally, In section. 4, the astronomical implications of the model are illustrated through an examination of the inflaton field as dominating matter during early universe evolution. We consider the Higgs inflationary potential, and we show how it can be saved within

gravity. As a follow-up, we provide some comments for future studies exemplified by constant-roll inflation, the Starobinsky model, and polytropic equations of state, and we provide some insights into how
F(R,w)
impacts them. We end up with a summary and conclusion in section. 5.

2
The model:

As a first step, we will discuss the most general case of
F(R,w)
gravity, in which the action is expressed as follows:

S=\frac{1}{16\pi G}\int F(\mathcal{R},w)\sqrt{-g}d^{4}x+\int\mathcal{L}_{M}\sqrt{-g}d^{4}x

(1)

Throughout this section, we discuss the the field equations in both cases of metric and Palatini formalism, where in the case of metric formalism, the connection is constructed from the metric tensor through the Levi-Civita connection
\Gamma^{\lambda}_{\mu\nu}=\{^{\lambda}\>_{\mu\nu}\}
, while in the Palatini formalism, the connection and the metric are considered independent.

According to the metric formalism, by taking the variation of the action (
1
) with respect to the metric tensor, the modified field equations take the following form:

\begin{split}F_{\mathcal{R}}(\mathcal{R},w)\mathcal{R}_{\mu\nu}-\frac{1}{2}F(\mathcal{R},w)g_{\mu\nu}+(g_{\mu\nu}\Box-\nabla_{\mu}\nabla_{\nu})\\
F_{\mathcal{R}}(\mathcal{R},w)=8\pi GT_{\mu\nu}\end{split}

(2)

By contracting equation (
2
), the relation between the Ricci scalar
\mathcal{R}
and the trace
T
of the stress-energy tensor is as follows:

F_{\mathcal{R}}(\mathcal{R},w)\mathcal{R}+3\Box F_{\mathcal{R}}(\mathcal{R},w)-2F(\mathcal{R},w)=8\pi GT

(3)

By eliminating the term
\Box F_{\mathcal{R}}(\mathcal{R},w)
between equations
(
2
) and (
3
), the gravitational field equations can be written in the form:

\begin{split}F_{\mathcal{R}}(\mathcal{R},w)(\mathcal{R}_{\mu\nu}-\frac{1}{3}\mathcal{R}g_{\mu\nu})+\frac{1}{6}F(\mathcal{R},w)g_{\mu\nu}=\\
8\pi G(T_{\mu\nu}-\frac{1}{3}Tg_{\mu\nu})\end{split}

(4)

In the Palatini formalism, the gravitational action (
1
) is now varied with respect to the metric tensor
g_{\mu\nu}
under the assumption
\delta R_{\mu\nu}=0
.

As a result we obtain the field equations:

F_{R}(R,w)R_{\mu\nu}-\frac{1}{2}F(R,w)g_{\mu\nu}=8\pi GT_{\mu\nu}

(5)

The expression for the Ricci scalar is obtained by contracting the equation (
5
) with
g_{\mu\nu}
as,

RF_{R}(R,w)+2F(R,w)=8\pi GT

(6)

In terms of the Einstein tensor one can write:

\begin{split}G_{\mu\nu}=R_{\mu\nu}-\frac{1}{2}g_{\mu\nu}R=\frac{1}{F_{R}(R,w)}\\
\bigg\{8\pi G\ (T_{\mu\nu}-\frac{g_{\mu\nu}}{2}T)-\frac{g_{\mu\nu}}{2}F(R,w)\bigg\}\end{split}

(7)

This paper focuses on palatini formalism due to its interesting characteristics in
F(R,w)
models, whereas future studies will examine the model in metric formalism.

3
Particular Cases:

This section discusses some possible choices regarding the function
F(R,w)
. There are many more forms that could be assumed and studied, but we only provide three examples here.

3.1
F(R,w)=f(R)k(w)

Let’s consider the following particular function of
F(R,w)
gravity as:

F(R,w)=f(R)k(w)

(8)

Taking the variation of action (
1
) with respect to the metric
g_{\mu\nu}
, we can express the field equation as follows:

f_{R}R_{\mu\nu}-\frac{1}{2}f(R)g_{\mu\nu}=8\pi GT^{eff}_{\mu\nu}

(9)

Where:

T^{eff}_{\mu\nu}=\frac{T_{\mu\nu}}{k(w)}

(10)

Interestingly, the model can be reduced to an
f(R)
model with an effective momentum-energy tensor.

Taking the variation of the action with respect to connection we can find:

\nabla_{\alpha}(\sqrt{-g}f_{R}g^{\mu\nu})=0

(11)

In order to solve equation (
11
), it is convinent to introduce a new metric:

\sqrt{h}h_{\mu\nu}=\sqrt{-g}f_{R}g_{\mu\nu}

(12)

The connection for this metric is a Levi-Civita connection. so both metrics are related by the conformal factor.

h_{\mu\nu}=f_{R}g_{\mu\nu}

(13)

For this reason one should assume that the conformal factor
f_{R}\neq 0
.

Considering perfect fluid

T_{\mu}^{\nu}=diag(-\rho,p,p,p)

we can find the effective equation of state equivalent to the standard one

w(\text{eff})=\frac{p_{\text{eff}}}{\rho_{\text{eff}}}=w=\frac{p}{\rho}

where,
\rho_{\text{eff}}=\rho/K(w)
and
p_{\text{eff}}=p/K(w)
.

As long as we are in the Jordan frame, we can continue working on the field equations (
9
). Alternatively, equations can also be displayed in the Einstein frame, where gravitational action is expressed formally in Hilbert-Einstein form.

In the Jordan frame, action (
1
) is dynamically equivalent to the first order Palatini gravitational action:

\begin{split}S=\frac{1}{16\pi G}\int d^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\big[\frac{1}{2}(f(\zeta)+f^{\prime}(\zeta)(R-\zeta))\big]+\\
\int d^{4}x\sqrt{-g}L_{M}^{eff}\end{split}

(14)

with
f^{\prime\prime}(R)\neq 0
.

The action (
14
) takes the Brans-Dicke model form by introducing an auxiliary scalar field
\Phi=f^{\prime}(\zeta)
and taking into consideration the constraint
\zeta=R
.

S=\frac{1}{16\pi G}\int d^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\big[\frac{1}{2}(\Phi R-U(\Phi)\big]+\int d^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\mathcal{L}_{M}^{eff}

(15)

where
U(\Phi)=\zeta(\Phi)\Phi-f(\zeta(\Phi))
.

The action (
15
) can be reduced to the minimal coupling case by applying a conformal transformation
g_{\mu\nu}^{E}=\Phi g_{\mu\nu}^{J}
:

S=\frac{1}{16\pi G}\int d^{4}x\sqrt{-g_{E}}(R_{E}-U_{E}(\Phi))+\int d^{4}x\sqrt{-g_{E}}\mathcal{L}_{M(E)}^{eff}

(16)

Where
U_{E}=U/\Phi^{2}
.

In FRW metric, Friedmann equations take the form:

H_{E}^{2}=\frac{8\pi G}{3}\bigg(\rho^{E}_{\Phi}+\rho^{E}_{M}\bigg)

(17)

(\dot{H}+H^{2})_{E}=-\frac{4\pi G}{3}\big(2\rho^{E}_{\Phi}-\rho^{E}_{M}+3P^{E}_{M}\big)

(18)

Subscript E denotes the evaluation of all quantities in the Einstein frame, with
\rho^{E}_{M}=\rho_{M}/\Phi k(w)
and
P^{E}_{M}=P_{M}/\Phi k(w)
.

3.2
F(R,w)=R+\alpha wR

In a specific case of
f(R)
, linear dependence of the Ricci scalar would be considered as:

F(R,w)=R+\alpha wR

(19)

The choice (
19
) appears to be a small perturbation to the theory for
\alpha\ll 1
.

Interesting features can be found in this choice. First of all, The model coincides naturally with general relativity during the matter and radiation era, in which
w=0
and
w=1/3
respectively. Second, even with the non-minimal coupling with matter through the equation of state, the model appears as an effective energy-momentum theory with minimal coupling. Thus, the model is free from Einstein-Jordan frame ambiguity.

The field equations are given as:

R_{\mu\nu}-\frac{1}{2}g_{\mu\nu}R=8\pi GT^{eff}_{\mu\nu}

(20)

with

T^{eff}_{\mu\nu}=\frac{T_{\mu\nu}}{(1+\alpha w)}

(21)

Friedmann equations can be shown as:

H^{2}=\frac{8\pi G}{3}\bigg(\frac{\rho^{2}}{\rho+\alpha p}\bigg)

(22)

\dot{H}+H^{2}=-\frac{4\pi G}{3}\rho\bigg(\frac{\rho+3p}{\rho+\alpha p}\bigg)

(23)

This class of models has great influence when considering scalar fields as the dominant matter in the universe to explain early or late inflationary expansions.

3.3
F(R,w)=f(R)+k(w)

As a third case of generalized
F(R,w)
gravity models, we consider that the action is given by,

F(R,w)=f(R)+k(w)

(24)

For an arbitrary matter source the gravitational field equations are given by:

f_{R}R_{\mu\nu}-\frac{1}{2}g_{\mu\nu}f(R)=8\pi G(T_{\mu\nu}+K_{\mu\nu})

(25)

where:

K_{\mu\nu}=\frac{k(w)}{8\pi G}g_{\mu\nu}

(26)

Once again, we can recover the minimal coupling case by applying the conformal transformation
g_{\mu\nu}^{E}=\Phi g_{\mu\nu}^{J}
.

The Friedman equations take the form,

H_{E}^{2}=\frac{8\pi G}{3}(\rho_{\Phi}+\rho_{M}+\rho_{w})_{E}

(27)

(\dot{H}+H^{2})_{E}=-\frac{4\pi G}{3}(2\rho_{\Phi}-\rho_{M}+3(p_{M}+p_{w}))_{E}

(28)

where
\rho_{w}=\frac{k(w)}{8\pi G}
, and
p_{w}=\frac{k(w)}{8\pi G}

4
Astrophysical implications:

In order to understand the astrophysical implications of this gravitational extension, the function
F(R,w)
needs to be defined specifically.
In this section, we examine the slow-roll inflationary scenario in the simplest case to demonstrate how
F(R,w)
gravity impacts the main observable quantities represented by
n_{s}
and
r
.
Following that, we present three possible future research directions. Firstly, we provide a brief look at constant-roll inflation in which the
F(R,w)
have a greater impact on the model. Next, we inspect the influence of
F(R,w)
on Starobinsky’s model, and finally we discuss the generalized polytropic equation of state.

4.1
Slow-roll Inflation:

Using scalar fields as dominated matter, this section examines the early accelerated expansion of the universe.

Let’s consider a canonical scalar field as follows:

L_{\phi}=-\frac{1}{2}g^{\mu\nu}\nabla_{\mu}\phi\nabla_{\nu}\phi-V(\phi)

(29)

with signature
(-,+,+,+)
.

Following the results of section (
3.2
) and the function (
19
) , we find the following:

S=\int\sqrt{-g}d^{4}x\bigg[\frac{1}{2}R+-\frac{1}{2}g^{\mu\nu}\nabla_{\mu}\varphi\nabla_{\nu}\varphi-U(\phi(\varphi))\bigg]

(30)

Where the re-scaled canonical field
\varphi
is given by:

\frac{d\chi}{d\phi}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{(1+\alpha w)}}

(31)

The potential
U
is given by:

U(\phi(\varphi))=\frac{V(\phi)}{(1+\alpha w)}

(32)

As a result we can find Friedmann equation in Jordan frame as:

H^{2}=\frac{1}{2}\dot{\chi}^{2}+U(\chi)

(33)

\dot{H}=-\frac{1}{2}\dot{\chi}

(34)

The equation of motion of the scalar field:

\ddot{\chi}+3H\dot{\chi}+U_{\chi}=0

(35)

Let us now consider the most extreme case where the potential energy dominates the kinetic energy
U\gg X
, so
w\sim-1
. then the slow-roll approximation for constant
\alpha
is given as:

\varepsilon=\frac{(1-\alpha)}{2}\bigg(\frac{V_{\phi}}{V}\bigg)^{2}

(36)

\eta=(1-\alpha)\bigg(\frac{V_{\phi\phi}}{V}\bigg)

(37)

Thus, the spectral index
n_{s}
and tensor to scalar ratio
r
are represented as:

n_{s}-1=(1-\alpha)(2\eta_{\phi}-6\varepsilon_{\phi})

(38)

r=16(1-\alpha)\varepsilon_{\phi}

(39)

where
\varepsilon_{\phi}=\frac{1}{2}\bigg(\frac{V_{\phi}}{V}\bigg)^{2}
, and
\eta_{\phi}=\bigg(\frac{V_{\phi\phi}}{V}\bigg)
.

Consider Higgs inflation with the potential given by:

V(\phi)=\lambda(\phi^{2}-\nu)^{2}

(40)

The potential takes the form of a quartic potential for

.

The observables

and

n_{s}=(1-\alpha)\bigg(1-\frac{24}{\phi^{2}}\bigg)

(41)

r=(1-\alpha)\bigg(\frac{128}{\phi^{2}}\bigg)

Although the value of

(1-\alpha)

\simeq 5.33

In this extremely simplified case of

\alpha(\phi)

(1-\alpha(\phi))

(1-\alpha(\phi))=(1-\xi\phi^{2})^{2}

As can be seen in this case, the model is equivalent numerically to the non-minimal coupling case in Einstein frame

Future Directions and Expected Implications

In this section we provide some suggestions for future works to investigate important cosmological phenomena in the field of

4.2.1
Constant-roll inflation:

The slow roll inflationary scenario was discussed in the previous section quickly to illustrate how
F(R,w)
models of gravity impact cosmological inflation.

However, in the slow-roll inflationary scenario the kinetic energy term is negligible in front of the potential energy and the equation of state is approximately
-1
. As discussed earlier,
F(R,w)
affects the inflationary model under the slow-roll approximation because of the coupling coefficient
\alpha
, which may be constant or depend on the scalar field.

In this section, we discuss constant-roll inflation, in which kinetic energy has a significant role to play.

Specifically, we consider
F(R,w)=k(w)R=(R+\alpha Rw)
.

Accordingly, Friedmann equations will be expressed in terms of
\phi
as follows:

3H^{2}=\frac{1}{k(w)}[\frac{1}{2}\dot{\phi}^{2}+V(\phi)]

(44)

-2\dot{H}=\frac{1}{k(w)}\dot{\phi}^{2}

(45)

The scalar field’s equation of motion is:

\ddot{\phi}+3H\dot{\phi}+k(w)U_{\phi}=0

(46)

Where
U_{\phi}
represents the derivative of the potential
U
with respect to scalar field
\phi
. A minimum ground state
\dot{w}=0
is assumed in the previous equations.

As part of the original work of the constant-roll inflation
Motohashi:2014ppa
, authors used the Hamiltonian-Jacobi formalism to extract the Hubble parameter from the inflaton field
H=H(\phi)
.

In this paper we follow same argument which is applicable as long as
t=t(\phi)
is a single-valued function, i.e
\dot{\phi}\neq 0
.

The constant rate of rolling is parameterized by the equation:

\ddot{\phi}=-(3+\beta)H\dot{\phi}

(47)

As shown in ref
Motohashi:2014ppa
,
H=H(\phi)
is independent of the inflationary model represented by
V(\phi)
, and its general solution can be formulated as follows:

H(\phi)=C_{1}\exp\bigg(\sqrt{\frac{3+\beta}{2}}\frac{\phi}{M_{pl}}\bigg)+C_{2}\exp\bigg(-\sqrt{\frac{3+\beta}{2}}\frac{\phi}{M_{pl}}\bigg)

(48)

Conversely, we can find in the
F(R,w)
from equation (
45
) that:

-2M_{pl}^{2}H_{\phi}=\frac{1}{k(w)}\dot{\phi}

(49)

We can determine
\ddot{\phi}
from the time derivatives of equation (
49
).

The differential equation for the Hubble parameter
H
can be found by replacing
\ddot{\phi}
in equation (
47
) as,

H_{\phi\phi}=\frac{(3+\beta)}{2M_{pl^{2}}k(w)}H

(50)

Solving equation (
44
) , one can find
\dot{\phi}(\phi)
as,

\dot{\phi}^{2}=3\alpha M_{pl}^{2}H^{2}+\sqrt{3\alpha M_{pl}^{2}H^{2}(3\alpha M_{pl}^{2}H^{2}-8V)-2V}

(51)

In the case of quartic potential
V=\lambda\phi^{4}
, we solved the differential equation (
50
) numerically.

In figure (
2
), we plot
H
as a function of
\phi
in both standard GR
Motohashi:2014ppa
and
F(R,w)
for comparison.

As shown in figure (
2
), the Hubble parameter’s function in GR and
F(R,w)
are exactly the same for
\alpha=0
, as it expected theoretically.

Here is a numerical example where each parameter value of the model is fixed to unity, so that
H
is represented in figure (
2
). Given
\phi=4
, we found
\frac{H_{F(R,w)}}{H_{\text{GR}}}=0.2
for
\alpha=1
. The ratio

4.2.2
Starobinsky cosmological model:

Assuming the function
F(R,T)=f(R)k(w)=(R+\gamma R^{n})(1+\alpha w)
, then the trace of equation (
9
) is shown as:

R=\gamma(n-2)R^{n}=-T^{\text{eff}}

(52)

For
\gamma\gg 1
and
n\neq 1
we can write (
52
) as:

R=\bigg(-\frac{T^{\text{eff}}}{\gamma(n-2)}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{n}}

(53)

In the Starobinsky model, where
f(R)=R+\gamma R^{2}
, the Ricci scalar is:

R=-T^{\text{eff}}=\frac{(3P-\rho)\rho}{\alpha P}

(54)

To simplify the analytical equation, we considered
\alpha w\gg 1
.

Considering
\Lambda
CDM model with flat FRW, we can derive Fiendmann equation as follows:

\begin{split}H^{2}=\big(\frac{b^{2}}{b+d/2}\big)^{2}\frac{3(\rho_{m}+\rho_{r}+\rho_{\Lambda})}{\alpha(\rho_{r}-3\rho_{\Lambda})}\\
\bigg[\frac{3(\rho_{m}+\rho_{r}+\rho_{\Lambda})}{\alpha(\rho_{r}-3+\rho_{\Lambda})}(\rho_{m}+\rho_{\Lambda})^{2}\\
\frac{(K-3)(K+1)}{2b}+(\rho_{m}+\rho_{\Lambda})+\rho_{r}/b\bigg]\end{split}

(55)

where:

K=\frac{3\rho_{\Lambda}}{(\rho_{m}+\rho_{\Lambda})}

(56)

b=1+\frac{6\gamma}{\alpha}(\rho_{m}+4\rho_{\Lambda})\frac{(\rho_{m}+\rho_{r}+\rho_{\Lambda})}{(\rho_{r}-3\rho_{\Lambda})}

(57)

d=\frac{\dot{b}}{H}

(58)

55

A significant impact is expected on the grand scheme of evolution of the universe as a whole as a result of

4.2.3
Polytropic equation of state:

All of the last discussions focused on the perfect fluid’s equation of state as
P=\kappa\rho
, where
\kappa=0
represents dust matters,
\kappa=1/3
corresponds to radiation, and
\kappa=0
represents vacuum energy. In order to understand different cosmological aspects, different equations of state may be required, such as a polytropic equation of state in which
P=\xi\rho^{\sigma}
.
where
\xi
and
\sigma
are constants.
\sigma
usually is written as,
\sigma=1-1/n
, where
n
is known as polytropic index.

Reference
Mardan:2020noh
shows how with a generalized polytropic equation of state we can obtain new classes of polytropic models from the solution of Einstein-Maxwell field equations for charged anisotropic fluid configurations. Various values of polytropic index
n=1,2
are used to develop the models. Models that have been developed have helped regain masses and radii of eight different stars. Analyses of the models indicate that they are well-behaved and physically feasible.

In
Chavanis:2021jwr
authors develop a cosmological model involving a scalar field with a power-law potential associated with polytropic equations of state. In order to simplify the equations of the problem, they consider a fast oscillation regime called ”spintessence”. Various values of polytropic constants and polytropic indexes are studied. The LambdaCDM model, the Chaplygin gas model, and the Bose-Einstein condensate model are recovered.

Considering such an equation of state and examining models in F(R,w) are worthy of investigation, and a significant impact can be expected.

5
Summary and Conclusion:

A modified gravitational model is proposed in this paper. The modification is represented by the function
F(R,w)
, where
w
is the equation of state.

Three different cases are discussed with functions
F(R,w)=f(R)k(w)
,
F(R,w)=f(R)+k(w)
and
F(R,w)=(R+\alpha Rw)
. The models are studied under the Palatini formalism.

The first and second choices appear as
F(R)
models with an effective momentum-energy tensor. In contrast, the third choice takes the form of a Hilbert-Einstein action with an effective momentum-energy tensor. The third case shows no Jordan-Einstein frame ambiguity.

The slow-roll inflation model is examined to illustrate how
F(R,w)
may appear to mimic the standard non-minimal coupling to gravity.

We consider the Higgs inflationary potential as a case study and demonstrate how the
F(R,w)
model effectively addresses the challenges posed by Higgs inflation.

Finally, we present three future directions for work and provide some initial indications of how
F(R,w)
models could impact cosmological results.

A constant-roll inflation model with
w=w(\phi)
is discussed, and the Hubble parameter is numerically impacted by this model.

In the
\Lambda
CDM model, specifically under the
F(R,w)=F(R)(1+\alpha w)
framework with
F(R)=R+\gamma R^{2}
, we examine the behavior of the scale factor and Hubble parameter using the Starobinsky function. We compare the results to a pure Starobinsky model to clarify the expected effect of
F(R,w)
.

As a final point, we introduce the Polytropic equation of state as a field that will be of interest to future works.

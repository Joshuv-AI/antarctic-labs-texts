Algebraic connectivity of Kronecker products of line graphs
Shivani Chauhan, A. Satyanarayana Reddy

Abstract
Let X X be a tree with n n vertices and L ⁡ ( X ) L(X) be its line graph. In this work, we completely characterize the trees for which the algebraic connectivity of L ⁡ ( X ) × K m L(X)\times K_{m} is equal to m − 1 m-1 , where × \times denotes the Kronecker product. We provide a few necessary and sufficient conditions for L ⁡ ( X ) × K m L(X)\times K_{m} to be Laplacian integral. The algebraic connectivity of L ⁡ ( X ) × K m L(X)\times K_{m} , where X X is a tree of diameter 4 and k k -book graph is discussed.



Keywords
: Tree, line graph, Kronecker product of graph, Laplacian matrix, algebraic connectivity.

.

Introduction

The
Laplacian matrix

\mathfrak{L}(X)
of a graph
X
is defined as
\mathfrak{D}(X)-A(X)
, where
A(X)
is the
adjacency matrix
and
\mathfrak{D}(X)
is the diagonal matrix with diagonal entries as the row sums of
A(X).
It is well known that
\mathfrak{L}(X)
is singular and positive semidefinite. Fiedler
[
10
]
, showed that the second smallest eigenvalue of
\mathfrak{L}(X)
is zero if and only if
X
is disconnected. This eigenvalue is termed as the
algebraic connectivity
of graph
X
, denoted by
a(X)
, and the corresponding eigenvector is known as the
Fiedler vector
. The collection of all the eigenvalues of a matrix together with its multiplicities is known as the
spectrum
of that matrix. The spectrum of the Laplacian matrix is called
Laplacian spectrum
.
For more information on Laplacian matrices and algebraic connectivity one can refer to the survey article of Merris
[
18
]
and Maria
[
7
]
, respectively. The
path graph, complete graph
and the
star graph
on
n
vertices are denoted by
P_{n},K_{n}
and
K_{1,n-1}
, respectively. Grone
et al.
[
11
]
proved that if
X\neq K_{1,n-1}
is a tree with
n\geq 6
vertices, then
a(X)<0.49.
Also, if
X\neq K_{2}
is a tree, then
a(X)\in(0,1]
, and it is 1 if and only if
X=K_{1,n-1}.

The
Kronecker product

X_{1}\times X_{2}
of graphs
X_{1}
and
X_{2}
is a graph such that the vertex set is
V(X_{1})\times V(X_{2})
, vertices
(x_{1},x_{2})
and
(x_{1}^{\prime},x_{2}^{\prime})
are adjacent if
x_{1}
is adjacent to
x_{1}^{\prime}
and
x_{2}
is adjacent to
x_{2}^{\prime}.
The adjacency matrix of
X_{1}\times X_{2}
is
A(X_{1})\otimes A(X_{2})
, where
A\otimes B
denotes the Kronecker product of matrices
A
and
B.
We use
\mathbb{I}_{n}
and
\mathbb{J}_{n}
to denote the identity matrix and the matrix with all entries 1 of order
n
, respectively, and
\mathbb{0_{m\times n}}
denotes the null matrix.
It is easy to see that

\mathfrak{L}(X\times K_{m})=\mathbb{J}_{m}\otimes(-A(X))+\mathbb{I}_{m}\otimes(A(X)+(m-1)\mathfrak{D}(X)).

The structure of
\mathfrak{L}(X\times K_{m})
is given by

\mathfrak{L}(X\times K_{m})=\begin{bmatrix}(m-1)\mathfrak{D}(X)&-A(X)&-A(X)&\cdots&-A(X)\\
-A(X)&(m-1)\mathfrak{D}(X)&-A(X)&\cdots&-A(X)\\
-A(X)&-A(X)&(m-1)\mathfrak{D}(X)&\cdots&-A(X)\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
-A(X)&-A(X)&-A(X)&\cdots&(m-1)\mathfrak{D}(X)\end{bmatrix}.

The spectrum of
\mathfrak{L}(X\times K_{m})
can be obtained from the spectrum of
(m-1)\mathfrak{L}(X)
and
A(X)+(m-1)\mathfrak{D}(X).

Let
\lambda
be an eigenvalue of
\mathfrak{L}(X)
with its corresponding eigenvector
v
, then for
V=(v,v,\ldots,v)^{T}
,
\mathfrak{L}(X\times K_{m})V=(m-1)\lambda V.
Suppose that
\lambda^{\prime}
be an eigenvalue of
A(X)+(m-1)\mathfrak{D}(X)
with corresponding eigenvector
v^{\prime}.
Then

\{(v^{\prime},-v^{\prime},\mathbb{0_{n\times 1}},\mathbb{0_{n\times 1}},\ldots,\mathbb{0_{n\times 1}})^{T},(v^{\prime},\mathbb{0_{n\times 1}},-v^{\prime},\mathbb{0_{n\times 1}},\ldots,\mathbb{0_{n\times 1}})^{T},\ldots,(v^{\prime},\mathbb{0_{n\times 1}},\mathbb{0_{n\times 1}},\ldots,-v^{\prime})^{T}\}

are
m-1
dimensional linearly independent eigenvectors of
\mathfrak{L}(X\times K_{m}).
As the sum of the multiplicities of the obtained eigenvalues equals the number of vertices in
X\times K_{m}
, it shows that the union of the spectrum of
A(X)+(m-1)\mathfrak{D}(X)
and
(m-1)\mathfrak{L}(X)
with multiplicity
m-1
and 1 respectively, yields the spectrum of
\mathfrak{L}(X\times K_{m}).
For the sake of convenience, we denote the matrix
A(X)+(m-1)\mathfrak{D}(X)
by
Q_{m-1}(X)
or simply
Q_{m-1}.
One can see that
Q_{1}(X)
is the
signless Laplacian matrix
of the graph
X.
For more information about
Q_{1}
one can refer
[
2
,
3
,
4
]
.
We illustrate the above process by computing the spectrum of
K_{n-1}\times K_{m}
in Example
1
.

Example 1
.

The eigenvalues of
\mathfrak{L}(K_{n-1}\times K_{m})
are given by the union of spectra of
\mathbb{J}_{n-1}+((m-1)(n-2)-1)\mathbb{I}_{n-1}
and
(m-1)(-\mathbb{J}_{n-1}+(n-1)\mathbb{I}_{n-1}).
The eigenvalues of
\mathbb{J}_{n}
are
0
and
n
with multiplicity
n-1
and

respectively. It is easy to check that the eigenvalues of
\mathfrak{L}(K_{n-1}\times K_{m})
are
0,(m-1)(n-2)-1,n+(m-1)(n-2)-2
and
(n-1)(m-1).
Therefore,
a(K_{n-1}\times K_{m})=(n-2)(m-1)-1.

If
X
is a tree, we denote the graph
L(X)\times K_{m}
by
{\beta_{m}}(X)
, where
L(X)
denotes the line graph of
X.
The objective of this paper is to compute
a({\beta_{m}}(X)).
Note that,
a({\beta_{m}}(X))=min\{(m-1)a(L(X)),q_{min}^{m-1}(L(X))\},
where

denotes the smallest eigenvalue of

Hence, the problem boils down to computing

and

From Example

a({\beta_{m}}(K_{1,n-1}))\linebreak=(n-2)(m-1)-1
as

The converse is also true

if

, then

It is well known that the line graph of a tree has a cut vertex and

, where

and

denotes the vertex connectivity of

This implies

The spectrum of
(m-1)\mathfrak{L}(L(X))
is contained in the spectrum of
\mathfrak{L}({\beta_{m}}(X))
, therefore
0\leq a({\beta_{m}}(X))\leq m-1.
Thus one can see that there is a large gap between
a({\beta_{m}}(K_{1,n}))
and

where
X
is different from
K_{1,n-1}
.

We know that
a({\beta_{m}}(X))\geq 0.
By the result of Imrich and Klav̌zar
[

]
, which states that

is connected if and only if both

and

are connected and at least one of them is non-bipartite, we deduce that

and

So we define,

Theorem 6
.

Let

Then

is a

, where

Proof.

Suppose that
a({\beta_{m}}(X))=m-1
. We know

a({\beta_{m}}(X))=min\{(m-1)a(L(X)),q_{min}^{m-1}(L(X))\}.

Since
a(L(X))\leq 1
, this implies that
a(L(X))=1
and
q_{min}^{m-1}(L(X))\geq m-1.
By Lemma
5
,
X
is a
T(1,s,t).
To prove

s=1
and
t\geq 1
, then
q_{min}^{m-1}L(X)<m-1.

Case1:
Let
s=t=1.
The characterstic polynomial for

is
\phi_{\lambda}=\lambda^{3}-4(m-1)\lambda^{2}+(5(m-1)^{2}-2)\lambda-2m(m-1)(m-2)=0.
Its roots are
m-1,\frac{3(m-1)\pm\sqrt{m^{2}-2m+9}}{2}.
It can be easily observed that,
\frac{3(m-1)-\sqrt{(m-1)^{2}+8}}{2}<m-1.

Case2:
Let
s=1
and
t>1
, then we show that
q_{min}^{m-1}(L(X))<m-1.
To prove this we choose the principal submatrix
\begin{bmatrix}(m-1)(t+1)&1\\
1&(m-1)\end{bmatrix}
of

Q_{m-1}=\begin{bmatrix}(m-1)(1+t)&1&\mathbb{1_{1\times t}}\\
1&m-1&\mathbb{0_{1\times(n-3)}}\\
\mathbb{1_{t\times 1}}&\mathbb{0_{(n-3)\times 1}}&\mathbb{J}_{t}+((m-1)t-1)\mathbb{I}_{t}\end{bmatrix},

where
\mathbb{1_{m\times n}}
denotes the
m\times n
matrix with all entries equal to
1.

Its eigenvalues are

\frac{(m-1)(t+2)\pm\sqrt{t^{2}(m-1)^{2}+4}}{2}.

By Cauchy’s interlacing theorem,
q_{min}^{m-1}(L(X))\leq\frac{(m-1)(t+2)-\sqrt{t^{2}(m-1)^{2}+4}}{2}<\frac{(t+2)(m-1)-t(m-1)}{2}=m-1.

Conversely, suppose that
X
is a
T(1,s,t)
, where
s,t\geq 2.
We first show

The eigenvalues of
\mathfrak{L}(\beta_{2}(X))

and

5
,
a(L(X))=1
. The matrix

has the following form

\mathbb{1_{s\times 1}}&U_{1}&\mathbb{0_{s\times(n-s-2)}}\\
\mathbb{1_{t\times 1}}&\mathbb{0_{t\times(n-t-2)}}&V_{1}\end{bmatrix},

where
U_{1}=\mathbb{J}_{s}+(s-1)\mathbb{I}_{s}
and
V_{1}=\mathbb{J}_{t}+(t-1)\mathbb{I}_{t}.
Let
v_{i},
where
1\leq i\leq s-1
be an eigenvector corresponding to eigenvalue
s-1
of
U_{1}.
A simple check shows that
Q_{1}V_{i}=(s-1)V_{i}
, where
V_{i}=\begin{pmatrix}0\\
v_{i}\\
\mathbb{0}_{t\times 1}\end{pmatrix}.
Similarly, it can be seen that
t-1
is an eigenvalue of
Q_{1}
with multiplicity
t-1.
Let
s\geq t
, for
v=(t-s,\underbrace{1,\ldots,1}_{s},\underbrace{-1,\ldots,-1}_{t})
, note that
Q_{1}v=(s+t-1)v
. Suppose that
\lambda_{t}
and
\lambda_{t}^{\prime}
are the remaining eigenvalues. Thus we have Equation
1

\lambda_{t}+\lambda_{t}^{\prime}=2(s+t)-1.

(1)

Since
Tr(Q_{1}^{2})=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}^{2}
, we obtain Equation
2

\lambda_{t}\lambda_{t}^{\prime}=-2(s+t)+4st.

(2)

From Equation
1
and
2

\lambda_{t}^{2}-(2(s+t)-1)\lambda_{t}+(-2s-2t+4st)=0.

Hence,

For

.

Corollary 7
.

Let
X\in\mathfrak{S}_{m}.
Then
{\beta_{m}}(X)
is Laplacian integral if and only if

is a
T(1,s,t)
, where

\lambda^{3}-a\lambda^{2}+b\lambda-c=0
has integral roots,

Proof.

We first give the spectrum of
\mathfrak{L}(L(X)),
where
X=T(1,s,t).

The Laplacian matrix of

can be expressed in the following form

\begin{bmatrix}s+t&\mathbb{-1_{1\times s}}&\mathbb{-1_{1\times t}}\\
\mathbb{-1_{s\times 1}}&U&\mathbb{0_{s\times(n-s-2)}}\\
\mathbb{-1_{t\times 1}}&\mathbb{0_{t\times(n-t-2)}}&V\end{bmatrix},

where

,

It is easy to see that
1,s+1,t+1
and
s+t-1
are eigenvalues of
\mathfrak{L}(L(X))
with multiplicity
1,s-1,t-1
and 1, respectively. The remaining eigenvalues are the eigenvalues of the following matrix

Q=Q_{m-1}(L(X))=\begin{bmatrix}(m-1)(s+t)&\mathbb{1_{1\times s}}&\mathbb{1_{1\times t}}\\
\mathbb{1_{s\times 1}}&\mathbb{J}_{s}+((m-1)s-1)\mathbb{I}_{s}&\mathbb{0_{s\times(n-s-2)}}\\
\mathbb{1_{t\times 1}}&\mathbb{0_{t\times(n-t-2)}}&\mathbb{J}_{t}+((m-1)t-1)\mathbb{I}_{t}\end{bmatrix}.

It can be easily seen that
(m-1)s-1
and
(m-1)t-1
are eigenvalues of
Q_{m-1}(L(X))
with multiplicity
s-1
and
t-1
, respectively. Let
{\lambda_{1}},{\lambda_{2}}
and
{\lambda_{3}}
be the remaining three eigenvalues of
Q_{m-1}
. As the trace of a matrix is equal to the sum of its eigenvalues, we have Equation
3

{\lambda_{1}}+{\lambda_{2}}+{\lambda_{3}}=(2m-1)(s+t)-2.

(3)

Since
Tr(Q^{2})=Tr(A^{2})+(m-1)^{2}Tr(\mathfrak{D}^{2})=s(s+1)+t(t+1)+(m-1)^{2}((s+t)^{2}+s^{3}+t^{3})
and

, we get Equation
4

\sum_{i=1}^{3}\lambda_{i}^{2}=(1+2(m-1)^{2}+2(m-1))s^{2}+(1+2(m-1)^{2}+2(m-1))t^{2}-2(m-1)s-2(m-1)t+2(m-1)^{2}st+2.

(4)

Now using Equation
4
and the algebraic identity of

, we deduce Equation

{\lambda_{1}}{\lambda_{2}}+{\lambda_{2}}{\lambda_{3}}+{\lambda_{3}}{\lambda_{1}}=m(m-1)s^{2}+m(m-1)t^{2}-(3m-1)s-(3m-1)t+m(3m-2)st+1.

(5)

A simple computation reveals that
Tr(Q^{3})=Tr(A^{3})+(m-1)^{3}Tr(\mathfrak{D}^{3})+3(m-1)Tr(\mathfrak{D}^{2})
.
Furthermore,
Tr(Q^{3})
is equal to
\sum_{i=1}^{3}\lambda_{i}^{3}+((m-1)s-1))^{3}(s-1)+((m-1)t-1))^{3}(t-1)

3
and
5

\sum_{i=1}^{3}\lambda_{i}^{3}-3{\lambda_{1}}{\lambda_{2}}{\lambda_{3}}=({\lambda_{1}}+{\lambda_{2}}+{\lambda_{3}})(({\lambda_{1}}+{\lambda_{2}}+{\lambda_{3}})^{2}-3({\lambda_{1}}{\lambda_{2}}+{\lambda_{2}}{\lambda_{3}}+{\lambda_{3}}{\lambda_{1}}))
and obtain Equation

{\lambda_{1}}{\lambda_{2}}{\lambda_{3}}=m(-m+1)s^{2}+m(-m+1)t^{2}+ms+mt-2m^{2}st+m^{2}(m-1)s^{2}t+m^{2}(m-1)st^{2}.

(6)

From Equation
3
,
5
and
6

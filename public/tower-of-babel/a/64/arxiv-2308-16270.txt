Limit theorems for unbounded cluster functionals of regularly varying time series
Zaoli Chen, Rafał Kulik

Abstract
A “blocks method” is used to define clusters of extreme values in stationary time series. The cluster starts at the first large value in the block and ends at the last one. The block cluster measure (the point measure at clusters) encodes different aspects of extremal properties. Its limiting behaviour is handled by vague convergence, hence the set of test functions consists of bounded, shift-invariant functionals that vanish around zero. If unbounded or non shift-invariant functionals are considered, we may obtain convergence at a different rate, depending on the type of the functional and the block size (small vs . large blocks). There are two prominent examples of such functionals: the locations of large jumps and the cluster length. We obtain a comprehensive characterization of the limiting behaviour of the block cluster measure evaluated at such functionals for stationary, regularly varying time series. Once the convergence of the block cluster measure is established, we can proceed with consistency of the empirical cluster measure. Consistency holds in the small and moderate blocks scenario, while fails in the large blocks situation. Next, we continue with weak convergence of the empirical cluster processes. The starting point is the seminal paper by Drees and Rootzen (2010). Under the appropriate uniform integrability condition (related to small blocks) the results in the latter paper are still valid. In the moderate and large blocks scenario, the Drees and Rootzen empirical cluster process diverges, but converges weakly when re-normalized properly.

Introduction

For the last several years, one of the main challenges in extreme value statistics has been modelling and estimation of clusters of extremes values. These clusters stem from temporal dependence in time series that represent real-world phenomena. Cluster indices can be used to quantify different aspects of clustering of large values. The classical extremal index, the quantity that appears as an additional parameter in the limiting distribution of maxima in stationary sequences, is one example of the cluster index. The extremal index achieves values in
(0,1]

Unbounded cluster functionals.

In this paper we want to study general functionals. Besides the natural mathematical generalisation, there are several motivations for this.
A cluster length, the distance between the location of the last and the first large value in a block, plays an important role in
[
6
]
. Furthermore, it plays a very special role in the context of asymptotic expansion of blocks estimators, the problem studied in
[
4
]
. Note that the cluster length is shift-invariant, but unbounded. Hence, at the first step of our analysis we need to extend vague convergence of the block cluster measure to convergence of unbounded cluster functionals. Their growth will be controlled by a power of the cluster length and as such we will refer to them as the
cluster length-type
functionals. It turns out that such an extension is non-trivial. Under the appropriate uniform integrability condition we can recover (
1.2

The latter in turn is related to
small blocks

in case of
large blocks
, (
1.2

Beyond shift-invariance.

Likewise, for some unbounded non shift-invariant functionals, (
1.2

In summary, in the first step of our analysis we study the limit

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\mathbb{E}[H(u_{n}^{-1}\boldsymbol{X}_{1,r_{n}})]}{a_{n}}\;,

for appropriately chosen functional

Consistency of the empirical cluster measure.

An empirical counterpart of

Weak convergence of empirical cluster process(es).

The empirical cluster process

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathbb{G}}_{n}(H)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{nw_{n}}}\sum_{j=1}^{m_{n}}\left\{H(\mathbb{X}_{j})-\mathbb{E}[H(\mathbb{X}_{j})]\right\}\;,

In case of functionals that record jump locations, again a modified empirical cluster process has to be introduced. See

Statistical consequences.

The take out message of this paper is that the ”classical” theory of weak convergence of clusters (as introduced in
[
6
]
) may fail in case of unbounded functionals. The asymptotics may depend on the type of the functional and the block size. Large blocks should be avoided, since they may lead to either different central limit theorem or inconsistency of blocks estimators.

On the other hand, the blocks estimators based on unbounded functionals may serve as alternative way of estimating cluster indices. In particular, they may help with a downward bias when estimating the classical extremal index. See
Example

4.2.2
.

1.2
What is missing?

We provide a comprehensive theory in the small blocks scenario. The behaviour in the large blocks situation is limited to some specific functionals and under rather restrictive dependence assumptions. This can be viewed as a limitation of the paper, however, one can argue that large blocks scenario is much less relevant from the statistical perspective.

Also, we do not consider here functionals that do not vanish around zero, for example those that lead to the large deviation index (see e.g.
[
11
]
). These functionals may be large due to a cumulation of small values. This may be prevented by imposing a ”negligibility of small values” condition, but it is still not suitable for the techniques used in the paper.

In particular, if
i_{1}=i_{2}=1
, then

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}g_{n}^{\rm left}(s;1,G)=\lim_{n\to\infty}g_{n}^{\rm right}(s;1,G)={\boldsymbol{\nu}}^{*}(G)\;.

(5.9)

If moreover
G\equiv 1
, then we will simplify the notation
g_{n}^{\rm left}(s;1,G)
as
g_{n}^{\rm left}(s)
and

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}g_{n}^{\rm left}(s)=\mathbb{P}(\boldsymbol{Y}_{-\infty,-1}^{\ast}\leqslant 1)={\vartheta}\;,\ \ \lim_{n\to\infty}g_{n}^{\rm right}(s)=\mathbb{P}(\boldsymbol{Y}_{1,\infty}^{\ast}\leqslant 1)={\vartheta}\;.

(5.10)

A function
f:\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
is
\gamma
-homogeneous if for all
a,s>0
,
f(as)=a^{\gamma}f(s)
. Denote

\displaystyle f_{n}(s)=f\left(\frac{[r_{n}s]}{r_{n}}\right)\;.

Since a homogeneous function is continuous, we have

\lim_{n\to\infty}f_{n}(s)=f(s)
.
Then, using (
5.6
) and the bounded convergence,

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{r_{n}^{\gamma+1}}\sum_{i=1}^{r_{n}}f(i)\mathbb{P}(\boldsymbol{X}_{1-i,-j_{1}}^{\ast}\leqslant u_{n},\boldsymbol{X}_{j_{2},r_{n}-i}^{\ast}\leqslant u_{n}\mid\left|\boldsymbol{X}_{0}\right|>u_{n})

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{0}^{1}f_{n}(s)g_{n}^{\rm two-sided}(s;j_{1},0,j_{2})\mathrm{d}s=\mathbb{P}(\boldsymbol{Y}_{-\infty,-j_{1}}^{\ast}\leqslant 1,\boldsymbol{Y}_{j_{2},\infty}^{\ast}\leqslant 1)\int_{0}^{1}f(s)\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=\frac{f(1)}{\gamma+1}\mathbb{P}(\boldsymbol{Y}_{-\infty,-j_{1}}^{\ast}\leqslant 1,\boldsymbol{Y}_{j_{2},\infty}^{\ast}\leqslant 1)\;,

(5.11)

Let now
G
be a bounded cluster functional such that
G(\boldsymbol{x})=0
whenever
\boldsymbol{x}^{\ast}\leqslant 1
. Then, using (
5.9
),

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{r_{n}^{\gamma+1}}\sum_{i=1}^{r_{n}}f(i)\mathbb{E}\left[G(\boldsymbol{X}_{1-i,r_{n}-i})\mathbbm{1}{\left\{\boldsymbol{X}_{1-i,-1}^{\ast}\leqslant u_{n}\mid\left|\boldsymbol{X}_{0}\right|>u_{n}\right\}}\right]

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{0}^{1}f_{n}(s)g_{n}^{\rm left}(s;1,G)\mathrm{d}s={\boldsymbol{\nu}}^{*}(G)\int_{0}^{1}f(s)\mathrm{d}s=\frac{f(1)}{\gamma+1}{\boldsymbol{\nu}}^{*}(G)\;,

(5.12a)

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{r_{n}^{\gamma+1}}\sum_{i=1}^{r_{n}}f(i)\mathbb{E}\left[G(\boldsymbol{X}_{1-i,r_{n}-i})\mathbbm{1}{\left\{\boldsymbol{X}_{1,r_{n}-i}^{\ast}\leqslant u_{n}\mid\left|\boldsymbol{X}_{0}\right|>u_{n}\right\}}\right]

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{0}^{1}f_{n}(s)g_{n}^{\rm right}(s;1,G)\mathrm{d}s={\boldsymbol{\nu}}^{*}(G)\int_{0}^{1}f(s)\mathrm{d}s=\frac{f(1)}{\gamma+1}{\boldsymbol{\nu}}^{*}(G)\;.

(5.12b)

Next, we will say that a function
f:\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
is a
(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})
-homogenous (with
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}\geqslant 0
) if for all
a,b,s,t>0
,
f(as,bt)=a^{\gamma_{1}}b^{\gamma_{2}}f(s,t)
. We will use the notation

\displaystyle f_{n}(s,t)=f\left(\frac{[r_{n}s]}{r_{n}},\frac{[r_{n}t]}{r_{n}}\right)\;.

Note that

\lim_{n\to\infty}f_{n}(s,t)=f(s,t)
.
As in (
5.11
),

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{r_{n}^{\gamma_{1}+\gamma_{2}+1}}\sum_{i=1}^{r_{n}}f(i,i)\mathbb{P}(\boldsymbol{X}_{1-i,-j_{1}}^{\ast}\leqslant u_{n},\boldsymbol{X}_{j_{2},r_{n}-i}^{\ast}\leqslant u_{n}\mid\left|\boldsymbol{X}_{0}\right|>u_{n})

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{0}^{1}f_{n}(s,s)g_{n}^{\rm two-sided}(s;j_{1},0,j_{2})\mathrm{d}s=\mathbb{P}(\boldsymbol{Y}_{-\infty,-j_{1}}^{\ast}\leqslant 1,\boldsymbol{Y}_{j_{2},\infty}^{\ast}\leqslant 1)\int_{0}^{1}f(s,s)\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=\frac{f(1,1)}{\gamma_{1}+\gamma_{2}+1}\mathbb{P}(\boldsymbol{Y}_{-\infty,-j_{1}}^{\ast}\leqslant 1,\boldsymbol{Y}_{j_{2},\infty}^{\ast}\leqslant 1)\;.

(5.13)

Using (
5.10
), a similar argument gives

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{r_{n}^{\gamma_{1}+\gamma_{2}+2}}\sum_{i_{1}=1}^{r_{n}}\sum_{i_{2}=i_{1}+1}^{r_{n}}f(i_{1},i_{2})\mathbb{P}(\boldsymbol{X}_{1-i_{1},-1}^{\ast}\leqslant u_{n}\mid\left|\boldsymbol{X}_{0}\right|>u_{n})\mathbb{P}(\boldsymbol{X}_{1,r_{n}-i_{2}}^{\ast}\leqslant u_{n}\mid\left|\boldsymbol{X}_{0}\right|>u_{n})

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{0}^{1}\left(\int_{s}^{1}f_{n}(s,t)g_{n}^{\rm right}(t)\mathrm{d}t\right)g_{n}^{\rm left}(s)\mathrm{d}s={\vartheta}^{2}f(1,1)\int_{0}^{1}s^{\gamma_{1}}\left(\int_{s}^{1}t^{\gamma_{2}}\mathrm{d}t\right)\mathrm{d}s\;.

(5.14)

We will refer to this type of convergence as the
vague convergence of clusters
.

Proof of

Proof of
Lemma

3.2.4
.

We use the first and the last jump decomposition simultaneously:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[f(t(1),t(N_{1}))\mathbbm{1}{\left\{A_{1}\right\}}]

\displaystyle=\sum_{i_{1}=1}^{r_{n}}\sum_{i_{2}=i_{1}}^{r_{n}}f(i_{1},i_{2})\mathbb{P}(\boldsymbol{X}_{1,i_{1}-1}^{\ast}\leqslant u_{n},\left|\boldsymbol{X}_{i_{1}}\right|>u_{n},\left|\boldsymbol{X}_{i_{2}}\right|>u_{n},\boldsymbol{X}_{i_{2}+1,r_{n}}^{\ast}\leqslant u_{n})

\displaystyle=w_{n}\sum_{i=1}^{r_{n}}f(i,i)\mathbb{P}(\boldsymbol{X}_{1-i,-1}^{\ast}\leqslant u_{n},\boldsymbol{X}_{1,r_{n}-i}^{\ast}\leqslant u_{n}\mid\left|\boldsymbol{X}_{0}\right|>u_{n})

\displaystyle\phantom{=}+\sum_{i=1}^{r_{n}}\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}f(i,j+i)\mathbb{P}(\boldsymbol{X}_{1-i,-1}^{\ast}\leqslant u_{n},\left|\boldsymbol{X}_{0}\right|>u_{n},\left|\boldsymbol{X}_{j}\right|>u_{n},\boldsymbol{X}_{j+1,r_{n}-i}^{\ast}\leqslant u_{n})

\displaystyle\phantom{=}+\sum_{i_{1}=1}^{r_{n}}\sum_{i_{2}=i_{1}+\ell+1}^{r_{n}}f(i_{1},i_{2})\mathbb{P}(\boldsymbol{X}_{1,i_{1}-1}^{\ast}\leqslant u_{n},\left|\boldsymbol{X}_{i_{1}}\right|>u_{n},\left|\boldsymbol{X}_{i_{2}}\right|>u_{n},\boldsymbol{X}_{i_{2}+1,r_{n}}^{\ast}\leqslant u_{n})

\displaystyle=:J_{1}(r_{n})+J_{2}(r_{n};\ell)+\widetilde{J}_{2}(r_{n};\ell)\;.

Using (
5.13
) we have

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{J_{1}(r_{n})}{r_{n}^{\gamma_{1}+\gamma_{2}+1}w_{n}}=\frac{f(1,1)}{\gamma_{1}+\gamma_{2}+1}\mathbb{P}(\boldsymbol{Y}_{-\infty,-1}^{\ast}\leqslant 1,\boldsymbol{Y}_{1,\infty}^{\ast}\leqslant 1)\;.

(5.15)

Set

\displaystyle D(r_{n};j)=\sum_{i=1}^{r_{n}}f(i,j+i)\mathbb{P}(\boldsymbol{X}_{1-i,-1}^{\ast}\leqslant u_{n},\left|\boldsymbol{X}_{0}\right|>u_{n},\left|\boldsymbol{X}_{j}\right|>u_{n},\boldsymbol{X}_{j+1,r_{n}-i}^{\ast}\leqslant u_{n})\;.

Then

\displaystyle J_{2}(r_{n};\ell)=\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}D(r_{n};j)\;,\ \ \widetilde{J}_{2}(r_{n};\ell)=\sum_{j=\ell+1}^{r_{n}}D(r_{n};j)\;.

For each
j=1,\ldots,\ell
, using the convergence (
5.6
) we obtain

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{r_{n}^{\gamma_{1}+\gamma_{2}+1}w_{n}}D(r_{n};j)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{0}^{1}f_{n}\left(s,\frac{j}{r_{n}}+s\right)g_{n}^{\rm two-sided}(s;1;j;j+1)\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=\frac{f(1,1)}{\gamma_{1}+\gamma_{2}+1}\mathbb{P}(\boldsymbol{Y}_{-\infty,-1}^{\ast}\leqslant 1,\left|\boldsymbol{Y}_{j}\right|>1,\boldsymbol{Y}_{j+1,\infty}^{\ast}\leqslant 1)\;.

Hence,

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{r_{n}^{\gamma_{1}+\gamma_{2}+1}w_{n}}J_{2}(r_{n};\ell)=\frac{f(1,1)}{\gamma_{1}+\gamma_{2}+1}\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}\mathbb{P}(\boldsymbol{Y}_{-\infty,-1}^{\ast}\leqslant 1,\left|\boldsymbol{Y}_{j}\right|>1,\boldsymbol{Y}_{j+1,\infty}^{\ast}\leqslant 1)\;.

(5.16)

Next,

\displaystyle\widetilde{J}_{2}(r_{n};\ell)\leqslant\mathrm{cst}\sum_{i_{1}=1}^{r_{n}}\sum_{i_{2}=i_{1}+\ell+1}^{r_{n}}i_{1}^{\gamma_{1}}i_{2}^{\gamma_{2}}\mathbb{P}(\left|\boldsymbol{X}_{0}\right|>u_{n},\left|\boldsymbol{X}_{i_{2}-i_{1}}\right|>u_{n})

\displaystyle=O(1)\sum_{i=1}^{r_{n}}i^{\gamma_{1}}\sum_{j=\ell+1}^{r_{n}-i}(j+i)^{\gamma_{2}}\mathbb{P}(\left|\boldsymbol{X}_{0}\right|>u_{n},\left|\boldsymbol{X}_{j}\right|>u_{n})

\displaystyle=O(r_{n}^{\gamma_{1}+1}r_{n}^{\gamma_{2}})\sum_{j=\ell+1}^{r_{n}}\mathbb{P}(\left|\boldsymbol{X}_{0}\right|>u_{n},\left|\boldsymbol{X}_{j}\right|>u_{n})

and hence

\displaystyle\frac{\widetilde{J}_{2}(r_{n};\ell)}{r_{n}^{\gamma_{1}+\gamma_{2}+1}w_{n}}=O(1)\frac{1}{w_{n}}\sum_{j=\ell+1}^{r_{n}}\mathbb{P}(\left|\boldsymbol{X}_{0}\right|>u_{n},\left|\boldsymbol{X}_{j}\right|>u_{n})

and the latter term vanishes by letting
n\to\infty
and then
\ell\to\infty
on account of the stronger anticlustering condition
\mathcal{S}^{(0)}(r_{n},u_{n})
.

Now, we combine (
5.15
)-(
5.16
) to get (keep in mind

):’

\displaystyle\frac{f(1,1)}{\gamma_{1}+\gamma_{2}+1}\sum_{j=0}^{\ell}\mathbb{P}(\boldsymbol{Y}_{-\infty,-1}^{\ast}\leqslant 1,\left|\boldsymbol{Y}_{j}\right|>1,\boldsymbol{Y}_{j+1,\infty}^{\ast}\leqslant 1)\;.

Letting
\ell\to\infty
gives

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}(\boldsymbol{Y}_{-\infty,-1}^{\ast}\leqslant 1,\left|\boldsymbol{Y}_{j}\right|>1,\boldsymbol{Y}_{j+1,\infty}^{\ast}\leqslant 1)=\mathbb{P}(\boldsymbol{Y}_{-\infty,-1}^{\ast}\leqslant 1,\boldsymbol{Y}_{0,\infty}^{\ast}>1)={\vartheta}\;.

∎

Proof of
Lemma

3.3.3
.

As in the proof of
Lemma

3.2.4
we use the decompositions on the first and last jumps simultaneously. Since
H\in\mathcal{H}(\gamma)
, we bound
|H|(u_{n}^{-1}\boldsymbol{X}_{1,r_{n}})\leqslant C(t(N_{1})-t(1))^{\gamma}
. Thus,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[|H|(u_{n}^{-1}\boldsymbol{X}_{1,r_{n}})\mathbbm{1}{\left\{|H|(u_{n}^{-1}\boldsymbol{X}_{1,r_{n}})>L\right\}}\mathbbm{1}{\left\{A_{1}\right\}}\right]

\displaystyle\leqslant\mathrm{cst}\sum_{{i_{1}=1}\atop{i_{2}=i_{1}+L}}^{r_{n}}\mathbb{E}\left[|H|(u_{n}^{-1}\boldsymbol{X}_{i_{1},i_{2}})\mathbbm{1}{\left\{\boldsymbol{X}_{1,i_{1}-1}^{\ast}\leqslant u_{n},\left|\boldsymbol{X}_{i_{1}}\right|>u_{n},\left|\boldsymbol{X}_{i_{2}}\right|>u_{n},\boldsymbol{X}_{i_{2}+1,r_{n}}^{\ast}\leqslant u_{n}\right\}}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathrm{cst}\sum_{{i_{1}=1}\atop{i_{2}=i_{1}+L}}^{r_{n}}\mathbb{E}\left[|H|(u_{n}^{-1}\boldsymbol{X}_{0,i_{2}-i_{1}})\mathbbm{1}{\left\{\boldsymbol{X}_{1-i_{1},-1}^{\ast}\leqslant u_{n},\left|\boldsymbol{X}_{0}\right|>u_{n},\left|\boldsymbol{X}_{i_{2}-i_{1}}\right|>u_{n},\boldsymbol{X}_{i_{2}-i_{1}+1,r_{n}-i_{1}}^{\ast}\leqslant u_{n}\right\}}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathrm{cst}\sum_{i=1}^{r_{n}}\sum_{j=L}^{r_{n}-i}\mathbb{E}\left[|H|(u_{n}^{-1}\boldsymbol{X}_{0,j})\mathbbm{1}{\left\{\boldsymbol{X}_{1-i,-1}^{\ast}\leqslant u_{n},\left|\boldsymbol{X}_{0}\right|>u_{n},\left|\boldsymbol{X}_{j}\right|>u_{n},\boldsymbol{X}_{j+1,r_{n}-i}^{\ast}\leqslant u_{n}\right\}}\right]

(5.17)

\displaystyle\leqslant C\sum_{i=1}^{r_{n}}\sum_{j=L}^{r_{n}-i}j^{\gamma}\mathbb{P}(\boldsymbol{X}_{1-i,-1}^{\ast}\leqslant u_{n},\left|\boldsymbol{X}_{0}\right|>u_{n},\left|\boldsymbol{X}_{j}\right|>u_{n},\boldsymbol{X}_{j+1,r_{n}-i}^{\ast}\leqslant u_{n})

\displaystyle\leqslant Cr_{n}\sum_{j=L}^{r_{n}}j^{\gamma}\mathbb{P}(\left|\boldsymbol{X}_{0}\right|>u_{n},\left|\boldsymbol{X}_{j}\right|>u_{n})\;.

We conclude by applying
\mathcal{S}^{(\gamma)}(r_{n},u_{n})
.

Proof of
Lemma

3.3.5
.

We continue with (
5.1.5
) by setting
L=1
. Here,
H=\mathcal{L}
. Hence,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[H(u_{n}^{-1}\boldsymbol{X}_{1,r_{n}})\mathbbm{1}{\left\{A_{1}\right\}}\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{r_{n}}j^{\gamma}\sum_{i=1}^{r_{n}-j}\mathbb{P}\left(\boldsymbol{X}_{1-i,-1}^{\ast}\leqslant u_{n},\left|\boldsymbol{X}_{0}\right|>u_{n},\left|\boldsymbol{X}_{j}\right|>u_{n},\boldsymbol{X}_{j+1,r_{n}-i}^{\ast}\leqslant u_{n}\right)

\displaystyle=:\sum_{j=1}^{r_{n}}j^{\gamma}D(r_{n};j)=\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}j^{\gamma}D(r_{n};j)+\sum_{j=\ell+1}^{r_{n}}j^{\gamma}D(r_{n};j)

\displaystyle=:J(r_{n};\ell)+\widetilde{J}(r_{n};\ell)\;.

We have

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{r_{n}w_{n}}D(r_{n};j)

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{0}^{1}\mathbb{P}(\boldsymbol{X}_{1-[r_{n}s],-1}^{\ast}\leqslant u_{n},\left|\boldsymbol{X}_{j}\right|>u_{n},\boldsymbol{X}_{j+1,r_{n}-[r_{n}s]}^{\ast}\leqslant u_{n}\mid\left|\boldsymbol{X}_{0}\right|>u_{n})\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}(\boldsymbol{Y}_{-\infty,-1}^{\ast}\leqslant 1,\left|\boldsymbol{Y}_{j}\right|>1,\boldsymbol{Y}_{j+1,\infty}^{\ast}\leqslant 1)\;.

Hence,
J(r_{n};\ell)=O_{P}(r_{n}w_{n})
.

Next, in case of
\ell
-dependence,

\displaystyle\widetilde{J}(r_{n};\ell)=w_{n}^{2}\sum_{j=\ell+1}^{r_{n}}j^{\gamma}\sum_{i=1}^{r_{n}-j}\mathbb{P}\left(\boldsymbol{X}_{1-i,-1}^{\ast}\leqslant u_{n}\mid\left|\boldsymbol{X}_{0}\right|>u_{n}\right)\mathbb{P}\left(\boldsymbol{X}^{\ast}_{1,r_{n}-i-j}\leqslant u_{n}\mid\left|\boldsymbol{X}_{0}\right|>u_{n}\right)

\displaystyle=:\widetilde{J}^{*}(r_{n};\ell)\;.

The term
\widetilde{J}^{*}(r_{n};\ell)
converges at the rate
r_{n}^{\gamma+2}w_{n}^{2}
. Indeed, using the notation (
5.7a
)-(
5.7b
) and the limit in (
5.10
), bearing in mind
[r_{n}s]+[r_{n}t]\sim[r_{n}(s+t)]
, we have

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{r_{n}^{\gamma+2}w_{n}^{2}}\mathbb{E}\left[H(u_{n}^{-1}\boldsymbol{X}_{1,r_{n}})\mathbbm{1}{\left\{A_{1}\right\}}\right]

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{0}^{1}t^{\gamma}\left(\int_{0}^{1-t}g_{n}^{\rm left}(s)g_{n}^{\rm right}(s+t)\mathrm{d}s\right)\mathrm{d}t=\frac{{\vartheta}^{2}}{(\gamma+1)(\gamma+2)}\;.

In case of
\beta
-mixing,

\displaystyle\widetilde{J}(r_{n};\ell)=\widetilde{J}^{*}(r_{n};\ell)+J_{\beta}(r_{n})\;,

where

\displaystyle J_{\beta}(r_{n})\leq\mathrm{cst}\sum_{j=\ell+1}^{r_{n}}j^{\gamma}\sum_{i=1}^{r_{n}-j}\beta_{j}=\mathrm{cst}\sum_{j=\ell+1}^{r_{n}}j^{\gamma}(r_{n}-j)\beta_{j}\leq\mathrm{cst}\ r_{n}\sum_{j=\ell+1}^{r_{n}}j^{\gamma}\beta_{j}\;.

By the assumption,
J_{\beta}(r_{n})=o(r_{n}^{\gamma+2}w_{n}^{2})
.
The proof for the large blocks is concluded.
∎

5.1.6

Additional comments for unbounded cluster functionals

Remark 5.1
.

The result of
Lemma

3.3.5
should be compared to
Lemma

3.2.4
. Take
\gamma=\gamma_{1}=\gamma_{2}=1
and
f(s,t)=(t-s)_{+}
. Then

Lemma

3.2.4
gives
\mathbb{E}[(t(N_{1})-t(1))\mathbbm{1}{\left\{A_{1}\right\}}]/(r_{n}^{3}w_{n})\to 0
.

Lemma

3.3.5
allows to recover non-zero limit when scaled by
r_{n}^{3}w_{n}^{2}
:
t(1)
and
t(N_{1})
become independent when normalized by a smaller value. Indeed,
assume that
U
and
V
are independent, standard uniform random variables. Then
\mathbb{E}[(U-V)_{+}^{\gamma}]=1/((\gamma+1)(\gamma+2))
, which can be recognized as the right hand side of
(
3.5
).

Remark 5.2
.

The mixing condition (
3.4
) is somehow restrictive. Assume that
\beta_{j}=O(j^{-\delta})
,
\delta>0
. Then (
3.4
) holds whenever
r_{n}^{\delta}w_{n}^{2}\to\infty
. Thus,
\delta
has to be sufficiently large. In particular, the condition holds if the mixing coefficients decay at the exponential rate.

Remark 5.3
.

The proof of tightness in
Lemma

3.3.2
requires the anticlustering

⁢
A
C

(

r
n

,

u
n

)

only. The convergence of the moments requires the stronger condition

⁢

S

(
γ
)

(

r
n

,

u
n

)

. In i.i.d. case, the stronger anticlustering condition is equivalent to
r_{n}^{\gamma+1}w_{n}=o(1)
; cf.
Remark

2.2
. We argue that the latter assumption is necessary for convergence of moments of
\mathcal{L}_{1}
.

Assume that
\boldsymbol{X}
is i.i.d. Then,
the cluster length of the tail process is
\mathcal{L}(\boldsymbol{Y})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{d}}}{{=}}1
. We find sufficient and necessary conditions so that

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\left[\mathcal{L}_{1}\mid A_{1}\right]=1\;.

(5.18)

We have as
n\to\infty
,

\displaystyle f(1)

\displaystyle:=\mathbb{P}\left(\mathcal{L}_{1}=1\mid A_{1}\right)=\binom{r_{n}}{1}w_{n}(1-w_{n})^{r_{n}-1}\bigg/\Big[1-(1-w_{n})^{r_{n}}\Big]\sim 1.

Next, for any
i\geqslant 2
, we have

\displaystyle f(i)

\displaystyle:=\mathbb{P}\left(\mathcal{L}_{1}=i\mid A_{1}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-(1-w_{n})^{r_{n}}}\sum_{j=1}^{r_{n}-i+1}\underbrace{(1-w_{n})^{j-1}w_{n}}_{t(1)=j}\cdot\underbrace{(1-w_{n})^{r_{n}-(j+i-1)}w_{n}}_{t(N_{1})=j+i-1}

\displaystyle=\frac{w_{n}^{2}(r_{n}-i+1)(1-w_{n})^{r_{n}-i}}{1-(1-w_{n})^{r_{n}}}\sim\frac{r_{n}-i+1}{r_{n}}w_{n}.

uniformly for all
2\leqslant i\leqslant r_{n}
.

Clearly, the limit in (
5.18
) is equivalent to
\sum_{i=2}^{r_{n}}i^{\gamma}f(i)\to 0
.
It suffices to note that

\displaystyle\sum_{i=2}^{r_{n}}i^{\gamma}f(i)

\displaystyle\sim\sum_{i=2}^{r_{n}}i^{\gamma}w_{n}\frac{r_{n}-i+1}{r_{n}}

\displaystyle=r_{n}^{\gamma+1}w_{n}\sum_{i=2}^{r_{n}}\left(\frac{i}{r_{n}}\right)^{\gamma}\cdot\frac{r_{n}-i+1}{r_{n}}\cdot\frac{1}{r_{n}}\sim r_{n}^{\gamma+1}w_{n}{\rm Beta}(\gamma+1,2)\;,

where
{\rm Beta}(\cdot,\cdot)
is the standard Beta function. This yields that the condition
r_{n}^{\gamma+1}w_{n}\to 0
is necessary (and sufficient) for the conditional convergence of moments of the cluster length.

Remark 5.4
.

From (
3.3
) we obtain for
q\geqslant 1
,

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\left(\mathcal{L}_{1}=q\mid A_{1}\right)=\frac{\mathbb{P}(\boldsymbol{Y}_{-\infty,-1}^{\ast}\leqslant 1,\left|\boldsymbol{Y}_{q-1}\right|>1,\boldsymbol{Y}_{q,\infty}^{\ast}\leqslant 1)}{{\vartheta}}=:f(q)\;.

Then, since
\left|\boldsymbol{Y}_{0}\right|>1
, bearing in mind (
5.1
),

\displaystyle f(1)+\sum_{q=2}^{\infty}f(q)

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathbb{P}(\boldsymbol{Y}_{-\infty,-1}^{\ast}\leqslant 1,\boldsymbol{Y}_{1,\infty}^{\ast}\leqslant 1)}{{\vartheta}}+\frac{1}{{\vartheta}}\sum_{q=2}^{\infty}{\mathbb{P}(\boldsymbol{Y}_{-\infty,-1}^{\ast}\leqslant 1,\left|\boldsymbol{Y}_{q-1}\right|>1,\boldsymbol{Y}_{q,\infty}^{\ast}\leqslant 1)}

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathbb{P}(\boldsymbol{Y}_{-\infty,-1}^{\ast}\leqslant 1,\boldsymbol{Y}_{1,\infty}^{\ast}\leqslant 1)}{{\vartheta}}+\frac{\mathbb{P}(\boldsymbol{Y}_{-\infty,-1}^{\ast}\leqslant 1,\boldsymbol{Y}_{1,\infty}^{\ast}>1)}{{\vartheta}}=1\;.

Hence, the limiting conditional distribution of the cluster length is a proper distribution.

Remark 5.5
.

Take
q=0
. The the left hand side of (
3.3
) is by the definition of the cluster length equal to 1. At the same time, since
|\boldsymbol{Y}_{0}|>1
, using (
5.1
), the right hand side of (
3.3
) also reduces to one.
In case of extremal independence,
\boldsymbol{Y}_{1,\infty}^{\ast}\equiv 0
and hence
\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{L}_{1}>1\mid A_{1})=0
as expected.

Proofs II - the empirical cluster processes

Proof of
Theorem

4.1.1
.

We start with consistency.
We note that
\mathcal{S}^{(\gamma(2+\delta))}(r_{n},u_{n})
implies

\mathcal{S}^{(\gamma)}(r_{n},u_{n})
. Hence

Corollary

3.3.4
gives
\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\left[\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\nu}}_{n,r_{n}}^{*}(H)\right]={\boldsymbol{\nu}}^{*}(H)
.
Thanks to the mixing assumption, we can treat the blocks as independent. Then

\displaystyle\mathrm{Var}\left(\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\nu}}_{n,r_{n}}^{*}(H)\right)\leq m_{n}\frac{\mathbb{E}[H^{2}(u_{n}^{-1}\boldsymbol{X}_{1,r_{n}})]}{n^{2}w_{n}^{2}}=\frac{\mathbb{E}[H^{2}(u_{n}^{-1}\boldsymbol{X}_{1,r_{n}})]}{r_{n}w_{n}}\frac{1}{nw_{n}}\;.

Since
\mathcal{S}^{(2\gamma)}(r_{n},u_{n})
holds, by
Corollary

3.3.4
and
nw_{n}\to\infty
, the variance of the estimator vanishes. Consistency has been established.

Next,
we verify that the class
\mathcal{H}(\gamma)
fulfills the conditions of Theorem 10.2.1 in
[
9
]
.

(BCLT1)

This conditions states that (
1.2
) holds. This in turn is guaranteed by
Lemma

3.3.3
.

(BCLT2)

We need to show that for any
\eta>0
we have

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{r_{n}w_{n}}\mathbb{E}[H^{2}(u_{n}^{-1}\boldsymbol{X}_{1,r_{n}})\mathbbm{1}{\left\{|H|(u_{n}^{-1}\boldsymbol{X}_{1,r_{n}})>\eta\sqrt{nw_{n}}\right\}}]=0\;.

The left hand-side is bounded by

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\mathbb{E}[|H|^{2+\delta}(u_{n}^{-1}\boldsymbol{X}_{1,r_{n}})]}{r_{n}w_{n}}\frac{1}{(nw_{n})^{\delta/2}}

which vanishes on account of the anticlustering assumption and
nw_{n}\to\infty
. Here, the anticlustering condition with
\gamma(2+\delta)
is needed. Indeed, if
H\in\mathcal{H}(\gamma)
, then
|H|^{2+\delta}
belongs to
\mathcal{H}(\gamma(2+\delta))
.

(BCLT3)

We need to verify that there exist functions
K_{n}:(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{\ell_{n}}\to\mathbb{R}
such that

\displaystyle\left|H(u_{n}^{-1}\boldsymbol{X}_{1,r_{n}})-H(u_{n}^{-1}\boldsymbol{X}_{1,r_{n}-\ell_{n}})\right|\leq K_{n}(\boldsymbol{X}_{r_{n}-\ell_{n}+1,r_{n}})

and

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\mathbb{E}[K_{n}(\boldsymbol{X}_{r_{n}-\ell_{n}+1,r_{n}})]}{r_{n}w_{n}}=0\;.

On account of

2.3.1
(iii),
H(u_{n}^{-1}\boldsymbol{X}_{1,r_{n}})\not=H(u_{n}^{-1}\boldsymbol{X}_{1,r_{n}-\ell_{n}})
if and only if

\boldsymbol{X}^{*}_{r_{n}-\ell_{n}+1,r_{n}}>u_{n}
. Thus, choose
K_{n}(\boldsymbol{X}_{r_{n}-\ell_{n}+1,r_{n}})=\mathbbm{1}{\left\{\boldsymbol{X}^{*}_{r_{n}-\ell_{n}+1,r_{n}}>u_{n}\right\}}
and then

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\mathbb{P}\left(\boldsymbol{X}^{*}_{r_{n}-\ell_{n}+1,r_{n}}>u_{n}\right)}{r_{n}w_{n}}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\mathbb{P}\left(\boldsymbol{X}^{*}_{1,\ell_{n}}>u_{n}\right)}{\ell_{n}w_{n}}\frac{\ell_{n}w_{n}}{r_{n}w_{n}}=0\;.

∎

Proof of
Theorem

4.1.3
.

The proof is almost identical to the one for
Theorem

4.1.1
.

Since

\mathcal{S}^{(\gamma)}(r_{n},u_{n})
holds,

Corollary

3.3.4
gives
\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\left[\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\nu}}_{n,r_{n}}^{*}(H)\right]={\boldsymbol{\nu}}^{*}(H)
.
Thanks to the mixing assumption, we can treat the blocks as independent. Then

\displaystyle\mathrm{Var}\left(\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\nu}}_{n,r_{n}}^{*}(H)\right)\leq m_{n}\frac{\mathbb{E}[H^{2}(u_{n}^{-1}\boldsymbol{X}_{1,r_{n}})]}{n^{2}w_{n}^{2}}=\frac{\mathbb{E}[H^{2}(u_{n}^{-1}\boldsymbol{X}_{1,r_{n}})]}{r_{n}^{2\gamma+2}w_{n}^{2}}\frac{r_{n}^{2\gamma+1}}{n}\;.

Since

3.3.5

At the same time.

We need to show that for any
\eta>0

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{r_{n}^{2\gamma+2}w_{n}^{2}}\mathbb{E}\left[H^{2}(u_{n}^{-1}\boldsymbol{X}_{1,r_{n}})\mathbbm{1}{\left\{|H|(u_{n}^{-1}\boldsymbol{X}_{1,r_{n}})>\eta\sqrt{nr_{n}^{2\gamma+1}}w_{n}\right\}}\right]=0\;.

The left hand-side is bounded by

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\mathbb{E}[|H|^{2+\delta}(u_{n}^{-1}\boldsymbol{X}_{1,r_{n}})]}{r_{n}^{(2+\delta)\gamma+2}w_{n}^{2}}\frac{1}{(nr_{n}w_{n}^{2})^{\delta/2}}\;.

The first ratio converges by

3.3.5
(thanks to the assumptions (ii)-(iii)), while the second one

∎

Abstract
In this paper, we analyze the null controllability property for a degenerate parabolic equation involving memory terms with a locally distributed control. We first derive a null controllability result for a nonhomogeneous degenerate heat equation via new Carleman estimates with weighted time functions that do not blow up at t = 0 t=0 . Then this result is successfully used with a classical fixed point to obtain null controllability for the initial memory system.



1
Introduction

In this work we are concerned with the null controllability result for a degenerate parabolic equation with memory by a distributed control force. More precisely, we consider the following controlled system:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\displaystyle y_{t}-(a(x)y_{x})_{x}=\int\limits_{0}^{t}b(t,s,x)y(s,x)\,ds+1_{\omega}u&&(t,x)\in Q=(0,T)\times(0,1),\\
y(t,1)=0,&&t\in(0,T),\\
\begin{cases}&y(t,0)=0,\qquad\quad(WD),\\
&(ay_{x})(t,0)=0,\quad(SD),\\
\end{cases}&&t\in(0,T),\\
y(0,x)=y_{0}(x),&&x\in(0,1).\end{array}\right.

(1.1)

Here,
\omega\Subset(0,1)
is a non-empty open set,
1_{\omega}
is the corresponding characteristic function,
u=u(t,x)
is the control function,
y=y(t,x)
is the
state and
b=b(t,s,x)\in L^{\infty}((0,T)\times Q)
is a memory kernel. Moreover, the diffusion coefficient
a

vanishes at the boundary
x=0
(i.e.,
a(0)=0
) and can be either weakly degenerate (WD), i.e.,

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}a\in C([0,1])\cap C^{1}((0,1]),\;a(0)=0,\;a>0\quad\text{in}\quad(0,1],\\
\exists\,\alpha\in[0,1),\quad\text{such that}\quad xa^{\prime}(x)\leq\alpha a(x),\quad\forall\,x\in[0,1],\end{array}\right.

(1.2)

or strongly degenerate (SD), i.e.,

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}a\in C^{1}([0,1]),\;a(0)=0,\;a>0\quad\text{in}\quad(0,1],\\
\exists\,\alpha\in[1,2),\quad\text{such that}\quad xa^{\prime}(x)\leq\alpha a(x),\quad\forall\,x\in[0,1],\\
\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\exists\,\beta\in(1,\alpha],\,x\mapsto\dfrac{a(x)}{x^{\beta}}\quad\text{is nondecreasing near}\quad 0,\quad\text{if}\quad\alpha>1,\\
\exists\,\beta\in(0,1),\,x\mapsto\dfrac{a(x)}{x^{\beta}}\quad\text{is nondecreasing near}\quad 0,\quad\text{if}\quad\alpha=1.\end{array}\right.\end{array}\right.

(1.3)

A typical example of coefficient
a
is the following:

a(x)=x^{\alpha},\quad\alpha\in(0,2).

The null controllability of parabolic equations without memory (i.e.
b\equiv 0
) is by now well understood, for both uniformly and degenerate diffusion coefficient, by means of distributed and boundary controls (see
[
1
,
2
,
6
,
9
,
13
,
14
,
15
]
and the references therein).

On the other hand, in the presence of memory terms, much less is known on the controllability of the underlying system.

When
a=b=1
, S. Guerrero and O. Imanuvilov prove in
[
17
]
that (
1.1
) fails to be null controllable with a boundary control. Indeed, there exists a set of initial states that cannot be driven to
0
in any positive final time. Then, similar result is proved by X. Zhou and H. Gao in
[
28
]
whenever
b
is a non-trivial constant; in this paper it is also proved that the approximate controllability holds. Later on, these results are extended in
[
29
]
to the context of one dimensional degenerate parabolic equation. In particular, the authors assume that
a(x)=x^{\alpha}
, being
x\in(0,1)
,
0\leq\alpha<1
and prove that the null controllability of (
1.1
) fails whereas the approximate property holds in a suitable state space with a boundary control acting at the extremity
x=0
or
x=1
.

Thus, it is important to see which kind of conditions on
b
we have to require so that the null controllability of (
1.1
) holds. In
[
19
,
25
]
R. Lavanya, K. Balachandran and B.R. Nagaraj obtained the null controllability of a nonlinear and non degenerate version of (
1.1
) assuming that the memory kernel is sufficiently smooth and vanishes at the neighborhood of initial and final times. In particular,

b(t,s,x)\equiv b(t,s)\quad\text{and}\quad\mathop{\rm supp}\nolimits b(\cdot,s)\Subset(t_{0},t_{1}),\quad 0<t_{0}<t<t_{1}<T,\quad\forall s\in(0,T).

(1.4)

The proof relies on Carleman estimates and a fixed point method. This assumption has been relaxed by Q. Tao and H. Gao in
[
26
]
, where the authors showed that null controllability holds provided
b
fulfills

e^{\frac{C}{(T-t)}}b\in L^{\infty}((0,T)\times Q)

(1.5)

for some positive constant
C
.

For related results on this subject, we refer to
[
22
]
for wave equation,
[
4
]
for viscoelasticity equation,
[
24
]
for thermoelastic system and
[
27
]
in the case of heat equation with hyperbolic memory kernel (see also the bibliography therein).

The purpose of this paper is to give a suitable condition on the memory kernel
b
in such a way that the degenerate parabolic equation with memory (
1.1
) is null controllable, that is there exists a control
u\in L^{2}(Q)
such that the associated solution of (
1.1
), corresponding to the initial data
y_{0}\in L^{2}(0,1)
,
satisfies

y(T,\cdot)=0\qquad\text{in}\;(0,1).

We include here a brief description of the proof strategy:
in a first step, we focus on the following nonhomogeneous degenerate parabolic system

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\displaystyle y_{t}-(a(x)y_{x})_{x}=f+1_{\omega}u&&(t,x)\in Q,\\
y(t,1)=0,&&t\in(0,T),\\
\begin{cases}&y(t,0)=0,\qquad\quad(WD),\\
&(ay_{x})(t,0)=0,\quad(SD),\\
\end{cases}&&t\in(0,T),\\
y(0,x)=y_{0}(x),&&x\in(0,1),\end{array}\right.

(1.6)

for a given function
f\in L^{2}(Q)
.

In particular, we establish suitable Carleman estimates for the associated adjoint problem using some classical weight time functions that blow up to
+\infty
as
t\rightarrow 0^{-},T^{+}
. Then, using a weight time function not exploding in the neighborhood of
t=0
, we derive a new modified Carleman estimate that would allow us to show null controllability of the underlying parabolic equation. As a consequence, we deduce null controllability result for some problems similar to the degenerate parabolic equation with memory. Finally, this controllability result combined with an appropriate application of Kakutani’s fixed point Theorem allows us to obtain the null controllability result for the original system (
1.1
) under a suitable condition on the kernel
b
.

Remark 1
.

We believe that the null controllability of system (
1.1
) can be obtained also following the same ideas in
[
25
,
19
]
. More precisely, by means of classical duality arguments, the null controllability property can be reduced to an observability inequality for the adjoint parabolic problem

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\displaystyle-v_{t}-(a(x)v_{x})_{x}=\int\limits_{t}^{T}b(s,t,x)v(s,x)\,ds&&(t,x)\in Q,\\
v(t,1)=0,&&t\in(0,T),\\
\begin{cases}&v(t,0)=0,\qquad\quad(WD),\\
&(av_{x})(t,0)=0,\quad(SD),\\
\end{cases}&&t\in(0,T),\\
v(T,x)=v_{T}(x),&&x\in(0,1),\end{array}\right.

(1.7)

where
v_{T}\in L^{2}(Q)
and
g\in L^{2}(Q)
.

Such an inequality is proved by R. Lavanya and K. Balachandran in the aforementioned reference through the use of a new Carleman estimate for (
1.7
) under a strict restriction on the memory kernel. Indeed, in order to treat the integral term in (
1.7
), the coefficient
b
need to be sufficiently smooth and to satisfy condition (
1.4
). One could expects the same condition for system (
1.1
).

However, in this paper, we follow the methodology used in
[
26
]
for the treatment of nondegenerate equation which permits us to show that system
(
1.1
) is null controllable provided the coefficient
b
satisfies only some exponential decay at the final time
t=T
(see (
5.3
)).

The outline of this paper is as follows: Section
2
is devoted to the well-posedness of systems (
1.1
) and (
1.6
) in suitable weighted spaces. In Section
3
, we develop a new Carleman estimate for the adjoint problem to the nonhomogeneous parabolic equation (
1.6
) and, in Section
4
, we apply such an estimate to deduce null controllability for (
1.6
). In Section
5
, using the Kakutani’s fixed point Theorem, we prove the null controllability result for the degenerate parabolic equation with memory (
1.1
) under suitable condition on the memory kernel. Finally, in Section
6
, we discuss various extensions of our result and give some perspectives related to this work.

2
Well-posedness results

The goal of this section is to study the well-posedness results for (
1.1
) and (
1.6
). First, we recall the following weighted Sobolev spaces (in the sequel, a.c. means absolutely continuous):

In the (WD) case:

\displaystyle H_{a}^{1}:=\Big\{y\in L^{2}(0,1):y\;

\displaystyle\text{a.c. in}\,[0,1],\;\sqrt{a}y_{x}\in L^{2}(0,1)\;\text{and}\;y(1)=y(0)=0\Big\}

and

\displaystyle H_{a}^{2}:=\Big\{y\in H_{a}^{1}(0,1):ay_{x}\in H^{1}(0,1)\Big\}.

In the (SD) case:

\displaystyle H_{a}^{1}:=\Big\{y\in L^{2}(0,1):y\,

\displaystyle\text{locally a.c. in}\,(0,1],\quad\sqrt{a}y_{x}\in L^{2}(0,1)\,\text{and}\,y(1)=0\Big\}

and

\displaystyle H_{a}^{2}:

\displaystyle=\Big\{y\in H_{a}^{1}(0,1):ay_{x}\in H^{1}(0,1)\Big\}

\displaystyle=\Big\{y\in L^{2}(0,1):y\,\text{locally a.c. in}\,(0,1],ay\in H^{1}_{0}(0,1),

\displaystyle\qquad ay_{x}\in H^{1}(0,1)\,\text{and}\,(ay_{x})(0)=0\Big\}.

In both cases, the norms are defined as follow

\displaystyle\|y\|_{H_{a}^{1}}^{2}:=\|y\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{2}+\|\sqrt{a}y_{x}\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{2},\qquad\|y\|_{H_{a}^{2}}^{2}:=\|y\|_{H_{a}^{1}}^{2}+\|(ay_{x})_{x}\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{2}.

We recall the following well-posedness result for system (
1.6
) (see, for instance,
[
2
,
5
]
).

Proposition 2.1
.

Assume that
y_{0}\in L^{2}(0,1)
,
f\in L^{2}(Q)
and
u\in L^{2}(Q)
. Then, system (
1.6
) admits a unique solution

y\in W_{T}:=L^{2}(0,T;H_{a}^{1}(0,1))\cap C([0,T];L^{2}(0,1))

(2.1)

such that

\|y\|_{L^{2}(0,T;H_{a}^{1}(0,1))}+\|y\|_{C([0,T];L^{2}(0,1))}\leq C\Big(\|y_{0}\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}+\|f\|_{L^{2}(Q)}+\|1_{\omega}u\|_{L^{2}(Q)}\Big),

(2.2)

for some positive constant
C
.
Moreover, if
y_{0}\in H_{a}^{1}(0,1)
, then

y\in Z_{T}:=L^{2}(0,T;H_{a}^{2}(0,1))\cap H^{1}(0,T;L^{2}(0,1))

and

\|y\|_{L^{2}(0,T;H_{a}^{2}(0,1))}+\|y\|_{H^{1}(0,T;L^{2}(0,1))}\leq C\Big(\|y_{0}\|_{H_{a}^{1}(0,1)}+\|f\|_{L^{2}(Q)}+\|1_{\omega}u\|_{L^{2}(Q)}\Big),

(2.3)

for some positive constant
C
.

Existence and uniqueness of solution for system (
1.1
) are established in the following result:

Proposition 2.2
.

Assume that
y_{0}\in L^{2}(0,1)
and
u\in L^{2}(Q)
. Then, system (
1.1
) admits a unique solution

y\in W_{T}
.

We emphasis that, in order to prove null controllability result for (
1.1
) (see Theorem
5.2
), we only need existence and uniqueness in the case
y_{0}\in L^{2}(0,1)
.

Proof.

The proof of this Proposition is a consequence of
[
16
, Theorem 1.1]
.

First of all, we transform (
1.1
) into the following Cauchy problem

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle y^{\prime}(t)+Ay(t)=\int\limits_{0}^{t}k(t,s,y(s))\,ds+f(t),\quad t\in(0,T),\\
y(0)=y_{0},\end{array}\right.

(2.4)

where

Ay(t):=-(ay_{x}(t))_{x},\quad f(t):=1_{\omega}u(t),\quad\text{for a.e. }t\in(0,T),

k(t,s,y(s)):=b(t,s,\cdot)y(s),\quad\text{for a.e. }(t,s)\in(0,T)^{2}.

Next, we are going to check that (
2.4
) satisfies the assumptions in the aforementioned Theorem.
To this aim, let
H_{a}^{-1}(0,1)
be the dual space of
H_{a}^{1}(0,1)
with respect to the pivot space

L^{2}(0,1)
, endowed with the natural norm

\|z\|_{H_{a}^{-1}}:=\sup_{\|y\|_{H_{a}^{1}}=1}\langle z,y\rangle_{H_{a}^{-1},H_{a}^{1}}.

Observe that

\langle Ay,z\rangle_{H_{a}^{-1},H_{a}^{1}}=\int_{0}^{1}ay_{x}z_{x}\,dx,\quad\forall z\in H_{a}^{1}(0,1),

\langle k(t,s,y),z\rangle_{H_{a}^{-1},H_{a}^{1}}=\int_{0}^{1}b(t,s,x)yz\,dx,\quad\text{for a.e.}\,(t,s)\in(0,T)^{2},\quad\forall z\in H_{a}^{1}(0,1),

for any
y\in H_{a}^{1}(0,1)
.

Hence, one can check easily that the operators
A
and
k
satisfy the following properties:

(a)

there exists a positive constant
C
such that
\|Ay\|_{H_{a}^{-1}}\leq C\|y\|_{H_{a}^{1}},\quad\forall y\in H_{a}^{1}(0,1);

(b)

there exists a positive constant
C
such that

\|Ay_{1}-Ay_{2}\|_{H_{a}^{-1}}\leq C\|y_{1}-y_{2}\|_{H_{a}^{1}},\quad\text{for any}\;y_{1},y_{2}\in H_{a}^{1}(0,1);

(c)

\exists\;\gamma>0\text{ and }\lambda>0
such that

\langle Ay_{1}-Ay_{2},y_{1}-y_{2}\rangle_{H_{a}^{-1},H_{a}^{1}}+\lambda\|y_{1}-y_{2}\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{2}\geq\gamma\|y_{1}-y_{2}\|_{H_{a}^{1}}^{2},

for any
y_{1},y_{2}\in H_{a}^{1}(0,1);

(d)

there exists a function

\beta:(0,T)^{2}\mapsto\mathbb{R}^{+}
such that

\|(k(t,s,y_{1})-k(t,s,y_{2}))\|_{H_{a}^{-1}}\leq\beta(t,s)\|y_{1}-y_{2}\|_{H_{a}^{1}},\quad\text{for a.e. }(t,s)\in(0,T)^{2},

for any
y_{1},y_{2}\in H_{a}^{1}(0,1).

Besides
\beta
is explicitly given by

\beta(t,s):=\|b(t,s,\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}(0,1)},\quad\text{for a.e. }(t,s)\in(0,T)^{2}.

Then, taking into account the fact that
b\in L^{\infty}((0,T)\times Q)
,
f\in L^{2}(Q)
and in view of
[
16
, Remark 1.2, 1.3]
,
we infer that all the assumptions of
[
16
, Theorem 1.1]
are fulfilled.
Consequently, the problem (
2.4
) has a unique solution

y\in L^{2}(0,T;H_{a}^{1}(0,1))\cap L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(0,1))

with
\quad y_{t}\in L^{2}(0,T;H_{a}^{-1}(0,1)).

Moreover, by Aubin Lions Theorem we also have

y\in C([0,T];L^{2}(0,1)).

Thus (
2.1
) is proved.

3
Carleman estimates

The goal of this section is to establish a suitable Carleman estimates for the following adjoint parabolic system

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\displaystyle-v_{t}-(a(x)v_{x})_{x}=g&&(t,x)\in Q,\\
v(t,1)=0,&&t\in(0,T),\\
\begin{cases}&v(t,0)=0,\qquad\quad(WD),\\
&(av_{x})(t,0)=0,\quad(SD),\\
\end{cases}&&t\in(0,T),\\
v(T,x)=v_{T}(x),&&x\in(0,1),\end{array}\right.

(3.1)

where
v_{T}\in L^{2}(0,1)
and
g\in L^{2}(Q)
.

As a first step, we introduce the following weight functions

\displaystyle\psi(x):=\gamma\Big(\int_{0}^{x}\frac{y}{a(y)}\,dy-d\Big),\quad\theta(t):=\frac{1}{\big[t(T-t)\big]^{4}},

(3.2)

\displaystyle\varphi(t,x):=\theta(t)\psi(x),

Now, let
\tilde{\omega}
be an arbitrary open subset of
\omega
and
\rho\in C^{2}([0,1])
be such that

\rho>0,\;\text{in}\;(0,1),\quad\rho(0)=\rho(1)=0\quad\text{and}\quad\rho_{x}\neq 0,\;\text{in}\;[0,1]\backslash\tilde{\omega}

and define

\Psi(x):=e^{\lambda\rho(x)}-e^{2\lambda\|\rho\|_{\infty}},\quad\eta(t,x):=\theta(t)\Psi(x).

(3.3)

The parameters
\lambda
,
d
and
\gamma
are positive constant satisfy

d>d^{\star}:=\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\frac{y}{a(y)}\,dy,\qquad\gamma>\frac{e^{2\lambda\|\rho\|_{\infty}}}{(d-d^{\ast})}

(3.4)

and to be specified later on. It clearly follows from (
3.4
) that

-\gamma d\leq\psi(x)<0,\quad\text{for all}\;x\in[0,1],

(3.5)

\psi(x)\leq\Psi(x),\;\,\text{for all}\;x\in[0,1],\;\,\varphi(t,x)\leq\eta(t,x),\;\,\text{for all}\;(t,x)\in Q

(3.6)

Moreover, we readily have from the definition of the function
\theta
that

|\theta^{\prime}(t)|\leq C\theta^{\frac{3}{2}}(t),\;\,\forall\,t\in[0,T],\;\,\text{and}\quad\theta(t)\rightarrow+\infty,\quad\text{as}\quad t\rightarrow 0^{-},T^{+}.

(3.7)

Remark 2
.

All the results stated in this paper remains true when the expression of the weighted time function

\theta(t):=\frac{1}{[t(T-t)]^{4}}
is replaced by
\theta(t):=\frac{1}{[t(T-t)]^{2}}

[

Moreover, if the problem is considered in
[t_{0},T]

\theta(t):=\frac{1}{[(t-t_{0})(T-t)]^{2}}

We also remind the following Hardy Poincaré inequality, which turns out to be a fundamental tool in proving Carleman estimates:

Proposition 3.1

[

There is a positive constant

\int_{0}^{1}\frac{a(x)}{x^{2}}y^{2}(x)\,dx\leq C_{HP}\int_{0}^{1}a(x)|y_{x}(x)|^{2}\,dx.

Then, we have the following Carleman estimate:

Theorem 3.1

Let

\displaystyle+s^{3}\theta^{3}\frac{x^{2}}{a(x)}v^{2}\Big)e^{2s\varphi}\,dx\,dt

for all

For the proof see
[

Next, by (

), we are going to derive a new modified Carleman inequality, that is an estimate with a weight time function exploding only at the final time
t=T
. This choice is done recalling the technique developed by A.V. Fursikov and O.Y. Imanuvilov in
[
15
]
in the context of uniformly parabolic equations. In our setting, this new weight allows us to derive a null controllability result for system (
1.1
) imposing a restriction on the kernel
b
only at the final time
t=T
(see (
5.3
)). To this end, let us introduce the following weight functions:

\beta(t):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\theta(\frac{T}{2})=\big(\frac{2}{T}\big)^{8},&\text{for}\;t\in\big[0,\frac{T}{2}\big],\\
\theta(t),&\text{for}\;t\in\big[\frac{T}{2},T\big],\end{array}\right.\Phi(t,x)=\beta(t)\psi(x),\quad\sigma(t,x):=\beta(t)\Psi(x)

and

\displaystyle\widehat{\Phi}(t):=\max\limits_{x\in[0,1]}\Phi(t,x)=\gamma(d^{\ast}-d)\beta(t),

(3.14)

\displaystyle{\displaystyle\Phi^{*}(t)}:=\min\limits_{x\in[0,1]}\Phi(t,x)=-\gamma d\beta(t).

In view of (
3.6
), we can see that the weight functions
\Phi
and
\sigma
satisfy the following inequality which is needed in what follows

\Phi(t,x)\leq\sigma(t,x),\quad\forall(t,x)\,\in Q.

(3.15)

Now, we are ready to state the following modified Carleman estimate, which reveals to be a major tool to obtain the null controllability result given in Theorem
5.2
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
k\geq 0
. Then, there exists two positive constants
C
and
s_{0}
such that every solution
v\in Z_{T}
of system (
3.1
) satisfies

\displaystyle s^{k}e^{2s\widehat{\Phi}(0)}\|v(0)\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{2}+\int\!\!\!\!\!\int_{Q}(s\beta)^{k}v^{2}e^{2s\Phi}\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle\qquad\leq Ce^{2s[\widehat{\Phi}(0)-\Phi^{*}(\frac{5T}{8})]}\Big(\int\!\!\!\!\!\int_{Q}(s\beta)^{k}g^{2}e^{2s\sigma}\,dx\,dt+\int\!\!\!\!\!\int_{Q_{\omega}}(s\beta)^{k+3}v^{2}e^{2s\sigma}\,dxdt\Big)

(3.16)

for all
s\geq s_{0}
.

Proof.

Let
\xi\in C^{\infty}([0,T])
be a cut-off function such that

\displaystyle 0\leq\xi\leq 1,\qquad\xi(t):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}1,&\text{for}\;t\in\Big[0,\dfrac{T}{2}\Big],\\
0,&\text{for}\;t\in\Big[\dfrac{5T}{8},T\Big]\end{array}\right.

and define
w=\tilde{\xi}v
, where
\tilde{\xi}=\beta^{\frac{k}{2}}\xi e^{s\widehat{\Phi}(0)}
and
v
solves (
3.1
).

Hence
w
satisfies

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\displaystyle-w_{t}-(a(x)w_{x})_{x}=-\tilde{\xi}^{\prime}v+\tilde{\xi}g,&&(t,x)\in Q,\\
w(t,1)=0,&&t\in(0,T),\\
\begin{cases}&w(t,0)=0,\qquad\quad(WD),\\
&(aw_{x})(t,0)=0,\quad(SD),\\
\end{cases}&&t\in(0,T),\\
w(T,x)=0,&&x\in(0,1).\end{array}\right.

(3.19)

Then, by (
2.2
) applied to the above system, one can see that

\|w(0)\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{2}+\|w\|_{L^{2}(Q)}^{2}\leq C\int\!\!\!\!\!\int_{Q}(-\tilde{\xi}^{\prime}v+\tilde{\xi}g)^{2}\,dx\,dt

(3.20)

for some constants
C>0
.

We estimate from below the two terms on the left hand side of (
3.20
) in the following way:

\|w(0)\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{2}=\|\beta^{\frac{k}{2}}(0)\xi(0)e^{s\widehat{\Phi}(0)}v(0)\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{2}=\Big(\dfrac{2}{T}\Big)^{8k}\|e^{s\widehat{\Phi}(0)}v(0)\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{2}

(3.21)

and

\|w\|_{L^{2}(Q)}^{2}=\int_{0}^{\frac{5T}{8}}\!\!\!\!\int_{0}^{1}\beta^{k}\xi^{2}e^{2s\widehat{\Phi}(0)}v^{2}\,dx\,dt\geq\int_{0}^{\frac{T}{2}}\!\!\!\!\int_{0}^{1}\beta^{k}v^{2}e^{2s\Phi}\,dx\,dt

(3.22)

since
\Phi\leq\widehat{\Phi}(0)\;\text{in}\,Q
.

Concerning the right hand side of (
3.20
), we have

\displaystyle\int\!\!\!\!\!\int_{Q}(-\tilde{\xi}^{\prime}v+\tilde{\xi}g)^{2}\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle=\int\!\!\!\!\!\int_{Q}\Big[\Big(-\frac{k}{2}\beta^{\prime}\beta^{\frac{k}{2}-1}\xi-\beta^{\frac{k}{2}}\xi^{\prime}\Big)e^{s\widehat{\Phi}(0)}v+\beta^{\frac{k}{2}}\xi e^{s\widehat{\Phi}(0)}g\Big]^{2}\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle\leq C\bigg(\int\!\!\!\!\!\int_{Q}(\beta^{\prime})^{2}\beta^{k-2}\xi^{2}e^{2s\widehat{\Phi}(0)}v^{2}\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle\quad+\int\!\!\!\!\!\int_{Q}\beta^{k}(\xi^{\prime})^{2}e^{2s\widehat{\Phi}(0)}v^{2}\,dx\,dt+\int\!\!\!\!\!\int_{Q}\beta^{k}\xi^{2}e^{2s\widehat{\Phi}(0)}g^{2}\,dx\,dt\bigg).

(3.23)

Observing that
\beta^{\prime}=0\;\text{in}\;[0,T/2]
,
\beta=\theta\;\text{in}\;[T/2,T]
and using (
3.7
), the fact that
\mathop{\rm supp}\nolimits\xi\subset[0,5T/8]
and
\mathop{\rm supp}\nolimits\xi^{\prime}\subset[T/2,5T/8]
, it follows that

e^{2s\widehat{\Phi}(0)}\int\!\!\!\!\!\int_{Q}(\beta^{\prime})^{2}\beta^{k-2}\xi^{2}v^{2}\,dx\,dt\leq Ce^{2s\widehat{\Phi}(0)}\int_{\frac{T}{2}}^{\frac{5T}{8}}\!\!\!\!\int_{0}^{1}\beta^{k+1}v^{2}\,dx\,dt

(3.24)

and

\displaystyle e^{2s\widehat{\Phi}(0)}\int\!\!\!\!\!\int_{Q}\beta^{k}(\xi^{\prime})^{2}v^{2}\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle\leq Ce^{2s\widehat{\Phi}(0)}\int_{\frac{T}{2}}^{\frac{5T}{8}}\!\!\!\!\int_{0}^{1}\beta^{k}v^{2}\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle\leq Ce^{2s\widehat{\Phi}(0)}\int_{\frac{T}{2}}^{\frac{5T}{8}}\!\!\!\!\int_{0}^{1}\beta^{k+1}v^{2}\,dx\,dt.

(3.25)

Hence, by the estimates (
3.20
)-(
3
), we find that

\displaystyle s^{k}\|e^{s\widehat{\Phi}(0)}v(0)

\displaystyle\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{2}+\int_{0}^{\frac{T}{2}}\!\!\!\!\int_{0}^{1}(s\beta)^{k}v^{2}e^{2s\Phi}\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle\leq C\Big(s^{k}\int_{\frac{T}{2}}^{\frac{5T}{8}}\!\!\!\!\int_{0}^{1}s^{k}\beta^{k+1}v^{2}e^{2s\widehat{\Phi}(0)}\,dx\,dt+\int_{0}^{\frac{5T}{8}}\!\!\!\!\int_{0}^{1}(s\beta)^{k}e^{2s\widehat{\Phi}(0)}g^{2}\,dx\,dt\Big).

(3.26)

Now, let us deal with the first term in the right-hand side of (
3
).

First, using the fact that
\beta=\theta
and
\Phi=\varphi\;\text{in}\;[T/2,T]
, one has

\displaystyle\int_{\frac{T}{2}}^{\frac{5T}{8}}\!\!\!\!\int_{0}^{1}s^{k}\beta^{k+1}v^{2}e^{2s\Phi}\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle=\int_{\frac{T}{2}}^{\frac{5T}{8}}\!\!\!\!\int_{0}^{1}s^{k}\theta^{k+1}v^{2}e^{2s\varphi}\,dx\,dt.

(3.27)

Then, applying Young’s inequality as in
[
2
]
, we see that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}v^{2}e^{2s\varphi}\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\bigg(\Big(\frac{a(x)}{x^{2}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{3}}v^{2}e^{2s\varphi}\bigg)^{\frac{3}{4}}\bigg(\frac{x^{2}}{a(x)}v^{2}e^{2s\varphi}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{4}}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\frac{3}{4}\int_{0}^{1}\Big(\frac{a(x)}{x^{2}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{3}}v^{2}e^{2s\varphi}\,dx+\frac{1}{4}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{x^{2}}{a(x)}v^{2}e^{2s\varphi}\,dx.

(3.28)

Let
p(x)=x^{4/3}a^{1/3}
, then since the function
x\mapsto\displaystyle\frac{x^{2}}{a}
is nondecreasing on
(0,1)
one has,

p(x)=a\Big(\frac{x^{2}}{a}\Big)^{\frac{2}{3}}\leq Ca(x).

Then, applying the Hardy-Poincaré inequality (
3.8
) to
ve^{s\varphi}
, we get

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\frac{a^{1/3}}{x^{2/3}}(ve^{s\varphi})^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{p(x)}{x^{2}}(ve^{s\varphi})^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{0}^{1}p(x)(ve^{s\varphi})_{x}^{2}\,dx\leq C\int_{0}^{1}a(x)(ve^{s\varphi})_{x}^{2}\,dx.

(3.29)

Using the definition of
\varphi
(see (
3.2
)), it follows that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\frac{a^{1/3}}{x^{2/3}}(ve^{s\varphi})^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{0}^{1}a(x)\big(v_{x}+s\varphi_{x}v)^{2}e^{2s\varphi}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{0}^{1}\Big(a(x)v_{x}^{2}+s^{2}\theta^{2}\frac{x^{2}}{a(x)}v^{2}\Big)e^{2s\varphi}\,dx.

(3.30)

By (
3
) and (
3
), we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}v^{2}e^{2s\varphi}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{0}^{1}\Big(a(x)v_{x}^{2}+s^{2}\theta^{2}\frac{x^{2}}{a(x)}v^{2}\Big)e^{2s\varphi}\,dx.

(3.31)

Hence, from (
3.27
) and (
3.31
), we get that

\displaystyle\int_{\frac{T}{2}}^{\frac{5T}{8}}\!\!\!\!\int_{0}^{1}

\displaystyle s^{k}\beta^{k+1}v^{2}e^{2s\Phi}\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{\frac{T}{2}}^{\frac{5T}{8}}\!\!\!\!\int_{0}^{1}s^{k}\theta^{k+1}\Big(a(x)v_{x}^{2}+s^{2}\theta^{2}\frac{x^{2}}{a(x)}v^{2}\Big)e^{2s\varphi}\,dxdt.

Thus, applying Carleman inequality (
3.2
), one has

\displaystyle s^{k}\int_{\frac{T}{2}}^{\frac{5T}{8}}\!\!\!\!\int_{0}^{1}\beta^{k+1}v^{2}e^{2s\Phi}\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{\frac{T}{2}}^{\frac{5T}{8}}\!\!\!\!\int_{0}^{1}\Big(s^{k}\theta^{k+1}a(x)v_{x}^{2}+s^{k+2}\theta^{k+3}\frac{x^{2}}{a(x)}v^{2}\Big)e^{2s\varphi}\,dxdt

\displaystyle\leq C\int\!\!\!\!\!\int_{Q}\Big((s\theta)^{k+1}a(x)v_{x}^{2}+(s\theta)^{k+3}\frac{x^{2}}{a(x)}v^{2}\Big)e^{2s\varphi}\,dxdt

\displaystyle\leq C\Big(\int\!\!\!\!\!\int_{Q}(s\theta)^{k}g^{2}e^{2s\eta}\,dx\,dt+\int\!\!\!\!\!\int_{Q_{\omega}}(s\theta)^{k+3}v^{2}e^{2s\eta}\,dxdt\Big).

(3.32)

Now observe that

\Phi^{*}\big(\frac{5T}{8}\big)\leq\Phi,\quad\text{in}\;\Big[0,\frac{5T}{8}\Big]\times\big[0,1\big].

(3.33)

Therefore

\displaystyle s^{k}\int_{\frac{T}{2}}^{\frac{5T}{8}}\!\!\!\!\int_{0}^{1}\beta^{k+1}v^{2}e^{2s\widehat{\Phi}(0)}\,dx\,dt=s^{k}\int_{\frac{T}{2}}^{\frac{5T}{8}}\!\!\!\!\int_{0}^{1}\beta^{k+1}v^{2}e^{2s[\widehat{\Phi}(0)-\Phi]}e^{2s\Phi}\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle\leq e^{2s[\widehat{\Phi}(0)-\Phi^{*}(\frac{5T}{8})]}s^{k}\int_{\frac{T}{2}}^{\frac{5T}{8}}\!\!\!\!\int_{0}^{1}\beta^{k+1}v^{2}e^{2s\Phi}\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle\leq Ce^{2s[\widehat{\Phi}(0)-\Phi^{*}(\frac{5T}{8})]}\Big(\int\!\!\!\!\!\int_{Q}(s\theta)^{k}g^{2}e^{2s\eta}\,dx\,dt+\int\!\!\!\!\!\int_{Q_{\omega}}(s\theta)^{k+3}v^{2}e^{2s\eta}\,dxdt\Big)

(3.34)

for
s
large enough.

Moreover, in view of (
3.15
) and (
3.33
), the second term in the right hand side of (
3
) reads as

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\frac{5T}{8}}\!\!\!\!\int_{0}^{1}(s\beta)^{k}e^{2s\widehat{\Phi}(0)}g^{2}\,dx\,dt=\int_{0}^{\frac{5T}{8}}\!\!\!\!\int_{0}^{1}(s\beta)^{k}e^{2s[\widehat{\Phi}(0)-\Phi]}e^{2s\Phi}g^{2}\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle\leq e^{2s[\widehat{\Phi}(0)-\Phi^{*}(\frac{5T}{8})]}\int_{0}^{\frac{5T}{8}}\!\!\!\!\int_{0}^{1}(s\beta)^{k}e^{2s\sigma}g^{2}\,dx\,dt.

Combining this last inequality with (
3
) and (
3
), it follows that

\displaystyle s^{k}\|e^{s\widehat{\Phi}(0)}v(0)\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{2}+\int_{0}^{\frac{T}{2}}\!\!\!\!\int_{0}^{1}(s\beta)^{k}v^{2}e^{2s\Phi}\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle\qquad\leq Ce^{2s[\widehat{\Phi}(0)-\Phi^{*}(\frac{5T}{8})]}\Big(\int\!\!\!\!\!\int_{Q}(s\theta)^{k}g^{2}e^{2s\eta}\,dx\,dt+\int\!\!\!\!\!\int_{Q_{\omega}}(s\theta)^{k+3}v^{2}e^{2s\eta}\,dxdt

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad\qquad+\int_{0}^{\frac{5T}{8}}\!\!\!\!\int_{0}^{1}(s\beta)^{k}g^{2}e^{2s\sigma}\,dx\,dt\Big).

(3.35)

On the other hand, proceeding as in (
3
), we also obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\frac{T}{2}}^{T}\!\!\!\!\int_{0}^{1}(s\beta)^{k}v^{2}e^{2s\Phi}\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle=\int_{\frac{T}{2}}^{T}\!\!\!\!\int_{0}^{1}(s\beta)^{k}v^{2}e^{2s\varphi}\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{\frac{T}{2}}^{T}\!\!\!\!\int_{0}^{1}\Big((s\theta)^{k}a(x)v_{x}^{2}+(s\theta)^{k+2}\frac{x^{2}}{a(x)}v^{2}\Big)e^{2s\varphi}\,dxdt

\displaystyle\leq C\Big(\int\!\!\!\!\!\int_{Q}(s\theta)^{k}g^{2}e^{2s\eta}\,dx\,dt+\int\!\!\!\!\!\int_{Q_{\omega}}(s\theta)^{k+3}v^{2}e^{2s\eta}\,dxdt\Big).

(3.36)

Note that, since the function
s\rightarrow s^{k}e^{cs},
with
k\geq 0
and
c<0
, is nonincreasing for larger values of
s
, then, from the fact that
\beta\leq\theta
in
(0,T)
we get that,

(s\theta)^{k}e^{2s\eta}=(s\theta)^{k}e^{2s\Psi(x)\theta(t)}\leq(s\beta)^{k}e^{2s\Psi(x)\beta(t)}=(s\beta)^{k}e^{2s\sigma},\quad\text{in}\;Q,

for
s
large enough, where we recall that
\Psi
is the weight function given in (
3.3
).

Finally, combining this fact with the estimates (
3
) and (
3
), we deduce that

\displaystyle s^{k}\|e^{s\widehat{\Phi}(0)}v(0)\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{2}+\int\!\!\!\!\!\int_{Q}(s\beta)^{k}v^{2}e^{2s\Phi}\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle\qquad\leq Ce^{2s[\widehat{\Phi}(0)-\Phi^{*}(\frac{5T}{8})]}\Big(\int\!\!\!\!\!\int_{Q}(s\beta)^{k}g^{2}e^{2s\sigma}\,dx\,dt+\int\!\!\!\!\!\int_{Q_{\omega}}(s\beta)^{k+3}v^{2}e^{2s\sigma}\,dxdt\Big).

This ends the proof of Lemma
3.1
.
∎

4
Null controllability for system (
1.6
)

In this section we will apply the Carleman estimates established in Section
3
to deduce the null controllability result for the nonhomogeneous problem (
1.6
). To this aim, following the arguments presented in
[
15
,
26
]
, we introduce, for all
k\geq 0
, the following weighted space

E_{s,k}=\big\{y\in Z_{T}:\quad(s\beta)^{-k/2}e^{-s\sigma}y\in L^{2}(Q)\big\}

endowed with the associated norm

\displaystyle\|y\|_{E_{s,k}}^{2}:=\int\!\!\!\!\!\int_{Q}(s\beta)^{-k}e^{-2s\sigma}y^{2}\,dxdt.

Observe that, if we consider
y
in
E_{s,k}
, then
y
is continuous in time and satisfies

\int\!\!\!\!\!\int_{Q}(s\beta)^{-k}e^{-2s\sigma}y^{2}\,dxdt<+\infty,

thus, from the definition of
\sigma
, in particular the fact that
\sigma<0
, we have that

y(T,\cdot)=0\quad\text{in}\;(0,1).

In the following, we denote by
s_{0}
the parameter defined in Lemma
3.1
.

Then, we are going to prove the following:

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
T>0
and
k\geq 0
. Assume
(s\beta)^{-k/2}e^{-s\Phi}f\in L^{2}(Q)
with
s\geq s_{0}
. Then, for any
y_{0}\in H_{a}^{1}(0,1)
,
there exists
u\in L^{2}(Q)
such that the associated solution
y
of system (
1.6
) belongs to
E_{s,k}.

Moreover, there exists a positive constant
C
such that the couple
(y,u)
satisfies

\displaystyle\int\!\!\!\!\!\int_{Q}(s\beta)^{-k}e^{-2s\sigma}y^{2}\,dx\,dt+\int\!\!\!\!\!\int_{Q_{\omega}}(s\beta)^{-(k+3)}e^{-2s\sigma}u^{2}\,dxdt

\displaystyle\qquad\leq Ce^{2s[\widehat{\Phi}(0)-\Phi^{*}(\frac{5T}{8})]}\Big(\int\!\!\!\!\!\int_{Q}(s\beta)^{-k}e^{-2s\Phi}f^{2}\,dx\,dt+s^{-k}\int_{0}^{1}e^{-2s\widehat{\Phi}(0)}y_{0}^{2}\,dx\Big).

(4.1)

Proof.

The proof of this Theorem is inspired by
[
15
,
26
]
. First of all, consider the following functional:

J(y,u)=\int\!\!\!\!\!\int_{Q}(s\beta)^{-k}e^{-2s\sigma}y^{2}\,dx\,dt+\int\!\!\!\!\!\int_{Q_{\omega}}(s\beta)^{-(k+3)}e^{-2s\sigma}u^{2}\,dxdt

where
(y,u)
satisfies system (
1.6
) with
u\in L^{2}(Q)
and

y(T,\cdot)=0\qquad\text{in}\;(0,1).

(4.2)

By classical arguments (see for instance
[
20
,
21
]
), one can show that
J
attains its minimizer at a unique point say,
(\tilde{y},\tilde{u})
.

We are going to prove the existence of a dual variable
\tilde{z}
such that

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\tilde{y}=(s\beta)^{k}e^{2s\sigma}\mathcal{L}^{*}\tilde{z}&\text{in}\;Q\\
\tilde{u}=-1_{\omega}(s\beta)^{k+3}e^{2s\sigma}\tilde{z}&\text{in}\;Q\end{cases}

where
\mathcal{L}^{*}\tilde{z}=-\tilde{z}_{t}-(a(x)\tilde{z}_{x})_{x}
and
\tilde{z}
satisfies the boundary conditions

\tilde{z}(\cdot,1)=0\quad\text{and}\;\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}&\tilde{z}(\cdot,0)=0,\qquad\quad(WD)\\
&(a\tilde{z}_{x})(\cdot,0)=0,\quad(SD)\\
\end{array}\qquad\text{on}\;(0,T)\right.

(4.3)

Let us define the following linear space

X_{a}=\Big\{w\in C^{\infty}(\overline{Q}):\quad w\quad\text{satisfies}\;\eqref{BConditions}\Big\}.

In addition, we set

\displaystyle\kappa(z,w)=\int\!\!\!\!\!\int_{Q}(s\beta)^{k}e^{2s\sigma}\mathcal{L}^{*}z\mathcal{L}^{*}w\,dx\,dt+\int\!\!\!\!\!\int_{Q_{\omega}}(s\beta)^{k+3}e^{2s\sigma}zw\,dxdt,\qquad\forall\,z,w\in X_{a}

(4.4)

and

\displaystyle\ell(w)=\int\!\!\!\!\!\int_{Q}fw\,dx\,dt+\int_{0}^{1}y_{0}w(0)dx,\qquad\forall\,w\in X_{a},

(4.5)

where
f,y_{0}
are the functions in (
1.6
).

Observe that Carleman estimate (
3.1
) holds for all
w\in X_{a}
. In particular, we have

s^{k}\int_{0}^{1}e^{2s\widehat{\Phi}(0)}w(0)^{2}\,dx+\int\!\!\!\!\!\int_{Q}(s\beta)^{k}e^{2s\Phi}w^{2}\,dx\,dt\leq Ce^{2s[\widehat{\Phi}(0)-\Phi^{*}(\frac{5T}{8})]}\kappa(w,w),\qquad\forall\,w\in X_{a}.

Now, let us denote by
\widetilde{X}_{a}
the completion of
X_{a}
with the norm
\|w\|_{\widetilde{X}_{a}}=(\kappa(w,w))^{1/2}
. Thus,
\widetilde{X}_{a}
is a Hilbert space with this norm.

Clearly,
\kappa
is a strictly positive, symmetric and continuous bilinear form in
\widetilde{X}_{a}
.

Moreover, in view of the above inequality, one can see that the linear form
\ell
is continuous in
\widetilde{X}_{a}
. Indeed, employing the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, for all
w\in\widetilde{X}_{a}
, we have

\displaystyle|\ell(w)|

\displaystyle=\int\!\!\!\!\!\int_{Q}fw\,dx\,dt+\int_{0}^{1}y_{0}w(0)dt

\displaystyle\leq\bigg(\Big(\int\!\!\!\!\!\int_{Q}(s\beta)^{-k}e^{-2s\Phi}f^{2}\,dx\,dt\Big)^{1/2}\Big(\int\!\!\!\!\!\int_{Q}(s\beta)^{k}e^{2s\Phi}w^{2}\,dx\,dt\bigg)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\qquad+\Big(s^{-k}\int_{0}^{1}e^{-2s\widehat{\Phi}(0)}y_{0}^{2}\,dx\Big)^{1/2}\Big(s^{k}\int_{0}^{1}e^{2s\widehat{\Phi}(0)}w(0)^{2}\,dx\Big)^{1/2}\bigg)

\displaystyle\leq\bigg(\Big[\Big(\int\!\!\!\!\!\int_{Q}(s\beta)^{-k}e^{-2s\Phi}f^{2}\,dx\,dt\Big)^{1/2}+\Big(s^{-k}\int_{0}^{1}e^{-2s\widehat{\Phi}(0)}y_{0}^{2}\,dx\Big)^{1/2}\Big]\times

\displaystyle\qquad\Big[\Big(\int\!\!\!\!\!\int_{Q}(s\beta)^{k}e^{2s\Phi}w^{2}\,dx\,dt\Big)^{1/2}+\Big(s^{k}\int_{0}^{1}e^{2s\widehat{\Phi}(0)}w(0)^{2}\,dx\Big)^{1/2}\Big]\bigg)

\displaystyle\leq Ce^{s[\widehat{\Phi}(0)-\Phi^{*}(\frac{5T}{8})]}\Big[\Big(\int\!\!\!\!\!\int_{Q}(s\beta)^{-k}e^{-2s\Phi}f^{2}\,dx\,dt\Big)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+\Big(s^{-k}\int_{0}^{1}e^{-2s\widehat{\Phi}(0)}y_{0}^{2}\,dx\Big)^{1/2}\Big]\|w\|_{\widetilde{X}_{a}}.

(4.6)

Hence, by Lax-Milgram Theorem, we infer that there exists a unique
\tilde{z}\in\widetilde{X}_{a}
such that

\kappa(\tilde{z},w)=\ell(w),\qquad\forall\,w\in\widetilde{X}_{a}.

(4.7)

This fact, together with (
4
), gives that

\displaystyle\kappa(\tilde{z},\tilde{z})=\ell(\tilde{z})\leq Ce^{s[\widehat{\Phi}(0)-\Phi^{*}(\frac{5T}{8})]}

\displaystyle\Big[\Big(\int\!\!\!\!\!\int_{Q}(s\beta)^{-k}e^{-2s\Phi}f^{2}\,dx\,dt\Big)^{1/2}

\displaystyle+\Big(s^{-k}\int_{0}^{1}e^{-2s\widehat{\Phi}(0)}y_{0}^{2}\,dx\Big)^{1/2}\Big]\|\tilde{z}\|_{\widetilde{X}_{a}}.

This implies

\displaystyle\|\tilde{z}\|_{\widetilde{X}_{a}}\leq Ce^{s[\widehat{\Phi}(0)-\Phi^{*}(\frac{5T}{8})]}

\displaystyle\Big[\Big(\int\!\!\!\!\!\int_{Q}(s\beta)^{-k}e^{-2s\Phi}f^{2}\,dx\,dt\Big)^{1/2}

\displaystyle+\Big(s^{-k}\int_{0}^{1}e^{-2s\widehat{\Phi}(0)}y_{0}^{2}\,dx\Big)^{1/2}\Big].

(4.8)

Setting

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\tilde{y}=(s\beta)^{k}e^{2s\sigma}\mathcal{L}^{*}\tilde{z}\\
\tilde{u}=-1_{\omega}(s\beta)^{k+3}e^{2s\sigma}\tilde{z}\end{cases}

(4.9)

and using the definition of the bilinear form
\kappa(\cdot,\cdot)
, we can write

\displaystyle\|\tilde{z}\|_{\widetilde{X}_{a}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\int\!\!\!\!\!\int_{Q}(s\beta)^{-k}e^{-2s\sigma}\big((s\beta)^{k}e^{2s\sigma}\mathcal{L}^{*}\tilde{z}\big)^{2}\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle\quad+\int\!\!\!\!\!\int_{Q_{\omega}}(s\beta)^{-(k+3)}e^{-2s\sigma}\big(1_{\omega}(s\beta)^{k+3}e^{2s\sigma}\tilde{z}\big)^{2}\,dxdt

\displaystyle=\int\!\!\!\!\!\int_{Q}(s\beta)^{-k}e^{-2s\sigma}\tilde{y}^{2}\,dx\,dt+\int\!\!\!\!\!\int_{Q_{\omega}}(s\beta)^{-(k+3)}e^{-2s\sigma}\tilde{u}^{2}\,dxdt

and, in view of (
4
), we can deduce

\displaystyle\int\!\!\!\!\!\int_{Q}(s\beta)^{-k}e^{-2s\sigma}\tilde{y}^{2}\,dx\,dt+\int\!\!\!\!\!\int_{Q_{\omega}}(s\beta)^{-(k+3)}e^{-2s\sigma}\tilde{u}^{2}\,dxdt

\displaystyle\qquad\leq Ce^{2s[\widehat{\Phi}(0)-\Phi^{*}(\frac{5T}{8})]}\Big(\int\!\!\!\!\!\int_{Q}(s\beta)^{-k}e^{-2s\Phi}f^{2}\,dx\,dt+s^{-k}\int_{0}^{1}e^{-2s\widehat{\Phi}(0)}y_{0}^{2}\,dx\Big).

(4.10)

Hence
\tilde{y}\in E_{s,k}
and satisfies the inequality (
4.1
).

In order to complete the proof, it remains to show that
(\tilde{y},\tilde{u})
, satisfies the parabolic problem (
1.6
) and the identity (
4.2
).
First of all, by (
4
) it is immediate that
\tilde{y},\tilde{u}\in L^{2}(Q)
.

Moreover, denote by
\hat{y}
the weak solution of system (
1.6
) associated to the control function
u=\tilde{u}
. Then,
\hat{y}
also solves this system in the sense of transposition, that is,
\hat{y}
is the unique function in
L^{2}(Q)
satisfying

\int\!\!\!\!\!\int_{Q}\hat{y}h\,dx\,dt=\int_{0}^{1}y_{0}w(0)dx+\int\!\!\!\!\!\int_{Q}1_{\omega}\tilde{u}w\,dx\,dt+\int\!\!\!\!\!\int_{Q}fw\,dx\,dt,\qquad\forall\,h\in L^{2}(Q),

(4.11)

where
w
is the solution of

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\displaystyle-w_{t}-(a(x)w_{x})_{x}=h&&(t,x)\in Q,\\
w(t,1)=0,&&t\in(0,T),\\
\begin{cases}&w(t,0)=0,\qquad\quad(WD),\\
&(aw_{x})(t,0)=0,\quad(SD),\\
\end{cases}&&t\in(0,T),\\
w(T,x)=0,&&x\in(0,1).\end{array}\right.

On the other hand, substituting the expressions of
\tilde{y}
and
\tilde{u}
, given in (
4.9
), in (
4.7
), we obtain

\int\!\!\!\!\!\int_{Q}\widetilde{y}h\,dx\,dt-\int\!\!\!\!\!\int_{Q_{\omega}}1_{\omega}\tilde{u}w\,dx\,dt=\int\!\!\!\!\!\int_{Q}fw\,dx\,dt+\int_{0}^{1}y_{0}w(0)dx,\qquad\forall\,h\in L^{2}(Q).

(4.12)

Hence, (
4.11
) and (
4.12
) imply that
\tilde{y}=\hat{y}
solves (
1.6
).

This completes the proof of Theorem

.
∎

We underline that

provides null controllability property for more regular solution of (

5

In this section, we analyze the null controllability result for the degenerate parabolic equation (
1.1
). First, for all
k\geq 0
,
we set

E_{s,k,R}=\big\{w\in E_{s,k}:\quad\|(s\beta)^{-k/2}e^{-s\sigma}w\|_{L^{2}(Q)}\leq R\big\},

(5.1)

where

is an arbitrary positive constant.
Clearly,

is a bounded, closed and convex subset of

Let

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\displaystyle y_{t}-(a(x)y_{x})_{x}=\int\limits_{0}^{t}b(t,s,x)w(s,x)\,ds+1_{\omega}u&&(t,x)\in Q,\\

&(ay_{x})(t,0)=0,\quad(SD),\\

(5.2)

Hence, the next null controllability result holds.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
T
and
R
strictly positive and
k\geq 0
. Assume that the memory kernel satisfies,

(T-t)^{2k}e^{\left(\frac{4}{T}\right)^{4}\frac{s\gamma d}{(T-t)^{4}}}b\in L^{\infty}((0,T)\times Q),

where

and

are the constants of (

is the same of Lemma

. Then, for all
w\in E_{s,kR}
and for any
y_{0}\in H_{a}^{1}(0,1)
, there exists
u\in L^{2}(Q)
such that the associated solution
y
of system (
5.2
)
belongs to
E_{s,k}
.

Notice that, condition (
5.3
) may appear as a quite strong restriction on the admissible kernel function

. Notwithstanding, it is instead a natural one, since the only thing that we are asking is its integrability with respect to the Carleman weight. In other words,

T^{-}
. Recall that this assumption is less restrictive, for larger values of

Proof.

Let

w\in E_{s,k,R}
and let
y\in Z_{T}
the solution of (
5.2
).
Using the fact that
-\gamma d\beta\leq\Phi
in

(see (

\displaystyle\int\!\!\!\!\!\int_{Q}

\displaystyle(s\beta)^{-k}e^{-2s\Phi}\Big(\int\limits_{0}^{t}b(t,s,x)w(s,x)\,ds\Big)^{2}\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle\leq C_{T}\int\!\!\!\!\!\int_{Q}\int\limits_{0}^{t}(s\beta)^{-k}e^{-2s\Phi}b^{2}(t,s,x)w^{2}(s,x)\,ds\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle\leq C_{T}\int\!\!\!\!\!\int_{Q}\int\limits_{0}^{t}(s\beta)^{-k}e^{2s\gamma d\beta}b^{2}(t,s,x)w^{2}(s,x)\,ds\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle\leq C_{T}s^{-k}\int\!\!\!\!\!\int_{Q}\int\limits_{0}^{t}(T-t)^{4k}e^{\frac{2s\gamma d}{(T/4)^{4}(T-t)^{4}}}b^{2}(t,s,x)w^{2}(s,x)\,ds\,dx\,dt.

Hence, by virtue of condition (
5.3
), we have

\displaystyle\int\!\!\!\!\!\int_{Q}(s\beta)^{-k}e^{-2s\Phi}

\displaystyle\Big(\int\limits_{0}^{t}b(t,s,x)w(s,x)\,ds\Big)^{2}\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle\leq C_{T}s^{-k}\int\!\!\!\!\!\int_{Q}w^{2}\,dx\,dt;

(5.4)

therefore, using Hölder’s inequality, the fact that
\sup\limits_{(t,x)\in\overline{Q}}(s\beta(t))^{k}e^{2s\sigma}(t,x)<+\infty
and

, we conclude that

\displaystyle\int\!\!\!\!\!\int_{Q}

\displaystyle(s\beta)^{-k}e^{-2s\Phi}\Big(\int\limits_{0}^{t}b(t,s,x)w(s,x)\,ds\Big)^{2}\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle\leq C_{T}s^{-k}\Big(\sup\limits_{(t,x)\in\overline{Q}}(s\beta(t))^{k}e^{2s\sigma}(t,x)\Big)\int\!\!\!\!\!\int_{Q}(s\beta)^{-k}e^{-2s\sigma}w^{2}\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle\leq C_{T}s^{-k}R^{2}<+\infty.

This implies that
(s\beta)^{-k/2}e^{-s\Phi}\Big(\int\limits_{0}^{t}b(t,s,x)w(s,x)\,ds\Big)\in L^{2}(Q)
. Hence, in view of Theorem
4.1
, we deduce that there exists
u\in L^{2}(Q)
such that the associated solution
y
of (
5.2
) belongs to

E_{s,k}
.
Hence, the conclusion follows.
∎

Remark 3

•

.

(5.5)

the associated solution

(5.6)

satisfies the assumptions of Kakutani’s fixed point Theorem. Next, we are going to check that all the conditions to apply such a theorem

Clearly,

\displaystyle\int\!\!\!\!\!\int_{Q}

\displaystyle(s\beta)^{-k}e^{-2s\sigma}y^{2}\,dx\,dt+\int\!\!\!\!\!\int_{Q_{\omega}}(s\beta)^{-(k+3)}e^{-2s\sigma}u^{2}\,dxdt

\displaystyle\qquad\leq Ce^{2s[\widehat{\Phi}(0)-\Phi^{*}(\frac{5T}{8})]}\Big(\int\!\!\!\!\!\int_{Q}(s\beta)^{-k}e^{-2s\Phi}\Big(\int\limits_{0}^{t}b(t,s,x)w(s,x)\,ds\Big)^{2}\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+s^{-k}\int_{0}^{1}e^{-2s\widehat{\Phi}(0)}y_{0}^{2}\,dx\Big)

\displaystyle\qquad\leq Ce^{2s[\widehat{\Phi}(0)-\Phi^{*}(\frac{5T}{8})]}\Big(s^{-k}\int\!\!\!\!\!\int_{Q}w^{2}\,dx\,dt+s^{-k}\int_{0}^{1}e^{-2s\widehat{\Phi}(0)}y_{0}^{2}\,dx\Big).

\displaystyle\int\!\!\!\!\!\int_{Q}

\displaystyle(s\beta)^{-k}e^{-2s\sigma}y^{2}\,dx\,dt+\int\!\!\!\!\!\int_{Q_{\omega}}(s\beta)^{-(k+3)}e^{-2s\sigma}u^{2}\,dxdt

\displaystyle\leq Cs^{-k}e^{2s[\widehat{\Phi}(0)-\Phi^{*}(\frac{5T}{8})]}\bigg(\Big(\sup\limits_{(t,x)\in\overline{Q}}(s\beta(t))^{k}e^{2s\sigma(t,x)}\Big)\Big(\int\!\!\!\!\!\int_{Q}(s\beta)^{-k}e^{-2s\sigma}w^{2}\,dx\,dt\Big)

In particular, since

,

(see (

\sup\limits_{(t,x)\in\overline{Q}}(s\beta(t))^{k}e^{\frac{s}{2}\sigma(t,x)}<+\infty

\displaystyle\int\!\!\!\!\!\int_{Q}

\displaystyle(s\beta)^{-k}e^{-2s\sigma}y^{2}\,dx\,dt+\int\!\!\!\!\!\int_{Q_{\omega}}(s\beta)^{-(k+3)}e^{-2s\sigma}u^{2}\,dxdt

\displaystyle\qquad\leq Cs^{-k}\bigg(e^{s[2\widehat{\Phi}(0)-2\Phi^{*}(\frac{5T}{8})+\frac{3}{2}\widehat{\sigma}(0)]}R^{2}+e^{-2s\Phi^{*}(\frac{5T}{8})}\int_{0}^{1}y_{0}^{2}\,dx\bigg).

(5.7)

On the other hand, by taking the parameter

\displaystyle\int\!\!\!\!\!\int_{Q}(s\beta)^{-k}e^{-2s\sigma}y^{2}

&(az_{x})(t,0)=0,\quad(SD),\\
\end{cases}&&t\in(0,T),\\
z(0,x)=z_{0}(x),&&x\in(0,1),\end{array}\right.

where
z_{0}=y_{0}-\overline{y}_{0}
.

According to Theorem
5.2
there exists
u\in L^{2}(Q)
such that

z(T,x)=0,\qquad x\in(0,1).

Consequently,

\overline{y}(T,x)=y(T,x),\qquad x\in(0,1).

6
Comments

In this section we discuss some extensions of the above null controllability results and describe some perspectives that are related to this paper.

6.1
Null controllability in the case
a(1)=0

In this subsection we address the null controllability result for the following degenerate parabolic equation with memory

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\displaystyle y_{t}-(a(x)y_{x})_{x}=\int\limits_{0}^{t}b(t,s,x)y(s,x)\,ds+1_{\omega}u&&(t,x)\in Q,\\
y(t,0)=0,&&t\in(0,T),\\
\begin{cases}&y(t,1)=0,\qquad\quad(WD),\\
&(ay_{x})(t,1)=0,\quad(SD),\\
\end{cases}&&t\in(0,T),\\
y(0,x)=y_{0}(x),&&x\in(0,1),\end{array}\right.

(6.1)

where
y_{0}\in L^{2}(0,1)
and
a
degenerates at the extremity
x=1
, i.e.,

a(1)=0
.
In order to present our main result we need to introduce the functional spaces where our problem will be well posed. As before, we distinguish the two following cases:

•

Weakly degenerate case (WD)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}a\in C([0,1])\cap C^{1}([0,1)),\;a(1)=0,\;a>0\quad\text{in}\quad[0,1),\\
\exists\;\tilde{\alpha}\in[0,1),\quad\text{such that}\quad(x-1)a^{\prime}(x)\leq\tilde{\alpha}a(x),\quad\forall\;x\in[0,1],\end{array}\right.

(6.2)

•

Strongly degenerate (SD)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}a\in C^{1}([0,1]),\;a(1)=0,\;a>0\quad\text{in}\quad[0,1),\\
\exists\,\tilde{\alpha}\in[1,2),\quad\text{such that}\quad(x-1)a^{\prime}(x)\leq\tilde{\alpha}a(x),\quad\forall\,x\in[0,1],\\
\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\exists\,\tilde{\beta}\in(1,\tilde{\alpha}],\,x\mapsto\dfrac{a(x)}{(1-x)^{\tilde{\beta}}}\quad\text{is nonincreasing near}\quad 0,\quad\text{if}\quad\tilde{\alpha}>1,\\
\exists\,\tilde{\beta}\in(0,1),\,x\mapsto\dfrac{a(x)}{(1-x)^{\tilde{\beta}}}\quad\text{is nonincreasing near}\quad 0,\quad\text{if}\quad\tilde{\alpha}=1.\end{array}\right.\end{array}\right.

(6.3)

Clearly, the prototype is
a(x)=(1-x)^{\tilde{\alpha}},\quad\tilde{\alpha}\in(0,2).

Let us introduce the weighted spaces
H_{a}^{1}
and
H_{a}^{2}
as follows:

Case (WD).

\displaystyle H_{a}^{1}:=\Big\{y\in L^{2}(0,1):y\;\text{a.c. in}\;[0,1],\quad\sqrt{a}y_{x}\in L^{2}(0,1)\;\text{and}\,y(0)=y(1)=0\Big\}

and

\displaystyle H_{a}^{2}:=\Big\{y\in H_{a}^{1}(0,1):ay_{x}\in H^{1}(0,1)\Big\}.

Case (SD).

\displaystyle H_{a}^{1}:=\Big\{y\in L^{2}(0,1):y\;\text{ locally a.c. in}\;[0,1),\quad\sqrt{a}y_{x}\in L^{2}(0,1)\;\text{and}\;y(0)=0\Big\}

and

\displaystyle H_{a}^{2}:

\displaystyle=\Big\{y\in H_{a}^{1}(0,1):ay_{x}\in H^{1}(0,1)\Big\}

\displaystyle=\Big\{y\in L^{2}(0,1):y\;\text{locally a.c. in}\;[0,1),\quad ay\in H^{1}_{0}(0,1),

\displaystyle\qquad ay_{x}\in H^{1}(0,1)\;\text{and}\;(ay_{x})(1)=0\Big\}.

Using the above spaces, one can prove that the well-posedness results given in Propositions
2.1
and
2.2
still hold. On the contrary, setting
\varphi:=\theta\tilde{\psi}
, where
\theta
is defined as in (
3.2
) and

\tilde{\psi}:=\tilde{\gamma}\Big(\int_{x}^{1}\frac{1-y}{a(y)}\,dy-\tilde{d}\Big),

(6.4)

with
\tilde{\gamma}
and
\displaystyle\tilde{d}>\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1-y}{a(y)}\,dy
positive constants, the next null controllability result holds.

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
T>0
,
k\geq 0
and assume that

(T-t)^{2k}e^{\big(\frac{4}{T}\big)^{4}\frac{s\tilde{\gamma}\tilde{d}}{(T-t)^{4}}}b\in L^{\infty}((0,T)\times Q),

(6.5)

with
s
as in Theorem
5.1
.
Then, for any
y_{0}\in L^{2}(0,1)
,
there exists
u\in L^{2}(Q)
such that the associated solution
y\in W_{T}
of (
6.1
) satisfies

\quad y(T,\cdot)=0\qquad\text{in}\;(0,1).

Proof.

The proof of this theorem follows the same strategy of Theorem
5.2
; of course using symmetric arguments. The main difference is that here, in place of (
3.8
) and (
3.1
), we use the following Hardy Poincaré inequality:

there is a positive constant
C
such that, for every
y\in H_{a}^{1}(0,1)
, the following inequality holds

\int_{0}^{1}\frac{a(x)}{(1-x)^{2}}y^{2}(x)\,dx\leq C\int_{0}^{1}a(x)|y_{x}(x)|^{2}\,dx,

and the following Carleman estimate:

there exist two positive constants
C
and
s_{0}
, such that the solution
v\in Z_{T}
of (
3.1
) satisfies

\displaystyle\int\!\!\!\!\!\int_{Q}\Big(s\theta a(x)v_{x}^{2}

\displaystyle+s^{3}\theta^{3}\frac{(1-x)^{2}}{a(x)}v^{2}\Big)e^{2s\varphi}\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle\leq C\Big(\int\!\!\!\!\!\int_{Q}g^{2}e^{2s\varphi}\,dx\,dt+\int\!\!\!\!\!\int_{Q_{\omega}}s^{2}\theta^{2}v^{2}e^{2s\varphi}\,dxdt\Big)

for all

.

As the procedure is completely similar, we omit the details of the proof.
∎

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\displaystyle y_{t}-(a(x)y_{x})_{x}=\int\limits_{0}^{t}b(t,s,x)y(s,x)\,ds+1_{\omega}u&&(t,x)\in Q,\\
\begin{cases}&y(t,0)=0=y(t,1),\qquad\qquad(WWD),\\
&(ay_{x})(t,0)=0=y(t,1),\qquad(SWD),\\
&y(t,0)=0=(ay_{x})(t,1),\qquad(WSD),\\
&(ay_{x})(t,0)=0=(ay_{x})(t,1),\quad(SSD),\\

y(0,x)=y_{0}(x),&&x\in(0,1),\end{array}\right.

(6.6)

where
y_{0}\in L^{2}(0,1)

(0,1)

weakly-weakly degenerate case (WWD):

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}a\in C([0,1])\cap C^{1}((0,1)),\;a(0)=a(1)=0,\;a>0\quad\text{in}\quad(0,1),\\

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}a\in C([0,1])\cap C^{1}([0,1)),\;a(0)=a(1)=0,\;a>0\quad\text{in}\quad(0,1),\\

weakly-strongly degenerate case (WSD):

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}a\in C([0,1])\cap C^{1}((0,1]),\;a(0)=a(1)=0,\;a>0\quad\text{in}\quad(0,1),\\

•

strongly-strongly degenerate case (SSD):

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}a\in C^{1}([0,1]),\;a(0)=a(1)=0,\;a>0\quad\text{in}\quad(0,1),\\

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\exists\,\beta\in(1,\alpha],\,x\mapsto\dfrac{a(x)}{x^{\beta}}\quad\text{is nondecreasing near}\quad 0,\quad\text{if}\quad\alpha>1,\\
\exists\,\beta\in(0,1),\,x\mapsto\dfrac{a(x)}{x^{\beta}}\quad\text{is nondecreasing near}\quad 0,\quad\text{if}\quad\alpha=1,\\
\end{array}\right.\\
\exists\,\tilde{\alpha}\in[1,2),\quad\text{such that}\quad(x-1)a^{\prime}(x)\leq\tilde{\alpha}a(x),\quad\forall\,x\in[0,1],\\
\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\exists\,\tilde{\beta}\in(1,\tilde{\alpha}],\,x\mapsto\dfrac{a(x)}{(1-x)^{\tilde{\beta}}}\quad\text{is nonincreasing near}\quad 0,\quad\text{if}\quad\tilde{\alpha}>1,\\

and

and

\displaystyle=\Big\{y\in H_{a}^{1}(0,1):ay_{x}\in H^{1}(0,1)\Big\}

Again, the well-posedness results proved in Propositions
2.1
and

Theorem 6.2

\bar{\gamma}=\max\{\gamma,\tilde{\gamma}\},\bar{d}=\max\{d,\tilde{d}\}

there exists

such that the associated solution

Here

Proof.

Consider the following parabolic system

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\displaystyle w_{t}-(a(x)w_{x})_{x}=\int\limits_{0}^{t}b(t,s,x)w(s,x)\,ds+1_{\omega}u_{1}&&(t,x)\in(0,T)\times(0,\beta^{\prime}),\\

\begin{cases}&w(t,0)=0,\qquad\quad(WD),\\
&(aw_{x})(t,0)=0,\quad(SD),\\
\end{cases}&&t\in(0,T),\\

(6.8)

where
\omega\Subset(\lambda^{\prime},\beta^{\prime})\Subset(0,1)
and

is the initial condition in (
6.6
).

Thus, by Theorem
5.2
, we know that there exists a control
u_{1}\in L^{2}((0,T)\times(0,\beta^{\prime}))

Now, define
\tilde{w}
the trivial extension of

in

\tilde{w}(T,\cdot)=0,\quad\text{in}\,(0,1).

In a similar way, we consider the following parabolic system

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\displaystyle z_{t}-(a(x)z_{x})_{x}=\int\limits_{0}^{t}b(t,s,x)z(s,x)\,ds+1_{\omega}u_{2}&&(t,x)\in(0,T)\times(\lambda^{\prime},1),\\

&(az_{x})(t,1)=0,\quad(SD),\\
\end{cases}&&t\in(0,T),\\
z(0,x)=y_{0}(x),&&x\in(\lambda^{\prime},1).\end{array}\right.

Then, thanks to Theorem

u_{2}\in L^{2}((0,T)\times(\lambda^{\prime},1))
such that the associated solution

Now, define
\tilde{z}
the trivial extension of

\tilde{z}(T,\cdot)=0,\quad\text{in}\,(0,1).

Next, consider

\tilde{u}_{1}(t,x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle u_{1}(t,x),&(t,x)\in(0,T)\times(0,\beta^{\prime}),\\

\displaystyle(ay_{x})_{x}=

\displaystyle y_{t}-(ay_{x})_{x}-

Final comments.

In the context of parabolic equation without memory, i.e.,
b=0
, it is well known that, once there exists a control function acting on a control region
\omega\subset(0,1)
that drives the system from an initial state
y_{0}
to the equilibrium at time
t=T
, i.e.,
y(T,\cdot)=0
, we can stop controlling, by setting
u\equiv 0
for
t\geq T
, and the underlying system naturally stays at rest for all
t\geq T
, i.e.,

y(t,\cdot)=0,\qquad\forall\,t\geq T.

Unfortunately, this is not the case for the parabolic equation with memory. Indeed, due to the effect of the accumulated memory at time
t=T
, i.e.,
\displaystyle\int\limits_{0}^{T}b(T,s,\cdot)y(s,\cdot)\,ds
, the null state of this system at
T
cannot be kept for
t\geq T
in the absence of control function.

Hence, it could be of interest to consider a more general concept of null controllability for system of type (
1.1
). In particular, we look for a control function that drives both the state and the memory term to
0
at time
t=T
.

This problem has been addressed by S. Ivanov and L. Pandolfi in
[
18
]
for the parabolic equation with memory and through a distributed control:

y_{t}-y_{xx}=\int_{0}^{t}b(t-s)y_{xx}(s)ds+1_{\omega}u,\qquad(t,x)\in Q.

In
[
18
]
it is proved that, this system cannot be controlled
to rest for large classes of memory kernels and controls. In fact, the presence of the memory terms makes the controllability of this system to be impossible if the control is located in a fixed subset
\omega
.

On the other hand, to obtain controllability result as explained in
[
10
]
and
[
11
]
, the support of the control function needs to move to cover the domain where the equation evolves in the control time horizon. We refer to
[
10
]
where this problem is discussed in the context of heat equation. The extension to the degenerate problem is the subject of a future work.

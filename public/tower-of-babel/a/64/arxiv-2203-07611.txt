Abstract
Let
\vec{p}\in(0,\,\infty)^{n}
,
A
be an expansive dilation on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
and
H^{\vec{p}}_{A}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
be the anisotropic mixed-norm Hardy space defined via the non-tangential grand
maximal function studied by
[
14
]
. In this paper, the authors establish
new atomic and molecular decompositions of
H^{\vec{p}}_{A}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
.
As an application, the authors obtain
a boundedness criterion for a class of linear operators
from
H^{\vec{p}}_{A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
to
H^{\vec{p}}_{A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.
Part of results are still new even in the classical isotropic setting (in the case
A:=2\mathrm{I}_{n\times n}
,
{\mathrm{I}}_{n\times n}
denotes the
n\times n
unit matrix).

1
Introduction

The theory of Hardy spaces on the Euclidean space
{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
has been developed and plays an important role in various fields of analysis and partial differential equations; see, for examples,
[
6
,
9
,
10
,
23
,
25
,
27
]
.
On the other hand,
the mixed-norm Lebesgue space
L^{\vec{p}}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
, with the exponent vector
\vec{p}\in(0,\,\infty]^{n}
, is a natural generalization of the classical Lebesgue space

L^{p}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
, via replacing the constant exponent
p
by an exponent vector
\vec{p}
. The study of mixed-norm Lebesgue spaces originates from Benedek and Panzone
[
3
]
. Later on, function spaces in mixed-norm setting have attracted considerable
attention and have rapidly been developed; see, for instance,

[
4
,
7
,
8
,
16
]
.

Let vector
\vec{p}:=(p_{1},\ldots,p_{n})\in(0,\,\infty]^{n}
. Recently, Cleanthous et al.
[
5
]
introduced
the anisotropic mixed-norm Hardy space
H_{\vec{a}}^{\vec{p}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
, where
\vec{a}:=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})\in[1,\,\infty)^{n}
, via the non-tangential grand maximal function, and then obtained its maximal function characterizations. Not long afterward, Huang et al.
[
12
]
further completed some real-variable characterizations of the space, such as the characterizations in terms of the atomic characterization and the Littlewood-Paley function characterization. Moreover, they obtained the boundedness of
\delta
-type Calderón-Zygmund operators from
H^{\vec{p}}_{\vec{a}}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
to
L^{\vec{p}}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
or from
H^{\vec{p}}_{\vec{a}}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
to itself. For more information about this space, see
[
11
,
13
,
17
]

Very recently, Huang et al.
[
14
]

also introduced the new anisotropic mixed-norm Hardy space
H^{\vec{p}}_{A}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
associated with a general expansive matrix
A
, via the non-tangential grand maximal function, and then established its various real-variable characterizations of
H_{A}^{\vec{p}}
, respectively, in terms of the atomic characterization and the Littlewood-Paley function characterization. For more information about Hardy space, see
[
14
,
15
,
22
,
24
,
26
]
. Nevertheless, the molecular decompositions of
H^{\vec{p}}_{A}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
has
not been established till now. Once its molecular decomposition is established,
it can be conveniently used to prove the boundedness of many important operators on the space

H^{\vec{p}}_{A}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
, for example, one of the most famous operator in harmonic analysis, Calderón-Zygmund operators.
To complete the theory of the new anisotropic mixed-norm Hardy space
H^{\vec{p}}_{A}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
, in this article, we establish the molecular decompositions of
H^{\vec{p}}_{A}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
. Then, as application, we further obtain
a boundedness criterion for a class of linear operators
from
H^{\vec{p}}_{A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
to
H^{\vec{p}}_{A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

Precisely, this article is organized as follows.

In Section
2
, we first recall some notations and definitions

, via the non-tangential grand maximal function.

The aim of Section

In Section

motivated by Liu et al.
[

20
]

When it comes back to the isotropic setting, i.e.,

Then there exists a positive constant

, when

(2.1)

\displaystyle C^{-1}(\lambda_{-})^{j}|x|\leq|A^{j}x|\leq C(\lambda_{+})^{j}|x|

and, when

(2.2)

\displaystyle C^{-1}(\lambda_{+})^{j}|x|\leq|A^{j}x|\leq C(\lambda_{-})^{j}|x|.

From
[
2

that, for a fixed dilation

\Delta\subset r\Delta\subset A\Delta,

\displaystyle\mathfrak{B}:=\{x+B_{k}:\ x\in{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}},\,k\in{\mathbb{Z}}\}.

Definition 3.1

Let

,

Let

.

Let

To prove Theorem

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
\vec{p}\in(0,\,\infty)^{n}
,
q\in(1,\infty)
and
s\in[\lfloor(1/{p_{-}}-1){\ln b/\ln\lambda_{-}}\rfloor,\,\infty)\cap{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}
with
p_{-}
as in (
2.6
). Then there exists a positive constant
C
such that, for any
(\vec{p},q,s)
-atom b and
\varphi\in\mathcal{S}
,

\left|\int_{{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}}b(y)\varphi(y)dy\right|\leq CM_{s}(\varphi),

where
M_{s}(\varphi):=\sup\{\|\varphi\|_{\alpha,\,0}:\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}_{+},|\alpha|\leq s+1\}.

Proof.

For any
(\vec{p},q,s)
-atom
b
, we assume supp
b\subset B_{k}.

Now we claim that, for any
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
,

\|\chi_{B_{k}}\|_{L^{\vec{p}}}^{-1}\lesssim\max\{b^{-k/p_{-}},\,b^{-k/p_{+}}\}.

In fact, there exists a
K\in{\mathbb{Z}}
large enough such that, when
k\in(K,\,\infty)\cap{\mathbb{Z}}
, the

\displaystyle\|\chi_{B_{k}}\|_{L^{\vec{p}}}

\displaystyle=\left\{\int_{\mathbb{R}}\cdots\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\chi_{B_{k}}(x_{1},\,\ldots x_{n})|^{p_{1}}\,dx_{1}\right]^{{p_{2}}/{p_{1}}}\cdots dx_{n}\right\}^{{1}/{p_{n}}}

\displaystyle\geq\left\{\int_{\mathbb{R}}\cdots\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\chi_{B_{k}}(x_{1},\,\ldots x_{n})|^{p_{+}}\,dx_{1}\right]^{{p_{+}}/{p_{+}}}\cdots dx_{n}\right\}^{{1}/{p_{+}}}

\displaystyle=|B_{k}|^{1/p_{+}}.

When
k\in(-\infty,\,K]
, by
[
14
, Lemma 6.8]
, we conclude that, for any
\nu\in(0,1)
,

\frac{\|\chi_{B_{K}}\|_{L^{\vec{p}}}}{\|\chi_{B_{k}}\|_{L^{\vec{p}}}}\lesssim b^{(1+\nu)(K-k)/p_{-}}.

Let
\nu\rightarrow 0
. Then

\frac{1}{\|\chi_{B_{k}}\|_{L^{\vec{p}}}}\lesssim\frac{b^{K/p_{-}}}{\|\chi_{B_{K}}\|_{L^{\vec{p}}}}b^{-k/p_{-}}\thicksim|B_{k}|^{-1/p_{-}}.

If
k>0
, we have

\|b\|_{L^{q}}\leq\frac{|B_{k}|^{{1}/{q}}}{\|\chi_{B_{k}}\|_{L^{\vec{p}}}}\lesssim|B_{k}|^{{1}/{q}-{1}/{p_{+}}}.

If
k\leq 0
, we have

\|b\|_{L^{q}}\leq\frac{|B_{k}|^{{1}/{q}}}{\|\chi_{B_{k}}\|_{L^{\vec{p}}}}\lesssim|B_{k}|^{{1}/{q}-{1}/{p_{-}}}.

Therefore, when
k>0
, we have

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}b(y)\varphi(y)dy\right|

\displaystyle\leq\|\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}}\|b\|_{{L^{q}}}|B_{k}|^{{1}/{q^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\leq\|\varphi\|_{\mathbf{0},\,0}\|b\|_{{L^{q}}}|B_{k}|^{{1}/{p_{+}}-{1}/{q}}

\displaystyle\leq M_{s}(\varphi).

When
k\leq 0
, by the vanishing moment of
b
and the Taylor reminder theorem, we obtain

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}b(y)\varphi(y)dy\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\int_{{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}}b(y)\left[\varphi(y)-\mathop{\sum}\limits_{|\alpha|\leq s}\frac{\partial^{\alpha}\varphi(\mathbf{0})}{\alpha!}y^{\alpha}\right]dy\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim M_{s}(\varphi)\left|\int_{B_{k}}b(y)|y|^{s+1}dy\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim M_{s}(\varphi)\|b\|_{{L^{q}}}b^{k(s+1)}|B_{k}|^{{1}/{q^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim M_{s}(\varphi)|B_{k}|^{{1}/{q}-{1}/{p_{-}}}|B_{k}|^{s+1-{1}/{q}}

\displaystyle\lesssim M_{s}(\varphi).

This finishes the proof of Lemma
3.4
.
∎

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
\vec{p}\in(0,\,1]^{n}
. Then, for any
\{\lambda_{i}\}_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}\subset\mathbb{C}
and

where

with
\underline{p}
as in (

).

Proof.

Since
\vec{p}\in(0,\,1]^{n}
and the well-known inequality that, for all

and

,

(3.1)

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}|\lambda_{i}|\right)^{\vartheta}\leq\sum_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}|\lambda_{i}|^{\vartheta}.

Then we have

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}|\lambda_{i}|

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}|\lambda_{i}|\left\|\frac{\chi_{B^{(i)}}}{\|\chi_{B^{(i)}}\|_{L^{\vec{p}}}}\right\|_{{L^{\vec{p}}}}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\sum_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}\frac{|\lambda_{i}|\chi_{B^{(i)}}}{\|\chi_{B^{(i)}}\|_{L^{\vec{p}}}}\right\|_{L^{\vec{p}}}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\left\{\sum_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}\left[\frac{|\lambda_{i}|\chi_{B^{(i)}}}{\|\chi_{B^{(i)}}\|_{L^{\vec{p}}}}\right]^{\eta}\right\}^{1/\eta}\right\|_{L^{\vec{p}}}.

This finishes the proof of Lemma
3.5
.
∎

Lemma 3.6
.

[
18
]

Let
\pi:\,\mathcal{S}^{{}^{\prime}}\rightarrow\mathcal{S}^{{}^{\prime}}_{\infty}
satisfy that, for any

and
\phi\in\mathcal{S}^{{}^{\prime}}_{\infty}

Then

\mathcal{P}=\{f\in\mathcal{S}^{{}^{\prime}}:\pi(f)=0\}.

where
\mathcal{P}
denote all polynomials on
{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
.
Moreover,
\mathcal{P}
is closed in
\mathcal{S}^{{}^{\prime}}
.

Proof of Theorem
3.2
.

By the definitions of
\mathcal{S}
and
\mathcal{S}_{\infty}
, we find that

\mathcal{S}_{\infty}\subset\mathcal{S},

which implies that

\mathcal{S}^{{}^{\prime}}\subset\mathcal{S}^{{}^{\prime}}_{\infty}.

From this, Lemma

, we conclude that

{H^{\vec{p}}_{A}}={H^{\vec{p},\,q,\,s}_{A}}\subset{\mathbb{H}^{\vec{p},\,q,\,s}_{A}}

and for any

Therefore, to prove Theorem

, it suffices to show that, for any

,
there exists a unique extension
F\in H^{\vec{p},\,q,\,s}_{A}
such that, for any
\phi\in\mathcal{S}_{\infty}
.

\langle F,\,\phi\rangle=\langle f,\,\phi\rangle

and

\|F\|_{H^{\vec{p},\,q,\,s}_{A}}\leq\|f\|_{\mathbb{H}^{\vec{p},\,q,\,s}_{A}}.

To show this, for any
f\in\mathbb{H}^{\vec{p},\,q,\,s}_{A}
, by Definition
3.1
,
we deduce that there exists a sequence of
(\vec{p},\,q,\,s)
-atom
\{a_{i}\}_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}
,
supported, respectively, on

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\mathbb{H}^{\vec{p},\,q,\,s}_{A}}\thicksim\left\|\left\{\sum_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}\left[\frac{|\lambda_{i}|\chi_{{B^{(i)}}}}{\|\chi_{{B^{(i)}}}\|_{L^{\vec{p}}}}\right]^{\eta}\right\}^{1/\eta}\right\|_{L^{\vec{p}}}<\infty

with
\eta\in(0,\,\min\{1,\,p_{-}\})
.

For any

, set

Then, using Lemmas
3.4
and

.

Finally, we only need to show that the distribution

In fact, if there exists distribution

such that,
for any

By the fact that any element of

vanishes weakly in infinity

in

. This finishes the proof of Theorem

.

4

H^{\vec{p}}_{A}

In this section, we introduce the definition of anisotropic mixed-norm molecules as follows.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
\vec{p}\in(0,\,\infty)^{n}
,
q\in(1,\,\infty]
,

(4.1)

\displaystyle s\in\left[\left\lfloor\left(\frac{1}{p_{-}}-1\right)\frac{\ln b}{\ln\lambda_{-}}\right\rfloor,\,\infty\right)\cap{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}.

and
\varepsilon\in(0,\,\infty)
. A measurable function

is called an

-molecule

if

(i)

for each

\int_{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}\mathfrak{M}(x)x^{\alpha}dx=0
.

Remark 4.2
.

Let

To deal with

\displaystyle A^{-k}(x-x_{i})+B_{\ell_{i}+j-k}

\displaystyle b^{-N(\ell_{i}+j-k+m)}.

\displaystyle M_{N}^{0}(\mathfrak{M}_{i})(x)=

\displaystyle\sup_{\varphi\in\mathcal{S}_{N}}\sup_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}|\mathfrak{M}_{i}*\varphi_{k}(x)|

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\frac{b^{\ell_{i}-k}}{\|\chi_{x_{i}+B_{\ell_{i}}}\|_{L^{\vec{p}}}}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}b^{(1/{q^{\prime}}-\varepsilon)j}\max\left\{b^{-N(\ell_{i}-k+j+m)},\right.

\displaystyle\ \ \ \left.b^{j(s+1)\log_{b}^{\lambda_{+}}}\lambda_{-}^{(s+1)(\ell_{i}-k)}b^{-N(\ell_{i}-k+j+m)}\right\}

\displaystyle\frac{b^{\ell_{i}-k}}{\|\chi_{x_{i}+B_{\ell_{i}}}\|_{L^{\vec{p}}}}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}b^{-j[\varepsilon-(s+1)\log_{b}^{\lambda_{+}}-1/{q^{\prime}}+1]}\max\left\{b^{-mN},\,(b\lambda_{-}^{s+1})^{-m}\right\}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\|\chi_{x_{i}+B_{\ell_{i}}}\|_{L^{\vec{p}}}}b^{-m}b^{-m(s+1)(\ln\lambda_{-}/\ln b)}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\|\chi_{x_{i}+B_{\ell_{i}}}\|_{L^{\vec{p}}}}b^{\ell_{i}[(s+1)(\ln\lambda_{-}/\ln b)+1]}b^{-(\ell_{i}+\sigma+m)[(s+1)(\ln\lambda_{-}/\ln b)+1]}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\|\chi_{x_{i}+B_{\ell_{i}}}\|_{L^{\vec{p}}}}|x_{i}+B_{\ell_{i}}|^{[(s+1)(\ln\lambda_{-}/\ln b)+1]}[\rho(x-x_{i})]^{-[(s+1)(\ln\lambda_{-}/\ln b)+1]}

\displaystyle\thicksim

\displaystyle\left\|\chi_{B^{(i)}}\right\|^{-1}_{L^{\vec{p}}}\frac{\left|{B^{(i)}}\right|^{\theta}}{[\rho(x-x_{i})]^{\theta}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\left\|\chi_{B^{(i)}}\right\|^{-1}_{L^{\vec{p}}}\left[M_{\mathrm{HL}}(\chi_{B^{(i)}})(x)\right]^{\theta},

,

\displaystyle\theta:=\left(\frac{\ln b}{\ln\lambda_{-}}+s+1\right)\frac{\ln\lambda_{-}}{\ln b}>\frac{1}{\underline{p}}.

By this and the fact that, for any

\displaystyle M_{N}(\mathfrak{M}_{i})(x)\lesssim\left\|\chi_{B^{(i)}}\right\|^{-1}_{L^{\vec{p}}}\frac{\left|{B^{(i)}}\right|^{\theta}}{[\rho(x-x_{i})]^{\theta}}\lesssim\left\|\chi_{B^{(i)}}\right\|^{-1}_{L^{\vec{p}}}\left[M_{\mathrm{HL}}(\chi_{B^{(i)}})(x)\right]^{\theta},

and

).
From this, Lemmas

,

, (

) and (

), we deduce that

(4.9)

\displaystyle\mathrm{I}_{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|\sum_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}\frac{|\lambda_{i}|}{\|\chi_{B^{(i)}}\|_{L^{\vec{p}}}}[M_{\mathrm{HL}}(\chi_{B^{(i)}})]^{\theta}\right\|_{L^{\vec{p}}}^{\underline{p}}

\displaystyle\thicksim\left\|\left\{\sum_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}\frac{|\lambda_{i}|}{\|\chi_{B^{(i)}}\|_{L^{\vec{p}}}}[M_{\mathrm{HL}}(\chi_{B^{(i)}})]^{\theta}\right\}^{1/\theta}\right\|_{L^{\theta\vec{p}}}^{{\theta}{\underline{p}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|\left\{\sum_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}\frac{|\lambda_{i}|\chi_{B^{(i)}}}{\|\chi_{B^{(i)}}\|_{L^{\vec{p}}}}\right\}^{1/\theta}\right\|_{L^{\theta\vec{p}}}^{{\theta}{\underline{p}}}\thicksim\left\|\sum_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}\frac{|\lambda_{i}|\chi_{B^{(i)}}}{\|\chi_{B^{(i)}}\|_{L^{\vec{p}}}}\right\|_{L^{\vec{p}}}^{\underline{p}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|\left\{\sum_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}\left[\frac{|\lambda_{i}|\chi_{B^{(i)}}}{\|\chi_{B^{(i)}}\|_{L^{\vec{p}}}}\right]^{\eta}\right\}^{1/\eta}\right\|_{L^{\vec{p}}}^{\underline{p}}\thicksim\|f\|_{H^{\vec{p},\,q,\,s,\,\varepsilon}_{A,\,\mathrm{mol}}}^{\underline{p}},

.

\displaystyle\|f\|_{H_{A}^{\vec{p}}}\thicksim\|M_{N}(f)\|_{L^{\vec{p}}}\lesssim\|f\|_{H^{\vec{p},\,q,\,s,\,\varepsilon}_{A,\,\mathrm{mol}}}.

5
Application

In this section, as an application, we obtain a boundedness criterion for some linear
operators from
H^{\vec{p}}_{A}
to itself.
Particularly, when
A:=2\rm I_{n\times n}
, this result is still new.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
\vec{p}\in(0,\,\infty)^{n}
,
q\in(1,\,\infty]\cap(p_{+},\,\infty)
with
p_{+}
as in (
2.6
), and
s
be as in (
4.1
). Suppose that
T
is a bounded linear operator on
L^{r}
, for any
r\in(1,\,\infty]
. If for any
(\vec{p},\,q,\,s)
-atom
a
, supported on dilated ball
B^{(i_{0})}\in\mathfrak{B}
, as in Definition
2.6
,
T(a)
is a harmless constant multiple of a
(\vec{p},\,q,\,s,\,\varepsilon)
-molecule, associated with dilated ball
B^{(i_{0})}\in\mathfrak{B}
, where
\varepsilon>(s+1)\log_{b}^{\lambda_{+}}
,
then
T
extends uniquely to a bounded linear operator on
H^{\vec{p}}_{A}
. Moreover, there exists a positive constant
C
such that, for all

f\in H^{\vec{p}}_{A}
,

(5.1)

\displaystyle\|T(f)\|_{H^{\vec{p}}_{A}}\leq C\,\|f\|_{H^{\vec{p}}_{A}}.

To prove Theorem
5.1
, we need some definitions and technical lemmas.

Definition 5.2
.

[
14
]

Let
\vec{p}\in(0,\,\infty)^{n}
,
q\in(1,\,\infty]
and

s\in[\lfloor(1/{p_{-}}-1){\ln b/\ln\lambda_{-}}\rfloor,\,\infty)\cap{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}
with
p_{-}
as in (
2.6
).
The
anisotropic mixed-norm finite atomic Hardy space

H^{\vec{p},\,q,\,s}_{A,\,\mathrm{fin}}

is defined to be the set of all distributions
f\in{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}
satisfying that there
exist
I\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
\{\lambda_{i}\}_{i\in[1,\,I]\cap{\mathbb{N}}}\subset{\mathbb{C}}
and a sequence of

(\vec{p},\,q,\,s)
-atoms,
\{a_{i}\}_{i\in[1,\,I]\cap{\mathbb{N}}}
, supported, respectively,
on
\{{B^{(i)}}\}_{i\in[1,\,I]\cap{\mathbb{N}}}\subset\mathfrak{B}
such that

\displaystyle f=\sum_{i=1}^{I}\lambda_{i}a_{i}\ \ \mathrm{in\ }\ \mathcal{S}^{\prime}.

Moreover, for any
f\in H^{\vec{p},\,q,\,s}_{A,\,\rm{fin}}
, let

\|f\|_{H^{\vec{p},\,q,\,s}_{A,\,\rm{fin}}}:=\inf\left\|\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{I}\left[\frac{|\lambda_{i}|\chi_{{B^{(i)}}}}{\|\chi_{{B^{(i)}}}\|_{L^{\vec{p}}}}\right]^{\eta}\right\}^{1/\eta}\right\|_{L^{\vec{p}}},

where
\eta\in(0,\,\min\{1,\,p_{-}\})
and the infimum is taken over all the decompositions of
f
as above.

Lemma 5.3
.

[
14
, Theorem 5.3]

Let
\vec{p}\in(0,\,\infty)^{n}
,
A
be a dilation and

s\in[\lfloor(1/{p_{-}}-1){\ln b/\ln\lambda_{-}}\rfloor,\,\infty)\cap{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}
with
p_{-}
as in (
2.6
)

(i)

If
q\in(\max\{p_{+},\,1\},\,\infty)
with
p_{+}
as in (
2.6
), then
\|\cdot\|_{H^{\vec{p},\,q,\,s}_{A,\,\rm{fin}}}
and
\|\cdot\|_{H^{\vec{p}}_{A}}
are equivalent quasi-norms on
H^{\vec{p}}_{A}
.

(ii)

\|\cdot\|_{H^{\vec{p},\,\infty,\,s}_{A,\,\rm{fin}}}
and
\|\cdot\|_{H^{\vec{p}}_{A}}
are equivalent quasi-norms on
H^{\vec{p},\,\infty,\,s}_{A,\,\rm{fin}}\cap C
,
where
C
denotes the set of all continuous functions on
{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
.

Lemma 5.4
.

[
14
, Theorem 4.7]

Let
\vec{p}\in(0,\,\infty)^{n}
,
q\in(1,\,\infty]\cap(p_{+},\,\infty)
with
p_{+}
as in (
2.6
),
r\in(1,\,\infty]
and
s\in[\lfloor(1/{p_{-}}-1){\ln b/\ln\lambda_{-}}\rfloor,\,\infty)\cap{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}
with
p_{-}
as in (
2.6
). Then, for any
f\in H^{\vec{p}}_{A}\cap L^{r}
, there exist
\{\lambda_{i}\}_{{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}}\subset\mathbb{C}
, dilated balls
\{x_{i}+B_{\ell_{i}}\}_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}\subset\mathfrak{B}
and

(\vec{p},\,q,\,s)
-atoms
\{a_{i}\}_{{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}}
such that

f=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\lambda_{i}a_{i}\ \ {\rm in}\ \ L^{q},

where the series also converge almost everywhere.

Lemma 5.5
.

[
14
]

Let
\vec{p}\in(0,\,\infty)^{n}
and
N\in\mathbb{N}\cap[\lfloor(\frac{1}{\min\{1,\,p_{-}\}}-1)\frac{\ln b}{\ln\lambda_{-}}\rfloor+2,\,\infty)
with
p_{-}
as in (
2.6
). Then
H^{\vec{p}}_{A}
is complete.

Now, we show Theorem
5.1
by borrowing some ideas from the proof of
[
14
, Theorem 8.4]
.

Proof of Theorem
5.1
.

Let
\vec{p}\in(0,\,\infty)^{n}
,
q\in(1,\,\infty]\cap(p_{+},\,\infty)
with
p_{+}
as in (
2.6
), and

s\in[\lfloor(1/{p_{-}}-1){\ln b/\ln\lambda_{-}}\rfloor,\,\infty)\cap{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}
with
p_{-}
as in (
2.6
).
Firstly, we show that (
5.1
) holds true for any
f\in H^{\vec{p},\,q,\,s}_{A,\,\mathrm{fin}}
. For any
f\in H^{\vec{p},\,q,\,s}_{A,\,\mathrm{fin}}
, by Definition
5.2
, we know that
f\in H^{\vec{p}}_{A}\cap L^{q}
.
From Lemma
5.4
,
we know that there exist
\{\lambda_{i}\}_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}\subset{\mathbb{C}}
and a sequence of

(\vec{p},\,q,\,s)
-atoms,
\{a_{i}\}_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}
, supported, respectively,
on
\{{B^{(i)}}\}_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}\subset\mathfrak{B}
, where
B^{(i)}:=x_{i}+B_{\ell_{i}}
with
x_{i}\in{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
and
\ell_{i}\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, such that

\displaystyle f=\sum_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}\lambda_{i}a_{i}\ \ \mathrm{in\ }\ L^{q},

and

(5.2)

\displaystyle\left\|\left\{\sum_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}\left[\frac{|\lambda_{i}|\chi_{{B^{(i)}}}}{\|\chi_{{B^{(i)}}}\|_{L^{\vec{p}}}}\right]^{\eta}\right\}^{1/\eta}\right\|_{L^{\vec{p}}}\lesssim\|f\|_{H^{\vec{p}}_{A}}.

where
\eta\in(0,\,\min\{1,\,p_{-}\})
.
From this and the linear operator
T
is bounded on

, we conclude that, for any
f\in H^{\vec{p},\,q,\,s}_{A,\,\mathrm{fin}}
,

T(f)=\sum_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}\lambda_{i}T(a_{i})
in
L^{q}
and hence in
{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}
.
Therefore, for all
N\in\mathbb{N}\cap[\lfloor({1/\underline{p}}-1)\ln b/\ln\lambda_{-}\rfloor+2,\,\infty)
,

(5.3)

\displaystyle\left\|M_{N}(T(f))\right\|_{L^{\vec{p}}}^{\underline{p}}\leq\left\|\sum_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}|\lambda_{i}|M_{N}(T(a_{i}))\right\|_{L^{\vec{p}}}^{\underline{p}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\|\sum_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}|\lambda_{i}|M_{N}(T(a_{i}))\chi_{A^{2\sigma}B^{(i)}}\right\|_{L^{\vec{p}}}^{\underline{p}}+\left\|\sum_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}|\lambda_{i}|M_{N}(T(a_{i}))\chi_{({A^{2\sigma}B^{(i)}})^{\complement}}\right\|_{L^{\vec{p}}}^{\underline{p}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\left\|\left\{\sum_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}\left[|\lambda_{i}|M_{N}(T(a_{i}))\chi_{A^{2\sigma}B^{(i)}}\right]^{\eta}\right\}^{1/\eta}\right\|_{L^{\vec{p}}}^{\underline{p}}+\left\|\sum_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}|\lambda_{i}|M_{N}(T(a_{i}))\chi_{({A^{2\sigma}B^{(i)}})^{\complement}}\right\|_{L^{\vec{p}}}^{\underline{p}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle:\mathrm{K_{1}}+\mathrm{K_{2}},

where
A^{2\sigma}B^{(i)}
is the
A^{2\sigma}
concentric expanse on
B^{(i)}

,

\underline{p}

For

, and the size condition of

From this, Lemma
4.6

\displaystyle\mathrm{K_{1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|\left\{\sum_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}\left[\frac{|\lambda_{i}|\chi_{{B^{(i)}}}}{\|\chi_{{B^{(i)}}}\|_{L^{\vec{p}}}}\right]^{\eta}\right\}^{1/\eta}\right\|_{L^{\vec{p}}}^{\underline{p}}\lesssim\|f\|_{H^{\vec{p}}_{A}}^{\underline{p}}.

To deal with

, for any
i\in{\mathbb{N}}
and

(\vec{p},\,q,\,s)
-atom

\displaystyle M_{N}(T(a_{i}))(x)\lesssim\left\|\chi_{B^{(i)}}\right\|^{-1}_{L^{\vec{p}}}\left[M_{\mathrm{HL}}(\chi_{B^{(i)}})(x)\right]^{\theta},

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|\left\{\sum_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}\left[\frac{|\lambda_{i}|\chi_{{B^{(i)}}}}{\|\chi_{{B^{(i)}}}\|_{L^{\vec{p}}}}\right]^{\eta}\right\}^{1/\eta}\right\|_{L^{\vec{p}}}^{\underline{p}}\lesssim\|f\|_{H^{\vec{p}}_{A}}^{\underline{p}}.

Next, we prove that (

Let

. By Lemma
5.3
and the obvious density of

as

\displaystyle\|T(h_{j})-T(f)\|_{{H^{\vec{p}}_{A}}}^{\underline{p}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|h_{j}-f_{j}\|_{H^{\vec{p}}_{A}}^{\underline{p}}+\|T(f_{j})-g\|_{{H^{\vec{p}}_{A}}}^{\underline{p}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|h_{j}-f\|_{H^{\vec{p}}_{A}}^{\underline{p}}+\|f-f_{j}\|_{H^{\vec{p}}_{A}}^{\underline{p}}+\|T(f_{j})-g\|_{{H^{\vec{p}}_{A}}}^{\underline{p}}\rightarrow 0\,\,\mathrm{as}\,\,j\rightarrow 0,

which is wished.

From this, we see that, for any

which implies that (
5.1
) also holds true for any

and hence completes the proof of Theorem
5.1

Abstract.

Combinatorial designs are closely related to linear codes. In recent year, there are a lot of
t
-designs constructed from certain linear codes. In this paper, we aim to construct
2
-designs from binary three-weight codes. For any binary three-weight code
\mathcal{C}
with length
n
, let
A_{n}(\mathcal{C})
be the number of codewords in
\mathcal{C}
with Hamming weight
n
, then we show that
\mathcal{C}
holds
2
-designs when
\mathcal{C}
is projective and
A_{n}(\mathcal{C})=1
. Furthermore, by extending some certain binary projective two-weight codes and basing on the defining set method, we construct two classes of binary projective three-weight codes which are suitable for holding
2
-designs.

Keywords.

Binary three-weight codes, Weight distributions,
2
-designs.

Preliminaries

2.1
The MacWilliam Identity and the Assmus-Mattson Theorem for binary linear codes

The MacWilliam Identity and the first two Pless power moments for binary linear codes are given in the following lemmas, respectively, which are needed to calculate weight distributions for dual codes of binary linear codes.

Lemma 2.1
(
[
15
]
, The MacWilliam Identity)

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a binary
[n,k,d]
code with weight enumerator

P_{\mathcal{C}}(z)=1+A_{1}(\mathcal{C})z+\cdots+A_{n}(\mathcal{C})z^{n},

then

\displaystyle P_{\mathcal{C^{\perp}}}(z)=2^{-k}(1+z)^{n}P_{\mathcal{C}}\left(\frac{1-z}{1+z}\right).

Pless power moments are the equivalence transformation of the MacWilliam Identity, the first three Pless power moments are listed in the following lemma.

Lemma 2.2
(
[
15
]
, The first three Pless power moments)

For any binary code
\mathcal{C}
with parameters
[n,k,d]
, the first three Pless power
moments are

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{n}A_{j}(\mathcal{C})=2^{k},

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{n}jA_{j}(\mathcal{C})=2^{k-1}(n-A_{1}(\mathcal{C}^{\perp})),

and

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{n}j^{2}A_{j}(\mathcal{C})=2^{k-2}\Big(n(n+1)-2nA_{1}(\mathcal{C}^{\perp})+2A_{2}(\mathcal{C}^{\perp})\Big).

The Assmus-Mattson Theorem for binary linear codes is stated as follows, which will be used for the construction of
2
-designs from binary projective three-weight codes satisfying some conditions.

Lemma 2.3
(
[
15
]
, The Assmus-Mattson Theorem)

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a binary
[n,k,d]
code. Suppose
\mathcal{C}^{\perp}
has minimum distance
d^{\perp}
. For a fixed integer
t
with
t<d
, and let
s
be the number of
i
with
A_{i}(\mathcal{C}^{\perp})\neq 0
for
0<i\leq n-t
. Suppose
s\leq d-t
. Then:

(1)
the codewords of weight
i
in
\mathcal{C}
hold a
t
-design provided
A_{i}(\mathcal{C})\neq 0
and
d\leq i\leq n
, and

(2)
the codewords of weight
i
in
\mathcal{C}^{\perp}
hold a
t
-design provided
A_{i}(\mathcal{C}^{\perp})\neq 0
and
d^{\perp}\leq i\leq n-t
.

2.2
Some known results of Weil sum

In the following, we list some basic knowledge of additive characters and results of Weil sums, which shall be useful for determining the weight distribution of three-weight codes constructed from the defining set method in Section
4.2
.

Let
q=2^{m}
, the additive character of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
is a function from
\mathbb{F}_{q}
to the multiplicative group
U=\{u\ |\ |u|=1,\ u\in\mathbb{C}\}
, such that
\chi(x+y)=\chi(x)\chi(y)
for all
x,\ y\in\ \mathbb{F}_{q}
. For each
b\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
,

\chi_{b}(x)=(-1)^{\mathrm{Tr}(bx)}

(x\in\mathbb{F}_{q})

defines an additive character of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, where
\mathrm{Tr}
is the absolute trace function from
\mathbb{F}_{q}
to
\mathbb{F}_{2}
. Especially,

\chi:=\chi_{1}
is called the canonical additive character of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, and every additive character of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
can be written as
\chi_{b}(x)=\chi(bx)
.
Furthermore, the orthogonal property for the additive character is given by

\displaystyle\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}(-1)^{\mathrm{Tr}(bx)}=\begin{cases}q,\quad&\text{if}~b=0;\\
0,\quad&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Weil sums are defined by
\sum\limits_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}\chi(f(x))
for any
f(x)\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
. For any
u\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
and
(a,b)\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2}
, Coulter determined the value of Weil sum as

\displaystyle S_{u}(a,b)=\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}(-1)^{\mathrm{Tr}(ax^{2^{u}+1}+bx)}.

Before presenting the explicit value of
S_{u}(a,b)
, we fixed some notations as follows:

\bullet

e
is the greatest common divisor of
m
and
u
, i.e.,
e=\gcd(m,u)
.

\bullet

\mathrm{Tr}_{e}
is the trace function from
\mathbb{F}_{q}
to
\mathbb{F}_{2^{e}}
.

When
\frac{m}{e}
is odd, the explicit value of
S_{u}(a,b)
is given in the following lemmas, which shall be used for calculating the weight distributions for binary three-weight linear codes constructed in Section
4.2
.

Lemma 2.4
(
[
1
]
, Theorem
4.1
)

If
\frac{m}{e}
is odd and
a\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}
, then

\displaystyle S_{u}(a,0)=0.

Lemma 2.5
(
[
1
]
, Theorem
4.2
)

If
\frac{m}{e}
is odd and
(a,b)\in(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*})^{2}
, then

\displaystyle S_{u}(a,b)=S_{u}(1,b\gamma^{-1}),

where
\gamma\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}
is the unique element satisfying
\gamma^{2^{u+1}}=a
. Furthermore, we have

\displaystyle S_{u}(1,b)=\begin{cases}0,&~\text{if}~\mathrm{Tr}_{e}(b)\neq 1;\\
\pm 2^{\frac{m+e}{2}},&~\text{if}~\mathrm{Tr}_{e}(b)=1.\end{cases}

Lemma 2.6
(
[
1
]
, Theorem
4.6
)

If
\frac{m}{e}
is odd, then

\displaystyle S_{u}(1,1)=\left(\frac{~2~}{\frac{m}{e}}\right)^{e}2^{\frac{m+e}{2}},

where
\left(\frac{~\cdot~}{\cdot}\right)
is the Jacobi symbol.

Remark 2.1

It is well-known that
\left(\frac{~2~}{s}\right)=(-1)^{\frac{s^{2}-1}{8}}
, where
s>1
is odd. Then

\displaystyle S_{u}(1,1)=(-1)^{\frac{m^{2}-e^{2}}{8e}}2^{\frac{m+e}{2}}.

3
2
-designs from binary three-weight linear codes with special parameters

In the following lemma, we determined the weight distributions for a class of binary three-weight codes and its dual codes.

Theorem 3.1

Let
\mathcal{C}
be an
[n,k,d]
binary projective three-weight code, if
A_{n}(\mathcal{C})>0
, then the three nonzero weights of
\mathcal{C}
are

\displaystyle w_{1}=\frac{1}{2}\left(n-\sqrt{n\left(\frac{2^{k-1}-n}{2^{k-1}-1}\right)}\right),~w_{2}=\frac{1}{2}\left(n+\sqrt{n\left(\frac{2^{k-1}-n}{2^{k-1}-1}\right)}\right),~~~w_{3}=n.

Furthermore,

\displaystyle P_{\mathcal{C}}(z)=1+(2^{k-1}-1)z^{d}+(2^{k-1}-1)z^{n-d}+z^{n},

and

\displaystyle P_{\mathcal{C}^{\perp}}(z)=1+\sum_{r=2}^{\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor}A_{2r}(\mathcal{C}^{\perp})z^{2r},

where for any
r=2,\ldots,\left\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\right\rfloor
,

\displaystyle A_{2r}(\mathcal{C}^{\perp})=2^{-(k-1)}\left(\binom{n}{2r}+(2^{k-1}-1)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq i\leq d\\
0\leq j\leq\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor-d\\
i+j=r\end{subarray}}(-1)^{i}\binom{d}{i}\binom{n-2d}{2j}\right).

Proof.
Let
w_{1}<w_{2}<w_{3}
be three nonzero weights of
\mathcal{C}
, by the assumptions that
\mathcal{C}
is a binary code and
A_{n}>0
, we have

\displaystyle w_{1}=d,~~w_{2}=n-d,~~w_{3}=n~~\text{and}~~A_{n}=1.

Since
\mathcal{C}
is projective, one has
A_{1}(\mathcal{C}^{\perp})=A_{2}(\mathcal{C}^{\perp})=0
, then by Lemma
2.2
, we immediately get

A_{w_{1}}(\mathcal{C})=A_{w_{2}}(\mathcal{C})=2^{k-1}-1.

Thus

\displaystyle P_{\mathcal{C}}(z)=1+(2^{k-1}-1)z^{d}+(2^{k-1}-1)z^{n-d}+z^{n}.

Then it follows from Lemma
2.1
that

\displaystyle P_{\mathcal{C}^{\perp}}(z)=

\displaystyle 2^{-k}(1+z)^{n}\left(1+(2^{k-1}-1)\left(\frac{1-z}{1+z}\right)^{d}+(2^{k-1}-1)\left(\frac{1-z}{1+z}\right)^{n-d}+\left(\frac{1-z}{1+z}\right)^{n}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2^{-k}\left((1+z)^{n}+(1-z)^{n}+(2^{k-1}-1)\Big((1+z)^{n-d}(1-z)^{d}+(1+z)^{d}(1-z)^{n-d}\Big)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2^{-k}\left((1+z)^{n}+(1-z)^{n}+(2^{k-1}-1)\big(1-z^{2}\big)^{d}\Big((1+z)^{n-2d}+(1-z)^{n-2d}\Big)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2^{-k}\left(2\sum_{r=0}^{\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor}\binom{n}{2r}z^{2r}+(2^{k-1}-1)\left(\sum_{i=0}^{d}(-1)^{i}\binom{d}{i}z^{2i}\right)\left(2\sum_{j=0}^{\lfloor\frac{n-2d}{2}\rfloor}\binom{n-2d}{2j}z^{2j}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2^{-(k-1)}\left(\sum_{r=0}^{\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor}\binom{n}{2r}z^{2r}+(2^{k-1}-1)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq i\leq d\\
0\leq j\leq\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor-d\\
i+j=r\end{subarray}}(-1)^{i}\binom{d}{i}\binom{n-2d}{2j}z^{2r}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1+\sum_{r=1}^{\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor}A_{2r}(\mathcal{C}^{\perp})z^{2r},

where for any
r=2,\ldots,\left\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\right\rfloor
,

\displaystyle A_{2r}(\mathcal{C}^{\perp})=2^{-(k-1)}\Bigg(\binom{n}{2r}+(2^{k-1}-1)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq i\leq d\\
0\leq j\leq\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor-d\\
i+j=r\end{subarray}}(-1)^{i}\binom{d}{i}\binom{n-2d}{2j}\Bigg).

Now by
A_{2}(\mathcal{C}^{\perp})=0
, one has

\displaystyle 2^{-(k-1)}\Bigg(\binom{n}{2}+(2^{k-1}-1)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq i\leq d\\
0\leq j\leq\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor-d\\
i+j=1\end{subarray}}(-1)^{i}\binom{d}{i}\binom{n-2d}{2j}\Bigg)=0,

i.e.,

\displaystyle 4d^{2}-4nd+\frac{2^{k-1}}{2^{k-1}-1}n(n-1)=0.

Thus

\displaystyle w_{1}=d=\frac{1}{2}\left(n-\sqrt{n\left(\frac{2^{k-1}-n}{2^{k-1}-1}\right)}\right)~~\text{and}~~w_{2}=n-d=\frac{1}{2}\left(n+\sqrt{n\left(\frac{2^{k-1}-n}{2^{k-1}-1}\right)}\right).

So far, we get the desired results.

4.1
Three-weight codes from some known two-weight codes

In this subsection, for any binary linear code
\mathcal{C}
with length
n
and dimension
k
, set
\overline{\bm{c}}=(c_{1},\ldots,c_{n},0)
for any
\bm{c}=(c_{1},\ldots,c_{n})\in\mathcal{C}
. Then the extended code of
\mathcal{C}
is defined as

\displaystyle\overline{\mathcal{C}}=\{\overline{\bm{c}}~|~\bm{c}\in\mathcal{C}\}\cup\{\bm{1}+\overline{\bm{c}}~|~\bm{c}\in\mathcal{C}\},

where
\bm{1}=(1,\ldots,1)\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n+1}
.
It is easy to check that
\overline{\mathcal{C}}
is a linear code with length
n+1
and dimension
k+1
.

In the following theorem, let
\mathcal{C}
be a two-weight code satisfying some conditions, we show that
\overline{\mathcal{C}}
is a three-weight code which is suitable for the conditions in Theorem
3.2
.

Theorem 4.1

For
n>2
, let
\mathcal{C}
be an
[n-1,k-1,d]
binary projective two-weight code with two nonzero weights
w_{1}
and
w_{2}
satisfying
w_{1}=d
and
w_{2}=n-d
, then
\overline{\mathcal{C}}
is an
[n,k,d]
binary projective three-weight code with
A_{n}(\overline{\mathcal{C}})=1
.

Proof
. For any
\bm{c}\in\mathcal{C}
, let
wt_{H}(\bm{c})
(resp.,
wt_{H}(\overline{\bm{c}})
) be the Hamming weight of
\bm{c}
(resp.,
wt_{H}(\overline{\bm{c}})
).
By the assumption, we know that
\mathcal{C}
is a binary two-weight code with two nonzero weights
w_{1}
and
w_{2}
satisfying
w_{1}+w_{2}=n
. Notice that
wt_{H}(\overline{\bm{c}})=wt_{H}(\bm{c})=w
, then

\displaystyle wt_{H}(\bm{1}+\overline{\bm{c}})=n-wt_{H}(\overline{\bm{c}})=\begin{cases}n,&\text{if~}wt_{H}({\bm{c}})=0,\\
n-d,&\text{if~}wt_{H}({\bm{c}})=w_{1};\\
d,&\text{if~}wt_{H}({\bm{c}})=w_{2}.\end{cases}

Hence
\overline{\mathcal{C}}
is an
[n,k,d]
binary three-weight code with three nonzero weights
w_{1}=d
,
w_{2}=n-d
and
w_{3}=n
. Furthermore,
\overline{\mathcal{C}}
is projective follows from the fact that
\mathcal{C}

4.2
Two classes of binary three-weight codes from defining sets

In 2015, Ding et al. proposed the defining set construction method to construct few-weight linear codes
[
7
]
. Let
D=\{d_{1},\ldots,d_{n}\}\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{p^{m}}^{*}
, the linear code over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
is defined as

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{D}=\big\{\mathbf{c}(b)=\big(\mathrm{Tr}(bd_{1}),\ldots,\mathrm{Tr}(bd_{n})\big)~\big|~b\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{m}}\big\}.

Here
D
is called the defining set of
\mathcal{C}_{D}
.

In this subsection, based on the defining set method, we construct two classes of binary projective three-weight linear codes, which are suitable to get
2
-designs.

4.2.1
Main results

Let
q=2^{m}
with
m\in\mathbb{N}^{+}

and

\displaystyle D_{\rho}=\left\{x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}~|~\mathrm{Tr}(x)=1,~\mathrm{Tr}(x^{2^{u}+1})=\rho\right\}.

In the following theorems, we show that
\mathcal{C}_{D_{\rho}}
is a projective three-weight linear code and determine the weight distribution of
\mathcal{C}_{D_{\rho}}
.

Theorem 4.4

For any odd
m\geq 5
, let
u\in N^{+}
with
\gcd(u,m)=1

is even and

\frac{m^{2}-1}{8}

,

and the weight enumerator

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{C}_{D_{\rho}}}(z)=1+\big(2^{m-1}-1\big)z^{2^{m-3}-2^{\frac{m-3}{2}}}+\big(2^{m-1}-1\big)z^{2^{m-3}}+z^{2^{m-2}-2^{\frac{m-3}{2}}}.

Theorem 4.5

For any odd
m\geq 5
, let
u\in N^{+}
with
\gcd(u,m)=1

\frac{m^{2}-1}{8}

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{C}_{D_{\rho}}}(z)=1+\big(2^{m-1}-1\big)z^{2^{m-3}}+\big(2^{m-1}-1\big)z^{2^{m-3}+2^{\frac{m-3}{2}}}+z^{2^{m-2}+2^{\frac{m-3}{2}}}.

4.2.2
The proofs of Theorems
4.4
-
4.5

For any codeword
\bm{c}_{b}\in\mathcal{C}_{D_{\rho}}
, let
D_{\rho}=\{d_{1},\ldots,d_{n}\}
, recall that

\displaystyle\bm{c}_{b}=(\mathrm{Tr}(bd_{1}),\ldots,\mathrm{Tr}(bd_{n})).

It is easy to see that the length of
\bm{c}_{b}
is
|D_{\rho}|
, and the Hamming weight of
\bm{c}_{b}
can be given as

\displaystyle wt_{H}(\bm{c}_{b})=\Big|\{x\in D_{\rho}~|~\mathrm{Tr}(bx)=1\}\Big|.

(4.1)

Now, we determine the length and the Hamming weight of
\bm{c}_{b}
in Lemmas
4.3
-
4.4
, respectively.

Lemma 4.3

For any odd
m\geq 5
, let
u\in N^{+}
with
\gcd(u,m)=1
, then

\displaystyle|D_{\rho}|=

\displaystyle\begin{cases}2^{m-2}-(-1)^{\frac{m^{2}-1}{8}}2^{\frac{m-3}{2}},~&\text{if}~\rho=0;\\
2^{m-2}+(-1)^{\frac{m^{2}-1}{8}}2^{\frac{m-3}{2}},~&\text{if}~\rho=1.\end{cases}

Proof
. It follows from the orthogonal property of additive characters that

\displaystyle|D_{\rho}|=

\displaystyle\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}\left(\frac{1}{2}\sum_{z_{1}\in\mathbb{F}_{2}}(-1)^{z_{1}\left(\mathrm{Tr}(x)-1\right)}\right)\left(\frac{1}{2}\sum_{z_{2}\in\mathbb{F}_{2}}(-1)^{z_{2}\left(\mathrm{Tr}(x^{2^{u}+1})-\rho\right)}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2^{m-2}+\frac{1}{4}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}(-1)^{\mathrm{Tr}(x)-1}+\frac{1}{4}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}(-1)^{\mathrm{Tr}(x^{2^{u}+1})-\rho}+\frac{1}{4}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}(-1)^{\mathrm{Tr}(x^{2^{u}+1}+x)-(1+\rho)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\begin{cases}2^{m-2}+\frac{1}{4}S_{u}(1,0)-\frac{1}{4}S_{u}(1,1),~&\text{if}~\rho=0;\\
2^{m-2}-\frac{1}{4}S_{u}(1,0)+\frac{1}{4}S_{u}(1,1),~&\text{if}~\rho=1.\end{cases}

By Lemmas
2.4
-
2.6
, we have
S_{u}(1,0)=0
and
S_{u}(1,1)=(-1)^{\frac{m^{2}-v^{2}}{8v}}2^{\frac{m+v}{2}}
, respectively, where
v=\gcd(m,u)=1
. Thus we get the desired results.

\hfill\Box

Lemma 4.4

For any odd
m\geq 5
, let
u\in N^{+}
with
\gcd(u,m)=1
. Let
b\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}
, then the following two assertions hold.

(1)
If
b=1
, then
wt_{H}(\bm{c}_{1})=|D_{\rho}|
.

(2)
If
b\in\mathbb{F}_{q}\backslash\mathbb{F}_{2}
, then
wt_{H}(\bm{c}_{b})\in\left\{\frac{\left|D_{\rho}\right|}{2}-2^{\frac{m-5}{2}},\frac{\left|D_{\rho}\right|}{2}+2^{\frac{m-5}{2}}\right\}
.

Proof
. For
b=1
, by
(\ref{wth})
and the definition of
D_{\rho}
, we have
wt_{H}(\bm{c}_{b})=|D_{\rho}|
.

For
b\in\mathbb{F}_{q}\backslash\mathbb{F}_{2}
, it follows from
(\ref{wth})
and the orthogonal property of additive characters that

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} wt_{H}(\bm{c}_{b})=&\sum_{x\in D_{\rho}}\left(\frac{1}{2}\sum_{y\in\mathbb{F}_{2}}(-1)^{y\left(\mathrm{Tr}(bx)-1\right)}\right)\\
=&\frac{|D_{\rho}|}{2}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{x\in D_{\rho}}\left((-1)^{\mathrm{Tr}(bx)-1}\right)\\
=&\frac{|D_{\rho}|}{2}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}\left((-1)^{\mathrm{Tr}(bx)-1}\right)\left(\frac{1}{2}\sum_{z_{1}\in\mathbb{F}_{2}}(-1)^{z_{1}\left(\mathrm{Tr}(x)-1\right)}\right)\left(\frac{1}{2}\sum_{z_{2}\in\mathbb{F}_{2}}(-1)^{z_{2}\left(\mathrm{Tr}(x^{2^{u}+1})-\rho\right)}\right)\\
=&\frac{|D_{\rho}|}{2}-\frac{1}{8}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}(-1)^{\mathrm{Tr}(bx)}\left(1-(-1)^{\mathrm{Tr}(x)}\right)\left(1+(-1)^{\rho}\cdot(-1)^{\mathrm{Tr}(x^{2^{u}+1})}\right)\\
=&\frac{|D_{\rho}|}{2}-\frac{1}{8}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}(-1)^{\mathrm{Tr}(bx)}+\frac{1}{8}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}(-1)^{\mathrm{Tr}((b+1)x)}\\
&\qquad-\frac{1}{8}(-1)^{\rho}\cdot\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}(-1)^{\mathrm{Tr}\left(x^{2^{u}+1}+bx\right)}+\frac{1}{8}(-1)^{\rho}\cdot\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}(-1)^{\mathrm{Tr}\left(x^{2^{u}+1}+(b+1)x\right)}\\
=&\frac{|D_{\rho}|}{2}-\frac{1}{8}(-1)^{\rho}\cdot\left(\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}(-1)^{\mathrm{Tr}\left(x^{2^{u}+1}+bx\right)}-\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}(-1)^{\mathrm{Tr}\left(x^{2^{u}+1}+(b+1)x\right)}\right).\end{aligned}

Since
m
is odd and
e=\gcd(m,u)=1
, we have
\mathrm{Tr}_{e}(1)=1
. Thus

\big(\mathrm{Tr}(b),\mathrm{Tr}(b+1)\big)\in\big\{(0,1),(1,0)\big\}.

Then by Lemma
2.5
, we can get

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}(-1)^{\mathrm{Tr}\left(x^{2^{u}+1}+bx\right)}-\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}(-1)^{\mathrm{Tr}\left(x^{2^{u}+1}+(b+1)x\right)}\right)\in\left\{-2^{\frac{m+1}{2}},2^{\frac{m+1}{2}}\right\},

which leads

\displaystyle wt_{H}(\bm{c}_{b})\in\left\{\frac{\left|D_{\rho}\right|}{2}-2^{\frac{m-5}{2}},\frac{\left|D_{\rho}\right|}{2}+2^{\frac{m-5}{2}}\right\}.

\hfill\Box

In the following, based on Lemma
3.1
and Lemmas
4.3
-
4.4
, the proofs of Theorems
4.4
-
4.5
are presented.

The proofs of Theorems
4.4
-
4.5
:

By Lemma
4.3
, we know that the length of
\mathcal{C}_{D_{\rho}}
is

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} |D_{\rho}|=\begin{cases}2^{m-2}-(-1)^{\frac{m^{2}-1}{8}}2^{\frac{m-3}{2}},~&\text{if}~\rho=0;\\
2^{m-2}+(-1)^{\frac{m^{2}-1}{8}}2^{\frac{m-3}{2}},~&\text{if}~\rho=1.\\
\end{cases}\end{aligned}

(4.2)

Furthermore, it follows from Lemma
4.4
that
\mathcal{C}_{D_{\rho}}
is a three-weight linear code with parameters
\left[|D_{\rho}|,m,\frac{|D_{\rho}|}{2}-2^{\frac{m-5}{2}}\right]
, and the three nonzero weights are

\displaystyle w_{1}=\frac{|D_{\rho}|}{2}-2^{\frac{m-5}{2}},~~w_{2}=\frac{|D_{\rho}|}{2}+2^{\frac{m-5}{2}},~~w_{3}=|D_{\rho}|.

(4.3)

Since

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{C}_{D_{\rho}}}(z)=\begin{cases}1+\big(2^{m-1}-1\big)\Big(z^{2^{m-3}-(I_{m}+1)2^{\frac{m-5}{2}}}+z^{2^{m-3}-(I_{m}-1)2^{\frac{m-5}{2}}}\Big)+z^{2^{m-2}-I_{m}2^{\frac{m-3}{2}}},&\text{if~}\rho=0;\\

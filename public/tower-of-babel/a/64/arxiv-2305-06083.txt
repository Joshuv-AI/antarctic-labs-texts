Representations of the small quasi-quantum group Thanks: 2010 Mathematics Subject Classification . 16G30, 16T99
Hua Sun, Hui-Xiang Chen, Yinhuo Zhang

Abstract
. In this paper, we study the representation theory of the small quantum group U ¯ q \overline{U}_{q} and the small quasi-quantum group U ~ q \widetilde{U}_{q} , where q q is a primitive n n -th root of unity and n > 2 n>2 is odd. All finite dimensional indecomposable U ~ q \widetilde{U}_{q} -modules are described and classified. Moreover, the decomposition rules for the tensor products of U ~ q \widetilde{U}_{q} -modules are given. Finally, we describe the structures of the projective class ring r p ​ ( U ~ q ) r_{p}(\widetilde{U}_{q}) and the Green ring r ⁡ ( U ~ q ) r(\widetilde{U}_{q}) . We show that r ⁡ ( U ¯ q ) r(\overline{U}_{q}) is isomorphic to a subring of r ⁡ ( U ~ q ) r(\widetilde{U}_{q}) , and the stable Green rings r s ​ t ​ ( U ~ q ) r_{st}(\widetilde{U}_{q}) and r s ​ t ​ ( U ¯ q ) r_{st}(\overline{U}_{q}) are isomorphic.



1.
Introduction

In recent years, breakthroughs have been made in the classification of fusion categories of low ranks. For example, Ostrik classified the fusion categories of rank 2 in
[
16
]
, and the pivotal fusion categories of rank 3 in
[
17
]
. Rowell, Strong and Wang classified the unitary modular categories of ranks less than 4 in
[
18
]
.
In
[
4
]
, Chen and Zhang showed that a finite tensor category
\mathcal{C}
(not necessarily rigid) is uniquely determined by its structure invariant data
(r(\mathcal{C}),A(\mathcal{C}),\phi,a_{i,j,l})
, where
r(\mathcal{C})
is the Green ring of
\mathcal{C}
,
A(\mathcal{C})
is the Auslander algebra of
\mathcal{C}
,
\phi
is an algebra map from
A({\mathcal{C}})\otimes_{\Bbbk}A({\mathcal{C}})
to an algebra
M({\mathcal{C}})
associated to
{\mathcal{C}}
, and
a_{i,j,l}
is a family of “invertible” matrices playing the role of the associator. Thus the classification of finite tensor categories (not necessarily rigid) of a fixed rank can be done (in theory) by classifying the aforementioned structure invariant data. However, in practice it is quite hard to classify all the invariant data, in particular the associators, which rely on both the Green rings and the Auslander algebras. So we ask ourselves whether we can begin with a finite tensor category and determine all finite tensor categories with the same Green ring or the same Auslander algebra. The classification of fusion categories or semisimple categories of a fixed rank is in this case with the same Auslander algebra.
The study of 3-cocycle twisting from a Hopf algebra to a quasi-Hopf algebra made it possible to deform the representation category of a Hopf algebra to a non-tensor equivalent representation category of a quasi-Hopf algebra, but with the same Green ring, see
[
11
, Example 2.3.8]
and
[
15
, Lemma 5.1]
. This is in fact to change the associator of the representation category of the Hopf algebra by a 3-cocycle and to keep the Green ring and the Auslander algebra unchanged. A different example of this phenomena comes from the group algebras
\Bbbk Q_{8}
and
\Bbbk D_{8}
without 3-cocycle twisting. The representation categories of the two group algebras are not tensor equivalent, but they have the same Green ring and the same Auslander algebra (over an algebraically closed field). Motivated by these examples we look at other constructions of quasi-Hopf algebras. In
[
22
]
the authors constructed a class of finite-dimensional quasi-Hopf algebras from Andruskiewitch-Schneider’s Hopf algebras, and found a quasi-version
\widetilde{u}_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2})
of the small quantum group
u_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2})
, called the small quasi-quantum group. Since this small quasi-quantum group does not come from a direct twisting of the small quantum group, we wonder if they have the same Green ring.
This lead us to study the representation theory of the small quantum group
\overline{U}_{q}:=u_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2})
and the representation theory of the small quasi-quantum group
\widetilde{U}_{q}:=\widetilde{u}_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2})
for
|q|>2
being odd. It turns out that the two do not have the same Green ring, but have the same stable Green ring.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we first recall some concepts, the structures of
\overline{U}_{q}
and
\widetilde{U}_{q}
. Then we give a set of central orthogonal idempotents
\{e_{0},e_{1},\cdots,e_{n-1}\}
of
\widetilde{U}_{q}
such that
\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}e_{i}=1
. Thus,
\widetilde{U}_{q}
is decomposed into a direct product of algebras
\widetilde{U}_{q}e_{i}
, i.e.,
\widetilde{U}_{q}=\widetilde{U}_{q}e_{0}\times\widetilde{U}_{q}e_{1}\times\cdots\times\widetilde{U}_{q}e_{n-1}
. In Section 3, we first describe the finite dimensional indecomposable
\overline{U}_{q}
-modules. Then we find out all indecomposable modules over
\widetilde{U}_{q}e_{i}
for each
0\leqslant i\leqslant n
so that we can classify the indecomposable
\widetilde{U}_{q}
-modules. In Section 4, we investigate the tensor products of finite dimensional indecomposable
\widetilde{U}_{q}
-modules, and give the decomposition rules for all such tensor product modules. It is shown that the Green ring
r(\overline{U}_{q})
is isomorphic to a subring of the Green ring
r(\widetilde{U}_{q})
, and that the stable Green rings
r_{st}(\widetilde{U}_{q})
and
r_{st}(\overline{U}_{q})
are isomorphic. In Section 5, we describe the structures of the Green rings
r(\overline{U}_{q})
and
r(\widetilde{U}_{q})
, and the projective class ring
r_{p}(\widetilde{U}_{q})
, respectively. It is shown that
r_{p}(\widetilde{U}_{q})
is generated by
2n+1
elements subject to certain relations, but
r(\overline{U}_{q})
and
r(\widetilde{U}_{q})
are generated by infinitely many elements subject to certain relations, respectively.

2.1.
Green ring and stable Green ring

For an algebra
A
, we denote by
\mathrm{Mod}A
the category of finite dimensional
A
-modules.
For any
M\in\mathrm{Mod}A
and any
n\in\mathbb{Z}
with
n\geqslant 0
,
nM
stand for the
direct sum of
n
copies of
M
. Thus
nM=0
if
n=0
. Let
P(M)
denote the projective
cover of
M
.

The Green ring
r(H)
of a Hopf algebra
H
is defined to be the abelian group generated by the
isomorphism classes
[V]
of
V
in
\mathrm{Mod}H

modulo the relations
[U\oplus V]=[U]+[V]
,
U,V\in\mathrm{Mod}H
. The multiplication of
r(H)

is determined by
[U][V]=[U\otimes V]
, the tensor product of
H
-modules. Then
r(H)
is an associative ring with the identity
[\Bbbk]
. The projective class ring
r_{p}(H)
of
H
is defined to be the subring of
r(H)
generated by
projective modules and simple modules (see
[
3
]
).
Notice that
r(H)
is a free abelian group with a
\mathbb{Z}
-basis

\{[V]|V\in{\rm Ind}(H)\}
, where
{\rm Ind}(H)
denotes the category
of indecomposable objects in
\mathrm{Mod}H
.

Recall that the stable module category
\mathrm{\underline{Mod}}H
is the quotient category of
\mathrm{Mod}H
modulo the morphisms factoring through the projective modules. This category is triangulated with the monoidal structure derived from that of
\mathrm{Mod}H
. The Green ring of the stable module category
\mathrm{\underline{Mod}}H
, denoted
r_{st}(H)
, is called stable Green ring of
H
.

2.2.
Small quantum group
\overline{U}_{q}

Let
q\in\Bbbk
with
q\neq\pm 1
.
The quantum enveloping algebra
U_{q}=U_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2})
of Lie algebra
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}

is generated, as an algebra, by
E
,
F
,
K
and
K^{-1}
subject to the following relations:

\begin{array}[]{c}KEK^{-1}=q^{2}E,\ KFK^{-1}=q^{-2}F,\ [E,F]=\frac{K-K^{-1}}{q-q^{-1}},\ KK^{-1}=K^{-1}K=1.\end{array}

U_{q}
is a Hopf algebra with the coalgebra structure and the antipode given by

\begin{array}[]{c}\bigtriangleup(K)=K\otimes K,\ \bigtriangleup(E)=E\otimes K+1\otimes E,\ \bigtriangleup(F)=F\otimes 1+K^{-1}\otimes F,\\
\varepsilon(K)=1,\ \varepsilon(E)=\varepsilon(F)=0,\ S(K)=K^{-1},\ S(E)=-EK^{-1},\ S(F)=-KF.\\
\end{array}

Note that
K
and
K^{-1}
are group-like elements and
E,\ F
are skew-primitive elements.

Now let
q\in\Bbbk
be a root of unity with the order
|q|>2
.
Set
n=|q|
if
|q|
is odd, and
n=\frac{|q|}{2}
if
|q|
is even.
Then
q^{2}
is a primitive
n^{th}
root of unity.
Let
I
be the ideal of
U_{q}
generated by
E^{n}
,
F^{n}
and
K^{n}-1
.
Then
I
is a Hopf ideal of
U_{q}
and hence one gets a quotient Hopf algebra
\overline{U}_{q}=U_{q}/I
,
called the small quantum group. For the details, one can refer to
[
13
]
.
When
|q|=2n
, the ideal
I^{\prime}
of
U_{q}
generated by
E^{n}
,
F^{n}
and
K^{2n}-1

is also a Hopf ideal of
U_{q}
, and hence one can form another quotient Hopf algebra

\widehat{U}_{q}=U_{q}/I^{\prime}
. In this case,
\overline{U}_{q}
is a quotient Hopf algebra of

\widehat{U}_{q}
. Therefore, the category
\mathrm{Mod}\overline{U}_{q}
can be regarded
as a monoidal full subcategory of the category
\mathrm{Mod}\widehat{U}_{q}
,
and the Green ring
r(\overline{U}_{q})
is a subring of the Green ring
r(\widehat{U}_{q})
.
The finite dimensional indecomposable
\widehat{U}_{q}
-modules are classified in
[
19
]

and the decomposition rules of these modules are given in
[
12
]
.
The Green ring
r(\widehat{U}_{q})
is computed in
[
20
]
.
By
[
12
]
,
r(\widehat{U}_{q})
is not a commutative ring.
However, the category
\mathrm{Mod}\overline{U}_{q}
is braided, and hence
the Green ring
r(\overline{U}_{q})
is commutative.

2.3.
Small quasi-quantum group
\widetilde{U}_{q}

Let
q\in\Bbbk
with
q\neq\pm 1
and let
U_{q}=U_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2})
be the quantum enveloping algebra of Lie algebra
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
given as in the last subsection.
Let

and

be two positive odd integers. Let
m=nd
and
G=\mathbb{Z}_{m}=\langle K\rangle
be the cyclic group of order

generated by

. Fix a primitive

-root
\xi_{m}
of unity in
\Bbbk
. Let
\mathbf{m}=m^{2}
and let
\xi_{\mathbf{m}}
be a primitive
\mathbf{m}^{th}
-root of unity such that
\xi_{\mathbf{m}}^{m}=\xi_{m}
.

In the next, let
q=\xi^{d}_{m}
. Then
q
is a primitive
n^{th}
root of unity. Let
\mathbb{G}=\mathbb{Z}_{\mathbf{m}}=\langle\mathbf{K}\rangle
and
K=\mathbf{K}^{m}
. Let
h_{1}=h_{2}=\mathbf{K}^{m}\in\mathbb{G}
,
\chi_{1}(\mathbf{K})=\xi_{\mathbf{m}}^{2d}\in\hat{\mathbb{G}}
, and
\chi_{2}(\mathbf{K})=\xi_{\mathbf{m}}^{-2d}\in\hat{\mathbb{G}}
, where
\hat{\mathbb{G}}
is the character group of
\mathbb{G}
. Let
W=\{kn|0\leqslant k<d\}
. By
[
22
, Difiniteion 5.1]
, for any fixed
c\in W
and
\lambda=\frac{1}{q^{-1}-q}\in\Bbbk
, one can construct a quasi-Hopf algebra, called a small quasi-quantum group, we denote by
\widetilde{U}_{q}
. Moreover, by
[
22
, Proposition 5.2]
, the Hopf algebra structure of
\widetilde{U}_{q}
is given as follows.

For the fixed
c\in W
,
define the functions
\Phi_{1},\Phi_{2}:G\times G\rightarrow\Bbbk^{*}
,
\Upsilon,\mathbf{F}_{1},\mathbf{F}_{2}:G\rightarrow\Bbbk^{*}
and
\phi_{c}:G\times G\times G\rightarrow\Bbbk^{*}
, respectively, by

\begin{array}[]{lll}\Phi_{1}(K^{s},K^{r})=\xi^{cr\varphi_{11}(K^{s})}_{\mathbf{m}},&\Phi_{2}(K^{s},K^{r})=\xi^{cr\varphi_{21}(K^{s})}_{\mathbf{m}},&\Upsilon(K^{s})=\xi^{-csm}_{\mathbf{m}},\\
\mathbf{F}_{1}(K^{s})=\xi^{-c(s-2d)\varphi_{11}(K^{s})}_{\mathbf{m}},&\mathbf{F}_{2}(K^{s})=\xi^{-c(s+2d)\varphi_{21}(K^{s})}_{\mathbf{m}},&\\
\phi_{c}(K^{s},K^{t},K^{r})=\xi^{cr[\frac{s+t}{m}]}_{m}.&&\\
\end{array}

where
0\leqslant s,t,r<m
,
\varphi_{11}(K^{s})=\overline{(s-2d)}-(s-2d)
,
\varphi_{21}(K^{s})=\overline{(s+2d)}-(s+2d)
, and
\overline{(s\pm 2d)}
is the remainder of
(s\pm 2d)
divided by
m
.

For any
g=K^{t}\in G
with
0\leqslant t<m
, define a character
\chi_{g}:G\rightarrow{\Bbbk}^{*}
by

\chi_{g}(K^{r})=\xi^{tr}_{m},\ 0\leqslant r<m,

and let
1_{g}=\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{h\in G}\chi_{g}(h)h\in\Bbbk G
. Let

I
be the ideal of
U_{q}
generated by
E^{n}
,
F^{n}
and
K^{dn}-1
, and
\widetilde{U}_{q}:=U_{q}/I
the corresponding quotient algebra. Then
\Bbbk G\subset\widetilde{U}_{q}
. It is easy to see that dim
(\widetilde{U}_{q})=dn^{3}
. One can check that
\widetilde{U}_{q}=U_{q}/I
is a quasi-Hopf algebra with the comultiplication, the counit given by

\begin{array}[]{ll}\Delta(E)=\sum_{f,g\in G}\Phi_{1}(f,g)E1_{f}\otimes 1_{g}+K\otimes E,&\varepsilon(E)=0,\\
\Delta(F)=\sum_{f,g\in G}\Phi_{2}(f,g)\chi_{g}(K)F1_{f}\otimes 1_{g}+1\otimes F,&\varepsilon(F)=0,\\
\Delta(K)=K\otimes K,&\varepsilon(K)=1.\\
\end{array}

The antipode
(S,\alpha,1)
is given by

\alpha=\sum_{g\in G}\Upsilon(g)1_{g}
, and

\begin{array}[]{l}S(F)=\chi^{-1}(K)\sum_{g\in G}\chi_{g}(K)\mathbf{F}_{2}(g)F1_{g},\ S(E)=\sum_{g\in G}\mathbf{F}_{1}(g)E1_{g},\ S(K)=K^{-1}.\end{array}

Moreover, the associator of
\widetilde{U}_{q}
is given by

\Phi_{c}=\Sigma_{f,g,h\in G}\phi_{c}(f,g,h)(1_{f}\otimes 1_{g}\otimes 1_{h}).

Throughout, unless otherwise stated, we assume
c=d=n>2
to be odd, and let
H=\widetilde{U}_{q}
be the small quasi-quantum groups given above. Then
m=n^{2}
. Let
\mathbf{q}=\xi_{m}
. Then
\mathbf{q}
is a primitive
m^{th}
-root of unity and
q=\mathbf{q}^{n}
.

For any
0\leqslant i\leqslant n-1
, let
e_{i}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{t=0}^{n-1}q^{ti}K^{tn}.

Then one can easily check that
\{e_{i}|0\leqslant i\leqslant n-1\}
forms a set of orthogonal central idempotents, and
\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}e_{i}=1
. Thus
H\cong He_{0}\times He_{1}\times\cdots\times He_{n-1}
as algebras.
Let
E_{i}:=Ee_{i},F_{i}:=Fe_{i},K_{i}:=Ke_{i}
in
He_{i}
,
0\leqslant i\leqslant n
. Then we have the following Lemma.

Lemma 2.1
.

For any
0\leqslant i\leqslant n-1
,
He_{i}
is generated, as an algebra, by
E_{i},F_{i},K_{i}
subject to following relations

\begin{array}[]{c}K_{i}E_{i}K^{-1}_{i}=q^{2}E_{i},\ K_{i}F_{i}K^{-1}_{i}=q^{-2}F_{i},\ [E_{i},F_{i}]=\frac{K_{i}-K^{-1}_{i}}{q-q^{-1}},\\
K_{i}K^{-1}_{i}=K^{-1}_{i}K_{i}=e_{i},\ E^{n}_{i}=F^{n}_{i}=0,K^{n}_{i}=q^{n-i}e_{i},\end{array}

where
e_{i}
is the identity of
He_{i}
.

Proof.

Since
H
is generated, as an algebra, by
E,F
and
K
,
He_{i}
is generated by
E_{i},F_{i},K_{i}
. A straightforward computation shows that the relations in the lemma are satisfied in
He_{i}
. Hence dim
(He_{i})\leqslant n^{3}
. Since
H\cong He_{0}\times He_{1}\times\cdots\times He_{n-1}
as algebras, it follows from dim
(H)=n^{4}
that dim
(He_{i})=n^{3}
for all
0\leqslant i\leqslant n-1
.
Thus, the lemma follows.
∎

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
0\leqslant i\leqslant n-1
and
1\leqslant r\leqslant n-1
. Then

\begin{array}[]{c}F_{i}E^{r}_{i}=E^{r}_{i}F_{i}+E^{r-1}_{i}(\frac{1-q^{-2r}}{(1-q^{-2})(q-q^{-1})}K^{-1}_{i}-\frac{1-q^{2r}}{(1-q^{2})(q-q^{-1})}K_{i}).\end{array}

Consequently,

\begin{array}[]{c}FE^{r}=E^{r}F+E^{r-1}(\frac{1-q^{-2r}}{(1-q^{-2})(q-q^{-1})}K^{-1}-\frac{1-q^{2r}}{(1-q^{2})(q-q^{-1})}K).\end{array}

Proof.

It follows from Lemma
2.1
and the induction on

.
∎

,

,

,
\eta\in\overline{\Bbbk}
.

3.2.
Simple
H
-modules

In this subsection, we investigate simple
H
-modules.
From last subsection, we see that there are
n
simple

-modules

,

. Hence via the algebra isomorphism

form

to

,

is an
l

H

\Bbbk

such that the
H
-module action is determined by

\begin{array}[]{ll}Em_{i}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}m_{i+1},&1\leqslant i<l,\\
0,&i=l,\\
\end{array}\right.&Fm_{i}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}0,&i=1,\\
\beta_{i-1}(l)m_{i-1},&1<i\leqslant l,\\
\end{array}\right.\\
Km_{i}=q^{2i-l-1}m_{i},\ 1\leqslant i\leqslant l,\\
\end{array}

where
\beta_{i}(l)=\frac{\alpha_{i}(l)}{q^{2i-l}-q^{2i-l-2}}
and
\alpha_{i}(l)=(i)_{q^{2}}(1-q^{2(i-l)})
,
1\leqslant i\leqslant l-1
.
V_{1}
is trivial.

For any
1\leqslant t\leqslant n-1
and
r\in\mathbb{Z}
, let
{\bf V}(t,r)
be a vector space of dimension
n
with a
\Bbbk
-basis
\{v_{1},v_{2},\cdots,v_{n}\}

{\bf V}(t,r)
is an
H
-module with the following action:

\begin{array}[]{ll}Ev_{i}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}v_{i+1},&1\leqslant i<n,\\
0,&i=n,\\
\end{array}\right.&Fv_{i}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}0,&i=1,\\
\gamma_{i-1}v_{i-1},&2\leqslant i\leqslant n,\\
\end{array}\right.\\
Kv_{i}=\mathbf{q}^{t}q^{2(r+i-1)}v_{i},\ 1\leqslant i\leqslant n,\\
\end{array}

where
\gamma_{j}=\frac{1}{q-q^{-1}}(\mathbf{q}^{-t}\frac{q^{-2r}-q^{-2(r+j)}}{1-q^{-2}}-\mathbf{q}^{t}\frac{q^{2r}-q^{2(r+j)}}{1-q^{2}})\neq 0

for
1\leqslant j\leqslant n-1
. Such a basis

.

For any
H
-module
W
, let
W_{F}=\{w\in W|Fw=0\}
. If
W\neq 0
then
W_{F}\neq 0
. Indeed, let
0\neq x\in W
. Then
F^{n}x=0
, and hence there is a positive integer

such that
F^{i}x=0
and
F^{i-1}x\neq 0
. Thus,
F^{i-1}x\in W_{F}
, and so
W_{F}\neq 0
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
1\leqslant t\leqslant n-1
and
r\in\mathbb{Z}
. Then
{\bf V}(t,r)
is a simple
H
-module.

Proof.

It follows from the structure of the
H
-module
{\bf V}(t,r)
that
{\bf V}(t,r)_{F}=\Bbbk v_{1}
is
1
-dimensional. Let
W
be a nonzero
H
-submodule of
{\bf V}(t,r)
. Then
W_{F}\neq 0
and
W_{F}\subseteq{\bf V}(t,r)_{F}
. Hence
W_{F}={\bf V}(t,r)_{F}=\Bbbk v_{1}
and
v_{1}\in W
. This implies
v_{i}\in W
for all
1\leqslant i\leqslant n
and so
W={\bf V}(t,r)
. Hence
{\bf V}(t,r)
is a simple
H
-module.
∎

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
1\leqslant t,t^{\prime}\leqslant n-1
and
r,r^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}
. Then
{\bf V}(t,r)\cong{\bf V}(t^{\prime},r^{\prime})
if and only if
t=t^{\prime}
and
r\equiv r^{\prime}

({\rm mod}\ n)
.

Proof.

If
t=t^{\prime}
and
r\equiv r^{\prime}

({\rm mod}\ n)
, then
{\bf V}(t,r)\cong{\bf V}(t^{\prime},r^{\prime})
is obvious. Conversely, assume that
\phi:{\bf V}(t,r)\rightarrow{\bf V}(t^{\prime},r^{\prime})
is an
H
-module isomorphism. Then
\phi({\bf V}(t,r)_{F})={\bf V}(t^{\prime},r^{\prime})_{F}
. Let
0\neq v\in{\bf V}(t,r)_{F}
. Then
0\neq\phi(v)\in{\bf V}(t^{\prime},r^{\prime})_{F}
. By the proof of Lemma
3.1
,
Kv=\mathbf{q}^{t}q^{2r}v
and
K\phi(v)=\mathbf{q}^{t^{\prime}}q^{2r^{\prime}}\phi(v)
. It follows from
\phi(Kv)=K\phi(v)
that
\mathbf{q}^{t}q^{2r}=\mathbf{q}^{t^{\prime}}q^{2r^{\prime}}
, which implies
t=t^{\prime}
and
r\equiv r^{\prime}

({\rm mod}\ n)
.
∎

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
V
be a simple
H
-module. Then
V
is isomorphic to
V_{l}
or
{\bf V}(t,r)
for some
1\leqslant l\leqslant n
,
1\leqslant t\leqslant n-1
and
r\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Proof.

Let
V
be a simple
H
-module, and let
\phi:

H\longrightarrow{\rm End}(V)
be the corresponding algebra map. One can easily check that
\phi(K)(V_{F})\subseteq V_{F}
. Let
v_{1}\in V
be an eigenvector of
\phi(K)
. Then there exists an integer
s
such that
Kv_{1}=\mathbf{q}^{s}v_{1}
because
K^{n^{2}}=1
. Since
E^{n}=0
, there exists an integer
l
with
1\leqslant l\leqslant n
such that
E^{l-1}v_{1}\neq 0
and
E^{l}v_{1}=0
. Thus, one can check that
N=
span
\{v_{1},Ev_{1},E^{2}v_{1},\cdots,E^{l-1}v_{1}\}
is a submodule of
V
. Therefore,
V=N
. Moreover, by Lemma
2.2
, a straightforward computation shows that

\begin{array}[]{ll}EE^{i}v_{1}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}E^{i+1}v_{1},&0\leqslant i<l-1,\\
0,&i=l-1,\\
\end{array}\right.&FE^{i}v_{1}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}0,&i=0,\\
\theta_{i}E^{i-1}v_{1},&1\leqslant i\leqslant l-1,\\
\end{array}\right.\\
KE^{i}v_{1}=\mathbf{q}^{s}q^{2i}E^{i}v_{1},\ 0\leqslant i\leqslant l-1,\\
\end{array}

where
\theta_{i}=\frac{1}{q-q^{-1}}(\mathbf{q}^{-s}\frac{q^{2i}-1}{q^{2i}}\frac{q^{2}}{q^{2}-1}-\mathbf{q}^{s}\frac{1-q^{2i}}{1-q^{2}})
for
1\leqslant i\leqslant l-1
. Since
V
is an
H
-module,
EFE^{l-1}v_{1}-FEE^{l-1}v_{1}=\frac{K-K^{-1}}{q-q^{-1}}E^{l-1}v_{1}
. Hence
\theta_{l-1}=\frac{1}{q-q^{-1}}(q^{2(l-1)}\mathbf{q}^{s}-q^{-2(l-1)}\mathbf{q}^{-s})
, and so

\begin{array}[]{c}\frac{1}{q-q^{-1}}(\mathbf{q}^{-s}\frac{q^{2(l-1)}-1}{q^{2(l-1)}}\frac{q^{2}}{q^{2}-1}-\mathbf{q}^{s}\frac{1-q^{2(l-1)}}{1-q^{2}})=\frac{1}{q-q^{-1}}(q^{2(l-1)}\mathbf{q}^{s}-q^{-2(l-1)}\mathbf{q}^{-s}),\end{array}

which is equivalent to
\mathbf{q}^{2s}(q^{2l}-1)=q^{2-2l}(q^{2l}-1)
.
If
1\leqslant l<n
, then
\mathbf{q}^{2s}=q^{2-2l}
, and hence
s\equiv n(1-l)
(mod
n^{2}
). In this case, one can check

. Now assume
l=n
and let
s=nk+t
with
0\leqslant t\leqslant n-1
. Since
V
is simple,
\theta_{i}\neq 0
for all
1\leqslant i\leqslant n-1
, or equivalently

n^{2}\nmid s+n(i-1)
for all
1\leqslant i\leqslant n-1
. If
t=0
, then
n\nmid k+i-1
for all
1\leqslant i\leqslant n-1
, or equivalently
n|k-1
. In this case, one can check

.
If
t\neq 0
and
k
is even, then one can check
V\cong{\bf V}(t,\frac{k}{2})
. If
t\neq 0
and
k
is odd, then one can check
V\cong{\bf V}(t,\frac{k+n}{2})
.
∎

For any
r\in\mathbb{Z}
, we still denote by
r
the image of
r
under the canonical epimorphism
{\mathbb{Z}}\rightarrow{\mathbb{Z}}_{n}
. Summarizing the above discussion, one obtains the following corollary.

Corollary 3.4
.

The set
\{V_{l},{\bf V}(t,r)|1\leqslant l\leqslant n,1\leqslant t\leqslant n-1,r\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{n}\}
is a representative set of isomorphic classes of simple
H
-modules.

In this subsection, we investigate all indecomposable

By Corollary

For any

For

\begin{array}[]{c}T^{i}_{j}=\frac{1}{n}(\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\mathbf{q}^{((j-1)n+i)k}K^{k}_{i}).\end{array}

Let

1\leqslant j\leqslant n

by

for

\begin{array}[]{c}a^{ij}_{k1}=\frac{q^{2(k-1)}\mathbf{q}^{(1-j)n-i}-q^{2(1-k)}\mathbf{q}^{i-(1-j)n}}{q-q^{-1}}\neq 0\ \text{ for }2\leqslant k\leqslant n,\end{array}

and

.
For

, define

\begin{array}[]{c}A^{ij}_{k}=\sum_{p=0}^{k-1}(\prod_{t=0}^{p}a^{ij}_{k,k-t})E^{k-1-p}_{i}F^{n-1-p}_{i}.\\
\end{array}

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
1\leqslant i\leqslant n-1
and
1\leqslant j,k\leqslant n
. Then
F_{i}A^{ij}_{k}T^{i}_{j}=0
.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.2
and

\begin{array}[]{c}A^{ij}_{k}=(\prod_{t=1}^{k}a^{ij}_{k,t})F^{n-k}_{i}+\sum_{p=0}^{k-2}(\prod_{t=0}^{p}a^{ij}_{k,k-t})E^{k-1-p}_{i}F^{n-1-p}_{i},\\
\end{array}

we have

\begin{array}[]{rl}&F_{i}A^{ij}_{k}T^{i}_{j}\\
\vskip 2.84544pt=&(\prod_{t=1}^{k}a^{ij}_{k,t})F^{n-k+1}_{i}T^{i}_{j}+\sum_{p=0}^{k-2}(\prod_{t=0}^{p}a^{ij}_{k,k-t})F_{i}E^{k-1-p}_{i}F^{n-1-p}_{i}T^{i}_{j}\\
\vskip 2.84544pt=&(\prod_{t=1}^{k}a^{ij}_{k,t})F^{n-k+1}_{i}T^{i}_{j}+\sum_{p=0}^{k-2}(\prod_{t=0}^{p}a^{ij}_{k,k-t})E^{k-1-p}_{i}F^{n-p}_{i}T^{i}_{j}\\
&+\sum_{p=0}^{k-2}(\prod_{t=0}^{p}a^{ij}_{k,k-t})E^{k-2-p}_{i}\frac{1-q^{-2(k-1-p)}}{(1-q^{-2})(q-q^{-1})}K^{-1}_{i}F^{n-1-p}_{i}T^{i}_{j}\\
&-\sum_{p=0}^{k-2}(\prod_{t=0}^{p}a^{ij}_{k,k-t})E^{k-2-p}_{i}\frac{1-q^{2(k-1-p)}}{(1-q^{2})(q-q^{-1})}K_{i}F^{n-1-p}_{i}T^{i}_{j}\\
\vskip 2.84544pt=&\sum_{p=1}^{k-1}(\prod_{t=0}^{p}a^{ij}_{k,k-t})E^{k-1-p}_{i}F^{n-p}_{i}T^{i}_{j}\\
&+\sum_{p=0}^{k-2}(\prod_{t=0}^{p}a^{ij}_{k,k-t})E^{k-2-p}_{i}F^{n-1-p}_{i}\frac{(1-q^{2(p+1-k)})q^{2(n-1-p)}\mathbf{q}^{i-(1-j)n}}{(1-q^{-2})(q-q^{-1})}T^{i}_{j}\\
&-\sum_{p=0}^{k-2}(\prod_{t=0}^{p}a^{ij}_{k,k-t})E^{k-2-p}_{i}F^{n-1-p}_{i}\frac{(1-q^{2(k-1-p)})q^{2(p+1-n)}\mathbf{q}^{(1-j)n-i}}{(1-q^{2})(q-q^{-1})}T^{i}_{j}\\
\vskip 2.84544pt=&\sum_{p=1}^{k-1}(\prod_{t=0}^{p}a^{ij}_{k,k-t})E^{k-1-p}_{i}F^{n-p}_{i}T^{i}_{j}+\sum_{p=0}^{k-2}(\prod_{t=0}^{p}a^{ij}_{k,k-t})\\
&E^{k-2-p}_{i}F^{n-1-p}_{i}T^{i}_{j}\frac{\mathbf{q}^{i-(1-j+2p)n}-\mathbf{q}^{i-(2k-j-1)n}+\mathbf{q}^{(3+2p-j)n-i}-\mathbf{q}^{(1-j+2k)n-i}}{(q^{2}-1)(q-q^{-1})}\\
=&0,\end{array}

where the last equality follows from the fact that

\begin{array}[]{c}-a^{ij}_{k,k-p}=\frac{\mathbf{q}^{i-(2p-j-1)n}-\mathbf{q}^{i-(2k-j-1)n}+\mathbf{q}^{(2p+1-j)n-i}-\mathbf{q}^{(1-j+2k)n-i}}{(q^{2}-1)(q-q^{-1})}.\end{array}

For any
1\leqslant i\leqslant n-1
and
1\leqslant j,k\leqslant n
, one can check
K_{i}A^{ij}_{k}=q^{2k}A^{ij}_{k}K_{i}
.
Let
r_{k,j}:=\frac{n+2k-j}{2}
if

is odd, and
r_{k,j}:=\frac{2k-j}{2}
if
2k-j
is even. Then we have the following proposition.

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
1\leqslant i\leqslant n-1
and
1\leqslant j,k\leqslant n
. Then the
He_{i}
-submodule
\langle A^{ij}_{k}T^{i}_{j}\rangle
of

is isomorphic to
{\bf V}(n-i,r_{k,j})
, where

is defined as above.

Proof.

One can easily check that

\{A^{ij}_{k}T^{i}_{j},E_{i}A^{ij}_{k}T^{i}_{j},E^{2}_{i}A^{ij}_{k}T^{i}_{j},\cdots,E^{n-1}_{i}A^{ij}_{k}T^{i}_{j}\}

is a
\Bbbk
-basis of

\langle A^{ij}_{k}T^{i}_{j}\rangle
.
Moreover, by Lemma
2.2
, the

-module action on
\langle A^{ij}_{k}T^{i}_{j}\rangle
is determined by

and

where

∎

Proposition 3.7
.

Let
1\leqslant i\leqslant n-1
and
1\leqslant j\leqslant n
. Then the sum

and

∎

Lemma 4.3

, then

1_{m-k}v=v\ {\rm and}\ 1_{j}v=0
for any
0\leqslant j\leqslant m-1
with
j\neq m-k
.

Proof.

It follows from a straightforward computation.
∎

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
1\leqslant t\leqslant n-1
and
r\in\mathbb{Z}
. Then

\begin{array}[]{c}V_{2}\otimes{\bf V}(t,r)\cong{\bf V}(t,r)\otimes V_{2}\cong{\bf V}(t,r+\frac{n-1}{2})\oplus{\bf V}(t,r+\frac{n+1}{2}).\end{array}

Proof.

Let
M=V_{2}\otimes{\bf V}(t,r)
. Let
\{m_{1},m_{2}\}
and
\{v_{1},v_{2},\cdots,v_{n}\}
be the standard bases of
V_{2}
and
{\bf V}(t,r)
respectively, as stated in Subsection
3.2
. By Lemma
4.3
, we have

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 5.69046ptE(m_{1}\otimes v_{j})&=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}q^{-t}m_{2}\otimes v_{j}+q^{-1}m_{1}\otimes v_{j+1},&1\leqslant j\leqslant n-1,\\
q^{-t}m_{2}\otimes v_{n},&j=n,\\
\end{array}\right.\\
\vskip 5.69046ptE(m_{2}\otimes v_{j})&=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}qm_{2}\otimes v_{j+1},&1\leqslant j\leqslant n-1,\\
0,&j=n,\\
\end{array}\right.\\
\vskip 5.69046ptF(m_{1}\otimes v_{j})&=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}0,&j=1,\\
\gamma_{j-1}m_{1}\otimes v_{j-1},&2\leqslant j\leqslant n,\\
\end{array}\right.\\
F(m_{2}\otimes v_{j})&=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\mathbf{q}^{(t-2r)n-t}m_{1}\otimes v_{1},&j=1,\\
\mathbf{q}^{(t-2r-2(j-1))n-t}m_{1}\otimes v_{j}+\gamma_{j-1}m_{2}\otimes v_{j-1},&2\leqslant j\leqslant n,\\
\end{array}\right.\\
\end{array}

K(m_{1}\otimes v_{j})=\mathbf{q}^{t}q^{2(r+j-1)-1}m_{1}\otimes v_{j},\ 1\leqslant j\leqslant n,

and

K(m_{2}\otimes v_{j})=\mathbf{q}^{t}q^{2(r+j-1)+1}m_{2}\otimes v_{j},\ 1\leqslant j\leqslant n.

Now let
\langle m_{1}\otimes v_{1}\rangle
be the submodule of
M
generated by
m_{1}\otimes v_{1}
. By a straightforward computation,
K(m_{1}\otimes v_{1})=\mathbf{q}^{t}q^{2r-1}m_{1}\otimes v_{1}
and
E^{n-1}(m_{1}\otimes v_{1})=-q^{t}m_{2}\otimes v_{n-1}+qm_{1}\otimes v_{n}\neq 0
. Moreover, it is easy to see that
\{m_{1}\otimes v_{1},E(m_{1}\otimes v_{1}),\cdots,E^{n-1}(m_{1}\otimes v_{1})\}
is a linearly independent set over
\Bbbk
. One can check that
F(m_{1}\otimes v_{1})=0
and
FE^{j}(m_{1}\otimes v_{1})=\gamma^{\prime}_{j}E^{j-1}(m_{1}\otimes v_{1})
, where
\gamma^{\prime}_{j}=\frac{1}{q-q^{-1}}(\mathbf{q}^{-t}\frac{q^{1-2r}-q^{1-2r-2j}}{1-q^{-2}}-\mathbf{q}^{t}\frac{q^{2r-1}-q^{2r-1+2j}}{1-q^{2}})
for
1\leqslant j\leqslant n-1
. Hence
\langle m_{1}\otimes v_{1}\rangle\cong{\bf V}(t,r+\frac{n-1}{2})
.
Similarly, let
u=m_{1}\otimes v_{2}-\gamma_{1}\mathbf{q}^{t-(t-2r)n}m_{2}\otimes v_{1}
, and let
\langle u\rangle
be the submodule of
M
generated by
u
. One can check that
Ku=\mathbf{q}^{t}q^{2r+1}u
,
E^{n-1}u=(-\gamma_{1}q^{-1}\mathbf{q}^{t-(t-2r)n}-q^{-t})m_{2}\otimes v_{n}\neq 0
, and
\{u,Eu,\cdots,E^{n-1}u\}
are linearly independent over
\Bbbk
. Moreover,
Fu=0
and
FE^{j}u=\gamma^{\prime\prime}_{j}E^{j-1}u
, where
\gamma^{\prime\prime}_{j}=\frac{1}{q-q^{-1}}(\mathbf{q}^{-t}\frac{q^{-2r-1}-q^{-2r-1-2j}}{1-q^{-2}}-\mathbf{q}^{t}\frac{q^{2r+1}-q^{2r+1+2j}}{1-q^{2}})
,
1\leqslant j\leqslant n-1
. Therefore, we have
\langle u\rangle\cong{\bf V}(t,r+\frac{n+1}{2})
. It follows that
M\cong{\bf V}(t,r+\frac{n-1}{2})\oplus{\bf V}(t,r+\frac{n+1}{2})
by comparing their dimensions.

Now let
N={\bf V}(t,r)\otimes V_{2}
. Then a similar argument to the above shows that
\langle v_{1}\otimes m_{1}\rangle\cong{\bf V}(t,r+\frac{n-1}{2})
and
\langle q\gamma_{1}v_{1}\otimes m_{2}-v_{2}\otimes m_{1}\rangle\cong{\bf V}(t,r+\frac{n+1}{2})
. Hence
N\cong{\bf V}(t,r+\frac{n-1}{2})\oplus{\bf V}(t,r+\frac{n+1}{2})
. This completes the proof.
∎

Proposition 4.5
.

Let
1\leqslant l\leqslant n
,
1\leqslant t\leqslant n-1
and
r\in\mathbb{Z}
.

(1)

If
l
is odd, then
V_{l}\otimes{\bf V}(t,r)\cong{\bf V}(t,r)\otimes V_{l}\cong\oplus_{j=0}^{l-1}{\bf V}(t,r-\frac{l-1}{2}+j).

(2)

If
l
is even, then
V_{l}\otimes{\bf V}(t,r)\cong{\bf V}(t,r)\otimes V_{l}\cong\oplus_{j=0}^{l-1}{\bf V}(t,r+\frac{n-l+1}{2}+j).

Proof.

We only consider
V_{l}\otimes{\bf V}(t,r)
since the proof for
{\bf V}(t,r)\otimes V_{l}
is similar. We show it by induction on
l
. For
l=1
and
l=2
, it follows from Lemma
4.4
and that
V_{1}
is the trivial
H
-module. Now let
2\leqslant l<n
. If
l
is odd, then
l-1
and
l+1
are even. By
[
10
, Proposition 3.1]
, Lemma
4.4
and the induction hypothesis, we have

\begin{array}[]{rl}V_{2}\otimes V_{l}\otimes{\bf V}(t,r)\cong&V_{l-1}\otimes{\bf V}(t,r)\oplus V_{l+1}\otimes{\bf V}(t,r)\\
\cong&(\oplus_{j=0}^{l-2}{\bf V}(t,r+\frac{n-l+2}{2}+j))\oplus V_{l+1}\otimes{\bf V}(t,r)\end{array}

and

\begin{array}[]{rl}V_{2}\otimes V_{l}\otimes{\bf V}(t,r)\cong&\oplus_{j=0}^{l-1}V_{2}\otimes{\bf V}(t,r-\frac{l-1}{2}+j)\\
\cong&(\oplus_{j=0}^{l-1}{\bf V}(t,r+\frac{n-l}{2}+j))\oplus(\oplus_{j=0}^{l-1}{\bf V}(t,r+\frac{n+2-l}{2}+j)).\end{array}

Thus, it follows from the Krull-Schmidt Theorem that

\begin{array}[]{c}V_{l+1}\otimes{\bf V}(t,r)\cong\oplus_{j=0}^{l}{\bf V}(t,r+\frac{n-l}{2}+j).\end{array}

If
l
is even, then
l-1
and
l+1
are odd. Similarly, by
[
10
, Proposition 3.1]
and the induction hypothesis, we have

\begin{array}[]{rl}V_{2}\otimes V_{l}\otimes{\bf V}(t,r)\cong&V_{l-1}\otimes{\bf V}(t,r)\oplus V_{l+1}\otimes{\bf V}(t,r)\\
\cong&(\oplus_{j=0}^{l-2}{\bf V}(t,r-\frac{l-2}{2}+j))\oplus V_{l+1}\otimes{\bf V}(t,r)\end{array}

and

\begin{array}[]{rl}V_{2}\otimes V_{l}\otimes{\bf V}(t,r)\cong&\oplus_{j=0}^{l-1}V_{2}\otimes{\bf V}(t,r+\frac{n-l+1}{2}+j)\\
\cong&(\oplus_{j=0}^{l-1}{\bf V}(t,r+\frac{2n-l}{2}+j))\oplus(\oplus_{j=0}^{l-1}{\bf V}(t,r+\frac{2n-l+2}{2}+j)).\end{array}

Thus, it follows from the Krull-Schmidt Theorem that

\begin{array}[]{c}V_{l+1}\otimes{\bf V}(t,r)\cong\oplus_{j=0}^{l}{\bf V}(t,r-\frac{l}{2}+j).\end{array}

This completes the proof.
∎

Let
M
and
N
be two
H
-modules. Recall from
[
11
, Proposition 4.2.12]
, that
M\otimes N
is projective if either
M
or
N
is projective.

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
M
be an
H
-module and
P
a projective
H
-module. Assume that
S_{1},S_{2},\cdots,S_{t}
are all non-isomorphic simple factors of
M
with the multiplicities
l_{1},l_{2},\cdots,l_{t}
, respectively. Then

M\otimes P\cong\oplus_{j=1}^{t}l_{j}S_{j}\otimes P\text{ and }P\otimes M\cong\oplus_{j=1}^{t}l_{j}P\otimes S_{j}.

Proof.

We prove the lemma by induction on the radical length
r:={\rm rl}(M)
. If
r=1
then
M\cong\oplus_{j=1}^{t}l_{j}S_{j}
.
Hence
M\otimes P\cong(\oplus_{j=1}^{t}l_{j}S_{j})\otimes P\cong\oplus_{j=1}^{t}l_{j}S_{j}\otimes P
. Now let
r>1
. Suppose
M/{\rm rad}(M)\cong\oplus_{j=1}^{t}n_{j}S_{j}
. Then
(M/{\rm rad}(M))\otimes P\cong\oplus_{j=1}^{t}n_{j}S_{j}\otimes P
. Obviously, there is an exact sequence of
H
-modules

0\rightarrow{\rm rad}(M)\rightarrow M\rightarrow M/{\rm rad}(M)\rightarrow 0.

By tensoring with
P
, one obtains the exact sequence of
H
-modules:

0\rightarrow{\rm rad}(M)\otimes P\rightarrow M\otimes P\rightarrow(M/{\rm rad}(M))\otimes P\rightarrow 0.

Since
(M/{\rm rad}(M))\otimes P
is projective, the above exact sequence is split. Thus, by the induction hypothesis, we have

\begin{array}[]{rl}M\otimes P\cong&{\rm rad}(M)\otimes P\oplus(M/{\rm rad}(M))\otimes P\\
\cong&(\oplus_{j=1}^{t}(l_{j}-n_{j})S_{j}\otimes P)\oplus(\oplus_{j=1}^{t}n_{j}S_{j}\otimes P)\\
\cong&\oplus_{j=1}^{t}l_{j}S_{j}\otimes P.\\
\end{array}

Similarly, one can show that
P\otimes M\cong\oplus_{j=1}^{t}l_{j}P\otimes S_{j}
.
∎

Proposition 4.7
.

and

. Then

P_{l}\otimes{\bf V}(t,r)\cong{\bf V}(t,r)\otimes P_{l}\cong\oplus_{j=0}^{n-1}2{\bf V}(t,j).

Proof.

By Corollary
3.9
,
{\bf V}(t,r)
is projective.
By the Section 2 of
[
7
]
,

.

4.5
and Lemma
4.6
, we have

In any case, we always have

.

Proposition 4.10
.

Let
1\leqslant t,t^{\prime}\leqslant n-1
with
t+t^{\prime}\neq n
, and
r,r^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}
.

(1)
If
t+t^{\prime}<n
, then
{\bf V}(t,r)\otimes{\bf V}(t^{\prime},r^{\prime})\cong\oplus_{j=0}^{n-1}{\bf V}(t+t^{\prime},j)
.

(2)
If
t+t^{\prime}>n
, then
{\bf V}(t,r)\otimes{\bf V}(t^{\prime},r^{\prime})\cong\oplus_{j=0}^{n-1}{\bf V}(t+t^{\prime}-n,j)
.

Proof.

Let
M={\bf V}(t,r)\otimes{\bf V}(t^{\prime},r^{\prime})
. Assume that
\{m_{1},m_{2},\cdots,m_{n}\}
and
\{v_{1},v_{2},\cdots,v_{n}\}
are the standard bases of
{\bf V}(t,r)

and
{\bf V}(t^{\prime},r^{\prime})
, respectively. Then
\{m_{i}\otimes v_{j}|1\leqslant i,j\leqslant n\}
is a basis of
M
. The
H
-module actions on
{\bf V}(t,r)
and on
{\bf V}(t^{\prime},r^{\prime})
are given respectively by

Km_{i}=\mathbf{q}^{t}q^{2(r+i-1)}m_{i},\ Kv_{i}=\mathbf{q}^{t^{\prime}}q^{2(r^{\prime}+i-1)}v_{i},\ 1\leqslant i\leqslant n,

\begin{array}[]{ll}Em_{i}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}m_{i+1},&1\leqslant i<n,\\
0,&i=n\\
\end{array}\right.&Ev_{i}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}v_{i+1},&1\leqslant i<n,\\
0,&i=n,\\
\end{array}\right.\\
\end{array}

\begin{array}[]{ll}Fm_{i}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}0,&i=1,\\
\gamma_{i-1}m_{i-1},&2\leqslant i\leqslant n\\
\end{array}\right.&Fv_{i}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}0,&i=1,\\
\gamma^{\prime}_{i-1}v_{i-1},&2\leqslant i\leqslant n,\\
\end{array}\right.\\
\end{array}

where
\gamma_{j}
and
\gamma^{\prime}_{j}
are given in Subsection 3.2,
1\leqslant j\leqslant n-1
. For
2\leqslant l\leqslant 2n
and
s\in\mathbb{Z}
, let
M_{l}={\rm span}\{m_{i}\otimes v_{j}|1\leqslant i,j\leqslant n,i+j=l\}
and
T^{s}=\{x\in M|Kx=\mathbf{q}^{t+t^{\prime}}q^{2(r+r^{\prime}+s-2)}x\}
. Then
T^{s}={\rm span}\{m_{i}\otimes v_{j}|i+j\equiv s\ ({\rm mod}\ n)\}
and
M=\oplus_{l=2}^{2n}M_{l}=\oplus_{s=2}^{n+1}T^{s}
. Moreover,

\begin{array}[]{ll}{\rm dim}(M_{l})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}l-1,&2\leqslant l\leqslant n+1,\\
2n-l+1,&n+1<l\leqslant 2n.\\
\end{array}\right.&\end{array}

Define a linear endomorphism
f
of
M
by
f(x)=Fx
,
x\in M
. It is easy to see that
f(M_{l})\subseteq M_{l-1}
for all
2\leqslant l\leqslant 2n
, where
M_{1}=0
. Hence
{\rm ker}f=\oplus_{l=2}^{2n}{\rm ker}f\cap M_{l}
and
FM_{2}=0
. Obviously, dim
({\rm ker}f\cap M_{2})=1
. Let
3\leqslant l\leqslant n+1
and
x\in M_{l}
. Then
x=\sum_{i=1}^{l-1}\alpha_{i}m_{i}\otimes v_{l-i}
for some
\alpha_{i}\in\Bbbk
, and hence

\begin{array}[]{rl}Fx=&\sum_{i=1}^{l-1}\alpha_{i}(F\otimes 1)(T_{3}\otimes K^{n^{2}-1}+T_{4}\otimes K^{n-1})(m_{i}\otimes v_{l-i})\\
&+\sum_{i=1}^{l-1}\alpha_{i}(1\otimes F)(m_{i}\otimes v_{l-i})\\
=&\sum_{i=2}^{l-1}\alpha_{i}\gamma_{i-1}\beta_{l-i}m_{i-1}\otimes v_{l-i}+\sum_{i=1}^{l-2}\alpha_{i}\gamma^{\prime}_{l-i-1}m_{i}\otimes v_{l-i-1}\\
=&\sum_{i=1}^{l-2}\alpha_{i+1}\gamma_{i}\beta_{l-i-1}m_{i}\otimes v_{l-i-1}+\sum_{i=1}^{l-2}\alpha_{i}\gamma^{\prime}_{l-i-1}m_{i}\otimes v_{l-i-1}\\
=&\sum_{i=1}^{l-2}(\alpha_{i}\gamma^{\prime}_{l-i-1}+\alpha_{i+1}\gamma_{i}\beta_{l-i-1})m_{i}\otimes v_{l-i-1},\end{array}

where
0\neq\beta_{j}\in\Bbbk
for
1\leqslant j\leqslant l-2
. It follows that
x\in{\rm ker}f\Leftrightarrow

\alpha_{i}\gamma^{\prime}_{l-i-1}+\alpha_{i+1}\gamma_{i}\beta_{l-i-1}=0
for all
1\leqslant i\leqslant l-2
, which implies that dim(
{\rm ker}f\cap M_{l})=1
.

Now let
n+1<l\leqslant 2n
and
x\in M_{l}
. Then
x=\sum_{i=l-n}^{n}\alpha_{i}m_{i}\otimes v_{l-i}
for some
\alpha_{i}\in\Bbbk
, and hence

\begin{array}[]{rl}Fx=&\sum_{i=l-n}^{n}\alpha_{i}(F\otimes 1)(T_{3}\otimes K^{n^{2}-1}+T_{4}\otimes K^{n-1})(m_{i}\otimes v_{l-i})\\
&+\sum_{i=l-n}^{n}\alpha_{i}(1\otimes F)(m_{i}\otimes v_{l-i})\\
=&\sum_{i=l-n}^{n}\alpha_{i}\gamma_{i-1}\beta_{l-i}m_{i-1}\otimes v_{l-i}+\sum_{i=l-n}^{n}\alpha_{i}\gamma^{\prime}_{l-i-1}m_{i}\otimes v_{l-i-1}\\
=&\sum_{i=l-n}^{n}\alpha_{i}\gamma_{i-1}\beta_{l-i}m_{i-1}\otimes v_{l-i}+\sum_{i=l-n+1}^{n+1}\alpha_{i-1}\gamma^{\prime}_{l-i}m_{i-1}\otimes v_{l-i}\\
=&\alpha_{l-n}\beta_{n}\gamma_{l-n-1}m_{l-n-1}\otimes v_{n}+\alpha_{n}\gamma^{\prime}_{l-n-1}m_{n}\otimes v_{l-n-1}.\\
&+\sum_{l-n+1\leqslant i\leqslant n}(\alpha_{i}\beta_{l-i}\gamma_{i-1}+\alpha_{i-1}\gamma^{\prime}_{l-i})m_{i-1}\otimes v_{l-i},\\
\end{array}

where
0\neq\beta_{j}\in\Bbbk
for
l-n\leqslant j\leqslant n.

It follows that
x\in{\rm ker}f\Leftrightarrow\alpha_{i}=0
for all
l-n\leqslant i\leqslant n

\Leftrightarrow x=0
. Consequently,
{\rm ker}f\cap M_{l}=\{0\}
for all
n+1<l\leqslant 2n
. Thus, one gets dim
({\rm ker}f\cap T^{s})=1
for all
2\leqslant s\leqslant n+1
,
and
{\rm ker}f=\oplus_{s=2}^{n+1}{\rm ker}f\cap T^{s}
.

Let
\phi_{K}
be the linear endomorphism of
M
defined by
\phi_{K}(x)=Kx
,
x\in M
. Then
\mathbf{q}^{t+t^{\prime}}q^{2(r+r^{\prime})},\mathbf{q}^{t+t^{\prime}}q^{2(r+r^{\prime}+1)},\cdots,\mathbf{q}^{t+t^{\prime}}q^{2(r+r^{\prime}+n-1)}
are all distinct eigenvalues of
\phi_{K}
. It follows from
t+t^{\prime}\neq n
that each simple submodule of
M
is isomorphic to
{\bf V}(l,i)
for some
1\leqslant l\leqslant n-1
and
0\leqslant i\leqslant n-1
. Thus by Theorem
3.10
, each indecomposable summand of
M
is isomorphic to some
{\bf V}(l,i)
. Moreover, by dim
({\rm ker}f\cap T^{s})=1
for all
0\leqslant s\leqslant n-1
and the structure of
{\bf V}(l,i)
, all the indecomposable summands of
M
are pairwise nonisomorphic, and the number of the indecomposable summands of
M
is equal to
n
.
Consequently, if
t+t^{\prime}<n
then
M\cong\oplus_{j=0}^{n-1}{\bf V}(t+t^{\prime},j)
, and if

then

M\cong\oplus_{j=0}^{n-1}{\bf V}(t+t^{\prime}-n,j)
.

Theorem 4.11
.

Let
1\leqslant t,t^{\prime}\leqslant n-1
and
r,r^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}
. Assume
t+t^{\prime}=n
and
r+r^{\prime}\equiv u\ ({\rm mod}\ n)
with
0\leqslant u\leqslant n-1
.
Let
P_{0}=0
.

(1)

If
0\leqslant u\leqslant\frac{n-1}{2}
, then

{\bf V}(t,r)\otimes{\bf V}(t^{\prime},r^{\prime})\cong(\oplus_{j=u}^{\frac{n-1}{2}}P_{2j})\oplus V_{n}\oplus(\oplus_{1\leqslant j\leqslant u-1}P_{n-2u+2j}).

(2)

If
\frac{n+1}{2}\leqslant u\leqslant n-1
, then

\begin{array}[]{c}{\bf V}(t,r)\otimes{\bf V}(t^{\prime},r^{\prime})\cong(\oplus_{n+1-u\leqslant j\leqslant\frac{n-1}{2}}P_{2j})\oplus(\oplus_{j=0}^{n-u}P_{2u+2j-n}).\\
\end{array}

Proof.

Let
M={\bf V}(t,r)\otimes{\bf V}(t^{\prime},r^{\prime})
. We use the notations in the proof of Proposition
4.10
. Then
T^{s+n}=T^{s}
for any
s\in\mathbb{Z}
, and
M=\oplus_{l=2}^{2n}M_{l}=\oplus_{s=2}^{n+1}T^{s}
. Moreover,
{\rm dim}({\rm ker}(f)\cap T^{s})=1
for any
s
,
{\rm dim}({\rm ker}(f)\cap M_{l})=1
for
2\leqslant l\leqslant n+1
,
{\rm ker}(f)\cap M_{l}=0
for
n+1<l\leqslant 2n
and
{\rm ker}(f)=\oplus_{l=2}^{n+1}{\rm ker}(f)\cap M_{l}=\oplus_{s=2}^{n+1}{\rm ker}(f)\cap T^{s}
. Clearly,
{\rm ker}(f)\cap M_{l}={\rm ker}(f)\cap T^{l}
for all for
2\leqslant l\leqslant n+1
. Let
g
be the linear endomorphism of
M
defined by
g(x)=Ex
,
x\in M
. Clearly,
g(M_{l})\subseteq M_{l+1}
for all
2\leqslant l\leqslant 2n
, where
M_{2n+1}=0
. An argument similar to the proof of Proposition
4.10
shows that
{\rm ker}(g)\cap M_{l}=0
for all
2\leqslant l\leqslant n
,
{\rm dim}({\rm ker}(g)\cap M_{l})=1
for all
n+1\leqslant l\leqslant 2n
and
{\rm ker}(g)=\oplus_{l=n+1}^{2n}{\rm ker}(g)\cap M_{l}
.

By Corollary
3.9
,
M
is projective. Hence the summands of
M
are all projective. Since
t+t^{\prime}=n
, any simple submodule of
M
is isomorphic to
V_{l}
for some
1\leqslant l\leqslant n
, which is a simple
He_{0}
-module. Hence any indecomposable summand of
M
is isomorphic to some
P_{l}
, where
1\leqslant l\leqslant n
and
P_{n}=V_{n}
. Note that
P_{l}
is both a projective cover of
V_{l}
and an injective envelope of
V_{l}
. It follows that
M
is an injective envelope of
{\rm soc}(M)
. By the structures of the simple modules
V_{l}
, any simple submodule of
M
is generated by a nonzero element of
{\rm ker}(f)\cap T^{s}
for some
s
. Moreover, for any
0\neq x\in{\rm ker}(f)\cap T^{s}
and
0\neq y\in{\rm ker}(f)\cap T^{s^{\prime}}
with
2\leqslant s\neq s^{\prime}\leqslant n+1
, if the submodules
\langle x\rangle
and

\langle y\rangle
are simple, then they are not isomorphic. Thus, it follows from the fact
{\rm dim}({\rm ker}(f)\cap T^{s})=1
that any two different simple submodules of
M
are not isomorphic. Consequently, any two different indecomposable summands of
M
are not isomorphic.

Assume
u=0
. Then
Kx=q^{2(s-2+\frac{1-n}{2})}x
for any
x\in T^{s}
. Let
N
be a simple
H
-submodule of
M
. We claim that
N
is not isomorphic to
V_{2k-1}
for any

. Suppose on the contrary that

for some
1\leqslant k\leqslant\frac{n-1}{2}
. Then
N
has a standard basis
\{x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,x_{2k-1}\}
such that
Kx_{i}=q^{2(i-k)}x_{i}
for all

,
Fx_{1}=Ex_{2k-1}=0
and
Ex_{i}=x_{i+1}
for all
1\leqslant i\leqslant 2k-2
. Hence
x_{1}\in{\rm ker}f\cap T^{\frac{n+5}{2}-k}
.
Since
1\leqslant k\leqslant\frac{n-1}{2}
, one has

. Hence

Lemma 5.19

Let

\begin{array}[]{rl}[{\bf V}(t,r)]=&\sum_{i=0}^{[\frac{r-1}{2}]}(-1)^{i}\binom{r-1-i}{i}(y^{2}-2)^{r-1-2i}\varepsilon_{t}\\
&-\sum_{i=0}^{[\frac{r-2}{2}]}(-1)^{i}\binom{r-2-i}{i}(y^{2}-2)^{r-2-2i}x_{t}.\\

Proof.

\begin{array}[]{rl}[{\bf V}(t,r+1)]=&(y^{2}-2)[{\bf V}(t,r)]-[{\bf V}(t,r-1)]\\
=&(y^{2}-2)(\sum_{i=0}^{[\frac{r-1}{2}]}(-1)^{i}\binom{r-1-i}{i}(y^{2}-2)^{r-1-2i}\varepsilon_{t}\\
&-\sum_{i=0}^{[\frac{r-2}{2}]}(-1)^{i}\binom{r-2-i}{i}(y^{2}-2)^{r-2-2i}x_{t})\\
&-\sum_{i=0}^{[\frac{r-2}{2}]}(-1)^{i}\binom{r-2-i}{i}(y^{2}-2)^{r-2-2i}\varepsilon_{t}\\
&+\sum_{i=0}^{[\frac{r-3}{2}]}(-1)^{i}\binom{r-3-i}{i}(y^{2}-2)^{r-3-2i}x_{t}\\
=&\sum_{i=0}^{[\frac{r}{2}]}(-1)^{i}\binom{r-i}{i}(y^{2}-2)^{r-2i}\varepsilon_{t}\\
&-\sum_{i=0}^{[\frac{r-1}{2}]}(-1)^{i}\binom{r-1-i}{i}(y^{2}-2)^{r-1-2i}x_{t}.\end{array}

This completes the proof.

Proposition 5.21

(2)

\begin{array}[]{l}\vskip 2.84544pt\sum_{i=0}^{\frac{r}{2}}(-1)^{i}\frac{r}{r-i}\binom{r-i}{i}y^{r-2i}x_{t}=[{\bf V}(t,-\frac{r}{2})]+[{\bf V}(t,\frac{r}{2})],\\

\begin{array}[]{rl}&\sum_{i=0}^{\frac{r}{2}}(-1)^{i}\frac{r}{r-i}\binom{r-i}{i}y^{r+1-2i}x_{t}=y[{\bf V}(t,-\frac{r}{2})]+y[{\bf V}(t,\frac{r}{2})]\\

\vskip 2.84544pt=&\sum_{i=0}^{\frac{r-1}{2}}(-1)^{i}\frac{r}{r-i}\binom{r-i}{i}y^{r+1-2i}x_{t}-\sum_{i=0}^{\frac{r-1}{2}}(-1)^{i}\frac{r-1}{r-1-i}\binom{r-1-i}{i}y^{r-1-2i}x_{t}\\

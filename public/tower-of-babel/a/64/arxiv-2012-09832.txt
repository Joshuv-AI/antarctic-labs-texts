Abstract
. In this article we construct a new motivic measure called the Jacques Tits motivic measure . As a first main application of the Jacques Tits motivic measure, we prove that two Severi-Brauer varieties (or, more generally, two twisted Grassmannian varieties), associated to 2 2 -torsion central simple algebras, have the same class in the Grothendieck ring of varieties if and only if they are isomorphic. In addition, we prove that if two Severi-Brauer varieties, associated to central simple algebras of period { 3 , 4 , 5 , 6 } \{3,4,5,6\} , have the same class in the Grothendieck ring of varieties, then they are necessarily birational to each other. As a second main application of the Jacques Tits motivic measure, we prove that two quadric hypersurfaces (or, more generally, two involution varieties), associated to quadratic forms of dimension 6 6 or to quadratic forms of arbitrary dimension defined over a base field k k with I 3 ​ ( k ) = 0 I^{3}(k)=0 , have the same class in the Grothendieck ring of varieties if and only if they are isomorphic. In addition, we prove that the latter main application also holds for products of quadric hypersurfaces.



1.
Introduction

Let
k
be a field and
\mathrm{Var}(k)
the category of
varieties
, i.e., reduced separated
k
-schemes of finite type. The
Grothendieck ring of varieties
K_{0}\mathrm{Var}(k)
, introduced in a letter from Grothendieck to Serre (consult
[
7
, letter of 16/08/1964]
), is defined as the quotient of the free abelian group on the set of isomorphism classes of varieties
[X]
by the “cut-and-paste” relations
[X]=[Y]+[X\backslash Y]
, where
Y
is a closed subvariety of
X
. The multiplication law is induced by the product of varieties. Despite the efforts of several mathematicians (consult, for example, the works of Bittner
[
3
]
and Larsen-Lunts
[
19
]
), the structure of the Grothendieck ring of varieties still remains nowadays poorly understood. In order to capture some of its flavor, a few
motivic measures
, i.e., ring homomorphisms
\mu\colon K_{0}\mathrm{Var}(k)\to R
, have been built. For example, when
k
is finite the assignment
X\mapsto\#X(k)
gives rise to the counting motivic measure
\mu_{\#}\colon K_{0}\mathrm{Var}(k)\to\mathbb{Z}
, and when
k=\mathbb{C}
the assignment
X\mapsto\chi(X):=\Sigma_{n}(-1)^{n}\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{Q}}\,H^{n}_{c}(X^{\mathrm{an}};\mathbb{Q})
gives rise to the Euler characteristic motivic measure
\mu_{\chi}\colon K_{0}\mathrm{Var}(k)\to\mathbb{Z}
. In this article we construct a new motivic measure
\mu_{\mathrm{JT}}
called the
Jacques Tits motivic measure
. Making use of it, we then establish several new properties of the Grothendieck ring of varieties.

Statement of results

Let
k
be a field of characteristic zero and
\Gamma:=\mathrm{Gal}(\overline{k}/k)
its absolute Galois group.

Recall that given a split semi-simple algebraic group
G
over
k
, a parabolic subgroup
P\subset G
, and a
1
-cocycle
\gamma\colon\Gamma\to G(\overline{k})
, we can consider the projective homogeneous variety
{\mathcal{F}}:=G/P
as well as its twisted form
{}_{\gamma}{\mathcal{F}}
. Let us write
\widetilde{G}
and
\widetilde{P}
for the universal covers of
G
and
P
, respectively,
R(\widetilde{G})
and
R(\widetilde{P})
for the associated representation rings,
n({\mathcal{F}})
for the index
[W(\widetilde{G}):W(\widetilde{P})]
of the Weyl groups,
\widetilde{Z}
for the center of
\widetilde{G}
, and
Ch
for the character group
\mathrm{Hom}(\widetilde{Z},\mathbb{G}_{m})
. As proved by Steinberg in
[
24
]
(consult also
[
22
, §12.5-§12.8]
), we have
R(\widetilde{P})=\oplus_{i}R(\widetilde{G})\rho_{i}
, where
\{\rho_{i}\}_{i}
is a canonical
Ch
-homogeneous basis of cardinality
n({\mathcal{F}})
. Let us denote by
A_{\rho_{i}}
the Jacques Tits central simple
k
-algebra associated to
\rho_{i}
; consult
[
17
, §27]
[
29
]
for details.

Notation 1.1
.

Let us write
K_{0}\mathrm{Var}(k)^{\mathrm{tw}}
for the smallest subring of
K_{0}\mathrm{Var}(k)
containing the Grothendieck classes
[{}_{\gamma}{\mathcal{F}}]
of all twisted projective homogeneous varieties
{}_{\gamma}{\mathcal{F}}
.

Consider the Brauer group
\mathrm{Br}(k)
of
k
, the associated group ring
\mathbb{Z}[\mathrm{Br}(k)]
, and the following quotient ring

R_{\mathrm{B}}(k):=\mathbb{Z}[\mathrm{Br}(k)]/\langle[k]+[A\otimes A^{\prime}]-[A]-[A^{\prime}]\,|\,(\mathrm{ind}(A),\mathrm{ind}(A^{\prime}))=1\rangle\,,

where
A
and
A^{\prime}
are central simple
k
-algebras with coprime indexes. Note that in the particular case where every element of
\mathrm{Br}(k)
is of
q
-primary torsion for some prime number
q
, it follows from the
p
-primary decomposition of the Brauer group
\mathrm{Br}(k)=\oplus_{p}\mathrm{Br}(k)\{p\}
that
R_{\mathrm{B}}(k)
reduces to the group ring
\mathbb{Z}[\mathrm{Br}(k)\{q\}]
.

Our main result is the following:

Theorem 1.2
.

The assignment
{}_{\gamma}{\mathcal{F}}\mapsto\Sigma_{i}[A_{\rho_{i}}]
gives rise to a motivic measure
\mu_{\mathrm{JT}}\colon K_{0}\mathrm{Var}(k)^{\mathrm{tw}}\to~R_{\mathrm{B}}(k)
.

Intuitively speaking, Theorem
1.2
shows that the Jacques Tits central simple algebras associated to a twisted projective homogeneous variety are preserved by the “cut-and-paste” relations. Motivated by this fact, we decided to call
\mu_{\mathrm{JT}}
the
Jacques Tits motivic measure
. The proof of Theorem
1.2
makes use, among other ingredients, of the recent theory of noncommutative motives; consult §
3
-§
4
below.

Applications

In this section we describe several applications of the Jacques Tits motivic measure to Severi-Brauer varieties, twisted Grassmannian varieties, quadric hypersurfaces, and involution varieties.

Notation 2.1
.

Given a central simple
k
-algebra
A
, let us write
\mathrm{deg}(A)
for its degree,
\mathrm{ind}(A)
for its index,
\mathrm{per}(A)
for its period,
[A]
for its class in the Brauer group

2.1.
Severi-Brauer varieties

Let
G
be the projective general linear group
PGL_{n}
, with
n\geq 2
. In this case, we have
\widetilde{G}=SL_{n}
. Consider the following parabolic subgroup:

\displaystyle\widetilde{P}:=\big\{\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
0&c\end{pmatrix}\,|\,a\cdot\mathrm{det}(c)=1\big\}\subset SL_{n}

\displaystyle a\in k^{\times}

\displaystyle c\in GL_{n-1}\,.

The associated projective homogeneous variety
{\mathcal{F}}:=G/P\simeq\widetilde{G}/\widetilde{P}
is the projective space
\mathbb{P}^{n-1}
and we have
R(\widetilde{P})=\oplus_{i=0}^{n-1}R(\widetilde{G})\rho_{i}
. Given a
1
-cocycle
\gamma\colon\Gamma\to PGL_{n}(\overline{k})
, let
A
be the corresponding central simple
k
-algebra of degree
n
. Under these notations, the twisted form
{}_{\gamma}\mathbb{P}^{n-1}
is the Severi-Brauer variety
\mathrm{SB}(A)
and the Jacques Tits central simple
k
-algebra
A_{\rho_{i}}
is the tensor product
A^{\otimes i}
.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
A
and
A^{\prime}
be two central simple
k
-algebras. If
[\mathrm{SB}(A)]=[\mathrm{SB}(A^{\prime})]
in the Grothendieck ring of varieties
K_{0}\mathrm{Var}(k)
, then the following holds:

(i)

We have
\mathrm{dim}(\mathrm{SB}(A))=\mathrm{dim}(\mathrm{SB}(A^{\prime}))
. Equivalently, we have
\mathrm{deg}(A)=\mathrm{deg}(A^{\prime})
.

(ii)

We have
\langle[A]\rangle=\langle[A^{\prime}]\rangle
. In particular, we have
\mathrm{per}(A)=\mathrm{per}(A^{\prime})
.

(iii)

When
[A]\in{}_{2}\mathrm{Br}(k)
, where
{}_{2}\mathrm{Br}(k)
is the
2
-torsion subgroup of
\mathrm{Br}(k)
, we have
\mathrm{SB}(A)\simeq\mathrm{SB}(A^{\prime})
.

(iv)

When
\mathrm{per}(A)\in\{3,4,5,6\}
, the Severi-Brauer varieties
\mathrm{SB}(A)
and
\mathrm{SB}(A^{\prime})
are birational to each other.

Note that item (i), resp. item (ii), shows that the dimension of a Severi-Brauer variety, resp. the subgroup generated by the Brauer class, is preserved by the “cut-and-paste” relations. Item (iii) shows that when the Brauer class is
2
-torsion (i.e., when
\mathrm{per}(A)\in\{1,2\}
), two Severi-Brauer varieties have the same Grothendieck class in
K_{0}\mathrm{Var}(k)
if and only if they are isomorphic! In other words, item (iii) yields the following inclusion:

(2.3)

\frac{\{\text{Severi-Brauer}\,\,\text{varieties}\,\,\mathrm{SB}(A)\,\,\text{with}\,\,[A]\in{}_{2}\mathrm{Br}(k)\}}{\text{isomorphism}}\subset K_{0}\mathrm{Var}(k)\,.

Note that thanks to the Artin-Wedderburn theorem, the left-hand side of (
2.3
) is in bijection with
{}_{2}\mathrm{Br}(k)\times\mathbb{N}
via the assignment
\mathrm{SB}(A)\mapsto([A],\mathrm{deg}(A))
. Note also that by restricting the inclusion (
2.3
) to central simple
k
-algebras of degree
2
, i.e., to quaternion algebras
Q=(a,b)
, we obtain the following inclusion

(2.4)

\frac{\{C(a,b):=(ax^{2}+by^{2}-z^{2}=0)\subset\mathbb{P}^{2}\,|\,a,b\in k^{\times}\}}{\text{isomorphism}}\subset K_{0}\mathrm{Var}(k)\,,

where
C(a,b)
stands for the smooth conic associated to the quaternion algebra
Q
.

Example 2.5

(Conics over
\mathbb{Q}
)
.

When
k=\mathbb{Q}
, there are infinitely many smooth conics in
\mathbb{P}^{2}
up to isomorphism. For example, given any two primes numbers
p\neq q
which are congruent to
3
modulo
4
, the conics
C(-1,p)
and
C(-1,q)
are
not
isomorphic. Consequently, since there are infinitely many prime numbers
p
which are congruent to
3
modulo
4
, the inclusion (
2.4
) yields the following
infinite
family of
distinct
Grothendieck classes
\{[(-x^{2}+py^{2}-z^{2}=0)]\}_{p\equiv 3\,(\text{mod}\,4)}\subset K_{0}\mathrm{Var}(\mathbb{Q})
.

Finally, item (iv) shows that, for small values of the period, if two Severi-Brauer varieties have the same Grothendieck class in
K_{0}\mathrm{Var}(k)
then they are necessarily birational to each other.

Remark 2.6

(Severi-Brauer surfaces)
.

Let
A
and
A^{\prime}
be two central simple

Remark 2.7

(Amitsur conjecture)

Let

be two central simple

Remark 2.8

(Stable birationality)

Let

The Severi-Brauer varieties
\mathrm{SB}(A)

\mathrm{SB}(A^{\prime})

2.2.
Products of conics

Recall that two quaternion

Proposition 2.9

Let

,

Q^{\prime\prime\prime}=(a^{\prime\prime\prime},b^{\prime\prime\prime})

(i)

(ii)

Note that item (i) shows that if two products of conics have the same Grothendieck class in

2.3.
Twisted Grassmannian varieties

Let

, with

. Recall that in this case we have
\widetilde{G}=SL_{n}
. Choose an integer

\displaystyle\widetilde{P}:=\big\{\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\

\displaystyle a\in GL_{d}

The associated projective homogeneous variety

is the Grassmannian variety

and we have

is a Young diagram inside the rectangle with
d

n-d

1
-cocycle
\gamma\colon\Gamma\to PGL_{n}(\overline{k})
, let
A
be the corresponding central simple
k
-algebra of degree
n
. Under these notations, the twisted form
{}_{\gamma}\mathrm{Gr}(d)
is the twisted Grassmannian variety
\mathrm{Gr}(d;A)
and the Jacques Tits central simple

Remark 2.12

(Generalization)
.

Note that in the particular case where

, the twisted Grassmannian variety
\mathrm{Gr}(d;A)
reduces to the Severi-Brauer variety
\mathrm{SB}(A)
.

Theorem 2.13
.

Let
A
and
A^{\prime}
be two central simple
k
-algebras and
1\leq d<\mathrm{deg}(A)
and
1\leq d^{\prime}<\mathrm{deg}(A^{\prime})

[\mathrm{Gr}(d;A)]=[\mathrm{Gr}(d^{\prime};A^{\prime})]

(i)

We have
\mathrm{dim}(\mathrm{Gr}(d;A))=\mathrm{dim}(\mathrm{Gr}(d^{\prime};A^{\prime}))

(ii)

(iii)

Note that, similarly to Theorem

(2.14)

\frac{\{\text{Twisted}\,\,\text{Grassmannian}\,\,\text{varieties}\,\,\mathrm{Gr}(d;A)\,\,\text{with}\,\,[A]\in{}_{2}\mathrm{Br}(k)\}}{\text{isomorphism}}\subset K_{0}\mathrm{Var}(k)\,.

Following Remark

, note that (
2.14

2.4.
Quadric hypersurfaces

Assume that
\mathrm{char}(k)\neq 2
. Let
G
be the special orthogonal group
SO_{n}
, with
n\geq 3
, with respect to the hyperbolic form
\frac{n}{2}\mathbb{H}
when

is even or to the form
\lfloor{\frac{n}{2}\rfloor}\mathbb{H}\!\perp\!\langle 1\rangle
when

is odd. In this case, we have
\widetilde{G}=\mathrm{Spin}_{n}
. Consider the action of
G
on
\mathbb{P}^{n-1}

[1:0:\cdots:0]

\widetilde{P}\subset\widetilde{G}

\displaystyle Q:=\begin{cases}(x_{1}y_{1}+\cdots+x_{\frac{n}{2}}y_{\frac{n}{2}}=0)\subset\mathbb{P}^{n-1}&\quad n\,\,\mathrm{even}\\
(x_{1}y_{1}+\cdots+x_{\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor}y_{\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor}+z^{2}=0)\subset\mathbb{P}^{n-1}&\quad n\,\,\mathrm{odd}\\

n
is odd. Given a
1
-cocycle
\gamma\colon\Gamma\to SO_{n}(\overline{k})
, let

be the corresponding non-degenerate quadratic form with trivial discriminant of dimension

{}_{\gamma}Q

\displaystyle A_{\rho_{i}}:=\begin{cases}k&0\leq i\leq n-3\quad n\,\,\mathrm{even}\\

Theorem 2.15
.

Let

and

be two non-degenerate quadratic forms with trivial discriminant of dimensions

, respectively. If

(i)

We have
\mathrm{dim}(Q_{q})=\mathrm{dim}(Q_{q^{\prime}})

.

(ii)

We have

(iii)

.

(iv)

When

, where

Note that item (i), resp. item (ii), shows that the dimension of the quadric hypersurface, resp. the Brauer class of the (simple components of the) even Clifford algebra, is preserved by the “cut-and-paste” relations. Item (iii) shows that when the dimension is equal to

, two quadric hypersurfaces have the same Grothendieck class in

if and only if they are isomorphic! Recall from
[

that, up to similarity, a non-degenerate quadratic form

\langle a,b,-ab,-a^{\prime},-b^{\prime},a^{\prime}b^{\prime}\rangle

(2.16)

\frac{\{Q_{q}:=(au^{2}+bv^{2}-abw^{2}-a^{\prime}x^{2}-b^{\prime}y^{2}+a^{\prime}b^{\prime}z^{2}=0)\subset\mathbb{P}^{5}\,|\,a,b,a^{\prime},b^{\prime}\in k^{\times}\}}{\text{isomorphism}}\subset K_{0}\mathrm{Var}(k)\,.

Example 2.17

(Quadric hypersurfaces over

)

When

Throughout the article
k

Notation 3.1
.

Given a central simple
k
-algebra
A
and a prime number
q
, let us write
[A]^{q}\in\mathrm{Br}(k)\{q\}
for the
q

[A]\in\mathrm{Br}(k)=\oplus_{p}\mathrm{Br}(k)\{p\}

Dg categories

A
differential graded (=dg) category
{\mathcal{A}}
is a category enriched over complexes of
k
-vector spaces; consult Keller’s survey
[
11
]
(and Bondal-Kapranov’s original article
[
4
]
). Every (dg)
k
-algebra
A
gives naturally rise to a dg category with a single object. Another source of examples is provided by schemes since the category of perfect complexes
\mathrm{perf}(X)
of every
k
-scheme
X
admits a canonical dg enhancement
\mathrm{perf}_{\mathrm{dg}}(X)
; consult
[
11
, §4.6]
. Let us denote by
\mathrm{dgcat}(k)
the category of (small) dg categories.

Let
{\mathcal{A}}
be a dg category. The
opposite dg category

{\mathcal{A}}^{\mathrm{op}}
has the same objects as
{\mathcal{A}}
and
{\mathcal{A}}^{\mathrm{op}}(x,y):={\mathcal{A}}(y,x)
. A
right dg
{\mathcal{A}}
-module
is a dg functor
M:{\mathcal{A}}^{\mathrm{op}}\to{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathrm{dg}}(k)
with values in the dg category of complexes of
k
-vector spaces. Let us denote by
{\mathcal{C}}({\mathcal{A}})
the category of right dg
{\mathcal{A}}
-modules. Following
[
11
, §3.2]
, the
derived category
{\mathcal{D}}({\mathcal{A}})
of
{\mathcal{A}}
is defined as the localization of
{\mathcal{C}}({\mathcal{A}})
with respect to the objectwise quasi-isomorphisms. In what follows, we will write
{\mathcal{D}}_{c}({\mathcal{A}})
for the subcategory of compact objects.

A dg functor
F\colon{\mathcal{A}}\to{\mathcal{B}}
is called a
Morita equivalence
if it induces an equivalence on derived categories
{\mathcal{D}}({\mathcal{A}})\simeq{\mathcal{D}}({\mathcal{B}})
; consult
[
11
, §4.6]
. As explained in
[
25
, §1.6.1]
,
\mathrm{dgcat}(k)
admits a Quillen model structure whose weak equivalences are the Morita equivalences. Let
\mathrm{Hmo}(k)
be the associated homotopy category.

The
tensor product
{\mathcal{A}}\otimes{\mathcal{B}}
of dg categories is defined as follows: the set of objects is the cartesian product of the sets of objects and
({\mathcal{A}}\otimes{\mathcal{B}})((x,w),(y,z)):={\mathcal{A}}(x,y)\otimes{\mathcal{B}}(w,z)
. As explained in
[
11
, §2.3]
, this construction gives rise to a symmetric monoidal structure on
\mathrm{dgcat}(k)
which descends to the homotopy category
\mathrm{Hmo}(k)
.

A
dg
{\mathcal{A}}\text{-}{\mathcal{B}}
-bimodule
is a dg functor
\mathrm{B}:{\mathcal{A}}\otimes{\mathcal{B}}^{\mathrm{op}}\to{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathrm{dg}}(k)
. For example, given a dg functor
F\colon{\mathcal{A}}\to{\mathcal{B}}
, we have the dg
{\mathcal{A}}\text{-}{\mathcal{B}}
-bimodule
{}_{F}{\mathcal{B}}\colon{\mathcal{A}}\otimes{\mathcal{B}}^{\mathrm{op}}\to{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathrm{dg}}(k),(x,z)\mapsto{\mathcal{B}}(z,F(x))
. Let
\mathrm{rep}({\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{B}})
be the full triangulated subcategory of
{\mathcal{D}}({\mathcal{A}}^{\mathrm{op}}\otimes{\mathcal{B}})
consisting of those dg
{\mathcal{A}}\text{-}{\mathcal{B}}
-bimodules
\mathrm{B}
such that for every object
x\in{\mathcal{A}}
the right dg
{\mathcal{B}}
-module
\mathrm{B}(x,-)
belongs to
{\mathcal{D}}_{c}({\mathcal{B}})
. Clearly, the dg
{\mathcal{A}}\text{-}{\mathcal{B}}
-bimodules
{}_{F}{\mathcal{B}}
belongs to
\mathrm{rep}({\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{B}})
.

Finally, following Kontsevich
[
14
,
15
,
16
]
, a dg category
{\mathcal{A}}
is called
smooth
if the dg
{\mathcal{A}}\text{-}{\mathcal{A}}
bimodule
{}_{\id}{\mathcal{A}}
belongs to
{\mathcal{D}}_{c}({\mathcal{A}}^{\mathrm{op}}\otimes{\mathcal{A}})
and
proper
if
\Sigma_{n}\mathrm{dim}\,H^{n}{\mathcal{A}}(x,y)<\infty
for any pair of objects
(x,y)
. Examples include finite-dimensional
k
-algebras of finite global dimension
A
as well as the dg categories of perfect complexes
\mathrm{perf}_{\mathrm{dg}}(X)
associated to smooth proper
k
-schemes
X
.

Noncommutative motives

For a book on noncommutative motives, we invite the reader to consult
[
25
]
. As explained in
[
25
, §1.6.3]
, given any two dg categories
{\mathcal{A}}
and
{\mathcal{B}}
, there is a natural bijection between
\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{Hmo}(k)}({\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{B}})
and the set of isomorphism classes of the category
\mathrm{rep}({\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{B}})
. Under this bijection, the composition in
\mathrm{Hmo}(k)
corresponds to the (derived) tensor product of bimodules.
The
additivization
of
\mathrm{Hmo}(k)
is the additive category
\mathrm{Hmo}_{0}(k)
with the same objects and with abelian groups of morphisms
\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{Hmo}_{0}(k)}({\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{B}})
given by the Grothendieck group
K_{0}\mathrm{rep}({\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{B}})
of the triangulated category
\mathrm{rep}({\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{B}})
. The composition law is induced by the (derived) tensor product of bimodules. Given a commutative ring of coefficients
R
, the
R
-linearization
of
\mathrm{Hmo}_{0}(k)
is the
R
-linear category
\mathrm{Hmo}_{0}(k)_{R}
obtained by tensoring the morphisms of
\mathrm{Hmo}_{0}(k)
with
R
. Note that we have the following (composed) symmetric monoidal functor

\displaystyle U(-)_{R}\colon\mathrm{dgcat}(k)\longrightarrow\mathrm{Hmo}_{0}(k)_{R}

\displaystyle{\mathcal{A}}\mapsto{\mathcal{A}}

\displaystyle({\mathcal{A}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle F}}{{\to}}{\mathcal{B}})\mapsto[{}_{F}{\mathcal{B}}]_{R}\,.

The category of
noncommutative Chow motives

\NChow(k)_{R}
is defined as the idempotent completion of the full subcategory of
\mathrm{Hmo}_{0}(k)_{R}
consisting of the objects
U({\mathcal{A}})_{R}
with
{\mathcal{A}}
a smooth proper dg category. This category is
R
-linear, additive, rigid symmetric monoidal, and idempotent complete. When
R=\mathbb{Z}
, we will write
\NChow(k)
instead of
\NChow(k)_{\mathbb{Z}}
and
U(-)
instead of
U(-)_{\mathbb{Z}}
.

Given an additive rigid symmetric monoidal category
{\mathcal{C}}
, recall that its
{\mathcal{N}}
-ideal
is defined as follows

{\mathcal{N}}(a,b):=\{f\in\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(a,b)\,|\,\forall g\in\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(b,a)\,\,\mathrm{we}\,\,\mathrm{have}\,\,\mathrm{tr}(g\circ f)=0\}\,,

where
\mathrm{tr}(g\circ f)
stands for the categorical trace of
g\circ f
. The category of
noncommutative numerical motives

\NNum(k)_{R}
is defined as the idempotent completion of the quotient of
\NChow(k)_{R}
by the
\otimes
-ideal
{\mathcal{N}}
. By construction, this category is
R
-linear, additive, rigid symmetric monoidal, and idempotent complete.

Remark 3.2

(Smooth proper schemes)

Given any two smooth projective

3.2

corresponds to the following bilinear pairing:

\displaystyle\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}\longrightarrow\mathbb{Z}

\displaystyle(n,m)\mapsto n\cdot\mathrm{ind}(A^{\mathrm{op}}\otimes A^{\prime})\cdot\mathrm{ind}(A^{\prime\mathrm{op}}\otimes A^{\prime\prime})\cdot m\,.

Since
\mathrm{ind}(A^{\mathrm{op}}\otimes A^{\prime})=\mathrm{ind}(A^{\prime\mathrm{op}}\otimes A)
, this implies, in particular, that the composition map

\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{CSA}(k)_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}}(U(A)_{\mathbb{F}_{p}},U(A^{\prime})_{\mathbb{F}_{p}})\times\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{CSA}(k)_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}}(U(A^{\prime})_{\mathbb{F}_{p}},U(A)_{\mathbb{F}_{p}})\longrightarrow\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{CSA}(k)_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}}(U(A)_{\mathbb{F}_{p}},U(A)_{\mathbb{F}_{p}})

corresponds to the following bilinear pairing

(4.4)

\displaystyle\mathbb{F}_{p}\times\mathbb{F}_{p}\longrightarrow\mathbb{F}_{p}

\displaystyle(n,m)\mapsto n\cdot\mathrm{ind}(A^{\mathrm{op}}\otimes A^{\prime})^{2}\cdot m\,;

similarly with
A
and
A^{\prime}
replaced by
A^{\prime}
and
A
, respectively. On the one hand, if
p\mid\mathrm{ind}(A^{\mathrm{op}}\otimes A^{\prime})
, the bilinear pairing (
4.4
) is equal to zero. This implies that
U(A)_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}\not\simeq U(A^{\prime})_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}
in the category
\mathrm{CSA}(k)_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}
and, by definition of the category
\underline{\mathrm{CSA}}(k)_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}
, that
\mathrm{Hom}_{\underline{\mathrm{CSA}}(k)_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}}(U(A)_{\mathbb{F}_{p}},U(A^{\prime})_{\mathbb{F}_{p}})=0
. In particular, we also have
U(A)_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}\not\simeq U(A^{\prime})_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}
in the category
\underline{\mathrm{CSA}}(k)_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}
. On the other hand, if
p\nmid\mathrm{ind}(A^{\mathrm{op}}\otimes A^{\prime})
, the integer
\mathrm{ind}(A^{\mathrm{op}}\otimes A^{\prime})
is invertible in
\mathbb{F}_{p}
. Consequently, we conclude from (
4.4
) that
U(A)_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}\simeq U(A^{\prime})_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}
in the category
\mathrm{CSA}(k)_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}
(and hence in the category
\underline{\mathrm{CSA}}(k)_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}
). Thanks to the definition of the category
\underline{\mathrm{CSA}}(k)_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}
, this implies moreover that
\mathrm{Hom}_{\underline{\mathrm{CSA}}(k)_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}}(U(A)_{\mathbb{F}_{p}},U(A^{\prime})_{\mathbb{F}_{p}})\simeq\mathbb{F}_{p}
. Finally, note that the
p
-primary decomposition of the Brauer group
\mathrm{Br}(k)=\oplus_{p}\mathrm{Br}(k)\{p\}
implies that
p\nmid\mathrm{ind}(A^{\mathrm{op}}\otimes A^{\prime})
if and only if
[A]^{p}=[A^{\prime}]^{p}
in
\mathrm{Br}(k)\{p\}
.
∎

Lemma 4.15
.

We have
N\!\!M_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}\simeq\oplus^{m}_{j=1}U(A_{j})_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}^{\oplus r_{j}}\oplus\oplus^{m^{\prime}}_{j=1}U(A^{\prime}_{j})_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}^{\oplus r^{\prime}_{j}}\oplus M\!\!N

By definition, the category

Notation 4.18
.

Let us write
K_{0}(\mathrm{CSA}(k)^{\oplus})

Proposition 4.19
.

The inclusion of categories
\mathrm{CSA}(k)^{\oplus}\subset\NChow(k)
gives rise to an injective ring homomorphism
K_{0}(\mathrm{CSA}(k)^{\oplus})\to K_{0}(\NChow(k))

Proof.

Recall first that the group completion of an arbitrary monoid
(M,+)
is defined as the quotient of the product

by the following equivalence relation:

(4.20)

(m,n)\sim(m^{\prime},n^{\prime}):=\exists\,r\in M\,\,\mathrm{such}\,\,\mathrm{that}\,\,m+n^{\prime}+r=n+m^{\prime}+r\,.

Let us write
K_{0}(\NChow(k))^{+}
for the semi-ring of the additive symmetric monoidal category
\NChow(k)
. Concretely,
K_{0}(\NChow(k))^{+}
is the set of isomorphism classes of noncommutative Chow motives equipped with the addition, resp. multiplication, law induced by
\oplus
, resp.
\otimes
. In the same vein, let us write
K_{0}(\mathrm{CSA}(k)^{\oplus})^{+}
for the semi-ring of the additive symmetric monoidal category
\mathrm{CSA}(k)^{\oplus}
. Clearly, the inclusion of categories
\mathrm{CSA}(k)^{\oplus}\subset\NChow(k)
gives rise to an injective homomorphism
K_{0}(\mathrm{CSA}(k)^{\oplus})^{+}\to K_{0}(\NChow(k))^{+}
. Therefore, by combining the above definition of group completion (
4.20
) with the cancellation Proposition
4.19
, we conclude that the induced (ring) homomorphism
K_{0}(\mathrm{CSA}(k)^{\oplus})\to K_{0}(\NChow(k))
is also injective.
∎

Consider the (composed) ring homomorphism

(4.21)

K_{0}\mathrm{Var}(k)^{\mathrm{tw}}\subset K_{0}\mathrm{Var}(k)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mu_{\mathrm{nc}}}}{{\longrightarrow}}K_{0}(\NChow(k))\,.

Let
{}_{\gamma}{\mathcal{F}}

gives rise to a (well-defined) motivic measure

Proposition 4.22
.

The assignment

gives rise to a ring isomorphism
R_{\mathrm{B}}(k)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\simeq}}{{\to}}K_{0}(\mathrm{CSA}(k)^{\oplus})

Let us write
K_{0}(\mathrm{CSA}(k)^{\oplus})^{+}

K_{0}(\mathrm{CSA}(k)^{\oplus})^{+}

\displaystyle\mathbb{N}[\mathrm{Br}(k)]\longrightarrow K_{0}(\mathrm{CSA}(k)^{\oplus})^{+}

\mathbb{N}[\mathrm{Br}(k)]/\{\Sigma^{m}_{j=1}[A_{j}]=\Sigma^{m}_{j=1}[A^{\prime}_{j}]\,|\,\,\forall\,p\,\,\,\exists\,\sigma_{p}\,\,\,[A^{\prime}_{j}]^{p}=[A_{\sigma_{p}(j)}]^{p}\,\,\,\forall\,1\leq j\leq m\}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\simeq}}{{\longrightarrow}}K_{0}(\mathrm{CSA}(k)^{\oplus})^{+}\,,

Lemma 4.26
.

The following two sets of relations on the semi-ring
\mathbb{N}[\mathrm{Br}(k)]
are equivalent:

(4.27)

\{\Sigma^{m}_{j=1}[A_{j}]=\Sigma^{m}_{j=1}[A^{\prime}_{j}]\,|\,\,\forall\,p\,\,\,\exists\,\sigma_{p}\,\,\,[A^{\prime}_{j}]^{p}=[A_{\sigma_{p}(j)}]^{p}\,\,\,\forall\,1\leq j\leq m\}

(4.28)

\{[A^{\prime\prime}]+[A\otimes A^{\prime}\otimes A^{\prime\prime}]=[A\otimes A^{\prime\prime}]+[A\otimes A^{\prime\prime}]\,|\,(\mathrm{ind}(A),\mathrm{ind}(A^{\prime}))=1\}\,.

Proof.

Note first that the set (
4.28
) is contained in the set (
4.27
). Note also that since every permutation
\sigma_{p}
can be written as a composition of transpositions, (
4.27
) is equivalent to the following set of relations

(4.29)

\{[A_{1}]+[A_{2}]=[A^{\prime}_{1}]+[A^{\prime}_{2}]\,\,|\,\,[A^{\prime}_{1}]^{q}=[A_{2}]^{q},[A^{\prime}_{2}]^{q}=[A_{1}]^{q}\,\,\,\mathrm{and}\,\,\,[A^{\prime}_{1}]^{p}=[A_{1}]^{p},[A^{\prime}_{2}]^{p}=[A_{2}]^{p}\,\,\forall\,p\neq q\}\,,

where
q\neq p
is a(ny) prime number. Therefore, it suffices to show that every relation in (
4.29
) is a particular case of a relation in (
4.28
). Recall from the Artin-Wedderburn theorem that
A_{1}
, resp.
A_{2}
, may be written as the matrix algebra of a
unique
central division
k
-algebra
D_{1}
, resp.
D_{2}
. Let us write
D_{1}=\otimes_{p}D_{1}^{p}
, resp.
D_{2}=\otimes_{p}D_{2}^{p}
, for the associated
p
-primary decomposition; consult
[
9
, Prop. 4.5.16]
. By construction, we have
[D_{1}^{p}]=[D_{1}]^{p}=[A_{1}]^{p}
, resp.
[D_{2}^{p}]=[D_{2}]^{p}=[A_{2}]^{p}
. Now, consider the following central simple
k
-algebras:

\displaystyle A:=(D_{1}^{q})^{\mathrm{op}}\otimes D_{2}^{q}

\displaystyle A^{\prime}:=(\otimes_{p\neq q}D_{1}^{p})^{\mathrm{op}}\otimes(\otimes_{p\neq q}D_{2}^{p})

\displaystyle A^{\prime\prime}:=A_{1}\,.

Note that
(\mathrm{ind}(A),\mathrm{ind}(A^{\prime}))=1
and that for these choices the relation in (
4.28
) reduces to the relation:

(4.30)

[A_{1}]+[D_{2}]=[(\otimes_{p\neq q}D_{1}^{p})\otimes D_{2}^{q}]+[D_{1}^{q}\otimes(\otimes_{p\neq q}D_{2}^{p})]\,.

Making use of the equalities
[D_{2}]=[A_{2}]
,
[(\otimes_{p\neq q}D_{1}^{p})\otimes D_{2}^{q}]=[A^{\prime}_{1}]
and
[D_{1}^{q}\otimes(\otimes_{p\neq q}D_{2}^{p})]=[A^{\prime}_{2}]
, we hence conclude that (
4.30
) agrees with the relation in (
4.29
). This finishes the proof.
∎

5.

Proposition 5.1
.

\{{}_{\gamma}{\mathcal{F}}_{j}\}_{1\leq j\leq m}
and
\{{}_{\gamma^{\prime}}{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime}_{j}\}_{1\leq j\leq m^{\prime}}
be two families of twisted projective homogeneous varieties with Jacques Tits central simple
k

\cup_{j=1}^{m}\{A_{\rho_{i_{j}}}\}_{1\leq i\leq n({\mathcal{F}}_{j})}

\cup_{j=1}^{m^{\prime}}\{A^{\prime}_{\rho^{\prime}_{i_{j}}}\}_{1\leq i\leq n({\mathcal{F}}^{\prime}_{j})}

(i)

We have an isomorphism
U(\mathrm{perf}_{\mathrm{dg}}(\Pi^{m}_{j=1}{}_{\gamma}{\mathcal{F}}_{j}))\simeq U(\mathrm{perf}_{\mathrm{dg}}(\Pi^{m^{\prime}}_{j=1}{}_{\gamma^{\prime}}{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime}_{j}))
in the category
\NChow(k)
if and only if
\mu_{\mathrm{JT}}([\Pi^{m}_{j=1}{}_{\gamma}{\mathcal{F}}_{j}])=\mu_{\mathrm{JT}}([\Pi^{m^{\prime}}_{j=1}{}_{\gamma^{\prime}}{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime}_{j}])
in
R_{\mathrm{B}}(k)
.

(ii)

If
\mu_{\mathrm{JT}}([\Pi^{m}_{j=1}{}_{\gamma}{\mathcal{F}}_{j}])=\mu_{\mathrm{JT}}([\Pi^{m^{\prime}}_{j=1}{}_{\gamma^{\prime}}{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime}_{j}])
in
R_{\mathrm{B}}(k)
, then
\langle\cup_{j=1}^{m}\{[A_{\rho_{i_{j}}}]\}_{1\leq i\leq n({\mathcal{F}}_{j})}\rangle=\langle\cup_{j=1}^{m^{\prime}}\{[A^{\prime}_{\rho^{\prime}_{i_{j}}}]\}_{1\leq i\leq n({\mathcal{F}}_{j}^{\prime})}\rangle
.

Proof.

Note first that an iterated application of Remark
3.2
leads to the following isomorphisms:

(5.2)

U(\mathrm{perf}_{\mathrm{dg}}(\Pi^{m}_{j=1}{}_{\gamma}{\mathcal{F}}_{j}))\simeq\otimes^{m}_{j=1}U(\mathrm{perf}_{\mathrm{dg}}({}_{\gamma}{\mathcal{F}}_{j}))\simeq\otimes^{m}_{j=1}\oplus_{i=1}^{n({\mathcal{F}}_{j})}U(A_{\rho_{i_{j}}})

(5.3)

U(\mathrm{perf}_{\mathrm{dg}}(\Pi^{m^{\prime}}_{j=1}{}_{\gamma^{\prime}}{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime}_{j}))\simeq\otimes^{m^{\prime}}_{j=1}U(\mathrm{perf}_{\mathrm{dg}}({}_{\gamma^{\prime}}{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime}_{j}))\simeq\otimes^{m^{\prime}}_{j=1}\oplus_{i=1}^{n({\mathcal{F}}^{\prime}_{j})}U(A^{\prime}_{\rho^{\prime}_{i_{j}}})\,.

Suppose that we have an isomorphism
U(\mathrm{perf}_{\mathrm{dg}}(\Pi^{m}_{j=1}{}_{\gamma}{\mathcal{F}}_{j}))\simeq U(\mathrm{perf}_{\mathrm{dg}}(\Pi^{m^{\prime}}_{j=1}{}_{\gamma^{\prime}}{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime}_{j}))
in the category
\NChow(k)
. Since the noncommutative Chow motives (
5.2
)-(
5.3

[C_{0}(q_{1})]=[C_{0}(q^{\prime}_{t})\otimes C_{0}(q^{\prime}_{u})]
, for integers
1\leq t\neq u\leq m-1
and when
[C_{0}^{+}(q^{\prime}_{m})]=[C_{0}^{+}(q^{\prime}_{v})]
, resp.
[C_{0}(q^{\prime}_{m})]=[C_{0}(q^{\prime}_{v})]
, for an integer
1\leq v\leq m-1
(recall that we are supposing by absurd that
[C_{0}^{+}(q_{r})]\neq[C_{0}^{+}(q^{\prime}_{s})]
for every

when

is even or
[C_{0}(q_{r})]\neq[C_{0}(q^{\prime}_{s})]
for every

when

\Sigma^{1}_{\mathrm{even}}(m,n,1)
when

\Sigma^{2}_{\mathrm{odd}}(m,n,1)

\Sigma^{1}_{\mathrm{even}}(m,n,1)=2\times(n-2)^{m-1}+2^{m}>4\times(n-2)^{m-2}+2\times(n-2)^{m-2}=\Sigma^{2}_{\mathrm{even}}(m,n,1)

\Sigma^{1}_{\mathrm{odd}}(m,n,1)=(n-2)^{m-1}+1>(n-2)^{m-2}+(n-2)^{m-2}=\Sigma^{2}_{\mathrm{odd}}(m,n,1)

lead to a contradiction with the fact that the above two sets (
11.20
) are the same up to permutation. Consequently, there exist integers

and

such that

when

is even or
[C_{0}(q_{r})]=[C_{0}(q^{\prime}_{s})]
when
n
is odd. Making use of Proposition
11.1
, we hence conclude that
Q_{q_{r}}\simeq Q_{q^{\prime}_{s}}
. This proves item (i).

We now prove item (i’). Let us suppose by absurd that
Q_{q_{r}}\not\simeq Q_{q^{\prime}_{s}}
for every
1\leq r,s\leq m
. Thanks to Proposition
11.1
, this is equivalent to the condition that
[C_{0}^{+}(q_{r})]\neq[C_{0}^{+}(q^{\prime}_{s})]
for every

[C_{0}(q_{r})]\neq[C_{0}(q^{\prime}_{s})]
for every
1\leq r,s\leq m
when
n

, we can assume, without loss of generality, that the

, resp.
\{[C_{0}^{+}(q^{\prime}_{j})]\}_{1\leq j\leq d}

\langle\{[C_{0}^{+}(q_{j})]\}_{1\leq j\leq m}\rangle
, resp.
\langle\{[C_{0}^{+}(q^{\prime}_{j})]\}_{1\leq j\leq m}\rangle
, are linearly independent when

d

\{[C_{0}(q_{j})]\}_{1\leq j\leq d}
, resp.
\{[C_{0}(q^{\prime}_{j})]\}_{1\leq j\leq d}
, of the
\mathbb{F}_{2}
-vector space
\langle\{[C_{0}(q_{j})]\}_{1\leq j\leq m}\rangle
, resp.
\langle\{[C_{0}(q^{\prime}_{j})]\}_{1\leq j\leq m}\rangle
, are linearly independent when

is odd. On the one hand, this assumption implies that the

, resp.
[C_{0}(q_{1})]

) is attained when

, resp.

. A simple computation shows that the corresponding number of copies is equal to

\Sigma^{1}_{\mathrm{odd}}(m,n,m-d)

12.
Proof of Theorem
2.23

Item (i).
Recall first that
\mathrm{dim}(\mathrm{Iv}(A,\ast))=\mathrm{deg}(A)
. Following §
2.6
, note that
\mu_{\rho}(\mathrm{Iv}(A,\ast))=\mathrm{deg}(A)
. Hence, if
[\mathrm{Iv}(A,\ast)]=[\mathrm{Iv}(A^{\prime},\ast^{\prime})]
in
K_{0}\mathrm{Var}(k)
, we conclude that
\mathrm{dim}(\mathrm{Iv}(A,\ast))=\mathrm{dim}(\mathrm{Iv}(A^{\prime},\ast^{\prime}))
. This is equivalent to the equality
\mathrm{deg}(A)=\mathrm{deg}(A^{\prime})
.

Item (ii).
If
[\mathrm{Iv}(A,\ast)]=[\mathrm{Iv}(A^{\prime},\ast^{\prime})]
in
K_{0}\mathrm{Var}(k)
, then it follows from Proposition
5.1
(ii) that

(12.1)

\langle[A],[C_{0}^{+}(A,\ast)],[C_{0}^{-}(A,\ast)]\rangle=\langle[A^{\prime}],[C_{0}^{+}(A^{\prime},\ast^{\prime})],[C_{0}^{-}(A^{\prime},\ast^{\prime})]\rangle\,.

When
\mathrm{deg}(A)\equiv 2
(mod
4
), we have the following relations in the Brauer group:

(12.2)

\displaystyle 2[C_{0}^{+}(A,\ast)]=[A]

\displaystyle 3[C_{0}^{+}(A,\ast)]=[C_{0}^{-}(A,\ast)]

\displaystyle 4[C_{0}^{+}(A,\ast)]=[k]\,.

Note that (
12.1
)-(
12.2
) imply that
[C_{0}^{+}(A,\ast)]=[C_{0}^{+}(A^{\prime},\ast^{\prime})]
and
[C_{0}^{-}(A,\ast)]=[C_{0}^{-}(A^{\prime},\ast^{\prime})]
or that
[C_{0}^{+}(A,\ast)]=[C_{0}^{-}(A^{\prime},\ast^{\prime})]
and
[C_{0}^{-}(A,\ast)]=[C_{0}^{+}(A^{\prime},\ast^{\prime})]
. Using the fact that
\mathrm{deg}(C_{0}^{+}(A,\ast))=\mathrm{deg}(C_{0}^{-}(A,\ast))=2^{\frac{\mathrm{deg}(A)}{2}-1}
and that
\mathrm{deg}(A)=\mathrm{deg}(A^{\prime})
(proved in item (i)), we hence conclude that
C_{0}^{\pm}(A,\ast)\simeq C_{0}^{\pm}(A^{\prime},\ast^{\prime})
.

When
\mathrm{deg}(A)\equiv 0
(mod
4
), we have the following relations in the Brauer group:

(12.3)

\displaystyle 2[C_{0}^{+}(A,\ast)]=[k]

\displaystyle 2[C_{0}^{-}(A,\ast)]=[k]

\displaystyle[C_{0}^{+}(A,\ast)]+[C_{0}^{-}(A,\ast)]=[A]\,.

In this case, we need to consider also the noncommutative Chow motive of the involution variety
\mathrm{Iv}(A,\ast)
. Recall from
[
26
, Example 3.11]
that we have the following computation

(12.4)

U(\mathrm{perf}_{\mathrm{dg}}(\mathrm{Iv}(A,\ast)))\simeq U(k)^{\oplus\frac{\mathrm{deg}(A)}{2}-1}\oplus U(A)^{\oplus\frac{\mathrm{deg}(A)}{2}-1}\oplus U(C^{+}_{0}(A,\ast))\oplus U(C_{0}^{-}(A,\ast))

in the category
\NChow(k)
. If
[\mathrm{Iv}(A,\ast)]=[\mathrm{Iv}(A^{\prime},\ast^{\prime})]
in the Grothendieck ring of varieties
K_{0}\mathrm{Var}(k)
, then
\mu_{\mathrm{JT}}([\mathrm{Iv}(A,\ast)])=\mu_{\mathrm{JT}}([\mathrm{Iv}(A^{\prime},\ast^{\prime})])
in
R_{\mathrm{B}}(k)
. Thanks to Proposition
5.1
(i), the latter equality holds if and only if we have an isomorphism
U(\mathrm{perf}_{\mathrm{dg}}(\mathrm{Iv}(A,\ast)))\simeq U(\mathrm{perf}_{\mathrm{dg}}(\mathrm{Iv}(A^{\prime},\ast^{\prime})))
in the category
\NChow(k)
. Note that the relations (
12.3
) imply, in particular, that
[A],[C_{0}^{+}(A,\ast)],[C_{0}^{-}(A,\ast)]\in{}_{2}\mathrm{Br}(k)
. Therefore, making use of Proposition
4.7
, we conclude that the noncommutative Chow motives
U(\mathrm{perf}_{\mathrm{dg}}(\mathrm{Iv}(A,\ast)))
and
U(\mathrm{perf}_{\mathrm{dg}}(\mathrm{Iv}(A^{\prime},\ast^{\prime})))
are isomorphic if and only if the following two sets of Brauer classes

\displaystyle\{\!\!\!\!\!\underbrace{[k]}_{\frac{\mathrm{deg}(A)}{2}-1},\underbrace{[A]}_{\frac{\mathrm{deg}(A)}{2}-1},\underbrace{[C_{0}^{+}(A,\ast)]}_{1},\underbrace{[C_{0}^{-}(A,\ast)]}_{1}\}

\displaystyle\{\!\!\!\!\!\underbrace{[k]}_{\frac{\mathrm{deg}(A^{\prime})}{2}-1},\underbrace{[A^{\prime}]}_{\frac{\mathrm{deg}(A^{\prime})}{2}-1},\underbrace{[C_{0}^{+}(A^{\prime},\ast^{\prime})]}_{1},\underbrace{[C_{0}^{-}(A^{\prime},\ast^{\prime})]}_{1}\}

are the same up to permutation, where the numbers below the parenthesis denote the number of copies. Since
\mathrm{deg}(A)\geq 6
, there are at least
2
copies of
[k]
and
[A]
. This fact, combined with the relations (
12.3
), implies that
[C_{0}^{+}(A,\ast)]=[C_{0}^{+}(A^{\prime},\ast^{\prime})]
and
[C_{0}^{-}(A,\ast)]=[C_{0}^{-}(A^{\prime},\ast^{\prime})]
or that
[C_{0}^{+}(A,\ast)]=[C_{0}^{-}(A^{\prime},\ast^{\prime})]
and
[C_{0}^{-}(A,\ast)]=[C_{0}^{+}(A^{\prime},\ast^{\prime})]
. As above, using the equalities
\mathrm{deg}(C_{0}^{+}(A,\ast))=\mathrm{deg}(C_{0}^{-}(A,\ast))=2^{\frac{\mathrm{deg}(A)}{2}-1}
and
\mathrm{deg}(A)=\mathrm{deg}(A^{\prime})
, we hence conclude that
C_{0}^{\pm}(A,\ast)\simeq C_{0}^{\pm}(A^{\prime},\ast^{\prime})
.

Item (iii).
When
\mathrm{deg}(A)=6
, the assignment
(A,\ast)\mapsto C_{0}^{+}(A,\ast)\times C_{0}^{-}(A,\ast)
gives rise to a one-to-one correspondence between isomorphism classes of central simple
k
-algebras of degree
6
with involution of orthogonal type and trivial discriminant and isomorphism classes of
k
-algebras of the form
Q\times Q^{\mathrm{op}}
, where
Q
is a quaternion algebra; consult
[
17
, Cor. 15.32]
. Note that the
k
-algebras
C_{0}^{+}(A,\ast)\times C_{0}^{-}(A,\ast)
and
C_{0}^{+}(A^{\prime},\ast^{\prime})\times C_{0}^{-}(A^{\prime},\ast^{\prime})
are isomorphic if and only if
C_{0}^{\pm}(A,\ast)\simeq C_{0}^{\pm}(A^{\prime},\ast^{\prime})
. Consequently, the proof follows from item (ii) and from the general fact that two central simple
k
-algebras with involution of orthogonal type
(A,\ast)
and
(A^{\prime},\ast^{\prime})
are isomorphic if and only if the involution varieties
\mathrm{Iv}(A,\ast)
and
\mathrm{Iv}(A^{\prime},\ast^{\prime})
are isomorphic.

Item (iv).
When
I^{3}(k)=0
, we have the following classification theorem: if
\mathrm{deg}(A)=\mathrm{deg}(A^{\prime})
and
C_{0}^{\pm}(A,\ast)\simeq C_{0}^{\pm}(A^{\prime},\ast^{\prime})
, then the central simple algebras with involution of orthogonal type
(A,\ast)
and
(A^{\prime},\ast^{\prime})
are isomorphic; consult
[
20
, Thm. A]
. Consequently, the proof follows from the combination of items (i)-(ii) with the general fact that two central simple
k
-algebras with involution of orthogonal type
(A,\ast)
and
(A^{\prime},\ast^{\prime})
are isomorphic if and only if the involution varieties
\mathrm{Iv}(A,\ast)
and
\mathrm{Iv}(A^{\prime},\ast^{\prime})
are isomorphic.

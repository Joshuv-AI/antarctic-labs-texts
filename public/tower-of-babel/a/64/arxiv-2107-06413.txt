Monotonicity and Noise-Tolerance in Case-Based Reasoning with Abstract Argumentation (with Appendix)
Guilherme Paulino-Passos, Francesca Toni \affiliations Imperial College London, Department of Computing \emails {g.passos18, f.toni}@imperial.ac.uk

Abstract
Recently, abstract argumentation-based models of case-based reasoning ( A ​ A − C ​ B ​ R {AA\mathchar 45\relax CBR} in short) have been proposed, originally inspired by the legal domain, but also applicable as classifiers in different scenarios. However, the formal properties of A ​ A − C ​ B ​ R {AA\mathchar 45\relax CBR} as a reasoning system remain largely unexplored. In this paper, we focus on analysing the non-monotonicity properties of a regular version of A ​ A − C ​ B ​ R {AA\mathchar 45\relax CBR} (that we call A ​ A − C ​ B ​ R ⪰ {AA\mathchar 45\relax CBR}_{\succeq} ). Specifically, we prove that A ​ A − C ​ B ​ R ⪰ {AA\mathchar 45\relax CBR}_{\succeq} is not cautiously monotonic, a property frequently considered desirable in the literature. We then define a variation of A ​ A − C ​ B ​ R ⪰ {AA\mathchar 45\relax CBR}_{\succeq} which is cautiously monotonic. Further, we prove that such variation is equivalent to using A ​ A − C ​ B ​ R ⪰ {AA\mathchar 45\relax CBR}_{\succeq} with a restricted casebase consisting of all “surprising” and “sufficient” cases in the original casebase. As a by-product, we prove that this variation of A ​ A − C ​ B ​ R ⪰ {AA\mathchar 45\relax CBR}_{\succeq} is cumulative, rationally monotonic, and empowers a principled treatment of noise in “incoherent” casebases. Finally, we illustrate A ​ A − C ​ B ​ R {AA\mathchar 45\relax CBR} and cautious monotonicity questions on a case study on the U.S. Trade Secrets domain, a legal casebase.

In terms of a supervised classifier, satisfying cautious monotonicity culminates in being “closed” under self-training. It is interesting that

This paper generalises our previous work

Concise dataset.

Firstly, let us present some general notions, defined in terms of the

We will define

Conclusion

We have studied regular
{AA\mathchar 45\relax CBR}_{\succeq}
frameworks, and proposed a new form of
{AA\mathchar 45\relax CBR}
, denoted
c{{AA\mathchar 45\relax CBR}_{\succeq}}
, which is cautiously monotonic and, as a by-product, cumulative and rationally monotonic.
We also show that it results in a principled way of dealing with incoherence in casebases, something which
{AA\mathchar 45\relax CBR}_{\succeq}
lacks.
Given that
{AA\mathchar 45\relax CBR}_{\succeq}
admits the original
{AA\mathchar 45\relax CBR}_{\supseteq}

[
2016a
]
as an instance, we have (implicitly) also defined a cautiously monotonic version thereof.

(Some incarnations of)
{AA\mathchar 45\relax CBR}
have been shown successful empirically in a number of settings
[
2020
]
.
The formal properties we have considered in this paper do not necessarily imply better empirical results at the tasks in which
{AA\mathchar 45\relax CBR}
has been applied. We thus leave for future work an empirical comparison between
{AA\mathchar 45\relax CBR}_{\succeq}
and
c{{AA\mathchar 45\relax CBR}_{\succeq}}
. Other issues open for future work are comparisons w.r.t. learnability (such as model performance in the presence of noise), as well as a full complexity analysis of the new model. Also,
we conjecture that the reduced size of the AF our method generates could possibly have advantages in terms of time and space complexity: we leave investigation of this issue to future work.

Before proving those theorems, an important property of the predictions of

The result is that when these nearest cases all agree on an outcome, the prediction is necessarily this outcome. It generalises

Optimal convergence rate for homogenization of convex Hamilton-Jacobi equations in the periodic spatial-temporal environment Thanks: This work is partly supported by grant U2022-01 at the VNU-HCM University of Science
Hoang Nguyen-Tien

Abstract
. We study the optimal convergence rate for homogenization problem of convex Hamilton-Jacobi equations when the Hamitonian is periodic with respect to spatial and time variables, and notably time-dependent. We prove a result similar to that of [ 8 ] , which means the optimal convergence rate is also O ⁡ ( ε ) O(\varepsilon) .

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Settings

For each
\varepsilon>0
, let
u^{\varepsilon}\in C(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times[0,\infty))
be an unique viscosity solution of the following Cauchy problem

(1.1)

\begin{cases}u_{t}^{\varepsilon}+H\left(\dfrac{x}{\varepsilon},\dfrac{t}{\varepsilon},Du^{\varepsilon}\right)&=0\quad\quad\ \ \text{in $\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(0,\infty)$},\\
u^{\varepsilon}(x,0)&=g(x)\quad\ \text{on $\mathbb{R}^{n}$}.\end{cases}

The Hamiltonian
H:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is a given continuous function satisfying:

(P1)

for each
p\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the map
(x,t)\mapsto H(x,t,p)
is
\mathbb{Z}^{n+1}-
periodic, which means

H(x,t,p)=H(x+u,t+v,p)\quad\forall(u,v)\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}\times\mathbb{Z};

(P2)

there exist some positive constants
\alpha_{0},\beta_{0},K_{0}
and
m_{0}>1
such that

\alpha_{0}|p|^{m_{0}}-K_{0}\leq H(x,t,p)\leq\beta_{0}|p|^{m_{0}}+K_{0};

(P3)

for
(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}/\mathbb{Z}^{n+1}
, the map
p\mapsto H(x,t,p)
is convex.

We assume the initial data
g\in\buc(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap\lip(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, where
\buc(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
\lip(\mathbb{R}^{n})
are respectively the set of bounded uniformly continuous, and Lipschitz functions, on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. By those assumptions,
u^{\varepsilon}
converges locally uniformly on
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times[0,\infty)
to a function
u
as
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
, and
u
solves the effective equation

(1.2)

\begin{cases}u_{t}+\overline{H}\left(Du\right)&=0\quad\quad\ \ \text{in $\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(0,\infty)$},\\
u(x,0)&=g(x)\quad\ \text{on $\mathbb{R}^{n}$}.\end{cases}

with
\overline{H}
is defined as follows. For each
p\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, there is a unique constant
\overline{H}(p)
such that

v_{s}+H(y,s,p+Dv)=\overline{H}(p)

has a continuous viscosity solution; see
[
5
,
4
,
7
]
for more details.

Condition (P2) is natural when the given Hamiltionian is time-dependent, see
[
4
]
. Also, there are some positive constants
\alpha,\beta,K
and
m>1
satisfying

\alpha|v|^{m}-K\leq L(x,t,v)\leq\beta|v|^{m}+K

Here,
L(x,t,v)
is the Legendre transform of
H(x,t,p)
defined by

L(x,t,v)=\sup_{p\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left(p\cdot v-H(x,t,p)\right)

and
\frac{1}{m}+\frac{1}{m_{0}}=1
.
The optimal control formula for solution of (
1.1
) is

u^{\varepsilon}(x,t)=\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}\varepsilon\eta(0)=x\\
\eta\in\AC([-\varepsilon^{-1}t,0],\mathbb{R}^{n})\end{subarray}}\left\{g\left(\varepsilon\eta\left(-\varepsilon^{-1}t\right)\right)+\varepsilon\int_{-\varepsilon^{-1}t}^{0}L\left(\eta(s),s,\dot{\eta}(s)ds\right)\right\}

where
\AC([-\varepsilon^{-1}t,0],\mathbb{R}^{n})
is the space of absolutely continuous curves in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
defined on
[-\varepsilon^{-1}t,0]
.

1.2.
Previous literature

We give a brief review on finding optimal convergence rate for homogenization of Hamilton-Jacobi equations.

(1)

If one consider general Hamiltonian, the best known convergence rate is
O(\varepsilon^{1/3})
by Capuzzo-Dolcetta and Ishii
[
2
]
; see a detailed explanation at
[
7
]
. It only relies on classical methods, hence one needs further assumptions on Hamiltonian, such as convexity or Lipschitz property, to improve the result.

(2)

When the Hamiltonian is convex, the lower bound
u^{\varepsilon}-u\geq C\varepsilon
always holds by a result of Mitake, Tran and Yu
[
6
]
, and a similar upper bound is also true for
n\leq 2
. Besides, if we consider multi-scaled Hamiltonian in one-dimensional settings, Tu also showed that the convergence rate is
O(\varepsilon)

[
9
]
.

(3)

When
n\geq 3
, by using idea from first passage percolation method, Cooperman pointed out an unconditional upper bound
|u^{\varepsilon}(x,t)-u(x,t)|\leq C\varepsilon\log(C+t\varepsilon^{-1})
, see
[
3
]
.

(4)

Later on, Tran and Yu established the
O(\varepsilon)
convergence rate
[
8
]
; their proof based on an unexpected lemma on periodic metrics by Burago, see
[
1
]
. It is likely that Burago’s result could be adapted for more complicated situations, such as time-dependent or multi-scaled convex Hamiltonian.

1.3.
Main results

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
u^{\varepsilon}
be the viscosity solution to (
1.1
) and
u
be the viscosity solution to (
1.2
). Then, there exists
C>0
depending only on
H,\left\lVert Dg\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}
and
n
such that, for each
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
,

\left\lVert u^{\varepsilon}-u\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(0,\infty))}\leq C\varepsilon.

Its proof generally follows that of
[
8
]
, but there are some new contributions. Since
L
depends on time variable, one cannot apply exactly the same techniques used for time-independent case, which requires boundedness of velocity of the minimizing curves. Additionally, rescaling and periodic shifting processes are far more complicated. Periodic variables could only be shifted via integer vectors, hence if those previous ideas are reused, one needs to carefully take care of subcurves decomposed from the minimizers and connectors joining them.

2.
Proof of the main result

Before starting, we restate an important lemma:

Lemma 2.1

(Burago,
[
1
]
)
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
and
\xi:[0,1]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a continuous path. Then, there is a positive integer
k\leq\frac{n+1}{2}
and a collection of disjoint intervals
\{[a_{i},b_{i}]\}_{1\leq i\leq k}
, which are subintervals of
[0,1]
, such that

\sum_{i=1}^{k}\left(\xi(b_{i})-\xi(a_{i})\right)=\dfrac{\xi(1)-\xi(0)}{2}.

Note that Burago’s lemma still holds for a path defined on
[a,b]
by a simple rescaling; this observation will be useful later. Now for
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
t>0
, denote by

m(t,x,y)=\inf\left\{\int_{0}^{t}L\left(\eta(s),s,\dot{\eta}(s)\right)ds\mid\eta\in\AC([0,t],\mathbb{R}^{n}),\eta(0)=x,\eta(t)=y\right\}.

One might imagine
m(t,x,y)
as the minimum cost to travel from
x
to
y
in a given time
t>0
. The homogenized cost is

\overline{m}(t,x,y)=\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\dfrac{1}{k}m(kt,kx,ky).

Thanks to the Hopf-Lax formula for (
1.2
), for any
t>0
,

\overline{m}(t,x,y)=t\overline{L}\left(\dfrac{y-x}{t}\right),

and also for any
s>0
,

\overline{m}(st,sx,sy)=st\overline{L}\left(\dfrac{s(y-x)}{st}\right)=s\overline{m}(t,x,y).

Lemma 2.2
.

With all above assumptions, the following properties hold:

i)

m
is
\mathbb{Z}^{n}-
periodic: for
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n},w\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
and
t>0
,

m(t,x+w,y+w)=m(t,x,y).

ii)

For
t>0
and
|y|\leq Mt
:

m(2t,0,2y)\leq 2m(t,0,y)+C.

In particular,

\overline{m}(t,0,y)\leq m(t,0,y)+C.

Here,
C>0
is a universal constant depending only on
K,M,n,\alpha
and
\beta
.

Proof.

i)

A minimizer
\eta_{1}
of
m(t,x,y)
induces a path
\eta_{2}
from
x+w
to
y+w
by shifting. From the
\mathbb{Z}^{n+1}-
periodicity of
L(y,t,q)
, we deduce:

\begin{split}m(t,x,y)&=\int_{0}^{t}L\left(\eta_{1}(s),s,\dot{\eta_{1}}(s)\right)ds=\int_{0}^{t}L\left(\eta_{1}(s)+w,s,\dot{\eta_{1}}(s)\right)ds\\
&=\int_{0}^{t}L\left(\eta_{2}(s),s,\dot{\eta_{2}}(s)\right)ds\geq m(t,x+w,y+w).\end{split}

The reverse inequality is obtained by shifting backward.

ii)

For each
t>0
and
y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
|y|\leq Mt
, consider
\eta\in\AC([0,t],\mathbb{R}^{n})
joining
0
and
y
with constant velocity. From the polynomial growth rate of
L(\ .\ ,q)
,

m(t,0,y)\leq\int_{0}^{t}\left(\beta\cdot\dfrac{|y|^{m}}{t^{m}}+K\right)dv\leq\beta\cdot\dfrac{(Mt)^{m}}{t^{m-1}}+Kt\leq Ct.

Similarly
-Ct\leq m(t,0,y)
. Hence the theorem is true if
t\leq 6
, since we will get

m(2t,0,2y)-2m(t,0,y)\leq 2Ct+2Ct\leq C.

Now only consider
t>6
. Let
k
be the greatest positive integer such that
6k\leq t
. Also, if we denote the minimizer of
m(t,0,y)
by
\eta
, then

\begin{split}\sum_{l=0}^{k-1}\int_{l}^{l+6}L\left(\eta(s),s,\dot{\eta}(s)\right)ds&=\int_{0}^{t}L\left(\eta(s),s,\dot{\eta}(s)\right)ds-\int_{6k}^{t}L\left(\eta(s),s,\dot{\eta}(s)\right)ds\\
&\leq m(t,0,y)-\int_{6k}^{t}\left(\alpha\left|\dot{\eta}(s)\right|^{m}-K\right)ds\\
&\leq Ct+(t-6k)K\leq Ct+C\leq Ct.\end{split}

Then, there exists
l\in\{0,1,\cdots,k-1\}
satisfying

\int_{l}^{l+6}L\left(\eta(s),s,\dot{\eta}(s)\right)ds\leq\dfrac{Ct}{k}\leq C.

From our assumption on
L(\ .\ ,v)
, we obtain

\int_{l}^{l+6}\left(\alpha\left|\dot{\eta}(s)\right|^{m}-K\right)ds\leq\int_{l}^{l+6}L\left(\eta(s),s,\dot{\eta}(s)\right)ds\leq C,

which can be rewritten as

\int_{l}^{l+6}\left|\dot{\eta}(s)\right|^{m}ds\leq C.

The idea here is straightforward: we “double” the trace of
m(t,0,y)
and shift it in an approriate way such that their endpoints are as close as possible. Hence one should “move faster” on some part of the initial curve such that its velocity is bounded, to save time for connectors. See the picture below.

We write down this idea rigourously. Let
w\in[0,1]^{n}
such that
y-w\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
. Construct a path
\mu\in\AC([0,2t],\mathbb{R}^{n})
with
\mu(0)=0,\mu(2t)=2y
as below:

\mu(s)=\begin{cases}\eta(s),&0\leq s<t;\\
\dfrac{-s+t}{\lceil t\rceil+2-t}w+y,&t\leq s\leq\lceil t\rceil+2;\\
\eta(s-\lceil t\rceil-2)+y-w,&\lceil t\rceil+2\leq s<\lceil t\rceil+l+2;\\
\eta\left(6\left(s-\lceil t\rceil-l-2\right)+l\right)+y-w,&\lceil t\rceil+l+2\leq s<\lceil t\rceil+l+3;\\
\eta(s-\lceil t\rceil+3)+y-w,&\lceil t\rceil+l+3\leq s<\lceil t\rceil+t-3;\\
\dfrac{s-\lceil t\rceil-t+3}{t-\lceil t\rceil+3}w+2y-w;&\lceil t\rceil+t-3\leq s\leq 2t.\end{cases}

For
1\leq i\leq 6
, denote the cost of
\mu
on
i-
th time interval by
K_{i}
. Note that

m(2t,0,2y)\leq\int_{0}^{2t}L\left(\mu(s),s,\dot{\mu}(s)\right)ds=\sum_{i=1}^{6}K_{i},

so the rest is to estimate each
K_{i}
to show that their sum is close to
2m(t,0,y)
. Indeed:

•

Estimations for
K_{1}
:

\begin{split}K_{1}&=\int_{0}^{t}L\left(\mu(s),s,\dot{\mu}(s)\right)ds=\int_{0}^{t}L\left(\eta_{1}(s),s,\dot{\eta_{1}}(s)\right)ds=m(t,0,y).\end{split}

•

Estimations for
K_{2}
:

\begin{split}K_{2}&=\int_{t}^{\lceil t\rceil+2}L\left(\mu(s),s,\dot{\mu}(s)\right)ds\leq\int_{t}^{\lceil t\rceil+2}\left(\beta\left|\dot{\mu}(s)\right|^{m}+K\right)ds\\
&\leq\int_{t}^{\lceil t\rceil+2}\left(\beta\cdot\dfrac{1}{\left(\lceil t\rceil+2-t\right)^{m}}+K\right)ds=\dfrac{\beta}{\left(\lceil t\rceil+2-t\right)^{m-1}}+K\left(\lceil t\rceil+2-t\right)\\
&\leq\dfrac{\beta}{2^{m}}+3K\leq C.\end{split}

•

Estimations for
K_{6}
:

\begin{split}K_{6}&=\int_{\lceil t\rceil+t-3}^{2t}L\left(\mu(s),s,\dot{\mu}(s)\right)ds\leq\int_{\lceil t\rceil+t-3}^{2t}\left(\beta\left|\dot{\mu}(s)\right|^{m}+K\right)ds\\
&\leq\int_{t}^{\lceil t\rceil+2}\left(\beta\cdot\dfrac{1}{\left(t-\lceil t\rceil+3\right)^{m}}+K\right)ds=\dfrac{\beta}{\left(t-\lceil t\rceil+3\right)^{m-1}}+K\left(t-\lceil t\rceil+3\right)\\
&\leq\dfrac{\beta}{2^{m}}+3K\leq C.\end{split}

•

Estimations for
K_{4}
:

\begin{split}K_{4}&=\int_{\lceil t\rceil+l+2}^{\lceil t\rceil+l+3}L\left(\mu(s),s,\dot{\mu}(s)\right)ds\\
&=\int_{\lceil t\rceil+l+2}^{\lceil t\rceil+l+3}L\bigl(\eta\left(6\left(s-\lceil t\rceil-l-2\right)+l\right)+y-w,s,\dot{\eta}\left(6\left(s-\lceil t\rceil-l-2\right)+l\right)\bigr)ds\\
&=\dfrac{1}{6}\int_{l}^{l+6}L\left(\eta(s),\dfrac{s-l}{6}+\lceil t\rceil+l+2,6\dot{\eta}(s)\right)ds\\
&\leq\dfrac{1}{6}\int_{l}^{l+6}\left(\beta\cdot\left|6\dot{\eta}(s)\right|^{m}+K\right)ds\\
&\leq\dfrac{\beta}{6^{m-1}}\int_{l}^{l+6}\left|\dot{\eta}(s)\right|^{m}ds+K\leq C.\end{split}

•

Estimation for
K_{3}
:

\begin{split}K_{3}&=\int_{\lceil t\rceil+2}^{\lceil t\rceil+l+2}L\left(\mu(s),s,\dot{\mu}(s)\right)ds\\
&=\int_{\lceil t\rceil+2}^{\lceil t\rceil+l+2}L\left(\eta(s-\lceil t\rceil-2)+y-w,s,\dot{\eta}(s-\lceil t\rceil-2)\right)ds\\
&=\int_{0}^{l}L\left(\eta(s)+y-w,s+\lceil t\rceil+2,\dot{\eta}(s)\right)ds=\int_{0}^{l}L\left(\eta(s),s,\dot{\eta}(s)\right)ds.\end{split}

•

Estimation for
K_{5}
:

\begin{split}K_{5}&=\int_{\lceil t\rceil+l+3}^{\lceil t\rceil+t-3}L\left(\mu(s),s,\dot{\mu}(s)\right)ds\\
&=\int_{\lceil t\rceil+l+3}^{\lceil t\rceil+t-3}L\left(\eta(s-\lceil t\rceil+3)+y-w,s,\dot{\eta}(s-\lceil t\rceil+3)\right)ds\\
&=\int_{l+6}^{t}L\left(\eta(s)+y-w,s+\lceil t\rceil-3,\dot{\eta}(s)\right)ds=\int_{l+6}^{t}L\left(\eta(s),s,\dot{\eta}(s)\right)ds.\end{split}

We combine all above results to yield

\begin{split}m(2t,0,2y)&\leq\sum_{i=1}^{6}K_{i}\\
&\leq m(t,0,y)+\int_{0}^{l}L\left(\eta(s),s,\dot{\eta}(s)\right)ds+\int_{l+6}^{t}L\left(\eta(s),s,\dot{\eta}(s)\right)ds+C\\
&=2m(t,0,y)+C-\int_{l}^{l+6}L\left(\eta(s),s,\dot{\eta}(s)\right)ds\\
&\leq 2m(t,0,y)+C-\int_{l}^{l+6}\left(\alpha\left|\dot{\eta}(s)\right|^{m}-K\right)ds\\
&\leq 2m(t,0,y)+C+6K\leq 2m(t,0,y)+C.\end{split}

Now we repeatly use this inequality to obtain

m(2^{k}t,0,2^{k}y)\leq 2^{k}m(t,0,y)+C(2^{k}-1),

which could be rewritten as

\dfrac{1}{2^{k}}m(2^{k}t,0,2^{k}y)\leq m(t,0,y)+C\left(1-\dfrac{1}{2^{k}}\right)

for every positive integer
k
. Now let
k
tend to infinity to finish the proof.

∎

Next, we prove the superadditive property.

Lemma 2.3
.

Given any constant
M>0
, if
|y|\leq Mt
then

2m(t,0,y)\leq m(2t,0,2y)+C.

In particular,

m(t,0,y)\leq\overline{m}(t,0,y)+C.

Here,
C>0
is a universal constant depending only on
K,M,n,\alpha
and
\beta
.

Proof.

By similar arguments in the proof of Lemma
2.2
, we get

-Ct\leq m(t,0,y),m(2t,0,2y)\leq Ct.

Hence if
t<4(n+4)^{2}
, the superadditive property is obvious since

2m(t,0,y)-m(2t,0,2y)\leq 2Ct+Ct\leq C.

Now only consider
t>4(n+4)^{2}
. Let
\eta
be a minimizer for
m(2t,0,2y)
. Note that
\eta\in\AC([0,2t],\mathbb{R}^{n})
, also
\eta(0)=0
and
\eta(2t)=2y
. By using Burago’s lemma for continuous curve
\zeta:[0,2t]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
satisfying
\zeta(s)=(\eta(s),s)
, there exist a positive integer
k\leq\frac{n+2}{2}
and a collection of disjoint subintervals
\{[a_{i},b_{i}]\}_{1\leq i\leq k}
of
[0,2t]
such that

\sum_{i=1}^{k}\left(\eta(b_{i})-\eta(a_{i})\right)=\dfrac{\eta(2y)-\eta(0)}{2}=\dfrac{2y}{2}=y,\quad\text{and }\sum_{i=1}^{k}(b_{i}-a_{i})=\dfrac{2t-0}{2}=t.

For convenience, let
d_{0}=0
. Inductively define
\{c_{i}\}
and
\{d_{i}\}
for
1\leq i\leq k
as follow:

•

for each
1\leq i\leq k
, let
c_{i}
be the smallest number such that
c_{i}-a_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}
and
c_{i}-d_{i-1}\geq 1
; note that from this definition,
1\leq c_{i}-d_{i-1}<2
;

•

after defining
c_{i}
, choose
d_{i}
satisfying
d_{i}-c_{i}=b_{i}-a_{i}
.

Those difference will be time-difference for connectors between corresponding curve segments. Moreover, their time endpoints should be far enough so that the velocity of connectors would not blow up. Now on each interval
[c_{i},d_{i}]
, we proceed periodic shifting by defining paths
\mu_{i}\in\AC([c_{i},d_{i}],\mathbb{R}^{n})
satisfying:

•

\mu_{1}(c_{i})\in[0,1]^{n}
;

•

there exists
w_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
such that

\mu_{i}(s+c_{i}-a_{i})-\eta(s)=w_{i}\quad\text{for each $s\in[a_{i},b_{i}]$},

which leads to

\sum_{i=1}^{k}(\mu_{i}(d_{i})-\mu_{i}(c_{i}))=\sum_{i=1}^{k}(\eta(b_{i})-\eta(a_{i}))=y;

•

for
1\leq i\leq k-1
,
\mu_{i}(d_{i})
and
\mu_{i+1}(c_{i+1})
land in the same unit cube, so

\left|\mu_{i}(d_{i})-\mu_{i+1}(c_{i+1})\right|\leq\sqrt{n}.

In other words, the spatial endpoints are as close as possible.

From the above definition and the periodicity of
L
, we have

\begin{split}\int_{c_{i}}^{d_{i}}L(\mu_{i}(s),s,\dot{\mu_{i}}(s))ds&=\int_{c_{i}}^{d_{i}}L(\mu_{i}(s),s+a_{i}-c_{i},\dot{\mu_{i}}(s))ds\\
&=\int_{c_{i}}^{d_{i}}L(\eta(s+a_{i}-c_{i}),s+a_{i}-c_{i},\dot{\eta}(s+a_{i}-c_{i}))ds\\
&=\int_{a_{i}}^{b_{i}}L(\eta(s),s,\dot{\eta}(s)).ds\end{split}

If we define
M
as a least integer such that

M>\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}(c_{i+1}-d_{i}),

then

M\leq\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}(c_{i+1}-d_{i})+1\leq 2k+1\leq n+3\leq 4n^{2}<t.

Now we point out an index
1\leq j\leq k
and a subinterval
[l,l+3M]
of
[c_{j},d_{j}]
satisfying

\int_{l}^{l+3M}L(\mu_{j}(s),s,\dot{\mu_{j}}(s))ds\leq C

to reuse our time-saving idea. First, there exists
j
such that
d_{j}-c_{j}>3M
and

\int_{c_{j}}^{d_{j}}L(\mu_{j}(s),s,\dot{\mu_{j}}(s))ds\leq Ct.

Indeed, the existence of
j
follows from

\begin{split}\max_{1\leq i\leq k}(d_{i}-c_{i})&\geq\dfrac{1}{k}\sum_{i=1}^{k}(d_{i}-c_{i})=\dfrac{1}{k}\sum_{i=1}^{k}(b_{i}-a_{i})=\dfrac{t}{k}\\
&\geq\dfrac{2t}{n+2}\geq\dfrac{8(n+4)^{2}}{n+2}>3(n+3)\geq 3M.\end{split}

And by defining
b_{0}=0
and
a_{k+1}=2t
, we obtain

\begin{split}&\int_{c_{j}}^{d_{j}}L(\mu_{i}(s),s,\dot{\mu_{i}}(s))ds\\
&=\int_{a_{j}}^{b_{j}}L(\eta(s),s,\dot{\eta}(s))ds\\
&=\int_{0}^{2t}L(\eta(s),s,\dot{\eta}(s))ds-\int_{0}^{a_{j}}L(\eta(s),s,\dot{\eta}(s))ds-\int_{b_{j}}^{2t}L(\eta(s),s,\dot{\eta}(s))ds\\
&\leq m(2t,0,2y)-\int_{0}^{a_{j}}(\alpha\left|\dot{\eta}(s)\right|^{m}-K)ds-\int_{b_{j}}^{2t}(\alpha\left|\dot{\eta}(s)\right|^{m}-K)ds\\
&\leq m(2t,0,2y)+K(a_{j}+2t-b_{j})\leq Ct+2Kt\leq Ct.\end{split}

Again by subdividing
[c_{i},d_{i}]
into consecutive segments with length
3M
and possibly a redundant part shorter than
3M
, then using arguments in the proof of Lemma
2.2
, there exists a subinterval
[l,l+3M]
of
[c_{j},d_{j}]
satisfying

\int_{l}^{l+3M}L(\mu_{i}(s),s,\dot{\mu_{i}}(s))ds\leq Ct\cdot\dfrac{3M}{t}\leq C.

From this we easily deduce

-C\leq\int_{l}^{l+3M}\left|\dot{\eta}(s)\right|^{m}ds\leq C.

That boundedness saves a time interval length
2M
for straight connectors. Now the essential step is to construct a path
\zeta\in\AC([0,t],\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that

\int_{0}^{t}L(\zeta(s),s,\dot{\zeta}(s))ds\leq\sum_{i=1}^{k}\int_{a_{i}}^{b_{i}}L(\eta(s),s,\dot{\eta}(s))ds+C.

We define
\zeta
as follows:

•

for
0\leq s<c_{1}
,

\zeta(s)=\dfrac{\mu_{1}(c_{1})s}{c_{1}};

•

if
1\leq i<j
, then

if

Keep in mind that
d_{0}=0
, then for
0\leq i<k
:

\begin{split}\int_{d_{i}}^{c_{i+1}}L(\zeta(s),s,\dot{\zeta}(s))ds&\leq\int_{d_{i}}^{c_{i+1}}\left(\beta\left|\dot{\zeta}(s)\right|^{m}+K\right)ds\\
&\leq\beta\int_{d_{i}}^{c_{i+1}}\left|\dot{\zeta}(s)\right|^{m}ds+K(c_{i+1}-d_{i})\\
&\leq\beta\int_{d_{i}}^{c_{i+1}}\max_{1\leq i<k}\left\{\left|\dfrac{\mu_{1}(c_{1})}{c_{1}}\right|^{m},\left|\dfrac{\mu_{i+1}(c_{i+1})-\mu_{i}(d_{i})}{c_{i+1}-d_{i}}\right|^{m}\right\}ds+2K\\
&\leq\beta(c_{i+1}-d_{i})\left(\dfrac{\sqrt{n}}{2}\right)^{m}+C\leq 2\beta\left(\dfrac{\sqrt{n}}{2}\right)^{m}+C\leq C.\end{split}

•

The cost of
\zeta
on
[d_{k}-2M,t]
is bounded by a constant. Indeed:

\begin{split}\int_{d_{k}-2M}^{t}L(\zeta(s),s,\dot{\zeta}(s))ds&\leq\int_{d_{k}-2M}^{t}\left(\beta\left|\dot{\zeta}(s)\right|^{m}+K\right)ds\\
&\leq\beta\int_{d_{k}-2M}^{t}\dfrac{\left|y-\mu_{k}(d_{k})\right|^{m}}{(t-d_{k}+2M)^{m}}ds+KM\\
&\leq\dfrac{\beta}{(t-d_{k}+2M)^{m}}\int_{t-M}^{t}{\left|\sum_{i=1}^{k}\left(\mu_{i}(d_{i})-\mu_{i}(c_{i})\right)-\mu_{k}(d_{k})\right|^{m}}ds+C\\
&\leq C\int_{t-M}^{t}\left(\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\left|\mu_{i}(d_{i})-\mu_{i+1}(c_{i+1})\right|\right)^{m}ds+C\\
&\leq C\int_{t-M}^{t}\left(k\sqrt{n}\right)^{m}ds+C=Ck^{m}\left(\sqrt{n}\right)^{m}+C\leq C.\end{split}

When
i=j

\int_{c_{i}}^{l}L(\zeta(s),s,\dot{\zeta}(s))ds=\int_{c_{i}}^{l}L(\mu_{i}(s),s,\dot{\mu_{i}}(s))ds

and also

\begin{split}\int_{l+M}^{d_{j}-2M}L(\zeta(s),s,\dot{\zeta}(s))ds&=\int_{l+M}^{d_{j}-2M}L(\mu_{i}(s+2M),s,\dot{\mu_{i}}(s+2M))ds\\
&=\int_{l+3M}^{d_{j}}L(\mu_{i}(s),s-2M,\dot{\mu_{i}}(s))ds\\
&=\int_{l+3M}^{d_{j}}L(\mu_{i}(s),s,\dot{\mu_{i}}(s))ds.\end{split}

Hence,

\begin{split}&\int_{c_{i}}^{l}L(\zeta(s),s,\dot{\zeta}(s))ds+\int_{l+M}^{d_{j}-2M}L(\zeta(s),s,\dot{\zeta}(s))ds\\
&=\int_{c_{i}}^{d_{j}}L(\mu_{i}(s),s,\dot{\mu_{i}}(s))ds-\int_{l}^{l+3M}L(\mu_{i}(s),s,\dot{\mu_{i}}(s))ds\\
&\leq\int_{c_{i}}^{d_{j}}L(\mu_{i}(s),s,\dot{\mu_{i}}(s))ds+C.\end{split}

Moreover, since velocity of
\zeta

\begin{split}\int_{l}^{l+M}L(\zeta(s),s,\dot{\zeta}(s))ds&=\int_{l}^{l+M}L(\mu_{j}(3s-2l),s,3\dot{\mu_{j}}(3s-2l))ds\\

&\leq\dfrac{1}{3}\int_{l}^{l+3M}\left(\beta\left|\dot{\zeta}(s)\right|^{m}+K\right)ds\\
&=\dfrac{\beta}{3}\int_{l}^{l+3M}\left|\dot{\zeta}(s)\right|^{m}ds+KM\leq C.\end{split}

Therefore by decomposing
\zeta

\int_{0}^{t}L(\zeta(s),s,\dot{\zeta}(s))ds\leq\sum_{i=1}^{k}\int_{a_{i}}^{b_{i}}L(\eta(s),s,\dot{\eta}(s))ds+C

as desired. From here, we conclude

(2.1)

m(t,0,y)\leq\sum_{i=1}^{k}\int_{a_{i}}^{b_{i}}L(\eta(s),s,\dot{\eta}(s))ds+C.

Replicating all previous arguments for a collection of

the below estimation also holds:

Adding (

Proof of Theorem 1.1.

Thanks to Lemma 2.2.ii) and Lemma 2.3, for
t>0
and
y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
|y|\leq Ct
,

|m(t,0,y)-\overline{m}(t,0,y)|\leq Ct.

By scaling and translation, it suffices to prove the result for
(x,t)=(0,1)
. Since
g\in\lip(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,

|g(x)|\leq|g(x)-g(0)|+|g(0)|\leq C(|x|+1)

for some constant
C
. Recall the optimal control formula:

u^{\varepsilon}(0,1)=\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}\eta(0)=0\\
\eta\in\AC([-\varepsilon^{-1},0],\mathbb{R}^{n})\end{subarray}}\left\{g\left(\varepsilon\eta\left(-\varepsilon^{-1}\right)\right)+\varepsilon\int_{-\varepsilon^{-1}}^{0}L\left(\eta(s),s,\dot{\eta}(s)\right)ds\right\}.

From the boundedness of
L
and Jensen’s inequality,

\begin{split}\varepsilon\int_{-\varepsilon^{-1}}^{0}L\left(\eta(s),s,\dot{\eta}(s)ds\right)&\geq\varepsilon\int_{-\varepsilon^{-1}}^{0}\left(\alpha\left|\dot{\mu}(s)\right|^{m}-K\right)ds\\
&\geq\alpha\left|\varepsilon\int_{-\varepsilon^{-1}}^{0}\dot{\eta}(s)ds\right|^{m}-K\\
&=\alpha\varepsilon^{m}\left|\eta(-\varepsilon^{-1})\right|^{m}-K.\end{split}

Thus, for some constant
C>0
depending only on
L,\left\lVert Dg\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}
and
n
,

\begin{split}u^{\varepsilon}(0,1)&=\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}\eta(0)=0\\
\varepsilon\left|\eta(-\varepsilon^{-1})\right|\leq C\end{subarray}}\left\{g\left(\varepsilon\eta\left(-\varepsilon^{-1}\right)\right)+\varepsilon\int_{-\varepsilon^{-1}}^{0}L\left(\eta(s),s,\dot{\eta}(s)\right)ds\right\}\\
&=\inf_{|y|\leq C}\left(g(y)+\varepsilon m(\varepsilon^{-1},-\varepsilon^{-1}y,0)\right)\\
&=\inf_{|y|\leq C}\left(g(y)+\overline{m}(1,0,-y)\right)+O(\varepsilon)\\
&=u(0,1)+O(\varepsilon).\end{split}

Hence the proof is completed.
∎

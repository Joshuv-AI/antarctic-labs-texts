Vector valued piecewise continuous almost automorphic functions and some consequences
Alan Chávez, Lenin Quiñones Huatangari

Abstract
In the present work, for 𝕏 \mathbb{X} a Banach space, the notion of piecewise continuous ℤ \mathbb{Z} -almost automorphic functions with values in finite dimensional spaces is extended to piecewise continuous ℤ \mathbb{Z} -almost automorphic functions with values in 𝕏 \mathbb{X} . Several properties of this class of functions are provided, in particular it is shown that if 𝕏 \mathbb{X} is a Banach algebra, then this class of functions constitute also a Banach algebra; furthermore, using the theory of ℤ \mathbb{Z} -almost automorphic functions, a new characterization of compact almost automorphic functions is given. As consequences, with the help of ℤ \mathbb{Z} -almost automorphic functions, it is presented a simple proof of the characterization of almost automorphic sequences by compact almost automorphic functions; the method permits us to give explicit examples of compact almost automorphic functions which are not almost periodic. Also, using the theory developed here, it is shown that almost automorphic solutions of differential equations with piecewise constant argument are in fact compact almost automorphic. Finally, it is proved that the classical solution of the 1 ​ D 1D heat equation with continuous ℤ \mathbb{Z} -almost automorphic source is also continuous ℤ \mathbb{Z} -almost automorphic; furthermore, we comment applications to the existence and uniqueness of the asymptotically continuous ℤ \mathbb{Z} -almost automorphic mild solution to abstract integro-differential equations with nonlocal initial conditions.

2
Continuous and discrete almost automorphic functions

In this section, we collect some basic facts about continuous and discrete almost automorphic functions, which will be of interest in subsequent sections.

In what follows,
\mathbb{X}
and
\mathbb{Y}
always represent (real or complex) Banach spaces, while
\mathbb{Z}
represents the group of integer numbers and
\mathbb{R}^{+}=[0,\infty)
. Also,
B(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{X})
represents the space of bounded functions from
\mathbb{R}
to
\mathbb{X}
, and
UC(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{X})
represents the space of uniformly continuous functions from
\mathbb{R}
to
\mathbb{X}
, also
\mathbb{F}\in\{\mathbb{R},\mathbb{C}\}
.

Definition 2.1
.

A continuous function
f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{X}
, is almost
automorphic if, for every sequence of real numbers
\{s^{\prime}_{n}\}
there
exists a subsequence
\left\{s_{n}\right\}\subseteq\left\{s^{\prime}_{n}\right\}
and a function

\tilde{f}
such that the following pointwise limits hold:

\lim_{n\to+\infty}f(t+s_{n})=\tilde{f}(t),\quad\lim_{n\to+\infty}\tilde{f}(t-s_{n})=f(t)\,.

(3)

If, instead of the limits in (
3
), we consider the following limits:

\lim_{n\to+\infty}\sup_{t\in K}||f(t+s_{n})-\tilde{f}(t)||=0,\quad\lim_{n\to+\infty}\sup_{t\in K}||\tilde{f}(t-s_{n})-f(t)||=0\,,

(4)

for any compact set
K\subset\mathbb{R}
, then
f
is called
compact almost automorphic
. Also, if the limit
\lim_{n\to+\infty}f(t+s_{n})=\tilde{f}(t)
in (
3
) is uniform in
\mathbb{R}
, then
f
is called almost periodic. In this work, we will refer mostly to almost automorphic functions.

We denote the classes of almost automorphic and compact almost automorphic functions by
AA(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{X})
and
\mathcal{K}AA(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{X})
respectively.

The following is a useful characterization of compact almost automorphic functions on the real line, see
[
31
]
. For the multidimensional euclidean space, see
[
15
]
.

Theorem 2.2
.

A function
f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{X}
is compact almost automorphic if and only if it is almost automorphic and uniformly continuous, thus:

\mathcal{K}AA(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{X})=AA(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{X})\cap UC(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{X})\,.

In the following theorem, we summarize some properties of almost automorphic functions. For the proofs, see, for instance
[
27
,
41
]
.

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
f,g\in AA(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{X})
, then

1.

For every
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
,
f+\alpha g\in AA(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{X})
.

2.

AA(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{X})
is a Banach space under the norm of uniform convergence and
\mathcal{K}AA(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{X})
is a closed subspace of
AA(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{X})
.

3.

f
is bounded and if
\tilde{f}
is the function in Definition
2.1
, then

||f||_{\infty}=||\tilde{f}||_{\infty}.

4.

The range of
f
; i.e.
\mathcal{R}_{f}=\{f(t)\;:\;t\in\mathbb{R}\}
, is relatively compact in
\mathbb{X}
.

We remark that,
\mathcal{K}AA(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{X})
and
AA(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{X})
are also unital Banach algebras as long as
\mathbb{X}
is, see
[
15
]
.

Example 2.4
.

The classical example of an almost automorphic function which is not almost periodic is the following one:
\psi:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
, defined by

\psi(t)=\sin\left(\frac{1}{2+\cos(t)+\cos(\sqrt{2}t)}\right)\;.

Definition 2.5
.

A function
f\in BC(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{X};\mathbb{Y})
is said to be almost automorphic (resp. compact almost automorphic) on bounded subsets of
\mathbb{X}
if, given any bounded subset
\mathcal{B}
of
\mathbb{X}
and a sequence
\{s_{n}^{\prime}\}
of real numbers, there exists a subsequence

\{s_{n}\}\subseteq\{s_{n}^{\prime}\}
and a function
\tilde{f}
, such that the following limits hold:

\lim_{n\to\infty}f(t+s_{n},x)=\tilde{f}(t,x)\;,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\tilde{f}(t-s_{n},x)=f(t,x)\;,

where the limits are pointwise in
t\in\mathbb{R}\,
(resp. uniform for
t
in compact subsets of
\mathbb{R}
) and uniformly for

in
\mathcal{B}
.

We denote this class of functions by
AA(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{X},\mathbb{Y})
(resp. by
\mathcal{K}AA(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{X},\mathbb{Y})
for the compact almost automorphic functions).

Now, Let us define the following function spaces

C_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{+};\mathbb{X})=\Big\{\phi\in C(\mathbb{R}^{+};\mathbb{X}):\lim_{t\to+\infty}||\phi(t)||=0\Big\}\;,

C_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{X};\mathbb{Y})=\Big\{\phi\in C(\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{X};\mathbb{Y}):\lim_{t\to+\infty}\sup_{x\in\mathcal{B}}||\phi(t,x)||=0,\,\mathcal{B}\,{\rm a\,bounded\ subset\ of}\ \mathbb{X}\Big\}\;.

Definition 2.6
.

A continuous function
f:\mathbb{R}^{+}\to\mathbb{X}
(respectively
f:\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{X}\to\mathbb{Y}
) is asymptotically almost
automorphic (respectively asymptotically almost automorphic in
t\in\mathbb{R}
, uniformly on bounded subsets of
\mathbb{X}
) if,

f=g+h
, where
g\in AA(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{X})
(respectively
g\in AA(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{X};\mathbb{Y})
) and
h\in C_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{+};\mathbb{X})

(respectively
h\in C_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{X};\mathbb{Y})
).

In definition
2.6
, if
g\in\mathcal{K}AA(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{X})
(resp.
g\in\mathcal{K}AA(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{X};\mathbb{Y})
), then
f
is called asymptotically compact almost automorphic (resp. asymptotically compact almost automorphic in
t\in\mathbb{R}
, uniformly on bounded subsets of
\mathbb{X}
).

We denote by
AAA(\mathbb{R}^{+};\mathbb{X})
the space of asymptotically almost automorphic functions and by
AAA(\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{X};\mathbb{Y})
the space of functions which are asymptotically almost automorphic in
t\in\mathbb{R}
uniformly on bounded subsets of
\mathbb{X}
. Also, we denote by
\mathcal{K}AAA(\mathbb{R}^{+};\mathbb{X})
the space of asymptotically compact almost automorphic functions. Obviously, we have
\mathcal{K}AAA(\mathbb{R}^{+};\mathbb{X})\subset AAA(\mathbb{R}^{+};\mathbb{X})
.

In the space
AAA(\mathbb{R}^{+};\mathbb{X})
, we can define the following norm

||f||:=\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}||g(t)||+\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}^{+}}||h(t)||\,,\,\,f=g+h,\,\,g\in AA(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{X}),\,\,h\in C_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{+};\mathbb{X})\,.

(5)

The following theorem summarizes some properties of asymptotically almost automorphic functions (c.f.
[
41
]
)

Theorem 2.7
.

We have

1.

The space
AAA(\mathbb{R}^{+};\mathbb{X})
becomes a Banach space under the norm
(\ref{NormAAA})
.

2.

\{g(t):t\in\mathbb{R}\}\subset\overline{\{f(t):t\in\mathbb{R}\}}
, where
f=g+h
, with
g\in AA(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{X})
and
h\in C_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{+};\mathbb{X})
.

3.

AAA(\mathbb{R}^{+};\mathbb{X})=AA(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{X})\oplus C_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{+};\mathbb{X})

4.

Let

Definition 2.8
.

A function
f:\mathbb{Z}\to\mathbb{X}
is called discrete almost automorphic if, for any sequence
\{s_{n}^{\prime}\}\subset\mathbb{Z}
, there exists a subsequence
\{s_{n}\}\subset\{s_{n}^{\prime}\}
such that the following pointwise limits hold:

\lim_{n\to+\infty}f(k+s_{n})=:\tilde{f}(k),\;\;\lim_{n\to+\infty}\tilde{f}(k-s_{n})=f(k),\;k\in\mathbb{Z}.

We denote this space by
\mathbb{AA}(\mathbb{Z},\mathbb{X})
. A discrete almost automorphic function is also called almost automorphic sequence. We have the following result, see
[
4
]

Theorem 2.9
.

Let
f\in\mathbb{AA}(\mathbb{Z},\mathbb{X})
, then there exists
M>0
such that
\sup_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}||f(n)||=M
. Moreover,
\mathbb{AA}(\mathbb{Z},\mathbb{X})
is a Banach space under the supremum norm
||\cdot||_{\infty}=\sup_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}||\cdot||
.

3
\mathbb{Z}
-almost automorphic type functions

In this section, we introduce and develop the theory of discontinuous almost automorphic functions from
\mathbb{R}
to the Banach space
\mathbb{X}
, which we call
\mathbb{Z}
-almost automorphic functions and also present some of its basic properties.

3.1
\mathbb{Z}
-almost automorphic functions

Definition 3.10
.

The set
PC_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{X})
consists of functions defined on all
\mathbb{R}
and which satisfy the following:

1.

f
is continuous in the intervals
[n,n+1),\,n\in\mathbb{Z}
.

2.

For each
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, the lateral limit
\lim_{t\to n^{-}}f(t)
exists, and we define

f(n^{-}):=\lim_{t\to n^{-}}f(t)\,.

Note that, every continuous function belongs to
PC_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{X})
, therefore
PC_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{X})\not=\emptyset
.

Theorem 3.11
.

The space
PC_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{X})\cap B(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{X})
is complete under the supremum norm.

Proof.

Let
\{f_{k}\}
be a Cauchy sequence in
PC_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{X})\cap B(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{X})
. Then, for each
t\in\mathbb{R}
,
\{f_{k}(t)\}
is a Cauchy sequence in
\mathbb{X}
, therefore there exists a function
f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{X}
such that
f_{k}(t)\to f(t)
, as
n\to\infty
. Now, pick the compact interval
[n,\theta_{n}]
for
n\leq\theta_{n}<n+1
and also take
t_{m},t_{0}\in[n,\theta_{n}]
such that
t_{m}\to t_{0}
as
m\to\infty
; then, because of uniform convergence and continuity of
f_{k}
in
[n,n+1)
, for each
k
; we have

\displaystyle||f(t_{m})-f(t_{0})||

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle||f_{k}(t_{m})-f(t_{m})||+||f_{k}(t_{m})-f_{k}(t_{0})||+

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle||f_{k}(t_{0})-f(t_{0})||

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[n,\theta_{n}]}||f(t)-f_{k}(t)||+||f_{k}(t_{m})-f_{k}(t_{0})||

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[n,\theta_{n}]}||f_{k}(t)-f(t)||

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle\epsilon\,,

which implies that
f(t_{m})\to f(t_{0})
, as
m\to\infty
. Therefore,
f
is continuous in each interval
[n,n+1),\,n\in\mathbb{Z}
. Now, we need to prove that
\lim_{\zeta\to n^{-}}f(\zeta)
exists, i.e.
||\lim_{\zeta\to n^{-}}f(\zeta)||<\infty
. In fact, by uniform convergence and since
\{f_{k}\}\subset PC_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{X})
, we obtain, for
k_{0}
large enough

\displaystyle||\lim_{\zeta\to n^{-}}f(\zeta)||

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\lim_{\zeta\to n^{-}}||f(\zeta)-f_{k_{0}}(\zeta)||

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle||f_{k_{0}}(n^{-})||

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle\infty\,.

It follows that,

f(n^{-}):=\lim_{\zeta\to n^{-}}f(\zeta)\,,

is well defined. Therefore, the limit function
f
belongs to
PC_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{X})
and also is bounded.

∎

In the following definition, we introduce the class of piecewise continuous almost automorphic functions

Definition 3.12
.

A function
f\in PC_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{X})
is called
\mathbb{Z}
-almost automorphic if, for any sequence of integers
\{s_{n}^{\prime}\}\subset\mathbb{Z}
, there exists a subsequence
\{s_{n}\}\subset\{s_{n}^{\prime}\}
and a function
\tilde{f}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{X}
such that the following pointwise limits hold

\lim_{n\to+\infty}f(t+s_{n})=\tilde{f}(t),\,\,\,\lim_{n\to+\infty}\tilde{f}(t-s_{n})=f(t),\,\,\,t\in\mathbb{R}.

(6)

Let us denote this class of functions by
\mathbb{Z}AA(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{X})
.

Example 3.13
.

If
f\in AA(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{X})
, then
f([\cdot])\in\mathbb{Z}AA(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{X})
.

In particular, for
\mathbb{X}=\mathbb{R}
let
\psi:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
be the almost automorphic function defined by
\psi(t)=\sin(\dfrac{1}{2+\cos(t)+\cos(t\sqrt{2})})
, then the discontinuous function
g:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
defined by

g(t)=\psi([t])=\sin(\dfrac{1}{2+\cos([t])+\cos([t]\sqrt{2})})

belongs to
\mathbb{Z}AA(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{R})
.

Lemma 3.14
.

AA(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{X})\subsetneq\mathbb{Z}AA(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{X})
.

Proof.

It is easy to see that, every almost automorphic functions belongs to
\mathbb{Z}AA(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{X})
. Now, taking
x_{0}\in\mathbb{X}\setminus\{0\}
and by the previous example, we conclude that the discontinuous function

g(t)=f([t])=\sin(\dfrac{1}{2+\cos([t])+\cos([t]\sqrt{2})})x_{0}\,,

belongs to
\mathbb{Z}AA(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{X})
, but not to
AA(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{X})
.
∎

The following function is important in the proof of some results

Definition 3.15
.

Let
f\in\mathbb{Z}AA(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{X})
. We define the function
F_{f}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{X}
in the following way: If
n\leq t\leq n+1
with

, then

F_{f}(t)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcc}f(t)&if&n\leq t<n+1\,,\\
\\
f((n+1)^{-})&if&t=n+1\,.\end{array}\right.

(7)

Note that
F_{f}
is uniformly continuous in each interval of the form
[n,n+1]
, for

Observe also that, if
f\in\mathbb{Z}AA(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{X})

\mathbb{Z}
is an almost automorphic sequence; therefore, using Theorem

Lemma 3.16

Let

\sup_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}||f(n)||<\infty\,.

In the following Lemma, we prove that the jump of discontinuities of a function in
\mathbb{Z}AA(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{X})

Lemma 3.17
.

Let
f\in\mathbb{Z}AA(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{X})
, then

\sup_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}||f(n)-f(n^{-})||<\infty\,.

Proof.

Firstly, by Lemma
3.16
and since
f(n^{-})
exists for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}

\sup_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}||f(n)-f(n^{-})||<\infty\,,

is equivalent to

\sup_{n\in\mathbb{Z}:|n|\geq K}||f(n^{-})||<\infty\,,

for an arbitrary large real number
K
, which is equivalent to

\sup_{n\in\mathbb{Z}:n\geq K}||f(n^{-})||<\infty\,\,\,\wedge\ \,\sup_{n\in\mathbb{Z}:n\leq-K}||f(n^{-})||<\infty\,,

for an arbitrary large real number
K
.

Let us suppose that

\sup_{n\in\mathbb{Z}:n\geq K}||f(n^{-})||=+\infty;

that is, there exists a sequence (strictly increasing) of integer numbers
\{\eta_{n}^{\prime}\}\subset\mathbb{Z}
, such that

\lim_{n\to+\infty}||f((\eta_{n}^{\prime})^{-})||=+\infty\,.

Since
f\in\mathbb{Z}AA(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{X})
, there exists a subsequence
\{\eta_{n}\}\subset\{\eta_{n}^{\prime}\}
and a function
\tilde{f}
such that (
6
) holds, and also

\lim_{n\to+\infty}||f((\eta_{n})^{-})||=+\infty\,.

(8)

Now, let
\epsilon>0
be arbitrarily small, then

1.

By definition of
f((\eta_{n})^{-})
, there exists a sequence
\{\delta_{n}\}\subset(0,1)
such that if
0<\eta_{n}-\tau<\delta_{n}
, then
||f(\tau)-f(\eta_{n}^{-})||<\epsilon
. Moreover, the sequence
\{\delta_{n}\}
may be chosen in such a way that
\delta_{n}\to 0
if
n\to\infty
. That is, there exists
N_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that

||f(\eta_{n}^{-})-f(\eta_{n}-\dfrac{\delta_{n}}{2})||<\dfrac{\epsilon}{3},\,\,\forall n\geq N_{0}\,.

Also, there exists
N_{1}\in\mathbb{N}
such that,

|\delta_{n}-\delta_{m}|<\dfrac{\epsilon}{3},\,\,\forall n,m\geq N_{1}\,.

2.

There exists
N_{2}\in\mathbb{N}
such that, for

(fixed) we have

||f(\eta_{n}-\dfrac{\delta_{m}}{2})-\tilde{f}(-\dfrac{\delta_{m}}{2})||<\dfrac{\epsilon}{3},\,\,\forall n\geq N_{2}\,.

3.

Note also that, (
8
) is equivalent to: given

, there exist

and

3.4
\mathbb{Z}
-almost automorphic type functions and convolution products

In this subsection, we prove invariance of the introduced discontinuous
\mathbb{Z}
-almost automorphic type functions under the action of convolution products. We start with the following Proposition

Proposition 3.39
.

Let
\Phi\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{R})
, then the operator
L
defined by

(Lf)(t)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\Phi(t-s)f(s)ds=\Phi*f(t)\,,

leaves invariant the space
\mathbb{Z}AA(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{X})
.

Proof.

Let
f\in\mathbb{Z}AA(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{X})
.

1.

First, let us prove that
\Phi*f
belongs to the space
PC_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{X})
. Note that, for
t_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
we have

\displaystyle\Phi*f(t)-\Phi*f(t_{0})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Phi(\eta)\big(f(t-\eta)-f(t_{0}-\eta)\big)d\eta

(16)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{k}^{k+1}\Phi(\eta)\big(f(t-\eta)-f(t_{0}-\eta)\big)d\eta\,.

a)
For
t_{0}=n\in\mathbb{Z}
and a sequence
\{t_{m}\}\subset[n,n+1)
such that
t_{m}\to n^{+}
when
m\to\infty
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{k}^{k+1}\Phi(\zeta)\big(f(t_{m}-\zeta)-f(n-\zeta)\big)d\zeta

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{n-k-1}^{n-k}\Phi(n-\zeta)\big(f(t_{m}-n+\zeta)-f(\zeta)\big)d\eta\,.

Then, since we have the pointwise limit
\lim_{m\to\infty}\big(f(t_{m}-n+\zeta)-f(\zeta)\big)=0
in
[n-k-1,n-k)
,
f
is bounded and
\Phi\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R})
, we conclude

\lim_{m\to\infty}\int_{n-k-1}^{n-k}\Phi(n-\zeta)\big(f(t_{m}-n+\zeta)-f(\zeta)\big)d\eta=0\,.

b)
For
t_{0}\in(n,n+1),\,n\in\mathbb{Z}
. Pick a sequence
\{t_{m}\}\subset(n,n+1)
such that
t_{m}\to t_{0}
when
m\to\infty
. Also, for
\delta>0
arbitrarily small, denote by
I_{\delta}(n,k)
the open interval
I_{\delta}(n,k):=(n-k-\delta,n-k+\delta)
and by
\bigtriangleup f(t_{m},t_{0},\zeta)
the difference
\bigtriangleup f(t_{m},t_{0},\zeta):=f(t_{m}-t_{0}+\zeta)-f(\zeta)
, then

\displaystyle\int_{k}^{k+1}\Phi(\zeta)\big(f(t_{m}-\zeta)-f(t_{0}-\zeta)\big)d\zeta

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{t_{0}-k-1}^{t_{0}-k}\Phi(t_{0}-\zeta)\bigtriangleup f(t_{m},t_{0},\zeta)d\zeta

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{t_{0}-k-1}^{n-k-\delta}\Phi(t_{0}-\zeta)\bigtriangleup f(t_{m},t_{0},\zeta)d\zeta+

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\int_{n-k+\delta}^{t_{0}+k}\Phi(t_{0}-\zeta)\bigtriangleup f(t_{m},t_{0},\zeta)d\zeta+

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\int_{I_{\delta}(n,k)}\Phi(t_{0}-\zeta)\bigtriangleup f(t_{m},t_{0},\zeta)d\zeta\,.

From this decomposition, we have (analogously to the step
a)
)

\lim_{m\to\infty}\int_{t_{0}-k-1}^{n-k-\delta}\Phi(t_{0}-\zeta)\bigtriangleup f(t_{m},t_{0},\zeta)d\zeta=0\,,

\lim_{m\to\infty}\int_{n-k+\delta}^{t_{0}+k}\Phi(t_{0}-\zeta)\bigtriangleup f(t_{m},t_{0},\zeta)d\zeta=0\,,

and, since

\displaystyle\big|\int_{I_{\delta}(n,k)}\Phi(t_{0}-\zeta)\bigtriangleup f(t_{m},t_{0},\zeta)d\zeta\big|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2||f||_{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\Phi(t_{0}-\zeta)|\mathds{1}_{I_{\delta}(m,k)}(\zeta)d\zeta\,;

where,
\mathds{1}_{I_{\delta}(m,k)}(\cdot)
is the indicator function of
I_{\delta}(m,k)
, then

\lim_{\delta\to 0}\int_{I_{\delta}(n,k)}\Phi(t_{0}-\zeta)\bigtriangleup f(t_{m},t_{0},\zeta)d\zeta=0\,.

Therefore, from
a)
,
b)
and (
16
) we conclude that the convolution
\Phi*f
is continuous in each interval
[n,n+1),\,\,n\in\mathbb{Z}
. Also, since
\Phi*f
is bounded, the limit
\lim_{\eta\to n^{-}}\Phi*f(\eta)
exists, therefore

\Phi*f(n^{-}):=\lim_{\eta\to n^{-}}\Phi*f(\eta)\,,

is well defined. Thus,
\Phi*f\in PC_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{X})
.

2.

Let
\{s_{n}^{\prime}\}
be a sequence in
\mathbb{Z}
; then, there exists a subsequence
\{s_{n}\}\subset\{s_{n}^{\prime}\}
and a function
\tilde{f}
such that the following pointwise limits hold

\lim_{n\to\infty}f(t+\eta_{n})=\tilde{f}(t)\,,\,\,\,\lim_{n\to\infty}\tilde{f}(t-\eta_{n})=f(t)\,.

Now, defining the following function

g(t):=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\Phi(t-s)\tilde{f}(s)ds\,,

we have

Lf(t+s_{n})-g(t)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\Phi(\zeta)\Big(f(t-\zeta+s_{n})-\tilde{f}(t-\zeta)\Big)d\zeta\,.

Therefore, using the Lebesgue’s Dominated Convergence Theorem, we conclude the following pointwise limit

\lim_{n\to\infty}Lf(t+s_{n})=g(t)\,.

Analogously, we obtain

\lim_{n\to\infty}g(t-s_{n})=Lf(t)\,.

∎

The proofs of the following propositions are omitted, because they are deduced from the previous one and also because they are very similar to the almost automorphic situation; see for instance

4
Some consequences

4.1
Extension of almost automorphic sequences to compact almost automorphic functions: A simple proof

A classical problem within the theory of almost automorphic functions is the following: given an almost automorphic sequence
S:\mathbb{Z}\to\mathbb{X}
, is there an almost automorphic function
f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{X}
such that
f|_{\mathbb{Z}}=S
?. This problem is known as the extension problem of almost automorphic sequences to almost automorphic functions defined in
\mathbb{R}
and was answered positively by Cuevas et. al.
[
22
]
, Fink
[
36
]
and R. Yuan
[
50
]
.

In this subsection, using the theory of
\mathbb{Z}
-almost automorphic functions we give a very simple proof of the extension Theorem for almost automorphic sequences. The Theorem can be viewed as a characterization result of almost automorphic sequences.

Theorem 4.43
.

Let
S:\mathbb{Z}\to\mathbb{X}
be an almost automorphic sequence, then there exists a compact almost automorphic function
f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{X}
such that
f|_{\mathbb{Z}}=S
.

Proof.

Let us define the following function

f(t):=S(k)+(t-k)(S(k+1)-S(k)),\,\,\,k\leq t<k+1\,.

(17)

We claim that
f\in\mathbb{Z}AA(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{X})
. Indeed, let
\{s_{n}\}
be a sequence of integer numbers. Then, there exists a subsequence
\{\eta_{n}\}
of
\{s_{n}\}
and a discrete function
\tilde{S}
such that the following pointwise limits are satisfied

\lim_{n\to\infty}S(k+\eta_{n})=\tilde{S}(k)\,,

(18)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\tilde{S}(k-\eta_{n})=S(k)\,.

(19)

Now, if we define the function
\tilde{f}(t)=\tilde{S}(k)+(t-k)(\tilde{S}(k+1)-\tilde{S}(k))
for
k\leq t<k+1
, then we must show that
f(t+\eta_{n})\to\tilde{f}(t)
and that
\tilde{f}(t-\eta_{n})\to f(t)
.
First, let us observe that if
k\leq t<k+1
, then
k+\eta_{n}\leq t+\eta_{n}<k+1+\eta_{n}
, then

f(t+\eta_{n})=S(k+\eta_{n})+(t+\eta_{n}-(k+\eta_{n}))(S(k+1+\eta_{n})-S(k+\eta_{n}))

=S(k+\eta_{n})+(t-k)(S(k+1+\eta_{n})-S(k+\eta_{n}))\,,

from this equality and by the limit in (
18
) we have

\lim_{n\to\infty}f(t+\eta_{n})=\tilde{f}(t)\,.

Analogously, if
k\leq t<k+1
, then
k-\eta_{n}\leq t-\eta_{n}<k+1-\eta_{n}
; then

\tilde{f}(t-\eta_{n})=S(k-\eta_{n})+(t-\eta_{n}-(k-\eta_{n}))(S(k+1-\eta_{n})-S(k-\eta_{n}))

=S(k-\eta_{n})+(t-k)(S(k+1-\eta_{n})-S(k-\eta_{n}))\,.

Therefore, using the limit in (
19
) we obtain

\lim_{n\to\infty}\tilde{f}(t-\eta_{n})=f(t)\,.

Now, let us prove that
f
is uniformly continuous. Since
S
is bounded, let
M>\sup_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}||S(n)||
with
M>1
; then, given
\epsilon>0
there exists
\delta<\dfrac{\epsilon}{4M}
such that if
|t_{1}-t_{2}|<\delta
, then,

1.

If
t_{1},t_{2}\in[k,k+1[
, we have

\displaystyle||f(t_{1})-f(t_{2})||

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle||(t_{1}-t_{2})(S(k+1)-S(k))||

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|t_{1}-t_{2}|2M

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle\dfrac{\epsilon}{2M}<\epsilon\,.

2.

If
k<t_{1}<k+1<t_{2}<k+2
, then
|t_{2}-t_{1}|<\delta
implies
|t_{2}-(k+1)|<\delta
; and, since

f(t_{1})-f(t_{2})=(t_{1}-t_{2})(S(k+1)-S(k))-(t_{2}-(k+1))(S(k+2)-S(k))\,,

we have

\displaystyle||f(t_{1})-f(t_{2})||

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|t_{1}-t_{2}|||(S(k+1)-S(k))||

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle|t_{2}-(k+1)||S(k+2)-S(k)||

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle\delta 4M<\epsilon\,.

∎

The reader may compare the proof of this Theorem with those given for
[
22
, Theorem 2.1]
,
[
36
, Theorem 1]
and
[
50
, Theorem 2.8]
.

In addition to the previous Theorem of continuous extension, we can also provide the following discontinuous extension Theorem:

Theorem 4.44
.

Let
S:\mathbb{Z}\to\mathbb{X}
be an almost automorphic sequence, then there exists a function
f\in\mathbb{Z}AA(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{X})
such that
f|_{\mathbb{Z}}=S
.

Proof.

We define the function
f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{X}
through
f(t)=S([t])
.
∎

Compact almost automorphic solutions of DEPCA

For
p\in\{1,2,\cdots\}
, let us consider the following DEPCAs on

Equations (

Proposition 4.48

Let

Proof.

Let
y

\;k\in\mathbb{Z}

\displaystyle|y(t_{1})-y(t_{2})|

\displaystyle=|y^{\prime}(\xi)(t_{1}-t_{2})|<\varepsilon.

(23)

On the other hand, if

\displaystyle|y(t_{1})-y(t_{2})|

\displaystyle\leq\displaystyle\lim_{\eta\to k^{-}}|y(t_{1})-y({\eta})|+\displaystyle\lim_{\varphi\to k^{+}}|y({\varphi})-y(t_{2})|

\displaystyle\leq\displaystyle\lim_{\eta\to k^{-}}|\displaystyle\int_{t_{1}}^{\eta}y^{\prime}(t)dt|+\displaystyle\lim_{\varphi\to k^{+}}|\int_{\varphi}^{t_{2}}y^{\prime}(t)dt|

\displaystyle\leq\displaystyle\lim_{\eta\to k^{-}}\displaystyle\int_{t_{1}}^{\eta}|y^{\prime}(t)dt|+\displaystyle\lim_{\varphi\to k^{+}}\int_{\varphi}^{t_{2}}|y^{\prime}(t)dt|

\displaystyle\leq M(k-t_{1})+M(t_{2}-k)<\varepsilon\,.

(24)

Let
\gamma
be small enough. Then, the piecewise constant delayed Lasota–Wazewska model:

y^{\prime}(t)=-\delta(t)y(t)+p(t)e^{-\gamma y([t])}\,,

(30)

has a unique compact almost automorphic solution.

4.4
Continuous
\mathbb{Z}
-almost automorphic solution of
1D
heat equation

In this subsection, we present a regularity result for the
1D
heat equation in the space
\mathbb{Z}AA(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{R})\cap C(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{R})
. Note that,
\mathbb{Z}AA(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{R})\cap C(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{R})
is a Banach space under the supremum norm. Let us consider the
1D
heat equation

\displaystyle u_{t}-\bigtriangleup u

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0\,\,\,\,{\rm in}\,\,\,\,(0,+\infty)\times\mathbb{R}

(31)

\displaystyle u(x,0^{+})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle u_{0}(x)\,\,\,\,{\rm in}\,\,\,\,\mathbb{R}\,.

Theorem 4.53
.

If
u_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}AA(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{R})\cap C(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{R})
; then, the solution
u
of the heat equation (
31
) satisfies

u(t,\cdot)\in\mathbb{Z}AA(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{R})\cap C(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{R}),\,\,\forall t>0
.

Proof.

It is well known that, the solution
u
of the heat equation (
31
) has the following integral representation

u(t,x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Phi(t,x-y)u_{0}(y)dy\,,

(32)

where
\Phi(t,x)
is the fundamental solution with

\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Phi(t,\xi)d\xi=1,\,\,\forall t>0\,.

(33)

Now, using Proposition
3.39
we conclude that
u(t,\cdot)\in\mathbb{Z}AA(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{R})\cap C(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{R}),\,\,\forall t>0
.
∎

Finally, we would like to comment that, as in
[
16
,
30
]
, it is possible to study existence and uniqueness of the mild solution in the space
\mathbb{Z}AAA(\mathbb{R}^{+};\mathbb{X})\cap C(\mathbb{R}^{+};\mathbb{X})
of the following integro-differential equation with nonlocal initial condition:

\displaystyle u^{\prime}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Au(t)+\int_{0}^{t}B(t-s)u(s)ds+f(t,u(t)),\ t\in\,\,\mathbb{R}^{+},

(34)

\displaystyle u(0)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle u_{0}+h(u),

(35)

where
u_{0}\in\mathbb{X}
,
A
and
B(t)_{t\geq 0}
are dense and closed linear operators in
\mathbb{X}
;
A,B(\cdot),f,h
satisfy some concrete conditions outlined, for instance, in
[
16
,
30
]
. Of course, applications to the heat conduction in materials with memory can be improved.

4.5
Open problem

The Banach space
\mathbb{Z}AA(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{R})\cap C(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{R})
was introduced in the previous subsection. Obviously, the inclusion

AA(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{R})\subseteq\mathbb{Z}AA(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{R})\cap C(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{R})\,,

holds.

The current problem is to identify a continuous
\mathbb{Z}
-almost automorphic function that does not fall under the category of classical almost automorphic functions. Therefore, the question arises: is the following strict inclusion

AA(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{R})\varsubsetneq\mathbb{Z}AA(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{R})\cap C(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{R})\,,

valid?.

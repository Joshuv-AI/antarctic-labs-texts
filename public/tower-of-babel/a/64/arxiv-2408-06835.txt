Consider an arbitrary set
X

\sigma

\mathcal{A}
on
X

consists of
\mu

\int_{X}\lvert h\rvert^{p}d\mu<\infty.

Denote by
\|h\|_{p}
the
L^{p}(\mu)
-norm of
h,
where
h\in L^{p}(\mu).
The
expression of
\|h\|_{p}

\|h\|_{p}=\left(\int_{X}\lvert h\rvert^{p}d\mu\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

In this case, the functional
\|\cdot\|_{p}:L^{p}(\mu)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
acts as a semi-norm. Since the functions in
L^{p}(\mu)
that are equal a.e. with respect to
\mu
are considered identical, which converts

\|\cdot\|_{p}
into a norm, thus making
L^{p}(\mu)
a normed linear space.

In this paper, we investigate two measurable spaces:

as

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}hd\lambda

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}hd\mu_{n}
by
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}h(x)|x|^{2}dx
. For convenience, we denote either
\lambda
-measurable functions or
\mu_{n}

D\in\mathscr{B}

\mu_{n}
and
\lambda
as a.e.. The

Lemma 5
.

Suppose that
n\geq 2
and
\xi\in\widetilde{A}(\mathbb{R})
, then the operator
\Psi:L^{p}(\mu_{n})\rightarrow\langle\mathbb{M}^{n},+\rangle

\Psi(h)=\mathrm{K}(\xi\circ h),

is a continuous
\mathrm{SL}(n)
covariant valuation.

Proof.
Because that
|\xi(t)|\leq d|t|^{p}
for any

, thereby

\displaystyle|\mathrm{K}(\xi\circ h)|

\displaystyle=|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}xx^{t}|\xi(h(x))|dx|

\displaystyle\leq d\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|h(x)|^{p}|x|^{2}dx.

In addition, since
h\in L^{p}(\mu_{n})
, it can be concluded that
\mathrm{K}(\xi\circ h)<\infty
.

Now, we intend to demonstrate that
\Psi
is a valuation in
L^{p}(\mu_{n})
. Let
h,f\in L^{p}(\mu_{n})
, then

\displaystyle\Psi(h\vee f)+\Psi(h\wedge f)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}xx^{t}|\xi\circ(h\vee f)|dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}xx^{t}|\xi\circ(h\wedge f)|dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\{h\geq f\}}xx^{t}|\xi\circ h|dx+\int_{\{h<f\}}xx^{t}|\xi\circ f|dx

\displaystyle+\int_{\{h\geq f\}}xx^{t}|\xi\circ f|dx+\int_{\{h<f\}}xx^{t}|\xi\circ h|dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}xx^{t}|\xi\circ h|dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}xx^{t}|\xi\circ f|dx

\displaystyle=\Psi(h)+\Psi(f).

Hence
\Psi
is a valuation on
L^{p}(\mu_{n})
.

Next we prove that
\Psi
is continuous. Suppose that
h_{k}\rightarrow h
as
k\rightarrow\infty
in
L^{p}(\mu_{n})
, then we have

\displaystyle|\Psi(h_{k})-\Psi(h)|

\displaystyle=|\mathrm{K}(\xi\circ h_{k})-\mathrm{K}(\xi\circ h)|

\displaystyle=|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}xx^{t}|\xi\circ(h_{k}(x))|dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}xx^{t}|\xi\circ(h(x))|dx|

\displaystyle\leq d\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\lvert h_{k}(x)|^{p}-|h(x)|^{p}\rvert\lvert x\rvert^{2}dx.

By Lemma
1
we obtain
\Psi(h_{k})\rightarrow\Psi(h)
as
h_{k}\rightarrow h
in
L^{p}(\mu_{n})
. Therefore

is continuous in
L^{p}(\mu_{n})
.

To demonstrate that
\Psi
is
\mathrm{SL}(n)
covariant, observe that due to the

for all
\phi\in\mathrm{SL}(n)
. Thus,

is
\mathrm{SL}(n)
covariant.

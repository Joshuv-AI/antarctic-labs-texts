Quantum Representation Theory and Manin matrices II: super case
Alexey Silantyev

Abstract
We construct super-version of Quantum Representation Theory. The quadratic super-algebras and operations on them are described. We also describe some important monoidal functors. We proved that the monoidal category of graded super-algebras with Manin product is coclosed relative to the subcategory of finitely generated quadratic super-algebras. The super-version of the ( A , B ) (A,B) -Manin matrices is introduced and related with the quadratic super-algebras. We define a super-version of quantum representations and of quantum linear actions, relate them to each other and describe them by the super-Manin matrices. Some operations on quantum representations/quantum linear actions are described. We show how the classical representations lift to the quantum level.

Introduction

It is well-known that in the usual differential/algebraic geometry the spaces contravariantly corresponds to the algebras of functions. The idea to generalise different kinds of spaces by substituting the commuting algebras of functions by non-commutative algebras is quite old. One source of such generalisation is quantisation in physics. By this reason such ‘non-commutative’ generalisation of a space are often called ‘quantum space’. A generalisation of Lie/algebraic group was called ‘quantum group’ by Drinfeld
[
Dr
]
.

In
[
Man88
]
Yuri Manin proposed to consider quadratic algebras as quantum analogues of the vector spaces. This was a starting point to generalise classical representation theory to the quantum case in
[
S2
]
; namely, the notion of representation was generalised to the case of a quantum representation space. Such quantum representations are described by so-called Manin matrices.

In some form the Manin matrices appeared in
[
Man87
,
Man88
]
in connection with the quadratic algebras. The Manin matrices for polynomial algebras and their
q
-deformations were investigated and applied in the works
[
GLZ
,
CF
,
CM
,
CFR
,
RST
,
CFRS
,
IO
,
Molev
]
. For general quadratic algebras they were described in
[
S1
]
. Super-analogues of the Manin matrices was considered in
[
Man89
,
MR
]
.

The main tool of Quantum Representation Theory in
[
S2
]
is the theory of monoidal categories. The correspondence between linear actions and representations in a closed monoidal category was generalised to a wider class of monoidal categories. This general representation theory was developed in
[
S3
]
.

In the present paper we consider super-versions of quadratic algebras, of Manin matrices and of operations with the quadratic algebras. We apply the general approach of
[
S3
]
to an appropriate monoidal category and describe quantum representations by the super-Manin matrices.

The article is organised as follows. Section
2
is preliminary: we recall and introduce some terms and notations here. The quadratic super-algebras are investigated in Section
3
. The binary operations (monoidal products) on quadratic super-algebras are described in Subsection
3.1
. In Subsection
3.2
we collect useful functors and describe their properties. Subsection
3.3
is devoted to the coclosed structure on a monoidal category of the quadratic super-algebras given by so-called internal cohom-functor. In Subsection
4.1
we introduce super-Manin matrices for a pair of quadratic super-algebras in terms idempotent operators; also we introduce universal super-Manin matrices and use them to describe the internal cohom-functor. Quantum Representation Theory for the super-case is described in Subsection
4.2
.

2.1
Notations and conventions

2.1.1.

Monoidal categories.
We denote a monoidal category by
({\mathbf{C}},\otimes)
or
({\mathbf{C}},\otimes,I)
, where
{\mathbf{C}}
is a category with a bifunctor
\otimes\colon{\mathbf{C}}\times{\mathbf{C}}\to{\mathbf{C}}
and a unit object
I=I_{\mathbf{C}}
. We suppose monoidal categories to be strict. The monoids and comonoids in this monoidal category form the categories
\mathop{\mathbf{Mon}}\nolimits({\mathbf{C}},\otimes)
and
\mathop{\mathbf{Comon}}\nolimits({\mathbf{C}},\otimes)=\big(\mathop{\mathbf{Mon}}\nolimits({\mathbf{C}}^{\mathrm{op}},\otimes)\big)^{\mathrm{op}}
. For any
{\mathbb{M}}\in\mathop{\mathbf{Mon}}\nolimits({\mathbf{C}},\otimes)
and
{\mathbb{O}}\in\mathop{\mathbf{Comon}}\nolimits({\mathbf{C}},\otimes)
we have categories of (left) actions and coactions
\mathop{\mathbf{Lact}}\nolimits({\mathbb{M}})
and
\mathop{\mathbf{Lcoact}}\nolimits({\mathbb{O}})
.

Let the monoidal category
({\mathbf{C}},\otimes)
be symmetric. Then
\mathop{\mathbf{Mon}}\nolimits({\mathbf{C}},\otimes)
and
\mathop{\mathbf{Comon}}\nolimits({\mathbf{C}},\otimes)
are monoidal and also symmetric (the monoidal product in this category is usually denoted by the same symbol). They have full subcategories of commutative monoids and cocommutative comonoids:
\mathop{\mathbf{{\vphantom{M}}{}_{c}Mon}}\nolimits({\mathbf{C}},\otimes)\subset\mathop{\mathbf{Mon}}\nolimits({\mathbf{C}},\otimes)
,
\mathop{\mathbf{{\vphantom{C}}{}_{coc}Comon}}\nolimits({\mathbf{C}},\otimes)\subset\mathop{\mathbf{Mon}}\nolimits({\mathbf{C}},\otimes)
. In this case one can also define bimonoids, which form the category

\mathop{\mathbf{Bimon}}\nolimits({\mathbf{C}},\otimes)=\mathop{\mathbf{Comon}}\nolimits\big(\mathop{\mathbf{Mon}}\nolimits({\mathbf{C}},\otimes),\otimes\big)=\mathop{\mathbf{Mon}}\nolimits\big(\mathop{\mathbf{Comon}}\nolimits({\mathbf{C}},\otimes),\otimes\big).

Any bimonoid
{\mathbb{B}}\in\mathop{\mathbf{Bimon}}\nolimits({\mathbf{C}},\otimes)
gives the monoidal categories
\mathop{\mathbf{Lact}}\nolimits({\mathbb{B}})
and
\mathop{\mathbf{Lcoact}}\nolimits({\mathbb{B}})
. They are symmetric if
{\mathbb{B}}
is cocommutative or commutative respectively.

Any category
{\mathbf{C}}
with finite products is a symmetric monoidal category
({\mathbf{C}},\times)
, where
\times
is the categorical product and the unit object is the terminal object. An example of such category is the category of sets
{\mathbf{Set}}
.

2.1.2.

Monoidal functors.
A
lax
monoidal structure of a functor
F\colon{\mathbf{C}}\to{\mathbf{D}}
between two monoidal categories
({\mathbf{C}},\otimes)
and
({\mathbf{D}},\odot)
is given by morphisms
\varphi\colon I_{\mathbf{D}}\to FI_{\mathbf{C}}
and
\phi_{X,Y}\colon FX\odot FY\to F(X\otimes Y)
satisfying some conditions. A
colax
monoidal structure of
F\colon{\mathbf{C}}\to{\mathbf{D}}
is given by
\varphi\colon FI_{\mathbf{C}}\to I_{\mathbf{D}}
and
\phi_{X,Y}\colon F(X\otimes Y)\to FX\odot FY
. Lax/colax monoidal functor is the triple
F=(F,\varphi,\phi)\colon({\mathbf{C}},\otimes)\to({\mathbf{D}},\odot)
. A monoidal functor between symmetric monoidal categories is called
symmetric
if it respects the symmetric structures.

Any (co)lax monoidal functor translates (co)monoids to (co)monoids and (co)actions to (co)actions, so we have the induced functors
\mathop{\mathbf{Mon}}\nolimits(F)\colon\mathop{\mathbf{Mon}}\nolimits({\mathbf{C}},\otimes)\to\mathop{\mathbf{Mon}}\nolimits({\mathbf{D}},\odot)
,
F_{\mathbb{M}}\colon\mathop{\mathbf{Lact}}\nolimits({\mathbb{M}})\to\mathop{\mathbf{Lact}}\nolimits\big(\mathop{\mathbf{Mon}}\nolimits(F){\mathbb{M}}\big)
for the lax case and
\mathop{\mathbf{Comon}}\nolimits(F)\colon\mathop{\mathbf{Comon}}\nolimits({\mathbf{C}},\otimes)\to\mathop{\mathbf{Comon}}\nolimits({\mathbf{D}},\odot)
,
F^{\mathbb{O}}\colon\mathop{\mathbf{Lcoact}}\nolimits({\mathbb{O}})\to\mathop{\mathbf{Lcoact}}\nolimits\big(\mathop{\mathbf{Comon}}\nolimits(F){\mathbb{O}}\big)
for the colax case.

If all
\varphi
and
\phi_{X,Y}
are isomorphisms, then the monoidal functor
F=(F,\varphi,\phi)\colon({\mathbf{C}},\otimes)\to({\mathbf{D}},\odot)
is called
strong
monoidal. A symmetric strong monoidal functor induces the functors
\mathop{\mathbf{Bimon}}\nolimits(F)\colon\mathop{\mathbf{Mon}}\nolimits({\mathbf{C}},\otimes)\to\mathop{\mathbf{Bimon}}\nolimits({\mathbf{D}},\odot)
,
F_{\mathbb{B}}\colon\mathop{\mathbf{Lact}}\nolimits({\mathbb{B}})\to\mathop{\mathbf{Lact}}\nolimits\big(\mathop{\mathbf{Mon}}\nolimits(F){\mathbb{B}}\big)
and
F^{\mathbb{B}}\colon\mathop{\mathbf{Lcoact}}\nolimits({\mathbb{B}})\to\mathop{\mathbf{Lcoact}}\nolimits\big(\mathop{\mathbf{Comon}}\nolimits(F){\mathbb{B}}\big)
.

A contravariant functor
F\colon{\mathbf{C}}\to{\mathbf{D}}
is called (symmetric) lax/colax/strong monoidal iff the corresponding covariant functor
\bar{F}\colon{\mathbf{C}}^{\mathrm{op}}\to{\mathbf{D}}
is (symmetric) lax/colax/strong monoidal (or, equivalently, the opposite functor
\bar{F}^{\mathrm{op}}\colon{\mathbf{C}}\to{\mathbf{D}}^{\mathrm{op}}
is (symmetric) colax/lax/strong monoidal).

2.1.3.

Relative adjoints

[
Ulm
]
,
[
S3
, § 2.3]
. Consider categories
{\mathbf{C}}
and
{\mathbf{D}}
. We say that a functor
F\colon{\mathbf{C}}\to{\mathbf{D}}
has a
right adjoint relative
to a full subcategory
{\mathbf{D}}^{\prime}\subset{\mathbf{D}}
iff there exists a functor
G\colon{\mathbf{D}}^{\prime}\to{\mathbf{C}}
(called right adjoint for
F
relative to
{\mathbf{D}}^{\prime}
) and a bijection

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathrm{Hom}}\nolimits(FX,Z)\cong\mathop{\mathrm{Hom}}\nolimits(X,GZ)

(2.1)

natural in
X\in{\mathbf{C}}
,
Z\in{\mathbf{D}}^{\prime}
. We say that a functor
G\colon{\mathbf{D}}\to{\mathbf{C}}
has a
left adjoint relative
to a full subcategory
{\mathbf{C}}^{\prime}\subset{\mathbf{C}}
iff there exists a functor
F\colon{\mathbf{C}}^{\prime}\to{\mathbf{D}}
(called left adjoint for
G
relative to
{\mathbf{C}}^{\prime}
) and a bijection (
2.1
) natural in
X\in{\mathbf{C}}^{\prime}
and
Z\in{\mathbf{D}}
. The right/left adjoint with the
relative adjunction
(
2.1
) is unique up to an isomorphism. A functor
F\colon{\mathbf{C}}\to{\mathbf{D}}
has a right adjoint relative to
{\mathbf{D}}^{\prime}\subset{\mathbf{D}}
iff there exist a universal morphism from
F
to each object
Z\in{\mathbf{D}}^{\prime}
. A functor
G\colon{\mathbf{D}}\to{\mathbf{C}}
has a left adjoint relative to
{\mathbf{C}}^{\prime}\subset{\mathbf{C}}
iff there exist universal morphism from each object
X\in{\mathbf{C}}^{\prime}
to
G
.

2.1.4.

Relatively closed monoidal categories.
In
[
S3
, § 2.4]
we generalised the notions of closed and coclosed monoidal category in terms of relative adjoints. We call a monoidal category
({\mathbf{C}},\otimes)

closed/coclosed relative
to a full subcategory
{\mathbf{P}}\subset{\mathbf{C}}
or
relatively closed/coclosed with parametrising subcategory

{\mathbf{P}}
iff for any
Y\in{\mathbf{P}}
the functor
F_{Y}=-\otimes Y
has a right/left adjoint relative to
{\mathbf{P}}
.

Fix parametrising subcategory
{\mathbf{P}}
. Then
({\mathbf{C}},\otimes)
is relatively closed iff there exists a bifunctor
\mathop{\mathrm{\bf hom}}\nolimits\colon{\mathbf{P}}^{\mathrm{op}}\times{\mathbf{P}}\to{\mathbf{C}}
(called internal hom-functor) and a bijection

\displaystyle\theta\colon\mathop{\mathrm{Hom}}\nolimits\big(X,\mathop{\mathrm{\bf hom}}\nolimits(Y,Z)\big)=\mathop{\mathrm{Hom}}\nolimits(X\otimes Y,Z)

(2.2)

natural in
X\in{\mathbf{C}}
,
Y,Z\in{\mathbf{P}}
. In this case the internal end-object
\mathop{\mathrm{\bf end}}\nolimits(Y)=\mathop{\mathrm{\bf hom}}\nolimits(Y,Y)
has a structure of monoid in
({\mathbf{C}},\otimes)
for any
Y\in{\mathbf{P}}
.

Dually, the monoidal category
({\mathbf{C}},\otimes)
is relatively coclosed iff there exists a bifunctor
\mathop{\mathrm{\bf cohom}}\nolimits\colon{\mathbf{P}}^{\mathrm{op}}\times{\mathbf{P}}\to{\mathbf{C}}
(called internal cohom-functor) and a bijection

\displaystyle\vartheta\colon\mathop{\mathrm{Hom}}\nolimits\big(\mathop{\mathrm{\bf cohom}}\nolimits(Y,X),Z\big)=\mathop{\mathrm{Hom}}\nolimits(X,Z\otimes Y)

(2.3)

natural in
X,Y\in{\mathbf{P}}
,
Z\in{\mathbf{C}}
. In this case the internal coend-object
\mathop{\mathrm{\bf coend}}\nolimits(Y)=\mathop{\mathrm{\bf cohom}}\nolimits(Y,Y)
has a structure of comonoid in
({\mathbf{C}},\otimes)
for any
Y\in{\mathbf{P}}
.

The monoidal category is closed/coclosed iff it is closed/coclosed relative to the whole category
{\mathbf{C}}
.

2.1.5.

Vector spaces and algebras.
Fix an infinite field
{\mathbb{K}}
of characteristic
\mathop{\mathrm{char}}\nolimits{\mathbb{K}}\neq 2
. We consider all vector spaces over
{\mathbb{K}}
. The category of such vector spaces is denoted by
{\mathbf{Vect}}
. This is a monoidal category with the standard tensor product
V\otimes W=V\otimes_{\mathbb{K}}W
. The monoids in this category form the category
{\mathbf{Alg}}=\mathop{\mathbf{Mon}}\nolimits({\mathbf{Vect}},\otimes)
. We call its objects simply
algebras
(these are associative unital algebras over
{\mathbb{K}}
). An identity element of an algebra
{\mathcal{A}}
is denoted by
1_{\mathcal{A}}
or
1
.

The category
{\mathbf{Alg}}
is also monoidal with respect to the tensor product of algebras. Its full monoidal subcategory consisting of the commutative algebras is opposite to the category of the affine schemes over
{\mathbb{K}}
:
({\mathbf{CommAlg}},\otimes)^{\mathrm{op}}=({\mathbf{AffSch}},\times)
. Denote the categories of finite-dimensional vector spaces and algebras by
{\mathbf{FVect}}
and
{\mathbf{FAlg}}=\mathop{\mathbf{Mon}}\nolimits({\mathbf{FVect}},\otimes)
. These are monoidal subcategories of
({\mathbf{Vect}},\otimes)
and
({\mathbf{Alg}},\otimes)
.

2.1.6.

{\mathbb{A}}
-graded vector spaces.
Let
{\mathbb{A}}\in\mathop{\mathbf{{\vphantom{M}}{}_{c}Mon}}\nolimits({\mathbf{Set}},\times)
be an Abelian monoid with the binary operation
{\mathbb{A}}\times{\mathbb{A}}\to{\mathbb{A}}
written additively:
(a,b)\mapsto a+b
. Denote by
{{\mathbb{A}}}
-
\mathbf{GrVect}
the category of
{\mathbb{A}}
-graded vector spaces
V=\bigoplus\limits_{g\in{\mathbb{A}}}V_{g}
. Elements of the component
V_{g}
are called homogeneous of degree
g
. The morphisms in this category are graded linear maps
f\colon V\to W
, i.e.
f(V_{g})\subset W_{g}
. Each such map is given by a family of arbitrary linear maps
f_{g}\colon V_{g}\to W_{g}
. In other words,
{{\mathbb{A}}}
-
\mathbf{GrVect}
is the category of functors
{\mathbb{A}}\to{\mathbf{Vect}}
.

The tensor product of two
{\mathbb{A}}
-graded vector spaces decomposed as

\displaystyle V\otimes W=\bigoplus_{g\in{\mathbb{A}}}(V\otimes W)_{g},

\displaystyle(V\otimes W)_{g}=\bigoplus_{h,h^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{A}}\atop h+h^{\prime}=g}V_{h}\otimes W_{h^{\prime}}

(2.4)

(if
{\mathbb{A}}
is a group then
(V\otimes W)_{g}=\bigoplus\limits_{h\in{\mathbb{A}}}V_{h}\otimes W_{g-h}
). In this way we obtain a monoidal category
({\text{${{\mathbb{A}}}$-$\mathbf{GrVect}$}},\otimes)
. Its unit object is the vector space
{\mathbb{K}}
with the grading
({\mathbb{K}})_{0}={\mathbb{K}}
,
{\mathbb{K}}_{g}=0
for
g\neq 0
, where
0
is the neutral element of
{\mathbb{A}}
.

The monoidal category
({\text{${{\mathbb{A}}}$-$\mathbf{GrVect}$}},\otimes)
is closed. The component
\mathop{\mathrm{\bf hom}}\nolimits(W,Z)_{g}
is the vector space consisting of the operators
f\colon W\to Z
such that
f(W_{h})=V_{g+h}
.

2.1.7.

{\mathbb{A}}
-graded algebras.
A monoid
{\mathcal{A}}\in\mathop{\mathbf{Mon}}\nolimits({\text{${{\mathbb{A}}}$-$\mathbf{GrVect}$}},\otimes)
is an algebra with a grading
{\mathcal{A}}=\bigoplus\limits_{g\in{\mathbb{A}}}{\mathcal{A}}_{g}
such that
A_{g}A_{h}\subset A_{g+h}
and
1_{\mathcal{A}}\in{\mathcal{A}}_{0}
(if
{\mathbb{A}}
is a group or a submonoid of a group, then the former condition implies the latter one). We obtain the category
{\text{${{\mathbb{A}}}$-$\mathbf{GrAlg}$}}=\mathop{\mathbf{Mon}}\nolimits({\text{${{\mathbb{A}}}$-$\mathbf{GrVect}$}},\otimes)
, where the morphisms are graded homomorphisms of algebras. Its objects are called
{\mathbb{A}}
-graded algebras.

If
({\text{${{\mathbb{A}}}$-$\mathbf{GrVect}$}},\otimes)
is equipped with a symmetric structure, then it gives a monoidal structure on
{{\mathbb{A}}}
-
\mathbf{GrAlg}
with the symmetric structure.
Recall that the standard symmetric structure of
({\mathbf{Vect}},\otimes)
is defined by the permutation operators
\sigma_{V,W}\colon V\otimes W\to W\otimes V
,
v\otimes w\mapsto v\otimes w
. These operators are graded due to the commutativity of
{\mathbb{A}}
, so it gives a simplest symmetric structure on
({\text{${{\mathbb{A}}}$-$\mathbf{GrVect}$}},\otimes)
. However, it is not unique.

Let
{\mathbb{A}}^{\prime}\subset{\mathbb{A}}
be a submonoid. Then
{{\mathbb{A}}^{\prime}}
-
\mathbf{GrVect}
and
{{\mathbb{A}}^{\prime}}
-
\mathbf{GrAlg}
are full monoidal subcategories of
{{\mathbb{A}}}
-
\mathbf{GrVect}
and
{{\mathbb{A}}}
-
\mathbf{GrAlg}
, consisting of the
{\mathbb{A}}
-graded vector spaces (algebras)
V
such that
V_{g}=0
for
g\in{\mathbb{A}}\backslash{\mathbb{A}}^{\prime}
. In particular,
{\mathbf{Vect}}={\text{${\{0\}}$-$\mathbf{GrVect}$}}\subset{\text{${{\mathbb{A}}}$-$\mathbf{GrVect}$}}
,
{\mathbf{Alg}}\subset{\text{${{\mathbb{A}}}$-$\mathbf{GrAlg}$}}
.

2.2
Graded super-algebras

2.2.1.

Super-vector spaces and super-algebras.

Let
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}={\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}}=\{{\bar{0}},{\bar{1}}\}
. This is an Abelian additive group, which has a structure of ring given by the multiplication
\bar{k}\cdot\bar{l}=\overline{kl}
. Consider the category
{\mathbf{SVect}}:={\text{${{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}}$-$\mathbf{GrVect}$}}
. Its objects
V\in{\mathbf{SVect}}
are called
super-vector spaces
. The homogeneous elements
v\in V_{\bar{0}}
are called
even
, while
v\in V_{\bar{1}}
are called
odd
. For
v\in V_{\bar{k}}
we use the notation
[v]:=\bar{k}
.

A subspace
\widetilde{V}
of a super-vector space
V\in{\mathbf{SVect}}
is called super-subspace iff
\widetilde{V}
has a structure of
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
-grading such that the embedding
\widetilde{V}\hookrightarrow V
is
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
-graded, i.e.
\widetilde{V}=\widetilde{V}_{\bar{0}}\oplus\widetilde{V}_{\bar{1}}
where
\widetilde{V}_{\bar{0}}=\widetilde{V}\cap V_{\bar{0}}
and
\widetilde{V}_{\bar{1}}=\widetilde{V}\cap V_{\bar{1}}
.

The objects of
{\mathbf{SAlg}}:={\text{${{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}}$-$\mathbf{GrAlg}$}}
are monoids in the monoidal category
({\mathbf{SVect}},\otimes)
called
super-algebras
. The super-vector space
\mathop{\mathrm{\bf end}}\nolimits(V)=\mathop{\mathrm{\bf hom}}\nolimits(V,V)
is equipped with a structure of super-algebra.
The categories of finite-dimensional super-vector spaces and super-algebras are full monoidal subcategories
{\mathbf{FSVect}}
and
{\mathbf{FSAlg}}=\mathop{\mathbf{Mon}}\nolimits({\mathbf{FVect}},\otimes)
of
({\mathbf{SVect}},\otimes)
and
({\mathbf{SAlg}},\otimes)
respectively.

Fix the following symmetric structure on this monoidal category:

\displaystyle\sigma(v\otimes w)=(-1)^{[v][w]}w\otimes v,

(2.5)

where
(-1)^{\bar{k}}=(-1)^{k}
(the usage of the notation
[v]
supposes that
v
is homogeneous). We obtain the symmetric monoidal category
({\text{${{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}}$-$\mathbf{GrAlg}$}},\otimes)
. The product
{\mathcal{R}}\otimes\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}
of two super-algebras
{\mathcal{R}}
and
\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}
is defined by the formula

\displaystyle(a\otimes b)\cdot(c\otimes d)=(-1)^{[b][c]}(a\cdot c)\otimes(b\cdot d),

\displaystyle a,c\in{\mathcal{R}},\quad b,d\in\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}.

(2.6)

The unit object of
({\text{${{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}}$-$\mathbf{GrAlg}$}},\otimes)
is the algebra
{\mathbb{K}}\in{\mathbf{Alg}}\subset{\mathbf{SAlg}}
, the symmetric structure is defined by the same formula (
2.5
). A commutative super-algebra is a super-algebra
{\mathcal{R}}
satisfying the condition
ab=(-1)^{[a][b]}ba
,
a,b\in{\mathcal{R}}
. The commutative super-algebras form a full monoidal subcategory
{\mathbf{CommSAlg}}\subset{\mathbf{SAlg}}
, where the functor
\otimes
coincides with categorical coproduct, so
({\mathbf{CommSAlg}},\otimes)
is opposite to
({\mathbf{SAffSch}},\times)
, where
{\mathbf{SAffSch}}={\mathbf{CommSAlg}}^{\mathrm{op}}
is the category of the affine super-schemes over
{\mathbb{K}}
. The objects of
{\mathbf{SAlg}}^{\mathrm{op}}
are the quantum analogues of the affine super-schemes.

Affine algebraic super-group is a group in
{\mathbf{SAffSch}}
. More generally affine algebraic super-monoid is a monoid in
{\mathbf{SAffSch}}
, i.e. a monoid in
({\mathbf{CommSAlg}}^{\mathrm{op}},\otimes)
. The monoids in
({\mathbf{SAlg}}^{\mathrm{op}},\otimes)
are called
quantum super-monoids
. Their category is opposite to the category of super-bialgebras:
\mathop{\mathbf{Mon}}\nolimits({\mathbf{SAlg}}^{\mathrm{op}},\otimes)=\mathop{\mathbf{Bimon}}\nolimits({\mathbf{SVect}}^{\mathrm{op}},\otimes)=\mathop{\mathbf{Bimon}}\nolimits({\mathbf{SVect}},\otimes)^{\mathrm{op}}
. The quantum super-groups correspond to the Hopf super-algebras.

The symmetric monoidal category
({\mathbf{SVect}},\otimes)
is closed. For
V,W\in{\mathbf{SVect}}
the internal hom-object
\mathop{\mathrm{\bf hom}}\nolimits(V,W)
is a super-vector space consisting of all the linear maps
f\colon V\to W
. The elements
f\in\mathop{\mathrm{\bf hom}}\nolimits(V,W)_{\bar{0}}
preserve the
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
-grading and we identify them with the morphisms
f\in\mathop{\mathrm{Hom}}\nolimits(V,W)
. The elements
f\in\mathop{\mathrm{\bf hom}}\nolimits(V,W)_{\bar{1}}
are operators
f\colon V\to W
such that
f(V_{\bar{0}})\subset W_{\bar{1}}
and
f(V_{\bar{1}})\subset W_{\bar{0}}
.

Note that direct sum of vector spaces provides another monoidal product on the category
{\mathbf{SVect}}
. The direct sum of
V,W\in{\mathbf{SVect}}
is a super-vector space
V\oplus W
with the components
(V\oplus W)_{\bar{0}}=V_{\bar{0}}\oplus W_{\bar{0}}
,
(V\oplus W)_{\bar{1}}=V_{\bar{1}}\oplus W_{\bar{1}}
. The monoidal category
({\mathbf{SVect}},\oplus)
has a canonical symmetric structure.

2.2.2.

{\mathbb{A}}
-graded super-algebras.
Consider the product of an Abelian monoid

and the group
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}

{\mathbb{A}}
-graded super-vector spaces
and
{\mathbb{A}}
-graded super-algebras

,

. Thus an
{\mathbb{A}}
-graded super-vector space (super-algebra) is a vector space (an algebra) equipped with two gradings (
{\mathbb{A}}
- and

-grading) which are compatible with each other. We have the following commutative diagrams of the forgetful functors:

{{\mathbb{A}}}

3
Categories of quadratic super-algebras

The main ingredient of Quantum Representation Theory is the category of representation spaces. In the super-case this is the category of the (finitely generated) quadratic super-algebras (more precisely, its opposite category). Here we describe different types of operations on quadratic super-algebras: binary operations (monoidal products), non-binary operations realised as functors from the category of quadratic algebras. We also derive the internal (co)hom-functors, which play a key role in the theory.

{\mathbb{O}}
on
{\mathcal{B}}
. It defines the fully faithful functor
\mathop{\mathbf{Corep}}\nolimits_{\mathbf{FQSA}}({\mathbb{O}})\hookrightarrow\mathop{\mathbf{Lcoact}}\nolimits({\mathbb{O}})
,
({\mathcal{B}},\omega)\mapsto\big({\mathcal{B}},\vartheta(\omega)\big)
.

4.2.4.

Multiplicative Manin matrices.

The matrix
M=(M^{i}_{j})
over a super-bialgebra
({\mathcal{A}},\Delta_{\mathcal{A}},\varepsilon_{\mathcal{A}})
is called
multiplicative
iff

\displaystyle\Delta_{\mathcal{A}}(M^{i}_{j})=\sum_{l}M^{i}_{l}\otimes M^{l}_{j},

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{\mathcal{A}}(M^{i}_{j})=\delta^{i}_{j}.

(4.45)

Let
{\mathbb{O}}=({\mathcal{A}},\Delta,\varepsilon)
be a quantum super-algebra and
({\mathcal{A}},\Delta_{\mathcal{A}},\varepsilon_{\mathcal{A}})
be the corresponding super-bialgebra. The matrix
M
with entries
M^{i}_{j}\in{\mathcal{A}}
is called multiplicative over
{\mathbb{O}}
iff it is multiplicative over this super-bialgebra. It is called
first order
matrix iff
M^{i}_{j}\in{\mathcal{A}}_{1}
. For instance, the universal
B
-Manin matrix is a first order multiplicative matrix over
{\mathcal{U}}_{B}=\mathop{\mathrm{\bf end}}\nolimits\big({\mathfrak{X}}_{B}({\mathbb{K}})\big)
.

Theorem 4.7
.

Let
{\mathbb{O}}\in\mathop{\mathbf{Comon}}\nolimits({\text{${{\mathbb{Z}}}$-$\mathbf{GrSAlg}$}},\circ)
and
{\mathcal{B}}={\mathfrak{X}}_{B}({\mathbb{K}})
for some idempotent
B
. Let
{\mathcal{M}}=({\mathcal{M}}^{i}_{j})
be the universal
B
-Manin matrix. Any quantum representation of
{\mathbb{O}}
on
{\mathcal{B}}
has the form
\omega({\mathcal{M}}^{i}_{j})=M^{i}_{j}
for some first order multiplicative
B
-Manin matrix
M=(M^{i}_{j})
over
{\mathbb{O}}
. Conversely, any such matrix defines a quantum representation
\omega\colon\mathop{\mathrm{\bf coend}}\nolimits({\mathcal{B}})\to{\mathbb{O}}
. The quantum linear action
\delta=\vartheta(\omega)\colon{\mathcal{B}}\to{\mathcal{A}}\circ{\mathcal{B}}
has the form
\delta(x^{i})=\sum_{j}M^{i}_{j}\otimes x^{j}
, i.e.
\delta_{\mathcal{A}}=f_{M}
. Let
{\mathcal{C}}={\mathfrak{X}}_{C}({\mathbb{K}})
. Consider two quantum representations
\omega\colon\mathop{\mathrm{\bf coend}}\nolimits({\mathcal{B}})\to{\mathbb{O}}
and
\nu\colon\mathop{\mathrm{\bf coend}}\nolimits({\mathcal{C}})\to{\mathbb{O}}
corresponding to the multiplicative first order
B
- and
C
-Manin matrices
M
and
N
over
{\mathbb{O}}
. A homomorphism
f\colon{\mathcal{B}}\to{\mathcal{C}}
is a morphism
({\mathcal{B}},\omega)\to({\mathcal{C}},\nu)
in
\mathop{\mathbf{Corep}}\nolimits_{\mathbf{FQSA}}({\mathbb{O}})
(=morphism
\big({\mathcal{B}},\vartheta(\omega)\big)\to\big({\mathcal{C}},\vartheta(\nu)\big)
in
\mathop{\mathbf{Lcoact}}\nolimits({\mathbb{O}})
) iff
f=f_{K}
for a
(B,C)
-Manin matrix
K
over
{\mathbb{K}}
such that
MK=KN
.

This theorem follows from Theorems
4.2
and
4.5
. The proof is similar to
[
S2
, Th. 5.2, Prop. 5.3, 5.5, 5.6]
.

4.2.5.

Opposite and coopposite quantum representations.

Let
{\mathcal{R}}=(V,\mu_{\mathcal{R}},\eta_{\mathcal{R}})
be a super-algebra with a multiplication
\mu_{\mathcal{R}}\colon V\otimes V\to V
and a unity map
\eta_{\mathcal{R}}\colon V\to{\mathbb{K}}
, where
V\in{\mathbf{SVect}}
. The opposite super-algebra is
{\mathcal{R}}^{\mathrm{op}}=(V,\mu_{\mathcal{R}}^{\mathrm{op}},\eta_{\mathcal{R}})
. If
{\mathcal{R}}\in{\text{${{\mathbb{Z}}}$-$\mathbf{GrSAlg}$}}
, then
{\mathcal{R}}^{\mathrm{op}}\in{\text{${{\mathbb{Z}}}$-$\mathbf{GrSAlg}$}}
, see p.
3.2
.

The opposite and coopposite to the comonoid
{\mathbb{O}}=({\mathcal{A}},\Delta,\varepsilon)\in\mathop{\mathbf{Comon}}\nolimits({\text{${{\mathbb{Z}}}$-$\mathbf{GrSAlg}$}},\circ)
are
{\mathbb{O}}^{\mathrm{op}}=({\mathcal{A}}^{\mathrm{op}},\Delta,\varepsilon)\in\mathop{\mathbf{Comon}}\nolimits({\text{${{\mathbb{Z}}}$-$\mathbf{GrSAlg}$}},\circ)
,
{\mathbb{O}}^{\mathrm{cop}}=({\mathcal{A}},\Delta^{\mathrm{cop}},\varepsilon)\in\mathop{\mathbf{Comon}}\nolimits({\text{${{\mathbb{Z}}}$-$\mathbf{GrSAlg}$}},\circ)
, where
\Delta^{\mathrm{cop}}=\sigma\cdot\Delta
. The bialgebras corresponding to these comonoids are
({\mathcal{A}}^{\mathrm{op}},\Delta_{\mathcal{A}},\varepsilon_{\mathcal{A}})
and
({\mathcal{A}},\Delta_{\mathcal{A}}^{\mathrm{cop}},\varepsilon_{\mathcal{A}})
.

Denote
B^{(21)}=\sigma\cdot B\sigma
. This is an operator
W\otimes W\to W\otimes W
with the entries
(B^{(21)})^{st}_{ij}=(-1)^{\bar{k}_{s}\bar{k}_{t}+\bar{k}_{i}\bar{k}_{j}}B^{ts}_{ji}
. The quadratic super-algebra
{\mathfrak{X}}_{B^{(21)}}({\mathbb{K}})
is defined by the commutation relations
\sum_{i,j}B^{ts}_{ji}x^{i}x^{j}=0
, hence
{\mathfrak{X}}_{B^{(21)}}({\mathbb{K}})={\mathfrak{X}}_{B}({\mathbb{K}})^{\mathrm{op}}
.
Also, we obtain
{\mathcal{U}}_{B^{(21)}}={\mathcal{U}}_{B}^{\mathrm{op}}
, which is an isomorphism of quantum super-algebras:
\mathop{\mathrm{\bf coend}}\nolimits({\mathcal{B}}^{\mathrm{op}})=\mathop{\mathrm{\bf coend}}\nolimits({\mathcal{B}})^{\mathrm{op}}
.

Consider a quantum representation
\omega\colon\mathop{\mathrm{\bf coend}}\nolimits({\mathcal{B}})\to{\mathbb{O}}
of
{\mathbb{O}}=({\mathcal{A}},\Delta,\varepsilon)
on
{\mathcal{B}}={\mathfrak{X}}_{B}({\mathbb{K}})
corresponding to a multiplicative first order
B
-Manin matrix
M
over
{\mathcal{A}}
.
It gives the comonoid morphism
\omega^{\mathrm{op}}\colon\mathop{\mathrm{\bf coend}}\nolimits({\mathcal{B}}^{\mathrm{op}})\to{\mathbb{O}}^{\mathrm{op}}
. This is a quantum representation of
{\mathbb{O}}^{\mathrm{op}}
on
{\mathcal{B}}^{\mathrm{op}}={\mathfrak{X}}_{B^{(21)}}({\mathbb{K}})
defined by the multiplicative first order
B^{(21)}
-Manin matrix
M
over
{\mathcal{A}}^{\mathrm{op}}
.

Due to (
3.31
) the quantum super-algebra
\mathop{\mathrm{\bf coend}}\nolimits({\mathcal{B}})
coincides with
\mathop{\mathrm{\bf coend}}\nolimits({\mathcal{B}}^{!})
as a graded super-algebra, but it has different comultiplication
d_{{\mathcal{B}}^{!}}
. Note that the matrix
{\mathcal{M}}^{\mathbf{ist}}
is multiplicative over the comonoid
\mathop{\mathrm{\bf coend}}\nolimits({\mathcal{B}}^{!})
, i.e.
d_{{\mathcal{B}}^{!}}\colon(M^{\mathbf{ist}})^{i}_{j}\mapsto\sum_{l}(M^{\mathbf{ist}})^{i}_{l}\otimes(M^{\mathbf{ist}})^{l}_{j}
, where
(M^{\mathbf{ist}})^{i}_{j}=(-1)^{(\bar{k}_{j}+\bar{k}_{i})\bar{k}_{j}}M^{j}_{i}
are entries of
{\mathcal{M}}^{\mathbf{ist}}
, see p.
4.1
. Hence we have
\mathop{\mathrm{\bf coend}}\nolimits({\mathcal{B}}^{!})=\mathop{\mathrm{\bf coend}}\nolimits({\mathcal{B}})^{\mathrm{cop}}
.
The quantum representation
\omega\colon\mathop{\mathrm{\bf coend}}\nolimits({\mathcal{B}})\to{\mathbb{O}}
gives the comonoid morphism
\omega^{\mathrm{cop}}\colon\mathop{\mathrm{\bf coend}}\nolimits({\mathcal{B}}^{!})\to{\mathbb{O}}^{\mathrm{cop}}
. This is a quantum representation of
{\mathbb{O}}^{\mathrm{cop}}
on
{\mathcal{B}}^{!}=\Xi_{B}({\mathbb{K}})
defined by the multiplicative first order
B^{*}
-Manin matrix
M^{\mathbf{ist}}
over
{\mathcal{A}}^{\mathrm{cop}}
. The corresponding coaction is
\delta^{\mathrm{cop}}(\psi_{i})=\sum_{j}(M^{\mathbf{ist}})^{i}_{j}\psi_{j}=\sum_{j}\psi_{j}M^{j}_{i}
.

4.2.6.

Parity change.

Let us apply the functor
\widehat{\Pi}\colon{\mathbf{FQSA}}\to{\mathbf{FQSA}}
to the quantum representation space
{\mathcal{B}}={\mathfrak{X}}_{B}({\mathbb{K}})
. By definition we need to change
W
by
\Pi W
, which is equivalent to the substitution
\bar{k}_{i}\to\bar{k}_{i}+{\bar{1}}
. Denote by
\Pi B
the operator
\Pi W\otimes\Pi W\to\Pi W\otimes\Pi W
given by the same matrix, but with the changed format. Since
B^{st}_{ij}\neq 0
implies
\bar{k}_{s}+\bar{k}_{t}=\bar{k}_{i}+\bar{k}_{j}
the relations
B^{st}_{ij}(-1)^{\bar{k}_{1}\bar{k}_{j}}x^{i}x^{j}=0
keep the same, so that
\widehat{\Pi}{\mathfrak{X}}_{B}({\mathbb{K}})={\mathfrak{X}}_{\Pi B}({\mathbb{K}})
.

We do not change the
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
-grading of
{\mathcal{A}}
. If
M=(M^{i}_{j})
is a multiplicative first order
B
-Manin matrix over
{\mathcal{A}}
, then the parity
[M^{i}_{j}]=\bar{k}_{i}+\bar{k}_{j}
does not change, hence
M
is a
\Pi B
-Manin matrix. Thus we obtain a quantum representation of the same
{\mathbb{O}}=({\mathcal{A}},\Delta,\varepsilon)
on the quadratic super-algebra
\widehat{\Pi}{\mathcal{B}}={\mathfrak{X}}_{\Pi}({\mathbb{K}})
defined by the multiplicative first order
\Pi B
-Manin matrix
M
.

4.2.7.

Classical left modules over finite-dimensional super-algebras.

Let
{\mathcal{R}}
be a finite-dimensional super-algebra, that is
{\mathcal{R}}\in\mathop{\mathbf{Mon}}\nolimits({\mathbf{FSVect}},\otimes)
. It can be lifted to the level of quantum super-algebras by the contravariant colax monoidal functor
S^{*}\colon({\mathbf{FSVect}},\otimes)\to({\mathbf{FQSA}},\circ)
. We obtain the quantum algebra
S^{*}{\mathcal{R}}=S{\mathcal{R}}^{*}
. This is a commutative super-algebra of polynomials on the super-vector space
{\mathcal{R}}
. The multiplication and unity of
{\mathcal{R}}
induces the structure of super-bialgebra on
S{\mathcal{R}}^{*}
.

A structure of (left)
{\mathcal{R}}
-module on a super-vector space
W
is given by a super-algebra morphism
\rho\colon{\mathcal{R}}\to\mathop{\mathrm{\bf end}}\nolimits(W)
or by an action
a\colon{\mathcal{R}}\otimes W\to W
. In the basis
(e_{i})
they have the form
\rho(r)e_{j}=a(r\otimes e_{j})=\sum_{i}\rho^{i}_{j}(r)e_{i}
for some linear functions
\rho^{i}_{j}\in{\mathcal{R}}^{*}
such that
[\rho^{i}_{j}]=\bar{k}_{i}+\bar{k}_{j}
and the matrix
M=(\rho^{i}_{j})
is multiplicative over
S{\mathcal{R}}^{*}
. Since
S{\mathcal{R}}^{*}
is a commutative super-algebra this matrix is an
A_{W}
-Manin matrix. The functor
S^{*}
translates the representation
\rho
to the quantum representation
\omega\colon\mathop{\mathrm{\bf coend}}\nolimits(SW^{*})\to S{\mathcal{R}}^{*}
of
S{\mathcal{R}}^{*}
on
S^{*}W=SW^{*}={\mathfrak{X}}_{A_{W}}({\mathbb{K}})
(see details in
[
S2
,
S3
]
). This quantum representation is defined by the multiplicative first order
A_{W}
-Manin matrix
M=(\rho^{i}_{j})
. The functor
S^{*}
translates the action
a
to a coaction
\delta\colon SW^{*}\to S{\mathcal{R}}^{*}\circ SW^{*}
.

Alternatively one can also use the contravariant strong monoidal functor
T^{*}
. In this case the classical representation
\rho
is lifted to the quantum representation of the quantum algebra
T^{*}{\mathcal{R}}=T{\mathcal{R}}^{*}
on the quadratic super-algebra
T^{*}W=TW^{*}={\mathfrak{X}}_{0}({\mathbb{K}})
given by the same matrix
M=(\rho^{i}_{j})
.

4.2.8.

Quantum representations of quantum super-monoids.
A quantum super-monoid (super-group) is a super-bialgebra (Hopf super-algebra)
{\mathbb{B}}\in\mathop{\mathbf{Bimon}}\nolimits({\mathbf{SVect}},\otimes)=\mathop{\mathbf{Comon}}\nolimits({\mathbf{SAlg}},\otimes)
. The strong monoidal functor

\displaystyle({\mathbf{SAlg}},\otimes)\to({\text{${{\mathbb{Z}}}$-$\mathbf{GrSAlg}$}},\circ),

\displaystyle{\mathcal{R}}\mapsto{\mathcal{R}}\otimes{\mathbb{K}}[u,u^{-1}]=\bigoplus_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}{\mathcal{R}}

(4.46)

is symmetric and faithful. It induces the non-full categorical embedding
\mathop{\mathbf{Bimon}}\nolimits({\mathbf{SVect}},\otimes)\to\mathop{\mathbf{Comon}}\nolimits({\text{${{\mathbb{Z}}}$-$\mathbf{GrSAlg}$}},\circ)
, which identifies
{\mathbb{B}}=({\mathcal{R}},\Delta_{\mathcal{R}},\varepsilon_{\mathcal{R}})
with the quantum algebra
{\mathbb{O}}_{\mathbb{B}}:=\big({\mathcal{R}}\otimes{\mathbb{K}}[u,u^{-1}],\Delta_{\mathcal{R}}\otimes{\mathbb{K}}[u,u^{-1}],\varepsilon_{\mathcal{R}}\otimes{\mathbb{K}}[u,u^{-1}]\big)
.

By
quantum representation of a quantum monoid

{\mathbb{B}}
on
{\mathcal{B}}={\mathfrak{X}}_{B}({\mathbb{K}})
we mean a quantum representation of the corresponding comonoid
{\mathbb{O}}_{\mathbb{B}}
, i.e. a graded homomorphism of bialgebras
\omega\colon\mathop{\mathrm{\bf coend}}\nolimits({\mathcal{B}})\to{\mathbb{O}}_{\mathbb{B}}
. It is given by a multiplicative
B
-Manin matrix
M
over
{\mathbb{B}}
.

Concluding remarks

We generalised Manin’s theory of quadratic algebras and Quantum Representation Theory introduced in
[
S2
]
to the super-case. To do the latter we applied the general approach described in
[
S3
]
to the monoidal category
({\text{${{\mathbb{Z}}}$-$\mathbf{GrSAlg}$}},\circ)
with the parametrising subcategory
{\mathbf{FQSA}}
. The abstract algebraic formulation and the language of monoidal categories allowed us to write all the formulae in the exactly the same form as for the non-super case
[
S2
]
. We expect that the approach
[
S3
]
works well for more general cases. The list of planed developments of the theory is written in the end of the article
[
S2
]
.

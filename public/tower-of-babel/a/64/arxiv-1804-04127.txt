2.2.
The big picture

The discrete groups introduced before are best understood via their action on projective classes of
2
-dimensional integral lattices.

Let
L_{1}=\langle\vec{e}_{1},\vec{e}_{2}\rangle=\mathbb{Z}\vec{e}_{1}\oplus\mathbb{Z}\vec{e}_{2}
be a fixed
2
-dimensional integral lattice. A lattice
L
is said to be
commensurable
with
L_{1}
if their intersection has finite index in both of them. Two such lattices
L
and
L^{\prime}
are in the same
projective class
if there is a non-zero rational number
\lambda
such that
L^{\prime}=\lambda.L
.

Any lattice
L
commensurable with
L_{1}
is in the same projective class as a unique lattice in
standard form

L_{M,\frac{g}{h}}=\langle M\vec{e}_{1}+\frac{g}{h}\vec{e}_{2},\vec{e}_{2}\rangle=\mathbb{Z}(M\vec{e}_{1}+\frac{g}{h}\vec{e}_{2})\oplus\mathbb{Z}\vec{e}_{2}

with
M
a strictly positive rational number and
\frac{g}{h}
is a proper fraction in its least terms, that is
0\leq g<h
with
(g,h)=1
. That is, the set of projective classes of
2
-dimensional lattices commensurable with
L_{1}
can be identified with
\Gamma\backslash PGL_{2}^{+}(\mathbb{Q})=\mathbb{Q}_{+}\times\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}
. Here,
PGL_{2}^{+}(\mathbb{Q})
is the group of all elements in
PGL_{2}(\mathbb{Q})
with strictly positive determinant, and the bijection is given by the assignment

\mathbb{Q}_{+}\times\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}\rTo\Gamma\backslash PGL_{2}^{+}(\mathbb{Q})\qquad\begin{bmatrix}M&\frac{g}{h}\\
0&1\end{bmatrix}\mapsto\Gamma.\begin{bmatrix}M&\frac{g}{h}\\
0&1\end{bmatrix}=L_{M,\frac{g}{h}}

see
[
11
, Prop. 2.6]
.

If
M\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
we omit the comma and write
L_{M\frac{g}{h}}
and call the lattice
number-like
. If in addition
g=0
we write
L_{M}
and call the lattice a
number-lattice
. With
X=M,\frac{g}{h}
we denote the projective class of
L_{M,\frac{g}{h}}
. It will be convenient to associate to this class the matrix

\alpha_{X}=\begin{bmatrix}M&\frac{g}{h}\\
0&1\end{bmatrix}\in GL_{2}^{+}(\mathbb{Q})

For two classes
X,Y\in\mathbb{Q}_{+}\times\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}
let
a_{XY}
be the smallest positive rational number such that
a_{XY}.\alpha_{X}\alpha_{Y}^{-1}\in GL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
. The
hyper-distance
between the two classes is then

\delta(X,Y)=det(a_{XY}.\alpha_{X}\alpha_{Y}^{-1})\in\mathbb{N}_{+}

Conway showed in
[
8
, p. 329]
(see also
[
11
, §2.5]
and
[
14
, §2.2]
for a slightly different treatment) that this definition is symmetric and that the
log
of the hyper-distance is a proper distance function on the projective classes of all lattices commensurable with
L_{1}
.

Further, if
g\in PGL_{2}^{+}(\mathbb{Q})
such that
\alpha_{X}.g\in\Gamma.\alpha_{X^{\prime}}
and
\alpha_{Y}.g\in\Gamma.\alpha_{Y^{\prime}}
(here, and elsewhere, we write
\alpha_{Z}
for its image in
PGL_{2}(\mathbb{Q})
), then it follows immediately from the definition of hyper-distance that

\delta(X,Y)=\delta(X.g,Y.g)=\delta(X^{\prime},Y^{\prime})

that is, the maps induced by by left multiplication by elements of
PSL_{2}(\mathbb{Q})^{+}
are isometries, where defined.

Definition 1
.

Conway’s big picture
\mathfrak{P}
is the graph having as vertices the projective classes of lattices commensurable with
L_{1}
, that is all elements of
\mathbb{Q}_{+}\times\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}
, and there is an edge between classes
X
and
Y
if and only if

\delta(X,Y)=p

with
p
a prime number. Alternatively,
\mathfrak{P}
is the Hasse diagram of the poset structure on
\mathbb{Q}_{+}\times\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}
with unique minimal element
1
defined by

X\leq Y\quad\text{iff}\quad\delta(1,X)\leq\delta(1,Y)~\text{and}~\delta(1,Y)=\delta(1,X).\delta(X,Y)

The subgraph
\mathfrak{C}
on all classes of number-lattices is called the big cell. It is the Hasse diagram of the poset-structure on
\mathbb{N}_{+}
given by division, that is,
M\leq N
iff
M|N
.

For a number-like class
X=M\frac{g}{h}
we have that the hyper-distance from
1
is equal to
h^{2}M
. In fact we have

1\leq hM\leq M\frac{g}{h}

and the number class
hM
is the unique class in the big cell
\mathfrak{C}
at minimal hyper-distance from
M\frac{g}{h}
.

Conway showed that each class
X=M,\frac{g}{h}
has exactly
p+1
neighbors at hyper-distance
p
for a prime number
p
. These are the classes

X_{k}=\frac{M}{p},\frac{g}{hp}+\frac{k}{p}~\text{mod}~1~\quad\text{for $0\leq k<p$}~\quad~\text{and}~\quad X_{p}=pM,\frac{pg}{h}~\text{mod}~1

It is convenient to consider these
p
-neighbors as the classes corresponding to the matrices obtained by multiplying
\alpha_{X}

on the left
with the matrices

P_{k}=\begin{bmatrix}\frac{1}{p}&\frac{k}{p}\\
0&1\end{bmatrix}~\quad~\text{for $0\leq k<p$, and}~\quad~P_{p}=\begin{bmatrix}p&0\\
0&1\end{bmatrix}

The classes at hyper-distance a
p
-power from
1
form a
p+1
-valent tree, and the big picture
\mathfrak{P}
itself ’factorizes’ as a product of these
p
-adic trees, see
[
8
, p. 332]
.

We will now clarify the relevance of the big picture
\mathfrak{P}
for the groups appearing in monstrous moonshine.

Definition 2
.

Let
\mathcal{X}=\{X_{i}~|~i\in I\}
be a set of classes in the big picture
\mathfrak{P}
. We define the following subgroups of
PGL_{2}^{+}(\mathbb{Q})

\Gamma_{0}(\mathcal{X})=\{g\in PGL_{2}^{+}(\mathbb{Q})~|~\forall i\in I~:~~\alpha_{X_{i}}.g\in\Gamma.\alpha_{X_{i}}\}

and

N(\mathcal{X})=\{g\in PGL_{2}^{+}(\mathbb{Q})~|~\forall i\in I,\exists j\in I~:~\alpha_{X_{i}}.g\in\Gamma.\alpha_{X_{j}}\}

as, respectively, the point-wise and set-wise stabilizer subgroups of
\mathcal{X}
.

Lemma 1
.

With these notations, we have (for
n,h,N\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
)

(1)

\Gamma_{0}(\{1\})=\Gamma
and
\Gamma_{0}(\{X\})=\alpha_{X}^{-1}.\Gamma.\alpha_{X}
.

(2)

\Gamma_{0}(\{1,N\})=\Gamma_{0}(N)=\Gamma_{0}(\{e~:~e|N\})
.

(3)

\Gamma_{0}(\{n,h\})=\Gamma_{0}(n|h)=\Gamma_{0}(\{e~:~h|e|n\})

Proof.

(1)
follows from the definition.
(2)
follows as
\Gamma\cap\alpha_{N}.\Gamma.\alpha_{N}^{-1}=\Gamma_{0}(N)
. The second part then follows from the isometric property and the fact that there is a unique number lattice in
\{e:1|e|N\}
at hyper-distance
k
from
1
and
\tfrac{N}{k}
from
N
.
(3)
is proved in a similar manner.
∎

2.4.
Roots of unity

In noncommutative geometry, the big picture
\mathfrak{P}
shows up as the basis of the regular representation of the Bost-Connes algebra
\mathbb{BC}
, see
[
14
, §4.3]
and
[
13
, §2.4]
. This algebra is at the heart of Connes’ approach to the Riemann hypothesis and encodes the action of the abelianised Galois group
Gal(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})^{ab}
on the group of all roots of unity, which is compatible with the
\lambda
-ring structure on the representation rings of finite groups.

For this connection, it is useful to view a number-like class
M\frac{g}{h}\in\mathfrak{P}
as a
primitive
h
-th root of unity centered at the number-lattice
h.M
. Note that we have a full copy of the group
\boldsymbol{\mu}_{h}
of
h
-th roots of unity centered at
h.M
consisting of the number-like classes
M\frac{e}{d}
with
d|h
, which are all at hyper-distance
h
from
h.M
.

Recall that the number of classes at hyper-distance
h
from a fixed class is equal to
\psi(h)=h\prod_{p|h}(1+\tfrac{1}{p})
where
\psi
is Dedekind’s psi function. Among the
\psi(h)
classes at hyper-distance
h
from
h.M
there are several copies of
\boldsymbol{\mu}_{h}
, depending on the chosen standard from of projective classes, all containing the same set of
\phi(h)
classes corresponding to the canonical primitive
h
-th roots of unity centered at
h.M
, where
\phi(h)
is the Euler function. What singles these classes out is the fact that on the path to them from
h.M
we never used the operator
P_{p}
for a prime
p
dividing
h
.

For the construction of the moonshine groups
n|h+e,f,\ldots
it is useful to consider also the
reverse canonical form
for a class
L_{M,\frac{g}{h}}
which occurs by swapping the roles of the standard basis vectors
\vec{e}_{1}
and
\vec{e}_{2}
. Then, the lattice
L_{M,\frac{g}{h}}
lies in the same class as the lattice

\langle\frac{1}{h^{2}M}\vec{e}_{2}+\frac{g^{\prime}}{h}\vec{e}_{1},\vec{e}_{1}\rangle=\mathbb{Z}(\frac{1}{h^{2}M}\vec{e}_{2}+\frac{g^{\prime}}{h}\vec{e}_{1})\oplus\mathbb{Z}\vec{e}_{1}

with
g^{\prime}
the inverse of
g
modulo
h
, and we write
(\frac{1}{h^{2}M},\frac{g^{\prime}}{h})
for the corresponding projective class. That is we will use these two notations for the same class in
\mathfrak{P}

X=M,\frac{g}{h}=(\frac{1}{h^{2}M},\frac{g^{\prime}}{h})

and use the matrix
\beta_{X}=

\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\
\frac{g^{\prime}}{h}&\frac{1}{M.h^{2}}\end{bmatrix}
instead of the matrix
\alpha_{X}
.

As the character
\lambda
, used in defining

from
\Gamma_{0}(n|h)+e,f,\ldots
, is trivial on
\Gamma_{0}(N)
and on the relevant Atkin-Lehner involutions it suffices in order to describe

to know the structure of the finite group

\Gamma_{0}(n|h)^{*}=\Gamma_{0}(n|h)/\Gamma_{0}(N)

This group is generated by the action of

\begin{bmatrix}1&\frac{1}{h}\\
0&1\end{bmatrix}
and

\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\

on the classes in the
(n|h)
-snake. The relevance of the introduction of the two sets of roots of unity centered at a number-class is that

will act as a power-map on the first set, whereas
y
acts as a power-map on the second set.

3.2.
The local structures

Our strategy to determine the structure of the moonshine picture(s) will be similar: for each
(n|k)
-serpent (or
(N|1)
-snake) we will first determine all number-like classes appearing in it. Next we will partition the number classes with respect to the set of primitive roots of unity centered at them. It then remains to determine, for a given set of
n
-roots of unity where
n|24
, the local structure of the picture at the number-class.

To draw these local structures we will not draw all edges, but just one choice of path from the number class to the number-like class. We will now explain how to add the remaining edges. Whereas the big cell
\mathfrak{C}
is commutative (being the Hasse diagram of the monoid
\mathbb{N}^{\times}_{+}
partially ordered under division), the big picture
\mathfrak{P}
is non-commutative, that is, if we add labels
P_{i}
to the edges determined by the operator used to draw the edge (in the partial order direction) it is not always true that
P_{i}.Q_{j}=Q_{j}P_{i}
.

We have seen that the subgraph of
\mathfrak{P}
consisting of all classes at hyper-distance a
p
-power from
1
is a free
p+1
-valent tree
T_{p}
. Hence, the class
X
at hyper-distance
p^{k}
from
1
corresponds to a unique product
P_{i_{b}}.\ldots.P_{i_{2}}.P_{i_{1}}P_{p}^{a}
of the matrices
P_{i}
with
0\leq ip
and
P_{p}
introduced before with
a+b=k
. Here,
p^{a}
is the maximal number-class on the unique path from
X
to
1
.

That is, the matrices
P_{i}
with
0\leq i<p
generated a free monoid and for all
0\leq i<p
we have
P_{p}.P_{i}=id
. In factoring
\mathfrak{P}=\ast_{p}~T_{p}
we have to take into account that the matrices
P_{i}
and
Q_{j}
corresponding to different prime numbers
p
and
q
do not necessarily commute. Still, they satisfy a
meta-commutation relation
similar to that of factorization in the Hurwitz quaternions, see
[
10
, §5.2]
.

Lemma 2
.

For distinct primes
p
and
q
and for all
0\leq i<p
and
0\leq j<q
there exist unique
0\leq k<p
and
0\leq l<q
such that

P_{i}.Q_{j}=Q_{l}.P_{k}\quad\text{and}\quad P_{p}.Q_{j}=Q_{a}.P_{p}~\text{with $a=pi~\text{mod}~q$}

Proof.

P_{i}.Q_{j}=\begin{bmatrix}\frac{1}{pq}&\frac{iq+j}{pq}\\
0&1\end{bmatrix}

and as
0\leq iq+j<pq
we have unique
0\leq k<p
and
0\leq l<q
such that
iq+j=lp+k
. ∎

Lemma 3
.

Any class
M,\frac{g}{h}\in\mathfrak{M}
at hyper-distance
N=p^{k}.q^{l}.\dots r^{s}
from
1
can be identified uniquely with a product

P_{i_{1}}P_{i_{2}}\ldots P_{i_{b}}Q_{j_{1}}Q_{j_{2}}\ldots Q_{j_{d}}\ldots R_{z_{1}}R_{z_{2}}\ldots R_{z_{v}}P_{p}^{a}Q_{q}^{c}\ldots R_{r}^{u}

for unique
0\leq i_{a}<p
,
0\leq j_{b}<q,\ldots,0\leq z_{u}<r
and with
a+b=k,c+d=l,\ldots,u+v=s
. Here
K=P_{p}^{a}Q_{q}^{b}\ldots R_{r}^{u}
is the unique number-class in
\mathfrak{C}
of minimal hyper-distance from
M,\frac{g}{h}
. For a number-like class
M\frac{g}{h}
we have
K=Mh
and
N=Mh^{2}
.

Proof.

A path from
1
to
M,\frac{g}{h}
of minimal length can be viewed as a product (left multiplication)
X_{l}X_{l-1}\ldots X_{2}X_{1}
with each
X_{i}
one of the matrices
P_{a},Q_{b},\ldots,R_{u}
. The claim follows from using the meta-commutation relations of the previous lemma.
∎

For applications in moonshine, we only need the meta-commutation relations for the operators
2_{0},2_{1}
with respect to

2_{0}.3_{0}=3_{0}.2_{0},~2_{0}.3_{1}=3_{0}.2_{1},~2_{0}.3_{2}=3_{1}.2_{0},

~2_{1}.3_{0}=3_{1}.2_{1},~2_{1}.3_{1}=3_{2}.2_{0},~2_{1}.3_{2}=3_{2}.2_{1}

These relations make it easy to add the forgotten edges in the pictures below. Further, for convenience, we start a path of hyper-length divisible by

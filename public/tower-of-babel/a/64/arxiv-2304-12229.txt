Abstract
The Euclidean hull of a linear code C C is defined as C ∩ C ⟂ C\cap C^{\perp} , where C ⟂ C^{\perp} denotes the dual of C C under the Euclidean inner product. A linear code with zero hull dimension is called a linear complementary dual (LCD) code. A pair ( C , D ) (C,D) of linear codes of length n n over 𝔽 q \mathbb{F}_{q} is called a linear complementary pair (LCP) of codes if C ⊕ D = 𝔽 q n C\oplus D=\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n} . In this paper, we give a characterization of LCD and LCP of cyclic codes of length q m − 1 q^{m}-1 , m ≥ 1 m\geq 1 , over the finite field 𝔽 q \mathbb{F}_{q} in terms of their basic dual zeros and their trace representations. We also formulate the hull dimension of a cyclic code of arbitrary length over 𝔽 q \mathbb{F}_{q} with respect to its basic dual zero. Moreover, we provide a general formula for the dimension of the intersection of two cyclic codes of arbitrary length over 𝔽 q \mathbb{F}_{q} based on their basic dual zeros.

Introduction

The Euclidean hull of a linear code
C
over the finite field
\mathbb{F}_{q}
is defined as the intersection of
C
with its dual, i.e.,

\hbox{Hull}(C):=C\cap C^{\perp},

where
C^{\perp}
is the Euclidean dual of
C
. Obviously,
\hbox{Hull}(C)
is also a linear code over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. We denote the dimension of
\hbox{Hull}(C)
by
h(C)
.

The concept of the hull has been introduced by Assumus and Key in
[
1
]
in order to classify finite projective planes. The hull of a linear code has applications in classical linear codes and quantum error-correction codes, see
[
9
]
,
[
12
]
,
[
13
]
,
[
14
]
. It turns out that the algorithms for determining permutation equivalence between codes, and determining the automorphism group of a linear code are more effective when the size of the hull dimension of the code is small.

A zero-dimensional hull linear code is called linear complementary dual (LCD) code, which is introduced by Massey in
[
11
]
. If
C
is an LCD code of length
n
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, then
C\oplus C^{\perp}=\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
. More generally, a pair
(C,D)
of linear codes of length
n
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
is called a linear complementary pair (LCP) of codes if
C\oplus D=\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
. Clearly, if
C
is an LCD code, then the pair
(C,C^{\perp})
is LCP.

The study of LCD and LCP of codes has a cryptographic motivation. It has been shown that certain cryptosystems, which are defined via linear codes, are more secure against side channel attacks (SCA) and fault-injection attacks (FIA) when LCD or LCP of codes are used in their constructions, see
[
2
]
,
[
3
]
,
[
6
]
.

Due to the above-mentioned applications, codes with small hull dimension (especially one-dimensional hull codes) are studied in the recent literature, see
[
4
]
,
[
10
]
,
[
14
]
and references therein.

Cyclic codes, their hull dimensions, LCD and LCP classes of cyclic codes are studied in the literature. The characterization of LCD and LCP of cyclic codes in terms of their generator polynomials has been given in
[
5
]
and
[
17
]
, respectively. The hull of cyclic codes in terms of their generator polynomials has been formulated in
[
15
]
. The class of one-dimensional hull cyclic codes has been studied in
[
10
]
, where the authors used the defining set of a cyclic code to obtain their results. They have also shown that there exist no binary or ternary one-dimensional hull cyclic codes.

This paper presents results on the hull LCD and LCP classes of cyclic codes with respect to their basic dual zero sets, and organized as follows. In Section 1, we recall the basic definitions and results on the cyclic codes and polynomials over finite fields. In Sections 3 and 4, LCD and one-dimensional hull cyclic codes of length
q^{m}-1
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
are studied, respectively. Moreover, the hull of a cyclic code of arbitrary length
n
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
is formulated. In Section 5, we study LCP of cyclic codes of length
q^{m}-1
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Furthermore, a general formula for the
\ell
-intersection pair
(C,D)
of cyclic codes (i.e.,
\dim(C\cap D)=\ell
) of arbitrary length over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
is provided.

2
Preliminaries

In this section, we recall basic properties of cyclic codes and polynomials over finite fields.

2.1
Cyclic Codes

Throughout the paper,
\mathbb{F}_{q}
denotes the finite field of
q
elements,
q
is a prime power and
n
is a positive integer such that
\gcd(n,q)=1
, where
\gcd(n,q)
denotes the greatest common divisor of
n
and
q
. A linear code over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
of length
n
and dimension
k
is a
k
-dimensional subspace of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
, and a codeword is an element of the linear code. For a linear code
C
of length
n
, the (Euclidean) dual of
C
, which is denoted by
C^{\perp}
, is defined as

C^{\perp}:=\{x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}\;|\;<c,x>=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}c_{i}x_{i}=0\;\text{ for all }\;c\in C\}.

A linear code over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
of length
n
is called cyclic, if any cyclic shift of a codeword is again a codeword, i.e.,
(c_{0},\ldots,c_{n-1})\in C
implies
(c_{n-1},c_{0},\ldots,c_{n-2})\in C
. Clearly, the dual of a cyclic code is also cyclic.

Let
C
be a linear code of length
n
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
and
c
be a codeword. If
c=(c_{0},\ldots,c_{n-1})
is identified with the polynomial
c(x)=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}c_{i}x^{i}
, then the code
C
can be seen as a subset of the ring
\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]/(x^{n}-1)
. If
C
is a cyclic code, then any cyclic shift of a codeword is also a codeword, i.e., the set
\{c(x)\ |\ c\in C\}
is an ideal of
\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]/(x^{n}-1)
. Since
\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]/(x^{n}-1)
is a principal ideal domain, any ideal of
\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]/(x^{n}-1)
has a unique monic generator.

We recall that for a polynomial
f(x)\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
with
f(0)\neq 0
, the polynomial
f^{*}(x)=\frac{1}{f(0)}x^{\deg f(x)}f(\frac{1}{x})
is called the reciprocal polynomial of
f(x)
, where
\deg{f(x)}
denotes the degree of
f(x)
. A polynomial
f(x)
is called self-reciprocal if
f(x)=f^{*}(x)
.
Let
g(x)
be the generator polynomial of a cyclic code
C
, i.e.,
C=<g(x)>
. Then
C^{\perp}=<h^{*}(x)>
, where
h(x)=\frac{x^{n}-1}{g(x)}
and
h^{\ast}(x)
is the reciprocal polynomial of
h(x)
. The polynomial
h(x)
is called the parity check polynomial of
C
.

Since the generator polynomial of a cyclic code of length
n
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
is a factor of
x^{n}-1
, we recall the factorization of
x^{n}-1
into monic irreducible polynomials over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
.

2.2
Factorization of
x^{n}-1

We recall that
n
and
q
are relatively prime. Let
a
be a positive integer. Then the
q
-cyclotomic coset
B_{a}
of
a
modulo
n
is defined as follows:

\displaystyle B_{a}:=\{a,aq,\ldots,aq^{\delta_{a}-1}\},

where
\delta_{a}
is the smallest positive integer such that

aq^{\delta_{a}}\equiv a\quad(\hbox{mod }n).
Note that the number
\delta_{a}
is the cardinality of
B_{a}
, and it is denoted by
|B_{a}|
. Clearly, for two positive integers
a_{1},a_{2}
, either
B_{a_{1}}=B_{a_{2}}
or
B_{a_{1}}\cap B_{a_{2}}=\emptyset
.
Let
B(n,q)
be the set of all the
q
-cyclotomic coset leaders modulo
n
. Then
{\cup}_{a\in B(n,q)}B_{a}=\mathbb{Z}_{n}.
That is, the set of
q
-cyclotomic cosets modulo
n
forms a partition of
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
.

Let
\alpha
be a primitive
n
-th root of unity over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Then the minimal polynomial
m_{\alpha^{i}}(x)
of
\alpha^{i}
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
is

m_{\alpha^{i}}(x)=\prod_{s\in B_{i}}(x-\alpha^{s}).

By using the above notation, the factorization of
x^{n}-1
into monic irreducible factors over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
can be given as below:

x^{n}-1=\prod_{i\in B(n,q)}m_{\alpha^{i}}(x).

(1)

Lemma 1
.

Let
\alpha
be a primitive
n
-th root of unity over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Then
m_{\alpha^{i}}(x)
is self-reciprocal if and only if
B_{i}=B_{-i}
.

Proof.

Suppose that
m_{\alpha^{i}}(x)\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
is self-reciprocal. That is,
m_{\alpha^{i}}(x)=m_{\alpha^{i}}^{\ast}(x)
. This implies that the set of roots of
m_{\alpha^{i}}^{\ast}(x)
is equal to the set of roots of
m_{\alpha^{i}}(x)
, i.e.,
B_{i}=B_{-i}
.

Conversely, assume that
B_{i}=B_{-i}
. This means that
\alpha^{-i}
is a root of
m_{\alpha^{i}}(x)
, hence the minimal polynomial
m_{\alpha^{i}}^{\ast}(x)
of
\alpha^{-i}
divides
m_{\alpha^{i}}(x)
. Since

m_{\alpha^{i}}^{\ast}(x)
and
m_{\alpha^{i}}(x)
are both monic and irreducible, we obtain that
m_{\alpha^{i}}^{\ast}(x)=m_{\alpha^{i}}(x)
, Hence, the polynomial
m_{\alpha^{i}}(x)
is self-reciprocal.
∎

Let
\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{t}\}
be the set of all
q
-cyclotomic coset leaders modulo
n
, and
T=\{\alpha^{i_{j}}\;|\;1\leq j\leq t\}
. Suppose
T_{1},T_{2}\subseteq T
such that
T_{1}=\{\alpha^{i_{j}}\;|\;B_{j}=B_{-j}\}
and
T_{2}=T\setminus T_{1}
. By Lemma
1
, for any
\alpha^{i_{j}}\in T_{1}
,
m_{\alpha^{i_{j}}}(x)
is self-reciprocal. Thus, Equation (
1
) can be rewritten as follows:

\displaystyle x^{n}-1=\prod_{\alpha^{i_{j}}\in T_{1}}m_{\alpha^{i_{j}}(x)}\prod_{\alpha^{i_{j}}\in T_{2}}m_{\alpha^{i_{j}}}(x)m_{\alpha^{i_{j}}}^{\ast}(x).

(2)

2.3
Trace representation of cyclic codes

In this subsection, we consider the case
n=q^{m}-1
for some positive integer
m
. Let
C
be a cyclic code of length
n
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
with the generator polynomial
g(x)
. Let
\alpha
be a primitive
n
-th root of unity over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
and
\{{i_{1}},\ldots,{i_{t}}\}
be the set of all
q
-cyclotomic coset leaders modulo
n
.
Suppose that
h(x)
is the parity check polynomial of
C
and
S\subseteq\{1,\ldots,t\}
such that
h^{\ast}(x)=\prod_{j\in S}m_{\alpha^{i_{j}}}(x)
.
Then the basic dual zero of
C
is defined as

\hbox{BZ}(C^{\perp})=\{\alpha^{i_{j}}\;|\;j\in S\}.

The following theorem gives a trace representation of a cyclic code
C
of length
q^{m}-1
, where
{\rm Tr}_{q^{m}q^{k}}
denotes the relative trace map from
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
to
\mathbb{F}_{q^{k}}
, for a divisor
k
of

.

Proposition 2
.

[
16
, Proposition 2.1]

Let
\alpha
be a primitive
n
-th root of unity with
n=q^{m}-1
.
Suppose that
C
is a cyclic code, where the generator polynomial of
C^{\perp}
is equal to
\prod_{j\in S}m_{\alpha^{i_{j}}}(x)
, i.e.,
\hbox{BZ}(C^{\perp})=\{\alpha^{i_{j}}\;|\;j\in S\}
. Then

C=\Bigl\{\Bigl(\sum_{j\in S}{\rm Tr}_{q^{m}q}(\lambda_{j}x^{i_{j}})\Bigr)_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}^{\ast}}\;\big|\;\lambda_{j}\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}\Bigr\}.

In connection with the trace representation above, we will use the following theorem for our results.

Theorem 1
.

(
[
7
]
, Theorem 2.5)
For
1\leq j\leq t
, let
i_{j}\geq 1
be positive integers which are in different
q
-cyclotomic cosets modulo

, where
n=q^{m}-1
. For
\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{t}\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
,

\displaystyle{\rm Tr}_{q^{m}q}(\lambda_{1}x^{i_{1}}+\cdots+\lambda_{t}x^{i_{t}})=0\;\;\textit{for all}\;\;x\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}

3
Linear complementary dual cyclic codes

We recall that the hull of a linear code
C
is defined as
\hbox{Hull}(C)=C\cap C^{\perp}
and we denote the dimension of
\hbox{Hull}(C)
by
h(C)
. A linear code
C
is called linear complementary dual (LCD) if
h(C)=0
. The characterization of an LCD cyclic code of length
q^{m}-1
with respect to its basic dual zero is given in the following theorem.

Theorem 2
.

Let
C
be a cyclic code of length
q^{m}-1
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
,
\alpha
be a primitive
n
-th root of unity over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Let
\{{i_{1}},\ldots,{i_{t}}\}
be the set of all
q
-cyclotomic coset leaders modulo
n
. Then
C
is LCD if and only if
\alpha^{i_{j}}\in\hbox{BZ}(C^{\perp})
implies that either
B_{j}=B_{-j}
or
\alpha^{-i_{j}}\in\hbox{BZ}(C^{\perp})
for all
1\leq j\leq t
.

Proof.

Suppose that
C=<g(x)>
and
C^{\perp}=<h^{*}(x)>
. That is,
g(x)h(x)=x^{n}-1
. We also have
\gcd(g(x),h(x))=1
, since the polynomial
x^{n}-1
has no repeated factors as
\gcd(n,q)=1
.
These together imply that

g^{\ast}(x)h^{\ast}(x)=x^{n}-1
and
\gcd(g^{\ast}(x),h^{\ast}(x))=1.

Since
(C^{\perp})^{\perp}=C
the basic dual zero of
C^{\perp}
is a set of representatives of the roots of
g(x)
, which is equal to
\hbox{BZ}(C)
.

Suppose on the contrary that
C
is LCD and there exists
1\leq j\leq t
such that
\alpha^{i_{j}}\in\hbox{BZ}(C^{\perp})
,
B_{j}\cap B_{-j}=\emptyset
and
\alpha^{-i_{j}}\notin\hbox{BZ}(C^{\perp})
. We without loss of generality assume that
j=1
. The assumptions
\alpha^{i_{1}}\in\hbox{BZ}(C^{\perp})
and
\alpha^{-i_{1}}\notin\hbox{BZ}(C^{\perp})
imply that
m_{\alpha^{i_{1}}}(x)\mid h^{\ast}(x)
and
m_{\alpha^{-i_{1}}}(x)\nmid h^{\ast}(x)
, respectively. Since
g^{*}(x)h^{*}(x)=x^{n}-1
and
\gcd(g^{*}(x),h^{*}(x))=1
, we obtain
m_{\alpha^{-i_{1}}}(x)\mid g^{\ast}(x)
, consequently
m_{\alpha^{i_{1}}}(x)\mid g(x)
. Therefore,
\alpha^{i_{1}}\in\hbox{BZ}(C^{\perp})\cap\hbox{BZ}(C)
.
Assume that
\hbox{BZ}(C^{\perp})=\{\alpha^{i_{1}}\}\cup T_{1}
and
\hbox{BZ}(C)=\hbox{BZ}((C^{\perp})^{\perp})=\{\alpha^{i_{1}}\}\cup T_{2}
. By Proposition
2
, the trace representations of
C
and
C^{\perp}
are as follows:

\displaystyle C=\Big\{\Big({\rm Tr}_{q^{m}q}(\lambda_{1}x^{i_{1}}+\sum_{\alpha^{i_{j}}\in T_{1}}\lambda_{j}x^{i_{j}})\Big)_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}^{\ast}}\;\big|\;\lambda_{j}\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}\Big\},

\displaystyle C^{\perp}=\Big\{\Big({\rm Tr}_{q^{m}q}(\beta_{1}x^{i_{1}}+\sum_{\alpha^{i_{j}}\in T_{2}}\beta_{j}x^{i_{j}})\Big)_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}^{\ast}}\;\big|\;\beta_{j}\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}\Big\}.

We can take
\lambda_{h}=\beta_{l}=0
for all
\alpha^{i_{h}}\in T_{1}
,
\alpha^{i_{l}}\in T_{2}
, and
\lambda_{1}=\beta_{1}=\lambda
such that
{\rm Tr}_{q^{m}q^{\delta_{j}}}(\lambda)\neq 0
. Then we obtain

c=\big({\rm Tr}_{q^{m}q}(\lambda x^{i_{1}})\big)_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}^{\ast}}\in C\cap C^{\perp}.
Since
{\rm Tr}_{q^{m}q}(\lambda)\neq 0
,
c\neq 0
by Theroem
1
. This contradicts the assumption that
C
is LCD.

Conversely, assume that
\alpha^{i_{j}}\in\hbox{BZ}(C^{\perp})
implies that either
B_{j}=B_{-j}
or
\alpha^{-i_{j}}\in\hbox{BZ}(C^{\perp})
for all
i_{j}
,
1\leq j\leq t
.
If
B_{j}=B_{-j}
, then we have
m_{\alpha^{i_{j}}}(x)=m_{\alpha^{i_{j}}}^{\ast}(x)
by Lemma
1
.
If
B_{j}\cap B_{-j}=\emptyset
and
\alpha^{-i_{j}}\in\hbox{BZ}(C^{\perp})
, then we have
m_{\alpha^{i_{j}}}(x)\ |\ h(x)
and
m_{\alpha^{i_{j}}}^{\ast}(x)\ |\ h^{*}(x)
. These together imply that
h^{\ast}(x)
is self-reciprocal. That is,

g(x)h^{\ast}(x)=x^{n}-1
and
\gcd(g(x),h^{\ast}(x))=1.

Therefore,
\hbox{BZ}(C^{\perp})\cap\hbox{BZ}(C)=\emptyset
. As
C\oplus C^{\perp}=\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
, we have
\hbox{BZ}(C)\cup\hbox{BZ}(C^{\perp})=T
. Thus, we assume without loss of generality that
\hbox{BZ}(C^{\perp})=\{\alpha^{i_{1}},\ldots,\alpha^{i_{s}}\}
and
\hbox{BZ}(C)=\{\alpha^{i_{s+1}},\ldots,\alpha^{i_{t}}\}
. Then by Proposition
2
, the trace representations of
C
and
C^{\perp}
are as follows:

\displaystyle C

\displaystyle=\Big\{\Big({\rm Tr}_{q^{m}q}(\lambda_{1}x^{i_{1}}+\cdots+\lambda_{s}x^{i_{s}})\Big)_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}^{\ast}}\;\big|\;\lambda_{j}\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}},1\leq j\leq s\Big\},

\displaystyle C^{\perp}

\displaystyle=\Big\{\Big({\rm Tr}_{q^{m}q}(\lambda_{s+1}x^{i_{s+1}}+\cdots+\lambda_{t}x^{i_{t}})\Big)_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}^{\ast}}\;\big|\;\lambda_{j}\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}},s+1\leq j\leq t\Big\}.

Suppose on the contrary that
\hbox{Hull}(C)\neq\{0\}
. Then there exists
0\neq c\in\hbox{Hull}(C)
, and
\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{t}\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
such that

\displaystyle c=\Big({\rm Tr}_{q^{m}q}(\lambda_{1}x^{i_{1}}+\cdots+\lambda_{s}x^{i_{s}})\Big)_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}^{\ast}}=\Big({\rm Tr}_{q^{m}q}(\lambda_{s+1}x^{i_{s+1}}+\cdots+\lambda_{t}x^{i_{t}})\Big)_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}^{\ast}}

Equivalently,

\displaystyle\Big({\rm Tr}_{q^{m}q}(\lambda_{1}x^{i_{1}}+\cdots+\lambda_{s}x^{i_{s}}-\lambda_{s+1}x^{i_{s+1}}-\cdots-\lambda_{t}x^{i_{t}})\Big)_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}^{\ast}}=0.

(3)

By Theorem
1
, the equality in (
3
) holds if and only if
|B_{j}|=\delta_{j}<m
and
{\rm Tr}_{q^{m}q^{\delta_{j}}}(\lambda_{j})=0
for all
1\leq j\leq t
. We know that the set
\hbox{BZ}(C^{\perp})\cup\hbox{BZ}(C)
contains all the leaders of
q
-cyclotomic cosets modulo
n
, in particular, the coset leader that contains 1. Since the cyclotomic coset that contains 1 has cardinality
m
, we have a contradiction. Hence,
C
is LCD.
∎

Remark 1
.

By Theorem
1
, a cyclic code
C
is LCD if and only if for any divisor
m_{\alpha^{i_{j}}}(x)
of
h^{\ast}(x)
, we have
m_{\alpha^{i_{j}}}^{\ast}(x)
is also a divisor. Therefore,
C
is LCD if and only if
h^{\ast}(x)
is self-reciprocal, which implies

g^{\ast}(x)=\frac{x^{n}-1}{h^{\ast}(x)}=\frac{x^{n}-1}{h(x)}=g(x)

is self-reciprocal. This has been also observed by Massey in
[
17
]
.

Corollary 3
.

Let
\{{i_{1}},\ldots,{i_{t}}\}
be the set of all q-cyclotomic coset leaders modulo
n
. If
B_{j}=B_{-j}
for all
1\leq j\leq t
, then any cyclic code of length
n
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
is LCD.

Proof.

Since
B_{j}=B_{-j}
for all
1\leq j\leq t
, by Lemma
1
, the polynomial
m_{\alpha^{i_{j}}}(x)
is self-reciprocal for any
1\leq j\leq t
. This means that any factor
g(x)
of
x^{n}-1
is self-reciprocal. Thus the corresponding cyclic code
C
is LCD.
∎

Example 3.1
.

If
q=2
and
m=9
, then the polynomial
x^{9}-1
has the following factorization into monic irreducible polynomials over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
:
x^{9}-1=(x+1)(x^{2}+x+1)(x^{6}+x^{3}+1).
Since
all the factors of
x^{9}-1
are self-reciprocal, any binary cyclic code of length
9
is LCD by Corollary
3
.

If
q=3
and
m=10
, then the polynomial
x^{10}-1
has the following factorization into monic irreducible polynomials over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
:

x^{10}-1=(x+1)(x+2)(x^{4}+x^{3}+x^{2}+x+1)(x^{4}+2x^{3}+x^{2}+2x+1),

Since all the factors of
x^{10}-1
are self-reciprocal, any ternary cyclic code of length

is LCD by Corollary

.

4
One-dimensional hull cyclic codes

In this section, we present a condition for a cyclic code to have one-dimensional hull in terms of its basic dual zero set. We will use the following theorem.

Theorem 3
.

(
[
8
]
, Theorem 4.3.7)
Let
C_{i}
be a cyclic code of length
n
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
with the generator polynomial
g_{i}(x)
for
i=1,2
. Then
C_{1}\cap C_{2}
has generator polynomial
\hbox{lcm}(g_{1}(x),g_{2}(x))
, where
\hbox{lcm}(g_{1}(x),g_{2}(x))
denotes the least common multiple of the polynomials
g_{1}(x)
and
g_{2}(x)
.

We recall that
\beta\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
is called a normal element over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
if the set
\{\beta,\beta^{q},\ldots,\beta^{q^{m-1}}\}
forms a basis of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. We need the following lemma for the main result of this section.

Lemma 4
.

Let
\beta
be a normal element of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Suppose that
k,l
are positive integers with
B_{k}\cap B_{l}=\emptyset
. Then the vectors
\big({\rm Tr}_{q^{m}q}(\beta x^{k})\big)_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}^{\ast}}
and
\big({\rm Tr}_{q^{m}q}(\beta x^{l})\big)_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}^{\ast}}
are linearly independent over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
.

Proof
: The proof is by contradiction.
Suppose that the vectors
\big({\rm Tr}_{q^{m}q}(\beta x^{k})\big)_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}^{\ast}}
and
\big({\rm Tr}_{q^{m}q}(\beta x^{l})\big)_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}^{\ast}}
are linearly dependent over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Then there exist nonzero
c_{1},c_{2}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
such that

Linear complementary pair of cyclic codes

A pair
(C,D)
of linear codes of length
n
over the finite field
\mathbb{F}_{q}
is called linear complementary pair (LCP) of codes if
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}=C\oplus D
. The LCP of codes can be considered as a generalization of LCD codes. Namely, if
C
is an LCD code, then the pair
(C,C^{\perp})
is LCP.

The following lemma is required to obtain the main result of this section.

Lemma 6
.

Let
C
and
D
be two cyclic codes of length
n
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Then

\hbox{BZ}((C\cap D)^{\perp})=\hbox{BZ}(C^{\perp})\cap\hbox{BZ}(D^{\perp}).

Proof.

Let
g_{C}(x)
,
g_{D}(x)
and
g(x)
denote the generator polynomials of
C
,
D
and
C\cap D
, and
h_{C}(x)
,
h_{D}(x)
and
h(x)
denote their parity check polynomials, respectively. Take
\alpha^{i_{j}}\in\hbox{BZ}((C\cap D)^{\perp})
. Suppose that
0\neq c\in C\cap D
, and
c(x)
is the polynomial corresponding to the codeword
c
. Since
c\in C\cap D
, we have

c(x)h(x)=c(x)h_{C}(x)=c(x)h_{D}(x)=0.
This implies that
c^{\ast}(x)h^{\ast}(x)=c^{\ast}(x)h^{\ast}_{C}(x)=c^{\ast}(x)h^{\ast}_{D}(x)=0
.
As
\alpha^{i_{j}}\in\hbox{BZ}((C\cap D)^{\perp})
, there exists
q(x)\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
such that
h^{\ast}(x)=m_{\alpha^{i_{j}}}(x)q(x)
. Then

\displaystyle c^{\ast}(x)m_{\alpha^{i_{j}}}(x)q(x)=c^{\ast}(x)h^{\ast}_{C}(x)=c^{\ast}(x)h^{\ast}_{D}(x)=0.

Since
c(x)\neq 0
, we obtain that
m_{\alpha^{i_{j}}}(x)\ |\ h^{\ast}_{C}(x)
, and
m_{\alpha^{i_{j}}}(x)\ |\ h^{\ast}_{D}(x)
. Hence,

\alpha^{i_{j}}\in\hbox{BZ}(C^{\perp})\cap\hbox{BZ}(D^{\perp})
, i.e.,
\alpha^{i_{j}}\in\hbox{BZ}((C\cap D)^{\perp})\subseteq\hbox{BZ}(C^{\perp})\cap\hbox{BZ}(D^{\perp})
.

We prove the reverse inclusion by contradiction. Suppose that
\alpha^{i_{j}}\in\hbox{BZ}(C^{\perp})\cap\hbox{BZ}(D^{\perp})
and
\alpha^{i_{j}}\notin\hbox{BZ}((C\cap D)^{\perp})
. Then
m_{\alpha^{i_{j}}}(x)\nmid h^{*}(x)
, i.e., there exist
q(x),r(x)\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
such that
h^{\ast}(x)=m_{\alpha^{i_{j}}}(x)q(x)+r(x)
with
0\neq r(x)
and
\deg r(x)<\deg m_{\alpha^{i_{j}}}(x)
. Take
0\neq c\in C\cap D
, and consider the corresponding polynomial
c(x)
. Since
c\in C\cap D
, we have
c(x)h^{\ast}(x)=ch^{\ast}_{C}(x)=0
. As
\alpha^{i_{j}}\in\hbox{BZ}(C^{\perp})
, the polynomial
m_{\alpha^{i_{j}}}(x)
divides
h_{C}^{*}(x)
. That is, there exists
q_{1}(x)\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
such that
h_{C}^{\ast}(x)=m_{\alpha^{i_{j}}}(x)q_{1}(x)
. Then

0=c(x)m_{\alpha^{i_{j}}}(x)q_{1}(x)=c(x)m_{\alpha^{i_{j}}}(x)q(x)+c(x)r(x).

Thus,

c(x)r(x)=c(x)m_{\alpha^{i_{j}}}(x)(q_{1}(x)-q(x)),

and as
c(x)\neq 0
,

r(x)=m_{\alpha^{i_{j}}}(x)(q(x)-q_{1}(x)).

This implies that
m_{\alpha^{i_{j}}}(x)\mid r(x)
, which contradicts
\deg r(x)<m_{\alpha^{i_{j}}}(x)
. Therefore
\alpha^{i_{j}}\in\hbox{BZ}((C\cap D)^{\perp})
, i.e.,
\hbox{BZ}(C^{\perp})\cap\hbox{BZ}(D^{\perp})\subseteq\hbox{BZ}((C\cap D)^{\perp})
.
∎

The following theorem characterizes the LCP of cyclic codes
(C,D)
of length
q^{m}-1
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
in terms of the basic dual zeros of
C
and
D
.

Theorem 6
.

Let
n=q^{m}-1
,
C
and
D
be cyclic codes of length
n
. Let
\{{i_{1}},\ldots,{i_{t}}\}
be the set of all leaders of
q

n
, and

\hbox{BZ}(C^{\perp})=T\setminus\hbox{BZ}(D^{\perp})

Proof.

Assume that the pair
(C,D)
is an LCP of codes, and there exists
\alpha^{i_{j}}\in\hbox{BZ}(C^{\perp})
and
\alpha^{i_{j}}\ \notin T\setminus\hbox{BZ}(D^{\perp})

\hbox{BZ}(C^{\perp})=\{\alpha^{i_{j}}\}\cup T_{1}

2

This contradicts the assumption that the pair

Conversely, suppose on the contrary that

(C,D)

\hbox{BZ}(C^{\perp})=T\setminus\hbox{BZ}(D^{\perp})

By assumption,
\hbox{BZ}(C^{\perp})\cup\hbox{BZ}(D^{\perp})
contains all the leaders of
q
-cyclotomic cosets modulo

1
.
∎

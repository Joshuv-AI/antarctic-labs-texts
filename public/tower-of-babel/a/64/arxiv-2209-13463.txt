A constrained mean curvature flow and Alexandrov-Fenchel inequalities
Xinqun Mei, Guofang Wang, Liangjun Weng

Abstract
. In this article, we study a locally constrained mean curvature flow for star-shaped hypersurfaces with capillary boundary in the half-space. We prove its long-time existence and the global convergence to a spherical cap. Furthermore, the capillary quermassintegrals defined in [ 29 ] evolve monotonically along the flow, and hence we establish a class of new Alexandrov-Fenchel inequalities for convex hypersurfaces with capillary boundary in the half-space.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
Primary 53C21, Secondary 35K55, 35K93.

1.
Introduction

The mean curvature flow plays an important role in geometric analysis and differential geometry and has been extensively studied. One of the
classical results proved by Huisken
[
16
]
states that it contracts a closed convex hypersurface into a round point and converges to a sphere after a suitable rescaling. Later he constructed a normalized mean curvature flow to prove the isoperimetric inequality in
[
17
]
. Inspired by the Minkowski formulas for closed hypersurfaces,
Guan-Li
[
11
]
introduced a new locally constrained mean curvature flow to study the isoperimetric problem, which is defined by

(1.1)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}x=(n-\langle x,\nu\rangle H)\nu,\qquad\quad\hbox{ for }x:M\times[0,T)\to\mathbb{R}^{n+1},

where
M
is a
n
-dimensional closed manifold,
H
and
\nu
are the mean curvature and unit outward normal vector of hypersurface
x(M,\cdot)
respectively. Along flow (
1.1
),
the enclosed domain has a fixed volume, while the
area is monotone decreasing by the Minkowski formulas. Guan-Li obtained the long-time existence of this flow and proved that it smoothly converges to a round
sphere if the initial hypersurface is star-shaped.
As a result, it also yields a flow proof of classical Alexandrov-Fenchel inequalities of quermassintegrals in convex geometry.
For more details
see
[
11
, Corollary 1.2]
.
Using similar ideas and new locally constrained curvature flows, there have been a lot of work developed in the last decades. See for instance
[
5
,
6
,
10
,
12
,
13
,
14
,
15
,
26
]
and references therein.

In this paper, we are interested in the isoperimetric inequalities and Alexandrov-Fenchel inequalities for capillary hypersurfaces in the half-space. We first introduce a new locally constrained mean curvature type flow which is suitable for capillary hypersurfaces in the half-space. The flow is also motivated by the Minkowski formula (i.e. (
1.6
)) for capillary hypersurfaces in
\bar{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}_{+}
. Besides, this paper is inspired by a new research on capillary hypersurfaces in
[
29
]
, where the authors establish a class of new Alexandrov-Fenchel inequalities for capillary hypersurfaces in the half-space.

By abusing a little bit the terminology, a capillary hypersurface is defined in this paper as the following.

Definition 1.1
.

A hypersurface in
\bar{{\mathbb{R}}}_{+}^{n+1}
with boundary supported on
\partial\bar{{\mathbb{R}}}_{+}^{n+1}
is called capillary hypersurface if
it intersects with
\partial\bar{{\mathbb{R}}}_{+}^{n+1}
at a constant angle
\theta\in(0,\pi)
.

Let
\Sigma_{t}
be a family of hypersurfaces with boundary in
\bar{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}_{+}
given by a family of isometric embeddings
x(\cdot,t):M\rightarrow\bar{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}_{+}
from a compact
n
-dimensional manifold
M
with boundary
\partial M
(
n\geq 2
) such that

\displaystyle{\rm int}(\Sigma_{t})=x\left({\rm int}(M),t\right)\subset\mathbb{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}_{+},\quad\partial\Sigma_{t}=x(\partial M,t)\subset\partial\mathbb{\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{n+1}.

In this paper, we study the following locally constrained mean curvature flow
for capillary hypersurfaces defined by

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcll}(\partial_{t}x)^{\perp}&=&\left(n+n\cos\theta\langle\nu,e\rangle-H\langle x,\nu\rangle\right)\nu,&\hbox{ in }M\times[0,T),\\
\langle\nu,e\rangle&=&-\cos\theta&\hbox{ on }\partial M\times[0,T),\\
x(\cdot,0)&=&x_{0}(\cdot)&\text{ in }M,\end{array}\right.

where
\nu,H
are the unit normal, the mean curvature of
\Sigma
respectively,
e:=-E_{n+1}=(0,\cdots,0,-1)
is the unit outward normal of
\partial\bar{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}_{+}
in
\bar{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}_{+}
.

Compared to flow (
1.1
), there is an extra term
n\cos\theta\langle\nu,e\rangle
in the speed function
f
of our flow (
1
), which comes naturally from the Minkowski formula, namely

(1.6)

\displaystyle n\int_{\Sigma}\left(1+\cos\theta\langle\nu,e\rangle\right)dA=\int_{\Sigma}H\langle x,\nu\rangle dA,

for any capillary hypersurfaces. For a proof we refer to
[
2
,
29
]
.
There is an interesting family of capillary hypersurfaces, which are the spherical caps lying entirely in
\bar{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}_{+}
and intersecting
\partial{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}_{+}
with a constant contact angle
\theta\in(0,\pi)
given by

(1.7)

\displaystyle C_{\theta,r}:=\Big\{x\in\bar{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}_{+}\big||x-r\cos\theta e|=r\Big\},\penalty\ r\penalty\ \in[0,\infty).

One can easily check that
C_{\theta,r}
is a static solution to flow (
1
), that is

(1.8)

\displaystyle 1+\cos\theta\langle\nu,e\rangle-\frac{H}{n}\langle x,\nu\rangle=0.

In fact,
C_{\theta,r}
consist of all solutions of (
1.8
). See Proposition
3.12
below.
We also denote
\mathbb{S}_{\theta}^{n}
,
\widehat{\partial\mathbb{S}_{\theta}^{n}}
,
{\mathbb{B}}^{n+1}_{\theta}
as

\mathbb{S}^{n}_{\theta}:=\{x\in\mathbb{S}^{n}\,|\,\langle x,E_{n+1}\rangle>\cos\theta\},\quad{\mathbb{B}}^{n+1}_{\theta}:=\{x\in{\mathbb{B}}^{n+1}\,|\,\langle x,E_{n+1}\rangle>\cos\theta\},

{\widehat{\partial\mathbb{S}^{n}_{\theta}}:=\{x\in{\mathbb{B}}^{n+1}\,|\,\langle x,E_{n+1}\rangle=\cos\theta\}.}

Now we state one of the main theorems in this paper.

Theorem 1.2
.

If the initial capillary hypersurface is star-shaped with respect to origin in
\bar{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}_{+}
and the contact angle
\theta\in(0,\pi)
,
then flow (
1
) exists for all time. Moreover,

x(\cdot,t)
smoothly converges to a uniquely determined spherical cap
C_{\theta,r_{0}}
around
e
, as
t\to\infty
.

It is worth noting that we could obtain the uniform a priori estimates for flow (
1
) in the whole range
\theta\in(0,\pi)
instead of only a partial range of contact angle compared with results in
[
28
]
or
[
32
]
.

It is easy to see that flow (
1
) preserves the enclosed volume

\mathcal{V}_{0,\theta}(\widehat{\Sigma}):=|\widehat{\Sigma}|,

by using (
1.6
).
A nice feature of flow (
1
) is that along the flow, the capillary area (one can refer to
[
8
,
24
]
for more interpretation)

\mathcal{V}_{1,\theta}(\widehat{\Sigma}):=\frac{1}{n+1}(|\Sigma|-\cos\theta|\widehat{\partial\Sigma}|),

is decreasing, which follows from
the higher order Minkowski formulas, proved recently in

[
29
, Proposition 2.5]
,

(1.9)

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma}H_{k-1}\left(1+\cos\theta\langle\nu,e\rangle\right)dA=\int_{\Sigma}H_{k}\langle x,\nu\rangle dA,

where
H_{k}
(
1\leq k\leq n
) is the normalized
k
-th mean curvature of
\Sigma\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
and
H_{0}=1
.

As a result, Theorem
1.2
yields a flow proof for capillary isoperimetric inequality for star-shaped hypersurfaces in
\bar{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}_{+}
.

Corollary 1.3
.

For
n\geq 2
, let
\Sigma\subset\bar{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}_{+}
be a star-shaped capillary
hypersurface with a contact angle
\theta\in(0,\pi)
,
then
there holds

(1.10)

\displaystyle\frac{|\Sigma|-\cos\theta|\widehat{\partial\Sigma}|}{|{\mathbb{S}}_{\theta}^{n}|-\cos\theta|{\widehat{\partial\mathbb{S}^{n}_{\theta}}}|}\geq\left(\frac{|\widehat{\Sigma}|}{|{\mathbb{B}}^{n+1}_{\theta}|}\right)^{\frac{n}{n+1}}.

Moreover, equality holds if and only if
\Sigma
is a spherical cap in (
1.7
).

The capillary isoperimetric inequality holds true in fact for any hypersurface in
\bar{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}_{+}
. For the proof we refer to

[
22
, Chapter 19]
, using the spherical symmetrization.

Recently, as the generalization of the capillary area
\mathcal{V}_{1,\theta}(\widehat{\Sigma})
, a class of new quermassintegrals
\mathcal{V}_{k,\theta}(\widehat{\Sigma})
for capillary hypersurfaces
\Sigma\subset\bar{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}_{+}
have been introduced in
[
29
]
, which are, for
1\leq k\leq n
,

(1.11)

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}_{k+1,\theta}(\widehat{\Sigma})

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n+1}\left(\int_{\Sigma}H_{k}dA-\frac{\cos\theta\sin^{k}\theta}{n}\int_{\partial\Sigma}H_{k-1}^{\partial\Sigma}ds\right),

where
H_{k-1}^{\partial\Sigma}
is the normalized
(k-1)
-th mean curvature of
\partial\Sigma\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Another motivation to
study flow (
1
) is to establish the following Alexandrov-Fenchel inequalities.

Theorem 1.4
.

For
n\geq 2
, let
\Sigma\subset\bar{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}_{+}
be a convex capillary hypersurface with a contact angle
\theta\in(0,\frac{{\pi}}{2}]
, then
there holds

(1.12)

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\mathcal{V}_{k,\theta}(\widehat{\Sigma})}{\textbf{b}_{\theta}}\right)^{\frac{1}{n+1-k}}\geq\left(\frac{\mathcal{V}_{0,\theta}(\widehat{\Sigma})}{\textbf{b}_{\theta}}\right)^{\frac{1}{n+1}},\quad\forall\,1\leq k<n,

where
\mathbf{b}_{\theta}
is the
(n+1)
dimensional volume of
{\mathbb{B}}^{n+1}_{\theta}
, with equality if and only if
\Sigma
is a spherical cap in (
1.7
).

Note that (
1.12
) gives partially an affirmative answer to
a question in
[
29
, Conjecture 1.5]
, where the authors proved

\displaystyle\frac{\mathcal{V}_{n,\theta}(\widehat{\Sigma})}{\textbf{b}_{\theta}}\geq\left(\frac{\mathcal{V}_{k,\theta}(\widehat{\Sigma})}{\textbf{b}_{\theta}}\right)^{\frac{1}{n+1-k}},\quad\forall\,1\leq k<n,

by using a locally constrained inverse curvature type flow.
When
k=1
in (
1.12
), it is the capillary isoperimetric inequality considered above. We remark that the restriction of range
\theta\in(0,\frac{\pi}{2}]
is only used in showing the convexity preserving property along the flow (
1
). See Proposition
3.13
or Eq. (
3.13
).

As a direct consequence of Theorem
1.4
, for
k=2
in (
1.12
), we obtain a volumetric Minkowski inequality for convex
capillary hypersurfaces in
\bar{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}_{+}
.

Corollary 1.5
.

For
n\geq 2
, let
\Sigma\subset\bar{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}_{+}
be a convex capillary hypersurface with a contact angle
{\theta\in(0,\frac{{\pi}}{2}]}
, then
there holds

(1.13)

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma}HdA-\sin\theta\cos\theta|\partial\Sigma|\geq n(n+1)\mathbf{b}_{\theta}^{\frac{2}{n+1}}|\widehat{\Sigma}|^{\frac{n-1}{n+1}},

with equality if and only if
\Sigma
is a spherical cap in (
1.7
).

It is remarkable to note that Agostiniani-Fogagnolo-Mazzieri obtained a volumetric Minkowski inequality in
[
1
, Theorem 1.5]
without any convexity assumption for closed hypersurfaces in Euclidean space, see also
[
4
,
9
,
23
]
for the relevant results. We expect that (
1.13
) holds true for general capillary hypersurfaces without any convexity assumption by replacing
\int_{\Sigma}HdA
with
\int_{\Sigma}|H|dA
.

We end the introduction by mentioning some interesting results on curvature flows with free or capillary boundaries in the Euclidean space. The classical mean curvature flow with free boundary was studied by Stahl in
[
27
]
, which showed that evolving a strictly convex, embedded hypersurface in the interior of a ball or half-space by mean curvature flow with free boundary produces a Type I singularity which, after rescaling, is asymptotically hemispherical. See also
[
3
,
7
]
for further analysis of the behavior of singularities that develop on the free boundary of the evolving hypersurface. Lambert-Scheuer
[
20
]
proved that the inverse mean curvature flow for hypersurfaces with free boundary in a ball would drive a strictly convex hypersurfaces into a flat disk. Recently, Wang-Xia
[
30
]
studied a locally constrained mean curvature type flow for embedded hypersurface with free boundary in the unit ball and showed that it converges to a spherical cap, which motivated by a new Minkowski formula in
[
31
]
. See also
[
28
]
for the capillary case. Furthermore, Scheuer-Wang-Xia
[
25
]
introduced a new inverse nonlinear curvature flow for embedded hypersurfaces with free boundary in the ball and obtained a class of new Alexandrov-Fenchel inequalities after obtaining that flow converges to a spherical cap. See also
[
29
,
32
]
for the very recent development of hypersurfaces with capillary boundary.

In Section

) and prove its long-time existence and smooth

1.2
. In Section

), which implies the Alexandrov-Fenchel inequalities, that is, Theorem
1.4
.

2.
Capillary hypersurface in half-space

Let
\Sigma\subset\bar{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}_{+}
be a smooth, properly embedded capillary hypersurface, given by the embedding
x:M\to\bar{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}_{+}
, where
M
is a compact, orientable smooth manifold of dimension
n
with non-empty boundary.
If there is no confusion, we do not distinguish
\Sigma

and the embedding
x
.
Let
\mu
be the unit outward co-normal of
\partial\Sigma
in
\Sigma
and
\overline{\nu}
be the unit normal to
\partial\Sigma
in
\partial\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}
such that
\{\nu,\mu\}
and
\{\overline{\nu},e\}
have the same orientation in normal bundle of
\partial\Sigma\subset\bar{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}_{+}
, where
e:=-E_{n+1}
. We define
the contact angle
\theta
between the hypersurface
\Sigma
and the support
\partial\bar{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}_{+}
by

\langle\nu,e\rangle=\cos(\pi-\theta).

It follows

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{rcl}e&=&\sin\theta\mu-\cos\theta\nu,\\
\overline{\nu}&=&\cos\theta\mu+\sin\theta\nu.\end{array}

We use
D
to denote the Levi-Civita connection of
\bar{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}_{+}
w.r.t the Euclidean metric
\delta
,
and
\nabla
the Levi-Civita connection on
\Sigma
w.r.t the induced metric
g
from the immersion
x
.
The operator
{\rm div},\Delta
, and
\nabla^{2}
are the divergence, Laplacian, and Hessian operator on
\Sigma
respectively.
The second fundamental form
h
of
x
is defined by

D_{X}Y=\nabla_{X}Y-h(X,Y)\nu.

Denote
\kappa=(\kappa_{1},\kappa_{2},\cdots,\kappa_{n})
be the set of principal curvatures, i.e, the set of eigenvalues of
h
. For other notations, we following the paper
[
29
]
.

The second fundamental form of
\partial\Sigma
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is given by

\widehat{h}(X,Y):=-\langle\nabla^{\mathbb{R}^{n}}_{X}Y,\bar{\nu}\rangle=-\langle D_{X}Y,\bar{\nu}\rangle,\quad X,Y\in T(\partial\Sigma).

The second equality holds since
\langle\bar{\nu},\bar{N}\circ x\rangle=0
.
The second fundamental form of
\partial\Sigma
in
\Sigma
is given by

\widetilde{h}(X,Y):=-\langle\nabla_{X}Y,\mu\rangle=-\langle D_{X}Y,\mu\rangle,\quad X,Y\in T(\partial\Sigma).

The second equality holds since
\langle\nu,\mu\rangle=0
. Besides,
\widehat{h},\widetilde{h}
and
h
have some nice relationships, cf.
[
29
, Proposition 2.3]
.

Let
\Sigma_{t}
be a family of smoothly embedded hypersurfaces with
\theta
-capillary boundary in
\bar{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}_{+}
, given by the embeddings
x(\cdot,t):M\rightarrow\bar{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}_{+}
, which evolves by the general flow

(2.4)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}x=f\nu+T,

with some normal speed function
f
and
T\in T\Sigma_{t}
. In this paper,
f
consists mainly of mean curvature, and hence (
2.4
) is a mean curvature type flow, where
T
is added such that flow preserves the capillary condition.

Along flow (
2.4
), we have the following evolution equations for the induced metric
g_{ij}
, the unit outward normal
\nu
, the second fundamental form
h_{ij}
, the Weingarten
matrix
h^{i}_{j}
, the mean curvature
H
of the hypersurfaces
\Sigma_{t}
. These evolution equations have been shown in
[
32
, Proposition 2.11]
and will be used in deriving the a priori estimates.

Proposition 2.1

(
[
32
]
)
.

Along flow (
2.4
), it holds that

(1)

\partial_{t}g_{ij}=2fh_{ij}+\nabla_{i}T_{j}+\nabla_{j}T_{i}
.

(2)

\partial_{t}\nu=-\nabla f+h(e_{i},T)e_{i}
.

(3)

\partial_{t}h_{ij}=-\nabla^{2}_{ij}f+fh_{ik}h_{j}^{k}+\nabla_{T}h_{ij}+h_{j}^{k}\nabla_{i}T_{k}+h_{i}^{k}\nabla_{j}T_{k}.

(4)

\partial_{t}h^{i}_{j}=-\nabla^{i}\nabla_{j}f-fh_{j}^{k}h^{i}_{k}+\nabla_{T}h^{i}_{j}.

(5)

\partial_{t}H=-\Delta f-|h|^{2}f+\nabla_{T}H
.

3.
A priori estimate

In this section, we obtain the uniform a priori estimates for flow (
1
).

3.1.
Further evolution equations

We need evolution equations for various geometric quantities.
For simplicity, we introduce the linearized operator with respect to (
1
) as

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}:=\partial_{t}-\langle x,\nu\rangle\Delta-\langle T+Hx-n\cos\theta e,\nabla\rangle.

We derive the evolution equations for various geometric quantities
under flow (
1
). That is, the normal speed function in (
2.4
) is chosen to be

f:=n\left(1+\cos\theta\langle\nu,e\rangle\right)-H\langle x,\nu\rangle.

Proposition 3.1
.

Along flow (
1
), the support function
u=\langle x,\nu\rangle
satisfies

(3.1)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}u=n+n\cos\theta\langle\nu,e\rangle-2uH+u^{2}|h|^{2},

and

(3.2)

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mu}u=\cot\theta h(\mu,\mu)u,\quad{\rm on}\penalty\ \partial\Sigma_{t}.

Proof.

Direct computation yields

\displaystyle\nabla_{i}u=h_{ik}\langle x,e_{k}\rangle,

and

\displaystyle\nabla_{ij}u=h_{ij;k}\langle x,e_{k}\rangle+h_{ij}-u(h^{2})_{ij},

then

\displaystyle\Delta u=\langle x,\nabla H\rangle+H-u|h|^{2}.

In view of these formulas and Proposition
2.1
, we have

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\partial_{t}x,\nu\rangle+\langle x,\partial_{t}\nu\rangle=f-\langle x,\nabla f\rangle+h(x^{T},T)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(n+n\cos\theta\langle\nu,e\rangle-uH\right)-n\cos\theta\left<x,\nabla\langle\nu,e\rangle\right>+u\langle x,\nabla H\rangle

\displaystyle+\langle Hx,\nabla u\rangle+h(x^{T},T)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle x,\nu\rangle\Delta u-n\langle\cos\theta e,\nabla u\rangle+\langle Hx,\nabla u\rangle+\left(n+n\cos\theta\langle\nu,e\rangle-2uH\right)

\displaystyle+u^{2}|h|^{2}+\langle T,\nabla u\rangle,

that is,

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}u=n+n\cos\theta\langle\nu,e\rangle-2uH+u^{2}|h|^{2}.

On
\partial\Sigma_{t}
, since
\mu
is a principal direction of
\Sigma
by
[
29
, Proposition 2.3]
, together with (
2
), it yields

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mu}\langle x,\nu\rangle=\langle x,h(\mu,\mu)\mu\rangle=\cos\theta h(\mu,\mu)\langle x,\bar{\nu}\rangle=\cot\theta h(\mu,\mu)\langle x,\nu\rangle.

∎

For later use, now we introduce a new function.

Definition 3.2
.

We call the function defined by

(3.3)

\displaystyle\bar{u}=\frac{\langle x,\nu\rangle}{1+\cos\theta\langle\nu,e\rangle},

the
capillary support function
of capillary hypersurface
\Sigma
in
\bar{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}_{+}
.

It is interesting to see that the capillary support function has nice properties.

Proposition 3.3
.

Along flow (
1
), the capillary support function
\bar{u}
satisfies

(3.4)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}\bar{u}=n-2\bar{u}H+\bar{u}^{2}|h|^{2}+2u\left\langle\nabla\bar{u},\frac{\nabla(1+\cos\theta\langle\nu,e\rangle)}{1+\cos\theta\langle\nu,e\rangle}\right\rangle,

and

(3.5)

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mu}\bar{u}=0,\quad\text{ on }\partial\Sigma_{t}.

Proof.

From the Codazzi formula, we have

(3.6)

\displaystyle\langle\nu,e\rangle_{;kl}=h_{kl;s}\langle e_{s},e\rangle-(h^{2})_{kl}\langle\nu,e\rangle,

which yields

(3.7)

\displaystyle\Delta\langle\nu,e\rangle=\langle\nabla H,e\rangle-|h|^{2}\langle\nu,e\rangle.

Combining it with

\displaystyle\langle\nabla f,e\rangle=n\cos\theta h(e^{T},e^{T})-\langle x,\nu\rangle\langle\nabla H,e\rangle-Hh(x^{T},e^{T}),

we obtain

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\langle\nu,e\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\langle\nabla f,e\rangle+h(e_{i},T)\langle e_{i},e\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-n\cos\theta h(e^{T},e^{T})+u\langle\nabla H,e\rangle+Hh(x^{T},e^{T})+h(T,e^{T})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle u\left(\Delta\langle\nu,e\rangle+|h|^{2}\langle\nu,e\rangle\right)-n\cos\theta h(e^{T},e^{T})+Hh(x^{T},e^{T})+h(T,e^{T}).

Thus we conclude that

(3.8)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}\langle\nu,e\rangle=u|h|^{2}\langle\nu,e\rangle.

Recall
\bar{u}:=\frac{u}{1+\cos\theta\langle\nu,e\rangle}
. (
3.1
) and (
3.8
) yield

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}\bar{u}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{1+\cos\theta\langle\nu,e\rangle}\mathcal{L}u-\frac{\langle x,\nu\rangle}{(1+\cos\theta\langle\nu,e\rangle)^{2}}\mathcal{L}(1+\cos\theta\langle\nu,e\rangle)

\displaystyle-\langle x,\nu\rangle\left(\frac{2u|\nabla(1+\cos\theta\langle\nu,e\rangle)|^{2}}{(1+\cos\theta\langle\nu,e\rangle)^{3}}-2\frac{\langle x,\nu_{;i}\rangle\cos\theta\langle\nu_{;i},e\rangle}{(1+\cos\theta\langle\nu,e\rangle)^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle n-\frac{2uH}{1+\cos\theta\langle\nu,e\rangle}+\frac{u^{2}|h|^{2}}{(1+\cos\theta\langle\nu,e\rangle)^{2}}+2u\left\langle\nabla\bar{u},\frac{\nabla(1+\cos\theta\langle\nu,e\rangle)}{1+\cos\theta\langle\nu,e\rangle}\right\rangle.

Since
\mu
is a principal direction of
\Sigma
by
[
29
, Proposition 2.3]
,
we have on
\partial\Sigma_{t}

(3.9)

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mu}\left(1+\cos\theta\langle\nu,e\rangle\right)=-\sin\theta h(\mu,\mu)\langle\nu,e\rangle=\cot\theta h(\mu,\mu)(1+\cos\theta\langle\nu,e\rangle),

which, together with (
3.2
), implies

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mu}\bar{u}=0.

∎

Now we compute the evolution equation for the mean curvature
H
.

Proposition 3.4
.

Along flow (
1
), we have

(3.10)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}H=2\langle\nabla H,\nabla u\rangle-n|h|^{2}+H^{2},

and

(3.11)

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mu}H=0,\quad{\rm on}\penalty\ \partial\Sigma_{t}.

Proof.

From (
3.6
)
it is easy to see

\displaystyle f_{;ij}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle n\cos\theta h_{ij;k}\langle e_{k},e\rangle-n\cos\theta(h^{2})_{ij}\langle\nu,e\rangle-H_{;ij}\langle x,\nu\rangle-H_{;i}h_{jk}\langle x,e_{k}\rangle

\displaystyle-H_{;j}h_{ik}\langle x,e_{k}\rangle-Hh_{ij}-Hh_{ij;k}\langle x,e_{k}\rangle+H(h^{2})_{ij}\langle x,\nu\rangle.

Combining it with Proposition
2.1
(6), we have

\displaystyle\partial_{t}H

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\Delta f-f|h|^{2}+\langle\nabla H,T\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\langle n\cos\theta e,\nabla H\rangle+n\cos\theta\langle\nu,e\rangle|h|^{2}+\langle x,\nu\rangle\Delta H+2H_{;i}h_{ik}\langle x,e_{k}\rangle

\displaystyle+\langle Hx,\nabla H\rangle-uH|h|^{2}-(n+n\cos\theta\langle\nu,e\rangle-Hu)|h|^{2}

\displaystyle+\langle\nabla H,T\rangle+H^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle x,\nu\rangle\Delta H+\langle Hx+T-n\cos\theta e,\nabla H\rangle+2H_{;i}h_{ik}\langle x,e_{k}\rangle-n|h|^{2}+H^{2},

that is,

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}H=2\langle\nabla H,\nabla u\rangle-n|h|^{2}+H^{2}.

It has been shown in
[

, Proposition 4.3]
, on
\partial\Sigma_{t}

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mu}f=\cot\theta h(\mu,\mu)f.

Combining with (

) and (

), altogether implies

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mu}H

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mu}\left(\frac{n(1+\cos\theta\langle\nu,e\rangle)-f}{\langle x,\nu\rangle}\right)=0.

∎

If a hypersurface is strictly convex, we let
(b^{ij})
denote the inverse of
(h_{ij})
, and set

\bar{H}:=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{\kappa_{i}}=g_{ij}b^{ji},

which is the harmonic curvature. The harmonic curvature satisfies the following evolution equation,
together a nice boundary inequality, provided that
\theta\in(0,\frac{\pi}{2}]
. It will be used to prove that the flow preserves the convexity.

Proposition 3.5
.

Along flow (
1
),
\bar{H}
satisfies

(3.12)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}\bar{H}=-2u(b^{ii})^{2}b^{jj}h_{ij;k}^{2}-2b^{i}_{k}H_{;k}\langle x,e_{i}\rangle-u|h|^{2}\bar{H}-H\bar{H}+nH\langle x,\nu\rangle+n^{2}.

For
\theta\in(0,\frac{\pi}{2}]
, it holds

(3.13)

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mu}\bar{H}\leq 0,\quad{\rm on}\penalty\ \partial\Sigma_{t}.

Proof.

Direct computation yields

\displaystyle\nabla_{k}\bar{H}=-b^{is}\nabla_{k}h_{st}b^{t}_{i},

and

(3.14)

\displaystyle\Delta\bar{H}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-b^{is}\Delta h_{st}b^{t}_{i}+2b^{ip}\nabla^{k}h_{pq}b^{qs}\nabla_{k}h_{st}b^{t}_{i}.

The Ricci equation and the Codazzi equation yield

\displaystyle h_{kl;ii}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle h_{ki;li}=h_{ki;il}+R^{p}_{ili}h_{pk}+R^{p}_{kli}h_{pi}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle h_{ii;kl}+(h_{pl}H-h_{pi}h_{li})h_{pk}+(h_{pl}h_{ki}-h_{pi}h_{kl})h_{pi}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle H_{;kl}+h_{pk}h_{pl}H-|h|^{2}h_{kl},

and hence

\displaystyle H_{;ij}=\Delta(h_{ij})-H(h^{2})_{ij}+|h|^{2}h_{ij}.

Together with (
3.14
), it implies

\displaystyle b^{i}_{k}b^{l}_{i}H_{;kl}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle b^{i}_{k}b^{l}_{i}\big(\Delta(h_{kl})-H(h^{2})_{kl}+|h|^{2}h_{kl}\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\Delta\bar{H}+2(b^{ii})^{2}b^{jj}h_{ij;k}^{2}-nH+|h|^{2}\bar{H}.

On the other hand,

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\bar{H}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\partial_{t}b^{i}_{i}=-b^{i}_{k}\partial_{t}h_{l}^{k}b^{l}_{i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-b^{i}_{k}\left(-\nabla^{k}\nabla_{l}f-fh_{l}^{p}h^{k}_{p}+\nabla_{T}h^{k}_{l}\right)b^{l}_{i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle b^{i}_{k}b^{l}_{i}\nabla^{k}\nabla_{l}f+nf-b^{i}_{k}\nabla_{T}h^{k}_{l}b^{l}_{i}.

Using again

\displaystyle\langle x,\nu\rangle_{;kl}

\displaystyle=h_{kl}+h_{kl;s}\langle x,e_{s}\rangle-(h^{2})_{kl}\langle x,\nu\rangle,

and

\displaystyle\langle\nu,e\rangle_{;kl}=h_{kl;s}\langle e_{s},e\rangle-(h^{2})_{kl}\langle\nu,e\rangle,

we obtain

\displaystyle\nabla^{k}\nabla_{l}f

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\nabla^{k}\nabla_{l}(n+n\cos\theta\langle\nu,e\rangle-H\langle x,\nu\rangle)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle n\cos\theta(h_{kl;s}\langle e_{s},e\rangle-(h^{2})_{kl}\langle\nu,e\rangle)-H_{;k}\langle x,h_{ls}e_{s}\rangle-H_{;l}\langle x,h_{ks}e_{s}\rangle

\displaystyle-H_{;kl}\langle x,\nu\rangle-H(h_{kl}+h_{kl;s}\langle x,e_{s}\rangle-(h^{2})_{kl}\langle x,\nu\rangle),

and

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\bar{H}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle b^{i}_{k}b^{l}_{i}\Big[n\cos\theta\Big(h_{kl;s}\langle e_{s},e\rangle-(h^{2})_{kl}\langle\nu,e\rangle\Big)-H_{;k}\langle x,h_{ls}e_{s}\rangle-H_{;l}\langle x,h_{ks}e_{s}\rangle

\displaystyle-H_{;kl}\langle x,\nu\rangle-H\big(h_{kl}+h_{kl;s}\langle x,e_{s}\rangle-(h^{2})_{kl}\langle x,\nu\rangle\big)\Big]+n^{2}(1+\cos\theta\langle\nu,e\rangle)

\displaystyle-nH\langle x,\nu\rangle+\langle T,\nabla\bar{H}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-u\big(-\Delta\bar{H}+2(b^{ii})^{2}b^{jj}h_{ij;k}^{2}-nH+|h|^{2}\bar{H}\big)-n\cos\theta\langle e,\nabla\bar{H}\rangle

\displaystyle-n^{2}\cos\theta\langle\nu,e\rangle-2b^{i}_{k}H_{;k}\langle x,e_{i}\rangle-H\bar{H}+H\langle x,\nabla\bar{H}\rangle+nH\langle x,\nu\rangle

\displaystyle+n^{2}(1+\cos\theta\langle\nu,e\rangle)-nH\langle x,\nu\rangle+\langle T,\nabla\bar{H}\rangle.

It follows

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}\bar{H}=-2u(b^{ii})^{2}b^{jj}h_{ij;k}^{2}-2b^{i}_{k}H_{;k}\langle x,e_{i}\rangle-u|h|^{2}\bar{H}-H\bar{H}+nH\langle x,\nu\rangle+n^{2}.

Along
\partial\Sigma_{t}
, choosing an orthonormal frame
\{e_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha=2}^{n}
of

such that

forms an orthonormal frame for

, from
[
29
, Proposition 2.3 (3)]
, we know

\displaystyle h_{\alpha\alpha;\mu}=\cot\theta h_{\alpha\alpha}(h_{11}-h_{\alpha\alpha}),

which, together with (
3.11
) and

, implies

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mu}\bar{H}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-(b^{11})^{2}h_{11;\mu}-\sum_{\alpha=2}^{n}(b^{\alpha\alpha})^{2}h_{\alpha\alpha;\mu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\alpha=2}^{n}\left[(b^{11})^{2}-(b^{\alpha\alpha})^{2}\right]h_{\alpha\alpha;\mu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\cot\theta\sum_{\alpha=2}^{n}h_{\alpha\alpha}(h_{11}-h_{\alpha\alpha})\left[(b^{11})^{2}-(b^{\alpha\alpha})^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 0.

∎

3.2.
Gradient estimates and the convergence

One can show the short-time existence of flow (
1
) for any capillary hypersurface by using the method given in
[
18
]
. Since we consider here only for capillary hypersurfaces of star-shaped, i.e.,
\langle x,\nu\rangle>0
on
\Sigma_{0}
, we can reduce flow to a scalar flow, i.e., (
3.2
),
and hence the short-time existence follows easily. For the convenience of the reader and for our use later, we present the reduction argument here.

Assume that a capillary hypersurfaces

\Sigma\subset\bar{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}_{+}
is strictly star-shaped with respect to the origin.
One can reparametrize it as a graph over
\overline{\mathbb{S}}^{n}_{+}
. Namely,
there exists a positive smooth function
\rho\in C^{\infty}
defined on
\overline{\mathbb{S}}^{n}_{+}
such that

\displaystyle\Sigma=\left\{\rho(X)X|X\in\overline{\mathbb{S}}^{n}_{+}\right\},

where
X:=(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n})
is a local coordinate of
\overline{\mathbb{S}}^{n}_{+}
.

We denote
\nabla^{0}
as the Levi-Civita connection on
\mathbb{S}^{n}_{+}
with respect to the standard round metric
\sigma:=g_{{}_{\mathbb{S}^{n}_{+}}}
,
\partial_{i}:=\partial_{X_{i}}
,
\sigma_{ij}:=\sigma(\partial_{i},\partial_{j})
,
\rho_{i}:=\nabla^{0}_{i}\rho
, and
\rho_{ij}:=\nabla^{0}_{i}\nabla^{0}_{j}\rho
.
Moreover we denote
\partial_{\beta}
the unit outward normal of
\partial\mathbb{S}^{n}_{+}
in
\overline{\mathbb{S}}^{n}_{+}
. The induced metric
g
on
\Sigma
is given by

\displaystyle g_{ij}=\rho^{2}\sigma_{ij}+\rho_{i}\rho_{j}=e^{2\varphi}\left(\sigma_{ij}+\varphi_{i}\varphi_{j}\right),

where
\varphi(X):=\log\rho(X)
. Its inverse
g^{-1}
is given by

\displaystyle g^{ij}=\frac{1}{\rho^{2}}\left(\sigma^{ij}-\frac{\rho^{i}\rho^{j}}{\rho^{2}+|\nabla^{0}\rho|^{2}}\right)=e^{-2\varphi}\left(\sigma^{ij}-\frac{\varphi^{i}\varphi^{j}}{v^{2}}\right),

where
\rho^{i}:=\sigma^{ij}\rho_{j}
,
\varphi^{i}:=\sigma^{ij}\varphi_{j}
and
v:=\sqrt{1+|\nabla^{0}\varphi|^{2}}
.
The unit outward normal vector field on
\Sigma
is given by

\displaystyle\nu=\frac{1}{v}\left(\partial_{\rho}-\rho^{-2}\nabla^{0}\rho\right)=\frac{1}{v}\left(\partial_{\rho}-\rho^{-1}\nabla^{0}\varphi\right).

Hence the capillary condition, i.e.
\Sigma
intersects with
\partial\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}
at an angle
\theta
, is
expressed by

\displaystyle-\cos\theta=\langle\nu,\frac{1}{\rho}\partial_{\beta}\rangle=-\frac{1}{v}\nabla^{0}_{\partial_{\beta}}\varphi,

which is equivalent to

\nabla^{0}_{\partial_{\beta}}\varphi=\cos\theta\sqrt{1+|\nabla^{0}\varphi|^{2}}.

The second fundamental form
h
on
\Sigma
is

\displaystyle h_{ij}=\frac{e^{\varphi}}{v}\left(\sigma_{ij}+\varphi_{i}\varphi_{j}-\varphi_{ij}\right),

and its Weingarten matrix
(h^{i}_{j})
is

\displaystyle h_{j}^{i}=g^{ik}h_{kj}=\frac{1}{e^{\varphi}v}\left[\delta^{i}_{j}-(\sigma^{ik}-\frac{\varphi^{i}\varphi^{k}}{v^{2}})\varphi_{kj}\right].

We also have for the mean curvature,

(3.15)

\displaystyle H=\frac{1}{e^{\varphi}v}\left[n-\left(\sigma^{ij}-\frac{\varphi^{i}\varphi^{j}}{v^{2}}\right)\varphi_{ij}\right].

Now under the assumption of star-shapedness, the
flow (
1
)
can be reduced to a scalar evolution equation for
\varphi(z,t)=\log\rho(X(z,t),t)
,

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{rcll}\partial_{t}\varphi&=&F,&\text{ in }\mathbb{S}^{n}_{+}\times[0,T^{*}),\\
\nabla_{\partial_{\beta}}^{0}\varphi&=&\cos\theta\sqrt{1+|\nabla^{0}\varphi|^{2}},&\text{ on }\partial\mathbb{S}^{n}_{+}\times[0,T^{*}),\\
\varphi(\cdot,0)&=&\varphi_{0}(\cdot),&\text{ on }\mathbb{S}^{n}_{+},\end{array}

where
\varphi_{0}
is the parameterization radial function of
x_{0}(M)
over
\overline{\mathbb{S}}^{n}_{+}
, and

\displaystyle F:=\frac{1}{e^{\varphi}}{\rm div}_{{}_{\mathbb{S}^{n}_{+}}}\left(\frac{\nabla^{0}\varphi}{v}\right)+\frac{v}{e^{\varphi}}\left[1-\frac{\cos\theta}{v}\left(\cos\beta+\sin\beta\nabla^{0}_{\partial_{\beta}}\varphi\right)-\frac{n}{v^{2}}\right].

The short-time existence of this scalar flow follows from the standard PDE theory. It is clear that it provides
the short-time existence of flow (
1
). Now assume
T^{*}>0
is
the maximal time of existence of a solution to (
1
),
in the class of star-shaped hypersurfaces.

The star-shapedness of
\Sigma_{0}
implies that there exist some
0<r_{1}<r_{2}<\infty
, such that

\Sigma_{0}\subset\widehat{C_{\theta,r_{2}}}\setminus\widehat{C_{\theta,r_{1}}}.

Following the argument in
[
29
, Proposition 4.2]
, which is based on the avoidable principle, we have

Proposition 3.6
.

For any
t\in[0,T^{*})
, along flow (
1
), it holds

(3.20)

\displaystyle\Sigma_{t}\subset\widehat{C_{\theta,r_{2}}}\setminus\ \widehat{C_{\theta,r_{1}}}.

where
C_{\theta,r}
defined by (
1.7
) and
r_{1},r_{2},c_{0}
only depend on
\Sigma_{0}
.

Next we show the star-shapedness is preserved along flow (
1
), which follows the uniform gradient estimate of
\varphi
in (
3.2
).

Proposition 3.7
.

Let
\Sigma_{0}
be a star-shaped hypersurface with capillary boundary in
\bar{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}_{+}
, and
\theta\in(0,\pi)
, then there exists
c_{0}>0
depending only on
\Sigma_{0}
, such that

(3.21)

\displaystyle\langle x,\nu\rangle(p,t)\geq c_{0}.

for all
(p,t)\in M\times[0,T^{*})
.

Proof.

From Proposition
3.3
and
|h|^{2}\geq\frac{H^{2}}{n}
, we see

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}\bar{u}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle n-\frac{2uH}{1+\cos\theta\langle\nu,e\rangle}+\frac{u^{2}|h|^{2}}{(1+\cos\theta\langle\nu,e\rangle)^{2}}\quad\text{ mod}\quad\nabla\bar{u}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\left(\frac{u|H|}{\sqrt{n}(1+\cos\theta\langle\nu,e\rangle)}-\sqrt{n}\right)^{2}\geq 0,

together with with
\nabla_{\mu}\bar{u}=0
on
\partial M
, it implies

\displaystyle\bar{u}\geq\min_{M}\bar{u}(\cdot,0).

Since
1+\cos\theta\langle\nu,e\rangle\geq 1-\cos\theta>0
, we conclude

\displaystyle\langle x,\nu\rangle=\bar{u}\cdot(1+\cos\theta\langle\nu,e\rangle)\geq c_{0},

for some positive constant
c_{0}>0
independent of
t
.
∎

Proposition 3.8
.

If the initial hypersurface
\Sigma_{0}\subset\bar{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}_{+}
is star-shaped, then flow (
1
) exists for all time. Moreover, it smoothly converges to a uniquely determined spherical cap
C_{\theta,r}(e)
given by (
1.7
) with capillary boundary, as
t\rightarrow+\infty
.

Proof.

From (
3.20
) we have a uniform bound for
\varphi
, and from (
3.21
), we have a uniform bound for
v
, and hence a bound for
\nabla^{0}\varphi
. Therefore

\varphi
is uniformly bounded in
C^{1}(\bar{\mathbb{S}}^{n}_{+}\times[0,T^{*}))
and the scalar equation in (
3.2
) is uniformly parabolic. Since
|\cos\theta|<1
, from the standard quasi-linear parabolic theory with a strictly oblique boundary condition theory (cf.
[
19
,

]
), we conclude the uniform

estimates and the long-time existence. And the convergence can be shown similarly by using the argument as in
[
25
,
32
,
29
]
, we omit it here.
∎

3.3.
Preserving Convexity

In this subsection, we show flow (
1
) preserves the convexity and finish the proof of Theorem
1.4
. First, we show the uniform upper bound of

along flow (
1

Proposition 3.9
.

If
\Sigma_{t}
solves flow (
1

\displaystyle H(p,t)\leq\max_{M}H(\cdot,0),\quad\quad\forall(p,t)\in M\times[0,T^{*}).

Proof.

From equation (
3.10
) and
n|h|^{2}\geq H^{2}

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}H\leq 0,\quad\text{ mod }\nabla H,

and

on

Next we show the uniform lower bound of mean curvature, that is, the mean convexity is preserved along flow (
1

Proposition 3.10
.

If
\Sigma_{t}
solves flow (
1

\Sigma_{0}

\displaystyle H(p,t)\geq C,\quad\quad\forall(p,t)\in M\times[0,T^{*}),

where the positive constant
C
depends on the initial datum.

Proof.

Define the function

P:=H\bar{u}.

Using (
3.5
) and (
3.11

(3.23)

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mu}P=0,\quad\text{ on }\partial M.

Using (
3.4
) and (

), we obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}P

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bar{u}\mathcal{L}H+H\mathcal{L}\bar{u}-2u\langle\nabla\bar{u},\nabla H\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bar{u}^{2}H|h|^{2}-\bar{u}H^{2}-n\bar{u}|h|^{2}+nH+2\bar{u}\langle\nabla(1+\cos\theta\langle\nu,e\rangle),\nabla P\rangle.

\displaystyle\bar{u}H(p_{0},t)\geq c,

solves flow (

\displaystyle\min_{1\leq i\leq n}\kappa_{i}(p,t)\geq c,\quad\quad\forall(p,t)\in M\times[0,T^{*}),

\Sigma_{0}

It is equivalent to show the uniform upper bound for
\bar{H}
.

\bar{H}

\nabla_{\mu}\bar{H}\leq 0

\bar{H}

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j}b^{jj}h_{ij;k}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j}\frac{1}{H}\left(1+\sum_{l\neq j}\frac{h_{ll}}{h_{jj}}\right)\left(h_{jj;k}^{2}+\sum_{i\neq j}h_{ij;k}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\frac{H_{;k}^{2}}{H},

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}\bar{H}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-2H^{-1}u(b^{ii})^{2}H_{;i}^{2}-2b^{ii}H_{;i}\langle x,e_{i}\rangle-u|h|^{2}\bar{H}-H\bar{H}+n^{2}+nuH

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{Hu^{-1}|x^{T}|^{2}}{2}-u|h|^{2}\bar{H}-H\bar{H}+n^{2}+nuH,

which
together with Proposition
3.10
, implies
\bar{H}\leq C
, the desired estimate. Hence we complete the proof.
∎

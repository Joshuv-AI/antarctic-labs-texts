Let

As exemplified by Auslander that concepts in the study of representation
theory of Artin algebras have natural analogues in study of Cohen-Macaulay local rings. In this paper we study

2

rings.

Let
(A,\mathfrak{m})
be a Noetherian local ring.
We index complexes of
A
-modules cohomologically

\mathbf{X}_{\bullet}\colon\cdots\rightarrow\mathbf{X}_{\bullet}^{n-1}\xrightarrow{\partial_{n-1}}\mathbf{X}_{\bullet}^{n}\xrightarrow{\partial_{n}}\mathbf{X}_{\bullet}^{n+1}\rightarrow\cdots.

By a
2
-periodic complex we mean a complex
\mathbf{X}_{\bullet}
with
\mathbf{X}_{\bullet}^{2n+1}=\mathbf{X}_{\bullet}^{1}
,
\mathbf{X}_{\bullet}^{2n}=\mathbf{X}_{\bullet}^{0}
,
\partial_{2n+1}=\partial_{1}
and
\partial_{2n}=\partial_{0}
for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
.
Let
\mathcal{A}
denote a full additive subcategory of
\operatorname{Mod}(A)
(for most of our applications
\mathcal{A}
will be
\operatorname{Mod}(A)
,
\operatorname{mod}(A)
or

\operatorname{proj}(A)
).
One can define a category
\mathcal{C}^{2}(\mathcal{A})
of
2
-periodic
\mathcal{A}
-complexes with
2
-periodic chain maps (see
3.1
for definition). It is readily verified that
\mathcal{C}^{2}(\mathcal{A})
is an additive category (and abelian if
\mathcal{A}
is). We can define
2
-periodic chain-homotopy (see
3.2
for definition) and define the
2
-periodic homotopy category
\mathcal{K}^{2}(\mathcal{A})
. It is readily proved that
\mathcal{K}^{2}(A)
is a triangulated category. By inverting quasi-isomorphisms one obtains the
2
-periodic derived category,
\mathcal{D}^{2}(\mathcal{A})
. Our interest is to study
\mathcal{D}^{2}_{fg}(A)=\mathcal{D}^{2}_{fg}(\operatorname{Mod}A)
, the thick subcategory of
\mathcal{D}^{2}(\operatorname{Mod}A)
consisting of
2
-periodic complexes
\mathbf{X}_{\bullet}
with
H^{i}(\mathbf{X}_{\bullet})
a finitely generated
A
-module for all
i\in\mathbb{Z}
.

In general one cannot say much about
\mathcal{D}^{2}_{fg}(A)
. However the situation drastically improves when
A
is regular local. Let
\mathcal{K}^{2}(\operatorname{proj}A)
denote the homotopy category of
2
-periodic complexes of finitely generated free
A
-modules.
We have an obvious exact functor
\phi\colon\mathcal{K}^{2}(\operatorname{proj}A)\rightarrow\mathcal{D}^{2}_{fg}(A)
. We prove

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
(A,\mathfrak{m})
be regular local. Then
\phi
is an equivalence of triangulated categories.

Remark 1.2
.

Theorem
1.1
also implies that the natural map
\psi\colon\mathcal{K}^{2}(\operatorname{proj}A)\rightarrow\mathcal{D}^{2}(\operatorname{mod}(A))
is also an equivalence. However we need Theorem
1.1
for our applications.

In view of Theorem
1.1
it is a natural question on when
\mathcal{K}^{2}(\operatorname{proj}A)
is a Krull-Schmidt category. We prove quite generally.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
(A,\mathfrak{m})
be a Henselian Noetherian local ring. Then
\mathcal{K}^{2}(\operatorname{proj}A)
is a Krull-Schmidt category.

A natural question for a Krull-Schmidt triangulated category is whether it has Auslander-Reiten (AR) triangles. Let
\mathcal{K}^{2}_{f}(\operatorname{proj}A)
denote the full subcategory of
\mathcal{K}^{2}(\operatorname{proj}A)
consisting of complexes with finite length cohomology. We prove

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
(A,\mathfrak{m})
be a complete regular local ring. Then
\mathcal{K}^{2}_{f}(\operatorname{proj}A)
has AR-triangles.

In the last section we explicitly compute the AR-quiver when
A
is a complete DVR with algebraically closed residue field. It consists of two infinite tubes, see
12.6
.
Thus this is an example of a triangulated category with infinitely many indecomposables but finitely many components of the AR-quiver.
In the famous book
[
1
, p. 409]
one of the conjectures is that if an artin algebra
\Gamma
is of infinite representation type then its AR-quiver has infinitely many components. Thus this conjecture does not hold more generally.

Remark 1.5
.

A natural question is whether analogues of Theorem
1.1
,
1.4
for
m
-periodic complexes. We believe that analogues results hold. However it is technically very challenging. So
in this paper we only discuss
2
-periodic complexes.

A
) when

In section three we discuss some preliminaries on

-periodic complexes that we need. In the next section we describe some constructions on two periodic complexes that we need. In section five we discuss minimal complexes in

2.
Some preliminaries on
\mathcal{D}(\operatorname{Mod}A)

Although we are primarliy interested in cyclic complexes we need a few prelimnary results on the (unbounded) derived category of modules (not necessarily finitely generated) over regular local rings.
In this section
(A,\mathfrak{m})
is a regular local ring
. The results of this section are probably already known. We give a proof due to lack of a reference.

2.1
.

Let
\operatorname{Mod}(A)
denote the category of all
A
-modules. Let
\operatorname{Proj}A
denote the category all projective
A
-modules. Note if
P
is a projective
A
-module (not necessarily finitely generated) then it is a free
A
-module,
[
11
, 2.5]
.
Let
\mathcal{C}(\operatorname{Mod}A)
denote the category of (co-chain) complexes of
A
-modules.
We do not impose any boundedness condition on complexes in
\mathcal{C}(\operatorname{Mod}A)
.
Let
\mathcal{K}(\operatorname{Mod}A)
denote the homotopy category of complexes of
A
-modules and let
K(\operatorname{Proj}A)
denote the homotopy categoy of projective
A
-modules. Let
\mathcal{D}(\operatorname{Mod}A)
denote the (unbounded) derived category of
A
-modules.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\mathbf{X}_{\bullet}\in\mathcal{K}(\operatorname{Proj}A)
. If
\mathbf{X}_{\bullet}
is acyclic (i.e.,
H^{*}(\mathbf{X}_{\bullet})=0
) then
\mathbf{X}_{\bullet}=0
in
\mathcal{K}(\operatorname{Proj}A)
.

Proof.

Let the differential in
\mathbf{X}_{\bullet}
be given by
\partial_{n}\colon\mathbf{X}_{\bullet}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbf{X}_{\bullet}^{n+1}
. Set
P_{n}=\operatorname{image}\partial_{n}
.
As
\mathbf{X}_{\bullet}
is acyclic we have that
P_{n}
is a
d^{th}
-syzygy of
P_{n+d}
(here
d=\dim A
). So
P_{n}
is a projective module.
It is then readily proved that the identity map on
\mathbf{X}_{\bullet}
is null-homotopic. So
\mathbf{X}_{\bullet}=0
in
\mathcal{K}(\operatorname{Proj}A)
.
∎

As a consequence we obtains

Corollary 2.3
.

Let
f\colon\mathbf{P}_{\bullet}\rightarrow\mathbf{Q}_{\bullet}
be a quism where
\mathbf{P}_{\bullet},\mathbf{Q}_{\bullet}\in\mathcal{K}(\operatorname{Proj}A)
. Then
f
is an isomorphism in
\mathcal{K}(\operatorname{Proj}A)
.

Proof.

We have a triangle
\mathbf{P}_{\bullet}\xrightarrow{f}\mathbf{Q}_{\bullet}\rightarrow C(f)\rightarrow\mathbf{P}_{\bullet}[1]
. As
f
is a quism we get
H^{*}(C(f))=0
. So by Lemma
2.2
we get
C(f)=0
in
\mathcal{K}(\operatorname{Proj}A)
. Thus
f
is an isomorphism in
\mathcal{K}(\operatorname{Proj}A)
.
∎

Remark 2.4
.

Let
\mathbf{X}_{\bullet}\in\mathcal{C}(\operatorname{Mod}A)
. Then there exists
\mathbf{P}_{\bullet}\in\mathcal{C}(\operatorname{Proj}A)
and a quism
p\colon\mathbf{P}_{\bullet}\rightarrow\mathbf{X}_{\bullet}
, see
[
7
, 5.1.7]
.

Next we show

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
\mathbf{P}_{\bullet}\in\mathcal{K}(\operatorname{Proj}A)
and let
\mathbf{X}_{\bullet}\in\mathcal{K}(\operatorname{Mod}A)
. Assumme there is a quism
s\colon\mathbf{X}_{\bullet}\rightarrow\mathbf{P}_{\bullet}
. Then there exists a quism
t\colon\mathbf{P}_{\bullet}\rightarrow\mathbf{X}_{\bullet}
such that
s\circ t=1_{\mathbf{P}_{\bullet}}
in
\mathcal{K}(\operatorname{Mod}A)
.

Proof.

Let
\eta\colon\mathbf{Q}_{\bullet}\rightarrow\mathbf{X}_{\bullet}
be a quism with
\mathbf{Q}_{\bullet}\in\mathcal{C}(\operatorname{Proj}A)
. Then
g=s\circ\eta\colon\mathbf{Q}_{\bullet}\rightarrow\mathbf{P}_{\bullet}
is a quism and so invertibe in
\mathcal{K}(\operatorname{Proj}A)
. Set
t=\eta\circ g^{-1}
. The result follows.
∎

Finally we show

Theorem 2.6
.

Let
\mathbf{P}_{\bullet}\in\mathcal{K}(\operatorname{Proj}A)
and let
\mathbf{X}_{\bullet}\in\mathcal{K}(\operatorname{Mod}A)
. Then the natural map

\theta\colon\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{K}(\operatorname{Mod}A)}(\mathbf{P}_{\bullet},\mathbf{X}_{\bullet})\rightarrow\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{D}(\operatorname{Mod}A)}(\mathbf{P}_{\bullet},\mathbf{X}_{\bullet})

is an isomorphism.

Proof.

Let
f\circ s^{-1}\colon\mathbf{P}_{\bullet}\xleftarrow{s}\mathbf{Z}_{\bullet}\xrightarrow{f}\mathbf{X}_{\bullet}
be a left fraction. Note
s
is a quism. By
2.5
there exists a quism
t\colon\mathbf{P}_{\bullet}\rightarrow\mathbf{Z}_{\bullet}
such that
s\circ t=1_{\mathbf{P}_{\bullet}}
in
\mathcal{K}(\operatorname{Mod}A)
. Then check that

f\circ s^{-1}=f\circ t
. Thus
\theta
is surjective.

Let
f\colon\mathbf{P}_{\bullet}\rightarrow\mathbf{X}_{\bullet}
be such that
\theta(f)=0
. Then by

[
12
, 2.1.26]
there exists a quism
s\colon\mathbf{Z}_{\bullet}\rightarrow\mathbf{P}_{\bullet}
such that
f\circ s=0
. By
2.5
there exists a quism
t\colon\mathbf{P}_{\bullet}\rightarrow\mathbf{Z}_{\bullet}
such that
s\circ t=1_{\mathbf{P}_{\bullet}}
in
\mathcal{K}(\operatorname{Mod}A)
.
Then
f=f\circ s\circ t=0
. So
\theta
is injective.
∎

3.
some preliminaries on
2
-periodic complexes

In this section
(A,\mathfrak{m})
will denote a Noetherian ring. Let
\operatorname{Mod}(A)
be the category of all
A
-modules. By
\operatorname{mod}(A)
we will denote the category of all finitely generated
A
-modules.

3.1
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
denote a full additive subcategory of
\operatorname{Mod}(A)
(for most of our applications
\mathcal{A}
will be
\operatorname{Mod}(A)
,
\operatorname{mod}(A)
or

\operatorname{proj}(A)
). By an
\mathcal{A}
-
2
-periodic complex we mean an
\mathcal{A}
(co-chain) complex
\mathbf{X}_{\bullet}
such that
\mathbf{X}_{\bullet}^{2n}=\mathbf{X}_{\bullet}^{0}
,
\mathbf{X}_{\bullet}^{2n+1}=\mathbf{X}_{\bullet}^{1}
and differentials
\partial_{2n}=\partial_{0}
and
\partial_{2n+1}=\partial_{1}
for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
.
Let
\mathbf{X}_{\bullet},\mathbf{Y}_{\bullet}
be
2
-periodic
\mathcal{A}
-complexes. By a
2
-periodic map
\phi\colon\mathbf{X}_{\bullet}\rightarrow\mathbf{Y}_{\bullet}
we mean a chain map
\phi
with
\phi_{2n}=\phi_{0}
and
\phi_{2n+1}=\phi_{1}
for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
.
Let
\mathcal{C}^{2}(\mathcal{A})
be the class of all
2
-periodic
\mathcal{A}
-complexes and
2
-periodic morphisms. Clearly
\mathcal{C}^{2}(\mathcal{A})
is an additive category. If
\mathcal{A}=\operatorname{mod}(A)
or
\operatorname{Mod}(A)
then it is also clearly abelian. Let
\mathbf{X}_{\bullet}
be a
2
-periodic
\mathcal{A}
-complex. By
\mathbf{X}_{\bullet}[1]
we mean the
\mathcal{A}
-complex with
\mathbf{X}_{\bullet}[1]^{n}=\mathbf{X}_{\bullet}^{n+1}
and
\partial_{\mathbf{X}_{\bullet}[1],n}=-\partial_{\mathbf{X}_{\bullet},n+1}
.

3.2
.

Let
\mathbf{X}_{\bullet},\mathbf{Y}_{\bullet}\in\mathcal{C}^{2}(\mathcal{A})
and let
\phi,\psi\colon\mathbf{X}_{\bullet}\rightarrow\mathbf{Y}_{\bullet}
be
2
-periodic maps. We say
\phi,\psi
are
2
-homotopic if there exists maps
s_{n}\colon\mathbf{X}_{\bullet}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbf{Y}_{\bullet}^{n-1}
with
s_{2n}=s_{0}
and
s_{2n+1}=s_{1}
for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
with
\phi-\psi=\partial_{\mathbf{Y}_{\bullet}}\circ s+s\circ\partial_{\mathbf{X}_{\bullet}}.
It can be easily seen that if
\phi,\psi\colon\mathbf{X}_{\bullet}\rightarrow\mathbf{Y}_{\bullet}
are
2
-homotopic and if
u\colon\mathbf{P}_{\bullet}\rightarrow\mathbf{X}_{\bullet}
and
v\colon\mathbf{Y}_{\bullet}\rightarrow\mathbf{Q}_{\bullet}
are
2
-periodic maps then

\phi\circ u,\psi\circ u
(and
v\circ\phi
,
v\circ\psi
) are
2
-homotopic. So we may form the
2
-periodic homotopy category
\mathcal{K}^{2}(\mathcal{A})
.

3.3
.

Let
\mathbf{X}_{\bullet},\mathbf{Y}_{\bullet}
be
2
-periodic
\mathcal{A}
-complexes and let
f\colon\mathbf{X}_{\bullet}\rightarrow\mathbf{Y}_{\bullet}
be a
2
-periodic map.
We define
\operatorname{cone}(f)
as follows:
\operatorname{cone}(f)^{n}=\mathbf{X}_{\bullet}^{n+1}\oplus\mathbf{Y}_{\bullet}^{n}
and differentials defined as

\partial_{n}(x,y)=(-\partial_{\mathbf{X}_{\bullet},n+1}(x),\partial_{\mathbf{Y}_{\bullet},n}(y)-f_{n+1}(x)).

Clearly

\operatorname{cone}(f)\in\mathcal{C}^{2}(\mathcal{A})
. We also have the usual exact sequence complexes

0\rightarrow\mathbf{Y}_{\bullet}\xrightarrow{u}\operatorname{cone}(f)\xrightarrow{v}\mathbf{X}_{\bullet}[1]\rightarrow 0

where
u_{n}(y)=(0,y)
and
v_{n}(x,y)=-x
. Clearly
u,v
are
2
-periodic maps.

3.4
.

Triangles in
\mathcal{K}^{2}(\mathcal{A})
:
By a strict triangle in
\mathcal{K}^{2}(\mathcal{A})
we mean

\mathbf{X}_{\bullet}\xrightarrow{f}\mathbf{Y}_{\bullet}\xrightarrow{u}\operatorname{cone}(f)\xrightarrow{v}\mathbf{X}_{\bullet}[1],

where
f\colon\mathbf{X}_{\bullet}\rightarrow\mathbf{Y}_{\bullet}
is a
2
-periodic chain map and
u,v
are as above. All triangles isomorphic to a strict triangle are exact triangles. With this triangulated structure a standard and routine argument yields that
\mathcal{K}^{2}(A)
is a triangulated category.

3.5
.

Let
\mathcal{T}
be the full subcategory in
\mathcal{K}^{2}(\mathcal{A})
consisting of
2
-periodic complexes
\mathbf{X}_{\bullet}
with
H^{*}(\mathbf{X}_{\bullet})=0
. Then
\mathcal{T}
is a thick subcategory in
\mathcal{K}^{2}(\mathcal{A})
. The Verdier quotient
\mathcal{D}^{2}(\mathcal{A})=\mathcal{K}^{2}(\mathcal{A})/\mathcal{T}
is the derived category of
2
-periodic
\mathcal{A}
-complexes. We also let
\mathcal{K}^{2}_{fg}(\mathcal{A})
denote the thick subcategory
\mathcal{K}^{2}(\mathcal{A})
consisting of
2
-periodic complexes
\mathbf{X}_{\bullet}
with
H^{i}(\mathbf{X}_{\bullet})
finitely generated
A
-module for all
i\in\mathbb{Z}
. The Verdier quotient
\mathcal{D}^{2}_{fg}(\mathcal{A})=\mathcal{K}^{2}_{fg}(\mathcal{A})/\mathcal{T}
is the derived category of
2
-periodic
\mathcal{A}
-complexes with finitely generated cohomology.

4.
Some construction on
2
-Periodic complexes

In this section
A
is a Noetherian ring.
In this section we describe some constructions that we need. For convenience we work with
\mathcal{C}^{2}(\operatorname{Mod}A)
, although some of the constructions hold more generally.

4.1
.

Let
\mathbf{X}_{\bullet}\in\mathcal{C}^{2}(\operatorname{Mod}A)
and let
M
be an
A
-module. By
\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(\mathbf{X}_{\bullet},M)
we mean a complex with

\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(\mathbf{X}_{\bullet},M)^{n}=\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(\mathbf{X}_{\bullet}^{-n},M)
. The differentials are given as if
f\in\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(\mathbf{X}_{\bullet},M)^{n}
then

\partial(f)=(-1)^{n+1}f\circ\partial_{\mathbf{X}_{\bullet},-n-1}.

Clearly
\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(\mathbf{X}_{\bullet},M)\in\mathcal{C}^{2}(\operatorname{Mod}A)
. We will primarily use this construction when
M=A
or when
M=E
, the injective hull of
A/\mathfrak{m}
(when
(A,\mathfrak{m})
is local).

4.2
.

Let
\mathbf{X}_{\bullet},\mathbf{Y}_{\bullet}\in\mathcal{C}^{2}(\operatorname{Mod}A)
. By
\mathbf{Z}_{\bullet}=\mathbf{X}_{\bullet}\widehat{\otimes}\mathbf{Y}_{\bullet}
(the
2
-periodic tensor of
\mathbf{X}_{\bullet}
with
\mathbf{Y}_{\bullet}
) we mean a complex with

(1)

\mathbf{Z}_{\bullet}^{2n}=\mathbf{Z}_{\bullet}^{0}=(\mathbf{X}_{\bullet}^{0}\otimes_{A}\mathbf{Y}_{\bullet}^{0})\oplus(\mathbf{X}_{\bullet}^{1}\otimes_{A}\mathbf{Y}_{\bullet}^{1})
for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
.

(2)

\mathbf{Z}_{\bullet}^{2n+1}=\mathbf{Z}_{\bullet}^{1}=(\mathbf{X}_{\bullet}^{0}\otimes_{A}\mathbf{Y}_{\bullet}^{1})\oplus(\mathbf{X}_{\bullet}^{1}\otimes_{A}\mathbf{Y}_{\bullet}^{0})
for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
.

(3)

\partial(x\otimes y)=\partial(x)\otimes y+(-1)^{|x|}x\otimes\partial(y)
.

Here
|x|=0
if
x\in\mathbf{X}_{\bullet}^{0}
and
|x|=1
if
x\in\mathbf{X}_{\bullet}^{1}
. It is readily verified that
\mathbf{Z}_{\bullet}
is complex. Clearly it is circular.

4.3
.

Let
\mathbf{X}_{\bullet},\mathbf{Y}_{\bullet}\in\mathcal{C}^{2}(\operatorname{Mod}A)
. By
\mathbf{Z}_{\bullet}=\operatorname{\mathcal{H}om}^{c}(\mathbf{X}_{\bullet},\mathbf{Y}_{\bullet})
(the
2
-periodic Hom of
\mathbf{X}_{\bullet}
with
\mathbf{Y}_{\bullet}
) we mean a complex with

(1)

\mathbf{Z}_{\bullet}^{2n}=\mathbf{Z}_{\bullet}^{0}=\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(\mathbf{X}_{\bullet}^{0},\mathbf{Y}_{\bullet}^{0})\oplus\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(\mathbf{X}_{\bullet}^{1},\mathbf{Y}_{\bullet}^{1})
for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
.

(2)

\mathbf{Z}_{\bullet}^{2n+1}=\mathbf{Z}_{\bullet}^{1}=\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(\mathbf{X}_{\bullet}^{0},\mathbf{Y}_{\bullet}^{1})\oplus\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(\mathbf{X}_{\bullet}^{1},\mathbf{Y}_{\bullet}^{0})
for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
.

(3)

\partial(f)=\partial_{\mathbf{Y}_{\bullet}}\circ f-(-1)^{|f|}f\circ\partial_{\mathbf{X}_{\bullet}}
.

It is readily verified that
\mathbf{Z}_{\bullet}
is infact a
2
-periodic complex. It is readily verified that

H^{0}(\operatorname{\mathcal{H}om}^{c}(\mathbf{X}_{\bullet},\mathbf{Y}_{\bullet}))=\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{K}^{2}(\operatorname{Mod}A)}(\mathbf{X}_{\bullet},\mathbf{Y}_{\bullet}).

4.4
.

Let
\mathbf{X}_{\bullet},\mathbf{Y}_{\bullet}\in\mathcal{C}^{2}(\operatorname{Mod}A)
. We have a natural map

\displaystyle\delta_{\mathbf{X}_{\bullet},\mathbf{Y}_{\bullet}}\colon\mathbf{Y}_{\bullet}\widehat{\otimes}\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(\mathbf{X}_{\bullet},A)

\displaystyle\rightarrow\operatorname{\mathcal{H}om}^{c}(\mathbf{X}_{\bullet},\mathbf{Y}_{\bullet})

\displaystyle y\otimes\phi

\displaystyle\rightarrow y\widehat{\otimes}\phi

Here
y\widehat{\otimes}\phi(x)=\phi(x)y
. It is readily verified that if
\mathbf{Y}_{\bullet}\in\mathcal{C}^{2}(\operatorname{mod}(A))
and
\mathbf{X}_{\bullet}\in\mathcal{C}^{2}(\operatorname{proj}A)
then

\delta_{\mathbf{X}_{\bullet},\mathbf{Y}_{\bullet}}
is an isomorphism.

5.
Minimal
2
-periodic complexes

In this section we assume
(A,\mathfrak{m})
is a Noetherian local ring. We say
\mathbf{X}_{\bullet}\in\mathcal{C}^{2}(\operatorname{proj}A)
is minimal if
\partial(\mathbf{X}_{\bullet})\subseteq\mathfrak{m}\mathbf{X}_{\bullet}
.
We first need a large class of complexes (whose image) in
\mathcal{K}^{2}(\operatorname{proj}A)
is zero.

Lemma 5.1
.

(1)

Let
\mathbf{W}_{\bullet}
be the complex
\mathbf{W}_{\bullet}^{i}=A
for all
i
and
\partial^{2i+1}=1_{A}
and
\partial^{2i}=0
for all
i
.. Then
\mathbf{W}_{\bullet}
is zero in
\mathcal{K}^{2}(\operatorname{proj}A)
.

(2)

Let
\mathbf{W}_{\bullet}
be the complex
\mathbf{W}_{\bullet}^{i}=A
for all
i
and
\partial^{2i}=1_{A}
and
\partial^{2i+1}=0
for all
i
.. Then
\mathbf{W}_{\bullet}
is zero in
\mathcal{K}^{2}(\operatorname{proj}A)
.

Proof.

(1) Let
s_{i}\colon\mathbf{W}_{\bullet}^{i}\rightarrow\mathbf{W}_{\bullet}^{i-1}
be given by
1_{A}
for all
i
. This gives a
2
-periodic homotopy of
1_{\mathbf{W}_{\bullet}}
with
0_{\mathbf{W}_{\bullet}}
. It follows that
\mathbf{W}_{\bullet}
is zero in
\mathcal{K}^{2}(\operatorname{proj}A)
.

(2) This is similar to (1).
∎

The main result of this section is

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
\mathbf{X}_{\bullet}\in\mathcal{C}^{2}(\operatorname{proj}A)
. Then
\mathbf{X}_{\bullet}=\mathbf{Y}_{\bullet}\oplus\mathbf{Z}_{\bullet}
where
\mathbf{Y}_{\bullet}
is minimal and
\mathbf{Z}_{\bullet}
is a finite direct sum of complexes of the type given in Lemma
5.1
. In particular
\mathbf{Z}_{\bullet}=0
in
\mathcal{K}^{2}(\operatorname{proj}A)
.

Proof.

We induct on
r(\mathbf{X}_{\bullet})=\operatorname{rank}\mathbf{X}_{\bullet}^{0}+\operatorname{rank}\mathbf{X}_{\bullet}^{1}
. If

6.
Krull-Schmidt property of
\mathcal{K}^{2}(\operatorname{proj}A)

In this section
(A,\mathfrak{m})
is a Henselian local ring. Recall an
A
-linear additive category
\mathcal{C}
is Krull-Schmidt if every object in
\mathcal{C}
decomposes into a finite direct sum of objects having local endomorphism rings. In this section we prove Theorem
1.3
.

We first prove

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
(A,\mathfrak{m})
be a Henselian local ring. Then
\mathcal{C}^{2}(\operatorname{mod}(A))
is Krull-Schmidt.

Proof.

Set
\mathcal{C}=\mathcal{C}^{2}(\operatorname{mod}(A))
.
It is clear that
\mathcal{C}
is an abelian category. So it has split idempotents.

If
M
is a finitely generated
A
-module then let
\mu(M)=\dim_{A/\mathfrak{m}}M/\mathfrak{m}M
be its minimal number of generators. If
\mathbf{X}_{\bullet}\in\mathcal{C}
then set
\mu(\mathbf{X}_{\bullet})=\mu(\mathbf{X}_{\bullet}^{1})+\mu(\mathbf{X}_{\bullet}^{0})
. An easy induction on
\mu(\mathbf{X}_{\bullet})
yields that
\mathbf{X}_{\bullet}
decomposes into a finite direct sum of indecomposables.

Let
\mathbf{X}_{\bullet},\mathbf{Y}_{\bullet}\in\mathcal{C}
. Then we have an inclusion of
A
-modules

\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(\mathbf{X}_{\bullet},\mathbf{Y}_{\bullet})\subseteq\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(\mathbf{X}_{\bullet}^{0},\mathbf{Y}_{\bullet}^{0})\times\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(\mathbf{X}_{\bullet}^{1},\mathbf{Y}_{\bullet}^{1}).

In particular
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(\mathbf{X}_{\bullet},\mathbf{Y}_{\bullet})
is a finitely generated
A
-module.

Let
\mathbf{X}_{\bullet}\in\mathcal{C}
be indecomposable. As idempotents split in
\mathcal{C}
, the finitely generated
A
-algebra
\operatorname{End}_{\mathcal{C}}(\mathbf{X}_{\bullet})
has no idempotents, see
[
10
, 1.1]
. Let
J
be the Jacobson radical of
\operatorname{End}_{\mathcal{C}}(\mathbf{X}_{\bullet})
. We note that
\operatorname{End}_{\mathcal{C}}(\mathbf{X}_{\bullet})/J
is semi-simple,
see
[
9
, 20.6]
.
As
A
is Henselian it follows that idempotents in
\operatorname{End}_{\mathcal{C}}(\mathbf{X}_{\bullet})/J
can be lifted to
\operatorname{End}_{\mathcal{C}}(\mathbf{X}_{\bullet})
. But
\operatorname{End}_{\mathcal{C}}(\mathbf{X}_{\bullet})
has no idempotents. It follows that
\operatorname{End}_{\mathcal{C}}(\mathbf{X}_{\bullet})/J
is a divison ring. So
\operatorname{End}_{\mathcal{C}}(\mathbf{X}_{\bullet})
is local. Thus
\mathcal{C}
is a Krull-Schmidt category.
∎

We next show

.

(with hypotheses as in
6.1
)

is a Krull-Schmidt category.

Proof.

This follows from the fact that if

\mathbf{Y}_{\bullet},\mathbf{Z}_{\bullet}\in\mathcal{C}^{2}(\operatorname{proj}A)

Next we give

.

Let

10.
Preliminaries on AR-triangles and irreducible maps

The notion of AR-triangles in a triangulated Krull-Schmidt category was introduced by Happel
[
8
, Chapter 1]
.
In this section we recall the definition of AR-triangles, discuss a result from
[
14
]
on the existence of AR-triangles and discuss some facts about irreducible maps.

10.1
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a Krull-Schmidt triangulated category with shift functor
\sum
.

A triangle
N\xrightarrow{f}E\xrightarrow{g}M\xrightarrow{h}\sum N
in
\mathcal{C}
is called a
right AR-triangle
(ending at
M
) if

(RAR1)
M,N
are indecomposable.

(RAR2)
h\neq 0
.

(RAR3) If
D
is indecomposable then for every non-isomorphism
t\colon D\rightarrow M
we have
h\circ t=0
.

Dually, a triangle
\sum^{-1}M\xrightarrow{w}N\xrightarrow{f}E\xrightarrow{g}M
in
\mathcal{C}
is called a
left AR-triangle
(starting at
N
) if

(LAR1)
M,N
are indecomposable.

(LAR2)
w\neq 0
.

(LAR3) If
D
is indecomposable then for every non-isomorphism
t\colon N\rightarrow D
we have
t\circ w=0
.

Definition 10.2
.

We say a Krull-Schmidt triangulated category
\mathcal{C}
has AR-triangles if for any indecomposable
M\in\mathcal{C}
there exists a right AR-triangle ending at
M
and a left AR-triangle starting at
M
.

10.3
.

In a fundamental work Reiten and Van den Bergh,
[
14
]
, related existence of AR-triangles to existence of a Serre functor.
Let
(A,\mathfrak{m})
be a Noetherian local ring and let
E
be the injective hull of
k=A/\mathfrak{m}
.
Set
(-)^{\vee}=\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(-,E)
.
Let
\mathcal{C}
be a Hom-finite
A
-triangulated category. By a right Serre-functor on
\mathcal{C}
we mean an additive functor
F\colon\mathcal{C}\rightarrow\mathcal{C}
such that we have isomorphism

\eta_{C,D}\colon\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(C,D)\rightarrow\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(D,F(C))^{\vee},

for any
C,D\in\mathcal{C}
which are natural in
C
and
D
. It can be shown that if
F
is a right Serre functor
then
F
is fully faithful. If
F
is also dense then we say
F
is a Serre functor.
We will use the following result:

Theorem 10.4
.

[see
[
14
, Theorem I.2.4]
]
Let
\mathcal{C}
be a Hom-finite
A
-linear triangulated category. Then the following are equivalent

(i)

\mathcal{C}
has AR-triangles.

(ii)

\mathcal{C}
has a Serre-functor.

Remark 10.5
.

In
[
14
]
, Theorem I.2.4 is proved for
k
-linear Hom-finite triangulated categories where

is a field. However the same proof generalizes to our situation.

First note that the notion of split

11.
Proof of Theorem
1.4

In this section we give:

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

Let
\mathbf{X}_{\bullet},\mathbf{Y}_{\bullet}\in\mathcal{K}=\mathcal{K}^{2}_{f}(\operatorname{proj}A)
and let
\mathcal{D}=\mathcal{D}^{2}_{fg}(\operatorname{Mod}A)
. Let
\mathbf{p}\colon\mathcal{D}\rightarrow\mathcal{K}
be the projective resolution functor. Then we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{K}}(\mathbf{X}_{\bullet},\mathbf{Y}_{\bullet})^{\vee}

\displaystyle=\left(H^{0}(\operatorname{\mathcal{H}om}^{c}_{\mathcal{K}}(\mathbf{X}_{\bullet},\mathbf{Y}_{\bullet}))\right)^{\vee}

\displaystyle\cong H^{0}\left(\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(\operatorname{\mathcal{H}om}^{c}_{\mathcal{K}}(\mathbf{X}_{\bullet},\mathbf{Y}_{\bullet}),E)\right)

\displaystyle\cong H^{0}\left(\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(\mathbf{Y}_{\bullet}\widehat{\otimes}\mathbf{X}_{\bullet}^{*},E)\right)\

\displaystyle\cong H^{0}\left(\operatorname{\mathcal{H}om}^{c}_{\mathcal{K}}(\mathbf{Y}_{\bullet},(\mathbf{X}_{\bullet}^{*})^{\vee})\right)

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{K}}(\mathbf{Y}_{\bullet},(\mathbf{X}_{\bullet}^{*})^{\vee})

\displaystyle\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{D}}(\mathbf{Y}_{\bullet},(\mathbf{X}_{\bullet}^{*})^{\vee})

\displaystyle\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{D}}(\mathbf{Y}_{\bullet},\mathbf{p}\left((\mathbf{X}_{\bullet}^{*})^{\vee}\right))

\displaystyle\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{K}}(\mathbf{Y}_{\bullet},\mathbf{p}\left((\mathbf{X}_{\bullet}^{*})^{\vee}\right))

Thus we have an isomorphism

\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{K}}(\mathbf{X}_{\bullet},\mathbf{Y}_{\bullet})\cong\left(\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{K}}(\mathbf{Y}_{\bullet},\mathbf{p}\left((\mathbf{X}_{\bullet}^{*}\right))^{\vee})\right)^{\vee}.

Clearly the above ismorphism is natural in
\mathbf{X}_{\bullet},\mathbf{Y}_{\bullet}
. Thus the functor
F
defined as
F(\mathbf{X}_{\bullet}^{*})=\mathbf{p}\left((\mathbf{X}_{\bullet}^{*})\right)^{\vee}
is a right Serre-functor.
It suffices to show
F
is dense to conclude.

The functor
F
is the composite of the following functors

\mathcal{K}\xrightarrow{*}\mathcal{K}^{op}\xrightarrow{\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(-,E)}\mathcal{K}_{f}^{c}(add(E))\xrightarrow{\mathbf{p}}\mathcal{K}

It suffices to show each functor above is an equivalence. The functors

*=\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(-,A)
and
\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(-,E)
are clearly equivalences. It suffices to prove that
\mathbf{p}
is an equivalence.

We first prove that for any
\mathbf{X}_{\bullet}\in\mathcal{K}
there exists
\mathbf{Y}_{\bullet}\in\mathcal{K}_{f}^{c}(add(E))
and a quism
\mathbf{X}_{\bullet}\rightarrow\mathbf{Y}_{\bullet}
. Consider
\mathbf{Z}_{\bullet}=\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(\mathbf{X}_{\bullet},E)
then
\mathbf{Z}_{\bullet}\in\mathcal{K}_{f}^{c}(add(E))
. Let
\mathbf{W}_{\bullet}\rightarrow\mathbf{Z}_{\bullet}
be a minimal
projective resolution of
\mathbf{Z}_{\bullet}
which exists as
H^{i}(\mathbf{Z}_{\bullet})
has finite length for all
i
.
Then dualizing we obtain a quism
\mathbf{Z}_{\bullet}^{\vee}\rightarrow\mathbf{W}_{\bullet}^{\vee}=\mathbf{Y}_{\bullet}
. It remains to note that

\mathbf{Z}_{\bullet}^{\vee}\cong\mathbf{X}_{\bullet}
. Thus we have a functor
\mathbf{i}\colon\mathcal{K}\rightarrow\mathcal{K}_{f}^{c}(add(E))

(the injective resolution functor) which is clearly the inverse of
\mathbf{p}
.

Thus
F
is an equivalence and so
F
is a Serre-functor. Hence
\mathcal{K}
has AR-triangles
∎

12.

\dim A=1

In this section
(A,\mathfrak{m})
is a DVR with
\mathfrak{m}=(x)
and
k=A/\mathfrak{m}
an algebraically closed field. In this section we describe the AR-quiver of
\mathcal{K}=\mathcal{K}^{2}_{f}(\operatorname{proj}A)
. We first need to describe all the indecomposables in
\mathcal{K}
.

12.1
.

Fix
j\geq 1
. Let
\mathbf{K}_{\bullet}(j)
be the complex defined as
\mathbf{K}_{\bullet}(j)^{n}=A
for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
. Define
\partial^{2n+1}(t)=x^{j}t
and
\partial^{2n}=0
.
We first prove

Proposition 12.2
.

Let
\mathbf{X}_{\bullet}\in\mathcal{K}
be indecomposable. Then
\mathbf{X}_{\bullet}\cong\mathbf{K}_{\bullet}(j)
or
\mathbf{X}_{\bullet}\cong\mathbf{K}_{\bullet}(j)[1]
for some
j\geq 1
.

Before proving the above result we need to recall the following result from
[
13
, Lemma 7.6]
.

Lemma 12.3
.

Let
\mathbf{Y}_{\bullet}\in\mathcal{K}
. Then
\operatorname{rank}_{A}\mathbf{Y}_{\bullet}^{i}=\operatorname{rank}\mathbf{Y}_{\bullet}^{i+1}
for all
i\in\mathbb{Z}
.

We now give

Proof of Proposition
12.2
.

By Lemma
12.3
it follows that
\mathbf{K}_{\bullet}(j)
(and hence

\mathbf{K}_{\bullet}(j)[1]
) are indecomposable for all
j\geq 1
.

Let
\mathbf{X}_{\bullet}\in\mathcal{K}
be indecomposable. We may assume
\mathbf{X}_{\bullet}
is minimal and
\partial^{1}\neq 0
.
Also note that by Lemma
12.3
we have
\operatorname{rank}_{A}\mathbf{X}_{\bullet}^{i}=\operatorname{rank}\mathbf{X}_{\bullet}^{i+1}
for all
i\in\mathbb{Z}
.
We consider the Smith form of
\partial^{1}
. Thus there exists basis
\{u_{1},\ldots,u_{m}\}
of
\mathbf{X}_{\bullet}^{1}
and a basis
\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{m}\}
of
\mathbf{X}_{\bullet}^{2}
with
\partial^{1}(u_{j})=x^{a_{j}}v_{j}
for
j=1,\ldots,r
and
\partial^{1}(u_{j})=0
for
j>r
. It is elementary to see that
\partial^{2}(v_{1})=0
and
\partial^{2}(v_{j})\in Au_{2}+\cdots Au_{m}
. Thus
\mathbf{K}_{\bullet}(a_{1})
is a direct summand of
\mathbf{X}_{\bullet}
. As
\mathbf{X}_{\bullet}
is indecomposable it follows that
\mathbf{X}_{\bullet}\cong\mathbf{K}_{\bullet}(a_{1})
.
∎

Lemma 12.4
.

There does not exist any irreducible maps from
\mathbf{K}_{\bullet}(1)
to itself.

Proof.

Let
f\colon\mathbf{K}_{\bullet}(1)\rightarrow\mathbf{K}_{\bullet}(1)
be a
2
-periodic map. Let
f_{i}\colon\mathbf{K}_{\bullet}(1)^{i}\rightarrow\mathbf{K}_{\bullet}(1)^{i}
be the components of
f
. Then note that
f_{1}=f_{2}
is multiplication by some
a_{f}
. Set
a_{f}=x^{m}u
. If
m\geq 1
then note
f
is null-homotopic (a hompotopy is given by
s_{2n}=x^{m-1}u
and
s_{2n+1}=0
). If
u\neq 0
and
m=0
then note that

f
is an isomorphism. Thus any map
f\colon\mathbf{K}_{\bullet}(1)\rightarrow\mathbf{K}_{\bullet}(1)
is an isomorphism or zero. The result follows.
∎

We need the following computation of the Auslander-Reiten translate of
\mathbf{K}_{\bullet}(i)
.

Lemma 12.5
.

Fix
j\geq 1
. The Auslander-Reiten translate of
\mathbf{K}_{\bullet}(i)
is itself.

Proof.

Let
F\colon\mathcal{K}\rightarrow\mathcal{K}
be the Serre-functor on
\mathcal{K}
. Then by
[
14
, 1.2.3]
the Auslander-Reiten translate of
\mathbf{K}_{\bullet}(i)
is given by

We note that
\mathbf{K}_{\bullet}(i)^{*}\cong\mathbf{K}_{\bullet}(i)[1]
. The cohomology of
(\mathbf{K}_{\bullet}(i)^{*})^{\vee}
is
(A/(x^{i})^{\vee}=A/(x^{i})
in degrees odd and zero in degrees even. It follows that
F(\mathbf{K}_{\bullet}(i))=\mathbf{K}_{\bullet}(i)[1]
. The result follows.
∎

We now compute the AR-quiver of
\mathcal{K}
.

Theorem 12.6
.

(1)

The AR-triangles of
\mathcal{K}
are as follows

\displaystyle\mathbf{K}_{\bullet}(1)

\displaystyle\rightarrow\mathbf{K}_{\bullet}(2)\rightarrow\mathbf{K}_{\bullet}(1)\rightarrow\mathbf{K}_{\bullet}(1)[1],

\displaystyle\mathbf{K}_{\bullet}(i)

\displaystyle\rightarrow\mathbf{K}_{\bullet}(i-1)\oplus\mathbf{K}_{\bullet}(i+1)\rightarrow\mathbf{K}_{\bullet}(i)\rightarrow\mathbf{K}_{\bullet}(i)[1]\quad\text{for $i\geq 2$,}

\displaystyle\mathbf{K}_{\bullet}(1)[1]

\displaystyle\rightarrow\mathbf{K}_{\bullet}(2)[1]\rightarrow\mathbf{K}_{\bullet}(1)[1]\rightarrow\mathbf{K}_{\bullet}(1),

\displaystyle\mathbf{K}_{\bullet}(i)[1]

\displaystyle\rightarrow\mathbf{K}_{\bullet}(i-1)[1]\oplus\mathbf{K}_{\bullet}(i+1)[1]\rightarrow\mathbf{K}_{\bullet}(i)[1]\rightarrow\mathbf{K}_{\bullet}(i)\quad\text{for $i\geq 2$.}

(2)

The AR-quiver of
\mathcal{K}
is

\displaystyle\mathbf{K}_{\bullet}(1)

\displaystyle\leftrightarrows\mathbf{K}_{\bullet}(2)\leftrightarrows\mathbf{K}_{\bullet}(3)\leftrightarrows\cdots\leftrightarrows\mathbf{K}_{\bullet}(i)\leftrightarrows\cdots

\displaystyle\mathbf{K}_{\bullet}(1)[1]

\displaystyle\leftrightarrows\mathbf{K}_{\bullet}(2)[1]\leftrightarrows\mathbf{K}_{\bullet}(3)[1]\leftrightarrows\cdots\leftrightarrows\mathbf{K}_{\bullet}(i)[1]\leftrightarrows\cdots

Proof.

The assertion (2) follows from (1). Also the third and fourth assertion of (1) follows by taking the shift operator
[1]

[2]

Let the Auslander-Reiten triangle ending at
\mathbf{K}_{\bullet}(1)

\mathbf{K}_{\bullet}(1)\rightarrow E\rightarrow\mathbf{K}_{\bullet}(1)\rightarrow\mathbf{K}_{\bullet}(1)[1]

Taking cohomology we get that
H^{1}(E)=0
and
\ell(H^{0}(E))=2
. It follows that

or

. If the latter case holds then there is an irreducible map
\mathbf{K}_{\bullet}(1)\rightarrow\mathbf{K}_{\bullet}(1)
which is not possible by Lemma

We prove the second assertion of (1) by induction on
i
.
Let the Auslander-Reiten triangle ending at
\mathbf{K}_{\bullet}(2)
be given as

\mathbf{K}_{\bullet}(2)\rightarrow E\oplus\mathbf{K}_{\bullet}(1)\rightarrow\mathbf{K}_{\bullet}(2)\rightarrow\mathbf{K}_{\bullet}(2)[1]

Taking cohomology we get that
H^{1}(E)=0

E=\mathbf{K}_{\bullet}(3)
or
E=\mathbf{K}_{\bullet}(2)\oplus\mathbf{K}_{\bullet}(1)

We now assume that for all
i\leq r

\mathbf{K}_{\bullet}(i)
is given by

\mathbf{K}_{\bullet}(i)\rightarrow\mathbf{K}_{\bullet}(i-1)\oplus\mathbf{K}_{\bullet}(i+1)\rightarrow\mathbf{K}_{\bullet}(i)\rightarrow\mathbf{K}_{\bullet}(i)[1].

Let the AR-triangle ending at

Taking cohomology we get that
H^{1}(E)=0

\ell(H^{0}(E))=r+2
.
We note that

(a) if
i<r

\mathbf{K}_{\bullet}(r+1)

(b) If

Thus the only possible summand of

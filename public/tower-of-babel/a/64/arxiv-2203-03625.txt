Self-consistent equation for torsion arising as a consequence of the Dirac sea quantum fluctuations in external classical electromagnetic and gravitational fields
S.N. Vergeles

Abstract
The quantum fluctuations of the Dirac field in external classical gravitational and electromagnetic fields are studied. A self-consistent equation for torsion is calculated, which is obtained using one-loop fermion diagrams.

I
Introduction

In this paper, we study the theory of gravity, minimally related to the massive Dirac field, formulated in the form of Cartan-Palatini. The Dirac field is considered to be quantized, while the gravitational (and electromagnetic) fields are classical.
Since the gauge fields are classical, the mean values of bilinear forms constructed using Dirac fields are exhausted by one-loop diagrams. The torsion tensor appears to be one of such mean value.
This tensor appears as the fermion part of one of the equations of motion
(see the next Section, Eq. (
5
)):

\displaystyle e^{\mu}_{b}e^{\nu}_{c}\left({\cal D}_{\mu}e^{a}_{\nu}-{\cal D}_{\nu}e^{a}_{\mu}\right)=i\frac{l_{P}^{2}}{4}\overline{\Psi}(\gamma^{a}\sigma_{bc}+\sigma_{bc}\gamma^{a})\Psi

(1)

By definition, the left-hand side of the last equation is the torsion tensor
\mathop{{\mathfrak{T}}}\nolimits_{bc}^{a}
.

In this work, the mean value of the right-hand side of Eq. (
1
) is calculated explicitly. Only divergent contributions (in momentum space) are taken into account. Therefore, the result can depend only on local geometric quantities (curvature and torsion tensors and their covariant derivatives). As a result of these calculations, the right-hand side of Eq. (
1
) turns out to be a local function of the curvature and torsion tensors and their covariant derivatives.
If we assume that the curvature tensor is given, then in this way a self-consistent equation for the torsion tensor arises. This equation is one of the equations of motion in the studied model.

The self-consistent equation for torsion tensor (
33
) may be interesting for the following reasons. Even a superficial study of this equation shows that in the present cosmological epoch the torsion tensor generated by the Dirac field must be zero. This fact is consistent with experimental data.
However, conditions near the Big Bang may occur when the torsion tensor turns out to be nonzero.
To study the cosmological consequences arising from this, separate studies are needed.

In this paper, the question of the contribution to the right-hand side of Eq. (
1
) from the Weyl fermion fields (neutrinos) remained open. This contribution must be calculated since Weyl fermions exist.
However, the corresponding computational procedure must be somewhat modified, since the Weyl fields are either massless or their masses are extremely small.

There is a good reason to consider gravitational fields as classical.
The quantum theory of gravity is a non-renormalizable theory. In particular, this means that quantum fluctuations of gravitational dynamical variables (tetrads and connections) are large on ultra-small scales of the order of the Planck length
Vergeles 2021
. But on the scales much larger than the Planck scale, these fluctuations decrease rapidly (according to a power law). Therefore, we will assume that when considering physics on scales that are much larger than Planck’s, fluctuations of the gravitational degrees of freedom are insignificant, that is, these degrees of freedom are described by classical fields. That’s why the gravitational fields
e^{a}_{\mu}
and
\omega^{ab}_{\mu}
are considered as classical. However, the quantum fluctuations of the Dirac field are important for wavelengths shorter than the Compton wavelength of the Dirac particle.

Recently, the problems of the dynamics of the Dirac and Weyl spin current in gravitational and electromagnetic fields have been actively studied. For example, in the
work
de Camargo et al. 2021
the master equations governing
the interaction between gravitational fields and gauge
invariant spin-current is derived. Note that the canonical spin current (
7
) is proportional to the torsion tensor, and therefore such a spin current in an external electromagnetic field is considered here.
In the paper
Chu and Leung 2021
a variant of the spin current constructed with the help of the spin operator in the Pauli-Lubanski form is studied. In this paper, it is shown that the vacuum expectation value of such a spin in an external electromagnetic field is nonzero. It is shown below that the same vacuum expectation value of the spin current (
7
) is zero.
We refer the reader for the interesting results and a significant number of citations on this topic to the review
Cranganore 2021
.
The one-loop calculation of the effective action arising due to integration over the Dirac field in the same model (
2
) is contained in
Nascimento et al. 2022
.

We also point to the review
Hehl et al. 1976
, entitled ”General relativity with spin and torsion”,
which may be useful in getting acquainted with the problem under study here.

This article is structured as follows. Section II defines the model under study and introduces notation. Section III presents the results of calculations and writes out a self-consistent equation for the torsion tensor. Then comes the Conclusion.

II
Definition of the model and technical means

Let us write out the action of gravity coupled with a massive Dirac field in external classical electromagnetic and gravitational fields:

\displaystyle{\mathop{{\mathfrak{A}}}\nolimits}={\mathop{{\mathfrak{A}}}\nolimits}_{g}+{\mathop{{\mathfrak{A}}}\nolimits}_{\Psi},

\displaystyle{\mathop{{\mathfrak{A}}}\nolimits}_{g}=-\frac{1}{4l_{P}^{2}}\varepsilon_{abcd}\varepsilon^{\mu\nu\lambda\rho}\int\mathop{{\mathfrak{R}}}\nolimits^{ab}_{\mu\nu}e^{c}_{\lambda}e^{d}_{\rho}\mathop{\rm d}\nolimits^{4}x,

\displaystyle\mathop{{\mathfrak{R}}}\nolimits^{ab}_{\mu\nu}=\partial_{\mu}\omega^{ab}_{\nu}-\partial_{\nu}\omega^{ab}_{\mu}+\omega^{a}_{c\mu}\omega^{cb}_{\nu}-\omega^{a}_{c\nu}\omega^{cb}_{\mu}=-\mathop{{\mathfrak{R}}}\nolimits^{ba}_{\mu\nu},

\displaystyle{\mathop{{\mathfrak{A}}}\nolimits}_{\Psi}=\int\mathop{\rm d}\nolimits^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\left\{\frac{i}{2}\tilde{e}^{\mu}_{a}\left(\overline{\Psi}\gamma^{a}{\cal D}_{\mu}\Psi-\overline{{\cal D}_{\mu}\Psi}\gamma^{a}\Psi\right)-m\overline{\Psi}\Psi\right\}.

(2)

Here

\displaystyle{\cal D}_{\mu}\Psi=\left(\partial_{\mu}-ieA_{\mu}+\omega_{\mu}\right)\Psi,

\displaystyle\omega_{\mu}\equiv\frac{1}{2}\omega_{ab\mu}\sigma^{ab},\quad\sigma^{ab}=\frac{1}{4}[\gamma^{a},\gamma^{b}],

(3)

The vector fields

form an local orthonormal basis, so that

\displaystyle g_{\mu\nu}\tilde{e}^{\mu}_{a}\tilde{e}^{\nu}_{b}=\eta_{ab}=\mathop{\rm diag}\nolimits(1,-1,-1,-1),

(4)

and
\tilde{e}^{\mu}_{a}e^{b}_{\mu}=\delta^{b}_{a}
.
Levi-Civita symbols are equal to units if their indices are ordered as
(0123)

We regard the lattice theory of gravity
Vergeles 2021
as a regularization of the continuum theory (

Equation

\displaystyle\mathop{{\mathfrak{T}}}\nolimits^{a}_{\mu\nu}\equiv{\cal D}_{\mu}e^{a}_{\nu}-{\cal D}_{\nu}e^{a}_{\mu}=\frac{il^{2}_{P}}{4}\overline{\Psi}\left(\gamma^{a}\sigma_{bc}+\sigma_{bc}\gamma^{a}\right)\Psi e^{b}_{\mu}e^{c}_{\nu}.

(5)

Or, equivalently

\displaystyle\mathop{{\mathfrak{T}}}\nolimits^{a}_{bc}=\frac{l^{2}_{P}}{4}\varepsilon^{a}_{\,\,\,bcd}\overline{\Psi}\gamma^{5}\gamma^{d}\Psi.

\displaystyle{\mathop{{\mathfrak{S}}}\nolimits}^{a}_{bc}=-\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon^{a}_{\,\,\,bcd}\overline{\Psi}\gamma^{5}\gamma^{d}\Psi.

III
Calculations

Obviously, the term of degree zero relative to
V
on the right-hand side of Eq. (
9

\mathop{\rm tr}\nolimits\gamma^{5}\gamma^{d}S^{(0)}(-y)\equiv 0

III.1
The contribution of the electromagnetic field to the torsion tensor

Let us first calculate the contribution to the torsion from the electromagnetic field in the first order and without taking into account gravity. In this case
V=e\gamma^{\mu}A_{\mu}
. According to (
9
) and
(
13
), this contribution is

\displaystyle\delta^{(1)}_{A}{\mathop{{\mathfrak{T}}}\nolimits}_{abc}=\frac{i}{4}el^{2}_{P}\varepsilon_{abcd}\mathop{\rm tr}\nolimits\gamma^{5}\gamma^{d}\gamma^{\nu}\gamma^{\mu}\gamma^{\lambda}

\displaystyle\times\int\mathop{\rm d}\nolimits^{4}x\left(\partial_{\nu}D^{(0)}(-x)\right)\left(\partial_{\lambda}D^{(0)}(x)\right)A_{\mu}(x)\equiv 0,

(18)

since
D^{(0)}(-x)=D^{(0)}(x)
. Here
D^{(0)}(x)
is the causal propagator of a free Boson field.

Comparison of Eqs. (
8
) and (
18
) shows that the mean of the spin current in the canonical representation (
7
) in an external electromagnetic field is equal to zero in the first order in the field.
Note an interesting fact: a similar mean of the spin current, constructed using the representation of the spin operator in the Pauli-Lubanski form, is not equal to zero in an external electromagnetic field
Chu and Leung 2021
.
Consequently, these two representations of spin current differ fundamentally in quantum field theory.

III.2
Self-consistent equation for the torsion tensor

Everywhere below, we assume that the electromagnetic field is zero.

1.
Let us calculate the contribution to the right-hand side of Eq. (
9
) in the first order in
V
.

a) Contribution from
V^{(0)}

\displaystyle\delta_{V^{(0)}}{\mathop{{\mathfrak{T}}}\nolimits}_{abc}=-\frac{3}{4}l^{2}_{P}\int_{E}\frac{\mathop{\rm d}\nolimits^{4}k}{(2\pi)^{4}}\frac{k^{2}_{E}}{(k^{2}_{E}+m^{2})^{2}}\cdot\mathop{{\mathfrak{T}}}\nolimits_{abc}(p),

\displaystyle k^{2}_{E}=(k^{1})^{2}+(k^{2})^{2}+(k^{3})^{2}+(k^{4})^{2}.

(19)

Hereinafter, the Wick rotation is used to bring the integrals to a convenient form.

The squarely diverging integral in (
19

\displaystyle k_{\mbox{max}}\sim\frac{2\pi}{l_{P}}.

(20)

Therefore we have

\displaystyle\left(C_{V^{(0)}}\right)^{2}\equiv\frac{3}{4}l^{2}_{P}\int_{E}^{k_{\mbox{max}}}\frac{\mathop{\rm d}\nolimits^{4}k}{(2\pi)^{4}}\frac{k^{2}_{E}}{(k^{2}_{E}+m^{2})^{2}}\sim 1.

(21)

b) Contribution from
V^{(1)}
.

It is easy to see that this contribution is zero.
Indeed, such a contribution is proportional to integrals of the form

\displaystyle\int_{E}\frac{\mathop{\rm d}\nolimits^{4}k}{(2\pi)^{4}}\frac{k^{\mu}}{(k^{2}_{E}+m^{2})^{m}}=0,

\displaystyle\int_{E}\frac{\mathop{\rm d}\nolimits^{4}k}{(2\pi)^{4}}\frac{k^{\mu}k^{\nu}k^{\lambda}}{(k^{2}_{E}+m^{2})^{m+1}}=0,

(22)

which are equal to zero after integration over the angles. Thus

\displaystyle\delta_{V^{(1)}}^{(1)}{\mathop{{\mathfrak{T}}}\nolimits}_{abc}=0.

(23)

Further, we take into account only logarithmically divergent contributions, which are obtained by taking into account the potential
V^{(2)}
in the first order, as well as

in the second and

\displaystyle\ln\frac{2\pi}{l_{P}m}=\ln\frac{2\pi\hbar}{l_{P}mc}\sim 50\quad\mbox{for electron mass}.

(24)

For this reason, near the Big Bang, we also have

IV
Discussion

It would be interesting vacuum average the bilinear form of the Dirac fields in the Einstein equation
\delta\mathop{{\mathfrak{A}}}\nolimits/\delta e^{a}_{\mu}=0
in a similar way. Thus, the Einstein equation would arise, in which the contribution of the Dirac field to the matter energy-momentum tensor would be expressed in terms of the curvature and torsion tensors. In deriving this equation, there would be additional physical difficulties: it would be necessary to make a subtraction from the cosmological constant, which diverges as the fourth power of the cutoff momentum and is due to the Dirac sea. This renormalization should lead to a very small cosmological constant. There was no such difficulty in deriving of Eq. (
33
). Another essential difference between the Einstein equation thus obtained and Eq. (
33
) is as follows. The energy-momentum tensor of real particles and antiparticles does not vanish if the curvature and torsion tensors are equal to zero (outside the mass shell).
Therefore, in the presence of real particles and antiparticles, the curvature tensor cannot vanish due to the Einstein equation (on the mass shell). The statement remains valid for zero torsion tensor.
On the contrary, Eq. (
33
) allows zeroing of the torsion tensor.

Let’s assume that we have explicitly described Einstein equation.
In this case, we have a closed system of equations for variables
\mathop{{\mathfrak{t}}}\nolimits^{a}
,
e^{a}_{\mu}
and
\omega^{ab}_{\mu}
: this is Einstein equation and Eqs. (
33
), (
34
)). To do this, it is necessary to express all the geometric quantities in Einstein equation in terms of the indicated variables. However, in this paper, Einstein equation (in the specified context) is not studied.

A step in this direction was taken in
Nascimento et al. 2022
: the topological correction to the Hilbert-Einstein-Cartan action was calculated, which is contained in the integral over the Dirac field in the one-loop approximation. However, this problem remains unresolved in its entirety at present.

According to the equation (
33
), the torsion field cannot propagate in space-time like the field of scalar (or vector) particles. This statement is not a new result, it can be found in the excellent review of Hehl et al
Hehl et al. 1976
.
To clarify this statement, consider the Klein-Gordon-Fock equation for a scalar field in Minkowski space-time

\displaystyle(\partial_{b}\partial^{b}\phi+\mu^{2}\phi)=0

(35)

and an elementary consequence of this equation. In the case of a plane monochromatic wave,

\phi\propto\mathop{\rm exp}\nolimits(-ikx)
, Eq. (
35
) has a nonzero solution only if

\displaystyle k^{0}=\pm\sqrt{\mu^{2}+{\bf k}^{2}}.

(36)

In the case of a scalar particle, we have
\mu^{2}>0
. Therefore, scalar particles can propagate in space-time.

However, in Eq. (
33
) the role of the ”squared mass” is played by the constant

\displaystyle\mu^{2}=-32\pi^{2}l_{P}^{-2}\left(\ln\frac{2\pi}{l_{P}m}\right)^{-1}\left[1+\left(C_{V^{(0)}}\right)^{2}\right]<0.

(37)

Indeed, consider the case
\mathop{{\mathfrak{t}}}\nolimits\longrightarrow 0
and
\mathop{{\mathfrak{R}}}\nolimits^{a}_{\ \ bcd}\longrightarrow 0
.
In this case, one can choose a gauge such as
\omega_{\mu}\longrightarrow 0
(see (
11
), so that
{\cal D}_{\mu}\longrightarrow\partial_{\mu}
.
As a result, we can omit the last two terms in Eq. (
33
),
and this equation breaks down into the following two equations:

\displaystyle\left(\partial_{b}\partial^{b}\mathop{{\mathfrak{t}}}\nolimits^{a}-32\pi^{2}l_{P}^{-2}\left(\ln\frac{2\pi}{l_{P}m}\right)^{-1}\left[1+\left(C_{V^{(0)}}\right)^{2}\right]\right)=0,

(38)

and
\partial_{b}\mathop{{\mathfrak{t}}}\nolimits^{b}=0
. Comparison of Eqs. (
35
) and (
38
) leads to equality (
37
).
But then for
{\bf k}^{2}\ll|\mu^{2}|
we have
k^{0}=-i|\mu|
, and the wave decays exponentially in time:

\mathop{{\mathfrak{t}}}\nolimits^{a}\propto\mathop{\rm exp}\nolimits(-|\mu|x^{0})
. Thus, only homogeneous stationary solutions or those close to them can be nonzero.

Such a situation (with an inverted mass square) takes place in Landau’s theory of second-order phase transitions
below critical temperature.

There is also a significant difference between Eq. (
33
) and the corresponding equation in Landau’s theory: in Eq. (
33
) there is no cubic term, but there is a quadratic term containing the first derivative. Obviously, Eq. (
33
) always has a zero solution for the torsion tensor. Finding nonzero solutions to this equation will be the subject of further research.
Here we only point out the possible existence of a certain solution. Let
\mathop{{\mathfrak{t}}}\nolimits^{a}\longrightarrow 0
and gradient terms are unimportant,
and we will expand the solution in this small quantity. Then in the equations (
26
) and (
27
) the right parts can be set equal to zero in the leading approximation, and the curvature tensor can be taken in the form:

{\mathop{{\mathfrak{R}}}\nolimits}^{ab}_{cd}=-H^{2}\left(\delta^{a}_{c}\delta^{b}_{d}-\delta^{a}_{d}\delta^{b}_{c}\right)
(curvature of de Sitter space).
Then
{\mathop{{\mathfrak{R}}}\nolimits}^{a}_{b}=-3H^{2}\delta^{a}_{b}
, and in the case
\mathop{{\mathfrak{t}}}\nolimits^{a}_{;b}=0
Eq. (
33
) is rewritten as

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{3}\mu^{2}+H^{2}\right)\mathop{{\mathfrak{t}}}\nolimits^{a}=0.

(39)

Here
\mu^{2}
is given in (
37
). An equation (
39
) can have a non-zero solution if the parenthesis in that equation vanishes.
This effect is achieved with a certain and sufficiently large value of the parameter

\displaystyle H^{2}=\frac{32\pi^{2}}{3l_{P}^{2}}\left(\ln\frac{2\pi}{l_{P}m}\right)^{-1}\left[1+\left(C_{V^{(0)}}\right)^{2}\right]\ll\frac{4\pi^{2}}{l_{P}^{2}}\sim k_{\mbox{max}}^{2}.

This superficial consideration shows that if there are no conditions for the existence of the torsion tensor at the present epoch, then such conditions could take place near the Big Bang point.

Finally, we point out that, according to (
6
), we have the relation

\displaystyle J^{5a}=\left(4/l_{P}^{2}\right)\mathop{{\mathfrak{t}}}\nolimits^{a},\quad J^{5a}\equiv\overline{\Psi}\gamma^{5}\gamma^{a}\Psi.

(40)

Equation (
40
) shows that if the torsion tensor is not equal to zero, then the axial current is also not equal to zero. The physical consequences following from this are not clear to the author.

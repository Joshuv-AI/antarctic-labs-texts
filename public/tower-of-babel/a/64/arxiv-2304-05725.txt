Topological aspects of quasi *-algebras with sufficiently many *-representations
Giorgia Bellomonte, Camillo Trapani

Abstract
. Quasi *-algebras possessing a sufficient family ℳ \mathcal{M} of invariant positive sesquilinear forms carry several topologies related to ℳ \mathcal{M} which make every *-representation continuous. This leads to define the class of locally convex quasi GA*-algebras whose main feature consists in the fact that the family of their bounded elements, with respect to the family ℳ \mathcal{M} , is a dense C*-algebra.

Primary 46K05, 46K10, 47L60, 47A07; Secondary 08A55

1.
Introduction

Locally convex quasi *-algebras
(\mathfrak{A}[\tau],{\mathfrak{A}}_{\scriptscriptstyle 0})
arise often when taking
the completion
\mathfrak{A}:=\widetilde{\mathfrak{A}_{0}}[\tau]
of a locally convex *-algebra

\mathfrak{A}_{0}[\tau]

with separately (but not jointly) continuous multiplication (this was, in fact, the case considered at an early stage of the theory, concerning applications in quantum physics). Concrete examples are provided by families of operators acting in rigged Hilbert spaces or by certain families of unbounded operators acting on a common domain
\mathcal{D}
of a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
.
For a synthesis of the theory and of its applications we refer to
[
7
]
.

The study of this structure and the analysis performed also in
[
2
,
5
,
6
,
8
,
10
]
made it clear that the most regular situation occurs when the locally convex quasi *-algebra
(\mathfrak{A}[\tau],{\mathfrak{A}}_{\scriptscriptstyle 0})
under consideration possesses a
sufficiently rich
family
{\mathcal{I}}_{{\mathfrak{A}}_{\scriptscriptstyle 0}}(\mathfrak{A})
of invariant positive sesquilinear forms on
\mathfrak{A}\times\mathfrak{A}
(see below for definitions); they allow a GNS construction similar to that defined by a positive linear functional on a *-algebra
{\mathfrak{A}}_{\scriptscriptstyle 0}
.
The basic idea where this paper moves from, is to consider a quasi *-algebra
(\mathfrak{A},{\mathfrak{A}}_{\scriptscriptstyle 0})
where one can introduce a locally convex topology by means of the set of sesquilinear forms
{\mathcal{I}}_{{\mathfrak{A}}_{\scriptscriptstyle 0}}(\mathfrak{A})
itself. In the best circumstances we expect a behavior analogous to that of a *-algebra
{\mathfrak{B}}_{0}
whose topology can be defined via families of C*-seminorms:

p_{M}(x)=\sup_{\omega\in M;\omega({\sf e})=1}\omega(x^{*}x)^{1/2}

where
M
is a convenient set of positive linear functionals on
{\mathfrak{B}}_{0}

[
11
]
.

For this reason, we start from a pure algebraic setup, i.e.,
(\mathfrak{A},{\mathfrak{A}}_{\scriptscriptstyle 0})
is a quasi *-algebra and we suppose that it has a sufficiently large
{\mathcal{I}}_{{\mathfrak{A}}_{\scriptscriptstyle 0}}(\mathfrak{A})
(in the sense that, for some convenient subset
\mathcal{M}\subset{\mathcal{I}}_{{\mathfrak{A}}_{\scriptscriptstyle 0}}(\mathfrak{A})
and for every
a\in\mathfrak{A},a\neq 0
there exists
\varphi\in\mathcal{M}
such that
\varphi(a,a)>0
).
Starting from this set
\mathcal{M}
, we undertake the construction of locally convex topologies on
\mathfrak{A}
selecting in particular those under which each (sufficiently regular) *-representation is continuous. This analysis leads to the selection of a class of locally convex quasi *-algebras
(\mathfrak{A}[\tau],{\mathfrak{A}}_{\scriptscriptstyle 0})
(called locally convex quasi GA*-algebras) whose bounded elements constitute a C*-algebra. The paper is organized as follows.
In Section
2
some preliminary notions on quasi *-algebras, their topologies and their representations are summarized.
In Section
3
we introduce the order defined by a family
\mathcal{M}\subset{\mathcal{I}}_{{\mathfrak{A}}_{\scriptscriptstyle 0}}(\mathfrak{A})
whose related wedge becomes a cone when the family
\mathcal{M}
is sufficiently rich.
In Section
4
, given
\mathcal{M}\subset{\mathcal{I}}_{{\mathfrak{A}}_{\scriptscriptstyle 0}}(\mathfrak{A})
, we introduce two notions of bounded elements: those bounded with respect to a family
\mathcal{M}
and those related to the order defined by
\mathcal{M}
. These two notions turn out to be equivalent and every *-representation produces a bounded operator when acting on a bounded element.
In Section
5
the topologies generated by a family
\mathcal{M}\subset{\mathcal{I}}_{{\mathfrak{A}}_{\scriptscriptstyle 0}}(\mathfrak{A})
are investigated and in Section
6
we finally introduce locally convex quasi GA*-algebras and study some properties of them. Locally convex quasi GA*-algebras are characterized by the fact that their topology is equivalent to that generated by some
\mathcal{M}\subset{\mathcal{I}}_{{\mathfrak{A}}_{\scriptscriptstyle 0}}(\mathfrak{A})
as in Section
5
.

2.
Basic definitions and facts

We begin with some preliminaries; we refer to
[
7
]
for details.

A
quasi *-algebra

(\mathfrak{A},{\mathfrak{A}}_{\scriptscriptstyle 0})
is a pair consisting of a vector space
\mathfrak{A}
and a *-algebra
{\mathfrak{A}}_{\scriptscriptstyle 0}
contained in
\mathfrak{A}
as a subspace and such that

(i)

\mathfrak{A}
carries an involution
a\mapsto a^{*}
extending the involution of
{\mathfrak{A}}_{\scriptscriptstyle 0}
;

(ii)

\mathfrak{A}
is a bimodule over
\mathfrak{A}_{0}
and the module multiplications extend the multiplication of
{\mathfrak{A}}_{\scriptscriptstyle 0}
. In particular, the following associative laws hold:

(xa)y=x(ay);\ \ a(xy)=(ax)y,\quad\forall\ a\in\mathfrak{A},\ x,y\in{\mathfrak{A}}_{\scriptscriptstyle 0};

(iii)

(ax)^{*}=x^{*}a^{*}
, for every
a\in\mathfrak{A}
and
x\in{\mathfrak{A}}_{\scriptscriptstyle 0}
.

The

identity
of
(\mathfrak{A},{\mathfrak{A}}_{\scriptscriptstyle 0})
, if any, is a necessarily unique element
{\sf e}\in{\mathfrak{A}}_{\scriptscriptstyle 0}
, such that

a{\sf e}=a={\sf e}a
, for all
a\in\mathfrak{A}
.

We will always suppose that

\displaystyle ax=0,\;\forall x\in{\mathfrak{A}}_{\scriptscriptstyle 0}\Rightarrow a=0

\displaystyle ax=0,\;\forall a\in\mathfrak{A}\Rightarrow x=0.

These two conditions are clearly satisfied if
(\mathfrak{A},{\mathfrak{A}}_{\scriptscriptstyle 0})
has an identity
{\sf e}
.

Definition 2.1
.

A quasi *-algebra
(\mathfrak{A},{\mathfrak{A}}_{\scriptscriptstyle 0})
is said to be
locally convex
if
\mathfrak{A}
is a locally convex vector space, with a topology
\tau
enjoying the following properties

(lc1)

x\mapsto x^{*}
,
x\in\mathfrak{A}_{\scriptscriptstyle 0}
, is continuous;

(lc2)

for every
a\in\mathfrak{A}
, the maps
x\mapsto ax
and

x\mapsto xa
, from
\mathfrak{A}_{\scriptscriptstyle 0}
into
\mathfrak{A}
,
x\in\mathfrak{A}_{\scriptscriptstyle 0}
, are continuous;

(lc3)

\overline{{\mathfrak{A}}_{\scriptscriptstyle 0}}^{\tau}=\mathfrak{A}
; i.e.,
{\mathfrak{A}}_{\scriptscriptstyle 0}
is dense in
\mathfrak{A}[\tau]
.

In particular, if
\tau
is a norm topology, with norm
\|\cdot\|
, and

(bq*)

\|a^{*}\|=\|a\|,\;\forall a\in\mathfrak{A}

then,
(\mathfrak{A}[\|\cdot\|],{\mathfrak{A}}_{\scriptscriptstyle 0})
is called a
normed quasi *-algebra
and a
Banach quasi *-algebra
if the normed vector space
\mathfrak{A}[\|\cdot\|]
is complete.

Let
\mathcal{D}
be a dense vector subspace of a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
. Let us consider the following families of linear operators acting on
\mathcal{D}
:

\displaystyle{{\mathcal{L}}^{\mbox{\scriptsize${\dagger}$}}(\mathcal{D},\mathcal{H})}

\displaystyle=\{X\mbox{ closable},D(X)=\mathcal{D};\;D(X{\mbox{*}})\supset\mathcal{D}\}

\displaystyle{{\mathcal{L}}^{\dagger}(\mathcal{D})}

\displaystyle=\{X\in{\mathcal{L}}^{\mbox{\scriptsize${\dagger}$}}(\mathcal{D},\mathcal{H}):X\mathcal{D}\subset\mathcal{D};\;X{\mbox{*}}\mathcal{D}\subset\mathcal{D}\}

\displaystyle{{\mathcal{L}}^{\dagger}(\mathcal{D})_{b}}

\displaystyle=\{Y\in{\mathcal{L}}^{\dagger}(\mathcal{D});\,\overline{Y}\mbox{ bounded}\},

where
\overline{Y}
denotes the closure of

.
The involution in
{\mathcal{L}}^{\mbox{\scriptsize${\dagger}$}}(\mathcal{D},\mathcal{H})
is defined by
X^{\dagger}:=X{\mbox{*}}\upharpoonright\mathcal{D}
, the restriction of
X{\mbox{*}}
, the adjoint of

\mathcal{D}
.

The set

is a *-algebra; more precisely, it is the maximal O*-algebra on
\mathcal{D}
.
(for the theories of O*-algebras and *-representations we refer to
[
9
]
).

Furthermore,
{\mathcal{L}}^{\mbox{\scriptsize${\dagger}$}}(\mathcal{D},\mathcal{H})
is also a
partial *-algebra

[
3
]
with respect to the following operations: the usual sum
X_{1}+X_{2}
,
the scalar multiplication
\lambda X
, the involution
X\mapsto X^{\mbox{\scriptsize${\dagger}$}}:=X{\mbox{*}}\raisebox{1.99168pt}{$\upharpoonright$}{\mathcal{D}}
and the
(weak)

partial multiplication

(2.1)

X_{1}\mbox{\raisebox{1.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\square}$}}X_{2}={X_{1}}^{\mbox{\scriptsize${\dagger}$}}{\mbox{*}}X_{2},

defined whenever
X_{2}
is a weak right multiplier of

X_{1}
(we shall write

or

), that is, whenever
X_{2}{\mathcal{D}}\subset{\mathcal{D}}({X_{1}}^{\mbox{\scriptsize${\dagger}$}}{\mbox{*}})
and
X_{1}{\mbox{*}}{\mathcal{D}}\subset{\mathcal{D}}(X_{2}{\mbox{*}}).

The following topologies on
{\mathcal{L}}^{\mbox{\scriptsize${\dagger}$}}(\mathcal{D},\mathcal{H})
will be used in this paper.

The
weak topology

{\sf t}_{w}
on
{\mathcal{L}}^{\mbox{\scriptsize${\dagger}$}}(\mathcal{D},\mathcal{H})
is defined by the seminorms

r_{\xi,\eta}(X)=|\langle{X\xi}|{\eta}\rangle|,\quad X\in{\mathcal{L}}^{\mbox{\scriptsize${\dagger}$}}(\mathcal{D},\mathcal{H}),\,\xi,\eta\in\mathcal{D}.

The
strong topology

{\sf t}_{s}
on
{\mathcal{L}}^{\mbox{\scriptsize${\dagger}$}}(\mathcal{D},\mathcal{H})
is defined by the seminorms

p_{\xi}(X)=\|X\xi\|,\quad X\in{\mathcal{L}}^{\mbox{\scriptsize${\dagger}$}}(\mathcal{D},\mathcal{H}),\,\xi\in\mathcal{D}.

The
strong* topology

{\sf t}_{s^{\ast}}
on
{\mathcal{L}}^{\mbox{\scriptsize${\dagger}$}}(\mathcal{D},\mathcal{H})
is usually defined by the seminorms

p^{*}_{\xi}(X)=\max\{\|X\xi\|,\|X^{\mbox{\scriptsize${\dagger}$}}\xi\|\},\,\xi\in\mathcal{D}.

Then,
({{\mathcal{L}}^{\mbox{\scriptsize${\dagger}$}}(\mathcal{D},\mathcal{H})}[{\sf t}_{s^{\ast}}],{{\mathcal{L}}^{\dagger}(\mathcal{D})_{b}})

is a complete locally convex quasi *-algebra
[
7
, Section 6.1]
.

Let us denote by
t_{\dagger}

the graph topology on
\mathcal{D}
defined by the set of seminorms

\xi\in\mathcal{D}\to\|X\xi\|;\;X\in{\mathcal{L}}^{\mbox{\scriptsize${\dagger}$}}(\mathcal{D},\mathcal{H}).

The family of all bounded subsets of
\mathcal{D}[t_{\dagger}]
is denoted by
{\mathfrak{B}}
.

We will indicate by
{\sf t}\!{\sf t}_{u}
,
{\sf t}\!{\sf t}^{u}
and
{\sf t}\!{\sf t}^{u}_{*}
, respectively, the
uniform
topologies defined by the following families of seminorms:

for

:
p_{\mathcal{B}}(X)=\sup_{\xi,\eta\in\mathcal{B}}|\langle{X\xi}|{\eta}\rangle|,\quad\mathcal{B}\in{\mathfrak{B}}
;

for
{\sf t}\!{\sf t}^{u}
:
p^{\mathcal{B}}(X)=\sup_{\xi\in\mathcal{B}}\|X\xi\|,\quad\mathcal{B}\in{\mathfrak{B}}
;

for
{\sf t}\!{\sf t}^{u}_{*}

p^{\mathcal{B}}_{*}(X)=\max\{p^{\mathcal{B}}(X),p^{\mathcal{B}}(X^{\mbox{\scriptsize${\dagger}$}})\},\quad\mathcal{B}\in{\mathfrak{B}}
.

It is easy to see that

p_{\mathcal{B}}(X)\leq\gamma_{\mathcal{B}}\,p^{\mathcal{B}}(X)\leq\gamma_{\mathcal{B}}p^{\mathcal{B}}_{*}(X),\quad\forall X\in{\mathcal{L}}^{\mbox{\scriptsize${\dagger}$}}(\mathcal{D},\mathcal{H});

moreover,

p_{\mathcal{B}}(X^{\mbox{\scriptsize${\dagger}$}}\mbox{\raisebox{1.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\square}$}}X)=p^{\mathcal{B}}(X)^{2}\mbox{ whenever }X^{\mbox{\scriptsize${\dagger}$}}\mbox{\raisebox{1.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\square}$}}X\mbox{ is well--defined}.

As shown in
[
3
, Proposition 4.2.3]

{\mathcal{L}}^{\mbox{\scriptsize${\dagger}$}}(\mathcal{D},\mathcal{H})[{\sf t}\!{\sf t}^{u}_{*}]
is complete.

.

Let

. An inner product

\displaystyle\langle{\lambda_{\varphi}(a)}|{\lambda_{\varphi}(b)}\rangle:=\varphi(a,b),\quad\forall\;a,b\in\mathfrak{A}.

Denote by

.

We denote by

Moreover, if

The sesquilinear forms of

.

Let

{\sf e}

(ii)

Families of forms and order structure

As discussed extensively in

Definition 3.1

Theorem 4.8
.

Let
(\mathfrak{A},{\mathfrak{A}}_{\scriptscriptstyle 0})
be a quasi *-algebra with sufficient
\mathcal{M}\subset{\mathcal{I}}_{{\mathfrak{A}}_{\scriptscriptstyle 0}}(\mathfrak{A})

i)

ii)

a
is
\mathcal{M}
-bounded; i.e.,
(Definition
4.1

there exists
\gamma_{a}>0

\varphi(ax,ax)\leq{\gamma^{\prime}_{a}}\varphi(x,x),\,\forall x\in{\mathfrak{A}}_{\scriptscriptstyle 0},

for every

We prove it for symmetric elements.

i)\Rightarrow ii)

It is clear that if

\gamma>0

\mathcal{M}
-bounded.

ii)\Rightarrow iii)

Assume that

is
\mathcal{M}
-bounded. If
\varphi\in\mathcal{M}
, denote by

the corresponding GNS representation. Then,

\displaystyle|\langle{\pi_{\varphi}(a)\lambda_{\varphi}(x)}|{\lambda_{\varphi}(y)}\rangle|

\displaystyle=|\varphi(ax,y)|\leq\|a\|^{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathcal{M}}}_{\scriptscriptstyle{\rm b}}\varphi(x,x)^{1/2}\varphi(y,y)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\|a\|^{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathcal{M}}}_{\scriptscriptstyle{\rm b}}\|\lambda_{\varphi}(x)\|=\|a\|^{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathcal{M}}}_{\scriptscriptstyle{\rm b}}\varphi(x,x)^{1/2},\quad\,\forall x,y\in\mathfrak{A}_{0}.

Suppose that

satisfies

. Let

and

|\varphi(ax,x)|\leq\varphi(ax,ax)^{1/2}\varphi(x,x)^{1/2}\leq{\gamma^{\prime}_{a}}^{1/2}\varphi(x,x).

iv)\Rightarrow i)
It is straightforward.
∎

Throughout this section we will suppose that
(\mathfrak{A},{\mathfrak{A}}_{\scriptscriptstyle 0})
is a quasi *-algebra with unit
{\sf e}

is a sufficient set of forms.
Then
\mathcal{M}

(again,
\frac{1}{0}=\infty
). It is easily seen that
\mathcal{M}
is strongly well-behaved. In this case, the set of
\mathcal{M}
-bounded elements is the C*-algebra
L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}},m)
.
The pair
(L^{p}_{\rm loc}({\mathbb{R}},m),L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}},m))
is a locally convex quasi GA*-algebra.

The following theorem motivates in our opinion the attention devoted to locally convex quasi GA*-algebras.

Theorem 6.6
.

Let
(\mathfrak{A}[\tau],{\mathfrak{A}}_{\scriptscriptstyle 0})
be a locally convex quasi GA*-algebras with unit and a well-behaved
\mathcal{M}\subset{\mathcal{I}}_{{\mathfrak{A}}_{\scriptscriptstyle 0}}(\mathfrak{A})
. Then:

(a)

every
\varphi\in\mathcal{M}
is jointly
\tau
-continuous;

(b)

every
\mathcal{M}
-regular *-representation of
(\mathfrak{A}[\tau],{\mathfrak{A}}_{\scriptscriptstyle 0})
is
(\tau,{\sf t}_{s^{\ast}})
-continuous;

(c)

the set
\mathfrak{A}_{\scriptscriptstyle{\rm b}}^{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathcal{M}}}
of bounded elements is a C*-algebra with respect to the norm
\|\cdot\|^{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathcal{M}}}_{\scriptscriptstyle{\rm b}}
.

Proof.

(a): Each
\varphi
is
\tau^{\mathfrak{F}}_{*}
-continuous by the construction itself of
\tau^{\mathfrak{F}}_{*}
; the statement then follows from the assumption
\tau\approx\tau^{\mathfrak{F}}_{*}
.

(b): This follows from (a). Indeed if
\pi
is
\mathcal{M}
-regular, then for every
\xi\in\mathcal{D}_{\pi}
, the sesquilinear form
\varphi_{\xi}
(see (
4.3
)) is in
\mathcal{M}
; then, it is
\tau^{\mathfrak{F}}_{*}
-continuous. Then, there exists
\mathcal{F}\in\mathfrak{F}
such that

|\langle{\pi(a)\xi}|{\pi(b)\xi}\rangle|\leq p_{*}^{\mathcal{F}}(a)p_{*}^{\mathcal{F}}(b)

hence

\|\pi(a)\xi\|\leq p_{*}^{\mathcal{F}}(a),\quad\mbox{and }\quad\|\pi(a^{*})\xi\|\leq p_{*}^{\mathcal{F}}(a^{*})=p_{*}^{\mathcal{F}}(a),\quad\forall a\in\mathfrak{A}

then for every
\xi\in\mathcal{D}_{\pi}
there exists
\mathcal{F}\in\mathfrak{F}
such that

p^{*}_{\xi}(\pi(a))=\max\{\|\pi(a)\xi\|,\|\pi(a)^{\mbox{\scriptsize${\dagger}$}}\xi\|\}\leq p_{*}^{\mathcal{F}}(a).

(c): We have just to prove the completeness of the set
\mathfrak{A}_{\scriptscriptstyle{\rm b}}^{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathcal{M}}}
with respect to the norm
\|\cdot\|^{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathcal{M}}}_{\scriptscriptstyle{\rm b}}
. Let
\{a_{n}\}\subset\mathfrak{A}_{\scriptscriptstyle{\rm b}}^{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathcal{M}}}
be a
\|\cdot\|^{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathcal{M}}}_{\scriptscriptstyle{\rm b}}
-Cauchy sequence, then for every
\epsilon>0
there exists
n_{\epsilon}\in\mathbb{N}
such that forall
n,m\geq n_{\epsilon}
it is both
\|a_{n}-a_{m}\|^{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathcal{M}}}_{\scriptscriptstyle{\rm b}}<\epsilon
and
\|a_{n}^{*}-a_{m}^{*}\|^{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathcal{M}}}_{\scriptscriptstyle{\rm b}}<\epsilon
.

Since
\{a_{n}\}\subset\mathfrak{A}_{\scriptscriptstyle{\rm b}}^{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathcal{M}}}
, for every
\varphi\in\mathcal{M}
and every
x_{0}\in{\mathfrak{A}}_{\scriptscriptstyle 0}
, it is

\varphi((a_{n}-a_{m})x,(a_{n}-a_{m})x)\leq(\|a_{n}-a_{m}\|^{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathcal{M}}}_{\scriptscriptstyle{\rm b}})^{2}\varphi(x,x),\,\forall n,m\in\mathbb{N}

and

\varphi((a_{n}^{*}-a_{m}^{*})x,(a_{n}^{*}-a_{m}^{*})x)\leq(\|a_{n}^{*}-a_{m}^{*}\|^{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathcal{M}}}_{\scriptscriptstyle{\rm b}})^{2}\varphi(x,x),\,\forall n,m\in\mathbb{N}

hence, if
\mathcal{F}\in\mathfrak{F}
:

\sup_{\varphi\in\mathcal{F}}\varphi((a_{n}-a_{m})x,(a_{n}-a_{m})x)^{1/2}\leq\|a_{n}-a_{m}\|^{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathcal{M}}}_{\scriptscriptstyle{\rm b}}\sup_{\varphi\in\mathcal{F}}\varphi(x,x)^{1/2},\,\,\forall n,m\in\mathbb{N}

and

\sup_{\varphi\in\mathcal{F}}\varphi((a_{n}^{*}-a_{m}^{*})x,(a_{n}^{*}-a_{m}^{*})x)^{1/2}\leq\|a_{n}^{*}-a_{m}^{*}\|^{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathcal{M}}}_{\scriptscriptstyle{\rm b}}\sup_{\varphi\in\mathcal{F}}\varphi(x,x)^{1/2},\,\,\forall n,m\in\mathbb{N}

by the previous inequalities, for every
\mathcal{F}\in\mathfrak{F}
, we get

p^{\mathcal{F}}_{*}(a_{n}-a_{m})=\max\left\{p^{\mathcal{F}}(a_{n}-a_{m}),p^{\mathcal{F}}((a_{n}-a_{m})^{*})\right\}<\epsilon p^{\mathcal{F}}({\sf e}),\,\forall n,m\geq n_{\epsilon}.

Then,
\{a_{n}\}
is a
\tau^{\mathfrak{F}}_{*}
-Cauchy sequence. Since
\mathfrak{A}
is
\tau^{\mathfrak{F}}_{*}
-complete, there exists
a\in\mathfrak{A}
such that
a_{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\tau^{\mathfrak{F}}_{*}}}{{\to}}a
.

The limit
a
is
\mathcal{M}
-bounded; indeed, if
\varphi\in\mathcal{M}
and
x\in{\mathfrak{A}}_{\scriptscriptstyle 0}
, we have

\varphi(ax,ax)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\varphi(a_{n}x,a_{n}x)\leq\limsup_{n\to\infty}{(\|a_{n}\|^{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathcal{M}}}_{\scriptscriptstyle{\rm b}})}^{2}\varphi(x,x).

The sequence
\{\|a_{n}\|\}
is Cauchy too and bounded; therefore
a
is
\mathcal{M}
-bounded.

It remains to prove that
\|a_{n}-a\|^{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathcal{M}}}_{\scriptscriptstyle{\rm b}}\to 0
as
n\to\infty
. For every
\epsilon>0
let
n,m>n_{\epsilon}
then

\|a_{n}-a_{m}\|^{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathcal{M}}}_{\scriptscriptstyle{\rm b}}<\epsilon
. Now let
m\to\infty
, then

\|a_{n}-a\|^{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathcal{M}}}_{\scriptscriptstyle{\rm b}}=\lim_{m\to\infty}\|a_{n}-a_{m}\|^{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathcal{M}}}_{\scriptscriptstyle{\rm b}}\leq\epsilon.

∎

Conclusion

In this paper we have constructed some topologies on a quasi *-algebra
(\mathfrak{A},{\mathfrak{A}}_{\scriptscriptstyle 0})
starting from a sufficiently rich family of sesquilinear forms that behave regularly.
This study led us to introduce a new class of locally convex quasi *-algebras, that we have named GA* since their definition closely recalls that one of A*-algebras. Several questions remain however still open. We mention some of them.

(a) When does a (locally convex) quasi *-algebra
(\mathfrak{A},{\mathfrak{A}}_{\scriptscriptstyle 0})
possess a sufficient family
\mathcal{M}
of sesquilinear forms of
{\mathcal{I}}_{{\mathfrak{A}}_{\scriptscriptstyle 0}}(\mathfrak{A})
?

(b) Under what conditions is a locally convex quasi *-algebra
(\mathfrak{A}[\tau],{\mathfrak{A}}_{\scriptscriptstyle 0})
a locally convex quasi GA*-algebra? We already know that there exist Banach quasi *-algebras
(\mathfrak{A}[\|\cdot\|],{\mathfrak{A}}_{\scriptscriptstyle 0})
for which the set of continuous elements of
{\mathcal{I}}_{{\mathfrak{A}}_{\scriptscriptstyle 0}}(\mathfrak{A})
reduces to
\{0\}

[
7
, Example 3.1.29]
and the sesquilinear forms of a well-behaved family
\mathcal{M}
of ips-forms are automatically continuous in a locally convex quasi GA*-algebra. Hence, in general, the two notions do not coincide.

(c) Under which conditions is it possible to lighten the definition of
well-behaved
family of ips-forms (Definition
6.1
) by removing (
wb
3
) and/or (
wb
4
)?

We hope to discuss these problems in a future paper.

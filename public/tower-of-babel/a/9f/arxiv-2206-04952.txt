Abstract
. Let X X be a closed subscheme of codimension e e in a projective space. One says that X X satisfies property 𝐍 d , p {\bf N}_{d,p} , if the i i -th syzygies of the homogeneous coordinate ring are generated by elements of degree < d + i <d+i for 0 ≤ i ≤ p 0\leq i\leq p . The geometric and algebraic properties of smooth projective varieties satisfying property 𝐍 2 , e {\bf N}_{2,e} are well understood, and the complete classification of these varieties is a classical result. 1.
Introduction

A classical problem in algebraic geometry and commutative algebra is the study of
the equations defining projective varieties and of their syzygies. In
[
14
]
, D. Eisenbud et al. introduced the condition
{\bf N}_{d,p}
for a graded ring as an indication of the presence of simple syzygies. Let us recall the definition.
Let
R=k[x_{0},\ldots,x_{n}]
denote the homogeneous coordinate ring of the projective space
\mathbb{P}^{n}
over an algebraically closed field
k
of characteristic zero, and let
I_{X}\subset R
denote the homogeneous ideal of an algebraic set
X\subset\mathbb{P}^{n}
of codimension
e

We say that
X
satisfies property
{\bf N}_{d,p}
(
p\leq\infty

\beta_{i,j}(X):=\dim\mathrm{Tor}^{R}_{i}(R/I_{X},k)_{i+j}=0,

for
i\leq p
and
j\geq d
. This is the same as property
{\bf N}_{p}
defined by Green and Lazarsfeld in
[
18
]
if
X
is projectively normal and
d=2
. Notice that property
{\bf N}_{d,\infty}
means that
X
is
d
-regular. One says that

is arithmetically Cohen–Macaulay (ACM) with a
d
-linear resolution if the only non-zero Betti numbers are
\beta_{0,0}(X)
and
\beta_{i,d-1}(X)
for
i=1,2,\ldots,e
.

One of the main interesting problems is to understand geometric properties of algebraic sets
satisfying property
{\bf N}_{d,p}

[
2

satisfies property
{\bf N}_{d,e}

The set of algebraic sets satisfying the above equality is quite large. It contains many known examples such as the algebraic set defined by the ideal of maximal minors of a
1
-generic
d\times(e+d-1)
-matrix of linear forms, or the
(d-1)
-secant variety of a rational normal curve of degree
(e+2d-3)

For

2.
preliminaries

In this section, we will give the basic definitions and results that are needed. For a smooth surface
X
in a projective space we denote by
H_{X}
and
K_{X}
the class of a hyperplane and the canonical divisor, respectively. The structure sheaf of
X
is
\mathcal{O}_{X}
, and the Euler-Poincare characteristic of its structure sheaf is
\chi_{X}=\chi(\mathcal{O}_{X})=1-q+p_{g}
, where
q
is irregularity and
p_{g}
is geometric genus of
X
. The degree of
X
is
d=H^{2}
and the sectional genus
\pi=\frac{1}{2}H(H+K)+1
is the genus of its general hyperplane section. The following lemma is a special case of the Riemann-Roch Theorem.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
X\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{5}
be a smooth surface of degree
d
, sectional genus
\pi
, irregularity
q
and geometric genus
p_{g}
. Then we have

\chi(\mathcal{I}_{X}(m))=\chi(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{5}}(m))-\binom{m+1}{2}d+m(\pi-1)-1+q-p_{g}.

In fact, we proceed in a similar fashion as in
[
11
, Section 8 and 9]
or
[
39
]
and give a complete geometric classification or characterization of projective complex surfaces of degree
10
satisfying property
{\bf N}_{3,3}
via the adjunction process. Thus we recall a result of Sommese and Van de Ven for a surface over
\mathbb{C}
.

Theorem 2.2

(
[
38
,
42
,
37
]
, see also
[
11
, Theorem 8.1]
)
.

Let
X
be a smooth surface in
\mathbb{P}^{n}
over

is non-special of dimension

and it defines a birational morphism

onto a smooth surface

(-1)
–curves

(i)

(ii)

(iii)

Proposition 2.3

(
[

)
.

Let

be a surface over a field of arbitrary characteristic. Suppose

is base point free and that the image

under the adjunction map

is a surface of expected degree

We also need the Hodge index theorem.

Theorem 2.4
.

[
6
, Lemma 2.5.2]

Let
X\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{n}
be an irreducible surface. Then the intersection matrix

\begin{pmatrix}H^{2}&H\cdot K\\
H\cdot K&K^{2}\end{pmatrix}

is indefinite, unless
H
and

are numerical dependent.

To apply Theorem

. We have

H_{1}^{2}=(H+K)^{2}\text{ and }H_{1}\cdot K_{1}=(H+K)\cdot K.

, where

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
X=\mathbb{P}^{2}(p_{1},\ldots,p_{\ell})
be the blow–up of
\mathbb{P}^{2}
in
\ell

the corresponding exceptional divisors and by

the pullback of a general line in

to

is a very ample linear system of dimension

is a rational surface of degree

Proof.

Set

\pi=\frac{H(H+K)}{2}+1=\binom{b_{0}-1}{2}-\sum\limits_{i=1}^{\ell}\binom{b_{i}}{2}.

∎

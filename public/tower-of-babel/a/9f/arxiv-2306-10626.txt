Exclusive η c \eta_{c} production from small- x x evolved Odderon at a electron-ion collider Preprint: ZTF-EP-23-03
Sanjin Benić, Davor Horvatić, Abhiram Kaushik, Eric Andreas Vivoda

Abstract
We compute exclusive η c \eta_{c} production in high energy electron-nucleon and electron-nucleus collisions that is sensitive to the Odderon. In perturbative QCD the Odderon is a C C -odd color singlet consisting of at least three t t -channel gluons exchanged with the target. By using the Color Glass Condensate effective theory our result describes the Odderon exchange at the high collision energies that would be reached at a future electron-ion collider. The Odderon distribution is evolved to small- x x using the Balitsky-Kovchegov evolution equation with running coupling corrections. We find that while at low momentum transfers t t the cross section off a proton is dominated by the Primakoff process, the Odderon becomes relevant at larger momentum transfers of | t | ≥ 1.5 |t|\geq 1.5 GeV 2 . We point that the Odderon could also be extracted at low- t t using neutron targets since the Primakoff component is strongly suppressed. In the case of nuclear targets, the Odderon cross section becomes enhanced thanks to the mass number of the nuclear target. The gluon saturation effect induces a shift in the diffractive pattern with respect to the Primakoff process that could be used as a signal for the Odderon.

is a Wilson line accounting for multiple scattering on a electromagnetic field of the target
-ZeT_{A}(\boldsymbol{x}_{\perp})/\boldsymbol{\partial}_{\perp}^{2}

Baltz:2001dp
;
Gelis:2001da
;
Gelis:2001dh
. Here the transverse charge density is given as
ZT_{A}(\boldsymbol{x}_{\perp})
.
Because of the
\alpha
suppression we are ignoring multiple scatterings and expand the eikonal phase to the first nontrivial order. Passing to the variable
\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{\perp}
instead of
\boldsymbol{b}_{\perp}
we have

\Omega(\boldsymbol{r}_{\perp},\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{\perp})=-8\pi\mathrm{i}q_{c}Z\alpha\sin\left(\frac{\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{\perp}\cdot\boldsymbol{r}_{\perp}}{2}\right)\frac{T_{A}(\Delta_{\perp})}{\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{\perp}^{2}}\,,

(31)

which is the same as Eq. (22) in
Dumitru:2019qec
up to a factor due to the difference in the definition. We also obtain the Fourier moments as

\Omega_{2k+1}(r_{\perp},\Delta_{\perp})=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\mathrm{d}\phi_{r\Delta}\Omega(\boldsymbol{r}_{\perp},\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{\perp})\cos((2k+1)\phi_{r\Delta})=-8\pi\mathrm{i}q_{c}Z\alpha(-1)^{k}J_{2k+1}\left(\frac{r_{\perp}\Delta_{\perp}}{2}\right)\frac{T_{A}(\Delta_{\perp})}{\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{\perp}^{2}}\,,

(32)

that are to be used directly in (
24
).

At this point it is useful to obtain an estimate in the
m_{c}\to\infty
limit, similar to what was done for the Odderon in (

). We get

\frac{\mathrm{d}\sigma}{\mathrm{d}|t|}\simeq\frac{\pi q_{c}^{4}\alpha^{3}Z^{2}N_{c}\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{P}}^{2}(0)}{m_{c}^{5}|t|}T_{A}^{2}(\sqrt{|t|})\,,

(33)

which displays the characteristic
1/t
Coulomb behavior in contrast to the Odderon case (
27
) where we have instead a suppression factor
|t|/m_{c}^{4}
. Note that
T_{A}(\Delta_{\perp})
is nothing but the electromagnetic charge form factor from the Rosenbluth formula
Peskin:1995ev
.

In order to evaluate the Primakoff cross section numerically we must specify the
profile function
T_{A}(\boldsymbol{b}_{\perp})
. For the proton (neutron) targets we are replacing
ZT_{A}(\Delta_{\perp})\to F^{p,n}_{1}(\Delta_{\perp})
, respectively, with
F_{1}^{p,n}(\Delta_{\perp})
being the proton (neutron) charge form factors for which we are using a recent determination from
Ye:2017gyb
.
For a nucleus we use a Woods-Saxon distribution, see (
44
) below. In this work we do not attempt to differentiate between the nuclear electromagnetic distribution and the strong interaction distribution of a nucleus, although in principle they could be different, see
STAR:2022wfe
;
Mantysaari:2022sux
.

III
Numerical solutions of the Odderon evolution at small-
x

Denoting the dipole distribution in the fundamental representation as

\mathcal{D}(\boldsymbol{r}_{\perp},\boldsymbol{b}_{\perp})\equiv\frac{1}{N_{c}}{\rm tr}\left\langle V^{{\dagger}}\left(\boldsymbol{b}_{\perp}+\frac{\boldsymbol{r}_{\perp}}{2}\right)V\left(\boldsymbol{b}_{\perp}-\frac{\boldsymbol{r}_{\perp}}{2}\right)\right\rangle\,,

(34)

the fully impact parameter dependent BK equation reads
Balitsky:1995ub
;
Kovchegov:1999yj

\frac{\partial\mathcal{D}(\boldsymbol{r}_{\perp},\boldsymbol{b}_{\perp})}{\partial Y}=\frac{\alpha_{S}N_{c}}{2\pi^{2}}\int_{\boldsymbol{r}_{1\perp}}\frac{\boldsymbol{r}_{\perp}^{2}}{\boldsymbol{r}_{1\perp}^{2}\boldsymbol{r}_{2\perp}^{2}}\left[\mathcal{D}(\boldsymbol{r}_{1\perp},\boldsymbol{b}_{1\perp})\mathcal{D}(\boldsymbol{r}_{2\perp},\boldsymbol{b}_{2\perp})-\mathcal{D}(\boldsymbol{r}_{\perp},\boldsymbol{b}_{\perp})\right]\,,

(35)

where
\boldsymbol{r}_{2\perp}=\boldsymbol{r}_{\perp}-\boldsymbol{r}_{1\perp}
. In general, we have
\boldsymbol{b}_{1\perp}=\boldsymbol{b}_{\perp}+(\boldsymbol{r}_{\perp}-\boldsymbol{r}_{1\perp})/2
and
\boldsymbol{b}_{2\perp}=\boldsymbol{b}_{\perp}-\boldsymbol{r}_{1\perp}/2
and so (
35
) is non-local in
\boldsymbol{b}_{\perp}
. Solutions of (
35
) lead to unphysically large Coulomb tails in
\boldsymbol{b}_{\perp}
originating from a lack of confining interactions in the BK kernel
Golec-Biernat:2003naj
. This issue has been addressed
Ikeda:2004zp
;
Berger:2010sh
;
Hagiwara:2016kam
;
Cepila:2018faq
, at different levels of sophistication, by various modifications of the kernel in the infrared. In this work we make no attempt to tackle this difficult problem and instead resort to a local approximation
\boldsymbol{b}_{1\perp}\to\boldsymbol{b}_{\perp}
and
\boldsymbol{b}_{2\perp}\to\boldsymbol{b}_{\perp}
used in
Lappi:2013zma
(see also a discussion in
Kowalski:2008sa
) where the
\boldsymbol{b}_{\perp}
-dependence effectively becomes an external parameter.

Splitting the dipole into Pomeron and Odderon pieces as
\mathcal{D}(\boldsymbol{r}_{\perp},\boldsymbol{b}_{\perp})=1-\mathcal{N}(\boldsymbol{r}_{\perp},\boldsymbol{b}_{\perp})+\mathrm{i}\mathcal{O}(\boldsymbol{r}_{\perp},\boldsymbol{b}_{\perp})

leads to
Kovchegov:2003dm
;
Hatta:2005as

\begin{split}\frac{\partial\mathcal{N}(\boldsymbol{r}_{\perp},\boldsymbol{b}_{\perp})}{\partial Y}=\int_{\boldsymbol{r}_{1\perp}}\mathcal{K}_{\rm Bal}(\boldsymbol{r}_{\perp},\boldsymbol{r}_{1\perp},\boldsymbol{r}_{2\perp})&\big[\mathcal{N}(\boldsymbol{r}_{1\perp},\boldsymbol{b}_{\perp})+\mathcal{N}(\boldsymbol{r}_{2\perp},\boldsymbol{b}_{\perp})-\mathcal{N}(\boldsymbol{r}_{\perp},\boldsymbol{b}_{\perp})\\
&+\mathcal{N}(\boldsymbol{r}_{1\perp},\boldsymbol{b}_{\perp})\mathcal{N}(\boldsymbol{r}_{2\perp},\boldsymbol{b}_{\perp})-\mathcal{O}(\boldsymbol{r}_{1\perp},\boldsymbol{b}_{\perp})\mathcal{O}(\boldsymbol{r}_{2\perp},\boldsymbol{b}_{\perp})\big]\,,\end{split}

(36)

\begin{split}\frac{\partial\mathcal{O}(\boldsymbol{r}_{\perp},\boldsymbol{b}_{\perp})}{\partial Y}=\int_{\boldsymbol{r}_{1\perp}}\mathcal{K}_{\rm Bal}(\boldsymbol{r}_{\perp},\boldsymbol{r}_{1\perp},\boldsymbol{r}_{2\perp})&\big[\mathcal{O}(\boldsymbol{r}_{1\perp},\boldsymbol{b}_{\perp})+\mathcal{O}(\boldsymbol{r}_{2\perp},\boldsymbol{b}_{\perp})-\mathcal{O}(\boldsymbol{r}_{\perp},\boldsymbol{b}_{\perp})\\
&-\mathcal{N}(\boldsymbol{r}_{1\perp},\boldsymbol{b}_{\perp})\mathcal{O}(\boldsymbol{r}_{2\perp},\boldsymbol{b}_{\perp})-\mathcal{O}(\boldsymbol{r}_{1\perp},\boldsymbol{b}_{\perp})\mathcal{N}(\boldsymbol{r}_{2\perp},\boldsymbol{b}_{\perp})\big]\,.\end{split}

(37)

In the above Eqs. (
36
), (
37
) we have replaced the conventional BK kernel with the running-coupling kernel (according to the Balitsky’s prescription)
Balitsky:2006wa

\frac{\alpha_{S}N_{c}}{2\pi^{2}}\frac{\boldsymbol{r}_{\perp}^{2}}{\boldsymbol{r}_{1\perp}^{2}\boldsymbol{r}_{2\perp}^{2}}\to\mathcal{K}_{\rm Bal}(\boldsymbol{r}_{\perp},\boldsymbol{r}_{1\perp},\boldsymbol{r}_{2\perp})=\frac{\alpha_{S}(\boldsymbol{r}_{\perp}^{2})N_{c}}{2\pi^{2}}\left[\frac{1}{\boldsymbol{r}_{1\perp}^{2}}\left(\frac{\alpha_{S}(\boldsymbol{r}_{1\perp}^{2})}{\alpha_{S}(\boldsymbol{r}_{2\perp}^{2})}-1\right)+\frac{\boldsymbol{r}_{\perp}^{2}}{\boldsymbol{r}_{1\perp}^{2}\boldsymbol{r}_{2\perp}^{2}}+\frac{1}{\boldsymbol{r}_{2\perp}^{2}}\left(\frac{\alpha_{S}(\boldsymbol{r}_{2\perp}^{2})}{\alpha_{S}(\boldsymbol{r}_{1\perp}^{2})}-1\right)\right]\,,

(38)

that will be used in our numerical computations. Here

\alpha_{S}(\boldsymbol{r}_{\perp}^{2})=\frac{12\pi}{(33-2N_{f})\log\left(\frac{4C^{2}}{\boldsymbol{r}_{\perp}^{2}\Lambda_{\rm QCD}^{2}}+\hat{a}\right)}\,,

(39)

with
Lappi:2013zma

N_{f}=3
,
C^{2}=7.2
,
\Lambda_{\rm QCD}=0.241
GeV and
\hat{a}
is a parameter determined by the condition
\lim_{\boldsymbol{r}_{\perp}^{2}\to\infty}\alpha_{S}(\boldsymbol{r}_{\perp}^{2})=\alpha_{\rm fr}
where
\alpha_{\rm fr}=0.7
.

A similar system of equations was solved in
Motyka:2005ep
;
Lappi:2016gqe
;
Yao:2018vcg
;
Braun:2020vmd
;
Contreras:2020lrh
, but the
b_{\perp}
dependence was not addressed. Nevertheless,
some generic conclusions from these works
also apply to our computations. Thanks to the non-linearity of the BK equation (
35
), the Pomeron and the Odderon do not evolve separately. Only in the small-
r_{\perp}
limit where
\mathcal{N}(\boldsymbol{r}_{\perp},\boldsymbol{b}_{\perp})\to 0
the nonlinear terms in (
37
) can be neglected and the system is decoupled. When this happens, the first two terms in the square brackett (
37
) cancel each other and the Odderon will become exponentially suppressed in rapidity
Kovchegov:2003dm
;
Lappi:2016gqe
;
Yao:2018vcg
. In contrast, in the large
r_{\perp}
region, where
\mathcal{N}(\boldsymbol{r}_{\perp},\boldsymbol{b}_{\perp})\to 1
, the nonlinear terms play an important role to
cancel the first and the second term in the square bracket in (
37
) causing again an exponential suppression
Kovchegov:2003dm
;
Motyka:2005ep
;
Lappi:2016gqe
;
Yao:2018vcg
. Such a lack of geometric scaling seems to be a general feature of not only the Odderon but also higher dipole moments in general
Hagiwara:2016kam
.

III.1
Initial conditions

For the Pomeron initial conditions we use a fit to HERA data from Ref.
Lappi:2013zma
. Therein, the Pomeron for the proton is modelled as

\mathcal{N}(\boldsymbol{r}_{\perp},\boldsymbol{b}_{\perp})=1-\exp\left[-\frac{1}{4}\boldsymbol{r}_{\perp}^{2}Q_{0,p}^{2}(\boldsymbol{r}_{\perp},\boldsymbol{b}_{\perp})\right]\,,

(40)

where

Q_{0,p}^{2}(\boldsymbol{r}_{\perp},\boldsymbol{b}_{\perp})\equiv T_{p}(\boldsymbol{b}_{\perp})\frac{\sigma_{0}}{2}Q_{S,0}^{2}\log\left(\frac{1}{r_{\perp}\Lambda_{\rm QCD}}+e_{c}\mathrm{e}\right)\,,

(41)

T_{p}(\boldsymbol{b}_{\perp})=\frac{1}{\pi R_{p}^{2}}e^{-\boldsymbol{b}_{\perp}^{2}/R_{p}^{2}}\,.

(42)

where we pick up
R_{p}
from the relationship
\pi R_{p}^{2}=\sigma_{0}/2=4\pi B_{p}
. In a recent work by Dumitru, Mäntysaari and Paatelainen
Dumitru:2022ooz
the Odderon for a proton target was calculated starting from quark light-cone wavefunctions at NLO. We refer to this as the DMP model and employ it in our numerical computations.

In case of a nucleus we use again the results from Ref.
Lappi:2013zma
, with the Pomeron distribution given as in (
40
) but
with

Q_{0,A}^{2}(\boldsymbol{r}_{\perp},\boldsymbol{b}_{\perp})\equiv AT_{A}(\boldsymbol{b}_{\perp})\frac{\sigma_{0}}{2}Q_{S,0}^{2}\log\left(\frac{1}{r_{\perp}\Lambda_{\rm QCD}}+e_{c}\mathrm{e}\right)\,,

(43)

in place of
Q_{0,p}^{2}(\boldsymbol{r}_{\perp},\boldsymbol{b}_{\perp})
.
T_{A}(\boldsymbol{b}_{\perp})
is the transverse profile of a nuclear target. The parameters in (
41
) are given as
Q_{S,0}^{2}=0.06
GeV
2
,
e_{c}=18.9
and
\frac{\sigma_{0}}{2}=16.36
mb
Lappi:2013zma
.
T_{A}(\boldsymbol{b}_{\perp})
is obtained by integrating the Woods-Saxon distribution
Lappi:2013zma

T_{A}(\boldsymbol{b}_{\perp})=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}z\frac{n_{A}}{1+\exp\left[\frac{\sqrt{\boldsymbol{b}_{\perp}^{2}+z^{2}}-R_{A}}{d}\right]}\,,

(44)

which is normalized to unity
\int_{\boldsymbol{b}_{\perp}}T_{A}(\boldsymbol{b}_{\perp})=1
. This fixes
n_{A}
as
-8\pi n_{A}d{\rm Li}_{3}(-\mathrm{e}^{R_{A}/d})=1
.
Here
d=0.54
fm,
R_{A}=1.12A^{1/3}-0.86A^{-1/3}
fm
Lappi:2013zma
. These Woods-Saxon parameters are numerically very close to the fit values from
DeVries:1987atn
.

The initial condition of the Odderon for a nuclear target is based on the Jeon-Venugopalan (JV) model
Jeon:2005cf
, which involves a cubic term added to the standard McLerran-Venugopalan small-
x
functional

W[\rho]=\exp\left[-\int_{\boldsymbol{x}_{\perp}}\left(\frac{\delta_{ab}\rho^{a}(\boldsymbol{x}_{\perp})\rho^{b}(\boldsymbol{x}_{\perp})}{2\mu^{2}}-\frac{d_{abc}\rho^{a}(\boldsymbol{x}_{\perp})\rho^{b}(\boldsymbol{x}_{\perp})\rho^{c}(\boldsymbol{x}_{\perp})}{\kappa}\right)\right]\,,

(45)

where

\mu^{2}=\frac{g^{2}}{2}\frac{A}{\pi R_{A}^{2}}\,,\qquad\kappa=g^{3}N_{c}\frac{A^{2}}{(\pi R_{A}^{2})^{2}}\,.

(46)

In
Jeon:2005cf
(see also
Kovchegov:2003dm
), it was found that the Odderon distribution from the above functional takes the following form

\mathcal{O}(\boldsymbol{x}_{\perp},\boldsymbol{y}_{\perp})=-g^{3}C_{3F}\frac{\mu^{6}}{\kappa}\Theta(\boldsymbol{x}_{\perp},\boldsymbol{y}_{\perp})\exp\left[-\frac{g^{2}C_{F}\mu^{2}}{2}\Gamma(\boldsymbol{x}_{\perp},\boldsymbol{y}_{\perp})\right]\,,

(47)

where

C_{F}=\frac{N_{c}^{2}-1}{2N_{c}}\,,\qquad C_{3F}=\frac{(N_{c}^{2}-1)(N_{c}^{2}-4)}{4N_{c}^{2}}\,,

(48)

and

\begin{split}&\Gamma(\boldsymbol{x}_{\perp},\boldsymbol{y}_{\perp})=(\pi R_{A}^{2})\int_{\boldsymbol{z}_{\perp}}T_{A}(\boldsymbol{z}_{\perp})\left[G(\boldsymbol{x}_{\perp}-\boldsymbol{z}_{\perp})-G(\boldsymbol{y}_{\perp}-\boldsymbol{z}_{\perp})\right]^{2}\,,\\
&\Theta(\boldsymbol{x}_{\perp},\boldsymbol{y}_{\perp})=(\pi R_{A}^{2})\int_{\boldsymbol{z}_{\perp}}T_{A}(\boldsymbol{z}_{\perp})\left[G(\boldsymbol{x}_{\perp}-\boldsymbol{z}_{\perp})-G(\boldsymbol{y}_{\perp}-\boldsymbol{z}_{\perp})\right]^{3}\,,\end{split}

(49)

where
G(\boldsymbol{x}_{\perp}-\boldsymbol{z}_{\perp})
is a 2D Green function (
55
) and we have inserted the target profile
T_{A}(\boldsymbol{b}_{\perp})
,
see the discussion in the Appendix
A
. Eq. (
47
) can be interpreted as a single perturbative Odderon with any number of perturbative Pomeron insertions. Starting from (
47
) we deduce the following result for the Odderon initial condition

\begin{split}\mathcal{O}(\boldsymbol{r}_{\perp},\boldsymbol{b}_{\perp})&=\frac{\lambda}{8}\left[R_{A}\frac{\mathrm{d}T_{A}(\boldsymbol{b}_{\perp})}{\mathrm{d}b_{\perp}}A^{2/3}\frac{\sigma_{0}}{2}\right]Q^{3}_{S,0}A^{1/2}r_{\perp}^{3}(\hat{\boldsymbol{r}}_{\perp}\cdot\hat{\boldsymbol{b}}_{\perp})\log\left(\frac{1}{r_{\perp}\Lambda_{\rm QCD}}+e_{c}\mathrm{e}\right)\exp\left[-\frac{1}{4}\boldsymbol{r}_{\perp}^{2}Q_{0,A}^{2}(\boldsymbol{r}_{\perp},\boldsymbol{b}_{\perp})\right]\,,\end{split}

(50)

where in the JV model we would have

\lambda_{\rm JV}=-\frac{3}{16}\frac{N_{c}^{2}-4}{(N_{c}^{2}-1)^{2}}\frac{Q_{S,0}^{3}A^{1/2}R_{A}^{3}}{\alpha_{S}^{3}A^{2}}\,.

(51)

The details of the computation leading to (
50
) are given in the Appendix
A
.

|t|
-slope remaining roughly the same. Our conclusion for proton targets is thus similar to that of
Dumitru:2019qec
where the computation was performed at moderate
x\sim 0.1
. The Odderon extraction from collisions on the proton target would thus require measurements of the cross section at potentially large momentum transfers even when
x
is small
x\lesssim 0.01
. For neutron targets the Primakoff cross section is only a very small contribution and the Odderon can be probed even at low
|t|
and/or low
x
– see Fig.

.

\lambda=\lambda_{\rm max}
), the Odderon cross section can reach up to about 10 nb/GeV
2

\lambda=0.026\lambda_{\rm JV}
(the factor
0.026

\sim 5

Both the Odderon and the Primakoff contributions show characteristic diffractive patterns that are mostly of a geometric origin. However, it is clearly visible that the diffractive pattern for the Odderon cross section is altered compared to the Primakoff case: the diffractive dips are shifted to smaller
|t|
even for the initial condition and the shift becomes more pronounced as
x
gets smaller or
|t|
gets larger. To understand this result, notice that according to the leading twist estimates in (
27
) and (
33

\mathrm{d}\sigma/\mathrm{d}|t|\propto|t|T^{2}_{A}(\sqrt{|t|})
and
\mathrm{d}\sigma/\mathrm{d}|t|\propto T^{2}_{A}(\sqrt{|t|})/|t|
, respectively.
We are lead to the conclusion that the shift of the diffractive pattern when comparing the Odderon and the Primakoff cross section is a consequence of multiple scatterings in the Odderon amplitude. This finds additional support by the evolution to smaller
x
where, as a consequence of the growth of the saturation scale, multiple scattering effects become increasingly important, acting to further increase the shift.

Considering the total cross section, where the Odderon and the Primakoff contributions must be added coherently, the relative sign between the two amplitudes determines whether they interfere constructively or destructively. In our computation this is controlled by the sign of the Odderon coupling parameter
\lambda
. Using the JV model the sign is negative, see (
51

\mathrm{d}T_{A}/\mathrm{d}b_{\perp}
term, this gives a positive
\mathcal{O}_{1}(r_{\perp},b_{\perp})
overall, see Fig.
4

Dumitru:2022ooz
also yields a positive
\mathcal{O}_{1}(r_{\perp},b_{\perp})
, see Fig.
3

\mathcal{O}_{1}(r_{\perp},b_{\perp})

\mathcal{O}_{1}(r_{\perp},b_{\perp})
(or, equivalently,
\lambda

\mathcal{O}_{1}(r_{\perp},b_{\perp})>0

\eta_{c}
production off nuclear targets. Considering different nuclear species could be a valuable tool in verifying this suggestion.

V
Conclusion

In this work we have computed the exclusive
\eta_{c}
production in
ep
and
eA
collisions as a potential probe of the Odderon. Our computation relies on the CGC formalism where the effect of multiple scatterings is taken explicitly into account in a description of scattering off a dense target at small-
x
. We have numerically solved the BK evolution equation in impact parameter
b_{\perp}
and dipole size
r_{\perp}
for the coupled Pomeron-Odderon system. The numerical results demonstrate a rapid drop in the Odderon with evolution in line with the results in the literature
Kovchegov:2003dm
;
Lappi:2016gqe
;
Yao:2018vcg
.

Due to a large Primakoff background we find that in order to isolate the Odderon component of the cross section for the proton target, it is required to have relatively large momentum transfers:
|t|\gtrsim
1.5-3 GeV
2
for
x\sim 10^{-2}-10^{-4}
. On a qualitative level this is rather similar to the conclusions drawn in the previous works
Engel:1997cga
;
Czyzewski:1996bv
;
Bartels:2001hw
;
Ma:2003py
;
Dumitru:2019qec
. A new result is that the
|t|
-slope is not altered by small-
x
evolution, although the cross section does reduce in magnitude. Exclusive scattering off a neutron leads to a negligible Primakoff component and represents a new opportunity to probe the Odderon at low
|t|
. In practice this could be done using deuteron or He
3
targets with spectator proton tagging in the near forward direction, see for example
CLAS:2011qvj
;
Friscic:2021oti
.

For the nuclear targets we have found that the saturation effects in the Odderon distribution distorts the diffractive pattern in comparison to the Primakoff process. The effect is a few percent in magnitude and accumulates for smaller
x
and/or larger momentum transfers. Depending on the coupling of the Odderon, it is possible that the diffractive dips of the Primakoff process get filled by the Odderon component of the cross section. Such a distortion of the diffractive pattern in comparison to the known nuclear charge form factors might be a new way to measure the Odderon component in the nuclear wave function.

As our final remark, we wish to clearly state that the actual experimental measurement of the Odderon component of the exclusive
\eta_{c}
cross section is certainly challenging. Firstly, the Odderon itself is small, and so the cross section with proton (or neutron) targets tends to be low (
\sim 10^{2}
fb/GeV
2
). This could be circumvented by considering nuclear targets instead as the Odderon cross section is enhanced roughly as
\sim A^{2}
. With the maximal Odderon coupling allowed by the group theory constraint the cross section can be in the range of nb/GeV
2
. However, experimental extractions of a shift in the diffractive pattern in
\gamma^{*}A\to\eta_{c}A
, found at moderate/high
|t|
, calls for a good control of the incoherent background - a related discussion, albeit for the Pomeron, can be found in
Klein:2019qfb
;
AbdulKhalek:2021gbh
. Secondly, the branching ratio for
\eta_{c}
to charged hadrons is only a few percent
Workman:2022ynf
with a serious background from feed-down of
J/\psi
subsequently decaying as
J/\psi\to\eta_{c}\gamma
with
\gamma
undetected
Czyzewski:1996bv
;
Klein:2018ypk
;
Harland-Lang:2018ytk
. Nevertheless,
\eta_{c}
has been measured through its hadronic channel in
e^{+}e^{-}
by BABAR
BaBar:2010siw
and so such difficulties might be overcome also at EIC. Measuring at least the Primakoff component seems to be a feasible starting point
Babiarz:2023cac
. In any case, we consider the conclusions drawn from our results to be rather generic that would also be present in case of other quarkonia states or light mesons.

Initial condition for the Odderon

In Ref.
Jeon:2005cf
(see also
Dumitru:2011zz
) Jeon and Venugopalan used a model functional with quadratic and cubic interaction (
45
) in order to find the Odderon operator. The parameters
\mu^{2}
and
\kappa
(
46
) were treated as constants. In order to include the impact parameter dependence we assume that
\mu^{2}
and
\kappa
have a transverse profile
T_{A}(\boldsymbol{x}_{\perp})
with
\int_{\boldsymbol{x}_{\perp}}T_{A}(\boldsymbol{x}_{\perp})=1
such that the average couplings are given by (
46
). In this case we are lead to a straightforward generalization of (34) in
Jeon:2005cf
given by (
47
). We note in passing that in the Gaussian approximation the Pomeron
\mathcal{N}(\boldsymbol{x}_{\perp},\boldsymbol{y}_{\perp})
takes the form

\mathcal{N}(\boldsymbol{x}_{\perp},\boldsymbol{y}_{\perp})=1-\exp\left[-\frac{g^{2}C_{F}\mu^{2}}{2}\Gamma(\boldsymbol{x}_{\perp},\boldsymbol{y}_{\perp})\right]\,.

(54)

Inserting (
46
) into (
47
), with
T_{A}(\boldsymbol{z}_{\perp})\to\frac{1}{\pi R_{A}^{2}}
, formally recovers (34) in
Jeon:2005cf
. The 2D Green function
G(\boldsymbol{x}_{\perp}-\boldsymbol{y}_{\perp})
in (
47
) and (
49
) is explicitly given as

G(\boldsymbol{x}_{\perp}-\boldsymbol{y}_{\perp})=\int_{\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp}}\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp}\cdot(\boldsymbol{x}_{\perp}-\boldsymbol{y}_{\perp})}}{\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp}^{2}+m^{2}}\,,

(55)

with
m
an IR cutoff. Eq. (
47
) is the basis point to derive the initial condition for the Odderon. Its derivation essentially rests on the assumption that the cubic (
\rho^{3}
) term in (
45
) is parametrically suppressed as
A^{-1/6}

Jeon:2005cf
;
Dumitru:2018vpr
for a large nuclei, as compared to the quadratic (
\rho^{2}
) term
Jeon:2005cf
and so (
47
) is obtained by expanding to first order in
\rho^{3}/\kappa
while summing in
\rho^{2}/\mu^{2}
to all orders. In the following we compute
\Gamma(\boldsymbol{x}_{\perp},\boldsymbol{y}_{\perp})
and
\Theta(\boldsymbol{x}_{\perp},\boldsymbol{y}_{\perp})
. Going to momentum space we have

\begin{split}\Gamma(\boldsymbol{x}_{\perp},\boldsymbol{y}_{\perp})&=(\pi R_{A}^{2})\int_{\boldsymbol{p}_{\perp}\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp}}T_{A}(\boldsymbol{p}_{\perp})\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\boldsymbol{p}_{\perp}\cdot\boldsymbol{b}_{\perp}}\frac{1}{\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp}^{2}+m^{2}}\frac{1}{(\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp}-\boldsymbol{p}_{\perp})^{2}+m^{2}}\\
&\times\left[\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp}\frac{\boldsymbol{r}_{\perp}}{2}}-\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp}\frac{\boldsymbol{r}_{\perp}}{2}}\right]\left[\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}(\boldsymbol{p}_{\perp}-\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp})\frac{\boldsymbol{r}_{\perp}}{2}}-\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}(\boldsymbol{p}_{\perp}-\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp})\frac{\boldsymbol{r}_{\perp}}{2}}\right]\,,\end{split}

(56)

where we used
\boldsymbol{r}_{\perp}=\boldsymbol{x}_{\perp}-\boldsymbol{y}_{\perp}
and
\boldsymbol{b}_{\perp}=(\boldsymbol{x}_{\perp}+\boldsymbol{y}_{\perp})/2
. Assuming
p_{\perp}
is small (
b_{\perp}
is large) and expanding the phase around small
\boldsymbol{r}_{\perp}
we have

\begin{split}\Gamma(\boldsymbol{x}_{\perp},\boldsymbol{y}_{\perp})&\simeq(\pi R_{A}^{2})T_{A}(\boldsymbol{b}_{\perp})\int_{\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp}}\frac{(\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp}\cdot\boldsymbol{r}_{\perp})^{2}}{(\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp}^{2}+m^{2})^{2}}\simeq\frac{T(\boldsymbol{b}_{\perp})}{4\pi}\boldsymbol{r}_{\perp}^{2}\int_{0}^{\Lambda}k_{\perp}\mathrm{d}k_{\perp}\frac{\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp}^{2}}{(\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp}^{2}+m^{2})^{2}}\\
&\simeq(\pi R_{A}^{2})\frac{T_{A}(\boldsymbol{b}_{\perp})}{4\pi}\boldsymbol{r}_{\perp}^{2}\log\left(\frac{1}{r_{\perp}m}+\mathrm{e}\right)\,,\end{split}

(57)

where in the last equality we extracted the leading log and the UV cuttoff is placed on the
k_{\perp}
integral as
\Lambda\propto 1/r_{\perp}
. Using (
57
) in the argument of the exponential in (
54
) the result coincides with
Lappi:2013zma
with the conventional definition

Q_{S}^{2}\equiv\frac{C_{F}g^{2}\mu^{2}}{2\pi}\,.

(58)

For
\Theta(\boldsymbol{x}_{\perp},\boldsymbol{y}_{\perp})
we similarly have

\begin{split}\Theta(\boldsymbol{x}_{\perp},\boldsymbol{y}_{\perp})&\simeq(\pi R_{A}^{2})\mathrm{i}\int_{\boldsymbol{p}_{\perp}\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp}\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp}^{\prime}}T(\boldsymbol{p}_{\perp})\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\boldsymbol{p}_{\perp}\cdot\boldsymbol{b}_{\perp}}(\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp}\cdot\boldsymbol{r}_{\perp})(\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp}^{\prime}\cdot\boldsymbol{r}_{\perp})(\boldsymbol{p}_{\perp}-\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp}-\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp}^{\prime})\cdot\boldsymbol{r}_{\perp}\frac{1}{\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp}^{2}+m^{2}}\\
&\frac{1}{\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp}^{\prime 2}+m^{2}}\frac{1}{(\boldsymbol{p}_{\perp}-\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp}-\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp}^{\prime})^{2}+m^{2}}\,,\end{split}

(59)

where we already expanded for
\boldsymbol{r}_{\perp}\to 0
. Assuming also small
\boldsymbol{p}_{\perp}
we have

\frac{(\boldsymbol{p}_{\perp}-\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp}-\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp}^{\prime})\cdot\boldsymbol{r}_{\perp}}{(\boldsymbol{p}_{\perp}-\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp}-\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp}^{\prime})^{2}+m^{2}}\simeq\frac{(\boldsymbol{p}_{\perp}\cdot\boldsymbol{r}_{\perp})}{(\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp}+\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp}^{\prime})^{2}+m^{2}}-\frac{2\left((\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp}+\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp}^{\prime})\cdot\boldsymbol{r}_{\perp}\right)\left((\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp}+\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp}^{\prime})\cdot\boldsymbol{p}_{\perp}\right)}{\left[(\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp}+\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp}^{\prime})^{2}+m^{2}\right]^{2}}\,.

(60)

The zeroth order term above vanishes by rotation invariance. Using the second term we perform the angular integrals

\begin{split}&\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{\mathrm{d}\phi}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{\mathrm{d}\phi^{\prime}}{2\pi}\frac{(\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp}\cdot\boldsymbol{r}_{\perp})(\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp}^{\prime}\cdot\boldsymbol{r}_{\perp})(\boldsymbol{p}_{\perp}-\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp}-\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp}^{\prime})\cdot\boldsymbol{r}_{\perp}}{(\boldsymbol{p}_{\perp}-\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp}-\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp}^{\prime})^{2}+m^{2}}\\

(61)

Integrating further over
k^{\prime}_{\perp}
leads to

\begin{split}\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}&\frac{\mathcal{J}(k_{\perp},k^{\prime}_{\perp})k^{\prime}_{\perp}\mathrm{d}k^{\prime}_{\perp}}{\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp}^{\prime 2}+m^{2}}\\
&=-\frac{3}{16\pi}\frac{\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp}^{2}+2m^{2}}{\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp}^{2}+4m^{2}}-\frac{3}{16\pi}\frac{m^{4}}{k_{\perp}(\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp}^{2}+4m^{2})^{3/2}}\log\left[\frac{(\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp}^{2}+2m^{2})\left(\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp}^{2}+2m^{2}-k_{\perp}\sqrt{\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp}^{2}+4m^{2}}\right)-2m^{2}}{(\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp}^{2}+2m^{2})\left(\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp}^{2}+2m^{2}+k_{\perp}\sqrt{\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp}^{2}+4m^{2}}\right)-2m^{2}}\right]\,.\end{split}

(62)

For the final integration over
k_{\perp}
we are only interested in extracting the leading log. We can drop the second term in (
62
) as it vanishes in the limit
m\to 0
. Focusing on the first term, we eventually find

\begin{split}&\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{\Lambda}\frac{k_{\perp}\mathrm{d}k_{\perp}}{\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp}^{2}+m^{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\mathcal{J}(k_{\perp},k^{\prime}_{\perp})k^{\prime}_{\perp}\mathrm{d}k^{\prime}_{\perp}}{\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp}^{\prime 2}+m^{2}}\simeq-\frac{3}{32\pi^{2}}\int_{0}^{\Lambda}\frac{k_{\perp}\mathrm{d}k_{\perp}}{\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp}^{2}+m^{2}}\frac{\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp}^{2}+2m^{2}}{\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp}^{2}+4m^{2}}\simeq-\frac{3}{32\pi^{2}}\log\left(\frac{1}{r_{\perp}m}+\mathrm{e}\right)\,.\end{split}

(63)

In total we have

\Theta(\boldsymbol{x}_{\perp},\boldsymbol{y}_{\perp})=r_{\perp}^{3}(\hat{\boldsymbol{r}}_{\perp}\cdot\hat{\boldsymbol{b}}_{\perp})(\pi R_{A}^{2})\frac{\mathrm{d}T_{A}(\boldsymbol{b}_{\perp})}{\mathrm{d}b_{\perp}}\frac{3}{32\pi^{2}}\log\left(\frac{1}{r_{\perp}m}+\mathrm{e}\right)\,.

(64)

(65)

\begin{split}\mathcal{O}(\boldsymbol{r}_{\perp},\boldsymbol{b}_{\perp})&=-\frac{3}{128}\frac{N_{c}^{2}-4}{(N_{c}^{2}-1)^{2}}\frac{Q_{S}^{3}R_{A}^{3}}{\alpha_{S}^{3}A^{2}}\left(R_{A}\frac{\mathrm{d}T_{A}(\boldsymbol{b}_{\perp})}{\mathrm{d}b_{\perp}}(\pi R_{A}^{2})\right)(Q_{S}^{3}r_{\perp}^{3})(\hat{\boldsymbol{r}}_{\perp}\cdot\hat{\boldsymbol{b}}_{\perp})\log\left(\frac{1}{r_{\perp}m}+\mathrm{e}\right)\\

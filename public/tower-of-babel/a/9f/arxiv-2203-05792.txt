A nonuniform mesh method for wave scattered by periodic surfaces
Tilo Arens, Ruming Zhang

Abstract
In this paper, we propose a new nonuniform mesh method to simulate acoustic scattering problems in two dimensional periodic structures with non-periodic incident fields numerically. As existing methods are difficult to extend to higher dimensions, we have designed the new method with such extensions in mind. With the help of the Floquet-Bloch transform, the solution to the original scattering problem is written as an integral of a family of quasi-periodic problems. These are defined in bounded domains for each value of the Floquet parameter which varies in a bounded interval. The key step in our method is the numerical approximation of the integral by a quadrature rule adapted to the regularity of the family of quasi-periodic solutions. We design a nonuniform mesh with a Gaussian quadrature rule applied on each subinterval. We prove that the numerical method converges exponentially with respect to both the number of subintervals and the number of Gaussian quadrature points. Some numerical experiments are provided to illustrate the results.

Wave propagating in periodic structures has been a challenging and interesting topic in both theoretical analysis and numerical simulations for the past decades. For quasi-periodic incident fields, there is a well-established framework (see

One possible approach is to apply techniques available for rough surface scattering. The basis is provided by an analysis of variational formulations for such problems in weighted Sobolev spaces
[

,

]
. Possible numerical approaches include the integral equation method as studied in
[

,

,
19
,

,

,

]
for the Helmholtz equation in two dimensions and
[

]

[
30
,
13
,
11
]
for its applications in both boundary integral equations and finite element methods.

The principal drawback of this approach is the loss of all information and structure relying on the periodic nature of the scatterer. A powerful tool to exploit such structure is provided by the Floquet-Bloch transform, and efficient numerical methods have been developed based on this transform.

For example, penetrable periodic media are considered in
[
14
,
18
]
, for scattering by periodic surfaces we refer to
[
23
,
24
,
25
]
. Note that the approach has also been extended to the three dimensional bi-periodic surfaces in
[
26
]
. The method is proved to be convergent for 2D cases in
[
24
,
25
]
, but for 3D cases, convergence proofs are available only for some special situations in
[
26
]
.

After application of the Floquet-Bloch transform, the problem is reduced to solving a family of fully quasi-periodic problems for a range of the Floquet parameter. The solution of the original problem is obtained by inverting this transform. Although, numerically, this amounts to the approximate evaluation of an integral, it nevertheless proves to be a challenging task due to the presence of singularities.

Based on a detailed study of the regularity of the integrand, a high order method has been developed for 2D cases in
[
35
]
, achieving any algebraic order of convergence. However, this scheme cannot easily be extended to the 3D case due to the complicated structure of the singularities. This has motivated the research presented in this paper, in which we design a new nonuniform mesh method for the 2D case, which is much more readily extendable to 3D cases.

Based on the singularities of the quasi-periodic solution with respect to the Floquet parameter, we first generate graded meshes in the integration interval, and then apply Gaussian quadrature rule in each sub-interval. The convergence analysis is based on bounds for analytic extensions of the solutions of the quasi-periodic problems with respect to the Floquet parameter. We prove exponential convergence with respect to both the number of graded mesh points and the number of Gaussian nodal points in each subinterval. These estimates are then coupled to error-estimates for the finite element method used to solve each quasi-periodic problem. Finally we give some numerical examples to illustrate our theoretical results.

The rest of this paper is organized as follows. The mathematical model is introduced in Section
2
and the Floquet-Bloch transform is reviewed in Section
3

4

5
, nonuniform meshes are designed for definite integrals with square root singularities. With the results in Section
4
, we prove exponential convergence of the numerical method, and also give some numerical experiments in the last section.

2
Mathematical model of scattering problems

We consider the propagation of time-harmonic waves in the two-dimensional domain
\Omega
bounded from below by the curve
\Gamma
given as the graph of a
2\pi
-periodic function
\zeta
, i.e.

\Omega=\Big\{(x_{1},x_{2}):\,x_{1}\in\mathbb{R}\,,\;x_{2}>\zeta(x_{1})\Big\}\,,\qquad\Gamma=\Big\{(x_{1},\zeta(x_{1})):\,x_{1}\in\mathbb{R}\Big\}\,.

The total field
u
is assumed to satisfy the Helmholtz equation for some positive wave number
k
,

\Delta u+k^{2}\,u=0\qquad\text{in }\Omega

(1)

and to satisfy a Dirichlet boundary condition

u=0\qquad\text{on }\Gamma\,.

(2)

As is usual, the total field is split into the given incident field and the unknown scattered field,
u=u^{i}+u^{s}
. A suitable radiation condition must be imposed on the scattered field to ensure uniqueness and existence of solution, and this requires some additional definitions. For detailed derivations and proofs of the statements made below we refer to
[
12
,
11
]
.

Let
H>\max_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\{\zeta(t)\}
and
\Gamma_{H}:=\mathbb{R}\times\{H\}
be a straight horizontal line above
\Gamma
. Let
\Omega_{H}
be the periodic strip between
\Gamma
and
\Gamma_{H}
. For a visualization of the geometric setting we refer to Figure
1
.

Figure 1:
Periodic structure.

The appropriate spaces for solutions of such a rough surface scattering problem are horizontally weighted Sobolev spaces. Let
H^{s}(\Omega_{H})
and
H^{s}_{loc}(\Omega_{H})
denote the standard Sobolev spaces with real exponent
s
. For any
r\in\mathbb{R}
, let the weighted space
H^{s}_{r}(\Omega_{H})
be defined by

H^{s}_{r}(\Omega_{H}):=\Big\{\varphi\in H^{s}_{loc}(\Omega_{H}):\,(1+x_{1}^{2})^{r/s}\varphi(x_{1},x_{2})\in H^{s}(\Omega_{H})\Big\}\,.

To accomodate the Dirichlet boundary condition, we also define

\widetilde{H}_{r}^{1}(\Omega_{H}):=\Big\{\varphi\in H_{r}^{1}(\Omega_{H}):\,\varphi\big|_{\Gamma}=0\Big\}\,.

To guarantee that the scattered field
u^{s}
is propagating upwards, we require that
u^{s}
satisfies the following radiation condition:

u^{s}(x_{1},x_{2})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}x_{1}\xi+\mathrm{i}\sqrt{k^{2}-\xi^{2}}(x_{2}-H)}\,\widehat{u}^{s}(\xi,H)\,\mathrm{d}\xi\,,\quad x_{2}>H,

(3)

where the square root takes non-negative real- and imaginary parts,
\widehat{u}^{s}(\xi,H)
is the Fourier transform of
u^{s}(\cdot,H)
. This condition is also known as the
spectral amplitude representation
and formally expresses the scattered field as a linear superposition of plane upward-propagating and evanescent waves. Detailed arguments on why and in what sense the expression on the right hand side of (
3
) makes sense for
u^{s}\in H^{1}_{r}(\Omega_{H})
for all
|r|<1
are given in
[
11
]
. Combining (
3
) with the Neumann trace operator on
\Gamma_{H}
gives rise to the Dirichlet-to-Neumann map
T^{+}:H^{1/2}_{r}(\Gamma_{H})\to H^{-1/2}_{r}(\Gamma_{H})
,

(T^{+}\varphi)(x_{1})=\mathrm{i}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\sqrt{k^{2}-\xi^{2}}\,\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}x_{1}\xi}\,\widehat{\varphi}(\xi)\,\mathrm{d}\xi\,\,,\quad\text{ where }\varphi(x_{1})=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}x_{1}\xi}\,\widehat{\varphi}(\xi)\,\,\mathrm{d}\xi\,\,.

With these definitions, we are able to formulate the scattering problem under consideration: Given an incident field
u^{i}\in H^{1}_{r}(\Omega_{H})
, where
|r|<1
, find
u\in\widetilde{H}^{1}_{r}(\Omega_{H})
such that
u
satisfies the Helmholtz equation (
1
) in
\Omega_{H}
in the weak sense and the boundary condition

\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{2}}-T^{+}u=\frac{\partial u^{i}}{\partial x_{2}}-T^{+}u^{i}=:f\quad\text{ in }H^{-1/2}_{r}(\Gamma_{H})\,..

(4)

Explicitly, the weak form of this scattering problem is the following variational formulation: given
f\in H_{r}^{-1/2}(\Gamma_{H})
, where
|r|<1
, find
u\in\widetilde{H}_{r}^{1}(\Omega_{H})
such that

\int_{\Omega_{H}}\left[\nabla u\cdot\nabla\overline{\varphi}-k^{2}u\overline{\varphi}\right]\,\mathrm{d}x\,-\int_{\Gamma_{H}}T^{+}\left(u\big|_{\Gamma_{H}}\right)\overline{\varphi}\,\mathrm{d}s\,=\int_{\Gamma_{H}}f\overline{\varphi}\,\mathrm{d}s\,

(5)

for all
v\in\widetilde{H}_{-r}^{1}(\Omega_{H})
.

Theorem 1

(
[
11
]
)
.

For any
|r|<1
, given
f\in H^{-1/2}_{r}(\Gamma_{H})
, there is a unique solution
u\in\widetilde{H}_{r}^{1}(\Omega_{H})
of the problem (
5
).

Remark 2
.

In this paper, we only consider scattering problems with Dirichlet boundary condition on
\Gamma
. However, the method can also extended to other boundary conditions (e.g., impedance boundary conditions) or penetrable inhomogeneous media.

3
Floquet-Bloch transformed field and the regularity

In this section, we recall the definition and some properties of the Floquet-Bloch transform, and apply it to the solution of the original problem (
5
). Finally, we introduce the regularity result of the transformed field. For details we refer to
[
22
,
35
]
.

3.1
The Floquet-Bloch transform

We define the Floquet-Bloch transform of
\varphi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega_{H})
as

(\mathcal{J}\varphi)(\alpha,x)=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\varphi(x_{1}+2\pi j,x_{2})e^{-\mathrm{i}\alpha(x_{1}+2\pi j)},

where
\alpha\in(-1/2,1/2]
(called Floquet parameter) and
x\in\Omega_{H}^{2\pi}:=\Omega_{H}\cap[-\pi,\pi]\times\mathbb{R}
(see Figure
1
). As
\varphi
has a compact support, the transform is well-defined for
\alpha\in(-1/2,1/2]
and
x\in\Omega_{H}^{2\pi}
. It is also easy to check that when
\alpha
is fixed,
(\mathcal{J}\varphi)(\alpha,\cdot)
is
2\pi
-periodic in
x_{1}
, i.e.,

(\mathcal{J}\varphi)\left(\alpha,\left(\begin{matrix}x_{1}+2\pi\\
x_{2}\end{matrix}\right)\right)=(\mathcal{J}\varphi)(\alpha,x).

Moreover,
e^{\mathrm{i}\alpha x_{1}}w(\alpha,x)
is
1
-periodic in
\alpha
for fixed
x
.

To introduce properties of the Bloch transform, we define the space
H^{m}\left((-1/2,1/2];H^{s}(\Omega_{H}^{2\pi})\right)
(
m\in\mathbb{N}
) equipped with the norm:

\|\varphi\|_{H^{m}\left((-1/2,1/2];H^{s}(\Omega_{H}^{2\pi})\right)}:=\left[\sum_{\ell=0}^{m}\int_{-1/2}^{1/2}\left\|\partial^{\ell}_{\alpha}\varphi(\alpha,\cdot)\right\|^{2}_{H^{s}(\Omega^{2\pi}_{H})}\right]^{2}.

This definition is extended to any real number
r
by interpolation and duality arguments. We also define the subspace
H^{r}\left((-1/2,1/2];H^{s}_{\mathrm{per}}(\Omega^{\Lambda}_{H})\right)
that contains functions which are
2\pi
-periodic with respect to
x_{1}
with fixed
\alpha
. We conclude our overview of the properties of the Floquet-Bloch transform with the following theorem.

Theorem 3
.

The transform
\mathcal{J}
is extended to an isomorphism between
H^{s}_{r}(\Omega_{H})
and
H^{r}\left((-1/2,1/2];H^{s}_{\mathrm{per}}(\Omega^{\Lambda}_{H})\right)
for any
s,\,r\in\mathbb{R}
.

\left(\mathcal{J}^{-1}w\right)(x)=\int_{-1/2}^{1/2}w(\alpha,x)e^{\mathrm{i}\alpha x_{1}}\,\mathrm{d}\alpha\,,\quad x\in\Omega_{H}.

When
s=r=0
,
\mathcal{J}
is an isometry with its inverse and
\mathcal{J}^{-1}=\mathcal{J}^{*}
.

3.2
Bloch transformed field and regularity

Following the process in
[
22
]
, we apply the Floquet-Bloch transform to the total field
u
, then
w(\alpha,x):=(\mathcal{J}u)(\alpha,x)
satisfies the following variational equation with the test function
\psi(\alpha,x)=(\mathcal{J}\varphi)(\alpha,x)
:

\int_{-1/2}^{1/2}a_{\alpha}(w(\alpha,\cdot),\psi(\alpha,\cdot))\,\mathrm{d}\alpha\,=\int_{-1/2}^{1/2}\int_{\Gamma_{H}^{2\pi}}F(\alpha,\cdot)\overline{\psi}(\alpha,\cdot)\,\mathrm{d}s\,\,\mathrm{d}\alpha\,.

(6)

where

\displaystyle a_{\alpha}(\xi,\eta)=\int_{\Omega^{2\pi}_{H}}\left[\nabla\xi\cdot\nabla\overline{\eta}-2\mathrm{i}\alpha\frac{\partial\xi}{\partial x_{1}}\overline{\eta}+(\alpha^{2}-k^{2})\xi\overline{\eta}\right]\,\mathrm{d}x\,-\int_{\Gamma^{2\pi}_{H}}T^{+}_{\alpha}\left[\xi\big|_{\Gamma^{2\pi}_{H}}\right]\overline{\eta}\,\mathrm{d}s\,\,,

\displaystyle F(\alpha,x)=(\mathcal{J}f)(\alpha,x)\,.

and
T^{+}_{\alpha}
is the periodic Dirichlet-to-Neumann map with index
\alpha
from
H^{1/2}_{\mathrm{per}}(\Gamma_{H}^{2\pi})
to
H^{-1/2}_{\mathrm{per}}(\Gamma_{H}^{2\pi})
. It has the following form:

(T^{+}_{\alpha}\varphi)(x_{1})=\mathrm{i}\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\sqrt{k^{2}-(\alpha+j)^{2}}\widehat{\varphi}(j)e^{\mathrm{i}jx_{1}}\qquad\text{where}\quad\varphi(x_{1})=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\widehat{\varphi}(j)e^{\mathrm{i}jx_{1}}.

From the equivalence between (
5
) and (
6
), we have the following results. For proofs we refer to
[
22
,
24
,
25
]
.

Theorem 4
.

Given
f\in H_{r}^{-1/2}(\Gamma_{H})
for
|r|<1
, the variational problem (
6
) is uniquely solvable in
H^{r}\left((-1/2,1/2];\widetilde{H}^{1}_{\mathrm{per}}(\Omega_{H}^{2\pi})\right)
. Moreover,

1.

when
f\in H_{r}^{1/2}(\Gamma_{H})
and
\zeta\in C^{2,1}
,
w\in H^{r}\left((-1/2,1/2];\widetilde{H}^{2}_{\mathrm{per}}(\Omega^{2\pi}_{H})\right)
and
u\in H^{2}_{r}(\Omega_{H})
;

2.

when
f\in H_{r}^{-1/2}(\Gamma_{H})
for
r\in(1/2,1)
, then
w\in L^{2}\left((-1/2,1/2];\widetilde{H}^{1}_{\mathrm{per}}(\Omega_{H}^{2\pi})\right)
equivalently satisfies

a_{\alpha}(w(\alpha,\cdot),\varphi)=\int_{\Gamma^{2\pi}_{H}}F(\alpha,\cdot)\overline{\varphi}\,\mathrm{d}s\,

(7)

for any
\alpha\in(-1/2,1/2]
and
\varphi\in\widetilde{H}^{1}_{\mathrm{per}}(\Omega^{2\pi}_{H})
.

In particular for numerical applications, it is important to have a more detailed understanding of the regularity properties of
w(\alpha,x)
with respect to the Floquet parameter
\alpha
. Before the study of these properties, we first introduce the following notations and spaces. For any fixed positive wavenumber
k
, let

\underline{k}:=\min\{|n-k|:\,n\in\mathbb{Z}\}.

From
1
-periodicity of
\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\alpha x_{1}}w(\alpha,x)
with respect to
\alpha
, the inverse Bloch transform (see Theorem
3
) is written equivalently as

\left(\mathcal{J}^{-1}w\right)(x)=\int_{-\underline{k}}^{1-\underline{k}}w(\alpha,x)\,\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\alpha x_{1}}\,\,\mathrm{d}\alpha\,,\quad x\in\Omega_{H}.

Let

E:=\left\{\alpha\in[-\underline{k},1-\underline{k}]:\,|n-\alpha|=k\text{ for some }n\in\mathbb{Z}\right\}.

Then. from direct calculation,

E=\begin{cases}\{-\underline{k},\,1-\underline{k}\},\quad\text{ when }k=n/2\text{ for some }n\in\mathbb{N}_{+};\\
\{-\underline{k},\,\underline{k},\,1-\underline{k}\},\quad\text{ otherwise.}\end{cases}

This implies that
E
contains the edge of
[-\underline{k},1-\underline{k}]
, and may also contain a point in the interior this interval.

The formulation of the regularity properties of the Bloch transformed field requires some appropriate function spaces. Let
\mathcal{I}\subset\mathbb{R}
denote a bounded open interval,
{D}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
a bounded domain, and
S({D})
a Sobolev space independent of
\alpha
of functions defined in
{D}
. First, define a space of functions that depend analytically on
\alpha
:

\displaystyle C^{\omega}(\mathcal{I},S({D})):=

\displaystyle\Bigg\{\varphi\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\mathcal{I}\times{D})):\,\forall\alpha_{0}\in\mathcal{I},\,\exists\,\delta>0,\,s.t.,\,\forall\alpha\in(\alpha_{0}-\delta,\alpha_{0}+\delta)\cap\mathcal{I},\Bigg.

\displaystyle\left.\,\exists C>0,\,\varphi_{n}\in S(D),\,s.t.,\varphi(\alpha,x)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(\alpha-\alpha_{0})^{n}\varphi_{n}(x),\,\|\varphi_{n}\|_{S(D)}\leq C^{n}\right\}.

Also, let the subspace of functions that are
C^{n}
-continuous with respect to
\alpha
be defined as:

\displaystyle C^{n}(\mathcal{I},S(D)):=

\displaystyle\Bigg\{\varphi\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\mathcal{I}\times D):\,\forall\alpha\in\mathcal{I},\,j=0,1,\dots,n,\,\frac{\partial^{j}f(\alpha,\cdot)}{\partial\alpha^{j}}\in S(D),\Bigg.

\displaystyle\left.\text{moreover},\,\left\|\frac{\partial^{j}f(\alpha,\cdot)}{\partial\alpha^{j}}\right\|_{S(D)}\text{ is uniformly bounded for }\alpha\in\mathcal{I}\right\}.

The regularity of the Bloch transformed field can be characterized through two properties that we here formulate for a function
\varphi\in C^{0}(\mathcal{I};S(D))
:

1.

For any subinterval
\mathcal{I}_{0}\subset\mathcal{I}\setminus E
,
\varphi\in C^{\omega}(\mathcal{I}_{0};S(D))
.

2.

For any
\alpha_{0}\in\mathcal{I}\cap E
, there is a sufficiently small
\delta>0
and a pair
\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2}\in C^{\omega}(\mathcal{I}_{0};S(D))
such that

\varphi(\alpha,\cdot)=\varphi_{1}(\alpha,\cdot)+\sqrt{\alpha-\alpha_{0}}\,\varphi_{2}(\alpha,\cdot),

where
\mathcal{I}_{0}=(\alpha_{0}-\delta,\alpha_{0}+\delta)\cap\mathcal{I}
.

The space of functions that satisfies both these properties will be denoted as

\mathcal{A}^{\omega}(\mathcal{I};S(D);E):=\left\{\varphi\in C^{0}(\mathcal{I};S(D)):\,\varphi\text{ satisfies condition 1 and 2}\right\}.

With the help of all these definitions, the regularity of the Bloch transformed field
w(\alpha,x)
can now be stated in the following theorem. For a proof we refer to Theorem 16 in
[
35
]
.

Theorem 5
.

Given any
f\in H^{-1/2}_{r}(\Gamma_{H})
such that
\mathcal{J}f=F\in\mathcal{A}^{\omega}\left((-\underline{k},1-\underline{k}];H^{-1/2}_{\mathrm{per}}(\Gamma_{H}^{2\pi});E\right)
where
r\in(1/2,1)
, then the transformed solution
\mathcal{J}u=w\in\mathcal{A}^{\omega}\left((-\underline{k},1-\underline{k}];\widetilde{H}^{1}_{\mathrm{per}}(\Omega_{H}^{2\pi});E\right)
. Moreover, if
f\in H^{1/2}_{r}(\Gamma_{H})
and
\zeta\in C^{2,1}(\mathbb{R})
,
w\in\mathcal{A}^{\omega}\left((-\underline{k},1-\underline{k}];\widetilde{H}^{2}_{\mathrm{per}}(\Omega_{H}^{2\pi});E\right)
.

From Theorem
5
, the transformed field
w(\alpha,\cdot)
has only a finite number of square-root singularities. As
w(\alpha,\cdot)
can be readily computed by well-established methods, the only difficulty is to approximate the inverse Bloch transform. Note that although in
[
35
]
, one of the authors has proposed a highly efficient numerical method for the approximation, it is extremely difficult to extend this approach to scattering problems with bi-periodic structures in three dimensional space. With the ultimate goal of such an extension in mind, we introduce a nonuniform mesh method for the numerical approximation below. The estimation of quadrature errors arising in this method relies on bounds of analytic extensions of
\varphi_{1}
and
\varphi_{2}
in property 2 in the complex plane with respect to
\alpha
. Thus, before the introduction to the adaptive method, we have to investigate the extension of the solutions to a neighbourhood of
(-\underline{k},1-\underline{k}]
in
\mathbb{C}
.

4
Extension to complex quasi-periodicities

The convergence analysis of our method of inversion for the Floquet-Bloch transform relies on estimates for analytic extensions with respect to
\alpha
of the solution
w(\alpha,\cdot)
of the quasi-periodic problem. It is the goal of this section to characterize complex neighborhoods of the real axis to which
w(\alpha,\cdot)
may be extended analytically and to provide estimates for these extensions. Our approach is, first, to precisely define analytic extensions of the variational formulation and of the corresponding operators and, secondly, to estimate the difference between these operators and their counterparts for real
\alpha
for small deviations from the real axis. From these results, standard perturbation theory will yield analyticity of the solution
w(\alpha,\cdot)
as well as the required bounds.

To simplify our estimates, let us slightly modify the spaces. Denote by
V_{H}^{\mathrm{per}}
the space
\widetilde{H}^{1}_{\mathrm{per}}(\Omega_{H}^{2\pi})
with the norm replaced by

\|\varphi\|_{V_{H}^{\mathrm{per}}}:=\left[\int_{\Omega^{2\pi}_{H}}\left[\left|\nabla\varphi\right|^{2}+k^{2}|\varphi|^{2}\right]\,\mathrm{d}x\,\right]^{1/2}.

Let the norm in
H^{s}_{\mathrm{per}}(\Gamma^{2\pi}_{H})
be defined by

\|\varphi\|_{H^{s}_{\mathrm{per}}(\Gamma^{2\pi}_{H})}=\left[\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}(k^{2}+j^{2})^{s}\big|\widehat{\varphi}_{j}\big|^{2}\right]^{1/2}.

As the sesquilinear form
a_{\alpha}(\cdot,\cdot)
is well defined in
V_{H}^{\mathrm{per}}\times V_{H}^{\mathrm{per}}
, from Riesz’s Lemma, there is a
\mathcal{B}_{\alpha}:\,V_{H}^{\mathrm{per}}\rightarrow\left(V_{H}^{\mathrm{per}}\right)^{*}
such that

a_{\alpha}(\varphi,\psi)=\left<\mathcal{B}_{\alpha}\varphi,\psi\right>,

where
\varphi,\psi\in V_{H}^{\mathrm{per}}
and
\left<\cdot,\cdot\right>
denotes the extension of the
L^{2}(\Omega^{2\pi}_{H})
inner product to the
\left(V_{H}^{\mathrm{per}}\right)^{*}
-
V_{H}^{\mathrm{per}}
duality.
Moreover, let
F(\alpha,\cdot)\in H^{-1/2}_{\mathrm{per}}(\Gamma^{2\pi}_{H})
.

Let
\delta
denote a small complex number, and
\mathcal{D}_{\delta,\alpha}
the perturbation of
\mathcal{B}_{\alpha}
obtained from replacing
\alpha
by
\alpha+\mathrm{i}\delta
, i.e.

\left\langle\left(\mathcal{B}_{\alpha}+\mathcal{D}_{\delta,\alpha}\right)v,\varphi\right\rangle=\int_{\Omega^{2\pi}_{H}}\left[\nabla v\cdot\nabla\overline{\varphi}-2\mathrm{i}\,(\alpha+\mathrm{i}\delta)\,\frac{\partial v}{\partial x_{1}}\,\overline{\varphi}+((\alpha+\mathrm{i}\delta)^{2}-k^{2})\,v\,\overline{\varphi}\right]\,\mathrm{d}x\,-\int_{\Gamma^{2\pi}_{H}}T^{+}_{\alpha+\mathrm{i}\delta}v\,\overline{\varphi}\,\,\mathrm{d}x\,\,.

(8)

For the moment, we consider
T^{+}_{\alpha+\mathrm{i}\delta}
as a formal symbol only, a precise definition of this operator will be given below. A direct calculation shows

\left\langle\mathcal{D}_{\delta,\alpha}v,\varphi\right\rangle=\int_{\Omega^{2\pi}_{H}}\left[2\delta\,\frac{\partial v}{\partial x_{1}}\,\overline{\varphi}+(2\mathrm{i}\alpha\delta-\delta^{2})\,v\,\overline{\varphi}\right]\,\mathrm{d}x\,-\int_{\Gamma^{2\pi}_{H}}\left[T^{+}_{\alpha+\mathrm{i}\delta}-T^{+}_{\alpha}\right]v\,\overline{\varphi}\,\,\mathrm{d}s\,\,.

(9)

Obviously, the first integral depends analytically on
\delta
in all of
\mathbb{C}
. Thus we only need to investigate the analytic extension of the operator
T^{+}_{\alpha}
which involves a countable number of functions with square-root singularities. As
{T}^{+}_{\alpha}
is real analytic in
(-\underline{k},1-\underline{k}]
except for the finite set
E
, we extend the operator also analytically in the neighbourhood of
(-\underline{k},1-\underline{k}]
except for a finite number of vertical lines
\{\alpha_{0}\}\times\mathbb{R}
, where
\alpha_{0}\in E
.

To this end, we redefine the square root operator “
\sqrt{\quad}
” as follows.

Definition 6
.

For any
z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}
, there is a unique representation such that

z=r\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\theta},\quad r=|z|>0\,,\quad\theta\in\left(-\frac{\pi}{2},\frac{3\pi}{2}\right].

Define
\sqrt{z}=\sqrt{r}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\theta/2}
, where
\sqrt{r}
denotes the usual square root for a positive real number. Moreover, when
z=0
,
\sqrt{z}=0
.

Via Definition
6
, the square root function is analytically extended to the complex plane except for the negative imaginary axis. Thus for each term in the formal expression for
T^{+}_{\alpha+\mathrm{i}\delta}
, the map
\delta\mapsto\sqrt{k^{2}-(\alpha+j+\mathrm{i}\delta)^{2}}
is real analytic in
\mathbb{R}
when
|\alpha+j|\neq k
.

Consider now the analytic extension of the terms in the definition of the operator
{T}^{+}_{\alpha}
. Let

A_{1}=(-\underline{k},\underline{k}),\qquad A_{2}=(\underline{k},1-\underline{k})\,.

Note that if
\underline{k}=0
,
A_{1}=\emptyset
while when
\underline{k}=1/2
,
A_{2}=\emptyset
; while neither is empty otherwise. The observations above show that for each

, the function
\delta\mapsto\sqrt{k^{2}-(\alpha+j+\mathrm{i}\delta)^{2}}
is analytic in the strips
A_{m}+\mathrm{i}\mathbb{R}
,
m=1
,
2
. We will show in Theorem

below that the series over all these terms indeed converges and bound its difference from
T^{+}_{\alpha}
. Before we can establish this result, however, we require two technical estimates for these square roots terms.

Lemma 7
.

For any
\alpha\in A_{m}
,
m=1
,
2
,
\delta\in\mathbb{R}
and
j\in\mathbb{Z}
,

\left|\sqrt{(\alpha+j+\mathrm{i}\delta)^{2}-k^{2}}-\sqrt{(\alpha+j)^{2}-k^{2}}\right|\leq\frac{|\delta|}{2\left|\sqrt{k^{2}-(\alpha+j)^{2}}\right|}\left(\max\{k,|\alpha+j|\}+\frac{|\delta|^{2}}{4\left|\alpha+j-k\right|}\right).

(10)

Proof.

From Definition
6
, we have for
x\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}
and
y\in\mathbb{R}
that

\left|\sqrt{x+\mathrm{i}y}+\sqrt{x}\right|=|\sqrt{x}|\,\left|1+\sqrt{1+\mathrm{i}\,\frac{y}{x}}\right|\geq 2\,|\sqrt{x}|\,.

Hence, by an elementary calculation, we obtain for
\alpha\in A_{m}
that

\displaystyle\left|\sqrt{(\alpha+j+\mathrm{i}\delta)^{2}-k^{2}}\right.

\displaystyle\left.{}-\sqrt{(\alpha+j)^{2}-k^{2}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\sqrt{\alpha+j+\mathrm{i}\delta+k}-\sqrt{\alpha+j+k}\right|\left|\sqrt{\alpha+j+\mathrm{i}\delta-k}\right|

\displaystyle\quad{}+\left|\sqrt{\alpha+j+k}\right|\left|\sqrt{\alpha+j+\mathrm{i}\delta-k}-\sqrt{\alpha+j-k}\right|

\displaystyle=\frac{|\delta|\left|\sqrt{\alpha+j+\mathrm{i}\delta-k}\right|}{\left|\sqrt{\alpha+j+\mathrm{i}\delta+k}+\sqrt{\alpha+j+k}\right|}+\frac{|\delta||\sqrt{\alpha+j+k}|}{\left|\sqrt{\alpha+j+\mathrm{i}\delta-k}+\sqrt{\alpha+j-k}\right|}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{|\delta|}{2}\left(\frac{|\sqrt{\alpha+j+\mathrm{i}\delta-k}|}{|\sqrt{\alpha+j+k}|}+\frac{|\sqrt{\alpha+j+k}|}{|\sqrt{\alpha+j-k}|}\right).

We further estimate, again for
x\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}
and
y\in\mathbb{R}
,

\left|\sqrt{x+\mathrm{i}y}\right|=(x^{2}+y^{2})^{1/4}\leq|x|^{1/2}\left(1+\frac{y^{2}}{4x^{2}}\right)

Thus

\left|\sqrt{\alpha+j+\mathrm{i}\delta-k}\right|\leq\left|\sqrt{\alpha+j-k}\right|+\frac{\delta^{2}}{4\,|\alpha+j-k|^{3/2}}\,,

and the assertion follows.
∎

In the next step we further estimate the leading factor in (

for

, when

Lemma 8
.

Let
\alpha\in A_{m}=(a_{0},a_{1})
for
m=1
,
2
. Then

\min_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\left|\sqrt{k^{2}-(\alpha+j)^{2}}\right|\geq\sigma\,\min\left\{\sqrt{\alpha-a_{0}}\,,\,\sqrt{a_{1}-\alpha}\right\}

with
\sigma=1
if
k>1/2
and
\sigma=\sqrt{\underline{k}}>0
if
k\leq 1/2
.

Proof.

Note first, that all factors occurring on the right hand side of the asserted lower bound are less than or equal to 1.

Suppose that
k=\hat{j}+\underline{k}
,
\hat{j}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
and write
j=\hat{j}+n
,
n\in\mathbb{Z}
. Then

\displaystyle\left|k^{2}-(\alpha+j)^{2}\right|=\left|(\hat{j}+\underline{k})^{2}-(\alpha+\hat{j}+n)^{2}\right|=\big|\underline{k}-\alpha-n\big|\,\big|\underline{k}+\alpha+n+2\hat{j}\big|.

First, let
\alpha\in A_{1}=(-\underline{k},\underline{k})
. Then,

\displaystyle|\underline{k}-\alpha-n|

\displaystyle\geq\min\{|\underline{k}-\alpha|\,,|\underline{k}-\alpha-1|\geq\min\{|\underline{k}-\alpha|\,,|\underline{k}+\alpha|\}\,,

\displaystyle|\underline{k}+\alpha+n+2\hat{j}|

\displaystyle\geq\min\{|\underline{k}+\alpha|\,,|\underline{k}+\alpha-1|\}\geq\min\{|\underline{k}+\alpha|\,,|\underline{k}-\alpha|\}\,,

as well as

|\underline{k}-\alpha-n|\geq 1\,,\quad n\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0,\,1\}\,,\qquad\text{and}\qquad|(\underline{k}+\alpha+n+2\hat{j})|\geq 1\,,\quad n+2\hat{j}\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{-1,\,0\}\,.

This proves the assertion for
\alpha\in A_{1}
unless
n=\hat{j}=0
. In this case we can obviously estimate

\left|\underline{k}-\alpha\right|\,\left|\underline{k}+\alpha\right|\geq\underline{k}\,\min\{|\underline{k}-\alpha|\,,\,|\underline{k}+\alpha|\}\,.

Taking the square root gives the estimate for
\alpha\in A_{1}
.

Now let
\alpha\in A_{2}=(\underline{k},1-\underline{k})
. In this case,

\displaystyle|\underline{k}-\alpha-n|

\displaystyle\geq\min\{|\underline{k}-\alpha|\,,|\underline{k}-\alpha+1|\}\geq\min\{|\underline{k}-\alpha|\,,|1-\underline{k}-\alpha|\}\,,

\displaystyle|\underline{k}+\alpha+n+2\hat{j}|

\displaystyle\geq\min\{|\underline{k}+\alpha-1|\,,|\underline{k}+\alpha|\}\geq\min\{|\underline{k}+\alpha-1|\,,|\underline{k}-\alpha|\}\,,

as well as

|\underline{k}-\alpha-n|\geq 1\,,\quad n\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{-1,\,0\}\,,\qquad\text{and}\qquad|(\underline{k}+\alpha+n+2\hat{j})|\geq 1\,,\quad n+2\hat{j}\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{-1,\,0\}\,.

The assertion is proven for
\alpha\in A_{2}
unless
\hat{j}=0
and
n\in\{-1\,,\,0\}
. In these exceptional cases we have

\left|\underline{k}-\alpha\right|\,\left|\underline{k}+\alpha\right|\geq 2\underline{k}\,|\underline{k}-\alpha|\,,\qquad\left|\underline{k}-\alpha+1\right|\,\left|\underline{k}+\alpha-1\right|\geq 2\underline{k}\,|1-\underline{k}-\alpha|\,.

All arguments are repeated with obvious sign changes for
k=\hat{j}-\underline{k}

∎

With the previous two lemmas, we are now able to prove boundedness of
T^{+}_{\alpha+\mathrm{i}\delta}
and bound the difference of the two DtN maps.

Theorem 9
.

Let
\alpha\in A_{m}=(a_{0},a_{1})
,
m=1
,
2
. Let
{\varrho}=|\delta|/\min\left\{\sqrt{\alpha-a_{0}}\,,\,\sqrt{a_{1}-\alpha}\right\}

T^{+}_{\alpha+\mathrm{i}\delta}:H^{1/2}_{\mathrm{per}}(\Gamma^{2\pi}_{H})\to H^{-1/2}_{\mathrm{per}}(\Gamma^{2\pi}_{H})
is bounded for any
\delta\in\mathbb{R}
. Moreover, its difference from
T^{+}_{\alpha}
is bounded by

\left\|{T}^{+}_{\alpha+\mathrm{i}\delta}-{T}^{+}_{\alpha}\right\|\leq\frac{{\varrho}}{\sigma}+\frac{{\varrho}^{3}}{8\sigma k}\,,

where
\sigma
is the constant defined in Lemma
8
.

Proof.

Consider
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{\mathrm{per}}(\Gamma^{2\pi}_{H})
and its Fourier series,
\varphi(x_{1})=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\widehat{\varphi}_{j}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}jx_{1}}
. Then

\left({T}^{+}_{\alpha+\mathrm{i}\delta}-{T}^{+}_{\alpha}\right)\varphi=\mathrm{i}\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\left[\sqrt{k^{2}-(\alpha+j+\mathrm{i}\delta)^{2}}-\sqrt{k^{2}-(\alpha+j)^{2}}\right]\widehat{\varphi}_{j}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}(\alpha+j)x_{1}},

with convergence of the series ensured by the smoothness of
\varphi
. Hence

\left\|\left({T}^{+}_{\alpha+\mathrm{i}\delta}-{T}^{+}_{\alpha}\right)\varphi\right\|^{2}_{H^{-1/2}_{\mathrm{per}}(\Gamma^{2\pi}_{H})}=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}(k^{2}+|j|^{2})^{-1/2}\left|\sqrt{k^{2}-(\alpha+j+\mathrm{i}\delta)^{2}}-\sqrt{k^{2}-(\alpha+j)^{2}}\right|^{2}\left|\widehat{\varphi}_{j}\right|^{2}.

Thus, by the previous two lemmas,

\displaystyle\left\|\left({T}^{+}_{\alpha+\mathrm{i}\delta}-{T}^{+}_{\alpha}\right)\varphi\right\|_{H^{-1/2}_{\mathrm{per}}(\Gamma^{2\pi}_{H})}

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{\left|\sqrt{k^{2}-(\alpha+j+\mathrm{i}\delta)^{2}}-\sqrt{k^{2}-(\alpha+j)^{2}}\right|}{\sqrt{k^{2}+|j|^{2}}}\,\|\varphi\|_{H^{1/2}_{\mathrm{per}}(\Gamma^{2\pi}_{H})}

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{|\delta|\,\left(\max\{k,|\alpha+j|\}+\frac{|\delta|^{2}}{4\left|\alpha+j-k\right|}\right)}{2\sigma\,\min\left\{\sqrt{|\alpha-a_{0}|}\,,\,\sqrt{|\alpha-a_{1}|}\right\}}\,\frac{1}{\sqrt{k^{2}+|j|^{2}}}\,\|\varphi\|_{H^{1/2}_{\mathrm{per}}(\Gamma^{2\pi}_{H})}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{|\delta|\left(1+\delta^{2}\,\sup\limits_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{1}{8\,|\alpha+j-k|\,\sqrt{k^{2}+|j|^{2}}}\right)}{\sigma\,\min\left\{\sqrt{|\alpha-a_{0}|}\,,\,\sqrt{|\alpha-a_{1}|}\right\}}\,\|\varphi\|_{H^{1/2}_{\mathrm{per}}(\Gamma^{2\pi}_{H})}\,.

The proof is finished by observing
|\alpha+j-k|\geq\min\left\{\alpha-a_{0}\,,\,a_{1}-\alpha\right\}
.
∎

Our goal is to prove that the operator

\left|\int_{\Omega^{2\pi}_{H}}\left[2\delta\frac{\partial v}{\partial x_{1}}\overline{\varphi}+(2\mathrm{i}\alpha\delta-\delta^{2})v\overline{\varphi}\right]\,\mathrm{d}x\,\right|\leq\left(4|\delta|+\delta^{2}/k\right)\|v\|_{V_{H}^{\mathrm{per}}}\|\varphi\|_{V_{H}^{\mathrm{per}}}\,.

(11)

Theorem 10
.

Let
\alpha\in A_{m}=(a_{0},a_{1})
,
m=1
,
2

Let

In this case, the set

Let us recall some basic definitions and properties of an ellipses. An ellipse with center at

\mathcal{E}=\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:\frac{(x_{1}-s)^{2}}{a^{2}}+\frac{(x_{2}-t)^{2}}{b^{2}}\leq 1\right\},

The number

(s-a,0)

Lemma 11
.

Let
a
and set

\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}^{r}_{-a,a}\subseteq P=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:x_{1}\geq-a+x_{2}^{2}/\mathcal{C}^{2}\text{ and }x_{1}\leq a-x_{2}^{2}/\mathcal{C}^{2}\}.

Proof.

As both

\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}^{r}_{-a,a}
are symmetric with respect to the

-axis, it is sufficient to consider case

and the first inequality in the definition of

. For

we have

and hence

∎

In the next lemma, we prove that a certain family of smaller ellipses are contained in

Lemma 12
.

Let

,
b>0
,
0<\lambda\leq a
,
0<\mu\leq\frac{b}{a}\lambda
and

\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}^{\lambda+\mu}_{z-\lambda,z+\lambda}\subseteq\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}^{a+b}_{-a,a}
.

Proof.

Let
(c+\lambda\cos\theta,\mu\sin\theta)\in\partial\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}^{\lambda+\mu}_{c-\lambda,c+\lambda}
where
\theta\in[0,2\pi)
. As
c+\lambda\cos\theta\in[-a,a]
, there is a
\varphi\in[0,\pi)
such that

c+\lambda\cos\theta=a\cos\varphi\,.

Thus
(a\cos\varphi,\pm b\sin\varphi)\in\partial\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}^{a+b}_{-a,a}
. We compare the squares of the

-coordinates.

\displaystyle\mu^{2}\sin^{2}\theta

\displaystyle\leq\frac{b^{2}\lambda^{2}}{a^{2}}\,\sin^{2}\theta=\frac{b^{2}}{a^{2}}\left(\lambda^{2}-\lambda^{2}\,\cos^{2}\theta\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{b^{2}}{a^{2}}\left(\lambda^{2}-(a\cos\varphi-c)^{2}\right)=b^{2}\sin^{2}\varphi+\frac{b^{2}}{a^{2}}\left(\lambda^{2}-a^{2}+2ac\,\cos\varphi-c^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=b^{2}\sin^{2}\varphi+\frac{b^{2}}{a^{2}}\left(\lambda^{2}-(a-c)^{2}+2ac\,(\cos\varphi-1)\right).

As
\lambda\leq a-c
, the second term is negative and we conclude
\mu^{2}\sin^{2}\theta\leq b^{2}\sin^{2}\varphi

is inside the boundary of

5
Nonuniform meshes for the inverse Bloch transform

In this section, we introduce a method based on nonuniform meshes for numerical integration of functions of one variable with a square root singularity. Later on, we extend the method to approximate the inverse Bloch transform.

Let us start by considering the numerical approximation of the integral

I(\xi):=\int_{0}^{h}\xi(t)\,\,\mathrm{d}t\,\,,

(13)

where
\xi(t)=\xi_{1}(t)+\sqrt{t}\,\xi_{2}(t)
,
t\in[0,h]
, and both
\xi_{1}
and
\xi_{2}
are analytic in
[0,h]
.

Given a positive parameter
p\in(0,1)
and
N\in\mathbb{N}
, the method is described as follows. Let the nodal points be defined as

t_{0}=0,\qquad t_{n}=p^{n-1}h\,,\quad n=1,\ldots,N+1\,.

These points are the end points of the subintervals

J_{0}=[t_{0},t_{N+1}],\qquad J_{n}=[t_{n+1},t_{n}],\quad n=1,\dots,N\,.

The integrand
\xi
is analytic in any
J_{n}
,
n=1,\dots,N
, and we use an
M
-point Gauss–Legendre quadrature to approximate the integral on any such interval. On the interval
J_{0}
, the trapezoidal rule is used. Let the points and weights for the
M
-point Gauss–Legendre quadrature in
[-1,1]
be denoted by

\left\{(\tau_{j},\,w_{j}):\,j=1,\dots,M\right\}.

For
n=1,\ldots,N
, the integral
I_{n}(\xi):=\int_{t_{n+1}}^{t_{n}}\xi(t)\,\,\mathrm{d}t\,
is approximated by

I_{n}^{M}(\xi)=\frac{p^{n-1}h-p^{n}h}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{M}\xi\left(\frac{p^{n-1}h-p^{n}h}{2}\,\tau_{j}+\frac{p^{n-1}h+p^{n}h}{2}\right)w_{j}.

For
n=0
, the integral
I_{0}(\xi):=\int_{0}^{p^{N}h}\xi(t)\,\mathrm{d}t\,
is approximated by

I_{0}^{N}(\xi)=\frac{p^{N}h}{2}\xi(0)+\frac{p^{N}h}{2}\xi(p^{N}h).

Thus, the complete composite quadrature formula is

I_{N,M}(\xi)=\sum_{n=1}^{N}I_{n}^{M}(\xi)+I_{N}^{0}(\xi)=\frac{p^{N}h}{2}\xi(0)+\frac{p^{N}h}{2}\xi(p^{N}h)\\
{}+\sum_{n=1}^{N}\left[\frac{p^{n-1}h-p^{n}h}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{M}\xi\left(\frac{p^{n-1}h-p^{n}h}{2}\tau_{j}+\frac{p^{n-1}h+p^{n}h}{2}\right)w_{j}\right].

(14)

Our goal is to estimate the error of the approximation of
I(\xi)
by
I_{N,M}(\xi)
. We first quote a derivative free error estimate for Gaussian quadrature, for details we refer to
[
32
]
. We also recall our notation for ellipses from before Lemma
11
.

Theorem 14

(Theorem 5.3.13,
[
32
]
)
.

Let
{\varrho}>1/2
and consider the ellipse
\mathcal{E}_{0,1}^{\varrho}
as a subset of the complex plane. Let
\zeta:\,[0,1]\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
be real analytic with complex analytic extension to
\mathcal{E}_{0,1}^{\varrho}
. Denote by
I
the integral over
(0,1)
with integrand
\zeta
and by
Q_{M}
its approximation by the
M
-point Gauss-Legendre quadrature. Then

|I-Q_{M}|\leq C\,(2{\varrho})^{-2M}\max_{z\in\partial\mathcal{E}^{\varrho}_{0,1}}|\zeta(z)|\,.

The result can be extended to more general cases. Suppose
\zeta
is an analytic function in
[\alpha,\beta]
and let
{\varrho}>\frac{\beta-\alpha}{2}
. If
\zeta
can be analytically extended to
\mathcal{E}_{\alpha,\beta}^{\varrho}
, then with analogous notation,

|I-Q_{M}|\leq C\,\left(\frac{2{\varrho}}{\beta-\alpha}\right)^{-2M}\max_{z\in\partial\mathcal{E}^{\varrho}_{\alpha,\beta}}|\zeta(z)|\,.

(15)

We apply these results to estimating the error in our quadrature rule for each interval
J_{n}
,
n=1,\dots,N
.

Lemma 15
.

Suppose
\xi
can be analytically extended to an ellipse
\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{0,2h}^{{\varrho}h}
with vertices
0
,
2h
and sum of semi-axis
{\varrho}h
where
1<{\varrho}<2
. For any
n=1,\dots,N
, there is a constant
C>0
such that

\left|I_{n}(\xi)-I_{n}^{M}(\xi)\right|\leq C\left(\min\left\{\frac{1+\sqrt{p}}{1-\sqrt{p}}\,,\;\sqrt{\frac{{\varrho}}{2-{\varrho}}}\right\}\right)^{-2M}\max_{z\in\partial\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}^{{\varrho}h}_{0,2h}}|\xi(z)|.

(16)

Proof.

We wish to apply (
15
) and thus need to find the largest ellipse with foci at
p^{n}h
and
p^{n-1}h
that lies inside of
\mathcal{E}^{{\varrho}h}_{2h}
. Denote the semi-axis by
\lambda>\mu
, respectively and note that the linear eccentricity is
c=\left[\frac{p^{n-1}-p^{n}}{2}\right]h
. Hence, we have the necessary condition

\mu^{2}+\left[\frac{p^{n-1}-p^{n}}{2}\right]^{2}h^{2}=\lambda^{2}\,.

We wish to apply Corollary
13
and in the notation there we have
\hat{z}=h
,
z=\left[\frac{p^{n-1}+p^{n}}{2}\right]h
,
a=h
and
b=({\varrho}-1)\,h
. The necessary conditions to apply the corollary hence are

0<\lambda\leq\left[\frac{p^{n-1}+p^{n}}{2}\right]h\,,\qquad\qquad\frac{\mu}{\lambda}\leq{\varrho}-1\,.

Our goal is to maximize
\lambda+\mu
within these constraints. Note that for
\lambda>0
, the line
\mu=({\varrho}-1)\,\lambda
intersects the hyperbola
\mu^{2}+\left[\frac{p^{n-1}-p^{n}}{2}\right]^{2}h^{2}=\lambda^{2}
in exactly one point
(\hat{\lambda},\hat{\mu})
, where

\hat{\lambda}=\frac{1-p}{2\,\sqrt{2{\varrho}-{\varrho}^{2}}}\,p^{n-1}h\,,\qquad\hat{\lambda}+\hat{\mu}=\sqrt{\frac{{\varrho}}{2-{\varrho}}}\,\frac{1-p}{2}\,p^{n-1}h\,.

If
(1+p)/2\,p^{n-1}h<\hat{\lambda}
, we obtain the maximal value

\lambda+\mu=\left(\frac{1+p}{2}+\sqrt{p}\right)p^{n-1}h=\frac{(1+\sqrt{p})^{2}}{2}\,p^{n-1}h\,.

Thus

\lambda=\min\left\{\frac{1+p}{2}\,,\;\frac{1-p}{2\,\sqrt{2{\varrho}-{\varrho}^{2}}}\right\}p^{n-1}h

and

\frac{\lambda+\mu}{c}=\min\left\{\frac{1+\sqrt{p}}{1-\sqrt{p}}\,,\;\sqrt{\frac{{\varrho}}{2-{\varrho}}}\right\}.

Using (
15
) and the maximum modulus principle in complex analysis, we obtain

\displaystyle|I_{n}(\xi)-I^{n}_{M}(\xi)|\leq C\left(\min\left\{\frac{1+\sqrt{p}}{1-\sqrt{p}}\,,\;\sqrt{\frac{{\varrho}}{2-{\varrho}}}\right\}\right)^{-2M}\max_{z\in\partial\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}^{{\varrho}h}_{0,2h}}\,|\xi(z)|\,.

The proof is finished.
∎

We also estimate the error of the trapezoidal rule on
J_{0}
.

Lemma 16
.

The error of the trapezoidal rule on
J_{0}
is bounded by

\left|I_{0}(\xi)-I_{0}^{N}(\xi)\right|\leq C(p^{N}h)^{3/2}.

(17)

Proof.

Recall the representation
\xi(t)=\xi_{1}(t)+\sqrt{t}\,\xi_{2}(t)
. It is a standard result that the error in approximating the integral over the analytic function
\xi_{1}
by the trapezoidal rule is of order
\mathrm{O}\left((p^{N}h)^{2}\right)
. Thus we only consider the second term, which we approximate by linear interpolation

\xi_{\text{lin}}(t)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{p^{N}h}}\,\xi_{2}(p^{N}h)\,t\,.

For any
t\in J_{0}=[0,p^{N}h]
,

\left|\sqrt{t}\,\xi_{2}(t)-\xi_{\text{lin}}(t)\right|=\left|\sqrt{t}\,\xi_{2}(t)-\frac{\xi_{2}(p^{N}h)}{\sqrt{p^{N}h}}t\right|\leq 2\sqrt{p^{N}h}\,\sup_{t\in J_{0}}\left|\xi_{2}(t)\right|\leq C(p^{N}h)^{1/2}.

and the application of the trapezoidal rule can be estimated by

\left|\int_{0}^{p^{N}h}\left[\sqrt{t}\,\xi_{2}(t)-\xi_{\text{lin}}(t)\right]\,\mathrm{d}t\,\right|\leq C(p^{N}h)^{3/2}.

∎

With Lemma
16
and
15
, we are now prepared to state an error estimate for the complete composite quadrature rule:

Theorem 17
.

When
N
and
M
are two positive integers, there is a constant
C>0
such that

\left|I(\xi)-I_{N,M}(\xi)\right|\leq CN\left(\min\left\{\frac{1+\sqrt{p}}{1-\sqrt{p}}\,,\;\sqrt{\frac{{\varrho}}{2-{\varrho}}}\right\}\right)^{-2M}+C(p^{N}h)^{3/2}.

(18)

Proof.

Combine Lemmas
15
and
16
.
∎

We conclude by apply the method introduced above to the approximation of the inverse Bloch transform,

(\mathcal{J}^{-1}w)(x)=\int_{-\underline{k}}^{1-\underline{k}}w(\alpha,x)\,\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\alpha x_{1}}\,\mathrm{d}\alpha\,,\quad x\in\Omega^{2\pi}_{H}.

(19)

Depending on the different cases in the definition of
E
, this equation is

(\mathcal{J}^{-1}w)(x)=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\int_{-\underline{k}}^{1/2-\underline{k}}w(\alpha,x)\,\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\alpha x_{1}}\,\,\mathrm{d}\alpha\,+\int_{1/2-\underline{k}}^{1-\underline{k}}w(\alpha,x)\,\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\alpha x_{1}}\,\,\mathrm{d}\alpha\,\,,\quad\text{ when }\underline{k}=0,\,\frac{1}{2};\\
\\
\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\int_{-\underline{k}}^{0}w(\alpha,x)\,\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\alpha x_{1}}\,\,\mathrm{d}\alpha\,+\int_{0}^{\underline{k}}w(\alpha,x)\,\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\alpha x_{1}}\,\,\mathrm{d}\alpha\,\\
&\quad{}+\int_{\underline{k}}^{1/2}w(\alpha,x)\,\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\alpha x_{1}}\,\,\mathrm{d}\alpha\,+\int_{1/2}^{1-\underline{k}}w(\alpha,x)\,\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\alpha x_{1}}\,\,\mathrm{d}\alpha\,\,,\quad\text{otherwise.}\end{aligned}\end{cases}

(20)

Note that in each interval,
w(\alpha,x)
depends analytically on
\alpha
except for a square root singularity at one edge point. From the definition of
\underline{k}
, the length of each interval is not larger than
1/2
.
With a change of variables, we can rewrite any integral in the form

\int_{0}^{h}\varphi(\alpha,x)\,\mathrm{d}\alpha\,\,,

where
\varphi
has the form

\varphi(\alpha,x)=\varphi_{1}(\alpha,x)+\sqrt{\alpha}\,\varphi_{2}(\alpha,x)

with
\varphi_{1}
,
\varphi_{2}\in C^{\omega}\left([0,2h];S(D)\right)
. From Theorem
10
and Corollary
13
we know that
\varphi
can be extended analytically to
\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}^{r}_{0,2h}
with
r=h+\mathcal{C}\,\sqrt{h/2}
. Thus
{\varrho}
in Lemma and Theorem can be chosen as

{\varrho}=\min\left\{\frac{r}{h},\frac{3}{2}\right\}=\min\left\{1+\frac{\mathcal{C}}{\sqrt{2h}},\frac{3}{2}\right\}.

We redefine the integrals with new integrand as

I_{0}(\varphi)(x)=\int_{0}^{p^{N}h}\varphi(\alpha,x)\,\,\mathrm{d}\alpha\,,\qquad I_{n}(\varphi)(x)=\int_{p^{n}h}^{p^{n-1}h}\varphi(\alpha,x)\,\,\mathrm{d}\alpha\,,\quad n=1,2,\dots,N.

The numerical approximations are

\displaystyle I_{N}^{0}(\varphi)(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{p^{N}h}{2}\,\varphi(0,x)+\frac{p^{N}h}{2}\,\varphi(p^{N}h,x)\,;

\displaystyle I^{n}_{M}(\varphi)(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{p^{n-1}h-p^{n}h}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{M}\varphi\left(\frac{p^{n-1}h-p^{n}h}{2}\tau_{j}+\frac{p^{n-1}h+p^{n}h}{2},x\right)w_{j}\,.

We can now apply Theorem
17
to the approximation of any of the integrals in (
20
).

Theorem 18
.

There exists constants
C>0
and
\Theta>1
such that

\|I(\varphi)-I_{N,M}(\varphi)\|_{S(D)}\leq C\left(N\,\Theta^{-2M}+(p^{N}h)^{3/2}\right).

(21)

Proof.

Set

\Theta=\min\left\{\frac{1+\sqrt{p}}{1-\sqrt{p}}\,,\;\sqrt{\frac{\sqrt{2h}+\mathcal{C}}{\sqrt{2h}-\mathcal{C}}}\,,\;\sqrt{3}\right\}>1\,.

From our choice of
{\varrho}
and Theorem
17
, the result follows.
∎

6
Numerical approximation of scattering problems

6.1
Error estimation

In this section, we conclude our analysis by providing error estimates for the numerical solution of the original scattering problem (
1
)-(
4
). The algorithm can be divided into three steps:

Algorithm 19
.

1.

Depending on
k
, find all the nodal points
\alpha_{j}
and weights
\sigma_{j}
where
j=1,2,\dots,L
.

2.

For any
\alpha_{j}
, compute the numerical solution of
w_{\varepsilon}(\alpha_{j},x)
, where
{\varepsilon}>0
is a parameter corresponding to the discretization (see below).

3.

Compute
u_{N,M,{\varepsilon}}
by the inverse Bloch transform:

u_{N,M,{\varepsilon}}(x):=\sum_{j=1}^{L}w_{\varepsilon}\left(\alpha_{j},x\right)e^{\mathrm{i}\alpha_{j}x_{1}}\sigma_{j}.

In this algorithm, it remains to discuss the second step, i.e. how to approximate
w(\alpha_{j},x)
numerically for any fixed
\alpha_{j}
. In principle, this may be carried out by any preferred numerical method for solving a boundary value problem in a periodic domain such as the integral equation method or the finite element method. In the present work, we have chosen the latter approach.

Assume that
\mathcal{M}_{\varepsilon}
is a family of regular, quasi-uniform triangular meshes in the finite domain
\Omega_{H}^{2\pi}
with mesh width
0<{\varepsilon}\leq{\varepsilon}_{0}
, for some sufficiently small
{\varepsilon}_{0}
. For simplicity, we assume that the nodal points on the left and right boundaries have got identical
x_{2}
-coordinates. We omit all the nodal points on the left boundary by imposing periodic boundary conditions at
-\pi
and
\pi
, as well as those on
\Gamma^{2\pi}
due to the Dirichlet boundary conditions, and number the remaining nodes from
1
to
M_{0}
. Let
\psi^{j}_{M}
,
j=1,2,\dots,M_{0}
, denote the globally continuous function that is
2\pi
-periodic with respect to
x_{1}
, linear on each mesh triangle and equals to
1
at nodal point
j
as well as to
0
at all other nodal points. We define the space spanned by these functions by

V_{\text{per},{\varepsilon}}:={\rm span}\left\{\psi^{j}_{M_{0}}(x):\,j=1,2,\dots,M_{0}\right\}\subset\widetilde{H}^{1}_{0}(\Omega^{2\pi}_{H})\,.

For any fixed
\alpha
, we have the following error estimate for the Galerkin approximation to the solution of (
7
). For details we refer to
[
24
, Theorem 14]
.

Theorem 20
.

Suppose that
\zeta\in C^{1,1}(\mathbb{R})
. For any
\alpha\in W
, let
F(\alpha,\cdot)\in H^{1/2}_{\mathrm{per}}(\Gamma^{2\pi}_{H})
and denote by
w(\alpha,\cdot)
the solution of the variational equation (
7
). Then
w(\alpha,\cdot)\in H^{2}(\Omega^{2\pi}_{H})
. Moreover, when
{\varepsilon}_{0}>0
is sufficiently small and
w_{\varepsilon}(\alpha,\cdot)\in V_{{\mathrm{per}},{\varepsilon}}
solves

a_{\alpha}(w_{\varepsilon}(\alpha,\cdot),\varphi_{\varepsilon})=\int_{\Gamma^{2\pi}_{H}}F(\alpha,\cdot)\,\overline{\varphi_{\varepsilon}}\,\,\mathrm{d}s\,\quad\text{ for all }\varphi_{\varepsilon}\in V_{{\mathrm{per}},{\varepsilon}},

(22)

then

\|w_{\varepsilon}(\alpha,\cdot)-w(\alpha,\cdot)\|_{H^{\ell}(\Omega_{H}^{2\pi})}\leq C{\varepsilon}^{2-\ell}\|F(\alpha,\cdot)\|_{H^{1/2}_{\mathrm{per}}(\Gamma^{2\pi}_{H})},\quad\ell=0,1,

where
C
is independent of
\alpha\in W
.

From Theorems
18
and
20
, we can immediately derive an error estimate for the solution computed using Algorithm
19
.

Theorem 21
.

Suppose that
f\in H^{1/2}_{r}(\Omega_{H})
with
r\in(1/2,1)
such that
F(\alpha,x):=(\mathcal{J}f)(\alpha,x)\in\mathcal{A}^{\omega}\left((-\underline{k},1-\underline{k}];H^{1/2}_{\mathrm{per}}(\Gamma^{2\pi}_{H});E\right)
. Then the error between numerical approximation
u_{N,M,{\varepsilon}}
from Algorithm
19
and the exact solution
u
is bounded by

\|u_{N,M,{\varepsilon}}-u\|_{H^{\ell}(\Omega^{2\pi}_{H})}\leq C\left[{\varepsilon}^{2-\ell}+N\Theta^{-2M}+p^{3N/2}\right],\quad\ell=0,1,

where
\Theta>1
is defined as in Theorem
18
and
C
depends on
\|f\|_{H^{1/2}_{r}(\Omega_{H})}
,
k
and
p
.

Proof.

We only present detailed arguments for the case that
\underline{k}=0,0.5
. For the other cases, the proof is carried out similarly. Using the estimate from Theorem
18
, we obtain

\displaystyle\|u_{N,M,{\varepsilon}}-u\|_{H^{\ell}(\Omega^{2\pi}_{H})}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\sum_{\ell=1}^{2}\sum_{m=1}^{NM+2}w_{\varepsilon}\left(\alpha_{m}^{\ell},\cdot\right)e^{\mathrm{i}\alpha_{m}^{\ell}()_{1}}w_{m}^{\ell}-\sum_{\ell=1}^{2}\sum_{m=1}^{NM+2}w\left(\alpha_{m}^{\ell},\cdot\right)e^{\mathrm{i}\alpha_{m}^{\ell}()_{1}}w_{m}^{\ell}\right\|_{H^{\ell}(\Omega^{2\pi}_{H})}

\displaystyle+\left\|\sum_{\ell=1}^{2}\sum_{m=1}^{NM+2}w\left(\alpha_{m}^{\ell},\cdot\right)e^{\mathrm{i}\alpha_{m}^{\ell}()_{1}}w_{m}^{\ell}-u\right\|_{H^{\ell}(\Omega^{2\pi}_{H})}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\ell=1}^{2}\sum_{m=1}^{NM+2}w_{m}^{\ell}\left\|w_{\varepsilon}\left(\alpha_{m}^{\ell},\cdot\right)-w\left(\alpha_{m}^{\ell},\cdot\right)\right\|_{H^{\ell}(\Omega^{2\pi}_{H})}+C\,N\,\Theta^{-2M}+Cp^{3N/2}

\displaystyle\leq C{\varepsilon}^{2-\ell}\sum_{\ell=1}^{2}\sum_{m=1}^{NM+2}w_{m}^{\ell}\|F(\alpha_{m}^{\ell},\cdot)\|_{H^{1/2}_{\mathrm{per}}(\Gamma^{2\pi}_{H})}+C\,N\,\Theta^{-2M}+Cp^{3N/2},

where
()_{1}
denotes the first coordinate of a two dimensional argument vector. As
f\in H^{1/2}_{r}(\Gamma^{2\pi}_{H})
for
r>1/2
, by Theorem
5
we have
F=\mathcal{J}f\in H^{r}((-\underline{k},1-\underline{k}];H^{1/2}_{\mathrm{per}}(\Gamma^{2\pi}_{H}))
, and

\|F\|_{H_{0}^{r}((-\underline{k},1-\underline{k}];H^{1/2}_{\mathrm{per}}(\Gamma^{2\pi}_{H}))}=\|f\|_{H^{1/2}_{r}(\Gamma^{2\pi}_{H})}.

From Sobolev’s embedding theorem,
F\in C^{0}((-\underline{k},1-\underline{k}];H^{1/2}_{\mathrm{per}}(\Gamma^{2\pi}_{H}))
and

\|F\|_{C^{0}((-\underline{k},1-\underline{k}];H^{1/2}_{\mathrm{per}}(\Gamma^{2\pi}_{H}))}\leq C\|F\|_{H_{0}^{r}((-\underline{k},1-\underline{k}];H^{1/2}_{\mathrm{per}}(\Gamma^{2\pi}_{H}))}=\|f\|_{H^{1/2}_{r}(\Gamma^{2\pi}_{H})}.

From the fact that
\sum_{\ell=1}^{2}\sum_{m=1}^{NM+2}w_{m}^{\ell}=h
,

\|u_{N,M,{\varepsilon}}-u\|_{H^{\ell}(\Omega^{2\pi}_{H})}\leq C{\varepsilon}^{2-\ell}\|f\|_{H^{1/2}_{r}(\Gamma^{2\pi}_{H})}+C\,N\,\Theta^{-2M}+Cp^{3N/2}\leq C\left[{\varepsilon}^{2-\ell}+N\,\Theta^{-2M}+p^{3N/2}\right].

The proof is finished.
∎

6.2
Numerical experiments

We present six numerical examples that demonstrate the convergence properties of Algorithm
19
. In all of the examples, we use the same periodic surface given by

\zeta(t)=\frac{3}{2}+\frac{\sin t}{3}-\frac{\cos 2t}{4}.

The following parameters are also fixed:

We use two different wave numbers,

and

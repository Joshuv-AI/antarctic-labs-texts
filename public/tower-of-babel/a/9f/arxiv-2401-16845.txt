Reading yesterday’s news. Layout recognition by segmentation of historical newspaper pages

Abstract
Newspapers are important sources for historians interested in past societies’ cultural values, social structures, and their changes. Since the 19th century, newspapers have been widely available and spread regionally. Today, historical newspapers are digitized but unavailable in a separate metadata-enhanced form. Machine-readable metadata, however, is a prerequisite for a mass statistical analysis of this source. This paper focuses on parsing the complex layout of historic newspaper pages, which today’s machines do not understand well. We argue for using neural networks, which require detailed annotated data in large numbers. Our Bonn newspaper dataset consists of 486 pages of the Kölnische Zeitung from the years 1866 and 1924. We propose solving the newspaper-understanding problem by training a U-Net on our new dataset, which delivers satisfactory performance.

2
Related Work

Very early text recognition systems worked with multiple disjunct steps such as line discovery, baseline fitting, word finding, and word recognition
(
Smith, 2007
)
. With the increased adoption of deep learning methodology in the field, neural networks took over many of these tasks until only text line discovery and text detection remained as separate tasks
(
Zhang et al., 2016
)
.
The process culminated recently.
Kim et al., 2022
proposed to train transformer networks directly on images and annotated text without any intermediate steps. Their networks combine a swin transformer
(
Liu et al., 2021
)
with Bart decoder
(
Lewis et al., 2019
)
. During an initial pretraining, their encoder was trained on 2 million synthetics and 11 million scanned documents. The decoder initially starts from weights pre-trained on multilingual text data. Full system training relies on 800 Latin alphabet receipts, 1500 Chinese train ticket images, 20 thousand business cards, and 40 thousand Korean receipts.
Using a similar system
Blecher et al., 2023
trained an OCR engine to recover latex code from scans or PDFs of academic documents. Surprisingly, their network generalizes to old mathematical literature. Arxiv papers do not resemble historical newspapers, however. Consequently, these tools are not useful in this case.

2.1
Document processing in Digital History

Optical character recognition (OCR) is an important tool in digital history. Architecturally
Liebl and Burghard, 2020
, for example, propose an OCR pipeline based on existing open source components like Tesseract
(
Smith, 2007
)
for the digitization of the
Berliner Börsen Zeitung
. Unfortunately, from a digital history perspective, many modern systems focus on recent data and suffer from poor performance in a historical setting. The current situation has led to a large body of OCR error correction work
(
Carlson et al., 2023
)
, highlighting the need for specialized data sets and software.

Related work on historical newspaper recognition includes the Europeana corpus
(
Clausner et al., 2015
)
. The dataset contains
528
annotated pages. While the Europeana data set is a huge step forward, we found the layout recognition performance of networks trained exclusively on Europeana insufficient for our applications. We do, however, benefit from pretraining on the Europeana data set. We believe both historical newspaper datasets are valuable together.

2.2
Network architecture

DhSegment
(
Oliveira et al., 2018
)
proposed a UNet structure based on the popular ResNet50 architecture
(
He et al., 2016
)
. As described by
Ronneberger et al., 2015
, the network features a contracting and an expanding part. The contracting subnetwork follows the Resnet50 architecture.
It uses the first six resnet blocks as encoder. Initially, optimization of the contracting network part can start from pre-trained ImageNet weights, while optimization of the expanding path has to start from scratch. Additionally, an expanding subnetwork starts with the encoding from the contracting network and produces a segmentation output at the input resolution.
We do not limit our tests to convolution. We additionally explore adding a Convolutional Block Attention Module (CBAM)
(
Woo et al., 2018
)
module to DhSegment. Block attention computes channels and spatial attention masks. When applied to the features, these allow the network to re-weight the features at
a particular network level.

Bonn Newspapers Dataset

We include the following classes;

article,

header,

heading,

caption,

separator_vertical,

separator_horizontal,

separator_short,

table,

image
, and

unknown_region
.
For the ten aforementioned categories, we annotated all elements on every page in the dataset with bounding polygons in the XML-format. Polygons capture the newspaper header, headlines, articles, tables, and different separators on each page. Vertical separators highlight different columns of a page. Short horizontal separators can indicate the beginning of a new article or a subsection of an article.
Long and thick horizontal separators have often separated different sections of a page in past newspapers. For example, when a new smaller article populates the bottom of a page. The header category covers the newspaper’s name that appears on the top of the front page. To the left and right of the newspaper name, historical newspapers often have smaller blocks with additional information, such as the name of the editor-in-chief, the publication date, or the subscription price, as well as further information. These polygons are annotated as captions. Polygons that cover articles, headlines, and tables are annotated, respectively. The result of this annotation process is shown in Figure
3

Our dataset also includes annotated advertisements. We primarily use a combination of the categories described above to annotate the historic ads. We have decided against new categories so as not to confound the model’s training. This applies, in particular, to the separator classes. Therefore, we use the categories
separator_short
,
separator_vertical
, and
separator_horizontal
for the annotation of thick and thin frames around individual advertisements. Advertisements tend to use text blocks with bigger fonts. In order to be consistent with our annotations, we mark these as
heading
. For the same reason, the normal-sized text is annotated as
article
. However, to cover the special features of the layout of the advertisements, three additional categories must be introduced. White text on a black background is annotated as
inverted_text
, decorative borders are assigned the category
separator_fancy
, and graphic elements that start to appear at first in advertisements are tagged as
image
. Finally, a text field annotated as
advertisement
is placed around the entire advertisement area. This allows advertisements to be separated from the rest of the newspaper layout.

5
Conclusion and future work

This work introduces the
Bonn historic newspaper dataset
and the first layout segmentation results.
Our dataset also includes ads, where we would like to improve the performance in the future. Furthermore, we will build upon the layout detection to extract full text. To do so, we will additionally annotate ground truth text for a subset of the data.

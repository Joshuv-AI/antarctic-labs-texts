The Eikonal Phase and Spinning Observables
Juan Pablo Gatica

Abstract
We set up the Kosower, Maybee, O’Connell formalism for generic spin following a previous field theory construction and use it to extract a next-to-leading order eikonal formula for the linear-in-spin momentum impulse, Δ ​ p μ \Delta p^{\mu} , and spin kick, Δ ​ S μ ​ ν \Delta S^{\mu\nu} . We explicitly test the formalism for the case of electrodynamics and find agreement with previous QFT and worldline results. This includes the case where multiple spins propagate with allowed spin transitions, or equivalently where no spin supplementary condition is imposed on the worldline equations of motion.

2
Review

2.1
The Classical Scaling with Spin

We will be taking the classical limit of two-body scattering of particles mediated by electrodynamics separated by impact parameter
b
with charge
eq_{i}
, momentum
p_{i}
, mass
m_{i}
and spin
S_{i}
. In electrodynamics, the classical limit implies a hierarchy between the particle Compton wavelength,
\lambda_{c_{i}}={\hbar}/{m_{i}c}
, and the classical charge radius,
r_{Q_{i}}={e^{2}q_{i}^{2}}/{4\pi m_{i}}
; namely,
\lambda_{c_{i}}\ll r_{Q_{i}}
, which corresponds to the large charge limit in QED
Bern:2021xze
;
Kosower:2018adc
. We will also be performing the Post-Lorentzian (PL) expansion, whose expansion parameter
r_{Q_{i}}/b\ll 1
, amounts to a large separation limit. Performed together, the classical limit and PL expansion gives us the following hierarchy of length scales

\lambda_{c_{i}}\ll r_{Q_{i}}\ll b.

(1)

For scattering amplitudes, it is more natural to consider the above hierarchy in momentum space. In two-body scattering the momentum transfer
q
is the conjugate variable to the impact parameter
b
,

|b|\sim\frac{1}{|q|}\gg\frac{1}{m_{i}},

(2)

which implies

|q|\ll m_{i}\sim|p_{i}|,

(3)

where
p
is some external four-momentum with natural units
\hbar=c=1
. We also know, for two-body classical systems, the angular momentum can be expressed as

|J_{i}|\equiv|p_{i}||b|\gg 1.

(4)

We make the same assumption as Ref.
Bern:2020buy
for the magnitudes of the spin and angular momentum,
|J_{i}|\sim|S_{i}|
. The above is summarized in the following scaling,

p\rightarrow\lambda^{0}p,\hskip 22.76228ptq\rightarrow\lambda q,\hskip 22.76228ptb\rightarrow\lambda^{-1}b,\hskip 22.76228ptS\rightarrow\lambda^{-1}S,

(5)

where
\lambda\ll 1
, which helps us organize the classical limit.

2.2
Definition of Spin

We follow Refs.
Bern:2023ity
;
Bern:2020buy
;
Kosmopoulos:2021zoq
, and model the massive scattering bodies with complex general spin-s fields
\Phi^{\mu_{1}\cdots\mu_{s}}_{s}=\Phi_{s}^{\mu(s)}
, where we choose
s\in\mathbb{Z}^{0+}
. These fields are symmetric and traceless but not transverse; therefore we do not need to include lower-spin auxiliary fields, such as those in Refs.
Lindwasser:2023dcv
;
Lindwasser:2023zwo
;
Singh:1974qz
. It has been argued in Ref.
Bern:2023ity
that issues arising from these non-transverse states do not contribute to the classical limit. As a result, from the rank-
s
polarization tensors associated with the non-transverse fields, we construct the following completeness relation

\delta^{\mu(s)}_{\,\,\,\,\,\nu(s)}=\sum_{a}\epsilon^{\mu(s)}_{a}(p)\epsilon^{*\,a}_{\nu(s)}(p),

(6)

where we sum over the little group indices
a
.

We use the spin tensor, which we define as

[S^{\mu\nu}(p)]^{a^{\prime}}_{\,\,a}\equiv\epsilon^{*\,a^{\prime}}_{\alpha(s)}(p)\left(\mathit{M}^{\mu\nu}\right)^{\alpha(s)}_{\,\,\,\beta(s)}\epsilon^{\beta(s)}_{a}(p),

(7)

where
\left(\mathit{M}^{\mu\nu}\right)^{\alpha(s)}_{\,\,\,\beta(s)}
is the spin-
s
representation of the Lorentz generator

\left(\mathit{M}^{\mu\nu}\right)^{\alpha(s)}_{\,\,\,\beta(s)}=is\,\delta^{[\mu}_{(\beta_{1}}\eta^{\nu](\alpha_{1}}\delta^{\alpha_{2}}_{\beta_{2}}\cdots\delta^{\alpha_{s})}_{\beta_{s})},

(8)

satisfying the Lorentz algebra

[\mathit{M}^{\mu\nu},\mathit{M}^{\rho\sigma}]=-i\left(\eta^{\mu\rho}\mathit{M}^{\sigma\nu}+\eta^{\nu\rho}\mathit{M}^{\mu\sigma}-\eta^{\mu\sigma}\mathit{M}^{\rho\nu}-\eta^{\nu\sigma}\mathit{M}^{\mu\rho}\right).

(9)

Note that because we have constructed the spin tensor from our unconstrained polarization tensors, the spin tensor does not necessarily obey the covariant spin supplementary condition (SSC),
p_{\mu}S^{\mu\nu}(p)=0

Bern:2023ity
. We relax the SSC in order to include boost degrees of freedom in the spin tensor, as in Ref.
Bern:2023ity
, although these can be set to zero at any point.

2.3
Basics of Observable Impulses

We now review the methods covered in Refs.
Kosower:2018adc
;
Herrmann:2021tct
to arrive at different observable impulses. First, we measure the expectation value of some quantum operator
\mathbb{O}
from the asymptotic past (“in”) to the asymptotic future (“out”) and take the difference; this results in an impulse of some corresponding observable
\mathcal{O}
,

\Delta\mathcal{O}=\langle{\rm out}|\mathbb{O}|{\rm out}\rangle-\langle{\rm in}|\mathbb{O}|{\rm in}\rangle.

(10)

We then relate these out-states to in-states by the S matrix,
S=1+iT
. We model our in-state by some
N
-particle wavepacket
|\Psi\rangle
, which depends on scalar wavefunctions
\phi(p_{i})
, little group dependent parts of the wavefunction
\xi_{a_{i}}
, and momentum eigenstates
|p_{i};a_{i}\rangle
, where
a_{i}
are little group indices. Doing so gives a natural separation that has come to be known as the “virtual” and “real” part of the impulse
Kosower:2018adc
;
Herrmann:2021tct

\Delta\mathcal{O}=\langle{\Psi}|i[\mathbb{O},T]|{\Psi}\rangle+\langle{\Psi}|T^{\dagger}[\mathbb{O},T]|{\Psi}\rangle,

(11)

where

|\Psi\rangle=\bigotimes_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{a_{i}}\frac{1}{N!}\int d\Phi(p_{i})\,\phi(p_{i})\,\xi^{a_{i}}\,e^{ib_{i}\cdot p_{i}}\,|p_{i};a_{i}\rangle.

(12)

Here we write the integration over Lorentz invariant phase space as

\int d\Phi(p_{i})\equiv\int\hat{d}^{D}p_{i}\,\hat{\delta}^{(+)}(p_{i}^{2}-m_{i}^{2}),

(13)

where
D=4-2\epsilon
,
\hat{d}^{D}p_{i}\equiv{d^{D}p_{i}}/{(2\pi)^{4}}
, and
\hat{\delta}^{(+)}(k^{2}-m^{2})\equiv 2\pi\delta^{(+)}(k^{2}-m^{2})
, which only takes into account positive energy states; in the future, we will drop the
(+)
superscript on the
\delta
-function, since in the classical limit we do not have to make this distinction. We also assume a displacement
b_{i}
for each single-particle wavepacket with respect to some arbitrary origin. We now fix ourselves to
N=2
body scattering, where we take

\frac{|p_{1};a_{1}\rangle\otimes|p_{2};a_{2}\rangle}{2!}=|p_{1}p_{2};a_{1}a_{2}\rangle.

(14)

Now we can write the observable impulse for two-body scattering as

\Delta\mathcal{O}=\prod_{i=1}^{2}\,\sum_{a_{i},a_{i}^{\prime}}\int d\Phi(p_{i})\,d\Phi(p_{i}^{\prime})\,\phi^{*}(p_{i}^{\prime})\phi(p_{i})\,\xi^{*}_{a_{i}^{\prime}}\,\xi^{a_{i}}\,e^{-ib_{i}\cdot(p_{i}^{\prime}-p_{i})}\,(\mathcal{I}^{\{a^{\prime}_{i}\}}_{v\,\,\{a_{i}\}}+\mathcal{I}^{\{a^{\prime}_{i}\}}_{r\,\,\{a_{i}\}}),

(15)

where

\mathcal{I}^{\{a^{\prime}_{i}\}}_{v\,\,\{a_{i}\}}=\langle{p_{1}^{\prime}p_{2}^{\prime};a_{1}^{\prime}a_{2}^{\prime}}|i[\mathbb{O},T]|{p_{1}p_{2};a_{1}a_{2}}\rangle,\hskip 14.22636pt\mathcal{I}^{\{a^{\prime}_{i}\}}_{r\,\,\{a_{i}\}}=\langle{p_{1}^{\prime}p_{2}^{\prime};a_{1}^{\prime}a_{2}^{\prime}}|T^{\dagger}[\mathbb{O},T]|{p_{1}p_{2};a_{1}a_{2}}\rangle,

(16)

are known as the virtual kernel and real kernel, respectively.

For simplicity, we consider only one of our scattering bodies to be spinning; therefore, we can neglect one of these bodies’ little group information. Using these states, we define our two-body scattering amplitude as

\displaystyle\langle{p_{1}^{\prime}p_{2}^{\prime};a_{1}^{\prime}}|T|p_{1}p_{2};a_{1}\rangle

\displaystyle=\hat{\delta}^{(4)}(p_{1}^{\prime}+p_{2}^{\prime}-p_{1}-p_{2})\left[\mathcal{A}(p_{1},p_{2},p_{1}^{\prime},p_{2}^{\prime},S_{1})\right]^{a_{1}^{\prime}}_{\,\,a_{1}}

\displaystyle\equiv\hat{\delta}^{(4)}(p_{1}^{\prime}+p_{2}^{\prime}-p_{1}-p_{2})\,\epsilon^{*\,a_{1}^{\prime}}(p_{1}^{\prime})\cdot\mathbb{A}(p_{1},p_{2},p_{1}^{\prime},p_{2}^{\prime})\cdot\epsilon_{a_{1}}(p_{1}),

(17)

where in the last line we stripped the amplitude of its polarizations, allowing us to work with the Lorentz generator as opposed to the spin tensor; we also define
S^{\mu\nu}(p_{1})\equiv S^{\mu\nu}_{1}
. We leave the summation over representation indices in the second line of Eq. (
2.3
) implicit.

We expand the amplitudes in powers of the coupling constant
g
and powers of spin,

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}(p_{i},p_{i}^{\prime},S_{1})=

\displaystyle g^{2}\left(\mathcal{A}^{(1,0)}(p_{i},p_{i}^{\prime})+\mathcal{A}^{(1,1)}(p_{i},p_{i}^{\prime},S_{1})+\cdots\right)

\displaystyle\hbox{}+g^{4}\left(\mathcal{A}^{(2,0)}(p_{i},p_{i}^{\prime})+\mathcal{A}^{(2,1)}(p_{i},p_{i}^{\prime},S_{1})+\cdots\right)+\cdots

(18)

where, for
\mathcal{A}^{(i,j)}
,
i
tracks the powers of
g^{2}
and
j
tracks the powers of the spin tensor. In the case of two-body scattering of general spin fields coupled to electrodynamics, we expand in
g^{2}=4\pi\alpha q_{1}q_{2}
, where
\alpha=e^{2}/4\pi
is the QED fine structure constant.
Note that our amplitude in the first line of Eq. (
2.3
) is a matrix with respect to the little group indices, which it inherits from its dependence on
S^{\mu\nu}
. When contracted with the
\xi^{a_{i}},\xi^{*}_{a_{i}^{\prime}}
and integrated over the initial momentum phase space, we arrive at the expectation value of the spin tensor

\int d\Phi(p_{1})\sum_{a_{1}a_{1}^{\prime}}\xi^{*}_{a_{1}^{\prime}}\,\xi^{a_{1}}\left[S^{\mu\nu}_{1}\right]^{a_{1}^{\prime}}_{\,\,a_{1}}=\langle{S^{\mu\nu}_{1}}\rangle.

(19)

We are only concerned with the classical limit of these impulses. Therefore, following the arguments of Refs.
Kosower:2018adc
;
Cristofoli:2021jas
;
Aoude:2021oqj
, these wavepackets sharply peak about their classical values, amounting to the appearance of on-shell
\delta
-functions. Similarly, since we are taking the classical limit of quantum expectation values, we must take our states to be spin coherent states, i.e. they minimize the standard deviations of the spin degrees of freedom of the observables
Bern:2020buy
;
Aoude:2021oqj
. As a result, we are allowed to simplify Eq. (
15
)

\Delta\mathcal{O}=\int\hat{d}^{D}q\,\hat{\delta}(2p_{1}\cdot q)\,\hat{\delta}(2p_{2}\cdot q)\,e^{-ib\cdot q}\,\langle{(\mathcal{I}_{v}+\mathcal{I}_{r})}\rangle\equiv\int\cancel{D}q\,e^{-ib\cdot q}\,(\mathcal{I}_{v}+\mathcal{I}_{r}),

(20)

where
q^{\mu}=p^{\prime\mu}_{1}-p^{\mu}_{1}
is the small momentum transfer conjugate to the impact parameter
b^{\mu}=b^{\mu}_{2}-b^{\mu}_{1}
, and we have absorbed the
\delta
-functions into the the measure via the
\cancel{D}
notation. We will leave the expectation value brackets implicit. It should be emphasized that the amplitudes,
\mathcal{A}
or
\mathbb{A}
, spin tensor
S^{\mu\nu}
, and Lorentz generator
\mathit{M}^{\mu\nu}
are matrix valued in their respective representation indices; in the case of
\mathcal{A}
and
S^{\mu\nu}
, these are the little group indices
a_{i},a_{i}^{\prime}
; for
\mathbb{A}
and
\mathit{M}^{\mu\nu}
, these are the Lorentz group representation indices
\alpha(s)
.

We will be calculating both the momentum impulse
\Delta p^{\mu}
and the spin kick
\Delta S^{\mu\nu}
to linear order in spin and next to leading order in the coupling constant; therefore, we need to consider the corresponding operators
\mathbb{P}^{\mu}
and
\mathbb{S}^{\mu\nu}
, respectively, and their expectation values.

2.4
Momentum Impulse Set Up

Starting with the easier case, we consider the momentum impulse. The following treatment was presented in Refs.
Herrmann:2021tct
;
Kosower:2018adc
;
Maybee:2019jus
;
Cristofoli:2021jas
;
Aoude:2021oqj
for the scalar and fixed spin cases; however, we are working with general spin fields and therefore some new nuances need to be considered.

First, we consider the virtual kernel of the momentum impulse,

\displaystyle\langle{\Psi}|i[\mathbb{P}^{\mu},T]|{\Psi}\rangle=\int\cancel{D}q\,e^{-ib\cdot q}\,\mathcal{I}^{\mu}_{v}

\displaystyle=\int\cancel{D}q\,e^{-ib\cdot q}\,iq^{\mu}\,\epsilon^{*}(p_{1}+q)\cdot\mathbb{A}(p_{1},p_{2},q)\cdot\epsilon(p_{1})

\displaystyle=\int\cancel{D}q\,e^{-ib\cdot q}\,iq^{\mu}\,\epsilon^{*}(p_{1}+q)\cdot\epsilon(p_{1})\,\mathcal{A}(p_{1},p_{2},S_{1}),

(21)

where we insert a complete set of states, Eq. (
6
), on either side of the polarization stripped amplitude to arrive at the last line. The product of polarizations in the second line of Eq. (
2.4
) can be re-expressed as an exponential

\epsilon^{*}(p_{1}+q)\cdot\epsilon(p_{1})=\text{exp}\left(iq_{\mu}\frac{S^{\mu\nu}_{1}(k_{\nu}+p_{1\,\nu})}{p_{1}\cdot k+m_{1}^{2}}\right)=\text{exp}(iq\cdot\omega),

(22)

where
k
is some arbitrary reference frame from which one boosts from to arrive at either momentum
p_{1}
or
p_{1}+q
; in our case, it is the rest frame of the mass
m_{1}
body. A derivation can be found in Appendix
B
as well as in Refs.
Bern:2023ity
;
Chung:2018kqs
. This naturally leads us to introduce a new conjugate variable to
q
, which we call the covariant impact parameter
Bern:2020buy
;
Kosmopoulos:2021zoq

b^{\mu}_{\text{cov}}=b^{\mu}-\omega^{\mu}=b^{\mu}-\frac{S^{\mu\nu}_{1}(k_{\nu}+p_{1\,\nu})}{p_{1}\cdot k+m_{1}^{2}}.

(23)

This leaves us with the all orders in spin and coupling constant result

\langle{\Psi}|i[\mathbb{P}^{\mu},T]|{\Psi}\rangle=\int\cancel{D}q\,e^{-ib_{\text{cov}}\cdot q}\,iq^{\mu}\,\mathcal{A}(p_{1},p_{2},S_{1}).

(24)

Now we consider the real kernel of the momentum impulse,

\int\cancel{D}q\,e^{-ib\cdot q}\,\mathcal{I}^{\mu}_{r}=\langle{\Psi}|T^{\dagger}[\mathbb{P}^{\mu}_{1},T]|\Psi\rangle=\bigotimes_{i}^{N}\int\frac{d\rho_{i}}{N!}\langle{\Psi}|T^{\dagger}|\rho_{i}\rangle\langle{\rho_{i}}|[\mathbb{P}^{\mu}_{1},T]|\Psi\rangle,

(25)

where we inserted some complete set of states
\rho_{i}
. By choosing the content of our complete set of states, we automatically fix the order in the coupling constant
g
to which we can calculate. By virtue of the external states, we must insert a minimum of one massive state per matter line so as to preserve the mass content. In principle, we can insert additional massless states but this leads to higher-order contributions in
g
, which we are not concerned with in this paper. Choosing the minimal complete set of states fixes us to the next to leading order in

\displaystyle=e^{iq\cdot\omega}\int\cancel{D}l\,l^{\mu}\,\mathcal{A}^{(1)}(q-l,p_{1}+l,p_{2}-l,S_{1})\,\mathcal{A}^{(1)}(l,p_{1},p_{2},S_{1})

From Eq. (

\mathit{M}^{\mu\nu}
. For example, in the case of a quadratic product

\mathit{M}^{\mu\nu}\mathit{M}^{\rho\sigma}=\frac{1}{2}\left\{\mathit{M}^{\mu\nu},\mathit{M}^{\rho\sigma}\right\}+\frac{1}{2}\left[\mathit{M}^{\mu\nu},\mathit{M}^{\rho\sigma}\right],

(27)

where the curly braces are an anti-commutator and the last term, by virtue of the Lorentz algebra, is linear in the Lorentz generator. At the level of the spin tensor one can summarize this in the following spin counting

S^{\mu\nu}\sim\mathcal{O}(s),\hskip 28.45274pt[\hskip 14.22636pt,\hskip 14.22636pt]\sim\mathcal{O}(s^{-1}),\hskip 28.45274pt\{\hskip 14.22636pt,\hskip 14.22636pt\}\sim\mathcal{O}(1),

(28)

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}^{(2,1)\,\mu}_{r}=

\displaystyle\hskip 25.6073pte^{iq\cdot\omega}\int\cancel{D}l\,l^{\mu}\left(\mathcal{A}^{(1,0)}(q-l)\mathcal{A}^{(1,1)}(l,p_{1},p_{2},S_{1})+\mathcal{A}^{(1,1)}(q-l,p_{1}+l,p_{2}-l,S_{1})\mathcal{A}^{(1,0)}(l)\right.

\displaystyle\left.\hskip 91.04872pt\hbox{}+\frac{1}{2}\left[\mathcal{A}^{(1,1)}(q-l,p_{1}+l,p_{2}-l,S_{1}),\mathcal{A}^{(1,1)}(l,p_{1},p_{2},S_{1})\right]\right).

(29)

2.5
Spin Kick Set Up

Whereas the momentum operator was easy to work with because we are using momentum eigenstates, the spin operator expectation value is more complicated

\langle{p^{\prime};a^{\prime}}|\mathbb{S}^{\mu\nu}|p;a\rangle=-\frac{1}{2}\,\hat{\delta}_{\Phi}(p^{\prime}-p)\,\epsilon^{*\,a^{\prime}}(p^{\prime})\cdot\mathit{M}^{\mu\nu}\cdot\epsilon_{a}(p),

(30)

where
\hat{\delta}_{\Phi}(p^{\prime}-p)
is the
\delta
-function for the Lorentz invariant phase space integral such that
\int d\Phi(p)\,f(p)\,\hat{\delta}_{\Phi}(p^{\prime}-p)=f(p^{\prime})
. A derivation for Eq. (
30
) can be found in Appendix
A
.

Plugging in Eq. (
30
) to the virtual kernel we get

\displaystyle\langle{\Psi}|i[\mathbb{S}^{\mu\nu}_{1},T]|{\Psi}\rangle=\int\cancel{D}q\,e^{-ib\cdot q}\,\mathcal{I}^{\mu\nu}_{v}

\displaystyle=\frac{i}{2}\int\cancel{D}q\,e^{-ib\cdot q}\,\epsilon^{*}(p_{1}+q)\cdot\left[\mathbb{A}(q,p_{1},p_{2}),\mathit{M}^{\mu\nu}\right]\cdot\epsilon(p_{1})

\displaystyle=\frac{i}{2}\int\cancel{D}q\,e^{-ib_{\text{cov}}\cdot q}\,\left[\mathcal{A}(q,p_{1},p_{2},S_{1}),S^{\mu\nu}(p_{1})\right].

(31)

For the real kernel at next to leading order in the coupling constant to all orders in spin

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}^{(2)\,\mu\nu}_{r}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int\cancel{D}l\,\epsilon^{*}(p_{1}+q)\cdot\mathbb{A}^{(1)}(q-l,p_{1}+l,p_{2}-l)\cdot\left[\mathbb{A}^{(1)}(l,p_{1},p_{2}),\mathit{M}^{\mu\nu}\right]\cdot\epsilon(p_{1})

\displaystyle=\frac{e^{iq\cdot\omega}}{2}\int\cancel{D}l\,\mathcal{A}^{(1)}(q-l,p_{1}+l,p_{2}-l,S_{1})\left[\mathcal{A}^{(1)}(l,p_{1},p_{2},S_{1}),S^{\mu\nu}_{1}\right],

(32)

where if we use the spin scaling in Eq. (
28
) and expand to linear order in spin counting
s
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}^{(2,1)\,\mu\nu}_{r}=

\displaystyle\,\frac{e^{iq\cdot\omega}}{2}\int\cancel{D}l\left(\mathcal{A}^{(1,0)}(q-l)\left[\mathcal{A}^{(1,1)}(l,p_{1},p_{2}),S^{\mu\nu}_{1}\right]\right.

\displaystyle\left.\hskip 48.36958pt\hbox{}+\frac{1}{2}\left[\mathcal{A}^{(1,1)}(q-l,p_{1}+l,p_{2}-l,S_{1}),\left[\mathcal{A}^{(1,1)}(l,p_{1},p_{2},S_{1}),S^{\mu\nu}_{1}\right]\right]\right).

(33)

Before we move on to deriving the eikonal formulae for the momentum impulse and the spin kick, we must first consider some properties of general spin amplitudes.

3
General Spin Amplitudes

We use general spin fields whose specifics beyond their non-transversality are not important for our derivation. For the derivation of the eikonal formula, we will keep our amplitudes general and grounded on minimal assumptions.

To verify our results, we use the following Lagrangian density from Ref.
Bern:2023ity
,

\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{L}_{\text{min}}+\mathcal{L}_{\text{EM}}+\sum_{i}\mathcal{L}^{(i)}_{\text{non-min}},

(34)

where the sum is over powers of the Lorentz generator. In this paper we expand only to leading order in powers of the Lorentz generator

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\text{min}}=(D^{\mu}\Phi_{s})^{\dagger}\,D_{\mu}\Phi_{s}-m^{2}\,\Phi^{\dagger}_{s}\,\Phi_{s},

(35)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\text{EM}}=-\frac{1}{4}F^{\mu\nu}F_{\mu\nu},

(36)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{(1)}_{\text{non-min}}=C_{1}F_{\mu\nu}\,\Phi^{\dagger}_{s}\,\mathbb{M}^{\mu\nu}\,\Phi_{s}+\frac{D_{1}}{m^{2}}\,F_{\mu\nu}\,\left((D_{\rho}\Phi_{s})^{\dagger}\,\mathbb{M}^{\rho\mu}\,D^{\nu}\Phi_{s}+(D^{\nu}\Phi_{s})^{\dagger}\,\mathbb{M}^{\rho\mu}\,D_{\rho}\Phi_{s}\right),

(37)

where

D^{\mu}\Phi_{s}=\partial^{\mu}\Phi_{s}-i\sqrt{4\pi\alpha}\,qA^{\mu}\Phi_{s},\,\,\,\,\,\,F_{\mu\nu}=2\,\partial_{[\mu}A_{\nu]},

(38)

\Phi_{s}
are the general spin fields,
C_{1}
and
D_{1}
are free parameters that, in principle, depend on macroscopic properties of the scattering bodies. In the usual worldline construction
Levi:2015msa
;
Steinhoff:2014kwa
;
Steinhoff:2015ksa
;
Vines:2016unv
;
citeycite
, where a spin supplementary condition is imposed, only the analog of the
C_{1}
coefficients appears.
D_{1}
is an example of an “extra Wilson coefficient” that appears when no SSC is imposed on the worldline, or when multiple spin states with allowed transitions propagate on field theory, as we allow here.
Summations over representation indices of the fields and the Lorentz generators are left implicit. We are not including lower spin auxiliary fields to
\Phi_{s}
, meaning we do not have transverse fields and therefore we are able to construct a simple completeness relation as in Eq. (
6
).

We take the classical limit of two-body, general spin scattering amplitudes by using the scaling in Eq. (
5
) and expanding in the
\lambda\rightarrow 0
limit. We also demand that we are calculating the long-range contributions of these amplitudes, which amounts to the omission of any term that may cancel any massless exchange propagators in the two-body amplitude. To facilitate taking the classical limit of amplitudes, we use special kinematics
Herrmann:2021tct
;
Bern:2021xze
;
Parra-Martinez:2020dzs

\bar{p}_{1}=p_{1}+q/2\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\bar{p}_{2}=p_{2}-q/2\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,y=\frac{\bar{p}_{1}\cdot\bar{p}_{2}}{\bar{m}_{1}\bar{m}_{2}}=\bar{u}_{1}\cdot\bar{u}_{2}\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\bar{m}_{i}^{2}=\bar{p}_{i}^{2}=m_{i}^{2}-q^{2}/4,

(39)

where
\bar{u}_{i}=\bar{p}_{i}/\bar{m}_{i}
; this simplifies the scale of our calculation by making
\bar{u}_{i}\cdot q=0
.

3.1
Tree Level

At tree level, we expect the the classical limit of the scalar amplitude to be of the form

\displaystyle\mathbb{A}^{(1,0)}(q)=\frac{\mathcal{N}^{(1,0)}}{q^{2}}\sim\mathcal{O}(\lambda^{-2}),

(40)

where, for Eq. (
34
),
\mathcal{N}^{(1,0)}=4y\bar{m}_{1}\bar{m}_{2}
. Note that the numerator is taken to be a constant and therefore the amplitude only has a dependence on the momentum transfer
q
.

For the linear-in-spin tree amplitude, we have a more complicated structure. We expect the same propagator structure as in the scalar case, however we can have the following monomials in the numerator

\bar{u}_{1\,\mu}\mathit{M}^{\mu\nu}q_{\nu},\hskip 28.45274pt\bar{u}_{2\,\mu}\mathit{M}^{\mu\nu}q_{\nu},\hskip 28.45274pt\bar{u}_{1\,\mu}\mathit{M}^{\mu\nu}\bar{u}_{2\,\nu}.

(41)

If we expect all tree level amplitudes to scale classically i.e.
\mathcal{O}(\lambda^{-2})
and if we require only long-range contributions, then only the first two monomials contribute to our amplitudes. With this in mind, we write our amplitude as

\displaystyle\mathbb{A}^{(1,1)}(q,\bar{u}_{1},\bar{u}_{2})=\frac{iq_{\nu}\mathit{M}^{\mu\nu}\left(c_{1}\bar{u}_{1\,\mu}+c_{2}\bar{u}_{2\,\mu}\right)}{q^{2}}\equiv\frac{iq_{\nu}\mathit{M}^{\mu\nu}\mathcal{N}^{(1,1)}_{\mu}(\bar{u}_{1},\bar{u_{2}})}{q^{2}},

(42)

where we have assumed that the Lorentz generator scales the same way as the spin tensor since they are simply related by Eq. (
7
).

3.2
One Loop

As has been shown in Refs.
Bern:2023ity
;
Forde:2007mi
;
Bern:2021xze
, when calculating the classical limit of the two-body, one loop amplitude using the soft expansion, it is possible to separate contributions into box diagram and triangle diagram contributions

\mathcal{A}^{(2)}=c_{\Delta}I_{\Delta}+c_{\nabla}I_{\nabla}+d_{\text{B}}(I_{\text{B}}+I_{\text{xB}})+\cdots.

(43)

The associated integrals are the following

\displaystyle I_{\text{B}}=\int\hat{d}^{D}l\frac{1}{l^{2}(q-l)^{2}(2\bar{u}_{1}\cdot l+i0)(-2\bar{u}_{2}\cdot l+i0)}\sim\mathcal{O}(\lambda^{-2}),

(44)

\displaystyle I_{\text{xB}}=\int\hat{d}^{D}l\frac{1}{l^{2}(q-l)^{2}(2\bar{u}_{1}\cdot l+i0)(2\bar{u}_{2}\cdot l+i0)}\sim\mathcal{O}(\lambda^{-2}),

(45)

\displaystyle I_{\Delta}=\int\hat{d}^{D}l\frac{1}{l^{2}(q-l)^{2}(2\bar{u}_{1}\cdot l+i0)}\sim\mathcal{O}(\lambda^{-1}),

(46)

\displaystyle I_{\nabla}=\int\hat{d}^{D}l\frac{1}{l^{2}(q-l)^{2}(-2\bar{u}_{2}\cdot l+i0)}\sim\mathcal{O}(\lambda^{-1}),

(47)

where we see two different scalings for the same amplitude. The triangle contributions have the expected one loop classical scaling,
\mathcal{O}(\lambda^{-1})
. On the other hand, the box integral terms scale lower than the expected classical scaling; this is known as super-classical or classically singular scaling
Kosower:2018adc
;
Herrmann:2021tct
;
Bern:2021xze
.

\mathcal{O}(\lambda^{-2})

We now consider
\text{Im}\,\mathbb{A}^{(2,1)}
explicitly

\displaystyle 2\,\text{Im}\,

\displaystyle\mathbb{A}^{(2,1)}(q,p_{1},p_{2})

\displaystyle=\int\cancel{D}l\left(\mathbb{A}^{(1,1)}(q-l,p_{1}+l,p_{1}-l)\mathbb{A}^{(1,0)}(l)+\mathbb{A}^{(1,0)}(q-l)\mathbb{A}^{(1,1)}(l,p_{1},p_{2})\right.

\displaystyle\left.\hskip 48.36958pt\hbox{}+\frac{1}{2}\left[\mathbb{A}^{(1,1)}(q-l,p_{1}+l,p_{2}-l),\mathbb{A}^{(1,1)}(l,p_{1},p_{2})\right]\right).

(50)

We are interested in the amplitude with special kinematic variables, Eq. (
39
); therefore, we have to consider shifts in the external momentum by the small momentum transfer
q
as well as the shifts coming from the amplitudes with propagator
(q-l)^{-2}
. These shifts cause the promotion of certain terms from classically singular to classical contributions. It should be noted that the last line in Eq. (
3.2
) already scales classically and therefore is not promoted. The promotions come from the following transformations

\displaystyle\mathbb{A}^{(1,1)}(l,p_{1},p_{2})\rightarrow\left(1-\left(\frac{q-l}{2}\right)\cdot\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial\bar{p}_{1}}-\frac{\partial}{\partial\bar{p}_{2}}\right)\right)\mathbb{A}^{(1,1)}(l,\bar{p}_{1},\bar{p}_{2}),

(51)

\displaystyle\mathbb{A}^{(1,1)}(q-l,p_{1}+l,p_{2}-l)\rightarrow\left(1+\frac{l}{2}\cdot\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial\bar{p}_{1}}-\frac{\partial}{\partial\bar{p}_{2}}\right)\right)\mathbb{A}^{(1,1)}(q-l,\bar{p}_{1},\bar{p}_{2}).

(52)

We also exploit a feature of the combination of amplitudes we are working with,

\displaystyle\mathbb{A}^{(1,0)}(q-l)\left(\frac{q-l}{2}\right)\cdot\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial\bar{p}_{1}}-\frac{\partial}{\partial\bar{p}_{2}}\right)\mathbb{A}^{(1,1)}(l,\bar{p}_{1},\bar{p}_{2})=

\displaystyle\hbox{}-\mathbb{A}^{(1,0)}(l)\frac{l}{2}\cdot\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial\bar{p}_{1}}-\frac{\partial}{\partial\bar{p}_{2}}\right)\mathbb{A}^{(1,1)}(q-l,\bar{p}_{1},\bar{p}_{2}),

(53)

to rewrite Eq. (
3.2
) in terms of the special kinematics variables,

\displaystyle 2\,\text{Im}\,

\displaystyle\mathbb{A}^{(2,1)}(q,p_{1},p_{2})=\int\cancel{D}l\left(\mathbb{A}^{(1,1)}(q-l,\bar{p}_{1},\bar{p}_{2})\mathbb{A}^{(1,0)}(l)+\mathbb{A}^{(1,0)}(q-l)\mathbb{A}^{(1,1)}(l,\bar{p}_{1},\bar{p}_{2})\right.

\displaystyle\left.\hskip 122.34692pt\hbox{}+\frac{1}{2}\left[\mathbb{A}^{(1,1)}(q-l,\bar{p}_{1},\bar{p}_{2}),\mathbb{A}^{(1,1)}(l,\bar{p}_{1},\bar{p}_{2})\right]\right.

\displaystyle\left.\hskip 122.34692pt\hbox{}+l\cdot\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial\bar{p}_{1}}-\frac{\partial}{\partial\bar{p}_{2}}\right)\mathbb{A}^{(1,1)}(q-l,\bar{p}_{1},\bar{p}_{2})\mathbb{A}^{(1,0)}(l)\right).

(54)

Now only the top line of Eq. (
54
) is classically singular while the rest of the terms scale classically; later on, these same shifts and transformations will occur in the real kernels of the momentum impulse and the spin kick.

4
The Eikonal Formula

Before we begin deriving the eikonal formula, we first briefly review the eikonal phase. A detailed discussion on the eikonal phase can be found in Refs.
DiVecchia:2021bdo
;
DiVecchia:2022piu
;
DiVecchia:2023frv
. In impact parameter space, one can write the following exponentiation of the two-body scattering amplitude

1+i\,\mathcal{A}(b)=\left(1+i\Delta(b)\right)e^{i\delta(b)},

(55)

where
\delta(b)
is the eikonal phase,
\Delta(b)
is a quantum remainder, and

\mathcal{A}(b)=\int\cancel{D}q\,\mathcal{A}(q)\,e^{-ib\cdot q}\equiv\text{FT}[\mathcal{A}(q)],

(56)

where in a center of mass momentum configuration, with total energy
E
and center of mass momentum
\vec{p}
,

\delta(2\bar{p}_{1}\cdot q)\delta(2\bar{p}_{2}\cdot q)\rightarrow\frac{1}{4|\vec{p}|E}\delta(q^{0})\delta(q^{i})=\frac{1}{4\bar{m}_{1}\bar{m}_{2}\sqrt{y^{2}-1}}\delta(q^{0})\delta(q^{i}),

(57)

where
i=1,2,3
. We are suppressing the amplitude’s dependence on other parameters such as the momentum or masses, for the sake of clarity. By expanding Eq. (
55
) in powers of the coupling constant
g
and in powers of classical scaling parameter
\lambda
, one can relate the classical scaling amplitude order by order to the eikonal phase. To next to leading order in the coupling constant,

\delta^{(1)}(b)=\text{FT}[\mathcal{A}^{(1)}(q)],\hskip 28.45274pt\delta^{(2)}(b)=\text{FT}[\text{Re}\,\mathcal{A}^{(2)}].

(58)

The relationship between the eikonal phase and classical amplitudes becomes more complicated at higher orders in the coupling constant; however, this is beyond the scope of this paper. We assume that Eq. (
58
) holds to all orders in the spin expansion.

In Ref.
Bern:2020buy
, based on the structure of their results for the momentum impulse and spin kick to one loop order, the authors introduced an ansatz relating the eikonal phase to the observables in the center of mass frame, which can be expressed as

\displaystyle\Delta\mathbf{p}_{1}=\frac{\partial\delta}{\partial\mathbf{b}}+\frac{1}{2}\left\{\delta,\frac{\partial\delta}{\partial\mathbf{b}}\right\}+\mathcal{D}_{L}\left(\delta,\frac{\partial\delta}{\partial\mathbf{b}}\right)-\frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial\mathbf{b}}\mathcal{D}_{L}(\delta,\delta)-\frac{\mathbf{p}}{2\mathbf{p}^{2}}\left(\frac{\partial\delta}{\partial\mathbf{b}}\right)^{2}+\mathcal{O}(\delta^{3}),

(59)

\displaystyle\Delta\mathbf{S}_{1}=\left\{\delta,\mathbf{S}_{1}\right\}+\frac{1}{2}\left\{\delta,\left\{\delta,\mathbf{S}_{1}\right\}\right\}+\mathcal{D}_{L}(\delta,\left\{\delta,\mathbf{S}_{1}\right\})-\frac{1}{2}\left\{\mathcal{D}_{L}(\delta,\delta),\mathbf{S}_{1}\right\}+\mathcal{O}(\delta^{3}),

(60)

where the bold variables are three-vectors,
\mathbf{p}
is the center of mass momentum,
\mathbf{b}
is the canonical impact parameter, and
\mathbf{S}
is the rest frame spin three-vector. The spin three-vector is defined as

S^{i}=\frac{1}{2}\epsilon^{ijk}S_{jk},

(61)

where
i,j,k=1,2,3
and we use
\epsilon^{123}
= 1 as our convention for the Levi-Civita tensor.
\mathcal{D}_{L}
is defined as

\mathcal{D}_{L}(f,g)\equiv-\epsilon_{ijk}S^{i}_{\,1}\frac{\partial f}{\partial S_{1\,j}}\frac{\partial g}{\partial L_{k}},

(62)

where we are ignoring the explicit inclusion of boost degrees of freedom since we are not making this distinction.
The brackets are defined by the
SO(3)
algebra

\left\{S^{i},S^{j}\right\}=\epsilon^{ijk}S_{k},

(63)

and
L_{k}
is the orbital angular momentum
L^{i}=\epsilon^{ijk}\,b_{j}\,p_{k}
.

As of the writing of this paper, Eqs. (
59
) and (
60
) have been verified to quadratic order in spin, next to leading order in the coupling constant for QED and GR
Bern:2023ity
;
Kosmopoulos:2021zoq
; however, there is no derivation. While we will be deriving an eikonal formula, we will not be deriving Eqs. (
59
) and (
60
) exactly because we will not be fixing our system to the center of mass frame and we will not explicitly break covariance. Nonetheless, we will derive an eikonal formula similar to Eqs. (
59
) and (
60
) that are written in terms of the spin tensor as opposed to the spin vector and in a covariant form.

4.1
Eikonal Formula Derivation

We now derive an eikonal formula for the momentum impulse and the spin kick. We start with the comparatively simpler, linear-in-spin momentum impulse starting at tree level and then move to the one loop correction. We then do the same for the linear-in-spin spin kick. We compare all calculations to the results in
Bern:2023ity
by applying the two-body scattering amplitudes associated with Eq. (
34
). We expand the general spin QED impulses in the following manner

\Delta\mathcal{O}=\alpha q_{1}q_{2}(\Delta^{(1,0)}\mathcal{O}+\Delta^{(1,1)}\mathcal{O}+\cdots)+(\alpha q_{1}q_{2})^{2}(\Delta^{(2,0)}\mathcal{O}+\Delta^{(2,1)}\mathcal{O}+\cdots)+\cdots

(64)

where the superscript count in the same way as in the amplitudes in Eq. (
2.3
).

4.2
Momentum Impulse

4.2.1
Tree Level

At tree level, the momentum impulse only receives contributions from the virtual kernel. Also, because the virtual kernel already scales classically, the momentum shift to special kinematics does not result in new promoted contributions. Therefore, we have the following result to all orders in spin

\displaystyle\Delta^{(1)}p^{\mu}_{1}

\displaystyle=\int\cancel{D}q\,e^{-ib_{\text{cov}}\cdot q}\,iq^{\mu}\,\mathcal{A}^{(1)}(q,\bar{p}_{1},\bar{p}_{2},S_{1})

\displaystyle=-\frac{\partial}{\partial b_{\mu}}\int\cancel{D}qe^{-ib_{\text{cov}}\cdot q}\,\mathcal{A}^{(1)}(q,\bar{p}_{1},\bar{p}_{2},S_{1}),

(65)

where the singular superscript counts the powers in the coupling constant. We can now identify the Fourier transform with respect to the new impact parameter
b_{\text{cov}}
as the “covariant” eikonal phase

\Delta^{(1)}p^{\mu}_{1}=-\frac{\partial}{\partial b_{\mu}}\delta^{(1)}_{\text{cov}},

(66)

where
\delta(b_{\text{cov}},\bar{u}_{1},\bar{u}_{2},S_{1})\equiv\delta_{\text{cov}}
.

Fixing to the linear-in-spin momentum impulse and using the linear-in-spin tree level amplitude for general spin QED,

\mathbb{A}^{(1,1)}(q,\bar{u}_{1},\bar{u}_{2})=\frac{iq_{\beta}\mathit{M}^{\alpha\beta}(yD_{1}\bar{u}_{1\,\alpha}-C_{1}\bar{u}_{2\,\alpha})}{q^{2}},

(67)

we compute the result

\Delta^{(1,1)}p^{\mu}_{1}=\frac{2}{\bar{m}_{1}\sqrt{y^{2}-1}}\frac{S_{1}^{\nu\rho}}{|b_{\text{cov}}|^{2}}\left(\Pi^{\mu}_{\,\,\nu}+\frac{2\,b^{\mu}_{\text{cov}}\,b_{\text{cov}\,\nu}}{|b_{\text{cov}}|^{2}}\right)\left(yD_{1}\bar{u}_{1\,\rho}-C_{1}\bar{u}_{2\,\rho}\right),

(68)

which matches the result in Ref.
Bern:2023ity
. Here
|b_{\text{cov}}|^{2}=-b_{\text{cov}}^{2}
and
\Pi^{\mu}_{\,\,\nu}
is the projective derivative of the impact parameter

\Pi^{\mu}_{\,\,\nu}=\delta^{\mu}_{\,\,\nu}+\frac{1}{y^{2}-1}\left(\bar{u}^{\mu}_{1}\check{\bar{u}}_{1\,\nu}+\bar{u}^{\mu}_{2}\check{\bar{u}}_{2\,\nu}\right),

(69)

where
\check{\bar{u}}_{1,2}\equiv\bar{u}_{1,2}-y\,\bar{u}_{2,1}
. To satisfy the orthogonality constraints of
b^{\mu}
with respect to the scattering plane, i.e.
\bar{u}_{i}\cdot b=0
, we require the projector
\Pi^{\mu}_{\,\,\nu}
in place of the naive derivative, which does not take this into account.

4.2.2
One Loop

At one loop, we must include the real kernel in order to ensure that all classically singular terms cancel. In the same way as
\text{Im}\,\mathbb{A}^{(2,1)}
, we need to change to special kinematic variables in Eq. (
29
) and keep track of terms promoted to classical scaling. This results in

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}^{(2,1)\,\mu}_{r}=

\displaystyle\int\cancel{D}l\,\,l^{\mu}\,\epsilon^{*}(p_{1}+q)\cdot\left\{\mathbb{A}^{(1,0)}(q-l)\mathbb{A}^{(1,1)}(l,\bar{p}_{1},\bar{p}_{2})+\mathbb{A}^{(1,1)}(q-l,\bar{p}_{1},\bar{p}_{2})\mathbb{A}^{(1,0)}(l)\right.

\displaystyle\left.\hskip 93.89418pt\hbox{}+\mathbb{A}^{(1,0)}(l)\,l\cdot\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial\bar{p}_{1}}-\frac{\partial}{\partial\bar{p}_{2}}\right)\mathbb{A}^{(1,1)}(q-l,\bar{p}_{1},\bar{p}_{2})\right.

\displaystyle\left.\hskip 93.89418pt\hbox{}+\frac{1}{2}\left[\mathbb{A}^{(1,1)}(q-l,\bar{p}_{1},\bar{p}_{2}),\mathbb{A}^{(1,1)}(l,\bar{p}_{1},\bar{p}_{2})\right]\right\}\cdot\epsilon(p_{1}),

(70)

which is near identical to Eq. (
54
) except for the explicit factor of
l^{\mu}
. In fact, when combining the virtual kernel,
\mathcal{I}^{(2,1)\,\mu}_{v}
, with Eq. (
4.2.2
) and focusing on the classically singular pieces, we observe the following

\displaystyle\int\cancel{D}q\,e^{-iq\cdot b_{\text{cov}}}\int\cancel{D}l\left(\frac{2l^{\mu}-q^{\mu}}{2}\right)

\displaystyle\left\{\mathcal{A}^{(1,0)}(q-l)\mathcal{A}^{(1,1)}(l,\bar{p}_{1},\bar{p}_{2},S_{1})\right.

\displaystyle\left.\hskip 14.22636pt\hbox{}+\mathcal{A}^{(1,1)}(q-l,\bar{p}_{1},\bar{p}_{2},S_{1})\mathcal{A}^{(1,0)}(l)\right\}.

(71)

The cancellation of the classically singular pieces must occur upon integration; to make this manifest at the integrand level we make the following decomposition of factors of loop momentum vector

l^{\mu}\rightarrow\frac{q^{\mu}}{2}-\frac{\left(l\cdot\bar{u}_{1}\,\check{\bar{u}}^{\mu}_{1}+l\cdot\bar{u}_{2}\,\check{\bar{u}}^{\mu}_{2}\right)}{y^{2}-1},

(72)

and apply the
\delta
-functions in the integrand,
\hat{\delta}(2\bar{p}_{1}\cdot l-q\cdot l)\hat{\delta}(2\bar{p}_{2}\cdot l+q\cdot l)
. Because we are only interested in long range interactions, we can make the replacement
q\cdot l\rightarrow q^{2}/2
; this results in the promotion of the remaining term in Eq. (
4.2.2
) to scale classically. We now have a uniformly classical scaling expression for the momentum impulse

\displaystyle\Delta^{(2,1)}p^{\mu}_{1}=

\displaystyle\int\cancel{D}q\,e^{-iq\cdot b_{\text{cov}}}\,iq^{\mu}\,\text{Re}\,\mathcal{A}^{(2,1)}(q,\bar{u}_{1},\bar{u}_{2},S_{1})

\displaystyle\hbox{}+

\displaystyle\int\cancel{D}qe^{-iq\cdot b_{\text{cov}}}\int\cancel{D}l\left\{\left(\frac{2l^{\mu}-q^{\mu}}{4}\right)\left[\mathcal{A}^{(1,1)}(q-l,\bar{u}_{1},\bar{u}_{2},S_{1}),\mathcal{A}^{(1,1)}(l,\bar{u}_{1},\bar{u}_{2},S_{1})\right]\right.

\displaystyle\left.\hskip 34.14322pt\hbox{}+\mathcal{A}^{(1,0)}(l)\left(\frac{2l^{\mu}-q^{\mu}}{2}\right)l^{\alpha}\left(\frac{1}{\bar{m}_{1}}\frac{\partial}{\partial\bar{u}_{1}^{\alpha}}-\frac{1}{\bar{m}_{2}}\frac{\partial}{\partial\bar{u}_{2}^{\alpha}}\right)\mathcal{A}^{(1,1)}(q-l,\bar{u}_{1},\bar{u}_{2},S_{1})\right.

\displaystyle\left.\hskip 34.14322pt\hbox{}+\frac{-q^{2}}{4(y^{2}-1)}\left(\frac{\check{\bar{u}}^{\mu}_{1}}{\bar{m}_{1}}-\frac{\check{\bar{u}}^{\mu}_{2}}{\bar{m}_{2}}\right)\left(\mathcal{A}^{(1,0)}(q-l)\mathcal{A}^{(1,1)}(l,\bar{u}_{1},\bar{u}_{2},S_{1})\right.\right.

\displaystyle\left.\left.\hskip 165.02606pt\hbox{}+\mathcal{A}^{(1,1)}(q-l,\bar{u}_{1},\bar{u}_{2},S_{1})\mathcal{A}^{(1,0)}(l)\right)\right\}.

(73)

To be able to re-express the amplitudes that depend on the loop momentum in terms of their respective eikonal phases, we make the shift
q\rightarrow q+l
and substitute explicit factors of
q,l\rightarrow i\frac{\partial}{\partial b}
,

\displaystyle\Delta^{(2,1)}p^{\mu}_{1}=

\displaystyle-\frac{\partial\,\delta_{\text{cov}}^{(2,1)}}{\partial b_{\mu}}+\frac{i}{2}\left[\delta_{\text{cov}}^{(1,1)},\frac{\partial\delta_{\text{cov}}^{(1,1)}}{\partial b_{\mu}}\right]+\frac{1}{(y^{2}-1)}\left(\frac{\check{\bar{u}}^{\mu}_{1}}{\bar{m}_{1}}-\frac{\check{\bar{u}}^{\mu}_{2}}{\bar{m}_{2}}\right)\frac{\partial\delta_{\text{cov}}^{(1,0)}}{\partial b}\cdot\frac{\partial\delta_{\text{cov}}^{(1,1)}}{\partial b}

\displaystyle\hbox{}+\frac{1}{2}\left[\frac{\partial}{\partial b_{\mu}},\left(\frac{1}{\bar{m}_{1}}\frac{\partial}{\partial\bar{u}_{1}^{\alpha}}-\frac{1}{\bar{m}_{2}}\frac{\partial}{\partial\bar{u}_{2}^{\alpha}}\right)\delta_{\text{cov}}^{(1,1)}\right]\frac{\partial\delta_{\text{cov}}^{(1,0)}}{\partial b_{\alpha}}.

(74)

To verify Eq. (
4.2.2
), we plug in the appropriate tree level amplitudes and linear-in-spin, one loop amplitude,

\displaystyle\text{Re}\mathbb{A}^{(2,1)}=

\displaystyle\mathbb{A}_{\triangle+\triangledown}(q,\bar{u}_{1},\bar{u}_{2})=\frac{1}{4\sqrt{-q^{2}}}iq_{\nu}\mathbb{M}^{\mu\nu}\left(\beta_{(1)}\bar{u}_{1\,\mu}+\beta_{(2)}\bar{u}_{2\,\mu}\right),

(75)

\displaystyle\beta_{(1)}=

\displaystyle\frac{-1}{(y^{2}-1)\bar{m}_{1}}\left(\left[(y^{2}+1)C_{1}+(y^{2}-1)D_{1}\right]\bar{m}_{1}\right.

\displaystyle\left.\hskip 56.9055pt\hbox{}+\left[C_{1}^{2}-(y^{2}+1)C_{1}D_{1}+y^{2}D_{1}^{2}+(3y^{2}-1)D_{1}\right]\bar{m}_{2}\right),

(76)

\displaystyle\beta_{(2)}=

\displaystyle\frac{y}{(y^{2}-1)\bar{m}_{1}}\left(2C_{1}\bar{m}_{1}+\left[C_{1}^{2}-2C_{1}D_{1}+D_{1}^{2}+2D_{1}\right]\bar{m}_{2}\right),

(77)

and compute the result

\displaystyle\Delta^{(2,1)}p^{\mu}_{1}=

\displaystyle\,\frac{\pi{}\left(3\,b^{\mu}_{\text{cov}}\,b_{\text{cov}\,\nu}+|b_{\text{cov}}|^{2}\,\Pi^{\mu}_{\,\,\nu}\right)S^{\nu\rho}_{1}}{2|b_{\text{cov}}|^{5}(y^{2}-1)^{3/2}\,\bar{m}_{1}^{2}\,\bar{m}_{2}}\left\{\check{\bar{u}}_{1\,\rho}[\bar{m}_{1}(D_{1}-C_{1})-\bar{m}_{2}(C_{1}^{2}-C_{1}D_{1}-D_{1})]\right.

\displaystyle\left.\hskip 153.6447pt\hbox{}+y{}\check{\bar{u}}_{2\,\rho}[\bar{m}_{1}(C_{1}+D_{1})-\bar{m}_{2}D_{1}(C_{1}-D_{1}-3)]\right\}

\displaystyle\hbox{}-\frac{2b^{\nu}_{\text{cov}}S_{1\,\nu\rho}\Pi^{\mu}_{\,\,\rho}}{|b_{\text{cov}}|^{4}(y^{2}-1)\,\bar{m}_{1}^{2}\,\bar{m}_{2}}\left\{2C_{1}y\bar{m}_{1}+\bar{m}_{2}(C_{1}^{2}-y^{2}D_{1}(2C_{1}-D_{1}-2))\right\}

\displaystyle\hbox{}+\frac{4y{}(\bar{m}_{2}\check{\bar{u}}_{1}^{\mu}-\bar{m}_{1}\check{\bar{u}}_{2}^{\mu})b^{\nu}_{\text{cov}}S_{1\,\nu\rho}}{|b_{\text{cov}}|^{4}(y^{2}-1)^{3}\,\bar{m}_{1}^{2}\,\bar{m}_{2}}\left\{\check{\bar{u}}_{1}^{\rho}(D_{1}-C_{1})+\check{\bar{u}}_{2}^{\rho}(y^{2}D_{1}-C_{1})\right\},

(78)

where we match the result in Ref.
Bern:2023ity
, taking into account our impact parameters point in opposite directions.

We notice a similarity between Eq. (
4.2.2
) and Eq. (

4.3
Spin Kick

4.3.1
Tree Level

Same as in the momentum impulse case, for the tree level spin kick we only need the contribution from the virtual kernel. We also do not have to worry about any promotions coming from the shift when changing to special kinematics variables because the tree level amplitude already scales classically

\Delta^{(1)}S^{\mu\nu}_{1}=\frac{i}{2}\int\cancel{D}q\,e^{-ib_{\text{cov}}\cdot q}\left[\mathcal{A}^{(1)}(q,\bar{u}_{1},\bar{u}_{2},S_{1}),S^{\mu\nu}_{1}\right].

(79)

Re-expressing Eq. (
79
) in terms of the eikonal phase and covariant coordinates we get

\Delta^{(1)}S^{\mu\nu}_{1}=\frac{i}{2}\left[\delta^{(1)}_{\text{cov}},S^{\mu\nu}_{1}\right].

(80)

We check Eq. (
80
) by fixing ourselves to linear order in spin and plugging in Eq. (
67
) for the amplitude

\Delta^{(1,1)}S^{\mu\nu}_{1}=\frac{4}{\bar{m}_{1}\sqrt{y^{2}-1}}\frac{\left(yD_{1}b_{\text{cov}\,[\sigma}\bar{u}_{1\,\rho]}-C_{1}b_{\text{cov}\,[\sigma}\bar{u}_{2\,\rho]}\right)\eta^{\rho[\mu}S^{\nu]\sigma}_{1}}{|b_{\text{cov}}|^{2}},

(81)

where the brackets around the indices signals their anti-symmetrization,

a_{[\sigma}c_{\rho]}\equiv\frac{1}{2}\left(a_{\sigma}c_{\rho}-a_{\rho}c_{\sigma}\right),

(82)

for some four-vectors
a,c
, and
\eta^{\rho\mu}
is the Minkowski metric. Eq. (
81
) matches the result in Ref.
Bern:2023ity
taking into account our impact parameters point in opposite directions and an overall factor of 1/2, which comes from our definition of the spin operator expectation value.

4.3.2
One Loop

Same as in the momentum impulse case, for the one loop calculation we need to include the real kernel contribution. After shifting to special kinematics and making explicit the classical scaling, we have the following real kernel contribution to the spin kick

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}^{(2,1)\,\mu\nu}_{r}=

\displaystyle\frac{e^{iq\cdot\omega}}{2}\int\cancel{D}l\,\left\{\mathcal{A}^{(1,0)}(q-l)\left[\mathcal{A}^{(1,1)}(l,\bar{u}_{1},\bar{u}_{2},S_{1}),S_{1}^{\mu\nu}\right]\right.

\displaystyle\left.\hskip 56.9055pt\hbox{}+\mathcal{A}^{(1,0)}(l)\frac{l^{\alpha}}{2}\left(\frac{1}{\bar{m}_{1}}\frac{\partial}{\partial\bar{u}^{\alpha}_{1}}-\frac{1}{\bar{m}_{2}}\frac{\partial}{\partial\bar{u}^{\alpha}_{2}}\right)\left[\mathcal{A}^{(1,1)}(q-l,\bar{u}_{1},\bar{u}_{2},S_{1}),S_{1}^{\mu\nu}\right]\right.

\displaystyle\left.\hskip 56.9055pt\hbox{}+\frac{1}{2}\left[\mathcal{A}^{(1,1)}(q-l,\bar{u}_{1},\bar{u}_{2},S_{1}),\left[\mathcal{A}^{(1,1)}(l,\bar{u}_{1},\bar{u}_{2},S_{1}),S_{1}^{\mu\nu}\right]\right]\right\},

(83)

where only the top line of Eq. (
4.3.2
) has classically singular scaling. The cancellation of the classically singular terms in the real kernel and
\text{Im}\,\mathbb{A}^{(2,1)}
is more subtle in the spin kick case; namely, when attempting to cancel the classically singular terms, we end up with the following

\frac{e^{iq\cdot\omega}}{4}\int\cancel{D}l\,\left[\mathcal{A}^{(1,0)}(q-l)\mathcal{A}^{(1,1)}(l,\bar{u}_{1},\bar{u}_{2},S_{1})-\mathcal{A}^{(1,0)}(l)\mathcal{A}^{(1,1)}(q-l,\bar{u}_{1},\bar{u}_{2},S_{1}),S_{1}^{\mu\nu}\right],

(84)

which does not obviously cancel. However, if we schematically plug in for the amplitudes using Eq. (
40
) and Eq. (
42
), the necessary steps become clearer

\frac{e^{iq\cdot\omega}}{4}\int\cancel{D}l\,\frac{i\mathcal{N}^{(1,0)}\mathcal{N}^{(1,1)}_{\alpha}(\bar{u}_{1},\bar{u}_{2})}{l^{2}(q-l)^{2}}(2l_{\beta}-q_{\beta})\left[S_{1}^{\alpha\beta},S_{1}^{\mu\nu}\right].

(85)

Because the cancellation of classically singular terms occurs upon integration, we can make the same replacement for the loop momentum as in Eq. (
72
) and apply the
\delta
-functions in
\cancel{D}l
. Doing so gives us the uniformly classical spin kick

\displaystyle\Delta^{(2,1)}S^{\mu\nu}_{1}=\,

\displaystyle\frac{i}{2}\int\cancel{D}q\,e^{-iq\cdot b_{\text{cov}}}\left\{\left[\text{Re}\mathcal{A}^{(2,1)}(q,\bar{u}_{1},\bar{u}_{2},S_{1}),S^{\mu\nu}_{1}\right]\right.

\displaystyle\left.\hbox{}+\int\cancel{D}l\left\{\frac{(-q^{2})}{4(y^{2}-1)}\frac{\mathcal{A}^{(1,0)}(l)\mathcal{N}^{(1,1)}_{\alpha}(\bar{u}_{1},\bar{u}_{2})}{(q-l)^{2}}\left(\frac{\check{\bar{u}}_{1\,\beta}}{\bar{m}_{1}}-\frac{\check{\bar{u}}_{2\,\beta}}{\bar{m}_{2}}\right)\left[S^{\alpha\beta}_{1},S^{\mu\nu}_{1}\right]\right.\right.

\displaystyle\left.\left.\hskip 39.83368pt\hbox{}-\frac{i}{2}\left[\mathcal{A}^{(1,1)}(q-l,\bar{u}_{1},\bar{u}_{2},S_{1}),\left[\mathcal{A}^{(1,1)}(l,\bar{u}_{1},\bar{u}_{2},S_{1}),S^{\mu\nu}_{1}\right]\right]\right.\right.

\displaystyle\left.\left.\hskip 39.83368pt\hbox{}+\frac{i}{4}\left[\left[\mathcal{A}^{(1,1)}(q-l,\bar{u}_{1},\bar{u}_{2},S_{1}),\mathcal{A}^{(1,1)}(l,\bar{u}_{1},\bar{u}_{2},S_{1})\right],S^{\mu\nu}_{1}\right]\right\}\right\}.

(86)

Note that the last line in Eq. (
4.3.2
) evaluates to zero due to the horizontal flip symmetry of the cut, i.e.
l\rightarrow q-l
. Once again, we systematically replace our amplitudes with eikonal phases by shifting
q\rightarrow q+l
and replacing explicit factors of
q,l
with
i\frac{\partial}{\partial b}
,

\displaystyle\Delta^{(2,1)}S^{\mu\nu}_{1}=

\displaystyle\frac{i}{2}\left[\delta_{\text{cov}}^{(2,1)},S^{\mu\nu}_{1}\right]+\frac{1}{4}\left[\delta_{\text{cov}}^{(1,1)},\left[\delta_{\text{cov}}^{(1,1)},S^{\mu\nu}_{1}\right]\right]

\displaystyle\hbox{}+\frac{i\mathcal{N}^{(1,1)}_{\alpha}(\bar{u}_{1},\bar{u}_{2})}{32\pi\bar{m}_{1}\bar{m}_{2}(y^{2}-1)^{3/2}}\left(\frac{\check{\bar{u}}_{1\,\beta}}{\bar{m}_{1}}-\frac{\check{\bar{u}}_{2\,\beta}}{\bar{m}_{2}}\right)\left[S^{\alpha\beta}_{1},S^{\mu\nu}_{1}\right]\frac{\partial\delta_{\text{cov}}^{(1,0)}}{\partial b^{\gamma}}\frac{b^{\gamma}_{\text{cov}}}{|b_{\text{cov}}|^{2}}.

(87)

We verify Eq. (
4.3.2

\displaystyle\Delta^{(2,1)}S^{\mu\nu}_{1}=\frac{\pi S^{\rho[\nu}_{1}\eta^{\mu]\sigma}}{|b_{\text{cov}}|^{3}(y^{2}-1)^{3/2}\,\bar{m}_{1}^{2}\,\bar{m}_{2}}\left[b_{\text{cov}\,[\sigma}\check{\bar{u}}_{1\,\rho]}\left(\bar{m}_{1}(D_{1}-C_{1})-\bar{m}_{2}(C_{1}^{2}-D_{1}-C_{1}D_{1})\right)\right.

\displaystyle\left.\hskip 173.56198pt\hbox{}+b_{\text{cov}\,[\sigma}\check{\bar{u}}_{2\,\rho]}y\left(\bar{m}_{1}(D_{1}+C_{1})-\bar{m}_{2}D_{1}(C_{1}-D_{1}-3)\right)\right]

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt\hbox{}-\frac{2}{|b_{\text{cov}}|^{4}(y^{2}-1)\,\bar{m}_{1}^{2}}\left[2b_{\text{cov}\,\rho}S_{1}^{\rho\sigma}\left(C_{1}\bar{u}_{2\,\sigma}-D_{1}y\bar{u}_{1\,\sigma}\right)\left(C_{1}b_{\text{cov}}^{[\mu}\bar{u}_{2}^{\nu]}\hbox{}-yD_{1}b_{\text{cov}}^{[\mu}\bar{u}_{1}^{\nu]}\right)\right.

\displaystyle\left.\hskip 159.3356pt\hbox{}-b_{\text{cov}\,\rho}S_{1}^{\rho[\nu}b_{\text{cov}}^{\mu]}(C_{1}^{2}-D_{1}(2C_{1}-D_{1})y^{2})\right]

\displaystyle\left.\hskip 99.58464pt\hbox{}+S_{1}^{\rho[\nu}\check{\bar{u}}^{\mu]}_{1}\check{\bar{u}}_{2\,\rho}\left(\bar{m}_{1}(C_{1}-D_{1})y^{2}+y\bar{m}_{2}(C_{1}-y^{2}D_{1})(C_{1}-D_{1}+1)\right)\right.

\displaystyle\left.\hskip 99.58464pt\hbox{}-S_{1}^{\rho[\nu}\check{\bar{u}}^{\mu]}_{2}\check{\bar{u}}_{1\,\rho}(\bar{m}_{1}(C_{1}-D_{1})y^{2}-y\bar{m}_{2}(C_{1}-y^{2}D_{1})(C_{1}-D_{1}-1))\right],

which matches the results in Ref.

) using the result in Ref.
Bern:2023ity

Bern:2023ity
.

5
Conclusion and Discussion

We derived covariant eikonal formulae relating the eikonal phase to the momentum impulse and spin kick to linear order in spin and next to leading order in the coupling constant. To do so we used the KMOC formalism
Kosower:2018adc
, which directly gives physical observables in terms of scattering amplitudes in impact parameter space, which is connected to the eikonal phase. This is the first next-to-leading order spin dependent calculation using the KMOC formalism and general spin QFT.
To simplify the evaluation, we used the non-transverse property of unconstrained massive general spin fields
Bern:2023ity
to construct the completeness relation Eq. (
6
) for the polarization tensors. This non-transverse property also allows us to construct SSC-violating spin tensors, as in the worldline and field theory calculations of
Bern:2023ity
. The choice of special kinematics
Herrmann:2021tct
;
Parra-Martinez:2020dzs
simplified the cancellation of classically singular terms in the integrand and facilitated the appearance of terms proportional to external momentum derivatives. We verified our results by comparing to the results in Ref.
Bern:2023ity
, which were based on worldline and field theory matched to a general spin EFT. Based on this match, we also conclude that our eikonal formulae, Eqs. (
4.2.2
) and (
4.3.2
), capture the effects of the
\mathcal{D}_{L}(f,g)
terms in Eqs. (
59
) and (
60
).

There are obvious future directions, including extending this work to higher order in spin and coupling constant. The eikonal formulae, based on rest-frame spin introduced in Ref.
Bern:2020buy
, have been explicitly verified to quadratic order in spin
Kosmopoulos:2021zoq
and next to leading order in the coupling. Though our covariant construction is different, extending this work to quadratic-in-spin order (and beyond) will provide insight to the relationship between the eikonal phase and spinning scattering observables. It would, of course, be very interesting to extend the derivation of the eikonal formulae to higher orders in the coupling as well.

A more thorough investigation of the relation between the eikonal formulae for the impulse and spin kick presented here and those in Refs.
Bern:2023ity
would be very interesting. It would also be interesting to use the procedure presented here to incorporate spin in the calculation of other observables such as the direct waveform calculations
Aoude:2023dui
;
Brandhuber:2023hhl
;
DeAngelis:2023lvf
;
Herderschee:2023fxh
;
Adamo:2022qci
, absorption effects
Jones:2023ugm
, or even to observables in the three-body case
Jones:2022aji
.

Appendix A
The Spin Operator

We consider the operator promotion of angular momentum, as done in Appendix B of Ref.
Maybee:2019jus

\mathbb{J}^{\mu\nu}=\int d^{3}x\,M^{0\mu\nu}=\int d^{3}x\left(x^{\mu}T^{0\nu}-x^{\nu}T^{0\mu}\right),

(89)

where
M^{\alpha\mu\nu}
is the conserved Noether current associated with Lorentz symmetry and
T^{\mu\nu}
is the Belinfante tensor

T^{\mu\nu}=\Theta^{\mu\nu}-\frac{i}{2}\partial_{\rho}\left(\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}_{0}}{\partial(\partial_{\rho}\Phi_{s})}\cdot\mathit{M}^{\mu\nu}\cdot\Phi_{s}-\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}_{0}}{\partial(\partial_{\mu}\Phi_{s})}\cdot\mathit{M}^{\rho\nu}\cdot\Phi_{s}-\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}_{0}}{\partial(\partial_{\nu}\Phi_{s})}\cdot\mathit{M}^{\rho\mu}\cdot\Phi_{s}\right),

(90)

where
\Theta^{\mu\nu}
is the stress-energy tensor,
\partial_{\rho}=\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{\rho}}
,
(\mathit{M}^{\mu\nu})^{\alpha(s)}_{\,\,\,\,\,\beta(s)}
is the Lorentz generator, the dots surrounding the Lorentz generator implies summation over the representation indices, and
\Phi_{s}(x)
is the complex free general spin field

\Phi_{s}(x)=\sum_{a}\int d\Phi(k)\left(a_{a}(k)\epsilon_{a}(k)e^{-ik\cdot x}+b^{\dagger}_{a}(k)\epsilon^{*}_{a}(k)e^{ik\cdot x}\right),

(91)

where
a
is the little group index and
a_{a},b^{\dagger}_{a}
are the particle and anti-particle annihilation operators, respectively. Assuming a reasonable free kinetic term from some Lagrangian
\mathcal{L}_{0}
, like Eq. (
35
) with
\alpha\rightarrow 0
, we obtain a natural separation of angular momentum in to what we call “orbital” and “spin” parts:

\mathbb{J}^{\mu\nu}=\int d^{3}x\>\left(x^{\mu}\Theta^{0\nu}(x)-x^{\nu}\Theta^{0\mu}(x)+i\Pi_{s}(x)\cdot\mathit{M}^{\mu\nu}\cdot\Phi_{s}(x)\right)=\mathbb{L}^{\mu\nu}+\mathbb{S}^{\mu\nu},

(92)

\Pi_{s}(x)=\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial_{0}\Phi_{s}}
is the conjugate momentum to
\Phi_{s}
. We call the term with the Lorentz generator the spin operator,
\mathbb{S}^{\mu\nu}
. Taking the normal ordered expectation value of the spin operator gives us the following for the free theory,

\langle{p^{\prime};a^{\prime}}|\mathbb{S}^{\mu\nu}|p;a\rangle=\langle{0}|a^{a^{\prime}}(p^{\prime}):\int d^{3}x\>i\>\Pi_{s}\,\mathit{M}^{\mu\nu}\Phi_{s}:a^{\dagger}_{a}(p)|0\rangle=-\frac{1}{2}\hat{\delta}_{\Phi}(p^{\prime}-p)\,\epsilon^{a^{\prime}}(p^{\prime})\cdot\mathit{M}^{\mu\nu}\cdot\epsilon_{a}(p),

(93)

where we use the commutation relation

[a_{i}(p^{\prime}),a^{\dagger}_{j}(p)]=2E_{p}\,\hat{\delta}^{(3)}(\vec{p}\,^{\prime}-\vec{p}\,)\,\delta_{i,j}=\hat{\delta}_{\Phi}(p^{\prime}-p)\,\delta_{i,j},

(94)

and ignore terms proportional to
b^{\dagger}
since we are only interested in positive energy states in the classical limit.

Appendix B
Polarization Tensors and their Products

We know that polarization tensors are not tensors in the usual sense. To boost from one arbitrary frame to another, one has to first perform a standard boost to some arbitrary reference frame followed by a boost to the final frame: Ref.
Weinberg:1995mt
provides a thorough explanation using the rest frame as a reference frame explicitly. Refs.
Guevara:2019fsj
;
Chung:2019duq
provide an alternative expression for the standard boost with some arbitrary reference frame
k
without explicitly breaking covariance.
As in Ref.
Weinberg:1995mt
;
Chung:2019duq
, we write our spin-s polarization tensors as

\epsilon^{\mu(s)}(p)=D(L(p;k))^{\mu(s)}_{\,\,\,\,\,\nu(s)}\epsilon^{\nu(s)}(k),

(95)

where this
D
matrix boosts
\epsilon^{\mu(s)}
from reference frame
k
to arbitrary frame
p
via some standard boost
L(p;k)
and furnishes a representation of the homogeneous Lorentz group. For example, the scalar representation
D=1
and the vector representation
D=\Lambda^{\mu}_{\,\,\,\nu}
, which is what we normally think of as a standard Lorentz transformation.

While Refs.
Weinberg:1995mt
;
Bern:2023ity
;
Chung:2018kqs
have a thorough discussion on how to deal with this feature of polarization tensors, they eventually break covariance explicitly, which is inconvenient for the KMOC formalism. Ref.
Chung:2019duq
provides an alternative expression for these
D
matrices

\displaystyle D(L(p;k))^{\alpha(s)}_{\,\,\,\,\,\beta(s)}

\displaystyle=\text{Exp}[-i\lambda(p;k)p^{\mu}k^{\nu}\mathit{M}_{\mu\nu}]^{\alpha(s)}_{\,\,\,\,\,\beta(s)},

(96)

\displaystyle\lambda(p;k)

\displaystyle=\frac{\text{arcosh}\left(\frac{p\cdot k}{m^{2}}\right)}{\sqrt{(p\cdot k)^{2}-m^{4}}}\equiv\frac{\Theta}{\sqrt{\Gamma}}.

(97)

The use of
\Theta
and
\Gamma
is for later convenience and holds no significance beyond this discussion. We implicitly take
k
to be the rest frame but do not plug in this fact explicitly.

We now consider a small perturbation in the desired frame
p\rightarrow p+q
. We perturbatively expand the following polarization product about small
q

\displaystyle\epsilon^{*}(p+q)\cdot\epsilon(p)=

\displaystyle\,\epsilon^{*}(p+q)|_{q\rightarrow 0}\cdot\epsilon(p)+q^{\alpha}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial p^{\alpha}}\epsilon^{*}(p+q)\right)|_{q\rightarrow 0}\cdot\epsilon(p)

\displaystyle\hbox{}+\frac{q^{\beta}q^{\alpha}}{2!}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial p^{\beta}}\frac{\partial}{\partial p^{\alpha}}\epsilon^{*}(p+q)\right)|_{q\rightarrow 0}\cdot\epsilon(p)+\cdots

(98)

where we suppress little group indices for clarity.

We start with the first non-trivial order in the expansion. Note we are now taking derivatives of a matrix exponential, therefore we must keep in mind the derivative of the exponential map

\frac{d}{dt}e^{X(t)}=\left(\frac{1-e^{-\text{ad}_{X}}}{\text{ad}_{X}}\frac{dX(t)}{dt}\right)e^{X(t)},

(99)

where

\frac{1-e^{-\text{ad}_{X}}}{\text{ad}_{X}}=\sum^{\infty}_{k=0}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{(k+1)!}(\text{ad}_{X})^{k},

(100)

and

\text{ad}_{X}(Y)=[X,Y].

(101)

Using this information, we expand the first derivative to sixth order in the exponential map expansion

\displaystyle q^{\alpha}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial p^{\alpha}}\epsilon(p+q)\right)|_{q\rightarrow 0}=

\displaystyle\left\{p_{\mu}k_{\nu}\frac{m^{2}q^{2}}{2}\left(\frac{\lambda^{3}}{3!}+\frac{\lambda^{5}\Gamma}{5!}+\frac{\lambda^{7}\Gamma^{2}}{7!}+\cdots\right)\right.

\displaystyle\left.\hskip 7.11317pt\hbox{}+k_{\mu}q_{\nu}\left(\left(\lambda+\frac{\lambda^{3}\Gamma}{3!}+\frac{\lambda^{5}\Gamma^{2}}{5!}+\frac{\lambda^{7}\Gamma^{3}}{7!}+\cdots\right)\right.\right.

\displaystyle\left.\left.\hskip 64.01869pt\hbox{}-k\cdot p\left(\frac{\lambda^{2}}{2!}+\frac{\lambda^{4}\Gamma}{4!}+\frac{\lambda^{6}\Gamma^{2}}{6!}+\cdots\right)\right)\right.

\displaystyle\left.\hskip 7.11317pt\hbox{}+p_{\mu}q_{\nu}m^{2}\left(\frac{\lambda^{2}}{2!}+\frac{\lambda^{4}\Gamma}{4!}+\frac{\lambda^{6}\Gamma^{2}}{6!}+\cdots\right)\right\}i\mathit{M}^{\mu\nu}\epsilon(p),

(102)

where we apply
k\cdot q=0
since
q
is assumed to be space-like and
k
is the rest frame, and
p\cdot q=-q^{2}/2
due to momentum conservation. Recall that the
\lambda
carry factors of
\Gamma
; taking this into account results in equal scaling of
\Gamma
in each series resulting in the following resummation

\displaystyle q^{\alpha}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial p^{\alpha}}\epsilon(p+q)\right)|_{q\rightarrow 0}=

\displaystyle\left\{p_{\mu}k_{\nu}\frac{m^{2}q^{2}}{2\Gamma}\left(\frac{\sinh{\Theta}}{\sqrt{\Gamma}}-\lambda\right)\right.

\displaystyle\left.\hskip 7.11317pt\hbox{}+k_{\mu}q_{\nu}\left(\frac{\sinh{\Theta}}{\sqrt{\Gamma}}-\frac{k\cdot p}{\Gamma}\left(\cosh{\Theta}-1\right)\right)\right.

\displaystyle\left.\hskip 7.11317pt\hbox{}+p_{\mu}q_{\nu}\frac{m^{2}}{\Gamma}\left(\cosh{\Theta}-1\right)\right\}i\mathit{M}^{\mu\nu}\epsilon(p).

(103)

Finally, a couple of useful relations to know are
\cosh{(\text{arcosh}x)}=x
and
\sinh{(\text{arcosh}x)}=\sqrt{x^{2}-1}
. With this in mind we arrive at the first term in the expansion

q^{\alpha}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial p^{\alpha}}\epsilon(p+q)\right)|_{q\rightarrow 0}=\left((k+p)_{\mu}q_{\nu}+p_{\mu}k_{\nu}\frac{q^{2}(1-m^{2}\lambda)}{2(p\cdot k-m^{2})}\right)\frac{i\mathit{M}^{\mu\nu}}{p\cdot k+m^{2}}\epsilon(p),

(104)

where we should note that the second term in the parentheses is higher order in our expansion parameter
q
, and will therefore be ignored in the future.

We now have an answer for the first order expansion of the product of polarization tensors

q^{\alpha}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial p^{\alpha}}\epsilon^{*}(p+q)\right)|_{q\rightarrow 0}\cdot\epsilon(p)=iq_{\mu}\frac{S^{\mu\nu}(p)(k_{\nu}+p_{\nu})}{p\cdot k+m^{2}}\equiv iq_{\mu}\omega^{\mu}.

(105)

Calculating higher orders in the perturbation series streamlines. We consider the second derivative of the exponential map

\frac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}}e^{X(t)}=\frac{d}{dt}\left(\frac{1-e^{-\text{ad}_{X}}}{\text{ad}_{X}}\frac{dX(t)}{dt}\right)e^{X(t)}+\left(\frac{1-e^{-\text{ad}_{X}}}{\text{ad}_{X}}\frac{dX(t)}{dt}\right)^{2}e^{X(t)}.

(106)

We have just calculated the terms in the parentheses, so all we have to do is take our previous expression and simply plug in

\displaystyle\frac{q^{\beta}q^{\alpha}}{2!}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial p^{\beta}}\frac{\partial}{\partial p^{\alpha}}\epsilon(p+q)\right)|_{q\rightarrow 0}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left[-ik_{\mu}q_{\nu}\mathit{M}^{\mu\nu}\frac{q^{2}(1-m^{2}\lambda)}{2(p\cdot k^{2}-m^{4})}\right.

\displaystyle\left.\hskip 17.07182pt\hbox{}+\left((k+p)_{\mu}q_{\nu}\frac{i\mathit{M}^{\mu\nu}}{p\cdot k+m^{2}}\right.\right.

\displaystyle\left.\left.\hskip 36.98866pt\hbox{}+p_{\mu}k_{\nu}\frac{q^{2}(1-m^{2}\lambda)}{2(p\cdot k-m^{2})}\frac{i\mathit{M}^{\mu\nu}}{p\cdot k+m^{2}}\right)^{2}\right]\epsilon(p).

(107)

Once again, looking at the
q
-scaling of our expression, we only have one term that contributes to the leading order, which gives us the truncated second order term

\displaystyle\frac{q^{\beta}q^{\alpha}}{2!}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial p^{\beta}}\frac{\partial}{\partial p^{\alpha}}\epsilon^{*}(p+q)\right)|_{q\rightarrow 0}\cdot\epsilon(p)=

\displaystyle q_{\mu}q_{\rho}\frac{-(k_{\nu}+p_{\nu})(k_{\sigma}+p_{\sigma})}{2(p\cdot k+m^{2})^{2}}\epsilon^{*}(p)\frac{1}{2}\left\{\mathit{M}^{\mu\nu},\mathit{M}^{\rho\sigma}\right\}\epsilon(p)

\displaystyle=q_{\mu}q_{\rho}\frac{-S^{\mu\nu}(p)(k_{\nu}+p_{\nu})S^{\rho\sigma}(p)(k_{\sigma}+p_{\sigma})}{2(p\cdot k+m^{2})^{2}},

(108)

where we preemptively decomposed the quadratic product of Lorentz generators in to symmetric and anti-symmetric parts. We also use the identity introduced in Ref.
Bern:2020buy
relating symmetric products of Lorentz generators and products of the spin tensor. Here we begin to notice a pattern

\displaystyle\frac{q^{\beta}q^{\alpha}}{2!}

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial p^{\beta}}\frac{\partial}{\partial p^{\alpha}}\epsilon^{*}(p+q)\right)|_{q\rightarrow 0}\cdot\epsilon(p)=\frac{\left(iq_{\mu}\omega^{\mu}\right)\left(iq_{\rho}\omega^{\rho}\right)}{2!}.

At this point, we see the same pattern of exponentiation as seen in Refs.
Bern:2023ity
;
Bern:2020buy
;
Bern:2022kto
;
Kosmopoulos:2021zoq
and use the exponentiated form of the polarization tensor product Eq. (
22
).

On some new families of k k -Mersenne and generalized k k -Gaussian Mersenne numbers and their polynomials
Munesh Kumari, Jagmohan Tanti, Kalika Prasad

Abstract
In this paper, we define generalized k k -Mersenne numbers and give a formula of generalized Mersenne polynomials and further we study their properties. Moreover, we define Gaussian Mersenne numbers and obtain some identities like Binet Formula, Cassini’s identity, D’Ocagne’s Identity, and generating functions. The generalized Gaussian Mersenne numbers are described and the relation with classical Mersenne numbers are explained. We also introduce a generalization of Gaussian Mersenne polynomials and establish some properties of these polynomials.

3.1
Gaussian Mersenne Numbers

Definition 3.1
.

The
Gaussian Mersenne sequence
\{GM_{k}\}
is defined by the recurrence relation,

\displaystyle GM_{k+2}=3GM_{k+1}-2GM_{k},\hskip 22.76228ptk\geq 0

(3.1)

with
GM_{0}=-i/2,~~GM_{1}=1
.

The first few Gaussian Mersenne numbers are
-i/2,1,3+i,7+3i,15+7i,...

Further, the relation between Gaussian Mersenne numbers and classical Mersenne numbers is

GM_{k+2}=M_{k+2}+iM_{k+1},

where
GM_{k}
is the
k^{th}
-Gaussian Mersenne number.

Theorem 3.1
.

For every
n\in\mathbb{N}
, Binet formula for the Gaussian Mersenne numbers is

GM_{n}=(\lambda_{1}^{n}-\lambda_{2}^{n})+i(\lambda_{1}^{n-1}-\lambda_{2}^{n-1}),

(3.2)

where
\lambda_{1}
and
\lambda_{2}
are the roots of the characteristic equation(
1.2
).

Proof.

The
n^{th}
term of Gaussian Mersenne numbers for the difference equation(
3.1
) is,

GM_{n}=a\lambda_{1}^{n}+b\lambda_{2}^{n}.

(3.3)

To eliminate constants
a

\&

b
, we use initial conditions given in eqn.(
3.1
).

Since, we have
GM_{0}=a+b
and
GM_{1}=a\lambda_{1}+b\lambda_{2}
.

It yields,
a=1+i/2
and
b=-1-i
.

So, from eqn.(
3.3
) we get

GM_{n}=(\lambda_{1}^{n}-\lambda_{2}^{n})+i(\lambda_{1}^{n-1}-\lambda_{2}^{n-1}).

Furthermore, by using values of
\lambda_{1}
and
\lambda_{2}
in eqn.(
3.2
), we get another form of Binet formula which is

GM_{n}=(2^{n}-1)+i(2^{n-1}-1).

(3.4)

∎

Theorem 3.2

(Catlan’s Identity)
.

For
n,m\geq 1
, we have

GM_{n+m}GM_{n-m}-GM_{n}^{2}=\left[(2^{n}-2^{n+m-1})+(2^{n-m-1}-2^{n-m})\right]+i3(2^{n}-2^{n+m-1}-2^{n-m-1}).

(3.5)

Proof.

Using the Binet formula(
3.4
), we have

\displaystyle GM_{n+m}GM_{n-m}-GM_{n}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[(2^{n+m}-1)+i(2^{n+m-1}-1)(2^{n+m}-1)+i(2^{n+m-1}-1)\right]

\displaystyle-\left[(2^{n}-1)+i(2^{n-1}-1)\right]^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[(2^{n}-2^{n+m-1})+(2^{n-m-1}-2^{n-m})\right]+i3(2^{n}-2^{n+m-1}-2^{n-m-1}).

∎

From eqn.(

\displaystyle GM_{m+1}GM_{n}-GM_{m}GM_{n+1}

\displaystyle-\left[\ (2^{m}-1)+i(2^{m-1}-1)(2^{n+1}-1)+i(2^{n}-1)\right]

\displaystyle(2^{n-1}-2^{m-1})+i3(2^{n-1}-2^{m-1}).

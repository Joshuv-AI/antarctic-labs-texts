Homotopy Epimorphisms and Derived Tate’s Acyclicity for Commutative C ∗ C^{*} -algebras
Federico Bambozzi, Tomoki Mihara

Abstract
We study homotopy epimorphisms and covers formulated in terms of derived Tate’s acyclicity for commutative C ∗ C^{*} -algebras and their non-Archimedean counterparts. We prove that a homotopy epimorphism between commutative C ∗ C^{*} -algebras precisely corresponds to a closed immersion between the compact Hausdorff topological spaces associated to them, and a cover of a commutative C ∗ C^{*} -algebra precisely corresponds to a topological cover of the compact Hausdorff topological space associated to it by closed immersions admitting a finite subcover. This permits us to prove derived and non-derived descent for Banach modules over commutative C ∗ C^{*} -algebras.

Introduction

Since Tate’s invention of rigid geometry (cf.
[
Tat71
]
), the geometrical study of commutative Banach rings has been an important topic in number theory. The geometrical study of commutative rings has brought various benefits such as cohomological approaches to construct Galois representations, but the geometrical study of commutative Banach rings presents more difficult challenges than the geometrical study of commutative rings. One of the main difficulties is that the additive category of Banach modules over a Banach ring is not an Abelian category. In particular, this creates the problem of the correct use of the methods of homological algebra in this context. One solution is to work in Banach modules under appropriate finiteness assumptions to obtain an Abelian category. For example, P. Schneider and J. Teitelbaum constructed in
[
ST02
]
§3 an Abelian subcategory of the additive category of Banach representations of a compact
p
-adic Lie group by introducing the notion of admissibility, which can be characterised in terms of the finiteness over the Iwasawa algebra through their duality theory (cf.
[
ST02
]
Theorem 2.3 and
[
ST02
]
Theorem 3.5). Another solution is to work within the theory of quasi-Abelian categories and apply the extension of the derived categorical approach to a quasi-Abelian category as introduced by J.-P. Schniders in
[
Sch99
]
. This approach has been recently developed in the series of papers (cf.
[
BB16
]
,
[
BK17
]
,
[
BBK19
]
, and
[
BK20
]
). Once the notions of derived categories and derived functors are correctly extended to the analytic setting, it is natural to consider the derived variant of Tate’s acyclicity. In order to explain the benefit to consider derived Tate’s acyclicity, we recall the historical background on Tate’s acyclicity.

Tate’s acyclicity is a desired property of commutative Banach rings in rigid geometry, that permits to equip their associated spectra with structure sheaves. Although commutative Banach algebras over a complete valuation field satisfying a finiteness condition called
affinoid algebras
satisfy Tate’s acyclicity, it is known that a general commutative Banach ring does not necessarily satisfy Tate’s acyclicity. The lack of Tate’s acyclicity was one of the biggest obstructions to develop analytic geometry for general commutative Banach rings, e.g. commutative Banach algebras over
\mathbb{Z}
and topologically infinitely generated commutative Banach algebras over a field. Recently, P. Scholze invented a novel foundation of rigid geometry called
perfectoid theory
, in which he verified Tate’s acyclicity for topologically infinitely generated commutative Banach algebras over a field called
perfectoid algebras
, and asked several related open questions on Tate’s acyclicity. Although several affirmative answers and negative answers to Scholze’s conjectures were given in
[
Mih16
]
and
[
BV18
]
, it is still difficult to prove or disprove Tate’s acyclicity for explicit examples.

On the other hand, derived Tate’s acyclicity for a rational cover always hold as proved in
[
Sch19
]
Proposition 13.16 and
[
BK20
]
Theorem 4.15, and Tate’s acyclicity for a general cover consisting of flat objects in the analytic sense implies derived Tate’s acyclicity (cf. Proposition
1.10
). As evidence of the reasonability and the naturality of the formulation, we give a complete characterisation of derived Tate’s acyclicity for commutative
C^{*}
-algebras in terms of topological covers of the corresponding compact Hausdorff topological spaces by closed immersions and show the equivalence between Tate’s acyclicity and derived Tate’s acyclicity for them in Theorem
3.1
and Theorem
3.6
.

The results include the non-Archimedean counterparts, while the characterisation becomes a little more complicated. For example, the projection
[0,1]\to\mathchoice{\left\{0\right\}}{\{0\}}{\{0\}}{\{0\}}
is not a closed immersion but induces an isometric isomorphism between the algebras of continuous functions with values in a complete valuation field
k
. Conversely, the closed immersion
\mathchoice{\left\{0,1\right\}}{\{0,1\}}{\{0,1\}}{\{0,1\}}\hookrightarrow[0,1]
induces a
k
-algebra homomorphism between the algebras which cannot be part of a cover in our setting, as we will show in Remark
2.25
. Instead of a closed immersion, we introduce the notion of an
\text{\rm Ab}^{\text{\rm nA}}
-embedding in section

X

X
by
\text{\rm Ab}^{\text{\rm nA}}
-embeddings precisely corresponds to a cover of the algebra of continuous functions

formulated in terms of derived Tate’s acyclicity (or equivalently Tate’s acyclicity).

As an application, we show in Theorem
4.1
that a finite set of closed immersions into a compact Hausdorff topological space (resp. a totally disconnected compact Hausdorff topological space)

is a topological cover of
X
if and only if it satisfies effective descent for Banach modules over the algebra of continuous functions with values in
\mathbb{R}
or
\mathbb{C}
(resp. in

It is quite remarkable that Tate’s acyclicity is not directly used in the proof of effective descent, because the category of Banach spaces is not balanced and hence a faithful functor from it might not be conservative. Contrary, derived Tate’s acyclicity plays the most important role in the proof, because the derived category is balanced and hence a faithful functor from it is conservative as we will show in Lemma
4.4
. First, we show Kiehl’s Theorem B for both Banach modules and objects in the derived category in Corollary
3.4
. Using Kiehl’s Theorem B for objects in the derived category and Balmer’s criterion for effective descent (cf.
[
Bal12
]
Corollary 3.1), which is only applicable to a triangulated category, we show that any descent problem for Banach modules has a solution in the derived category. Using general results by Schneiders on the left heart cohomology for a quasi-Abelian category, we show that the solution in the derived category is isomorphic to a Banach module to conclude the solvability of the descent problem within the category of Banach modules.

is not equivalent to that of a chain homomorphism which induces isomorphisms between the “naively defined” cohomology groups, i.e. the cohomology groups defined as

\displaystyle\text{\rm H}_{\text{\rm naive}}^{n}(M)\coloneqq\frac{\ker(d_{M_{n}})}{\text{\rm im}(d_{M_{n-1}})}

for a chain complex

\mathchoice{\left|\cdot\right|}{|\cdot|}{|\cdot|}{|\cdot|}_{0}
denote the trivial norm on

\displaystyle\cdots\to 0\to(\mathbb{Z},\mathchoice{\left|\cdot\right|}{|\cdot|}{|\cdot|}{|\cdot|}_{\infty})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{\rm id}_{\mathbb{Z}}}}{{\to}}(\mathbb{Z},\mathchoice{\left|\cdot\right|}{|\cdot|}{|\cdot|}{|\cdot|}_{0})\to 0\to\cdots

is exact as a chain complex of

\displaystyle\cdots\to 0\to\ell^{1}(\mathbb{N},(\mathbb{R},\mathchoice{\left|\cdot\right|}{|\cdot|}{|\cdot|}{|\cdot|}_{\infty}))\hookrightarrow\ell^{2}(\mathbb{N},(\mathbb{R},\mathchoice{\left|\cdot\right|}{|\cdot|}{|\cdot|}{|\cdot|}_{\infty}))\to 0\to\cdots

\text{\rm Ch}_{\mathscr{C}}(R)
but these involve the use of the left or right t-structure of

that will not be used in this work until §

Proposition 1.4
.

The class of quasi-isomorphisms is closed under homotopy equivalences, and forms a saturated multiplicative system in
\text{\rm Ch}_{\mathscr{C}}(R)
.

Proof.

The null system in
\mathscr{K}_{\mathscr{C}}(R)
of strictly exact sequences coincides with the kernel of a cohomology functor by
[
Sch99
]
Corollary 1.2.20, and hence the class of homotopy classes of quasi-isomorphisms in
\text{\rm Ch}_{\mathscr{C}}(R)
is a saturated multiplicative system in
\mathscr{K}_{\mathscr{C}}(R)
by
[
StackP
]
Lemma 13.6.11. This implies that the class of morphisms in
\text{\rm Ch}_{\mathscr{C}}(R)
homotopic to quasi-isomorphisms is a saturated multiplicative system in
\text{\rm Ch}_{\mathscr{C}}(R)
. Therefore, it suffices to show that for any objects
M=(M_{n},d_{M_{n}})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
and
N=(N_{n},d_{N_{n}})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
, any morphism
f\colon M\to N
in
\text{\rm Ch}_{\mathscr{C}}(R)
homotopic to a quasi-isomorphism
g\colon M\to N
in
\text{\rm Ch}_{\mathscr{C}}(R)
is a quasi-isomorphism.

Consider the factorization
r\circ i_{0}=f
and
r\circ i_{1}=g
through the cylinder object
\text{\rm Cyl}(M)
. We obtain the distinguished triangles

\displaystyle\text{\rm Cone}(i_{0})\to\text{\rm Cone}(f)

\displaystyle\to

\displaystyle\text{\rm Cone}(r)\to\text{\rm Cone}(i_{0})[1]

\displaystyle\text{\rm Cone}(i_{1})\to\text{\rm Cone}(g)

\displaystyle\to

\displaystyle\text{\rm Cone}(r)\to\text{\rm Cone}(i_{1})[1]

by the octahedral axiom, because
\mathscr{K}_{\mathscr{C}}(R)
naturally forms a triangulated category (cf.
[
Sch99
]
§1.2.1). Since
g
is a quasi-isomorphism,
\text{\rm Cone}(g)
is strictly exact. Therefore, in order to show that
f
is a quasi-isomorphism, it suffices to show that
\text{\rm Cone}(i_{0})
and
\text{\rm Cone}(i_{1})
are strictly exact, by
[
Sch99
]
Proposition 1.2.14. For this purpose, it suffices to show that a morphism in
\text{\rm Ch}_{\mathscr{C}}(R)
which represents an isomorphism in
\mathscr{K}_{\mathscr{C}}(R)
is a quasi-isomorphism. By
[
KS06
]
Exercise 11.5 the cone of any such morphism is isomorphic to
0
in
\mathscr{K}_{\mathscr{C}}(R)
, and hence it is strictly exact by
[
Sch99
]
Remark 1.2.2. This completes the proof.
∎

\text{\rm Ch}^{-}_{\mathscr{C}}(R)
is a quasi-isomorphism.

Proof.

The assertion (i) follows from the symmetry and the associativity of the total complex. The assertion (ii) (resp. (iii)) follows from
[
BK20
]
Proposition 3.11 and the assertion (iv) applied to the natural morphism

\displaystyle P_{\mathscr{C}}^{R}(M)\widehat{\otimes}{}_{R}P_{\mathscr{C}}^{R}(N)\to M\widehat{\otimes}{}_{R}P_{\mathscr{C}}^{R}(N)

in
\text{\rm Ch}^{-}_{\mathscr{C}}(\text{\rm Ch}^{-}_{\mathscr{C}}(R))
for any
N\in\text{\rm Mod}_{\mathscr{C}}(R)
(resp. the natural morphisms

\displaystyle P_{\mathscr{C}}^{R}(M)\widehat{\otimes}{}_{R}P_{\mathscr{C}}^{R}(N)\to P_{\mathscr{C}}^{R}(M)\widehat{\otimes}{}_{R}N\to M\widehat{\otimes}{}_{R}N

in
\text{\rm Ch}^{-}_{\mathscr{C}}(\text{\rm Ch}^{-}_{\mathscr{C}}(R))
). We show the assertion (iv).

Put
M=((M_{i,j},d^{\text{\rm h}}_{M_{i,j}})_{i\in\mathbb{Z}},(d^{\text{\rm v}}_{M_{i,j}})_{i\in\mathbb{Z}})_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}
,
((N_{i,j},d^{\text{\rm h}}_{N_{i,j}})_{i\in\mathbb{Z}},(d^{\text{\rm v}}_{N_{i,j}})_{i\in\mathbb{Z}})_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}
, and

for each

. We have

\displaystyle\text{\rm Cone}(\text{\rm Tot}(f))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}(M_{i+1,n-i}\oplus N_{i,n-i}),\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}-d^{\text{\rm v}}_{M_{i+1,n-i}}-(-1)^{i+1}d^{\text{\rm h}}_{M_{i+1,n-i}}&d^{\text{\rm v}}_{N_{i,n-i}}+(-1)^{i}d^{\text{\rm h}}_{N_{i,n-i}}\\
f_{i+1,n-i}&0\\
\end{array}\right)\right)_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\text{\rm Cone}(f_{n-i})_{i},\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}(d_{\text{\rm Cone}(f_{n-i})_{i}}+(-1)^{i}(d^{\text{\rm h}}_{M_{i+1,n-i}}+d^{\text{\rm h}}_{N_{i,n-i}}))\right)_{n\in\mathbb{Z}},

and hence the strict exactness of
\text{\rm Cone}(\text{\rm Tot}(f))
at degree

follows from the strict exactness of
\text{\rm Cone}(f_{j})
at degree

applied inductively on

.
∎

In particular, the derived tensor product can be computed by resolving only one argument by a flat resolution as usual in the algebraic setting.

1.3
Derived analytic geometry

We introduce the notion of homotopy epimorphism. When we deal with an appropriate formulation of the spectrum, the map of spectra induced by a homotopy epimorphism called a
formal homotopy Zariski open immersion
, because it plays the role of an open immersion in the derived geometry and especially in derived analytic geometry as formulated in
[
BBK19
]
. Therefore a homotopy epimorphism is a derived counterpart of an open localisation. We usually drop the adjective “formal” when talking about formal homotopy Zariski open immersions as we will not study any “non-formal” ones.

Let
R
be a monoid object of
\mathscr{C}
. We denote by
\text{\rm Alg}_{\mathscr{C}}(R)
the category of monoid objects of
\text{\rm Mod}_{\mathscr{C}}(R)
and monoid homomorphisms in
\text{\rm Mod}_{\mathscr{C}}(R)
. Let
\pi\colon A_{0}\to A_{1}
be a morphism in
\text{\rm Alg}_{\mathscr{C}}(R)
. We say that
\pi
is a
homotopy epimorphism in
\text{\rm Alg}_{\mathscr{C}}(R)
if the multiplication
A_{1}\widehat{\otimes}{}_{A_{0}}^{\mathbb{L}}A_{1}\to A_{1}
is an isomorphism in
\text{\rm Der}^{-}_{\mathscr{C}}(A_{0})
. We note that
\widehat{\otimes}{}_{A_{0}}
gives a binary coproduct in
\text{\rm Alg}_{\mathscr{C}}(A_{0})
by
[
BK20
]
Proposition 2.16, and hence the non-derived multiplication
A_{1}\widehat{\otimes}{}_{A_{0}}A_{1}\to A_{1}
is an isomorphism in
\text{\rm Mod}_{\mathscr{C}}(A_{0})
if and only if
\pi
is an epimorphism in
\text{\rm Alg}_{\mathscr{C}}(R)
. This fact clearly justifies the terminology “homotopy epimorphism”.

Since the simplest examples of epimorphisms in the category of
R
-algebras and
R
-algebra homomorphisms are given by quotients of localisations, which corresponds to pro-locally closed immersions of affine schemes over
\text{\rm Spec}(R)
, it is not difficult to imagine how a homotopy Zariski open immersion formally plays a role of an “immersion”. We show the relation between the multiplications
A_{1}\widehat{\otimes}{}_{A_{0}}A_{1}\to A_{1}
and
A_{1}\widehat{\otimes}{}_{A_{0}}^{\mathbb{L}}A_{1}\to A_{1}
.

Proposition 1.7
.

If
\pi
is a homotopy epimorphism in
\text{\rm Alg}_{\mathscr{C}}(R)
, then
\pi
is an epimorphism in
\text{\rm Alg}_{\mathscr{C}}(R)
.

Proof.

We put
P_{\mathscr{C}}^{A_{0}}(A_{1})=(P_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
, and by
\rho\colon P_{\mathscr{C}}^{A_{0}}(A_{1})\to A_{1}
the canonical chain homomorphism of chain complexes of
\text{\rm Mod}_{\mathscr{C}}(A_{0})
. By Proposition
1.6
(ii), the natural morphism
\iota\colon\text{\rm Tot}(P_{\mathscr{C}}^{A_{0}}(A_{1})\widehat{\otimes}{}_{A_{0}}P_{\mathscr{C}}^{A_{0}}(A_{1}))\to A_{1}\widehat{\otimes}{}_{A_{0}}P_{\mathscr{C}}^{A_{0}}(A_{1})
in
\text{\rm Ch}^{-}_{\mathscr{C}}(A_{0})
is a quasi-isomorphism. We denote by
\mu
the multiplication
A_{1}\widehat{\otimes}{}_{A_{0}}A_{1}\to A_{1}
, and by
\mu^{\mathbb{L}}
the composite
\mu\circ(\text{\rm id}_{A_{1}}\widehat{\otimes}{}_{A_{0}}\rho)\circ\iota
. By definition,
\mu^{\mathbb{L}}
is a morphism in
\text{\rm Ch}^{-}_{\mathscr{C}}(A_{0})
representing the multiplication
A_{1}\widehat{\otimes}{}_{A_{0}}^{\mathbb{L}}A_{1}\to A_{1}
. Since
\mu^{\mathbb{L}}
and
\iota
represent isomorphisms in
\text{\rm Der}^{-}_{\mathscr{C}}(A_{1})
,
\mu\circ(\text{\rm id}_{A_{1}}\widehat{\otimes}{}_{A_{0}}\rho)
represents an isomorphism in
\text{\rm Der}^{-}_{\mathscr{C}}(A_{1})
. This implies that
\mu\circ(\text{\rm id}_{A_{1}}\widehat{\otimes}{}_{A_{0}}\rho)
is a quasi-isomorphism in

by Corollary

(i), and hence its mapping cone

\displaystyle\cdots\to A_{1}\widehat{\otimes}{}_{A_{0}}P_{1}\to A_{1}\widehat{\otimes}{}_{A_{0}}P_{0}\to A_{1}\to 0\to\cdots

is strictly exact. On the other hand, the segment

\displaystyle A_{1}\widehat{\otimes}{}_{A_{0}}P_{1}\to A_{1}\widehat{\otimes}{}_{A_{0}}P_{0}\to A_{1}\widehat{\otimes}{}_{A_{0}}A_{1}\to 0

of the mapping cone of

A_{1}\widehat{\otimes}{}_{A_{0}}(\cdot)
and the strict coexactness of
P_{1}\to P_{0}\to A_{1}\to 0
. In particular,

is a morphism in
\text{\rm Mod}_{\mathscr{C}}(A_{0})
under
A_{1}\widehat{\otimes}{}_{A_{0}}P_{0}
between two cokernels of

, and hence is an isomorphism in

by the universality of the cokernel. This implies that
\pi
is an epimorphism in
\text{\rm Alg}_{\mathscr{C}}(R)
.
∎

.

Suppose that the natural morphism

.

We use the notion of homotopy epimorphism to define the notion of cover for an object of
\text{\rm Alg}_{\mathscr{C}}(R)
. In this context, objects of
\text{\rm Alg}_{\mathscr{C}}(R)
are identified with the objects of the dual category and hence formally considered as their own spectra. For a set

the subset of

of

in

is a subset

satisfying the following:

(i)

For any

, the structure morphism

is a homotopy epimorphism in
\text{\rm Alg}_{\mathscr{C}}(R)
.

(ii)

There exists a finite subset

satisfying derived Tate’s acyclicity (cf.
[
BK20
]

\check{\text{C}}

\displaystyle 0\to A\to\prod_{B\in S_{0}}B\to\prod_{(B_{0},B_{1})\in[S_{0}]^{2}}B_{0}\widehat{\otimes}{}_{A}^{\mathbb{L}}B_{1}\to\cdots

is strictly exact in
\text{\rm Mod}_{\mathscr{C}}(R)
.

Similarly, we introduce a terminology for the classical (non-derived) setting in order to contrast it with a derived cover. A

of

\check{\text{C}}

\displaystyle 0\to A\to\prod_{B\in S_{0}}B\to\prod_{(B_{0},B_{1})\in[S_{0}]^{2}}B_{0}\widehat{\otimes}{}_{A}B_{1}\to\cdots

This notion of cover never defines a Grothendieck topology on

3
Derived covers versus topological covers

Let
\mathscr{C}
be either
\text{\rm Ab}^{\text{\rm A}}
or
\text{\rm Ab}^{\text{\rm nA}}
,
R
a monoid object of
\mathscr{C}
, and
X
a compact Hausdorff topological space. We continue to use the abbreviations
\mathscr{D}_{2}
and
\mathscr{D}_{3}
as in §

. We have shown that a closed immersion of topological spaces corresponds to a homotopy Zariski localisations of the associated algebras of continuous functions, under mild assumptions on
\mathscr{D}_{3}
. Then, natural questions arise: Does a homotopy Zariski open immersion always correspond to a closed immersion? Does a topological cover by closed immersions correspond to a cover in the sense of derived analytic geometry and vice versa? We will answer these two questions in this section in Theorem
3.1
, Theorem
3.6
, and Lemma
3.9
.

3.1
Topological covers

We introduce the notion of a topological cover. We denote by
\text{\rm TDCH}\subset\text{\rm CH}
(cf. the proof of Proposition

is not totally disconnected.

We denote by

When
\mathscr{C}=\text{\rm Ab}^{\text{\rm A}}
, we denote by
\beta_{\mathscr{C}}
the identity functor
\text{\rm CH}\to\text{\rm CH}
, and by
\iota_{\mathscr{C}}
the identity natural transformation
\text{\rm id}_{\text{\rm CH}}\Rightarrow\beta_{\mathscr{C}}
. When
\mathscr{C}=\text{\rm Ab}^{\text{\rm nA}}
, we denote by
\beta_{\mathscr{C}}
the Banaschewski compactification functor
\text{\rm CH}\to\text{\rm TDCH}
, and by
\iota_{\mathscr{C}}
the natural transformation
\text{\rm id}_{\text{\rm CH}}\Rightarrow\beta_{\mathscr{C}}
assigning the canonical map

with unit

, and is explicitly described in

]

.

A
\mathscr{C}
-embedding
is a morphism

in

such that
\beta_{\mathscr{C}}(\phi)
is a homeomorphism onto the image. In particular, the notion of an
\text{\rm Ab}^{\text{\rm A}}
-embedding is equivalent to that of a homeomorphism onto image between compact Hausdorff topological spaces, and every homeomorphism onto the image between totally disconnected compact Hausdorff topological spaces is an
\text{\rm Ab}^{\text{\rm nA}}
-embedding. On the other hand, an
\text{\rm Ab}^{\text{\rm nA}}
-embedding is not necessarily a homeomorphism onto the image, and a homeomorphism onto the image between (not necessarily totally disconnected) compact Hausdorff topological spaces is not necessarily an
\text{\rm Ab}^{\text{\rm nA}}
-embedding. Indeed, the projection

\text{\rm Ab}^{\text{\rm nA}}
-embedding which is not a homeomorphism onto the image, and the inclusion
\mathchoice{\left\{0,1\right\}}{\{0,1\}}{\{0,1\}}{\{0,1\}}\hookrightarrow[0,1]

-embedding.

A
\mathscr{C}
-cover of

is a subset

(i)

For any

-embedding.

(ii)

such that the composite of

and the coproduct

We now describe the relations between
\mathscr{C}
-covers of
X
, derived covers of
\text{\rm C}(X,R)
in
\mathscr{C}
, and non-derived covers of
\text{\rm C}(X,R)
in
\mathscr{C}
.

Coherent insulator at arbitrary frequency in a driven atomtronic transistor
Wenxi Lai

Abstract
We use numerical approach to study non-equilibrium transport of atomic gas in a driven optical lattice atomtronic transistor. The shaken optical lattice transistor displays a property of insulator within some regions of shaking frequency and shaking strength. It is proved that appearance of the insulation is directly connected to coherence of the system. Coherence of the system is accompanied by coherent trapping of non-equilibrium atomic gas in one of the optical wells, which stops atomic currents. Comparing with the effective Hamiltonian approach in Floquet engineering, the time-dependent Hamiltonian approach could be used in any frequency regime of periodically driven quantum system.

Driven atomtronic transisor
-
The system is schematically illustrated in Fig.
1
. The shaken open system is described with the time dependent Hamiltonian
\mathcal{H}(t)=\mathcal{H}_{S}(t)+\mathcal{H}_{E}+\mathcal{H}_{C}
, where the system Hamiltonian which is composed of two shaken optical potential could be written as

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{S}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{l=1}^{2}(\varepsilon_{l}+\omega_{l}(t))n_{l}-J(a_{1}^{{\dagger}}a_{2}+a_{2}^{{\dagger}}a_{1}),

(1)

where
a_{l}
,
a_{l}^{{\dagger}}
and
n_{l}=a_{l}^{{\dagger}}a_{l}
are annihilation, creation and number operators of atoms at lattice site
l
(
l=1
,
2
), respectively.
\varepsilon_{l}
denotes bare potential at lattice site
l
. When the potential oscillates periodically, in the reference frame of the potential, cold atoms should feel a oscillating force of inertia
F(t)
. The oscillating inertial force leads to shaken potential that has the form
\omega_{l}(t)=-r_{l}\cdot F(t)
, in which the force get the form
F(t)=F_{\omega}\cos(\omega t)e_{x}
. In the reference frame of a laboratory, positions of the center of optical wells are denoted by
r_{1}=-\frac{d}{2}e_{x}
and
r_{2}=\frac{d}{2}e_{x}
. The two potentials are coupled with a tunneling strength
J
of cold atoms. The atomic electrodes are regarded as free atomic gas with the bare energy,

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{E}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\alpha,k}\mu_{\alpha k}n_{\alpha k},

(2)

where
a_{\alpha k}
,
a_{\alpha k}^{{\dagger}}
and
n_{\alpha k}=a_{\alpha k}^{{\dagger}}a_{\alpha k}
are annihilation, creation and atom number operators in the left (
\alpha=L
) and right (
\alpha=R
) electrodes, respectively. Here,
\mu_{k}
represents energy of a single atom with momentum
k
. Coupling between the shaken system and atomic electrodes are described by the Hamiltonian,

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{C}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\sum_{k}(t_{L}a_{1}^{{\dagger}}a_{Lk}+t_{R}a_{2}^{{\dagger}}a_{Rk}+H.c.),

(3)

where
t_{\alpha}
indicates coupling amplitude between the system and any of the two electrodes.

Quantum master equation
-
Quantum state of the whole configuration is denoted by the total density matrix
\varrho_{tot}
which consists of the states of the system and its environment. Time evolution of the density matrix
\varrho_{tot}
satisfies the Liouville-von Neumann equation,

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\varrho_{tot}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{i}{\hbar}[\mathcal{H}(t),\varrho_{tot}(t)].

(4)

Under the transformation of a unitary operator
U(t)=\exp[i\sum_{l=1}^{2}\chi_{l}(t)n_{l}]
, where
\chi_{l}(t)=-\frac{1}{\hbar}\int_{0}^{t}dt^{\prime}\omega_{l}(t^{\prime})
in which
\omega_{l}(t)
is given in the Hamiltonian (
1
), Eq. (
4
) would be renewed to be

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\rho_{tot}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{i}{\hbar}[H(t),\rho_{tot}(t)],

(5)

where the density matrix is related to
\rho_{tot}(t)=U^{{\dagger}}(t)\varrho_{tot}(t)U(t)
and the Hamiltonian becomes
H(t)=U^{{\dagger}}(t)\mathcal{H}(t)U(t)-i\hbar U^{{\dagger}}(t)\frac{\partial}{\partial t}U(t)
, which has the detail expression

\displaystyle H(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{l=1}^{2}\varepsilon_{l}n_{l}-J(e^{i\phi(t)}a_{1}^{{\dagger}}a_{2}+H.c.)+\sum_{\alpha,k}\mu_{\alpha k}n_{\alpha k}

(6)

\displaystyle-\sum_{k}(t_{L}(t)a_{1}^{{\dagger}}a_{Lk}+t_{R}(t)a_{2}^{{\dagger}}a_{Rk}+H.c.),

where the relative phase is
\phi(t)=\chi_{2}(t)-\chi_{1}(t)
, at the same time, additional phases appear beside the bare tunneling amplitudes as
t_{L}(t)=t_{L}e^{-i\chi_{1}(t)}
and
t_{R}(t)=t_{R}e^{-i\chi_{2}(t)}
, respectively. From the above definition, one can deduce that
\chi_{2}(t)=-\chi_{1}(t)=\frac{K}{2\hbar\omega}\sin(\omega t)
and
\phi(t)=\frac{K}{\hbar\omega}\sin(\omega t)
, in which
K
is the shaking strength
K=F_{\omega}d
. Hamiltonian (
6
) is important in our work as the starting point of all the following investigations.

Using the gauge-transformed Hamiltonian (
7
), one can derive the quantum master equation of the system with time-varying coefficients,

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\rho

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-i[\sum_{l=1}^{2}\varepsilon_{l}n_{l}-J(e^{i\phi(t)}a_{1}^{{\dagger}}a_{2}+H.c.),\rho]

(7)

\displaystyle+\mathcal{L}_{L}\rho+\mathcal{L}_{R}\rho,

where the system density matrix is
\rho=e^{-\frac{it}{\hbar}H_{0}}\hat{\rho}e^{\frac{it}{\hbar}H_{0}}
. The Lindblad super operators reveals atom leakage between the system and atomic electrodes, they have the typical formulations as

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{L}\rho

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\Gamma_{L}}{2}(1-f_{L}(\varepsilon_{1}))(2a_{1}\rho a_{1}^{{\dagger}}-\{a_{1}^{{\dagger}}a_{1},\rho\})

(8)

\displaystyle+\frac{\Gamma_{L}}{2}f_{L}(\varepsilon_{1})(2a_{1}^{{\dagger}}\rho a_{1}-\{a_{1}a_{1}^{{\dagger}},\rho\})

and

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{R}\rho

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\Gamma_{R}}{2}(1-f_{R}(\varepsilon_{2}))(2a_{2}\rho a_{2}^{{\dagger}}-\{a_{2}^{{\dagger}}a_{2},\rho\})

(9)

\displaystyle+\frac{\Gamma_{R}}{2}f_{R}(\varepsilon_{2})(2a_{2}^{{\dagger}}\rho a_{2}-\{a_{2}a_{2}^{{\dagger}},\rho\}).

where the braces here represents anti commutations. The coupling rates read
\Gamma_{L}=2\pi D(\varepsilon_{1})|t_{L}|^{2}
and
\Gamma_{R}=2\pi D(\varepsilon_{2})|t_{R}|^{2}
. In addition,
f_{L}(\varepsilon_{1})
and
f_{R}(\varepsilon_{2})
represent the Fermi-Dirac distribution functions
f_{L}(\varepsilon_{1})=\frac{1}{e^{(\varepsilon_{1}-\mu_{L})/k_{B}T}+1}
and
f_{R}(\varepsilon_{2})=\frac{1}{e^{(\varepsilon_{2}-\mu_{R})/k_{B}T}+1}
with Boltzmann constant
k_{B}
and the temperature of the environment
T
.

We solve equation of motion of the system Eq.(

,
|01\rangle
,
|10\rangle
and

Floquet theory approach
-
In the end of this work, we would like to compare our time dependent Hamiltonian method with Floquet effective Hamiltonian method. When shaken frequency
\omega
is much larger than other energy scale, Floquet theory can be used to deal with the time dependent Hamiltonian. According to the Floquet theory, the equality
H_{eff}=\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}H(t^{\prime})dt^{\prime}
is satisfied, where
T=\frac{2\pi}{\omega}
. This process gives us an effective Hamiltonian
H_{eff}
,

\displaystyle H_{eff}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{l=1}^{2}\varepsilon_{l}n_{l}-J_{eff}(a_{1}^{{\dagger}}a_{2}+a_{2}^{{\dagger}}a_{1})+\sum_{\alpha,k}\mu_{\alpha k}n_{\alpha k}

(12)

\displaystyle-\sum_{k}(t_{eff}^{L}a_{1}^{{\dagger}}a_{Lk}+t_{eff}^{R}a_{2}^{{\dagger}}a_{Rk}+H.c.),

where
J_{eff}=JJ_{0}(W_{2}-W_{1})
,
t_{eff}^{L}=t_{L}J_{0}(W_{1})
,
t_{eff}^{R}=t_{R}J_{0}(W_{2})
and
W_{l}=\frac{F_{\omega}}{\hbar\omega}(x_{l}\cos(\alpha)+y_{l}\sin(\alpha))
with
l=1
,
2
correspondingly. Here,
J_{0}
is the zero order Bessel function.

From the effective Hamiltonian (
12
), we could derive the following mater equation which is similar to the equation of motion (
7
) except the second term on the right side of the equation,

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\varrho

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-i[\sum_{l=1}^{2}\varepsilon_{l}n_{l}-J_{eff}(a_{1}^{{\dagger}}a_{2}+a_{2}^{{\dagger}}a_{1}),\varrho]

(13)

\displaystyle+\mathcal{L}_{eff}^{L}\varrho+\mathcal{L}_{eff}^{R}\varrho,

where
\varrho
represents density matrix for atomic states evaluated from the effective Hamiltonian (
12
). The super operators are
\mathcal{L}_{eff}^{L}\varrho=\frac{\Gamma_{eff}^{L}}{2}(1-f_{L}(\varepsilon_{1}))(2a_{1}\varrho a_{1}^{{\dagger}}-\{a_{1}^{{\dagger}}a_{1},\varrho\})+\frac{\Gamma_{eff}^{L}}{2}f_{L}(\varepsilon_{1})(2a_{1}^{{\dagger}}\varrho a_{1}-\{a_{1}a_{1}^{{\dagger}},\varrho\})
and
\mathcal{L}_{eff}^{R}\varrho=\frac{\Gamma_{eff}^{R}}{2}(1-f_{R}(\varepsilon_{2}))(2a_{2}\varrho a_{2}^{{\dagger}}-\{a_{2}^{{\dagger}}a_{2},\varrho\})+\frac{\Gamma_{eff}^{R}}{2}f_{R}(\varepsilon_{2})(2a_{2}^{{\dagger}}\varrho a_{2}-\{a_{2}a_{2}^{{\dagger}},\varrho\})
.
Here, we have
\Gamma_{eff}^{L}=\Gamma_{L}J_{0}^{2}(W_{1})
and
\Gamma_{eff}^{R}=\Gamma_{R}J_{0}^{2}(W_{2})
.

The Floquet effective Hamiltonian approach requires shaking (driving) frequency is large enough compared with other energy scales. Indeed, Fig.
6
shows that the Floquet effective Hamiltonian approach consists with time-dependent Hamiltonian approach in the high frequency regime,
\omega\gg J
. In the low frequency region, difference between the two methods become very large. Interestingly, in the low frequency region, the Floquet effective Hamiltonian approach weakly reflect a few insulation points. However, in the limit of shaking frequency tends to zero, the Floquet effective Hamiltonian approach gives rise to infinitely large current value. In contrast, the time-dependent Hamiltonian approach offers frequently changing current. The time-dependent Hamiltonian approach developed here should be more general protocol to deal with dynamics of the shaken quantum system.

Conclusions
-
Using the time-dependent Hamiltonian numerical method, we studied shaken optical lattice transistor within low shaking frequency regime. Abrupt drop of atomic current was predicted under external chemical potential bias and optical potential shaking. We find the current drop is originated from coherence of the atom states in optical wells. Time dependent phase term in the Hamiltonian plays important role for this coherent process. The present work offers supplemental research of the shaken optical lattice in low shaking frequency band.

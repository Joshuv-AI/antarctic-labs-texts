1.
Introduction and statement of main results

Consider the differential equation,

(1)

f^{\prime\prime}+A(z)f^{\prime}+B(z)f=0

where
A(z)
and
B(z)(\not\equiv 0)
are entire functions. Then, it is well known that all solutions of equation (
1
) are entire functions(see
[
9
]
).

Definition 1
.

The order of growth
\rho(f)
and lower order of growth
\mu(f)
of an entire function
f
are defined by

\displaystyle{\rho(f)=\limsup\limits_{r\to\infty}\displaystyle{\frac{\log^{+}\log^{+}{M(r,f)}}{\log{r}}}}

\displaystyle{\mu(f)=\liminf\limits_{r\to\infty}\displaystyle{\frac{\log^{+}\log^{+}{M(r,f)}}{\log{r}}}}

respectively, where
M(r,f)=max_{|z|=r}|f(z)|
.

Definition 2
.

Exponent of convergence of zeros of function
f
is defined by

\displaystyle{\lambda(f)=inf\{\alpha>0\displaystyle{|}\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}|a_{n}|^{-\alpha}<\infty\}},

where
(a_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a sequence of non-zero complex numbers(not-necessarily distinct) with
\lim_{n\to\infty}a_{n}=\infty
.

If
A(z)
and
B(z)
are polynomials, then all solutions of equation (
1
) are of finite order(see
[
14
]
). Moreover, if either of
A(z)
and
B(z)
or both are entire transcendental function, then equation (
1
) have atleast one solution of infinite order(see
[
4
]
). Following classical result is summary of
[
6
]
and
[
8
]
.

Theorem 1
.

Suppose that
A(z)
and
B(z)
are entire functions satisfying one of the following conditions:

(i)

[
6
]

\rho(A)<\rho(B)
;

(ii)

[
6
]

A(z)
is a polynomial and
B(z)
is a transcendental entire function;

(iii)

[
8
]

\rho(B)<\rho(A)\leq\frac{1}{2}
.

Then every non-trivial solution of equation (1) are of infinite order.

Following are the examples of differential equations having finite order solutions when
\rho(B)\leq\rho(A)
.

Example 1
.

In the differential equation
f^{\prime\prime}+(e^{z}-1)f^{\prime}+e^{z}f=0
,
f(z)=e^{z}
is a non-trivial solution of order
1
and order of both coefficients are equal.

Example 2
.

The differential equation
f^{\prime\prime}+(ze^{z}+z+1)f^{\prime}+zf=0
has a solution
f=e^{-z}+1
.

Above examples shows the differential equation (
1
) has finite order solution when
\rho(B)\leq\rho(A)
.
Consider
A(z)=h_{1}(z)e^{P_{1}(z)}
and
B(z)=h_{0}(z)e^{P_{0}(z)}
, where
h_{1}(z)
and
h_{0}(z)
are transcendental entire functions;
P_{1}(z)
and
P_{0}(z)
are polynomials satisfying
\rho(h_{1})<\rho(h_{0})
. Gundersen
[

]
proved that all non-trivial solutions are of infinite order when
\deg P_{1}\neq\deg P_{0}
. What if
\deg P_{1}=\deg P_{0}
?

Example 3
.

f^{\prime\prime}(z)-z^{2}e^{z}+ze^{z}f=0
has finite order non-trivial solution
f(z)=2z
.

In example 3, we have
\rho(A)=\rho(B)=\deg(z)=1
, where
\lambda(A)<\rho(A)
and
\lambda(B)<\rho(B)
. Let

(2)

P_{1}(z)=a_{n}z^{n}+a_{n-1}z^{n-1}+\cdots+a_{0}

(3)

Lemma 1

[

\rho<\infty

0

so that for all z satisfying
\arg z=\psi_{0}
and

and for all

(5)

\left|\frac{f^{(k)}(z)}{f^{(j)}(z)}\right|\leq|z|^{(k-j)(\rho-1+\epsilon))}.

(ii)

(iii)

\left|\frac{f^{(k)}(z)}{f^{(j)}(z)}\right|\leq|z|^{(k-j)(\rho+\epsilon)}.

Besicovitch’s
\cos\pi\rho

M(r,f)=\max\{|f|:|z|=r\}
. Barry

2
]
proved this result for

Lemma 2

[

Assume that

\overline{\log dens}F\geq 1-\frac{\mu(f)}{\beta}

[

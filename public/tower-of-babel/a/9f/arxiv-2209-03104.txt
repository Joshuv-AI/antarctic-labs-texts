On Multiple L p L_{p} -curvilinear-Brunn-Minkowski inequalities
Michael Roysdon, Sudan Xing

Abstract
. We construct the extension of the curvilinear summation for bounded Borel measurable sets to the L p L_{p} space for multiple power parameter α ¯ = ( α 1 , ⋯ , α n + 1 ) \bar{\alpha}=(\alpha_{1},\cdots,\alpha_{n+1}) when p > 0 p>0 . Based on this L p , α ¯ L_{p,\bar{\alpha}} -curvilinear summation for sets and concept of compression of sets, the L p , α ¯ L_{p,\bar{\alpha}} -curvilinear-Brunn-Minkowski inequality for bounded Borel measurable sets and its normalized version are established. Furthermore, by utilizing the hypo-graphs for functions, we enact a brand new proof of L p , α ¯ L_{p,\bar{\alpha}} Borell-Brascamp-Lieb inequality, as well as its normalized version, for functions containing the special case of L p L_{p} Borell-Brascamp-Lieb inequality through the L p , α ¯ L_{p,\bar{\alpha}} -curvilinear-Brunn-Minkowski inequality for sets. Moreover, we propose the multiple power L p , α ¯ L_{p,\bar{\alpha}} -supremal-convolution for two functions together with its properties. Last but not least, we introduce the definition of the surface area originated from the variation formula of measure in terms of the L p , α ¯ L_{p,\bar{\alpha}} -curvilinear summation for sets as well as L p , α ¯ L_{p,\bar{\alpha}} -supremal-convolution for functions together with their corresponding Minkowski type inequalities and isoperimetric inequalities for p ≥ 1 , p\geq 1, etc.

(1) Choose any point

\displaystyle M_{p,\alpha}^{(t,\lambda)}(a,b)

,
L_{2}
, are mutually almost disjoint, and we see that

V_{1}(K)=V_{1}(K_{1}\cup K_{2})=V_{1}(K_{1})+V_{1}(K_{2})
and

V_{1}(L)=V_{1}(L_{1}\cup L_{2})=V_{1}(L_{1})+V_{1}(L_{2}).

Denote
k_{1},k_{2},
and
l_{1},l_{2}
, the number of intervals in
K_{1},K_{2}
, and
L_{1},L_{2}
, respectively. Without loss of generality, we chose
k,l
satisfying conditions below:

k_{1}+l_{1}\leq m+n-1,\quad k_{2}+l_{2}\leq m+n-1,

and

\frac{V_{1}(K_{1})}{V_{1}(L_{1})}=\frac{V_{1}(K_{2})}{V_{1}(L_{2})}=r>0.

We obtain by definition of
L_{p,\alpha}
-curvilinear summation for sets in (
2.2
) that

\displaystyle(1-t)\times_{p,\alpha}K+_{p,\alpha}t\times_{p,\alpha}L

\displaystyle=\bigcup_{0<\lambda<1}\left(\bigcup_{i,j=1}^{2}\left\{M_{p,\alpha}^{(t,\lambda)}(u,v):(u,v)\in K_{i}\times L_{j}\right\}\right)

\displaystyle\supset\bigcup_{0<\lambda<1}\bigcup_{i=1}^{2}\left\{M_{p,\alpha}^{(t,\lambda)}(u,v):(u,v)\in K_{i}\times L_{i}\right\}

\displaystyle=\bigcup_{i=1}^{2}\left\{(1-t)\times_{p,\alpha}K_{i}+_{p,\alpha}t\times_{p,\alpha}L_{i}\right\},

where the final union is an almost disjoint union. Further applying the inductive step, we observe by (
3.2
) that

\begin{split}&\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!V_{1}((1-t)\times_{p,\alpha}K+_{p,\alpha}t\times_{p,\alpha}L)\\
&\geq V_{1}(\bigcup_{i=1}^{2}(1-t)\times_{p,\alpha}K_{i}+_{p,\alpha}t\times_{p,\alpha}L_{i})\\
&=V_{1}((1-t)\times_{p,\alpha}K_{1}+_{p,\alpha}t\times_{p,\alpha}L_{1})+V_{1}((1-t)\times_{p,\alpha}K_{2}+_{p,\alpha}t\times_{p,\alpha}L_{2})\\
&\geq\sup_{0<\lambda<1}\left\{M_{p,\alpha}^{(t,\lambda)}(V_{1}(K_{1}),V_{1}(L_{1}))+M_{p,\alpha}^{(t,\lambda)}(V_{1}(K_{2}),V_{1}(L_{2}))\right\}.\end{split}

(3.3)

For each fixed
\lambda\in(0,1)
, we notice using the assumptions placed on
k,l
, we may write

\begin{split}&\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!M_{p,\alpha}^{(t,\lambda)}(V_{1}(K_{1}),V_{1}(L_{1}))+M_{p,\alpha}^{(t,\lambda)}(V_{1}(K_{2}),V_{1}(L_{2}))\\
&=\left[C_{p,\lambda,t}V_{1}(K_{1})^{\alpha}+D_{p,\lambda,t}V_{1}(L_{1})^{\alpha}\right]^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}+\left[C_{p,\lambda,t}V_{1}(K_{2})^{\alpha}+D_{p,\lambda,t}V_{1}(L_{2})^{\alpha}\right]^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}\\
&=(V_{1}(K_{1})+V_{1}(K_{2}))\left[C_{p,\lambda,t}+D_{p,\lambda,t}r^{\alpha}\right]^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}\\
&=\left[C_{p,\lambda,t}(V_{1}(K_{1})+V_{1}(K_{2}))^{\alpha}+D_{p,\lambda,t}(V_{1}(L_{1})+V_{1}(L_{2}))^{\alpha}\right]^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}\\
&=M_{p,\alpha}^{(t,\lambda)}(V_{1}(K),V_{1}(L)).\end{split}

(3.4)

It follows from (
3.3
) and (
3.4
), together with the extreme point of the function
f(\lambda)=[(1-\lambda)^{\frac{1}{q}}r+\lambda^{\frac{1}{q}}s]^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}
with
r,s>0
, on
(0,1)
depending on the choice of
\alpha\leq 1

[
49
,
50
]
that (
3.1
) holds for
N>2.

Therefore based on Steps 1 and 2, we obtain the desired result.

Recall the definition of compression for a bounded Borel set
\tilde{A}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}
,

\tilde{A}=\{(x,r)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}\colon 0\leq r\leq V_{1}(A\cap(x+\mathbb{R}_{+})),A\cap(x+\mathbb{R}_{+})\neq\emptyset\}.

(3.5)

It is easy to check that the compression operator is volume invariant,
V_{n+1}(A)=V_{n+1}(\tilde{A})
. In the following, we first further analyze another monotone properties under compression operation
A\mapsto\tilde{A}
in terms of power parameters, particularly, for the
L_{p,\alpha}
-curvilinear summation for sets, i.e., the case when
\bar{\alpha}=(1,\dots,1,\alpha)
,
+_{p,\alpha},+^{p,\alpha}
, etc. Then based on this property, we will present the detailed proof of
L_{p,\alpha}
-curvilinear-Brunn-Minkowski inequality for sets in
n+1
-dimensional setting.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
p\geq 1
,
t\in(0,1)
, and
\alpha\in[-\infty,1]
. Let
K,L\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}
be bounded Borel sets such that
V_{n+1}(K),V_{n+1}(L)>0
. Then

V_{n+1}((1-t)\times_{p,\alpha}A+_{p,\alpha}t\times_{p,\alpha}B)\geq V_{n+1}((1-t)\times_{p,\alpha}\tilde{A}+_{p,\alpha}t\times_{p,\alpha}\tilde{B}).

Proof.

Based on the definition for compression in (
3.5
), for any set
C\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}
, denote the segment projection on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
as

C(z)=\{r\geq 0\colon(z,r)\in C\}=C\cap(z+\mathbb{R}_{+}),\quad z\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Firstly, we verify the statement for sets
A,B\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}
of the form

A=\bigcup_{m=1}^{M}A_{m},\quad B=\bigcup_{l=1}^{L}B_{l},

where the unions are almost disjoint, and
A_{m},B_{l}
are rectangular boxes with sides parallel to the coordinate axes. More precisely, we can write

A_{m}=\left(\Pi_{i=1}^{n}[a_{im},c_{im}]\right)\times[a,c],\quad B_{l}=\left(\Pi_{i=1}^{n}[b_{il},d_{il}]\right)\times[b,d]

for appropriate choices of constants
a_{im}<c_{im}
,
b_{il}<d_{il}
and
a,b,c,d\geq 0
.

Applying Fubini’s theorem, the volume of
n+1
-dimensional set can be interpreted as the integral with respect to the segment function, i.e.,

\displaystyle V_{n+1}((1-t)\times_{p,\alpha}A+_{p,\alpha}t\times_{p,\alpha}B)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}V_{[(1-t)\times_{p,\alpha}A+_{p,\alpha}t\times_{p,\alpha}B]}(z)dz;

\displaystyle V_{n+1}((1-t)\times_{p,\alpha}\tilde{A}+_{p,\alpha}t\times_{p,\alpha}\tilde{B})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}V_{[(1-t)\times_{p,\alpha}\tilde{A}+_{p,\alpha}t\times_{p,\alpha}\tilde{B}]}(z)dz.

Based on these two identities, it suffices to show the inequality related to the segment functions

V_{[(1-t)\times_{p,\alpha}A+_{p,\alpha}t\times_{p,\alpha}B]}(z)\geq V_{[(1-t)\times_{p,\alpha}\tilde{A}+_{p,\alpha}t\times_{p,\alpha}\tilde{B}]}(z)

holds for almost every
z\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Observe by definition of
L_{p,\alpha}
curvilinear summation in (
2.1
) that

\begin{split}[(1-t)\times_{p,\alpha}A+_{p,\alpha}t\times_{p,\alpha}B](z)&=\{r\geq 0\colon(z,r)\in(1-t)\times_{p,\alpha}A+_{p,\alpha}t\times_{p,\alpha}B\}\\
&=\left\{M_{p,\alpha}^{(t,\lambda)}(a,b):\left(C_{p,\lambda,t}x+D_{p,\lambda,t}y,M_{p,\alpha}^{(t,\lambda)}(a,b)\right)\text{ such that }\right.\\
&\left.z=C_{p,\lambda,t}x+D_{p,\lambda,t}y\text{ with }(x,a)\in A,(y,b)\in B,0<\lambda<1\right\}\\
&=\bigcup_{0<\lambda<1}\left\{\bigcup_{z=C_{p,\lambda,t}x+D_{p,\lambda,t}y}\left[C_{p,\lambda,t}\times_{\alpha}A(x)+_{\alpha}D_{p,\lambda,t}\times_{\alpha}B(y)\right]\right\}.\end{split}

(3.6)

Analogously, its compression version has the following expression

\begin{split}[(1-t)\times_{p,\alpha}\tilde{A}+_{p,\alpha}t\times_{p,\alpha}\tilde{B}](z)&=\bigcup_{0<\lambda<1}\left\{\bigcup_{z=C_{p,\lambda,t}x+D_{p,\lambda,t}y}\left[C_{p,\lambda,t}\times_{\alpha}\tilde{A}(x)+_{\alpha}D_{p,\lambda,t}\times_{\alpha}\tilde{B}(y)\right]\right\}\\
&=\bigcup_{0<\lambda<1}\left\{\bigcup_{z=C_{p,\lambda,t}x+D_{p,\lambda,t}y}\left[C_{p,\lambda,t}\times_{\alpha}\tilde{A}(\bar{x})+_{\alpha}D_{p,\lambda,t}\times_{\alpha}\tilde{B}(\bar{y})\right]\right\}\end{split}

(3.7)

for some
\bar{x},\bar{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

The fact that

\widetilde{A(x)}=\tilde{A}(x),
and
\widetilde{B(y)}=\tilde{B}(y),

together with the representations (
3.6
), (
3.7
), and Lemma
3.1
, yields

\displaystyle V_{[(1-t)\times_{p,\alpha}A+_{p,\alpha}t\times_{p,\alpha}B]}(z)

\displaystyle\geq V_{1}(\bigcup_{0<\lambda<1}\left[C_{p,\lambda,t}\times_{\alpha}A(\bar{x})+_{\alpha}D_{p,\lambda,t}\times_{\alpha}B(\bar{y})\right])

\displaystyle=V_{1}((1-t)\times_{p,\alpha}A(\bar{x})+_{p,\alpha}t\times_{p,\alpha}B(\bar{y}))

\displaystyle\geq\sup_{0<\lambda<1}M_{p,\alpha}^{(t,\lambda)}\left(V_{1}(\widetilde{A(\bar{x})}),V_{1}(\widetilde{B(\bar{y})})\right)

\displaystyle=\sup_{0<\lambda<1}M_{p,\alpha}^{(t,\lambda)}(V_{1}(\tilde{A}(\bar{x})),V_{1}(\tilde{B}(\bar{y})))

\displaystyle\geq V_{1}\Big(\bigcup_{0<\lambda<1}\left[C_{p,\lambda,t}\times_{\alpha}\tilde{A}(\bar{x})+_{\alpha}D_{p,\lambda,t}\times_{\alpha}\tilde{B}(\bar{y})\right]\Big)

\displaystyle=V_{[(1-t)\times_{p,\alpha}\tilde{A}+_{p,\alpha}t\times_{p,\alpha}\tilde{B}]}(z).

All in all, we see that

\displaystyle V_{n+1}((1-t)\times_{p,\alpha}A+_{p,\alpha}t\times_{p,\alpha}B)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}V_{[(1-t)\times_{p,\alpha}A+_{p,\alpha}t\times_{p,\alpha}B]}(z)dz

\displaystyle\geq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}V_{[(1-t)\times_{p,\alpha}\tilde{A}+_{p,\alpha}t\times_{p,\alpha}\tilde{B}]}(z)dz

\displaystyle=V_{1}((1-t)\times_{p,\alpha}\tilde{A}+_{p,\alpha}t\times_{p,\alpha}\tilde{B}),

as desired.

∎

Next we will need the following Hölder type inequalities for the generalized averages with
L_{p}
coefficients to prove the
L_{p,\alpha}
-curvilinear-Brunn-Minkowski inequality.
Let
p\geq 1
,
p^{-1}+q^{-1}=1
,
t,\lambda\in(0,1)
, and
a,b,c,d\geq 0
. Then

\begin{split}M_{p,\alpha}^{(t,\lambda)}(a,b)M_{p,\beta}^{(t,\lambda)}(c,d)\geq\begin{cases}M_{p,\gamma}^{(t,\lambda)}(ac,bd),&\text{if }\alpha+\beta\geq 0,\\
\min\left\{\left[C_{p,\lambda,t}\right]^{\frac{1}{\gamma}}ac,\left[D_{p,\lambda,t}\right]^{\frac{1}{\gamma}}bd\right\},&\text{if }\alpha+\beta,\alpha\cdot\beta<0,\end{cases}\end{split}

(3.8)

where
\gamma=(\alpha\beta)/(\alpha+\beta)
.
Here we give a brief proof of this inequality as follows. Assume that
\alpha+\beta\geq 0
, and
\alpha,\beta\neq 0
. The case for
\alpha+\beta\geq 0
comes from
[
50
]
.
We now handle the case when
\alpha+\beta,\alpha\cdot\beta<0
. Assume that

\left[C_{p,\lambda,t}\right]^{\frac{1}{\gamma}}ac\leq\left[D_{p,\lambda,t}\right]^{\frac{1}{\gamma}}bd,

and set

x=\frac{\left[D_{p,\lambda,t}\right]^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}b}{\left[C_{p,\lambda,t}\right]^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}a},\quad y=\frac{\left[D_{p,\lambda,t}\right]^{\frac{1}{\beta}}d}{\left[C_{p,\lambda,t}\right]^{\frac{1}{\beta}}c}.

Then proving (
3.8
) amounts to establishing the inequality

(1+x^{\alpha})^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}(1+y^{\beta})^{\frac{1}{\beta}}\geq 1

for
x\cdot y\geq 1
,
\alpha+\beta,\alpha\cdot\beta<0
, which origins from
[
59
, Page 296]
, as desired.

Based on the monotone properties of compression for
L_{p}
-curvilinear summations above, next we present the high dimensional
L_{p,\alpha}
-curvilinear-Brunn-Minkowski inequality, which extends both the classical curvilinear-Brunn-Minkowski inequality by Uhrin
[
57
]
, as well as, the
L_{p}
-Brunn-Minkowski inequality for non-convex sets proved by Lutwak, Yang, and Zhang
[
37
]
. The proof is processed through approximation which is also a typical method used in the proof for the Brunn-Minkowski inequality (see
[
56
]
).

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
p\geq 1
,
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=1
,
t\in(0,1)
, and
\alpha\in(-\infty,\infty)
. Suppose that
A,B\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}
are bounded Borel sets of positive measure such that

A=\tilde{A},B=\tilde{B}.

Then the following inequality holds:

\begin{split}&\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!V_{n+1}((1-t)\times_{p,\alpha}A+_{p,\alpha}t\times_{p,\alpha}B)\\
&\geq\begin{cases}M_{p\gamma}^{t}(V_{n+1}(A),V_{n+1}(B)),&\text{if }\alpha\geq-\frac{1}{n},\\
\sup_{0<\lambda<1}\min\left\{\left[C_{p,\lambda,t}\right]^{\frac{1}{\gamma}}V_{n+1}(A),\left[D_{p,\lambda,t}\right]^{\frac{1}{\gamma}}V_{n+1}(B)\right\},&\text{if }\alpha<-\frac{1}{n},\end{cases}\end{split}

(3.9)

where
\gamma=\frac{\alpha}{1+n\alpha}
.

Proof.

To begin with, we consider the box-type sets
A
and
B
with the form

A=\bigcup_{m=1}^{M}(A_{m}\times[0,a_{m}]),\quad B=\bigcup_{l=1}^{L}(B_{l}\times[0,b_{l}]),

where the
A_{m}
,
B_{l}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
are rectangular boxes with sides parallel to the coordinates axes, and the union are almost disjoint when
\alpha\geq-\frac{1}{n}
. The proof is similar to the 1-dimensional case by induction on
N=M+L
in two steps below.

Step 1:
Assume that
N=2\ (M=L=1)
. Then
A
and
B
are of the form

A=\left(\prod_{i=1}^{n}[a_{i},c_{i}]\right)\times[0,a],\quad B=\left(\prod_{i=1}^{n}[b_{i},d_{i}]\right)\times[0,b].

Notice that

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!(1-t)\times_{p,\alpha}A+_{p,\alpha}t\times_{p,\alpha}B

\displaystyle=\bigcup_{0<\lambda<1}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{n}\big[C_{p,\lambda,t}a_{i}+D_{p,\lambda,t}b_{i},C_{p,\lambda,t}c_{i}+D_{p,\lambda,t}d_{i}\big]\right)\times\left[0,M_{p,\alpha}^{(t,\lambda)}(a,b)\right]

\displaystyle=\bigcup_{0<\lambda<1}\left(C_{p,\lambda,t}\prod_{i=1}^{n}[a_{i},c_{i}]+D_{p,\lambda,t}\prod_{i=1}^{n}[b_{i},d_{i}]\right)\times\left[0,M_{p,\alpha}^{(t,\lambda)}(a,b)\right].

For each
\lambda\in(0,1)
, by applying Fubini’s theorem and the Brunn-Minkowski inequality in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
(
1.1
), we obtain

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!V_{n+1}\left(\left(C_{p,\lambda,t}\prod_{i=1}^{n}[a_{i},c_{i}]+D_{p,\lambda,t}\prod_{i=1}^{n}[b_{i},d_{i}]\right)\times\left[0,M_{p,\alpha}^{(t,\lambda)}(a,b)\right]\right)

\displaystyle=V_{n}\left(\left(C_{p,\lambda,t}\prod_{i=1}^{n}[a_{i},c_{i}]+D_{p,\lambda,t}\prod_{i=1}^{n}[b_{i},d_{i}]\right)\right)\cdot V_{1}(\left[0,M_{p,\alpha}^{(t,\lambda)}(a,b)\right])

\displaystyle\geq M_{p,\beta}^{(t,\lambda)}\left(V_{n}(\prod_{i=1}^{n}[a_{i},c_{i}]),V_{n}(\prod_{i=1}^{n}[b_{i},d_{i}])\right)\cdot M_{p,\alpha}^{(t,\lambda)}(a,b),

with
\beta=\frac{1}{n}
.

Therefore, we have

\displaystyle V_{n+1}\left((1-t)\times_{p,\alpha}A+_{p,\alpha}t\times_{p,\alpha}B\right)\geq\sup_{0<\lambda<1}\left\{M_{p,\beta}^{(t,\lambda)}\left(V_{n}(\prod_{i=1}^{n}[a_{i},c_{i}]),V_{n}\big(\prod_{i=1}^{n}[b_{i},d_{i}]\big)\right)\cdot M_{p,\alpha}^{(t,\lambda)}(a,b)\right\},

with
\beta=\frac{1}{n}
. Applying the Hölder type inequality (
3.8
) with the above choice of
\beta=\frac{1}{n}
yields the result in this case.

Step 2:
Assume that we have established the result in the case
M+L<N
for some
N>2
. Let
\bar{H}
be a coordinate hyperplane in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and set
H=\bar{H}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}
. We cut
A
and
B
by translates
H+x
and
H+y
for some
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Denote by
(H+x)^{-}
,
(H+x)^{+}
the closed half-spaces of
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}
determined by
x
, and similarly define
(H+y)^{-}
and
(H+y)^{+}
.
Consider the sets

\displaystyle A_{1}

\displaystyle=A\cap(H+x)^{-},\quad A_{2}=A\cap(H+x)^{+},

\displaystyle B_{1}

\displaystyle=B\cap(H+y)^{-},\quad B_{2}=B\cap(H+y)^{+}.

Notice that

A=A_{1}\cup A_{2},\quad B=B_{1}\cup B_{2},

where the unions are almost disjoint.
Denote by
m_{1},m_{2}
and
l_{1},l_{2}
the numbers of rectangular boxes comprising
A_{1},A_{2}
and
B_{1},B_{2}
, respectively. We can select
\bar{H}
,
x
and
y
in such a way that

m_{1}+l_{1}\leq M+L-1,\quad m_{2}+l_{2}\leq M+L-1,

and

\frac{V_{n+1}(A_{1})}{V_{n+1}(B_{1})}=\frac{V_{n+1}(A_{2})}{V_{n+1}(B_{2})}=r>0.

Notice

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!(1-t)\times_{p,\alpha}A+_{p,\alpha}t\times_{p,\alpha}B

\displaystyle=\bigcup_{0<\lambda<1}\left(\bigcup_{i,j=1}^{2}\left\{\left(C_{p,\lambda,t}x+D_{p,\lambda,t}y,M_{p,\alpha}^{(t,\lambda)}(a,b)\right)\colon((x,a),(y,b))\in A_{i}\times B_{j}\right\}\right)

\displaystyle\supset\bigcup_{0<\lambda<1}\left(\bigcup_{i=1}^{2}\left\{\left(C_{p,\lambda,t}x+D_{p,\lambda,t}y,M_{p,\alpha}^{(t,\lambda)}(a,b)\right)\colon((x,a),(y,b))\in A_{i}\times B_{i}\right\}\right)

\displaystyle=\bigcup_{i=1}^{2}(1-t)\times_{p,\alpha}A_{i}+_{p,\alpha}t\times_{p,\alpha}B_{i}

where the last union is almost disjoint.

By taking volumes, and using the inductive hypothesis, we see that

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!V_{n+1}((1-t)\times_{p,\alpha}A+_{p,\alpha}t\times_{p,\alpha}B)

\displaystyle\geq V_{n+1}\left(\bigcup_{i=1}^{2}(1-t)\times_{p,\alpha}A_{i}+_{p,\alpha}t\times_{p,\alpha}B_{i}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{2}V_{n+1}((1-t)\times_{p,\alpha}A_{i}+_{p,\alpha}t\times_{p,\alpha}B_{i})

\displaystyle\geq\begin{cases}\sup_{0<\lambda<1}\sum_{i=1}^{2}M_{p,\gamma}^{(t,\lambda)}(V_{n+1}(A_{i}),V_{n+1}(B_{i})),&\text{if }\alpha\geq-\frac{1}{n},\\
\sup_{0<\lambda<1}\sum_{i=1}^{2}\min\left\{\left[C_{p,\lambda,t}\right]^{\frac{1}{\gamma}}V_{n+1}(A_{i}),\left[D_{p,\lambda,t}\right]^{\frac{1}{\gamma}}V_{n+1}(B_{i})\right\},&\text{if }\alpha<-\frac{1}{n},\end{cases}

where
\gamma=\frac{\alpha}{1+n\alpha}
.

For each
\lambda\in(0,1)
, the assumptions on
m_{i},l_{i}
,
i=1,2
yield, for
\alpha\geq-\frac{1}{n}
,

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{2}M_{p,\gamma}^{(t,\lambda)}(V_{n+1}(A_{i}),V_{n+1}(B_{i}))

\displaystyle=(V_{n+1}(A_{1})+V_{n+1}(A_{2}))M_{p,\gamma}^{(t,\lambda)}(1,r)

\displaystyle=M_{p,\gamma}^{(t,\lambda)}\big(V_{n+1}(A_{1})+V_{n+1}(A_{2}),V_{n+1}(B_{1})+V_{n+1}(B_{2})\big)

\displaystyle=M_{p,\gamma}^{(t,\lambda)}\big(V_{n+1}(A),V_{n+1}(B)\big).

With the maximal point for
0<\lambda<1
, we obtain the inequality for
\alpha\geq-\frac{1}{n}
in (
3.9
).

A similar argument works for the case
\alpha<-\frac{1}{n}
. This completes the proof for sets which are comprised of unions of almost disjoint rectangular boxes in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and an interval in
\mathbb{R}_{+}
.
To complete the proof, we use standard density considerations (see, for example,
[
56
,
59
]
).

Combining Theorems
3.2
and
3.3
, we obtain the following corollary, which shows that, Theorem

is indeed a sharper form of the

Corollary 3.4
.

Let
p\geq 1
,
p^{-1}+q^{-1}=1
,
t\in(0,1)
, and
-\frac{1}{n}\leq\alpha\leq 1
. Then, for any pair of bounded Borel sets
A,B\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}
, each of positive volume, one has

\begin{split}V_{n+1}((1-t)\times_{p,\alpha}A+_{p,\alpha}t\times_{p,\alpha}B)&\geq V_{n+1}((1-t)\times_{p,\alpha}\tilde{A}+_{p,\alpha}t\times_{p,\alpha}\tilde{B})\\
&\geq M_{p\gamma}^{t}(V_{n+1}(A),V_{n+1}(B)),\end{split}

where
\gamma=\frac{\alpha}{1+n\alpha}
.

Naturally, by repeating the above Theorem with respect to the multiple power parameters for
L_{p,\alpha}
-curvilinear-Brunn-Minkowski inequality, we obtain the following theorem in terms of the
L_{p,\bar{\alpha}}
-curvilinear-Brunn-Minkowski inequality for sets with the vector power
\bar{\alpha}=(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n+1})
.

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
p\geq 1
,
t\in(0,1)
, and
\bar{\alpha}=(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n+1})
with
\alpha_{i}\in(0,1]
for each
i=1,\dots,n
. Suppose that
A,B\subset(\mathbb{R}_{+})^{n+1}
are bounded Borel sets of positive measure such that
A=\tilde{A}
and
B=\tilde{B}
. Then the following inequality holds:

\begin{split}&\!\!\!\!V_{n+1}((1-t)\otimes_{p,\bar{\alpha}}A\oplus_{p,\bar{\alpha}}t\otimes_{p,\bar{\alpha}}B)\\
&\geq\begin{cases}M_{p\gamma}^{t}(V_{n+1}(A),V_{n+1}(B)),&\text{if }\alpha_{n+1}\geq-\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}^{-1}\right)^{-1},\\
\sup_{0<\lambda<1}\min\left\{\left[C_{p,\lambda,t}\right]^{\frac{1}{\gamma}}V_{n+1}(A),\left[D_{p,\lambda,t}\right]^{\frac{1}{\gamma}}V_{n+1}(B)\right\},&\text{if }\alpha_{n+1}<-\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}^{-1}\right)^{-1},\end{cases}\end{split}

where

\gamma=\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}\alpha_{i}^{-1}\right)^{-1}.

Remark 3.6
.

If
\bar{\alpha}=(1,\cdots,1)
, the
L_{p,\bar{\alpha}}
-curvilinear summation recovers the
L_{p}
Minkowski summation by Lutwak, Yang and Zhang
[
37
]
and the above
L_{p,\bar{\alpha}}
-curvilinear-Brunn-Minkowski inequality goes back to the
L_{p}
Brunn-Minkowski inequality for sets.

3.2.
Normalized
L_{p}
-curvilinear-Brunn-Minkowski inequalities

In the following we will examine the normalized version of the
L_{p,\bar{\alpha}}
-curvilinear-Brunn-Minkowski inequality in previous Subsection.

Although all results in this section may be formulated for general bounded Borel sets in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}
, due to standard density considerations, it suffices to establish all results for compact
F_{\sigma}
-sets. To this end, we assume that
A,B\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}
of the form

A=\bigcup_{m=1}^{M}(\bar{A}_{m}\times A_{m}),\quad B=\bigcup_{l=1}^{M}(\bar{B}_{l}\times B_{l}),\quad M\leq\infty,

where
\bar{A}_{m},\bar{B}_{m}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
A_{m},B_{m}\subset\mathbb{R}_{+}
are compact sets for all
m,l
. Moreover, we have the following identities by definitions of
L_{p,\alpha}
operations

\begin{split}&(1-t)\times_{p,\alpha}A+_{p,\alpha}t\times_{p,\alpha}B=\bigcup_{m,l=1}^{M}\bigcup_{\lambda\in(0,1)\cap\mathbb{Q}}\big(C_{p,\lambda,t}\times_{\alpha}(\bar{A}_{m}\times A_{m})+_{\alpha}D_{p,\lambda,t}\times_{\alpha}(\bar{B}_{l}\times B_{l})\big);\\
&(1-t)\times^{p,\alpha}A+^{p,\alpha}t\times^{p,\alpha}B=\bigcap_{m,l=1}^{M}\bigcup_{\lambda\in(0,1)\cap\mathbb{Q}}\big(C_{p,\lambda,t}\times^{\alpha}(\bar{A}_{m}\times A_{m})+^{\alpha}D_{p,\lambda,t}\times^{\alpha}(\bar{B}_{l}\times B_{l})\big),\end{split}

where
\mathbb{Q}
denotes the set of all rational numbers.
It is not difficult to see that each
C_{p,\lambda,t}\times_{\alpha}(\bar{A}_{m}\times A_{m})+_{\alpha}D_{p,\lambda,t}\times_{\alpha}(\bar{B}_{l}\times B_{l})
and each
C_{p,\lambda,t}\times^{\alpha}(\bar{A}_{m}\times A_{m})+^{\alpha}D_{p,\lambda,t}\times^{\alpha}(\bar{B}_{l}\times B_{l})
are compact sets, and consequently,
(1-t)\times_{p,\alpha}A+_{p,\alpha}t\times_{p,\alpha}B
and
(1-t)\times^{p,\alpha}A+^{p,\alpha}t\times^{p,\alpha}B
are bounded
F_{\sigma}
-subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}
.

Next we consider the generalized section function and the corresponding compression.
Let
\bar{H}=H\times\mathbb{R}_{+}
for
H\subset G_{n,k}
when
k\in\{0,1,\cdots,n\}
. Fix any bounded Borel measurable subset
A\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}
, and the generalized segment function with respect to
H
on
H^{\perp},

V_{A,H}\colon H^{\perp}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
is defined by

V_{A,H}(y)=V_{k+1}(A\cap(\bar{H}+y)).

When
k=0
,
H=\{o\}
, and
H^{\perp}=\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
V_{A,H}(\cdot)
recovers
V_{A}(\cdot)
, the classical segment function.
It is easy to see that the function
V_{A,H}
is integrable and bounded.

Denote the
optimal norm
\|V_{A,H}\|_{\infty}
, normalized super-level set
C_{r}(V_{A,H})
for
V_{A,H}
, and the normalized compression
A_{H}
, correspondingly as

\begin{split}&\|V_{A,H}\|_{\infty}=\sup_{y\in H^{\perp}}V_{A,H}(y),\\
&C_{r}(V_{A,H})=\left\{y\in H^{\perp}\colon V_{A,H}(y)\geq r\|V_{A,H}\|_{\infty}\right\},\quad 0\leq r\leq 1,\\
&A_{H}=\left\{(y,r)\in H^{\perp}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}\colon 0\leq r\leq\frac{V_{A,H}(y)}{\|V_{A,H}\|_{\infty}},\ A\cap(\bar{H}+y)\neq\emptyset\right\}.\end{split}

(3.10)

When
k=0
, we denote the above as
\|V_{A_{0}}\|
,
C_{r}(V_{A_{0}})
and
A_{0}
, respectively.

Proposition 3.7
.

Let
p\geq 1
,
p^{-1}+q^{-1}=1
,
t\in(0,1)
, and
\alpha\in(-\infty,\infty)
. Then, for any bounded
F_{\sigma}
-sets
A,B\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}
of positive volume,

\begin{split}V_{n+1}((1-t)\times_{p,\alpha}A_{0}+_{p,\alpha}t\times_{p,\alpha}B_{0})&\geq V_{n+1}((1-t)\times_{p,-\infty}A_{0}+_{p,-\infty}t\times_{p,-\infty}B_{0})\\
&\geq\int_{0}^{1}V_{n}((1-t)\cdot_{p}C_{r}\left(V_{A_{0}}\right)+_{p}t\cdot_{p}C_{r}\left(V_{B_{0}}\right))dr.\end{split}

Proof.

All of the sets
A_{0},B_{0},C_{r}(V_{A_{0}})
, and
C_{r}(V_{B_{0}})
are
F_{\sigma}
-sets, and
A_{0},B_{0}
satisfy the compression condition

A_{0}=\tilde{A}_{0},\quad B_{0}=\tilde{B}_{0}.

Let
z\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
be such that
[(1-t)\times_{p,-\infty}A_{0}+_{p,-\infty}t\times_{p,-\infty}B_{0}]\cap(\mathbb{R}_{+}\cap z)\neq\emptyset
. Then we see that

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\![(1-t)\times_{p,-\infty}A_{0}+_{p,-\infty}t\times_{p,-\infty}B_{0}]\cap(\mathbb{R}_{+}+z)

\displaystyle=[(1-t)\times_{p,-\infty}\tilde{A}_{0}+_{p,-\infty}t\times_{p,-\infty}\tilde{B}_{0}]\cap(\mathbb{R}_{+}+z)

\displaystyle=\left(\bigcup_{\lambda\in\mathbb{Q}\cap(0,1)}C_{p,\lambda,t}\times_{-\infty}\tilde{A}_{0}+_{-\infty}D_{p,\lambda,t}\times_{-\infty}\tilde{B}_{0}\right)\cap(\mathbb{R}_{+}+z)

\displaystyle=\bigcup_{\lambda\in\mathbb{Q}\cap(0,1)}\left\{\left[C_{p,\lambda,t}\times_{-\infty}\tilde{A}_{0}+_{-\infty}D_{p,\lambda,t}\times_{-\infty}\tilde{B}_{0}\right]\cap(\mathbb{R}_{+}+z)\right\}

\displaystyle=\bigcup_{\lambda\in\mathbb{Q}\cap(0,1)}\left[\left\{\left(C_{p,\lambda,t}x+D_{p,\lambda,t}y,\min\{a,b\}\right)\colon(x,a)\in\tilde{A}_{0},(y,b)\in\tilde{B}_{0}\right\}\cap(\mathbb{R}_{+}+z)\right]

\displaystyle=\bigcup_{\lambda\in\mathbb{Q}\cap(0,1)}\bigcup_{z=C_{p,\lambda,t}x+D_{p,\lambda,t}y}\left\{\left(z,0\right)\times\left[0,\min\left\{\frac{V_{A_{0}}(x)}{\|V_{A_{0}}\|_{\infty}},\frac{V_{B_{0}}(y)}{\|V_{B_{0}}\|_{\infty}}\right\}\right]\right\}.

By taking volumes, we see that, for each such
z\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!V_{1}([(1-t)\times_{p,-\infty}A_{0}+_{p,-\infty}t\times_{p,-\infty}B_{0}]\cap(\mathbb{R}_{+}+z))

\displaystyle=\sup_{0<\lambda<1}\left[\sup_{z=C_{p,\lambda,t}x+D_{p,\lambda,t}y}\min\left\{\frac{V_{A_{0}}(x)}{\|V_{A_{0}}\|_{\infty}},\frac{V_{B_{0}}(y)}{\|V_{B_{0}}\|_{\infty}}\right\}\right],

which implies the inclusion relationship

\begin{split}C_{r}\left(V_{(1-t)\times_{p,-\infty}A_{0}+_{p,-\infty}t\times_{p,-\infty}B_{0}}\right)\supset(1-t)\cdot_{p}C_{r}\left(V_{A_{0}}\right)+_{p}t\cdot_{p}C_{r}\left(V_{B_{0}}\right).\end{split}

(3.11)

Fubini’s theorem together with the inclusion (
3.11
) yields

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!V_{n+1}((1-t)\times_{p,-\infty}A_{0}+_{p,-\infty}t\times_{p,-\infty}B_{0})

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}V_{1}(\left[(1-t)\times_{p,-\infty}A_{0}+_{p,-\infty}t\times_{p,-\infty}B_{0}\right]\cap(\mathbb{R}_{+}+z))dz

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}V_{n}\left(C_{r}\left(V_{(1-t)\times_{p,-\infty}A_{0}+_{p,-\infty}t\times_{p,-\infty}B_{0}}\right)\right)dr

\displaystyle\geq\int_{0}^{1}V_{n}\left((1-t)\cdot_{p}C_{r}\left(V_{A_{0}}\right)+_{p}t\cdot_{p}C_{r}\left(V_{B_{0}}\right)\right)dr.

Finally, utilizing the monotone properties in Proposition
2.2
(1), we have

\displaystyle V_{n+1}((1-t)\times_{p,\alpha}A_{0}+_{p,\alpha}t\times_{p,\alpha}B_{0})

\displaystyle\geq V_{n+1}((1-t)\times_{p,-\infty}A_{0}+_{p,-\infty}t\times_{p,-\infty}B_{0})

\displaystyle\geq\int_{0}^{1}V_{n}((1-t)\cdot_{p}C_{r}\left(V_{A_{0}}\right)+_{p}t\cdot_{p}C_{r}\left(V_{B_{0}}\right))dr,

completing the proof.
∎

Next we will obtain the following

-curvilinear-Brunn-Minkowski inequality for sets when

.

Theorem 3.8
.

Let
p\geq 1
,
p^{-1}+q^{-1}=1
, and
t\in(0,1)
. Suppose that
A,B\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}
are bounded
F_{\sigma}
-sets having positive volume such that
A=\tilde{A}
and
B=\tilde{B}
. Then, for any
\alpha\in(-\infty,\infty)

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!V_{n+1}((1-t)\times_{p,\alpha}A+_{p,\alpha}t\times_{p,\alpha}B)\cdot M_{-p\alpha}^{t}\left(\|V_{A}\|_{\infty}^{-1},\|V_{B}\|_{\infty}^{-1}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\int_{0}^{1}V_{n}((1-t)\cdot_{p}C_{r}\left(V_{A}\right)+_{p}t\cdot_{p}C_{r}\left(V_{B}\right))dr.

Proof.

Arguing as in proof of Proposition
3.7
and using the fact that
A=\tilde{A},B=\tilde{B}
, we see that, for each
z\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that

[(1-t)\times_{p,\alpha}A+_{p,\alpha}t\times_{p,\alpha}B]\cap(\mathbb{R}_{+}+z)\neq\emptyset,

and

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\![(1-t)\times_{p,\alpha}A+_{p,\alpha}t\times_{p,\alpha}B]\cap(\mathbb{R}_{+}+z)

\displaystyle=\bigcup_{\lambda\in\mathbb{Q}\cap(0,1)}\bigcup_{z=C_{p,\lambda,t}x+D_{p,\lambda,t}y}\left\{\left(z,0\right)\times\left[0,M_{p,\alpha}^{(t,\lambda)}(V_{A}(x),V_{B}(y))\right]\right\}.

Further taking volumes, we conclude that for each fixed

,

\displaystyle V_{1}([(1-t)\times_{p,\alpha}A+_{p,\alpha}t\times_{p,\alpha}B]\cap(\mathbb{R}_{+}+z))=\sup_{0<\lambda<1}\sup_{z=C_{p,\lambda,t}x+D_{p,\lambda,t}y}M_{p,\alpha}^{(t,\lambda)}\left(V_{A}(x),V_{B}(y)\right).

Applying the inequality (
3.8
) with
\beta=-\alpha

-curvilinear summation for sets and the normalized segment function for

-curvilinear summation below, i.e.,

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!V_{1}([(1-t)\times_{p,\alpha}A+_{p,\alpha}t\times_{p,\alpha}B]\cap(\mathbb{R}_{+}+z))\cdot\sup_{0<\lambda<1}\left(M_{p,-\alpha}^{(t,\lambda)}\left(\|V_{A}\|_{\infty}^{-1},\|V_{B}\|_{\infty}^{-1}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\geq\sup_{0<\lambda<1}\left[\sup_{z=C_{p,\lambda,t}x+D_{p,\lambda,t}y}M_{p,\alpha}^{(t,\lambda)}\left(V_{A}(x),V_{B}(y)\right)\cdot M_{p,-\alpha}^{(t,\lambda)}\left(\|V_{A}\|_{\infty}^{-1},\|V_{B}\|_{\infty}^{-1}\right)\right]

\displaystyle\geq\sup_{0<\lambda<1}\left[\sup_{z=C_{p,\lambda,t}x+D_{p,\lambda,t}y}\min\left\{\frac{V_{A}(x)}{\|V_{A}\|_{\infty}},\frac{V_{B}(y)}{\|V_{B}\|_{\infty}}\right\}\right]

\displaystyle=V_{1}([(1-t)\times_{p,-\infty}A_{0}+_{p,-\infty}t\times_{p,-\infty}B_{0}]\cap(\mathbb{R}_{+}+z)).

Integrating this inequality for all

and using Fubini’s theorem, we obtain

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!V_{n+1}((1-t)\times_{p,\alpha}A+_{p,\alpha}t\times_{p,\alpha}B)\cdot M_{-p\alpha}^{t}\left(\|V_{A}\|_{\infty}^{-1},\|V_{B}\|_{\infty}^{-1}\right)

\displaystyle=V_{n+1}((1-t)\times_{p,\alpha}A+_{p,\alpha}t\times_{p,\alpha}B)\cdot\sup_{0<\lambda<1}\left(M_{p,-\alpha}^{(t,\lambda)}\left(\|V_{A}\|_{\infty}^{-1},\|V_{B}\|_{\infty}^{-1}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}V_{1}([(1-t)\times_{p,\alpha}A+_{p,\alpha}t\times_{p,\alpha}B]\cap(\mathbb{R}_{+}+z))dz\cdot\sup_{0<\lambda<1}\left(M_{p,-\alpha}^{(t,\lambda)}\left(\|V_{A}\|_{\infty}^{-1},\|V_{B}\|_{\infty}^{-1}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\geq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\sup_{0<\lambda<1}\left[\sup_{z=C_{p,\lambda,t}x+D_{p,\lambda,t}y}\min\left\{\frac{V_{A}(x)}{\|V_{A}\|_{\infty}},\frac{V_{B}(y)}{\|V_{B}\|_{\infty}}\right\}\right]dz

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}V_{1}(\left[(1-t)\times_{p,-\infty}A_{0}+_{p,-\infty}t\times_{p,-\infty}B_{0}\right]\cap(\mathbb{R}_{+}+z))dz

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}V_{n}(C_{r}\left(V_{(1-t)\times_{p,-\infty}A_{0}+_{p,-\infty}t\times_{p,-\infty}B_{0}}\right))dr

\displaystyle\geq\int_{0}^{1}V_{n}((1-t)\cdot_{p}C_{r}\left(V_{A}\right)+_{p}t\cdot_{p}C_{r}\left(V_{B}\right))dr,

by Proposition
3.7
,

∎

Theorem 3.9
.

Let
p\geq 1
,
p^{-1}+q^{-1}=1
,
t\in(0,1)
, and let
A,B\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}
be bounded

-sets of positive volume such that
A=\tilde{A}
and
B=\tilde{B}
. Let
H\subset G_{n,k}
for
k\in\{0,\cdots,n\}
, and let
\|V_{A,H}\|_{\infty},\|V_{B,H}\|_{\infty}
and
C_{r}(V_{A,H})
,
C_{r}(V_{B,H})
be defined as in (
3.10
). Then, for any
\alpha,\beta\in(-\infty,\infty)
with
\alpha+\beta\geq 0
, we obtain

\begin{split}&\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!V_{n+1}((1-t)\times_{p,\alpha}A+_{p,\alpha}t\times_{p,\alpha}B)\cdot M_{p\beta}^{t}(\|V_{A,H}\|_{\infty}^{-1},\|V_{B,H}\|_{\infty}^{-1})\\
&\geq\begin{cases}V_{n-k+1}((1-t)\times_{p,\delta}A_{H}+_{p,\delta}t\times_{p,\delta}B_{H}),&\text{if }\frac{\alpha\beta}{\alpha+\beta}\geq-\frac{1}{k},\\

where
\delta=(\alpha^{-1}+\beta^{-1}+k)^{-1}
.

Proof.

Using similar arguments in Theorem
3.8
, we can write

\begin{split}&\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!V_{n+1}((1-t)\times_{p,\alpha}A+_{p,\alpha}t\times_{p,\alpha}B)\cdot M_{p\beta}^{t}(\|V_{A,H}\|_{\infty}^{-1},\|V_{B,H}\|_{\infty}^{-1})\\
&=V_{n+1}((1-t)\times_{p,\alpha}A+_{p,\alpha}t\times_{p,\alpha}B)\cdot\sup_{0<\lambda<1}M_{p,\beta}^{(t,\lambda)}(\|V_{A,H}\|_{\infty}^{-1},\|V_{B,H}\|_{\infty}^{-1})\\
&\geq\int_{H^{\perp}}\int_{H}\sup_{0<\lambda<1}\sup_{z=C_{p,\lambda,t}x+D_{p,\lambda,t}y}\Phi(x,y)dz,\end{split}

(3.12)

where

\Phi(x,y)=M_{p,\alpha}^{(t,\lambda)}(V_{A}(x),V_{B}(y))\cdot M_{p,\beta}^{(t,\lambda)}(\|V_{A,H}\|_{\infty}^{-1},\|V_{B,H}\|_{\infty}^{-1}).

Applying (
3.8
) to
\Phi
, we see that

\displaystyle\Phi(x,y)\geq M_{p,\gamma}^{(t,\lambda)}\left(\frac{V_{A}(x)}{\|V_{A,H}\|_{\infty}},\frac{V_{B}(y)}{\|V_{B,H}\|_{\infty}}\right)=:\tilde{\Phi}(x,y)

where
\gamma=\frac{\alpha\beta}{\alpha+\beta}
.

Write

. Therefore based on (
3.12
), we obtain

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\int_{H^{\perp}}\int_{H}\sup_{0<\lambda<1}\sup_{z=C_{p,\lambda,t}x+D_{p,\lambda,t}y}\tilde{\Phi}(x,y)dz

where we have

\displaystyle\tilde{\Phi}(u+v,s+w)

\displaystyle=M_{p,\gamma}^{(t,\lambda)}\left(\frac{V_{A}(u+v)}{\|V_{A,H}\|_{\infty}},\frac{V_{B}(s+w)}{\|V_{B,H}\|_{\infty}}\right)

\displaystyle=M_{p,\gamma}^{(t,\lambda)}\left(\frac{V_{1}(A\cap(\mathbb{R}_{+}+u+v))}{\|V_{A,H}\|_{\infty}},\frac{V_{1}(B\cap(\mathbb{R}_{+}+s+w))}{\|V_{B,H}\|_{\infty}}\right).

For each fixed
v,w
consider the following normalized compression in (
3.10
)

\displaystyle C_{H}(v)=\left\{(h,r)\colon r\in\left[0,\frac{V_{1}(A\cap(\mathbb{R}_{+}+h+v))}{\|V_{A,H}\|_{\infty}}\right],A\cap(\mathbb{R}+h+v)\neq\emptyset\right\},

\displaystyle D_{H}(w)=\left\{(h,r)\colon r\in\left[0,\frac{V_{1}(B\cap(\mathbb{R}+h+w))}{\|V_{B,H}\|_{\infty}}\right],B\cap(\mathbb{R}+h+w)\neq\emptyset\right\}.

and

\begin{split}&\int_{H}\sup_{0<\lambda<1}\sup_{z_{1}=C_{p,\lambda,t}u+D_{p,\lambda,t}s}\tilde{\Phi}(u+v,s+w)dz_{1}\\
&=\int_{H}\sup_{0<\lambda<1}\sup_{z_{1}=C_{p,\lambda,t}u+D_{p,\lambda,t}s}\left(M_{p,\gamma}^{(t,\lambda)}\left(\frac{V_{1}(A\cap(\mathbb{R}_{+}+u+v))}{\|V_{A,H}\|_{\infty}},\frac{V_{1}(B\cap(\mathbb{R}_{+}+s+w))}{\|V_{B,H}\|_{\infty}}\right)\right)dz_{1}\\

&=V_{k+1}((1-t)\times_{p,\gamma}C_{H}(v)+_{p,\gamma}t\times_{p,\gamma}D_{H}(w))\end{split}

(3.13)

By applying Theorem
3.9
to the last quantity appearing in (
3.13
),
we see that

\displaystyle\int_{H^{\perp}}\sup_{0<\lambda<1}\sup_{z_{2}=C_{p,\lambda,t}v+D_{p,\lambda,t}w}\left(\int_{H}\sup_{0<\lambda<1}\sup_{z_{1}=C_{p,\lambda,t}u+D_{p,\lambda,t}s}\tilde{\Phi}(u+v,s+w)dz_{1}\right)dz_{2}

\min\left\{\left[C_{p,\lambda,t}\right]^{\frac{1}{\delta}}V_{k+1}(C_{H}(v)),\left[D_{p,\lambda,t}\right]^{\frac{1}{\delta}}V_{k+1}(D_{H}(w))\right\},&\text{if }\gamma<-\frac{1}{k},\end{cases}\right]dz_{2},

\displaystyle V_{k+1}(C_{H}(v))=V_{1}(A_{H}\cap(\mathbb{R}_{+}+v)),

Putting everything together, by integrating these inequalities over

, and using Fubini’s theorem appropriately, we conclude the proof.

Theorem 4.1
.

t,\lambda\in(0,1)
. Then the following integral inequality holds:

\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}h(x)dx&\geq\begin{cases}M_{p\gamma}^{t}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}g\right),&\text{if }\alpha\geq-\frac{1}{n},\\
\sup_{0<\lambda<1}\left\{\min\left\{\left[C_{p,\lambda,t}\right]^{\frac{1}{\gamma}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f,\left[D_{p,\lambda,t}\right]^{\frac{1}{\gamma}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}g\right\}\right\},&\text{if }\alpha<-\frac{1}{n},\end{cases}\end{split}

where
\gamma=\frac{\alpha}{1+n\alpha}
.

Proof.

As
f
and
g
are integrable functions, we may assume without loss of generality that
f,g
are bounded functions supported on a bounded sets. For a bounded integrable function
m\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
with bounded support, consider its hypo-graph

\text{hyp}(m)=\{(x,r)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}\colon r\in[0,f(x)],x\in\text{supp}(m)\},

which is a bounded Borel set. It is easy to check that

\text{hyp}(m)=\widetilde{\text{hyp}(m)},\quad V_{n+1}(\text{hyp}(m))=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}m(x)dx.

Observe, for suitable choices of
z\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we have

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\![(1-t)\times_{p,\alpha}\text{hyp}(f)+_{p,\alpha}t\times_{p,\alpha}\text{hyp}(g)]\cap(\mathbb{R}_{+}+z)

\displaystyle=\bigcup_{\lambda\in(0,1)}\bigcup_{z=C_{p,\lambda,t}x+D_{p,\lambda,t}y}\left(z,0\right)\times\left[0,M_{p,\alpha}^{(t,\lambda)}(f(x),g(y)\right],

where
x\in supp(f)
and
y\in supp(g)
.
In particular, the above implies that

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!V_{1}([(1-t)\times_{p,\alpha}\text{hyp}(f)+_{p,\alpha}t\times_{p,\alpha}\text{hyp}(g)]\cap(\mathbb{R}_{+}+z))

\displaystyle=\sup_{0<\lambda<1}\left[\sup_{z=C_{p,\lambda,t}x+D_{p,\lambda,t}y}M_{p,\alpha}^{(t,\lambda)}(f(x),g(y))\right].

Due to the assumption (
4.1
), this shows that

\displaystyle V_{n+1}(\text{hyp}(h))

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}h(z)dz

\displaystyle\geq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\sup_{0<\lambda<1}\left[\sup_{z=C_{p,\lambda,t}x+D_{p,\lambda,t}y}M_{p,\alpha}^{(t,\lambda)}(f(x),g(y))\right]dz

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}V_{1}([(1-t)\times_{p,\alpha}\text{hyp}(f)+_{p,\alpha}t\times_{p,\alpha}\text{hyp}(g)]\cap(\mathbb{R}_{+}+z))dz

\displaystyle=V_{n+1}((1-t)\times_{p,\alpha}\text{hyp}(f)+_{p,\alpha}t\times_{p,\alpha}\text{hyp}(g)).

Consequently, we employ the
L_{p,\alpha}
-curvilinear-Brunn-Minkowski inequality for sets, i.e., Theorem
3.3
to the sets
\text{hyp}(f)
and
\text{hyp}(g)
to obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}h(x)dx

\displaystyle\geq V_{n+1}((1-t)\times_{p,\alpha}\text{hyp}(f)+_{p,\alpha}t\times_{p,\alpha}\text{hyp}(g))

\displaystyle\geq\begin{cases}\sup_{0<\lambda<1}\left\{M_{p,\gamma}^{(t,\lambda)}\left(V_{n+1}(\text{hyp}(f)),V_{n+1}(\text{hyp}(g))\right)\right\},&\text{if }\alpha\geq-\frac{1}{n},\\
\sup_{0<\lambda<1}\left\{\min\left\{\left[C_{p,\lambda,t}\right]^{\frac{1}{\gamma}}V_{n+1}(\text{hyp}(f)),\left[D_{p,\lambda,t}\right]^{\frac{1}{\gamma}}V_{n+1}(\text{hyp}(g))\right\}\right\},&\text{if }\alpha<-\frac{1}{n},\end{cases}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\sup_{0<\lambda<1}\left\{M_{p,\gamma}^{(t,\lambda)}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}g\right)\right\},&\text{if }\alpha\geq-\frac{1}{n},\\
\sup_{0<\lambda<1}\left\{\min\left\{\left[C_{p,\lambda,t}\right]^{\frac{1}{\gamma}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f,\left[D_{p,\lambda,t}\right]^{\frac{1}{\gamma}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}g\right\}\right\},&\text{if }\alpha<-\frac{1}{n},\end{cases}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}M_{p\gamma}^{t}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}g\right),&\text{if }\alpha\geq-\frac{1}{n},\\
\sup_{0<\lambda<1}\left\{\min\left\{\left[C_{p,\lambda,t}\right]^{\frac{1}{\gamma}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f,\left[D_{p,\lambda,t}\right]^{\frac{1}{\gamma}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}g\right\}\right\},&\text{if }\alpha<-\frac{1}{n},\end{cases}

where
\gamma=\frac{\alpha}{1+n\alpha}
, completing the proof.
∎

Repeating the proof of Theorem
4.1
, similar to the process in Theorem
3.3
and Theorem
3.5
, we obtain the following generalization and strengthening of Uhrin’s curvilinear Prékopa-Leindler inequality in
[
57
]
—the
L_{p,\bar{\alpha}}
-Borell-Brascamp-Lieb inequality.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
p\geq 1
,
p^{-1}+q^{-1}=1
,
t\in(0,1)
, and
\bar{\alpha}=(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n+1})
with
\alpha_{i}\in[0,1]
for all
i=1,\dots,n
. Suppose that
f,g,h\colon(\mathbb{R}_{+})^{n}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
are a triple of bounded integrable functions having bounded support that satisfy the condition

\displaystyle h\left(M_{p,\alpha_{1}}^{(t,\lambda)}(x_{1},y_{1}),\dots,M_{p,\alpha_{n}}^{(t,\lambda)}(x_{n},y_{n})\right)\geq M_{p,\alpha_{n+1}}^{(t,\lambda)}(f(x_{1},\dots,x_{n}),g(y_{1},\dots,y_{n}))

(4.2)

for all
x=(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}),y=(y_{1},\cdots,y_{n})\in(\mathbb{R}_{+})^{n}
with
f(x)g(y)=f(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n})g(y_{1},\cdots,y_{n})>0
, and for all
\lambda\in(0,1)
. Then the following inequality holds:

\displaystyle\int_{(\mathbb{R}_{+})^{n}}h(x)dx

\displaystyle\geq\begin{cases}M_{p\gamma}^{t}\left(\int_{(\mathbb{R}_{+})^{n}}f(x)dx,\int_{(\mathbb{R}_{+})^{n}}g(x)dx\right),&\text{if }\alpha_{n+1}\geq-\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}^{-1}\right)^{-1},\\
\sup_{0<\lambda<1}\min\left\{\left[C_{p,\lambda,t}\right]^{\frac{1}{\gamma}}\int_{(\mathbb{R}_{+})^{n}}f(x)dx,\left[D_{p,\lambda,t}\right]^{\frac{1}{\gamma}}\int_{(\mathbb{R}_{+})^{n}}g(x)dx\right\},&\text{if }\alpha_{n+1}<-\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}^{-1}\right)^{-1},\end{cases}

where

\gamma=\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}\alpha_{i}^{-1}\right)^{-1}.

4.2.
Normalized
L_{p}
-Borell-Brascamp-Lieb type inequality

The main focus of this subsection is the following normalized version of the
L_{p,\bar{\alpha}}
Borell-Brascamp-Lieb inequality to more general space in previous Subsection.

Let
H\subset G_{n,k}
be a linear subspace for
k\in\{0,1,\cdots,n\}
and denote by
H^{\perp}
its orthogonal complement in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. For a integrable function
h\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
, Fubini’s theorem implies that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}h(z)dz=\int_{H^{\perp}}\left(\int_{H}h(x+y)dy\right)dx.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
p\geq 1
,
p^{-1}+q^{-1}=1
,
t\in(0,1)
, and
\alpha,\beta\in[-\infty,\infty]
be such that
\alpha+\beta\geq 0
. Let
H\in G_{n,k}
,
k\in\{1,\dots,n\}
. Let
f,g,h\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
be a triple of integrable functions, and assume that the hypo-graphs
A=\text{hyp}(f)
and
B=\text{hyp}(g)
are bounded
F_{\sigma}
-sets in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Assume that
f,g,h
satisfy the condition

h\left(C_{p,\lambda,t}x+D_{p,\lambda,t}y\right)\geq M_{p,\alpha}^{(t,\lambda)}(f(x),g(y))

(4.3)

for all
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
f(x)g(y)>0
and all
\lambda\in(0,1)
.
Consider the norms of marginal functions of
f,g
with respect to
H\in G_{n,k}
where
k\in\{0,1,\cdots,n\},

\displaystyle\|f\|_{H}=\sup_{z\in H^{\perp}}\int_{H}f(x+z)dx=:\sup_{z\in H^{\perp}}I(f,z),\quad\|g\|_{H}=\sup_{z\in H^{\perp}}\int_{H}g(x+z)dx=:\sup_{z\in H^{\perp}}I(g,z).

Then the following inequality holds:

\begin{split}&\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}h(x)dx\right)\cdot M_{p\beta}^{t}(\|f\|_{H}^{-1},\|g\|_{H}^{-1})\\
&\geq\begin{cases}\int_{H^{\perp}}M_{p\delta}^{t}\left(\frac{I(f,u)}{\|f\|_{H}},\frac{I(g,v)}{\|g\|_{H}}\right)dw&\text{if }\frac{\alpha\beta}{\alpha+\beta}\geq-\frac{1}{k},\\
\int_{H^{\perp}}\sup_{0<\lambda<1}\left\{\min\left\{\left[C_{p,\lambda,t}\right]^{\frac{1}{\delta}}\frac{I(f,u)}{\|f\|_{H}},\left[D_{p,\lambda,t}\right]^{\frac{1}{\delta}}\frac{I(g,v)}{\|g\|_{H}}\right\}\right\}dw,&\text{if }\frac{\alpha\beta}{\alpha+\beta}<-\frac{1}{k},\end{cases}\end{split}

(4.4)

where the suprema are taken over all ways to write appropriately selected
w\in H^{\perp}
in the form
w=C_{p,\lambda,t}u+D_{p,\lambda,t}v
, and where
\delta=(\alpha^{-1}+\beta^{-1}+k)^{-1}
.

Proof.

The goal of the proof is to invoke Theorem
3.9
at the right moment. As we have seen before in the proof of Theorem
4.1
,

\text{hyp}(f)=\widetilde{\text{hyp}(f)},\quad V_{n+1}(\text{hyp}(f))=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x)dx,

and the same with
g
and
h
. Additionally, for
z\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we have

\begin{split}&\!\!\!\!\!V_{1}([(1-t)\times_{p,\alpha}\text{hyp}(f)+_{p,\alpha}t\times_{p,\alpha}\text{hyp}(g)]\cap(\mathbb{R}_{+}+z))\\
&=\sup_{0<\lambda<1}\left[\sup_{z=C_{p,\lambda,t}x+D_{p,\lambda,t}y}M_{p,\alpha}^{(t,\lambda)}(f(x),g(y))\right].\end{split}

(4.5)

Integrating (
4.5
) over
z\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and taking into account the condition (
4.6
), we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}h(z)dz

\displaystyle\geq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\sup_{0<\lambda<1}\left[\sup_{z=C_{p,\lambda,t}x+D_{p,\lambda,t}y}M_{p,\alpha}^{(t,\lambda)}(f(x),g(y))\right]dz

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}V_{1}([(1-t)\times_{p,\alpha}\text{hyp}(f)+_{p,\alpha}t\times_{p,\alpha}\text{hyp}(g)]\cap(\mathbb{R}_{+}+z))dz.

The definitions in equation (
3.10
) yield

\|V_{\text{hyp}(f),H}\|_{\infty}=\|f\|_{H},\quad\|V_{\text{hyp}(g),H}\|=\|g\|_{H},

and

\displaystyle|(1-t)\times^{p,\delta}\text{hyp}(f)_{H}+^{p,\delta}t\times^{p,\delta}\text{hyp}(g)_{H}|=\int_{H^{\perp}}\Psi(w)dw,

where

\Psi(w)=\sup_{0<\lambda<1}\left[\sup_{w=C_{p,\lambda,t}u+D_{p,\lambda,t}v}\min\left\{\left(C_{p,\lambda,t}\right)^{\frac{1}{\delta}}\frac{I(f,u)}{\|V_{A,H}\|_{\infty}},\left(D_{p,\lambda,t}\right)^{\frac{1}{\delta}}\frac{I(g,v)}{\|V_{B,H}\|_{\infty}}\right\}\right],

with
A=\text{hyp}(f)
and
B=\text{hyp}(g)
. Finally, by applying Theorem
3.9
, we conclude the proof.

∎

When
k=n,
we obtain the normalized
L_{p}
-Borell-Brascamp-Lieb inequality as follows.

Corollary 4.4
.

Let
p\geq 1
,
p^{-1}+q^{-1}=1
,
t\in(0,1)
, and
\alpha,\beta\in[-\infty,\infty]
be such that
\alpha+\beta\geq 0
. Let
f,g,h\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
be a triple of integrable functions, and assume that the hypo-graphs
A=\text{hyp}(f)
and
B=\text{hyp}(g)
are bounded
F_{\sigma}
-sets in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Assume that
f,g,h
satisfy the condition

h\left(C_{p,\lambda,t}x+D_{p,\lambda,t}y\right)\geq M_{p,\alpha}^{(t,\lambda)}(f(x),g(y))

(4.6)

for all
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
f(x)g(y)>0
and all
\lambda\in(0,1)
.
Then the following inequality holds:

\begin{split}&\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}h(x)dx\right)\cdot M_{p\beta}^{t}(\|f\|_{\infty}^{-1},\|g\|_{\infty}^{-1})\\
&\geq\begin{cases}M_{p\omega}^{t}\left(\frac{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x)dx}{\|f\|_{\infty}},\frac{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}g(x)dx}{\|g\|_{\infty}}\right)&\text{if }\frac{\alpha\beta}{\alpha+\beta}\geq-\frac{1}{n},\\
\sup_{0<\lambda<1}\left\{\min\left\{\left[C_{p,\lambda,t}\right]^{\frac{1}{\delta}}\frac{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x)dx}{\|f\|_{H}},\left[D_{p,\lambda,t}\right]^{\frac{1}{\delta}}\frac{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}g(x)dx}{\|g\|_{H}}\right\}\right\},&\text{if }\frac{\alpha\beta}{\alpha+\beta}<-\frac{1}{n},\end{cases}\end{split}

(4.7)

where the suprema are taken over all ways to write appropriately selected
w\in H^{\perp}
in the form
w=C_{p,\lambda,t}u+D_{p,\lambda,t}v
, and where
\omega=(\alpha^{-1}+\beta^{-1}+n)^{-1}
.

4.3.
Normalized
L_{p}
-curvilinear-Brunn-Minkowski inequality for measures

In this Subsection, we further consider the form for normalized
L_{p,\alpha}
-curvilinear-Brunn-Minkowski inequality for sets in terms of measure.
Before stating our next results, we introduce some preliminary notions.
Let
\mu
be a measure on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
having a bounded density
\phi\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
, and let
A\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a compact set of positive
\mu
measure, and
H\in G_{n,k}
for some
0\leq k\leq n
. Consider the function

\mu_{A,H}(u)=\mu(A\cap(H-u)),\ u\in H^{\perp},

and set

m_{A,H}^{\mu}:=\sup\{\mu_{A,H}(u):u\in H^{\perp}\}.

We consider the normalized super-level sets of
\mu_{A,H}
given for
r\in[0,1]

C_{A,H}^{\mu}(r)=\left\{u\in H^{\perp}\colon\mu_{A,H}(u)\geq r\cdot m_{A,H}^{\mu}\right\}\subset H^{\perp}\in G_{n,n-k}.

In particular, using Fubini’s theorem, we see that

\begin{split}\mu(A)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(\phi\chi_{A})(z)dz=\int_{H^{\perp}}\int_{H}(\phi\chi_{A})(u+w)dwdu=\int_{H^{\perp}}\mu_{A,H}(u)du=m_{A,H}\int_{0}^{1}\mu(C_{A,H}^{\mu}(r))dr.\end{split}

(4.8)

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
p\geq 1
,
p^{-1}+q^{-1}=1
,
t\in(0,1)
, and
\alpha,\beta\in(-\infty,+\infty)
, with
\alpha+\beta\geq 0
, and
H\in G_{n,k}
for
k\in\{0,\cdots,n\}
. Let
\mu
be a measure on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
given by
d\mu(x)=\phi(x)dx
, where
\phi\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
is bounded
\alpha
-concave function whose support contains the origin. For any compact sets
A,B\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, each having positive
\mu
-measure, one has

\begin{split}&\!\!\!\!\mu((1-t)\cdot_{p}A+_{p}t\cdot_{p}B)\cdot M_{p\beta}^{t}\left(m_{A,H}^{-1},m_{B,H}^{-1}\right)\\
&\geq\begin{cases}\int_{0}^{1}\mu((1-t)\cdot_{p}C_{A,H}(r)+_{p}t\cdot_{p}C_{B,H}(r))dr,&\text{if }\frac{\alpha\beta}{\alpha+\beta}\geq-\frac{1}{k},\\
\int_{H^{\perp}}\sup\left\{\min\left\{\left[C_{p,\lambda,t}\right]^{\frac{1}{\delta}}\frac{\mu_{A,H}(u)}{m_{A,H}},\left[D_{p,\lambda,t}\right]^{\frac{1}{\delta}}\frac{\mu_{B,H}(v)}{m_{B,H}}\right\}\right\}dw,&\text{if }\frac{\alpha\beta}{\alpha+\beta}<-\frac{1}{k},\end{cases}\end{split}

(4.9)

where the suprema are taken over all
\lambda\in(0,1)
and all ways to write
w=C_{p,\lambda,t}u+D_{p,\lambda,t}v
with
u,v\in H^{\perp}
, and where

\delta=(\alpha^{-1}+\beta^{-1}+k)^{-1}
.

Proof.

Let
\phi\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
be the density of the measure
\mu
. Consider the functions
f=\phi\chi_{A}
,
g=\phi\chi_{B}
, and
h=\phi\chi_{(1-t)\cdot_{p}A+_{p}t\cdot_{p}B}.
Then, for
\lambda,t\in(0,1)
, using Hölder’s inequality together with the
\alpha
-concavity of
\phi
and the fact that the support of
\phi
contains the origin, we may write

\displaystyle h\left(C_{p,\lambda,t}x+D_{p,\lambda,t}y\right)

\displaystyle=\phi\chi_{(1-t)\cdot_{p}A+_{p}t\cdot_{p}B}\left(C_{p,\lambda,t}x+D_{p,\lambda,t}y\right)

\displaystyle=\phi\left(C_{p,\lambda,t}x+D_{p,\lambda,t}y\right)

\displaystyle\geq M_{p,\alpha}^{(t,\lambda)}(f(x),g(y)),

holds for any
x\in A,y\in B
, and so the triple
f,g,h
satisfy the assumptions (
4.6
) of Theorem
4.3
.

Moreover, we see that

\displaystyle\|f\|_{H}=\sup_{u\in H^{\perp}}\int_{H}f(x+u)du=\sup_{x\in H^{\perp}}\int_{H}(\phi\chi_{A})(x+u)du=\sup_{u\in H^{\perp}}\mu(A\cap(H-u))=m_{A,H},

and also that, for each
u\in H^{\perp}
, the marginal
I(f,u)=\mu(A\cap(H-u))=\mu_{A,H}(u)
. Similarly,
\|g\|_{H}=m_{B,H}
, and for any
v\in H^{\perp}
, we have
I(g,u)=\mu(B\cap(H-v))=\mu_{B,H}(v)
.

Suppose that
\beta\in(-\infty,\infty)
is such that
\alpha+\beta\geq 0
. By applying inequality (
4.7
) to the triple
f,g,h,
we obtain

\begin{split}&\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\mu((1-t)\cdot_{p}A+_{p}t\cdot_{p}B)\cdot\sup_{0<\lambda<1}M_{p,\beta}^{(t,\lambda)}(m_{A,H}^{-1},m_{B,H}^{-1})\\
&=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}h(z)dz\cdot\sup_{0<\lambda<1}M_{p,\beta}^{(t,\lambda)}(\|f\|_{H}^{-1},\|g\|_{H}^{-1})\\
&\geq\begin{cases}\int_{H^{\perp}}\sup\left\{M_{p,\delta}^{(t,\lambda)}\left(\frac{I(f,u)}{\|f\|_{H}},\frac{I(g,v)}{\|g\|_{H}}\right)\right\}dw,&\text{if }\frac{\alpha\beta}{\alpha+\beta}\geq-\frac{1}{k},\\
\int_{H^{\perp}}\sup\left\{\min\left\{\left[C_{p,\lambda,t}\right]^{\frac{1}{\delta}}\frac{I(f,u)}{\|f\|_{H}},\left[D_{p,\lambda,t}\right]^{\frac{1}{\delta}}\frac{I(g,v)}{\|g\|_{H}}\right\}\right\}dw,&\text{if }\frac{\alpha\beta}{\alpha+\beta}<-\frac{1}{k},\end{cases}\\
&=\begin{cases}\int_{H^{\perp}}\sup\left\{M_{p,\delta}^{(t,\lambda)}\left(\frac{\mu_{A,H}(u)}{m_{A,H}},\frac{\mu_{B,H}(v)}{m_{B,H}}\right)\right\}dw,&\text{if }\frac{\alpha\beta}{\alpha+\beta}\geq-\frac{1}{k},\\
\int_{H^{\perp}}\sup\left\{\min\left\{\left[C_{p,\lambda,t}\right]^{\frac{1}{\delta}}\frac{\mu_{A,H}(u)}{m_{A,H}},\left[D_{p,\lambda,t}\right]^{\frac{1}{\delta}}\frac{\mu_{B,H}(v)}{m_{B,H}}\right\}\right\}dw,&\text{if }\frac{\alpha\beta}{\alpha+\beta}<-\frac{1}{k},\end{cases}\end{split}

(4.10)

where the suprema are taken over all
\lambda\in(0,1)
and over all ways to write appropriately selected
w\in H^{\perp}
in the form
w=C_{p,\lambda,t}u+D_{p,\lambda,t}v
where
\delta=(\alpha^{-1}+\beta^{-1}+k)^{-1}
.

For the case when
\frac{\alpha\beta}{\alpha+\beta}\geq-\frac{1}{k}
, we claim that

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\int_{H^{\perp}}\sup\left\{M_{p,\delta}^{(t,\lambda)}\left(\frac{\mu_{A,H}(u)}{m_{A,H}},\frac{\mu_{B,H}(v)}{m_{B,H}}\right)\colon w=C_{p,\lambda,t}u+D_{p,\lambda,t}v,0<\lambda<1\right\}dw

\displaystyle\geq\int_{0}^{1}\mu((1-t)\cdot_{p}C_{A,H}(r)+_{p}t\cdot_{p}C_{B,H}(r))dr.

Indeed, if we consider the function
M\colon H^{\perp}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
defined by

M(w)=\sup\left\{M_{p,\delta}^{(t,\lambda)}\left(\frac{\mu_{A,H}(u)}{m_{A,H}},\frac{\mu_{B,H}(v)}{m_{B,H}}\right)\colon w=C_{p,\lambda,t}u+D_{p,\lambda,t}v,0<\lambda<1\right\},

then it is not difficult to see that

\displaystyle\{w\in H^{\perp}\colon M(w)\geq r\}

\displaystyle\supset(1-t)\cdot_{p}\left\{u\in H^{\perp}\colon\frac{\mu_{A,H}(u)}{m_{A,H}}\geq r\right\}+_{p}t\cdot_{p}\left\{v\in H^{\perp}\colon\frac{\mu_{B,H}(v)}{m_{B,H}}\geq r\right\}

\displaystyle=(1-t)\cdot_{p}C_{A,H}(r)+_{p}t\cdot_{p}C_{B,H}(r).

Therefore, for any
\beta
such that
\alpha+\beta\geq 0
and
\frac{\alpha\beta}{\alpha+\beta}\geq-\frac{1}{k}
, using the inequality (
4.10
) together with Fubini’s theorem and the above inclusion, we obtain

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\mu((1-t)\cdot_{p}A+_{p}t\cdot_{p}B)\cdot M_{p\beta}^{t}\left(m_{A,H}^{-1},m_{B,H}^{-1}\right)

\displaystyle=\mu((1-t)\cdot_{p}A+_{p}t\cdot_{p}B)\cdot\sup_{0<\lambda<1}M_{p,\beta}^{(t,\lambda)}(m_{A,H}^{-1},m_{B,H}^{-1})

\displaystyle\geq\int_{H^{\perp}}M(w)dw

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\mu(\{w\in H^{\perp}\colon M(w)\geq r\})dr

\displaystyle\geq\int_{0}^{1}\mu((1-t)\cdot_{p}C_{A,H}(r)+_{p}t\cdot_{p}C_{B,H}(r))dr,

which verifies Theorem
4.5
in the case that
\alpha+\beta\geq 0
and
\frac{\alpha\beta}{\alpha+\beta}\geq-\frac{1}{k}
. The case for
\frac{\alpha\beta}{\alpha+\beta}<-\frac{1}{k}
can be proved in a similar way as in Theorem
4.3
.
∎

By choosing
k=n
in Theorem
4.5
, the right-hand side of inequality (
4.9
) satisfies

\int_{0}^{1}\mu((1-t)\cdot_{p}C_{A,H}(r)+_{p}t\cdot_{p}C_{B,H}(r))dr\geq\sup_{0<\lambda<1}M_{p,1}^{(t,\lambda)}\left(\frac{\mu(A)}{m_{A,H}},\frac{\mu(B)}{m_{B,H}}\right).

Therefore, using the identity (
4.8
), we obtain the following corollary.

Corollary 4.6
.

Let
p\geq 1
,
p^{-1}+q^{-1}=1
,
t\in(0,1)
, and
H\in G_{n,k}
for some
k\in\{0,\cdots,n\}
. Let
\alpha,\beta\in(-\infty,\infty)
be such that

\begin{split}&\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\mu((1-t)\cdot_{p}A+_{p}t\cdot_{p}B)\cdot\sup_{0<\lambda<1}M_{p,\beta}^{(t,\lambda)}\left(m_{A,H}^{-1},m_{B,H}^{-1}\right)\\
&\geq\sup_{0<\lambda<1}M_{p,1}^{(t,\lambda)}\left(\frac{\mu(A)}{m_{A,H}},\frac{\mu(B)}{m_{B,H}}\right).\end{split}

In particular, choosing

\displaystyle\mu((1-t)A+tB)

Definition 5.1

Theorem 5.3

Let

\bar{\alpha}=(\alpha_{1},\cdots,\alpha_{n+1})\in[0,\infty]^{n+1}

-concave of

-curvilinear summation for any two

Then the following inequality holds:

S_{\mu,p,\bar{\alpha}}(A,B)\geq S_{\mu,p,\bar{\alpha}}(A,A)+\frac{F\left(\mu(B)\right)-F\left(\mu(A)\right)}{F^{\prime}\left(\mu(A)\right)}.

(5.2)

When
\mu(A)=\mu(B)

S_{\mu,p,\bar{\alpha}}(A,B)\geq S_{\mu,p,\bar{\alpha}}(A,A).

Proof.

Consider first that

\displaystyle\mu(A\oplus_{p,\bar{\alpha}}(\varepsilon\times_{p,\bar{\alpha}}B))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mu\Big(\left[(1-\varepsilon)\times_{p,\bar{\alpha}}\left(\frac{1}{1-\varepsilon}\times_{p,\bar{\alpha}}A\right)\right]\oplus_{p,\bar{\alpha}}(\varepsilon\times_{p,\bar{\alpha}}B)\Big)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle F^{-1}\left\{(1-\varepsilon)F\left(\mu(\frac{1}{1-\varepsilon}\times_{p,\bar{\alpha}}A)\right)+\varepsilon F\left(\mu(B)\right)\right\}.

For convenience, we denote the following function with parameter

Q_{F,\mu,s,p}(\varepsilon):=F^{-1}\left[(1-\varepsilon)F\left(\mu(\frac{1}{1-\varepsilon}\times_{p,\bar{\alpha}}A)\right)+\varepsilon F\left(\mu(B)\right)\right].

(5.3)

It is easy to check that the equality in (

\displaystyle S_{\mu,p,\bar{\alpha}}(A,B)

\displaystyle=\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\frac{\mu(A+_{p,\bar{\alpha}}t\times_{p,\bar{\alpha}}B)-\mu(A)}{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\geq\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\frac{Q_{F,\mu,s,p}(\varepsilon)-Q_{F,\mu,s,p}(0)}{\varepsilon}=Q_{F,\mu,s,p}^{\prime}(0).

Observe that

\displaystyle\frac{d}{d\varepsilon}\left[\mu(\frac{1}{1-\varepsilon}\times_{p,\bar{\alpha}}A)\right]\biggr\rvert_{\varepsilon=0}

\displaystyle=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\frac{\mu([(1+\varepsilon+\varepsilon^{2}+\cdots)\times_{p,\bar{\alpha}}A])-\mu(A)}{\varepsilon}=S_{\mu,p,\bar{\alpha}}(A,A).

Therefore, it follows from (
5.3

\displaystyle S_{\mu,p,\bar{\alpha}}(A,B)\geq G_{F,\mu,s,p}^{\prime}(0)

\displaystyle=\frac{(1-\varepsilon)S_{\mu,p,\bar{\alpha}}(A,A)\cdot F^{\prime}\left(\mu(\frac{1}{1-\varepsilon}\times_{p,\bar{\alpha}}A)\right)\mid_{\varepsilon=0}}{F^{\prime}\left(\mu(A)\right)}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{-F\left(\mu([(1-\varepsilon)\times_{p,\bar{\alpha}}A])\right)\mid_{\varepsilon=0}+F\left(\mu(B)\right)}{F^{\prime}\left(\mu(A)\right)}

\displaystyle=S_{\mu,p,\bar{\alpha}}(A,A)+\frac{F\left(\mu(B)\right)-F\left(\mu(A)\right)}{F^{\prime}\left(\mu(A)\right)},

as desired.

The proofs when

In particular, we obtain a natural corollary of Theorem

Corollary 5.4

Following the ideas in

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!F(\mu(A+_{p,\bar{\alpha}}((1-t)\varepsilon_{1}+t\varepsilon_{2})\times_{p,\bar{\alpha}}B))

Theorem 5.6
.

Let
p\geq 1
,
F\colon\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}
be a strictly increasing differentiable function and
\mu
be a measure on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
that is absolutely continuous with respect to the Lebesgue measure. Suppose that
\mu
is
F(t)
-concave with respect bounded Borel sets. Then for any bounded Borel sets

, one has

\mathbb{V}_{p,F}^{\bar{\alpha},\mu}(A,B)+F^{\prime}(1)\mathbb{M}_{p,F}^{\bar{\alpha},\mu}(A)\geq\frac{F^{\prime}(1)[F(\mu(B))-F(\mu(A))]}{F^{\prime}(\mu(A))}+\mu(A),

with equality only if and only if
A=B
.

Proof.

Denote the function
f\colon[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
as

f(t)=F(\mu((1-t)\times_{p,\bar{\alpha}}A+_{p,\bar{\alpha}}t\times_{p,\bar{\alpha}}B))-\left[(1-t)F(\mu(A))+tF(\mu(B))\right].

As

is concave with respect the

-curvilinear summation for bounded Borel sets, the functions
f
is concave and such that
f(0)=f(1)=0,
and the right-derivative of
f
at
t=0
exists satisfying

\frac{d^{+}}{dt}\biggr\rvert_{t=0}f(t)\geq 0,

with equality if and only if
f(t)=0
for all
t\in[0,1]
.

We only need to compute the right derivative at
0
of
\mu((1-t)\times_{p,\bar{\alpha}}A+_{p,\bar{\alpha}}t\times_{p,\bar{\alpha}}B)
since

\frac{d^{+}}{dt}\biggr\rvert_{t=0}f(t)=F^{\prime}(\mu(A))\cdot\frac{d^{+}}{dt}\biggr\rvert_{t=0}\mu((1-t)\times_{p,\bar{\alpha}}A+t\times_{p,\bar{\alpha}}B)+F(\mu(A))-F(\mu(B)).

Setting
g(r,s)=\mu(r\times_{p,\bar{\alpha}}(A+_{p,\bar{\alpha}}s\times_{p,\bar{\alpha}}B))
, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{d^{+}}{dt}\biggr\rvert_{t=0}\mu((1-t)\times_{p,\bar{\alpha}}A+t\times_{p,\bar{\alpha}}B)

\displaystyle=\frac{d^{+}}{dt}\biggr\rvert_{t=0}g\left(1-t,\frac{t}{1-t}\right)

\displaystyle=-\frac{d^{-}}{dt}\biggr\rvert_{t=1}\mu(t\times_{p,\bar{\alpha}}A)+\frac{d^{+}}{dt}\biggr\rvert_{t=0}\mu(A+_{p,\bar{\alpha}}t\times_{p,\bar{\alpha}}B)

\frac{d^{+}}{dt}\biggr\rvert_{t=0}f(t)=F^{\prime}(\mu(A))\left[\mathbb{M}_{p,F}^{\bar{\alpha},\mu}(A)-\frac{1}{F^{\prime}(1)}\mu(A)+\frac{1}{F^{\prime}(1)}V_{p}^{\mu}(A,B)\right]+F(\mu(A))-F(\mu(B)).

It follows from the fact

and the equality case of
\frac{d^{+}}{dt}\rvert_{t=0}f(t)=0
the desired inequality holds true.
∎

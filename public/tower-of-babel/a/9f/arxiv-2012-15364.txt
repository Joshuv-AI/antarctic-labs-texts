Lifting spectral triples to noncommutative principal bundles
Kay Schwieger and Stefan Wagner, Kay Schwieger, Stefan Wagner

Abstract
Given a free action of a compact Lie group G G on a unital C ∗ -algebra 𝒜 \mathcal{A} and a spectral triple on the corresponding fixed point algebra 𝒜 G \mathcal{A}^{G} , we present a systematic and in-depth construction of a spectral triple on 𝒜 \mathcal{A} that is build upon the geometry of 𝒜 G \mathcal{A}^{G} and G G . We compare our construction with a selection of established examples. MSC2010: 58B34, 46L87 (primary), 55R10 (secondary)

Introduction

The tremendous work of Hopf, Stiefel, and Whitney in the 1930’s demonstrated the importance of principal bundles for various applications to algebraic topology, geometry and mathematical physics.
In the noncommutative setting the notion of a free action of a quantum group on a C
∗
-algebra provides a natural framework for noncommutative principal bundles (see,
e. g.
,
[
5
,
24
,
51
,
58
]
and ref. therein).
Their structure theory and their relation to
K
-theory (see,
e. g.
,
[
9
,
12
,
18
,
25
,
56
,
57
,
58
]
and ref. therein) certainly appeal to operator algebraists and functional analysts.
In addition, noncommutative principal bundles are becoming increasingly prevalent in various applications of geometry (
cf.

[
35
,
36
,
43
,
59
]
) and mathematical physics (see,
e. g.
,
[
6
,
10
,
19
,
20
,
23
,
32
,
41
,
62
]
and ref. therein).

Geometric aspects of noncommutative principal bundles, however, have not been studied yet in a cohesive way.
Using a universal differential calculus, the algebraic framework of Hopf-Galois extensions yields abstract notions of connections and curvature (see,
e. g.
,
[
18
,
31
,
41
]
and ref. therein).
In this article we take a different approach and investigate the geometry of principal bundles by means of Connes’ spectral triples.

Spectral triples lay the foundation for noncommutative geometric spaces.
Along with some additional structure they allow to extend many techniques from Riemannian spin geometry (see,
e. g.
,
[
13
,
14
,
31
,
61
]
and ref. therein).
Another reason for looking at spectral triples is the role they play in mathematical physics.
For instance, they provide a noncommutative formulation of the standard model of particle physics and of the integrality of the quantum Hall current (
cf.

[
11
,
16
,
42
]
).
Spectral triples and their applications in quantum field theory is also an area of active research (see,
e. g.
,
[
1
,
16
]
and ref. therein).

The purpose of this paper is to give a methodical construction of spectral triples on noncommutative principal bundles that are build upon the geometry of the “quantum base spaces” and the underlying structure groups.
Since the subject of noncommutative principal bundles or, equivalently, free actions is better understood in the compact case (see,
e. g.
,
[
5
,
12
,
56
,
58
]
and ref. therein), we will restrict our study to free actions of compact Lie groups on unital C
∗
-algebras.
More precisely, for a free action of a compact Lie group
G
on a unital C
∗
-algebra
\mathcal{A}
and a spectral triple on the corresponding fixed point algebra
\mathcal{A}^{G}
, we provide a systematic construction of a spectral triple on
\mathcal{A}
by means of the geometry of
\mathcal{A}^{G}
and
G
.
To the best of our knowledge, such spectral triples on nontrivial noncommutative principal bundles with higher dimension non-Abelian structure groups have not been worked out yet.

Survey of the field

Let us briefly summarize the current research frontiers covered by this article.
Regarding the notation, we refer the reader to Section
2
below.

Let
\mathcal{B}
be a C
∗
-algebra, let
\mathbb{D}
be a spectral triple on
\mathcal{B}
, and let
\alpha
be a
∗
-automorphism of
\mathcal{B}
.
Bellissard, Marcolli, and Reihani established in their seminal work
[
7
]
that there is a canonical spectral triple on the crossed product
\mathcal{B}\rtimes_{\alpha}\mathbb{Z}
that is built upon
\mathbb{D}
and a differential operator on the circle (via Fourier transform) and characterizes the metric properties of the C
∗
-dynamical system
(\mathcal{B},\mathbb{Z},\alpha)
.
The Bellissard-Marcolli-Reihani theory was developed further in the articles
[
33
,
35
,
48
]
for actions of more general classes of groups (
e. g.
, discrete and second-countable locally compact).
In this context we would like to recall that, given a C
∗
-dynamical system
(\mathcal{A},G,\alpha)
with a countable discrete Abelian group
G
, the natural dual action of the compact dual group
\hat{G}
on the crossed product
\mathcal{A}\rtimes_{\alpha}G
is free (
cf.

[
57
, Sec. 4]
).
For this reason crossed products provide a natural source of noncommutative principal bundles.

Ammann and Bär
[
3
,
4
]
looked into the properties of the Riemannian spin geometry of a smooth principal
U(1)
-bundle.
Under suitable hypotheses they related the Riemannian spin geometry of the total space to the Riemannian spin geometry of the base space.
A noncommutative generalization of these results for the Hopf-Galois analogue of principal torus bundles was developed about a decade later by Dabrowski, Sitarz, and Zucca
[
19
,
20
]
.
Of particular interest is also Zucca’s unpublished PhD-thesis
[
63
, Sec. 8]
, in which he presented conditions in order to build a real spectral triple for cleft extensions of compact connected semisimple Lie groups.
Moreover, Sitarz and Venselaar
[
60
]
studied spectral triples on quantum lens spaces as orbit spaces of free actions of cyclic groups on the spectral geometry of
\SU_{q}(2)
.
Aiello, Guido, and Isola
[
2
]
provided examples of noncommutative coverings and extended spectral triples on the base space to spectral triples on the inductive family of coverings in such a way that the covering projections are locally isometric.

We would also like to mention some connections to unbounded
KK
-theory, which was developed over the last decade by Kaad, Lesch, Mesland
et al.
Indeed, Gabriel and Grensing
[
27
]
proposed a construction of spectral triples for a class of crossed product-like algebras that gives an unbounded representative of the Kasparov product of the original spectral triple and the Pimsner-Toeplitz extension associated with the crossed product by a Hilbert module.
Forsyth and Rennie
[
25
]
provided sufficient conditions to factorize an equivariant spectral triple as a Kasparov product of unbounded classes constructed from the compact Abelian group action on the algebra and from the fixed point spectral triple.
In addition, they showed that each equivariant Dirac-type spectral triple on the total space of a torus principal bundle factorizes and that the Kasparov product
[
37
,
44
]
can be used to recover the original triple.
Kaad and van Suijlekom
[
39
,
40
]
established that the Dirac operator on the total space of an almost-regular fibration can be written, up to an explicit “obstructing” curvature term, as the tensor sum of a vertically elliptic family of Dirac operators with the horizontal Dirac operator representing the interior Kasparov product in bivariant
K
-theory (see
[
38
]
for a similar discussion in the context of toric noncommutative manifolds).

Finally, we would like to draw attention to a recent paper by Ćaćić and Mesland
[
10
]
. There the authors presented a new, general approach to gauge theory on principal
G
-spectral triples, where
G
is a compact connected Lie group.
It is our hope that this work will contribute to the development and understanding of a noncommutative gauge theory, for instance, by providing a geometric oriented notion of “parallel transport” or, more generally, of “parallelity” on a noncommutative principal bundle - a concept whose importance cannot be overemphasized.

Organization of the article

After some preliminaries, we present in Section
3
, Lemma
3.1
, yet another characterization of freeness for C
∗
-dynamical systems, which is well-adapted to our purposes.
Given a free C
∗
-dynamical system
(\mathcal{A},G,\alpha)
, we make use of this characterization in Section
4
to get a faithful covariant representation of
(\mathcal{A},G,\alpha)
on some suitable amplification of its fixed point algebra
\mathcal{A}^{G}
(Lemma
4.1
).
We also classify all faithful covariant representations of
(\mathcal{A},G,\alpha)
up to unitary equivalence (Theorem
4.4
).
As a further application we show that the isotypic components of
\mathcal{A}
act nondegenerately (Corollary
4.6
).

Now, let
(\mathcal{A},G,\alpha)
be a free C
∗
-dynamical system with a compact Lie group
G
and let
\mathbb{D}
be a spectral triple on
\mathcal{A}^{G}
.
Section
5
, the main body of this article, is devoted to the construction of a spectral triple on
\mathcal{A}
in terms of
\mathbb{D}
and the geometry of
G
.
As a preliminary step we briefly recall the notion of a factor system.
Our procedure then naturally splits into the following five main steps:

1.

We form a “quantum manifold” or, in greater detail, a dense unital
∗
-subalgebra
\mathcal{A}_{0}\subseteq\mathcal{A}
of smooth elements (Theorem
5.4
).

2.

We extend the faithful
∗
-representation of
\mathcal{A}^{G}
to a faithful covariant representation of
(\mathcal{A},G,\alpha)
(Theorem
5.7
).

3.

We lift the Dirac operator on
\mathcal{A}^{G}
to a Dirac-type operator
D_{h}
on
\mathcal{A}
(Corollary
5.8
) and show that
D_{h}
has, under some suitable conditions, bounded commutators with
\mathcal{A}_{0}
(Theorem
5.9
). The operator
D_{h}
later becomes the horizontal component of a Dirac operator.

4.

Using the group action, we construct a Dirac operator
D_{v}
, which later becomes the vertical component of the Dirac operator (Corollary
5.12
).

5.

Finally, it remains to tie everything together. More precisely, we suitably assemble the operators
D_{h}
and
D_{v}
to a Dirac operator
D_{\mathcal{A}}
on
\mathcal{A}
(Theorem
5.13
), and in this way we get a spectral triple on
\mathcal{A}
(Corollary
5.16
).

The remaining Sections,
6
,
7
and
8
, are devoted to thoroughly treating examples or, more precisely, to investigating how our construction compares to well-established examples.
Indeed, in Section
6
we show that our construction generalizes the construction given in the pioneering article
[
7
, Sec. 3.4]
by Bellissard, Marcolli, and Reihani.
In Section
7
and Section
8
we explore our construction for a free 2-torus action on the quantum 4-torus and for homogeneous spaces, respectively.
The analysis in Section
7
is straightforward and we recover the standard Dirac operator on the quantum 4-torus. The computations in Section
8
are more technical and we find that our construction differs from the standard Dirac operator on the group by a central term (Theorem
8.2
and Remark
8.3
).

Last but not least, we would like to mention that we have put in enough detail so that this paper will be accessible to a broad readership.

Preliminaries and notation

Our study revolves around permanence properties of noncommutative principal bundles with respect to spectral triples. Along the way we use various tools from geometry and operator algebras. This preliminary section exhibits the most fundamental definitions and notations in use.

To begin with, we provide some standard references. For a recent account of the theory of spectral triples and, more generally, of noncommutative geometry we refer to the excellent expositions
[
31
,
61
]
by Gracia-Bondía, Figueroa, and Varilly. Our standard references for operator algebras are the opuses
[
8
,
50
]
by Blackadar and Pedersen, respectively. For a thorough treatment of Hilbert module structures we refer to the book
[
53
]
by Raeburn and Williams and the memoirs
[
22
]
by Echterhoff
et al.

Hilbert spaces

All Hilbert spaces are assumed to be complex and come equipped with an inner product that is linear in the second component if not explicitly mentioned otherwise.

About tensor products

In this paper tensor products of C
∗
-algebras are taken with respect to the minimal tensor product, which is simply denoted by
\otimes
.
Let
\mathcal{A}
,
\mathcal{B}
, and
\mathcal{C}
be unital C
∗
-algebras. If there is no ambiguity, then we consider each one of them as a C
∗
-subalgebra of
\mathcal{A}\otimes\mathcal{B}\otimes\mathcal{C}
and extend maps on
\mathcal{A}
,
\mathcal{B}
, or
\mathcal{C}
canonically by tensoring with the respective identity map.
For the sake of clarity, we may make use of the leg numbering notation, for instance, given
x\in\mathcal{A}\otimes\mathcal{C}
, we write
x_{13}
to denote the corresponding element in
\mathcal{A}\otimes\mathcal{B}\otimes\mathcal{C}
.

About groups

Let
G
be a compact group.
We write
\Irrep(G)
for the set of equivalence classes of irreducible representations of
G
and let
1\in\Irrep(G)
stand for the class of the trivial representation.
There are two C
∗
-algebras associated with

: the algebra
C(G)
of continuous complex-valued functions on
G
and the (reduced) group C
∗

C^{*}_{r}(G)
.
If
G
is a Lie group, then we denote its Lie algebra by
\Lie(G)
and the algebra of smooth functions by
C^{\infty}(G)
.
For each
X\in\Lie(G)
we write
\partial_{X}^{G}
for the skew-adjoint operator on

given by

\displaystyle\partial_{X}^{G}f(g):=\tfrac{d}{dt}\Big|_{t=0}f\bigl(\exp(-tX)g).

About Clifford algebras

For a real Hilbert space
\mathfrak{H}
we write
\mathbb{C}\ell(\mathfrak{H})
for the associated complex Clifford algebra and use the symbol “
\cdot
” for its product.
We consider
\mathfrak{H}
as a linear subspace of
\mathbb{C}\ell(\mathfrak{H})
and follow the convention that the defining relation is

\displaystyle\xi\cdot\eta+\eta\cdot\xi=-2\langle\xi,\eta\rangle_{{#1}}\,1

for all
\xi,\eta\in\mathfrak{H}
.
In particular, if
G
is a compact Lie group, then we simply write
\mathbb{C}\ell(G)
instead of
\mathbb{C}\ell\bigl((\Lie(G)\bigr)
for ease of notation.

About unbounded operators

By an unbounded operator on a Hilbert space
\mathfrak{H}
we mean a linear map
D:\dom(D)\to\mathfrak{H}
defined on a dense subspace
\dom(D)\subseteq\mathfrak{H}
. The following results about unbounded self-adjoint operators are well-known to experts, but we could not spot a prominent reference. The proofs are postponed to Appendix
A
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
D
be an unbounded self-adjoint operator on a Hilbert space
\mathfrak{H}
with domain
\dom(D)
and let
p\in\mathcal{L}(\mathfrak{H})
be an orthogonal projection such that
p\dom(D)\subseteq\dom(D)
and such that the commutator
[D,p]
is bounded. Consider the unbounded operator

D_{p}:=pDp\qquad\text{with domain}\penalty\ \dom(D_{p}):=p\dom(D)

on the Hilbert space
p(\mathfrak{H})
. Then the following assertions hold:

1.

D_{p}
is self-adjoint.

2.

If
D
has compact resolvent, then
D_{p}
has compact resolvent.

We also make use of the following construction of an essentially self-adjoint operator from a continuous unitary representation
u:G\to\mathcal{\mathcal{U}}(\mathfrak{H})
of a finite-dimensional compact Lie group
G
. For this, let
\mathfrak{H}^{\infty}\subseteq\mathfrak{H}
stand for the subspace of smooth vectors, which is
G
-invariant and dense in
\mathfrak{H}
due to
[
30
]
(see also
[
45
,
46
]
). Equip
\Lie(G)
with an
\Ad
-invariant inner product and let
\pi_{\text{spin}}:\mathbb{C}\ell(G)\to\mathcal{L}(\mathfrak{H}_{\text{spin}})
be a finite-dimensional
∗
-representation of the Clifford algebra
\mathbb{C}\ell(G)
. Finally, for each
X\in\Lie(G)
set
F_{X}:=\pi_{\text{spin}}(X)
and denote by
\partial_{X}u
the unbounded operator on
\mathfrak{H}
with domain
\dom(\partial_{X}u):=\mathfrak{H}^{\infty}
defined as

\displaystyle\partial_{X}u(\eta):=\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{u_{\exp(tX)}(\eta)-\eta}{t}.

Then
[
28
, Prop. 4.1]
establishes that for any orthonormal basis
X_{1},\dots,X_{n}
of
\Lie(G)
we obtain an essentially self-adjoint operator with
\dom(D):=\mathfrak{H}^{\infty}\otimes\mathfrak{H}_{\text{spin}}
by putting

\displaystyle D:=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\partial_{X_{k}}u\otimes F_{X_{k}}.

Lemma 2.2
.

Suppose the unitary representation
u:G\to\mathcal{\mathcal{U}}(\mathfrak{H})
has finite-dimensional multiplicity spaces. Then the closure of
D
has compact resolvent.

About spectral triples

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a unital C
∗
-algebra. By a
spectral triple
on
\mathcal{A}
we mean a quadruple
\mathbb{D}_{\mathcal{A}}=(\mathcal{A}_{0},\pi,\mathfrak{H},D)
consisting of a dense unital
∗
-subalgebra
\mathcal{A}_{0}\subseteq\mathcal{A}
, a faithful
∗
-representation
\pi:\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{L}(\mathfrak{H})
on a Hilbert space
\mathfrak{H}
, and a possibly unbounded self-adjoint operator
D
on
\mathfrak{H}
such that

1.

D
has compact resolvent,

2.

\pi(\mathcal{A}_{0})\dom(D)\subseteq\dom(D)
,

3.

and all commutators
[D,\pi(x)]
for
x\in\mathcal{A}_{0}
are bounded.

About C
∗
-dynamical systems

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a unital C
∗
-algebra and
G
a compact group that acts on
\mathcal{A}
by
∗
-automorphisms
\alpha_{g}:\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{A}
,
g\in G
, such that
G\times\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{A}
,
(g,x)\mapsto\alpha_{g}(x)
is continuous. Throughout this article we call such data a
C
∗
-dynamical system
, denote it briefly by
(\mathcal{A},G,\alpha)
, and typically write
\mathcal{B}:=\mathcal{A}^{G}
for its fixed point algebra. In case
G
is a compact Lie group, we make use of the symbol
\mathcal{A}^{\infty}\subseteq\mathcal{A}
to denote the
∗
-subalgebra of smooth elements.

For
\sigma\in\Irrep(G)
we let
A(\sigma)
stand for the corresponding isotypic component of
\mathcal{A}
and regard it as a correspondence over
\mathcal{B}
in terms of the usual left and right multiplication and the right
\mathcal{B}
-valued inner product given by
\langle x,y\rangle_{{#1}}:=\int_{G}\alpha_{g}(x^{*}y)\,dg
for all
x,y\in A(\sigma)
. On account of the Peter-Weyl Theorem (see,
e. g.
,
[
34
, Thm. 4.22]
)
\mathcal{A}
decomposes into its isotypic components, which amounts to saying that the algebraic direct sum
\bigoplus_{\sigma\in\Irrep(G)}^{\text{alg}}A(\sigma)
is a dense
∗
-subalgebra of
\mathcal{A}
.

Aside from the isotypic components, it is expedient to look at the corresponding multiplicity spaces, by which we mean the sets

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\mathcal{A}}(\sigma):=\{x\in\mathcal{A}\otimes V_{\sigma}:(\alpha_{g}\otimes\sigma_{g})(x)=x\quad\forall g\in G\},\qquad\sigma\in\Irrep(G).

They also play the role of modules of sections of a vector bundle (with standard fibre
V_{\sigma}
) associated with
(\mathcal{A},G,\alpha)
.
Each
\Gamma_{\mathcal{A}}(\sigma)
,
\sigma\in\Irrep(G)
, is naturally a correspondence over
\mathcal{B}
with respect to the canonical left and right action and the restriction of the right
\mathcal{A}
-valued inner product on
\mathcal{A}\otimes V_{\sigma}
determined by
\langle a\otimes v,b\otimes w\rangle_{{#1}}:=\langle v,w\rangle a^{*}b
for all
a,b\in\mathcal{A}
and
v,w\in V_{\sigma}
.
The corresponding mapping
\sigma\mapsto\Gamma_{\mathcal{A}}(\sigma)
can be extended to an additive functor from the representation category of
G
into the category of C
∗
-correspondences over
\mathcal{B}
.
Most notably, for each
\sigma\in\Irrep(G)
we get a
G
-equivariant isomorphism

\Phi_{\sigma}:\Gamma_{\mathcal{A}}(\sigma)\otimes\bar{V}_{\sigma}\to A(\bar{\sigma})

(1)

of correspondences over
\mathcal{B}
, where the
G
-action on
\Gamma_{\mathcal{A}}(\sigma)\otimes\bar{V}_{\sigma}
is the obvious one on the second tensor factor, by restricting the map

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}\otimes V_{\sigma}\otimes\bar{V}_{\sigma}\to\mathcal{A},\qquad a\otimes v\otimes\bar{w}\mapsto\langle w,v\rangle\,a.

More generally, if
(\sigma,V_{\sigma})
is a finite-dimensional representation of
G
and
\sigma=\sigma_{1}\oplus\dots\oplus\sigma_{n}
is a decomposition into irreducible subrepresentations, then the same recipe as above gives a
G
-equivariant morphism
\Phi_{\sigma}:\Gamma_{\mathcal{A}}(\sigma)\otimes\bar{V}_{\sigma}\to A(\bar{\sigma})
of correspondences over
\mathcal{B}
, where
A(\bar{\sigma}):=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}A(\bar{\sigma_{i}})\subseteq\mathcal{A}
.
The functor
\sigma\mapsto\Gamma_{\mathcal{A}}(\sigma)
together with the family of transformations

\Gamma_{\mathcal{A}}(\sigma)\otimes_{\mathcal{B}}\Gamma_{\mathcal{A}}(\tau)\to\Gamma_{\mathcal{A}}(\sigma\otimes\tau),\qquad x\otimes y\mapsto x_{12}y_{13}

(2)

for all finite-dimensional representations
\sigma
and
\tau
of
G
constitute a so-called weak tensor functor and allows to reconstruct the reduced form of the C
∗
-dynamical system
(\mathcal{A},G,\alpha)
up to isomorphism (see
[
47
, Sec. 2]
).

3
Free C
∗
-dynamical systems

A C
∗
-dynamical system
(\mathcal{A},G,\alpha)
is called
free
if the so-called
Ellwood map

\displaystyle\Phi:\mathcal{A}\otimes_{\text{alg}}\mathcal{A}\rightarrow C(G,\mathcal{A}),\qquad\Phi(x\otimes y)(g):=x\alpha_{g}(y)

has dense range with respect to the canonical C
∗
-norm on
C(G,\mathcal{A})
. This condition was originally introduced for actions of quantum groups on C
∗
-algebras by Ellwood
[
24
]
and is known to be equivalent to Rieffel’s saturatedness
[
54
]
and the Peter-Weyl-Galois condition
[
5
]
. Additionally, we recall that Phillips
[
52
]
considered some stronger variants of freeness.

One of the key tools used in this article is a characterization of freeness that we provided in
[
58
, Lem. 3.2]
, namely that a C
∗
-dynamical system
(\mathcal{A},G,\alpha)
is free if and only if for each irreducible representation
(\sigma,V_{\sigma})
of
G
there is a finite-dimensional Hilbert space
\mathfrak{H}_{\sigma}
and an isometry
s(\sigma)\in\mathcal{A}\otimes\mathcal{L}(V_{\sigma},\mathfrak{H}_{\sigma})
satisfying
\alpha_{g}\bigl(s(\sigma)\bigr)=s(\sigma)(1_{\mathcal{A}}\otimes\sigma_{g})
for all
g\in G
. However, to simplify notation we patch this family of isometries together and use the following characterization instead.

Lemma 3.1
.

For a C
∗
-dynamical system
(\mathcal{A},G,\alpha)
the following statements are equivalent:

(a)

(\mathcal{A},G,\alpha)
is free.

(b)

There is a unitary representation
\mu:G\to\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{H})
with finite-dimensional multiplicity spaces and, given any faithful covariant representation
(\pi,u)
of
(\mathcal{A},G,\alpha)
on some Hilbert space
\mathfrak{H}_{\mathcal{A}}
, an isometry
s\in\mathcal{L}(\mathfrak{H}_{\mathcal{A}}\otimes L^{2}(G),\mathfrak{H}_{\mathcal{A}}\otimes\mathfrak{H})
satisfying

\displaystyle s\mathcal{A}\otimes\mathcal{K}(L^{2}(G))

\displaystyle\subseteq\mathcal{A}\otimes\mathcal{K}(L^{2}(G),\mathfrak{H}),

(3)

\displaystyle(u_{g}\otimes 1_{\mathfrak{H}})s

\displaystyle=s(u_{g}\otimes r_{g})\qquad

\displaystyle\forall g\in G,

(4)

\displaystyle(1_{\mathcal{A}}\otimes\mu_{g})s

\displaystyle=s(1_{\mathcal{A}}\otimes\lambda_{g})\qquad

\displaystyle\forall g\in G.

(5)

Here, we do not distinguish between
\mathcal{A}
and
\pi(\mathcal{A})\subseteq\mathcal{L}(\mathfrak{H}_{\mathcal{A}})
for the sake of brevity, and the tensor product
\mathcal{A}\otimes\mathcal{K}(L^{2}(G),\mathfrak{H})
is closed with respect to the operator norm, where
\mathcal{K}(L^{2}(G),\mathfrak{H})
is regarded as the respective corner of
\mathcal{K}(L^{2}(G)\oplus\mathfrak{H})
.

As we will mainly be concerned with the implication “
(a)\Rightarrow(b)
”, we have decided to give the proof of this implication at this point only and to move the proof of the implication “
(b)\Rightarrow(a)
” to Appendix
A
.

Proof.

If the C
∗
-dynamical system
(\mathcal{A},G,\alpha)
is free, then for each
\sigma\in\Irrep(G)
there is a finite-dimensional Hilbert space
\mathfrak{H}_{\sigma}
and an isometry
s(\sigma)\in\mathcal{A}\otimes\mathcal{L}(V_{\sigma},\mathfrak{H}_{\sigma})
satisfying
\alpha_{g}\bigl(s(\sigma)\bigr)=s(\sigma)(1_{\mathcal{A}}\otimes\sigma_{g})
for all
g\in G
(
cf.

[
58
, Lem. 3.2]
). To establish the claims in (b), we consider the unitary representation
\mu:G\to\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{H})
defined by

\displaystyle\mathfrak{H}

\displaystyle:=\bigoplus_{\sigma\in\Irrep(G)}\mathfrak{H}_{\sigma}\otimes\bar{V}_{\sigma}

and

\displaystyle\mu_{g}

\displaystyle:=\bigoplus_{\sigma\in\Irrep(G)}1_{\mathfrak{H}_{\sigma}}\otimes\bar{\sigma}_{g}.

(6)

Additionally, we choose a faithful covariant representation
(\pi,u)
of
(\mathcal{A},G,\alpha)
on some Hilbert space
\mathfrak{H}_{\mathcal{A}}
and decompose
L^{2}(G)=\bigoplus_{\sigma\in\Irrep(G)}V_{\sigma}\otimes\bar{V}_{\sigma}
into its isotypic components such that the left and right regular representation read as
\lambda_{g}=\bigoplus_{\sigma\in\Irrep(G)}1_{V_{\sigma}}\otimes\bar{\sigma}_{g}
and
r_{g}=\bigoplus_{\sigma\in\Irrep(G)}\sigma_{g}\otimes 1_{\bar{V}_{\sigma}}
for all
g\in G
, respectively. We also patch together the isometries:

s:=\bigoplus_{\sigma\in\Irrep(G)}s(\sigma)\otimes 1_{\bar{V}_{\sigma}}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathfrak{H}_{\mathcal{A}}\otimes L^{2}(G),\mathfrak{H}_{\mathcal{A}}\otimes\mathfrak{H}).

(7)

By construction,
\mu:G\to\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{H})
has finite-dimensional multiplicity spaces. Furthermore, straightforward computations reveal that

\displaystyle s^{*}s

\displaystyle=\bigoplus_{\sigma\in\Irrep(G)}s(\sigma)^{*}s(\sigma)\otimes 1_{\bar{V}_{\sigma}}=1_{\mathcal{A}}\otimes 1_{G},

\displaystyle(u_{g}\otimes 1_{\mathfrak{H}})s(u_{g}^{*}\otimes r_{g}^{*})

\displaystyle=\bigoplus_{\sigma\in\Irrep(G)}\alpha_{g}\bigl(s(\sigma)\bigr)(1_{\mathcal{A}}\otimes\sigma_{g}^{*})\otimes 1_{\bar{V}_{\sigma}}=s\qquad\forall g\in G,

\displaystyle\mu_{g}s

\displaystyle=\bigoplus_{\sigma\in\Irrep(G)}s(\sigma)\otimes\bar{\sigma}_{g}=s\lambda_{g}\qquad\forall g\in G.

In other words,
s
is an isometry satisfying the Equations (
4
) and (
5
). It therefore remains to deal with Equation (
3
). Indeed, given
a\in\mathcal{A}
and an operator
T
on
L^{2}(G)
such that
T\bigl(L^{2}(G)\bigr)\subseteq V_{\sigma}\otimes\bar{V}_{\sigma}
for some
\sigma\in\Irrep(G)
, it is easily seen that
a\otimes T\in\mathcal{A}\otimes\mathcal{K}\bigl(L^{2}(G)\bigr)
has the following property:

s(a\otimes T)=(s(\sigma)\otimes 1_{\bar{V}_{\sigma}})(a\otimes T)\in\mathcal{A}\otimes\mathcal{K}(L^{2}(G),\mathfrak{H}_{\sigma}\otimes\bar{V}_{\sigma})\subseteq\mathcal{A}\otimes\mathcal{K}(L^{2}(G),\mathfrak{H}).

But from the above we also obtain Equation (
3
), because the set of such operators is total in
\mathcal{A}\otimes\mathcal{K}\bigl(L^{2}(G)\bigr)
. This completes the proof of the implication “
(a)\Rightarrow(b)
”.
∎

For the trivial representation of
G
we may without loss of generality choose
\mathfrak{H}_{1}=\mathbb{C}
and
s(1)=1_{\mathcal{A}}
.
If this holds, then we refer to the isometry
s
in Equation (
7
) as
normalized
and notice that the projection
ss^{*}
acts trivially on
\mathfrak{H}_{\mathcal{A}}\otimes\mathfrak{H}_{1}=\mathfrak{H}_{\mathcal{A}}\otimes\mathbb{C}
.

Remark 3.2
.

In much the same way as in the proof of Lemma
3.1
we see that the adjoint
s^{*}\in\mathcal{L}\bigl(\mathfrak{H}_{\mathcal{A}}\otimes\mathfrak{H},\mathfrak{H}_{\mathcal{A}}\otimes L^{2}(G)\bigr)
of
s
satisfies
s^{*}\mathcal{A}\otimes\mathcal{K}(\mathfrak{H})\subseteq\mathcal{A}\otimes\mathcal{K}\bigl(\mathfrak{H},L^{2}(G)\bigr)
or, equivalently, that the isometry
s
additionally satisfies

\mathcal{A}\otimes\mathcal{K}(\mathfrak{H})s\subseteq\mathcal{A}\otimes\mathcal{K}(L^{2}(G),\mathfrak{H}).

For this reason, we can assert that
s
is a multiplier for
\mathcal{A}\otimes\mathcal{K}(L^{2}(G)\oplus\mathfrak{H})
, that is,
s\in\mathcal{M}\bigl(\mathcal{A}\otimes\mathcal{K}(L^{2}(G)\oplus\mathfrak{H})\bigr)
, with
(1_{\mathcal{A}}\otimes p_{\mathfrak{H}})s=0=s(1_{\mathcal{A}}\otimes p_{L^{2}(G)})
, where
p_{\mathfrak{H}}
and
p_{L^{2}(G)}
denote the canonical projections onto
\mathfrak{H}
and
L^{2}(G)
, respectively.

Remark 3.3
.

Let
s^{\prime}(\sigma)\in\mathcal{A}\otimes\mathcal{L}(V_{\sigma},\mathfrak{H}^{\prime}_{\sigma})
,
\sigma\in\Irrep(G)
, be another family of isometries such that
\alpha_{g}(s^{\prime}(\sigma))=s^{\prime}(\sigma)(1_{\mathcal{A}}\otimes\sigma_{g})
for all
g\in G
. Moreover, let
\mu^{\prime}:G\to\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{H}^{\prime})
and
s^{\prime}
be the corresponding unitary representation and isometry, respectively. Then there is a partial isometry
t:\mathfrak{H}_{\mathcal{A}}\otimes\mathfrak{H}\to\mathfrak{H}_{\mathcal{A}}\otimes\mathfrak{H}^{\prime}
satisfying
(1_{\mathcal{A}}\otimes\mu^{\prime}_{g})t=t(1_{\mathcal{A}}\otimes\mu_{g})
for all
g\in G
as well as
s^{\prime}(s^{\prime})^{*}=tt^{*}
and
ss^{*}=t^{*}t
(
cf.

[
58
, Lem. 4.3]
). In particular, the projections
s^{\prime}(s^{\prime})^{*}
and
ss^{*}
are Murray-von Neumann equivalent. This will be relevant later on for our attempt to characterize the faithful covariant representations of free C
∗
-dynamical systems.

Remark 3.4
.

A rich class of free actions is given by so-called cleft actions (see
[
57
]
), which we now briefly recall. We say that a C
∗
-dynamical system
(\mathcal{A},G,\alpha)
is
cleft
if there is a unitary
u\in\mathcal{M}\bigl(\mathcal{A}\otimes C^{*}_{r}(G)\bigr)
satisfying

\bar{\alpha}_{g}(u)=u(1_{\mathcal{A}}\otimes r_{g})\qquad\forall g\in G,

(8)

where
\bar{\alpha}_{g}
denotes the strictly continuous extension of
\alpha_{g}\otimes\id_{C^{*}_{r}(G)}
to
\mathcal{M}\bigl(\mathcal{A}\otimes C^{*}_{r}(G)\bigr)
. It is clear that each cleft C
∗
-dynamical system is free with a possible choice for
\mu
and
\mathfrak{H}
given by
\lambda
and
L^{2}(G)
, respectively. Regarded as noncommutative principal bundles, these actions are essentially characterized by the fact that all associated noncommutative vector bundles are trivial.

4
Representations of free C
∗
-dynamical systems

In this section we look more closely into covariant representations of free C
∗
-dynamical systems.
For this purpose we fix a free C
∗
-dynamical system
(\mathcal{A},G,\alpha)
and let
\mathcal{B}
be its fixed point algebra.

Our first goal is to characterize all faithful covariant representations of
(\mathcal{A},G,\alpha)
up to unitary equivalence.
For a start we consider a faithful covariant representation
(\pi,u)
of
(\mathcal{A},G,\alpha)
with representation space
\mathfrak{H}_{\mathcal{A}}
.
In accordance with Lemma
3.1
we choose a unitary representation
\mu:G\to\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{H})
with finite-dimensional multiplicity spaces as well as an isometry
s\in\mathcal{L}(\mathfrak{H}_{\mathcal{A}}\otimes L^{2}(G),\mathfrak{H}_{\mathcal{A}}\otimes\mathfrak{H})
satisfying the Equations (
3
), (
4
), and (
5
).
We assume that
s
is normalized.
Then
ss^{*}
acts trivially on
\mathfrak{H}_{\mathcal{A}}\otimes\mathfrak{H}_{1}=\mathfrak{H}_{\mathcal{A}}\otimes\mathbb{C}
and, by Equation (
5
), we have
(1_{\mathcal{A}}\otimes\mu_{g})ss^{*}=ss^{*}(1_{\mathcal{A}}\otimes\mu_{g})
for all
g\in G
. Furthermore, Equation (
4
) implies that
ss^{*}
may be restricted to a projection on
\mathfrak{H}_{\mathcal{A}}(1)\otimes\mathfrak{H}
, where
\mathfrak{H}_{\mathcal{A}}(1)
denotes the trivial isotypic component of
\mathfrak{H}_{\mathcal{A}}
. We shall use the letter
p
for this projection in
\mathcal{L}\bigl(\mathfrak{H}_{\mathcal{A}}(1)\otimes\mathfrak{H}\bigr)
.

Next, let us take into account the isometry

J_{u}:\mathfrak{H}_{\mathcal{A}}\to\mathfrak{H}_{\mathcal{A}}\otimes L^{2}(G)=L^{2}(G,\mathfrak{H}_{\mathcal{A}}),\qquad(J_{u}\eta)(g):=u_{g}^{*}\eta.

(9)

We write
\mathfrak{K}_{\mathcal{A}}
for its range, which is the fixed point space of
\mathfrak{H}_{\mathcal{A}}\otimes L^{2}(G)
under the action
u_{g}\otimes r_{g}
,
g\in G
.
Moreover, we denote by
j_{\alpha}
the
∗
-homomorphism

\displaystyle j_{\alpha}:\mathcal{A}\rightarrow C\bigl(G,\pi(\mathcal{A})\bigr)\subseteq\mathcal{L}\bigl(\mathfrak{H}_{\mathcal{A}}\otimes L^{2}(G)\bigr),

\displaystyle j_{\alpha}(x)(g)=\pi\bigl(\alpha_{g^{-1}}(x)\bigr)=u_{g}^{*}\pi(x)u_{g}.

It is evident that
j_{\alpha}
is injective and that
\Ad[1_{\mathcal{A}}\otimes\lambda_{g}]\circ j_{\alpha}=j_{\alpha}\circ\alpha_{g}
for all
g\in G
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Consider the Hilbert space
\mathfrak{H}_{s}:=ss^{*}(\mathfrak{H}_{\mathcal{A}}\otimes\mathfrak{H})
along with the map

\displaystyle\pi_{s}:\mathcal{A}\rightarrow\mathcal{L}(\mathfrak{H}_{s}),\qquad\pi_{s}(x):=sj_{\alpha}(x)s^{*}.

(10)

Then
\pi_{s}
is a faithful
∗
-representation. Additionally,
\pi_{s}
is equivariant
w. r. t.
the action
\alpha_{g}
,
g\in G
, on
\mathcal{A}
and the action
\Ad[1_{\mathcal{A}}\otimes\mu_{g}]
,
g\in G
, on
\mathcal{L}(\mathfrak{H}_{s})
, respectively.

Proof.

Using the isometry property of
s
and the injectivity of
j_{\alpha}
, we easily infer that
\pi_{s}
is a well-defined faithful
∗
-representation. Next, let
g\in G
and let
x\in\mathcal{A}
. Then

\Ad[1_{\mathcal{A}}\otimes\mu_{g}]\bigl(\pi_{s}(x)\bigr)\overset{\eqref{eq:SOPcommuting}}{=}s\bigl(\Ad[1_{\mathcal{A}}\otimes\lambda_{g}](j_{\alpha}(x)\bigr)s^{*}=\pi_{s}\bigl(\alpha_{g}(x)\bigr),

which proves that
\pi_{s}
is also
G
-equivariant.
∎

Because
ss^{*}
lies in
\mathcal{M}\bigl(\mathcal{B}\otimes\mathcal{K}(\mathfrak{H})\bigr)
(see Remark
3.2
) and the compact operators form an ideal, we instantly get the following statement.

Corollary 4.2
.

\pi_{s}(\mathcal{A})
is a subset of the multiplicator algebra
\mathcal{M}\bigl(ss^{*}\bigl(\mathcal{B}\otimes\mathcal{K}(\mathfrak{H})\bigr)ss^{*}\bigr)
.

The preceding discussion entails that
(\mathcal{A},G,\alpha)
can be covariantly represented as multiplicators on
\mathcal{B}\otimes\mathcal{K}(\mathfrak{H})
.
In consequence, any
∗
-representation
\pi_{\mathcal{B}}:\mathcal{B}\to\mathcal{L}(\mathfrak{H}_{\mathcal{B}})
gives rise to a covariant representation,
\bigl((\pi_{\mathcal{B}}\otimes\id)\circ\pi_{s},1_{\mathcal{B}}\otimes\mu\bigr)
, of
(\mathcal{A},G,\alpha)
on the Hilbert space
\pi_{\mathcal{B}}\otimes\id(ss^{*})(\mathfrak{H}_{\mathcal{B}}\otimes\mathfrak{H})
that has trivial isotypic component
\mathfrak{H}_{\mathcal{B}}
.
If
\pi_{\mathcal{B}}
is faithful, then so is the covariant representation.
We shall now demonstrate that, in fact, each faithful covariant representation of
(\mathcal{A},G,\alpha)
is of this form.

To begin with, we notice that each
\pi_{s}(x)
,
x\in\mathcal{A}
, intertwines the unitary representation
G\ni g\mapsto u_{g}\otimes 1_{\mathfrak{H}}\in\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{H}_{s})
. Hence the subspace
\mathfrak{H}_{p}:=p\bigl(\mathfrak{H}_{\mathcal{A}}(1)\otimes\mathfrak{H}\bigr)\subseteq\mathfrak{H}_{s}
is invariant under
\pi_{s}
and, thus, we obtain a
∗
-representation of
\mathcal{A}
on
\mathfrak{H}_{p}
by putting

\pi_{p}:\mathcal{A}\rightarrow\mathcal{L}(\mathfrak{H}_{p}),\qquad\pi_{p}(x):=\pi_{s}(x)|_{\mathfrak{H}_{p}}^{\mathfrak{H}_{p}}.

(11)

Our next result establishes that
\mathfrak{H}_{p}
is isomorphic to
\mathfrak{K}_{\mathcal{A}}
, the range of the map
J_{u}
from Equation (
9
).

Lemma 4.3
.

For the map
\Phi_{s}:\mathfrak{K}_{\mathcal{A}}\rightarrow\mathfrak{H}_{p}
,
\Phi_{s}(f):=s(f)
the following assertions hold:

1.

\Phi_{s}
is a unitary map such that
(1_{\mathcal{A}}\otimes\mu_{g})\Phi_{s}=\Phi_{s}(1_{\mathcal{A}}\otimes\lambda_{g})
for all
g\in G
.

2.

\Phi_{s}(\eta\otimes 1_{L^{2}(G)}):=\eta\otimes 1_{\mathbb{C}}
for all
\eta\in\mathfrak{H}_{\mathcal{A}}(1)
.

3.

\Phi_{s}j_{\alpha}(x)=\pi_{p}(x)\Phi_{s}
for all
x\in\mathcal{A}
.

Proof.

For the first statement we claim that
s(\mathfrak{K}_{\mathcal{A}})=\mathfrak{H}_{p}
.
From this it follows that
\Phi_{s}
is well-defined, surjective, and hence unitary, because
s
is an isometry.
To prove the claim, we apply
ss^{*}s=s
and
s
to the inclusions
s(\mathfrak{K}_{\mathcal{A}})\subseteq\mathfrak{H}_{\mathcal{A}}(1)\otimes\mathfrak{H}
and
s^{*}(\mathfrak{H}_{\mathcal{A}}(1)\otimes\mathfrak{H})\subseteq\mathfrak{K}_{\mathcal{A}}
, respectively, which in turn are a consequence of Equation (
4
).
Furthermore, the commutation relations
\lambda_{g}r_{g}=r_{g}\lambda_{g}
,
g\in G
, and Equation (
5
) make it obvious that
\Phi_{s}
is
G
-equivariant.
The second statement is due to the fact that
s
is normalized, while the last statement is clear from the defining Equation (
10
).
∎

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
(\mathcal{A},G,\alpha)
be a free C
∗
-dynamical system with fixed point algebra
\mathcal{B}
. Furthermore, let
\mathfrak{H}_{\mathcal{B}}
be a nontrivial representation space of
\mathcal{B}
. Then each faithful covariant representation of
(\mathcal{A},G,\alpha)
such that the trivial isotypic component of the underlying representation space is isomorphic to
\mathfrak{H}_{\mathcal{B}}
is, up to unitary equivalence, of the form

\displaystyle\bigl(p(\mathfrak{H}_{\mathcal{B}}\otimes\mathfrak{H}),\pi_{p},1_{\mathcal{B}}\otimes\mu\bigr),

(12)

where
\mathfrak{H}
,
\mu
,
p
, and
\pi_{p}
are as above (
cf.
Lemma
3.1
and Equation (
11
)).

Proof.

We shall have established the theorem if we show that there is a unitary map
\Phi:\mathfrak{H}_{\mathcal{A}}\to\mathfrak{H}_{p}
such that
\Phi u_{g}=(1_{\mathcal{A}}\otimes\mu_{g})\Phi
for all
g\in G
,
\Phi(\eta)=\eta\otimes 1_{\mathbb{C}}
for all
\eta\in\mathfrak{H}_{\mathcal{A}}(1)
, and
\Phi\pi(x)=\pi_{p}(x)\Phi
for all
x\in\mathcal{A}
.
For this, we simply need to look at the composition
\Phi_{s}J_{u}
, because the map
J_{u}
happens to satisfy equations similar to those of Lemma
4.3
.
∎

Taking advantage of Remark
3.3
, we even get the following result:

Corollary 4.5
.

All faithful covariant representations of
(\mathcal{A},G,\alpha)
are unitarily equivalent.

We continue by considering an arbitrary covariant representation
(\pi,u)
of
(\mathcal{A},G,\alpha)
on a Hilbert space
\mathfrak{H}_{\mathcal{A}}
and bring to mind that
\pi\bigl(A(\sigma)\bigr)\,.\,\mathfrak{H}_{\mathcal{A}}(1)\subseteq\mathfrak{H}_{\mathcal{A}}(\sigma)
for all
\sigma\in\Irrep(G)
.
Our second goal is to establish that these inclusions are, in fact, equalities.
To prove this, we proceed as follows.
For each
\sigma\in\Irrep(G)
we identify

\Gamma_{\mathfrak{H}_{\mathcal{A}}}(\sigma):=\{\eta\in\mathfrak{H}_{\mathcal{A}}\otimes V_{\sigma}:u_{g}\otimes\sigma_{g}(\eta)=\eta\quad\forall g\in G\}

with the multiplicity space of
\mathfrak{H}_{\mathcal{A}}(\bar{\sigma})
and note that
\Phi_{u}\otimes\id_{V_{\sigma}}
provides an isomorphism between
\Gamma_{\mathfrak{H}_{\mathcal{A}}}(\sigma)
an the multiplicity space of
\mathfrak{K}_{\mathcal{A}}(\bar{\sigma})
, the latter being the
\bar{\sigma}
-isotypic component of
\mathfrak{K}_{\mathcal{A}}
under the action
1_{\mathcal{A}}\otimes\lambda_{g}
,
g\in G
.
Furthermore, for each
\sigma\in\Irrep(G)
we write
\mathfrak{H}_{\sigma}
for the multiplicity space of
\mathfrak{H}(\bar{\sigma})
(
cf.
Equation (
6
)) and infer that
p(\mathfrak{H}_{\mathcal{A}}(1)\otimes\mathfrak{H}_{\sigma})
may be regarded as the multiplicity space of
\mathfrak{H}_{p}(\bar{\sigma})=p(\mathfrak{H}_{\mathcal{A}}(1)\otimes\mathfrak{H})(\bar{\sigma})
.
From this and Lemma
4.3
it follows that for each
\sigma\in\Irrep(G)
the map

\mathfrak{H}_{\mathcal{A}}(1)\otimes\mathfrak{H}_{\sigma}\to\Gamma_{\mathfrak{H}_{\mathcal{A}}}(\sigma),\qquad\zeta\mapsto\pi\bigl(s(\sigma)\bigr)^{*}(\zeta)

(13)

is surjective, where
\pi\bigl(s(\sigma)\bigr)\in\pi(\mathcal{A})\otimes\mathcal{L}(V_{\sigma},\mathfrak{H}_{\sigma})\subseteq\mathcal{L}(\mathfrak{H}_{\mathcal{A}}\otimes V_{\sigma},\mathfrak{H}_{\mathcal{A}}\otimes\mathfrak{H}_{\sigma})
denotes the isometry coming from disassembling
s
(
cf.
Lemma
3.1
).
Finally, we recall that for each
\sigma\in\Irrep(G)
the map
\Gamma_{\mathfrak{H}_{\mathcal{A}}}(\bar{\sigma})\otimes V_{\sigma}\to\mathfrak{H}_{\mathcal{A}}(\sigma)
given on simple tensors by
\zeta\otimes v\mapsto\id\otimes\text{ev}_{v}(\zeta)
is unitary, where
\text{ev}_{v}(\bar{w}):=\langle w,v\rangle_{{#1}}
for all
\bar{w}\in\bar{V}_{\sigma}
.
Having disposed of these preliminary steps, for each
\sigma\in\Irrep(G)
we may now conclude that

\displaystyle\mathfrak{H}_{\mathcal{A}}(\sigma)

\displaystyle=\lin\{\ev_{v}(\zeta):v\in V_{\sigma},\zeta\in\Gamma_{\mathfrak{H}_{\mathcal{A}}}(\bar{\sigma})\}

\displaystyle=\lin\bigl\{\ev_{v}\bigl(\pi\bigl(s(\bar{\sigma})\bigr)^{*}(\xi\otimes\eta)\bigr):v\in V_{\sigma},\xi\in\mathfrak{H}_{\mathcal{A}}(1),\eta\in\mathfrak{H}_{\bar{\sigma}}\bigr\}.

Since for each
v\in V_{\sigma}
and
\eta\in\mathfrak{H}_{\bar{\sigma}}
the operator
\mathfrak{H}_{\mathcal{A}}(1)\ni\xi\mapsto\ev_{v}\bigl(\pi\bigl(s(\bar{\sigma})\bigr)^{*}(\xi\otimes\eta)\bigr)\in\mathfrak{H}_{\mathcal{A}}
lies in
\pi\bigl(A(\sigma)\bigr)
, we have established our claim:

Corollary 4.6
.

Let
(\pi,u)
be a covariant representation of
(\mathcal{A},G,\alpha)
on a Hilbert space
\mathfrak{H}_{\mathcal{A}}
. Then for each
\sigma\in\Irrep(G)
we have
\pi\bigl(A(\sigma)\bigr)\,.\,\mathfrak{H}_{\mathcal{A}}(1)=\mathfrak{H}_{\mathcal{A}}(\sigma)
.

5
Lifting spectral triples

In this section we study permanence properties of free C
∗
-dynamical systems with respect to spectral triples.
For a start we fix the following data:

•

a compact Lie group
G
of dimension
n
with Lie algebra
\Lie(G)
;

•

a free C
∗
-dynamical system
(\mathcal{A},G,\alpha)
with fixed point algebra
\mathcal{B}
;

•

for each
\sigma\in\Irrep(G)
a finite-dimensional Hilbert space
\mathfrak{H}_{\sigma}
and an isometry
s(\sigma)
in
\mathcal{A}\otimes\mathcal{L}(V_{\sigma},\mathfrak{H}_{\sigma})
satisfying
\alpha_{g}\bigl(s(\sigma)\bigr)=s(\sigma)(1_{\mathcal{A}}\otimes\sigma_{g})
for all
g\in G
(
cf.

[
58
, Lem. 3.2]
).
In particular, for
1\in\Irrep(G)
, we choose
\mathfrak{H}_{1}:=\mathbb{C}
and
s(1):=1_{\mathcal{A}}
;

•

a spectral triple
\mathbb{D}_{\mathcal{B}}:=(\mathcal{B}_{0},\pi_{\mathcal{B}},\mathfrak{H}_{\mathcal{B}},D_{\mathcal{B}})
on
\mathcal{B}
.

Our main objective is to construct a spectral triple
\mathbb{D}_{\mathcal{A}}
on
\mathcal{A}
by means of the above data that extends
\mathbb{D}_{\mathcal{B}}
in the sense of Definition
5.1
and incorporates the geometry of
G
.

Definition 5.1
.

Let
\mathbb{D}_{\mathcal{A}}=(\mathcal{A}_{0},\pi_{\mathcal{A}},\mathfrak{H}_{\mathcal{A}},D_{\mathcal{A}})
and
\mathbb{D}_{\mathcal{B}}=(\mathcal{B}_{0},\pi_{\mathcal{B}},\mathfrak{H}_{\mathcal{B}},D_{\mathcal{B}})
be two spectral triples and suppose that
\mathcal{B}_{0}\subseteq\mathcal{A}_{0}
. We say that
\mathbb{D}_{\mathcal{A}}
is a
lift
of
\mathbb{D}_{\mathcal{B}}
or that
\mathbb{D}_{\mathcal{A}}

lifts

\mathbb{D}_{\mathcal{B}}
if there is an isometry
t:\mathfrak{H}_{\mathcal{B}}\to\mathfrak{H}_{\mathcal{A}}
such that the following conditions are satisfied:

1.

\pi_{\mathcal{A}}(b)t=t\pi_{\mathcal{B}}(b)
for all
b\in\mathcal{B}_{0}
;

2.

t\bigl(\dom(D_{B})\bigr)\subseteq\dom(D_{A})
and
D_{A}t=tD_{B}
on
\dom(D_{B})
.

Combining
.1.
and
.2.
gives
[D_{\mathcal{A}},\pi_{\mathcal{A}}(b)]t=t[D_{\mathcal{B}},\pi_{\mathcal{B}}(b)]
for all
b\in\mathcal{B}
.

Remark 5.2
.

Definition
5.1
is weak in the sense that it only addresses the Hilbert space
t(\mathfrak{H}_{\mathcal{B}})\subseteq\mathfrak{H}_{\mathcal{A}}
.
We may look at the sets of differential 1-forms
\Omega^{1}(\mathcal{A}_{0})
and
\Omega^{1}(\mathcal{B}_{0})
associated with the spectral triples
\mathbb{D}_{\mathcal{A}}
and
\mathbb{D}_{\mathcal{B}}
, respectively (see,
e. g.
,
[
29
, Sec. 8.1]
and ref. therein).
If
\mathbb{D}_{\mathcal{A}}
is a lift of
\mathbb{D}_{\mathcal{B}}
, then it follows immediately that

\pi_{\mathcal{A}}(x)[D_{\mathcal{A}},\pi_{\mathcal{A}}(y)]\,t=t\,\pi_{\mathcal{B}}(x)[D_{\mathcal{B}},\pi_{\mathcal{B}}(y)]

for all
x,y\in\mathcal{B}_{0}
, and hence
\Omega^{1}(\mathcal{B}_{0})=t^{*}\Omega^{1}(\mathcal{A}_{0})t
.
However, in general
\Omega^{1}(\mathcal{B}_{0})
does not embed into
\Omega^{1}(\mathcal{A}_{0})
as a
\mathcal{B}_{0}
-bimodule.
For the naïve construction of a lift given in this article the natural embedding
\pi_{\mathcal{B}}(x)[D_{\mathcal{B}},\pi_{\mathcal{B}}(y)]\mapsto\pi_{\mathcal{A}}(x)[D_{\mathcal{A}},\pi_{\mathcal{A}}(y)]
for all
x,y\in\mathcal{B}_{0}
is well-defined only under additional assumptions.
Without further requirements we do not yet know whether this map becomes well-defined with a more refined construction.

Example 5.3
.

In
[
19
,
21
]
the authors consider spectral triples that are equivariant with respect to a torus action. Given such a spectral triple
\mathbb{D}_{\mathcal{A}}=(\mathcal{A}_{0},\pi_{\mathcal{A}},\mathfrak{H}_{\mathcal{A}},D_{\mathcal{A}},J_{\mathcal{A}})
, they show that
\mathbb{D}_{\mathcal{A}}
may under certain conditions (
cf.

[
21
, Def. 3.3 and Def. 3.4]
) be restricted to a spectral triple
\mathbb{D}_{\mathcal{B}}=(\mathcal{B}_{0},\pi_{\mathcal{B}},\mathfrak{H}_{\mathcal{B}},D_{\mathcal{B}},J_{\mathcal{B}})
of the respective fixed point algebra
\mathcal{B}_{0}
. Then it is an easy matter to check that
\mathbb{D}_{\mathcal{A}}
lifts
\mathbb{D}_{\mathcal{B}}
in the sense of Definition
5.1
.

A key feature of our free C
∗
-dynamical system
(\mathcal{A},G,\alpha)
is the factor system associated with the isometries
s(\sigma)
,
\sigma\in\Irrep(G)
, (see
[
58
, Def. 4.1]
), which we now recall for the convenience of the reader.
Given a finite-dimensional representation
(\sigma,V_{\sigma})
of
G
, we decompose it into irreducible subrepresentations
\sigma=\sigma_{1}\oplus\dots\oplus\sigma_{n}
and define an isometry
s(\sigma)\in\mathcal{A}\otimes\mathcal{L}(V_{\sigma},\mathfrak{H}_{\sigma})
satisfying
\alpha_{g}\bigl(s(\sigma)\bigr)=s(\sigma)(1_{\mathcal{A}}\otimes\sigma_{g})
for all

In this way we extend the mapping

to an additive functor from the representation category of

to a family of

Notably, it is easy to check that for each finite-dimensional representation
\sigma

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\mathcal{A}}(\sigma)=s(\sigma)^{*}(\mathcal{B}\otimes\mathfrak{H}_{\sigma}).

Furthermore, we obtain a
∗

of the factor system.
For each pair

we obtain an element

of the factor system.
Here

and

and

The most important relations of the coactions and the cocycles are captured by the following equations:

\displaystyle\omega(\sigma,\tau)\omega(\sigma,\tau)^{*}=\gamma_{\sigma\otimes\tau}(1_{\mathcal{B}}),

\displaystyle\qquad\omega(\sigma,\tau)^{*}\omega(\sigma,\tau)=(\gamma_{\tau})_{13}\bigl(\gamma_{\sigma}(1_{\mathcal{B}})\bigr),

(14)

\displaystyle\gamma_{\sigma\otimes\tau}(b)\omega(\sigma,\tau)

\displaystyle=\omega(\sigma,\tau)(\gamma_{\tau})_{13}\bigl(\gamma_{\sigma}(b)\bigr),

\displaystyle\omega(\sigma,\tau\otimes\rho)\omega(\tau,\rho)_{134}

\displaystyle=\omega(\sigma\otimes\tau,\rho)(\gamma_{\rho})_{14}\bigr(\omega(\sigma,\tau)\bigr)

for all finite-dimensional representations
\sigma,\tau,\rho
of

and

5.3
Lifting the Dirac operator

We now turn to the construction of a Dirac operator, which is the only point remaining in our endeavour to establish a spectral triple on
\mathcal{A}
.
The procedure naturally falls into three parts.
First, we construct a “horizontal” lift of

to an operator on
\mathfrak{H}_{p}

Second, we associate a “vertical” Dirac operator with the unitary representation
\mu:G\to\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{H})

Finally, we put together the horizontal and the vertical part in a suitable way.

5.3.1
The horizontal lift

By the definition of the

. Passing over to the Hilbert space direct sum, we may conclude, from

\displaystyle p(\sigma_{1}):=s(\sigma_{1})s^{*}(\sigma_{1})=\frac{1}{2}\begin{pmatrix}1+x&0&w_{1}&w_{2}\\

\bar{w}_{1}&-\bar{z}_{\theta}w_{2}&1-x&0\\
\bar{w}_{2}&z_{\theta}w_{1}&0&1-x\end{pmatrix}.

For a Dirac operator on
\mathcal{A}(\mathbb{S}_{\theta}^{4})
one has the following construction.
Let
D
be the classical Dirac operator on
\mathbb{S}^{4}
, that is, the unbounded self-adjoint operator on the space
L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{4},\mathcal{S})
of square integrable spinors on
\mathbb{S}^{4}
.
Furthermore, let
L^{2}(\mathbb{A}^{2}_{\smash{\theta/2}})
be the GNS space of the quantum 2-torus
\mathbb{A}^{2}_{\smash{\theta/2}}
with respect to its tracial state (
cf.
Section
7
) and consider the essentially self-adjoint operator
D\otimes 1
on
L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{4},\mathcal{S})\otimes L^{2}(\mathbb{A}^{2}_{\smash{\theta/2}})
with domain
\dom(D\otimes 1):=\dom(D)\otimes_{\text{alg}}L^{2}(\mathbb{A}^{2}_{\smash{\theta/2}})
.
The latter Hilbert space comes equipped with a natural action of the classical
\mathbb{T}^{2}
-torus and the restriction
D_{\theta}
of the self-adjoint extension of
D\otimes 1
to the corresponding fixed point algebra
L^{2}(\mathbb{S}_{\theta}^{4},\mathcal{S})
is the Dirac operator on
\mathcal{A}(\mathbb{S}_{\theta}^{4})
.
It is now a consequence of
[
41
, Sec. 4]
that the commutator
[D_{\theta}\otimes 1_{\mathbb{C}^{4}},\omega(\sigma_{1},1)]=[D_{\theta}\otimes 1_{\mathbb{C}^{4}},p(\sigma_{1})]
does not vanish.

Summarizing, we have seen that the operator
D_{h}
has a number of good properties and is almost a Dirac operator for our noncommutative principal bundle
(\mathcal{A},G,\alpha)
.
In fact, one missing feature for
D_{h}
to be a Dirac operator on
\mathcal{A}
is that it does not incorporate the geometry of the “fibre”
G
.
Another one is that
D_{h}
does in general not need to have compact resolvent.
In order to resolve these issues, we will add a vertical term, whose construction is the concern of the upcoming section.

5.3.2
The vertical Dirac operator

In this section we associate a vertical Dirac operator with the free C
∗
-dynamical system
(\mathcal{A},G,\alpha)
.
For this purpose, we endow
\Lie(G)
with an
\Ad
-invariant inner product, consider a finite-dimensional
∗
-representation
\pi_{\text{spin}}:\mathbb{C}\ell(G)\to\mathcal{L}(\mathfrak{H}_{\text{spin}})
of the Clifford algebra
\mathbb{C}\ell(G)
of
\Lie(G)
, and put
F_{X}:=\pi_{\text{spin}}(X)
for each
X\in\Lie(G)
.

For the construction of a Dirac operator, we proceed similar to
[
28
]
.
In greater detail, we again consider the unitary representation
\mu:G\to\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{H})
and recall from Section
2
that for any orthonormal basis
X_{1},\dots,X_{n}
of
\Lie(G)
the unbounded operator

\displaystyle D_{v}:=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\partial_{X_{k}}\mu\otimes F_{X_{k}}

on
\mathfrak{H}\otimes\mathfrak{H}_{\text{spin}}
with domain
\dom(D_{v}):=\mathfrak{H}^{\infty}\otimes\mathfrak{H}_{\text{spin}}
is essentially self-adjoint.
For simplicity of notation, we utilize the same letter for its unique self-adjoint extension. Because the unitary representation
\mu:G\to\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{H})
has finite-dimensional multiplicity spaces,
D_{v}
has compact resolvent by Lemma
2.2
. Throughout the remainder of this article, we refer to
D_{v}
as the
vertical Dirac operator
associated with
(\mathcal{A},G,\alpha)
.

Next, let us look at the unbounded operator associated with the unitary representations
u_{\mathcal{A}}:G\to\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{H}_{p})
, which is the self-adjoint extension of

\displaystyle\hat{D}_{v}:=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\partial_{X_{k}}u_{\mathcal{A}}\otimes F_{X_{k}}

on
\mathfrak{H}_{p}\otimes\mathfrak{H}_{\text{spin}}
with domain
\dom(\hat{D}_{v}):=\mathfrak{H}_{p}^{\infty}\otimes\mathfrak{H}_{\text{spin}}
.
Furthermore, for each
X\in\Lie(G)
, we have
[\partial_{X}u_{\mathcal{A}},\pi_{\mathcal{A}}(x)]=\partial_{X}\pi_{\mathcal{A}}\bigl(\alpha(x)\bigr)
for all
x\in\mathcal{A}^{\infty}
.

Corollary 5.12

(
cf.

[
27
, Prop. 2.10]
)
.

The following assertions hold for the unbounded operator
\hat{D}_{v}
on
\mathfrak{H}_{p}\otimes\mathfrak{H}_{\text{spin}}
:

1.

\hat{D}_{v}
is self-adjoint and
\hat{D}_{v}=1_{\mathfrak{H}_{\mathcal{B}}}\otimes D_{v}
on
\mathfrak{H}_{p}^{\infty}\otimes\mathfrak{H}_{\text{spin}}
.

2.

[\hat{D}_{v},\pi_{\mathcal{A}}(x)]
is bounded for all
x\in\mathcal{A}^{\infty}
. In greater detail, for each
x\in\mathcal{A}^{\infty}
we have
\pi_{\mathcal{A}}(x)\bigl(\dom(\hat{D}_{v})\bigr)\subseteq\dom(\hat{D}_{v})
and
[\hat{D}_{v},\pi_{\mathcal{A}}(x)]=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\partial_{X_{k}}\pi_{\mathcal{A}}\bigl(\alpha(x)\bigr)\otimes F_{X_{k}}
.

We note that
\hat{D}_{v}=0
on
\mathfrak{H}_{\mathcal{B}}\otimes\mathfrak{H}_{\text{spin}}\subseteq\mathfrak{H}_{p}^{\infty}\otimes\mathfrak{H}_{\text{spin}}
and that
[\hat{D}_{v},\pi_{\mathcal{A}}(b)]=0
for all
b\in\mathcal{B}
, which may justify the expression “vertical”.

5.3.3
The assembled Dirac operator

We are finally in a position to present a spectral triple on
\mathcal{A}
that lifts the spectral triple
\mathbb{D}_{\mathcal{B}}
and incorporates the geometry of
G
.
Let
D_{h}
be the horizontal lift of
D_{\mathcal{B}}
to
\mathfrak{H}_{p}
from Section
5.3.1
and let
D_{v}
be the vertical Dirac operator on
\mathfrak{H}\otimes\mathfrak{H}_{\text{spin}}
as introduced in the preceding section.
Additionally, let
\gamma_{\text{spin}}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathfrak{H}_{\text{spin}})
be a self-adjoint operator satisfying
\gamma_{\text{spin}}^{2}=1_{\mathfrak{H}_{\text{spin}}}
and
D_{v}(1_{\mathfrak{H}}\otimes\gamma_{\text{spin}})=-(1_{\mathfrak{H}}\otimes\gamma_{\text{spin}})D_{v}
.
Such an operator can always be found by increasing
\mathfrak{H}_{\text{spin}}
if necessary.
Then we may look at the symmetric operator

\displaystyle D_{\text{ref}}:=D_{\mathcal{B}}\otimes 1_{\mathfrak{H}}\otimes\gamma_{\text{spin}}+1_{\mathfrak{H}_{\mathcal{B}}}\otimes D_{v}

with domain

\displaystyle\dom(D_{\text{ref}}):=\dom(D_{\mathcal{B}})\otimes_{\text{alg}}\dom(D_{v})\subseteq\mathfrak{H}_{\mathcal{B}}\otimes\mathfrak{H}\otimes\mathfrak{H}_{\text{spin}}.

According to
[
17
, Sec. 4]
, its closure
\bar{D}_{\text{ref}}
is self-adjoint with pure point spectrum consisting of countably many real eigenvalues, each with finite multiplicity, and the only limit point of their absolute values is given by
+\infty
. In particular,
\bar{D}_{\text{ref}}
has compact resolvent. Applying Lemma
2.1
and combining Theorem
5.9
with Corollary
5.12
, we get the main results of this paper:

Theorem 5.13
.

For the operator

D_{\mathcal{A}}:=p\bar{D}_{\text{ref}}\,p=D_{h}\otimes\gamma_{\text{spin}}+p(1_{\mathfrak{H}_{\mathcal{B}}}\otimes D_{v})p=D_{h}\otimes\gamma_{\text{spin}}+\hat{D}_{v}

(25)

with domain
p\dom(\bar{D}_{\text{ref}})
on
\mathfrak{H}_{\mathcal{A}}:=p(\mathfrak{H}_{\mathcal{B}}\otimes\mathfrak{H}\otimes\mathfrak{H}_{\text{spin}})
the following assertions hold:

1.

D_{\mathcal{A}}
is self-adjoint and has compact resolvent.

2.

Under the hypotheses of Theorem
5.9
, the commutator
[D_{\mathcal{A}},\pi_{\mathcal{A}}(x)]
is bounded for all
x\in\mathcal{A}_{0}
, where
\pi_{\mathcal{A}}
is understood to be amplified in the obvious way.

We occasionally write
\hat{D}_{h}:=D_{h}\otimes\gamma_{\text{spin}}
for the horizontal part of
D_{\mathcal{A}}
if no confusion regarding the operator
\gamma_{\text{spin}}
can arise. Equation (
25
) then reads as
D_{\mathcal{A}}=\hat{D}_{h}+\hat{D}_{v}
.

Remark 5.14
.

Suppose the spectral triple
\mathbb{D}_{\mathcal{B}}
is even, which amounts to saying that there is a self-adjoint unitary operator
\gamma_{\mathcal{B}}
on
\mathfrak{H}_{\mathcal{B}}
such that
\gamma_{\mathcal{B}}\pi_{\mathcal{B}}(b)=\pi_{\mathcal{B}}(b)\gamma_{\mathcal{B}}
for all
b\in\mathcal{B}
and
\gamma_{\mathcal{B}}D_{\mathcal{B}}=-D_{\mathcal{B}}\gamma_{\mathcal{B}}
. Then one may work with the operator

\displaystyle D^{\prime}_{\text{ref}}:=D_{\mathcal{B}}\otimes 1_{\mathfrak{H}\otimes\mathfrak{H}_{\text{spin}}}+\gamma_{\mathcal{B}}\otimes D_{v}

with domain
\dom(D^{\prime}_{\text{ref}}):=\dom(D_{\mathcal{B}})\otimes_{\text{alg}}\dom(D_{v})\subseteq\mathfrak{H}_{\mathcal{B}}\otimes\mathfrak{H}\otimes\mathfrak{H}_{\text{spin}}
. All arguments of this article considering
D_{\text{ref}}
likewise apply to
D^{\prime}_{\text{ref}}
.

Remark 5.15
.

Theorem
5.13
has a clear meaning in unbounded
KK
-theory as was kindly pointed out to us by van Suijlekom.
Indeed, our Dirac operator
D_{\mathcal{A}}
in Equation (

) is an example of the Kasparov product
[
37
,
44
]
of the unbounded Kasparov
\mathcal{A}
-
\mathcal{B}
-module
(\mathcal{A}_{0},\pi_{\mathcal{A}},\mathfrak{H}_{p},D_{v})
with the spectral triple
\mathbb{D}_{\mathcal{B}}
and the
D_{\mathcal{B}}
-connection induced by
D_{h}
.

Corollary 5.16
.

\mathbb{D}_{\mathcal{A}}:=(\mathcal{A}_{0},\pi_{\mathcal{A}},\mathfrak{H}_{\mathcal{A}},D_{\mathcal{A}})
is a spectral triple on
\mathcal{A}
that lifts
\mathbb{D}_{\mathcal{B}}
in the sense of Definition
5.1

w. r. t.
the isometry
t:\mathfrak{H}_{\mathcal{B}}\to\mathfrak{H}_{\mathcal{A}}
,
t(\xi):=\xi\otimes 1_{\mathfrak{H}_{\text{spin}}}\otimes 1_{\mathbb{C}^{2}}
.

Remark 5.17
.

At this point we briefly resume the discussion in Remark
5.2
. Indeed, a moment’s thought shows that
\Omega^{1}(\mathcal{B}_{0})
embeds into
\Omega^{1}(\mathcal{A}_{0})
if, for instance,
\gamma_{\sigma}(b)=b\otimes 1
for all
\sigma\in\Irrep(G)
and

(

,
[

In the following sections we investigate how the lift constructed here compares to established examples.

6
Example: Crossed products

Let
\mathcal{B}
be a unital C
∗
-algebra and let
\alpha\in\Aut(\mathcal{B})
.
This example focuses on the crossed product
\mathcal{A}:=\mathcal{B}\rtimes_{\alpha}\mathbb{Z}
equipped with the natural dual circle action
\hat{\alpha}
.
We consider
\mathcal{B}
as a subalgebra of
\mathcal{A}
and write
v\in\mathcal{B}\rtimes_{\alpha}\mathbb{Z}
for the generator of the
\mathbb{Z}
-action defined by
\alpha
.
Then the action
\hat{\alpha}
is given by

\displaystyle\hat{\alpha}_{z}(b)

\displaystyle=b\qquad\forall b\in\mathcal{B}

and

\displaystyle\hat{\alpha}_{z}(v)

\displaystyle=z\cdot v

for all
z\in\mathbb{T}
.
The fixed point algebra
\mathcal{A}^{\mathbb{T}}
is equal to
\mathcal{B}
.
More generally, each isotypic component
\mathcal{A}(k)
,
k\in\mathbb{Z}\cong\Irrep(\mathbb{T})
, is given by
\mathcal{B}v^{k}
.
From this it may be concluded that the C
∗
-dynamical system
(\mathcal{A},\mathbb{T},\hat{\alpha})
is cleft and, therefore, free (
cf.

[
57
, Lem. 4.5]
).
Moreover, we may put
\mathfrak{H}_{k}:=\mathbb{C}
for every
k\in\mathbb{Z}
and let the isometries
u(k):=v^{-k}
,
k\in\mathbb{Z}
, serve as initial data for the construction provided in Section
5
.

Next, let
\mathbb{D}_{\mathcal{B}}:=(\mathcal{B}_{0},\pi_{\mathcal{B}},\mathfrak{H}_{\mathcal{B}},D_{\mathcal{B}})
be a spectral triple on
\mathcal{B}
.
For the sake of simplicity, we identify
\mathcal{B}
with
\pi_{\mathcal{B}}(\mathcal{B})\subseteq\mathcal{L}(\mathfrak{H}_{\mathcal{B}})
.
Then the dense subalgebra
\mathcal{A}_{0}\subseteq\mathcal{A}
from Section
5.1
consists of all elements of the form
\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}b_{k}v^{k}
with only finitely many nonzero elements
b_{k}\in\mathcal{B}_{0}
.
The lifted
∗
-representation
\pi_{\mathcal{A}}:\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{L}(\mathfrak{H}_{p})
introduced in Section
5.2
is given by the Hilbert space
\mathfrak{H}_{p}:=\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z},\mathfrak{H}_{\mathcal{B}})
and the relations

\displaystyle\bigl(\pi_{\mathcal{A}}(b)\eta\bigr)_{k}

\displaystyle=\alpha^{-k}(b)\eta_{k}

and

\displaystyle\bigl(\pi_{\mathcal{A}}(v)\eta\bigr)_{k}

\displaystyle=\eta_{k-1}

for all
b\in\mathcal{B}
,
\eta\in\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z},\mathfrak{H}_{\mathcal{B}})
, and
k\in\mathbb{Z}
.
The horizontal Dirac operator constructed in Section
5.3.1
is given by the self-adjoint extension of the operator
D_{h}=D_{\mathcal{B}}\otimes 1_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z})}
on the domain
\dom(D_{h})=\dom(D_{\mathcal{B}})\otimes\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z})
.
The vertical Dirac operator
D_{v}
, as presented in Section
5.3.2
, is the tensor product of the standard operator on the circle
\mathbb{T}\cong\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}
, that is,
-\imath\frac{d}{dt}
, with the Pauli matrix

\sigma_{2}:=\begin{pmatrix}0&-\imath\\
\imath&0\end{pmatrix}
or, equivalently,

\displaystyle\bigl(D_{v}(\zeta)\bigr)_{k}=k\cdot\sigma_{2}(\zeta_{k})

for all
\zeta=(\zeta_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\in\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z})\otimes\mathbb{C}^{2}=\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z},\mathbb{C}^{2})
.

Now, we additionally assume that
\alpha
generates an equicontinuous group (see
[
7
, Def. 3]
). In particular, we may choose

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{0}:=\Bigl\{b\in\mathcal{B}:\sup_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\big\lVert[D,\pi_{\mathcal{A}}\bigl(\alpha^{k}(b)\bigr)]\big\rVert<\infty\Bigr\}

as a dense unital
∗
-subalgebra of
\mathcal{B}
, which ensures that the operators in Equation (
23
) are uniformly bounded. Corollary
5.16
therefore implies that
\mathbb{D}_{\mathcal{A}}:=(\mathcal{A}_{0},\pi_{\mathcal{A}},\mathfrak{H}_{\mathcal{A}},D_{\mathcal{A}})
is a spectral triple on
\mathcal{A}
that lifts
\mathbb{D}_{\mathcal{B}}
in the sense of Definition
5.1
and builds in the geometry of circle, where
\mathfrak{H}_{\mathcal{A}}:=\mathfrak{H}_{p}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{2}=\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z},\mathfrak{H}_{\mathcal{B}})\otimes\mathbb{C}^{2}
and

\displaystyle\bigr(D_{\mathcal{A}}(\eta)\bigl)_{k}=\bigl(D_{\mathcal{B}}\otimes\sigma_{1}+(k\cdot 1_{\mathfrak{H}_{\mathcal{B}}})\otimes\sigma_{2}\bigr)(\eta_{k})

for all
\eta=(\eta_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\in\mathfrak{H}_{\mathcal{A}}
and the Pauli matrix
\sigma_{1}:=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
1&0\end{pmatrix}
.
In summary, our construction extends the construction provided in the seminal work
[
7
, Sec. 3.4]
by Bellissard, Marcolli, and Reihani.

7
Example: Quantum 4-tori

Let
\theta
be a real skew-symmetric
4\times 4
-matrix and, for
1\leq k,\ell\leq 4
, put
\lambda_{k,\ell}:=\exp(2\pi\imath\theta_{k,\ell})
for short.
In this example we consider the quantum 4-torus
\mathbb{A}^{4}_{\theta}
, which is the universal C
∗
-algebra with unitary generators
u_{1},\dots,u_{4}
satisfying the relation
u_{k}u_{\ell}=\lambda_{k,\ell}u_{\ell}u_{k}
for all
1\leq k,\ell\leq 4
.
The classical torus
\mathbb{T}^{4}
acts naturally on
\mathbb{A}^{4}_{\theta}
via the
∗
-automorphisms given by
\tau(u_{k})=z_{k}\cdot u_{k}
for all
z=(z_{1},\dots,z_{4})\in\mathbb{T}^{4}
and
1\leq k\leq 4
.
This is the so-called
gauge action
.
We write
L^{2}(\mathbb{A}^{4}_{\theta})
for the GNS space with respect to the unique
\tau
-invariant tracial state on
\mathbb{A}_{\theta}^{4}
and assume
\mathbb{A}^{4}_{\theta}\subseteq\mathcal{L}\bigl(L^{2}(\mathbb{A}^{4}_{\theta})\bigr)
.
For this example it is expedient to consider the canonical inner product on
\Lie(\mathbb{T}^{4})=\mathbb{R}^{4}
and to work with the standard orthonormal basis.
Because the gauge action is implemented by unitaries on
L^{2}(\mathbb{A}^{4}_{\theta})
, the directional derivatives for each coordinate provide unbounded skew-symmetric operators
\partial_{1},\dots,\partial_{4}
on
L^{2}(\mathbb{A}^{4}_{\theta})
.
For a Dirac operator on
\mathbb{A}^{4}_{\theta}
, we take into account the irreducible
∗
-representation of
\mathbb{C}\ell(\mathbb{T}^{4})
on the spinors
\mathfrak{H}_{\text{spin}}:=\mathbb{C}^{2}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{2}
.
That is, we choose operators
\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2},\sigma_{3}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{C}^{2})
satisfying
\sigma_{k}^{2}=-1
and
\sigma_{k}\sigma_{\ell}=-\sigma_{\ell}\sigma_{k}
for all
1\leq k\neq\ell\leq 3
and put

\displaystyle F_{1}

\displaystyle:=\sigma_{1}\otimes\sigma_{3},

\displaystyle F_{2}

\displaystyle:=\sigma_{2}\otimes\sigma_{3},

\displaystyle F_{3}

\displaystyle:=1\otimes\sigma_{1},

\displaystyle F_{4}

\displaystyle:=1\otimes\sigma_{2}.

Then the canonical Dirac operator on
\mathbb{A}^{4}_{\theta}
is the self-adjoint extension of

D_{4}:=\partial_{1}\otimes F_{1}+\partial_{2}\otimes F_{2}+\partial_{3}\otimes F_{3}+\partial_{4}\otimes F_{4}

defined on some suitable domain in
L^{2}(\mathbb{A}^{4}_{\theta})\otimes\mathfrak{H}_{\text{spin}}
.

Our study revolves around the restricted gauge action
\alpha:\mathbb{T}^{2}\to\Aut(\mathbb{A}^{4}_{\theta})
defined by

\displaystyle\alpha_{z}(u_{1})

\displaystyle:=u_{1},

\displaystyle\alpha_{z}(u_{2})

\displaystyle:=u_{2},

\displaystyle\alpha_{z}(u_{3})

\displaystyle:=z_{1}\cdot u_{3},

\displaystyle\alpha_{z}(u_{4})

\displaystyle:=z_{2}\cdot u_{4}

for all
z=(z_{1},z_{2})\in\mathbb{T}^{2}
.
Its fixed point algebra is the quantum 2-torus
\mathbb{A}^{2}_{\theta^{\prime}}
generated by the unitaries
u_{1}
and
u_{2}
, where
\theta^{\prime}
denotes the real skew-symmetric
2\times 2
-matrix with upper right off-diagonal entry
\theta_{12}
.
More generally, for each
(k,\ell)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
the corresponding isotypic component is
\mathbb{A}^{4}_{\theta}(k,\ell)
takes the form
u(k,\ell)\mathbb{A}^{2}_{\theta^{\prime}}
for the unitary
u(k,\ell):=u_{4}^{\ell}u_{3}^{k}
.
In particular, the C
∗
-dynamical system
(\mathbb{A}^{4}_{\theta},\mathbb{T}^{2},\alpha)
is cleft and therefore free. Next, let
L^{2}(\mathbb{A}_{\theta^{\prime}}^{2})
be the GNS space of
\mathbb{A}_{\theta^{\prime}}^{2}
with respect to its gauge-invariant trace.
By Section
4
, the unitaries give rise to the
∗
-representation
\pi_{u}:\mathbb{A}^{4}_{\theta}\to\mathcal{L}\bigl(L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2})\otimes L^{2}(\mathbb{A}^{2}_{\theta^{\prime}})\bigr)
uniquely determined by

\displaystyle\pi_{u}(u_{1})

\displaystyle=r_{\theta_{31},\theta_{41}}\otimes u_{1},

\displaystyle\pi_{u}(u_{2})

\displaystyle=r_{\theta_{32},\theta_{42}}\otimes u_{2},

\displaystyle\pi_{u}(u_{3})

\displaystyle=z_{1}\otimes 1,

\displaystyle\pi_{u}(u_{3})

\displaystyle=r_{\theta_{34},0}z_{2}\otimes 1

where
r_{t}
,
t=(t_{1},t_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
, denotes the rotation operator given by
(r_{t}f)(z):=f(z_{1}^{t_{1}},z_{2}^{t_{2}})
for all
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
and
z=(z_{1},z_{2})\in\mathbb{T}^{2}
and
z_{1/2}
stands for the multiplication operator
(z_{1/2})f(z):=z_{1/2}f(z)
for all
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
and
z\in\mathbb{T}^{2}
.
Now, a standard computation establishes that this
∗
-representation is unitarily equivalent to the GNS-representation.

In order to apply the construction of Section
5.3
, we pick the canonical Dirac operator
D_{2}=\partial_{1}\otimes F_{1}+\partial_{2}\otimes F_{2}
on
L^{2}(\mathbb{A}^{2}_{\theta^{\prime}})\otimes\mathbb{C}^{2}
. Then Section
5.3.1
immediately provides the horizontal lift
D_{h}
of
D_{2}
, which is the self-adjoint extension of

D_{h}=1\otimes\partial_{1}\otimes\sigma_{1}+1\otimes\partial_{2}\otimes\sigma_{2}

on some suitable dense domain in

.
The vertical Dirac operator constructed in Section
5.3.2
is given by

D_{v}=\partial_{1}^{\mathbb{T}^{2}}\otimes\sigma_{1}+\partial_{2}^{\mathbb{T}^{2}}\otimes\sigma_{2}

on some suitable dense domain in
L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2})\otimes\mathbb{C}^{2}
, where
\partial_{1}^{\mathbb{T}^{2}}
and
\partial_{2}^{\mathbb{T}^{2}}
denote the derivatives of the translation on
L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
along the respective coordinates.
According to Section
5.3.3
, the assembled Dirac operator on
\mathbb{A}^{4}_{\theta}
then reads

\displaystyle D_{\mathbb{A}^{4}_{\theta}}

\displaystyle=D_{h}\otimes\sigma_{3}+1\otimes D_{v}

\displaystyle=1\otimes\partial_{1}\otimes F_{1}+1\otimes\partial_{2}\otimes F_{2}+\partial_{1}^{\mathbb{T}^{2}}\otimes 1\otimes F_{3}+\partial_{2}^{\mathbb{T}^{2}}\otimes 1\otimes F_{4}

on some suitable dense domain in

Remark 7.1
.

Let
\gamma_{k,\ell}
for

It is straightforward to check that

(26)

.

are fulfilled.
Furthermore, coming back to Remark

, it is an easy task to show that Equation (

) implies that

naturally embeds into

.

Example: Homogeneous spaces

In this example we consider a compact Lie group

-bundle over

. Algebraically we look at the C

-algebra

endowed with the action

(r_{h}f)(g):=f(gh)

In what follows, we write

For the adjoint action of

8.1
Regarding freeness

To establish freeness algebraically, we regard

\mathfrak{H}:=L^{2}(G)

The

8.2
Dirac operators

For the Dirac operators on
G
and
G/H
we follow Rieffel
[
55
]
up to a conventional sign.
For the convenience of the reader we briefly recall their construction.
For this purpose, we endow
\Lie(G)
with an
\Ad
-invariant inner product and decompose it into the direct sum of
\Lie(H)
and its orthogonal complement, denoted by
\Lie(G/H)
.
We utilize
P_{H}
and
P_{G/H}
to be the corresponding orthogonal projections onto
\Lie(H)
and
\Lie(G/H)
, respectively, and we write
\mathbb{C}\ell(G/H)
for the Clifford algebras of
\Lie(G/H)

8.2.1
Dirac operators on
G

For the rest of the paper, we let
F_{X}:\mathbb{C}\ell(G)\to\mathbb{C}\ell(G)
stand for the multiplication by a vector
X\in\Lie(G)
,
i. e.
,
F_{X}(\varphi):=X\cdot\varphi
for all
\varphi\in\mathbb{C}\ell(G)
. To construct a Dirac operator on
G
, we first look at the representation of
C(G)
on the Hilbert space

\mathfrak{S}^{2}(G):=L^{2}\bigl(G,\mathbb{C}\ell(G)\bigr)

by pointwise multiplication operators and identify
\mathcal{T}(G):=C^{\infty}\bigl(G,\Lie(G)\bigr)
with the space of smooth sections of the tangent bundle of
G
in terms of left translations. Obviously,
C^{\infty}(G)
acts on
\mathcal{T}(G)
by pointwise multiplication. Second, we define both a connection and a Clifford multiplication on the subspace
\mathfrak{S}^{\infty}(G)\subseteq\mathfrak{S}^{2}(G)
of all smooth functions by putting for each
X\in\mathcal{T}(G)
and
\varphi\in\mathfrak{S}^{\infty}(G)

\displaystyle\partial_{X}^{G}\varphi(g)

\displaystyle:=\tfrac{d}{dt}\Big|_{t=0}\varphi\bigl(\exp(-tX(g))g\bigr)

and

\displaystyle F_{X}\varphi(g):=F_{X(g)}\varphi(g)

for all
g\in G
, respectively. Finally, we fix a standard module frame
(X_{k})_{k}
of
\mathcal{T}(G)
and extend the essentially self-adjoint operator

D_{G}:=\sum_{k}F_{X_{k}}\;\partial_{X_{k}}^{G}

from the domain
\mathfrak{S}^{\infty}(G)
to a self-adjoint Dirac operator, which we again denote by
D_{G}
.
This operator is independent of the choice of standard module frame (see
[
55
, Sec. 8]
).
However, there is a convenient choice of a standard module frame of
\mathcal{T}(G)
in order to work with the quotient.
Since the identification of
\mathcal{T}(G)
with the tangent bundle is done via left translation and
H
acts on
G
from the right, it is expedient to decompose
\Lie(G)
in the range of elements of
\mathcal{T}(G)
at a point
g\in G
into

\Lie(G)=\Ad_{g}\bigl(\Lie(G/H)\bigr)\oplus\Ad_{g}\bigl(\Lie(H)\bigr).

We may then fix an orthonormal basis
(X_{k})_{k}
of
\Lie(G)
and put

\displaystyle Y_{k}(g)

\displaystyle:=\Ad_{g}P_{G/H}\Ad_{g}^{-1}(X_{k})

and

\displaystyle Z_{k}(g)

\displaystyle:=\Ad_{g}P_{H}\Ad_{g}^{-1}(X_{k})

(27)

for all
g\in G
. Together
(Y_{k})_{k}
and
(Z_{k})_{k}
form a standard module frame of
\mathcal{T}(G)
and we call the corresponding summands of
D_{G}
,

\displaystyle D_{h}

\displaystyle:=\sum_{k}F_{Y_{k}}\;\partial_{Y_{k}}^{G}

and

\displaystyle D_{v}

\displaystyle:=\sum_{k}F_{Z_{k}}\;\partial_{Z_{k}}^{G}

the
horizontal part
and the
vertical part
of
D_{G}
, respectively.

8.2.2
Dirac operators on
G/H

For a Dirac operator on
G/H
we consider the Hilbert space

\mathfrak{S}^{2}(G/H):=\{\varphi\in L^{2}\bigl(G,\mathbb{C}\ell(G/H)\bigr):\varphi(gh)=\Ad_{h}^{-1}\bigl(\varphi(g)\bigr)\quad\forall g\in G,h\in H\}

and let

stand for the
∗
-representation of

on
\mathfrak{S}^{2}(G/H)
by multiplication operators. Moreover, we denote by
\mathfrak{S}^{\infty}(G/H)\subseteq\mathfrak{S}^{2}(G/H)

can be recognized as smooth sections of the tangent bundle of
G/H

F_{X}
for the pointwise Clifford multiplication on
\mathfrak{S}^{\infty}(G/H)
by some

\mathfrak{S}^{2}(G/H)
and
\mathcal{T}(G/H)

\mathfrak{S}^{2}(G)
and
\mathcal{T}(G)

\displaystyle\hat{\varphi}(g)

\displaystyle:=\Ad_{g}\bigl(\varphi(g)\bigr)

\displaystyle\hat{X}(g)

\displaystyle:=\Ad_{g}\bigl(X(g)\bigr)

for all

(Y_{k})_{k}
for
\mathcal{T}(G/H)
and define an operator

It follows from
[
55

that

8.2.3
The horizontal and the vertical part of the lifted Dirac operator

The task is now to lift the Hodge-Dirac operator
\smash{D_{G/H}}
to a Dirac type operator on
G
.
For this purpose, we consider the possibly degenerated
∗
-representation of
C(G)
on the Hilbert space
\mathfrak{S}^{2}(G/H)\otimes\mathfrak{H}\otimes\mathbb{C}\ell(H)
given by

\pi(f):=\bigl(\pi_{G/H}\otimes\id_{\mathcal{L}(\mathfrak{H})}\bigr)\bigl(\pi_{s}(f)\bigr)\otimes 1_{\mathbb{C}\ell(H)},\qquad f\in C(G).

To simplify notation, we swap tensor factors and restrict

to its non-degenerate range, which is the subspace
\mathfrak{S}^{2}\subseteq L^{2}\bigl(G,\mathfrak{H}\otimes\mathbb{C}\ell(G/H)\otimes\mathbb{C}\ell(H)\bigr)
of functions

with

\varphi(gh)=\bigl(p(g)\otimes\Ad_{h}^{-1}\otimes\Ad_{h}^{-1}\bigr)\varphi(g)\qquad\forall g\in G,h\in H.

(28)

Moreover, we consider the subspace
\mathfrak{S}^{\infty}\subseteq\mathfrak{S}^{2}
of functions with range in the algebraic tensor product

\mathfrak{H}^{\infty}
denotes the smooth domain of the unitary representation
\mu:H\to\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{H})

Following the construction of Section

of the Dirac operator is given by

\displaystyle\hat{D}_{h}\varphi

\displaystyle=(p\otimes 1_{\mathbb{C}\ell(G/H)}\otimes 1_{\mathbb{C}\ell(H)})(1_{\mathfrak{H}}\otimes D_{G/H}\otimes\Omega)\varphi,

\mathbb{C}\ell(H)

The vertical part
\hat{D}_{v}:\mathfrak{S}^{\infty}\to\mathfrak{S}^{\infty}
is given by

\hat{D}_{v}\varphi(g)=\sum_{k}\tfrac{d}{dt}\Big|_{t=0}(\mu_{\exp(tX_{k})}\otimes 1_{\mathbb{C}\ell(G/H)}\otimes F_{X_{k}})\varphi(g),\qquad g\in G,

for an arbitrarily chosen orthonormal basis

of

on

\hat{D}:=\hat{D}_{h}+\hat{D}_{v}

8.2.4
Comparing the Dirac operators on

We are finally in a position to compare the lifted Dirac operator

with the canonical Dirac operator

To this end, we let

for all
X\in\mathbb{C}\ell(G/H)
and

We also write
\ev_{1}:L^{2}(H)\supseteq C^{\infty}(H)\to\mathbb{C}
for the evaluation at the unit element of

\varphi\mapsto\varphi(1)

Then the map
U:\mathfrak{S}^{\infty}\to L^{2}\bigl(G,\mathbb{C}\ell(G)\bigr)
given by

\displaystyle(U\varphi)(g)

\displaystyle:=U(g)\varphi(g)

with

\displaystyle U(g)

\displaystyle:=\ev_{1}s(g)^{*}\otimes\Ad_{g}W

(29)

for all
\varphi\in\mathfrak{S}^{\infty}
and

Lemma 8.1
.

The covariant representations
(\pi_{G},r_{h})
and
(\pi,\mu)
are unitarily equivalent.
More precisely, for all
f\in C(G)
and
h\in H
we have

\displaystyle U\pi(f)

\displaystyle=\pi_{G}(f)U

and

\displaystyle r_{h}U

\displaystyle=U\mu_{h}.

Proof.

Let us first fix
f\in C(G)
.
Then for each
g\in G
and
\varphi\in C^{\infty}(H)
we find

(\ev_{1}\circ\pi_{H})\bigl(jf(g,\;\cdot\;)\bigr)\varphi=f(g)\cdot\varphi(1)=f(g)\cdot\ev_{1}(\varphi).

From this, for each
\varphi\in\mathfrak{S}^{\infty}(G)
and
g\in G
we obtain that

\displaystyle\bigl(U\pi(f)\varphi\bigr)(g)

\displaystyle=\bigl(\ev_{1}s(g)^{*}\otimes\Ad_{g}W\bigr)\;

\displaystyle\qquad\Bigl(\bigl(s(g)\,\pi_{H}\bigl(jf(g,\;\cdot\;)\bigr)\,s(g)^{*}\bigr)\otimes 1_{\mathbb{C}\ell(G/H)}\otimes 1_{\mathbb{C}\ell(H)}\Bigr)\varphi(g)

\displaystyle=f(g)\cdot\bigl(\ev_{1}s(g)^{*}\otimes\Ad_{g}W\bigr)\varphi(g)=\bigl(\pi_{G}(f)U\varphi\bigr)(g).

In other words, we have
U\pi(f)=\pi_{G}(f)U
as claimed.
To deal with the second assertion, we fix
h\in H
and note that

\ev_{1}s(gh)^{*}\overset{\eqref{eq:SOPequivariance}}{=}\ev_{1}r_{h}^{*}s(g)^{*}\ev_{1}\lambda_{h}s(g)^{*}\overset{\eqref{eq:SOPcommuting}}{=}\ev_{1}s(g)^{*}\mu_{h}

(30)

for all
g\in G
. Hence for each
\varphi\in\mathfrak{S}^{\infty}
and
g\in G
we deduce that

\displaystyle(r_{h}U\varphi)(g)

\displaystyle\overset{\hphantom{\eqref{eq:nu_to_mu}}}{=}\bigl(\ev_{1}s(gh)^{*}\otimes\Ad_{gh}W\bigr)\varphi(gh)

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq:nu_to_mu}}{=}\bigl(\ev_{1}s(g)^{*}\otimes\Ad_{g}W\bigr)(\mu_{h}\otimes\Ad_{h}\otimes\Ad_{h})\varphi(gh)

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq:equivariant}}{=}\bigl(\ev_{1}s(g)^{*}\otimes\Ad_{g}W\bigr)(\mu_{h}\otimes 1_{\mathbb{C}\ell(G/H)\otimes\mathbb{C}\ell(H)})\varphi(g)=(U\mu_{h}\varphi)(g).

That is,
r_{h}U=U\mu_{h}
, and so the proof is complete.
∎

For the module frame in Equation (

Proof.

For the computation we fix an orthonormal basis of

Accordingly, we choose
(Y^{\prime}_{k})_{k}

Appendix A
Complementary results and proofs

In this appendix we provide complementary results and proofs for the sake of completeness.

Lemma A.1
.

Let
D
be an unbounded self-adjoint operator on a Hilbert space
\mathfrak{H}
with compact resolvent and let
x\in\mathcal{L}(\mathfrak{H})
. Then
D+x
has compact resolvent.

Proof.

Let
\lambda\in\imath\mathbb{R}
such that
\|x(\lambda-D)^{-1}\|<1
. Then the operator
\bigl(1-x(\lambda-D)^{-1}\bigr)
is invertible and thus the identity
\bigl(\lambda-(D+x)\bigr)=\bigl(1-x(\lambda-D)^{-1}\bigr)(\lambda-D)
implies that
\lambda
is also a regular value for
D+x
. In particular, we deduce that

\bigl(\lambda-(D+x)\bigr)^{-1}=(\lambda-D)^{-1}\bigl(1-x(\lambda-D)^{-1}\bigr)^{-1}.

Since
(\lambda-D)^{-1}
is compact, this operator is compact, too. Hence
D+x
has compact resolvent.
∎

Proof of Lemma
2.1
.

We first note that
D_{p}
has dense domain, because
p
is contractive. That is,
D_{p}
is indeed an unbounded operator on
p(\mathfrak{H})
.

1.

For all
\xi,\eta\in\dom(D_{p})
we have

\langle\xi,D_{p}\eta\rangle=\langle\xi,D\eta\rangle=\langle D\xi,\eta\rangle=\langle D_{p}\xi,\eta\rangle,

which entails that
D_{p}
is symmetric. Therefore, it suffices to prove that
\dom(D_{p}^{*})\subseteq\dom(D_{p})
. To do this, let us fix
\xi\in\dom(D_{p}^{*})
. Then for each
\eta\in\dom(D)
we find

\langle\xi,D\eta\rangle=\langle\xi,pD\eta\rangle=\langle\xi,Dp\eta\rangle-\langle\xi,[D,p]\eta\rangle=\langle\xi,D_{p}(p\eta)\rangle-\langle\xi,[D,p]\eta\rangle.

Since
[D,p]
is bounded, the right-hand side of the above equation is a continuous function of
\eta
. Consequently,
\xi\in\dom(D^{*})=\dom(D)
, and
\xi=p\xi
thus belongs to
p\,\dom(D)=\dom(D_{p})
.

2.

Suppose
D
has compact resolvent. Due to
p\,\dom(D)\subseteq\dom(D)
, we can assert that the linear operator
\tilde{D}_{p}:=pDp+(1-p)D(1-p)
on
\mathfrak{H}
is a well-defined unbounded operator with domain
\dom(\tilde{D}_{p}):=\dom(D)
. Moreover, rewriting
\tilde{D}_{p}
as

\tilde{D}_{p}=D-(1-p)Dp-pD(1-p)=D+\bigl[[D,p],(1-p)\bigr]

we see that
\tilde{D}_{p}
has compact resolvent by Lemma
A.1
. From this and the fact that
\tilde{D}_{p}
commutes with
p
it follows that
D_{p}=p\tilde{D}_{p}
has compact resolvent, too. ∎

1.
Introduction

Since the birth of theory of stability, which was first proposed by Ulam
[
16
]
in the form of a question and after the first answer due to Hyers
[
7
]
, many results have been obtained for many functional equations in different spaces. In this work we are interested in the case of reciprocal functional equations.

In 2010, Ravi and Senthil Kumar
[
9
]
studied the stability od the
reciprocal type functional equation

(1.1)

f(x+y)=\frac{f(x)f(y)}{f(x)+f(y)},

where
f:X\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is a mapping with
X
as the space of non-zero real numbers.

In 2014, Kim and Bodaghi
[
3
]
introduced and studied the stability of the quadratic reciprocal functional equation

(1.2)

f(2x+y)+f(2x-y)=\frac{2f(x)f(y)[4f(y)+f(x)]}{(4f(y)-f(x))^{2}}

In the same year, Ravi et al.
[
11
]
investigated the generalized Hyers-Ulam-Rassias stability of a reciprocal-quadratic functional equation of the form

(1.3)

r(x+2y)+r(2x+y)=\frac{r(x)r(y)[5r(x)+5r(y)+8\sqrt{r(x)r(y)}]}{[2r(x)+2r(y)+5\sqrt{r(x)r(y)}]^{2}}

in intuitionistic fuzzy normed spaces.

In 2017, Kim et al.
[
15
]
introduced and studied the stability of the reciprocal-cubic functional equation

(1.4)

c(2x+y)+c(x+2y)=\frac{9c(x)c(y)[c(x)+c(y)+2c(x)^{\frac{1}{3}}c(y)^{\frac{1}{3}}(c(x)^{\frac{1}{3}}+c(y)^{\frac{1}{3}})]}{[2c(x)^{\frac{2}{3}}+2c(y)^{\frac{2}{3}}+5c(x)^{\frac{1}{3}}c(y)^{\frac{1}{3}}]^{3}}

and the reciprocal-quartic functional equation

(1.5)

q(2x+y)+q(2x-y)=\frac{2q(x)q(y)[q(x)+16q(y)+24\sqrt{q(x)q(y)}]}{[4\sqrt{q(y)}-\sqrt{q(x)}]^{4}}

in non-Archimedean fields.

Very recently, in
[
2

, Kumar et al. focused on the stability of reciprocal-septic functional equation

(1.6)

S(2x+y)+S(2x-y)=\\
\frac{2S(x)S(y)[128S(x)+672S(x)^{\frac{2}{7}}S(y)^{\frac{5}{7}}+280S(x)^{\frac{4}{7}}S(y)^{\frac{3}{7}}+14S(x)^{\frac{6}{7}}S(y)^{\frac{1}{7}}]}{[4S(y)^{\frac{2}{7}}-S(x)^{\frac{2}{7}}]^{7}},

and the reciprocal-octic functional equation

(1.7)

\mathcal{O}(2x+y)+\mathcal{O}(2x-y)=\\
\frac{2\mathcal{O}(x)\mathcal{O}(y)[\mathcal{O}(x)+1792\mathcal{O}(x)^{\frac{1}{4}}\mathcal{O}(y)^{\frac{3}{4}}+1120\mathcal{O}(x)^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathcal{O}(y)^{\frac{1}{2}}+112\mathcal{O}(x)^{\frac{3}{4}}\mathcal{O}(y)^{\frac{1}{4}}+256\mathcal{O}(y)]}{[4\mathcal{O}(y)^{\frac{1}{4}}-\mathcal{O}(x)^{\frac{1}{4}}]^{8}}.

In this paper, we introduce the following new reciprocal functional equation

(1.8)

f(2x+y)+f(2x-y)=\frac{2f(x)f(y)\displaystyle{\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=0\\
\text{$k$ is even}\end{subarray}}^{l}2^{l-k}\binom{l}{k}f(x)^{\frac{k}{l}}f(y)^{\frac{l-k}{l}}}}{\left(4f(y)^{\frac{2}{l}}-f(x)^{\frac{2}{l}}\right)^{l}}

then, we investigate the general solution and its generalized Hyers-Ulam stability in the non-zero real numbers and in non-Archimedean fields.

2.
General solution of (
1.8
)

In this section, the solution of the functional equation (
1.8
) is given. Throughout this section, we assume that
\mathbb{R}^{*}
is the set of non-zero real numbers.

Theorem 1
.

Let
f:\mathbb{R}^{*}\to\mathbb{R}
be a continuous real-valued function of a two real variables satisfying the reciprocal type functional equation (
1.8
) and assume that
f(x)\neq 0
and
4f(y)^{\frac{2}{l}}-f(x)^{\frac{2}{l}}\neq 0
for all
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{*}
. Then
f
is of the form

f(x)=\frac{c}{x^{l}},

for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{*}
, where
c\neq 0
.

Proof.

Let
f:\mathbb{R}^{*}\to\mathbb{R}
satisfies the functional equation (
1.8
). Replacing
(x,y)
by
(x,x)
in (
1.8
), we obtain

f(3x)+f(x)=\frac{2f(x)\displaystyle{\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=0\\
\text{$k$ is even}\end{subarray}}^{l}2^{l-k}\binom{l}{k}}}{3^{l}}

By induction, it is straightforward to show that

(2.1)

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=0\\
\text{$k$ is even}\end{subarray}}^{l}2^{l-k}\binom{l}{k}=\frac{3^{l}+1}{2},l\geq 0.

Then,

f(3x)=\frac{1}{3^{l}}f(x)

for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{*}
.
Therefore
f
is a reciprocal function of the form
\frac{c}{x^{l}}
. This completes the proof.
∎

3.
Stability of (
1.8
) in non-zero Real Numbers

For future use and convenience, we introduce the following difference operator
\Lambda:\mathbb{R}^{*}\to\mathbb{R}
as

\Lambda(x,y)=f(2x+y)+f(2x-y)-\frac{2f(x)f(y)\displaystyle{\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=0\\
\text{$k$ is even}\end{subarray}}^{l}2^{l-k}\binom{l}{k}f(x)^{\frac{k}{l}}f(y)^{\frac{l-k}{l}}}}{\left(4f(y)^{\frac{2}{l}}-f(x)^{\frac{2}{l}}\right)^{l}},

with
l\geq 1
and
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{*}.

Theorem 2
.

Let
Q:\mathbb{R}^{*}\times\mathbb{R}^{*}\to\mathbb{R}
a function satisfying

(3.1)

\sum_{s=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{3^{ls}}Q\left(\frac{x}{3^{s+1}},\frac{y}{3^{s+1}}\right)<\infty

for all
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{*}
. If a function
f:\mathbb{R}^{*}\to\mathbb{R}
satisfies functional inequality

(3.2)

\left|\Lambda(x,y)\right|\leq Q(x,y)

for all
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{*}
, then there exists a unique reciprocal function
g:\mathbb{R}^{*}\to\mathbb{R}
which satisfies (
1.8
) and the inequality

(3.3)

|f(x)-g(x)|\leq\sum_{s=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{3^{ls}}Q\left(\frac{x}{3^{s+1}},\frac{x}{3^{s+1}}\right)

for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{*}
.

Proof.

We substitute
(x,y)
by
(x,x)
in (
3.2
) and using (
2.1
) we get

(3.4)

\left|f(3x)-\frac{f(x)}{3^{l}}\right|\leq Q(x,x)

for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{*}
. Substituting
x
by
\frac{x}{3}
in (
3.4
), we obtain

(3.5)

\left|f(x)-\frac{1}{3^{l}}f\left(\frac{x}{3}\right)\right|\leq Q\left(\frac{x}{3},\frac{x}{3}\right)

for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{*}
. Now, by replacing
x
into
\frac{x}{3}
in (
3.5
), dividing by
3^{l}
and then adding the ensuing inequality with (
3.5
), we get

(3.6)

\left|f(x)-\frac{1}{3^{2l}}f\left(\frac{x}{3^{2}}\right)\right|\leq Q\left(\frac{x}{3},\frac{x}{3}\right)+\frac{1}{3^{l}}Q\left(\frac{x}{3^{2}},\frac{x}{3^{2}}\right)

for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{*}
. In a similar way, proceeding further and applying induction procedure on a positive integer
m
, we get

(3.7)

\left|f(x)-\frac{1}{3^{ml}}f\left(\frac{x}{3^{m}}\right)\right|\leq\sum_{s=0}^{m-1}\frac{1}{3^{ls}}Q\left(\frac{x}{3^{s+1}},\frac{x}{3^{s+1}}\right)

for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{*}
.

Next, for any integers
m^{\prime},m
with
m^{\prime}>m>0
, we obtain

(3.8)

\left|\frac{1}{3^{lm^{\prime}}}f\left(\frac{x}{3^{m^{\prime}}}\right)-\frac{1}{3^{lm}}f\left(\frac{x}{3^{m}}\right)\right|=\left|\frac{1}{3^{lm^{\prime}}}f\left(\frac{x}{3^{m^{\prime}}}\right)-\frac{1}{3^{l(m^{\prime}-1)}}f\left(\frac{x}{3^{l(m^{\prime}-1)}}\right)+\cdots\right.\\
\quad{}\left.\left.+\frac{1}{3^{l(m^{\prime}-1)}}f\left(\frac{x}{3^{l(m^{\prime}-1)}}\right)-\cdots+\frac{1}{3^{l(m+1)}}f\left(\frac{x}{3^{m+1}}\right)-\frac{1}{3^{lm}}f\left(\frac{x}{3^{m}}\right)\right|\right.\\
\leq\frac{1}{3^{l(m^{\prime}-1)}}Q\left(\frac{x}{3^{m^{\prime}}},\frac{x}{3^{m^{\prime}}}\right)+\cdots+\frac{1}{3^{lm}}Q\left(\frac{x}{3^{m+1}},\frac{x}{3^{m+1}}\right)\\
\leq\sum_{j=m}^{m^{\prime}-1}\frac{1}{3^{lj}}Q\left(\frac{x}{3^{j+1}},\frac{x}{3^{j+1}}\right)

for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{*}
. Taking
m^{\prime}\rightarrow\infty
in (
3.8
) and applying (
3.1
), we observe that the sequence
\{\frac{1}{3^{lm}}f(\frac{x}{3^{m}})\}
is a Cauchy sequence for each
x\in\mathbb{R}^{*}
. Since
\mathbb{R}
is complete, we can define
g:\mathbb{R}^{*}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
by
g(x)=\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{3^{lm}}f\left(\frac{x}{3^{m}}\right)
. To prove that
g
satisfies (
1.8
), substuting
(x,y)
by
(3^{-m}x,3^{-m}y)
in (
3.2
) and dividing by
3^{lm}
, we arrive

(3.9)

|3^{-lm}\Lambda(3^{-m}x,\ 3^{-m}y)|\leq 3^{-lm}Q(3^{-m}x,\ 3^{-m}y)

for all
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{*}
and for all positive integer
m
. Letting
m\rightarrow\infty
in (
3.9
) and using (
3.1
), we see that
g
satisfies (
1.8
) for all
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{*}
. Again, by taking
m\rightarrow\infty
in (
3.7
), we achieve (
3.3
). Now, it remains to show that
g
is unique. Let
g^{\prime}:\mathbb{R}^{*}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be another reciprocal mapping which satisfies (
1.8
) and the inequality (
3.3
).
Obviously, we have
g^{\prime}(3^{-m}x)=3^{lm}g^{\prime}(x),g(3^{-m}x)=3^{lm}g(x)
and using (
3.3
), we get

(3.10)

\displaystyle|g^{\prime}(x)-g(x)|

\displaystyle=3^{-lm}\left|g^{\prime}(3^{-m}x)-g(3^{-m}x)\right|

\displaystyle\leq 3^{-lm}\left(\left|g^{\prime}(3^{-m}x)-f(3^{-m}x)\right|+\left|f(3^{-m}x)-g(3^{-m}x)\right|\right)

\displaystyle\leq 2\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{3^{l(m+j)}}Q\left(\frac{x}{3^{m+j+1}},\ \frac{x}{3^{m+j+1}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq 2\sum_{j=m}^{\infty}\frac{1}{3^{lj}}Q\left(\frac{x}{3^{j+1}},\frac{x}{3^{j+1}}\right)

for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{*}
. Letting
m\rightarrow\infty
in (
3.10
), we find that
g
is unique. The proof of Theorem
2
is complete.
∎

4.
Stability of (
1.8
) in non-Archimedean field

Throughout this section,
\mathbb{A}
and
\mathbb{B}
will denote a non-Archimedean
field and a complete non-Archimedean field, respectively. Moreover, for a non-Archimdean field
\mathbb{A}
, we put
\mathbb{A}^{*}=\mathbb{A}-\left\{0\right\}
. We also assume that the reader is familiar with the basic properties of non-Archimedean fields.

Theorem 3
.

Let
G:\mathbb{A}^{*}\times\mathbb{A}^{*}\rightarrow[0,\ \infty[
be a mapping such that

(4.1)

\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}\left|\frac{1}{3^{l}}\right|^{m}G\left(\frac{x}{3^{m+1}},\ \frac{y}{3^{m+1}}\right)=0

for all
x,y\in\mathbb{A}^{*}
. Suppose that
g:\mathbb{A}^{*}\rightarrow\mathbb{B}
is a mapping satisfying the inequality

(4.2)

|\Lambda(x,y)|\leq G(x,y)

for all
x,y\in\mathbb{A}^{*}
. Then, there exists a unique reciprocal mapping
g:\mathbb{A}^{*}\rightarrow\mathbb{B}
such that such that

(4.3)

|f(x)-g(x)|\leq\max\left\{\left|\frac{1}{3^{l}}\right|^{k+1}G\left(\frac{x}{3^{k+1}},\frac{x}{3^{k+1}}\right):k\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}\right\}

for all
x\in\mathbb{A}^{*}
.

Proof.

Replacing
(x,y)
by
(x,\ x)
in (
4.2
), one finds

(4.4)

\left|f(x)-\frac{1}{3^{l}}f\left(\frac{x}{3}\right)\right|\leq|3^{l}|G\left(\frac{x}{3},\frac{x}{3}\right)

for all
x\in\mathbb{A}^{*}
. Now, considering
x
as
\frac{x}{3^{m}}
in (
4.4
) and multiplying by
\left|\frac{1}{3^{l}}\right|^{m}
, we get

(4.5)

\left|\frac{1}{3^{lm}}f\left(\frac{x}{3^{m}}\right)-\frac{1}{3^{l(m+1)}}f\left(\frac{x}{3^{(m+1)}}\right)\right|\leq\left|\frac{1}{3^{l}}\right|^{m}G\left(\frac{x}{3^{m+1}},\frac{x}{3^{m+1}}\right)

for all
x\in\mathbb{A}^{*}
. It is easy to obtain from the inequalities (
4.1
) and (
4.5
) that the sequence
\left\{\frac{1}{3^{lm}}f\left(\frac{x}{3^{lm}}\right)\right\}
is Cauchy. Since
\mathbb{B}
is complete, we can say that this sequence converges to a mapping
g:\mathbb{A}^{*}\rightarrow\mathbb{B}
defined by

(4.6)

g(x)=\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{3^{lm}}f\left(\frac{x}{3^{m}}\right)

Besides, for each
x\in\mathbb{A}^{*}
and nonnegative integers
m
, we obtain

\displaystyle\left|\frac{1}{3^{lm}}f\left(\frac{x}{3^{m}}\right)-g(x)\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\left[\frac{1}{3^{l(k+1)}}f\left(\frac{x}{3^{(k+1)}}\right)-\frac{1}{3^{lm}}f\left(\frac{x}{3^{m}}\right)\right]\right|

(4.7)

\displaystyle\leq\max\left\{\left|\frac{1}{3^{l(k+1)}}\right|f\left(\frac{x}{3^{(k+1)}}\right)-\frac{1}{3^{lm}}f\left(\frac{x}{3^{m}}\right)\right|:0\leq k<m\}

\displaystyle\leq\max\left\{\left|\frac{1}{3^{l}}\right|^{m}G\left(\frac{x}{3^{m+1}},\frac{x}{3^{m+1}}\right):0\leq k<m\right\}.

Taking
m\rightarrow\infty
in the inequality (
4
) and using (
4.6
), we observe that the inequality (
4.3
) is valid. By the application of inequalities (
4.1
), (
4.2
) and (
4.6
), for all
x,y\in\mathbb{A}^{*},
we arrive at

\displaystyle|\Lambda(x,y)|

\displaystyle=\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}\left|\frac{1}{3^{l}}\right|^{m}\left|\Lambda\left(\frac{x}{3^{m}},\ \frac{y}{3^{m}}\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}\left|\frac{1}{3^{l}}\right|^{m}G\left(\frac{x}{3^{m}},\ \frac{y}{3^{m}}\right)

\displaystyle=0.

Hence, the mapping
g
satisfies (
4.1
) and so it is reciprocal mapping. In order to confirm the uniqueness of
g
, let us presume
g^{\prime}
:
\mathbb{A}^{*}\rightarrow\mathbb{B}
be another reciprocal mapping verifying (
4.3
). Then

|g(x)-g^{\prime}(x)|=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left|\frac{1}{3^{l}}\right|^{n}\left|g\left(\frac{x}{3^{n}}\right)-g^{\prime}\left(\frac{x}{3^{n}}\right)\right|\\
\leq\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left|\frac{1}{3^{l}}\right|^{n}\max\left\{\left|g\left(\frac{x}{3^{n}}\right)-f\left(\frac{x}{3^{n}}\right)\right|,\left|f\left(\frac{x}{3^{n}}\right)-g^{\prime}\left(\frac{x}{3^{n}}\right)\right|\right\}\\
\leq\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}\max\left\{\max\left\{\left|\frac{1}{3^{l}}\right|^{k+n}G\left(\frac{x}{3^{k+n+1}},\frac{x}{3^{k+n+1}}\right)\right.\right.\\
\quad{}\left.\left.\!\!:n\leq k\leq m+n\right\}\right\}\\
=0\\

for all
x\in\mathbb{A}^{*}
. This implies that
g
is unique, which completes the proof.
∎

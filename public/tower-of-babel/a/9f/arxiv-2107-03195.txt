Formality of A ∞ A_{\infty} -Algebras
Carl Felix Waller

Abstract
This master’s thesis contains an introduction to A ∞ A_{\infty} -algebras and homological perturbation theory. We then discuss the formality of compact Kähler manifolds and present a direct proof of a homotopy transfer principle of A ∞ A_{\infty} -algebras, also known as Kadeishvili’s Theorem.

We begin with a short discussion of the history of
A_{\infty}
-algebras. The topological origin of
A_{\infty}
-algebras goes back to the PhD thesis of James Stasheff
[
Sta63
]
,
[
Sta63a
]
. In it, he uses
A_{\infty}
-spaces to characterize loop spaces. The story of recognizing loop spaces began with Milnor, who proved that many topological groups have the homotopy type of loop spaces
[
Mil56

H

[
DL59

[

]
,
[
Sug57

Stasheff attempted to further relax Sugawara’s assumption of the existence of a homotopy inverse and found that he had to introduce higher homotopies. He was able to improve upon Sugawara’s results and show that under mild assumptions a space
X
has the homotopy type of a loop space if and only if
X
is an
A_{\infty}
-space, i.e.
X
admits a unital multiplication
M_{2}
and higher homotopies

M_{n}:K_{n}\times X^{n}\rightarrow X,\quad\forall n\geq 3

which measure the failure of

to be associative. Here

are the Stasheff polytopes which are convex polytopes similar to simplices but with a boundary that is built inductively out of copies of
K_{1},...,K_{n-1}
. For more detail on Stasheff’s proof see
[
Kan88
, §6-2]
. A summary of research on
H
-spaces and
A_{\infty}
-spaces is given, for example, in the lecture notes
[
Sta06
]
.

A_{\infty}

[

2  Notation

Let
k

(C,\Delta,\epsilon)
a coalgebra over
k

iterated coproduct

\Delta_{1}:=\Delta

\Delta_{n+1}:=(\Delta\otimes\mathrm{id}^{\otimes n})\circ\Delta_{n}

\Delta_{n-1}(c)=\sum_{(c)}c_{(1)}\otimes...\otimes c_{(n)}.

We refer to Sweedler’s book
[
Swe69
]

\displaystyle\bar{\Delta}:

\displaystyle\bar{C}\rightarrow\bar{C}\otimes\bar{C}

\displaystyle c\mapsto\Delta(c)-c\otimes 1-1\otimes c.

(\bar{C},\bar{\Delta})
is a “coalgebra without a counit”, which we refer to as a

\bar{\Delta}^{1}:=\bar{\Delta}

\bar{\Delta}^{n+1}:=(\bar{\Delta}\otimes\mathrm{id}^{\otimes n})\circ\bar{\Delta}^{n}

\bar{\Delta}^{n}(c)=:\sum_{(c)}c^{(1)}\otimes...\otimes c^{(n)}.

We use superscripts to distinguish this from the other iterated product.

Definition 3.1
.

An
A_{\infty}
-algebra
is a graded vector space
A
together with a family of linear maps
m_{n}:A^{\otimes n}\rightarrow A
of degree
n-2
, such that for all
n\in\mathbb{N}

\sum_{s=1}^{n}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}r+t=n-s\\
r,t\geq 0\end{subarray}}(-1)^{rs+t}m_{r+t+1}(\mathrm{id}^{\otimes r}\otimes m_{s}\otimes\mathrm{id}^{\otimes t})=0.

(3)

These equations are called the
higher associativity conditions
.

Writing out the first three higher associativity conditions we get

\displaystyle n=1:\quad m_{1}m_{1}=0,

\displaystyle n=2:\quad m_{1}m_{2}-m_{2}(\mathrm{id}\otimes m_{1}+m_{1}\otimes\mathrm{id})=0,

\displaystyle\mathrm{and}\

\displaystyle n=3:\quad m_{2}(\mathrm{id}\otimes m_{2}-m_{2}\otimes\mathrm{id})+m_{1}m_{3}

\displaystyle\qquad+m_{3}(m_{1}\otimes\mathrm{id}\otimes\mathrm{id}+\mathrm{id}\otimes m_{1}\otimes\mathrm{id}+\mathrm{id}\otimes\mathrm{id}\otimes m_{1})=0.

The first equation implies that
m_{1}
is a differential. The second equation implies that
m_{1}
behaves similarly to a derivation with respect to the
m_{2}
map. The third equation implies that in general
m_{2}
is not associative. We say that
m_{2}
is associative up to the homotopy
m_{3}
.

Definition 3.2
.

Let
(A,m),(A^{\prime},m^{\prime})
be two
A_{\infty}
-algebras. A
morphism of

A_{\infty}
-algebras

f:(A,m)\rightarrow(A^{\prime},m^{\prime})
is a family of linear maps
f_{n}:A^{\otimes n}\rightarrow A^{\prime}
of degree

\sum_{r+s+t=n}(-1)^{rs+t}f_{r+t+1}(\mathrm{id}^{\otimes r}\otimes m_{s}\otimes\mathrm{id}^{\otimes t})=\sum_{i_{1}+...+i_{r}=n}(-1)^{l(i_{1},...,i_{r})}m_{r}^{\prime}(f_{i_{1}}\otimes...\otimes f_{i_{r}})

(4)

where the sign on the right-hand side is given by

l(i_{1},...,i_{r}):=\sum_{1\leq j<k\leq n}(i_{k}-1)i_{j}.

(5)

If
f^{\prime}:(A^{\prime},m^{\prime})\rightarrow(A^{\prime\prime},m^{\prime\prime})
is another morphism we define the composition
f^{\prime}\circ f:(A,m)\rightarrow(A^{\prime\prime},m^{\prime\prime})
as

(f^{\prime}\circ f)_{n}:=\sum_{i_{1}+...+i_{r}=n}(-1)^{l(i_{1},...,i_{r})}f^{\prime}_{r}(f_{i_{1}}\otimes...\otimes f_{i_{r}}).

(6)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=r+s+t}(-1)^{rs+t}(\mathrm{id}_{A})_{r+t+1}(\mathrm{id}_{A}^{\otimes r}\otimes m_{s}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{A}^{\otimes t})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i_{1}+...+i_{r}=n}(-1)^{l(i_{1},...,i_{r})}m_{r}((\mathrm{id}_{A})_{i_{1}}\otimes...\otimes(\mathrm{id}_{A})_{i_{r}}).

A morphism of

r>1\end{subarray}}(-1)^{l}f_{r}(g_{i_{1}}\otimes...\otimes g_{i_{r}})\big).

(7)

5.1  Quasi-Cofree Coalgebras

Searching for heavy neutrino in terms of tau lepton at future hadron collider
Chengcheng Han, Tong Li, Chang-Yuan Yao

Abstract
The tau lepton plays important role in the correlation between the low-energy neutrino oscillation data and the lepton flavor structure in heavy neutrino decay. We investigate the lepton flavor signatures with tau lepton at hadron collider through lepton number violating (LNV) processes. In the Type I Seesaw with U ( 1 ) B − L (1)_{\rm B-L} extension, we study the pair production of heavy neutrinos via a Z ′ Z^{\prime} resonance. We present a detailed assessment of the search sensitivity to the channels with tau lepton in the subsequent decay of heavy neutrinos. For the benchmark model with Z ′ Z^{\prime} only coupled to the third generation fermions, we find that the future circular collider (FCC-hh) can discover the LNV signal with tau lepton for M Z ′ M_{Z^{\prime}} up to 2.2 (3) TeV with the gauge coupling g ′ = 0.6 g^{\prime}=0.6 and the integrated luminosity of 3 (30) ab -1 . The test on the flavor combinations of SM charged leptons would reveal the specific nature of different heavy neutrinos.

Introduction

It is well-known that, in the context of the Standard Model (SM), the non-zero but small Majorana neutrino masses can be realized at leading order through a dimension-five operator

\displaystyle{\kappa\over\Lambda}l_{L}l_{L}HH,

To receive the above “Weinberg operator”, the minimal extension of the SM content permits only the Type I, Type II or Type III Seesaw mechanism at tree level

On the other hand, as proved in Refs.

In other words, lepton number is nearly conserved in low-scale seesaw models with fermionic singlets in light of non-zero neutrino masses. Tiny neutrino mass is proportional to small lepton number violation (LNV) parameters. Thus, for low-scale Type I Seesaw, the observable lepton number violation is decoupled in high energy processes. Any observation of lepton number violation at colliders implies an extension of the canonical Type I Seesaw. Below we consider the introduction of a new U
(1)_{\rm B-L}
gauge symmetry in which the SM matter fields and heavy neutrinos are charged under the B-L (baryon minus lepton number) symmetry. However, the minimal B-L model is highly constrained by the di-lepton search at the LHC. The lower

;
Cox et al. 2018

In addition, leptogenesis prefers much heavy right-handed neutrinos and the usual B-L breaking scale should be high. However, it is sufficient to have two heavy right-handed neutrinos to explain the baryon asymmetry of the Universe, which leaves a possibility that the third right-handed neutrino could be light enough to be probed at future colliders. It is thus plausible to consider this non-universal U
(1)_{\rm(B-L)_{3}}

We study the pair production of heavy neutrino via gauge interaction in the U

II.1
U

To reveal the origins of the B-L accidental symmetry in the SM and Majorana neutrino masses, one considers the extension of the SM with U

In the well-studied U

In this model, right-handed neutrinos (RH) are introduced to cancel gauge anomalies and realize the Type I Seesaw mechanism. The relevant covariant derivatives can be generally written as

\displaystyle D_{\mu}\ni ig_{1}YB_{\mu}+i(g_{1}^{\prime}Y+g^{\prime}Y_{BL})B^{\prime}_{\mu}=ig_{1}YB_{\mu}+ig_{X}(x_{1}^{\prime}Y+x^{\prime}Y_{BL})B^{\prime}_{\mu},

, a new scalar field

is introduced to break the local U
(1)_{\rm B-L}
symmetry and generate the masses of right-handed neutrinos
N_{R}\sim(1,1,0,-x^{\prime})

(1)_{\rm Y}
and U

;

Aaboud et al. 2017

.

On the other hand, in this model, the anomaly-free U
(1)^{\prime}
extension allows non-universal U
(1)^{\prime}
charges for the SM fermions.
With the presence of three right-handed neutrinos, the most general requirement of the U
(1)^{\prime}
charges is

\displaystyle 3(Q^{\prime}_{1}+Q^{\prime}_{2}+Q^{\prime}_{3})+Q^{\prime}_{e}+Q^{\prime}_{\mu}+Q^{\prime}_{\tau}=0\;,

(4)

where
Q^{\prime}_{1,2,3}
are the quark charges and

To relax the constraint from dilepton channel, there proposed a flavored model under the U

Note that the constraint can also be relaxed by maximizing the ratio

II.2
Type I Seesaw and heavy neutrino decay

The neutrino Yukawa interactions are

\displaystyle-\mathcal{L}_{Y}^{I}=Y^{D}_{\nu}\bar{l}_{L}\tilde{H}N_{R}+{1\over 2}Y^{M}_{\nu}\overline{(N^{c})_{L}}N_{R}S+h.c.,

(5)

where

With the right-handed neutrino mass matrix

in this model follow the same as those in the canonical Type I Seesaw. After

\langle H\rangle=v/\sqrt{2}

and the Dirac masses

\displaystyle\mathbb{N}^{\dagger}\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&m_{D}\\
m_{D}^{T}&M_{R}\\
\end{array}\right)\mathbb{N}^{\ast}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}m_{\nu}&0\\
0&M_{N}\\
\end{array}\right),

and the eigenstate transformation

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\nu_{L}\\
(N^{c})_{L}\\
\end{array}\right)=\mathbb{N}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\nu_{L}\\
(N^{c})_{L}\\
\end{array}\right)_{mass},\ \ \ \mathbb{N}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}U&V\\
X&Y\\
\end{array}\right)\;.

With a matrix
E

\displaystyle E^{\dagger}U\equiv U_{PMNS}\;,~~~E^{\dagger}V\equiv V_{\ell N}\;,

(18)

One can derive a relation between diagonalized neutrino mass matrices and mixing matrices

\displaystyle U^{\ast}_{PMNS}m_{\nu}^{diag}U^{\dagger}_{PMNS}+V^{\ast}_{\ell N}M_{N}^{diag}V^{\dagger}_{\ell N}=0\ ,

\displaystyle V_{\ell N}=U_{PMNS}(m_{\nu}^{diag})^{1/2}\Omega(M_{N}^{diag})^{-1/2},

\displaystyle\Gamma(N_{i}\rightarrow\ell^{\pm}W^{\mp})={G_{F}\over 8\sqrt{2}\pi}|V_{\ell N_{i}}|^{2}M_{N_{i}}(M_{N_{i}}^{2}+2M_{W}^{2})\left(1-{M_{W}^{2}\over M_{N_{i}}^{2}}\right)^{2},

\displaystyle\Gamma(N_{i}\rightarrow\sum_{\ell}\ell^{\pm}W^{\mp})\approx\Gamma(N_{i}\rightarrow\sum_{\nu}\nu Z+\bar{\nu}Z)\approx\Gamma(N_{i}\rightarrow\sum_{\nu}\nu h+\bar{\nu}h)\;,

(22)

where
\ell=e,\mu,\tau

Note that, more generally, the Dirac mass matrix can be quite arbitrary with three complex angles parameterizing the orthogonal matrix
\Omega

;
Fileviez Pérez and Plascencia 2020

25\%

for any possible

IV
Conclusions

Low energy neutrino oscillation data lead to correlation with the flavor structure of the heavy neutrino decays and tau lepton plays manifest role in such correlation. Due to the low tau identification efficiencies, future colliders with high energy and luminosity enables us to investigate and search for heavy neutrino decaying to tau lepton. Moreover, the decoupled lepton number violation in high energy process for low-scale Type I Seesaw encourages us to consider the extension of the canonical Type I Seesaw. We thus examine the lepton flavor signatures with tau lepton at FCC-hh, through lepton number violating processes in the U
(1)_{\rm B-L}
extended Type I Seesaw. In the U
(1)_{\rm B-L}
extended Type I Seesaw, we consider a flavored U
(1)_{\rm(B-L)_{3}}
model where only the third generation fermions couple to the new gauge boson
Z^{\prime}
and thus the constraint on the new gauge interaction is much weak.
We investigate the impact of up-to-date neutrino oscillation results on neutrino mass models and consequently examine the lepton flavor signatures of heavy neutrinos in the Type I Seesaw.

We study the pair production of heavy neutrinos via
Z^{\prime}
mediation in the U
(1)_{\rm(B-L)_{3}}
model, with one tau lepton in the subsequent decays:
pp\rightarrow Z^{\prime}\rightarrow NN\rightarrow\ell^{\pm}\tau^{\pm}W^{\mp}W^{\mp}\rightarrow\ell^{\pm}\tau^{\pm}+{\rm jets}
.
The
\tau
lepton’s hadronic decay is assumed in the above channels. We take into account the realistic decay branching ratios of heavy neutrinos determined by neutrino oscillation measurements. The general sensitivities of LNV production cross sections are shown for 5
\sigma
discovery and 95% C.L. exclusion.
We find that HL-LHC cannot reach 5
\sigma
discovery for heavy neutrinos in the U
(1)_{\rm(B-L)_{3}}
model.
The FCC-hh can discover the LNV signal with tau lepton for
M_{Z^{\prime}}
up to 2.2 (3) TeV with
g^{\prime}=0.6
and the integrated luminosity of 3 (30) ab
-1
.
The gauge coupling
g^{\prime}
down to 0.12 (0.07) can be probed through the LNV signature with tau lepton by the FCC-hh with the integrated luminosity of 3 (30) ab
-1
and
M_{Z^{\prime}}=1
TeV.
The test on the flavor combinations of SM charged leptons would reveal the specific nature of different heavy neutrinos and the three heavy neutrinos in the Casas-Ibarra parametrization with diagonal unity matrix can be distinguished.

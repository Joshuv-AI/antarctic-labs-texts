GALERKIN FINITE ELEMENT METHOD FOR GENERALIZED FORCHHEIMER EQUATION OF SLIGHTLY COMPRESSIBLE FLUIDS IN POROUS MEDIA
Thinh Kieu

Abstract
We consider the generalized Forchheimer flows for slightly compressible fluids. Using Muskat’s and Ward’s general form of Forchheimer equations, we describe the fluid dynamics by a nonlinear degenerate parabolic equation for the density. We study Galerkin finite elements method for the initial boundary value problem. The existence and uniqueness of the approximation are proved. The prior estimates for the solutions in L ∞ ​ ( 0 , T , L q ​ ( Ω ) ) , q ≥ 2 L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{q}(\Omega)),q\geq 2 , time derivative in L ∞ ​ ( 0 , T , L 2 ​ ( Ω ) ) L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega)) and gradient in L ∞ ​ ( 0 , T , W 1 , 2 − a ​ ( Ω ) ) , L^{\infty}(0,T;W^{1,2-a}(\Omega)), with a ∈ ( 0 , 1 ) a\in(0,1) are established. Error estimates for the density variable are derived in several norms for both continuous and discrete time procedures. Numerical experiments using backward Euler scheme confirm the theoretical analysis regarding convergence rates.



keywords

Introduction

Fluid flow through porous media has been studied in many fields such as chemistry, physics, engineering, and geology. The most common equation to describe fluid flows in porous media is the Darcy law

-\nabla p=\frac{\mu}{\kappa}v,

(1)

where
p
,
v
,
\mu
,
\kappa
are, respectively (resp.), the pressure, velocity, absolute viscosity and permeability.

For the Reynolds number large, for instance the high-velocity flow
[
30
,
3
]
, Darcy’s law is not valid any more. A nonlinear relationship between velocity and gradient of pressure is introduced, as suggested by Forchheimer in
[
15
,
16
]
, by adding the higher order term of velocity to the Darcy law. It is known as generalized Forchheimer laws, see
[
30
,
36
,
3
,
31
,
34
]
and references therein. Forchheimer established the following three nonlinear empirical models:

-\nabla p=av+b|v|v,\quad-\nabla p=av+b|v|v+c|v|^{2}v,\quad-\nabla p=av+d|v|^{m-1}v,m\in(1,2).

(2)

Above, the positive constants
a,b,c,d
are obtained from experiments.

The generalized Forchheimer equation of (
1
) and (
2
) were proposed in
[
2
,
17
,
18
]
of the form

-\nabla p=\sum_{i=0}^{N}a_{i}|v|^{\alpha_{i}}v.

(3)

These equations are analyzed numerically in
[
8
,
32
,
26
]
,
theoretically in
[
2
,
18
,
19
,
24
,
21
,
20
]
for single phase flows, and also in
[
22
,
23
]
for two phase flows.

In order to take into account the the dependence on density in generalized Forchheimer equation, we modify (
3
) using dimension analysis by Muskat
[
30
]
and Ward
[
36
]
. They proposed the following equation for both laminar and turbulent flows in porous media:

-\nabla p=F(v^{\alpha}\kappa^{\frac{\alpha-3}{2}}\rho^{\alpha-1}\mu^{2-\alpha}),\text{ where $F$ is a function of one variable.}

(4)

In particular, when
\alpha=1,2
, Ward
[
36
]
established from experimental data that

-\nabla p=\frac{\mu}{\kappa}v+c_{F}\frac{\rho}{\sqrt{\kappa}}|v|v,\quad\text{where }c_{F}>0.

(5)

Combining (
3
) with the suggestive form (
4
) for the dependence on
\rho
and
v
, we propose the following equation

-\nabla p=\sum_{i=0}^{N}a_{i}\rho^{\alpha_{i}}|v|^{\alpha_{i}}v,

(6)

where
N\geq 1
,
\alpha_{0}=0<\alpha_{1}<\ldots<\alpha_{N}
are real numbers, the coefficients
a_{0},\ldots,a_{N}
are positive.
Here, the viscosity and permeability are considered constant and we do not specify the dependence of
a_{i}
’s on them.

Multiplying both sides of previous equation to
\rho
, we obtain

g(|\rho v|)\rho v=-\rho\nabla p,

(7)

where the function
g
is a generalized polynomial with non-negative coefficients. More precisely, the function
g:\mathbb{R}^{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{+}
is of the form

g(s)=a_{0}s^{\alpha_{0}}+a_{1}s^{\alpha_{1}}+\cdots+a_{N}s^{\alpha_{N}},\quad s\geq 0,

(8)

where
N\geq 1,\alpha_{0}=0<\alpha_{1}<\ldots<\alpha_{N}
are fixed real numbers, the coefficients
a_{0},\ldots,a_{N}
are non-negative numbers with
a_{0}>0
and
a_{N}>0
.

The equation of state which, for slightly compressible fluids, is

\frac{1}{\rho}\frac{d\rho}{dp}=\frac{1}{\kappa}=const.>0.

Hence

\nabla\rho=\frac{1}{\kappa}\rho\nabla p,\quad\text{ or }\quad\rho\nabla p=\kappa\nabla\rho.

(9)

Combining (
8
) and (
9
) implies that

g(|\rho v|)\rho v=-\kappa\nabla\rho.

(10)

By rescaling coefficients of
g
we have

g(|\rho v|)\rho v=-\nabla\rho.

Hence

\rho v=-K(|\nabla\rho|)\nabla\rho,

(11)

where the function
K:\mathbb{R}^{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{+}
is defined for
\xi\geq 0
by

K(\xi)=\frac{1}{g(s(\xi))},\text{ with }s=s(\xi)\text{ being the unique non-negative solution of }sg(s)=\xi.

(12)

The continuity equation is

\phi\rho_{t}+{\rm div}(\rho v)=f.

(13)

where constant
\phi\in(0,1)
is the porosity,
f
is external mass flow rate .

By combining (
11
) and (
13
) we obtain

\phi\rho_{t}-\nabla\cdot(K(|\nabla\rho|)\nabla\rho)=f.

(14)

Then by scaling the time variable in (
14
) implies

\rho_{t}-\nabla\cdot(K(|\nabla\rho|)\nabla\rho)=f.

(15)

The system of equations describing the fluid motion reduces to a scalar equation of
density function. This is a nonlinear parabolic equation which degenerates as the gradient of density approach to infinity.

For the existence and regularity theory of degenerate parabolic equations, see e.g.
[
7
,
27
,
19
]
. The popular numerical method for modeling flow in porous media are mixed finite element method. Arbogast, Wheeler and Zhang
[
1
]
first analyzed mixed finite element approximations of degenerate parabolic equation arising in flow in porous media. Not so long later, Woodward and Dawson in
[
37
]
study of expanded mixed finite element methods for a nonlinear parabolic equation modeling flow into variably saturated porous media. In recently years, the Galerkin finite element method for a coupled nonlinear degenerate system of advection-diffusion equations has studied in
[
12
,
11
,
9
,
10
,
14
,
13
]
. In their analysis, the Kirchhoff transformation is used to move the nonlinearity from coefficient
K
to the gradient and thus simplifies analysis of the equations. This transformation does not applicable for the equation (
15
).

In this paper, we combine techniques in
[
17
,
18
,
19
,
21
,
20
,
24
]
utilizing the special structures of equation to obtain the error estimates for the solutions in several norms of interest. We obtain these results without any extra regularity assumption, though the order of error estimates are far from optimal order.

The paper is organized as follows:
In §
2
we introduce notations, relevant results in
[
2
,
17
,
26
]
and suitable trace estimates in Lemma
4
for the nature of our equation.
In §
3
we consider the semidiscrete finite element Galerkin approximation and an implicit backward difference time discretization to solve problem (
33
).
In §

we establish many bounds for solutions, its time derivative and gradient to problem (
34
) and (
36
) in Lebesgue norms.
In §

we analyze two version of a Galerkin finite element approximations, the continuous Galerkin method and the discrete Galerkin method. Using the monotonicity properties of Forchheimer equation and the boundedness of solutions, the
priori
error estimates are derived for solution in
L^{q}
-norm with
2\leq q\leq\infty
and for gradient of solution in
L^{2-a}
-norm.
Finally, in §

The Galerkin finite element method

Our aim is to study equation (
15
) for density of slightly compressible fluids in bounded

We consider the initial boundary value problem associated with (
15

\begin{cases}\rho_{t}-\nabla\cdot(K(|\nabla\rho|)\nabla\rho)=f&\text{in }\Omega\times I,\\

Find

]

(i)

such that if the
\Delta t
is sufficiently small then

\max_{1\leq n\leq N}\left\|{\rho^{n}-\rho_{h}^{n}}\right\|\leq C\left(h^{\frac{r-1}{2}}+\sqrt{\Delta t}\right).

(104)

Proof.

Subtracting (
103
) from (
37
) gives the error equation

(\partial\theta_{h}^{n},w_{h})+\left(K(|\nabla\rho_{h}^{n}|)\nabla\rho_{h}^{n}-K(|\nabla\rho^{n}|)\nabla\rho^{n},\nabla w_{h}\right)=(\pi\rho_{t}^{n}-\partial\pi\rho^{n},w_{h}).

(105)

Selecting
w_{h}=\theta_{h}^{n},
we rewrite above equation as form

\begin{split}&(\partial\theta_{h}^{n},\theta_{h}^{n})+\left(K(|\nabla\rho^{n}|)\nabla\rho^{n}-K(|\nabla\rho_{h}^{n}|)\nabla\rho_{h}^{n},\nabla\rho^{n}-\nabla\rho_{h}^{n}\right)\\
&\hskip 56.9055pt=\left(K(|\nabla\rho^{n}|)\nabla\rho^{n}-K(|\nabla\rho_{h}^{n}|)\nabla\rho_{h}^{n},\nabla\vartheta^{n}\right)+(\pi\rho_{t}^{n}-\partial\pi\rho^{n},\theta_{h}^{n}).\end{split}

(106)

For the first term, we have the identity

\begin{split}(\partial\theta_{h}^{n},\theta_{h}^{n})&=\left(\partial\theta_{h}^{n},\frac{\theta_{h}^{n}+\theta_{h}^{n-1}}{2}+\frac{\Delta t}{2}\partial\theta_{h}^{n}\right)\\
&=\frac{1}{2\Delta t}\left(\left\|{\theta_{h}^{n}}\right\|^{2}-\left\|{\theta_{h}^{n-1}}\right\|^{2}\right)+\frac{\Delta t}{2}\left\|{\partial\theta_{h}^{n}}\right\|^{2}.\end{split}

(107)

For the second term, the monotonicity of
K(\cdot)
in (
24
) yields

(K(|\nabla\rho^{n}|)\nabla\rho^{n}-K(|\nabla\rho_{h}^{n}|)\nabla\rho_{h}^{n},\nabla\rho^{n}-\nabla\rho_{h}^{n})\geq C_{0}\omega^{n}\left\|{\nabla\chi^{n}}\right\|_{L^{\beta}}^{2}

(108)

with

\omega^{n}=\omega(t_{n})=\left(1+\max\left\{\left\|{\nabla\rho_{h}^{n}}\right\|_{L^{\beta}(\Omega)},\left\|{\nabla\rho^{n}}\right\|_{L^{\beta}(\Omega)}\right\}\right)^{-a}.

For third term, using (
19
), Holder’s inequality, there is a positive constant
C
independence of
h,\Delta t,n
such that

\begin{split}\left(K(|\nabla\rho^{n}|)\nabla\rho^{n}-K(|\nabla\rho_{h}^{n}|)\nabla\rho_{h}^{n},\nabla\vartheta^{n}\right)\leq C\mathcal{M}^{\frac{1}{\beta\lambda}}\left\|{\nabla\vartheta^{n}}\right\|_{L^{\beta}}.\end{split}

(109)

For the last term, it follows from using
L^{2}
-projection and Taylor expand that

\begin{split}(\pi\rho_{t}^{n}-\partial\pi\rho^{n},\theta_{h}^{n})&=(\rho_{t}^{n}-\partial\rho^{n},\theta_{h}^{n})=\left(\frac{1}{\Delta t}\int_{t_{n-1}}^{t_{n}}\rho_{tt}(\tau)(\tau-t_{n-1})d\tau,\theta_{h}^{n}\right).\end{split}

(110)

Differentiating equation (
15
) in time provides,

\rho_{tt}=\nabla\cdot(K(|\nabla\rho|)\nabla\rho)_{t}+f_{t}.

This and (
110
) imply

\begin{split}(\pi\rho_{t}^{n}-\partial\pi\rho^{n},\theta_{h}^{n})&=\left(\frac{1}{\Delta t}\int_{t_{n-1}}^{t_{n}}f_{t}(\tau)(\tau-t_{n-1})d\tau,\theta_{h}^{n}\right)\\
&-\left(\frac{1}{\Delta t}\int_{t_{n-1}}^{t_{n}}(K(|\nabla\rho|)\nabla\rho)_{t}(\tau-t_{n-1})d\tau,\nabla\theta_{h}^{n}\right)=I_{1}+I_{2}.\end{split}

(111)

For the first term of left hand side in (
111
),

\begin{split}I_{1}&\leq\frac{1}{\Delta t}\left\|{\int_{t_{n-1}}^{t_{n}}f_{t}(\tau)(\tau-t_{n-1})d\tau}\right\|\left\|{\theta_{h}^{n}}\right\|\\
&\leq\frac{1}{\Delta t}\left\|{f_{t}}\right\|_{L^{2}(I_{n},L^{2})}\left[\int_{t_{n-1}}^{t_{n}}(\tau-t_{n-1})^{2}d\tau\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}\left\|{\theta_{h}^{n}}\right\|\\
&\leq\frac{1}{6}\Delta t\left\|{f_{t}}\right\|_{L^{2}(I_{n},L^{2})}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\left\|{\theta_{h}^{n}}\right\|^{2},\end{split}

(112)

where
I_{n}=[t_{n-1},t_{n}].

For the second term of left hand side in (
111
), using integration by part,
triangle inequality, (
19
), and Hölder’s inequality we find that

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\Delta t}\left|\Big(\int_{t_{n-1}}^{t_{n}}K(|\nabla\rho|)\nabla\rho d\tau,\nabla\theta_{h}^{n}\Big)\right|+\left|\left(K(|\nabla\rho^{n}|)\nabla\rho^{n},\nabla\theta_{h}^{n}\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{\Delta t}\int_{t_{n-1}}^{t_{n}}\left(|\nabla\rho|^{\beta-1},|\nabla\theta_{h}^{n}|\right)d\tau+C(|\nabla\rho^{n}|^{\beta-1},|\nabla\theta_{h}^{n}|)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{\Delta t}\left[\int_{t_{n-1}}^{t_{n}}\left\|{\nabla\rho}\right\|_{L^{\beta}}^{\beta-1}d\tau\right]\left\|{\nabla\theta_{h}^{n}}\right\|_{L^{\beta}}+C\left\|{\nabla\rho^{n}}\right\|_{L^{\beta}}^{\beta-1}\left\|{\nabla\theta_{h}^{n}}\right\|_{L^{\beta}}.

According to (
75
),

\begin{split}I_{2}&\leq C\mathcal{M}^{\frac{\beta-1}{\beta}}\left\|{\nabla\theta_{h}^{n}}\right\|_{L^{\beta}}\\
&\leq C\mathcal{M}^{\frac{\beta-1}{\beta}}\left\|{\nabla\chi^{n}}\right\|_{L^{\beta}}+C\mathcal{M}^{\frac{\beta-1}{\beta}}\left\|{\nabla\vartheta^{n}}\right\|_{L^{\beta}}\\
&\leq\varepsilon\left\|{\nabla\chi^{n}}\right\|_{L^{\beta}}^{2}+C\varepsilon^{-1}\mathcal{M}^{2\frac{\beta-1}{\beta}}+C\mathcal{M}^{\frac{\beta-1}{\beta}}\left\|{\nabla\vartheta^{n}}\right\|_{L^{\beta}}.\end{split}

(113)

Combining (
111
), (
112
) and (
113
) and using the fact that
\frac{1}{\lambda}=\frac{\beta-1}{\beta}
,

\begin{split}(\pi\rho_{t}^{n}-\partial\pi\rho^{n},\theta_{h}^{n})&\leq\frac{1}{6}\Delta t\left\|{f_{t}}\right\|_{L^{2}(I_{n},L^{2})}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\left\|{\theta_{h}^{n}}\right\|^{2}\\
&\quad+\varepsilon\left\|{\nabla\chi^{n}}\right\|_{L^{\beta}}^{2}+C\varepsilon^{-1}\mathcal{M}^{\frac{2}{\lambda}}+C\mathcal{M}^{\frac{1}{\lambda}}\left\|{\nabla\vartheta^{n}}\right\|_{L^{\beta}}.\end{split}

(114)

It follows from (
107
), (
108
), (
109
) and (
114
) that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\Delta t}\left(\left\|{\theta_{h}^{n}}\right\|^{2}-\left\|{\theta_{h}^{n-1}}\right\|^{2}\right)+C_{0}\omega^{n}\left\|{\nabla\chi^{n}}\right\|_{L^{\beta}}^{2}\leq\varepsilon\left\|{\nabla\chi^{n}}\right\|_{L^{\beta}}^{2}+C(\mathcal{M}^{\frac{1}{\beta\lambda}}+\mathcal{M}^{\frac{1}{\lambda}})\left\|{\nabla\vartheta^{n}}\right\|_{L^{\beta}}

(115)

\displaystyle+C\Delta t\left\|{f_{t}}\right\|_{L^{2}(I_{n},L^{2})}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\left\|{\theta_{h}^{n}}\right\|^{2}+C\varepsilon^{-1}\mathcal{M}^{\frac{2}{\lambda}}.

Selecting
\varepsilon=\frac{C_{0}\omega^{n}}{2}
in (
115
), we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\Delta t}\left(\left\|{\theta_{h}^{n}}\right\|^{2}-\left\|{\theta_{h}^{n-1}}\right\|^{2}\right)+C_{0}\omega^{n}\left\|{\nabla\chi^{n}}\right\|_{L^{\beta}}^{2}\leq C(\mathcal{M}^{\frac{1}{\beta\lambda}}+\mathcal{M}^{\frac{1}{\lambda}})\left\|{\nabla\vartheta^{n}}\right\|_{L^{\beta}}

(116)

\displaystyle+C\Delta t\left\|{f_{t}}\right\|_{L^{2}(I_{n},L^{2})}^{2}+\left\|{\theta_{h}^{n}}\right\|^{2}+C(\omega^{n})^{-1}\mathcal{M}^{\frac{2}{\lambda}}+C\mathcal{M}^{\frac{1}{\lambda}}\left\|{\nabla\vartheta^{n}}\right\|_{L^{\beta}}.

Dropping the second term in (
116
) and using (
81
) and the fact
\frac{2}{\lambda}+\gamma=1
, we get

\begin{split}\frac{1}{\Delta t}\left(\left\|{\theta_{h}^{n}}\right\|^{2}-\left\|{\theta_{h}^{n-1}}\right\|^{2}\right)&\leq\left\|{\theta_{h}^{n}}\right\|^{2}+C\left\{(\mathcal{M}^{\frac{1}{\beta\lambda}}+\mathcal{M}^{\frac{1}{\lambda}})\left\|{\nabla\vartheta^{n}}\right\|_{L^{\beta}}+\mathcal{M}^{\frac{2}{\lambda}+\gamma}\right\}+C\Delta t\left\|{f_{t}}\right\|_{L^{2}(I_{n},L^{2})}^{2}.\end{split}

Summing from
n=0
to
n=m-1
with
0<m\leq N
and using the fact that
\theta_{h}(0)=0
, we obtain

\displaystyle\left\|{\theta_{h}^{m}}\right\|^{2}\leq\Delta t\sum_{n=0}^{m-1}\left\|{\theta_{h}^{n}}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle+C\Big\{(\mathcal{M}^{\frac{1}{\beta\lambda}}+M^{\frac{1}{\lambda}})\sum_{n=0}^{m-1}\Delta t\left\|{\nabla\vartheta^{n}}\right\|_{L^{\beta}}

(117)

\displaystyle+(\Delta t)^{2}\sum_{n=0}^{m-1}\left\|{f_{t}}\right\|_{L^{2}(I_{n},L^{2})}^{2}+(m-1)\mathcal{M}\Delta t\Big\}.

An application of the discrete Gronwall’s inequality shows that for
\Delta t
sufficiently small,

\displaystyle\max_{0\leq n\leq N}\left\|{\theta_{h}^{n}}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\Big\{(\mathcal{M}^{\frac{1}{\beta\lambda}}+\mathcal{M}^{\frac{1}{\lambda}})\sum_{n=0}^{m-1}\Delta t\left\|{\nabla\vartheta^{n}}\right\|_{L^{\beta}}+\mathcal{M}\Delta t\Big\}+C(\Delta t)^{2}\left\|{f_{t}}\right\|_{L^{2}(I,L^{2})}^{2},

(118)

which implies

\displaystyle\max_{0\leq n\leq N}\left\|{\theta_{h}^{n}}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq C\Big\{(\mathcal{M}^{\frac{1}{2\beta\lambda}}+\mathcal{M}^{\frac{1}{2\lambda}})h^{\frac{r-1}{2}}\Big(\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\Delta t\left\|{\rho^{n}}\right\|_{W^{r,\beta}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}+\mathcal{M}^{\frac{1}{2}}\sqrt{\Delta t}\Big\}

(119)

\displaystyle+C\Delta t\left\|{f_{t}}\right\|_{L^{2}(I,L^{2})}.

Finally, the result follows from (
119
) and

\left\|{\rho^{n}-\rho_{h}^{n}}\right\|\leq\left\|{\theta_{h}^{n}}\right\|+\left\|{\vartheta^{n}}\right\|.

∎

Now we give the error estimate for gradient in fully discrete version.

Theorem 15
.

Let
\rho
solve problem (
34
) and
\rho_{h}^{n}
solve the fully discrete Galerkin finite element approximation (
37
) for each time step
n
,
n=1,\ldots,N
. Then there exists a positive constant
C
independent of
h
and
\Delta t
satisfying

\left\|{\nabla\rho^{n}_{h}-\nabla\rho^{n}}\right\|_{L^{\beta}}\leq C(h^{\frac{r-1}{4}}+\sqrt[4]{\Delta t}).

(120)

Proof.

Selecting
w_{h}=\theta_{h}^{n},
in (
105
) and rewriting the resulting equation as form

\left((K(|\nabla\rho^{n}|)\nabla\rho^{n}-K(|\nabla\rho_{h}^{n}|)\nabla\rho_{h}^{n},\nabla\rho^{n}-\nabla\rho_{h}^{n}\right)\\
=\left(K(|\nabla\rho^{n}|)\nabla\rho^{n}-K(|\nabla\rho_{h}^{n}|)\nabla\rho_{h}^{n},\nabla\vartheta^{n}\right)+(\rho_{t}^{n}-\partial\rho_{h}^{n},\theta_{h}^{n}).

Due to (
25
), Cauchy-Schwartz and triangle inequality, one has

\omega^{n}\left\|{\nabla\rho^{n}_{h}-\nabla\rho^{n}}\right\|_{L^{\beta}}^{2}\leq C\left(\left\|{\partial\rho_{h}^{n}}\right\|+\left\|{\rho_{t}^{n}}\right\|\right)\left\|{\theta_{h}^{n}}\right\|+C(|\nabla\rho^{n}-\nabla\rho_{h}^{n}|,|\nabla\vartheta^{n}|).

Since

\displaystyle\left\|{\partial\rho_{h}^{n}}\right\|=(\Delta t)^{-1}\left\|{\int_{t_{n-1}}^{t_{n}}p_{h,t}dt}\right\|\leq(\Delta t)^{-1}\int_{t_{n-1}}^{t_{n}}\left\|{p_{h,t}}\right\|dt\leq\max_{[T/N,T]}\left\|{p_{h,t}}\right\|\leq\mathcal{D}^{2},

\left\|{p_{t}^{n}}\right\|\leq\max_{[T/N,T]}\left\|{p_{t}}\right\|\leq\mathcal{D}^{2}
and (
109
), we obtain

\omega^{n}\left\|{\nabla\rho^{n}_{h}-\nabla\rho^{n}}\right\|_{L^{\beta}}^{2}\leq C\mathcal{D}^{2}\left\|{\theta_{h}^{n}}\right\|+C\mathcal{M}^{\frac{1}{\beta\lambda}}\left\|{\nabla\vartheta^{n}}\right\|_{L^{\beta}}.

Using estimate in (
119
) and (
81
) shows that

\begin{split}\left\|{\nabla\rho^{n}_{h}-\nabla\rho^{n}}\right\|_{L^{\beta}}^{2}&\leq C\mathcal{D}^{2}\mathcal{M}^{\gamma}\Big\{(\mathcal{M}^{\frac{1}{2\beta\lambda}}+\mathcal{M}^{\frac{1}{2\lambda}})h^{\frac{r-1}{2}}\Big(\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\Delta t\left\|{\rho^{n}}\right\|_{W^{r,\beta}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}+\mathcal{M}^{\frac{1}{2}}\sqrt{\Delta t}\\
&\quad+\Delta t\left\|{f_{t}}\right\|_{L^{2}(I,L^{2})}\Big\}+C\mathcal{M}^{\gamma+\frac{1}{\beta\lambda}}h^{r-1}\left\|{\rho^{n}}\right\|_{W^{r,\beta}},\end{split}

which proves (
120
). The proof is complete.
∎

6
Numerical results

In this section, we give a two simple numerical experiments using Galerkin finite element method in the two dimensional region to illustrate the convergent theory. For simplicity, we test the convergence of our method with the Forchheimer two-term law
g(s)=1+s
. Equation (
12
)
sg(s)=\xi,

s\geq 0
gives

The region is selected is unit square, i.e

A trichotomy for hitting times and escape rates for a class of unimodal maps Thanks: Part of this work was completed during visits of MT to Fairfield University in 2022 and 2023. MD was partially supported by NSF grant DMS 2055070.
Mark F. Demers, Mike Todd

Abstract
. We consider local escape rates and hitting time statistics for unimodal interval maps of Misiurewicz-Thurston type. We prove that for any point z z in the interval there is a local escape rate and hitting time statistics which is one of three types. While it is key that we cover all points z z , the particular interest here is when z z is periodic and in the postcritical orbit which yields the third part of the trichotomy. We also prove generalised asymptotic escape rates of the form first shown by Bruin, Demers and Todd.

1.
Introduction and main result

Much attention has been paid recently to the connection between hitting times on the one hand
[
FFT2
,
GKV
,
AHV
]
and
escape rates on the other
[
KL2
,
PU
,
DT2
]
. Given a map
f
of the interval preserving a probability measure
\mu
,
the principal connection is that in many situations the extremal index, defined by

-\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\log\mu(\{x:f^{j}(x)\notin B_{\varepsilon}(z),j=0,1,\ldots,\lfloor\mu(B_{\varepsilon}(z))^{-1}\rfloor\}),

where
B_{\varepsilon}(z)
denotes the ball of radius
\varepsilon
centred at
z
,
yields the same limit
[
BDT
]
as the local (or asymptotic) escape rate given by,

\lim_{\varepsilon\to\infty}\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{-1}{\mu(B_{\varepsilon}(z))}\frac{1}{n}\log\mu(\{x:f^{j}(x)\notin B_{\varepsilon}(z),j=0,1,\ldots n\})\,.

The limit typically obeys a dichotomy: if
z
is not periodic, the limit is 1; if
z
is periodic with prime period
p
, then
the limit is
1-\frac{1}{|Df^{p}(z)|}
if
\mu
is an acip. More generally, when
\mu
is an equilibrium state for a Hölder continuous potential
\phi
, the limit in the periodic case is
1-e^{S_{p}\phi(z)}
, where

S_{p}\phi(z)=\sum_{i=0}^{p-1}\phi(f^{i}(x))
.

Such dichotomies for asymptotic escape rates have been proved for all
z
in some uniformly hyperbolic contexts

[
BY
,
KL2
,
FP
,
HY
]
.
In the non-uniformly hyperbolic setting, the asymptotic escape rate has been achieved for large sets of
z
in e.g.
[
DT1
,
PU
,
BDT
,
DT2
]
.

In this paper we expand this dichotomy to a trichotomy by establishing conditions in which a third limit
is possible. We do this principally by considering unimodal maps whose post-critical orbit is eventually periodic and centring holes at such periodic points
z
. We show that in this case, the asymptotic escape rate is a function of
both
|Df^{p}(z)|
as well as the order of the spike in the invariant density at
z
.

Before stating our main result, we precisely define the class of maps to which our results will apply.

We consider S-unimodal maps
f:[0,1]\to[0,1]
in the sense of
[
NS
]
. That is

•

f
is
C^{2}
with one critical point
c
;

•

in a neighbourhood of
c
,
f(x)=f(c)-A(x-c)^{\ell}
for
\ell>1
and
A>0
;

•

|Df|^{-\frac{1}{2}}
is convex on each of
[0,c]
and
[c,1]
.

We assume that the map is Misiurewicz-Thurston: there is a minimal
k_{0}\geqslant 2
such that
f^{k_{0}}(c)
is periodic, i.e., there is a minimal
p\geqslant 1
such that
f^{p}(f^{k_{0}}(c))=f^{k_{0}}(c)
. Moreover this is a repelling periodic point:
|Df^{p}(f^{k_{0}}(c))|>1
. This implies that the postcritical orbit of
c
,
\mbox{\rm orb}(f(c))=\{f(c),f^{2}(c),\ldots,f^{k_{0}+p-1}(c)\}
, is finite and that there is an absolutely continuous
f
-invariant probability measure (acip)
\mu
. Then the density of
\mu
has a spike at each point of
\mbox{\rm orb}(f(c))
of type
x^{\frac{1}{\ell}-1}
.
We let
I:=[f^{2}(c),f(c)]
denote the
dynamical core
: all points except 0 and 1 eventually map into
I
and remain there, so
\mu
is supported in
I
.

Define a hole centred at
z
by
H_{\varepsilon}(z)=(z-\varepsilon,z+\varepsilon)
. The
escape rate
of the open system
with hole
H_{\varepsilon}(z)
with respect to the measure
\mu
is defined by

(1.1)

\mathfrak{e}(H_{\varepsilon}(z))=-\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\mu\left(\left\{x\in I:f^{j}(x)\notin H_{\varepsilon}(z),\ j=0,\ldots,n-1\right\}\right),

when the limit exists.
Define the
local (asymptotic) escape rate at
z
by

\mbox{esc}(z):=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{\mathfrak{e}(H_{\varepsilon}(z)))}{\mu(H_{\varepsilon}(z))}\,.

If
z
is a periodic point with prime period
p
, we write
\lambda_{z}:=|Df^{p}(z)|
.

Our main result is the following trichotomy regarding possible values of the local escape rate.
The only model where such a result has appeared previously is in
[
DT2
, Remark 3.11]
,
which is a very special case (the full quadratic map).

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
f
be as defined above. For any
z\in I
,

{\rm esc}(z)=\begin{cases}1&\text{ if $z$ is not periodic},\\
1-\frac{1}{\lambda_{z}}&\text{ if $z$ is periodic and not in orb}(f(c)),\\
1-\frac{1}{\lambda_{z}^{1/\ell}}&\text{ if $z$ is periodic and in orb}(f(c)).\\
\end{cases}

The cases when
z
is outside the postcritical orbit orb
(f(c))
follow from
[
DT2
, Theorem 3.7]
, so our focus here is on the case when
z\in\mbox{orb}(f(c))
: when

z\in\mbox{orb}(f(c))
is preperiodic (which falls into the first case in the above theorem), and when
z\in\mbox{orb}(f(c))
is periodic. The techniques to show the former are essentially a subset of the latter.
Our proofs exploit the fact that the finite post-critical orbit allows us to define a finite Markov partition
for the map (albeit with unbounded distortion) which we then use to define a first return map to a conveniently
chosen set.

1.1.
Hitting time statistics

Setting
r_{A}(x):=\inf\{k\geqslant 1:f^{k}(x)\in A\}
, the
first hitting time to
A
, the
Hitting Time Statistics (HTS)
at
z
is given by

T_{z}(t):=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\mu\left(r_{H_{\varepsilon}(z)}\geqslant\frac{t}{\mu(H_{\varepsilon}(z))}\right)

for
t>0
, provided this limit exists.
A generalisation of this limit is formulated in
[
BDT
]
, where the link between HTS and asymptotic escape rates was explored by scaling the hitting time asymptotic as

t\mu(H_{\varepsilon}(z))^{-\alpha}
for some
\alpha>0
.
Accordingly, define for
t,\alpha>0
,

L_{\alpha,t}(z):=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{-1}{t\mu(H_{\varepsilon}(z))^{1-\alpha}}\log\mu\left(r_{H_{\varepsilon}(z)}\geqslant\frac{t}{\mu(H_{\varepsilon}(z))^{\alpha}}\right)\,.

Note that
T_{z}(t)
corresponds to
\alpha=1
.
Using Theorem
1.1
we prove the following in Section
6
.

Theorem 1.2
.

For any
z\in I
and all
\alpha,t>0
,
L_{\alpha,t}(z)
exists and equals

L_{\alpha,t}(z)=\begin{cases}1&\text{ if $z$ is not periodic},\\
1-\frac{1}{\lambda_{z}}&\text{ if $z$ is periodic and not in }orb(f(c)),\\
1-\frac{1}{\lambda_{z}^{\frac{1}{\ell}}}&\text{ if $z$ is periodic and in }orb(f(c)).\\
\end{cases}

As with Theorem
1.1
, the main focus here is on the case when
z\in\mbox{orb}(f(c))
; the other cases follow from adaptations of
[
BDT
]
, as we describe in Section
6.4
.

We note that a full description of
T_{z}(t)
in uniformly hyperbolic cases can be seen in
[
FFT1
,

]
. In the non-uniformly hyperbolic case, the only full dichotomy (i.e. the result applies to all
z
with no
excluded values), and only for

rather than

as above, has been demonstrated for Manneville-Pomeau maps in

]
; see also the recent preprint

]
.

2.3.
Extra cuts for a ‘Markovian’ property

Suppose that
f^{p}
is orientation preserving around
z
. Let
a_{0}=a
and then inductively let
f^{p}(a_{i+1})=a_{i}
, so that
a_{i}\to z
as
i\to\infty
. We similarly define
(b_{i})_{i}
to the right of
z
, accumulating on
z
. Observe that domains
I_{i}^{k}
of
F
which map into
[a,b]
by some
f^{s}
with
1\leqslant s<\tau_{i}
must map onto some
(a_{k-1},a_{k})
or
(b_{k},b_{k-1})
.

In the orientation reversing case we define
a_{0}=a
, and
a_{1}
such that
f^{p}(b)=a_{1}
and inductively define
a_{i}
by
f^{2p}(a_{i+2})=a_{i}
. Similarly we define
(b_{i})_{i}
. Here if
f^{s}(I_{i}^{k})\subset[a,b]
for
1\leqslant s<\tau_{i}
then
f^{s}(I_{i}^{k})\in\{(a_{k-1},a_{k}),(b_{k},b_{k-1})\}
.

Note that by bounded distortion, the ratios
\frac{z-a_{i}}{b_{i}-z}
will be comparable to
\frac{z-a}{b-z}
.

The above implies that if we were to take our holes around
z
as, say
(a_{i},b_{j})
, these would be Markov holes in the sense that either an interval of the inducing scheme enters the hole before returning to
Y
, or it never intersects it. Note that by topological transitivity the only way
z
cannot be accumulated by domains on both the left and the right as above is if
z
is a boundary point of the dynamical core
[f^{2}(c),f(c)]
, a simpler case which needs only obvious modifications.

For generic
\varepsilon_{L},\varepsilon_{R}\in(0,\varepsilon_{1})
the corresponding hole will not have this Markov property, so we make an extra cut here, namely if
z-\varepsilon_{L}\in f^{s}(I_{i})
(in the sketch in Figure
1
,
I_{i}=I_{i}^{k-2}
) then we split
I_{i}
into two so that one of the resulting intervals maps by
f^{s}
into the hole and one maps outside, and similarly for
z+\varepsilon_{R}
, thus defining
F_{\bm{\varepsilon}}
from
F=F_{0}
.
For this map, in the generic case, the chains no longer have common boundary points, but now, in the orientation preserving case, they come in pairs
\{I_{i}^{k}\}_{k},\{\hat{I}_{i}^{k}\}
, where
I_{i}^{k+1}\bar{\cup}\hat{I}_{i}^{k}
is a domain of
F
, and
\bar{\cup}
denotes the union of the open intervals along with their common boundary point.

In the case
f^{p}
is orientation reversing around
z
, we may need to cut a domain of
F
twice to produce
F_{\bm{\varepsilon}}
. For simplicity, we will not address this case here, but all the ideas below go through similarly. From now on, unless indicated otherwise, when we discuss our induced map we mean
F_{\bm{\varepsilon}}
.

Remark that due to the extra cuts, although the domains of
F_{\bm{\varepsilon}}
have the property that each one either maps into
H_{\bm{\varepsilon}}(z)
or never intersects it, for generic holes this does not define a countable Markov partition for
F_{\bm{\varepsilon}}
.
This is because
F_{\bm{\varepsilon}}(I_{i})
may not be a union of 1-cylinders if a boundary point of
I_{i}
is created by one of the
extra cuts.

We next observe that in the orientation preserving case there is a unique pair of subchains we denote
\{I_{L}^{k}\}_{k\geqslant k_{L,\bm{\varepsilon}}},\{\hat{I}_{L}^{k}\}_{k\geqslant k_{L,\bm{\varepsilon}}}\subset(c-\delta(\bm{\varepsilon}),c)
and a unique pair of subchains
\{I_{R}^{k}\}_{k\geqslant k_{R,\bm{\varepsilon}}},\{\hat{I}_{R}^{k}\}_{k\geqslant k_{R,\bm{\varepsilon}}}\subset(c,c+\delta(\bm{\varepsilon}))
. We call these the
principal subchains
. Note
\overline{\cup_{k\geqslant k_{L,\bm{\varepsilon}}}\left(I_{L}^{k}\cup\hat{I}_{I_{L}}^{k}\right)\cup\left(\cup_{k\geqslant k_{R,\bm{\varepsilon}}}I_{R}^{k}\cup\hat{I}_{I_{R}}^{k}\right)}=[c-\delta(\bm{\varepsilon}),c+\delta(\bm{\varepsilon})]
. We can naturally extend this idea to the orientation reversing case.
In either case,
\delta(\bm{\varepsilon})
is determined by only one of
\varepsilon_{L}
or
\varepsilon_{R}
, depending on whether

f^{k_{1}}(c-\delta(\bm{\varepsilon}),c+\delta(\bm{\varepsilon}))=(z-\varepsilon_{L},z]
or
f^{k_{1}}(c-\delta(\bm{\varepsilon}),c+\delta(\bm{\varepsilon}))=[z,z+\varepsilon_{R})
.
Let us call
\varepsilon_{*}
this value in
\{\varepsilon_{L},\varepsilon_{R}\}
.

3.
Measuring the holes

The aim of this section is to estimate
\mu(H_{\bm{\varepsilon}})
,
\mu(H_{\bm{\varepsilon}}^{\prime})
and thus
\frac{\mu(H_{\bm{\varepsilon}}^{\prime})}{\mu(H_{\bm{\varepsilon}})}

3.1.
The contribution from principal subchains

In order to estimate
\mu(H_{\bm{\varepsilon}}^{\prime})
and
\mu(H_{\bm{\varepsilon}})
, we first estimate the contribution from the principal subchains.

Lemma 3.1
.

For
x
close to
c
,

|x-c|\sim\left(\frac{|f^{k_{1}}(x)-z|}{A|Df^{k_{1}-1}(f(c))|}\right)^{\frac{1}{\ell}}.

Proof.

By the Mean Value Theorem there exists
\theta\in(f(x),f(c))
such that
|f^{k_{1}}(x)-f^{k_{1}}(c)|=Df^{k_{1}-1}(\theta)|f(x)-f(c)|
.
Then,

\frac{|f^{k_{1}}(x)-f^{k_{1}}(c)|}{|x-c|}=\frac{|f(x)-f(c)|}{|x-c|}\frac{|f^{k_{1}}(x)-f^{k_{1}}(c)|}{|f(x)-f(c)|}=\frac{A|x-c|^{\ell}}{|x-c|}|Df^{k_{1}-1}(\theta)|\,,

where
A
is from Section
1
. The result follows using
|Df^{k_{1}-1}(\theta)|\sim|Df^{k_{1}-1}(f(c))|
by bounded distortion.
∎

We can see from Lemma
3.1
that
\left|\overline{\cup_{k\geqslant k_{L,\bm{\varepsilon}}}\left(I_{L}^{k}\cup\hat{I}_{L}^{k}\right)}\right|\sim C_{p}(\varepsilon_{*})^{\frac{1}{\ell}}
where
C_{p}:=\frac{1}{(A|Df^{k_{1}-1}(f(c))|)^{\frac{1}{\ell}}}
,
i.e.
\delta(\bm{\varepsilon})\sim C_{p}(\varepsilon_{*})^{1/\ell}
.
Since
c
is bounded away from the critical orbit, as in
[
M
, Theorem 6.1]
(see also
[
N
, Theorem A]
) the invariant density is continuous at
c
and defining
\rho:=\frac{d\mu}{dm}(c)
,
the
\mu_{Y}
-measure of the set can be estimated by,

(3.1)

\displaystyle\sum_{k\geqslant k_{L,\bm{\varepsilon}}}\mu_{Y}(I_{L}^{k}\cup\hat{I}_{L}^{k})

\displaystyle\sim\frac{C_{p}\rho}{\mu(Y)}(\varepsilon_{*})^{\frac{1}{\ell}}.

Similarly for
\sum_{k\geqslant k_{L,\bm{\varepsilon}}}\mu_{Y}(I_{R}^{k}\cup\hat{I}_{R}^{k})
. As in (
2.2
), the sum of these two quantities scaled by
\mu(Y)
is the relevant contribution to
\mu(H_{\bm{\varepsilon}}^{\prime})
.

Now for the contribution to
\mu(H_{\bm{\varepsilon}})
,
let us suppose that
f^{p}
is locally orientation preserving at
z
and

is also orientation preserving at

(which implies that

\mu_{Y}(J^{k_{L,\bm{\varepsilon}}+k})\sim\tfrac{\rho C_{p}}{\mu(Y)}|(z-\varepsilon_{L})_{k}-z|^{\frac{1}{\ell}}\sim\tfrac{\rho C_{p}}{\mu(Y)}\lambda_{z}^{-\frac{k}{\ell}}\varepsilon_{L}^{\frac{1}{\ell}}=\tfrac{\rho C_{p}}{\mu(Y)}\lambda_{z}^{-\frac{k}{\ell}}\varepsilon_{*}^{\frac{1}{\ell}}\,.

3.2.

We will deal with the orientation preserving case here, the orientation reversing case is similar.
Suppose that

2\sum_{s\geqslant 1}K_{\delta}^{-1}e^{s(\eta-\log\lambda_{\delta})}\varepsilon^{\prime}=O(1)\varepsilon^{\prime},

3.3.
The limiting ratio in the periodic postcritical case

We will assume that
\frac{\varepsilon_{L}}{\varepsilon_{R}}
is uniformly bounded away from 0 and infinity (so in particular
\varepsilon_{L},\varepsilon_{R}=O(\varepsilon_{*})
), so that the density spike dominates the measure of our holes.
Now recalling (
2.2
), (
3.1
) together with (
3.3
) imply

\mu(H_{\bm{\varepsilon}}^{\prime})\sim 2C_{p}\rho(\varepsilon_{*})^{\frac{1}{\ell}}+O(1)\varepsilon_{*}

while (
3.2
) and the analogue of (
3.3
) imply

\mu(H_{\bm{\varepsilon}})\sim 2C_{p}\frac{\rho(\varepsilon_{*})^{\frac{1}{\ell}}}{1-\lambda_{z}^{-\frac{1}{\ell}}}+O(1)\varepsilon_{*},

so the two principal chains dominate this estimate and the limiting ratio is

(3.4)

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{\mu(H_{\bm{\varepsilon}}^{\prime})}{\mu(H_{\bm{\varepsilon}})}=1-\lambda_{z}^{-\frac{1}{\ell}}\,.

3.4.
The preperiodic postcritical case

For the case that

\begin{split}|\nu_{\bm{\varepsilon}}(\psi)-\nu_{{\bm{\varepsilon}^{\prime}}}(\psi)|&\leqslant|\mathring{e}_{\bm{\varepsilon}}(\mathring{g}_{\bm{\varepsilon}}\psi-\mathring{g}_{\bm{\varepsilon}^{\prime}}\psi)|+|\mathring{e}_{\bm{\varepsilon}}(\mathring{g}_{\bm{\varepsilon}^{\prime}}\psi)-e_{\bm{\varepsilon}^{\prime}}(\mathring{g}_{\bm{\varepsilon}^{\prime}}\psi)|\\
&\leqslant\left|\int(\mathring{g}_{\bm{\varepsilon}}-\mathring{g}_{\bm{\varepsilon}^{\prime}})\psi\,d\mathring{m}_{\bm{\varepsilon}}\right|+\left|\int\big(\Pi_{\bm{\varepsilon}}(\mathring{g}_{\bm{\varepsilon}^{\prime}}\psi)-\Pi_{\bm{\varepsilon}^{\prime}}(\mathring{g}_{\bm{\varepsilon}^{\prime}}\psi)\big)\,dm\right|\\
&\leqslant|\psi|_{\infty}|\mathring{g}_{\bm{\varepsilon}}-\mathring{g}_{\bm{\varepsilon}^{\prime}}|_{L^{1}(\mathring{m}_{\bm{\varepsilon}})}+|||\Pi_{\bm{\varepsilon}}-\Pi_{\bm{\varepsilon}^{\prime}}|||\,\|\mathring{g}_{\bm{\varepsilon}^{\prime}}\psi\|_{BV}\,.\end{split}

Both differences go to 0 as
\bm{\varepsilon}^{\prime}\to\bm{\varepsilon}
by Theorem
4.7
, while
\|\mathring{g}_{\bm{\varepsilon}^{\prime}}\|_{BV}
is uniformly
bounded in
\bm{\varepsilon}^{\prime}
by Proposition
4.5
.
We conclude that
\nu_{\bm{\varepsilon}}
is continuous in
\bm{\varepsilon}
for fixed
\beta
when acting on
BV
functions.

It remains to prove (
4.14
).
Unfortunately,

\tau\notin BV(Y)
so first we must show that
\nu_{\bm{\varepsilon}}(\tau)
is well-defined.
Indeed, it is easy to check that
\mathring{\mathcal{L}}_{\bm{\varepsilon}}\tau\in BV
. This holds since

\tau
is constant on each 1-cylinder
Y_{i,\bm{\varepsilon}}
for
F_{\bm{\varepsilon}}
. Thus
\mathring{\mathcal{L}}_{\bm{\varepsilon}}\tau
has discontinuities
only at the endpoints of
\{\mathring{F}_{\bm{\varepsilon}}(Y_{i,\bm{\varepsilon}})\}_{i}
, which
is a finite collection of intervals.

It follows also that
\mathring{\mathcal{L}}_{\bm{\varepsilon}}(\tau\mathring{g}_{\bm{\varepsilon}})\in BV(Y)
. Thus using (
4.11
),

\begin{split}\lim_{n\to\infty}\Lambda_{\bm{\varepsilon}}^{-n}&\int_{\mathring{Y}^{n}_{\bm{\varepsilon}}}\tau\mathring{g}_{\bm{\varepsilon}}\,dm=\lim_{n\to\infty}\Lambda_{\bm{\varepsilon}}^{-n}\int_{Y}\mathring{\mathcal{L}}_{\bm{\varepsilon}}^{n-1}(\mathring{\mathcal{L}}_{\bm{\varepsilon}}(\tau\mathring{g}_{\bm{\varepsilon}}))\,dm\\
&=\Lambda_{\bm{\varepsilon}}^{-1}\int\Pi_{\bm{\varepsilon}}(\mathring{\mathcal{L}}_{\bm{\varepsilon}}(\tau\mathring{g}_{\bm{\varepsilon}}))\,dm+\lim_{n\to\infty}\int\Lambda_{\bm{\varepsilon}}^{-n}{\mathcal{R}}_{\bm{\varepsilon}}^{n-1}(\mathring{\mathcal{L}}_{\bm{\varepsilon}}(\tau\mathring{g}_{\bm{\varepsilon}}))\,dm\,,\end{split}

and the second term converges to 0 by Theorem
4.7
. Thus the limit defining

\nu_{\bm{\varepsilon}}(\tau)
exists and is uniformly bounded in
\bm{\varepsilon}
for fixed
\beta
. More than this,
the above calculation can be improved to show that
\tau
is uniformly (in
\bm{\varepsilon}
) integrable with respect to
\nu_{\bm{\varepsilon}}
,
as follows.
For each
N>0
, we use the above to estimate,

\nu_{\bm{\varepsilon}}(1_{\tau>N}\cdot\tau)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\Lambda_{\bm{\varepsilon}}^{-n}\int\mathring{\mathcal{L}}_{\bm{\varepsilon}}^{n-1}(\mathring{\mathcal{L}}_{\bm{\varepsilon}}(1_{\tau>N}\cdot\tau\mathring{g}_{\bm{\varepsilon}}))\,dm\leqslant\Lambda_{\bm{\varepsilon}}^{-1}|\mathring{\mathcal{L}}_{\bm{\varepsilon}}(1_{\tau>N}\cdot\tau\mathring{g}_{\bm{\varepsilon}})|_{\infty}\,.

Then using bounded distortion as in (
4.19
), one has for
x\in Y
,

\begin{split}|\mathring{\mathcal{L}}_{\bm{\varepsilon}}(1_{\tau>N}\cdot\tau\mathring{g}_{\bm{\varepsilon}})(x)|&\leqslant\frac{(1+C_{d})|\mathring{g}_{\bm{\varepsilon}}|_{\infty}}{C_{\beta}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}y\in\mathring{F}_{\bm{\varepsilon}}^{-1}x\\
\tau(y)>N\end{subarray}}\tau(y)m(Y_{i,\bm{\varepsilon}}(y))\\
&\leqslant C\sum_{k>N}k\,m(\tau=k)\leqslant C^{\prime}\sum_{k>N}ke^{-k(\log\lambda_{per}-\eta)}\,,\end{split}

where we have used Proposition
2.5
(c).

It follows that for each
\kappa>0
, there exists
N>0
such that
\sup_{\bm{\varepsilon}\in[0,\varepsilon_{\beta})}\{\nu_{\bm{\varepsilon}}(1_{\tau>N}\cdot\tau)\}<\kappa

where the sup is restricted to
\beta
-allowable values of
\bm{\varepsilon}
.
Let
\tau^{(N)}=\min\{\tau,N\}
and note that
\tau^{(N)}\in BV(Y)
.
Then taking a limit along
\beta
-allowable
\bm{\varepsilon}^{\prime}
yields,

\lim_{\bm{\varepsilon}^{\prime}\to\bm{\varepsilon}}|\nu_{\bm{\varepsilon}}(\tau)-\nu_{{\bm{\varepsilon}^{\prime}}}(\tau)|\leqslant\lim_{\bm{\varepsilon}^{\prime}\to\bm{\varepsilon}}|\nu_{\bm{\varepsilon}}(\tau^{(N)})-\nu_{{\bm{\varepsilon}^{\prime}}}(\tau^{(N)})|+|\nu_{\bm{\varepsilon}}(1_{\tau>N}\cdot\tau)|+|\nu_{{\bm{\varepsilon}^{\prime}}}(1_{\tau>N}\cdot\tau)|\leqslant 2\kappa\,,

since we have shown that
\nu_{\bm{\varepsilon}^{\prime}}\to\nu_{\bm{\varepsilon}}
as elements of
BV(Y)^{*}
. Since
\kappa>0
was arbitrary, this
proves that
\nu_{\bm{\varepsilon}}(\tau)
varies continuously in
\bm{\varepsilon}
.

Recall that
\{Y_{i}\}_{i}
denotes the set of 1-cylinders for
F_{0}
and let
{\mathcal{J}}_{0}=\{F_{0}(Y_{i})\}_{i}
denote the finite
set of images. The covering property of
f
implies that the set of preimages of endpoints of elements
of
{\mathcal{J}}_{0}
is dense in
I
.
Thus there is a dense set of
\bm{\varepsilon}<\varepsilon_{\beta}
such that

F_{\bm{\varepsilon}}
admits a countable Markov partition with finite images. For such
\bm{\varepsilon}
,

[
DT1
, Section 6.4.1]
implies that
\nu_{\bm{\varepsilon}}
is an equilibrium state for the potential

-\Xi_{\bm{\varepsilon}}\cdot\log(DF_{\bm{\varepsilon}})-\log\Lambda_{\bm{\varepsilon}}
, where

\Xi_{\bm{\varepsilon}}(x)=1
if
x\in Y\setminus H_{\bm{\varepsilon}}^{\prime}
and
\Xi_{\bm{\varepsilon}}=\infty
if
x\in H_{\bm{\varepsilon}}^{\prime}
.
Similarly,
[
BDM
, Lemma 5.3]
implies
that
\nu_{\bm{\varepsilon}}
is a Gibbs measure for the potential

-\Xi_{\bm{\varepsilon}}\cdot\log(DF_{\bm{\varepsilon}})-\tau\mathfrak{e}(H_{\bm{\varepsilon}}(z))
with pressure equal to 0.
Putting these together yields (
4.14
) for such ‘Markov holes.’

Finally, we extend the relation to all
\bm{\varepsilon}
via the continuity of
\Lambda_{\bm{\varepsilon}}
and
\nu_{\bm{\varepsilon}}(\tau)
.
Since
\mathfrak{e}(H_{\bm{\varepsilon}}(z))
is monotonic in
\bm{\varepsilon}
and equals
-\log\Lambda_{\bm{\varepsilon}}/\nu_{\bm{\varepsilon}}(\tau)
on a dense set
of
\bm{\varepsilon}
, it must also be continuous in
\bm{\varepsilon}
. Thus the relation (
4.14
) holds for all
\bm{\varepsilon}<\varepsilon_{\beta}

which are
\beta
-allowable.
∎

With Lemma
4.8
in hand, we see that the limit we would like to compute to prove
Theorem
1.1
can be expressed as follows,

(4.16)

\frac{\mathfrak{e}(H_{\bm{\varepsilon}}(z))}{\mu(H_{\bm{\varepsilon}}(z))}=\frac{-\log\Lambda_{\bm{\varepsilon}}}{\mu_{Y}(H_{\bm{\varepsilon}}^{\prime})}\cdot\frac{\int\tau\,d\mu_{Y}}{\int\tau\,d\nu_{\bm{\varepsilon}}}\cdot\frac{\mu(H_{\bm{\varepsilon}}^{\prime})}{\mu(H_{\bm{\varepsilon}}(z))}\,,

where as before
\mu_{Y}=\frac{\mu|_{Y}}{\mu(Y)}
, and
1/\mu(Y)=\int_{Y}\tau\,d\mu_{Y}
by Kac’s Lemma since
F_{\varepsilon}
is a first-return map to
Y
. Theorem
1.1
will follow once
we show that as
\bm{\varepsilon}\to 0
,

(4.17)

\frac{-\log\Lambda_{\bm{\varepsilon}}}{\mu_{Y}(H_{\bm{\varepsilon}}^{\prime})}\to 1\,\quad\frac{\int\tau\,d\mu_{Y}}{\int\tau\,d\nu_{\bm{\varepsilon}}}\to 1\,,\quad\frac{\mu(H_{\bm{\varepsilon}}^{\prime})}{\mu(H_{\bm{\varepsilon}}(z))}\to 1-\lambda_{z}^{-1/\ell}\,.

The third limit above is precisely (
3.4
) when
z\in\mbox{orb}(f(c))
is periodic
(and is simply 1 by Section
3.4
when
z\in\mbox{orb}(f(c))
is preperiodic)
, so we proceed to prove the first two.
We first prove these limits for
\beta
-allowable holes with a fixed
\beta>0

in Lemmas
4.9
and
4.10
. Then in Section
5

we show how
to obtain the general limit as
\bm{\varepsilon}\to 0
.

Lemma 4.9
.

For fixed
\beta>0
and any sequence of
\beta
-allowable holes
H_{\bm{\varepsilon}}(z)
,

\lim_{\bm{\varepsilon}\to 0}\frac{-\log\Lambda_{\bm{\varepsilon}}}{\mu_{Y}(H_{\bm{\varepsilon}}^{\prime})}=1\,.

Proof.

The lemma could follow using the results of
[
KL2
]
, yet since
z\notin Y
in our setting, it is not clear how to
verify the aperiodicity of
H_{\bm{\varepsilon}}^{\prime}
without imposing an additional condition on the reentry of points to
Y

which have spent some time in a neighbourhood of
z
. To avoid this, we will argue directly, as in

[
DT2
, Proof of Theorem 7.2]
, yet our argument is simpler since our operators
\mathring{\mathcal{L}}_{\bm{\varepsilon}}
approach a
fixed operator
\mathcal{L}_{0}
via Lemma
4.6
in contrast to the situation in
[
DT2
]
.

We assume that
\bm{\varepsilon}<\varepsilon_{\beta}
so that we are in the setting of Theorem
4.7
. Then
since
g_{0}\in BV(Y)
, iterating
(
4.11
) yields for any
n\geqslant 1
,

\mathring{\mathcal{L}}_{\bm{\varepsilon}}^{n}g_{0}=\Lambda_{\bm{\varepsilon}}^{n}\mathring{e}_{\bm{\varepsilon}}(g_{0})\mathring{g}_{\bm{\varepsilon}}+{\mathcal{R}}_{\bm{\varepsilon}}g_{0}\implies\mathring{g}_{\bm{\varepsilon}}=\frac{1}{\mathring{e}_{\bm{\varepsilon}}(g_{0})}(\Lambda_{\bm{\varepsilon}}^{-n}\mathring{\mathcal{L}}_{\bm{\varepsilon}}^{n}g_{0}-\Lambda_{\bm{\varepsilon}}^{-n}{\mathcal{R}}_{\bm{\varepsilon}}^{n}g_{0})\,.

Using this relation and Lemma
4.6
yields,

(4.18)

\begin{split}1-\Lambda_{\bm{\varepsilon}}&=\int\mathring{g}_{\bm{\varepsilon}}\,dm-\int\mathring{\mathcal{L}}_{\bm{\varepsilon}}\mathring{g}_{\bm{\varepsilon}}\,dm=\int(\mathcal{L}_{0}-\mathring{\mathcal{L}}_{\bm{\varepsilon}})\mathring{g}_{\bm{\varepsilon}}\,dm=\int_{H_{\bm{\varepsilon}}^{\prime}}\mathring{g}_{\bm{\varepsilon}}\,dm\\
&=\frac{1}{\mathring{e}_{\bm{\varepsilon}}(g_{0})}\int_{H_{\bm{\varepsilon}}^{\prime}}(\Lambda_{\bm{\varepsilon}}^{-n}\mathring{\mathcal{L}}_{\bm{\varepsilon}}^{n}g_{0}-\Lambda_{\bm{\varepsilon}}^{-n}{\mathcal{R}}_{\bm{\varepsilon}}^{n}g_{0})\,dm\\
&=\frac{1}{\mathring{e}_{\bm{\varepsilon}}(g_{0})}\left(\int_{H_{\bm{\varepsilon}}^{\prime}}g_{0}\,dm-\int_{H_{\bm{\varepsilon}}^{\prime}}(1-\Lambda_{\bm{\varepsilon}}^{-n}\mathring{\mathcal{L}}_{\bm{\varepsilon}})g_{0}\,dm-\int_{H_{\bm{\varepsilon}}^{\prime}}\Lambda_{\bm{\varepsilon}}^{-n}{\mathcal{R}}_{\bm{\varepsilon}}^{n}g_{0}\,dm\right)\,.\end{split}

By Theorem
4.7
, the third term on the right side of (
4.18
) is bounded by

\int_{H_{\bm{\varepsilon}}^{\prime}}\Lambda_{\bm{\varepsilon}}^{-n}{\mathcal{R}}_{\bm{\varepsilon}}^{n}g_{0}\,dm\leqslant B_{\beta}e^{-\eta_{\beta}n}\|g_{0}\|_{BV}c_{g}^{-1}\int_{H_{\bm{\varepsilon}}^{\prime}}g_{0}\,dm\,,

where
c_{g}=\inf_{Y}g_{0}>0
according to (
4.12
).

Next, the second term on the right side of (
4.18
) can be expressed as,

\int_{H_{\bm{\varepsilon}}^{\prime}}(1-\Lambda_{\bm{\varepsilon}}^{-n}\mathring{\mathcal{L}}_{\bm{\varepsilon}})g_{0}\,dm=(1-\Lambda_{\bm{\varepsilon}}^{-n})\int_{H_{\bm{\varepsilon}}^{\prime}}g_{0}\,dm+\Lambda_{\bm{\varepsilon}}^{-n}\int_{H_{\bm{\varepsilon}}^{\prime}}(\mathcal{L}_{0}^{n}-\mathring{\mathcal{L}}_{\bm{\varepsilon}}^{n})g_{0}\,dm\,,

using the fact that
\mathcal{L}_{0}g_{0}=g_{0}
. We claim that
(\mathcal{L}_{0}^{n}-\mathring{\mathcal{L}}_{\bm{\varepsilon}}^{n})g_{0}
can be made small in

L^{\infty}(Y)
. To see this, choose
n=kn_{0}
and write for
x\in Y
,

(\mathcal{L}_{0}^{n}-\mathring{\mathcal{L}}_{\bm{\varepsilon}}^{n})g_{0}(x)=\sum_{y\in F_{0}^{-n}x}\frac{g_{0}(y)1_{Y\setminus\mathring{Y}^{n}_{\bm{\varepsilon}}}(y)}{|DF_{0}^{n}(y)|}=\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\sum_{y\in F_{0}^{-n}x}\frac{g_{0}(y)1_{\mathring{Y}_{\bm{\varepsilon}}^{jn_{0}}\setminus\mathring{Y}_{\bm{\varepsilon}}^{(j+1)n_{0}}}(y)}{|DF_{0}^{n}(y)|}\,,

where we have used the fact that when
n=kn_{0}
,

Y\setminus\mathring{Y}_{\bm{\varepsilon}}^{n}=\cup_{j=0}^{k-1}(\mathring{Y}_{\bm{\varepsilon}}^{jn_{0}}\setminus\mathring{Y}_{\bm{\varepsilon}}^{(j+1)n_{0}})
and
the union is disjoint.
Next, if
Y_{i,j,\varepsilon}(y)
is the
jn_{0}
-cylinder for
F_{\bm{\varepsilon}}^{jn_{0}}
containing
y
, then by bounded distortion and Lemma
4.3
,

(4.19)

|DF^{jn_{0}}(y)|\geqslant\frac{m(F^{jn_{0}}(Y_{i,j,\varepsilon}(y))}{(1+C_{d})m(Y_{i,j,\varepsilon}(y))}\geqslant\frac{C_{\beta}}{(1+C_{d})m(Y_{i,j,\varepsilon}(y))}\,.

Then, applying also (
4.2
), we have

(\mathcal{L}_{0}^{n}-\mathring{\mathcal{L}}_{\bm{\varepsilon}}^{n})g_{0}(x)\leqslant\frac{(1+C_{d})|g_{0}|_{\infty}}{C_{\beta}}\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}m(\mathring{Y}^{jn_{0}}_{\bm{\varepsilon}}\setminus\mathring{Y}^{(j+1)n_{0}}_{\bm{\varepsilon}})3^{k-j}=:\rho_{n}(\bm{\varepsilon})\,,

and note that
\rho_{n}(\bm{\varepsilon})\to 0
as
\bm{\varepsilon}\to 0
for fixed
n=kn_{0}
. Thus, the second term on the right side of (
4.18
)
is estimated by,

\int_{H_{\bm{\varepsilon}}^{\prime}}(1-\Lambda_{\bm{\varepsilon}}^{-n}\mathring{\mathcal{L}}_{\bm{\varepsilon}})g_{0}\,dm\leqslant\big(1-\Lambda_{\bm{\varepsilon}}^{-n}+c_{g}^{-1}\Lambda_{\bm{\varepsilon}}^{-n}\rho_{n}(\bm{\varepsilon})\big)\mu_{Y}(H_{\bm{\varepsilon}}^{\prime}).

Putting these estimates into (
4.18
) yields,

\frac{1-\Lambda_{\bm{\varepsilon}}}{\mu_{Y}(H_{\bm{\varepsilon}}^{\prime})}=\frac{1}{\mathring{e}_{\bm{\varepsilon}}(g_{0})}\left(1+\mathcal{O}(e^{-\eta_{\beta}n}+(1-\Lambda_{\bm{\varepsilon}}^{-n})+\Lambda_{\bm{\varepsilon}}^{-n}\rho_{n}(\bm{\varepsilon})\right).

Fixing
\kappa>0
, first choose
n=kn_{0}
so that
e^{-\eta_{\beta}n}<\kappa
. Next, choose

\bm{\varepsilon}
so small that by the continuity of the spectral data from the proof of Theorem
4.7
,

|1-\Lambda_{\bm{\varepsilon}}^{-n}|<\kappa
,
\Lambda_{\bm{\varepsilon}}^{-n}\leqslant 2
,
\rho_{n}(\bm{\varepsilon})<\kappa
and

|\mathring{e}_{\bm{\varepsilon}}(g_{0})-1|<\kappa
. This last bound is possible since
e_{0}(g_{0})=\int g_{0}\,dm=1
. Thus the
relevant expression is
1+\mathcal{O}(\kappa)
for
\bm{\varepsilon}
sufficiently small and
\beta
-allowable. Since

\kappa
is arbitrary, the lemma is proved.
∎

Recall that the inducing time
\tau
for
F_{\bm{\varepsilon}}
does not depend on
\bm{\varepsilon}
.

Lemma 4.10
.

For fixed
\beta>0
and any sequence of
\beta
-allowable holes
H_{\bm{\varepsilon}}(z)
,

\lim_{\bm{\varepsilon}\to 0}\frac{\int\tau\,d\mu_{Y}}{\int\tau\,d\nu_{\bm{\varepsilon}}}=1\,.

Proof.

As above, we assume
\bm{\varepsilon}<\varepsilon_{\beta}
. Recall that
\nu_{0}=\mu_{Y}
and
e_{0}(\psi)=\int\psi\,dm
since

m
is conformal for
\mathcal{L}_{0}
. Thus the estimates (
4.15
) and following in the proof
of Lemma
4.8
hold equally well with
{\bm{\varepsilon}^{\prime}}=0
throughout. Thus the continuity
of
\nu_{\bm{\varepsilon}}(\tau)
extends to
\bm{\varepsilon}=0
. This, plus the fact that
\nu_{\bm{\varepsilon}}(\tau)\geqslant 1
since
\tau\geqslant 1
implies the
required limit.
∎

5.
Completion of the proof of Theorem
1.1
via approximation

Putting together Section
3.3
and Lemmas
4.9
and
4.10
, we have
proved Theorem
1.1
for each
\beta>0
along sequences
(\bm{\varepsilon}_{n})_{n}
where each
\bm{\varepsilon}_{n}
is
\beta
-allowable.
It remains to consider the alternative case where we have to approximate
a given sequence
\varepsilon_{n}
by
\beta
-allowable
\bm{\varepsilon}_{n}^{\prime}
. We will focus our estimates on approximating on the left, with the right-hand side following similarly. We also start by assuming
z\in\mbox{orb}(f(c))
is periodic.

We remark that if
H_{\bm{\varepsilon}}
is
\beta
-allowable, then it is also
\beta^{\prime}
-allowable for any
\beta^{\prime}<\beta
,
so we will take our approximating sequence with
\beta
tending to 0. Without loss of generality and for convenience, we assume

\beta<(2\lambda_{z})^{-1}
.

Recall the notation
(a_{i})_{i},(b_{i})_{i}
from Section
2.3
, i.e.
(a_{i},a_{i+1})

are the
f^{s}
-images of subchains of intervals accumulating on
z
from the left, while
(b_{i+1},b_{i})
accumulate
on
z
from the right. Recall that
\frac{z-a_{i}}{b_{i}-z}
are uniformly bounded away from 0 and
\infty
. As in Lemma
2.9
,
we have
f^{p}(a_{i},a_{i+1})=(a_{i-1},a_{i})
if
f^{p}
is orientation preserving at
z
, and

f^{2p}(a_{i},a_{i+1})=(a_{i-2},a_{i-1})
if
f^{p}
is orientation reversing. We will assume the
orientation preserving case in the following, the orientation reversing case being similar.

Each interval
(a_{i},a_{i+1})
is
f^{s}(I_{j})
for some one-cylinder
I_{j}
for
F_{\bm{\varepsilon}}
. Each

I_{j}
in turn is subdivided into a countable union of
n_{0}
-cylinders for
F_{\bm{\varepsilon}}

and so
(a_{i},a_{i+1})
is subdivided into a countable union of
f^{s}
images of these
n_{0}
-cylinders.
Indeed, given the chain structure, the arrangement of
f^{s}
images of the
n_{0}
-cylinders
in
(a_{i},a_{i+1})
maps precisely under
f^{p}
onto the
f^{s}
images of the
n_{0}
-cylinders
in
(a_{i-1},a_{i})
.

We describe this structure as follows: each
(a_{i},a_{i+1})
is subdivided into finitely many
intervals (depending on
n_{0}
), which we index by
j
,
j=1,\ldots,J_{i}
. The end points of these intervals are
preimages of the boundaries of one-cylinders in
Y
, some of which may map onto
c
before

n_{0}
iterates of
F_{\varepsilon}
,
in which case they are in fact accumulation points of preimages of the chains recalled above.
We label these chains of intervals
(c_{i,j,k},c_{i,j,k+1})
which accumulate on the
j
th point
in
(a_{i},a_{i+1})
from the left, and similarly
(d_{i,j,k+1},d_{i,j,k})
accumulate from the right.

Set
v_{i,j,k}=c_{i,j,k+1}-c_{i,j,k}
. According to our definition, if
\varepsilon
is
\beta
-left-allowable,
then

z-\varepsilon\in A_{L,\beta}:=\cup_{i,j,k}[c_{i,j,k}+\beta v_{i},c_{i,j,k+1}-\beta v_{i}]\,.

So if
\varepsilon
is not
\beta
-left-allowable, then
z-\varepsilon\in(c_{i,j,k+1}-\beta v_{i,j,k},c_{i,j,k+1}+\beta v_{i,j,k})

for some
i,j,k
. Remark that if there is no accumulation of chains at the
j
th point, then the index
k
is redundant.
Also, there is some duplication since
c_{i,0}=a_{i}
, while
c_{i,J_{i}}=a_{i+1}
, yet for uniformity of notation we
denote each range of non-
\beta
-left-allowable values of
z-\varepsilon
as above.

We approximate
\varepsilon
from above by

\varepsilon_{o}^{L}:=z-(c_{i,j,k+1}-\beta v_{i,j,k})
and from below by

\varepsilon_{u}^{L}:=z-(c_{i,j,k+1}+\beta v_{i,j,k})
. Both
\varepsilon_{o}^{L}
and
\varepsilon_{u}^{L}
are
\beta
-left-allowable
and
\varepsilon\in(\varepsilon_{u}^{L},\varepsilon_{o}^{L})
.
Thus,

\frac{\varepsilon_{u}^{L}}{\varepsilon_{o}^{L}}\leqslant\frac{\varepsilon_{u}^{L}}{\varepsilon}\leqslant\frac{\varepsilon_{o}^{L}}{\varepsilon}\leqslant\frac{\varepsilon_{o}^{L}}{\varepsilon_{u}^{L}}\,,

so we focus on estimating the outer two quantities. Notice also that the above implies
\frac{\varepsilon_{u}^{L}}{\varepsilon_{u}^{R}},\frac{\varepsilon_{o}^{L}}{\varepsilon_{o}^{R}}
are uniformly bounded away from 0 and
\infty
, due to the uniform bound on
\frac{z-a_{i}}{b_{i}-z}
, so we may apply (
3.4
).

Now,

\frac{\varepsilon_{u}^{L}}{\varepsilon_{o}^{L}}=\frac{z-(c_{i,j,k+1}+\beta v_{i,j,k})}{z-(c_{i,j,k+1}-\beta v_{i,j,k})}=1-\frac{2\beta v_{i,j,k}}{z-(c_{i,j,k}-\beta v_{i,j,k})}\,.

Note that
z-(c_{i,j,k+1}-\beta v_{i,j,k})\geqslant z-a_{i+1}
while
v_{i,j,k}\leqslant e_{i}:=a_{i+1}-a_{i}
.
But due to the chain structure around
z
, we have
z-a_{i+1}>e_{i+1}\sim\lambda_{z}^{-1}e_{i}
, so that

(5.1)

\frac{\varepsilon_{u}^{L}}{\varepsilon_{o}^{L}}\geqslant 1-\frac{2\beta e_{i}}{e_{i+1}}\gtrsim 1-2\beta\lambda_{z}\,.

In the same way,
\frac{\varepsilon_{o}^{L}}{\varepsilon_{u}^{L}}\lesssim 1+2\beta\lambda_{z}
. The right hand estimates for
the analogous
\varepsilon_{u}^{R}
and
\varepsilon_{o}^{R}
are similar.

To complete the proof of Theorem
1.1
, we must evaluate the following limit as
\varepsilon\to 0
,

(5.2)

\frac{1}{\mu(z-\varepsilon,z+\varepsilon)}\frac{-1}{n}\log\mu\left(\left\{x\in I:f^{i}(x)\notin(z-\varepsilon,z+\varepsilon),\ i=1,\ldots,n\right\}\right)\,.

We estimate this from below by

\frac{\mu(z-\varepsilon_{u}^{L},z+\varepsilon_{u}^{R})}{\mu(z-\varepsilon,z+\varepsilon)}\frac{1}{\mu(z-\varepsilon_{u}^{L},z+\varepsilon_{u}^{R})}\frac{-1}{n}\log\mu\left(\left\{x\in I:f^{i}(x)\notin(z-\varepsilon_{u}^{L},z+\varepsilon_{u}^{L}),\ i=1,\ldots,n\right\}\right).

By the above estimates,

\frac{\mu(z-\varepsilon_{u}^{L},z+\varepsilon_{u}^{R})}{\mu(z-\varepsilon,z+\varepsilon)}\sim(1-2\beta\lambda_{z})^{\frac{1}{\ell}}
,
so taking the limit as
\varepsilon\to 0
yields an lower bound of

(1-2\beta\lambda_{z})^{\frac{1}{\ell}}\cdot\left(1-\lambda_{z}^{-1/\ell}\right)\,,

where the second factor comes from the application of Theorem
1.1
to
\beta
-allowable holes
via (
4.17
). Similarly, one obtains an upper bound for (
5.2
) of

(1+2\beta\lambda_{z})^{\frac{1}{\ell}}\cdot\left(1-\lambda_{z}^{-1/\ell}\right)
.
Since these bounds hold for all sufficiently small
\beta
, we take
\beta\to 0
to obtain the required
limit for Theorem
1.1
along an arbitrary sequence
(\varepsilon_{n})_{n}

in the case that
z\in\mbox{orb}(f(c))
is periodic.

Finally notice that if
z\in\mbox{orb}(f(c))
is preperiodic then the above calculations all go through similarly: from the construction of
(Y,F)
in Section
3.4
, the periodic structure of the postcritical orbit can be pulled back to
z
to generate the
(a_{i})_{i},\ (b_{i})_{i}
required, but our bounds are of the form
(1\pm 2\beta\lambda)
where
\lambda=|Df^{p}(f^{k_{0}}(z))|
.

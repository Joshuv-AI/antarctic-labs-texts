Quantisation of semisimple real Lie groups
K. De Commer

Abstract
. We provide a novel construction of quantized universal enveloping ∗ * -algebras of real semisimple Lie algebras, based on Letzter’s theory of quantum symmetric pairs. We show that these structures can be ‘integrated’, leading to a quantization of the group C ∗ -algebra of an arbitrary semisimple algebraic real Lie group.

Introduction

The theory of quantum groups, as it was initiated through the works of M. Jimbo
[
Jim85
]
and V. Drinfeld
[
Dri87
]
, is by now an extensive framework with ramifications in many different areas of mathematics. The main object is a Hopf algebra
U_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
, depending on a parameter
q
(either formal or scalar) and a semisimple (complex) Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
, with
U_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
deforming the classical universal enveloping algebra
U(\mathfrak{g})
of
\mathfrak{g}
. Suitably interpreted, one can say that
U(\mathfrak{g})
arises in the limit as
q\rightarrow 1
.

One can easily make sense of a
compact
form of
U_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
. This entails providing
U_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
with the structure of a Hopf
*
-algebra
, where
*
is a particular anti-linear, anti-multiplicative and comultiplicative map on
U_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
. Classically, when
q=1
, this
*
-structure will restrict to an anti-multiplicative, anti-linear map
\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}
leading to the compact form

\mathfrak{u}=\{X\in\mathfrak{g}\mid X^{*}=-X\}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}.

The representation theory of
U_{q}(\mathfrak{u})
, meaning
*
-representations on (finite-dimensional) Hilbert spaces, can then be directly compared to the one of
\mathfrak{u}
. For example, in both cases the irreducible representations can be naturally parametrized by (the same set of) highest weights. Through duality, it can also be connected directly to the operator algebraic framework of
compact quantum groups

[
Wor87
,
LS91
,
DK94
]
.

The quantisation of other real forms of
\mathfrak{g}
has known a much slower progress. Although there is a direct approach through the consideration of appropriate Hopf algebra automorphisms of
U_{q}(\mathfrak{g})

[
Twi92
]
, the ensuing representation theory of these quantized real forms of
\mathfrak{g}
, now necessarily on infinite-dimensional Hilbert spaces, has met with many stumbling blocks and analytical difficulties, see e.g.
[
Wor91
,
Kor94
,
Wor00
,
KK03
]
. This has made it very difficult to build a satisfying theory in arbitrary rank.

In this paper, we will consider a novel approach towards the quantisation problem for real semisimple Lie groups. This method requires more preparations on the algebraic side, but has as an immediate payoff that there are no longer any analytic issues at stake when considering the associated representation theory. The construction is built on two fundamental principles:

•

The maximal compact Lie subalgebra
\mathfrak{k}
of our real form
\mathfrak{l}
of
\mathfrak{g}
should be given by a
symmetric pair coideal subalgebra

U_{q}(\mathfrak{k})
of
U_{q}(\mathfrak{u})

[
Let99
]
.

•

The associated quantized enveloping algebra
U_{q}(\mathfrak{l})
should be obtained from
U_{q}(\mathfrak{k})
through a generalisation of the
Drinfeld double construction
to coideal subalgebras (see Section
3
).

This strategy was already explored in
[
DCDz24
]
, where the specific case of
\mathfrak{l}=\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{R})
was considered, and in
[
DCDz21
]
, where in more generality the concept of Drinfeld double coideals was developed in an operator algebraic setting.

Here we take the opportunity to specifically set up the framework and formalism to consider quantizations of arbitrary semisimple algebraic real Lie groups. Our scope is rather modest - we will simply gather the necessary ingredients from the literature to introduce and motivate our definition. Much of the remaining analysis (and hard work) will be left for future occasions.

This paper is organized as follows: in the
first section
, we introduce the algebraic framework of
Doi-Koppinen data and Doi-Koppinen modules
. In the
second section
, we endow such Doi-Koppinen data with a unitary structure, so that the resulting representation theory on (pre-)Hilbert spaces can be considered. In the
third section
, we show how Doi-Koppinen modules can be understood through a generalisation of the Drinfeld double construction. In the
fourth section
, we then explain how Doi-Koppinen data can be obtained from a given Hopf
*
-algebra
U
with a good representation theory, and a given left coideal
*
-subalgebra
I\subseteq U
. In the
fifth section
, we explain how such inclusions
I\subseteq U
arise naturally from the theory of symmetric pair coideal subalgebras as developed by G. Letzter
[
Let99
]
. In the
sixth section
, we then apply the Drinfeld double construction to these latter coideals to arrive at the quantization of (the convolution algebra of) semisimple algebraic real Lie groups.

Notation
: if
V
is a complex vector space, we denote by
\Lin_{\mathbb{C}}(V,\mathbb{C})
its linear dual. We occasionally use

V^{\circ}=\Lin_{\mathbb{C}}(V,\mathbb{C})

as a short-hand. We write a generic
\mathbb{C}
-valued bilinear pairing as

\tau:V\times W\rightarrow\mathbb{C},\qquad(v,w)\mapsto\tau(v,w).

We sometimes use this generic notation also when
V\subseteq\Lin_{\mathbb{C}}(W,\mathbb{C})
or
W\subseteq\Lin_{\mathbb{C}}(V,\mathbb{C})
. Similarly, when
V_{i}
is paired with
W_{i}
for
i\in\{1,2\}
, we write by default
\tau
for the unique bilinear pairing between
V_{1}\otimes V_{2}
and
W_{1}\otimes W_{2}
such that

\tau(v_{1}\otimes v_{2},w_{1}\otimes w_{2})=\tau(v_{1},w_{1})\tau(v_{2},w_{2}),\qquad\forall v_{i}\in V_{i},w_{i}\in W_{i}.

1.
Doi-Koppinen modules

We recall some well-known constructions in the setting of Hopf algebras, see e.g.
[
DNR01
]
.

We work over the ground field
\mathbb{C}
, although for the moment a greater generality would be allowed. If
A=(A,m_{A})
is a unital algebra, we denote its unit by
1=1_{A}
, and if
(C,\Delta_{C})
is a (co-unital) coalgebra, we denote its counit by
\varepsilon=\varepsilon_{C}
. We then use the sumless Sweedler notation for the coproduct on
C
:

\Delta_{C}(c)=c_{(1)}\otimes c_{(2)},\qquad c\in C.

If
(A,\Delta_{A})
is a Hopf algebra, we denote its antipode by
S=S_{A}
.

Modules over a unital algebra will always be assumed to be unital, and comodules over a counital coalgebra will be assumed counital. If
\delta_{M}:M\rightarrow M\otimes C
is a right
C
-comodule and
\delta_{N}:N\rightarrow C\otimes N
a left
C
-comodule, we accordingly write

\delta_{M}(m)=m_{(0)}\otimes m_{(1)},\qquad\delta_{N}(n)=n_{(-1)}\otimes n_{(0)},\qquad m\in M,n\in N.

We can construct from the pair
(M,N)
the
cotensor product
, which is the vector space

M\overset{C}{\square}N:=\{z\in M\otimes N\mid(\delta_{M}\otimes\id)z=(\id\otimes\delta_{N})z\}.

We recall that a coalgebra
C
is
cosemisimple
if and only if there exist finite-dimensional vector spaces
(V_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in\mathfrak{I}}
, indexed by some set
\mathfrak{I}
, with

C\cong\oplus_{\alpha\in\mathfrak{I}}\End_{\mathbb{C}}(V_{\alpha})^{\circ}.

(1.1)

Here we consider the direct sum coalgebra on the right, where each
\End_{\mathbb{C}}(V_{\alpha})^{\circ}
is equipped with the coalgebra structure dual to the usual algebra structure on
\End_{\mathbb{C}}(V_{\alpha})
. If we choose a basis

\{e_{i}^{\alpha}\mid 1\leq i\leq\dim(V_{\alpha})\}

of each
V_{\alpha}
, and write
e_{ij}^{\alpha}
for the associated matrix units and
\omega_{ij}^{\alpha}
for the dual basis, the coproduct can be written explicitly as

\Delta_{C}(\omega_{ij}^{\alpha})=\sum_{k=1}^{\dim(V_{\alpha})}\omega_{ik}^{\alpha}\otimes\omega_{kj}^{\alpha},\qquad\alpha\in\mathfrak{I},1\leq i,j\leq\dim(V_{\alpha}).

The following structures were considered in
[
Doi92
,
Kop95
]
. We follow the terminology of
[
Sch00
]
, although the terminology of Doi-Hopf datum/Doi-Hopf module is also common
[
CMS97
]
.

Definition 1.1
.

A (left-right)
Doi-Koppinen

datum
consists of a triple
(A,B,C)
with
A=(A,\Delta_{A})
a Hopf algebra,
B=(B,\delta_{B})
a right
A
-comodule algebra and
C=(C,\Delta_{C})
a left
A
-module coalgebra.

A
Doi-Koppinen

module

(V,\pi_{V},\delta_{V})
consists of a right
C
-comodule
V=(V,\delta_{V})
, equipped with a left
B
-module structure
V=(V,\pi_{V})
such that the following compatibility condition holds:

\delta_{V}(bv)=(\pi_{V}\otimes\id)(\delta_{B}(b))\delta_{V}(v),\qquad\forall b\in B,v\in V.

(1.2)

As usual, we drop the notation
\pi_{V}
whenever it is clear by the context.

We write the associated linear category of Doi-Koppinen modules as
{}_{B}\mathscr{M}^{C}
.

An important class of Doi-Koppinen data can be constructed as follows. Let
(A,\Delta_{A})
be a Hopf algebra, and let
C
be a quotient left
A
-module coalgebra of
A
via a map

\pi_{C}:A\twoheadrightarrow C.

If then
M,N
are resp. right and left
C
-comodules, we consider the subspaces

M^{C}=\{m\in M\mid(\id\otimes\pi_{C})\delta_{M}(m)=m\otimes\pi_{C}(1_{A})\}\subseteq M,

{}^{C}N=\{n\in N\mid(\pi_{C}\otimes\id)\delta_{N}(n)=\pi_{C}(1_{A})\otimes n\}\subseteq N.

In particular, we obtain the unital
subalgebra

B:={}^{C}A.

Here we think of
A
as the function algebra on a quantum group,
C
as the function algebra on a quantum subgroup, and
B
as the algebra of functions on the associated homogeneous space of left cosets. The triple
(A,B,C)
indeed forms a Doi-Koppinen datum, since one easily checks that

\Delta_{A}(B)\subseteq B\otimes A,

i.e.
B\subseteq A
is a
right coideal
subalgebra. So,
\delta_{B}:=(\Delta_{A})_{\mid B}
turns
B
into a right
A
-comodule algebra.

Definition 1.2
.

We call a Doi-Koppinen datum of
coideal type

(A,{}^{C}A,C,\pi_{C})
any Doi-Koppinen datum arising in the above way from a Hopf algebra
A
with left
A
-module coalgebra quotient
\pi_{C}:A\twoheadrightarrow C
.

In case of Doi-Koppinen data of coideal type, there is a tighter link between
B
and
C
, leading one to expect more structure on
{}_{B}\mathscr{M}^{C}
. This is indeed the case, as exemplified by the following theorem. The version we need is found in
[
MS99
]
, but the general principle is already in
[
Tak79
]
. We will need the category
{}_{A}^{C}\mathscr{M}^{C}
of
C
-bicomodules
(V,\delta_{l},\delta_{r})
equipped with a compatible left
A
-module structure, the compatibility being that

(av)_{(0)}\otimes(av)_{(1)}=a_{(1)}v_{(0)}\otimes a_{(2)}v_{(1)},\quad(av)_{(-1)}\otimes(av)_{(0)}=a_{(1)}v_{(-1)}\otimes a_{(2)}v_{(0)},\qquad\forall a\in A,v\in V.

The latter category naturally carries a monoidal structure through the cotensor product:

(M,N)\mapsto{}_{\bullet}^{\bullet}M\overset{C}{\square}\,{}_{\bullet}N^{\bullet}.

The bullets indicate where the relevant structure acts, e.g. the left
A
-module structure is the diagonal one.

Theorem 1.3
.

Assume that
(A,B,C)
is a Doi-Koppinen datum of coideal type, and assume that the antipode
S_{A}
of
A
is invertible. Assume moreover that
C
is cosemisimple. Then the following hold:

(1)

\Ker(\pi_{C})=AB_{+}
, where
B_{+}=\Ker((\varepsilon_{A})_{\mid B})
.

(2)

A
is faithfully flat as a left and as a right
B
-module.

(3)

There are quasi-inverse equivalences of categories

{}_{B}\mathscr{M}^{C}\underset{F}{\overset{G}{\leftrightarrows}}{}_{A}^{C}\mathscr{M}^{C},\qquad F(V)={}^{\bullet}_{\bullet}A^{\bullet}\otimes_{B}V^{\bullet},\quad G(M)={}_{\bullet}^{C}M^{\bullet}.

(1.3)

The equivalence is implemented concretely by the isomorphisms

V\rightarrow G(F(V)),\quad v\mapsto 1_{A}\otimes v,\qquad F(G(M))\rightarrow M,\quad a\otimes m\mapsto am.

A consequence of (
1.3
) is that
{}_{B}\mathscr{M}^{C}
inherits the monoidal structure of
{}^{C}_{A}\mathscr{M}^{C}
, resulting in the following monoidal structure on
{}_{B}\mathscr{M}^{C}
:

V\boxtimes W:=G(F(V)\overset{C}{\square}F(W))={}^{C}((A\otimes_{B}V)\overset{C}{\square}(A\otimes_{B}W))\cong{}_{\bullet}V\overset{C}{\square}({}_{\bullet}A^{\bullet}\otimes_{B}W^{\bullet}).

(1.4)

There seems however to be no general way to implement the resulting tensor product more directly on
{}_{B}\mathscr{M}^{C}
, say by endowing the usual vector space tensor product with an appropriate Doi-Koppinen module structure.

The following example is considered also in
[
CMS97
]
.

Example 1.4
.

Let
H
be a Hopf algebra with invertible antipode. Write
H^{\mathrm{op}}
for
H
with the opposite product, but the original coproduct. Then on the tensor product Hopf algebra
A=H\otimes H^{\mathrm{op}}
we can consider

\pi_{H}:A\rightarrow H,\quad h\otimes k\mapsto hk,

realizing
C:=H
as quotient left
A
-module coalgebra for the
A
-module structure

(h\otimes k)\cdot c:=hck,\qquad h,k,c\in A.

It is easily checked that we can identify as algebras

H\cong B={}^{H}A,\qquad h\mapsto h_{(2)}\otimes S_{H}^{-1}(h_{(1)}),

(1.5)

the resulting right
A
-comodule structure on
H
being

\delta_{B}(h)=h_{(2)}\otimes(h_{(3)}\otimes S_{H}^{-1}(h_{(1)})).

We thus obtain a Doi-Koppinen datum
(A,B,C,\pi_{H})=(A,H,H,\pi_{H})
of coideal type. A Doi-Koppinen module is then nothing but a Yetter-Drinfeld module for
H
, i.e. a right
H
-comodule
(V,\delta)
with left
H
-module structure interacting via

(hv)_{(0)}\otimes(hv)_{(1)}=h_{(2)}v_{(0)}\otimes h_{(3)}v_{(1)}S_{H}^{-1}(v_{(3)}).

Equivalence (
1.3
) is the well-known equivalence between the category of Yetter-Drinfeld modules and the category
{}^{H}_{H}\mathscr{M}^{H}_{H}={}_{H\otimes H^{\mathrm{op}}}\!\!\!{}^{H}\mathscr{M}^{H}
of tetramodules (a cosemisimplicty assumption on
H
or
A
is in this case not needed).

We also have compatibility of the Doi-Koppinen and Yetter-Drinfeld tensor product for Yetter-Drinfeld modules under the above correspondence. Indeed, by the isomorphism

{}^{\bullet}H\otimes H_{\bullet}\cong{}^{\bullet}A_{\bullet},\qquad h\otimes k\mapsto hk_{(2)}\otimes S_{H}^{-1}(k_{(1)}),\quad xy_{(1)}\otimes S_{H}(y_{(2)})\mapsfrom x\otimes y.

as left
H
-comodule and right
H
-module, it is clear that, for
V,W
Yetter-Drinfeld modules, we can identify

V\otimes W\cong{}_{\bullet}V\overset{H}{\square}(A^{\bullet}\otimes_{B}W^{\bullet}),\qquad v\otimes w\mapsto v_{(0)}\otimes((v_{(1)}\otimes 1)\otimes w),

the resulting tensor product on
V\otimes W
indeed coinciding with the Yetter-Drinfeld one:

h(v\otimes w)=h_{(1)}v\otimes h_{(2)}w,\qquad\delta(v\otimes w)=v_{(0)}\otimes w_{(0)}\otimes v_{(1)}w_{(1)}.

2.
Unitary Doi-Koppinen modules

Definition 2.1
.

A (unital)
*
-algebra
is a (unital) algebra
B
equipped with an involutive, anti-linear, anti-multiplicative map

*:B\rightarrow B,\quad b\mapsto b^{*},\qquad(ab)^{*}=b^{*}a^{*}.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
B
be a
*
-algebra. If
\mathcal{H}_{0}
is a pre-Hilbert space, a
*
-representation

\pi_{0}
of
B
on
\mathcal{H}_{0}
is a
B
-module structure
\pi_{0}
on
\mathcal{H}_{0}
such that

\langle v,\pi_{0}(b)w\rangle=\langle\pi_{0}(b^{*})v,w\rangle,\qquad\forall v,w\in\mathcal{H}_{0},\forall b\in B.

We say that the
*
-representation is
bounded
if
\pi_{0}(b)
is a bounded operator on
\mathcal{H}_{0}
for all
b\in B
.

If
(\mathcal{H}_{0},\pi_{0})
is a bounded
*
-representation, we can uniquely extend
\pi_{0}
to a
*
-representation
\pi
of
B
on the Hilbert space completion
\mathcal{H}
of
\mathcal{H}_{0}
. On the other hand, any Hilbert space
*
-representation is automatically bounded, by the uniform boundedness principle.

Definition 2.3
.

We say that a
*
-algebra
B
is
uniformly C
∗
-bounded
if any element of
B
has a uniform bound for its norm with respect to any Hilbert space
*
-representation.

We say that
B
is
strongly
uniformly C
∗
-bounded if moreover all of its pre-Hilbert space
*
-representations are bounded.

We say that
B
is
C
∗
-faithful
if the
*
-representations of
B
on Hilbert spaces separate the elements of
B
.

We say that a
*
-representation
\pi
of
B
on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
is
non-degenerate
if
\pi(B)\mathcal{H}
is dense in
\mathcal{H}
.

If
B
is unital, then non-degeneracy of a
*
-representation is equivalent with
\pi
being a unital
*
-representation, i.e.
\pi(1_{B})=\id_{\mathcal{H}}
.

We now consider a dual notion
[
ASH09
,
Chi18
]
.

Definition 2.4
.

A
{\dagger}
-structure
on a coalgebra
C
is an involutive, anti-linear, anti-comultiplicative map

{\dagger}:C\rightarrow C,\qquad c\mapsto c^{{\dagger}},\qquad\Delta_{C}(c^{{\dagger}})=c_{(2)}^{{\dagger}}\otimes c_{(1)}^{{\dagger}}.

For example, if
\mathcal{H}
is a finite-dimensional Hilbert space, then
\End_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathcal{H})^{\circ}
is a
{\dagger}
-coalgebra by

\omega^{{\dagger}}(x):=\overline{\omega(x^{*})},\qquad x\in\End_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathcal{H}),\omega\in\End_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathcal{H})^{\circ}.

(2.1)

We will call a
{\dagger}
-coalgebra of this form a
basic (or simple)
C^{{\dagger}}
-coalgebra
.

Definition 2.5
.

If
C
is a
{\dagger}
-coalgebra, a
unitary right
C
-comodule
is any pre-Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{0}
equipped with a right
C
-comodule structure satisfying the following condition:

\langle v,w_{(0)}\rangle w_{(1)}=\langle v_{(0)},w\rangle v_{(1)}^{{\dagger}},\qquad\forall v,w\in\mathcal{H}_{0}.

(2.2)

We then write this element as
U(v,w)\in C
, and refer to it as a
matrix coefficient
of
\mathcal{H}_{0}
.

We call
\mathcal{H}_{0}

locally complete
if, for any finite-dimensional subcoalgebra
D\subseteq C
, the space

\mathcal{H}_{D}=\{v\in\mathcal{H}_{0}\mid\delta(v)\in\mathcal{H}_{0}\otimes D\}

is complete, i.e. a
Hilbert
space.

We call
\mathcal{H}_{0}

locally finite
if moreover the
\mathcal{H}_{D}
above are all finite-dimensional.

In the following, we write
\Hilb^{C}
for the category of locally complete unitary right
C
-comodules, with adjointable linear maps as morphisms. It is then in fact a
*
-category
[
Chi18
, Definition 3.1]
.

Definition 2.6
.

We call
C
a
C
†
-coalgebra
if it equals its set of matrix coefficients of unitary
C
-comodules.

It is elementary to show that a
{\dagger}
-coalgebra
C
is a C
†
-coalgebra if and only if
C
is a direct sum of basic
{\dagger}
-coalgebras, i.e. there exist finite-dimensional Hilbert spaces
(\mathcal{H}_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in\mathfrak{I}}
with

C\cong\oplus_{\alpha\in\mathfrak{I}}\End_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathcal{H}_{\alpha})^{\circ}.

(2.3)

We write

\mathscr{I}=\Lin_{\mathbb{C}}(C,\mathbb{C})

for the
full linear dual
of
C
. Then
\mathscr{I}
is a unital algebra under the convolution product

\omega\chi:=(\omega\otimes\chi)\Delta_{C},

and, if
C
is a
{\dagger}
-coalgebra,
\mathscr{I}
becomes a
*
-algebra by means of (
2.1
).

Definition 2.7
.

If
C
is a
{\dagger}
-coalgebra, we define its
restricted dual
to be

\mathcal{I}=\{x\in\mathscr{I}\mid\mathscr{I}x\mathscr{I}\textrm{ is finite-dimensional}\}.

The subspace
\mathcal{I}
is an ideal inside
\mathscr{I}
, and hence typically a
non-unital

*
-subalgebra of
\mathscr{I}
. In general,
\mathcal{I}
can be quite small. If however
C
is a C
†
-coalgebra, the concrete identification (
2.3
) allows us to write

\mathscr{I}\cong\prod_{\alpha\in\mathfrak{I}}\End_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathcal{H}_{\alpha}),\qquad\mathcal{I}\cong\oplus_{\alpha\in\mathfrak{I}}\End_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathcal{H}_{\alpha}),

so in particular
\mathcal{I}
is enough to determine
\mathscr{I}
.

Recall that we use
\tau
as a generic notation for a bilinear pairing. Then when
C
is a C
†
-coalgebra, any unitary right
C
-comodule
(\mathcal{H}_{0},\delta)
leads to a
*
-representation of
\mathscr{I}
on
\mathcal{H}_{0}
via

\hat{\pi}_{0}(x)v=(\id\otimes\tau(-,x))\delta(v),\qquad x\in\mathscr{I},v\in\mathcal{H}_{0}.

(2.4)

The following proposition is elementary to prove from the fact that
\mathcal{I}
is isomorphic to a direct sum of finite-dimensional matrix
*
-algebras.

Proposition 2.8
.

Let
(C,\Delta_{C},{\dagger})
be a C
†
-coalgebra, and let
\mathcal{I}
be the
*
-algebra introduced above. We then have that
\mathcal{I}
is strongly uniformly C
∗
-bounded and C
∗
-faithful, and there is a one-to-one correspondence

(\mathcal{H}_{0},\delta)\quad\leftrightarrow\quad(\mathcal{H},\hat{\pi})

(2.5)

between locally complete unitary right
C
-comodules and non-degenerate
*
-representations of
\mathcal{I}
on Hilbert spaces.

Here
(\mathcal{H},\hat{\pi})
is the completion of
(\mathcal{H}_{0},\hat{\pi}_{0})
, and conversely given
\mathcal{H}
we define

\mathcal{H}_{0}:=\hat{\pi}(\mathcal{I})\mathcal{H}

(2.6)

together with the
C
-comodule structure dual to the restriction
\hat{\pi}_{0}
of
\hat{\pi}
to operators
\mathcal{H}_{0}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}_{0}
.

Note that Proposition
2.8
presents one small subtlety: if we denote
{}_{\mathcal{I}}\Hilb
the category of non-degenerate
\mathcal{I}
-representations on Hilbert spaces, we do
not
obtain an equivalence of
*
-categories

\Hilb^{C}\rightarrow{}_{\mathcal{I}}\Hilb,

(2.7)

since the morphism spaces of the former are larger than the ones of the latter: norms of intertwiners need
not
be uniformly bounded across the different components on the side of
\Hilb^{C}
!

We now recall the notion of CQG (= Compact Quantum Group) Hopf
*
-algebra
[
DK94
]
.

Definition 2.9
.

A
Hopf
*
-algebra
is a Hopf algebra
A
with a
*
-algebra structure preserving the coproduct:

\Delta_{A}(a^{*})=a_{(1)}^{*}\otimes a_{(2)}^{*}.

It is called a
CQG Hopf
*
-algebra
if moreover there exists an invariant state
\Phi_{A}:A\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
, so

(\id\otimes\Phi_{A})\Delta_{A}(a)=\Phi_{A}(a)1=(\Phi_{A}\otimes\id)\Delta_{A}(a),\qquad\forall a\in A,

\Phi_{A}(1_{A})=1,\qquad\Phi_{A}(a^{*}a)\geq 0,\qquad\forall a\in A.

We note that the invariant state on a CQG Hopf
*
-algebra is necessarily unique.

We can endow a Hopf
*
-algebra
A
with its canonical
{\dagger}
-structure as a coalgebra,

a^{{\dagger}}:=S_{A}(a)^{*},\qquad a\in A.

Proposition 2.10
.

The following conditions are equivalent for a Hopf
*
-algebra
A
:

•

A
is a CQG Hopf
*
-algebra.

•

The underlying
{\dagger}
-coalgebra is a C
†
-coalgebra.

•

The underlying
*
-algebra is C
∗
-faithful and uniformly C
∗
-bounded.

The
*
-algebra
A
is then automatically strongly uniformly C
∗
-bounded.

Definition 2.11
.

Let
A
be a Hopf
*
-algebra.

We call
left
A
-module
{\dagger}
-coalgebra
a left
A
-module coalgebra
C
with a
{\dagger}
-structure satisfying

(ac)^{{\dagger}}=a^{{\dagger}}c^{{\dagger}},\qquad\forall a\in A,c\in C.

We call
right
A
-comodule
*
-algebra
a right comodule algebra
B
with a
*
-algebra structure satisfying

\delta_{B}(b)=b_{(0)}^{*}\otimes b_{(1)}^{*},\qquad\forall b\in B.

We can now introduce unitary Doi-Koppinen data and their unitary modules.

Definition 2.12
.

A
unitary Doi-Koppinen datum
consists of a Doi-Koppinen datum
(A,B,C)
with
A
a Hopf
*
-algebra,
C
a left
A
-module
{\dagger}
-coalgebra, and
B
a right
A
-comodule
*
-algebra.

A
unitary Doi-Koppinen module

(\mathcal{H}_{0},\pi_{0},\delta_{0})
for
(A,B,C)
is a pre-Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{0}
with a Doi-Koppinen module structure for which its
C
-comodule is unitary and its
B
-module is a
*
-representation.

A unitary Doi-Koppinen module is called

•

locally complete
if its underlying unitary
C
-comodule is locally complete,

•

admissible
if its underlying
C
-comodule is locally finite, and

•

of
Harish-Chandra type
if its underlying
C
-comodule is locally finite and finitely generated as a
B
-module.

We call two unitary Doi-Koppinen modules
\mathcal{H}_{0},\mathcal{G}_{0}

weakly equivalent
if there exists an invertible adjointable intertwiner
\mathcal{H}_{0}\rightarrow\mathcal{G}_{0}
, and
strongly equivalent
if there exists a
unitary
intertwiner
\mathcal{H}_{0}\rightarrow\mathcal{G}_{0}
.

A locally complete unitary Doi-Koppinen module is called
irreducible
if there are no non-trivial
B
-stable locally complete unitary
C
-subcomodules.

A special class of unitary Doi-Koppinen data is obtained by looking at
quotient
left
A
-module
{\dagger}
-coalgebras
C
, i.e. the quotient map
\pi_{C}:A\twoheadrightarrow C
is
{\dagger}
-preserving. Now
B={}^{C}A
will be a unital
*
-subalgebra of
A
: If
b\in B
, then

\pi_{C}(b_{(1)}^{*})\otimes b_{(2)}^{*}=\pi_{C}(S_{A}^{-1}(b_{(1)}))^{{\dagger}}\otimes b_{(2)}^{*}=\pi_{C}(S_{A}^{-1}(b_{(2)})b_{(1)})^{{\dagger}}\otimes b_{(3)}^{*}=\pi_{C}(1_{A})\otimes b^{*},

where in the second equality we used
b_{(1)}\in B
. It follows in particular that
B
is a right
A
-comodule
*
-algebra.

Definition 2.13
.

A unitary Doi-Koppinen datum
(A,B,C,\pi_{C})
is of
coideal type
if it comes from a quotient
A
-module
{\dagger}
-coalgebra
\pi_{C}:A\twoheadrightarrow C
with
B={}^{C}A
. If moreover
A
is a CQG Hopf
*
-algebra, we say that
(A,B,C,\pi_{C})
is a CQG Doi-Koppinen datum of coideal type.

Proposition 2.14
.

If
(A,B,C,\pi_{C})
is a CQG Doi-Koppinen datum of coideal type, the following holds:

•

B
is strongly uniformly C
∗
-bounded and C
∗
-faithful.

•

C
is a C
†
-coalgebra.

Proof.

Let
b\in B
. Then
b
lies in a finite-dimensional subcomodule
V\subseteq B
. Choosing an orthonormal basis
e_{i}\in V
with respect to the inner product
\langle v,w\rangle=\Phi_{A}(v^{*}w)
, we find that
c=\sum_{i}e_{i}e_{i}^{*}
satisfies
\Delta(c)=c\otimes 1
, hence
c
is a (positive) scalar. Then in any pre-Hilbert space
*
-representation
(\mathcal{H}_{0},\pi_{0})
, we must have that each
\pi_{0}(e_{i}^{*})
is bounded, with norm
\leq c^{1/2}
. But then clearly also
\|\pi_{0}(b)\|\leq C_{b}
for some
\pi_{0}
-independent constant
C_{b}
.

The C
∗
-faithfulness of
B
is immediate since
B\subseteq A
and
A
embeds (say) in its universal C
∗
-envelope.

The fact that
C
is a
C^{{\dagger}}
-coalgebra is proven in
[
Chi18
]
.
∎

3.
Drinfeld doubles

In the following, we fix a CQG Doi-Koppinen datum
(A,B,C,\pi_{C})
of coideal type as in Definition
2.13
.

Consider the restricted and full dual

\mathcal{I}\subseteq\mathscr{I}=\Lin_{\mathbb{C}}(C,\mathbb{C}).

If
\mathcal{H}_{0}
is a unitary Doi-Koppinen module, we obtain in particular a
*
-representation
\hat{\pi}_{0}
of
\mathscr{I}
on
\mathcal{H}_{0}
. It interacts with the
*
-representation
\pi_{0}
of
B
as follows:

\hat{\pi}_{0}(x)\pi_{0}(b)v=\tau(b_{(1)}v_{(1)},x)\pi_{0}(b_{(0)})v_{(0)}=\pi_{0}(b_{(0)})\hat{\pi}_{0}(x\lhd b_{(1)})v,\qquad x\in\mathscr{I},b\in B,v\in V,

(3.1)

where

\tau(c,x\lhd a)=\tau(ac,x),\qquad x\in\mathscr{I},a\in A,c\in C.

Note that
\lhd
is simply the natural right
A
-module structure on the linear dual
\mathscr{I}
of
C
.

This leads us to make the following definition, introduced in
[
DCDz24
]
. It is a direct modification of
[
Tak80
,
CMS97
]
to the case of Hopf
*
-algebras.

Definition 3.1
.

We define
\mathscr{D}(B,\mathscr{I})
as the unital
*
-algebra generated by
B
and
\mathscr{I}
with interchange relation

xb=b_{(0)}(x\lhd b_{(1)}),\qquad x\in\mathscr{I},b\in B.

It is easily seen that we then also have

bx=(x\lhd S_{A}^{-1}(b_{(1)}))b_{(0)},\qquad x\in\mathscr{I},b\in B,

and that the following multiplication maps are bijective:

B\otimes\mathscr{I}\rightarrow\mathscr{D}(B,\mathscr{I}),\quad b\otimes x\mapsto bx,\qquad\mathscr{I}\otimes B\rightarrow\mathscr{D}(B,\mathscr{I}),\quad x\otimes b\mapsto xb.

Now using that

(xy)\lhd a=(x\lhd a_{(1)})(y\lhd a_{(2)}),\qquad x,y\in\mathscr{I},a\in A,

we see that

z(x\lhd a)y=((z\lhd S_{A}(a_{(1)}))x(y\lhd S_{A}^{-1}(a_{(3)})))\lhd a_{(2)},\qquad a\in A,x,y,z\in\mathscr{I}.

It follows that
\mathcal{I}
is stable under
\lhd A
, and so we obtain the following result.

Proposition 3.2
.

Define
\mathcal{D}(B,\mathcal{I}):=B\mathcal{I}\subseteq\mathscr{D}(B,\mathscr{I})
. Then
\mathcal{D}(B,\mathcal{I})
is a
*
-subalgebra, and moreover the following multiplication maps are bijective:

B\otimes\mathcal{I}\rightarrow\mathcal{D}(B,\mathcal{I}),\quad b\otimes x\mapsto bx,\qquad\mathcal{I}\otimes B\rightarrow\mathcal{D}(B,\mathcal{I}),\quad x\otimes b\mapsto xb.

The following theorem states that
\mathcal{D}(B,\mathcal{I})
governs the theory of Doi-Koppinen modules.

Theorem 3.3
.

If
\mathcal{H}_{0}
is a unitary Doi-Koppinen module, then the completion
\mathcal{H}
of
\mathcal{H}_{0}
carries a non-degenerate
*
-representation of
\mathcal{D}(B,\mathcal{I})
by

\pi_{\mathcal{D}}(bx):=\pi(b)\hat{\pi}(x),\qquad b\in B,x\in\mathcal{I}.

(3.2)

Conversely, if
(\mathcal{H},\pi_{\mathcal{D}})
is a non-degenerate
*
-representation of
\mathcal{D}(B,\mathcal{I})
, then
\mathcal{H}_{0}:=\pi_{\mathcal{D}}(\mathcal{I})\mathcal{H}
carries a unique structure of unitary Doi-Koppinen module such that (
3.2
) holds.

The above sets up a one-to-one correspondence between locally complete unitary Doi-Koppinen modules and non-degenerate
*
-representations of
\mathcal{D}(B,\mathcal{I})
.

Proof.

We already know from (
2.8
) that this theorem holds on the level of
\mathcal{I}
-representations. By Proposition
2.14
, we also know that indeed a
*
-representation
\pi_{0}
of
B
on a pre-Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{0}
completes to a bounded
*
-representation on
\mathcal{H}
.

If then
\mathcal{H}_{0}
is a unitary Doi-Koppinen module, it follows directly from (
3.1
) that (
3.2
) is a well-defined
*
-representation of
\mathcal{D}(B,\mathcal{I})
on
\mathcal{H}
. It is clearly non-degenerate, since
\hat{\pi}_{0}(\mathcal{I})\mathcal{H}_{0}=\mathcal{H}_{0}
.

Conversely, if
\mathcal{H}
is a a non-degenerate
*
-representation, it is only left to verify that
\mathcal{H}_{0}=\pi_{\mathcal{D}}(\mathcal{I})\mathcal{H}
is stable under
\pi(B)
. But this is again an immediate consequence of (
3.1
).
∎

In general, we again as in (
2.7
) have that

{}_{B}\Hilb^{C}\rightarrow{}_{\mathcal{D}(B,\mathcal{I})}\Hilb

(3.3)

is not an equivalence of
*
-categories. However, if we restrict the left hand side to the category
{}_{B}\mathscr{H}\mathscr{C}^{C}
of
unitary Harish-Chandra Doi-Koppinen modules
, we do get an equivalence upon its image, which we then also refer to as (the
*
-category of) Harish-Chandra
*
-representations of
\mathcal{D}(B,\mathcal{I})
.

We similarly transport the notion of admissibility from Definition
2.12
to the setting of
*
-representations of
\mathcal{D}(B,\mathcal{I})
. As to the notion of irreducibility, we have the following lemma. Its proof is immediate by the fact that (
3.3
) respects and reflects isometric inclusions.

Lemma 3.4
.

A locally complete unitary Doi-Koppinen module
\mathcal{H}_{0}
is irreducible if and only if
\mathcal{H}
is irreducible as a
\mathcal{D}(B,\mathcal{I})
-representation.

Proposition 3.5
.

There exists a universal C
∗
-envelope
C^{*}(\mathcal{D}(B,\mathcal{I}))
of
\mathcal{D}(B,\mathcal{I})
, into which
\mathcal{D}(B,\mathcal{I})
embeds.

Proof.

It is sufficient to prove that
\mathcal{D}(B,\mathcal{I})
is uniformally C
∗
-bounded and C
∗
-separated.

The uniform C
∗
-boundedness of
\mathcal{D}(B,\mathcal{I})
is immediate, since both
B
and
\mathcal{I}
are (strongly) uniformly C
∗
-bounded.

To see that
\mathcal{D}(B,\mathcal{I})
is C
∗
-separated, we check that
\mathcal{D}(B,\mathcal{I})
has a faithful
*
-representation on the pre-Hilbert space
A
with inner product

\langle a,b\rangle=\Phi_{A}(a^{*}b),\qquad a,b\in A.

Indeed, consider on
A
the unitary Doi-Koppinen module structure

\pi_{A}(b)a=ba,\qquad\delta_{A}(a)=(\id\otimes\pi_{C})\Delta_{A}(a),\qquad a\in A,b\in B.

To verify that the associated
*
-representation
\pi_{\mathcal{D}}
of
\mathcal{D}(B,\mathcal{I})
is faithful, consider finitely many
b_{i}\in B
and
\omega_{i}\in\mathcal{I}
with

\sum_{i}b_{i}(\id\otimes\omega_{i})\delta_{A}(a)=0,\qquad\forall a\in A,

(3.4)

Then since
\Delta_{A}(A)(A\otimes 1)=A\otimes A
, multiplying (
3.4
) on the right with an arbitrary
a^{\prime}\in A
shows that

\sum_{i}b_{i}a\omega_{i}(c)=0,\qquad\forall a\in A,c\in C,

hence
\sum_{i}b_{i}\otimes\omega_{i}=0
. This implies the faithfulness of
\pi_{\mathcal{D}}
.
∎

Theorem 3.6
.

Assume every irreducible
\mathcal{D}(B,\mathcal{I})
-representation is admissible. Then
C^{*}(\mathcal{D}(B,\mathcal{I}))
is type
I
.

Proof.

It is sufficient to prove that every irreducible
*
-representation
\pi
of
C^{*}(\mathcal{D}(B,\mathcal{I}))
contains a compact operator (which may be taken as the definition of being type
I
). But by admissibility, any element
x\in\mathcal{I}
with
\pi(x)\mathcal{H}\neq\{0\}
will give a non-zero finite rank operator
\pi(x)\in\pi(\mathcal{D}(B,\mathcal{I}))
.
∎

We do not comment here on the monoidal structure that
{}_{\mathcal{D}(B,\mathcal{I})}\Hilb
possesses. Roughly speaking, it is obtained by upgrading the equivalence of Theorem
1.3
to the analytic level. See
[
DCDz21
, Section 2.2]
for more details.

4.
A general construction method

A common way to construct CQG Hopf
*
-algebras is as follows (on the abstract level, this is really just an instance of the Tannaka-Krein duality
[
Wor88
]
). We first introduce the following notion.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
U
be a Hopf
*
-algebra. A finite-dimensional unital
*
-representation
\hat{\pi}:U\rightarrow B(\mathcal{H}_{\hat{\pi}})
is called
S_{U}^{2}
-compatible
if there exists an invertible positive
T_{\hat{\pi}}\in B(\mathcal{H}_{\hat{\pi}})
such that

\hat{\pi}(S^{2}_{U}(x))=T_{\hat{\pi}}\hat{\pi}(x)T_{\hat{\pi}}^{-1},\qquad\forall x\in U.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
U
be a Hopf
*
-algebra. Let
\mathscr{F}=\{\hat{\pi}\}
be a collection of
S_{U}^{2}
-compatible finite-dimensional unital
*
-representations of
U
. Then the unital
*
-algebra
A=A_{\mathscr{F}}\subseteq\Lin_{\mathbb{C}}(U,\mathbb{C})
generated by the matrix coefficients

U_{\hat{\pi}}(\xi,\eta)\in\Lin(U,\mathbb{C}),\quad x\mapsto\langle\xi,\hat{\pi}(x)\eta\rangle

is a CQG Hopf
*
-algebra for the convolution
*
-algebra structure

\tau(ab,x)=\tau(a\otimes b,\Delta_{U}(x)),\qquad\tau(a^{*},x)=\overline{\tau(a,S_{U}(x)^{*})},\qquad a,b\in A,x\in U,

and with the coproduct uniquely determined by

\tau(\Delta_{A}(U_{\hat{\pi}}(\xi,\eta)),x\otimes y)=\langle\xi,\hat{\pi}(xy)\eta\rangle,\qquad\xi,\eta\in\mathcal{H}_{\hat{\pi}}.

Definition 4.3
.

Under the assumptions of Proposition
4.2
, we call a finite-dimensional unital
*
-representation
\hat{\pi}
of
U

of
\mathscr{F}
-type
if
U_{\hat{\pi}}(\xi,\eta)\in A
for each
\xi,\eta\in\mathcal{H}_{\hat{\pi}}
. More generally, we call a
*
-representation
\hat{\pi}_{0}
of
U
on a pre-Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{0}

of
\mathscr{F}
-type
if

•

\mathcal{H}_{\xi}:=\hat{\pi}_{0}(U)\xi
is finite-dimensional for each
\xi\in\mathcal{H}_{0}
, and

•

the restriction of
\hat{\pi}_{0}
to each
\mathcal{H}_{\xi}
is of
\mathscr{F}
-type.

We call
A
the Hopf
*
-algebra of
\mathscr{F}
-type matrix coefficients.

There is then a one-to-one correspondence between
\mathscr{F}
-type
*
-representations
(\mathcal{H}_{0},\hat{\pi}_{0})
of
U
and unitary
A
-comodules
(\mathcal{H}_{0},\delta_{0})
, the correspondence being that

\hat{\pi}_{0}(x)\xi=(\id\otimes\tau(-,x))\delta_{0}(\xi),\qquad x\in U,\xi\in\mathcal{H}_{0}.

(4.1)

Alternatively, if we denote
\mathscr{U}
the full dual of
A
, then we obtain a unital
*
-algebra homomorphism

U\rightarrow\mathscr{U},\quad x\mapsto\tau(-,x)\in\Lin_{\mathbb{C}}(A,\mathbb{C}),

(4.2)

and it is easily seen that (
4.1
) is just the factorisation through this
*
-homomorphism of the
\mathscr{U}
-representation determined by (
2.4
).

The above set-up also gives a convenient way to construct unitary Doi-Koppinen data of coideal type
[
MS99
,
Chi18
]
.

Proposition 4.4
.

Assume that
U
is a Hopf
*
-algebra, and assume
A=A_{\mathscr{F}}
is a CQG Hopf
*
-algebra as above. Assume that
I
is a left coideal
*
-subalgebra of
U
. Then the coimage
\pi_{C}:A\twoheadrightarrow C
of

A\rightarrow\Lin_{\mathbb{C}}(I,\mathbb{C}),\qquad a\mapsto\tau(a,-)_{\mid I}

(4.3)

defines a left
A
-module quotient
{\dagger}
-coalgebra
(C,\pi_{C})
of
A
. Moreover, if
B={}^{C}A
, then

B=\{b\in A\mid\forall x\in I:\tau(b_{(1)},x)b_{(2)}=\varepsilon_{U}(x)b\}.

(4.4)

We can also lift the construction of the Drinfeld double to this setting.

Definition 4.5
.

Assume the set-up of Proposition
4.4
. We define
D(B,I)
to be the universal
*
-algebra generated by copies of
B,I
with interchange relations

xb=\tau(b_{(1)},x_{(-1)})b_{(0)}x_{(0)},\qquad x\in I,b\in B.

We then have again bijectivity of the multiplication maps

B\otimes I\cong D(B,I),\qquad I\otimes B\cong D(B,I).

Definition 4.6
.

A
*
-representation
\hat{\pi}_{0}
of
I
on a pre-Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{0}
is of
\mathscr{F}
-type if

•

\mathcal{H}_{\xi}:=\hat{\pi}_{0}(I)\xi
is finite-dimensional for each
\xi\in\mathcal{H}_{0}
, and

•

Each resulting restriction
\hat{\pi}_{0,\xi}:I\rightarrow B(\mathcal{H}_{\xi})
is isomorphic to an
I
-subrepresentation of an
\mathscr{F}
-type
*
-representation
(\mathcal{G}_{0},\hat{\theta}_{0})
of
U
:

\mathcal{H}_{\xi}\subseteq\mathcal{G}_{0},\qquad\hat{\pi}_{0,\xi}(x)=\hat{\theta}_{0}(x),\qquad\forall x\in I.

We call a
*
-representation of
D(B,I)
on a pre-Hilbert space of
\mathscr{F}
-type if the underlying
I
-representation is of
\mathscr{F}
-type.

Theorem 4.7
.

Assume
U
is a Hopf
*
-algebra, and
\mathscr{F}=\{\hat{\pi}\}
a collection of
S_{U}^{2}
-compatible finite-dimensional unital
*
-representations of
U
. Assume
I
is a left coideal
*
-subalgebra of
U
, and let
(A_{\mathscr{F}},B,C,\pi_{C})
be the associated unitary Doi-Koppinen datum of coideal type as above.

Then there is a one-to-one correspondence between

•

\mathscr{F}
-type
*
-representations of
D(B,I)
, and

•

unitary Doi-Koppinen modules for
(A_{\mathscr{F}},B,C,\pi_{C})
.

Proof.

Let us first note that there is a one-to-one correspondence between
\mathscr{F}
-type
*
-representations
(\mathcal{H}_{0},\hat{\pi}_{0})
of
I
and unitary right
C
-comodules
(\mathcal{H}_{0},\delta_{0})
. Indeed, starting from the latter, we obtain by definition of
C
a well-defined
*
-representation of
I
through

\hat{\pi}_{0}(x)\xi=(\id\otimes\tau(-,x))\delta_{0}(\xi),\qquad x\in I,\xi\in\mathcal{H}_{0}.

(4.5)

Then clearly each
\mathcal{H}_{\xi}=\hat{\pi}_{0}(I)\xi
is finite-dimensional.

To see that
\hat{\pi}_{0}
is of
\mathscr{F}
-type, it is enough to consider the case where
(\mathcal{H}_{0},\delta_{0})
is finite-dimensional and irreducible. Then by virtue of
C
being a quotient coalgebra of
A
, we can find a finite-dimensional
\mathscr{F}
-type
*
-representation
\hat{\rho}
of
U
such that

\{(\xi^{*}\otimes\id)\delta_{0}(\eta)\mid\xi,\eta\in V\}\subseteq\{\pi_{C}(U_{\hat{\rho}}(\xi,\eta))\mid\xi,\eta\in\mathcal{H}_{\hat{\rho}}\}.

But this means that
\hat{\pi}_{0}
factors throught
\hat{\rho}(I)
, which by irreducibility of
\hat{\pi}_{0}
is the same as
\hat{\pi}_{0}
being a
*
-subrepresentation of
\hat{\rho}_{\mid I}
.

Conversely, we note that if
\hat{\pi}_{0}
is an
\mathscr{F}
-type
*
-representation of
I
, we get by definition that there exists for each
\xi\in\mathcal{H}_{0}
a unitary right
C
-comodule structure
\delta_{\xi}:\mathcal{H}_{\xi}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}_{\xi}\otimes C
such that

\hat{\pi}_{0}(x)\hat{\pi}_{0}(y)\xi=(\id\otimes\tau(-,x))\delta_{\xi}(\hat{\pi}_{0}(y)\xi),\qquad\forall x,y\in I.

However, by definition of
C
this comodule structure is then uniquely determined by this condition. It is then straightforward to conclude that

\delta_{0}:\mathcal{H}_{0}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}_{0}\otimes C,\quad\xi\mapsto\delta_{\xi}(\xi)

is a well-defined unitary
C
-comodule structure, related to
\hat{\pi}_{0}
via (
4.5
).

The theorem is now easily concluded by noticing that if
(\mathcal{H}_{0},\hat{\pi}_{0})
is an
\mathscr{F}
-type
*
-representation of
I
with associated
C
-comodule
\delta_{0}:\mathcal{H}_{0}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}_{0}\otimes C
, and
\pi_{0}:B\rightarrow\End(\mathcal{H}_{0})
is a unital
*
-representation, then

\hat{\pi}_{0}(x)\pi_{0}(b)\xi=\tau(b_{(1)},x_{(-1)})\pi_{0}(b_{(0)})\hat{\pi}_{0}(x_{(0)})\xi\textrm{ for all }x\in I,b\in B,\xi\in\mathcal{H}_{0}\\
\iff\quad(b\xi)_{(0)}\otimes(b\xi)_{(1)}=b_{(0)}\xi_{(0)}\otimes b_{(1)}\xi_{(1)}\textrm{ for all }b\in B,\xi\in\mathcal{H}_{0}.

∎

Let us now say that a
D(B,I)
-representation of
\mathscr{F}
-type is
locally complete
if its associated unitary Doi-Koppinen module is locally complete. Then by combining Theorem
4.7
with Theorem
3.3
, we obtain the following corollary.

Corollary 4.8
.

There is a one-to-one correspondence between locally complete
\mathscr{F}
-type
*
-representations of
D(B,I)
, and non-degenerate
*
-representations of
\mathcal{D}(B,\mathcal{I})
.

Similarly, let us say that an
\mathscr{F}
-type
*
-representation of
D(B,I)
is
admissible
if each of its irreducible
I
-subrepresentations has finite multiplicity. Then we have the following compatibility between a priori different notions of irreducibility.

Proposition 4.9
.

Assume that
(\mathcal{H}_{0},\theta_{0})
is an admissible
\mathscr{F}
-type
D(B,I)
-representation. Then the associated
\mathcal{D}(B,\mathcal{I})
-representation
(\mathcal{H},\theta)
is irreducible if and only if

L_{F}
to be

C^{*}_{q}(L_{F})=C^{*}(\mathcal{U}_{q}(L_{F})).

(6.3)

This requires a word of explanation. Recall that if consider
\mathfrak{g}
as a real Lie algebra and put

\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{u}\oplus\mathfrak{a}\oplus\mathfrak{n}

for the associated
Iwasawa decomposition
, then
(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{u},\mathfrak{a}\oplus\mathfrak{n})
has the structure of a
Manin triple
(see e.g.
[
CP95
]
). We can then view the associated Iwasawa decomposition

G_{F}=U_{F}A_{F}N_{F}

of
G_{F}
as a particular integrated version
(G_{F},U_{F},A_{F}N_{F})
of this Manin triple. By Drinfeld duality
[
Dri87
,
ES98
]
, this means that we should interpret
\mathcal{O}_{q}(U_{F})
as a quantized enveloping algebra of
\mathfrak{a}\oplus\mathfrak{n}
, or more accurately as a quantized convolution
*
-algebra
\mathcal{U}_{q}(A_{F}N_{F})
of
A_{F}N_{F}
(meaning a quantization of the convolution
*
-algebra of compact support functions on
A_{F}N_{F}
).

Now as
\mathfrak{l}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}
was chosen to be in Satake form, it follows that the Iwasawa decomposition of
\mathfrak{l}
is obtained immediately from the one for
\mathfrak{g}
:

\mathfrak{l}=\mathfrak{k}\oplus\mathfrak{a}^{0}\oplus\mathfrak{n}^{0},\quad\mathfrak{k}=\mathfrak{u}\cap\mathfrak{l},\quad\mathfrak{a}^{0}=\mathfrak{a}\cap\mathfrak{l},\quad\mathfrak{n}^{0}=\mathfrak{n}\cap\mathfrak{l}.

If we then view the associated integrated Iwasawa decomposition

L_{F}=K_{F}A_{F}^{0}N_{F}^{0},

another instance of Drinfeld duality, this time for coisotropic Lie subalgebras
[
Dri93
,
CG06
]
, allows us to view

\mathcal{O}_{q}(K_{L}\backslash G_{L})=\mathcal{U}_{q}(A_{F}^{0}N_{F}^{0}).

It is now clear that (
6.1
) and (
6.2
) may be seen more precisely as quantizations of
U(\mathfrak{l})
, resp.
\mathcal{U}(L_{F})
, together with their Iwasawa decomposition. Here
\mathcal{U}(L_{F})
is to be seen as those functions in the convolution
*
-algebra of compact support functions on
L_{F}
that generate a finite-dimensional subspace when translated from the left (or right) with
U_{F}
.

The precise way in which
\mathcal{U}_{q}(L_{F})
gives rise to
\mathcal{U}(L_{F})
in the classical limit will not be dealt with here.

We believe the above setup to be the appropriate one for the quantisation of (linear, algebraic) semisimple real Lie groups. For example, when viewing the constructions of Definition
2.12
for the triple

(\mathcal{O}_{q}(U_{F}),\mathcal{O}_{q}(K_{F}\backslash U_{F}),\mathcal{O}_{q}(K_{F})),

one immediately gets the appropriate notions for the corresponding ones in the classical setting of semisimple real Lie groups. We also stress that, although
U_{q}(\mathfrak{l})
or
\mathcal{U}_{q}(L_{F})
do not have any Hopf algebra structure themselves (for example
U_{q}(\mathfrak{l})
is only a left coideal inside the Drinfeld double
\mathcal{D}(U_{q}(\mathfrak{u}),\mathcal{O}_{q}(U_{P}))
), there is nevertheless a monoidal structure on the representation category

\Rep_{q}(L_{F}):=\Rep_{*}(C_{q}^{*}(L_{F})),

as elaborated on in
[
DCDz21
, Section 2.2]
.

At the moment, there are still some natural extensions of the classical theory to be considered:

•

A theory of induction from quantum parabolic subalgebras needs to be developed. Here there are two questions to address:

–

What are the appropriate general quantum parabolic subalgebras to consider in the case of
U_{q}(\mathfrak{l})
?

–

How to correctly implement the associated induction of
*
-representations on the operator algebraic level? This question can already be addressed directly for the quantum parabolic subalgebra
\mathcal{O}_{q}(K_{L}\backslash U_{L})
(i.e. the construction of
principal series
representations).

•

Through the formalism of
[
DCDz21
]
, a natural weight should become available on
C^{*}_{q}(L_{F})
. It should then be feasible to obtain an associated Plancherel formula for
C^{*}_{q}(L_{F})
. The case of
L_{F}=SL(2,\mathbb{R})
is currently being examined by the author and J. Dzokou Talla.

•

A classification, or at least a construction of large classes of irreducible representations of the
C^{*}_{q}(L)
should be feasible through either classical techniques, or by constructions accessible only in the quantum realm. For
L_{F}=SL(2,\mathbb{R})
, a full classification was achieved in
[
DCDz24
]
.

One question that should be feasible to answer quite directly, is whether any irreducible
*
-representation of
\mathcal{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{l})
is admissible. Unfortunately, a resolution of this problem could not be obtained in time for the submission of this article.

Note that for the diagonal inclusion considered in Example
5.9
, we find through Example
4.10
that the above considerations become the ordinary ones for the usual Drinfeld double of
U_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
and
\mathcal{O}_{q}(U_{P})
. In this case, the resulting
*
-algebra
U_{q}(\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{R}})
will quantize the complex Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
as a real Lie algebra, and
will
be equipped with a coproduct (as a Drinfeld double of Hopf
*
-algebras). In this case, the above questions have been (mostly) fully answered through the works of
[
Ar17
,
Ar18
,
VY20
,
VY23
]
.

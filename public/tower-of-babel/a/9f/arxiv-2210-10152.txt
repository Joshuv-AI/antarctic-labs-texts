1.
Introduction

Let
p
be an odd prime number and
n>1
be an integer. The inverse Galois problem posits that Galois extensions of
\mathbb{Q}
(and other fields of interest) may be constructed so as to realize any finite group. Similarly, it is of natural interest for one to ponder over the existence of Galois representations with large image in a group of the form
\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
, where
\mathcal{G}
is a smooth reductive group-scheme over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
. We consider the simplest case of natural interest, i.e., that of
\operatorname{GL}_{n}
. The study of Galois representations is of central importance in number theory. The case when
n=2
is special, since in this case, such representations abundantly arise from elliptic curves and modular forms. However, for larger values of
n
, it is natural to consider higher dimensional motives. For
n>2
, it proves difficult to construct geometric Galois representations whose image contains an open subgroup of
\operatorname{SL}_{n}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
. For instance, a Galois representation that arises from the
p
-primary torsion in an abelian variety defined over
\mathbb{Q}
, must respect the Weil pairing, and this restricts the image.

Instead of relying on the study of motives and their associated Galois representations, we resort to purely Galois theoretic methods, and our constructions are based in Galois deformation theory. One hopes to construct large Galois representations with special ramification properties that do not necessarily arise from automorphic forms or motives in algebraic geometry. Let
E\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\mathbb{Q}(\mu_{p})
be the cyclotomic field extension of
\mathbb{Q}
generated by the
p
-th roots of unity and
\widetilde{E}
the maximal pro-
p
extension of
E
which is unramified at all finite primes
v
of
E
such that
v\nmid p
. A result of Shafarevich shows that when
p
is a regular prime (i.e.,
p
does not divide the class number of
E
), the Galois group
\operatorname{Gal}(\widetilde{E}/E)
is a free pro-
p
group with
\left(\frac{p+1}{2}\right)
-generators. R. Greenberg uses this result to construct Galois representations
\rho\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{Gal}(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})\rightarrow\operatorname{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
that are unramified at all finite primes outside
\{p\}
, whenever
p\geq 4\lfloor n/2\rfloor+1
is a regular prime (cf.
[
Gre16
, Proposition 1.1]
). N. Katz
[
Kat20
]
adopts a different approach, and constructs finitely ramified Galois representations with large image in
\operatorname{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
for
p\equiv 1\mod{3}
and
p\equiv 1\mod{4}
and all
n\geq 6
. Such Galois representations are defined over certain cyclotomic fields
\mathbb{Q}(\mu_{N})
and are constructed via geometric techniques and a beautiful application of the Hilbert irreducibility theorem. Galois representations that are finitely ramified and with big image in more general reductive groups are constructed by S. Tang (cf.
[
Tan
]
). The above mentioned Galois representations are typically ramified at a large number of primes, especially when the dimension of the group
\mathcal{G}
is large. Returning to the original result of Greenberg, consider the following refined question
[
Ray21
, Question 1.1]
.

Question 1.1
.

Let
p
be any prime number and
n>1
be an integer. Does there exist a continuous Galois representation
\rho\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{Gal}(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})\rightarrow\operatorname{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
with suitably large image? If so, can one control the set of primes at which it may ramify? In particular, can such Galois representations be constructed so as to be unramified at all primes
\ell\notin\{p,\infty\}
?

By
having suitably large image
, we mean that the image contains an open subgroup of
\operatorname{SL}_{n}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
. There are parallels between the above question certain refinements of the inverse Galois problem. For instance, Abhyankar’s conjecture posits the existence of finite Galois extensions of function fields in positive characteristic, with prescribed Galois group and a predetermined set of ramification subject to certain constraints. Such Galois extensions were constructed via the
patching method
by D. Harbater (cf.
[
Har94
]
). We also refer to
[
HOPS18
]
and references therein for further details. It does lead us to wonder if there are indeed constraints on the number of primes that may ramify in Galois representations to
\operatorname{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
with suitably large image.

There has been some recent interest Question
1.1
. Let
\mathbb{Q}_{\{p\}}
be the maximal algebraic extension of
\mathbb{Q}
which is unramified outside
\{p,\infty\}
, and set
\operatorname{G}_{\{p\}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\operatorname{Gal}\left(\mathbb{Q}_{\{p\}}/\mathbb{Q}\right)
. Let
\bar{\chi}
denote the mod-
p
cyclotomic character. We shall let
\mathcal{C}
denote the mod-

. Recall that

and irregular otherwise. Note that
\mathcal{C}
is a module over
\operatorname{Gal}\left(\mathbb{Q}(\mu_{p})/\mathbb{Q}\right)
, and therefore over

. As a Galois module,

decomposes into eigenspaces
\mathcal{C}=\bigoplus_{i=0}^{p-2}\mathcal{C}(\bar{\chi}^{i})
,

is

. The

is the number of integers

In

, the author shows that for

, there are infinitely many Galois representations

whose image contains

1.1.
Statement of the main result

We now state the main result, which entirely resolves Question
1.1
for all
n>1
and all primes
p\geq 7
. The case
n=1
is an easy exercise. When
p\equiv 1\mod{4}
, we need to assume a weak form of Vandiver’s conjecture, which we briefly explain.

Assumption

(Assumption
2.2
)
.

There is an odd positive integer
k
that lies in the range
3\leq k\leq\left(\frac{p-1}{2}\right)
such that
\mathcal{C}(\bar{\chi}^{1+k})=\mathcal{C}(\bar{\chi}^{p-k})=0
.

The above assumption is clearly a consequence of Vandiver’s conjecture, which predicts that all even eigenspaces of
\mathcal{C}
must vanish. In fact, it is easy to see that if the number of non-zero even eigenspaces of
\mathcal{C}
is strictly less than
\lfloor\frac{p-2}{4}\rfloor
, then the above assumption is automatically satisfied (cf. Proposition
2.1
). Also, for primes
p\equiv 3\mod{4}
such that
p\geq 7
, the above assumption is automatically satisfied (cf. Remark
2.3
).

Theorem

(Theorem
5.5
)
.

Let
p\geq 7
be a prime and
n>1
be a natural number. Suppose that the above assumption holds. Then, there exist infinitely many Galois representations
\varrho\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{Gal}(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})\rightarrow\operatorname{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
which are unramified outside
\{p,\infty\}
whose image contains
\mathcal{U}_{t}
, where
t\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=8(n^{2}-n)\left(3+\lfloor\operatorname{log}_{p}(2^{n}+1)\rfloor\right)+8
.

1.2.
Method of proof

Let us briefly summarize the construction. We begin with a suitable choice of a residual represenation

\bar{\rho}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{G}_{\{p\}}\rightarrow\operatorname{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})

which is carefully lifted to a characteristic zero representation via a step by step process. This residual representation is a
diagonal representation
, i.e., a direct sum of
1
-dimensional characters, defined in section
2.1
. Set
M\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=(n^{2}-n)\left(3+\lfloor\operatorname{log}_{p}(2^{n}+1)\rfloor\right)+1
and
N\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=2M
. A diagonal representation

\rho_{\beta}=\left({\begin{array}[]{ccccc}\beta_{1}&&&&\\
&\beta_{2}&&&\\
&&\ddots&&\\
&&&\beta_{n-1}&\\
&&&&\beta_{n}\end{array}}\right)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{G}_{\{p\}}\rightarrow\operatorname{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{Z}/p^{N}\mathbb{Z}),

that lifts
\bar{\rho}
is carefully chosen. We set
\rho_{N}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\rho_{\beta}
and set
\rho_{M}
to denote the mod-
p^{M}
reduction of
\rho_{\beta}
. Any other lift
\rho_{N}^{\prime}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{G}_{\{p\}}\rightarrow\operatorname{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{Z}/p^{N}\mathbb{Z})
of
\rho_{M}
may be constructed by
twisting

\rho_{N}
by a suitable cohomology class taking values in the adjoint representation
\operatorname{Ad}\rho_{M}
(cf. the discussion following Definition
3.2
). We carefully construct a suitable cohomology class
f\in H^{1}(\operatorname{G}_{\{p\}},\operatorname{Ad}\rho_{M})
and then set
\rho_{N}^{\prime}
to denote the twist of
\rho_{N}
by
f
(cf. (
5.4
)). This twist has many advantageous properties. Let
L\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\mathbb{Q}(\rho_{M})
be the finite Galois extension of
\mathbb{Q}
which is fixed by the kernel of
\rho_{M}
. One of the key properties exhibited by
\rho_{N}^{\prime}
is that
\rho_{N}^{\prime}\left(\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}_{\{p\}}/L)\right)
has suitably large image in

\mathcal{A}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\operatorname{ker}\left(\operatorname{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{Z}/p^{N}\mathbb{Z})\rightarrow\operatorname{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{Z}/p^{M}\mathbb{Z})\right).

It is easy to see that
\mathcal{A}
is an abelian group and can be identified with
n\times n
-matrices with coefficients in
\mathbb{Z}/p^{M}\mathbb{Z}
. By virtue of being an abelian group,
\mathcal{A}
acquires a natural Galois action and is isomorphic to the adjoint representation
\operatorname{Ad}\rho_{M}
. Of crucial importance is that this adjoint representation decomposes into a direct sum of a trivial module arising from the diagonal torus, and a direct sum of distinct cyclic Galois-modules. It should be noted that the residual adjoint representation
\operatorname{Ad}\bar{\rho}
does not necessarily have this favorable property. The infinitesimal deformation problem associated to
\bar{\rho}
is unobstructed, i.e.,
H^{2}(\operatorname{G}_{\{p\}},\operatorname{Ad}\bar{\rho})=0
(cf. Proposition
2.6
). As a result, it follows (cf. Proposition
4.1
) that
\rho_{N}^{\prime}
exhibits infinitely many lifts to characteristic zero. The twist
\rho_{N}^{\prime}
satisfies a number of key properties, and Proposition
4.5
implies that the lifts of
\rho_{N}^{\prime}
all have image containing a large open subgroup of
\operatorname{SL}_{n}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
.

1.3.
Organization

Including the introduction, the manuscript consists of five sections. In section
2
, we introduce notation and prove a number of preliminary results. In this section, we recall the main assumption when
p\equiv 1\mod{4}
(i.e., Assumption
2.2
) and explain why it is significantly weaker than Vandiver’s conjecture. We then introduce the choice of residual representation
\bar{\rho}
, and show that it is unobstructed (cf. Proposition
2.6
). Also in this section, we discuss properties of diagonal representations and introduce the notion of
admissibility
. In section
3
, we systematically introduce the deformation theory of Galois representations, and discuss how deformations of a given Galois representation may be
twisted
by cohomology classes valued in the adjoint module. In section
4
, we prove results that allow us to state conditions for the image of a Galois representation to be suitably large in
\operatorname{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
. The main criterion proven in this section is Proposition
4.5
, which is crucially used in the proof of the main theorem. Finally, in section
5
, we give a proof of the main result of the manuscript.

k_{i}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=2^{i}p+\epsilon_{i}
, and set
\beta_{i}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\chi^{k_{i}}\mod{p^{N}}
. Then,
\beta=(\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{n})
is an
m
-admissible
N
-lift of
\alpha
.

Proof.

Note that since
p^{m-1}>p(2^{n}+1)
, it follows that
\frac{1}{2}p^{m-1}(p-1)>k_{n}=p2^{n}+\epsilon_{n}
. Since
k_{i}\neq k_{j}
, it follows that
k_{i}\not\equiv k_{j}\mod{p^{m-1}(p-1)}
. On the other hand, suppose there are two distinct pairs
(i,j)
and
(s,t)
such that
i\neq j
,
s\neq t
, and

k_{i}-k_{j}\equiv k_{s}-k_{t}\mod{p^{m-1}(p-1)}.

Then, since

\operatorname{max}\{|k_{i}-k_{j}|,|k_{s}-k_{t}|\}<\frac{1}{2}p^{m-1}(p-1),

we deduce that
k_{i}-k_{j}=k_{s}-k_{t}
. Without loss of generality, assume that
i>j,s,t
. Then, we find that
p(2^{i}-2^{j})=p(2^{s}-2^{t})+b
, where
|b|<p
. Then, we find that
2^{i}-2^{j}=2^{s}-2^{t}
and
b=0
. But then,
2^{i}=2^{j}+2^{s}-2^{t}<2\cdot 2^{i-1}=2^{i}
, a contradiction. Hence, we have proven that
\beta
is
m
-admissible.
∎

2.2.
The adjoint representation

Let
\mathfrak{g}=M_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
denote the Lie algebra of
\operatorname{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
, consisting of
n\times n
matrices with entries in
\mathbb{F}_{p}=\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}
. Denote by
\mathfrak{g}_{0}
the subspace of
\mathfrak{g}
consisting of matrices with trace equal to
0
. We give
\mathfrak{g}
the structure of a Galois module as follows. Let
v\in\mathfrak{g}
and
g\in\operatorname{G}_{\{p\}}
. Then,
g\cdot v
is defined to be
\bar{\rho}(g)v\bar{\rho}(g)^{-1}
. The action is referred to as the adjoint action. When referring to the Galois module, we simply use
\operatorname{Ad}\bar{\rho}
, and when referring to the underlying space, we use
\mathfrak{g}
. Note that
\mathfrak{g}_{0}
which consists of trace zero matrices is a submodule, which we also denote by
\operatorname{Ad}^{0}\bar{\rho}
. Set
\alpha_{i,j}
to denote
\alpha_{i}\alpha_{j}^{-1}
. Recall that
\alpha_{i}=\bar{\chi}^{\left(\frac{1+(-1)^{i}}{2}\right)k}
, and therefore, we find that

\alpha_{i,j}=\begin{cases}&1\text{ if }i\equiv j\mod{2},\\
&\nu=\bar{\chi}^{k}\text{ if i is even and j is odd},\\
&\nu^{-1}=\bar{\chi}^{-k}\text{ if i is odd and j is even.}\end{cases}

Let
\mathfrak{t}
(resp.
\mathfrak{t}_{0}
) denote the submodule of
\operatorname{Ad}\bar{\rho}
(resp.
\operatorname{Ad}^{0}\bar{\rho}
) consisting of diagonal matrices. Note that the Galois action on
\mathfrak{t}
is trivial and we have the following decompositions

\begin{split}&\operatorname{Ad}\bar{\rho}=\mathfrak{t}\oplus\left(\bigoplus_{i\neq j}\mathbb{F}_{p}(\alpha_{i,j})\right),\\
&\operatorname{Ad}^{0}\bar{\rho}=\mathfrak{t}_{0}\oplus\left(\bigoplus_{i\neq j}\mathbb{F}_{p}(\alpha_{i,j})\right).\end{split}

Given a module
M
over
\operatorname{G}_{S}
and an integer
i\geq 0
, we denote by
H^{i}(\operatorname{G}_{S},M)
the
i
-th cohomology group. Denote by
\Sh^{i}_{S}(M)
the kernel of the restriction maps

\Sh^{i}_{S}(M)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\operatorname{ker}\left\{H^{i}(\operatorname{G}_{S},M)\rightarrow\bigoplus_{\ell\in S}H^{i}(\operatorname{G}_{\ell},M)\right\}.

Given a representation
\bar{\rho}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{G}_{S}\rightarrow\operatorname{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
, we say that
\bar{\rho}
is
S
-unobstructed
if the cohomology group
H^{2}(\operatorname{G}_{S},\operatorname{Ad}\bar{\rho})
vanishes. When
\bar{\rho}
is unramified at all primes
\ell\neq p
, we shall say that
\bar{\rho}
is unobstructed to mean that it is
\{p\}
-unobstructed. When
\bar{\rho}
is the residual representation
\operatorname{diag}\left(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n}\right)
, the representation
\bar{\rho}
is always unobstructed, as the following result shows.

Proposition 2.6
.

Let
n>1
be an integer, and let
\bar{\rho}
be the Galois representation
\operatorname{diag}\left(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n}\right)
, where
\alpha_{i}
is given by (
2.1
). Then, we find that
H^{2}(\operatorname{G}_{\{p\}},\operatorname{Ad}\bar{\rho})=0
.

Proof.

Consider the exact sequence

0\rightarrow\Sh_{\{p\}}^{2}(\operatorname{Ad}\bar{\rho})\rightarrow H^{2}(\operatorname{G}_{\{p\}},\operatorname{Ad}\bar{\rho})\rightarrow H^{2}(\operatorname{G}_{p},\operatorname{Ad}\bar{\rho}).

It suffices to show that
\Sh_{\{p\}}^{2}(\operatorname{Ad}\bar{\rho})=0
and that
H^{2}(\operatorname{G}_{p},\operatorname{Ad}\bar{\rho})=0
. Note that the Galois module
\operatorname{Ad}\bar{\rho}
decomposes as follows

\operatorname{Ad}\bar{\rho}=\mathfrak{t}\oplus\left(\ \bigoplus_{i\neq j}\mathbb{F}_{p}(\alpha_{i,j})\right).

Note that the Galois action on
\mathfrak{t}
is the trivial one. Since the characters in the above decomposition all belong to
\{1,\nu,\nu^{-1}\}
, we are to show that
\Sh^{2}_{\{p\}}\left(\mathbb{F}_{p}(\psi)\right)=0
and that
H^{2}(\operatorname{G}_{\{p\}},\mathbb{F}_{p}(\psi))=0
for all characters
\psi\in\{1,\nu,\nu^{-1}\}
.

First let us show that
\Sh^{2}_{\{p\}}\left(\mathbb{F}_{p}(\psi)\right)=0
for the above mentioned characters. Note that by global duality of
\Sh
-groups
[
NSW13
]
, we have that
\Sh^{2}_{\{p\}}\left(\mathbb{F}_{p}(\psi)\right)
are
\Sh^{1}_{\{p\}}\left(\mathbb{F}_{p}(\bar{\chi}\psi^{-1})\right)
are naturally dual to one another. Therefore, we are to check to that

\Sh^{1}_{\{p\}}\left(\mathbb{F}_{p}(\bar{\chi}\psi^{-1})\right)=0

for the characters
\psi\in\{1,\nu,\nu^{-1}\}
. A straightforward application of inflation-restriction shows that
\Sh^{1}_{\{p\}}\left(\mathbb{F}_{p}(\bar{\chi}\psi^{-1})\right)=0
if
\mathcal{C}(\bar{\chi}\psi^{-1})=0
(cf.
[
Ray21
, Lemma 3.2]
). It is a well known fact that the first eigenspace
\mathcal{C}(\bar{\chi})=0
, cf.
[
Was97
, Proposition 6.16]
. On the other hand, the vanishing of
\mathcal{C}(\bar{\chi}\nu^{\pm 1})
follows from Assumption
2.2
. Therefore, we deduce that
\Sh^{2}_{\{p\}}(\operatorname{Ad}\bar{\rho})=0
.

Next, we show that
H^{2}(\operatorname{G}_{p},\operatorname{Ad}\bar{\rho})=0
. It suffices to show that
H^{2}\left(\operatorname{G}_{p},\mathbb{F}_{p}(\psi)\right)=0
for each character
\psi\in\{1,\nu,\nu^{-1}\}
. By local duality, we have that
H^{2}\left(\operatorname{G}_{p},\mathbb{F}_{p}(\psi)\right)
is dual to
H^{0}\left(\operatorname{G}_{p},\mathbb{F}_{p}(\bar{\chi}\psi^{-1})\right)
. Since
\psi_{|\operatorname{G}_{p}}
is not equal to
\bar{\chi}
, it follows that
H^{0}\left(\operatorname{G}_{p},\mathbb{F}_{p}(\bar{\chi}\psi^{-1})\right)=0
, and thus,
H^{2}\left(\operatorname{G}_{p},\mathbb{F}_{p}(\psi)\right)=0
for
\psi
as above. It follows that
H^{2}(\operatorname{G}_{p},\operatorname{Ad}\bar{\rho})=0
and it follows that
H^{2}(\operatorname{G}_{\{p\}},\operatorname{Ad}\bar{\rho})=0
.
∎

3.
Deformation theory

In this section, we study the deformation theory of diagonal representations. The results in this section shall further aid our lifting construction. For a comprehensive treatment of the subject, the reader may refer to
[
Maz97
]
.

Let
G
be a group,
R
be a commutative unital local ring, with maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}_{R}
. Recall that
M_{n}(R)
is the ring of
n\times n
-matrices with entries in
R
. Given a representation
\varrho\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}G\rightarrow\operatorname{GL}_{n}(R)
, the associated
adjoint module
denoted
\operatorname{Ad}\varrho
, is the module
M_{n}(R)
on which
g\in G
acts via
g\cdot v\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\varrho(g)v\varrho(g)^{-1}
. There is a direct sum decomposition

\operatorname{Ad}\varrho=\operatorname{Ad}^{0}\varrho\oplus\operatorname{R}\cdot\operatorname{I}_{n},

where
\operatorname{Ad}^{0}(R)
consists of matrices with trace equal to
0
and
\operatorname{I}_{n}
is the identity in
M_{n}(R)
. We note that the Galois action on the second factor in the above decomposition is trivial.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
\pi\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}S\rightarrow R
be a homomorphism of commutative unital local rings and let
I
denote the kernel of
\pi
. In this manuscript, we shall say that
\pi
is a
small-extension
if the following conditons hold

(1)

\pi
is surjective,

(2)

I^{2}=0
,

(3)

I
is principal.

Given a small extension
\pi
, let
\pi^{*}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{GL}_{n}(S)\rightarrow\operatorname{GL}_{n}(R)
be the homomorphism obtained by applying
\pi^{*}
to each entry.

The following example illustrates the above definition. Note that if
M
and
N
are integers such that
M<N
, then, the quotient map

\pi_{M,N}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathbb{Z}/p^{N}\mathbb{Z}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}/p^{M}\mathbb{Z}

is a small extension provided
N\leq 2M
and that the kernel of
\pi_{M,N}
is generated by
p^{M}
.

Let us now introduce some further notation. The ideal
I
is generated by the element
t
. Consider the map
R\rightarrow I
sending
r
to
tr
. Let
J
denote the kernel of this map and set
R^{\prime}
to denote the quotient
R/J
. Thus the map sending
r
to
tr
induces an isomorphism of
R
-modules
R^{\prime}\xrightarrow{\sim}I
. Let us introduce a little bit of notation which will be used throughout the manuscript. Given a matrix
A\in M_{n}(R^{\prime})
let
\widetilde{A}
be a lift of
A
to
M_{n}(S)
. Let

t\widetilde{A}\in\operatorname{ker}\left\{M_{n}(S)\rightarrow M_{n}(R)\right\}

be the matrix obtained by multiplying all entries of
\widetilde{A}
by
t
. Clearly, the matrix
t\widetilde{A}
is independent of the choice of lift
\widetilde{A}
, and
we denote this product by
tA
.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
\pi
be a small extension. Then, the kernel of
\pi^{*}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{GL}_{n}(S)\rightarrow\operatorname{GL}_{n}(R)
defined above is abelian and can be identified with
M_{n}(R^{\prime})
. The association takes a matrix
A\in M_{n}(R^{\prime})
to
\operatorname{I}_{n}+tA
in
\operatorname{ker}\left(\operatorname{GL}_{n}(S)\rightarrow\operatorname{GL}_{n}(R)\right)
.

Proof.

Since
t^{2}=0
, it follows that
\operatorname{I}_{n}+tA
is an invertible matrix with inverse
\operatorname{I}_{n}-tA
. Moreover, the map sending
A\mapsto\operatorname{I}_{n}+tA
is seen to be a homomorphism, due to the relation

\begin{split}&\left(\operatorname{I}_{n}+tA\right)\left(\operatorname{I}_{n}+tB\right)\\
=&\left(\operatorname{I}_{n}+t\widetilde{A}\right)\left(\operatorname{I}_{n}+t\widetilde{B}\right)\\
=&\operatorname{I}_{n}+t(\widetilde{A}+\widetilde{B})\\
=&\operatorname{I}_{n}+t(A+B).\end{split}

It is also clear that any element in the kernel of
\pi^{*}
is of the form
\operatorname{I}_{n}+tA
, where
A\in M_{n}(R^{\prime})
.
∎

Our discussion returns to the map
\pi_{M,N}
(subject to the condition that
N\leq 2M
). We set
k\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=N-M
and we find that
R^{\prime}=\mathbb{Z}/p^{k}\mathbb{Z}
. The Proposition
3.1
asserts that the kernel of the reduction map
\operatorname{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{Z}/p^{N}\mathbb{Z})\rightarrow\operatorname{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{Z}/p^{M}\mathbb{Z})
is identified with
M_{n}(\mathbb{Z}/p^{k}\mathbb{Z})
, upon associating a matrix
A\in M_{n}(\mathbb{Z}/p^{k}\mathbb{Z})
with
\operatorname{I}_{n}+p^{M}A
. For instance, say
n=2
,
M=2
,
N=4
and
k=2
. Consider the matrix
A=\left({\begin{array}[]{cc}0&1+p\\
-1&0\\
\end{array}}\right)
and let
t=p^{2}
. Then, the product
p^{2}A
is given by
\left({\begin{array}[]{cc}0&p^{2}+p^{3}\\
-p^{2}&0\\
\end{array}}\right)
, and
\operatorname{I}_{2}+p^{2}\left({\begin{array}[]{cc}0&1+p\\
-1&0\\
\end{array}}\right)
is given by
\left({\begin{array}[]{cc}1&p^{2}+p^{3}\\
-p^{2}&1\\
\end{array}}\right)\in M_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/p^{4}\mathbb{Z})
.

Let us now discuss the notion of a
deformation
. Let
\pi\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}S\rightarrow R
be any surjective map of commutative rings and
G
be a group. Let
\rho\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}G\rightarrow\operatorname{GL}_{n}(R)
be a representation of

on a free

-module of rank
n
.

of lifts.

\pi\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}S\rightarrow R
is a small extension, and that
I\simeq R^{\prime}\simeq R/J
as an

-module.

\rho\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}G\rightarrow\operatorname{GL}_{n}(R)
, let us assume that there exists a deformation
\widetilde{\rho}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}G\rightarrow\operatorname{GL}_{n}(S)
. Then, any other deformation is given by
r=(\operatorname{I}_{n}+tf)\tilde{\rho}
for a uniquely determined cohomology class
f\in H^{1}(G,\operatorname{Ad}\rho_{0})
, where
\rho_{0}
is the reduction of
\rho
modulo
J
. In greater detail, let
F
be a cocycle representing
f
, consider the representation given by the formula
(\operatorname{I}_{n}+tF)\widetilde{\rho}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}G\rightarrow\operatorname{GL}_{n}(S)
. This prescribes a well-defined lift of
\rho
. The cocyle relation for
F
implies that the following relation holds for all
h_{1},h_{2}\in G
,

\left(\operatorname{I}_{n}+tF(h_{1})\right)\tilde{\rho}(h_{1})\left(\operatorname{I}_{n}+tF(h_{2})\right)\tilde{\rho}(h_{2})=\left(\operatorname{I}_{n}+tF(h_{1}h_{2})\right)\tilde{\rho}(h_{1}h_{2}).

Thus,
(\operatorname{I}_{n}+tF)\widetilde{\rho}
defines a well defined homomorphism, and
(\operatorname{I}_{n}+tf)\widetilde{\rho}
defines a well defined deformation of
\rho
. Indeed, if
F_{1}
and
F_{2}
are two cocycles that differ by a coboundary
z=F_{2}-F_{1}
, we have that

(3.1)

(\operatorname{I}_{n}+tF_{2})\widetilde{\rho}=(\operatorname{I}_{n}+tF_{1})(\operatorname{I}_{n}+tz)\widetilde{\rho}.

By virtue of being a coboundary,

z(h)=x-hx=x-\rho_{0}(h)x\rho_{0}(h)^{-1}.

Therefore, we find that

(3.2)

Lemma 5.1
.

Suppose that

is a character with mod

\bar{\psi}

\bar{\psi}(c)=-1

(2)

(3)

Proof.

First, we prove the result when

, in which case,
\psi
is identified with its reduction
\bar{\psi}
. Since
\bar{\psi}
is odd and
H^{i}\left(\operatorname{G}_{\{p\}},\mathbb{F}_{p}(\bar{\psi})\right)=0
for

, it follows from the global Euler characteristic formula (cf.
[
NSW13
, Theorem 8.7.4]
) that
H^{1}\left(\operatorname{G}_{\{p\}},\mathbb{F}_{p}(\bar{\psi})\right)\simeq\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}
. For

, let

be the mod-

\psi

. Consider the long exact sequence in cohomology associated with the following short exact sequence of Galois modules

Thus, we have the short exact sequence of cohomology groups

(5.1)

By induction, assume that the group

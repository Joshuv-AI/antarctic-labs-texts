Threshold even solutions to the nonlinear Schrödinger equation with delta potential at high frequencies
Stephen Gustafson, Takahisa Inui

Abstract
. We consider the nonlinear Schrödinger equation with a repulsive Dirac delta potential in one dimensional Euclidean space. We classify the global dynamics of even solutions with the same action as the high-frequency ground state standing wave solutions.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
35Q55,37K40 etc.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Main result

We consider the following nonlinear Schrödinger equation with Dirac delta potential:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}i\partial_{t}u+\partial_{x}^{2}u+\gamma\delta u+|u|^{p-1}u=0,&(t,x)\in I\times\mathbb{R},\\
u(0,x)=u_{0}(x),&x\in\mathbb{R},\end{cases}

(
\delta
NLS)

where
\gamma<0
,
\delta
denotes the Dirac delta at the origin,
p>5
, and
I\ni 0
is a time interval.
Here the Schrödinger operator
-\Delta_{\gamma}=-\partial_{x}^{2}-\gamma\delta
is defined by

\displaystyle-\Delta_{\gamma}f

\displaystyle:=-\partial_{x}^{2}f\text{ for }f\in\mathcal{D}(-\Delta_{\gamma}),

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}(-\Delta_{\gamma})

\displaystyle:=\{f\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R})\cap H^{2}(\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}):f^{\prime}(0+)-f^{\prime}(0-)=-\gamma f(0)\}.

We assume here that
\gamma<0
, meaning that the potential is repulsive. In this case,
-\Delta_{\gamma}
is a nonnegative self-adjoint operator on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
(see
[
3
]
for more details), which implies that the linear Schrödinger propagator
e^{it\Delta_{\gamma}}
is well-defined on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
by Stone’s theorem. The operator
-\Delta_{\gamma}
may also be defined by its quadratic form:

\displaystyle\langle-\Delta_{\gamma}f,g\rangle:=\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(x)\overline{g(x)}dx-\gamma f(0)\overline{g(0)}

for
f,g\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
.

It is known that the equation (

δ

NLS
) is locally well-posed in the energy space
H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
. Moreover, the energy and mass

\displaystyle E_{\gamma}(f)

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{2}\|f\|_{\dot{H}_{\gamma}^{1}}^{2}-\frac{1}{p+1}\|f\|_{L^{p+1}}^{p+1},

\displaystyle M(f)

\displaystyle:=\|f\|_{L^{2}}^{2},

are conserved (see
[
15
,
14
]
), where

\|f\|_{\dot{H}_{\gamma}^{1}}^{2}:=\|\partial_{x}f\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\gamma|f(0)|^{2}=\langle-\Delta_{\gamma}f,f\rangle.

We are interested in the global behavior of the solutions. For this,
the ground state solution plays an important role, as we now explain.
For a frequency
\omega>0
, define the action and virial functionals
on
H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
:

\displaystyle S_{\omega,\gamma}(f)

\displaystyle:=E_{\gamma}(f)+\frac{\omega}{2}M(f),

\displaystyle K_{\gamma}(f)

\displaystyle:=\|\partial_{x}f\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{-\gamma}{2}|f(0)|^{2}-\frac{p-1}{2(p+1)}\|f\|_{L^{p+1}}^{p+1}.

We consider the following two minimizing problems:

\displaystyle n_{\omega,\gamma}

\displaystyle:=\inf\{S_{\omega,\gamma}(f):f\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R})\setminus\{0\},K_{\gamma}(f)=0\},

\displaystyle r_{\omega,\gamma}

\displaystyle:=\inf\{S_{\omega,\gamma}(f):f\in H_{{\mathord{\rm even}}}^{1}(\mathbb{R})\setminus\{0\},K_{\gamma}(f)=0\},

where
H_{{\mathord{\rm even}}}^{1}(\mathbb{R})
denotes the even functions in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
. We have:

Proposition 1.1

(
[
13
,
23
]
)
.

Let
\gamma<0
. The following hold.

(1)

For all
\omega>0
,
n_{\omega,\gamma}=n_{\omega,0}
and
n_{\omega,\gamma}
is not attained.

(2)

n_{\omega,\gamma}<r_{\omega,\gamma}
and

\displaystyle r_{\omega,\gamma}\begin{cases}=2n_{\omega,0}&\text{ if }0<\omega\leq\frac{\gamma^{2}}{4},\\
<2n_{\omega,0}&\text{ if }\omega>\frac{\gamma^{2}}{4}.\end{cases}

(3)

If
0<\omega\leq\gamma^{2}/4
, then
r_{\omega,\gamma}
is not attained.
While, if
\omega>\gamma^{2}/4
, then
r_{\omega,\gamma}
is attained by

\displaystyle Q_{\omega,\gamma}(x):=\left[\frac{(p+1)\omega}{2}\sech^{2}\left\{\frac{(p-1)\sqrt{\omega}}{2}|x|+\tanh^{-1}\left(\frac{\gamma}{2\sqrt{\omega}}\right)\right\}\right]^{\frac{1}{p-1}},

which is the unique (up to a complex phase) solution of

\displaystyle-\partial_{x}^{2}Q-\gamma\delta Q+\omega Q=|Q|^{p-1}Q.

(1.1)

If
\omega>\gamma^{2}/4
, the function
e^{i\omega t}Q_{\omega,\gamma}
is a non-scattering global solution to (

δ

NLS
), which is called the ground state (standing wave) solution. In the non-even setting,

n_{\omega,\gamma}=n_{\omega,0}=S_{\omega,0}(Q_{\omega,0})
.
Though
e^{i\omega t}Q_{\omega,0}
is not a solution to (

δ

NLS
), it gives a threshold for a scattering-blowup dichotomy result.
Indeed, we have the following global dynamics result below
n_{\omega,0}
:

Theorem 1.2

(Global dynamics below
Q_{\omega,0}
(Ikeda–Inui
[
23
]
))
.

Let
\omega>0
. Assume that
S_{\omega,\gamma}(u_{0})<n_{\omega,\gamma}(=n_{\omega,0})
. Then the following hold for (

δ

NLS
).

(1)

If
K_{\gamma}(u_{0})>0
, the solution is global and scatters in both time directions.

(2)

If
K_{\gamma}(u_{0})<0
, the solution blows up or grows up in both time directions.

We also have a dichotomy result in the threshold case:

Theorem 1.3

(Global dynamics on the threshold (Ardila–Inui
[
4
]
, Inui
[
25
]
))
.

Let
\omega>0
. Assume that
S_{\omega,\gamma}(u_{0})=n_{\omega,\gamma}(=n_{\omega,0})
. Then the following hold for (

δ

NLS
).

(1)

If
K_{\gamma}(u_{0})>0
, the solution is global and scatters in both time directions.

(2)

If
K_{\gamma}(u_{0})<0
and
\int_{\mathbb{R}}|xu_{0}(x)|^{2}dx<\infty
, the solution blows up in both time directions.

Remark 1.1
.

(a)

We say a solution
u
scatters in the positive time direction if there exists
u_{+}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
such that

\displaystyle\|u(t)-e^{it\Delta_{\gamma}}u_{+}\|_{H^{1}}\to 0\text{ as }t\to\infty.

By Mizutani
[
28
]
, if the above holds, then there exists
\widetilde{u}_{+}
such that

\displaystyle\|u(t)-e^{it\partial_{x}^{2}}\widetilde{u}_{+}\|_{H^{1}}\to 0\text{ as }t\to\infty.

(b)

We say a solution
u
grows up in positive time if it exists at least on
[0,\infty)
and

\limsup_{t\to\infty}\|\partial_{x}u(t)\|_{L^{2}}=\infty
.
On the threshold
S_{\omega,\gamma}(u_{0})=n_{\omega,\gamma}
, it was not known if the blow-up or grow-up result holds without finite variance. The recent work
[
21
]
shows that it does.

(c)

The action condition
S_{\omega,\gamma}(u_{0})\leq n_{\omega,\gamma}
can be rewritten into the mass-energy condition
M(u_{0})^{(1-s_{c})/s_{c}}E_{\gamma}(u_{0})\leq M(Q_{1,0})^{(1-s_{c})/s_{c}}E_{\gamma}(Q_{1,0})
by using the scaling structure of
S_{\omega,0}(Q_{\omega,0})
, where
s_{c}:=1/2-2/(p-1)
. Moreover, the functional condition
K_{\gamma}(u_{0})>0
can be also written as
\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{1-s_{c}}\|u_{0}\|_{\dot{H}_{\gamma}^{1}}^{s_{c}}<\|Q_{1,0}\|_{L^{2}}^{1-s_{c}}\|Q_{1,0}\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{s_{c}}
. See e.g.
[
4
, Lemma 2.6]
. This condition is also equivalent to
\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{1-s_{c}}\|\partial_{x}u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{s_{c}}<\|Q_{1,0}\|_{L^{2}}^{1-s_{c}}\|Q_{1,0}\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{s_{c}}
. See
[
22
]
.

(d)

It is worth emphasizing that, in the threshold case
S_{\omega,\gamma}(u_{0})=n_{\omega,\gamma}
, there is no non-scattering global solution like the ground state because of the repulsive potential (see
[
10
,
27
,
20
]
for NLS with other repulsive effects).

For even solutions, we have the following global dynamics result below
r_{\omega,\gamma}
.

Theorem 1.4

(Global dynamics of even solutions below
r_{\omega,\gamma}

(Ikeda–Inui
[
23
]
))
.

Let
\omega>0
. Assume that
u_{0}
is even and
S_{\omega,\gamma}(u_{0})<r_{\omega,\gamma}
. Then the following hold for (

δ

NLS
).

(1)

If
K_{\gamma}(u_{0})>0
, the solution is global and scatters in both time directions.

(2)

If
K_{\gamma}(u_{0})<0
, the solution blows up or grows up in both time directions.

Since
r_{\omega,\gamma}
is strictly larger than
n_{\omega,\gamma}
by Proposition
1.1
, the above theorem shows that the symmetry assumption raises the threshold. When
\omega>\gamma^{2}/4
, it is clear that the ground state solution is on the threshold, and is a non-scattering global solution. Thus, at least in the high-frequency case
\omega>\gamma^{2}/4
, we cannot expect a dichotomy result like Theorem
1.3
.

In the present paper, we are interested in the global dynamics of solutions on the threshold in the high-frequency and radial (i.e., even) setting.
In that case, we expect the situation to be similar to that of the nonlinear Schrödinger equation without potential
[
11
,
7
]
.
Indeed, we get the following main results.

Theorem 1.5

(Existence of special solutions)
.

Let
\omega>\gamma^{2}/4
be fixed. There exist two even solutions
U^{\pm}
to (

δ

NLS
) such that

•

M(U^{\pm})=M(Q_{\omega,\gamma})
,
E_{\gamma}(U^{\pm})=E_{\gamma}(Q_{\omega,\gamma})
,
U^{\pm}
exist at least on
[0,\infty)
and there exists
c>0
such that

\displaystyle\|U^{\pm}(t)-e^{i\omega t}Q_{\omega,\gamma}\|_{H^{1}}\lesssim e^{-ct}\text{ for }t\geq 0.

•

K_{\gamma}(U^{+}(0))<0
and
U^{+}
blows up in finite negative time.

•

K_{\gamma}(U^{-}(0))>0
and
U^{-}
scatters backward in time.

Theorem 1.6

(Global dynamics on the threshold)
.

Let
\omega>\gamma^{2}/4
. Assume that
u_{0}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
is even and satisfies the mass-energy condition

\displaystyle M(u_{0})=M(Q_{\omega,\gamma})\text{ and }E_{\gamma}(u_{0})=E_{\gamma}(Q_{\omega,\gamma}).

(ME)

Then the following are true for (

δ

NLS
).

(1)

If
K_{\gamma}(u_{0})>0
, the solution
u
scatters in both time directions, or else
u=U^{-}
up to symmetry.

(2)

If
K_{\gamma}(u_{0})=0
, then
u=e^{i\omega t}Q_{\omega,\gamma}
up to symmetry.

(3)

If
K_{\gamma}(u_{0})<0
and
\int_{\mathbb{R}}|xu_{0}(x)|^{2}dx<\infty
, the solution
u
blows up in both time directions, or else
u=U^{+}
up to symmetry.

Remark 1.2
.

(1)

The phrase “up to symmetry” means up to multiplication by a complex phase, time translation, and time reversibility.

(2)

We need not consider
S_{\omega,\gamma}(f)=r_{\omega,\gamma}
for
\omega>\gamma^{2}/4
since we have the following statement:
If
f
satisfies
S_{\omega,\gamma}(f)=r_{\omega,\gamma}
for some
\omega>\gamma^{2}/4
, then either
f
satisfies
S_{\omega^{\prime},\gamma}(f)<r_{\omega^{\prime},\gamma}
for some
\omega^{\prime}>0
or else
M(f)=M(Q_{\omega,\gamma})
and
E_{\gamma}(f)=E_{\gamma}(Q_{\omega,\gamma})
. We will give the proof in Appendix
A
.

(3)

We assume finite variance to show finite time blow-up. This can be removed by allowing for grow-up, and using the method of
[
21
]
,
but we do not pursue it here.

1.2.
Background and idea of proof

Global dynamics below and at the ground state level for the nonlinear Schrödinger equation without potential is well investigated. See
[
12
,
2
,
17
,
9
,
18
]
for the dichotomy result below the ground state. Duyckaerts and Roundenko
[
11
]
clarified the global dynamics on the threshold for 3d cubic NLS. Campos, Farah, and Roudenoko
[
7
]
generalized it to any dimensions and all mass-supercritical and energy-subcritical powers (and also treat the energy-critical nonlinearity). See also
[
21
]
for the blow-up or grow-up result on the threshold.

Our proof relies heavily on those of Duyckaerts and Roudenko
[
11
]
and Campos, Farah, and Roudenko
[
7
]
. First, we show the existence of a family of solutions going to the ground state exponentially in positive time. In this step, the eigenfunctions of the linearized operator around the ground state play an important role. In the potential-free case, the corresponding eigenfunctions are of Schwartz class on
\mathbb{R}
, while in the present case, they are not smooth at the origin due to the delta interaction. We must introduce a cut-off function to remove the origin, somewhat complicating the proof. In the next step, we show that a threshold solution tends to the ground state if
K_{\gamma}>0
and it it non-scattering, or if
K_{\gamma}<0
and it is global. A modulation argument and the virial identity are crucial to this argument, but here we meet a difficulty. To control the modulation parameters, we use the functional

\mu(u):=\|Q_{\omega,\gamma}\|_{\dot{H}_{\gamma}^{1}}^{2}-\|u\|_{\dot{H}_{\gamma}^{1}}^{2}
,
while the functional appearing in the virial identity is
K_{\gamma}(u)
.
When
\gamma=0
, these are same under the assumption (
ME
), and so the modulation argument and the virial identity are compatible. However, these are different when
\gamma<0
. To overcome this, we show that
K_{\gamma}(u)-c\mu(u)>0
(resp.
<0
) when
K_{\gamma}>0
(resp.
<0
), by characterizing the ground state in terms of a functional
K_{\omega,\gamma}^{\alpha,\beta}
generalizing the
virial and Nehari functionals. Though such an argument can be also seen in Ikeda and Inui
[
23
]
, we need to treat a more general functional than that used there.
Finally, we show that the solutions tending to the ground state exponentially are unique up to symmetries, by a bootstrap argument,
and investigate the behavior of those solutions in negative time. To determine the behavior of a solution with
K_{\gamma}(u_{0})<0
in negative time, we must show the solution has finite variance. In the higher dimensional cases, this fact follows from the radial symmetry (see e.g. Remark 5.2 in
[
11
]
) since we can use the radial Sobolev inequality. However, the argument does not work in one dimensional case. Instead of radiality, we use the convergence to the ground state, which itself is of finite variance. See
[
21
]
for such an argument.

In the low frequency case
0<\omega\leq\gamma^{2}/4
, there is no ground state at the threshold
r_{\omega,\gamma}
. The global dynamics is an interesting open problem.

Though the global dynamics for NLS without potential above the ground state has been studied (see e.g.
[
31
,
30
,
29
,
1
]
), the global dynamics of (

δ

NLS
) above
n_{\omega,\gamma}
in the non-even setting, and above
r_{\omega,\gamma}
in the even setting are open problems.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we prepare some lemmas for variational arguments, linearized operators, and such. In Proposition
2.5
, we relate
K_{\gamma}
and
\mu
, as described above. Section
3
is devoted to the construction of the family of special solutions. Section
4
shows the convergence of threshold solutions to the ground state. In particular, we set up the modulation in Section
4.1
, we treat the case of
K_{\gamma}>0
in Section
4.2.1
, and the case of
K_{\gamma}<0
in Section
4.2.2
. In Section
5
, we prove that the family is the unique solution up to symmetries. Appendix
A
discusses the envelope of the threshold condition’s family in terms of
\omega
. In Appendix
B
, we give some remarks about Schwartz class with cut-off.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we give some lemmas which are used in the sequel. In what follows,

2.1.
Variational argument

In this section, we revisit the variational argument not only for the
virial and Nehari functionals but also for more general functionals
K_{\omega,\gamma}^{\alpha,\beta}
, defined by

\displaystyle K_{\omega,\gamma}^{\alpha,\beta}(f)

\displaystyle:=\partial_{\lambda}S_{\omega,\gamma}(e^{\alpha\lambda}f(e^{\beta\lambda}\cdot))|_{\lambda=0}

\displaystyle=\frac{2\alpha+\beta}{2}\|\partial_{x}f\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\alpha\gamma|f(0)|^{2}+\omega\frac{2\alpha-\beta}{2}\|f\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\frac{(p+1)\alpha-\beta}{p+1}\|f\|_{L^{p+1}}^{p+1}.

By definition,
(\alpha,\beta)=(1/2,1)
gives the virial functional, that is
K_{\gamma}(f)=K_{\omega,\gamma}^{1/2,1}(f)
, and
(\alpha,\beta)=(1,0)
gives the Nehari functional, that is

\displaystyle I_{\omega,\gamma}(f):=K_{\omega,\gamma}^{1,0}(f)=\|\partial_{x}f\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\gamma|f(0)|^{2}+\omega\|f\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\|f\|_{L^{p+1}}^{p+1}.

If
(\alpha,\beta)
satisfies

\displaystyle\alpha>0,2\alpha-\beta\geq 0,2\alpha+\beta\geq 0,

(2.1)

we obtain the following lemma by
[
23
, Lemmas 2.7–2.10]
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
(\alpha,\beta)
satisfy (
2.1
).
We have

\displaystyle S_{\omega,\gamma}(Q_{\omega,\gamma})=\inf\{S_{\omega,\gamma}(f):f\in H_{{\mathord{\rm even}}}^{1}(\mathbb{R})\setminus\{0\},K_{\omega,\gamma}^{\alpha,\beta}(f)=0\}.

We define

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{\omega,\gamma}^{\alpha,\beta,+}

\displaystyle:=\{f\in H_{{\mathord{\rm even}}}^{1}(\mathbb{R}):M(f)=M(Q_{\omega,\gamma}),E_{\gamma}(f)=E_{\gamma}(Q_{\omega,\gamma}),K_{\omega,\gamma}^{\alpha,\beta}(f)>0\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{\omega,\gamma}^{\alpha,\beta,0}

\displaystyle:=\{f\in H_{{\mathord{\rm even}}}^{1}(\mathbb{R}):M(f)=M(Q_{\omega,\gamma}),E_{\gamma}(f)=E_{\gamma}(Q_{\omega,\gamma}),K_{\omega,\gamma}^{\alpha,\beta}(f)=0\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{\omega,\gamma}^{\alpha,\beta,-}

\displaystyle:=\{f\in H_{{\mathord{\rm even}}}^{1}(\mathbb{R}):M(f)=M(Q_{\omega,\gamma}),E_{\gamma}(f)=E_{\gamma}(Q_{\omega,\gamma}),K_{\omega,\gamma}^{\alpha,\beta}(f)<0\}.

We know that
\mathcal{K}_{\omega,\gamma}^{\alpha,\beta,0}=\{e^{i\theta}Q_{\omega,\gamma}:\theta\in\mathbb{R}\}
by Proposition
1.1
and Lemma
2.1
.

Lemma 2.2
.

\mathcal{K}_{\omega,\gamma}^{\alpha,\beta,+}
is independent of
(\alpha,\beta)
; that is,
\mathcal{K}_{\omega,\gamma}^{\alpha,\beta,+}=\mathcal{K}_{\omega,\gamma}^{\alpha^{\prime},\beta^{\prime},+}
for
(\alpha,\beta)
and
(\alpha^{\prime},\beta^{\prime})
satisfying (
2.1
). The same holds for
\mathcal{K}_{\omega,\gamma}^{\alpha,\beta,-}
.

Proof.

Let
(\alpha,\beta)
and
(\alpha^{\prime},\beta^{\prime})
satisfy (
2.1
).
If
\mathcal{K}_{\omega,\gamma}^{\alpha,\beta,+}\subsetneq\mathcal{K}_{\omega,\gamma}^{\alpha^{\prime},\beta^{\prime},+}
, then there exists a function
f\in H_{\mathord{\rm even}}^{1}
such that

\displaystyle M(f)=M(Q_{\omega,\gamma}),\ E_{\gamma}(f)=E_{\gamma}(Q_{\omega,\gamma}),\ K_{\omega,\gamma}^{\alpha,\beta}(f)\leq 0,\text{ and }K_{\omega,\gamma}^{\alpha^{\prime},\beta^{\prime}}(f)>0.

If
K_{\omega,\gamma}^{\alpha,\beta}(f)=0
, then
f=e^{i\theta}Q_{\omega,\gamma}
and thus
K_{\omega,\gamma}^{\alpha^{\prime},\beta^{\prime}}(f)=0
. This is a contradiction. Hence
K_{\omega,\gamma}^{\alpha,\beta}(f)<0
. Let
f_{\lambda}^{\alpha,\beta}(x)=e^{\alpha\lambda}f(e^{\beta\lambda}x)
. Then we have

\displaystyle 0>K_{\omega,\gamma}^{\alpha,\beta}(f)=\partial_{\lambda}S_{\omega,\gamma}(f_{\lambda}^{\alpha,\beta})|_{\lambda=0}.

This means that
S_{\omega,\gamma}(f_{\lambda}^{\alpha,\beta})
is strictly decreasing in
\lambda
near
\lambda=0
and thus
S_{\omega,\gamma}(f_{\lambda}^{\alpha,\beta})<S_{\omega,\gamma}(f)=S_{\omega,\gamma}(Q_{\omega,\gamma})
for
\lambda>0
sufficiently close to
0
. Since we have
K_{\omega,\gamma}^{\alpha,\beta}(f)<0
, it holds that
K_{\omega,\gamma}^{\alpha,\beta}(f_{\lambda}^{\alpha,\beta})<0
for
\lambda>0
sufficiently small. This means that
f_{\lambda}^{\alpha,\beta}
belongs to

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{\omega,\gamma}^{\alpha,\beta,-}

\displaystyle:=\{f\in H_{{\mathord{\rm even}}}^{1}(\mathbb{R}):S_{\omega,\gamma}(f)<S_{\omega,\gamma}(Q_{\omega,\gamma}),K_{\omega,\gamma}^{\alpha,\beta}(f)<0\}.

By Proposition 2.15 in
[
23
]
, it holds that
\mathcal{R}_{\omega,\gamma}^{\alpha,\beta,-}=\mathcal{R}_{\omega,\gamma}^{\alpha^{\prime},\beta^{\prime},-}
and thus we get

A Gagliardo–Nirenberg type inequality follows easily from the usual one: for

The optimal constant

\displaystyle C_{GN}^{-1}=\frac{\|Q_{1,0}\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{p+3}{2}}\|Q_{1,0}\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{\frac{p-1}{2}}}{\|Q_{1,0}\|_{L^{p+1}}^{p+1}}.

See e.g.

22

.
This is not suitable to our problem since we consider a higher threshold than

Lemma 2.8

(Gagliardo–Nirenberg type inequality w.r.t

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{p+1}}^{2}\leq C_{\omega,\gamma}\|f\|_{{H}_{\omega,\gamma}^{1}}^{2}.

Moreover, the optimal constant

\displaystyle C_{\omega,\gamma}^{-1}=\frac{\|Q_{\omega,\gamma}\|_{{H}_{\omega,\gamma}^{1}}^{2}}{\|Q_{\omega,\gamma}\|_{L^{p+1}}^{2}}=\left\{\frac{2(p+1)}{p-1}S_{\omega,\gamma}(Q_{\omega,\gamma})\right\}^{\frac{p-1}{p+1}}=\|Q_{\omega,\gamma}\|_{L^{p+1}}^{p-1}.

\displaystyle S_{\omega,\gamma}(Q_{\omega,\gamma})=\inf\{S_{\omega,\gamma}(f):f\in H_{\mathord{\rm even}}^{1}(\mathbb{R})\setminus\{0\},I_{\omega,\gamma}(f)=0\},

Let

2.5.1.

Here we consider the linearized operators around

(

]

Lemma 2.13

(Smoothness of eigenfunctions)

We have
\mathcal{Y}_{1},\mathcal{Y}_{2}\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\})

Proof.

Let

. Assume that

for an arbitrary function
\psi\in BC_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})

By the equation

, we have

\displaystyle\psi\partial_{x}^{2}\mathcal{Y}_{1}=\psi(\omega\mathcal{Y}_{1}-pQ_{\omega,\gamma}^{p-1}\mathcal{Y}_{1}-e_{\omega}\mathcal{Y}_{2})\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}),

where we note that
\psi Q_{\omega,\gamma}^{p-1}\in BC_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})

\mathcal{Y}_{1}\in H^{1}

\displaystyle\partial_{x}(\psi\partial_{x}\mathcal{Y}_{1})=(\partial_{x}\psi)\partial_{x}\mathcal{Y}_{1}+\psi\partial_{x}^{2}\mathcal{Y}_{1}\in H^{\min\{0,s\}}

and thus
\psi\partial_{x}\mathcal{Y}_{1}\in H^{\min\{1,s+1\}}
. Since
\psi
is arbitrary, we get

\displaystyle\partial_{x}(\psi\partial_{x}\mathcal{Y}_{1})=(\partial_{x}\psi)\partial_{x}\mathcal{Y}_{1}+\psi\partial_{x}^{2}\mathcal{Y}_{1}\in H^{\min\{1,s\}}

and thus
\psi\partial_{x}\mathcal{Y}_{1}\in H^{\min\{2,s+1\}}

\psi\partial_{x}\mathcal{Y}_{1}\in H^{s+1}

\displaystyle\partial_{x}(\psi\mathcal{Y}_{1})=\partial_{x}\psi\mathcal{Y}_{1}+\psi\partial_{x}\mathcal{Y}_{1}\in H^{s}

(2.4)

Thus
\psi\mathcal{Y}_{1}\in H^{s+1}
. By this,
\psi\partial_{x}\mathcal{Y}_{1}\in H^{s+1}
, and (

) again, we get
\psi\mathcal{Y}_{1}\in H^{s+2}
. Similarly,
\psi\mathcal{Y}_{2}\in H^{s+2}
. We start this argument with

and thus we get
\psi\mathcal{Y}_{1},\psi\mathcal{Y}_{2}\in H^{m}
for any integer

\mathcal{Y}_{1},\mathcal{Y}_{2}\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\})
since
\psi
is arbitrary.
∎

Since we have
L_{\omega,\gamma}^{+}\mathcal{Y}_{1}=e_{\omega}\mathcal{Y}_{2}
and
L_{\omega,\gamma}^{-}\mathcal{Y}_{2}=-e_{\omega}\mathcal{Y}_{1}
, we get

\displaystyle\{(-\partial_{x}^{2}+\omega)^{2}+e_{\omega}^{2}\}(\psi\mathcal{Y}_{1})

\displaystyle=L_{\omega,\gamma}^{-}\{-2\partial_{x}\psi\partial_{x}\mathcal{Y}_{1}-(\partial_{x}^{2}\psi)\mathcal{Y}_{1}\}+e_{\omega}\{-2\partial_{x}\psi\partial_{x}\mathcal{Y}_{2}-(\partial_{x}^{2}\psi)\mathcal{Y}_{2}\}

\displaystyle\quad+p(-\partial_{x}^{2}+\omega)(V\psi\mathcal{Y}_{1})+V(-\partial_{x}^{2}+\omega)(\psi\mathcal{Y}_{1})-pV^{2}(\psi\mathcal{Y}_{1}),

(2.5)

where
V:=Q_{\omega,\gamma}^{p-1}
, for
\psi\in BC_{0,{\mathord{\rm even}}}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
. We note that the above calculation is valid since
\psi
vanishes near the origin and so the functions
are in the domain
\mathcal{D}
.

Lemma 2.14
.

For any function
\varphi\in C_{c,0,{\mathord{\rm even}}}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
and
k,s\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists
C_{s,k,\varphi}>0
such that

\displaystyle\|\varphi(x/R)\mathcal{Y}_{1}\|_{H^{s}}<\frac{C_{s,k,\varphi}}{R^{k}}

for any
R\geq 1
. A similar estimate for
\mathcal{Y}_{2}
also holds.

Proof.

For
j=1,2
, we say that
P_{s,k}^{j}
is true if there exists a constant
C=C_{s,k,\varphi}>0
such that

\displaystyle\|\varphi(x/R)\mathcal{Y}_{j}\|_{H^{s}}<\frac{C}{R^{k}}.

holds. We note that
P_{\sigma,k}^{j}
is true for
\sigma\leq s
if
P_{s,k}^{j}
is valid.
Now,
P_{s,0}^{1}
and
P_{s,0}^{2}
are true for all
s\in\mathbb{N}
since we have
\varphi(x/R)\mathcal{Y}_{j}\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R})
by the proof of Lemma
2.13
for
j=1,2
. Let
k\geq 0
. We will show that
P_{s+1,k+1}^{1}
is valid if
P_{s,k}^{1}
is true for all
s\in\mathbb{N}
. Assume that
P_{s,k}^{1}
is true for all
s\in\mathbb{N}
.
For given
\varphi
, take a function
\widetilde{\varphi}\in C_{c,0,{\mathord{\rm even}}}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
satisfying
\widetilde{\varphi}(x)=1
on the support of
\varphi
.
Then we have

\displaystyle\|\varphi(x/R)\mathcal{Y}_{1}\|_{H^{s+1}}

\displaystyle=\|\varphi(x/R)\widetilde{\varphi}(x/R)\mathcal{Y}_{1}\|_{H^{s+1}}

\displaystyle\approx\|\{(-\partial_{x}^{2}+\omega)^{2}+e_{\omega}^{2}\}\varphi(x/R)\widetilde{\varphi}(x/R)\mathcal{Y}_{1}\|_{H^{s-3}}

\displaystyle\leq\|[\{(-\partial_{x}^{2}+\omega)^{2}+e_{\omega}^{2}\},\varphi(x/R)]\widetilde{\varphi}(x/R)\mathcal{Y}_{1}\|_{H^{s-3}}

\displaystyle\quad+\|\varphi(x/R)\{(-\partial_{x}^{2}+\omega)^{2}+e_{\omega}^{2}\}\widetilde{\varphi}(x/R)\mathcal{Y}_{1}\|_{H^{s-3}}.

Now we know that
\|[\{(-\partial_{x}^{2}+\omega)^{2}+e_{\omega}^{2}\},\varphi(x/R)]\|_{H^{s}\to H^{s-3}}\lesssim R^{-1}
.

Thus we get

\displaystyle\|\varphi(x/R)\mathcal{Y}_{1}\|_{H^{s+1}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{R}\|\widetilde{\varphi}(x/R)\mathcal{Y}_{1}\|_{H^{s}}+\|\varphi(x/R)\{(-\partial_{x}^{2}+\omega)^{2}+e_{\omega}^{2}\}\widetilde{\varphi}(x/R)\mathcal{Y}_{1}\|_{H^{s-3}}.

The second term can be estimated by using (
2.5
) as
\psi=\widetilde{\varphi}(\cdot/R)
. Indeed, by
\varphi\widetilde{\varphi}=\varphi
,
\varphi\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\widetilde{\varphi}=0
for
\alpha\geq 1
, and (
2.5
), we obtain

\displaystyle\|\varphi(x/R)\{(-\partial_{x}^{2}+\omega)^{2}+e_{\omega}^{2}\}(\widetilde{\varphi}(x/R)\mathcal{Y}_{1})\|_{H^{s-3}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\varphi(x/R)V\mathcal{Y}_{1}\|_{H^{s-3}}+\|\varphi(x/R)\partial_{x}V\partial_{x}\mathcal{Y}_{1}\|_{H^{s-3}}

\displaystyle\quad+\|\varphi(x/R)\partial_{x}^{2}V\mathcal{Y}_{1}\|_{H^{s-3}}+\|\varphi(x/R)V\partial_{x}^{2}\mathcal{Y}_{1}\|_{H^{s-3}}+\|\varphi(x/R)V^{2}\mathcal{Y}_{1}\|_{H^{s-3}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{\alpha+\beta+\gamma\leq s-1}\|\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\varphi(x/R)\partial_{x}^{\beta}V\partial_{x}^{\gamma}\mathcal{Y}_{1}\|_{L^{2}}.

Since
V=Q_{\omega,\gamma}^{p-1}
decays exponentially, we have
\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\varphi(x/R)\partial_{x}^{\beta}V\lesssim R^{-1}\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\varphi(x/R)
.
Thus, it holds that

\displaystyle\sum_{\alpha+\beta+\gamma\leq s-1}\|\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\varphi(x/R)\partial_{x}^{\beta}V\partial_{x}^{\gamma}\mathcal{Y}_{1}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim R^{-1}\sum_{\alpha+\gamma\leq s-1}\|\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\varphi(x/R)\partial_{x}^{\gamma}\mathcal{Y}_{1}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim R^{-1}\|\varphi(x/R)\mathcal{Y}_{1}\|_{H^{s-1}}.

This means that

\displaystyle\|\varphi(x/R)\{(-\partial_{x}^{2}+\omega)^{2}+e_{\omega}^{2}\}\widetilde{\varphi}(x/R)\mathcal{Y}_{1}\|_{H^{s-3}}\lesssim R^{-1}\|\varphi(x/R)\mathcal{Y}_{1}\|_{H^{s-1}}.

Therefore, since
P_{s,k}^{1}
is true, we get

\displaystyle\|\varphi(x/R)\mathcal{Y}_{1}\|_{H^{s+1}}\leq\frac{C}{R}\|\widetilde{\varphi}(x/R)\mathcal{Y}_{1}\|_{H^{s}}+\frac{C}{R}\|\varphi(x/R)\mathcal{Y}_{1}\|_{H^{s-1}}\leq\frac{C_{s,k,\varphi}}{R^{k+1}}.

This means that
P_{s+1,k+1}^{1}
is true. In the same way, we also can show that
P_{s+1,k+1}^{2}
is true.
∎

Lemma 2.15

(Decay of eigenfunctions)
.

We have
\mathcal{Y}_{1},\mathcal{Y}_{2}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}(\mathbb{R})
.

Proof.

We only consider
\mathcal{Y}_{1}
.
Let
\psi\in C_{0,{\mathord{\rm even}}}^{\infty}
satisfy

\displaystyle\psi(x)=\begin{cases}0,&|x|\leq 1,\\
1,&|x|\geq 2.\end{cases}

Let
s,l\in\mathbb{N}
.
Now,
x^{l}\psi\mathcal{Y}_{1}
is even or odd. Thus we have

\displaystyle\|x^{l}\psi\mathcal{Y}_{1}\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}=2\|x^{l}\psi\mathcal{Y}_{1}\|_{H^{s}(1,\infty)}^{2}=2\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\|x^{l}\psi\mathcal{Y}_{1}\|_{H^{s}([2^{n},2^{n+1}])}^{2}.

Let
\varphi\in C_{c,0,{\mathord{\rm even}}}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
satisfy

\displaystyle\varphi(x)=\begin{cases}1&\text{ if }1\leq|x|\leq 2,\\
0&\text{ if }|x|>4,|x|<1/2.\end{cases}

\displaystyle\|x^{l}\psi\mathcal{Y}_{1}\|_{H^{s}([2^{n},2^{n+1}])}=\|x^{l}\psi\varphi(\cdot/2^{n})\mathcal{Y}_{1}\|_{H^{s}([2^{n},2^{n+1}])}\lesssim 2^{nl}\|\varphi(\cdot/2^{n})\mathcal{Y}_{1}\|_{H^{s}}.

2.14
with

\displaystyle 2^{nl}\|\varphi(\cdot/2^{n})\mathcal{Y}_{1}\|_{H^{s}([2^{n},2^{n+1}])}\lesssim 2^{nl}\frac{C_{s,l,\varphi}}{2^{n(l+1)}}\lesssim 2^{-n}.

and thus

. This and the Sobolev embedding imply

A similar argument gives us

Lemma 2.18
.

For

, the following estimates hold:

\displaystyle\|Q_{\omega,\gamma}^{-1}e^{\eta|x|}\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\mathcal{Y}_{\pm}\|_{L^{\infty}}<\infty

2.15

where

\displaystyle:=\{(-\partial_{x}^{2})^{2}+\omega)^{2}+e_{\omega}^{2}\}(\psi\mathcal{Y}_{1})

Lemma 2.19
.

Let

and let

\displaystyle\|Q_{\omega,\gamma}^{-1}e^{\eta|x|}\partial_{x}^{\beta}\{(\mathcal{L}_{\omega,\gamma}-\lambda)^{-1}f\}\|_{L^{\infty}}<\infty

We have

\displaystyle\|Q_{\omega,\gamma}^{-1}e^{\eta|x|}\partial_{x}^{\beta}\{(\mathcal{L}_{\omega,\gamma}-\lambda)^{-1}f\}\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq\|Q_{\omega,\gamma}^{-1}e^{\eta|x|}\partial_{x}^{\beta}\{\psi(\mathcal{L}_{\omega,\gamma}-\lambda)^{-1}f\}\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\quad+\|Q_{\omega,\gamma}^{-1}e^{\eta|x|}\partial_{x}^{\beta}\{(1-\psi)(\mathcal{L}_{\omega,\gamma}-\lambda)^{-1}f\}\|_{L^{\infty}}.

Since we have

and

, the second term in the right hand side is finite. It is enough to estimate the first term.

\displaystyle\begin{cases}-(-\partial_{x}^{2}+\omega-Q_{\omega,\gamma}^{p-1})g_{2}-\lambda g_{1}=f_{1},\\

\displaystyle(-\partial_{x}^{2}+\omega-Q_{\omega,\gamma}^{p-1})(\psi g_{2})

\displaystyle=-2\partial_{x}\psi\partial_{x}g_{2}-\partial_{x}^{2}\psi g_{2}+\psi(-\partial_{x}^{2}+\omega-Q_{\omega,\gamma}^{p-1})g_{2}

\displaystyle=2\partial_{x}\psi\partial_{x}g_{2}+\partial_{x}^{2}\psi g_{2}-\lambda\psi g_{1}+\psi f_{1}.

2.15

\psi g\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})
.

\displaystyle(-\partial_{x}^{2}+\omega-pQ_{\omega,\gamma}^{p-1})(-\partial_{x}^{2}+\omega-Q_{\omega,\gamma}^{p-1})(\psi g_{2})

\displaystyle=(-\partial_{x}^{2}+\omega-pQ_{\omega,\gamma}^{p-1})(-2\partial_{x}\psi\partial_{x}g_{2}-\partial_{x}^{2}\psi g_{2})

\displaystyle\quad-\lambda(-2\partial_{x}\psi\partial_{x}g_{1}-\partial_{x}^{2}\psi g_{1}+\lambda\psi g_{2}+\psi f_{2})

\displaystyle\quad+(-\partial_{x}^{2}+\omega-pQ_{\omega,\gamma})(\psi f_{1}).

\displaystyle\{(-\partial_{x}^{2}+\omega)^{2}+\lambda^{2}\}(\psi g_{2})

\displaystyle=pQ_{\omega,\gamma}^{p-1}(-\partial_{x}^{2}+\omega)(\psi g_{2})+(-\partial_{x}^{2}+\omega)(Q_{\omega,\gamma}^{p-1}\psi g_{2})

2.5.3.
Coercivity

In this section, we show the coercivity of the linearized operators on sets with orthogonality conditions.

We define bilinear operators
B_{\omega,\gamma}^{\pm}
by

\displaystyle B_{\omega,\gamma}^{+}(f,g):=\langle L_{\omega,\gamma}^{+}f,g\rangle,\quad B_{\omega,\gamma}^{-}(f,g):=\langle L_{\omega,\gamma}^{-}f,g\rangle

for
f,g\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{R})
. More precisely, for example, the first one is defined by

\displaystyle B_{\omega,\gamma}^{+}(f,g)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\partial_{x}f\partial_{x}gdx+\omega\int_{\mathbb{R}}fgdx-\gamma f(0)g(0)-p\int_{\mathbb{R}}Q_{\omega,\gamma}^{p-1}fgdx.

We also define

\displaystyle B_{\omega,\gamma}(f,g):=\frac{1}{2}B_{\omega,\gamma}^{+}(f_{1},g_{1})+\frac{1}{2}B_{\omega,\gamma}^{-}(f_{2},g_{2})

for
f=f_{1}+if_{2},g=g_{1}+ig_{2}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
and

\displaystyle\Phi(f):=B_{\omega,\gamma}(f,f)

for
f\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
.

We consider the following orthogonality conditions:

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}f_{2}(x)Q_{\omega,\gamma}(x)dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}}f_{1}(x)Q_{\omega,\gamma}^{p}(x)dx=0,

(2.6)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}f_{2}(x)Q_{\omega,\gamma}(x)dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}}f_{2}(x)\mathcal{Y}_{1}(x)dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}}f_{1}(x)\mathcal{Y}_{2}(x)dx=0,

(2.7)

where
f_{1}=\re f
and
f_{2}=\im f
.
The sets in
H_{{\mathord{\rm even}}}^{1}(\mathbb{R})
satisfying (
2.6
) and (
2.7
) are denoted by
G^{\perp}
and
\widetilde{G}^{\perp}
, respectively. That is, we set

\displaystyle G^{\perp}:=\{f\in H_{{\mathord{\rm even}}}^{1}(\mathbb{R}):(iQ,f)_{L^{2}}=(Q^{p},f)_{L^{2}}=0\},

\displaystyle\widetilde{G}^{\perp}:=\{f\in H_{{\mathord{\rm even}}}^{1}(\mathbb{R}):(iQ,f)_{L^{2}}=(\mathcal{Y}_{1},f_{2})_{L^{2}}=(\mathcal{Y}_{2},f_{1})_{L^{2}}=0\}.

Then we have the following coercivity of
\Phi
on
G^{\perp}
and
\widetilde{G}^{\perp}
.

Lemma 2.20

(Coercivity)
.

There exists a positive constant
c
such that

\displaystyle\Phi(f)\geq c\|f\|_{H^{1}}^{2}

for all
f\in G^{\perp}\cup\widetilde{G}^{\perp}
.

Proof.

Let
f=f_{1}+if_{2}\in G^{\perp}
. We show that

\langle L_{\omega,\gamma}^{+}f_{1},f_{1}\rangle\geq c\|f_{1}\|_{H^{1}}^{2}

for some constant
c>0
. For given small
\varepsilon>0
, we set

\displaystyle u_{\varepsilon}:=\lambda_{\varepsilon}(Q_{\omega,\gamma}+\varepsilon f_{1})

(2.8)

with
\lambda_{\varepsilon}\in\mathbb{R}
chosen such that

\displaystyle 0=I_{\omega,\gamma}(u_{\varepsilon})=\lambda_{\varepsilon}^{2}\left\{\|Q_{\omega,\gamma}+\varepsilon f_{1}\|_{H_{\omega,\gamma}^{1}}^{2}-\lambda_{\varepsilon}^{p-1}\|Q_{\omega,\gamma}+\varepsilon f_{1}\|_{L^{p+1}}^{p+1}\right\}.

(2.9)

Since it follows from the equation (
1.1
) and the orthogonality assumption (
2.6
) that

\displaystyle(Q_{\omega,\gamma},f_{1})_{H_{\omega,\gamma}^{1}}=(Q_{\omega,\gamma}^{p},f_{1})_{L^{2}}=0,

we get

\displaystyle\|Q_{\omega,\gamma}+\varepsilon f_{1}\|_{H_{\omega,\gamma}^{1}}^{2}=\|Q_{\omega,\gamma}\|_{H_{\omega,\gamma}^{1}}^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}\|f_{1}\|_{H_{\omega,\gamma}^{1}}^{2}.

Moreover, we also have

\displaystyle\|Q_{\omega,\gamma}+\varepsilon f_{1}\|_{L^{p+1}}^{p+1}

\displaystyle=\|Q_{\omega,\gamma}\|_{L^{p+1}}^{p+1}+\varepsilon(p+1)(Q^{p},f_{1})_{L^{2}}+O(\varepsilon^{2})

\displaystyle=\|Q_{\omega,\gamma}\|_{L^{p+1}}^{p+1}+O(\varepsilon^{2}).

Thus (
2.9
) and these equalities yield that

\displaystyle\lambda_{\varepsilon}^{p-1}=\frac{\|Q_{\omega,\gamma}+\varepsilon f_{1}\|_{H_{\omega,\gamma}^{1}}^{2}}{\|Q_{\omega,\gamma}+\varepsilon f_{1}\|_{L^{p+1}}^{p+1}}=\frac{\|Q_{\omega,\gamma}\|_{H_{\omega,\gamma}^{1}}^{2}+O(\varepsilon^{2})}{\|Q_{\omega,\gamma}\|_{L^{p+1}}^{p+1}+O(\varepsilon^{2})}=1+O(\varepsilon^{2}),

where we have used
I_{\omega,\gamma}(Q_{\omega,\gamma})=0
, that is,
\|Q_{\omega,\gamma}\|_{H_{\omega,\gamma}^{1}}^{2}=\|Q_{\omega,\gamma}\|_{L^{p+1}}^{p+1}
. This means that

\displaystyle\lambda_{\varepsilon}=1+O(\varepsilon^{2}).

Substituting this into (
2.8
), we get

\displaystyle u_{\varepsilon}=Q_{\omega,\gamma}+\varepsilon f_{1}+O(\varepsilon^{2}).

Since
u_{\varepsilon}
satisfies
I_{\omega,\gamma}(u_{\varepsilon})=0
by (
2.9
) and
S_{\omega,\gamma}(Q_{\omega,\gamma})=\min\{S(f):f\in H_{{\mathord{\rm even}}}^{1}(\mathbb{R})\setminus\{0\},I_{\omega,\gamma}(f)=0\}
, we get

\displaystyle 0\leq S_{\omega,\gamma}(u_{\varepsilon})-S_{\omega,\gamma}(Q_{\omega,\gamma})

\displaystyle=\langle S_{\omega,\gamma}^{\prime}(Q_{\omega,\gamma}),\varepsilon f_{1}+O(\varepsilon^{2})\rangle+\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon^{2}\langle L_{\omega,\gamma}^{+}f_{1},f_{1}\rangle+O(\varepsilon^{3})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon^{2}\langle L_{\omega,\gamma}^{+}f_{1},f_{1}\rangle+O(\varepsilon^{3})

by the Taylor expansion and the equation (
1.1
). By taking
\varepsilon>0
sufficiently small, this means that

\displaystyle\langle L_{\omega,\gamma}^{+}f_{1},f_{1}\rangle\geq 0.

In the similar way to Step 2 in the proof of Lemma 3.5 (intercritical case) in
[
7
]
, we get the coercivity on
G^{\perp}
. We can also get the coercivity on
\widetilde{G}^{\perp}
in the same way as in
[
11
]
. We omit the details of the proof.
∎

2.5.4.
Estimates on the linearized equation

In this section, we give estimates of
P
and
R
.

Lemma 2.21
.

Let
I
be a bounded time interval.
For
f\in L_{t}^{\infty}H^{1}
, we have

\displaystyle\|P(f)\|_{L_{t}^{1}H^{1}(I)}\lesssim|I|\|f\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}H^{1}(I)}.

Proof.

Since we have
P(v)=\frac{p+1}{2}Q_{\omega,\gamma}^{p-1}v+\frac{p-1}{2}Q_{\omega,\gamma}^{p-1}\overline{v}
,
it follows from the Hölder inequality that

\displaystyle\|P(f)\|_{L_{t}^{1}L^{2}(I)}\lesssim\|Q_{\omega,\gamma}^{p-1}f\|_{L^{1}L^{2}(I)}\lesssim\|f\|_{L_{t}^{1}L^{2}(I)}\lesssim|I|\|f\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L^{2}(I)}.

Moreover, we also have

\displaystyle\|\partial_{x}P(f)\|_{L_{t}^{1}L^{2}(I)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|Q_{\omega,\gamma}^{p-2}\partial_{x}Q_{\omega,\gamma}f\|_{L_{t}^{1}L^{2}(I)}+\|Q_{\omega,\gamma}^{p-1}\partial_{x}f\|_{L_{t}^{1}L^{2}(I)}

\displaystyle\lesssim|I|\|f\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L^{2}(I)}+|I|\|\partial_{x}f\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L^{2}(I)}

since
\partial_{x}Q_{\omega,\gamma}\in L^{\infty}
. Thus we obtain the desired estimate.
∎

Lemma 2.22
.

Let
I
be a time interval with
|I|\leq 1
.
For
f,g\in L_{t}^{\infty}H^{1}
, we have

\displaystyle\|R(f)-R(g)\|_{L_{t}^{1}H^{1}(I)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f-g\|_{L^{\infty}H^{1}}(\|f\|_{L^{\infty}H^{1}}+\|g\|_{L^{\infty}H^{1}}+\|f\|_{L^{\infty}H^{1}}^{p-1}+\|g\|_{L^{\infty}H^{1}}^{p-1}).

Proof.

Since
R(f)=Q_{\omega,\gamma}^{p}N(Q_{\omega,\gamma}^{-1}f)
, where
N(z)=|1+z|^{p-1}(1+z)-1-\frac{p+1}{2}z-\frac{p-1}{2}\overline{z}
, we get

\displaystyle R(f)-R(g)=Q_{\omega,\gamma}^{p}(N(Q_{\omega,\gamma}^{-1}f)-N(Q_{\omega,\gamma}^{-1}g))

\displaystyle=Q_{\omega,\gamma}^{p-1}\int_{0}^{1}N_{z}(Q_{\omega,\gamma}^{-1}(sf+(1-s)g))(f-g)+N_{\overline{z}}(Q_{\omega,\gamma}^{-1}(sf+(1-s)g))(\overline{f-g})ds.

Now it holds that

\displaystyle|N_{z}(z_{1})-N_{z}(z_{2})|+|N_{\overline{z}}(z_{1})-N_{\overline{z}}(z_{2})|\lesssim|z_{1}-z_{2}|(1+|z_{1}|^{p-2}+|z_{2}|^{p-2}).

(2.10)

Thus we have

\displaystyle|R(f)-R(g)|

\displaystyle\leq Q_{\omega,\gamma}^{p-1}|f-g|\int_{0}^{1}|N_{z}(Q_{\omega,\gamma}^{-1}(sf+(1-s)g))|+|N_{\overline{z}}(Q_{\omega,\gamma}^{-1}(sf+(1-s)g))|ds

\displaystyle\lesssim Q_{\omega,\gamma}^{p-1}|f-g|Q_{\omega,\gamma}^{-1}(|f|+|g|)\{1+Q_{\omega,\gamma}^{-(p-2)}(|f|^{p-2}+|g|^{p-2})\}

\displaystyle\lesssim|f-g|(Q_{\omega,\gamma}^{p-2}|f|+Q_{\omega,\gamma}^{p-2}|g|+|f|^{p-1}+|g|^{p-1}).

(2.11)

Thus, by the Sobolev embedding
H^{1}(\mathbb{R})\hookrightarrow L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
and
|I|\leq 1
, we get

\displaystyle\|R(f)-R(g)\|_{L_{t}^{1}L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f-g\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L^{2}}(\|Q_{\omega,\gamma}\|_{L^{p-2}L^{\infty}}^{p-2}\|f\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L^{\infty}}+\|Q_{\omega,\gamma}\|_{L^{p-2}L^{\infty}}^{p-2}\|g\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\quad+\|f\|_{L^{p-2}L^{\infty}}^{p-2}\|f\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L^{\infty}}+\|g\|_{L^{p-2}L^{\infty}}^{p-2}\|g\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L^{\infty}})

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f-g\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L^{2}}(\|f\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}H^{1}}+\|g\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}H^{1}}+\|f\|_{L^{\infty}H^{1}}^{p-1}+\|g\|_{L^{\infty}H^{1}}^{p-1}).

Moreover, by a direct calculation, we have

\displaystyle\partial_{x}(R(f)-R(g))

\displaystyle=pQ^{p-1}\partial_{x}Q(N(Q^{-1}f)-N(Q^{-1}g))

\displaystyle\quad-Q^{p-2}(N_{z}(Q^{-1}f)(\partial_{x}Q)f-N_{z}(Q^{-1}g)(\partial_{x}Q)g)

\displaystyle\quad+Q^{p-1}(N_{z}(Q^{-1}f)\partial_{x}f-N_{z}(Q^{-1}g)\partial_{x}g)

\displaystyle\quad-Q^{p-2}(N_{\overline{z}}(Q^{-1}f)(\partial_{x}Q)\overline{f}-N_{\overline{z}}(Q^{-1}g)(\partial_{x}Q)\overline{g})

\displaystyle\quad+Q^{p-1}(N_{\overline{z}}(Q^{-1}f)\overline{\partial_{x}f}-N_{\overline{z}}(Q^{-1}g)\overline{\partial_{x}g})

\displaystyle=(i)+(ii)+(iii)+(iv)+(v),

where we denote
Q=Q_{\omega,\gamma}
for simplicity. It holds that

\displaystyle|(i)|\lesssim|\partial_{x}Q|Q^{-1}|R(f)-R(g)|\lesssim|f-g|(Q^{p-2}(|f|+|g|)+|f|^{p-1}+|g|^{p-1}),

where we used
|\partial_{x}Q|Q^{-1}\lesssim 1
by Lemma
2.16
. We have

\displaystyle|(ii)|

\displaystyle\lesssim Q^{p-2}|\partial_{x}Q||N_{z}(Q^{-1}f)f-N_{z}(Q^{-1}g)g|

\displaystyle=Q^{p-2}|\partial_{x}Q||N_{z}(Q^{-1}f)f-N_{z}(Q^{-1}f)g+N_{z}(Q^{-1}f)g-N_{z}(Q^{-1}g)g|

\displaystyle\lesssim Q^{p-2}|\partial_{x}Q|\{|N_{z}(Q^{-1}f)||f-g|+|N_{z}(Q^{-1}f)-N_{z}(Q^{-1}g)||g|\}.

From (
2.10
), this can be estimated by

\displaystyle Q^{p-2}|\partial_{x}Q|\{|N_{z}(Q^{-1}f)||f-g|+|N_{z}(Q^{-1}f)-N_{z}(Q^{-1}g)||g|\}

\displaystyle\lesssim Q^{p-2}|\partial_{x}Q|\{(|Q^{-1}f|+|Q^{-1}f|^{p-1})|f-g|+Q^{-1}|f-g|(1+|Q^{-1}f|^{p-2}+|Q^{-1}g|^{p-2})|g|\}

\displaystyle\approx Q^{p-2}|\partial_{x}Q||f-g|\{|Q^{-1}f|+|Q^{-1}f|^{p-1}+Q^{-1}|g|+Q^{-1}|g||Q^{-1}f|^{p-2}+|Q^{-1}g|^{p-1})\}.

By the Young inequality, we have

\displaystyle Q^{-1}|g||Q^{-1}f|^{p-2}\lesssim(Q^{-1}|g|)^{p-1}+|Q^{-1}f|^{p-1}.

Thus we obtain

\displaystyle Q^{p-2}|\partial_{x}Q||f-g|\{|Q^{-1}f|+|Q^{-1}f|^{p-1}+Q^{-1}|g|+Q^{-1}|g||Q^{-1}f|^{p-2}+|Q^{-1}g|^{p-1})\}

\displaystyle\lesssim Q^{p-2}|\partial_{x}Q||f-g|\{|Q^{-1}f|+Q^{-1}|g|+|Q^{-1}f|^{p-1}+|Q^{-1}g|^{p-1})\}

Combining these estimates give us that

\displaystyle|(ii)|

\displaystyle\lesssim Q^{p-2}|\partial_{x}Q||f-g|\{|Q^{-1}f|+Q^{-1}|g|+|Q^{-1}f|^{p-1}+|Q^{-1}g|^{p-1})\}

\displaystyle\lesssim|f-g|(Q^{p-2}|f|+Q^{p-2}|g|+|f|^{p-1}+|g|^{p-1}),

where we used
Q^{-1}|\partial_{x}Q|\lesssim 1
. We can estimate (iv) similarly.

\displaystyle|(iii)|

\displaystyle\lesssim Q^{p-1}\{|N_{z}(Q^{-1}f)||\partial_{x}f-\partial_{x}g|+|N_{z}(Q^{-1}f)-N_{z}(Q^{-1}g)||\partial_{x}g|\}

\displaystyle\lesssim(Q^{p-2}|f|+|f|^{p-1})|\partial_{x}f-\partial_{x}g|+|f-g|(Q^{p-2}+|f|^{p-2}+|g|^{p-2}))|\partial_{x}g|.

(v) can be estimated in the similar way to (iii). We omit the calculation.

As a consequence, we get

\displaystyle\|(i)\|_{L_{t}^{1}L^{2}}\lesssim\|f-g\|_{L^{\infty}L^{2}}(\|f\|_{L^{\infty}H^{1}}+\|g\|_{L^{\infty}H^{1}}+\|f\|_{L^{\infty}H^{1}}^{p-1}+\|g\|_{L^{\infty}H^{1}}^{p-1}),

\displaystyle\|(ii)\|_{L_{t}^{1}L^{2}}+\|(iv)\|_{L_{t}^{1}L^{2}}\lesssim\|f-g\|_{L^{\infty}L^{2}}(\|f\|_{L^{\infty}H^{1}}+\|g\|_{L^{\infty}H^{1}}+\|f\|_{L^{\infty}H^{1}}^{p-1}+\|g\|_{L^{\infty}H^{1}}^{p-1}),

and

\displaystyle\|(iii)\|_{L_{t}^{1}L^{2}}+\|(v)\|_{L_{t}^{1}L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim(\|f\|_{L^{\infty}H^{1}}+\|f\|_{L^{\infty}H^{1}}^{p-1})\|\partial_{x}f-\partial_{x}g\|_{L^{\infty}L^{2}}

\displaystyle\quad+\|f-g\|_{L^{\infty}L^{\infty}}(1+\|f\|_{L^{\infty}H^{1}}^{p-2}+\|g\|_{L^{\infty}H^{1}}^{p-2})\|\partial_{x}g\|_{L^{\infty}L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f-g\|_{L^{\infty}H^{1}}(\|f\|_{L^{\infty}H^{1}}+\|f\|_{L^{\infty}H^{1}}^{p-1}+\|g\|_{L^{\infty}H^{1}}+\|g\|_{L^{\infty}H^{1}}^{p-1}).

Combining all the above estimates, we get

\displaystyle\|\partial_{x}(R(f)-R(g))\|_{L^{1}L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f-g\|_{L^{\infty}H^{1}}(\|f\|_{L^{\infty}H^{1}}+\|f\|_{L^{\infty}H^{1}}^{p-1}+\|g\|_{L^{\infty}H^{1}}+\|g\|_{L^{\infty}H^{1}}^{p-1}).

This estimate and the estimate for the
L_{t}^{1}L^{2}
-norm finish the proof.
∎

3.
Construction of the family of special solutions

In this section, we prove the existence of the family of the special solutions. We first construct an approximate solution. Secondly, we show the existence of the special solutions by contraction mapping principle.

3.1.
Approximate solutions

To estimate the error term, we define the following notion.

Definition 3.1
.

We say that
f(t,x)=O(g(t))
as
t\to\infty
in
\mathscr{S}^{1}(\mathbb{R})
if it satisfies

\displaystyle\limsup_{t\to\infty}\frac{\|x^{\beta}\partial_{x}^{\alpha}f(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}}{g(t)}<C_{\alpha,\beta}

for
\alpha=0,1
and
\beta\in\mathbb{N}
.

We construct an approximate solution.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
A\in\mathbb{R}
. There exists a sequence
\{Z_{k}^{A}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
of functions in
\mathcal{D}_{{\mathord{\rm even}}}\cap\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}
such that
Z_{1}^{A}=A\mathcal{Y}_{+}
and, if
k\geq 1
and
\mathcal{V}_{k}^{A}=\sum_{j=1}^{k}e^{-je_{\omega}t}Z_{j}^{A}
, then we have

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\mathcal{V}_{k}^{A}+\mathcal{L}_{\omega,\gamma}\mathcal{V}_{k}^{A}=iR(\mathcal{V}_{k}^{A})+O(e^{-(k+1)e_{\omega}t})

(3.1)

as
t\to\infty
in
\mathscr{S}^{1}(\mathbb{R})
.

Proof.

We use an induction argument. First, we define
Z_{1}^{A}:=A\mathcal{Y}_{+}\in\mathcal{D}_{\mathord{\rm even}}\cap\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}
and
\mathcal{V}_{1}^{A}:=e^{-e_{\omega}t}Z_{1}^{A}
. Then it clearly holds that

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\mathcal{V}_{1}^{A}+\mathcal{L}_{\omega,\gamma}\mathcal{V}_{1}^{A}-iR(\mathcal{V}_{1}^{A})=-e_{\omega}e^{-e_{\omega}t}A\mathcal{Y}_{+}+Ae^{-e_{\omega}t}\mathcal{L}_{\omega,\gamma}\mathcal{Y}_{+}-iR(\mathcal{V}_{1}^{A})=-iR(\mathcal{V}_{1}^{A})

by
\mathcal{L}_{\omega,\gamma}\mathcal{Y}_{+}=e_{\omega}\mathcal{Y}_{+}
. Therefore, it is enough to show
iR(\mathcal{V}_{1}^{A})=O(e^{-2e_{\omega}t})
as
t\to\infty
in
\mathscr{S}^{1}
.
Now, we have
R(f)=Q_{\omega,\gamma}^{p}N(Q_{\omega,\gamma}^{-1}f)
, where
N(z)=|1+z|^{p-1}(1+z)-1-\frac{p+1}{2}z-\frac{p-1}{2}\overline{z}
. Since
N
is real-analytic in the open disc
\{z:|z|<1\}
and
N(0)=\partial_{z}N(0)=\partial_{\overline{z}}N(0)=0
, it follows from the Taylor expansion that

\displaystyle N(z)=\sum_{l+m\geq 2}a_{lm}z^{l}\overline{z}^{m}.

The convergence of the series and the derivatives is uniform on the compact disc
\{z:|z|\leq 1/2\}
. We know that
\|Q_{\omega,\gamma}^{-1}e^{\eta|x|}\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\mathcal{Y}_{+}\|_{L^{\infty}}<\infty
for
\alpha=0,1
by Lemma
2.18
. We note that we also have
\|Q_{\omega,\gamma}^{-1}e^{\eta|x|}\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\mathcal{L}_{\omega,\gamma}\mathcal{Y}_{+}\|_{L^{\infty}}<\infty
for
\alpha=0,1
.
By taking large
t_{1}>0
, we get

\displaystyle\|Q_{\omega,\gamma}^{-1}e^{\eta|x|}\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\mathcal{V}_{1}^{A}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\frac{1}{2},

for
t\geq t_{1}
. This ensures the above Taylor expansion and thus we obtain

\displaystyle|N(Q_{\omega,\gamma}^{-1}\mathcal{V}_{1}^{A})|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{l+m\geq 2}|a_{lm}||Q_{\omega,\gamma}^{-1}\mathcal{V}_{1}^{A}|^{l+m}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sum_{l+m\geq 2}\frac{|a_{lm}|}{2^{l+m-2}}\right)|Q_{\omega,\gamma}^{-1}\mathcal{V}_{1}^{A}|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C|Q_{\omega,\gamma}^{-1}\mathcal{V}_{1}^{A}|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq Ce^{-2e_{\omega}t}A^{2}e^{-2\eta|x|}\|Q_{\omega,\gamma}^{-1}e^{\eta|x|}\mathcal{Y}_{+}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq Ce^{-2e_{\omega}t}e^{-2\eta|x|}.

Thus we get

\displaystyle|R(\mathcal{V}_{1}^{A})|\leq\|Q_{\omega,\gamma}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{p}|N(Q_{\omega,\gamma}^{-1}\mathcal{V}_{1}^{A})|\lesssim e^{-2e_{\omega}t}e^{-2\eta|x|}.

Next, we consider the estimate of the derivative. For
x\neq 0
, we have

\displaystyle\partial_{x}R(\mathcal{V}_{1}^{A})

\displaystyle=p(\partial_{x}Q_{\omega,\gamma})Q_{\omega,\gamma}^{p-1}N(Q_{\omega,\gamma}^{-1}\mathcal{V}_{1}^{A})

\displaystyle\quad+Q_{\omega,\gamma}^{p}\{N_{z}(Q_{\omega,\gamma}^{-1}\mathcal{V}_{1}^{A})+N_{\overline{z}}(Q_{\omega,\gamma}^{-1}\mathcal{V}_{1}^{A})\}((\partial_{x}Q_{\omega,\gamma})Q_{\omega,\gamma}^{-2}\mathcal{V}_{1}^{A}+Q_{\omega,\gamma}^{-1}\partial_{x}\mathcal{V}_{1}^{A})

and thus, by Lemma
2.16
,

\displaystyle|\partial_{x}R(\mathcal{V}_{1}^{A})|

\displaystyle\lesssim\|Q_{\omega,\gamma}^{-1}\partial_{x}Q_{\omega,\gamma}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|Q_{\omega,\gamma}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{p}|N(Q_{\omega,\gamma}^{-1}\mathcal{V}_{1}^{A})|

\displaystyle+\|Q_{\omega,\gamma}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{p}\{|N_{z}(Q_{\omega,\gamma}^{-1}\mathcal{V}_{1}^{A})|+|N_{\overline{z}}(Q_{\omega,\gamma}^{-1}\mathcal{V}_{1}^{A})|\}

\displaystyle\quad\times e^{-\eta|x|}(\|Q_{\omega,\gamma}^{-1}\partial_{x}Q_{\omega,\gamma}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|Q_{\omega,\gamma}^{-1}e^{\eta|x|}\mathcal{V}_{1}^{A}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|Q_{\omega,\gamma}^{-1}e^{\eta|x|}\partial_{x}\mathcal{V}_{1}^{A}\|_{L^{\infty}}).

Now we have

\displaystyle|N_{z}(Q_{\omega,\gamma}^{-1}\mathcal{V}_{1}^{A})|+|N_{\overline{z}}(Q_{\omega,\gamma}^{-1}\mathcal{V}_{1}^{A})|\lesssim|Q_{\omega,\gamma}^{-1}\mathcal{V}_{1}^{A}|\lesssim e^{-e_{\omega}t}e^{-\eta|x|}

and thus

\displaystyle|\partial_{x}R(\mathcal{V}_{1}^{A})|\lesssim e^{-2e_{\omega}t}e^{-2\eta|x|}.

These show that
\|x^{\beta}\partial_{x}^{\alpha}R(\mathcal{V}_{1}^{A})\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim e^{-2e_{\omega}t}
for
\alpha=0,1
and
\beta\in\mathbb{N}
. Hence, we get (
3.1
) for
k=1
.

Secondly, let
k\geq 1
and we assume that
Z_{j}^{A}\in\mathcal{D}_{\mathord{\rm even}}\cap\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}
(
j=1,...,k
) are defined and
\mathcal{V}_{j}^{A}=\sum_{l=1}^{j}e^{-le_{\omega}t}Z_{l}^{A}
satisfies (
3.1
) for any
j\leq k
. Moreover, we assume that

\displaystyle\|Q_{\omega,\gamma}^{-1}e^{\eta|x|}\partial_{x}^{\alpha}Z_{j}^{A}\|_{L^{\infty}}<\infty,

(3.2)

\displaystyle\|Q_{\omega,\gamma}^{-1}e^{\eta|x|}\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\mathcal{L}_{\omega,\gamma}Z_{j}^{A}\|_{L^{\infty}}<\infty,

(3.3)

for
\alpha=0,1
and all
j\leq k
.
We define

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{k}:=\partial_{t}\mathcal{V}_{k}^{A}+\mathcal{L}_{\omega,\gamma}\mathcal{V}_{k}^{A}-iR(\mathcal{V}_{k}^{A}).

Since we have

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\mathcal{V}_{k}^{A}=\partial_{t}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{k}e^{-je_{\omega}t}Z_{j}^{A}\right)=\sum_{j=1}^{k}(-je_{\omega})e^{-je_{\omega}t}Z_{j}^{A},

we obtain

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{k}=\sum_{j=1}^{k}e^{-je_{\omega}t}(-je_{\omega}Z_{j}^{A}+\mathcal{L}_{\omega,\gamma}Z_{j}^{A})-iR(\mathcal{V}_{k}^{A}).

Since it holds that
\mathcal{V}_{k}^{A}=\sum_{j=1}^{k}e^{-je_{\omega}t}Z_{j}^{A}
and
\|Q_{\omega,\gamma}^{-1}e^{\eta|x|}\partial_{x}^{\alpha}Z_{j}^{A}\|_{L^{\infty}}<\infty
for
\alpha=0,1
and
j\leq k
, we have

\displaystyle\|Q_{\omega,\gamma}^{-1}e^{\eta|x|}\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\mathcal{V}_{k}^{A}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\sum_{j=1}^{k}e^{-je_{\omega}t}\|Q_{\omega,\gamma}^{-1}e^{\eta|x|}\partial_{x}^{\alpha}Z_{j}^{A}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\frac{1}{2}

for
t\geq t_{k}
, where
t_{k}
is sufficiently large.

\displaystyle R(\mathcal{V}_{k}^{A})

\displaystyle=Q_{\omega,\gamma}^{p}\sum_{l+m\geq 2}a_{lm}Q_{\omega,\gamma}^{-(l+m)}(\mathcal{V}_{k}^{A})^{l}\overline{\mathcal{V}_{k}^{A}}^{m}

\displaystyle=Q_{\omega,\gamma}^{p}\sum_{l+m\geq 2}a_{lm}Q_{\omega,\gamma}^{-(l+m)}

\displaystyle\quad\times\sum_{\mathit{\Delta}}c_{\mathit{\Delta}}e^{-(\sum_{j=1}^{k}jl_{j})e_{\omega}t}e^{-(\sum_{j=1}^{k}jm_{j})e_{\omega}t}(Z_{1}^{A})^{l_{1}}\cdots(Z_{k}^{A})^{l_{k}}\overline{(Z_{1}^{A})^{m_{1}}\cdots(Z_{k}^{A})^{m_{k}}},

where
\sum_{\mathit{\Delta}}
denotes the summation on the divisions of
l
and
m
, that is,
\sum_{\mathit{\Delta}}=\sum_{l_{1}+...+l_{k}=l,m_{1}+...+m_{k}=m}
, and
c_{\mathit{\Delta}}=\frac{l!}{l_{1}!...l_{k}!}\frac{m!}{m_{1}!...m_{k}!}
.

Now we have
Z_{j}^{A}\in\mathcal{D}_{\mathord{\rm even}}\cap\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}
and
\mathcal{L}_{\omega,\gamma}Z_{j}^{A}\in L^{2}_{\mathord{\rm even}}\cap\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}
for any
j=1,...,k
. We also have
Q_{\omega,\gamma}^{p}Q_{\omega,\gamma}^{-(l+m)}(Z_{1}^{A})^{l_{1}}\cdots(Z_{k}^{A})^{l_{k}}\overline{(Z_{1}^{A})^{m_{1}}\cdots(Z_{k}^{A})^{m_{k}}}\in L^{2}_{\mathord{\rm even}}\cap\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}
, where
l_{1}+\cdots+l_{k}=l
,
m_{1}+\cdots+m_{k}=m
, and
l,m\in\mathbb{N}
.
Moreover, by the induction’s assumption, we have (
3.2
), (
3.3
), and

\displaystyle\|Q_{\omega,\gamma}^{-1}e^{\eta(l+m)|x|}\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\{Q_{\omega,\gamma}^{p}Q_{\omega,\gamma}^{-(l+m)}(Z_{1}^{A})^{l_{1}}\cdots(Z_{k}^{A})^{l_{k}}\overline{(Z_{1}^{A})^{m_{1}}\cdots(Z_{k}^{A})^{m_{k}}}\}\|_{L^{\infty}}<\infty.

These give us that

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{k}=\sum_{j=1}^{k+1}e^{-je_{\omega}t}F_{j}(x)+O(e^{-(k+2)e_{\omega}t})

(3.4)

as
t\to\infty
in
\mathscr{S}^{1}
, where
F_{j}\in L^{2}_{\mathord{\rm even}}\cap\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}
and it satisfies

\displaystyle\|Q_{\omega,\gamma}^{-1}e^{\eta|x|}\partial_{x}^{\alpha}F_{j}\|_{L^{\infty}}<\infty.

(3.5)

Since
\mathcal{V}_{k}^{A}
satisfies (
3.1
) by assumption, we must have
F_{j}=0

for
j=1,...,k
. Hence we have

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{k}=e^{-(k+1)e_{\omega}t}F_{k+1}(x)+O(e^{-(k+2)e_{\omega}t}).

Since
(k+1)e_{\omega}
is not the spectrum of
\mathcal{L}_{\omega,\gamma}
,
Z_{k+1}^{A}:=(-\mathcal{L}_{\omega,\gamma}+(k+1)e_{\omega})^{-1}F_{k+1}
is well-defined. We have
Z_{k+1}^{A}\in\mathcal{D}_{\mathord{\rm even}}\cap\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}
by definition. By Lemma
2.19
, we also have

\displaystyle\|Q_{\omega,\gamma}^{-1}e^{\eta|x|}\partial_{x}^{\alpha}Z_{k+1}^{A}\|_{L^{\infty}}<\infty.

(3.6)

Moreover, since
\mathcal{L}_{\omega,\gamma}Z_{k+1}^{A}=(k+1)e_{\omega}Z_{k+1}^{A}-F_{k+1}
, by (
3.6
) and (
3.5
), we also have

\displaystyle\|Q_{\omega,\gamma}^{-1}e^{\eta|x|}\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\mathcal{L}_{\omega,\gamma}Z_{k+1}^{A}\|_{L^{\infty}}<\infty.

We define
\mathcal{V}_{k+1}^{A}:=\mathcal{V}_{k}^{A}+e^{-(k+1)e_{\omega}t}Z_{k+1}^{A}
. Then, by the definition, we have

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{k+1}

\displaystyle=\partial_{t}\mathcal{V}_{k}^{A}+\mathcal{L}_{\omega,\gamma}\mathcal{V}_{k}^{A}-e^{-(k+1)e_{\omega}t}F_{k+1}-iR(\mathcal{V}_{k+1}^{A})

\displaystyle=\varepsilon_{k}-e^{-(k+1)e_{\omega}t}F_{k+1}+i\{R(\mathcal{V}_{k}^{A})-R(\mathcal{V}_{k+1}^{A})\}.

By (
3.4
), we have
\varepsilon_{k}-e^{-(k+1)e_{\omega}t}F_{k+1}=O(e^{-(k+2)e_{\omega}t})
as
t\to\infty
in
\mathscr{S}^{1}(\mathbb{R})
. We can show that
R(\mathcal{V}_{k}^{A})-R(\mathcal{V}_{k+1}^{A})=O(e^{-(k+2)e_{\omega}t})
as
t\to\infty
in
\mathscr{S}^{1}(\mathbb{R})
. Indeed, in a similar way to Lemma
2.22
, it holds that

\displaystyle\|x^{\beta}(R(f)-R(g))\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq C(\|f\|_{W^{1,\infty}},\|g\|_{W^{1,\infty}})\|x^{\beta}(f-g)\|_{L^{\infty}},

\displaystyle\|x^{\beta}\partial_{x}(R(f)-R(g))\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq C(\|f\|_{W^{1,\infty}},\|g\|_{W^{1,\infty}})

\displaystyle\quad\times(\|x^{\beta}(f-g)\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|x^{\beta}\partial_{x}(f-g)\|_{L^{\infty}}),

where
C(a,b)
is a
C^{1}
-function of
a,b
and
C(0,0)=0
. Since
\mathcal{V}_{k+1}^{A}-\mathcal{V}_{k}^{A}=e^{-(k+1)e_{\omega}t}Z_{k+1}^{A}
and
\|\mathcal{V}_{k}^{A}\|_{W^{1,\infty}}+\|\mathcal{V}_{k+1}^{A}\|_{W^{1,\infty}}\lesssim e^{-e_{\omega}t}
, we have
R(\mathcal{V}_{k}^{A})-R(\mathcal{V}_{k+1}^{A})=O(e^{-(k+2)e_{\omega}t})
. This completes the proof.
∎

3.2.
Existence by contraction mapping theorem

Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
and let
t_{k}>0
be sufficiently large.
We define a norm

\displaystyle\|h\|_{\mathcal{E}}

4.
Non-scattering solutions converge to the ground state

In this section, we show that a global non-scattering solution on the threshold goes to the ground state solution.

4.1.
Modulation

Lemma 4.1
.

There exist
\mu_{0}>0
and a function
\varepsilon:(0,\mu_{0})\to(0,\infty)
with
\varepsilon(\mu)\to 0
as
\mu\to 0
such that, for all
f\in H_{{\mathord{\rm even}}}^{1}(\mathbb{R})
satisfying
M(f)=M(Q_{\omega,\gamma})
,
E_{\gamma}(f)=E_{\gamma}(Q_{\omega,\gamma})
and
|\mu(f)|\leq\mu_{0}
, there exists a
C^{1}
-function
\theta=\theta(f)
such that

\displaystyle\|e^{-i\theta}f-Q_{\omega,\gamma}\|_{H^{1}}\leq\varepsilon(\mu)

and

\displaystyle\im\int_{\mathbb{R}}gQ_{\omega,\gamma}dx=0,

where
g=e^{-i\theta}f-Q_{\omega,\gamma}
.

Proof.

First, we will show the statement when
f
is close to
Q_{\omega,\gamma}
.
We define

\displaystyle G(\theta,v):=\im\int_{\mathbb{R}}(e^{-i\theta}v-Q_{\omega,\gamma})Q_{\omega,\gamma}dx

for
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
and
v\in H_{{\mathord{\rm even}}}^{1}(\mathbb{R})
. Then we have
G(0,Q_{\omega,\gamma})=0
. Moreover, we also have

\displaystyle\frac{\partial G}{\partial\theta}(0,Q_{\omega,\gamma})=-\|Q_{\omega,\gamma}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\neq 0

Therefore, by the implicit function theorem, we get a
C^{1}
function
\theta
defined on a neighborhood of
Q_{\omega,\gamma}
such that

\displaystyle\im\int_{\mathbb{R}}(e^{-i\theta(v)}v-Q_{\omega,\gamma})Q_{\omega,\gamma}dx=0.

If
f
is a function satisfying the assumption, we can take
\theta_{0}
such that
\widetilde{f}:=e^{-i\theta_{0}}f-Q_{\omega,\gamma}
and
\|\widetilde{f}\|_{H^{1}}\leq\widetilde{\varepsilon}(\mu)
, where
\widetilde{\varepsilon}(\mu)\to 0
as
\mu\to 0
. Namely,
e^{-i\theta_{0}}f
is close to
Q_{\omega,\gamma}
. The implicit function theorem ensures the uniqueness of
\theta
and the regularity of the map. We get the statement.
∎

Let
u
be an even solution satisfying the mass-energy condition

\displaystyle M(u)=M(Q_{\omega,\gamma})\text{ and }E_{\gamma}(u)=E_{\gamma}(Q_{\omega,\gamma}).

We set
I_{\mu_{0}}:=\{t\in I_{\max}:|\mu(u(t))|<\mu_{0}\}
, where
I_{\max}
denotes the maximal existence time interval. By Lemma
4.1
, we obtain a
C^{1}
-function
\widetilde{\theta}=\widetilde{\theta}(t)
satisfying the statement of Lemma
4.1
for
t\in I_{\mu_{0}}
. Set
\theta:=\widetilde{\theta}-\omega
and

\displaystyle u(t,x)

\displaystyle=e^{i\theta(t)+i\omega t}(Q_{\omega,\gamma}+g(t,x))

\displaystyle=e^{i\theta(t)+i\omega t}(Q_{\omega,\gamma}+\rho(t)Q_{\omega,\gamma}+h(t,x)),

where

\displaystyle\rho(t):=\frac{\int_{\mathbb{R}}(e^{-i\theta(t)-i\omega t}u-Q_{\omega,\gamma})Q_{\omega,\gamma}^{p}dx}{\|Q_{\omega,\gamma}\|_{L^{p+1}}^{p+1}}=\frac{\int_{\mathbb{R}}gQ_{\omega,\gamma}^{p}dx}{\|Q_{\omega,\gamma}\|_{L^{p+1}}^{p+1}}.

Then
h
satisfies the following orthogonality condition:

\displaystyle\im\int_{\mathbb{R}}hQ_{\omega,\gamma}dx=\re\int_{\mathbb{R}}hQ_{\omega,\gamma}^{p}dx=0.

We give estimates of the parameters
\theta,\rho,g,h
.

Proposition 4.2

(Estimates of parameters)
.

We have

\displaystyle|\rho|\approx\|g\|_{H^{1}}\approx\|h\|_{H^{1}}\approx|\mu(u)|

for
t\in I_{\mu_{0}}
.

Lemma 4.3
.

We have

\displaystyle|\rho|\leq\|g\|_{L^{2}},

\displaystyle\|g\|_{H^{1}}\lesssim|\rho|+\|h\|_{H^{1}},

\displaystyle\|h\|_{H^{1}}\lesssim\|g\|_{H^{1}}+|\rho|\lesssim\|g\|_{H^{1}}.

Proof.

By the definition of
\rho
and the Hölder inequality, the first inequality is obtained.
The second inequality comes from
g=\rho Q_{\omega,\gamma}+h
.
The last one is obtained by
h=g-\rho Q_{\omega,\gamma}
and the first one.
∎

From this lemma, we may assume that
|\rho|
and
\|h\|_{H^{1}}
are sufficiently small since
\|g\|_{H^{1}}
is small for
t\in I_{\mu_{0}}
.

Proof of Proposition
4.2
.

Claim 1.

|\rho|\lesssim\|h\|_{H^{1}}
.

Since
M(u)=M(Q_{\omega,\gamma})
, we have

\displaystyle M(Q_{\omega,\gamma})=M(Q_{\omega,\gamma})+2\re\int_{\mathbb{R}}Q_{\omega,\gamma}(\rho Q_{\omega,\gamma}+h)dx+\|\rho Q_{\omega,\gamma}+h\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

and thus

\displaystyle 2\re\int_{\mathbb{R}}Q_{\omega,\gamma}(\rho Q_{\omega,\gamma}+h)dx+\|\rho Q_{\omega,\gamma}+h\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=0.

Thus this implies that

\displaystyle 2\rho\|Q_{\omega,\gamma}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+2\re\int_{\mathbb{R}}Q_{\omega,\gamma}hdx+\|\rho Q_{\omega,\gamma}+h\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=0

(4.1)

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\rho=-\frac{2\re\int_{\mathbb{R}}Q_{\omega,\gamma}hdx+\|\rho Q_{\omega,\gamma}+h\|_{L^{2}}^{2}}{2\|Q_{\omega,\gamma}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}}.

Therefore, we obtain

\displaystyle|\rho|\lesssim\|h\|_{L^{2}}+O(|\rho|^{2}+\|h\|_{L^{2}}^{2}).

Since
|\rho|
and
\|h\|_{L^{2}}
can be taken small, we get

\displaystyle|\rho|\lesssim\|h\|_{L^{2}}.

This finishes the proof of the claim.

Claim 2.

\|h\|_{H^{1}}\lesssim|\rho|
.

Since we have
S_{\omega,\gamma}(u)=S_{\omega,\gamma}(Q_{\omega,\gamma})
, by the Taylor expansion, we obtain

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=S_{\omega,\gamma}(Q_{\omega,\gamma}+g)-S_{\omega,\gamma}(Q_{\omega,\gamma})

\displaystyle=\langle S_{\omega,\gamma}^{\prime}(Q_{\omega,\gamma}),g\rangle+\frac{1}{2}\langle S_{\omega,\gamma}^{\prime\prime}(Q_{\omega,\gamma})g,g\rangle+o(\|g\|_{H^{1}}^{2}).

Since

For the second term, we have

\displaystyle\langle S_{\omega,\gamma}^{\prime\prime}(Q_{\omega,\gamma})g,g\rangle

\displaystyle=\rho^{2}\langle S_{\omega,\gamma}^{\prime\prime}(Q_{\omega,\gamma})Q_{\omega,\gamma},Q_{\omega,\gamma}\rangle+2\rho\langle S_{\omega,\gamma}^{\prime\prime}(Q_{\omega,\gamma})Q_{\omega,\gamma},h\rangle+\langle S_{\omega,\gamma}^{\prime\prime}(Q_{\omega,\gamma})h,h\rangle.

Thus we get

\displaystyle\langle S_{\omega,\gamma}^{\prime\prime}(Q_{\omega,\gamma})h,h\rangle

\displaystyle\leq-(\rho^{2}\langle S_{\omega,\gamma}^{\prime\prime}(Q_{\omega,\gamma})Q_{\omega,\gamma},Q_{\omega,\gamma}\rangle

\displaystyle\quad+2\rho\langle S_{\omega,\gamma}^{\prime\prime}(Q_{\omega,\gamma})Q_{\omega,\gamma},h\rangle)+o(\|g\|_{H^{1}}^{2})

\langle S_{\omega,\gamma}^{\prime\prime}(Q_{\omega,\gamma})h,h\rangle\gtrsim c\|h\|_{H^{1}}^{2}

\displaystyle\|h\|_{H^{1}}^{2}\lesssim\rho^{2}+|\rho|\|h\|_{H^{1}}+o(\|g\|_{H^{1}}^{2})

\displaystyle\|h\|_{H^{1}}^{2}\lesssim\rho^{2}+|\rho|\|h\|_{H^{1}}.

It follows from the Young inequality that

\displaystyle\|h\|_{H^{1}}^{2}\leq C\rho^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|h\|_{H^{1}}^{2}.

Thus it holds that

and this completes the proof of Claim 2.

Claim 3.

|\mu(u)|\approx|\rho|
.

We have

\displaystyle\mu(u)=-2\rho\|Q_{\omega,\gamma}\|_{\dot{H}_{\gamma}^{1}}^{2}-2(Q_{\omega,\gamma},h)_{\dot{H}_{\gamma}^{1}}+\|\rho Q_{\omega,\gamma}+h\|_{\dot{H}_{\gamma}^{1}}^{2}.

Since

is the solution of the elliptic equation and

, we get

\displaystyle-2\langle Q_{\omega,\gamma},h\rangle_{\dot{H}_{\gamma}^{1}}=2\omega\re\int_{\mathbb{R}}Q_{\omega,\gamma}hdx.

By (

), we obtain

\displaystyle 2\omega\re\int_{\mathbb{R}}Q_{\omega,\gamma}hdx=-2\rho\omega\|Q_{\omega,\gamma}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\omega\|\rho Q_{\omega,\gamma}+h\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Therefore, it holds that

\displaystyle\mu(u)=2(-\|Q_{\omega,\gamma}\|_{\dot{H}_{\gamma}^{1}}^{2}-\omega\|Q_{\omega,\gamma}\|_{L^{2}}^{2})\rho-\omega\|\rho Q_{\omega,\gamma}+h\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\rho Q_{\omega,\gamma}+h\|_{\dot{H}_{\gamma}^{1}}^{2}

and the coefficient

of

This means that

\displaystyle|\mu(u)|\lesssim|\rho|+o(|\rho|^{2}+\|h\|_{H^{1}}^{2})

and thus
|\mu(u)|\lesssim|\rho|
since we have
|\rho|\approx\|h\|_{H^{1}}
and they are small. Moreover, we also have

\displaystyle|\rho|\lesssim|\mu(u)|+o(|\rho|^{2}+\|h\|_{H^{1}}^{2})

and thus

. This completes the proof of Claim 3.

By combining those claims, the proof is done.
∎

Lemma 4.13
.

Let

with
\phi(0)=0
and

\displaystyle\left(\im\int_{\mathbb{R}}\partial_{x}\phi(x)\partial_{x}f(x)\overline{f(x)}dx\right)^{2}\lesssim|\mu(f)|^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\partial_{x}\phi(x)|^{2}|f(x)|^{2}dx.

Proof.

The proof is similar to
[
11
, Claim 5.4]
(see also
[
5
, Lemma 2.1]
and
[
7
, Claims A.2 and A.4]
). However, we give the proof since we use a different type of Gagliardo–Nirenberg inequality.

By the Gagliardo–Nirenberg type inequality with respect to
\delta
potential, Lemma
2.8
, we have

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{p+1}}^{2}=\|e^{i\lambda\phi}f\|_{L^{p+1}}^{2}\leq C_{\omega,\gamma}\|e^{i\lambda\phi}f\|_{H_{\omega,\gamma}^{1}}^{2}=C_{\omega,\gamma}(\|e^{i\lambda\phi}f\|_{\dot{H}_{\gamma}^{1}}^{2}+\omega\|f\|_{L^{2}}^{2})

for
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
.
Since we have

\displaystyle\|e^{i\lambda\phi}f\|_{\dot{H}_{\gamma}^{1}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\lambda^{2}\|(\partial_{x}\phi)f\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+2\lambda\im\int_{\mathbb{R}}(\partial_{x}\phi)\partial_{x}f\overline{f}dx+\|f\|_{\dot{H}_{\gamma}^{1}}^{2},

where we used
\phi(0)=0
, we get

\displaystyle\lambda^{2}\|(\partial_{x}\phi)f\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+2\lambda\im\int_{\mathbb{R}}(\partial_{x}\phi)\partial_{x}f\overline{f}dx+\|f\|_{H_{\omega,\gamma}^{1}}^{2}-C_{\omega,\gamma}^{-1}\|f\|_{L^{p+1}}^{2}\geq 0.

Since
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
is arbitrary, it must hold that

\displaystyle\left(\im\int_{\mathbb{R}}(\partial_{x}\phi)\partial_{x}f\overline{f}dx\right)^{2}\leq\|(\partial_{x}\phi)f\|_{L^{2}}^{2}(\|f\|_{H_{\omega,\gamma}^{1}}^{2}-C_{\omega,\gamma}^{-1}\|f\|_{L^{p+1}}^{2}).

We show that
\|f\|_{H_{\omega,\gamma}^{1}}^{2}-C_{\omega,\gamma}^{-1}\|f\|_{L^{p+1}}^{2}=O(\mu(f)^{2})
. By the definition of
\mu
and
M(f)=M(Q_{\omega,\gamma})
, we have

\displaystyle\|f\|_{H_{\omega,\gamma}^{1}}^{2}-C_{\omega,\gamma}^{-1}\|f\|_{L^{p+1}}^{2}=-\mu(f)+\|Q_{\omega,\gamma}\|_{H_{\omega,\gamma}^{1}}^{2}-C_{\omega,\gamma}^{-1}\|f\|_{L^{p+1}}^{2}.

By the energy condition
E_{\gamma}(f)=E(Q_{\omega,\gamma})
, it holds that

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{p+1}}^{p+1}=\frac{p+1}{2}\|f\|_{\dot{H}_{\gamma}^{1}}^{2}-E(Q_{\omega,\gamma})=\|Q_{\omega,\gamma}\|_{L^{p+1}}^{p+1}-\frac{p+1}{2}\mu(f).

Therefore, by the Taylor expansion
x^{\frac{2}{p+1}}=a^{\frac{2}{p+1}}+\frac{2}{p+1}a^{\frac{2}{p+1}-1}(x-a)+O(|x-a|^{2})
with

and

, we obtain

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{p+1}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\left(\|Q_{\omega,\gamma}\|_{L^{p+1}}^{p+1}-\frac{p+1}{2}\mu(f)\right)^{\frac{2}{p+1}}

\displaystyle=\|Q_{\omega,\gamma}\|_{L^{p+1}}^{2}-\|Q_{\omega,\gamma}\|_{L^{p+1}}^{-(p-1)}\mu(f)+O(|\mu(f)|^{2})

From these equalities and

\displaystyle-\mu(f)+\|Q_{\omega,\gamma}\|_{H_{\omega,\gamma}^{1}}^{2}-C_{\omega,\gamma}^{-1}\|f\|_{L^{p+1}}^{2}=O(|\mu(f)|^{2}).

This completes the proof.

Finally, we are ready to prove Theorem

.

Proof of Theorem
1.5
.

First, we prove that
(Q_{\omega,\gamma},\mathcal{Y}_{1})_{H_{\omega,\gamma}^{1}}\neq 0
.
Indeed, if not, by the equation of
Q_{\omega,\gamma}
, we have

\displaystyle B_{\omega,\gamma}(Q_{\omega,\gamma},\mathcal{Y}_{\pm})

\displaystyle=\frac{p+1}{2}(Q_{\omega,\gamma},\mathcal{Y}_{1})_{H_{\omega,\gamma}^{1}}=0

and thus we find
Q_{\omega,\gamma}\in\widetilde{G}^{\perp}
since we have

\displaystyle B_{\omega,\gamma}(Q_{\omega,\gamma},\mathcal{Y}_{\pm})=\frac{1}{2}(L_{\omega,\gamma}^{+}Q_{\omega,\gamma},\mathcal{Y}_{1})_{L^{2}}=\frac{1}{2}(Q_{\omega,\gamma},L_{\omega,\gamma}^{+}\mathcal{Y}_{1})_{L^{2}}=\frac{e_{\omega}}{2}(Q_{\omega,\gamma},\mathcal{Y}_{2})_{L^{2}}.

Now, we have

\displaystyle\Phi(Q_{\omega,\gamma})=-\frac{p-1}{2}\|Q_{\omega,\gamma}\|_{L^{p+1}}^{p+1}<0.

This contradicts the fact that
\Phi
is nonnegative on
\widetilde{G}^{\perp}
.
We may assume that
(Q_{\omega,\gamma},\mathcal{Y}_{1})_{H_{\omega,\gamma}^{1}}>0

replacing
\mathcal{Y}_{1}
by
-\mathcal{Y}_{1}
, that is,
\mathcal{Y}_{\pm}
by
\mathcal{Y}_{\mp}
, if necessary.

We define
U^{+}:=U^{1}
and
U^{-}:=U^{-1}
.
Since
e^{-i\omega t}U^{\pm}(t)\to Q
in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
, we have
M(U^{\pm})=M(Q_{\omega,\gamma})
and
E_{\gamma}(U^{\pm})=E_{\gamma}(Q_{\omega,\gamma})
. Moreover, we also have

\displaystyle\|U^{\pm}\|_{H_{\omega,\gamma}^{1}}^{2}=\|Q_{\omega,\gamma}\|_{H_{\omega,\gamma}^{1}}^{2}\pm 2e^{-2e_{\omega}t}(Q_{\omega,\gamma},\mathcal{Y}_{1})_{H_{\omega,\gamma}^{1}}+O(e^{-4e_{\omega}t}).

This shows that

\displaystyle\|U^{+}\|_{H_{\omega,\gamma}^{1}}^{2}-\|Q_{\omega,\gamma}\|_{H_{\omega,\gamma}^{1}}^{2}>0\text{ and }\|U^{-}\|_{H_{\omega,\gamma}^{1}}^{2}-\|Q_{\omega,\gamma}\|_{H_{\omega,\gamma}^{1}}^{2}<0

for large
t
. Since
\|U^{\pm}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=\|Q_{\omega,\gamma}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}
, this means that

\displaystyle\mu(U^{+}(t))<0\text{ and }\mu(U^{-}(t))>0

for large
t
. Since this sign is conserved by the flow, these inequalities hold for all existence time. These inequalities and the exponential convergence give that
U^{-}(t)
exists globally and scatters in the negative time direction by Proposition
4.5
and
U^{+}(t)
blows up in the negative time direction by Proposition
4.12
, where we note that
U^{+}
has finite variance by Lemma
5.7
. This completes the proof.
∎

Appendix A
Threshold condition

In this section, we consider the envelope of the family of the lines
\{(M,E):E+\frac{\omega}{2}M=r_{\omega,\gamma}\}_{\omega>0}
.
By the explicit formula of
Q_{\omega,\gamma}
, we have the following lemmas.

Lemma A.1
.

Let
\omega>\gamma^{2}/4
.
We have

\displaystyle Q_{\omega,\gamma}(x)=\omega^{\frac{1}{p-1}}Q_{1,\frac{\gamma}{\sqrt{\omega}}}(\sqrt{\omega}x).

Moreover, setting
\xi=\xi(\omega,\gamma):=\frac{2}{(p-1)\sqrt{\omega}}\tanh^{-1}\left(\frac{\gamma}{2\sqrt{\omega}}\right)
, we have

\displaystyle Q_{\omega,\gamma}(x)=\begin{cases}Q_{\omega,0}(x+\xi)&\text{ if }x\geq 0,\\
Q_{\omega,0}(x-\xi)&\text{ if }x<0.\end{cases}

Let
F(M,E,\omega):=E+\frac{\omega}{2}M-r_{\omega,\gamma}
for
M>0
,
E\in\mathbb{R}
, and
\omega>0
.
We define a family
\{C_{\omega}\}_{\omega>0}
of lines by

\displaystyle C_{\omega}:=\{(M,E)\in(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}:F(M,E,\omega)=0\}.

Let us define

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}(\omega):=\begin{cases}M(Q_{\omega,\gamma})&\text{if }\omega>\frac{\gamma^{2}}{4},\\
2M(Q_{\omega,0})&\text{if }0<\omega\leq\frac{\gamma^{2}}{4},\end{cases}\text{ and }\mathcal{E}(\omega):=\begin{cases}E_{\gamma}(Q_{\omega,\gamma})&\text{if }\omega>\frac{\gamma^{2}}{4},\\
2E_{0}(Q_{\omega,0})&\text{if }0<\omega\leq\frac{\gamma^{2}}{4}.\end{cases}

Then we have
F(\mathcal{M}(\omega),\mathcal{E}(\omega),\omega)=0
for all
\omega>0
.

Lemma A.2
.

The functions
\mathcal{M}
and
\mathcal{E}
are continuous on
(0,\infty)
.
\mathcal{M}
and
\mathcal{E}
are continuously differentiable on
(0,\gamma^{2}/4)\cup(\gamma^{2}/4,\infty)
.

Proof.

The continuous differentiability for
\omega>\gamma^{2}/4
follows from the composition of differentiable functions and for
\omega<\gamma^{2}/4
can be shown easily from the scaling.

We give a proof of the continuity of
\mathcal{M}
at
\omega=\gamma^{2}/4
. It is enough to show right continuity. For
\omega>\gamma^{2}/4
, we have

\displaystyle M(Q_{\omega,\gamma})=\omega^{-\frac{p-5}{2(p-1)}}M(Q_{1,\frac{\gamma}{\sqrt{\omega}}})

and

\displaystyle M(Q_{1,\frac{\gamma}{\sqrt{\omega}}})=2\int_{0}^{\infty}\left|Q_{1,0}(x+\xi(1,\frac{\gamma}{\sqrt{\omega}}))\right|^{2}dx=2\int_{\xi(1,\frac{\gamma}{\sqrt{\omega}})}^{\infty}\left|Q_{1,0}(x)\right|^{2}dx.

Thus, since
\xi(1,\frac{\gamma}{\sqrt{\omega}})\to-\infty
as
\omega\to\gamma^{2}/4
, it holds that

\displaystyle M(Q_{\omega,\gamma})\to\left(\frac{\gamma^{2}}{4}\right)^{-\frac{p-5}{2(p-1)}}2\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|Q_{1,0}(x)|^{2}dx=\mathcal{M}(\gamma^{2}/4)\text{ as }\omega\to\gamma^{2}/4.

Noting
|Q_{\omega,\gamma}(0)|^{2}=|Q_{\omega,0}(\xi)|^{2}\to 0
as
\omega\to\gamma^{2}/4
, we can show the continuity of
\mathcal{E}
by the same argument as above.
∎

Remark A.1
.

\mathcal{M}
is not differentiable at
\omega=\gamma^{2}/4
.

Proof of Remark
A.1
.

We show that
\mathcal{M}
is not differentiable at
\omega=\gamma^{2}/4
. Let
\omega>\gamma^{2}/4
. Setting

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}(\omega):=\begin{cases}M(Q_{1,\frac{\gamma}{\sqrt{\omega}}})&\text{if }\omega>\gamma^{2}/4,\\
2M(Q_{1,0})&\text{if }0<\omega\leq\gamma^{2}/4,\end{cases}

we have
\mathcal{M}(\omega)=\omega^{-\frac{p-5}{2(p-1)}}\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}(\omega)
. Thus it is enough to show that
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}
is not differentiable at
\omega=\gamma^{2}/4
. It holds that

\displaystyle\frac{\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}(\omega)-\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}(\gamma^{2}/4)}{\omega-\gamma^{2}/4}=-2\frac{\int_{-\infty}^{\xi(1,\frac{\gamma}{\sqrt{\omega}})}|Q_{1,0}(x)|^{2}dx}{\omega-\gamma^{2}/4}.

Since
Q_{1,0}(x)\approx e^{-|x|}
and
\xi<0
, we get

\displaystyle\frac{\int_{-\infty}^{\xi(1,\frac{\gamma}{\sqrt{\omega}})}|Q_{1,0}(x)|^{2}dx}{\omega-\gamma^{2}/4}\approx\frac{\int_{-\infty}^{\xi(1,\frac{\gamma}{\sqrt{\omega}})}e^{2x}dx}{\omega-\gamma^{2}/4}\approx\frac{e^{2\xi(1,\frac{\gamma}{\sqrt{\omega}})}}{4\omega-\gamma^{2}}.

Here, we have

\displaystyle e^{2\xi(1,\frac{\gamma}{\sqrt{\omega}})}=\left(\frac{1+\frac{\gamma}{2\sqrt{\omega}}}{1-\frac{\gamma}{2\sqrt{\omega}}}\right)^{\frac{2}{p-1}}=\left(\frac{4\omega-\gamma^{2}}{(2\sqrt{\omega}-\gamma)^{2}}\right)^{\frac{2}{p-1}}

since
\xi(1,\frac{\gamma}{\sqrt{\omega}})=\frac{2}{p-1}\tanh^{-1}\left(\frac{\gamma}{2\sqrt{\omega}}\right)
and
\tanh^{-1}x=\frac{1}{2}\log\left(\frac{1+x}{1-x}\right)
for
x\in(-1,1)
. Therefore, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}(\omega)-\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}(\gamma^{2}/4)}{\omega-\gamma^{2}/4}\approx(4\omega-\gamma^{2})^{\frac{2}{p-1}-1}\to\infty

as
\omega\to\gamma^{2}/4
when
p>5
.
∎

Lemma A.3
.

\partial_{\omega}\mathcal{M}(\omega)<0
for
\omega\in(0,\infty)\setminus\{\gamma^{2}/4\}
.

Proof.

The result in the case of
\omega>\gamma^{2}/4
is already known by Fukuizumi and Jeanjean
[
13
]
. In the case of
\omega<\gamma^{2}/4
, it is trivial by the scaling structure.
∎

Lemma A.4
.

\{\mathcal{M}(\omega):\omega>0\}=(0,\infty)
.

Proof.

We have
\mathcal{M}(\omega)\to\infty
as
\omega\to 0
. Since it holds that

\displaystyle M(Q_{\omega,\gamma})=\omega^{-\frac{p-5}{2(p-1)}}M(Q_{1,\frac{\gamma}{\sqrt{\omega}}})=2\omega^{-\frac{p-5}{2(p-1)}}\int_{\xi(1,\frac{\gamma}{\sqrt{\omega}})}^{\infty}\left|Q_{1,0}(x)\right|^{2}dx

and
\xi(1,\frac{\gamma}{\sqrt{\omega}})=\frac{2}{p-1}\tanh^{-1}\left(\frac{\gamma}{2\sqrt{\omega}}\right)\to 0
as
\omega\to\infty
, we get
\mathcal{M}(\omega)\to 0
.
∎

We set
\nu(\omega):=(\mathcal{M}(\omega),\mathcal{E}(\omega))
for simplicity.

Lemma A.5
.

We have
F(\nu(\omega),\omega)=0
for
\omega>0
and
(\partial_{\omega}F)(\nu(\omega),\omega)=0
for
\omega\in(0,\infty)\setminus\{\gamma^{2}/4\}
.

Proof.

The first statement is trivial. We prove the second statement. When
\omega>\gamma^{2}/4
, it holds that

\displaystyle(\partial_{\omega}F)(\nu(\omega),\omega)=\frac{1}{2}M(Q_{\omega,\gamma})-\partial_{\omega}r_{\omega,\gamma}=\frac{1}{2}M(Q_{\omega,\gamma})-\partial_{\omega}\{S_{\omega,\gamma}(Q_{\omega,\gamma})\}=0

since
Q_{\omega,\gamma}
is a solution of the elliptic equation
E_{\gamma}^{\prime}(Q_{\omega,\gamma})+\frac{\omega}{2}M^{\prime}(Q_{\omega,\gamma})=0
. When
\omega<\gamma^{2}/4
, it holds that

\displaystyle(\partial_{\omega}F)(\nu(\omega),\omega)=2\left\{\frac{1}{2}M(Q_{\omega,0})-\partial_{\omega}(S_{\omega,0}(Q_{\omega,0}))\right\}=0

since
Q_{\omega,0}
is a solution of the elliptic equation with
\gamma=0
.
∎

Lemma A.6
.

\{\nu(\omega):\omega>0\}
is the envelope of
\{C_{\omega}\}_{\omega>0}
and is a continuous curve. The curve is
C^{1}
except for
\omega=\gamma^{2}/4
.

Proof.

Let
G:=\begin{bmatrix}F(M,E,\omega)\\
\partial_{\omega}F(M,E,\omega)\end{bmatrix}
for
\omega\neq\gamma^{2}/4
. We note that
G
is
C^{1}
in
(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}\times(0,\infty)\setminus\{\gamma^{2}/4\}
. Now we have

\displaystyle\det\frac{\partial G}{\partial(M,E)}=\det\begin{bmatrix}\frac{\omega}{2}&1\\
\frac{1}{2}&0\end{bmatrix}\neq 0

and
G(\nu(\omega),\omega)=0
. By the implicit function theorem,
\nu(\omega)
is a unique
C^{1}
-function in
\omega\neq\gamma^{2}/4
satisfying
G(\nu(\omega),\omega)=0
.
∎

Since
\partial_{\omega}\mathcal{M}<0
for
\omega\neq\gamma^{2}/4
, there exists a function
\phi:(0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
such that
\mathcal{E}(\omega)=\phi(\mathcal{M}(\omega))

Lemma A.7
.

The function
\phi
is strictly convex.

Proof.

Since
\nu(\omega)
is a tangent point on

with the line

, we have
\phi^{\prime}(\mathcal{M}(\omega))=-\frac{\omega}{2}
. This means that
\phi^{\prime}
is increasing in

\partial_{\omega}\mathcal{M}<0
and
\omega=\mathcal{M}^{-1}
is decreasing in

\phi

By this convexity, we know that the line
C_{\omega}
is less than
E=\phi(M)
except for the tangent point
(\mathcal{M}(\omega),\mathcal{E}(\omega))
. This gives us the following lemma:

Lemma A.8
.

The following are equivalent.

(1).
f
satisfies
S_{\omega,\gamma}(f)\leq r_{\omega,\gamma}
for some
\omega>0
.

(2).
f
satisfies
S_{\omega^{\prime},\gamma}(f)<S_{\omega^{\prime},\gamma}(Q_{\omega^{\prime},\gamma})
for some
\omega^{\prime}>0
or
M(f)=M_{\omega}
and
E_{\gamma}(f)=E_{\omega}
for some
\omega>0

Corollary A.9
.

If
f
satisfies
S_{\omega,\gamma}(f)=r_{\omega,\gamma}
for some
\omega>\gamma^{2}/4
, then
f
satisfies
S_{\omega^{\prime},\gamma}(f)<r_{\omega^{\prime},\gamma}
for some
\omega^{\prime}>0
or

and
E_{\gamma}(f)=E_{\gamma}(Q_{\omega,\gamma})

By this corollary, in order to show the main theorem, it is enough to consider

and

Remark A.2

When

Appendix B
Cut-off Schwartz class

Lemma B.1
.

Let
f\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\})
. Then the following are equivalent:

(1)

There exists
\psi\in BC_{0}^{\infty}
with
\psi(x)=1
on
(-\infty,R)\cup(R,\infty)
for some
R>0
such that
\psi f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})
.

(2)

f\in\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}(\mathbb{R})
.

Proof.

It is trivial that (2) implies (1) since
\psi\in BC_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
. We show that (1) implies (2). Take an arbitrary function
\varphi\in BC_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
. We denote the interval including
0
and
\varphi=0
(resp.
\psi
) by
I_{\varphi}
(resp.
I_{\psi}
).
We take a function
\widetilde{\psi}\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
satisfying
\widetilde{\psi}(x)=1
on an interval
(-\infty,R)\cup(R,\infty)
including
\supp\varphi\cup\supp\psi
and
\widetilde{\psi}(x)=0
on an open interval included in
I_{\varphi}\cap I_{\psi}
. Then we have

\displaystyle\widetilde{\psi}f

\displaystyle=\widetilde{\psi}\psi f+\widetilde{\psi}(1-\psi)f=\psi f+\widetilde{\psi}(1-\psi)f.

Since
\psi f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})
by (1) and
\widetilde{\psi}(1-\psi)f
is smooth and compactly supported, we get
\widetilde{\psi}f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})
. It follows from this and
\varphi f=\varphi(\widetilde{\psi}f)

that
\varphi f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})
. Indeed, since
\varphi\in BC_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
and
\widetilde{\psi}f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})
, we have

\displaystyle\|x^{\beta}\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\{\varphi(\widetilde{\psi}f)\}\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim\sum_{k=0}^{\alpha}\|x^{\beta}\partial_{x}^{k}\varphi\partial_{x}^{\alpha-k}(\widetilde{\psi}f)\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim\sum_{k=0}^{\alpha}\|x^{\beta}\partial_{x}^{\alpha-k}(\widetilde{\psi}f)\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C_{\alpha,\beta}.

This completes the proof.
∎

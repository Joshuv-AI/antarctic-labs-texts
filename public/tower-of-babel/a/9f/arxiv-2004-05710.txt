Abstract
. In the present paper we propose some generalization of the topological Brauer group that includes higher homotopical information and contains the classical one as a direct summand. Our approach is based on some kind of bundle-like objects called “lax algebra bundles” which are glued from bundles by a weakened version of cocycles. The main results of the paper include the descripion of the homotopy type of their classifying space and a geometric description of elements [ X , BBU ⊗ ] [X,\,\mathop{\rm BBU}\nolimits_{\otimes}] that have finite order. We also propose a model of higher bundle gerbes and modules over them that give a geometric model of higher twisted complex K K -theory for twists of finite order.

Introduction

The Brauer group
first appeared in Algebra and Number Theory as an important invariant of an arbitrary field
k
.
Namely, it is the group of Morita equivalence classes of central simple algebras over
k

with respect to the operation induced by the tensor product of algebras.
Then A. Grothendieck,
using Azumaya algebras,
some generalizations of central simple algebras to
R

translated the notion of the Brauer group to schemes.
The birational invariance of the Brauer group found important applications in Algebraic Geometry:
for example, it was used to construct counterexamples to Lüroth’s problem.

The Brauer group can also be translated to the setting of Algebraic Topology
as the group of Morita equivalence classes of locally trivial matrix algebra bundles
(the last are natural counterpart of Azumaya algebras in this context).
There is a nice cohomological description
Br(X)\cong H^{3}_{tors}(X,\,\mathbb{Z})

(due to A. Grothendieck and J.-P. Serre,
[
18
]

all elements of the group

were interpreted as Morita equivalence classes of bundles with fibers

-algebra

of compact operators in a separable Hilbert space.

It was found out that the Brauer group

classifies twistings of the topological
K
-theory.
The general idea of twisted cohomology is the following: if a cohomology

of sections of the bundle
\mathcal{B}
. Obviously, the details are a bit
messy and probably best carried out in the context of higher

Our approach to the generalization of the Brauer group is based on some kind of bundle-like objects which we call “lax algebra bundles” (LABs for short) which are glued from trivial matrix algebra bundles
by a weakened version
of cocycle condition.
There is a natural transformation which assigns a LAB to a matrix algebra bundle (MAB).
For example, two MABs
A_{k},\,A_{k}^{\prime}
over
X
give rise to equivalent LABs iff there is a MAB

B_{kl^{m}}\rightarrow X
with fiber
M_{kl^{m}}(\mathbb{C})
(for some

A_{k}\rightarrow B_{kl^{m}}\leftarrow A^{\prime}_{k}.

We also describe the homotopy type
\lim\limits_{\longrightarrow\atop{n}}\mathop{\rm BFr}\nolimits_{k^{n},\,l^{\infty}}
of the classifying space of the
homotopy functor defined by equivalence classes of LABs.

There is an analogy between the group (with respect to the tensor product) of equivalence classes of
embedded bundles (embeddable bundles with a chosen embedding) and the Picard group consisting of (isomorphism classes of) line bundles.
Indeed, the first functor is represented by the
H
-space

\lim\limits_{\longrightarrow\atop{n}}\mathop{\rm Gr}\nolimits_{k^{n},\,l^{n}}\cong\mathop{\rm BSU}\nolimits_{\otimes}
while the Picard group
by
\mathop{\rm BU}\nolimits(1)\simeq K(\mathbb{Z},\,2)
and both are factors of

\mathop{\rm BU}\nolimits_{\otimes}\cong\mathop{\rm BU}\nolimits(1)\times\mathop{\rm BSU}\nolimits_{\otimes}.

So it is natural to conjecture that there is also the corresponding Brauer group counterpart.
In order to develop this analogy we need
a natural transformation from the functor
MAB

(which assigns to a space the set of equivalence classes
of MABs) that takes embeddable bundles
to some “trivial” class. The assignment
to a MAB

the corresponding LAB
satisfies this property.

This allows us to define the
k

Generalized Brauer group consists of equivalence classes of
LABs modulo those that come from MABs of the form
{\rm End}(\xi_{k^{n}})
for some

\mathbb{C}^{k^{n}}
-vector bundle
\xi_{k^{n}}\rightarrow X
.
The classical topological Brauer group is a direct summand of the generalized one.
It can be shown that this approach describes
all finite order higher twistings of the
complex
K

description of elements in
[X,\,\mathop{\rm BBU}\nolimits_{\otimes}]

that have finite order.

2.2 Definition
.

The

Equivalently,

The

2.3 Theorem
.

(Grothendieck-Serre,
[
18
]
)
There is the natural group isomorphism
{\rm Br}(X)\cong H^{3}_{tors}(X;\,\mathbb{Z})
.

In particular, for the
k

we have the isomorphism

{\rm Br}_{k}(X)\cong H^{3}_{k-tors}(X;\,\mathbb{Z}).

Let us give a sketch proof of the theorem. The homotopy functors
X\mapsto MAB(X)

and
X\mapsto MAB_{k^{\infty}}(X)
are representable by spaces

\lim\limits_{\longrightarrow\atop{k}}\mathop{\rm BPU}\nolimits(k)
and
\lim\limits_{\longrightarrow\atop{n}}\mathop{\rm BPU}\nolimits(k^{n})
respectively (recall that all maps
in the direct limits
are induced by the tensor product). The former one we denote by
\mathop{\rm BPU}\nolimits_{\mathbb{Q}},

the second one by
\mathop{\rm BPU}\nolimits(k^{\infty}).
After passing to the localization all the
Postnikov invariants vanish, so

\mathop{\rm BPU}\nolimits_{\mathbb{Q}}

is homotopy equivalent to
K(\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z};2)\times\prod_{n\geq 2}K(\mathbb{Q};2n).

Morita trivial classes are in the image of the map of represented spaces

\lim\limits_{\longrightarrow\atop{k}}\mathop{\rm BU}\nolimits(k)\rightarrow\lim\limits_{\longrightarrow\atop{k}}\mathop{\rm BPU}\nolimits(k),
i.e.
\mathop{\rm BU}\nolimits_{\mathbb{Q}}\rightarrow\mathop{\rm BPU}\nolimits_{\mathbb{Q}}

(whose homotopy fiber is
K(\mathbb{Z};2)
). Note that
\mathop{\rm BU}\nolimits_{\mathbb{Q}}\simeq\prod_{n\geq 1}K(\mathbb{Q};2n)

and the cokernel of the homomorphism
[X,\mathop{\rm BU}\nolimits_{\mathbb{Q}}]\rightarrow[X,\mathop{\rm BPU}\nolimits_{\mathbb{Q}}]
is isomorphic to

{\rm coker}\,\{H^{2}(X;\mathbb{Q})\rightarrow H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})\}\cong{\rm im}\,\{H^{2}(X;\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})\rightarrow H^{3}(X;\mathbb{Z})\}\cong H^{3}_{tors}(X;\mathbb{Z})
as claimed
(here all homomorphisms of cohomology groups are induced by the exact sequence
of coefficients
0\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}\rightarrow\mathbb{Q}\rightarrow\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}\rightarrow 0
).

5.2 Remark
.

Morita autoequivalences of the trivial gerbe form the Picard group.
We see that in our case the Picard group is
GenPic(\ldots)\cong[\ldots,\mathop{\rm BU}\nolimits(l^{\infty})_{\otimes}]
, this implies that our construction
gives rise to twisted

l
(in the sense that

invertible).

Note that (as in the case of ordinary bundle gerbes)
we can not expect that every generalized bundle gerbe admits a
finite-dimensional module, some finite-order condition is
necessary.

7.3 Remark
.

We can give a more explicit description of the map
\beta_{k}.

Recall that in subsection
3.3
we reduced
the classification of LABs over suspensions
X=\Sigma Y

to the homotopy classification of clutching functions

\mu^{\prime}\colon(U\cap V)\times M_{k}(\mathbb{C})\rightarrow(U\cap V)\times M_{kl^{N}}(\mathbb{C})
(we use the notation from subsection
3.3
). The obstruction to lift a LAB to a MAB
is the non-triviality of the centralizer subbundle

Z(\mu^{\prime})\subset(U\cap V)\times M_{kl^{N}}(\mathbb{C})
. But after taking the tensor product
with
\widehat{M}_{l^{\infty}}
the centralizer trivializes
and the clutching function
\mu^{\prime}
lifts to a clutching function
(unique up to homotopy)
for a
\widehat{M}_{kl^{\infty}}
-bundle over
\Sigma Y
.

In fact, we have proven the following theorem.

7.4 Theorem
.

The natural transformation
\Theta_{k}\colon LAB_{k}\Rightarrow UHF_{k}

determines an isomorphism of functors on CW-complexes.

7.5 Corollary
.

For a
M_{k}(\mathbb{C})
-bundle
A_{k}\rightarrow X

the bundle
A_{k}\otimes\widehat{M}_{l^{\infty}}
is trivial
(as a bundle with the structure group

{\rm Aut}(\widehat{M}_{kl^{\infty}})
) iff
A_{k}

is embeddable.

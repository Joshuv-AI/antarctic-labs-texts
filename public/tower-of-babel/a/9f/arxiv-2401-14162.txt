A view toward homomorphisms and cv-polynomials between double Ore extensions Thanks: This work was supported by Faculty of Science, Universidad Nacional de Colombia - Sede Bogotá, Colombia [grant number 53880].
María Camila Ramírez, Armando Reyes

Abstract
. Motivated by the theory of homomorphisms and cv-polynomials of Ore extensions formulated by several mathematicians, the rol of double Ore extensions introduced by Zhang and Zhang in the classification of Artin-Schelter regular algebras of dimension four, and that there are no inclusions between the classes of all double Ore extensions of an algebra and of all length two iterated Ore extensions of the same algebra, our aim in this paper is to present a first approach toward a theory of homomorphisms and cv-polynomials between double Ore extensions. We obtain several results on the characterizations of cv-polynomials and their relations with inner derivations of the ring of coefficients of the double algebra, and show that the computation of homomorphisms corresponding to these polynomials is non-trivial. We illustrate our results with different examples including Nakayama automorphisms of trimmed double Ore extensions.

For

a commutative ring, Gilmer
[

determined all the automorphisms of the commutative polynomial ring
R[x]
which fix

elementwise. Parmenter
[
38
]
extended this result to the case where
R
is commutative and
\sigma
is any automorphism of
R
. The case where
\sigma
is the identity on any ring
R
was considered by Coleman and Enochs
[
13
]
and Brewer and Rutter
[
5
]
. These three results are corollaries of Rimmer’s paper
[
41
, Theorem 1]
, since for a unital ring (not necessarily commutative)
R
and
\sigma
any automorphism of
R
, he determined all the automorphisms of the Ore extension (introduced by Ore
[
36
,
37
]
) of automorphism type
R[x;\sigma]
that fix
R
elementwise (he called them
R
-linear maps). As an application of his results, he investigated isomorphisms between different skew polynomial rings which preserve an underlying ring isomorphism
[
41
, Theorem 3]
. On the other hand, Ferrero and Kishimoto
[
15
]
and Kikumasa
[
19
]
studied automorphisms of Ore extensions of derivation type
R[x;\delta]
. They discussed conditions on

for the
R
-linear map
R[x;\delta]\to R[x;\delta]
defined by
x^{k}\mapsto f(x)^{k}
to be a
R
-ring automorphism. Later, Lam and Leroy
[
20
]
studied “transformations” from one Ore extension of mixed type
D[x^{\prime};\sigma^{\prime},\delta^{\prime}]
to another extension
D[x;\sigma,\delta]
over a division ring
D
. They stated that a
D
-homomorphism
\phi
from

to

is determined by

must satisfy the condition

, for every

. Since the polynomial

allows us to make a “change of variables” (from
x^{\prime}
to

), they called
p(x)
a
change-of-variable polynomial
(or
cv-polynomial
for short) respect to the quasi-derivation
(\sigma^{\prime},\delta^{\prime})
on
D
. In their paper, they proved different results about these polynomials and their corresponding morphisms. Another important result establishes when two Ore extensions are isomorphic under certain conditions
[
20
, Theorem 5.6]
. Several mathematicians have investigated the topic of morphisms between noncommutative polynomial extensions (e.g.
[
1
,
4
,
9
,

,

,

,
25
,
26
,

,

,
33
,

,
40
,

,

]
, and references therein). In particular, recently the authors
[
39
]
have presented a first approach to homomorphisms and cv-polynomials of two-step, three-step, and

-step iterated Ore extensions.

On the other hand, in the setting of noncommutative algebras appearing in noncommutative geometry,
Artin-Schelter regular algebras
introduced by Artin And Schelter
[
2
]
are considered as noncommutative analogues of commutative polynomial rings due to its important role in noncommutative geometry. As one can appreciate in the literature, these algebras have been extensively studied (see the excellent survey on these algebras carried out by Rogalski
[
42
]
). With the aim of presenting new examples of Artin-Schelter regular algebras of dimension four, Zhang and Zhang
[
50
,
51
]
introduced algebra extensions which they called

and constructed 26 families of these algebras. Many regular of these algebras are new and are not isomorphic to either a normal extension or an Ore extension of an Artin-Schelter regular algebra of global dimension three. Rather than Ore extensions very few properties are known to be preserved under double Ore extension. Several researchers have investigated different relations of double Ore extensions with Poisson, Hopf, Koszul and Calabi-Yau algebras (e.g.
[

,

,

,

,

Nevertheless, there are no inclusions between the classes of all double Ore extensions of an algebra and of all length two iterated Ore extensions of the same algebra. Precisely, Carvalho et al.
[
7
]
formulated necessary and sufficient conditions for a double Ore extension to be presented as two-step iterated Ore extensions.

Taking into account the above and the developments formulated by Lam and Leroy
[
20
]
and Ramírez and Reyes
[

Notice that Condition (a)(iv) from Definition

\sigma=\begin{bmatrix}\sigma_{11}&\sigma_{12}\\
\sigma_{21}&\sigma_{22}\end{bmatrix}:R\to M_{2\times 2}(R)\quad{\rm and}\quad\delta=\begin{bmatrix}\delta_{1}\\

In this section, we present a first approach toward a theory of homomorphisms and cv-polynomials between right double Ore extensions. Consider the notation in Definition

Definition 3.1
.

Let
B^{\prime}=R_{P^{\prime}}[y^{\prime}_{1},y^{\prime}_{2};\sigma^{\prime},\delta^{\prime},\tau^{\prime}]
and
B=R_{P}[y_{1},y_{2};\sigma,\delta,\tau]
be right double extensions of
R
. Consider the map
\phi:B^{\prime}\to B
given by

\phi\left(\sum_{i,j}a_{i,j}y^{{}^{\prime}n(i)}_{1}y^{{}^{\prime}m(j)}_{2}\right)=\sum_{i,j}a_{i,j}q_{1}^{n(i)}q_{2}^{m(j)},\quad\text{with}\ q_{1},q_{2}\in B.

If we want
\phi
to be an
R
-homomorphism, it must satisfies

\phi(y_{2}^{\prime}y_{1}^{\prime})=\phi(y_{2}^{\prime})\phi(y_{1}^{\prime})=q_{2}q_{1},

and given the relation (
2.1
), necessarily

\displaystyle\phi(y^{\prime}_{2}y^{\prime}_{1})

\displaystyle=\phi(p^{\prime}_{12}y^{\prime}_{1}y^{\prime}_{2}+p^{\prime}_{11}y_{1}^{{}^{\prime}2}+\tau^{\prime}_{1}y^{\prime}_{1}+\tau^{\prime}_{2}y^{\prime}_{2}+\tau^{\prime}_{0})

In this way, the relation

(3.1)

q_{2}q_{1}=p^{\prime}_{12}q_{1}q_{2}+p^{\prime}_{11}q_{1}^{2}+\tau^{\prime}_{1}q_{1}+\tau^{\prime}_{2}q_{2}+\tau^{\prime}_{0}

must be satisfied. These facts motivate the following definition. Let
B^{\oplus 2}:=\begin{bmatrix}B\\
B\end{bmatrix}
.

Definition 3.2
.

Let
B^{\prime}=R_{P^{\prime}}[y^{\prime}_{1},y^{\prime}_{2};\sigma^{\prime},\delta^{\prime},\tau^{\prime}]
and
B=R_{P}[y_{1},y_{2};\sigma,\delta,\tau]
be right double extensions of
R
. We define the map

y^{\prime}_{2}\end{bmatrix}:=\begin{bmatrix}\phi(y^{\prime}_{1})\\

q_{2}\end{bmatrix},

\phi(y^{\prime}_{2})\end{bmatrix}r=\begin{bmatrix}q_{1}\\

Definition 3.3
.

Theorem 3.11
.

Let

Let

and

. We can find elements

dc\end{bmatrix},

q_{1}=c\in R
, and

g\end{bmatrix}:=\begin{bmatrix}\phi(f)\\

\displaystyle\phi(y_{2}y_{1})=q_{2}q_{1}=\lambda^{2}y_{2}y_{1}=-\lambda^{2}y_{1}y_{2}=p_{12}q_{1}q_{2}+p_{11}y_{1}=\phi(p_{12}y_{1}y_{2}+p_{11}y_{1}^{2}),

by Theorem
3.12
,
\begin{bmatrix}q_{1}\\
q_{2}\end{bmatrix}
is a dcv-matrix. Note also that

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}\lambda y_{1}x_{1}\\
\lambda y_{2}x_{1}\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}\lambda x_{1}y_{2}\\
\lambda x_{1}y_{1}\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}0&x_{1}\\
x_{1}&0\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}\lambda y_{1}\\
\lambda y_{2}\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}\sigma_{11}(x_{1})&\sigma_{12}(x_{1})\\
\sigma_{21}(x_{1})&\sigma_{22}(x_{1})\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}\lambda y_{1}\\
\lambda y_{2}\end{bmatrix},

and a simple calculation shows that the properties of the dcv-matrix are satisfied for every
a\in R_{P}
. By Theorem
3.11
, we get an isomorphism.

Example 3.14
.

From

\displaystyle y_{1}x_{2}

\displaystyle y_{2}x_{1}

\displaystyle y_{2}x_{2}

\displaystyle=x_{1}y_{1}+x_{2}y_{2},

where

\displaystyle x_{2}x_{1}

\displaystyle y_{2}y_{1}

\displaystyle=py_{1}y_{2},

\displaystyle y_{1}x_{1}

\displaystyle=x_{1}y_{2}+x_{2}y_{2},

\displaystyle y_{1}x_{2}

\displaystyle=x_{1}y_{2}-x_{2}y_{2},

\displaystyle y_{2}x_{1}

\displaystyle y_{2}x_{2}

\displaystyle=x_{1}y_{1}+x_{2}y_{1},

with

is the double Ore extension
R_{P}[y_{1},y_{2};\sigma,\delta,\tau]

Consider the homomorphism

\displaystyle\varphi:\mathbb{D}^{\oplus 2}

\displaystyle\ \to\mathbb{E}^{\oplus 2}

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}y^{\prime}_{1}\\
y^{\prime}_{2}\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle\ \mapsto\begin{bmatrix}\phi(y^{\prime}_{1})\\
\phi(y^{\prime}_{2})\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}q_{1}\\
q_{2}\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}x_{1}\\

If we suppose

\mathbb{D}
we have

and

\displaystyle=p^{\prime}_{12}x_{1}x_{2}+p^{\prime}_{11}x_{1}^{2}+\tau^{\prime}_{1}x_{1}+\tau^{\prime}_{2}x_{2}+\tau^{\prime}_{0}=-x_{1}x_{2},

whence

Theorem 3.15
.

Let
B^{\prime}=R_{P^{\prime}}[y^{\prime}_{1},y^{\prime}_{2};\sigma^{\prime},\delta^{\prime},\tau^{\prime}]
and
B=R_{P}[y_{1},y_{2};\sigma,\delta,\tau]
be right double extensions of the
\Bbbk
-algebra
R
. Consider the homomorphism
\varphi
between
B^{{}^{\prime}\oplus 2}
and
B^{\oplus 2}
as in Definition
3.2
. If the conditions

\displaystyle p^{\prime}_{12}\phi(y^{\prime}_{1})=\sigma^{\prime}_{2}(\phi(y^{\prime}_{1})),\quad p^{\prime}_{11}=0,\quad\tau^{\prime}_{1}\phi(y^{\prime}_{1})=\delta^{\prime}_{2}(\phi(y^{\prime}_{1})),\quad\tau^{\prime}_{2}=\tau^{\prime}_{0}=0,

\displaystyle\sigma^{\prime}_{11}(r)=\sigma^{\prime}_{1}(r),\quad\sigma^{\prime}_{22}(r)=\sigma^{\prime}_{2}(r),\quad\sigma^{\prime}_{12}(-)=\sigma^{\prime}_{21}(-)=0,

are satisfied, then
\varphi
can be presented as a homomorphism between iterated Ore extension.

Proof.

Consider the relation (
3.1
). If
p^{\prime}_{12}\phi(y^{\prime}_{1})=\sigma^{\prime}_{2}(\phi(y^{\prime}_{1})),\ p^{\prime}_{11}=0,\ \tau^{\prime}_{1}\phi(y^{\prime}_{1})=\delta^{\prime}_{2}(\phi(y^{\prime}_{1}))
, and
\tau^{\prime}_{2}=\tau^{\prime}_{0}=0
, then

\phi(y_{2}^{\prime}y_{1}^{\prime})=q_{2}q_{1}=\sigma^{\prime}_{2}(\phi(y^{\prime}_{1}))q_{2}+\delta^{\prime}_{2}(\phi(y^{\prime}_{1}))=\sigma^{\prime}_{2}(q_{1})q_{2}+\delta^{\prime}_{2}(q_{1}),

which is precisely the condition in the definition of cv-polynomials. On the other hand, condition (

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}q_{1}\\
q_{2}\end{bmatrix}r=

\displaystyle\ \begin{bmatrix}\sigma^{\prime}_{11}(r)&\sigma^{\prime}_{12}(r)\\
\sigma^{\prime}_{21}(r)&\sigma^{\prime}_{22}(r)\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}q_{1}\\
q_{2}\end{bmatrix}+\begin{bmatrix}\delta^{\prime}_{1}(r)\\
\delta^{\prime}_{2}(r)\end{bmatrix},

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}q_{1}\\
q_{2}\end{bmatrix}r=

\displaystyle\ \begin{bmatrix}\sigma^{\prime}_{1}(r)&0\\
0&\sigma^{\prime}_{2}(r)\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}q_{1}\\
q_{2}\end{bmatrix}+\begin{bmatrix}\delta^{\prime}_{1}(r)\\
\delta^{\prime}_{2}(r)\end{bmatrix},

or equivalently,

\displaystyle q_{1}r=\sigma^{\prime}_{1}(r)q_{1}+\delta^{\prime}_{1}(r),\quad{\rm and}\quad q_{2}r=\sigma^{\prime}_{2}(r)q_{2}+\delta^{\prime}_{2}(r),

which are precisely the other two conditions in

4.
Future work

As we said above, it is a pending task to exemplify dcv-matrices consisting of polynomials of degree two.

On the other hand,
skew PBW extensions
were introduced by Gallego and Lezama
[
16
]
with the aim of generalizing Ore extensions of injective type and PBW extensions defined by Bell and Goodearl
[
3
]
. Over the years, ring-theoretic, geometric and homological properties of the objects have been studied, and it has shown that these extensions generalize several families of noncommutative algebras (see
[
14
, Chapter 2]
and
[
35
, Section 2]
for more details). Suárez
[
44
]
defined a subclass of these extensions, the
graded skew PBW extensions
, and then, by using the comparison carried out by Carvalho et al.
[
7
]
, in
[
18
]
he presented necessary and sufficient conditions for a graded trimmed double Ore extension to be a graded skew PBW extension, and in fact, they proved the Artin-Schelter regularity and the property of being skew Calabi-Yau for graded skew PBW extensions. Having in mind these works and the theory established in this paper, a question arises whether it is possible to develop the theory of homomorphisms and cv-polynomials for graded skew PBW extensions, and if so, compare it with the results obtained here and those corresponding in
[
7
,
18
,
20
,
39
,
45
,
46
,
47
]
.

Last but not least, Lü et al.
[
32
]
proved that the universal enveloping algebra of a Poisson-Ore extension is a length two iterated Ore extension of the original universal enveloping algebra, and then showed that the Poisson enveloping algebra of a double Poisson-Ore extension is an iterated double Ore extension
[
31
]
. Related with this, Lou et al.
[
30
]
gave a definition of Poisson double extension - which may be considered as an analogue of double Ore extension -, and showed that algebras in a class of double Ore extensions are deformation quantizations of Poisson double extensions. These works together Zambrano’s paper
[
49
]
where he gave a description of Poisson brackets on some families of skew PBW extensions, motivate us to ask ourselves about the relations between all these algebras through the notion of homomorphism and cv-polynomial developed here.

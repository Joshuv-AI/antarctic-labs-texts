differential operators with singular coefficients. Our results cover

Contents of the paper

The first part contains mostly background material, but presented in

2.4.
Clean intersections

In order to study the iterate the blow-up construction in the next section, we will need

clean intersections,
which we recall next. For simplicity, we discuss only
the case of p-submanifolds.

Definition 2.11
.

Let
P
and
Q
be two p-submanifolds of a manifold with corners
M

such that
P\cap Q
is also a p-submanifold of
M
. We say that

P
and
Q

intersect
cleanly
or that
they have a clean intersection

if, for every
x\in P\cap Q
, we have
T_{x}(P\cap Q)=T_{x}P\cap T_{x}Q
.

For example, if
P
is a p-submanifold of
M
and
F
is a boundary face of
M
, then

F
and
P
have a clean intersection. This is not, however, the case, in general,
if
P
is just a
weak submanifold
of
M
. This explains, in particular,
the need to consider p-submanifolds. We next recall how to extend the
definition of clean intersection to semilattices of p-submanifolds.

Definition 2.12
.

Let
\mathcal{S}
be a finite semilattice of p-submanifolds of
M
.
We call
\mathcal{S}
a
clean semilattice of  p-submanifolds (of
M
)
if
any
P,Q\in\mathcal{S}
intersect cleanly.

Remark 2.13
.

If
\mathcal{S}
is a clean semilattice of p-submanifolds  then,
for all
P_{1},\ldots,P_{k}\in\mathcal{S}
and all
x\in\bigcap_{j=1}^{k}P_{j}
,
we obtain that

(12)

T_{x}\Big(\bigcap_{j=1}^{k}P_{j}\Big)\ =\ \bigcap_{j=1}^{k}T_{x}P_{j}\,.

In particular, our definition of clean semilattices coincides with
Definition 2.7 in
[
1
]
. This property does not hold anymore,
if
\mathcal{S}
is not a semilattice, as seen in the next example.

Example 2.14
.

Assume that
\mathcal{S}
is a set of p-submanifolds with
k
elements, such
that each pair
\{P_{i},P_{j}\}\subset\mathcal{S}
intersections cleanly, then we
cannot conclude, in general, that we have the property
expressed in Equation (
12
) for
k\geq 3
.
Indeed, let us consider the family consisting of the following
three surfaces
P_{1}
,
P_{2}
and
P_{3}
in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
(i.e.
2
-dimensional submanifolds of
\mathbb{R}^{3}
):

\begin{gathered}P_{1}\ \coloneqq\ \left\{(x,y,z)^{T}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\mid y=0\right\},\qquad P_{2}\ \coloneqq\ \left\{(x,y,z)^{T}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\mid z=0\right\}\,,\\
P_{3}\ \coloneqq\ \left\{(x,y,z)^{T}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\mid y+z=x^{2}\right\}\,.\end{gathered}

Each pair intersects cleanly in a
1
-dimensional submanifold. However
P_{1}\cap P_{2}\cap P_{3}=\{0\}
, but
T_{0}P_{1}\cap T_{0}P_{2}\cap T_{0}P_{3}=\mathbb{R}(1,0,0)^{T}
. Thus the triple
(P_{1},P_{2},P_{3})
does not intersect cleanly.

The following Lemma will provide the needed examples of pairs of cleanly intersecting
p-submanifolds.

Lemma 2.15
.

Let
Y,Z
be two linear subspaces of
X
. Then all subsets of

\{\overline{Y},\overline{Z},\mathbb{S}_{Y},\mathbb{S}_{Z}\}
intersect cleanly.

The proof is straightforward but involves many cases, so we relegate
it to Appendix
D
,
see Corollary
D.2
.

3.
Blow-ups

In this section we gather the needed results on blow-ups,
following the approach of
[
6
]
. Most of the results in this
section were discussed in a similar form in the literature, see

[
1
,
6
,
28
,
29
,
38
,
43
]
for more details.
In particular, the basic construction of the blow-up along a p-submanifold coincides
with the one in these references. However, the lifting (or pull-back) of submanifolds
differs in certain cases from the one used in, for instance,
[
30
,
38
,
43
]
. As a consequence, our notion of iterated blow-up exhibits some subtle
differences to the iterated blow-up discussed in the aforementioned papers. Our slightly different
approach to the iterated blow-up has better locality and functorial properties, in particular it is
compatible with restriction to open subsets, and thus avoids discussing additional special cases.
However, our modified approach requires additional attention when citing existing literature: for
example
[
30
, Lemma 7.2 (b)]
does not hold using our definitions.
As a consequence, the current section will be written in a self-contained way,
both in order to provide reliable foundations and for better readability. The subtle difference
in the approaches, however will finally have no effect in our applications: in these applications,
our iterated blow-up spaces coincide with the iterated blow-up spaces in the work of Melrose and
in the related work cited above.

Lemma 3.6

Let us assume that

3.3.
Admissible orders

A natural question when we do iterated blow-ups of a manifold with
corners
M
along an ordered family
\mathcal{P}=(P_{0},P_{1},\ldots,P_{k})
,
is to decide how the order of the blow-up influences the final space

[
38
]

whether the iterated blow-up is defined for a given order.
The aim of this subsection is to recall the results of
[
1
,
6
,
38
]

that give a positive answer to these questions if “admissible orders” are
used. Before stating the main result from
[
6
]
, we first introduce
admissible orders and graph blow-ups, which will be needed for the

If, in the definition of a blow-up-suitable
k
-tuple
\mathcal{P}
,
we further require
P_{1}
to be minimal for inclusion, we obtain the notion
of an “admissible”

Definition 3.14
.

Let

be a manifold with corners and let
\mathcal{P}\coloneqq(P_{i})_{i=1}^{k}
be a

k
-tuple of closed subsets of

,

. By induction on

we say that

is

if:

(i)

is a

of

,

(ii)

there is no

such that

, and

(iii)

if

,

is admissible in

, where
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}=(\beta_{M,P_{1}}^{*}(P_{j}))_{j=2}^{k}

is the pull-back of

as before (in particular,
\beta^{*}_{M,P_{1}}
is as defined

Of course,

. If

is admissible in

This definition of and admissible

Remark 3.15
.

We notice the following

(1)

A

-tuple with an admissible order is, in particular,
also blow-up-suitable.

(2)

\mathcal{P}
is admissible if, and only if,

(3)

Assume the

-tuple

\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}\coloneqq\bigl(\emptyset,\beta_{M,P_{1}}^{*}(P_{2}),\beta_{M,P_{1}}^{*}(P_{3}),...,\beta_{M,P_{1}}^{*}(P_{k})\bigr)

This explains why we sometimes consider

We now consider a different type of blow-up with respect to a

-tuple
of p-submanifolds that is immediately seen not to depend on the

Let

shows that, if

Let

We now investigate how several geometric quantities (metrics, distance functions,
Sobolev spaces, natural differential operators, …) change when performing a
blow-up. The manifolds
M
we consider
have a complete metric in the interior
M_{0}
described precisely in terms of
Lie manifolds, Definition
4.20
.
One important case will be that when
M_{0}
is a Euclidean vector space
X
with
its spherical compactification
M=\overline{X}
, which was described in the
Introduction. Some other times,
M
will be a blow-up of
\overline{X}
.

We distinguish here the case of a blow-up along a submanifold
contained
in the boundary
(the easy case) and the case of a manifold
not contained in
the boundary
(the difficult case, but treated already in
[
1
]
and in other
papers). In the first case, the
boundary case,
we will additionally require
that our vector fields are tangent to the submanifold contained in the boundary. In
that case, the metric in the interior remains the same, only the compactification
is altered, which makes many investigations much easier. In the second case, the

interior case,
the metric will be changed conformally
by multiplication with
r^{-2}
, where
r
is a “smoothed distance function,”
(a concept that will be defined in this section).
The main technical result of this section is the behavior of “smoothed
distance functions” when performing iterated blow-ups, Proposition

4.15
. We also recall in this section the needed regularity
result on Lie manifolds from
[
3
]
.

4.1.
Smoothed distance function to a p-submanifold

Let
M
denote a manifold with corners, as before. We assume that

\mathcal{P}\subset 2^{M}
– equipped with a suitable ordering – is a blow-up-suitable

k
-tuple of subsets of
M
(so that
[M:\mathcal{P}]
is defined). As always, we shall
write

\mathchoice{{\bigcup}\,\mathcal{P}}{\mathord{\raise 0.49991pt\hbox{$\displaystyle\textstyle\bigcup$}}\,\mathcal{P}}{\mathord{\raise 0.49991pt\hbox{$\displaystyle\scriptstyle\bigcup$}}\mathcal{P}}{\mathord{\raise 0.49991pt\hbox{$\displaystyle\scriptscriptstyle\bigcup$}}\mathcal{P}}\ \coloneqq\ \bigcup\limits_{P\in\mathcal{P}}P\,.

We shall need the following simple concept of “equivalent functions”
on
[M:\mathcal{P}]
.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
M
be a manifold with
corners, let
\mathcal{P}
be a blow-up-suitable
k
-tuple
of p-submanifolds of
M
(so, in particular,
[M:\mathcal{P}]
is defined), and
let
k\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0,\infty\}
. Assume that we have two continuous functions

f_{i}:M\to[0,\infty)
,
i=0,1
, such that
the functions
f_{i}
are
\mathcal{C}^{k}
on
M\smallsetminus\bigcup\mathcal{P}
and nowhere
vanishing. We shall say that
f_{0}
and
f_{1}
are
\mathcal{C}^{k}
-equivalent
(on
[M:\mathcal{P}]
)
or that
f_{0}
is
\mathcal{C}^{k}
-equivalent (on
[M:\mathcal{P}]
)
to
f_{1}
if

\frac{f_{1}\mathchoice{\lower 3.24005pt\hbox{\big|}\lower 6.84009pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle M\smallsetminus\bigcup\mathcal{P}$}}{\lower 2.88007pt\hbox{\big|}\lower 5.75995pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle M\smallsetminus\bigcup\mathcal{P}$}}{\lower 2.52008pt\hbox{|}\lower 5.03998pt\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle M\smallsetminus\bigcup\mathcal{P}$}}{\lower 2.15991pt\hbox{|}\lower 4.68pt\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle M\smallsetminus\bigcup\mathcal{P}$}}}{f_{0}\mathchoice{\lower 3.24005pt\hbox{\big|}\lower 6.84009pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle M\smallsetminus\bigcup\mathcal{P}$}}{\lower 2.88007pt\hbox{\big|}\lower 5.75995pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle M\smallsetminus\bigcup\mathcal{P}$}}{\lower 2.52008pt\hbox{|}\lower 5.03998pt\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle M\smallsetminus\bigcup\mathcal{P}$}}{\lower 2.15991pt\hbox{|}\lower 4.68pt\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle M\smallsetminus\bigcup\mathcal{P}$}}}

extends to a nowhere vanishing
\mathcal{C}^{k}
function on
[M:\mathcal{P}]
.
In the case
k=\infty
, we shall say that
f_{0}
is

smoothly equivalent
or just
equivalent
to
f_{1}
and write
f_{0}\sim f_{1}
.
In the case
k=0
, we say
f_{0}
and
f_{1}
are
continuously equivalent.

The family
\mathcal{P}
as well as the space
[M:\mathcal{P}]
, which we assumed to be
defined, will sometimes be understood, so we may occasionally not mention them.
Recall that two functions
f_{1},f_{2}:U\to[0,\infty)

are called
Lipschitz equivalent
if there exists
C>0
such that

(16)

C^{-1}f_{1}\leq f_{2}\leq Cf_{1}\,.

Two functions
f_{1},f_{2}:M\to[0,\infty)
are called

locally Lipschitz equivalent
if any point in
M
has an open neighborhood

U
such that
f_{1}|_{U}
and
f_{2}|_{U}
are Lipschitz equivalent.

Remark 4.2
.

Let us record a few easy consequences of the definition:

(i)

Clearly, the
\mathcal{C}^{k}
-equivalence of functions on
[M:\mathcal{P}]
is an equivalence
relation.

(ii)

Since
M\smallsetminus\mathchoice{{\bigcup}\,\mathcal{P}}{\mathord{\raise 0.49991pt\hbox{$\displaystyle\textstyle\bigcup$}}\,\mathcal{P}}{\mathord{\raise 0.49991pt\hbox{$\displaystyle\scriptstyle\bigcup$}}\mathcal{P}}{\mathord{\raise 0.49991pt\hbox{$\displaystyle\scriptscriptstyle\bigcup$}}\mathcal{P}}
is dense in
[M:\mathcal{P}]

(Proposition
3.10
) the extension of the quotient

\dfrac{f_{1}|_{M\smallsetminus\bigcup\mathcal{P}}}{f_{0}|_{M\smallsetminus\bigcup\mathcal{P}}}
to
[M:\mathcal{P}]
by continuity is unique (when it exists).

(iii)

Obviously
\mathcal{C}^{k}
-equivalence implies
\mathcal{C}^{\ell}
-equivalence for
\ell\leq k
.

(iv)

If two functions
f_{i}:M\to[0,\infty)
as above are continuously
equivalent, then they are locally Lipschitz equivalent.
As a consequence, for
M
compact,
\mathcal{C}^{0}
-equivalence implies Lipschitz equivalence.

(v)

If
H
is a boundary hyperface of
M
with at least one defining
function, then any two defining functions of
H
will be equivalent on
[M:H]\simeq M
.

Let us now discuss the type of metrics that we will use.
Let
M
be a manifold with corners. In the following, we shall make use of two
kinds of metrics on
M
. The first kind of metrics will consist of
what we are calling “true
Riemannian metrics” on
M
. A
true Riemannian metric
on
M
is, by definition,
nothing but a smooth, fiberwise positive definite symmetric section of

T^{*}M\otimes T^{*}M\to M
(the usual kind of metrics on
M
). The second kind of metrics will
consist of the so-called
compatible metrics,
which will be defined below, Definition

4.20
, and whose definition requires some additional data (a structural
Lie algebra of vector fields on
M
). Compatible metrics are Riemannian metrics
on
M_{0}\coloneqq M\smallsetminus\partial M
and do not extend to a true metric on
M
,
unless
\partial M=\emptyset
.

Let
P
be a closed p-submanifold of
M
. Recall that
\beta_{M,P}:[M:P]\to M

denotes the blow-down map (see Definition
3.1
).

Definition 4.3
.

Let
M
be a manifold with corners and
P\subset M

be a closed p-submanifold. A function
r_{P}:M\to[0,\infty)
will be called

a smoothed distance function to
P
(in
M
)
if its lift

\beta_{M,P}^{*}(r_{P})\coloneqq r_{P}\circ\beta_{M,P}
is a boundary defining
function (Definition
2.4
) for
\mathbb{S}N_{+}^{M}P=\beta_{M,P}^{-1}(P)
,
the hyperface (or union of hyperfaces) of
[M:P]
obtained by blowing up
M
along
P
.

We also remark that a smoothed distance function to
P
in
M
is continuous since
it lifts to a continuous function on
[M:P]
and
M
has the quotient topology.
The following remark explains the name “smoothed distance function to
P
” in

M
for
r_{P}
.

Remark 4.4
.

The function
f:M\to[0,1]
is a smoothed distance function to
P
in

M
if, and only if, it satisfies the following conditions:

(i)

it is continuous on
M
and smooth on
M\smallsetminus P
,

(ii)

f^{-1}(\{0\})=P
, and most importantly,

(iii)

there is a neighborhood
V
of
P
such that

f
is (smoothly) equivalent on
[V:P]
to
the distance to
P
with respect to some suitable true metric on
M
.

We omit details here, as this fact
will not be used in our article.

Remark 4.5
.

In general, a smoothed distance to
P
in
M
function
r_{P}
will
not

be smooth on
M
; in fact, smoothness on
M
will only hold if
P
is empty,
or a union of connected components of
M
. However, it will be continuous on

M
and smooth on
[M:P]
. This is one of the main reasons for considering
the blow-up
[M:P]
.

Remark 4.6
.

Given a closed p-submanifold
P\subset M
,
we see that a smoothed distance to
P
in
M
(Definition
4.3
)
is
not uniquely determined
.
However, any two such smoothed distance functions are equivalent
on
[M:P]
, and hence they are equivalent on any other iterated blow-up
[M:\mathcal{P}]

as well, as long as
P\in\mathcal{P}
. This is, in fact, our motivation for introducing
the notion of equivalence
of such functions. For this reason, we will sometimes talk about
the
smoothed
distance to
P
, although we will really mean the equivalence class of the
smoothed distances to
P
in
M
.

Remark 4.7
.

In the case that
M=\overline{X}
is the spherical compactificaction of some Euclidean
vector space
X
as explained in Subsection
1.1
and if
Y
is a
linear subspace of
X
, we may consider the Euclidean distance function

d_{Y}:=\min\bigl\{\|x-y\|\mid y\in Y\bigr\}
introduced in Equation
(
2
). If
r_{\overline{Y}}:\overline{X}\to[0,\infty)
is a

smoothed
distance function to

\overline{Y}
, then the function
r_{\overline{Y}}
is clearly not Lipschitz
equivalent to
d_{Y}
, as the first one is bounded and the second one is not.
One can also show that
r_{\overline{Y}}
is also not bi-Lipschitz
to
\arctan\circ d_{Y}
, as these two functions behave differently close to
\mathbb{S}_{X}
.
The precise behavior of
r_{\overline{Y}}
is that it is continuously equivalent
and Lipschitz equivalent to
x\mapsto\arctan\bigl((\|x\|^{2}+1)^{-1/2}d_{Y}(x)\bigr)
.
Furthermore,
r_{\overline{Y}}
is continuously equivalent and thus locally Lipschitz
equivalent to
x\mapsto(\|x\|^{2}+1)^{-1/2}d_{Y}(x)
.

We now consider as in
[
6
]
and
[
38
]
pairs
(P,Q)
of closed p-submanifolds of
M
. As in those papers, we need to consider the cases
Q\subset P
and
Q\cap P=\emptyset
. We begin with the first case.

Lemma 4.8
.

Let
P\subset M
be a closed p-submanifold of the manifold with corners
M
, let
Q\subset P

be a closed p-submanifold of
P
, and let
r_{P}
(respectively,
r_{Q}
) be a smoothed distance
function to
P
(respectively, to
Q
) in
M
.
Then

(r_{Q}^{-1}r_{P})\mathchoice{\lower 3.24005pt\hbox{\big|}\lower 6.84009pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle M\smallsetminus P$}}{\lower 2.88007pt\hbox{\big|}\lower 5.75995pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle M\smallsetminus P$}}{\lower 2.52008pt\hbox{|}\lower 5.03998pt\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle M\smallsetminus P$}}{\lower 2.15991pt\hbox{|}\lower 4.68pt\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle M\smallsetminus P$}}

extends to a smoothed distance function to
\beta_{M,Q}^{*}(P)\coloneqq\overline{\beta_{M,Q}^{-1}(P\smallsetminus Q)}
in
[M:Q]
.

Of course, in the above lemma, we have
\beta_{M,Q}^{*}(P)\coloneqq\overline{\beta_{M,Q}^{-1}(P\smallsetminus Q)}\simeq[P:Q]
see
Proposition
3.3
. Here
\simeq
denotes the existence of a
diffeomorphism extending the identity on
P\smallsetminus Q
. Also, for the simplicity of
the presentation, we may and will assume in the following that
\beta_{M,Q}^{*}(P)=[P:Q]
.

Remark 4.9
.

Note that the assumptions in the lemma allow the case when, for all

q\in Q
, we have
\dim_{q}(Q)=\dim_{q}(P)
. In this case
\beta_{M,Q}^{*}(P)=P\smallsetminus Q

is a union of some connected components of
P
, and thus for any
q\in Q
we have

(\beta_{M,Q})^{-1}(q)\cap[P:Q]=\emptyset
. If
U
is an open neighborhood of
q
,
then any positive function defined on
\beta_{M,Q}^{-1}(U)
is thus a smoothed distance
function to
[P:Q]\cap\beta_{M,Q}^{-1}(U)=\emptyset
.

On the other hand, in this special case,
r_{Q}^{-1}r_{P}
, defined on
U\smallsetminus\{q\}
,
where
U
is an open
neighborhood of
q
with
U\cap P=Q
, extends to a positive smooth function on

\beta_{[M:Q],[P:Q]}^{*}(\beta_{M,Q}^{*}(U))=\beta_{M,Q}^{*}(U)
. The latter statement
follows as
r_{P}|U
is a smooth distance function to
Q
in
U
and using
Remark
4.5
. These argument provide a proof of the lemma in this exceptional case.

This special case is also included in the following proof, if we use the convention
\mathbb{S}^{-1}=\emptyset

(here
\mathbb{S}^{-1}
is the unit sphere in
\mathbb{R}^{0}
).

Proof of Lemma
4.8
.

We need to prove that the function

, extends to a smooth function on

[M:Q,P]\ \coloneqq\ \bigl[[M:Q]:[P:Q]\bigr]

and that this extension of
r_{Q}^{-1}r_{P}
is a defining function
for the hyperface

of
[M:Q,P]
. Let

\beta\ \coloneqq\ \beta_{M,Q,P}\ \coloneqq\ \beta_{M,Q}\circ\beta_{[M:Q],[P:Q]}:[M:Q,P]\to M

be the blow-down map and
z\in P^{\prime}

(see Definition
3.8
). Then
\beta(z)\in P
.

Recall that by the definition of

, we have that

is a boundary defining function

of

as a hyperface of
[M:P]
.
The map
[M:Q,P]\to[M:P]
, defined by Lemma

is a diffeomorphism outside the preimage of

. Thus,
if
\beta(z)\notin Q
, the function
r_{Q}^{-1}r_{P}:M\smallsetminus P=[M:Q]\smallsetminus[P:Q]\to[0,\infty)
extends locally – i.e. in a neighborhood

– to a defining function for
P^{\prime}\subset[M:Q,P]
, since

r_{Q}>0
at and near

and since
r_{P}
is a
defining function of
\mathbb{S}(N^{M}_{P})
, the pull-back of
P

in
[M:P]
, as we have just explained.

On the other hand, if
\beta(z)\in Q
, we use (again) the fact that our
problem is local. Thus by choosing a suitable chart around
\beta(z)
,
we can reduce the lemma to the special case

\begin{cases}\ M=\mathbb{R}_{\ell}^{k}\times\mathbb{R}_{\ell^{\prime}}^{k^{\prime}}\times\mathbb{R}_{\ell^{\prime\prime}}^{k^{\prime\prime}}\ni(x,x^{\prime},x^{\prime\prime})\\
\ P=\mathbb{R}_{\ell}^{k}\times\mathbb{R}_{\ell^{\prime}}^{k^{\prime}}\times\{0\}\ni(x,x^{\prime},0)\\
\ Q=\mathbb{R}_{\ell}^{k}\times\{0\}\times\{0\}\ni(x,0,0)\\
\ \beta(z)=(0,0,0)\,.\end{cases}

Assume first
that
\ell^{\prime}=\ell^{\prime\prime}=0
.
In this very special case we have

\displaystyle[M:Q]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{R}_{\ell}^{k}\times\mathbb{S}^{k^{\prime}+k^{\prime\prime}-1}\times[0,\infty)\ni(x,\xi,r),

\displaystyle{}[P:Q]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{R}_{\ell}^{k}\times\mathbb{S}^{k^{\prime}-1}\times[0,\infty),

where

and
\xi=(x^{\prime},x^{\prime\prime})/r

Q
.
Furthermore, the inclusion
[P:Q]\subset[M:Q]
is

on the first

\mathbb{S}^{k^{\prime}+k^{\prime\prime}-1}

\mathbb{R}_{\ell}^{k}

).
For
\xi\in\mathbb{S}^{k^{\prime}+k^{\prime\prime}-1}
let

\theta(\xi)
be the length of the shortest geodesic from

to
\mathbb{S}^{k^{\prime}-1}
, unless

.
In the case

Then

We now turn to the second case, that when
P
and
Q
are disjoint.
For later use, we prove a more general statement.

Lemma 4.10
.

Let
M
be a compact manifold with corners, let
P\subset M
be a closed
p-submanifold and
\mathcal{Q}=(Q_{1},Q_{2},\ldots,Q_{k})
be a blow-up-suitable
k
-tuple
of closed subsets of
M

disjoint
from
P
. Let
\beta:[M:\mathcal{Q}]\to M
be the blow-down map and let
r_{P}
be a smoothed distance to
P

(in
M
). Let
\widehat{P}:=\beta^{-1}(P)=\beta^{*}(P)
. Then

r_{P}\circ\beta
is a smoothed distance function to
\widehat{P}

in
[M:\mathcal{Q}]
.

Proof.

Let us prove our result by induction on
k
. For
k=0
(i. e. for
\mathcal{Q}=\emptyset
) there is nothing to prove according to our conventions for the
blow-up with respect to an empty family. Let us write
Q=Q_{1}
, for the
simplicity of the notation. Let us prove our result for
k=1
. The blow-down

[M:Q,P]\to[M:P]
induces a diffeomorphism
[M:Q,P]\smallsetminus\beta^{-1}(Q)\to[M\smallsetminus Q:P]
(see Lemma
3.6
).
The function
r_{P}\circ\beta_{M,Q}
is a smoothed distance function to

\beta^{-1}(P)
on
M\smallsetminus Q\subset[M:Q]\textasciiacute
(since it coincides with
r_{P}

there). Moreover,
r_{P}\circ\beta_{M,Q}
is smooth everywhere on the
iterated blow-up
[M:Q,P]
and vanishes only on the preimage of
P

(in particular, it is
>0
on the preimage of
Q
, which is disjoint
from the preimage of
P
). Hence
r_{P}\circ\beta_{M,Q}

is a smoothed distance function to
\beta_{M,Q}^{*}(P)=\beta_{M,Q}^{-1}(P)

in
[M:Q]
. The induction step is always to perform an additional blow-up,
so it reduces to the case
k=1
just proved.
∎

4.2.
Smoothed distance functions to a family

We need to extend the definition of the smoothed distance function
to families. (The reader may want to review Definition
3.8

at this point.)

Definition 4.11
.

Let
\mathcal{P}\coloneqq(P_{i})_{i=1}^{k}
,
P_{i}\subset M
be a blow-up-suitable

k
-tuple (that is, an ordered family of closed subsets of
M
such that the
iterated blow-up
[M:P_{1},P_{2},\ldots,P_{k}]
is defined, Definition
3.7
).
Let
\beta:[M:P_{1}]\to M
be the blow-down map and
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}\coloneqq\bigl(\beta^{*}(P_{2}),\beta^{*}(P_{3}),\ldots,\beta^{*}(P_{k})\bigr)
, which,
we recall, is a family of closed p-submanifolds of
[M:P_{1}]
. We then define

a smoothed distance function

\rho_{\mathcal{P}}
to
\mathcal{P}
in
M
to be a function

\rho_{\mathcal{P}}:M\to[0,\infty)
given by induction on
k
by the formula

\rho_{\mathcal{P}}(x)\ \coloneqq\ \begin{cases}\ \ r_{P_{1}}(x)&\mbox{ if }k=1\\
\ r_{P_{1}}(x)\rho_{\mathcal{P}^{\prime}}(y)&\mbox{ if }k>1\ \mbox{ and }y\in\beta^{-1}(\{x\})\,,\end{cases}

where
r_{P_{1}}:M\to[0,\infty)
is a smoothed distance function to
P_{1}

in
M
(Definition
4.3
)
and
\rho_{\mathcal{P}^{\prime}}
is a smoothed distance function to
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}

in
[M:P_{1}]
.
(We note that the last expression is well-defined since, for
x\notin P_{1}
,
y
is unique
whereas, for
x\in P_{1}
, we have
r_{P_{1}}(x)=0
.)

Again, we use the notation introduced in Definition
3.8
(which is, in turn,
the same as the one in
[
6
, Definition
]
). We have then,
similarly to
[
6
, Remark
]
.

Remark 4.12
.

We have that
\rho_{\mathcal{P}}
is continuous on

and that
\rho_{\mathcal{P}}^{-1}(\{0\})=\mathchoice{{\bigcup}\,\mathcal{P}}{\mathord{\raise 0.49991pt\hbox{$\displaystyle\textstyle\bigcup$}}\,\mathcal{P}}{\mathord{\raise 0.49991pt\hbox{$\displaystyle\scriptstyle\bigcup$}}\mathcal{P}}{\mathord{\raise 0.49991pt\hbox{$\displaystyle\scriptscriptstyle\bigcup$}}\mathcal{P}}=\bigcup_{j=1}^{k}P_{j}
. Let
\gamma_{0}=\operatorname{\mathrm{id}}
,
\gamma_{1}\coloneqq\beta^{*}_{1}

and
\gamma_{j}\coloneqq\beta^{*}_{j}\circ\gamma_{j-1}=\beta^{*}_{j}\circ...\circ\beta^{*}_{1}
, where

\beta_{k}\ \coloneqq\ \beta_{[M:P_{1},\ldots,P_{k-1}],[P_{k}:P_{1},\ldots,P_{k-1}]}:[M:P_{1},\ldots,P_{k}]\to[M:P_{1},\ldots,P_{k-1}]\,.

If,

In particular, it follows that the pull-back of
\rho_{\mathcal{P}}
to

[M:\mathcal{P}]
is smooth, or equivalently, suppressing all pull-backs from the notation,

\rho_{\mathcal{P}}\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}\bigl([M:\mathcal{P}]\bigr)

5.1.2.
Boundary blow-ups and the data on
X_{\mathrm{GV}}

Let us now see how the constructions of Lie manifolds and of their
geometric objects of the previous section particularize
to our setting, taking into account the two types of blow-ups: boundary
blow-up (along a p-submanifold contained in the boundary) and
interior blow-up (along a p-submanifold
not
contained in the boundary).
We begin with the blow-up of
\overline{X}
with respect to
\mathbb{S}_{\mathcal{F}}
, which,
we recall, is obtained using a sequence of boundary blow-ups.
We will let
\mathcal{W}_{\mathrm{eucl}}
denote the structural Lie algebra of vector fields on
\overline{X}
.

Remark 5.6
.

We will now define a Lie manifold structure on
\overline{X}
, as well as on any of the
intermediate blow-ups leading all the way up to
X_{\mathrm{GV}}\coloneqq[\overline{X}:\mathbb{S}_{\mathcal{F}}]

At first, the Lie manifold structure on
\overline{X}
is defined by the action of
X

on itself and on
\overline{X}
as described in Example
4.19
, namely,
the structural Lie algebra of vector fields
\mathcal{W}_{\mathrm{eucl}}
on
\overline{X}
is

\mathcal{W}_{\mathrm{eucl}}\coloneqq{\mathcal{C}}^{\infty}(\overline{X})X
. We have already described in
[

and
in then in the Introduction of this paper how to get from

to

by iterated blow-ups.
On any of these intermediate blow-ups

Theorem

defines an action of
X
(as a Lie group) on
\widehat{X}
extending smoothly the translation action.
Deriving this Lie group action yields a Lie algebra action of
X
on
\widehat{X}
, given by a linear map

, which in fact provides smooth extensions of all constant
vector fields on
X
to the compactification
\widehat{X}
. In general

will not
vanish at the boundary, but it will be tangent to the boundary, see below for further
discussion.

On all these blow-ups leading to the Georgescu–Vasy space

the Euclidean metric will be compatible with the Lie manifold
structure on the (intermediate) blow-ups,
since we are performing only boundary blow-ups.

An alternative way to obtain the Lie manifold structure on all the iterated blow-ups

is to use Remark
4.23

\overline{X}

We stress that, as a consequence of Remark

, the resulting

and

will be the same. Namely,
they are the
usual Sobolev spaces
on
X
.
Recall that we identify
X
with
constant vector fields on itself. The algebra of differential operators, however, will
change with each blow-up (along some

,

), eventually leading on

\operatorname{Diff}_{\mathcal{W}_{\mathrm{bdry}}}(X_{\mathrm{GV}})\ =\ {\mathcal{C}}^{\infty}(X_{\mathrm{GV}})\mathbb{C}[X]\,,

where

denotes the algebra of polynomials on
X
and where a monomial of degree
d

is interpreted as a differential operator of degree
d
with constant coefficients.

Remark 5.7

We use the notation introduced in Example

5.3.
More on smooth distance functions on blow-ups

As before let
\beta_{\mathrm{GV}}:X_{\mathrm{GV}}\coloneqq[\overline{X}:\mathbb{S}_{\mathcal{F}}]\to\overline{X}
be the blow-down map from the Georgescu–Vasy compactification
to the ball compactification. Again for

, we write
\beta_{\mathrm{GV}}^{*}(Y)
for the lift of
\overline{Y}\subset\overline{X}
to
[\overline{X}:\mathbb{S}_{\mathcal{F}}]
, i.e. it
is the closure of

in
[\overline{X}:\mathbb{S}_{\mathcal{F}}]
. In this subsection we use the notation

. (Note that now

only has this specific meaning, whereas it denoted a more general class of lifts previously.)

The goal of the current subsection is to compare a smoothed distance to

in

to the Euclidean distance to

is the Euclidean distance function to

,
extend to a smoothed distance function to
\widehat{Y}
in

. This is mostly a question about the asymptotic of

at infinity.

The reader should be aware that a smoothed distance
to
\overline{Y}
in
\overline{X}
will not pull-back to a smoothed distance function to
\widehat{Y}
in
X_{\mathrm{GV}}
,
thus it is important to keep in mind with respect to which of the two compactifications,

\overline{X}
or
\widehat{X}
, the smoothed distance functions is defined. Again, we let

\widehat{\mathcal{F}}\coloneqq\beta_{\mathrm{GV}}^{*}(\overline{\mathcal{F}})=\{\widehat{Y}\mid Y\in\mathcal{F}\}
and recall from Corollary
5.4

that
[X_{\mathrm{GV}}:\widehat{\mathcal{F}}]=[\overline{X}:\mathbb{S}_{\mathcal{F}}\cup\overline{\mathcal{F}}]=:X_{\mathcal{F}}
.

Lemma 5.12
.

Let
\phi_{0}:[0,\infty)\to[0,1]
be a smooth, non-decreasing function
satisfying
\phi_{0}(t)=t
for

and
\phi_{0}(t)=1
for

.
Let
Y\in\mathcal{F}
and

be the distance function
from
x\in X
to
Y
with respect to the Euclidean distance on
X
,
as before. Then
\phi_{0}\circ d_{Y}
extends to a smoothed distance function

to
\widehat{Y}
in
X_{\mathrm{GV}}\coloneqq[\overline{X}:\mathbb{S}_{\mathcal{F}}]
such that

extends to a smooth function on
X_{\mathrm{GV}}
.

It follows trivially from the lemma that
r_{\widehat{Y}}/d_{Y}
extends to a smooth
function on
X_{\mathcal{F}}\coloneqq[X_{\mathrm{GV}}:\widehat{\mathcal{F}}]
.

Proof.

Let

, where

.
Then

extends to a smooth distance function to 0 in
\overline{X/Y}
. Let
b

be the map of Corollary
5.11
. Then that corollary
gives that

is a smoothed distance function to
\widehat{Y}

in

that coincides with
\phi_{0}\circ d_{Y}
on

.

Next, the function

extends

.
This function is the desired smooth extension of

.
∎

Remark 5.13
.

Note that

is a smoothed distance function to
Y
in
X
, but
not in

\overline{X}
as it does not extend to a continuous (or smooth) function

.

The purpose of the framework developed in Subsection

was to prove
the following result.

Proposition 5.14
.

Let
Y\in\mathcal{F}
and
d_{Y}(x)\coloneqq\operatorname{dist}(x,Y)=\inf\bigl\{\|x-y\|\mid y\in Y\bigr\}

be the Euclidean distance function from

to

and
\widehat{\mathcal{F}}

be the lift of

to

in

.
Then

extends smoothly to a function in

Proof.

We use the notation introduced in Lemma

, in particular,

6.
Regularity results for eigenfunctions

We now formulate and prove our main regularity results for certain differential operators
with singular coefficients on
X
. These results apply, in particular,
to Schrödinger operators with
“inverse square potentials”, a class of potentials which will be defined below and that includes the classical Schrödinger operator with Coulomb potential, which are used in physics and chemistry. The more general class of Schrödinger operators with inverse square potentials became of renewed interest

[

[

The Lie manifold structure on

4.23

Theorem 6.1
.

We use the usual notation, recalled for instance in the last paragraph.

Proof.

Example 6.5

(The Schrödinger operator in quantum physics)
.

The Schrödinger operator of an atom with heavy nucleus and
with
N

The potential
V
is an inverse square potential with singularities
on the collision planes (more precisely, on the semilattice
generated by the collision planes). We thus may apply Theorem
6.1

to eigenfunctions of the differential operator

.

Remark 6.7
.

The methods in this article also generalize to non-linear equations.
As an example we consider equations of the form

Let

. We define the
Yamabe operator

L^{g}\coloneqq\Delta^{g}-\frac{n-2}{4(n-1)}\mathrm{scal}^{g}
, and one may find in any reference about the Yamabe problem or conformal geometry that

After multiplication with

Appendix B
Group actions on compactifications of vector spaces

Here we include the details on how the constant vector fields on a vector
space extend to various compactifications.
In order for the paper to be self-contained, we also recall and extend here some facts about
several compactifications of a vector space
X
. We will start by considering the spherical
compactification. We use the definitions of
\mathbb{R}^{n}_{k}
and
\mathbb{S}^{n}_{k}
as in (
7
).

Lemma B.1
.

Let
X
be a finite-dimensional real vector space. Let
A\in\mathop{\mathrm{GL}}(X)
and
V\in X
.
Then the affine map
p\mapsto L_{A,V}(p)\ \coloneqq\ Ap+V
extends to a diffeomorphism of
\overline{X}
.

Proof.

Let us assume first that
V=\mathbb{R}^{n}
. As a first, step we want to construct a smooth map

f_{A,V}:\mathbb{S}^{n}_{1}\to\mathbb{S}^{n}_{1}
such that
\Theta_{n}^{-1}\circ f_{A,V}\circ\Theta_{n}
is an
extension of the affine map
L_{A,V}
, where
\Theta_{n}
is the map
\overline{X}\to\mathbb{S}^{n}_{1}
given
by (
8
). As a second step we prove that it is a diffeomorphism.

At first we consider the linear map
F_{A,V}:\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\to\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
,
(t,p)\mapsto(t,Ap+tV)
.
Obviously
F_{A,V}
maps
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{1}
to itself and
F_{A,V}((1,p))=(1,L_{A,V}(p))
. It is
easy to check that the smooth map

f_{A,V}\bigl((t,p)\bigr)\ \coloneqq\ \frac{F_{A,V}((t,p))}{\|F_{A,V}((t,p))\|}

has the extension property for
L_{A,V}
described above. It is obvious to see that

F_{A,V}\circ F_{A^{\prime},V^{\prime}}=F_{AA^{\prime},V+AV^{\prime}}
and as a consequence

f_{A,V}\circ f_{A^{\prime},V^{\prime}}=f_{AA^{\prime},V+AV^{\prime}}
. It follows that
f_{A^{-1},-A^{-1}V}
is the inverse
to
f_{A,V}
, and hence
f_{A,V}
is a diffeomorphism. The case of a general vector space
X
is obtained
by choosing a linear isomorphism
X\simeq\mathbb{R}^{n}
. In view of the results proved, the
resulting smooth structure on
\overline{X}
and the smoothness of the affine maps on
\overline{X}

do not depend on the choice of the isomorphism
X\simeq\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
∎

Obviously the diffeomorphism
f_{A,V}:\overline{X}\to\overline{X}
restricts to a diffeomorphism
of the boundary, namely the sphere at infinity
f_{A,V}|_{\mathbb{S}_{X}}\colon\mathbb{S}_{X}\to\mathbb{S}_{X}
.
From the proof of the last lemma, we also get:

Corollary B.2
.

The extension constructed in Lemma
B.1

above yields a group homomorphism
\Aff(X)\to\operatorname{Diff}(\overline{X})
,

L_{A,V}\mapsto f_{A,V}
. For a translation
L_{1,V}:X\to X
by
V
, we have

f_{1,V}|_{\mathbb{S}_{X}}=\operatorname{\mathrm{id}}_{\mathbb{S}_{X}}.

Proof.

The proof is immediate from the formula defining the extensions of
affine maps to the spherical compactification (see the proof of Lemma

B.1
).
∎

The map in the corollary defines a Lie group action of
\Aff(X)
on
\overline{X}
, and the derivative
of this Lie group action yields a Lie algebra action of
\aff(X)
, the Lie algebra of the
affine group of
X
on
\overline{X}
. We may restrict to the Lie group of translations, resp. to the
corresponding Lie algebra. This Lie algebra is
X
with the trivial bracket. We thus have a
Lie algebra action of
X
on
\overline{X}
. If a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
acts on a manifold with corners

M
, then this is given by a Lie algebra homomorphism
\mathcal{L}:\mathfrak{g}\to\Gamma(TM)
.
In the above situation
X=\mathfrak{g}
and
\mathcal{L}
maps a vector of
X
to the corresponding constant
vector field in
\Gamma(X)
. As the Lie algebra
X
acts on
\overline{X}
, any constant vector field on
X

extends to a vector field on
\overline{X}
. As the Lie group of translations acts by the identity on

\mathbb{S}_{X}
, the Lie group
X
acts trivially on
\mathbb{S}_{X}
which proves that the smooth extension of
a constant vector field to
\overline{X}
vanishes on
\mathbb{S}_{X}
.
We thus have proven the following.

Corollary B.3
.

For any
V\in X
, the vector field

0&\text{ if }\ p\in\mathbb{S}_{X}\,,\end{cases}

is a smooth vector field on
\overline{X}
.

We now turn to more involved compactifications. Let
\mathcal{S}
be a finite, clean
semilattice of closed submanifolds of
\mathbb{S}_{X}
. We consider
the compactification,
M\ \coloneqq\ [\overline{X}:\mathcal{S}]
, an example being the Georgescu–Vasy
compactification

discussed in

The action of the Lie group
X
on
\overline{X}
constructed above is the identity on
any

, as
P\subset\mathbb{S}_{X}
. Thus
[
6
, Theorem
]

or Theorem
3.18
tells us that the Lie group action of

on
\overline{X}

lifts (uniquely) to an action of
X
on
M
. The action is by diffeomorphisms, and,
as
X
is connected, the action of an element of

on

maps each boundary face
of
M
to itself. The corresponding Lie algebra action yields a Lie algebra homomorphism

\mathcal{L}\colon X=\Lie(X)\to\Gamma(TM)
. Its image consists
of vector fields tangent to the boundary of
M
as the corresponding Lie group action
preserves all boundary faces. We have thus obtained the following result:

Lemma B.4
.

Let
X
and
\mathcal{F}
be as above and
M\ \coloneqq\ [\overline{X}:\mathcal{F}]
. Then any constant vector field
on
X
extends to a smooth vector field on
M
that is tangent to the boundary.

We will now discuss whether the smooth extension of a constant vector field to

M\coloneqq[\overline{X}:\mathcal{S}]
still vanishes on the boundary.

Example B.5
.

Let
X
and
\mathcal{S}=\{\mathbb{S}_{Y}\}
,
Y\subset X
,
Y\neq\{0\}
. Then we have seen
that the constant vector fields

extend to a vector fields on
\overline{X}

vanishing at the boundary
\mathbb{S}_{X}
. One can show that the lift of

to
[\overline{X}:\mathbb{S}_{Y}]
vanishes at all the boundary faces of

if, and only if

If

, then its lift
does not vanish on the boundary hyperface
emerging from

, but it still vanishes on the other boundary hyperface of

(respectively, in the exceptional case

).

A splitting lemma

We now include the promised details explaining how Lemma

]

we consider the relations between (blow-ups of) the (spherical)

So let

We have shown in

In order to work on the space

In the following lemma we write elements

\mathbb{R}^{m+m^{\prime}+1}_{k+k^{\prime}}\cong\mathbb{R}^{m}_{k}\times\mathbb{R}^{m^{\prime}+1}_{k^{\prime}}

\mathbb{R}^{m+m^{\prime}+1}_{k+k^{\prime}}

with respect to the product

\mathbb{R}^{m}_{k}\times\mathbb{R}^{m^{\prime}+1}_{k^{\prime}}

Lemma C.1
.

Let

then

\widetilde{\Psi}:[\mathbb{S}_{k,k^{\prime}}^{m,m^{\prime}}:\{0\}\times\mathbb{S}^{m^{\prime}}_{k^{\prime}}]\ \stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\longrightarrow}}\ \mathbb{S}^{m-1}_{k}\times\mathbb{S}^{m^{\prime}+1}_{k^{\prime}+1}

we obtain a diffeomorphism :

\widetilde{\Psi}:[\mathbb{S}_{k+k^{\prime}}^{m+m^{\prime}}:\{0\}\times\mathbb{S}^{m^{\prime}}_{k^{\prime}}]\ \stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\longrightarrow}}\ \mathbb{S}^{m-1}_{k}\times\mathbb{S}^{m^{\prime}+1}_{k^{\prime}+1}

A proof of this result with our notations can be found in

In the following proposition, we shall identify

with

, for
some fixed scalar product
\langle\mathchoice{\,\raisebox{1.30005pt}{\scalebox{.5}{$\bullet$}}\,}{\raisebox{1.30005pt}{\scalebox{.5}{\,$\bullet$\,}}}{\,\raisebox{0.70007pt}{\scalebox{.4}{$\bullet$}}\,}{\,\raisebox{0.59998pt}{\scalebox{.35}{$\bullet$}}\,}{,}\mathchoice{\,\raisebox{1.30005pt}{\scalebox{.5}{$\bullet$}}\,}{\raisebox{1.30005pt}{\scalebox{.5}{\,$\bullet$\,}}}{\,\raisebox{0.70007pt}{\scalebox{.4}{$\bullet$}}\,}{\,\raisebox{0.59998pt}{\scalebox{.35}{$\bullet$}}\,}\rangle

Proposition C.2
.

Let

Then there is a diffeomorphism

such that the restriction of

to

is the extension
of the canonical projection

and

.
Moreover, the blow-down map
\beta_{\overline{X},\mathbb{S}_{Y}}
is determined by

\Xi_{Y}(z,y)\ =\ \begin{cases}\ y&\ \mbox{ if }y\in\mathbb{S}_{Y}\,,\\

Proof.

The proof is a modification of the proof of
[
6
, Proposition 5.2]
.
The map
\psi_{Y}
is the same as the one defined in the aforementioned Proposition
and used before in this paper. Let us recall briefly the construction of this
map
\psi_{Y}
and then introduce the map
\chi
. Let
\dim Y=p
and
\dim X=n
. Without

where

is the lift of

to the two blow-up spaces.

Appendix D
More about clean intersections

Here we include some technical details for the
fact that our semilattices coming from linear subspaces are clean.

Lemma D.1
.

Let
Y,Z
be two linear subspaces of
X
. Then each of the following pairs
intersects cleanly:

(1)

\overline{Y}
and
\overline{Z}
,

(2)

\mathbb{S}_{Y}
and
\mathbb{S}_{Z}
,

(3)

\mathbb{S}_{Y}
and
\overline{Z}
.

Proof.

The clean intersection property for
\overline{Y}
and
\overline{Z}
is obvious in

X
, so study this property in
p\in\mathbb{S}_{Y}=\overline{Y}\cap\mathbb{S}
. Let

\dim(X)=n
,
\dim(Y)=k
,
\dim(Z)=\ell
,
\dim(Y\cap Z)=m
. We may choose
a basis
e_{1},\ldots,e_{n}
of
X
such that
e_{1},\ldots,e_{m}
is a basis of
Y\cap Z
, such
that
e_{1},\ldots,e_{k}
is a basis of
Y
, and such that
e_{1},\ldots,e_{m},e_{k+1},\ldots,e_{\ell+k-m}
is a basis of
Z
. It remains to check the clean intersection of the above
pairs in any given
p\in\mathbb{S}_{Y}\cap\mathbb{S}_{Z}
, and without loss of generality we
can assume that
p
is the ray through
e_{1}
.
As
\Theta_{n}
is a diffeomorphism from
\overline{X}
to
\mathbb{S}^{n}_{1}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
, we will argue in this submanifold. To simplify the indices, we assume that the basis of
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
is
e_{0},\ldots,e_{n}
. We calculate

T_{\Phi_{n}(p)}\overline{Y}=\mathop{\mathrm{span}}\{e_{0},e_{2},e_{3},\ldots e_{k}\}
,
T_{\Phi_{n}(p)}(\overline{Y}\cap\overline{Z})=\mathop{\mathrm{span}}\{e_{0},e_{2},e_{3},\ldots e_{m}\}
,
T_{\Phi_{n}(p)}\overline{Z}=T_{\Phi_{n}(p)}(\overline{Y}\cap\overline{Z})\oplus\mathop{\mathrm{span}}\{e_{k+1},\ldots,e_{\ell+k-m}\}
,
T_{\Phi_{n}(p)}\mathbb{S}_{Y}=\mathop{\mathrm{span}}\{e_{2},e_{3},\ldots e_{k}\}
,
T_{\Phi_{n}(p)}(\mathbb{S}_{y}\cap\mathbb{S}_{Z})=\mathop{\mathrm{span}}\{e_{2},e_{3},\ldots e_{m}\}
, and finally we have
T_{\Phi_{n}(p)}\mathbb{S}_{Z}=T_{\Phi_{n}(p)}(\mathbb{S}_{y}\cap\mathbb{S}_{Z})\oplus\mathop{\mathrm{span}}\{e_{k+1},\ldots,e_{\ell+k-m}\}
.
From these calculations the claimed statement follows.
∎

Corollary D.2
.

Let
V,W
be two linear subspaces of

. Then all subsets of
\{\overline{V},\overline{W},\mathbb{S}_{V},\mathbb{S}_{W}\}
intersect cleanly.

Proof.

For subsets with at most one element the statement is trivial. For subsets with two elements, the corollary follows from Lemma
D.1

by setting
(Y,Z)=(V,W)
or
(Y,Z)=(W,V)
.

In order two prove it for subset with 3 elements, let us consider first the case

.
Applying Lemma
D.1
(3) for
Z=V
and

, we see that

\overline{V}
and

intersect cleanly. We already know that
\overline{W}
and
\mathbb{S}_{V}
intersect cleanly, thus the triple intersects cleanly. In the case
\{\overline{W},\mathbb{S}_{V},\mathbb{S}_{W}\}
we apply Lemma

(2) for

and

, and we get that
\mathbb{S}_{W}
and
\mathbb{S}_{V\cap W}=\overline{W}\cap\mathbb{S}_{V}
intersect cleanly, the rest of the proof is as above. The other cases with three elements are obtained by switching the role of

and

.

The set
\{\overline{V},\overline{W},\mathbb{S}_{V},\mathbb{S}_{W}\}
intersects cleanly by applying the Lemma

(3) for

and

, which yields the clean intersection of
\overline{V}
and

∎

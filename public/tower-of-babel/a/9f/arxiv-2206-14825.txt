Optimal Narain CFTs from Codes
Nikolaos Angelinos, Debarghya Chakraborty, Anatoly Dymarsky

Abstract
Recently established connection between additive codes and Narain CFTs provides a new tool to construct theories with special properties and solve modular bootstrap constraints by reducing them to algebraic identities. We generalize previous constructions to include many new theories, in particular we show that all known optimal Narain theories, i.e. those maximizing the value of spectral gap, can be constructed from codes. For asymptotically large central charge c c we show there are code theories with the spectral gap growing linearly with c c , with the coefficient saturating the conjectural upper bound. We therefore conjecture that optimal Narain theories for any value of c c can be obtained from codes.

Introduction

Conformal modular bootstrap program aims to establish universal constraints on two-dimensional CFTs and elucidate properties of those special theories which saturate these constraints. One of the central goals of the modular bootstrap is to study theories maximizing the value of spectral gap for given fixed value of central charge
[
1
]

2

[
2

[

[
4
]
.

2
Additive codes and Lorentzian lattices

The main ingredient of our construction is a 2-dimensional even lattice
\Lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{1,1}
, which we call a glue lattice. Starting from such a lattice, we define an additive group
{\sf G}
, which serves as the alphabet of the code. Then standard Construction A maps a code
{\mathcal{C}}\subset{\sf G}^{c}
into a lattice

\displaystyle\underbrace{\Lambda\oplus\dots\oplus\Lambda}_{c\,{\rm times}}\subset\Lambda_{\mathcal{C}}\subset\underbrace{\Lambda^{\perp}\oplus\dots\Lambda^{\perp}}_{c\,{\rm times}}\subset\mathbb{R}^{c,c}.

(2.2)

When
{\mathcal{C}}
satisfies additional conditions, the lattice
\Lambda_{\mathcal{C}}
is even and self-dual, thus defining a Narain theory.

2.1
Even lattices in
\mathbb{R}^{1,1}

We equip
\mathbb{R}^{2}
with a Lorentzian metric

g=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
1&0\end{pmatrix},

(2.3)

thus turning it into
\mathbb{R}^{1,1}
.
A two-dimensional “glue” lattice
\Lambda\subset\mathbb{R}^{1,1}
is called integral if
v^{T}gu\in\mathbb{Z}
for any
u,v\in\Lambda
. A lattice called even if
v^{T}gv\in 2\mathbb{Z}
for any
v\in\Lambda
. Any even lattice is automatically integral.

It is convenient to parametrize a lattice by a generating matrix
\Lambda
, such that
v=\Lambda n
,
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
generates all lattice vectors. Here we abuse the notations by using
\Lambda
to denote both the lattice and its generating matrix. The generating matrix is not unique, obviously
\Lambda
and
\Lambda S
for
S\in SL(2,\mathbb{Z})
generate the same lattice. So far we are only interested in the scalar product defined by (
2.3
),
we can identify all lattices related by
O(1,1)
,
\Lambda\sim O\Lambda
for
O\in O(1,1)
.

By dual lattice
\Lambda^{\perp}
we understand all vectors
v\in\Lambda^{\perp}\subset\mathbb{R}^{1,1}
such that
v^{T}gu\in\mathbb{Z}
for any vector
u\in\Lambda
. At the level of generating matrix
\Lambda^{\perp}=g(\Lambda^{-1})^{T}
.

We can parametrize all even lattices in
\mathbb{R}^{1,1}
as follows. In full generality

g_{\Lambda}=\Lambda^{T}g\Lambda=\begin{pmatrix}2m&k\\
k&2n\end{pmatrix},~n,m,k\in\mathbb{Z},

(2.4)

assuming
\det(\Lambda)=\sqrt{k^{2}-4mn}>0
.
Then using
SL(2,\mathbb{Z})
we can bring
n,m,k
to satisfy (see chapter 15 of
[
18
]
)

\displaystyle 0<k<\sqrt{k^{2}-4nm}<\min(k+2|n|,k+2|m|),

(2.5)

unless
k^{2}-4nm
is a full square, in which case one can choose new
n,m,k
such that

\displaystyle n=0,\quad-k<m\leq k.

(2.6)

With the help of an appropriate
O(1,1)
transformation the corresponding generating matrix can be brought to the form

\displaystyle\Lambda=\begin{pmatrix}1&{n\over a}\\
m&a\end{pmatrix},~a={1\over 2}(k+\sqrt{k^{2}-4mn}).

(2.7)

Since
\Lambda
is integral, it is contained in its dual
\Lambda^{\perp}\supseteq\Lambda
.
We call the following quotient the “glue group”

{\sf G}\>=\Lambda^{\perp}/\Lambda=\mathbb{Z}^{2}/g_{\Lambda}=\mathbb{Z}_{p}\times\mathbb{Z}_{q}.

(2.8)

Here

\displaystyle p=gcd(2n,2m,k),\qquad q=|{\sf G}|/p,

(2.9)

where
|{\sf G}|
is the order of the group. This follows from the invariant factor decomposition of finitely generated abelian groups.

|{\sf G}|=|\det(\Lambda)/\det(\Lambda^{\perp})|=k^{2}-4mn.

(2.10)

One possible parametrization of the elements of
{\sf G}
is as pairs
{\sf g}=(a,b)
of integer numbers
0\leq a<p,\,0\leq b<q
. Another useful parametrization is as integer vectors
\ell({\sf g})\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
modulo columns of
g_{\Lambda}
. An explicit map between these two parametrizations may be nontrivial.

The metric on
\mathbb{R}^{1,1}
induces a scalar product on
{\sf G}
, which we denote by
\eta
. It is defined up to shifts by integer numbers, which reflects different choices of representatives in the quotient
\Lambda^{\perp}/\Lambda
.
One can consider codes over
{\sf G}
, namely additive linear subspaces
{\mathcal{C}}\subset{\sf G}^{c}
. A code would be called even if the norm of each element defined with
\eta
is even

\displaystyle\eta({\sf c},{\sf c})=\sum_{i=1}^{c}\eta({\sf g}_{i},{\sf g}_{i})=\sum_{i=1}^{c}\ell({\sf g}_{i})^{T}g_{\Lambda}^{-1}\ell({\sf g_{i}})\in 2\mathbb{Z},\quad{\sf c}\equiv({\sf g}_{1},\dots,{\sf g}_{c})\in{\mathcal{C}}.

(2.11)

Despite that
\eta
is defined only up to certain integer shifts, whether
\eta({\sf g},{\sf g})
is even does not depend on the representative of
\sf g
.
With help of
\eta
one can also define dual code
{\mathcal{C}}^{\perp}
, as the group of all elements
{\sf c}_{1}\in{\mathcal{C}}^{\perp}\subset{\sf G}^{c}
satisfying
\eta({\sf c}_{1},{\sf c}_{2})\in\mathbb{Z}
for any
{\sf c}_{2}\in{\mathcal{C}}
. A code is called self-dual when
{\mathcal{C}}={\mathcal{C}}^{\perp}
.

2.2
Construction A

Starting from a code
{\mathcal{C}}\subset{\sf G}^{c}
, Construction A associates to it the lattice
\Lambda_{\mathcal{C}}
,

\displaystyle\underbrace{\Lambda\oplus\dots\oplus\Lambda}_{c\,{\rm times}}\subset\Lambda_{\mathcal{C}}\subset\underbrace{\Lambda^{\perp}\oplus\dots\oplus\Lambda^{\perp}}_{c\,{\rm times}}\subset\mathbb{R}^{c,c},

(2.12)

defined as the set of vectors in
\Lambda^{\perp}\oplus\dots\oplus\Lambda^{\perp}
mapped into
{\mathcal{C}}\subset{\sf G}^{c}
under (
2.8
). Then it is straightforward to see that an even
{\mathcal{C}}
would give rise to an even lattice
\Lambda_{\mathcal{C}}
and a self-dual
{\mathcal{C}}
to a self-dual
\Lambda_{\mathcal{C}}
, both understood with respect to Lorentzian scalar product
g_{L}=g\oplus\dots\oplus g
in
\mathbb{R}^{c,c}
.

To define a Narain theory, besides Lorentzian scalar product, even self-dual lattice should also be equipped with the Euclidean scalar product. For each
\Lambda
defined in previous section there is
O(1,1)
ambiguity how it can be embedded in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. Thus, very explicitly we can write

\displaystyle v=\begin{pmatrix}O(\Lambda^{\perp}\ell_{1}+\Lambda k_{1})\\
\vdots\\
O(\Lambda^{\perp}\ell_{c}+\Lambda k_{c})\\
\end{pmatrix}\in\Lambda_{\mathcal{C}},\quad(\ell_{1},\dots,\ell_{c})\in{\mathcal{C}},\quad k_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}^{2},

(2.13)

where in (
2.13
) we parametrize elements of
{\sf G}=\mathbb{Z}^{2}/g_{\Lambda}
by vectors
\ell
.
Matrix
O
is an arbitrary element from
O(1,1)
. In principle we can introduce
c
different transformations
O_{i}\in O(1,1)
acting in each
\mathbb{R}^{2}
plane. In this case most of the construction remains intact, but the permutation of factors of
{\sf G}
in
{\mathcal{C}}\subset{\sf G}^{c}
, which is conventionally considered to be a code equivalence, would no longer yield physically equivalent lattices. In what follows we assume that all factors
O
are the same.

The main result of this section is as follows. For any glue group
{\sf G}
defined via (
2.8
) with the help of an appropriate even lattice
\Lambda\subset\mathbb{R}^{1,1}
, any even self-dual code
{\mathcal{C}}\subset{\sf G}^{c}
via Construction A defines Narain lattice
\Lambda_{\mathcal{C}}
(
2.13
) and hence a Narain CFT. We will call such CFTs code theories.

2.3
Example: square glue lattice

Consider the following glue lattice generating matrix

\Lambda=\sqrt{p}\,g,\quad p\in\mathbb{N}.

(2.14)

The dual lattice is generated by
\Lambda^{\perp}=I/\sqrt{p}
. Clearly this is the case of
n=m=0,k=p
and the glue group
{\sf G}=\mathbb{Z}_{p}\times\mathbb{Z}_{p}
is parametrized by
{\sf g}=(a,b)\in{\sf G}
,
0\leq a,b<p
and

\displaystyle\ell({\sf g})=(a,b)^{T}.

(2.15)

It is convenient to write codewords
{\sf c}=({\sf g_{1}},\dots,{\sf g}_{c})\in{\mathcal{C}}\subset{\sf G}^{c}
as

\displaystyle{\sf c}=(a_{1},\dots,a_{c}|b_{1},\dots,b_{c})\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{2c}.

(2.16)

A code can be defined with a
2c\times d
generating matrix
G_{\mathcal{C}}
such that

\displaystyle{\sf c}=G_{\mathcal{C}}\,r,\quad r\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{d},

(2.17)

where
d
depends on
p
. For prime
p
generating matrix is
2c\times c
and using permutations can always be brought to the form

\displaystyle G_{\mathcal{C}}=\left(I\,|\,B^{T}\right),

(2.18)

where
B
is an integer values antisymmetric
c\times c
matrix defined mod
p
,

\displaystyle B^{T}=-B\mod\,p,

(2.19)

and
B_{ii}=0
. Applying Construction A to such codes leads to Narain lattices generated by

\displaystyle\Lambda_{\mathcal{C}}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}pI_{c}&B\\
0&I_{c}\end{array}\right)/\sqrt{p},\qquad g_{L}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&I_{c}\\
I_{c}&0\end{array}\right),

associated with the Lorentzian scalar product
g_{L}
. Here
I_{c}
is a
c
-dimensional identity matrix.

The form of
\Lambda_{\mathcal{C}}
provides a clear interpretation – corresponding Narain theory describes
c
scalars compactified on a
c
-dimensional cube of size
1/\sqrt{p}
in presence of B-field
B
.

The construction described above is a straightforward generalization of the original construction of
[
14
,
9
]
, which considered the case of
p=2
. It has been recently introduced independently in
[
16
]
.

2.4
Generalization: isodual codes

Permutation of factors
S:{\sf G}^{c}\rightarrow{\sf G}^{c}

\displaystyle S:({\sf g}_{1},\dots,{\sf g}_{c})\rightarrow({\sf g}_{i_{1}},\dots,{\sf g}_{i_{c}})

(2.25)

is the simplest example of code equivalences, defined as a linear transformation

S:{\sf G}^{c}\rightarrow{\sf G}^{c}
which preserves scalar product
\eta
.
Provided dual code is equivalent to the original one

\displaystyle{\mathcal{C}}^{\perp}=S({\mathcal{C}}),

(2.26)

such a code is called isodual. From this follows
S^{2}=1
, i.e. when
S
is a permutation, it is a pair-wise permutation, with the corresponding matrix satisfying
S^{T}=S
.

We can introduce evenness as the condition for all codeword
({\sf g}_{1},\dots,{\sf g}_{c})\in{\mathcal{C}}\subset{\sf G}^{c}
to have even scalar product with its permuted self,

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{c}\eta({\sf g}_{i},{\sf g}_{S(i)})\in 2\mathbb{Z}.

(2.27)

An even, isodual code
{\mathcal{C}}
with respect to some pairwise permutation
S
, via Construction A (
2.13
) gives rise to an even lattice, which is self-dual with respect to Lorentzian scalar product

\displaystyle g_{L}=g\otimes S.

(2.28)

In this way isodual codes also can be used to define Narain lattices and code CFTs.

2.5
CFT spectral gap and code modified Hamming distance

The capacity of a code to preserve information is rooted in the ability to unambiguously restore
{\sf c}\in{\mathcal{C}}
in case it got corrupted during transmission process. Speaking colloquially, the code is better (stronger), provided all codewords are maximally distinct from each other. To quantify that coding theory uses Hamming distance
d({\sf c},{\sf c}^{\prime})
, defined as the number of different components of
{\sf c}
,
{\sf c}^{\prime}
. For the binary linear code, when the components of
{\sf c}
are either zero or one,
d({\sf c},{\sf c}^{\prime})=|{\sf c}-{\sf c}^{\prime}|_{1}=|{\sf c}-{\sf c}^{\prime}|_{2}^{2}
. When the code is not binary, there could be various generalizations of Hamming distance, relevant for different practical scenarios. Thus, if the transmission error changes a codeword component’s value randomly, with the new value being unrelated to the original one, then Hamming distance defined as the number of distinct components is still relevant. Yet, in many practical settings the nature of errors are such that the new values tend to be closer to old ones, which suggest using various metrics. In particular in case of codes over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
one often defines

\displaystyle d({\sf c},{\sf c}^{\prime})=|{\sf c}-{\sf c}^{\prime}|_{2}^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{c}|a_{i}-a^{\prime}_{i}|^{2},

(2.29)

\displaystyle{\sf c}=(a_{1},\dots,a_{c})\in{\mathcal{C}}\subset\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{c},\quad 0\leq a_{i}<p.

(2.30)

For a linear code, the code distance is

\displaystyle d({\mathcal{C}})=\min_{0\leq i,j\leq|{\mathcal{C}}|-1}d({\sf c}_{i},{\sf c}_{j})=\min_{{\sf c}\in{\mathcal{C}},{\sf c}\neq 0}d(0,{\sf c}).

(2.31)

The value
w({\sf c}):=d(0,{\sf c})
is usually called the weight of
{\sf c}
.

Construction A (
2.13
) relates each codeword
{\sf c}\in{\mathcal{C}}
to a family of vectors

v({\sf c})
. We define the weight of
{\sf c}
to be the minimal length square among all such
v
,

\displaystyle w({\sf c})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\min_{k_{1},\dots,k_{c}\in\mathbb{Z}}|v|^{2},

(2.32)

\displaystyle v

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\ell_{1}+\Lambda k_{1},\dots,\ell_{c}+\Lambda k_{c}),\qquad{\sf c}=(\ell_{1},\dots,\ell_{c})\in{\mathcal{C}}\subset{\sf G}^{c},\quad{\sf G}=\mathbb{Z}^{2}/g_{\Lambda}.

The weight
w({\sf d})
is closely related to the norm of
{\sf c}
calculated with the scalar product
\eta
inherited from
\mathbb{R}^{1,1}
on
{\sf G}
.

Clearly, from this definition follows that code theory spectral gap
\Delta^{*}
, defined as the length-squared of the shortest non-zero vector divided by two, is simply related to code’s generalized Hamming distance

\Delta^{*}={d({\mathcal{C}})\over 2}.

This relation is transparent, but there is one caveat: zero codeword
{\sf c}=0
is mapped into the origin of
\Lambda_{\mathcal{C}}
, as well as many vectors of the form
\Lambda(k_{1},\dots,k_{c})
. The origin is excluded from the consideration, while minimizing over
k_{i}
yields shortest vector of
\Lambda
. We thus have

\displaystyle\Delta^{*}={1\over 2}\min\left({d({\mathcal{C}})},{|v_{\Lambda}|^{2}}\right),

(2.33)

where by
|v_{\Lambda}|
we denoted the length of shortest non-trivial vector of
\Lambda
. This length depends non-trivially on the choice of
n,m,k
and the
O(1,1)
factor (which we absorbed into the definition of
\Lambda
), but an upper bound (
2.36
) is readily available, see below.

For the square lattice of subsection
2.3
we find that
d({\sf c},{\sf c^{\prime}})
is given by (
2.29
)
where by
|a-a^{\prime}|^{2}
with
0\leq a,a^{\prime}<p
we understand

\displaystyle|a-a^{\prime}|^{2}:=\min_{k\in Z}\,(a-a^{\prime}+pk)^{2},

(2.34)

and
|v_{\Lambda}|^{2}=p
.

To obtain the upper bound on the length, in the sense of Euclidean norm, of the shortest vector
v_{\Lambda}
belonging to
(
2.13
) we consider all
\ell_{i}=0
, arbitrary
k_{1}
and
k_{i}=0
for
i>1
. Then the Euclidean norm of corresponding two-dimensional vectors is

\displaystyle|v|^{2}=k_{1}^{T}\Lambda^{T}O^{T}O\Lambda k_{1},

(2.35)

which defines a positive-definite scalar product in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. The shortest vector will necessarily be shorter than
|v_{\Lambda}|^{2}\leq 2|{\sf G}|^{1/2}/\sqrt{3}
, see Appendix
A
, and therefore corresponding code CFT would have the spectral gap not exceeding

\displaystyle\Delta^{*}\leq{\sqrt{k^{2}-4nm}\over\sqrt{3}}.

(2.36)

This is a standard weakness of the Construction A lattices; they always include short vectors of a certain length, which does not increase with
c
. Therefore to construct large spectral gap CFTs with
\Delta^{*}
scaling linearly with
c
we would need to consider a cascade of different constructions by adjusting
k,n,m
together with
c
.

3
Torus partition function of code theories

3.1
Enumerator polynomial and theta-series

One of the central properties of code theories which make them interesting is that their torus partition function can be expressed in a compact way in terms of the so-called code enumerator polynomial which characterizes the corresponding code. Generalization of this result to higher genus partition function is also possible
[
19
,
20
]
.

We first define the full enumerator polynomial of a code
{\mathcal{C}}
as a vehicle to count how many times each element
{\sf g}\in{\sf G}
appears in each codeword of
{\mathcal{C}}
,

P_{C}(\{x_{\sf g}\})=\sum_{({\sf g}_{1},\dots,{\sf g}_{c})\in{\mathcal{C}}}\prod_{i=1}^{c}x_{{\sf g}_{i}}.

(3.37)

This is a degree
n
homogeneous polynomial of
|{\sf G}|
variables
x_{\sf g}
.

The polynomial of the dual code
{\mathcal{C}}^{\perp}
is related to the polynomial of
{\mathcal{C}}
by the MacWilliams identity

P_{{\mathcal{C}}^{\perp}}(\{\tilde{x}_{\sf g}\})=P_{\mathcal{C}}(\{x_{\sf g}\}),

(3.38)

where

\tilde{x}_{\tilde{\sf g}}={1\over\sqrt{\left|{\sf G}\right|}}\sum_{{\sf g}\in{\sf G}}\exp(-2\pi i\eta(\tilde{\sf g},{\sf g}))x_{\sf g}.

(3.39)

In particular, this means that the enumerator polynomial of a self-dual code is invariant under the above transformation.

The torus partition function of a code CFT, associated with
\Lambda_{\mathcal{C}}
obtained by Construction A, is given in terms of
P_{\mathcal{C}}
,

\mathcal{Z}_{{\mathcal{C}}}(\tau)={P_{\mathcal{C}}(\{\psi_{\sf g}(\tau)\})},

(3.40)

where

\displaystyle\psi_{\ell}(\tau)={1\over|\eta(\tau)|^{2}}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\exp(i\pi v^{T}\Omega v),

(3.41)

\displaystyle v=\Lambda^{\perp}\ell+\Lambda\,k,\quad\Omega=\begin{pmatrix}i\tau_{2}&\tau_{1}\\
\tau_{1}&i\tau_{2}\end{pmatrix},

(3.42)

\tau=\tau_{1}+i\tau_{2}
is the torus modular parameter and in (
3.41
) we parametrize elements of
{\sf G}
with help of vectors
\ell\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}/g_{\Lambda}
. We also absorbed
O\in O(1,1)
into the definition of
\Lambda
.

The modular group, generated by
T:\tau\to\tau+1
and
S:\tau\to-1/\tau
, transforms
\Omega
as follows

T\circ\Omega=\Omega+g,

(3.43)

S\circ\Omega=-\Omega^{-1}.

(3.44)

Functions
\psi_{\ell}
transform accordingly

\displaystyle T\circ\psi_{\ell}(\tau)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\psi_{\ell}(\tau+1)=\exp(i\pi v^{T}g_{\Lambda}v)\psi_{\ell}(\tau),\quad v=\Lambda^{\perp}\ell,

(3.45)

\displaystyle S\circ\psi_{\ell}(\tau)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\psi_{\ell}(-1/\tau)={1\over\sqrt{\left|{\sf G}\right|}}\sum_{\ell^{\prime}\in{\sf G}=\mathbb{Z}^{2}/g_{\Lambda}}\exp(-2\pi iu^{T}g_{\Lambda}v)\psi_{\ell^{\prime}}(\tau),\quad u=\Lambda^{\perp}\ell^{\prime}.\qquad

(3.46)

Evenness and self-duality of
{\mathcal{C}}
ensure that
\mathcal{Z}_{{\mathcal{C}}}(\tau)
is invariant under
T
and
S
respectively. Indeed, since the code is even, for any
(\ell_{1},\dots,\ell_{n})\in{\mathcal{C}}
we have
\sum_{i}\ell_{i}^{T}g_{\Lambda}^{-1}\ell_{i}\in 2\mathbb{Z}
, and therefore
T
is a symmetry of
P_{\mathcal{C}}(\{\psi_{\ell}\})
, while it is invariant under (
3.46
) because of self-duality (
3.38
,
3.39
).

3.2
Example: theta series for square glue lattice

For the lattice (
2.14
) functions
\psi_{\sf g}
defined in (
3.41
) read

\psi_{ab}={1\over|\eta|^{2}}\sum_{k_{1},k_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}}q^{{p\over 4}({a+b\over p}+k_{1}+k_{2})^{2}}\bar{q}^{{p\over 4}({a-b\over p}+k_{1}-k_{2})^{2}},

(3.47)

where
(a,b)\in{\sf G}=\mathbb{Z}_{p}\times\mathbb{Z}_{p}

5
Asymptotically large
c

When
c\gg 1
asymptotic behavior of spectral gap is not known. Spinless modular bootstrap bounds
\Delta^{*}/c
to be less than or equal to
1/\pi^{2}
(with this value being obtained numerically)
[
6
]
, while the full set of bootstrap constraints is likely to significantly decrease this value. Averaging over the whole moduli space of Narain theories provides a lower bound on
\Delta^{*}/c
to be
1/(2\pi e)

[
21
]
. Ref.
[
3
]
conjectured this value to be asymptotically saturated,

\displaystyle\lim_{c\rightarrow\infty}{\Delta^{*}\over c}={1\over 2\pi e}.

(5.171)

For this to be true, i.e. for the mean value to (asymptotically) be the largest possible value, the distribution of spectral gaps on the Narain moduli space for large
c
must be very sharply peaked around the mean without outliers. Thus, for consistency, as a necessary condition, variance should be very small. Using the ensemble of code CFTs, as well as chiral cousins of Narain theories, ref.
[
3
]
has shown the variance of density of states distribution to be exponentially suppressed
\sim e^{-{\mathcal{O}}(c)}
, the conclusion consequently confirmed for the Narain theories in
[
22
]
. This does not constitute a proof of (
5.171
) as
variance is not sensitive to possible outliers.

The conjecture of
[
3
]
is based on similarity between the ensemble of codes, ensemble of sphere packings, and the ensemble (space) of CFTs, and the problems of maximizing code Hamming distance, density of sphere packing and CFT spectral gap. Specifically for codes, there is an expectation that the Gilbert-Varshamov bound (the value resulting from averaging over all codes) would asymptotically yield the best value of Hamming distance to code size ratio
[
23
,
24
]
. Similar expectation holds for the maximal density of lattice sphere packing: the densest packing to asymptotically saturate the Minkowski bound, which is simply averaged value over all possible lattices. (For sphere packings of general kind stronger asymptotic value is expected
[
25
]
.) While we leave validity of (
5.171
) for future studies, here we show there are codes theories achieving this value of
\Delta^{*}
for large
c
.

The Construction A used in this paper has a fundamental limitation: the corresponding lattices have vectors of certain length no matter how big the dimension
c
is. This is formalized in equation (
2.36
), which provides an upper bound on
\Delta^{*}
. Thus, to obtain
\Delta^{*}
scaling linearly with
c
one has to adjust
n,m,k
together with
c
such that
|{\sf G}|
grows as or faster than
c
. Here for simplicity we focus on the square gluing lattice
n=m=0
, with prime
k=p
, discussed in sections
2.3
,
3.2
.
The spectral gap is given by (
2.33
) with
|v_{\Lambda}|=\sqrt{p}
,

\displaystyle\Delta^{*}={1\over 2}\min\left({d({\mathcal{C}})},p\right).

(5.172)

The best (maximal) generalized Hamming distance
d
beyond small
c
values is not known. One nevertheless can bound
d
from below by consider ensemble averaging, the so-called Gilbert-Varshamov bound. Then, similarly to the case of binary linear codes one may expect best
d/c
to asymptotically approach the bound when
c\rightarrow\infty
.

By averaged polynomial
\bar{P}(\{x_{ab}\})
we mean enumerator polynomial averaged over all
p^{c(c-1)/2}
possible codes parametrized by
B
, see (
2.18
). From the CFT point of view this is the calculation of averaged torus partition function.
So far we are interested only in
d
, or alternatively only in mass but not spin of the lightest non-trivial state, we can consider torus parameter to be purely imaginary
\tau=i\tau_{2}
. Then function (
3.2
) factorizes

\displaystyle\psi_{ab}(i\tau_{2})={1\over|\eta(\tau)|^{2}}\psi_{a}\psi_{b},\qquad\psi_{a}=\Theta_{2a,p}(i\tau_{2}/2)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}e^{-\pi\tau_{2}(a+kp)^{2}/p}.

(5.173)

Going back to enumerator polynomial, instead of variables
x_{ab}
we use

\displaystyle x_{ab}=t_{a}t_{b},\qquad t_{a}=t_{-a},

(5.174)

where the last property reflects
\psi_{a}=\psi_{-a}
.
We conjecture the form of corresponding averaged enumerator polynomial based on invariance under MacWilliams identity and explicit checks for sufficiently small
n
and prime
p
, for which direct evaluation of
\bar{P}(\{t_{a}t_{b}\})
using computer algebra is feasible,

\displaystyle\bar{P}(\{t_{a}t_{b}\})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{1\over p^{c(c-1)/2}}\sum_{B}P_{{\mathcal{C}}(B)}(\{t_{a}t_{b}\})=

(5.175)

\displaystyle t_{0}^{2c}+{\sum\limits_{k=0}^{p=1}\left(\sum\limits_{a=0}^{p-1}\sum\limits_{b=0}^{p-1}\cos\left({2\pi kab\over p}\right)t_{a}t_{b}\right)^{c}-p\,t_{0}^{c}\left(\sum\limits_{a=0}^{p-1}t_{a}\right)^{c}\over p^{c}}.

Now we are ready to analyze this expression to deduce the lower bound on
\Delta^{*}
. For large
c
, the main contribution to (
5.175
) comes from
k=0
, leading to the averaged partition function

\displaystyle\bar{Z}\approx{1\over|\eta|^{2c}}{\left(\sum\limits_{a=0}^{p-1}\psi_{a}\right)^{2c}\over p^{c}}={1\over|\eta|^{2c}}{\left(\sum\limits_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}e^{-\pi\tau_{2}n^{2}/p}\right)^{2c}\over p^{c}}.

(5.176)

Interpreted as sum over lattice points, the numerator is simply the sum over
2c
-dimensional square lattice of size
1/\sqrt{p}
. On the length scales of
\sim 1/\sqrt{p}
or larger this is just the homogeneous distribution of points with the averaged density
1/(1/\sqrt{p}^{2c})=p^{c}
. This factor exactly cancels
p^{c}
in the denominator of (
5.176
) and we find density of states

\displaystyle\rho(\Delta)={(2\pi)^{c}\Delta^{c-1}\over\Gamma(c)}

(5.177)

valid on scales
\Delta\gtrsim 1/\sqrt{p}
. This is exactly the density of states of “
U(1)
-gravity” – Narain theory averaged over the whole moduli space. Accordingly, the threshold for the density to become of order one is
\Delta=c/(2\pi e)
, which is our Gilbert-Varshamov bound. For this result to be valid we must require

p/2>c/(2\pi e)
, otherwise shortest vector of
\Lambda_{\mathcal{C}}
would have length
\sqrt{p}
.

To conclude, for sufficiently large
p
we find that the averaged density of states (with zero chemical potential for spin) of
n=m=0
,
and prime
k=p
code theories is the same as the averaged density of states for all Narain theories. In particular in the limit
c\rightarrow\infty
, for
p>c/(\pi e)
there are code theories with
\Delta^{*}/c=1/(2\pi e)
. Provided the conjecture of
[
3
]
is correct, it would mean similar conjecture applies to
n=m=0
, prime
k=p
codes, in the sense that their averaged Hamming distance is asymptotically the best one.

6
Discussion

In this paper we proposed a family of constructions mapping additive codes over abelian groups
{\sf G}=\mathbb{Z}_{p}\times\mathbb{Z}_{q}
to Narain lattices and hence Narain CFTs. Each construction is parametrized by a triple of integer numbers
n,m,k
and an element from
O(1,1)
parameterizing an even “glue” lattice
\Lambda\subset\mathbb{R}^{1,1}
. The resulting Narain lattice
\Lambda_{\mathcal{C}}
associated with a code
{\mathcal{C}}\subset{\sf G}^{c}
obeys

\displaystyle\underbrace{\Lambda\oplus\dots\oplus\Lambda}_{c\,{\rm times}}\subset\Lambda_{\mathcal{C}}\subset\underbrace{\Lambda^{\perp}\oplus\dots\oplus\Lambda^{\perp}}_{c\,{\rm times}}\subset\mathbb{R}^{c,c}.

(6.178)

We call this glue construction following
[
18
]
. This construction generalizes and encompasses those of
[
14
,
9
]
,
[
15
]
and
[
16
]
. We call the CFTs obtained from codes “code theories.” Their torus partition functions
Z_{\mathcal{C}}
are given in terms of the so-called code enumerator polynomials, which are multi-variable polynomials satisfying certain algebraic identities, which guarantee modular invariance of
Z_{\mathcal{C}}
. In this way one can construct many new solutions to modular bootstrap constraints.

We have shown that all conjectural optimal Narain theories for
c\leq 8
identified in
[
7
]
, meaning those with the largest spectral gap of
U(1)^{c}\times U(1)^{c}
primaries are code theories. Furthermore we have shown there are code theories with the spectral gap
\Delta^{*}
scaling linearly with
c\gg 1
,
\Delta^{*}\propto c/(2\pi e)
, with the coefficient which has been conjectured in
[
3
]
to be maximal possible. The message of our work is clear: we conjecture that optimal Narain theories for any
c
are code theories, either following from the constructions outlined in this paper, or their possible generalizations.

Speaking of the latter, one can straightforwardly generalize our construction by starting with a glue lattice
\Lambda\subset\mathbb{R}^{3,3}
or in fact
\Lambda\subset\mathbb{R}^{r,r}
for any
r\geq 2
. Another important direction would be to connect the bottom-up approach of this paper with the top-down approach of
[
13
]
where quantum codes were given an interpretation in terms of CFT Hilbert space extended by defect operators. Finally, given our conjecture that optimal theories are code theories, it would be interesting to develop our approach into a practical way of constructing optimal theories with
c>8
, thus complementing conventional modular bootstrap. This would be an important but challenging task because there is no known efficient methods to construct “good” codes with largest or even large (generalized) Hamming distance. And though there is a finite number of codes for any given
{\sf G}
and
c
, their number grows exponentially with
c
. Furthermore, there is an infinite number of constructions, i.e. infinite number of different
{\sf G}
and
\Lambda
, making the problem seemingly incomprehensible. This pessimistic assessment could be too naive, we expect only finite number of constructions to be relevant for any given
c
. The inequality (
2.36
) as well as the results of section
5
clearly indicate
|{\sf G}|
can not be too small,
|{\sf G}|^{1/2}\geq ac
, for
c\gg 1
with some positive constant
a
. We also strongly suspect large generalized Hamming distance, for given
c
, would require
|{\sf G}|
not be too large. We conjecture this may come from the linear programming constraints stemming from the algebraic identities satisfied by code enumerator polynomial (the MacWilliam identity and the condition due to code evenness), i.e. generalization of Delsarte’s bounds
[
26
]
to the types of codes of interest. For
c\gg 1
we expect the bound to have the form
|{\sf G}|^{1/2}\leq bc
with some
b>a
. Thus for large but finite
c
we expect large but finite number of glue groups satisfying

bc\geq|{\sf G}|^{1/2}\geq ac
. This form of the bound on
|{\sf G}|
is merely a guess; the important point here is the expectation that the problem of identifying the code with largest generalized Hamming distance can be reduced to an optimization problem over a finite set.
Of course even for moderate
c
naive brute-force approaches such as going through all possible codes very quickly becomes unfeasible. The resulting optimization problem over a discrete set would be NP-hard, but novel quantum platforms promise an exciting hope of solving medium-sized discrete optimization problems in real time, the avenue we hope to pursue in the future
[
27
]
.

To conclude, we would like to point out another very important direction for future work – to extend the connection between codes and CFTs beyond the Narain theories.

Appendix A
Shortest vector bound

Let us consider a two-dimensional Euclidean lattice
\sf\Lambda
with the scalar product
g_{2}
. Using rotation and up to an overall rescaling basis vectors can be chosen to be
1
and
\tau=\tau_{1}+i\tau_{2}
, where we introduced complex coordinates on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. In other words

\displaystyle g_{2}={\sf\Lambda}^{T}{\sf\Lambda},\qquad{\sf\Lambda}=\alpha\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&\tau_{1}\\
0&\tau_{2}\end{array}\right),

and
\alpha
is some scalar factor. Using
GL(2,\mathbb{Z})
transformations, together with an appropriate rotation and rescaling, we can bring
\tau
to belong to fundamental domain

\displaystyle|\tau_{1}|\leq 1/2,\quad\tau_{2}\geq 0,\quad|\tau|\geq 1.

(1.182)

In this case the shortest vector is
\alpha(1,0)^{T}
and its norm is
\alpha^{2}
.
From (
A
) we find
\alpha^{4}={\rm det}g_{2}/\tau_{2}^{2}
and from (
1.182
) we know
\tau_{2}^{2}\geq 3/4
. We therefore find the bound

\displaystyle\alpha^{2}\leq{2\over\sqrt{3}}\sqrt{{\rm det}g_{2}},

(1.183)

which in many cases is conservative. Applying that to (
2.35
) we obtain (
2.36
).

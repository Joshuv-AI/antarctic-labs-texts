Rigidity for inscribed radius estimate of asymptotically hyperbolic Einstein manifold
Xiaoshang Jin

Abstract
The inscribed radius of a compact manifold with boundary is bounded above if its Ricci curvature and mean curvature are bounded from below. The rigidity result implies that the upper bound can be achieved only in space form. In this paper, we generalize this result to asymptotically hyperbolic Einstein manifold. We get an upper bound of the relative volume of AH manifold and if we combine it with the recent work of Wang and Zhou, then the rigidity is obtained.



1
Introduction

Let
(N,g)
be an
n+1
dimensional complete manifold satisfying that
Ric_{g}\geq nk>0,
the classic Myers theorem tells us that the diameter of
N
satisfies
\mathrm{diam}(N,g)\leq\frac{\pi}{\sqrt{k}}.
In 1975, Cheng
[
4
]
showed that the equality holds if and only if
(N,g)
is isometric to

S^{n+1}_{k}.
Here
S^{n+1}_{k}
denote the
n+1
dimensional standard sphere of curvature
k.

If the lower bound of Ricci curvature is negative, then we could consider the manifold with boundary and have the following estimate of inscribed radius. Let
N
be the
n+1
dimensional manifold with smooth boundary
\partial N.
If
Ric_{g}\geq-n
and the mean curvature of
\partial N
satisfies
H\geq nk>n
for some constant
k>1,
then

\mathrm{Inraj}(N,g)=\sup\limits_{x\in N}d_{g}(x,\partial N)\leq\coth^{-1}k.

(1.1)

It is showed in
[
12
]
and
[
15
]
that the equality holds if and only if
(N,g)
is isometric to a geodesic ball of radius
\coth^{-1}k
in hyperbolic space.

In this paper, we study the rigidity problem via inscribed radius estimate in AHE manifolds. Let’s recall some definitions of AH and AHE manifolds.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
\overline{X}
be an
n+1-
dimensional compact manifold with interior
X
and with boundary
\partial X=\Sigma
which is a smooth
n-
dimensional compact manifold. We say that a complete and noncompact manifold
(X,g^{+})
is a conformally compact manifold if there exists a smooth defining function
\rho
on
\overline{X},
such that

\rho>0\ \ in\ \ X,\ \ \ \ \rho=0\ \ on\ \ \Sigma,\ \ \ \ d\rho\neq 0\ \ on\ \ \Sigma.

(1.2)

and
g=\rho^{2}g^{+}
can extend to a sooth Riemannian metric on
\overline{X}.

The induced metric
\hat{g}=g|_{\Sigma}
is called the boundary metric. It is well-known that
g^{+}
determines a conformal structure on the boundary
\Sigma
and we call
(\Sigma,[\hat{g}])
the conformal infinity of
g^{+}.
If in addition
|d\rho|^{2}_{g}=1
on the boundary
\Sigma,
then we say that
(X,g^{+})
is an asymptotically hyperbolic (or AH for short) manifold.

Let
(X^{n+1},g^{+})
be a conformally compact manifold satisfying
Ric[g^{+}]=-ng^{+},
then
|d\rho|^{2}_{g}=1
automatically holds on
\Sigma.
We call
(X,g^{+})
an asymptotically hyperbolic Einstein (or AHE for short) manifold in this case.

For an AH manifold
(X,g^{+})
with conformal boundary
(\Sigma,\hat{g}),
there exists a special defining function
x
such that
the compactification
g=x^{2}g^{+}
satisfies
|dx|^{2}_{g}=1
in a neighbourhood of
\Sigma
and we call
x
the geodesic defining function.

Since the AHE manifold is noncompact, we could consider the exhaust
\{E_{x}\}_{x\in(0,\delta)}
of
X
by compact sets. For each
E_{x}
we define the difference of inscribed radius between
E_{x}
and the geodesic ball in hyperbolic space. Then we study the limit as
x
tends to
0
and get the rigidity result. More concretely,

Theorem 1.2
.

Suppose that
(X,g^{+})
is an
n+1-
dimensional AHE manifold with minimized Yamabe boundary metric
(\Sigma,\hat{g})
of positive scalar curvature and
x
is the geodesic defining function. For sufficiently small
x>0,
let

F(x)=\coth^{-1}{h_{x}}-\mathrm{Inraj}(E_{x},g^{+})

(1.3)

where
E_{x}=X\setminus\Sigma\times(0,x)
and
h_{x}=\min\limits_{p\in\partial E_{x}}\frac{H_{x}(p)}{n}
and
H_{x}
is the mean curvature of
\partial E_{x}.
Then
F(x)\geq 0
and the limit
\lim\limits_{x\rightarrow 0^{+}}F(x)=F(0)
exists. Furthermore,
F(0)=0
if and only if
(X,g^{+})
is isometric to the hyperbolic space.

Here is the outline of this paper. In section 2, we firstly review some materials about the relative volume of AH manifold, and then research some properties of the level sets in AH manifold, including the mean curvature, the inscribed radius and the cut locus. In section 3, we study the upper bound of the relative volume and prove a new relative volume inequality. As a consequence, the rigidity theorem can be obtained by utilizing the recent results of Wang and Zhou.

2
The analysis on AH manifold

In this section we assume that
(X,g^{+})
is an AH manifold satisfying

Ric[g^{+}]\geq-ng^{+},\ \ R[g^{+}]+n(n+1)=o(x^{2}),

and the boundary metric
(\Sigma,\hat{g})
is the minimized Yamabe metric of positive scalar curvature.
g=x^{2}g^{+}
is the geodesic comactification near conformal infinity. We could assume that
x\in[0,\delta)
for some
\delta>0.

2.1
The relative volume function

For any
p\in X,
by the volume comparison theorem, the limit of
\frac{V(\partial B(p,t),g^{+})}{\omega_{n}\sinh^{n}t}
always exists as
t
tends to infinity. In
[
10
]
, the author showed that the limit is dependent on
p
for AH manifold. Then we could define the relative volume function
\mathcal{A}:X\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
by

\mathcal{A}(p)=2^{n}\cdot\lim\limits_{t\rightarrow+\infty}e^{-nt}\cdot V(\partial B(p,t),g^{+})

(2.1)

Let
E
be any compact set in
X,
we can use the same method as lemma 4.1 in
[
14
]
to show that the following limit also exists
(one can see section 3 in
[
10
]
for more details).

\mathcal{A}(E)=2^{n}\cdot\lim\limits_{t\rightarrow+\infty}e^{-nt}\cdot V(\partial B(E,t),g^{+})

(2.2)

Then we have the following estimate of relative volume (lemma 3.1 in
[
10
]
:

\forall p\in E,\mathcal{A}(p)\leq\mathcal{A}(E)\leq e^{n\cdot diam(E,g^{+})}\mathcal{A}(p).

(2.3)

We recall the important relative volume inequality in
[
14
]
:

\forall p\in X,\ (\frac{Y(\partial X,[\hat{g}])}{Y(\mathbb{S}^{n},[g_{\mathbb{S}}])})^{\frac{n}{2}}\leq\frac{\mathcal{A}(p)}{\omega_{n}}\leq\frac{V(B(p,t),g^{+})}{V(B(o,t),g^{\mathbb{H}})}\leq 1.

(2.4)

2.2
The mean curvature of the level set

Set
X_{x}=\Sigma\times(0,x)
for
x\in(0,\delta)
and
E_{x}=X\setminus X_{x}.
Let

\Sigma_{x}=\partial X_{x}=\partial E_{x}=\Sigma\times\{x\}

be the level set of the geodesic defining function.
Let
H_{x}
denote the mean curvature of
\Sigma_{x}
in
(E_{x},g^{+})
with respect to the outer unit vector.
A direct calculation shows that

H_{x}=\Delta_{g^{+}}(-\ln x)=n-x\Delta_{g}x

(2.5)

The standard formulas for conformal changes imply that the scalar curvature

S|_{\Sigma_{x}}=-2n\frac{\Delta_{g}x}{x}+tr_{g}(Ric[g^{+}]+ng^{+})=-2n\frac{\Delta_{g}x}{x}+o(1).

(2.6)

Hence

H_{x}=n+\frac{x^{2}\cdot S|_{\Sigma_{x}}}{2n}+o(x^{2})

(2.7)

Set

s_{x}=\min\{S(p):p\in\Sigma_{x}\},\ \ \ \ \ h_{x}=\min\limits_{p\in\Sigma_{x}}\frac{H_{x}(p)}{n}=1+\frac{x^{2}\cdot s_{x}}{2n^{2}}+o(x^{2})

(2.8)

When restricted on the boundary, by lemma 4.1 in
[
9
]
(or lemma 1.2 in
[
1
]
),

s_{0}=S|_{\Sigma}=\frac{n}{n-1}S_{\hat{g}}.

(2.9)

Recall we assume that the conformal infinity is of positive Yamabe type and we choose the minimized Yamabe metric
\hat{g},
hence the scalar curvature
S_{\hat{g}}
is a positive constant on
\Sigma.
Then
H_{x}\geq nh_{x}>n
at least for sufficiently small
x.
Now we can consider
\coth^{-1}h_{x}
and it denotes the radius of the geodesic ball in hyperbolic space whose mean curvature is
nh_{x}.

Then

\coth^{-1}h_{x}=\frac{1}{2}\ln(1+\frac{4n^{2}}{x^{2}\cdot s_{x}+o(x^{2})})

(2.10)

Lemma 2.1
.

The function
\coth^{-1}h_{x}+\ln x
is convergent to
\frac{1}{2}\ln\frac{4n(n-1)}{S_{\hat{g}}}
as
x
tends to
0.

Proof.

This is trivial as

\lim\limits_{x\rightarrow 0}(\coth^{-1}h_{x}+\ln x)=\lim\limits_{x\rightarrow 0}\frac{1}{2}\ln(x^{2}+\frac{4n^{2}}{s_{x}+o(1)})=\frac{1}{2}\ln\frac{4n(n-1)}{S_{\hat{g}}}.

∎

2.3
The inscribed radius and the cut locus of level sets

Since
E_{x}
is closed of the compact manifold
\overline{X},

E_{x}
and its boundary
\Sigma_{x}
are also compact sets. Consider the inscribed radius
of
E_{x}:

\mathrm{Inraj}(E_{x},g^{+})=\sup\limits_{p\in E_{x}}d_{g^{+}}(p,\Sigma_{x}).

(2.11)

Definition 2.2
.

Foot point: For any
p\in E_{x},
by the compactness of
(E_{x},g^{+}),
there exists at least one point
q\in\Sigma_{x}
such that

d_{g^{+}}(p,\Sigma_{x})=d_{g^{+}}(p,q).
We call
q
a foot point on
\Sigma_{x}
of
p.

For any
q\in\Sigma_{x},
we set
\sigma_{q}:[0,T)\rightarrow E_{x}
to be the normal geodesic satisfying
\sigma_{q}(0)=q
and
\sigma_{q}^{\prime}(0)\bot T_{q}\Sigma_{x}.

If
q
is a foot point on
\Sigma_{x}
of some point
p\in E_{x},
then
\sigma_{q}:[0,l]\rightarrow E_{x}
is the unique normal minimal geodesic connecting
q
and
p
where
l=d_{g^{+}}(p,q).

We define the function
\tau_{x}:\Sigma_{x}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
by

\tau_{x}(q)=\sup\{t>0:d_{g^{+}}(\sigma_{q}(t),\Sigma_{x})=t\}.

(2.12)

Then it is easy to prove that
\mathrm{Inraj}(E_{x},g^{+})=\sup\{\tau_{x}(q):q\in\Sigma_{x}\}.

Definition 2.3
.

Cut locus: We call
Cut(\Sigma_{x})=\{\sigma_{q}(\tau_{x}(q)):q\in\Sigma_{x}\}
the cut
locus for the boundary
\Sigma_{x}
in
E_{x}.

The classic Riemann geometry theory tells us that
Cut(\Sigma_{x})
is a closed subset and the distance function
d_{g^{+}}(\Sigma_{x},\cdot)
is smooth in
E_{x}\setminus Cut(\Sigma_{x}).
Furthermore, if
p=\sigma_{q}(\tau_{x}(q))\in Cut(\Sigma_{x}),
then either
p
is the first focal point of
\Sigma_{x}
along
\sigma_{q},
or there exists another foot point
q^{\prime}
on
\Sigma_{x}
of
p.

Lemma 2.4
.

For any different number
x_{1},x_{2}\in(0,\delta),
the cut locus are the same:
Cut(\Sigma_{x_{1}})=Cut(\Sigma_{x_{2}}).

Proof.

We may assume that
x_{1}<x_{2}.
For any
q_{1}\in\Sigma_{x_{1}},
consider the normal geodesic
\sigma_{q_{1}}(t)
with
\sigma_{q_{1}}(0)=q_{1}
and
\sigma_{q_{1}}^{\prime}(0)\bot T_{q_{1}}\Sigma_{x_{1}}.
Notice that
g(\nabla x,\nabla x)=1
on
\overline{X}_{\delta}
implies that

g^{+}(\nabla[g^{+}](-\ln x),\nabla[g^{+}](-\ln x))=1.

(2.13)

Hence
-\ln x
is the distance function on
(X_{\delta},g^{+}).

\Sigma_{x_{1}}
is the level set of the distance function
x
in
\overline{X}_{\delta},
and it is also the level set of
-\ln x
in
E_{x_{1}}.
We get that

\sigma_{q_{1}}^{\prime}(0)=\nabla[g^{+}](\ln x)|_{q_{1}}
and
d_{g^{+}}(q_{1},\Sigma_{x_{2}})=\ln\frac{x_{2}}{x_{1}}.
By gauss lemma,

\sigma_{q_{1}}(\ln\frac{x_{2}}{x_{1}})=q_{2}\in\Sigma_{x_{2}},\ \ \ \sigma_{q_{1}}^{\prime}(\ln\frac{x_{2}}{x_{1}})=\nabla[g^{+}](\ln x)|_{q_{2}}\bot T_{q_{2}}\Sigma_{x_{2}}.

(2.14)

Then
\tau_{x_{1}}(q_{1})=\tau_{x_{2}}(q_{2})+\ln\frac{x_{2}}{x_{1}},
which would imply that
\sigma_{q_{1}}(\tau_{x_{1}}(q_{1}))=\sigma_{q_{2}}(\tau_{x_{2}}(q_{2}))
Hence
Cut(\Sigma_{x_{1}})\subseteq Cut(\Sigma_{x_{2}}).
The opposite direction could be obtained in the same way.
∎

From the above lemma, we may define the cut locus of
(\Sigma,\hat{g})
in
X
by

Cut(\Sigma)=Cut(\Sigma_{x})

(2.15)

for any
x\in(0,\delta).
We also get that for any
p\in E_{x_{2}}\subseteq E_{x_{1}},

d_{g^{+}}(p,\Sigma_{x_{1}})=d_{g^{+}}(p,\Sigma_{x_{2}})+\ln\frac{x_{2}}{x_{1}}.

(2.16)

Then
d_{g^{+}}(p,\Sigma_{x})+\ln x=c(p)
is a constant only depending on
p.
As a consequence, we deduce the following lemma:

Lemma 2.5
.

\mathrm{Inraj}(E_{x},g^{+})+\ln x
is a constant function for
x\in(0,\delta).

Furthermore, the constant
c_{0}=\mathrm{Inraj}(E_{x},g^{+})+\ln x=\max\limits_{p\in Cut(\Sigma)}c(p).

Combining lemma 2.1 and lemma 2.5, we get that

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{x\rightarrow 0^{+}}F(x)

\displaystyle=\lim\limits_{x\rightarrow 0^{+}}[(\coth^{-1}h_{x}+\ln x)-(\mathrm{Inraj}(E_{x},g^{+})+\ln x)]

(2.17)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\ln\frac{4n(n-1)}{S_{\hat{g}}}-c_{0}

Now we claim that
F(0)
is a scaling invariant. In fact, let
\hat{h}=K^{2}\hat{g}\in[\hat{g}]
for some positive constant
K.

Then
Kx
is the unique geodesic defining function of
X
with boundary metric
(\Sigma,\hat{h})
and the scalar curvature
S_{\hat{h}}=K^{-2}S_{\hat{g}}.
Let
h=(Kx)^{2}g^{+}=K^{2}g,
then
\Sigma\times(0,\varepsilon)_{g}=\Sigma\times(0,K\varepsilon)_{h}
for small
\varepsilon>0.
If we use
E_{y}(h)
to denote the complementary set of
\Sigma\times(0,y)_{h}
in
X,
then the constant

c_{0}(\hat{h})\equiv\mathrm{Inraj}(E_{y}(h),g^{+})+\ln y=\mathrm{Inraj}(E_{\frac{y}{K}}(g),g^{+})+\ln y=c_{0}(\hat{g})+\ln K.

(2.18)

Hence

F_{\hat{h}}(0)=\frac{1}{2}\ln\frac{4n(n-1)}{S_{\hat{h}}}-c_{0}(\hat{h})=F_{\hat{g}}(0)

(2.19)

Then we obtained that
F(0)
is a scaling invariant.

Proof of theorem 1

3.1
A new relative volume inequality

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
(X,g^{+})
be an
n+1-
dimensional AH manifold with positive boundary minimized Yamabe metric
(\Sigma,\hat{g}).
Assume that
x

is the geodesic defining function and

Ric[g^{+}]\geq ng^{+},\ \ R[g^{+}]+n(n+1)=o(x^{2}).

Let
C=Cut(\Sigma)
be the cut locus of
(\Sigma,\hat{g}).
Then there exists
p\in C
such that

\frac{\mathcal{A}(p)}{\omega_{n}}\leq(\frac{Y(\Sigma,[\hat{g}])}{Y(\mathbb{S}^{n},[g_{\mathbb{S}}])})^{\frac{n}{2}}\cdot e^{nF(0)}\leq\frac{\mathcal{A}(C)}{\omega_{n}}.

(3.1)

We use
B(p,t)
(resp.
B(C,t)
) to denote the geodesic ball of radius
t
centred at
p
(resp.
C
) in
(X,g^{+})
and
V
to denote the volume with respect to
g^{+}.
Let
g=x^{2}g^{+}
be the geodesic compactification and
s_{C}(\cdot)=d_{g^{+}}(C,\cdot)
be the distance function of
C.
Set
g^{C}=e^{-2s_{C}}g^{+}.
Then

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}(C)

\displaystyle=2^{n}\lim\limits_{t\rightarrow\infty}e^{-nt}V(\partial B(C,t))=2^{n}\int_{\Sigma}dV_{g^{C}}

(3.2)

\displaystyle=2^{n}\lim\limits_{x\rightarrow 0}\int_{\Sigma_{x}}dV_{g^{C}}=2^{n}\lim\limits_{x\rightarrow 0}\int_{\Sigma_{x}}\frac{1}{(xe^{s_{C}})^{n}}dV_{g}

\displaystyle\geq 2^{n}\lim\limits_{x\rightarrow 0}\int_{\Sigma_{x}}\frac{1}{(xe^{\mathrm{Inraj}(E_{x})})^{n}}dV_{g}

\displaystyle=2^{n}\lim\limits_{x\rightarrow 0}\int_{\Sigma_{x}}\frac{1}{(xe^{\coth^{-1}h_{x}})^{n}}\cdot e^{n[\coth^{-1}h_{x}-{\mathrm{Inraj}(E_{x})}]}dV_{g}

\displaystyle=2^{n}\int_{\Sigma}(\frac{S_{\hat{g}}}{4n(n-1)})^{\frac{n}{2}}\cdot e^{nF(0)}dV_{\hat{g}}=\omega_{n}\cdot(\frac{Y(\Sigma,[\hat{g}])}{Y(\mathbb{S}^{n},[g_{\mathbb{S}}])})^{\frac{n}{2}}\cdot e^{nF(0)}

On the other hand, firstly we recall the Heintze Karcher inequality (theorem 2.1 in
[
8
]
),

\frac{V(E_{x})}{V(\Sigma_{x})}\leq\int_{0}^{\coth^{-1}h_{x}}(\cosh r-h_{x}\sinh r)^{n}dr

(3.3)

Where
h_{x}=\min\frac{H|_{\Sigma_{x}}}{n}.
Let
x
tends to 0, then

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{x\rightarrow 0}\int_{0}^{\coth^{-1}h_{x}}

\displaystyle(\cosh r-h_{x}\sinh r)^{n}dr=\lim\limits_{y\rightarrow+\infty}\int_{0}^{y}(\cosh r-\coth y\sinh r)^{n}dr

(3.4)

\displaystyle=\lim\limits_{y\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{\int_{0}^{y}\sinh^{n}(y-r)dr}{\sinh^{n}y}=\lim\limits_{y\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{\int_{0}^{y}\sinh^{n}tdt}{\sinh^{n}y}=\frac{1}{n}.

For the left side, there exists a point
p\in C
such that for all
x\in(0,\delta),\ d_{g^{+}}(p,\Sigma_{x})={\mathrm{Inraj}(E_{x})}.
Then
E_{x}\supseteq B(p,{\mathrm{Inraj}(E_{x})}).
Notice that
V(\Sigma_{x})=x^{-n}V(\Sigma_{x},g),
we deduce

\displaystyle\varlimsup\limits_{x\rightarrow 0}\frac{V(E_{x})}{V(\Sigma_{x})}

\displaystyle\geq\varlimsup_{x\rightarrow 0}\frac{V[B(p,{\mathrm{Inraj}(E_{x})})]}{x^{-n}V(\Sigma_{x},g)}

(3.5)

\displaystyle=\varlimsup_{x\rightarrow 0}\frac{V[B(p,{\mathrm{Inraj}(E_{x})})]}{e^{n{\mathrm{Inraj}(E_{x})}}}\cdot\frac{1}{V(\Sigma_{x},g)}\cdot(xe^{{\mathrm{Inraj}(E_{x})}})^{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathcal{A}(p)}{n2^{n}}\cdot\frac{1}{V(\Sigma,\hat{g})}\cdot(\frac{4n(n-1)}{S_{\hat{g}}})^{\frac{n}{2}}e^{-nF(0)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}\frac{\mathcal{A}(p)}{\omega_{n}}\cdot(\frac{Y(\Sigma,[\hat{g}])}{Y(\mathbb{S}^{n},[g_{\mathbb{S}}])})^{-\frac{n}{2}}\cdot e^{-nF(0)}

Then we finish the proof of theorem 3.1 from (
3.3
),(
3.4
) and (
3.5
).

.

If we use

)

3.2
The fractional Yamabe problem on AH manifold

In order to prove the rigidity result of theprem, here we would introduce some background materials about the fractional Yamabe constant. Let
(X^{n+1},g^{+})
be an AH manifold with boundary
(\Sigma,\hat{g})
and
x
is the unique geodesic defining function near infinity. It is showed in
[
16
]
that given
f\in C^{\infty}(\Sigma),
and
s\in\mathbb{C}
satisfying
Re(s)>\frac{n}{2}
and
s(n-s)
is not an
L^{2}
-eigenvalue for
-\Delta_{g^{+}},
the Poisson equation

-\Delta_{g^{+}}u-s(n-s)u=0\ \ in\ X

has a unique solution of the form

u=Fx^{n-s}+Gx^{s},\ \ F,G\in C^{\infty}(\overline{X}),\ \ F|_{\Sigma}=f

The scattering operator on
\Sigma
is then defined as

S(s)f=G|_{\Sigma}.

Let
\gamma\in(0,\frac{n}{2}),
Graham and Zworski defined the fractional GJMS operator

P_{\gamma}^{\hat{g}}
as the normalized scattering operator in
[
7
]

P_{\gamma}^{\hat{g}}f=d_{\gamma}P(\frac{n}{2}+\gamma)f,\ \ d_{\gamma}=2^{2\gamma}\frac{\Gamma(\gamma)}{\Gamma(-\gamma)}

(3.7)

It is a conformally covariant operator and we call

Q_{\gamma}^{\hat{g}}=\frac{2}{n-2\gamma}P_{\gamma}^{\hat{g}}1

(3.8)

the fractional scalar curvature. Then the fractional Yamabe constant is defined as:

Convergence to the Plancherel measure of Hecke Eigenvalues
Peter Sarnak, Nina Zubrilina

Abstract
We give improved uniform estimates for the rate of convergence to Plancherel measure of Hecke eigenvalues of holomorphic forms of weight 2 2 and level N N . These are applied to determine the sharp cutoff for the non-backtracking random walk on arithmetic Ramanujan graphs and to Serre’s problem of bounding the multiplicities of modular forms whose coefficients lie in number fields of degree d ≥ 1 d\geq 1 .



1
Introduction

It is well known that the distribution of Hecke eigenvalues of modular forms at primes
p_{1},\ldots,p_{r}
converges to the product of the corresponding
p
-adic Plancherel measures as one varies over certain families (
[
Sar87
]
,
[
Ser97
]
,
[
CDF97
]
). Our aim in this paper is to establish uniform rates on this convergence and to apply these to problems of sharp cutoff for random walks on Ramanujan graphs (
[
NS21
]
) and to the factorization of the Jacobian of the modular curve
X_{0}(N)
as in
[
Ser97
]
.

The Eichler-Selberg trace formula expresses the trace of the Hecke operator
T_{n}
on the space
S(N):=S_{2}(N)
of holomorphic cusp forms of weight
2
for
\Gamma_{0}(N)
in terms of class numbers of binary quadratic forms. Using this, one can show (
[
Ser97
]
,
\text{Prop. }4
) that as
f
runs over a Hecke basis
H(N)
of such eigenforms with eigenvalues

T_{n}f=:\lambda_{f}(n)\sqrt{n}\cdot f,\ \ (n,N)=1,

we will have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\absolutevalue{H(N)}}\absolutevalue{\sum_{f \in H(N)} \lambda_f(n) - \frac{\delta_{N}(n, \square)}{12}\psi(N)}\ll_{n}(n/N)^{1/2}\cdot d(N),

(1)

where

\displaystyle\delta_{N}(n,\square):=\begin{cases}1&\text{ if }n\text{ is a square modulo }N,\\
0&\text{ otherwise,}\end{cases}

d(N):=\sum_{d|N}1\ll_{\varepsilon}N^{\varepsilon}
is the divisor function, and
\psi(N):=N\prod_{p|N}(1+1/p)
is the Dedekind psi function. Murty and Sinha (
[
MS09
]
) give explicit and effective bounds in (
1
).

To extend the range of
n
for which the left-hand side of (
1
) goes to
0
with
N
, we introduce and remove
1/L(1,\mathrm{Sym}^{2}f)
weights into the sum. This allows us to use the Petersson trace formula, replacing class numbers with Kloosterman sums, which enjoy sharp bounds coming from the arithmetic geometry of curves (
[
Wei48
]
). This technique applied to a similar problem is outlined in
[
Sar02
]
and is used on other problems in
[
Iwa84
]
and
[
ILS00
]
. It allows us to double the exponent in the range of
n
, which is crucial for certain applications.

In what follows, our aim is to establish sharp estimates, and to simplify the analysis, we assume that
N
is prime. In much of what we do, this assumption can be removed. We address this further in the final section
6
.

Theorem 1
.

Let
\varepsilon>0
and let
m,n
be integers coprime to
N
. Then:

\frac{1}{\absolutevalue{H(N)}}\sum_{f\in H(N)}\lambda_{f}(m)\lambda_{f}(n)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d|(m,n)\\
d^{2}k^{2}=mn\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{k}+\mathrm{O}_{\varepsilon}\left(\frac{(mn)^{1/8}}{N^{1/2}}(mnN)^{\varepsilon}\right).

With this theorem, we can formulate and prove a corresponding uniform convergence to the Plancherel measure. Let
r\geq 1
, and for
\ell_{1},\ldots,\ell_{r}\geq 0
, let
\mathcal{P}^{\ell_{1},\ldots,\ell_{r}}
denote the set of polynomials in
x_{1},\ldots,x_{r}
of degrees at most
\ell_{1},\ldots,\ell_{r}
, respectively, that is,

\mathcal{P}^{\ell_{1},\ldots,\ell_{r}}:=\Big\{\sum_{j_{1}=0}^{\ell_{1}}\cdots\sum_{j_{r}=0}^{\ell_{r}}a_{j_{1},\ldots,j_{r}}x_{1}^{j_{1}}\cdots x_{r}^{j_{r}}\ \large|\ a_{j_{1},\ldots,j_{r}}\in\mathbb{C}\Big\}.

For
p\nmid N
, let
\theta_{f}(p)\in[0,\pi]
be such that

\lambda_{f}(p)=2\cos(\theta_f(p))

(such a
\theta_{f}
exists because of self-adjointness of
T_{p}
and thanks to the Ramanujan bound
\absolutevalue{\lambda_f(p)}\leq 2
due to Eichler
[
Eic54
]
).
Let
\mu_{p}
be the
p
-adic Plancherel measure:

\displaystyle d\mu_{p}:=\frac{2}{\pi}\cdot\dfrac{(p+1)\sin^{2}\theta}{(p^{1/2}+p^{-1/2})^{2}-4\cos^{2}\theta}d\theta.

(2)

We have the following uniform convergence result:

Theorem 2
.

Let
r\geq 1
and
\eta>0
. Then uniformly for
p_{1}^{\ell_{1}}\cdots p_{r}^{\ell_{r}}<N^{2-\eta}
and
P\in\mathcal{P}^{\ell_{1},\ldots,\ell_{r}}
,

\frac{1}{\absolutevalue{H(N)}}\sum_{f\in H(N)}\absolutevalue{P\left(\cos\theta_f(p_1), \ldots, \cos\theta_f(p_r)\right)}^{2}=(1+o(1))\int_{0}^{\pi}\cdots\int_{0}^{\pi}\absolutevalue{P}^{2}d\mu_{p_{1}}\dots d\mu_{p_{r}}

as
N\to\infty
.

This result with an exponent of
N
larger than
1
(which corresponds to
mn
going up to
N^{2-\varepsilon}
in Theorem
1
) is what is needed to settle the cutoff window for the non-backtracking random walks on Ramanujan graphs (
[
NS21
]
). In fact, it yields the conjectured asymptotics for the variance for these walks (see end of section
3
).

Another application of Theorem
2
is to multiplicities of
f
’s in a Hecke basis with given
\lambda_{f}(p)
’s for
p\in\{p_{1},\ldots,p_{r}\}
. Let
s(N):=\absolutevalue{H(N)}=\mathrm{dim}\ S(N)
, so for
N
prime,
s(N)=\lfloor\frac{N+1}{12}\rfloor-1
when
N\equiv 1\pmod{12}
and
s(N)=\lfloor\frac{N+1}{12}\rfloor
otherwise. For
\varphi\in S(N)
, let

M_{N}(y,\varphi):=\#\absolutevalue{ \left\{ f \in H(N) : \lambda_f(p) = \lambda_\phi(p) \text{ for } p \leq y, (p, N) = 1\right\}}.

For a fixed
y
, Theorem
2
implies that uniformly in
\varphi
,

\displaystyle M_{N}(y,\varphi)\ll\frac{s(N)}{(\log N)^{r}},

(3)

where
r=\pi(y)
is the number of primes up to
y
.
If
y
is allowed to increase with
N
, then one can exploit that for
f
in the set defining
M_{N}(y,\varphi)
, we also have
\lambda_{f}(m)=\lambda_{\varphi}(m)
for all
y
-smooth numbers
m
(which are numbers all of whose prime factors are at most
y
). This allows one to improve (
3
) vastly.

Such an argument using the large sieve for Dirichlet characters is due to Linnik (
[
Lin41
]
). In the modular form setting, Duke and Kowalski (
[
DK00
]
) establish that the number of non-monomial newforms of square-free level up to
N
that have prescribed eigenvalues
\lambda_{\varphi}(p)
at primes
p\leq y=(\log N)^{\beta}
satisfies

M_{\leq N}(y,\varphi)^{\#}\ll_{\beta}N^{10/\beta+\varepsilon},

which is non-trivial for
\beta>5
. Lau and Wu (
[
LW08
]
) show that for
y=C\log N
with
C
a large constant, there is
c>0
s.t.

M_{N}(y,\varphi)\ll\exp\left(\dfrac{-c\log N}{\log\log N}\right)s(N).

Our interest is in smaller
y
’s, namely
y=(\log N)^{\beta}
with
0<\beta<1
.

Theorem 3
.

Fix
\beta\in(0,1)
. Then for
y=(\log N)^{\beta}
and uniformly in
\varphi
,

M_{N}(y,\varphi)\leq\exp\left(-\frac{1-\beta}{\beta}(\log N)^{\beta}+o\left((\log N)^{\beta}\right)\right)s(N)

as
N\to\infty
.

We apply this to a question of Serre (
[
Ser97
]
). Assume that all
f=\sum_{n\geq 1}a(n)e(nz)\in H(N)
are normalized so
a(1)=1
. The Fourier coefficients
a(n)
are algebraic integers in a totally real field of degree
d(f)
. For
d\geq 1
, let
s(N)_{d}
denote the number of
f
’s for which
d(f)=d
. Serre shows that for
d
fixed,
s(N)_{d}=o(s(N))
(see also
[
MS09
]
, Theorem
5
), and asks for stronger upper bounds. Theorem
3
implies such a bound.

Theorem 4
.

Fix
d\geq 1
and
\beta<\frac{2}{d+2}
. Then as
N\to\infty
,

s(N)_{d}\leq\exp( - c(d, \beta) (\log N)^\beta+ o((\log N)^\beta))s(N),

where
c(d,\beta):=(1-\beta)/\beta-d/2>0.

This falls short of Serre’s conjecture, which asserts that Theorem
4
holds for
\beta=1
and
c=c(d)>0
(i.e.,
s(N)_{d}\ll s(N)^{\alpha}
for some
\alpha<1
).

2
Weight Removal in the Petersson Formula

Throughout this section we assume
N
is prime. Let
H(N)
denote a simultaneous eigenbasis of Hecke operators
T_{k},(k,N)=1
, acting on the space
S(N)
of dimension
s(N)
of weight
2
level
N
cusp forms for
\Gamma_{0}(N)
, and for
f\in H(N)
, let
a_{f}(n)
and
\lambda_{f}(n)
be such that

f(z)=\sum_{n\geq 1}a_{f}(n)e(nz)=\sum_{n\geq 1}\sqrt{n}\ \lambda_{f}(n)e(nz),

where
e(z):=e^{2\pi iz}
. Assume
f
are normalized so
a_{f}(1)=1
.

Our starting point for this section is the Petersson trace formula estimated via the Weil bound on Kloosterman sums, as presented in
[
ILS00
]
, Corollary
2.2
or
[
IK04
]
, Corollary
14.24
:

Petersson Formula
.

With
H(N)
as above,
(mn,N)=1
, and
\varepsilon>0
,

\displaystyle\sideset{}{{}^{h}}{\sum}_{f\in H(N)}\lambda_{f}(m)\lambda_{f}(n)=\delta(m,n)+\mathrm{O}_{\varepsilon}\left(\frac{(mn)^{1/4}}{N}(mnN)^{\varepsilon}\right).

(4)

Here the
h
superscript signifies adding "harmonic" weights:
\sum_{f\in H(N)}^{h}\alpha_{f}=\frac{1}{4\pi}\sum_{f\in H(N)}\frac{\alpha_{f}}{\norm{f}^{2}}
, where
\norm{\cdot}
denotes the Petersson norm.

We derive Theorem
1
from the Petersson formula by removing the harmonic weights. The Petersson norm is related to the special value of the symmetric square
L
-function at
1
(
[
ILS00
]
, Lemma
2.5
) via:

4\pi\norm{f}^{2}=\frac{s(N)}{\zeta(2)}L(\mathrm{Sym}^{2}f,1)

(where
L(\mathrm{Sym}^{2}f,s)=\zeta(2s)(1-N^{-2s})\sum_{n\geq 1}\lambda_{f}(n^{2})n^{-s}:=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{\mathrm{sym}^{2}f}(n)n^{-s}
), so

\displaystyle\frac{1}{s(N)}\sum_{f\in H(N)}\lambda_{f}(m)\lambda_{f}(n)

\displaystyle=\sideset{}{{}^{h}}{\sum}_{f\in H(N)}\lambda_{f}(m)\lambda_{f}(n)\cdot\frac{4\pi\norm{f}^{2}}{s(N)}=\sideset{}{{}^{h}}{\sum}_{f\in H(N)}\lambda_{f}(m)\lambda_{f}(n)\frac{L(\mathrm{Sym}^{2}f,1)}{\zeta(2)},

(5)

and to prove Theorem
1
, we need to derive a suitable approximation for
L(\mathrm{Sym}^{2}f,s)
.

Let
\Psi(x)\geq 0
be a smooth function supported on
[-1,1]
with
\Psi(0)=1
. The Mellin transform

\tilde{\Psi}(s)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\Psi(x)x^{s}\frac{dx}{x}

is an analytic function of
s=\sigma+it
for
\sigma>-1
, except for a simple pole at
0
with residue
1
, and decreases rapidly as
\absolutevalue{t}\to\infty
for
-1\leq\sigma\leq 2
.

For a parameter
x>0
, let

\displaystyle\textbf{A}:

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\sigma=2}L(\mathrm{Sym}^{2}f,s+1)x^{s}\tilde{\Psi}(s)ds

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\sigma=2}\sum_{\nu\geq 1}\frac{\lambda_{\mathrm{sym}^{2}f}(\nu)}{\nu^{s+1}}x^{s}\tilde{\Psi}(s)ds

\displaystyle=\sum_{\nu\geq 1}\frac{\lambda_{\mathrm{sym}^{2}f}(\nu)}{\nu}\cdot\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\sigma=2}\left(\frac{\nu}{x}\right)^{-s}\tilde{\Psi}(s)ds

\displaystyle=\sum_{\nu\leq x}\frac{\lambda_{\mathrm{sym}^{2}f}(\nu)}{\nu}\Psi\left(\frac{\nu}{x}\right)

by the Mellin inversion theorem.
Shifting the integral defining
A
to
\real(s)=-1/2
picks up the simple pole of
\tilde{\Psi}
at
s=0
, so by the residue theorem,

\displaystyle L(\mathrm{Sym}^{2}f,1)=\sum_{\nu\leq x}\frac{\lambda_{\mathrm{sym}^{2}f}(\nu)}{\nu}\Psi\left(\frac{\nu}{x}\right)+R(f,x),

(6)

where

R(f,x):=-\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}L(\mathrm{Sym}^{2}f,1/2+it)x^{-\frac{1}{2}+it}\tilde{\Psi}(-1/2+it)dt.

Now, by Cauchy-Schwarz,

\displaystyle\absolutevalue{R(f, x)}^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}x}\absolutevalue{\int_{- \infty}^\infty L(\sym^2 f, 1/2 + it) x^{it} \tilde{\Psi}(-1/2 + it) dt}^{2}

\displaystyle\ll x^{-1}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\absolutevalue{L(\sym^2 f, 1/2 + it)}^{2}\cdot\big|\tilde{\Psi}(-1/2+it)\big|dt,

so

\sideset{}{{}^{h}}{\sum}_{f\in H(N)}\absolutevalue{R(f, x)}^{2}\ll x^{-1}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\big|\tilde{\Psi}(-1/2+it)\big|\sideset{}{{}^{h}}{\sum}_{f\in H(N)}\absolutevalue{L(1/2 + it, \sym^2 f)}^{2}dt.

According to the Lindelöf on average result due to Iwaniec and Michel for this family of
L
-functions (
[
IM01
]
),

\sideset{}{{}^{h}}{\sum}_{f\in H(N)}\absolutevalue{L(1/2 + it, \sym^2 f)}^{2}\ll_{\varepsilon}N^{\varepsilon}(\absolutevalue{t}+1)^{8},

so

\displaystyle\sideset{}{{}^{h}}{\sum}_{f\in H(N)}\absolutevalue{R(f, x)}^{2}\ll_{\varepsilon}x^{-1}N^{\varepsilon}.

(7)

Substituting (
7
) and (
6
) into (
5
),

\displaystyle\frac{1}{s(N)}\sum_{f\in H(N)}\lambda_{f}(m)\lambda_{f}(n)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\zeta(2)}\sideset{}{{}^{h}}{\sum}_{f\in H(N)}\lambda_{f}(m)\lambda_{f}(n)\left(\sum_{\nu\leq x}\frac{\lambda_{\mathrm{sym}^{2}f}(\nu)}{\nu}\Psi\left(\frac{\nu}{x}\right)+R(f,x)\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\zeta(2)}(\textbf{I}+\textbf{II}),

(8)

where

\textbf{I}=\sum_{\nu\leq x}\frac{\lambda_{\mathrm{sym}^{2}f}(\nu)}{\nu}\Psi\left(\frac{\nu}{x}\right)\sideset{}{{}^{h}}{\sum}_{f\in H(N)}\lambda_{f}(m)\lambda_{f}(n)

and

\absolutevalue{\textbf{II}}\leq\sideset{}{{}^{h}}{\sum}_{f\in H(N)}\absolutevalue{\lambda_f(m)\lambda_f(n)}\absolutevalue{R(f, x)}\ll_{\varepsilon}{N^{\varepsilon}}\left(\sideset{}{{}^{h}}{\sum}_{\ f\in H(N)}(mn)^{\varepsilon}\right)^{1/2}\left(\sideset{}{{}^{h}}{\sum}_{\ f\in H(N)}\absolutevalue{R(f, x)}^{2}\right)^{1/2}\ll_{\varepsilon}\frac{(mnN)^{\varepsilon}}{\sqrt{x}},

where we used Cauchy-Schwartz and (
7
).

To estimate
I
, we use Hecke relations

\lambda_{f}(m)\lambda_{f}(n)=\sum_{d|(m,n)}\lambda_{f}\left(\frac{mn}{d^{2}}\right)

and the formula

\lambda_{\mathrm{Sym}^{2}f}(\nu)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}t^{2}k=\nu\\
(t,N)=1\end{subarray}}\lambda_{f}(k^{2}).

From this,

\textbf{I}=\sum_{t^{2}k\leq x}\frac{\Psi\left(\frac{t^{2}k}{x}\right)}{t^{2}k}\sum_{d|(m,n)}\sum_{f\in H(N)}^{h}\lambda_{f}\left(\frac{mn}{d^{2}}\right)\lambda_{f}\left(k^{2}\right),

so by the Petersson formula,

I

\displaystyle=\sum_{t^{2}k\leq x}\frac{\Psi\left(\frac{t^{2}k}{x}\right)}{t^{2}k}{t^{2}k}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d|(m,n)\\
mn=d^{2}k^{2}\end{subarray}}1+\mathrm{O}_{\varepsilon}\left(\sum_{t^{2}k\leq x}\frac{(mn)^{\varepsilon}}{t^{2}k}\frac{(mnk^{2})^{1/4}}{N}(Nmn)^{\varepsilon}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d|(m,n)\\
mn=d^{2}k^{2}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{k}\right)\sum_{t^{2}k\leq x/k}\frac{\Psi\left(\frac{t^{2}k}{x}\right)}{t^{2}}+\mathrm{O}_{\varepsilon}\left(\frac{x^{1/2}(mn)^{1/4}}{N}(mnN)^{\varepsilon}\right)

\displaystyle=\zeta(2)\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d|(m,n)\\
mn=d^{2}k^{2}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{k}\right)+\mathrm{O}_{\varepsilon}\left(\frac{x^{1/2}(mn)^{1/4}}{N}(mnN)^{\varepsilon}\right).

Combining estimates of
I
and
II
with (
8
),

\frac{1}{s(N)}\sum_{f\in H(N)}\lambda_{f}(m)\lambda_{f}(n)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d|(m,n)\\
mn=d^{2}k^{2}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{k}+\mathrm{O}_{\varepsilon}((mnN)^{\varepsilon}/x^{1/2})+\mathrm{O}_{\varepsilon}\left(\frac{x^{1/2}(mn)^{1/4}}{N}(mnN)^{\varepsilon}\right).

Choosing
x=\frac{N}{(mn)^{1/4}}
, we finish the proof of Theorem
1
.

3
Convergence to the Plancherel Product Measure

In this section we address Theorem
2
. Fix an integer
r>0
, let
\ell_{1},\ldots,\ell_{r}>0
, and let
p_{1},\ldots,p_{r}
be distinct primes with
(p_{j},N)=1
.

Consider a polynomial
P(x_{1},\ldots,x_{r})\in\mathbb{C}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{r}]
of degree at most
\ell_{i}
in
x_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq r
.
For
n\geq 0
, let

U_{n}(\cos\theta):=\frac{\sin((n + 1) \theta)}{\sin\theta}

be the
n
th Chebyshev polynomial of the second kind.
U_{n}
is a degree
n
polynomial in
\cos\theta
with real coefficients, so we can find
a_{t_{1},\ldots,t_{r}}\in\mathbb{C}
such that

\displaystyle P(x_{1},\ldots,x_{r})=\sum_{t_{1}=0}^{\ell_{1}}\cdots\sum_{t_{r}=0}^{\ell_{r}}a_{t_{1},\ldots,t_{r}}U_{t_{1}}(x_{1})\cdots U_{t_{r}}(x_{r}).

(9)

Moreover, Hecke relations imply that for
(p,N)=1
,
U_{n}(\lambda(p))=\lambda(p^{n})
.
From this,

\displaystyle\absolutevalue{P(\theta_f(p_1), \ldots, \theta_f(p_r))}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{t_{1},s_{1}=0}^{\ell_{1}}\cdots\sum_{t_{r},s_{r}=0}^{\ell_{r}}a_{t_{1},\ldots,t_{r}}\overline{a_{s_{1},\ldots,s_{r}}}U_{t_{1}}(\theta_{f}(p_{1}))\overline{U_{s_{1}}(\theta_{f}(p_{1}))}\cdots U_{t_{r}}(\theta_{f}(p_{r}))\overline{U_{s_{r}}(\theta_{f}(p_{r}))}=

\displaystyle=\sum_{t_{i},s_{i}}a_{t_{1},\ldots,t_{r}}\overline{a_{s_{1},\ldots,s_{r}}}\lambda_{f}(p_{1}^{t_{1}})\overline{\lambda_{f}(p_{1}^{s_{1}})}\cdots\lambda_{f}(p_{r}^{t_{r}})\overline{\lambda_{f}(p_{r}^{s_{r}})}

\displaystyle=\sum_{t_{i},s_{i}}a_{t_{1},\ldots,t_{r}}\overline{a_{s_{1},\ldots,s_{r}}}\lambda_{f}(p_{1}^{t_{1}}\cdots p_{r}^{t_{r}})\overline{\lambda_{f}(p_{1}^{s_{1}}\cdots p_{r}^{s_{r}})},

so by Theorem
1
,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\absolutevalue{H(N)}}\sum_{f\in H(N)}\absolutevalue{P(\theta_f(p_1), \ldots, \theta_f(p_r))}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{t_{i},s_{i}}a_{t_{1},\ldots,t_{r}}\overline{a_{s_{1},\ldots,s_{r}}}\cdot\frac{1}{\absolutevalue{H(N)}}\sum_{f\in H(N)}\lambda_{f}(p_{1}^{t_{1}}\cdots p_{r}^{t_{r}})\overline{\lambda_{f}(p_{1}^{s_{1}}\cdots p_{r}^{s_{r}})}

\displaystyle=\textbf{I}+\textbf{II},

(10)

where

\textbf{I}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}s_{i},t_{i}\leq\ell_{i}\\
m=p_{1}^{t_{1}}\cdots p_{r}^{t_{r}}\\
n=p_{1}^{t_{1}}\cdots p_{r}^{t_{r}}\end{subarray}}a_{t_{1},\ldots,t_{r}}\overline{a_{s_{1},\ldots,s_{r}}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d|(m,n)\\
mn=d^{2}k^{2}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{k},

and

II

\displaystyle\ll_{\varepsilon}\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}s_{i},t_{i}\leq\ell_{i}\\
m=p_{1}^{t_{1}}\cdots p_{r}^{t_{r}}\\
n=p_{1}^{t_{1}}\cdots p_{r}^{t_{r}}\end{subarray}}\absolutevalue{a_{t_1, \ldots, t_r} \overline{a_{s_1, \ldots, s_r}}}(mn)^{1/8}(mnN)^{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\ll\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\left(\sum_{0\leq t_{i}\leq\ell_{i}}\absolutevalue{a_{t_1, \ldots, t_r}}p_{1}^{t_{1}/8}\cdots p_{r}^{t_{r}/8}\right)^{2}(p_{1}^{2\ell_{1}}\cdots p_{r}^{2\ell_{r}}N)^{\varepsilon}=(*).

Applying Cauchy-Schwartz and summing the geometric series,

(*)\leq\frac{(p_{1}^{2\ell_{1}}\cdots p_{r}^{2\ell_{r}}N)^{\varepsilon}}{\sqrt{N}}\hskip-3.61371pt\sum_{0\leq t_{i}\leq\ell_{i}}p_{1}^{t_{1}/4}\cdots p_{r}^{t_{r}/4}\hskip-3.61371pt\sum_{0\leq t_{i}\leq\ell_{i}}\absolutevalue{a_{t_1, \ldots, t_r}}^{2}\leq(p_{1}^{2\ell_{1}}\cdots p_{r}^{2\ell_{r}}N)^{\varepsilon}\left(\frac{p_{1}^{\ell_{1}}\cdots p_{r}^{\ell_{r}}}{N^{2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{4}}\hskip-7.22743pt\sum_{0\leq t_{i}\leq\ell_{i}}\absolutevalue{a_{t_1, \ldots, t_r}}^{2}.

Let
\mu_{\infty}(\theta):=\frac{2}{\pi}\sin^{2}\theta d\theta
be the Sato-Tate measure on
[0,\pi]
. From the definition of
d\mu_{p}
, it follows that

d\mu_{\infty}\cdots d\mu_{\infty}\ll_{r}d\mu_{p_{1}}\cdots d\mu_{p_{r}}.

Hence, from (
9
) and the orthonormality of
U_{n}
with respect to
d\mu_{\infty}
, we have

\sum_{0\leq t_{i}\leq\ell_{i}}\absolutevalue{a_{t_1, \ldots, t_r}}^{2}\ll\norm{P}^{2}_{\mu_{p_{1}},\ldots,\mu_{p_{r}}}.

We conclude that

\textbf{II}\ll_{\varepsilon}(p_{1}^{\ell_{1}}\cdots p_{r}^{\ell_{r}}N)^{2\varepsilon}\left(\frac{p_{1}^{\ell_{1}}\cdots p_{r}^{\ell_{r}}}{N^{2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{4}}\norm{P}^{2}_{\mu_{p_{1}},\ldots,\mu_{p_{r}}}.

It remains to evaluate
I
. To interpret
I
as the integral against the Plancherel measure, we need the following observation:

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
m,n\geq 0
, and let
d\mu_{p}
be the
p
-adic Plancherel measure. Then:

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\pi}U_{n}(\theta)U_{m}(\theta)d\mu_{p}=\left[\begin{array}[]{ll}\dfrac{p}{(p-1)}\left(\dfrac{1}{p^{\frac{\absolutevalue{m - n}}{2}}}-\dfrac{1}{p^{\frac{m+n}{2}+1}}\right)&\text{if }m\equiv n\pmod{2}\\
0&\text{otherwise.}\end{array}\right.

We leave the proof until the end of the section. From (
9
),

Proposition 4.1
.

\displaystyle\max\left\{\absolutevalue{\sin kx/\sin x},\absolutevalue{\sin((k+1)x)/\sin x}\right\}\geq 1/2.

(where the functions are extended continuously to the

Proof.

If

\absolutevalue{\cos kx}=\sqrt{1-\varepsilon^{2}\sin^{2}x}=\sqrt{1-\varepsilon^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}\cos^{2}x},

\displaystyle\absolutevalue{\frac{\sin(kx + x)}{\sin x}}

\displaystyle=\absolutevalue{\frac{\sin kx }{\sin x}\cos x + \cos kx}\geq\absolutevalue{\cos kx}-\varepsilon\absolutevalue{\cos x }=\sqrt{1-\varepsilon^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}\absolutevalue{\cos x}^{2}}-\varepsilon\absolutevalue{\cos x}.

and so

\max\left\{\absolutevalue{\frac{\sin kx}{\sin x}},\absolutevalue{\frac{\sin((k+1)x)}{\sin x}}\right\}\geq\max\{\varepsilon,1-\varepsilon\}\geq 1/2.

Using the Hecke relation

\displaystyle\#\absolutevalue{T_N(y)_d}\leq\exp\left(yd/2+o_{d}(y)\right).

Lemma 5.3

[-M,M]^{d}
is bounded by
O_{d}(1)M^{d}
(this can be seen, for example, by placing (disjoint) balls of diameter
1
at each lattice point in the box and comparing volumes).
∎

Proof of Proposition
5.1
.

From Lemma
5.3
, we see that for a fixed degree
K
number field, the number of possible tuples
(a_{f}(p_{1}),\ldots,a_{f}(p_{r}))
with
a_{f}(p_{i})\in K
is at most

\prod_{p\leq y}C(d)(2\sqrt{p})^{d}=(2C(d))^{r}\exp((d/2) \sum_{p \leq y} \log p )=\exp(dy/2 + o_d(y)),

where the last step uses the prime number theorem. On the other hand, from Lemma
5.2
, the number of choices for
K
is
\exp(O_d(\log y))=\exp(o_d(y))
, so multiplying the two proves the proposition statement.
∎

Combining this Proposition with Theorem
3
,

\log s(N)_{d}/s(N)\leq-\left(\frac{1-\beta}{\beta}-\frac{d}{2}\right)y+o_{d}(y),

which concludes the proof of Theorem
4
.

Note that for the coefficient of
y
to be negative, we have to choose
\beta
small, which is why we dealt with multiplicity bounds in Theorem
3
only for
0\leq\beta\leq 1
.

6
Composite Level

The discussion up to this point was restricted to weight
k=2
and level
N
being prime. Theorems
1
and
2
can be extended without much change to allow varying weight and general
N
, as long as the relatively prime conditions
(mn,N)=1
and
(p_{j},N)=1
are maintained. Indeed, the starting point, which is an application of the Petersson formula (
[
ILS00
]
, Corollary
2.2
or
[
IK04
]
, Corollary
14.24
), gives the desired uniformity in the "harmonic" weighted form. To remove the weights, one has to take care with new and old forms and the Atkin-Lehner involutions in relating
\norm{f}^{2}_{2}
and
L(1,\mathrm{Sym}^{2}f)
.

On the other hand, in the proof of the multiplicity bounds in Theorem
3
, we used
y
-smooth numbers and the assumption that
(p,N)=1
for
p\leq y<\log N
. As we show in Theorem
5
below, similar bounds can be proved for
N
’s that do not have an abnormal number of small prime factors. For "super-smooth" numbers, such as
N=\prod_{p\leq t}p
, we cannot make use of the approach to the Plancherel measure of the Hecke eigenvalues for small primes, and our bounds in Theorem
3
and
4
don’t apply.

In what follows, we restrict ourselves to the
s^{*}(N)
-dimensional space
S^{*}(N)
of weight
2
level
N
newforms, which admits a simultaneous eigenbasis
H^{*}(N)
with respect to Hecke operators
T_{n}
with
(n,N)=1
(we assume these forms are normalized to have constant Fourier coefficient
1
).

For a positive integer
N
, the number of distinct prime divisors of
N
is at most

\displaystyle(1+o(1))\frac{\log N}{\log\log N}=:\bf{P}.

(26)

Let
y=y(N)=o(\log N)
be a parameter going to infinity with
N
, and let
r:=\pi(y)\sim y/\log y
. Let
q_{1},\ldots,q_{r}
be the first
r
primes which don’t divide
N
. Since
q_{k}
is at most the
(k+\mathbf{P})^{\text{th}}
prime and
k\leq r=o(\bf{P}),

we can conclude via the prime number theorem that

\displaystyle q_{k}\leq(1+o(1))(\bf{P}\log\bf{P}).

(27)

In the spirit of section
4
, we want to give a lower bound for the function

\Phi(q_{1},\ldots,q_{r},X):=\#\absolutevalue{\{(\alpha_1, \ldots, \alpha_r): q_1^{\alpha_1} \cdots q_r^{\alpha_r} \leq X\}}

for
X
(to be chosen later) satisfying

\displaystyle\log X=(1+o(1))\log N.

(28)

By (
27
),

\displaystyle\Phi(q_{1},\ldots,q_{r},X)

\displaystyle=\#\absolutevalue{\{(\alpha_1, \ldots, \alpha_r) : q_1^{\alpha_1} \cdots q_r^{\alpha_r} \leq X\}}\geq

\displaystyle\geq\#\absolutevalue{\{(\alpha_1, \ldots, \alpha_r) : \big((1 + o(1))\mathbf{P}\log\mathbf{P}\big)^{\alpha_1 + \cdots+ \alpha_r} \leq X\}}=

\displaystyle=\#\absolutevalue{\{(\alpha_1, \ldots, \alpha_r) : \alpha_1 + \cdots+ \alpha_r \leq\log X/\log\big((1 + o(1))(\mathbf{P} \log\mathbf{P}) \big)\}}\overset{\tiny{\text{ by }}(\ref{sizeofp}),(\ref{xislogn})}{=}

\displaystyle=\#\absolutevalue{\{(\alpha_1, \ldots, \alpha_r) : \alpha_1 + \cdots+ \alpha_r \leq(1 + o(1)) \log X/\log\log X\}}.

(29)

The number of non-negative integer solutions to
x_{1}+\ldots+x_{A}\leq B
is

{A+B\choose A}\geq\left(\frac{B}{A}\right)^{A},

so (
6
) implies

\displaystyle\log\Phi(q_{1},\ldots,q_{r},X)\geq r\log\frac{(1+o(1))\log X}{r\log\log X}.

(30)

For
\varphi\in S^{*}(N)
a weight
2
holomorphic cusp newform for
\Gamma_{0}(N)
, we let
M^{*}_{N}(q_{1},\ldots,q_{r},\varphi)
be the multiplicity of the tuple of eigenvalues of
\varphi
at primes
q_{i}
, i.e.

M^{*}_{N}(q_{1},\ldots,q_{r},\varphi):=\#\absolutevalue{ \left\{ f\in H^*(N) : \lambda_f(q_i) = \lambda_\phi(q_i) \text{ for all } i \leq r\right\}}.

We bound
M^{*}_{N}(q_{1},\ldots,q_{r},\varphi)
for a fixed
\varphi
via the large sieve inequality identically to section
4
. Taking

\displaystyle c_{n}:=\begin{cases}\overline{\lambda_{\varphi}(n)}&\text{ if }n=q_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots q_{r}^{\alpha_{r}}\leq X\\
0&\text{ otherwise,}\end{cases}

we get

\displaystyle M^{*}_{N}(q_{1},\ldots,q_{r},\varphi)\ll N(\log N)^{2}/\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n=q_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots q_{r}^{\alpha_{r}}\\
n\leq N\end{subarray}}\absolutevalue{\lambda_\phi(n)}^{2}.

(31)

Recreating the proof in section
4
, we can see that

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq N\\
n\in\mathcal{S}_{q_{1},\ldots,q_{r}}\end{subarray}}\absolutevalue{\lambda_\phi(n)}^{2}\gg 8^{-r}\Phi\left(q_{1},\ldots,q_{r},\frac{N}{q_{1}\cdots q_{r}}\right)\geq 8^{-r}\Phi\left(q_{1},\ldots,q_{r},\frac{N}{\mathbf{P}^{2r}}\right).

(32)

Let
X:=N/\mathbf{P}^{2r}
. Recall that
r=\pi(o(\log N))=o(\log N/\log\log N),
so

\log X=\log N-2r(1+o(1))\log\log N=\log N(1+o(1)),

which means this choice of
X
satisfies (
28
) and hence also satisfies (
30
).
Let
0<\beta<1
and let

y:=(\log N)^{\beta},

so

r=(1+o(1))(\log X)^{\beta}/\beta\log\log X.

Then (
30
) becomes

\log\Phi(q_{1},\ldots,q_{r},X)\geq(1+o(1))\frac{(\log X)^{\beta}}{\beta\log\log X}\log\frac{\beta\log X}{(\log X)^{\beta}}=(1+o(1))\frac{1-\beta}{\beta}(\log X)^{\beta},

i.e.,

\log\Phi(q_{1},\ldots,q_{r},N)\geq(1+o(1))\frac{1-\beta}{\beta}(\log N)^{\beta}.

Finally, by (
31
) and (
32
),

\displaystyle\log M^{*}_{N}(q_{1},\ldots,q_{r},\varphi)/N\leq\log\log N+r\log 8-(1+o(1))\frac{1-\beta}{\beta}(\log N)^{\beta}=-(1+o(1))\frac{1-\beta}{\beta}(\log N)^{\beta}

(33)

(note that this bound is identical to the one in section
4
which is sharp).

We apply (
33
) to extend Theorem
4
to more general
N
’s. For
T\geq 1
fixed and for some
y=y(N)
with
\log\log N\ll y\ll\log N
, we say that a large
N
is
T
-super-smooth if

\frac{\pi(y^{T};N)}{\pi(y)}=o(1),

where
\pi(z;N)=\#\absolutevalue{\{p \leq z: (p, N) = 1 \}}
is the number of primes up to
z
that don’t divide
N
. Clearly, very few numbers are
T
-super-smooth for all
T
.

Let
H^{*}(N)_{d}:=\{f\in H^{*}(N):d(f)=d\}
be the set of Hecke newforms whose Fourier coefficients span a number field of degree
d
,
s^{*}(N)_{d}=\absolutevalue{H^*(N)_d}
. The following theorem extends Theorem
4
to non-super-smooth numbers.

Theorem 5
.

Let
0\leq\beta\leq 1
,
y=(\log N)^{\beta}
,
T\geq 1
, and
d\geq 1
. Then for
N
not
T
-super-smooth,

s^{*}(N)_{d}\leq\exp\left(-\left(\frac{1-\beta}{\beta}-\frac{dT}{2}\right)y+o_{T,d}(y)\right)s^{*}(N)

as
N\to\infty
.

Proof.

The proof emulates that of section
5
. For
f\in H^{*}(N)_{d}
, let
a_{f}(q_{i})=\lambda_{f}(q_{i})\sqrt{q_{i}}
be the eigenvalue of
f
for the Hecke operator
T_{q_{i}}
, and let

T^{*}_{N}(q_{1},\ldots,q_{r})_{d}:=\{(a_{f}(q_{1}),a_{f}(q_{2}),\ldots,a_{f}(q_{r}))|f\in H^{*}(N)_{d}\}

denote the set of possible eigenvalue tuples of a form in
H^{*}(N)_{d}
at the first
r
primes not dividing
N
. Repeating verbatim the proof of Lemma
5.2
, there are
\ll_{d}y^{T\kappa(d)}
possible number fields of the form
\mathbb{Q}(a_{f}(q_{1}),\ldots,a_{f}(q_{r}))
. Tautologically, for
N
as in the statement of the theorem, the first
r
primes
q_{1},\ldots,q_{r}
not dividing
N
satisfy
q_{i}\leq y^{T}
, so using Lemma
5.3
, we get

\#\absolutevalue{T^*_N(q_1, \ldots, q_r)}\leq y^{T\kappa(d)}\prod_{i\leq r}C(d)y^{Td/2}\leq\exp((d/2)T r \log y + o_{T,d}(y))\leq\exp((d/2)T y + o_{d,T}(y)).

Combined with (
33
), this gives

\log s(N)_{d}/N\leq-\left(\frac{1-\beta}{\beta}-\frac{dT}{2}\right)y+o_{T,d}(y).

It remains to note that
s^{*}(N)\asymp\varphi(N)
, the Euler’s totient function, and
\log\varphi(N)=\log N+O(\log\log\log N),
which completes the proof.

∎

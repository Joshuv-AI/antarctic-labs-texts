Cooperative Evasion by Translating Targets with Variable Speeds
Shivam Bajaj, Eloy Garcia, Shaunak D. Bopardikar

Abstract
We consider a problem of cooperative evasion between a single pursuer and multiple evaders in which the evaders are constrained to move in the positive Y Y direction. The evaders are slower than the vehicle and can choose their speeds from a bounded interval. The pursuer aims to intercept all evaders in a given sequence by executing a Manhattan pursuit strategy of moving parallel to the X X axis, followed by moving parallel to the Y Y axis. The aim of the evaders is to cooperatively pick their individual speeds so that the total time to intercept all evaders is maximized. We first obtain conditions under which evaders should cooperate in order to maximize the total time to intercept as opposed to each moving greedily to optimize its own intercept time. Then, we propose and analyze an algorithm that assigns evasive strategies to the evaders in two iterations as opposed to performing an exponential search over the choice of evader speeds. We also characterize a fundamental limit on the total time taken by the pursuer to capture all evaders when the number of evaders is large. Finally, we provide numerical comparisons against random sampling heuristics.

I-B

Contributions

We consider an optimal evasion problem between a single pursuer and
n
evaders. The pursuer moves with unit speed. The evaders are constrained to move in the positive
Y
direction such that their speeds
v_{i},i\in\{1,\dots,n\}
, lie in the interval
[{u}_{\textup{min}},{u}_{\textup{max}}]
with
0<{u}_{\textup{min}}<{u}_{\textup{max}}<1
. The evaders need to choose their speeds in order to maximize the total intercept time. We first present a complete solution to the optimal evasion problem for

{u}_{\textup{min}}
or

We present an algorithm which assigns the evasive strategies to the evaders in two iterations as opposed to performing an exponential search over the choice of evader speeds. For sufficiently large

, for which the global optimum is difficult to compute, we establish a fundamental upper bound to the total intercept time taken by the pursuer to capture all evaders. Finally, we provide comparisons through numerical results.

We consider an optimal evasion problem played between a single pursuer with simple motion and

Definition 1

(Simple Manhattan pursuit)

Given initial locations
(x_{i},y_{i})
and
(X,Y)
of an evader

and the pursuer

respectively, the pursuer

1.

moves with unit speed along the positive or negative
X

and then,

2.

moves with unit speed along positive or negative
Y

The evaders, initially located at
\{(x_{1},y_{1}),\dots,(x_{n},y_{n})\}

Y
direction with simple motion such that their instantaneous speeds

,

[{u}_{\textup{min}},{u}_{\textup{max}}]
with
0<{u}_{\textup{min}}<{u}_{\textup{max}}<1
(Fig.

). The pursuer is said to

i^{th}
target. The game
\emph{terminates}
when the pursuer intercepts the last evader. A

for an evader

is a measurable function, defined as
v_{i}(\{X(t),Y(t)\},\{x_{i}(t),y_{i}(t)\}_{i=1}^{n})\to[{u}_{\textup{min}},{u}_{\textup{max}}]

denotes the set of all locations
(X(\tau),Y(\tau)),\forall\tau\in[0,t]

Problem II.1

(Optimal evasion)

Given that the pursuer follows a fixed order to intercept the evaders, determine strategies

(x_{1},y_{1})

(X,Y)

Lemma III.1

(Time to intercept a single evader)

The time
T_{1}^{0}
taken by
P

is

\displaystyle T_{1}^{0}(v)=\begin{cases}\frac{\Delta x_{1}^{0}+y_{1}-Y}{1-v},\text{ if }\Delta x_{1}^{0}>(Y-y_{1})/v,\\
\frac{\Delta x_{1}^{0}+Y-y_{1}}{1+v},\text{ otherwise.}\end{cases}

Lemma III.2

(Monotonicity of time to intercept)

The time
T_{1}^{0}(v)
is a monotonically increasing function of
v
if
\Delta x^{0}_{1}>\frac{Y-y_{1}}{v}
. Otherwise,
T_{1}^{0}(v)
is a monotonically decreasing function of
v
.

Remark 1

The time to intercept is monotonic even when the pursuer follows a Euclidean strategy, i.e., given the initial locations of
E_{1}
and
P
, as
(x_{1},y_{1})
and
(X,Y)
respectively, the vehicle moves towards
(x_{1},y_{1}+vT_{1}^{0})
, where
T_{1}^{0}
is

\displaystyle\frac{(y_{1}-Y)v}{1-v^{2}}+\sqrt{\frac{(X-x_{1})^{2}}{1-v^{2}}+\frac{(Y-y_{1})^{2}}{(1-v^{2})^{2}}}.

Lemma
III.2
characterizes the monotonic nature of
T_{1}^{0}(v)
. This only means that the maximum is achieved at one of the extremes. The next theorem characterizes the evader’s optimal choice of speed.

Theorem III.3

(Single evader optimal strategy)

Given the initial locations
(x_{1},y_{1})
and
(X,Y)
of the evader and the pursuer respectively,
the optimal strategy
v^{*}
for the evader is

\displaystyle v^{*}=\begin{cases}{u}_{\textup{min}},\text{ if }y_{1}<Y-\Delta x_{1}^{0}(\frac{{u}_{\textup{min}}+{u}_{\textup{max}}}{2+{u}_{\textup{min}}-{u}_{\textup{max}}})\\
{u}_{\textup{max}},\text{ otherwise. }\end{cases}

Proof.

We provide only an outline. We find a location
(x_{1},y^{\prime})
such that
T_{1}^{0}({u}_{\textup{min}})=T_{1}^{0}({u}_{\textup{max}})
, where
T_{1}^{0}({u}_{\textup{min}})
(resp.
T_{1}^{0}({u}_{\textup{max}})
) is the time to intercept when evader moves with
{u}_{\textup{min}}
(resp.
{u}_{\textup{max}}
). From Lemma
III.1
,
T_{1}^{0}({u}_{\textup{min}})=T_{1}^{0}({u}_{\textup{max}})\Rightarrow y^{\prime}=Y-\Delta x_{1}^{0}\big(\frac{{u}_{\textup{min}}+{u}_{\textup{max}}}{2+{u}_{\textup{min}}-{u}_{\textup{max}}}\big)
.
This means that if
y_{1}=y^{\prime}
, then from Lemma
III.2
, it would not matter if the evader moves with
{u}_{\textup{min}}
or
{u}_{\textup{min}}
as
T_{1}^{0}({u}_{\textup{min}})=T_{1}^{0}({u}_{\textup{max}})
, and
T_{1}^{0}
will be maximum at both
{u}_{\textup{min}}
and
{u}_{\textup{max}}
. If
y_{1}<y^{\prime}
, then, from Lemma
III.2
, either
T_{1}^{0}({u}_{\textup{min}})<T_{1}^{0}({u}_{\textup{max}})
or
T_{1}^{0}({u}_{\textup{min}})>T_{1}^{0}({u}_{\textup{max}})
. Assuming
T_{1}^{0}({u}_{\textup{min}})<T_{1}^{0}({u}_{\textup{max}})\Rightarrow y_{1}>y^{\prime}
and thus, by contradiction, we get the result. The second case is analogous and we get the result.
∎

\Delta u={u}_{\textup{min}}-{u}_{\textup{max}}

2\Delta u^{4}\mathcal{A}n\gg(1-{u}_{\textup{min}}^{2})^{\frac{3}{2}}\Delta x^{2}(1-{u}_{\textup{max}})^{3}
, we get the result. Furthermore, since
\frac{d^{2}T}{d{n}_{\textup{max}}^{2}}<0
at the critical point
{n}_{\textup{max}}^{*}
,
{n}_{\textup{max}}^{*}
is indeed the point of maximum. This concludes the proof.

VI
Conclusions and Future Work

An optimal evasion problem between single a pursuer and multiple evaders was addressed. It is shown that by enforcing cooperation among evaders, they are able to maximize the total interception time. Conditions where cooperation is optimal are also presented which are crucial to implement the cooperative strategies. An upper bound on the total time to intercept all evaders is also presented.

In subsequent work, a generalized setup of multiple pursuers and evaders will be considered. Constant factor approximations for both, the evaders and the pursuers will also be addressed. Identifying which evaders should move with
{u}_{\textup{max}}
is another possible extension.

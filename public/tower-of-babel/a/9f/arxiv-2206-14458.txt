Spectral central limit theorem for additive functionals of isotropic and stationary Gaussian fields
Leonardo Maini, Ivan Nourdin

Abstract
. Let B = ( B x ) x ∈ ℝ d B=(B_{x})_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}} be a collection of N ⁡ ( 0 , 1 ) N(0,1) random variables forming a real-valued continuous stationary Gaussian field on ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d} , and set C ⁡ ( x − y ) = 𝔼 ⁡ [ B x ​ B y ] C(x-y)=\mathbb{E}[B_{x}B_{y}] . The common belief, representing the intuition that specialists in the subject have developed over the last four decades, is that as t → ∞ t\to\infty the fluctuations of Y t Y_{t} around its mean are, in general (i.e. except possibly in very special cases), Gaussian when B B has short memory, and non Gaussian when B B has long memory and R ≥ 2 R\geq 2 . Our main mathematical tools are the Malliavin-Stein method and Fourier analysis techniques. Theorem 2

B=(B_{x})_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}
be a real-valued continuous centered Gaussian field on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and
assume that
B
is stationary, isotropic and has unit-variance.
Let
\varphi:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
be
not odd
and such that
\mathbb{E}[\varphi(N)^{2}]<\infty
with
N\sim N(0,1)
, let
R
(resp.
R^{\prime}
) be the Hermite rank (resp. second Hermite rank) of
\varphi
, where
(R,R^{\prime})\notin\{(1,3)\}\cup\{(2k+1,n):k\geq 1,n\in\mathbb{N}\}

Consider

defined by (
3
), where
D\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
compact and
{\rm Vol}(D)>0
. Set
m_{t}=\mathbb{E}[Y_{t}]
and
\sigma_{t}=\sqrt{{\rm Var}(Y_{t})}>0
, and
recall the definition (
15
) of the isotropic spectral measure
\mu
.
Set

(19)

w_{q,t}=\int_{\{|z|\leq t\}}C(z)^{q}dz,

and assume that the following spectral condition holds

(20)

\int_{0}^{\infty}s^{-\frac{d}{R}}\mu(ds)<\infty.

Finally, when
R=2

\left|\mathcal{F}[\mathbf{1}_{D}](x)\right|=O\left(\frac{1}{|x|^{d/2}}\right)

\sigma_{t}^{2}\asymp\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}t^{d}w_{R,t}&&\mbox{if $R$ even}\\

t^{d}&&\mbox{if $R=1$ and $R^{\prime}\geq 5$}\end{array}\right.

\frac{Y_{t}-m_{t}}{\sigma_{t}}\overset{\rm law}{\to}N(0,1)\quad\mbox{as $t\to\infty$}.

By a simple Fubini argument, we observe that the spectral condition (

\int_{0}^{\infty}s^{-1-\frac{d}{R}}\mu(s)ds<\infty.

When

we obtain

(22)

{\rm Var}(Y_{t})={\rm const}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{t^{d}}{|\lambda|^{d}}|t\lambda|^{d}|\mathcal{F}[\mathbf{1}_{D}](t\lambda)|^{2}G(d\lambda).

In particular, for
D=\{|z|\leq 1\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
and Berry’s Random Wave Model
B=(B_{x})_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}}
we get (using (
26
))

\sigma_{t}^{2}={\rm Var(Y_{t})}={\rm const}\,t^{2}J^{2}_{1}(t);

although
Y_{t}
is Gaussian the conclusion of Theorem
2
cannot hold in this case, the
limit of
\frac{Y_{t}-m_{t}}{\sigma_{t}}
being ill-defined due to the fact that
{\rm Card}\{t:\,\sigma_{t}=0\}\cap[T,\infty)=+\infty
for all
T>0
.

To understand the significance of our spectral CLT, let us go back to BRWM and explain
how Theorem
2
together with Breuer-Major theorem allow to prove (
11
).
Since
C(x)=\rho(|x|)=J_{0}(|x|)
, it follows immediately from the representation (
16
)
that the isotropic spectral measure associated with BRWM
B=(B_{x})_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}}
is
\mu=\delta_{1}
, with
\delta_{1}

the Dirac mass at 1.
In particular, the spectral condition (
20
) is obviously satisfied, whatever the value of
R
.
The convergence (
11
) thus follows from the Breuer-Major Theorem
1
(see also (
9
)) if
R\geq 5
and from Theorem
2
in all the other cases in (
10
).

1.4.
Possible natural extensions of Theorem
2

As a natural extension of the present work, it would be interesting to study the
joint convergence
associated with Theorem
2
. More precisely, taking
D_{1},\dots,D_{n}
compact domains in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
is it possible to identify conditions that resemble those in Theorem
2

ensuring that the random vector

\left(\int_{tD_{1}}\varphi(B_{x})dx,\dots,\int_{tD_{n}}\varphi(B_{x})dx\right)

converges, after proper normalization, to a Gaussian random vector? In other words, can we prove a
multivariate
spectral central limit theorem?
Such an extension is not immediate, because the spectral condition (
20
) alone looks too general to capture the asymptotic behavior of the covariances between the components of the random vector. A partial answer to this question (covering also Berry’s model) will be given in the forthcoming work
[
13
]
by the first author.
Note that a multivariate result was obtained in the particular case of the nodal length of Berry’s model restricted to a finite collection of smooth compact domains
D_{1},\ldots,D_{n}
of the plane
in the recent paper
[
24
]
.

A further stronger generalization of Theorem
2
would be to prove a
functional
spectral central limit theorem.
This problem has been investigated in the particular case of nodal set volumes in
[
20
]
by ad hoc techniques.
In our general framework,
we believe however that proving such a functional extension would require novel ideas that go beyond the scope of the present paper.

1.5.
Plan of the paper

Apart from Section
6
that illustrates a use of Theorem
2
, the rest of the paper is fully devoted to the proof of this latter. More precisely, after a few needed preliminaries given in Section
2
,

(i)

in Section
3
we will first consider the situation where the Hermite rank
R
of
\varphi
is even and bigger or equal than
4
.
As we will see, in this case we have that
\int_{tD}|C(x)|^{R}dx=O(\log t)
, meaning that we are somehow in the ‘domain of attraction’ of the Breuer-Major theorem
1
.;

(ii)

we will then deal with the remaining cases, namely
R=2
in Section
4
and (
R=1
,
\varphi
non-odd and
R^{\prime}\neq 3
) in Section

.
We regard this part as the most important contribution of this paper, especially since among the Hermite ranks,

They turn out to be also the most difficult cases. To deal with them, we will have to introduce novel ideas, by making heavy use of Fourier techniques in a way that, to the best of our knowledge, has never been introduced before this work.

2.
A few preliminaries for the proof of Theorem
2

2.1.
Well-posedness of
Y_{t}

The following proposition explains why the random variable
Y_{t}
defined by (
3
) is well-defined for all
t
.

Proposition 3
.

Let
B=(B_{x})_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}
be a real-valued continuous centered Gaussian field on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, assumed to be stationary and to have unit-variance.
Let
D\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be compact with
{\rm Vol}(D)>0
and let
\varphi:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
be such that
\mathbb{E}[\varphi(N)^{2}]<\infty
with
N\sim N(0,1)
.
Consider the Hermite expansion of
\varphi
given by (
4
), and set

\varphi_{n}=\mathbb{E}[\varphi(N)]+\sum_{q=R}^{n}a_{q}H_{q},\quad n\geq R.

For each fixed
t>0
, the sequence
\int_{tD}\varphi_{n}(B_{x})dx
,
n\geq R
, is a.s. well-defined and converges in
L^{2}(\Omega)
. The limit is noted
Y_{t}
and we write, possibly with a slight abuse of notation:

Y_{t}=\int_{tD}\varphi(B_{x})dx.

Proof
.
That
\int_{tD}\varphi_{n}(B_{x})dx
is a.s. well-defined is because
tD
is compact,
\varphi_{n}
is a polynomial and
B
is continuous.
For any
n,m\geq R
and
q\in\mathbb{N}
, we have (since
|C(z)|\leq C(0)=1
,
a_{0}=\mathbb{E}[\varphi(N)]
and
a_{q}=0
if
q\in\{1,\dots,R-1\}
)

\left|\mathbf{1}_{[0,n\wedge m]}(q)\int_{(tD)^{2}}q!a_{q}^{2}C(x-y)^{q}dxdy\right|\leq{\rm Vol}(tD)^{2}q!a_{q}^{2},

with
\sum_{q=0}^{\infty}{\rm Vol}(tD)^{2}q!a_{q}^{2}={\rm Vol}(tD)^{2}\mathbb{E}[\varphi(N)^{2}]<\infty
by assumption. Then, by dominated convergence we obtain as
n,m\rightarrow\infty

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{tD}\varphi_{n}(B_{x})dx\int_{tD}\varphi_{m}(B_{y})dy\right]=\sum_{q=0}^{n\wedge m}q!a_{q}^{2}\int_{(tD)^{2}}C(x-y)^{q}dxdy

\displaystyle\to

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\varphi(N)]^{2}{\rm Vol}(tD)^{2}+\sum_{q=R}^{\infty}q!a_{q}^{2}\int_{(tD)^{2}}C(x-y)^{q}dxdy.

That is,
\left\{\int_{tD}\varphi_{n}(B_{x})dx\right\}_{n\geq R}
is an
L^{2}(\Omega)
-Cauchy sequence, and the
desired conclusion follows.
∎

2.2.
Bessel functions and Fourier transform of the indicator of the unit ball

The Bessel function
J_{\nu}
,
\nu\geq 0
, is defined by the series

J_{\nu}(t)=\left(\frac{t}{2}\right)^{\nu}\,\,\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{j}\frac{(t^{2}/4)^{j}}{j!\Gamma(j+\nu+1)},\quad t\in\mathbb{R},

and it is a solution to the ODE

t^{2}J^{\prime\prime}_{\nu}(t)+tJ^{\prime}_{\nu}(t)+(t^{2}-\nu^{2})J_{\nu}(t)=0.

It satisfies the classical Schläfli’s representation

(23)

J_{\nu}(t)=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\pi}\cos(\nu\theta-t\sin\theta)d\theta-\frac{\sin(\nu\pi)}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\nu\theta-t\sinh\theta}d\theta,

as well as the
Mehler-Sonine formula

(24)

J_{\nu}(t)=\frac{(t/2)^{\nu}}{\sqrt{\pi}\,\Gamma(\nu+\frac{1}{2})}\int_{-1}^{1}e^{its}(1-s^{2})^{\nu-\frac{1}{2}}ds.

Schläfli’s representation (
23
) immediately implies that
J_{\nu}
is bounded on
\mathbb{R}
. This, combined with an inequality found e.g. in
[
11
]
(see also the references therein), leads to

(25)

\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}\sqrt{t}|J_{\nu}(t)|<\infty\quad\mbox{for all $\nu\geq 0$},

a simple property that we will use several times in the forthcoming proof of Theorem
2
.

The Fourier transform of the indicator of the unit ball is given by

\mathcal{F}\left[{\bf 1}_{\{|x|\leq 1\}}\right](y)=\int_{\{|x|\leq 1\}}e^{i\langle x,y\rangle}dx.

Since
\mathcal{F}\left[{\bf 1}_{\{|x|\leq 1\}}\right]
is rotationally symmetric, we can write, with
\alpha_{d}
the volume of the unit ball
\{|x|\leq 1\}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

(26)

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}\left[{\bf 1}_{\{|x|\leq 1\}}\right](y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}\left[{\bf 1}_{\{|x|\leq 1\}}\right]((0,\ldots,0,|y|))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{-1}^{1}(1-x_{d}^{2})^{\frac{d-1}{2}}\alpha_{d-1}e^{ix_{d}|y|}dx_{d}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\alpha_{d-1}\sqrt{\pi}\,\Gamma\left(\frac{d+1}{2}\right)2^{\nu}\,|y|^{-\frac{d}{2}}J_{d/2}(|y|),

the last equality being a consequence of (
24
).

2.3.
Reduction to the
R
th chaos

In the short memory case, that is when
C\in L^{R}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, the Breuer-Major theorem
1
yields Gaussian fluctuations for
Y_{t}
.
In its modern proof given by
[
18
]
(see also
[
17
, Chapter 7]
) the chaotic expansion of
Y_{t}-m_{t}
is considered, namely

(27)

Y_{t}-m_{t}=\sum_{q=R}^{\infty}a_{q}Y_{q,t},

and the proof goes as follows.
It is first shown that
t^{-d}\sigma_{t}^{2}\to\sigma^{2}
in (
7
) by means of the isometry property of Hermite polynomials.
Then, it is proved using the Fourth Moment Theorem (see
[
17
, Theorem 5.2.7]
) that

t^{-d/2}Y_{q,t}\overset{\rm law}{\to}N(0,\sigma^{2}_{q})
for
all

q\geq R

from which it is deduced that
t^{-\frac{d}{2}}(Y_{t}-m_{t})\overset{\rm law}{\to}N(0,\sum_{q=R}^{\infty}a_{q}^{2}\sigma_{q}^{2})
thanks to
[
17
, Theorem 5.2.7]
.
In particular, we observe that no term is asymptotically dominant in (
27
): they all contribute to the limit.

As the following result will show, the situation is totally opposite in the critical and long memory cases (when
R
is even): here, it is the term
Y_{R,t}
alone which is
responsible of the limit.

Proposition 4

(Reduction to the
R
th chaos)
.

Let
B=(B_{x})_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}
be a real-valued continuous centered Gaussian field on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and
assume that
B
is stationary and has unit-variance (note that
d\geq 2
and isotropy are not required here).
Let
\varphi:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
be such that
\mathbb{E}[\varphi(N)^{2}]<\infty
(with
N\sim N(0,1)
) and have Hermite decomposition (
4
) and Hermite rank
R
,
and consider
Y_{t}
defined by (
3
), where
D\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
compact and
{\rm Vol}(D)>0
. Set
m_{t}=\mathbb{E}[Y_{t}]
,
\sigma_{t}=\sqrt{{\rm Var}(Y_{t})}>0
,
and recall the definition (
1
) of
C(x)
and the definition (
12
) of
Y_{q,t}
.

If
R
is
even
, if
t^{-d}{\rm Var}(Y_{R,t})\to\infty
and if
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|C(x)|^{M}dx<\infty
for some
M\geq R+1

then, as
t\to\infty
, we have
\sigma_{t}^{2}\asymp{\rm Var}(Y_{R,t})
and

(28)

Y_{R,t}/\sqrt{{\rm Var}(Y_{R,t})}\overset{\rm law}{\to}N(0,1)\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\frac{Y_{t}-m_{t}}{\sigma_{t}}\overset{\rm law}{\to}N(0,1).

Proof
. We have

{\rm Var}(Y_{q,t})=\int_{(tD)^{2}}\mathbb{E}[H_{q}(B_{y})H_{q}(B_{x})]dxdy=q!\int_{(tD)^{2}}C(x-y)^{q}dxdy.

Applying the change of variable
z=y-x
and then Fubini, we obtain that

{\rm Var}(Y_{q,t})=q!t^{d}v_{q,t},

where

(29)

v_{q,t}=\int_{\{|z|\leq{\rm diam}(D)t\}}C(z)^{q}\,g_{D}\left(\frac{z}{t}\right)dz,

where
{\rm diam}(D)=\sup{\{|x-y|:x,y\in D\}}<\infty
(because
D
is compact) and
g_{D}(x)
is the covariogram of
D
at
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, that is,
g_{D}(x)
is the Lebesgue measure of
D\cap(x+D)
.
Also, set

(30)

\widetilde{v}_{q,t}=\int_{\{|z|\leq{\rm diam}(D)t\}}|C(z)|^{q}\,g_{D}\left(\frac{z}{t}\right)dz,

and observe that
v_{R,t}=\widetilde{v}_{R,t}>0
since
R
is even.

For any
q>R
, we have

(31)

\frac{{\rm Var}(Y_{q,t})}{{\rm Var}(Y_{R,t})}=\frac{q!v_{q,t}}{R!v_{R,t}}.

Applying Cauchy-Schwarz inequality we obtain, for
q>R
,

\displaystyle\frac{|v_{q,t}|}{v_{R,t}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\int_{\{|z|\leq{\rm diam}(D)t\}}|C(z)|^{q}g_{D}(\frac{z}{t})dz}{v_{R,t}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\int_{\{|z|\leq{\rm diam}(D)t\}}|C(z)|^{q-\frac{R}{2}}|C(z)|^{\frac{R}{2}}g_{D}\left(\frac{z}{t}\right)dz}{v_{R,t}}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{\int_{\{|z|\leq{\rm diam}(D)t\}}|C(z)|^{2q-R}g_{D}\left(\frac{z}{t}\right)dz}{v_{R,t}}\right)^{1/2}=\left(\frac{\widetilde{v}_{2q-R,t}}{v_{R,t}}\right)^{1/2}.

Applying Cauchy-Schwarz again, but this time with
2q-R>R
instead of
q>R
, we obtain

\left(\frac{\widetilde{v}_{2q-R,t}}{v_{R,t}}\right)^{1/2}\leq\left(\frac{\widetilde{v}_{4q-3R,t}}{v_{R,t}}\right)^{\frac{1}{4}}.

By iterating the process, we get, for every
n\geq 3

\frac{|v_{q,t}|}{v_{R,t}}\leq\left(\frac{\widetilde{v}_{2q-R,t}}{v_{R,t}}\right)^{1/2}\leq\left(\frac{\widetilde{v}_{4q-3R,t}}{v_{R,t}}\right)^{\frac{1}{4}}\leq\dots\leq\left(\frac{\widetilde{v}_{R+2^{n}(q-R),t}}{v_{R,t}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2^{n}}}.

When
q>R
, we have
R+2^{n}(q-R)\geq 2^{n}
, so we may and will choose
n
large enough so that
R+2^{n}(q-R)\geq M
for all
q>R
(recall from the statement of Proposition
4
that
M
is an integer supposed to be such that
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|C(x)|^{M}dx<\infty
).
Using
|C(x)|\leq C(0)=1
and
g_{D}(x)\leq{\rm Vol}(D)
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we deduce that
\widetilde{v}_{R+2^{n}(q-R),t}\leq{\rm Vol}(D)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\big|C(x)\big|^{M}dx
. Combining all these facts together,
we finally get that

(32)

\frac{|v_{q,t}|}{v_{R,t}}\leq\left({\rm Vol}(D)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|C(x)|^{M}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2^{n}}}\,v_{R,t}^{-\frac{1}{2^{n}}}\quad\mbox{for all $q>R$}.

Since
{\rm Var}(Y_{t})=\sum_{q=R}^{\infty}a_{q}^{2}\,{\rm Var}(Y_{q,t})
, we deduce

(33)

\left|\frac{{\rm Var}(Y_{t})}{{\rm Var}(Y_{R,t})}-a^{2}_{R}\right|\leq\frac{1}{R!}\,\left({\rm Vol}(D)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|C(x)|^{M}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2^{n}}}\,\left(\sum_{q=R+1}^{\infty}a_{q}^{2}q!\right)\,v_{R,t}^{-\frac{1}{2^{n}}},

from which it comes that
{\rm Var}(Y_{t})\sim a^{2}_{R}{\rm Var}(Y_{R,t})
as
t\to\infty
, since
v_{R,t}=\frac{1}{R!}t^{-d}{\rm Var}(Y_{R,t})\to\infty
by assumption.

To conclude the proof of Proposition
4
,
it remains to prove
(
28
).
Using the decomposition

\displaystyle\frac{Y_{t}-m_{t}}{\sqrt{{\rm Var}(Y_{t})}}-{\rm sgn}(a_{R})\frac{Y_{R,t}}{\sqrt{{\rm Var}(Y_{R,t})}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{{\rm sgn}(a_{R})(Y_{t}-m_{t}-a_{R}Y_{R,t})}{a_{R}\sqrt{{\rm Var}(Y_{R,t})}}+\frac{Y_{t}-m_{t}}{\sqrt{{\rm Var}(Y_{t})}}\left\{1-\frac{1}{|a_{R}|}\sqrt{\frac{{\rm Var}(Y_{t})}{{\rm Var}(Y_{R,t})}}\right\}

we get that

(34)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\frac{Y_{t}-m_{t}}{\sqrt{{\rm Var}(Y_{t})}}-{\rm sgn}(a_{R})\frac{Y_{R,t}}{\sqrt{{\rm Var}(Y_{R,t})}}\right)^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\frac{\mathbb{E}[(Y_{t}-m_{t}-a_{R}Y_{R,t})^{2}]}{a_{R}^{2}{\rm Var}(Y_{R,t})}+2\,\left(1-\frac{1}{|a_{R}|}\,\sqrt{\frac{{\rm Var}(Y_{t})}{{\rm Var}(Y_{R,t})}}\right)^{2}.

Since
\mathbb{E}\left[\left({Y_{t}}-m_{t}-a_{R}Y_{R,t}\right)^{2}\right]=\sum_{q=R+1}^{\infty}a^{2}_{q}\,{{\rm Var}(Y_{q,t})}
, we deduce from (
31
) and (
32
) that

\displaystyle\frac{\mathbb{E}[(Y_{t}-m_{t}-a_{R}Y_{R,t})^{2}]}{a_{R}^{2}{\rm Var}(Y_{R,t})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{R!a^{2}_{R}}\left({\rm Vol}(D)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|C(x)|^{M}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2^{n}}}\,\left(\sum_{q=R+1}^{\infty}q!a^{2}_{q}\right)v_{R,t}^{-\frac{1}{2^{n}}},

and this tends to zero as
t\to\infty
.
By plugging this into (
34
) and taking into account that (
33
) holds, we deduce that

\frac{Y_{t}}{\sqrt{{\rm Var}(Y_{t})}}-{\rm sgn}(a_{R})\frac{Y_{R,t}}{\sqrt{{\rm Var}(Y_{R,t}})}\overset{L^{2}(\Omega)}{\to}0\quad\mbox{as $t\to\infty$},

from which the implication (
28
) now follows easily.∎

2.4.
Elements of Malliavin calculus and the Fourth Moment theorem

To obtain the
N(0,1)
distribution in the limit in Theorem
2
, we rely on the Fourth Moment Theorem of Nualart and Peccati
(see
[
17
, Theorem 5.2.7]
). Before stating and reformulating it in our framework, we start with some notions on Malliavin calculus.
For all the missing details we refer to
[
17
]
and
[
22
]
.

2.4.1.
The Wiener-Itô integral

Let
B=(B_{x})_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}
be a real-valued continuous centered Gaussian field on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and
assume that
B
is stationary with unit-variance.
Define

\mathcal{H}=\overline{{\rm span}{\{B_{x},\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\}}}^{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

Since
\mathcal{H}
is a real, separable Hilbert space, there is an isometry
\Phi:\mathcal{H}\rightarrow L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
. If we set
e_{x}:=\Phi(B_{x})
for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we have

\mathbb{E}[B_{x}B_{y}]=C(x-y)=\langle e_{x},e_{y}\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}.

Consider now the Gaussian noise
W=\{W(h),h\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})\}
, i.e. a family of centered Gaussian random variables with covariance given by

\mathbb{E}[W(h)W(g)]=\langle h,g\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}.

Since in this paper we are only interested in distributions, we can assume without loss of generality that
(B_{x})_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}=(W(e_{x}))_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}
.

For
q\geq 1
, we also define the
q
th Wiener chaos as the linear subspace of
L^{2}(\Omega)
generated by
\{H_{q}(W(h)),h\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})\}
. For every
h\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
with
\left\lVert h\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}=1
, we define the
q
th
Wiener-Itô integral

I_{q}(h^{\otimes q})=H_{q}(W(h)),

where
h^{\otimes q}:\mathbb{R}_{+}^{q}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is defined by

h^{\otimes q}(x_{1},\dots,x_{q})=\prod_{r=1}^{q}h(x_{r}).

Note that the definition of
I_{q}
can be extended to every function in the space
L^{2}_{s}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{q})
of symmetric functions in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{q})
so that
I_{q}:L^{2}_{s}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{q})\rightarrow L^{2}(\Omega)
is a linear map, because
{\rm span}\{h^{\otimes q},h\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})\}
is dense in
L^{2}_{s}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{q})
(see, e.g.,
[
7
]
).

2.4.2.
Contractions and the Fourth Moment Theorem

For
q\in\mathbb{N}
,
r\in\{1,\dots,q-1\}
and
h_{1},h_{2}
symmetric functions with unit norm in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
, we can define the
r
th
contraction
of
h_{1}^{\otimes q}
and
h_{2}^{\otimes q}
as the (non-symmetric) element of
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2q-2r})
given by

h_{1}^{\otimes q}\otimes_{r}h_{2}^{\otimes q}=\langle h_{1},h_{2}\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}^{r}\,h_{1}^{\otimes q-r}\otimes h_{2}^{\otimes q-r}.

Here again this definition can be extended (taking the closure in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{q})
) to every
h_{1},h_{2}
in
L^{2}_{s}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{q})
. We will denote the norm in the space
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{q})
as
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{q}
. We can finally state the celebrated
Fourth Moment Theorem
, proved in
[
23
]
by Nualart and Peccati.

Theorem 5

(Fourth Moment Theorem)
.

Fix
q\geq 2
, consider
(h_{t})_{t>0}\subset L^{2}_{s}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{q})
and assume that
\mathbb{E}[I_{q}(h_{t})^{2}]\rightarrow 1
as
t\rightarrow\infty
. Then the following assertions are equivalent:

•

I_{q}(h_{t})
converges in distribution to a standard Gaussian
N\sim N(0,1)
.

•

\mathbb{E}[I_{q}(h_{t})^{4}]\rightarrow 3=\mathbb{E}[N^{4}]
, where
N\sim N(0,1)
.

•

\left\lVert h_{t}\otimes_{r}h_{t}\right\rVert_{2q-2r}\rightarrow 0
as
t\rightarrow\infty
, for all
r=1,\dots,q-1
.

An important consequence of the previous result (in our framework) is the following.

Theorem 6
.

Let
B=(B_{x})_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}
be a real-valued continuous centered Gaussian field on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and
assume that
B
is stationary and has unit-variance. Assume that
D\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
is compact with
{\rm Vol}(D)>0
.
Recall the definition (
1
) of
C
.
Fix also
q\geq 2
,
recall the definition (
12
) of
Y_{q,t}
and
assume
{\rm Var}(Y_{q,t})>0
for all
t
large enough.
If we have, for any
r\in\{1,\dots,q-1\}
, that

(35)

\frac{t^{d}}{{\rm Var}^{2}(Y_{q,t})}\int_{\{|u|\leq diam(D)t\}^{3}}|C(x)|^{r}|C(y)|^{r}|C(z)|^{q-r}|C(x+y+z)|^{q-r}dxdydz

converges to
0
as
t\to\infty
,
then
Y_{q,t}/\sqrt{{\rm Var}(Y_{q,t})}\overset{law}{\rightarrow}N(0,1)
as
t\rightarrow\infty
.

Proof.

Let us first write
Y_{q,t}
as a
q
-th multiple Wiener-Itô integral with respect to
B
:

Y_{q,t}=\int_{tD}H_{q}(B_{x})dx=\int_{tD}H_{q}(W(e_{x}))dx=\int_{tD}I_{q}(e_{x}^{\otimes q})dx=I_{q}(f_{t,q}),

where

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{(tD)^{4}}C(x_{1}-x_{3})^{r}C(x_{2}-x_{4})^{r}C(x_{1}-x_{2})^{q-r}

\displaystyle\hskip 113.81102pt\times C(x_{3}-x_{4})^{q-r}dx_{1}dx_{2}dx_{3}dx_{4}.

a=x_{4}

d
.

The existence of the Bessel Gaussian field of order
\nu
and dimension
d\geq 2
is neither obvious, nor always true.
The following result provides a complete picture,
and also shows that
d
-dimensional Berry’s Random Wave Model appears to be the critical case for the existence of the Bessel Gaussian field.

Proposition 15
.

Fix
d\geq 2
and
\nu\geq 0
. There exists a Bessel Gaussian field
B^{\nu}=(B^{\nu}_{x})_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}
of order
\nu

and dimension
d
if and only if
\nu\geq\frac{d}{2}-1
. In this case, the isotropic spectral measure
associated to
B^{\nu}
(see (
16
)) is

(52)

\mu_{\nu}(ds)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}c_{d,\nu}s^{d-1}(1-s^{2})^{\nu-\frac{d}{2}}\mathbf{1}_{(0,1)}ds&&\mbox{if $\nu>\frac{d}{2}-1$}\\
\delta_{1}(ds)&&\mbox{if $\nu=\frac{d}{2}-1$}\end{array}\right.,

where
c_{d,\nu}>0
is chosen so that
\mu_{\nu}
is a probability measure.

Proof.

Observe that
\rho_{\nu}
has representation

\rho_{\nu}(r)=c_{\nu}\frac{J_{\nu}(r)}{r^{\nu}}={\rm const}\times\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{j}\frac{\Gamma(\nu+1)(r^{2}/4)^{j}}{j!\Gamma(j+\nu+1)}.

Combining this representation with
[
9
, Proposition 2.2]
, one has that
\rho_{\nu}
is not positive definite when
\nu<\frac{d}{2}-1
, showing that the Bessel Gaussian field does not exist in this case.

The statement for the critical case
\nu=\frac{d}{2}-1
immediately follows from (
16
) and (
18
), after observing that
\rho_{\frac{d}{2}-1}=b_{d}
.

For
\nu>\frac{d}{2}-1
, one can actually check that
\mu_{\nu}
given in the statement is the distribution of the square root of the Beta random variable
\beta(\frac{d}{2},\nu-\frac{d}{2}+1)
. Then, using Fubini and
b_{d}=\rho_{\frac{d}{2}-1}
we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}b_{d}(rt)\mu_{\nu}(dr)={\rm const}\int_{0}^{1}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{k}\Gamma(d/2)}{\Gamma(k+d/2)k!}((rt)^{2}/4)^{k}\right)\mu_{\nu}(dr)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\rm const}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{k}\Gamma(d/2)}{\Gamma(k+d/2)k!}\left(\frac{t}{2}\right)^{2k}\int_{0}^{1}r^{2k}\mu_{\nu}(dr)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\rm const}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{\Gamma(\nu+1)(-1)^{k}}{\Gamma(\nu+k+1)k!}\left(\frac{t}{2}\right)^{2k}=\rho_{\nu}(t),

where the second-last equality comes from the fact that the
k
-th moment of a random variable
\beta(\frac{d}{2},\nu-\frac{d}{2}+1)
is

\frac{\Gamma(d/2+k)\Gamma(\nu+1)}{\Gamma(d/2)\Gamma(\nu+k+1)}.

This means (see (
16
)) that
\rho_{\nu}
defines a Bessel Gaussian field when
\nu>\frac{d}{2}-1
and that its spectral measure is
\mu_{\nu}
as defined in the statement.

The fact that any Bessel Gaussian field is continuous can be proved using standard results, see e.g.
[
1
, Theorem 1.4.1]
.
∎

Now let us apply our Theorem
2
to the Bessel Gaussian field with parameter
\nu\geq\frac{d}{2}-1
and dimension
d\geq 2
.

Corollary 16

(Spectral CLT applied to
B^{\nu}
)
.

Fix
d\geq 2
and
\nu\geq\frac{d}{2}-1
. Let
B^{\nu}=(B^{\nu}_{x})_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}
be a Bessel Gaussian field on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
Let
\varphi:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
be not odd and such that
\mathbb{E}[\varphi(N)^{2}]<\infty
with
N\sim N(0,1)
, let
R
be the Hermite rank of
\varphi

and consider
Y_{t}
defined by (
3
), where
B=B^{\nu}
and
D
is compact,
Vol(D)>0
. Set
m_{t}=\mathbb{E}[Y_{t}]
and
\sigma_{t}=\sqrt{{\rm Var}(Y_{t})}>0
.
If
R=2
, assume that
|\mathcal{F}[\mathbf{1}_{D}](x)|=O\left(\frac{1}{|x|^{d/2}}\right)
as
|x|\rightarrow\infty
. Then, if
R=2
or
R\geq 4
, we have that
\sigma_{t}^{2}\asymp t^{d}w_{R,t}
and

\frac{Y_{t}-m_{t}}{\sigma_{t}}\overset{\rm law}{\to}N(0,1)\quad\mbox{as $t\to\infty$}.

Proof.

Using standard asymptotics for Bessel functions (see e.g.
[
11
]
), one has

\rho_{\nu}(r)=c_{\nu}\frac{\cos(r-a_{\nu})}{r^{\nu+1/2}}+O\left(\frac{1}{r^{\nu+3/2}}\right)\quad\mbox{as $r\rightarrow\infty$}

for some constants
c_{\nu}
and
a_{\nu}
. Then, it follows that

\int_{0}^{2t}|\rho_{\nu}(r)|^{q}r^{d-1}dr=O\left(\int_{1}^{2t}\frac{dr}{r^{q\nu+\frac{q}{2}+1-d}}\right)\quad\mbox{as $t\rightarrow\infty$}.

Since
\nu\geq\frac{d}{2}-1
, when
R\geq 5
we have that
|\rho|^{q}(r)r^{d-1}
is integrable, and the result follows by Breuer Major Theorem
1
. For
R=2
and
R=4
, the result follows by a direct application of Theorem
2
, since the negative moment of order
d/R
of
\mu_{\nu}
exists for every
d\geq 2
and
R\geq 2
.
∎

Remark 1
.

For simplicity, in the previous statement we did not consider all the cases which can be solved applying our Theorem
2
. For example, the spectral condition (
20
) for
R=1
is not verified by
\mu_{\nu}
if
\nu>\frac{d}{2}-1
, but holds in the
d
-dimensional Berry case
\nu=\frac{d}{2}-1
.
Moreover, relying on a slight variation of the proof of Proposition
14
, it would have not been very difficult to also cover the non-critical cases where
\nu\in[\frac{d}{2}-1,\frac{d}{2}-\frac{1}{2})
. We skip the details for the sake of brevity.

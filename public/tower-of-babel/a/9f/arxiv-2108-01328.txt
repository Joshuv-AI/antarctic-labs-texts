Abstract
. Let 𝔤 \mathfrak{g} be a Lie superalgebra of type 𝔰 ​ 𝔩 \mathfrak{sl} or 𝔬 ​ 𝔰 ​ 𝔭 \mathfrak{osp} with an odd principal nilpotent element f f . 1.
Introduction

The notion of
W
-algebra appeared first in physics, in the works of Zamolodchikov
[
32
]
and of Lukyanov and Fateev
[
16
]
. In this context, it appeared as an extension of the Virasoro algebra, which is a symmetry of two-dimensionnal conformal field theories. It was then realized that the classical version of
W
-algebras was the new Poisson algebra introduced in
[
13
]
by Drinfeld and Sokolov in the study of the center of an affine Lie algebra. Generalizations of classical
W
-algebras were introduced in relation to
Hamiltonian reduction of Wess-Zumino-Witten (WZW) to Toda models
[
3
,
2
]
.
They were noted
W(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h})
where
\mathfrak{g}
is the finite dimensional Lie algebra of the underlying affine Lie algebra.

\mathfrak{h}
is a subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}
, defining a nilpotent element in
\mathfrak{g}
. The nilpotent element is defined as the principal nilpotent of
\mathfrak{h}
, so that the
W
-algebras defined by Drinfeld and Sokolov were just the classical version of
W(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})\equiv W(\mathfrak{g})
.

The case where
\mathfrak{g}
is a superalgebra was studied first in
[
15
,
25
]
, and led to
W
-superalgebras. Then, in
[
7
]
, the superfield formalism was introduced to deal with the supersymmetric (SUSY) version of
W
-algebras. A complete classification of classical
W
-algebras and of SUSY classical
W
-algebras can be found in
[
20
]
. For classical
W
-algebras and superalgebras, it relies on the classification of
\mathfrak{su}(2)
embeddings in
\mathfrak{g}

[
14
]
. For SUSY
W
-algebras, it uses the classification of
\mathfrak{osp}(1|2)
embeddings
[
26
,
20
]
in
\mathfrak{g}
.
Reviews on the subject can be found in
[
17
,
4
]
.

The quantum version of these algebras is done in the framework of BRST cohomology, which deals, at the quantum level, of the Hamiltonian reduction introduced in the classical case. They appeared first in
[
18
]
, and the cohomology was resolved in

[
8
]
for quantum
W
-algebras. The supersymmetric version was done in
[
27
]
.

BRST cohomologies can be understood in the theory of vertex algebras
and vertex algebra structures of quantum
W
-algebras are written in
[
23
,
24
,
10
]
. On the other hand, classical
W
-algebras can be unerstood as quasi-classical limit of quantum
W
-algebras so that they are Poisson vertex algebras
[
11
,
12
,
29
]
. In these articles, the authors often denote by
W(\mathfrak{g},F,k)
and
\mathcal{W}(\mathfrak{g},F,k)
the quantum and classical
W
-algebras
W(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h})
and
\mathcal{W}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h})
where
F
is the
even
principal nilpotent element of
\mathfrak{h}
and
k
is the central element of the affine Lie superalgebra
\hat{\mathfrak{g}}
. For the principal nilpotent element
F
of
\mathfrak{g}
, an explicit construction of generators of
W(\mathfrak{g},F,k)
was built in
[
1
]
when
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{sl}(N)
. Analogous results on
\mathcal{W}(\mathfrak{g},F,k)
were written in
[
31
]
when
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{sl}(N),\mathfrak{so}(N),\mathfrak{sp}(2n)
and
G_{2}
.
See also
[
9
]
for a review on the subject.

The SUSY quantum
W
-algebras
[
28
]
and the SUSY classical
W
-algebras
[
30
]
are defined as SUSY vertex algebras and SUSY Poisson vertex algebras
[
22
,
21
]
. Since a SUSY
W
-algebra is governed by a Lie superalgebra
\mathfrak{g}
and an
\mathfrak{osp}(1|2)
embedding which is determined by an
odd
nilpotent element
f
, quantum and classical SUSY
W
-algebras can be denoted
by
W(\bar{\mathfrak{g}},f,k)
and
\mathcal{W}(\bar{\mathfrak{g}},f,k)
. In addition, in
[
28
]
,
explicit forms of generators of the SUSY quantum
W
-algebra
W(\overline{\mathfrak{sl}}(n|n\pm 1),f,k)
has been constructed when

is odd principal nilpotent.

In this paper, we consider a finite simple Lie superalgebra of type
\mathfrak{sl}
or
\mathfrak{osp}
which possesses an odd principal nilpotent element
f
. Such a Lie superalgebra
\mathfrak{g}
is of type
\mathfrak{sl}(n|n\pm 1)
or of type
\mathfrak{osp}(M|2n)
with

to determine the structure of

\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{so}(N),\mathfrak{sp}(2n)

Lie (super)algebras. We rephrase it in the framework of SUSY Poisson vertex algebras and find generators of

The paper is organized as follows. Section

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we review definitions and properties of Lie superalgebras and supersymmetric Poisson vertex algebras. For more details, we refer to

[

]

[
21
]

Throughout this paper, we assume that the base field is
\mathbb{C}

2.1.
Lie superalgebras

A vector superspace
V=V_{\bar{0}}\oplus V_{\bar{1}}
is a vector space with a
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
-grading.
An element
a\in V_{i}
is called even (resp. odd) if
i=\bar{0}
(resp.
i=\bar{1}
). The parity
p(a)
of an even (resp. odd) element
a
is defined by
p(a)=0
(resp.
p(a)=1
).
An algebra
A=A_{\bar{0}}\oplus A_{\bar{1}}
is a superalgebra if it is a vector superspace satisfying

A_{i}A_{j}=A_{i+j}
for
i,j\in\{\bar{0},\bar{1}\}
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a vector superspace endowed with a
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
-grade preserving bilinear bracket

\ [\ ,\ ]:\mathfrak{g}\times\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}.

If the bracket satisfies

•

(skewsymmetry)
[a,b]=-(-1)^{p(a)p(b)}[b,a]
,

•

(Jacobi identity)
[a,[b,c]]=[[a,b],c]+(-1)^{p(a)p(b)}[b,[a,c]]
,

for any
a,b,c\in\mathfrak{g}
, then
\mathfrak{g}
is called a
Lie superalgebra.

For a vector superspace
V
, the algebra
\text{End}(V)
of endomorphisms on
V
is a superalgebra such that

\text{End}(V)_{i}=\{f\in\text{End}(V)|f(V_{j})\subset V_{i+j}\}
for
i,j=\bar{0},\bar{1}
.
One can define a Lie superalgebra structure on
\text{End}(V)
by considering the super commutator

(2.1)

[f,g]=fg-(-1)^{p(f)p(g)}gf\quad\text{ for }f,g\in\text{End}(V).

Such Lie superalgebra is called the
general linear Lie superalgebra
of
V
and denoted by
\mathfrak{gl}(V)
.
When the dimensions of
V_{\bar{0}}
and
V_{\bar{1}}
are
m
and
n
, respectively,
\mathfrak{gl}(V)
is also denoted by
\mathfrak{gl}(m|n)
.

Let us describe
\mathfrak{gl}(m|n)
more explicitly. Let
I=\{1,2,\cdots,m+n\}
and let
V
be a vector superspace
with a basis
\{v_{i}|i\in I\}
which consists of
m
even elements and
n
odd elements. Then there are subsets
I_{\bar{0}}
and
I_{\bar{1}}
of
I
such that
\{v_{i}|i\in I_{\bar{0}}\}
and
\{v_{i}|i\in I_{\bar{1}}\}
are bases of
V_{\bar{0}}
and
V_{\bar{1}}
.
Then

(2.2)

I=I_{\bar{0}}\sqcup I_{\bar{1}}.

Note that
\mathfrak{gl}(m|n)
is spanned by

\mathbb{e}_{ij}
such that
\mathbb{e}_{ij}v_{k}=\delta_{jk}v_{i}
for
i,j,k\in I
.
For convenience, denote

(2.3)

p(i):=p(v_{i}).

Then the parity
p(\mathbb{e}_{ij})\in\{0,1\}
is defined by

(-1)^{p(\mathbb{e}_{ij})}=(-1)^{p(i)+p(j)}.

The bracket (
2.1
) and the even supersymmetric invariant bilinear form
(\ |\ )
on
\mathfrak{gl}(m|n)
are written as follows:

(2.4)

[\mathbb{e}_{ij},\mathbb{e}_{kl}]=\delta_{j,k}\mathbb{e}_{il}-(-1)^{(p(i)+p(j))(p(k)+p(l))}\delta_{i,l}\mathbb{e}_{kj},\quad(\mathbb{e}_{ij}|\mathbb{e}_{kl})=\delta_{jk}\delta_{il}(-1)^{p(i)}.

In addition, the
supertrace
of
a=\sum_{i\in I}a_{ij}\mathbb{e}_{ij}\in\mathfrak{gl}(m|n)
for
a_{ij}\in\mathbb{C}
is

(2.5)

str(a)=\sum_{i\in I}(-1)^{p(i)}a_{ii}\in\mathbb{C},

and the
supertranspose
of
a
is

(2.6)

a^{st}=\sum_{i,j\in I}(-1)^{(p(i)+1)p(j)}a_{ij}\mathbb{e}_{ji}\in\mathfrak{gl}(m|n).

If we assume
p(i)=0
for
i=1,2,\cdots,m
and
p(i)=1
for
i=m+1,m+2,\cdots,m+n
, then

(2.7)

\mathfrak{gl}(m|n)=\left\{\left.\begin{pmatrix}\begin{matrix}A\end{matrix}&\hskip-5.0pt\vline\hskip-5.0pt&B\\
\hline\cr C&\hskip-5.0pt\vline\hskip-5.0pt&\begin{matrix}D\end{matrix}\end{pmatrix}\right|\left.\begin{array}[]{cc}A:m\times m,&B:m\times n,\\
C:n\times m,&D:n\times n\end{array}\right.\text{ matrices }\right\},

where
A
and
D
correspond to the even part and
B
and
C
to the odd part.

Example 2.2
.

The following examples are simple Lie super-subalgebras of general linear Lie superalgebras.

(1)

Let
n\neq m
and
m,n\geq 1
. The special linear Lie superalgebra of type
A(m|n)
is

(2.8)

\mathfrak{sl}(m+1|n+1)=\{X\in\mathfrak{gl}(m+1|n+1)|str(X)=0\}.

(2)

Consider
I=\{1,2,\cdots,2m+2n+1\}
for
m\geq 0
and
n\geq 1
and assume that

p(i)=0
for
i=1,2,\cdots,2m+1
and
p(i)=1
for
i=2m+2,2m+3,\cdots,2m+2n+1
.
Then the ortho-symplectic Lie superalgebra
\mathfrak{osp}(2m+1|2n)\subset\mathfrak{gl}(2m+1|2n)
is defined by

(2.9)

\mathfrak{osp}(2m+1|2n)=\{\,a\in\mathfrak{gl}(2m+1|2n)\,|\,a^{st}\mathcal{J}^{stan}_{2m+1|2n}+\mathcal{J}^{stan}_{2m+1|2n}a=0\,\},

where

(2.10)

\mathcal{J}^{stan}_{2m+1|2n}=\begin{pmatrix}\begin{matrix}0&I_{m}&0\\
I_{m}&0&0\\
0&0&1\end{matrix}&\hskip-5.0pt\vline\hskip-5.0pt&0\\
\hline\cr 0&\hskip-5.0pt\vline\hskip-5.0pt&\begin{matrix}0&I_{n}\\
-I_{n}&0\end{matrix}\end{pmatrix}\in\mathfrak{gl}(2m+1|2n).

Here,
I_{k}
stands for the identity matrix of size
k\times k
. The Lie superalgebra (
2.9
) is called type
B(m,n)
.

(3)

Consider
I=\{1,2,\cdots,2m+2n\}
for
m\geq 1
and
n\geq 1
and assume that

p(i)=0
for
i=1,2,\cdots,2m
and
p(i)=1
for
i=2m+1,2m+2,\cdots,2m+2n
.
Then the ortho-symplectic Lie superalgebra
\mathfrak{osp}(2m|2n)\subset\mathfrak{gl}(2m|2n)
is defined by

(2.11)

\mathfrak{osp}(2m|2n)=\{\,a\in\mathfrak{gl}(2m|2n)\,|\,a^{st}\mathcal{J}^{stan}_{2m|2n}+\mathcal{J}^{stan}_{2m|2n}a=0\,\},

where

(2.12)

\mathcal{J}^{stan}_{2m|2n}=\begin{pmatrix}\begin{matrix}0&I_{m}\\
-I_{m}&0\\
\end{matrix}&\hskip-5.0pt\vline\hskip-5.0pt&0\\
\hline\cr 0&\hskip-5.0pt\vline\hskip-5.0pt&\begin{matrix}0&I_{n}\\
-I_{n}&0\end{matrix}\end{pmatrix}.

If
m=1
, it is called type
C(n+1)
and if
m\geq 2
, it is called type
D(m,n)
.

In the Lie superalgebras in Example
2.2
, the bilinear form

(2.13)

(X|Y):=str(XY)

is nondegenerate, even, supersymmetric and invariant.

2.2.
Lie superalgebras with odd principal nilpotent elements

Proposition 2.3
.

[
26
,
20
]

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a finite simple Lie superalgebra isomorphic to
\mathfrak{sl}(m|n)
or
\mathfrak{osp}(m|2n)
for
m,n\geq 1
. If
\mathfrak{g}
has an odd principal nilpotent element then
\mathfrak{g}
is isomorphic to one of the following algebras:

\mathfrak{sl}(n\pm 1|n),\quad\mathfrak{osp}(M|2n)\text{ with }M=2n\pm 1,\ 2n+2,\ 2n,\quad D(2,1;\alpha)\text{ with }\alpha\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0,\pm 1\}.

For the rest of the paper, we deal with Lie superalgebras of type
\mathfrak{gl}
,
\mathfrak{sl}
and
\mathfrak{osp}
possessing odd principal nilpotent elements.

Now, let us describe matrix presentations of Lie superalgebras in Proposition
2.3
.
They possess a odd principal nilpotent element that is expressed as a triangular matrix.

Example 2.4
.

Let
I=\{1,2,\cdots,2n+1\}=I_{\bar{0}}\sqcup I_{\bar{1}}
where

I_{0}=\{1,3,\cdots,2n+1\}\text{ and }I_{\bar{1}}=\{2,4,\cdots,2n\}.

The Lie superalgebras
\mathfrak{gl}(n+1|n)
and
\mathfrak{sl}(n+1|n)
are defined by (
2.4
)
and
(
2.8
).Then

(2.14)

f=\sum_{i=1}^{2n}\mathbb{e}_{i+1\,i}

is an odd principal nilpotent element.

Example 2.5
.

Let
I=\{1,2,\cdots,2n-1\}=I_{\bar{0}}\sqcup I_{\bar{1}}
where

I_{0}=\{2,4,\cdots,2n-2\}\text{ and }I_{\bar{1}}=\{1,3,\cdots,2n-1\}.

The Lie superalgebras
\mathfrak{gl}(n-1|n)
and
\mathfrak{sl}(n-1|n)
are defined by (
2.4
) and (
2.8
). Then

(2.15)

f=\sum_{i=1}^{2n-2}\mathbb{e}_{i+1\,i}

is an odd principal nilpotent element.

Example 2.6
.

Let
I=\{1,2,\cdots,M+2n\}
for
M=2n-1\text{ or }2n+1
. If
M=2n+1
(resp.
M=2n-1
), we define
I_{\bar{0}}
and
I_{\bar{1}}
as in Example
2.4
(resp. Example
2.5
).
We introduce the map

(2.16)

\delta:I\to\{0,1\},\quad k\mapsto\delta_{k}

such that

•

if
M=2n+1
,
\delta_{k}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1&\text{ if }k\in\{2n+2,2n+4,\cdots,4n\},\\
0&\text{ otherwise;}\end{array}\right.

•

if
M=2n-1
,
\delta_{k}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1&\text{ if }k\in\{2n+1,2n+3,\cdots,4n-1\},\\
0&\text{ otherwise}.\end{array}\right.

Then
\mathcal{J}_{M|2n}
in Example
2.2
can be written as

(2.17)

\mathcal{J}_{M|2n}=\sum_{k\in I}(-1)^{\delta_{k}}\mathbb{e}_{k\,k^{\prime}}\text{ for }k^{\prime}=|I|+1-k.

Consider the automorphism of
\mathfrak{gl}(M|2n)
defined by

(2.18)

\theta:\mathbb{e}_{ij}\mapsto\tau(i,j)\mathbb{e}_{j^{\prime}\,i^{\prime}}:=(-1)^{p(i)p(j)+p(j)+1+\delta_{i}+\delta_{j}}\mathbb{e}_{j^{\prime}\,i^{\prime}}.

Then, the Lie superalgebra
\mathfrak{osp}(M|2n)
is spanned by

(2.19)

F_{ij}=\mathbb{e}_{ij}+\theta(\mathbb{e}_{ij}),\quad i,j\in I.

For
i,j\in I
,
F_{j^{\prime}\,i^{\prime}}=\tau(i,j)F_{ij}

and
\{F_{ij}\}_{(i,j)\in\mathcal{B}}
is a basis of
\mathfrak{osp}(M|2n)
, where

(2.20)

\mathcal{B}=\{(i,j)|\ i+j\leq|I|+1\ \}\setminus\{(i,i^{\prime})|\ p(i)=0\}\subset I\times I.

Finally,

(2.21)

f=\sum_{i=1}^{\frac{|I|-1}{2}}F_{i+1\,i}

is an odd principal nilpotent element.

Example 2.7
.

Let
I=\{1,2,\cdots,M+2n\}
for
M=2n\text{ or }2n+2
. Then
I_{\bar{0}}
and
I_{\bar{1}}=I\setminus I_{\bar{0}}
are defined by

•

if
M=2n,\qquad I_{\bar{0}}=\{2k\,|\,1\leq k\leq n\}\cup\{2n+2k-1\,|\,1\leq k\leq n\},

•

if
M=2n+2,\quad I_{\bar{0}}=\{2k-1\,|\,1\leq k\leq n+1\}\cup\{2n+2k\,|\,1\leq k\leq n+1\}.

We define the map

(2.22)

\delta:I\to\{0,1\},\quad k\mapsto\delta_{k}

such that

•

if
M=2n
,
\delta_{k}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1&\text{ if }k\in\{1,3,\cdots,2n-1\},\\
0&\text{ otherwise;}\end{array}\right.

•

if
M=2n+2
,
\delta_{k}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1&\text{ if }k\in\{2,4,\cdots,2n\},\\
0&\text{ otherwise.}\end{array}\right.

As in Example
2.6
, we get
\mathfrak{osp}(M|2n)
via
\mathcal{J}_{M|2n}=\sum_{k\in I}(-1)^{\delta_{k}}\mathbb{e}_{k\,k^{\prime}}
for
k^{\prime}=|I|+1-k.

We can also define
F_{ij}
and
\mathcal{B}
through (
2.19
) and (
2.20
).
Then,
\{F_{ij}|(i,j)\in\mathcal{B}\}
is a basis of
\mathfrak{osp}(M|2n)
. Finally, the element

(2.23)

f=F_{\frac{|I|}{2}+1\,\frac{|I|}{2}-1}+\sum_{i=1}^{\frac{|I|}{2}-1}F_{i+1\,i}

is an odd principal nilpotent element.

2.3.
Supersymmetric Poisson vertex algebras

Let
\mathcal{R}
be a vector superspace with an odd operator
D:\mathcal{R}\to\mathcal{R}
, i.e.
\mathcal{R}
is a
\mathbb{C}[D]
-module. Consider a super non-commutative associative algebra
\mathbb{C}[\chi]
generated by an odd indeterminate
\chi
. We define the
\mathbb{C}[D]
-module structure of

\mathbb{C}[\chi]\otimes\mathcal{R}
by

(2.24)

D(\chi\otimes R)=-\chi\otimes D(R)-2\chi^{2}\otimes R

for
R\in\mathcal{R}
. Note that we usually write elements in
\mathbb{C}[\chi]\otimes\mathcal{R}
without tensor product. In this way, (
2.24
) can be simply written as follows:

(2.25)

D\chi+\chi D=-2\chi^{2}.

A bilinear bracket

\,[\ {}_{\chi}{}\ ]:\mathcal{R}\times\mathcal{R}\to\mathbb{C}[\chi]\otimes\mathcal{R}

on the
\mathbb{C}[D]
-module
\mathcal{R}
is called a
\chi
-bracket if it satisfies the
sesquilinearity

property:

(2.26)

\,[Da\,{}_{\chi}\,b\,]=-\chi[\,a\,{}_{\chi}\,b\,],\quad[\,a\,{}_{\chi}\,Db\,]=(-1)^{p(a)+1}(D+\chi)[\,a\,{}_{\chi}\,b\,].

Definition 2.8
.

Let
\mathcal{R}
be a
\mathbb{C}[D]
-module with a
\chi
-bracket
[\ {}_{\chi}\ ]
. If the bracket satisfies the following properties, then we call
\mathcal{R}
a (
N_{k}=1
)
supersymmetric (SUSY) Lie conformal algebra (LCA)
: for any
a,b,c\in\mathcal{R}
,

•

(skewsymmetry)
[a{}_{\chi}b]=(-1)^{p(a)p(b)}[b{}_{-\chi-D}a]
,

•

(Jacobi identity)

\quad{}[a{}_{\chi}[b{}_{\gamma}c]]=(-1)^{p(a)+1}[[a{}_{\chi}b]_{\chi+\gamma}c]+(-1)^{(p(a)+1)(p(b)+1)}[b{}_{\gamma}[a_{\chi}c]]\in\mathbb{C}[\chi,\gamma]\otimes\mathcal{R}
,

where
\mathbb{C}[\chi,\gamma]
is a super noncommutative associative algebra generated by odd indeterminates
\chi
and
\gamma
such that
\chi\gamma=-\gamma\chi
. In addition,
\mathbb{C}[\chi,\gamma]\otimes\mathcal{R}
is a
\mathbb{C}[D]
-module via
\gamma D+D\gamma=-2\gamma^{2}
and (
2.25
).

Let
\mathcal{R}
be a SUSY LCA and denote

(2.27)

\ \ [\,a\,{}_{\chi}\,b\,]=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}}\chi^{n}a_{(n)}b

for
a,b\in\mathcal{R}
. Then the skewsymmetry in Definition
2.8
can be written as

(2.28)

\ \ \sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}}\chi^{n}a_{(n)}b=(-1)^{p(a)p(b)}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}}(-\chi-D)^{n}b_{(n)}a,

where the RHS is computed by (
2.25
).
The Jacobi identity in Definition
2.8
can be computed by

(2.29)

\displaystyle[\,a\ {}_{\chi}\,\gamma^{n}b\,]=(-1)^{n(p(a)+1)}\gamma^{n}[\,a\,{}_{\chi}\,b\,],

\displaystyle[\,\chi^{n}a\,{}_{\chi+\gamma}\,b\,]=(-1)^{n}\chi^{n}[\,a\,{}_{\chi+\gamma}\,b\,]=(-1)^{n}\chi^{n}\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}}(\chi+\gamma)^{m}a_{(m)}b.

Definition 2.9
.

(1)

Let
A
be a superalgebra and
D:A\to A
be a linear operator of parity
p(D)
. If
D
is a derivation, that is,

D(ab)=D(a)b+(-1)^{p(D)p(a)}aD(b)

for
a,b\in A
, then
A
is called a
differential algebra
associated with
D
.

(2)

Let a
\mathbb{C}[D]
-module
\mathcal{V}
be a SUSY LCA endowed with a
\chi
-bracket
\{\ {}_{\chi}\ {}\}
. If
\mathcal{V}
is a unital supercommutative associative algebra such that

•

\mathcal{V}
is a differential algebra with respect to the odd derivation
D
,

•

(Leibniz rule)
\{\,a\,{}_{\chi}\,bc\,\}=(-1)^{(p(a)+1)p(b)}b\{\,a\,{}_{\chi}\,c\,\}+\{\,a\,{}_{\chi}\,b\,\}c
for
a,b,c\in\mathcal{V}
.

Then,
\mathcal{V}
is called a
SUSY Poisson vertex algebra (PVA)
.

The Leibniz rule in Definition
2.9
and the skewsymmetry of SUSY PVA imply the right Leibniz rule

(2.30)

\{\,ab\,{}_{\chi}\,c\,\}=(-1)^{p(c)p(b)}\{\,a\,{}_{\chi+D}\,c\,\}_{\to}b+(-1)^{p(a)(p(b)+p(c))}\{\,b\,{}_{\chi+D}\,c\,\}_{\to}a,

where
\{\,a\,{}_{\chi+D}\,c\,\}_{\to}b=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}}a_{(n)}c\,(\chi+D)^{n}b
. Hence, if a SUSY PVA
\mathcal{V}
is generated by a set
\mathcal{B}
as a differential algebra, then the
\chi
-bracket on
\mathcal{B}
completely determines the
\chi
-bracket on
\mathcal{V}
by the Leibniz rule and the sesquilinearity. Moreover, using the following theorem, one can derive a SUSY PVA from a given SUSY LCA.

Theorem 2.10
.

[
5
]

Let
\mathcal{R}
be a SUSY LCA . Then the supersymmetric algebra

S(\mathcal{R}):=S(\mathcal{R}_{\bar{0}})\otimes\bigwedge(\mathcal{R}_{\bar{1}})

endowed with the
\chi
-bracket induced from the
\chi
-bracket of
\mathcal{R}
and the Leibniz rule is a SUSY PVA.

In addition, the following theorem is useful to check axioms of SUSY LCAs.

Theorem 2.11
.

[
5
]

Let
V
be a vector superspace with a basis
\mathcal{B}=\{v_{1},\cdots,v_{m}\}
and let
\mathcal{R}=\mathbb{C}[D]\otimes V

\mathbb{C}[D]

[\ {}_{\chi}\ ]

[\ {}_{\chi}\ ]
.

Example 2.12
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a finite simple Lie superalgebra with a supersymmetric invariant bilinear form
(\ |\ )
. Consider the vector superspace
\bar{\mathfrak{g}}:=\{\bar{a}|a\in\mathfrak{g}\}

(2.31)

By Theorem

associated with

\ \ \mathfrak{g}=\bigoplus_{i\in\frac{\mathbb{Z}}{2}}\mathfrak{g}_{i},

3.2.
\chi
-adjoint action

Let
\mathcal{P}
be a SUSY PVA with an odd derivation
D
.
We consider a
\mathbb{C}(\!(D^{-1})\!)
-module
\mathcal{P}(\!(D^{-1})\!)
such that

\ \ D\big(aD^{n}\big)=a^{\prime}D^{n}+(-1)^{p(a)}aD^{n+1}

and

\displaystyle\ \ D^{-1}\big(aD^{n}\big)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{p(a)}aD^{n-1}+a^{\prime}D^{n-2}-(-1)^{p(a)}a^{(2)}D^{n-3}-a^{(3)}D^{n-4}+\cdots

\displaystyle=\sum_{m\geq 0}(-1)^{m}\left((-1)^{p(a)}a^{(2m)}D^{-1-2m}+a^{(2m+1)}D^{-2-2m}\right)D^{n}

where
n\in\mathbb{Z}
,
a\in\mathcal{P}
,
a^{\prime}:=D(a)
and
a^{(m)}=D^{m}(a)
for
m\geq 0
.

Definition 3.4
.

The
\chi
-adjoint action
of
\mathcal{P}
in
\mathcal{P}(\!(D^{-1})\!)
is defined by

(3.5)

\ \ \text{ad}_{\chi}P:\mathcal{P}(\!(D^{-1})\!)\to\mathbb{C}[\![\chi]\!]\otimes\mathcal{P}(\!(D^{-1})\!),\quad\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}a_{n}D^{n}\mapsto\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\{P{}_{\chi}a_{n}\}D^{n}

for
P\in\mathcal{P}
.

Note that we also consider
\mathbb{C}[\![\chi]\!]\otimes\mathcal{P}(\!(D^{-1})\!)
as a
\mathbb{C}(\!(D^{-1})\!)
-module via

D\chi=-\chi D-2\chi^{2},\quad D^{-1}\chi=-\chi D^{-1}-2\chi^{2}D^{-2}.

Since

\left[\sum_{n\geq 0}\chi^{n}D^{-n-1}\right](\chi+D)=(\chi+D)\left[\sum_{n\geq 0}\chi^{n}D^{-n-1}\right]
is the identity on
\mathbb{C}[\![\chi]\!]\otimes\mathcal{P}(\!(D^{-1})\!)
, we denote

(\chi+D)^{-1}:=\sum_{n\geq 0}\chi^{n}D^{-n-1}.

Lemma 3.5
.

For any
P\in\mathcal{P}
and
A(D)\in\mathcal{P}(\!(D^{-1})\!)
, we have

(1)

\textup{ad}_{\chi}P\,\big(D\,A(D)\big)=(-1)^{p(P)+1}(\chi+D)\textup{ad}_{\chi}P\,\big(A(D)\big),

(2)

\textup{ad}_{\chi}P\,\big(D^{-1}A(D)\big)=(-1)^{p(P)+1}(\chi+D)^{-1}\textup{ad}_{\chi}P\,\big(A(D)\big).

Proof.

The equation (1) is a consequence of sesquilinearity in a SUSY PVA. Apply (1) to
D^{-1}A(D)\in\mathcal{P}(\!(D^{-1})\!)
. Then

(\chi+D)\,\text{ad}_{\chi}P\,\big(D^{-1}A(D)\big)=(-1)^{p(P)+1}\text{ad}_{\chi}P\,\big(DD^{-1}A(D)\big)=(-1)^{p(P)+1}\text{ad}_{\chi}P\,\big(A(D)\big).

Since
(\chi+D)^{-1}
is the left inverse of
(\chi+D)
, we get the equality (2).
∎

Proposition 3.6
.

For
P\in\mathcal{P}
and
A(D),B(D)\in\mathcal{P}(\!(D^{-1})\!)
, we have the following property:

(3.6)

\displaystyle\textup{ad}_{\chi}P\,\big(A(D)B(D)\big)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{p(A(D))(p(P)+1)}A(D+\chi)\ \textup{ad}_{\chi}P\,\big(B(D)\big)+\textup{ad}_{\chi}P\,\big(A(D)\big)B(D).

Proof.

Let
a,b\in\mathcal{P}
and
m,n\in\mathbb{Z}
. It is enough to show (
3.6
) for
A(D)=aD^{m}
and
B(D)=bD^{n}
. If
m=0
, it directly follows from the Leibniz rule of
\chi
-bracket on
\mathcal{P}
.
If
m=1
, then

(3.7)

\displaystyle\text{ad}_{\chi}P\,\big(aD\,bD^{n}\big)=\text{ad}_{\chi}P\,(ab^{\prime}D^{n}+(-1)^{p(b)}abD^{n+1})

\displaystyle=\text{ad}_{\chi}P\,(a)\,(b^{\prime}D^{n}+(-1)^{p(b)}bD^{n+1})

\displaystyle+(-1)^{p(a)(p(P)+1)}a\big(\text{ad}_{\chi}P\,(b^{\prime})\,D^{n}+(-1)^{p(b)}\text{ad}_{\chi}P\,(b)\,D^{n+1}\big)

\displaystyle=\text{ad}_{\chi}P\,(aD)\,bD^{n}+(-1)^{(p(a)+1)(p(P)+1)}a(D+\chi)\text{ad}_{\chi}P\,(bD^{n}).

Inductively, one can prove the proposition for
m\geq 1
.
If
m<0
, similar arguments work by Lemma
3.5
.
∎

Corollary 3.7
.

Let
P\in\mathcal{P}
and
A_{1}(D),A_{2}(D),\cdots,A_{l}(D)\in\mathcal{P}(\!(D^{-1})\!)
. Then

\displaystyle\textup{ad}_{\chi}P\,\big(A_{1}(D)A_{2}(D)\cdots A_{l}(D)\big)

\displaystyle=\ \ \sum_{i=1}^{l}(-1)^{\sum_{k=1}^{i-1}p(A_{k}(D))\left(p(P)+1\right)}\left[\prod_{j=1}^{i-1}\left(A_{j}(D+\chi)\right)\textup{ad}_{\chi}P(A_{i}(D))\prod_{j^{\prime}=i+1}^{l}\left(A_{j^{\prime}}(D)\right)\right].

Recall the notations in Theorem-Definition
3.1
and Remark
3.3
. Consider the differential algebra homomorphism

\pi:\mathcal{V}(\bar{\mathfrak{g}})(\!(D^{-1})\!)\to\mathcal{V}(\bar{\mathfrak{p}})(\!(D^{-1})\!)

defined by
\pi(\bar{a}D^{n})=\bar{a}D^{n}
for
a\in\mathfrak{p}
and
\pi(\bar{a}D^{n})=(f|a)D^{n}
for
a\in\mathfrak{n}
. One can canonically extend the map

is used to find generators of

5.1.
\mathcal{W}(\overline{\mathfrak{osp}}(2n+1|2n),f)

Let
\mathfrak{gl}:=\mathfrak{gl}(2n+1|2n)
and
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{osp}(2n+1|2n)
.
Consider the automorphism
\check{\theta}=-\theta
of
\mathfrak{gl}
, built from the automorphism
\theta
in (

As vector superspaces, we have

(5.1)

\mathfrak{gl}=\mathfrak{gl}^{\theta}\oplus\mathfrak{gl}^{\check{\theta}}=\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{gl}^{\check{\theta}},

where
\mathfrak{gl}^{\check{\theta}}
is spanned by
\mathbb{e}_{ij}+\check{\theta}(\mathbb{e}_{ij})
for
i,j\in I
. By direct computations, one can check the following lemma.

Lemma 5.1
.

(1)
[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{gl}^{\check{\theta}}]\subset\mathfrak{gl}^{\check{\theta}}
(2)
[\mathfrak{gl}^{\check{\theta}},\mathfrak{gl}^{\check{\theta}}]\subset\mathfrak{g}
.

Consider the
\mathbb{C}[D]
-module

(5.2)

R=(\mathbb{C}[D]\otimes\overline{\mathfrak{g}})\oplus(\mathbb{C}[D]\otimes\overline{\mathfrak{gl}}^{\check{\theta}})^{\otimes 2}.

By Lemma
5.1
, the supersymmetric algebra
S(R)
is a SUSY PVA
endowed with the
\chi
-bracket induced from the bracket of SUSY affine PVA
\mathcal{V}(\overline{\mathfrak{gl}})
:

[\bar{a}{}_{\chi}\bar{b}]=(-1)^{p(a)p(b)+p(a)}\overline{[a,b]}+\chi\,(a|b).

Then as
\mathbb{C}[D]
-modules,

(5.3)

S(\mathbb{C}[D]\otimes\overline{\mathfrak{gl}})=S(R)\oplus(\mathbb{C}[D]\otimes\overline{\mathfrak{gl}}^{\check{\theta}})S(R).

Consider the composition

(5.4)

\kappa:S(\mathbb{C}[D]\otimes\overline{\mathfrak{gl}})\xrightarrow{\pi_{1}}S(R)\xrightarrow{\psi}S(R)/\mathcal{M}\simeq S(\mathbb{C}[D]\otimes\bar{\mathfrak{g}})

of the projection map
\pi_{1}
via (
5.3
) and the canonical quotient map
\psi

where
\mathcal{M}
is the differential algebra ideal generated by

\ (\mathbb{C}[D]\otimes\overline{\mathfrak{gl}}^{\check{\theta}})^{\otimes 2}.

Since
\mathcal{M}
is also a SUSY PVA ideal of
S(R)
, the map
\psi
is a SUSY PVA homomorphism.

Lemma 5.2
.

Consider the matrix

(5.5)

\ \ \mathcal{A}:=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\leq j\\
\ i,j\in I\end{subarray}}\mathbb{e}_{ij}\otimes\left(\delta_{ij}D+\bar{\mathbb{e}}^{ji}\right)-f\otimes 1\in\mathfrak{gl}\otimes\mathcal{V}(\overline{\mathfrak{gl}})[D].

If
rdet(\mathcal{A})=\sum_{i=0}^{4n+1}w_{4n+1-i}D^{i}
for
w_{1},w_{2},\cdots,w_{4n+1}\in\mathcal{V}(\overline{\mathfrak{gl}}_{\leq 0})
, then
w_{1},w_{2},\cdots,w_{4n+1}
freely generate
\mathcal{W}(\overline{\mathfrak{gl}},f)
.

Proof.

The row determinant of the matrix
\mathcal{A}
is

(5.6)

rdet(\mathcal{A}):=\sum_{N=0}^{4n}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0=i_{0}<i_{1}<\cdots\\
\cdots<i_{N+1}=4n+1\end{subarray}}(-1)^{n+\sum_{j\in J_{N}}\delta_{j}}\mathcal{A}_{i_{0}+1,i_{1}}\mathcal{A}_{i_{1}+1,i_{2}}\cdots\mathcal{A}_{i_{N}+1,i_{N+1}},

where
J_{N}=\{i_{1},i_{2},\cdots,i_{N+1}\}
,
\mathcal{A}_{ij}=\delta_{ij}D+\bar{\mathbb{e}}^{ji}
for
j\geq i
and
\mathbb{e}^{ji}=(-1)^{j+1}\mathbb{e}_{ji}
. Let
\mathfrak{J}
be the differential algebra ideal of
\mathcal{V}(\overline{\mathfrak{gl}})
generated by
\bar{n}-(f|n)
for
n\in\mathfrak{gl}_{>0}
.
Observe that

\displaystyle\text{ad}_{\chi}^{\mathfrak{J}}\,\mathbb{\bar{e}}_{i,i+1}(\mathcal{A}_{ik})=\mathcal{A}_{i+1,k},\quad\text{ad}_{\chi}^{\mathfrak{J}}\,\mathbb{\bar{e}}_{i,i+1}(\mathcal{A}_{ii}\mathcal{A}_{i+1,k})=-(-1)^{\delta_{i}}\mathcal{A}_{i+1,k}\ \text{ for }\ k>i+1,

\displaystyle\text{ad}_{\chi}^{\mathfrak{J}}\,\mathbb{\bar{e}}_{i,i+1}(\mathcal{A}_{l,i+1})=(-1)^{l+i+1}\mathcal{A}_{li},\quad\text{ad}_{\chi}^{\mathfrak{J}}\,\mathbb{\bar{e}}_{i,i+1}(\mathcal{A}_{li}\mathcal{A}_{i+1,i+1})=(-1)^{l+i+\delta_{i}}\mathcal{A}_{li}\ \text{ for }\ l<i.

In addition,

\displaystyle\text{ad}_{\chi}^{\mathfrak{J}}\,\mathbb{\bar{e}}_{i,i+1}(\mathcal{A}_{i,i+1})=(-1)^{i}\bar{\mathbb{e}}_{ii}+(-1)^{i}\bar{\mathbb{e}}_{i+1,i+1}-\chi,

\displaystyle\text{ad}_{\chi}^{\mathfrak{J}}\,\mathbb{\bar{e}}_{i,i+1}(\mathcal{A}_{i,i}\mathcal{A}_{i+1,i+1})=-(-1)^{i+\delta_{i}}\bar{\mathbb{e}}_{ii}-(-1)^{i+\delta_{i}}\bar{\mathbb{e}}_{i+1,i+1}+(-1)^{\delta_{i}}\chi.

Finally, one can check
\text{ad}_{\chi}^{\mathfrak{J}}\,\mathbb{\bar{e}}_{i,i+1}(rdet{\mathcal{A}})=0
so that
w_{m}\in\mathcal{W}(\overline{\mathfrak{gl}},f)
for any
m\in I
.
Furthermore, one can find a basis
\{v_{1},v_{2}\cdots,v_{4n+1}\}
of
\mathfrak{gl}^{f}
such that
\Delta_{\bar{v}_{i}}=\frac{i}{2}
. Hence, by checking the conditions of Proposition
3.2
, one can show that
w_{1},w_{2},\cdots,w_{4n+1}
freely generate
\mathcal{W}(\overline{\mathfrak{gl}},f)
.
∎

\displaystyle\tiny\left(\begin{array}[]{ccccccccc}D+\bar{E}_{11}&\bar{E}_{21}&\cdots&\bar{E}_{n+1\,1}&\bar{E}_{n+2\,1}&\cdots&\bar{E}_{4n\,1}&\bar{E}_{4n+1\,1}\\

Corollary 5.4
.

Let
\{F^{ij}\}_{(i,j)\in\mathcal{C}}
be the basis of
\mathfrak{p}
such that
(F^{ij}|F_{kl})=\delta_{jk}\delta_{il}
and
let

q_{\mathfrak{osp}}^{(2n+1|2n)}=\sum_{(i,j)\in\mathcal{C}}F_{ji}\otimes\bar{F}^{ij}\in\mathfrak{p}_{+}\otimes\mathcal{V}(\bar{\mathfrak{p}}).

Then

\mathcal{A}_{\mathfrak{osp}}^{(2n+1|2n)}=\sum_{i\in I}\mathbb{e}_{ii}D+q_{\mathfrak{osp}}^{(2n+1|2n)}-f\otimes 1\in\mathfrak{gl}\otimes\mathcal{V}(\bar{\mathfrak{p}})[D].

Moreover, if we denote
rdet(\mathcal{A}_{\mathfrak{osp}}^{(2n+1|2n)})=\sum_{i=0}^{4n+1}w_{4n+1-i}D^{i}
for

w_{1},w_{2},\cdots,w_{4n+1}\in\mathcal{W}(\bar{\mathfrak{g}},f).

Proof.

Note that

F^{ij}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\ E_{ij}&\text{ if }(i,j)\in\mathcal{C}\text{ and }i+j\neq 4n+2,\\
\frac{1}{2}E_{ij},&\text{ if }(i,j)\in\mathcal{C}\text{ and }i+j=4n+2,\end{array}\right.

and

Hence, for
(i,j)\in\mathcal{C}

\kappa(\bar{\mathbb{e}}^{ji})=\bar{E}^{ji}

\ \ q_{\mathfrak{osp}}^{(2n+1|2n)}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\leq j\\

(1)

\ (\mathrm{mod}\ 4)

equals to the dimension of
\mathfrak{g}_{0}
, that is,
\dim\mathfrak{g}^{f}=2n
. Furthermore, we have that for
-n<k\leq 0

(6.42)

\dim\mathfrak{g}_{k}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}k+2n+\frac{1}{2}&\text{ if }2k\text{ is odd, }\\

k+2n+1&\text{ if }2k\equiv 2\ (\mathrm{mod}\ 4).\end{array}\right.

Also for
-2n+1\leq k\leq-n
,

(6.43)

\dim\mathfrak{g}_{k}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}k+2n-\frac{1}{2}&\text{ if }2k\text{ is odd, }\\

k+2n&\text{ if }2k\equiv 2\ (\mathrm{mod}\ 4).\end{array}\right.

Hence, one can find a basis

(6.44)

V^{f}:=\big\{v_{l}\in\mathfrak{g}_{\frac{1-l}{2}}\,|\,1<l\leq 4n-1,\ l\equiv 0\text{ or }3\ (\mathrm{mod}\ 4)\big\}\cup\big\{\widetilde{v}_{2n}\in\mathfrak{g}_{\frac{1-2n}{2}}\big\}

of
\mathfrak{g}^{f}
, consisting of homogeneous elements. Explicitly,
v_{l}
and
\widetilde{v}_{2n}
can be written as follows.

Lemma 6.6
.

(1)

For an integer
l
such that
2n+1\leq l\leq 4n-1
, let

(6.45)

\displaystyle v_{l}:=

\displaystyle 2\sum_{k=1}^{4n-l}(-1)^{T_{2n}(k)+kl+k+l+1}E_{k^{\prime}\,4n+1-l-k}.

Then
v_{l}=0\text{ if }l\equiv 1\text{ or }2\ (\mathrm{mod}\ 4)
and
v_{l}\neq 0\text{ if }l\equiv 0\text{ or }3\ (\mathrm{mod}\ 4)
.

(2)

For an integer
l
such that
1<l\leq 2n
, let
t=2n-l-2
and

(6.46)

\displaystyle v_{l}:=

\displaystyle(-1)^{t}\left(E_{2n\,t+1}-E_{2n+1\,t+1}\right)+(-1)^{T_{2n}(t+1)+1}\left(E_{(t+1)^{\prime}\,(2n)^{\prime}}-E_{(t+1)^{\prime}\,2n}\right)

\displaystyle+\sum_{k=1}^{t}(-1)^{t(k+1)}E_{2n-1-t+k\,k}+\sum_{k=1}^{t}(-1)^{tk+1+T_{2n-1-t+k}(k)}E_{k^{\prime}\,(2n-1-t+k)^{\prime}}

\displaystyle+2\sum_{k=t+1}^{2n}(-1)^{T_{2n}(k)+kl+k+l}E_{k^{\prime}\,l+2-k}

Then
v_{l}=0\text{ if }l\equiv 1\text{ or }2\ (\mathrm{mod}\ 4)
and
v_{l}\neq 0\text{ if }l\equiv 0\text{ or }3\ (\mathrm{mod}\ 4)
.

(3)

Let
\tilde{v}_{2n}:=E_{2n\,1}-E_{2n+1\,1}
. Then for
v_{l}
’s in (
6.45
) and (
6.46
),

(6.47)

V^{f}:=\{v_{l}\,|\,1<l\leq 4n-1,\ l\equiv 0\text{ or }3\ (\mathrm{mod}\ 4)\}\cup\{\widetilde{v}_{2n}\}

forms a basis of
\mathfrak{g}^{f}
.

Proof.

(1) and (2) follow from direct computations. Let us show (3). Since
v_{l}
’s for
1<l\leq 4n-1
are in distinct homogeneous spaces with respect to the
\frac{\mathbb{Z}}{2}
-grading on
\mathfrak{g}
, the linear independency of
V^{f}
is clear. Also,
|V^{f}|=2n=\dim{\mathfrak{g}^{f}}
, so we only need to show that each
v_{l}\in V^{f}
and
\widetilde{v}_{2n}
are indeed in
\mathfrak{g}^{f}
. Again, by direct computations, we get
[f,v_{l}]=[f,\widetilde{v}_{2n}]=0
.
∎

Now we need to check if the elements of
V^{f}
satisfy the conditions of Proposition
3.2
. In particular, to check the condition (i), define a gradation
\Delta
on
\mathcal{V}(\bar{\mathfrak{p}})(\!(D^{-1})\!)
by

(6.48)

\Delta_{\bar{a}}=\frac{1}{2}-j_{a},\quad\Delta_{D}=\frac{1}{2},\quad\Delta_{D^{-1}}=-\frac{1}{2},\quad\Delta_{AB}=\Delta_{A}+\Delta_{B}

where

A,B\in\mathcal{V}(\bar{\mathfrak{p}})
. Note that on
\mathcal{V}(\bar{\mathfrak{p}})
,
\Delta
coincides with the gradation defined in (
3.4
).

Lemma 6.7
.

Recall the elements
w_{1},\cdots,w_{4n-1},\widetilde{w}_{2n}
of
\mathcal{W}(\bar{\mathfrak{g}},f)

6.5
and the elements
v_{l}
’s and

V^{f}
in Lemma
6.6
. Then we have

(1)

\Delta_{\bar{v}_{l}}=\frac{l}{2}
and
\Delta_{\overline{\widetilde{v}}_{2n}}=n
,

(2)

\Delta_{w_{l}}=\frac{l}{2}
for
l=1.\cdots,4n-1
and
\Delta_{\widetilde{w}_{2n}}=n
.

Proof.

For
i\in I
, let

(6.49)

\bar{\imath}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}i&\text{ if }1\leq{i}\leq{2n},\\
i-1&\text{ if }2n+1\leq{i}\leq{4n}.\end{array}\right.

Then the degree of

F_{ij}
is
\frac{\bar{\jmath}-\bar{\imath}}{2}
with respect to the
\frac{\mathbb{Z}}{2}
-grading of
\mathfrak{g}
, so
\Delta_{F_{ij}}=\frac{1-\bar{\jmath}+\bar{\imath}}{2}
. Hence, (1) follows. Now recall that
rdet\big(\mathcal{A}_{\mathfrak{osp}}^{(2n|2n)}\big)
can be computed as (
6.11
). Using Lemma
6.1

\Delta_{A_{k}}=\frac{k}{2}=\Delta_{B_{k}}

\Delta_{rdet\left(\mathcal{A}_{\mathfrak{osp}}^{(2n|2n)}\right)}=\frac{4n-1}{2}

. Since

, we also have
\Delta_{\widetilde{w}_{2n}}=n

∎

Theorem 6.8
.

As in (
6.32
), write

rdet(\mathcal{A}_{\mathfrak{osp}}^{(2n|2n)})=\sum_{l=0}^{4n-1}w_{4n-1-l}D^{l}+(-1)^{n}\widetilde{w}_{2n}D^{-1}\widetilde{w}_{2n}.

Then the set

\{w_{l}\,|\,1\leq l\leq 4n-1,i\equiv 0\text{ or }3\ (\mathrm{mod}\ 4)\}\cup\{\widetilde{w}_{2n}\}

freely generates
\mathcal{W}(\bar{\mathfrak{g}},f)

Proof.

We use Proposition
3.2
. By Lemma
6.6
and Lemma
6.7
,
it is enough to show that

w_{l}^{1}=\bar{v}_{l},\quad w_{2n}^{1}=\bar{\tilde{v}}_{2n}

where
w_{l}^{1}\in\bar{\mathfrak{g}}
and
w_{2n}^{1}
are the linear parts of
w_{l}
and
w_{2n}
which are not total derivatives.

For
0\leq l<2n-1
, the element

is in the coefficient of
D^{l}
of the following sum:

(6.50)

\ \ 2\sum_{j+k=l+2}(-1)^{T_{2n}(k)+k}D^{j-1}\bar{E}_{k^{\prime}\,j}D^{k-1},

where the sum is taken over

A simple computation shows that the linear part in (

\ \ A_{2n}D^{-1}B_{2n}+2\sum_{j+k=l+2}(-1)^{T_{2n}(k)+k}D^{j-1}\bar{E}_{k^{\prime}\,j}D^{k-1}

Finally, it is clear that
\overline{\tilde{v}}_{2n}=\widetilde{w}^{1}_{2n}

∎

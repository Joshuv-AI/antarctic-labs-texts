Second-Order Subdifferential Optimality Conditions in Nonsmooth Optimization
Pham Duy Khanh, Vu Vinh Huy Khoa, Boris S. Mordukhovich, Vo Thanh Phat

Abstract




Abstract.
The paper is devoted to deriving novel second-order necessary and sufficient optimality conditions for local minimizers in rather general classes of nonsmooth unconstrained and constrained optimization problems in finite-dimensional spaces. The established conditions are expressed in terms of second-order subdifferentials of lower semicontinuous functions and mainly concern prox-regular objectives that cover a large territory in nonsmooth optimization and its applications. Our tools are based on the machinery of variational analysis and second-order generalized differentiation. The obtained general results are applied to problems of nonlinear programming, where the derived second-order optimality conditions are new even for problems with twice continuously differential data, being expressed there in terms of the classical Hessian matrices.

Keywords.
variational analysis and nonsmooth optimization, second-order optimality conditions, second-order subdifferentials, variational convexity, nonlinear programming

Mathematics Subject Classification (2010).
49J52, 49J53, 90C30

Introduction

It has been well recognized in optimization and variational analysis that second-order optimality conditions (both necessary and sufficient) play a highly important role in qualitative aspects of optimization as well as in the design and justification of numerical algorithms. The derivation of such conditions in problems of
second-order nonsmooth optimization
(by which we understand those where at least one of the involved functions is not twice continuously differentiable) naturally requires appropriate notions of second-order generalized differentiation and the machinery of second-order variational analysis.

In this paper, we explore for these purposes
second-order subdifferentials
(or
generalized Hessians
) of lower semicontinuous (l.s.c.) functions initiated by Mordukhovich
[
20
]
in the general extended-real-valued framework. These constructions have been largely studied and utilized in numerous aspects of variational analysis, variational stability, optimization, control, and various applications; see, e.g., the books
[
22
,
23
,
24
]
and the references therein, where the reader can find, in particular, comprehensive calculus rules and explicit computations of second-order subdifferentials and related constructions for broad classes of extended-real-valued functions that overwhelmingly arise in optimization.

A prominent role of second-order subdifferentials was revealed in the study of
tilt-stable local minimizers
, the notion introduced by Poliquin and Rockafellar
[
35
]
in the framework of unconstrained extended-real-valued optimization, where the basic second-order subdifferential of
[
20
]
was employed to establish
pointbased
(i.e., formulated
at
the point in question)
characterizations
of tilt-stable minimizers for the large class of prox-regular and subdifferentially continuous functions. Using second-order subdifferential calculus rules and other techniques of second-order variational analysis, both pointbased and
neighborhood
(i.e., involving points
nearby
the reference one) characterizations of tilt-stable local minimizers were established for various classes of problems in constrained optimization; see
[
3
,
9
,
11
,
18
,
24
,
26
,
27
,
29
,
30
,
31
,
33
,
40
]
among many publications in this direction. Furthermore, the obtained characterizations of tilt stability laid the foundation for the design and justification of generalized Newtonian methods to solve nonsmooth optimization and related problems by using second-order subdifferentials; see the recent developments in
[
14
,
16
,
17
,
24
,
32
]
.

Besides applications to tilt-stable minimizers, second-order subdifferentials have been instrumental to study arbitrary local minimizers in some special classes of nonsmooth optimization problems. Using a symmetric version of the basic second-order subdifferential, Huy and Tuyen
[
12
]
derived pointbased no-gap necessary and sufficient conditions for local solutions to unconstrained problems of minimizing objectives of class
{\cal C}^{1,1}
, i.e., continuously differentiable functions with locally Lipschitzian gradients. Neighborhood conditions in terms of some modifications of the basic second-order subdifferential were obtained by Chieu et al.
[
7
]
for unconstrained problems with
{\cal C}^{1,1}
objectives and also for merely continuously differentiable (
{\cal C}^{1}
-smooth) functions under additional assumptions. Some extensions of these results to optimization problems with specific types of constraints were given in
[
1
,
2
]
.

The aim of this paper is to derive new second-order subdifferential necessary and sufficient conditions for local minimizers and its strong counterparts in various formats of nonsmooth unconstrained and constrained optimization. First we consider minimization problems written in the
unconstrained extended-real-valued
form. In contrast to the previous developments in
[
7
,
12
]
for intrinsically unconstrained problems with smooth data, we now address problems with extended-real-valued
prox-regular
objectives, which cover a much broader class of optimization problems and allow us to implicitly incorporate
geometric constraints
as in
[
1
,
2
]
via the domain sets of the corresponding objectives. For the general class of prox-regular functions without any additional assumptions, we establish a
second-order necessary optimality condition
expressed in the
pointbased
form via the so-called combined version of the basic second-order subdifferential; see below. To derive next its
no-gap neighborhood
counterpart as a
second-order sufficient optimality condition
, we first obtain a new
characterization
of
variational convexity
of extended-real-valued functions, the property of its own great importance as documented in
[
36
]
and
[
13
,
15
]
which yields the sufficiency for local minimizers. Then we consider nonsmooth optimization problems with
explicit functional-geometric constraints
including, in particular, problems of
conic programming
. The usage of second-order subdifferential calculus rules allows us to establish no-gap second-order necessary and sufficient optimality conditions under fairly mild constraint qualifications. Finally, the paper develops constructive applications of the obtained second-order subdifferential conditions to problems of
nonlinear programming
(NLP), where we arrive at new results even in the classical NLP framework with twice continuously differentiable (
{\cal C}^{2}
-smooth) data.

The remainder of the paper is structured as follows. Section
2
provides an overview of some key concepts in variational analysis and generalized differentiation, which are widely employed in the formulations and proofs of our major results. In Section
3
, we establish the equivalence between local minimizers of a given l.s.c. function and local minimizers of its Moreau envelope. The main result of Section
4
presents a novel necessary second-order subdifferential condition for local minimizers of extended-real-valued prox-regular functions. Section
5
provides a coderivative characterization of variational convexity for l.s.c. functions and applies it to deriving a second-order subdifferential sufficient optimality condition. In Section
6
, we obtain counterparts of the above second-order optimality conditions for the case of strong minimizers. Sections
7
and
8
are devoted, respectively, to establishing second-order subdifferential optimality conditions for local and strong local minimizers of problems with functional-geometric constraints. Their further specifications and applications to nonlinear programming are given in Section
9
. Finally, Section
10
serves as a conclusion with summarizing the major findings of the paper and listing significant topics of our future research.

2
Preliminaries from Variational Analysis

In this section, we review some background information from variational analysis and first-order generalized differentiation in finite-dimensional spaces used in what follows. Proofs and related material can be found in the books
[
23
,
38
]
and the references therein. Recall that
B_{\varepsilon}(\bar{x})
and
\mathbb{B}_{\varepsilon}(\bar{x})
stand for the open and closed balls around
\bar{x}
with radius
\varepsilon>0
, respectively, and that
{\rm I\!N}:=\{1,2,\ldots\}
.

Let
F\colon{\rm I\!R}^{n}\rightrightarrows{\rm I\!R}^{m}
be a set-valued mapping/multifunction between finite-dimensional Euclidean spaces with its
graph
and
kernel
defined, respectively, by

\text{gph}\,F:=\big\{(x,y)\in{\rm I\!R}^{n}\times{\rm I\!R}^{m}\;\big|\;y\in F(x)\big\}\;\mbox{ and }\;\mbox{\rm ker}\,F:=\big\{x\in{\rm I\!R}^{n}\;\big|\;0\in F(x)\big\}.

The
inverse
mapping
F^{-1}\colon{\rm I\!R}^{m}\rightrightarrows{\rm I\!R}^{n}
of
F
is
F^{-1}(y):=\{x\in{\rm I\!R}^{n}\;|\;y\in F(x)\big\}
, and the
(Painlevé-Kuratowski)
outer limit
of
F
as
x\rightarrow\bar{x}
is given by

\underset{x\rightarrow\bar{x}}{\mathop{{\rm Lim}\,{\rm sup}}}\;F(x):=\big\{y\in{\rm I\!R}^{n}\;\big|\,\exists\mbox{ sequences }x_{k}\to\bar{x},\ y_{k}\rightarrow y\;\mbox{with }\;y_{k}\in F(x_{k}),\;k\in{\rm I\!N}\big\}.

(2.1)

Let
\varphi:{\rm I\!R}^{n}\rightarrow\overline{{\rm I\!R}}:=(-\infty,\infty]
be a proper extended-real-valued function with the domain
\mbox{\rm dom}\,\varphi:=\{x\in{\rm I\!R}^{n}\;|\;\varphi(x)<\infty\}\neq\emptyset
. For
x\in\mbox{\rm dom}\,\varphi
, define the (Fréchet)
regular subdifferential
of
\varphi
at
x
by

\widehat{\partial}\varphi(x):=\Big\{v\in{\rm I\!R}^{n}\;\Big|\liminf_{u\to x}\;\frac{\varphi(u)-\varphi(x)-\langle v,u-x\rangle}{\|u-x\|}\geq 0\Big\}

(2.2)

with
\widehat{\partial}\varphi(x):=\emptyset
at
x\notin\mbox{\rm dom}\,\varphi
, and the (Mordukhovich)
limiting subdifferential
of
\varphi
at
\bar{x}\in\mbox{\rm dom}\,\varphi
by

\partial\varphi(\bar{x}):=\underset{x\overset{\varphi}{\to}\bar{x}}{\mathop{{\rm Lim}\,{\rm sup}}}\;\widehat{\partial}\varphi(x),

(2.3)

where
x\overset{\varphi}{\to}\bar{x}
means that
x\rightarrow\bar{x}
with
\varphi(x)\rightarrow\varphi(\bar{x})
. Both regular and limiting subdifferentials of
\varphi
reduce to the classical gradient
\nabla\varphi(\bar{x})
of
{\cal C}^{1}
-smooth functions and to the classical subdifferential of convex analysis if
\varphi
is convex.

Given further a set
\Omega\subset{\rm I\!R}^{n}
with its indicator function
\delta_{\Omega}(x)
equal to
0
for
x\in\Omega
and to
\infty
otherwise, the regular and the limiting
normal cones
to
\Omega
at
\bar{x}\in\Omega
are defined via the corresponding subdifferentials (
2.2
) and (
2.3
) by, respectively,

\widehat{N}_{\Omega}(\bar{x}):=\widehat{\partial}\delta_{\Omega}(\bar{x})\hskip 10.00002pt\text{and}\hskip 10.00002ptN_{\Omega}(\bar{x}):=\partial\delta_{\Omega}(\bar{x}).

(2.4)

The (Bouligand-Severi)
tangent/contingent cone
to
\Omega
at
\bar{x}
is

T_{\Omega}(\bar{x}):=\big\{w\in{\rm I\!R}^{n}\;\big|\;\exists\,t_{k}\downarrow 0,\;w_{k}\to w\;\mbox{ as }\;k\to\infty\;\mbox{ with }\;\bar{x}+t_{k}w_{k}\in\Omega\big\}.

(2.5)

Note the duality correspondence
\widehat{N}_{\Omega}(\bar{x})=T_{\Omega}(\bar{x})^{*}
between the regular normal and tangent cones. Observe to this end that the limiting normal cone in (
2.4
) cannot be tangentially generated due to its intrinsic nonconvexity.
The
critical cone
to
\Omega
at
(\bar{x},\bar{v})\in\mbox{\rm gph}\,{N}_{\Omega}
is defined by

K_{\Omega}(\bar{x},\bar{v}):=T_{\Omega}(\bar{x})\cap\{\bar{v}\}^{\perp}.

(2.6)

For a set-valued mapping
F\colon{\rm I\!R}^{n}\rightrightarrows{\rm I\!R}^{m}
, the regular and limiting
coderivatives
of
F
at
(\bar{x},\bar{y})\in\text{gph}\,F
are defined via corresponding normal cones (
2.4
) by, respectively,

\widehat{D}^{*}F(\bar{x},\bar{y})(u):=\big\{v\in{{\rm I\!R}^{n}}\;\big|\;({v},-{u})\in\widehat{N}_{\text{gph}F}(\bar{x},\bar{y})\big\},

(2.7)

D^{*}F(\bar{x},\bar{y})(u):=\big\{v\in{{\rm I\!R}^{n}}\;\big|\;({v},-{u})\in{N}_{\text{gph}F}\,(\bar{x},\bar{y})\big\}

(2.8)

for all
u\in{\rm I\!R}^{m}
. We omit
\bar{y}
in (
2.7
) and (
2.8
) if
F
is single-valued. Let us mention that, despite their nonconvexity, the limiting constructions in (
2.3
), (
2.4
), and (
2.8
) enjoy comprehensive
calculus rules
based on
variational/extremal principles
of variational analysis. On the other hand, the regular constructions in (
2.2
), (
2.4
), and (
2.7
), which are usually easier to compute, can be viewed by (
2.1
) as efficient approximation tools to deal with the limiting ones.

Next we formulate the
metric regularity
and
subregularity
properties of multifunctions that are highly recognized in variational
analysis and optimization. These properties are frequently used in what follows. To proceed, recall that the distance function associated with a set
\Omega\subset{\rm I\!R}^{n}
is

{\rm dist}(x;\Omega):=\inf\big\{\|w-x\|\;\big|\;w\in\Omega\big\},\hskip 10.00002ptx\in{\rm I\!R}^{n}.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
F\colon{\rm I\!R}^{n}\rightrightarrows{\rm I\!R}^{m}
be a set-valued mapping, and let
(\bar{x},\bar{y})\in\mbox{\rm gph}\,F
. We say that:

(i)

F
is
metrically regular
around
(\bar{x},\bar{y})
with modulus
\mu>0
if there exist neighborhoods
U
of
\bar{x}

and
V
of
\bar{y}
ensuring that

{\rm dist}\big(x;F^{-1}(y)\big)\leq\mu\,{\rm dist}\big(y;F(x)\big)\;\text{ for all }\;(x,y)\in U\times V.

(ii)

F
is
metrically subregular
at
(\bar{x},\bar{y})

with modulus
\mu>0
if there exists a neighborhood
U
of
\bar{x}
such that we have the estimate

{\rm dist}\big(x;F^{-1}(\bar{y})\big)\leq\mu\,{\rm dist}\big(\bar{y};F(x)\big)\;\text{ for all }\;x\in U.

A serious advantage of the limiting coderivative (
2.8
) is that it provides the following complete pointbased characterization of the metric regularity of any closed-graph multifunction
F\colon{\rm I\!R}^{n}\rightrightarrows{\rm I\!R}^{m}
around
(\bar{x},\bar{y})
known as the
Mordukhovich coderivative criterion

[
21
,
38
]
:

\mbox{\rm ker}\,D^{*}F(\bar{x},\bar{y})=\{0\}.

(2.9)

The following notions were introduced by Poliquin and Rockafellar
[
34
]
.

Definition 2.2
.

A function
\varphi:{\rm I\!R}^{n}\to\overline{{\rm I\!R}}
is said to be
prox-regular
at
\bar{x}
for
\bar{v}\in\partial\varphi(\bar{x})
if
\varphi
is finite and locally l.s.c. around
\bar{x}
, and if there exist numbers
\varepsilon>0
and
r\geq 0
such that

\varphi(x)\geq\varphi(u)+\langle v,x-u\rangle-\frac{r}{2}\|x-u\|^{2}\hskip 10.00002pt\text{for all}\hskip 10.00002ptx\in B_{\varepsilon}(\bar{x})\;\mbox{ and }\;v\in\partial\varphi(x)

(2.10)

with
\|v-\bar{v}\|<\varepsilon
,
\|u-\bar{x}\|<\varepsilon
, and
\varphi(u)<\varphi(\bar{x})+\varepsilon
.
The function
\varphi
is said to be
subdifferentially continuous
at
\bar{x}
for
\bar{v}
if
\bar{v}\in\partial\varphi(\bar{x})
and, whenever
(x_{k},v_{k})\to(\bar{x},\bar{v})
as
k\to\infty
and
v_{k}\in\partial\varphi(x_{k})
for all
k\in{\rm I\!N}
, we have
\varphi(x_{k})\to\varphi(\bar{x})
as
k\to\infty
.

If
\varphi
is subdifferentially continuous at
\bar{x}
for
\bar{v}
, the inequality “
\varphi(u)<\varphi(\bar{x})+\varepsilon
” in the definition of prox-regularity above can be omitted. Functions that are both prox-regular and subdifferentially continuous are called
continuously prox-regular
. As discussed in
[
38
]
, the class of continuously prox-regular functions is fairly broad containing, besides
\mathcal{C}^{2}
-smooth and l.s.c. convex functions, also functions of class
\mathcal{C}^{1,1}
, lower-
\mathcal{C}^{2}
functions, strongly amenable functions, etc. It has been recognized that continuously prox-regular functions play a central role in second-order variational analysis and its applications; see the books
[
38
]
and
[
24
]
with the commentaries and references therein. Nevertheless, our major results obtained in this paper show that the subdifferential continuity assumption can be generally dropped in the second-order necessary and sufficient conditions for local and strong local minimizers.

3
Local Minimizers via Moreau Envelopes

In this section, we verify the preservation of local minimizers of l.s.c. functions under taking the Moreau envelope associated with the function in question. Besides being important for their own sake, such a preservation occurs to be useful in our subsequent second-order derivations.

For a proper l.s.c. function
\varphi:{\rm I\!R}^{n}\to\overline{{\rm I\!R}}
and a parameter value
\lambda>0
, the
Moreau envelope

e_{\lambda}\varphi
and
proximal mapping

\text{\rm Prox}_{\lambda\varphi}
are defined for all
x\in{\rm I\!R}^{n}
as follows:

e_{\lambda}\varphi(x):=\inf\Big\{\varphi(y)+\frac{1}{2\lambda}\|y-x\|^{2}\;\Big|\;y\in{\rm I\!R}^{n}\Big\},

(3.1)

\operatorname{Prox}_{\lambda\varphi}(x):=\operatorname{argmin}\Big\{\varphi(y)+\frac{1}{2\lambda}\|y-x\|^{2}\;\Big|\;y\in{\rm I\!R}^{n}\Big\}.

(3.2)

The function
\varphi
is called
prox-bounded
if there exists
\lambda>0
such that
e_{\lambda}\varphi(x)>-\infty
for some
x\in{\rm I\!R}^{n}
. Equivalent descriptions of prox-boundedness are given in
[
38
, Exercise 1.24]
. To present now the important properties of Moreau envelopes and proximal mappings, which are taken from
[
38
, Proposition 13.37]
, we first recall that the
\varphi
-attentive
\varepsilon
-localization
of the subgradient mapping
\partial\varphi
around
(\bar{x},\bar{v})\in\mbox{\rm gph}\,\partial\varphi
is the set-valued mapping
T\colon{\rm I\!R}^{n}\rightrightarrows{\rm I\!R}^{n}
defined by

T(x):=\begin{cases}\big\{v\in\partial\varphi(x)\;\big|\;\|v-\bar{v}\|<\varepsilon\big\}&\text{if}\quad\|x-\bar{x}\|<\varepsilon\;\text{ and }\;|\varphi(x)-\varphi(\bar{x})|<\varepsilon,\\
\emptyset&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

(3.3)

where we skip indicating the subscript “
\varepsilon
” for notation simplicity. If
\varphi
is an l.s.c. function, the above localization can be taken with just
\varphi(x)<\varphi(\bar{x})+\varepsilon
in (
3.3
). Here is the aforementioned result.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
\varphi\colon{\rm I\!R}^{n}\to\overline{{\rm I\!R}}
be an l.s.c. and prox-bounded function that is prox-regular at
\bar{x}
for
\bar{v}\in\partial\varphi(\bar{x})
with respect to a radius
\varepsilon>0
. Then for all
\lambda>0
sufficiently small, we find a convex neighborhood
U_{\lambda}
of
\bar{x}+\lambda\bar{v}
on which the following properties hold:

(i)
The Moreau envelope
e_{\lambda}\varphi
from (
3.1
) is of class
{\cal C}^{1,1}
on the set
U_{\lambda}
.

(ii)
The proximal mapping
\text{\rm Prox}_{\lambda\varphi}
from (
3.2
) is single-valued, monotone, and Lipschitz continuous on
U_{\lambda}
satisfying there the condition
{\rm Prox}_{\lambda\varphi}(x)=(I+\lambda T)^{-1}(x)
, where
T
is the
\varphi
-attentive
\varepsilon
-localization of
\partial\varphi
from (
3.3
). In particular,
{\rm Prox}_{\lambda\varphi}(\bar{x}+\lambda\bar{v})=\{\bar{x}\}
.

(iii)
The gradient of
e_{\lambda}\varphi
is calculated by

\nabla e_{\lambda}\varphi(x)=\frac{1}{\lambda}\big(x-\text{\rm Prox}_{\lambda\varphi}(x)\big)=\big(\lambda I+T^{-1}\big)^{-1}(x)\;\mbox{ for all }\;x\in U_{\lambda}.

(3.4)

If
\varphi
is subdifferentially continuous at
\bar{x}
for
\bar{v}
, then
T
in (
3.4
) can be replaced by
\partial\varphi
.

It follows from Proposition
3.1
that
\bar{x}
is a stationary point of the Moreau envelope
e_{\lambda}\varphi
if and only if
\bar{x}
is a stationary point of
\varphi
in the sense that
0\in\partial\varphi(\bar{x})
, which is known as the
limiting/M
(
ordukhovich
)
-stationarity
. Recall that
\bar{x}\in\mbox{\rm dom}\,\varphi
is called a
local minimizer
to
\varphi:{\rm I\!R}^{n}\rightarrow\overline{{\rm I\!R}}
if there is a neighborhood
U
of
\bar{x}
such that

\varphi(x)\geq\varphi(\bar{x})\ \text{ for all }\ x\in U.

The natural question arises on whether we have the
local minima equivalence

\bar{x}\;\text{is a local minimizer of }\;\varphi\iff\;\bar{x}\;\text{is a local minimizer of }e_{\lambda}\varphi.

(3.5)

The next theorem shows that the above equivalence holds. Recall that
\bar{v}\in{\rm I\!R}^{n}
is a
proximal subgradient
of
\varphi:{\rm I\!R}^{n}\to\overline{{\rm I\!R}}
at
\bar{x}\in\mbox{\rm dom}\,\varphi
, denoted as
\bar{v}\in\partial_{P}\varphi(\bar{x})
, if there are positive numbers
r
and
\varepsilon
for which

\varphi(x)\geq\varphi(\bar{x})+\langle\bar{v},x-\bar{x}\rangle-\frac{r}{2}\|x-\bar{x}\|^{2}\;\mbox{ whenever }\;x\in B_{\varepsilon}(\bar{x}).

It follows from Definition
2.2
that if
\varphi
is prox-regular at
\bar{x}
for
\bar{v}\in\partial\varphi(\bar{x})
, then
\bar{v}
is a proximal subgradient of
\varphi
. Note that the inverse statement is not true. Indeed, the function

\varphi(x):=\begin{cases}|x|\left(1+\sin\left(\frac{1}{x}\right)\right)&\text{if }\;x\neq 0,\\
0&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

taken from
[
38
, p. 618]
is not prox-regular at
\bar{x}
for
\bar{v}:=0
, but
\bar{v}
is a proximal subgradient of
\varphi
at
0
. Also, if
\bar{x}
is a local minimizer to
\varphi
, then

is a proximal subgradient of
\varphi
at
\bar{x}
.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
\varphi:{\rm I\!R}^{n}\to\overline{{\rm I\!R}}
be prox-bounded and l.s.c. around
\bar{x}\in\mbox{\rm dom}\,\varphi
. Then for all
\lambda>0
sufficiently small, the following are equivalent:

(i)

\bar{x}
is a local minimizer of
\varphi
.

(ii)

\bar{x}
is a local minimizer of
e_{\lambda}\varphi
.

[
Proof.]
To verify (i)
\Longrightarrow
(ii), assume that
\bar{x}
is a local minimizer of
\varphi
, i.e., there exists
\varepsilon>0
such that

\varphi(x)\geq\varphi(\bar{x})\hskip 10.00002pt\text{for all}\hskip 10.00002ptx\in\mathbb{B}_{\varepsilon}(\bar{x}),

(3.6)

which implies that
\bar{v}=0
is a proximal subgradient of
\varphi
at
\bar{x}
. We claim that
\text{Prox}_{\lambda\varphi}(\bar{x})=\{\bar{x}\}

\varphi(\bar{x})-(2\bar{\lambda})^{-1}\|x-\bar{x}\|^{2}<e_{\lambda^{\prime}}\varphi(\bar{x})-(2\lambda^{\prime})^{-1}\|x-\bar{x}\|^{2}\leq\varphi(x)

(3.7)

hold for all
x
with
\|x-\bar{x}\|>\varepsilon
. For any
0<\lambda<\bar{\lambda}
, it follows from (
3.6
) and (
3.7
) that

\varphi(x)+(2\lambda)^{-1}\|x-\bar{x}\|^{2}>\varphi(\bar{x})\;\mbox{ whenever }\;x\neq\bar{x},

which is equivalent to the condition
\mathrm{Prox}_{\lambda\varphi}(\bar{x})=\{\bar{x}\}
. We therefore have that
\text{Prox}_{\lambda\varphi}(\bar{x})=\{\bar{x}\}
for all
\lambda>0
sufficiently small. Fixing such a small

\bar{x}
is not a local minimizer of

\bar{x}

e_{\lambda}\varphi(x_{k})<e_{\lambda}\varphi(\bar{x})\text{ whenever }\;k\in{\rm I\!N}.

(3.8)

It follows from
[
38
, Theorem 1.25]
that
\text{\rm Prox}_{\lambda\varphi}(x_{k})\neq\emptyset
, which allows us to pick
w_{k}\in\text{\rm Prox}_{\lambda\varphi}(x_{k})
for all
k\in{\rm I\!N}
. Since

as

\text{Prox}_{\lambda\varphi}(\bar{x})=\{\bar{x}\}

Therefore,
w_{k}\in\mathbb{B}_{\varepsilon}(\bar{x})
for sufficiently large

, which leads us by (
3.6
) to the relationships

\displaystyle e_{\lambda}\varphi(\bar{x})\leq\varphi(\bar{x})\leq\varphi(w_{k})=e_{\lambda}\varphi(x_{k})-\frac{1}{2\lambda}\|x_{k}-w_{k}\|^{2},

which clearly contradict (
3.8
). Thus
\bar{x}
is a local minimizer of
e_{\lambda}\varphi
for all
\lambda>0
sufficiently small.

To verify the opposite implication, assume that (ii) holds and get
\text{\rm Prox}_{\lambda\varphi}(\bar{x})\neq\emptyset
by
[
38
, Theorem 1.25]
. Moreover, the l.s.c. and prox-boundedness of
\varphi
imply due to
[
38
, Example 10.32]
that

\partial e_{\lambda}\varphi(\bar{x})\subset\dfrac{1}{\lambda}\big[\bar{x}-\text{\rm Prox}_{\lambda\varphi}(\bar{x})\big].

(3.9)

By the generalized Fermat rule for limiting subgradients in
[
22
, Proposition 1.114]
, we have
0\in\partial e_{\lambda}\varphi(\bar{x})
. Combining the latter with (
3.9
) ensures that

0\in\partial e_{\lambda}\varphi(\bar{x})\subset\dfrac{1}{\lambda}\big[\bar{x}-\text{\rm Prox}_{\lambda\varphi}(\bar{x})\big],

and so
\bar{x}\in\text{\rm Prox}_{\lambda\varphi}(\bar{x})
. The minimization in (ii) yields the existence of a neighborhood
U
of
\bar{x}
with

\varphi(x)\geq e_{\lambda}\varphi(x)\geq e_{\lambda}\varphi(\bar{x})=\varphi(\bar{x})+\frac{1}{2\lambda}\|\bar{x}-\bar{x}\|^{2}=\varphi(\bar{x})\hskip 10.00002pt\text{whenever}\;x\in U,

which therefore completes the proof of the theorem.

4
Second-Order Necessary Conditions under Prox-Regularity

This section is devoted to deriving second-order necessary optimality conditions in the unconstrained extended-real-valued format of minimization with general prox-regular objectives. The tools of variational analysis used for these purposes are
second-order subdifferentials
, which are defined below in the scheme of
[
20
]
as coderivatives of subgradient mappings. The following second-order constructions of this type are described via combinations of the subgradients and coderivatives discussed in Section
2
.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
\varphi:{\rm I\!R}^{n}\to\overline{{\rm I\!R}}
be a function finite at some point
\bar{x}
.

(i)
For any
\bar{v}\in\partial\varphi(\bar{x})
, the multifunction
\partial^{2}\varphi(\bar{x},\bar{v}):{\rm I\!R}^{n}\rightrightarrows{\rm I\!R}^{n}
with the values

\partial^{2}\varphi(\bar{x},\bar{v})(u):=(D^{*}\partial\varphi)(\bar{x},\bar{v})(u),\hskip 10.00002ptu\in{\rm I\!R}^{n},

is the
limiting/basic second-order subdifferential
of
\varphi
at
\bar{x}
relative to
\bar{v}
.

(ii)
For any
\bar{v}\in{\partial}\varphi(\bar{x})
, the multifunction
\breve{\partial}^{2}\varphi(\bar{x},\bar{v}):{\rm I\!R}^{n}\rightrightarrows{\rm I\!R}^{n}
with the values

\breve{\partial}^{2}\varphi(\bar{x},\bar{v})(u):=(\widehat{D}^{*}{\partial}\varphi)(\bar{x},\bar{v})(u),\hskip 10.00002ptu\in{\rm I\!R}^{n},

is the
combined second-order subdifferential
of
\varphi
at
\bar{x}
relative to
\bar{v}
.

(iii)
For any
\bar{v}\in\widehat{\partial}\varphi(\bar{x})
, the multifunction
\widehat{\partial}^{2}\varphi(\bar{x},\bar{v}):{\rm I\!R}^{n}\rightrightarrows{\rm I\!R}^{n}
with the values

\widehat{\partial}^{2}\varphi(\bar{x},\bar{v})(u):=(\widehat{D}^{*}{\widehat{\partial}}\varphi)(\bar{x},\bar{v})(u),\hskip 10.00002ptu\in{\rm I\!R}^{n},

is the
regular second-order subdifferential
of
\varphi
at
\bar{x}
relative to
\bar{v}
.

We skip
\bar{v}=\nabla\varphi(\bar{x})
in the above second-order subdifferentials if
\varphi
is
\mathcal{C}^{1}
-smooth around
\bar{x}
. The inclusions given below are obvious:

\breve{\partial}^{2}\varphi(x,v)(u)\subset\partial^{2}\varphi(x,v)(u)\;\text{ for all }\;(x,v)\in\text{\rm gph}\,\partial\varphi,\;u\in{\rm I\!R}^{n}.

\breve{\partial}^{2}\varphi(x,v)(u)\subset\widehat{\partial}^{2}\varphi(x,v)(u)\;\text{ for all }\;(x,v)\in\text{\rm gph}\,\partial\varphi,\;u\in{\rm I\!R}^{n}.

In general, the regular second-order subdifferential and the limiting one are incomparable. However, if
\varphi\in\mathcal{C}^{1}
around
\bar{x}
, then we have the relationships

\breve{\partial}^{2}\varphi(\bar{x})(u)=\widehat{\partial}^{2}\varphi(\bar{x})(u)\subset\partial^{2}\varphi(\bar{x})(u)\;\mbox{for all }\;u\in{\rm I\!R}^{n}.

(4.1)

If
\varphi\in\mathcal{C}^{1,1}
around
\bar{x}
, then the second-order subdifferential calculation can be simplified by the scalarization formulas below taken from
[
25
, Proposition 3.5]
and
[
22
, Proposition 1.120]
, respectively:

\widehat{\partial}^{2}\varphi(\bar{x})(u)=\breve{\partial}^{2}\varphi(\bar{x})(u)=\widehat{\partial}\langle u,\nabla\varphi\rangle(\bar{x}),\hskip 10.00002pt{\partial}^{2}\varphi(\bar{x})(u)=\partial\langle u,\nabla\varphi\rangle(\bar{x}),\hskip 10.00002ptu\in{\rm I\!R}^{n}.

If
\varphi
is
\mathcal{C}^{2}
-smooth around
\bar{x}
, then the above second-order subdifferentials reduce to the classical Hessian

\partial^{2}\varphi(\bar{x})(u)=\breve{\partial}^{2}\varphi(\bar{x})(u)=\widehat{\partial}^{2}\varphi(\bar{x})(u)=\{\nabla^{2}\varphi(\bar{x})u\}\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{whenever }\;u\in{\rm I\!R}^{n}.

To derive second-order necessary optimality conditions for local minimizers of prox-regular functions, we begin with the following useful remark.

Remark 4.2
.

Let
\varphi:{\rm I\!R}^{n}\to\overline{{\rm I\!R}}
be prox-regular at
\bar{x}\in\mbox{\rm dom}\,\varphi
for
\bar{v}\in\partial\varphi(\bar{x})
with respect to
\varepsilon>0
and
r\geq 0
. Then the function
\psi:{\rm I\!R}^{n}\to\overline{{\rm I\!R}}
given by

\psi(x):=\varphi(x)+\delta_{B_{\varepsilon}(\bar{x})}(x)\;\text{ whenever }\;x\in{\rm I\!R}^{n}

(4.2)

is prox-bounded and prox-regular at
\bar{x}
for
\bar{v}\in\partial\psi(\bar{x})
with respect to the same
\varepsilon>0
and
r\geq 0
. Furthermore, we have

\breve{\partial}^{2}\psi(x,y)(u)=\breve{\partial}^{2}\varphi(x,y)(u)\;\mbox{ for all }\;(x,y)\in\mbox{\rm gph}\,\partial\psi\cap(B_{\varepsilon}(\bar{x})\times{\rm I\!R}^{n})\;\mbox{ and }\;u\in{\rm I\!R}^{n}.

(4.3)

The next theorem provides the desired
second-order subdifferential necessary optimality condition
for local minimizers of the general class of prox-regular functions.

Theorem 4.3
.

If
\bar{x}\in\mbox{\rm dom}\,\varphi
is a local minimizer of an extended-real-valued function
\varphi\colon{\rm I\!R}^{n}\to\overline{{\rm I\!R}}
, then
0\in\partial\varphi(\bar{x})
. Assuming in addition that
\varphi
is prox-regular at
\bar{x}
for
0
, we have the pointbased second-order necessary optimality condition for local minimizers

\langle z,w\rangle\geq 0\;\text{ whenever }\;z\in\breve{\partial}^{2}\varphi(\bar{x},0)(w)\;\mbox{ and }\;w\in{\rm I\!R}^{n}

(4.4)

expressed via the combined second-order subdifferential of
\varphi
at
\bar{x}
relative to
0
from Definition
4.1
(ii)
.

[
Proof.]
The stationary condition
0\in\partial\varphi(\bar{x})
for a local minimizer
\bar{x}
follows immediately from
[
23
, Proposition 1.30]
. By Remark
4.2
, one finds an
\varepsilon>0
such that
\psi:=\varphi+\delta_{B_{\varepsilon}(\bar{x})}
from (
4.2
) is prox-bounded and prox-regular at
\bar{x}
for
0\in\partial\psi(\bar{x})
with respect to such radius
\varepsilon
. Obviously,
\psi
is l.s.c. around
\bar{x}
as well. It follows from Proposition
3.1
that there exists a neighborhood
U_{\lambda}
of
\bar{x}
for all
\lambda>0
sufficiently small such that the Moreau envelope
e_{\lambda}\psi
is
\mathcal{C}^{1,1}
on
U_{\lambda}
and that representation (
3.4
) is satisfied in terms of
\psi
. Theorem
3.2
tells us that
\bar{x}
is also a local minimizer of
e_{\lambda}\psi
for all small
\lambda>0
. Then it follows from
[
7
, Theorem 3.3]
that the multifunction
\breve{\partial}^{2}e_{\lambda}\psi(\bar{x})
is positive-semidefinite, i.e.,

\langle z,u\rangle\geq 0\;\text{ whenever }\;z\in\breve{\partial}^{2}e_{\lambda}\psi(\bar{x})(u),\hskip 10.00002ptu\in{\rm I\!R}^{n}.

(4.5)

Pick any
w\in{\rm I\!R}^{n}
and
z\in\breve{\partial}^{2}\varphi(\bar{x},0)(w)
. It follows from (
4.3
) that
z\in\breve{\partial}^{2}\psi(\bar{x},0)(w)
. Since
\mbox{\rm gph}\,T\subset\mbox{\rm gph}\,\partial\psi
, we have the inclusion

\widehat{N}_{{\rm\small gph}\,\partial\psi}(\bar{x},0)\subset\widehat{N}_{{\rm\small gph}\,T}(\bar{x},0),

which tells us by the definition of the regular coderivative that

\breve{\partial}^{2}\psi(\bar{x},0)(w)=(\widehat{D}^{*}\partial\psi)(\bar{x},0)(w)\subset\widehat{D}^{*}T(\bar{x},0)(w).

(4.6)

It follows from (
4.6
) and
z\in\breve{\partial}^{2}\psi(\bar{x},0)(w)
that
-w\in\widehat{D}^{*}(T^{-1})(0,\bar{x})(-z)
. Using further (
3.4
) and then applying the coderivative sum rule from
[
22
, Theorem 1.62]
lead us to the equalities

\widehat{D}^{*}\big(\nabla e_{\lambda}\psi\big)^{-1}(0,\bar{x})(-z)=\widehat{D}^{*}(\lambda I+T^{-1})(0,\bar{x}\big)(-z)=-\lambda z+\widehat{D}^{*}(T^{-1})(0,\bar{x})(-z),

which imply in turn the inclusion

-\lambda z-w\in\widehat{D}^{*}(\nabla e_{\lambda}\psi)^{-1}(0,\bar{x})(-z).

This tells us that
z\in\widehat{D}^{*}(\nabla e_{\lambda}\psi)(\bar{x})(\lambda z+w)
meaning that
z\in\breve{\partial}^{2}e_{\lambda}\psi(\bar{x})(\lambda z+w)
. By (
4.5
), we get therefore that
\langle z,\lambda z+w\rangle\geq 0
. Letting
\lambda\downarrow 0
in the latter inequality, we arrive at
\langle z,w\rangle\geq 0
and thus justify the claimed condition (
4.4
), which completes the proof of the theorem.

Remark 4.4
.

Note that we
cannot replace
the combined second-order subdifferential in (
4.4
) by the limiting one, i.e., the pointbased relationship

\langle z,w\rangle\geq 0\;\text{ for all }\;z\in{\partial}^{2}\varphi(\bar{x},0)(w)\;\mbox{ and }\;w\in{\rm I\!R}^{n}

(4.7)

fails to be a necessary condition for the local optimality of
\bar{x}
in minimizing a prox-regular function
\varphi
. Indeed, it is discussed in
[
9
, Example 4.14]
that there is an example in
[
12
]
showing that (
4.7
) fails for a local minimizer
\bar{x}
of a function
\varphi\colon{\rm I\!R}^{2}\to\overline{{\rm I\!R}}
that is
fully amenable
at
\bar{x}
being surely continuously prox-regular at
\bar{x}
for
0\in\partial\varphi(\bar{x})
; see
[
38
]
. Furthermore, Example 3.2 in
[
7
]
illustrates the violation of (
4.7
) for a local minimizer
\bar{x}
of a function
\varphi\colon{\rm I\!R}\to{\rm I\!R}
from class
{\cal C}^{1,1}
around

\bar{x}
, which is also continuously prox-regular at
\bar{x}
for
0=\nabla\varphi(\bar{x})
.

5
Variational Convexity and Second-Order Sufficient Conditions

Our goal here is to derive a
neighborhood
counterpart of the second-order subdifferential necessary condition (
4.4
) that are
sufficient
for local optimality in the extended-real-valued framework of prox-regular functions. In fact, we will do more. Namely, the main result of this section provides a new
coderivative characterization
of the
variational convexity
property for general l.s.c. functions
\varphi\colon{\rm I\!R}^{n}\to\overline{{\rm I\!R}}
, which yields the sufficiency of local minimizers.

We start with the definition of variational convexity (and its strong version used in the next section). These properties have been recently introduced by Rockafellar
[
36
]
and then have been further investigated and applied in
[
13
,
15
,
37
,
39
]
.

Definition 5.1
.

An l.s.c. function
\varphi:{\rm I\!R}^{n}\to\overline{{\rm I\!R}}
is
variationally convex
at
\bar{x}
for
\bar{v}\in\partial\varphi(\bar{x})
if there exist a convex neighborhood
U\times V
of
(\bar{x},\bar{v})
and an l.s.c. convex function
\psi\leq\varphi
on
U
such that

(U_{\varepsilon}\times V)\cap\mbox{\rm gph}\,\partial\varphi=(U\times V)\cap\mbox{\rm gph}\,\partial\psi\;\text{and}\;\varphi(x)=\psi(x)\;\text{at the common elements}\;(x,v)

(5.1)

for some
\varepsilon>0
, where
U_{\varepsilon}:=\{x\in U\;|\;\varphi(x)<\varphi(\bar{x})+\varepsilon\}
. We say that
\varphi
is
strongly variationally convex
at
\bar{x}
for
\bar{v}
with modulus
\sigma>0
if (
5.1
) holds with
\psi
being strongly convex on
U
with this modulus.

First we provide a coderivative characterization of the
cocoercivity property
of single-valued continuous mappings. This result is of its own importance while being useful to derive the aforementioned characterizations of variational convexity.

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
f:U\rightarrow{\rm I\!R}^{n}
be a continuous mapping defined on an open set
U\subset{\rm I\!R}^{n}
, and let
\sigma>0
. Then the following assertions are equivalent:

(i)

f
is cocoercive on
U
with modulus
\sigma
, i.e., for arbitrary
x_{1},x_{2}\in U
it holds that

\langle f(x_{1})-f(x_{2}),x_{1}-x_{2}\rangle\geq\sigma\|f(x_{1})-f(x_{2})\|^{2}.

(5.2)

(ii)
We have the coderivative estimate

\langle w,z\rangle\geq\sigma\|w\|^{2}\ \text{ whenever }\ w\in D^{*}f(x)(z),\ z\in{\rm I\!R}^{n},\;\text{ and }\ x\in U.

(iii)
We have the coderivative estimate

\langle w,z\rangle\geq\sigma\|w\|^{2}\ \text{ whenever }\ w\in\widehat{D}^{*}f(x)(z),\ z\in{\rm I\!R}^{n},\;\text{ and }\ x\in U.

(5.3)

[
Proof.]
To verify implication (i)
\Longrightarrow
(ii), pick any
x\in U
,
z\in{\rm I\!R}^{n}
, and
w\in D^{*}f(x)(z)
. By applying the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality to (i), we clearly get the Lipschitz continuity of
f
on
U
with modulus
\frac{1}{\sigma}
. Define the mapping
g_{z}\colon U\to{\rm I\!R}
by

g_{z}(y):=\big\langle z,2\sigma f(y)-y\big\rangle\;\text{ for all }\;y\in U.

We aim to show that
g_{z}
is Lipschitz continuous on
U
with modulus
\|z\|
by taking arbitrary
x_{1},x_{2}\in U
and employing
the cocoercive condition (
5.2
) for them. This yields

\left\|2\sigma\left[f\left(x_{1}\right)-f\left(x_{2}\right)\right]-(x_{1}-x_{2})\right\|\leq\left\|x_{1}-x_{2}\right\|,

and thus
\big|\left\langle z,2\sigma\left[f\left(x_{1}\right)-f\left(x_{2}\right)\right]-(x_{1}-x_{2})\right\rangle\big|\leq\|z\|\cdot\left\|x_{1}-x_{2}\right\|
. By the definition of
g_{z}
, we get

\left|g_{z}(x_{1})-g_{z}(x_{2})\right|\leq\|z\|\cdot\|x_{1}-x_{2}\|,

which verifies the Lipschitz continuity of
g_{z}
on
U
with modulus
\|z\|
. Then the subdifferential estimate for Lipschitz continuous functions taken from
[
22
, Corollary 1.81]
ensures that

\|x^{*}\|\leq\|z\|\ \text{ for all }\ x^{*}\in\partial g_{z}(x).

(5.4)

Taking into account the structure of the function
g_{z}
and the Lipschitz continuity of
f
allows us to employ the limiting subdifferential sum rule from
[
22
, Theorem 1.107(ii)]
and the scalarization formula for the limiting coderivative from
[
22
, Theorem 1.90]
to obtain the relationships

\displaystyle 2\sigma w-z

\displaystyle\in

\displaystyle 2\sigma D^{*}f(x)(z)+\nabla\langle-z,\cdot\rangle(x)=D^{*}(2\sigma f)(x)(z)+\nabla\langle-z,\cdot\rangle(x)=\partial\langle z,2\sigma f\rangle(x)+\nabla\langle-z,\cdot\rangle(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\partial\langle z,2\sigma f-\cdot\rangle(x)=\partial g_{z}(x),

bringing us to
2\sigma w-z\in\partial g_{z}(x)
. It follows from the basic subgradient estimate (
5.4
) that
\|2\sigma w-z\|\leq\|z\|
, which amounts to
4\sigma^{2}\|w\|^{2}-4\sigma\langle w,z\rangle\leq 0
, i.e.,
\langle w,z\rangle\geq\sigma\|w\|^{2}
, and thus (ii) holds.

Implication (ii)
\Longrightarrow
(iii) is trivial since
\widehat{D}^{*}f(x)(z)\subset D^{*}f(x)(z)
for all
x\in U
and
z\in{\rm I\!R}^{n}
.

Let us proceed next with verifying (iii)
\Longrightarrow
(i). Assuming the coderivative estimate (
5.3
), we intend to show that
f-\frac{1}{2\sigma}I
is Lipschitz continuous on
U
with modulus
\frac{1}{2\sigma}
, where
I
is the identity operator on
{\rm I\!R}^{n}
. To this end, pick any
x\in U
,
z\in{\rm I\!R}^{n}
, and
w\in\widehat{D}^{*}\left(f-\frac{1}{2\sigma}I\right)(x)(z)
. Employing the aforementioned sum rule for the regular coderivative tells us that

w\in\widehat{D}^{*}\left(f-\frac{1}{2\sigma}I\right)(x)(z)=\widehat{D}^{*}f(x)(z)-\frac{1}{2\sigma}z,

which yields
w+\frac{1}{2\sigma}z\in\widehat{D}^{*}f(x)(z)
. Then we deduce from (
5.3
) that

\Big\langle w+\frac{1}{2\sigma}z,z\Big\rangle\geq\sigma\left\|w+\dfrac{1}{2\sigma}z\right\|^{2},\text{ i.e., }\dfrac{\|z\|^{2}}{4\sigma}\geq\sigma\|w\|^{2},

which is equivalent to
\|w\|\leq\dfrac{1}{2\sigma}\|z\|
. It follows from the regular coderivative characterization of the Lipschitz continuity in
[
22
, Theorem 4.7]
that the mapping
f-\frac{1}{2\sigma}I
is Lipschitz continuous on
U
with modulus
\frac{1}{2\sigma}
. Using this and applying
[
41
, Proposition 2.3]
verify the cocoercivity property of
f
in (
5.2
), which gives us (i) and thus completes the proof of the proposition.

Remark 5.3
.

It is pointed out to us by one of the referees that Proposition
5.2
can be deduced from
[
28
, Lemma 3.3 and Theorem 3.4]
via the fact that
f
is cocoercive if and only if
f^{-1}
is strongly monotone. Still, we present a direct proof of this proposition for the sake of self-containment.

Next we present a sufficient condition for the cocoercivity of the proximal mappings.

Proposition 5.4
.

Let
\varphi:{\rm I\!R}^{n}\rightarrow\overline{{\rm I\!R}}
be prox-bounded and prox-regular at
\bar{x}\in\mbox{\rm dom}\,\varphi
for
\bar{v}\in\partial\varphi(\bar{x})
with respect to a radius
\varepsilon>0
. Suppose that there exists a neighborhood
W
of
(\bar{x},\bar{v})
for which

\displaystyle\langle z,w\rangle\geq 0\ \text{ whenever }\ z\in\widehat{D}^{*}T(u,v)(w),\ w\in{\rm I\!R}^{n}\ \text{ and }\ (u,v)\in\mbox{\rm gph}\,T\cap W,

(5.5)

where
T
is the
\varphi
-attentive
\varepsilon
-localization of
\partial\varphi
around
(\bar{x},\bar{v})
taken from Proposition
3.1
. Then for any sufficiently small
\lambda>0
the proximal mapping
\text{\rm Prox}_{\lambda\varphi}
is cocoercive with modulus
1
on a neighborhood of
\bar{x}+\lambda\bar{v}
.

[
Proof.]
By Proposition
3.1
, for any
\lambda>0
sufficiently small, there exists a neighborhood
U_{\lambda}
of
\bar{x}+\lambda\bar{v}
on which assertions (i)–(iii) of Proposition
3.1
hold in terms of
T
. Fixing such a small number
\lambda
, we claim that
\text{\rm Prox}_{\lambda\varphi}
is cocoercive with modulus
\sigma=1
on
U_{\lambda}
by employing Proposition
5.2
. Note first the continuity of
{\rm Prox}_{\lambda\varphi}
on
U_{\lambda}
yields this property for the mapping
\Phi:U_{\lambda}\rightarrow{\rm I\!R}^{n}\times{\rm I\!R}^{n}
defined by

\displaystyle y\mapsto\left({\rm Prox}_{\lambda\varphi}(y),\dfrac{y-{\rm Prox}_{\lambda\varphi}(y)}{\lambda}\right).

Observe that
\Phi(\bar{x}+\lambda\bar{v})=(\bar{x},\bar{v})\in W
and then shrink if necessary the neighborhood
U_{\lambda}
of
\bar{x}+\lambda\bar{v}
so that
\Phi(U_{\lambda})\subset W
. Picking any
y\in U_{\lambda}
, let
x:={\rm Prox}_{\lambda\varphi}(y)
and
z\in\widehat{D}^{*}{\rm Prox}_{\lambda\varphi}(y)(w)
for
w\in{\rm I\!R}^{n}
. This implies by Proposition
3.1
(ii) and the coderivative sum rule from
[
22
, Theorem 1.62(i)]
that

\displaystyle-w\in\widehat{D}^{*}({\rm Prox}_{\lambda\varphi})^{-1}(x,y)(-z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\widehat{D}^{*}(I+\lambda T)(x,y)(-z)=-z+\widehat{D}^{*}(\lambda T)(x,y-x)(-z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-z+\lambda\widehat{D}^{*}T\left(x,\dfrac{y-x}{\lambda}\right)(-z).

Therefore, we arrive at the coderivative inclusion

\dfrac{z-w}{\lambda}\in\widehat{D}^{*}T\left(x,\dfrac{y-x}{\lambda}\right)(-z).

(5.6)

Since
\left(x,\dfrac{y-x}{\lambda}\right)=\Phi(y)\in W
, it follows from (
5.9
) and (
5.6
) that
\big\langle\frac{z-w}{\lambda},-z\big\rangle\geq 0
, i.e.,

\langle w,z\rangle\geq\|z\|^{2}\;\text{ whenever }\;z\in\widehat{D}^{*}\text{\rm Prox}_{\lambda\varphi}(y)(w).

(5.7)

Applying now Proposition
5.2
to the continuous mapping
\text{\rm Prox}_{\lambda\varphi}
on
U_{\lambda}\ni\bar{x}+\lambda\bar{v}
satisfying the coderivative estimate (
5.7
) tells us that
\text{\rm Prox}_{\lambda\varphi}
is cocoercive with modulus
\sigma=1
on
U_{\lambda}
.

Now we are ready to establish the new coderivative-based
characterizations of variational convexity
. Note that characterizations of this type first appeared in
[
15
]
while validating subdifferential continuity.

Theorem 5.5
.

Let
\varphi:{\rm I\!R}^{n}\rightarrow\overline{{\rm I\!R}}
be an l.s.c. function with
\bar{x}\in\mbox{\rm dom}\,\varphi
and
\bar{v}\in\partial\varphi(\bar{x})
. Then the following assertions are equivalent:

(i)

\varphi
is variationally convex at
\bar{x}
for
\bar{v}
.

(ii)

\varphi
is prox-regular at
\bar{x}
for
\bar{v}
with respect to a radius
\varepsilon>0
, and there is a neighborhood
W
of
(\bar{x},\bar{v})
such that

\displaystyle\langle z,w\rangle\geq 0\ \text{ whenever }\ z\in D^{*}T^{\varphi}(u,v)(w)\ \text{ and }\ (u,v)\in\mbox{\rm gph}\,T^{\varphi}\cap W.

(5.8)

Remark 6.4

(

As advised by one of the referees, the usage of Moreau envelopes to obtain second-order optimality conditions was initiated by Eberhard and Wenczel

10

[

]
cannot be easily simplified under the prox-regularity of the function in consideration. Below the main ideas in two approaches are provided:

•

The assumed prox-regularity of

at
\bar{x}
for

•

On the other hand, the device in

]
finally get the positive-semidefiniteness of tghe multifunction
(\widehat{D}^{*}\partial_{p}\varphi)(\bar{x},0)
.

As demonstrated in
[
8
]
by an illustrative example, a significant limitation of strong local minimizers lies in their high sensitivity with respect to minor perturbations. Addressing this issue, the authors of
[
8
]
introduce a more robust notion labeled
stable strong local minimizers
. Remarkably, it is proved in
[
8
]
that the subclass of strong local minimizers agrees with tilt-stable ones mentioned in Section
1
.

Recall that
\bar{x}\in\mbox{\rm dom}\,\varphi
is a
tilt-stable local minimizer
of a function
\varphi\colon{\rm I\!R}^{n}\to\overline{{\rm I\!R}}
if there exists a number
\gamma>0
such that the mapping

M_{\gamma}\colon v\mapsto{\rm argmin}\big\{\varphi(x)-\langle v,x\rangle\;\big|\;x\in\mathbb{B}_{\gamma}(\bar{x})\big\}

is single-valued and Lipschitz continuous on some neighborhood of
0\in{\rm I\!R}^{n}
with
M_{\gamma}(0)=\{\bar{x}\}
. By a
modulus
of tilt stability of
\varphi
at
\bar{x}
we understand a Lipschitz constant of
M_{\gamma}
around the origin. It has been well recognized starting with
[
36
]
that tilt-stable local minimizers of continuously prox-regular functions are closely related to strong variational stability of such functions with the moduli interplay. The following proposition is taken from
[
15
, Proposition 2.9]
.

Proposition 6.5
.

Let
\varphi:{\rm I\!R}^{n}\to\overline{{\rm I\!R}}
be continuously prox-regular at
\bar{x}\in\mbox{\rm dom}\,\varphi
for
0\in\partial\varphi(\bar{x})
. Then we have the equivalent assertions:

(i)

\varphi
is strongly variationally convex at
\bar{x}
for
\bar{v}=0
with modulus
\sigma>0
.

(ii)

\bar{x}
is a tilt-stable local minimizer of
\varphi
with modulus
\sigma^{-1}
.

Next we discuss some
second-order sufficient conditions
for strong local minimizers of continuously prox-regular functions that are complement the necessary optimality conditions from Theorem
6.3
.

Remark 6.6
.

Let
\varphi:{\rm I\!R}^{n}\to\overline{{\rm I\!R}}
be continuously prox-regular at
\bar{x}
for
0\in\partial\varphi(\bar{x})
. One of the second-order subdifferential conditions to guarantee the
strong
local optimality of the stationary point
\bar{x}
of
\varphi
is the
pointbased second-order sufficient optimality condition
(PSOSC)

\langle z,w\rangle>0\;\text{ whenever }\;z\in{\partial}^{2}\varphi(\bar{x},0)(w),\;w\in{\rm I\!R}^{n}\setminus\{0\},

(6.14)

which is different from (
6.10
) only by the type of the second-order subdifferential. Indeed, it is shown in
[
35
, Theorem 1.3]
that PSOSC (
6.14
) is
equivalent
to the

of
\bar{x}

\bar{x}
is a strong local minimizer of
\varphi
. Another second-order subdifferential condition ensuring the local optimality of
\bar{x}
for
\varphi
is the

(NSOSC)

\langle z,w\rangle\geq\sigma\|w\|^{2}\;\text{ whenever }\;z\in\breve{\partial}^{2}\varphi(x,v)(w),\;(x,v)\in\mbox{\rm gph}\,\partial\varphi\cap(U\times V),\;w\in{\rm I\!R}^{n}

(6.15)

valid for some
\sigma>0
and neighborhoods
U
of
\bar{x}
and
V
of
\bar{v}=0
as a
no-gap neighborhood
counterpart of the second-order necessary optimality condition in (

[
15
, Theorem 5.4]
that NSOSC (
6.15
) is a
characterization
of the

of a continuously prox-regular function
\varphi
at
\bar{x}
for
0\in\partial\varphi(\bar{x})
, which implies that
\bar{x}
is a strong (even tilt-stable) local minimizer of
\varphi
while not vice versa; see
[
15
, Remarks 2.6(ii)]
.

Second-Order Necessary Conditions in Constrained Optimization

In this section, we aim at establishing second-order necessary optimality conditions for the class of
constrained optimization problems
in the functional-geometric form given by:

\mbox{\rm minimize}\;f(x)\;\text{ subject to }\;g(x)\in\Theta,

(7.1)

where
f:{\rm I\!R}^{n}\to{\rm I\!R}
and
g\colon{\rm I\!R}^{n}\to{\rm I\!R}^{m}
are
{\cal C}^{1}
-smooth at the reference points, and where
\Theta\subset{\rm I\!R}^{m}
is a nonempty, closed, and convex set. These are our
standing assumptions
in this section unless otherwise stated. Problem (
7.1
) encompasses various particular classes of problems in constrained optimization such as conic programs, nonlinear programs, etc.

Denoting the set of feasible solutions to (
7.1
) by

\Gamma:=\big\{x\in{\rm I\!R}^{n}\;\big|\;g(x)\in\Theta\big\},

(7.2)

we can rewrite (
7.1
) in the unconstrained extended-real-valued format

\mbox{\rm minimize}\hskip 10.00002pt\varphi(x):=f(x)+\delta_{\Gamma}(x)\;\text{ subject to }\;x\in{\rm I\!R}^{n}

(7.3)

for which the second-order optimality conditions are established in Sections
4
–
6
via the second-order subdifferentials of the cost function
\varphi\colon{\rm I\!R}^{n}\to\overline{{\rm I\!R}}
. Our goal is to obtain second-order optimality conditions in terms of the initial data of problem (
7.1
). This section addresses necessary optimality conditions, while the corresponding sufficient conditions for (
7.1
) are derived in Section
8
.

It follows from
[
23
, Proposition 1.30]
that the first-order necessary condition for the local optimality of
\bar{x}
in the unconstrained optimization problem (
7.3
) is

0\in\partial\varphi(\bar{x})=\nabla f(\bar{x})+\partial\delta_{\Gamma}(\bar{x})=\nabla f(\bar{x})+N_{\Gamma}(\bar{x}),

(7.4)

which can be equivalently expressed in the form

\nabla f(\bar{x})+\nabla g(\bar{x})^{*}\bar{y}=0\;\text{ for some }\;\bar{y}\in N_{\Theta}(g(\bar{x}))

(7.5)

under appropriate constraint qualification conditions. One of the most well-known constraint qualification conditions, which is called the first-order constrained qualification condition (FOCQ), to guarantee the equivalence between (
7.4
) and (
7.5
) is

N_{\Theta}(g(\bar{x}))\cap\mbox{\rm ker}\,\nabla g(\bar{x})^{*}=\{0\}.

(7.6)

For particular classes of optimization problems, the general FOCQ (
7.6
)
reduces to the classical
Mangasarian-Fromovitz constraint qualification
(MFCQ) in nonlinear programming and to the
Robinson constraint qualification
in conic programming. Moreover, it follows from the Mordukhovich criterion in (
2.9
) (see
[
21
, Theorem 3.6]
and
[
38
, Theorem 9.40]
) that condition (
7.6
) is equivalent to the
metric regularity
of the mapping
(x,\alpha)\mapsto(g(x),\alpha)-\Theta\times{\rm I\!R}_{+}
around the point
((\bar{x},0),(0,0))
. We are also going to use the qualification condition labeled as the
second-order qualification condition
(SOCQ) from
[
31
]
for problem (
9.1
) at
\bar{x}\in\Gamma
, which is formulated as follows:

D^{*}N_{\Theta}(g(\bar{x}),\bar{y})(0)\cap\mbox{\rm ker}\,\nabla g(\bar{x})^{*}=\{0\},

(7.7)

where
\bar{y}\in{\rm I\!R}^{m}
satisfies (
7.5
). It follows from
[
15
, Lemma 7.2]
that SOCQ (
7.7
) reduces to the classical
linear independence constraint
qualification
(LICQ) in nonlinear programming. Next we recall the weaker qualification condition, which is labeled as the
metric subregularity constraint qualification condition
(MSCQ) for (
7.1
), introduced in
[
11
]
. Namely, we say that MSCQ holds at
\bar{x}\in\Gamma
if the mapping
x\mapsto g(x)-\Theta
is
metrically subregular
at
(\bar{x},0)
, i.e.,
there exist a neighborhood
U
of
\bar{x}
and a number
\kappa>0
such that the distance estimate

{\rm dist}(x;\Gamma)\leq\kappa\,{\rm dist}(g(x);\Theta)\;\text{ for all }\;x\in U

(7.8)

is satisfied. It follows from the definition that MSCQ is a
robust
property, i.e., MSCQ is fulfilled at every
x\in\Gamma
near
\bar{x}
whenever it holds
at
\bar{x}\in\Gamma
. The next remark summarizes relationships between the above constraint qualification conditions that are broadly used in this paper.

Remark 7.1
.

Observe the following:

(i)
We get from
[
15
, Proposition 6.3]
that SOCQ always yields FOCQ while the inverse implication is not true in general. This distinction is evident in classical nonlinear programming, where FOCQ is equivalent to MFCQ while SOCQ reduces to LICQ, which is stronger than MFCQ.

(ii)
As discussed above, FOCQ is equivalent to the metric regularity of the mapping
(x,\alpha)\mapsto(g(x),\alpha)-\Theta\times{\rm I\!R}_{+}
around the point
((\bar{x},0),(0,0))
. Using
[
19
, Proposition 3.1]
, we deduce that the latter property implies that
g(x)-\Theta
is metrically subregular at
(\bar{x},0)
, i.e., MSCQ is satisfied at
\bar{x}
. The inverse implication fails in general. This is demonstrated by

[
19
, Example 3.3(b)]
.

(\longrightarrow)
read “implies” while the arrows
(\dashrightarrow)
read “may not imply”.

Next we recall a useful formula for calculating the regular coderivative of the normal cone mapping obtained in
[
6
, Corollary 3.8]
under MSCQ.

Proposition 7.2
.

Let
\Theta\subset{\rm I\!R}^{m}
be a nonempty polyhedral convex set, let the set
\Gamma\subset{\rm I\!R}^{n}
be given in (
7.2
) where
g:{\rm I\!R}^{n}\to{\rm I\!R}^{m}
is a
\mathcal{C}^{2}
-smooth mapping. Suppose that MSCQ is satisfied at
\bar{x}\in\Gamma
and for each
\bar{v}\in N_{\Gamma}(\bar{x})
define the sets

P(\bar{x},\bar{v}):=\big\{y\in{\rm I\!R}^{m}\;\big|\;\bar{v}=\nabla g(\bar{x})^{*}y,\;y\in N_{\Theta}\left(g(\bar{x})\right)\big\},

S(v):=\text{\rm argmax}\left\{\langle v,\nabla^{2}\langle y,g\rangle(\bar{x})v\rangle\;\big|\;y\in P(\bar{x},\bar{v})\right\}\;\text{for all }v\in K_{\Gamma}(\bar{x},\bar{v}).

(7.9)

Then we have the calculation formula

\widehat{D}^{*}N_{\Gamma}(\bar{x},\bar{v})(w)=\begin{cases}\big\{u\;\big|\;\langle u,v\rangle-\langle w,\nabla^{2}\langle y,g\rangle(\bar{x})v\rangle\leq 0\;\forall v\in K_{\Gamma}(\bar{x},\bar{v}),\;y\in S(v)\big\}&\text{if }\;w\in-K_{\Gamma}(\bar{x},\bar{v}),\\
\emptyset&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

where
K_{\Gamma}
stands for the critical cone (
2.6
)
associated with the set of feasible solutions (
7.2
).

Employing the above coderivative calculation together with other results of variational analysis leads us to the following novel
second-order necessary optimality conditions
for local and strong local minimizers of the constrained problem (
7.1
) involving the initial data of this problem.

Theorem 7.3
.

Consider the constrained optimization problem (
7.1
) in which
\Theta
is a nonempty convex polyhedron. Given a local minimizer
\bar{x}
of (
7.1
), assume that
f
is of class
\mathcal{C}^{1,1}
and
g
is
\mathcal{C}^{2}
-smooth around
\bar{x}
, and that MSCQ holds at this point. Then
\bar{x}
satisfies the first-order optimality
condition (
7.5
). If in addition
\bar{y}
in (
7.5
) is unique, then we have the second-order necessary optimality condition

\langle z,w\rangle+\langle w,\nabla^{2}\langle\bar{y},g\rangle(\bar{x})w\rangle\geq 0

(7.10)

for all
w\in-K_{\Gamma}(\bar{x},-\nabla f(\bar{x}))
and
z\in\breve{\partial}^{2}f(\bar{x})(w)
. In the case where
\bar{x}
is a strong local minimizer of (
7.1
) with modulus
\sigma>0
, the second-order optimality condition

\langle z,w\rangle+\langle w,\nabla^{2}\langle\bar{y},g\rangle(\bar{x})w\rangle\geq\sigma\|w\|^{2}

(7.11)

holds for all
w\in-K_{\Gamma}(\bar{x},-\nabla f(\bar{x}))
and
z\in\breve{\partial}^{2}f(\bar{x})(w)
.

[
Proof.]
By combining
[
23
, Proposition 1.30]
and
[
6
, Lemma 2.5]
with the assumed local optimality at
\bar{x}
, we get the equalities

0\in\partial\varphi(\bar{x})=\nabla f(\bar{x})+N_{\Gamma}(\bar{x})=\nabla f(\bar{x})+\nabla g(\bar{x})^{*}N_{\Theta}\big(g(\bar{x})\big),

which give us the first-order optimality condition (
7.5
). The MSCQ at
\bar{x}
and the polyhedral convexity of
\Theta
yield the continuous prox-regularity of
\delta_{\Gamma}
at
\bar{x}
due to
[
19
, Theorem 7.1]
. Using this together with the
\mathcal{C}^{1,1}
property of
f
around
\bar{x}
, we get that the function
\varphi=f+\delta_{\Gamma}
is continuously prox-regular at
\bar{x}
for
0
due to
[
38
, Exercise 13.35]
.

Next we verify the second-order condition (
7.10
) under the additional assumption that
\bar{y}
in (
7.5
) is unique. Employing Theorem
4.3
tells us that if
\bar{x}
is a local minimizer of
\varphi
, then

\langle z,w\rangle\geq 0\;\text{ whenever }\;z\in\breve{\partial}^{2}\varphi(\bar{x},0)(w),\;w\in{\rm I\!R}^{n}.

(7.12)

Furthermore, the coderivative sum rule from Lemma
6.2
ensures that

\breve{\partial}^{2}f(\bar{x})(w)+\breve{\partial}^{2}\delta_{\Gamma}(\bar{x},-\nabla f(\bar{x}))(w)\subset\breve{\partial}^{2}\varphi(\bar{x},0)(w)\;\text{ for all }\;w\in{\rm I\!R}^{n}.

(7.13)

Since
-\nabla f(\bar{x})\in N_{\Gamma}(\bar{x})
and
\bar{y}
in (
7.5
) is unique, it follows from Proposition
7.2
that

\widehat{D}^{*}N_{\Gamma}(\bar{x},-\nabla f(\bar{x}))(w)=\big\{u\;\big|\;\langle u,v\rangle-\langle w,\nabla^{2}\langle\bar{y},g\rangle(\bar{x})v\rangle\leq 0\;\forall v\in K_{\Gamma}(\bar{x},-\nabla f(\bar{x}))\big\}

for any
w\in-K_{\Gamma}(\bar{x},-\nabla f(\bar{x}))
, and therefore

\nabla^{2}\langle\bar{y},g\rangle(\bar{x})w\in\widehat{D}^{*}N_{\Gamma}(\bar{x},-\nabla f(\bar{x}))(w)=\breve{\partial}^{2}\delta_{\Gamma}(\bar{x},-\nabla f(\bar{x}))(w).

(7.14)

Combining (
7.13
) and (
7.14
) brings us to the inclusion

z+\nabla^{2}\langle\bar{y},g\rangle(\bar{x})w\in\breve{\partial}^{2}\varphi(\bar{x},0)(w)\;\mbox{ for all }\;w\in-K_{\Gamma}(\bar{x},-\nabla f(\bar{x}))\;\mbox{ and }\;z\in\breve{\partial}^{2}f(\bar{x})(w).

(7.15)

Using the latter together with (
7.12
) yields

\langle z,w\rangle+\langle w,\nabla^{2}\langle\bar{y},g\rangle(\bar{x})w\rangle\geq 0\;\mbox{ for all }\;w\in-K_{\Gamma}(\bar{x},-\nabla f(\bar{x}))\;\mbox{ and }\;z\in\breve{\partial}^{2}f(\bar{x})(w),

which verifies (
7.10
). If
\bar{x}
is a strong local minimizer of (
7.1
) with modulus
\sigma>0
, then we have by Theorem
6.3
that

\langle z,w\rangle\geq\sigma\|w\|^{2}\;\text{ whenever }\;z\in\breve{\partial}^{2}\varphi(\bar{x},0)(w),\;w\in{\rm I\!R}^{n},

(7.16)

and thus deduce from (
7.16
) and (
7.15
) the second-order estimate

\langle z,w\rangle+\langle w,\nabla^{2}\langle\bar{y},g\rangle(\bar{x})w\rangle\geq\sigma\|w\|^{2}\;\mbox{ for all }\;w\in-K_{\Gamma}(\bar{x},-\nabla f(\bar{x}))\;\mbox{ and }\;z\in\breve{\partial}^{2}f(\bar{x})(w).

This justifies (
7.11
) and completes the proof of the theorem.

\bar{y}

.

. Then the following assertions hold:

(i)
If
\bar{x}

\bar{x}

\bar{x}
satisfies the first-order optimality condition (

\bar{y}
is unique, and the second-order condition (

[
Proof.]

\bar{x}
. It follows from

in (

\bar{y}
in (

Example 7.5
.

Consider problem (

) with the initial data

Remark 7.6
.

Second-order necessary conditions for constrained optimization problems of type (

[

]

(i)

\mathcal{C}^{1}

[

in (

(ii)
In

) with

and the surjective Jacobian

8
Second-Order Sufficient Conditions in Constrained Optimization

In this section, we derive second-order subdifferential sufficient conditions for local and strong local minimizers of problem (
7.1
) under the mild MSCQ condition in the case when
\Theta
is convex. For each
(x,v)\in{\rm I\!R}^{n}\times{\rm I\!R}^{n}
, define the
set of multipliers
for (
7.1
) as follows:

\Lambda(x,v):=\big\{y\in{\rm I\!R}^{m}\;\big|\;v=\nabla f(x)+\nabla g(x)^{*}y,\;y\in N_{\Theta}\left(g(x)\right)\big\}.

(8.1)

The next proposition shows that the multiplier
mapping in (
8.1
) possesses the local boundedness property. Recall that a set-valued mapping
F:{\rm I\!R}^{n}\rightrightarrows{\rm I\!R}^{m}
is
locally bounded
around
x\in\mbox{\rm dom}\,F
if there is a neighborhood
U
of
x
for which
F(U)
is bounded in
{\rm I\!R}^{m}
.

Proposition 8.1
.

Consider problem (
7.1
), where FOCQ (
7.6
) holds. Then the set-valued mapping of Lagrange multipliers
\Lambda:{\rm I\!R}^{n}\times{\rm I\!R}^{n}\rightrightarrows{\rm I\!R}^{m}
defined in (
8.1
) is locally bounded around
(\bar{x},0)
.

[
Proof.]
Assuming the contrary, construct a sequence
(x_{k},v_{k},y_{k})\in\mbox{\rm gph}\,\Lambda
for which
(x_{k},v_{k})\rightarrow(\bar{x},0)
while
\|y_{k}\|\rightarrow\infty
as
k\rightarrow\infty
. By the definition of

9
Applications to Nonlinear Programming

The main goal of this section is to establish
second-order necessary and sufficient optimality conditions
for local and strong local minimizers of
nonlinear programs
(NLPs) formulated as follows:

\mbox{\rm minimize}\hskip 10.00002ptf(x)\;\text{ subject to }\;\begin{cases}\varphi_{i}(x)\leq 0&\text{for }\;i=1,\ldots,s,\\
\varphi_{i}(x)=0&\text{for }\;i=s+1,\ldots,m,\end{cases}

(9.1)

where
f
is a function of class
\mathcal{C}^{1,1}
, and where
\varphi_{i}
for
i=1,\ldots,m
are
\mathcal{C}^{2}
-smooth functions around the reference points. The optimality conditions for NLPs obtained below will be derived as specifications of those for the general constrained optimization problems in Sections
7
and
8
while being expressed in term of the initial data of (
9.1
) under the corresponding version of the fairly mild MSCQ.

Problem (
9.1
) can be obviously written in the form of (
7.1
) as

\mbox{\rm minimize}\hskip 10.00002ptf(x)\;\text{ subject to }\;g(x)\in\Theta:={\rm I\!R}_{-}^{s}\times\{0_{m-s}\}

with
g(x):=(\varphi_{1}(x),\ldots,\varphi_{m}(x))
and the set of the NLP feasible solutions is
\Gamma:=\big\{x\in{\rm I\!R}^{n}\;\big|\;g(x)\in\Theta\big\}
. Define the corresponding
Lagrangian function

L:{\rm I\!R}^{n}\times{\rm I\!R}^{m}\to{\rm I\!R}
by

L(x,y):=f(x)+\langle y,g(x)\rangle\;\text{ for all }\;x\in{\rm I\!R}^{n},\;y\in{\rm I\!R}^{m}

(9.2)

together with the index collections

I(x):=\big\{i\in\{1,\ldots,s\}\;\big|\;\varphi_{i}(x)=0\big\}\;\text{ and }\;I_{+}(x,y):=\big\{i\in I(x)\;\big|\;y_{i}>0\big\}.

(9.3)

Further, for each
(x,y)\in{\rm I\!R}^{n}\times{\rm I\!R}^{m}
consider the cone

S(x,y):=\left\{w\in{\rm I\!R}^{n}\;\bigg|\;\begin{cases}\langle\nabla\varphi_{i}(x),w\rangle=0&\text{if }\;i\in\big\{s+1,\ldots,m\big\}\cup I_{+}(x,y),\\
\langle\nabla\varphi_{i}(x),w\rangle\geq 0&\text{if }\;i\in I(x)\setminus I_{+}(x,y)\end{cases}\right\}.

(9.4)

Next we recall for the reader’s convenience the classical constraint qualification conditions in NLP. Given a feasible solution
\bar{x}
to (
9.1
), it is said that the
linear independence constraint qualification
(LICQ) holds at
\bar{x}
of (
9.1
) if the vectors

\nabla\varphi_{i}(\bar{x})\;\mbox{ for }\;i\in I(\bar{x})\cup\{s+1,\ldots,m\}\;\text{ are linearly independent},

(9.5)

where
I(\bar{x})
stands for the collection of active inequality constraint indices at
\bar{x}
taken from (
9.3
). The
positive linear independence constraint qualification
(PLICQ) is satisfied at
\bar{x}
if

{\bigg[\displaystyle\sum_{i\in I(\bar{x})\cup\{s+1,\ldots,m\}}\alpha_{i}\nabla\varphi_{i}(\bar{x})=0,\;\alpha_{i}\geq 0,\;i\in I(\bar{x})\bigg]\Longrightarrow\big[\alpha_{i}=0\;\mbox{ if }\;i\in I(\bar{x})\cup\{s+1,\ldots,m\}\big].}

(9.6)

Note that both of these constraint qualifications are robust with respect to small perturbations of
\bar{x}
. In fact, (
9.6
) is a dual form of the
Mangasarian-Fromovitz constraint qualification
(MFCQ) at
\bar{x}
.
It follows from
[
15
, Lemma 7.2]
that PLICQ and LICQ are equivalent to FOCQ (
7.6
) and SOCQ (
7.7
), respectively, and thus our major MSCQ (
7.8
) is weaker than PLICQ. For the NLP model (
9.1
), we can equivalently describe MSCQ (
7.8
) via the existence of a neighborhood

of
\bar{x}
and

such that

{\rm dist}(x;\Gamma)\leq\kappa\left(\sum_{i=1}^{s}\max\{\varphi_{i}(x),0\}+\sum_{i=s+1}^{m}|\varphi_{i}(x)|\right)\;\text{ for all }\;x\in U.

(9.7)

To derive second-order necessary and sufficient conditions for (

10
Concluding Remarks

This paper establishes new second-order necessary and sufficient optimality conditions for both unconstrained and constrained optimization problems in finite-dimensional spaces that utilize second-order subdifferentials of extended-real-valued prox-regular functions. The obtained results resolve, in particular, some challenging open questions formulated in the recent paper
[
1
, Section 5]
.

Our future research will address developing the second-order subdifferential approach of this paper to deriving second-order necessary and sufficient optimality conditions for nonpolyhedral problems of conic programming and extended nonlinear programming expressed entirely in terms of the given data under the weakest constraint qualifications. We also aim at applying the obtained optimality conditions and associated results on variational convexity and variational sufficiency to the design and justification of advanced second-order numerical algorithms.

Mixed local-nonlocal quasilinear problems with critical nonlinearities
João Vitor da Silva, Alessio Fiscella, Victor A. Blanco Viloria

Abstract
. We study existence and multiplicity of nontrivial solutions of the following problem { − Δ p ​ u + ( − Δ p ) s ​ u = λ ​ | u | q − 2 ​ u + | u | p ∗ − 2 ​ u in ​ Ω , u = 0 on ​ ℝ N ∖ Ω , \left\{\begin{array}[]{rcll}-\Delta_{p}u+(-\Delta_{p})^{s}u&=&\lambda|u|^{q-2}u+|u|^{p^{\ast}-2}u&\mbox{ in }\Omega,\\ u&=&0&\mbox{ on }\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega,\end{array}\right. where Ω ⊂ ℝ N \Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N} is a bounded open set with smooth boundary, dimension N ≥ 2 N\geq 2 , parameter λ > 0 \lambda>0 , exponents 0 < s < 1 < p < N 0<s<1<p<N , while q ∈ ( 1 , p ∗ ) q\in(1,p^{\ast}) with p ∗ = N ​ p N − p p^{\ast}=\frac{Np}{N-p} . The problem is driven by an operator of mixed order obtained by the sum of the classical p p -Laplacian and of the fractional p p -Laplacian. We analyze three different scenarios depending on exponent q q . For this, we combine variational methods with some topological techniques, such as the Krasnoselskii genus and the Lusternik-Schnirelman category theories.

1.
Introduction

In the present paper we deal with the following problem

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcll}-\Delta_{p}u+(-\Delta_{p})^{s}u&=&\lambda|u|^{q-2}u+|u|^{p^{\ast}-2}u&\mbox{ in }\Omega,\\
u&=&0&\mbox{ on }\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega,\end{array}\right.

(1.1)

where
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
is a bounded open set with smooth boundary, dimension
N\geq 2
, parameter
\lambda>0
, exponents
0<s<1<p<N
, while
q\in(1,p^{\ast})
with
p^{\ast}=\frac{Np}{N-p}
.
In the left hand side of (
1.1
) we have the classical
p
-Laplace operator
\Delta_{p}u=\text{div}(|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u)
plus its fractional counterpart which, up to normalization factor, can be set as

(-\Delta_{p})^{s}u(x)=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{\varepsilon}(x)}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p-2}(u(x)-u(y))}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}dy,

along any
u\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
.

Recently, the interest on nonlinear problems driven by operators of mixed type has grown more and more, in connection with the study of optimal animal foraging strategies, see for example
[
13
]
. We should also highlight the relevant contributions in the following topics: Hong-Krahn-Szegö inequality
[
6
]
, Faber-Krahn inequality and applications
[
7
]
, system of eigenvalue problems and their asymptotic limit as
p\to\infty

[
9
]
, mixed models with concave-convex nonlinearities and their asymptotic limit as
p\to\infty

[
11
]
, local/non-local equations of Hénon type
[
26
]
, regularity theory for mixed models
[
12
]
,
[
16
]
and
[
29
]
, and higher Hölder regularity for mixed local and nonlocal
elliptic models
[
17
]
. Mathematically speaking, this operator offers quite relevant challenges caused by the combination of nonlocal difficulties with the lack of invariance under scaling.

In this context, (
1.1
) can be considered as a ”Brézis-Nirenberg” problem of mixed local-nonlocal type, being a generalization of the classical one studied in
[
5
]
. Actually, the problem in
[
5
]
was generalized in a quasilinear situation in
[
20
]
and in the fractional case in
[
27
]
. We also recall
[
8
]
where the authors studied (
1.1
) when
p=2
, proving the existence of a nontrivial solution of (
1.1
) when
q\in[p,p^{\ast})
. The main aim of the present paper is to generalize
[
8
]
with
p\not=2
and
q\in(1,p^{\ast})
, providing existence and multiplicity results for (
1.1
) depending on the value of
q
.

We first study (
1.1
) in the sublinear case, namely with
q\in(1,p)
. By topological arguments based on the Krasnoselskii genus theory, we can state the following result.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
0<s<1<p<N
and let
q\in(1,p)
.

Then, there exists
\lambda_{\ast}>0
such that for any
\lambda\in(0,\lambda_{\ast})
problem (
1.1
) admits infinite many nontrivial solutions with negative energy.

Theorem
1.1
generalizes the multiplicity result proved in the classical case in
[
20
, Theorem 4.5]
.

In order to study (
1.1
) in the linear case, we apply the
classical cohomological index theory
as in
[
1
]
. For this, we first need to introduce the eigenvalues of nonlinear operator
\mathcal{L}_{p,s}(\cdot):=-\Delta_{p}(\cdot)+(-\Delta_{p})^{s}(\cdot)
. We recall here the eigenvalue problem

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcll}-\Delta_{p}u+(-\Delta_{p})^{s}u&=&\lambda|u|^{p-2}u&\mbox{ in }\Omega,\\
u&=&0&\mbox{ on }\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega.\end{array}\right.

(1.2)

Then, we set as
\{\lambda_{k}\}_{k}
the sequence of eigenvalues nontrivially solving (
1.2
) with parameter
\lambda=\lambda_{k}
. We also recall the best constant of Sobolev embedding for
W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
as

\mathcal{S}=\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}v\in W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)\\
v\not\equiv 0\end{subarray}}\displaystyle\frac{\|\nabla v\|_{p}^{p}}{\|v\|_{p^{\ast}}^{p}},

(1.3)

which is fundamental to state the range of
\lambda
in the following bifurcation result.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
0<s<1<p<N
and let
q=p
. Let
\widetilde{\lambda}=\frac{\mathcal{S}}{|\Omega|^{p/N}}
, where
\mathcal{S}
is set in (
1.3
).

Then, we have:

(i)

if
\lambda_{k+1}-\widetilde{\lambda}<\lambda<\lambda_{1}
, problem (
1.1
) admits a pair of nontrivial solutions
\pm u_{\lambda}
such that
u_{\lambda}\to 0
when
\lambda\to\lambda_{1}
;

(ii)

if
\lambda_{k}\leq\lambda<\lambda_{k+1}=\ldots=\lambda_{k+m}<\lambda_{k+m+1}
for some
k
,
m\in\mathbb{N}
and
\lambda>\lambda_{k+1}-\widetilde{\lambda}
, problem (
1.1
) admits
m
distinct pairs of nontrivial solutions
\pm u_{j,\lambda}
, with
j=1,\ldots,m
, such that
u_{j,\lambda}\to 0
when
\lambda\to\lambda_{k+1}
.

Theorem
1.2
complements the multiplicity result proved in
[
24
, Theorem 1.1]
.

For the superlinear case, with
q\in(p,p^{\ast})
, we need to set the parameters

m_{N,p,s}:=\min\left\{\frac{N-p}{p-1},p(1-s)\right\},\qquad\beta_{N,q,p}:=N-q\frac{N-p}{p}.

(1.4)

Thus, we have the following multiplicity result, strongly depending on
\Omega
.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
N>p
,
0<s<1
and
2\leq p<q<p^{\ast}
satisfy
m_{N,p,s}>\beta_{N,q,p}
.

Then, there exists
\lambda_{{\ast}{\ast}}>0
such that for any
\lambda\in(0,\lambda_{{\ast}{\ast}})
problem (
1.1
) admits
\text{cat}_{\Omega}(\Omega)
nontrivial solutions.

For the proof of Theorem
1.3
, we exploit the Lusternik-Schnirelman category theory. Theorem
1.3
complements
[
2
, Theorem 1]
and it is consistent with the pure fractional case in
[
15
, Theorem 1.1]
with
p=2
. However, the presence of local terms in (
1.1
) affects the multiplicity in Theorem
1.3
. Indeed, when
p=2
, if we consider
N>4-2s
(which occurs when either
N\geq 4
or
N=3
and
s>1/2
) then
m_{N,2,s}:=\min\left\{N-2,2(1-s)\right\}=2(1-s)
, so that

m_{N,2,s}>\beta_{N,q,2}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad q>2^{\ast}-\frac{2(2-2s)}{N-2}.

On the other hand, in
[
15
, Theorem 1.1]
the authors assume that

N>\frac{2s(q+2)}{q}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad q>2^{\ast}_{s}-2,

with
2^{\ast}_{s}=\frac{2N}{N-2s}
. By a simple calculation, we can see that

2^{\ast}-\frac{2(2-2s)}{N-2}>2^{\ast}_{s}-2,

whenever
N>4-2s
.

Last but not least, we want to investigate (
1.1
) when
\lambda
is sufficiently large. Indeed, in Theorem
1.3
we need to control
\lambda
in a suitable bounded range. However, we can state the following existence result, covering also the case when
1<p<2
.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
N>p
and
0<s<1<p<q<p^{\ast}
.

Then, problem (
1.1
) admits at least one nontrivial solution, under the following cases:

(i)

if
m_{N,p,s}>\beta_{N,q,p}
, for any
\lambda>0
;

(ii)

if
m_{N,p,s}\leq\beta_{N,q,p}
, for any
\lambda\geq\lambda^{\ast}
with a suitable
\lambda^{\ast}>0
.

We observe that the dichotomy in Theorem
1.4
is strongly affected by the presence of the nonlocal term in (
1.1
). Indeed, when
s=1
we get
m_{N,p,1}=0
so that always
m_{N,p,1}\leq\beta_{N,q,p}
from (
1.4
), since
q<p^{\ast}
.
Furthermore, as well explained in Remark
5.1
, the joint requests
p<q<p^{\ast}
and
m_{N,p,s}>\beta_{N,q,p}
imply that

q>\max\left\{p,p^{\ast}-\frac{p}{p-1}\right\},

which is assumed in
[
20
, Theorem 3.3]
. For this, Theorem
1.4
complements the classical situation contained in
[
20
, Theorems 3.2 and 3.3]
and generalizes
[
8
, Theorem 1.5]
.

we introduce the variational setting of (

) and we provide the compactness property of the related energy functional. In Section

we prove Theorem

. In Section

we first set the eigenvalues of (

) and then we prove Theorem

. In Section

. In Section

.

2.
Variational setting

From now on, without further mentioning, we suppose that
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
is an open bounded set with smooth boundary and dimension
N\geq 2
. For any
r\in[1,\infty)
and any
E\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N}
, we denote by
L^{r}(E)
and
W^{1,r}(E)
the usual Lebesgue and Sobolev spaces, endowed with the norms
\|\cdot\|_{r}
and
\|\cdot\|_{r}+\|\nabla\cdot\|_{r}
, respectively.

We recall the homogeneous Sobolev space
W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
as the closure of
C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
with respect to the norm
\|\nabla\cdot\|_{p}
. However, in order to handle the boundary condition in (
1.1
), we need to work with functions set in the whole space
\mathbb{R}^{N}
. For this,
W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
is not enough and we consider the following functional space

\mathrm{X}_{0}(\Omega)=\left\{u\in W^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N}):\,\,u|_{\Omega}\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)\mbox{ and }u=0\mbox{ a.e. in }\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega\right\}

endowed with the norm

\|u\|_{\mathrm{X}_{0}}=\left(\|\nabla u\|_{p}^{p}+[u]_{s,p}^{p}\right)^{1/p},

where
\left[\,\cdot\,\right]_{s,p}
stands for the Gagliardo seminorm, given by

\displaystyle[u]_{s,p}=\left(\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2N}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+ps}}\,dx\,dy\right)^{1/p}.

It is well known that
\mathrm{X}_{0}(\Omega)
is a real separable and uniformly convex space. Also, by
[
9
, Lemma 2.1]
we have the following relation between the gradient seminorm and the Gagliardo one.

Lemma 2.1
.

There exists a constant
\mathrm{c}=\mathrm{c}(N,p,s,\Omega)>0
such that

\displaystyle\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2N}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+ps}}\,dx\,dy\leq\mathrm{c}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{p}\,dx,\qquad\mbox{for any }u\in\mathrm{X}_{0}(\Omega).

A function
u\in\mathrm{X}_{0}(\Omega)
is called a (weak) solution of problem (
1.1
) if

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u\cdot\nabla\varphi\,dx+\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2N}}\mathcal{A}u(x,y)\left[\varphi(x)-\varphi(y)\right]\,dx\,dy=\lambda\int_{\Omega}|u|^{q-2}u\varphi\,dx+\int_{\Omega}|u|^{p^{\ast}-2}u\varphi\,dx

for any
\varphi\in\mathrm{X}_{0}(\Omega)
, where for simplicity

\mathcal{A}u(x,y):=\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p-2}(u(x)-u(y))}{|x-y|^{N+ps}}.

(2.1)

Hence, problem (
1.1
) has a variational structure, since the (weak) solutions of (
1.1
) are critical points of
J_{\lambda}:\mathrm{X}_{0}(\Omega)\to\mathbb{R}
set as

J_{\lambda}(u):=\frac{1}{p}\|\nabla u\|_{\mathrm{X}_{0}}^{p}-\frac{\lambda}{q}\|u\|_{q}^{q}-\frac{1}{p^{\ast}}\|u\|_{p^{\ast}}^{p^{\ast}}.

We conclude this section studying the compactness properties of
J_{\lambda}
.
We say that
J_{\lambda}
satisfies the Palais-Smale condition at level
c\in\mathbb{R}
, (PS)
c
for short, if any sequence
\{u_{n}\}_{n}\subset\mathrm{X}_{0}(\Omega)
such that

\displaystyle J_{\lambda}(u_{n})\to c\quad\text{and}\quad J^{\prime}_{\lambda}(u_{n})\to 0\quad\text{in }\left(\mathrm{X}_{0}(\Omega)\right)^{\ast}\quad\text{as }n\to\infty,

(2.2)

admits a convergent subsequence in
\mathrm{X}_{0}(\Omega)
.

In order to prove the
(PS)_{c}
condition at a suitable range for
c
, we first need to handle the gradient seminorm appearing in
\|\cdot\|_{\mathrm{X}_{0}}
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\lambda>0
and let
\{u_{n}\}_{n}\subset\mathrm{X}_{0}(\Omega)
be a bounded sequence satisfying (
2.2
) with
c\in\mathbb{R}
. Then, up to a subsequence,
\nabla u_{n}(x)\to\nabla u(x)
a.e. in
\Omega
as
n\to\infty
.

Proof.

Since
\{u_{n}\}_{n}
is bounded in
\mathrm{X}_{0}(\Omega)
, there exists a subsequence, still denoted by
\{u_{n}\}_{n}
, and
u\in\mathrm{X}_{0}(\Omega)
such that

\begin{array}[]{ll}u_{n}\rightharpoonup u\quad\text{ in }\quad\mathrm{X}_{0}(\Omega),&\nabla u_{n}\rightharpoonup\nabla u\mbox{ in }\left[L^{p}(\Omega)\right]^{N},\\
|u_{n}(x)|\leq h(x)\,\text{ a.e. in }\,\Omega,\\
u_{n}\rightarrow u\mbox{ in }L^{r}(\Omega),&u_{n}(x)\to u(x)\hskip 1.42262pt\text{ a.e. in }\Omega,\end{array}

(2.3)

as
n\to\infty
with
r\in[1,p^{\ast})
and
h\in L^{p}(\Omega).

For any
\kappa\in\mathbb{N}
, let
T_{\kappa}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
be the truncation function defined by

T_{\kappa}(t):=\begin{cases}t&\text{if }|t|\leq\kappa,\\[4.30554pt]
\displaystyle\kappa\frac{t}{|t|}&\text{if }|t|>\kappa.\end{cases}

Let
\kappa\in\mathbb{N}
be fixed.
Then, by (
2.2
) we have

\displaystyle\text{o}_{n}(1)=

\displaystyle\left\langle J_{\lambda}^{\prime}\left(u_{n}\right),T_{\kappa}\left(u_{n}-u\right)\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\left|\nabla u_{n}\right|^{p-2}\nabla u_{n}\cdot\nabla T_{\kappa}\left(u_{n}-u\right)dx+\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2N}}\mathcal{A}u_{n}(x,y)[T_{\kappa}(u_{n}-u)(x)-T_{\kappa}(u_{n}-u)(y)]\,dx\,dy

\displaystyle-\lambda\int_{\Omega}\left|u_{n}\right|^{q-2}u_{n}T_{\kappa}\left(u_{n}-u\right)dx-\int_{\Omega}\left|u_{n}\right|^{p^{\ast}-2}u_{n}T_{\kappa}\left(u_{n}-u\right)dx,

(2.4)

as
n\to\infty
, since
\{T_{k}(u_{n}-u)\}_{n}
is bounded in
\mathrm{X}_{0}(\Omega)
.
By Hölder’s inequality and (

) we get

\displaystyle

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u\cdot\nabla T_{\kappa}\left(u_{n}-u\right)dx=0

(2.5)

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2N}}\mathcal{A}u(x,y)[T_{\kappa}(u_{n}-u)(x)-T_{\kappa}(u_{n}-u)(y)]\,dx\,dy=0.

By the boundedness of
\{u_{n}\}_{n}
and (
1.3
) we have

\left|\int_{\Omega}u_{n}^{p^{\ast}-2}u_{n}T_{\kappa}\left(u_{n}-u\right)dx\right|\leq\kappa\int_{\Omega}|u_{n}|^{p^{\ast}-1}dx\leq C\kappa,

(2.6)

with a constant

independent of

and
\kappa
.
Thus, by using (
2
)-(

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}

\displaystyle{\left[\int_{\Omega}\left[\left|\nabla u_{n}\right|^{p-2}\nabla u_{n}-|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u\right]\cdot\nabla T_{\kappa}\left(u_{n}-u\right)dx\right.}

\displaystyle\left.+\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2N}}\left[\mathcal{A}u_{n}(x,y)-\mathcal{A}u(x,y)\right]\left[T_{\kappa}(u_{n}-u)(x)-T_{\kappa}(u_{n}-u)(y)\right]\,dx\,dy\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\int_{\Omega}\left|u_{n}\right|^{p^{\ast}-2}u_{n}T_{\kappa}\left(u_{n}-u\right)dx.

Now, we prove the following claim.

Claim.

For any
(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2N}
we have that

\left[\mathcal{A}u_{n}(x,y)-\mathcal{A}u(x,y)\right]\left[T_{\kappa}(u_{n}-u)(x)-T_{\kappa}(u_{n}-u)(y)\right]\geq 0.

(2.8)

\mathbb{R}^{2N}
in 4 sets

\displaystyle S_{1}

\displaystyle=\left\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2N}:\,\,|(u_{n}-u)(x)|\leq\kappa,\quad|(u_{n}-u)(y)|\leq\kappa\right\}

\displaystyle S_{2}

\displaystyle=\left\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2N}:\,\,|(u_{n}-u)(x)|\leq\kappa<|(u_{n}-u)(y)|\right\}

\displaystyle S_{3}

\displaystyle=\left\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2N}:\,\,|(u_{n}-u)(y)|\leq\kappa<|(u_{n}-u)(x)|\right\}

\displaystyle S_{1}

\displaystyle=\left\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2N}:\,\,|(u_{n}-u)(x)|>\kappa,\quad|(u_{n}-u)(y)|>\kappa\right\}

and we study case by case.

\bullet

Case 1. Let
(x,y)\in S_{1}
.
We have that

\displaystyle\left[\mathcal{A}u_{n}(x,y)-\mathcal{A}u(x,y)\right]

\displaystyle\left[T_{\kappa}(u_{n}-u)(x)-T_{\kappa}(u_{n}-u)(y)\right]

\displaystyle=\left[\mathcal{A}u_{n}(x,y)-\mathcal{A}u(x,y)\right]\left[(u_{n}-u)(x)-(u_{n}-u)(y)\right]\geq 0

where the last inequality follows by the well-known Simon’s inequalities, see
[
28

\bullet

Case 2. Let
(x,y)\in S_{2}
.
From

, we have

(u_{n}-u)(x)-\kappa\leq 0,&{\mbox{ if $u_{n}(x)<u(x)$ and $u_{n}(y)\geq u(y)$}}\\

While, since

, we get

\left\{\begin{matrix}\mathcal{A}u_{n}(x,y)-\mathcal{A}u(x,y)\leq 0,&{\mbox{ if $u_{n}(x)\geq u(x)$ and $u_{n}(y)\geq u(y)$}}\\
\mathcal{A}u_{n}(x,y)-\mathcal{A}u(x,y)\geq 0,&{\mbox{ if $u_{n}(x)\geq u(x)$ and $u_{n}(y)<u(y)$}}\\

Thus, summing up we obtain (

).

\bullet

(x,y)\in S_{3}
.
It is specular to the Case 2.

\bullet

.
It is trivial, since

This concludes the proof of the Claim.

Then, by (

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\int_{\Omega}\left[\left|\nabla u_{n}\right|^{p-2}\nabla u_{n}-|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u\right]\cdot\nabla T_{\kappa}\left(u_{n}-u\right)dx

\displaystyle\leq C\kappa.

We set

By using again the Simon’s inequalities in

, we see that

Lemma 2.3

\bullet

\bullet

3.
Sublinear case

In this section, we prove Theorem
1.1
. For this, we assume along the section that
q\in(1,p)
without further mentioning. Firstly, we recall the definition of genus and some its fundamental properties; see
[
25
]
for more details.

Let
E
be a Banach space and
A
a subset of
E
. We say that
A
is symmetric if
u\in A
implies that
-u\in A
. For a closed symmetric set
A
which does not contain the origin, we define the genus
\gamma(A)
of
A
as the smallest integer
k
such that there exists an odd mapping
\phi\in C^{0}(A;\mathbb{R}^{k}\setminus\{0\})
. If there does not exist such a
k
, we put
\gamma(A)=\infty
. Moreover, we set
\gamma(\emptyset)=0
.

Then we have the following result.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
A
and
B
be closed symmetric subsets of
E
which do not contain the origin. Then we have:

(i)

if there exists an odd continuous mapping from
A
to
B
, then
\gamma(A)\leq\gamma(B)
;

(ii)

if there is an odd homeomorphism from
A
onto
B
, then
\gamma(A)=\gamma(B)
;

(iii)

if
\gamma(B)<\infty
, then
\gamma(A\setminus B)\geq\gamma(A)-\gamma(B)
;

(iv)

the
k
-dimensional sphere
\mathbb{S}^{k}
has a genus of
k+1
by the Borsuk-Ulam Theorem;

(v)

if
A
is compact, then
\gamma(A)<\infty
and there exist
\delta>0
and a closed and symmetric neighborhood
N_{\delta}(A)=\{x\in E:\,\,\text{dist}(x,A)\leq\delta\}
of
A
such that
\gamma(N_{\delta}(A))=\gamma(A)
.

We also need the following deformation lemma, as given in
[
4
, Lemma 1.3]
. For this, considering
I\in C^{1}(E;\mathbb{R})
, we set for any

K_{c}=\left\{u\in E:\,\,I^{\prime}(u)=0\mbox{ and }I(u)=c\right\}.

(3.1)

Also, we use the following notation

for

.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
E
be an infinite-dimensional Banach space and let

be a functional satisfying the
(PS)_{c}
, with

. Let

be any neighborhood of

. Then, there exists
\eta\in C^{0}([0,1]\times E;E)

\eta_{t}(x)=\eta(t,x)
, and constants

,
\varepsilon>0
with
|c|>d_{1}
, such that:

(i)

\eta_{0}=Id_{E}
;

(ii)

\eta_{t}(u)=u
for any

and

;

(iii)

\eta_{t}
is a homeomorphism for any

;

(iv)

I(\eta_{t}(u))\leq I(u)
for any

(v)

\eta_{1}(I^{c+d_{1}}\setminus U)\subseteq I^{c-d_{1}}

(vi)

(vii)

is odd for any

.

Being

, let us note that

.
Thus, we will apply a truncation argument, inspired by
[

]
.
By (

) and Sobolev embedding, for any

J_{\lambda}(u)\geq\displaystyle\frac{1}{p}\|u\|_{\mathrm{X}_{0}}^{p}-\lambda\frac{\,C_{q}}{q}\|u\|_{\mathrm{X}_{0}}^{q}-\frac{\mathcal{S}^{p^{\ast}/p}}{p^{\ast}}\|u\|_{\mathrm{X}_{0}}^{p^{\ast}}=g_{\lambda}(\|u\|_{\mathrm{X}_{0}})

where

Lemma 3.4

Let

.

. By parts

and
(iv)

concluding the proof.

Let us set the numbers

c_{k}=\displaystyle\inf_{A\in\Sigma_{k}}\sup_{u\in A}\widetilde{J}_{\lambda}(u),

(3.8)

with

\Sigma_{k}=\left\{A\subset\mathrm{X}_{0}(\Omega):\,\,A\mbox{ is closed,\, symmetric such that }\,0\not\in A\,\mbox{ and }\,\gamma(A)\geq k\right\}.

Clearly

for any

.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
\lambda>0
and let
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Then,
c_{k}<0
.

Proof.

Fix
\lambda>0
and
k\in\mathbb{N}
.
By Lemma
3.4
, there exists
\varepsilon>0
such that
\gamma(\widetilde{J}_{\lambda}^{-\varepsilon})\geq k
. Furthermore,
\widetilde{J}_{\lambda}^{-\varepsilon}\in\Sigma_{k}
since
\widetilde{J}_{\lambda}
is continuous and even. Taking into account that
\widetilde{J}_{\lambda}(0)=0
, we have

and
\displaystyle\sup_{u\in\widetilde{J}_{\lambda}^{-\varepsilon}}\widetilde{J}_{\lambda}(u)\leq-\varepsilon
. Therefore, remembering also that

is bounded from below, we conclude

-\infty<c_{k}=\inf_{A\in\Sigma_{k}}\sup_{u\in A}\widetilde{J}_{\lambda}(u)\leq\sup_{u\in\widetilde{J}_{\lambda}^{-\varepsilon}}\widetilde{J}_{\lambda}(u)\leq-\varepsilon<0.

∎

Now, we state the following technical lemma, with

K_{c}=\left\{u\in\mathrm{X}_{0}(\Omega):\,\,\widetilde{J}^{\prime}_{\lambda}(u)=0\,\mbox{ and }\,\widetilde{J}_{\lambda}(u)=c\right\}.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
\lambda\in(0,\lambda_{\ast})
, with
\lambda_{\ast}
as in Lemma
3.3

. If

, for some

Proof.

Fix
\lambda\in(0,\lambda_{\ast})
and
k\in\mathbb{N}
.
By Lemma
3.5
we have

, so we can apply part
(ii)
of Lemma
3.3
to see

is compact.

Let us assume by contradiction that

.
Then, by part
(v)
of Proposition
3.1
there exists

such that

\gamma(N_{\delta}(K_{c}))=\gamma(K_{c})\leq m
where

Thus, by Proposition
3.2
there exists
\varepsilon\in(0,-c)
and an odd homeomorphism

\eta:\mathrm{X}_{0}(\Omega)\to\mathrm{X}_{0}(\Omega)
such that

\eta(\widetilde{J}_{\lambda}^{c+\varepsilon}\setminus N_{\delta}(K_{c}))\subset\widetilde{J}_{\lambda}^{c-\epsilon}.

(3.9)

By definition of

in (

), there exists

that is

and so by (

(3.10)

On the other hand, by parts
(i)
and
(iii)
of Proposition
3.1
, we get

Hence,
\eta(\overline{A\setminus N_{\delta}(K_{c})})\in\Sigma_{k}
, and so

\sup_{u\in\eta(\overline{A\setminus N_{\delta}(K_{c})})}\widetilde{J}_{\lambda}(u)\geq c_{k}=c

which contradicts (

). Thus, we conclude
\gamma(K_{c})\geq m+1
.
∎

Finally, we are in a position to prove Theorem
1.1
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Let
\lambda\in(0,\lambda_{\ast})
with
\lambda_{\ast}
given in Lemma
3.3
.
By Lemma
3.5
we have

. Hence, from part

of Lemma

we know that the functional

satisfies the

for any

We distinguish two situations.

, then

admits infinitely many critical values.
If there exist

Then, by part

∎

Lemma 4.3

(A_{2})

\bullet

\displaystyle\text{o}_{n}(1)=

\lambda^{\ast}=\lambda^{\ast}(\varepsilon)>0
such that

\sup_{t\geq 0}J_{\lambda}(tv_{\varepsilon})<\frac{1}{N}\mathcal{S}^{N/p}\quad\mbox{ for any }\lambda\geq\lambda^{\ast}.

Proof.

Let
\varepsilon>0
and
\lambda>0
, we have

J_{\lambda}(tv_{\varepsilon})=\frac{t^{p}}{p}\left(\|\nabla v_{\varepsilon}\|_{p}^{p}+[v_{\varepsilon}]_{s,p}^{p}\right)-\lambda\frac{t^{q}}{q}\|v_{\varepsilon}\|_{q}^{q}-\frac{t^{p^{\ast}}}{p^{\ast}},

(5.14)

from which we distinguish the two cases.

\bullet

Case (i).

Since by
[
19
, Lemma 7.1]
we know that

\|u_{\varepsilon}\|_{p^{\ast}}^{p^{\ast}}=\mathcal{S}^{N/p}+\mathrm{O}(\varepsilon^{N/(p-1)}),

by Lemmas
5.1
-
5.3
it follows that

J_{\lambda}(tv_{\varepsilon})\leq\frac{t^{p}}{p}\left(\mathcal{S}+\mathrm{O}(\varepsilon^{m_{N,p,s}})\right)-C\lambda\frac{t^{q}}{q}\varepsilon^{\beta_{N,q,p}}-\frac{t^{p^{\ast}}}{p^{\ast}}.

(5.15)

Let us set

f_{\varepsilon,\lambda}(t):=\frac{t^{p}}{p}\left(\mathcal{S}+C\varepsilon^{m_{N,p,s}}\right)-C\lambda\frac{t^{q}}{q}\varepsilon^{\beta_{N,q,p}}-\frac{t^{p^{\ast}}}{p^{\ast}},

such that
f_{\varepsilon,\lambda}(0)=0
and
f_{\varepsilon,\lambda}(t)\to-\infty
as
t\to\infty
. Hence, there exists
t_{\varepsilon,\lambda}\geq 0
such that

\sup_{t\geq 0}f_{\varepsilon,\lambda}(t)=f_{\varepsilon,\lambda}(t_{\varepsilon,\lambda}).

If
t_{\varepsilon,\lambda}=0
, then we can conclude easily the proof of the lemma.
While, for
t_{\varepsilon,\lambda}>0
, we get

0=f^{\prime}_{\varepsilon,\lambda}(t_{\varepsilon,\lambda})=t_{\varepsilon,\lambda}^{p-1}\left(\mathcal{S}+C\varepsilon^{m_{N,p,s}}\right)-C\lambda t_{\varepsilon,\lambda}^{q-1}\varepsilon^{\beta_{N,q,p}}-t_{\varepsilon,\lambda}^{p^{\ast}-1},

(5.16)

from which

t_{\varepsilon,\lambda}<\left(\mathcal{S}+C\varepsilon^{m_{N,p,s}}\right)^{1/(p^{\ast}-p)}.

On the other hand, for
\varepsilon>0
sufficiently small, by (
5.16
) we get
t_{\varepsilon,\lambda}\geq\mu_{\lambda}>0
so that, using also the fact that the map

t\longmapsto\frac{t^{p}}{p}\left(\mathcal{S}+C\varepsilon^{m_{N,p,s}}\right)-\frac{t^{p^{\ast}}}{p^{\ast}}

is increasing in the closed interval
\left[0,\left(\mathcal{S}+C\varepsilon^{m_{N,p,s}}\right)^{1/(p^{\ast}-p)}\right]
containing
t_{\varepsilon,\lambda}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\sup_{t\geq 0}f_{\varepsilon,\lambda}(t)

\displaystyle=f_{\varepsilon,\lambda}(t_{\varepsilon,\lambda})

\displaystyle<\frac{\left(\mathcal{S}+C\varepsilon^{m_{N,p,s}}\right)^{1+p/(p^{\ast}-p)}}{p}-C\varepsilon^{\beta_{N,q,p}}-\frac{\left(\mathcal{S}+C\varepsilon^{m_{N,p,s}}\right)^{p^{\ast}/(p^{\ast}-p)}}{p^{\ast}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\left(\mathcal{S}+C\varepsilon^{m_{N,p,s}}\right)^{N/p}-C\varepsilon^{\beta_{N,q,p}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{N}\mathcal{S}^{N/p}+C\varepsilon^{m_{N,p,s}}-C\varepsilon^{\beta_{N,q,p}}

\displaystyle<\frac{1}{N}\mathcal{S}^{N/p}

for still
\varepsilon>0
sufficiently small, since
m_{N,p,s}>\beta_{N,q,p}
in the last inequality. This concludes the proof joint with (
5.15
).

\bullet

Case (ii).

We can not apply the previous proof, as discussed in Remark
5.1
. Since
p<q<p^{\ast}
, by (
5.14
) we have
J_{\lambda}(tv_{\varepsilon})\to-\infty
as
t\to\infty
. From this, considering also that
J_{\lambda}(0)=0
and
J_{\lambda}
is continuous, there exists
\overline{t}_{\lambda,\varepsilon}>0
such that
J_{\lambda}(\overline{t}_{\lambda,\varepsilon}v_{\varepsilon})=\sup_{t\geq 0}J_{\lambda}(tv_{\varepsilon})
.
Hence, we have

\langle J^{\prime}_{\lambda}(\overline{t}_{\lambda,\varepsilon}v_{\varepsilon}),v_{\varepsilon}\rangle=0

which yields

\overline{t}_{\lambda,\varepsilon}^{\,p-1}\|v_{\varepsilon}\|_{\mathrm{X}_{0}}^{p}-\lambda\overline{t}_{\lambda,\varepsilon}^{\,q-1}\|v_{\varepsilon}\|_{q}^{q}-\overline{t}_{\lambda,\varepsilon}^{\,p^{\ast}-1}=0.

(5.17)

From this, we get that
\{\overline{t}_{\lambda,\varepsilon}\}_{\lambda}\subset\mathbb{R}^{+}
is bounded since
p<q<p^{\ast}
. Thus, combining (
5.17
) with

\lambda\overline{t}_{\lambda,\varepsilon}^{\,q-1}\|v_{\varepsilon}\|_{q}^{q}\to\infty,\quad\mbox{as }\lambda\to\infty,

we deduce that
\overline{t}_{\lambda,\varepsilon}\to 0
as
\lambda\to\infty
. From this, since
J_{\lambda}
is continuous, we obtain

\sup_{t\geq 0}J_{\lambda}(tv_{\varepsilon})=J_{\lambda}(\overline{t}_{\lambda,\varepsilon}v_{\varepsilon})\to 0,\quad\mbox{as }\lambda\to\infty,

concluding the proof.
∎

Now, we can verify the mountain pass geometry for
J_{\lambda}
.

Lemma 5.5
.

Let
\lambda>0
and
\varepsilon>0
be set as in Lemma
5.4
. Then, there exist
\alpha
,
\rho>0
such that:

(i)

for any
u\in\mathrm{X}_{0}(\Omega)
with
\|u\|_{\mathrm{X}_{0}}=\rho
we have
J_{\lambda}(u)\geq\alpha
;

(ii)

there exists a
t_{\varepsilon}>0
sufficiently large, so that
\|t_{\varepsilon}v_{\varepsilon}\|_{\mathrm{X}_{0}}>\rho
and
J_{\lambda}(t_{\varepsilon}v_{\varepsilon})<\alpha
, with
v_{\varepsilon}
as given in Lemma
5.4
.

Proof.

By the Sobolev embedding and (
1.3
), we have

J_{\lambda}(u)\geq\frac{1}{p}\|u\|_{\mathrm{X}_{0}}^{p}-\lambda C_{q}\|u\|_{\mathrm{X}_{0}}^{q}-\mathcal{S}^{p^{\ast}/p}\|u\|_{\mathrm{X}_{0}}^{p^{\ast}},

for any
u\in\mathrm{X}_{0}(\Omega)
. Since
p<q<p^{\ast}
, we can easily get part
(i)
assuming
\|u\|_{\mathrm{X}_{0}}
sufficiently small.
On the other hand, we have

\lim_{t\to\infty}J_{\lambda}(tv_{\varepsilon})=-\infty,

from which we can conclude the proof.
∎

Now, we are able to prove Theorem
1.4
.

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

Let
\lambda>0
and
\varepsilon>0
be set as in Lemma
5.4
.
Thanks to Lemma
5.5
, we can set the critical mountain pass level as

m_{\lambda}=\inf_{\xi\in\Gamma}\max_{t\in[0,1]}J_{\lambda}(\xi(t)),

(5.18)

where

\Gamma=\left\{\xi\in C^{0}([0,1];\mathrm{X}_{0}(\Omega)):\,\,\xi(0)=0,\,\,\xi(1)=t_{\varepsilon}v_{\varepsilon}\right\}.

By Lemma
5.4
we have

m_{\lambda}\leq\sup_{t\geq 0}J_{\lambda}(tv_{\varepsilon})<\frac{1}{N}\mathcal{S}^{N/p},

in both cases
(i)
and
(ii)
, so that we can apply part
(i)
of Lemma
2.4
at level
m_{\lambda}
. Thus, condition
(PS)_{m_{\lambda}}
is verified and by the classical mountain pass theorem, see
[
31
, Theorem 1.15]
, we can conclude the proof.

∎

6.
Superlinear case: category theory

In this section, we prove Theorem
1.3
. For this, we assume along the section that
2\leq p<q<p^{\ast}
satisfy
m_{N,p,s}>\beta_{N,q,p}
given in (
1.4
), without further mentioning.

In order to get the existence of multiple critical points for the functional
J_{\lambda}
, we will use the Lusternik-Schnirelman theory, as developed in
[
23
]
. This approach allows to find critical points of our functional
J_{\lambda}
on a suitable manifold, exploiting the topological properties of the manifold itself. For this, we set the
Nehari manifold
associated to
J_{\lambda}
as

\mathcal{N}_{\lambda}=\left\{v\in\mathrm{X}_{0}(\Omega)\setminus\{0\}:\,\,\langle J^{\prime}_{\lambda}(v),v\rangle=0\right\}.

It is easy to see that
\mathcal{N}_{\lambda}
is not empty.

The idea is to apply the theory as written in
[
31
, Chapter 5]
. For this, let us define
\psi_{\lambda}:\mathrm{X}_{0}(\Omega)\to\mathbb{R}
as

\psi_{\lambda}(u)=\langle J^{\prime}_{\lambda}(u),u\rangle=\|\nabla u\|_{p}^{p}+[u]_{s,p}^{p}-\lambda\|u\|_{q}^{q}-\|u\|_{p^{\ast}}^{p^{\ast}}.

We have that
\psi_{\lambda}\in C^{2}(\mathrm{X}_{0}(\Omega);\mathbb{R})
being
p\geq 2
, with in particular

\langle\psi^{\prime}_{\lambda}(u),u\rangle=p\left(\|\nabla u\|_{p}^{p}+[u]_{s,p}^{p}\right)-\lambda q\|u\|_{q}^{q}-p^{\ast}\|u\|_{p^{\ast}}^{p^{\ast}}.

From this, we obtain

\displaystyle\langle\psi^{\prime}_{\lambda}(u),u\rangle=\lambda(p-q)\|u\|_{q}^{q}+(p-p^{\ast})\|u\|_{p^{\ast}}^{p^{\ast}}<0\qquad\mbox{for any }u\in\mathcal{N}_{\lambda},

(6.1)

so that the structural assumptions on
[
31
, Section 5.3]
are satisfied by
J_{\lambda}
,
\psi_{\lambda}
and
\mathcal{N}_{\lambda}
.

In the spirit of
[
31
]
, we look for critical points of
J_{\lambda}
on
\mathcal{N}_{\lambda}
. In order to construct these critical points, by
[
31
, Proposition 5.12 and Definition 5.17]
we set a new compactness condition. We say that functional
J_{\lambda}
restricted on
\mathcal{N}_{\lambda}
satisfies the Palais-Smale condition at level
c
,
(PS)_{c}^{\mathcal{N}_{\lambda}}
for short, if any sequence
\{u_{n}\}_{n}\subset\mathcal{N}_{\lambda}
such that

\displaystyle J_{\lambda}(u_{n})\to c\quad\text{and}\quad\min_{\gamma\in\mathbb{R}}\|J^{\prime}_{\lambda}(u_{n})-\gamma\psi^{\prime}_{\lambda}(u_{n})\|_{\left(\mathrm{X}_{0}(\Omega)\right)^{\ast}}\to 0\quad\text{as }n\to\infty,

(6.2)

admits a convergent subsequence in
\mathrm{X}_{0}(\Omega)
.

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
\lambda>0
and let
\mathcal{S}
be as in (
1.3
). Then, the
(PS)_{c}^{\mathcal{N}_{\lambda}}
condition holds true for any
c<\frac{1}{N}\mathcal{S}^{N/p}
.

Proof.

Let
\{u_{n}\}_{n}\subset\mathcal{N}_{\lambda}
satisfy (
6.2
) with
c<\frac{1}{N}\mathcal{S}^{N/p}
. Since
u_{n}\in\mathcal{N}_{\lambda}
, so that
\langle J^{\prime}_{\lambda}(u_{n}),u_{n}\rangle=0
, we can argue as in Lemma
2.3
to prove that
\{u_{n}\}_{n}
is bounded in
\mathrm{X}_{0}(\Omega)
. From this, we can see that
\left\{\langle\psi^{\prime}_{\lambda}(u_{n}),u_{n}\rangle\right\}_{n}\subset\mathbb{R}
is bounded so that, taking into account (
6.1
), there exists
\ell\in(-\infty,0]
such that, up to a subsequence, we have

\langle\psi^{\prime}_{\lambda}(u_{n}),u_{n}\rangle\to\ell\quad\text{as }n\to\infty.

(6.3)

Furthermore, by (
6.2
) there exists
\{\gamma_{n}\}_{n}\subset\mathbb{R}
such that

\displaystyle\|J^{\prime}_{\lambda}(u_{n})-\gamma_{n}\psi^{\prime}_{\lambda}(u_{n})\|_{\left(\mathrm{X}_{0}(\Omega)\right)^{\ast}}\to 0\quad\text{as }n\to\infty.

(6.4)

Now, we split the proof in two situations.

•

If
\ell<0
, then by (
6.4
) combined with (
6.3
) and the fact that
\{u_{n}\}_{n}\subset\mathcal{N}_{\lambda}
is bounded, we get that
\gamma_{n}\to 0
as
n\to\infty
. From this fact used in (
6.4
), we practically see that
\{u_{n}\}_{n}
satisfies (
2.2
), so that we conclude by Lemma
2.4
.

•

If
\ell=0
, by (
6.1
) and (
6.3
) we obtain

\|u_{n}\|_{q}\to 0\quad\mbox{and}\quad\|u_{n}\|_{p^{\ast}}\to 0\quad\mbox{as }\,\,n\to\infty.

From this, since
u_{n}\in\mathcal{N}_{\lambda}
we get that
u_{n}\to 0
in
\mathrm{X}_{0}(\Omega)
as
n\to\infty
.

∎

Now, arguing as in case
(i)
of Lemma
5.4
we provide the following control for
J_{\lambda}
in
\mathcal{N}_{\lambda}
. For this, we strongly need that
m_{N,p,s}>\beta_{N,q,p}
.

Lemma 6.2
.

Let
\lambda>0
and let
\mathcal{S}
be set as in (
1.3
). Then, we have

\inf_{v\in\mathcal{N}_{\lambda}}J_{\lambda}(v)<\frac{1}{N}\mathcal{S}^{N/p},

(6.5)

and there exists
u_{\lambda}\in\mathcal{N}_{\lambda}
such that

J_{\lambda}(u_{\lambda})=\inf_{v\in\mathcal{N}_{\lambda}}J_{\lambda}(v).

Proof.

Let
\varepsilon>0
and let
v_{\varepsilon}
be as in (
5.3
). Let us consider

\psi_{\lambda}(tv_{\varepsilon})=t^{p}\left(\|\nabla v_{\varepsilon}\|_{p}^{p}+[v_{\varepsilon}]_{s,p}^{p}\right)-\lambda t^{q}\|v_{\varepsilon}\|_{q}^{q}-t^{p^{\ast}}=t^{p}h_{\varepsilon,\lambda}(t)

where

h_{\varepsilon,\lambda}(t):=\|\nabla v_{\varepsilon}\|_{p}^{p}+[v_{\varepsilon}]_{s,p}^{p}-\lambda t^{q-p}\|v_{\varepsilon}\|_{q}^{q}-t^{p^{\ast}-p}.

Of course,
h_{\varepsilon,\lambda}
is a continuous function with
h_{\varepsilon,\lambda}(0)>0
and
h_{\varepsilon,\lambda}(t)\to-\infty
as
t\to\infty
. Hence, there exists
t_{\varepsilon,\lambda}>0
such that
h_{\varepsilon,\lambda}(t_{\varepsilon,\lambda})=0
, namely

\displaystyle\psi_{\lambda}(t_{\varepsilon,\lambda}v_{\varepsilon})=\langle J^{\prime}_{\lambda}(t_{\varepsilon,\lambda}v_{\varepsilon}),t_{\varepsilon,\lambda}v_{\varepsilon}\rangle=t_{\varepsilon,\lambda}^{p}\left(\|\nabla v_{\varepsilon}\|_{p}^{p}+[v_{\varepsilon}]_{s,p}^{p}\right)-\lambda t_{\varepsilon,\lambda}^{q}\|v_{\varepsilon}\|_{q}^{q}-t_{\varepsilon,\lambda}^{p^{\ast}}=0

(6.6)

so that
t_{\varepsilon,\lambda}u_{\varepsilon}\in\mathcal{N}_{\lambda}
.

By (
6.6
), Lemmas
5.1
and
5.3
, we get

0=t_{\varepsilon,\lambda}^{p}\left(\|\nabla v_{\varepsilon}\|_{p}^{p}+[v_{\varepsilon}]_{s,p}^{p}\right)-\lambda t_{\varepsilon,\lambda}^{q}\|v_{\varepsilon}\|_{q}^{q}-t_{\varepsilon,\lambda}^{p^{\ast}}\leq t_{\varepsilon,\lambda}^{p}\left(\mathcal{S}+C\varepsilon^{m_{N,p,s}}\right)-t_{\varepsilon,\lambda}^{p^{\ast}}.

from which

t_{\varepsilon,\lambda}\leq\left(\mathcal{S}+C\varepsilon^{m_{N,p,s}}\right)^{1/(p^{\ast}-p)}.

(6.7)

By (
6.6
), considering (
1.3
) with
\|v_{\varepsilon}\|_{p^{\ast}}=1
, we also have

\lambda t_{\varepsilon,\lambda}^{q-p}\|v_{\varepsilon}\|_{q}^{q}+t_{\varepsilon,\lambda}^{p^{\ast}-p}=\|\nabla v_{\varepsilon}\|_{p}^{p}+[v_{\varepsilon}]_{s,p}^{p}\geq\mathcal{S}

so that by Sobolev embedding and Lemma
5.1
, we get

\mathcal{S}\leq\lambda t_{\varepsilon,\lambda}^{q-p}C\left(\mathcal{S}+C\varepsilon^{(N-p)/(p-1)}\right)+t_{\varepsilon,\lambda}^{p^{\ast}-p},

which implies that
t_{\varepsilon,\lambda}\geq\mu_{\lambda}>0
for
\varepsilon>0
sufficiently small. From this point, we can argue exactly as in the proof of case
(i)
of Lemma
5.4
to show that

J_{\lambda}(t_{\varepsilon,\lambda}v_{\varepsilon})<\frac{1}{N}\mathcal{S}^{N/p},

which immediately yields (
6.5
).

Finally, by (
6.2
), Lemma
6.1
and
[
3
, Theorem 7.12]
, we get that there exists
u_{\lambda}\in\mathcal{N}_{\lambda}
such that

J_{\lambda}(u_{\lambda})=\inf_{v\in\mathcal{N}_{\lambda}}J_{\lambda}(v),

concluding the proof.
∎

Let us introduce the following notation

\mathcal{D}^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})=\left\{u\in L^{p^{\ast}}(\mathbb{R}^{N}):\,\,\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{i}}\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),\,\,\text{weak derivatives},\,\,i=1,\ldots,N\right\}.

Then, by a classical argument in
[
22
, Theorem I.1]
, see also
[
2
, Lemma 3.1]
, we can state the following technical lemma.

Lemma 6.3
.

Let
\{u_{n}\}_{n}\subset\mathrm{X}_{0}(\Omega)
be a sequence with
\|u_{n}\|_{p^{\ast}}=1
and
\|\nabla u_{n}\|_{p}^{p}\to\mathcal{S}
. Then, there exists a sequence
\{(y_{n},\theta_{n})\}_{n}\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{+}
such that
\{v_{n}\}_{n}
with

v_{n}(x):=\theta_{n}^{(N-p)/p}u_{n}(\theta_{n}x+y_{n})

admits a convergent subsequence, still denoted by
\{v_{n}\}_{n}
, such that
v_{n}\to v
in
\mathcal{D}^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. Moreover,
\theta_{n}\to 0
and
y_{n}\to y\in\overline{\Omega}
.

Since
\Omega
is a smooth and bounded domain of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, we can choose
r>0
small enough so that the following sets

\Omega_{r}^{+}=\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}:\,\,\text{dist}(x,\Omega)<r\right\},\qquad\Omega_{r}^{-}=\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}:\,\,\text{dist}(x,\Omega)>r\right\}

are homotopically equivalent to
\Omega
. We can also assume that
B_{r}(0)\subset\Omega
.

From now on, we consider

\mathrm{X}_{0,rad}(B_{r}(0))=\left\{u\in\mathrm{X}_{0}(B_{r}(0)):\,\,u\mbox{ is radial}\right\}

and we set
J_{\lambda,B_{r}}:\mathrm{X}_{0,rad}(B_{r}(0))\to\mathbb{R}
given as

J_{\lambda,B_{r}}(u)=\frac{1}{p}\left(\int_{B_{r}(0)}|\nabla u|^{p}dx+\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2N}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+ps}}\,dx\,dy\right)-\frac{\lambda}{q}\int_{B_{r}(0)}|u|^{q}dx-\frac{1}{p^{\ast}}\int_{B_{r}(0)}|u|^{p^{\ast}}dx.

Then, setting for simplicity

\mathcal{N}_{\lambda,B_{r}}=\left\{v\in\mathrm{X}_{0,rad}(B_{r}(0))\setminus\{0\}:\,\,\langle J_{\lambda,B_{r}}^{\prime}(v),v\rangle=0\right\}

we can easily see that all previous results proved for
J_{\lambda}
are valid also for
J_{\lambda,B_{r}}
. In particular, considering that
v_{\varepsilon}
in (
5.3
) is radial, by Lemma
6.2
there exists
u_{\lambda,B_{r}}\in\mathcal{N}_{\lambda,B_{r}}
such that

J_{\lambda,B_{r}}(u_{\lambda,B_{r}})=\inf_{v\in\mathcal{N}_{\lambda,B_{r}}}J_{\lambda,B_{r}}(v)<\frac{1}{N}\mathcal{S}^{N/p}.

(6.8)

From this function
u_{\lambda,B_{r}}
, denoting
m(\lambda):=J_{\lambda,B_{r}}(u_{\lambda,B_{r}})
for simplicity, we can set the sublevel

J_{\lambda}^{m(\lambda)}=\left\{u\in\mathcal{N}_{\lambda}:\,\,J_{\lambda}(u)\leq m(\lambda)\right\}

and the function
\zeta_{\lambda}:\Omega_{r}^{-}\to J_{\lambda}^{m(\lambda)}
as

\zeta_{\lambda}(y)(x)=\begin{cases}u_{\lambda,B_{r}}(x-y)&\text{if }x\in B_{r}(y),\\[4.30554pt]
0&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

(6.9)

for any
y\in\Omega_{r}^{-}
, which is well defined.
Let us set the barycenter map
\beta_{\lambda}:\mathcal{N}_{\lambda}\to\mathbb{R}^{N}
as

\beta_{\lambda}(u):=\frac{1}{\mathcal{S}^{N/p}}\int_{B_{r}(y)}x|u|^{p^{\ast}}dx.

Then, for any
y\in\Omega_{r}^{-}
, we have

\displaystyle\left(\beta_{\lambda}\circ\zeta_{\lambda}\right)(y)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\mathcal{S}^{N/p}}\int_{\Omega}x|u_{\lambda,B_{r}}(x-y)|^{p^{\ast}}dx=\frac{1}{\mathcal{S}^{N/p}}\int_{\Omega}(z+y)|u_{\lambda,B_{r}}(z)|^{p^{\ast}}dz

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\mathcal{S}^{N/p}}\int_{\Omega}z|u_{\lambda,B_{r}}(z)|^{p^{\ast}}dz+\frac{y}{\mathcal{S}^{N/p}}\int_{\Omega}|u_{\lambda,B_{r}}(z)|^{p^{\ast}}dz

\displaystyle=y\frac{\|u_{\lambda,B_{r}}\|_{p^{\ast}}^{p^{\ast}}}{\mathcal{S}^{N/p}}

where last equality follows from the fact that
u_{\lambda,B_{r}}
is radial.

Lemma 6.4
.

There exists
\lambda_{{\ast}{\ast}}>0
such that for any
\lambda\in(0,\lambda_{{\ast}{\ast}})
we have that if
u\in J_{\lambda}^{m(\lambda)}
then
\beta_{\lambda}(u)\in\Omega_{r}^{+}
.

Proof.

Let us argue by contradiction, assuming there exist
\{\lambda_{n}\}_{n}\subset\mathbb{R}^{+}
and
\{u_{n}\}_{n}\subset J_{\lambda_{n}}^{m(\lambda_{n})}
such that
\lambda_{n}\to 0
but
\beta_{\lambda_{n}}(u_{n})\notin\Omega_{r}^{+}
. Since
u_{n}\in J_{\lambda_{n}}^{m(\lambda_{n})}
, we get

\frac{1}{p}\|u_{n}\|_{\mathrm{X}_{0}}^{p}-\frac{\lambda_{n}}{q}\|u_{n}\|_{q}^{q}-\frac{1}{p^{\ast}}\|u_{n}\|_{p^{\ast}}^{p^{\ast}}=J_{\lambda_{n}}(u_{n})\leq m(\lambda_{n})

(6.10)

and

\|u_{n}\|_{\mathrm{X}_{0}}^{p}-\lambda_{n}\|u_{n}\|_{q}^{q}-\|u_{n}\|_{p^{\ast}}^{p^{\ast}}=\langle J^{\prime}_{\lambda_{n}}(u_{n}),u_{n}\rangle=0.

(6.11)

Combining (
6.10
) and (
6.11
), arguing as in Lemma
2.3
, we easily get the boundedness of
\{u_{n}\}_{n}\subset\mathrm{X}_{0}(\Omega)
and so of
\{\|u_{n}\|_{q}\}_{n}\subset\mathbb{R}^{+}
. Thus, by (
6.10
) we get

\frac{1}{p}\|u_{n}\|_{\mathrm{X}_{0}}^{p}-\frac{1}{p^{\ast}}\|u_{n}\|_{p^{\ast}}^{p^{\ast}}\leq m(\lambda_{n})+\text{o}_{n}(1)

(6.12)

and by (
6.11
)

\|u_{n}\|_{\mathrm{X}_{0}}^{p}-\lambda_{n}\|u_{n}\|_{q}^{q}-\|u_{n}\|_{p^{\ast}}^{p^{\ast}}=\text{o}_{n}(1),

(6.13)

as
n\to\infty
. By (
6.12
) and (
6.13
), it follows that

\frac{1}{N}\|u_{n}\|_{\mathrm{X}_{0}}^{p}\leq m(\lambda_{n})+\text{o}_{n}(1)

which yields, jointly with (
6.8
), that

\|u_{n}\|_{\mathrm{X}_{0}}^{p}\leq\mathcal{S}^{N/p}+\text{o}_{n}(1)

(6.14)

as
n\to\infty
.

Let us set
w_{n}=u_{n}/\|u_{n}\|_{p^{\ast}}
. Using (
1.3
), (
6.13
) and (
6.14
), we have

\mathcal{S}\leq\|\nabla w_{n}\|_{p}^{p}\leq\|w_{n}\|_{\mathrm{X}_{0}}^{p}=\frac{\|u_{n}\|_{\mathrm{X}_{0}}^{p}}{\|u_{n}\|_{p^{\ast}}^{p}}\leq\|u_{n}\|_{\mathrm{X}_{0}}^{p-p^{2}/p^{\ast}}+\text{o}_{n}(1)\leq\mathcal{S}+\text{o}_{n}(1)

as
n\to\infty
. Hence, we construct a sequence
\{w_{n}\}_{n}\subset\mathrm{X}_{0}(\Omega)
such that
\|w_{n}\|_{p^{\ast}}=1
and
\|\nabla w_{n}\|_{p}^{p}\to S
as
n\to\infty
.
By Lemma
6.3
, there exists a sequence
\{(y_{n},\theta_{n})\}_{n}\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{+}
such that the sequence
\{v_{n}\}_{n}
set as

v_{n}(x)=\theta_{n}^{(N-p)/p}w_{n}(\theta_{n}x+y_{n})

converges, up to a subsequence, to some
v\in W^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. By (
6.13
) we get

\mathcal{S}\|u_{n}\|_{p^{\ast}}^{p}+\text{o}_{n}(1)=\|\nabla u_{n}\|_{p}^{p}\leq\|u_{n}\|_{\mathrm{X}_{0}}^{p}=\|u_{n}\|_{p^{\ast}}^{p^{\ast}}+\text{o}_{n}(1)

which implies

\|u_{n}\|_{p^{\ast}}^{p^{\ast}}\geq\mathcal{S}^{N/p}+\text{o}_{n}(1)

(6.15)

as
n\to\infty
.
Similarly, by (
6.14
) we get

\mathcal{S}\|u_{n}\|_{p^{\ast}}^{p}+\text{o}_{n}(1)=\|\nabla u_{n}\|_{p}^{p}\leq\|u_{n}\|_{\mathrm{X}_{0}}^{p}\leq\mathcal{S}^{N/p}+\text{o}_{n}(1)

\|u_{n}\|_{p^{\ast}}^{p^{\ast}}\leq\mathcal{S}^{N/p}+\text{o}_{n}(1)

(6.16)

as
n\to\infty
.
Thus, by (
6.15
) and (
6.16
) we conclude that

\|u_{n}\|_{p^{\ast}}^{p^{\ast}}\to\mathcal{S}^{N/p}\quad\mbox{as }n\to\infty.

(6.17)

We observe that

\beta_{\lambda_{n}}(u_{n})=\frac{1}{\mathcal{S}^{N/p}}\int_{\Omega}x|u_{n}|^{p^{\ast}}dx=\frac{\|u_{n}\|_{p^{\ast}}^{p^{\ast}}}{\mathcal{S}^{N/p}}\int_{\Omega}x|w_{n}|^{p^{\ast}}dx.

For
\phi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
be such that
\phi(x)=x

, we get by the Lebesgue Dominated Convergence Theorem

\beta_{\lambda_{n}}(u_{n})=\frac{\|u_{n}\|_{p^{\ast}}^{p^{\ast}}}{\mathcal{S}^{N/p}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\phi(x)|w_{n}(x)|^{p^{\ast}}dx=\frac{\|u_{n}\|_{p^{\ast}}^{p^{\ast}}}{\mathcal{S}^{N/p}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\phi(\theta_{n}x+y_{n})|v_{n}(x)|^{p^{\ast}}dx\to y\in\overline{\Omega}

as

, since also

by Lemma
6.3
and considering (

). This gives the desired contradiction.
∎

Lemma 6.5
.

Let
\lambda_{{\ast}{\ast}}>0
be as in Lemma
6.4
. Then, for any
\lambda\in(0,\lambda_{{\ast}{\ast}})
we have that

\text{cat}_{J_{\lambda}^{m(\lambda)}}\left(J_{\lambda}^{m(\lambda)}\right)\geq cat_{\Omega}(\Omega).

Proof.

Suppose that
\text{cat}_{J_{\lambda}^{m(\lambda)}}\left(J_{\lambda}^{m(\lambda)}\right)=M
, that is

J_{\lambda}^{m(\lambda)}=\Theta_{1}\cup\ldots\cup\Theta_{M},

(6.18)

where
\Theta_{j}
is closed and contractible in

, for

. This means that there exist

By (

∎

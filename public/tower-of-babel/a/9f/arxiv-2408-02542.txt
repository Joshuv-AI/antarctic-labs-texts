Given a regular pair

Theorem 1.1

(
[

]
)
.

Let

be a regular pair of codimension

, and let

be the associated closed immersion. Let

(1.1)

Ri^{!}\mathbb{Z}/l(n)[2r]\cong i^{*}\mathbb{Z}/l(n-r).

1.2.
Semi-purity of the étale cohomology with coefficients in the log de Rham-Witt sheaves

Assume that
(X,Z)
is a smooth pair over a perfect field
k
of characteristic
p
. Let
S:=\textrm{Spec}(k)
. Our goal is to construct a purity isomorphism analogous to (
1.1
), replacing
\mathbb{Z}/l
with
\mathbb{Z}/p^{m}
. However, defining
\mathbb{Z}/p^{m}(n)
as
\mu_{p^{m}}^{\otimes n}
does not make sense, since the group scheme
\mu_{p^{m}}\cong\textrm{Spec}(k[T]/(T^{p^{m}}-1))
is not an étale sheaf.

In
[
22
, Theorem 2.1]
and
[
8
, Théorème 3.5.8]
, Milne and Gros presented an alternative, namely the logarithmic de Rham-Witt sheaf
\nu_{m}(n)
(cf.
Definition

4.22
) and proved a semi-purity theorem for it. Namely that canonical isomorphisms

(1.2)

R^{j}i^{!}\nu_{X,m}(n)=\begin{cases}0&\textrm{ if }j\neq r,r+1\\
\nu_{Z,m}(n-r)&\textrm{ if }j=r\end{cases}

exist.

The remaining term
R^{r+1}i^{!}\nu_{X,m}(n)
is the obstruction to full purity, and it may fail to vanish, as we show in
Example

1.2
.

The logarithmic de Rham-Witt sheaves
\nu_{X,1}(n)
are characterized by the short exact sequence (cf.
[
21
]
):

(1.3)

0\to\nu_{X,1}(n)\to Z\Omega^{n}_{X/S}\xrightarrow{C-1}\Omega^{n}_{X/S}\to 0,

where
C
denotes the Cartier operator (see
[
21
, Lemma 1.1]
for its definition and existence) and
Z\Omega^{n}_{X/S}
is the sheaf of closed
n
-forms.

Example 1.2
.

Consider the case where
X=\textrm{Spec}(k[T])
and
Z=V(T)
. Then
R^{2}i^{!}\nu_{X,1}(0)=R^{2}i^{!}\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}
is non-zero.

Proof that
R^{2}i^{!}\nu_{X,1}(0)
is non-zero.

The claim is étale local, hence we may assume that
X
is strictly henselian. Let
A:=k[T]^{\mathrm{sh}}_{(T)}
be a strict henselization of
k[T]
at the ideal
(T)
and replace
X
with
\textrm{Spec}(A)
.
As in the proof of
[
22
, Theorem 2.1]
, the long exact sequence of cohomology groups associated to (
1.3
) is

0\to R^{1}i^{!}\nu_{X,1}(n)\to R^{1}i^{!}Z\Omega_{X/k}^{n}\xrightarrow{C-1}R^{1}i^{!}\Omega_{X/k}^{n}\to R^{2}i^{!}\nu_{X,1}(n)\to 0.

Hence it is enough to prove that
R^{1}i^{!}Z\Omega_{X/k}^{n}\xrightarrow{C-1}R^{1}i^{!}\Omega_{X/k}^{n}
is not surjective.

When
n=0
,
\Omega^{0}_{X/k}=\mathcal{O}_{X}
and
Z\Omega^{0}_{X/k}=\mathcal{O}_{X}^{p}
. Thus
R^{1}i^{!}Z\Omega^{0}_{X/k}
and
R^{1}i^{!}\Omega^{0}_{X/k}
are isomorphic to the quasi-coherent sheaves associated to
(A[T^{-1}])^{p}/A^{p}
and
A[T^{-1}]/A
respectively.

The map
C-1
is identified with the Artin-Schreier map

F^{-1}-1:(A[T^{-1}])^{p}/A^{p}\to A[T^{-1}]/A,\quad\gamma^{p}\mapsto\gamma-\gamma^{p}.

Since the equation
\gamma-\gamma^{p}=1/T
has no solution
\gamma
in
A[T^{-1}]^{p}
, the element
1/T
does not lie in the image of
F^{-1}-1
, and hence
R^{2}i^{!}\nu_{X,1}(0)
is non-zero.
∎

1.3.
Purity of the tame cohomology in positive characteristic

As purity fails in the étale topology, we work with the
tame topology
instead, defined in
[
11
]
. Roughly speaking, this is the topology whose covers are étale morphisms that are tamely ramified at the boundary. For instance, Artin–Schreier covers that ramify at the boundary are excluded. For more details, see
[
11
]
or
Section

3
of this document. For a scheme
X
over a field
k
, we denote the tame site by
(X/k)_{t}
.

We now state the main theorem of this article as well as a sketch of the proof.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
X
be a smooth scheme over a perfect field
k
. Let
i:Z\hookrightarrow X
be a smooth closed immersion of pure codimension
r
. Assume that resolution of singularities and embedded resolution of singularities (as stated in
Conjecture

B.1
and
Conjecture

B.3
) hold. Then there is an isomorphism in the derived category of tame sheaves
D((Z/k)_{t})
:

\nu_{Z,m}(n-r)[-r]\xrightarrow{\sim}Ri^{!}\nu_{X,m}(n).

Remark 1.4
.

The following sketch of the proof uses discretely ringed adic spaces, introduced in
Section

3
. We define
\mathcal{X}:=\textrm{Spa}(X,k)
and
\mathcal{Z}:=\textrm{Spa}(Z,k)
. The idea of the proof should remain accessible even without a full familiarity with adic spaces.

The adic version of the theorem states that

\nu_{\mathcal{Z},m}(n-r)[-r]\xrightarrow{\sim}Ri^{!}\nu_{\mathcal{X},m}(n)

is an isomorphism in
D(\mathcal{Z}_{t})
. The two formulations are equivalent by
[
11
, §14]
.

Sketch of the proof.

(1)

Under the assumption of resolution of singularities, we prove the existence of the short exact sequence (
Theorem

)

0\to\nu_{\mathcal{X}}(n)\to Z\Omega^{+,n}_{\mathcal{X}/k}\xrightarrow{C-1}\Omega^{+,n}_{\mathcal{X}/k}\to 0.

by the main theorem of

is a log smooth log scheme. It is well-known that

(2)

2.
Logarithmic schemes and their differentials

Logarithmic schemes provide a natural framework for defining logarithmic differentials, which arise in the study of purity for
p
-torsion sheaves in characteristic
p
. This section recalls the basic definitions and properties of logarithmic schemes and their differentials, and discusses normal crossing divisors and the acyclicity of blow-ups, which are key tools for our proof of purity.

For more details on logarithmic schemes and their differentials, we refer the reader to
[
25
]
.

2.1.
Logarithmic schemes

In this paper, a monoid is assumed to be commutative and to have a unit element.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
X
be a scheme. A
prelog structure
on
X
is a morphism of sheaves of monoids

\alpha_{X}:\mathcal{M}_{X}\to\mathcal{O}_{X}

on the small étale site
X_{\textrm{\'{e}t}}
, where
\mathcal{O}_{X}
is considered as a multiplicative monoid. We say that
\alpha_{X}
is a
log structure
if

\alpha_{X}^{-1}(\mathcal{O}_{X}^{\times})\xrightarrow{\alpha_{X}}\mathcal{O}_{X}^{\times}

is an isomorphism. A log scheme is a pair
(X,\mathcal{M}_{X})
where
X
is a scheme and
\alpha_{X}:\mathcal{M}_{X}\to\mathcal{O}_{X}
is a log structure. Here the morphism
\alpha_{X}
is a part of the structure but we generally choose to omit it from the notation.

A morphism
(X,\mathcal{M}_{X})\to(Y,\mathcal{M}_{Y})
of log schemes is a pair
(f,f^{\flat})
where
f:X\to Y
is a morphism of schemes and
f^{\flat}:f^{-1}\mathcal{M}_{Y}\to\mathcal{M}_{X}
is a morphism of monoids which respects the log structure.

To each prelog structure
\alpha_{X}:\mathcal{M}_{X}\to\mathcal{O}_{X}
we define the associated log structure as the pushout
\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{log}}_{X}:=\mathcal{M}_{X}\bigoplus_{\alpha_{X}^{-1}(\mathcal{O}_{X}^{\times})}\mathcal{O}_{X}^{\times}
together with the natural morphism
\tilde{\alpha}_{X}:\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{log}}_{X}\to\mathcal{O}_{X}
. The log structure
\tilde{\alpha}_{X}
is called the
logification
of the prelog structure
\alpha_{X}
.

A morphism of logarithmic schemes
Y\to X
is
strict
, if the log structure of
Y
is the pullback of the log structure of
X
, i.e.
\mathcal{M}_{Y}=f^{*}\mathcal{M}_{X}
which is the logification of the prelog structure
f^{-1}\mathcal{M}_{X}\to f^{-1}\mathcal{M}_{X}\to\mathcal{M}_{Y}
.

Example 2.2
.

Let
X
be a scheme and
U\subseteq X
an open subscheme. Then we define a log structure, called the
compactifying log structure

\alpha_{U/X}:\mathcal{M}_{U/X}\to\mathcal{O}_{X}

as follows. For every étale morphism
V\to X
we set

\mathcal{M}_{U/X}(V)=\{f\in\mathcal{O}_{X}(V)\mid f|_{U\times_{X}V}\in\mathcal{O}_{X}(U\times_{X}V)^{\times}\},

and
\alpha_{U/X}
is the natural inclusion
\mathcal{M}_{U/X}(V)\hookrightarrow\mathcal{O}_{X}(V)
.

Notation 2.3
.

Let
X
be a log scheme. Denote the underlying scheme by
X^{\circ}
. Define

X_{\textrm{tr}}:=\{x\in X^{\circ}|\mathcal{M}_{X,x}=\mathcal{O}_{X,x}^{\times}\}.

2.2.
Logarithmic differentials

Let
f:X\to S
be a morphism of prelog schemes. Denote by
\alpha:\mathcal{M}_{X}\to\mathcal{O}_{X}
the structure morphism of
X
. Let
\mathcal{E}
be an
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-module. An
S
-derivation
from
(\mathcal{O}_{X},\mathcal{M}_{X})
to
\mathcal{E}
is a pair
(d,\mathrm{dlog})
of maps, where
d:\mathcal{O}_{X}\to\mathcal{E}
is an
S
-derivation and
\mathrm{dlog}:\mathcal{M}_{X}\to\mathcal{E}
is a morphism of monoids satisfying:

•

d(\alpha(m))=\alpha(m)\mathrm{dlog}(m)
for each section
m\in\mathcal{M}_{X}
,

•

\mathrm{dlog}(f^{\flat}(m))=0
for each section
m\in\mathcal{M}_{S}
.

Note that if
f:X\to S
is a morphism of log schemes induced by a morphism of prelog schemes
X^{\mathrm{pre}}\to S^{\mathrm{pre}}
, then the derivations are the same.

Lemma 2.4
.

[
25
, Chapter IV, Theorem 1.2.4]

Let
f:X\to S
be a morphism of prelog schemes. The functor associating to an
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-module
\mathcal{E}
the set of
S
-derivations is represented by the
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-module of
logarithmic Kähler differentials

\Omega^{\log}_{X/S}
, together with the
universal
S
-derivation

(d,\mathrm{dlog})
.

Proof Idea.

The module
\Omega^{\log}_{X/S}
can be described as the quotient of

\Omega_{X^{\circ}/S^{\circ}}\bigoplus(\mathcal{O}_{X}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathcal{M}_{X}^{\textrm{gp}})

by the submodule generated by all elements of the form:

•

d(\alpha_{X}(a))-\alpha_{X}(a)\otimes a
where
a
is a section of
\mathcal{M}_{X}

•

0+1\otimes f^{\flat}(b)
where
b
is a section of
\mathcal{M}_{S}
.∎

Elements of the form
0+1\otimes a
are denoted by
d\log a
for a section
a
of
\mathcal{M}_{X}
. The first equivalence relation should tautologically correspond to

\mathrm{dlog}(x)=dx/x.

Notation 2.5
.

•

For simplicity, we write
\Omega_{X/S}
instead of
\Omega_{X^{\circ}/S^{\circ}}
.

•

X
be a scheme over a base scheme
S
. Let

with the compactifying log structure for

Example 2.6

(
[

2.3.
Normal Crossing Divisors

Let
D=D_{1}\cup\cdots\cup D_{r}
be a strict normal crossing divisor (snc divisor) on a scheme
X

25
, Chapter III, Definition 1.8.1]
. We consider the compactifying log structure on

which is associated to the open subscheme
X\backslash D
. By definition, the irreducible components

are locally cut out by certain

\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r}\mathbb{N}\to\mathcal{O}_{X},\;(n_{1},\dots,n_{r})\mapsto(f_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdots f_{r}^{n_{r}}).

In this subsection we are interested in properties of such log structures and their differentials.

Lemma 2.7

(
[

Let

The following proposition describes the structure of logarithmic differentials

(Residue sequences, cf.

Let

We follow the proof in the reference. The condition that

For the first sequence define the map locally as

After applying the snake lemma, we get the sequence

\displaystyle 0\to

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces x}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\epsilon}

3.2.
The Riemann-Zariski open site

Horizontal specializations

Let
\mathcal{X}
be a discretely ringed adic space. We say that a point
y\in\mathcal{X}
specializes to
x\in\mathcal{X}
if every open neighborhood of
x
contains
y
. We recall the definition of horizontal (or primary) specializations.

Definition 3.2

(horizontal specialization)
.

Let
\mathcal{X}=\textrm{Spa}(A,A^{+})
be a discretely ringed affinoid adic space. A point of
\mathcal{X}
is a valuation
v:A\to\Gamma_{v}\cup\{0\}
satisfying
v(a)\leq 1
for all
a\in A^{+}
. Consider a convex subgroup
H\subset\Gamma_{v}
which bounds
A
, i.e. for all
a\in A
, there exists
h\in H
s.t.
v(a)\leq h
. One defines

v|H:A\to H\cup\{0\}:a\mapsto\begin{cases}v(a)&\textrm{if }v(a)\in H,\\
0&\textrm{ otherwise.}\end{cases}

This is a specialization of
v
. Such specializations are called
horizontal
(or primary).

A specialization
y\succ x
in a discretely ringed adic space
\mathcal{Y}
is horizontal if there exists an affinoid open set containing
x
for which
y\succ x
is horizontal.

A point
x\in\mathcal{Y}
is called
Riemann-Zariski
(RZ) if it has no nontrivial horizontal specialization.

The set of Riemann-Zariski points is called the Riemann-Zariski space. For a separated morphism
X\to S
of qcqs schemes, we define the topological space

\textrm{RZ}(X,S)=\{\textrm{Riemann-Zariski points of }\textrm{Spa}(X,S)\},

together with the underlying topology of
\textrm{Spa}(X,S)

Comparison between the open and Riemann-Zariski open sites

Consider the following morphisms

\textrm{RZ}(X,S)\xrightarrow{\iota}\textrm{Spa}(X,S)\xrightarrow{\pi}\textrm{RZ}(X,S).

defined by Temkin
[
29
]
. The first morphism is the inclusion, which is a topological embedding. The second morphism sends
x\in\textrm{Spa}(X,S)
to its RZ-point
x_{\textrm{RZ}}
, which is the maximal horizontal specialization of
x
. The composition
\pi\circ\iota
is the identity on
\textrm{RZ}(X,S)
. In particular,
\textrm{RZ}(X,S)
is a retract of
\textrm{Spa}(X,S)
.

The next proposition is featured in greater generality in
[
16
, Corollary 5.7]

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
X\to S
be a separated morphism of qcqs schemes. We have a natural isomorphism of cohomology groups

H^{i}(\textrm{Spa}(X,S),\mathcal{F})\cong H^{i}(RZ(X,S),\pi_{*}\mathcal{F}).

Proof.

It suffices to check that
R\pi_{*}\mathcal{F}
is concentrated in degree zero. To prove that we check that
\pi_{*}
is equal to
\iota^{*}
, which is exact.

Let
\mathcal{U}
be an open subset of
\textrm{RZ}(X,S)
. Then
\mathcal{U}=\pi^{-1}(\mathcal{U})\cap\textrm{RZ}(X,S)
. Furthermore,
\pi^{-1}(\mathcal{U})
is the smallest open subset of
\textrm{Spa}(X,S)
containing
\mathcal{U}
: indeed, it is the set of horizontal generalizations of points in
\mathcal{U}
, and every open subset containing
\mathcal{U}
must contain all these generalizations.

By definition,
\iota^{*}\mathcal{F}
is the sheafification of

\mathcal{U}\mapsto\textrm{colim}_{\mathcal{V}}\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{V}),

where the colimit is taken over
\mathcal{V}
open in
\textrm{Spa}(X,S)
, such that
\mathcal{U}\to\textrm{RZ}(X,S)
factors through
\mathcal{V}\cap\textrm{RZ}(X,S)
. The open
\pi^{-1}(\mathcal{U})
is initial for such open morphisms, hence

\textrm{colim}_{\mathcal{V}}\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{V})=\mathcal{F}(\pi^{-1}(\mathcal{U}))=\pi_{*}\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{U}).

It follows that
\iota^{*}\mathcal{F}=\pi_{*}\mathcal{F}
, which concludes the proof.
∎

Let
X\to S
be a separated morphism of qcqs schemes. In

]

\textrm{RZ}(X,S)=\lim_{X\to\bar{Y}\to S}\bar{Y},

where

of

.

Notation 3.4
.

Let

be an

be a sheaf on the topological space

where

We apply

Differentials over adic spaces

4.1.
Rangers and definition of
\Omega^{+}

As usual, let
\mathcal{X}=\textrm{Spa}(X,S)
be a discretely ringed adic space over a perfect field
k
of positive characteristic
p
. The sheaf
\Omega^{+}
was introduced in
[
15
]
and is defined by

\Omega^{+}(\mathcal{U}):=\left\{\eta\in\Omega(\mathcal{U})\;\middle|\;|\eta|_{x}\leq 1\text{ for all }x\in\mathcal{U}\right\}

for a suitable definition of the seminorm

|\cdot|_{x}:\Omega_{A_{\mathrm{supp}(x)}}\to\mathcal{R}_{\Gamma_{x}},

known as the
Kähler seminorm
(see below). This construction originates in Temkin’s paper
[
30
]
, where he defines a similar Kähler seminorm on the module of differentials
\Omega_{K/k}
for an extension of real-valued fields
K/k
as

|\eta|_{\Omega}:=\inf_{\eta=\sum f_{i}dg_{i}}\max_{i}\{|f_{i}|_{K}|g_{i}|_{K}\}.

This definition, however, does not directly extend to local Huber pairs
(A,A^{+})
, as the value group
\Gamma_{A}
may lack infima. To address this, Hübner introduced the concept of
rangers
in
[
15
, §5]
, which were further applied to the study of adic curves in recent work by Hübner and Temkin
[
12
, 2.1.5]
.

Definition 4.1

(Rangers,
[
15
, §5]
,
[
12
, 2.1.5]
)
.

Let
\Gamma
be an ordered abelian group (or more generally, an ordered set). A
ranger
is a pair
(\Gamma^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime})
, where
\Gamma^{\prime}
is an ordered group (or ordered set) containing
\Gamma
, and
\gamma^{\prime}\in\Gamma^{\prime}
. Two rangers
(\Gamma_{1},\gamma_{1})
and
(\Gamma_{2},\gamma_{2})
are
equivalent
if there exists a ranger
(\Gamma^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime})
such that
\Gamma^{\prime}\subseteq\Gamma_{1}\cap\Gamma_{2}
, and
\gamma^{\prime}
maps to
\gamma_{1}
in
\Gamma_{1}
and to
\gamma_{2}
in
\Gamma_{2}
.

The set of equivalence classes of rangers is denoted
\mathcal{R}_{\Gamma}
. It is a totally ordered set.

Rangers are used to define the Kähler seminorm on modules of differentials over
local Huber pairs
. Let us shortly recall the definition of local Huber pairs: Let
\mathcal{X}
be an adic space and
x\in\mathcal{X}
. The
localization
of
\mathcal{X}
is the adic space

\mathcal{X}_{x}:=\lim\limits_{x\in\mathcal{U}\subseteq\mathcal{X}}\mathcal{U}.

If
\mathcal{X}
is a discretely ringed affinoid
\textrm{Spa}(A,A^{+})
, then

\textrm{Spa}(A,A^{+})_{x}=\textrm{Spa}(B,B^{+}),

where
B=A_{\mathrm{supp}(x)}
and
B^{+}
is the preimage of
k^{+}(x)\subset k(x)=A_{\mathrm{supp}(x)}/\mathfrak{m}_{\mathrm{supp}(x)}
in
A_{\mathrm{supp}(x)}=B
. In particular,
\mathcal{X}
is an affinoid adic space.

More generally, a Huber pair
(A,A^{+})
is called
local
if
A
is local with maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}
and
A^{+}
is the preimage of a valuation ring
\mathcal{O}\subseteq A/\mathfrak{m}
in
A
. In particular,
A^{+}
is a local ring with a valuation
|\cdot|
and a maximal ideal

\mathfrak{m}^{+}=\{a\in A|,|a|<1\}.

Note also that
A^{+}=\{a\in A|,|a|\leq 1\}
and

Definition 4.6
.

Let
(A,A^{+})
be a local Huber pair over a field

. Let

on

Proposition 4.7
.

For every integer
n
, we have the equality

(4.1)

\Omega_{(A,A^{+})}^{+,n}=\big\{\omega\in\Omega_{A}^{n};|\omega|\leq 1\big\}

Proof.

Let
\omega
be a differential in
\Omega_{(A,A^{+})}^{+,n}
. Then
\omega
is a sum of wedge products of elements of the form
fdg
satisfying
|fg|\leq 1

[
15
, Lemma 7.3.]
(cf.
Lemma

). Therefore
\omega=\sum\limits_{i}f_{i}dg_{1,i}\wedge\dots\wedge dg_{n,i}
such that
|f_{i}g_{1,i}\dots g_{n,i}|\leq 1
. Hence
\omega
is in the RHS of (
4.1
).

Consider
\omega\in\Omega_{A}^{n}
lying in the RHS of (
4.1
). According to
[
15
, Lemma 7.2]
, one may express
\omega
as a sum
\sum\limits_{i}f_{i}dg_{1,i}\wedge\dots\wedge dg_{n,i}
such that
|f_{i}g_{1,i}\dots g_{n,i}|\leq 1
. Thus the proof of
Proposition

4.7
is reduced to the following claim.

Claim 4.8
.

Let
f,g_{1},\dots,g_{n}
be elements in
A
such that
|fg_{1}\dots g_{n}|\leq 1
, then
\eta:=fdg_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge g_{n}\in\Omega_{(A,A^{+})}^{+,n}
.

We prove
Claim

4.8
inductively. When
n=1
this is true by definition. Assume that the claim is true for
n-1
.

Case 1:
|g_{1}|=\dots=|g_{n}|=0
. Then
fdg_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge d_{n-1}\in\Omega_{(A,A^{+})}^{+,n-1}
and
dg_{n}\in\Omega_{(A,A^{+})}^{+}
. Hence
\eta\in\Omega_{(A,A^{+})}^{+,n}
.

Case 2:
|fg_{1}\dots g_{n-1}|=0
and
|g_{n}|\neq 0
. Then express
\eta
as the wedge product

(fg_{n}dg_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge dg_{n-1})\wedge(dg_{n}/g_{n}).

Then
fg_{n}dg_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge dg_{n-1}\in\Omega_{(A,A^{+})}^{+,n-1}
due to the induction step and
dg_{n}/g_{n}\in\Omega_{(A,A^{+})}^{+}
by definition.

Case 3: We may now assume, without loss of generality, that
|f|,|g_{1}|,\dots,|g_{n}|\neq 0
. Then

\eta=(fg_{1}\dots g_{n})(dg_{1}/g_{1})\wedge\dots\wedge(dg_{n}/g_{n}),

lies in
\Omega_{(A,A^{+})}^{+,n}
.
∎

4.3.
The Cartier Operator

The Cartier operator for logarithmic differentials

Let
k
be a perfect field of positive characteristic. We write
S=\textrm{Spec}(k)
.

Let
\mathcal{X}=\textrm{Spa}(X,T)
be a smooth discretely ringed adic space over
k
. Assume that
\mathcal{X}=\textrm{Spa}(X,\bar{X})
where
\bar{X}
is a log smooth log scheme with the compactifying log structure associated to the open subscheme
X
. Denote the open immersion
X\to\bar{X}
by
j
.

We start by introducing some notation. Consider the absolue Frobenius endomorphism

F_{\textrm{abs}}:X\to X

induced by sending a section
\alpha
in
\mathcal{O}_{X}
to
\alpha^{p}
. We denote by
X^{(p)}
the pullback

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X^{(p)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces S}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces S}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces W}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi^{(p)}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F_{\textrm{abs}}}

The morphism
W
induces an isomorphism on
X^{(p)}\to X
. The structure morphism
\pi^{(p)}:X^{(p)}\to S
is associated to the morphism
\mathcal{O}_{S}\to\mathcal{O}_{X^{(p)}}(\cong\mathcal{O}_{X}):f\mapsto f^{\frac{1}{p}}
. On the level of topological spaces, the morphism
W
is just the identity.

The absolute Frobenius
F_{\textrm{abs}}:X\to X
induces the relative Frobenius
F:X\to X^{(p)}
.

Now note that
\Omega_{X^{(p)}/S}=F_{*}\Omega_{X/S}
. Similarly, if
D\subseteq X
is a normal crossing divisor, then
\Omega_{X^{(p)}/S}(\mathrm{log}D^{(p)})=F_{*}\Omega_{X/S}(\mathrm{log}D)
.

Let us first recall a theorem of Cartier, see
[
17
, Theorem 7.2]
.

Proposition 4.15

(Inverse Cartier operator)
.

Assume that
X
is smooth over the prefect field
k
.

There exists for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
an isomorphism

\bar{C}^{-1}:\Omega^{n}_{X^{(p)}/S}\to F_{*}\big(Z\Omega^{n}_{X/S}/B\Omega^{n}_{X/S}\big)

of
\mathcal{O}_{X^{(p)}}
-modules, which satisfies

•

\bar{C}^{-1}(1)=1
when
n=0
.

•

\bar{C}^{-1}(df)=[f^{p-1}df]
when
n=1
.

•

\bar{C}^{-1}(\omega\wedge\eta)=\bar{C}^{-1}(\omega)\wedge\bar{C}^{-1}(\eta)
.

If on the other hand
\bar{X}
has the structure of a log smooth log scheme then the same holds for the log differentials:

\bar{C}^{-1}:\Omega^{n,\mathrm{log}}_{\bar{X}^{(p)}/S}\to F_{*}(Z\Omega^{n,\mathrm{log}}_{\bar{X}/S}/B\Omega^{n,\log}_{\bar{X}/S}).

This isomorphism is compatible with

j_{*}\bar{C}^{-1}:j_{*}\Omega^{n}_{X^{(p)}/S}\to j_{*}F_{*}(Z\Omega^{n}_{X/S}/B\Omega^{n}_{X/S}),

where
j_{*}:X\to\bar{X}
is the open immersion associated to the trivializing subscheme
X
of
X
.

We apply the fact that
\Omega_{X^{(p)}/S}^{n,\mathrm{log}}
is isomorphic to
F_{*}\Omega_{X/S}^{n,\mathrm{log}}
as an
\mathcal{O}_{X^{(p)}}
-module, and hence as a group, to directly obtain the following proposition.

Proposition 4.16
.

There exists a unique family of additive maps
C:Z\Omega_{\bar{X}/k}^{n,\mathrm{log}}\to\Omega_{\bar{X}/k}^{n,\mathrm{log}}
with the properties

•

C(1)=1
in
\mathcal{O}_{\bar{X}}=\Omega_{\bar{X}/k}^{0,\mathrm{log}}
,

•

C(f^{p}\omega)=fC(\omega)
for each
f\in\Omega_{X},\omega\in Z\Omega_{X/k}^{r,\mathrm{log}}
,

•

C(\omega\wedge\omega^{\prime})=C(\omega)\wedge C(\omega^{\prime})
for closed
\omega
and
\omega^{\prime}
in
Z\Omega_{\bar{X}/k}^{r,\mathrm{log}}
,

•

C(\omega)=0
if and only if
\omega=d\eta
, where
\eta
is a logarithmic differential,

•

C(f^{p-1}df)=df
.

Furthermore, this is compatible with the pushforward of the Cartier operator

j_{*}C:j_{*}Z\Omega_{X/k}\to j_{*}\Omega_{X/k}.

Proof.

This follows directly from the previous proposition. The
\mathcal{O}_{X{(p)}}
-linearity translates to the second condition. The fact that
\bar{C}^{-1}
is an isomorphism corresponds to the fourth condition. The uniqueness of
\bar{C}^{-1}
is equivalent to that of
C
.
∎

The next proposition is a motivation for the use of logarithmic differentials.

Proposition 4.17

(
[
26
, Lemma 2.4.6]
)

Let
X
be a log smooth log scheme over a perfect field
k

0\to j_{*}\nu_{X_{\textrm{tr}}}(r)\to Z\Omega^{r,\log}_{X/k}\xrightarrow{C-1}\Omega_{X/k}^{r,\log}\to 0

is exact in
\textrm{Sh}(X_{\textrm{\'{e}t}})
. The sheaf
j_{*}\nu_{X_{\textrm{tr}}}(r)
is the pushforward of
\nu_{X_{\textrm{tr}}}
via
j:X_{\textrm{tr}}\to X
.

\mathcal{X}

Recall that we have the inclusions

Proposition 4.18

The Cartier operator defined in

Corollary 4.19

The sheaf

Proof.

This follows from the fact that the

is the kernel of the surjective morphism

between unramified sheaves and from

.
∎

Applying the functor

Proposition 4.20

There exists an exact sequence of sheaves on

0\to B\Omega_{\mathcal{X}/k,\lim}^{n,\mathrm{log}}\to Z\Omega_{\mathcal{X}/k,\lim}^{n,\mathrm{log}}\xrightarrow{C}\Omega_{\mathcal{X}/k,\lim}^{n,\mathrm{log}}.

.

Under the assumption of
Conjecture

4.12
we have a short exact sequence of sheaves on

Since we assumed that
\Omega^{+,n}_{\mathcal{X}/k}\cong\Omega^{n,\log}_{\mathcal{X}/k,\lim}
by

4.12
, the exactness of the LHS follows from

.

and

. Let

\eta=\sum_{i}f_{i}dg_{i,1}\wedge\dots\wedge dg_{i,n},

Proposition 4.26
.

Assume

Tame and strongly étale topology

We assign to an adic space

5.2.
Purity for the tame topology in positive characteristic

Étale cohomology fails to satisfy purity for smooth pairs. Consider for example
i:\{0\}\to\mathbb{A}^{1}
. We have
\nu_{\mathbb{A}^{1}}(0)=\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}
. Hence there is an exact sequence

0\to R^{1}i^{!}\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}\to R^{1}i^{!}\mathbb{G}^{p}_{a,\mathbb{A}^{1}}\xrightarrow{F^{-1}-1}R^{1}i^{!}\mathbb{G}_{a,\mathbb{A}^{1}}\to R^{2}i^{!}\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}\to 0.

Note that
R^{1}i^{!}\mathbb{G}_{a,\mathbb{A}^{1}}
is locally isomorphic to
H^{1}_{Z}(X,\mathbb{G}_{a})
, which is isomorphic to
\textrm{coker}(\mathcal{O}_{X,x}\to\mathcal{O}_{X,x}[t^{-1}])
because of the excision sequence and the fact that étale cohomology of quasi-coherent sheaves is zero on affines.

But
F^{-1}-1
is not surjective on
\mathcal{O}_{X,x}[t^{-1}]
. Indeed, for
x=0\in\mathbb{A}^{1}
, finding a preimage of
1/t
corresponds to solving the equation
T^{p}-T=1/t
, or equivalently finding roots of the polynomial
f(T)=tT^{p}-tT-1
. But
f^{\prime}(T)=-t
which lies in
m_{x}
, thus
k[T]/(tT^{p}-tT-1)\to k[T]
is not étale. In conclusion the preimage of
1/t
under

does not exist in the étale topology.

The fact that

is not surjective seems to be an obstruction to purity. It is worth noting that the Cartier operator and
F^{-1}
coincide when

.

To remedy this, an idea is to replace the sheaf

and étale topology with tame topology.

Appendix A
Compactifications

Let
\mathcal{X}:=\textrm{Spa}(X,S)
be a discretely ringed adic space, where
X
and
S
are qcqs schemes, and
X\to S
is separated. In our applications, we often seek an étale cover of
\mathcal{X}
by affinoids of the form
\textrm{Spa}(U,\bar{U})
, where
U\subset\bar{U}
is an open immersion. Such opens form a basis for the topology on
\mathcal{X}
. This construction relies on Nagata’s compactification theorem and its refinement by Conrad
[
1
]
. This is the subject of
Appendix

A
.

Our goal is to replace pairs
(U,\bar{U})
by log regular log schemes, since logarithmic differentials admit a simpler and more explicit description in this context (see
[
15
]
). However, this approach runs into the classical problem of resolution of singularities, which remains open in dimension
>3
in positive characteristic.

We recall the following version of Nagata’s compactification theorem:

Proposition A.1

(
[
1
, Theorem 4.3.]
)
.

Let
S
be a qcqs scheme, and let
X
be a separated
S
-scheme of finite type. Then there exists an open immersion
j:X\hookrightarrow\bar{X}
over
S
such that
\bar{X}\to S
is proper. We call such an immersion a
compactification
of
X
over
S
.

This implies that the topology on a discretely ringed adic space
\mathcal{X}=\textrm{Spa}(X,S)
, where
X\to S
is separated and of finite type, is generated by affinoids
\textrm{Spa}(U,\bar{U})
, where
U\subset\bar{U}
is an open immersion. After replacing
\bar{U}
by a blow-up along
\bar{U}\setminus U
, we may assume that the morphism
U\to\bar{U}
is affine. Indeed, the complement
Z:=\bar{U}\setminus U
is a Cartier divisor, and one can cover
\bar{U}
by affine opens
\textrm{Spec}(A)
where
Z\cap\textrm{Spec}(A)=V(f)
for some
f\in A
, so
U\cap\textrm{Spec}(A)=\textrm{Spec}(A[f^{-1}])
.

Given a separated morphism
X\to S
of qcqs schemes, the category of compactifications forms a filtered system. In particular, if
X\hookrightarrow\bar{X}_{1}
and
X\hookrightarrow\bar{X}_{2}
are two compactifications, then the induced map
X\to\bar{X}_{1}\times_{S}\bar{X}_{2}
is also a compactification.

Proposition A.2

(
[
1
, Theorem 2.11]
)
.

Let
f:X^{\prime}\to X
be a proper morphism of qcqs schemes that restricts to an isomorphism on a quasi-compact dense open subscheme
U\subseteq X,X^{\prime}
. Then there exist
U
-admissible blow-ups
\bar{X}\to X
and
\bar{X}^{\prime}\to X^{\prime}
and an isomorphism
\bar{X}^{\prime}\cong\bar{X}
over
f
.

In particular, the category of compactifications is dominated by those in which all morphisms are blow-ups.

Appendix B
Resolutions

Let
X\to S
be a smooth separated morphism of finite type, and assume
S=\textrm{Spec}(k)
for a perfect field
k
of characteristic
p
. Then
X
is quasi-excellent. We would like to find a smooth compactification
\bar{X}
of
X
such that
\bar{X}\setminus X
is an snc divisor. Ideally, we also want every such compactification to be dominated by a sequence of blow-ups in smooth centers lying within the boundary. This allows us to apply
Proposition

2.11
to compute cohomology of logarithmic differentials. These goals remain conjectural in dimensions
>3
.

Conjecture B.1
.

Let
\bar{X}
be a reduced, separated, Noetherian, quasi-excellent scheme. Then there exists a proper birational morphism
\pi:\bar{X}^{\prime}\to\bar{X}
such that:

•

\bar{X}^{\prime}
is regular;

•

\pi
is an isomorphism over the regular locus of
\bar{X}
;

•

The exceptional divisor
\pi^{-1}(\bar{X}_{\mathrm{sing}})
is an snc divisor in
\bar{X}^{\prime}
.

Conjecture

B.1
holds when
\bar{X}
is of dimension
\leq 3

[
3
]
. Now back to our situation, where
X\to S
is separated and smooth and assume that
S=\textrm{Spec}(k)
, where
k
is a perfect field of characteristic
p
. Then
X
is quasi-excellent. Under the assumption of
Conjecture

B.1
, one can find a smooth compactification
\bar{X}
of
X
such that
\bar{X}\setminus X
is an snc divisor, and hence
(X,\bar{X})
is a log smooth pair.

Conjecture B.2

(Embedded resolution)
.

Let
Z
be a reduced, excellent, Noetherian scheme, and
X
a regular excellent scheme. Suppose
Z\hookrightarrow X
is a closed immersion. Then there exists a projective, surjective morphism
\pi:X^{\prime}\to X
, which is an isomorphism over

, such that
\pi^{-1}(Z)
is an snc divisor in
X^{\prime}
.

This is known when
\dim(Z)\leq 2

[
2
, Corollary 1.5]
. Together, Conjectures

and

imply that every smooth discretely ringed adic space
\textrm{Spa}(X,S)
over a perfect field
k
of characteristic

has a cover by affinoids
\textrm{Spa}(U,\bar{U})
such that
\bar{U}
is smooth and
\bar{U}\setminus U
is an snc divisor.
This holds up to dimension
3
since
Conjecture

B.2
holds up to dimension
2
and
Conjecture

B.1
holds up to dimension
3
.

We present a stronger version of the conjecture, which implies the Conjecture of embedded resolution when
X
is a defined over a perfect field
k
.

Conjecture B.3

(Embedded resolution of singularities with boundary)
.

Consider a smooth scheme
\bar{X}
over a perfect field

. Let

with exceptional divisor

. Set

, and let

D_{i}

has dimension

.

Remark B.4
.

Assuming Conjectures

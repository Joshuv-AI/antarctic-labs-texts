Abstract
. We prove that smooth Wigner-Weyl spectral sums at an energy level E E exhibit Airy scaling asymptotics across the classical energy surface Σ E \Sigma_{E} . This was proved earlier by the authors for the isotropic harmonic oscillator and the proof is extended in this article to all quantum Hamiltonians − ℏ 2 ​ Δ + V -\hbar^{2}\Delta+V where V V is a confining potential with at most quadratic growth at infinity. The main tools are the Herman-Kluk initial value parametrix for the propagator and the Chester-Friedman-Ursell normal form for complex phases with a one-dimensional cubic degeneracy. This gives a rigorous account of Airy scaling asymptotics of spectral Wigner distributions of M.V. Berry, A. Ozorio de Almeida and other physicists.

Finally, we conjecture that when the smooth test function

is replaced by the indicator function of the spectral interval, then the sharp Wigner-Weyl sum has the asymptotics,

\sum_{j:|E_{j}(\hbar)-E|\leq C\hbar}W_{\psi_{j},\psi_{j}}(x,\xi)=\hbar^{-d}\left(\hbar^{\frac{1}{3}}\rm{Ai}(-\hbar^{-\frac{2}{3}}\rho(x,\xi))\sum_{\nu=0}^{\infty}u_{0,\nu}(x,\xi)\hbar^{\nu}\right)+O(\hbar^{-d+\frac{2}{3}}).

Classically such asymptotics for spectral intervals are obtained from the smoothed results (
17
) by applying cosine Tauberian theorems. But Tauberian theorems make the hypothesis that the terms of the sums are non-negative, whereas it is a well-known and important phenomenon that Wigner functions are almost never globally positive. This raises the question whether

W_{\psi_{j},\psi_{j}}(x,\xi)\geq 0
for
(x,\xi)
in an
\hbar^{2/3}
tube around
\Sigma_{E}
when the eigenvalue of
\psi_{j}
satisfies
|E_{j}(\hbar)-E|\leq C\hbar
. This is plausible, since asymptotically the Airy function is only evaluated where it is positive;
moreover, in a weak sense, the Wigner functions tend to a delta function on
\Sigma_{E}
. But this is far from sufficient to
prove positivity. A possible source of counter-examples where
W_{\psi_{j},\psi_{j}}(x,\xi)
can be negative at some points of
\Sigma_{E}
is the Wigner distribution of product eigenstates of the isotropic
harmonic oscillator,

\varphi_{\alpha,h}(x)=h^{-d/4}p_{\alpha}\left(x\cdot h^{-1/2}\right)e^{-x^{2}/2h},

(20)

where
\alpha=\left(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{d}\right)\geq(0,\ldots,0)
is a

d-
dimensional multi-index and

p_{\alpha}(x)
is the product
\prod_{j=1}^{d}p_{\alpha_{j}}(x_{j})
of the
hermite polynomials
p_{k}
(of degree
k
)
in one variable. There are similar products for generic oscillators, where the multiplicity of each eigenvalue equals one. The Wigner distribution is the product of those of the factors and are given by products in the variables
(x_{1},\dots,x_{d})
of one-dimensional Laguerre polynomials of the variables
x_{j}^{2}
. The product is positive if and only if the number of negative factors is even. It seems non-obvious whether one can construct products with an odd number of negative factors when
|E_{j}(\hbar)-E|\leq C\hbar
and
\sum_{j=1}^{d}x_{j}^{2}=E
. Another source of counter-examples could come from Wigner distributions of coherent states along elliptic periodic orbits. In the case of the isotropic harmonic oscillator, the Wigner
distribution is positive near the periodic orbit on
\Sigma_{E}
but appears to be negative at some points on
\Sigma_{E}
away from the orbit
[
Lo
]
. As far as we know, the study of negativity sets of Wigner distributions of eigenfunctions under the above constraints has never been studied.

Pointwise Semi-Classical Asymptotics of Wigner Transform of the Propagator

As a interesting and useful warm-up to the proof of Theorem
0.2
, we first give a new proof of what is essentially a well-known result from the physics literature (e.g.
[
Ber89
]
) giving pointwise semi-classical asymptotics for the Wigner transformation of the Schrödinger operator
\widehat{H}_{\hbar}
. Before stating the precise result in Proposition
0.3
, we recall some notation. First, the semi-classical Wigner transform is defined to be the unitary operator

\mathcal{W}_{\hbar}:L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d})\to L^{2}(T^{*}{\mathbb{R}}^{d}),

taking Hilbert-Schmidt kernels
K_{\hbar}\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d})
to their Wigner distributions. The semi-classical Wigner transform extends to temperate (i.e. Schwartz) distributions
K_{\hbar}\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d})
in the dual space of Schwartz space by,

\mathcal{W}_{\hbar}(K_{\hbar})(x,\xi):=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}K_{\hbar}\left(x+\frac{v}{2},x-\frac{v}{2}\right)e^{-\frac{i}{\hbar}v\xi}\frac{dv}{(2\pi h)^{d}}.

(21)

In particular, each Wigner function
W_{\psi_{j},\psi_{j}}
appearing in Theorem
0.2
can be written as the Wigner transform

W_{\psi_{j},\psi_{j}}(x,\xi)=\mathcal{W}_{\hbar}(\Pi_{j,\hbar})(x,\xi),

of the kernel

\Pi_{j,\hbar}(x,y):=\psi_{j}(\hbar,x)\psi_{j}(\hbar,y)

of the rank one projection onto the state
\psi_{j}(\hbar,\cdot)
. One advantage of viewing Wigner functions in this way is that, while Wigner functions
W_{\psi_{j},\psi_{j}}
are quadratic in
\psi_{j}
, the Wigner transform
\mathcal{W}_{\hbar}(K_{\hbar})
is linear in
K_{\hbar}
. We will make use of this when proving Theorem
0.2
. But first we consider the ‘propagator’
U_{\hbar}(t)=e^{-{\frac{i}{h}}t\widehat{H}_{h}}
, i.e. the solution operator of the Cauchy problem for the Schrödinger equation

i\hbar\frac{\partial}{\partial t}u=\widehat{H}_{\hbar}u.

More precisely, we will consider the Schwartz kernel

U_{h}(t,x,y)=e^{-{\frac{i}{h}}t\widehat{H}_{h}}(x,y)=\sum_{j\geq 0}e^{-{\frac{i}{h}}tE_{j}(\hbar)}\Pi_{j,\hbar}(x,y)

(22)

of
U_{\hbar}(t)
and its Wigner transform

\mathcal{U}_{\hbar}(t,x,\xi):=\mathcal{W}(U_{\hbar}(t,\cdot,\cdot))(x,\xi)=\int e^{-{\frac{i}{h}}2\pi\xi\cdot v}U_{\hbar}\left(t,x+\frac{v}{2},x-\frac{v}{2}\right)\frac{dv}{(2\pi\hbar)^{d}}.

(23)

[
Ber89
]
, our first task will be to obtain, for fixed
(t,x,\xi)
, semi-classical pointwise asymptotics for
\mathcal{U}_{\hbar}(t,x,\xi)
. Specifically, we prove the following formula for pointwise semi-classical asymptotics of the Wigner function of the propagator, stated in a heuristic way by M.V. Berry
[

Further scaling results

The results of M.V. Berry
[
Ber89
]
pertain mainly to the the contribution of periodic orbits to the Wigner spectral
asymptotics. It is evident from (

(q,p)
is a periodic point of period

then one gets further
solutions by replacing
(q,p)
by
\Phi^{T}(q,p)

\Sigma_{E}
at
t=0
also occurs
at time
t=T
at the periodic points of
\Sigma_{E}
of period
T
. The order of the asymptotics is higher than it would
be for non-degenerate critical points, and Berry therefore referred to the caustic enhancement of such periodic orbits
as ‘scars’. The methods of this article extend to the periodic orbit case with few modifications but for the sake of
brevity we do not include them here.

In
[
HZb
]
, further scaling asymptotics are proved in the case of the isotropic harmonic oscillator. The essential difference to
Theorem
0.2
is that much larger spectral intervals are assumed. We do not generalize these results to
general Hamiltonians satisfying (
2
) or (
6
) in this article, but it is very likely that generalizations do exist. We briefly review
these additional scaling results.

Instead of spectral intervals of width
\hbar
we consider intervals of width
\hbar^{2/3}
, e.g.

[E-\lambda_{-}\hbar^{2/3},E+\lambda_{+}\hbar^{2/3}
. In the smoothing asymptotics we let
\delta=\frac{2}{3}
and consider

W_{\hbar,f,\frac{2}{3},E}(x,\xi):=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}f\left(\hbar^{-\frac{2}{3}}(E-E_{j}(\hbar))\right)W_{\psi_{j},\psi_{j}}(x,\xi).

We define the rescaled variable
u=u(x,\xi)
centered at the energy surface
\Sigma_{E}
by

H(x,\xi)=E+u\left(\hbar/2E\right)^{2/3}.

We then prove that,

W_{2/3,E,\lambda_{\pm}}(x,\xi)=(2\pi\hbar)^{-d}C_{E}\int_{-\lambda_{+}}^{-\lambda_{-}}\Ai\left(\frac{u}{E}+\lambda C_{E}\right)d\lambda+O(\hbar^{-d+1/3-\delta}),\qquad C_{E}=(E/4)^{1/3}.

Instead of getting the value of the Airy function at the scaled parameter
u
, we get an integral over the values due to the larger
spectral interval.

Furthermore, in the even larger interval
I_{\hbar}=[0,E]
,
for any
\varepsilon>0
, we obtained the bulk asymptotics,

W_{\hbar,[0,E]}(x,\xi)=\left(2\pi\hbar\right)^{-d}\left[\int_{0}^{\infty}\Ai\left(\frac{u}{E}+\lambda\right)d\lambda+O(\hbar^{1/3-\varepsilon}\left\lvert u\right\rvert^{1/2})+O(\left\lvert u\right\rvert^{5/2}\hbar^{2/3-\varepsilon})\right],

(29)

where the
O
-symbol depends only on
d,\varepsilon.

The articles
[
Ber89
,
O98
]
use the special (Lorentzian) test function
f_{\varepsilon}(E-E_{j}(\hbar))=\delta_{\varepsilon}(E-E_{j}(\hbar)):=-\frac{1}{\pi}{\operatorname{Im}\,}(E+i\varepsilon-x)^{-1}
, with various choices of

stated in

and the semi-classical scaling

where

\Sigma_{E}
. The same test functions and energy scales are used in
[
O98
, Section 7]
. The mathematical techniques of this article (along with other spectral asymptotics articles in the mathematics literature) are not valid below the semi-classical scale, and in particular do not give scaling results on the length scale

with

Besides linking the somewhat heuristic asymptotics calculations of

1.
Background on Classical mechanics

In this section, we recall some basic results from classical mechanics and set notation. With
\Phi^{t}
the Hamiltonian flow defined in (
10
), we continue to denote its derivative at
(q,p)
by

D_{q,p}\Phi^{t}:=M_{t}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}A_{t}&B_{t}\\
C_{t}&D_{t}\end{array}\right),

(30)

where

A_{t}=\partial_{q}q_{t},\quad B_{t}=\partial_{p}q_{t},\quad C_{t}=\partial_{q}p_{t},\quad D_{t}=\partial_{p}p_{t},

with rows corresponding to components and columns to derivatives:

\left(\partial_{q_{j}}q_{t}\right)_{k}=\left(A_{t}\right)_{k,j}.

Since
\Phi^{t}
is a Hamiltonian flow, we have

\Omega=M_{t}^{T}\Omega M_{t}

where
\Omega=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&I\\
-I&0\end{array}\right)
is the symplectic form
[
F
]
and we therefore have

\displaystyle A_{t}C_{t}=C_{t}^{T}A_{t},\qquad A_{t}^{T}D_{t}-C_{t}^{T}B_{t}=\Id,\qquad B_{t}^{T}D_{t}=D_{t}B_{t}.

(31)

The action along the
\Phi^{t}
orbit with initial value
(q,p)
is defined by,

S(t,q,p):=\int_{0}^{t}(\dot{q}_{s}\cdot p_{s}-H(s,q_{s},p_{s}))ds

(32)

We only deal with autonomous Hamiltonians, for which
H(s,q_{s},p_{s})=H(q_{s},p_{s})
and then
H(q_{s},p_{s})

is constant along Hamilton orbits and the second term is
tH(q,p)
. We will have occasion to use the following elementary result.

Lemma
1.1
.

We have

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\partial_{q}S(t,q,p)=\left(\partial_{q}q_{t}\right)\cdot p_{t}-p,\\
\\
\partial_{p}S(t,q,p)=\left(\partial_{p}q_{t}\right)\cdot p_{t},\\
\\
\partial_{t}S(t,q,p)=\dot{q}_{t}\cdot p_{t}-H(q_{t},p_{t}).\end{array}\right.

Proof.

Using the equations of motion
\partial_{p}H=\dot{q}_{s}
and
\partial_{q}H=-\dot{p}_{s}
, we get

\displaystyle\partial_{q}S(t,q,p)

\displaystyle=\partial_{q}\left(\int_{0}^{t}\dot{q}_{s}\cdot p_{s}-H(q_{s},p_{s})ds\right)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\left(\partial_{q}\dot{q}_{s}\cdot p_{s}+\dot{q}_{s}\cdot\partial_{q}p_{s}-\partial_{q}H(q_{s},p_{s})\partial_{q}q_{s}-\partial_{p}H(q_{s},p_{s})\partial_{q}p_{s}\right)ds

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\langle\partial_{q}\dot{q}_{s},p_{s}\rangle+\langle\dot{q}_{s},\partial_{q}p_{s}\rangle+\langle\dot{p}_{s},\partial_{q}q_{s}\rangle-\langle\dot{q}(s),\partial_{q}p_{s}\rangle ds

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\langle\partial_{q}\dot{q}_{s},p_{s}\rangle+\langle\dot{p}_{s},\partial_{q}q_{s}\rangle ds=\int_{0}^{t}\partial_{s}\langle\partial_{q}q_{s},p_{s}\rangle ds=\langle\partial_{q}q_{s},p_{s}\rangle|_{0}^{t},

proving the first statement. Similarly,

\displaystyle\partial_{p}S(t,q,p)

\displaystyle=\partial_{p}\left(\int_{0}^{t}\dot{q}_{s}\cdot p_{s}-H(q_{s},p_{s})ds\right)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\left(\partial_{p}\dot{q}_{s}\cdot p_{s}+\dot{q}_{s}\cdot\partial_{p}p_{s}-\partial_{q}H(q_{s},p_{s})\partial_{p}q_{s}-\partial_{p}H(q_{s},p_{s})\partial_{p}p_{s}\right)ds

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\left(\partial_{p}\dot{q}_{s}\cdot p_{s}+\dot{q}_{s}\cdot\partial_{p}p_{s}+\langle\dot{p}_{s},\partial_{p}q_{s}\rangle-\langle\dot{q}_{s},\partial_{p}p_{s}\rangle\right)ds

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\left(\partial_{p}\dot{q}_{s}\cdot p_{s}+\langle\dot{p}_{s},\partial_{p}q_{s}\rangle\right)ds=\int_{0}^{t}\frac{d}{ds}\langle p_{s},\partial_{p}q_{s}\rangle ds=\langle p_{s},\partial_{p}q_{s}\rangle|_{0}^{t},

proving the second statement. The third statement is obvious.

∎

Lemma
2.2

Fix

(q_{c},p_{c},v_{c})

\det\left(\text{Hess}(\Psi)\right)(q_{c},p_{c},v_{c})
is given by,

(-1)^{d}\cdot\det(1+M_{t}(q_{c},p_{c}))\det\left(A_{t}(q_{c},p_{c})+D_{t}(q_{c},p_{c})+i(B_{t}(q_{c},p_{c})-C_{t}(q_{c},p_{c}))\right).

Proof.

We claim that at the critical point,

\displaystyle\partial_{pp}\Psi

\displaystyle=B_{t}^{T}\left(-D_{t}+iB_{t}\right),\qquad

\displaystyle\partial_{pq}\Psi=B_{t}^{T}\left(-C_{t}+iA_{t}\right)

\displaystyle\partial_{pv}\Psi

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(\Id+D_{t}-iB_{t}\right)^{T},\qquad

\displaystyle\partial_{vv}\Psi=\frac{i}{2}\Id

\displaystyle\partial_{qq}\Psi

\displaystyle=-A_{t}^{T}C_{t}+i\left(A_{t}^{T}A_{t}+\Id\right),\qquad

\displaystyle\partial_{qv}\Psi=\frac{1}{2}\left(C_{t}+i\left(-A_{t}+\Id\right)\right).

We summarize the key points of the calculations. Apriori, the Hessian involves second derivatives in
(q,p)
of
(q_{t},p_{t},\dot{q}_{t},\dot{p}_{t})

\partial_{p_{j}}\Psi=\partial_{p_{j}}p_{t}\left(x+\frac{v}{2}-q_{t}\right)-\left(x_{j}-\frac{v_{j}}{2}-q_{j}\right)+i\left(-\partial_{p_{j}}q_{t}\left(x+\frac{v}{2}-q_{t}\right)\right),

(43)

\partial_{p_{k},p_{j}}\Psi=-\partial_{p_{j}}p_{t}\partial_{p_{k}}q_{t}+i\left(\partial_{p_{j}}q_{t}\partial_{p_{k}}q_{t}\right)=(-\partial_{p_{j}}(p_{t}+i\partial_{p_{j}}q_{t})\partial_{p_{k}}q_{t}=\left((-D_{t}+iB_{t})^{T}B_{t}\right)_{k,j}

Also,

\partial_{q_{j}}\Psi=\partial_{q_{j}}p_{t}\left(x+\frac{v}{2}-q_{t}\right)-i\left(\partial_{q_{j}}q_{t}\left(x+\frac{v}{2}-q_{t}\right)+\left(x_{j}-\frac{v_{j}}{2}-q_{j}\right)\right),

(44)

and at a critical point,

\partial_{p_{k},q_{j}}\Psi=-\partial_{q_{j}}p_{t}\partial_{p_{k}}q_{t}+i\left(\partial_{q_{j}}q_{t}\partial_{p_{k}}q_{t}\right).

Starting from (

\partial_{v_{k}p_{j}}\Psi=\frac{1}{2}\left((\partial_{p_{j}}p_{t})_{k}+\delta_{j,k}-i(\partial_{p_{j}}q_{t})_{k}\right)=\frac{1}{2}\left(\Id+D_{t}-iB_{t}\right)_{k,j}.

Again starting from (
43

\partial_{tp_{j}}\Psi=\left(-\partial_{p_{j}}p_{t}+i\partial_{p_{j}}q_{t}\right)\dot{q}_{t}=\left[\dot{q}_{t}(-D_{t}+iB_{t})\right]_{j}.

Starting from (

\partial_{q_{k}q_{j}}\Psi=\partial_{q_{j}}p_{t}\partial_{q_{k}}q_{t}+i\left(\partial_{q_{j}}q_{t}\partial_{q_{k}}q_{t}+\delta_{j,k}\right)=\left[A_{t}^{T}C_{t}+i\left(A_{t}^{T}A_{t}+\Id\right)\right]_{k,j}.

Finally, from (
44
) we obtain

\partial_{v_{k}q_{j}}\Psi=\frac{1}{2}\left((\partial_{q_{j}}p_{t})_{k}+i\left(-(\partial_{q_{j}}q_{t})_{k}+\delta_{k,j}\right)\right).

The Hessian of
\Psi

(q_{c},p_{c},v_{c})
is therefore

\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}\frac{i}{2}\Id&\frac{1}{2}\left(C_{t}+i(-A_{t}+\Id)\right)&\frac{1}{2}\left(D_{t}+\Id-iB_{t}\right)\\
\frac{1}{2}\left(C_{t}^{T}+i(-A_{t}+\Id)\right)&-A_{t}C_{t}+i(A_{t}^{2}+\Id)&-C_{t}^{T}B_{t}+iA_{t}B_{t}\\

as claimed. We now calculate the determinant of the Hessian. Multiplying the first row on the left by
A_{t}
and adding the result to the second row and then multiplying the first row by
B_{t}^{T}
on the left and adding the result to the third row shows that the determinant of
\text{Hess}(\Psi)(q_{c},p_{c},v_{c})
is the same as the determinant of

3.2.
Background on folds

In the next Section

we discuss fold singularities of Lagrangian
submanifolds associated to the Wigner spectral function. In preparation, we review the definitions and
properties of folding maps

for general manifolds. References include
[
G
,
HoIII
]
.

We follow the exposition in
[
G
, Appendix 1, Page 109]
. Let

be a smooth map of n-dimensional
manifolds and let

be a hypersurface (codimension one submanifold) of
X
. Let

be a volume
form on

Then

is a folding map with

as the fold locus if the following are satisfied,

•

S
is set of critical points of

, i.e.

fails to be surjective ;

•

At every

is one-dimensional and is transversal to

;

•

vanishes to first order at each

, i.e.

vanishes to first order on

In this case, one can find coordinates

on

and coordinates

on

so
that

and

and so that

, for

and
f^{*}y_{n}=x_{n}^{2}
.
Associated to the folding map is a canonical involution
\sigma:V\to V
in a neighborhood

of

in

,
fixing
S
and having the local form
\sigma(x_{1},\dots,x_{n-1},x_{n})=(x_{1}\dots,x_{n-1},-x_{n})
.

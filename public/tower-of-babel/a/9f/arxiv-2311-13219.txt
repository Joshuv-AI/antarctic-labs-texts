Robust Outlier Bound Condition to Phase Retrieval with Adversarial Sparse Outliers This work was supported in part by the NSFC under grant numbers U21A20426, 12071426 and the National Key Research and Development Program of China under grant number 2021YFA1003500, and in part by the fundamental research funds for the central universities.
Gao Huang, Song Li, Hang Xu

Abstract
We consider the problem of recovering an unknown signal 𝒙 0 ∈ ℝ n \boldsymbol{x}_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n} from phaseless measurements. In this paper, we study the convex phase retrieval problem via PhaseLift from linear Gaussian measurements perturbed by ℓ 1 \ell_{1} -bounded noise and sparse outliers that can change an adversarially chosen s s -fraction of the measurement vector. We show that the Robust-PhaseLift model can successfully reconstruct the ground-truth up to global phase for any s < s ∗ ≈ 0.1185 s\textless s^{*}\approx 0.1185 with 𝒪 ⁡ ( n ) \mathcal{O}(n) measurements, even in the case where the sparse outliers may depend on the measurement and the observation. The recovery guarantees are based on the robust outlier bound condition and the analysis of the product of two Gaussian variables. Moreover, we construct adaptive counterexamples to show that the Robust-PhaseLift model fails when s > s ∗ s\textgreater s^{*} with high probability.



Keywords:

PhaseLift, Adversarial sparse outliers,
\ell_{1}
-minimization, Robust outlier bound condition, Product of two Gaussian variables.

Robust Outlier Bound Condition

The mixed-norm
\ell_{1}/\ell_{2}
-restricted isometry property (
\ell_{1}/\ell_{2}
-RIP) has been usually used in the context of structured signal recovery in the previous literature
[
15
,
10
,
3
,
1
]
.

Definition 1

(
\ell_{1}/\ell_{2}
-RIP)
.

An operator
\mathcal{A}:\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}\times n_{2}}\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
is said to satisfy the mixed-norm
\ell_{1}/\ell_{2}
-restricted isometry property, if there exists a constant
\delta_{2r}
such that

\displaystyle(1-\delta_{2r})\left\|\boldsymbol{X}\right\|_{F}\leq\frac{1}{m}\left\|\mathcal{A}(\boldsymbol{X})\right\|_{1}\leq(1+\delta_{2r})\left\|\boldsymbol{X}\right\|_{F}

holds for all rank-
2r
matrices
\boldsymbol{X}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}\times n_{2}}
. The RIP constant
\delta_{2r}
is the smallest constant satisfying the above inequality.

Let
\left[m\right]
denote the set
\{1,2,\cdots,m\}
,
\mathcal{S}
be a subset of
\left[m\right]
and
\mathcal{S}^{c}
be the complement set of
\mathcal{S}
with respect to
\left[m\right]
. Let
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{S}}
be the mapping operator
\mathcal{A}
constrained on
\mathcal{S}
, which is defined as

[\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{S}}(\boldsymbol{X})]_{i}=\boldsymbol{a}_{i}^{\texttt{T}}\boldsymbol{X}\boldsymbol{a}_{i}
if
i\in\mathcal{S}
and
[\mathcal{A}_{S}(\boldsymbol{X})]_{i}=0
if
i\in\mathcal{S}^{c}
. If the support of the sparse outlier
\mathcal{S}
is known as prior knowledge, the mixed-norm
\ell_{1}/\ell_{2}
-RIP can lead to the restricted outlier bound condition
[
16
,
9
,
50
]
:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{m}\left\|\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{S}^{c}}(\boldsymbol{X})\right\|_{1}-\frac{1}{m}\left\|\boldsymbol{A}_{\mathcal{S}}(\boldsymbol{X})\right\|_{1}\geq C(\delta)\left\|\boldsymbol{X}\right\|_{F}.

Here, we need the partial measurements
\boldsymbol{A}_{\mathcal{S}^{c}}
,
\boldsymbol{A}_{\mathcal{S}}
to satisfy the
\ell_{1}/\ell_{2}
-RIP when the support
\mathcal{S}
of sparse outlier is fixed
[
24
,
36
]
.

In order to deal with the adversarial sparse outliers whose support may probably depend on the observation
\boldsymbol{b}
and the measurement operator
\mathcal{A}
, we use the following
s
-robust outlier bound condition to characterize the upper and lower bound associated with partial measurements.

Definition 2

(
s
-robust outlier bound condition)
.

An operator
\mathcal{A}:\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}\times n_{2}}\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
is said to satisfy the

such that the following holds for all matrix
\boldsymbol{X}\in T

\displaystyle\frac{1}{m}\min_{\mathcal{S}\subset[m],|\mathcal{S}|\leq sm}\Bigl[\|\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{S}^{c}}(\boldsymbol{X})\|_{1}-\|\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{S}}(\boldsymbol{X})\|_{1}\Bigr]

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle C(s)\cdot\|\boldsymbol{X}\|_{F}.

(6)

Here,
T
is a subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}\times n_{2}}
.

\|\boldsymbol{X}\|_{F},\|\boldsymbol{X}_{0}\|_{F}\geq\sqrt{2}
.
We now consider the intersection of two events

\displaystyle E_{1}:=\left\{\frac{1}{m}\|\mathcal{A}(\boldsymbol{X})\|_{1}\leq(1+\delta)\|\boldsymbol{X}\|_{1}\text{ for all symmetric $\boldsymbol{X}$}\right\}

and

\displaystyle E_{2}:=\left\{\ \frac{1}{m}\min_{\mathcal{S}\subset[m],|\mathcal{S}|\leq sm}\frac{\|\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{S}^{c}}(\boldsymbol{X}_{0})\|_{1}-\|\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{S}}(\boldsymbol{X}_{0})\|_{1}}{\|\boldsymbol{X}_{0}\|_{F}}\geq\mathcal{H}^{*}(s+\gamma)-\frac{l_{0}\gamma}{4}\text{ for all
}\boldsymbol{X}_{0}\in\mathcal{N}_{\epsilon}\right\}.

\mathcal{S}_{1}
be the index set of the largest
s
-fraction of

in absolute value. Similarly,
\mathcal{S}_{2}
be the subset of
\mathcal{S}_{\boldsymbol{X}_{0}}
, which collects the index of the largest
s
-fraction of
\mathcal{S}_{\boldsymbol{X}_{0}}
in absolute value.
Then based on the triangle inequality, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{m}\min_{\mathcal{S}\subset[m],|\mathcal{S}|\leq sm}\frac{\|\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{S}^{c}}(\boldsymbol{X})\|_{1}}{{\|\boldsymbol{X}\|_{F}}}=\frac{1}{m}\frac{\|\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{S}_{1}^{c}}(\boldsymbol{X})\|_{1}}{{\|\boldsymbol{X}\|_{F}}}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{m}\left[\left\|\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{S}_{1}^{c}}\left(\frac{\boldsymbol{X}_{0}}{\|\boldsymbol{X}_{0}\|_{F}}\right)\right\|_{1}-\left\|\mathcal{A}_{S_{1}^{c}}\left(\frac{\boldsymbol{X}}{\|\boldsymbol{X}\|_{F}}-\frac{\boldsymbol{X}_{0}}{\|\boldsymbol{X}_{0}\|_{F}}\right)\right\|_{1}\right]

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{m}\left[\left\|\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{S}_{2}^{c}}\left(\frac{\boldsymbol{X}_{0}}{\|\boldsymbol{X}_{0}\|_{F}}\right)\right\|_{1}-\left\|\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{S}_{1}^{c}}\left(\frac{\boldsymbol{X}}{\|\boldsymbol{X}\|_{F}}-\frac{\boldsymbol{X}_{0}}{\|\boldsymbol{X}_{0}\|_{F}}\right)\right\|_{1}\right].

Similarly, we can get

\displaystyle\frac{1}{m}\max_{\mathcal{S}\subset[m],|\mathcal{S}|\leq sm}\frac{\|\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{S}}(\boldsymbol{X})\|_{1}}{{\|\boldsymbol{X}\|_{F}}}=\frac{1}{m}\frac{\|\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{S}_{1}}(\boldsymbol{X})\|_{1}}{{\|\boldsymbol{X}\|_{F}}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{m}\left[\left\|\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{S}_{1}}\left(\frac{\boldsymbol{X}_{0}}{\|\boldsymbol{X}_{0}\|_{F}}\right)\right\|_{1}+\left\|\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{S}_{1}}\left(\frac{\boldsymbol{X}}{\|\boldsymbol{X}\|_{F}}-\frac{\boldsymbol{X}_{0}}{\|\boldsymbol{X}_{0}\|_{F}}\right)\right\|_{1}\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{m}\left[\left\|\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{S}_{2}}\left(\frac{\boldsymbol{X}_{0}}{\|\boldsymbol{X}_{0}\|_{F}}\right)\right\|_{1}+\left\|\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{S}_{1}}\left(\frac{\boldsymbol{X}}{\|\boldsymbol{X}\|_{F}}-\frac{\boldsymbol{X}_{0}}{\|\boldsymbol{X}_{0}\|_{F}}\right)\right\|_{1}\right].

Combining with the above two inequalities, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{m}\min_{\mathcal{S}\subset[m],|\mathcal{S}|\leq sm}\frac{\|\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{S}^{c}}(\boldsymbol{X})\|_{1}-\|\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{S}}(\boldsymbol{X})\|_{1}}{{\|\boldsymbol{X}\|_{F}}}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{m}\frac{\|\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{S}_{2}^{c}}(\boldsymbol{X}_{0})\|_{1}-\|\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{S}_{2}}(\boldsymbol{X}_{0})\|_{1}}{\|\boldsymbol{X}_{0}\|_{F}}-\frac{1}{m}\left\|\mathcal{A}\left(\frac{\boldsymbol{X}}{\|\boldsymbol{X}\|_{F}}-\frac{\boldsymbol{X}_{0}}{\|\boldsymbol{X}_{0}\|_{F}}\right)\right\|_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{m}\min_{\mathcal{S}\subset[m],|\mathcal{S}|\leq sm}\frac{\|\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{S}^{c}}(\boldsymbol{X}_{0})\|_{1}-\|\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{S}}(\boldsymbol{X}_{0})\|_{1}}{{\|\boldsymbol{X}_{0}\|_{F}}}-\frac{1}{m}\left\|\mathcal{A}\left(\frac{\boldsymbol{X}}{\|\boldsymbol{X}\|_{F}}-\frac{\boldsymbol{X}_{0}}{\|\boldsymbol{X}_{0}\|_{F}}\right)\right\|_{1}.

Lemma

and all symmetric matrices
\boldsymbol{X}
with probability at least

when

Finally, by setting

\displaystyle\frac{1}{m}\min_{\mathcal{S}\subset[m],|\mathcal{S}|\leq sm}\frac{\|\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{S}^{c}}(\boldsymbol{X})\|_{1}-\|\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{S}}(\boldsymbol{X})\|_{1}}{{\|\boldsymbol{X}\|_{F}}}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{*}(s+\gamma)-\frac{l_{0}\gamma}{4}-(1+\delta)\left\|\frac{\boldsymbol{X}}{\|\boldsymbol{X}\|_{F}}-\frac{\boldsymbol{X}_{0}}{\|\boldsymbol{X}_{0}\|_{F}}\right\|_{1}\geq\mathcal{H}^{*}(s+\gamma)-\frac{l_{0}\gamma}{4}-12(1+\delta)\epsilon

In conclusion,

We first construct a dual certificate for any

Our construction mainly follows the ideas of

,

Some auxiliary hypotheses are given first. Without loss of generality, we assume
\|\boldsymbol{x}_{0}\|_{2}=1
. Let

, then let
\alpha_{0}:=\mathbb{E}X^{2}\mathbbm{1}(|X|\leq 3)\approx 0.9709,\,\beta_{0}:=\mathbb{E}X^{4}\mathbbm{1}(|X|\leq 3)\approx 2.6728,\,\eta_{0}:=\mathbb{E}X^{6}\mathbbm{1}(|X|\leq 3)\approx 11.2102

Let
\varepsilon

\varepsilon
is independent of other random elements.
Specifically, our construction is :

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{Y}=\sum_{i\in[m]}y_{i}\boldsymbol{a}_{i}\boldsymbol{a}_{i}^{\texttt{T}}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{m}\left[\sum_{i\in\mathcal{S}^{c}}[\beta_{0}-|\langle\boldsymbol{a}_{i},\boldsymbol{x}_{0}\rangle|^{2}\mathbbm{1}(|\langle\boldsymbol{a}_{i},\boldsymbol{x}_{0}\rangle|\leq 3)]\boldsymbol{a}_{i}\boldsymbol{a}_{i}^{\texttt{T}}+\sum_{i\in\mathcal{S}}(9-\beta_{0})\varepsilon_{i}\boldsymbol{a}_{i}\boldsymbol{a}_{i}^{\texttt{T}}\right]

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{Y}^{(0)}-\boldsymbol{Y}^{(1)}+\boldsymbol{Y}^{(2)}.

It is clear that
\|\boldsymbol{y}\|_{\infty}\leq\frac{9-\beta_{0}}{m}
. Note that the existence of
\varepsilon
makes
\mathbb{E}\left[\boldsymbol{Y}^{(2)}\right]=\boldsymbol{0}
. This condition can be weakened to that

and

in the analysis of

in the future work.

We simply account for the motivation of the above structure. Due to the rotation invariance of the Gaussian vector, we let
\boldsymbol{x}_{0}=\boldsymbol{e}_{1}
. We denote the support of

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\boldsymbol{X}_{T}=(\boldsymbol{x}_{0}\boldsymbol{x}_{0}^{\texttt{T}})\boldsymbol{X}+(\boldsymbol{I}-\boldsymbol{x}_{0}\boldsymbol{x}_{0}^{\texttt{T}})\boldsymbol{X}(\boldsymbol{x}_{0}\boldsymbol{x}_{0}^{\texttt{T}}),\\

(21)

\mathbb{E}\left[\boldsymbol{Y}\right]=\frac{|\mathcal{S}^{c}|}{m}\begin{bmatrix}0&\boldsymbol{0}\\
\boldsymbol{0}&(\beta_{0}-\alpha_{0})\boldsymbol{I}_{n-1}\end{bmatrix}\succeq(1-s^{*})(\beta_{0}-\alpha_{0})\boldsymbol{I}_{T^{\perp}}

Lemma 5

Set

Part 1:
First of all, following

Part 2:
Secondly, set
X_{i}=\langle\boldsymbol{a}_{i},\boldsymbol{x}_{0}\rangle\sim\mathcal{N}(0,1)

are

for sufficiently small

Part 3:
Finally, as

\displaystyle\mu_{i}=\begin{cases}\beta_{0}X_{i}-X_{i}^{3}\mathbbm{1}(|X_{i}|\leq 3)&i\in\mathcal{S}^{c}\\
(9-\beta_{0})\varepsilon_{i}X_{i}&i\in\mathcal{S}\\

and

are the projection of
\boldsymbol{a}_{i}
onto the orthogonal complement of span(
\boldsymbol{x}_{0}

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\mu_{i}^{2}\right]=\begin{cases}\eta_{0}-\beta_{0}^{2}&i\in\mathcal{S}^{c}\\
(9-\beta_{0})^{2}&i\in\mathcal{S}.\\
\end{cases}

Similar to
Part 2

are sub-exponential variables with bounded sub-exponential norm

.
Thus, by Bernstein Inequality in Lemma
10
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\left\lVert\boldsymbol{\mu}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}-\mathbb{E}\left\lVert\boldsymbol{\mu}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\geq m\right)=\mathbb{P}\left(\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{m}(\mu_{i}^{2}-\mathbb{E}\mu_{i}^{2})}{m}\geq 1\right)\leq 2e^{-\gamma m},

where
\gamma=\min\left(\frac{c_{6}}{K_{\mu}^{2}},\frac{c_{6}}{K_{\mu}}\right)
and the expectation of
\left\lVert\boldsymbol{\mu}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}
is

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left\lVert\boldsymbol{\mu}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|\mathcal{S}^{c}|(\eta_{0}-\beta_{0}^{2})+|\mathcal{S}|(9-\beta_{0})^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(1-s^{*})m(\eta_{0}-\beta_{0}^{2})+s^{*}m(9-\beta_{0})^{2}:=\tau_{0}m\leq 10m.

This gives that with probability at least
1-2e^{-\gamma m}
,

\displaystyle\left\lVert\boldsymbol{\mu}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\leq(1+\tau_{0})m.

(29)

Besides, for fixed
\boldsymbol{x}\in\mathcal{S}^{n-1}
,
\left\lVert\boldsymbol{\widetilde{U}}\boldsymbol{x}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}

is a
\chi^{2}(n-1)
random variable, and Chernoff upper bound implies

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\left\lVert\boldsymbol{\widetilde{U}}\boldsymbol{x}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\geq\frac{m\Delta}{66}\right)\leq e^{-cm\Delta},

(30)

for some numerical constant
c\textgreater 0
and sufficiently large constant
C
with
m\geq C\Delta^{-1}n
.
Now combining (
29
) with (
30
), we can get with probability at least
1-3e^{-cm\Delta}
,

\displaystyle\left\|\left(\boldsymbol{I}-\boldsymbol{x}_{0}\boldsymbol{x}^{\texttt{T}}_{0}\right)\boldsymbol{Y}\boldsymbol{x}_{0}\right\|_{2}^{2}=\frac{1}{m^{2}}\left\lVert\boldsymbol{\widetilde{U}}\boldsymbol{\mu}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\leq\frac{(1+\tau_{0})\Delta}{66}\leq\Delta/6,

provided
m\geq C\Delta^{-1}n
.

\boldsymbol{z}

satisfying (
24
) and (

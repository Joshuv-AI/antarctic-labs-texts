Generalized recursive atom ordering and equivalence to CL-shellability Thanks: P. Hersh is supported by NSF grant DMS-1953931.
Patricia Hersh, Grace Stadnyk

Abstract
. Björner and Wachs introduced CL-shellability as a technique for studying the topological structure of order complexes of partially ordered sets (posets). They also introduced the notion of recursive atom ordering, and they proved that a finite bounded poset is CL-shellable if and only if it admits a recursive atom ordering. In this paper, a generalization of the notion of recursive atom ordering is introduced. A finite bounded poset is proven to admit such a generalized recursive atom ordering if and only if it admits a traditional recursive atom ordering. This is also proven equivalent to admitting a CC-shelling (a type of shelling introduced by Kozlov) with a further property called self-consistency. Thus, CL-shellability is proven equivalent to self-consistent CC-shellability. As an application, the uncrossing posets, namely the face posets for stratified spaces of planar electrical networks, are proven to be dual CL-shellable.

Introduction

This paper introduces a new technique for studying the topological structure of simplicial complexes known as order complexes
of finite partially ordered sets (posets), namely we introduce
generalized recursive atom orderings. The objects that these generalize, the recursive atom orderings of Björner and Wachs (introduced in
[
BW83
]
), are one of the most fundamental and widely used methods available for studying the topological structure of order complexes (also known as nerves) of posets. Any recursive atom ordering (a notion that is reviewed in Section
2
) of any finite bounded poset gives rise to a lexicographic shelling for the poset,
thereby proving that the order complex of the poset (an simplicial complex whose
i
-dimensional faces are the chains of
i+1
comparable poset elements)
is homotopy equivalent to a wedge of spheres, doing so in a way that
enumerates the spheres of each dimension.

We establish a number of fundamental properties of these generalized recursive atom orderings (GRAOs), including proving that any generalized recursive atom ordering may be transformed into a traditional recursive atom ordering (RAO) by a process we introduce that we call the atom reordering process.
These generalized recursive atom orderings allow us to prove that several different forms of lexicographic shellability are all equivalent to each other.
In this manner, we clarify
the hierarchy of different techniques for proving a poset to be lexicographically shellable.

Any shelling, lexicographic or otherwise, of a finite simplicial complex
\Delta
is a total order on the maximal faces (known as the facets) of
\Delta
satisfying conditions that ensure that if the complex is built up by sequentially attaching the facets (along with all of their proper faces) in this “shelling order”, then each such attachment
either preserves the homotopy type of the complex built so far
or closes off a sphere. Thus, a shelling for
\Delta
implies that
\Delta
is contractible or homotopy equivalent to a wedge of spheres.
The nature of the attachment of a facet
F_{j}
for
j>1
to the partial complex comprised of earlier facets is entirely governed by which of the
codimension one faces of
F_{j}
are shared with earlier facets. A facet
F_{j}
with
j>1

attaches to earlier facets along its entire boundary, thereby closing off a sphere of dimension

\dim(F_{j})
,
if and only if every codimension one face of
F_{j}
is shared with an earlier facet. Counting the number of

i
-dimensional spheres in the resulting wedge of spheres
may be accomplished for any
i
by counting the
i
-dimensional facets that attach along their entire boundary in the shelling.

For order complexes of finite bounded posets, one may often obtain a shelling by assigning sequences of labels (from an ordered set such as the integers)
to the maximal chains of the poset. If certain conditions are met, one may
use these label sequences to construct a “lexicographic shelling”
for the poset order complex as follows.
The maximal chains are totally ordered by ordering the label sequences in dictionary order, breaking ties between maximal chains
arbitrarily. Since the maximal chains of
P
are the facets of
\Delta(P)
, this ordering of the maximal chains of
P
gives a
total order on the facets of
\Delta(P)
. Whenever the requirements for a lexicographic shelling are met, this will be a shelling order for
\Delta(P)
.
In the most prevalent types of lexicographic shellings, namely EL-shellings and CL-shellings, each
descent
\lambda_{i}>\lambda_{i+1}
in consecutive labels in a label sequence forces
any facet
F_{j}
with this label sequence to have a codimension one face
G_{i,i+1}

that is shared with an earlier facet in the shelling order.
G_{i,i+1}
is obtained by omitting from the maximal chain
F_{j}
the poset element where the descent is located.
In this manner, the descents in the label sequences dictate how each facet attaches topologically
to the earlier facets in the shelling.
To calculate the Möbius function
\mu_{P}(u,v)
, it suffices to
count the number of strictly descending label sequences from
u
to
v
of each length since
\mu_{P}(u,v)
equals the alternating
sum of these “descending chain” counts.
See e.g.
[
Bj95
]

[
St96
]
or
[

The original techniques (and still the predominant ones) for proving posets to be

Both CL-shellability and the related notion

The recursive atom orderings of
[
BW83
]
put a total order on the elements just above the unique minimal element
\hat{0}

in a finite bounded poset
P
, namely on the
“atoms” of
P

\hat{1}
denote the unique maximal element of
P

one of the requirements of an RAO is that for each atom
a_{j}
of
P
the closed interval
[a_{j},\hat{1}]
in

must also admit its own

of

[a_{j},\hat{1}]
, and continuing in this manner to choose atoms of progressively smaller and smaller intervals some finite number of times,
this is equivalent to choosing a path

\hat{0}

[u,\hat{1}]
have an RAO with respect to this choice of
r

[u,\hat{1}]

An RAO yields a total order on the maximal chains of

[u,\hat{1}]_{r}

[u,\hat{1}]_{r}

the total order on maximal chains. This is proven to be a shelling order for
\Delta(P)
in
[
BW83
]
.

In Section
3
, we introduce the notion of generalized recursive atom ordering (see Definition
3.3
). In order to describe what differentiates
the concept of a GRAO from that of an RAO, we first give a few definitions that this will require. Define a chain-atom ordering for a finite bounded poset
P
to be a choice for each
u\in P
and each root
r
of a total ordering for the atoms of the rooted interval
[u,\hat{1}]_{r}
.
While readers familiar with the notion of recursive atom ordering may typically think of an RAO as a total ordering on only the atoms of
P
,
the definition of RAO ensures that any RAO is extendible to one of these chain-atom orderings in a particular way.
It will be convenient throughout this paper to regard RAOs and GRAOs both as especially nice types of chain-atom
orderings.
Next we recall the definitions of
the sets
F_{r}(u)
and
G_{r}(u)
that were
introduced by Björner and Wachs in
[
BW83
]
. For any rooted interval
[u,\hat{1}]_{r}
in a finite bounded poset
P
,
let
u^{-}
be the element in
r
that is covered by
u
and let
r^{-}
be the saturated chain from
\hat{0}
to
u^{-}
obtained from
r
by omitting
u
.
Given any chain-atom ordering for
P
, let
F_{r}(u)
be the set of atoms of
[u,\hat{1}]_{r}
that cover an earlier atom of
[u^{-},\hat{1}]_{r^{-}}
than
u
.
Let
G_{r}(u)
be the set of atoms of
[u,\hat{1}]_{r}
not in
F_{r}(u)
.

Now to the distinction between GRAO and RAO.
An RAO requires for each
u\in P
and each choice of root
r
for
[u,\hat{1}]
that all of the elements of
F_{r}(u)
come earlier in the induced RAO of
[u,\hat{1}]_{r}
than all of the elements of
G_{r}(u)
. In a GRAO, this is replaced by a requirement on only the earliest atom of
[u,\hat{1}]_{r}
(and of
[u,v]_{r}
for each
v>u
), thereby giving much more flexibility in how atoms of rooted intervals
are ordered.

In one of the main results of the paper, Theorem
4.12
, we prove that a finite bounded
poset admits a recursive atom ordering if and only if it admits a generalized recursive atom ordering.
While every RAO is a GRAO, as is proven in Lemma
4.2
, the converse it not true; Figure 2 gives an example showing this.
However, we provide
an algorithm that transforms any GRAO into an RAO on the same poset
P
, a procedure we call the “atom reordering process” (see Definition
4.4
).
This proceeds from bottom to top in
P
, reordering the atoms of each rooted interval
[u,\hat{1}]_{r}
in turn, doing so in a way that moves the elements of
F_{r}(u)
ahead of the elements of
G_{r}(u)
for each
u
and each
r
while preserving the relative order of the elements within
F_{r}(u)
and within
G_{r}(u)
.
This procedure transforms the GRAO shown on the left in Figure 2 into the RAO shown on the right in Figure 2.

We establish some fundamental properties of GRAOs and of the atom
reordering process in Sections
3
and
4
, respectively, before proving Theorem
4.12
. In Lemma
3.9
, we show that the restriction of a GRAO to any
rooted interval is itself a GRAO. In Theorem
3.17
, we prove that any local move on a GRAO switching the order of two consecutive atoms in a rooted interval

[u,\hat{1}]_{r}
where neither is the first atom, subject to closely related further conditions, yields a new chain-atom ordering that is itself a GRAO.
We prove in Lemma
4.8
that whichever atom comes first in any given rooted interval of a GRAO still comes first at the conclusion of the atom reordering process.
We use Lemma
4.8
to deduce in Lemma
4.9
that the atom reordering process may be carried out by a series of local moves of the type arising in Theorem

3.17
above, implying that applying the atom reordering process to a GRAO yields a GRAO. Equipped with these results,
Theorem
4.12
shows that applying the reordering process to a GRAO yields not only a GRAO but an RAO.

Sections
5
and
6
establish a link between a finite bounded poset
P
admitting a GRAO and being CC-shellable (a type of lexicographic shellability introduced in
[
Ko
]
).
In CC-shellability, the notion from CL-shellability of ascent in a pair of consecutive labels is relaxed
to a much less restrictive topological notion where consecutive cover relations
u\lessdot v\lessdot w
comprise a topological ascent in a CC-labeling (with respect to a choice of root
r
) if and only if
u\lessdot v\lessdot w
is the lexicographically earliest saturated chain in the rooted interval
[u,w]_{r}
.
Instead of requiring each rooted interval to have a unique ascending chain and for this to be lexicographically first, one instead requires a unique topologically ascending chain (i.e. one comprised entirely of topological ascents).
Theorems
6.1
,
5.8
and
6.3
combine to show
that a finite bounded poset admits a GRAO if and only if it admits a CC-shelling with a further property we introduce in Definition
5.1

called self-consistency. This gives a GRAO analogue of the result of Björner and Wachs (see
[
BW83
]
and
[
BW96
]
)
that a finite bounded poset is CL-shellable if and only if it admits a recursive atom ordering. We deduce
that the class of CL-shellable posets is the same as the class of self-consistent CC-shellable posets.

Example
5.6
(and other such examples) lead us to believe that self-consistency is quite possibly a necessary condition
on a CC-shelling in order for this to imply the existence of a CL-shelling for the same poset. However, it remains an open question
whether CC-shellability implies CL-shellability without the self-consistency hypothesis.
Probably the most important (i.e. the most prevalent and most easily checkable)
class of self-consistent chain-edge labelings are those with a property
we dub
the “unique earliest property” (abbreviated as “UE property”), a condition that is less technical to define than self-consistency. We say that a chain-edge labeling
for a finite bounded poset
P
has the UE property if for each
u\in P
and each choice of root
r
,
the smallest label appearing on any cover relation upward from
u
in
[u,v]_{r}
appears on only one such cover relation.

Our work relating CC-shellability to the property of admitting a GRAO also led us to introduce a variation on the notion of CC-shellability
in Section
5.1
, namely topological CL-shellability (see Definition
5.7
).
It is easy to see that every CL-labeling is a topological CL-labeling, though it is not true that every
CL-labeling is a CC-labeling. We prove that CC-shellability implies topological CL-shellability.
This mild variation on Kozlov’s notion of
CC-shellability (and similarly on Kozlov’s closely related edge-labeling condition called EC-shellability) seems like it could be a worthwhile gentle
update to his theory.

Another main result in our paper is Theorem
6.4
. This collects together in one place many of the implications proven throughout the paper
(along with two of the main results from
[
BW83
]
). The statement of Theorem
6.4
is as follows:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
P
be a finite, bounded poset. Then the following are equivalent:

(1)

P
admits a recursive atom ordering

(2)

P
admits a generalized recursive atom ordering

(3)

P
admits a CL-labeling

(4)

P
admits a CL-labeling with the UE property

(5)

P
admits a self-consistent CC-labeling.

(6)

P
admits a CC-labeling with the UE property

(7)

P
admits a self-consistent topological CL-labeling

(8)

P
admits a topological CL-labeling with the UE property

Moreover, all of these implications are constructive.

In Section
7
, we apply our results to deduce that a class of posets previously proven CC-shellable in
[
HK
]
is in fact CL-shellable. That is, we prove that the dual posets to the uncrossing orders (conjectured lexicographically shellable by Lam in
[
La14a
]
) are CL-shellable. These uncrossing orders arise naturally as face posets of stratified spaces of planar electrical networks (see e.g.
[
Ke
]
,
[
La14a
]
, and references therein). They are CW posets, i.e. face posets of regular CW complexes, by virtue of being Eulerian (proven in
[
La14a
]
) and shellable.
To deduce dual CL-shellability of uncrossing orders, we check that the EC-labeling (a special type of CC-labeling) from
[

]
for the dual uncrossing orders has the UE property. Equipped with this, we may use our upcoming results (1) that the UE property implies self-consistency, (2) that any finite bounded poset with a self-consistent CC-labeling has a GRAO, and (3) that any such GRAO may be transformed into an RAO. Thus, we show that the dual uncrossing posets have an RAO. But
[
BW83
]
proved that having an
RAO implies CL-shellability, giving the final step in the proof
that dual uncrossing orders are CL-shellable.

The paper concludes with further results, observations, and open questions. In Section
8.1
, we show how several well-known EL-labelings and CL-labelings in the literature have the UE property in ways that are easy to verify.
Section
8.2
shows how to go directly from a GRAO to a self-consistent CC-labeling without needing to construct an RAO as an intermediate step.
Section
8.3
reviews the construction of Björner and Wachs of a CL-labeling from any RAO, included as a point of comparison to our constructions.
Section
8.4
notes how a result of Björner relating shellability of

-complexes to dual CL-shellability of their face posets may be modified in light of our results
to a slightly stronger statement about self-consistent dual CC-shellability instead. This result of Björner that is discussed in Section
8.4
is also
combined with our result in Theorem

that uncrossing orders are dual CL-shellable to deduce shellability of the

d
-complexes having uncrossing posets as their face posets.
Finally, in Section
8.5
we raise the question of whether the notion of GRAO may be extended to balanced complexes, and we describe some potential applications this could have.

Background

Let
P
denote a partially ordered set (poset). All posets throughout this paper are assumed to be finite and bounded but are not assumed to be graded. For background on posets, poset topology, and shellability beyond what appears below, we refer readers to
[
St12
]
,
[
Wa
]
,
[
Bj95
]
,
[
St96
]
, and
[
Zi
]
.

A
cover relation

u\lessdot v
in a poset
P
is an order relation
u<v
with the further requirement that there does not exist any
z\in P
with
u<z<v
. In this case, we say that
v

covers

u
.

A poset
P
is
bounded
if it has both a unique least element (often denoted
\hat{0}
) and a unique greatest element (often denoted
\hat{1}
).
A
closed interval
, denoted
[u,v]
, in a poset
P
is the subposet consisting of all elements
z\in P
such that
u\leq z\leq v
.

The
atoms
of a bounded poset
P
(resp. a closed interval
[u,v]
)
are those elements
a
in
P
(resp.
[u,v]
)
that cover
\hat{0}
(resp.
u
).
Likewise the
coatoms
of a bounded poset
P
(resp. a closed interval
[u,v]
)
are the elements in
P
(resp.
[u,v]
)
covered by
\hat{1}
(resp.
v
).

A
chain
in a poset
P
is a totally ordered subset
u_{1}<\cdots<u_{r}
of
P
. The
length
of a chain
u_{1}<\cdots<u_{r}
is the number
r-1
of order relations in the chain. When we speak of the
length
of a poset
P
, we mean the length of the longest chain in
P
.
A chain is
maximal
in
P
if no additional elements of
P
may be inserted in it. A chain
u_{1}<u_{2}<\cdots<u_{k}
is
saturated
in
P
if it is a maximal chain of
[u_{1},u_{k}]
; we will also make the convention of sometimes referring to the maximal chains of a closed interval
[u_{1},u_{k}]
as the saturated chains of
[u_{1},u_{k}]
. If all maximal chains in a poset
P
are of the same length, the poset is said to be
graded
.

Definition 2.1
.

The
order complex
of a poset
P
, denoted
\Delta(P)
, is the abstract simplicial complex whose
k-
faces are the chains of length
k
of
P
, so in particular the vertices are the chains consisting of single elements of
P
. Thus, a face in
\Delta(P)
is contained in another face in
\Delta(P)
if and only if the chain corresponding to the first face is contained in the chain corresponding to the second face.

In what follows, when we say that a poset
P
has a topological property (such as shellability or homotopy equivalence to a wedge of spheres), we mean by this that
\Delta(P)
has this topological property.

The
dual
of a poset
P
, denoted
P^{*}
, has the same elements as
P
with
u\leq v
in
P^{*}
if and only if
v\leq u
in
P
. We will soon use the fact that
\Delta(P)=\Delta(P^{*})
and specifically its consequence that any “lexicographic shelling” for
P^{*}
will give a shelling for
\Delta(P)
.

Definition 2.2
.

Given any simplicial complex
K
, its
face poset

P(K)
consists of the faces of
K
with order relation

\sigma\leq\tau
if and only if the set of vertices in
\sigma
is a subset of the set of vertices in
\tau
. The
closure
of a face
\tau
, denoted
\overline{\tau}
, is the set of faces
\sigma
such that
\sigma\leq\tau
in
P(K)
.

The
augmented face poset

\hat{P}(K)
is
P(K)
with a maximal element
\hat{1}
adjoined, provided that
P(K)
does not already have a unique maximal element, and
\hat{P}(K)=P(K)
otherwise.

Definition 2.3
.

A simplicial complex is
shellable
if there is a total order
F_{1},\dots,F_{k}
on its maximal faces (known as facets) such that

\overline{F_{j}}\cap(\cup_{i<j}\overline{F_{i}})
is a pure subcomplex of
\overline{F_{j}}
of codimension one for each
j\geq 2
. Such a facet ordering is known as a
shelling
.

Definition 2.4
.

A poset
P
is said to be
shellable
if its order complex
\Delta(P)
is shellable.

Next we review various types of edge and chain-edge labelings of a finite poset
P
that will induce lexicographic shellings for
\Delta(P)
, i.e. shellings obtained by ordering the facets of
P
by ordering label sequences on the corresponding maximal chains of
P

lexicographically (namely in dictionary order).
Let
E(P)
be the set of edges in the Hasse diagram of a finite poset
P
, namely the pairs
x,y\in P
such that
x\lessdot y
. An
edge-labeling
of
P
is
a map
\lambda:E(P)\rightarrow Q
for
Q
a poset (e.g.
Q
is quite often the integers with their usual order).

We say that
x\lessdot y\lessdot z
is an
ascent
with respect to the edge-labeling
\lambda
if

\lambda(x,y)<_{Q}\lambda(y,z)
. Any
x\lessdot y\lessdot z
not giving an ascent with respect to
\lambda
is said to be a
descent
.
A maximal chain in a finite poset
P
(or more generally in a closed interval
[u,v]
in
P
)
is an
ascending chain
if it consists entirely of ascents.

Definition 2.5

(
[
Bj80
]
,
[
BW97
]
)
.

An edge labeling of a finite, bounded poset
P
is called an
EL-labeling
(or edge lexicographical labeling) if for every interval
[x,y]\in P
the following conditions are both met.

(i)

There is a unique ascending maximal chain
c
in
[x,y]
.

(ii)

The label sequence associated to
c
lexicographically precedes the label sequences associated to every other maximal chain in
[x,y]
.

A finite, bounded poset admitting such a labeling is
EL-shellable
.

Björner first introduced EL-labelings and EL-shellability in the graded case in
[
Bj80
]
, and then this was generalized by Björner and Wachs to the not necessarily
graded case when they introduced the notion on nonpure shellability in
[
BW96
]
and
[
BW97
]
.
The following fundamental result from
[
Bj80
]
(which was generalized to the not necessarily graded
case in
[
BW96
]
) explains the usage of the term EL-shellable above.

Theorem 2.6

(
[
Bj80
]
, Theorem 2.3)
.

If
P
is a graded poset with an EL-labeling, then the lexicographic order of the maximal chains of
P
is a shelling order for the corresponding facets of
\Delta(P)
.

Now we turn to a relaxation of EL-shellability due to Björner and Wachs (see
[
BW83
]
)
in which edge labelings are replaced by more general chain-edge labelings (defined next).
In this context, we replace
E(P)
by the set
E^{*}(P)
defined as follows.

E^{*}(P)=\{(c,x,y):\mbox{$c$ is a maximal chain};x,y\in c;x\lessdot y\}.

Definition 2.7
.

Let
P
be a finite bounded poset and let
Q
be any poset. A
chain-edge labeling
(or
CE-labeling
) of
P
is a map
\lambda:E^{*}(P)\rightarrow Q
, that satisfies the following condition:
If two maximal chains coincide along their first
d
edges, then their labels also coincide along these edges. In other words, if
c
is the chain
\hat{0}=x_{0}\lessdot x_{1}\lessdot\ldots\lessdot x_{n}=\hat{1}
and
c^{\prime}
is the chain
\hat{0}=x_{0}^{\prime}\lessdot x_{1}^{\prime}\lessdot\ldots\lessdot x_{n}^{\prime}=\hat{1}
where
x_{i}=x_{i}^{\prime}
for
i=0,1,2,\dots,d
, then this implies
\lambda(c,x_{i-1},x_{i})=\lambda(c^{\prime},x_{i-1}^{\prime},x_{i}^{\prime})
for
i=1,2,\dots,d
.

Definition 2.8
.

If
[x,y]
is an interval in
P
and
r
is a saturated chain from
\hat{0}
to
x
, then the pair
([x,y],r)
is called a
rooted interval
with
r
as the
root
of this rooted interval. This is denoted by
[x,y]_{r}
.

Given a chain-edge labeling
\lambda
of a finite bounded poset
P
,
\lambda
associates to each maximal chain of
P
a label sequence as follows. If
m=(\hat{0}=x_{0}\lessdot x_{1}\lessdot\ldots\lessdot x_{n}=\hat{1})
, then the associated label sequence is

\lambda(m):=(\lambda(m,x_{0},x_{1}),\lambda(m,x_{1},x_{2}),\ldots\lambda(m,x_{n-1},x_{n})).

By definition of chain-edge labeling, any two maximal chains both containing the same root
r
from
\hat{0}
to
x
and the same saturated chain
c
in the interval
[x,y]
will both assign the same label sequence to
c
. We will denote this label sequence by
\lambda_{r}(c)
and the individual labels comprising it as
\lambda_{r}(x_{i},x_{i+1})
for
i=0,\dots,n-1
. Typically we will write
\lambda(c)
for the label sequence and
\lambda(x_{i},x_{i+1})
for
i=0,1,\dots,n-1
if the choice of root
r
is clear from context.

Given a label sequence
(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{r})
, we say that a pair of consecutive labels
\lambda_{i},\lambda_{i+1}
comprises an
ascent
if and only if
\lambda_{i}<\lambda_{i+1}
. The pair
\lambda_{i},\lambda_{i+1}
comprises a
descent
otherwise.

Definition 2.9
.

A maximal chain
c
in a rooted interval
[x,y]_{r}
is
ascending
with respect to a chain-edge labeling
\lambda
if the label sequence
\lambda_{r}(c)=(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\dots,\lambda_{r})
has
\lambda_{i}<\lambda_{i+1}
for
i=1,2,\dots,r-1
.

Definition 2.10

(
[
BW83
]
)
.

A CE-labeling
\lambda
of a finite bounded poset

P
is called a
CL-labeling
(for chain-lexicographical labeling) if for every rooted interval
[x,y]_{r}
in
P
,

(i)

there is a unique ascending chain
c
in
[x,y]_{r}
and

(ii)

the label sequence
\lambda_{r}(c)
lexicographically precedes the label sequence for every other maximal chain in
[x,y]_{r}
.

If a finite bounded poset
P
admits a CL-labeling, then
P
is said to be
CL-shellable
.

Björner and Wachs proved in
[
BW83
]
(resp.
[
BW96
]
) that whenever a finite bounded poset
P
that is graded (resp. is not necessarily graded) admits a CL-labeling, then any linear extension of the lexicographic order on its maximal chains given by the CL-labeling is a shelling order on the corresponding facets of
\Delta(P)
.

Since
\Delta(P)=\Delta(P^{*})
,
a CL-labeling for
P^{*}
also gives a shelling for
\Delta(P)
. We call a CL-labeling of
P^{*}
a
dual CL-labeling
of
P
, and we call a poset
P
such that
P^{*}
admits a CL-labeling
dual CL-shellable
.

One of the primary techniques for proving that a finite bounded poset is CL-shellable is to construct a recursive atom ordering (cf. Definition
2.11
). The notion of recursive atom ordering (RAO) was introduced by Björner and Wachs in
[
BW83
]
. They extended it to posets that are not necessarily graded
in
[
BW97
]
.

Definition 2.11
.

A finite bounded poset
P
admits a
recursive atom ordering
if
P
has length 1 or if the atoms of
P
can be ordered
a_{1},a_{2},\ldots a_{t}
such that:

(i)

(a)

For all
j=1,\ldots,t
,
[a_{j},\hat{1}]
admits a recursive atom ordering.

(b)

For
j\neq 1
, the atoms that come first in this recursive atom ordering for
[a_{j},\hat{1}]
are those that are greater than some atom
a_{k}
of
P
for
k<j
.

(ii)

For all
i<j
and
y\in P
satisfying
y>a_{i}
and
y>a_{j}
, there exists
k<j
and
z\in P
such that
a_{j}\lessdot z
and
a_{k}<z\leq y
.

The following theorem of Björner and Wachs from
[
BW83
]
(which they extended to the not necessarily graded case in
[
BW97
]
) established a very useful relationship between the existence of a recursive atom ordering and of a CL-labeling for any finite bounded poset
P
.

Theorem 2.12

(
[
BW83
]
, Theorem 3.2)
.

A finite bounded poset
P
admits a recursive atom ordering if and only if
P
is CL-shellable.

A key ingredient in their proof (that we will also use and generalize later) is the pair of sets
F_{r}(u)
and
G_{r}(u)
, defined as follows.

Definition 2.13
.

Let
\lambda
be a recursive atom ordering for a finite bounded poset
P
. Consider any rooted interval
[u,\hat{1}]_{r}
in
P
, letting
u^{-}
be the element of
r
covered by
u
and
letting
r^{-}
be the root for
[u^{-},\hat{1}]
obtained by omitting
u
from
r
and otherwise preserving
r
.

Define

F_{r}(u)
to be the set of atoms
a\in[u,\hat{1}]_{r}
such that
a^{-}<a
for some atom
a^{-}
in
[u^{-},\hat{1}]_{r^{-}}
that comes earlier than
u
in the RAO. Define

G_{r}(u)
to be the set of all atoms of
[u,\hat{1}]_{r}
that are not contained in
F_{r}(u)
.

Björner and Wachs also introduced the following dual notion to recursive atom ordering:

Definition 2.14
.

A finite bounded poset
P
admits a
recursive coatom ordering
if its dual poset
P^{*}
admits a recursive atom ordering.

We next review the notions of CC-shellability and EC-shellability from
[
Ko
]
, doing so using the language of topological ascents and descents (defined next) that was
introduced in
[
He03A
]
. Let
\lambda
be an edge labeling on the cover relations of a poset
P
by elements of some poset
Q
.
A
topological ascent
occurs in
P
at such
u\lessdot v\lessdot w
whenever the ordered pair of labels
(\lambda(u,v)
,
\lambda(v,w))
is lexicographically earlier than all other ordered sequences of labels on all other saturated chains from
u
to
w
. If
(\lambda(u,v),\lambda(v,w))
is not a topological ascent, then it is a
topological descent
.

For
\lambda
a chain-edge labeling, we define topological ascents and descents in the same way, but now with respect to a choice of root. That is, we have a
topological ascent
at
u\lessdot v\lessdot w
with respect to root
r
if the ordered pair
(\lambda(u,v),\lambda(v,w))
is lexicographically smaller than all other label sequences on saturated chains from
u
to
w
with this same root
r
. We have a
topological descent
at
u\lessdot v\lessdot w
otherwise.

If
c
is a saturated chain from an element
u
to an element
v
consisting entirely of topological ascents (in either an edge labeling or a chain-edge labeling), then we say that
c
is a
topologically ascending
chain from
u
to
v
. If
c
is a saturated chain from
u
to
v
consisting entirely of topological descents, then it is said to be
topologically descending
.

The next definition is rephrased from how it appears in
[
Ko
]
, using the language of topological ascents and descents, but is entirely equivalent to his definition.

Definition 2.15

(
[
Ko
]
)
.

An
EC-labeling
of a finite bounded poset
P
is an edge labeling
\lambda:E(P)\rightarrow Q
on the cover relations of
P
with labels belonging to a poset
Q
, subject to the requirements that (1) every interval
[x,y]
has a unique saturated chain consisting entirely of topological ascents, and (2) the label sequences for the saturated chains of
P
(and hence of each interval
[x,y]
) are all distinct with no label sequence being a prefix of any other label sequence.

If
P
admits an EC-labeling, then
P
is said to be
EC-shellable
.

Just as EL-shellability was generalized to CL-shellability, the notion of EC-shellability was likewise generalized by Kozlov
from edge labelings to chain-edge labelings as follows.

Definition 2.16

(
[
Ko
]
)
.

A
CC-labeling
of a finite bounded poset
P
is a chain-edge labeling
\lambda:E^{*}(P)\rightarrow Q
with labels belonging to a poset
Q
, subject to the requirements that (1) every rooted interval
[u,v]_{r}
has a unique saturated chain consisting entirely of topological ascents, and (2) the label sequences for the saturated chains of
[u,v]_{r}
are all distinct with no label sequence being a prefix of another label sequence.

If a finite bounded poset
P
admits a CC-labeling, then
P
is said to be
CC-shellable
.

Definition 2.17
.

If the dual poset
P^{*}
to a finite bounded poset
P
admits an EC-labeling (resp. CC-labeling), then

P
is said to be
dual EC-shellable
(resp.
dual CC-shellable
).

Kozlov proved in Theorem 3.8 of
[
Ko
]
that the order complex of any finite bounded poset
P
admitting a CC-labeling is
shellable, doing so by taking any linear extension of the lexicographic
order on the label sequences for the maximal chains of
P
as the shelling order for the
corresponding facets of
\Delta(P)
.

Kozlov noted in
[
Ko
]
that any finite bounded poset that is CL-shellable is also CC-shellable, leaving the proof to the reader. We include now a proof of this result as
Proposition
2.18
. Our proof below was inspired by
the proof of Björner and Wachs that any recursive atom ordering induces a CL-labeling (using the sets
F_{r}(u)
and
G_{r}(u)
which we review in Definition

2.13
). This proof is included because it
introduces several important ideas that will be used in various ways in other proofs
later in the paper.

Proposition 2.18
.

Any recursive atom ordering
a_{1},\dots,a_{t}
of a finite bounded poset
P
gives rise to a CC-labeling of
P
. Thus,
any finite bounded poset that is CL-shellable is CC-shellable.

Proof.

We start by describing the desired CC-labeling
\lambda
. First to each cover relation of the form
\hat{0}\lessdot a_{i}
, we assign the label

\lambda(\hat{0},a_{i})=i
. Now for any
u\in P
and any root leading up to
u
, consider any recursive atom ordering
a_{1}^{\prime},\dots,a_{t^{\prime}}^{\prime}
for
[u,\hat{1}]_{r}

of the type guaranteed to exist recursively in the definition of RAO, so in particular choose one with suitable consistency with
a_{1},\dots,a_{t}
and the atom
orderings already specified lower in the poset. Assign the labels
\lambda_{r}(u,a_{i}^{\prime})=i
for
i=1,2,\dots,t^{\prime}
.

By construction, this labeling will have the requisite properties of a CC-labeling that no two saturated chains of
[u,\hat{1}]_{r}
have the same label sequence and also that neither
label sequence is a prefix of the other. One may use the reasoning from the proof of Björner and Wachs that any RAO gives rise to a chain-edge labeling in which each rooted interval has a unique ascending chain and that this label sequence is lexicographically smallest to show likewise that for
labeling
\lambda
that we have constructed (which is a close relative of the labeling of Björner and Wachs) that each rooted interval has a unique topologically ascending chain and that this is the lexicographically smallest label sequence on the rooted interval. Specifically, we use the observation from
[
BW83
]
that
for each
u\in P
and each root
r
for
[u,\hat{1}]
all of the elements of
F_{r}(u)
come earlier than all of the elements of
G_{r}(u)
in the atom ordering for
[u,\hat{1}]_{r}
given
by the recursive atom ordering. Letting
u^{-}
be the element covered by
u
in
r
, one may readily check that our proposed CC-labeling will have a topological descent at

u^{-}\lessdot u\lessdot a
for
a\in[u,\hat{1}]_{r}
with respect to the saturated chain upward from
\hat{0}
to
u^{-}
that coincides with
r
if and only if
a\in F(u)
, allowing the argument
of Björner and Wachs to be applied with ascents replaced by topological ascents and descents replaced
by topological descents throughout their argument. Thus, we confirm that
this labeling is a CC-labeling.

The final claim is obtained by combining the above argument with the result of
[
BW83
]
that a finite bounded poset is CL-shellable if and only if it admits a recursive atom ordering.
∎

3.
Generalized recursive atom ordering

In this section we introduce a generalization of
the notion of recursive atom ordering and prove several fundamental properties of these generalized recursive atom orderings.

Definition 3.1
.

A
chain-atom ordering
of a finite bounded poset
P
is a choice of ordering on the atoms of each rooted interval

[u,\hat{1}]_{r}
of
P
.

Remark 3.2
.

A recursive atom ordering is an ordering of just the atoms of a finite graded poset
P
, but it is an atom ordering for which their exists one or more extensions
to chain-atom orderings of
P
of the type that is guaranteed to exist by the various requirements in the definition of an RAO.
It is often useful to abuse notation and think of a recursive atom ordering as such a chain-atom ordering rather than as an ordering of just the atoms.

The generalized recursive atom orderings we are about to introduce will likewise be defined as orderings on the atoms of a finite graded poset
P
that are extendible to chain-atom orderings of
P
in the manner guaranteed to exist by the requirements for a GRAO. In upcoming proofs, it will often be convenient to abuse notation and think of a generalized recursive atom ordering as a type of chain-atom ordering rather than as an ordering of just the atoms. That is, many of our results regarding GRAOs are proven by taking the atom ordering of any GRAO and proving the result for every chain-atom ordering of the type guaranteed to exist as an extension of the atom ordering of the given GRAO.

Having just made this critical remark, now we are ready to give our main new definition:

Definition 3.3
.

A finite bounded poset
P
admits a
generalized recursive atom ordering
(GRAO) if the length of
P
is 1 or if the length of
P
is greater than 1 and there is an ordering
a_{1},a_{2},\ldots a_{t}
on the atoms of
P
satisfying:

(i)

(a)

For
1\leq j\leq t
,
[a_{j},\hat{1}]
admits a GRAO.

(b)

For any atom
a_{j}
and any
x,w\in P
satisfying
a_{j}\lessdot x\lessdot w
, the following property holds when the chain-atom ordering given by the
GRAO from (i)(a) is restricted to
[a_{j},w]
: either the first atom of
[a_{j},w]
is above an
atom
a_{i}
with
i<j
, or no atom of
[a_{j},w]
is above any atom
a_{i}
with
i<j
.

(ii)

Given any atoms
a_{i},a_{j}
satisfying
a_{i}<y
and
a_{j}<y
with
i<j
, then there exists an atom
a_{k}
with
k<j
and an element

z\in P
such that
a_{j}\lessdot z\leq y
and
a_{k}<z\leq y
.

Remark 3.4
.

Conditions (i)(a) and (ii) in the definition of GRAO are exactly equivalent to corresponding statements within the definition of recursive atom ordering. However,
condition (i)(b) above is considerably less restrictive than (i)(b) of the definition of RAO.

One of the main points of this relaxation of the notion of recursive atom ordering
is that it is designed to give considerably more flexibility than a recursive atom ordering in ordering those atoms in a rooted interval
[u,\hat{1}]_{r}
that are not the
first atom of that interval. For this reason, the following rephrasing of condition (ii) also seems quite useful to note:

Remark 3.5
.

Condition (ii) in Definition
3.3
is logically equivalent to the following statement: if
a_{i}<y
and
a_{j}<y
for
i<j
,
then there exists
z\in P
such that
a_{j}\lessdot z\leq y
where
a_{j}
is not the earliest atom in
[\hat{0},z]
.

Naturally, it also makes sense to establish the following dual notion:

Definition 3.7
.

A
generalized recursive coatom ordering
(GRCO) of a finite bounded poset
P
is
a GRAO of the dual poset
P^{*}
.

Remark 3.8
.

Implicit in the definition of GRAO is that a GRAO for a finite bounded poset
P
induces a GRAO for
[u,\hat{1}]_{r}
for each
u\in P
and each root
r
.
Lemma
3.9
below will show that it also induces a GRAO for each
[u,v]_{r}
. This result will allow us henceforth to refer
to the restriction of any GRAO for a finite bounded poset to its rooted interval
[u,v]_{r}
as the GRAO for
[u,v]_{r}
induced by the GRAO for
P
, as we indeed do in Lemmas
3.15
and
3.16
as well as Theorem
3.17
.

Lemma 3.9
.

Let
P
be a finite bounded poset that admits a GRAO
\Gamma
. Then for any
u<v
and any root
r
for
[u,v]
, restricting the GRAO for
[u,\hat{1}]_{r}
induced by
\Gamma
to
[u,v]_{r}
yields a GRAO for
[u,v]_{r}
.

Proof.

Our proof will be by induction on the length of the longest saturated chain from
u
to
\hat{1}
. We can use maximum length 2 for our base case, obtaining this case by using the fact that every possible chain-atom ordering on a finite bounded poset with maximum chain length of 1 or 2 is a GRAO.
We then obtain condition (i)(a) by induction as follows. Given any
a_{i}
satisfying
u\lessdot a_{i}\leq v
, the length of the longest saturated chain from
u
to
\hat{1}
is strictly larger than the length of the longest saturated chain from
a_{i}
to
\hat{1}
.
Thus, our inductive hypothesis gives us that the GRAO for
[a_{i},\hat{1}]_{r\cup a_{i}}
will restrict to a GRAO for
[a_{i},v]_{r\cup a_{i}}
. But it is immediate from the definition of GRAO that the properties of a GRAO given in conditions (i)(b) and (ii) from the definition of GRAO will restrict from
[u,\hat{1}]_{r}
to
[u,v]_{r}
. Thus, the GRAO for
P
restricts just as desired.
∎

Next we show how the statement about cover relations in condition (i)(b) in the definition of GRAO can be strengthened to a corresponding
statement about all order
relations.

Lemma 3.10
.

Let
P
be a finite bounded poset that admits a GRAO
a_{1},a_{2},\ldots a_{t}
. Then
for each
\hat{0}\lessdot a_{j}<v
,
the induced GRAO for
[a_{j},v]

has the following property: either (a) the first atom of
[a_{j},v]
is greater than some atom
a_{i}
satisfying
i<j
or (b) no atom of
[a_{j},v]
is greater than any atom

a_{i}
satisfying
i<j
.

Proof.

Let
x
be the first atom in the GRAO for
[a_{j},v]
. Suppose that some other atom
y
of
[a_{j},v]
is greater than some atom
a_{i}
with
i<j
. Since
x
and
y
are both less than
v
with
x
coming before
y
in the GRAO of
[a_{j},\hat{1}]
, Definition
3.3
(ii) guarantees the existence of elements
w_{1}
and
y_{1}
, where
y_{1}
comes before
y
in the GRAO of
[a_{j},\hat{1}]
and where
y\lessdot w_{1}<v
and
y_{1}<w_{1}
. Since
y
covers
a_{i}
with
i<j
, Definition
3.3
(i)(b) ensures that the first atom in
[a_{j},w_{1}]
must be greater than some atom
a_{i^{\prime}}
satisfying
i^{\prime}<j
. We may take
y_{1}
to be the first atom in
[a_{j},w_{1}]
, i.e. we replace
y_{1}
by this atom if it is not already this atom.
If
y_{1}
also equals
x
, then
x
is greater than some atom that comes before
a_{j}

4.
Equivalence of admitting a generalized recursive atom ordering to admitting a recursive atom ordering

In this section we prove that a finite bounded poset admits a generalized recursive atom ordering (see Definition
3.3
) if and only if it admits a recursive atom ordering.
We accomplish this constructively by a procedure that transforms any generalized recursive atom ordering into a recursive atom ordering, a process we call “atom reordering.”
Along the way, we will develop several properties of such reorderings.

We begin with the much easier half of the equivalence.

Remark 4.1
.

In the proof of the next result, we think of both an RAO and a GRAO as a type of chain-atom ordering rather than just as an atom ordering, following the conventions
explained already in Remark
3.2
.

Lemma 4.2

Every recursive atom ordering is a generalized recursive atom ordering.

Proof.

of a finite bounded poset whose longest saturated chain is of length 1 or 2 is a generalized recursive atom ordering, giving the base case.
Let

By induction, we may assume for each atom

that the RAO on

guaranteed to exist by condition (i)(a) of the definition of RAO is a GRAO, giving (i)(a) from the definition of GRAO.

Now we turn to condition

3.3

By the definition of

(see Definition
2.13

[a_{j},\hat{1}]

This implies that if any atom of

is greater than
a_{i}
for some
i<j

for some

[

[

]

Condition (ii) in the definition of GRAO is the same as condition (ii) in the definition of RAO, hence is guaranteed to hold for any RAO.

Remark 4.3
.

It is not true that every generalized recursive atom ordering is a recursive atom ordering. See Figure
3
for an example illustrating this.

Next we describe the atom reordering process that will allow us to transform any generalized recursive atom ordering into a recursive atom ordering. It may be useful to keep in
mind that the reordering process
is designed to produce a chain-atom ordering that will satisfy condition (i)(b) from the definition of recursive atom ordering, doing so in such a way that when the reodering process
is applied to a GRAO it
will preserve useful structure that is already present in a GRAO including condition
(ii) from the definition of GRAO.

Definition 4.4
.

Given a finite bounded poset
P
equipped with a chain-atom ordering
\Omega
, define the
reordering of
P
obtained from
\Omega
to be the new chain-atom ordering obtained by the following procedure. We will also sometimes call this procedure the
reordering of
\Omega
.

We proceed from bottom to top in
P
, reordering the atoms of the interval
[u,\hat{1}]_{r}
for each
u\in P
in turn with respect to every choice of root
r
, for
[u,\hat{1}]
. More precisely, we choose a linear extension
u_{1},u_{2},\dots,u_{N}
of
P
and apply our reordering procedure below to the atoms of
[u_{i},\hat{1}]_{r}
for each root
r
before applying it to the atoms of
[u_{j},\hat{1}]_{r^{\prime}}
for each root
r^{\prime}
whenever we have
i<j
. In particular, when reordering the atoms of a rooted interval
[u,\hat{1}]_{r^{\prime}}
we will have already reordered the atoms of
[u^{\prime},\hat{1}]_{r}
for each
u^{\prime}<u
.

Any linear extension of
P
must start with
u=\hat{0}
.
We leave the order on the atoms of
[\hat{0},\hat{1}]
unchanged. Next consider the case of
u=a_{q}
for
a_{q}
an atom of
P
. Here the reordering puts all of the elements of
F_{\hat{0}\lessdot a_{q}}(a_{q})
(see Definition
3.11
) before all of the elements of
G_{\hat{0}\lessdot a_{q}}(a_{q})
, while
preserving the relative order of the elements of
F_{\hat{0}\lessdot a_{q}}(a_{q})
and also preserving the relative order of the elements of
G_{\hat{0}\lessdot a_{q}}(a_{q})
.

We continue proceeding upward in
P
in this manner, reordering the atoms of
[u,\hat{1}]_{r}
for each
u\in P

encountered in turn
for each choice of root
r
. That is, we first calculate the sets
F_{r}(u)
and
G_{r}(u)

using the atom reordering done so far strictly lower in the poset. Then we put all of the elements of
F_{r}(u)
ahead of all of the elements of
G_{r}(u)
in our reordering of
the atoms of
[u,\hat{1}]_{r}
while preserving the relative order within the set
F_{r}(u)
and within the set
G_{r}(u)
.
Once the atoms of
[u,\hat{1}]_{r}
have been reordered for every
u\in P
and every choice of root
r
for
[u,\hat{1}]

proceeding from bottom to top through the elements
u\in P
,
the end result of this process is
the
reordering of
P
.

But this means that we may express the reordering process as a series of such steps by considering each

Now we are ready to prove the main result of this section.

Theorem 4.12
.

A finite bounded poset admits a generalized recursive atom ordering (GRAO) if and only if it admits a recursive atom ordering (RAO). Moreover, the atom reordering procedure given in Definition
4.4
transforms any GRAO into an RAO.

Proof.

By Lemma
4.2
, any RAO is a GRAO.
What remains is to prove that applying the reodering process from Definition
4.4
to any GRAO for a finite bounded poset
P
yields an RAO.
We will do this by induction on the length
d
of the longest saturated chain in
P

For
d=2
, any ordering of the atoms of
P
is a recursive atom ordering, giving the base case for our induction.

Now assuming the result for
d\leq n
, it will suffice to show that this implies the result for
d=n+1
.
Let
a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{q}
be the ordering of the atoms of
P
with
d=n+1
in a GRAO for
P
.
In this case, for each atom
a_{j}\in P
, we have that

[a_{j},\hat{1}]
is an interval whose longest saturated chain is of length at most
n
.
By condition (i)(a) in the definition of GRAO, the restriction of our GRAO for
P
to

[a_{j},\hat{1}]
is a GRAO for
[a_{j},\hat{1}]
.
By Lemma
4.9
, applying the reordering process to the GRAO for all of
P
and then restricting the resulting chain-atom ordering to

[a_{j},\hat{1}]
yields the same chain-atom ordering for
[a_{j},\hat{1}]
that
we get by instead permuting the atoms of
[a_{j},\hat{1}]
in a way that yields a new GRAO for
[a_{j},\hat{1}]
and then applying the reordering process directly to this
new GRAO on
[a_{j},\hat{1}]
. By our inductive hypothesis, the result of this process is an RAO for
[a_{j},\hat{1}]
. This implies that condition (i)(a) of Definition
2.11
holds for the chain-atom ordering resulting from the reordering process.
By definition of the reordering process and by

the atoms that come first in the reordering of

are those that are above some earlier atom than

What remains is to prove that condition (ii) of Definition

5.
Self-consistency, the UE property and topological CL-shellability

In this section, we introduce a condition that a CC-labeling may have called self-consistency. We also introduce a fairly natural and readily checkable property called the UE property that will imply self-consistency. We prove that all CL-labelings have the UE property.

These notions are introduced in preparation for a result later in the paper where we will prove that a finite bounded poset is CL-shellable if and only if it is CC-shellable by way of a self-consistent CC-labeling. To help us further clarify the relationship between the established notions of CC-shellability and CL-shellability, we also introduce a variation on CC-shellability that we call “topological CL-shellability”.

Definition 5.1
.

Consider a chain-edge labeling
\lambda
such that each rooted interval has a unique lexicographically earliest saturated chain. We define such
\lambda
to be
self-consistent
if for any rooted interval
[u,v]_{r}
we have the following condition: if
a
is the atom in the lexicographically first saturated chain of
[u,v]_{r}
and
b\neq a
is also an atom of
[u,v]_{r}
, then for any
[u,v^{\prime}]_{r}
containing
a
and
b
all saturated chains of
[u,v^{\prime}]_{r}
containing
b
come lexicographically later than all saturated chains of
[u,v^{\prime}]_{r}
containing
a
.
If a chain-edge labeling is not self-consistent, then it is said to be
self-inconsistent
.

Next we introduce a property that will imply self-consistency that is more readily checkable.

Definition 5.2
.

A chain-edge labeling
\lambda
of a finite bounded poset
P
has the
unique earliest (UE) property
if for each rooted interval
[u,v]_{r}
in
P
, the smallest label occurring on any cover relation upward from
u
only occurs on one such cover relation.

Lemma 5.3
.

If a CC-labeling has the UE property, then it is self-consistent.

Proof.

Let
a
be the unique atom of
[u,v]_{r}
for which
\lambda(u,a)
is smallest among all labels upward from
u
.
Consider any other atom
b\in[u,v]_{r}
and any
v^{\prime}
satisfying
u<v^{\prime}
with
a,b\in[u,v^{\prime}]
. The label sequences on saturated chains of
[u,v^{\prime}]_{r}
containing
a
must be lexicographically smaller than those containing
b
by virtue of
\lambda(u,a)
being smaller than
\lambda(u,b)
with respect to root
r
.
∎

Corollary 5.4
.

Any recursive atom ordering for a finite bounded poset gives rise to a CC-labeling with the UE property, hence to a self-consistent CC-labeling.

Proof.

Simply observe that the CC-labeling constructed in the proof of Proposition
2.18
has the UE property, then apply Lemma
5.3
.
∎

The next result gives some evidence that the UE property is not an unduly burdensome condition to impose.

.

Consider a pair of elements

5.1.
Topological CL-shellability: a variation on CC-shellability

One may replace Kozlov’s requirements of a CC-labeling (a) that saturated chains have distinct label sequences and (b) that no label sequence be a prefix of any other label sequence by simply requiring each rooted interval to have a unique lexicographically earliest saturated chain. With just this requirement, the proof of Björner and Wachs that a CL-labeling yields a shelling of the order complex will carry over using topological descents in place of descents and using topological ascents in place of ascents. In particular, the UE-property suffices in place of these two aforementioned conditions of Kozlov to guarantee we get a shelling.

This yields a class of poset chain-edge labelings inducing shellings that includes all CL-labelings, whether or not the labels upward from the lowest element
u
in any rooted interval
[u,v]_{r}
are all distinct from each other.
In justifying his assertion that CC-shellability is the most general possible version of lexicographic shellability, Kozlov notes in
[
Ko
]
that any CL-labeling may be modified into a CC-labeling, implying that every CL-shellable poset is CC-shellable.

Definition 5.7
.

A
topological CL-labeling
of a finite bounded poset
P
is a chain-edge labeling
\lambda
for
P
such that (i) each rooted interval
[u,v]_{r}
has a unique saturated chain from

u
to
v
with lexicographically smallest label sequence, and (ii) every other saturated chain from
u
to
v
in
[u,v]_{r}
has at least one topological descent.
Topological CL-shellability
is the property of having a topological CL-labeling.

We will sometimes refer to condition (i) in Definition
5.7
as the
UE-chain property
. Notice that it is implied by the UE property, but does not itself imply the UE property. In particular, there can be self-inconsistent topological CL-labelings (see, for example, Figure
6
).

Theorem 5.8
.

Any CC-labeling is a topological CL-labeling. However, not every topological CL-labeling is a CC-labeling.

Proof.

First notice that the requirement of a CC-labeling that the saturated chains in any rooted interval have distinct label sequences in particular implies the uniqueness of the lexicographically earliest label sequence on each rooted interval.
Also notice that condition (CC) in the definition of CC-labeling amounts exactly to the requirement that each rooted interval have a unique topologically ascending chain.
Thus, any self-consistent CC-labeling is a topological CL-labeling.

To see that the converse fails, simply consider the CL-labeling
\lambda
(which is also a topological CL-labeling) for a graded bounded poset of rank 2 consisting of elements

\hat{0},\hat{1},x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}
with
\hat{0}\lessdot x_{i}\lessdot\hat{1}
for
i=1,2,3
having
\lambda(\hat{0},x_{1})=\lambda(x_{2},\hat{1})=\lambda(x_{3},\hat{1})=1
and

\lambda(\hat{0},x_{2})=\lambda(\hat{0},x_{3})=\lambda(x_{1},\hat{1})=2
. This fails the requirement for CC-labelings that distinct saturated chains have distinct label sequences.
∎

Remark 5.9
.

It may very well be true that a finite bounded poset is CC-shellable if and only if it admits a topological CL-labeling. The labeling given in the second half of the
proof of Theorem
5.8
can easily be modified into a CC-labeling, and indeed it remains open whether these two notions of shellability are equivalent in terms
of which posets admit such shellings. Likewise, as far as we know it is open whether CC-shellability is equivalent to CL-shellability, though this seems to be quite a tricky question.

is a topological CL-labeling. One may also invoke Theorem

for the latter claim.

Theorem 6.4
.

(2)

admits a generalized recursive atom ordering

(3)

(4)

P
admits a CL-labeling with the UE property

(5)

P
admits a self-consistent CC-labeling.

(6)

P
admits a CC-labeling with the UE property

(7)

P
admits a self-consistent topological CL-labeling

(8)

P
admits a topological CL-labeling with the UE property

For each of the implications that we will prove, we will not only show
that any finite bounded poset endowed with one type of structure is also endowed
with another type of structure, but also that we have
an explicit construction to obtain the latter structure from the former. In some cases, we do this by showing that the given structure is always an instance of the
desired type of structure, implying that no construction is necessary to transform the one to the other.
In all other cases, the results we cite below are proven constructively, making all of our implications below constructive.

8.
Further results, remarks, and open questions

We conclude with some further connections between our work and results in the literature, some open questions and assorted other remarks.

8.1.
Classical EL-labelings and CL-labelings with the UE property

In light of the role of self-consistency in constructing a GRAO or a CL-labeling from a CC-labeling and also given the fact that self-consistency is implied by the UE property (see Lemma
5.3
), it is natural to ask how readily checkable the UE property is. We already proved that every CL-labeling (and hence every EL-labeling) is self-consistent in Lemma
5.5
. We now show how easy it is to verify the UE property directly for many of the classic examples of EL-labelings and CL-labelings. This together with the proof in
Section
7
of the UE property for the EC-labeling for dual uncrossing orders from
[
HK
]
may give readers some sense of how the UE property may well be readily
verifiable for many of
the sorts of CC-labelings and EC-labelings that people are likely to construct for posets of interest in the future.

Proposition 8.1
.

Let
L
be a geometric lattice and let
a_{1},\dots,a_{t}
be any total order on the atoms of
L
. For each
x\in L
, denote by
A(x)
the set of atoms
a\in L
satisfying
a\leq x
.
Then the EL-labeling
\lambda
for geometric lattices which labels each cover relation
u\lessdot v
with the smallest atom in
A(v)\setminus A(u)
satisfies the UE property.

Proof.

Notice for each
u\in L
and any cover relation
u\lessdot v
with edge label
a_{i}
that we must have
v=u\vee a_{i}
. In particular,
v
may be recovered from
u
and

\lambda(u,v)
, implying that the labels on the cover relations upward from
u
are all distinct from each other. Thus, the smallest such label can only occur once, as needed.
∎

Proposition 8.2
.

Let
L
be a finite, distributive lattice. Consider the EL-labeling obtained by first interpreting
L
as the poset
J(P)
of order ideals in
P
ordered by containment and then labeling the cover
relation
I\lessdot I\cup\{x\}
with the label
x
, using any linear extension on
P
to order the set of edge labels. Then this EL-labeling
\lambda
satisfies the UE property.

Proof.

Again the proof is based on observing for the cover relations upward from
u
that any element
v\in L
covering
u
may be determined from knowing
u
and
\lambda(u,v)
. This implies that the edge labels on cover relations upward from a fixed element
u
are all distinct. Since the smallest such label only occurs once, this implies the UE property.
∎

Next we consider an example in which some labels upward from a poset element
u
occur more than once, but where the smallest upward from
u
occurs only once:

Proposition 8.3
.

Let
\Pi_{n}
be the partition lattice, namely the lattice of set partitions of
\{1,2,\dots,n\}
ordered by refinement. That is,
\Pi_{n}
has
\pi\leq\tau
if and only if

\pi
is a refinement
of
\tau
or in other words each block of
\pi
is contained in a block of
\tau
.
Consider the EL-labeling
\lambda
of
\Pi_{n}
given by

\lambda(u,v)=\max(\min B_{1},\min B_{2})

where
B_{1}
and
B_{2}
are the two blocks of
u
being merged to obtain
v
.
Then
\lambda
has the UE property.

Proof.

Order the blocks
B_{1},\dots,B_{r}
of a set partition
u
in such a way
that the smallest element of
B_{i}
is smaller than the smallest element of
B_{j}
for
i<j
. By definition of
\lambda
, the smallest edge label that is achievable
on any cover relation upward from
u

is
\min B_{2}
. The only way this label arises is by merging blocks
B_{1}
and
B_{2}
. In particular, this smallest label only occurs on one cover relation upward from

u
.
This completes the proof of the UE property for this EL-labeling of
\Pi_{n}
. ∎

Proposition 8.4
.

Consider the dual CL-labeling for Bruhat order for any finite Coxeter group
W
that was given by Björner and Wachs in
[
BW82
]
. This dual CL-labeling has the UE property.

Proof.

First we recall the dual CL-labeling from
[
BW82
]
. Choose a reduced expression for the longest element
w_{0}(W)
. Proceed downward in Bruhat order from the maximal element
w_{0}(W)
, labeling each downward cover relation encountered in turn with the position of the letter being deleted from the chosen reduced expression for
w_{0}(W)
; here we are utilizing the fact that labels in a dual CL-labeling are allowed to depend not just on the cover relation being labeled but on the series of cover relations leading downward from
\hat{1}
to that cover relation.

Since the label downward from an element
v
to an element
u
covered by
v
is the position of the letter being deleted from a fixed reduced expression for
v
, using this same reduced expression for every cover relation downward from
v
, this label uniquely determines the resulting reduced expression. Hence the element
u
is uniquely determined by the value of the label on the cover relation. As all labels downward from
u
are therefore distinct, this dual CL-labeling has the UE property.
∎

8.2.
Obtaining a self-consistent CC-labeling directly from a GRAO

Our next result shows that one does not need to use the reordering process to convert a GRAO to an RAO in order to show that every finite bounded poset with a
GRAO has a self-consistent CC-labeling, that alternatively one may obtain a self-consistent CC-labeling directly from any GRAO.

Definition 8.5
.

Any CC-labeling

\lambda
for a finite bounded poset
P
gives rise to a total order on the maximal chains of
P
by ordering the label sequences for the maximal chains
lexicographically. On the other hand, any GRAO
\Gamma
for this same poset
P
gives rise to a total order on the maximal chains of
P
as we describe shortly.
We say that
\Gamma
is
consistent
with
\lambda
if
\Gamma
and
\lambda
both give rise to
the same total order on the maximal chains of
P
.

Now we describe the total order on maximal chains induced by
\Gamma
.
Given any two maximal chains
m_{1},m_{2}
of
P
, let
x
(resp.
x^{\prime}
) be the lowest
element in
m_{1}
(resp.
m_{2}
) that is not in

m_{2}
(resp.
m_{1}
). Let
u
be the element in both
m_{1}
and
m_{2}
that is covered by both
x
and
x^{\prime}
.
Denote by
r
the unique saturated chain from
\hat{0}
to
u
with the property that
r
is contained in both
m_{1}
and
m_{2}
.
In case this,
m_{1}
comes earlier than
m_{2}
in the total order on maximal chains induced by
\Gamma
if and only if
x
is an earlier atom than
x^{\prime}
in the GRAO for

[u,\hat{1}]_{r}
induced by
\Gamma
.

Theorem 8.6
.

Any generalized recursive atom ordering of a finite bounded poset
P
gives rise to a self-consistent CC-labeling which orders the maximal
chains of
P
consistently with the GRAO.

Proof.

Given a generalized recursive atom ordering (GRAO) for the poset
P
, construct the following
chain-labeling
\lambda
for
P
. Label the cover relation
u\lessdot a_{i}
in a rooted interval

[u,v]_{r}
with the label
i
for
a_{i}
the
i
th atom in the GRAO of
[u,v]_{r}
that is induced by the GRAO of
P
as justified in Lemma
3.9
.

By construction,
\lambda
has the property that distinct cover relations upward from
u
within
[u,v]_{r}
will have distinct labels on them, ensuring that this
chain-labeling will have the UE property and hence be self-consistent.
Also by construction,
\lambda
has the following pair of properties required for a CC-labeling (see Definition
2.16
):

(1)

The saturated chains on a rooted interval
[u,v]_{r}
have distinct label sequences.

(2)

No saturated chain in
[u,v]_{r}

has as its label sequence a prefix of the label sequence of another saturated chain in
[u,v]_{r}
.

For any rooted interval
[u,v]_{r}
, consider any saturated chain
C

from
u
to
v
other than the one which comes earliest with respect to
\lambda
for the choice of root
r
.
We will show that
C
must have a topological descent with respect to the labeling
\lambda
, thereby proving that
\lambda
is indeed a self-consistent CC-labeling.

Suppose there exists some
u<v
in
P
and some root
r
with a saturated chain
M=u\lessdot u_{1}\lessdot u_{2}\lessdot\cdots\lessdot u_{s}\lessdot v
in
[u,v]_{r}
that is not the lexicographically earliest saturated chain of
[u,v]_{r}
but where
M
nonetheless does not have any topological descents. Among all such triples
u,v,r
, choose one for which the length of the longest saturated chain from
u
to
v
is as small as possible. This choice
ensures that
u\lessdot u_{1}
does not belong to the lexicographically smallest saturated chain in
[u,v]_{r}
, since
otherwise
M
restricted to
[u_{1},v]_{r\cup u}
would have the same properties within an interval with strictly shorter longest saturated chain, contradicting the sort of minimality
[u,v]_{r}
was
chosen to have.

We may deduce that
u_{1}
does not come first amongst the atoms of
[u,v]_{r}
in the GRAO, as
M
is not the lexicographically earliest saturated chain in
[u,v]_{r}
.
Consider first the case where
u_{2}
is the first atom in the GRAO of
[u_{1},v]_{r\cup u}
. Condition (ii) in Definition
3.3
then ensures that there exists an atom
u_{1}^{\prime}
of
[u,v]_{r}
where
u_{1}^{\prime}
comes earlier than
u_{1}
in our GRAO and that there exists some element
z
such that
u_{1}\lessdot z<v
and

u_{1}^{\prime}<z<v
. Lemma
3.10
then guarantees that
u_{2}
must satisfy
u_{1}^{\prime\prime}<u_{2}
for some atom
u_{1}^{\prime\prime}
in
[u,v]_{r}
with
u_{1}^{\prime\prime}
coming earlier than
u_{1}
in the GRAO.
This implies that
(\lambda(u,u_{1}),\lambda(u_{1},u_{2}))
is a topological descent, contradicting our assumption that
M
does not have any topological descents.

Next consider the case where
u_{2}
is not the smallest atom in the GRAO of
[u_{1},v]_{r\cup u}
. Our minimality assumption
in choosing
[u,v]_{r}
above implies that

M
restricted to
[u_{1},v]_{r\cup u}
must have a topological descent. But this topological descent also gives a topological descent in
M
itself, again giving a contradiction.
This completes the proof that
\lambda
is a self-consistent CC-labeling.
∎

Remark 8.7
.

The key difference between this construction and our usage of a GRAO to construct an RAO (and thereby a CL-labeling and hence a self-consistent CC-labeling) is that this construction just above goes directly from a
GRAO to a self-consistent CC-labeling without needing to invoke the reordering process or construct an RAO as intermediate steps.

8.3.
Björner-Wachs construction of CL-labeling from RAO

We next describe how Björner and Wachs construct a CL-labeling from any recursive atom ordering. This is included for several reasons:
(1) to clarify what the reordering process says about the assortment of lexicographic shellings guaranteed to exist by a GRAO in comparison to those resulting from an RAO,
(2) because we use many of these ideas later in developing an analogous relationship between having a GRAO and having a self-consistent CC-labeling,
and (3) to help clarify how the usage of topological ascents and descents in a CC-labeling simplify matters considerably in give an analogue for GRAOs and CC-shellability to
the result of Björner and Wachs we are about to review.

Theorem 8.8

(
[
BW83
]
, Theorem 3.2)
.

Any recursive atom ordering for a finite bounded poset
P
induces a CL-labeling for
P
.

Proof.

Given an RAO
a_{1},\dots,a_{n}
for
P
, start by labeling each cover relation of the form
\hat{0}\lessdot a_{i}
with the integer
i
.
By induction on the length of the longest saturated chain in
P
,
one may assume that
each rooted interval
[a_{i},\hat{1}]
has its own RAO that induces a CL-labeling for
[a_{i},\hat{1}]
. The plan is to modify this CL-labeling for
[a_{i},\hat{1}]
for each
i
so that the modified labels may be taken together with the labels
\lambda(\hat{0},a_{i})=i
for
i=1,2,\dots,n
to give a CL-labeling
\lambda
for all of
P
.
It will suffice to describe how to modify the labels on the cover relations upward from
a_{i}
to the atoms of
[a_{i},\hat{1}]
, then to apply this same label modification process inductively to handle the rooted intervals higher in the poset.

Letting
x_{1},\dots,x_{u}
be our RAO for

[a_{i},\hat{1}]
which is guaranteed to exist by the definition of an RAO for
P
, Björner and Wachs made the key observation that there always exists some
j\in\{1,\dots,u\}
such that

x_{1},\dots,x_{j}\in F_{\hat{0}\lessdot a_{i}}(a_{i})
while
x_{j+1},\dots,x_{u}\in G_{\hat{0}\lessdot a_{i}}(a_{i})
(sets whose definition is reviewed in Definition
2.13
). They labelled each cover relation
a_{i}\lessdot x_{i^{\prime}}
for

i^{\prime}\leq j
with the label
\lambda(a_{i},x_{i^{\prime}})=i^{\prime}-(j-\lambda(\hat{0},a_{i}))-1
and labelled each cover relation
a_{i}\lessdot x_{i^{\prime}}
for
i^{\prime}\geq j+1
with the label

\lambda(a_{i},x_{i^{\prime}})=i^{\prime}+(\lambda(\hat{0},a_{i})-j)+1
. Björner and Wachs show that the resulting labeling has the following two properties:

(1)

\lambda(\hat{0},a_{i})>\lambda(a_{i},x_{i^{\prime}})
if and only if
x_{i^{\prime}}\in F_{\hat{0}\lessdot a_{i}}(a_{i})

(2)

\lambda(a_{i},x_{i^{\prime}})<\lambda(a_{i},x_{i^{\prime\prime}})
if and only if
i^{\prime}<i^{\prime\prime}
.

In other words, the shifting of label values preserves the relative order of the labels on cover relations upward from a fixed element with the same fixed choice of root below, but at the same time it creates ascents and descents exactly where they are needed in order to have a CL-labeling.
See
[
BW83
]
for further details of the proof.
∎

8.4.
Dual CC-shellability of face lattices of
d
-complexes

Björner proved in
[

that shellability of a
d

is equivalent to dual CL-shellability of its face lattice.

Recall that a
d-complex
is a polyhedral complex whose maximal faces are all of dimension
d
for a fixed
d
. The
face lattice

L(\Delta)
of a

d
-complex
\Delta
is the partial order on its cells in which one cell is less than or equal to another if the former is in the closure of the latter, with a unique minimal element

\hat{0}
representing the empty cell and with an additional maximal element denoted by
\hat{1}

Proposition 8.9
.

Shellability of a
d
-complex
\Delta
is equivalent to
L(\Delta)
admitting a self-consistent dual CC-labeling. This is also equivalent to

L(\Delta)
admitting a self-consistent dual topological CL-labeling.

Proof.

We use Björner’s result from
[
Bj84
]
that a
d
-complex
\Delta
is shellable if and only if its face lattice
L(\Delta)

is dual CL-shellable. We combine this with Theorem
6.4
applied to
L(\Delta)^{*}
, giving the equivalence of CL-shellability to self-consistent topological CL-shellability and to self-consistent CC-shellability.
∎

As an application of Björner’s aforementioned result from
[
Bj84
]
,
we may deduce directly from Theorem
7.1
that the
d
-complexes having the uncrossing posets as their posets of closure relations are shellable complexes.
The recursive nature of the definition of shelling for polyhedral complexes means that in particular this yields shellability of the boundary of the unique maximal cell in such a

d

8.5.
A possible further direction

The notions of CC-shellability and CL-shellability are generalized in
[
He03A
]
from poset order complexes to more general balanced simplicial complexes (and balanced
boolean cell complexes). The class of balanced quotient complexes
\Delta(B^{2n})/S_{2}\wr S_{n}
is proven to be CC-shellable in
[
He03A
]
but is not proven to be CL-shellable.

Question 8.10
.

It remains open whether self-consistent CC-shellability is equivalent to CL-shellability in the more general setting of balanced complexes.

This could be an interesting avenue for further study, with potential applications to quotient cell
complexes such as
\Delta(B^{2n})/S_{2}\wr S_{n}
(see
[
He03B
]
for non-shellability of

\Delta(B^{kn})/S_{k}\wr S_{n}
for
k>2
and see
[
He03A
]
for CC-shellability when
k=2
).

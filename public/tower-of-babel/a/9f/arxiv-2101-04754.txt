Algorithms for optimal control of hybrid systems with sliding motion
RadosŁaw Pytlak, Damian Suski

Abstract
This paper concerns two algorithms for solving optimal control problems with hybrid systems. The first algorithm aims at hybrid systems exhibiting sliding modes. The first algorithm has several features which distinguishes it from the other algorithms for problems described by hybrid systems. First of all, it can cope with hybrid systems which exhibit sliding modes. Secondly, the systems motion on the switching surface is described by index 2 differential–algebraic equations and that guarantees accurate tracking of the sliding motion surface. Thirdly, the gradients of the problems functionals are evaluated with the help of adjoint equations. The adjoint equations presented in the paper take into account sliding motion and exhibit jump conditions at transition times. We state optimality conditions in the form of the weak maximum principle for optimal control problems with hybrid systems exhibiting sliding modes and with piecewise differentiable controls. The second algorithm is for optimal control problems with hybrid systems which do not exhibit sliding motion. In the case of this algorithm we assume that control functions are ℒ ∞ {\mathcal{L}}^{\infty} measurable functions. For each algorithm, we show that every accumulation point of the sequence generated by the algorithm satisfies the weak maximum principle.

3
Optimal control problem with sliding modes and piecewise smooth controls

Taking into account the considerations and definitions presented in the previous section, in particular including the possibility of the system entering (and spending some time) in the sliding mode, we now turn attention to the optimal control problem of interest—
(P)
:

\displaystyle\min_{u}\phi(x(t_{f}))

(53)

subject to the constraints

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{lc}x^{\prime}=f_{1}(x,u)&\ \ {\rm if}\ q=1\\
x^{\prime}=f_{2}(x,u)&\ \ {\rm if}\ q=2\\
\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}x^{\prime}&=&f_{F}(x,u)+h_{x}(x)^{T}z\\
0&=&h(x)\end{array}\right.&\ \ {\rm if}\ q=3\end{array}

(56)

and the terminal constraints

\displaystyle g^{1}_{i}(x(t_{f}))=0\ \forall i\in E

(57)

\displaystyle g^{2}_{j}(x(t_{f}))\leq 0\ \forall j\in I.

(58)

We also assume that the initial state
x(t_{0})=x_{0}
is fixed.

Crucial assumption concerns the class of control functions. Our algorithm requires that control functions are piecewise smooth functions. The justification for that is given in
[
20
]
by referring to properties of Filippov’s solutions.

Thus the admissible control
u
is a function
u
from the set
{\mathcal{U}}
, where

\displaystyle{\displaystyle{\mathcal{U}}=\left\{u\in{\mathcal{C}}^{N}_{m}[T]:\ u(t)\in U\subset\mathbb{R}^{m},\ t\in[t_{0},t_{f}],\right\}}

(59)

\displaystyle{\displaystyle{\mathcal{C}}_{m}^{N}[T]=\left\{u\in{\mathcal{L}}^{2}_{m}[T]:\ u(t)=\sum_{j=1}^{N}\psi_{j}(t)u^{N}_{j}(t),\right.}

\displaystyle{\displaystyle\left.u_{j}^{N}\in\mathcal{C}_{m}^{1}[t_{j-1},t_{j}],\ j=1,\ldots,N,\ t\in[t_{0},t_{f}]\right\},}

\displaystyle{\displaystyle\psi_{j}(t)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1,&{\rm if}\ t\in[t_{j-1},t_{j})\\
0,&{\rm if}\ t\not\in[t_{j-1},t_{j})\end{array}\right.,}

t_{j}=jt_{f}/N,\ j\in\{0,1,\ldots,N\}
.

Regarding the control constraints we make the hypothesis:

(CC)

The set of admissible controls
{\mathcal{U}}
is a convex compact subset of
{\mathcal{L}}^{2}_{m}[T]
and there exists
0<L_{d}<\infty
such that

\displaystyle{\displaystyle\|u^{\prime}-v^{\prime}[\|_{{\mathcal{L}}^{\infty}}\leq L_{d}\|u-v\|_{{\mathcal{L}}^{\infty}}\ \ \forall u,v\in{\mathcal{U}}.}

(61)

For the lack of space we do not analyse in the paper under which conditions imposed on functions
u_{j}^{N}\in\mathcal{C}_{m}^{1}[t_{j-1},t_{j}]
(constant, linear, quadratic, etc.)
{\bf(CC)}
is satisfied.

The method we propose for solving the problem
{\bf(P)}
is based on an exact penalty function.
By using an exact penalty function approach, instead of solving the problem
{\bf(P)}
we solve the problem
{\bf P_{c}}

\displaystyle{\displaystyle\min_{u\in{\mathcal{U}}}\bar{F}_{c}(u)}

(62)

in which the exact penalty function
\bar{F}_{c}(u)
is defined as follows

\displaystyle{\displaystyle\bar{F}_{c}(u)=\bar{F}_{0}(u)+c\max\left[0,\max_{i\in E}\left|\bar{g}^{1}_{i}(u)\right|,\max_{j\in I}\bar{g}^{2}_{j}(u)\right]}

(63)

and, since under our assumptions
x
uniquely depends on
u
, we can write:
\bar{F}_{0}(u)=\phi(x^{u}(t_{f}))
,
\bar{g}_{i}^{1}(u)=g_{i}^{1}(x^{u}(t_{f}))
,
i\in E
,
\bar{g}_{j}^{2}(u)=g_{j}^{2}(x^{u}(t_{f}))
,
j\in I
.

For fixed
c
and
u
the direction finding subproblem,
{\bf P_{c}(u)}
,
for the problem
{\bf(P_{c})}
is:

\displaystyle{\displaystyle\min_{d\in{\mathcal{D}}_{u},\beta\in\mathbb{R}}\left[\left\langle\nabla\bar{F}_{0}(u),d\right\rangle+c\beta+1/2\|d\|^{2}_{{\mathcal{L}}^{2}}\right]}

subject to

\displaystyle\left|\bar{g}^{1}_{i}(u)+\left\langle\nabla\bar{g}^{1}_{i}(u),d\right\rangle\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\beta\ \ \forall i\in E

\displaystyle\bar{g}^{2}_{j}(u)+\left\langle\nabla\bar{g}^{2}_{j}(u),d\right\rangle

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\beta\ \ \forall j\in I.

Here (notice
(H3)
. part
iii)
),

\displaystyle{\displaystyle{\mathcal{D}}_{u}\left\{d\in{\mathcal{C}}^{N}_{m}[T]:\ d\in{\mathcal{U}}-u\right\}.}

The direction finding subproblem is based on first order approximations to the problem functionals, defined as follows

\displaystyle\langle\nabla\bar{F}_{0}(u),d\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\phi_{x}(x^{u}(t_{f}))y^{x,d}(t_{f})

(64)

\displaystyle\langle\nabla\bar{g}_{i}^{1}(u),d\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(g_{i}^{1}\right)_{x}(x^{u}(t_{f}))y^{x,d}(t_{f}),\ i\in E

(65)

\displaystyle\langle\nabla\bar{g}_{j}^{2}(u),d\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(g_{j}^{2}\right)_{x}(x^{u}(t_{f}))y^{x,d}(t_{f}),\ j\in I.

(66)

The subproblem can be reformulated as an optimization problem
with the objective function which is strictly convex.
The problem therefore has the unique solution
(\bar{d},\bar{\beta})
.
Since this solution depends on
c
and
u
, we may define
descent function

\sigma_{c}(u)
and
penalty test function

t_{c}(u)
(see
[
16
]
for details),
to be used to test optimality of a control

u
and to adjust
c
, respectively, as

\displaystyle{\displaystyle\sigma_{c}(u)=\left\langle\nabla\bar{F}_{0}(u),\bar{d}\right\rangle+c\left[\bar{\beta}-M(u)\right]}

(67)

and

\displaystyle{\displaystyle t_{c}(u)=\sigma_{c}(u)+M(u)/c}

(68)

for given
c>0
and
u\in{\mathcal{U}}
. Here,

\displaystyle{\displaystyle M(u)=\max\left[0,\max_{i\in E}\left|\bar{g}^{1}_{i}(u)\right|,\max_{j\in I}\bar{g}^{2}_{j}(u)\right],}

A general algorithm is as follows.

Algorithm

Fix parameters:
\gamma,\ \eta\in(0,1)
,
c^{0}>0
,
\kappa>1
.

1.

Choose the initial control
u_{0}\in{\cal U}
.
Set
k=0
,
c_{-1}=c^{0}
.

2.

Let
c_{k}
be the smallest number chosen from
\{c_{k-1},\kappa c_{k-1},\kappa^{2}c_{k-1},\ldots\}
such that
the solution
(d_{k},\beta_{k})
to the direction finding subproblem
{\bf P_{c_{k}}(u_{k})}

satisfies

\displaystyle{\displaystyle t_{c_{k}}(u_{k})\leq 0.}

(69)

If
\sigma_{c_{k}}(u_{k})=0
then STOP.

3.

Let
\alpha_{k}
be the largest number chosen from the set

\{1,\eta,\eta^{2},\ldots,\}
such that

\displaystyle{\displaystyle u_{k+1}=u_{k}+\alpha_{k}d_{k}}

satisfies the relation

\displaystyle\bar{F}_{c_{k}}(u_{k+1})-\bar{F}_{c_{k}}(u_{k})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\gamma\alpha_{k}\sigma_{c_{k}}(u_{k}).

Increase k by one. Go to Step 2.

Since

\displaystyle{\displaystyle\left\langle\nabla\bar{F}_{0}(u_{k}),d\right\rangle+c_{k}\beta+1/2\|d\|^{2}_{{\mathcal{L}}^{2}}\leq c_{k}M(u_{k}),}

which holds because
0\in{\mathcal{U}}-u_{k}
, we also have

\displaystyle{\displaystyle\left\langle\nabla\bar{F}_{0}(u_{k}),d\right\rangle+c_{k}\left[\beta-M(u_{k})\right]\leq-1/2\|d\|^{2}_{{\mathcal{L}}^{2}}\leq 0}

(70)

which implies that the descent function
\sigma_{c_{k}}(u_{k})
is nonpositive valued at each iteration.

Algorithm
generates a sequence of controls
\{u_{k}\}
and the corresponding sequence of penalty parameters
\{c_{k}\}
such that
\{c_{k}\}
is bounded and any accumulation point of

\{u_{k}\}
satisfies optimality conditions in the form of the weak maximum principle for the problem
{\bf(P)}
, i.e.
\sigma_{\bar{c}}(\bar{u})=0
for the limit point
\bar{u}
and the limit point of the sequence
\{c_{k}\}
.
But
\sigma_{\bar{c}}(\bar{u})=0
implies that
M(\bar{u})=0

due to the definition (
68
) and since (
69
) holds.

It can be shown that the set
{\mathcal{U}}
is compact (since we assume that
U
is compact) and this fact can be used to prove the convergence of
Algorithm
without referring to relaxed controls (cf.
[
16
]
). However, we still need to introduce two additional hypotheses. The first one concerns the functions defining the objective and constraints:

(H4)

\phi
,
g_{i}^{1}
,

,

,

The second one is related to a constraint qualification which is needed to prove the convergence of
Algorithm
. To this end we first introduce the set

\displaystyle{\displaystyle{\mathcal{D}}=\left\{d\in{\mathcal{C}}^{N}_{m}[T]:\ \exists u\in{\mathcal{U}}\ {\rm such\ that}\ u+d\in{\mathcal{U}}\right\}}

and the set

\displaystyle{\displaystyle{\mathcal{F}}(u)=\left\{d\in{\mathcal{D}}:\ \max_{j\in I}\left\langle\nabla\bar{g}_{j}^{2}(u),d\right\rangle<0\right\}.}

Then the constraint qualification condition takes the form

(CQ)

for each
u\in{\mathcal{U}}
,
{\mathcal{F}}(u)\neq\emptyset
, and in the case

we have

\displaystyle{\displaystyle 0\in{\rm interior}{\mathcal{E}}(u)}

(71)

where

\displaystyle{\displaystyle{\mathcal{E}}(u)=\left\{\left\{\langle\nabla\bar{g}^{1}_{i},d\rangle\right\}_{i\in E}\in\mathbb{R}^{|E|}:\ d\in{\mathcal{F}}(u)\right\}.}

The constraint qualification
{\bf(CQ)}
is similar to these stated in
[
15
]
and
[
10
]
, if we observe that it must take into account the constraint
u\in{\mathcal{U}}
. We will show that under the constraint qualification
{\bf(CQ)}
any limit point of the sequence
\{u_{k}\}
generated by
Algorithm
—
\bar{u}\in{\mathcal{U}}
will satisy the conditions
{\bf(NC)}
:

(NC)

:

\displaystyle{\displaystyle 0\leq\min_{d\in{\mathcal{D}}_{\bar{u}}}\phi_{x}(\bar{x}(t_{f}))y^{\bar{x},d}(t_{f})}

(72)

subject to the constraints

\displaystyle g_{i}^{1}(\bar{x}(t_{f}))+\left(g_{i}^{1}\right)_{x}(\bar{x}(t_{f}))y^{\bar{x},d}(t_{f})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0,\ i\in E

(73)

\displaystyle g_{j}^{2}(\bar{x}(t_{f}))+\left(g_{j}^{2}\right)_{x}(\bar{x}(t_{f}))y^{\bar{x},d}(t_{f})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 0,\ j\in I_{0,\bar{u}}

(74)

together with
g_{i}^{1}(\bar{x}(t_{f}))=0
,
i\in E
,
g_{j}^{2}(\bar{x}(t_{f}))\leq 0
,
j\in I
. Here,

\displaystyle{\displaystyle I_{\varepsilon,u}=\left\{j\in I:\ \bar{g}_{j}^{2}(u)\geq\max_{j\in I}\bar{g}_{j}^{2}(u)-\varepsilon\right\},\ \varepsilon\geq 0.}

is globally convergent in the following sense.

4
Adjoint equations

The efficient implementation of
Algorithm
needs computationally tractable algorithm for solving the direction finding subproblem
{\bf P_{c}(u)}
which at every iteration requires the evaluation of
\langle\nabla\bar{F}_{0}(u),d\rangle
,
\langle\nabla\bar{g}_{i}^{1}(u),d\rangle
,
i\in E
,
\langle\nabla\bar{g}_{j}^{2}(u),d\rangle
,
j\in I
. If we do that by using solutions to the linearized equations then each update of
d
would request solving these equations. If we use adjoint equations the evaluation comes down to the calculations of scalar products since
\nabla\bar{F}_{0}(u)
,
\nabla\bar{g}_{i}^{1}(u)
,
i\in E
,
\nabla\bar{g}_{j}^{2}(u)
,
j\in I
are available prior to solving
{\bf P_{c}(u)}
. It is sufficient to show how
\nabla\bar{F}_{0}(u)
could be calculated with the help of adjoint equations since the adjoint equations for the other functionals will differ only by terminal conditions.

We formulate the adjoint equations for two cases, they are derived on the basis of a variational approach presented in
[
4
]
and by taking into account our results on solutions to linearized equations of ordinary differential equations and differential–algebraic equations of the special form arising when a system remains in the sliding mode.

Case 1).
In the first case we assume that in the time interval
[t_{0},t_{t}]
the system evolves according to

x^{\prime}=f_{1}(x,u).

(79)

At a transition time
t_{t}
the continuous state trajectory meets the switching surface such that the following condition holds

h(x(t_{t}))=0.

(80)

After the transition the system evolves according to DAEs

\displaystyle x^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f_{F}(x,u)+h_{x}^{T}(x)z

(81)

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle h(x)

(82)

up to an ending time
t_{f}
.

To derive the adjoint equations we construct the following augmented functional

\displaystyle{\displaystyle\Phi(x,z,u,\lambda_{f},\lambda_{h},\pi)=\phi(x(t_{f}))+\pi h(x(t_{t}))+\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{t}^{-}}\lambda_{f}^{T}(t)\left(x^{\prime}(t)-f_{1}(x(t),u(t))\right)dt}

\displaystyle{\displaystyle+\int_{t_{t}^{+}}^{t_{f}}\left[\lambda_{f}^{T}(t)\left(x^{\prime}(t)-f_{F}(x(t),u(t))-h_{x}^{T}(x(t))z(t)\right)+\lambda_{h}^{T}(t)h(x(t))\right]dt.}

Suppose now that we calculate the variation of the augmented functional with respect to
(x,z)
only.

\displaystyle{\displaystyle d\Phi(x,z,u,\lambda_{f},\lambda_{h},\pi)=\phi_{x}(x(t_{f}))y^{x,d}(t_{f})+\pi h_{x}(x(t_{t}))dx(t_{t})+}

\displaystyle{\displaystyle\lambda_{f}^{T}(t_{t}^{-})\left(x^{\prime}(t_{t}^{-})-f_{1}(x(t_{t}^{-}),u(t_{t}^{-}))\right)dt_{t}}

\displaystyle{\displaystyle+d\left[\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{t}^{-}}\lambda_{f}^{T}(t)\left(x^{\prime}(t)-f_{1}(x(t),u(t))\right)dt\right]}

\displaystyle{\displaystyle-\lambda_{f}^{T}(t_{t}^{+})\left(x^{\prime}(t_{t}^{+})-f_{F}(x(t_{t}^{+}),u(t_{t}^{+}))-h_{x}^{T}(x(t_{t}^{+}))z(t_{t}^{+})\right)dt_{t}-\lambda_{h}^{T}(t_{t}^{+})h(x(t_{t}^{+}))dt_{t}}

\displaystyle{\displaystyle+d\left[\int_{t_{t}^{+}}^{t_{f}}\left[\lambda_{f}^{T}(t)\left(x^{\prime}(t)-f_{F}(x(t),u(t))-h_{x}^{T}(x(t))z(t)\right)+\lambda_{h}^{T}(t)h(x(t))\right]dt\right].}

By integrating by parts the formulas
\int\lambda_{f}(t)x(t)dt
(since
\lambda_{f}
is sufficiently smooth) we obtain

\displaystyle{\displaystyle d\Phi(x,z,u,\lambda_{f},\lambda_{h},\pi)=\phi_{x}(x(t_{f}))y^{x,d}(t_{f})+\pi h_{x}(x(t_{t}))dx(t_{t})}

\displaystyle{\displaystyle+\lambda_{f}^{T}(t_{t}^{-})\left(x^{\prime}(t_{t}^{-})-f_{1}(x(t_{t}^{-}),u(t_{t}^{-}))\right)dt_{t}+d\left[\left[\lambda_{f}^{T}(t)x(t)\right]_{t_{0}}^{t_{t}^{-}}\right]-}

\displaystyle{\displaystyle d\left[\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{t}^{-}}\left[(\lambda_{f}^{T})^{\prime}(t)x(t)+\lambda_{f}^{T}(t)f_{1}(x(t),u(t))\right]dt\right]}

\displaystyle{\displaystyle-\lambda_{f}^{T}(t_{t}^{+})\left(x^{\prime}(t_{t}^{+})-f_{F}(x(t_{t}^{+}),u(t_{t}^{+}))-h_{x}^{T}(x(t_{t}^{+}))z(t_{t}^{+})\right)dt_{t}}

\displaystyle{\displaystyle-\lambda_{h}^{T}(t_{t}^{+})h(x(t_{t}^{+}))dt_{t}+d\left[\left[\lambda_{f}^{T}(t)x(t)\right]_{t_{t}^{+}}^{t_{f}}\right]-}

\displaystyle{\displaystyle d\left[\int_{t_{t}^{+}}^{t_{f}}\left[(\lambda_{f}^{T})^{\prime}(t)x(t)+\lambda_{f}^{T}(t)\left(f_{F}(x(t),u(t))+h_{x}^{T}(x(t))z(t)\right)-\lambda_{h}^{T}(t)h(x(t))\right]dt\right].}

Expanding further the variations (also with respect to u) and taking into account the initial conditions of the linearized equations we obtain

\displaystyle{\displaystyle d\Phi(x,z,u,\lambda_{f},\lambda_{h},\pi)=\phi_{x}(x(t_{f}))y^{x,d}(t_{f})+\pi h_{x}(x(t_{t}))dx(t_{t})+}

\displaystyle{\displaystyle\lambda_{f}^{T}(t_{t}^{-})x^{\prime}(t_{t}^{-})dt_{t}-\lambda_{f}^{T}(t_{t}^{-})f_{1}(x(t_{t}^{-}),u(t_{t}^{-}))dt_{t}+\lambda_{f}^{T}(t_{t}^{-})y^{x,d}(t_{t}^{-})}

\displaystyle{\displaystyle-\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{t}^{-}}\left[(\lambda_{f}^{T})^{\prime}(t)y^{x,d}(t)+\lambda_{f}^{T}(t)(f_{1})_{x}(x(t),u(t))y^{x,d}(t)\right.}

\displaystyle{\displaystyle\left.+\lambda_{f}^{T}(t)(f_{1})_{u}(x(t),u(t))d(t)\right]dt-\lambda_{f}^{T}(t_{t}^{+})x^{\prime}(t_{t}^{+})dt_{t}+}

\displaystyle{\displaystyle\lambda_{f}^{T}(t_{t}^{+})f_{F}(x(t_{t}^{+}),u(t_{t}^{+}))dt_{t}+\lambda_{f}^{T}(t_{t}^{+})h_{x}^{T}(x(t_{t}^{+}))z(t_{t}^{+})dt_{t}-}

\displaystyle{\displaystyle\lambda_{h}^{T}(t_{t}^{+})h(x(t_{t}^{+}))dt_{t}+\lambda_{f}^{T}(t_{f})y^{x,d}(t_{f})-\lambda_{f}^{T}(t_{t}^{+})y^{x,d}(t_{t}^{+})-}

\displaystyle{\displaystyle\int_{t_{t}^{+}}^{t_{f}}\left[(\lambda_{f}^{T})^{\prime}(t)y^{x,d}(t)+\lambda_{f}^{T}(t)(f_{F})_{x}(x(t),u(t))y^{x,d}(t)\right.}

\displaystyle{\displaystyle+\lambda_{f}^{T}(t)(f_{F})_{u}(x(t),u(t))d(t)+\lambda_{f}^{T}(t)h_{x}^{T}(x(t))y^{z,d}(t)+}

\displaystyle{\displaystyle\left.\lambda_{f}^{T}(t)(h_{x}^{T}(x(t))z(t))_{x}y^{x,d}(t)-\lambda_{h}^{T}(t)h_{x}(x(t))y^{x,d}(t)\right]dt.}

Now, if we take into account the formula (
35
)–(

) for the differential of
x(t_{t})
,
and rearrange the components with respect to differentials

\displaystyle{\displaystyle d\Phi(x,z,u,\lambda_{f},\lambda_{h},\pi)=\left(\phi_{x}(x(t_{f}))+\lambda_{f}^{T}(t_{f})\right)y^{x,d}(t_{f})+\left(\pi h_{x}(x(t_{t}))+\right.}

\displaystyle{\displaystyle\left.\lambda_{f}^{T}(t_{t}^{-})-\lambda_{f}^{T}(t_{t}^{+})\right)dx(t_{t})+\left(-\lambda_{f}^{T}(t_{t}^{-})f_{1}(x(t_{t}^{-}),u(t_{t}^{-}))+\right.}

7
Conclusions

The paper presents the computational approach to hybrid optimal control problems with sliding modes. It seems to be the first method for optimal control problems with hybrid systems which can exhibit sliding modes. We show that under the assumption that controls are represented by piecewise constant functions the presented method is globally convergent in the sense that every accumulation point of a sequence generated by the method satisfies necessary optimality conditions in the form of the weak maximum principle. In the accompanying papers (
[
18
]
,
[
19
]
) we present the implementable version of our computational method by relaxing the condition that continuous state trajectories are available. In these papers we assume that system equations are integrated by an implicit Runge–Kutta method and we show that the global convergence of the implementable version of our algorithm is preserved provided that the integration procedure step sizes converge to zero.

The algorithms presented in the paper are first order methods since they are based on linearizations of functions defining our optimal control problem. We can provide second order versions of these algorithms by applying an SQP approach in which second order terms in the functions expansions are placed in a quadratic term of the objective function of
{\bf P_{c}(u)}
—then instead of using the term
1/2\|d\|_{{\mathcal{L}}^{2}}
we use
1/2d^{T}Hd
.
H
represents the approximation to the Hessian of the Lagrange function of the problem. Of course the second order approach can be applied if the optimal control problem can be stated as the problem in the Euclidean space, for example when control functions are piecewise constant functions (
[
16
]
). The convergence analysis presented in this paper can be used to prove global convergence of the second order method provided that matrices
H
used at iterations are uniformly positive definite and bounded.

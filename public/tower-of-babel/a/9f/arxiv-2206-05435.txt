Forward-backward doubly stochastic systems and classical solutions of path-dependent stochastic PDEs
Yufeng Shi, Jiaqiang Wen, Jie Xiong

Abstract
In this paper, a class of non-Markovian forward-backward doubly stochastic systems is studied. By using the technique of functional Itô (or path-dependent) calculus, the relationship between the systems and related path-dependent quasi-linear stochastic partial differential equations (SPDEs in short) is established, and the well-known nonlinear stochastic Feynman-Kac formula of Pardoux and Peng [Backward doubly stochastic differential equations and systems of quasilinear SPDEs, Probab. Theory Relat. Fields 98 (1994), pp. 209–227] is developed to the non-Markovian situation. Moreover, we obtain the differentiability of the solution to the forward-backward doubly stochastic systems and some properties of solutions to the path-dependent SPDEs.



Keywords
:
Backward doubly stochastic differential equations;
path-dependence; functional Itô calculus;
nonlinear stochastic Feynman-Kac formula

: 60H10; 60H30

1
Introduction

It is well-known that quasi-linear parabolic partial differential equations (PDEs in short) are related to Markovian forward-backward stochastic
differential equations behind Bismut
[
2
]
introduced the linear backward stochastic differential equations (BSDEs in short).
Then, after Pardoux and Peng
[
17
]
proved the existence and uniqueness for nonlinear BSDEs,
Peng
[
21
]
and Pardoux and Peng
[
18
]
introduced the nonlinear Feynman-Kac formula,
which provides a probabilistic representation for a large class of systems of quasi-linear PDEs
and generalizes the classical Feynman-Kac formula for linear parabolic PDEs.
Along this way, Pardoux and Peng
[
19
]
introduced a new class of BSDEs, called backward doubly stochastic differential equations (BDSDEs in short),
related to a class of quasi-linear parabolic backward stochastic PDEs (SPDEs in short).
They gave a probabilistic representation for solutions of such systems of semi-linear SPDEs,
and used it to prove the existence and uniqueness result of such SPDEs.
This result is summarized as the nonlinear stochastic Feynman-Kac formula,
which permits us to solve BDSDE by SPDE and, conversely, we can also use BDSDE to solve SPDE.
Since then, this important theory has attracted a lot of attention.
For example, Buckdahn and Ma
[
3
,
4
]
introduced a definition of stochastic viscosity solution to the nonlinear SPDEs.
They also proved the existence and uniqueness of the stochastic viscosity solution,
and further extended the nonlinear stochastic Feynman-Kac formula.
The generalized BDSDEs and SPDEs with nonlinear Neumann boundary conditions have been investigated by Boufoussi, Casteren and Mrhardy
[
1
]
.
Diehl and Friz
[
9
]
established the connection between BSDEs with rough drivers and BDSDEs, and Xiong
[
29
]
obtained the strong uniqueness for the solution to a class of SPDEs with non-Lipschitz coefficients. Zhang and Zhao
[
31
,
31
]
obtained the pathwise stationary solutions of SPDEs and BDSDEs on the infinite horizon under non-Lipschitz coefficients.
In application, Han, Peng and Wu
[
13
]
investigated the optimal control problems for backward doubly stochastic control systems, and obtained the maximum principle for backward doubly stochastic control systems. Shi, Wen and Xiong
[
26
]
developed BDSDEs of Volterra type, obtained the well-poseness of solutions, and studied the optimal control problems.
For more recent developments concerning BSDEs and BDSDEs, we refer the readers to
[
14
,
27
,
32
]
, and the references therein.

During the past two decades, tremendous efforts have been made to extend the nonlinear stochastic Feynman-Kac formula to the non-Markovian situation. To the best of our knowledge, very few works have been done for nonlinear stochastic Feynman-Kac formula in non-Markovian case. The main difficulty is that, in the non-Markovian situation, one can not find a proper probabilistic representation for the solution of SPDE.

In this paper, we will overcome the difficulties to establish the nonlinear stochastic Feynman-Kac formula for non-Markovian BDSDEs of path-dependence, and obtain a proper probabilistic representation for the solution of related SPDEs.
The key technique of our approach is the functional Itô/path-dependent calculus introduced by Dupire
[
8
]
recently (see
[
6
,
7
,
30
]
and references cited therein). Dupire’s main idea is to define new path derivatives, which provides an excellent tool for studying path-dependence.
This is an extension of the classical Itô calculus to functionals depending on whole paths of a stochastic process instead of its current values only.
By using the functional Itô calculus,
Ekren, Touzi and Zhang
[
11
,
12
]

introduced a new notion of viscosity solution and proved the comparison and the existence
for a class of fully nonlinear path-dependent PDEs by the dynamical nonlinear expectation theory.
Buckdahn, Ma and Zhang
[
5
]
proposed a notion of pathwise viscosity solution for fully nonlinear forward SPDEs, and connected a forward SPDE with a path-dependent PDE.
Peng and Song
[
22
]
established the existence and uniqueness theorem of Sobolev solutions to semi-linear (fully nonlinear) path-dependent PDEs.
Ren, Touzi and Zhang
[
24
]
provides a probabilistic proof of the comparison result for viscosity solutions of path-dependent semilinear PDEs.
In this non-Markovian situation, by using the technique of functional Itô calculus,
the nonlinear Feynman-Kac formula was established by Ekren et al.
[
10
]
, Peng and Wang
[
23
]
, and Ji and Yang
[
15
]
.
Motivated by functional Itô formulas,
Zong et al.
[
33
]

investigates asymptotic properties of systems represented by stochastic functional differential equations, and uses degenerate Lyapunov functionals to investigate the stability of general delay-dependent stochastic functional differential equations.

By virtue of the functional Itô calculus, in this paper,
we will systematically study the nonlinear stochastic Feynman-Kac formula for non-Markovian BDSDEs, and research the relation between the non-Markovian BDSDEs and related path-dependent SPDEs.
In detail, consider the following system of non-Markovian forward-backward doubly stochastic differential equations,

\displaystyle X^{\gamma_{t}}(s)=

\displaystyle\ \gamma_{t}(t)+\int_{t}^{s}b(X_{r}^{\gamma_{t}})dr+\int_{t}^{s}\sigma(X_{r}^{\gamma_{t}})dW(r),\quad~s\in[t,T],

(1.1)

\displaystyle Y^{\gamma_{t}}(s)=

\displaystyle\ \Phi(X_{T}^{\gamma_{t}})+\int_{s}^{T}f(X_{r}^{\gamma_{t}},Y^{\gamma_{t}}(r),Z^{\gamma_{t}}(r))dr

\displaystyle\ +\int_{s}^{T}g(X^{\gamma_{t}}_{r},Y^{\gamma_{t}}(r),Z^{\gamma_{t}}(r))d\overleftarrow{B}(r)-\int_{s}^{T}Z^{\gamma_{t}}(r)dW(r),

(1.2)

where the integral with respect to
d\overleftarrow{B}
is a backward Itô integral, the integral with respect to
dW
is a forward Itô integral, the coefficients
b(\cdot),\sigma(\cdot),\Phi(\cdot),f(\cdot)
and
g(\cdot)
are given continuous functions, and
\gamma(\cdot)
is a given initial function with
\gamma_{t}
denotes its path up to time
t
.

First, we study the differentiability of the solution to the BDSDE (
1.2
)
by the method of “frozen method” introduced by Peng
[
20
]
.
Second, by virtue of the classical nonlinear stochastic Feynman-Kac formula of Pardoux and Peng
[
19
]
, the path regularity of the process
Z
is given.
Finally, we connect the non-Markovian BDSDE (
1.2
) to the following path-dependent backward SPDE:

\displaystyle u(\gamma_{t})=

\displaystyle\ \Phi(\gamma_{T})+\int_{t}^{T}\big[\mathcal{L}u(\gamma_{s})+f(\gamma_{s},u(\gamma_{s}),(\sigma D_{x}u)(\gamma_{s}))\big]ds

\displaystyle\ +\int_{t}^{T}g(\gamma_{s},u(\gamma_{s}),(\sigma D_{x}u)(\gamma_{s}))d\overleftarrow{B}(s),\quad~0\leq t\leq T,

(1.3)

where

\mathcal{L}u=\frac{1}{2}tr\big[\big(\sigma\sigma^{T}\big)D_{xx}u\big]+\langle b,D_{x}u\rangle.

On the one hand, we prove that BDSDE (
1.2
) can be seen as the solution of the quasi-linear path-dependent SPDE (
1
). Roughly speaking, the path-function
u(\gamma_{t})\triangleq Y^{\gamma_{t}}(t)

is the unique smooth solution of the path-dependent SPDE (
1
), which provides the probabilistic representation for the solution of SPDEs in non-Markovian situation.
On the other hand, we can also use the path-dependent SPDE (
1
) to get the solution of BDSDE (
1.2
).
The results non-trivially develop the famous nonlinear stochastic Feynman-Kac formula of Pardoux and Peng
[
19
]
to non-Markovian situation, which provides a new way for the study of BDSDE theory and SPDE theory.
Recently, by using the technique of Malliavin calculus, Wen and Shi
[
28
]
obtained a result for the nonlinear stochastic Feynman-Kac formula in the non-Markovian situation, where they consider the case of random coefficients.
Different with the tool of Malliavin calculus, we would like to emphasize that comparing with
[
28
]
, the approaches and conclusions of this paper are of fundamental difference.

This paper is organized as follows.
In Section 2, some preliminary results about the functional Itô calculus and BDSDEs are presented.
In Section 3, some regularities and estimates results for the solutions of non-Markovian BDSDEs will be established. In Section 4, we prove the main results–the nonlinear stochastic Feynman-Kac formula in non-Markovian situation (Theorem
4.1
and Theorem
4.3
), which provide a one to one correspondence between non-Markovian BDSDEs and path-dependent PDEs.

2.1
Functional Itô calculus

Let
T>0
be fixed.
For each
t\in[0,T]
, we denote
\Lambda_{t}
the set of bounded càdlàg (right continuous with left limit)
\mathbb{R}^{d}
-valued functions on
[0,t]
and

\Lambda=\bigcup_{t\in[0,T]}\Lambda_{t}.

For each
\gamma\in\Lambda
, its value at time
t
is denoted by
\gamma(t)
and its path up to time
t
is
denoted by
\gamma_{t}
, i.e.,
\gamma_{t}=\gamma(r)_{0\leq r\leq t}\in\Lambda_{t}
.
Then
\gamma(r)=\gamma_{t}(r)
, for
r\in[0,t]
.
For each
\gamma\in\Lambda,\ t\leq s
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we denote

\begin{split}\gamma_{t}^{x}(r)\triangleq&\ \gamma(r)\mathbf{1}_{[0,t)}(r)+(\gamma(t)+x)\mathbf{1}_{\{t\}},\quad~r\in[0,t],\\
\gamma_{t,s}(r)\triangleq&\ \gamma(r)\mathbf{1}_{[0,t)}(r)+\gamma(t)\mathbf{1}_{[t,s]}(r),\quad~r\in[0,s].\end{split}

It is clear that
\gamma_{t}^{x}\in\Lambda_{t}
and
\gamma_{t,s}\in\Lambda_{s}
.
For each
0\leq t\leq s\leq T
and
\gamma,\overline{\gamma}\in\Lambda
, we denote

\begin{split}\|\gamma_{t}\|\triangleq&\sup_{r\in[0,t]}|\gamma_{t}(r)|,\\
\|\gamma_{t}-\overline{\gamma}_{s}\|\triangleq&\sup_{r\in[0,s]}|\gamma_{t,s}(r)-\overline{\gamma}_{s}(r)|,\\
d_{\infty}(\gamma_{t},\overline{\gamma}_{s})\triangleq&\ \sup_{r\in[0,t\vee s]}|\gamma(r\wedge t)-\overline{\gamma}(r\wedge s)|+|t-s|^{\frac{1}{2}}.\end{split}

(2.1)

It is obvious that
\Lambda_{t}
is a Banach space with respect to
\|\cdot\|
.

Definition 2.1
.

A functional
F
defined on
\Lambda
is said to be continuous at
\gamma_{t}\in\Lambda
,
if for any
\varepsilon>0
there exists
\delta>0
such that
for each
\overline{\gamma}_{\overline{t}}\in\Lambda
with
d_{\infty}(\gamma_{t},\overline{\gamma}_{\overline{t}})<\delta
,
we have
|F(\gamma_{t})-F(\overline{\gamma}_{\overline{t}})|<\varepsilon
.

F
is said to be
\Lambda
-continuous if it is continuous at each
\gamma_{t}\in\Lambda
.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
F:\Lambda\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be given. If there exists
p\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, such that

\displaystyle F(\gamma_{t}^{x})=F(\gamma_{t})+\langle p,x\rangle+o(|x|)\quad~as\quad~x\rightarrow 0,\quad~x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

then we say that
F
is vertically differentiable at
\gamma_{t}\in\Lambda
and define
D_{x}F(\gamma_{t})\triangleq p
.
A functional
F
is said to be vertically differentiable if
D_{x}F(\gamma_{t})
exists for each
\gamma_{t}\in\Lambda
.
We can similarly define the Hessian
D_{xx}F(\gamma_{t})
.
It is an
\mathbb{S}(d)
-valued function defined on
\Lambda
, where
\mathbb{S}(d)
is the space of all
d\times d
symmetric matrices.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
F:\Lambda\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
and
\gamma_{t}\in\Lambda
be given. If there is
a\in\mathbb{R}
, such that

F(\gamma_{t,s})=F(\gamma_{t})+a(s-t)+o(|s-t|)\quad~as\quad~s\rightarrow t,\quad~s\geq t,

then we say
F
is horizontally differentiable at
\gamma_{t}
and define
D_{t}F(\gamma_{t})\triangleq a
.
A functional
F
is said to be horizontally differentiable if
D_{t}F(\gamma_{t})
exists for each
\gamma_{t}\in\Lambda
.

The following space shall be used frequently in the following sections.

Definition 2.4
.

F
is said to be in
\mathbb{C}^{1,2}_{l,\text{Lip}}(\Lambda)
, if
D_{t}F
,
D_{x}F
, and
D_{xx}F
exist and we have

|\varphi(\gamma_{t})-\varphi(\overline{\gamma}_{\overline{t}})|\leq C(1+\|\gamma_{t}\|^{m}+\|\overline{\gamma}_{\overline{t}}\|^{m})d_{\infty}(\gamma_{t},\overline{\gamma}_{\overline{t}}),\quad~\forall\gamma_{t},\overline{\gamma}_{\overline{t}}\in\Lambda,

where
\varphi=F
,
D_{t}F
,
D_{x}F
,
D_{xx}F
and
C
and
m
are two constants depending only on
\varphi
.
Similarly, we can define
\mathbb{C}^{0,1}_{l,\text{Lip}}(\Lambda)
,
\mathbb{C}^{0,2}_{l,\text{Lip}}(\Lambda)
.

Example 2.5
.

If
F(\gamma_{t})=f(t,\gamma(t))
with
f\in\mathbb{C}^{1,2}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R})
, then the path-derivatives reduce to the classical derivatives:

D_{t}F=\partial_{t}f,\quad~D_{x}F=\partial_{x}f,\quad~D_{xx}F=\partial_{xx}f.

In general, these derivatives also satisfy the classical properties: Linearity, product and chain rules.

The following functional Itô’s formula was firstly obtained by Dupire
[
8
]
and then generalized by Cont and Fournié
[

,

]
.

Theorem 2.6
.

Let
(\Omega,{\cal F},({\cal F}_{t})_{t\in[0,T]},\mathbb{P})
be a probability space, if
X
is a continuous semi-martingale
and
F
is in
\mathbb{C}^{1,2}_{l,\text{Lip}}(\Lambda)
, then for any
t\in[0,T)
,

\displaystyle F(X_{t})=\ F(X_{0})+\int_{0}^{t}D_{s}F(X_{s})ds+\int_{0}^{t}D_{x}F(X_{s})dX(s)+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}{\rm tr}[D_{xx}F(X_{s})]d\langle X\rangle(s).

2.2
BDSDEs

Let
(\Omega,{\cal F},\mathbb{F},\mathbb{P})
be a complete filtered probability space and
T>0
be a fixed terminal time.
Let
\{W(t);0\leq t\leq T\}
and
\{B(t);0\leq t\leq T\}
be two mutually independent standard
Brownian motions defined on
(\Omega,\mathcal{B}(\Omega),\mathbb{P})
with values in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and in
\mathbb{R}^{l}
, respectively.
Let
\mathcal{N}
denote the class of
P
-null sets in
\Omega
.
For each
t\in[0,T]
, we define

\mathcal{F}_{t}\triangleq\mathcal{F}_{t}^{W}\vee\mathcal{F}_{t,T}^{B},

where for any process
\{\eta(t)\},\ \mathcal{F}_{s,t}^{\eta}=\sigma\{\eta(r)-\eta(s);s\leq r\leq t\}\vee\mathcal{N}
and

\mathcal{F}_{t}^{\eta}=\mathcal{F}_{0,t}^{\eta}
.
Note that the collection
\{\mathcal{F}_{t};t\in[0,T]\}
is neither increasing nor decreasing, and it does not constitute a filtration.

For any
t\in[0,T]
, we denote by
M^{2}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{n})
the set of
n
-dimensional

\mathcal{F}_{t}
-measurable stochastic process

\{\varphi(t);t\in[0,T]\}
which satisfies
\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}|\varphi(t)|^{2}dt<\infty
.
Similarly, we denote by
S^{2}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{n})
the set of
n
-dimensional continuous stochastic process
\{\varphi(t);t\in[0,T]\}
which is
\mathcal{F}_{t}
-measurable and satisfies
\mathbb{E}(\sup\limits_{0\leq t\leq T}|\varphi(t)|^{2})<\infty
.

Now consider the following assumptions:

(H1)

Let
b:\Lambda\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\sigma:\Lambda\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
be two given continuous functions,
and there exists a constant
L\geq 0
and
m\geq 0
, such that for each
\gamma_{t},\overline{\gamma}_{t}\in\Lambda
,

|b(\gamma_{t})-b(\overline{\gamma}_{t})|+|\sigma(\gamma_{t})-\sigma(\overline{\gamma}_{t})|\leq L(1+\|\gamma_{t}\|^{m}+\|\overline{\gamma}_{t}\|^{m})\|\gamma_{t}-\overline{\gamma}_{t}\|.

(H2)

Suppose
f:\Lambda\times\mathbb{R}^{k}\times\mathbb{R}^{k\times d}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{k}
and

g:\Lambda\times\mathbb{R}^{k}\times\mathbb{R}^{k\times d}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{k\times l}
are two given continuous functions.
Moreover, there exist constants
m\geq 0
,
C>0
and
0<\alpha<1
such that for every

\gamma_{t},\overline{\gamma}_{t}\in\Lambda,\ y,\overline{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{k},z,\overline{z}\in\mathbb{R}^{k\times d}
,

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle|f(\gamma_{t},y,z)-f(\overline{\gamma}_{t},\overline{y},\overline{z})|\leq C\big((1+\|\gamma_{t}\|^{m}+\|\overline{\gamma}_{t}\|^{m})\|\gamma_{t}-\overline{\gamma}_{t}\|+|y-\overline{y}|+|z-\overline{z}|\big);\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle|g(\gamma_{t},y,z)-g(\overline{\gamma}_{t},\overline{y},\overline{z})|\leq C\big((1+\|\gamma_{t}\|^{m}+\|\overline{\gamma}_{t}\|^{m})\|\gamma_{t}-\overline{\gamma}_{t}\|+|y-\overline{y}|\big)+\alpha|z-\overline{z}|.\end{array}\right.

Proposition 2.8
.

\xi\in

L^{2}(\Omega,\mathcal{F}_{T},\mathbb{P};\mathbb{R}^{k})

Proposition 2.9
.

Let
\Phi\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{k})
,
\alpha\in S^{2}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{k})
,
\beta\in M^{2}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{k})
,

,

be such that

\displaystyle\alpha(t)=\alpha(0)+\int_{0}^{t}\beta(s)ds+\int_{0}^{t}\gamma(s)d\overleftarrow{B}(s)+\int_{0}^{t}\delta(s)dW(s),\quad~t\in[0,T].

Then

\displaystyle\Phi(\alpha(t))=

\displaystyle\ \Phi(\alpha(0))+\int_{0}^{t}\big\langle\Phi^{\prime}(\alpha(s)),\beta(s)\big\rangle ds+\int_{0}^{t}\big\langle\Phi^{\prime}(\alpha(s)),\gamma(s)d\overleftarrow{B}(s)\big\rangle+\int_{0}^{t}\big\langle\Phi^{\prime}(\alpha(s)),\delta(s)dW(s)\big\rangle

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}tr\big[\Phi^{\prime\prime}(\alpha(s))\gamma(s)\gamma(s)^{\top}\big]ds+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}tr\big[\Phi^{\prime\prime}(\alpha(s)))\delta(s)\delta(s)^{\top}\big]ds.

.

Let

,

3
Non-Markovian BDSDEs

In this section, we study the properties of solutions to non-Markovian BDSDEs by a stochastic calculus approach, which is helpful for us to study the path-dependent SPDEs. First, let us recall some notations which will be used later.

\bullet

\mathbb{C}_{b,\text{Lip}}^{n}(\mathbb{R}^{p};\mathbb{R}^{q})
: The space of all bounded functions of class
C^{n}(\mathbb{R}^{p};\mathbb{R}^{q})

such that all their partial derivatives up to order
n
are bounded Lipschtiz continuous functions.

\bullet

\mathbb{C}_{l,\text{Lip}}(\mathbb{R}^{p};\mathbb{R}^{q})
:
The space of all
\mathbb{R}^{q}
-valued and continuous functions
\varphi
defined on

\mathbb{R}^{p}
such that

|\varphi(x)-\varphi(y)|\leq C(1+|x|^{m}+|y|^{m})|x-y|,\quad~\forall x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{p},

where
C,m
are two constants depending only on
\varphi
.

\bullet

\mathbb{C}_{l,\text{Lip}}^{n}(\mathbb{R}^{p};\mathbb{R}^{q})
: The space of all functions of class
C^{n}(\mathbb{R}^{p};\mathbb{R}^{q})

such that all their partial derivatives up to order
n
are in
\mathbb{C}_{l,\text{Lip}}(\mathbb{R}^{p};\mathbb{R}^{q})
.

\bullet

\mathbb{C}_{l,\text{Lip}}^{n,\bar{n}}(\mathbb{R}^{p}\times\mathbb{R}^{\bar{p}};\mathbb{R}^{q})
:
The space of all
\mathbb{R}^{q}
-valued continuous functions
\varphi(x,y)
defined on
\mathbb{R}^{p}\times\mathbb{R}^{\bar{p}}
such that

x\mapsto\varphi(x,y)\in C^{n}(\mathbb{R}^{p};\mathbb{R}^{q})
,
y\mapsto\varphi(x,y)\in C^{\bar{n}}(\mathbb{R}^{\bar{p}};\mathbb{R}^{q})

and all these partial derivatives belong to
\mathbb{C}_{l,\text{Lip}}(\mathbb{R}^{p}\times\mathbb{R}^{\bar{p}};\mathbb{R}^{q})
.

Definition 3.1
.

Given
\mathbb{R}^{k}
-valued function
\Phi
defined on
\Lambda_{T}
.
We say that
\Phi
is in
\mathbb{C}^{1}(\Lambda_{T})
if for each
\gamma\in\Lambda_{T}
and
t\in[0,T]
,
there exists a vector
p=(p_{1},...,p_{k})\in\mathbb{R}^{k\times d}
such that for each
i\leq k
,

\Phi_{i}(\gamma_{\gamma_{t}^{x}})-\Phi_{i}(\gamma)=\langle p_{i},x\rangle+o(|x|)\quad as\quad x\rightarrow 0,\ \ x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

where
\gamma_{\gamma_{t}^{x}}(r)=\gamma(r)\mathbf{1}_{[0,t)}(r)+(\gamma(r)+x)\mathbf{1}_{[t,T]}(r)
.
Then we denote
\Phi_{t}^{\prime}(\gamma)\triangleq p
. Similarly, the space
\mathbb{C}^{2}(\Lambda_{\bar{T}})
could be defined for each

Definition 3.2
.

We say
\Phi\in\mathbb{C}^{2}(\Lambda_{T})

if there exist two constants

|\phi_{r}^{\prime}(\gamma)-\phi_{s}^{\prime}(\overline{\gamma})|+|\phi_{r}^{\prime\prime}(\gamma)-\phi_{s}^{\prime\prime}(\overline{\gamma})|\leq C(1+\|\gamma\|^{m}+\|\overline{\gamma}\|^{m})\big(|r-s|+\|\gamma-\overline{\gamma}\|\big).\end{cases}

The spaces
\mathbb{C}_{l,\text{Lip}}(\Lambda_{\bar{T}})
and
\mathbb{C}_{l,\text{Lip}}^{1}(\Lambda_{\bar{T}})

.

3.1
Regularity of solution of BDSDE

In this subsection, we make some the estimates of the solution of BDSDE (
1.2
).
Assume the Lipschitz constants with respect to
b,\sigma,\Phi,f,g
are
C
,
m
and
\alpha
.
The following result concerning the estimates of solution of BDSDE (
1.2
), is useful for our later discussion.

Lemma 3.4
.

For any
p\geq 2
, there exist two constants
C_{p}
and
q
depending only on
C,T,m,\alpha
and
p
such that

\displaystyle E\bigg[\sup_{s\in[t,T]}|Y^{\gamma_{t}}(s)|^{p}\bigg]\leq C_{p}\big(1+\|\gamma_{t}\|^{q}\big),

(3.1)

\displaystyle E\bigg[\bigg|\int_{t}^{T}|Z^{\gamma_{t}}(s)|^{2}ds\bigg|^{\frac{p}{2}}\bigg]\leq C_{p}\big(1+\|\gamma_{t}\|^{q}\big).

(3.2)

Proof.

The proof is similar to the proof of Theorem 1.4 in Pardoux and Peng
[
19
]
, and to simplify the presentation, we only consider the case of
d=k=1
and
p=2
. First, using the classical method of SDEs theory, there is a constant
C_{0}
(which may be change line by line) such that

E\bigg[\sup_{s\in[t,T]}|X^{\gamma_{t}}(s)|^{2}\bigg]\leq C_{0}\big(1+\|\gamma_{t}\|^{2m}\big).

(3.3)

Indeed, applying Itô’s formula to
|X^{\gamma_{t}}(r)|^{2}
on
[t,s]
yields that

\displaystyle|X^{\gamma_{t}}(s)|^{2}=

\displaystyle|\gamma_{t}(t)|^{2}+2\int_{t}^{s}b(X_{r}^{\gamma_{t}})X^{\gamma_{t}}(r)dr+2\int_{t}^{s}\sigma(X_{r}^{\gamma_{t}})X^{\gamma_{t}}(r)dW(r)+\int_{t}^{s}\sigma^{2}(X_{r}^{\gamma_{t}})dr.

By inequality
2ab\leq a^{2}+b^{2}
and Burkholder-Davis-Gundy’s inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle E\bigg[\sup_{r\in[t,s]}|X^{\gamma_{t}}(r)|^{2}\bigg]\leq

\displaystyle C_{0}\bigg[|\gamma_{t}(t)|^{2}+E\int_{t}^{s}\big(|b(X_{r}^{\gamma_{t}})|^{2}+|\sigma(X_{r}^{\gamma_{t}})|^{2}\big)dr+E\int_{t}^{s}|X^{\gamma_{t}}(r)|^{2}dr\bigg].

Then, from assumption (H1) and Gronwall’s inequality, we obtain (
3.3
).

Second, we prove (
3.1
) and (
3.2
). Using Itô’s formula (Proposition
2.9
) to
|Y^{\gamma_{t}}(r)|^{2}
on
[s,T]
, one has

\displaystyle|Y^{\gamma_{t}}(s)|^{2}+\int_{s}^{T}|Z^{\gamma_{t}}(r)|^{2}dr

\displaystyle=|\Phi(X_{T}^{\gamma_{t}})|^{2}+2\int_{s}^{T}Y^{\gamma_{t}}(r)f(X^{\gamma_{t}}_{r},Y^{\gamma_{t}}(r),Z^{\gamma_{t}}(r))dr+2\int_{s}^{T}Y^{\gamma_{t}}(r)g(X^{\gamma_{t}}_{r},Y^{\gamma_{t}}(r),Z^{\gamma_{t}}(r))d\overleftarrow{B}(r)

\displaystyle\quad-2\int_{s}^{T}Y^{\gamma_{t}}(r)Z^{\gamma_{t}}(r)dW(r)+\int_{s}^{T}|g(X^{\gamma_{t}}_{r},Y^{\gamma_{t}}(r),Z^{\gamma_{t}}(r))|^{2}dr.

(3.4)

Hence

\displaystyle E|Y^{\gamma_{t}}(s)|^{2}+E\int_{s}^{T}|Z^{\gamma_{t}}(r)|^{2}dr

\displaystyle=E|\Phi(X_{T}^{\gamma_{t}})|^{2}+2E\int_{s}^{T}Y^{\gamma_{t}}(r)f(X^{\gamma_{t}}_{r},Y^{\gamma_{t}}(r),Z^{\gamma_{t}}(r))dr+E\int_{s}^{T}|g(X^{\gamma_{t}}_{r},Y^{\gamma_{t}}(r),Z^{\gamma_{t}}(r))|^{2}dr.

Then from (H4), and using Hölder’s and Young’s inequalities, we obtain that there exists a constant
\theta>0
such that for any
s\in[t,T]
,

\displaystyle E|Y^{\gamma_{t}}(s)|^{2}+\theta E\int_{s}^{T}|Z^{\gamma_{t}}(r)|^{2}dr

\displaystyle\leq E|\Phi(X_{T}^{\gamma_{t}})|^{2}+C_{0}E\int_{s}^{T}\big[|f(X^{\gamma_{t}}_{r},0,0)|^{2}+|g(X^{\gamma_{t}}_{r},0,0)|^{2}\big]dr+C_{0}E\int_{s}^{T}|Y^{\gamma_{t}}(r)|^{2}dr.

It then follows from (H3), (H4) and (
3.3
), and by using Gronwall’s inequality that

\displaystyle\sup_{s\in[t,T]}E|Y^{\gamma_{t}}(s)|^{2}+E\int_{t}^{T}|Z^{\gamma_{t}}(r)|^{2}dr\leq C_{p}\big(1+\|\gamma_{t}\|^{2m}\big).

Therefore, we obtain (
3.2
).
On the other hand, by applying the same inequalities to Eq. (
3.4
),

\displaystyle|Y^{\gamma_{t}}(s)|^{2}\leq

\displaystyle|\Phi(X_{T}^{\gamma_{t}})|^{2}+C_{0}\int_{s}^{T}\big[|f(X^{\gamma_{t}}_{r},0,0)|^{2}+|g(X^{\gamma_{t}}_{r},0,0)|^{2}+|Y^{\gamma_{t}}(r)|^{2}\big]dr

\displaystyle+2\int_{s}^{T}Y^{\gamma_{t}}(r)g(X^{\gamma_{t}}_{r},Y^{\gamma_{t}}(r),Z^{\gamma_{t}}(r))d\overleftarrow{B}(r)-2\int_{s}^{T}Y^{\gamma_{t}}(r)Z^{\gamma_{t}}(r)dW(r).

Hence, from Burkholder-Davis-Gundy’s inequality,

\displaystyle E\bigg[\sup_{s\in[t,T]}|Y^{\gamma_{t}}(s)|^{2}\bigg]\leq

\displaystyle E|\Phi(X_{T}^{\gamma_{t}})|^{2}+C_{0}E\int_{t}^{T}\big[|f(X^{\gamma_{t}}_{r},0,0)|^{2}+|g(X^{\gamma_{t}}_{r},0,0)|^{2}+|Y^{\gamma_{t}}(r)|^{2}\big]dr

\displaystyle+C_{0}E\sqrt{\int_{t}^{T}|Y^{\gamma_{t}}(r)|^{2}|g(X^{\gamma_{t}}_{r},Y^{\gamma_{t}}(r),Z^{\gamma_{t}}(r))|^{2}dr}

(3.5)

\displaystyle+C_{0}E\sqrt{\int_{t}^{T}|Y^{\gamma_{t}}(r)|^{2}|Z^{\gamma_{t}}(r)|^{2}dr}.

(3.6)

We estimate the term (
3.6
) as follows:

\displaystyle E\sqrt{\int_{t}^{T}|Y^{\gamma_{t}}(r)|^{2}|Z^{\gamma_{t}}(r)|^{2}dr}\leq

\displaystyle E\bigg[\sup_{s\in[t,T]}|Y^{\gamma_{t}}(s)|\sqrt{\int_{t}^{T}|Z^{\gamma_{t}}(r)|^{2}dr}\bigg]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\delta}{2}E\bigg[\sup_{s\in[t,T]}|Y^{\gamma_{t}}(s)|^{2}\bigg]+\frac{1}{2\delta}E\bigg[\int_{t}^{T}|Z^{\gamma_{t}}(r)|^{2}dr\bigg],

where
\delta>0
is a constant, and we choose it small enough so that
C\delta<\frac{1}{3}
.
The term (
3.5
) can be treated analogously, and we deduce that

\displaystyle E\bigg[\sup_{s\in[t,T]}|Y^{\gamma_{t}}(s)|^{2}\bigg]\leq C_{p}\big(1+\|\gamma_{t}\|^{2m}\big).

Similarly, we can get the same result for
p\geq 2.
Finally, for multi-dimensional case, one can similarly obtain the results without substantial difficulty. This completes the proof.
∎

Based on the above lemma, one could obtain the following general result for the estimates of the solution of BDSDEs.

Lemma 3.5
.

Suppose that functions
u
and
v
are in
M^{2}(0,T;\mathbb{H})
. Let
(Y^{\gamma_{t}}(\cdot),Z^{\gamma_{t}}(\cdot))

be the solution of the following BDSDE:

\displaystyle Y^{\gamma_{t}}(s)=

\displaystyle\ \Phi(X_{T}^{\gamma_{t}})+\int_{s}^{T}\big[f(X_{r}^{\gamma_{t}},Y^{\gamma_{t}}(r),Z^{\gamma_{t}}(r))+u(r)\big]dr

\displaystyle\ +\int_{s}^{T}\big[g(X_{r}^{\gamma_{t}},Y^{\gamma_{t}}(r),Z^{\gamma_{t}}(r))+v(r)\big]d\overleftarrow{B}(r)-\int_{s}^{T}Z^{\gamma_{t}}(r)dW(r),

where
(X^{\gamma_{t}}(\cdot))
is the adapted solution of SDE (
1.1
). Then for every
p\geq 2
, there exist two constants
C_{p}
and
q
depending only on
C,T,m,\alpha
and
p
such that

\displaystyle E\bigg[\sup_{s\in[t,T]}|Y^{\gamma_{t}}(s)|^{p}+\bigg|\int_{t}^{T}|Z^{\gamma_{t}}(s)|^{2}ds\bigg|^{\frac{p}{2}}\bigg]

\displaystyle\leq C_{p}\bigg(1+\|\gamma_{t}\|^{q}+E\bigg[\bigg|\int_{t}^{T}|u(s)|ds\bigg|^{p}\bigg]+E\bigg[\bigg|\int_{t}^{T}|v(s)|^{2}ds\bigg|^{\frac{p}{2}}\bigg]\bigg).

(3.7)

Proof.

For
s\in[t,T]
, for simplicity of presentation, we denote

U(s)=\int_{t}^{s}u(r)dr,\qquad V(s)=\int_{t}^{s}v(r)d\overleftarrow{B}(r).

Then, it is easy to see that
(Y^{\gamma_{t}}(s)+U(s)+V(s),Z^{\gamma_{t}}(s))
is the solution of the following BDSDE:

\displaystyle\bar{Y}^{\gamma_{t}}(s)=

\displaystyle\ \Phi(X_{T}^{\gamma_{t}})+U(T)+V(T)+\int_{s}^{T}\bar{f}(X_{r}^{\gamma_{t}},\bar{Y}^{\gamma_{t}}(r),\bar{Z}^{\gamma_{t}}(r))dr

\displaystyle\ +\int_{s}^{T}\bar{g}(X^{\gamma_{t}}_{r},\bar{Y}^{\gamma_{t}}(r),\bar{Z}^{\gamma_{t}}(r))d\overleftarrow{B}(r)-\int_{s}^{T}\bar{Z}^{\gamma_{t}}(r)dW(r),

where
\bar{f}(\gamma_{s},y,z)=f(\gamma_{s},y-U(s)-V(s),z)
and
\bar{g}(\gamma_{s},y,z)=g(\gamma_{s},y-U(s)-V(s),z)
.
Now, applying Lemma
3.4
, we conclude that for some constant (we still denoted by
C_{p}
),

\displaystyle E\bigg[\sup_{s\in[t,T]}|Y^{\gamma_{t}}(s)|^{p}+\bigg|\int_{t}^{T}|Z^{\gamma_{t}}(s)|^{2}ds\bigg|^{\frac{p}{2}}\bigg]

\displaystyle\leq C_{p}E\bigg[\big|\Phi(X_{T}^{\gamma_{t}})\big|^{p}+\big|U(T)\big|^{p}+\big|V(T)\big|^{p}+\int_{t}^{T}|f(X_{s}^{\gamma_{t}},0,0)|^{p}ds+\int_{t}^{T}|g(X_{s}^{\gamma_{t}},0,0)|^{p}ds\bigg].

By the definition of
\bar{f}
and
\bar{g}
, using (H2), we have that

\displaystyle|\bar{f}(X_{s}^{\gamma_{t}},0,0)|\leq|f(X_{s}^{\gamma_{t}},0,0)|+C(|U(s)|+|V(s)|),

\displaystyle|\bar{g}(X_{s}^{\gamma_{t}},0,0)|\leq|g(X_{s}^{\gamma_{t}},0,0)|+C(|U(s)|+|V(s)|),

which, combining (
3.3
) and Burkholder-Davis-Gundy’s inequality, implies that (
3.7
) hold. This completes the proof.
∎

4
Path-dependent SPDE

It is well known that BDSDE with time-state dependent coefficients provide probabilistic representation for solution to semi-linear parabolic SPDE.
In this section, we shall establish the nonlinear stochastic Feynman-Kac formula for BDSDE with path-dependent coefficients,
which generates a new class of SPDE called path-dependent SPDE.
Based on this view, a (path-dependent) SPDE can be regarded as a BDSDE,
which provides an alternative approach for the study of SPDE theory and BDSDE theory.

We now relate our non-markovian BDSDE to the following system of path-dependent semilinear backward stochastic partial differential equation:

\displaystyle u(\gamma_{t})=

\displaystyle\ \Phi(\gamma_{T})+\int_{t}^{T}\big[\mathcal{L}u(\gamma_{s})+f(\gamma_{s},u(\gamma_{s}),(\sigma D_{x}u)(\gamma_{s}))\big]ds

\displaystyle\ +\int_{t}^{T}g(\gamma_{s},u(\gamma_{s}),(\sigma D_{x}u)(\gamma_{s}))d\overleftarrow{B}(s),\quad~0\leq t\leq T,

(4.1)

where
u:\Lambda\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{k}
, with

\mathcal{L}u=\frac{1}{2}tr\big[\big(\sigma\sigma^{\top}\big)D_{xx}u\big]+\langle b,D_{x}u\rangle.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
u(\gamma_{t})\in\mathbb{C}^{1,2}_{l,\text{Lip}}(\Lambda)
be a random field such that

\gamma_{t}\in\Lambda

4

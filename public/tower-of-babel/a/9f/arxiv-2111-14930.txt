Orthogonality Hilbert 𝒜 \mathscr{A} -modules and operators preserving multi- 𝒜 \mathscr{A} -linearity
Paweł Wójcik 1 and Ali Zamani 2

Abstract
. In this paper we present results concerning orthogonality in Hilbert C ∗ C^{*} -modules. Moreover, for a C ∗ C^{*} -algebra 𝒜 \mathscr{A} , we prove theorems concerning the multi- 𝒜 \mathscr{A} -linearity and its preservation by 𝒜 \mathscr{A} -linear operators. New version of solution of the orthogonality equation on Hilbert C ∗ C^{*} -modules and mappings preserving orthogonality are also investigated.

46B20, 46C50, 47B49, 46L05, 15A69, 15A86

1.
Introduction and preliminaries

Let us recall some basic facts about
C^{*}
-algebras and
Hilbert
C^{*}
-modules and introduce our notation.
A
C^{*}
-algebra
is a complex Banach
*
-algebra
\big(\mathscr{A},\|\!\cdot\!\|)

such that
\|a\|^{2}=\|a^{*}a\|
for every
a\in\mathscr{A}
. An element
a\in\mathscr{A}

is called
positive
(we write
a\geq 0
) if
a=c^{*}c
for some
c\in\mathscr{A}
.
The relation
``\geq"
on
\mathscr{A}
is given by
a\geq b
if and only if
a-b\geq 0
.
If
a\in\mathscr{A}
is positive, then there exists a unique positive
p\in\mathscr{A}
such that

a=p^{2}
; such an element
p
is called the
positive square root
of
a
.
A linear functional
\varphi
of
\mathscr{A}
is positive if
\varphi(a)\geq 0
for every positive element

a\in\mathscr{A}
. A
state
is a positive linear functional whose norm is equal to one.
Let
\mathscr{A}
be a
C^{*}
-algebra and let
\mathscr{X}
be an algebraic
right
\mathscr{A}
-module
which is a complex linear space with
compatible scalar multiplication
(
i.e.,
(\alpha x)a=x(\alpha a)=\alpha(xa)
for all
x\in\mathscr{X},a\in\mathscr{A},\alpha\in\mbox{$\mathbb{C}$}
)
.
The space
\mathscr{X}
is called a
(right) pre-Hilbert
\mathscr{A}
-module
(inner product
\mathscr{A}
-module) if there
exists an
\mathscr{A}
-valued inner product, i.e., a mapping
\left\langle\cdot,\cdot\right\rangle\mathscr{X}\times\mathscr{X}\to\mathscr{A}
satisfying

(C1)
\left\langle x,x\right\rangle\geq 0
and
\left\langle x,x\right\rangle=0
if and only if
x=0
,

(C2)
\left\langle x,\alpha y+\beta z\right\rangle=\alpha\left\langle x,y\right\rangle+\beta\left\langle x,z\right\rangle
,

(C3)
\left\langle x,ya\right\rangle=\left\langle x,y\right\rangle a
,

(C4)
\left\langle x,y\right\rangle=\left\langle y,x\right\rangle^{*}
,

for all
x,y,z\in\mathscr{X},a\in\mathscr{A},\alpha,\beta\in\mbox{$\mathbb{C}$}
.
For
x\in\mathscr{X}
, by
|x|
we denote the unique positive square of
\left\langle x,x\right\rangle\in\mathscr{A}
.
A pre-Hilbert
\mathscr{A}
-module
\mathscr{X}
is called a
Hilbert
\mathscr{A}
-module
if it is complete with
respect to the norm
\|x\|:=\sqrt{\|\left\langle x,x\right\rangle\|}
.
Hilbert spaces are left Hilbert
\mathbb{C}
-modules.
Any
C^{*}
-algebra
\mathscr{A}
is a Hilbert
C^{*}
-module over itself via

\langle a,b\rangle:=a^{*}b
. The corresponding norm is just the norm on
\mathscr{A}

because of the
C^{*}
-condition.
Given a positive functional
\varphi
on
\mathscr{A}
, we have the following useful version of the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality:

\displaystyle\forall_{x,y\in\mathscr{X}}\ \ \ |\varphi(\left\langle x,y\right\rangle)|^{2}\leq\varphi(\left\langle x,x\right\rangle)\varphi(\left\langle y,y\right\rangle).

(1.1)

A Hilbert
\mathscr{A}
-module
\mathscr{X}
is said to be
full
if the two sided ideal
generated by all products
\langle x,y\rangle,\,x,y\in\mathscr{X}
,
is dense in
\mathscr{A}
. By
\mathcal{K}(\mathscr{X})
we denote the
C^{*}
-algebra spanned by

\big\{\theta_{x,y}:\,x,y\in\mathscr{X}\big\}
, where map

\theta_{x,y}\colon\mathscr{X}\rightarrow\mathscr{X}
is defined by
\theta_{x,y}(z):=x\langle y,z\rangle
.
We refer the reader to
[
14
,
19
]
for more information on the basic theory of
C^{*}
-algebras and Hilbert
C^{*}
-modules.

A concept of orthogonality in a Hilbert
C^{*}
-module can be defined with respect to the

C^{*}
-valued inner product in a natural way, that is, two elements
x
and
y
of a Hilbert
C^{*}
-module

\mathscr{X}
are called
orthogonal
, in short
x\perp y
, if
\langle x,y\rangle=0
.
Let us recall other types of orthogonality in Hilbert
C^{*}
-modules.
One of the most important is the concept of the Birkhoff–James orthogonality:
if
x,y
are elements of a Hilbert
C^{*}
-module
\mathscr{X}
,
then
x
is
orthogonal to

y

in the Birkhoff–James sense

[
1
,
6
]
, in short
x\perp_{B}y
, if

\displaystyle\forall_{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}}\ \ \|x+\lambda y\|\geq\|x\|.

As a natural generalization of the notion of Birkhoff–James orthogonality,
the concept of strong Birkhoff–James orthogonality,
which involves modular structure of a Hilbert
C^{*}
-module was introduced in
[
2
]
.
When
x
and
y
are elements of a Hilbert
\mathscr{A}
-module
\mathscr{X}
,

x
is
orthogonal to

y

in the strong Birkhoff–James sense
, in short
x\perp^{s}_{B}y
, if

\displaystyle\forall_{a\in\mathscr{A}}\ \ \|x+ya\|\geq\|x\|.

This orthogonality is ”between”
\perp
and
\perp_{B}
, i.e.,

\displaystyle\forall_{x,y\in\mathscr{X}}\ \ \ x\perp y\,\Longrightarrow\,x\perp^{s}_{B}y\,\Longrightarrow\,x\perp_{B}y,

while the converses do not hold in general (see
[
2
]
).

In this paper we first present results concerning
orthogonality in Hilbert
C^{*}
-modules.
In particular, we give a characterization of orthogonality in term of norm inequalities.
Then we prove theorems concerning the multi-
\mathscr{A}
-linearity and its
preservation by
\mathscr{A}
-linear operators. Some other related results are also discussed.
Finally, new version of solution of the orthogonality equation on Hilbert
C^{*}
-modules
and mappings preserving orthogonality are investigated.

2.
Orthogonality in Hilbert
C^{*}
-modules

Over the years many mathematicians have studied these concepts of orthogonality
in Hilbert
C^{*}
-modules in
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
6
,
18
,
21
]
.
In particular, it was proved in
[
4
,
12
,
23
]
that for elements
x
and
y
of a Hilbert
\mathscr{A}
-module
\mathscr{X}
,

x\perp y
is equivalent to each of the following statements:

(i)

\forall_{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}}\ \ \ |x+\lambda y|=|x-\lambda y|
.

(ii)

\forall_{a\in\mathscr{A}}\ \ \ |x+ya|=|x-ya|
.

(iii)

\forall_{a\in\mathscr{A}}\ \ \ \|x+ya\|=\|x-ya\|
.

(iv)

\forall_{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}}\ \ \ |x+\lambda y|^{2}\geq|x|^{2}
.

(v)

\forall_{a\in\mathscr{A}}\ \ \ |x+ya|^{2}\geq|x|^{2}
.

(vi)

\forall_{a\in\mathscr{A}}\ \ \ |x+ya|\geq|x|
.

We begin with a major motivation for stating this section.
Suppose
\mathscr{X}
is a Hilbert
C^{*}
-module over a
C^{*}
-algebra
\mathscr{A}
and
x,y\in\mathscr{X}
.
By the definition of the Birkhoff–James orthogonality, we have

\displaystyle x\perp_{B}y

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow\forall_{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}}\ \|x+\lambda y\|\geq\|x\|\Longleftrightarrow\forall_{\alpha\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}}\ \left\|x+\frac{1}{\alpha}y\right\|\geq\|x\|

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow\forall_{\alpha\in\mathbb{C}}\ \|\alpha x+y\|\geq\|\alpha x\|,

and hence

\displaystyle x\perp_{B}y\ \Longleftrightarrow\ \forall_{\alpha\in\mathbb{C}}\ \ \|\alpha x+y\|\geq\|\alpha x\|.

The above property is the key notion of the section. Namely, since in
Hilbert
C^{*}
-modules the role of scalars is played by the elements of the
underlying
C^{*}
-algebra, therefore, the question arises whether it is also true for
the strong Birkhoff–James orthogonality, i.e. whether
x\perp^{s}_{B}y
is equivalent to the assertion that

\displaystyle\forall_{a\in\mathscr{A}}\ \ \|xa+y\|\geq\|xa\|.

(2.1)

The answer is negative in general, as shown in the following example.

Example 2.1
.

Let us take the
C^{*}
-algebra
\mathscr{A}
of all complex
2\times 2
matrices,
and regard it as a Hilbert
C^{*}
-module over itself.
Let
X=\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\
0&1\end{bmatrix}
and
Y=\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\
0&0\end{bmatrix}
. Then
X\perp^{s}_{B}Y
since for any
A=\begin{bmatrix}a_{11}&a_{12}\\
a_{21}&a_{22}\end{bmatrix}

we have

\displaystyle\|X+YA\|=\left\|\begin{bmatrix}1+a_{11}&a_{12}\\
0&1\end{bmatrix}\right\|\geq 1=\|X\|.

But (
2.1
) does not hold since for
A=-Y
we have
\|XA+Y\|=0<\|XA\|=1
.

In the next theorem, we prove that (
2.1
) is actually equivalent
to the orthogonality with respect to the
C^{*}
-valued inner product.
The following lemma, that was obtained in
[
1
, Theorem 2.7]
and
[
6
, Theorem 4.4]

independently, is essential for supporting our conclusion.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\mathscr{X}
be a Hilbert
\mathscr{A}
-module and
x,y\in\mathscr{X}
.
Then the following conditions are equivalent:

(i)

x\perp_{B}y
,

(ii)

There exists a state
\varphi
on
\mathscr{A}
such that

\varphi(\langle x,x\rangle)=\|x\|^{2}
and
\varphi(\langle x,y\rangle)=0
.

As another motivation for our work
in this section, recall the following results from the paper
[
4
]
.

Theorem 2.3
.

[
4
, Theorem 2, Corollary 1]

Let
\mathscr{X}
be a Hilbert
\mathscr{A}
-module and
x,y\in\mathscr{X}
.
Then the following conditions are equivalent:

(i)

\forall_{a\in\mathscr{A}}\ \ \ \|x+ya\|=\|x-ya\|
,

(ii)

y\left\langle y,x\right\rangle\bot_{B}x
,

(iii)

x\perp y
.

Now, we want to prove that the above result can be strengthen as follows.

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
\mathscr{X}
be a Hilbert
\mathscr{A}
-module and
x,y\in\mathscr{X}
.
Then the following conditions are equivalent:

(i)

\forall_{a\in\mathscr{A}}\ \ \ |xa+y|^{2}\geq|xa|^{2}
,

(ii)

\forall_{a\in\mathscr{A}}\ \ \ |xa+y|\geq|xa|
,

(iii)

\forall_{a\in\mathscr{A}}\ \ \ \|xa+y\|\geq\|xa\|
,

(iv)

x\perp y
.

Moreover, since the relation
\perp
is symmetric, each of the above
conditions is equivalent to each of the three below:

(v)

\forall_{a\in\mathscr{A}}\ \ \ |ya+x|^{2}\geq|ya|^{2}
,

(vi)

\forall_{a\in\mathscr{A}}\ \ \ |ya+x|\geq|ya|
,

(vii)

\forall_{a\in\mathscr{A}}\ \ \ \|ya+x\|\geq\|ya\|
.

Proof.

(i)
\Rightarrow
(ii) The implication follows from Theorem 2.2.6 of
[
19
]
.

(ii)
\Rightarrow
(iii) Let
a\in\mathscr{A}
such that
|xa+y|\geq|xa|
.
By
[
19
, Theorem 2.2.5 (3)]
it follows that
\big\||xa+y|\big\|\geq\big\||xa|\big\|
.
Then
\|xa+y\|\geq\|xa\|
since
\big\||z|\big\|=\|z\|
for all
z\in\mathscr{X}
.

(iii)
\Rightarrow
(iv) Suppose (iii) holds. Let
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}

and let
a=\frac{\langle x,y\rangle}{\lambda}\in\mathscr{A}
. Then

\displaystyle\left\|x\frac{\langle x,y\rangle}{\lambda}+y\right\|\geq\left\|x\frac{\langle x,y\rangle}{\lambda}\right\|,

and so

\displaystyle\big\|x\langle x,y\rangle+\lambda y\big\|\geq\big\|x\langle x,y\rangle\big\|.

The above inequality is also obvious for
\lambda=0
, and therefore

\displaystyle\forall_{\lambda\in\scriptsize\mathbb{C}}\ \ \big\|x\langle x,y\rangle+\lambda y\big\|\geq\big\|x\langle x,y\rangle\big\|.

Thus
x\langle x,y\rangle\perp_{B}y
. By Lemma
2.2
, there exists a state
\varphi
on
\mathscr{A}
such that

\displaystyle\varphi\Big(\big\langle x\langle x,y\rangle,x\langle x,y\rangle\big\rangle\Big)=\big\|x\langle x,y\rangle\big\|^{2}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\varphi\Big(\big\langle x\langle x,y\rangle,y\big\rangle\Big)=0,

and equivalently,

\displaystyle\varphi\Big(\left\langle x\left\langle x,y\right\rangle,x\right\rangle\left\langle x,y\right\rangle\Big)=\big\|x\langle x,y\rangle\big\|^{2}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\varphi\Big(\langle y,x\rangle\langle x,y\rangle\Big)=0.

(2.2)

Hence

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leq\big\|x\langle x,y\rangle\big\|^{4}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{I.1.T.2.1}}}{{=}}\left|\varphi\Big(\left\langle x\left\langle x,y\right\rangle,x\right\rangle\left\langle x,y\right\rangle\Big)\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{inequality-positive-functional}}}{{\leq}}\varphi\Big(\left\langle x\left\langle x,y\right\rangle,x\right\rangle\left\langle x,x\left\langle x,y\right\rangle\right\rangle\Big)\varphi\Big(\left\langle y,x\right\rangle\left\langle x,y\right\rangle\Big)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{I.1.T.2.1}}}{{=}}0.

Therefore, we get
\big\|x\langle x,y\rangle\big\|^{4}=0
,
which gives
x\langle x,y\rangle=0
.
Then we have

\displaystyle\big\|\langle x,y\rangle\big\|^{2}=\big\|\langle y,x\rangle\langle x,y\rangle\big\|=\big\|\big\langle y,x\langle x,y\rangle\big\rangle\big\|=\|\langle y,0\rangle\|=0,

and so
\|\langle x,y\rangle\big\|=0
. Thus
\langle x,y\rangle=0
. Hence
x\perp y
.

(iv)
\Rightarrow
(i) Let
x\perp y
. Then
\langle x,y\rangle=\langle y,x\rangle=0
.
So, for every
a\in\mathscr{A}
, we have

\displaystyle|xa+y|^{2}=\langle xa,xa\rangle+a^{*}\langle x,y\rangle+\langle y,x\rangle a+\langle y,y\rangle=|xa|^{2}+|y|^{2},

which implies
|xa+y|^{2}\geq|xa|^{2}
.
Thus the implication (vi)
\Rightarrow
(i) is proved.

To summarize, we may consider (i)
\Leftrightarrow
(ii)
\Leftrightarrow
(iii)
\Leftrightarrow
(iv) as
shown. Since
\bot
is symmetric, we see
that (iv)
\Leftrightarrow
(v)
\Leftrightarrow
(vi)
\Leftrightarrow
(vii), and the proof is complete.
∎

3.
On
\mathscr{A}
-linear mappings preserving orthogonality

Chmieliński
[
8
]
had proved that a linear mapping
T\colon X\to Y
(between inner product
spaces) which preserves orthogonality
(i.e.
x\bot y\ \Rightarrow Tx\bot Ty
for all
x,y\in X
) has to be a
similarity (scalar multiple of an isometry).

It has been proved by Koldobsky
[
13
]
(for real normed spaces) and Blanco and Turnšek

[
7
]
(for real and complex ones) that a linear mapping
T\colon X\to Y
preserving
the Birkhoff–James orthogonality, i.e., satisfying
x\bot_{B}y\Rightarrow Tx\bot_{B}Ty
, has to be a similarity.
Another proof was presented by Wójcik
[
22
]
. Moreover, he
extended the Koldobsky theorem. Namely, for real spaces he showed
that the linearity assumption may be replaced
by additivity (see
[
22
]
).

We again focus on Hilbert
C^{*}
-modules. So, let us consider
now
\mathscr{A}
-linear mappings
T\colon\mathscr{X}\to\mathscr{Y}
(between Hilbert
\mathscr{A}
-modules
\mathscr{X}
and
\mathscr{Y}
) preserving
an orthogonality, i.e., satisfying
x\bot y\Rightarrow Tx\bot Ty

with respect to given
\mathscr{A}
-valued inner
products
\left\langle\cdot,\cdot\right\rangle\colon\mathscr{X}\times\mathscr{X}\to\mathscr{A}
and
\left\langle\cdot,\cdot\right\rangle\colon\mathscr{Y}\times\mathscr{Y}\to\mathscr{A}
.

Let
\mathcal{K}(H)
and
\mathcal{B}(H)
denote the
C^{*}
-algebra of all compact operators and, respectively, the
C^{*}
-algebra of
all bounded operators on a Hilbert space
H
.

Before proving the main theorems
of this section, let us look at a few
classical results. Ilišević and Turnšek
[
12
]
obtained
the following result in the case where
\mathscr{A}
is
a standard
C^{*}
-algebra, that is, when
\mathcal{K}(H)\subseteq\mathscr{A}\subseteq\mathcal{B}(H)
(see also
[
10
]
).

Theorem 3.1
.

[
12
]

Let
\mathscr{A}
be a standard
C^{*}
-algebra.
Let
\mathscr{X}
and
\mathscr{Y}
be Hilbert
\mathscr{A}
-modules. For a
nonzero mapping
T\colon\mathscr{X}\to\mathscr{Y}
, the following assertions are equivalent:

(i)

T
is
\mathscr{A}
-linear and orthogonality preserving mapping,

(ii)

T
is a similarity, i.e.,
\exists_{\gamma>0}\ \forall_{x,y\in\mathscr{X}}\ \ \left\langle Tx,Ty\right\rangle=\gamma\left\langle x,y\right\rangle
.

Recall that a linear mapping
T\colon\mathscr{X}\to\mathscr{Y}
,
where
\mathscr{X}
and
\mathscr{Y}
are Hilbert
\mathscr{A}
-modules, is called local if

\displaystyle\forall_{a\in\mathscr{A}}\ \forall_{x\in\mathscr{X}}\ \ xa=0\,\Longrightarrow\,(Tx)a=0.

Note that every
\mathscr{A}
-linear mapping is local, but the converse is not true,
in general (take linear differential operators into account).
Let
\mathcal{C}_{0}(\Omega)
denote the
C^{*}
-algebra of all continuous
\mathbb{C}
-valued functions
on a locally compact Hausdorff space
\Omega
, which vanish at
\infty
with the supremum
norm. Leung, Ng and Wong
[
15
]
obtained
the following result in the case where
\mathscr{A}=\mathcal{C}_{0}(\Omega)
.

Theorem 3.2
.

[
15
]

Let
\Omega
be a locally compact Hausdorff space, and let
\mathscr{X}
and
\mathscr{Y}
be two
Hilbert
\mathcal{C}_{0}(\Omega)
-modules. Suppose that
T\colon\mathscr{X}\to\mathscr{Y}
is an orthogonality preserving local

\mathbb{C}
-linear map. The following assertions hold.

(i)

T
is
\mathscr{A}
-linear and orthogonality preserving mapping.

(ii)

\forall_{x,y\in\mathscr{X}}\left\langle Tx,Ty\right\rangle\!=\!\varphi\left\langle x,y\right\rangle
, for some bounded
nonnegative function
\varphi
on
\Omega
.

The same mathematicians extended their results and they
obtain another strong theorem
[
16
]
. It is worth mentioning
that they did not assume again continuity of a mapping
T\colon\mathscr{X}\to\mathscr{Y}
.

Theorem 3.3
.

[
16
]

Let
\mathscr{X}
and
\mathscr{Y}
be Hilbert
\mathscr{A}
-modules. For a
nonzero mapping
T\colon\mathscr{X}\to\mathscr{Y}
, the following assertions are equivalent:

(i)

T
is
\mathscr{A}
-linear and orthogonality preserving mapping,

(ii)

T
is a
\mathscr{A}
-similarity, i.e.,
\exists_{c\in\mathscr{A}}\ \forall_{x,y\in\mathscr{X}}\ \ \left\langle Tx,Ty\right\rangle=c\left\langle x,y\right\rangle
.

The above results have motivated our work, which is
devoted to multi-
\mathscr{A}
-linear mappings defined on the Cartesian product of Hilbert
C^{*}
-modules.
They will be essentially generalized.
In particular, we will replace
\mathscr{A}
-valued inner products
by multi-
\mathscr{A}
-linear functionals.

If
\mathscr{X}
is a right
\mathscr{A}
-module, a function
F\colon\mathscr{X}^{n}\to\mathscr{A}

is a
multi-
\mathscr{A}
-linear function
if
for
x_{1},\ldots,x_{j},\ldots,x_{n},y_{j}\in\mathscr{X}
and
\alpha\in\mbox{$\mathbb{C}$}
and
a\in\mathscr{A}
and
j=1,\ldots,n
,

(A)
F(x_{1},\ldots,x_{j}+y_{j},\ldots,x_{n})=F(x_{1},\ldots,x_{j},\ldots,x_{n})+F(x_{1},\ldots,y_{j},\ldots,x_{n})
,

(B)
F(x_{1},\ldots,\alpha x_{j}a,\ldots,x_{n})=\alpha F(x_{1},\ldots,x_{j},\ldots,x_{n})a
, if
j=2k
,

(C)
F(x_{1},\ldots,\alpha x_{j}a,\ldots,x_{n})=\overline{\alpha}a^{*}F(x_{1},\ldots,x_{j},\ldots,x_{n})
, if
j=2k+1
.

Now suppose, in addition, that
\mathscr{X}
is a normed
space. A multi-
\mathscr{A}
-linear function
F\colon\mathscr{X}^{n}\to\mathscr{A}
is
bounded
if there is
a constant
M
such that

\|F(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\|\leq M\!\cdot\!\|x_{1}\|\cdot\ldots\cdot\|x_{n}\|
for all
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\in\mathscr{X}
.

Let
\left\langle\cdot,\cdot\right\rangle\colon\mathscr{X}\times\mathscr{X}\to\mathscr{A}
and
\left\langle\cdot,\cdot\right\rangle\colon\mathscr{Y}\times\mathscr{Y}\to\mathscr{A}
be

\mathscr{A}
-valued inner products.
If
T\colon\mathscr{X}\to\mathscr{Y}
is
\mathscr{A}
-linear, then a function
F\colon\mathscr{X}^{2}\to\mathscr{A}
defined
by
F(x,y):=\left\langle Tx,Ty\right\rangle
is a multi-
\mathscr{A}
-linear mapping. Define
E\colon\mathscr{X}^{2}\to\mathscr{A}
by
E(x,y):=\left\langle x,y\right\rangle
.
Then we can rewrite the orthogonality preserving property in the form:

\displaystyle\forall_{x,y\in\mathscr{X}}\quad E(x,y)=0\,\Longrightarrow\,F(x,y)=0.

(3.1)

Summarizing, the condition (
3.1
) will be the key notion of this section.

Suppose that
\mathscr{A}
has an identity. Put
G_{\mathscr{A}}:=\{b\in\mathscr{A}:b\ {\rm is\ invertible}\}
. It is known
that
G_{\mathscr{A}}
are open subsets of
\mathscr{A}
.
We say that a multi-
\mathscr{A}
-linear function
F\colon\mathscr{X}^{n}\to\mathscr{A}
is
strong
, if there
exists
w\in\mathscr{X}
such that
F(w,w,\ldots,w)\in G_{\mathscr{A}}
.
Consider
\mathscr{A}^{\prime}_{m}:=\big\{\varphi\colon\mathscr{A}\to\mbox{$\mathbb{C}$}\,|\,\varphi\ {\rm is\ linear\ and\ multiplicative}\big\}
.
We include the following useful property about multiplicative functionals. This basic fact
perhaps appears elsewhere but we present its with proof for sake of completeness.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
\mathscr{A}
be a
C^{*}
-algebra. If the set
\mathscr{A}^{\prime}_{m}
is total, i.e.,

\left(\forall_{\varphi\in\mathscr{A}^{\prime}_{m}}\ \ \varphi(a)=0\right)\Longrightarrow a=0
,

then
\mathscr{A}
is abelian.

Proof.

Fix
a,b\in\mathscr{A}
and
\varphi\in\mathscr{A}^{\prime}_{m}
. Then
\varphi(ab-ba)=\varphi(a)\varphi(b)-\varphi(b)\varphi(a)=0
.
Since
\mathscr{A}^{\prime}_{m}
is total,
ab-ba=0
. Thus
ab=ba
.
∎

Finally, we are ready to present that Theorems
3.1
,
3.2
and

3.3
can be extended for the case that
\mathscr{A}
is abelian.
These will not be only shallow and cosmetic changes.
We will show new methods to tackle this kind of
theorem. So, we state our first main result of this section.

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
\mathscr{A}
be a
C^{*}
-algebra with identity. Assume that
\mathscr{A}^{\prime}_{m}
is
a total set. Let
\mathscr{X}
be a right
\mathscr{A}
-module, and suppose that
\mathscr{X}
be a normed space.
Let
E\colon\mathscr{X}^{2}\to\mathscr{A}
and
F\colon\mathscr{X}^{2}\to\mathscr{A}
be
multi-
\mathscr{A}
-linear functions. Moreover, assume that
E
is bounded and strong.
The following assertions are equivalent:

(i)

\forall_{x,y\in\mathscr{X}}\quad E(x,y)=0\,\Longrightarrow\,F(x,y)=0
,

(ii)

\exists_{c\in\mathscr{A}}\ \forall_{x,y\in\mathscr{X}}\quad F(x,y)=cE(x,y)
.

Moreover, each of the above conditions implies:

(iii)

F
is bounded.

Proof.

The implication (ii)
\Rightarrow
(i) is obvious. We show (i)
\Rightarrow
(ii).
Suppose (i) holds. We may assume without loss of generality that
E
is a bounded multi-
\mathscr{A}
-linear function with bound

M=1
, i.e.
\|E(x,y)\|\leq\|x\|\!\cdot\!\|y\|
for all
x,y\in\mathscr{X}
.

Since
E
is strong, there exists
w\in\mathscr{X}
such that
E(w,w)\in G_{\mathscr{A}}
. It follows
easily that
w\neq 0
. Put
p:=E(w,w)
.
Since
G_{\mathscr{A}}
is open, there is an open ball

B(p,r)\subseteq\mathscr{A}
such that
B(p,r)\subseteq G_{\mathscr{A}}
with some
r>0
.
Now there is a small number
\beta>0
such that
(\beta+2\|w\|)\cdot\beta<r
.
Let us consider an open
ball
B\left(w,\beta\right)\subseteq\mathscr{X}
. Fix
x\in B\left(w,\beta\right)
.
Thus we have

\displaystyle\|E(x,x)-p\|

\displaystyle=\|E(x,x)-E(w,w)\|

\displaystyle=\|E(x,x)-E(x,w)+E(x,w)-E(w,w)\|

\displaystyle=\|E(x,x\!-\!w)+E(x\!-\!w,w)\|

\displaystyle\leq\|E(x,x\!-\!w)\|+\|E(x\!-\!w,w)\|

\displaystyle\leq\|x\|\cdot\|x-w\|+\|x-w\|\cdot\|w\|=(\|x\|+\|w\|)\cdot\|x-w\|

\displaystyle\leq(\|x-w\|+\|w\|+\|w\|)\cdot\|x-w\|<(\beta+2\|w\|)\cdot\beta<r.

Hence
E(x,x)\in B(p,r)\subseteq G_{\mathscr{A}}
.
In other words, we proved the following property:

\displaystyle x\in B\left(w,\beta\right)\ \ \Longrightarrow\ \ E(x,x)\ {\rm is\ invertible}.

(3.2)

Thus, we may define
h\colon B\left(w,\beta\right)\to\mathscr{A}
by
h(z):=F(z,z)E(z,z)^{-1}
.

Fix
y\in\mathscr{X}
and fix
z\in B\left(w,\beta\right)
. Since
E(z,z)
is invertible, we may
define

\displaystyle b:=-E(z,z)^{-1}E(z,y)\quad\mbox{and}\quad d:=\left(-E(z,z)^{-1}E(y,z)\right)^{*}.

It is clear
that
E(z,zb+y)=0
and
E(zd+y,z)=0
. From (i) it follows
that
F(z,zb+y)=0
and
F(zd+y,z)=0
. We thus
get
F(z,y)=-F(z,z)b
and
similarly
F(y,z)=-d^{*}F(z,z)
. Lemma
3.4
shows
that
\mathscr{A}
is abelian. Hence

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{ll}F(z,y)=F(z,z)E(z,z)^{-1}E(z,y)&{\rm and}\\
F(y,z)=F(z,z)E(z,z)^{-1}E(y,z)&{\rm for}\ z\in B\left(w,\beta\right),\ y\in\mathscr{X}.\end{array}

Since
B\left(w,\beta\right)
is open, it follows that there is
t_{o}>0
such
that
z+ty\in B\left(w,\beta\right)
for
all
t\in(-t_{o},t_{o})
. Hence, by (
3.2
),
E(z+ty,z+ty)
is invertible
for all
t\in(-t_{o},t_{o})
.
So, fix a number
t\in(-t_{o},t_{o})\setminus\{0\}
. Therefore, we may write

\displaystyle h(z+ty)-h(z)

\displaystyle=F(z+ty,z+ty)E(z+ty,z+ty)^{-1}-F(z,z)E(z,z)^{-1}

\displaystyle=E(z+ty,z+ty)^{-1}E(z,z)^{-1}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\Big(E(z,z)\big(F(z+ty,z+ty)-F(z,z)\big)\Big.

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad-\Big.F(z,z)\big(E(z+ty,z+ty)-E(z,z)\big)\Big)

\displaystyle=E(z+ty,z+ty)^{-1}E(z,z)^{-1}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\Big(E(z,z)\big(tF(y,z)+tF(z,y)+t^{2}F(y,y)\big)\Big.

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad-\Big.F(z,z)\big(tE(y,z)+tE(z,y)+t^{2}E(y,y)\big)\Big).

Fix
\varphi\in\mathscr{A}^{\prime}_{m}
. It follows from the above equalities that

\displaystyle\frac{\varphi(h(z+ty))-\varphi(h(z))}{t}

\displaystyle=\varphi\Big(E(z+ty,z+ty)^{-1}E(z,z)^{-1}\Big)

\displaystyle\quad\quad\Big[\varphi\Big(E(z,z)\big(F(y,z)+F(z,y)+tF(y,y)\big)\Big)

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad-\varphi\Big(F(z,z)\big(E(y,z)+E(z,y)+tE(y,y)\big)\Big)\Big].

From this it may be conclude that

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{t\rightarrow 0}\frac{\varphi(h(z+ty))-\varphi(h(z))}{t}

\displaystyle=\varphi\Big(E(z,z)^{-1}E(z,z)^{-1}\Big)

\displaystyle\quad\quad\Big[\varphi\Big(E(z,z)\big(F(y,z)+F(z,y)\big)\Big)

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad-\varphi\Big(F(z,z)\big(E(y,z)+E(z,y)\big)\Big)\Big].

Since
\varphi
is multiplicative (and linear), we have

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{t\rightarrow 0}\frac{\varphi(h(z+ty))-\varphi(h(z))}{t}

\displaystyle=\varphi\big(E(z,z)^{-1}\big)\varphi\Big(F(z,y)-E(z,z)^{-1}F(z,z)E(z,y)\Big)

\displaystyle+\varphi\big(E(z,z)^{-1}\big)\varphi\Big(F(y,z)-E(z,z)^{-1}F(z,z)E(y,z)\Big)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{FE-equality-1}}}{{=}}\varphi\big(E(z,z)^{-1}\big)0+\varphi\big(E(z,z)^{-1}\big)0=0.

From this we conclude that
\varphi\circ h
is a constant
function. Since
\mathscr{A}_{m}^{\prime}
is total,
h\colon B\left(w,\beta\right)\to\mathscr{A}
is a constant
function, too.
So, we define
c:=h(\cdot)
.
Putting
c
in place of
F(z,z)E(z,z)^{-1}
in the first equality (
3
) we get

\displaystyle F(z,y)=cE(z,y)\ {\rm for}\ z\in B\left(w,\beta\right),\ y\in\mathscr{X}.

(3.5)

Fix
y\in\mathscr{X}
. Let
L_{y}\colon\mathscr{X}\to\mathscr{A}
be a linear
operator defined by the formula

\displaystyle L_{y}(\cdot):=F(\cdot,y)-cE(\cdot,y).

It is worth mentioning that the continuity of
L_{y}
is not needed; more precisely:
we do not know that
F
is bounded.
The condition (
3.5
) says that
L_{y}
is constant on
the open set
B\left(w,\beta\right)
. Therefore the linear operator
L_{y}
is constant
on the whole space
\mathscr{X}
.
Now it is clear that
F(z,y)-cE(z,y)=0
for all
z\in\mathscr{X}
.
Since
y
was arbitrarily chosen from
\mathscr{X}
, we
obtain
F(z,y)-cE(z,y)=0
for all
z,y\in\mathscr{X}
. But now we know that
F
is bounded.
∎

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
\mathscr{A}
be a
C^{*}
-algebra with identity. Assume that
\mathscr{A}^{\prime}_{m}
is a total set.
Let
\mathscr{X}
be a right
\mathscr{A}
-module, and suppose that
\mathscr{X}
be a normed space.
Let
n\geq 2
and let
E\colon\mathscr{X}^{n}\to\mathscr{A}
and
F\colon\mathscr{X}^{n}\to\mathscr{A}
be
multi-
\mathscr{A}
-linear functions. Moreover, assume that
E
is bounded and strong.
The following assertions are equivalent:

(i)

\forall_{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\in\mathscr{X}}\quad E(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})=0\,\Longrightarrow\,F(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})=0
,

(ii)

\exists_{c\in\mathscr{A}}\ \forall_{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\in\mathscr{X}}\quad F(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})=cE(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
.

Moreover, each of the above conditions implies:

(iii)

F
is bounded.

Proof.

The proof of Theorem
3.6
is by induction. For
n=2
we have
proved that it is true. Indeed, see Theorem
3.5
.
Suppose that Theorem
3.6
holds for
n
. Now, assume
that
E\colon\mathscr{X}^{n+1}\to\mathscr{A}
,
F\colon\mathscr{X}^{n+1}\to\mathscr{A}
are
multi-
\mathscr{A}
-linear functions. Moreover, assume that
E
is bounded and strong. Suppose that

\forall_{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n},x_{n+1}\in\mathscr{X}}\quad E(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n},x_{n+1})=0\,\Longrightarrow\,F(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n},x_{n+1})=0
.

Since
E
is strong, it follows that there
exists
w\in\mathscr{X}
with
E(w,\ldots,w,w)\in G_{\mathscr{A}}
.
It is clear that
w\neq 0
. Put
p:=E(w,\ldots,w,w)
.
We know that
G_{\mathscr{A}}
is open and
E
is bounded. Analysis similar to that
in the proof of Theorem
3.5
shows that there is
an open ball
B(w,\beta)\subseteq\mathscr{X}
such that

\displaystyle x\in B\left(w,\beta\right)\ \ \Longrightarrow\ \ E(x,\ldots,x,x)\in G_{\mathscr{A}},

(3.6)

i.e.
E(x,\ldots,x,x)
is invertible. Fix
b\in B(w,\beta)
. The continuity of
E
implies
that there is an open ball
B(u,\eta)\subseteq B(w,\beta)
such that

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{ll}\forall_{z\in B(u,\eta)}\ \forall_{y\in\mathscr{X}}\ \exists_{t_{z,y}>0}\ \forall_{|t|<t_{z,y}}&z+ty\in B(u,\eta),\ \ {\rm and}\\
&E\left(\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(1)}}{{z}},\ldots,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(n-1)}}{{z}},\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(n)}}{{z+ty}},\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(n+1)}}{{z+ty}}\right)\in G_{\mathscr{A}}.\end{array}

Define multi-
\mathscr{A}
-linear functions

E_{b}\colon\mathscr{X}^{n}\to\mathscr{A}
and
F_{b}\colon\mathscr{X}^{n}\to\mathscr{A}
by
the following formulas

\displaystyle E_{b}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}):=E(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n},b)\quad\mbox{and}\quad F_{b}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}):=F(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n},b).

It is easily seen that

\forall_{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\in\mathscr{X}}\quad E_{b}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})=0\,\Longrightarrow\,F_{b}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})=0
,

and
E_{b}
is strong by (
3.6
).
It follows from the induction hypothesis that there
exists a function
\widehat{c}\colon\ B(w,\beta)\to\mathscr{A}
such that

\displaystyle\forall_{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\in\mathscr{X}}\quad F_{b}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})=\widehat{c}(b)E_{b}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}).

(3.9)

Now the property (
3.9
) becomes

\displaystyle\forall_{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\in\mathscr{X}}\quad F(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n},b)=\widehat{c}(b)E(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n},b).

(3.10)

It is helpful to recall
that
z+ty\in B(w,\beta)
. Combining (
3.10
)
with (
3
) yields

\widehat{c}(z+ty)=F(z,\ldots,z,z+ty,z+ty)E(z,\ldots,z,z+ty,z+ty)^{-1}
.

Fix
\varphi\in\mathscr{A}^{\prime}_{m}
.
In a similar way as in the proof of Theorem
3.5
we
obtain
\lim\limits_{t\rightarrow 0}\frac{\varphi\left(\widehat{c}(z+ty)\right)-\varphi\left(\widehat{c}(z)\right)}{t}=0
.
Since
\mathscr{A}_{m}^{\prime}
is total,
\widehat{c}\colon B\left(w,\beta\right)\to\mathscr{A}
is constant on
B(u,\eta)
; so we
define
c:=\widehat{c}(\cdot)|_{B(u,\eta)}
. Now the property (
3.10
) becomes

\displaystyle\forall_{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\in\mathscr{X}}\ \forall_{z\in B(u,\eta)}\quad F(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n},z)=cE(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n},z).

Thus a linear operator

F(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n},\cdot)-cE(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n},\cdot)\colon\mathscr{X}\to\mathscr{A}

is constant
on the open set
B(u,\eta)
. Therefore

F(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n},x_{n+1})-cE(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n},x_{n+1})=0
for all
x_{j}\in\mathscr{X}
with
j=1,\ldots,n,n+1
(see also the
proof of Theorem
3.5
).
∎

T\colon\mathscr{X}\to\mathscr{Y}
be a nonvanishing mapping. Let
\gamma\in(0,+\infty)
.
Then, the following conditions are equivalent:

(i)

\forall_{x,y\in\mathscr{X}}\ \ \langle Tx,Ty\rangle=\gamma^{2}\langle x,y\rangle
,

(ii)

T
is
\mathscr{A}
-linear and
|Tx|=\gamma|x|
for all
x\in\mathscr{X}
,

(iii)

T
is linear and
|Tx|=\gamma|x|
for all
x\in\mathscr{X}
.

Before launching into the proof, a few words motivating the proof are
appropriate. It is easy to see that (ii)
\Rightarrow
(iii) is trivial. The
\mathscr{A}
-linearity implies
linearity, but the reverse is not true. Therefore the
the implication (iii)
\Rightarrow
(ii) seems interesting.

Proof.

Taking into account Theorem
4.5
, it remains to
prove only (iii)
\Rightarrow
(i). For two fixed
vectors
x,y\in\mathscr{X}
, we can derive the following chain of equalities

\displaystyle\langle Tx,Ty\rangle

\displaystyle=\!\frac{1}{4}\left(|Ty\!+\!Tx|^{2}\!-\!|Ty\!-\!Tx|^{2}\!-\!i|iTy\!+\!Tx|^{2}\!+\!i|iTy\!-\!Tx|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\left(|T(y\!+\!x)|^{2}\!-\!|T(y\!-\!x)|^{2}\!-\!i|T(iy\!+\!x)|^{2}\!+\!i|T(iy\!-\!x)|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\gamma^{2}\left(|y\!+\!x|^{2}\!-\!|y\!-\!x|^{2}\!-\!i|iy\!+\!x|^{2}\!+\!i|iy\!-\!x|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\gamma^{2}\langle x,y\rangle,

which means that
T
satisfies the property (i), and we are done.

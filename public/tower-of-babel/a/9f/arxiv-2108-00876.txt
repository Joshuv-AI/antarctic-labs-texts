The supercritical deformed Hermitian Yang–Mills equation on compact projective manifolds
Aashirwad Ballal

Abstract
. In this paper, we extend a result of [ Che21 ] regarding the solvability of the twisted deformed Hermitian Yang-Mills equations on compact Kähler manifolds to allow for the twisting function to be non-constant and slightly negative in all dimensions. Using this result along with the methods in [ DP20 ] , we prove that the twisted dHYM equation on compact, projective manifolds can be solved provided certain numerical conditions are satisfied. As a corollary, we obtain a new proof in the projective case of a recent theorem of [ CLT21 ] addressing a conjecture of [ CJY20 ] .



1 Introduction

Let
M
be a compact, connected Kähler manifold of complex dimension
n
with a Kähler form
\chi
and let
\omega_{0}
be a smooth, real
(1,1)
-form on
M
. Any other such
\omega
in the same cohomology class as
\omega_{0}
can be written as
\omega=\omega_{0}+\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}\phi
for some smooth function
\phi
and we will denote by
\lambda_{1}(x),\dots,\lambda_{n}(x)
the eigenvalues of
\omega
with respect to the metric
\chi
at a point
x\in M
. The deformed Hermitian Yang-Mills equation can then be written as

(1)

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\text{arccot}(\lambda_{i}(x))=\theta

where
\theta
is a real constant called the “phase angle” and the range of
arccot
is chosen to be
(0,\pi)
. The connections of the dHYM equation to string theory and mirror symmetry are discussed in
[
CXY17
]
. Necessarily, we must have
\theta\in(0,n\pi)
. When
\theta\in(0,\pi)
the phase is said to be supercritical and this is the case we consider in this paper.

When the phase is supercritical, it can be shown that (see the next section) solving the dHYM equation is equivalent to finding an
\omega\in[\omega_{0}]
such that

(2)

\Re(\omega+\sqrt{-1}\chi)^{n}-\cot(\theta)\Im(\omega+\sqrt{-1}\chi)^{n}=0

and

(3)

\Re(\omega+\sqrt{-1}\chi)^{k}-\cot(\theta)\Im(\omega+\sqrt{-1}\chi)^{k}>0

where
k=0,1,\dots,n-1
. In fact, the positivity condition
3
alone is sufficient, assuming the integral of the left side of equation
2
is zero (
[
CJY20
]
). More generally,
[
Che21
]
proved the following result:

Proposition 1
.

[
Che21
, Proposition 5.5]

Let
(M,\chi)
be a compact Kähler manifold,
0<\theta<\Theta<\pi
be constants, and
f
a smooth function on
M
. Assume that there exists a smooth
\omega_{0}
satisfying
\omega_{0}\in\Gamma_{\chi,\theta,\Theta}
(for this notation, see the next section). Suppose that

\int_{M}\Re(\omega_{0}+\sqrt{-1}\chi)^{n}-\cot(\theta)\Im(\omega_{0}+\sqrt{-1}\chi)^{n}=\int_{M}f\chi^{n}\geq 0

Then there exists a constant
\epsilon>0
depending only on
n,\theta,\Theta
such that if

\begin{split}n\geq 4\text{ and }f>-\epsilon,\text{ or}\\
n\leq 3\text{ and }f\geq 0\text{ is constant}\\
\end{split}

there exists a smooth
\omega\in[\omega_{0}]
with
\omega\in\Gamma_{\chi,\theta,\Theta}
and

\Re(\omega+\sqrt{-1}\chi)^{n}-\cot(\theta)\Im(\omega+\sqrt{-1}\chi)^{n}=f\chi^{n}

The “twisted” dHYM equation is

\Re(\omega+\sqrt{-1}\chi)^{n}-\cot(\theta)\Im(\omega+\sqrt{-1}\chi)^{n}=f\chi^{n}

When the dimension

of the Kähler manifold is greater than

is allowed to be “slightly negative” whereas in dimensions less than
4

is assumed to be non-negative and constant. The first main result of this paper is to extend this theorem to allow a slightly negative and non-constant

We now sketch an outline of the approaches to the above theorems.

The

follows immediately from the fact that

. For

[

]
of the Calabi conjecture. The case of most interest to us here is hence

[

]
. The new complications in the twisted dHYM equation as opposed to the non-twisted one will mainly show up while proving the Laplacian estimates.

To prove Theorem

[

]
. Using

2 The cone conditions

In this section we prove and quote some results regarding the dHYM equation which will be used throughout the paper. As before, let
(M,\chi)
be a Kähler manifold of dimension
n
. A smooth, real
(1,1)
-form
\omega
is said to satisfy the deformed Hermitian Yang-Mills equation on
(M,\chi)
with phase angle
\theta
if

(4)

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\text{arccot}(\lambda_{i})=\theta

where
(\lambda_{i})_{i}
are the eigenvalues of
\omega
with respect to the Kähler form
\chi
.

Throughout the paper, we assume that the phase angle is supercritical i.e.
\theta\in(0,\pi)
. In this case, equation
4
is equivalent to the following:

(5)

\Re(\omega+\sqrt{-1}\chi)^{n}-\cot(\theta)\Im(\omega+\sqrt{-1}\chi)^{n}=0

\displaystyle\Re(\omega+\sqrt{-1}\chi)^{k}-\cot(\theta)\Im(\omega+\sqrt{-1}\chi)^{k}>0

\displaystyle k=1,\dots,n-1

(the positivity of forms above is as defined in
[
Dem
]
. The notions of strong positivity and positivity in
[
Dem
]
coincide when we deal with polynomial expressions of two real
(1,1)
-forms, of which is Kähler, as in
5
).

To see the equivalence of
4
and
5
(when the phase is supercritical), choose a point
x\in M
and a basis
(\xi_{i})_{i}
of
\bigwedge^{(1,0)}T^{*}_{x}
such that
\chi(x)=\sqrt{-1}\sum_{i}\xi_{i}\wedge\bar{\xi_{i}}
and
\omega(x)=\sqrt{-1}\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}(x)\xi_{i}\wedge\bar{\xi_{i}}
. In this basis,

(6)

\displaystyle\Re((\omega+\sqrt{-1}\chi)^{k})-\cot(\theta)\Im((\omega+\sqrt{-1}\chi)^{k})(x)=

(7)

\displaystyle\sum_{K\subset\{1,\dots,n\},|K|=k}k!\sqrt{-1}^{k}\prod_{i\in K}\sqrt{1+\lambda_{i}(x)^{2}}\frac{\sin(\theta-\sum_{i\in K}\theta_{i}(x))}{\sin(\theta)}\bigwedge_{i\in K}\xi_{i}\wedge\bar{\xi_{i}}

where
\theta_{i}(x):=\text{arccot}(\lambda_{i}(x))
. So the required equivalence follows immediately from the following elementary lemma:

Lemma 1
.

Let
\theta,\theta_{1},\dots,\theta_{k}\in(0,\pi)
, then
\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k}\theta_{i}<\theta
if and only if
\displaystyle\sin(\theta-\sum_{i\in I}\theta_{i})>0
for all subsets
I
of
\{1,\dots,k\}
.

Proof.

If
\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k}\theta_{i}<\theta
, then certainly we must have
0<\displaystyle\sum_{i\in I}\theta_{i}<\theta
for all non-empty subsets
I
of
\{1,\dots,k\}
and consequently
\displaystyle\sin(\theta-\sum_{i\in I}\theta_{i})>0
. If
I
is empty, then
\sin(\theta)>0
as
\theta\in(0,\pi)

For the converse, we use induction on
k
. If
k=0
, there is nothing to prove. Assume the lemma has been proved for
k-1
. By the induction hypothesis, we must have
\displaystyle 0<\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}\theta_{i}<\theta
. Hence,
\displaystyle-\pi<\theta-\sum_{i=1}^{k}\theta_{i}<\theta
. As we have
\displaystyle\sin(\theta-\sum_{i=1}^{k}\theta_{i})>0
, it follows that
\displaystyle\theta>\sum_{i=1}^{k}\theta_{i}
.
∎

For any two real
(1,1)
-forms
\alpha
,
\beta
, we define the polynomial
G_{\theta}^{k}(\alpha,\beta)
as

G_{\theta}^{k}(\alpha,\beta)=\Re(\alpha+\sqrt{-1}\beta)^{k}-\cot(\theta)\Im(\alpha+\sqrt{-1}\beta)^{k}

From the discussion above, we see that for a real
(1,1)
-form
\omega
on
M
and
\theta\in(0,\pi)
, the following conditions are equivalent

(8)

\displaystyle\sup_{I\subset\{1,\dots,n\},|I|=m}\sum_{i\in I}\text{arccot}(\lambda_{i})<\theta

\displaystyle\iff

(9)

\displaystyle G^{k}_{\theta}(\omega,\chi)>0\text{ for }k=1,\dots,m

For some small values of
k
, the polynomials
G^{k}_{\theta}
are as below:

\displaystyle G_{\theta}^{1}(\alpha,\beta)

\displaystyle=\alpha-\cot(\theta)\beta

\displaystyle G_{\theta}^{2}(\alpha,\beta)

\displaystyle=\alpha^{2}-2\cot(\theta)\alpha\beta-\beta^{2}

\displaystyle G_{\theta}^{3}(\alpha,\beta)

\displaystyle=\alpha^{3}-3\cot(\theta)\alpha^{2}\chi-3\alpha\chi^{2}+\cot(\theta)\chi^{3}

Definition 1

(
\Gamma^{m}_{\chi,\theta},\Gamma_{\chi,\theta},\Gamma_{\chi,\theta,\Theta}
)
.

Let

(1,1)
-form and
\omega

We see that if

\displaystyle P_{\theta}^{1}(\alpha,\beta)

\displaystyle=\alpha

\displaystyle P_{\theta}^{2}(\alpha,\beta)

\displaystyle=\alpha^{2}-\csc^{2}(\theta)\beta^{2}

\displaystyle P_{\theta}^{3}(\alpha,\beta)

\displaystyle=\alpha^{3}-3\csc^{2}(\theta)\alpha\beta^{2}-2\csc^{2}(\theta)\cot(\theta)\beta^{3}

Definition 2

(Cone condition for smooth forms)

Let

(1,1)

It is known that the sets

\displaystyle\rho

(Cone condition for positive currents)

We now prove some useful results to be used later in the paper. The following lemma shows that one can ensure that one can perturb the background positive-definite matrix and still maintain the cone condition by assuming a little more positivity.

Lemma 2
.

If
\chi_{0}
is a positive-definite matrix,
\omega
is Hermitian with respect to
\chi_{0}
,
\omega\in\Gamma_{\chi_{0},\theta}
and
\epsilon_{1}>0
, then there exists an
\epsilon_{2}>0
depending only on
\epsilon_{1},\theta,n
such that for all
\chi_{0}\leq\chi\leq\chi_{0}(1+\epsilon_{2})
, we have
\omega+2\epsilon_{1}\chi\in\Gamma_{\chi,\theta}

Proof.

We first show that for all
\chi\geq\chi_{0}
,
\omega+\epsilon_{1}\chi\in\Gamma_{\chi_{0},\theta-\epsilon_{3}}
some
\epsilon_{3}
depending only on
\epsilon_{1},\theta,n
. Let
(\lambda_{i})_{i=1,\dots,n}
denote the eigenvalues of
\omega
with respect to
\chi_{0}
ordered so that
\lambda_{1}\leq\dots\leq\lambda_{n}
and
(\gamma_{i})_{i=1,\dots,n}

with respect to
\chi_{0}
ordered similarly. It is not hard to see that we must have

Let

of

\displaystyle\lambda_{i}\geq\cot\bigg(\frac{\theta}{2(1+n^{2})}\bigg)

\forall i

. If not, then

\epsilon_{4}>0

\text{arccot}(\lambda+\epsilon_{1})<\text{arccot}(\lambda)-\epsilon_{4}

As before, let
(\lambda_{i})_{i=1,\dots,n}

If

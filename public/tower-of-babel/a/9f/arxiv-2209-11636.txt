On symmetric closed subsets of real affine root systems
Dipnit Biswas, Irfan Habib, R. Venkatesh

Abstract
. Any symmetric closed subset of a finite crystallographic root system must be a closed subroot system. This is not, in general, true for real affine root systems. In this paper, we determine when this is true and also give a very explicit description of symmetric closed subsets of real affine root systems. At the end, using our results, we study the correspondence between symmetric closed subsets of real affine root systems and the regular subalgebras generated by them.



1.
Introduction

One of the main motivations to study closed subsets of a given finite crystallographic root system is that they are closely related to the regular subalgebras of the corresponding semi-simple Lie algebra (see
[
15
,
12
]
).
They also appear in various other contexts. For example, they appear in

•

the classification of maximal closed connected subgroups of maximal rank of a connected compact Lie group
[
3
]

•

the theory of abelian/ad-nilpotent ideals of Borel subalgebras
[
23
,
9
]

•

the theory Chevalley groups
[
18
]

•

the classification of reflection subgroups of finite and affine Weyl groups
[
13
]
.

Various special classes of closed subsets of a finite root system were classified by many authors. For example,

•

A. Borel and J. De Siebenthal classified maximal closed subroot systems of finite root systems in
[
3
]

•

E. B. Dynkin came up with an algorithm to classify all subroot systems (upto the Weyl group conjugacy) of a finite root system in
[
15
]

•

Z. I. Borevič used topological methods in
[
4
]
to determine all closed subsets of root systems of type
A_{n},1\leq n\leq 8
, and this work was subsequently taken forward by others, see for e.g.
[
6
]

•

invertible closed subsets were classified in
[
11
]

•

recently, A. Douglas and W.A. de Graaf have given an algorithm for classifying all closed subsets of a finite root system, up to conjugation by the associated Weyl group, in
[
12
]
.

The problem of classifying closed subsets of (real) affine root systems is wide open. Again some very particular classes were studied in this setting.
The invertible closed subsets of affine root systems were classified in
[
10
]
and the parabolic subsets were classified in
[
17
]
.
Anna Felikson et al. started the classification regular subalgebras of affine Kac-Moody algebras (which are related to the closed subroot systems of real affine root systems) in
[
16
]
and it was completed in
[
22
]
, see also
[
21
]
.
The classification of the reflection subgroups of finite and affine Weyl groups has been achieved in
[
13
]
by classifying
subroot systems finite and real affine root systems.
It is well-known that the classification of all subroot systems may be deduced from that of the closed subroot systems in the finite setting, see
[
7
]
. A combinatorial description of biclosed sets of real affine root systems has been given very recently in
[
1
]
.

We are mainly interested in the closed subsets of real affine root systems in this paper, as they are closely related to the Cartan invariant subalgebras of corresponding affine Kac-Moody algebras and this will be discussed elsewhere.
Suppose
\Psi
is a closed subset of a real affine root system
\Phi
, i.e., if
\alpha,\beta\in\Psi
and
\alpha+\beta\in\Phi
implies that
\alpha+\beta\in\Psi
, then it is easy see that
\Psi
can be written as a union of its symmetric part
\Psi^{r}=\Psi\cap-\Psi
and special part
\Psi^{s}=\Psi\backslash\Psi^{r}.

Both symmetric and special parts of
\Psi
are closed in
\Phi
. So the classification of closed subsets of
\Phi
reduces to the classification of
symmetric and special closed subsets of
\Phi
. In general the problem of classifying special closed subsets is very hard and it will be discussed in
[
2
]
.
In this paper we only focus on symmetric closed subsets of real affine root systems. The main motivation for this work comes from the following two assertions about the finite root system:

•

each symmetric closed subset of a finite root system must be a closed subroot system and

•

there is a one to one correspondence between closed subsets of a finite root system and the Cartan invariant subalgebras of corresponding semi-simple Lie algebra (see
[
12
, Proposition 4.1]
for the precise statement).

Both these statements are not true in general for real affine root systems and this naturally motivates us to ask the following questions:

(1)

When a given symmetric closed subset of a real affine root system is a closed subroot system?

(2)

Is it possible to classify all symmetric closed subsets of a real affine root system?

(3)

Consider the map
\Psi\mapsto\mathfrak{g}(\Psi)
where
\Psi
is a symmetric closed subset of a real affine root system and
\mathfrak{g}(\Psi)
is the subalgebra generated by
\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha},\,\alpha\in\Psi.
We will see that this map is not injective for any affine Kac-Moody algebras (see Section
6
). Is it possible to determine the preimage of given
\mathfrak{g}(\Psi)
?

We will address all these questions in this paper. The paper is organized as follows: we recall the definitions and set up all the notations in Section
2
.
The symmetric closed subsets of real affine root systems whose gradient is closed are studied in Section
3
, and
the symmetric closed subsets of real affine root systems whose gradient is semi-closed are studied in Section
4
.
The correspondence between symmetric closed subsets of real affine root systems and regular subalgebras of corresponding affine Lie algebras is discussed in Section
6
.
We summarize all our results at the end, in Section
7
.

2.
Preliminaries

Throughout this paper, we denote by
\mathbb{C}
(resp.
\mathbb{R}
), the field of complex numbers (resp. real numbers) and by
\mathbb{Z}
(resp.
\mathbb{Z}_{+}
), the set of integers (resp. non-negative integers).

2.1.

Let
E
be an Euclidean space over
\mathbb{R}
endowed with a positive definite symmetric bilinear form
(\cdot,\cdot)
. A finite (crystallographic) root system

\mathring{\Phi}
is a finite subset of
E
satisfying the following properties (see
[
5
, Chapter VI]
or
[
19
, Section 9.2]
):

0\notin\mathring{\Phi},\ \ \text{Span}_{\mathbb{R}}\mathring{\Phi}=E,\ \ s_{\alpha}(\mathring{\Phi})=\mathring{\Phi},\ \forall\,\alpha\in\mathring{\Phi},\ \ (\beta,\alpha^{\vee})\in\mathbb{Z},\ \forall\,\alpha,\beta\in\mathring{\Phi},

where
\alpha^{\vee}:=2\alpha/(\alpha,\alpha)
and
s_{\alpha}
is the reflection acting on
E
defined by
s_{\alpha}(x)=x-(x,\alpha^{\vee})\alpha
,
x\in E
. For the rest of this paper, we denote
\mathring{\Phi}
by a finite root system in
E
. In addition, if
\mathring{\Phi}
satisfies
\mathbb{R}\alpha\cap\mathring{\Phi}=\{\pm\alpha\}
for
\alpha\in\mathring{\Phi}
, then we call
\mathring{\Phi}
reduced. Moreover, we call any subset
\Psi\subseteq\mathring{\Phi}
irreducible whenever
\Psi=\Psi^{\prime}\cup\Psi^{\prime\prime}
with
(\Psi^{\prime},\Psi^{\prime\prime})=0
implies
\Psi^{\prime}=\emptyset
or
\Psi^{\prime\prime}=\emptyset
. Any root system can be written as a direct sum of irreducible root systems (see
[
5
, Chapter VI]
) and the reduced irreducible root systems were classified in terms of their Dynkin diagrams (see
[
19
, Theorem 11.4]
).
They are the classical types
A_{n}\,(n\geq 1),B_{n}\,(n\geq 2),C_{n}\,(n\geq 3),D_{n}\,(n\geq 4)
and the exceptional types
E_{6,7,8},F_{4}
and
G_{2}
. For a direct construction of these root systems we refer to
[
19
, Section 12.1]
. Moreover, there is only one non–reduced irreducible root system of rank
n
, namely

BC_{n}=B_{n}\cup C_{n}=\{\pm\epsilon_{i}:1\leq i\leq n\}\cup\{\pm\epsilon_{i}\pm\epsilon_{j}:1\leq i\neq j\leq n\}\cup\{\pm 2\epsilon_{i}:1\leq i\leq n\},

where
\epsilon_{1},\dots,\epsilon_{n}
denotes an orthonormal basis of
E
with respect to
(\cdot,\cdot)
.
Recall that a subset
\mathring{\Psi}
of
\mathring{\Phi}
is said to be symmetric if
\mathring{\Psi}=-\mathring{\Psi}
, and it is called
closed in
\mathring{\Phi}
if
\alpha,\beta\in\mathring{\Psi}
and
\alpha+\beta\in\mathring{\Phi}
implies
\alpha+\beta\in\mathring{\Psi}
.
We record the following simple and important fact on finite root systems, and we include a proof for the reader’s convenience.

Lemma 1
.

Let
\mathring{\Psi}
be a symmetric closed subset of
\mathring{\Phi}
, then
\mathring{\Psi}
is a closed subroot system of
\mathring{\Phi}
.

Proof.

Let
\alpha,\beta\in\mathring{\Psi}
. Suppose
(\beta,\alpha^{\vee})=0
, then
s_{\alpha}(\beta)=\beta\in\mathring{\Psi}
. So assume that
(\beta,\alpha^{\vee})\neq 0
.
If
(\beta,\alpha^{\vee})<0
, then
\beta,\beta+\alpha,\ldots,\beta+(-(\beta,\alpha^{\vee}))\alpha
are elements of
\mathring{\Phi}
by
[
19
, Proposition 8.4, Page 89]
. Since
\mathring{\Psi}
is closed, we have
\beta,\beta+\alpha,\ldots,\beta+(-(\beta,\alpha^{\vee}))\alpha\in\mathring{\Psi}
which implies
s_{\alpha}(\beta)=\beta-(\beta,\alpha^{\vee})\alpha\in\mathring{\Psi}
.
Since
\mathring{\Psi}
is symmetric, the case
(\beta,\alpha^{\vee})>0
can be done similarly.
∎

2.2.

The Weyl group
W
of a finite root system
\mathring{\Phi}
is defined to be the subgroup of
GL(E)
generated by
s_{\alpha},\alpha\in\mathring{\Phi}
. At most two root lengths occur in any reduced irreducible finite root system
\mathring{\Phi}
and all roots of a given length are conjugate under the Weyl group
W
of
\mathring{\Phi}
(see for example
[
19
, Section 10.4]
). We denote the set of short roots (resp. long roots) by
\mathring{\Phi}_{s}
(resp.
\mathring{\Phi}_{\ell}
) and if there is only one root length then we say that every root is short by convention. If
\mathring{\Phi}
is non–reduced irreducible finite root system, we define:

\mathring{\Phi}_{s}=\{\pm\epsilon_{i},\ 1\leq i\leq n\},\ \ \mathring{\Phi}_{\ell}=\{\pm\epsilon_{i}\pm\epsilon_{j}:1\leq i\neq j\leq n\},\ \ \mathring{\Phi}_{d}=\{\pm 2\epsilon_{i},\ 1\leq i\leq n\}.

Note that
\mathring{\Phi}_{d}=\{\alpha\in\mathring{\Phi}:\alpha/2\in\mathring{\Phi}\}
is the set of divisible roots in
BC_{n}
and define the non–divisible roots of
BC_{n}
by
\mathring{\Phi}_{nd}=\mathring{\Phi}\backslash\mathring{\Phi}_{d}
. Further, set by
\mathring{\Pi}
a base of
\mathring{\Phi}
and set

m_{\mathring{\Phi}}=\begin{cases}1,&\text{$\mathring{\Phi}$ is of type $A_{n}$, $D_{n}$ or $E_{n}$}\\
2,&\text{$\mathring{\Phi}$ is of type $B_{n}$, $C_{n}$ or $F_{4}$}\\
3,&\text{$\mathring{\Phi}$ is of type $G_{2}$}\\
\end{cases}

We often use
m
instead of
m_{\mathring{\Phi}}
if the underlying
\mathring{\Phi}
is understood.
We end with the following fact (see
[
8
, Proposition 8.17]
).

Lemma 2
.

Let
\mathring{\Phi}
be a finite irreducible root system. Let
\beta\in\mathring{\Phi}
and write
\beta=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathring{\Pi}}k_{\alpha}\alpha
. Then
\beta
is a long root if and only if
m
divides
k_{\alpha}
for each short root
\alpha\in\mathring{\Pi}
. ∎

2.3.

Let
\mathring{\Phi}
be an irreducible reduced finite root system. Let
\mathring{\mathfrak{g}}
be a finite–dimensional semi-simple Lie algebra over
\mathbb{C}
and
\mathring{\mathfrak{h}}
a Cartan subalgebra of
\mathring{\mathfrak{g}}
such that the root system corresponding to the pair
(\mathring{\mathfrak{g}},\mathring{\mathfrak{h}})
is

\mathring{\Phi}
. Let
\sigma
be a Dynkin diagram automorphism
of
\mathring{\mathfrak{h}}
with respect to
\mathring{\Phi}
and denote
m
by the order of
\sigma
. We know that
m\in\{1,2,3\}
. Let
\xi
be a primitive
m
–th root of unity. We have

\mathring{\mathfrak{g}}=\bigoplus_{j\in\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}}\mathfrak{g}_{j},\ \ \mathfrak{g}_{j}=\{x\in\mathfrak{g}:\sigma(x)=\xi^{j}x\}.

It is known that
\mathfrak{g}_{0}
is again a finite-dimensional simple Lie algebra over
\mathbb{C}
with a Cartan subalgebra
\mathfrak{h}_{0}=\mathring{\mathfrak{h}}\cap\mathfrak{g}_{0}
. Moreover,
\mathfrak{g}_{j}
is a
\mathfrak{g}_{0}
–module and we denote the set of non–zero weights of
\mathfrak{g}_{j}
with respect to
\mathfrak{h}_{0}
by
\mathring{\Phi}_{j}
.
Then we have

\mathring{\Phi}:=\mathring{\Phi}_{0}\cup\cdots\cup\mathring{\Phi}_{m-1}.

The types of
\mathring{\Phi},\mathring{\Phi}_{0},\ldots,\mathring{\Phi}_{m-1}
can be extracted from
[
20
, Section 7.8, 7.9, 8.3]
.
The corresponding affine Kac–Moody algebra
\mathfrak{g}=\widehat{\mathcal{L}}(\mathring{\mathfrak{g}},\sigma)
is defined by

\mathfrak{g}=\mathcal{L}(\mathring{\mathfrak{g}},\sigma)\oplus\mathbb{C}c\oplus\mathbb{C}d,\ \ \mathcal{L}(\mathring{\mathfrak{g}},\sigma)=\bigoplus_{j\in\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}}\mathfrak{g}_{j}\otimes\mathbb{C}[t^{\pm m}]t^{j},

where
\mathcal{L}(\mathring{\mathfrak{g}},\sigma)
is called the loop algebra,
\mathcal{L}(\mathring{\mathfrak{g}},\sigma)\oplus\mathbb{C}c
is the universal central extension of the loop algebra and
d=t\frac{d}{dt}
is the degree derivation. For more details we refer the reader to
[
20
, Section 7,8]
. The set of roots of
\mathfrak{g}
with respect to the Cartan subalgebra
\mathfrak{h}=\mathfrak{h}_{0}\oplus\mathbb{C}c\oplus\mathbb{C}d
is exactly
\Delta\backslash\{0\}
, where

\Delta:=\{\alpha+r\delta:\alpha\in\mathring{\Phi}_{j}\cup\{0\},\ \ r\in j+m\mathbb{Z},\ 0\leq j<m\},

Denote
\Phi
(resp.
\Phi^{\text{im}}
) by the set of real (resp. imaginary) roots of
\mathfrak{g}
. Then we have

\Phi=\bigcup_{\alpha\in\mathring{\Phi}}\big(\alpha+\Lambda_{\alpha}\big),\ \Phi^{\text{im}}=\mathbb{Z}\delta.

where
\Lambda_{\alpha}=\Lambda_{\beta}
if
\alpha
and
\beta
have same length and common
\Lambda_{s},\Lambda_{\ell},\Lambda_{d}
can be found in the following table for each case.

{\begin{array}[]{|c |c| c| c| c | c| c| c|}\hline\cr(\mathfrak{g},m)&\mathring{\Phi}_{0}&\mathring{\Phi}_{1}&\mathring{\Phi}_{2}&\mathring{\Phi}&\Lambda_{s}&\Lambda_{\ell}&\Lambda_{d}\\
\hline\cr(\mathfrak{g}(\mathring{\Phi}),1)&\mathring{\Phi}&/&/&\mathring{\Phi}&\mathbb{Z}&\mathbb{Z}&/\\
\hline\cr(A_{2n},2)&B_{n}&\mathring{\Phi}_{0}\cup\{\pm 2\epsilon_{i}:1\leq i\leq n\}&/&BC_{n}&\frac{1}{2}+\mathbb{Z}&\mathbb{Z}&2\mathbb{Z}\\
\hline\cr(A_{2n-1},2)&C_{n}&(C_{n})_{s}&/&C_{n}&\mathbb{Z}&2\mathbb{Z}&/\\
\hline\cr(D_{n+1},2)&B_{n}&(B_{n})_{s}&/&B_{n}&\mathbb{Z}&2\mathbb{Z}&/\\
\hline\cr(E_{6},2)&F_{4}&(F_{4})_{s}&/&F_{4}&\mathbb{Z}&2\mathbb{Z}&/\\
\hline\cr(D_{4},3)&G_{2}&(G_{2})_{s}&(G_{2})_{s}&G_{2}&\mathbb{Z}&3\mathbb{Z}&/\\
\hline\cr\end{array}}

We end this section with the following definitions.

Definition 1
.

Let
\Psi
be a subset of
\Delta
(resp.
\Phi
). Set
\Psi_{\text{re}}:=\Psi\cap\Phi\ \text{and}\ \Psi_{\text{im}}:=\{r\in\mathbb{Z}:r\delta\in\Psi\}\cup\{0\}.

(1)

We say

is

\Psi=-\Psi
, where

.

(2)

We say
\Psi
is

or

\Phi
if

(a)

\Psi
is a non-empty subset of
\Phi

(b)

if
\alpha,\beta\in\Psi

, then we have

.

(3)

We say

,

2.4.

Let
\Phi
be a real irreducible affine root system as in
Section

2.3
and let
\Psi
be a closed subset of
\Phi
.
The symmetric part of
\Psi
defined to be
\Psi^{r}:=\{\alpha\in\Psi\mid-\alpha\in\Psi\}
and the special part of
\Psi
defined to be
\Psi^{s}:=\{\alpha\in\Psi\mid-\alpha\notin\Psi\}
. It is clear that
\Psi^{r}
is a symmetric closed subset of
\Phi
and
\Psi^{s}
is a closed subset of
\Phi
, and we have

\Psi=\Psi^{r}\sqcup\Psi^{s}.

The gradient of
\Psi
is
Gr(\Psi)\,:=\{\alpha\in\mathring{\Phi}\mid\alpha+k\delta\,\in\Psi
for some
k\in\mathbb{Z}\}
.
For given
\alpha\in Gr(\Psi)
, we define
Z_{\alpha}(\Psi)\,:=\{k\in\mathbb{Z}\mid\alpha+k\delta\in\Psi\}
.
We will simply use
Z_{\alpha}
for
Z_{\alpha}(\Psi)
if the dependence of the underlying
\Psi
is understood.
Clearly we have

\Psi=\bigcup_{\alpha\in Gr(\Psi)}\{\alpha+k\delta\mid k\in Z_{\alpha}\}.

For a given
(p_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in Gr(\Psi)}
, where
p_{\alpha}\in Z_{\alpha}
, define

Z_{\alpha}^{\prime}:=Z_{\alpha}-p_{\alpha}
for
\alpha\in Gr(\Psi)
. We often make specific choices of
(p_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in Gr(\Psi)}
and make sure that the map
\alpha\mapsto p_{\alpha}
gives us a
\mathbb{Z}
-linear function from
p:Gr(\Psi)\to\mathbb{Z}.
For example, we have (see
[
14
, Lemma 13]
and
[
22
, Lemma 2.1.1]
):

Lemma 3
.

Let
\Psi
be a symmetric closed subset of
\Phi
. Suppose

Gr(\Psi)
is a closed, reduced subroot system with a base
B
and assume that we have

(2.1)

Z_{\alpha}+Z_{\beta}\subseteq Z_{\alpha+\beta}\ \ \text{for all}\ \ (\alpha,\beta,\alpha+\beta)\in Gr(\Psi)^{\times 3}.

Choose
p_{\alpha}\in Z_{\alpha}
arbitrarily for
\alpha\in B
and extend the map

\alpha\mapsto p_{\alpha},\alpha\in B
to
Gr(\Psi)

\mathbb{Z}
-linearly. Then we have
p_{\alpha}\in Z_{\alpha}
, for all
\alpha\in Gr(\Psi)
.

Proof.

Define a
\mathbb{Z}
-linear function
p:Gr(\Psi)\to\mathbb{Z}
extending
\alpha\mapsto p_{\alpha},\alpha\in B
.
We claim that
p_{\alpha}\in Z_{\alpha}
for all
\alpha\in Gr(\Psi).
Since
p_{-\alpha}=-p_{\alpha}
and
Z_{-\alpha}=-Z_{\alpha}
, it is enough to prove that
p_{\alpha}\in Z_{\alpha}
for all positive roots
\alpha\in Gr(\Psi).
Let
\alpha\in Gr(\Psi)
be a positive root, then we can write
\alpha=\alpha_{1}+\cdots+\alpha_{r}
with
each partial sum
\alpha_{1}+\cdots+\alpha_{i}
is again a root in
Gr(\Psi)
for
1\leq i\leq r.
Since
\Psi
is closed and using the condition (
2.1
), we have

\alpha_{1}+\cdots+\alpha_{i}+(p_{\alpha_{1}}+\cdots+p_{\alpha_{i}})\delta\in\Psi
for
1\leq i\leq r.
In particular, we have
p_{\alpha}=p_{\alpha_{1}}+\cdots+p_{\alpha_{r}}\in Z_{\alpha}.

This completes the proof.
∎

2.5.

We collect here some basic facts in this subsection. Let
\Psi
be a subset of
\Phi
and let

(2.2)

\Psi=\Psi_{1}\sqcup\dots\sqcup\Psi_{r}

be the decomposition of
\Psi
into irreducible subsets.
Then we have

(2.3)

Gr(\Psi)=Gr(\Psi_{1})\sqcup\dots\sqcup Gr(\Psi_{r})

is the decomposition of
Gr(\Psi)
into irreducible subsets.

Proposition 1
.

Let
\Psi
be a subset of
\Phi.

(1)

Suppose
\Psi
is symmetric, then each
\Psi_{i}
in (
2.2
) and each
Gr(\Psi_{i})
in (
2.3
) are symmetric.

(2)

Suppose
\Psi
is closed in
\Phi
, then each
\Psi_{i}
in (
2.2
) is closed in
\Phi
. In addition if
Gr(\Psi)
is closed in
\mathring{\Phi}
, then each
Gr(\Psi_{i})
in (
2.3
) is closed in
\mathring{\Phi}
.

(3)

Suppose
\Psi
is symmetric closed in
\Phi
. Then
\alpha+\beta\notin\Phi
if
\alpha\in\Psi_{i}\text{ and }\beta\in\Psi_{j}
for
i\neq j
.

(4)

Assume that
Gr(\Psi)
is closed in
\mathring{\Phi}
. Then

(a)

\Psi
is symmetric closed in
\Phi
if and only if each
\Psi_{i}
is symmetric closed in
\Phi
.

(b)

\Psi
is a closed subroot system of
\Phi
if and only if each
\Psi_{i}
is a closed subroot system of
\Phi
.

Proof.

We only prove the statement
4(a)
as all other statements are easy to check. The forward direction follows from
(3)
. For the converse part, we assume that each
\Psi_{i}
is symmetric closed in
\Phi.

Since
Gr(\Psi)
is symmetric closed in
\mathring{\Phi}
, it must be a closed subroot system. Hence each

Gr(\Psi_{i})
is a closed subroot system of
\mathring{\Phi}
and it has a base say
B_{i}
. Then
B:=\cup_{i}B_{i}
is a base for
Gr(\Psi)
. Let
\alpha+r\delta\in\Psi_{i}
and
\beta+s\delta\in\Psi_{j}
with
i\neq j
. If
\alpha+\beta+(r+s)\delta\in\Phi
, then
\alpha+\beta+(r+s)\delta\in\Psi
since
\Psi
is closed. But this implies that
\alpha+\beta\in Gr(\Psi)
, which is impossible because any root of a finite root system has connected support, see
[
8
, Proposition 16.21]
. This completes the proof.
∎

Remark 1
.

The fourth statement in
1
is false in general if we drop the condition
Gr(\Psi)
is closed. For example, let us consider
\Phi
be of type
D_{n+1}^{(2)},n\geq 2
. Set
I_{n}=\{1,2,\dots,n\}
. For
I\subseteq I_{n}
, let

\Psi_{I}:=\{\pm\epsilon_{k}+(2\mathbb{Z}+1)\delta:k\in I\}\cup\{\pm(\epsilon_{k}\pm\epsilon_{\ell})+2\mathbb{Z}\delta:k\neq\ell\in I\}.

It is clear that
\Psi_{I}
is a symmetric closed subset of
\Phi
for each
I.
Now take
\Psi=\Psi_{I}\cup\Psi_{J}
where
I,J
form a partition of
I_{n}
, then
\Psi
is not a closed subset of
\Phi.

2.6.

Given
S\subseteq\Phi
, we define
\mathfrak{g}(S)
by the subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}
generated by
\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha},\alpha\in S.
Let
\Delta(S)
be the set of roots of

\mathfrak{g}(S)
. We end this section with the following proposition.

Proposition 2
.

Let
\Psi
be a symmetric subset of
\Phi
. Then
\Delta(\Psi)\cap\Phi
is a minimal closed subroot system of
\Phi
containing
\Psi.

Proof.

By
[
22
, Lemma 11.1.2, Page 1301]
, it is enough to prove that
\Delta(\Psi)=-\Delta(\Psi)
. Suppose
\beta\in\Delta(\Psi)
, then there exists
\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{r}\in\Psi
such that
\beta=\beta_{1}+\cdots+\beta_{r}
.
Since the Chevalley involution
[
20
, Chapter 1, Page 7]
of
\mathfrak{g}
takes
\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}
to
\mathfrak{g}_{-\alpha}
for all
\alpha\in\Delta
, we have

[\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha_{1}},\cdots[\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha_{r-1}},\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha_{r}}]]\neq 0\iff[\mathfrak{g}_{-\alpha_{1}},\cdots[\mathfrak{g}_{-\alpha_{r-1}},\mathfrak{g}_{-\alpha_{r}}]]\neq 0.

Since
\Psi
is symmetric, we have
-\beta_{1},\ldots,-\beta_{r}\in\Psi

and
0\neq[\mathfrak{g}_{-\alpha_{1}},\cdots[\mathfrak{g}_{-\alpha_{r-1}},\mathfrak{g}_{-\alpha_{r}}]]\subseteq\mathfrak{g}(\Psi)
.
This implies
-\beta\in\Delta
. Hence we have
\Delta(\Psi)=-\Delta(\Psi).

Suppose
\Psi\subseteq S
is a closed subroot system of
\Phi.
Then we have

\mathfrak{g}(\Psi)\subseteq\mathfrak{g}(S)
and the real root of
\mathfrak{g}(S)
is equal to
S
by
[
22
, Corollary 11.1.5, Page 1304]
. Since the real roots of
\mathfrak{g}(\Psi)
are also real roots of
\mathfrak{g}(S)
, we must have
\Delta(\Psi)\cap\Phi\subseteq S.

∎

3.
Symmetric closed subsets of affine root systems

In this section, we fix a real affine root system
\Phi
which is not of type
A_{2n}^{(2)}
. We need the following notation: for
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+},
denote by
\pi_{k}:\mathbb{Z}\to\mathbb{Z}/k\mathbb{Z}
the qutioent map
x\to x\ (\mathrm{mod}\ k)
. Recall that
m
is the lacing number associated with
\mathring{\Phi}.

3.1.

We define semi-closed subset of finite root systems as in
[
22
, Definition 4.4.1]
.

Definition 2
.

A symmetric subset
\mathring{\Psi}
of
\mathring{\Phi}
is called semi-closed subset of
\mathring{\Phi}
if

(1)

\mathring{\Psi}
is not closed in
\mathring{\Phi}
.

(2)

If
\alpha,\beta\in\mathring{\Psi}
such that
\alpha+\beta\in\mathring{\Phi}\setminus\mathring{\Psi}
, then

(\alpha,\beta,\alpha+\beta)
is of type
(s,s,\ell)
.

We need the following simple lemma, see
[
22
, Proposition 4.1.2]
.

Lemma 4
.

Let
\Phi
be a real twisted affine root system not of type
A_{2n}^{(2)}
. Let
\Psi\leq\Phi
be a closed subset. Then
Gr(\Psi)
is either closed or semi-closed subset of
\mathring{\Phi}
. ∎

3.2.

We now consider the case when
Gr(\Psi)
is a closed subroot system of
\mathring{\Phi}
.
The following proposition will be used as a primary tool to prove our main theorem in most of the cases.

Proposition 3
.

Let
\Phi
be a real affine root system not of type
A_{2n}^{(2)}
.
Let
\Psi
be a symmetric closed subset of
\Phi
such that
Gr(\Psi)
is closed in
\mathring{\Phi}
and no irreducible component of
Gr(\Psi)
is of type
A_{1}
. Suppose that there is a
\mathbb{Z}
-linear function
p:Gr(\Psi)\to\mathbb{Z}
,
\alpha\mapsto p_{\alpha}
, such that
p_{\alpha}\in Z_{\alpha},
and
|\pi_{m}(Z_{\alpha})|=1,\text{for all}\ \alpha\in Gr(\Psi)

Then we have

(1)

Z_{\alpha}^{\prime}=Z_{\beta}^{\prime}=Z_{\alpha+\beta}^{\prime}:=A
for

(\alpha,\beta,\alpha+\beta)\in Gr(\Psi)^{\times 3}
and
A
is a subgroup of
m\mathbb{Z}
.

(2)

For any
\alpha,\beta\in\Psi
, we have
s_{\alpha}(\beta)\in\Psi
. In particular,
\Psi
is a closed subroot system of
\Phi
.

Proof.

Recall that
Z_{\alpha}^{\prime}=Z_{\alpha}-p_{\alpha}
for
\alpha\in Gr(\Psi).

Let
(\alpha,\beta,\alpha+\beta)\in Gr(\Psi)^{\times 3}
. We claim that

Z_{\alpha}+Z_{\beta}\subseteq Z_{\alpha+\beta}.

Let
r\in Z_{\alpha}
and
s\in Z_{\beta}
. If
\alpha+\beta
is short or
\Phi
is untwisted then the result is immediate. So assume that
\alpha+\beta
is long. Then we have

p_{\alpha+\beta}\equiv 0\,(\mathrm{mod}\,m)
.
Since
r\equiv p_{\alpha}\,(\mathrm{mod}\,m)\text{ and }s\equiv p_{\beta}\,(\mathrm{mod}\,m)
and the map
p
is
\mathbb{Z}
-linear we have
r+s\equiv 0\,(\mathrm{mod}\,m)
.
This implies
\alpha+\beta+(r+s)\delta\in\Phi
. Since
\Psi
is closed, it follows that
\alpha+\beta+(r+s)\delta\in\Psi
and so
r+s\in Z_{\alpha+\beta}
.
Hence
Z_{\alpha}+Z_{\beta}\subseteq Z_{\alpha+\beta}.
Now using the linearity of
p
, we get

(3.1)

Z_{\alpha}^{\prime}+Z_{\beta}^{\prime}\subseteq Z_{\alpha+\beta}^{\prime}

for all
(\alpha,\beta,\alpha+\beta)\in Gr(\Psi)^{\times 3}
.
Since (
3.1
) is true for all
tuples
(\alpha,\beta,\alpha+\beta)
such that
\alpha,\beta,\alpha+\beta\in Gr(\Psi)
.
We have

Z_{\alpha}^{\prime}+Z_{\beta}^{\prime}\subseteq Z_{\alpha+\beta}^{\prime},\ \ Z_{\alpha+\beta}^{\prime}+Z_{-\alpha}^{\prime}\subseteq Z_{\beta}^{\prime},\ \text{and}\ Z_{\alpha+\beta}^{\prime}+Z_{-\beta}^{\prime}\subseteq Z_{\alpha}^{\prime}.

This implies
A=Z_{\alpha}^{\prime}=Z_{\beta}^{\prime}=Z_{\alpha+\beta}^{\prime}
.
It is easy to see that
Z_{-\mu}^{\prime}=-Z_{\mu}^{\prime}
for all
\mu\in
Gr
(\Psi)
. Thus
A\subseteq m\mathbb{Z}
satisfies
A=-A
and
A+A\subseteq A
, so it must be a subgroup of
m\mathbb{Z}
.

To prove
(2)
, let
\alpha,\beta\in\Psi.
Write
\alpha=\alpha^{\prime}+s\delta,\,\beta=\beta^{\prime}+r\delta
. We claim that

s_{\alpha}(\beta)=\beta-(\beta,\alpha^{\vee})\alpha\in\Psi
. Suppose
\beta^{\prime}\neq\pm\alpha^{\prime}
, then using “unbroken string property” of
\Delta
we get

s_{\alpha}(\beta)\in\Psi
as
\Psi
is closed and any root of the form
\beta+p\alpha,p\in\mathbb{Z},
must be real.
Now assume that
\beta^{\prime}=\pm\alpha^{\prime}
. Since no irreducible component of
Gr(\Psi)
is of type
A_{1}
, there is a
\gamma^{\prime}\in Gr(\Psi)
such that
\alpha^{\prime}+\gamma^{\prime}\in
Gr
(\Psi)
.
Applying the Part (1) for the triple
(\alpha^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime},\alpha^{\prime}+\gamma^{\prime})
, we get that
Z_{\alpha^{\prime}}^{\prime}
is a subgroup of
m\mathbb{Z}
.
We have

s_{\alpha^{\prime}+s\delta}(\pm\alpha^{\prime}+r\delta)=\mp\alpha^{\prime}+(r\mp 2s)\delta

Since
s\in Z_{\alpha^{\prime}},\,r\in Z_{\pm\alpha^{\prime}}
, there are
z_{1},z_{2}\in Z_{\alpha^{\prime}}^{\prime}
such that
s=z_{1}+p_{\alpha^{\prime}}
and
r=z_{2}+p_{\pm\alpha^{\prime}}
.
Now we have
r\mp 2s=z_{2}\pm p_{\alpha^{\prime}}\mp 2(z_{1}+p_{\alpha^{\prime}})=(z_{2}\mp 2z_{1})\mp p_{\alpha^{\prime}}\in Z_{\alpha^{\prime}}^{\prime}+p_{\mp\alpha^{\prime}}
as
Z_{\alpha^{\prime}}^{\prime}
is a group.
Hence
s_{\alpha}(\beta)\in\Psi
and this completes the proof.
∎

3.3.

When
Gr(\Psi)
is closed in
\mathring{\Phi}
, using
1
4(a), we see that to classify a symmetric closed subsets of
\Phi
we only need to classify irreducible symmetric closed subsets of
\Phi.

So without loss of any generality we assume that
\Psi
is irreducible symmetric and closed in
\Phi
in what follows.

Proposition 4
.

Let
\Phi
be a real affine root system not of type
A_{2n}^{(2)}
. Let
\Psi
be an irreducible symmetric closed subset of
\Phi
such that
Gr(\Psi)
is closed in
\mathring{\Phi}
and it is not of type
A_{1}
. If there exists a short root
\beta
such that

|\pi_{m}(Z_{\beta})|=1
, then
there exists
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
and a
\mathbb{Z}
-linear function
p:Gr(\Psi)\to\mathbb{Z}
,
\alpha\mapsto p_{\alpha}
, with
p_{\alpha}\in Z_{\alpha},\,\text{for all}\ \alpha\in Gr(\Psi)
such that

\Psi=\{\alpha+(p_{\alpha}+n\mathbb{Z})\delta:\alpha\in Gr(\Psi)\}

In particular
\Psi
is a closed subroot system of
\Phi
.

Proof.

Note that
Gr(\Psi)
must be a closed subroot system of
\mathring{\Phi}.

Fix a short simple root
\beta\in Gr(\Psi)
such that
|\pi_{m}(Z_{\beta})|=1
. We first show that
|\pi_{m}(Z_{\alpha})|=1
for all simple roots
\alpha
. We prove this by induction on
k
where
\beta=\alpha_{0},\cdots,\alpha_{k}=\alpha
is the unique path from
\beta
to
\alpha
.
Set
\gamma_{j}=\sum_{i=0}^{j}\alpha_{i}
for
0\leq j\leq k
, and note that
\gamma_{j}\in Gr(\Psi)
, for all
0\leq j\leq k
.
We claim that
|\pi_{m}(Z_{\alpha_{j}})|=1
and
|\pi_{m}(Z_{\gamma_{j}})|=1
, for all
0\leq j\leq k
.
For
k=1
, we have
\alpha
is adjacent to

\beta
and
\alpha+\beta\in Gr(\Psi)
.
Consider
Z_{\alpha+\beta}+Z_{-\alpha}\subseteq Z_{\beta}
which is always true as
\beta
is short. This gives
Z_{\alpha+\beta}
and

Z_{-\alpha}
must contain only one congruence class modulo
m.
Since
Z_{-\alpha}=-Z_{\alpha}
, we have
|\pi_{m}(Z_{\gamma})|=1
for
\gamma=\alpha,\alpha+\beta.

For general
k
, we must have
\gamma_{k-1}
is short by
2
since
\beta
is short. Since
\gamma_{k}=\gamma_{k-1}+\alpha
, we have
Z_{\gamma_{k}}+Z_{-\alpha}\subseteq Z_{\gamma_{k-1}}
. By induction hypothesis, we have
|\pi_{m}(Z_{\gamma_{k-1}})|=1
, and this implies the desired claim.

We shall now show that
|\pi_{m}(Z_{\alpha})|=1
for every positive root
\alpha.
We proceed by induction on ht
(\alpha)
.
If
\alpha
is long or ht
(\alpha)=1
, then there is nothing to prove.
Assume that ht
(\alpha)>1
, then we can write
\alpha=\gamma+\alpha_{k}
where
\gamma\in\mathring{\Phi}^{+}
and
\alpha_{k}
is a simple root.
If
\alpha_{k}
is short, we have
Z_{\gamma}+Z_{-\alpha}\subseteq Z_{-\alpha_{k}}
and this immediately implies that
|\pi_{m}(Z_{\alpha})|=1
.
If
\alpha_{k}
is long, then
\gamma
is short and we have
Z_{\alpha}+Z_{-\alpha_{k}}\subseteq Z_{\gamma}
. By induction hypothesis, we have
|\pi_{m}(Z_{\gamma})|=1
, hence we get
|\pi_{m}(Z_{\alpha})|=1
. We now check the condition (
2.1
)
is satisfied. Since
\Psi
is closed, the only non-trivial case is when
\Phi
is twisted and

(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2})\in Gr(\Psi)^{\times 3}
is of type
(s,s,\ell)
.
Since
Z_{\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}}+Z_{-\alpha_{1}}\subseteq Z_{\alpha_{2}}
holds, we must have
r+s\equiv 0\ (\mathrm{mod}\ m)
for all
r\in Z_{\alpha_{1}}
and
s\in Z_{\alpha_{2}}
.
This implies
Z_{\alpha_{1}}+Z_{\alpha_{2}}\subseteq Z_{\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}}
. Now
3
completes the proof.

∎

Corollary 1
.

Let
\Phi
be a real untwisted irreducible affine root system.
Let
\Psi
be a symmetric closed subset of
\Phi
such that none of the irreducible components of
Gr(\Psi)
is of type
A_{1}
. Then
\Psi
is a closed subroot system of
\Phi
.

Proof.

Since
m=1,
we get the result from
4
.
∎

Remark 2

3.3.1.

Now we assume that there is a short root

separately. First we assume that

Proposition 5

\Phi
,
\Psi

.

. Let

. If

(which is of type

\mathring{\Phi}

Since

Again since

This proves that
Z_{\alpha,0}\neq\emptyset
for

for all

.

Clearly the condition (
2.1

\Psi_{0}
. Hence we have a
\mathbb{Z}
-linear function

\Psi_{0}

\gamma\in Gr(\Psi)

Now let
Gr(\Psi)

Z_{\alpha}^{\prime}=Z_{\alpha+\gamma}^{\prime}

\alpha\in Gr(\Psi)
.
Now if
\alpha
and

(Z_{\alpha}^{\prime}+Z_{\gamma}^{\prime})\cap m\mathbb{Z}\subseteq Z_{\alpha+\gamma}^{\prime},\ \ Z_{\alpha+\gamma}^{\prime}+Z_{-\gamma}^{\prime}\subseteq Z_{\alpha}^{\prime}

\gamma

p_{-\epsilon_{i}}+a_{i}
is odd and
p_{-\epsilon_{i}}+(-a_{i})+n_{\ell}\mathbb{Z}
is the coset in
Z_{-\epsilon_{i}}
which contains odd integers, we must have
a_{i}\equiv-a_{i}(\mathrm{mod}\ n_{\ell})
. Hence
a_{i}=n_{\ell}/2
for
i=1,2.

∎

4.
Semi-closed gradient case

As before, we assume that
\Phi
is a real twisted affine root system which is not of type
A^{(2)}_{2n}
. In this section,
we consider the case when
\Psi
is a symmetric closed subset of
\Phi
with

is semi-closed in
\mathring{\Phi}.
We cannot assume that
\Psi
is irreducible as in
Section

3.3
, because we may miss the interaction between two roots coming from the different orthogonal components of (
2.3
) (see
1

4.1.
Twisted real affine root system of type
D_{n+1}^{(2)}

In this case, the real affine roots are given by

\Phi=\{\pm\epsilon_{i}\,+r\,\delta\,\mid r\in\mathbb{Z},1\leq i\leq n\}\cup\,\{\pm\epsilon_{i}\pm\epsilon_{j}\,+2r\delta\,\mid r\in\mathbb{Z},\,1\leq i\neq j\leq n\}

and
\mathring{\Phi}
is of type

. We assume that
\Psi
is a symmetric closed subset of
\Phi
such that
Gr(\Psi)
is semi-closed in
\mathring{\Phi}
. For
J\subseteq I_{n}
, we define

B_{J}:=\{\pm\epsilon_{j}:j\in J\}\cup\{\pm\epsilon_{j}\pm\epsilon_{j^{\prime}}:j\neq j^{\prime}\in J\}.

Recall that by definition, a semi-closed subset is symmetric.
First we will determine the semi-closed subsets of
\mathring{\Phi}
which occur as the gradient of some symmetric closed subset of
D_{n+1}^{(2)}
. Maximal semi-closed subroot systems of
\mathring{\Phi}
were determined in
[
21
]
. The following result generalizes
[
21
, Theorem 2(2)]
for
B_{n}
type.

Lemma 5
.

Let
\Phi
be of type
D_{n+1}^{(2)}
and
\Psi
a symmetric closed subset of
\Phi
. Suppose that
\mathring{\Psi}
is a semi-closed subset of
\mathring{\Phi}
such that
\mathring{\Psi}=Gr(\Psi)
, then there exist non-empty subsets
J_{o}
and
J_{e}
of
I_{n}
such that
J_{e}\cap J_{o}=\emptyset
and
\mathring{\Psi}\backslash(B_{J_{e}}\cup B_{J_{o}})
is a closed subroot system of long roots of
B_{n}
.

Proof.

Assume that
\mathring{\Psi}=Gr(\Psi)
for some symmetric closed subset
\Psi
of
D_{n+1}^{(2)}
. Set
Z_{p}=Z_{\epsilon_{p}}
for each
p\in I
where

I:=\{p\in I_{n}\mid\epsilon_{p}\in\mathring{\Psi}\}.

Note that
\epsilon_{p}+\epsilon_{q}\in\mathring{\Psi}
for
p,q\in I
if and only if
(Z_{p}+Z_{q})\cap 2\mathbb{Z}\neq\emptyset
.
Since
Z_{-\epsilon_{p}}=-Z_{p}
, we have
\epsilon_{p}+\epsilon_{q}\in\mathring{\Psi}
for
p,q\in I
if and only if
\epsilon_{p}-\epsilon_{q}\in\mathring{\Psi}
for
p,q\in I
.
Since
\mathring{\Psi}
is semi-closed, there exist
\epsilon_{i},\epsilon_{j}\in\mathring{\Psi}
such that
\epsilon_{i}+\epsilon_{j}\notin\mathring{\Psi}
, this implies
I\neq\emptyset
and

(Z_{i}+Z_{j})\cap 2\mathbb{Z}=\emptyset
. Without loss of generality, assume that
Z_{i}\subseteq 2\mathbb{Z}
and
Z_{j}\subseteq 2\mathbb{Z}+1
.
We claim that for any
k\in I
, we have either
Z_{k}\subseteq 2\mathbb{Z}
or
Z_{k}\subseteq 2\mathbb{Z}+1
. Suppose that for some
k\in I
,
Z_{k}
contains both even and odd elements. Then
\exists\,r,s\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
\epsilon_{k}+2r\delta,\epsilon_{k}+(2s+1)\delta\in\Psi
. Let
2q\in Z_{i}
and
2p+1\in Z_{j}
be arbitrary. Then we have

\epsilon_{k}+\epsilon_{i}+2(r+q)\delta,\epsilon_{k}-\epsilon_{j}+2(s-p)\delta\in\Psi
and

\epsilon_{k}+\epsilon_{i}+2(r+q)\delta-(\epsilon_{k}-\epsilon_{j}+2(s-p)\delta)=\epsilon_{i}+\epsilon_{j}+2(r+q+p-s)\delta\in\Psi

as
\Psi
is closed in
\Phi
. This implies

\epsilon_{i}+\epsilon_{j}\in\mathring{\Psi}
, a contradiction. Hence the claim.

Now define
J_{e}:=\{p\in I\,\mid Z_{p}\subseteq 2\mathbb{Z}\}
and
J_{o}:=I\backslash J_{e}
.
We claim that
(s,t),(t,s)\in J_{e}\times J_{o}
implies that
\pm\epsilon_{s}\pm\epsilon_{t}\notin\mathring{\Psi}
.
Suppose that
\pm\epsilon_{s}\pm\epsilon_{t}+2k\delta\in\Psi
with
(s,t)\in J_{e}\times J_{o}
. Then we have

\pm\epsilon_{t}+2(k+r^{\mp})\delta\,=(\pm\epsilon_{s}\pm\epsilon_{t}+2k\delta)+(\mp\epsilon_{s}+2r^{\mp}\delta)\in\Psi,

where
r^{\mp}\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
\mp\epsilon_{s}+2r^{\mp}\delta\in\Psi
. This is a contradiction since
t\in J_{o}
. By symmetry we get
(t,s)\in J_{e}\times J_{o}
implies that
\pm\epsilon_{s}\pm\epsilon_{t}\notin\mathring{\Psi}
.
Note that
\pm\epsilon_{i}\pm\epsilon_{j}\in\mathring{\Psi}
for all
i,j\in J_{e}\
(or
J_{o}
). Hence
\{\pm\epsilon_{i}:i\in I\}
generates a root system of type
B_{J_{e}}\sqcup B_{J_{o}}
in
\mathring{\Psi}
. If
\mathring{\Psi}=B_{J_{e}}\sqcup B_{J_{o}}
, then there is nothing to prove. So assume that

\mathring{\Psi}\neq B_{J_{e}}\sqcup B_{J_{o}}
. It is clear that
\mathring{\Psi}\backslash(B_{J_{e}}\sqcup B_{J_{o}})
is a symmetric closed subset of
(B_{n})_{\ell}
since

\mathring{\Psi}
is semi-closed in
\mathring{\Phi}.
Hence it is a closed subroot system of
(B_{n})_{\ell}
by
1
.
∎

4.2.
Twisted real affine root system of type
A_{2n-1}^{(2)}

In this case, the real affine roots are given by

\Phi=\{\pm 2\epsilon_{i}+2r\delta\,\mid r\in\mathbb{Z},\,1\leq i\leq n\}\cup\{\pm\epsilon_{i}\pm\epsilon_{j}+r\delta,\mid r\in\mathbb{Z},\,1\leq i\neq j\leq n\}.

The next proposition is the main result of this subsection.

Proposition 8
.

Let
\Psi
be a symmetric closed subset of
\Phi
such that
Gr(\Psi)
is semi-closed in
\mathring{\Phi}
.
Let
\Psi=\Psi_{1}\sqcup\cdots\sqcup\Psi_{r}
be the decomposition of
\Psi
into irreducible components.
Assume that
Gr(\Psi_{i})
is not of type
A_{1}
or
B_{2}
for each
1\leq i\leq r.

Then we have
\Psi
is a closed subroot system.

Proof.

Define
I:=\{i\in I_{n}\mid 2\epsilon_{i}\in Gr(\Psi)\}
. Since
Gr(\Psi)
is semi-closed, there exist
\epsilon_{s}-\epsilon_{t},\epsilon_{s}+\epsilon_{t}\in Gr(\Psi)
but
2\epsilon_{s}\notin Gr(\Psi)
. Hence
I\neq I_{n}
. Let

\Psi^{\prime}:=\left(\bigcup_{i\in I}(\pm 2\epsilon_{i}+2\mathbb{Z}\delta)\cup\bigcup_{i\neq j\in I}(\pm\epsilon_{i}\pm\epsilon_{j}+\mathbb{Z}\delta)\right)\cap\Psi,\ \ \Psi^{\prime\prime}:=\Psi\backslash\Psi^{\prime}.

If
\epsilon_{k}\pm\epsilon_{\ell}\in Gr(\Psi)
for some
k\in I
, but
\ell\in I_{n}\backslash I
. Then we have

2\epsilon_{k}+2p\delta,\epsilon_{k}\pm\epsilon_{\ell}+r\delta\in\Psi
for some
p,r\in\mathbb{Z}.
This implies that

\epsilon_{k}\mp\epsilon_{\ell}+(2p-r)\delta=\,(2\epsilon_{k}+2p\delta)-(\epsilon_{k}\pm\epsilon_{\ell}+r\delta)\in\Psi,

and hence
\mp 2\epsilon_{\ell}+2(p-r)\delta=(\epsilon_{k}\mp\epsilon_{\ell}+(2p-r))-(\epsilon_{k}\pm\epsilon_{\ell}+r\delta)\in\Psi
, which is a contradiction as
\ell\notin I
. So if
\epsilon_{i}\pm\epsilon_{j}\in Gr(\Psi)
, then either both
i,j\in I
or both
i,j\notin I.
In particular, we have

\Psi^{\prime\prime}=\left(\bigcup_{i\neq j\in I_{n}\backslash I}(\pm\epsilon_{i}\pm\epsilon_{j}+\mathbb{Z}\delta)\right)\cap\Psi

is a closed subroot system of
\Phi
,
and
\Psi=\Psi^{\prime}\sqcup\Psi^{\prime\prime}
is an orthogonal decomposition.

It is easy to see that
\Psi^{\prime}
is a symmetric closed subset of
\Phi
with
Gr(\Psi^{\prime})
is a symmetric closed subset of
\mathring{\Phi}.

Then by
5
,
\Psi^{\prime}
is also a closed subroot systems of
\Phi
, and hence
\Psi
is a closed subroot system of
\Phi
.
∎

is not of type

or

cannot be dropped from

.

4.3.
Twisted real affine root system of type
D_{4}^{(3)}

In this case, the real roots are given by

\Phi=\{\pm\,(\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{j})+r\delta,\,\pm\,(2\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{j}-\epsilon_{k})+3r\delta\,\mid i,j,k\in I_{3},i\neq j,\,r\in\mathbb{Z}\}

Note that
\mathring{\Phi}
is of type
G_{2}
and both
\mathring{\Phi}_{s}
and
\mathring{\Phi}_{\ell}
are of type
A_{2}.
The next lemma is a generalization of
[
21
, Theorem 2]
and
[
22
, Lemma 7.1.1]
.

Lemma 6
.

Let
\Psi
be a symmetric closed subset of
\Phi
with
Gr(\Psi)
semi-closed. Then
Gr(\Psi)
is
\mathring{\Phi}_{s}
.

Proof.

Since
Gr(\Psi)
is semi-closed, there exist short roots
\alpha,\beta\in Gr(\Psi)
such that
\alpha+\beta\in\mathring{\Phi}_{\ell}
and
\alpha+\beta\notin Gr(\Psi)
.
Now since
\alpha+\beta
is long in type
G_{2}
,
\alpha-\beta
must be short and hence
\alpha-\beta\in Gr(\Psi)
. Since
\alpha-\beta\not=\alpha,\beta
and
Gr(\Psi)
is symmetric, we have
\mathring{\Phi}_{s}\subseteq Gr(\Psi)
.
Note that
Gr(\Psi)
can not contain two long positive roots as
Gr(\Psi)\subsetneq\mathring{\Phi}
. We claim that
Gr(\Psi)
can not contain any long root. On the contrary assume that
Gr(\Psi)
contains a long root, say
2\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{j}-\epsilon_{k}\in Gr(\Psi)
. Let
S=\pi_{m}(Z_{\epsilon_{j}-\epsilon_{k}}).
It is clear that
|S|\neq 3.
Suppose that
|S|=2
. Let
\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{j}+p_{1}\delta,\epsilon_{j}-\epsilon_{k}+p_{2}\delta,\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{k}+p_{3}\delta\in\Psi
. Since
Gr(\Psi)=\mathring{\Phi}_{s}\cup\{\pm(2\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{j}-\epsilon_{k})\}
we have

(4.1)

p_{1}\not\equiv p_{2}\ (\mathrm{mod}\ 3),\ \ p_{3}\not\equiv-p_{2}\ (\mathrm{mod}\ 3)

Let
\ell
be the element which is not in
S
. Then
p_{1}\equiv\ell(\mathrm{mod}\ 3)
and
p_{3}\equiv-\ell(\mathrm{mod}\ 3)
which gives
|\pi_{m}(Z_{\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{k}})|=1,
but this is impossible since we have
Z_{\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{j}}+Z_{\epsilon_{j}-\epsilon_{k}}\subseteq Z_{\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{k}}.

Now assume that
|S|=1
and let
S=\{\ell^{\prime}\}
. In this case, we have one more relation along with (
4.1
) namely

(4.2)

p_{3}-p_{1}\equiv\ell^{\prime}\ (\mathrm{mod}\ 3)

which implies that
|\pi_{m}(Z_{\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{j}})|=1
and
|\pi_{m}(Z_{\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{k}})|=1.
Suppose that
\pi_{m}(Z_{\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{j}})=\{r\}
. Then we have
\pi_{m}(Z_{\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{k}})=\{r+\ell^{\prime}\}
. Since
\Psi
is closed, for any
2\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{j}-\epsilon_{k}+3a\delta\in\Psi,
we have

\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{k}+(3a-p_{1})\delta\,=\,(2\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{j}-\epsilon_{k}+3a\delta)+(\epsilon_{j}-\epsilon_{i}-p_{1}\delta)\in\Psi.

This implies
3a-p_{1}\equiv r+\ell^{\prime}\ (\mathrm{mod}\ 3),
which in turn implies that
\ell^{\prime}\equiv-2r\equiv r\ (\mathrm{mod}\ 3).
This contradicts
Equation

4.1
. So
|S|\neq 1
and hence the result.
∎

semi-closed in
\mathring{\Phi}
. Then
\Psi

\Psi

\Phi
.

By
6
, it follows that

. Note that we can consider
\Psi
as an irreducible symmetric closed subset of the untwisted root system of type

. By

, there is a
\mathbb{Z}
-linear function

In particular,

is a closed subroot system of

Next we determine

explicitly.

\beta=\frac{1}{2}\,(\lambda_{s_{1}}\epsilon_{s_{1}}+\lambda_{s_{2}}\epsilon_{s_{2}}+\lambda_{s_{3}}\epsilon_{s_{3}}+\lambda_{s_{4}}\epsilon_{s_{4}})

\gamma=\frac{1}{2}\,(\lambda_{s_{1}}\epsilon_{s_{1}}+\lambda_{s_{2}}\epsilon_{s_{2}}-\lambda_{s_{3}}\epsilon_{s_{3}}-\lambda_{s_{4}}\epsilon_{s_{4}})
. Note that we have
(Z_{\beta}+Z_{\gamma})\cap 2\mathbb{Z}=\emptyset
, so we have
\beta-\gamma\notin Gr(\Psi)
and
|\pi_{m}(Z_{\beta})|=1
.
First we assume that
I\neq\emptyset
. We claim that
\epsilon_{s_{1}}\,(
resp.
\epsilon_{s_{3}})\in Gr(\Psi)
if and only if
\epsilon_{s_{2}}\,(
resp.
\epsilon_{s_{4}})\in Gr(\Psi)
.
Suppose

, then we have
\gamma^{\prime}=\gamma-\lambda_{s_{1}}\epsilon_{s_{1}}\in Gr(\Psi).
This implies that
\lambda_{s_{2}}\epsilon_{s_{2}}=\gamma^{\prime}+\beta\in Gr(\Psi)
and the case

s_{3}\in I
is done similarly.
This argument also shows that for any
i\in I
and a special short root

\gamma\in Gr(\Psi)
, we can always change the sign of the co-efficient of
\epsilon_{i}
in
\gamma
and that resulted element is again in
Gr(\Psi).

It is easy to see that we always have
D_{\beta}(\Psi,I)\subseteq Gr(\Psi)\cap\mathring{\Phi}_{s}.

We claim that
Gr(\Psi)\cap\mathring{\Phi}_{s}=D_{\beta}(\Psi,I).
Suppose
|I|=4,
then it is clear as
D_{\beta}(\Psi,I)=\mathring{\Phi}_{s}
. So assume that
|I|=2.

If possible let
Gr(\Psi)\cap\mathring{\Phi}_{s}\backslash D_{\beta}(\Psi,I)\neq\emptyset
, then
there exists
\mu\in Gr(\Psi)\cap\mathring{\Phi}_{s}\backslash D_{\beta}(\Psi,I)
and unique

j\notin I
such that the co-efficient of
\epsilon_{j}
in both
\mu
and
\beta
is different.
But this implies
\beta-\mu=\pm\epsilon_{j}\in Gr(\Psi)\cap\mathring{\Phi}_{s}
, which is a contradiction.
Thus we get

Gr(\Psi)\cap\mathring{\Phi}_{s}=D_{\beta}(\Psi,I).

Also since sum of a long root and a short root is short, it follows that
\epsilon_{s}\pm\epsilon_{t}\in Gr(\Psi)
only if
\{s,t\}\subseteq I
or
\{s,t\}\subseteq I_{4}\backslash I
. Note that every special short root
\mu\in D_{\beta}(\Psi,I)
can be written as
\mu=\beta+\sum\pm\epsilon_{i}
with
\epsilon_{i}\in Gr(\Psi)
such that each partial sum is a root. Since all the roots appearing are short and
|\pi_{m}(Z_{\beta})|=1,
it follows that
|\pi_{m}(Z_{\mu})|=1
for each
\mu\in D_{\beta}(\Psi,I).

So we have
|\pi_{m}(Z_{\alpha})|=1
for all
\alpha\in Gr(\Psi).

For
|I|=2,
we show that no long root can occur in
Gr(\Psi)
. We shall show the case for
I=\{s_{1},s_{2}\}
and the remaining cases are similar. Since
\lambda_{s_{1}}\epsilon_{s_{1}}+\lambda_{s_{2}}\epsilon_{s_{2}}\notin Gr(\Psi)
, it follows that
(Z_{\epsilon_{s_{1}}}+Z_{\epsilon_{s_{2}}})\cap 2\mathbb{Z}=\emptyset
. Hence
\lambda_{s_{1}}\epsilon_{s_{1}}-\lambda_{s_{2}}\epsilon_{s_{2}}\notin Gr(\Psi)
as well. Recall that
\epsilon_{s}\pm\epsilon_{t}\in Gr(\Psi)
only if
\{s,t\}\subseteq I
or
\{s,t\}\subseteq I_{4}\backslash I
. So we only can have
\lambda_{s_{3}}\epsilon_{s_{3}}\pm\lambda_{s_{4}}\epsilon_{s_{4}}\in Gr(\Psi)
.
Since
Gr(\Psi)
has no component of type
A_{1}
and
\lambda_{s_{3}}\epsilon_{s_{3}}-\lambda_{s_{4}}\epsilon_{s_{4}}
is orthogonal to
D_{\beta}(\Psi,I)
and

\lambda_{s_{3}}\epsilon_{s_{3}}+\lambda_{s_{4}}\epsilon_{s_{4}}
, we have

\lambda_{s_{3}}\epsilon_{s_{3}}-\lambda_{s_{4}}\epsilon_{s_{4}}\notin Gr(\Psi).

Suppose
\lambda_{s_{3}}\epsilon_{s_{3}}+\lambda_{s_{4}}\epsilon_{s_{4}}\in Gr(\Psi)
. We have
(Z_{\beta}+Z_{\gamma})\cap 2\mathbb{Z}\not=\emptyset

since
\beta=\gamma+\lambda_{s_{3}}\epsilon_{s_{3}}+\lambda_{s_{4}}\epsilon_{s_{4}}\in Gr(\Psi)
, which in turn implies that
\beta+\gamma\in Gr(\Psi)
, a contradiction.
Hence no long root can occur in
Gr(\Psi)
if
|I|=2.

In this case we have
Gr(\Psi)=D_{\beta}(\Psi,I)
which is irreducible.
When
|I|=4
we have
D_{\beta}(\Psi,I)=\mathring{\Phi}_{s}\subseteq\Psi
. So we must have
Gr(\Psi)
irreducible in this case.
Now using
3
, we get a
\mathbb{Z}
-linear function
p
and
n\in 2\mathbb{Z}_{+}
such that

\Psi=\{\alpha+(p_{\alpha}+n\mathbb{Z})\delta:\alpha\in Gr(\Psi)\}.
This completes the proof for
I\neq\emptyset
.
Moreover when
I=I_{4}
we have the following restrictions:

|\pi_{m}(Z_{\epsilon_{s_{i}}})|=1,\ \ \forall i\in I_{4},\ \ (Z_{\epsilon_{s_{1}}}+Z_{\epsilon_{s_{2}}})\cap 2\mathbb{Z}=\emptyset,\ \ (Z_{\epsilon_{s_{3}}}+Z_{\epsilon_{s_{4}}})\cap 2\mathbb{Z}=\emptyset.

Set
J_{1}:=\{s_{i}\in I_{4}:Z_{\epsilon_{s_{i}}}\subseteq 2\mathbb{Z}\}
and
J_{2}:=\{s_{i}\in I_{4}:Z_{\epsilon_{s_{i}}}\subseteq 2\mathbb{Z}+1\}.
From the relations above, we have
|J_{1}|=|J_{2}|=2.
Then there exists a
\mathbb{Z}
–linear function
p:Gr(\Psi)\to\mathbb{Z}
such that
p_{\epsilon_{s_{i}}}\in 2\mathbb{Z}
(resp. in
2\mathbb{Z}+1
) if
s_{i}\in J_{1}
(resp. in
J_{2}
) and
\Psi
is of the form

\Psi:=\bigcup_{\alpha\in\mathring{\Phi}_{s}}\{\alpha+(p_{\alpha}+n\mathbb{Z})\delta\}\cup\bigcup_{J\in\{J_{1},J_{2}\}}\{\pm(\epsilon_{i}\pm\epsilon_{j})+(p_{\pm(\epsilon_{i}\pm\epsilon_{j})}+n\mathbb{Z})\delta:i\neq j\in J\}.

Assume that
I=\emptyset
. It is clear that the only short roots can appear in
Gr(\Psi)
are
\alpha_{1}:=\beta,\alpha_{2}:=\gamma,\alpha_{3}=\frac{1}{2}\,(\lambda_{s_{1}}\epsilon_{s_{1}}-\lambda_{s_{2}}\epsilon_{s_{2}}+\lambda_{s_{3}}\epsilon_{s_{3}}-\lambda_{s_{4}}\epsilon_{s_{4}}),\alpha_{4}=\frac{1}{2}\,(-\lambda_{s_{1}}\epsilon_{s_{1}}+\lambda_{s_{2}}\epsilon_{s_{2}}+\lambda_{s_{3}}\epsilon_{s_{3}}-\lambda_{s_{4}}\epsilon_{s_{4}})
and their negatives (see
[
22
, Proposition 8.1.3]
). We claim that all of these roots occur in
Gr(\Psi)
. As
(\alpha_{i},\alpha_{j})=0,\ 1\leq i\neq j\leq 4,

Gr(\Psi)\not=\{\pm\alpha_{i}\mid i=1,2\}.
Hence there is a long root in
Gr(\Psi)
. Since
\alpha_{1}\pm\alpha_{2}\notin Gr(\Psi)
, there is a long root in Gr
(\Psi)
of the form
\lambda_{s_{i}}\epsilon_{s_{i}}\pm\lambda_{s_{j}}\epsilon_{s_{j}}
with
i\in\{1,2\}
and
j\in\{3,4\}
. We shall prove the claim for one case, other cases are similar. Let
\lambda_{s_{1}}\epsilon_{s_{1}}+\lambda_{s_{3}}\epsilon_{s_{3}}\in Gr(\Psi)
. Then
-\alpha_{3}=\alpha_{1}-(\lambda_{s_{1}}\epsilon_{s_{1}}+\lambda_{s_{3}}\epsilon_{s_{3}})\in Gr(\Psi)
. Furthermore since
Z_{\alpha_{1}}
contains elements of same parity, it follows that
(Z_{\alpha_{1}}+Z_{\alpha_{3}})\cap(2\mathbb{Z}+1)=\emptyset.
Hence
\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{3}=\lambda_{s_{2}}\epsilon_{s_{2}}+\lambda_{s_{4}}\epsilon_{s_{4}}\in Gr(\Psi)
. Since
Gr(\Psi)
has no irreducible component of type
A_{1}
and
\alpha_{2}
is orthogonal to
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{3},\lambda_{s_{1}}\epsilon_{s_{1}}+\lambda_{s_{3}}\epsilon_{s_{3}}
, it follows that
\lambda_{s_{2}}\epsilon_{s_{2}}-\lambda_{s_{4}}\epsilon_{s_{4}}\in Gr(\Psi)
. Consequently
-\alpha_{4}=\alpha_{2}-(\lambda_{s_{2}}\epsilon_{s_{2}}-\lambda_{s_{4}}\epsilon_{s_{4}})\in Gr(\Psi)
. By a similar argument we get that
\lambda_{s_{1}}\epsilon_{s_{1}}-\lambda_{s_{3}}\epsilon_{s_{3}}\in Gr(\Psi)
.
Hence
|\pi_{m}(Z_{\gamma})|=1
for all
\gamma\in Gr(\Psi).
Moreover there exist a partition
J_{0}\sqcup J_{1}
of
I_{4}
with

such that

where
\mathring{\Psi}_{i}=\{\pm\alpha_{k},\pm\alpha_{\ell},\pm(\alpha_{k}\pm\alpha_{\ell}):J_{i}=\{k,\ell\}\}.
We also have
\alpha_{k}\pm\alpha_{\ell}\notin Gr(\Psi)
if

but
\ell\notin J_{i}.
We can choose
\mathring{\Psi}_{0}
to be the component so that

for all

In each component, all the hypothesis of

is satisfied. Hence
\Psi
is of the required form
with exactly two of

’s being even.

∎

5.
Twisted real affine root system with non-reduced gradient

In this section, we shall consider the twisted affine root system of type
A_{2n}^{(2)}.
The real affine roots are

\Phi=\left\{\pm\epsilon_{i}+(r+1/2)\delta,\,\pm 2\epsilon_{i}+2r\delta,\,\pm\epsilon_{i}\pm\epsilon_{j}+r\delta\mid 1\leq i\neq j\leq n,r\in\mathbb{Z}\right\}.

The gradient root system is an irreducible reduced root system of type
BC_{n}
. We set

\mathring{\Phi}_{d}:=\{\pm 2\epsilon_{i}\mid 1\leq i\leq n\},\ \mathring{\Phi}_{s}:=\{\pm\epsilon_{i}\mid 1\leq i\leq n\},\ \mathring{\Phi}_{\ell}:=\{\pm\epsilon_{i}\pm\epsilon_{j}\mid 1\leq i\neq j\leq n\}

and

\widehat{C_{n}}=\left\{\pm 2\epsilon_{i}+2r\delta,\,\pm\epsilon_{i}\pm\epsilon_{j}+r\delta\mid 1\leq i\neq j\leq n,r\in\mathbb{Z}\right\}\subseteq\Phi.

Note that
\widehat{C_{n}}
is a closed subroot system of type
A_{2n-1}^{(2)}
. Let
\Psi\subseteq\widehat{C_{n}}
, then we have
\Psi
is a symmetric closed in
\widehat{C_{n}}
if and only if

\Psi
is a symmetric closed in
\Phi.
We will fix some notations before proceeding further.
For any
I\subseteq I_{n}
, set

B_{I}=\{\pm\epsilon_{i},\pm\epsilon_{i}\pm\epsilon_{j}\mid i\neq j\in I\},\,\,C_{I}=\{\pm 2\epsilon_{i},\pm\epsilon_{i}\pm\epsilon_{j}\mid i\neq j\in I\}

and

BC_{I}=\{\pm\epsilon_{i},\pm 2\epsilon_{i},\pm\epsilon_{i}\pm\epsilon_{j}\mid i\neq j\in I\}
.

5.1.

Let
\Psi
be a symmetric closed subset of
\Phi
. The next lemma shows that it is enough to classify irreducible symmetric, closed subsets of
\Phi
which contains at least one short root.

Lemma 7
.

Let
\Psi
be a symmetric closed subset of
\Phi
and
Equation

2.2
be the decomposition of
\Psi
into irreducible components. Then there exists at most one
\Psi_{i},1\leq i\leq r
such that
Gr(\Psi_{i})\cap\mathring{\Phi}_{s}\neq\emptyset.

Conversely, suppose that
\Psi^{\prime}
is an irreducible symmetric, closed subset of
\Phi
such that
Gr(\Psi^{\prime})\cap\mathring{\Phi}_{s}\neq\emptyset
and
\Psi^{\prime\prime}
is a symmetric, closed subset of
\widehat{C_{n}}
such that
(\Psi^{\prime},\Psi^{\prime\prime})=0.
Then
\Psi^{\prime}\cup\Psi^{\prime\prime}
is a symmetric, closed subset of
\Phi.

Proof.

If possible, assume that there exists
i,j
with
1\leq i\neq j\leq r
such that
\epsilon_{k}\in Gr(\Psi_{i}),\epsilon_{\ell}\in Gr(\Psi_{j}).
Then we have
\epsilon_{k}+q\delta\in\Psi,\epsilon_{\ell}+s\delta\in\Psi
for some
q,s\in\mathbb{Z}+1/2.
Since
\Psi
is closed, we have
\epsilon_{k}+\epsilon_{\ell}+(q+s)\delta\in\Psi.
But the root
\epsilon_{k}+\epsilon_{\ell}+(q+s)\delta
is non-orthogonal to both
\Psi_{i}
and
\Psi_{j}
, which is impossible. For the converse part, it is easy to see that
\Psi^{\prime}\cup\Psi^{\prime\prime}
is a symmetric subset of
\Phi.
Now we claim if
\alpha\in\Psi^{\prime}
and
\beta\in\Psi^{\prime\prime}
then
\alpha+\beta\notin\Phi.
This clearly implies
\Psi^{\prime}\cup\Psi^{\prime\prime}
is closed in
\Phi
as both
\Psi^{\prime}
,
\Psi^{\prime\prime}
are closed in
\Phi.

Set

I^{\prime}=\{i\in I_{n}\mid\epsilon_{i}\in Gr(\Psi^{\prime})\}.

Since
\Psi
is closed, we have
\epsilon_{i}\pm\epsilon_{j}\in Gr(\Psi^{\prime})
for all
i\neq j\in I^{\prime}
and
\epsilon_{i}\pm\epsilon_{j}\notin Gr(\Psi^{\prime})
for all
i\in I^{\prime},j\in I_{n}\backslash I^{\prime}.
Moreover, since
\Psi^{\prime}
is irreducible, we have
B_{I^{\prime}}\subseteq Gr(\Psi^{\prime})\subseteq BC_{I^{\prime}}.

Also we have that
Gr(\Psi^{\prime\prime})\subseteq\mathring{\Phi}_{\ell}\cup\mathring{\Phi}_{d}
and it is orthogonal to
Gr(\Psi^{\prime}).
So it follows that

Gr(\Psi^{\prime\prime})\subseteq C_{I_{n}\backslash I^{\prime}}.
Hence if
\alpha\in Gr(\Psi^{\prime}),\beta\in Gr(\Psi^{\prime\prime}),
then
\alpha+\beta\notin\mathring{\Phi}
. This gives our claim and completes the proof.

∎

5.2.

Now onwards, we assume that
\Psi
is an irreducible symmetric, closed subset of
\Phi
such that
Gr(\Psi)\cap\mathring{\Phi}_{s}\neq\emptyset.
Let

I:=\{i\in I_{n}\mid\epsilon_{i}\in
Gr
(\Psi)\}
, and we have
I\neq\emptyset.

Let
q_{i}\in Z_{\epsilon_{i}}
for each

and set
\tilde{Z}_{\epsilon_{i}}:=Z_{\epsilon_{i}}-q_{i}
. Then we have
\tilde{Z}_{\epsilon_{i}}\subseteq\mathbb{Z}
for
i\in I.

The next two lemmas are important and we give proof for the first one and skip the details of the second one as it is similar to the first one. Recall that
m=2.

Lemma 8
.

Let
\Psi
be an irreducible symmetric closed subset of
\Phi
and
I
be defined as above. Suppose that
|\pi_{m}(\tilde{Z}_{\epsilon_{i}})|=1
for some
i\in I.
Then

(1)

|\pi_{m}(\tilde{Z}_{\epsilon_{j}})|=1
for all
j\in I
.

(2)

Gr(\Psi)=B_{I}
and
|\pi_{m}(Z_{\epsilon_{i}\pm\epsilon_{j}})|=1.

Proof.

Let
j\in I
such that
j\neq i
. Since
\Psi
is closed, we have
\pm(\epsilon_{i}\pm\epsilon_{j})\in Gr(\Psi)
and
Z_{\epsilon_{i}\pm\epsilon_{j}}+Z_{\mp\epsilon_{j}}=Z_{\epsilon_{i}}.
Hence we have
\tilde{Z}_{\epsilon_{i}\pm\epsilon_{j}}+\tilde{Z}_{\mp\epsilon_{j}}=\tilde{Z}_{\epsilon_{i}}
,
\tilde{Z}_{\epsilon_{i}\pm\epsilon_{j}}\subseteq\tilde{Z}_{\epsilon_{i}}
and
\tilde{Z}_{\mp\epsilon_{j}}\subseteq\tilde{Z}_{\epsilon_{i}}
. This implies
(1)
and second part of
(2)
. Since
\Psi
is closed, we also get
\epsilon_{r}\pm\epsilon_{s}\notin Gr(\Psi)
if
r\in I
and
s\notin I.
As
Gr(\Psi)
is irreducible, we must have

B_{I}\subseteq Gr(\Psi^{\prime})\subseteq BC_{I}
. If possible assume that
2\epsilon_{i}\in Gr(\Psi)
for some
i\in I.
Since
\Psi
is closed, we have
Z_{2\epsilon_{i}}+Z_{-\epsilon_{i}}\subseteq Z_{\epsilon_{i}}.

For
s\in\tilde{Z_{i}}
and
2k\in Z_{2\epsilon_{i}},
we have
2k-s-q_{i}=2k-2q_{i}-s+q_{i}\in Z_{\epsilon_{i}}.
This implies both
s
and
2k-2q_{i}-s\in\tilde{Z}_{\epsilon_{j}}

which is a contradiction as they have different parities as

. Thus we must have
Gr(\Psi)=\{\pm\epsilon_{i},\pm(\epsilon_{i}\pm\epsilon_{j}):i\neq j\in I\}
.
∎

Lemma 9
.

Let
\Psi
be an irreducible symmetric closed subset of
\Phi
and
I
be defined as above. Suppose that

.

(2)

∎

5.3.

Generalizing

We first consider the situation that appears in

.

Let
\Psi

5.4.

For
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+},I\subseteq I_{n}

.

Let

.

, we have
Gr(\Psi)=BC_{I}.

∎

\Phi,
and
define a map

\iota_{\mathfrak{g}}:\mathcal{C}_{\text{sym}}(\mathfrak{g})\to\mathcal{R}(\mathfrak{g})

by
\iota_{\mathfrak{g}}(\Psi)=\mathfrak{g}(\Psi)
.
The following result is well-known in the finite setting (see, for example
[
12
, Proposition 4.1]
):

Proposition 13
.

Let
(\mathring{\mathfrak{g}},\mathring{\mathfrak{h}})
be the pair of finite-dimensional simple Lie algebra and its Cartan subalgebra corresponding to the root system
\mathring{\Phi}
. Then
the map
\mathring{\Psi}\mapsto\mathfrak{g}(\mathring{\Psi})
from the set of closed subsets of
\mathring{\Phi}
to
the set of
\mathring{\mathfrak{h}}
-invariant subalgebras of
\mathring{\mathfrak{g}}
is bijective. ∎

It is easy to see that unlike in the finite case, the map
\iota_{\mathfrak{g}}
is not injective for any
\mathfrak{g}
, even if we restrict it to
symmetric closed subsets of
\Phi.

Example 1
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be any affine Lie algebra not of type
A_{2n}^{(2)}
and
\alpha
be a short root in
\mathring{\Phi}.
Consider two symmetric, real closed subsets
\Psi_{1},\Psi_{2}
of
\Phi
defined by

\Psi_{1}:=\{\alpha+\delta,-\alpha+\delta,-\alpha-\delta,\alpha-\delta\},\ \ \Psi_{2}:=\{\alpha+3\delta,\alpha+\delta,-\alpha-3\delta,-\alpha-\delta\}.

The following relations show that
\pm 2\delta\in\Delta(\Psi_{i}),\ i=1,2:

(6.1)

\displaystyle\mathbb{C}\alpha^{\vee}\otimes t^{\pm 2}

\displaystyle=[\mathfrak{g}_{\pm\alpha}\otimes t^{\pm 1},\mathfrak{g}_{\mp\alpha}\otimes t^{\pm 1}]\subseteq\mathfrak{g}(\Psi_{1}),

\displaystyle\mathbb{C}\alpha^{\vee}\otimes t^{\pm 2}

\displaystyle=[\mathfrak{g}_{\pm\alpha}\otimes t^{\pm 3},\mathfrak{g}_{\mp\alpha}\otimes t^{\mp 1}]\subseteq\mathfrak{g}(\Psi_{2}).

Since
[\alpha^{\vee}\otimes t^{r},\mathfrak{g}_{\pm\alpha}\otimes t^{s}]\neq 0
for any
r,s\in\mathbb{Z}
, it follows that
\mathfrak{g}_{\beta\pm 2\delta}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}(\Psi_{i})
for all
\beta\in\Psi_{i},\ i=1,2.
Again using the commutation relations as in
Equation

6.1
we get
\mathbb{C}\alpha^{\vee}\otimes t^{\pm 4}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}(\Psi_{i}),\ i=1,2.
Proceeding in this way we get that
\mathfrak{g}_{\beta\pm 2\mathbb{Z}\delta}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}(\Psi_{i})
for all
\beta\in\Psi_{i},\ i=1,2.
Hence we have

\mathfrak{g}(\Psi_{1})=\bigoplus_{r\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathfrak{g}_{\pm\alpha+(2r+1)\delta}\oplus\bigoplus_{r\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{C}\alpha^{\vee}\otimes t^{2r},\ \ \mathfrak{g}(\Psi_{2})=\bigoplus_{r\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathfrak{g}_{\pm\alpha+(2r+3)\delta}\oplus\bigoplus_{r\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{C}\alpha^{\vee}\otimes t^{2r}

So
\Psi_{1}\neq\Psi_{2}
but
\iota_{\mathfrak{g}}(\Psi_{1})=\iota_{\mathfrak{g}}(\Psi_{2}).

Remark 7
.

It is possible to get similar examples using
Equation

3.4
when
Gr(\Psi)
is of type
B_{2}
, e.g.
\Psi_{1}
be given by
Equation

3.4
and
\Psi_{2}
be defined by switching the role of
a_{1}
and
a_{2}.
We will leave to the details, one can prove that
\Psi_{1}\neq\Psi_{2}
, but
\iota_{\mathfrak{g}}(\Psi_{1})=\iota_{\mathfrak{g}}(\Psi_{2}).

6.1.

The previous discussion motivates us to look for the best possible subclass of
\mathcal{C}_{\text{sym}}(\mathfrak{g})
for which the map
\iota_{\mathfrak{g}}
restricted to that class
is injective. Recall a result from
[
22
, Corollary 11.1.5]
, the map
\Psi\mapsto\mathfrak{g}(\Psi)
is injective if we restrict to the
set of closed subroot systems of
\Phi
. That means the map
\iota_{\mathfrak{g}}
restricted to all closed subroot systems of
\Phi
is injective.
We indeed prove that this is only the best possible subclass of symmetric closed subsets of
\Phi
for which we can have injective:

Proposition 14
.

Suppose
\mathcal{S}
is a subclass of the set of symmetric closed subsets of
\Phi
such that the restriction of
\iota_{\mathfrak{g}}
is injective, then
\mathcal{S}
must be the set of all closed subroot systems of
\Phi
.

Proof.

Let
\Psi
be a symmetric closed subset of
\Phi,
which is not a subroot system of
\Phi.
Then by
2
, we know that

\Psi^{\prime}=\Delta(\Psi)\cap\Phi
is the minimal closed subroot system of
\Phi
that contains
\Psi.
It is clear that
\Psi\neq\Psi^{\prime}
, but

\mathfrak{g}(\Psi)=\mathfrak{g}(\Psi^{\prime}),
i.e.,

\iota_{\mathfrak{g}}(\Psi)=\iota_{\mathfrak{g}}(\Psi^{\prime}).
So the largest subclass
\mathcal{S}
of symmetric closed subsets of
\Phi
for which the restriction

\iota|_{\mathcal{S}}
is injective is the set of all closed subroot systems of
\Phi
.
∎

Corollary 2
.

Let
\Psi
be a symmetric closed subset of
\Phi.
Then
\Delta(\mathfrak{g}(\Psi))\cap\Phi=\Psi
if and only if
\Psi
is a closed subroot system of
\Phi.

6.2.

Fix
\Psi\in\mathcal{C}_{\text{sym}}(\mathfrak{g})
, now we will determine the preimage
\iota^{-1}_{\mathfrak{g}}(\mathfrak{g}(\Psi))
using our results, i.e., we will determine all possible

\Psi^{\prime}\in\mathcal{C}_{\text{sym}}(\mathfrak{g})
such that
\mathfrak{g}(\Psi^{\prime})=\mathfrak{g}(\Psi).
Note that
\Delta(\Psi)\cap\Phi
is the unique real closed subroot system in
\iota_{\mathfrak{g}}^{-1}(\mathfrak{g}(\Psi))
, so it is enough to determine
\iota_{\mathfrak{g}}^{-1}(\mathfrak{g}(\Psi))
when
\Psi
is a real closed subroot system. If
\Psi^{\prime}\in\iota_{\mathfrak{g}}^{-1}(\mathfrak{g}(\Psi)),
then from the definition it is clear that
Gr(\Psi)=Gr(\Psi^{\prime})
and by
2
we have

\iota_{\mathfrak{g}}^{-1}(\mathfrak{g}(\Psi))=\{\Psi^{\prime}\in\mathcal{C}_{\text{sym}}(\mathfrak{g}):\Delta(\Psi^{\prime})\cap\Phi=\Psi\}.

To determine the preimage we need the following example.

Example 2
.

Let
\Psi
be a symmetric closed subset of
\Phi
such that
Gr(\Psi)
is of type
B_{2}.
Then by
6
there is a
\mathbb{Z}
-linear function
p:Gr(\Psi)\to\mathbb{Z},n_{\ell}\in 2\mathbb{Z}_{+}
and odd integers
1\leq a_{1},a_{2}\leq n_{\ell}
with
a_{1}+a_{2}\equiv 0(\mathrm{mod}\ n_{\ell})
such that

\Psi^{+}=\{\alpha_{i}+(p_{i}+A_{i})\delta:i=1,2\}\cup\{\pm(\alpha_{1}\pm\alpha_{2})+(\pm p_{1}\pm p_{2}+n_{\ell}\mathbb{Z})\delta:\alpha\in\mathring{\Phi}^{+}_{\ell}\}

where
A_{i}=n_{\ell}\mathbb{Z}\cup(a_{i}+n_{\ell}\mathbb{Z}),i=1,2.
We shall determine
\Delta(\Psi)
in this case and we shall show that
\Delta(\Psi)\cap\Phi
is of the form
Equation

3.5
with
n_{s}=\mathrm{gcd}(a_{1},n)=\mathrm{gcd}(a_{2},n).
Since
a_{1}
is odd, we have
2\nmid\ n_{s}.
Since
a_{1}+a_{2}\equiv 0\ (\mathrm{mod}\ n_{\ell})
we have

\displaystyle Z_{\alpha_{1}}(\Psi)=n_{\ell}\mathbb{Z}\cup(a_{1}+n_{\ell}\mathbb{Z}),\ \ Z_{-\alpha_{1}}(\Psi)=n_{\ell}\mathbb{Z}\cup(a_{2}+n_{\ell}\mathbb{Z})\ \text{ and }

\displaystyle Z_{\alpha_{2}}(\Psi)=n_{\ell}\mathbb{Z}\cup(a_{2}+n_{\ell}\mathbb{Z}),\ \ Z_{-\alpha_{2}}(\Psi)=n_{\ell}\mathbb{Z}\cup(a_{1}+n_{\ell}\mathbb{Z})

Hence
Z_{\alpha_{i}}(\Psi)+Z_{-\alpha_{i}}(\Psi)=n_{\ell}\mathbb{Z}\cup(a_{1}+n_{\ell}\mathbb{Z})\cup(a_{2}+n_{\ell}\mathbb{Z})
for
i=1,2.
We shall show that
Z_{\pm\alpha_{i}}(\Delta(\Psi))\supseteq\pm p_{i}+\mathrm{gcd}(a_{i},n_{\ell})\mathbb{Z}.
Since for any
r\in\mathbb{Z},

[\alpha_{1}^{\vee},\mathfrak{g}_{\pm\alpha_{1}+r\delta}]\neq 0
holds, for any
s\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
we have
p_{1}+sa_{1}+n_{\ell}\mathbb{Z}\subseteq Z_{\alpha_{1}}(\Delta(\Psi)).
Also since
a_{1}+a_{2}\equiv 0\ (\mathrm{mod}\ n_{\ell}),

a_{2}+n_{\ell}\mathbb{Z}
lies in the set
\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}ka_{1}+n_{\ell}\mathbb{Z}.
Exchanging the role of
a_{1}
and
a_{2}
in the argument we get

Z_{\alpha_{i}}(\Delta(\Psi))\supseteq p_{i}+\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}(ka_{i}+n_{\ell}\mathbb{Z})=p_{i}+\mathrm{gcd}(a_{1},n_{\ell})\mathbb{Z},\ \ i=1,2.

Although
[\alpha_{i}^{\vee},\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha_{1}\pm\alpha_{2}+r\delta}]\neq 0,i=1,2,
we have
p_{1}\pm p_{2}+sa_{1}+n_{\ell}\mathbb{Z}\subseteq Z_{\alpha_{1}\pm\alpha_{2}}(\Delta(\Psi))
if and only if
s
is even. Hence it follows that
Z_{\alpha_{1}\pm\alpha_{2}}(\Delta(\Psi))\supseteq p_{1}\pm p_{2}+2\mathrm{gcd}(a_{1},n_{\ell})\mathbb{Z}.
But

\Psi^{\prime}:=\{\pm\alpha_{i}+\lceil\pm p_{i}+\mathrm{gcd}(a_{1},n_{\ell})\mathbb{Z}\rceil\delta\}\cup\{\pm(\alpha_{1}\pm\alpha_{2})+\lceil\pm p_{1}\pm p_{2}+2\mathrm{gcd}(a_{1},n_{\ell})\mathbb{Z}\rceil\delta\}

is a real closed subroot system of
\Phi
containing
\Psi
and contained in
\Delta(\Psi)\cap\Phi.
By
2
we have
\Psi^{\prime}=\Delta(\Psi)\cap\Phi
and hence the result.

6.3.

We first determine the preimage for
\Psi
, which is irreducible and of the form
Equation

3.5
.

Lemma 10
.

Let
\Psi
be an irreducible closed subroot system of
\Phi
of the form
Equation

3.5
and
n_{s}
as given in
Equation

3.5
.
For any given positive integer
r
, there are exactly
\varphi(2r)
symmetric, real closed subsets
\Psi^{\prime}
in
\iota_{\mathfrak{g}}^{-1}(\mathfrak{g}(\Psi))
such that

n_{\ell}(\Psi^{\prime})=2rn_{s}
, where
\varphi
is the Euler’s totient function. In particular,
\iota_{\mathfrak{g}}^{-1}(\mathfrak{g}(\Psi))
is infinite in this case. For a fixed
n_{s}
and
r,
all
\Psi^{\prime}\in\iota_{\mathfrak{g}}^{-1}(\mathfrak{g}(\Psi))
with
n_{\ell}(\Psi^{\prime})=2rn_{s}
are given by
Equation

3.4
where
a_{1}
is a cyclic generator of the group
\langle n_{s}\rangle
in
\mathbb{Z}/(2rn_{s})\mathbb{Z}.

Proof.

If
\Psi^{\prime}\in\iota_{\mathfrak{g}}^{-1}(\mathfrak{g}(\Psi))
, then
\Psi^{\prime}
must be of the form
Equation

3.4
. For a fixed
r,

\Psi^{\prime}\in\iota_{\mathfrak{g}}^{-1}(\mathfrak{g}(\Psi))
such that
n_{\ell}(\Psi^{\prime})=2rn_{s}
are in one to one correspondence with all
a_{1}
such that
1\leq a_{1}\leq 2rn_{s}
and
\mathrm{gcd}(a_{1},2rn_{s})=n_{s}
by
Example

2
. It is elementary to check that
\mathrm{gcd}(a_{1},2rn_{s})=n_{s}
if and only if
a_{1}
is a generator of the cyclic group
\langle n_{s}\rangle
in
\mathbb{Z}/({2rn_{s}})\mathbb{Z}.
Hence the number of such
a_{1}
is equal to
\varphi(2rn_{s}/n_{s})=\varphi(2r).

∎

We are now ready to prove our main result of this section.

Proposition 15
.

For any irreducible real closed subroot system
\Psi
of
\Phi,
we have

\iota_{\mathfrak{g}}^{-1}(\mathfrak{g}(\Psi))=\{\Psi\}

if either
Gr(\Psi)\text{ is not of type }A_{1},B_{2}
or
\text{ if }Gr(\Psi)=B_{2}\text{ and }\Psi\text{ is as in }\lx@cref{creftype\lx@tilde refnum}{prop:equal1}.

And
\iota_{\mathfrak{g}}^{-1}(\mathfrak{g}(\Psi))
is infinite for all other cases.

Proof.

Suppose that no irreducible component of
Gr(\Psi)
is of type
A_{1}
or
B_{2}
, then any
\Psi^{\prime}\in\iota_{\mathfrak{g}}^{-1}(\mathfrak{g}(\Psi))
is a closed subroot system by our previous results and in this case we have
\iota_{\mathfrak{g}}^{-1}(\mathfrak{g}(\Psi))=\{\Psi\}.

Now suppose that
Gr(\Psi)
is of type
B_{2}
. From
Example

2
, a symmetric, real closed subset
\Psi^{\prime}
of the form
Equation

3.4
generates a closed subroot system only of the form
Equation

3.5
. Hence if
Gr(\Psi)
is of type
B_{2}
and
\Psi
is of the form of
4
, then any
\Psi^{\prime}\in\iota_{\mathfrak{g}}^{-1}(\mathfrak{g}(\Psi))
is a closed subroot system of
\Phi
and hence in this case
\iota_{\mathfrak{g}}^{-1}(\mathfrak{g}(\Psi))=\{\Psi\}.

Now let
\Psi
be of the form
Equation

3.5
and let
n_{s}
as in
Equation

3.5
.
If
\Psi^{\prime}\in\iota_{\mathfrak{g}}^{-1}(\mathfrak{g}(\Psi)),
then
\Psi^{\prime}
is of the form
Equation

3.4
where
n_{\ell}(\Psi^{\prime})
is an even multiple of
n_{s}.
Remaining follows from
10
.

Remark 8
.

One of the drastic differences between the finite and affine root system theory is that even maximal symmetric closed subsets are not necessarily closed subroot systems.
For example, let
n_{\ell}
be any even integer
\geq 4
and
a_{1}
be any integer which is
\leq n_{\ell}
,
a_{1}\neq n_{\ell}/2
and
\mathrm{gcd}(a_{,}n_{\ell})=1.
Then define
\Psi
with this
n_{\ell}
and
a_{1}
as in
6
. Then
\Psi
is a proper symmetric closed subset which is not a closed subroot system and by
Example

2
,
\Delta(\Psi)\cap\Phi=\Phi.

Suppose
\Psi^{\prime}\supseteq\Psi
is a symmetric closed subset that is not a subroot system, then
we have that
Z_{\epsilon_{i}}(\Psi^{\prime})
is a union of two cosets of
n_{\ell}\mathbb{Z}
. This implies that
\Psi^{\prime}=\Psi.
Hence
\Psi
is a maximal symmetric closed subset of
\Phi
, but not a closed subroot system.

∎

Abstract
. Unitally nondistributive quantales are unital quantales such that the unit is approximable by the totally below relation and does not meet-distribute over arbitrary joins. It is shown that the underlying nondistributive complete lattice contains at least 7 7 elements. Moreover, under mild conditions, every quantale has an extension to a unitally nondistributive quantale by the addition of an isolated unit. As a byproduct of this construction we prove that there exist 30 30 non-isomorphic, unitally nondistributive quantales on the set of 7 7 elements. Keywords. Unitally nondistributive quantale, nondistributive lattice, quantale extension

Introduction

In this paper we present a new class of quantales arising from a question posed by Dirk Hofmann and Maria Manuel Clementino during the XIV Portuguese Category Seminar celebrated at Coimbra, Portugal in October 2023 (see
[
1
]
). The essence of this question can be formulated as follows. Let
\mathfrak{Q}
be a unital quantale. What impact has the approximability of the unit of
\mathfrak{Q}
by the totally below relation on the distributivity of the underlying lattice of
\mathfrak{Q}
. In this context it is important to point out that under the existence of a dualizing element and the distributivity of the quantale multiplication over nonempty meets the approximability of the unit of
\mathfrak{Q}
by the totally below relation is equivalent to the completely distributivity of the underling lattice (cf. Proposition
1.1
). Therefore the question arises what happens in the absence of a dualizing element or the missing distributivity of the quantale multiplication over nonempty meets.

The aim of this paper is to show that the approximability of the unit of a quantale by the totally below relation is completely unrelated to any kind of distributivity of the underlying lattice. For this purpose we introduce the class of unitally nondistributive quantales.
As a first step we investigate their underlying complete lattice and encounter a new class of nondistributive lattices, in which the approximability of the unit by the totally below relation entails a stronger form of nondistributivity being called here strict nondistributivity (cf. Definition
2.1
).
The investigation of this notion leads us to the first important result (cf. Proposition
2.4
),
which provides a necessary condition for a complete lattice
to be strictly nondistributive in terms of
4
specific complete sublattices. Further, every nondistributive complete lattice has an extension to a strictly nondistributive one (cf. Lemma
2.2
).
In a next step we present the second important result of this paper (cf. Theorem
3.3
), a construction showing how, under mild conditions, quantales on nondistributives lattices can be extended to unitally nondistributive quantales by addition of an isolated unit.
On this background, it is not difficult to see that every unitally nondistributive quantale contains at least
7
elements (cf. Corollary
2.6
). This is the reason why we provide the classification of all unitally nondistributive quantales on the set of
7
elements and obtain that on the set of
7
elements there exist exactly
30
non-isomorphic, unitally nondistributive quantales (cf. Corollary
4.3
and Examples
4.5
and
4.6
). All of them could be seen as a simple counterexample concerning the question motivating our study (cf. Subsection
4.1
). Two classes of some more advanced counterexamples dealing with at least
8
elements appear in Subsection
4.2
completing the picture given by Proposition
2.4
.

1.
Preliminaries on quantales and motivation

Let
\mathsf{Sup}
be the symmetric, monoidal closed category of complete lattices and join-preserving maps. A
quantale
is a semigroup in
\mathsf{Sup}
, a
unital
quantale is a monoid in
\mathsf{Sup}
, and an
integral
quantale is a unital quantale such that the unit coincides with the upper universal bound
\top
(cf.
[
3
]
). Due to the tensor product in
\mathsf{Sup}
, every quantale
\mathfrak{Q}
can be identified with a complete lattice provided with a semigroup operation
\ast
which is join-preserving in each variable separately, i.e.
\bigl(\mathop{\textstyle\bigvee}A\bigr)\ast\beta=\mathop{\textstyle\bigvee}_{\alpha\in A}\alpha\ast\beta
and
\beta\ast\bigl(\mathop{\textstyle\bigvee}A\bigr)=\mathop{\textstyle\bigvee}_{\alpha\in A}\beta\ast\alpha,
for each
A\subseteq\mathfrak{Q}
and
\beta\in\mathfrak{Q}
.
The right- and left-implication are given by

\alpha\searrow\beta=\mathop{\textstyle\bigvee}\{\,\gamma\in\mathfrak{Q}\mid\alpha\ast\gamma\leq\beta\,\}\quad\text{and}\quad\beta\swarrow\alpha=\mathop{\textstyle\bigvee}\{\,\gamma\in\mathfrak{Q}\mid\gamma\ast\alpha\leq\beta\,\},\qquad\alpha,\beta\in\mathfrak{Q}.

Let
\mathfrak{Q}
be a quantale.
An element
\delta\in\mathfrak{Q}
is
dualizing
if for all
\alpha\in\mathfrak{Q}
the relation
{\delta\swarrow(\alpha\searrow\delta)}=\alpha={(\delta\swarrow\alpha)}\searrow\delta
holds. If
\mathfrak{Q}
has a dualizing element, then it is unital.
\mathfrak{Q}
is
semi-unital
if
\alpha\leq\top\ast\alpha
and
\alpha\leq\alpha\ast\top
hold for all
\alpha\in\mathfrak{Q}
. An element
\alpha\in\mathfrak{Q}
is
two-sided
if
\top\ast\alpha\leq\alpha
and
\alpha\ast\top\leq\alpha
, and
\mathfrak{Q}
is
two-sided
if every element of
\mathfrak{Q}
is two-sided. Every integral quantale is two-sided, but not vice versa.

Let
L
be a complete lattice and
\vartriangleleft
be the totally below relation on
L
, i.e. given
\alpha,\beta\in L
, we write
\beta\vartriangleleft\alpha
if for any subset
A\subseteq L
with
\alpha\leq\mathop{\textstyle\bigvee}A
there is a
\gamma\in A
such that
\beta\leq\gamma
. We recall that a complete lattice is
completely distributive
if every
\alpha\in L\setminus\{\,\bot\,\}
is
\vartriangleleft
-approximable
, i.e.
\alpha\leq\mathop{\textstyle\bigvee}\{\,\beta\in L\mid\beta\vartriangleleft\alpha\,\}
.
Note that if
\alpha\vartriangleleft\alpha
, i.e.
\alpha
is
completely join-prime
(cf.
[
2
]
), then
\alpha
is
\vartriangleleft
-approximable.

Before we proceed we first show that under certain algebraic properties of a unital quantale the
\vartriangleleft
-approximability of its unit implies the complete distributivity of its underlying complete lattice.

Proposition 1.1
.

Let
\mathfrak{Q}=(\mathfrak{Q},\ast,e)

be a unital quantale with a dualizing element such that the
quantale multiplication is distributive over nonempty meets. If the unit
e
is
\vartriangleleft
-approximable, then the underlying lattice of
\mathfrak{Q}
is completely
distributive.

Proof.

Let
\alpha\in\mathfrak{Q}\setminus\{\,\bot\,\}
,
\delta
be a dualizing element of
\mathfrak{Q}
and
e
be the unit of
\mathfrak{Q}
. Further, let
\beta\in\mathfrak{Q}
such that
\beta\vartriangleleft e
and
\varnothing\neq A\subseteq\mathfrak{Q}
with
\alpha\leq\mathop{\textstyle\bigvee}A
.
If the quantale multiplication distributes over nonempty
meets, then

\displaystyle\beta\vartriangleleft e

\displaystyle\leq\alpha\searrow\bigl(\mathop{\textstyle\bigvee}A\bigr)=\alpha\searrow\bigl(\bigl(\delta\swarrow\bigl(\mathop{\textstyle\bigvee}A\bigr)\bigr)\searrow\delta\bigr)=\alpha\searrow\bigl(\bigl(\mathop{\textstyle\bigwedge}\limits_{\gamma\in A}\bigl(\delta\swarrow\gamma\bigr)\bigr)\searrow\delta\bigr)

\displaystyle=\bigl(\bigl(\mathop{\textstyle\bigwedge}_{\gamma\in A}(\delta\swarrow\gamma)\bigr)\ast\alpha\bigr)\searrow\delta=\mathop{\textstyle\bigvee}_{\gamma\in A}\bigl(\bigl((\delta\swarrow\gamma)\ast\alpha\bigr)\searrow\delta\bigr)=\mathop{\textstyle\bigvee}_{\gamma\in A}(\alpha\searrow\gamma).

Hence there exists
\gamma\in A
such that
\beta\leq\alpha\searrow\gamma
, i.e.
\alpha\ast\beta\leq\gamma
and it follows that
(\alpha\ast\beta)\vartriangleleft\alpha
.
Since
e
is
\vartriangleleft
-approximable, we obtain

\alpha\leq\alpha\ast\bigl(\mathop{\textstyle\bigvee}\{\,\beta\in\mathfrak{Q}\mid\beta\vartriangleleft e\,\}\bigr)=\mathop{\textstyle\bigvee}\{\,\alpha\ast\beta\mid\beta\in\mathfrak{Q}\text{ and }\beta\vartriangleleft e\,\}\leq\mathop{\textstyle\bigvee}\{\,\gamma\in\mathfrak{Q}\mid\gamma\vartriangleleft\alpha\,\}

We conclude that
\alpha
is
\vartriangleleft
-approximable and consequently the underlying lattice of
\mathfrak{Q}
is completely
distributive.
∎

If in a unital quantale we abandon the existence of a dualizing element
or the property that
the quantale multiplication is distributive over nonempty meets,
then Proposition
1.1
suggests the question which
relationship exists between the
\vartriangleleft
-approximability of the the unit
e
and
distributivity properties of the underlying lattice. As we already pointed out in the introduction, this question arose also during the XIV Portuguese Category Seminar celebrated at Coimbra, Portugal in October 2023. More precisely, Dirk Hofmann and Maria Manuel Clementino asked the following:

Question 1.2
.

If the unit of a unital quantale is
\vartriangleleft
-approximable, does this property imply
that then the unit
\wedge
-distributes over arbitrary joins?

The motivation of this article is to answer this question negatively by proving that there exist a large class of unital quantales
\mathfrak{Q}=(\mathfrak{Q},\ast,e)
such that the unit
e
is

\vartriangleleft
-approximable and there exists a subset
A
of
\mathfrak{Q}
satisfying the property
e\wedge\bigl(\mathop{\textstyle\bigvee}A\bigr)\not\leq\mathop{\textstyle\bigvee}_{\alpha\in A}(e\wedge\alpha)
.
We shall call these quantales
unitally nondistributive
.
It follows immediately that every unitally nondistributive quantale is non-integral and its
underlying lattice is far from being a frame. However, it is interesting to see that every
quantale induced by an arbitrary group can be extended to a
unitally nondistributive quantale (cf. Example
3.5
(2)).

2.
Strictly nondistributive lattices

First we recall some terminology from lattice theory. Let
L
be
a lattice and
\alpha,\beta,\gamma\in L
.
\alpha
is
join-irreducible
if
\alpha\neq 0
and
\alpha=\beta\vee\gamma
implies
that
\alpha=\beta
or
\alpha=\gamma
, and
\alpha
and
\beta
are

incomparable
, if
\alpha\not\leq\beta
and
\beta\not\leq\alpha
.
We will also use the following notation:

{{\hbox to0.0pt{$\ $\hss}\hbox{$\downarrow$}}}\alpha=\{\,\beta\in L\mid\beta\leq\alpha\,\}\quad\text{ and }\quad{{\hbox to0.0pt{$\ $\hss}\hbox{$\uparrow$}}}\alpha={\{\,\beta\in L\mid\alpha\leq\beta\,\}}.

An element
\alpha\in L\setminus\{\bot,\top\}
is
isolated

1

1

The property being isolated means also to be doubly
(completely) irreducible.
in
L
if there
exist two elements
\alpha^{-}\leq\alpha
and
\alpha^{+}\geq\alpha
of
L
such that
following properties hold:

({{\hbox to0.0pt{$\ $\hss}\hbox{$\downarrow$}}}\alpha)\setminus\{\,\alpha\,\}={{\hbox to0.0pt{$\ $\hss}\hbox{$\downarrow$}}}\alpha^{-}\text{ and}\quad({{\hbox to0.0pt{$\ $\hss}\hbox{$\uparrow$}}}\alpha)\setminus\{\,\alpha\,\}={{\hbox to0.0pt{$\ $\hss}\hbox{$\uparrow$}}}\alpha^{+}.

It is easy to see that monomorphisms in the category
\mathsf{Sup}
are join-preserving and injective maps.

Motivated by the concept of quantales (cf. Section
1
) the categorical framework in this paper will be always
\mathsf{Sup}
. Hence a
complete sublattice

S
of a complete lattice
L
is a complete lattice
S
such that the inclusion map
S\xhookrightarrow{\,\,\,}L
is join-preserving. Therefore joins in
S
are joins in the sense of
L
, but not meets.

In what follows we are interested in nondistributive lattices. Recall that a lattice
L
is
nondistributive
if and only if there exist three elements
\alpha,\beta,\gamma\in L
such that

(2.1)

\gamma\wedge(\alpha\vee\beta)\not\leq(\gamma\wedge\alpha)\vee(\gamma\wedge\beta).

It is well know that without loss of generality we can always choose
\gamma
in (
2.1
) satisfying the property
\gamma\leq\alpha\vee\beta
. But then (
2.1
) implies that
\gamma
fails to be
\vartriangleleft
-approximable. Typical examples of nondistributive lattices provided with this structure are the diamond with three atoms or the pentagon (see
\mathsf{M}_{3}
and
\mathsf{N}_{5}
in Figure
1
).

Since our main interest is to provide an answer to Question
1.2
and to investigate nondistributive, complete lattices, in which certain elements are
\vartriangleleft
-approximable, we introduce the following terminology.

Definition 2.1
.

A lattice
L
is
strictly nondistributive
if there exist
\alpha,\beta,x\in L
such that

(2.2)

x\wedge(\alpha\vee\beta)\not\leq(x\wedge\alpha)\vee(x\wedge\beta)\quad\text{and}\quad x\not\leq\alpha\vee\beta.

As we have seen above, not every nondistributive lattice is strictly nondistributive.
In fact, if in
\mathsf{M}_{3}
or
\mathsf{N}_{5}
we choose
\alpha,\beta,\gamma\in L
satisfying (
2.1
),
then
\alpha\vee\beta
coincides necessarily with the universal upper bound. But we will show now that every nondistributive lattice can be
extended
to a strictly nondistributive lattice.
This construction will be based on the addition of an isolated element and will play a significant role in what follows.
Due to the lattice structure this isolated element
x
will always be completely join-prime, i.e.
x\vartriangleleft x
.
For this purpose we first fix some further terminology.

Let us recall that we are working in
\mathsf{Sup}
.
Then a complete sublattice
S
of
a complete lattice
L
is called a
pentagon
, respectively a
diamond with
3
atoms

if
S
is isomorphic to
\mathsf{N}_{5}
, respectively to
\mathsf{M}_{3}

They are
typical

minimal

Consequently any nondistributive lattice must have at least
5

We are also interested in another two nondistributive lattices with

A complete lattice

The next lemma describes a special extension of a complete lattice by two different elements, one of them being isolated.
In particular, this extension will always transfer a nondistributive lattice into a
strictly
nondistributive one.
Note also that since we are working in
\mathsf{Sup}

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
L
be a complete lattice
and
\overline{\top}
and
x
be two different elements satisfying the condition
L\cap\{\,\overline{\top},x\,\}=\varnothing
. Then for every
\gamma\in L
there exists a unique complete lattice-structure on
\overline{L}^{\gamma}=L\cup\{\,\overline{\top},x\,\}
satisfying the following conditions
:

(i)

\overline{L}^{\gamma}
is an extension of
L
.

(ii)

The element
\overline{\top}
is the universal upper bound of
\overline{L}^{\gamma}
.

(iii)

The element
x
is isolated and satisfies the conditions
x^{-}=\gamma
and
x^{+}=\overline{\top}
.

Moreover, if there exist
\alpha,\beta\in L
such that
\{\,\alpha,\beta,\gamma\,\}
satisfies
(
2.1
)
, then
\{\,\alpha,\beta,x\,\}
satisfies
(
2.2
)
in
\overline{L}^{\gamma}
, i.e.
\overline{L}^{\gamma}
is strictly nondistributive.

Proof.

(Uniqueness) With regard to
(i)
and
(ii)
it is sufficient to investigate the order relation between the element
x
and all other elements of
L
. It is easy to see that
(iii)
is equivalent to the following properties in
\overline{L}^{\gamma}
:

(iv)

if
\alpha\leq\gamma
in
L
, then
\alpha\leq x
in
\overline{L}^{\gamma}
,

(v)

if
\alpha\not\leq\gamma
in
L
, then
\alpha
is incomparable with
x
in
\overline{L}^{\gamma}
.

(Existence) Obviously, the partial order on
L
can be extended to
\overline{L}^{\gamma}
by using the relations
(iv)
and
(v)
and fixing
\overline{\top}
as universal upper bound. Then
(i)
—
(iii)
are satisfied. In order to confirm the completeness of
\overline{L}^{\gamma}
it is sufficient to observe that for all
\alpha\in L
the following relations hold:

(vi)

x\wedge\alpha=\gamma\wedge\alpha
,

(vii)

if
\alpha\leq\gamma
in
L
then
\alpha\vee x=x
,

(viii)

if
\alpha\not\leq\gamma
in
L
then
\alpha\vee x=\overline{\top}
.

Finally, if
\{\,\alpha,\beta,\gamma\,\}
satisfies (
2.1
) in
L
,
then
\alpha\vee\beta\not\leq\gamma
, and consequently
{\alpha\vee\beta}
and
x
are incomparable. Further, referring to
x^{-}=\gamma
, we observe
({\alpha\vee\beta})\wedge\gamma={({\alpha\vee\beta})\wedge x}
,
\alpha\wedge\gamma=\alpha\wedge x
and
\beta\wedge\gamma=\beta\wedge x
. Hence
\{\,\alpha,\beta,x\,\}
satisfies (
2.2
) in
\overline{L}^{\gamma}
, i.e.
\overline{L}^{\gamma}
is strictly nondistributive.

Our next result shows the necessity of the existence of one of these
4
strictly nondistributive lattices as complete sublattices in any complete strictly nondistributive lattice.

Proposition 2.4
.

If
L
is a complete and strictly nondistributive lattice, then there exists a complete sublattice of
L
being isomorphic to
\overline{\mathsf{N}}_{5}
,
\overline{\mathsf{M}}_{3}
,
\overline{\mathsf{L}}_{6}
or
\overline{\mathsf{L}}_{7}
.

Proof.

Let
L
be a complete and strictly nondistributive lattice. If
\alpha,\beta,x\in L
satisfy (
2.2
), i.e. the relations
x\not\leq\alpha\vee\beta
and
x\wedge(\alpha\vee\beta)\not\leq(x\wedge\alpha)\vee(x\wedge\beta)
hold, then
L
has at least
5
distinct elements
\bot
,
\alpha
,
\beta
,
\alpha\vee\beta
and
x
satisfying the properties

(2.3)

\alpha\vee\beta\not\leq x,\quad x\wedge(\alpha\vee\beta)\not\leq\alpha\quad\text{and}\quad x\wedge(\alpha\vee\beta)\not\leq\beta.

Since
x\not\leq\alpha\vee\beta
, (

) implies that
\alpha\vee\beta

and
x
are incomparable, and consequently we have
x\vee(\alpha\vee\beta)\notin\{\,\bot,\alpha,\beta,\alpha\vee\beta,x\,\}
.
Referring again to (
2.3
), the relation
{x\wedge(\alpha\vee\beta)}\notin\{\,\bot,\alpha,\beta,\alpha\vee\beta,x,x\vee(\alpha\vee\beta)\,\}

and

is a complete sublattice of

with at least
7

)
implies

and
\gamma\not\leq\beta
and a fortiori

and
x\not\leq\beta
. Hence
\alpha
and

are atoms in

and
\gamma\neq\bot

and

\alpha\wedge\gamma=\begin{cases}\bot,&\alpha\not\leq\gamma,\\
\alpha,&\alpha\leq\gamma,\end{cases}\quad\text{and}\quad\beta\wedge\gamma=\begin{cases}\bot,&\beta\not\leq\gamma,\\
\beta,&\beta\leq\gamma.\end{cases}

The size of

,

3.
Construction of unitally nondistributive quantales

As we announced in the introduction, we say that a unital quantale
\mathfrak{Q}=(\mathfrak{Q},\ast,e)
is

unitally nondistributive
if it satisfies the
following properties:

(1)

\mathop{\textstyle\bigvee}\{\,\alpha\in\mathfrak{Q}\mid\alpha\vartriangleleft e\,\}=e
(the unit
e
is

\vartriangleleft
-approximable).

(2)

There exists a subset
A
of
\mathfrak{Q}
such that

e\wedge\bigl(\mathop{\textstyle\bigvee}A\bigr)\not\leq\mathop{\textstyle\bigvee}_{\alpha\in A}(e\wedge\alpha)
.

Proposition 3.1
.

If
\mathfrak{Q}
is a finite unitally nondistributive quantale with unit
e
then
there exist
\alpha,\beta\in\mathfrak{Q}
such that

(3.1)

e\wedge(\alpha\vee\beta)\not\leq(e\wedge\alpha)\vee(e\wedge\beta)\quad\text{and}\quad e\not\leq\alpha\vee\beta.

Hence the underlying lattice of
\mathfrak{Q}
is strictly nondistributive.

Proof.

Let
A=\{\,\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n}\,\}
be a subset of
\mathfrak{Q}
satisfying (2). Then the cardinality of
A
is at least
2
, and we define
\beta_{i}:={\alpha_{i}\vee\cdots\vee\alpha_{n}}
for each
i\in\{\,1,\ldots,n\,\}
. If we assume
e\wedge\beta_{i}\leq(e\wedge\alpha_{i})\vee(e\wedge\beta_{i+1})
for each
i\in\{\,1,\ldots,n-1\,\}
, then
we would have

\displaystyle e\wedge\bigl(\mathop{\textstyle\bigvee}A\bigr)

\displaystyle=e\wedge\beta_{1}\leq(e\wedge\alpha_{1})\vee(e\wedge\beta_{2})\leq\cdots\leq(e\wedge\alpha_{1})\vee\cdots\vee(e\wedge\alpha_{n}),

which is a contradiction to (2). Hence
there exists
i\in\{\,1,\ldots,n-1\,\}
such that the relation
e\wedge\beta_{i}\not\leq(e\wedge\alpha_{i})\vee(e\wedge\beta_{i+1})
holds.
If we now put
\alpha:=\alpha_{i}
and
\beta:=\beta_{i+1}
,
then
\alpha\vee\beta=\beta_{i}
, and the first part of (
3.1
) is verified.
Further, since the unit
e
is
\vartriangleleft
-approximable, the first part of (
3.1
) implies the second part of (
3.1
).
∎

The same arguments can be used for unitally nondistributive quantales such that the underlying
lattice is meet continuous, i.e. binary meets distribute over
directed suprema. Hence Proposition
3.1
holds also for this class of quantales.

In view of Proposition
3.1
and Corollary
2.6
we conclude that every unitally nondistributive and meet continuous quantale contains at least
7
elements. This observation is a motivation to give a classification of all unitally nondistributive quantales on the set with
7
elements (see Subsection
4.1
infra).

Based on the observation and results of Section
2
we will
present a procedure describing an extension of a quantale to a unitally
nondistributive quantale. This construction is based on the extension of the underlying lattice by an isolated element (cf. Lemma
2.2
) which in this case will be the unit of the new quantale. In contrast with the previous situation, where
any
lattice could be extended in this way, we need some additional condition on the quantale in order to have the required extension. We first note the following:

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
L
be a complete lattice,
\gamma\in L\setminus\{\,\top\,\}
and let
\mathfrak{Q}
be a unital quantale on the extension
\overline{L}^{\gamma}=L\cup\{\,e,\overline{\top}\,\}
of
L
in the sense of Lemma
2.2
such that the isolated element
e
is the unit of
\mathfrak{Q}
. Then
:

(1)

(\gamma\ast\alpha)\vee(\alpha\ast\gamma)\leq\alpha
for all
\alpha\in L
,

(2)

\overline{\top}\ast\alpha=(\beta\ast\alpha)\vee\alpha
and
\alpha\ast\overline{\top}=(\alpha\ast\beta)\vee\alpha
for all
\beta\not\leq\gamma
and
\alpha\in L
,

(3)

L
is a subquantale of
\mathfrak{Q}
if and only if
\top\ast\top\leq\top
.

(4)

If
\bigwedge\{\,\beta\vee\gamma\mid\beta\in L,\ \beta\not\leq\gamma\,\}\leq\gamma
, then
\gamma
is two-sided in
\overline{L}^{\gamma}
.

(5)

If
L
is a subquantale of
\mathfrak{Q}
, then the quantale multiplication in
\mathfrak{Q}
with unit
e
is uniquely determined by its values in
L
.

Proof.

The first assertion follows immediately since
\gamma\leq e
and
e
is the unit of
\mathfrak{Q}
. Since
\gamma\neq\top
, the set
\{\,\beta\in L\mid\beta\not\leq\gamma\,\}
is nonempty, and consequently
(2)
follows from
\overline{\top}=\beta\vee e
for all
\beta\not\leq\gamma
(cf.
(viii)
). The third one is obvious. The assertion
(4)
follows from
(1)
and
(2)
.
Finally
(5)
is an immediate corollary of
(2)
.
∎

Now we have the following:

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
\mathfrak{Q}
be a quantale,
\gamma\in\mathfrak{Q}\setminus\{\top\}
and let
\overline{\mathfrak{Q}}^{\gamma}=\mathfrak{Q}\cup\{\,\overline{\top},e\,\}
be the extension of the underlying lattice by an isolated element

in the sense of Lemma

\overline{\mathfrak{Q}}^{\gamma}
with unit

and subquantale

if and only if

satisfies the following conditions for all
\alpha\in\mathfrak{Q}
and
\beta\in\mathfrak{Q}
with

(3.2)

\displaystyle(\gamma\ast\alpha)\vee(\alpha\ast\gamma)\leq\alpha,

(3.3)

\displaystyle\top\ast\alpha\leq(\beta\ast\alpha)\vee\alpha\quad\text{and}\quad\alpha\ast\top\leq(\alpha\ast\beta)\vee\alpha.

Moreover, if there exist two elements
\alpha,\beta\in\mathfrak{Q}

satisfying the property

(3.4)

\gamma\wedge(\alpha\vee\beta)\not\leq(\gamma\wedge\alpha)\vee(\gamma\wedge\beta),

then
\overline{\mathfrak{Q}}^{\gamma}
is a unitally
nondistributive quantale.

Proof.

The necessity follows from Lemma
3.2

(1)
and
(2)
, and the uniqueness of the quantale structure with unit
e
and
\mathfrak{Q}
as subquantale follows from Lemma
3.2

(5)
. In order to establish the existence of the quantale structure on
\overline{\mathfrak{Q}}^{\gamma}
with unit

and subquantale
\mathfrak{Q}
we extend the quantale multiplication on
\mathfrak{Q}
to the additional elements

and
\overline{\top}
as follows. First we require that
e
is the unit in
\overline{\mathfrak{Q}}^{\gamma}
, and secondly the multiplication involving
\overline{\top}
is determined by:

(3.5)

\overline{\top}\ast\overline{\top}=\overline{\top},\quad\overline{\top}\ast\alpha=(\top\ast\alpha)\vee\alpha,\quad\alpha\ast\overline{\top}=(\alpha\ast\top)\vee\alpha,\qquad\alpha\in\mathfrak{Q}.

Since
\gamma=e^{-}
, the property (
3.2
) implies that this extension of the quantale multiplication is isotone in
\overline{\mathfrak{Q}}^{\gamma}
. Further, the property (
3.3
) guarantees that this extension preserves arbitrary joins in each variable separately, i.e. it is a quantale multiplication in
\overline{\mathfrak{Q}}^{\gamma}
with unit

.
In fact, it follows from (

) that the left and right multiplications with
\overline{\top}
are obviously join-preserving. The interesting situation occurs when we consider
\alpha\in\mathfrak{Q}
and a subset

\mathfrak{Q}\cup\{\,e\,\}
such that

(\mathop{\textstyle\bigvee}A)\ast\alpha=(\top\ast\alpha)\vee\alpha\leq(\beta\ast\alpha)\vee\alpha\leq\mathop{\textstyle\bigvee}\{\,\varepsilon\ast\alpha\mid\varepsilon\in A\,\}\leq(\top\ast\alpha)\vee\alpha.

Hence
(\mathop{\textstyle\bigvee}A)\ast\alpha=\mathop{\textstyle\bigvee}\{\,\varepsilon\ast\alpha\mid\varepsilon\in A\,\}
, and analogously we verify
\alpha\ast(\mathop{\textstyle\bigvee}A)=\mathop{\textstyle\bigvee}\{\,\alpha\ast\varepsilon\mid\varepsilon\in A\,\}
.
Finally, by Lemma
3.2

(3)
,
\mathfrak{Q}
is a subquantale of
\overline{\mathfrak{Q}}^{\gamma}
.

On the other hand, if there exist

\alpha,\beta\in\mathfrak{Q}
satisfying (
3.4
), then Lemma
2.2
implies that the underlying lattice of
\overline{\mathfrak{Q}}^{\gamma}
is strictly nondistributive with isolated unit

, and so

holds. Since

is completely join-prime (cf. Remark

\vartriangleleft
-approximable, we conclude that

∎

Unitally nondistributive quantales on finite sets

Let
\mathfrak{Q}
be a (finite) unitally nondistributive quantale with isolated unit
e
. Then the underlying lattice is strictly nondistributive
(cf. Proposition
3.1
). In particular, there exist elements
\alpha,\beta\in\mathfrak{Q}
such that

satisfies (

\alpha
and

are incomparable. We put

,

are incomparable and the relations

, and finally,

In general

such that the element

holds in

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
\mathfrak{Q}
be a unital quantale on

with unit

such that
e^{-}=\gamma
and
e^{+}=e\vee a

holds, i.e.

Since

Now we assume

e\leq a\ast a=(\alpha\ast\alpha)\vee(\alpha\ast\beta)\vee(\beta\ast\alpha)\vee(\beta\ast\beta).

Since

, there exists

Hence we have

. With regard to (

\mathsf{L}_{7}
. If
\mathfrak{Q}
satisfies (
3.2
) and (
3.3
), then
a\ast\gamma=\gamma\ast a=\bot
. In fact, since
{(\alpha\vee\gamma)\wedge(\beta\vee\gamma)}=\gamma
, Lemma
3.2

(4)
and Theorem
3.3
imply that
\gamma
is two-sided in
\overline{\mathsf{L}}_{7}
, and consequently
a\ast\gamma\leq\gamma

a\ast\gamma=(\alpha\ast\gamma)\vee(\beta\ast\gamma)\leq(\alpha\wedge\gamma)\vee(\beta\wedge\gamma)=\bot

follows. Analogously we can verify
\gamma\ast a=\bot
.

Hence there exists a relationship between unitally nondistributive quantales on
\overline{\mathsf{L}}_{7}
and
\overline{\mathsf{M}}_{3}
as follows:
Every unitally nondistribitive quantale on
\overline{\mathsf{M}}_{3}
being an extension of the non-unital quantales
[
6
, 5.2.1 — 5.2.4]
by an isolated unit
e
is a quotient of a unital quantale on
\overline{\mathsf{L}}_{7}
with unit
e
in the sense of the category of unital quantales.

We finish this remark with some details of this construction. Since
\top\ast\gamma=\bot=\gamma\ast\top
holds in all non-unital quantales on the diamond
\mathsf{M}_{3}
(cf. Example
4.5
), the quantale multiplications in
[
6
, 5.2.1 — 5.2.4]
have an extension to a quantale multiplication in
\mathsf{L}_{7}
by defining:

x\ast(\alpha\vee\gamma):=x\ast\alpha,\quad x\ast(\beta\vee\gamma):=x\ast\beta,\quad(\alpha\vee\gamma)\ast x:=\alpha\ast x,\quad(\beta\vee\gamma)\ast x:=\beta\ast x,

for each
x\in\mathsf{M}_{3}
.
In a second step we realize that all these quantales satisfy the properties (
3.2
) and (
3.3
). Hence we can apply Theorem
3.3
and consider their extension by an isolated unit on
\overline{\mathsf{L}}_{7}
. Finally,
\overline{\mathsf{L}}_{7}\xrightarrow{\,c\,}\overline{\mathsf{L}}_{7}
defined by (cf. Figure
2

c(\alpha\vee\gamma)=\top=c(\beta\vee\gamma),\quad c(z)=z\quad\text{for}\,\,\,z\in\overline{\mathsf{L}}_{7}\setminus\{\,\alpha\vee\gamma,\beta\vee\gamma\,\}

is a nucleus, and the respective quotients w.r.t.
c
are isomorphic to the extension of the respective non-unital quantales on
\mathsf{M}_{3}
by an isolated unit.

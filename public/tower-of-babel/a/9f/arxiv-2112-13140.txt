Asymptotic flatness in higher dimensions
Peter Cameron, Piotr T. Chruściel

Abstract
We show that ( n + 1 ) (n+1) -dimensional Myers-Perry metrics, n ≥ 4 n\geq 4 , have a conformal completion at spacelike infinity of C n − 3 , 1 C^{n-3,1} differentiability class, and that the result is optimal in even spacetime dimensions. The associated asymptotic symmetries are presented.



1
Introduction

One of the classical questions in general relativity is that of the behaviour of the gravitational field when receding to infinity in spacelike directions. This has been studied in detail in many works in spacetime dimension four, see e.g.
[
18
,
2
,
6
,
13
,
3
,
5
,
8
,
9
,
4
,
14
,
15
]
.
In particular, as is well known, Minkowski spacetime has a conformal completion that includes a point at spacelike infinity, called
i_{0}
, such that the conformally extended metric is smooth near this point. It is also well known that the usual extension of the Minkowskian construction to four-dimensional Schwarzschild spacetime leads to a conformal completion with a metric which is continuous
[
9
,
2
,
5
]
, but no such extension which is
C^{1}
is known.
Now, in standard examples of higher dimensional spacetimes the metric decays faster to the Minkowski metric at spacelike infinity, and therefore one expects that such constructions will lead to metrics with better differentiability properties at
i_{0}
in higher dimensions. The question then arises about the precise degree of differentiability that one can achieve.

Now, the conformal method of constructing solutions of the general relativistic constraint equations shows that the decay of the metric towards the flat one, when receding in spacelike directions, can be arbitrarily slow. Therefore nothing striking can be said in this generality. But the study of asymptotics of initial data sets, and thus of the associated spacetimes, has been given new life by the work of Corvino and Schoen
[
12
]
(compare
[
10
]
), who show that rather general initial data sets can be deformed at large distance to members of any family of metrics which contains a complete set of asymptotic charges.
A particularly useful such family is provided by the Myers-Perry metrics, to which boosts have been applied. The glued metric is then stationary near
i_{0}
, which gives one useful information concerning the resulting evolution. This leads one naturally to raise the question of conformal properties near
i_{0}
of spacetimes within this family.

The object of this note is to show that
(n+1)
-dimensional Myers-Perry metrics admit a conformal completion which includes a point
i_{0}
at spatial infinity so that past and future null infinities
{\mycal I}^{\pm}
form the null cone emanating from this point, with a metric which is of
C^{n-3,1}
differentiability class near this point. Thus, all derivatives of order less than
n-3
extend continuously to
i_{0}
and
{\mycal I}
, while the derivatives of the metric of order
n-3
remain bounded as
i_{0}
is approached, but do not necessarily extend to functions which are continuous at
i_{0}
.

We conclude that the conformal structure at spacelike and null infinity is quite similar to that of Minkowski spacetime, except for a loss of differentiability at
i_{0}
which is less severe than in the
(3+1)
-dimensional case. We also show that the result is optimal in
even
spacetime dimensions in the following sense: no
C^{n-2}
completions exist unless the total mass vanishes.

Recall, now, that the Riemannian metric obtained from quotienting the space-time metric of stationary, odd-dimensional, electrovacuum metrics by the stationary isometries admits a conformal completion at a point at infinity which is real-analytic
[
4
]
. But this result does not translate in any obvious way to the associated spacetime metric. And we do not know whether or not our result is optimal in such dimensions either.

Our observation concerning the differentiability of the conformally rescaled metric has immediate consequences concerning causality near
i_{0}
.
Recall that the usual results of causality theory, except perhaps the ones explicitly involving geodesics, apply for metrics which are Lipschitz continuous
[
11
,
16
]
, and hence in dimensions
n\geq 4
for metrics with the Myers-Perry asymptotics by our results here. Some care has to be taken in the borderline case
n=4
, since not all statements involving geodesics remain true for
C^{1,1}
metrics. However, when
n>4
the full standard causality theory, including arguments involving geodesics, applies.

Clearly the case
n=3
requires special attention, since the conformal metric is only Hölder continuous at
i_{0}
as constructed here or in
[
2
,
9
]
. Recall that a function
f
is Hölder continuous if there exist constants
C\geq 0
,
\alpha>0
such that
|f(x)-f(y)|\leq C|x-y|^{\alpha}
for all
x
and
y
in the domain of
f
. If
f
and all its partial derivatives up to and including order
k
are Hölder continuous with exponent
\alpha
, then we write
f\in C^{k,\alpha}
. It is not obvious whether or not the constructions can be improved to yield a conformally rescaled metric which is Lipschitz-continuous at
i_{0}
. However, the conformal metric is smooth everywhere except at
i_{0}
. It is then easily seen that the causal bubbles of
[
11
]
do not occur. So, as emphasised in that last reference, standard causality theory, except possibly some arguments involving geodesics, applies also in this case.

This note is organised as follows: We start with a short discussion of three dimensional spacetimes in Section
2
. We continue in Section
3
with the somewhat simpler case of the Schwarzschild metric in all spacetime dimensions
n+1\geq 4
.
The
Myers-Perry metrics are analysed in Section
4
. We show in Section
5
that the ADM mass provides an obstruction to
C^{n-2}
-differentiability in even spacetime dimensions. Asymptotic symmetries of metrics with
C^{n-3,1}
-conformal completions are discussed in Section
6
.

2
Three dimensional spacetimes

Three-dimensional spacetimes are of course special. The requirement that the metric be empty at large spacelike distances imposes flatness. If one assumes the existence of a well-behaved spacelike hypersurface, the space-geometry at large distances is that of a flat cone with a deficit angle. The deficit angle can
a priori
have either sign, but positivity of matter energy implies positivity of this angle (cf., e.g.
[
7
, Section 1.1.1]
). Then
the spacetime metric is flat in the Minkowskian domain of dependence of the angular sector

\{z\in{\mathbb{C}},\ |z|\geq R,\arg z\in[0,\alpha]\}\subset\{t=0\}\,,

(2.1)

with
\alpha\in(0,2\pi)
except if there is no matter, and with
R>0
, where the boundary
\arg z=0
should be identified with the boundary
\arg z=\alpha
.
The Lorentzian Kelvin transformation (
3.3
) with
r^{*}=|z|
leads, after addition of a point
i_{0}=\{t=1/|z|=0\}
, to a conformal completion where the conformally rescaled metric has a deficit angle of
2\pi-\alpha
at
i_{0}
on the spacelike hyperplane
\{\tau=0\}
, and the null conformal boundary at infinity,
{\mycal I}
, is the light-cone emanating from
i_{0}
with the same deficit angle.

4
Myers-Perry metrics

In Kerr-Schild-type coordinates the Myers-Perry metrics take the form
[
17
]

g_{\mu\nu}=\eta_{\mu\nu}+hk_{\mu}k_{\nu}\,,

(4.1)

where

\eta

In the calculations to follow we assume

In order to handle even and odd dimensions simultaneously, we introduce a parameter

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle(x^{0},x^{i})=(t,x_{1},y_{1},\ldots,x_{d},y_{d},\epsilon z)

\displaystyle\big(t,r_{1}\cos(\varphi_{1}),r_{1}\sin(\varphi_{1}),\ldots,r_{d}\cos(\varphi_{d}),r_{d}\sin(\varphi_{d}),r_{d+1}\big)\,,\phantom{XXX}

\displaystyle F

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1-\sum_{i=1}^{d}\frac{a_{i}^{2}r^{2}m_{i}^{2}}{(\hat{r}^{2}+a_{i}^{2})^{2}}=1-\rho^{2}f_{4}\,,

(4.21)

\displaystyle\Pi^{-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{d}(\hat{r}^{2}+a_{i}^{2})^{-1}=\rho^{n-\epsilon}\big(1-\frac{\tau^{2}}{\rho^{2}}\big)^{n}\big(1+\rho^{2}f_{5})\big)\,,

(4.22)

\displaystyle h

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2m\hat{r}^{2-\epsilon}}{\Pi F}=2m\rho^{n-2}\big(1-\frac{\tau^{2}}{\rho^{2}}\big)^{n-2}\big(1+\rho^{2}f_{6})\big)\,,

(4.23)

\displaystyle\tilde{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho^{2}\big(1-\frac{\tau^{2}}{\rho^{2}}\big)\big(dt+\sum_{i=1}^{d}\frac{\hat{r}r_{i}dr_{i}+a_{i}r_{i}^{2}d\varphi_{i}}{\hat{r}^{2}+a_{i}^{2}}+\epsilon\frac{zdz}{\hat{r}}\big)

(4.24)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho^{2}\big(1-\frac{\tau^{2}}{\rho^{2}}\big)\Big(dt+\sum_{i=1}^{d+1}\big(m_{i}d(rm_{i})(1+\rho^{2}f_{7,i})+a_{i}m_{i}^{2}(1+\rho^{2}f_{8,i})d\varphi_{i}\big)\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho^{2}\big(1-\frac{\tau^{2}}{\rho^{2}}\big)\Big(\underbrace{d(t+r)}_{(d\tau-d\rho)(\rho-\tau)^{-2}}+\underbrace{\sum_{i=1}^{d+1}\Big(rm_{i}dm_{i}}_{=0}

\displaystyle+\underbrace{\rho^{2}f_{7,i}m_{i}d(rm_{i})}_{\rho^{3}f_{7,i}m_{i}dm_{i}/(\rho^{2}-\tau^{2})+\rho^{2}f_{7,i}m_{i}^{2}dr}+a_{i}m_{i}^{2}(1+\rho^{2}f_{8,i})d\varphi_{i}\Big)\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1+\frac{\tau}{\rho}}{1-\frac{\tau}{\rho}}(d\tau-d\rho)+\sum_{i=1}^{d+1}\Big(\rho^{3}f_{7,i}m_{i}dm_{i}

\displaystyle+\rho^{2}\big(1-\frac{\tau^{2}}{\rho^{2}}\big)\big(\rho^{2}f_{7,i}m_{i}^{2}dr+a_{i}m_{i}^{2}(1+\rho^{2}f_{8,i})d\varphi_{i}\big)\Big)\,.

Using

dr=\rho^{-2}\big(1-\frac{\tau^{2}}{\rho^{2}}\big)^{-2}\big(\frac{2\tau}{\rho}d\tau-\big(1+\frac{\tau^{2}}{\rho^{2}}\big)d\rho\big)\,,

(4.25)

we can rewrite
\tilde{k}
as

\displaystyle\tilde{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1+\frac{\tau}{\rho}}{1-\frac{\tau}{\rho}}(d\tau-d\rho)+\sum_{i=1}^{d+1}\big(\rho^{3}f_{7,i}m_{i}dm_{i}+a_{i}\rho^{2}m_{i}^{2}f_{9,i}d\varphi_{i}\big)\Big)

(4.26)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{1-\frac{\tau^{2}}{\rho^{2}}}\sum_{i=1}^{d+1}f_{7,i}m_{i}^{2}\big(2\rho\tau d\tau-\big(\rho^{2}+\tau^{2})d\rho\big)\,.

It follows that
h\,\tilde{k}\otimes\tilde{k}
takes the form

\displaystyle h\,\tilde{k}\otimes\tilde{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2m\rho^{n-2}\big(1-\frac{\tau^{2}}{\rho^{2}}\big)^{n-4}\psi_{\mu\nu}dy^{\mu}dy^{\nu}\,,

(4.27)

where
(y^{\mu})\equiv(y^{0},y^{i}):=(\tau,\rho m^{i})
, and where the functions
\psi_{\mu\nu}
satisfy, for all
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n+1}
,

\rho^{\alpha_{0}+\ldots+\alpha_{n}}(\partial_{y^{0}})^{\alpha_{0}}\cdots(\partial_{y^{n}})^{\alpha_{n}}\psi_{\mu\nu}=O(1)\,.

If
n\geq 4
this leads, as in the Schwarzschild case, to a conformally extended metric of differentiability class
C^{n-3,1}
.
When
n=3
the behaviour of this completion at
i_{0}
along spacelike directions is better than in the completion of Section
3
, but is worse when the light cone is approached.

5
The ADM mass as an obstruction to differentiability

So far we have transformed the metric to the form

g=\frac{1}{(\rho^{2}-\tau^{2})^{2}}\tilde{g}\,,

(5.1)

with

\tilde{g}_{\mu\nu}=\eta_{\mu\nu}+{\tilde{h}}_{\mu\nu}\,,\quad\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}{\tilde{h}}_{\mu\nu}=O(\rho\ln\rho),&\hbox{$n=3$,}\\
{\tilde{h}}_{\mu\nu}=O(\rho^{n-2}),&\hbox{$n\geq 4$,}\end{array}\right.

(5.2)

and with
\rho\equiv|\vec{y}|
.
We want to show that our differentiability conditions obtained so far are optimal, in the following sense:

Suppose for contradiction that, in
odd
space dimensions with
n\geq 3
, there exists a coordinate system
(y^{\mu})\equiv(\tau,\vec{y})
in which
{\tilde{h}}_{\mu\nu}=O(\rho^{n-2})
and
{\tilde{h}}_{\mu\nu}
is of differentiability class
C^{n-2}
.
Consider the metric
\gamma:=g_{ij}dx^{i}dx^{j}
induced by
g
on the zero level-set of
\tau
, where the coordinates
x^{i}
are obtained by a Kelvin-inversion from the coordinates
y^{i}
:

x^{i}=\frac{y^{i}}{\rho^{2}}\,.

(5.3)

We find

g_{ij}=\delta_{ij}+{\tilde{h}}_{ij}-4\frac{{\tilde{h}}_{k(i}x^{k}x_{j)}}{r^{2}}+4\frac{{\tilde{h}}_{k\ell}x^{k}x^{\ell}x_{i}x_{j}}{r^{4}}\,,

(5.4)

where for visual convenience we have set
x_{i}:=x^{i}
.
This leads to the following ADM integrand, up to a constant multiplicative factor and the measure on
S^{n-1}
:

\displaystyle\mu

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle U_{i}\frac{x^{i}}{r}\times r^{n-1}:=\big(\frac{\partial g_{ij}}{\partial x^{j}}-\frac{\partial g_{jj}}{\partial x^{i}}\big)\frac{x^{i}}{r}\times r^{n-1}

(5.5)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big(\frac{\partial{\tilde{h}}_{ij}}{\partial x^{j}}-4\frac{\frac{\partial{\tilde{h}}_{k(i}}{\partial x_{j}}x^{k}x_{j)}}{r^{2}}+4\frac{\frac{\partial{\tilde{h}}_{k\ell}}{\partial x_{j}}x^{k}x^{\ell}x_{i}x_{j}}{r^{4}}-\frac{\partial{\tilde{h}}_{jj}}{\partial x^{i}}

\displaystyle-4{\tilde{h}}_{k(i}\partial_{j}\big(\frac{x^{k}x_{j)}}{r^{2}}\big)+4{\tilde{h}}_{k\ell}\partial_{j}\big(\frac{x^{k}x^{\ell}x_{i}x_{j}}{r^{4}}\big)\Big)\frac{x^{i}}{r}\times r^{n-1}\,.

Suppose that
{\tilde{h}}_{ij}
is of differentiability class
C^{n-2}
. Then
{\tilde{h}}_{ij}
is a homogeneous polynomial of order
n-2
in
\vec{y}
up to terms
o(\rho^{n-2})
and
\partial{\tilde{h}}_{ij}/\partial y^{k}
is a homogeneous polynomial of order
n-3
in
\vec{y}
up to terms
o(\rho^{n-3})
.
Using

\frac{\partial{\tilde{h}}_{ij}}{\partial x^{k}}=\frac{\partial{\tilde{h}}_{ij}}{\partial y^{\ell}}\frac{(r^{2}\delta^{\ell}_{k}-2x^{\ell}x^{k})}{r^{4}}=\frac{\partial{\tilde{h}}_{ij}}{\partial y^{\ell}}(\rho^{2}\delta^{l}_{k}-y^{\ell}y^{k})\,,

this implies that the right-hand side of (
5.5
)
is a sum of homogeneous polynomials in
\vec{x}/r
of odd orders, up to
o(1)
terms.

When
n
is odd this implies immediately that the ADM mass vanishes. We recall that the constructions in
[
9
, Section III]
in spacetime dimension
n+1=4
provide completions of the Schwarzschild metric at
i_{0}

either
without
\rho\log\rho
terms
or
without the
(1-\tau^{2}/\rho^{2})\log(1-\tau^{2}/\rho^{2})
terms. It is not known whether such terms can be removed altogether to obtain a
C^{0,1}
metric.

While the above calculations apply for all
n\geq 3
, the vanishing of mass is not true if the spatial dimension,
n
, is even.
As pointed out at the end of Section
3
, in even dimensions the conformal compactification of the Riemannian hypersurfaces of constant
t
, induced by the spacetime compactification of the Schwarzschild metric described there, leads to a real-analytic conformal metric with a real-analytic conformal factor.

6
Asymptotic symmetries

Somewhat more generally, one might wish to consider the collection of metrics with asymptotic behavior captured by (
5.1
) and (
5.2
).

A natural question is then that of the set of coordinate transformations compatible with a conformal compactification of this form; compare
[
19
]
. A detailed analysis of this in four-dimensional spacetimes can be found in
[
9
]
.

First, there are Lorentz transformations in the coordinates
y^{\mu}
, which preserve both the conformal factor
\Omega
and the asymptotic behavior of the metric.

Next, there are coordinate transformations of the form

y^{\mu}\mapsto y^{\mu}+\xi^{\mu}(\tau/\rho,y^{i}/\rho)\rho^{n-1}\,,

(6.1)

with
\xi^{\mu}
a smooth functions of its arguments. These generalise to higher dimensions the usual “supertranslations” at
i_{0}
. There is, however, a key difference: in four spacetime dimensions the Hamiltonian charges associated with the coordinate transformations (
6.1
) are non-zero in general, while in higher dimensions the decay in (
6.1
) is too fast, leading to vanishing Hamiltonian generators.

We note that the four-dimensional
logarithmic ambiguities
,

x^{\mu}\mapsto x^{\mu}+\eta_{\alpha\beta}(C^{\mu}x^{\alpha}-2C^{\alpha}x^{\mu})x^{\beta}\ln|\vec{x}|\,,

(6.2)

with constants
C^{\mu}
(see
[
9
, Theorem 2]
, compare
[
8
,
1
,
5
]
) are absent in higher dimensions.

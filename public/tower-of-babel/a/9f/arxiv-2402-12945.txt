Abstract
This paper examines Federated learning (FL) in a Stochastic Approximation (SA) framework. FL is a collaborative way to train neural network models across various participants or clients without centralizing their data. Each client will train a model on their respective data and send the weights across to a the server periodically for aggregation. The server aggregates these weights which are then used by the clients to re-initialize their neural network and continue the training. SA is an iterative algorithm that uses approximate sample gradients and tapering step size to locate a minimizer of a cost function. In this paper the clients use a stochastic approximation iterate to update the weights of its neural network. It is shown that the aggregated weights track an autonomous ODE. Numerical simulations are performed and the results are compared with standard algorithms like FedAvg and FedProx. It is observed that the proposed algorithm is robust and gives more reliable estimates of the weights, in particular when the clients data are not identically distributed.

This paper provides a stochastic approximation frame work for the Federated learning problem. Under certain assumptions the updates (
I
) are shown to converge, with probability
1
, to the local minimizer of the averaged cost function. If the cost functions for the individual clients are convex then the iterates will converge to the global minimizer of the averaged cost function.

The contributions of this paper are :

1.

A Stochastic approximation frame work has been applied to the Federated learning problem (to the best of author’s knowledge for the first time).

2.

In this framework the iterates are shown to track the solution of an autonomous ODE with the governing function being the average or mean of the cost functions of individual clients.

3.

In cases where ODE is asymptotically stable, the iterates converge to the equilibrium point of the tracked ODE which are the local minimizers.

4.

Numerical simulations suggesting good convergence properties for both identically and non-identically distributed cases are presented.

V-C

Dataset partitioning Strategies

The dataset partitioning strategies define how the training data is distributed over the clients. The following three data partitioning strategies are considered on the above mentioned datasets. The first method (IID) is the Independent and Identical split of data where equal portions of the data is randomly sampled from the training set and assigned to each client. In this split, the data distribution across the client’s are similar. In the second strategy (DIR) the training data for each client is allocated according to Dirichlet distribution (
Dir(\alpha)
)
[
20
]
. In this split varying proportions of data from each class is allocated to the clients. This partitioning simulates a non-identical data distribution across clients. The third technique (SC-DIR) is the recent approach for creating real world non-identical data distributions among the client. In this method, the training data is segregated into multiple disjoint subsets called Super clusters
[
21
]
. Each subset contains data pertaining to some class. Due to the disjoint nature of the subset no two super clusters have overlapping classes. The clients are allowed data only from one of the many super clusters and the allocation of the data is again based on a Dirichlet distribution mentioned in the second method.

V-E

Observations

From Fig

This shows that the proposed algorithms learns the features of the data better than its counterparts. In DIR and SC-DIR splits, the algorithm achieves a similar level of accuracy at a better loss values comparable to that of the other algorithms. Also the proposed method exhibits a notable stability in its performance across the rounds. This consistency in accuracy and loss, suggests that the proposed algorithm is robust in its learning process and has better convergence behaviour when the data across the clients are distributed in a Non-identical fashion.

VI
Conclusion

This paper presented a Federated learning problem in the Stochastic Approximation frame work. In this frame work it is first shown that the iterates of the global model track the solution of an autonomous ODE. Since the autonomous ODE is asymptotically stable, the iterates converge to an equilibrium point which is a local minimizer. Numerical simulations were performed and the results were compared with benchmark algorithms like FedAvg and FedProx. The comparison reveals that the proposed method is more robust and converges to local minima without much oscillations. The future work will be to come up with a convergence rate for the proposed algorithm and also to analyse the presented algorithm with varying step size i.e
a^{(i)}_{n}\neq a^{(j)}_{n}
for two different clients
i
and
j
.

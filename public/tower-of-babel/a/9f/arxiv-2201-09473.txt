Abstract
Integrable delay analogues of the two-dimensional Toda lattice equation are presented and their muti-soliton solutions are constructed by applying the delay reduction to the Gram determinant solution.

Introduction

Delay-differential equations have been used as mathematical models
in various fields of science and engineering, such as traffic flow and infectious diseases.
Studies on integrability and exact solutions of delay-differential equations
have been carried out from mathematical and applied point of view

Integrable delay-differential equations have been obtained by
similarity reductions or symmetry reductions in previous studies,
and it was confirmed that these integrable delay-differential equations exhibit
singularity confinement type behavior which is one of important properties
of discrete integrable systems
[
15
]
.
Villarroel and Ablowitz considered a delay analogue of the
two-dimensional Toda lattice (2DTL) equation and found
the Lax pair and established the inverse scattering transform
[
7
]
.
However multi-soliton solutions have not been known until now.

In this article, we present integrable delay analogues of
the 2DTL equation and construct their multi-soliton
solutions by a delay reduction.
As far as we know,
this is the first time that multi-soliton solutions of
delay-differential equations has been obtained.

2
Delay analogues of the 2DTL equation and their multi-soliton solutions

We consider the 2DTL equation

\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x\partial y}\log(1+V_{n})=V_{n+1}-2V_{n}+V_{n-1}

(1)

which can be written as

\frac{\partial^{2}r_{n}}{\partial x\partial y}=e^{r_{n+1}}-2e^{r_{n}}+e^{r_{n-1}}

(2)

or

\frac{\partial^{2}u_{n}}{\partial x\partial y}=e^{u_{n-1}-u_{n}}-e^{u_{n}-u_{n+1}}

(3)

via the dependent variable transformation
r_{n}=u_{n}-u_{n+1}=\log(1+V_{n})
.
The 2DTL equation (
1
) is transformed into the bilinear equation

D_{x}D_{y}\tau_{n}\cdot\tau_{n}=2(\tau_{n+1}\tau_{n-1}-\tau_{n}^{2})

(4)

via the dependent variable transformation

V_{n}(x,y)=\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x\partial y}\log\tau_{n}=\frac{\tau_{n+1}\tau_{n-1}}{\tau_{n}^{2}}-1\,.

(5)

Here
D_{x}
and
D_{y}
are Hirota’s
D
-operator which is defined as

D_{x}^{m}D_{y}^{n}f\cdot g=(\partial_{x}-\partial_{x^{\prime}})^{m}(\partial_{y}-\partial_{y^{\prime}})^{n}f(x,y)g(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})|_{x=x^{\prime},y=y^{\prime}}\,.

(6)

The Gram determinant form of the
N
-soliton solution to the 2DTL equation (
1
)
is given as follows
[
16
]
:

\displaystyle V_{n}(x,y)=\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x\partial y}\log\tau_{n}=\frac{\tau_{n+1}\tau_{n-1}}{\tau_{n}^{2}}-1\,,

(7)

\displaystyle\tau_{n}=\det\left(\delta_{ij}+\frac{\phi_{i}\psi_{j}}{p_{i}-q_{j}}\right)_{{}_{1\leq i,j\leq N}}

\displaystyle\quad=\frac{\prod_{i=1}^{N}\phi_{i}}{\prod_{j=1}^{N}\phi_{j}}\det\left(\delta_{ij}\frac{\phi_{j}}{\phi_{i}}+\frac{\phi_{j}\psi_{j}}{p_{i}-q_{j}}\right)_{{}_{1\leq i,j\leq N}}

\displaystyle\quad=\det\left(\delta_{ij}+\frac{\phi_{j}\psi_{j}}{p_{i}-q_{j}}\right)_{{}_{1\leq i,j\leq N}},

\displaystyle\phi_{i}=p_{i}^{n}e^{p_{i}x-p_{i}^{-1}y+\phi_{i}^{(0)}}\,,\quad\psi_{i}=q_{i}^{-n}e^{-q_{i}x+q_{i}^{-1}y+\psi_{i}^{(0)}}\,,

\displaystyle\quad(i=1,2,\cdots,N)\,,

where
p_{i},q_{i}
are positive constants and

\phi_{i}^{(0)},\psi_{i}^{(0)}
are real constants.

Applying the delay reduction

V_{n}(x,y)=w(z,y)\,,\qquad z=x+hn\,,

(8)

to the 2DTL equation (
1
),
where
h
is a nonzero real constant,
we obtain the delay 2DTL equation

\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial z\partial y}\log(1+w(z,y))=w(z+h,y)-2w(z,y)+w(z-h,y)\,,

(9)

which can be written as

\frac{\partial^{2}r(z,y)}{\partial z\partial y}=e^{r(z+h,y)}-2e^{r(z,y)}+e^{r(z-h,y)}

(10)

or

\frac{\partial^{2}u(z,y)}{\partial z\partial y}=e^{u(z-h,y)-u(z,y)}-e^{u(z,y)-u(z+h,y)}

(11)

via the dependent variable transformation
r(z,y)=u(z,y)-u(z+h,y)=\log(1+w(z,y))
.
Note that this delay 2DTL equation is a delay partial differential equation.
The delay 2DTL equation (
9
) is transformed into the delay bilinear equation

D_{z}D_{y}f(z,y)\cdot f(z,y)=2(f(z+h,y)f(z-h,y)-f(z,y)^{2})

(12)

via the dependent variable transformation

w(z,y)=\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial z\partial y}\log f(z,y)=\frac{f(z+h,y)f(z-h,y)}{f(z,y)^{2}}-1\,.

(13)

We impose the reduction condition

\log p_{i}-\log q_{i}=h(p_{i}-q_{i})

(14)

to the parameters
p_{i},q_{i}

(i=1,2,\cdots,N)
in the
N
-soliton solution (
7
).
Note that the reduction condition (
14
)
includes the delay parameter
h
.
Then, by setting
z=x+hn
, we have

\displaystyle\phi_{j}\psi_{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle p_{j}^{n}e^{p_{j}x-p_{j}^{-1}y+\phi_{j}^{(0)}}q_{j}^{-n}e^{-q_{j}x+q_{j}^{-1}y+\psi_{j}^{(0)}}

(15)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{p_{j}x-p_{j}^{-1}y+n\log p_{j}+\phi_{j}^{(0)}}e^{-q_{j}x+q_{j}^{-1}y-n\log q_{j}+\psi_{j}^{(0)}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{(p_{j}-q_{j})x+n(\log p_{j}-\log q_{j})-(p_{j}^{-1}-q_{j}^{-1})y+\phi_{j}^{(0)}+\psi_{j}^{(0)}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{(p_{j}-q_{j})x+(p_{j}-q_{j})hn-(p_{j}^{-1}-q_{j}^{-1})y+\phi_{j}^{(0)}+\psi_{j}^{(0)}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{(p_{j}-q_{j})(x+hn)-(p_{j}^{-1}-q_{j}^{-1})y+\phi_{j}^{(0)}+\psi_{j}^{(0)}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{(p_{j}-q_{j})z-(p_{j}^{-1}-q_{j}^{-1})y+\phi_{j}^{(0)}+\psi_{j}^{(0)}}\,.

Thus, by imposing the reduction condition (
14
) and setting
z=x+hn
,

f(z,y)=\tau_{n}(x,y)
,
we obtain the following
N
-soliton solution of the delay 2DTL equation (
9
):

\displaystyle w(z,y)=\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial z\partial y}\log f(z,y)

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt=\frac{f(z+h,y)f(z-h,y)}{f(z,y)^{2}}-1\,,

(16)

\displaystyle f(z,y)=\det\left(\delta_{ij}+\frac{\Phi_{j}(z,y)}{p_{i}-q_{j}}\right)_{{}_{1\leq i,j\leq N}}\,,

\displaystyle\Phi_{j}(z,y)=e^{(p_{j}-q_{j})z-(p_{j}^{-1}-q_{j}^{-1})y+\Phi_{j}^{(0)}}\,,

where
p_{i},q_{i}
must satisfy

\log p_{i}-\log q_{i}=h(p_{i}-q_{i})\,,\quad(i=1,2,\cdots,N)

(17)

and
\Phi_{i}^{(0)}
are real constants.

Next, we consider the delay reduction of the 2DTL equation in a different coordinate and
its one-dimensional reduction.
By the independent variable transformation

x=\frac{t+s}{2}\,,\quad y=\frac{t-s}{2}\,,

(18)

the 2DTL equation (
1
) leads to

\left(\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial t^{2}}-\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial s^{2}}\right)\log(1+V_{n}(t,s))=V_{n+1}(t,s)-2V_{n}(t,s)+V_{n-1}(t,s)\,,

(19)

which can be written as

\frac{\partial^{2}r_{n}}{\partial t^{2}}-\frac{\partial^{2}r_{n}}{\partial y^{2}}=e^{r_{n+1}}-2e^{r_{n}}+e^{r_{n-1}}\,,

(20)

or

\frac{\partial^{2}u_{n}}{\partial t^{2}}-\frac{\partial^{2}u_{n}}{\partial y^{2}}=e^{u_{n-1}-u_{n}}-e^{u_{n}-u_{n+1}}\,.

(21)

via the dependent variable transformation
r_{n}=u_{n}-u_{n+1}=\log(1+V_{n})
.
The
N
-soliton solution of the 2DTL equation (
19
)
is given as follows:

\displaystyle V_{n}(t,s)=\left(\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial t^{2}}-\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial s^{2}}\right)\log\tau_{n}

(22)

\displaystyle\hskip 34.14322pt=\frac{\tau_{n+1}\tau_{n-1}}{\tau_{n}^{2}}-1\,,

\displaystyle\tau_{n}=\det\left(\delta_{ij}+\frac{\phi_{i}\psi_{j}}{p_{i}-q_{j}}\right)_{{}_{1\leq i,j\leq N}}

\displaystyle\quad=\det\left(\delta_{ij}+\frac{\phi_{j}\psi_{j}}{p_{i}-q_{j}}\right)_{{}_{1\leq i,j\leq N}},

\displaystyle\phi_{i}=p_{i}^{n}e^{\frac{1}{2}(p_{i}-p_{i}^{-1})t+\frac{1}{2}(p_{i}+p_{i}^{-1})s+\phi_{i}^{(0)}}\,,

\displaystyle\psi_{i}=q_{i}^{-n}e^{-\frac{1}{2}(q_{i}-q_{i}^{-1})t-\frac{1}{2}(q_{i}+q_{i}^{-1})s+\psi_{i}^{(0)}}\,,

\displaystyle\quad(i=1,2,\cdots,N)\,,

where
p_{i},q_{i}
are positive constants and

\phi_{i}^{(0)},\psi_{i}^{(0)}
are real constants.

Applying the delay reduction

V_{n}(t,s)=w(z,s)\,,\qquad z=t+hn\,,

(23)

to the 2DTL equation (
19
),
we obtain the delay 2DTL equation

\left(\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial z^{2}}-\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial s^{2}}\right)\log(1+w(z,s))=w(z+h,s)-2w(z,s)+w(z-h,s)\,,

(24)

which can be written as

\frac{\partial^{2}r(z,s)}{\partial z^{2}}-\frac{\partial^{2}r(z,s)}{\partial s^{2}}=e^{r(z+h,s)}-2e^{r(z,s)}+e^{r(z-h,s)}

(25)

or

\frac{\partial^{2}u(z,s)}{\partial z^{2}}-\frac{\partial^{2}u(z,s)}{\partial s^{2}}=e^{u(z-h,s)-u(z,s)}-e^{u(z,s)-u(z+h,s)}

(26)

via the dependent variable transformation
r(z,s)=u(z,s)-u(z+h,s)=\log(1+w(z,s))
.
Note that this delay 2DTL equation was considered by Villarroel and Ablowitz,
and they found the Lax pair and established the inverse scattering transform
[
7
]

The delay 2DTL equation (
24
) is transformed into the delay bilinear equation

(D_{z}^{2}-D_{s}^{2})f(z,s)\cdot f(z,s)=2(f(z+h,s)f(z-h,s)-f(z,s)^{2})

(27)

via the dependent variable transformation

w(z,s)=\left(\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial z^{2}}-\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial s^{2}}\right)\log f(z,s)=\frac{f(z+h,s)f(z-h,s)}{f(z,s)^{2}}-1\,.

(28)

We impose the reduction condition

\log p_{i}-\log q_{i}=\frac{h}{2}(p_{i}-p_{i}^{-1}-q_{i}+q_{i}^{-1})

(29)

to the
parameters
p_{i},q_{i}

(i=1,2,\cdots,N)
in the
N
-soliton solution (
22

Then, by setting
z=x+hn
, we have

\displaystyle\hskip-22.76228pt\phi_{j}\psi_{j}=p_{j}^{n}e^{\frac{1}{2}(p_{j}-p_{j}^{-1})t+\frac{1}{2}(p_{j}+p_{j}^{-1})s+\phi_{j}^{(0)}}q_{j}^{-n}e^{-\frac{1}{2}(q_{j}-q_{j}^{-1})t-\frac{1}{2}(q_{j}+q_{j}^{-1})s+\psi_{j}^{(0)}}

\displaystyle=e^{\frac{1}{2}(p_{j}-p_{j}^{-1}-q_{j}+q_{j}^{-1})t+n(\log p_{j}-\log q_{j})+\frac{1}{2}(p_{j}+p_{j}^{-1}-q_{j}-q_{j}^{-1})s+\phi_{j}^{(0)}+\psi_{j}^{(0)}}

\displaystyle=e^{\frac{1}{2}(p_{j}-p_{j}^{-1}-q_{j}+q_{j}^{-1})(t+hn)+\frac{1}{2}(p_{j}+p_{j}^{-1}-q_{j}-q_{j}^{-1})s+\phi_{j}^{(0)}+\psi_{j}^{(0)}}

\displaystyle=e^{\frac{1}{2}(p_{j}-p_{j}^{-1}-q_{j}+q_{j}^{-1})z+\frac{1}{2}(p_{j}+p_{j}^{-1}-q_{j}-q_{j}^{-1})s+\phi_{j}^{(0)}+\psi_{j}^{(0)}}\,.

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt=\frac{f(z+h,s)f(z-h,s)}{f(z,s)^{2}}-1\,,

3
Conclusions

We have considered delay reductions of the 2DTL equation and obtained
the
N
-soliton solution of the delay analogues of the 2DTL equation.
To the best of our knowledge,
this is the first time that multi-soliton solutions of
delay-differential equations has been obtained.
We believe that our result is useful to study delay-differential equations.
In our forthcoming paper, we will propose a systematic method to generate
delay soliton equations having multi-soliton solutions
[
17
]
.

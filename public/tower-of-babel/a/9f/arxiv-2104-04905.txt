L 2 L^{2} -Dolbeault resolution of the lowest Hodge piece of a Hodge module
Junchao Shentu, Chen Zhao

Abstract
. In this paper, we introduce a coherent subsheaf of Saito’s S S -sheaf, which is a combination of the S S -sheaf and the multiplier ideal sheaf. We construct its L 2 L^{2} -Dolbeault resolution, which generalizes MacPherson’s conjecture on the L 2 L^{2} resolution of the Grauert-Riemenschneider sheaf. We also prove various vanishing theorems for the S S -sheaf (Saito’s vanishing theorem, Kawamata-Viehweg vanishing theorem and some new ones like Nadel vanishing theorem) transcendentally. Finally, we discuss some applications of our results on the relative version of Fujita’s conjecture (e.g. Kawamata’s conjecture).

(5)

Assume that

)

\displaystyle R^{i}f_{\ast}(S(IC_{X}(\mathbb{V}),\varphi)\otimes L)=0,\quad\forall i>0.

When

[

[

29

20

1.2.
Application to the relative version of Fujita’s conjecture

As a relative version of Fujita’s conjecture
[
15
]
, Kawamata raised the following conjecture in
[
22
]
with the case
\dim_{\mathbb{C}}X\leq 4
settled therein.

Conjecture 1.4

(Kawamata
[
22
]
)
.

Let
f:Y\to X
be a proper morphism between smooth projective algebraic varieties. Assume that the degenerate loci of
f
is contained in a normal crossing divisor
D\subset X
. Let
L
be an ample line bundle on
X
. Then
R^{q}f_{\ast}\omega_{Y}\otimes L^{\dim X+1}
is generated by global sections for every
0\leq q\leq\dim_{\mathbb{C}}X
.

With the help of the
L^{2}
-Dolbeault resolution (Theorem
1.1
) on
R^{q}f_{\ast}\omega_{Y}
(an example of Saito’s
S
-sheaf), we are able to investigate the separation of jets of
R^{q}f_{\ast}\omega_{Y}\otimes L^{\dim X+1}
using the transendental method developed by Angehrn-Siu
[
2
]
and Demailly
[
11
, Theorem 7.4]
.

Corollary 1.5
.

Let
f:Y\to X
be a proper holomorphic morphism from a Kähler manifold to a projective algebraic variety where
\dim_{\mathbb{C}}X=n
. Assume that the degenerate loci of
f
is contained in a normal crossing divisor
D\subset X
. Let
L
be an ample line bundle on
X
. Assume that there is a positive number
\kappa>0
such that

L^{k}\cdot W\geq\left(\frac{1}{2}n(n+2r-1)+\kappa\right)^{d}

for any irreducible subvariety
W
of dimension
0\leq d\leq n
in
X
. Let
0\leq q\leq n
. Then the global holomorphic sections of
R^{q}f_{\ast}\omega_{Y}\otimes L
separate any set of
r
distinct points
x_{1},\dots,x_{r}\in X
, i.e. there is a surjective map

H^{0}(X,R^{q}f_{\ast}\omega_{Y}\otimes L)\to\bigoplus_{1\leq k\leq r}R^{q}f_{\ast}\omega_{Y}\otimes L\otimes\mathscr{O}_{X,x_{k}}/m_{X,x_{k}}.

A similar result is obtained by Wu
[
57
]
Hodge theoretically. When
f={\rm Id}
, this reduces to the result in
[
2
]
.

By using Demailly’s singular metric on the adjoint bundles (
[
11
, Theorem 7.4]
) we are able obtain the relative version of
[
11
, Theorem 7.4]
.

Corollary 1.6
.

Let
f:Y\to X
be a proper holomorphic morphism from a Kähler manifold to a projective algebraic variety where
\dim_{\mathbb{C}}X=n
. Assume that the degenerate loci of
f
is contained in a normal crossing divisor
D\subset X
. Let
L
be an ample line bundle and
G
a nef line bundle on
X
. Then there is a surjective map

H^{0}(X,R^{q}f_{\ast}\omega_{Y}\otimes\omega_{X}\otimes L^{\otimes m}\otimes G)\to\bigoplus_{1\leq k\leq r}R^{q}f_{\ast}\omega_{Y}\otimes\omega_{X}\otimes L^{\otimes m}\otimes G\otimes\mathscr{O}_{X,x_{k}}/m^{s_{k}+1}_{X,x_{k}}

at arbitrary points
x_{1},\dots,x_{r}\in X
for every
0\leq q\leq\dim_{\mathbb{C}}Y-\dim_{\mathbb{C}}X
,
provided that
m\geq 2+\sum_{1\leq k\leq r}\binom{3n+2s_{k}-1}{n}
.

Since Theorem
1.2
holds for a general Hodge module, we actually prove the analogues of Corollary
1.5
and Corollary
1.6
for Hodge modules (Theorem
7.3
and Theorem
7.4
). We also obtain the results on separating jets with an optimal bound (Theorem
7.5
) when
L
is ample and base point free.

Remark 1.7
.

For a general Hodge module
M
, the semi-simplicity of
M
provides a unique decomposition
M=\bigoplus IC_{Z_{i}}(\mathbb{V}_{i})
where
IC_{Z_{i}}(\mathbb{V}_{i})
is a Hodge module with its strict support an irreducible Zariski closed subset
Z_{i}\subset X
for each
i
. Then
S(M,\varphi)
could be defined as
\bigoplus S(IC_{Z_{i}}(\mathbb{V}_{i}),\varphi|_{Z_{i}})
. The main results in the present paper hold for a general Hodge module as long as they are valid for Hodge modules with strict support. Therefore, we only consider Hodge modules with strict support in the present paper.

Let

The

(1)

and the eigenvalues of the residue operator

lie in

.
Let

denote the open immersion. By

, Theorem 1.1]

\displaystyle R(IC_{X}(\mathbb{V}))=j_{\ast}(S(\mathbb{V}))\cap\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}_{-1},\quad S(\mathbb{V}):=\mathcal{F}^{\max\{k|\mathcal{F}^{k}\neq 0\}}.

Moreover,

(2)

(General case): Let

]

2.2.

(z_{1},\dots,z_{n},w_{1},\dots,w_{n^{\prime}})\mapsto(e^{2\pi\sqrt{-1}z_{1}},\dots,e^{2\pi\sqrt{-1}z_{n}},w_{1},\dots,w_{n^{\prime}})

be the monodromy weight filtrations on

The following important norm estimate for flat sections is proved by Cattani-Kaplan-Schmid in
[
6
, Theorem 5.21]
for the case when

Theorem 2.1
.

For any
0\neq v\in{\rm Gr}_{l_{n}}^{W^{(n)}}\cdots{\rm Gr}_{l_{1}}^{W^{(1)}}V
, one has

\displaystyle|v|_{h_{\mathbb{V}}}\sim\left(\frac{\log|s_{1}|}{\log|s_{2}|}\right)^{l_{1}}\cdots\left(-\log|s_{n}|\right)^{l_{n}}

over any region of the form

\left\{(s_{1},\dots s_{n},w_{1},\dots,w_{n^{\prime}})\in(\Delta^{\ast})^{n}\times\Delta^{n^{\prime}}\bigg|\frac{\log|s_{1}|}{\log|s_{2}|}>\epsilon,\dots,-\log|s_{n}|>\epsilon,(w_{1},\dots,w_{n^{\prime}})\in K\right\}

for any
\epsilon>0
and an arbitrary compact subset

.

Lemma 2.2
.

Assume that

E_{i}:=\{z_{i}=0\},\forall i=1,\dots,r
and denote
j:X^{o}:=X\backslash E\to X
to be the inclusion. Let
\mathbb{V}=(\mathcal{V},\nabla,\mathcal{F}^{\bullet},h_{\mathbb{V}})
be an
\mathbb{R}
-polarized variation of Hodge structure on
X^{o}
and let
\varphi
be a quasi-psh function on
X
which has generalized analytic singularities. Let
{\bf 0}=(0,\dots,0)\in X
and let
\widetilde{v_{1}},\dots,\widetilde{v_{m}}
be an
L^{2}
-adapted frame of
R(IC_{X}(\mathbb{V}))
locally at
{\bf 0}
as in Proposition
2.5
. Let
f_{1},\dots,f_{m}\in(j_{\ast}\mathscr{O}_{X^{o}})_{\bf 0}
. Then

\sum_{i=1}^{m}f_{i}[\widetilde{v_{i}}dz_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge dz_{n}]_{\bf 0}\in S(IC_{X}(\mathbb{V}),\varphi)_{\bf 0}

if and only if

f_{i}\in\mathscr{I}\big(\varphi-\sum_{j=1}^{r}2\alpha_{E_{j}}(\widetilde{v_{i}})\log|z_{j}|\big)_{\bf 0}

for every
i=1,\dots,m
. In conclusion, there is an isomorphism

\displaystyle S(IC_{X}(\mathbb{V}),\varphi)_{\bf 0}\simeq\omega_{X,{\bf 0}}\otimes\bigoplus_{i=1}^{m}\mathscr{I}(\varphi-\sum_{j=1}^{r}2\alpha_{E_{j}}(\widetilde{v_{i}})\log|z_{j}|)_{\bf 0}\widetilde{v_{i}}.

Proof.

Denote
ds^{2}_{0}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}dz_{i}d\bar{z}_{i}
.
Since
\widetilde{v_{1}},\dots,\widetilde{v_{m}}
is an
L^{2}
-adapted frame as in Proposition
2.5
, the integral

\int|\sum_{i=1}^{m}f_{i}\widetilde{v_{i}}dz_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge dz_{n}|^{2}e^{-\varphi}{\rm vol}_{ds^{2}_{0}}=\int|\sum_{i=1}^{m}f_{i}\widetilde{v_{i}}|^{2}e^{-\varphi}{\rm vol}_{ds^{2}_{0}}

is finite near
{\bf 0}
if and only if

\int|f_{i}\widetilde{v_{i}}|^{2}e^{-\varphi}{\rm}{\rm vol}_{ds^{2}_{0}}\sim\int|f_{i}|^{2}e^{-\varphi}{\rm}\prod_{j=1}^{r}|z_{j}|^{2\alpha_{E_{j}}(\widetilde{v_{i}})}\lambda_{i}{\rm vol}_{ds^{2}_{0}}

is finite near
{\bf 0}
for every
i=1,\dots,m
. Here
\lambda_{i}
is a positive real function so that

(4.5)

\displaystyle 1\lesssim\lambda_{i}\lesssim|z_{1}\cdots z_{r}|^{-\epsilon},\quad\forall\epsilon>0.

We are going to show that
|f_{i}\widetilde{v_{i}}|^{2}e^{-\varphi}{\rm}{\rm vol}_{ds^{2}_{0}}
is locally integrable if and only if
f_{i}\in\mathscr{I}\big(\varphi-\sum_{j=1}^{r}2\alpha_{E_{j}}(\widetilde{v_{i}})\log|z_{i}|\big)
.

Since
\varphi
has generalized analytic singularities,
Z:=\{e^{\varphi}=0\}
is a closed analytic subspace. Let
\pi:\widetilde{X}\to X
be a desingularization so that

(1)

\pi
is biholomorphic over
X^{o}\backslash Z^{\prime}
where
Z^{\prime}
is the union of the irreducible components of
Z
that is not a component of
E
.

(2)

\pi^{-1}(Z\cup E)
and
\pi^{-1}(E)
are normal crossing divisors on
\widetilde{X}
.

Let
w_{1},\dots,w_{n}
be holomorphic local coordinates of
\widetilde{X}
such that
\pi^{-1}(Z\cup E)=\{w_{1}\cdots w_{s}=0\}
. Then we obtain that

\displaystyle e^{\pi^{\ast}\varphi}\sim\prod_{i=1}^{s}|w_{i}|^{2a_{i}},\quad\pi^{\ast}\left(\prod_{j=1}^{r}|z_{j}|^{2\alpha_{E_{j}}(\widetilde{v_{i}})}\right)\sim\prod_{i=1}^{s}|w_{i}|^{-2b_{i}}

for some nonnegative constants
a_{1},\dots,a_{s},b_{1},\dots,b_{s}
. Let
ds^{2}_{1}
be a hermitian metric on
\widetilde{X}
. Then

\pi^{\ast}{\rm vol}_{ds^{2}_{0}}\sim\prod_{i=1}^{s}|w_{i}|^{2c_{i}}{\rm vol}_{ds^{2}_{1}}

for some nonnegative constants
c_{1},\dots,c_{s}
.
If
f_{i}\in\mathscr{I}\big(\varphi-\sum_{j=1}^{r}2\alpha_{E_{j}}(\widetilde{v_{i}})\log|z_{j}|\big)
, then

(4.6)

\displaystyle\int\pi^{\ast}\left(|f_{i}|^{2}e^{-\varphi+\sum_{j=1}^{r}2\alpha_{E_{j}}(\widetilde{v_{i}})\log|z_{j}|}{\rm vol}_{ds^{2}_{0}}\right)\sim\int|\pi^{\ast}f_{i}|^{2}\prod_{i=1}^{s}|w_{i}|^{2(-a_{i}-b_{i}+c_{i})}{\rm vol}_{ds^{2}_{1}}

is locally integrable.
Denote

v_{j}(g):=\min\{l|g_{l}\neq 0\textrm{ in the Laurent expansion }g=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}g_{i}w_{j}^{i}\},\quad j=1,\dots,s.

By Lemma
4.6
, the local integrability of (
4.6
) implies that

\displaystyle v_{j}(\pi^{\ast}f_{i})-a_{j}-b_{j}+c_{j}>-1+\epsilon,\quad j=1,\dots,s

for some
\epsilon>0
.
By (
4.5
), we obtain that

\displaystyle\int\pi^{\ast}\left(|f_{i}\widetilde{v_{i}}|^{2}e^{-\varphi}{\rm}{\rm vol}_{ds^{2}_{0}}\right)

\displaystyle\sim\int|\pi^{\ast}f_{i}|^{2}\prod_{j=1}^{s}|w_{j}|^{2(-a_{j}-b_{j}+c_{j})}\pi^{\ast}\lambda_{i}{\rm vol}_{ds^{2}_{1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int|\pi^{\ast}f_{i}|^{2}\prod_{j=1}^{s}|w_{j}|^{2(-a_{j}-b_{j}+c_{j}-\epsilon)}{\rm vol}_{ds^{2}_{1}}

is locally integrable. Since
\pi
is a proper map, we see that
\int|f_{i}\widetilde{v_{i}}|^{2}e^{-\varphi}{\rm}{\rm vol}_{ds^{2}_{0}}
is locally integrable.

Conversely, let
f_{i}
be a holomorphic function on
X^{o}
such that
\int|f_{i}\widetilde{v_{i}}|^{2}e^{-\varphi}{\rm}{\rm vol}_{ds^{2}_{0}}
is locally integrable. We know that

\displaystyle\int|f_{i}|^{2}e^{-\varphi+\sum_{j=1}^{r}2\alpha_{E_{j}}(\widetilde{v_{i}})\log|z_{j}|}{\rm vol}_{ds^{2}_{0}}\lesssim\int|f_{i}|^{2}e^{-\varphi}{\rm}\prod_{j=1}^{r}|z_{j}|^{2\alpha_{E_{j}}(\widetilde{v_{i}})}\lambda_{i}{\rm vol}_{ds^{2}_{0}}\sim\int|f_{i}\widetilde{v_{i}}|^{2}e^{-\varphi}{\rm}{\rm vol}_{ds^{2}_{0}}

is locally integrable by (
4.5
). The proof is finished.
∎

Proposition 4.9
.

Assume that
X
is a complex manifold and
X\backslash X^{o}\subset X
is a (possibly empty) normal crossing divisor. Let
\mathbb{V}
be an
\mathbb{R}
-polarized variation of Hodge structure on

which has unipotent local monodromies. Let

\varphi
be a psh function with generalized analytic singularities along

. Then there is an isomorphism

S(IC_{X}(\mathbb{V}),\varphi)\simeq S(IC_{X}(\mathbb{V}))\otimes\mathscr{I}(\varphi).

Proof.

Since
S(IC_{X}(\mathbb{V}))
is locally free, it suffices to show that

(4.7)

\displaystyle S(IC_{X}(\mathbb{V}),\varphi)=\mathscr{I}(\varphi)S(IC_{X}(\mathbb{V}))

as subsheaves of
j_{\ast}(\omega_{X^{o}}\otimes S(\mathbb{V}))
. Here we regard
S(IC_{X}(\mathbb{V})):=(j_{\ast}(S(\mathbb{V}))\cap\mathcal{V}_{-1})\otimes\omega_{X^{o}}
as a subsheaf of
j_{\ast}(\omega_{X^{o}}\otimes S(\mathbb{V}))
.

Since the problem is local, we assume that
X\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
is a germ of open subset at
{\bf 0}=(0,\dots,0)
with the standard coordinates
z_{1},...,z_{n}
such that
E:=X\backslash X^{o}=\{z_{1}\cdots z_{r}=0\}
. Denote
E_{i}:=\{z_{i}=0\},\forall i=1,\dots,r
. Let
\widetilde{v_{1}},\dots,\widetilde{v_{m}}
be an
L^{2}
-adapted frame of

locally at
{\bf 0}
as in Proposition
2.5
and let
f_{1},\dots,f_{m}\in(j_{\ast}\mathscr{O}_{X^{o}})_{\bf 0}
. Proposition
4.8
tells us that

\sum_{i=1}^{m}f_{i}[\widetilde{v_{i}}dz_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge dz_{n}]_{x}\in S(IC_{X}(\mathbb{V}),\varphi)_{\bf 0}

Since the local monodromies of

,

4.7
).
∎

Theorem 4.10
.

S(IC_{X}(\mathbb{V}),0)\simeq S(IC_{X}(\mathbb{V}))
. In particular,
S(IC_{X}(\mathbb{V}))
is independent of the choice of the desingularization.

Proof.

Let
\widetilde{X}\to X
be a desingularization so that
\pi
is biholomorphic over
X^{o}
and
\pi^{-1}(X\backslash X^{o})
is a simple normal crossing divisor. We claim that

(4.8)

\displaystyle S(IC_{\widetilde{X}}(\pi^{\ast}\mathbb{V}),0)\simeq S(IC_{\widetilde{X}}(\pi^{\ast}\mathbb{V})).

If the claim is true, it follows from Proposition
4.3
that

S(IC_{X}(\mathbb{V}),0)\simeq\pi_{\ast}S(IC_{\widetilde{X}}(\pi^{\ast}\mathbb{V}),0)\simeq\pi_{\ast}S(IC_{\widetilde{X}}(\pi^{\ast}\mathbb{V}))\simeq S(IC_{X}(\mathbb{V})).

Now it suffices to show (
4.8
). Since the problem is local, we assume that
\widetilde{X}=\Delta^{n}
is the polydisc with the standard holomorphic coordinates
z_{1},\dots,z_{n}
such that
E:=\pi^{-1}(X\backslash X^{o})=\{z_{1}\cdots z_{r}=0\}
. Let
j:\widetilde{X}\backslash E\to\widetilde{X}
denote the inclusion and denote
E_{i}:=\{z_{i}=0\},\forall i=1,\dots,r
. Let
{\bf 0}=(0,\dots,0)\in\widetilde{X}
and let
\widetilde{v_{1}},\dots,\widetilde{v_{m}}
be an
L^{2}
-adapted frame of
R(IC_{\widetilde{X}}(\pi^{\ast}\mathbb{V}))
locally at
{\bf 0}
as in Proposition
2.5
. Let
f_{1},\dots,f_{m}\in(j_{\ast}\mathscr{O}_{\pi^{-1}(X^{o})})_{\bf 0}
. Proposition
4.8
shows that

\sum_{i=1}^{m}f_{i}[\widetilde{v_{i}}dz_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge dz_{n}]_{\bf 0}\in S(IC_{X}(\mathbb{V}),0)_{\bf 0}

if and only if

f_{i}\in\mathscr{I}\big(-\sum_{j=1}^{r}2\alpha_{E_{j}}(\widetilde{v_{i}})\log|z_{j}|\big)_{\bf 0}

for every
i=1,\dots,m
. Since
\alpha_{E_{j}}(\widetilde{v_{i}})\in(-1,0],\forall j=1,\dots,r
, Lemma
4.6
shows that

{\rm exp}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{r}2\alpha_{E_{j}}(\widetilde{v_{i}})\log|z_{j}|\right)
is integrable. Hence

\displaystyle\mathscr{I}\big(-\sum_{j=1}^{r}2\alpha_{E_{j}}(\widetilde{v_{i}})\log|z_{j}|\big)_{\bf 0}\simeq\mathscr{O}_{\widetilde{X},\bf 0},\quad i=1,\dots,m.

The proof of the theorem is finished.
∎

Proposition 4.11
.

S(IC_{X}(\mathbb{V}),\varphi)
is a coherent subsheaf of
S(IC_{X}(\mathbb{V}))
for an arbitrary general quasi-psh function
\varphi
.

Proof.

First we show that
S(IC_{X}(\mathbb{V}),\varphi)\subset S(IC_{X}(\mathbb{V}))
. By Theorem
4.10
, it is equivalent to show that

(4.9)

\displaystyle S(IC_{X}(\mathbb{V}),\varphi)\subset S(IC_{X}(\mathbb{V}),0).

Since
\varphi
is quasi-psh, it has an upper bound
c_{x}
locally at every point
x\in X
. Thus we have

\int|\alpha|^{2}{\rm vol}\sim\int|\alpha|^{2}e^{-c_{x}}{\rm vol}\leq\int|\alpha|^{2}e^{-\varphi}{\rm vol}

locally at
x
.
This proves (
4.9
).

Next we prove its coherence. Let
\pi:\widetilde{X}\to X
be a desingularization so that
\pi
is biholomorphic over
X^{o}
and
E:=\pi^{-1}(X\backslash X^{o})
is a simple normal crossing divisor. By abuse of notations we regard
X^{o}\subset\widetilde{X}
as a subset. Denote
\psi=\pi^{\ast}\varphi
. Then
\psi
is a quasi-psh function. There is an isomorphism

\displaystyle S(IC_{X}(\mathbb{V}),\varphi)\simeq\pi_{\ast}\left(S(IC_{\widetilde{X}}(\mathbb{V}),\psi)\right)

by Proposition
4.3
.
Since
\pi
is proper, it suffices to show that
S(IC_{\widetilde{X}}(\mathbb{V}),\psi)
is a coherent sheaf on
\widetilde{X}
. Since the problem is local and
\widetilde{X}
is smooth, we may assume that
\widetilde{X}\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
is the unit ball so that
E=\{z_{1}\cdots z_{s}=0\}
and
\psi
has an upper bound. Denote
E_{i}=\{z_{i}=0\},\forall i=1,\dots,s
.
Notice that there is a complete Kähler metric on
X^{o}
by Lemma
3.4
. Since
S(IC_{\widetilde{X}}(\mathbb{V}))
is coherent, the space
\Gamma(\widetilde{X},S(IC_{\widetilde{X}}(\mathbb{V}),\psi))
generates a coherent subsheaf
\mathscr{J}
of
S(IC_{\widetilde{X}}(\mathbb{V}))
by the strong Noetherian property. By the construction we have the inclusion

\mathscr{J}\subset S(IC_{\widetilde{X}}(\mathbb{V}),\psi)
. It remains to prove the converse. By Krull’s theorem (
[
3
, Corollary 10.19]
), it suffices to show that

(4.10)

\displaystyle\mathscr{J}_{x}+S(IC_{\widetilde{X}}(\mathbb{V}),\psi)_{x}\cap m_{\widetilde{X},x}^{k+1}S(IC_{\widetilde{X}}(\mathbb{V}))=S(IC_{\widetilde{X}}(\mathbb{V}),\psi)_{x},\quad\forall k\geq 0.

Let
\alpha\in S(IC_{\widetilde{X}}(\mathbb{V}),\psi)_{x}
be defined in a precompact neighborhood
V
of
x
. Choose a
C^{\infty}
cut-off function
\lambda
so that
\lambda\equiv 1
near
x
and
{\rm supp}\lambda\subset V
. Denote
|z|^{2}:=\sum_{i=1}^{n}|z_{i}|^{2}
. Since
\psi
is quasi-psh, there is a constant
c>0
such that
\psi(z)+c|z|^{2}
is psh.
Let

\displaystyle\psi_{k}(z):=\psi(z)+2(n+k+1)\log|z-x|+(c+1)|z|^{2}

and
h_{\psi_{k}}=e^{-\psi_{k}}h_{\mathbb{V}}
. Denote
\omega_{0}:=\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}|z|^{2}
.
By Theorem
2.3
, we have

\displaystyle\sqrt{-1}\Theta_{h_{\psi_{k}}}(S(\mathbb{V}))=\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}\psi_{k}+\sqrt{-1}\Theta_{h_{\mathbb{V}}}(S(\mathbb{V}))\geq\omega_{0}.

Since
{\rm supp}\lambda\alpha\subset V
and
\bar{\partial}(\lambda\alpha)=0
near
x
, we have

\displaystyle\|\bar{\partial}(\lambda\alpha)\|^{2}_{\omega_{0},h_{\psi_{k}}}\sim\|\bar{\partial}(\lambda\alpha)\|^{2}_{\omega_{0},h_{\psi}}\leq\|\bar{\partial}\lambda\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}}\|\alpha\|^{2}_{\omega_{0},h_{\psi}}+|\lambda|^{2}\|\bar{\partial}\alpha\|^{2}_{\omega_{0},h_{\psi}}<\infty

Hence, Theorem
3.3
provides a solution of the equation
\bar{\partial}\beta=\bar{\partial}(\lambda\alpha)
so that

(4.11)

\displaystyle\|\beta\|^{2}_{\omega_{0},h_{\psi}}\lesssim\int_{X^{o}}|\beta|^{2}_{\omega_{0},h_{\mathbb{V}}}e^{-\psi}|z-x|^{-2(n+k+1)}{\rm vol}_{\omega_{0}}\lesssim\|\bar{\partial}(\lambda\alpha)\|^{2}_{\omega_{0},h_{\psi_{k}}}<\infty.

Thus
\gamma=\lambda\alpha-\beta
is holomorphic and
\gamma\in\Gamma(\widetilde{X},S(IC_{\widetilde{X}}(\mathbb{V}),\psi))
.
Using the notations in §
2
, we have

\displaystyle S(IC_{\widetilde{X}}(\mathbb{V}))=\left(j_{\ast}(S(\mathbb{V}))\cap\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}_{-1}\right)\otimes\omega_{\widetilde{X}}

where
j:X^{o}\to\widetilde{X}
is the open immersion.
Since
\psi
has an upper bound, we have

\|\beta\|^{2}_{\omega_{0},h_{\mathbb{V}}}\lesssim\int_{X^{o}}|\beta|^{2}_{\omega_{0},h_{\mathbb{V}}}|z-x|^{-2(n+k+1)}{\rm vol}_{\omega_{0}}\lesssim\int_{X^{o}}|\beta|^{2}_{\omega_{0},h_{\mathbb{V}}}e^{-\psi}|z-x|^{-2(n+k+1)}{\rm vol}_{\omega_{0}}<\infty.

Hence
\beta\in S(IC_{\widetilde{X}}(\mathbb{V}))\subset\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}_{-1}
by Theorem
4.10
.

Let
\widetilde{{\bf v}}=(\widetilde{v}_{1},\dots,\widetilde{v}_{r})
be the local frame of
R(IC_{\widetilde{X}}(\mathbb{V}))\subset\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}_{-1}
where
{\bf v}=e^{-\sum_{i}2\pi\sqrt{-1}z_{i}{\rm Res}_{E_{i}}\widetilde{\nabla}}\widetilde{{\bf v}}
is an orthogonal basis of
\mathbb{V}
(c.f. §
2.3
). Then there are holormorphic functions
f_{1},\dots,f_{r}\in\mathscr{O}_{\widetilde{X}}(X^{o})
such that

\beta=\sum_{i=1}^{r}f_{i}\widetilde{v}_{i}dz_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge dz_{n}.

It follows from Theorem
2.1
that

\displaystyle(z_{1}\cdots z_{s})^{\epsilon}\lesssim|v_{i}|,\quad\forall\epsilon>0,\forall i=1,\dots,r.

Thus one gets that

\displaystyle\int_{X^{o}}\sum_{i=1}^{r}|f_{i}|^{2}(z_{1}\cdots z_{r})^{2\epsilon}|z-x|^{-2(n+k+1)}{\rm vol}_{\omega_{0}}\lesssim\int_{X^{o}}|\beta|^{2}_{\omega_{0},h_{\mathbb{V}}}|z-x|^{-2(n+k+1)}{\rm vol}_{\omega_{0}}<\infty

for every
\epsilon>0
.
This implies that
f_{i}\in m_{\widetilde{X},x}^{k+1}
for every
i=1,\dots,r
(
[
11
, Lemma 5.6]
). Hence

\alpha_{x}-\gamma_{x}=\beta_{x}\in m_{\widetilde{X},x}^{k+1}S(IC_{\widetilde{X}}(\mathbb{V})).

On the other hand,
\beta\in S(IC_{\widetilde{X}}(\mathbb{V}),\psi)
because of (
4.11
).
This proves (
4.10
).
∎

Lemma 4.12
.

Let
(E,h_{\varphi})
be a holomorphic vector bundle on
X
with a possibly singular hermitian metric
h_{\varphi}=e^{-\varphi}h_{0}
. Then

S(IC_{X}(\mathbb{V}),\varphi)\otimes E\simeq S_{X}(S(\mathbb{V})\otimes E,h_{\mathbb{V}}\otimes h_{\varphi})
.

Proof.

Let
x\in X
be a point and let
U
be an open neighborhood of
x
so that
E|_{U}\simeq\mathscr{O}_{U}^{\oplus r}
and
h_{0}
is quasi-isometric to the trivial metric, i.e.

|\sum_{i=1}^{r}a_{i}e_{i}|^{2}_{h_{0}}\sim\sum_{i=1}^{r}|a_{i}|^{2}

where
\{e_{i}\}
is the standard frame of
\mathscr{O}_{U}^{\oplus r}
and
a_{i}
s are measurable functions on
U
.
Let
ds^{2}
be an arbitrary hermitian metric on
X^{o}
and let
\alpha=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\alpha_{i}\otimes e_{i}
be a measurable section of
S(\mathbb{V})\otimes E|_{U\cap X^{o}}
. Then

\|\alpha\|^{2}_{ds^{2},h_{\mathbb{V}}\otimes h_{\varphi}}\sim\sum_{i=1}^{r}\|\alpha_{i}\|^{2}_{ds^{2},e^{-\varphi}h_{\mathbb{V}}}

is finite if and only if each
\|\alpha_{i}\|^{2}_{ds^{2},e^{-\varphi}h_{\mathbb{V}}}
is finite. This proves the lemma.
∎

S(IC_{X}(\mathbb{V}),\varphi)
.

Proposition 4.13
.

Let
\varphi
and
\psi
be quasi-psh functions on
X
which have generalized analytic singularities. Then

S(IC_{X}(\mathbb{V}),\varphi)=S(IC_{X}(\mathbb{V}),\varphi+\epsilon\psi),\quad 0<\epsilon\ll 1.

Proof.

Since the problem is local, we assume that
X
is a germ of complex space.
Since
\varphi
and
\psi
have generalized analytic singularities,
Z_{1}:=\{e^{\varphi}=0\}
and
Z_{2}:=\{e^{\psi}=0\}
are closed analytic subspaces. Let
\pi:\widetilde{X}\to X
be a desingularization so that

(1)

\pi
is biholomorphic over
X^{o}\backslash(Z_{1}\cup Z_{2})
;

(2)

\pi^{-1}(Z_{1}\cup Z_{2}\cup(X\backslash X^{o}))
and
\pi^{-1}(X\backslash X^{o})
are normal crossing divisors of
\widetilde{X}
.

Let
w_{1},\dots,w_{n}
be holomorphic local coordinates on
\widetilde{X}
such that
\pi^{-1}(Z_{1}\cup Z_{2}\cup(X\backslash X^{o}))=\{w_{1}\cdots w_{s}=0\}
and
\pi^{-1}(X\backslash X^{o})=\{w_{1}\cdots w_{r}=0\}
with
0\leq r\leq s
. Notice that
r=0
if we consider the problem on
X^{o}
. Denote
E_{i}=\{z_{i}=0\},\forall i=1,\dots,r
. Then we know that

\displaystyle e^{\pi^{\ast}\varphi}\sim\prod_{i=1}^{s}|w_{i}|^{2a_{i}},\quad e^{\pi^{\ast}\psi}\sim\prod_{i=1}^{s}|w_{i}|^{2b_{i}}

for some nonnegative constants
a_{1},\dots,a_{s},b_{1},\dots,b_{s}
.
Denote
j:\pi^{-1}(X^{o})\to\widetilde{X}
to be the open immersion. Let
\widetilde{v_{1}},\dots,\widetilde{v_{m}}
be an
L^{2}
-adapted frame of
R(IC_{\widetilde{X}}(\pi^{\ast}\mathbb{V}))
locally at
{\bf 0}=(0,\dots,0)\in\widetilde{X}
as in Proposition
2.5
. Let
f_{1},\dots,f_{m}\in(j_{\ast}\mathscr{O}_{\pi^{-1}(X^{o})})_{\bf 0}
and
\alpha=\sum_{i=1}^{m}f_{i}\widetilde{v_{i}}dz_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge dz_{n}
. Then Proposition
4.8
shows that

[\alpha]_{x}\in S(IC_{\widetilde{X}}(\pi^{\ast}\mathbb{V}),\pi^{\ast}\varphi)_{\bf 0}\quad\left(\textrm{resp. }[\alpha]_{x}\in S(IC_{\widetilde{X}}(\pi^{\ast}\mathbb{V}),\pi^{\ast}\varphi+\pi^{\ast}\psi)_{\bf 0}\right)

if and only if

f_{i}\in\mathscr{I}\big(\pi^{\ast}\varphi-\sum_{j=1}^{r}2\alpha_{E_{j}}(\widetilde{v_{i}})\log|w_{j}|\big)_{\bf 0}\quad\bigg(\textrm{resp. }f_{i}\in\mathscr{I}\big(\pi^{\ast}\varphi+\pi^{\ast}\psi-\sum_{j=1}^{r}2\alpha_{E_{j}}(\widetilde{v_{i}})\log|w_{j}|\big)_{\bf 0}\bigg)

for every
i=1,\dots,m
. Hence
[\alpha]_{x}\in S(IC_{\widetilde{X}}(\pi^{\ast}\mathbb{V}),\pi^{\ast}\varphi)_{\bf 0}
is equivalent to that the integral

\displaystyle\int|f_{i}|^{2}\prod_{j=1}^{s}|w_{j}|^{-2a_{j}}\prod_{j=1}^{r}|w_{j}|^{2\alpha_{E_{j}}(\widetilde{v_{i}})}{\rm vol}_{ds^{2}_{0}}

is finite near
{\bf 0}
for every
i=1,\dots,m
. Denote

v_{j}(f):=\min\{l|f_{l}\neq 0\textrm{ in the Laurent expansion }f=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}f_{i}w_{j}^{i}\}.

By Lemma
4.6
, we observe that

f\in\mathscr{I}\big(\pi^{\ast}\varphi-\sum_{j=1}^{r}2\alpha_{E_{j}}(\widetilde{v_{i}})\log|w_{j}|\big)

if and only if

(4.12)

\displaystyle v_{j}(f)-a_{j}+\alpha_{E_{j}}(\widetilde{v_{i}})>-1,\quad\forall j=1,\dots,s.

Here we set
\alpha_{E_{j}}(\widetilde{v_{i}})=0,\forall j=r+1,\dots,s
.

Similar arguments show that

f\in\mathscr{I}\big(\pi^{\ast}(\varphi+\epsilon\psi)-\sum_{j=1}^{r}2\alpha_{E_{j}}(\widetilde{v_{i}})\log|w_{j}|\big)

if and only if

(4.13)

\displaystyle v_{j}(f)-a_{j}-\epsilon b_{j}+\alpha_{E_{j}}(\widetilde{v_{i}})>-1,\quad\forall j=1,\dots,s.

Conditions (
4.12
) and (
4.13
) are equivalent when
\epsilon>0
is small enough. We obtain therefore that

S(IC_{\widetilde{X}}(\pi^{\ast}\mathbb{V}),\pi^{\ast}\varphi)\simeq S(IC_{\widetilde{X}}(\pi^{\ast}\mathbb{V}),\pi^{\ast}(\varphi+\epsilon\psi)),\quad 0<\epsilon\ll 1.

By Proposition
4.3
,

S(IC_{X}(\mathbb{V}),\varphi)\simeq\pi_{\ast}(S(IC_{\widetilde{X}}(\pi^{\ast}\mathbb{V}),\pi^{\ast}\varphi))\simeq\pi_{\ast}(S(IC_{\widetilde{X}}(\pi^{\ast}\mathbb{V}),\pi^{\ast}(\varphi+\epsilon\psi)))\simeq S(IC_{X}(\mathbb{V}),\varphi+\epsilon\psi)

when
\epsilon>0
is small enough.
∎

4.3.
Extension and adjunction

In this section we consider the extension and adjunction properties for
S(IC_{X}(\mathbb{V}),\varphi)
. These results will be used in proving the Demailly-Kawamata-Viehweg vanishing theorem for Saito’s
S
-sheaf
S(IC_{X}(\mathbb{V}))
(Corollary
6.2
).

Theorem 4.14

(Ohsawa-Takegoshi extension theorem for
S(IC_{X}(\mathbb{V}),\varphi)
)
.

Let
X
be a complex space and
\Omega\subset X_{\rm reg}
a Zariski open subset which is a Stein manifold. Let
\mathbb{V}
be an
\mathbb{R}
-polarized variation of Hodge structure on some Zariski open subset
X^{o}\subset X_{\rm reg}
. Let
H\subset X
be a Cartier divisor such that
\mathscr{O}_{X}(H)\simeq\mathscr{O}_{X}
and
H_{\rm reg}\cap\Omega\cap X^{o}
is dense in
H_{\rm reg}
. Let
(E,h_{\varphi})
be a vector bundle on
X
with a singular hermitian metric
h_{\varphi}=e^{-\varphi}h_{0}
. Assume that
\varphi
is smooth over some Zariski open subset of
X
and
\sqrt{-1}\Theta_{h_{\varphi}}(E)\geq 0
as a current. Let
h_{H}
be a smooth hermitian metric on
\mathscr{O}_{X}(H)
with zero curvature. Then there is a constant
C>0
such that, for every
\alpha\in\Gamma(H,S(IC_{H}(\mathbb{V}|_{H\cap X^{o}}),\varphi|_{H})\otimes E|_{H}\otimes\mathscr{O}_{X}(H)|_{H})
satisfying that
\|\alpha\|_{h_{\mathbb{V}}h_{\varphi}h_{H}}<\infty
, there is
\beta\in\Gamma(X,S(IC_{X}(\mathbb{V}),\varphi)\otimes E)
which satisfies that
\beta|_{H}=\alpha
and
\|\beta\|_{h_{\mathbb{V}}h_{\varphi}}\leq C\|\alpha\|_{h_{\mathbb{V}}h_{\varphi}h_{H}}
.

Proof.

Assume that
\varphi
is smooth over a Zariski open subset
U\subset X
. Let
H^{\prime}
be a Cartier divisor of
\Omega
so that
Y:=\Omega\backslash H^{\prime}\subset X^{o}\cap U
and
Y
is a Stein manifold. Let

\alpha\in\Gamma(H,S(IC_{H}(\mathbb{V}|_{H\cap X^{o}}),\varphi|_{H})\otimes E|_{H}\otimes\mathscr{O}_{X}(H)|_{H})

such that
\|\alpha\|_{h_{\mathbb{V}}h_{\varphi}h_{H}}<\infty
. By the Ohsawa-Takegoshi extension theorem
[
37
]
(see also
[
17
, Theorem 2.2]
), there is
\beta\in\Gamma(Y,K_{Y}\otimes S(\mathbb{V}|_{Y})\otimes E|_{Y})
such that
\beta|_{Y\cap H}=\alpha|_{Y\cap H}
and
\|\beta\|_{Y,h_{\mathbb{V}}h_{\varphi}}\leq C\|\alpha|_{Y\cap H}\|_{h_{\mathbb{V}}h_{\varphi}h_{H}}
for some constant
C>0
. It follows from Lemma
4.12
that

\beta\in\Gamma(X,S(IC_{X}(\mathbb{V}|_{Y}),\varphi)\otimes E)=\Gamma(X,S(IC_{X}(\mathbb{V}),\varphi)\otimes E).

Since
\alpha
and
\beta
are both holomorphic sections, we get
\beta|_{H}=\alpha
.
∎

An immediate consequence of the extension theorem is the following

Corollary 4.15

(Restriction Formula)
.

Let
X
be a complex space and
X^{o}\subset X_{\rm reg}
a Zariski open subset. Let
\mathbb{V}
be an
\mathbb{R}
-polarized variation of Hodge structure on some Zariski open subset
X^{o}\subset X_{\rm reg}
. Let
\varphi
be a quasi-psh function on
X
which is smooth over some Zariski open subset of
X
. Let
H
be a reduced Cartier divisor of
X
such that
H_{\rm reg}\cap X^{o}
is dense in
H_{\rm reg}
. Then

(4.14)

\displaystyle S(IC_{H}(\mathbb{V}|_{H\cap X^{o}}),\varphi|_{H})\subset S(IC_{X}(\mathbb{V}),\varphi)|_{H}\otimes\mathscr{O}_{X}(H)|_{H}

as subsheaves of
S(IC_{X}(\mathbb{V}),0)|_{H}\otimes\mathscr{O}_{X}(H)|_{H}
.

Proof.

By Theorem
4.14
, there is an inclusion

S(IC_{H}(\mathbb{V}|_{H\cap X^{o}}),\varphi|_{H})\subset S_{X}(S(\mathbb{V})\otimes\mathscr{O}_{X}(H),h_{\mathbb{V}}\otimes e^{-\varphi}h_{0})|_{H}

where
h_{0}
is an arbitrary smooth hermitian metric on
\mathscr{O}_{X}(H)
. By Lemma
4.12
there is an isomorphism

S_{X}(S(\mathbb{V})\otimes\mathscr{O}_{X}(H),h_{\mathbb{V}}\otimes e^{-\varphi}h_{0})\simeq S(IC_{X}(\mathbb{V}),\varphi)\otimes\mathscr{O}_{X}(H).

The corollary is proved.
∎

Generally, the inclusion (
4.14
) is strict even if
\varphi=0
. From the perspective of Hodge modules,
IC_{X}(\mathbb{V})|_{H}
could be a mixed Hodge module while
IC_{H}(\mathbb{V}|_{H\cap X^{o}})
is pure. When
\varphi
has generalized analytic singularities, (
4.14
) is an equality if
H
is in a general position.

Proposition 4.16
.

Let
X
be a projective algebraic variety and let
\mathbb{V}
be an
\mathbb{R}
-polarized variation of Hodge structure on some Zariski open subset
X^{o}\subset X_{\rm reg}
. Let
\varphi
be a quasi-psh function on
X
which has generalized analytic singularities. Let
\Lambda
be a free linear system on
X
. Then there is a canonical isomorphism

S(IC_{X}(\mathbb{V}),\varphi)|_{H}\otimes\mathscr{O}_{X}(H)|_{H}=S(IC_{H}(\mathbb{V}|_{H\cap X^{o}}),\varphi|_{H})

for a general
H\in\Lambda
.

Proof.

Let
\pi:\widetilde{X}\to X
be a desingularization so that
E:=\pi^{-1}(X\backslash X^{o})
is a simple normal crossing divisor and
\pi
is biholomorphic over
X^{o}
. Let
H
be in a general position so that
\widetilde{H}:=\pi^{\ast}H
is smooth and intersects transversally with every stratum of
E
.
By Proposition
4.3
, it suffices to show that

(4.15)

\displaystyle S(IC_{\widetilde{X}}(\pi^{\ast}\mathbb{V}),\pi^{\ast}\varphi)|_{\widetilde{H}}\otimes\mathscr{O}_{\widetilde{X}}(\widetilde{H})|_{\widetilde{H}}=S(IC_{\widetilde{H}}(\pi^{\ast}\mathbb{V}|_{\pi^{\ast}(H\cap X^{o})}),\pi^{\ast}\varphi|_{\widetilde{H}})

for a general
H\in\Lambda
.

Since the problem is local, we assume that
\widetilde{X}=\Delta^{n}
is a polydisc with the standard holomorphic coordinates
z_{1},\dots,z_{n}
such that
E=\{z_{1}\cdots z_{r}=0\}
. Denote
E_{i}=\{z_{i}=0\}
,
\forall i=1,\dots,r
. Let
(\widetilde{v_{1}},\dots,\widetilde{v_{m}})
be an
L^{2}
-adapted frame of
R(IC_{X}(\mathbb{V}))
as in Proposition
2.5
. Denote

\psi_{i}:=-\sum_{j=1}^{r}\alpha_{E_{j}}(\widetilde{v_{i}})\log|z_{j}|^{2}.

By Proposition
4.8
, we see that

\displaystyle S(IC_{\widetilde{X}}(\pi^{\ast}\mathbb{V}),\pi^{\ast}\varphi)\simeq\omega_{\widetilde{X}}\otimes\bigoplus_{i=1}^{m}\mathscr{I}(\pi^{\ast}\varphi+\psi_{i})\widetilde{v_{i}},

\displaystyle S(IC_{\widetilde{H}}(\pi^{\ast}\mathbb{V}|_{\pi^{\ast}(H\cap X^{o})})),\pi^{\ast}\varphi|_{\widetilde{H}})\simeq\omega_{{\widetilde{H}}}\otimes\bigoplus_{i=1}^{m}\mathscr{I}(\pi^{\ast}\varphi|_{\widetilde{H}}+\psi_{i}|_{\widetilde{H}})\widetilde{v_{i}}|_{\widetilde{H}}

and

\mathscr{I}(\pi^{\ast}\varphi+\psi_{i})|_{\widetilde{H}}\simeq\mathscr{I}(\pi^{\ast}\varphi|_{\widetilde{H}}+\psi_{i}|_{\widetilde{H}})

since
H
is in a general position.
Consequently, (
4.15
) is obtained.
∎

5.
L^{2}
-Dolbeault resolution of multiplier
S
-sheaf

The purpose of this section is to prove Theorem
1.1
.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
X
be a complex space of dimension
n
and
ds^{2}
a hermitian metric on a Zariski open subset
X^{o}\subset X_{\rm reg}
with
\omega
its fundamental form. Let
\mathbb{V}=(\mathcal{V},\nabla,\mathcal{F}^{\bullet},h_{\mathbb{V}})
be an
\mathbb{R}
-polarized variation of Hodge structure on
X^{o}
. Let
(E,h_{\varphi})
be a holomorphic vector bundle on
X
with a (possibly) singular hermitian metric
h_{\varphi}:=e^{-\varphi}h_{0}
. Assume that, locally at every point
x\in X
, there is a neighborhood
U
of
x
, a strictly psh function
\lambda\in C^{2}(U)
and a bounded psh function
\Phi\in C^{2}(U\cap X^{o})
such that
\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}\lambda|_{U\cap X^{o}}\lesssim\omega|_{U\cap X^{o}}\lesssim\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}\Phi
.
Then the canonical map

(5.1)

\displaystyle S(IC_{X}(\mathbb{V}),\varphi)\otimes E\to\mathscr{D}^{n,\bullet}_{X,ds^{2}}(S(\mathbb{V})\otimes E,h_{\mathbb{V}}\otimes h_{\varphi})

is a quasi-isomorphism.
If
X
is moreover compact, then there is an isomorphism

\displaystyle H^{q}(S(IC_{X}(\mathbb{V}),\varphi)\otimes E)\simeq H^{n,q}_{(2),\rm max}(X^{o},S(\mathbb{V})\otimes E|_{X^{o}};ds^{2},h_{\mathbb{V}}\otimes h_{\varphi}),\quad\forall q.

Proof.

By Lemma
4.12
we have

S(IC_{X}(\mathbb{V}),\varphi)\otimes E\simeq{\rm Ker}\left(\mathscr{D}^{n,0}_{X,ds^{2}}(S(\mathbb{V})\otimes E,h_{\mathbb{V}}\otimes h_{\varphi})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\bar{\partial}}}{{\to}}\mathscr{D}^{n,1}_{X,ds^{2}}(S(\mathbb{V})\otimes E,h_{\mathbb{V}}\otimes h_{\varphi})\right).

It remains to show that (
5.1
) is exact at
\mathscr{D}^{n,q}_{X,ds^{2}}(S(\mathbb{V})\otimes E,h_{\mathbb{V}}\otimes h_{\varphi})
for
q>0
. Since the problem is local, we consider a point
x\in X
and an open neighborhood
U
of
x
such that
E|_{U}\simeq\mathscr{O}_{U}^{\oplus{\rm rk}E}
and
h_{0}
is quasi-isometric to the trivial metric
h_{1}
on
\mathscr{O}_{U}^{\oplus{\rm rk}E}
. Since

\sqrt{-1}\Theta_{e^{-\varphi}h_{1}}(E|_{U\cap X^{o}})=\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi\otimes{\rm Id}_{E}\geq c\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}\lambda\otimes{\rm Id}_{E}\geq C\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}\Phi\otimes{\rm Id}_{E}

for some negative constants
c,C
.
By assumptions, there is a constant
C^{\prime}>0
such that

(C^{\prime}+C)\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}\Phi\geq\omega|_{U\cap X^{o}}.

Let
h^{\prime}=e^{-\varphi-C^{\prime}\Phi}h_{1}
. The boundedness of
\Phi
implies that
h^{\prime}\sim h_{\varphi}
.
It follows from Theorem
2.3
that

\sqrt{-1}\Theta_{h_{\mathbb{V}}\otimes h^{\prime}}(S(\mathbb{V})\otimes E|_{U\cap X^{o}})\geq C^{\prime}\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}\Phi\otimes{\rm Id}_{S(\mathbb{V})\otimes E}+\sqrt{-1}\Theta_{e^{-\varphi}h_{1}}(E|_{U\cap X^{o}})\otimes{\rm Id}_{S(\mathbb{V})}\geq\omega\otimes{\rm Id}_{S(\mathbb{V})\otimes E}

holds on
U\cap X^{o}
.
By Lemma
3.4
we may assume that
U\cap X^{o}
admits a complete Kähler metric.
Consequently, we have

H^{n,q}_{(2)}(U\cap X^{o},S(\mathbb{V})\otimes E|_{U\cap X^{o}};ds^{2},h_{\mathbb{V}}h_{\varphi})=H^{n,q}_{(2)}(U\cap X^{o},S(\mathbb{V})\otimes E|_{U\cap X^{o}};ds^{2},h_{\mathbb{V}}h^{\prime})=0,\quad\forall q>0

by Theorem
3.3
.
This proves the exactness of (
5.1
) at
\mathscr{D}^{n,q}_{X,ds^{2}}(S(\mathbb{V})\otimes E,h_{\mathbb{V}}\otimes h_{\varphi})
for all
q>0
.
Since
\mathscr{D}^{n,\bullet}_{X,ds^{2}_{0}}(E,h_{\varphi})
is a complex of fine sheaves (Lemma
3.2
), we obtain the second claim.
∎

A typical example of the metric
ds^{2}
that satisfies the conditions in Theorem
5.1
is the hermitian metric on
X
, which always exists by partition of unity. For applications we require
ds^{2}
to be a complete Kähler metric.
Such kind of metric exists if
X
is the truncation of a weakly pseudoconvex Kähler space.
Recall that a complex space is a weakly pseudoconvex (Kähler) space if it is a (Kähler) complex space that admits a smooth psh exhausted function.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
X
be a weakly pseudoconvex Kähler space with
\psi
a smooth psh exhausted function on
X
. Denote
X_{c}:=\{x\in X|\psi(x)<c\}
. Let
X^{o}\subset X_{\rm reg}
be a Zariski open subset. Then, for every
c\in\mathbb{R}
, there exists a complete Kähler metric
ds^{2}
on
X_{c}\cap X^{o}
satisfying that for every point
x\in X_{c}
, there is a neighborhood
U
of
x
, a bounded function
\Phi\in C^{\infty}(U)
and a smooth strictly psh function
\lambda
on
U
such that
\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}\lambda\lesssim ds^{2}\sim\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}\Phi
.

Proof.

The construction of the metric is the motivated by Ohsawa
[
38
, Lemma 2.6]
.
Let
U
be a neighborhood of a point
x\in X\backslash X^{o}
. Assume that
U\backslash X^{o}\subset U
is defined by
f_{1},\dots,f_{r}\in\mathscr{O}_{U}(U)
. Let

\varphi_{U}:=\frac{1}{\log(-\log\sum_{i=1}^{r}|f_{i}|^{2})}+\phi_{U},

where
\phi_{U}
is a strictly
C^{\infty}
psh function on
U
so that
\varphi_{U}
is strictly psh.
Then the quasi-isometric class of
\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi_{U}
is independent of the choice of
\{f_{1},\dots,f_{r}\}
and
\phi_{U}
. By partition of unity, the potential functions
\varphi_{U}
can be glued to a global function
\varphi
(not necessarily psh) on
X
so that

\displaystyle\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi|_{U}\sim\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi_{U}

near every point
x\in X\backslash X^{o}
and
\varphi\equiv 0
away from a neighborhood of
X\backslash X^{o}
.

Denote
u=-\log\sum_{i=1}^{r}|f_{i}|^{2}
. Then

\displaystyle\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi|_{U}

\displaystyle\sim\sqrt{-1}\frac{2+\log u}{u^{2}\log^{3}u}\partial u\wedge\bar{\partial}u+\sqrt{-1}\frac{-\partial\bar{\partial}u}{u\log^{2}u}+\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}\phi_{U}

\displaystyle\sim\sqrt{-1}\frac{\partial u\wedge\bar{\partial}u}{u^{2}\log^{2}u}+\sqrt{-1}\frac{-\partial\bar{\partial}u}{u\log^{2}u}+\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}\phi_{U}.

Hence
\log\log u
is a smooth psh exhausted function near
X\backslash X^{o}
such that

|d\log\log u|_{\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi}\lesssim 2.

By the Hopf-Rinow theorem,
\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi
is locally complete near
X\backslash X^{o}
.

Let
c\in\mathbb{R}
and let
\omega_{0}
be a Kähler hermitian metric on
X
. By adding a constant to
\psi
we assume that
\psi\geq 0
. Then
\psi_{c}:=\psi+\frac{1}{c-\psi}
is a smooth psh exhausted function on
X_{c}=\{x\in X|\psi(x)<c\}
.
Hence,
\omega_{0}+\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}\psi^{2}_{c}

is a complete Kähler metric on
X_{c}
(
[
10
, Theorem 1.3]
).
Since
\overline{X_{c}}
is compact,

\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi+K(\omega_{0}+\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}\psi^{2}_{c}),\quad K\gg 0

is positive definite and it provides the desired complete Kähler metric on
X_{c}\cap X^{o}
.
∎

6.
Vanishing theorems for
S
-sheaf

Vanishing theorems and injectivity theorems for the
S
-sheaf are deduced from Theorem
5.1
in this section.

6.1.
Nadel vanishing theorem

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
X
be a weakly pseudoconvex Kähler space and
\omega
a Kähler hermitian metric on
X
. Let
\mathbb{V}
be an
\mathbb{R}
-polarized variation of Hodge structure on some Zariski open subset
X^{o}\subset X_{\rm reg}
and
(L,h_{\varphi})
a holomorphic line bundle with a possibly singular hermitian metric
h_{\varphi}:=e^{-\varphi}h
. If
\sqrt{-1}\Theta_{h_{\varphi}}(L)\geq\epsilon\omega
as currents for some
\epsilon>0
, then

H^{q}(X,S(IC_{X}(\mathbb{V}),\varphi)\otimes L)=0,\quad\forall q>0.

Proof.

By Theorem
5.1

there is a quasi-isomorphism

S(IC_{X}(\mathbb{V}),\varphi)\otimes L\simeq_{\rm q.i.s.}\mathscr{D}^{n,\bullet}_{X,\omega}(S(\mathbb{V})\otimes L,h_{\mathbb{V}}\otimes h_{\varphi}).

Lemma
3.2
implies that
\mathscr{D}^{n,\bullet}_{X,\omega}(S(\mathbb{V})\otimes L,h_{\mathbb{V}}\otimes h_{\varphi})
is a complex of fine sheaves. Thus

\displaystyle H^{q}(X,S(IC_{X}(\mathbb{V}),\varphi)\otimes L)\simeq H^{q}\left(\Gamma(X,\mathscr{D}^{n,\bullet}_{X,\omega}(S(\mathbb{V})\otimes L,h_{\mathbb{V}}\otimes h_{\varphi}))\right).

Now let
q>0
and let
\alpha\in\Gamma(X,\mathscr{D}^{n,q}_{X,\omega}(S(\mathbb{V})\otimes L,h_{\mathbb{V}}\otimes h_{\varphi}))
be a locally
L^{2}
form such that
\bar{\partial}\alpha=0
. We would like to show that there exists
\beta\in\Gamma(X,\mathscr{D}^{n,q-1}_{X,\omega}(S(\mathbb{V})\otimes L,h_{\mathbb{V}}\otimes h_{\varphi}))
satisfying that
\bar{\partial}\beta=\alpha
.

Let
\psi
be a smooth psh exhausted function on
X
and let
\chi
be a convex increasing function which is of fast growth at infinity so that

\displaystyle\int_{X^{o}}|\alpha|_{\omega,h_{\mathbb{V}}\otimes h_{\varphi}}^{2}e^{-\chi\circ\psi}{\rm vol_{\omega}}<\infty.

Let
h^{\prime}:=e^{-\chi\circ\psi}h_{\varphi}
. Consequently, we have

\displaystyle\sqrt{-1}\Theta_{h_{\mathbb{V}}\otimes h^{\prime}}(S(\mathbb{V})\otimes L)

\displaystyle=\sqrt{-1}\Theta_{h_{\mathbb{V}}}(S(\mathbb{V}))+\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}(\chi\circ\psi)\otimes{\rm Id}_{S(\mathbb{V})}+\sqrt{-1}\Theta_{h_{\varphi}}(L)\otimes{\rm Id}_{S(\mathbb{V})}

\displaystyle\geq\epsilon\omega\otimes{\rm Id}_{S(\mathbb{V})}

by Theorem
2.3
.
Moreover, Lemma
5.2
implies that
X_{c}\cap X^{o}:=\{x\in X^{o}|\psi(x)<c\}

admits a complete Kähler metric for every
c\in\mathbb{R}
.
It follows from Theorem
3.3
that for every
c\in\mathbb{R}
there is

\beta_{c}\in L^{n,q-1}_{(2)}(X_{c}\cap X^{o},S(\mathbb{V})\otimes L;\omega,h_{\mathbb{V}}\otimes h^{\prime})\subset\Gamma(X_{c},\mathscr{D}^{n,q-1}_{X,\omega}(S(\mathbb{V})\otimes L,h_{\mathbb{V}}\otimes h_{\varphi}))

such that
\bar{\partial}\beta_{c}=\alpha|_{X_{c}}
and
\|\beta_{c}\|_{\epsilon\omega,h_{\mathbb{V}}\otimes h^{\prime}}\leq\frac{1}{q}\|\alpha\|_{\epsilon\omega,h_{\mathbb{V}}\otimes h^{\prime}}
. By taking a weak limit of
\beta_{c}
we obtain a solution of the equation
\alpha=\bar{\partial}\beta
such that
\beta\in\Gamma(X,\mathscr{D}^{n,q-1}_{X,\omega}(S(\mathbb{V})\otimes L,h_{\mathbb{V}}\otimes h_{\varphi}))
.
∎

Corollary 6.2
.

Let
X
be a projective algebraic variety of dimension
n
and let
\mathbb{V}
be an
\mathbb{R}
-polarized variation of Hodge structure on some Zariski open subset
X^{o}\subset X_{\rm reg}
. Let
L
be a line bundle such that some positive multiple
mL=F+D
where
F
is a nef line bundle and
D
is an effective divisor. Then

H^{q}(X,S(IC_{X}(\mathbb{V}),\frac{\varphi_{D}}{m})\otimes L)=0,\quad\forall q>n-{\rm nd}(F).

Here
\varphi_{D}
is the psh function associated to
D
and

{\rm nd}(F)=\max\{k=1,\dots,n|c_{1}(F)^{k}\neq 0\in H^{2k}(X,\mathbb{R})\}.

Remark 6.3
.

Let
s_{D}\in\Gamma(X,\mathscr{O}_{X}(D))
and
h^{\prime}_{D}
a hermitian metric on
\mathscr{O}_{X}(D)
. Let
\varphi_{D}:=\log|s_{D}|^{2}_{h^{\prime}_{D}}
. Then the singular metric
h_{D}:=e^{-\varphi_{D}}h^{\prime}_{D}
is independent of the choice of
h^{\prime}_{D}
and
\sqrt{-1}\Theta_{h_{D}}(\mathscr{O}_{X}(D))\geq 0
as a current.
Since
\varphi_{D}
is differed by a smooth function for different choices of
s_{D}
and
h^{\prime}_{D}
, Lemma
4.5
shows that
S(IC_{X}(\mathbb{V}),\frac{\varphi_{D}}{m})
is independent of the choice of
s_{D}
and
h^{\prime}_{D}
.

Proof.

Case I:
{\rm nd}(F)=n
.

In this case
F
is big and nef. Then
bF=A+E
for some constant
b>0
, an ample line bundle
A
and an effective Cartier divisor
E
. Let
h_{A}
be a metric with positive curvature on
A
and
h^{\prime}_{E}
a metric on
\mathscr{O}_{X}(E)
. Let
h_{E}:=e^{-\varphi_{E}}h^{\prime}_{E}
(Remark
6.3
). Then
h_{F,0}=(h_{A}\otimes h_{E})^{\frac{1}{b}}
is a singular metric such that

\displaystyle\sqrt{-1}\Theta_{h_{F,0}}(F)\geq\frac{1}{b}\omega.

Here
\omega=\sqrt{-1}\Theta_{h_{A}}(A)
is a hermitian metric on
X
. Since
F
is nef, there is a metric
h_{F,\epsilon}
such that
\sqrt{-1}\Theta_{h_{F,\epsilon}}(F)\geq-\epsilon\omega
for every
\epsilon>0
. Let
h_{D}=e^{-\varphi_{D}}h^{\prime}_{D}
be the singular metric on
\mathscr{O}_{X}(D)
as in Remark
6.3
. Define

\displaystyle h_{L}:=(h_{F,\epsilon}^{1-\delta}\otimes h_{F,0}^{\delta}\otimes h_{D})^{\frac{1}{m}}=e^{-\frac{\delta\varphi_{E}}{bm}-\frac{\varphi_{D}}{m}}(h_{F,\epsilon}^{1-\delta}\otimes h_{A}^{\delta/b}\otimes h^{\prime\delta/b}_{E}\otimes h^{\prime}_{D})^{\frac{1}{m}},\quad 0<b\epsilon\ll\delta\ll 1.

Then

\displaystyle\sqrt{-1}\Theta_{h_{L}}(L)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{m}\left((1-\delta)\sqrt{-1}\Theta_{h_{F,\epsilon}}(F)+\delta\sqrt{-1}\Theta_{h_{F,0}}(F)+\sqrt{-1}\Theta_{h_{D}}(D)\right)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{m}\left(-(1-\delta)\epsilon\omega+\frac{\delta}{b}\omega\right)\geq\frac{\delta\epsilon}{m}\omega.

Consequently, Theorem
6.1
yields

H^{q}(X,S(IC_{X}(\mathbb{V}),\varphi_{L})\otimes L)=0,\quad\forall q>0,

where
\varphi_{L}=\frac{\delta\varphi_{E}}{bm}+\frac{\varphi_{D}}{m}
.
Moreover, Proposition
4.13
implies that
S(IC_{X}(\mathbb{V}),\varphi_{L})=S(IC_{X}(\mathbb{V}),\frac{1}{m}\varphi_{D})
when
\delta>0
is small enough. This proves the theorem under the condition that
{\rm nd}(F)=n
.

Case II:
{\rm nd}(F)<n
.
Let
\pi:\widetilde{X}\to X
be a desingularization so that
\pi
is biholomorphic over
X^{o}\backslash D
and the exceptional loci
E:=\pi^{-1}((X\backslash X^{o})\cup D)
is a simple normal crossing divisor. Let
H
be a reduced ample hypersurface in a general position so that
\pi^{-1}H
is smooth and has normal crossings with
E
. By taking
H
sufficiently ample we assume that
F+mH
is ample.

By Proposition
4.16
we have

(6.1)

\displaystyle S(IC_{X}(\mathbb{V}),\frac{1}{m}\varphi_{D})|_{H}\otimes\mathscr{O}_{X}(H)|_{H}\simeq S(IC_{H}(\mathbb{V}|_{X^{o}\cap H}),\frac{1}{m}\varphi_{D\cap H})

when
H
is in a general position. We further assume that
H
contains no associated points of
S(IC_{X}(\mathbb{V}),\frac{1}{m}\varphi_{D})
. This implies that the sequence

0\to S(IC_{X}(\mathbb{V}),\frac{1}{m}\varphi_{D})\otimes L\to S(IC_{X}(\mathbb{V}),\frac{1}{m}\varphi_{D})\otimes L\otimes\mathscr{O}_{X}(H)\to S(IC_{X}(\mathbb{V}),\frac{1}{m}\varphi_{D})\otimes L\otimes\mathscr{O}_{X}(H)\otimes\mathscr{O}_{H}\to 0

is exact.
There is therefore a long exact sequence

(6.2)

\displaystyle\cdots

\displaystyle\to H^{q}\left(X,S(IC_{X}(\mathbb{V}),\frac{1}{m}\varphi_{D})\otimes L\right)\to H^{q}\left(X,S(IC_{X}(\mathbb{V}),\frac{1}{m}\varphi_{D})\otimes L\otimes\mathscr{O}_{X}(H)\right)

\displaystyle\to H^{q}\left(H,S(IC_{X}(\mathbb{V}),\frac{1}{m}\varphi_{D})|_{H}\otimes L|_{H}\otimes\mathscr{O}_{X}(H)|_{H}\right)\to\cdots.

Since
F+mH
is ample,
{\rm nd}(F+mH)=n
. Then we have

\displaystyle H^{q}\left(X,S(IC_{X}(\mathbb{V}),\frac{1}{m}\varphi_{D})\otimes L\otimes\mathscr{O}_{X}(H)\right)=0,\quad\forall q>0

by Case I.
It follows from (
6.1
) and (
6.2
)that there are isomorphisms

(6.3)

\displaystyle H^{q}\left(X,S(IC_{X}(\mathbb{V}),\frac{1}{m}\varphi_{D})\otimes L\right)\simeq H^{q-1}\left(H,S(IC_{H}(\mathbb{V}|_{X^{o}\cap H}),\frac{1}{m}\varphi_{D\cap H})\otimes L|_{H}\right),\quad\forall q>1.

Subsequently, since
{\rm nd}(L|_{H})={\rm nd}(L)
, we have

\displaystyle H^{q}\left(H,S(IC_{H}(\mathbb{V}|_{X^{o}\cap H}),\frac{1}{m}\varphi_{D\cap H})\otimes L|_{H}\right)=0,\quad\forall q>n-1-{\rm nd}(L|_{H})

by induction on
\dim X
.
Consequently, (
6.3
) implies that

\displaystyle H^{q}\left(X,S(IC_{X}(\mathbb{V}),\frac{1}{m}\varphi_{D})\otimes L\right)=0,\quad\forall q>n-{\rm nd}(L).

∎

6.2.
Relative vanishing theorem

Let
f:X\to Y
be a proper holomorphic map between complex spaces. A
(1,1)
-current
\alpha
on
X
is
f
-positive if, for every point
y\in Y
there is a neighborhood
U
of
y
, a hermitian metric
\omega_{U}
on
U
and a hermitian metric
\omega^{\prime}
on
f^{-1}(U)
such that

\alpha|_{f^{-1}U}+f^{\ast}\omega_{U}\geq\omega^{\prime}
.

Corollary 6.4
.

Let
f:X\to Y
be a surjective proper Kähler holomorphic map between complex spaces. Let
\mathbb{V}
be an
\mathbb{R}
-polarized variation of Hodge structure on some Zariski open subset
X^{o}\subset X_{\rm reg}
. Let
(L,h_{\varphi})
be a holomorphic line bundle on
X
with a possibly singular hermitian metric
h_{\varphi}:=e^{-\varphi}h
. Assume that
\sqrt{-1}\Theta_{h_{\varphi}}(L)
is
f
-positive. Then

\displaystyle R^{i}f_{\ast}(S(IC_{X}(\mathbb{V}),\varphi)\otimes L)=0,\quad\forall i>0.

Proof.

Since the problem is local, we may assume that
Y
admits a non-negative smooth strictly psh exhausted function
\psi
so that
\psi^{-1}\{0\}=\{y\}\subset Y
is a point. To achieve this, one may embed
Y
into
\mathbb{C}^{N}
as a closed Stein analytic subspace and take
\psi=|z|^{2}
. Moreover, since
f
is proper,
f^{\ast}\psi
is a smooth psh exhausted function on
X
.

By Lemma
5.2
, for every
c\in\mathbb{R}
there is a complete Kähler metric
\omega_{c}
on
X^{o}\cap f^{-1}(Y_{c})
where
Y_{c}=\{y\in Y|\psi(y)<c\}
.
Since
\sqrt{-1}\Theta_{h_{\varphi}}(L)
is
f
-positive, we assume that

\displaystyle\sqrt{-1}\Theta_{e^{-C\psi}h_{\varphi}}(L)=\sqrt{-1}\Theta_{h_{\varphi}}(L)+Cf^{\ast}\left(\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}\psi\right)\geq\omega^{\prime}

for some hermitian metric
\omega^{\prime}
on
f^{-1}(Y_{c})
and some constant
C>0
. It follows from Theorem
6.1
that

\displaystyle H^{i}(f^{-1}(Y_{c}),S(IC_{X}(\mathbb{V}),\varphi)\otimes L)\simeq H^{i}(f^{-1}(Y_{c}),S(IC_{X}(\mathbb{V}),\varphi+C\psi)\otimes L)=0,\quad\forall i>0.

Here the isomorphism follows from the boundedness of
\psi
(Lemma
4.5
). Taking the limit
c\to 0
we see that

\displaystyle R^{i}f_{\ast}(S(IC_{X}(\mathbb{V}),\varphi)\otimes L)_{y}=0,\quad\forall i>0.

This proves the corollary.
∎

6.3.
Kodaira-Nakano-Kazama type vanishing theorem

Theorem 6.5
.

Let
X
be a weakly pseudoconvex Kähler space of dimension
n
. Let
\mathbb{V}
be an
\mathbb{R}
-polarized variation of Hodge structure on some Zariski open subset
X^{o}\subset X_{\rm reg}
. Let
(E,h)
be a hermitian vector bundle on
X
. Assume that
(S(\mathbb{V})\otimes E,h_{\mathbb{V}}\otimes h)
is
m
-Nakano positive. Then

H^{q}(X,S(IC_{X}(\mathbb{V}))\otimes E)=0

whenever
q\geq 1
and
m\geq{\rm min}\{n-q+1,{\rm rk}S(\mathbb{V})+{\rm rk}E\}
.

Proof.

By Theorem
5.1
there is a quasi-isomorphism

S(IC_{X}(\mathbb{V}))\otimes E\simeq_{\rm q.i.s.}\mathscr{D}^{n,\bullet}_{X,\omega}(S(\mathbb{V})\otimes E,h_{\mathbb{V}}\otimes h)

where
\omega
is a Kähler hermitian metric on
X
.
Notice that
\mathscr{D}^{n,\bullet}_{X,\omega}(S(\mathbb{V})\otimes E,h_{\mathbb{V}}\otimes h)
is a complex of fine sheaves by Lemma
3.2
. Hence

\displaystyle H^{q}(X,S(IC_{X}(\mathbb{V}))\otimes E)\simeq H^{q}\left(\Gamma(X,\mathscr{D}^{n,\bullet}_{X,\omega}(S(\mathbb{V})\otimes E,h_{\mathbb{V}}\otimes h))\right).

Now let
q>0
such that
m\geq{\rm min}\{n-q+1,{\rm rk}S(\mathbb{V})+{\rm rk}E\}
. Let
\alpha\in\Gamma(X,\mathscr{D}^{n,q}_{X,\omega}(S(\mathbb{V})\otimes E,h_{\mathbb{V}}\otimes h))
be a locally
L^{2}
form such that
\bar{\partial}\alpha=0
. It suffices to find
\beta\in\Gamma(X,\mathscr{D}^{n,q-1}_{X,\omega}(S(\mathbb{V})\otimes E,h_{\mathbb{V}}\otimes h))
so that
\bar{\partial}\beta=\alpha
.

Denote
A=[\sqrt{-1}\Theta_{h_{\mathbb{V}}\otimes h}(S(\mathbb{V})\otimes E),\Lambda_{\omega}]
.
Let
\psi
be a smooth psh exhausted function on
X
and
\chi
a convex increasing function. Denote
h_{\chi}:=e^{-\chi\circ\psi}h
. Then

\displaystyle\sqrt{-1}\Theta_{h_{\mathbb{V}}\otimes h_{\chi}}(S(\mathbb{V})\otimes E)

\displaystyle=\sqrt{-1}\Theta_{h_{\mathbb{V}}\otimes h}(S(\mathbb{V})\otimes E)+\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}(\chi\circ\psi)\otimes{\rm Id}_{S(\mathbb{V})\otimes E}

\displaystyle\geq\sqrt{-1}\Theta_{h_{\mathbb{V}}\otimes h}(S(\mathbb{V})\otimes E).

Denote
A_{\chi}=[\sqrt{-1}\Theta_{h_{\mathbb{V}}\otimes h_{\chi}}(S(\mathbb{V})\otimes E),\Lambda_{\omega}]
. Then
A_{\chi}\geq A>0
in bidegree
(n,q)
. Thus the integrals

\int_{X^{o}}|\alpha|_{\omega,h_{\chi}}^{2}{\rm vol_{\omega}}=\int_{X^{o}}|\alpha|_{\omega,h}^{2}e^{-\chi\circ\psi}{\rm vol_{\omega}}

and

\int_{X^{o}}(A^{-1}_{\chi}\alpha,\alpha)_{\omega,h_{\chi}}^{2}{\rm vol_{\omega}}\leq\int_{X^{o}}(A^{-1}\alpha,\alpha)_{\omega,h}^{2}e^{-\chi\circ\psi}{\rm vol_{\omega}}

are finite if
\chi
grows fast enough at infinity. By Lemma
5.2
,

X_{c}\cap X^{o}:=\{x\in X^{o}|\chi\circ\psi(x)<c\}

admits a complete Kähler metric for every
c\in\mathbb{R}
.
Subsequently,
[
11
, Theorem 5.1]
implies that there exists

\beta_{c}\in L^{n,q-1}_{(2)}(X_{c}\cap X^{o},S(\mathbb{V})\otimes E;\omega,h_{\mathbb{V}}\otimes h_{\chi})\subset\Gamma(X_{c},\mathscr{D}^{n,q-1}_{X,\omega}(S(\mathbb{V})\otimes E,h_{\mathbb{V}}\otimes h))

such that
\bar{\partial}\beta_{c}=\alpha|_{X_{c}}
and

\|\beta_{c}\|_{\omega,h_{\mathbb{V}}\otimes h_{\chi}}\leq\frac{1}{q}\int_{X^{o}}(A^{-1}\alpha,\alpha)_{\omega,h}^{2}e^{-\chi\circ\psi}{\rm vol_{\omega}}.

By taking a weak limit of a certain subsequence of
\{\beta_{c}\}
we obtain the solution of the equation
\alpha=\bar{\partial}\beta
such that
\beta\in\Gamma(X,\mathscr{D}^{n,q-1}_{X,\omega}(S(\mathbb{V})\otimes E,h_{\mathbb{V}}\otimes h))
. This proves the theorem.
∎

. Then

(7.1)

\displaystyle\sqrt{-1}\Theta_{h}(L)\geq\sqrt{-1}\Theta_{h^{\prime\prime}}(L^{\otimes\frac{\kappa-\epsilon}{m_{0}+\kappa}})>0.

Let
\varphi
be the weight of
h
, i.e.
h=e^{-\varphi}h_{0}
for some smooth hermitian metric
h_{0}
. Let
\varphi^{\prime}
be a quasi-psh function so that

(1)

\varphi^{\prime}
is smooth on
X\backslash\{x_{2},\dots,x_{r}\}
.

(2)

e^{-\varphi^{\prime}}
is not locally integrable at any of
x_{2},\cdots,x_{r}
.

(3)

\varphi^{\prime}\geq\varphi+C
for some
C\in\mathbb{R}
.

Such
\varphi^{\prime}
can be constructed as follows. Since
\varphi
has analytic singularities, we assume that there is an open subset
U_{1}
such that
\overline{U_{1}}\cap\{x_{1},\dots,x_{r}\}=\{x_{1}\}
and

\varphi=a\log(\sum_{j=1}^{s}|g_{j}|^{2})+\lambda
for some holomorphic functions
g_{1},\dots,g_{s}
on
U_{1}
, a constant
a>0
and a bounded function
\lambda
. Since
x_{1},\dots,x_{r}\in{\rm supp}\mathscr{O}_{X}/\mathscr{I}(h)
,
\{x_{1}\}\subset\{g_{1}=\cdots=g_{s}=0\}
. Let
\varphi^{\prime}_{1}=a\log(\sum_{j=1}^{l}|g_{j}|^{2})+\lambda
where
g_{s+1},\dots,g_{l}
are holomorphic functions such that
\{g_{1}=\cdots=g_{l}=0\}=\emptyset
. Let
U^{\prime}
be an open neighborhood of
\{x_{2},\dots,x_{r}\}
such that
\overline{U_{1}}\cap\overline{U^{\prime}}=\emptyset
. Choose a smooth extension
\varphi^{\prime}\in C^{\infty}(X\backslash\{x_{2},\dots,x_{r}\})
of
\varphi^{\prime}_{1}\in C^{\infty}(U_{1})
and
\varphi\in C^{\infty}(U^{\prime}\backslash\{x_{2},\dots,x_{r}\})
. Then
\varphi^{\prime}
is the desired function.

Consider the short exact sequence of sheaves

\displaystyle 0\to S(IC_{X}(\mathbb{V}),\varphi)\otimes L\to S(IC_{X}(\mathbb{V}),\varphi^{\prime})\otimes L\to S(IC_{X}(\mathbb{V}),\varphi^{\prime})/S(IC_{X}(\mathbb{V}),\varphi)\otimes L\to 0.

Taking the cohomologies we obtain the exact sequence

\displaystyle H^{0}(X,S(IC_{X}(\mathbb{V}),\varphi^{\prime})\otimes L)\to H^{0}(X,S(IC_{X}(\mathbb{V}),\varphi^{\prime})/S(IC_{X}(\mathbb{V}),\varphi)\otimes L)\to H^{1}(X,S(IC_{X}(\mathbb{V}),\varphi)\otimes L).

By (
7.1
) and Theorem
6.1
we have

\displaystyle H^{1}(X,S(IC_{X}(\mathbb{V}),\varphi)\otimes L)=0.

So the canonical map

(7.2)

\displaystyle H^{0}(X,S(IC_{X}(\mathbb{V}),\varphi^{\prime})\otimes L)\to H^{0}(X,S(IC_{X}(\mathbb{V}),\varphi^{\prime})/S(IC_{X}(\mathbb{V}),\varphi)\otimes L)

is surjective.

Now let us investigate the structure of
S(IC_{X}(\mathbb{V}),\varphi^{\prime})/S(IC_{X}(\mathbb{V}),\varphi)
at
x_{1}
. Let
z_{1},\dots,z_{n}
be holomorphic coordinates centered at
x_{1}
such that
D=\{z_{1}\cdots z_{s}=0\}
. Let
U\subset X
denote the domain of the coordinate and denote
D_{i}=\{z_{i}=0\}
,
\forall i=1,\dots,s
. Let
\widetilde{v_{1}},\dots,\widetilde{v_{m}}
be an
L^{2}
-adapted frame of
R(IC_{X}(\mathbb{V}))
as in Proposition
2.5
. Denote

\psi_{i}:=-\sum_{j=1}^{s}\alpha_{E_{j}}(\widetilde{v_{i}})\log|z_{j}|^{2}.

By Proposition
4.8
, we see that

\displaystyle S(IC_{X}(\mathbb{V}),\varphi)|_{U}\simeq\omega_{U}\otimes\bigoplus_{i=1}^{m}\mathscr{I}(\varphi+\psi_{i})|_{U}\widetilde{v_{i}}

and

\displaystyle S(IC_{X}(\mathbb{V}),\varphi^{\prime})|_{U}\simeq\omega_{U}\otimes\bigoplus_{i=1}^{m}\mathscr{I}(\varphi^{\prime}+\psi_{i})|_{U}\widetilde{v_{i}}.

Thus

(7.3)

\displaystyle S(IC_{X}(\mathbb{V}),\varphi^{\prime})/S(IC_{X}(\mathbb{V}),\varphi)|_{U}\simeq\omega_{U}\otimes\bigoplus_{i=1}^{m}\mathscr{I}(\varphi^{\prime}+\psi_{i})/\mathscr{I}(\varphi+\psi_{i})|_{U}\widetilde{v_{i}}.

After a possible shrinking of
U
we assume that
\varphi
is smooth on
U\backslash\{x_{1}\}
. Recall that

\alpha_{E_{j}}(\widetilde{v_{i}})\in(-1,0]

for every
j=1,\cdots,s
in Proposition
2.5
. Then
e^{-\psi_{1}},\dots,e^{-\psi_{m}}
are locally integrable on
U
. This implies that

\mathscr{I}(\varphi+\psi_{i})_{x}\simeq\mathscr{O}_{U,x},\quad i=1,\dots,m,\quad x\in U\backslash\{x_{1}\}.

As a consequence

(7.4)

\displaystyle U\cap{\rm supp}\mathscr{I}(\varphi^{\prime}+\psi_{i})/\mathscr{I}(\varphi+\psi_{i})\subset\{x_{1}\}.

Since
\varphi^{\prime}
is smooth at
x_{1}
and
e^{-\psi_{1}},\dots,e^{-\psi_{m}}
are locally integrable on
U
, one gets that

\displaystyle\mathscr{I}(\varphi^{\prime}+\psi_{i})_{x_{1}}\simeq\mathscr{O}_{U,x_{1}},\quad i=1,\dots,m.

Since
x_{1}\in{\rm supp}\mathscr{O}_{U}/\mathscr{I}(\varphi)
, one sees that

(7.5)

\displaystyle\mathscr{I}(\varphi+\psi_{i})_{x_{1}}\subset\mathscr{I}(\varphi)_{x_{1}}\subset m_{U,x_{1}},\quad i=1,\dots,m.

By combining (
7.3
) with (
7.4
) and (
7.5
), we obtain a canonical surjective map

H^{0}(X,S(IC_{X}(\mathbb{V}),\varphi^{\prime})/S(IC_{X}(\mathbb{V}),\varphi)\otimes L)\to S(IC_{X}(\mathbb{V}))\otimes L\otimes\mathscr{O}_{X,x_{1}}/m_{X,x_{1}}.

Since (
7.2
) is surjective, we get a surjective map

H^{0}(X,S(IC_{X}(\mathbb{V}),\varphi^{\prime})\otimes L)\to S(IC_{X}(\mathbb{V}))\otimes L\otimes\mathscr{O}_{X,x_{1}}/m_{X,x_{1}}.

For every
v\in S(IC_{X}(\mathbb{V}))\otimes L\otimes\mathscr{O}_{X,x_{1}}/m_{X,x_{1}}
, there is a section

s_{1}\in H^{0}(X,S(IC_{X}(\mathbb{V}),\varphi^{\prime})\otimes L)

such that
s_{1}(x_{1})=v
. Since
\varphi^{\prime}
is not locally integrable at any of
x_{2},\dots,x_{r}
, we know that
s_{1}(x_{i})=0
,
i=2,\dots,r
.
This proves the theorem.
∎

Theorem 7.4
.

Let
X
be a smooth projective algebraic variety and
D
a (possibly empty) normal crossing divisor of
X
. Let
\mathbb{V}
be an
\mathbb{R}
-polarized variation of Hodge structure on
X^{o}:=X\backslash D
. Let
L
be an ample line bundle and
G
a nef line bundle on
X
. Then
S(IC_{X}(\mathbb{V}))\otimes\omega_{X}\otimes L^{\otimes m}\otimes G
generates simultaneous jets of order
s_{1},\dots,s_{r}\in\mathbb{N}
at arbitrary points
x_{1},\dots,x_{r}\in X
, i.e. there is a surjective map

H^{0}(X,S(IC_{X}(\mathbb{V}))\otimes\omega_{X}\otimes L^{\otimes m}\otimes G)\to\bigoplus_{1\leq j\leq r}S(IC_{X}(\mathbb{V}))\otimes\omega_{X}\otimes L^{\otimes m}\otimes G\otimes\mathscr{O}_{X,x_{k}}/m^{s_{k}+1}_{X,x_{k}},

provided that
m\geq 2+\sum_{1\leq k\leq r}\binom{3n+2s_{k}-1}{n}
. In particular,
S(IC_{X}(\mathbb{V}))\otimes\omega_{X}\otimes L^{\otimes m}\otimes G
is globally generated for
m\geq 2+\binom{3n-1}{n}
.

Proof.

Denote
m_{0}=2+\sum_{1\leq k\leq p}\binom{3n+2s_{k}-1}{n}
. Let
h^{\prime}
be the singular hermitian metric on
\omega_{X}\otimes L^{\otimes m_{0}}
as constructed in Proposition
7.2
. Since
L
and
G
are nef, there is a smooth hermitian metric
h^{\prime\prime}
on
L^{\otimes(m-m_{0})}\otimes G
such that

\sqrt{-1}\Theta_{h^{\prime\prime}}(L^{\otimes(m-m_{0})}\otimes G)\geq-\frac{\epsilon}{2}\omega

where
\omega
is a Kähler form on
X
.

Let
h:=h^{\prime}h^{\prime\prime}
be the singular hermitian metric on
A:=\omega_{X}\otimes L^{\otimes m}\otimes G
. Then

(1)

\sqrt{-1}\Theta_{h}(A)\geq\frac{\epsilon}{2}\omega
,

(2)

{\rm supp}(\mathscr{O}_{X}/\mathscr{I}(h))
is
0
-dimensional and the weight
\varphi
of
h
satisfies

\nu(\varphi,x_{k})\geq n+s_{k},\quad k=1,\dots,r.

Consider the short exact sequence of sheaves

\displaystyle 0\to S(IC_{X}(\mathbb{V}),\varphi)\otimes A\to S(IC_{X}(\mathbb{V}))\otimes A\to S(IC_{X}(\mathbb{V}))/S(IC_{X}(\mathbb{V}),\varphi)\otimes A\to 0.

Taking the cohomology of this sequence, we obtain the exact sequence

\displaystyle H^{0}(X,S(IC_{X}(\mathbb{V}))\otimes A)\to H^{0}(X,S(IC_{X}(\mathbb{V}))/S(IC_{X}(\mathbb{V}),\varphi)\otimes A)\to H^{1}(X,S(IC_{X}(\mathbb{V}),\varphi)\otimes A).

Since
\sqrt{-1}\Theta_{h}(A)\geq\frac{\epsilon}{2}\omega
, we have

\displaystyle H^{1}(X,S(IC_{X}(\mathbb{V}),\varphi)\otimes A)=0

by Theorem
6.1
.
So the canonical map

(7.6)

\displaystyle H^{0}(X,S(IC_{X}(\mathbb{V}))\otimes A)\to H^{0}(X,S(IC_{X}(\mathbb{V}))/S(IC_{X}(\mathbb{V}),\varphi)\otimes A)

is surjective.

Now let us investigate the structure of
S(IC_{X}(\mathbb{V}))/S(IC_{X}(\mathbb{V}),\varphi)
at
x_{k}
,
k=1,\dots,r
.

Let
z_{1},\dots,z_{n}
be holomorphic coordinates on a neighborhood
U_{k}
of
x_{k}
, centered at
x_{k}
, such that
D=\{z_{1}\cdots z_{s}=0\}
. Denote
D_{i}=\{z_{i}=0\},\forall i=1,\dots,s
. Let
\widetilde{v_{1}},\dots,\widetilde{v_{m}}
be an
L^{2}
-adapted frame of
R(IC_{X}(\mathbb{V}))
at
x_{k}
as in Proposition
2.5
. Denote

\psi_{i}:=-\sum_{j=1}^{s}\alpha_{E_{j}}(\widetilde{v_{i}})\log|z_{j}|^{2}.

It follows from Proposition
4.8
that

\displaystyle S(IC_{X}(\mathbb{V}),\varphi)|_{U_{k}}\simeq\omega_{U_{k}}\otimes\bigoplus_{i=1}^{m}\mathscr{I}(\varphi+\psi_{i})|_{U_{k}}\widetilde{v_{i}}.

Hence

(7.7)

\displaystyle S(IC_{X}(\mathbb{V}))/S(IC_{X}(\mathbb{V}),\varphi)|_{U_{k}}\simeq\omega_{U_{k}}\otimes\bigoplus_{i=1}^{m}\mathscr{O}_{X}/\mathscr{I}(\varphi+\psi_{i})|_{U_{k}}\widetilde{v_{i}}.

After a possible shrinking of
U_{k}
we assume that
\varphi
is smooth on
U_{k}\backslash\{x_{k}\}
. Since

\alpha_{E_{j}}(\widetilde{v_{i}})\in(-1,0]

for every
j=1,\cdots,s
,
e^{-\psi_{1}},\dots,e^{-\psi_{m}}
are locally integrable on
U_{k}
. This implies that

\mathscr{I}(\varphi+\psi_{i})_{x}\simeq\mathscr{O}_{U,x},\quad i=1,\dots,m,\quad\forall x\in U_{k}\backslash\{x_{k}\}.

As a consequence,

(7.8)

\displaystyle U\cap{\rm supp}\mathscr{O}_{X}/\mathscr{I}(\varphi+\psi_{i})\subset\{x_{k}\},\quad i=1,\dots,m.

It is evident that
\nu(\varphi,x_{k})\geq n+s_{k}
, which implies that

(7.9)

\displaystyle\mathscr{I}(\varphi+\psi_{i})_{x_{k}}\subset\mathscr{I}(\varphi)_{x_{k}}\subset m^{s_{k}+1}_{X,x_{k}},\quad i=1,\dots,m.

Combining (
7.7
), (
7.8
) and (
7.9
) we obtain a canonical surjective map

H^{0}(X,S(IC_{X}(\mathbb{V}))/S(IC_{X}(\mathbb{V}),\varphi)\otimes A)\to\bigoplus_{1\leq k\leq r}S(IC_{X}(\mathbb{V}))\otimes A\otimes\mathscr{O}_{X,x_{k}}/m^{s_{k}+1}_{X,x_{k}}.

Since (
7.6
) is surjective, we get the desired surjective map

H^{0}(X,S(IC_{X}(\mathbb{V}))\otimes A)\to\bigoplus_{1\leq k\leq r}S(IC_{X}(\mathbb{V}))\otimes A\otimes\mathscr{O}_{X,x_{k}}/m^{s_{k}+1}_{X,x_{k}}.

∎

Theorem 7.5
.

\mathbb{V}
be an
\mathbb{R}
-polarized variation of Hodge structure on some Zariski open subset
X^{o}\subset X_{\rm reg}
. Let
L
be an ample line bundle on

S(IC_{X}(\mathbb{V}))\otimes L^{\otimes m}\otimes G
generates simultaneous jets of order
s_{1},\dots,s_{r}\in\mathbb{N}

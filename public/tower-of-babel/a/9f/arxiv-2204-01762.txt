Restriction theorems for semistable sheaves
Mihai Pavel

Abstract
In this paper we prove restriction theorems for torsion-free sheaves that are (semi)stable with respect to the truncated Hilbert polynomial over a smooth projective variety. Our results apply in particular to Gieseker-semistable sheaves and generalize the well-known restriction theorems of Mehta and Ramanathan. As an application we construct a moduli space of sheaves in higher dimensions.



1
Introduction

In the moduli theory of sheaves, the so-called restriction theorems for slope semistability play a particularly important role. In many cases they provide a useful instrument to reduce the study of sheaves to lower dimensions. More exactly, they ensure that slope-semistability (or slope-stability) is preserved by restriction to a general divisor of sufficiently large degree on a smooth projective variety. For example, they were used to show Bogomolov’s inequality in higher dimensions (see
[
10
, Thm. 7.3.1]
). Another noteworthy application was in the construction of moduli spaces of slope-semistable sheaves over surfaces by Le Potier
[
14
]
and Jun Li
[
17
]
.

At this point there are several different proofs of the restriction theorems in the literature. The first general results were given by Mehta and Ramanathan
[
20
,
21
]
. In zero characteristic, Flenner later proved in
[
4
]
an effective restriction theorem but only for slope-semistability. He found a bound on the degree of the divisor that depends only on the numerical invariants of the sheaf. Finally, by a different approach, Langer gave in
[
11
]
stronger effective restriction theorems that work also in positive characteristic.

Main results
.

Let
X
be a smooth projective variety over an algebraically closed field
k
, and fix
\mathcal{O}_{X}(1)
a very ample line bundle. In this paper we prove restriction theorems for torsion-free sheaves on
X
that are semistable with respect to the truncated Hilbert polynomial. We call such sheaves
\ell
-
semistable
(see Definition
2.1
), where
\ell
marks the level of truncation. In particular one recovers the notion of slope-(semi)stability for
\ell=1
and of Gieseker-(semi)stability for
\ell=\dim(X)
. Our aim is to prove the following two theorems, thus answering in the affirmative a conjecture posed by Langer in
[
12
, Conj. 3.13]
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\ell<\dim(X)
and
E
be an
\ell
-semistable torsion-free sheaf on
X
. The restriction
E|_{D}
to a general divisor
D\in|\mathcal{O}_{X}(a)|
remains
\ell
-semistable for
a\gg 0
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\ell<\dim(X)
and
E
be an
\ell
-stable torsion-free sheaf on
X
. The restriction
E|_{D}
to a general divisor
D\in|\mathcal{O}_{X}(a)|
remains
\ell
-stable for
a\gg 0
.

As a consequence of Theorem
1.1
, we also obtain a restriction theorem for Gieseker-semistable sheaves. However the range for
\ell
is sharp in Theorem
1.2
. Indeed, the result is false for a Gieseker-stable but not slope-stable sheaf on a surface. For a different example see also
[
2
, Exam. 3.1]
.

Our proof is by induction on
\ell
and follows the strategy applied by Mehta and Ramanathan
[
20
,
21
]
in the case of slope-semistability. However, these methods give no effective bound on the degree of the divisor from which the restriction remains
\ell
-semistable. Finding such effective bounds may be an incentive for future studies. One may also ask whether the theorems hold for pure sheaves supported in positive codimension. In this regard, we proved in
[
23
]
an effective restriction theorem for slope-(semi)stable pure sheaves, by following a different approach due to Langer
[
11
]
. We expect the result to hold more generally for
\ell
-semistable pure sheaves:

Conjecture
.

Let
E
be an
\ell
-(semi)stable pure sheaf of dimension
d
on
X
with
\ell<d
. The restriction
E|_{D}
to a general divisor
D\in|\mathcal{O}_{X}(a)|
remains
\ell
-(semi)stable for
a\gg 0
.

Our restriction theorems may find applications in the moduli theory of sheaves. To give an example already envisioned by Le Potier
[
14
]
, one may use the restriction theorems to construct moduli spaces of
\ell
-semistable sheaves on
X
. In this respect, here we give the construction when
\ell=\dim(X)-1
, to obtain a moduli space of sheaves in higher dimensions over the field of complex numbers. We recover a result due to Huybrechts–Lehn
[
10
, Sect. 8]
when
X
is a surface. By similar methods, Greb and Toma
[
7
]
constructed moduli spaces of slope-semistable sheaves in higher dimensions, but they had to work over the category of weakly normal varieties. In contrast to their result, we need not to impose such restrictive assumptions on schemes (compare Theorem
4.2
with
[
7
, Main Thm.]
), but on the other hand we have a stronger stability condition on sheaves. Furthermore, in our case we are able to give a complete description of the geometric points of the moduli space (see Theorem
4.3
).

The moduli spaces of slope-semistable sheaves constructed by Huybrechts–Lehn and Greb–Toma are closely related to the Donaldson–Uhlenbeck compactification of Hermitian Yang–Mills (HYM) connections (see
[
17
]
and
[
6
]
). This is partly due to the so-called Kobayashi–Hitchin correspondence, which says that vector bundles over compact complex manifolds admit HYM connections (or Hermite–Einstein metrics) if and only if they are slope-polystable. There is an analogue correspondence between Gieseker-stable sheaves and almost Hermite–Einstein metrics (see
[
16
]
). For this reason, we expect that the moduli space we construct here may find applications in the context of gauge theory.

Preliminaries

In this section we set up notation and terminology. First we introduce the notion of
\ell
-(semi)stability and recall some of its basic properties, following
[
10
, Sect. 1]
.

Throughout this paper, let
X
be a smooth integral projective scheme of dimension
n
over a field
k
, and
\mathcal{O}_{X}(1)
a very ample line bundle on
X
. For the moment
k
is a field of arbitrary characteristic, which is not necessarily algebraically closed.

Let
E
be a pure sheaf of dimension
d
on
X
, i.e. any non-zero subsheaf
F\subset E
has dimension
d
. The Hilbert polynomial of
E
is given by its Euler characteristic
P(E,m)\coloneqq\chi(X,E\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(m))
and has the form

\displaystyle P(E,m)=\sum_{j=0}^{d}\alpha_{d-j}(E)\frac{m^{d-j}}{(d-j)!},

with rational coefficients
\alpha_{d-j}(E)
. In what follows, a pure sheaf on
X
of dimension
n=\dim(X)
will be called
torsion-free
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
E
be a coherent sheaf of dimension
d
on
X
and take
1\leq\ell\leq d
. Then
E
is called
\ell
-semistable
(resp.
\ell
-stable
) if it is pure and

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\alpha_{d-1}(F)}{\alpha_{d}(F)},\ldots,\frac{\alpha_{d-\ell}(F)}{\alpha_{d}(F)}\right)\leq\left(\frac{\alpha_{d-1}(E)}{\alpha_{d}(E)},\ldots,\frac{\alpha_{d-\ell}(E)}{\alpha_{d}(E)}\right)\quad(\textnormal{resp.\penalty\ }<)

for all subsheaves
F\subset E
such that
0<\alpha_{d}(F)<\alpha_{d}(E)
, where
\leq
is the lexicographic order.

With this definition we recover the notion of slope-(semi)stability for
\ell=1
, and of Gieseker-(semi)stability for
\ell=d
. More generally, one may define
\ell
-stability in the quotient category
\Coh_{d}(X)/\Coh_{d-\ell-1}(X)
(see
[
10
, Sect. 1.6]
), where
\Coh_{d}(X)
is the category of coherent sheaves of dimension
\leq d
. However, for the limited purpose of this paper, we restrict to work within the category
\Coh(X)
.

Sometimes it is convenient to formulate the
\ell
-semistability conditions in terms of truncated Hilbert polynomials. If
\mathbb{Q}[T]_{j}
denotes the vector subspace of polynomials of degree
\leq j
in
\mathbb{Q}[T]
, let
P_{\ell}(E)\in\mathbb{Q}[T]_{d}/\mathbb{Q}[T]_{d-\ell-1}
be the
\ell
-truncation of the Hilbert polynomial of
E
and
p_{\ell}(E)\coloneqq P_{\ell}(E)/\alpha_{d}(E)
its reduced form. Then, clearly,
E
is
\ell
-semistable (resp.
\ell
-stable) if and only if

\displaystyle p_{\ell}(F)\leq p_{\ell}(E)\quad(\text{resp.}\penalty\ <)

for all subsheaves
F\subset E
such that
0<\alpha_{d}(F)<\alpha_{d}(E)
. Above we compare the polynomials with respect to the natural lexicographic order of their coefficients.

Properties of
\ell
-semistability

Most of the basic properties of Gieseker-(semi)stability, such as the ones treated in

-(semi)stability with almost identical proofs. Furthermore, one can show by using known techniques that families of

Lemma 2.1

Assume that

is algebraically closed. The family of

-semistable sheaves of fixed Hilbert polynomial on

is bounded.

Proof.

See
[

.

Lemma 2.2

The property of being
\ell
-semistable, resp. simple, geometrically

-stable, is open in flat families of sheaves.

Proof.

The proof is adapted from
[

\dim_{k(s)}(\End(\mathcal{F}_{s}))=1

[
1
, Satz 3]

\ell
-semistability, see the cited reference for the remaining case.

Let
S
be a
k
-scheme of finite type and
\mathcal{F}
an
S
-flat family of sheaves on
X
with Hilbert polynomial

\mathcal{F}_{s}
are pure of dimension

We first show that all destabilizing quotients
\mathcal{F}_{s}\to F^{\prime}
fit inside a proper Quot scheme. Consider the following set

\displaystyle H=\{

\displaystyle P(F^{\prime})\in\mathbb{Q}[T]:\mathcal{F}_{s}\to F^{\prime}\textnormal{ is a pure quotient of dimension $d$}

\displaystyle\text{for a closed point $s\in S$ such that $p_{\ell}(F^{\prime})<p_{\ell}$}\}.

where

denotes the reduced

-truncation of

.
By a boundedness result due to Grothendieck (see

\varphi:Q\coloneqq\Quot(\mathcal{F},H)\to S

is
\ell

.
∎

If

The Hilbert polynomial

Below we relate the coefficients of the Hilbert polynomial of a sheaf
E
to those of its restriction
E|_{D}
to a divisor. For
a>0
, let
\Pi_{a}\coloneqq|\mathcal{O}_{X}(a)|
be the linear system of hypersurfaces of degree
a
on
X
. For a pure sheaf
E
of dimension
d
on
X
, consider the short exact sequence

\displaystyle 0\to E(-a)\to E\to E|_{D}\to 0,

for a divisor
D\in\Pi_{a}
avoiding the associated points of
E
. By the additivity of the Hilbert polynomial in short exact sequences we obtain

\displaystyle\alpha_{d-j}(E|_{D})={}

\displaystyle\alpha_{d-j}(E)-\alpha_{d-j}(E(-a))

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\sum_{l=1}^{j}\frac{(-1)^{l-1}a^{l}}{l!}\alpha_{d-j+l}(E)

(2.1)

for
1\leq j\leq d
.

Lemma 2.11
.

Let
E
and
F
be pure sheaves of dimension
d
on
X
and
D\in\Pi_{a}
a divisor such that
E|_{D}
and
F|_{D}
are still pure. If
\ell<d
, then
p_{\ell}(E|_{D})\geq p_{\ell}(F|_{D})
(resp.
>
) if and only if
p_{\ell}(E)\geq p_{\ell}(F)
(resp.
>
).

Proof.

The inequalities follow in an iterative manner from (
2
).
∎

Note that (
2
) gives us no information about the free coefficient
\alpha_{0}(E)
, which explains the upper bound
\ell<d
in the statement above.

For
1\leq j\leq d
and
D\in\Pi_{a}
, set

\displaystyle\beta_{d-j}(E|_{D})\coloneqq\alpha_{d-j}(E|_{D})+\frac{a}{2}\alpha_{d-j+1}(E|_{D})+\sum_{l=1}^{j-1}\frac{(-1)^{l-1}B_{2l}}{(2l)!}a^{2l}\alpha_{d-j+1+l}(E|_{D}),

where
B_{2l}
denotes the
l
-th Bernoulli number. Recall that the Bernoulli numbers can be defined by the generating power series

\displaystyle Q(x)=\frac{x}{1-e^{-x}}=1+\frac{x}{2}+\sum_{l\geq 1}\frac{(-1)^{l-1}B_{2l}}{(2l)!}x^{2l}=1+\frac{x}{2}+\frac{x^{2}}{12}-\frac{x^{4}}{720}+\ldots

By using its inverse

\displaystyle Q(x)^{-1}=\frac{1-e^{-x}}{x}=\sum_{l\geq 1}\frac{(-1)^{l-1}x^{l-1}}{l!},

one can derive

\displaystyle\alpha_{d-j}(E|_{D})=\sum_{l=1}^{j}\frac{(-1)^{l-1}a^{l-1}}{l!}\beta_{d-j+l-1}(E|_{D}).

(2.2)

A direct comparison of (
2
) and (
2.2
) shows that
\beta_{d-j}(E|_{D})/a=\alpha_{d-j+1}(E)
whenever
D
avoids the associated points of
E
.

\displaystyle K(X)\xrightarrow{p_{2}^{*}}K^{0}(S\times X)\xrightarrow{\cdot[\mathcal{E}]}K^{0}(S\times X)\xrightarrow{{p_{1}}_{!}}K^{0}(S)\xrightarrow{\det}\Pic(S),

.

).

over

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Pi_{a}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces q}

Remark 3.1
.

If
\mathcal{K}
denotes the kernel of the evaluation map

\displaystyle H^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(a))\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}\to\mathcal{O}_{X}(a),

then

. Also, there exists an isomorphism
\Pic(Z_{a})\cong\Pic(X)\times\Pic(\Pi_{a})
such that
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{K}^{\vee})}(1)\cong p^{*}(\mathcal{O}_{\Pi_{a}}(1))
(see the proof in
[

, Prop. 2.1]
).

As a matter of notation, we will denote by

, or simply by

, the closed point of
\Pi_{a}
coressponding to an effective divisor

. Also we will often identify the fibre

with

via

.

By Lemma
2.7
there exists a relative
\ell
-Harder-Narasimhan filtration of

, which one can use to prove the following result:

Lemma 3.1
.

For
a>0
there exists a dense open subset
U_{a}\subset\Pi_{a}
and a quotient
q^{*}E\to\mathcal{F}_{a}
such that

1.

each divisor
D\in U_{a}
is smooth and integral,

2.

\mathcal{F}_{a}
is flat over
U_{a}
,

3.

for
[D]\in U_{a}
,
E|_{D}\to\mathcal{F}_{a}|_{D}
is the minimal

-destabilizing quotient of

,

4.

for

,
E|_{D}
is
(\ell-1)
-semistable.

Proof.

(2)
and
(3)
are direct consequences of Lemma
2.7
. We may also assume by induction that
E|_{D}
is
(\ell-1)
-semistable for general
[D]\in\Pi_{a}
of sufficiently large degree. Finally
(1)
holds by Bertini’s Theorem.
∎

Since
\mathcal{F}_{a}
is flat over
U_{a}
, the Hilbert polynomial of
\mathcal{F}_{a}|_{D}
does not vary with

. Hence we set
P(a)\coloneqq P(\mathcal{F}_{a}|_{D})
,
\rk(a)\coloneqq\rk(\mathcal{F}_{a}|_{D})
,
\alpha_{n-j}(a)\coloneqq\alpha_{n-j}(\mathcal{F}_{a}|_{D})
and so forth.

Remark 3.2
.

Since
E|_{D}
is already
(\ell-1)
-semistable, we have
p_{\ell-1}(a)=p_{\ell-1}(E|_{D})
for
a\gg 0
. Then

\displaystyle\frac{\beta_{n-j}(a)}{a\rk(a)}=\frac{\beta_{n-j}(E|_{D})}{a\rk(E|_{D})}=\frac{\alpha_{n-j+1}(E)}{\rk(E)}

is constant for any
1\leq j\leq\ell
and
a\gg 0
.

Let
D=D_{1}+D_{2}
be a simple normal crossing divisor of degree
a=a_{1}+a_{2}
with
[D_{1}]\in U_{a_{1}}
and
[D_{2}]\in U_{a_{2}}
. Then there exists a smooth (locally closed) curve
C\subset\Pi_{a}
passing through
[D]
such that
C\setminus[D]\subset U_{a}
and
Z_{C}\coloneqq Z_{a}\times_{\Pi_{a}}C
is smooth in codimension 2 (see
[
10
, Lem. 7.2.3]
). Restricting
q^{*}E\to\mathcal{F}_{a}
to
Z_{C\setminus[D]}
gives a quotient
q^{*}E|_{Z_{C\setminus[D]}}\to\mathcal{F}_{a}|_{Z_{C\setminus[D]}}
, which induces a morphism of schemes

\displaystyle C\setminus[D]\to\Quot_{Z_{a}/\Pi_{a}}(q^{*}E,P(a))

As
C
is a smooth curve and the Quot scheme is projective, we can extend the above morphism over
[D]
and get a quotient
q^{*}E|_{Z_{C}}\to\mathcal{F}_{C}
.

In what follows
i=1,2
. Let
F\coloneqq(\mathcal{F}_{C}|_{D})/T(\mathcal{F}_{C}|_{D})
,
F_{i}\coloneqq F|_{D_{i}}
and denote by
T_{i}\coloneqq T(F_{i})
the torsion of
F_{i}
. For
1\leq j\leq\ell+1
, consider the following property
(P_{j})
:

1.

for
a\gg 0
,
T(\mathcal{F}_{C}|_{D})
is supported in codimension
\geq j
in
D
and

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha_{n-j}(F)}{\rk(F)}=\frac{\alpha_{n-j}(a)}{\rk(a)},

2.

for
a_{i}\gg 0
,
T_{i}
is supported in codimension
\geq j
in
D_{i}
and

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha_{n-j}(F_{i}/T_{i})}{\rk(F_{i}/T_{i})}=\frac{\alpha_{n-j}(a_{i})}{\rk(a_{i})}.

We will show by induction that
(P_{j})
holds true for
1\leq j\leq\ell+1
. First note that
\alpha_{n-1}(F)=\alpha_{n-1}(\mathcal{F}_{C}|_{D})=\alpha_{n-1}(a)
by the flatness of
\mathcal{F}_{C}
. The second part of
(P_{1})
is also clearly satisfied. We next show the induction step, whose proof we divided in a sequence of lemmas. In what follows, let
j>1
and suppose that
(P_{1}),\ldots,(P_{j-1})
hold true.

Lemma 3.2
.

For
a_{1},a_{2}\gg 0
we have

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha_{n-j}(a)}{\rk(a)}\geq{}

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha_{n-j}(F_{1}/T_{1})}{\rk(F_{1}/T_{1})}+\frac{\alpha_{n-j}(F_{2}/T_{2})}{\rk(F_{2}/T_{2})}-

\displaystyle\sum_{l=1}^{j-1}\frac{(-1)^{l-1}}{2l!}\left(a_{2}^{l}\frac{\alpha_{n-j+l}(F_{1}/T_{1})}{\rk(F_{1}/T_{1})}+a_{1}^{l}\frac{\alpha_{n-j+l}(F_{2}/T_{2})}{\rk(F_{2}/T_{2})}\right).

(3.1)

Proof.

Consider the short exact sequence

\displaystyle 0\to\mathcal{O}_{D}\to\mathcal{O}_{D_{1}}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{D_{2}}\to\mathcal{O}_{D_{1}\cap D_{2}}\to 0

corresponding to the normal crossing divisor
D=D_{1}+D_{2}
. After tensoring with the pure sheaf
F
the sequence

\displaystyle 0\to F\to F_{1}\oplus F_{2}\to F|_{D_{1}\cap D_{2}}\to 0

(3.2)

remains exact. We get by additivity

\displaystyle\alpha_{n-j}(F)=\alpha_{n-j}(F_{1})+\alpha_{n-j}(F_{2})-\alpha_{n-j}(F|_{D_{1}\cap D_{2}}).

From the short exact sequence

\displaystyle 0\to T_{i}\to F_{i}\to F_{i}/T_{i}\to 0,

(3.3)

we also have

\displaystyle\alpha_{n-j}(F_{i})=\alpha_{n-j}(T_{i})+\alpha_{n-j}(F_{i}/T_{i}).

As
F_{i}/T_{i}
has no torsion over
D_{1}\cap D_{2}
, the restriction of (
3.3
) to
D_{1}\cap D_{2}
gives the short exact sequence

\displaystyle 0\to T_{i}|_{D_{1}\cap D_{2}}\to F|_{D_{1}\cap D_{2}}\to(F_{i}/T_{i})|_{D_{1}\cap D_{2}}\to 0.

Thus

\displaystyle\alpha_{n-j}(F|_{D_{1}\cap D_{2}})=\alpha_{n-j}(T_{i}|_{D_{1}\cap D_{2}})+\alpha_{n-j}((F_{i}/T_{i})|_{D_{1}\cap D_{2}}).

Putting together the above identities and using
\alpha_{n-j}(T_{i})\geq\alpha_{n-j}(T_{i}|_{D_{1}\cap D_{2}})
, we get

\displaystyle\alpha_{n-j}(F)\geq{}

\displaystyle\alpha_{n-j}(F_{1})+\alpha_{n-j}(F_{2}/T_{2})-\alpha_{n-j}((F_{2}/T_{2})|_{D_{1}\cap D_{2}})

\displaystyle\geq{}

\displaystyle\alpha_{n-j}(F_{1}/T_{1})+\alpha_{n-j}(F_{2}/T_{2})-\alpha_{n-j}((F_{2}/T_{2})|_{D_{1}\cap D_{2}}),

(3.4)

where the last inequality holds since the torsion is supported in codimension
\geq j-1
by the induction hypothesis.

On the other hand, from the short exact sequence

\displaystyle 0\to(F_{2}/T_{2})(-D_{1})\to F_{2}/T_{2}\to(F_{2}/T_{2})|_{D_{1}\cap D_{2}}\to 0,

it follows by formula (
2
) that

\displaystyle\alpha_{n-j}((F_{2}/T_{2})|_{D_{1}\cap D_{2}})=\sum_{l=1}^{j-1}\frac{(-1)^{l-1}a_{1}^{l}}{l!}\alpha_{n-j+l}(F_{2}/T_{2}).

From this we can rewrite (
3
) as

\displaystyle\alpha_{n-j}(F)\geq\alpha_{n-j}(F_{1}/T_{1})+\alpha_{n-j}(F_{2}/T_{2})-\sum_{l=1}^{j-1}\frac{(-1)^{l-1}a_{1}^{l}}{l!}\alpha_{n-j+l}(F_{2}/T_{2}).

By a similar computation, we also have

\displaystyle\alpha_{n-j}(F)\geq\alpha_{n-j}(F_{1}/T_{1})+\alpha_{n-j}(F_{2}/T_{2})-\sum_{l=1}^{j-1}\frac{(-1)^{l-1}a_{2}^{l}}{l!}\alpha_{n-j+l}(F_{1}/T_{1}).

Adding the last two inequalities and dividing by 2 we obtain

\displaystyle\alpha_{n-j}(F)\geq{}

\displaystyle\alpha_{n-j}(F_{1}/T_{1})+\alpha_{n-j}(F_{2}/T_{2})-

\displaystyle\sum_{l=1}^{j-1}\frac{(-1)^{l-1}}{2l!}(a_{2}^{l}\alpha_{n-j+l}(F_{1}/T_{1})+a_{1}^{l}\alpha_{n-j+l}(F_{2}/T_{2})).

(3.5)

By the flatness of
\mathcal{F}_{C}
over
C
, we have
\alpha_{n-j}(\mathcal{F}_{C}|_{D})=\alpha_{n-j}(a)
. Moreover, since
\Supp(T(\mathcal{F}_{C}|_{D}))
has codimension
\geq j-1
in
D
by
(P_{j-1})
, we get

\displaystyle\alpha_{n-j}(a)=\alpha_{n-j}(\mathcal{F}_{C}|_{D})\geq\alpha_{n-j}(F).

(3.6)

We also have
\rk(F_{i}/T_{i})=\rk(F)=\rk(a)
(see
[
5
, Lem. 2.12]
). From these considerations, dividing (
3
) by
\rk(a)
we finally deduce (
3.2
).
∎

Lemma 3.3
.

For
a_{1},a_{2}\gg 0
we have

\displaystyle\frac{\beta_{n-j}(a)}{\rk(a)}\geq\frac{\beta_{n-j}(a_{1})}{\rk(a_{1})}+\frac{\beta_{n-j}(a_{2})}{\rk(a_{2})}.

Proof.

The basic idea is to use (
2.2
) in order to replace the
\alpha
-terms in (

) by

-terms. This way the LHS of (
3.2
) can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha_{n-j}(a)}{\rk(a)}=\frac{\beta_{n-j}(a)}{\rk(a)}+\sum_{l=1}^{j-1}\frac{(-1)^{l}}{(l+1)!}a^{l}\frac{\beta_{n-j+l}(a)}{\rk(a)}.

We are now concerned with the RHS of (
3.2
). A straightforward computation gives

\displaystyle\sum_{l=1}^{j-1}\frac{(-1)^{l-1}a_{2}^{l}}{2l!}\frac{\alpha_{n-j+l}(F_{1}/T_{1})}{\rk(F_{1}/T_{1})}=

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\sum_{l=1}^{j-1}\frac{(-1)^{l-1}a_{2}^{l}}{2l!}\sum_{s=1}^{j-l}\frac{(-1)^{s-1}a_{1}^{s-1}}{s!}\frac{\beta_{n-j+l+s-1}(a_{1})}{\rk(a_{1})}

(by (
2.2
) and
(P_{<j}.2
))

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\sum_{p=1}^{j-1}\frac{\beta_{n-j+p}(a_{1})}{\rk(a_{1})}\sum_{l=1}^{p}\frac{(-1)^{l-1}a_{2}^{l}}{2l!}\frac{(-1)^{p-l}a_{1}^{p-l}}{(p-l+1)!}

(by the change of variables

)

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\sum_{p=1}^{j-1}\frac{\beta_{n-j+p}(a_{1})}{a_{1}\rk(a_{1})}\frac{(-1)^{p-1}}{2(p+1)!}((a_{1}+a_{2})^{p+1}-a_{1}^{p+1}-a_{2}^{p+1})

(by binomial formula)

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\sum_{l=1}^{j-1}\frac{(-1)^{l-1}}{2(l+1)!}\frac{\beta_{n-j+l}(a_{1})}{a_{1}\rk(a_{1})}(a^{l+1}-a_{1}^{l+1}-a_{2}^{l+1}).

(by the change of variables
l=p
)

In a similar fashion,

\displaystyle\sum_{l=1}^{j-1}\frac{(-1)^{l-1}a_{1}^{l}}{2l!}\frac{\alpha_{n-j+l}(F_{2}/T_{2})}{\rk(F_{2}/T_{2})}=\sum_{l=1}^{j-1}\frac{(-1)^{l-1}}{2(l+1)!}\frac{\beta_{n-j+l}(a_{2})}{a_{2}\rk(a_{2})}(a^{l+1}-a_{2}^{l+1}-a_{1}^{l+1}).

Adding the last two identities, for

we obtain by Remark

that

\displaystyle\sum_{l=1}^{j-1}\frac{(-1)^{l-1}}{2l!}(a_{2}^{l}\frac{\alpha_{n-j+l}(F_{1}/T_{1})}{\rk(F_{1}/T_{1})}+a_{1}^{l}\frac{\alpha_{n-j+l}(F_{2}/T_{2})}{\rk(F_{2}/T_{2})}=

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\sum_{l=1}^{j-1}\frac{(-1)^{l-1}}{(l+1)!}\left(\frac{\beta_{n-j+l}(a)}{a\rk(a)}a^{l+1}-\frac{\beta_{n-j+l}(a_{1})}{a_{1}\rk(a_{1})}a_{1}^{l+1}-\frac{\beta_{n-j+l}(a_{2})}{a_{2}\rk(a_{2})}a_{2}^{l+1}\right).

(3.7)

As

is a torsion-free quotient of

, it must satisfy

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha_{n-j}(F_{i}/T_{i})}{\rk(F_{i}/T_{i})}\geq\frac{\alpha_{n-j}(\mathcal{F}_{a_{i}}|_{D_{i}})}{\rk(\mathcal{F}_{a_{i}}|_{D_{i}})}=\frac{\alpha_{n-j}(a_{i})}{\rk(a_{i})}.

(3.8)

Hence the RHS of
(

)
satisfies

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha_{n-j}(F_{1}/T_{1})}{\rk(F_{1}/T_{1})}+\frac{\alpha_{n-j}(F_{2}/T_{2})}{\rk(F_{2}/T_{2})}-\sum_{l=1}^{j-1}\frac{(-1)^{l-1}}{2l!}\left(a_{2}^{l}\frac{\alpha_{n-j+l}(F_{1}/T_{1})}{\rk(F_{1}/T_{1})}+a_{1}^{l}\frac{\alpha_{n-j+l}(F_{2}/T_{2})}{\rk(F_{2}/T_{2})}\right)

\displaystyle\geq{}

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha_{n-j}(a_{1})}{\rk(a_{1})}+\frac{\alpha_{n-j}(a_{2})}{\rk(a_{2})}-

(by (

) and (

))

\displaystyle\sum_{l=1}^{j-1}\frac{(-1)^{l-1}}{(l+1)!}\left(\frac{\beta_{n-j+l}(a)}{a\rk(a)}a^{l+1}-\frac{\beta_{n-j+l}(a_{1})}{a_{1}\rk(a_{1})}a_{1}^{l+1}-\frac{\beta_{n-j+l}(a_{2})}{a_{2}\rk(a_{2})}a_{2}^{l+1}\right)

\displaystyle\geq{}

\displaystyle\frac{\beta_{n-j}(a_{1})}{\rk(a_{1})}+\frac{\beta_{n-j}(a_{2})}{\rk(a_{2})}+\sum_{l=1}^{j-1}\frac{(-1)^{l}}{(l+1)!}a^{l}\frac{\beta_{n-j+l}(a)}{\rk(a)}.

(by (

))

Comparing both sides of (

), we conclude that for

\displaystyle\frac{\beta_{n-j}(a)}{\rk(a)}\geq\frac{\beta_{n-j}(a_{1})}{\rk(a_{1})}+\frac{\beta_{n-j}(a_{2})}{\rk(a_{2})}.

∎

Lemma 3.4
.

\frac{\beta_{n-j}(a)}{a\rk(a)}
is constant for

.

Proof.

We already know the result holds true for

by Remark

, so it only remains to prove it when

.

First, we show that
\{\frac{\beta_{n-1-\ell}(a)}{a\rk(a)}:a>0\}
is a discrete subset of
\mathbb{Q}
. Recall that

is isomorphic to the projective bundle
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{K}^{\vee})\to X
and, via this isomorphism, we have
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{K}^{\vee})}(1)\cong p^{*}(\mathcal{O}_{\Pi_{a}}(1))
(see Remark
3.1
). Denote by
\xi
the class of
\mathcal{O}_{\Pi_{a}}(1)
in the Grothendieck group

and set

. We have the following isomorphism of

-algebras (see
[

)

\displaystyle K(Z_{a})\cong K(\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{K}^{\vee}))\cong K(X)[T]/(\sum_{i=0}^{r}(-1)^{i}cl(\Lambda^{r-i}(\mathcal{K}^{\vee}))T^{i}),

where
cl(-)
denotes the corresponding Grothendieck class. Moreover

\displaystyle T\text{ mod }(\sum_{i=0}^{r}(-1)^{i}cl(\Lambda^{r-i}(\mathcal{K}^{\vee}))T^{i})=cl(p^{*}\xi)\in K(Z_{a}).

Using this isomorphism we can write

\displaystyle cl(\mathcal{F}_{a})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{r-1}cl(G_{i})cl(p^{*}\xi)^{i}

(3.9)

for
G_{i}
coherent sheaves on
X
. As
X
is smooth and projective, any

has a finite free resolution. Thus, there is a finite sum

\displaystyle cl(G_{i})=\sum_{p}n_{i,p}cl(G_{i,p})

in

with

and
G_{i,p}
locally free sheaves on
X
.
Restricting (
3.9
) to a fiber over

, we obtain

\displaystyle i^{*}cl(\mathcal{F}_{a})=\sum_{i=0}^{r-1}\sum_{p}n_{i,p}cl(G_{i,p}|_{D})

in

, where

is the natural immersion. For
1\leq j\leq\ell+1
, we get by additivity

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha_{n-j}(a)}{a\rk(a)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{r-1}\sum_{p}n_{i,p}\frac{\alpha_{n-j}(G_{i,p}|_{D})}{a\rk(a)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{r-1}\sum_{p}n_{i,p}\sum_{l=1}^{j}\frac{(-1)^{l-1}a^{l-1}}{l!}\frac{\alpha_{n-j+l}(G_{i,p})}{\rk(a)}\in\frac{\mathbb{Z}}{(\ell+1)!\rk(E)!}.

from which we obtain that
\{\frac{\beta_{n-1-\ell}(a)}{a\rk(a)}:a>0\}
is discrete in
\mathbb{Q}
.

Second, let us show that the function
a\mapsto\frac{\beta_{n-1-\ell}(a)}{a\rk(a)}
is bounded from above. By Remark
3.2
we have

\displaystyle\frac{\beta_{n-j}(a)}{a\rk(a)}=\frac{\alpha_{n-j+1}(E)}{\rk(E)}

(3.10)

for
1\leq j\leq\ell
. Then

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha_{n-1-\ell}(E|_{D})}{a\rk(E|_{D})}={}

\displaystyle\sum_{l=1}^{\ell+1}\frac{(-1)^{l-1}a^{l}}{l!}\frac{\alpha_{n-1-\ell+l}(E)}{a\rk(E|_{D})}

(by (
2
))

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha_{n-\ell}(E)}{\rk(E|_{D})}+\sum_{l=2}^{\ell+1}\frac{(-1)^{l-1}a^{l-1}}{l!}\frac{\beta_{n-1-\ell+l}(a)}{a\rk(a)}.

(by (
3.10
))

On the other hand, as

is an
\ell
-destabilizing quotient, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha_{n-1-\ell}(E|_{D})}{a\rk(E|_{D})}\geq{}

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha_{n-1-\ell}(a)}{a\rk(a)}

\displaystyle\geq{}

\displaystyle\frac{\beta_{n-1-\ell}(a)}{a\rk(a)}+\sum_{l=2}^{\ell+1}\frac{(-1)^{l-1}a^{l-1}}{l!}\frac{\beta_{n-1-\ell+l}(a)}{a\rk(a)}.

(by (
2.2
))

Then

\displaystyle\frac{\beta_{n-1-\ell}(a)}{a\rk(a)}\leq\frac{\alpha_{n-\ell}(E)}{\rk(E)}.

Finally, we prove that
\frac{\beta_{n-1-\ell}(a)}{a\rk(a)}
is constant for
a\gg 0
. Since the map is bounded from above and discrete, its maximum is attained at an integer
b>0
. Consider the set

\displaystyle B=\{\frac{\beta_{n-1-\ell}(b^{\prime})}{b^{\prime}\rk(b^{\prime})}:b^{\prime}\text{ and }b\text{ are relatively prime}\}.

There exists
b^{\prime}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\frac{\beta_{n-1-\ell}(b^{\prime})}{b^{\prime}\rk(b^{\prime})}
is the maximum of

. Suppose that
a=cb+c^{\prime}b^{\prime}
for some integers
c,c^{\prime}>0
. By Lemma (
3.3
) there is an integer
c_{0}>0
such that for
c,c^{\prime}\geq c_{0}

\displaystyle\frac{\beta_{n-1-\ell}(a)}{a\rk(a)}\geq{}

\displaystyle\frac{c}{a}\frac{\beta_{n-1-\ell}(b)}{\rk(b)}+\frac{c^{\prime}}{a}\frac{\beta_{n-1-\ell}(b^{\prime})}{\rk(b^{\prime})}

\displaystyle\geq{}

\displaystyle\frac{cb}{a}\frac{\beta_{n-1-\ell}(b)}{b\rk(b)}+\frac{c^{\prime}b^{\prime}}{a}\frac{\beta_{n-1-\ell}(b)}{b^{\prime}\rk(b^{\prime})}

\displaystyle\geq{}

\displaystyle\frac{\beta_{n-1-\ell}(b^{\prime})}{b^{\prime}\rk(b^{\prime})}.

But
a
and
b^{\prime}
are coprime, hence we have equality above. In particular

\displaystyle\frac{\beta_{n-1-\ell}(b)}{b\rk(b)}=\frac{\beta_{n-1-\ell}(b^{\prime})}{b^{\prime}\rk(b^{\prime})},

which further gives

\displaystyle\frac{\beta_{n-1-\ell}(a)}{a\rk(a)}=\frac{\beta_{n-1-\ell}(b)}{b\rk(b)}.

As
b
and
b^{\prime}
are coprime, for
a\gg 0
there are always integers

such that
a=cb+c^{\prime}b^{\prime}
, which concludes the proof.
∎

Corollary 3.1

(P_{j})

Proof.

By Lemma

\displaystyle\frac{\beta_{n-j}(a)}{\rk(a)}=\frac{\beta_{n-j}(a_{1})}{\rk(a_{1})}+\frac{\beta_{n-j}(a_{2})}{\rk(a_{2})}

Geometric points of the moduli space

We end this section by studying the geometric points of
M
. If
E
is an
(n-1)
-semistable sheaf on
X
, then there exists a Jordan-Hölder filtration

\displaystyle 0=E_{0}\subset E_{1}\subset\ldots\subset E_{l}=E

such that its factors
E_{i}/E_{i-1}
are
(n-1)
-stable with
p_{n-1}(E_{i}/E_{i-1})=p_{n-1}(E)
. We denote by
\gr(E)\coloneqq\oplus_{i}E_{i}/E_{i-1}
its corresponding graded torsion-free module. We say that
E
is
(n-1)
-
polystable
if
E
is isomorphic to a direct sum of
(n-1)
-stable sheaves.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
E
be an
(n-1)
-semistable sheaf on
X
and
\gr(E)
the graded module corresponding to a Jordan-Hölder filtration of
E
. Then
E
and
\gr(E)
give the same point in
M
.

Proof.

It is not difficult to construct an
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-flat family
\mathcal{E}
of
(n-1)
-semistable sheaves on
X
such that
\mathcal{E}_{0}\cong\gr(E)
and
\mathcal{E}_{t}\cong E
for all
t\neq 0
. Then the classifying morphism
\Psi_{\mathcal{E}}:\mathbb{A}^{1}\to M
corresponding to
\mathcal{E}
is constant, which shows that
E
and
\gr(E)
define the same point in
M
.
∎

Thus, it will suffice to analyze the separation of polystable sheaves. If
E
is
(n-1)
-polystable, we denote by
E^{[n-1]}
the corresponding torsion-free sheaf satisfying property
(\star)
given by Lemma
2.4
. Note that
E^{[n-1]}
is also
(n-1)
-polystable as it differs from
E
only in a finite number of points. We let
l_{E}
denote the zero-cycle associated to
E^{[n-1]}/E
.

Next we aim to prove the following result:

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
be two
(n-1)
-polystable sheaves of class
c
on
X
. Then
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
define the same point in
M
if and only if
E_{1}^{[n-1]}\cong E_{2}^{[n-1]}
and
l_{E_{1}}=l_{E_{2}}
.

Remark 4.2
.

Let
E
be an
(n-1)
-semistable sheaf on
X
and
\gr(E)
the graded module corresponding to a Jordan-Hölder filtration of
E
. Note that
\gr(E)
is uniquely determined only in codimension
n-1
. However, one can use the result above to show that the pair
(\gr(E)^{[n-1]},l_{\gr(E)})
is in fact uniquely determined by
E
. Indeed, suppose we have two graded modules
\gr_{1}(E)
and
\gr_{2}(E)
corresponding to two different Jordan-Hölder filtrations of
E
. Then, by Lemma
4.2
and Theorem
4.3
, we must have
\gr_{1}(E)^{[n-1]}\cong\gr_{2}(E)^{[n-1]}
and
l_{\gr_{1}(E)}=l_{\gr_{2}(E)}
.

We divided the proof of Theorem
4.3
in a sequence of lemmas.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
be two
(n-1)
-polystable sheaves of class
c
on
X
such that
E_{1}^{[n-1]}\ncong E_{2}^{[n-1]}
. Then
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
give distinct points in
M
.

Proof.

By Theorem
1.2
and
[
10
, Lem. 1.1.13]
, we can choose a general divisor
D\in\Pi_{a}
, with
a\gg 0
, such that

1.

E_{1}|_{D}
and
E_{2}|_{D}
remain polystable,

2.

E_{1}|_{D}=E_{1}^{[n-1]}|_{D}
and
E_{2}|_{D}=E_{2}^{[n-1]}|_{D}
are torsion-free and satisfy
(\star)
.

As in the proof of Lemma
3.7
, one can show that

\displaystyle\Hom(E_{1}^{[n-1]},E_{2}^{[n-1]})\to\Hom(E_{1}^{[n-1]}|_{D},E_{2}^{[n-1]}|_{D})

is an isomorphism for
a\gg 0
. By assumption
E_{1}^{[n-1]}\ncong E_{2}^{[n-1]}
, and thus
E_{1}|_{D}
and
E_{2}|_{D}
are non-isomorphic polystable sheaves on

. Then
E_{1}|_{D}
and
E_{2}|_{D}
give distinct points in the Simpson moduli space

of Gieseker-semistable sheaves on

. Hence there exists a section
\sigma\in H^{0}(\overline{R}_{D},H_{l^{\prime}}^{\nu^{\prime}})^{G^{\prime}}
, for some
\nu^{\prime}>0
, that separates

and
E_{2}|_{D}
(see Remark

for notation). Following the proof of Theorem
4.1
, we find a section
\Gamma(\sigma)\in H^{0}(R^{ss},\mathcal{L}^{\nu})^{G}
, for
\nu>0
, that separates
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
in
R^{ss}
. This implies that
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
give distinct points in
M
.
∎

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
E_{1}
and

be two
(n-1)
-polystable sheaves of class

on

. Then

and

give distinct points in

Proof.

The proof goes as in
[

, we omit it.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
be two
(n-1)
-polystable sheaves of class
c
on
X
such that
E_{1}^{[n-1]}\cong E_{2}^{[n-1]}
and
l_{E_{1}}=l_{E_{2}}
. Then
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
define the same point in
M
.

Proof.

Set
F\coloneqq E_{1}^{[n-1]}\cong E_{2}^{[n-1]}
,
\xi\coloneqq l_{E_{1}}=l_{E_{2}}
and
l\coloneqq\length(\xi)
. Then
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
correspond to two closed points

and

respectively in the Quot scheme
\Quot(F,l)
. Denote by
\rho:\Quot(F,l)\to S^{l}(X)
the Quot-to-Chow morphism that associates to any zero-dimensional quotient of
F
of length

its corresponding zero-cycle. By assumption
y_{1}
and
y_{2}
lie inside the fiber
\rho^{-1}(\xi)
.

Suppose that
y_{1}
and
y_{2}
lie in the same connected component
C
of
\rho^{-1}(\xi)
. Let
\mathcal{U}
be the universal family of quotients over
\Quot(F,l)
, which fits in a short exact sequence

\displaystyle 0\to\mathcal{K}\to\mathcal{O}_{\Quot}\otimes F\to\mathcal{U}\to 0

such that
\mathcal{K}
is flat over
\Quot(F,l)
. As
F
is
(n-1)
-semistable, it follows that
\mathcal{K}|_{C}
is a
C
-flat family of
(n-1)
-semistable sheaves of class
c
on
X
. Choose a general divisor
D\in\Pi_{a}
avoiding the support of
\xi
such that

is Gieseker-polystable, which exists for

by Theorem

. Then there exists a short exact sequence

\displaystyle 0\to\mathcal{K}|_{C}(-D)\to\mathcal{K}|_{C}\to\mathcal{K}|_{C\times D}\to 0

such that
\mathcal{K}|_{C\times D}
is a

-flat family of fiber

, cf.

. This induces a constant morphism

, where

denotes the Simpson moduli space of Gieseker-semistable sheaves of Hilbert polynomial

on

, there exists a natural determinant line bundle

over

such that

\displaystyle\Psi_{\mathcal{K}}^{*}(\lambda(w|_{D}))\cong\lambda_{\mathcal{K}|_{C\times D}}(w|_{D})\cong\lambda_{\mathcal{K}|_{C}}(w\cdot h),

to the short exact sequence (

) and using

. But since

is constant, we obtain that

is trivial. Therefore, the classifying morphism

corresponding to the family

, as given by Theorem

and

if they lie in the same connected component of

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
F
be a torsion-free sheaf on
X

\xi\in S^{l}(X)
a zero-cycle of length

. Then the fiber
\Quot_{\xi}(F,l)\coloneqq\rho^{-1}(\xi)
over
\xi
of the Quot-to-Chow morphism
\rho:\Quot(F,l)\to S^{l}(X)
is connected.

Proof.

The proof is by induction on
l
. It is enough to prove the statement when
\xi
is supported at a single closed point of
X
, say
x\in X
. If
l=1
, then
\xi
corresponds to the closed point
x\in X
, and so
\Quot_{\xi}(F,1)
is isomorphic to the projective space
\mathbb{P}(F(x))
, with
F(x)=F_{x}\otimes k(x)
, which is clearly connected.

Now suppose that
\Quot_{\xi}(F,l)
is connected for some
l>0
. Let
\mathcal{U}
be the universal family of quotients over
\Quot_{\xi}(F,l)
and consider the short exact sequence

\displaystyle 0\to\mathcal{K}\to\mathcal{O}_{\Quot}\otimes F\to\mathcal{U}\to 0

over
\Quot_{\xi}(F,l)
. Then
Z\coloneqq\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{K})
is a projective space over
\Quot_{\xi}(F,l)\times X
. Note that
Z
is connected, since
\Quot_{\xi}(F,l)\times X
is so.

Next we want to construct a surjective morphism
\varphi:Z\to\Quot_{\xi}(F,l+1)
. Let us first describe this map set-theoretically, at the level of geometric points. By construction, a closed point of
Z
corresponds to a quotient
[\mu:K\to k(x)]\in\mathbb{P}(K)
, where
K
is the kernel of some closed point
[F\to Q]\in\Quot_{\xi}(F,l)
. If
K^{\prime}\coloneqq\Ker(\mu)
, then

is a zero-dimensional quotient of
F
of length

on
X
. We send
[K\to k(x)]\in Z
through
\varphi
to the quotient
[F\to F/K^{\prime}]\in\Quot_{\xi}(F,l+1)
. In fact, one can do this construction in families, as described in
[
3
, Prop. 5]
, to define an algebraic morphism
\varphi:Z\to\Quot_{\xi}(F,l+1)
. It remains to show that
\varphi
is surjective. Let
[q:F\to T]\in\Quot_{\xi}(F,l+1)
be a closed point and denote by
K\coloneqq\Ker(q)
its kernel. Let
M\subset T_{x}
be the subsheaf of all elements in
T_{x}
annihilated by the maximal ideal in
\mathcal{O}_{X,x}
and choose a point
[\lambda:k(x)\to M]\in\mathbb{P}(M^{\vee})
. This induces a diagram as follows
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces k(x)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces T}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Q}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}

such that

is a zero-dimensional quotient of length

on

. Then
[\mu:K\to k(x)]\in Z
is mapped to

by

Abstract
. Let C C be smooth irreducible projective curve of genus g ≥ 2 g\geq 2 . Let ℳ C ​ ( n , δ ) \mathcal{M}_{C}(n,\delta) be the moduli space of stable vector bundles on C C of rank n n and fixed determinant δ \delta of degree d d . Then the locus of wobbly bundles is known to be closed in ℳ C ​ ( n , δ ) \mathcal{M}_{C}(n,\delta) . 1.
Introduction

Let
C
be a smooth projective complex curve of genus
g\geq 2
and let
K_{C}
be its canonical line bundle.
Fixing a line bundle
\delta
of degree
d
we consider the coarse moduli space

\mathcal{M}_{C}(n,\delta)
parameterizing semi-stable rank-
n
vector bundles of fixed
determinant of degree
d
over
C
. We recall that a vector bundle
E
is called very stable if
E
has no nonzero nilpotent Higgs field

\phi\in H^{0}(C,\text{End}(E)\otimes K_{C})
. A stable vector bundle is called wobbly if it is not very stable. G. Laumon
[
3
, Proposition 3.5]
proved, assuming

\mathcal{M}_{C}(n,\delta)
. Hence the locus of wobbly bundles is a closed subset

\mathcal{W}_{C}\subset\mathcal{M}_{C}(n,\delta).

Conjecture 1.1

(Drinfeld)
.

[
3
, Remarque 3.6 (ii)]

\mathcal{W}_{C}
is of pure codimension
1
.

The aim of this article is to give a proof of the above conjecture.

The term “wobbly" was introduced in the paper
[
2
]
. The conjecture has been proved completely for rank
2
case by current author of this article and jointly with C. Pauly in
[
4
]
and
[
5
]
.

Our approach in this article is completely different from
[
4
]
and
[
5
]
.

Note that the stable locus
\mathcal{M}^{s}_{C}(n,\delta)
is smooth. It is also known that
\mathcal{M}^{s}_{C}(n,\delta)
is a Fano manifold of Picard number one.
Hence, it can be covered by rational curves. It is known that a general rational curve in
\mathcal{M}^{s}_{C}(n,\delta)
is free (for definition of free rational curves, see Section
2
).
Our main idea to prove the conjecture is to
study the nonfree rational curves in
\mathcal{M}_{C}(n,\delta)
. In fact we show that a vector bundles on
C
is wobbly if and only if there is a nonfree rational curve through it and then we show that
the locus of nonfree rational curves forms a divisor.

Organization of the paper
:

In section
2

In section
3

Theorem 1.1
.

A stable vector bundle
E\in\mathcal{M}_{C}(n,\delta)
is wobbly if and only if it passes through a nonfree minimal rational curve.

.

In section
4

. In fact, we will show that this locus forms a divisor. Finally, using theorem
1.1

we prove Conjecture

H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{1},R^{1}{p_{2}}_{*}(\text{ Ad}(\mathcal{E}_{2})\otimes p_{2}^{*}{\mathcal{O}}(-1)))=H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{1},R^{1}{p_{2}}_{*}\text{Ad}(\mathcal{E}_{1}\otimes p_{2}^{*}\mathcal{O}(-1)\oplus H^{1}(\mathcal{E}_{1}\otimes p_{1}^{*}E_{3}^{*})\oplus H^{1}(\mathcal{E}_{1}^{*}\otimes p_{1}^{*}E_{3}\otimes p_{2}^{*}\mathcal{O}(-2)).

Tensoring
3.2
, by
p_{1}^{*}E_{3}^{*}
and considering the cohomology, we have

(3.16)

H^{1}(\mathcal{E}_{1}\otimes p_{1}^{*}E_{3}^{*})=H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{1},\mathcal{O}(1))\otimes H^{1}(C,E_{1}\otimes E_{3}^{*})\oplus H^{1}(C,E_{2}\otimes E_{3}^{*})

and tensoring by
p_{1}^{*}E_{3}\otimes p_{2}^{*}\mathcal{O}(-2)
to the dual of
3.2
and considering the cohomologies, we have

(3.17)

\displaystyle 0\to H^{0}(C,E_{3}\otimes E_{2}^{*})\to H^{1}(\mathcal{E}_{1}^{*}\otimes p_{1}^{*}E_{3}\otimes\mathcal{O}(-2))\to H^{0}(C,E_{1}^{*}\otimes E_{3})\otimes H^{1}(\mathbb{P}^{1},\mathcal{O}(-3))

\to H^{1}(C,E_{3}\otimes E_{2}^{*})\to H^{2}(\mathcal{E}_{1}^{*}\otimes p_{1}^{*}E_{3}\otimes p_{2}^{*}\mathcal{O}(-2))\to H^{1}(E_{1}^{*}\otimes E_{3})\otimes H^{1}(\mathbb{P}^{1},\mathcal{O}(-3))\to 0.

Since
H^{0}(E_{3}^{*}\otimes E_{1}\otimes K_{c})=0
, the map

H^{1}(C,E_{3}\otimes E_{2}^{*})\to H^{2}(\mathcal{E}_{1}^{*}\otimes p_{1}^{*}E_{3}\otimes p_{2}^{*}\mathcal{O}(-2))

in
3.17
, is surjective. The kernel of this map is the co-kernel of the dual map

H^{0}(\mathcal{E}_{1}\otimes p_{1}^{*}E_{3}^{*}\otimes p_{1}^{*}K_{C})\to H^{0}(E_{3}^{*}\otimes E_{2}\otimes K_{C}).

Tensoring
3.1
by
p_{1}^{*}E_{3}^{*}\otimes p_{1}^{*}K_{C}
and sonsidering the cohomology, we have

H^{0}(\mathcal{E}_{1}\otimes p_{1}^{*}E_{3}^{*}\otimes p_{1}^{*}K_{C})\simeq H^{0}(E_{3}^{*}\otimes E_{2}\otimes K_{C}).

(3.18)

H^{1}(\mathcal{E}_{1}^{*}\otimes p_{1}^{*}E_{3}\otimes\mathcal{O}(-2))=H^{0}(C,E_{3}\otimes E_{2}^{*})\oplus H^{0}(C,E_{1}^{*}\otimes E_{3})\otimes H^{1}(\mathbb{P}^{1},\mathcal{O}(-3)).

By
3.8
,
3.15
,
3.16
and
3.18
, we have

(3.19)

\displaystyle h^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{1},R^{1}{p_{2}}_{*}(\text{ Ad}(\mathcal{E})\otimes p_{2}^{*}{\mathcal{O}}(-1)))=2(r_{1}d_{2}-r_{2}d_{1})+h^{0}(C,E_{1}\otimes E_{2}^{*}\otimes K_{C})+

2h^{1}(C,E_{1}\otimes E_{3}^{*})\oplus h^{1}(C,E_{2}\otimes E_{3}^{*})+h^{0}(C,E_{3}\otimes E_{2}^{*})\oplus 2h^{0}(C,E_{1}^{*}\otimes E_{3}).

By stability of
E,h^{0}(E_{3}^{*}\otimes E_{1})=h^{0}(E_{2}\otimes E_{3}^{*})=0
, and by typothesis,
h^{0}(E_{3}^{*}\otimes E_{1}\otimes K_{C})=0
. Thus we have,

h^{1}(E_{1}\otimes E_{3}^{*})=r_{1}r_{3}(g-1)+(r_{1}d_{3}-r_{3}d_{1}),

h^{1}(E_{2}\otimes E_{3}^{*})=r_{2}r_{3}(g-1)+(r_{2}d_{3}-r_{3}d_{2}),

h^{0}(E_{3}\otimes E_{1}^{*})=(r_{1}d_{3}-r_{3}d_{1})-r_{1}r_{3}(g-1)

h^{0}(E_{3}\otimes E_{2}^{*})=(r_{2}d_{3}-d_{2}r_{3})-r_{2}r_{3}(g-1)+h^{0}(E_{3}^{*}\otimes E_{2}\otimes K_{C}).

Substituting all these in
3.19
, we get,

(3.20)

\displaystyle h^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{1},R^{1}{p_{2}}_{*}(\text{ Ad}(\mathcal{E})\otimes p_{2}^{*}{\mathcal{O}}(-1)))=2(r_{1}d_{2}-r_{2}d_{1})+4(r_{1}d_{3}-d_{1}r_{3})+2(r_{2}d_{3}-d_{2}r_{3})

+h^{0}(E_{3}^{*}\otimes E_{2}\otimes K_{C})+h^{0}(E_{2}^{*}\otimes E_{1}\otimes K_{C})=p+h^{0}(E_{3}^{*}\otimes E_{2}\otimes K_{C})+h^{0}(E_{2}^{*}\otimes E_{1}\otimes K_{C}).

For arbitrary
m
, using the following lemma and by induction one can conclude the theorem.
Since,
h^{0}(C,E_{2},\otimes E_{3}^{*}\otimes K_{C})\geq 1,h^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{1},R^{1}(p_{2})_{*}(\text{Ad}(\mathcal{E})\otimes\mathcal{O}(-1)))>p
.
Thus, by the discussion in the first paragraph of the converse part of the theorem, the rational curve
f:\mathbb{P}^{1}\to\mathcal{M}_{C}(n,\delta)
through
E
is not-free.

Lemma 3.4
.

Consider the sequence of exact sequences of vector bundles on
C\times\mathbb{P}^{1}
, obtained by successive extensions as in the above theorem:

(3.21)

0\to p_{1}^{*}E_{1}\otimes p_{2}^{*}{\mathcal{O}(1)}\to\mathcal{E}_{1}\to p_{1}^{*}E_{2}\to 0,

(3.22)

0\to\mathcal{E}_{1}\otimes p_{2}^{*}\mathcal{O}(1)\to\mathcal{E}_{2}\to p_{1}^{*}E_{3}\to 0

\cdots\cdots\cdots

(3.23)

0\to\mathcal{E}_{m-1}\otimes p_{2}^{*}\mathcal{O}(1)\to\mathcal{E}\to p_{1}^{*}E_{m+1}\to 0

Then for all
i+1<j
we have

(1).
H^{0}(\mathcal{E}_{i}^{*}\otimes p_{2}^{*}\mathcal{O}(-2)\otimes p_{1}^{*}E_{j})=H^{2}(\mathcal{E}_{i}\otimes p_{1}^{*}E_{j}^{*})=H^{2}(\mathcal{E}_{i}\otimes p_{2}^{*}\mathcal{O}(1)\otimes p_{1}^{*}E_{j}^{*})=0
.

(2).
H^{1}(\mathcal{E}_{i}\otimes p_{2}^{*}\mathcal{O}(k)\otimes p_{1}^{*}E_{j}^{*})=H^{1}(C,E_{i+1}\otimes E_{j}^{*})\otimes H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{1},\mathcal{O}(k))\oplus H^{1}(\mathcal{E}_{i-1}\otimes p_{2}^{*}\mathcal{O}(k+1)\otimes p_{1}^{*}E_{j}^{*})
.

Hence
H^{1}(\mathcal{E}_{i}\otimes p_{1}^{*}E_{j}^{*})=\oplus_{k=0}^{i}H^{1}(C,E_{j}^{*}\otimes E_{i+1-k})\otimes H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{1},\mathcal{O}(k))
.

(3).
H^{1}(\mathcal{E}_{i}^{*}\otimes p_{2}^{*}\mathcal{O}(-2)\otimes p_{1}^{*}E_{j})=\oplus_{k=0}^{i}H^{0}(C,E_{j}\otimes E_{i+1-k})\otimes H^{1}(\mathbb{P}^{1},\mathcal{O}(-2-k))
.

Proof.

We will prove the lemma by induction on
i
. For
i=1
, the lemma follows from the proof of the above theorem.

(1). Consider the dual exact sequence of
(i-1)
-th exact sequence in the sequence in the lemma

0\to E_{i+1}^{*}\to\mathcal{E}_{i}^{*}\to\mathcal{E}_{i-1}^{*}\otimes p_{2}^{*}\mathcal{O}(-1)\to 0.

Tensoring by
p_{1}^{*}E_{j}\otimes p_{2}^{*}\mathcal{O}(-2)
we have,

0\to p_{1}^{*}E_{i+1}^{*}\otimes p_{1}^{*}E_{j}\otimes p_{2}^{*}\mathcal{O}(-2)\to\mathcal{E}_{i}^{*}\otimes p_{1}^{*}E_{j}\otimes p_{2}^{*}\mathcal{O}(-2)\to\mathcal{E}_{i-1}^{*}\otimes p_{2}^{*}\mathcal{O}(-3)\otimes p_{1}^{*}E_{j}\to 0.

By induction hypothesys,
H^{0}(\mathcal{E}_{i-1}^{*}\otimes p_{2}^{*}\mathcal{O}(-3)\otimes p_{1}^{*}E_{j})=0
. Therefore, we have
H^{0}(\mathcal{E}_{i}^{*}\otimes p_{2}^{*}\mathcal{O}(-2)\otimes p_{1}^{*}E_{j})=0
.

Again tensoring the exact sequence

0\to\mathcal{E}_{i-1}\otimes p_{2}^{*}\mathcal{O}(1)\to\mathcal{E}_{i}\to p_{1}^{*}E_{i+1}\to 0.

by
p_{1}^{*}E_{j}^{*}
, we have

0\to\mathcal{E}_{i-1}\otimes p_{2}^{*}\mathcal{O}(1)\otimes p_{1}^{*}E_{j}^{*}\to\mathcal{E}_{i}\otimes p_{1}^{*}E_{j}^{*}\to p_{1}^{*}E_{i+1}\otimes p_{1}^{*}E_{j}^{*}\to 0.

Note that
H^{2}(p_{1}^{*}E_{i+1}\otimes p_{1}^{*}E_{j}^{*})=0
. Thus, we have a surjection from

H^{2}(\mathcal{E}_{i-1}\otimes p_{1}^{*}E_{j}^{*}\otimes p_{2}^{*}\mathcal{O}(1))\to H^{2}(\mathcal{E}_{i}\otimes p_{1}^{*}E_{j}^{*}).

Thus inductively we have a surjection from

H^{2}(p_{1}^{*}E_{1}\otimes p_{1}^{*}E_{j}\otimes p_{2}^{*}\mathcal{O}(i))\to H^{2}(\mathcal{E}_{i}\otimes p_{1}^{*}E_{j}^{*}).

But
H^{2}(p_{1}^{*}E_{1}\otimes p_{1}^{*}E_{j}\otimes p_{2}^{*}\mathcal{O}(i))=0
. Hence,
H^{2}(\mathcal{E}_{i}\otimes p_{1}^{*}E_{j}^{*})=0
, which also proves the last part of (1).

(2). Consider the exact sequence

0\to\mathcal{E}_{i-1}\otimes p_{2}^{*}\mathcal{O}(1)\to\mathcal{E}_{i}\to p_{1}^{*}E_{i+1}\to 0.

Tensoring by
p_{1}^{*}E_{j}\otimes p_{2}^{*}\mathcal{O}(k)
, we have,

0\to\mathcal{E}_{i-1}\otimes p_{2}^{*}\mathcal{O}(k+1)\otimes p_{1}^{*}E_{j}\to\mathcal{E}_{i}\otimes p_{1}^{*}E_{j}\otimes p_{2}^{*}\mathcal{O}(k)\to p_{1}^{*}E_{i+1}\otimes p_{1}^{*}E_{j}\otimes p_{2}^{*}\mathcal{O}(k)\to 0.

Note that
H^{0}(C,E_{i+1}\otimes E_{j}^{*})=0
and
H^{2}(\mathcal{E}_{i-1}\otimes p_{2}^{*}\mathcal{O}(k+1)\otimes p_{1}^{*}E_{j})=0
by (1),

H^{1}(\mathcal{E}_{i}\otimes p_{1}^{*}E_{j}\otimes p_{2}^{*}\mathcal{O}(k))=H^{1}(\mathcal{E}_{i-1}\otimes p_{2}^{*}\mathcal{O}(k+1)\otimes p_{1}^{*}E_{j})\oplus H^{1}(C,E_{i+1}\otimes E_{j}^{*})\otimes H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{1},\mathcal{O}(k)).

(3). Similar as (2).

∎

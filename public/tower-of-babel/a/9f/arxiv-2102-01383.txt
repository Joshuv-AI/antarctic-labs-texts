Stability-Constrained Markov Decision Processes Using MPC footnoteinfo
Mario Zanon, Sébastien Gros, Michele Palladino

Abstract
In this paper, we consider solving discounted Markov Decision Processes (MDPs) under the constraint that the resulting policy is stabilizing. In practice MDPs are solved based on some form of policy approximation. We will leverage recent results proposing to use Model Predictive Control (MPC) as a structured policy in the context of Reinforcement Learning to make it possible to introduce stability requirements directly inside the MPC-based policy. This will restrict the solution of the MDP to stabilizing policies by construction. The stability theory for MPC is most mature for the undiscounted MPC case. Hence, we will first show in this paper that stable discounted MDPs can be reformulated as undiscounted ones. This observation will entail that the MPC-based policy with stability requirements will produce the optimal policy for the discounted MDP if it is stable, and the best stabilizing policy otherwise.

Markov Decision Processes, Model Predictive Control, Stability, Safe Reinforcement Learning

Introduction

Markov Decision Processes (MDPs) include a wide class of problems in which a controlled stochastic system needs to minimize a prescribed cost function. A special case is obtained for deterministic systems, in which case the problem is often labelled optimal control. MDPs have been extensively studied
[
6
,
7
,
23
,
26
]
. Most of the existing literature focuses on studying the theoretical properties of MDPs from an optimization point of view, and on deriving algorithms to solve them, i.e., to compute optimal control policies.

Solving MDPs exactly is notoriously difficult, and practical approaches often rely on approximate Dynamic Programming or Reinforcement Learning (RL), using some form of function approximation
[
7
,
26
]
. The latter approximation approach has recently demonstrated the ability to solve problems that were previously considered intractable, see, e.g.,
[
1
,
28
]
. A recently proposed function approximator for RL is Model Predictive Control (MPC)
[
13
,
14
,
15
,
29
,
30
,
32
]
. One of the benefits of using MPC as a function approximator is the existence of a strong theory proving desirable properties such as safety, stability and some form of explainability
[
16
,
24
]
. This fact has motivated recent interest in combining MPC with learning techniques, see, e.g.,
[
3
,
4
,
10
,
17
,
21
,
27
]
.

To the best of our knowledge, limited attention has been devoted to the enforcement of stability conditions in MDPs. The study of the stability properties of Markov Chains has been extensively studied in
[
20
]
, while the stability properties of undiscounted optimal control for both deterministic systems and stochastic systems with bounded noise have been studied, e.g., in
[
16
,
24
]
. The derivation of conditions for the stability of discounted problems is harder and some results have been obtained in, e.g.,
[
12
,
22
]
.

Unfortunately, deriving conditions for stability is in general difficult; moreover, such conditions are often difficult to verify in practice. In this paper we aim at imposing stability conditions by explicitly constraining the candidate policies to be stabilizing. We will do so by leveraging the existing stability theory for undiscounted MPC. As proven in
[
13
]
, under mild conditions, the MDP optimal policy can be captured via an MPC scheme with an approximate model provided that it is discounted by the same factor as the MDP.

Because we want to capture the solution of a discounted MDP using an undiscounted MPC, the theoretical gap between the discounted and undiscounted case must be closed first. To that end we will prove that, under a weak stability condition, a given discounted MDP can be formulated as an undiscounted MDP delivering the same optimal policy. Using this result, the MPC-based policy restricted to stability will deliver the optimal policy for the discounted MDP if it is stable, and the optimal policy among the stable policies if it is not.

This paper is structured as follows. In Section
2
we will provide the mathematical background and the required definitions. In Section
3
we will derive an undiscounted MDP whose optimal policy is also optimal for a given discounted MDP; we will relate the optimality criterion for this undiscounted MDP to commonly used optimality criteria; and we will illustrate the theory in the context of the linear quadratic regulator. We will then formulate the stability constraints in Section
4
, where we will exploit the properties of MPC as a function approximator to solve MDPs. We will provide numerical examples in Section
5
and conclude with Section
6
.

2
Preliminaries

We will consider that the system is described by a Markov Decision Process (MDP) having the (possibly) stochastic state transition dynamics

\mathbb{P}\left[{\boldsymbol{{s}}}_{+}\,|\,{\boldsymbol{{s}}},{\boldsymbol{{a}}}\right],

(1)

where
{\boldsymbol{{s}}},{\boldsymbol{{a}}}
is the current state-action pair and
{\boldsymbol{{s}}}_{+}
is the subsequent state. We will generally assume that the state-action space is continuous but the theory proposed here is valid in general. Note that notation (
1
) is standard in the literature on MDPs, while the control literature typically uses the notation
{\boldsymbol{{s}}}_{+}={\boldsymbol{{f}}}({\boldsymbol{{s}}},{\boldsymbol{{a}}},{\boldsymbol{{\zeta}}})
, where
{\boldsymbol{{\zeta}}}
is a stochastic variable and
{\boldsymbol{{f}}}
a possibly nonlinear function. For discrete state spaces, (
1
) is a probability, while for continuous state spaces it is a probability density.

We will label
L({\boldsymbol{{s}}},{\boldsymbol{{a}}})
the stage cost associated to the MDP, which we will assume can take the form

L\left({\boldsymbol{{s}}},{\boldsymbol{{a}}}\right)=l\left({\boldsymbol{{s}}},{\boldsymbol{{a}}}\right)+\mathcal{I}_{\infty}\left({\boldsymbol{{h}}}\left({\boldsymbol{{s}}},{\boldsymbol{{a}}}\right)\right)+\mathcal{I}_{\infty}\left({\boldsymbol{{g}}}\left({\boldsymbol{{a}}}\right)\right),

(2)

where we use the indicator function

\mathcal{I}_{\infty}({\boldsymbol{{x}}})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\infty&&\text{if }{\boldsymbol{{x}}}_{i}>0\text{ for some }i\\
0&&\text{otherwise}\end{array}\right..

(3)

In (
2
), function
l
captures the cost given to different state-input pairs, while the constraints

{\boldsymbol{{g}}}({\boldsymbol{{a}}})\leq 0,\qquad{\boldsymbol{{h}}}({\boldsymbol{{s}}},{\boldsymbol{{a}}})\leq 0,

(4)

capture undesirable state and inputs, and infinite values are given to
L
when (
4
) is violated.

Assumption 1

The cost
l({\boldsymbol{{s}}},{\boldsymbol{{a}}})
is finite for all finite states and inputs
{\boldsymbol{{s}}}
,
{\boldsymbol{{a}}}
. Additionally, for continuous state spaces, (
1
) satisfies

\lim_{\alpha\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{P}\left[\alpha{\boldsymbol{{s}}}_{+}\,|\,{\boldsymbol{{s}}},{\boldsymbol{{a}}}\right]=0,\qquad\forall\,{\boldsymbol{{s}}}_{+},{\boldsymbol{{s}}},{\boldsymbol{{a}}}\,\,\text{finite}.

(5)

The mild assumption (
5
) entails that the stage cost
l
remains bounded over finite horizons with probability 1. This allows us to avoid cumbersome technicalities in the proofs.

In this paper, we aim at solving the stability-constrained discounted MDP

\displaystyle\min_{{\boldsymbol{{\pi}}}\in\Pi_{\mathrm{s}}}\ \ \mathbb{E}_{{{\boldsymbol{{\pi}}}}}\left[\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\gamma^{k}L({\boldsymbol{{s}}}_{k},{\boldsymbol{{\pi}}}({\boldsymbol{{s}}}_{k}))\right],

(6)

where
\Pi_{\mathrm{s}}
denotes the set of all policies
{\boldsymbol{{\pi}}}
which stabilize system (
1
). At this stage, we leave the stability concept in use here intentionally vague, and we will discuss it more in detail in Section
4
.

Since in this paper we will compare discounted MDPs with undiscounted MDPs, we summarize next the corresponding optimality concepts. All definitions we will provide are given without stability constraints, but we remark that the same definitions hold also if the additional constraint
{\boldsymbol{{\pi}}}\in\Pi_{\mathrm{s}}
is introduced.
For more details on MDPs and optimality notions we refer to, e.g.,
[
23
]
and references therein.

2.1
Optimality of Discounted MDPs

With the introduction of a discount factor
0<\gamma\leq 1
, given (
1
) and (
2
), the optimal discounted policy
{\boldsymbol{{\pi}}}_{\star}^{\gamma}
is the policy minimizing the expected total discounted cost

{\boldsymbol{{\pi}}}_{\star}^{\gamma}=\arg\min_{{\boldsymbol{{\pi}}}}\ \ \mathbb{E}_{{{\boldsymbol{{\pi}}}}}\left[\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\gamma^{k}L({\boldsymbol{{s}}}_{k},{\boldsymbol{{\pi}}}({\boldsymbol{{s}}}_{k}))\right].

(7)

where the expected value
\mathbb{E}_{{{\boldsymbol{{\pi}}}}}\left[\cdot\right]
is taken over the state transition dynamics (
1
) in closed loop with policy
{\boldsymbol{{\pi}}}
.
For any policy we define the associated action-value function
Q_{{\boldsymbol{{\pi}}}}^{\gamma}
and value function
V_{{\boldsymbol{{\pi}}}}^{\gamma}
as

\displaystyle V_{{\boldsymbol{{\pi}}}}^{\gamma}({\boldsymbol{{s}}})

\displaystyle:=\mathbb{E}_{{{\boldsymbol{{\pi}}}}}\left[\left.\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\gamma^{k}L({\boldsymbol{{s}}}_{k},{\boldsymbol{{\pi}}}({\boldsymbol{{s}}}_{k}))\,\right|\,{\boldsymbol{{s}}}_{0}={\boldsymbol{{s}}}\right],

(8a)

\displaystyle Q_{{\boldsymbol{{\pi}}}}^{\gamma}({\boldsymbol{{s}}},{\boldsymbol{{a}}})

\displaystyle:=L({\boldsymbol{{s}}},{\boldsymbol{{a}}})+\gamma\mathbb{E}\left[V_{{\boldsymbol{{\pi}}}}^{\gamma}({\boldsymbol{{s}}}_{+})\,|\,{\boldsymbol{{s}}},{\boldsymbol{{a}}}\right].

(8b)

The optimal action-value function
Q_{\star}^{\gamma}=Q_{{\boldsymbol{{\pi}}}_{\star}^{\gamma}}^{\gamma}
and value function
V_{\star}^{\gamma}=V_{{\boldsymbol{{\pi}}}_{\star}^{\gamma}}^{\gamma}
associated with the discounted MDP are defined by the Bellman equations
[
5
]
:

\displaystyle Q_{\star}^{\gamma}\left({\boldsymbol{{s}}},{\boldsymbol{{a}}}\right)

\displaystyle=L\left({\boldsymbol{{s}}},{\boldsymbol{{a}}}\right)+\gamma\mathbb{E}{}\left[V_{\star}^{\gamma}({\boldsymbol{{s}}}_{+})\,|\,{\boldsymbol{{s}}},{\boldsymbol{{a}}}\right],

(9a)

\displaystyle V_{\star}^{\gamma}\left({\boldsymbol{{s}}}\right)

\displaystyle=Q_{\star}^{\gamma}\left({\boldsymbol{{s}}},{\boldsymbol{{\pi}}}_{\star}^{\gamma}\left({\boldsymbol{{s}}}\right)\right)=\min_{{\boldsymbol{{a}}}}\,Q_{\star}^{\gamma}\left({\boldsymbol{{s}}},{\boldsymbol{{a}}}\right).

(9b)

Throughout the paper we will assume that the MDP underlying the system, the associated stage cost
L
and the discount factor
\gamma
yields a well-posed problem, i.e., the value functions defined by (
9
) are well-posed, and finite over some non-empty sets. This well-posedness is formulated in the following assumption.

Assumption 2

There exists a nonempty set
\mathcal{S}
such that for all
{\boldsymbol{{s}}}\in\mathcal{S}
it holds that

|V_{\star}^{\gamma}({\boldsymbol{{s}}})|<\infty.

(10)

We state next an immediate consequence of this assumption, which will be useful afterwards.

Lemma 1

Suppose that Assumptions
1
-
2
hold. Then,

-\infty<\mathbb{E}_{{\boldsymbol{{\pi}}}_{\star}}\left[V_{\star}^{\gamma}({\boldsymbol{{s}}}_{k})\,|\,{\boldsymbol{{s}}}_{0}={\boldsymbol{{s}}}\right]<\infty,\ \ \forall\ k=0,\ldots,N,

(11)

holds for all
{\boldsymbol{{s}}}\in\mathcal{S}
, and
N
finite.

PROOF.

We observe that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{{\boldsymbol{{\pi}}}_{\star}}\left[V_{\star}^{\gamma}({\boldsymbol{{s}}}_{k})\,|\,{\boldsymbol{{s}}}_{0}\right]=

(12)

\displaystyle\qquad\gamma^{-k}V_{\star}^{\gamma}({\boldsymbol{{s}}}_{0})-\gamma^{-k}\,\mathbb{E}_{{\boldsymbol{{\pi}}}_{\star}}\left[\left.\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\gamma^{i}L\left({\boldsymbol{{s}}}_{i},{\boldsymbol{{\pi}}}_{\star}\left({\boldsymbol{{s}}}_{i}\right)\right)\,\right|\,{\boldsymbol{{s}}}_{0}\right].

Since
\gamma^{-k}V_{\star}^{\gamma}({\boldsymbol{{s}}}_{0})
is bounded for any
k<\infty
and
{\boldsymbol{{s}}}_{0}\in\mathcal{S}
, due to Assumption
1
and Equation (
5
), the second term of the right-hand side of (
12
) must be lower bounded for all
k
finite. Additionally, in order for
V_{\star}^{\gamma}({\boldsymbol{{s}}}_{0})
to be finite, it must also be upper bounded. Consequently,
\mathbb{E}_{{\boldsymbol{{\pi}}}_{\star}}\left[V_{\star}^{\gamma}({\boldsymbol{{s}}}_{k})\,|\,{\boldsymbol{{s}}}_{0}\right]
must be bounded.

\hfill\qed

2.2
Optimality of Undiscounted MDPs

For undiscounted MDPs, a discount factor
\gamma=1
is selected, such that the cost in (
7
) can be unbounded. Optimal policies are then typically defined according to a hierarchy of criteria. The first criterion is based on the expected average total cost

{\boldsymbol{{\bar{\pi}}}}_{\star}=\arg\min_{{\boldsymbol{{\pi}}}}\ \lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\mathbb{E}_{{{\boldsymbol{{\pi}}}}}\left[\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}L({\boldsymbol{{s}}}_{k},{\boldsymbol{{\pi}}}({\boldsymbol{{s}}}_{k}))\right],

(13)

with
gain
, or average cost

\bar{L}_{\infty}\left({\boldsymbol{{s}}}_{0}\right)=\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\mathbb{E}_{{\boldsymbol{{\bar{\pi}}}}_{\star}\hskip-3.0pt}\left[\left.\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}L({\boldsymbol{{s}}}_{k},{\boldsymbol{{\bar{\pi}}}}_{\star}({\boldsymbol{{s}}}_{k}))\,\right|{\boldsymbol{{s}}}_{0}\right].

(14)

Any policy satisfying (
13
) is called
gain optimal
. The average cost
\bar{L}_{\infty}
is often assumed to be independent of the initial state
{\boldsymbol{{s}}}_{0}
and we will also make this assumption for the sake of simplicity. For more information on the general case, we refer the interested reader to
[
23
]
. We ought to stress here that gain optimal policies are in general not unique.

The gain optimality criterion only ensures that the optimal steady-state distribution of the closed-loop Markov chain is asymptotically reached, but it disregards transients. The concept of
bias optimality
has been introduced to account for the optimality of transients.
A
bias optimal
policy
{\boldsymbol{{\tilde{\pi}}}}_{\star}
minimizes the total undiscounted cost

{\boldsymbol{{\tilde{\pi}}}}_{\star}=\arg\min_{{\boldsymbol{{\pi}}}}\ \mathbb{E}_{{{\boldsymbol{{\pi}}}}}\left[\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\left(L({\boldsymbol{{s}}}_{k},{\boldsymbol{{\pi}}}({\boldsymbol{{s}}}_{k}))-\bar{L}_{\infty}\right)\right].

(15)

Optimality notions which are more stringent than bias optimality are beyond the scope of this paper. We simply recall that bias optimal policies are gain optimal and that the most stringent optimality criterion for undiscounted MDPs is
Blackwell optimality

[
8
,
23
]
.

3
Equivalent Undiscounted MDP Formulation

In this section, we discuss the equivalence between discounted MDPs and suitably formulated undiscounted MDPs. To that end, let us define the modified stage cost

\tilde{L}^{\gamma}({\boldsymbol{{s}}},{\boldsymbol{{a}}}):=L({\boldsymbol{{s}}},{\boldsymbol{{a}}})+(\gamma-1)\mathbb{E}\left[V_{\star}^{\gamma}({\boldsymbol{{s}}}_{+})|{\boldsymbol{{s}}},{\boldsymbol{{a}}}\right],

(16)

where we explicitly state the dependence on the discount factor
\gamma
to stress the fact that its definition is based on a discounted MDP formulation.

In the following, we construct the theory allowing one to support the discounted MDP solution by using undiscounted, finite-horizon stochastic MPC schemes. The latter are themselves undiscounted finite-horizon MDPs. Hence, it will be useful to first connect infinite-horizon discounted MDPs to finite-horizon undiscounted ones. We establish the equivalence between infinite-horizon discounted and infinite-horizon undiscounted MDPs later in the text.

In order to build these equivalences, let us define the
N
-steps undiscounted value and action-value functions as

\displaystyle\tilde{V}_{{\boldsymbol{{\pi}}}}^{\gamma,N}({\boldsymbol{{s}}})\hskip-1.0pt:=\hskip-1.0pt\mathbb{E}_{{\boldsymbol{{\pi}}}\hskip-3.0pt}\left[\left.V_{\star}^{\gamma}({\boldsymbol{{s}}}_{N})+\hskip-1.0pt\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\tilde{L}^{\gamma}({\boldsymbol{{s}}}_{k},{\boldsymbol{{\pi}}}({\boldsymbol{{s}}}_{k}))\,\right|{\boldsymbol{{s}}}_{0}={\boldsymbol{{s}}}\right]\hskip-1.0pt,

(17a)

\displaystyle\tilde{Q}_{{\boldsymbol{{\pi}}}}^{\gamma,N}({\boldsymbol{{s}}},{\boldsymbol{{a}}})\hskip-1.0pt:=\hskip-1.0pt\tilde{L}^{\gamma}({\boldsymbol{{s}}},{\boldsymbol{{a}}})+\mathbb{E}_{\hskip-1.0pt}\left[\left.\tilde{V}_{{\boldsymbol{{\pi}}}}^{\gamma,N-1}({\boldsymbol{{s}}}_{+})\,\right|{\boldsymbol{{s}}},{\boldsymbol{{a}}}\right]\hskip-1.0pt.

(17b)

We define the corresponding optimal policy as

{\boldsymbol{{\tilde{\pi}}}}_{\star}^{\gamma,N}:=\mathrm{arg}\min_{{\boldsymbol{{\pi}}}}\,\tilde{V}_{{\boldsymbol{{\pi}}}}^{\gamma,N}({\boldsymbol{{s}}}),

(18)

with associated optimal value and action-value function
\tilde{V}_{\star}^{\gamma,N}:=\tilde{V}_{{\boldsymbol{{\tilde{\pi}}}}_{\star}^{\gamma,N}}^{\gamma,N}
, and
\tilde{Q}_{\star}^{\gamma,N}:=\tilde{Q}_{{\boldsymbol{{\tilde{\pi}}}}_{\star}^{\gamma,N}}^{\gamma,N}
.
We will justify in Section
3.1
the use of the
\tilde{\cdot}
notation, which we also used to define bias-optimal quantities.
We deliver next our first result, which proves the equivalence between the optimal undiscounted
N
-steps value functions and the optimal discounted value functions.

Theorem 1

Suppose that Assumption
2
holds for all
{\boldsymbol{{s}}}\in\mathcal{S}
. Then
\forall\,{\boldsymbol{{s}}}\in\mathcal{S}
,
\forall\,{\boldsymbol{{a}}},\,N<\infty
it holds that

\tilde{V}_{\star}^{\gamma,N}({\boldsymbol{{s}}})=V_{\star}^{\gamma}({\boldsymbol{{s}}}),\qquad\tilde{Q}_{\star}^{\gamma,N}({\boldsymbol{{s}}},{\boldsymbol{{a}}})=Q_{\star}^{\gamma}({\boldsymbol{{s}}},{\boldsymbol{{a}}}).

PROOF.

We use the Bellman Equation (
9
) to obtain

\displaystyle L({\boldsymbol{{s}}},{\boldsymbol{{\pi}}}_{\star}^{\gamma}({\boldsymbol{{s}}}))=V_{\star}^{\gamma}({\boldsymbol{{s}}})-\gamma\mathbb{E}\left[V_{\star}^{\gamma}({\boldsymbol{{s}}}_{+})|{\boldsymbol{{s}}},{\boldsymbol{{\pi}}}_{\star}^{\gamma}({\boldsymbol{{s}}})\right],

(19)

which we use together with (
16
) to obtain

\tilde{L}^{\gamma}({\boldsymbol{{s}}},{\boldsymbol{{\pi}}}_{\star}^{\gamma}({\boldsymbol{{s}}}))=V_{\star}^{\gamma}({\boldsymbol{{s}}})-\mathbb{E}\left[V_{\star}^{\gamma}({\boldsymbol{{s}}}_{+})|{\boldsymbol{{s}}},{\boldsymbol{{a}}}\right].

(20)

Equation (
17a
) then becomes the telescopic sum:

\tilde{V}_{{\boldsymbol{{\pi}}}_{\star}^{\gamma}}^{\gamma,N}({\boldsymbol{{s}}}_{0})=\mathbb{E}_{{\boldsymbol{{\pi}}}_{\star}^{\gamma}}\left[V_{\star}^{\gamma}({\boldsymbol{{s}}}_{N})+\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}V_{\star}^{\gamma}({\boldsymbol{{s}}}_{k})-V_{\star}^{\gamma}({\boldsymbol{{s}}}_{k+1})\right].

Using Assumption
2
, we simplify the terms in the telescopic sum to obtain

\tilde{V}_{{\boldsymbol{{\pi}}}_{\star}^{\gamma}}^{\gamma,\bar{N}}({\boldsymbol{{s}}})=V_{\star}^{\gamma}({\boldsymbol{{s}}}),\qquad\forall\ \bar{N}\leq N.

(21)

Consequently, by (
17b
) we have

\displaystyle\tilde{Q}^{\gamma,N}_{{\boldsymbol{{\pi}}}_{\star}^{\gamma}}({\boldsymbol{{s}}},{\boldsymbol{{a}}})

\displaystyle=\tilde{L}^{\gamma}({\boldsymbol{{s}}},{\boldsymbol{{a}}})+\mathbb{E}\left[\tilde{V}_{{\boldsymbol{{\pi}}}_{\star}^{\gamma}}^{\gamma,N-1}({\boldsymbol{{s}}}_{+})|{\boldsymbol{{s}}},{\boldsymbol{{a}}}\right]

\displaystyle=L({\boldsymbol{{s}}},{\boldsymbol{{a}}})+\gamma\mathbb{E}\left[V_{\star}^{\gamma}({\boldsymbol{{s}}}_{+})|{\boldsymbol{{s}}},{\boldsymbol{{a}}}\right]=Q_{\star}^{\gamma}({\boldsymbol{{s}}},{\boldsymbol{{a}}}),

where we exploited (
16
) and (
21
).
Since

{\boldsymbol{{\pi}}}_{\star}^{\gamma}=\arg\min_{{\boldsymbol{{a}}}}\,Q_{\star}^{\gamma}\left({\boldsymbol{{s}}},{\boldsymbol{{a}}}\right)=\arg\min_{{\boldsymbol{{a}}}}\,\tilde{Q}_{{\boldsymbol{{\pi}}}_{\star}^{\gamma}}^{\gamma,N}\left({\boldsymbol{{s}}},{\boldsymbol{{a}}}\right)={\boldsymbol{{\tilde{\pi}}}}_{\star}^{\gamma,N},

we immediately obtain

\displaystyle\tilde{V}_{\star}^{\gamma,N}({\boldsymbol{{s}}})

\displaystyle=\tilde{V}_{{\boldsymbol{{\tilde{\pi}}}}^{\gamma,N}_{\star}}^{\gamma,N}({\boldsymbol{{s}}})=\tilde{V}_{{\boldsymbol{{\pi}}}_{\star}^{\gamma}}^{\gamma,N}({\boldsymbol{{s}}})=V_{\star}^{\gamma}({\boldsymbol{{s}}}),

\displaystyle\tilde{Q}_{\star}^{\gamma,N}({\boldsymbol{{s}}},{\boldsymbol{{a}}})

\displaystyle=\tilde{Q}_{{\boldsymbol{{\tilde{\pi}}}}^{\gamma,N}_{\star}}^{\gamma,N}({\boldsymbol{{s}}},{\boldsymbol{{a}}})=\tilde{Q}_{{\boldsymbol{{\pi}}}_{\star}^{\gamma}}^{\gamma,N}({\boldsymbol{{s}}},{\boldsymbol{{a}}})=Q_{\star}^{\gamma}({\boldsymbol{{s}}},{\boldsymbol{{a}}}).

\hfill\qed

If the value function
\tilde{V}_{\star}^{\gamma,N}
remains bounded for all
{\boldsymbol{{s}}}\in\mathcal{S}
as
N\to\infty
, then the result above holds also as
N\to\infty
, but the terminal cost
V_{\star}^{\gamma}
is still required in forming
\tilde{V}_{{\boldsymbol{{\pi}}}}^{\gamma,N}
,
\tilde{Q}_{{\boldsymbol{{\pi}}}}^{\gamma,N}
. In order to dismiss that terminal cost for
N\rightarrow\infty
we need an additional stronger assumption.

Assumption 3

Assumption
2
holds. Moreover,

-\infty<\lim_{k\to\infty}\ \mathbb{E}_{{\boldsymbol{{\pi}}}_{\star}^{\gamma}}\left[V_{\star}^{\gamma}({\boldsymbol{{s}}}_{k})\,|\,{\boldsymbol{{s}}}_{0}\right]:=v_{\infty}^{\gamma}<\infty,

(22)

holds
\forall\,{\boldsymbol{{s}}}_{0}\in\mathcal{S}
for some constant
v_{\infty}^{\gamma}
.

Assumption
3
entails a weak form of stability for the discounted MDP, as we discuss next. It is stronger than requiring the existence of a bounded value function, i.e.,

\left|\lim_{N\to\infty}\ \mathbb{E}_{{\boldsymbol{{\pi}}}_{\star}^{\gamma}}\left[\left.\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\gamma^{k}L({\boldsymbol{{s}}}_{k},{\boldsymbol{{\pi}}}^{\gamma}_{\star}({\boldsymbol{{s}}}_{k}))\,\right|\,{\boldsymbol{{s}}}_{0}\right]\right|\leq\infty,

(23)

holds on a non-empty set of initial conditions
{\boldsymbol{{s}}}_{0}
. Indeed, (
23
) is finite provided that the stage cost
L({\boldsymbol{{s}}}_{k},{\boldsymbol{{\pi}}}^{\gamma}_{\star}({\boldsymbol{{s}}}_{k}))
diverges in expectation at a rate no larger than
\gamma_{\mathrm{D}}^{-k}
for some
\gamma_{\mathrm{D}}>\gamma
. It follows that (
23
) allows the cost to grow unbounded over time. Hence, the existence of a bounded value function (Assumption
2
) does not entail that the value function remains bounded over time.
Moreover, assuming (
22
), i.e., that the limit exists and converges to the constant
v_{\infty}^{\gamma}
, introduces further restrictions, which rule out, e.g., periodic oscillations of the cost.
We cast Assumption
3
as a weak form of stability: if the MDP converges to a unique steady-state distribution, then Assumption
3
automatically holds. However, converging to a steady-state distribution is a stronger requirement, since Assumption
3
might hold also for non-steady-state, and even diverging distributions, see, e.g., Section
3.2
.

In order to formulate the next theorem, let us use (
16
) to first define the undiscounted value and action-value functions without terminal cost as

\displaystyle\tilde{V}^{\gamma}_{{\boldsymbol{{\pi}}}}({\boldsymbol{{s}}}):=\lim_{N\to\infty}\hskip-1.0pt\mathbb{E}_{{\boldsymbol{{\pi}}}\hskip-3.0pt}\left[\left.\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\hskip-2.0pt\tilde{L}^{\gamma}({\boldsymbol{{s}}}_{k},{\boldsymbol{{\pi}}}({\boldsymbol{{s}}}_{k}))\,\right|{\boldsymbol{{s}}}_{0}={\boldsymbol{{s}}}\right],

(24a)

\displaystyle\tilde{Q}^{\gamma}_{{\boldsymbol{{\pi}}}}({\boldsymbol{{s}}},{\boldsymbol{{a}}}):=\tilde{L}^{\gamma}({\boldsymbol{{s}}},{\boldsymbol{{a}}})+\mathbb{E}\left[\left.\tilde{V}^{\gamma}_{{\boldsymbol{{\pi}}}}({\boldsymbol{{s}}}_{+})\,\right|{\boldsymbol{{s}}},{\boldsymbol{{a}}}\right],

(24b)

which do not necessarily match the limit for
N\to\infty
of the value functions defined in (
17
). Furthermore, we define the optimal undiscounted policy and the associated value functions as:

\displaystyle{\boldsymbol{{\tilde{\pi}}}}_{\star}^{\gamma}:=\mathrm{arg}\min_{{\boldsymbol{{\pi}}}}\,\lim_{N\to\infty}\mathbb{E}_{{\boldsymbol{{\pi}}}}\left[\ \sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\tilde{L}^{\gamma}({\boldsymbol{{s}}}_{k},{\boldsymbol{{\pi}}}({\boldsymbol{{s}}}_{k}))\ \right],

(25a)

\displaystyle\tilde{V}^{\gamma}_{\star}({\boldsymbol{{s}}}):=\tilde{V}^{\gamma}_{{\boldsymbol{{\tilde{\pi}}}}_{\star}^{\gamma}}({\boldsymbol{{s}}}),\qquad\tilde{Q}^{\gamma}_{\star}({\boldsymbol{{s}}},{\boldsymbol{{a}}}):=\tilde{Q}^{\gamma}_{{\boldsymbol{{\tilde{\pi}}}}_{\star}^{\gamma}}({\boldsymbol{{s}}},{\boldsymbol{{a}}}).

(25b)

Theorem 2

Suppose that Assumption
3
holds. Then
\forall\,{\boldsymbol{{s}}}\in\mathcal{S}
,
\forall\,{\boldsymbol{{a}}}
it holds that

\displaystyle\hskip-5.0pt\tilde{V}^{\gamma}_{\star}({\boldsymbol{{s}}})\hskip-1.0pt=\hskip-1.0ptV_{\star}^{\gamma}({\boldsymbol{{s}}})\hskip-1.0pt-\hskip-1.0ptv_{\infty}^{\gamma},\ \ \tilde{Q}^{\gamma}_{\star}({\boldsymbol{{s}}},{\boldsymbol{{a}}})\hskip-1.0pt=\hskip-1.0ptQ_{\star}^{\gamma}({\boldsymbol{{s}}},{\boldsymbol{{a}}})\hskip-1.0pt-\hskip-1.0ptv_{\infty}^{\gamma},

(26a)

\displaystyle\hskip-5.0pt{\boldsymbol{{\tilde{\pi}}}}_{\star}^{\gamma}\left({\boldsymbol{{s}}}\right)\hskip-1.0pt={\boldsymbol{{\pi}}}_{\star}^{\gamma}\left({\boldsymbol{{s}}}\right).

(26b)

PROOF.

Similar to the proof of Theorem
1
, we use the Bellman Equation (
9
) together with (
19
)-(
20
) to write (
24a
) as a telescopic sum in which all terms are bounded due to Lemma
1
. By simplifying the terms in the sum we obtain

\tilde{V}^{\gamma}_{{\boldsymbol{{\pi}}}_{\star}^{\gamma}}({\boldsymbol{{s}}})\hskip-1.0pt=\hskip-1.0ptV_{\star}^{\gamma}({\boldsymbol{{s}}})-\hskip-1.0pt\lim_{k\to\infty}\mathbb{E}_{{\boldsymbol{{\pi}}}_{\star}^{\gamma}\hskip-2.0pt}\left[V_{\star}^{\gamma}({\boldsymbol{{s}}}_{k})\right]\hskip-1.0pt=\hskip-1.0ptV_{\star}^{\gamma}({\boldsymbol{{s}}})-v_{\infty}^{\gamma}.

(27)

Consequently,

\displaystyle\tilde{Q}^{\gamma}_{{\boldsymbol{{\pi}}}_{\star}^{\gamma}}({\boldsymbol{{s}}},{\boldsymbol{{a}}})

\displaystyle=\tilde{L}^{\gamma}({\boldsymbol{{s}}},{\boldsymbol{{a}}})+\mathbb{E}\left[\tilde{V}^{\gamma}_{{\boldsymbol{{\pi}}}_{\star}^{\gamma}}({\boldsymbol{{s}}}_{+})|{\boldsymbol{{s}}},{\boldsymbol{{a}}}\right]

\displaystyle=L({\boldsymbol{{s}}},{\boldsymbol{{a}}})+\gamma\mathbb{E}\left[V_{\star}^{\gamma}({\boldsymbol{{s}}}_{+})|{\boldsymbol{{s}}},{\boldsymbol{{a}}}\right]-v_{\infty}^{\gamma}

\displaystyle=Q_{\star}^{\gamma}({\boldsymbol{{s}}},{\boldsymbol{{a}}})-v_{\infty}^{\gamma},

where we used (
16
) and (
27
).
Then,

{\boldsymbol{{\tilde{\pi}}}}_{\star}^{\gamma}\hskip-1.0pt=\hskip-1.0pt\arg\min_{{\boldsymbol{{a}}}}\,\tilde{Q}_{{\boldsymbol{{\pi}}}_{\star}^{\gamma}}^{\gamma}\hskip-1.0pt\left({\boldsymbol{{s}}},{\boldsymbol{{a}}}\right)\hskip-1.0pt=\hskip-1.0pt\arg\min_{{\boldsymbol{{a}}}}\,Q^{\gamma}_{\star}\hskip-3.0pt\left({\boldsymbol{{s}}},{\boldsymbol{{a}}}\right)\hskip-1.0pt=\hskip-1.0pt{\boldsymbol{{\pi}}}^{\gamma}_{\star},

(28)

which immediately entails (
26b
) and, in turn,

\displaystyle\tilde{V}^{\gamma}_{\star}({\boldsymbol{{s}}})

\displaystyle=\tilde{V}^{\gamma}_{{\boldsymbol{{\tilde{\pi}}}}^{\gamma}_{\star}}({\boldsymbol{{s}}})=\tilde{V}^{\gamma}_{{\boldsymbol{{\pi}}}_{\star}^{\gamma}}({\boldsymbol{{s}}})=V_{\star}^{\gamma}({\boldsymbol{{s}}})-v_{\infty}^{\gamma},

\displaystyle\tilde{Q}^{\gamma}_{\star}({\boldsymbol{{s}}},{\boldsymbol{{a}}})

\displaystyle=\tilde{Q}^{\gamma}_{{\boldsymbol{{\tilde{\pi}}}}^{\gamma}_{\star}}({\boldsymbol{{s}}},{\boldsymbol{{a}}})=\tilde{Q}^{\gamma}_{{\boldsymbol{{\pi}}}_{\star}^{\gamma}}({\boldsymbol{{s}}},{\boldsymbol{{a}}})=Q_{\star}^{\gamma}({\boldsymbol{{s}}},{\boldsymbol{{a}}})-v_{\infty}^{\gamma}.

\hfill\qed

This theorem establishes that any discounted MDP satisfying Assumption
3
can be reformulated as an undiscounted MDP which delivers the same policy and the same value functions up to a constant term.

We ought to stress here that in the following we are interested in forming policies which are stabilizing by construction, i.e., we aim at solving the discounted MDP under the constraint of preserving stability. The equivalence proposed in Theorem
2
will be instrumental in allowing us to formulate such constraints in the undiscounted setting, while optimizing the cost in a discounted sense.
This is of particular interest, since the stability analysis is much simpler and more developed for the undiscounted setting. We will discuss the introduction of stability constraints in Section
4
.
Before detailing how one can introduce stability constraints, we first prove that the obtained undiscounted MDP (
24
)-(
25
) yields bias optimal policies; and we then illustrate the theoretical developments in the simple case of a Linear Quadratic Regulator (LQR).

3.1
Equivalence of Optimality Notions

The undiscounted MDP used in Theorem
2
minimizes the cost in (
25a
), which is not directly related to standard optimality concepts such as gain or bias optimality. We therefore prove next that the policy
{\boldsymbol{{\tilde{\pi}}}}_{\star}^{\gamma}={\boldsymbol{{\pi}}}_{\star}^{\gamma}
obtained from (
25a
) is in fact bias optimal. To that end, we will first prove gain optimality. We will then prove that the optimal gain is
0
, which we will relate to bias optimality.

Theorem 3

Suppose that Assumption
3
holds. Then, policy
{\boldsymbol{{\pi}}}_{\star}^{\gamma}
is gain optimal for stage cost
\tilde{L}^{\gamma}
.

PROOF.

By Theorem
2
policy
{\boldsymbol{{\pi}}}_{\star}^{\gamma}
solves (
25a
) with a finite optimal cost, such that
\forall\,{\boldsymbol{{\pi}}}
we have

\mathbb{E}_{{\boldsymbol{{\pi}}}_{\star}^{\gamma}\hskip-3.0pt}\left[\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\hskip-1.0pt\tilde{L}^{\gamma}({\boldsymbol{{s}}}_{k},{\boldsymbol{{\pi}}}({\boldsymbol{{s}}}_{k}))\right]\hskip-3.0pt\leq\hskip-1.0pt\mathbb{E}_{{\boldsymbol{{\pi}}}\hskip-3.0pt}\left[\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\hskip-1.0pt\tilde{L}^{\gamma}({\boldsymbol{{s}}}_{k},{\boldsymbol{{\pi}}}({\boldsymbol{{s}}}_{k}))\right]\hskip-3.0pt.

(29)

Therefore, the policy is gain optimal, i.e.,
\forall\,{\boldsymbol{{\pi}}}
we have

\displaystyle\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\mathbb{E}_{{\boldsymbol{{\pi}}}_{\star}^{\gamma}}\left[\ \sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\tilde{L}^{\gamma}({\boldsymbol{{s}}}_{k},{\boldsymbol{{\pi}}}({\boldsymbol{{s}}}_{k}))\ \right]

\displaystyle\hskip 70.0001pt\leq\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\mathbb{E}_{{\boldsymbol{{\pi}}}}\left[\ \sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\tilde{L}^{\gamma}({\boldsymbol{{s}}}_{k},{\boldsymbol{{\pi}}}({\boldsymbol{{s}}}_{k}))\ \right].

\hfill\qed

Theorem 4

Suppose that Assumption
3
holds. Then, policy
{\boldsymbol{{\pi}}}_{\star}^{\gamma}
is bias optimal for stage cost
\tilde{L}^{\gamma}
.

PROOF.

Because
\tilde{V}_{\star}^{\gamma}({\boldsymbol{{s}}}_{0})
is finite for all
{\boldsymbol{{s}}}_{0}\in\mathcal{S}
, we have that the average cost is

\displaystyle\bar{\tilde{L}}^{\gamma}_{\infty}

\displaystyle=\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\tilde{L}^{\gamma}({\boldsymbol{{s}}}_{k},{\boldsymbol{{\tilde{\pi}}}}_{\star}^{\gamma}({\boldsymbol{{s}}}_{k}))\right]

\displaystyle=\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\tilde{V}_{\star}^{\gamma}({\boldsymbol{{s}}}_{0})=0.

(30)

By Theorem
3
policy
{\boldsymbol{{\tilde{\pi}}}}_{\star}^{\gamma}={\boldsymbol{{\pi}}}_{\star}^{\gamma}
is gain optimal, such that by (
3.1
) the gain-optimal average cost is
\bar{\tilde{L}}^{\gamma}_{\infty}=0
. Therefore, bias optimality for
\tilde{L}^{\gamma}
is obtained by minimizing

\mathbb{E}_{{{\boldsymbol{{\pi}}}}\hskip-3.0pt}\left[\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\tilde{L}^{\gamma}({\boldsymbol{{s}}}_{k},{\boldsymbol{{\pi}}}({\boldsymbol{{s}}}_{k}))-\bar{\tilde{L}}^{\gamma}_{\infty}\right]\hskip-2.0pt=\mathbb{E}_{{{\boldsymbol{{\pi}}}}\hskip-3.0pt}\left[\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\tilde{L}^{\gamma}({\boldsymbol{{s}}}_{k},{\boldsymbol{{\pi}}}({\boldsymbol{{s}}}_{k}))\right],

(31)

which is the total undiscounted reward. Since by Theorem
2
the policy
{\boldsymbol{{\tilde{\pi}}}}_{\star}^{\gamma}={\boldsymbol{{\pi}}}_{\star}^{\gamma}
solves (
25a
), i.e., minimizes (
31
), it is bias optimal by construction.

\hfill\qed

3.2
The LQR Case

In order to clarify the previous developments, let us consider the case of a stochastic linear system

{\boldsymbol{{s}}}_{+}=A{\boldsymbol{{s}}}+B{\boldsymbol{{a}}}+{\boldsymbol{{w}}},

(32)

with
{\boldsymbol{{w}}}\sim\mathcal{N}({\boldsymbol{{0}}},W)
i.i.d.,
\mathbb{E}\left[{\boldsymbol{{w}}}{\boldsymbol{{s}}}^{\top}\right]=0
,
\mathbb{E}\left[{\boldsymbol{{w}}}{\boldsymbol{{a}}}^{\top}\right]=0
,

L({\boldsymbol{{s}}},{\boldsymbol{{a}}})=\begin{bmatrix}{\boldsymbol{{s}}}\\
{\boldsymbol{{a}}}\end{bmatrix}^{\top}H\begin{bmatrix}{\boldsymbol{{s}}}\\
{\boldsymbol{{a}}}\end{bmatrix},\qquad H=\begin{bmatrix}T&U^{\top}\\
U&R\end{bmatrix}\succ 0.

The value and action-value function are given by

\displaystyle V_{\star}^{\gamma}({\boldsymbol{{s}}})

\displaystyle={\boldsymbol{{s}}}^{\top}P{\boldsymbol{{s}}}+V_{0},\qquad\qquad V_{0}=\frac{\gamma}{1-\gamma}\mathrm{Tr}\left(PW\right),

\displaystyle Q_{\star}^{\gamma}({\boldsymbol{{s}}},{\boldsymbol{{a}}})\hskip-1.0pt

\displaystyle=\hskip-2.0pt\begin{bmatrix}\hskip-1.0pt{\boldsymbol{{s}}}\\
\hskip-1.0pt{\boldsymbol{{a}}}\end{bmatrix}^{\hskip-2.0pt\top}\hskip-3.0pt\begin{bmatrix}T\hskip-1.0pt+\hskip-1.0pt\gamma A^{\top}PA&\hskip 2.0ptU^{\top}\hskip-3.0pt+\hskip-1.0pt\gamma A^{\top}PB\\
U\hskip-1.0pt+\hskip-1.0pt\gamma B^{\top}PA&R\hskip-1.0pt+\hskip-1.0pt\gamma B^{\top}PB\end{bmatrix}\hskip-3.0pt\begin{bmatrix}\hskip-1.0pt{\boldsymbol{{s}}}\\
\hskip-1.0pt{\boldsymbol{{a}}}\end{bmatrix}\hskip-2.0pt+\hskip-1.0ptV_{0},

with

\displaystyle P

\displaystyle=T+\gamma A^{\top}PA-(U^{\top}+\gamma A^{\top}PB)K,

(33a)

\displaystyle K

\displaystyle=(R+\gamma B^{\top}PB)^{-1}(U+\gamma B^{\top}PA),

(33b)

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\prec R+\gamma B^{\top}PB.

(33c)

Note that
P
can be finite even in case
\rho(A-BK)\geq 1
, where
\rho(\cdot)
denotes the spectral radius, in which case the value function is defined and bounded for bounded states, but Assumption
3
does not hold.

Checking Assumption
3

We observe that, under feedback
{\boldsymbol{{a}}}=-K{\boldsymbol{{s}}}
we have

S_{+}=A_{K}SA_{K}^{\top}+W,

where we used
A_{K}:=A-BK
,
S:=\mathbb{E}\left[{\boldsymbol{{s}}}{\boldsymbol{{s}}}^{\top}\right]
.
If
\rho(A_{K})<1
, then there exists a unique matrix
S_{\infty}
solving the Lyapunov equation
A_{K}S_{\infty}A_{K}^{\top}-S_{\infty}+W=0
.
Assume that
P
is full rank (which is typically the case), then we have

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}\hskip-1.0pt\mathbb{E}_{{\boldsymbol{{\pi}}}_{\star}\hskip-2.0pt}\left[V_{\star}^{\gamma}({\boldsymbol{{s}}}_{k})\right]\hskip-2.0pt=\hskip-1.0pt\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\hskip-2.0pt\infty&\text{if }\rho(A_{K})\hskip-2.0pt\geq\hskip-1.0pt1\\
\hskip-2.0pt\mathrm{Tr}\left(PS_{\infty}\right)\hskip-1.0pt+\hskip-1.0ptV_{0}\hskip-1.0pt<\hskip-1.0pt\infty&\text{otherwise}\end{array}\right..

The condition
\rho(A_{K})<1
distinguishes the case
\lim_{k\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\left[{\boldsymbol{{s}}}_{k}\right]=0
,
\lim_{k\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\left[{\boldsymbol{{s}}}_{k}{\boldsymbol{{s}}}_{k}^{\top}\right]=S_{\infty}
from the case
\lim_{k\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\left[{\boldsymbol{{s}}}_{k}\right]=\pm\infty
: in the former, convergence in expectation of the value function is guaranteed; in the latter we immediately have
\lim_{k\to\infty}\mathbb{E}_{{\boldsymbol{{\pi}}}_{\star}}\left[V_{\star}^{\gamma}({\boldsymbol{{s}}}_{k})\right]=\infty
.

Undiscounted Equivalent MDP

We observe that

\displaystyle\hskip-20.00003pt\mathbb{E}\left[V_{\star}^{\gamma}({\boldsymbol{{s}}}_{+})|{\boldsymbol{{s}}},{\boldsymbol{{a}}}\right]

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}{\boldsymbol{{s}}}\\
{\boldsymbol{{a}}}\end{bmatrix}^{\top}\hskip-1.0pt\begin{bmatrix}A^{\top}PA&A^{\top}PB\\
B^{\top}PA&B^{\top}PB\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}{\boldsymbol{{s}}}\\
{\boldsymbol{{a}}}\end{bmatrix}+\frac{1}{1-\gamma}\mathrm{Tr}\left(PW\right).

In case Assumption
3
holds, the stage cost for the corresponding undiscounted MDP is then given by

\displaystyle\tilde{L}^{\gamma}({\boldsymbol{{s}}},{\boldsymbol{{a}}})

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}{\boldsymbol{{s}}}\\
{\boldsymbol{{a}}}\end{bmatrix}^{\top}\hskip-1.0pt\tilde{H}\begin{bmatrix}{\boldsymbol{{s}}}\\
{\boldsymbol{{a}}}\end{bmatrix}-\mathrm{Tr}\left(PW\right),

(34)

\displaystyle\tilde{H}

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}T+(\gamma-1)A^{\top}PA&U^{\top}+(\gamma-1)A^{\top}PB\\
U+(\gamma-1)B^{\top}PA&R+(\gamma-1)B^{\top}PB\end{bmatrix}.

Consequently, we have

\displaystyle\tilde{V}_{\star}^{\gamma}({\boldsymbol{{s}}})

\displaystyle\hskip-1.0pt=\hskip-1.0ptV_{\star}^{\gamma}({\boldsymbol{{s}}})\hskip-1.0pt-\hskip-1.0pt\mathrm{Tr}\left(PS_{\infty}\right)\hskip-1.0pt-\hskip-1.0ptV_{0}\hskip-1.0pt=\hskip-1.0pt{\boldsymbol{{s}}}^{\top}\hskip-1.0ptP{\boldsymbol{{s}}}\hskip-1.0pt-\hskip-1.0pt\mathrm{Tr}\left(PS_{\infty}\right)\hskip-1.0pt,

\displaystyle\tilde{Q}_{\star}^{\gamma}({\boldsymbol{{s}}},{\boldsymbol{{a}}})

\displaystyle\hskip-1.0pt=\hskip-1.0ptQ_{\star}^{\gamma}({\boldsymbol{{s}}},{\boldsymbol{{a}}})-\mathrm{Tr}\left(PS_{\infty}\right)-V_{0}.

Then, if
P
is full rank, the following holds:

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}\mathbb{E}_{{\boldsymbol{{\pi}}}_{\star}}\left[\tilde{V}_{\star}^{\gamma}({\boldsymbol{{s}}}_{k})\right]=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\infty&\text{if }\rho(A-BK)\geq 1\\
0&\text{otherwise}\end{array}\right..

In case
P=0
, Assumption
3
is automatically satisfied, even though the Markov chain might diverge. A simple example is given by
A=2
,
B=1
,
T=0
,
U=0
,
R=1
, for any
\gamma\in]0,1]
. The system is unstable since
K=0
but
V_{\star}^{\gamma}({\boldsymbol{{s}}})=0
, such that
\lim_{k\to\infty}V_{\star}^{\gamma}({\boldsymbol{{s}}})=0
holds. In this case we have
\tilde{L}^{\gamma}({\boldsymbol{{s}}},{\boldsymbol{{a}}})=L({\boldsymbol{{s}}},{\boldsymbol{{a}}})
.

A Simple Example

Consider a linear system of the form (
32
) with
A=2
,
B=1
,
T=1
,
U=0
,
R=1
, and set
W=0
in order to have a deterministic system. One can verify that this system is stabilized only for feedback matrices
K\in\mathcal{K}:=\ ]1,3[
, since this implies
A-BK\in\ ]-1,1[
.
The discounted LQR solution is

\displaystyle P

\displaystyle=\frac{5\gamma-1+\sqrt{(1-5\gamma)^{2}+4\gamma}}{2\gamma},

(35a)

\displaystyle K

\displaystyle=\frac{4\gamma}{1-3\gamma+\sqrt{(1-5\gamma)^{2}+4\gamma}},

(35b)

such that
\gamma\in\ ]1/3,1]\implies K\in\mathcal{K}
.

We can write the stability-constrained MDP problem as

K_{\mathrm{stab}}:=\arg\min_{K}\ P_{K}(K)\qquad\mathrm{s.t.}\ K\in\mathcal{K},

(36)

where
V_{-Ks}^{\gamma}({\boldsymbol{{s}}})=P_{K}(K){\boldsymbol{{s}}}^{2}
and

P_{K}(K):=\frac{K^{2}+1}{1-\gamma(2-K)^{2}}.

We ought to stress that (

\mathcal{K}
in order to (a) make the problem well-posed; and (b) avoid being too close to the stability margin and have some robustness to numerical errors.
Since the closure
\mathrm{cl}\left(\mathcal{K}\right):=[1,3]
of

K_{\mathrm{mstab}}:=\arg\min_{K}\ P_{K}(K)\qquad\mathrm{s.t.}\ K\in\mathrm{cl}\left(\mathcal{K}\right),

One can verify that, using
K
given by (

),

K_{\mathrm{mstab}}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1&\gamma\leq 1/3\\
K&\gamma>1/3\end{array}\right..

The solution is shown in Figure

.
The undiscounted equivalent problem is obtained by computing stage cost
\tilde{L}^{\gamma}
as per (

\tilde{L}^{\gamma}
is defined for all

is bounded, Theorem

applies only if Assumption

. In turn, this entails that in such cases the stability-constrained discounted MDP with stage cost

4
Stability Constraints based on MPC

In the previous section, we proved that a discounted MDP can be reformulated as an undiscounted MDP and we mentioned that this fact can be useful to introduce stability constraints in the MDP formulation. However, we did not discuss how this can be done in the general case.
In this section, we exploit the theoretical results of this paper in order to propose a solution method for stability-constrained MDPs which can be implemented in practice.

In order to solve an MDP, one must compute either the value function
V_{\star}^{\gamma}
, the policy
{\boldsymbol{{\pi}}}^{\gamma}_{\star}
, the action-value function
Q_{\star}^{\gamma}
or a combination of these. Since their functional form is not known a priori and can be rather complicated, solving MDPs exactly is a notoriously difficult task.
Therefore, practical approaches typically rely on some parametric function approximation
V_{\boldsymbol{{\theta}}}
,
{\boldsymbol{{\pi}}}_{\boldsymbol{{\theta}}}
, and
Q_{\boldsymbol{{\theta}}}
, where
{\boldsymbol{{\theta}}}
denotes a set of parameter adjusting the function approximations, in order to make the problem tractable
[
6
,
7
]
. The stability-constrained discounted MDP (
6
) can then be formulated using function approximation as

\displaystyle\min_{{\boldsymbol{{\theta}}}\in\Theta_{\mathrm{s}}}\ \ \mathbb{E}_{{{\boldsymbol{{\pi}}}_{\boldsymbol{{\theta}}}}}\left[\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\gamma^{k}L({\boldsymbol{{s}}}_{k},{\boldsymbol{{\pi}}}_{\boldsymbol{{\theta}}}({\boldsymbol{{s}}}_{k}))\right],

(37)

where
\Theta_{\mathrm{s}}:=\{\ {\boldsymbol{{\theta}}}\ |\ {\boldsymbol{{\pi}}}_{\boldsymbol{{\theta}}}\in\Pi_{\mathrm{s}}\ \}
is the set of parameters which yield a stabilizing policy.
We propose to introduce the stability constraint
{\boldsymbol{{\theta}}}\in\Theta_{\mathrm{s}}
by selecting an ad-hoc parametrization of these functions defining the MDP solution, which yields stabilizing policies by construction. These parametrizations will be based on MPC and will rely on tools from control theory.

4.1
MPC: a Structured Function Approximator

In order to enforce closed-loop stability, we propose to rely on Model Predictive Control (MPC) to support the necessary function approximations. This approach has been proposed in
[
13
]
in the context of Reinforcement Learning. We recall next how MPC provides a very convenient way to support a parametric approximation of
V_{\boldsymbol{{\theta}}}
,
{\boldsymbol{{\pi}}}_{\boldsymbol{{\theta}}}
, and
Q_{\boldsymbol{{\theta}}}
, as it solves the optimal control problem

\displaystyle\hskip-8.00003ptQ_{\boldsymbol{{\theta}}}({\boldsymbol{{s}}},{\boldsymbol{{a}}})=\min_{{\boldsymbol{{z}}}}\

\displaystyle\lambda_{\boldsymbol{{\theta}}}({\boldsymbol{{s}}})+V^{\mathrm{f}}_{\boldsymbol{{\theta}}}({\boldsymbol{{x}}}_{N})+\hskip-1.0pt\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\ell_{\boldsymbol{{\theta}}}({\boldsymbol{{x}}}_{k},{\boldsymbol{{u}}}_{k})

(38a)

\displaystyle\mathrm{s.t.}\

\displaystyle{\boldsymbol{{x}}}_{0}={\boldsymbol{{s}}},\quad{\boldsymbol{{u}}}_{0}={\boldsymbol{{a}}},

(38b)

\displaystyle{\boldsymbol{{x}}}_{k+1}={\boldsymbol{{f}}}_{\boldsymbol{{\theta}}}\left({\boldsymbol{{x}}}_{k},{\boldsymbol{{u}}}_{k}\right),

(38c)

\displaystyle{\boldsymbol{{g}}}\left({\boldsymbol{{u}}}_{k}\right)\leq 0,

(38d)

\displaystyle{\boldsymbol{{h}}}_{{\boldsymbol{{\theta}}}}\left({\boldsymbol{{x}}}_{k},{\boldsymbol{{u}}}_{k}\right)\leq 0,\quad{\boldsymbol{{h}}}^{\mathrm{f}}_{\boldsymbol{{\theta}}}({\boldsymbol{{x}}}_{N})\leq 0,

(38e)

where
{\boldsymbol{{z}}}=({\boldsymbol{{x}}}_{0},{\boldsymbol{{u}}}_{0},\ldots,{\boldsymbol{{x}}}_{N})
. The stage and terminal cost
\ell_{\boldsymbol{{\theta}}},V^{\mathrm{f}}_{\boldsymbol{{\theta}}}
, the system dynamics and constraints
{\boldsymbol{{f}}}_{\boldsymbol{{\theta}}},{\boldsymbol{{h}}}_{\boldsymbol{{\theta}}},{\boldsymbol{{h}}}^{\mathrm{f}}_{\boldsymbol{{\theta}}}
and the initial cost
\lambda_{\boldsymbol{{\theta}}}
are all parametric functions of
{\boldsymbol{{\theta}}}
, while actuator limitations
{\boldsymbol{{g}}}
are known. Note that in MPC the initial constraint (
38b
) typically only involves the state, i.e.,
{\boldsymbol{{u}}}_{0}={\boldsymbol{{a}}}
is not present, since the goal is to compute an optimal policy. The policy
{\boldsymbol{{\pi}}}_{\boldsymbol{{\theta}}}({\boldsymbol{{s}}})
and value function
V_{\boldsymbol{{\theta}}}({\boldsymbol{{s}}})
are obtained by solving Problem (
38
) with constraint
{\boldsymbol{{u}}}_{0}={\boldsymbol{{a}}}
removed. This is fully equivalent to

\hskip-5.0pt{\boldsymbol{{\pi}}}_{\boldsymbol{{\theta}}}({\boldsymbol{{s}}})=\mathrm{arg}\min_{\boldsymbol{{a}}}\,Q_{\boldsymbol{{\theta}}}({\boldsymbol{{s}}},{\boldsymbol{{a}}}),\quad V_{\boldsymbol{{\theta}}}({\boldsymbol{{s}}})=\min_{\boldsymbol{{a}}}\,Q_{\boldsymbol{{\theta}}}({\boldsymbol{{s}}},{\boldsymbol{{a}}}).

(39)

We briefly comment on this particular MPC formulation.
Function
\lambda_{\boldsymbol{{\theta}}}
has been introduced in
[
13
]
to make it possible to use a positive-definite stage cost
\ell_{\boldsymbol{{\theta}}}
even when the true stage cost
l
is not. This choice is related to the stability theory of economic MPC, where
\lambda
is called a
storage function
. In
[
13
]
it is discussed in detail how the use of a parametrized stage cost
\ell_{\boldsymbol{{\theta}}}
makes it possible to recover the optimal policy and value functions using (36)-(37) even if the MPC model (
38c
) does not accurately capture the system dynamics (
1
).

We ought to stress that, though we formulated (
38
) using a notation which is easily interpreted as a deterministic formulation, any MPC formulation can be used, including stochastic and robust formulations. Note also that in some cases, the scheme is reformulated using time-varying constraints, e.g., in case of tube-based robust MPC (used in the safety-constrained MDP context in
[
29
]
), where the constraints are tightened in order to guarantee that the original constraints are satisfied for all possible perturbations acting on a nominal model of the system. We stress that this is purely a matter of notation and implementation, and (
38
) covers this case, provided that
{\boldsymbol{{f}}}_{\boldsymbol{{\theta}}}
yields a set-valued state propagation.

After having recalled that MPC can be used as a function approximator when solving MDPs, we now discuss how stability can be enforced directly in MPC, such that the obtained MDP solution must also be stabilizing by construction. There exists a plethora of MPC formulations relying on different assumptions and providing different stability and safety guarantees and we refer to, e.g.,
[
24
,
16
,
18
,
19
]
and references therein for an overview.
In this paper, for brevity we focus on two special cases: nominal and tube-based robust MPC.

Nominal MPC.

Nominal asymptotic stability builds on the assumption that the deterministic model used in MPC is exact. This is clearly a simplified setting and we will consider a more general approach with robust MPC.

Assumption 4

The system dynamics and stage cost are continuous at
({\boldsymbol{{s}}}_{\mathrm{s}},{\boldsymbol{{a}}}_{\mathrm{s}})
and satisfy
{\boldsymbol{{f}}}_{\boldsymbol{{\theta}}}({\boldsymbol{{s}}}_{\mathrm{s}},{\boldsymbol{{a}}}_{\mathrm{s}})={\boldsymbol{{s}}}_{\mathrm{s}}
,
\ell_{\boldsymbol{{\theta}}}({\boldsymbol{{s}}}_{\mathrm{s}},{\boldsymbol{{a}}}_{\mathrm{s}})=0
. Moreover,

\ell_{\boldsymbol{{\theta}}}({\boldsymbol{{s}}},{\boldsymbol{{a}}})\geq\alpha_{1}(\|{\boldsymbol{{s}}}-{\boldsymbol{{s}}}_{\mathrm{s}}\|)
, for some
\mathcal{K}_{\infty}
function
\alpha_{1}
.

Assumption 5

There exists a terminal control law
{\boldsymbol{{\kappa}}}_{\boldsymbol{{\theta}}}({\boldsymbol{{s}}})
such that
\forall\ {\boldsymbol{{s}}}\ \mathrm{s.t.}\ {\boldsymbol{{h}}}^{\mathrm{f}}_{\boldsymbol{{\theta}}}({\boldsymbol{{s}}})\leq 0
, it holds that

\displaystyle{\boldsymbol{{h}}}^{\mathrm{f}}_{\boldsymbol{{\theta}}}({\boldsymbol{{f}}}_{\boldsymbol{{\theta}}}({\boldsymbol{{s}}},{\boldsymbol{{\kappa}}}({\boldsymbol{{s}}})))

\displaystyle\leq 0,

\displaystyle{\boldsymbol{{g}}}\left({\boldsymbol{{\kappa}}}_{\boldsymbol{{\theta}}}({\boldsymbol{{s}}})\right)

\displaystyle\leq 0,

\displaystyle{\boldsymbol{{h}}}_{{\boldsymbol{{\theta}}}}\left({\boldsymbol{{s}}},{\boldsymbol{{\kappa}}}_{\boldsymbol{{\theta}}}({\boldsymbol{{s}}})\right)\leq 0,

and the terminal cost satisfies

V_{\boldsymbol{{\theta}}}^{\mathrm{f}}({\boldsymbol{{s}}})\geq V_{\boldsymbol{{\theta}}}^{\mathrm{f}}({\boldsymbol{{f}}}_{\boldsymbol{{\theta}}}({\boldsymbol{{s}}},{\boldsymbol{{\kappa}}}({\boldsymbol{{s}}})))+\ell_{\boldsymbol{{\theta}}}({\boldsymbol{{s}}},{\boldsymbol{{\kappa}}}_{\boldsymbol{{\theta}}}({\boldsymbol{{s}}}))).

Moreover,
{\boldsymbol{{h}}}^{\mathrm{f}}_{\boldsymbol{{\theta}}}({\boldsymbol{{s}}}_{\mathrm{s}})<0
, and
V_{\boldsymbol{{\theta}}}^{\mathrm{f}}({\boldsymbol{{s}}})\leq\alpha_{2}(\|{\boldsymbol{{s}}}-{\boldsymbol{{s}}}_{\mathrm{s}}\|)
, for some
\mathcal{K}_{\infty}
function
\alpha_{2}
.

Proposition 1

Suppose that the system (
1
) matches the MPC model
{\boldsymbol{{f}}}_{\boldsymbol{{\theta}}}
, and Assumptions
4
and
5
hold. Then, if MPC is feasible at the initial time instant, it will remain feasible at all future times and the closed-loop system is asymptotically stable.

PROOF.

This standard result is given in, e.g.,
[
24
,
16
]
.

The introduction of stability constraints can then be done based on the result given in Proposition
1
: in order for Assumptions
4
-
5
to hold, we select a parametrization which is smooth enough, define functions
\alpha_{1}
,
\alpha_{2}
and constant
\epsilon
to define the set of feasible parameters as

\displaystyle\Theta:=\{\ {\boldsymbol{{\theta}}}\ |\

\displaystyle\ell_{\boldsymbol{{\theta}}}({\boldsymbol{{s}}},{\boldsymbol{{a}}})\geq\alpha_{1}(\|{\boldsymbol{{s}}}-{\boldsymbol{{s}}}_{\mathrm{s}}\|),

\displaystyle V_{\boldsymbol{{\theta}}}^{\mathrm{f}}({\boldsymbol{{s}}})\leq\alpha_{2}(\|{\boldsymbol{{s}}}-{\boldsymbol{{s}}}_{\mathrm{s}}\|),

\displaystyle{\boldsymbol{{h}}}^{\mathrm{f}}_{\boldsymbol{{\theta}}}({\boldsymbol{{s}}}_{\mathrm{s}})\leq\epsilon,\ {\boldsymbol{{s}}}_{\mathrm{s}}={\boldsymbol{{f}}}_{\boldsymbol{{\theta}}}({\boldsymbol{{s}}}_{\mathrm{s}},{\boldsymbol{{a}}}_{\mathrm{s}}),

\displaystyle{\boldsymbol{{h}}}^{\mathrm{f}}_{\boldsymbol{{\theta}}}({\boldsymbol{{f}}}_{\boldsymbol{{\theta}}}({\boldsymbol{{s}}},{\boldsymbol{{\kappa}}}_{\boldsymbol{{\theta}}}({\boldsymbol{{s}}})))\leq 0,

\displaystyle{\boldsymbol{{g}}}\left({\boldsymbol{{\kappa}}}_{\boldsymbol{{\theta}}}({\boldsymbol{{s}}})\right)\leq 0,\ {\boldsymbol{{h}}}_{{\boldsymbol{{\theta}}}}\left({\boldsymbol{{s}}},{\boldsymbol{{\kappa}}}_{\boldsymbol{{\theta}}}({\boldsymbol{{s}}})\right)\leq 0

\displaystyle V_{\boldsymbol{{\theta}}}^{\mathrm{f}}(x)\geq V_{\boldsymbol{{\theta}}}^{\mathrm{f}}({\boldsymbol{{f}}}_{\boldsymbol{{\theta}}}({\boldsymbol{{s}}},{\boldsymbol{{\kappa}}}_{\boldsymbol{{\theta}}}({\boldsymbol{{s}}})))+\ell_{\boldsymbol{{\theta}}}({\boldsymbol{{s}}},{\boldsymbol{{\kappa}}}_{\boldsymbol{{\theta}}}({\boldsymbol{{s}}}))),

\displaystyle\forall\ {\boldsymbol{{s}}}\ \mathrm{s.t.}\ {\boldsymbol{{h}}}^{\mathrm{f}}_{\boldsymbol{{\theta}}}({\boldsymbol{{s}}})\leq 0\ \}.

(40)

While this formulation might seem rather abstract and difficult to implement, in practice it is common to select
\ell_{\boldsymbol{{\theta}}}
and
V_{\boldsymbol{{\theta}}}^{\mathrm{f}}
as convex functions, for which it is simpler to impose upper and lower bounds. The steady-state constraint
{\boldsymbol{{s}}}_{\mathrm{s}}={\boldsymbol{{f}}}_{\boldsymbol{{\theta}}}({\boldsymbol{{s}}}_{\mathrm{s}},{\boldsymbol{{a}}}_{\mathrm{s}})
might be necessary if either the model or the steady state depend on
{\boldsymbol{{\theta}}}
, unless one relies on the results of
[
25
]
for infeasible setpoint tracking. Constraint
{\boldsymbol{{h}}}^{\mathrm{f}}_{\boldsymbol{{\theta}}}({\boldsymbol{{s}}}_{\mathrm{s}})\leq\epsilon
might not be necessary, provided that some weak controllability assumption holds
[
24
]
. Finally, MPC formulations with a terminal point constraint only require the lower bound on the stage cost, provided that a weak controllability assumption holds.

Since this formulation only provides formal stability guarantees under the strong assumption that the deterministic MPC model is exact, we discuss next the case of robust MPC, which formally guarantees that the real system will be asymptotically stabilized to a set.

Robust MPC.

In tube-based robust MPC, model mismatch is handled as additive bounded process noise.
Since the nonlinear case can be computationally demanding, we focus on the linear case.

6
Conclusions

In this paper, we have provided a way to enforce stability constraints in discounted MDPs. In order to achieve that, we have proven that, under a weak assumption, any discounted MDP can be reformulated as an undiscounted MDP, and we have proven that the obtained undiscounted MDP yields bias-optimal policies. In order to introduce the stability constraints, we exploited the results of
[
13
]
in order to deploy stabilizing MPC formulations to support the value functions and policy approximations required to solve the MDP. Future work will further investigate the stability properties of discounted MDPs and the possibility of using the equivalence in order to derive new algorithms for solving undiscounted MDPs.

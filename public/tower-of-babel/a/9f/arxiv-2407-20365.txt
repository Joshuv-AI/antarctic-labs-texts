1.
Introduction

Let
S=k[x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}]
and
I=\left(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}\right)^{n}
. It is well known that
I
is an equigenerated Borel ideal with minimal resolution given by either the Eliahou-Kervaire or the complex-of-boxes resolution. The Eliahou-Kervaire construction is very nice algebraically and it is supported by a regular cellular complex (see
[
mermin2010cellular
]
); however, this complex is not polytopal. The complex-of-boxes resolution, introduced by Nagel and Reiner (see
[
NagelReiner
]
), is polytopal but not symmetric.

In this paper, based on the complex-of-boxes resolution, we construct a symmetric polytopal complex supporting the minimal resolution of
I
. Using this complex, we also construct a symmetric polytopal complex supporting the minimal resolution of the ideal

\widehat{I}
obtained by removing
m=x_{1}\cdots x_{n}
from the generators of
I=(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n})^{n}
.

The ideas involved in these constructions are more generally applicable; one can describe a minimal polytopal resolution of any power of the maximal ideal with cyclic symmetry about an arbitrary (fixed) generator, or of the ideal obtained by deleting an arbitrary monomial generator from a power of the maximal ideal. Unfortunately, doing so is extremely notational; the details will appear in the companion paper
[
Dao
]
.

We should note that the Betti numbers of
\widehat{I}
are already well-known to experts, at least in folklore. (See
[
VdB
]
for an approach using trimming complexes; alternatively, see
[
DE
]
for a proof that its resolution is linear until the last step.) The polytopal structure of the resolution produced in this paper allows us to write down an explicit basis and convenient formulas for the differentials. It also allows us to consider generalizations such as the removal of multiple generators; some analysis in this direction will appear in
[
Dao
]
.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we introduce some terminology, notation, and tools used throughout the paper. In Section
3
, we recall the Eliahou-Kervaire and the complex-of-boxes resolutions for Borel ideals. In Section
4
, we construct a new (minimal) symmetric polytopal resolution for
I
. In Section
5
, we construct a new (minimal) symmetric polytopal resolution for
\widehat{I}
in the case
n=3
. In Section
6
, we generalize this construction to an arbitrary number of variables.
We compute the Betti numbers of
\widehat{I}
in Section
7
. Finally, in Section
8
, we discuss some natural questions arising from this work.

Notation 2.2

Following

2.2.
Cellular resolutions and polyhedral complexes

The idea of parametrizing a resolution by a simplicial complex was first introduced in
[
BPS
]
by Bayer, Peeva, and Sturmfels, and then extended to more general cellular complexes in
[
BS
]
by Bayer and Sturmfels. For details, see the original papers or the textbooks
[
miller2004combinatorial
, Chapter 4]
or
[
Peeva
, Chapters 57–60]
. In this paper, we are specifically interested in resolutions supported on a polytopal complex; the definitions below loosely follow the treatment in
[
miller2004combinatorial
]
.

Definition 2.3
.

A
polyhedral cell complex

X
is a finite collection of convex polytopes (in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
), called
faces
of
X
, satisfying two properties:

•

If
\mathcal{P}\in X
and
\mathcal{F}
is a face of
\mathcal{P}
, then
\mathcal{F}\in X
.

•

If
\mathcal{P},\mathcal{Q}\in X
, then
\mathcal{P}\cap\mathcal{Q}
(computed as a subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
) is a face of both
\mathcal{P}
and
\mathcal{Q}
.

The embedding of
X
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
equips it with an
orientation function
,

\text{sign}:X\times X\to\{\pm 1,0\}

with the property that
\text{sign}(G,F)=\pm 1
if and only if
G
is a facet of
F
. For all
F,G\in X
we have
\displaystyle\sum_{H\in X}\text{sign}(G,H)\text{sign}(H,F)=0
.

Definition 2.4
.

The cell complex
X
is called a
labelled
complex if it is equipped with a labeling function

\ell:X\to\{\text{monomials of $S$}\}

that assigns a monomial
\ell(v)
to each vertex of
X
and satisfies
\ell(F)={\rm lcm}\{\ell(v):v\in F\}
for every face
F
of
X
. We refer to
\ell(F)
as the
label
of
F
.

Definition 2.5
.

Let
X
be a labelled polytopal complex.
We construct a complex of free modules
\mathcal{F}_{X}
with basis given by the faces of
X
and differential arising from the labels and the topological boundary maps. More precisely, for an
i

F\in X
, we create a basis element (also called
F
) with homological degree
i
and internal degree
\ell(F)
. Then the differential
\partial
of
\mathcal{F}_{X}
is

\partial(F)=\sum\limits_{\text{facets }G\text{ of }F}{\rm{sign}}(G,F)\dfrac{\ell(F)}{\ell(G)}G

If
\mathcal{F}_{X}
is acyclic, we say that it is a
polytopal resolution
.

For any multidegree
m
, denote

\displaystyle X_{\leq m}=\{F\in X:\ell(F)\text{ divides }m\},

\displaystyle X_{<m}=\{F\in X:\ell(F)\text{ strictly divides }m\}.

Theorem 2.6
.

Let
X
be a labelled polytopal complex. The free complex
\mathcal{F}_{X}
supported on
X
is a (polytopal) resolution if and only if

is acyclic for all

.
In this case, it is a free resolution of the quotient module
\dfrac{S}{I}
, where
I=(\ell(v):v\in X\text{ is a vertex})
is generated by the monomial labels of vertices.

There are several explicit ways to resolve a Borel ideal. Two of the most important are the Eliahou-Kervaire resolution and the complex-of-boxes resolution, which we discuss in the next section.

3.
Resolving powers of the maximal ideal

In this section, we recall two famous resolutions of Borel ideals which are the Eliahou-Kervaire and the complex-of-boxes resolutions.

For a monomial
m=x_{i_{1}}\dots x_{i_{d}}
with
i_{1}\leq\dots\leq i_{d}
, define
\max(m)=i_{d}
, the maximal index of a variable dividing
m
. If
I
is a Borel ideal, the Eliahou-Kervaire resolution is a minimal resolution of
S/I
with basis consisting of
1
(in homological degree 0) and the EK-symbols

where
m
is a monomial generator of
I
and
\alpha
is a squarefree monomial with
\max(\alpha)<\max(m)
. The EK-symbol
[m,\alpha]
has multidegree
m\alpha
and homological degree
(1+\deg(\alpha))
. See
[
PeevaStillman
]
for proofs, motivation, and the differential maps.

The Eliahou-Kervaire resolution allows us to compute the (graded) Betti numbers of a Borel ideal by counting the basis elements associated to each monomial generator.

4.
A new symmetric polytopal resolution of
(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n})^{n}

In this section, we build a resolution of
I=\mathfrak{m}^{n}=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})^{n}
which is a polytopal subdivision of the
(n-1)
-dimensional simplex, and is also symmetric about its center
m=x_{1}\cdots x_{n}
. (In particular, no variable is preferenced over the others, so all corners of the simplex will look the same.) Our strategy is to let the cyclic group act on the principal Borel ideal
I_{1}=\Borel(m)
and its complex of boxes. This produces
n
polytopal complexes which intersect in positive codimension; gluing them together yields the desired resolution.

Unfortunately the gluing process is quite technical. We begin with a string of lemmas.

Lemma 4.1
.

[
Sinefakopoulos2007
, Lemma 3.1]

Let
I
and
J
be two monomial ideals in
S
such that
G(I+J)=G(I)\cup G(J)
set-theoretically. Suppose that

(i)

X
and
Y
are regular cell complexes in
{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
that support a (minimal) free resolution of
I
and
J
, respectively, and

(ii)

X\cap Y
is a regular cell complex that supports a (minimal) free resolution of
I\cap J
.

Then
X\cup Y
supports a (minimal) free resolution of
I+J
.

Remark 4.2
.

[
Sinefakopoulos2007
, Remark 3.2]

For any two monomial ideals
I
and
J
, we have

G(I+J)\subseteq G(I)\cup G(J)
.
Our assumption that
G(I+J)=G(I)\cup G(J)
guarantees the right labelling of the
cell complex
X\cup Y
. A case where equality holds is when all elements of

G(I)\cup G(J)
are of the same degree.

Remark 4.3
.

From assumption (ii) of Lemma
4.1
,
I\cap J
is generated by the labels of the vertices of
X\cap Y
. We conclude that

G(I\cap J)=G(I)\cap G(J)
. This is an extremely strong assumption, as intersecting ideals almost always produces generators in higher degree. But it does hold in the cases of interest, where, among other things, the ideals in question all have linear quotients, and the boundaries of the complexes coincide.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
M_{1},M_{2},\cdots,M_{n}
be equigenerated monomial ideals, all generated in the same degree. Assume that

(i)

W_{1},\cdots,W_{n}
are regular cell complexes in
{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
that support a (minimal) free resolution of
M_{1},\cdots,M_{n}
, respectively, and

(ii)

For all subsets
\{i_{1},\dots,i_{\ell}\}
,
W_{i_{1}}\cap W_{i_{2}}\cap\cdots\cap W_{i_{\ell}}
is a regular cell complex that supports a (minimal) free resolution of
M_{i_{1}}\cap M_{i_{2}}\cap\cdots\cap M_{i_{\ell}}
.

Then
W_{1}\cup W_{2}\cup\cdots\cup W_{n}
supports a (minimal) free resolution of
M_{1}+M_{2}+\cdots+M_{n}
.

Proof.

Since
M_{1},M_{2},\cdots,M_{n}
are all equigenerated monomial ideals,

\displaystyle G\left(M_{i_{1}}+M_{i_{2}}+\cdots+M_{i_{\ell}}\right)=G\left(M_{i_{1}}\right)\cup G\left(M_{i_{2}}\right)\cup\cdots\cup G\left(M_{i_{\ell}}\right).

We will prove the statement by induction on the number of ideals,
n
.

The case
n=1
is trivial and the case
n=2
is given by Lemma
4.1
. Assume that
the statement holds for
n
. We shall show that it is also true for
n+1
. Let

\displaystyle M_{1}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=M_{1}+M_{n+1},

\displaystyle W_{1}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=W_{1}\cup W_{n+1},

Remark 6.6

The complex

[

We find it a useful heuristic to think of

as a subcomplex of the

[x_{n},x_{1}]\times[x_{1},x_{2}]\times[x_{2},x_{3}]\times\dots\times[x_{n-1},x_{n}]

with each

With this notation, the dimension of a face

is the number of

with cardinality

, and

if and only if

contains

for all

: For

.

The complex

supports a minimal resolution of

.

We will prove Lemma

is acyclic for all multidegrees

Lemma 6.8
.

[
NagelReiner
, Lemma 6.4]

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a polytopal complex and
v
a vertex in
\mathcal{C}
that lies in a uinique facet
P
, and assume that
P
has strictly positive dimension.

Then the vertex-induced subcomplex
\mathcal{C}\smallsetminus\{v\}
, obtained by deleting
v
and all faces that contain it, is homotopy equivalent to
\mathcal{C}
.

Proof of Lemma
6.7
.

Let
\alpha=x_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots x_{n}^{\alpha_{n}}
be a monomial. We will show that
\widehat{Y}_{\leq\alpha}
is acyclic.

We first observe that if
m=x_{1}\cdots x_{n}
does not divide
\alpha
, then
\widehat{Y}_{\leq\alpha}=Y_{\leq\alpha}
which is
acyclic because
Y
supports a minimal resolution by Corollary
6.4
.

Next observe that, if
\alpha
is a multiple of
m^{2}
, the least common multiple of the generators of
J
, then
\widehat{Y}_{\leq\alpha}=\widehat{Y}
, which is homeomorphic to
Y
.
Thus, since
Y=Y_{\leq\alpha}
and
Y
supports a minimal resolution by Corollary
6.4
, we have that
\widehat{Y}_{\leq\alpha}
is acyclic.

Finally, assume that
\alpha
is divisible by
m
but not by
m^{2}
. We claim that
m
is contained in a unique facet of
Y_{\leq\alpha}
. Indeed, let
\mathcal{P}_{m,\alpha}
be the box

\mathcal{P}_{m,\alpha}=\left(\prod_{x_{i}^{2}\text{ divides }\alpha}\{x_{i},x_{i+1}\}\right)\times\left(\prod_{x_{i}^{2}\text{ does not divide }\alpha}\{x_{i+1}\}\right).

We claim that
\mathcal{P}_{m,\alpha}
is the unique facet of
{Y}_{\alpha}
containing
m
. First, observe that
m\in\mathcal{P}_{m,\alpha}
since one can choose
x_{i+1}
from every interval in the product. Next, observe that the multidegree of
\mathcal{P}_{m,\alpha}
is
\gcd(\alpha,m^{2})
, which is divisible by

. Thus
\mathcal{P}_{m,\alpha}
is a face of

.

It remains to show that every face of

containing

is contained in
\mathcal{P}_{m,\alpha}
. But every such face must have the form

\left(\prod_{i\in A}\{x_{i},x_{i+1}\}\right)\times\left(\prod_{i\in B}\{x_{i+1}\}\right).

for some sets
A
and
B
satisfying
A\sqcup B=\{1,\dots,n\}
. If
A\subset\{i:x_{i}^{2}\text{ divides }\alpha\}
, then
F\subset\mathcal{P}_{m,\alpha}
as desired. If not, then

.

If
\alpha\neq m
, then
\mathcal{P}_{m,\alpha}
has positive dimension and satisfies the hypotheses of Lemma
6.8
, so

is acyclic. If
\alpha=m
, then
\widehat{Y}_{\leq\alpha}

7.
The Betti numbers

In this section, we represent the Betti numbers of
\widehat{I}
in terms of the Betti numbers of
I
. From the construction in Theorem
6.10
, the difference between the minimal resolutions of
I
and
\widehat{I}
is the same as the difference between the resolutions of
J
and
\widehat{J}
, namely the faces containing
m
in
Y
(which are collapsed to a single face in
\widehat{Y}
). We first compute the number of boxes of dimension
i
containing
m=x_{1}\cdots x_{n}
in
Y
.

Lemma 7.1
.

The number of boxes of dimension
p
containing
x_{1}\cdots x_{n}
in
Y
is
\gamma_{p}=\displaystyle\binom{n}{p}
for
0\leq p\leq n-1
. (Otherwise,
\gamma_{p}=0
.)

Proof.

Any box of dimension
0\leq p\leq n-1
containing
x_{1}\cdots x_{n}
in
Y
is of the form

F_{1}\times\dots\times F_{n}

where there are exactly
p
factors of the form
F_{j}=\{x_{j},x_{j+1}\}
and
(n-p)
factors of the form
F_{j}=\{x_{j+1}\}
. Therefore,
\gamma_{p}=\displaystyle\binom{n}{p}
, for
0\leq p\leq n-1
.

Since
Y
has dimension
n-1
, it does not contain any boxes of higher dimension. ∎

Now we are able to compute the Betti numbers of
\widehat{I}
,
J
, and
\widehat{J}
.

Proposition 7.2
.

The graded Betti numbers of
J
are

\displaystyle\beta_{p,p+n}(J)=\sum\limits_{\ell=1}^{n}(-1)^{\ell+1}2^{n-\ell-p}\binom{n}{\ell}\binom{n-\ell}{p},

\displaystyle\text{if }0\leq p\leq n-1

\displaystyle\beta_{p,q}(J)=0,

\displaystyle\text{otherwise }.

Proof.

Following Theorem
4.14
(3) and Remark
6.6
, set
M_{j}=\{x_{j-1},x_{j}\}
for all
j
(so
M_{1}=\{x_{n},x_{1}\}
). Then every box in
Y
has the form

\displaystyle B=L_{1}\times L_{2}\times\cdots\times L_{n},

where each
L_{j}
is a nonempty subset of
M_{j}
.

With this notation,
B\in Y_{i}
if and only if
L_{i}=\{x_{i}\}
. For example, boxes in
Y_{1}
have the form
\{x_{1}\}\times L_{2}\times L_{3}\times\cdots\times L_{n}
.

A box has dimension
p
if
p
of the
L_{j}
are equal to
M_{j}
. Thus
Y_{1}
contains
\displaystyle\binom{n-1}{p}2^{n-1-p}
boxes of dimension
p
, since there are
\displaystyle\binom{n-1}{p}
choices for which
L_{j}
to set equal to
M_{j}
and two choices for each of the singletons except
L_{1}
(which must equal
\{x_{1}\}
).

For an
\ell-
tuple
\left(i_{1},\cdots,i_{\ell}\right)
, there are
\displaystyle\binom{n-\ell}{p}2^{n-p-\ell}
boxes of dimension
p
in the intersection
Y_{i_{1}}\cap\cdots\cap Y_{i_{\ell}}
, since every such box must have

L_{i_{t}}=M_{i_{t}}
for all

,
p
of the
(n-\ell)
remaining
L_{j}
must equal
M_{j}
, and there are two choices for each of the surviving
(n-p-\ell)
singletons. The formulas are now obtained by the inclusion–exclusion principle.
∎

It follows from the construction of
\widehat{Y}
that

\displaystyle\beta_{p,p+n}(\widehat{J})=\beta_{p,p+n}(J)-\gamma_{p},

\displaystyle\beta_{n-1,2n}(\widehat{J})=1.

Then we obtain the following corollary.

Corollary 7.3
.

The Betti numbers of
\widehat{J}
are

\displaystyle\beta_{n-1,2n}(\widehat{J})=1,

\displaystyle\beta_{p,p+n}(J)=\left(\sum\limits_{\ell=1}^{n}(-1)^{\ell+1}2^{n-\ell-p}\binom{n}{\ell}\binom{n-\ell}{p}\right)-\binom{n}{p},

\displaystyle\text{if }0\leq p\leq n-1

\displaystyle\beta_{p,q}(\widehat{J})=\beta_{p,q}(J)=0,

otherwise.

may not be tiled by boxes of the appropriate orientation.

Question 8.3
.

To what extent can the techniques of this paper handle the deletion of multiple generators?

It is known by a result of Velasco
[
Velasco
]
that monomial ideals exist whose minimal resolutions cannot be supported on any CW complex. Consequently, our techniques, which produce polytopal complexes, cannot be expected to hold up in arbitrary generality.

It is clear that we cannot delete generators arbitrarily, since the complexes
\widehat{J}
corresponding to the removed generators may overlap or the orientations of the surviving boxes may not be consistent, causing the boxes to disagree outside of the gray regions as in Figures
16
and
17
.

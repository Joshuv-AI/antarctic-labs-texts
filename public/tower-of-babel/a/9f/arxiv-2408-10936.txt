Stochastic Currents of Fractional Brownian Motion: Existence and Regularity
Martin Grothaus, José Luís da Silva, Herry Pribawanto Suryawan, Thomas Ullrich

Abstract
By using white noise analysis, we study the integral kernel ξ ⁡ ( x ) \xi(x) , x ∈ ℝ d x\in\mathbb{R}^{d} , of stochastic currents corresponding to fractional Brownian motion with Hurst parameter H ∈ ( 0 , 1 ) H\in(0,1) . For x ∈ ℝ d \ { 0 } x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\backslash\{0\} and d ≥ 1 d\geq 1 we show that the kernel ξ ⁡ ( x ) \xi(x) is well-defined as a Hida distribution for all H ∈ ( 0 , 1 ) H\in(0,1) . For x = 0 x=0 and d = 1 d=1 , ξ ⁡ ( 0 ) \xi(0) is a Hida distribution for all H ∈ ( 0 , 1 ) H\in(0,1) . For d ≥ 2 d\geq 2 , then ξ ⁡ ( 0 ) \xi(0) is a Hida distribution only for H ∈ ( 0 , 1 / d ) H\in(0,1/d) . For d = 1 d=1 , x ≠ 0 x\neq 0 , and H ∈ ( 0 , 1 ) H\in(0,1) , we show that ξ ⁡ ( x ) ∈ 𝒢 ′ \xi(x)\in\mathcal{G}^{\prime} , the space of regular generalized functions. Elements of the space 𝒢 ′ \mathcal{G}^{\prime} and elements from the negative Sobolev–Watanabe distribution spaces share the property that partial sums of their chaos decomposition are square integrable functions. More precisely, we show that ξ ⁡ ( x ) ∈ 𝒢 − s ⊂ 𝒢 ′ \xi(x)\in\mathcal{G}_{-s}\subset\mathcal{G}^{\prime} for x ≠ 0 x\neq 0 , H ∈ ( 0 , 1 ) H\in(0,1) , and all s > 0 s>0 .

Introduction

The concept of currents has its origins in geometric measure theory. A typical
1
-current is given by

\varphi\mapsto\int_{0}^{T}(\varphi(\gamma(t)),\gamma^{\prime}(t))_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\,\mathrm{d}t,\qquad 0<T<\infty,\quad d\in\mathbb{N},

where
\varphi:\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
[0,T]\ni t\mapsto\gamma(t)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}

is a rectifiable curve. The interested reader
may find definitions, results, and applications on the subject in the
books
[
4
,
16
]
.

To obtain its integral kernel, one can propose the ansatz

\zeta(x):=\int_{0}^{T}\delta(x-\gamma(t))\gamma^{\prime}(t)\,\mathrm{d}t,\quad x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d},

where
\delta
is the Dirac delta function, and try to give a mathematically rigorous meaning in an appropriate space of generalized functions.

The stochastic analog of the integral kernel
\zeta(x)
rises if we substitute
the deterministic curve
\gamma
by the sample path
of a stochastic process
X
taking values in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Hence, we obtain the following kernel

\xi(x):=\int_{0}^{T}\delta(x-X(t))\,\mathrm{d}X(t),\quad x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}.

(1)

The stochastic integral (
1
) must be
properly defined. More precisely, we choose
X
to be a
d
-dimensional
fractional Brownian motion (fBm)
B_{H}
, with Hurst parameter
H\in(0,1)
. Therefore, the main objective of our study is

\xi(x):=\int_{0}^{T}\delta(x-B_{H}(t))\,\mathrm{d}B_{H}(t).

(2)

The stochastic integral is
interpreted as a fractional Itô integral developed in
[
1
]
. Other approaches, such as Malliavin calculus and stochastic integrals through regularization, were investigated in
[
5
,
6
,
7
]
to study
\xi
. In
[
5
,
6
,
7
]
, pathwise with probability one,
\xi
was constructed as a random variable taking values in a negative Sobolev space. I.e., for a fixed path,
\xi
is a generalized function and is therefore not pointwise defined in
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Moreover, in
[
7
]
, for all
x\in\mathbb{R}
, the kernel
\xi(x)
was constructed in a negative Sobolev–Watanabe distribution space using Malliavin calculus for
H\in[1/2,1)
.

In this work, we show that, if
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\backslash\{0\}
,
\xi(x)
is a Hida distribution for any
H\in(0,1)
and
d\geq 1
while for
x=0\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
\xi(x)
is a Hida distribution whenever
dH<1
; see Theorem
3.11
and Corollary
3.12
. For
d=1
,
x\neq 0
, and
H\in(0,1)
, we show that
\xi(x)\in\mathcal{G}^{\prime}
, the space of regular generalized functions. The new idea to prove this type of regularity can be found in the key Lemma
4.14
. Elements of the space
\mathcal{G}^{\prime}
and elements from the negative Sobolev–Watanabe distribution spaces share the property that the partial sums of their chaos decomposition are square integrable functions. More precisely, we show that
\xi(x)\in\mathcal{G}_{-s}\subset\mathcal{G}^{\prime}
for
x\neq 0
,
H\in(0,1)
, and all
s>0
; see Theorem
4.17
. The question of whether
\xi(x)\in\mathcal{G}_{0}=L^{2}(\mu)
remains open. Should this be the case, the integral kernel of stochastic currents would even exist as a real-valued random variable.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we recall
the background from the white noise analysis needed later. In Section

3
, we prove the existence result, and in Section
4
, we prove the regularity result. The appendix contains the proofs for certain technical lemmas.

2.1
Hida Test and Generalized Functions

The starting point of the white noise analysis is the real Gelfand triple

S_{d}\subset L_{d}^{2}\subset S^{\prime}_{d},

where
L_{d}^{2}:=L^{2}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
d\geq 1
, is
the real Hilbert space of all vector-valued square-integrable functions
with respect to the Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{R}
,
S_{d}
and

S^{\prime}_{d}
is the Schwartz space of vector-valued test functions
and tempered distributions, respectively. We denote the
L_{d}^{2}
-norm
by
\|\cdot\|_{2}
and the dual pairing between
S^{\prime}_{d}
and
S_{d}

by
\left\langle\cdot,\cdot\right\rangle
, which is a bilinear extension of the inner product on
L_{d}^{2}
, that is,

\langle\varphi,f\rangle=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi_{i}(x)f_{i}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x,

for all
f=(f_{1},...,f_{d})\in L_{d}^{2}
and all
\varphi=(\varphi_{1},...,\varphi_{d})\in S_{d}
.
By the Minlos theorem, there is a unique probability measure
\mu

on the
\sigma
-algebra
\mathcal{B}
generated by the cylinder
sets on
S^{\prime}_{d}
with characteristic function
C
given by

C(\varphi):=e^{-\frac{1}{2}\|\varphi\|_{2}^{2}}=\int_{S^{\prime}_{d}}e^{i\left\langle\varphi,\omega\right\rangle}\,\mathrm{d}\mu(\omega),\quad\varphi\in S_{d}.

In this way, we have defined the white noise measure space
(S^{\prime}_{d},\mathcal{B},\mu)
.
Within this formalism, one can show that

\left(\langle\mathbbm{1}_{[0,t)},w_{1}\rangle,...,\langle\mathbbm{1}_{[0,t)},w_{d}\rangle\right),\quad w=(w_{1},...,w_{d})\in S^{\prime}_{d},\,t\geq 0,

has a continuous modification
B(t,w)
, which is a
d
-dimensional Brownian motion. Here,
\mathbbm{1}_{A}
denotes the indicator function of the Borel
set
A\subset{\mathbb{R}}
and
\langle\mathbbm{1}_{A},w_{i}\rangle
,
i=1,\ldots,d
is defined as an
L^{2}(\mu)
-limit.
For an arbitrary Hurst parameter
0<H<1
,

\left(\langle\eta_{t},w_{1}\rangle,...,\langle\eta_{t},w_{d}\rangle\right),\quad w=(w_{1},...,w_{d})\in S^{\prime}_{d},\;\eta_{t}:=M_{-}^{H}\mathbbm{1}_{[0,t)},

has a continuous modification
B_{H}(t,w)
that is a
d
-dimensional fBm. On the one hand, for a real-valued function
f
and
\frac{1}{2}<H<1
, the operator
M_{-}^{H}
is defined by

(M_{-}^{H}f)(x):=K_{H}\cdot(I_{-}^{H}f)(x):=\frac{K_{H}}{\Gamma\left(H-\frac{1}{2}\right)}\int_{0}^{\infty}f(x+t)t^{H-\frac{3}{2}}\,\mathrm{d}t,

(3)

provided that the integral exists for almost all
x\in\mathbb{R}
and the normalization constant is given by

K_{H}:=\Gamma\left(H+\frac{1}{2}\right)\left(\frac{1}{2H}+\int_{0}^{\infty}\left((1+s)^{H-\frac{1}{2}}-s^{H-\frac{1}{2}}\right)\mathrm{d}s\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}}.

On the other hand, for a real-valued function
f
and
0<H<\frac{1}{2}
, the operator
M_{-}^{H}
has the form

(M_{-}^{H}f)(x):=K_{H}\cdot(D_{-}^{H}f)(x):=\frac{(\frac{1}{2}-H)K_{H}}{\Gamma\left(H+\frac{1}{2}\right)}\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\int_{\varepsilon}^{\infty}\frac{f(x)-f(x+y)}{y^{\frac{3}{2}-H}}\,\mathrm{d}y,

(4)

if the limit exists for almost all
x\in\mathbb{R}
. For
H=1/2
,
M_{-}^{1/2}
is the identity operator. For more
details, see, e.g.,
[
1
]
,
[
19
]
, and the references therein.

To introduce the corresponding fractional noise
W_{H}
, we first need to define the operator
M_{+}^{H}
, which is dual to the operator
M_{-}^{H}
. Therefore, for
\frac{1}{2}<H<1
we define

(M_{+}^{H}f)(x):=K_{H}\cdot(I_{+}^{H}f)(x):=K_{H}\cdot(f*g_{H})(x),

where
g_{H}(t):=\frac{1}{\Gamma(H)}t^{H}
,
t>0
, whenever the convolution integral exists for almost all
x\in\mathbb{R}
. For
0<H<\frac{1}{2}
the operator
M_{+}^{H}
is defined by

(M_{+}^{H}f)(x):=K_{H}\cdot(D_{+}^{H}f)(x):=\frac{(\frac{1}{2}-H)K_{H}}{\Gamma\left(H+\frac{1}{2}\right)}\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\int_{\varepsilon}^{\infty}\frac{f(x)-f(x-y)}{y^{\frac{3}{2}-H}}\,\mathrm{d}y,

if the limit exists for almost all
x\in\mathbb{R}
.

There are several examples of functions
f
for which
M_{\pm}^{H}f
exists for any
H\in(0,1)
. For example,
f=\mathbbm{1}_{[0,t)}
,

t\geq 0
, or
f\in S_{1}(\mathbb{R})
. For functions
f_{1}
,
f_{2}

being either of these two types, it is easy to prove the following
equality

\int_{\mathbb{R}}f_{1}(x)(M_{-}^{H}f_{2})(x)\,\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\mathbb{R}}(M_{+}^{H}f_{1})(x)f_{2}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x,

showing that
M_{-}^{H}
and
M_{+}^{H}
are dual operators, cf. Eq. (12) in
[
1
]
.

The corresponding
d
-dimensional fractional noise
W_{H}(t)
in the sense of Hida distributions
is given by

W_{H}(t):=(W_{H,1}(t),\ldots,W_{H,d}(t)):=(\langle M_{-}^{H}\delta_{t},P_{1}\rangle,\ldots,\langle M_{-}^{H}\delta_{t},P_{d}\rangle),

(5)

where
P_{i}:S^{\prime}_{d}\to S^{\prime}_{1},i=1,\ldots,d
, denotes the projection on the
i
-th component, see Definition 2.18 in
[
1
]
for
d=1
. The generalized function
M_{-}^{H}\delta_{t}\in S^{\prime}_{1}
is defined for any test function
\varphi\in S_{1}
by

\langle\varphi,M_{-}^{H}\delta_{t}\rangle:=(M_{+}^{H}\varphi)(t).

(6)

For
H=1/2
, the operator

M_{+}^{1/2}
is defined as the identity, and
W_{1/2}(t)=(\langle\delta_{t},P_{1}\rangle,\ldots,\langle\delta_{t},P_{d}\rangle)

coincides with the vector-valued white noise.

Let us now consider the complex Hilbert space
L^{2}(\mu):=L^{2}(S^{\prime}_{d},\mathcal{B},\mu;\mathbb{C})
.
This space is canonically isomorphic to the symmetric Fock space of
symmetric square-integrable functions,

L^{2}(\mu)\simeq\Big(\bigoplus_{k=0}^{\infty}\mathrm{Sym}\,L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{k},k!\mathrm{d}^{k}x)\Big)^{\otimes d},

leading to the chaos expansion of the elements in
L^{2}(\mu)
,

F(w_{1},...,w_{d})=\sum_{(n_{1},...,n_{d})\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}}\langle F_{(n_{1},...,n_{d})},:w_{1}^{\otimes n_{1}}:\otimes\cdots\otimes:w_{d}^{\otimes n_{d}}:\rangle,

(7)

with kernel functions
F_{(n_{1},...,n_{d})}\in(L^{2}_{d,\mathbb{C}})^{\hat{\otimes}n}
and
w=(w_{1},\dots,w_{d})\in S^{\prime}_{d}
.
For simplicity, we use the notation

\displaystyle\bm{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(n_{1},\cdots,n_{d})\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d},\quad n=\sum_{i=1}^{d}n_{i},\quad\bm{n}!=\prod_{i=1}^{d}n_{i}!,

\displaystyle:w^{\otimes\bm{n}}:

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle:w_{1}^{\otimes n_{1}}:\otimes\cdots\otimes:w_{d}^{\otimes n_{d}}:,

that reduces the chaos expansion in (
7
) to

F(w)=\sum_{\bm{n}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}}\langle F_{\bm{n}},:w^{\otimes\bm{n}}:\rangle,\quad w\in S^{\prime}_{d}.

To proceed further, we have to consider a Gelfand triple around the
space
L^{2}(\mu)
. We use the space
(S_{d})^{\prime}
of Hida
distributions and the corresponding
Gelfand triple

(S_{d})\subset L^{2}(\mu)\subset(S_{d})^{\prime}.

(8)

Here
(S_{d})
is the space of the white noise test functions such that
its dual space (with respect to
L^{2}(\mu)
) is the space
(S_{d})^{\prime}
.
Instead of reproducing the explicit construction of
\left(S_{d}\right)^{\prime}

(see e.g.,
[
9
]
),
we characterize this space by its
S
-transform in Theorem
2.3
. We recall that given a
\varphi\in S_{d}
, the Wick exponential is defined by

:\exp(\langle\varphi,w\rangle):\>:=\sum_{\bm{n}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}}\frac{1}{\bm{n}!}\langle\varphi^{\otimes\bm{n}},:w^{\otimes\bm{n}}:\rangle=C(\varphi)e^{\langle\varphi,w\rangle},

where
\varphi^{\otimes\bm{n}}:=\varphi_{1}^{\otimes n_{1}}\otimes\dots\otimes\varphi_{d}^{\otimes n_{d}}
and the
S
-transform of
\Phi\in(S_{d})^{\prime}
is defined by

S\Phi(\varphi):=\left\langle\!\left\langle:\exp(\left\langle\varphi,\cdot\right\rangle):,\Phi\right\rangle\!\right\rangle,\quad\varphi\in S_{d}.

(9)

Here
\left\langle\!\left\langle\cdot,\cdot\right\rangle\!\right\rangle

denotes the dual pairing between
\left(S_{d}\right)
and
\left(S_{d}\right)^{\prime}
,
which is a bilinear extension of
\left(\!\left(\cdot,\overline{\cdot}\right)\!\right)
, where
\left(\!\left(\cdot,\cdot\right)\!\right)
is the sesquilinear inner product on
L^{2}(\mu)
. We observe that the multilinear expansion
of (
9
),

S\Phi(\varphi):=\sum_{\bm{n}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}}\langle\varphi^{\otimes\bm{n}},\Phi_{\bm{n}}\rangle,

extends the chaos expansion to
\Phi\in\left(S_{d}\right)^{\prime}
with distribution
valued kernels
\Phi_{\bm{n}}\in(S^{\prime}_{d})^{\otimes\bm{n}}
such that

\left\langle\!\left\langle\varphi,\Phi\right\rangle\!\right\rangle=\sum_{\bm{n}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}}\bm{n}!\langle\varphi_{\bm{n}},\Phi_{\bm{n}}\rangle,

for every test function
\varphi\in(S_{d})
with kernel
functions
\varphi_{\bm{n}}\in(S_{d})^{\otimes\bm{n}}
. This allows us to represent
\Phi
by its generalized chaos expansion

\Phi=\sum_{\bm{n}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}}I_{\bm{n}}(\Phi_{\bm{n}}),\quad\Phi_{\bm{n}}\in(S^{\prime}_{d})^{\otimes\bm{n}},

where

\left\langle\!\left\langle\varphi,I_{\bm{n}}(\Phi_{\bm{n}})\right\rangle\!\right\rangle:=\bm{n}!\langle\varphi_{\bm{n}},\Phi_{\bm{n}}\rangle,\quad\varphi\in(S_{d}).

Definition 2.2

(

.

A function

2.2
Characterization of Regular Generalized Functions

In this subsection, we introduce the space of regular generalized functions, which is used in Section
4
.
We restrict the construction to
d=1
and adapt the notation from
Subsection
2.1
.

Consider the Wiener-Itô chaos decomposition of
F\in L^{2}(\mu)

F=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left\langle f^{(n)},:\cdot^{\otimes n}:\right\rangle,\quad f^{(n)}\in(L_{1,\mathbb{C}}^{2})^{\hat{\otimes}n},

with

\|F\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}^{2}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}n!|f^{(n)}|_{(L_{1,\mathbb{C}}^{2})^{\otimes n}}^{2},

(12)

where
:\omega^{\otimes n}:\in(S^{\prime}_{1,\mathbb{C}})^{\hat{\otimes}n}

denotes the Wick power of
n
-th order of
\omega\in S^{\prime}_{1,\mathbb{C}}
and
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle

denotes the bilinear dual pairing on
S_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathbb{R})^{\otimes n}\times S^{\prime}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathbb{R})^{\otimes n}
.
We remark that
\langle\cdot,:\cdot^{\otimes n}:\rangle
extends
in the first variable to
(L_{1,\mathbb{C}}^{2})^{\hat{\otimes}n}

in the sense of an
L^{2}(\mu)
-limit.

Now, let
s\in[0,\infty)
be given. If we require

\|F\|_{\mathcal{G}_{s}}^{2}:=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}n!2^{ns}|f^{(n)}|_{(L_{1,\mathbb{C}}^{2})^{\otimes n}}^{2}

to be finite, in addition to (
12
), we obtain

\mathcal{G}_{s}:=\left\{F=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left\langle f^{(n)},:\cdot^{\otimes n}:\right\rangle\in L^{2}(\mu)\mid f^{(n)}\in(L_{1,\mathbb{C}}^{2})^{\hat{\otimes}n},\|F\|_{\mathcal{G}_{s}}<\infty\right\}.

Thus,
\left(\mathcal{G}_{s}\right)_{s\in[0,\infty)}
is a nested
family of Hilbert subspaces of
L^{2}(\mu)
with
\mathcal{G}_{0}=L^{2}(\mu)
.
We write
\mathcal{G}:=\bigcap_{s\in[0,\infty)}\mathcal{G}_{s}

for the projective limit of this family. This yields the topological
dual space
\mathcal{G}^{\prime}=\bigcup_{s\in[0,\infty)}\mathcal{G}_{-s}
,
where
\mathcal{G}_{-s}:=\mathcal{G}^{\prime}_{s}
. The elements of
\mathcal{G}^{\prime}
are referred to as regular generalized functions because the partial sums within their chaos decomposition are square integrable functions.

Note that the construction of
\mathcal{G}
is almost identical to
that of
(S_{1})
. The difference lies, in addition to the slightly weaker summability assumption, in the fact that we do not require the kernels
f^{(n)}
to possess additional regularity. Hence, we have the
following refinement of the Gelfand triple (
8
)
with
d=1

(S_{1})\subset\mathcal{G}\subset\mathcal{G}_{s}\subset L^{2}(\mu)\subset\mathcal{G}_{-s}\subset\mathcal{G}^{\prime}\subset(S_{1})^{\prime},\qquad s\in[0,\infty).

(13)

Example 2.7
.

Let
t\geq 0
be fixed and
H=\frac{1}{2}
.
Then the Brownian motion

and

In the following, we provide the characterization of regular generalized
functions, see
[
8
]
for details. More precisely,
given a
\Phi\in(S_{1})^{\prime}
, we have a necessary and sufficient condition
to check whether
\Phi\in\mathcal{G}_{s}
for some
s\in\mathbb{Z}
.
Before we state this characterization theorem (cf. Theorem
2.10
below), we need some preparations.
Recall the Gaussian measure
\mu_{\sigma^{2}}
on
(S_{1}^{\prime},\mathcal{B})
with covariance
\sigma^{2}>0
is given by its characteristic functional

C_{\sigma^{2}}(\varphi):=\exp\left(-\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}\|\varphi\|_{2}^{2}\right),\quad\varphi\in S_{1}.

(14)

Definition 2.8
.

We identify
S^{\prime}_{1,\mathbb{C}}
with
S_{1}^{\prime}\times S_{1}^{\prime}

and define the product measure
\nu:=\mu_{\frac{1}{2}}\otimes\mu_{\frac{1}{2}}

on
(S^{\prime}_{1,\mathbb{C}},\mathcal{B}\otimes\mathcal{B})
.

To characterize elements
\Phi\in\mathcal{G}_{s}
, it suffices to consider their
S
-transform
S\Phi
, a function defined on
S_{1}
. Since it is also a
U
-functional by Theorem
2.3
,
there is a unique extension of
S\Phi
to an entire function on
S_{1,\mathbb{C}}
,
see Lemma 10 in
[
12
]
.

Starting with an element
z\in S^{\prime}_{1,\mathbb{C}}
, we want to project
it into
S_{1,\mathbb{C}}
to which we may apply
S\Phi
.

Definition 2.9
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
be given and
\left(\varphi_{i}\right)_{i=1}^{n}\subset S_{1}
be orthonormal in
L_{1}^{2}
.
We call

P:S^{\prime}_{1,\mathbb{C}}\longrightarrow S_{1,\mathbb{C}},\;z\mapsto Pz:=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\langle\varphi_{i},z\rangle\ \varphi_{i},

an
orthogonal projection
from
S^{\prime}_{1,\mathbb{C}}
into
S_{1,\mathbb{C}}
.
Here,
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
denotes the dual pairing on
S_{1,\mathbb{C}}\times S^{\prime}_{1,\mathbb{C}}

which is a bilinear extension of
\left(\cdot,\overline{\cdot}\right)_{L_{1,\mathbb{C}}^{2}}
.
The set of all orthogonal projections from
S^{\prime}_{1,\mathbb{C}}
into

S_{1,\mathbb{C}}
is denoted by
\mathbb{P}
.

Like orthogonal projections in the classical sense, any
P\in\mathbb{P}

has the following property

\langle Pz,z^{\prime}\rangle=\left\langle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\langle\varphi_{i},z\rangle\varphi_{i},z^{\prime}\right\rangle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\langle\varphi_{i},z\rangle\langle\varphi_{i},z^{\prime}\rangle=\langle Pz^{\prime},z\rangle,

(15)

for every
z,z^{\prime}\in S^{\prime}_{1,\mathbb{C}}
. We are now able to state
the characterization theorem and refer to Theorem 2.11 in
[
8
]
for the proof and more details.

Theorem 2.10
.

Let
\Phi\in(S_{1})^{\prime}
be given.
Then for
s\in\mathbb{R}
it holds

\Phi\in\mathcal{G}_{s}\Longleftrightarrow\sup\limits_{P\in\mathbb{P}}\int_{S^{\prime}_{1,\mathbb{C}}}\left|S\Phi\left(2^{\frac{s}{2}}Pz\right)\right|^{2}\mathrm{d}\nu(z)<\infty.

In particular, the spaces
\mathcal{G}
and
\mathcal{G}^{\prime}
are characterized
by

(i)

\Phi\in\mathcal{G}\Longleftrightarrow\forall\lambda>0,\;\sup_{P\in\mathbb{P}}\int_{S^{\prime}_{1,\mathbb{C}}}\left|S\Phi\left(\lambda Pz\right)\right|^{2}\mathrm{d}\nu(z)<\infty,

(ii)

\Phi\in\mathcal{G}^{\prime}\Longleftrightarrow\exists\varepsilon>0,\;\sup_{P\in\mathbb{P}}\int_{S^{\prime}_{1,\mathbb{C}}}\left|S\Phi\left(\varepsilon Pz\right)\right|^{2}\mathrm{d}\nu(z)<\infty.

3
Stochastic Current of Fractional Brownian Motion

As motivated in the introduction, using white noise analysis, we investigate for
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
the following (generalized) function

\displaystyle\xi(x)

\displaystyle:=\int_{0}^{T}\delta(x-B_{H}(t))\,\mathrm{d}B_{H}(t)

\displaystyle:=\left(\int_{0}^{T}\delta(x-B_{H}(t))\Diamond W_{H,1}(t)\,\mathrm{d}t,\ldots,\int_{0}^{T}\delta(x-B_{H})(t))\Diamond W_{H,d}(t)\,\mathrm{d}t\right)

\displaystyle=:\big(\xi_{1}(x),\ldots,\xi_{d}(x)\big),

where
W_{H}:=(W_{H,1},\dots,W_{H,d})
is the vector-valued fractional noise defined in (
5
). The above stochastic integral was
introduced in
[
1
, Equation (26)]
and is called the fractional Itô integral. If
H=1/2
and the integrand is an adapted, square integrable function, then this stochastic integral coincides with the classical Itô integral.

From this point onward,
C
will denote a positive finite constant whose value may change from line to line.

Theorem 3.11
.

For
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\backslash\{0\}
,
0<T<\infty
,
H\in(0,1)
,
d\geq 1
, and for each
i=1,\ldots,d
, the Bochner integral

\xi_{i}(x)=\int_{0}^{T}\delta(x-B_{H}(t))\Diamond W_{H,i}(t)\,\mathrm{d}t

(16)

is a Hida distribution, and its
S
-transform is given, for any
\varphi\in S_{d}
,
by

S\left(\xi_{i}(x)\right)(\varphi)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d/2}}\int_{0}^{T}\frac{1}{t^{Hd}}\mathrm{e}^{-\sum_{j=1}^{d}\frac{(x_{j}-\langle\varphi_{j},\eta_{t}\rangle)^{2}}{2t^{2H}}}(M_{+}^{H}\varphi_{i})(t)\,\mathrm{d}t.

(17)

Proof.

First, we compute the
S
-transform of the integrand
\Phi_{t}
,
t\in(0,T]
, in (
16
), that is,

\Phi_{t}:=\delta(x-B_{H}(t))\Diamond W_{H,i}(t).

It follows from Definition
2.6
, Examples
2.1
and
2.5
that, for any
\varphi\in S_{d}
, we obtain

\displaystyle S\Phi_{t}(\varphi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle S\big(\delta(x-B_{H}(t))\big)(\varphi)S\big(W_{H,i}(t)\big)(\varphi)

(18)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(2\pi t^{2H})^{d/2}}\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2t^{2H}}\sum_{j=1}^{d}(x_{j}-\langle\varphi_{j},\eta_{t}\rangle)^{2}\right)(M_{+}^{H}\varphi_{i})(t).

It is clear that
(0,T]\ni t\mapsto S\Phi_{t}(\varphi)\in{\mathbb{C}}
is Borel measurable for every
\varphi\in S_{d}
.
Furthermore, for any
z\in\mathbb{C}
and all
\varphi\in S_{d}
, we estimate

|S\Phi_{t}(z\varphi)|
as follows

\displaystyle|S\Phi_{t}(z\varphi)|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{(2\pi t^{2H})^{d/2}}\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2t^{2H}}|x|_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}^{2}\right)\exp\left(\frac{1}{2t^{2H}}\sum_{j=1}^{d}|x_{j}||z||\langle\varphi_{j},\eta_{t}\rangle|\right)

\displaystyle\times\exp\left(\frac{1}{2t^{2H}}|z|^{2}\sum_{j=1}^{d}|\langle\varphi_{j},\eta_{t}\rangle|^{2}\right)|z|\left|M_{+}^{H}\varphi_{i}(t)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{(2\pi t^{2H})^{d/2}}\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2t^{2H}}|x|_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}^{2}\right)\exp\left(\frac{1}{2t^{2H}}\sum_{j=1}^{d}\left(\frac{1}{4}|x_{j}|^{2}+|z|^{2}|\langle\varphi_{j},\eta_{t}\rangle|^{2}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\times\exp\left(\frac{1}{2t^{2H}}|z|^{2}t^{2H}\|\varphi\|_{2}^{2}\right)|z|\|M_{+}^{H}\varphi\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{(2\pi t^{2H})^{d/2}}\exp\left(-\frac{1}{4t^{2H}}|x|_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}^{2}\right)\exp\left(C|z|^{2}\|\varphi\|_{2}^{2}\right)\exp\left(|z|^{2}\|M_{+}^{H}\varphi\|_{\infty}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{(2\pi t^{2H})^{d/2}}\exp\left(-\frac{1}{4t^{2H}}|x|_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}^{2}\right)\exp\left(C|z|^{2}\|\varphi\|^{2}\right)

for some continuous norm
\|\cdot\|
on
S_{d}
. The third inequality is a consequence of

|\langle\varphi,\eta_{t}\rangle|^{2}\leq\|\varphi\|_{2}^{2}\|M_{-}^{H}\mathbbm{1}_{[0,t)}\|_{2}^{2}=t^{2H}\|\varphi\|_{2}^{2}.

The last inequality follows from
\|M_{+}^{H}\varphi\|_{\infty}^{2}\leq\|\varphi\|^{2}
, which is due to Theorem 2.3 in
[
1
]
. By using a gamma function argument, the function
(0,T]\ni t\mapsto\frac{1}{\left(2\pi t^{2H}\right)^{d/2}}\exp\left(-\frac{1}{4t^{2H}}|x|^{2}_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\right)
is integrable with respect to the Lebesgue measure on
[0,T]
.

As the second factor
\exp\big(C|z|^{2}\|\varphi\|_{2}^{2}\big)

is independent of
t\in[0,T]
,
this shows that the conditions of Corollary
2.4
are satisfied and

\int_{0}^{T}\delta(x-B_{H}(t))\Diamond W_{H,i}(t)\,\mathrm{d}t\in(S_{d})^{\prime}.\qed

, we see that it is also possible to include

.

4
Regularity of Stochastic Currents of Fractional Brownian Motion

Consider the
S
-transform of the stochastic current of 1-dimensional fBm with Hurst parameter
H\in(0,1)
:

S\left(\xi(x)\right)(\varphi)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{1/2}}\int_{0}^{T}\frac{1}{t^{H}}\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{(x-\langle\varphi,\eta_{t}\rangle)^{2}}{2t^{2H}}}(M_{+}^{H}\varphi)(t)\,\mathrm{d}t,\qquad\varphi\in S_{1}.

(19)

To show the integrability condition of Theorem
2.10

of the
S
-transform (
19
), more suitable representation of

is needed. Point evaluations are too singular. The test function should appear only in integrated form. The new idea for obtaining such a representation can be found in the following key lemma:

Lemma 4.14
.

For every
x\in\mathbb{R}
,
H\in(0,1)
, the
S
-transform
of the stochastic current
\xi(x)
of fBm has a representation
given by

\displaystyle S\left(\xi(x)\right)(\varphi)

\displaystyle=\frac{H}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1}{t^{H+1}}\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{(x-\tau\langle\varphi,\eta_{t}\rangle)^{2}}{2t^{2H}}}\langle\varphi,\eta_{t}\rangle\,\mathrm{d}\tau\,\mathrm{d}t

(20)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}T^{H}}\int_{0}^{1}\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{(x-\tau\langle\varphi,\eta_{T}\rangle)^{2}}{2T^{2H}}}\langle\varphi,\eta_{T}\rangle\,\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle-\frac{H}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{(x-\tau\langle\varphi,\eta_{t}\rangle)^{2}}{t^{3H+1}}\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{(x-\tau\langle\varphi,\eta_{t}\rangle)^{2}}{2t^{2H}}}\langle\varphi,\eta_{t}\rangle\,\mathrm{d}\tau\,\mathrm{d}t,

for any
\varphi\in S_{1}
.

Proof.

Step 1
:
For any test function
\varphi\in S_{1}
and every
x\in\mathbb{R}
we define the maps

\displaystyle f

\displaystyle:(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R},\;(t,u)\mapsto f(t,u):=\int_{0}^{u}\exp\left(-\frac{(x-v)^{2}}{2t^{2H}}\right)\mathrm{d}v,

\displaystyle F:

\displaystyle(0,\infty)\longrightarrow\mathbb{R},\;t\mapsto F(t):=f(t,\psi(t)),\quad\psi(t):=\langle\varphi,\eta_{t}\rangle.

By Corollary 2.8 in
[
1
]
,
\psi:(0,\infty)\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
is differentiable, and its derivative

is a continuous function. It follows that
F
is differentiable, and its derivative is given

\displaystyle F^{\prime}(t)=\frac{\partial f}{\partial t}(t,\psi(t))+\frac{\partial f}{\partial u}(t,\psi(t))\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial t}(t)

(21)

\displaystyle=H\int_{0}^{\psi(t)}\frac{(x-v)^{2}}{t^{2H+1}}\exp\left(-\frac{(x-v)^{2}}{2t^{2H}}\right)\mathrm{d}v+\exp\left(-\frac{(x-\psi(t))^{2}}{2t^{2H}}\right)(M_{+}^{H}\varphi)(t).

For every
t>0
, the change of variable
\psi(t)\tau=v
in the integral

\int_{0}^{\psi(t)}\frac{(x-v)^{2}}{t^{2H+1}}\exp\left(-\frac{(x-v)^{2}}{2t^{2H}}\right)\mathrm{d}v\\
=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{(x-\tau\psi(t))^{2}}{t^{2H+1}}\exp\left(-\frac{(x-\tau\psi(t))^{2}}{2t^{2H}}\right)\psi(t)\,\mathrm{d}\tau.

(22)

It follows from (
21
) and (

) that

\displaystyle\frac{F^{\prime}(t)}{\sqrt{2\pi}t^{H}}=\frac{H}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\frac{1}{t^{H}}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{(x-\tau\psi(t))^{2}}{t^{2H+1}}\exp\left(-\frac{(x-\tau\psi(t))^{2}}{2t^{2H}}\right)\psi(t)\mathrm{d}\tau

(23)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}t^{H}}\exp\left(-\frac{(x-\psi(t))^{2}}{2t^{2H}}\right)(M_{+}^{H}\varphi)(t).

Step 2
: The function

(0,T]\ni t\mapsto\int_{0}^{1}\frac{(x-\tau\psi(t))^{2}}{t^{2H+1}}\exp\left(-\frac{(x-\tau\psi(t))^{2}}{2t^{2H}}\right)\psi(t)\,\mathrm{d}\tau

(24)

is
\mathrm{d}t
-integrable on
[0,T]
. The details on the integrability of the function in (

) are given in Lemma
A.18
, below.
If we integrate both sides of (

) from

to
t=T
we obtain

S(\xi(x))(\varphi)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{0}^{T}\frac{F^{\prime}(t)}{t^{H}}\,\mathrm{d}t\\
-\frac{H}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{(x-\tau\psi(t))^{2}}{t^{3H+1}}\exp\left(-\frac{(x-\tau\psi(t))^{2}}{2t^{2H}}\right)\psi(t)\,\mathrm{d}\tau\,\mathrm{d}t.

(25)

Step 3
: An integration by parts shows that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\frac{F^{\prime}(t)}{t^{H}}\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{T^{H}}\int_{0}^{1}\exp\left(-\frac{(x-\tau\psi(T))^{2}}{2T^{2H}}\right)\psi(T)\,\mathrm{d}\tau

(26)

\displaystyle+H\int_{0}^{T}\frac{1}{t^{H+1}}\int_{0}^{1}\exp\left(-\frac{(x-\tau\psi(t))^{2}}{2t^{2H}}\right)\psi(t)\,\mathrm{d}\tau\,\mathrm{d}t.

We refer to Lemma
A.19
, below, for details on the integration by parts in Equation (

4.14
now follows from (

Theorem 4.17

Proof.

We have to show the condition of Theorem

\displaystyle S(\xi(x))(\varepsilon Pz)

(28)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{H}{(2\pi)^{1/2}}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1}{t^{H+1}}\exp\left(-\frac{(x-\tau\varepsilon\langle P\eta_{t},z\rangle)^{2}}{2t^{2H}}\right)\varepsilon\langle P\eta_{t},z\rangle\,\mathrm{d}\tau\,\mathrm{d}t

From the definition of the function

S^{\prime}_{1,\mathbb{C}}\times(0,T]\times[0,1]\ni(z,t,\tau)\mapsto G(x,\varepsilon Pz,t,\tau)\in\mathbb{C}

\int_{S^{\prime}_{1,\mathbb{C}}}G(x,\varepsilon Pz,t,\tau_{1})\overline{G(x,\varepsilon Pz,t,\tau_{2})}\,\mathrm{d}\nu(z)

\displaystyle Cx^{k_{1}}\varepsilon^{k_{2}}\tau_{1}^{k_{3}}\tau_{2}^{k_{4}}\exp\left(-\frac{x^{2}}{t^{2H}}\right)

(32)

\displaystyle\times\int_{S^{\prime}_{1,\mathbb{C}}}\exp\left(\frac{x(\tau_{1}+\tau_{2})\varepsilon\langle P\eta_{t},\mathfrak{R}z\rangle}{t^{2H}}+\frac{ix(\tau_{1}-\tau_{2})\varepsilon\langle P\eta_{t},\mathfrak{I}z\rangle}{t^{2H}}\right)

Note that the image of the measure
\nu
under the map

(S^{\prime}_{1,\mathbb{C}},\nu)\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{2},\;z\mapsto(\langle\varphi,\mathfrak{R}z\rangle,\langle\phi,\mathfrak{I}z\rangle),

We introduce the following notation.

\displaystyle c_{P}:=c_{P}(t)

\displaystyle:=\frac{\|P\eta_{t}\|_{2}}{t^{H}},

(33)

\displaystyle\alpha:=\alpha_{P,\varepsilon}(t,\tau_{1},\tau_{2})

\displaystyle:=\frac{x(\tau_{1}+\tau_{2})\varepsilon c_{P}}{t^{H}},

\displaystyle\beta:=\beta_{P,\varepsilon}(t,\tau_{1},\tau_{2})

\displaystyle:=\frac{x(\tau_{1}-\tau_{2})\varepsilon c_{P}}{t^{H}},

\displaystyle\gamma:=\gamma_{P,\varepsilon}(t,\tau_{1},\tau_{2})

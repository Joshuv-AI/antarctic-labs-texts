Abstract
. We derive sufficient conditions for sampling with derivatives in shift-invariant spaces generated by a periodic exponential B-spline. The sufficient conditions are expressed with a new notion of measuring the gap between consecutive samples. These conditions are near optimal, and, in particular, they imply the existence of sampling sets with lower Beurling density arbitrarily close to the necessary density.

1.
Introduction

By a sampling problem, we understand the question of whether and how a function in a given space can be recovered from discrete information, which is usually given by point evaluations (samples). The given space is an a priori signal model, and for a century this model has been the Paley-Wiener space of bandlimited functions. The significance of the
Paley-Wiener space and the associated Shannon-Whittaker-Kotelnikov sampling theorem cannot be overestimated;
it is at the origin of the information theory by Shannon, it is the technical basis for analog/digital conversion in signal processing, and it informs a whole branch of complex analysis. From the point of view of mathematics, the decisive results are the necessary density conditions of Landau
[
26
]
and the sufficient conditions for sampling by Beurling
[
10
]
. Their work remains the point of reference for the exploration of sampling problems in analysis and signal processing. See
[
4
,
8
,
35
]
for some representative expositions of modern sampling theory.

In this paper, we study sampling in a shift-invariant space generated by a periodic exponential
B
-spline (PEB-spline). Given a PEB-spline, or more
generally a generator
\varphi
subject to some mild decay
conditions, the shift-invariant space generated by
\varphi
in
L^{p}(\mathbb{R})
is defined to be

(1.1)

V^{p}(\varphi)\coloneqq\Big\{f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}):f(x)=\sum\limits_{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}}c_{\ell}\varphi(x-\ell),\ (c_{\ell})_{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}}\in\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z})\Big\}\subseteq L^{p}(\mathbb{R}).

If
\varphi(x)=\tfrac{\sin\pi x}{\pi x}
, then
V^{2}(\varphi)=\{f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}):\supp\hat{f}\subseteq[-1/2,1/2]\}
is the Paley-Wiener space
PW^{2}(\mathbb{R})
of band-limited functions.

Shift-invariant spaces form a natural class of
signal models
in sampling theory, as they generalize the Paley-Wiener space and are useful for practical and numerical reasons. The generator
\varphi
can be considered a parameter of the signal model that can be tuned to the specifics of the data acquisition. For instance, if
\varphi
is compactly supported, then every point evaluation can be computed as a finite sum, and a sample
f(x)
affects only the coefficients
c_{k}
in a neighbourhood of
x
. Additional local properties of
f\in V^{p}(\varphi)
, such as differentiability and analyticity, can be implemented by imposing them on the generator
\varphi
. By contrast, the Paley-Wiener space consists of entire functions, and every sample of
f
has a long-range effect on the values of
f
. Shift-invariant spaces are central in approximation theory
[
13
,
22
,
25
,
33
]
and are increasingly used in signal processing.

For the mathematical formulation of the sampling problem, we assume that the signal space
V^{p}(\varphi)
is known and that
f\in V^{p}(\varphi)
is sampled on a set
X
together with the first derivatives
f^{(s)}(x),s=0,\dots,\mu(x),
for some multiplicity function
\mu
. Whereas the standard sampling problem only takes samples on a discrete subset of
\mathbb{R}
, the inclusion of derivatives has become important in several applications. First derivatives model trends, and higher derivatives indicate convexity properties. In higher dimensional spaces, i.e., for images or physical fields, one speaks of “gradient-augmented measurements”
[
2
]
, in event-based sampling “information is transmitted only when a significant change in the signal occurs, justifying the acquisition of a new sample”
[
29
]
, in other words, the value of the first derivative determines the position of the next sample.

For the Paley-Wiener space, sampling with derivatives was studied early on
[
16
,
30
,
31
]
, the problem was taken up again recently with an emphasis on shift-invariant spaces in
[
1
,
15
,
17
,
36
]
.

The main objective is to recover a function from its samples and derivatives on a
set
X=(x_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}
. We indicate the number of derivatives at every point with a
multiplicity function

\mu_{X}:X\longrightarrow\{0,\dots,S\}
. The goal is to derive a sampling inequality of type

(1.2)

A_{p}\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{p}^{p}\leq\sum\limits_{x\in X}\sum\limits_{s=0}^{\mu_{X}(x)}\left\lvert f^{(s)}(x)\right\rvert^{p}\leq B_{p}\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{p}^{p}\qquad\text{for all
}f\in V^{p}(\varphi).

Note that the number of derivatives depends on the sampling point
x
. If (
1.2
) is satisfied, we call
(X,\mu_{X})
a sampling set
(or a set of stable sampling) for
V^{p}(\varphi)
. Once a sampling inequality is available, one can use standard reconstruction algorithms from frame theory to reconstruction
f
from
(X,\mu_{X})
.

Our final choice is a class of
generators
for the shift-invariant space. We suggest the use of PEB-splines as suitable and practical generators. These have compact support, they are well understood in approximation theory
[
12
,
34
]

and in numerics. In sampling theory, they lead to

\mathcal{O}(N)
algorithms, when
N
is the number of available data
[
18
]
. Most importantly and in contrast to other classes of generators, for PEB-spline generators one can prove optimal results. This is precisely the objective of this paper.

Before proving anything at all, one should understand what is possible. How much information is necessary to recover a function from its samples? This question is answered by a necessary
density condition. For shift-invariant spaces the relevant quantity is the weighted Beurling density defined as

(1.3)

D^{-}(X,\mu_{X}):=\liminf\limits_{R\rightarrow\infty}\inf\limits_{y\in\mathbb{R}}\frac{1}{2R}\sum\limits_{x\in X\cap[y-R,y+R]}(1+\mu_{X}(x)).

If
\mu\equiv 0
, then
D^{-}(X,\mu_{X})=D^{-}(X)
is just the usual lower Beurling density. The weighted Beurling density counts the average number of data per unit interval. The necessary density condition, first proved by Landau
[
27
]
for bandlimited functions in full generality (with arbitrary spectra and in higher dimensions), turns out to be a universal information-theoretic bound for sampling. In the context of sampling with derivatives in shift-invariant spaces it can be expressed as follows
[
21
, Prop. 3.7.]
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Assume that
\mu_{X}
is bounded by
M
and
\varphi,\varphi^{\prime},\dots,\varphi^{(M)}
satisfies a mild decay condition, e.g.,
|\varphi^{s}(x)|\leq C(1+|x|)^{-1-\varepsilon}
for some
\varepsilon>0
.

If
(X,\mu)
is a sampling set for
V^{p}(\varphi)
, then

(1.4)

D^{-}(X,\mu_{X})\geq 1\,.

The problem of sufficient conditions that guarantee the reconstruction of
f\in V^{p}(\varphi)
from its samples
(X,\mu_{X})
is more interesting and also more difficult. Under minimal conditions on the smoothness and decay of
\varphi
one can always show with elementary tools that any sufficiently dense set is sampling
[
3
]
.
For some types of generators, the density condition (
1.4
) is (almost) a
characterization. If
\varphi
is either the cardinal sine

\frac{\sin\pi x}{\pi x}
, whence
V^{2}(\varphi)=PW
, or the Gaussian or some related function, then
D^{-}(X,\mu)>1
is a sufficient condition for
sampling
[
10
,
21
]
. For other generators, (
1.4
) may not provide a characterization, in particular, if
\varphi
has compact support, then the maximal gap between sampling points is limited by the size of
\supp\varphi
, whereas for given Beurling density large gaps are permitted. Therefore one often uses different notions to measure the density of a set.

Contributions.

For PEB-splines as generators, we will derive a set of conditions that allow the construction of sampling sets arbitrarily close to the necessary condition.

Our first theorem is somewhat technical because the sufficient conditions are expressed in terms of local Schoenberg-Whitney conditions that are
well-known in spline theory. These conditions come close to a characterization of sampling sets with derivatives but are not complete. We postpone the precise formulation to Section
4
, and state the most important consequence.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\varphi
be a PEB-spline of order
m
with support in
[0,m]
. For every
\varepsilon>0
and given multiplicity sequence

\mu=(\mu_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}
with values in
\{0,\dots m-1\}
, there exists a set
X=(x_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}
, such that

(X,\mu_{X})
is sampling for
V^{p}(\varphi)
,
\mu_{X}(x_{j})=\mu_{j}
for all
j\in\mathbb{Z}
, and
D^{-}(X,\mu_{X})<1+\varepsilon
.

Our second result uses the maximum gap between consecutive samples, also called the mesh width or the covering density. The maximum gap of a set
X=(x_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\subseteq\mathbb{R}

consisting of consecutive samples is defined as

(1.5)

\mathfrak{mg}(X)=\sup_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}(x_{j+1}-x_{j})\,,

and is used frequently to formulate conditions for sampling, e.g., in
[
3
]
.
Our new contribution is a weighted maximum gap that takes into consideration the number of derivatives at each point. It is defined as

(1.6)

\mathfrak{mg}(X,\mu_{X})=\max\left\{\sup\limits_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{x_{j+1}-x_{j}}{1+\mu_{X}(x_{j})}\,,\,\sup\limits_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{x_{j+1}-x_{j}}{1+\mu_{X}(x_{j+1})}\right\}

The rationale for this definition is that several derivatives at a point
x
, say
f^{(s)}(x),s=0,\dots,\mu_{X}(x)
, amount to
\mu_{X}(x)+1
data, so the next data point should be at distance
<\mu_{X}(x)+1
from
x
, in other words,

\frac{x_{j+1}-x_{j}}{\mu_{X}(x)+1}<1
. The precise formulation is as follows.

Theorem 1.3

(
Maximum Gap Theorem
)
.

Let
\varphi
be a PEB-spline of order
m

and let
X\subseteq\mathbb{R}
be a separated set with multiplicity function
\mu_{X}:X\to\{0,\dots,m-2\}
. If the weighted maximum gap of
(X,\mu_{X})
satisfies

(1.7)

\mathfrak{mg}(X,\mu_{X})<1,

then
(X,\mu_{X})
is a sampling set for
V^{p}(\varphi)
.

In contrast to the more technical theorem, the maximum gap condition
is easy to understand and to verify.

As a special case, we mention sampling with the same number of derivatives at every point.

Corollary 1.4
.

Let
\varphi
be a PEB-spline of order
m\geq 2

and let
X\subseteq\mathbb{R}
be a separated set. Assume that
\mu_{X}
is constant,
\mu_{X}(x)=s<m-1
for all
x\in X
. If

(1.8)

\mathfrak{mg}(X)<s+1,

then
(X,\mu_{X})
is a sampling set for
V^{p}(\varphi)
,
1\leq p\leq\infty
.

In particular, a lattice
X=\alpha\mathbb{Z}
is a
sampling set for
V^{p}(\varphi),1\leq p\leq\infty
, if and only if
\alpha<s+1
.

When using
\mathfrak{mg}(X,\mu_{X})
as the relevant density and PEB-splines as generators, then the weighted gap condition (
1.7
) is best possible in the following sense. One can show that for
a>1
and given multiplicity sequence
(\mu_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}
there exist sets
X=(x_{j})
, such that
\mathfrak{mg}(X,\mu)=a
, but
(X,\mu)
is not a sampling set.

It is easy to see that
D^{-}(X,\mu_{X})\geq\mathfrak{mg}(X,\mu_{X})^{-1}
. This explains why the maximum gap condition is often problematic. So far there is only a handful of generators
[
4
,
14
,
20
,
21
]
for which the condition
\mathfrak{mg}(X)<1
or
\mathfrak{mg}(X,\mu_{X})<1
has been proved to be sufficient for sampling. In a surprisingly large number of publications, a
sufficient condition
\mathfrak{mg}(X)\leq\delta_{0}
has been derived for unknown or small
\delta_{0}
. Since the comparison to the necessary condition is missing, more research is required to understand the relevance of such results.

In the last section, we exploit a connection between sampling in shift-invariant spaces and the theory of Gabor frames and derive some new results about Gabor frames with PEB windows.

Methods.

Following
[
4
]
we partition
\mathbb{R}
into intervals of finite length and then study the local problem. Since a PEB-spline of order
m\in\mathbb{N}
has support in the interval
[0,m]
, the restriction of a function
f
in
V^{p}(\varphi)
to an interval
[a,b),a,b\in\mathbb{Z}
, sees only the finite sum

f(x)=\sum_{\ell=a-m+1}^{b-1}c_{\ell}\varphi(x-\ell)\qquad\text{ for }x\in[a,b)\,.

This is then a finite-dimensional problem, and the intervening matrix is the
collocation matrix
associated to the PEB-spline
\varphi
. Its invertibility is well understood and can be expressed with the help of the Schoenberg-Whitney conditions on the sampling points in

[a,b]
. The characterization of invertible collocation matrices is one of the cornerstones of spline theory
[
34
]
, and our proofs pay homage to this fundamental result. We
develop two approaches centred around the collocation matrix. In one approach we force uniform bounds on the collocation matrix and then glue together the local reconstructions. In an alternative approach, we use Beurling’s technique of weak limits to arrive at a sampling theorem.

The paper is organized as follows. Section
2
introduces shift-invariant spaces and sampling sets and prepares some technical tools for sampling with derivatives.
Section
3
is dedicated to the basic theory of ECC-systems and B-splines with a special focus on collocation matrices for sampling with derivatives.
The first main result and the existence of sampling sets near the optimal density are proven in Section
4
. Section
5
is devoted to Beurling’s technique adapted to sampling in shift-invariant spaces.
With this at hand, we prove the main sampling with the maximum gap condition in Section
6
.
We conclude with Section
7
with a discussion of the two methods and Section
8
, where we derive the implications of the two sampling theorems for Gabor systems. A technical result is postponed to
the appendix
.

2.
Shift-invariant spaces and sampling

We begin with properties of shift-invariant spaces and various formulations of the sampling problem.

2.1.
(Vector-valued) shift-invariant spaces

Let us consider a shift-invariant space
V^{p}(\varphi)
. Since each element
f\in V^{p}(\varphi)
is represented as
f=\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}}c_{\ell}T_{\ell}\varphi
for some sequence
\left(c_{\ell}\right)_{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}}\in\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z})
, the desirable generators are those which come with the norm equivalence

(2.1)

A_{p}\left\lVert c\right\rVert_{\ell^{p}}\leq\Big\lVert\,\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}}c_{\ell}T_{\ell}\varphi\,\Big\rVert_{L^{p}}\leq B_{p}\left\lVert c\right\rVert_{\ell^{p}},\qquad c\in\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z}),

where the constants
A_{p},B_{p}>0
depend only on
p
. If this holds, we say that
\varphi
has
p
-stable integer translates (shifts)
and denote (
2.1
) as

\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{L^{p}}\asymp\left\lVert c\right\rVert_{\ell^{p}}
.
If norm equivalence holds for all
p\in[1,\infty]
, we omit the reference to
p
and speak of
stable integer translates
and
a stable generator
.

The following theorem summarizes some well-known conditions for
stability.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
\varphi:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{C}
be a bounded, compactly supported function. Then the following statements are equivalent:

(i)

The generator
\varphi
has stable translates.

(ii)

The generator
\varphi
has stable
\infty
-translates.

(iii)

The translates
\{T_{\ell}\,\varphi:\ell\in\mathbb{Z}\}
are
\ell^{\infty}
-independent, i.e.,

(2.2)

\sum\limits_{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}}c_{\ell}\,T_{\ell}\,\varphi\not\equiv 0\qquad\text{ for all }c\in\ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})\setminus\{0\}.

(iv)

The Fourier transform
\widehat{\varphi}
does not have a real
1
-periodic zero, i.e.,

(2.3)

0<\sum\limits_{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}}\left\lvert\hat{\varphi}(\omega+\ell)\right\rvert^{2}\qquad\text{ for all }\omega\in\mathbb{R}.

We refer to Ron’s survey article
[
33
]
. The equivalences follow from the results in
[
23
, Thm. 3.5]
and
[
33
, Thm. 29]
. For further references, the reader can consult
[
28
, Eq. (9)]
,

[
23
, Thm. 3.3]
. For sampling in shift-invariant spaces the stability condition is explained and used in
[
4
,
5
]
and
[
20
, Sec. 2]
.

For the special case of a bounded, compactly supported generator
\varphi
above with
\mathrm{supp}(\varphi)\subseteq[-L,L]
, the important lower inequality is almost trivial:

(2.4)

\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{L^{p}}^{p}\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}}\sum\limits_{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}}\left\lvert c_{\ell}T_{\ell}\varphi(t)\right\rvert^{p}\,dt\leq\sum\limits_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\int\limits_{K}^{K+1}\sum\limits_{\ell=K-2L+1}^{L}\left\lvert c_{\ell}\right\rvert^{p}\left\lVert\varphi\right\rVert_{\infty}\,dt=4L\left\lVert\varphi\right\rVert_{\infty}\left\lVert c\right\rVert_{p}^{p},

if
p<\infty
, with the analogous estimate for
p=\infty
.

Following
[
21
, Sec. 2]
, we further consider vector-valued shift-invariant spaces

(2.5)

V^{p}(\Phi):=\left\{\sum\limits_{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}}c_{\ell}\,T_{\ell}\,\Phi:c\in\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z})\right\}\subseteq(L^{p}(\mathbb{R}))^{S+1}

generated by a vector
\Phi=\left(\Phi^{0},\dots,\Phi^{S}\right)^{t}\in\left(L^{1}(\mathbb{R})\right)^{S+1}
with norm

(2.6)

\left\lVert F\right\rVert_{p}=\left(\sum\limits_{s=0}^{S}\left\lVert F^{s}\right\rVert_{L^{p}}^{p}\right)^{1/p},

with the usual modification for
p=\infty
. We will assume that
\Phi
has stable integer shifts, i.e.,

(2.7)

\Big\lVert\,\sum\limits_{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}}c_{\ell}\,T_{\ell}\,\Phi\,\Big\rVert_{p}\asymp\left\lVert c\right\rVert_{p}\qquad\text{ for all }c\in\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z}).

2.2.
Sampling sets

To accommodate derivatives and multiplicities in sampling, we consider tuples of sets
\overrightarrow{X}=\left(X^{0},\dots,X^{S}\right)
with
X^{s}\subseteq\mathbb{R}
,
0\leq s\leq S
. Assume that the generator has stable integer shifts and that the shift-invariant space
V^{p}(\Phi)
contains tuples of functions
F=(F_{0},\dots,F^{S})
which are pointwise well-defined. In the following,
V^{p}(\Phi^{s})
contains only continuous or piecewise continuous functions with well-defined one-sided limits at the finitely many discontinuities.
We say that
\overrightarrow{X}
is a
sampling set
for
V^{p}(\Phi)
,
p\in[1,\infty]
, if

(2.8)

\sum\limits_{s=0}^{S}\sum\limits_{x\in X^{s}}\left\lvert F^{s}(x)\right\rvert^{p}\asymp\left\lVert F\right\rVert_{p}^{p}\qquad\text{for all }F\in V^{p}(\Phi),

where the inequality constants depend only on
p
and
\Phi
(with the usual adaptation for
p=\infty
).
A weaker notion is that of a uniqueness set. We call
\overrightarrow{X}
a
uniqueness set
if the linear operator
F\mapsto(F^{0}|_{X^{0}},\dots,F^{s}|_{X^{s}})
is injective on
V^{p}(\Phi)
, that is,

(2.9)

{F^{s}}\big|_{{X^{s}}}\equiv 0\ \ \text{ for all }s\in\{0,\dots,S\}\qquad\Leftrightarrow\qquad F\equiv 0.

The case
S=0
is the standard case of sampling sets. As usual, the upper bound for sampling is easy to obtain in a general setting, whereas, for the lower bound, the specific features of the generator have to be exploited.

Let
\varphi

(i)

All functions in

(ii)

The sampling operator

is bounded on

.
Here the samples at discontinuities are to be understood as samples of the right-sided limits.

The analogous statement holds with left-sided limits.

To relate sampling in vector-valued spaces to sampling with derivatives with a multiplicity function

we set

3.
Some Spline Theory

In this section, we introduce ECC-systems and Chebyshev B-splines and cite and prove statements about collocation matrices arising in the sampling problem. We follow the terminology in
[
34
]
.

3.1.
ECC-systems and Chebyshev B-splines

We call a system of functions
u_{\ell}:[a,b]\longrightarrow\mathbb{C}
,
1\leq\ell\leq m
,
a Chebyshev system
, if the collocation matrix with entries

(3.1)

\left(\,u_{\ell}\,(t_{i})\,\right)_{1\leq i,\ell\leq m}

has a strictly positive determinant for all
a\leq t_{1}<\dots<t_{m}\leq b
. It follows from the definition that a Chebyshev system is linearly independent. The span of
\left(u_{1},\dots,u_{m}\right)
is called a Chebyshev space.

The definition of a Chebyshev system implies immediately the following property of the zero set. One of the main properties of these spaces is the number of solutions
of
f(x)=0
.

Lemma 3.1
.

If
\left(u_{1},\dots,u_{m}\right)
is a Chebyshev system, then either
(c_{\ell})_{1\leq\ell\leq m}=0
or

(3.2)

\#\bigg\{t\in[a,b]:\sum\limits_{\ell=1}^{m}c_{\ell}u_{\ell}(t)=0\bigg\}\leq m-1.

Moving further towards splines, we choose positive weight functions
w_{s}\in C^{m-s}[a,b]
on an interval
[a,b]\subseteq\mathbb{R}
and define

(3.3)

\displaystyle u_{1}(x)

\displaystyle=w_{1}(x),

(3.4)

\displaystyle u_{2}(x)

\displaystyle=w_{1}(x)\int\limits_{a}^{x}w_{2}(x_{2})\,dx_{2},

(3.5)

(3.6)

\displaystyle u_{m}(x)

\displaystyle=w_{1}(x)\int\limits_{a}^{x}w_{2}(x_{2})\int\limits^{x_{2}}_{a}w_{3}(x_{3})\dots\int\limits_{a}^{x_{m-1}}w_{m}(x_{m})\,dx_{m}\dots\,dx_{2}.

These are functions in
\mathbb{C}^{m-1}[a,b]
and form a so-called
extended complete Chebyshev (ECC) system
on
[a,b]
. This means that for all
1\leq D\leq m
and all
t_{1}\leq\dots\leq t_{D}\in[a,b]
the
collocation matrix of Hermite interpolation
, i.e., the matrix with entries

(3.7)

\big(u_{\ell}^{(d_{i})}\,(t_{i})\big)_{1\leq i,\ell\leq D},

where

(3.8)

d_{i}:=\max\left\{\ell:t_{i}=\dots=t_{i-\ell}\right\},\qquad 1\leq i\leq D,

has a positive determinant. This is the matrix that arises in the Hermite interpolation problem

(3.9)

f=\sum\limits_{\ell=1}^{D}c_{\ell}u_{\ell},\quad f^{(d_{i})}(t_{i})=\xi_{i},\qquad 1\leq i\leq D.

The associated ECC-space is given by
\mathcal{U}_{m}:=\spanSpace_{\mathbb{C}}{(u_{1},\dots,u_{m})}.

As the simplest example (and the motivation), we consider
w_{1}(x)=1
and
w_{s}=s-1
,
2\leq s\leq m
. In this case, we obtain the polynomials
u_{s}=(x-a)^{s-1}
,
1\leq s\leq m
, i.e., the ECC-space
\mathcal{U}_{m}=\mathcal{P}_{m}
is the space of polynomials of degree at most
m-1
.
One of the particularly useful properties of polynomial spaces is the self-containment
with respect to the derivatives, i.e.,

(3.10)

p^{(s)}\in\mathcal{P}_{m-s},\qquad 0\leq s\leq m-1,\quad p\in\mathcal{P}_{m}.

To preserve this property for ECC-systems, one replaces the usual derivatives with related differential operators. We define
D_{0}\,f=f

and

(3.11)

D_{s}\,f:=\tfrac{d}{dx}\,\tfrac{f}{w_{s}},\quad L_{s}:=D_{s}D_{s-1}\dots D_{0},\quad 0\leq s\leq m.

By Leibniz’s rule,
L_{s}
is a differential operator of order
s
with
variable coefficients.

Just as with the polynomials and the well-known B-splines, we can now consider Chebyshev splines. For an ECC-system
(u_{1},\dots,u_{m})
on

[a,b]
and knots
a=y_{1}<\dots<y_{D+m}=b
, a
spline
with these knots is a function
B\in C^{m-2}(\mathbb{R})
satisfying

(3.12)

B|_{(y_{\ell},y_{\ell+1})}\in\mathcal{U}_{m},\qquad 1\leq\ell\leq D+m-1.

This condition implies that
B
is piecewise
C^{m-1}
and at the knots
B^{(m-1)}
has jump discontinuities and only one-sided
derivatives exist. The integer
m
is the order of the spline.
In this case we define
L_{m-1}B
at the knots
y_{i}
to be the right-sided limit

(3.13)

L_{m-1}B(y_{i})\coloneqq\lim\limits_{x\searrow y_{i}}L_{m-1}B(x).

A major result in spline theory is the existence of Chebyshev B-splines (CB-splines) associated to knots
a\leq y_{1}<\dots<y_{m+D}\leq b
. Precisely, the CB-splines are the splines
\big(B_{m}^{\ell}\big)_{1\leq\ell\leq D}
with controlled support

(3.14)

\supp(B_{m}^{\ell})=[y_{\ell},y_{\ell+m}],\qquad 1\leq\ell\leq D.

The CB-splines
B_{m}^{\ell}
are unique up to normalization. The traditional construction is based on divided differences and can be found in full detail in Schumaker’s book
[
34
, Sec. 9.4]
.

We conclude by considering matrices associated to CB-splines. The definition of ECC-systems calls for the collocation matrix with the standard derivatives. We adapt this to the new differential operators
L_{s}
. Given
t_{1}\leq t_{2}\leq\dots\leq t_{D}
, let

(3.15)

d_{i}:=\max\left\{\ell:t_{i}=\dots=t_{i-\ell}\right\}\leq m-1,\qquad 1\leq i\leq D.

Let
(B_{m}^{1},\dots,B_{m}^{D})
be CB-splines associated with the knots
y_{1}<y_{2}<\dots<y_{m+D}
. We consider the collocation matrix

(3.16)

\big(L_{d_{i}}B_{m}^{\ell}(t_{i})\big)_{1\leq i,\ell\leq D}.

This is the matrix tied to the Hermite interpolation problem

(3.17)

f=\sum\limits_{\ell=1}^{D}c_{\ell}B^{\ell}_{m},\quad L_{d_{i}}f(t_{i})=\zeta_{i},\qquad 1\leq i\leq D.

We can determine exactly when this matrix is invertible
[
34
, Thm. 9.33]
, cf.
[
34
, Thm. 4.67]
.

Theorem 3.2

(Interlacing property, Schoenberg-Whitney conditions)
.

The determinant of the collocation matrix in (
3.16
) is nonnegative. It is positive if and only if

(3.18)

t_{i}\in\begin{cases}(y_{i},y_{i+m}),&\text{ if }d_{i}<m-1\\
[y_{i},y_{i+m}),&\text{ if }d_{i}=m-1,\end{cases}\qquad 1\leq i\leq D.

The theorem says that it suffices for
t_{i}
to be in the support of
B_{m}^{i}
.
The invertibility of the collocation matrix is independent of the
normalization of the CB-splines, as the normalization corresponds to multiplying the collocation matrix with an invertible diagonal matrix.

.

3.2.
Periodic exponential B-splines

We have now gathered all the necessary background on CB-splines and continue with the special case of CB-splines which are shifts of a single CB-spline. For this, we require the following structure:

(S1)

the knots are the integers
y_{i}=i-1
,
i\in\mathbb{Z},
2
2

2

The choice
y_{i}=i-1
instead of
y_{i}=i
is for the support condition (
3.25
) and consistency of Theorem
3.2
with later applications.

(S2)

the weights
w_{s}
,
1\leq s\leq m
are given by

(3.24)

w_{s}(x)=e^{\gamma_{s}x}r_{s}(x),\qquad\gamma_{s}\in\mathbb{R},\qquad T_{\ell}r_{s}=r_{s}>0\quad\text{for all }\ell\in\mathbb{Z}.

CB-splines in this form are called
periodic exponential
B-splines
(PEB-splines) introduced in
[
7
,
32
]
. If
r_{s}\equiv 1
for all
1\leq s\leq m
, then we refer to them as
exponential B-splines
(EB-splines). The benefit of (S1) and (S2) is the fact that the CB-spline associated with the knots
y_{\ell},\dots,y_{\ell+m}
is (up to scaling) uniquely determined by any other of the B-splines, e.g., by
\varphi\coloneqq B_{m}^{1}
.
It is clear that any shift
T_{\ell}\varphi
is a Chebyshev spline with

(3.25)

\supp(T_{\ell}\varphi)=[0+\ell,m+\ell],\qquad\ell\in\mathbb{Z}.

By the uniqueness,
(T_{\ell}\varphi)_{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}}
differs up to positive factors
\left(\theta_{\ell}\right)_{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}}
from the classically constructed CB-splines with divided differences
\left(B_{m}^{\ell}\right)_{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}}

[
34
, Sec. 9.4]
. The invertibility of the collocation matrix in Theorem
3.2
is now tied to the integers. We take a closer look at the associated differential operators. For ease of reading, we define for all
1\leq s\leq m
the functions

(3.26)

q_{s}(x)\coloneqq\frac{1}{r_{s}(x)},\quad\text{so that}\quad w_{s}^{-1}(x)=e^{-\gamma_{s}x}q_{s}(x).

Since both
q_{s}
and
w_{s}^{-1}
are strictly positive functions, they are in the same differentiability class.
Moreover,
q_{s}
is periodic. A simple calculation shows

(3.27)

\begin{split}D_{s}f(x)&=\tfrac{d}{dx}\left[e^{-\gamma_{s}x}q_{s}(x)\,\cdot f(x)\right]\\
&=-\gamma_{s}e^{-\gamma_{s}x}q_{s}(x)\,\cdot f(x)+e^{-\gamma_{s}x}q_{s}^{\prime}(x)\,\cdot f(x)+e^{-\gamma_{s}x}q_{s}(x)\,\cdot f^{\prime}(x)\\
&=\tfrac{1}{w_{s}(x)}\,\Big(\Big(-\gamma_{s}+\tfrac{q_{s}^{\prime}(x)}{q_{s}(x)}\Big)\cdot\mathrm{id}+\tfrac{d}{dx}\Big)f(x)\end{split}

and

(3.28)

\displaystyle D_{s}\,T_{\ell}\,f(x)

\displaystyle=\tfrac{d}{dx}\left[\ e^{-\gamma_{s}x}q_{s}(x)f(x-\ell)\right]

(3.29)

\displaystyle=\tfrac{d}{dx}\left[e^{-\gamma_{s}\ell}\cdot e^{-\gamma_{s}(x-\ell)}q_{s}(x-\ell)f(x-\ell)\right]

(3.30)

\displaystyle=e^{-\gamma_{s}\ell}\ T_{\ell}\,D_{s}\,f(x),\qquad\ell\in\mathbb{Z}.

We obtain the commutator rule

(3.31)

L_{s}\,T_{\ell}f=e^{-\ell\,\sum_{k=1}^{s}\gamma_{k}}\,T_{\ell}\,L_{s}\,f.

The differential operators
L_{s}
are natural in the context of ECC-systems, but in practice, information usually comes as samples of standard derivatives.
Therefore, in the following statements we derive the relation between the standard derivatives
and the differential operators associated with an ECC-system.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
r_{s}\in C^{m-s}(\mathbb{R})
,
1\leq s\leq m
, be
1
-periodic, strictly positive functions and
\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{m}\in\mathbb{R}
. Let
L_{1},\dots,L_{m}
be the differential operators associated with the ECC-system induced by

(3.32)

w_{s}(x)=e^{\gamma_{s}x}r_{s}(x),\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}.

Set for
0\leq s\leq m

(3.33)

v_{s}(x)=\prod\limits_{k=1}^{s-1}\frac{1}{w_{k}(x)},\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}.

For each
0\leq s\leq m
there exist periodic functions
p_{s,k}\in C^{m-1-s}(\mathbb{R})
satisfying

(3.34)

L_{s}f(x)=v_{s}(x)\cdot\bigg(f^{(s)}(x)+\sum\limits_{k=0}^{s-1}p_{s,k}(x)\cdot f^{(k)}(x)\bigg).

In a matrix notation, this can be written as

(3.35)

\big(L_{0},\ L_{1},\ L_{2}\ ,\dots,\ L_{s}\big)^{t}=\mathcal{V}_{s}\cdot\mathcal{P}_{s}\cdot\Big(\id,\ \tfrac{d}{dx},\ \tfrac{d^{2}}{dx^{2}},\ \dots,\ \tfrac{d^{s}}{dx^{s}}\Big)^{t},

where
\mathcal{V}_{s}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}^{(s+1)\times(s+1)}
is
the diagonal matrix

(3.36)

\mathcal{V}_{s}(x)=\mathrm{diag}(v_{0}(x),\dots,v_{s}(x))

and
\mathcal{P}_{s}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}^{(s+1)\times(s+1)}
is the periodic lower triangular matrix

(3.37)

\mathcal{P}_{s}(x)=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0&\cdots&0\\[4.30554pt]
p_{1,0}(x)&1&0&\cdots&0\\[4.30554pt]
p_{2,0}(x)&p_{2,1}(x)&1&\cdots&0\\[4.30554pt]
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\[4.30554pt]
p_{s,0}(x)&p_{s,1}(x)&\cdots&p_{s,s-1}(x)&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}.

In particular,
\mathcal{V}_{s}(x)
and
\mathcal{P}_{s}(x)
are invertible for all
x\in\mathbb{R}
and are continuous on
\mathbb{R}
.

Proof.

We prove this formula by induction. Clearly,
f^{(0)}=L_{0}f
. For the induction step we use (
3.27
) and
w_{k}^{-1}(x)=e^{-\gamma_{k}x}q_{k}(x)
.

(3.38)

\displaystyle L_{s+1}f

\displaystyle=D_{s+1}L_{s}f\overset{\scriptscriptstyle\eqref{eq:D_s_exact}}{=}\frac{1}{w_{s+1}}\,\bigg(\bigg(-\gamma_{s+1}+\frac{q_{s+1}^{\prime}}{q_{s+1}}\bigg)\cdot L_{s}f+(L_{s}f)^{\prime}\bigg)

(3.39)

\displaystyle\underset{\scriptscriptstyle\text{step}}{\overset{\scriptscriptstyle\text{Induction}}{{\color[rgb]{1,1,1}=}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{w_{s+1}}\,\bigg\{\bigg(-\gamma_{s+1}+\frac{q_{s+1}^{\prime}}{q_{s+1}}\bigg)\cdot v_{s}\cdot\sum\limits_{k=0}^{s}p_{s,k}\cdot f^{(k)}

(3.40)

\displaystyle\qquad\quad+v_{s}\cdot\bigg(\,\sum\limits_{k=0}^{s}p_{s,k}\cdot f^{(k)}\bigg)^{\prime}+v_{s}^{\prime}\cdot\sum\limits_{k=0}^{s}p_{s,k}\cdot f^{(k)}\bigg)

(3.41)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{w_{s+1}}\,\bigg(-\gamma_{s+1}+\frac{q_{s+1}^{\prime}}{q_{s+1}}\bigg)\cdot v_{s}\cdot\sum\limits_{k=0}^{s}p_{s,k}\cdot f^{(k)}\bigg\}

(3.42)

\displaystyle\qquad\quad+\frac{v_{s}}{w_{s+1}}\cdot\sum\limits_{k=0}^{s}p_{s,k}^{\prime}\cdot f^{(k)}+\frac{v_{s}}{w_{s+1}}\cdot\sum\limits_{k=0}^{s}p_{s,k}\cdot f^{(k+1)}

(3.43)

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad+\frac{v_{s}}{w_{s+1}}\cdot\sum\limits_{n=1}^{s}\bigg(-\gamma_{n}+\frac{q_{n}^{\prime}}{q_{n}}\bigg)\cdot\sum\limits_{k=0}^{s}p_{s,k}\cdot f^{(k)},

where we used

(3.44)

v_{s}^{\prime}=\sum\limits_{n=1}^{s}{w_{n}}\cdot v_{s}\cdot\,\left(w_{n}^{-1}\right)^{\prime}=\sum\limits_{n=1}^{s}{w_{n}}\cdot v_{s}\cdot\,w_{n}^{-1}\bigg(-\gamma_{n}+\frac{q^{\prime}_{n}}{q_{n}}\bigg)=v_{s}\cdot\sum\limits_{n=1}^{s}\bigg(-\gamma_{n}+\frac{q^{\prime}_{n}}{q_{n}}\bigg).

for the last equality. We recall that
v_{s+1}=\frac{v_{s}}{w_{s+1}}
and
p_{s,s}=1
by the induction hypothesis. We set
p_{s,-1}\coloneqq 0
to obtain

(3.45)

\displaystyle L_{s+1}f

\displaystyle=v_{s+1}\cdot\bigg(f^{(s+1)}+\sum\limits_{k=0}^{s}\left(\left(-\gamma_{s+1}+\frac{q_{s+1}^{\prime}}{q_{s+1}}\right)\cdot p_{s,k}+p_{s,k}^{\prime}+p_{s,k-1}\right)\cdot f^{(k)}\bigg)

(3.46)

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad+v_{s+1}\sum\limits_{n=1}^{s}\left(-\gamma_{n}+\frac{q_{n}^{\prime}}{q_{n}}\right)\cdot\sum\limits_{k=0}^{s}p_{s,k}\cdot f^{(k)}

(3.47)

\displaystyle=v_{s+1}\cdot f^{(s+1)}+v_{s+1}\cdot\sum\limits_{k=0}^{s}\underbrace{\left(p_{s,k}^{\prime}+p_{s,k-1}+p_{s,k}\sum\limits_{n=1}^{s+1}\left(-\gamma_{n}+\frac{q_{n}^{\prime}}{q_{n}}\right)\,\right)}_{=:p_{s+1,k}}\cdot f^{(k)},

concluding the proof.
∎

Lemma 3.5
.

Let the knots
y_{i}=i-1,i=1,\dots,D
and the weights
w_{s}
,
1\leq s\leq m
be given and let
B_{m}^{1},\dots,B_{m}^{D}=T_{D-1}B_{m}^{1}
be the associated PEB-splines.
Then the determinant of the collocation matrix

(3.48)

M\begin{pmatrix}t_{1},\dots,t_{D}\\
B_{m}^{1},\dots,B_{m}^{D}\end{pmatrix}\coloneqq\Big((B_{m}^{\ell})^{(d_{i})}(t_{i})\Big)_{1\leq i,\ell\leq D}=\Big(T_{\ell-1}(B_{m}^{1})^{(d_{i})}(t_{i})\Big)_{1\leq i,\ell\leq D}

is nonnegative. It is positive if and only if

(3.49)

t_{i}\in\begin{cases}(i-1,i+m-1),&d_{i}<m-1\\
[i-1,i+m-1),&d_{i}=m-1,\end{cases}\qquad 1\leq i\leq D.

Proof.

We first address the second equality in the definition of the matrix
M
. As already noted in (
3.25
), the PEB-splines for equispaced knots are shifts of a single PEB-spline, namely,
B_{m}^{\ell}=T_{\ell-1}B_{m}^{1}
. Since differentiation and translation operators commute, we have
(B_{m}^{\ell})^{(d_{i})}(t_{i})=T_{\ell-1}(B_{m}^{1})^{(d_{i})}(t_{i})
.

Let as before
d_{i}
count the previous occurrences of the point
t_{i}
, i.e.,

(3.50)

d_{i}=\max\left\{\ell:t_{i}=\dots=t_{i-\ell}\right\}\leq m-1,\qquad 1\leq i\leq D.

Then
t=(t_{1},t_{2},t_{3},\dots,t_{D})
can be rewritten as

t=\Big(\underbrace{t_{1},\dots,t_{1}}_{{d_{1}+1}\text{-times}},\underbrace{t_{d_{1}+2},\dots,t_{d_{1}+2}}_{d_{2}+1\text{-times}},\underbrace{t_{d_{1}+d_{2}+3},\dots,t_{d_{1}+d_{2}+3}}_{d_{3}+1\text{-times}},\dots,\underbrace{t_{D},\dots,t_{D}}_{{d_{N}+1}\text{-times}}\Big).

We define the block-diagonal matrices

(3.51)

\mathbf{P}(t):=\begin{pmatrix}\mathcal{P}_{d_{1}}(t_{1})&0&0&\cdots&0\\[4.30554pt]
0&\mathcal{P}_{d_{2}}(t_{d_{1}+2})&0&\cdots&0\\[4.30554pt]
0&0&\mathcal{P}_{d_{3}}(t_{d_{1}+d_{2}+3})&\cdots&0\\[4.30554pt]
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\[4.30554pt]
0&0&\cdots&0&\mathcal{P}_{d_{N}}(t_{D})\end{pmatrix}\in\mathbb{R}^{D\times D},

(3.52)

\mathbf{V}(t):=\begin{pmatrix}\mathcal{V}_{d_{1}}(t_{1})&0&0&\cdots&0\\[4.30554pt]
0&\mathcal{V}_{d_{2}}(t_{d_{1}+2})&0&\cdots&0\\[4.30554pt]
0&0&\mathcal{V}_{d_{3}}(t_{d_{1}+d_{2}+3})&\cdots&0\\[4.30554pt]
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\[4.30554pt]
0&0&\cdots&0&\mathcal{V}_{d_{N}}(t_{D})\end{pmatrix}\in\mathbb{R}^{D\times D}.

Since each
\mathcal{P}_{d_{i}}
has determinant
1
, the block matrix
\mathbf{P}(t)
also has determinant
1
. By construction, the matrix
\mathbf{V}(t)
is a diagonal matrix with strictly positive diagonal entries.
By Lemma
3.4
, the collocation matrix with the associated differential operators and the collocation matrix with the standard derivatives are related by

(3.53)

\left(L_{d_{i}}B_{m}^{\ell}(t_{i})\right)_{1\leq i,\ell\leq D}=\mathbf{V}(t)\cdot\mathbf{P}(t)\cdot M\begin{pmatrix}t_{1},\dots,t_{D}\\
B_{m}^{1},\dots,B_{m}^{D}\end{pmatrix}.

The claim follows from the multiplicativity of the determinant and Theorem
3.2
with
y_{i}=i-1
.
∎

We finally relate the collocation matrix to sampling with multiplicities.

Corollary 3.6
.

Let
\varphi
be a PEB-spline of order
m
supported on the interval
[0,m]
. Further let
x=\left(x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{N}\right)\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
be a vector of pairwise distinct sampling points,
x_{1}<\dots<x_{N}
, with multiplicities
\mu=\left(\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{N}\right)\in\{0,\dots,m-1\}^{N}
. Set
D=\sum_{j=1}^{N}(1+\mu_{j})
. The Hermite interpolation problem

(3.54)

f=\sum\limits_{\ell=\ell_{0}}^{\ell_{0}+D-1}c_{\ell}T_{\ell}\varphi,\quad f^{(s)}(x_{j})=\xi_{j,s},\quad\ 0\leq s\leq\mu_{j},\ 1\leq j\leq N

has a unique solution if and only if

(3.55)

\begin{split}x_{j}&\in(\mu_{j}+\ell_{0},m+\ell_{0})+\sum\limits_{n=1}^{j-1}\left(1+\mu_{n}\right),\qquad\text{if}\ \ \mu_{j}<m-1,\\[2.15277pt]
x_{j}&\in[\mu_{j}+\ell_{0},m+\ell_{0})+\sum\limits_{n=1}^{j-1}\left(1+\mu_{n}\right),\qquad\text{if}\ \ \mu_{j}=m-1.\end{split}

In this case, the solution depends linearly on the interpolation data.

If the points satisfy (
3.55
), we say that they
satisfy the Schoenberg-Whitney conditions
.

Proof.

To apply the interlacing property to the interpolation problem (
3.54
), we set

(3.56)

t=\Big(\underbrace{x_{1},\dots,x_{1}}_{{\mu_{1}+1}\text{-times}},\underbrace{x_{2},\dots,x_{2}}_{{\mu_{2}+1}\text{-times}},\dots,\underbrace{x_{N},\dots,x_{N}}_{{\mu_{N}+1}\text{-times}}\Big)\in\mathbb{R}^{D},

to be precise,

(3.57)

t_{i}=x_{j}\qquad\text{ if }\qquad\sum\limits_{n=1}^{j-1}(1+\mu_{n})<i\leq\sum\limits_{n=1}^{j}(1+\mu_{n}).

Then the vector of multiplicities is given by

(3.58)

d=\Big(0,1,\dots,\mu_{1},0,1,\dots,\mu_{2},\dots,0,1,\dots,\mu_{N}\Big)\in\big\{0,\dots,m-1\big\}^{D}.

Accordingly, we set
\xi\in\mathbb{C}^{D}
to be the list of the samples
\xi=(\xi_{i})_{i=1,\dots,D}
,

(3.59)

\xi=\Big(\xi_{1,0},\,\xi_{1,1},\,\xi_{1,2},\,\dots,\,\xi_{1,\mu_{1}},\,\xi_{2,0},\,\xi_{2,1},\,\xi_{2,2},\,\dots,\,\xi_{2,\mu_{2}},\,\dots,\,\xi_{N,0},\,\xi_{N,1},\,\xi_{N,2}\dots,\,\xi_{N,\mu_{n}}\Big).

With this notation, the interpolation problem (
3.54
) can be recast as

(3.60)

f=\sum\limits_{\ell=\ell_{0}}^{\ell_{0}+D-1}c_{\ell}\,T_{\ell}\varphi,\quad f^{(d_{i})}(t_{i})=\xi_{i},\qquad 1\leq i\leq D.

The corresponding collocation matrix is

(3.61)

M\begin{pmatrix}t_{1},\dots,t_{D}\\
T_{\ell_{0}}\varphi,\dots,T_{\ell_{0}+D-1}\varphi\end{pmatrix}=\Big(T_{\ell-1}\varphi^{(d_{i})}(t_{i}-\ell_{0})\Big)_{1\leq i,\ell\leq D}.

For further reference, we denote it by

(3.62)

\mathbf{M}(x,\mu,\ell_{0})\coloneqq M\begin{pmatrix}t_{1},\dots,t_{D}\\
T_{\ell_{0}}\varphi,\dots,T_{\ell_{0}+D-1}\varphi\end{pmatrix}.

By Lemma
3.5
, the collocation matrix is
invertible, and thus a unique solution of (
3.54
) exists, if and only if
t_{i}-\ell_{0}\in(i-1,i+m-1)
for all
1\leq i\leq D
(with the modification
t_{i}-\ell_{0}\in[i-1,i+m-1)
if
d_{i}=m-1
). Expressed with the sampling points
x_{j}
, the collocation matrix is invertible if and only if

(3.63)

\displaystyle x_{j}\

\displaystyle\in\ \bigcap_{s=0}^{\mu_{j}}\ \Big(\left(s,s+m\right)\ +\ell_{0}+\sum\limits_{n=1}^{j-1}\left(1+\mu_{n}\right)\Big)=(\mu_{j}+\ell_{0},m+\ell_{0})+\sum\limits_{n=1}^{j-1}\left(1+\mu_{n}\right).

for all
1\leq j\leq N
. The second condition in (
3.55
) is the adaptation for
\mu_{j}=m-1
.
∎

3.3.
Stability of PEB-Splines

To use PEB-splines as generators of shift-invariant spaces, we need to verify the stability of PEB-splines and their derivatives.

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
\varphi
be a PEB-spline of order
m
. Then

(i)

The generator
\varphi
has stable integer shifts.

(ii)

For all
0\leq s\leq m-1
, the tuple
\left(\varphi,\dots,\varphi^{(s)}\right)
has stable integer shifts.

Proof.

(i) Without loss of generality, we can assume that
\varphi
is supported on
[0,m]
.
By Theorem
2.1
, it suffices to show the

\ell^{\infty}
-independence of
(T_{\ell}\varphi)_{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}}
. To that
end, let
c\in\ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{Z}),c\neq 0
, and assume that

c_{\ell_{0}}\neq 0
. Since

\supp(T_{\ell}\varphi)=[\ell,\ell+m]
, the restriction of
f
to the interval
(\ell_{0}+m-1,\ell_{0}+m)
is given by

(3.64)

f=\sum\limits_{\ell=\ell_{0}}^{\ell_{0}+m-1}c_{\ell}T_{\ell}\varphi.

The restriction of
f
to
(\ell_{0}+m-1,\ell_{0}+m)
clearly belongs to the ECC-system. By Lemma
3.1
, it has at most
m-1
zeros. In particular, it does not vanish on this interval.

(ii) follows from (i), because the inequality

(3.65)

A_{p}\left\lVert c\right\rVert_{\ell^{p}}^{p}\leq\Big\lVert\,\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}}c_{\ell}T_{\ell}\varphi\,\Big\rVert_{L^{p}}^{p}\leq\sum\limits_{s=0}^{S}\Big\lVert\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}}c_{\ell}T_{\ell}\varphi^{(s)}\,\Big\rVert_{L^{p}}^{p}.

implies that the generator
\Phi=(\varphi,\varphi^{\prime},\dots\varphi^{S})

has stable integer shifts. The upper inequality in (
2.7
) are
obvious.
∎

3.4.
EB-splines

Our initial motivation to consider PEB-splines was the particular
subclass of exponential B-splines. These have already been used in time-frequency analysis
in
[
6
,
24
]
. Whereas general
PEB-splines are usually defined recursively via divided differences, EB-splines also have a direct definition by their Fourier transform as follows from
[
11
]
. Let
m\in\mathbb{N}
and let
\alpha=\left(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{m}\right)\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
.
An EB-spline
\mathcal{E}_{m,\alpha}:\mathbb{R}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
of order
m
for the
m
-tuple
\alpha
is a function of the form

(3.66)

\mathcal{E}_{m,\alpha}(x):={\prod\limits_{s=1}^{m}}^{\mbox{\raisebox{-7.0pt}{\large$*$}}}\,e^{\alpha_{s}x}\raisebox{2.0pt}{$\chi$}_{[0,1)}(x),

where
\prod^{\mbox{{\large$*$}}}
denotes the convolution product. Its Fourier transform is given by

(3.67)

\widehat{\mathcal{E}}_{m,\alpha}(\omega)=\prod\limits_{s=1}^{m}\frac{e^{\alpha_{s}-2\pi i\omega}-1}{\alpha_{s}-2\pi i\omega}.

If
\alpha_{1}=\dots=\alpha_{m}=0
, then this is a classical B-spline.
To establish the connection with the original definition of PEB-splines, we set
\gamma_{1}:=\alpha_{1}
and
\gamma_{s}=\alpha_{s}-\alpha_{s-1}
for
2\leq s\leq m
. Then the weight functions
w_{s}(x)=e^{\gamma_{s}x}
induce the EB-spline
B_{m}=\mathcal{E}_{m,\alpha}
.
For an EB-spline, the stability follows directly from the factorization (
3.67
) and Theorem
2.1
(iv).

4.
A Sampling Theorem via Uniform Bounds on the Collocation Matrices

In this section, we prove a first sampling theorem with derivatives in a shift-invariant space generated by a PEB spline. The proof technique is inspired by
[
4
]
, and its main point is to establish uniform bounds on a family of collocation matrices. The use of derivatives requires more spline theory and leads to more technicalities.

Our standing assumption is that the generator
\varphi
is a periodic exponential B-spline of order
m
, as described in Subsection
3.2
. Without loss of generality, we assume that
\varphi
is supported on
[0,m]
. We always assume that
X
is
separated, i.e.,
\inf_{x,y\in X,x\neq y}|x-y|=\delta>0
, and
\delta
is called the separation constant of
X
.

As in
[
4
]
, we partition
\mathbb{R}
into suitable intervals and analyze the sampling problem locally. Given integers
M\in\mathbb{Z}
and
L\in\mathbb{N}
, we partition
\mathbb{R}
in intervals

(4.1)

\mathbb{R}=\bigcup\limits_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}[M+kL,M+kL+L)=\bigcup\limits_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}I_{M,L}(k).

Remark
.

Let
m\in\mathbb{N}
and let
\ell_{0}\leq x_{1}<\dots<x_{N}\leq\ell_{0}+L
be sampling locations with multiplicities
\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{N}\in\{0,1\dots,m-1\}
such that

\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N}(1+\mu_{j})=L+m-1

and
\mu_{N}\leq m-2
if
x_{N}=\ell_{0}+L
. Then for all
(c_{\ell})_{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}}\in\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z})
we have

(4.2)

\Big(\sum\limits_{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}}c_{\ell}T_{\ell}\varphi\Big)^{(s_{j})}(x_{j})=\Big(\sum\limits_{\ell=\ell_{0}-m+1}^{\ell_{0}+L-1}c_{\ell}T_{\ell}\varphi\Big)^{(s_{j})}(x_{j}),\qquad 0\leq s_{j}\leq\mu_{j},\ 1\leq j\leq N.

Since
\varphi
vanishes outside of
[0,m]
, (
4.2
) is clear for all
x_{1},\dots,x_{N-1}
.
The additional assumption that
\mu_{N}\leq m-2
if
x_{N}=\ell_{0}+L
implies that the measurements at the last sampling point do not include a point of discontinuity of
T_{\ell_{0}+L}\varphi^{(m-1)}
.
Thus for all
0\leq s\leq\mu_{N}
and all
\ell\geq\ell_{0}+L
,
T_{\ell}\varphi^{(s)}(\ell_{0}+L)=0
. These observations imply (
4.2
).

To avoid unnecessary banalities, we first settle the easy case of PEB-splines of order
1
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\varphi
be a PEB-spline of order 1 supported on
[0,1)
. Assume that
X\subseteq\mathbb{R}
is separated. Then the following are equivalent:

(i)

For every
k\in\mathbb{Z}
, there exists a point
x^{k}\in X\cap[k,k+1)
.

(ii)

(X,\mu)
is a sampling set for
V^{p}(\varphi)
for all
p\in[1,\infty]
.

(iii)

(X,\mu)
is a uniqueness set for
V^{p}(\varphi)
for all
p\in[1,\infty]
.

Proof.

The implication
(ii)\Rightarrow(iii)
is trivial. A PEB-spline of the first order is the cut-off of the continuous weight function, namely
\varphi=w_{1}\cdot\raisebox{2.0pt}{$\chi$}_{[0,1)}
. Thus, if there exists a
k\in\mathbb{Z}
with
X\cap[k,k+1)=\emptyset
, then
T_{k}\varphi|_{X}=0
, although this function is clearly not the zero function. This shows
(iii)\Rightarrow(i)
.

Now assume that
(i)
holds.
The upper sampling bound is satisfied by Theorem
2.2
. Since
w
does not vanish,
\varphi
has no zeros on
[0,1)
and vanishes outside this interval. Therefore,
\varphi(x^{k}-\ell)\neq 0
holds if and only if
k=\ell
.
Since
w
is continuous and is strictly positive on
\mathbb{R}
, it attains a minimum
a>0
on the compact interval
[0,1]
. For all
p\in[1,\infty)
we obtain

(4.3)

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{x\in X}\left\lvert f(x)\right\rvert^{p}

\displaystyle\geq\sum\limits_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\left\lvert f(x^{k})\right\rvert^{p}=\sum\limits_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\left\lvert c_{k}\varphi(x^{k}-k)\right\rvert^{p}\geq a^{p}\sum\limits_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\left\lvert c_{k}\right\rvert^{p},

with an analogous estimate for
p=\infty
. This shows
(i)\Rightarrow(ii)
and completes the proof.
∎

From now on, we assume without loss of
generality that
m\geq 2
. Our first theorem looks a bit technical, but is definitely useful.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
\varphi
be a PEB-spline of order
m\in\mathbb{N}
supported on
[0,m]
. Assume
X\subseteq\mathbb{R}
with multiplicity function
\mu_{X}
satisfies the following properties:

(i)

X
is separated.

(ii)

S:=\max\limits_{x\in X}\mu_{X}(x)\leq m-1
.

(iii)

There exist integers
M,L>0

and
\varepsilon>0
, such that for every
k\in\mathbb{Z}
, there exist points
x_{1}^{k}<x_{2}^{k}<\dots<x^{k}_{N(k)}
in
X\cap I_{M,L}(k)
and nonnegative integers
\mu_{1}^{k},\,\mu_{2}^{k},\,\dots,\,\mu^{k}_{N(k)}
with the following three properties:

(4.4)

\mu^{k}_{j}\leq\mu_{X}(x^{k}_{j})\qquad\text{ for all }k\in\mathbb{Z},\,1\leq j\leq N(k),

(4.5)

\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N(k)}\left(1+\mu_{j}^{k}\right)=L+m-1\qquad\text{ for all }k\in\mathbb{Z},

(4.6)

x_{j}^{k}\in X\cap\Big(\left[\mu^{k}_{j}-m+\varepsilon,-\varepsilon\right]+M+kL+1+\sum\limits_{n=1}^{j-1}\left(1+\mu^{k}_{n}\right)\Big).

Then
(X,\mu_{X})
is a sampling set for
V^{p}(\varphi)
for all
p\in[1,\infty]
.

Remark
.

The conditions (
4.4
) - (
4.6
)
are compatible with (
4.2
) and in some sense are necessary. Since the restriction of
f
to an interval
I_{M,L}(k)
belongs to an
(L+m-1)
-dimensional space, we need at least
L+m-1
samples, whence (
4.5
). If more information is available, we either remove points or the highest derivatives, which is
(ii)
and (
4.5
). As we intend to use the invertibility of the collocation matrix (Corollary
3.6
), the retained information must be chosen diligently and involves a Schoenberg-Whitney condition of type (
4.6
), which says that the support of each shift must contain a sampling point (see Figure
1
).

,

(dot-dashed), the second point is in the support of the next three shifts of

(dashed), and the last point - in the support of the last shift of

(solid).

Proof.

Fix
p\in[1,\infty)
and let

(4.7)

f=\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}}c_{\ell}T_{\ell}\varphi\in V^{p}(\varphi).

Since
\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{p}\asymp\left\lVert c\right\rVert_{p}
for
f\in V^{p}(\varphi)
by Lemma
3.7
, it suffices to show the inequality

(4.8)

\sum\limits_{x\in X}\sum\limits_{s=0}^{\mu_{X}(x)}\left\lvert f^{(s)}(x)\right\rvert^{p}\asymp\left\lVert c\right\rVert_{\ell^{p}}^{p}.

For given
M,\,L\in\mathbb{N}
every interval
I_{M,L}(k)=[M+kL,M+(k+1)L)
will be investigated separately. The aim is to use Corollary
3.6
.

Step 1: The upper bound.

Since
\varphi^{(s)}
,
0\leq s\leq m-1
, are piecewise continuous, compactly supported functions with finitely many jump discontinuities, Theorem
2.2
(ii) applies and yields the upper bound for the sampling inequality.

Step 2: A class of collocation matrices with uniform bounds.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be the set

(4.9)

\mathcal{M}\coloneqq\Big\{\mu\in\{0,\dots,S\}^{N}:N\in\mathbb{N},\ \sum\limits_{j=1}^{N}\left(1+\mu_{j}\right)=L+m-1\Big\}.

If
\mu\in\mathcal{M}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{N}
, then
N\leq L+m-1
. Therefore,

(4.10)

\mathcal{M}\subseteq\bigcup\limits_{N=1}^{L+m-1}\left\{0,\dots,S\right\}^{N}\,,

in particular, the set
\mathcal{M}
is finite. Let
\Omega_{\mu}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N}
for
\mu\in\mathcal{M}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{N}
be the set of vectors
z=(z_{1},z_{2},\dots,z_{N})^{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
with

(4.11)

\min\limits_{i\neq j}\left\lvert z_{i}-z_{j}\right\rvert\geq\delta

and

(4.12)

\begin{split}z_{j}&\in[0,L]\cap\ \Big(\left[\mu_{j}-m+\varepsilon,-\varepsilon\right]\ +1+\sum\limits_{n=1}^{j-1}\left(1+\mu_{n}\right)\Big)\\
&=\Big[\max\Big\{0,\mu_{j}-m+1+\varepsilon+\sum\limits_{n=1}^{j-1}\left(1+\mu_{n}\right)\Big\}\,,\,\min\Big\{L,1-\varepsilon+\sum\limits_{n=1}^{j-1}\left(1+\mu_{n}\right)\Big\}\Big].\end{split}

In other words,
\Omega_{\mu}
contains all point configurations in
[0,L]
that satisfy Condition (
4.6
). The set is bounded and closed, hence compact in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
. For each
z\in\Omega_{\mu}
we consider the collocation matrix

(4.13)

\mathbf{M}(z,\mu,-m+1)\in\mathbb{R}^{(L+m-1)\times(L+m-1)}

as defined in the proof of Corollary
3.6

in (
3.62
) with parameters
D=L+m-1
and
\ell_{0}=-m+1
.
By Corollary
3.6
and the positions of the points (cf. (
4.12
)), the matrix is invertible. In addition,
z\mapsto\mathbf{M}(z,\mu,-m+1)
is continuous on the compact set
\Omega_{\mu}
, so the matrices are bounded from above and below uniformly in
\omega\in\Omega_{\mu}
.
To see this, a trivial upper bound is a multiple of the maximum of the entries of
\mathbf{M}(z,\mu,-m+1)
. For the lower bound, we note that
z\mapsto\mathbf{M}(z,\mu,-m+1)^{-1}
is continuous by Cramer’s rule, thus we can again obtain a uniform upper bound
0<a_{p}(\mu)^{-1}
for
\mathbf{M}(z,\mu,-m+1)^{-1}
. Then
a_{p}(\mu)
is a strictly positive uniform lower bound of
\mathbf{M}(z,\mu,-m+1)
, i.e., for all
c\in\mathbb{R}^{L+m-1}

(4.14)

\left\lVert\mathbf{M}(z,\mu,-m+1)\cdot c\right\rVert_{p}\geq a_{p}(\mu)\left\lVert c\right\rVert_{p}.

Since
\mathcal{M}
is finite, we can set

(4.15)

A_{p}\coloneqq\min\{a_{p}(\mu):\mu\in\mathcal{M}\}>0.

Step 3: The lower estimate.

Let
x^{k}=(x_{1}^{k},\dots,x_{N(k)}^{k})
be the points in
X\cap I_{M,L}(k)
that satisfy Condition
(iii)
, i.e., they satisfy (
4.4
), (
4.5
), and (
4.6
). We set
\mu^{k}=\left(\mu_{1}^{k},\dots,\mu^{k}_{N(k)}\right)\in\mathcal{M}
and
z_{j}^{k}=x_{j}^{k}-(M+kL)
. Then
z^{k}\in\Omega_{\mu^{k}}
.
Let
\textbf{F}(x^{k})
be the complete list of evaluations

(4.16)

\displaystyle\textbf{F}(x^{k})

\displaystyle:=\left(f(x^{k}_{1}),\dots,f^{(\mu^{k}_{1})}(x^{k}_{1}),\dots,f(x_{N(k)}^{k}),\dots,f^{(\mu^{k}_{N(k)})}(x_{N(k)}^{k})\right)\in\mathbb{C}^{L+m-1}.

For
p\in[1,\infty)
, we estimate from below the sum

(4.17)

\sum\limits_{x\in X}\sum\limits_{s=0}^{\mu_{X}(x)}\left\lvert f^{(s)}(x)\right\rvert^{p}\geq\sum\limits_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N(k)}\sum\limits_{s=0}^{\mu_{j}^{k}}\left\lvert f^{(s)}(x_{j}^{k})\right\rvert^{p}=\sum\limits_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\left\lVert\textbf{F}(x^{k})\right\rVert_{p}^{p}.

We now consider the restriction of
f
to the interval
I_{M,L}(k)
and shift it to
[0,L)
. The resulting function is

(4.18)

T_{-(M+kL)}\big(f\big|_{I_{M,L}(k)}\big)=T_{-(M+kL)}\,\sum\limits_{\ell=M+kL-m+1}^{M+kL+L-1}c_{\ell}T_{\ell}\varphi=\sum\limits_{\ell=-m+1}^{L-1}c_{\ell+M+kL}T_{\ell}\varphi.

We denote by
\mathfrak{c}_{k}=\left(c_{M+kL-m+1},\dots,c_{M+kL+L-1}\right)^{t}
the extracted coefficients which are active on
I_{M,L}(k)
.
By Corollary
3.6
, specifically (
3.62
), the measurements
\textbf{F}(x^{k})
are given by

(4.19)

\displaystyle\textbf{F}(x^{k})=\mathbf{M}(z^{k},\mu^{k},-m+1)\cdot\mathfrak{c}_{k}.

From this follows

(4.20)

\begin{split}\sum\limits_{x\in X}\sum\limits_{s=0}^{\mu_{X}(x)}\left\lvert f^{(s)}(x)\right\rvert^{p}&\geq\sum\limits_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\left\lVert\mathbf{M}(z^{k},\mu^{k},-m+1)\cdot\mathfrak{c}_{k}\right\rVert_{p}^{p}\geq A_{p}^{p}\,\sum\limits_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\left\lVert\mathfrak{c}_{k}\right\rVert_{p}^{p}\geq A_{p}^{p}\,\left\lVert c\right\rVert_{p}^{p}\,.\end{split}

In the last inequality, we have used the fact that every
\ell\in\mathbb{Z}
is
contained in some interval
M+kL-m+1\leq\ell\leq M+kL+L-1.

The estimate for
p=\infty
is analogous, and we are done.
∎

5.
Weak limits

A particular case to consider is sampling with a constant multiplicity
S
for some integer
0\leq S\leq m-1
.
Since every point comes with
S+1
data, one expects that a suitably modified gap condition might be sufficient for sampling with derivatives. Based on Corollary
4.3
, it is natural to conjecture that

(5.1)

\frac{\mathfrak{mg}(X)}{1+S}<1

is sufficient for sampling. This is indeed correct, but it does not follow from Theorem
4.2
. One can show that there are sets that satisfy (
5.1
) but violate the conditions (
4.4
), (
4.5
), and (
4.6
). These examples will be addressed in Section
7
.

To study maximal gap conditions and achieve an optimal result, we will use the technique of weak limits. These go back to Beurling
[
10
]
and have been introduced for the investigation of shift-invariant spaces in
[
20
,
21
]
.

We recall Beurling’s notion of a weak limit of a sequence of sets. A sequence
\left(X_{n}\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of subsets of
\mathbb{R}

converges weakly
to a set
Y\subseteq\mathbb{R}
, denoted by
X_{n}\overset{w}{\longrightarrow}Y
, if for every open bounded interval
(a,b)
and every
\varepsilon>0
, there exists an
n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that for all
n\geq n_{0}
,

(5.2)

X_{n}\cap(a,b)\subseteq Y+(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)\qquad\text{and}\qquad Y\cap(a,b)\subseteq X_{n}+(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon).

Given a tuple of sets
\overrightarrow{X}=\left(X^{0},\dots,X^{S}\right)
, a tuple
\overrightarrow{Y}=\left(Y^{0},\dots,Y^{S}\right)
is called a
weak limit of integer translates
of
\overrightarrow{X}
if there exists a sequence
(k_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subseteq\mathbb{Z}
such that
X^{s}-k_{n}\overset{w}{\longrightarrow}Y^{s}
for all
0\leq s\leq S
. An important part of the definition is the fact that the sequence of translates is the same for all
Y^{s}
.
The set of weak limits of integer translates of
X
is denoted by
W_{\mathbb{Z}}(\overrightarrow{X})
.
Weak limits enter sampling theory through the following characterization of sampling sets
[
21
, Thm. 2.1]
.

Theorem 5.1
.

Assume that
\Phi=\left(\Phi^{0},\dots,\Phi^{S}\right)
consists of continuous functions with a minimal decay property
|\Phi^{s}(x)|\leq C(1+|x|)^{-1-\varepsilon}
and
\Phi
has stable integer shifts. Let
\overrightarrow{X}=\left(X^{0},\dots,X^{S}\right)
be a tuple of separated sets. Then the following are equivalent.

(i)

\overrightarrow{X}
is a sampling set for
V^{p}(\Phi)
for some
p\in[1,\infty]
.

(ii)

\overrightarrow{X}
is a sampling set for
V^{p}(\Phi)
for all
p\in[1,\infty]
.

(iii)

Every weak limit
\overrightarrow{Y}\in W_{\mathbb{Z}}(\overrightarrow{X})
is a sampling set for
V^{\infty}(\Phi)
.

(iv)

Every weak limit
\overrightarrow{Y}\in W_{\mathbb{Z}}(\overrightarrow{X})
is a uniqueness set for
V^{\infty}(\Phi)
.

When sampling on
(X,\mu_{X})
, this theorem is applied to the generator
\Phi=(\varphi,\varphi^{\prime},\dots,\varphi^{(S)})
and the sets

(5.3)

X^{s}:=\{x\in X:\mu_{X}(x)\geq s\},\qquad 0\leq s\leq\max\left\{\mu_{X}(x):x\in X\right\}.

In this case,
\overrightarrow{X}-k_{n}
can be identified with
(X,\mu_{X})-k_{n}\coloneqq(X-k_{n},\mu_{X-k_{n}})
, where

(5.4)

\mu_{X-k_{n}}(x-k_{n})=\mu_{X}(x),\qquad x\in X.

For separated sets, we can use an alternative characterization of weak convergence (cf.
[
19
, Lem. 4.4.]
,
[
21
, Prop. 3.2.]
).

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
(X,\mu_{X})
be a separated set with multiplicity function
\mu_{X}:X\to\{0,\dots,S\}
,
(Y,\mu_{Y})
be a set with multiplicity function
\mu_{Y}
, and
\left(k_{n}\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subseteq\mathbb{Z}
a sequence of integers. Then
X^{s}-k_{n}\overset{w}{\longrightarrow}Y^{s}
as
n\to\infty
for all

0\leq s\leq S
, if and only if

(5.5)

\sum\limits_{x\in X}\left(1+\mu_{X}(x)\right)\delta_{x-k_{n}}\longrightarrow\sum\limits_{y\in Y}\left(1+\mu_{Y}(y)\right)\delta_{y},\qquad\text{ as }n\longrightarrow\infty,

in the
(C_{c}^{*},C_{c})
-topology (where
C_{c}
denotes the class of continuous functions with compact support).

For splines, an additional difficulty arises that is not formally covered by Theorem
5.1
. The last derivative
\varphi^{(m-1)}
is only piecewise continuous with finitely many jump discontinuities. In this case, we have to impose an additional condition on the
m
-th sampling set. The modified version of Theorem

goes as follows:

Corollary 5.3

Let
\varphi\in C^{S-1}(\mathbb{R})
be a compactly supported, piecewise

-function with finitely many jump discontinuities

\varphi
has stable integer translates. Let

be a separated set with multiplicity function

\mathrm{dist}\big(\{x\in X:\mu_{X}(x)=S\}\,,\mathcal{J}+\mathbb{Z}\big)>0.

Then the following statements are equivalent:

(i)

(X,\mu_{X})
is a sampling set for

for some

(ii)

(X,\mu_{X})
is a sampling set for

for all

(iii)

Remark
.

The equivalence of

When we label a separated set

as a strictly increasing sequence, we can define the weighted maximum gap as

\displaystyle\mathfrak{mg}(X,\mu_{X})

\displaystyle\coloneqq\max\bigg\{\sup\limits_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{x_{j+1}-x_{j}}{1+\mu_{X}(x_{j})},\,\sup\limits_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{x_{j+1}-x_{j}}{1+\mu_{X}(x_{j+1})}\bigg\}

\displaystyle=\sup\limits_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\,\frac{x_{j+1}-x_{j}}{1+\min\{\mu_{X}(x_{j}),\mu_{X}(x_{j+1})\}}\,.

We will show that

is sufficient for a set to be a sampling set.

To apply Corollary

6.
Sampling via maximal gap and weak limits

In this section, we will prove two sampling theorems that use gap conditions for the sampling set.
These conditions are easier to check than the technical conditions of Theorem

We first relate the weighted maximum gap to the Schoenberg-Whitney conditions (

7.
Discussion of the methods

Comparison.
Both theorems (Theorems
4.2
and
6.3
) are based on similar ideas. We choose a suitable covering of
\mathbb{R}
and
study sampling locally on each interval of the partition by means of the active collocation matrix.

In Theorem
4.2
we control the distance of a sampling point to the
boundary of the support of
\varphi
as a security margin. This parameter guarantees uniform bounds for the inversion of the local collocation matrices (Step 2 in the proof).

In Theorem
6.3
, we omit that condition, and the Schoenberg-Whitney conditions imply only the invertibility of the local collocation matrices. As a consequence, we obtain only uniqueness sets. In this case, the theory of weak limits and subtle criteria for sampling sets help to derive stability estimates.

Both main theorems are optimal in some sense, but their assumptions are not comparable. To see this, let us consider a few simple examples. In all examples, the generator
\varphi
is a PEB-spline of order
m
with support in
[0,m]
.

Example 7.1
.

Let
X=\alpha\mathbb{Z}
and consider
\mu_{X}(\alpha j)=\tfrac{1}{2}(1+(-1)^{j})
so that
\mu_{X}(\alpha j)=1
for even
j
and
\mu_{X}(\alpha j)=0
for odd
j
. The weighted maximum gap is then

\mathfrak{mg}(X,\mu_{X})=\alpha\,.
Consequently, by Theorem
6.3

\alpha<1
implies the sampling inequality

\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}|f(\alpha j)|^{p}+\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}|f^{\prime}(2\alpha j)|^{p}\asymp\|f\|_{p}^{p}\qquad\text{ for all }f\in V^{p}(\varphi)\,.

However, in an interval of length
K
there are
K/(2\alpha)
samples with multiplicity
0
and
K/(2\alpha)
samples with multiplicity
1
(up to a bounded error term), so the weighted Beurling density of
(X,\mu_{X})
is

D^{-}(X,\mu_{X})=\frac{3}{2\alpha}>\frac{3}{2}\,,

i.e., the maximum gap does not deliver an optimal estimate.

By contrast, by Theorem
4.4
, for every
\varepsilon>0
, using the conditions of Theorem
4.2
, we can produce a set
(X,\mu_{X})
with weighted Beurling density

that is sampling. Hereby,
\mu_{X}
can be chosen to be an alternating function as above, and the explicit construction in the proof of Theorem
4.4
provides a subset of a lattice.

Example 7.2
.

We provide an example of a set uniformly bounded away from
\mathbb{Z}
with a constant multiplicity function
\mu\equiv S-1>0
and maximum gap
\mathfrak{mg}(X)=S\,\mathfrak{mg}(X,\mu_{X})<S

While the combinatorial theorem relied heavily on

, Theorem

asks for a

\begin{split}&[M+kL,M+kL+L)\cap[0-m+M+kL+1+\varepsilon,M+kL+1-\varepsilon)\\

Note that

, as

Implications for Gabor systems

In the final section, we exploit the connection between sampling in shift-invariant spaces and Gabor frames and improve a result in
[
24
]
.

We denote with
\pi(x,\omega)
,
(x,\omega)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}

\pi(x,\omega)f(t)=e^{2\pi i\omega t}f(t-x)
. In terms of stable expansions, given a
window function

\varphi
and a discrete set
\Lambda\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{2}
, one asks when a
Gabor system

\mathcal{G}(\varphi,\Lambda)
, defined as

(8.1)

\mathcal{G}(g,\Lambda)=\{\pi(\lambda)\varphi:\lambda\in\Lambda\},

is a
(Gabor) frame
, i.e., it satisfies the frame inequality

(8.2)

A\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\leq\sum\limits_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\left\lvert\left\langle f,\pi(\lambda)\varphi\right\rangle\right\rvert^{2}\leq B\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{2}^{2},\qquad f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}),

with frame bounds
0<A\leq B<\infty
independent of
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
. From the general frame theory, the inequality implies a reconstruction formula

(8.3)

f=\sum\limits_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\langle f,\pi(\lambda)\varphi\rangle\,\psi_{\lambda}=\sum\limits_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\langle f,\psi_{\lambda}\rangle\,\pi(\lambda)\varphi,\qquad f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})

with

\left(\langle f,\pi(\lambda)\varphi\rangle\right)_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
,
\left(\langle f,\psi_{\lambda}\rangle\right)_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\in\ell^{2}(\Lambda)
and
\psi_{\lambda}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
for all
\lambda\in\Lambda
.
Therefore, given
\varphi
, we are interested in determining those sets
\Lambda\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{2}
whose Gabor system
\mathcal{G}(\varphi,\Lambda)
is a frame.

While there is no manageable tool available for arbitrary point configurations, semi-regular sets of type
\Lambda=X\times\mathbb{Z}
can be treated by the connection between sampling in shift-invariant spaces and Gabor frames
[
20
, Thm. 3.1, Thm. 3.3]
.

Abstract
. We prove Lipschitz continuity results for solutions to a class of obstacle problems under standard growth conditions of p − p- type, p ≥ 2 p\geq 2 . The main novelty is the use of a linearization technique going back to [ 28 ] in order to interpret our constrained minimizer as a solution to a nonlinear elliptic equation, with a bounded right hand side. This lead us to start a Moser iteration scheme which provides the L ∞ L^{\infty} bound for the gradient. The application of a recent higher differentiability result [ 24 ] allows us to simplify the procedure of the identification of the Radon measure in the linearization technique employed in [ 32 ] . To our knowdledge, this is the first result for non-automonous functionals with standard growth conditions in the direction of the Lipschitz regularity.

1.
Introduction

The aim of this paper is the study of the Lipschitz continuity of the solutions
u\in W^{1,p}(\Omega)
to variational obstacle problems of the form

\min\left\{\int_{\Omega}f(x,Dw):w\in\mathcal{K}_{\psi,\Psi}(\Omega)\right\}.

(1.1)

The function
\psi:\,\Omega\rightarrow[-\infty,+\infty)
, called
obstacle
, belongs to the Sobolev space
W^{1,p}(\Omega)
and the class
\mathcal{K}_{\psi,\Psi}(\Omega)
is defined as follows, for a given
\Psi\in W^{1,p}(\overline{\Omega})
providing the boundary value

\mathcal{K}_{\psi,\Psi}(\Omega):=\left\{w\in W^{1,p}(\Omega):w(x)\geq\psi(x)\,\,\textnormal{a.e. in $\Omega$ and }\,w|_{\partial\Omega}=\Psi|_{\partial\Omega}\right\}.

(1.2)

In the following, we shall assume that
\mathcal{K}_{\psi,\Psi}(\Omega)
is not empty.

The study of the regularity theory for obstacle problems is a classical topic in Calculus of Variations and Partial Differential Equations. The first results concerning obstacle problems date back to the sixties to the pioneering work by G. Stampacchia
[
50
]
and G. Fichera
[
26
]
. The well known fact that solutions to the obstacle problem cannot be of class
\mathcal{C}^{2}
independently of the regularity of the obstacle led to the origin of the concept of
weak solution
and to the theory of
variational inequalities
, after the fundamental work of J.L. Lions and G. Stampacchia
[
39
]
. These problems can generally be solved by means of nowadays classical methods of functional analysis; in this respect the question to establish conditions so that weak solutions can be actually classical ones is of crucial importance (see
[
8
]
). For more details we refer to the following monographs
[
1
]
,
[
10
]
,
[
27
]
,
[
37
]
,
[

.

The regularity of solutions to the obstacle problems is influenced by the one of the obstacle: in the linear case, for instance, obstacle and solutions share the same regularity (
[
6
]
,
[

]
,
[
37
]
), while in the nonlinear setting the situation is more involved. This generated along the years an intense research activity aimed to establish the regularity of the solution compared with the regularity assumed for the obstacle. In particular many results concern the Hölder continuity of solutions to the obstacle problem when the obstacle itself is Hölder continuous: see for instance

As far as we know, such analysis has not been carried out in the direction of obtaining Lipschitz regularity results. The reason may be found in the difficulty of choosing the proper test function, in order to start the iteration process, necessary to get the desired regularity; this nowadays classical technique (
[
45
]
) has been adapted along the years in several situations, also in the case of non standard growth conditions, see also for instance
[

In
[
32
]
Fuchs and Mingione considered constrained local minimizers of elliptic variational integrals, assuming for the Lagrangian
f
nearly linear growth. In this respect, they were able to prove several regularity results; the one we are mainly interested in is the Lipschitz continuity of these constrained minimizers. This step has been achived through a linearization technique, going back to
[
28
]
, later refined in
[
16
]
, see also
[
29
]
,
[
30
]
,
[
31
]
.

The main idea of the linearization approach is the following: the constrained minimizer is interpreted as a solution to an elliptic equation with a bounded right hand side, which is obtained after the identification of a suitable Radon measure. At this point the main obstruction is overcome, and higher regularity for the solution to the obstacle problem can be shown.

We would like to remark that in
[
32
]
a lot of effort has been employed to identify the Radon measure and the authors explicitly say that this procedure could be significantly simplified if we would have a priori proved higher differentiability for local minimizers of the obstacle problem.

However this is exactly the case for our specific situation: in a very recent paper
[
24
]
Eleuteri and Passarelli di Napoli were able to establish the higher differentiability of integer and fractional order of the solutions to a class of obstacle problems (involving
p-
harmonic operators) assuming that the gradient of the obstacle possesses an extra (integer or fractional) differentiability property.
We will make use of this result to simplify the procedure outlined in
[
32
]
and obtain the desired result.

The paper is organized as follows: Section
2
contains some notations and the statement of the main result; Section
3
contains the detailed construction of the linearization procedure while the conclusion comes with Section
4
where the a priori estimate is presented.

We would like to mention that, in order to let the linearization technique be the main focus of our work, we assumed standard growth conditions for the lagrangians of our integral functionals and
L^{\infty}
dependence with respect to the
x-
variable. In a similar way, we assume on the gradient of the obstacle
W^{1,\infty}
regularity. We expect that future improvements can be obtained both in the direction of considering non-standard growth conditions (see for instance the recent papers
[
23
]
,
[
13
]
,
[
15
]
), for which a counterpart of
[
24
]
would be needed in the nonstandard setting, see
[
33
]
) and in the direction of weakening the assumptions on the obstacle and on the partial map
x\mapsto D_{\xi}f(x,\xi)
. We plan to continue our research in these directions.

2.
Notations and statement of the main result

For a domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
n\geq 2
, and functions
u:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R},
we consider the variational integral

\mathcal{F}(u):=\int_{\Omega}f(x,Du)\,dx,

(2.1)

where
f:\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow[0,+\infty)
is a Carathéodory function which is convex and of class
\mathcal{C}^{2}
with respect to the second variable. Moreover, we suppose that there exist two positive constants
\lambda,\Lambda
such that, for all
\mu,\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
\mu=\mu_{i},

\xi=\xi_{i}
,
i=1,2,\dots,n
, a.e. in
\Omega
and for
p\geq 2

\displaystyle\lambda\,(1+|\xi|^{2})^{\frac{p-2}{2}}|\mu|^{2}\leq\sum_{i,j}f_{\xi_{i}\xi_{j}}(x,\xi)\mu_{i}\mu_{j},

(2.2)

\displaystyle|f_{\xi_{i}\xi_{j}}(x,\xi)|\leq\,\Lambda\,(1+|\xi|^{2})^{\frac{p-2}{2}},

(2.3)

\displaystyle\,\,|f_{\xi x}(x,\xi)|\leq\,\Lambda\,(1+|\xi|^{2})^{\frac{p-1}{2}}.

(2.4)

Throughout the paper we will denote by
B_{\rho}
and
B_{R}
balls of radii respectively
\rho
and
R
(with
\rho<R
) compactly contained in
\Omega
and with the same center, let us say
x_{0}\in\Omega
.

Our main result reads as follows

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
u\in\mathcal{K}_{\psi,\Psi}(\Omega)
be a solution to the obstacle problem (
1.1
), under the assumptions (
2.2
)-(
2.4
).
If
\psi\in W^{2,\infty}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)
, then
u\in W^{1,\infty}_{\textnormal{loc}}(\Omega)
and the following estimate

\|(1+|Du|^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{\rho})}\leq\,\frac{C}{(R-\rho)^{\beta}}\,\left(\int_{B_{R}}(1+|Du|^{2})^{\frac{p}{2}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

(2.5)

holds for every
0<\rho<R
and with positive constants
C
and
\beta
depending on
n,p,\lambda,\Lambda,R,\rho
and the local bounds for
\|D\psi\|_{W^{1,\infty}}
.

Remark 2.2
.

Due to the local nature of our main result, we do not assume further regularity on the boundary datum
\Psi
.

3.
Proof of Theorem
2.1
: the linearization procedure

Consider a smooth function
h_{\varepsilon}:(0,\infty)\to[0,1]
such that
h^{\prime}(s)\leq 0
for all
s\in(0,\infty)
and

h(s)=\begin{cases}1&\quad\text{for}\quad s\leq\varepsilon,\\
0&\quad\text{for}\quad s\geq 2\varepsilon.\\
\end{cases}

We remark that
u\in W^{1,p}(\Omega)
is a solution to the obstacle problem in
\mathcal{K}_{\psi,\Psi}(\Omega)
if and only if
u\in\mathcal{K}_{\psi,\Psi}
solves the following variational inequality

\int_{\Omega}D_{\xi}f(x,Du)\cdot D(\varphi-u)\,dx\geq 0,

(3.1)

for all
\varphi\in\mathcal{K}_{\psi,\Psi}
. In particular the function

\varphi=u+t\cdot\eta\cdot h_{\varepsilon}(u-\psi)

with
\eta\in C_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
,
\eta\geq 0
and
0<t<<1
, is such that
\varphi\in\mathcal{K}_{\psi,\Psi}
. Using
\varphi
as test function, in the variational inequality (
3.1
) gives

\int_{\Omega}D_{\xi}f(x,Du)\cdot D(\eta h_{\varepsilon}(u-\psi))\,dx\geq 0\qquad\forall\,\eta\in C_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\,.

Since

\eta\mapsto L(\eta)=\int_{\Omega}D_{\xi}f(x,Du)\cdot D(\eta h_{\varepsilon}(u-\psi))\,dx

is a bounded positive linear functional, by the Riesz representation theorem there exists a nonnegative measure
\lambda_{\varepsilon}
such that

\int_{\Omega}D_{\xi}f(x,Du)\cdot D(\eta h_{\varepsilon}(u-\psi))\,dx=\int_{\Omega}\eta d\lambda_{\varepsilon}\qquad\forall\,\eta\in C_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\,.

We show that the measure
\lambda_{\varepsilon}
is independent from
\varepsilon
: indeed let
\varepsilon<\varepsilon^{\prime}
. The variation

\varphi_{+}=u+t\cdot\eta\cdot(h_{\varepsilon}-h_{\varepsilon^{\prime}})(u-\psi)

is admissibile for all
\eta\in C_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
and
0<t<<1
. This gives

\int_{\Omega}D_{\xi}f(x,Du)\cdot D(\eta(h_{\varepsilon}-h_{\varepsilon^{\prime}})(u-\psi))\,dx\geq 0\qquad\forall\,\eta\in C_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\,.

It’s easy to see that
(h_{\varepsilon}-h_{\varepsilon^{\prime}})(s)
is supported on
[\varepsilon,2\varepsilon^{\prime}]
, which implies

\int_{\{\varepsilon<u-\psi<2\varepsilon^{\prime}\}}D_{\xi}f(x,Du)\cdot D(\eta(h_{\varepsilon}-h_{\varepsilon^{\prime}})(u-\psi))\,dx\geq 0\qquad\forall\,\eta\in C_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\,.

Then, for
t<\frac{\varepsilon}{2},
also the variation

\varphi_{-}=u-t\cdot\eta\cdot(h_{\varepsilon}-h_{\varepsilon^{\prime}})(u-\psi)

is an admissibile test function; by the same argument we get

\int_{\{\varepsilon<u-\psi<2\varepsilon^{\prime}\}}D_{\xi}f(x,Du)\cdot D(\eta(h_{\varepsilon}-h_{\varepsilon^{\prime}})(u-\psi))\,dx\leq 0\qquad\forall\,\eta\in C_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\,;

together with the previous result, this implies

\int_{\Omega}D_{\xi}f(x,Du)\cdot D(\eta(h_{\varepsilon}-h_{\varepsilon^{\prime}})(u-\psi))\,dx=0\qquad\forall\,\eta\in C_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\,,

that is equivalent to

\int_{\Omega}\eta d\lambda_{\varepsilon}=\int_{\Omega}\eta d\lambda_{\varepsilon^{\prime}}\qquad\forall\,\eta\in C_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\,.

(3.2)

Now we can obtain equality between measures by a standard approximation argument, let
\phi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
be a smooth kernel with
\phi\geq 0
and
\int_{\Omega}\phi\,dx=1
, consider the corresponding family of mollifiers
(\phi_{s})_{s>0}
and any compact set
K
such that
K\subset\subset\Omega
. For every
s>0
such that
s<\text{dist}(K,\Omega)
we take in equation (
3.2
)

\eta=\chi_{K}*\phi_{s}

letting
s\searrow 0
gives

\int_{K}d\lambda_{\varepsilon}=\int_{K}d\lambda_{\varepsilon^{\prime}}\,,

in conclusion
\lambda_{\varepsilon}=\lambda_{\varepsilon^{\prime}}
.

At this point we can rewrite our representation equation without the
\varepsilon
dependence on the measure

\int_{\Omega}D_{\xi}f(x,Du)\cdot D(\eta h_{\varepsilon}(u-\psi))\,dx=\int_{\Omega}\eta\,d\lambda\qquad\forall\,\eta\in C_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\,.

By Theorem 2.1 in
[
24
]
, that holds in particular under the assumptions (
2.2
)-(
2.4
), we have that

V_{p}(Du):=(1+|Du|^{2})^{\frac{p-2}{2}}Du\in W^{1,2}_{\rm loc}(\Omega),

therefore we can integrate by parts and get

\int_{\Omega}-\text{div}(D_{\xi}f(x,Du))\eta h_{\varepsilon}(u-\psi)\,dx=\int_{\Omega}\eta\,d\lambda\qquad\forall\,\eta\in C_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\,.

Now, in order to identify the measure
\lambda
, we may pass to the limit as
\varepsilon\searrow 0

\int_{\Omega}-\text{div}(D_{\xi}f(x,Du))\chi_{\left[u=\psi\right]}\eta\,dx=\int_{\Omega}\eta\,d\lambda\qquad\forall\,\eta\in C_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\,.

(3.3)

Let us introduce

g:=\text{div}(D_{\xi}f(x,Du))\chi_{\left[u=\psi\right]};

(3.4)

then the equation above takes the form:

-\int_{\Omega}g\eta\,dx=\int_{\Omega}\eta\,d\lambda\qquad\forall\,\eta\in C_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\,.

Now observing that

\int_{\Omega}D_{\xi}f(x,Du)\cdot D(\eta(1-h_{\varepsilon})(u-\psi))\,dx=0\qquad\forall\,\eta\in C_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\,

since
(1-h_{\varepsilon})(s)
has support
[\varepsilon,+\infty)
, combining our results we get

\int_{\Omega}D_{\xi}f(x,Du)\cdot D\eta\,dx=-\int_{\Omega}g\eta\,dx\qquad\forall\,\eta\in C_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\,.

(3.5)

We are left to obtain an
L^{\infty}
estimate for
g
: since
Du=D\psi
a.e. on the contact set, by (2.3) and (2.4) and the assumption
D\psi\in W^{1,\infty}_{\rm loc}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})
, we have

\begin{split}|g|&=\left|\text{div}(D_{\xi}f(x,Du))\chi_{\left[u=\psi\right]}\right|=\left|\text{div}(D_{\xi}f(x,D\psi))\right|\\
&\leq\sum_{k=1}^{n}|f_{\xi_{k}x_{k}}(x,D\psi)|+\sum_{k,i=1}^{n}|f_{\xi_{k}\xi_{i}}(x,D\psi)\psi_{x_{k}x_{i}}|\\
&\leq\Lambda(1+|D\psi|^{2})^{\frac{p-1}{2}}+\Lambda(1+|D\psi|^{2})^{\frac{p-2}{2}}|D^{2}\psi|\,\end{split}

that is
g\in L^{\infty}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)
.

\displaystyle C(\Lambda)\int_{\Omega}\eta|D\eta||Du|\,(1+|Du|^{2})^{\gamma+\frac{p-1}{2}}\,dx

\displaystyle C\int_{\Omega}(\eta^{2}+|D\eta|^{2})(1+|Du|^{2})^{\gamma+\frac{p}{2}},

and also:

\displaystyle|{I}_{5}|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\Omega}\eta^{2}(1+|Du|^{2})^{\gamma}\sum_{i,s=1}^{n}f_{\xi_{i}x_{s}}(x,Du)u_{x_{s}x_{i}}\,dx\right|

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{gtx}}{\leq}

\displaystyle\Lambda\,\int_{\Omega}\eta^{2}(1+|Du|^{2})^{\gamma+\frac{p-1}{2}}\,\sum_{i,s=1}^{n}u_{x_{s}x_{i}}\,dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Lambda\,\int_{\Omega}\eta^{2}(1+|Du|^{2})^{\gamma+\frac{p-1}{2}}|D^{2}u|\,dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\int_{\Omega}\eta^{2}(1+|Du|^{2})^{\frac{p-2}{2}+\gamma}|D^{2}u|^{2}\,dx+C\,\int_{\Omega}\eta^{2}(1+|Du|^{2})^{\frac{p}{2}+\gamma}\,dx.

Finally, by similar arguments, the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, (
2.3
)
and
|D(|Du|)|\leq|D^{2}u|
give

\displaystyle|{I}_{6}|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\gamma\,\int_{\Omega}\sum_{i,s=1}^{n}f_{\xi_{i}x_{s}}(x,Du)\eta^{2}(1+|Du|^{2})^{\gamma-1}|Du|D_{x_{i}}(|Du|)u_{x_{s}}\,dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\gamma\,\int_{\Omega}\eta^{2}(1+|Du|^{2})^{\gamma-1}|Du|\sum_{i,s=1}^{n}f_{\xi_{i}x_{s}}(x,Du)D_{x_{i}}(|Du|)u_{x_{s}}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\,\gamma\,\int_{\Omega}\eta^{2}(1+|Du|^{2})^{\gamma-\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{i,s=1}^{n}f_{\xi_{i}x_{s}}(x,Du)D_{x_{i}}(|Du|)u_{x_{s}}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\,\gamma\,\Lambda\,\int_{\Omega}\eta^{2}(1+|Du|^{2})^{\gamma-\frac{1}{2}+\frac{p-1}{2}}\sum_{i,s=1}^{n}D_{x_{i}}(|Du|)u_{x_{s}}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\,n\,\gamma\,\Lambda\,\int_{\Omega}\eta^{2}(1+|Du|^{2})^{\gamma-\frac{1}{2}+\frac{p-1}{2}}|D(|Du|)||Du|\,dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\,n\,\gamma\,\Lambda\,\int_{\Omega}\,\eta^{2}(1+|Du|^{2})^{\gamma+\frac{p-1}{2}}|D^{2}u|\,dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\int_{\Omega}\eta^{2}|D^{2}u|^{2}(1+|Du|^{2})^{\frac{p-2}{2}+\gamma}\,dx+C\gamma^{2}\,\int_{\Omega}\eta^{2}(1+|Du|^{2})^{\frac{p}{2}+\gamma}\,dx,

where the constant
C
depends only on
n,p,\Lambda
but it is independent of
\gamma
.

Let us now deal with the terms containing the function
g
. We use the bound

\|g\|_{L^{\infty}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)}\leq\,C,

established in Section
3
; here the constant
C
is independent of
n
and
\gamma
.

We first have

\displaystyle|I_{7}|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|g\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{R})}\int_{\Omega}2|D\eta|(1+|Du|^{2})^{\gamma}\,|Du|\,dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\,C\,\int_{\Omega}|D\eta|^{2}(1+|Du|^{2})^{\gamma+p/2}\,dx.

Arguing similarly as in the estimate of
I_{6}
we deduce

\displaystyle|I_{8}|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\,2\gamma\int_{\Omega}|g|\,\eta^{2}(1+|Du|^{2})^{\gamma-1}|D(|Du|)|\,|Du|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\int_{\Omega}\eta^{2}|D^{2}u|^{2}(1+|Du|^{2})^{\frac{p-2}{2}+\gamma}\,dx+C\gamma^{2}\,\|g\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}(B_{R})}\,\int_{\Omega}\eta^{2}(1+|Du|^{2})^{\frac{p}{2}+\gamma}\,dx.

Finally

\displaystyle|I_{9}|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\int_{\Omega}\eta^{2}|D^{2}u|^{2}(1+|Du|^{2})^{\frac{p-2}{2}+\gamma}\,dx+C\|g\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}(B_{R})}\int_{\Omega}\eta^{2}(1+|Du|^{2})^{\frac{p}{2}+\gamma}\,dx.

We remark that in the estimates of the terms
I_{7}-I_{9}
we used in an essential way the fact
(1+|Du|^{2})\geq 1
. It is possible to overcome this limtation and dealing also with the degenerate case by proceeding as in
[
23
]
.

Summing up and using (
2.2
) we obtain

\int_{\Omega}\,\eta^{2}\,(1+|Du|^{2})^{\frac{p-2}{2}+\gamma}|D^{2}u|^{2}\,dx\leq\,C\,(1+\gamma^{2})\int_{\Omega}(\eta^{2}+|D\eta|^{2})\,(1+|Du|^{2})^{\frac{p}{2}+\gamma}\,dx,

for any
0<\rho<R
, where the constant
C
depends on
\lambda,\Lambda,n,p
but is independent of
\gamma
.

On the other hand

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\left|D\left[\eta(1+|Du|^{2})^{\frac{p}{4}+\frac{\gamma}{2}}\right]\right|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|D\eta|^{2}(1+|Du|^{2})^{\frac{p}{2}+\gamma}\,dx+\int_{\Omega}\eta^{2}(1+|Du|^{2})^{\frac{p-2}{2}+\gamma}|D^{2}u|\,dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\,C\,(1+\gamma^{2})\int_{\Omega}(\eta^{2}+|D\eta|^{2})\,(1+|Du|^{2})^{\frac{p}{2}+\gamma}\,dx.

Now, let
2^{*}=\frac{2n}{n-2}
for
n>2
while
2^{*}
equal to any fixed real number greater than 2, if
n=2
. By the Sobolev’s inequality there exists a constant
C
such that

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\Omega}\eta^{2^{*}}\left(1+|Du|^{2}\right)^{\left(\frac{p}{2}+\gamma\right)\frac{2^{*}}{2}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{2}{2^{*}}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\,C\,\int_{\Omega}\left|D\left[\eta(1+|Du|^{2})^{\frac{p}{4}+\frac{\gamma}{2}}\right]\right|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\,C\,(1+\gamma^{2})\int_{\Omega}(\eta^{2}+|D\eta|^{2})\,(1+|Du|^{2})^{\frac{p}{2}+\gamma}\,dx.

5.
Proof of Theorem
2.1
: an iteration scheme

By the definition of
\eta
we obtain

\left(\int_{B_{\rho}}\left(1+|Du|^{2}\right)^{\left(\frac{p}{2}+\gamma\right)\frac{2^{*}}{2}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{2}{2^{*}}}\leq\frac{C(1+\gamma^{2})}{(R-\rho)^{2}}\int_{B_{R}}(1+|Du|^{2})^{\frac{p}{2}+\gamma}\,dx,

(5.1)

for any
B_{\rho}
and
B_{R}
compactly contained in
\Omega
with
\rho<R
and the same center.

Let us define the sequence
(\gamma_{k})_{k}
by induction as follows

\gamma_{1}=0\,;\qquad\gamma_{k+1}=\left(\gamma_{k}+\frac{p}{2}\right)\frac{2^{*}}{2}-\frac{p}{2},\quad\forall\,k\geq 1.

(5.2)

If
(\gamma_{k})_{k}
is the sequence defined in (
5.2
), then the following representation formula holds

\gamma_{k}=\frac{p}{2}\left(\left(\frac{2^{*}}{2}\right)^{k-1}-1\right),

(5.3)

the proof easily follows by induction.

Fix two rays
R_{0}
and
\rho_{0}
such that
0<\rho_{0}<R_{0}<\rho_{0}+1
, let us define
R_{k}=\rho_{0}+(R_{0}-\rho_{0})2^{-k}
for every
k\geq 1
. Since
R_{k+1}<R_{k}\,\,\forall\,k\geq 1
, we are allowed to insert
\rho=R_{k+1}
and
R=R_{k}
in estimate (
5.1
), we also take
\gamma=\gamma_{k}
, where
(\gamma_{k})_{k}
has been defined in (
5.2
). With these choices, by (
5.1
) we obtain

\displaystyle\left(\int_{B_{R_{k+1}}}(1+|Du|^{2})^{\left(\frac{p}{2}+\gamma_{k}\right)\frac{2^{*}}{2}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{2}{2^{*}}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C(1+\gamma_{k}^{2})}{(R_{k}-R_{k+1})^{2}}\int_{B_{R_{k}}}(1+|Du|^{2})^{\frac{p}{2}+\gamma_{k}}\,dx

\displaystyle=\frac{C(1+\gamma_{k}^{2})4^{k+1}}{(R_{0}-\rho_{0})^{2}}\int_{B_{R_{k}}}(1+|Du|^{2})^{\frac{p}{2}+\gamma_{k}}\,dx,

(5.4)

for every
k\geq 1
. Since the constant
C
was independent of
\gamma
, it is clear that now
C
is independent on
k
.
Let us define

A_{k}=\left(\int_{B_{R_{k}}}(1+|Du|^{2})^{\frac{p}{2}+\gamma_{k}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p+2\gamma_{k}}},\quad\forall\,k\geq 1.

(5.5)

The estimate (
5.4
) directly gives us an inductive estimate on
(A_{k})_{k}
. By definition (
5.2
) of
\gamma_{k}
, and by (
5.4
) we have

\displaystyle A_{k+1}

\displaystyle=\left(\int_{B_{R_{k+1}}}(1+|Du|^{2})^{\frac{p}{2}+\gamma_{k+1}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p+2\gamma_{k+1}}}

\displaystyle=\left(\int_{B_{R_{k+1}}}(1+|Du|^{2})^{\left(\frac{p}{2}+\gamma_{k}\right)\frac{2^{*}}{2}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{\left(\frac{p}{2}+\gamma_{k}\right)2^{*}}}

\displaystyle\leq\left[\frac{C(1+\gamma_{k}^{2})4^{k+1}}{(R_{0}-\rho_{0})^{2}}\right]^{\frac{1}{p+2\gamma_{k}}}\cdot\left(\int_{B_{R_{k}}}(1+|Du|^{2})^{\frac{p}{2}+\gamma_{k}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p+2\gamma_{k}}}

\displaystyle=\left[\frac{C(1+\gamma_{k}^{2})4^{k+1}}{(R_{0}-\rho_{0})^{2}}\right]^{\frac{1}{p+2\gamma_{k}}}\cdot A_{k}\,,

(5.6)

for every
k\geq 1
. By iterating (
5.6
) we obtain

A_{k+1}\leq A_{1}\cdot\prod_{i=1}^{k}\left[\frac{C(1+\gamma_{i}^{2})4^{i+1}}{(R_{0}-\rho_{0})^{2}}\right]^{\frac{1}{p+2\gamma_{i}}}\leq C\cdot A_{1}\cdot(R_{0}-\rho_{0})^{-\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}1/(p/2+\gamma_{i})},

(5.7)

for every
k\geq 1
. Here we used the fact that
\prod_{i=1}^{k}\big[C(1+\gamma_{i}^{2})4^{i+1}\big]^{\frac{1}{p+2\gamma_{i}}}
is bounded, indeed

\prod_{i=1}^{\infty}\big[C(1+\gamma_{i}^{2})4^{i+1}\big]^{\frac{1}{p+2\gamma_{i}}}=\textnormal{exp}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\frac{\ln(C(1+\gamma_{i}^{2})4^{i+1})}{p+2\gamma_{i}}\right)<+\infty\,,

since the series is convergent by formula (
5.3
).
Since
\rho_{0}<R_{k}<R_{0}
, by exploting
A_{1}
in (
5.7
), for every
k\geq 2
we deduce that

\left(\int_{B_{\rho_{0}}}(1+|Du|^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}\left(p+2\gamma_{k}\right)}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p+2\gamma_{k}}}\leq\frac{C}{(R_{0}-\rho_{0})^{\beta}}\left(\int_{B_{R_{0}}}(1+|Du|^{2})^{\frac{p}{2}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},

(5.8)

where we defined

\beta:=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\frac{p}{2}+\gamma_{i}}=\frac{2^{*}}{p(2^{*}/2)-p}.

Since
p+2\gamma_{k}\to+\infty
, as
k\to+\infty
it is clear that the left hand side of (
5.8
) converges to the essential supremum of
(1+|Du|^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}
in
B_{\rho_{0}}
. Thus, provided the further regulatity that we will remove in the next section with an approximation procedure, we conclude the proof of Theorem
2.1
.

6.
Proof of Theorem
2.1
: approximation

First of all we state an approximation theorem for
f
through a suitable sequence of regular functions.
Let
B
be the unit ball of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
centered in the origin and consider a positive decreasing sequence
\varepsilon_{\ell}\rightarrow 0
. Let
\phi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(B)
be a smooth symmetric kernel with
\phi\geq 0
and
\int_{B}\phi\,dx=1
.

Let us define

f^{\ell}(x,\xi)=\int_{B\times B}\phi(y)\phi(\eta)f(x+\varepsilon_{\ell}y,\xi+\varepsilon_{\ell}\eta)\,d\eta\,dy,

(6.1)

and consequently

f^{\ell k}(x,\xi)=f^{\ell}(x,\xi)+\frac{1}{k}(1+|\xi|^{2})^{\frac{p}{2}}.

(6.2)

The proof of the following theorem follows from similar arguments as in
[
23
]
.

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
f
satisfy the growth conditions (
2.2
), (
2.3
), (
2.4
), and let
f(x,\xi)
be a convex
C^{2}
function in its second argument. Then the sequence
f^{\ell k}:\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow[0,+\infty)
defined in (
6.2
), convex in the last variable is such that
f^{\ell k}\to f
pointwise as
(\ell,k)\to+\infty
for a.e.
x\in\Omega
and for all
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Moreover,
f^{\ell k}\to f
uniformly in
\Omega_{0}\times K
for every
\Omega_{0}\subset\subset\Omega
and
K
compact set of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Moreover:

•

There exist constants
M_{1},M_{2}>0
, independent of
k,\ell
such that

M_{1}(1+|\xi|^{2})^{\frac{p}{2}}\leq f^{\ell k}(x,\xi)\leq M_{2}(1+|\xi|^{2})^{\frac{p}{2}}\qquad\textnormal{for a.e. $x\in\Omega$, for all $\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}$},

(6.3)

•

there exist constants
\lambda_{1},\Lambda_{1}>0
such that for all
(x,\xi)\in\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\mu\in\mathbb{R}^{n}

\lambda_{1}\,(1+|\xi|^{2})^{\frac{p-2}{2}}|\mu|^{2}\leq\sum_{i,j}f^{\ell k}_{\xi_{i}\xi_{j}}(x,\xi)\mu_{i}\mu_{j},

(6.4)

and

|f^{\ell k}_{\xi_{i}\xi_{j}}(x,\xi)|\leq\Lambda_{1}\,(1+|\xi|^{2})^{\frac{p-2}{2}}.

(6.5)

•

There exists a constant
\Lambda_{2}>0
such that for all
(x,\xi)\in\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{n}

|f^{\ell k}_{\xi x}(x,\xi)|\leq\Lambda_{2}(1+|\xi|^{2})^{\frac{p-1}{2}}.

(6.6)

Let
u\in W^{1,p}(\Omega)
denote a local minimizer of the problem

\min\left\{\int_{\Omega}f(x,Dw):w\in\mathcal{K}_{\psi,\Psi}(\Omega)\right\}.

(6.7)

Fix
x_{0}\in\Omega
and a radius
R>0
such that
B_{R}(x_{0})\subset\subset\Omega
. Consider the following variational problem

\inf\left\{\int_{B_{R}}f^{\ell k}(x,Dv)\,dx,\,\,\,v\in W^{1,p}_{0}(B_{R})+u\,,\,v\geq\psi\,\,\,\textnormal{a.e. in $\Omega$}\,\right\},

(6.8)

where
f^{\ell k}
have been defined in (
6.2
). By classical lower semicontinuity arguments, there exist
v^{\ell k}\in W^{1,p}_{0}(B_{R})+u
solution to problem (
6.8
). By the growth conditions (
6.3
) and the minimality of
v^{\ell k}
, we get

\displaystyle\int_{B_{R}}|Dv^{\ell k}|^{p}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\int_{B_{R}}(1+|Dv^{\ell k}|^{2})^{\frac{p}{2}}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{B_{R}}f^{\ell k}(x,Dv^{\ell k})\,dx\leq C\int_{B_{R}}f^{\ell k}(x,Du)\,dx

\displaystyle=C\int_{B_{R}}f^{\ell}(x,Du)\,dx+\frac{C}{k}\int_{B_{R}}(1+|Du|^{2})^{\frac{p}{2}}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{B_{R}}(1+|Du|^{2})^{\frac{p}{2}}\,dx.

Note that the right hand side of the previous estimate does not depend on
\ell
, thus
\|Dv^{\ell k}\|_{L^{p}(B_{R})}
is bounded for every fixed
k
. Since
W^{1,p}(B_{R})
is weakly compact, up to passing to a subsequence, we can assume there exist
v^{k}\in u+W^{1,p}_{0}(B_{R})
such that

v^{\ell k}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\ell\rightarrow\infty}}{{\rightarrow}}v^{k}\,\,\,\textnormal{weakly in $W^{1,p}_{0}(B_{R})+u$},

(6.9)

where, for every
k\geq 1
, the limit function
v^{k}
still belongs to
\mathcal{K}_{\psi,\Psi}
since this set is weakly closed.
Moreover, by classical convolution properties
f^{\ell}(x,Du)\to f(x,Du)
pointwise a.e. in
B_{R}
as
\ell\to+\infty
, and again by growth conditions (
6.3
)

\int_{B_{R}}f^{\ell}(x,Du)\,dx\leq C\int_{B_{R}}(1+|Du|^{2})^{\frac{p}{2}}\,dx,

(6.10)

for every
\ell\geq 1
. Thus by the Dominated Convergence Theorem we deduce

\lim_{\ell\rightarrow\infty}\int_{B_{R}}|Dv^{\ell k}|^{p}\,dx\leq C\int_{B_{R}}f(x,Du)\,dx+\frac{C}{k}\int_{B_{R}}(1+|Du|^{2})^{\frac{p}{2}}\,dx.

(6.11)

Now, in force of Theorem
6.1
, we know that
f^{\ell k}
satisfies the growth conditions needed to apply our a priori estimate for every
\ell,k
. Thus, applying the a priori estimate to the solutions to (
6.8
), using the minimality of
v^{\ell k}

\displaystyle\|Dv^{\ell k}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{\rho})}

\displaystyle\leq\widetilde{C}\left(\int_{B_{R}}(1+|Dv^{\ell k}|^{2})^{\frac{p}{2}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq\widetilde{C}\left(\int_{B_{R}}f^{\ell k}(x,Dv^{\ell k})\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq\widetilde{C}\left(\int_{B_{R}}f^{\ell k}(x,Du)\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq\widetilde{C}\left(\int_{B_{R}}(1+|Du|^{2})^{\frac{p}{2}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},

where the constant
\widetilde{C}=\frac{C}{(R-\rho)^{\beta}}
is independent of
\ell,k
. Therefore, for every fixed
k
, the sequence
\|Dv^{\ell k}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{R})}
is bounded. Since weak limits are unique, we conclude that

v^{\ell k}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\ell\rightarrow\infty}}{{\rightarrow}}v^{k}\,\,\,\textnormal{weakly star in $W^{1,\infty}_{\rm loc}(B_{R})$}.

(6.12)

By estimate (
6.11
)

\displaystyle\|Dv^{k}\|_{L^{p}(B_{R})}

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{\ell\to\infty}\|Dv^{\ell k}\|_{L^{p}(B_{R})}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\int_{B_{R}}f(x,Du)\,dx+\frac{1}{k}\int_{B_{R}}(1+|Du|^{2})^{\frac{p}{2}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\int_{B_{R}}f(x,Du)+(1+|Du|^{2})^{\frac{p}{2}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},

(6.13)

for every
k\geq 1
. Moreover, for any
\rho
such that
0<\rho<R
, we have

\displaystyle\|Dv^{k}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{\rho})}

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{\ell\to\infty}\|Dv^{\ell k}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{\rho})}

\displaystyle\leq\widetilde{C}\left(\int_{B_{R}}(1+|Du|^{2})^{\frac{p}{2}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

(6.14)

Thus we can deduce that there exists
\bar{v}\in u+W^{1,p}_{0}(B_{R})
such that, up to subsequences

v^{k}\rightarrow\bar{v}\,\,\,\textnormal{weakly in $W^{1,p}_{0}(B_{R})+u$},\qquad\qquad v^{k}\rightarrow\bar{v}\,\,\,\textnormal{weakly star in $W^{1,\infty}_{\rm loc}(B_{R})$}

and the limit function
\bar{v}
still belongs to
\mathcal{K}_{\psi,\Psi}
since this set is weakly closed. Since estimate (
6.14
) holds for every
k\geq 1
, for any
0<\rho<R
we obtain

\|D\bar{v}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{\rho})}\leq\frac{C}{(R-\rho)^{\beta}}\left(\int_{B_{R}}(1+|Du|^{2})^{\frac{p}{2}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

(6.15)

Hence combining lower semicontinuity, the minimality of
v^{\ell k}
for problem (
6.8
), and properties of mollification we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\rho}}f(x,D\bar{v})\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{k\to\infty}\int_{B_{\rho}}f(x,Dv^{k})\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{k\to\infty}\liminf_{\ell\to\infty}\int_{B_{\rho}}f(x,Dv^{\ell k})\,dx

\displaystyle=\liminf_{k\to\infty}\liminf_{\ell\to\infty}\int_{B_{\rho}}f^{\ell}(x,Dv^{\ell k})\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{k\to\infty}\liminf_{\ell\to\infty}\left(\int_{B_{R}}f^{\ell}(x,Dv^{\ell k})+\frac{1}{k}(1+|Dv^{\ell k}|^{2})^{\frac{p}{2}}\,dx\right)

\displaystyle=\liminf_{k\to\infty}\liminf_{\ell\to\infty}\int_{B_{R}}f^{\ell k}(x,Dv^{\ell k})\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{k\to\infty}\liminf_{\ell\to\infty}\int_{B_{R}}f^{\ell k}(x,Du)\,dx

\displaystyle=\liminf_{k\to\infty}\liminf_{\ell\to\infty}\left(\int_{B_{R}}f^{\ell}(x,Du)\,dx+\frac{1}{k}\int_{B_{R}}(1+|Du|^{2})^{\frac{p}{2}}\,dx\right)

\displaystyle=\liminf_{k\to\infty}\left(\int_{B_{R}}f(x,Du)\,dx+\frac{1}{k}\int_{B_{R}}(1+|Du|^{2})^{\frac{p}{2}}\,dx\right)

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{R}}f(x,Du)\,dx,

for every
0<\rho<R
. Then, letting
\rho\rightarrow R
in the previous inequality, we get

\int_{B_{R}}f(x,D\bar{v})\,dx\leq\int_{B_{R}}f(x,Du)\,dx.

(6.16)

Therefore,
u
and
\bar{v}
are two solutions to problem (
6.7
). We claim that
u=\bar{v}
on
B_{R}
. Indeed, if we suppose that
u\neq{v}
choose any
\theta\in(0,1)
and define
v_{\theta}=\theta\bar{v}+(1-\theta)u
, since
f(x,\xi)
is strictly convex in its second argument we have

\displaystyle\int_{B_{R}}f(x,Dv_{\theta})\,dx

\displaystyle<\theta\int_{B_{R}}f(x,D\bar{v})\,dx+(1-\theta)\int_{B_{R}}f(x,Du)\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{R}}f(x,Du)\,dx,

contradicting the minimality of
u
. Thus, regularity is preserved through the limit procedure, and this concludes the proof of Theorem
2.1
because we have removed the further regularity assumptions we needed to obtain the result.

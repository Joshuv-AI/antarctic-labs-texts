Abstract
Streaming codes (SCs) are packet-level codes that recover erased packets within a strict decoding-delay deadline. Streaming codes for various packet erasure channel models such as sliding-window (SW) channel models that admit random or burst erasures in any SW of a fixed length have been studied in the literature, and the optimal rate as well as rate-optimal code constructions of SCs over such channel models are known. In this paper, we study error-correcting streaming codes ( SC ERR \text{SC}_{\text{\tiny ERR}} s), i.e., packet-level codes which recover erroneous packets within a delay constraint. We study SC ERR \text{SC}_{\text{\tiny ERR}} s for two classes of SW channel models, one that admits random packet errors, and another that admits multiple bursts of packet errors, in any SW of a fixed length. For the case of random packet errors, we establish the equivalence of an SC ERR \text{SC}_{\text{\tiny ERR}} and a corresponding SC that recovers from random packet erasures, thus determining the optimal rate of an SC ERR \text{SC}_{\text{\tiny ERR}} for this setting, and providing a rate-optimal code construction for all parameters. We then focus on SCs that recover from multiple erasure bursts and derive a rate-upper-bound for such SCs. We show the necessity of a divisibility constraint for the existence of an SC constructed by the popular diagonal embedding technique, that achieves this rate-bound under a stringent delay requirement. We then show that a construction known in the literature achieves this rate-bound when the divisibility constraint is met. We further show the equivalence of the SCs considered and SC ERR \text{SC}_{\text{\tiny ERR}} s for the setting of multiple error bursts, under a stringent delay requirement.

Preliminaries

We will use the following straightforward lemma.

Lemma 1
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be an
[n,k]
code and let
H
be an
(n-k)\times n
parity-check matrix of
\mathcal{C}
. Let the coordinates in
\mathcal{E}\subseteq[0:n-1]
be erased from
\mathcal{C}
, and let
i\in\mathcal{E}
. Then, the
i
-th code symbol in any codeword can be recovered from the code symbols corresponding to the coordinates in
[0:n-1]\setminus\mathcal{E}
iff

H_{i}\notin\textnormal{span}\langle\{H_{j}\ |\ j\in\mathcal{E}\setminus\{i\}\}\rangle.

It follows from Lemma
1
that an
[n,k]
code
\mathcal{C}
can recover from an erasure pattern
\underline{e}
whose support is given by
\mathcal{E}\subseteq[0:n-1]
iff
\{H_{j}\ |\ j\in\mathcal{E}\}
is a linearly independent set, where
H
is a parity-check matrix of
\mathcal{C}
. As explained in
[
4
]
, if
\mathcal{C}
is a delay-
\tau
decodable code for an error pattern(s), and
\underline{c}^{T}=[c_{0},c_{1},\dots,c_{n-1}]\in\mathcal{C}
, then to recover code symbol
c_{i}
such that
i+\tau<n-1
, one must consider the parity-check matrix of the code obtained by puncturing
\mathcal{C}
on the coordinates
[i+\tau+1:n-1],
i.e., the parity-check matrix of the code obtained from
\mathcal{C}
be deleting the coordinates
[i+\tau+1:n-1]
from every codeword of
\mathcal{C}
. Since the dual of a punctured code is a shortened code, as explained in
[
4
]
, to find a parity-check matrix of the punctured code, we first find a basis for the subspace of the row-space of
H
which has the property that for every
\underline{v}^{T}=[v_{0},v_{1},\dots,v_{n-1}]
in the subspace,
v_{\lambda}=0,~\lambda\in[i+\tau+1:n-1]
, and then we delete the coordinates
[i+\tau+1:n-1]
from every vector in the basis. Specifically, if
\mathcal{C}
is a systematic code, and
H
is its parity-check matrix in systematic form, then the parity-check matrix of the code obtained from
\mathcal{C}
by puncturing
\mathcal{C}
on the coordinates
[i+\tau+1:n-1]
is given by
H^{(i)}
, when
\tau\geq k
. Thus,
c_{i}
is recoverable form
\underline{e}
within delay
\tau

H^{(i)}_{i}\notin\textnormal{span}\langle\{H^{(i)}_{j}\ |\ j\in{\mathcal{E}}\setminus\{i\}\}\rangle.

Lemma 4
.

Let
b\geq 2
and
m\geq 2
be positive integers. Consider a matrix
A\in\mathbb{F}^{2\times mb}_{q}
that satisfies the following properties:

•

P1:
A(i,[0:b-2])=\underline{0}^{T}_{b-1},

i\in\{0,1\}.

•

P2: In any row of
A
, no more than
(b-1)
consecutive entries can be
0
.

•

P3: For
j\in[b-1:(m-1)b],
rank
(A(:,[j:j+b-1]))=1
.

•

P4: For
j\in[0:(m-2)b],
rank
(A(:,[j:j+2b-1]))=2
.

Then,
A(i,mb-1)\neq 0,\ i\in\{0,1\}
.

Proof.
From P1 and P2, it follows that
A(i,b-1)\neq 0,~i\in\{0,1\}
. Let
A(1,b-1)=\beta\cdot A(0,b-1),
where
~\beta\in\mathbb{F}_{q}\setminus\{0\}
. Suppose that
A(i_{1},j_{1})\neq 0
for some
i_{1}\in\{0,1\}
and
j_{1}\in[b:2b-2]
. Then, considering
A(:,[b-1:2b-2]),
from P3 we have
A(1,j_{1})=\beta\cdot A(0,j_{1})
. Again, from P3 we have
A(1,[b:2b-1])^{T}=\beta\cdot A(0,[b:2b-1])^{T}
. Combining
A(1,[b-1:2b-2])^{T}=\beta\cdot A(0,[b-1:2b-2])^{T}
and
A(1,[b:2b-1])^{T}=\beta\cdot A(0,[b:2b-1])^{T},
we get
A(1,[0:2b-1])=\beta\cdot A(0,[0:2b-1])^{T}
, which violates P4. Thus, we have
A(i,[b:2b-2])=\underline{0}^{T}_{b-1},~i\in\{0,1\}
. From P2 we get
A(i,2b-1)\neq 0,~i\in\{0,1\}
. Proceeding similarly, the lemma follows.

Every CBER is smooth below the Carlson-Simpson generic partition
Aristotelis Panagiotopoulos, Allison Wang

Abstract
. Let E E be a countable Borel equivalence relation on the space ℰ ∞ \mathcal{E}_{\infty} of all infinite partitions of the natural numbers. We show that E E coincides with equality below a Carlson-Simpson generic element of ℰ ∞ \mathcal{E}_{\infty} . In contrast, we show that there is a hypersmooth equivalence relation on ℰ ∞ \mathcal{E}_{\infty} which is Borel bireducible with E 1 E_{1} on every Carlson-Simpson cube. Our arguments are classical and require no background in forcing.



1.
Introduction

One of the prominent ongoing research programs of descriptive set theory concerns the complexity theory of classification problems. Formally, a classification problem is any pair
(X,E)
where
X
is a Polish space and
E
is any equivalence relation on
X
which is analytic as a subset of
X\times X
. A classification problem
(X,E)
is considered to be of less or equal complexity to the classification problem
(Y,F)
if there is a
Borel reduction
from
E
to
F
,
i.e.
, if there is a Borel map
f\colon X\to Y
so that
xEx^{\prime}\iff f(x)Ff(x^{\prime})
for all
x,x^{\prime}\in X
. We write
(X,E)\leq_{B}(Y,F)
, or simply
E\leq_{B}F
, when
E
is Borel reducible to
F
. In the lower part of the Borel reduction hierarchy one finds the complexity class of all Countable Borel Equivalence Relations (CBERs).

1.1.
Countable Borel Equivalence Relations

The class of CBERs consists of all classification problems
(X,E)
so that
E
is Borel and each
E
-class
[x]_{E}\subseteq X
is countable. The class of CBERs has received significant attention over the years, partially due to its connections to ergodic theory. On the one hand, measure-preserving actions
\Gamma\curvearrowright X
of countable discrete groups
\Gamma
naturally give rise to CBERs by setting
xEx^{\prime}\iff\exists\gamma\in\Gamma(\gamma x=x^{\prime})
. On the other hand, it seems that the only methods we currently have at our disposal for discerning distinct complexities within the class of CBERs make essential use of measure-theoretic arguments.

To elaborate a bit more on the latter point, we recall some facts about the structure of the quasi-ordering
\leq_{B}
within the class of CBERs; for a comprehensive survey, see
[
Kec22
]
. The
\leq_{B}
-least CBER is equality
=
on
2^{\mathbb{N}}
. The
\leq_{B}
-successor of
(2^{\mathbb{N}},=)
is
(2^{\mathbb{N}},E_{0})
, where
E_{0}
is eventual equality:
xE_{0}y
if there is
n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
so that for all
n\geq n_{0}
we have
x(n)=y(n)
. We say that
(X,E)
is
smooth
if
(X,E)\leq_{B}(2^{\mathbb{N}},=)
. We say that it is
hyperfinite
if
(X,E)\leq_{B}(2^{\mathbb{N}},E_{0})
. The class of CBERs admits a
\leq_{B}
-top element
E_{\infty}
. This is the orbit equivalence relation of the Bernoulli shift
F_{2}\curvearrowright 2^{F_{2}}
of the free group with
2
generators. We say that
(X,E)
is
intermediate
if
E_{0}<_{B}E<_{B}E_{\infty}
.

The structure of intermediate CBERs is very complex. For example, the partial order of Borel subsets of the reals under inclusion embeds in the quasi-order induced by
\leq_{B}
on CBERs; see

[
AK00
]
. However, all known proofs of existence of intermediate CBERs, as well as all known arguments for comparing intermediate CBERs with respect to
\leq_{B}
, use measure-theoretic methods and cocycle superrigidity techniques of ergodic theory. A partial explanation as to why this is the case comes from a result of Hjorth and Kechris, which states that every CBER
E
on a Polish space
X
is hyperfinite when restricted to an appropriately chosen
E
-invariant comeager
C\subseteq X
; see
[
HK96
, Theorem 6.2]
. In other words, the intrinsic complexity of a CBER sitting above
E_{0}
hides within a negligible part of
X
from the point of view of Baire category. This renders Baire-category methods too crude for the fine structure of the complexity class of CBERs.

Given that the last four decades have generated several open problems about CBERs that remain open to this day (
e.g.
, Martin’s conjecture, see
[
Kec22
, Problem 11.5]
; or Weiss’ problem on amenable actions, see
[
Kec22
, Problem 7.27]
),
one would like to have methods aside from measure for discerning complexities beyond
E_{0}
. In particular, one would like to know which notions of “largeness,” alternative to measure or Baire category, interact non-trivially with the intrinsic complexity of intermediate CBERs. To make this precise, we recall some definitions from
[
KSZ13
]
. Let
\mathcal{I}
be a
\sigma
-ideal on a Polish space and let
E
be an analytic equivalence relation on
X
. We say that
E
is in the spectrum of
\mathcal{I}
if for every Borel
B\subseteq X
with
B\not\in\mathcal{I}
there is a Borel
C\subseteq B
with
C\not\in\mathcal{I}
so that
E
and
E\upharpoonright C
are Borel bireducible. On the other hand, we say that
\mathcal{I}

canonizes
E
to some
F\leq_{B}E
, if for every Borel
B\subseteq X
with
B\not\in\mathcal{I}
there is a Borel
C\subseteq B
with
C\not\in\mathcal{I}
so that
E\upharpoonright C\leq_{B}F
. In this terminology, all known intermediate CBERs are in the spectrum of the
\sigma
-ideal
\mathcal{I}_{\mu}
of
\mu
-null sets for some appropriately chosen
\sigma
-additive measure, while the aforementioned result
[
HK96
, Theorem 6.2]
shows that the
\sigma
-ideal
\mathcal{I}_{*}(X)
of all meager sets of a Polish space
X
canonizes every CBER to
E_{0}
in a very strong way.

1.2.
Ramsey notions of “largeness”

Ramsey theory provides a plethora of
\sigma
-ideals whose relationship with the complexity of CBERs is not yet well understood. Recall that every topological Ramsey space
(\mathcal{R},\leq,r)
comes together with two topologies: its Polish topology and its Ellentuck topology; see
[
Tod10
]
. We denote by
\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{R})
the collection of all Ellentuck-meager subsets of
\mathcal{R}
. By Borel or analytic subsets of
\mathcal{R}
or
\mathcal{R}^{2}
we mean Borel or analytic in the Polish topology of
\mathcal{R}
.
For the classical Ramsey space
[\mathbb{N}]^{\mathbb{N}}
of all infinite subsets of
\mathbb{N}
, classical results of Mathias
[
Mat77
]
regarding the extensions of a model of set theory by Mathias-generic reals show that
\mathcal{I}([\mathbb{N}]^{\mathbb{N}})
canonizes every CBER to
E_{0}
; see
[
KSZ13
, Theorem 8.17]
. Similar results hold for the Milliken space
(\mathbb{N})^{\mathbb{N}}
of infinite sequences
(A_{n})_{n}
of finite sets of natural numbers with the property that
\min(A_{n+1})>\max(A_{n})
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
, where
(A_{n})_{n}\leq(B_{n})_{n}
if for all
m\in\mathbb{N}
we have that
A_{m}
is a union of elements of
(B_{n})_{n}
. By
[
KSZ13
, Theorem 8.28]
,
\mathcal{I}((\mathbb{N})^{\mathbb{N}})
canonizes every CBER to
E_{0}
as well.

It turns out that there exist Ramsey spaces which canonize CBERs to
(2^{\mathbb{N}},=)
. In fact, all Ramsey spaces which have previously been shown to have this property satisfy a much stronger canonization property: they canonize
any analytic equivalence relation
to
(2^{\mathbb{N}},=)
. The earliest such example is the Halpern–Läuchli–Milliken space
\mathcal{S}_{\infty}(2^{<\mathbb{N}})
of strong subtrees of the complete binary tree
2^{<\mathbb{N}}
; see
[
KSZ13
, Theorem 8.1]
. The second known example of a Ramsey-theoretic ideal with similarly strong canonization properties comes from the Carlson-Simpson dual Ramsey theorem
[
CS84
]
for finite partitions of the natural numbers. For every positive
k\in\mathbb{N}
let
\mathcal{E}_{k}
be the collection of all equivalence relations
A
on
\mathbb{N}
with exactly
k
-many classes. Let
\mathcal{E}_{\infty}
be the collection of all equivalence relations
A
on
\mathbb{N}
with infinitely many classes. We write
A\leq B
if the equivalence relation
A
is a coarsening of
B
; see Section
3
for definitions. For every
\kappa\in\{1,2,\ldots\}\cup\{\infty\}
one can define a
\sigma
-ideal
\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{E}_{\kappa})
on
\mathcal{E}_{\kappa}
as follows. First, for every
m\in\mathbb{N}
and
B\in\mathcal{E}_{\infty}
, let
[m,B]_{\kappa}
be the set of all
A\in\mathcal{E}_{\kappa}
so that
A\leq B
and
A,B
agree on
\{0,\ldots,m-1\}
. Now suppose
\mathcal{X}\subseteq\mathcal{E}_{\kappa}
. We set
\mathcal{X}\in\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{E}_{\kappa})
if for every
B\in\mathcal{E}_{\infty}
and
m\in\mathbb{N}
, there is
B^{\prime}\in[m,B]_{\infty}
so that for all
A\in[m,B^{\prime}]_{\kappa}
we have that
A\not\in\mathcal{X}
; see
[
Tod10
, Section 5.6]
. In
[
Dou13
]
it is shown that if
\kappa
is finite, then
\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{E}_{\kappa})
canonizes any analytic equivalence relation to
(2^{\mathbb{N}},=)
.

1.3.
Main results

In this paper we study the canonization properties of the
\sigma
-ideal
\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{E}_{\infty})
associated to the topological Ramsey space
\mathcal{E}_{\infty}
of infinite partitions of
\mathbb{N}
. We first show that, unlike
\mathcal{E}_{\kappa}
with
\kappa
finite, there exist non-hyperfinite Borel equivalence relations which maintain their complexity on every
\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{E}_{\infty})
-positive subset of
\mathcal{E}_{\infty}
.
Recall that if
E_{1}
denotes the equivalence relation of eventual equality of sequences of reals, then
E_{0}\lneq_{B}E_{1}
.

Proposition 1.1
.

E_{1}
is in the spectrum of
\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{E}_{\infty})
. More precisely, there exists a Borel equivalence relation on
\mathcal{E}_{\infty}
whose restriction on every set of the form
[m,B]_{\infty}\subseteq\mathcal{E}_{\infty}
is Borel bireducible with
E_{1}
.

On the other hand, the content of our main theorem is that when it comes to CBERs,
\mathcal{E}_{\infty}
shares the same canonization properties as
\mathcal{S}_{\infty}(2^{<\mathbb{N}})
and
\mathcal{E}_{\kappa}
with
\kappa
finite. For the statement below, recall that
\mathcal{D}\subseteq\mathcal{E}_{\infty}
is
Ramsey conull
if
\mathcal{E}_{\infty}\setminus\mathcal{D}\in\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{E}_{\infty})
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
E
be a countable Borel equivalence relation on
\mathcal{E}_{\infty}
. Then there exists a Ramsey conull
\mathcal{D}\subseteq\mathcal{E}_{\infty}
so that for all
B\in\mathcal{D}
and
A,A^{\prime}\leq B
we have that

A\;E\;A^{\prime}\iff A=A^{\prime}.

In particular,
\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{E}_{\infty})
canonizes every CBER to
(2^{\mathbb{N}},=)
.

In contrast to all the previously known results regarding the canonization properties of Ramsey–type ideals
[
KSZ13
,
Dou13
,
Mat77
]
, our arguments here are entirely classical/combinatorial and require no background in set-theoretic forcing. They also differ in an essential way from earlier arguments for “canonization to
=
”,
and not merely on the level of language. Indeed, Proposition
1.1
illustrates that Theorem
1.2
cannot be attained by the “mutual generics” technique for (reduced) product forcings that was used in
[
KSZ13
, Theorem 8.1]
and
[
Dou13
, Theorem 0.1]
; finer techniques designed specifically for CBERs are necessary. In the process of developing such techniques, we prove Lemma
5.2
, which is interesting in its own right since it seems that it could serve as the starting point for similar canonization results for other topological Ramsey spaces. As an example, in Section
8
we illustrate how our arguments adapt to prove
[
KSZ13
, Theorem 8.1]
for the special case of CBERs.

1.4.
Some open problems

All topological Ramsey spaces that we know of canonize every CBER to either
(2^{\mathbb{N}},E_{0})
or
(2^{\mathbb{N}},=)
. This raises the following two natural questions:

Problem 1
.

Characterize all topological Ramsey spaces that canonize every CBER to
=
.

Problem 2
.

Is there a topological Ramsey space
\mathcal{R}
and some CBER
E
on
\mathcal{R}
so that:

(1)

E_{0}\lneq E
; and

(2)

E
is in the spectrum of
\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{R})
?

Recall that an equivalence relation
E
on a Polish space
X
is hypersmooth if
E=\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}E_{n}
, where
E_{n}
are smooth equivalence relations on
X
and
E_{n}\subseteq E_{n+1}
for all
n
. Another natural question that is raised by Proposition
1.1
and the general theory of hypersmooth equivalence relations
[
KL94
]
is the following:

Problem 3
.

Let
E
be an analytic equivalence relation on
\mathcal{E}_{\infty}
. Does
\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{E}_{\infty})
canonizes
E
to some hypersmooth equivalence relation?

3.
Background

As usual, natural numbers
n\in\mathbb{N}
are identified with the set
\{0,\ldots,n-1\}
of their predecessors. If
R
is a set of ordered pairs
(x,y)
then
\mathrm{dom}(R)=\{x\colon\exists y\;(x,y)\in R\}
.
Let
\mathcal{E}_{\infty}
be the collection of all equivalence relations
E
on
\mathbb{N}
that have an infinite quotient
\mathbb{N}/E
. We will think of
E
as a partition of
\mathbb{N}
into infinitely many equivalence classes. Let
\mu(E):=\{\mathrm{min}([x]_{E})\colon x\in\mathbb{N}\}
be the collection of all minimum representatives of the equivalence classes
[x]_{E}
of
E
, and let
\{\mu_{n}(E)\colon n\in\mathbb{N}\}
be the increasing enumeration of
\mu(E)
. Notice that
0=\mu_{0}(E)\in\mu(E)
for all
E\in\mathcal{E}_{\infty}
.

Let
A,B\in\mathcal{E}_{\infty}
. We say that
A
is coarser than
B
and write
A\leq B
if every
A
-class is a union of
B
-classes. The
n
-th approximation
r_{n}(A)
of
A\in\mathcal{E}_{\infty}
is the equivalence relation
A\upharpoonright\mu_{n}(A)
that is the restriction of
A
to the finite set
\{0,\ldots,\mu_{n}(A)-1\}
. Notice that
r_{0}(A)=\emptyset
for all
A\in\mathcal{E}_{\infty}
. We denote by
\mathcal{E}_{\infty}^{\mathrm{fin}}
the collection of all equivalence relations on all initial segments
k=\{0,\ldots,k-1\}
of
\mathbb{N}
. Notice that
\mathcal{E}_{\infty}^{\mathrm{fin}}=\{r_{n}(A)\colon A\in\mathcal{E}_{\infty},n\in\mathbb{N}\}
. If
s,t\in\mathcal{E}_{\infty}^{\mathrm{fin}}
, then we write
s\leq t
if
\mathrm{dom}(s)=\mathrm{dom}(t)
and
s
is coarser than
t
,
i.e.
, if every
s
-class is a union of
t
-classes.
Below, the implicit assumption is that
s,t,\ldots
denote elements of
\mathcal{E}_{\infty}^{\mathrm{fin}}
and
A,B,\ldots
elements of
\mathcal{E}_{\infty}
. We say that
A
is an
end-extension
of
s
and write
s\sqsubseteq A
if there is some
n\in\mathbb{N}
so that
s=r_{n}(A)
.

It follows by
[
CS84
]
that the triple
(\mathcal{E}_{\infty},\leq,r)
is a topological Ramsey space in the sense of
[
Tod10
, Section 5.1]
; see
[
Tod10
, Theorem 5.70]
. For every
s
and
A
let

[s,A]:=\{B\in\mathcal{E}_{\infty}\colon s\sqsubseteq B\text{ and }B\leq A\}

be the associated
Ellentuck cube
.
The
Polish topology
on
\mathcal{E}_{\infty}
is the topology generated by all Ellentuck cubes of the form
[s,\mathbb{N}_{\mathrm{ER}}]
, where
s
ranges over elements of
\mathcal{E}_{\infty}^{\mathrm{fin}}
and
\mathbb{N}_{\mathrm{ER}}
is the
\leq
-maximal element
\{(n,n)\colon n\in\mathbb{N}\}
of
\mathcal{E}_{\infty}
. The
Ellentuck topology
on
\mathcal{E}_{\infty}
is the topology generated by all Ellentuck cubes. Recall from
[
Tod10
]
:

Definition 3.1
.

A set
\mathcal{X}\subseteq\mathcal{E}_{\infty}
is
Ramsey
if for every
[s,A]\neq\emptyset
there exists
B\in[s,A]
such that either
[s,B]\subseteq\mathcal{X}
or
[s,B]\subseteq\mathcal{X}^{c}
. It is
Ramsey null
if for every
[s,A]\neq\emptyset
there exists
B\in[s,A]
such that
[s,B]\subseteq\mathcal{X}^{c}
.

The following is proved in
[
CS84
]
; see also
[
Tod10
]
.

Theorem 3.2

(Carlson-Simpson)
.

For every
\mathcal{X}\subseteq\mathcal{E}_{\infty}
we have that :

(1)

\mathcal{X}
has the Baire property in the Ellentuck topology if and only if
\mathcal{X}
is Ramsey;

(2)

\mathcal{X}
is meager in the Ellentuck topology if and only if
\mathcal{X}
is Ramsey null.

A subset
\mathcal{X}\subseteq\mathcal{E}_{\infty}
is
Borel
if it is Borel in the Polish topology of
\mathcal{E}_{\infty}
. Notice that this implies that
\mathcal{X}
is Borel in the Ellentuck topology of
\mathcal{E}_{\infty}
as well. It is not difficult to construct some meager (in the Polish topology)
\mathcal{X}\subseteq\mathcal{E}_{\infty}
which is Ramsey null and hence
\mathcal{X}^{c}
is comeager but Ramsey null.

is in the spectrum of
\mathcal{E}_{\infty}

Consider the equivalence relations
E_{1},E_{1}^{\mathrm{tail}}
on
2^{\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{N}}
given by

\displaystyle xE_{1}y

\displaystyle\iff\exists n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}\;\;\forall n,m\in\mathbb{N}\;\text{ so that }x(n_{0}+n,m)=y(n_{0}+n,m),

Consider also the
G_{\delta}

\mathcal{CS}\subseteq 2^{\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{N}}
consisting of all

A^{x}_{0},A^{x}_{1},A^{x}_{2},\ldots

\mathcal{E}_{\infty}

A^{x}_{n}:=\{m\in\mathbb{N}\colon m~A~\mu_{n}(A)\}

Lemma 4.1
.

The equivalence relations
(2^{\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{N}},E_{1})
and
(\mathcal{CS},E_{1}^{\mathrm{tail}})
are Borel bireducible.

Proof.

Clearly the inclusion map
i\colon\mathcal{CS}\to 2^{\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{N}}
induces a continuous reduction

(\mathcal{CS},E_{1}^{\mathrm{tail}})\leq_{B}(2^{\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{N}},E_{1}^{\mathrm{tail}})

By
[
DJK94
, Theorem 8.1]
we have that
(2^{\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{N}},E_{1}^{\mathrm{tail}})
is hypersmooth. In particular,
(2^{\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{N}},E_{1}^{\mathrm{tail}})\leq_{B}(2^{\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{N}},E_{1})
in a strong way; see
[
KL94
, Proposition 1.3]
. Composing these two Borel reductions we have that
(\mathcal{CS},E_{1}^{\mathrm{tail}})\leq_{B}(2^{\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{N}},E_{1})
.

For the other direction, we define a Borel reduction
f\colon 2^{\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{N}}\to\mathcal{CS}
as follows. First let

\mathbb{N}=N_{-1}\bigsqcup N_{0}\bigsqcup N_{1}\bigsqcup N_{2}\bigsqcup\cdots

be a partition of
\mathbb{N}
into infinitely many infinite sets and for every
k\geq-1
fix some bijection
t_{k}\colon\mathbb{N}\to N_{k}
. Let now
x\in 2^{\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{N}}
and consider the sets
B_{0},B_{1},B_{2},\ldots\subseteq\mathbb{N}
given by
B_{n}:=\{m\in\mathbb{N}\colon x(n,m)=1\}
. For every
n\in\mathbb{N}
we define the sets
A_{2n},A_{2n+1}
by

A_{2n}:=t_{n}(B_{n})\cup\{t_{-1}(2n)\}\quad\text{ and }\quad A_{2n+1}:=(N_{n}\setminus t_{n}(B_{n}))\cup\{t_{-1}(2n+1)\}

Notice that the sets
A_{0},A_{1},A_{2},A_{3},\ldots
are non-empty and form a partition of
\mathbb{N}
. Moreover, by making sure that
t_{-1}\colon\mathbb{N}\to N_{-1}
is increasing and that both

appear before the least element of

we have that
A_{0},A_{1},\ldots
is of the form
A^{y}_{0},A^{y}_{1},\ldots
for some

. Set
f(x)=y
for this

.

It remains to check that

is a reduction from
E_{1}
to
E_{1}^{\mathrm{tail}}
. Let
x,x^{\prime}\in 2^{\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{N}}
.
It is easy to see that if
x\;E_{1}\;x^{\prime}
implies
f(x)\;E_{1}\;f(x^{\prime})
, which implies
f(x)\;E_{1}^{\mathrm{tail}}\;f(x^{\prime})
.
The fact that
f(x)\;E_{1}^{\mathrm{tail}}\;f(x^{\prime})
implies

follows from the injectivity of
t_{-1}
.

∎

Lemma 4.2
.

Let

Let

.

5.
Borel maps are tracial on a Ramsey conull set

Let
A,B\in\mathcal{E}_{\infty}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
. The
trace of
A
in
B
at
n
, denoted
\mathrm{tr}(A,B,n)
, is the restriction of the equivalence relation
A
to the finite set
\{0,1,\ldots,\mu_{n}(B)-1\}
. Notice that
\mathrm{tr}(A,B,n)
may not be equal to
r_{m}(A)
for any
m\in\mathbb{N}
.

Definition 5.1
.

Let
f\colon\mathcal{E}_{\infty}\to\mathcal{E}_{\infty}
be a map and
\mathcal{X}\subseteq\mathcal{E}_{\infty}
. We say that
f
is
tracial on
\mathcal{X}
if for every
B\in\mathcal{X}
there is
n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
so that for all
n\geq n_{0}
and
A,A^{\prime}\leq B
for which there exist
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
s\in\mathcal{E}_{\infty}^{\mathrm{fin}}
with
s=r_{k}(A)=r_{k}(A^{\prime})
and
\mathrm{dom}(s)=\{0,\ldots,\mu_{n}(B)-1\}
, we have

(1)

\displaystyle\mathrm{tr}\big(f(A),B,n\big)=\mathrm{tr}\big(f(A^{\prime}),B,n\big).

The main result of this section is the following lemma.

Lemma 5.2

(Traciality)
.

Let
f\colon\mathcal{E}_{\infty}\to\mathcal{E}_{\infty}
be a Borel map. Then there exists a Ramsey conull set
\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathcal{E}_{\infty}
so that
f
is tracial on
\mathcal{C}
.

The proof of Lemma
5.2
does not rely on any special properties of the Carlson-Simpson space
\mathcal{E}_{\infty}
. Indeed, for any topological Ramsey space
\mathcal{R}
and any notion of “trace” on
\mathcal{R}
, one can state and prove a lemma similar to Lemma
5.2
. Since there are other topological Ramsey spaces admitting useful notions of a trace we will restate Definition
5.1
and prove Lemma
5.2
for any trace on the arbitrary topological Ramsey space
\mathcal{R}
.

Let
(\mathcal{R},\leq,r)
be a topological Ramsey space in the sense of
[
Tod10
, Section 5.1]
, and let
\mathcal{AR}
be its set of finite approximations. A
trace on
\mathcal{R}
is any family
\mathcal{T}=\{\mathcal{T}_{t}\colon t\in\mathcal{AR}\}
of finite Borel partitions
\mathcal{T}_{t}
of
\mathcal{R}
, one for every
t\in\mathcal{AR}
. Let
\mathcal{T}
be a trace on
\mathcal{R}
. For all
A,B\in\mathcal{R}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we write
\mathrm{tr}_{\mathcal{T}}\big(A,B,n\big)
to denote the piece in the partition
\mathcal{T}_{r_{n}(B)}
that contains
A
.

Example 5.3
.

Let
\mathcal{T}
be the trace on
\mathcal{E}_{\infty}
that is defined as follows: for all
t\in\mathcal{E}_{\infty}^{\mathrm{fin}}
, we have that
A,A^{\prime}
are in the same pieces of the partition
\mathcal{T}_{t}
if
A\upharpoonright\mathrm{dom}(t)=A^{\prime}\upharpoonright\mathrm{dom}(t)
. It follows that
\mathrm{tr}_{\mathcal{T}}\big(A,B,n\big)=\mathrm{tr}_{\mathcal{T}}\big(A^{\prime},B,n\big)
if and only if
\mathrm{tr}\big(A,B,n\big)=\mathrm{tr}\big(A^{\prime},B,n\big)
, where the latter is defined at the beginning of this section.

Example 5.4
.

Let
2^{<\mathbb{N}}
be the full binary tree. Recall that a strong subtree
A
of
2^{<\mathbb{N}}
is any subset of
2^{<\mathbb{N}}
for which there are is a sequence
k_{0}<k_{1}<\ldots
in
\mathbb{N}
so that
A
can be partitioned into sets
A(\ell)\subseteq 2^{k_{\ell}}
satisfying

(1)

A(0)
is a singleton (the root of
A
);

(2)

for every
a\in A(\ell)
and
i\in\{0,1\}
, there is exactly one
b\in A(\ell+1)
extending
a^{\frown}i
.

We denote by
\mathcal{S}_{\infty}(2^{<\mathbb{N}})
the Milliken space of all strong subtrees of
2^{<\mathbb{N}}
; see
[
Tod10
, Section 6.1]
.
For every
A\in\mathcal{S}_{\infty}(2^{<\mathbb{N}})
, let
\langle A\rangle
be the collection of all
b\in 2^{<\mathbb{N}}
for which there is some
a\in A
extending
b
. In particular, we always have that
\emptyset\in\langle A\rangle
. Let
\mathcal{T}
be the trace on
\mathcal{S}_{\infty}(2^{<\mathbb{N}})
that is defined as follows: for all
t\in\mathcal{S}_{<\infty}(2^{<\mathbb{N}})
with
t=T(0)\cup\cdots\cup T(\ell)
for some

,

A,A^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}_{\infty}(2^{<\mathbb{N}})
are in the same piece of the partition

if

\langle A\rangle\cap T(\ell)=\langle A^{\prime}\rangle\cap T(\ell).

For the rest of this section we fix some topological Ramsey space
\mathcal{R}
together with some trace
\mathcal{T}
on
\mathcal{R}
. Next we state analogues of Definition
5.1
and Lemma
5.2
for the pair
\mathcal{R},\mathcal{T}
.

Definition 5.5
.

Let
f\colon\mathcal{R}\to\mathcal{R}
be a map and
\mathcal{X}\subseteq\mathcal{R}
. We say that
f
is
tracial on
\mathcal{X}
if for every
B\in\mathcal{X}
there is
n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
so that for every

and all

and

with

, we have that

(2)

\displaystyle\mathrm{tr}_{\mathcal{T}}\big(f(A),B,n\big)=\mathrm{tr}_{\mathcal{T}}\big(f(A^{\prime}),B,n\big).

Notice that for the topological Ramsey space
\mathcal{E}_{\infty}
, if

and

Proposition 5.6
.

Let
f\colon\mathcal{R}\to\mathcal{R}
be a Borel map. Then there exists a Ramsey conull set

so that

is tracial on

.

Proof.

Let

and

with

so that for every

and all

for which there are some
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
s\in\mathcal{AR}
with
s=r_{k}(A)=r_{k}(A^{\prime})
and
\mathrm{depth}_{D}(s)=n
, we have that
\mathrm{tr}_{\mathcal{T}}\big(f(A),D^{\prime},n\big)=\mathrm{tr}_{\mathcal{T}}\big(f(A^{\prime}),D^{\prime},n\big)
.

To define

(3)

\displaystyle D_{n+1}\in[r_{n}(D_{n}),D_{n}],\text{ where }D_{n_{0}}=D,

so that for all

for which there are some
k\in\mathbb{N}
and

with
s=r_{k}(A)=r_{k}(A^{\prime})
and

, we have that

(4)

\displaystyle\mathrm{tr}_{\mathcal{T}}\big(f(A),D_{n},n\big)=\mathrm{tr}_{\mathcal{T}}\big(f(A^{\prime}),D_{n},n\big).

Assuming we have defined
(D_{n})_{n\geq n_{0}}
as above, we let
D^{\prime}
be the limit of
D_{n}
in the Polish topology of
\mathcal{R}
. This limit exists by (
3
) above and Axioms A.2 in
[
Tod10
, Section 5.1]
. It
is the unique

given by
r_{n}(D^{\prime})=r_{n}(D_{n})\text{ for all }n\geq n_{0}
.
Clearly

for all

. In particular, the case
n=n_{0}
implies

, while the general case
n\geq n_{0}
implies
that for all
n\geq n_{0}
and all
A,A^{\prime}\leq D^{\prime}
for which there are some
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
s\in\mathcal{AR}
with
s=r_{k}(A)=r_{k}(A^{\prime})
and
\mathrm{depth}_{D}(s)=n
, we have that
\mathrm{tr}\big(f(A),D^{\prime},n\big)=\mathrm{tr}\big(f(A^{\prime}),D^{\prime},n\big)
. Indeed, let
n\geq n_{0}
and
A,A^{\prime}\leq D^{\prime}
so that there are some
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
s\in\mathcal{AR}
with
s=r_{k}(A)=r_{k}(A^{\prime})
and
\mathrm{depth}_{D}(s)=n
.
Since

and

, by (
4
) we have that

\mathrm{tr}_{\mathcal{T}}(f(A),D_{n},n)=\mathrm{tr}_{\mathcal{T}}(f(A^{\prime}),D_{n},n).

Once again, since
r_{n}(D^{\prime})=r_{n}(D_{n})
, we have that
\mathrm{tr}_{\mathcal{T}}(f(A),D^{\prime},n)=\mathrm{tr}_{\mathcal{T}}(f(A^{\prime}),D^{\prime},n).

We turn now to the construction of
(D_{n})_{n\geq n_{0}}
. For the base case, set
D_{n_{0}}:=D\in[\tau,D]
. For the inductive step, assume that
D_{n}
has been defined. We define
D_{n+1}
as follows.

Set
r=r_{n}(D_{n})
and let
s_{1},\ldots,s_{N}
be an enumeration of all

with
\mathrm{depth}_{D_{n}}(s)=n
. Notice that for all such

and therefore, by Axiom A.2.(1) in

6.
A combinatorial lemma

For any
n\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{0\}
, let
\mathcal{Q}(n)
denote the set of partitions of
n=\{0,1,\ldots,n-1\}
. We treat members of
\mathcal{Q}(n)
as equivalence relations on
\{0,1,\ldots,n-1\}
and let
\mathcal{Q}(n)
inherit the relation
\leq
from
\mathcal{E}_{\infty}^{\mathrm{fin}}
. For
m\leq n
, let

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}^{m}(n):=\{s\in\mathcal{Q}(n)\colon i<j<m\implies\neg\;i\;s\;j\}.

In words,
\mathcal{Q}^{m}(n)
denotes the set of partitions of
n
such that
0,1,\ldots,m-1
belong to different equivalence classes.
The following is the main result of this section.

Lemma 6.1
.

For every
m,M\in\mathbb{N}
there exists
M^{\prime}\geq m,M
such that the following holds: for every map
e\colon\mathcal{Q}(M^{\prime})\to\mathcal{Q}(M^{\prime})
, there exists
s\in\mathcal{Q}^{m}(M^{\prime})
such that for all
t\leq s
, either

\displaystyle e(t)\leq t\;\mathrm{or}\;e(t)\nleq s.

For the proof of Lemma
6.1
we will rely on a probabilistic argument: we will show that the “random”
s\in\mathcal{Q}^{m}(M^{\prime})
satisfies the desired property with non-zero probability. To avoid dealing with expressions that involve Stirling numbers of the second kind in our counting arguments, we will endow
\mathcal{Q}^{m}(M^{\prime})
with the uniform measure supported only on “equipartitions.” This allows us to state and prove the following more specific result that implies Lemma
6.1
.

For
k,N\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{0\}
, let
\mathcal{Q}_{k}(kN)
denote the set of partitions of
kN
into
k
classes each having
N
elements. For
m\leq k
, we let
\mathcal{Q}_{k}^{m}(kN):=\mathcal{Q}^{m}(kN)\cap\mathcal{Q}_{k}(kN)
.

Lemma 6.2
.

Consider any
m\in\mathbb{N}
and
k\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{0\}
with
m\leq k
. Then there exists
N^{\prime}\in\mathbb{N}
such that for all
N\geq N^{\prime}
, the following holds: for every map
e\colon\mathcal{Q}(kN)\to\mathcal{Q}(kN)
, there exists
s\in\mathcal{Q}_{k}^{m}(kN)
such that for all
t\leq s
, either

(5)

\displaystyle e(t)\leq t\;\mathrm{or}\;e(t)\nleq s.

It is clear that Lemma
6.1
follows from Lemma
6.2
.

Proof of Lemma
6.2
.

We first prove a claim to simplify our later calculations.

Claim 6.3
.

For all
k\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{0\}
, there exists
M\in\mathbb{N}
such that for all
N\geq M
, if
a_{1},a_{2},b_{1},b_{2}\in\mathbb{N}
satisfy
0\leq b_{i}\leq a_{i}\leq k
and
a_{i}\neq 0
for
i\in\{1,2\}
, we have

(6)

\displaystyle\frac{(a_{1}N-b_{1})!}{(a_{1}-b_{1})!}\cdot\frac{(a_{2}N-b_{2})!}{(a_{2}-b_{2})!}\leq\frac{((a_{1}+a_{2})N-(b_{1}+b_{2}))!}{((a_{1}+a_{2})-(b_{1}+b_{2}))!}.

Proof of Claim.

Observe that (
6
) holds for all
N>0
if
a_{1}=b_{1}
and
a_{2}=b_{2}
. Also note that there are finitely many choices of
a_{1},a_{2},b_{1},
and
b_{2}
satisfying the hypotheses. So it suffices to show that

\displaystyle R_{a_{1},a_{2},b_{1},b_{2}}:=\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{(a_{1}N-b_{1})!}{(a_{1}-b_{1})!}\cdot\frac{(a_{2}N-b_{2})!}{(a_{2}-b_{2})!}\cdot\frac{((a_{1}+a_{2})-(b_{1}+b_{2}))!}{((a_{1}+a_{2})N-(b_{1}+b_{2}))!}<1

for all
a_{1},a_{2},b_{1},b_{2}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
0<a_{1},a_{2}\leq k
,
b_{1}<a_{1}
, and
b_{2}\leq a_{2}
.

The following bound is a consequence of Stirling’s approximation formula: for any
a\in\mathbb{N}
and
b\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{0\}
with
a\leq b
, we have

(7)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{b+1}2^{bH(a/b)}\leq\binom{b}{a}\leq 2^{bH(a/b)},

where
H
denotes the binary entropy function

\displaystyle H\bigg(\frac{a}{b}\bigg)=-\frac{a}{b}\log_{2}\frac{a}{b}-\bigg(1-\frac{a}{b}\bigg)\log_{2}\bigg(1-\frac{a}{b}\bigg).

Alternatively, this bound can be derived directly as in
[
CT06
, Example 11.1.3]
. Using (
7
), we obtain

\displaystyle R_{a_{1},a_{2},b_{1},b_{2}}

\displaystyle=\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{(a_{1}N-b_{1})!}{(a_{1}-b_{1})!}\cdot\frac{(a_{2}N-b_{2})!}{(a_{2}-b_{2})!}\cdot\frac{((a_{1}+a_{2})-(b_{1}+b_{2}))!}{((a_{1}+a_{2})N-(b_{1}+b_{2}))!}

\displaystyle=\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{\binom{(a_{1}+a_{2})-(b_{1}+b_{2})}{a_{1}-b_{1}}}{\binom{(a_{1}+a_{2})N-(b_{1}+b_{2})}{a_{1}N-b_{1}}}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{((a_{1}+a_{2})-(b_{1}+b_{2}))H(\frac{a_{1}-b_{1}}{(a_{1}+a_{2})-(b_{1}+b_{2})})}\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{(a_{1}+a_{2})N-(b_{1}+b_{2})+1}{2^{((a_{1}+a_{2})N-(b_{1}+b_{2}))H(\frac{a_{1}N-b_{1}}{(a_{1}+a_{2})N-(b_{1}+b_{2})})}}.

Since
a_{1},a_{2}>0
, note that
\lim_{N\to\infty}H(\frac{a_{1}N-b_{1}}{(a_{1}+a_{2})N-(b_{1}+b_{2})})=H(\frac{a_{1}}{a_{1}+a_{2}})>0
. Because
\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{c_{1}N+d_{1}}{2^{c_{2}N+d_{2}}}=0
in general for
c_{2}>0
, we have
R_{a_{1},a_{2},b_{1},b_{2}}=0
. In particular,
R_{a_{1},a_{2},b_{1},b_{2}}<1
, as desired.
∎

Fix
M
as in the claim. Consider any
N\geq M
and any map
e\colon\mathcal{Q}(kN)\to\mathcal{Q}(kN)
. Let
B_{e}
consist of all pairs
(s,t)\in\mathcal{Q}_{k}^{m}(kN)\times\mathcal{Q}(kN)
such that
t\leq s
and (
5
) fails. It suffices to find
N
such that
|B_{e}|<|\mathcal{Q}_{k}^{m}(kN)|
for every map
e\colon\mathcal{Q}(kN)\to\mathcal{Q}(kN)
.

Consider any
t\in\mathcal{Q}(kN)
, and let
h_{t}^{e}:=t\wedge e(t)
, the partition of
kN
such that

\displaystyle i\;h_{t}^{e}\;j\iff i\;t\;j\text{ and }i\;e(t)\;j.

Note that
t\leq h_{t}^{e}
and
e(t)\leq h_{t}^{e}
. Fix any
t
for which there exists
s\in\mathcal{Q}_{k}^{m}(kN)
such that
(s,t)\in B_{e}
. Then observe that
t\lneq h_{t}^{e}
. Hence for such
t
, for any
s\in\mathcal{Q}_{k}^{m}(kN)
,

(8)

\displaystyle(s,t)\in B_{e}\iff\big(t\leq s\text{ and }e(t)\leq s\big)\iff h_{t}^{e}\leq s.

Let

\displaystyle r_{t}^{e}:=\frac{|\{s\in\mathcal{Q}_{k}^{m}(kN)\colon h_{t}^{e}\leq s\}|}{|\{s\in\mathcal{Q}_{k}^{m}(kN)\colon t\leq s\}|}.

We will bound
|B_{e}|
by bounding
r_{t}^{e}
for all such
t
and using (
8
).

Write
t=\{X_{1},X_{2},\ldots X_{u}\}
. Since
t
is a coarsening of some
s\in\mathcal{Q}_{k}^{m}(kN)
, we can find positive
k_{1},k_{2},\ldots,k_{u}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
|X_{i}|=k_{i}N
for each
1\leq i\leq u
. For each
i
, let
m_{i}:=|X_{i}\cap m|
. Observe that
\sum_{1\leq i\leq u}k_{i}=k
,
\sum_{1\leq i\leq u}m_{i}=m
, and
m_{i}\leq k_{i}
for each
i
. Now write
h_{t}^{e}=\{X_{i,j}\colon 1\leq i\leq u,1\leq j\leq\ell_{i}\}
such that for each
i
,
\bigcup_{1\leq j\leq\ell_{i}}X_{i,j}=X_{i}
. Since we are assuming there exists
s\in\mathcal{Q}_{k}^{m}(kN)
with
(s,t)\in B_{e}
, we know from (
8
) that
h_{t}^{e}\leq s
for some
s\in\mathcal{Q}_{k}^{m}(kN)
. So for each
1\leq i\leq u
and
1\leq j\leq\ell_{i}
, we can write
|X_{i,j}|=k_{i,j}N
. Let
m_{i,j}=|X_{i,j}\cap m|
. Note that for each
i
,
\sum_{1\leq j\leq\ell_{i}}k_{i,j}=k_{i}
and
\sum_{1\leq j\leq\ell_{i}}m_{i,j}=m_{i}
. Also,
m_{i,j}\leq k_{i,j}
for all
i,j
since
h_{t}^{e}\leq s
for some
s\in\mathcal{Q}_{k}^{m}(kN)
.

We now bound
r_{t}^{e}
. Evaluating the denominator, we have

\displaystyle|\{s\in\mathcal{Q}_{k}^{m}(kN)\colon t\leq s\}|

\displaystyle=\prod_{1\leq i\leq u}\frac{(k_{i}N-m_{i})!}{(N-1)!^{m_{i}}\cdot N!^{k_{i}-m_{i}}\cdot(k_{i}-m_{i})!}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(N-1)!^{m}\cdot N!^{k-m}}\prod_{1\leq i\leq u}\frac{(k_{i}N-m_{i})!}{(k_{i}-m_{i})!}.

Similarly, evaluating the numerator of
r_{t}^{e}
yields

\displaystyle|\{s\in\mathcal{Q}_{k}^{m}(kN)\colon h_{t}^{e}\leq s\}|

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(N-1)!^{m}\cdot N!^{k-m}}\prod_{1\leq i\leq u}\prod_{1\leq j\leq\ell_{i}}\frac{(k_{i,j}N-m_{i,j})!}{(k_{i,j}-m_{i,j})!}.

Since
N\geq M
, we can apply the claim to obtain

\displaystyle|\{s\in\mathcal{Q}_{k}^{m}(kN)\colon h_{t}^{e}\leq s\}|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{(N-1)!^{m}\cdot N!^{k-m}}\prod_{1\leq i\leq u}\frac{(k_{i,1}N-m_{i,1})!}{(k_{i,1}-m_{i,1})!}\cdot\frac{((k_{i}-k_{i,1})N-(m_{i}-m_{i,1}))!}{((k_{i}-k_{i,1})-(m_{i}-m_{i,1}))!}.

So we have

\displaystyle r_{t}^{e}

\displaystyle\leq\prod_{1\leq i\leq u}\frac{\binom{k_{i}-m_{i}}{k_{i,1}-m_{i,1}}}{\binom{k_{i}N-m_{i}}{k_{i,1}N-m_{i,1}}}.

Observe that if

\displaystyle\leq\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i\leq u\\

(9)

\displaystyle\leq\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i\leq u\\
k_{i,1}<k_{i}\end{subarray}}\frac{2^{(k_{i}-m_{i})H(\frac{k_{i,1}-m_{i,1}}{k_{i}-m_{i}})}}{\frac{1}{k_{i}N-m_{i}+1}2^{(k_{i}N-m_{i})H(\frac{k_{i,1}N-m_{i,1}}{k_{i}N-m_{i}})}},

, and

for each

for each

(2)

(3)

for each

For each such choice of these values, the limit of (

is 0. Thus, we can fix

and

Now consider any

\displaystyle\frac{|B_{e}|}{|\{(s,t)\in\mathcal{Q}_{k}^{m}(kN)\times\mathcal{Q}(kN)\colon t\leq s\}|}

\displaystyle=\frac{\sum_{t\in\mathcal{Q}(kN)}|\{s\in\mathcal{Q}_{k}^{m}(kN)\colon(s,t)\in B_{e}\}|}{\sum_{t\in\mathcal{Q}(kN)}|\{s\in\mathcal{Q}_{k}^{m}(kN)\colon t\leq s\}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}t\in\mathcal{Q}(kN)\\
\exists s(s,t)\in B_{e}\end{subarray}}|\{s\in\mathcal{Q}_{k}^{m}(kN)\colon h_{t}^{e}\leq s\}|}{\sum_{t\in\mathcal{Q}(kN)}|\{s\in\mathcal{Q}_{k}^{m}(kN)\colon t\leq s\}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}t\in\mathcal{Q}(kN)\\
\exists s(s,t)\in B_{e}\end{subarray}}|\{s\in\mathcal{Q}_{k}^{m}(kN)\colon h_{t}^{e}\leq s\}|}{\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}t\in\mathcal{Q}(kN)\\

\displaystyle<\frac{1}{|\mathcal{Q}(k)|}

\displaystyle|B_{e}|

\displaystyle<\frac{1}{|\mathcal{Q}(k)|}\cdot|\{(s,t)\in\mathcal{Q}_{k}^{m}(kN)\times\mathcal{Q}(kN)\colon t\leq s\}|

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{Q}(k)|}\sum_{s\in\mathcal{Q}_{k}^{m}(kN)}|\{t\in\mathcal{Q}(kN)\colon t\leq s\}|

Hence, there exists

\mathcal{E}_{\infty}

We now prove Theorem

Theorem 7.1

The proof of Theorem

Let

\mathcal{D}\subseteq\mathcal{E}_{\infty}

Proof of Theorem

(

8.

In this section we sketch how our arguments adapt to prove

Theorem 8.1

(Kanovei-Sabok-Zapletal
[

]
)
.

\mathcal{S}_{\infty}(2^{<\mathbb{N}})

\mathcal{D}\subseteq\mathcal{S}_{\infty}(2^{<\mathbb{N}})

Let

Lemma 8.3

For every

there is some

e(F)\subseteq F\;\mathrm{or}\;e(F)\not\subseteq E.

Proof Sketch.

Let

such that the conclusion of the lemma holds for every

(k,N)
-partition

(k,N)
-partitions. Indeed, let

(k,N)

, and set

and

. Consider the map
\widehat{e}\colon\mathcal{T}(Q)\to\mathcal{P}(Z)

or
e(F)\not\subseteq E

So fix
N\in\mathbb{N}
and let

be a partition of some set

\displaystyle\frac{|\mathcal{B}|}{|\mathcal{CT}(P)|}\leq\frac{\sum_{m\leq k}{k\choose m}N^{m}\big(N^{k-m}-(N-1)^{k-m}\big)}{N^{k}}\leq

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{m\leq k}{k\choose m}\frac{N^{k}-(N-1)^{k}}{N^{k}}=2^{k}\big(1-\big((N-1)/N\big)^{k}\big).

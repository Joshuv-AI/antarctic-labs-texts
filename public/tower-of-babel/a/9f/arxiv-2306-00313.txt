1.
Introduction

Along the way of developing the theory of mixed Hodge modules in
[
23
,
24
]
, Saito proved a vanishing theorem
[
24
]
*2.g that can be understood as a far-reaching generalization of the Kodaira vanishing theorem, which plays a crucial role in algebraic geometry in characteristic 0. Almost all of the robust vanishing theorems involving ample line bundles are special cases of Saito vanishing. For example, if we apply the theorem to the trivial variation of Hodge structures on a smooth projective variety, then we recover the classical Kodaira–Akizuki–Nakano vanishing. Saito’s vanishing theorem concerns polarizable mixed Hodge modules for which key examples are provided by polarizable variations of Hodge structures. The theorem says the following:

Theorem 1.1
.

[
24
]
*2.g
Let
X
be a complex projective variety and let
\mathcal{M}\in\MHM(X)
be a polarizable Hodge module on
X
. For an ample line bundle
\mathcal{L}
on
X
, we have

\mathbb{H}^{l}\big(X,\gr_{p}\DR_{X}(\mathcal{M})\otimes\mathcal{L}\big)=0\qquad\textnormal{for all }l>0,p\in\mathbb{Z}.

In this article, we provide a proof of Saito’s vanishing theorem for mixed Hodge modules using analytic methods. We also mention that the proof works in a more general setup, by allowing complex coefficients, since our analytic method has nothing to do with the underlying
\mathbb{Q}
-structure.

We describe our general strategy for the proof of Saito’s vanishing theorem. First, we improve the result by
[
11
]
and prove a vanishing theorem (Theorem
1.3
) for a certain logarithmic de Rham complex associated to a variation of Hodge structures defined on the complement of a simple normal crossing (SNC) divisor. After that, we reduce Saito’s vanishing theorem to Theorem

using the structure theory for complex Hodge modules. We summarize the three main advantages of this argument. First, the proof of Theorem

In fact, analytic arguments naturally allow us to perturb a line bundle by an SNC divisor, leading to a Kawamata–Viehweg type vanishing theorem. Lastly, the general case of Saito vanishing almost immediately follows via the structure theory of Hodge modules and by resolving singularities.

1.1.
Vanishing theorem for logarithmic de Rham complexes

We give a vanishing theorem for logarithmic de Rham complexes for complex variations of Hodge structures defined on the complement of an SNC divisor. This is a key ingredient towards the proof of Saito’s vanishing theorem. We consider a compact Kähler manifold
\overline{X}
and an SNC divisor
D=\sum_{i=1}^{\nu}D_{i}
on
\overline{X}
. For a complex polarizable variation of Hodge structures
E
on the open locus
X=\overline{X}\setminus D
, we consider the Deligne extension
E_{\alpha}
, for
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}^{\nu}
, whose eigenvalues of the residue along
D_{i}
lie inside the interval
[-\alpha_{i},-\alpha_{i}+1)
. By the nilpotent orbit theorem, the graded pieces
E^{p,q}
also extend to vector bundles
E_{\alpha}^{p,q}
on
\overline{X}
. The graded piece of the logarithmic de Rham complex
\gr^{p}\DR_{(\overline{X},D)}(E_{\alpha})
is defined as follows:

\Big[E_{\alpha}^{p,q}\to\Omega_{\overline{X}}^{1}(\log D)\otimes E_{\alpha}^{p-1,q+1}\to\cdots\to\Omega_{\overline{X}}^{n}(\log D)\otimes E_{\alpha}^{p-n,q+n}\Big][n].

Note that this complex lives in cohomological degree between
-n
and
0
. In this situation, we have the following vanishing theorem:

Theorem 1.2
.

With the above notation, let
\mathcal{L}
be a line bundle on
\overline{X}
. Assume that
\mathcal{L}+\sum_{i=1}^{\nu}\alpha_{i}D_{i}
has a smooth hermitian metric whose curvature is positive semi-definite, and has at least
n-t
positive eigenvalues at each point
x\in\overline{X}
. If
B
is a nef line bundle on
\overline{X}
, then we have the following vanishing:

\mathbb{H}^{l}\left(\overline{X},\gr^{p}\Big(\DR_{(\overline{X},D)}(E_{\alpha})\Big)\otimes\mathcal{L}\otimes B\right)=0\qquad\textnormal{ for all }l>t,p\in\mathbb{Z}.

The curvature condition on
\mathcal{L}+\sum_{i=1}^{\nu}\alpha_{i}D_{i}
seems somewhat complicated, but a typical situation to get this condition is when the line bundle is the pull-back of an ample one by a smooth proper morphism
f\colon X\to A
, where
\dim A=n-t
. This is a similar generalization to that of Kodaira vanishing given by Girbau
[
14
]
. For convenience, we also give a simpler statement which is algebraic and is closer to that of the classical Saito vanishing theorem.

Theorem 1.3
.

With the same notation as in Theorem
1.2
, if
\mathcal{L}+\sum_{i=1}^{\nu}\alpha_{i}D_{i}
is ample, then

\mathbb{H}^{l}\left(\overline{X},\gr^{p}\Big(\DR_{(\overline{X},D)}(E_{\alpha})\Big)\otimes\mathcal{L}\right)=0\qquad\textnormal{ for all }l>0,p\in\mathbb{Z}.

Proof.
Let
B
be the trivial bundle. The ampleness condition corresponds to the case
t=0
in Theorem
1.2
.
\square

As an immediate corollary, we obtain the vanishing theorem in
[
15
,
2
]
, which is a generalization of log-Nakano vanishing where we allow an extra perturbation by an SNC divisor, as a direct corollary of Theorem
1.3
.

Corollary 1.4
.

Let
X
be a projective complex manifold of dimension
n
and let
\mathcal{L}
be a line bundle and
D=\sum_{i=1}^{\nu}D_{i}
be a reduced SNC divisor on
X
. If
\mathcal{L}+\sum_{i=1}^{\nu}\alpha_{i}D_{i}
is ample for some
0\leq\alpha_{i}\leq 1
, then

\displaystyle H^{q}(X,\Omega_{X}^{p}(\log D)\otimes\mathcal{L})=0

\displaystyle\qquad\textnormal{for }p+q>n.

Proof.
Apply Theorem
1.3
for the trivial variation of Hodge structures on the complement
X\setminus D
.
\square

1.2.
Comparison with previous results

We now compare our result with several related previous results. The most similar results and ideas can be found in
[
11
]
. The main theorem of that paper discusses vanishing theorems for tame harmonic bundles with nilpotent residues. The key input of the proof is the bound for the Higgs field near the SNC divisor. Then one can build a resolution of the logarithmic de Rham complex by fine sheaves, which is essentially the Dolbeault resolution but allowing measurable coefficients with certain
L^{2}
-conditions. The chain map is given by
\overline{\partial}+\theta
and the authors use appropriate
L^{2}
-estimates to solve the
(\overline{\partial}+\theta)
-equation.

Theorem
1.2
generalizes
[
11
]
in the case of a variation of Hodge structures. The main difference is that we get vanishing for a family of sheaves parameterized by
\mathbb{R}^{\nu}
, and assuming some positivity for the line bundle with an
\mathbb{R}
-twist
\mathcal{L}+\sum_{i=1}^{\nu}\alpha_{i}D_{i}
. This can be done by a careful construction of the Kähler metric on the open locus and by relating certain sheaves given by
L^{2}
-conditions and the Deligne extension. The result in
[
11
]
can be viewed as the case when
\alpha=0\in\mathbb{R}^{\nu}
. However, the statement in
[
11
]
is stronger since they work with tame harmonic bundles with nilpotent residues, a more general notion than variations of Hodge structures. However, we would like to stress that allowing a perturbation of
\alpha
is extremely important towards the proof of the Saito vanishing since it allows us to resolve singularities and use the standard perturbation trick to get positivity of the line bundle on the resolution.

We also compare our results with of that in
[
19
]
,
[
30
]
, and
[
33
]
which are Kawamata–Viehweg type generalizations of Saito vanishing. The results in
[
19
]
*§11 are somewhat orthogonal to ours since the author considers the situation when the line bundle
\mathcal{L}
is big and nef, while we discuss the issue of perturbing by an extra SNC divisor. The result in
[
30
]
is closely related to ours since it considers vanishing of a logarithmic de Rham complex when we have a perturbation by an SNC divisor. The main result
[
30
]
*Theorem 1 can be deduced from Theorem
1.3
of ours. The author shows the assertion when
\alpha=-(\epsilon,\ldots,\epsilon)
(with appropriate duality applied), under the assumption that
\mathcal{L}
is big and nef, and
\mathcal{L}+\sum_{i=1}^{\nu}\alpha_{i}D_{i}
is ample for all
0<-\alpha_{i}\ll 1
. The assumption of our result is weaker in the sense that we only need a single
\alpha
with
E_{\alpha}=E_{-\epsilon}
and
\mathcal{L}+\sum_{i=1}^{\nu}\alpha_{i}D_{i}
is ample and do not impose any positivity condition on
\mathcal{L}
itself. The vanishing result in
[
33
]
*Theorem 1.5 generalizes
[
19
]
and
[
30
]
simultaneously. The theorem achieves a general version of our main theorem by weakening the positivity condition to
\mathcal{L}+\sum\alpha_{i}D_{i}
being big and nef and showing the vanishing for the lowest graded piece. In general, vanishings for the higher graded pieces are no longer true for big and nef line bundles. We also mention a related result for twistor

.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Complex Polarized Variation of Hodge Structures

We begin by reviewing some basic definitions and notation related to complex variations of Hodge structures. Let
E
be a smooth vector bundle on a complex manifold
X
. We denote by
\mathcal{A}_{X}^{r,s}(E)
the sheaf of smooth differentiable
(r,s)
-forms with values in
E
. A complex variation of Hodge structures on
X
with weight
k
is a smooth vector bundle
E
on
X
with a decomposition

E=\bigoplus_{p+q=k}E^{p,q}

by smooth vector bundles and a flat connection
\nabla\colon E\to\mathcal{A}_{X}^{1}(E)
that maps each
\mathcal{A}_{X}(E^{p,q})
into

\mathcal{A}_{X}^{1,0}(E^{p,q})\oplus\mathcal{A}_{X}^{1,0}(E^{p-1,q+1})\oplus\mathcal{A}_{X}^{0,1}(E^{p,q})\oplus\mathcal{A}_{X}^{0,1}(E^{p+1,q-1}).

We define the filtration on
E
as
F^{p}E=\bigoplus_{p^{\prime}\geq p}E^{p^{\prime},k-p^{\prime}}
. The four components of the connection extend naturally to four operators

\displaystyle\nabla^{1,0}\colon\mathcal{A}_{X}^{r,s}(E^{p,q})\to\mathcal{A}_{X}^{r+1,s}(E^{p,q})

\displaystyle\theta:\mathcal{A}_{X}^{r,s}(E^{p,q})\to\mathcal{A}_{X}^{r+1,s}(E^{p-1,q+1})

\displaystyle\overline{\partial}\colon\mathcal{A}_{X}^{r,s}(E^{p,q})\to\mathcal{A}_{X}^{r,s+1}(E^{p,q})

\displaystyle\varphi\colon\mathcal{A}_{X}^{r,s}(E^{p,q})\to\mathcal{A}_{X}^{r,s+1}(E^{p+1,q-1}).

A polarization on
E
is a sesquilinear pairing
Q\colon E\otimes\overline{E}\to\mathcal{A}_{X}
such that

•

Q
is compatible with
\nabla
in the sense that

\nabla Q(u,v)=Q(\nabla u,v)+Q(u,\nabla v)

for all smooth sections
u,v
of
E
, and

•

The summands
E^{p,q}
of the decomposition are mutually orthogonal to each other, and

•

h(v,w)=\sum_{p+q=k}(-1)^{q}Q(v^{p,q},w^{p,q})
is positive definite.

We denote the hermitian metric on
E
by
h_{E}
. We will sometimes omit the subscript if there is no possibility for confusion. Note that the vector bundles
E
,
F^{p}E
, and
E^{p,q}
have holomorphic structures by the
(0,1)
-part of the connection, but the decomposition
E=\bigoplus E^{p,q}
does not preserve the holomorphic structures. However,
F^{\bullet}E
are holomorphic subbundles of
E
. This is essentially because of the presence of the Higgs field, which is the operator
\theta
. We say that
(E,\nabla,F^{\bullet},Q)
is a complex polarized variation of Hodge structures if the above properties are satisfied. However, we will simply say that
E
is a complex polarized variation of Hodge structures, without specifying the connection, filtration, and the hermitian paring if there is no possibility for confusion. By the flatness of connection
\nabla^{2}=0
and by type analysis, we have the following identities.

Lemma 2.1
.

The operators
\nabla^{1,0}
,
\overline{\partial}
,
\theta
,
\varphi
satisfy the following identities:

\displaystyle(\nabla^{1,0})^{2}=\theta^{2}=\overline{\partial}^{2}=\varphi^{2}=0

\displaystyle\nabla^{1,0}\theta+\theta\nabla^{1,0}=\overline{\partial}\varphi+\varphi\overline{\partial}=0

\displaystyle\overline{\partial}\theta+\theta\overline{\partial}=\nabla^{1,0}\varphi+\varphi\nabla^{1,0}=0

\displaystyle\nabla^{1,0}\overline{\partial}+\overline{\partial}\nabla^{1,0}+\theta\varphi+\varphi\theta=0.

Here are some more basic facts that will be used later. The proofs can be found in a self-contained manner in
[
22
]
*§47 - §50.

Lemma 2.2
.

(1)

For all smooth sections
u,v\in E
, we have

h_{E}(\theta u,v)=h_{E}(u,\varphi v).

(2)

For each
E^{p,q}
, the connection
\nabla^{1,0}+\overline{\partial}
is the metric connection with respect to
h
.

(3)

The curvature of the hermitian bundle
E^{p,q}
is equal to
-(\theta\varphi+\varphi\theta)
.
\square

Remark 2.3
.

We mention some notational differences from
[
22
]
. This is mainly because we wanted to reserve the notation
\theta^{*}
for the adjoint of the operator of
\theta
when
X
has a Kähler metric.

2.2.
Bound for the Norm of Higgs field

We introduce some useful terminology to describe the analysis near a simple normal crossing divisor.

Definition 2.4

(Admissible Coordinates)
.

Let
X
be a complex manifold and let
D
be a reduced simple normal crossing divisor on
X
. Let
x
be a point of
X
. An admissible coordinate centered at
x
is a tuple
(\Omega;z_{1},\ldots,z_{n})
where

•

\Omega
is an open subset of
X
containing
x
.

•

(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n})
is a coordinate system on
\Omega
centered at
x
, which gives a holomorphic isomorphism of
\Omega
with
\Delta^{n}=\{(\zeta_{1},\ldots,\zeta_{n})\in\mathbb{C}^{n}:|\zeta_{j}|<1\}
.

•

D\cap\Omega
is given by the equation
z_{1}\cdots z_{l}=0
for some
l\leq n
.

We shall write
\Omega^{*}=\Omega\setminus D
.

We define a Poincaré type metric
\omega_{\Poin}
on
(\Delta^{*})^{l}\times\Delta^{n-l}
by

\omega_{\Poin}=i\sum_{j=1}^{l}\frac{dz_{j}\wedge d\overline{z}_{j}}{|z_{j}|^{2}(-\log|z_{j}|^{2})^{2}}+\sum_{k=l+1}^{n}idz_{k}\wedge d\overline{z}_{k}.

Here,
\Delta=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|<1\}
is the unit disk and
\Delta^{*}=\Delta\setminus\{0\}
is the punctured disk.

For a variation of Hodge structures
E
on
(\Delta^{*})^{l}\times\Delta^{n-l}
, we have the following crucial norm estimate for its Higgs field
\theta
proved in
[
29
]
for the one-dimensional case and
[
18
]
for the general case. Its proof follows from a clever use of Ahlfors lemma.

Theorem 2.5

(Mochizuki)
.

If
E
is a variation of Hodge structures on
X=\overline{X}\setminus D
, then every point
x\in D
has an admissible coordinate
(\Omega;z_{1},\ldots,z_{n})
centered at
x
such that

|\theta|_{h,\omega_{\Poin}}^{2}\leq C

holds on
\Omega^{*}
for some constant
C>0
.
\square

Remark 2.6
.

We will use this constant
C
such that
|\theta u|_{h,\omega_{\Poin}}^{2}\leq C|u|_{h,\omega_{\Poin}}^{2}
for all
(r,s)
-forms valued in
E
. The original statement in
[
18
]
says that we can get a constant
C_{0}
such that if we write
\theta=\sum\theta_{j}dz_{j}
, then

\displaystyle|\theta_{i}|_{h,\omega}^{2}\leq\frac{C_{0}}{|z_{i}|^{2}(-\log|z_{i}|^{2})^{2}}\quad\text{for }1\leq i\leq l,\quad\text{and}

\displaystyle|\theta_{i}|_{h,\omega}^{2}\leq C_{0}\quad\text{for }l+1\leq i\leq n,

where
\omega
is the usual Euclidean metric. We can get the bound
|\theta u|_{h,\omega_{\Poin}}^{2}\leq C|u|_{h,\omega_{\Poin}}^{2}
for all
(r,s)
-forms
u
by multiplying
C_{0}
by an extra constant only depending on the dimension
n
.

2.3.
Prolongation via Norm Growth and the Nilpotent Orbit Theorem

We address the key consequences of the nilpotent orbit theorem (
[
25
,
5
]
for integral variations of Hodge structures and
[
22
,
12
]
for complex variations) and the asymptotics of the Hodge norms. In particular, we relate Deligne’s extension and the prolongation bundles via the growth of Hodge norms. We briefly summarize the relation between these two objects following
[
12
]
.

Definition 2.7

(Prolongation Bundles)
.

Let
\overline{X}
be a complex manifold and let
D=\sum_{i=1}^{\nu}D_{\nu}
be an SNC divisor on
\overline{X}
. Let
(E,h)
be a hermitian vector bundle on
X=\overline{X}\setminus D
. For each
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{\nu})\in\mathbb{R}^{\nu}
, we can prolong
E
to an
\mathcal{O}_{\overline{X}}
-module
\mathcal{P}_{\alpha}E
as follows. Let
(U;z_{1},\ldots,z_{n})
be an admissible coordinate and suppose that
D|_{U}
is defined by
z_{1}\cdots z_{l}=0
. For simplicity, assume that
D_{i}=(z_{i}=0)
. Then,

\mathcal{P}_{\alpha}E(U)=\left\{\sigma\in\Gamma(E,X\cap U):|\sigma|_{h}\lesssim\prod_{j=1}^{l}|z_{j}|^{-\alpha_{j}-\epsilon}\text{ on }U\text{ for all }\epsilon>0\right\}.

For two non-negative functions
f
and
g
, we write
f\lesssim g
on
U
if for each compact subset
K\subset U
, there exists some
C_{K}>0
such that
f\leq C_{K}g
on
K
.

We discuss several properties of prolongation bundles when
E
comes from a complex polarized variation of Hodge structures. The following theorem is proved in
[
18
]
in the language of acceptable bundles.

Theorem 2.8

(Mochizuki)
.

Let
\overline{X}
be a complex manifold and let
D=\sum_{i=1}^{\nu}D_{i}
be an SNC divisor on
\overline{X}
. Let
X=\overline{X}\setminus D
and
j\colon X\to\overline{X}
the open inclusion. Let
E
be a complex polarized variation of Hodge structures on
X
. For every
p,q
and every
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}^{\nu}
, the prolongation bundles
\mathcal{P}_{\alpha}E^{p,q}
satisfy the following properties:

(1)

\mathcal{P}_{\alpha}E^{p,q}
is a locally free sheaf.

(2)

\mathcal{P}_{\alpha}E\subset\mathcal{P}_{\beta}E
if
\alpha_{i}\leq\beta_{i}
for all
i
.

(3)

For
1\leq i\leq\nu
,
\mathcal{P}_{\alpha+e_{i}}E^{p,q}=\mathcal{P}_{\alpha}E^{p,q}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(D_{i})
where
e_{i}
is the
i
-th unit vector.

(4)

\mathcal{P}_{\alpha+\vec{\epsilon}}E^{p,q}=\mathcal{P}_{\alpha}E^{p,q}
for
\vec{\epsilon}=(\epsilon,\ldots,\epsilon)
for all
0<\epsilon\ll 1
.

(5)

The set
\{\alpha:\mathcal{P}_{\alpha}E/\mathcal{P}_{<\alpha}E\neq 0\}
is discrete in
\mathbb{R}^{\nu}
.
\square

We finally introduce the construction of Deligne’s extension and describe the relation to the prolongation via Hodge norms. Let
\overline{X}
be a complex manifold and
D
be an SNC divisor. Suppose we have a complex variation of Hodge structures
E
on
X=\overline{X}\setminus D
. Choose an admissible coordinate
(\Omega;z_{1},\ldots,z_{n})
and suppose that
D\cap\Omega
is given by the equation
z_{1}\cdots z_{l}=0
. The fundamental group
\pi_{1}(\Omega^{*})
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}^{l}
, with generators
\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{l}
, where
\gamma_{i}
corresponds to the loop going once around the puncture counterclockwise in the
i
-th coordinate. The universal cover of
\Omega^{*}
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{H}^{l}\times\Delta^{n-l}
where
\mathbb{H}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:\mathrm{Im}z>0\}
is the upper-half plane. The quotient map is given by

\pi\colon\mathbb{H}^{l}\times\Delta^{n-l}\to\Omega^{*},\qquad(w_{1},\ldots,w_{l},z_{l+1},\ldots,z_{n})\mapsto(e^{2\pi iw_{1}},\ldots,e^{2\pi iw_{l}},z_{l+1},\ldots,z_{n}).

Let
V
be the space of flat sections of
\pi^{*}E
on
\mathbb{H}^{l}\times\Delta^{n-l}
. Then we have the monodromy representation
\Phi\colon\pi_{1}(\Omega^{*})\to\GL(V)
given as follows.

\Phi(\gamma_{i})(v)(w_{1},\ldots,w_{l},z_{l+1},\ldots,z_{n})=v(w_{1},\ldots,w_{i}-1,\ldots,w_{l},z_{l+1},\ldots,z_{n}).

Denote by
T_{i}
as the image of
\gamma_{i}
. The monodromy theorem says that the eigenvalues of
T_{i}
have absolute value 1. Therefore, we can consider the Jordan decomposition

T_{j}=e^{2\pi i(S_{j}+N_{j})},

where
S_{j}
is a semisimple operator whose eigenvalues lie in the interval
[-\alpha_{j},-\alpha_{j}+1)
and
N_{j}
is a nilpotent operator. One can see that all
S_{j}
and
N_{j}
mutually commute since
\pi_{1}(\Omega^{*})
is abelian. For a flat section
v\in V
, consider the expression

\tilde{v}=\exp\left(\sum_{j=1}^{l}(S_{j}+N_{j})w_{j}\right)v.

Then we see that
\tilde{v}
is stable under the transformation
w_{j}\mapsto w_{j}+1
for all
1\leq j\leq l
hence,
\tilde{v}
descends to a section on
\Omega^{*}
. We define
E_{\alpha}
on
\Omega
to be the locally free sheaf generated by these sections
\tilde{v}
. For each subbundle
F^{p}E\subset E
, we define a filtration on
E_{\alpha}
by

F^{p}E_{\alpha}\colon=j_{*}F^{p}E\cap E_{\alpha}.

The nilpotent orbit theorem relates Deligne’s extension and the prolongation bundles via norm growth. Here, we use the version of
[
12
]
which is for complex polarized variations of Hodge structures.

Theorem 2.9

(Nilpotent Orbit Theorem
[
12
]
)
.

Let
\overline{X}
be a complex manifold and let
D=\sum_{i=1}^{\nu}D_{i}
be a simple normal crossing divisor on
\overline{X}
. Let
E
be a complex polarized variation of Hodge structures on
X=\overline{X}\setminus D
. Then for every
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}^{\nu}
,
F^{p}E_{\alpha}
and
E_{\alpha}^{p,q}=F^{p}E_{\alpha}/F^{p+1}E_{\alpha}
are locally free sheaves. Moreover,
E_{\alpha}^{p,q}
can be naturally identified to
\mathcal{P}_{\alpha}E^{p,q}
, which is the prolongation via growth of the Hodge norm.
\square

Note that the connection
\nabla\colon E\to\Omega_{X}^{1}\otimes E
extends to a logarithmic connection

\nabla\colon E_{\alpha}\to\Omega_{\overline{X}}^{1}(\log D)\otimes E_{\alpha}

and satisfies the Griffiths transversality condition, i.e., on each graded piece

induces an
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-linear map

\theta\colon E_{\alpha}^{p,q}\to\Omega_{\overline{X}}^{1}(\log D)\otimes E_{\alpha}^{p-1,q+1}.

This section is more or less standard, but we include it for more algebraically oriented readers. Let

be a Kähler manifold. We denote by

Proposition 2.10
.

Let

\displaystyle[\delta^{\prime\prime},L]=iD^{\prime}

\displaystyle=-iD^{\prime\prime}

\displaystyle=i\delta^{\prime\prime}.

Also, we have the Bochner-Kodaira-Nakano identity for the Laplacian operator:

We introduce a useful computation for the commutator operator

Lemma 2.12

3.2.
More Identities

We introduce more identities which exploit the curvature formula for Hodge bundles. Throughout this section, all identities will be considered as formal identities, which means that the identity holds for all smooth and compactly supported sections.

We fix a Kähler metric
\omega
on
X
. Since
\mathcal{A}_{X}^{r,s}
has a hermitian structure, the operators
\nabla^{1,0},\overline{\partial},\theta,
and
\varphi
have adjoints which we denote by
(\nabla^{1,0})^{*}
,
\overline{\partial}^{*}
,
\theta^{*}
and
\varphi^{*}
, respectively. These operators are of the following type

\displaystyle(\nabla^{1,0})^{*}

\displaystyle\colon\mathcal{A}_{X}^{r,s}(E^{p,q})\to\mathcal{A}_{X}^{r-1,s}(E^{p,q}),

\displaystyle\overline{\partial}^{*}

\displaystyle\colon\mathcal{A}_{X}^{r,s}(E^{p,q})\to\mathcal{A}_{X}^{r,s-1}(E^{p,q}),

\displaystyle\theta^{*}

\displaystyle\colon\mathcal{A}_{X}^{r,s}(E^{p,q})\to\mathcal{A}_{X}^{r-1,s}(E^{p+1,q-1}),

\displaystyle\varphi^{*}

\displaystyle\colon\mathcal{A}_{X}^{r,s}(E^{p,q})\to\mathcal{A}_{X}^{r,s-1}(E^{p-1,q+1}).

The first commutator relation is as follows.

Lemma 3.1
.

With the above notation, we have

[\Lambda,\varphi]=i\theta^{*}\qquad\textnormal{and}\qquad[\Lambda,\theta]=-i\varphi^{*}.

Proof.
All the operators are
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
-linear, hence we fix a point
x\in X
and calculate everything pointwise. Therefore, we diagonalize the Kähler form
\omega
and assume that
\omega=i\sum_{j=1}^{n}dz_{j}\wedge d\overline{z}_{j}
at
x
where
(z_{j})_{j=1}^{n}
is a coordinate system centered at
x
. For
J=(j_{1},\ldots,j_{p})
and
1\leq l\leq n
, we use the following notation

dz_{l\lrcorner J}=i_{\partial_{l}}(dz_{j_{1}}\wedge\cdots\wedge dz_{j_{p}})=\begin{cases}0&\textnormal{if }l\notin\{j_{1},\cdots,j_{p}\}\\
(-1)^{k-1}dz_{j_{1}}\wedge\cdots\wedge\widehat{dz_{j_{k}}}\wedge\cdots\wedge dz_{j_{p}}&\textnormal{if }l=j_{k}\end{cases}

and

dz_{jJ}=dz_{j}\wedge dz_{J}.

We define
d\overline{z}_{l\lrcorner K}
and
d\overline{z}_{kK}
analogously. Then for a
(p,q)
-form
u=\sum u_{J,K}dz_{J}\wedge d\overline{z}_{K}
, we have

\Lambda u=i(-1)^{|J|}\sum_{J,K,l}u_{J,K}dz_{l\lrcorner J}\wedge d\overline{z}_{l\lrcorner K}.

After writing
\theta=\left(\sum_{j}\theta_{j}dz_{j}\right)\wedge\bullet
, Lemma
2.2
implies that the operators
\theta,\theta^{*},\varphi,\varphi^{*}
satisfy the following formulas:

\displaystyle\theta(u)

\displaystyle=\sum_{J,K}\sum_{j\in J^{c}}\theta_{j}(u_{J,K})dz_{jJ}\wedge d\overline{z}_{K},

\displaystyle\theta^{*}(u)

\displaystyle=\sum_{J,K}\sum_{j\in J}\theta_{j}^{*}(u_{J,K})dz_{j\lrcorner J}\wedge d\overline{z}_{K},

\displaystyle\varphi(u)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{|J|}\sum_{J,K}\sum_{k\in K^{c}}\theta_{k}^{*}(u_{J,K})dz_{J}\wedge d\overline{z}_{kK},

\displaystyle\varphi^{*}(u)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{|J|}\sum_{J,K}\sum_{k\in K}\theta_{k}(u_{J,K})dz_{J}\wedge d\overline{z}_{k\lrcorner K}.

The rest of the proof is just brute force computation. If
u=\sum_{J,K}u_{J,K}dz_{J}\wedge d\overline{z}_{K}
, then

(1)

\begin{split}\Lambda\varphi(u)&=\Lambda\left((-1)^{|J|}\sum_{J,K}\sum_{k\in K^{c}}\theta_{k}^{*}(u_{J,K})dz_{J}\wedge d\overline{z}_{kK}\right)\\
&=i\Big[\sum_{J,K}\sum_{k\in J\setminus K}\theta_{k}^{*}(u_{J,K})dz_{k\lrcorner J}\wedge d\overline{z}_{K}\\
&\qquad+\sum_{J,K}\sum_{k\in K^{c}}\sum_{j\in J\cap K}\theta_{k}^{*}(u_{J,K})dz_{j\lrcorner J}\wedge d\overline{z}_{j\lrcorner kK}\Big].\end{split}

Also, we have

(2)

\begin{split}\varphi\Lambda(u)&=i(-1)^{|J|}\varphi\left(\sum_{J,K}\sum_{j\in J\cap K}u_{J,K}dz_{j\lrcorner J}\wedge d\overline{z}_{j\lrcorner K}\right)\\
&=-i\sum_{J,K}\sum_{j\in J\cap K}\Big[\theta_{j}^{*}(u_{J,K})dz_{j\lrcorner J}\wedge d\overline{z}_{K}\\
&\qquad+\sum_{k\in K^{c}}\theta_{k}^{*}(u_{J,K})dz_{j\lrcorner J}\wedge d\overline{z}_{k(j\lrcorner K)}\Big].\end{split}

Using
d\overline{z}_{j\lrcorner kK}=-d\overline{z}_{k(j\lrcorner K)}
, we see that the second terms in (
2
) and (
1
) cancel each other out after subtracting and we have

(\Lambda\varphi-\varphi\Lambda)(u)=i\sum_{J,K}\sum_{k\in J}\theta_{k}^{*}(u_{J,K})dz_{k\lrcorner J}\wedge d\overline{z}_{K}=i\theta^{*}(u).

This concludes the first part of the proof. We similarly obtain the second equality, as follows:

(3)

\begin{split}\theta\Lambda u&=i(-1)^{|J|}\theta\left(\sum_{J,K}\sum_{j\in J\cap K}u_{J,K}dz_{j\lrcorner J}\wedge d\overline{z}_{j\lrcorner K}\right)\\
&=i(-1)^{|J|}\Big[\sum_{J,K}\sum_{j\in J\cap K}\theta_{j}(u_{J,K})dz_{J}\wedge d\overline{z}_{j\lrcorner K}\\
&\qquad+\sum_{J,K}\sum_{j\in J\cap K}\sum_{k\in J^{c}}\theta_{k}(u_{J,K})dz_{k(j\lrcorner J)}\wedge d\overline{z}_{j\lrcorner K}\Big]\end{split}

and

(4)

\begin{split}\Lambda\theta u&=\Lambda\left(\sum_{J,K}\sum_{k\in J^{c}}\theta_{k}(u_{J,K})dz_{kJ}\wedge d\overline{z}_{K}\right)\\
&=i(-1)^{|J|+1}\Big[\sum_{J,K}\sum_{k\in J^{c}\cap K}\theta_{k}(u_{J,K})dz_{J}\wedge d\overline{z}_{k\lrcorner K}\\
&\qquad+\sum_{J,K}\sum_{k\in J^{c}}\sum_{j\in J\cap K}\theta_{k}(u_{J,K})dz_{j\lrcorner kJ}\wedge d\overline{z}_{j\lrcorner K}\Big].\end{split}

Again, using
dz_{k(j\lrcorner J)}=-dz_{j\lrcorner kJ}
, we see that the second terms in (
3
) and (
4
) cancel each other out after subtracting and we get

(\Lambda\theta-\theta\Lambda)u=i(-1)^{|J|+1}\sum_{J,K}\sum_{j\in K}\theta_{j}(u_{J,K})dz_{J}\wedge d\overline{z}_{j\lrcorner K}=-i\varphi^{*}(u).\qquad\square

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
E
be a variation of Hodge structures on a Kähler manifold
(X,\omega)
. If
\Theta_{E^{p,q}}
is the curvature operator of
E^{p,q}
with respect to the Hodge metric, then we have the following identity:

[i\Theta_{E^{p,q}},\Lambda]=-\theta^{*}\theta-\theta\theta^{*}+\varphi\varphi^{*}+\varphi^{*}\varphi.

Proof.
We compute

\displaystyle[i\Theta_{E^{p,q}},\Lambda]

\displaystyle=[-i(\theta\varphi+\varphi\theta),\Lambda]\qquad\because\text{Lemma }\ref{lemm-curvature}

\displaystyle=i(-\theta\varphi\Lambda-\varphi\theta\Lambda+\Lambda\theta\varphi+\Lambda\varphi\theta)

\displaystyle=i\Big((-\theta\varphi\Lambda+\theta\Lambda\varphi)+(-\varphi\theta\Lambda+\varphi\Lambda\theta)+(\Lambda\theta\varphi-\theta\Lambda\varphi)+(\Lambda\varphi\theta-\varphi\Lambda\theta)\Big)

\displaystyle=i(\theta[\Lambda,\varphi]+\varphi[\Lambda,\theta]+[\Lambda,\theta]\varphi+[\Lambda,\varphi]\theta)\qquad\because\text{Lemma }\ref{lemm-Higgs-comm}

\displaystyle=-\theta\theta^{*}+\varphi\varphi^{*}+\varphi\varphi^{*}-\theta^{*}\theta.\qquad\square

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
E
be a complex polarized variation of Hodge structures on
X
and let
\mathbb{E}
be the Dolbeault resolution for
\gr^{p}\DR_{X}(E)
as in Section
3.1
. Let
\mathcal{L}
be a line bundle on
X
with a smooth hermitian metric and let
\Theta_{\mathcal{L}}
be the curvature of
\mathcal{L}
. For
\mathbf{u}=(u^{(r)})\in\mathbb{E}^{l}\otimes\mathcal{L}
, where each
u^{(r)}\in\mathcal{A}_{X}^{r,n+l-r}(E^{p-r,q+r})\otimes\mathcal{L}
, we have the following formal identity:

\|\eth\mathbf{u}\|^{2}+\|\eth^{*}\mathbf{u}\|^{2}=\sum_{r}\langle[i\Theta_{\mathcal{L}},\Lambda]u^{(r)},u^{(r)}\rangle+\sum_{r}\|\nabla^{1,0}u^{(r)}+\varphi u^{(r+1)}\|^{2}+\sum_{r}\|\varphi^{*}u^{(r)}+(\nabla^{1,0})^{*}u^{(r+1)}\|^{2}.

Proof.
The expression
\langle\Delta_{\eth}\mathbf{u},\mathbf{u}\rangle
has the diagonal terms and the off-diagonal terms as follows:

\displaystyle\|\eth\mathbf{u}\|^{2}+\|\eth^{*}\mathbf{u}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{r}\langle\Delta_{\overline{\partial}}u^{(r)}+(\theta\theta^{*}+\theta^{*}\theta)u^{(r)},u^{(r)}\rangle

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{r}\langle(\theta\overline{\partial}^{*}+\overline{\partial}^{*}\theta)u^{(r)},u^{(r+1)}\rangle+\langle(\overline{\partial}\theta^{*}+\theta^{*}\overline{\partial})u^{(r+1)},u^{(r)}\rangle.

First, we examine the diagonal term. The classical Bochner–Kodaira–Nakano identity gives

\Delta_{\overline{\partial}}=\Delta_{\nabla^{1,0}}+[i\Theta_{E^{p,q}\otimes\mathcal{L}},\Lambda],

where
\nabla^{1,0}+\overline{\partial}
is the Chern connection for the bundle
E^{p,q}\otimes\mathcal{L}
. The curvature of
E^{p,q}\otimes\mathcal{L}
is given by

\Theta_{E^{p,q}\otimes\mathcal{L}}=\id_{E^{p,q}}\otimes\Theta_{\mathcal{L}}+\Theta_{E^{p,q}}\otimes\id_{\mathcal{L}}.

Summing all these term and using Lemma
3.2
, we have

\displaystyle\langle(\Delta_{\overline{\partial}}+\theta\theta^{*}+\theta^{*}\theta)u^{(r)},u^{(r)}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle[i\Theta_{\mathcal{L}},\Lambda]u^{(r)},u^{(r)}\rangle+\|\nabla^{1,0}u^{(r)}\|^{2}+\|(\nabla^{1,0})^{*}u^{(r)}\|^{2}+\|\varphi u^{(r)}\|^{2}+\|\varphi^{*}u^{(r)}\|^{2}.

We next examine the off-diagonal term. Note that

\langle(\theta\overline{\partial}^{*}+\overline{\partial}^{*}\theta)u^{(r)},u^{(r+1)}\rangle=\overline{\langle(\overline{\partial}\theta^{*}+\theta^{*}\overline{\partial})u^{(r+1)},u^{(r)}\rangle}

and we have

\displaystyle\overline{\partial}\theta^{*}+\theta^{*}\overline{\partial}

\displaystyle=-i(\overline{\partial}\Lambda\varphi-\overline{\partial}\varphi\Lambda+\Lambda\varphi\overline{\partial}-\varphi\Lambda\overline{\partial})\qquad\because\text{Lemma \ref{lemm-Higgs-comm}}

\displaystyle=(-i)\left(\Lambda\overline{\partial}\varphi+i(\nabla^{1,0})^{*}\varphi-\overline{\partial}\varphi\Lambda+\Lambda\varphi\overline{\partial}-\varphi\overline{\partial}\Lambda+i\varphi(\nabla^{1,0})^{*}\right)\qquad\because[\Lambda,\overline{\partial}]=-i(\nabla^{1,0})^{*}

\displaystyle=(\nabla^{1,0})^{*}\varphi+\varphi(\nabla^{1,0})^{*}\qquad\because\overline{\partial}\varphi+\varphi\overline{\partial}=0.

Therefore,

\langle u^{(r)},(\overline{\partial}\theta^{*}+\theta^{*}\overline{\partial})u^{(r+1)}\rangle=\langle\nabla^{1,0}u^{(r)},\varphi u^{(r+1)}\rangle+\langle\varphi^{*}u^{(r)},(\nabla^{1,0})^{*}u^{(r+1)}\rangle.

Adding all up, we get the desired identity.
\square

The following

-existence result will serve as a key tool for the proof of cohomology vanishing. The first proposition is a global version of the

-existence result. One can also find a similar result in

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
(X,\omega)
be a Kähler manifold (not necessarily compact) and let
\mathcal{L}
be a line bundle with smooth hermitian metric
h_{\mathcal{L}}
. Let
E
be a complex polarized variation of Hodge structures on
X
. Furthermore, we assume that

(1)

The geodesic distance
\delta_{\omega}
is complete on
X
.

(2)

The norm of the Higgs field
|\theta|_{h,\omega}^{2}
is globally bounded.

(3)

There exists
\epsilon>0
such that
\langle[i\Theta_{\mathcal{L}},\Lambda_{\omega}]u,u\rangle\geq\epsilon\|u\|_{\omega}^{2}
for all smooth and compactly supported
(r,s)
-forms
u
for
r+s=n+l
.

Let
\mathbf{u}
be a measurable section with values in
\mathbb{E}^{l}
such that
\eth\mathbf{u}=0
. Provided that the right hand side of the expression below is finite, there exists a measurable section
\mathbf{v}
with values in
\mathbb{E}^{l-1}
satisfying
\eth\mathbf{v}=\mathbf{u}
and the following inequality

\int_{X}\|\mathbf{v}\|_{h,\omega}^{2}dV_{\omega}\leq\frac{1}{\epsilon}\int_{X}\|\mathbf{u}\|_{h,\omega}^{2}dV_{\omega}.

Proof.
The only non-trivial part is to approximate measurable sections
\mathbf{u}
by smooth and compactly supported sections in a desirable way. Note that we have an a priori inequality
\|\eth\mathbf{u}\|^{2}+\|\eth^{*}\mathbf{u}\|^{2}\geq\epsilon\|\mathbf{u}\|^{2}
for smooth and compactly supported sections. Since
|\theta|_{h,\omega}^{2}
is globally bounded,

\theta\colon L^{2}(X,\mathcal{A}_{X}^{r,s}\otimes E^{p,q})\to L^{2}(X,\mathcal{A}_{X}^{r+1,s}\otimes E^{p,q})

is a bounded operator. Therefore, for
L^{2}
-sections
\mathbf{u}=(u^{(r)})
, the section
\eth\mathbf{u}
is
L^{2}
if and only if each derivative
\overline{\partial}u^{(r)}
is
L^{2}
. Similarly,
\eth^{*}\mathbf{u}
is
L^{2}
if and only if for each
r
, the section
\overline{\partial}^{*}u^{(r)}
is
L^{2}
. Moreover, provided a sequence
\mathbf{u}_{\nu}\to\mathbf{u}
in
L^{2}
, we have
\eth\mathbf{u}_{\nu}\to\eth\mathbf{u}
in
L^{2}
if and only if for each
r
,
\overline{\partial}u_{\nu}^{(r)}\to\overline{\partial}u^{(r)}
in
L^{2}
. Similarly,
\eth^{*}\mathbf{u}_{\nu}\to\eth^{*}\mathbf{u}
in
L^{2}
if and only if
\overline{\partial}^{*}u_{\nu}^{(r)}\to\overline{\partial}^{*}u^{(r)}
in
L^{2}
for each
r
. Since
(X,\omega)
is a complete Kähler manifold, the space of compactly supported smooth forms is dense in
\Dom(\overline{\partial})\cap\Dom(\overline{\partial}^{*})
under the graph norm

u\mapsto(\|u\|^{2}+\|\overline{\partial}u\|^{2}+\|\overline{\partial}^{*}u\|^{2})^{1/2}.

Therefore, the a priori inequality

\|\eth\mathbf{u}\|^{2}+\|\eth^{*}\mathbf{u}\|^{2}\geq\epsilon\|\mathbf{u}\|^{2}

also holds for measurable sections such that
\mathbf{u}
,
\eth\mathbf{u}
, and
\eth^{*}\mathbf{u}
are all
L^{2}
. The remaining part of the proof follows from Lemma
2.13
.
\square

Also, we have a local version of the

-Dolbeault resolution. The following result is motivated by

Proposition 3.5
.

Equip
\Omega^{*}=(\Delta^{*})^{l}\times\Delta^{n-l}
with the Poincaré metric

defined in Section

. Let
E
be a complex polarized variation of Hodge structures on
\Omega^{*}
. Let
C>0
be a number such that
\|\theta\|^{2}_{\omega_{\Poin}}<C
on
\Omega^{*}
. Define
\eta\colon\Omega^{*}\to\mathbb{R}
by the following formula, for
a_{j}\in\mathbb{R}
and
b_{j}>C+2
:

e^{-\eta}=\prod_{j=1}^{l}|z_{j}|^{2a_{j}}(-\log|z_{j}|^{2})^{b_{j}}\prod_{j=l+1}^{n}e^{-b_{j}|z_{j}|^{2}}.

If
u
is an
(r,s)
-form with values in
E^{p,q}
, with measurable coefficients such that
\overline{\partial}u=0
and

\int_{\Omega^{*}}\|u\|_{h,\omega_{\Poin}}^{2}e^{-\eta}dV_{\Poin}<+\infty,

then there exists an
(r,s-1)
-form with values in
E^{p,q}
, with measurable coefficients, such that
u=\overline{\partial}v
and

\int_{\Omega^{*}}\|v\|_{h,\omega_{\Poin}}^{2}e^{-\eta}dV_{\Poin}\leq\int_{\Omega^{*}}\|u\|_{h,\omega_{\Poin}}^{2}e^{-\eta}dV_{\Poin}.

Proof.
First, we prove the result for
(n,s)
-forms. A straightforward computation gives

i\partial\overline{\partial}\eta=i\sum_{j=1}^{l}\frac{b_{j}}{|z_{j}|^{2}(-\log|z_{j}|^{2})^{2}}dz_{j}\wedge d\overline{z}_{j}+i\sum_{j=l+1}^{n}b_{j}dz_{j}\wedge d\overline{z}_{j}.

We put
dz_{[n]}=dz_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge dz_{n}
. If
u=\sum_{K}u_{K}dz_{[n]}\wedge d\overline{z}_{K}
, we have

[i\partial\overline{\partial}\eta,\Lambda_{\omega_{\Poin}}]u=\sum_{K}\left(\sum_{j\in K}b_{j}\right)u_{K}dz_{[n]}\wedge d\overline{z}_{K}.

Using the Bochner–Kodaira–Nakano identity with a twist by
e^{-\eta}
, we have the a priori inequality

\displaystyle\|\overline{\partial}u\|^{2}+\|\overline{\partial}^{*}u\|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\langle[i\Theta_{E},\Lambda_{\omega_{\Poin}}]u,u\rangle+\langle[i\partial\overline{\partial}\eta,\Lambda_{\omega_{\Poin}}]u,u\rangle

\displaystyle\geq-\langle\theta\theta^{*}u,u\rangle+\langle[i\partial\overline{\partial}\eta,\Lambda_{\omega_{\Poin}}]u,u\rangle\qquad\because\text{Lemma }\ref{lemm-VHS-BKN}

\displaystyle\geq-C\|u\|^{2}+\langle[i\partial\overline{\partial}\eta,\Lambda_{\omega_{\Poin}}]u,u\rangle.

Here, the adjoint operator
\overline{\partial}^{*}
is computed with respect to the metric
h_{E}\cdot e^{-\eta}
on the Hodge bundles. In particular, if
b_{j}\geq C+1
for each
j
, then we have
\|\overline{\partial}u\|^{2}+\|\overline{\partial}^{*}u\|^{2}\geq\|u\|^{2}
. The assertion for
(n,s)
-forms now follows from Lemma
2.13
.
One technical part is that
\omega_{\Poin}
is not a complete metric on the domain
\Omega^{*}
. However, we can remedy this situation since
\Omega^{*}
is weakly pseudoconvex. This can be done by taking a plurisubharmonic exhaustion function
\psi\geq 0
of
\Omega^{*}
and considering the complete metric

\omega_{\Poin,\epsilon}=\omega_{\Poin}+i\epsilon\partial\overline{\partial}\psi^{2}.

Then we get the
L^{2}
-existence theorem for
\omega_{\Poin}
by applying the corresponding result for each
\omega_{\Poin,\epsilon}
and taking the weak limit. This is the strategy in
[
8
]
*Théorème 4.1.

We next prove the result for
(r,s)
-forms. Let
u=\sum_{J,K}u_{J,K}dz_{J}\wedge d\overline{z}_{K}
. We put

u_{(J)}=\sum_{K}u_{J,K}dz_{J}\wedge d\overline{z}_{K}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\tilde{u}_{(J)}=\sum_{K}u_{J,K}dz_{[n]}\wedge d\overline{z}_{K}.

Define
\eta^{\prime}
by

e^{-\eta^{\prime}}=e^{-\eta}\prod_{j\notin J,j\leq l}|z_{j}|^{-2}(-\log|z_{j}|^{2})^{-1}=\prod_{j=1}^{l}|z_{j}|^{2a_{j}^{\prime}}(-\log|z_{j}|^{2})^{b_{j}^{\prime}}\prod_{j=l+1}^{n}e^{-b_{j}^{\prime}|z_{j}|^{2}}.

Note that
\|u_{(J)}\|_{h,\omega_{\Poin}}^{2}e^{-\eta}=\|\tilde{u}_{(J)}\|_{h,\omega_{\Poin}}^{2}e^{-\eta^{\prime}}
. We have
\int_{\Omega^{*}}\|\tilde{u}_{(J)}\|_{h,\omega_{\Poin}}^{2}e^{-\eta^{\prime}}dV_{\Poin}<+\infty
. Since
b_{j}^{\prime}>C+1
, we can apply the result for
(n,s)
-forms so that there exists an
(n,s-1)
-form
\tilde{v}_{(J)}
such that
\overline{\partial}\tilde{v}_{(J)}=\tilde{u}_{(J)}
and

\int_{\Omega^{*}}\|\tilde{v}_{(J)}\|_{h,\omega_{\Poin}}^{2}e^{-\eta^{\prime}}dV_{\Poin}\leq\int_{\Omega^{*}}\|\tilde{u}_{(J)}\|_{h,\omega_{\Poin}}^{2}e^{-\eta^{\prime}}dV_{\Poin}.

Since wedging by holomorphic coordinate does not effect the operator
\overline{\partial}
(up to sign), there exists an
(r,s-1)
-form
v_{(J)}
such that
\overline{\partial}v_{(J)}=u_{(J)}
and

\int_{\Omega^{*}}\|v_{(J)}\|_{h,\omega_{\Poin}}^{2}e^{-\eta}dV_{\Poin}\leq\int_{\Omega^{*}}\|u_{(J)}\|_{h,\omega_{\Poin}}^{2}e^{-\eta}dV_{\Poin}.

At the end,
\sum_{J}v_{(J)}
gives the desired solution.
\square

3.4.
Construction of the Kähler metric on the complement

From now on, we work in the setting of the main theorem. Hence
D=\sum_{i=1}^{\nu}D_{i}
is an SNC divisor on a compact Kähler manifold
\overline{X}
, and
X=\overline{X}\setminus D
. We cover
D
with finitely many admissible coordinates
\{\Omega_{i}\}_{i\in I}
and use Theorem
2.5
to get a real number
C>0
such that
|\theta|_{h,\omega_{\Poin}}^{2}<C
for each admissible coordinate
\Omega_{i}
. Also,
\mathcal{L}
is a line bundle on
\overline{X}
such that
\mathcal{L}+\sum_{i=1}^{\nu}\alpha_{i}D_{i}
has a smooth hermitian metric with semi-positive curvature, and at each point
x\in\overline{X}
, the curvature has at least
n-t
positive eigenvalues. In addition,
B
is a nef line bundle on
\overline{X}
. The goal is to construct a Kähler metric
\omega
on the open locus
X
so that the following four conditions are satisfied:

(1)

(X,\omega)
is complete.

(2)

The norm of the Higgs field
|\theta|_{h,\omega}^{2}
is globally bounded.

(3)

If we write the curvature of
\mathcal{L}\otimes B
as
i\Theta_{\mathcal{L}\otimes B}
, then the operator
[i\Theta_{\mathcal{L}\otimes B},\Lambda_{\omega}]
should be positive definite for
(r,s)
-forms.

(4)

We can solve a local
\overline{\partial}
-equation in admissible coordinates with an appropriate twist.

The first three conditions will allow us to use the global
L^{2}
-existence result. Also, the last condition is crucial since we want to guarantee the exactness of a Dolbeault type complex. The correct guess based on the second and the fourth condition is that the Kähler metric
\omega
should somehow look like the Poincaré metric for each admissible coordinate (see Theorem
2.5
and Proposition
3.5
). However, we cannot get the positivity of the operator
[i\Theta_{\mathcal{L}\otimes B},\Lambda_{\omega}]
by using the metric that is naïvely constructed. The idea is to twist the metric of
\mathcal{L}
by a certain amount so that all conditions are true. The construction is essentially identical to
[
11
]
*§3.5, modulo the effect of the twist by
\sum\alpha_{i}D_{i}
.

Proposition 3.6
.

With the notation as above, there exists a Kähler metric
\omega
on
X
and smooth hermitian metrics
h_{\mathcal{L}}^{\prime}
on
\mathcal{L}|_{X}
and
h_{B}
on
B
satisfying the following:

(1)

(X,\omega)
is complete.

(2)

\omega
and
\omega_{\Poin}
are mutually bounded for each admissible coordinate
\Omega_{i}
.

(3)

|\theta|_{h,\omega}^{2}
is globally bounded.

(4)

If
\Theta_{\mathcal{L}\otimes B}^{\prime}
is the curvature associated to the metric
h_{\mathcal{L}}^{\prime}\otimes h_{B}
, then we have the inequality
\langle[i\Theta_{\mathcal{L}\otimes B}^{\prime},\Lambda_{\omega}]u,u\rangle\geq\frac{1}{10}|u|_{\omega}^{2}
for
(r,s)
-forms on
X
with
r+s>n+t
.

Proof.
First, we fix a Kähler metric
\omega_{0}
on
\overline{X}
. Choose hermitian metrics on
\mathcal{L}
and
\mathcal{O}_{\overline{X}}(D_{i})
and denote the corresponding curvatures (multiplied by
i
) by
\omega_{\mathcal{L}}
, and
\omega_{1},\cdots,\omega_{\nu}
. For each
x\in X
, we can simultaneously diagonalize
\omega_{0}
and
\omega_{\mathcal{L}}+\sum_{j=1}^{\nu}\alpha_{j}\omega_{j}
such that

\omega_{0}(x)=i\sum_{\mu}\zeta_{\mu}\wedge\bar{\zeta}_{\mu}\qquad\textnormal{and}\qquad\omega_{\mathcal{L}}+\sum_{j=1}^{\nu}\alpha_{j}\omega_{j}=i\sum_{\mu}\gamma_{\mu}(x)\zeta_{\mu}\wedge\bar{\zeta}_{\mu}.

The condition on the curvature of
\mathcal{L}+\sum_{j=1}^{\nu}\alpha_{j}D_{j}
guarantees that

0\leq\gamma_{1}(x)\leq\cdots\leq\gamma_{n}(x)

and
\gamma_{t}(x)>0
since there are at least
n-t
positive eigenvalues. Also, the
\gamma_{i}
are continuous functions. Let

m=\min_{x\in\overline{X}}\gamma_{t}(x)>0.

Choose
0<\epsilon_{1}<\epsilon
such that

t\cdot\frac{\epsilon_{1}-\epsilon}{\epsilon_{1}}+(s-t)\cdot\frac{m+(\epsilon_{1}-\epsilon)}{m+\epsilon_{1}}-(n-r)>\frac{1}{10}

for all
r+s>n+t
. This can be done since the above expression is identical to

(r+s-n-t)-\frac{\epsilon_{1}}{m+\epsilon_{1}}(s-t)-(\epsilon-\epsilon_{1})\left(\frac{t}{\epsilon_{1}}+\frac{s-t}{m+\epsilon_{1}}\right).

Let
2\epsilon_{2}=\epsilon-\epsilon_{1}>0
. Fix
\delta>0
such that

\omega_{0}+\sum_{j=1}^{\nu}\delta_{j}\omega_{j}>0\qquad\text{for all }\delta_{j}\in[-\delta,\delta].

Pick
\alpha_{j}<a_{j}<\alpha_{j}+\frac{\delta\epsilon_{2}}{2}
and
b_{j}=C+3
. Let
\sigma_{j}\in H^{0}(\overline{X},\mathcal{O}_{\overline{X}}(D_{j}))
that vanishes along
D_{j}
. Also, take
a_{j}
to be sufficiently close to
\alpha_{j}
so that
\mathcal{P}_{\alpha}E^{p,q}=\mathcal{P}_{a}E^{p,q}
. We can rescale
\sigma_{j}
so that
|\sigma_{j}|_{h_{j}}^{2}\leq\exp(-2(C+3)/\delta\epsilon_{2})
. Fix a smooth hermitian metric
h_{B}
on
B
so that
i\Theta_{B}+\epsilon_{2}\omega_{0}>0
. This can be done since
B
is a nef line bundle. Denote
\omega_{B}=i\Theta_{B}
. Let

\tau_{j}\colon X\to\mathbb{R},\qquad\tau_{j}(x)=-\log|\sigma_{j}|_{h_{j},x}^{2}

and let

\xi=\sum_{j=1}^{\nu}a_{j}\tau_{j}-b_{j}\log\tau_{j}.

Define the Kähler metric
\omega
on
X
as

\omega=\epsilon\omega_{0}+\omega_{\mathcal{L}}+\omega_{B}+i\partial\overline{\partial}\xi.

The computation of
\partial\overline{\partial}\xi
gives

i\partial\overline{\partial}\xi=\sum_{j=1}^{\nu}\left(a_{j}-\frac{b_{j}}{\tau_{j}}\right)\omega_{j}+\sum_{j=1}^{\nu}i\frac{b_{j}}{\tau_{j}^{2}}\partial\tau_{j}\wedge\overline{\partial}\tau_{j},

using
i\partial\overline{\partial}\tau_{j}=\omega_{j}
. Therefore, we can decompose
\omega
into five terms

\omega=\epsilon_{1}\omega_{0}+\left(\omega_{\mathcal{L}}+\sum_{j=1}^{\nu}\alpha_{j}\omega_{j}\right)+\epsilon_{2}\left(\omega_{0}+\sum_{j}\delta_{j}\omega_{j}\right)+(\epsilon_{2}\omega_{0}+\omega_{B})+i\sum_{j=1}^{\nu}\frac{b_{j}}{\tau_{j}^{2}}\partial\tau_{j}\wedge\overline{\partial}\tau_{j}\qquad\mathbf{(A)}.

Here, we have

\delta_{j}=\epsilon_{2}^{-1}\left(a_{j}-\alpha_{j}-\frac{b_{j}}{\tau_{j}}\right)\in[-\delta,\delta]

since
\epsilon_{2}^{-1}(a_{j}-\alpha_{j})\in[0,\delta/2]
and
\tau_{j}\geq 2(C+3)/\delta\epsilon_{2}
. Hence, we see that all the five terms are semi-positive and
\epsilon_{1}\omega_{0}>0
. Therefore,
\omega
is a Kähler form on
X
.

Now we prove that the metric
\omega
and
\omega_{\Poin}
are mutually bounded for each admissible coordinate
\Omega_{i}
. Clearly, this implies that
(X,\omega)
is complete. Fix an admissible coordinate
(\Omega;z_{1},\cdots,z_{n})
and assume that
D\cap\Omega
is defined by the equation
z_{1}\cdots z_{l}=0
. For convenience, suppose that
(z_{i}=0)=D_{i}\cap\Omega
. Note that
\tau_{i}=-\log|z_{i}|^{2}+g_{i}
for some smooth function
g_{i}
on
\Omega
. Then

\frac{b_{j}}{\tau_{j}^{2}}\partial\tau_{j}\wedge\overline{\partial}\tau_{j}=\frac{b_{j}}{(-\log|z_{j}|^{2}+g)^{2}}\left(\frac{dz_{j}}{z_{j}}-\partial g_{j}\right)\wedge\left(\frac{d\overline{z}_{j}}{\overline{z}_{j}}-\overline{\partial}g_{j}\right).

Since the first four terms on the right hand side of
(A)
are smooth on the entire space
\overline{X}
, we see that
\omega
and
\omega_{\Poin}
are mutually bounded on
\Omega
. This also shows that
|\theta|_{h,\omega}^{2}
is globally bounded.

The last thing to check is the positivity of the commutator operator
[i\Theta_{\mathcal{L}\otimes B},\Lambda_{\omega}]
. Here, the key idea is to twist the metric of
\mathcal{L}
by an extra factor of
e^{-\xi}
. This gives us a smooth hermitian metric
h_{\mathcal{L}}^{\prime}=h_{\mathcal{L}}e^{-\xi}
on
\mathcal{L}|_{X}
. We denote the corresponding curvature as
\Theta_{\mathcal{L}\otimes B}^{\prime}
. The formula for this curvature is

i\Theta_{\mathcal{L}\otimes B}^{\prime}=\omega_{\mathcal{L}}+\omega_{B}+i\partial\overline{\partial}\xi.

Simultaneously diagonalize
\omega_{0}
and
i\Theta_{\mathcal{L}\otimes B}^{\prime}
at
x\in X
and express

\omega_{0}=i\sum_{\mu}\zeta_{\mu}\wedge\bar{\zeta}_{\mu}\qquad\textnormal{and}\qquad i\Theta_{\mathcal{L}\otimes B}^{\prime}=i\sum_{\mu}\gamma_{\mu}^{\prime}(x)\zeta_{\mu}\wedge\bar{\zeta}_{\mu}.

Also, we assume
\gamma_{1}^{\prime}\leq\cdots\leq\gamma_{n}^{\prime}
. Using
(A)
and the fact that
\omega=\epsilon\omega_{0}+i\Theta_{\mathcal{L}\otimes B}^{\prime}
, we see that

\epsilon+\gamma_{\mu}^{\prime}(x)\geq\epsilon_{1}+\gamma_{\mu}(x).

If we diagonalize
i\Theta_{\mathcal{L}\otimes B}^{\prime}
with respect to
\omega
and denote the eigenvalues as
\gamma_{\mu,\epsilon}^{\prime}(x)
, then we have

\gamma_{\mu,\epsilon}^{\prime}=\frac{\gamma_{\mu}^{\prime}}{\gamma_{\mu}^{\prime}+\epsilon}.

Note that
\gamma_{\mu}^{\prime}(x)\geq\epsilon_{1}-\epsilon+\gamma_{\mu}(x)\geq\epsilon_{1}-\epsilon
. Also, if
\mu\geq t
, then we have the improved bound
\gamma_{\mu}^{\prime}(x)\geq\epsilon_{1}-\epsilon+m
. Hence, we have the following bounds for the quantities for
\gamma_{\mu,\epsilon}^{\prime}
:

\displaystyle 1\geq\gamma_{\mu,\epsilon}^{\prime}\geq\frac{\epsilon_{1}-\epsilon}{\epsilon_{1}}\qquad\text{ for }1\leq\mu\leq t-1,

\displaystyle 1\geq\gamma_{\mu,\epsilon}^{\prime}\geq\frac{m+\epsilon_{1}-\epsilon}{m+\epsilon_{1}}\qquad\text{ for }t\leq\mu\leq n.

Using Lemma
2.12
for
(r,s)
-forms
u
, we have

\displaystyle\langle[i\Theta_{\mathcal{L}\otimes B}^{\prime},\Lambda_{\omega}]u,u\rangle_{x}

\displaystyle\geq(\gamma_{1,\epsilon}^{\prime}(x)+\cdots+\gamma_{s,\epsilon}^{\prime}(x)-\gamma_{r+1,\epsilon}^{\prime}(x)-\cdots-\gamma_{n,\epsilon}^{\prime}(x))|u|_{\omega,x}^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\Big[\left(\frac{\epsilon_{1}-\epsilon}{\epsilon_{1}}\right)\cdot t+\left(\frac{m+\epsilon_{1}-\epsilon}{m+\epsilon_{1}}\right)\cdot(s-t)-(n-r)\Big]|u|_{\omega,x}^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{10}|u|_{\omega,x}^{2}.\qquad\square

Remark 3.7
.

The construction of the Kähler metric
\omega
becomes simpler when
\mathcal{L}+\sum\alpha_{i}D_{i}
is ample and
B=\mathcal{O}_{\overline{X}}
. We pick smooth hermitian metrics
h_{\mathcal{L}}
and
h_{i}
on
\mathcal{L}
and
\mathcal{O}_{\overline{X}}(D_{i})
such that
\omega_{\mathcal{L}}+\sum\alpha_{i}\omega_{i}
is a Kähler metric on
\overline{X}
. Then the identical construction of
\xi:X\to\mathbb{R}
(with appropriate coefficients
a_{i}
and
b_{i}
) gives the complete Kähler metric
\omega=\omega_{\mathcal{L}}+i\partial\overline{\partial}\xi
on
X
that we wanted. Also, the curvature
i\Theta_{\mathcal{L}}^{\prime}
associated to the metric
h_{\mathcal{L}}e^{-\xi}
is exactly
\omega
. Therefore,
[i\Theta_{\mathcal{L}}^{\prime},\Lambda_{\omega}]u=(r+s-n)u
for
(r,s)
-forms
u
. Hence, the positivity of
[i\Theta_{\mathcal{L}}^{\prime},\Lambda_{\omega}]
is obvious.

3.5.
L^{2}
-Dolbeault resolution

The construction of the Kähler metric on
X
in the previous section allows us to construct a Dolbeault-type resolution of the logarithmic de Rham complex of a variation of Hodge structures defined on the complement of an SNC divisor, extending the resolution described in §3.1 on
X
. The explicit construction is as follows. Consider the Kähler metric
\omega
on
X
and the smooth hermitian metric on
E\otimes\mathcal{L}\otimes B|_{X}
given by
h^{\prime}=(h_{E}\otimes h_{\mathcal{L}}\otimes h_{B})\cdot e^{-\xi}
where
\omega
,
h_{\mathcal{L}}^{\prime}=h_{\mathcal{L}}e^{-\xi}
and
h_{B}
are the metrics that we constructed in Proposition
3.6
. We define
\mathcal{H}_{(r)}^{2}(E^{p,q}\otimes\mathcal{L}\otimes B)
to be the sheaf on
\overline{X}
whose section on
U\subset\overline{X}
are the holomorphic sections
u
of
\Omega_{X}^{r}\otimes E^{p,q}\otimes\mathcal{L}\otimes B
on
U\cap X
such that
\|u\|_{h^{\prime},\omega}^{2}
is locally integrable with respect to the volume form
dV_{\omega}
. Similarly, define
L_{(r,s)}^{2}(E^{p,q}\otimes\mathcal{L}\otimes B)
to be the sheaf on
\overline{X}
whose sections
u
on
U\subset\overline{X}
are
(r,s)
-forms with values in
E^{p,q}\otimes\mathcal{L}\otimes B
with measurable coefficients on
U\cap X
such that both
\|u\|_{h^{\prime},\omega}^{2}
and
\|\overline{\partial}u\|_{h^{\prime},\omega}^{2}
are locally integrable with respect to the volume form
dV_{\omega}
. Then we can consider the following complex:

0\to\mathcal{H}_{(r)}^{2}(E^{p,q}\otimes\mathcal{L}\otimes B)\to L_{(r,0)}^{2}(E^{p,q}\otimes\mathcal{L}\otimes B)\xrightarrow{\overline{\partial}}L_{(r,1)}^{2}(E^{p,q}\otimes\mathcal{L}\otimes B)\xrightarrow{\overline{\partial}}\cdots\xrightarrow{\overline{\partial}}L_{(r,n)}^{2}(E^{p,q}\otimes\mathcal{L}\otimes B).

A priori, it is not clear that this complex is exact.

Proposition 3.8
.

The complex

0\to\mathcal{H}_{(r)}^{2}(E^{p,q}\otimes\mathcal{L}\otimes B)\to L_{(r,\bullet)}^{2}(E^{p,q}\otimes\mathcal{L}\otimes B)

is a resolution of
\mathcal{H}_{(r)}^{2}(E^{p,q}\otimes\mathcal{L}\otimes B)
by fine sheaves.

Proof.
The exactness at
\mathcal{H}_{(r)}^{2}(E^{p,q}\otimes\mathcal{L}\otimes B)
is clear by the regularity of the
\overline{\partial}
-equation. Also, it is clear that the sequence is exact on the open locus
X
. For the exactness on the boundary, we need to solve a
\overline{\partial}
-equation on a domain of type
\Omega^{*}=(\Delta^{*})^{l}\times\Delta^{n-l}
. By construction, we can cover
D
with admissible coordinates
\Omega\subset\overline{X}
such that
|\theta|_{h,\omega_{\Poin}}^{2}<C
. Let
D\cap\Omega
be defined by
z_{1}\cdots z_{l}=0
. For simplicity, assume
D_{i}\cap\Omega=(z_{i}=0)
. Also, assume that
\mathcal{L}\otimes B
is locally trivial on
\Omega
and is trivialized by a non-vanishing section
\sigma
. Let
u\otimes\sigma\in L_{(r,s)}^{2}(E^{p,q}\otimes\mathcal{L}\otimes B)(\Omega)
such that
\overline{\partial}u=0
. Let
\eta\colon\Omega^{*}\to\mathbb{R}
be such that

e^{-\eta}=\prod_{i=1}^{l}|z_{i}|^{-2a_{i}}(-\log|z_{i}|^{2})^{b_{i}}.

Possibly after shrinking the domain, we can assume that

\int_{\Omega^{*}}\|u\|_{h_{E},\omega_{\Poin}}^{2}e^{-\eta}dV_{\omega_{\Poin}}<+\infty,

since
\omega_{\Poin}
and
\omega
are mutually bounded on
\Omega^{*}
and
e^{-\eta}
and
e^{-\xi}
are mutually bounded on
\Omega^{*}
. Hence, there exists
v
such that
\overline{\partial}v=u
and

\int_{\Omega^{*}}\|v\|_{h_{E},\omega_{\Poin}}^{2}e^{-\eta}dV_{\omega_{\Poin}}<+\infty

by Proposition
3.5
. This implies that
v\otimes\sigma\in L_{(r,s-1)}^{2}(E^{p,q}\otimes\mathcal{L}\otimes B)
and we are done.
\square

3.6.
Computation of
\mathcal{H}_{(r)}^{2}(E^{p,q}\otimes\mathcal{L}\otimes B)
via Prolongation Bundles

We compute the sheaf
\mathcal{H}_{(r)}^{2}(E^{p,q}\otimes\mathcal{L}\otimes B)
explicitly and describe them using prolongation bundles.

Proposition 3.9
.

We have the following description of the sheaves
\mathcal{H}_{(r)}^{2}(E^{p,q}\otimes\mathcal{L}\otimes B)
in terms of prolongation bundles:

\mathcal{H}_{(r)}^{2}(E^{p,q}\otimes\mathcal{L}\otimes B)=\Omega_{\overline{X}}^{r}(\log D)\otimes\mathcal{P}_{\alpha}E^{p,q}\otimes\mathcal{L}\otimes B.

Proof.
These two sheaves agree on the open locus
X
since they are both extensions of
\Omega_{X}^{r}\otimes E^{p,q}\otimes\mathcal{L}\otimes B
. Hence, it is enough to examine what happens on the boundary. Fix an admissible coordinate
(\Omega;z_{1},\ldots,z_{n})
. Assume that
D\cap\Omega
is defined by
z_{1}\cdots z_{l}=0
and assume for simplicity that
D_{i}=(z_{i}=0)
. Let
\sigma
be a nonvanishing section on
\mathcal{L}\otimes B
and let
u=\sum u_{J}\otimes\sigma dz_{J}
for
u_{J}\in\Gamma(\Omega^{*},E^{p,q})
. Since
\omega
and
\omega_{\Poin}
are mutually bounded and
e^{-\xi}
and
\prod_{j=1}^{l}|z_{j}|^{2a_{j}}(-\log|z_{j}|^{2})^{b_{j}}
are mutually bounded on
\Omega^{*}
,
u\in\mathcal{H}_{(r)}^{2}(E^{p,q},\mathcal{L}\otimes B)
if and only if

(5)

\int_{\Omega^{*}}|u_{J}|_{h_{E}}^{2}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j\leq l\\
j\notin J\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{|z_{j}|^{2}(-\log|z_{j}|^{2})^{2}}\cdot\prod_{j=1}^{l}|z_{j}|^{2a_{j}}(-\log|z_{j}|^{2})^{b_{j}}d\mu<+\infty

for all
J
. Here
d\mu
is the usual Lebesgue measure on
\Omega
. Suppose

u\in\Gamma(\Omega^{*},\Omega_{\overline{X}}^{r}(\log D)\otimes\mathcal{P}_{\alpha}E^{p,q}\otimes\mathcal{L}\otimes B).

Then we can express
u
as

u=\sum_{J}\left(\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j\leq l\\
j\in J\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{z_{j}}\right)v_{J}\otimes\sigma dz_{J}

for
v_{J}\in\mathcal{P}_{\alpha}E^{p,q}
. In particular,
|v_{J}|_{h_{E}}\lesssim\prod_{j=1}^{l}|z_{j}|^{-(\alpha_{j}+a_{j})/2}
since
a_{j}>\alpha_{j}

4.
Proof of Saito’s vanishing theorem

In this section, we prove Saito’s vanishing theorem by reducing it to Theorem
1.3
. We first give a proof for Saito’s Hodge modules, and then explain how the same method works in the setting of complex Hodge modules. For a projective variety
X
, we denote by
\MHM(X)
the abelian category of graded polarizable mixed Hodge modules on
X
, in the sense of Saito. Roughly speaking, a mixed Hodge module is a filtered
\mathcal{D}
-module with extra structure. The Saito vanishing theorem concerns the graded piece of the de Rham complex of mixed Hodge modules.

Theorem 4.1

(
[
24
]
)
.

Let
X
be a complex projective variety and let
\mathcal{M}\in\MHM(X)
be a graded polarizable mixed Hodge module on
X
. For every ample line bundle
\mathcal{L}
on
X
, we have

\mathbb{H}^{l}\big(X,\gr_{p}\DR_{X}(\mathcal{M})\otimes\mathcal{L}\big)=0\qquad\textnormal{for all }l>0,p\in\mathbb{Z}.

We first review some necessary facts on Hodge modules and then prove the Saito vanishing at the end.

4.1.
Terminology and Properties of Hodge Modules

We give a very brief summary on Hodge modules. All of the results can be found in two foundational papers
[
23
,
24
]
, or in a nice survey
[
28
]
. Let
X
be a smooth complex algebraic variety. Saito defines two categories
\HM(X,w)
and
\MHM(X)
which are the category of pure Hodge modules with weight
w
and the category of graded polarizable mixed Hodge modules. The objects in
\HM(X,w)
are triples
((\mathcal{M},F_{\bullet}),K,\alpha)
where
(\mathcal{M},F_{\bullet})
is a holonomic filtered
\mathcal{D}
-module,
K
is a
\mathbb{Q}
-perverse sheaf on
X
, and an isomorphism
\alpha:\DR_{X}^{\mathrm{an}}\mathcal{M}\xrightarrow{\simeq}K\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathbb{C}
. For mixed Hodge modules, we have an extra
weight
filtration on
K
, hence on
\mathcal{M}
. The objects in this category are required to satisfy suitable compatibility conditions. If
\mathcal{N}
is a variation of Hodge structures on
X
of weight
w
and we switch from decreasing filtration to increasing filtration by the rule
F_{k}\mathcal{N}=F^{-k}\mathcal{N}
, then
\mathcal{N}
underlies a structure of a pure Hodge module of weight
w+\dim X
. As opposed to the previous sections, we switch to increasing filtrations for Hodge modules from this point in order to be consistent with the conventions on
\mathcal{D}
-modules. We introduce some terminology and properties of
\mathcal{D}
-modules and Hodge modules, without giving any proofs. In this article, a
\mathcal{D}
-module is understood to mean a left
\mathcal{D}
-module.

(1)

For a singular variety
X
, we embed
X
in a smooth variety
Z
and define
\MHM(X)
as the category of mixed Hodge modules on
Z
whose support is in
X
. The category
\MHM(X)
does not depend on the choice of closed embedding
X\hookrightarrow Z
. We can similarly define
\HM(X,w)
as well. We can still define these categories even if
X
cannot be embedded into a smooth variety by covering with embeddable open subsets and imposing some conditions on the intersections. However, we ignore this issue since we will work only on (quasi)-projective varieties.

(2)

The filtration on the de Rham complex of a mixed Hodge module
\mathcal{M}
on a smooth variety
X
is given by

F_{k}\DR_{X}(\mathcal{M})=\left[F_{k}\mathcal{M}\to\Omega_{X}^{1}\otimes F_{k+1}\mathcal{M}\to\cdots\to\Omega_{X}^{n}\otimes F_{k+n}\mathcal{M}\right][n].

After taking the graded pieces, we obtain a complex of
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-modules

\gr_{k}\DR_{X}(\mathcal{M})=\left[\gr_{k}\mathcal{M}\to\Omega_{X}^{1}\otimes\gr_{k+1}\mathcal{M}\to\cdots\to\Omega_{X}^{n}\otimes\gr_{k+n}\mathcal{M}\right][n].

We can extend the notion of graded de Rham complexes to objects in the derived category of mixed Hodge modules. In other words, for
u\in\mathcal{D}^{b}(\MHM(X))
, the graded de Rham complex
\gr_{k}\DR_{X}(u)
makes sense as an object in
\mathcal{D}_{\coh}^{b}(\mathcal{O}_{X})
.

(3)

[
27
]
*Lemma 7.3. For a singular variety
X
, we define the graded de Rham complex of
\mathcal{M}\in\MHM(X)
by embedding
X
in a smooth variety
Z
and taking the graded de Rham complex in

. It turns out that the complex
\gr_{k}\DR_{X}(\mathcal{M})
is actually a complex of
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-modules, and does not depend on the choice of the embedding

as an object in
\mathcal{D}_{\coh}^{b}(\mathcal{O}_{X})
.

(4)

For holonomic
\mathcal{D}
-modules there are functors

defined at the level of derived category of holonomic
\mathcal{D}
-modules. The same functors admit enrichment to functors between the corresponding derived categories of mixed Hodge modules.

(5)

We have a canonical transformation
\gamma_{f}:f_{!}\to f_{*}
and this transformation is an isomorphism if

is proper.

(6)

If

is a locally closed immersion between smooth varieties and
\mathcal{M}\in\MHM(U)
, then the transformation

induces a map
\mathcal{H}^{0}(j_{!}\mathcal{M})\to\mathcal{H}^{0}(j_{*}\mathcal{M})
. The image of this map is the intermediate extension of

which is denoted by

.

(7)

Furthermore, let’s suppose that

is open and

is a divisor. Then

and

are induced by an exact functor at the level of abelian categories. In other words, if

and

as objects inside

. Moreover, the underlying

(8)

For proper morphisms

\mathcal{M}
is a mixed Hodge module on

\gr_{p}\DR_{X}(\mu_{*}\mathcal{M})\simeq\mathbf{R}\mu_{*}(\gr_{p}\DR_{Y}\mathcal{M}).

We next describe the structure of the categories
\MHM(X)
and

(1)

For a mixed Hodge module

is a pure Hodge module of weight

.

(2)

We say that a pure Hodge module

has strict support

the subcategory of

consisting of objects with strict support

.

(3)

Every pure Hodge module

admits a decomposition by strict support. Hence

is of the form

\mathcal{M}=\bigoplus_{Z\subset X}\mathcal{M}_{Z}

where the sum runs over all irreducible closed subsets

(4)

Every object

. Conversely, every variation of Hodge structures

of weight

l>0
,
p\in\mathbb{Z}
and every ample line bundle
\mathcal{L}
on
X
.

Proof.

First, we reduce to the case when
X
is normal. Consider the normalization
\tau:X^{\mathrm{nor}}\to X
which is clearly an isomorphism over
U
and let
F=(\tau^{*}D)_{\mathrm{red}}
. Therefore, we have inclusions
i:U\to X^{\mathrm{nor}}
and
j:U\to X
. Since
\tau
is finite and hence proper, we have the following isomorphisms:

\displaystyle\gr_{p}\DR_{X}(\mathcal{M}[\ast D])\simeq\tau_{*}\left(\gr_{p}\DR_{X^{\mathrm{nor}}}(\mathcal{M}[\ast F])\right)

\displaystyle\gr_{p}\DR_{X}(\mathcal{M}[!D])\simeq\tau_{*}\left(\gr_{p}\DR_{X^{\mathrm{nor}}}(\mathcal{M}[!F])\right).

The isomorphism follows by the commutativity of the graded de Rham complex with proper pushforward. By the projection formula, it is enough to show the two following vanishings:

\displaystyle\mathbb{H}^{l}(X^{\mathrm{nor}},\gr_{p}\DR_{X^{\mathrm{nor}}}(\mathcal{M}[\ast F])\otimes\tau^{*}\mathcal{L})=0\qquad\text{for all }l>0,p\in\mathbb{Z},

\displaystyle\mathbb{H}^{l}(X^{\mathrm{nor}},\gr_{p}\DR_{X^{\mathrm{nor}}}(\mathcal{M}[!F])\otimes\tau^{*}\mathcal{L})=0\qquad\text{for all }l>0,p\in\mathbb{Z}.

Since
\tau^{*}\mathcal{L}
is still ample, it is enough to show the assertion when
X
is normal.

Now, we assume that
X
is normal. Consider a log resolution
\mu\colon Y\to X
of the pair
(X,D)
which is an isomorphism over
U
. Let
F=(\mu^{*}D)_{\mathrm{red}}
, which is an SNC divisor on
Y
. Write
F=\sum_{i=1}^{s}F_{i}
. By Lemma
4.6
, we can assume that there exists
c_{i}
such that
\sum_{i=1}^{s}c_{i}F_{i}
is
\mu
-ample. Since
\mu
is proper, we can compute the graded de Rham complex of
\mathcal{M}[\ast D]
and
\mathcal{M}[!D]
as follows:

\displaystyle\gr_{p}\DR_{X}(\mathcal{M}[\ast D])\simeq\mathbf{R}\mu_{*}\left(\gr_{p}\DR_{Y}(\mathcal{M}[\ast F])\right)\simeq\mathbf{R}\mu_{*}\left(\gr_{p}\DR_{(Y,F)}(\mathcal{M}_{0})\right)

\displaystyle\gr_{p}\DR_{X}(\mathcal{M}[!D])\simeq\mathbf{R}\mu_{*}\left(\gr_{p}\DR_{Y}(\mathcal{M}[!F])\right)\simeq\mathbf{R}\mu_{*}\left(\gr_{p}\DR_{(Y,F)}(\mathcal{M}_{-\epsilon})\right).

The isomorphism on the right hand side follows by logarithmic comparison (Proposition
4.3
). By the projection formula, it is enough to show the two following vanishings:

\displaystyle\mathbb{H}^{l}(Y,\gr_{p}\DR_{(Y,F)}(\mathcal{M}_{0})\otimes\mu^{*}\mathcal{L})=0\qquad\text{for all }l>0,p\in\mathbb{Z},

\displaystyle\mathbb{H}^{l}(Y,\gr_{p}\DR_{(Y,F)}(\mathcal{M}_{-\epsilon})\otimes\mu^{*}\mathcal{L})=0\qquad\text{for all }l>0,p\in\mathbb{Z}.

The only problem is that
\mu^{*}\mathcal{L}
is no longer ample. However, we can handle this by perturbing
\mu^{*}\mathcal{L}
. Note that for
0<d\ll c\ll 1
, we have
\mu^{*}(\mathcal{L}+cD)+d\cdot\sum_{i=1}^{s}c_{i}F_{i}
ample. Hence, there exist
0<a_{i}\ll 1
such that
\mu^{*}\mathcal{L}+\sum_{i=1}^{s}a_{i}F_{i}
is ample since the images of
F_{i}
are contained in
D
. In fact, we can choose
a_{i}
to be sufficiently small so that
\mathcal{P}_{0}\mathcal{M}^{p,q}=\mathcal{P}_{a}\mathcal{M}^{p,q}
as a sheaf on
Y
. Then we can use Theorem
1.2
to get

\mathbb{H}^{l}(Y,\gr_{p}\DR_{(Y,F)}(\mathcal{M}_{0})\otimes\mu^{*}\mathcal{L})=\mathbb{H}^{l}(Y,\gr_{p}\DR_{(Y,F)}(\mathcal{M}_{a})\otimes\mu^{*}\mathcal{L})=0\qquad\text{for all }l>0.

The second vanishing follows along the same lines. There exist
0<d\ll c\ll 1
such that
\mu^{*}(\mathcal{L}-cD)+d\cdot\sum_{i=1}^{s}c_{i}F_{i}
is ample. Hence, there exist
0<a_{i}\ll 1
such that
\mu^{*}\mathcal{L}-\sum_{i=1}^{s}a_{i}F_{i}
is ample. We can make these
a_{i}
be sufficiently small so that
0<a_{i}<\epsilon
, which implies
\mathcal{P}_{-\epsilon}\mathcal{M}^{p,q}=\mathcal{P}_{-a}\mathcal{M}^{p,q}
as a sheaf on
Y
. Therefore,

\mathbb{H}^{l}(Y,\gr_{p}\DR_{(Y,F)}(\mathcal{M}_{-\epsilon})\otimes\mu^{*}\mathcal{L})=\mathbb{H}^{l}(Y,\gr_{p}\DR_{(Y,F)}(\mathcal{M}_{-a})\otimes\mu^{*}\mathcal{L})=0\qquad\text{for all }l>0.

This concludes the proof.
\square

We finally give the proof of the Saito vanishing theorem.

Proof of Theorem
4.1
.
The strategy is to prove Theorems
4.1
and
4.5
simultaneously by Noetherian induction. As we have already seen, Theorem
4.5
in dimension
\leq n
implies Theorem
4.1
in dimension
\leq n
. Let
X
be an irreducible complex projective variety and let
\mathcal{M}
be a pure Hodge module with strict support
X
. Then there exists an open subset
U\subset X
such that
\mathcal{N}=\mathcal{M}|_{U}
is a variation of Hodge structures on
U
. By shrinking
U
, we may assume that
D=X\setminus U
is a divisor. Fix a very ample divisor
H
such that
|H-D|\neq\emptyset
and write
H\sim D+E
. By replacing
U
by
X\setminus(D\cup E)
and
D
by
D\cup E
, we may assume that
D
is a hyperplane section of
X
for some embedding
X\hookrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{N}
. Considering every
\mathcal{D}
-module as a
\mathcal{D}_{\mathbb{P}^{N}}
-module, we see that
\mathcal{N}[!D]
is an object in
\MHM(X)
because
\mathbb{P}^{N}\setminus H\to\mathbb{P}^{N}
is an affine morphism. Since
\mathcal{M}
is the intermediate extension of
\mathcal{N}
, we have an exact sequence

0\to\mathcal{K}\to\mathcal{N}[!D]\to\mathcal{M}\to 0

and the kernel
\mathcal{K}
is supported inside
D
. By Proposition
4.7
and Noetherian induction, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{H}^{l}(X,\gr_{p}\DR_{X}(\mathcal{N}[!D])\otimes\mathcal{L})=0\qquad\text{for all }l>0,p\in\mathbb{Z},

\displaystyle\mathbb{H}^{l}(X,\gr_{p}\DR_{X}(\mathcal{K})\otimes\mathcal{L})=0\qquad\text{for all }l>0,p\in\mathbb{Z}.

The long exact sequence and the exactness of
\gr_{p}\DR_{X}
gives

\mathbb{H}^{l}(X,\gr_{p}\DR_{X}(\mathcal{M})\otimes\mathcal{L})=0\qquad\text{for all }l>0,p\in\mathbb{Z}.

This proves the general version of Saito’s vanishing theorem.
\square

4.4.
Vanishing theorem for complex Hodge modules

We finally explain how to modify the previous proof of the Saito vanishing theorem in the case of
complex
Hodge modules. Before that, we briefly highlight the key differences between Saito’s Hodge modules and the Hodge modules of Sabbah–Schnell. In the theory of Sabbah–Schnell, they remove the underlying perverse sheaf
K
and the isomorphism
\alpha\colon\DR_{X}^{\mathrm{an}}\mathcal{M}\simeq K\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathbb{C}
from the picture by considering a sesquilinear pairing on
\mathcal{D}
-modules. More precisely, instead of considering a underlying perverse sheaf, they consider a
triple

(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{M}^{\prime},\mathfrak{s})
where
\mathcal{M}
and
\mathcal{M}^{\prime}
are filtered holonomic
\mathcal{D}
-modules, and

\mathfrak{s}\colon\mathcal{M}\otimes\overline{\mathcal{M}^{\prime}}\to\mathfrak{Db}_{X}

is a
\mathcal{D}_{X}\otimes\mathcal{D}_{\overline{X}}
-linear perfect pairing (which gives the data of the polarization). Here,
\mathfrak{Db}_{X}
is the sheaf of complex (0,0)-currents on
X
, hence carries a natural left action of
\mathcal{D}_{X}\otimes\mathcal{D}_{\overline{X}}
.

In
[
21
]
, for a complex manifold
X
, the authors construct the category
\mathrm{pHM}_{\mathbb{C}}(X,w)

2

\mathbb{C}
is not written in the MHM project, but we write it in order to distinguish it with Saito’s Hodge modules.
of complex pure polarizable Hodge modules on
X
with weight
w
. At the time of the writing,
[

]
does not contain the definition of complex
mixed
Hodge modules. We use
[
6
]
*§7.1 for the construction of the category
\MHM_{\mathbb{C}}(X)
of complex mixed Hodge modules, which follows the same strategy of
[

]
*§4. They define the category
\MHM_{\mathbb{C}}(X)
as the full-category of triples which are stable under certain operations. Then, the same six functor formalism works for complex mixed Hodge modules.

Finally, we illustrate how to modify §4.3 to get the vanishing theorem for complex Hodge modules.

(1)

The MHM project only deals with Hodge modules on complex manifolds. We consider the Hodge modules on singular varieties as objects supported on an ambient projective space. The induction on dimension changes to the induction of the dimension of the support of a Hodge module. For a mixed Hodge module
\mathcal{M}
supported on
X
, still the object
\gr_{p}\DR\mathcal{M}
is an object in
\mathcal{D}_{\coh}^{b}(X)
.

(2)

One can reduce to the strict support case as in the same way, since the structure theorem works in the same way as that of Saito’s Hodge modules (see

(3)

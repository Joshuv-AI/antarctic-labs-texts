Palindromic Polynomials Over Finite Fields Thanks: The first author was supported in part by the United States Naval Research Laboratory, Washington, D.C.
Geoffrey L. Price, Katherine Thompson

Abstract
. For any finite field 𝔽 \mathbb{F} and any positive integer n n we count the number of monic polynomials of degree n n over 𝔽 \mathbb{F} with nonzero constant coefficient and a self-reciprocal factor of any specified degree. An application is given for systems of linear equations over 𝔽 \mathbb{F} of index 2 2 . 2010 MSC Classification 11T06, 15A06 Keywords: finite fields, self-reciprocal polynomials, partially reciprocal polynomials, linear systems



1.
Introduction and Statement of Results

Fix a prime
p
. Let
\mathbb{F}_{q}
be the field of
q=p^{k}
elements where
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Let
f
be a monic polynomial over
\mathbb{F}
of degree
n\geq 1
and with non-zero constant coefficient. The
reciprocal
of
f
is the degree
n
polynomial
f^{*}
given by
f^{*}(x)=x^{n}f(1/x)
. We say that a polynomial
f
with non-zero constant coefficient is a
reciprocal polynomial
(or a self-reciprocal polynomial, or a palindrome polynomial) if
f=f^{*}
.

Given a nonnegative integer
n
let
z(n)
denote the number of monic polynomials of degree
n
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
with nonzero constant coefficient that have no reciprocal factors other than the constant function
1
. Our main result is:

Theorem 1
.

Fix
q=p^{k}
for
p
prime and
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Then

(i)

z(0)=1
;

(ii)

z(1)=q-2
;

(iii)

for
n\geq 2
,
z(n)=k(q)q^{n-1}+c(q,n)
where
k(q)=\frac{(q-1)^{2}}{q+1}
and
c(q,n)=(-1)^{n+1}2(\frac{q-1}{q+1})
.

Remark: In Theorem
4.4
of
[
6
]
(see also
[
7
]
) the first author proved Theorem
1
for the case
q=2
. This result was used in the classification (up to conjugacy) of the binary shifts on the hyperfinite
II_{1}
factor.

2.
Proof

Lemma 2
.

For
n\in\mathbb{N}
let
t(n)
be the number of monic polynomials of degree
n
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
with non-zero constant coefficient. Then
t(n)=(q-1)q^{n-1}
.

Proof.

There are
q-1
choices for the constant term and
q
choices for each of the degree
d
terms, for
1\leq d\leq n-1
.
∎

We will use the notation
pr(n)
to denote the number of
partially reciprocal
polynomials of degree
n
, i.e. the monic polynomials of degree
n
with nonzero constant term which have a reciprocal factor of degree at least
2
.

Lemma 3
.

For
n\geq 2
,
t(n)-pr(n)=z(n)+z(n-1)
.

Proof.

Clearly
t(n)-pr(n)
is the number of monic polynomials with nonzero constant coefficient which have a reciprocal factor of degree at most
1
. Note that such a polynomial
f
either has no palindrome factors or has
x+1
as its only palindrome factor. If
x+1
is the only palindrome factor of
f
, then
g(x)=\frac{1}{x+1}f(x)
is a polynomial of degree
n-1
with no palindrome factors. Conversely, if
g
is a monic polynomial over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
of degree
n-1
with no palindrome factors, then
f(x)=(x+1)g(x)
is a polynomial with
x+1
as its only palindrome factor, so there is a one-to-one correspondence between polynomials of degree
n-1
with no palindrome factors and polynomials of degree
n
with just a single linear palindrome factor. The result follows.
∎

Lemma 4
.

For each
n\in\mathbb{N}
let s(n) be the number of monic reciprocal polynomials. For any
j\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
,
s(2j)=q^{j}=s(2j+1)
.

Proof.

Direct, and left to the reader.
∎

We now proceed with the proof of Theorem
1
.

Proof.

(i)

The only monic constant is
f(x)=1
, so
z(0)=1
trivially.

(ii)

Let
f(x)=x+a
with
a\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
. We exclude
a=0,1
, leaving
q-2
polynomials. So
z(1)=q-2
.

(iii)

We proceed by induction on
n\geq 2
.

For
n=2
, we have
pr(2)=z(0)s(2)=1\cdot q=q
, so by Lemma
3
,
t(2)-pr(2)=(q-1)q-q=q^{2}-2q
. So then, since
z(1)=q-2
and
z(1)+z(2)=t(2)-pr(2)
, we have

\displaystyle z(2)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle q^{2}-2q-(q-2)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(q-1)(q-2)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(q-1)(q^{2}-q-2)}{q+1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(q-1)[q(q-1)-2]}{q+1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{(q-1)^{2}}{q+1}\cdot q^{2-1}+(-1)^{2+1}2\left(\dfrac{q-1}{q+1}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle k(q)q^{2-1}+c(q,2),

which completes the base case.

For the inductive step, we first suppose
n
is odd, and write
n=2m+1
for
m\in\mathbb{N}
, By assumption
z(2m)=k(q)q^{2m-1}+c(q,2m)
. Then

pr(n)=pr(2m+1)=s(2m+1)z(0)+s(2m)z(1)+\cdots+s(2)z(2m-1)=I+II,

where

\displaystyle I

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell=0}^{m-1}z(2\ell)s(2(m-\ell)+1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle z(0)s(2m+1)+\sum_{\ell=1}^{m-1}z(2\ell)s(2(m-\ell)+1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1\cdot q^{m}+\sum_{\ell=1}^{m-1}\left(k(q)q^{2\ell-1}-2\left(\frac{q-1}{q+1}\right)\right)q^{m-\ell}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle q^{m}+\sum_{\ell=1}^{m-1}\left(k(q)q^{m+\ell-1}-2\left(\dfrac{q-1}{q+1}\right)p^{m-\ell}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle q^{m}+k(q)(q^{m}+\cdots+q^{2m-2})-2\left(\dfrac{q-1}{q+1}\right)(q^{m-1}+\cdots+q)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle q^{m}+k(q)q^{m}\left(\frac{q^{m-1}-1}{q-1}\right)-2\left(\dfrac{q-1}{q+1}\right)q(\frac{q^{m-1}-1}{q-1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle q^{m}+k(q)\left(\frac{q^{2m-1}-q^{m}}{q-1}\right)-2\left(\dfrac{q-1}{q+1}\right)\left(\frac{q^{m}-q}{q-1}\right),

and

\displaystyle II

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle s(2m)z(1)+s(2m-2)z(3)+\cdots+s(2)z(2m-1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle s(2m)z(1)+\sum_{\ell=0}^{m-2}z(2\ell+3)s(2(m-\ell-1))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(q-2)q^{m}+\sum_{\ell=0}^{m-2}\left(k(q)q^{2\ell+2}+2\left(\dfrac{q-1}{q+1}\right)\right)q^{m-\ell-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(q-2)q^{m}+k(q)(q^{m+1}+\cdots+q^{2m-1})+2\left(\dfrac{q-1}{q+1}\right)(q^{m-1}+\cdots+q)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(q-2)q^{m}+k(q)q^{m+1}\left(\frac{q^{m-1}-1}{q-1}\right)+2\left(\dfrac{q-1}{q+1}\right)q\left(\frac{q^{m-1}-1}{q-1}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(q-2)q^{m}+k(q)\left(\frac{q^{2m}-q^{m+1}}{q-1}\right)+2\left(\dfrac{q-1}{q+1}\right)\left(\frac{q^{m}-q}{q-1}\right).

Then

\displaystyle pr(2m+1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle I+II

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle q^{m}+k(q)\left(\frac{q^{2m-1}-q^{m}}{q-1}\right)-2\left(\dfrac{q-1}{q+1}\right)\left(\frac{q^{m}-q}{q-1}\right)+

\displaystyle(q-2)q^{m}+k(q)\left(\frac{q^{2m}-q^{m+1}}{q-1}\right)+2\left(\dfrac{q-1}{q+1}\right)\left(\frac{q^{m}-q}{q-1}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle q^{m}+k(q)\left(\frac{q^{2m-1}-q^{m}}{q-1}\right)+(q-2)q^{m}+k(q)\left(\frac{q^{2m}-q^{m+1}}{q-1}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle q^{m}+\left(\frac{q-1}{q+1}\right)(q^{2m-1}-q^{m})+(q-2)q^{m}+\left(\frac{q-1}{q+1}\right)(q^{2m}-q^{m+1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(q-1)q^{m}+\left(\dfrac{q-1}{q+1}\right)(q^{2m-1}-q^{m}+q^{2m}-q^{m+1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{q-1}{q+1}(q^{m+1}+q^{m})+\dfrac{q-1}{q+1}(q^{2m-1}-q^{m}+q^{2m}-q^{m+1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(q-1)q^{2m-1}.

But now by Lemma
3

\displaystyle z(2m+1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle t(2m+1)-pr(2m+1)-z(2m)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(q-1)q^{2m}-(q-1)q^{2m-1}-\left(\dfrac{(q-1)^{2}}{q+1}\cdot q^{2m-1}-2\cdot\dfrac{q-1}{q+1}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(q-1)q^{2m-1}\left(q-1-\frac{q-1}{q+1}\right)+2\left(\frac{q-1}{q+1}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(q-1)^{2}q^{2m-1}\left(1-\frac{1}{q+1}\right)+2\left(\frac{q-1}{q+1}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle k(q)q^{2m}+c(q,2m+1).

We now suppose that
n
is even, and write
n=2m
where
m\in\mathbb{N}
. Assume
z(\ell)=k(q)q^{\ell-1}+c(q,n)
for

\ell=2,\dots,2m-1
. We have

pr(2m)=\sum_{k=0}^{2m-2}z(k)s(2m-k)=I+II,

where

\displaystyle I

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell=0}^{m-1}z(2\ell)s(2(m-\ell))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle z(0)s(2m)+\sum_{\ell=1}^{m-1}z(2\ell)s(2(m-\ell))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1\cdot q^{m}+\sum_{\ell=1}^{m-1}\left(k(q)\cdot q^{2\ell-1}-2\left(\dfrac{q-1}{q+1}\right)\right)q^{m-\ell}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle q^{m}+\sum_{\ell=1}^{m-1}\left(k(q)q^{m+\ell-1}-2\left(\dfrac{q-1}{q+1}\right)q^{m-\ell}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle q^{m}+k(q)q^{m}(1+\cdots+q^{m-2})-2\left(\dfrac{q-1}{q+1}\right)q(1+\cdots+q^{m-2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle q^{m}+k(q)q^{m}\left(\frac{q^{m-1}-1}{q-1}\right)-2\left(\dfrac{q-1}{q+1}\right)q\left(\frac{q^{m-1}-1}{q-1}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle q^{m}+k(q)\left(\frac{q^{2m-1}-q^{m}}{q-1}\right)-2\left(\dfrac{q-1}{q+1}\right)\left(\frac{q^{m}-q}{q-1}\right),

and

\displaystyle II

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell=1}^{m-1}z(2(\ell-1)+1)s(2(m-\ell)+1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle z(1)s(2(m-1)+1)+\sum_{\ell=2}^{m-1}z(2(\ell-1)+1)s(2(m-\ell)+1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(q-2)q^{m-1}+\sum_{\ell=2}^{m-1}\left(k(q)q^{2\ell-2}+2\left(\dfrac{q-1}{q+1}\right)\right)q^{m-\ell}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(q-2)q^{m-1}+k(q)(q^{m}+\cdots+q^{2m-3})+2\left(\dfrac{q-1}{q+1}\right)(q^{m-2}+\cdots+q)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(q-2)q^{m-1}+k(q)q^{m}(1+\cdots+q^{m-3})+2\left(\dfrac{q-1}{q+1}\right)q(1+\cdots+q^{m-3})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(q-2)q^{m-1}+k(q)q^{m}(\frac{q^{m-2}-1}{q-1})+2\left(\dfrac{q-1}{q+1}\right)q\left(\frac{q^{m-2}-1}{q-1}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(q-2)q^{m-1}+k(q)(\frac{q^{2m-2}-q^{m}}{q-1})+2\left(\dfrac{q-1}{q+1}\right)\left(\frac{q^{m-1}-q}{q-1}\right).

Then

\displaystyle pr(2m)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle I+II

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle q^{m}+k(q)\left(\frac{q^{2m-1}-q^{m}}{q-1}\right)-2\left(\dfrac{q-1}{q+1}\right)\left(\frac{q^{m}-q}{q-1}\right)+(q-2)q^{m-1}+k(q)\left(\frac{q^{2m-2}-q^{m}}{q-1}\right)+2\left(\dfrac{q-1}{q+1}\right)\left(\frac{q^{m-1}-q}{q-1}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle q^{m}+(q-2)q^{m-1}+k(q)\left(\dfrac{q^{2m-1}+q^{2m-2}-2q^{m}}{q-1}\right)-2\left(\dfrac{q-1}{q+1}\right)\left(\dfrac{q^{m}-q^{m-1}}{q-1}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2(q^{m}-q^{m-1})+\left(\dfrac{q-1}{q+1}\right)(q^{2m-1}+q^{2m-2}-2q^{m})-\dfrac{2(q^{m}-q^{m-1})}{q+1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{2(q^{m}-q^{m-1})(q+1)}{q+1}-\dfrac{2(q^{m}-q^{m-1})}{q+1}+\dfrac{q^{2m}-2q^{m+1}-q^{2m-2}+2q^{m}}{q+1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{2q^{m+1}-2q^{m}+q^{2m}-2q^{m+1}-q^{2m-2}+2q^{m}}{q+1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle q^{2m-2}(q-1).

But again by Lemma
3
we have

\displaystyle z(2m)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle t(2m)-pr(2m)-z(2m-1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(q-1)q^{2m-1}-(q-1)q^{2m-2}-\left(k(2m-1)q^{2m-2}+2\left(\dfrac{q-1}{q+1}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(q-1)^{2}q^{2m-2}-\left(\dfrac{(q-1)^{2}}{q+1}\right)q^{2m-2}-2\left(\dfrac{q-1}{q+1}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\dfrac{(q-1)^{2}}{q+1}\right)q^{2m-2}-2\left(\dfrac{q-1}{q+1}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle k(q)q^{2m-1}+c(q,2m).

∎

Corollary 5
.

Let
j
and
n
be positive integers with
1\leq j\leq n
. Let
p(n,j)
be the number of monic polynomials
f
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
with nonzero constant coefficient such that
f
has a self-reciprocal factor of degree
j
and no higher. Then
p(n,j)=z(n-j)s(j)
.

3.
Application to Systems of Linear Equations

As mentioned above, our counting result was established in
[
6
]
for
\mathbb{F}_{2}
. Here
z(0)=1,z(1)=z(2)=0
and for
n\geq 3
,
z(n)=\frac{1}{3}(2^{n-1})+\frac{2}{3}
(for
n
odd) and
\frac{1}{3}(2^{n-1})-\frac{2}{3}
(for
n
even). We present an application of these results to a family of linear systems of equations over
\mathbb{F}_{2}
. This application is related to the classification problem in
[
6
]
mentioned in Section 1 above.

Definition 6
.

Let
(a)=(\dots,a_{-2},a_{-1},a_{0},a_{1},a_{2},\dots)
be a sequence in
\mathbb{F}_{2}
with
a_{0}=0
and with
a_{j}=a_{-j}
. We shall say that
(a)
has index
2
if one of the following holds:

(1)

a_{-1}=a_{1}=1
, and
a_{j}=0
otherwise.

(2)

a_{0}=0
and
a_{j}=1
otherwise.

(3)

For some
m\geq 2
there is a vector
\vec{k}=[k_{0},k_{1},\dots,k_{m}]
, with
k_{0}=k_{m}=1

satisfying the following infinite linear system of equations:

\displaystyle 1=

\displaystyle k_{0}a_{1}+k_{1}a_{0}+k_{2}a_{1}+\cdots+k_{m}a_{m-1}

\displaystyle 0=

\displaystyle k_{0}a_{2}+k_{1}a_{1}+k_{2}a_{0}+\cdots+k_{m}a_{m-2}

\displaystyle 0=

\displaystyle k_{0}a_{3}+k_{1}a_{2}+k_{2}a_{1}+\cdots+k_{m}a_{m-3}

\displaystyle 0=

\displaystyle k_{0}a_{4}+k_{1}a_{3}+k_{2}a_{2}+\cdots+k_{m}a_{m-4}

Note that
(1)
may be rewritten (with
\vec{k}=[k_{0}]=[1]
and
(a)=(0100\cdots)
as

\displaystyle 1=

\displaystyle k_{0}a_{1}

\displaystyle 0=

\displaystyle k_{0}a_{2}

\displaystyle 0=

\displaystyle k_{0}a_{3}

\displaystyle 0=

\displaystyle k_{0}a_{4}

and
(2)
may be rewritten (with
\vec{c}=[k_{0},k_{1}]=[1,1]
and
(a)=(0111\cdots)
as

\displaystyle 1=

\displaystyle k_{0}a_{1}+k_{1}a_{0}

\displaystyle 0=

\displaystyle k_{0}a_{2}+k_{1}a_{1}

\displaystyle 0=

\displaystyle k_{0}a_{3}+k_{1}a_{2}

\displaystyle 0=

\displaystyle k_{0}a_{4}+k_{1}a_{3}

One can show that the sequence
a_{m-1},a_{m-2},\dots,a_{1},a_{0},a_{1},a_{2},\dots
in (3) is not periodic, whereas

a_{m-2},a_{m-3},\dots,a_{1},a_{0},a_{1},a_{2},\dots
is periodic, see
[
5
]
[Theorem 6.11]. From the proof of Theorem 3.3 of
[
6
]
it follows that a vector
\vec{k}=[k_{0},k_{1},\dots,k_{m}]
with
m\geq 2
satisfies a system of the form
(3)
for at most one sequence
(a)
. From the proof of Lemma 3.1 of
[
6
]
, a necessary condition for a vector
\vec{k}
to satisfy
(3)
is that the polynomial
k_{0}+k_{1}x+\cdots+k_{m}x^{m}
either has no palindrome factors or the single palindrome factor
1+x
over
\mathbb{F}_{2}
. There are exactly
z(m)+z(m-1)
such polynomials, with
z(m)
polynomials of degree
m
and constant coefficient
1
having no palindrome factors, and
z(m-1)
such polynomials of degree
m
having
1+x
as its only palindrome factor. Therefore there are
z(m)+z(m-1)=\frac{1}{3}(2^{m-1})+\frac{1}{3}(2^{m-2})=2^{m-2}
such polynomials. By the proof of Theorem 3.4 of
[
6
]
there are at least
2^{m-2}
vectors
\vec{k}=[k_{0},k_{1},\dots,k_{m}]
satisfying (3). Therefore we have the following result (cf.
[
6
]
[Theorem 5.1]).

Theorem 7
.

For
m\geq 3
there are exactly
2^{m-2}
linear systems of index
2
satisfying
(3)
over
\mathbb{F}_{2}
.

Remark
.

Note that every degree
2
polynomial with constant coefficient
1
is a palindrome so that there are no systems (3) with
m=2
.

Remark
.

There is a notion of a linear system of equations over
\mathbb{F}_{2}
of index
j
with
j>2
, but the analysis is considerably more complicated, as there is no longer a one-to-one correspondence between vectors
\vec{k}
and sequences
(a)
(see
[
7
]
for related results).

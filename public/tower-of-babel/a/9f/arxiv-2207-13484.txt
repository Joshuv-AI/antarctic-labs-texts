Equal rank local theta correspondence as a strong Morita equivalence
Bram Mesland, Mehmet Haluk Şengün

Abstract
. Let ( G , H ) (G,H) be one of the equal rank reductive dual pairs ( M ​ p 2 ​ n , O 2 ​ n + 1 ) \left(Mp_{2n},O_{2n+1}\right) or ( U n , U n ) \left(U_{n},U_{n}\right) over a nonarchimedean local field of characteristic zero. It is well-known that the theta correspondence establishes a bijection between certain subsets, say G ^ θ \widehat{G}_{\theta} and H ^ θ \widehat{H}_{\theta} , of the tempered duals of G G and H H . We prove that this bijection arises from an equivalence between the categories of representations of two C ∗ C^{*} -algebras whose spectra are G ^ θ \widehat{G}_{\theta} and H ^ θ \widehat{H}_{\theta} . This equivalence is implemented by the induction functor associated to a Morita equivalence bimodule (in the sense of Rieffel) which we construct using the oscillator representation. As an immediate corollary, we deduce that the bijection is functorial and continuous with respect to weak inclusion. We derive further consequences regarding the transfer of characters and preservation of formal degrees.



1.
Introduction

In this paper, we cast a new light onto equal rank tempered local theta correspondence by approaching it via the framework of the representation theory of
C^{*}
-algebras. As a result, we discover some fundamental new features and obtain conceptual new proofs for several known facts.

Local theta correspondence, founded by Roger Howe in the mid 1970’s, is a major theme in the theory of automorphic forms and representation theory. In a nutshell, local theta correspondence establishes a bijection between certain sets of smooth irreducible representations of reductive groups
G
and
H
which form a
dual pair
, that is,
G
and
H
sit inside a large enough symplectic group in such a way that they form each others’ centralisers. Roughly speaking, this bijection is obtained by considering how the so-called oscillator representation of the ambient symplectic group decomposes as a
G\times H
-representation. When the two groups have “the same size”, the local theta correspondence enjoys several attractive properties, in particular, it preserves temperedness. It is this tempered correspondence in the equal rank case that will be the first ingredient of our paper.

The second ingredient of our paper is the notion of strong Morita equivalence for
C^{*}
-algebras introduced by Mark Rieffel, again, in the mid 1970’s, as part of his
C^{*}
-algebraic generalisation of Mackey’s theory of induced representations of locally compact groups. Given two
C^{*}
-algebras
A
and
B
, roughly put, an
equivalence
A
-
B
-bimodule

X
is an
A
-
B
-bimodule which is equipped with an
A
-valued inner product and a
B
-valued inner product such that these inner products satisfy certain compatibility and continuity conditions. If such a bimodule exists, then
A
and
B
are said to be
strongly Morita equivalent
. This is an equivalence relation.

Given a representation
\pi
of
B
realized on a Hilbert space
V
and an equivalence
A
-
B
-bimodule
X
, one can “induce” it to a representation
\textnormal{\small Ind}_{A}^{B}(X,\pi)
of
A
captured on the Hilbert space
X\otimes_{B}V
obtained by
interior tensor product
(this process is sometimes called
Rieffel induction
). This association is functorial and has an inverse implemented by the dual module of
X
, thus leading to an equivalence of categories of representations of
A
and
B
. It identifies the lattices of two-sided closed ideals of
A
and
B
and furthermore, preserves weak containment and direct integrals.

1.1.
Description of the main result

We bring together the two themes above in the case where
(G,H)
is an equal rank dual pair of the form
\left(Mp_{2n},O_{2n+1}\right)
or
\left(U_{n},U_{n}\right)
over a nonarchimedean local field of characteristic zero. In this case, the theta correspondence
\pi\mapsto\theta(\pi)
establishes a bijection between certain subsets of the tempered duals of
G
and
H
. Let us name these subsets
\widehat{G}_{\theta}
and
\widehat{H}_{\theta}
.

We consider the reduced
C^{*}
-algebras associated to the groups
G
and
H
. These are algebras of operators, going back to Irving Segal, which are obtained from the convolution action of the
L^{1}
-algebra of a locally compact Hausdorff group on its
L^{2}
-space. As such, these
C^{*}
-algebras are directly related to tempered representations. We exhibit ideals
C^{*}_{\theta}(G)
and
C^{*}_{\theta}(H)
of the reduced
C^{*}
-algebras of
G
and
H
whose spectra are homeomorphic to
\widehat{G}_{\theta}
and
\widehat{H}_{\theta}
respectively.

We proceed to show that the (smooth) oscillator representation of
G{\times}H
provides a natural bimodule for the reduced
C^{*}
-algebras of
G
and
H
and that this bimodule can be promoted to an equivalence
C^{*}_{\theta}(G)
-
C^{*}_{\theta}(H)
-bimodule in the sense above. Remarkably, the crucial compatibility property between the
C^{*}_{\theta}(G)
-valued and
C^{*}_{\theta}(H)
-valued inner products turns out to be precisely the so-called
local Rallis inner product formula
of Gan and Ichino
[
9
]
.

We call the equivalence
C^{*}_{\theta}(G)
-
C^{*}_{\theta}(H)
-bimodule above the
oscillator bimodule
and denote it
{\bf\Theta}
. The key point is that given an irreducible representation of
C^{*}_{\theta}(H)
, which is the same as an element of
\widehat{H}_{\theta}
, the induced representation
\textnormal{\small Ind}_{C^{*}_{\theta}(H)}^{C^{*}_{\theta}(G)}({\bf\Theta},\pi)
of
C^{*}_{\theta}(G)
is the (integrated form) of the
G
-representation
\theta(\pi^{*})
where
\pi^{*}
is the contragradient of
\pi
. In fact,
\textnormal{\small Ind}_{C^{*}_{\theta}(H)}^{C^{*}_{\theta}(G)}({\bf\Theta},\pi)
is precisely the (integrated form) of the
G
-representation obtained from
\pi
via the influential “averaging of matrix coefficients” construction of Jian-Shu Li introduced in
[
25
]
in the so-called
stable range
case (roughly speaking, when
G
is at least twice the size of
H
). In the equal rank cases, it is known that Li’s construction, hence ours, agrees with
\theta(\pi^{*})
.

1.2.

At this point, we can point out some immediate implications on theta correspondence that seem to be previously unknown to the best of our knowledge. We see that the tempered theta correspondence, in the equal rank set-up, is simply the restriction of an equivalence of categories of representations of two
C^{*}
-algebras to the irreducible objects. As such it is functorial. Moreover, as it is implemented by an equivalence bimodule, it enjoys various properties, such as the preservation of weak containment. In particular, the tempered theta correspondence is a homeomorphism between
\widehat{G}_{\theta}
and
\widehat{H}_{\theta}
.

1.3.

The oscillator bimodule interpolates the oscillator representation of one group with the regular representation of the other group. This immediately implies that
\widehat{G}_{\theta}
lies in the support of the oscillator representation viewed as a
G
-representation. In fact, we show with an elementary analysis that the latter is precisely the closure of the former. This also follows from a Plancherel decomposition result of Sakellaridis
[
38
]
.

1.4.

As discussed in
[
35
]
, strong Morita equivalence is intimately related to the so-called generalized commutation relations. Our oscillator bimodule construction immediately implies, via Theorem 1.9 of
[
35
]
, that
G
and
H
generate each others’ commutants (in the sense of von Neumann) inside the algebra of bounded linear operators of the Hilbert space carrying the oscillator representation. In
[
20
, Thm. 6.1]
, Howe proves this for general real dual pairs.

1.5.

The oscillator bimodule
{\bf\Theta}
can be viewed as generalization of the
Heisenberg module
of Rieffel which plays an important role in the theory of non-commutative tori and also features in Gabor analysis. Let
W
be a symplectic vector space. Given a closed subgroup
\Gamma
of
W
, Rieffel shows in
[
37
]
that the (twisted)
C^{*}
-algebras associated to

\Gamma
and its dual/annihilator group
\Gamma^{\perp}
are strongly Morita equivalent. The equivalence is implemented by the Heisenberg module based on the (projective) Heisenberg representation of
W
. The critical compatibility condition for the two operator valued inner products on the bimodule reduces to the Poisson transformation in this case.

In the local theta correspondence set-up, we operate inside the symplectic group
Sp(W)
with the roles of
\Gamma,\Gamma^{\perp}
played by the equal rank dual pair
(G,H)
. Accordingly we consider not the Heisenberg representation but the more complicated oscillator representation. In this sense, the local Rallis inner product formula of Gan and Ichino that we used in our proof of the aforementioned compatibility property can be viewed as a non-commutative analogue of the Poisson transform.

1.6.
Applications

After promoting the equal rank tempered theta correspondence to a categorical equivalence, we move on to illustrate the fact that an equivalence bimodule allows the transfer of information between the two sides. We do this with two applications that are attractive in the simplicity of their statements and the elementary nature of their short proofs.

1.6.1.
Explicit transfer of characters

Let us continue with the set-up of the above section. If
\pi
is a tempered irreducible representation of
H

The oscillator bimodule forms a connection between parts of the Schwartz algebras of

1.6.3.
Preservation of formal degrees

It is well-known (see
[
8
]
) that the local theta correspondence takes discrete series representations to discrete series representations in the setting of equal rank pairs. Recall that the
formal degree
of a discrete series representation
\pi
of, say,
H
is the positive real number
{\rm deg}(\pi)
such that

\int_{H}\langle v,\pi(h)(v^{\prime})\rangle\overline{\langle w,\pi(h)(w^{\prime})\rangle}\mathrm{d}s=\dfrac{1}{{\rm deg}(\pi)}\langle v,w\rangle\langle v^{\prime},w^{\prime}\rangle

for all
v,v^{\prime},w,w^{\prime}\in V_{\pi}
. It depends on the chosen Haar measure on
H
.

Using a cohomological argument mixed with some known facts regarding the transfer of trace maps under equivalence bimodules, we obtain the following.

Corollary 1.6.4
.

Let
\pi
be a discrete series representation of
H
which enters the theta correspondence. Then

{\rm deg}(\pi)={\rm deg}(\theta(\pi)).

The Haar measures used in the above result are the ones that we use for the proof the compatibility property (Prop.
5.4.1
) of the oscillator bimodule. The key point of the proof is that discrete series give generators of
K_{0}
of the reduced group
C^{*}
-algebra and one can access their formal degrees using the canonical trace. The canonical trace is given by the orbital integral associated to the trivial conjugacy class. We did not pursue this as we did not need it, but one could also explicitly transfer traces arising for orbital integrals of other conjugacy classes.

Although the above result on preservation of formal degrees result is not new (Gan and Ichino proved it in
[
9
]
), we think that our proof is of interest as a simple application of
K
-theory, which is now readily available. This demonstrates the usefulness of the oscillator bimodule approach for the study of theta correspondence.

2.1.
The Heisenberg representation

Let
{\bf W}
denote the space
W\otimes V
equipped with the symplectic form

{\rm tr}_{E/F}\left(({\cdot},{\cdot})_{W}({\cdot},{\cdot})_{V}\right).

The
Heisenberg group

H({\bf W})

{\bf W}\oplus F
with the multiplication rule

(w,t){\cdot}(w^{\prime},t^{\prime}):=(w+w^{\prime},t+t^{\prime}+\tfrac{1}{2}\langle w,w^{\prime}\rangle).

We fix a non-trivial unitary character
\chi:F\to\mathbb{C}^{1}
. By the Stone-von Neumann Theorem, there exists, up to unitary equivalence, a unique irreducible unitary representation of
H({\bf W})
with central character
\chi

\rho_{\chi}

2.1.1.
The oscillator representation

The group
Sp({\bf W})
of isometries of the symplectic space
{\bf W}
acts on the Heisenberg group
H({\bf W})
as automorphisms via the rule
g{\cdot}(w,t):=(gw,t)

denote the group satisfying the exact sequence

1\to\mathbb{C}^{1}\to\mathcal{M}p({\bf W})\to Sp({\bf W})\to 1.

There is a unique, up to equivalence, unitary representation
\omega_{\chi}
of
\mathcal{M}p({\bf W})
on the Hilbert space of

satisfying the covariance property

(2.1.1)

\omega_{\chi}(\bar{g})\rho_{\chi}(h)\omega_{\chi}(\bar{g}^{-1})=\rho_{\chi}(g{\cdot}h)

and
g\in Sp({\bf W})

\textnormal{End}^{*}(X):=\left\{T:X\to X:\exists\ T^{*}:X\to X\,\,\forall x,y\in X\,\,\langle Tx,y\rangle=\langle x,T^{*}y\rangle\right\}.

Elements of

are automatically bounded and right

for all

). In fact

forms a

-algebra in the operator norm it derives from the norm on

. Thus, for a
C^{*}
-correspondence

, we in fact have a

between
C^{*}
-algebras.

Let
\mathcal{X}
be an
(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})
-correspondence for dense subalgebras
\mathcal{A}\subset A
and
\mathcal{B}\subset B
. We have discussed above that

can be completed to a right Hilbert

. We will now see that if

is a

can be promoted to a

-correspondence for

Proposition 4.2.3
.

Let
\mathcal{A}\subset A
and
\mathcal{B}\subset B
be dense subalgebras and
\mathcal{X}
an
(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})
-correspondence. If
\mathcal{A}
is local, then for all
x\in\mathcal{X}
and

the inequality

(4.2.2)

\langle ax,ax\rangle\hbox{\hbox{}\hskip 0.0pt\raisebox{-5.0pt}{\hbox{$\!\scriptstyle\mathcal{B}$}}\hskip-3.92368pt\hskip 3.92368pt}\leq\|a\|^{2}_{A}\langle x,x\rangle\hbox{\hbox{}\hskip 0.0pt\raisebox{-5.0pt}{\hbox{$\!\scriptstyle\mathcal{B}$}}\hskip-3.92368pt\hskip 3.92368pt},

holds true in the
C^{*}
-algebra

.

Proof.

Let
\varepsilon>0
and for
a\in\mathcal{A}
and
x\in\mathcal{X}
consider

(4.2.3)

\displaystyle(\|a\|^{2}_{A}+\varepsilon)\langle x,x\rangle\hbox{\hbox{}\hskip 0.0pt\raisebox{-5.0pt}{\hbox{$\!\scriptstyle\mathcal{B}$}}\hskip-3.92368pt\hskip 3.92368pt}-\langle a\cdot x,a\cdot x\rangle\hbox{\hbox{}\hskip 0.0pt\raisebox{-5.0pt}{\hbox{$\!\scriptstyle\mathcal{B}$}}\hskip-3.92368pt\hskip 3.92368pt}

\displaystyle=\langle(\|a\|^{2}_{A}+\varepsilon-a^{*}a)x,x\rangle_{\mathcal{B}}

and observe that by spectral invariance the element

is positive invertible, and thus has spectrum contained in

for some

. Since the square-root function on

we have that

\displaystyle\langle(\|a\|^{2}_{A}+\varepsilon-a^{*}a)x,x\rangle_{\mathcal{B}}

\displaystyle=\langle(\|a\|^{2}_{A}+\varepsilon-a^{*}a)^{1/2}x,(\|a\|^{2}_{A}+\varepsilon-a^{*}a)^{1/2}x\rangle_{\mathcal{B}}\geq 0,

and therefore by (

)

(4.2.4)

\langle ax,ax\rangle\hbox{\hbox{}\hskip 0.0pt\raisebox{-5.0pt}{\hbox{$\!\scriptstyle\mathcal{B}$}}\hskip-3.92368pt\hskip 3.92368pt}\leq\|a\|^{2}_{A}\langle x,x\rangle\hbox{\hbox{}\hskip 0.0pt\raisebox{-5.0pt}{\hbox{$\!\scriptstyle\mathcal{B}$}}\hskip-3.92368pt\hskip 3.92368pt}+\varepsilon\langle x,x\rangle\hbox{\hbox{}\hskip 0.0pt\raisebox{-5.0pt}{\hbox{$\!\scriptstyle\mathcal{B}$}}\hskip-3.92368pt\hskip 3.92368pt}.

Since (
4.2.4
) holds for all

) holds as desired.
∎

4.3.
Induction of representations of
C^{*}
-algebras

For
C^{*}
-correspondences over a pair of
C^{*}
-algebras
(A,B)
there is a far reaching theory of induced representations. Due to the Gelfand-Naimark-Segal theorem, a
C^{*}
-algebra usually admits numerous Hilbert space representations. A

-correspondence for

allows one to construct a representation of

4.3.1.
The interior tensor product

Let
A
and
B
be
C^{*}
-algebras and

a
C^{*}
-correspondence for
(A,B)
. Then we can “induce” representations of

to

via

via the following tensor product construction.

Proposition 4.3.2

(
[
23
, Proposition 4.5]
)
.

Let
X
be a
C^{*}
-correspondence for
(A,B)
and
\pi:B\to\mathcal{L}(V_{\pi})
a representation of
B
on a Hilbert space
V_{\pi}
. Consider
X\otimes^{\mathrm{alg}}V_{\pi}
the algebraic tensor product of vector spaces. The right sesquilinear form

(4.3.1)

(x\otimes v,x^{\prime}\otimes v^{\prime}):=\langle v,\pi(\langle x,x^{\prime}\rangle\hbox{\hbox{}\hskip 0.0pt\raisebox{-5.0pt}{\hbox{$\!\scriptstyle B$}}\hskip-4.71637pt\hskip 4.71637pt})v^{\prime}\rangle\hbox{\hbox{}\hskip 0.0pt\raisebox{-5.0pt}{\hbox{$\!\scriptstyle V_{\pi}$}}\hskip-9.43358pt\hskip 9.43358pt}

is positive and its radical

is equal to the

spanned by elements of the form

The completion of
(X\otimes^{\mathrm{alg}}V_{\pi})/N_{\pi}
with respect to the inner product (

) is a Hilbert space that we denote by

and is commonly called is the
internal tensor product of
X
and
V_{\pi}
over
B
.

Proof.

The proof of positivity is based on the fact that for
x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}\in X
the matrix
\varepsilon:=\pi(\langle x_{i},x_{j})_{B})_{ij}\in M_{n}(\mathcal{L}(V))
is a positive operator on
V^{n}
, so that for
\xi=\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}\otimes v_{i}
we have

\displaystyle(\xi,\xi)=(v,\varepsilon\cdot v)\geq 0,\quad v:=\begin{pmatrix}v_{1}\\
\vdots\\
v_{n}\end{pmatrix}\in V^{n}.

The fact that the radical coincides with the balancing subspace uses positivity of the matrix
\varepsilon
and the fact the
M_{n}(B)
is again a
C^{*}
-algebra, so we can extract square roots.
∎

It can then be shown that the action

a(x\otimes v):=ax\otimes v

of
A
on the space

gives rise to representation of
A
on the Hilbert space

which we will denote

\textnormal{\small Ind}_{B}^{A}(X,\pi),

and refer to as the
A
-representation
induced from
\pi
via

.

4.3.3.
Functoriality of induction

The induction procedure that we described above is functorial (see
[
34
, Prop. 2.69]
).

Proposition 4.3.4
.

Let
A,B
be two
C^{*}
-algebras and let

be a
C^{*}
-correspondence for
(A,B)
. Assume that the action of

on

is non-degenerate (i.e.

). Then the map

is a functor from the category of non-degenerate representations of

with bounded intertwining operators to the corresponding category of

,
which at the level of morphisms takes the form

It follows that induction respects unitary equivalence and direct sums.

12

12

4.4.
Equivalence bimodules

Definition 4.4.1
.

Let
\mathcal{A}\subset A
and
\mathcal{B}\subset B
be local subalgebras and
\mathcal{X}
an algebraic
(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})
-bimodule in the usual sense that the two actions commute. We call
\mathcal{X}
an
inner product bimodule
if

(1)

\mathcal{X}
is a left inner product
\mathcal{A}
-module and a right inner product
\mathcal{B}
-module,

(2)

for all
x,y\in\mathcal{X}
,
a\in\mathcal{A}
and
b\in\mathcal{B}
, we have

(4.4.1)

\langle a{\cdot}x,y\rangle\hbox{\hbox{}\hskip 0.0pt\raisebox{-5.0pt}{\hbox{$\!\scriptstyle\mathcal{B}$}}\hskip-3.92368pt\hskip 3.92368pt}=\langle x,a^{*}{\cdot}y\rangle\hbox{\hbox{}\hskip 0.0pt\raisebox{-5.0pt}{\hbox{$\!\scriptstyle\mathcal{B}$}}\hskip-3.92368pt\hskip 3.92368pt}\qquad\prescript{}{\mathcal{A}}{\langle x{\cdot}b,y\rangle}=\prescript{}{\mathcal{A}}{\langle x,y{\cdot}b^{*}\rangle},

(3)

for all
x,y,z\in\mathcal{X}
, we have

(4.4.2)

\prescript{}{\mathcal{A}}{\langle x,y\rangle}{\cdot}z=x{\cdot}\langle y,z\rangle\hbox{\hbox{}\hskip 0.0pt\raisebox{-5.0pt}{\hbox{$\!\scriptstyle\mathcal{B}$}}\hskip-3.92368pt\hskip 3.92368pt}.

4.4.2.
Strong Morita equivalence of
C^{*}
-algebras

Given an inner product bimodule
\mathcal{X}
for local subalgebras
\mathcal{A}\subset A
and
\mathcal{B}\subset B
, we obtain a norm on
\mathcal{X}
for each of the inner products. The following is well-known (see e.g.
[
34
, Prop. 3.11]
). We give the short proof here for the convenience of the reader.

Proposition 4.4.3
.

Let
\mathcal{A}\subset A
and
\mathcal{B}\subset B
be local subalgebras and
\mathcal{X}
an
(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})
inner product bimodule. Then the norms

(4.4.3)

\|x\|_{A}:=\|_{\mathcal{A}}\langle x,x\rangle\|_{A}^{1/2},\quad\|x\|_{B}:=\|\langle x,x\rangle_{\mathcal{B}}\|_{B}^{1/2},

on
\mathcal{X}
are equal.

Proof.

Using Definition
4.4.1
and the Cauchy-Schwartz inequality for Hilbert
C^{*}
-modules, we have

\displaystyle\left\lVert\langle x,x\rangle\hbox{\hbox{}\hskip 0.0pt\raisebox{-5.0pt}{\hbox{$\!\scriptstyle\mathcal{B}$}}\hskip-3.92368pt\hskip 3.92368pt}\right\rVert^{2}_{B}

\displaystyle=\left\lVert\langle x,x\rangle\hbox{\hbox{}\hskip 0.0pt\raisebox{-5.0pt}{\hbox{$\!\scriptstyle\mathcal{B}$}}\hskip-3.92368pt\hskip 3.92368pt}\langle x,x\rangle\hbox{\hbox{}\hskip 0.0pt\raisebox{-5.0pt}{\hbox{$\!\scriptstyle\mathcal{B}$}}\hskip-3.92368pt\hskip 3.92368pt}\right\rVert_{B}=\left\lVert\langle x,x{\cdot}\langle x,x\rangle\hbox{\hbox{}\hskip 0.0pt\raisebox{-5.0pt}{\hbox{$\!\scriptstyle\mathcal{B}$}}\hskip-3.92368pt\hskip 3.92368pt}\rangle\hbox{\hbox{}\hskip 0.0pt\raisebox{-5.0pt}{\hbox{$\!\scriptstyle\mathcal{B}$}}\hskip-3.92368pt\hskip 3.92368pt}\right\rVert_{B}=\left\lVert\langle x,\prescript{}{\mathcal{A}}{\langle x,x\rangle}{\cdot}x\rangle\hbox{\hbox{}\hskip 0.0pt\raisebox{-5.0pt}{\hbox{$\!\scriptstyle\mathcal{B}$}}\hskip-3.92368pt\hskip 3.92368pt}\right\rVert_{B}

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert\langle x,x\rangle\hbox{\hbox{}\hskip 0.0pt\raisebox{-5.0pt}{\hbox{$\!\scriptstyle\mathcal{B}$}}\hskip-3.92368pt\hskip 3.92368pt}\right\rVert^{1/2}_{B}\left\lVert\langle\prescript{}{\mathcal{A}}{\langle x,x\rangle}{\cdot}x,\prescript{}{\mathcal{A}}{\langle x,x\rangle}{\cdot}x\rangle\hbox{\hbox{}\hskip 0.0pt\raisebox{-5.0pt}{\hbox{$\!\scriptstyle\mathcal{B}$}}\hskip-3.92368pt\hskip 3.92368pt}\right\rVert^{1/2}_{B},

for all
x\in\mathcal{X}
. Applying Proposition
4.2.3
to the second factor in the last term, we conclude that

\left\lVert\langle x,x\rangle\hbox{\hbox{}\hskip 0.0pt\raisebox{-5.0pt}{\hbox{$\!\scriptstyle\mathcal{B}$}}\hskip-3.92368pt\hskip 3.92368pt}\right\rVert^{2}_{B}\leq\left\lVert\langle x,x\rangle\hbox{\hbox{}\hskip 0.0pt\raisebox{-5.0pt}{\hbox{$\!\scriptstyle\mathcal{B}$}}\hskip-3.92368pt\hskip 3.92368pt}\right\rVert^{1/2}_{B}\left\lVert\prescript{}{\mathcal{A}}{\langle x,x\rangle}\right\rVert_{A}\left\lVert\langle x,x\rangle\hbox{\hbox{}\hskip 0.0pt\raisebox{-5.0pt}{\hbox{$\!\scriptstyle\mathcal{B}$}}\hskip-3.92368pt\hskip 3.92368pt}\right\rVert^{1/2}_{B}.

Cancelling a factor of
\left\lVert\langle x,x\rangle\hbox{\hbox{}\hskip 0.0pt\raisebox{-5.0pt}{\hbox{$\!\scriptstyle\mathcal{B}$}}\hskip-3.92368pt\hskip 3.92368pt}\right\rVert_{B}
gives us
\left\lVert\langle x,x\rangle\hbox{\hbox{}\hskip 0.0pt\raisebox{-5.0pt}{\hbox{$\!\scriptstyle\mathcal{B}$}}\hskip-3.92368pt\hskip 3.92368pt}\right\rVert_{B}\leq\left\lVert\prescript{}{\mathcal{A}}{\langle x,x\rangle}\right\rVert_{A}
. Swapping the roles of
A
and
B
, we obtain the opposite inequality and obtain the desired equality of norms.
∎

Definition 4.4.4
.

Let
A
and
B
be
C^{*}
-algebras. A
Hilbert
C^{*}
-bimodule
for
(A,B)
is an inner product bimodule that is complete in the norms

\|x\|_{A}^{2}=\|\prescript{}{A}{\langle x,x\rangle}\|_{A}=\|x\|^{2}_{B}=\|\langle x,x\rangle\hbox{\hbox{}\hskip 0.0pt\raisebox{-5.0pt}{\hbox{$\!\scriptstyle B$}}\hskip-4.71637pt\hskip 4.71637pt}\|_{B}.

A Hilbert
C^{*}
-bimodule for
(A,B)
is a
(Morita) equivalence bimodule
if both inner products are full. Two
C^{*}
-algebras
A
and
B
are called
strongly Morita equivalent
if there exists an
(A,B)
Morita equivalence bimodule
X
.

While for unital
C^{*}
-algebras
A
and
B
, this notion coincides with classical Morita equivalence as rings, in general, it is stronger than the classical notion (hence the name).

4.5.

We have the notion of “dual” of an
(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})
-inner product bimodule. This is a
(\mathcal{B},\mathcal{A})
-inner product bimodule which is defined as follows.

Definition 4.5.1
.

Let
\mathcal{X}
be an
(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})
-inner product bimodule and
\mathcal{X}^{*}
its conjugate vector space. By definition, we have an anti-linear bijection
\beta:\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{X}^{*}
such that
\beta(\lambda{\cdot}x)=\overline{\lambda}{\cdot}\beta(x)
for every
x\in\mathcal{X}
and
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
a complex scalar. The
dual module of
\mathcal{X}
is
\mathcal{X}^{*}
equipped with the following
(\mathcal{B},\mathcal{A})
-inner product bimodule structure

b{\cdot}\beta(x):=\beta(xb^{*}),\qquad\prescript{}{\mathcal{B}}{\langle\beta(x),\beta(y)\rangle}:=\langle x,y\rangle\hbox{\hbox{}\hskip 0.0pt\raisebox{-5.0pt}{\hbox{$\!\scriptstyle\mathcal{B}$}}\hskip-3.92368pt\hskip 3.92368pt},

\beta(x){\cdot}a:=\beta(a^{*}x),\qquad\langle\beta(x),\beta(y)\rangle\hbox{\hbox{}\hskip 0.0pt\raisebox{-5.0pt}{\hbox{$\!\scriptstyle\mathcal{A}$}}\hskip-4.23619pt\hskip 4.23619pt}:=\prescript{}{\mathcal{A}}{\langle x,y\rangle}.

4.6.

If
X
is an
(A,B)
-equivalence bimodule, then the dual module
X^{*}
is a
(B,A)
-equivalence bimodule. In fact there are isomorphisms of interior tensor products

X\otimes_{B}X^{*}\simeq A,\quad X^{*}\otimes_{A}X\simeq B,

as
(A,A)
and
(B,B)

C^{*}
-bimodules, respectively.

It follows that the induction functors associated to
X
and to
X^{*}
are inverses to each other and therefore, by Prop.
4.3.6
, they implement a homeomorphism between the spectra of
A
and
B
. In particular, the categories of representations of
A
and
B
are equivalent.

4.7.

Given a two-sided closed ideal
J
of
B
, the space
X_{J}
given by the closure of the linear span of all
x{\cdot}b
with
x\in X
and
b\in J
forms an
(I,J)
-equivalence bimodule where
I
is the two-sided closed ideal of
A
given by the closure of the linear span of all
\prescript{}{A}{\langle x{\cdot}b,y\rangle}
with
x,y\in X
and
b\in J
. We will view this association as an
induction of ideals
implemented by
X
and accordingly denote
I
by

\textnormal{\small Ind}_{B}^{A}(X,J).

It is well-known (see
[
34
, Props. 3.24 and 3.25]
) that the map
J\mapsto\textnormal{\small Ind}_{B}^{A}(X,J)
sets up a bijection between the two-sided closed ideals of
B
and
A
which respects inclusion of ideals (thus, it identifies the lattices of ideals of
B
and
A
). Moreover, the induction of ideals is compatible with the induction of representations, that is, if
\pi
is a representation of
B
, then

\textnormal{\small Ind}_{B}^{A}(X,{\rm ker}(\pi))={\rm ker}(\textnormal{\small Ind}_{B}^{A}(X,\pi)).

4.8.

Lastly, we observe that any Hilbert
C^{*}
-bimodule induces a Morita equivalence between certain associated
C^{*}
-algebras. Suppose that
X
is a Hilbert
C^{*}
-bimodule for
(A,B)
. The sets

\displaystyle I_{X}

\displaystyle=\,_{A}\langle X,X\rangle:=\overline{\textnormal{span}\left\{{}_{A}\langle x,y\rangle:x,y\in X\right\}}\subset A,

\displaystyle J_{X}

\displaystyle=\langle X,X\rangle_{B}:=\overline{\textnormal{span}\left\{\langle x,y\rangle_{B}:x,y\in X\right\}}\subset B,

form closed two-sided ideals (in particular,
C^{*}
-subalgebras) of
A
and
B
respectively. Since
X
is an
(A,B)
Hilbert
C^{*}
-bimodule and the inner products in fact take their values in the ideals
I_{X}\subset A
and

, we can view
X
as Hilbert
C^{*}
-bimodule over
(I_{X},J_{X})
. By construction, the inner products are now full so
X
is a Morita equivalence bimodule for
(I_{X},J_{X})
. We now summarise our findings for future reference.

Proposition 4.8.1
.

Let
A
and
B
be
C^{*}
-algebras and
\mathcal{A}\subset A
and
\mathcal{B}\subset B
local subalgebras. Suppose that
\mathcal{X}
is a nondegenerate
(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})
inner product bimodule and denote by

the completion of
\mathcal{X}
in the norm (
4.4.3
). Then
X
is an
(A,B)
Hilbert
C^{*}
-bimodule and hence a Morita equivalence bimodule for the pair of ideals
(I_{X},J_{X})
.

For all

of

Proof.

This is well-known to the experts. It follows from the matrix coefficient estimates of Li (see Cor. 3.4 and proof of Thm 3.2 in

Observe that in particular, the oscillator representation, when restricted to

5.2.
Right inner product module structure

We equip
\mathbb{S}
with a
right

\mathcal{S}(H)
-module structure as follows: for
x\in\mathbb{S}

(5.2.1)

x{\cdot}b:=\int_{H}b(h)\omega(h^{-1})(x)\ \mathrm{d}h,\qquad b\in\mathcal{S}(H).

Note that
x{\cdot}b
is well-defined and belongs to
\mathbb{S}
since
\omega
is tempered as an
H
-representation (see Prop.
5.1.1
). Next, we equip
\mathbb{S}
with an
\mathcal{S}(H)
-valued sesquilinear map

(5.2.2)

\langle x,y\rangle_{H}(h):=\langle x,\omega(h)(y)\rangle,\qquad x,y\in\mathbb{S},\ h\in H

The form
\langle{\cdot},{\cdot}\rangle_{H}
is Hermitian: for all
h\in H
and
x,y\in\mathbb{S}
, we have

\langle x,y\rangle\hbox{\hbox{}\hskip 0.0pt\raisebox{-5.0pt}{\hbox{$\!\scriptstyle H$}}\hskip-5.4181pt\hskip 5.4181pt}^{*}(h)=\overline{\langle x,y\rangle\hbox{\hbox{}\hskip 0.0pt\raisebox{-5.0pt}{\hbox{$\!\scriptstyle H$}}\hskip-5.4181pt\hskip 5.4181pt}(h^{-1})}=\overline{\langle x,\omega(h^{-1})(y)\rangle}=\overline{\langle\omega(h)(x),y\rangle}=\langle y,x\rangle\hbox{\hbox{}\hskip 0.0pt\raisebox{-5.0pt}{\hbox{$\!\scriptstyle H$}}\hskip-5.4181pt\hskip 5.4181pt}(h).

It is routine to check that the form
\langle{\cdot},{\cdot}\rangle_{H}
is compatible with the right
\mathcal{S}(H)
-module structure given above, that is,

\langle x,y{\cdot}b\rangle\hbox{\hbox{}\hskip 0.0pt\raisebox{-5.0pt}{\hbox{$\!\scriptstyle H$}}\hskip-5.4181pt\hskip 5.4181pt}=\langle x,y\rangle\hbox{\hbox{}\hskip 0.0pt\raisebox{-5.0pt}{\hbox{$\!\scriptstyle H$}}\hskip-5.4181pt\hskip 5.4181pt}b.

Proposition 5.2.1
.

Equipped with the right module structure (
5.2.1
) and the form (
5.2.2
), the space
\mathbb{S}
becomes a nondegenerate right inner product module over
\mathcal{S}(H)
.

Proof.

We just need to prove that the form
\langle{\cdot},{\cdot}\rangle\hbox{\hbox{}\hskip 0.0pt\raisebox{-5.0pt}{\hbox{$\!\scriptstyle H$}}\hskip-5.4181pt\hskip 5.4181pt}
is positive definite, that is, for any
x\in\mathbb{S}
, we have

\langle x,x\rangle\hbox{\hbox{}\hskip 0.0pt\raisebox{-5.0pt}{\hbox{$\!\scriptstyle H$}}\hskip-5.4181pt\hskip 5.4181pt}\geq 0
as an element of the
C^{*}
-algebra
C^{*}_{r}(H)
and that
\langle x,x\rangle\hbox{\hbox{}\hskip 0.0pt\raisebox{-5.0pt}{\hbox{$\!\scriptstyle H$}}\hskip-5.4181pt\hskip 5.4181pt}=0
only when
x=0
.

To show the former, it is enough to exhibit an injective representation
\Pi
of
C^{*}_{r}(H)
with the property that
\Pi(\langle\varphi,\varphi\rangle\hbox{\hbox{}\hskip 0.0pt\raisebox{-5.0pt}{\hbox{$\!\scriptstyle H$}}\hskip-5.4181pt\hskip 5.4181pt})
is a positive operator for every
\varphi\in\mathbb{S}
. If we prove that
\pi(\langle\varphi,\varphi\rangle\hbox{\hbox{}\hskip 0.0pt\raisebox{-5.0pt}{\hbox{$\!\scriptstyle H$}}\hskip-5.4181pt\hskip 5.4181pt})
is a positive operator for every
\pi
in the spectrum of
C^{*}_{r}(H)
, then we will be done by considering the representation

\Pi=\bigoplus_{\pi\in\widehat{C^{*}_{r}(H)}}\pi

which is injective since for every
a\in C^{*}_{r}(H)
, there is an irreducible representation
\pi
such that
\left\lVert a\right\rVert=\left\lVert\pi(a)\right\rVert
(see e.g.
[
34
, Thm. A.14]
). Therefore it suffices to prove that

\pi(\langle x,x\rangle\hbox{\hbox{}\hskip 0.0pt\raisebox{-5.0pt}{\hbox{$\!\scriptstyle H$}}\hskip-5.4181pt\hskip 5.4181pt})\geq 0

as an operator on
V_{\pi}
for every
\pi
in the spectrum of
C^{*}_{r}(H)
.

Let
x,x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{S}
and consider the operator
\pi(\langle x,x^{\prime}\rangle\hbox{\hbox{}\hskip 0.0pt\raisebox{-5.0pt}{\hbox{$\!\scriptstyle H$}}\hskip-5.4181pt\hskip 5.4181pt})
on
V_{\pi}
. This operator is determined by the bilinear form

\bigl\langle v,\pi(\langle x,x^{\prime}\rangle\hbox{\hbox{}\hskip 0.0pt\raisebox{-5.0pt}{\hbox{$\!\scriptstyle H$}}\hskip-5.4181pt\hskip 5.4181pt})(v^{\prime})\bigr\rangle

for
v,v^{\prime}\in V_{\pi}
. We unfold the left hand side

\displaystyle\left\langle v,\pi(\langle x,x^{\prime}\rangle\hbox{\hbox{}\hskip 0.0pt\raisebox{-5.0pt}{\hbox{$\!\scriptstyle H$}}\hskip-5.4181pt\hskip 5.4181pt})(v^{\prime})\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle v,\int_{H}\langle x,x^{\prime}\rangle\hbox{\hbox{}\hskip 0.0pt\raisebox{-5.0pt}{\hbox{$\!\scriptstyle H$}}\hskip-5.4181pt\hskip 5.4181pt}(h)\pi_{h}(v^{\prime})\mathrm{d}h\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\int_{H}\langle x,x^{\prime}\rangle\hbox{\hbox{}\hskip 0.0pt\raisebox{-5.0pt}{\hbox{$\!\scriptstyle H$}}\hskip-5.4181pt\hskip 5.4181pt}(h)\langle v,\pi_{h}(v^{\prime})\rangle\mathrm{d}h

\displaystyle=\int_{H}\langle x,\omega(h)(x^{\prime})\rangle\langle v,\pi_{h}(v^{\prime})\rangle\mathrm{d}h

\displaystyle=(x{\otimes}v,x^{\prime}{\otimes}v^{\prime})_{\pi}

where
({\cdot},{\cdot})_{\pi}
is the Hermitian form on
\mathbb{S}\otimes V_{\pi}
(see
2.5.1
). The latter is non-negative thanks to Prop.
2.6.1
. Therefore, we conclude that for
x\in\mathbb{S}
and
v\in V_{\pi}
we have

\bigl\langle v,\pi(\langle x,x\rangle_{H})v\bigr\rangle=\bigl(x{\otimes}v,x{\otimes}v\bigr)_{\pi}\geq 0

which implies that
\langle x,x\rangle_{H}\geq 0
in
C^{*}_{r}(H)
.

Finally, to show definiteness, suppose
\langle x,x\rangle_{H}=0
, so that

\langle\omega(h)x,x\rangle=0,\quad\forall h\in H.

Then in particular, for
h=e
we find that
\langle x,x\rangle=0
, so that
x=0
in the oscillator representation. Since
\mathbb{S}
injects into the oscillator representation, we conclude that
x=0
in
\mathbb{S}
.∎

5.4.
Key compatibility property

In fact
\mathbb{S}
is an inner product bimodule for
(\mathcal{S}(G),\mathcal{S}(H))
in the sense of Definition
4.4.1
. We will prove the following compatibility between the two inner products: for
x,y,z\in\mathbb{S}

(5.4.1)

\prescript{}{G}{\langle x,y\rangle}{\cdot}z=x{\cdot}\langle y,z\rangle\hbox{\hbox{}\hskip 0.0pt\raisebox{-5.0pt}{\hbox{$\!\scriptstyle H$}}\hskip-5.4181pt\hskip 5.4181pt}

A convenient reformulation is as follows, for
x,y,z,u\in\mathbb{S}
,

\Bigl\langle\prescript{}{G}{\langle x,y\rangle}{\cdot}z,u\Bigr\rangle=\left\langle x{\cdot}\langle y,z\rangle\hbox{\hbox{}\hskip 0.0pt\raisebox{-5.0pt}{\hbox{$\!\scriptstyle H$}}\hskip-5.4181pt\hskip 5.4181pt},u\right\rangle

Unfolding two sides, we obtain

\displaystyle\Bigl\langle\prescript{}{G}{\langle x,y\rangle}{\cdot}z,u\Bigr\rangle=\left\langle\int_{G}\prescript{}{G}{\langle x,y\rangle}(g)\ \omega(g)(z)\mathrm{d}g,u\right\rangle=\int_{G}\langle x,\omega(g)(y)\rangle\overline{\langle u,\omega(g)(z)\rangle}\mathrm{d}g

and

\displaystyle\left\langle x{\cdot}\langle y,z\rangle\hbox{\hbox{}\hskip 0.0pt\raisebox{-5.0pt}{\hbox{$\!\scriptstyle H$}}\hskip-5.4181pt\hskip 5.4181pt},u\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle\int_{H}\langle y,z\rangle\hbox{\hbox{}\hskip 0.0pt\raisebox{-5.0pt}{\hbox{$\!\scriptstyle H$}}\hskip-5.4181pt\hskip 5.4181pt}(h)\ \omega(h^{-1})(x)\mathrm{d}h,u\right\rangle=\int_{H}\overline{\langle y,\omega(h)(z)\rangle}\langle x,\omega(h)(u)\rangle\mathrm{d}h

Thus we arrive at the formulation

(5.4.2)

\int_{G}\langle x,\omega(g)(y)\rangle\overline{\langle u,\omega(g)(z)\rangle}\mathrm{d}g=\int_{H}\langle x,\omega(h)(u)\rangle\overline{\langle y,\omega(h)(z)\rangle}\mathrm{d}h.

We will now prove the above equality holds once we scale the Haar measures on
G
and
H
appropriately.

Proposition 5.4.1
.

The Haar measures on
G
and
H
may be chosen in such a way that Equation (

Straight-forward calculations show that

where we consider

with the diagonal action of

6.
The
\theta
-subalgebras and induced representations

6.1.
Truncation

We will now apply the final statement of Proposition
4.8.1
to make
{\bf\Theta}
into an equivalence bimodule for the ideals
C^{*}_{\theta}(G)\subset C^{*}_{r}(G)
and
C^{*}_{\theta}(H)\subset C^{*}_{r}(H)
generated by the span of the left- and right inner products, respectively. We will then analyse their spectra in terms of the local theta correspondence.

6.1.1.
Truncate
C^{*}_{r}(H)

As mentioned in Section
4.2
, the linear span of the range of the form
\langle{\cdot},{\cdot}\rangle\hbox{\hbox{}\hskip 0.0pt\raisebox{-5.0pt}{\hbox{$\!\scriptstyle H$}}\hskip-5.4181pt\hskip 5.4181pt}
is a two-sided ideal of
\mathcal{S}(H)
. Let us denote this linear span by
\langle\mathbb{S},\mathbb{S}\rangle\hbox{\hbox{}\hskip 0.0pt\raisebox{-5.0pt}{\hbox{$\!\scriptstyle H$}}\hskip-5.4181pt\hskip 5.4181pt}
. Consider its
C^{*}
-closure

(6.1.1)

C^{*}_{\theta}(H):=\overline{\langle\mathbb{S},\mathbb{S}\rangle\hbox{\hbox{}\hskip 0.0pt\raisebox{-5.0pt}{\hbox{$\!\scriptstyle H$}}\hskip-5.4181pt\hskip 5.4181pt}}^{C^{*}_{r}(H)}.

Thus
C^{*}_{\theta}(H)
is closed two-sided ideal of
C^{*}_{r}(H)
.

Proposition 6.1.2
.

The spectrum of
C^{*}_{\theta}(H)
can be identified with the set of tempered irreducible representations
\pi
of
H
for which

\theta(\pi)\not=0
.

Proof.

Basic theory tells us that the spectrum of
C^{*}_{\theta}(H)
is simply the subset of the spectrum of
C^{*}_{r}(H)
made precisely of those elements which do not vanish on
C^{*}_{\theta}(H)
.

Let
\pi
be a tempered irreducible representation of
H
(in other words, an element of the spectrum of
C^{*}_{r}(H)
). Observe that
\pi
vanishes on
C^{*}_{\theta}(H)
if and only if it vanishes on the range of
\langle{\cdot},{\cdot}\rangle\hbox{\hbox{}\hskip 0.0pt\raisebox{-5.0pt}{\hbox{$\!\scriptstyle H$}}\hskip-5.4181pt\hskip 5.4181pt}
, thanks to density of the latter in the former. Let
x,x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{S}
. Then
\pi(\langle x,x^{\prime}\rangle\hbox{\hbox{}\hskip 0.0pt\raisebox{-5.0pt}{\hbox{$\!\scriptstyle H$}}\hskip-5.4181pt\hskip 5.4181pt})
is the zero operator on
V_{\pi}
if and only if

\bigl\langle v,\pi(\langle x,x^{\prime}\rangle\hbox{\hbox{}\hskip 0.0pt\raisebox{-5.0pt}{\hbox{$\!\scriptstyle H$}}\hskip-5.4181pt\hskip 5.4181pt})(v^{\prime})\bigr\rangle=0

for all
v,v^{\prime}\in V_{\pi}
. As seen in the proof of Prop.
5.2.1
, we have

\left\langle v,\pi(\langle x,x^{\prime}\rangle\hbox{\hbox{}\hskip 0.0pt\raisebox{-5.0pt}{\hbox{$\!\scriptstyle H$}}\hskip-5.4181pt\hskip 5.4181pt})(v^{\prime})\right\rangle=(x{\otimes}v,x^{\prime}{\otimes}v^{\prime})_{\pi}

where
({\cdot},{\cdot})_{\pi}
is the Hermitian form on
\mathbb{S}\otimes V_{\pi}
(see
2.5.1
). Therefore, we conclude that
\pi
vanishes on
C^{*}_{\theta}(H)
if and only the form
({\cdot},{\cdot})_{\pi}
, hence
L(\pi)
is zero. However, by Prop.
2.6.1
,
L(\pi)
is isomorphic to
\theta(\pi^{*})=\Theta(\pi^{*})
. The claim now follows from the fact that
\Theta(\pi^{*})
is non-zero if and only if
\Theta(\pi)
is non-zero, an immediate corollary of Lemma 6.1 of
[
9
]
.
∎

6.1.3.
Truncate
C^{*}_{r}(G)

The linear span of the range of
\prescript{}{G}{\langle\mathbb{S},\mathbb{S}\rangle}
is a two-sided ideal of
\mathcal{S}(G)
. Consider its
C^{*}
-closure

(6.1.2)

C^{*}_{\theta}(G):=\overline{\prescript{}{G}{\langle\mathbb{S},\mathbb{S}\rangle}}^{C^{*}_{r}(G)}.

Then
C^{*}_{\theta}(G)
is a closed two-sided ideal of
C^{*}_{r}(G)
, and hence is a

-subalgebra.

Proposition 6.1.4
.

The spectrum of
C^{*}_{\theta}(G)
can be identified with tempered irreducible (necessarily genuine) representations
\pi
of

for which

\theta(\pi)\not=0
.

Proof.

The proof is the same as that of Prop.
6.1.2
. Given tempered irreducible representation
\pi
of
G
,
x,x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{S}
and

, we observe that

(6.1.3)

\displaystyle\left\langle v,\pi(\prescript{}{G}{\langle x,x^{\prime}\rangle})(v^{\prime})\right\rangle=\int_{G}\langle\omega_{g}(x),x^{\prime}\rangle\langle v,\pi_{g}(v^{\prime})\rangle\mathrm{d}g=\int_{G}\overline{\langle x,\omega_{g}(x^{\prime})\rangle}\langle v,\pi_{g}(v^{\prime})\rangle\mathrm{d}g.

This is the conjugate of the Hermitian form
({\cdot},{\cdot})_{\overline{\pi}}
on
\mathbb{S}\otimes V_{\overline{\pi}}
(see
2.5.1
) where
\overline{\pi}
is the conjugate representation on
\overline{V_{\pi}}
. Therefore, we conclude that
\pi
vanishes on

if and only if
({\cdot},{\cdot})_{\overline{\pi}}
, hence
({\cdot},{\cdot})_{\pi}
, is zero. The claim now follows from Prop.
2.6.1
as explained in the proof of Prop.
6.1.2
.
∎

6.2.
The induced

Now, given an irreducible representation of

6.3.
Functoriality

The induction of representations implemented by
{\bf\Theta}
establishes an equivalence between the categories of representations of the ideal

of

and of the ideal

6.5.
Support of the oscillator representation

In this section, we make some elementary observations regarding the role played by the oscillator representation in our picture of the theta correspondence. Recall that the oscillator representation in the equal rank case is tempered both as a

Proposition 6.5.1
.

Denote by
(\omega,V_{\omega})
the oscillator representation and by
(\rho,L^{2}(H))
the left regular representation of

.
The map

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}_{{\bf\Theta}}:\mathbb{S}

\displaystyle\otimes^{\mathrm{alg}}\mathcal{S}(H)\to\mathbb{S}

\displaystyle\otimes f\mapsto x{\cdot}f,

induces a

\displaystyle U_{{\bf\Theta}}:{\bf\Theta}\otimes_{C^{*}_{r}(H)}L^{2}(H)

\displaystyle\to V_{\omega}.

Proof.

The map

has dense range since the unit element of

and thus for any approximate unit

converges to

. It thus suffices to show that

is an isometry. This is established by the following calculation.

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{U}_{{\bf\Theta}}(x\otimes f),\mathcal{U}_{{\bf\Theta}}(y\otimes g)\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle x\cdot f,y\cdot g\rangle=\int_{H}\int_{H}\langle\omega(s)f(s^{-1})x,\omega(t)g(t^{-1})y\rangle\mathrm{d}s\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\int_{H}\int_{H}\langle f(s^{-1})x,\omega(t)g(t^{-1}s^{-1})y\rangle\mathrm{d}s\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\int_{H}\int_{H}\langle f(s)x,\omega(t)g(t^{-1}s)y\rangle\mathrm{d}s\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\int_{H}\int_{H}\overline{f(s)}\langle x,\omega(t)y\rangle g(t^{-1}s)\mathrm{d}s\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\int_{H}\int_{H}\overline{f(s)}\langle x,\omega(t)y\rangle(\rho(t)g)(s)\mathrm{d}s\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\int_{H}\overline{f(s)}\rho(\langle x,y\rangle_{{\bf\Theta}})g(s)\mathrm{d}s\mathrm{d}t=\langle f,\rho(\langle x,y\rangle_{{\bf\Theta}})g\rangle_{L^{2}(H)}.

The

-equivariance now follows since
\mathcal{U}_{{\bf\Theta}}(gx\otimes f)=(gx)\cdot f=g(x\cdot f)=g(\mathcal{U}_{{\bf\Theta}}(x\otimes f))
.
∎

In accordance with the notation we introduced in Section

Corollary 6.5.2
.

Denote by

8.3.
Traces

Let

-algebra and let

denote its cone of positive elements. By a

\chi

\chi
can be extended to a linear functional on
C
(since

spans

\chi(cd)=\chi(dc)

We will be mainly interested unbounded traces. We say that

\{c\in C_{+}\mid\chi(c)<\infty\}

C_{+}
.
The

.

Upon restriction, one obtains a linear map

\tau_{H}

Given a densely defined trace

\widehat{\chi}(\prescript{}{G}{\langle x,x\rangle})=\chi(\langle x,x\rangle\hbox{\hbox{}\hskip 0.0pt\raisebox{-5.0pt}{\hbox{$\!\scriptstyle H$}}\hskip-5.4181pt\hskip 5.4181pt})

(see
[

[

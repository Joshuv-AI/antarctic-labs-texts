Problems involving the fractional g g -Laplacian with Lack of Compactness
Sabri Bahrouni, Hichem Ounaies, Olfa Elfalah

Abstract
. In this paper we prove compact embedding of a subspace of the fractional Orlicz-Sobolev space W s , G ​ ( ℝ N ) W^{s,G}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right) consisting of radial functions, our target embedding spaces are of Orlicz type. 2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we will define the fractional order Orlicz-Sobolev spaces and we introduce some technical results that will be used throughout the paper.

2.1.
Young functions

An application
G\colon\mathbb{R}_{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{+}
is said to be a
Young function
if it admits the integral formulation
G(t)=\int_{0}^{t}g(\tau)\,d\tau
, where the right continuous function
g
defined on
[0,\infty)
has the following properties:

(
g_{1}
)

\displaystyle g(0)=0,\quad g(t)>0\text{ for }t>0,

(
g_{2}
)

\displaystyle g\text{ is non-decreasing on }(0,\infty),

(
g_{3}
)

\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}g(t)=\infty.

From these properties, it is easy to see that a Young function
G
is continuous, nonnegative, strictly increasing and convex on
[0,\infty)
.

The following properties on Young functions are well-known. See for instance
[
10
]
for the proofs.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
G
be a Young function satisfying (

G
1

) and
a,b\geq 0
. Then

(
L_{1}
)

\displaystyle\min\{a^{p^{-}},a^{p^{+}}\}G(b)\leq G(ab)\leq\max\{a^{p^{-}},a^{p^{+}}\}G(b),

(
L_{2}
)

\displaystyle G(a+b)\leq 2^{p^{+}}(G(a)+G(b)),

(
L_{3}
)

\displaystyle G\text{ is Lipschitz continuous}.

The
complementary Young function

\tilde{G}
of a Young function
G
is defined as

\tilde{G}(t):=\sup\{tw-G(w):w>0\}.

The functions
G
and
\tilde{G}

are complementary each other and satisfy the inequality below

(9)

\tilde{G}\left(g(t)\right)\leq G(2t),\qquad\text{for all }t>0.

Moreover, we also have a Young type inequality given by

(10)

ab\leq G(a)+\tilde{G}(b)\qquad\text{for all }a,b\geq 0,

and the following Hölder’s type inequality

\int_{\Omega}|uv|\,dx\leq\|u\|_{L^{G}(\Omega)}\|v\|_{L^{\tilde{G}}(\Omega)}

for all
u\in L^{G}(\Omega)
and
v\in L^{\tilde{G}}(\Omega)
. Moreover, it is not hard to see that
\tilde{G}
can be written as

(11)

\tilde{G}(t)=\int_{0}^{t}\tilde{g}(\tau)\,d\tau,

where
\tilde{g}(t)=\sup\{s:\ g(s)\leq t\}
. If
g
is continuous then
\tilde{g}
is the inverse of
g
.

2.2.
Fractional Orlicz-Sobolev spaces

Given a Young function
G
such that
G^{\prime}=g
, a parameter
s\in(0,1)
and an open set
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N}
. We consider the spaces

\displaystyle L^{G}(\Omega):=\left\{u\colon\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\text{ measurable such that }\Phi_{G,\Omega}\left(\lambda u\right)<\infty\text{ for some }\lambda>0\right\},

\displaystyle W^{s,G}(\Omega):=\left\{u\in L^{G}(\Omega)\text{ such that }\Phi_{s,G,\Omega}\left(\lambda u\right)<\infty\text{ for some }\lambda>0\right\},

where the modulars
\Phi_{G,\Omega}
and
\Phi_{s,G,\mathbb{R}^{N}}
are defined as

\displaystyle\Phi_{G,\Omega}(u):=\int_{\Omega}G(|u(x)|)\,dx

\displaystyle\Phi_{s,G,\Omega}(u):=\iint_{\Omega\times\Omega}G(|D_{s}u(x,y)|)\,d\mu,

where the
s-
Hölder quotient
is defined as

D_{s}u(x,y):=\frac{u(x)-u(y)}{|x-y|^{s}},

being
d\mu(x,y):=\frac{dx\,dy}{|x-y|^{N}}
.
These spaces are endowed with the so-called
Luxemburg norms

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{G}(\Omega)}:=\inf\left\{\lambda>0\colon\Phi_{G,\Omega}\left(\frac{u}{\lambda}\right)\leq 1\right\},

\displaystyle\|u\|_{W^{s,G}(\Omega)}:=\|u\|_{L^{G}(\Omega)}+[u]_{W^{s,G}(\Omega)},

where the
(s,G)
-Gagliardo semi-norm
is defined as

\displaystyle[u]_{W^{s,G}(\Omega)}:=\inf\left\{\lambda>0\colon\Phi_{s,G,\Omega}\left(\frac{u}{\lambda}\right)\leq 1\right\}.

Under the assumption (

G
1

), the space
W^{s,G}(\Omega)
is a reflexive Banach space. Moreover
C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
is dense in
W^{s,G}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. Also, the spaces
L^{G}(\Omega)
and
W^{s,G}(\Omega)
coincide with the set of measurable functions
u
on
\Omega
such that
\Phi_{G,\Omega}\left(u\right)<\infty
and the set of functions in
L^{G}(\Omega)
such that
\Phi_{s,G,\Omega}\left(u\right)<\infty
respectively. See
[
10
, Proposition 2.11]
and
[
17
, Theorem 4.7.3]
for details.

The space of fractional Orlicz-Sobolev functions is the appropriated one to define the
fractional
g-
Laplacian operator

(-\Delta_{g})^{s}u:=2\,\text{p.v.}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}g(|D_{s}u|)\frac{D_{s}u}{|D_{s}u|}\frac{dy}{|x-y|^{N+s}},

where
p.v.
stands for
in principal value
. This operator is well defined between
W^{s,G}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and its dual space
W^{-s,\tilde{G}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
(see
[
10
]
for details). In fact,

\langle(-\Delta_{g})^{s}u,v\rangle=\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}}g(|D_{s}u|)\frac{D_{s}u}{|D_{s}u|}D_{s}v\,d\mu,\quad\text{for all }v\in W^{s,G}(\mathbb{R}^{N}).

In order to state some embedding results for fractional Orlicz-Sobolev spaces we recall that given two Young functions
A
and

, we say that

is essentially stronger than

or equivalently that

decreases essentially more rapidly than

, and denoted by

, if for each

there exists

such that

for

. This is the case if and only if for
every positive constant
\lambda
,

(12)

\lim\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}t\rightarrow\infty\end{subarray}}\frac{A(\lambda t)}{B(t)}=0.

Suppose that

(13)

\int_{0}^{1}\frac{G^{-1}(\tau)}{\tau^{(N+s)/N}}d\tau<\infty\quad\text{and}\quad\int_{1}^{+\infty}\frac{G^{-1}(\tau)}{\tau^{(N+s)/N}}d\tau=\infty,

we consider the function
G_{*}
, the Sobolev conjugate
function of
G
, defined by

(14)

G_{*}^{-1}(t)=\int_{0}^{t}\frac{G^{-1}(\tau)}{\tau^{(N+s)/N}}d\tau,\qquad\text{for }t>0.

Proof of the Strauss Radial embedding

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Let
(u_{n})\subset W^{s,G}_{rad}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
be a bounded sequence. Since
W^{s,G}_{rad}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
is a reflexive space, up to subsequence, still denoted by
u_{n}
,

u_{n}\rightharpoonup 0\ \text{in}\ W^{s,G}_{rad}(\mathbb{R}^{N}).

Using the continuous embedding
W^{s,G}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\hookrightarrow L^{G}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, there is
C>0
, such that

(15)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}G(u_{n})dx<C.

As
u_{n}
is radially symmetric, for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, then

(16)

\int_{B_{r}(y_{1})}G(u_{n})dx=\int_{B_{r}(y_{2})}G(u_{n})dx,\quad\text{for all }y_{1},y_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{N},\ |y_{1}|=|y_{2}|.

Let us fix
r>0
. In the sequel, for each
y\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
|y|>r
we denote by
\gamma(y)
the maximum of those integers
j\geq 1
such that there exist
y_{1},y_{2},\ldots,y_{j}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
, with
|y_{1}|=|y_{2}|=\ldots=|y_{j}|=|y|
and

B_{r}(y_{i})\cap B_{r}(y_{k})=\emptyset,

whenever
i\neq k
. It is easy to see that

(17)

\gamma(y)\rightarrow\infty,\ \text{as}\ |y|\rightarrow\infty.

Let
y\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
|y|>r
, we choose
y_{1},...,y_{\gamma(y)}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
as above,
then by (
15
), (
16
) and (
17
), we obtain

\displaystyle C

\displaystyle\geq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}G(u_{n}(x))dx\geq\sum_{i=1}^{\gamma(y)}\int_{B_{r}(y_{i})}G(u_{n}(x))dx

\displaystyle\geq\gamma(y)\int_{B_{r}(y)}G(u_{n}(x))dx.

Therefore

\int_{B_{r}(y)}G(u_{n}(x))dx\leq\frac{C}{\gamma(y)}\rightarrow 0,\ \ \text{as}\ \ |y|\rightarrow+\infty.

So that, for arbitrary
\epsilon>0
there exists

such that

(18)

\sup_{|y|\geq R_{\epsilon}}\int_{B_{r}(y)}G(u_{n})dx\leq\epsilon,\ n\in\mathbb{N}.

By Proposition
2.2
, the embedding

W^{s,G}(B_{r+R_{\epsilon}}(0))\hookrightarrow L^{G}(B_{r+R_{\epsilon}}(0))

is compact and thus

in
L^{G}(B_{r+R_{\epsilon}}(0))
, which implies

\int_{B_{r+R_{\epsilon}}(0)}G(u_{n})dx\rightarrow 0,\ \text{as}\ n\rightarrow\infty,

and so,

(19)

\sup_{|y|<R_{\epsilon}}\int_{B_{r}(y)}G(u_{n})dx\rightarrow 0,\ \text{as}\ n\rightarrow\infty.

Combining (

) and (

in

. The proof is completed.

.

If

Proof.

Since

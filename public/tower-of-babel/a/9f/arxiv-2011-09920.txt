rf measurements and tuning of the 1-meter-long 750 MHz radio-frequency quadrupole for artwork analysis
Hermann W. Pommerenke, Ursula van Rienen, Alexej Grudiev

Abstract
The 750 MHz PIXE-RFQ (radio-frequency quadrupole), developed and built at CERN, provides 2 MeV protons over a length of one meter for proton-induced X-ray emission analysis (PIXE) of cultural heritage artwork. In this paper, we report low-power rf measurements and tuning of the PIXE-RFQ, which have been completed mid-2020. Using a novel algorithm based on direct measurements of the response matrix, field and frequency could tuned at the same time in only two steps to satisfying accuracy. Additionally, we report measurements of single modules, quality factors, and coupling strength. In all cases, very good agreement between rf measurement and design values was observed.

II
Preliminary considerations

This section briefly describes the bead-pull measurement, lists the tuning goals, and discusses corrections of frequency measurements with respect to temperature and medium.

II.1
Bead-pull field measurement

In bead-pull measurement, a small object (the bead) is introduced into the rf cavity, effectively removing a small volume from the resonator. Following Slater’s perturbation theorem
Slater 1946
;
Maier and Slater 1952
this can be observed as a change in resonance frequency of the cavity, proportional to the squared field amplitudes at the location of the bead. In RFQs the bead is typically introduced into the four quadrants, i.e. the regions occupied by the magnetic field.

In the present case, the bead-pull measurements were performed using the phase
\Delta\phi=\arg S_{11}
of the reflection
S_{11}=\Gamma
measured through the single input power coupler (upper part of Fig.
1
). The same bead-pull bench and pulley system as for the HF-RFQ was used
Koubek et al. 2017b
;
Koubek et al. 2017a
. The bead was threaded on a
0.3\text{\,}\mathrm{mm}
fishing wire. The size of the perturbing bead must be small enough such that the frequency shift stays within the linear regime of the
\Delta\phi(\Delta f)
curve, but large enough such that an acceptable signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) is achieved. Following the measurements of the HF-RFQ, a
$7\text{\,}\mathrm{mm}$\times$4\text{\,}\mathrm{mm}$
aluminum bead was used. While the PIXE-RFQ features the same frequency and a comparable quality factor, its length and thus volume are approximately halved. Therefore, the same bead introduces roughly double the frequency shift when inserted into the PIXE-RFQ. With
\Delta f\approxeq$5\text{\,}\mathrm{kHz}$
, or
\Delta\phi\approxeq$17\text{\,}\mathrm{\SIUnitSymbolDegree}$
, the shift induced by the bead still remained well within the linear regime.

From the raw phase measurements of the RFQ quadrants the field components were extracted and aligned by means of a few data processing steps detailed in Ref.
Koubek et al. 2017b
and a smoothing Savitzky-Golay filter
Savitzky and Golay 1964
;
Press et al. 2007
. With
\Delta\phi
proportional to the squared magnetic field, the relative quadrant amplitudes
a_{1},\dots,a_{4}
arise by taking the square root and assigning the proper sign to account for the the alternating field orientation of the TE
210
mode. The field flatness is then quantified by one quadrupole two dipole components of orthogonal polarizations
Wangler 2008
:

\begin{split}Q\phantom{{}^{\text{S}}}&=\frac{1}{4}\left(a_{1}-a_{2}+a_{3}-a_{4}\right),\\
D^{\text{S}}&=\frac{1}{2}\left(a_{1}-a_{3}\right),\\
D^{\text{T}}&=\frac{1}{2}\left(a_{2}-a_{4}\right).\end{split}

II.2
Tuning goals

By design, the inter-vane voltage of the PIXE-RFQ is constant at
V_{0}=$35\text{\,}\mathrm{kV}$
, corresponding to a magnetic field that is constant along the RFQ and equal in all four quadrants. The goal of the tuning process can thus be defined as
Q=$100\text{\,}\mathrm{\%}$=\text{const.}
and
D^{\text{S}}=D^{\text{T}}=0
at all sampling points. Errors of
\pm$2\text{\,}\mathrm{\%}$
with respect to the average quadrupole component are acceptable in each of the three field components from a beam dynamics point of view. These tolerances have been established with respect to the
2\text{\,}\mathrm{m}
long
750\text{\,}\mathrm{MHz}
HF-RFQ
Koubek et al. 2017a
.

The PIXE-RFQ represents a stand-alone machine; no rf structures requiring frequency and phase stability are installed downstream of the RFQ. Therefore, frequency accuracy does not represent a critical tuning goal. A frequency shift is in fact foreseen by design during nominal operation in order to maintain a constant cooling water temperature
Pommerenke et al. 2019
. Deviations from the design resonant frequency as much as few
\mathrm{MHz}
are acceptable for the beam dynamics
Pommerenke et al. 2019
. Nevertheless, the frequency was tuned to match the design value:
749.480\text{\,}\mathrm{MHz}
under vacuum at
22\text{\,}\mathrm{\SIUnitSymbolCelsius}
with an error smaller than
\pm$60\text{\,}\mathrm{kHz}$
. This tolerance emerges from the water temperature range of a typical cavity cooling system:
\pm$5\text{\,}\mathrm{K}$
around the nominal value. The corresponding sensitivity of the PIXE-RFQ resonant frequency amounts to
-$13.3\text{\,}\mathrm{kHz}\text{/}\mathrm{K}$
, as determined in Ref.
Pommerenke et al. 2019
.

II.3
Frequency correction

Most of the measurements were conducted under air since the bead-pull setup denies evacuating the RFQ cavity. Furthermore, the ambient temperature was not controlled and deviated significantly from the RFQ design reference temperature of
22\text{\,}\mathrm{\SIUnitSymbolCelsius}
. The measured frequency was therefore affected by two main aspects: (i) thermal expansion of the RFQ cavity, and (ii) the relative permittivity
\varepsilon_{\text{r}}
of air.

The thermal behavior of the cavity during low-level rf measurements was dominated by the ambient temperature that changed during the day. Because of thermal expansion, the resonant frequency is anti-proportional to the bulk copper temperature:
\Delta f/f=-\alpha\Delta T
, where
\alpha=$1.66\text{\times}{10}^{-5}\text{\,}\text{/}\mathrm{K}$
is the secant thermal expansion coefficient of the copper cavity and
\Delta T
the difference between measured and reference temperature. The
\pm$0.2\text{\,}\mathrm{K}$
precision of the used thermometers translates to a frequency uncertainty of
\pm$2.6\text{\,}\mathrm{kHz}$
. This error is by an order of magnitude smaller than the error introduced by humidity uncertainty (see the following).

The speed of light and thus the resonant frequency in a medium are by a factor of
\sqrt{\varepsilon_{\text{r}}\mu_{\text{r}}}
lower than the vacuum values
Feynman et al. 2010
. For air we assume
\mu_{\text{r}}=1
and
\varepsilon_{\text{r}}=1.00058986

Hector and Schultz 1936
at standard temperature and pressure (STP,
0\text{\,}\mathrm{\SIUnitSymbolCelsius}
,
1\text{\,}\mathrm{atm}
,
900\text{\,}\mathrm{kHz}
). The measured frequency
f_{\text{meas}}
is thus corrected as follows:

f=f_{\text{meas}}\cdot\frac{\sqrt{\varepsilon_{\text{r}}}}{1-\alpha\Delta T}.

(2)

A major source of uncertainty is the dependence of
\varepsilon_{\text{r}}
on the ambient humidity. Analytical expressions for this relationship have been formulated based on experimental studies and can be found in Refs.
Santo Zarnik and Belavic 2012
;
Buck 1981
;
Buck Research Instruments
LLC 2012
. The influence can be mitigated by measuring the humidity and calculating the corresponding
\varepsilon_{\text{r}}
. Alternatively, the cavity can be flooded with dry nitrogen (N
2
). The latter represents a standard procedure for rf cavity measurements and has been done during the final tuning steps of the medical HF-RFQ
Koubek et al. 2017a
.

However, there are no strict accuracy requirements for the PIXE-RFQ frequency. Hence, we accepted the humidity uncertainty and worked with the constant STP value of
\varepsilon_{\text{r}}
. A humidity uncertainty of
\pm$30\text{\,}\mathrm{\%}$
translates to an error in frequency of approximately
\pm$30\text{\,}\mathrm{kHz}$
. Additional, but much smaller errors originate in pressure and temperature dependence of
\varepsilon_{\text{r}}

Heidary 2010
. Thus, we expected that the PIXE-RFQ could be tuned under air to an accuracy of approximately
\pm$30\text{\,}\mathrm{kHz}$
when measuring only the cavity temperature. If frequency stability was required, this error would still lie within the
\pm$5\text{\,}\mathrm{K}$
tuning range of a typical water cooling system.

In the following, the frequency is exclusively reported in terms of the corrected value (the value under vacuum at
22\text{\,}\mathrm{\SIUnitSymbolCelsius}
).

IV.2
Reliability measurements

Before the tuning process, the measurement error was estimated by means of reliability measurements. The authors differentiate between two distinct concepts: (i) repeatability, the error observed between multiple bead-pull measurements taken without any changes to the RFQ itself, in particular no tuner movements, and (ii) reproducibility, the error introduced by tuner movements and mechanical hysteresis effects. The errors arising from these two aspects pose the ultimate limit for the tuning accuracy.

IV.2.1
Repeatability of field measurement

To assess repeatability, thirteen bead-pull measurements were carried out over several days without moving any tuners. The deviations between the repeated measurements were larger than
0.5\text{\,}\mathrm{\%}
for one quadrant. Such an error would limit the dipole-component tuning accuracy to more than
1\text{\,}\mathrm{\%}
(Fig.
7
). The deviations comprise systematic errors caused by change of ambient parameters such as temperature and humidity. However, they were expected to be negligible in this case, being several orders of magnitude smaller than what could be resolved by bead-pull measurement. A significantly larger error source is random noise introduced by vibration and slippage of the wire and by the vector network analyzer (VNA) itself.

Therefore, we studied to which extent the error could be reduced by averaging over several repetitions. Based on the data from the thirteen runs, all possible combinations of three, six, or ten measurements where formed. The average of each combination was calculated at each sampling point, generating new artificial sets of measurements. The resulting error was calculated as
\delta X=\max\left|X-\left\langle X\right\rangle\right|
, where
X\in\{Q,D^{\text{S}},D^{\text{T}}\}
and
\langle\cdot\rangle
indicates averaging over all thirteen available measurements. The results are summarized in Fig.
7
, given as a percentage of the quadrupole component (
\left\langle Q\right\rangle=$100\text{\,}\mathrm{\%}$
). Averaging over three measurements guarantees a measurement error
<$0.5\text{\,}\mathrm{\%}$
for
D^{\text{S}},D^{\text{T}}
and
<$0.2\text{\,}\mathrm{\%}$
for
Q
, which lies well within the tuning requirements. Especially for sampling points with large spread, the
Q
error could be reduced by nearly a factor of two. With respect to the considerable effort for carrying out these measurements, it was decided to repeat all tuning-related bead-pull measurements three times.

Augmented tuning algorithm

The PIXE-RFQ was tuned with an improved version of the algorithm developed for the HF-RFQ by Koubek

IV.3.1
Previous tuning algorithm used for HF-RFQ

Koubek

defined (in a slightly different notation)

\boldsymbol{\mathrm{x}}_{\text{cur}}=\begin{bmatrix}Q_{1}&\cdots&Q_{n}&D^{\text{S}}_{1}&\cdots&D^{\text{S}}_{n}&D^{\text{T}}_{1}&\cdots&D^{\text{T}}_{n}\end{bmatrix}^{\mathrm{T}}\in\mathbb{R}^{3n}

(3)

as the vector of quadrupole and dipole field amplitudes
Q_{i}
,
D^{\text{S}}_{i}
,
D^{\text{T}}_{i}
currently measured in the RFQ at the discrete field sampling points
i=1,\ldots,n
. The corresponding target values are summarized in the vector

\boldsymbol{\mathrm{x}}_{\text{trg}}=\begin{bmatrix}Q_{\text{trg},1}&\cdots&D^{\text{T}}_{\text{trg},n}\end{bmatrix}^{\mathrm{T}}\in\mathbb{R}^{3n}.

(4)

Similarly, the current tuner positions
t_{j}
and target tuner positions
t_{\text{trg},j}
,
j=1,\ldots,m
are collected in

\boldsymbol{\mathrm{t}}_{\text{cur}}=\begin{bmatrix}t_{1}\\
\vdots\\
t_{m}\end{bmatrix}\in\mathbb{R}^{m},\quad\boldsymbol{\mathrm{t}}_{\text{trg}}=\begin{bmatrix}t_{\text{trg},1}\\
\vdots\\
t_{\text{trg},m}\end{bmatrix}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}.

(5)

By moving each tuner individually by some distance (all other tuners remain in nominal position), the response matrix
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{R}}=\partial\boldsymbol{\mathrm{x}}_{\text{cur}}/\partial\boldsymbol{\mathrm{t}}_{\text{cur}}\in\mathbb{R}^{3n\times m}
is obtained. Each matrix entry quantifies the effect of one tuner on

,
D^{\text{S}}
,
D^{\text{T}}
at one sampling point in first-order approximation. In a perfectly tuned constant-voltage RFQ the quadrupole component equals unity (

\,\begin{array}[t]{@{}c@{}}\underbrace{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{x}}_{\text{trg}}-\boldsymbol{\mathrm{x}}_{\text{cur}}}\\
\Delta\boldsymbol{\mathrm{x}}\end{array}\,=\,\begin{array}[t]{@{}c@{}}\underbrace{\left(\partial\boldsymbol{\mathrm{x}}_{\text{cur}}/\partial\boldsymbol{\mathrm{t}}_{\text{cur}}\right)}\\
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{R}}\end{array}\,\cdot\,\begin{array}[t]{@{}c@{}}\underbrace{\left(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{t}}_{\text{trg}}-\boldsymbol{\mathrm{t}}_{\text{cur}}\right)}\\
\Delta\boldsymbol{\mathrm{t}}\end{array}\,.

(6)

The new tuner positions required to correct the field distortion are obtained as

\boldsymbol{\mathrm{t}}_{\text{trg}}=\boldsymbol{\mathrm{t}}_{\text{cur}}+\Delta\boldsymbol{\mathrm{t}}=\boldsymbol{\mathrm{t}}_{\text{cur}}+\boldsymbol{\mathrm{R}}^{\dagger}\Delta\boldsymbol{\mathrm{x}},

(7)

where
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{R}}^{\dagger}
identifies the pseudo-inverse of
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{R}}
.

IV.3.2
Including the frequency

In this work, we augmented the presented system of equations to include also the frequency. The measured frequency is introduced as a dimensionless quantity
\bar{f}=w_{f}f
such that it can be combined with the likewise dimensionless measured field amplitudes
Q_{i},D^{\text{S}}_{i},D^{\text{T}}_{i}
by appending it to
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{x}}_{\text{cur}}
. Analogously, the target frequency
\bar{f}_{\text{trg}}=w_{f}f_{\text{trg}}
is appended to
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{x}}_{\text{trg}}
. The normalizing weight
w_{f}
(in units of
\text{/}\mathrm{Hz}
) can be used to control the influence of the frequency compared to the field amplitudes. More precisely,
w_{f}
determines the contribution of the frequency deviation
f_{\text{trg}}-f
to the residual
||\Delta\boldsymbol{\mathrm{x}}-\boldsymbol{\mathrm{R}}\Delta\boldsymbol{\mathrm{t}}||
, which is minimized when solving the over-determined system by means of the least-squares method. Larger
w_{f}
translate to higher importance. The frequency is incorporated by appending a new row to
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{R}}
:

\left[\boldsymbol{\mathrm{R}}\right]_{3n+1}=\frac{\partial\bar{f}}{\partial\boldsymbol{\mathrm{t}}_{\text{cur}}}=\begin{bmatrix}\dfrac{\partial\bar{f}}{\partial t_{1}}&\cdots&\dfrac{\partial\bar{f}}{\partial t_{m}}\end{bmatrix}.

(8)

The algorithm presented in Ref.
Koubek et al. 2017a
forces the normalized quadrupole component to equal unity,
Q_{\text{trg},i}=1\forall i
, which might not lead to an optimum solution when including the frequency. Instead, we use the relaxed condition
Q_{\text{trg},i}=\hat{Q}_{\text{trg}}\forall i
, which only requires all quadrupole component samples to be equal to some value
\hat{Q}_{\text{trg}}
, not necessarily unity, but close. As an unknown quantity,
\hat{Q}_{\text{trg}}
must be brought to the right-hand side. This is accomplished by normalizing the tuner positions by a weight
w_{t}
, i.e. replacing the
t_{i}
by

IV.3.3
Matrix inversion by SVD

Eq. (
9
) must be solved to obtain the tuner corrections. One possible solution is given by

\Delta\boldsymbol{\mathrm{t}}=\boldsymbol{\mathrm{R}}^{\dagger}\Delta\boldsymbol{\mathrm{x}},

(10)

where
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{R}}^{\dagger}=(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{R}}^{\mathrm{T}}\boldsymbol{\mathrm{R}})^{-1}\boldsymbol{\mathrm{R}}
denotes the Moore-Penrose inverse
Bronshtein et al. 2015
(pseudo-inverse) of the generally non-square
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{R}}
. Koubek
Koubek et al. 2017a
;
Koubek et al. 2017b
pointed out that
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{R}}
is potentially ill-conditioned and proposed to use a special method based on singular value decomposition (SVD)
Bronshtein et al. 2015
to compute the inverse.

To simplify the notation, we identify
N=3n+1
and
M=m+1
corresponding to a setup with
n
longitudinal field sampling points and
m
tuners. The SVD of
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{R}}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times M}
is given as

\boldsymbol{\mathrm{R}}=\boldsymbol{\mathrm{U}}\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\Sigma}}\boldsymbol{\mathrm{V}}^{\mathrm{T}},

(11)

where
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{U}}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times N}
,
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{V}}\in\mathbb{R}^{M\times M}
are orthonormal matrices, and
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\Sigma}}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times M}
is a rectangular diagonal matrix
whose diagonal entries
\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{M}
are the singular values of
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{R}}
in descending order. Note that
M\leq N
is an essential requirement of the algorithm, which can always be achieved by increasing the number of sampling points. The pseudo-inverse can then be constructed as
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{R}}^{\dagger}=\boldsymbol{\mathrm{V}}\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\Sigma}}^{\dagger}\boldsymbol{\mathrm{U}}^{\mathrm{T}}
,
where
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\Sigma}}^{\dagger}
is obtained by inverting each diagonal entry
\sigma_{i}
of
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\Sigma}}
and transposing the result.

For ill-conditioned, almost singular
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{R}}
, the reciprocals
1/\sigma_{i}
of the smaller singular values (larger
i
) approach infinity. This can lead to invalid solutions, i.e. tuner adjustments that lie outside of the physical tuner movement range. Koubek
Koubek et al. 2017a
;
Koubek et al. 2017b
proposed to circumvent this problem by consecutively setting the largest
1/\sigma_{i}
of
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\Sigma}}^{\dagger}
to zero. We define

\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\Sigma}}^{\dagger}_{k}=\diag\begin{bmatrix}\frac{1}{\sigma_{1}}&\cdots&\frac{1}{\sigma_{M-k}}&0&\cdots&0\end{bmatrix}\in\mathbb{R}^{M\times N}

(12)

as the matrix
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\Sigma}}^{\dagger}
where the
k
largest entries have been set to zero, with
k=0,\ldots,(M-1)
. Note that
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\Sigma}}^{\dagger}_{0}=\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\Sigma}}^{\dagger}
is the initial Moore-Penrose inverse, whereas
k=M
leads to
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{R}}^{\dagger}=\boldsymbol{\mathrm{0}}
and no tuner movements at all. The matrices give
M
possibly useful solutions

\Delta\boldsymbol{\mathrm{t}}_{k}=\boldsymbol{\mathrm{V}}\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\Sigma}}^{\dagger}_{k}\boldsymbol{\mathrm{U}}^{\mathrm{T}}\Delta\boldsymbol{\mathrm{x}}.

(13)

Naturally, solutions with tuner adjustments outside the physical movement range must be discarded. The remaining solutions are checked by computing the prediction
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\tilde{x}}}_{\text{trg},k}
for corrected field and frequency using the original response matrix:

\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\tilde{x}}}_{\text{trg},k}=\boldsymbol{\mathrm{x}}_{\text{cur}}+\boldsymbol{\mathrm{R}}\Delta\boldsymbol{\mathrm{t}}_{k}.

(14)

The tuner movement for the current tuning step is chosen as that
\Delta\boldsymbol{\mathrm{t}}_{k}
whose corresponding
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\tilde{x}}}_{\text{trg},k}
best fulfills the requirements: a quadrupole component as flat as possible, dipole components and frequency deviation as close to zero as possible.

After applying one tuner adjustment, field and frequency are measured again and a new vector
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{x}}_{\text{cur}}
emerges. The process is repeated until the measured field components match the requirements to a desired accuracy
Wangler 2008
;
Koubek et al. 2017a
;
Koubek et al. 2017b
. Koubek showed that it is sufficient to use only the initial
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{R}}
and choose a different solution [Eq. (
13
)], i.e. a different value for
k
, for each tuning iteration
Koubek et al. 2017a
;
Koubek et al. 2017b
. This way, time-consuming re-measurement of
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{R}}
for each tuning iteration is avoided.

IV.4
Tuning steps

The tuning procedure can be structured into two phases: at first, the response matrix
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{R}}
was measured by means of individual tuner movements. Then, two corrective tuner movements were carried out.

Measurement of response matrix

The entries of
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{R}}
from Eq. (
9

should resemble the anticipated corrective movement as close as possible as
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{R}}
contains only first-order approximations of the de facto nonlinear responses:

V
Quality factor measurement

After tuning, the quality factors of the PIXE-RFQ were measured under vacuum to validate the design and provide information on the input power reflection originating in potential over- or under-coupling.

The raw measurement data were the complex reflection coefficient
S_{11}=\Gamma
measured in a bandwidth of
1\text{\,}\mathrm{MHz}
symmetrically around the TE
210
eigenfrequency. The reflection coefficient describes a circle in the complex plane in very good approximation, from which unloaded (
Q_{0}
), external (
Q_{\text{ex}}
), and loaded quality factor (
Q_{\ell}
) can be obtained, where

\frac{1}{Q_{\ell}}=\frac{1}{Q_{0}}+\frac{1}{Q_{\text{ex}}}.

(15)

Additionally, the coupling coefficient

\beta_{\text{c}}=Q_{0}/Q_{\text{ex}}

(16)

emerges. The parameters were extracted by fitting an ideal circle to
\Gamma
following the method described in Refs.
Altar 1947
;
Kajfez 1994
;
Kajfez 2003
;
Goryashko et al. 2015
. Contrarily to e.g. the three-point method
Kummer 1986
;
Caspers 2010
, the circle-fitting method performs implicit averaging by solving a heavily over-determined system of equations, and is thus significantly less susceptible to measurement noise.

The technique is based on the Möbius transformation

\Gamma=\frac{b_{1}\bar{\omega}+b_{2}}{b_{3}\bar{\omega}+1},

(17)

with
\bar{\omega}=2(\omega/\omega_{0}-1)
, which describes a circle in the complex plane characterized by the parameters
b_{1}
,
b_{2}
,
b_{3}
. For
\bar{\omega}\to\pm\infty
,
\Gamma\to b_{1}/b_{3}
approaches the detuned reflection, whereas
\bar{\omega}=0
delivers
\Gamma(\Delta\omega=0)=b_{2}
as the reflection coefficient of the loaded resonator. Their distance equals the circle diameter
D=|b_{1}/b_{3}-b_{2}|
, and the coupling coefficient can be determined as
\beta_{\text{c}}=(2/D-1)^{-1}
.
Furthermore,
Q_{\ell}=\Im\left\{b_{3}\right\}
, from which the two remaining quality factors can be calculated using Eqs. (
15
) and (
16
)
Kajfez 1994
. The VNA measures
\Gamma(\omega_{i})
at
N
discrete sampling points
\omega_{i}
. Thus, Eq. (
17
) can be written in matrix form:

\,\begin{array}[t]{@{}c@{}}\underbrace{\begin{bmatrix}\Gamma(\omega_{1})\\
\vdots\\
\Gamma(\omega_{N})\end{bmatrix}}\\
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\Gamma}}\in\mathbb{C}^{N}\end{array}\,=\,\begin{array}[t]{@{}c@{}}\underbrace{\begin{bmatrix}\bar{\omega}_{1}&1&-\bar{\omega}_{1}\Gamma(\omega_{1})\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
\bar{\omega}_{N}&1&-\bar{\omega}_{N}\Gamma(\omega_{N})\end{bmatrix}}\\
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{K}}\in\mathbb{C}^{N\times 3}\end{array}\,\,\begin{array}[t]{@{}c@{}}\underbrace{\begin{bmatrix}b_{1}\\
b_{2}\\
b_{3}\end{bmatrix}}\\
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{b}}\in\mathbb{C}^{3}\end{array}\,,

(18)

a heavily overdetermined system of equations with each row representing one measurement point. The curve described by
\Gamma
is not a perfect circle—deviations become stronger with increasing distance from
\omega_{0}
(or
|\bar{\omega}|\gg 0
). Furthermore, equidistant frequency sampling implies lower density of measurement points around the critical
\omega_{0}
. Therefore, a weighting matrix
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{W}}
is introduced, which reduces the significance of measurement points further away from
\omega_{0}

w_{i}=1-\left|\Gamma(\omega_{i})\right|^{2},\qquad\boldsymbol{\mathrm{W}}=\diag\begin{bmatrix}w_{1}&\cdots&w_{N}\end{bmatrix}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times N}.

(19)

Then, the three fitting parameters are obtained as

\boldsymbol{\mathrm{b}}=\left(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{W}}\boldsymbol{\mathrm{K}}\right)^{\dagger}\boldsymbol{\mathrm{W}}\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\Gamma}}.

(20)

Pommerenke et al. 2019
. Subjected to the pressure difference between vacuum and exterior atmosphere, the PEEK window could deform and push inwards the inner conductor. This would slightly increase the effective area of the coupling loop, resulting in a larger coupling coefficient.

The discrepancy highlights that it is important to design the coupler with an over-coupling margin of few ten percent. Although the PIXE-RFQ coupler features a rotatable flange that allows for fine-tuning the coupling coefficient by means of measurements, we decided not to take advantage and rather accepted an over-coupling of
18\text{\,}\mathrm{\%}
(
\beta_{\text{c}}=1.18
). This translates to an input reflection of
-22\text{\,}\mathrm{dB}
. If the RFQ cavity consumes
65.0\text{\,}\mathrm{kW}
of peak power in terms of surface losses
Pommerenke et al. 2019
, an input power of
65.5\text{\,}\mathrm{kW}
is required, and
0.5\text{\,}\mathrm{kW}
, i.e. roughly
0.7\text{\,}\mathrm{\%}
of the input power, are reflected back towards the generator. These values are well acceptable for operation.

VI
Summary

In this paper, the low-power rf measurements and tuning procedure carried out at CERN on the one-meter-long
750\text{\,}\mathrm{MHz}
PIXE-RFQ are reported.

Before full assembly, bead-pull measurements were performed on mechanical modules to confirm that no severe errors occured during machining and brazing. The field showed near-perfect agreement with the simulation, whereas the frequency error was equal or smaller than
600\text{\,}\mathrm{kHz}
in both cases. The small observed deviations are well within the capabilities of the tuning system.

The main part of this paper is dedicated to the RFQ tuning procedure. Longitudinal and transverse field tilts were compensated by means of sixteen movable piston tuners. The aim of the tuning was to achieve the most flat possible quadrupole component and vanishing dipole components. Furthermore, the operating mode frequency was tuned to the design frequency of
749.480\text{\,}\mathrm{MHz}
under vacuum at
22\text{\,}\mathrm{\SIUnitSymbolCelsius}
.

Reliability measurements were carried out before the actual tuning procedure in order to assess the accuracy limits given by bead-pull measurement and tuner tooling. The bead-pull measurement error could be improved to
0.5\text{\,}\mathrm{\%}
by performing each measurement thrice. No significant mechanical hysteresis effects were observed.

The tuning algorithm used for HF-RFQ
Koubek et al. 2017a
was augmented for the PIXE-RFQ such that frequency and field could be tuned at the same time. The results suggest that including frequency and corresponding weights also leads to a more stable convergence of the tuning iterations. After measuring the response matrix, the PIXE-RFQ was tuned in only two steps.
Subsequently, the tuners were recut to their final lengths. The measurement under vacuum revealed a remaining frequency error of
23.5\text{\,}\mathrm{kHz}
, which can be explained with the measurement uncertainty with respect to air humidity. The error lies well within the
\pm$60\text{\,}\mathrm{kHz}$
tuning range of a typical water cooling system (
\pm$5\text{\,}\mathrm{K}$
) and is in any case acceptable since no rf accelerating structures are present downstream of the RFQ.

The three quality factors and the coupling coefficient were measured using a circle-fitting method. Excellent agreement with an error smaller than
0.5\text{\,}\mathrm{\%}
was observed in
Q_{0}
.
Q_{\text{ex}}
was measured to be
6\text{\,}\mathrm{\%}
higher than the design value. The discrepancy could be attributed to mechanical manufacturing error in the coupler of roughly
300\text{\,}\mathrm{\SIUnitSymbolMicro m}
. The larger
Q_{\text{ex}}
corresponds to a measured coupling coefficient of 1.18 compared to a design value of 1.25. The measured over-coupling of
18\text{\,}\mathrm{\%}
requires an extra
0.5\text{\,}\mathrm{kW}
(
0.7\text{\,}\mathrm{\%}
) of rf peak input power. It was decided to not make use of the possibility of rotating the coupler to closer approach critical coupling.

With the low-power rf measurements completed in mid-2020, the PIXE-RFQ has been transported to INFN, Florence, Italy for high-power rf conditioning and beam measurements. The world’s smallest RFQ is expected to commence operation towards the end of 2020.

Two Particles with Zero-Range Interaction in a Magnetic Field
Johannes Kirscher, Brian C. Tiburzi

Abstract
Energy levels are investigated for two charged particles possessing an attractive, momentum-independent, zero-range interaction in a uniform magnetic field. A transcendental equation governs the spectrum, which is characterized by a collective Landau-level quantum number incorporating both center-of-mass and relative degrees of freedom. Results are obtained for a system of one charged and one neutral particle, with the interaction chosen to produce a bound state in vanishing magnetic field. Beyond deriving the weak-field expansion of the energy levels, we focus on non-perturbative aspects. In the strong-field limit, or equivalently for a system in the unitary limit, a single bound level with universal binding energy exists. By contrast, excited states are resonances that disappear into the continuum as the magnetic field is raised beyond critical values. A hyperbola is derived that approximates the number of bound levels as a function of the field strength remarkably well.



I
Introductory Remarks

The study of quantum systems in external fields is central to many disciplines,
with applications ranging from the study of ultracold gases of trapped atoms
Bloch et al. 2008

to quantum field theories in condensed matter, nuclear, and particle physics
Miransky and Shovkovy 2015
.
In numerical gauge-theory calculations,
external fields present a practical
computational
method to study properties
of light nuclei using lattice quantum chromodynamics (LQCD).
For an overview,
see
Detmold and Savage 2004
;
Beane et al. 2011
;
Davoudi et al. 2021

and references therein.
The first LQCD results for light nuclei in external magnetic fields
Detmold et al. 2016
;
Beane et al. 2015
;
Chang et al. 2015

point to an intriguing possibility.
The energies of a subset of two-nucleon systems in large magnetic fields appear to approach binding thresholds,
which suggests an analogue of a Feshbach resonance
Feshbach 1958
;
Fano 1961
;
Feshbach 1962
.
While such resonances are extensively studied in atomic systems
Chin et al. 2010
,
the LQCD results hint at a similar effect in two-nucleon systems.
The magnetic field,
moreover,
could provide a lever with which to tune two-nucleon systems simultaneously to threshold,
arriving at a point of conformal symmetry
Braaten and Hammer 2003
.

Unlike atomic systems,
the magnetic fields required to alter strongly interacting systems are exceptionally large,

{\mathcal{O}}(10^{19})

Gauss.
Fields of this size
are routine in LQCD computations,
due to quantization conditions required of uniform fields on a torus
Al-Hashimi and Wiese 2009
.
In astrophysics,
strong magnetic fields have been conjectured to occur in the interiors of magnetars
Duncan and Thompson 1992
;
Broderick et al. 2000
;
Harding and Lai 2006
.
Such extreme fields,
have been estimated to exist in non-central heavy-ion collisions
Skokov et al. 2009
;
McLerran and Skokov 2014
,
moreover,
for which considerable work has explored the implications for QCD matter
Kharzeev et al. 2013
.

In the present work,
we investigate a two-body problem in uniform magnetic fields.
For atomic systems,
there is substantial work on Coulomb interactions in an external magnetic field,

e.g.

Ruder et al. 1994
.
The analogous study of QCD bound states has begun recently;
examples include
quarkonium in external magnetic fields
Alford and Strickland 2013
;
Bonati et al. 2015
,
as well as modification of the quark-antiquark potential computed from LQCD
Bonati et al. 2014
.
For two protons in a magnetic field,
the center-of-mass and relative motion can be separated.
In this system,
the effect of a sufficiently large magnetic field has been proven to result in a bound di-proton state
Allor et al. 2007
.
The argument hinges on magnetic confinement of the protons into narrow cyclotron orbits.
In such a scenario,
the short-range nuclear interaction results in an attractive one-dimensional potential well,
which always supports a bound state.

1

1

In atomic systems,
a related argument was made to prove the existence of bound negative ions in large magnetic fields
Avron et al. 1977
.
For this case,
the atomic polarizability leads to the attractive interaction with the electron.

To address the neutron-proton system in magnetic fields,
however,
one needs to confront the non-separability of the center-of-mass and relative degrees of freedom,
and we take a first step in this work.

General results for charged particles with translationally invariant interactions in a magnetic field were established in
Avron et al. 1978

In particular,
a quantum number exists that collectively incorporates the center-of-mass and relative degrees of freedom,
and each level is infinitely degenerate.
This provides a striking generalization of the Landau problem to all values of the magnetic field.
We exhibit these results by solving for the energy levels of two charged particles interacting with a contact potential.
The choice of an attractive,
momentum-independent,
zero-range interaction is motivated by the pion-less effective
theory of few-nucleon systems
Beane et al. 2000
;
Bedaque and van
Kolck 2002
;
Epelbaum et al. 2009
;
Hammer et al. 2020
.

2

2

Throughout this initial study,

Treatment of the two-nucleon problem requires

The zero-range interaction between particles produces valuable simplifications that facilitate determining the spectrum.

A bound state with energy

By virtue of the contact potential, the integration over the relative separation

While center-of-mass and relative coordinates are not completely decoupled in

partial factorization of the wave-function is possible.
Assuming a non-vanishing charge for the system

Q\equiv q_{1}+q_{2}
,
the center-of-mass wave-function is specified by the momentum parallel to the magnetic field, along with one other quantum number.
5
5

5

More formally,

so-called

Parallel to the magnetic field,

.
The

where

11

This quantum number can be chosen as an additional component of the total momentum,
for which the asymmetric gauge is natural.
Therefore,
we adopt

{\bm{A}}({\bm{r}})=\left(-By,0,0\right),

(5)

leading to the magnetic field

Accordingly,

- and

For any vector

The two-particle wave-function can be written in the partially factorized form

in which

The

\bm{\widetilde{P}}
-projected
non-interacting Green’s function is obtained through the Fourier decomposition

\displaystyle G^{(0)}({\bm{r}}^{\prime}_{1},{\bm{r}}^{\prime}_{2};{\bm{r}}_{1},{\bm{r}}_{2}|{\mathcal{E}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\frac{d\bm{\widetilde{P}}}{(2{\pi})^{2}}\,e^{i\bm{\widetilde{P}}\cdot\left(\bm{\widetilde{R}}^{\prime}-\bm{\widetilde{R}}\right)}\qquad\qquad

(7)

\displaystyle\phantom{sp}\times G^{(0)}_{P_{x}}(Y^{\prime},\bm{r}^{\prime};Y,\bm{r}|E),\,\,

where the shifted energy

E={\mathcal{E}}-\frac{P_{z}^{2}}{2M}

serves to remove all

P_{z}
-dependence
from the projected Green’s function.
Projecting
Eq. (
3
)
onto states of good

\bm{\widetilde{P}}
,
leads to the wave equation

\displaystyle\Psi_{P_{x}}(Y,\bm{r})=-c\int dY^{\prime}G^{(0)}_{P_{x}}(Y,\bm{r};Y^{\prime},{\bm{0}}|E)\Psi_{P_{x}}(Y^{\prime},{\bm{0}}).\quad\,\,\,

(8)

As with

3
),

Knowledge of this restricted wave-function

The restricted equation

\displaystyle\Psi_{P_{x}}(Y,{\bm{0}})=-c\int dY^{\prime}G^{(0)}_{P_{x}}(Y,{\bm{0}};Y^{\prime},{\bm{0}}|E)\Psi_{P_{x}}(Y^{\prime},{\bm{0}}),\quad\quad

(9)

)

,
and suffices to determine the energy

.
Simplifications arise because the configuration space is restricted to both particles being at the same point,
for which there remains only center-of-mass motion of a system having charge

This is manifest in the functional form of the restricted non-interacting Green’s function

(11)

where

-coordinate

.

For arbitrary charges and masses of the two particles,

is diagonal in the basis of Hermite functions

6

6

The dimensionless Hermite functions employed above form an orthonormal basis,

-th member is given by

\psi_{n}({\xi})=H_{n}({\xi})\,e^{-\frac{1}{2}{\xi}^{2}}\Big/\sqrt{2^{n}\,n!\sqrt{{\pi}}},

Projecting
Eq. (
9
)
onto the

III.3
Unitary Limit

To determine the behavior of the binding energy in the unitary limit

III.4
Binding Thresholds and the Number of Levels

The binding threshold is reached when

\chi{=}0
.
As remarked above,
the lowest level does not reach threshold;
it remains bound in the unitary limit,
which can alternately be viewed as the large-field limit with

held fixed.
The excited levels,
however,
reach threshold at critical values of the magnetic field.
10
10

10

Note that for all values of the magnetic field,
excited levels are unstable to radiative decay to the ground level.
Such decays require electrodynamics,
which is beyond the scope of this investigation.

According to Eq. (

the threshold for single-particle production occurs when the magnetic field has the value

\displaystyle b_{c}=\big[{\mathcal{I}}_{n}(0,x)\big]^{-2},

(30)

provided

For the first excited level

(31)

For the higher-lying levels,
the critical value of the magnetic field decreases monotonically.
Consequently,
we have the single-particle decay threshold at

b_{c}\ll 1
,
for

n\gg 1
.
Viewed as a function of the level number

the critical field

IV
Summary

Systems of two interacting charged particles in a magnetic field present an analytically challenging non-separable problem.
When the particles have an attractive,
momentum-independent,
zero-range
interaction,
we confirm in
Sec.
II

that the bound states have infinite degeneracy,
and are characterized by a quantum number
that incorporates both center-of-mass and relative degrees of freedom.
In small magnetic fields,
this quantum number is the Landau level of the center of mass,
as exhibited by
Eq. (
25
),
which is the small-field expansion of the energy of a system consisting of one charged and one neutral particle.
Near the unitary limit,
there is dramatically different behavior for the ground and excited levels of this system.
The lowest level is bound,
with a universal binding energy determined by
Eq. (
27
).
The excited states disappear into the continuum at critical values of the magnetic field,
which are determined by
Eq. (
30
).
The number of bound levels as a function of the magnetic field is well described by the remarkably simple formula in
Eq. (
33
).

A striking feature of this two-body system
in a magnetic background is
that its bound-state spectrum is identical to
that of an effective one-body problem.
The effective interaction in this restricted space accounts for the
background field and zero-range interaction.
The closed-form correlation function,
Eq. (
15
),
is
suggestive of an effective time-dependent Hamiltonian.
In this way,
complexity of the non-separable system manifests
as non-trivial time-dependence
of
the restricted system.
Nonetheless, renormalization can be carried out by hand,
thus enabling straightforward determination
of spectral properties.
In particular,
Eq. (
20
)
shows that the inverse of the solution,
namely the magnetic field as a function of the binding energy,
only requires evaluation of a one-dimensional integral.
The number of systems possessing such almost-in-closed-form
solutions can be enlarged by generalizing and extending the problem solved here.
Of particular interest would be a complete treatment of the two-nucleon system.

Appendix A
Green’s Function

The Green’s function

G^{(0)}

in Eq. (
4
)
is computed from a Laplace transform
using the inverse operator

\frac{1}{E-H^{(0)}+i{\epsilon}}=-\int_{0}^{\infty}dt\,e^{Et}\,e^{-H^{(0)}t},

(34)

where analytic continuation is necessary when

E>0
.
We refer to

t

as time;
although,
strictly speaking,
it is imaginary time.
As

H^{(0)}_{1}

and

H^{(0)}_{2}

in Eq. (
2
)
commute,

G^{(0)}

factorizes into the product of single-particle Green’s functions

\displaystyle G^{(0)}({\bm{r}}^{\prime}_{1},{\bm{r}}^{\prime}_{2};{\bm{r}}_{1},{\bm{r}}_{2}|t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\prod_{a=1}^{2}\,\big\langle{\bm{r}}^{\prime}_{a}\big|e^{-H^{(0)}_{a}t}\big|{\bm{r}}_{a}\big\rangle.

(35)

For the gauge chosen in Eq. (
5
),
the Green’s function of the charged particle takes the form

\displaystyle\big\langle{\bm{r}}^{\prime}_{1}\big|e^{-H^{(0)}_{1}t}\big|{\bm{r}}_{1}\big\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\frac{d\bm{\tilde{p}}_{1}}{(2\pi)^{2}}e^{i\bm{\tilde{p}}_{1}\cdot(\bm{\tilde{r}}^{\prime}_{1}-\bm{\tilde{r}}_{1})}e^{-\frac{p_{1,z}^{2}}{2m_{1}}t}

(36)

\displaystyle\qquad\times\sqrt{QB}\,\,\big\langle{\xi}^{\prime}_{1},\,{\omega}t\,\big|{\xi}_{1},0\big\rangle,

and has been written in terms of the Green’s function of the simple harmonic oscillator.
With dimensionless conjugate variables

{\xi}
and
p_{\xi}
,
the oscillator Hamiltonian appears as

{\mathcal{H}}=\frac{1}{2}\big(p_{\xi}^{2}+{\xi}^{2}\big)
,
for which the corresponding propagator we define as

\langle{\xi}^{\prime},s\,|{\xi},0\rangle=\langle{\xi}^{\prime}|e^{-{\mathcal{H}}\,s}|{\xi}\rangle
.
In Eq. (
36
),

{\omega}

is the charged particle’s cyclotron frequency,
and
the dimensionless charged-particle
coordinates are

\displaystyle{\xi}^{\prime}_{1}=\sqrt{QB}\left(y^{\prime}_{1}-\frac{p_{1,x}}{QB}\right)\,\text{and}\,\,\,{\xi}_{1}=\sqrt{QB}\left(y_{1}-\frac{p_{1,x}}{QB}\right).

The coordinate-space Green’s function of the neutral particle can be written as a Fourier transform

\displaystyle\big\langle{\bm{r}}^{\prime}_{2}\big|e^{-H^{(0)}_{2}t}\big|{\bm{r}}_{2}\big\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\frac{d{\bm{p}}_{2}}{(2{\pi})^{3}}e^{i{\bm{p}}_{2}\cdot({\bm{r}}^{\prime}_{2}-{\bm{r}}_{2})}e^{-\frac{{\bm{p}}_{2}^{2}}{2m_{2}}t}.

(38)

Following Sec.
II
,
we project
Eq. (
35
) onto good

\bm{\widetilde{P}}
,
and restrict the computation of the matrix element to vanishing relative separation

\bm{r}^{\prime}{=}\bm{r}{=}{\bm{0}}
.
The resulting time-dependent,
non-interacting Green’s function

g^{(0)}({\xi};{\xi}^{\prime}|t)

is both

P_{x}
independent
and diagonal in the basis of Hermite functions.
The diagonal matrix elements are the

g_{n}^{(0)}(t)

coefficients appearing in
Eq. (
15
).

Appendix B
The Function
{\mathcal{I}}_{n}(\chi,x)

The function

{\mathcal{I}}_{n}
appearing in
Eq. (
20
)
is defined as

{\mathcal{I}}_{n}(\chi,x)=-\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\,{\zeta}\left(\tfrac{1}{2},\tfrac{\chi+x}{2}\right)-{\mathcal{J}}_{n}(\chi,x),

(39)

with

{\zeta}(\frac{1}{2},z)

as a Hurwitz zeta-function,
and where

\displaystyle{\mathcal{J}}_{n}(\chi,x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}ds\frac{e^{(1-x-\chi)s}}{\sqrt{4\pi}s^{3/2}}\Big[\,{\mathcal{F}}_{n}(x,s)-\frac{s}{\sinh s}\Big].\quad\,

(40)

Appearing in the above integrand is

{\mathcal{F}}_{n}(x,s)
,
which has the explicit form

{\mathcal{F}}_{n}(x,s)=\frac{\sinh\frac{\Omega(s)\,s}{2}}{x\,\sinh[(1-x)s]}\,e^{-\left(n+\frac{1}{2}\right)\Omega(s)\,s},

(41)

using

\coth\frac{\Omega(s)\,s}{2}=\coth[(1-x)s]+(xs)^{-1}
.
The integral defining

{\mathcal{J}}_{n}(\chi,x)

converges in the infrared
(
s\to\infty
)
provided

\chi\geq 0
.
The definition of this function,
moreover,
is such that

\lim_{x\to 0}{\mathcal{J}}_{n}(\chi,x)=0
.

To investigate the critical field

b_{c}

in Eq. (
30
)
as a function of the level number,
we differentiate
Eq. (
20
)
with respect to

n

at

\chi=0
,
which produces

\frac{1}{b_{c}^{3/2}}\frac{db_{c}}{dn}=\int_{0}^{\infty}ds\frac{e^{(1-x)s}}{\sqrt{\pi}s^{3/2}}\frac{d}{dn}{\mathcal{F}}_{n}(x,s).

(42)

Scaling

s\to 2\,s/n
,
and treating

n\gg 1

leads to the approximate formula

\frac{1}{b_{c}^{3/2}}\frac{db_{c}}{dn}=-\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\,ds\,e^{-s}}{\sqrt{2\pi ns}}\,e^{\frac{s}{2n}}\left[1+{\mathcal{O}}(n^{-2})\right],

(43)

for the case of equal masses.
The exponential factor

e^{\frac{s}{2n}}

need not be expanded in powers of

n^{-1}
,
as the integral can be performed with this factor treated exactly.
Solving the above differential equation for

b_{c}

subject to the condition

\lim_{n\to\infty}b_{c}(n)=0

results in
Eq. (
32
).

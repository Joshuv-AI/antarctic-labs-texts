Deep-water and shallow-water limits of the intermediate long wave equation
Guopeng Li

Abstract
. In this paper, we study the low regularity convergence problem for the intermediate long wave equation (ILW), with respect to the depth parameter δ > 0 \delta>0 , on the real line and the circle. As a natural bridge between the Korteweg-de Vries (KdV) and the Benjamin-Ono (BO) equations, the ILW equation is of physical interest. We prove that the solutions of ILW converge in the H s H^{s} -Sobolev space for s > 1 2 s>\frac{1}{2} , to those of BO in the deep-water limit (as δ → ∞ \delta\to\infty ), and to those of KdV in the shallow-water limit (as δ → 0 \delta\to 0 ). This improves previous convergence results by Abdelouhab, Bona, Felland, and Saut (1989), which required s > 3 2 s>\frac{3}{2} in the deep-water limit and s ≥ 2 s\geq 2 in the shallow-water limit. Moreover, the convergence results also apply to the generalised ILW equation, i.e. with nonlinearity ∂ x ( u k ) \partial_{x}(u^{k}) for k ≥ 2 k\geq 2 . Furthermore, this work gives the first convergence results of generalised ILW solutions on the circle with regularity s ≥ 3 4 s\geq\frac{3}{4} . Overall, this study provides mathematical insights for the behaviour of the ILW equation and its solutions in different water depths, and has implications for predicting and modelling wave behaviour in various environments.

Introduction

1.1.
Background

The rigorous theory of internal wave propagation at the interface between two layers of immiscible fluids of differing densities has garnered significant attention from both mathematical and physical studies.
This is due to the system’s simplicity as an idealisation of internal wave propagation, its challenging nature from a modelling perspective, and the mathematical and numerical difficulties that arise when analysing the system.
The Korteweg-de Vries equation (KdV) and the Benjamin-Ono equation (BO) are the most fundamental models for shallow-water wave and deep-water wave propagations, respectively. The intermediate long wave equation (ILW), on the other hand, models wave behavior in different water depths, which builds a model-theoretical bridge between the BO equation and the KdV equation.
The recent book by Klein-Saut
[
33
]
and the survey article by Saut
[
54
]
provide a comprehensive overview of the subject, along with relevant references.

To be more precise, the ILW equation is a one-way propagation asymptotic model that describes internal waves at the interface between two layers of immiscible fluids, under the rigid lid assumption and with a flat bottom. The depth parameter is defined by the relative depths of the two fluid layers, and the interface between the layers is approximately governed by the ILW equation. It is therefore natural to expect that as the depth tends to zero and infinity, the ILW should converge to KdV (shallow-water limit) and BO (deep-water limit) respectively. However, the rigours justification of such convergences, in particular in the low regularity regime, raises mathematical challenges, which will be the main concern of this paper.

The convergence problem of ILW is rooted in the study of water waves
[

As a result,
ILW provides a good understanding of the wave motions in different water depths and can be useful for various practical applications, such as predicting the behavior of ocean waves and designing coastal defense structures.

When studying the nonlinear dispersive equations, it is important to comprehend the interplay between the nonlinear and dispersive effects that determine the behavior of solutions. 1.2.
Intermediate long wave equation

The ILW equation is given by:

(1.1)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u-\mathcal{G}_{\delta}\partial_{x}^{2}u=\partial_{x}(u^{2})\\
u|_{t=0}=u_{0}\end{cases}\qquad(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathcal{M},

where
0<\delta<\infty
,
u:\mathbb{R}\times\mathcal{M}\to\mathbb{R}

and
\mathcal{M}=\mathbb{R}
or
\mathbb{T}=\mathbb{R}/(2\pi\mathbb{Z})
.
Here, the operator
\mathcal{G}_{\delta}
is defined by

\mathcal{G}_{\delta}=-\coth(\delta\partial_{x})-\delta^{-1}\partial_{x}^{-1},

which characterises the phase speed and it
is understood as the Fourier multiplier by

\widehat{\mathcal{G}_{\delta}f}(n):=-i\Big(\coth(\delta n)-\frac{1}{\delta n}\Big)\widehat{f}(n)\qquad\text{for $n\in\widehat{\mathcal{M}}$},

\coth(x)
is the hyperbolic cotangent function defined by

\coth(x)=\frac{e^{x}+e^{-x}}{e^{x}-e^{-x}},

x\in\mathbb{R}\backslash\{0\},
with the convention
\coth(x)-\frac{1}{x}=0
for
x=0
,
and

\widehat{\mathcal{M}}
is the
Pontryagin dual of
\mathcal{M}
, i.e.,
\widehat{\mathcal{M}}=\mathbb{R}
, when
\mathcal{M}=\mathbb{R}
, and

\widehat{\mathcal{M}}=\mathbb{Z}
, when
\mathcal{M}=\mathbb{T}.

Let

When examining the convergence of the scaled gILW solutions,

The resonance functions of the (scaled) gILW equations are a result of the multi-linear interaction due to the nonlinearity. 2.3.
Function spaces and their basic properties

In this subsection, we introduce the function spaces and their properties. To start with, we present a sequence of positive numbers
\{\omega_{N}\}_{N}
, which is an increasing sequence that depends on the dyadic number
N\in 2^{\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}}
. This sequence of weights
\{\omega_{N}\}_{N}
is referred to as the frequency envelope in
[
60
, Section 5]
. Its main purpose is to be useful in proving continuity with respect to the initial data, see Remark
3.2
. This technique was first introduced in
[
30
]
.
Additionally, we extract the following result from
[
46
, Lemma 4.6]
, which will assist us in choosing our frequency envelope
\omega_{N}
.

Lemma 2.9

(
[
46
]
Lemma 4.6)
.

Let
\kappa>1
, suppose the dyadic sequence
\{\omega_{N}\}
of positive numbers satisfies

(2.12)

\displaystyle\omega_{N}\leq\omega_{2N}\leq\kappa\omega_{N}\quad\quad\text{for}\quad N\geq 1,

and
\omega_{N}\to\infty
as
N\to\infty
.
Then, for any
1<\kappa^{\prime}<\kappa
, there exists a dyadic sequence
\{\widetilde{\omega}_{N}\}
such that

\displaystyle\widetilde{\omega}_{N}\leq\omega_{N},\quad\widetilde{\omega}_{N}\leq\widetilde{\omega}_{2N}\leq\kappa^{\prime}\widetilde{\omega}_{N}\quad\quad\text{for}\quad N\geq 1

and
\widetilde{\omega}_{N}\to\infty
as
N\to\infty
.

With the aid of Lemma
2.9
, for a given dyadic sequence
{\omega_{N}}
of positive numbers, it is possible to choose
\kappa\leq 2
. This in turn allows us to define a new dyadic sequence. Given two dyadic numbers
N
and
M
such that
1\leq M\leq\lambda N
for some
\lambda\geq 2
, we can use the inequality
\omega_{2N}\leq\kappa\omega_{N}
to deduce that:

\frac{\omega_{M}}{\omega_{N}}\lesssim\kappa^{\log_{2}\lambda}\lesssim\lambda

which is uniformly in
\kappa
.

In light of the preceding discussion on the dyadic sequence
{\omega_{N}}
of positive numbers, we propose a slight modification to the definition of the
L^{2}
-based Sobolev spaces. For a given value of
s\geq 0
, we define the space
H_{\omega}^{s}(\mathbb{T})
with the following norm:

\displaystyle\|u\|_{H_{\omega}^{s}}:=\Big(\sum_{N,\textup{dyadic}}\omega_{N}^{2}(1\vee N)^{2s}\|P_{N}u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

One simple observation is by selecting
\omega_{N}\equiv 1
, we can recover the standard
L^{2}
-based Sobolev space. In other words, if we set
\omega_{N}\equiv 1
, then
H_{\omega}^{s}(\mathbb{T})=H^{s}(\mathbb{T})
.

For a given range of values
1\leq p\leq\infty
and a positive time value
T>0
, let
B_{x}
be an arbitrary Banach space. To facilitate our analysis, we introduce the following shorthand notation:

\displaystyle L_{t}^{p}B_{x}:=L^{p}(\mathbb{R};B_{x})\qquad\text{\rm and}\qquad L_{T}^{p}B_{x}:=L^{p}([0,T];B_{x})

equipped with the norms

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L_{t}^{p}B_{x}}=\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\|u(t,\cdot)\|_{B_{x}}^{p}dt\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}}\qquad\textrm{and}\qquad\|u\|_{L_{T}^{p}B_{x}}=\Big(\int_{0}^{T}\|u(t,\cdot)\|_{B_{x}}^{p}dt\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}},

respectively.
In the case where
p=\infty
, the physical space is modified to the space of essentially bounded measurable functions, equipped with the essential supremum norm.

As is typical in the low-regularity analysis of dispersive PDEs, the Fourier restriction norm method plays a crucial role. This method was introduced in the publications by Bourgain
[
11
,
12
]
. For given values of
s,b\in\mathbb{R}
, we define the space
X^{s,b,\delta}(\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R})
, denoted by
X^{s,b,\delta}
, as the completion of the test functions with respect to the following norm:

(2.13)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \|u\|_{X^{s,b,\delta}(\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R})}&=\Big(\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\langle n\rangle^{2s}\langle\tau-p^{\rm(d)}_{\delta}(n)\rangle^{2b}|\widehat{u}(\tau,n)|^{2}d\tau\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
&=\|\langle n\rangle^{s}\langle\tau-p^{\rm(d)}_{\delta}(n)\rangle^{b}\widehat{u}(\tau,n)\|_{\ell^{2}_{n}L^{2}_{\tau}}.\end{aligned}

Similarly, we define
Y^{s,b,\delta}(\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R})
(=
Y^{s,b,\delta}
) as a completion of the
test functions under the following norm:

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \|v\|_{Y^{s,b,\delta}(\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R})}&=\Big(\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\langle n\rangle^{2s}\langle\tau-p^{\rm(s)}_{\delta}(n)\rangle^{2b}|\widehat{v}(\tau,n)|^{2}d\tau\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
&=\|\langle n\rangle^{s}\langle\tau-p^{\rm(s)}_{\delta}(n)\rangle^{b}\widehat{v}(\tau,n)\|_{\ell^{2}_{n}L^{2}_{\tau}}.\end{aligned}

We also use a slightly stronger space
X_{\omega}^{s,b,\delta}
with the norm

\|u\|_{X_{\omega}^{s,b,\delta}}:=\Big(\sum_{N}\omega_{N}^{2}(1\vee N)^{2s}\|P_{N}u\|_{X^{0,b}}^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}},

and

Y_{\omega}^{s,b,\delta}
with the norm

\|v\|_{Y_{\omega}^{s,b,\delta}}:=\Big(\sum_{N}\omega_{N}^{2}(1\vee N)^{2s}\|P_{N}v\|_{Y^{0,b}}^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Moreover,
we define the function spaces
M^{s,\delta}
and
M^{s,\delta}_{\omega}
in the following way.

M^{s,\delta}:=L_{t}^{\infty}H^{s}\cap X^{s-1,1,\delta}\qquad\qquad M^{s,\delta}_{\omega}:=L_{t}^{\infty}H_{\omega}^{s}\cap X_{\omega}^{s-1,1,\delta},

endowed with the natural norm

\|u\|_{M^{s,\delta}}=\|u\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}H^{s}}+\|u\|_{X^{s-1,1,\delta}}\qquad\qquad\|u\|_{M^{s,\delta}_{\omega}}=\|u\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}H^{s}_{\omega}}+\|u\|_{X^{s-1,1,\delta}_{\omega}}.

In the same line as above, we
define the function spaces
N^{s,\delta}
and
N^{s,\delta}_{\omega}
:

N^{s,\delta}:=L_{t}^{\infty}H^{s}\cap Y^{s-1,1,\delta}\qquad\qquad N^{s,\delta}_{\omega}:=L_{t}^{\infty}H_{\omega}^{s}\cap Y_{\omega}^{s-1,1,\delta},

endowed with the natural norm

\|v\|_{N^{s,\delta}}=\|v\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}H^{s}}+\|v\|_{Y^{s-1,1,\delta}}\qquad\qquad\|v\|_{N^{s,\delta}_{\omega}}=\|v\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}H^{s}_{\omega}}+\|v\|_{Y^{s-1,1,\delta}_{\omega}}.

In addition, we can also consider the time-restricted versions of these spaces. Given a positive time value
T>0
and a normed space of space-time functions
B
, the restriction space
B_{T}
consists of functions
u:(0,T)\times\mathbb{T}\to\mathbb{R}
that satisfy:

\displaystyle\|u\|_{B_{T}}:=\inf\{\|\widetilde{u}\|_{B}\,|\,\widetilde{u}:\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}\to\mathbb{R}\,;\widetilde{u}=u\,\textrm{on}\,(0,T)\times\mathbb{T}\}<\infty.

\displaystyle\sum_{N\geq 1}N^{2s-2}\|T_{\delta,\gamma}P_{N}\partial_{x}u_{\gamma})\|_{L^{2}_{T,x}}\|P_{N}w\|_{L^{2}_{T,x}}\lesssim C\Big(\frac{1}{\delta}+\frac{1}{\gamma}\Big)^{2}\|u_{\gamma}\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}_{T}H^{s}_{x}}+c\|w\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}H^{s-1}_{x}}^{2},

In particular, any

we have

Hence, it can be inferred that (

\begin{split}\|w(t)\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}_{T}H^{s-1}_{x}}&\leq C\Big(\frac{1}{\delta}+\frac{1}{\gamma}\Big)^{2}\|u_{\gamma}\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}_{T}H_{x}^{s}}+c\|w(t)\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}_{T}H_{x}^{s-1}}\\
&\quad+T^{\frac{1}{4}}C(\|u_{\gamma}\|_{M^{s,\delta}_{T}}+\|u_{\delta}\|_{M^{s,\delta}_{T}})\|w\|_{M^{s-1,\delta}_{T}}\|w\|_{L_{T}^{\infty}H_{x}^{s-1}}.\end{split}

Now,
Lemmas
2.15
and
3.4
imply that
for any

,

(3.19)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \|u_{\gamma}\|_{M^{s,\delta}_{T}}+\|u_{\delta}\|_{M^{s,\delta}_{T}}&\lesssim\|u_{\gamma}\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}H^{s}_{x}}+C(\|u_{\gamma}\|_{L^{\infty}_{T,x}})\|u_{\gamma}\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}H^{s}_{x}}\\
&\quad+\|u_{\delta}\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}H^{s}_{x}}+C(\|u_{\delta}\|_{L^{\infty}_{T,x}})\|u_{\delta}\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}H^{s}_{x}}.\end{aligned}

Next, we also need to estimate

in (

and

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \|w\|_{M_{T}^{s-1,\delta}}&\lesssim C(\|u_{\gamma}\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}H^{s}_{x}},\|u_{\gamma}\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}H^{s}_{x}})\|w\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}H^{s-1}_{x}}\\

, for any value of

), we can deduce that there exists a universal constant

\|u_{\delta}\|_{C_{T}H^{s}_{x}}\lesssim\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s}}<M.

Consequently, based on equations (

), (
3.20
), and (
3.21
), it can be inferred that there exists a universal constant

that satisfies the the following

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \|w\|_{M_{T}^{s-1,\delta}}+\|u_{\gamma}\|_{M^{s,\delta}_{T}}+\|u_{\delta}\|_{M^{s,\delta}_{T}}&\leq C+C\|w\|_{C_{T}H^{s-1}_{x}}\\
&\quad+C(\delta^{-1}+\gamma^{-1})\|u_{\gamma}\|_{C_{T}H^{s-1}_{x}}.\end{aligned}

Therefore, by combining equations (

\displaystyle\|w(t)\|^{2}_{C_{T}H^{s-1}_{x}}

\displaystyle\leq C\Big(\frac{1}{\delta}+\frac{1}{\gamma}\Big)^{2}M^{2}+c\|w(t)\|_{C_{T}H_{x}^{s-1}}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+CT^{\frac{1}{4}}\|w(t)\|^{2}_{C_{T}H_{x}^{s-1}}+\frac{T^{\frac{1}{2}}M^{2}}{2}\Big(\frac{1}{\delta}+\frac{1}{\gamma}\Big)^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|w(t)\|^{2}_{C_{T}H_{x}^{s-1}}

To do so, we apply the triangle inequality and write the following

\begin{split}\|u_{\delta}-u_{\gamma}\|_{C_{T}H^{s}_{x}}&\lesssim\|u_{\delta}-P_{\leq K}u_{\delta}\|_{C_{T}H_{x}^{s}}+\|P_{\leq K}u_{\delta}-P_{\leq K}u_{\gamma}\|_{C_{T}H_{x}^{s}}\\

)

\displaystyle\|P_{\leq K}(u_{\delta})-P_{\leq K}(u_{\gamma})\|_{C_{T}H_{x}^{s}}\leq 2K\|u_{\delta}-u_{\gamma}\|_{C_{T}H^{s-1}_{x}}<\frac{CK}{\delta}

,

As

When

As we saw above, equation (

∎

To conclude the proof of Theorem
1.3
.
By invoking Proposition
3.3
, there exists a function
u\in C([0,T];H^{s}(\mathbb{T}))
such that as
\delta
approaches infinity,

converges to

in the space

. Our goal now is to demonstrate that

is indeed a solution to the gBO equation.
We observe from (

) that for any value of
\delta\geq 2
, the following inequality holds:

(3.23)

\|\mathcal{Q}_{\delta}u_{\delta}\|_{C_{T}H_{x}^{s}}\leq\frac{2}{\delta}\|u_{\delta}\|_{C_{T}H_{x}^{s}}\leq\frac{c}{\delta},

for some universal constant

.
Thus, it becomes evident that

is indeed the solution to the gBO equation with the initial data

. This is because we have established that the gILW equation can be represented as a perturbed gBO equation.

\partial_{t}u_{\delta}+\mathcal{H}(\partial_{x}^{2}u_{\delta})+\partial_{x}(u_{\delta}^{k})+\mathcal{Q}_{\delta}(\partial_{x}u_{\delta})=0.

Due to the almost everywhere convergence of the linear part, the following convergence is achieved as

\displaystyle\partial_{t}{u_{\delta}}+\mathcal{H}\partial_{x}^{2}{u_{\delta}}+\partial_{x}(u_{\delta}^{k})\ \xrightarrow{\mathcal{D}^{\prime}}\ \partial_{t}u+\mathcal{H}\partial_{x}^{2}u+\partial_{x}u^{k},

i.e. convergent in the distributional sense.
Furthermore, as indicated in equation (
3.23
),
\mathcal{Q}_{\delta}(\partial_{x}u_{\delta})
vanishes as

.
As a result, it can be concluded that

is a solution to the gBO equation.

3.3.
The shallow-water limit

In this subsection, we aim to compare the solutions of the gILW equation to those of the gKdV equation as the limit
\delta\to 0
is approached. As was discussed in subsection
1.3
, a rescaling of the gILW equation is necessary, which is given by:

(3.24)

\partial_{t}v_{\delta}-\frac{3}{\delta}\mathcal{G}_{\delta}(\partial_{x}^{2}v_{\delta})=\partial_{x}(v_{\delta}^{k}).

It is worth mentioning from Lemma
2.1
that

(3.25)

\displaystyle\frac{3}{\delta}\widehat{\mathcal{G}_{\delta}\partial_{x}}(n)=\frac{3}{\delta}(n\coth(\delta n)-\frac{1}{\delta})=n^{2}-n^{2}\frac{h(n,\delta)}{\delta},

where
h(n,\delta)
is a bounded function that approaches
O(\delta^{3})
as
\delta\to 0
, uniformly for all values of
n
in any bounded set of
\mathbb{R}
. This property is discussed in more detail in Remark
2.2
.

In the shallow-water limit, one of the key challenges is that uniform convergence (with respect to frequency
n
) in
\delta
is not guaranteed, as opposed to the deep-water case. To address this, we need to perform a frequency truncation argument, which can be explained as follows:
Our goal is to prove that, given
\varepsilon>0
,
\|v_{\delta}-v_{\gamma}\|_{C_{T}H^{s}}\lesssim\varepsilon
. This will break into two steps:
(i) Show that, given
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
N=N(\varepsilon)
such that
\|v_{\delta}-v_{\delta,N}\|_{C_{T}H^{s}}\lesssim\varepsilon
, uniformly in
0<\delta<1
.
Here,
v_{\delta,N}
is a solution to the truncated equation. Thus, in estimating the difference of the nonlinearities, we have the difference between the low-frequency part and also the high-frequency part of nonlinearity
f(v_{\delta})=(u_{\delta})^{k}
, where the latter is to be controlled uniformly in
\delta
and shown to be less than
\varepsilon
for large
N
(via the Koch-Tzvetkov argument as discussed in Remark
3.2
).
(ii) Show that with the frequency truncation parameter
N
as above, there exists
\delta_{0}>0
such that
\|v_{\delta,N}-v_{\gamma,N}\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{T})}\lesssim\varepsilon
for any
0<\delta,\gamma<\delta_{0}
.

For any values of
0<\delta,\gamma\ll 1
, let
v_{\delta}
and
v_{\gamma}
be two solutions of the scaled
{\rm gILW}_{\delta}
and scaled
\textrm{gILW}_{\gamma}
equations, respectively, in the form of (
3.24
), with the same initial data. Then, the difference between these two solutions,
w=v_{\delta}-v_{\gamma}
, satisfies the following equation:

(3.26)

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}w+\partial_{x}^{3}w+H_{\delta}(\partial_{x}w)=(H_{\gamma}-H_{\delta})\partial_{x}v_{\gamma}-\partial_{x}(v_{\delta}^{k}-v_{\gamma}^{k})\\
w(x,0)=0,\end{cases}

where the Fourier multiplier defines
H_{\delta}
:

(3.27)

\widehat{H}_{\delta}(n):=-{n^{2}}\frac{h(n,\delta)}{\delta}

Moreover, for convenience, we denote

(3.28)

L_{\delta,\gamma}=H_{\gamma}-H_{\delta}.

In order to fully utilize our findings on
h(n,\delta)
, we consider the following frequency truncated scaled gILW equation, where a frequency truncation is applied both to the nonlinearity
f(u)=u^{k}
and the initial data:

(3.29)

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}v_{\delta,K}-\frac{3}{\delta}\mathcal{G}_{\delta}\partial_{x}^{2}v_{\delta,K}=\partial_{x}(f_{K}(v_{\delta,K})),\\
v_{\delta,K}|_{t=0}=v_{0,K}.\end{cases}

In the shallow-water limit, this leads to the frequency truncated gKdV equation

(3.30)

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}v_{K}+\partial_{x}^{3}v_{K}=\partial_{x}(f_{K}(v_{K})),\\
v_{K}|_{t=0}=v_{0,K}.\end{cases}

The corresponding solution
v_{\delta,N},v_{N}
are supported on frequency
{|n|\leq K}
.
Additionally, we use
f_{K}=P_{\leq K}f
to denote the frequency truncation applied to the nonlinearity. The first step in our analysis is the following proposition.

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
k\geq 2
,
s\geq\frac{3}{4}
and
K\in 2^{\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}}
to be fixed.
Assume that
v_{\delta,K}
to be the solution of (
3.29
) with initial data
v_{0,K}
.
Then, for any
0<T<1
, we have that

(3.31)

\displaystyle\|v_{\delta,K}-v_{K}\|_{C([0,T];H^{s}(\mathbb{T}))}\longrightarrow 0\qquad\text{as}\quad\delta\to 0,

where
v_{K}
is the solution of (
3.30
) with initial data
v_{0,K}
.
Moreover, when
k=2
only the statement holds for
s>\frac{1}{2}
.

To prove Proposition
3.6
, we will use the following auxiliary lemmas.

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
k\geq 2
,
and
0<T<1

as

Moreover, we follow the same

for

Proof of Proposition
3.6
.

Let

,

,

. We first show that for

), the the one-parameter family of solutions

is a Cauchy sequence in

as
\delta\to 0
.

For any
0<\gamma<\delta<1
, let us set
w_{K}=v_{\gamma,K}-v_{\delta,K}
. Then, we consider the frequency truncated version of equation (

)

(3.46)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\partial_{t}w_{K}+\partial_{x}^{3}w_{K}+H_{\delta}(\partial_{x}w_{K})=L_{\delta,\gamma}(\partial_{x}v_{\gamma,K})-\partial_{x}(f_{K}(v_{\delta,K})-f_{K}(v_{\gamma,K})),\\

We use a similar approach as in equation (
3.15
) for the equation (
3.46
):

(3.47)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \|w_{K}(t)\|^{2}_{H^{s-1}_{x}}&\lesssim\sum_{1\leq N\leq K}N^{2s-2}\|L_{\delta,\gamma}(\partial_{x}P_{N}v_{\gamma,K})\|_{L^{2}_{T,x}}\|P_{N}w_{K}\|_{L^{2}_{T,x}}\\
\quad&+2\sum_{k\geq 2}\max_{i\in\{0,..,k-1\}}I_{k,i,K}^{t,\delta,\gamma},\end{aligned}

where
I_{k,i,K}^{t,\delta,\gamma}
is now defined by

\displaystyle I_{k,i,K}^{t,\delta,\gamma}:=\sum_{1\leq N\leq K}N^{2(s-1)}\bigg|\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}}v_{\delta,K}^{i}v_{\gamma,K}^{k-1-i}w_{K}P_{N}^{2}\partial_{x}w_{K}dxdt^{\prime}\bigg|.

Regarding the first term on the right-hand-side of (

Proof of Corollary

.

To prove this corollary, let us consider a fixed BO initial condition

The method developed in Section

.

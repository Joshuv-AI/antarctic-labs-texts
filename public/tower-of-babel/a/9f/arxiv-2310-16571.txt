On the average hitting times of weighted Cayley graphs
Yuuho Tanaka

Abstract
. In the present paper, we give the exact formula for the average hitting time (HT, as an abbreviation) of random walks from one vertex to any other vertex on the some weighted Cayley graphs.



Keywords: random walk, hitting time, weighted graph, Cayley graph, Kichhoff index

1.
Introduction

Let
G=(V,E)
be a graph.
A
weight
on graph
G
is a function
w:V\times V\to[0,+\infty]
such that for
u,v\in V
,

\begin{cases}w((u,v))>0&\text{if $(u,v)\in E$,}\\
w((u,v))=0&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

In other words,
w
is a function that assigns to each edge
((u,v))\in E
a positive number, namely
w((u,v))
.
Such a graph
G
is called a
weighted graph
.
Every graph
G=(V,E)
can be given the
trivial weight

w((u,v))=1
if
(u,v\in E)
and
w((u,v))=0
otherwise. In this case, we refer to
G
as to an
unweighted graph
.

A random walk on a graph
G
is a discrete stochastic model
such that a random walker on a vertex
u\in V
moves to a vertex
v

adjacent to the vertex
u
at the next step with the probability of

\frac{w((u,v))}{W(u)}
, where
W(u)=\sum_{x\in V}w((u,x))
.
A random walk on an unweighted graph (namely
w((u,v))/W(u)=1/\deg(u)
, where
\deg(u)=|\{v\in V\mid(u,v)\in V\}|
) is called a
simple random walk
.
The number of steps required until the random walker starting from
a vertex
u
of
G
will first arrive at a vertex
v
of
G
is called

the hitting time
from
u
to
v
of the random walk on
G
.

The average hitting time
(HT, as an abbreviation) from
u
to
v
on
G

which is denoted by
h(G;u,v)
, means the expected value of the hitting times
from
u
to
v
of random walks on
G
.

By using an elementary method, Y. Doi et al.
[
4
]
gave the exact formula for the HT’s of simple random walks on the unweighted Cayley graph
\Cay(\mathbb{Z}_{N},\{\pm 1,\pm 2\})
, as follows:

Theorem 1.1

(Y. Doi et al., 2022
[
4
]
)
.

The exact formula for the HT’s of random walks on the unweighted Cayley graph
\Cay(\mathbb{Z}_{N},\{\pm 1,\pm 2\})

(N\geq 5)
is,

h(\Cay(\mathbb{Z}_{N},\{\pm 1,\pm 2\});0,\ell)=\frac{2}{5}\left(\ell\left(N-\ell\right)+2N\frac{F_{\ell}F_{N-\ell}}{F_{N}}\right),

where
F_{i}
is the
i
-th Fibonacci number.

Y. Tanaka
[
10
]
gave the exact formula for the HT’s of simple random walks on the unweighted Cayley graph
\Cay(\mathbb{Z}_{N},\{+1,+2\})
likewise
:

Theorem 1.2

(Y. Tanaka, 2023
[
10
]
)
.

The exact formula for the HT’s of random walks on the unweighted Cayley graph
\Cay(\mathbb{Z}_{N},\{+1,+2\})
is,

\displaystyle h(\Cay(\mathbb{Z}_{N},\{+1,+2\});0,\ell)=\frac{1}{3J_{N}}(2J_{\ell-1}(3\ell J_{N-\ell-1}+2\ell J_{N-\ell})

\displaystyle~+J_{\ell}((N+\ell+3)J_{N-\ell-1}+(N+3\ell+1)J_{N-\ell})),

where
J_{i}
is the
i
-th Jacobsthal number.

The purpose of the present paper is to give the exact formula for the HT’s of random walk on weighted Cayley graphs by using the matrix equation and the decomposition of matrices.
X. Chang et al.
[
2
]
obtained the explicit formula for the average hitting times of random walks on weighted undirected graphs by using the formula for the number of spanning trees of the graphs.
Therefore, the aim herein is to obtain the exact formula for the average hitting times of random walks on weighted Cayley graphs
\Cay(\mathbb{Z}_{2n},\{\pm 1\})
and
\Cay(\mathbb{Z}_{N},\{+1,+2\})
.

For undirected Cayley graphs, in the method using matrix equations, the components of the inverse of the coefficient matrix can be expressed using the sequence of numbers appearing in the formula for the number of spanning trees
[
4
,
8
]
.
Therefore, we reason that the same might be true for weighted Cayley graphs.
For a weighted graph, we can obtain the weighted spanning tree enumerator from the weighted matrix-tree theorem
[
6
,
7
]
.
So, we can guess the components of the inverse of the coefficient matrix in this case.

Our proof is considerably short and fully combinatorial.
In particular, it has no-need of any spectral graph theoretical arguments.
For the weighted directed Cayley graph
\Cay(\mathbb{Z}_{N},\{+1,+2\})
, we derive the original method by combining the method for solving matrix equations using the Laplacian matrix and the
LU
decomposition
[
5
]
.

This paper is organized as follows.
In Section 2, we give some definitions of weighted graphs. In Section 3, we give the exact formula for the HT’s of random walk on the weighted Cayley graph
\Cay(\mathbb{Z}_{2n},\{\pm 1\})
in a special case, decomposing the coefficient matrix and expressing it as a product of the simplicial matrices after defining the matrix equations for the average hitting times.
We also consider the inverse of each of these simple matrices and calculate them to give the exact formula.
In Section 4, we give the exact formula for the HT’s of random walk on the weighted Cayley graph
\Cay(\mathbb{Z}_{N},\{+1,+2\})
in a special case.
Finally, in Section 5, we give the exact formula for the Kirchhoff index and weighted Kichhoff index of random walk on the weighted Cayley graphs.

2.
Preliminaries

A
simple graph
without
loops
is a couple
G=(V,E)
, where
V
is a set of
vertices
and
E
is a family of ordered pairs
(x,y)
of elements in
V
, possibly collapsing to a singleton (namely when
x=y
), which is called the set of
edges
.
If
(x,y)\in E
, then we say that vertices
x
and
y
are
incident
with the edge
(x,y)

joining

x
and
y
.
Moreover, if
(x,x)\in E
, then we say that
\{x\}
is a
loop
.

The
Laplacian matrix

\mathrm{Lap}_{G}=[\mathrm{Lap}_{G}(i,j)]
for a weighted graph
G
is defined as

\mathrm{Lap}_{G}(i,j)=\begin{cases}\sum_{x\in V}w((i,x))&\text{if $i=j$,}\\
-w((i,j))&\text{if $i\neq j,\;(i,j)\in E$,}\\
0&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Let
\mathrm{Lap}_{G}^{\prime}
(resp.
\mathrm{Lap}_{G}^{\prime\prime}
) be the matrix obtained from
\mathrm{Lap}_{G}
by deleting the first (resp. last) row and column.

Let
G
be a finite group and let
S\subseteq G
be a subset.
The corresponding
Cayley graph

\Cay({\it G,S})
has vertices set equal to
G
.
Two vertices
g,h\in G
are joined by a directed edge from
g
to
h
if and only if there exists
s\in S
such that
g=sh
.
For any vertex
x
of a Cayley graph
\Cay({\it G,S})
,
\deg(x)=|S|
is constant.

3.
Weighted Cayley graph
\Cay(\mathbb{Z}_{2n},\{\pm 1\})

We defined a weight
w(\{v_{i},v_{j}\})
on the weighted undirected Cayley graph
\Cay(\mathbb{Z}_{2n},\{\pm 1\})
as follows:

w(\{v_{i},v_{j}\})=\begin{cases}p&\text{if $j-i=1$, $i$ is even,}\\
q&\text{if $j-i=1$, $i$ is odd,}\\
0&\text{otherwise,}\end{cases}\quad\text{($v_{i},v_{j}\in\mathbb{Z}_{2n},i,j\in\mathbb{Z}$),}

where
v_{i}=i+2n\mathbb{Z}\in\mathbb{Z}_{2n}
.

The average hitting time from a vertex
v_{k}
to a vertex
v_{\ell}
on the weighted undirected Cayley graph
\Cay(\mathbb{Z}_{2n},\{\pm 1\})
by
h_{2n}(k,\ell)
is as follows.
Let
\vec{h}
be the column vector whose
i
-th entry is

\begin{cases}h_{2n}(1,2n-i+1)&\text{if $1\leq i\leq 2n-1$,\;$i$ is odd,}\\
h_{2n}(0,2n-i)&\text{if $1\leq i\leq 2n-1$,\;$i$ is even,}\\
h_{2n}(1,4n-i)&\text{if $2n\leq i\leq 2(2n-1)$,\;$i$ is odd,}\\
h_{2n}(0,4n-i-1)&\text{if $2n\leq i\leq 2(2n-1)$,\;$i$ is even,}\end{cases}

and let
\vec{1}
be a column vector of proper dimensions whose entries are all
1
.

On the weighted undirected Cayley graph
\Cay(\mathbb{Z}_{2n},\{\pm 1\})
, a walker can start a simple random walk from vertex
0
or
1
without loss of generality.
By definition, in a random walk on
\Cay(\mathbb{Z}_{2n},\{\pm 1\})
, the walker moves with probability
p/(p+q)
or
q/(p+q)
to an arbitrary vertex adjacent to the vertex where the walker is.
Moreover, by the symmetry of
\Cay(\mathbb{Z}_{2n},\{\pm 1\})
, we have

\displaystyle h_{2n}(0,\ell)

\displaystyle=h_{2n}(2k,2k+\ell)=h_{2n}(2k,2k-2n+\ell),

\displaystyle h_{2n}(1,\ell)

\displaystyle=h_{2n}(2k+1,2k+\ell)=h_{2n}(2k+1,2k-2n+\ell)

for all
k,\ell\in\mathbb{Z}_{2n}
.
By the above properties, we have that
\forall\ell\in\mathbb{Z}_{2n}\setminus\{0\}
,

h_{2n}(0,\ell)=\frac{1}{p+q}\left(q(1+h_{2n}(-1,\ell))+p(1+h_{2n}(1,\ell))\right),

and hence
\forall\ell\in\mathbb{Z}_{2n}\setminus\{0\}
,

(p+q)h_{2n}(0,\ell)-qh_{2n}(1,\ell+2)-ph_{2n}(1,\ell)=p+q.

Similarly, we have that
\forall\ell\in\mathbb{Z}_{2n}\setminus\{1\}
,

h_{2n}(1,\ell)=\frac{1}{p+q}\left(p(1+h_{2n}(0,\ell))+q(1+h_{2n}(2,\ell))\right),

and hence
\forall\ell\in\mathbb{Z}_{2n}\setminus\{1\}
,

(p+q)h_{2n}(1,\ell)-ph_{2n}(0,\ell)-qh_{2n}(0,\ell-2)=p+q.

So we have
\begin{bmatrix}\mathrm{Lap}^{\prime}_{\Cay(\mathbb{Z}_{2n},\{\pm 1\})}&O\\
O&\mathrm{Lap}^{\prime\prime}_{\Cay(\mathbb{Z}_{2n},\{\pm 1\})}\end{bmatrix}\vec{h}=(p+q)\vec{1}
.

Let
H_{N}
be the
2(2n-1)\times 2(2n-1)
matrix whose
(i,j)
-th entry is

\begin{cases}\mathrm{Lap}^{\prime}_{\Cay(\mathbb{Z}_{2n},\{\pm 1\})}(i,j)&\text{if $1\leq i,j\leq 2n-1$,}\\
\mathrm{Lap}^{\prime\prime}_{\Cay(\mathbb{Z}_{2n},\{\pm 1\})}(i,j)&\text{if $2n\leq i,j\leq 2(2n-1)$,}\\
0&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Then we have
H_{2n}\vec{h}=(p+q)\vec{1}
.

Our goal is to obtain an exact formula for the average hitting times of a random walk on
\Cay(\mathbb{Z}_{2n},\{\pm 1\})
, so we need to solve the above matrix equation.

3.1.
Main theorem 1

For this problem, it suffices to solve the matrix equation
H_{2n}\vec{h}=(p+q)\vec{1}
.
First, we decompose the coefficient matrix
H_{2n}
.

Let
p+q=1
, and let
U_{2n}
be the upper triangular matrix whose
(i,j)
-th entry is
1
if
j\geq i
, and define
R_{2n}
as

R_{2n}(i,j):=\begin{cases}2&\text{if $i$ is odd,\;$1\leq i=j<2n$,}\\
3-2p&\text{if $i$ is even,\;$1\leq i=j<2n$,}\\
2p&\text{if $i$ is odd,\;$2n\leq i=j\leq 2(2n-1)$,}\\
2-p&\text{if $i\neq j$,\;$1\leq i,j<2n$,}\\
p&\text{if $i\neq j$,\;$2n<i\leq 2(2n-1)$,}\\
&\text{ $i\neq j$,\;$2n<j\leq 2(2n-1)$,}\\
1&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Then its inverse
R_{2n}^{-1}
is as follows:

R_{2n}^{-1}(i,j)=\frac{1}{n}\begin{cases}\frac{n-(1-p)}{p}&\text{if $i$ is odd,\;$i=j$,}\\
\frac{n-p}{1-p}&\text{if $i$ is even,\;$i=j\neq 2n$,}\\
\frac{2(n-p)}{1-p}&\text{if $i=j=2n$,}\\
-\frac{1-p}{p}&\text{if $i,j$ are odd,\;$i<2n<j$,}\\
&\text{ $i,j$ are odd,\;$j<2n<i$,}\\
-\frac{p}{1-p}&\text{if $i,j$ are even,\;$i\neq j$,\;$i\leq 2n\leq j$,}\\
&\text{ $i,j$ are even,\;$i\neq j$,\;$j\leq 2n\leq i$,}\\
-1&\text{if $i\not\equiv j\pmod{2}$,\;$i\leq 2n\leq j$,}\\
&\text{ $i\not\equiv j\pmod{2}$,\;$j\leq 2n\leq i$,}\\
0&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Then we have the following:

Theorem 3.1
.

H_{2n}=U^{-1}_{2n}\,R_{2n}\,{}^{t}U^{-1}_{2n}
.

Proof.

Let
S_{2n}
denote the matrix
U_{2n}^{-1}R_{2n}{}^{t}U_{2n}^{-1}
.
By simple matrix-computation, the entries of
S_{2n}
can be calculated as follows.

\displaystyle S_{2n}(i,j)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}2-(2-p)-(2-p)+3-2p&\text{if $1\leq i=j<2n-1$,\;$i$ is odd,}\\
2-1-1+1&\text{if $i=j=2n-1$}\\
2p-p-p+1&\text{if $2n-1<i=j<2(2n-1)$,\;$i$ is odd,}\\
3-2p-(2-p)-(2-p)+2&\text{if $1\leq i=j<2n$,\;$i$ is even,}\\
1-p-p+2p&\text{if $2n\leq i=j<2(2n-1)$,\;$i$ is even,}\\
1&\text{if $i=j=2(2n-1)$,}\\
2-p-(2-p)-(3-2p)+2-p&\text{if $i=j-1,\;1\leq i<2n-2$,\;$i$ is odd,}\\
2-p-(2-p)-2+2-p&\text{if $i=j-1,\;1\leq i<2n-2$,\;$i$ is even,}\\
2-p-1-2+1&\text{if $(i,j)=(2n-2,2n-1)$,}\\
1-p-1+p&\text{if $(i,j)=(2n-1,2n)$,}\\
p-p-1+p&\text{if $i=j-1,\;2n-1<i<4n-3$,\;$i$ is odd,}\\
p-p-2p+p&\text{if $i=j-1,\;2n-1<i\leq 2(2n-1)$,\;$i$ is even,}\\
p-1&\text{if $(i,j)=(4n-3,4n-2)$,}\\
2-p-2-(2-p)+2-p&\text{if $i=j+1,\;1\leq i<2n-1$,\;$i$ is odd,}\\
2-p-(3-2p)-(2-p)+2-p&\text{if $i=j+1,\;1\leq i<2n-1$,\;$i$ is even,}\\
2-p-2-1+1&\text{if $(i,j)=(2n-1,2n-2)$,}\\
1-1-p+p&\text{if $(i,j)=(2n,2n-1)$,}\\
p-2p-p+p&\text{if $i=j+1,2n<i\leq 2(2n-1)$,\;$i$ is odd,}\\
p-1-p+p&\text{if $i=j+1,2n<i<2(2n-1)$,\;$i$ is even,}\\
p-1&\text{if $(i,j)=(2(2n-1),4n-3)$,}\\
2-p-(2-p)-(2-p)+(2-p)&\text{if $|i-j|>1,\;1\leq i,j<2n-1$,}\\
2-p-(2-p)-1+1&\text{if $i-j>1,\;i=2n-1$,}\\
2-p-1-(2-p)+1&\text{if $j-i>1,\;j=2n-1$,}\\
1-p-1+p&\text{if $j-i>1,\;,j=2n$,}\\
1-1-p+p&\text{if $i-j>1,\;i=2n$,}\\
p-p-p+p&\text{if $|i-j|>1,\;2n<j<2(2n-1)$,}\\
&\text{$|i-j|>1,\;2n<i<2(2n-1)$,}\\
p-p&\text{if $|i-j|>1,\;i=2(2n-1)$,}\\
&\text{$|i-j|>1,\;j=2(2n-1)$,}\end{cases}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}1&\text{if $i=j$,}\\
-p&\text{if $i=j+1$,\;$i$ is odd,}\\
&\text{$i=j-1$,\;$i$ is even,}\\
-1+p&\text{if $i=j+1,i\neq 2n$,\;$i$ is even,}\\
&\text{$i=j-1,\;i\neq 2n-1$,\;$i$ is odd,}\\
0&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Therefore, we have
S_{2n}=H_{2n}
.
∎

Defining a new variable vector
\vec{h^{\prime}}={}^{t}U_{2n}^{-1}\vec{h}
, the matrix equation
H_{2n}\vec{h}=\vec{1}
can be expressed by a single matrix equation as

\vec{h^{\prime}}=\begin{bmatrix}h^{\prime}_{1}\\
h^{\prime}_{2}\\
\vdots\\
h^{\prime}_{2n-1}\\
h^{\prime}_{2n}\\
h^{\prime}_{2n+1}\\
\vdots\\
h^{\prime}_{2(2n-1)-1}\\
h^{\prime}_{2(2n-1)}\\
\end{bmatrix}:=\begin{bmatrix}h_{2n}(1,2n)\\
h_{2n}(0,2n-2)-h_{2n}(1,2n)\\
\vdots\\
h_{2n}(1,2)-h_{2n}(0,2)\\
h_{2n}(0,2n-1)-h_{2n}(1,2)\\
h_{2n}(1,2n-1)-h_{2n}(0,2n-1)\\
\vdots\\
h_{2n}(1,3)-h_{2n}(0,3)\\
h_{2n}(0,1)-h_{2n}(1,3)\\
\end{bmatrix}=R_{2n}^{-1}\begin{bmatrix}2(2n-1)\\
2(2n-1)-1\\
\vdots\\
2n\\
2n-1\\
2n-2\\
\vdots\\
2\\
1\\
\end{bmatrix}.

Solving this matrix equation, we obtain the following lemma.

Lemma 3.1
.

For
1\leq\ell\leq 2(2n-1)
,

h^{\prime}_{\ell}=\begin{cases}\frac{n-\ell+p}{p}&\text{if $1\leq\ell\leq 2n-1$,\;$\ell$ is odd,}\\
\frac{n-\ell+p}{1-p}&\text{if $1\leq\ell\leq 2n-1$,\;$\ell$ is even,}\\
0&\text{if $\ell=2n$,}\\
\frac{3n-\ell-p}{p}&\text{if $2n+1\leq\ell\leq 2(2n-1)$,\;$\ell$ is odd,}\\
\frac{3n-\ell-p}{1-p}&\text{if $2n+1\leq\ell\leq 2(2n-1)$,\;$\ell$ is even.}\end{cases}

Proof.

For the case that
1\leq\ell\leq 2n-1
(
\ell
is odd), we have

\displaystyle h^{\prime}_{\ell}

\displaystyle=R_{2n}^{-1}{}^{t}\begin{bmatrix}2(2n-1),4n-3,\cdots,1\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{2n-1}R_{2n}^{-1}(\ell,k)(4n-k-2)

\displaystyle=\frac{n-(1-p)}{np}(4n-\ell-1)-\frac{1-p}{np}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq k\leq n\\
k\neq\frac{\ell+1}{2}\end{subarray}}2(2n-k)-\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}(2(2n-k)-1)

\displaystyle=\frac{4n-\ell-1}{p}-\frac{1-p}{np}\sum_{k=1}^{n}2(2n-k)-\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}(2(2n-k)-1)

\displaystyle=\frac{4n-\ell-1}{p}-\frac{1-p}{np}(4n^{2}-n(n+1))-\frac{1}{n}(n(4n-1)-n(n+1))

\displaystyle=\frac{4n-\ell-1}{p}-\frac{(3n-1)(1-p)}{p}-(3n-2)

\displaystyle=\frac{n-\ell+p}{p}.

For the case that
1\leq\ell\leq 2n-1
(
\ell
is even), we have

\displaystyle h^{\prime}_{\ell}

\displaystyle=R_{2n}^{-1}{}^{t}\begin{bmatrix}2(2n-1),4n-3,\cdots,1\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{2n-1}R_{2n}^{-1}(\ell,k)(4n-k-2)

\displaystyle=\frac{n-p}{n(1-p)}(4n-\ell-1)-\frac{p}{n(1-p)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq k\leq n\\
k\neq\frac{\ell}{2}\end{subarray}}(2(2n-k)-1)-\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}2(2n-k)

\displaystyle=\frac{4n-\ell-1}{1-p}-\frac{p}{n(1-p)}\sum_{k=1}^{n}(2(2n-k)-1)-\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}2(2n-k)

\displaystyle=\frac{4n-\ell-1}{1-p}-\frac{p}{n(1-p)}(n(4n-1)-n(n+1))-\frac{1}{n}(4n^{2}-n(n+1))

\displaystyle=\frac{4n-\ell-1}{1-p}-\frac{(3n-2)p}{1-p}-(3n-1)

\displaystyle=\frac{n-\ell+p}{1-p}

In the case of
\ell=2n
, we have

\displaystyle h^{\prime}_{\ell}

\displaystyle=R_{2n}^{-1}{}^{t}\begin{bmatrix}2(2n-1),4n-3,\cdots,1\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{4n-3}R_{2n}^{-1}(2n,k)(4n-k-2)

\displaystyle=\frac{2(n-p)}{n(1-p)}(2n-1)-\frac{p}{n(1-p)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq k\leq 2n-1\\
k\neq n\end{subarray}}(2(2n-k)-1)-\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{2n-1}2(2n-k)

\displaystyle=\frac{(2n-1)(2n-p)}{n(1-p)}-\frac{p}{n(1-p)}((2n-1)(4n-1)-2n(2n-1))

\displaystyle~-\frac{1}{n}(4n(2n-1)-2n(2n-1))

\displaystyle=\frac{(2n-1)(2n-p)}{n(1-p)}-\frac{(2n-1)^{2}p}{n(1-p)}-2(2n-1)

\displaystyle=0.

For the case that
2n+1\leq\ell\leq 2(2n-1)
(
\ell
is odd), we have

\displaystyle h^{\prime}_{\ell}

\displaystyle=R_{2n}^{-1}{}^{t}\begin{bmatrix}2(2n-1),4n-3,\cdots,1\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=2n-1}^{4n-3}R_{2n}^{-1}(\ell,k)(4n-k-2)

\displaystyle=\frac{n-(1-p)}{np}(4n-\ell-1)-\frac{1-p}{np}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n+1\leq k\leq 2n-1\\
k\neq\frac{\ell}{2}\end{subarray}}2(2n-k)-\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=n}^{2n-1}(2(2n-k)-1)

\displaystyle=\frac{4n-\ell-1}{p}-\frac{1-p}{np}\sum_{k=n+1}^{2n-1}2(2n-k)-\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=n}^{2n-1}(2(2n-k)-1)

\displaystyle=\frac{4n-\ell-1}{p}-\frac{1-p}{np}\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}2(n-k)-\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}(2(n-k)-1)

\displaystyle=\frac{4n-\ell-1}{p}-\frac{1-p}{np}(2n(n-1)-n(n-1))-\frac{1}{n}(n(2n-1)-n(n-1))

\displaystyle=\frac{4n-\ell-1}{p}-\frac{(n-1)(1-p)}{p}-n

\displaystyle=\frac{3n-\ell-p}{p}.

For the case that
2n+1\leq\ell\leq 2(2n-1)
(
\ell
is even), we have

\displaystyle h^{\prime}_{\ell}

\displaystyle=R_{2n}^{-1}{}^{t}\begin{bmatrix}2(2n-1),4n-3,\cdots,1\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=2n-1}^{4n-3}R_{2n}^{-1}(\ell,k)(4n-k-2)

\displaystyle=\frac{3n-\ell-p}{1-p}.

4.
Weighted Cayley graph
\Cay(\mathbb{Z}_{N},\{+1,+2\})

We define a weight
w((v_{i},v_{j}))
on the weighted Cayley graph
\Cay(\mathbb{Z}_{N},\{+1,+2\})
as follows:

w((v_{i},v_{j}))=\begin{cases}p&\text{if $j-i=1$,}\\
q&\text{if $j-i=2$,}\\
0&\text{otherwise,}\end{cases}\hskip 9.24994pt\text{($v_{i},v_{j}\in\mathbb{Z}_{N},i,j\in\mathbb{Z}$),}

where
v_{i}=i+N\mathbb{Z}\in\mathbb{Z}_{N}
.

The average hitting time from a vertex
v_{k}
to a vertex
v_{\ell}
on the weighted directed Cayley graph
\Cay(\mathbb{Z}_{N},\{+1,+2\})
is denoted by
h_{N}(k,\ell)
.
Let
\vec{h}
be the column vector whose
i
-th entry is
h_{N}(0,i)
, and
let
\vec{1}
be a column vector of proper dimensions whose entries are all
1
.

On the weighted directed Cayley graph
\Cay(\mathbb{Z}_{N},\{+1,+2\})
, a walker can start a random walk from vertex
0
without loss of generality.
By the definition of a random walk, in a random walk on
\Cay(\mathbb{Z}_{N},\{+1,+2\})
, a walker moves with probability
p/(p+q)
or
q/(p+q)
to an arbitrary vertex adjacent to the vertex where the walker is.
Moreover, by the symmetry of
\Cay(\mathbb{Z}_{N},\{+1,+2\})
, we have

h_{N}(0,\ell)=h_{N}(k,k+\ell)=h_{N}(k+N-\ell,k)\hskip 9.24994pt\text{for all $k,\ell\in\mathbb{Z}_{N}$.}

Note that
h_{N}(0,0)=0
.
By the above properties, we have that
\forall\ell\in\mathbb{Z}_{N}\setminus\{0\}
,

h_{N}(0,\ell)=\frac{p}{p+q}(1+h_{N}(1,\ell))+\frac{q}{p+q}(1+h_{N}(2,\ell)),

and hence
\forall\ell\in\mathbb{Z}_{N}\setminus\{0\}
,

(p+q)h_{N}(0,\ell)-ph_{N}(0,\ell-1)-qh_{N}(0,\ell-2)=p+q.

We thus have
{}^{t}\mathrm{Lap}_{\Cay(\mathbb{Z}_{N},\{+1,+2\})}^{\prime\prime}\vec{h}=(p+q)\vec{1}
.
Let
H_{N}
be the
(N-1)\times(N-1)
matrix whose
(i,j)
-th entry is
{}^{t}\mathrm{Lap}_{\Cay(\mathbb{Z}_{N},\{+1,+2\})}^{\prime\prime}(i,j)
.
Then we have
H_{N}\vec{h}=(p+q)\vec{1}
.

Our goal is to obtain an exact formula for the average hitting times of random walks on
\Cay(\mathbb{Z}_{N},\{+1,+2\})

, we have

\displaystyle S_{N}(i,j)

\displaystyle=(-1+p)\frac{(p-1)^{i-1}-p+1}{p-2}-p\frac{(p-1)^{i}-p+1}{p-2}+\frac{(p-1)^{i+1}-p+1}{p-2}

\displaystyle=\frac{(p-1)^{i}-(p-1)^{2}-p(p-1)^{i}+p(p-1)+(p-1)^{i+1}-(p-1)}{p-2}

\displaystyle=\frac{-(p-1)^{i+1}-(p-1)^{2}+(p-1)^{2}+(p-1)^{i+1}}{p-2}

\displaystyle=0.

For the case where
i=j=N-1
, we have

\displaystyle S_{N}(i,j)

\displaystyle=(-1+p)\frac{(p-1)^{N-2}-p+1}{p-2}-p\frac{(p-1)^{N-1}-p+1}{p-2}+\frac{(p-1)^{N}-1}{p-2}

\displaystyle=\frac{(p-1)^{N-1}-(p-1)^{2}-p(p-1)^{N-1}+p(p-1)+(p-1)^{N}-1}{p-2}

\displaystyle=\frac{-(p-1)^{N}-(p^{2}-2p+1)+p^{2}-p+(p-1)^{N}-1}{p-2}

\displaystyle=1.

Therefore, we have
S_{N}=H_{N}
.
∎

From Theorem
4.1
, the matrix equation
H_{N}\vec{h}=\vec{1}
can be expressed by the single matrix equation

\vec{h}=\begin{bmatrix}h_{N}(0,1)\\
h_{N}(0,2)\\
\vdots\\
h_{N}(0,\ell)\\
\vdots\\
h_{N}(0,N-1)\end{bmatrix}=U_{N}^{-1}L_{N}^{-1}\begin{bmatrix}1\\
1\\
\vdots\\
1\\
\vdots\\
1\end{bmatrix}.

Solving this matrix equation, we obtain an exact formula for
h_{N}(0,\ell)
.

Theorem 4.2
.

For
1\leq\ell\leq N-1
, an exact formula for the average hitting times of random walks on the weighted Cayley graph
\Cay(\mathbb{Z}_{N},\{+1,+2\})
is

h_{N}(0,\ell)=\frac{N(p-1)((p-1)^{\ell}-1)-\ell((p-1)^{N}-1)}{(p-2)((p-1)^{N}-1)}.

Proof.

For the case where
\ell=1
, we have

\displaystyle h_{N}(0,1)

\displaystyle=1+\frac{(p-1)^{2}-p+1}{1-(p-1)^{N}}\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}\frac{(p-1)^{i}-1}{p-2}

\displaystyle=1+\frac{(p-1)^{2}-p+1}{(p-2)(1-(p-1)^{N})}(\frac{(p-1)(1-(p-1)^{N-1})}{1-(p-1)}-N+1)

\displaystyle=1-\frac{p-1}{(p-1)^{N}-1}(\frac{(p-1)^{N}-1}{p-2}-N)

\displaystyle=\frac{(p-2)((p-1)^{N}-1)-(p-1)((p-1)^{N}-1)+N(p-1)(p-2)}{(p-2)((p-1)^{N}-1)}

\displaystyle=\frac{N(p-1)((p-1)^{1}-1)-((p-1)^{N}-1)}{(p-2)((p-1)^{N}-1)}.

For the case where
\ell=2
, we have

\displaystyle h_{N}(0,2)

\displaystyle=1+p+\frac{(p-1)^{3}-p+1}{1-(p-1)^{N}}\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}\frac{(p-1)^{i}-1}{p-2}

\displaystyle=1+p+\frac{(p-1)^{3}-p+1}{(p-2)(1-(p-1)^{N})}(\frac{(p-1)(1-(p-1)^{N-1})}{1-(p-1)}-N+1)

\displaystyle=1+p-\frac{(p-1)((p-1)^{2}-1)}{(p-2)((p-1)^{N}-1)}(\frac{(p-1)^{N}-1}{p-2}-N)

\displaystyle=\frac{(p+1)(p-2)^{2}}{(p-2)^{2}}-\frac{(p-1)((p-1)^{2}-1)}{(p-2)^{2}}+\frac{N(p-1)((p-1)^{2}-1)}{(p-2)((p-1)^{N}-1)}

\displaystyle=\frac{(p+1)(p-2)^{2}}{(p-2)^{2}}-\frac{p(p-1)(p-2)}{(p-2)^{2}}+\frac{N(p-1)((p-1)^{2}-1)}{(p-2)((p-1)^{N}-1)}

\displaystyle=\frac{(p+1)(p-2)-p(p-1)}{(p-2)}+\frac{N(p-1)((p-1)^{2}-1)}{(p-2)((p-1)^{N}-1)}

\displaystyle=\frac{N(p-1)((p-1)^{2}-1)-2((p-1)^{N}-1)}{(p-2)((p-1)^{N}-1)}.

For the case where
3\leq\ell\leq N-2
, we have

\displaystyle h_{N}(0,\ell)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\frac{(p-1)^{i}-1}{p-2}+\frac{(p-1)^{\ell+1}-p+1}{1-(p-1)^{N}}\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}\frac{(p-1)^{i}-1}{p-2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{p-2}(\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}(p-1)^{i}-l)+\frac{(p-1)^{\ell+1}-p+1}{(p-2)(1-(p-1)^{N})}(\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}(p-1)^{i}-N+1)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{p-2}(\frac{(p-1)(1-(p-1)^{\ell})}{1-(p-1)}-\ell

\displaystyle~+\frac{(p-1)^{\ell+1}-p+1}{1-(p-1)^{N}}(\frac{(p-1)(1-(p-1)^{N-1})}{1-(p-1)}-N+1))

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{p-2}(\frac{(p-1)^{\ell+1}-p+1}{p-2}-\ell

\displaystyle~-\frac{(p-1)^{\ell+1}-p+1}{(p-1)^{N}-1}(\frac{(p-1)^{N}-p+1}{p-2}-N+1))

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{p-2}(\frac{(p-1)^{\ell+1}-p+1}{p-2}-\ell-\frac{(p-1)^{\ell+1}-p+1}{(p-1)^{N}-1}(\frac{(p-1)^{N}-1}{p-2}-N))

\displaystyle=\frac{N(p-1)((p-1)^{\ell}-1)-\ell((p-1)^{N}-1)}{(p-2)((p-1)^{N}-1)}.

For the case where
\ell=N-1
, we have

\displaystyle h_{N}(0,N-1)

\displaystyle=\frac{p-2}{(p-1)^{N}-1}\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}\frac{(p-1)^{i}-1}{p-2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(p-1)^{N}-1}(\frac{(p-1)(1-(p-1)^{N-1})}{1-(p-1)}-N+1)

\displaystyle=\frac{-N(p-2)+(p-1)^{N}-1}{(p-2)((p-1)^{N}-1)}

\displaystyle=\frac{N((p-1)^{N}-p+1)-N((p-1)^{N}-1)+(p-1)^{N}-1}{(p-2)((p-1)^{N}-1)}

\displaystyle=\frac{N(p-1)((p-1)^{N-1}-1)-(N-1)((p-1)^{N}-1)}{(p-2)((p-1)^{N}-1)}.

∎

From Theorem
4.1
, we have
H_{N}^{-1}=U_{N}^{-1}L_{N}^{-1}P_{N}^{-1}
, and so the entries of
H^{-1}_{N}
can also be calculated as follows.
For
1\leq i,j\leq N-1
, we have

\displaystyle H_{N}^{-1}(i,j)=\frac{1}{(p-2)((p-1)^{N}-1)}

\displaystyle~\begin{cases}(p-2)^{2}&\text{if $i=j=1$,}\\
(p-2)((p-1)^{N}-p(p-1)^{N-1}+p^{2}-p-1)&\text{if $i=j=2$,}\\
-(p-2)((p-1)^{N-1}-p+1)&\text{if $(i,j)=(1,2)$,}\\
p(p-2)^{2}&\text{if $(i,j)=(2,1)$,}\\
-(p-2)((p-1)^{N-j+1}-p+1)&\text{if $i=1,\;3\leq j\leq N-1$,}\\
-p(p-2)((p-1)^{N-j+1}-p+1)&\text{if $i=2,\;3\leq j\leq N-1$,}\\
(p-2)((p-1)^{i}-1)&\text{if $3\leq i\leq N-2,\;j=1$,}\\
(p-2)(p(p-1)^{i-1}-(p-1)^{N-1}-1)&\text{if $3\leq i\leq N-2,\;j=2$,}\\
-((p-1)^{i}-1)((p-1)^{N-j+1}-p+1)&\text{if $3\leq i<j\leq N-1$,}\\
(p-2)((p-1)^{i}-1)+((p-1)^{-j+1}-1)((p-1)^{N}-(p-1)^{i})&\text{if $3\leq j\leq i\leq N-2$,}\\
(p-2)((p-1)^{N-j}-1)&\text{if $i=N-1$.}\end{cases}

5.
Effective resistance and Kirchhoff index

The effective resistance between a pair of vertices
u,v
of a weighted graph
G=(V,E)
is the electrical resistance seen between the vertices
u
and
v
of a resistor network with branch conductances given by the edge weights, denoted by
r(G;u,v)
.

In 1996, Chandra et al.
[
1
]
proved the following formula between the effective resistances and the average hitting times.

Theorem 5.1

(Chandra et al., 1996
[
1
]
)
.

r(G;u,v)=\frac{h(G;u,v)+h(G;v,u)}{\sum_{(x,y)\in V\times V}w((x,y))}.

Let
G=(V,E)
be a weighted graph with
V=\{v_{1},v_{2},\ldots,v_{n}\}
.
The Kirchhoff index
\Kf(G)
of the graph
G

is defined as
\Kf(G):=\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}r(G;v_{i},v_{j})
.

We can obtain the following theorems.

Theorem 5.2
.

We defined a weight
w(\{v_{i},v_{j}\})
on the weighted undirected Cayley graph
\Cay(\mathbb{Z}_{2n},\{\pm 1\})
as follows:

w(\{v_{i},v_{j}\})=\begin{cases}p&\text{if $j-i=1$, $i$ is even,}\\
q&\text{if $j-i=1$, $i$ is odd,}\\
0&\text{otherwise,}\end{cases}\quad\text{($v_{i},v_{j}\in\mathbb{Z}_{2n},i,j\in\mathbb{Z}$),}

where
v_{i}=i+2n\mathbb{Z}\in\mathbb{Z}_{2n}
.

Then,

\Kf(\Cay(\mathbb{Z}_{2n},\{\pm 1\}))=\frac{n((n-1)(n+1)+3p(1-p))}{3p(1-p)}.

Proof.

\displaystyle\Kf(\Cay(\mathbb{Z}_{2n},\{\pm 1\}))

\displaystyle=\sum_{0\leq i<j\leq 2n-1}r(\Cay(\mathbb{Z}_{2n},\{\pm 1\});v_{i},v_{j})

\displaystyle=\sum_{0\leq i<j\leq 2n-1}\frac{h(\Cay(\mathbb{Z}_{2n},\{\pm 1\});i,j)+h(\Cay(\mathbb{Z}_{2n},\{\pm 1\});j,i)}{\sum_{0\leq k,\ell\leq 2n-1}w((v_{k},v_{\ell}))}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2n}\sum_{0\leq i\leq 2n-1}\sum_{0\leq j\leq 2n-1}h(\Cay(\mathbb{Z}_{2n},\{\pm 1\});i,j)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(\sum_{\ell=1}^{2n-1}h(\Cay(\mathbb{Z}_{2n},\{\pm 1\});0,\ell)+\sum_{\ell=1}^{2n-1}h(\Cay(\mathbb{Z}_{2n},\{\pm 1\});1,\ell+1)\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}(\sum_{\ell=1}^{n-1}h(\Cay(\mathbb{Z}_{2n},\{\pm 1\});0,2\ell)+\sum_{\ell=0}^{n-1}h(\Cay(\mathbb{Z}_{2n},\{\pm 1\});0,2\ell+1)

\displaystyle~+\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}h(\Cay(\mathbb{Z}_{2n},\{\pm 1\});1,2\ell)+\sum_{\ell=1}^{n-1}h(\Cay(\mathbb{Z}_{2n},\{\pm 1\});1,2\ell+1))

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{8p(1-p)}(\sum_{\ell=1}^{n-1}2\ell(2n-2\ell)

\displaystyle~+\sum_{\ell=0}^{n-1}(2\ell(2n-(2\ell+1)+1)+4(1-p)(n-(2\ell+1)+p))

\displaystyle~+\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}((2\ell-2)(2n-(2\ell-1)+1)+4p(n-(2\ell-1)+1-p))

\displaystyle~+\sum_{\ell=1}^{n-1}2\ell(2n-2\ell))

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2p(1-p)}(\sum_{\ell=1}^{n-1}\ell(n-\ell)+\sum_{\ell=1}^{n-1}\ell(n-\ell)

\displaystyle~+\sum_{\ell=0}^{n-1}(1-p)(n-2\ell-1+p)+\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}(\ell-1)(n-\ell+1)

\displaystyle~+\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}p(n-2\ell+2-p)+\sum_{\ell=1}^{n-1}\ell(n-\ell))

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2p(1-p)}(4\sum_{\ell=1}^{n-1}\ell(n-\ell)-\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}(1-p)(n-2\ell+1)

\displaystyle~+\sum_{\ell=0}^{n-1}(1-p)(n-2\ell-1+p)+\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}p(1-p))

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2p(1-p)}(4\sum_{\ell=1}^{n-1}\ell(n-\ell)

\displaystyle~+(1-p)(\sum_{\ell=0}^{n-1}(n-2\ell)-\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}(1-p)-\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}(n-2\ell)-\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}1+\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}p))

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2p(1-p)}\left(4\sum_{\ell=1}^{n-1}\ell(n-\ell)+2(1-p)(n-\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}(1-p))\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{p(1-p)}\left(n^{2}(n-1)-\frac{n(n-1)(2n-1)}{3}+(1-p)(n-n(1-p))\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{n((n-1)(n+1)+3p(1-p))}{3p(1-p)}.

∎

Theorem 5.3
.

We define a weight
w((v_{i},v_{j}))
on the weighted Cayley graph
\Cay(\mathbb{Z}_{N},\{+1,+2\})
as follows:

w((v_{i},v_{j}))=\begin{cases}p&\text{if $j-i=1$,}\\
q&\text{if $j-i=2$,}\\
0&\text{otherwise,}\end{cases}\quad\text{($v_{i},v_{j}\in\mathbb{Z}_{N},i,j\in\mathbb{Z}$),}

where
v_{i}=i+N\mathbb{Z}\in\mathbb{Z}_{N}
.

Then,

\displaystyle\Kf(\Cay(\mathbb{Z}_{N},\{+1,+2\}))=

\displaystyle~\frac{N(8+((p-1)^{N}+2p-9)p-N(p-2)((p-1)^{N}+2p-3))}{2(p-2)^{2}((p-1)^{N}-1)}.

Proof.

\displaystyle\Kf(\Cay(\mathbb{Z}_{N},\{+1,+2\}))

\displaystyle=\sum_{0\leq i<j\leq N-1}r(\Cay(\mathbb{Z}_{N},\{+1,+2\});v_{i},v_{j})

\displaystyle=\sum_{0\leq i<j\leq N-1}\frac{h(\Cay(\mathbb{Z}_{N},\{+1,+2\});i,j)+h(\Cay(\mathbb{Z}_{N},\{+1,+2\});j,i)}{\sum_{0\leq k,\ell\leq N-1}w((v_{k},v_{\ell}))}

\displaystyle=\sum_{0\leq i<j\leq N-1}\frac{h(\Cay(\mathbb{Z}_{N},\{+1,+2\});i,j)+h(\Cay(\mathbb{Z}_{N},\{+1,+2\});j,i)}{N}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}(\sum_{\ell=1}^{N-1}(N-\ell)h(\Cay(\mathbb{Z}_{N},\{+1,+2\});0,\ell)

\displaystyle~+\sum_{\ell=1}^{N-1}\ell h(\Cay(\mathbb{Z}_{N},\{+1,+2\});0,\ell))

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=1}^{N-1}h(\Cay(\mathbb{Z}_{N},\{+1,+2\});0,\ell)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=1}^{N-1}\frac{N(p-1)((p-1)^{\ell}-1)-\ell((p-1)^{N}-1)}{(p-2)((p-1)^{N}-1)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(p-2)((p-1)^{N}-1)}\times

\displaystyle~\left(N(p-1)(\sum_{\ell=1}^{N-1}(p-1)^{\ell}-\sum_{\ell=1}^{N-1}1)-((p-1)^{N}-1)\sum_{\ell=1}^{N-1}\ell\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(p-2)((p-1)^{N}-1)}\times

\displaystyle~\left(N(p-1)(\frac{1-(p-1)^{N-1}}{1-(p-1)}-N+1)-((p-1)^{N}-1)\frac{N(N-1)}{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{N(8+((p-1)^{N}+2p-9)p-N(p-2)((p-1)^{N}+2p-3))}{2(p-2)^{2}((p-1)^{N}-1)}.

∎

On the coadjoint Virasoro action
Anton Alekseev, Eckhard Meinrenken

Abstract
. The set of coadjoint orbits of the Virasoro algebra at level 1 is in bijection with the set of conjugacy classes in a certain open subset SL ~ ​ ( 2 , ℝ ) + \widetilde{\operatorname{SL}}(2,\mathbb{R})_{+} of the universal cover of SL ⁡ ( 2 , ℝ ) \operatorname{SL}(2,\mathbb{R}) . We strengthen this bijection to a Morita equivalence of quasi-symplectic groupoids, integrating the Poisson structure on 𝔳 ​ 𝔦 ​ 𝔯 𝟣 ∗ ​ ( S 1 ) \mathfrak{vir}^{*}_{\mathsf{1}}(S^{1}) and the Cartan-Dirac structure on SL ~ ​ ( 2 , ℝ ) + \widetilde{\operatorname{SL}}(2,\mathbb{R})_{+} , respectively.



1.
Introduction

Let
\mathsf{C}
be an unparametrized circle: a compact, connected, oriented 1-manifold. The Virasoro Lie algebra
\mathfrak{vir}(\mathsf{C})
is the canonical central extension of the Lie algebra
\operatorname{Vect}(\mathsf{C})
of vector fields. Its smooth dual at level
1
, denoted
\mathfrak{vir}^{*}_{1}(\mathsf{C})
, has a geometric interpretation as the space of projective structures on
\mathsf{C}
, and also as a space of Hill operators on
\mathsf{C}
.
The group
\operatorname{Diff}_{+}(\mathsf{C})
of orientation preserving diffeomorphisms acts on
\mathfrak{vir}^{*}_{1}(\mathsf{C})
by affine-linear transformations;
we shall refer to this action as the
coadjoint Virasoro action
.

A classification of the coadjoint Virasoro orbits was achieved in the work of Kirillov
[
17
]
, Lazutkin-Pankratova
[
19
]
, Goldman
[
13
]
, Segal
[
27
]
, and Witten
[
31
]
. In the approach of
[
13
,
27
]
, this classification is described in terms of a diagram

(1)

\textstyle{\mathcal{D}(\mathsf{C})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathfrak{vir}^{*}_{1}(\mathsf{C})}
\textstyle{\widetilde{\operatorname{SL}}(2,\mathbb{R})_{+}}

Here
\mathcal{D}(\mathsf{C})
is the space of
developing maps
for projective structures on
\mathsf{C}
. A developing map is an orientation-preserving immersion

\gamma\colon\widetilde{\mathsf{C}}\to\mathbb{R}\!\operatorname{P}(1)

of the simply connected cover of
\mathsf{C}
, such that
\gamma
is
quasi-periodic
, with monodromy given by the action of an element of
\operatorname{PSL}(2,\mathbb{R})
. The right arrow takes
\gamma
to its
lifted
monodromy in the universal cover, and has as its image a certain open subset indicated by a subscript ‘
+
’. The left arrow takes
\gamma
to the corresponding projective structure. The space
\mathcal{D}(\mathsf{C})
has natural commuting actions of
\operatorname{PSL}(2,\mathbb{R})
and of the universal cover of
\operatorname{Diff}_{+}(\mathsf{C})
, and the two arrows are the quotient maps for these two actions. Consequently, the diagram sets up a bijection between the coadjoint orbits in
\mathfrak{vir}^{*}_{1}(\mathsf{C})
and conjugacy classes in
\widetilde{\operatorname{SL}}(2,\mathbb{R})_{+}
.

In this article, we show that this correspondence of group actions extends canonically to a correspondence between the Poisson structure on
\mathfrak{vir}^{*}_{1}(\mathsf{C})
and the Cartan-Dirac structure on
\widetilde{\operatorname{SL}}(2,\mathbb{R})_{+}
. More precisely, we construct a Morita equivalence, in the sense of Xu
[
32
]
:

(2)

\textstyle{(\mathcal{G}_{1},\omega_{1})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{(\mathcal{D}(\mathsf{C}),\varpi_{\mathcal{D}})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{(\mathcal{G}_{2},\omega_{2})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathfrak{vir}^{*}_{1}(\mathsf{C})}
\textstyle{\widetilde{\operatorname{SL}}(2,\mathbb{R})_{+}}

In this diagram,
(\mathcal{G}_{1},\omega_{1})
is a symplectic groupoid integrating the Poisson structure on
\mathfrak{vir}^{*}_{1}(\mathsf{C})
, and

(\mathcal{G}_{2},\omega_{2})
is a quasi-symplectic groupoid integrating the Cartan-Dirac structure on
\widetilde{\operatorname{SL}}(2,\mathbb{R})_{+}
.
The groupoid
\mathcal{G}_{2}
is the action groupoid for the conjugation action of
\operatorname{PSL}(2,\mathbb{R})
. On the other hand,
\mathcal{G}_{1}
is
not
simply an action
groupoid – rather, it is obtained as a
quotient
of an action groupoid. The Morita equivalence between these quasi-symplectic groupoids has the space of developing maps as its Hilsum-Skandalis bimodule, and comes with a distinguished bi-invariant
2-form

\varpi_{\mathcal{D}}\in\Omega^{2}(\mathcal{D}(\mathsf{C})).

For
\mathsf{C}=S^{1}
, writing quasi-periodic paths as
\gamma=(\sin\phi:\cos\phi)

with
\phi\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
, this 2-form is given by an expression

(3)

\varpi_{\mathcal{D}}=\int_{0}^{1}{\mathsf{d}}\Big((\phi^{\prime})^{2}+{\frac{1}{2}}\mathcal{S}(\phi)\Big)\wedge\ \frac{{\mathsf{d}}\phi}{\phi^{\prime}}+\mbox{boundary terms}

involving the Schwarzian derivative
\mathcal{S}(\phi)
; the boundary terms are needed to make the expression invariant under diffeomorphisms.

The Morita equivalence (
2
) of quasi-symplectic groupoids gives rise to a 1-1 correspondence of the associated
Hamiltonian spaces. It hence allows us to associate (certain) Hamiltonian Virasoro spaces to finite-dimensional
\operatorname{PSL}(2,\mathbb{R})
-spaces
with
\widetilde{\operatorname{SL}}(2,\mathbb{R})_{+}
-valued moment maps and vice versa. One such example, motivated by the recent physics literature on Jackiw-Teitelboim gravity (e.g.,
[
26
,
29
]
), is the Teichmüller moduli space of conformally compact hyperbolic metrics on surfaces with boundary. Details of these and similar examples will be discussed in forthcoming work.

The Morita equivalence for the Virasoro Lie algebra is related to a similar
Morita equivalence for loop groups, via Drinfeld-Sokolov reduction. For any connected Lie group
G
with an invariant metric on its Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
, the
loop algebra
L\mathfrak{g}
has a central extension
\widehat{L\mathfrak{g}}
. Taking
G=\operatorname{PSL}(2,\mathbb{R})
, the Drinfeld-Sokolov procedure
[
10
]
realizes
\mathfrak{vir}^{*}_{1}(S^{1})
as a Marsden-Weinstein reduced space of
\widehat{L\mathfrak{g}}^{*}_{1}
; we shall work with the coordinate-free description of this method, as described by Segal
[
28
]
. An analogue of the 2-form
\varpi_{\mathcal{D}}
for the loop group setting (for more general
G
) was given in our earlier work
[
1
,
2
]
, and used to establish a 1-1 correspondence between Hamiltonian loop group spaces and finite-dimensional q-Hamiltonian spaces. See
[
32
]
for its interpretation as a Morita equivalence.

Throughout this article, we will treat infinite-dimensional manifolds as Fréchet manifolds
[
14
]
, but without entering any technical discussion. Recent references giving a detailed treatment of the relevant techniques in a related context are
[
9
,
25
]
.
Alternatively, one could work with diffeologies
[
15
]
. Recall that diffeology is particularly well-suited for dealing with differential forms, which actually suggests diffeological symplectic and quasi-symplectic groupoids as convenient stand-ins for infinite-dimensional Poisson and Dirac manifolds.

The organization of the paper is as follows. Section
2
discusses Hill operators and the Virasoro algebra. The approach will be coordinate-free; the coordinate expressions are spelled out towards the end of the section. Section
3
studies the geometry of the space of developing maps. In particular, we determine the stabilizers for the actions of diffeomorphisms; this leads us to the construction of the groupoid
\mathcal{G}_{1}
. Section
4
describes the 2-form
\varpi_{\mathcal{D}}

on the space of developing maps, giving the Morita equivalence of quasi-symplectic groupoids and resulting in a correspondence of their Hamiltonian spaces. Section
5
gives the construction of
\varpi_{\mathcal{D}}
by Drinfeld-Sokolov reduction, which accounts for its basic properties. The appendix gives a general review of Morita equivalence of quasi-symplectic Lie groupoids, as defined by Xu
[
32
]
.

2.
Virasoro Lie algebra

We begin by reviewing some background on the Virasoro Lie algebra, and its relationship with Hill operators. We shall follow the coordinate free approach from
[
11
,
27
]
; further information may be found in
[
16
,

]
.

2.1.
Hill operators

Let
\mathsf{C}
be a 1-dimensional oriented manifold. For
r\in\mathbb{R}
, we denote by

|\Lambda|^{r}_{\mathsf{C}}\to\mathsf{C}
the
r
-density bundle on
\mathsf{C}
; its space of sections is denoted by

|\Omega|^{r}_{\mathsf{C}}=\Gamma(|\Lambda|^{r}_{\mathsf{C}}).

The orientation on
\mathsf{C}
identifies
|\Lambda|^{r}_{\mathsf{C}}
with the cotangent bundle for
r=1
, and with the
tangent bundle for
r=-1
.
The principal symbol of a
k
-th order differential operator

D\colon|\Omega|^{r_{1}}_{\mathsf{C}}\to|\Omega|^{r_{2}}_{\mathsf{C}}

is an element

\sigma_{k}(D)\in\Gamma\big(\operatorname{Sym}^{k}(T\mathsf{C})\otimes\operatorname{Hom}(|\Lambda|^{r_{1}}_{\mathsf{C}},|\Lambda|^{r_{2}}_{\mathsf{C}})\big)\cong|\Omega|^{r_{2}-r_{1}-k}_{\mathsf{C}};

the principal symbol of a composition of two such operators is the product of the principal symbols.
The formal adjoint
of
D
is the
k
-th order differential operator

D^{*}\colon|\Omega|_{\mathsf{C}}^{1-r_{2}}\to|\Omega|_{\mathsf{C}}^{1-r_{1}}

given by
\int_{\mathsf{C}}(Du)v=\int_{\mathsf{C}}u(D^{*}v)
whenever
u
has compact support; its principal symbol is

\sigma_{k}(D^{*})=(-1)^{k}\sigma_{k}(D)
.

Example 2.1
.

.

(a)

Every
v\in\operatorname{Vect}(\mathsf{C})
defines a first order differential operator
\mathcal{L}_{v}\colon|\Omega|^{r_{1}}_{\mathsf{C}}\to|\Omega|^{r_{1}}_{\mathsf{C}}
given by Lie derivative, with
\sigma_{1}(\mathcal{L}_{v})=v
. The formal adjoint is

\mathcal{L}_{v}^{*}=-\mathcal{L}_{v}
as an operator
|\Omega|^{1-r_{1}}_{\mathsf{C}}\to|\Omega|^{1-r_{1}}_{\mathsf{C}}
.

(b)

Every
u\in|\Omega|^{r}_{\mathsf{C}}
defines a
0
-th order differential operator
M_{u}\colon|\Omega|^{r_{1}}_{\mathsf{C}}\to|\Omega|^{r_{1}+r}_{\mathsf{C}}
given by multiplication, with
\sigma_{0}(M_{u})=u
. The formal adjoint is

M_{u}^{*}=M_{u}\colon|\Omega|^{1-r_{1}-r}_{\mathsf{C}}\to|\Omega|^{1-r_{1}}_{\mathsf{C}}
.

A
Hill operator
is a second order differential operator

L\colon|\Omega|^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{\mathsf{C}}\to|\Omega|^{\frac{3}{2}}_{\mathsf{C}}

with
L^{*}=L
and with principal symbol
\sigma_{2}(L)=1
. (The choices
r_{1}=-\frac{1}{2},\ r_{2}=\frac{3}{2}
are the unique ones for which these conditions make sense.) We denote by
\operatorname{Hill}(\mathsf{C})
the affine space of all Hill operators; the underlying linear space is the space
|\Omega|^{2}_{\mathsf{C}}
of
quadratic differentials.

It is often convenient to choose a connection on the bundle
|\Lambda|^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{\mathsf{C}}
, or equivalently a first order differential operator

(4)

\partial\colon|\Omega|^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{\mathsf{C}}\to|\Omega|^{\frac{1}{2}}_{\mathsf{C}}

with
\sigma_{1}(\partial)=1
. (Any two such differ by
M_{u}
for some

u\in|\Omega|^{1}_{\mathsf{C}}
.)
Then
\partial^{*}\colon|\Omega|^{\frac{1}{2}}_{\mathsf{C}}\to|\Omega|^{\frac{3}{2}}_{\mathsf{C}}
, and the
Hill operators on
\mathsf{C}
are of the
form

L=-\partial^{*}\circ\partial+\mathcal{T},

where
\mathcal{T}\in|\Omega|^{2}_{\mathsf{C}}
is a quadratic differential.
Using (
4
) we may define the
Wronskian
of two
-{\frac{1}{2}}
-densities as

(5)

W(u_{1},u_{2})=u_{1}\partial u_{2}-u_{2}\partial u_{1}\in|\Omega|^{0}_{\mathsf{C}};

this does not depend on the choice of
\partial
.

The group
\operatorname{Diff}_{+}(\mathsf{C})
of orientation preserving diffeomorphisms acts on the spaces of
r
-densities by push-forward.
This induces an affine action on
\operatorname{Hill}(\mathsf{C})
by

(6)

{\mathsf{F}}\cdot L={\mathsf{F}}_{*}\circ L\circ({\mathsf{F}}^{-1})_{*},

with underlying linear action the push-forward of
quadratic differentials. Given a Hill operator
L
and any
v\in\operatorname{Vect}(\mathsf{C})
, the Lie derivative
\mathcal{L}_{v}(L)=[\mathcal{L}_{v},L]
is a differential operator of order
0
, and hence is multiplication by a quadratic differential. Denoting the latter by
-D_{L}v
, this defines a linear map

(7)

D_{L}\colon|\Omega|^{-1}_{\mathsf{C}}\to|\Omega|^{2}_{\mathsf{C}},

satisfying
D_{L}[v_{1},v_{2}]=\mathcal{L}_{v_{1}}(D_{L}v_{2})-\mathcal{L}_{v_{2}}(D_{L}v_{1})
. By definition,

(8)

v\cdot L=-D_{L}v

describes the infinitesimal action on the space of Hill operators. From the coordinate description below, we
will see below that
D_{L}
is a third order differential operator, with

D_{L}^{*}=-D_{L},\ \ \sigma_{3}(D_{L})=-{\frac{1}{2}}.

Finally, we note that the Hill operator determines for all
x_{0}\in\mathsf{C}
, a symmetric bilinear form (‘bilinear concomitant’)
on the 2-jet bundle
J^{2}(T\mathsf{C})

B_{L}\colon J^{2}(T\mathsf{C})\times_{\mathsf{C}}J^{2}(T\mathsf{C})\to\mathbb{R}.

At
x_{0}\in\mathsf{C}
, this bilinear form is given by

(9)

B_{L,x_{0}}(j^{2}_{x_{0}}(v_{1}),\,j^{2}_{x_{0}}(v_{2}))=\int_{I_{+}}\big((D_{L}v_{1})v_{2}+v_{1}(D_{L}v_{2})\big)

where
I\subseteq\mathsf{C}
is an open interval around
x_{0}
,

I_{+}=\{x\in I\colon x\geq x_{0}\}
is its positive side with respect to the orientation of
\mathsf{C}

and
v_{1},v_{2}
are representatives of the given 2-jets with compact support in
I
. From now on, we will write

B_{L,x_{0}}(v_{1},v_{2})
in place of
B_{L,x_{0}}(j^{2}_{x_{0}}(v_{1}),\,j^{2}_{x_{0}}(v_{2}))
. If
x_{0},x_{1}\in\mathsf{C}
are the end points of a
positively oriented arc in
\mathsf{C}
, we obtain

(10)

\int_{x_{0}}^{x_{1}}\big((D_{L}v_{1})v_{2}+v_{1}(D_{L}v_{2})\big)=B_{L,x_{1}}(v_{1},v_{2})-B_{L,x_{0}}(v_{1},v_{2}).

2.2.
Virasoro Lie algebra

The construction of
\mathfrak{vir}(\mathsf{C})
(following
[
11
]
) uses the following well-known principle.
Let
K
be a Lie group, with an affine
K
-action on an affine space
E
such that the underlying linear action is the coadjoint action of
K
on
\mathfrak{k}^{*}
. Then one obtains a central extension

0\to\mathbb{R}\to\widehat{\mathfrak{k}}\to\mathfrak{k}\to 0

of the Lie algebra. As a vector space,

\widehat{\mathfrak{k}}
is the space of affine-linear maps
\widehat{\xi}\colon E\to\mathbb{R}
; the associated linear map
\xi\colon\mathfrak{k}^{*}\to\mathbb{R}
is an element of
\mathfrak{k}
. The Lie bracket is given by

[\widehat{\xi}_{1},\widehat{\xi}_{2}](\mu)=-\langle\xi_{1}\cdot\mu,\xi_{2}\rangle,

where
\xi\cdot\mu=\xi_{E}|_{\mu}\in T_{\mu}E=\mathfrak{k}^{*}
are the infinitesimal
generators corresponding to
\xi\in\mathfrak{k}
.
A choice of base point
\mu_{0}\in E
defines a splitting
\widehat{\mathfrak{k}}=\mathfrak{k}\oplus\mathbb{R}
, and the corresponding Lie algebra cocycle is

(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})\mapsto-\langle\xi_{1}\cdot\mu_{0},\xi_{2}\rangle
.

Suppose
\mathsf{C}
is compact, connected, and oriented. Using the integration pairing between
|\Omega|^{2}_{\mathsf{C}}

and
|\Omega|^{-1}_{\mathsf{C}}\cong\operatorname{Vect}(\mathsf{C})
, the action of
\operatorname{Diff}_{+}(\mathsf{C})
on quadratic differentials is indeed the coadjoint action on the smooth dual of
\operatorname{Vect}(\mathsf{C})
. As remarked above, this is the linear action underlying the affine action of
\operatorname{Diff}_{+}(\mathsf{C})
on the space of Hill operators. The resulting
central extension

(11)

0\to\mathbb{R}\to\mathfrak{vir}(\mathsf{C})\to\operatorname{Vect}(\mathsf{C})\to 0

is the
Virasoro Lie algebra
. As a vector space,
\mathfrak{vir}(\mathsf{C})
consists of affine-linear functionals
\widehat{v}\colon\operatorname{Hill}(\mathsf{C})\to\mathbb{R}
for which the underlying linear functional
|\Omega|^{2}_{\mathsf{C}}\to\mathbb{R}
is given by pairing with an element
v\in\operatorname{Vect}(\mathsf{C})
.
Using the description (
8
) of the infinitesimal action, we see that the
Lie bracket on
\mathfrak{vir}(\mathsf{C})
is given by

(12)

[\widehat{v}_{1},\widehat{v}_{2}](L)=\int_{\mathsf{C}}(D_{L}v_{1})v_{2}.

From now on, we refer to the action of
\operatorname{Diff}_{+}(\mathsf{C})
on the affine space

(13)

\mathfrak{vir}^{*}_{1}(\mathsf{C})=\operatorname{Hill}(\mathsf{C})

as the
coadjoint Virasoro action
.

2.3.
Coordinate descriptions

Choose an identification
\mathsf{C}\cong S^{1}=\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}
, and denote by
x
the local coordinate on
S^{1}
. The
r
-density bundles
|\Lambda|^{r}_{\mathsf{C}}
are canonically trivialized by
|\partial x|^{r}
. For
u=f\,|\partial x|^{r}
, with
coefficient function
f
, we write

(14)

u^{\prime}=\frac{\partial f}{\partial x}|\partial x|^{r+1},

an
(r+1)
-density.
The Hill operators on
\mathsf{C}
are of the form

(15)

Lu=u^{\prime\prime}+\mathcal{T}u,

with a
Hill potential

\mathcal{T}\in|\Omega|^{2}_{S^{1}}
. For
{\mathsf{F}}\in\operatorname{Diff}_{+}(S^{1})
, the Hill potential of

{\mathsf{F}}^{-1}\cdot L={\mathsf{F}}^{*}\circ L\circ{\mathsf{F}}_{*}
is given by

(16)

{\mathsf{F}}^{*}\mathcal{T}+{\frac{1}{2}}\mathcal{S}({\mathsf{F}})

where

\mathcal{S}\colon\operatorname{Diff}(S^{1})\to|\Omega|^{2}_{S^{1}}
is the Schwarzian derivative

(17)

\mathcal{S}({\mathsf{F}})=\frac{{\mathsf{F}}^{\prime\prime\prime}}{{\mathsf{F}}^{\prime}}-\frac{3}{2}\big(\frac{{\mathsf{F}}^{\prime\prime}}{{\mathsf{F}}^{\prime}}\big)^{2}.

Here we used (
14
) for
{\mathsf{F}}
regarded as an
\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}
-valued function; for example
{\mathsf{F}}^{\prime\prime\prime}
is a 3-density.
The infinitesimal generators of the action are

(18)

-D_{L}v=\mathcal{T}^{\prime}v+2\mathcal{T}v^{\prime}+{\frac{1}{2}}v^{\prime\prime\prime}.

One notes that
(D_{L}v_{1})v_{2}+v_{1}(D_{L}v_{2})
is the total derivative of

-\big(2\mathcal{T}v_{1}v_{2}+{\frac{1}{2}}(v_{1}^{\prime\prime}v_{2}-v_{1}^{\prime}v_{2}^{\prime}+v_{1}v_{2}^{\prime\prime})\big)
,
which verifies that
D_{L}^{*}=-D_{L}
, and gives the formula

(19)

B_{L,x_{0}}(v_{1},v_{2})=\big(2\mathcal{T}v_{1}v_{2}+{\frac{1}{2}}(v_{1}^{\prime\prime}v_{2}-v_{1}^{\prime}v_{2}^{\prime}+v_{1}v_{2}^{\prime\prime})\big)\Big|_{x_{0}}.

for the bilinear form (
9
).

Taking the Hill operator
L_{0}
with the zero Hill potential (i.e.,
L_{0}u=u^{\prime\prime}
) as the base point for the affine space

\operatorname{Hill}(S^{1})
, we see that
\mathfrak{vir}(S^{1})
is the central extension of
\operatorname{Vect}(S^{1})
defined by the Gelfand-Fuchs cocycle

(20)

c_{\operatorname{GF}}(v_{1},v_{2})={\frac{1}{2}}\int_{S^{1}}v_{1}^{\prime\prime\prime}\ v_{2},\ \ \ v_{1},v_{2}\in\operatorname{Vect}(S^{1}).

For
{\mathsf{F}}\in\operatorname{Diff}_{+}(S^{1})
and
(\mathcal{T},\mathsf{c})\in\mathfrak{vir}^{*}(\mathsf{C})\cong|\Omega|^{2}_{S^{1}}\times\mathbb{R}

we have

F^{-1}\cdot(\mathcal{T},\mathsf{c})=\big({\mathsf{F}}^{*}\mathcal{T}+\frac{\mathsf{c}}{2}\mathcal{S}({\mathsf{F}}),\mathsf{c}\big).

3.
The space of developing maps

For the rest of this paper, we take
\mathsf{C}
to be compact, connected, and oriented (i.e., diffeomorphic to an oriented circle). We let
\widetilde{\mathsf{C}}
be a simply connected covering space, so that
\mathsf{C}=\widetilde{\mathsf{C}}/\mathbb{Z}
. The diffeomorphism of
\widetilde{\mathsf{C}}
corresponding to translation by
1\in\mathbb{Z}
will be denoted
\kappa
.

3.1.
Developing maps

Let
L\in\operatorname{Hill}(\mathsf{C})
be a Hill operator, and
u_{1},u_{2}\in|\Omega|^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{\widetilde{\mathsf{C}}}
a fundamental system of solutions (defined on the universal cover), with normalized Wronskian

W(u_{1},u_{2})=-1.

The normalization determines the fundamental system uniquely up to the action of
\operatorname{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})
. Since
u_{1},u_{2}
have no common zeroes,
their ratio

\gamma=(u_{1}:u_{2})\colon\widetilde{\mathsf{C}}\to\mathbb{R}\!\operatorname{P}(1)

is a well-defined local diffeomorphism. Under the action of
\kappa
, this map transforms according to

\gamma(\kappa\cdot x)=h\cdot\gamma(x)
(with the standard action of
\operatorname{PSL}(2,\mathbb{R})
on
\mathbb{R}\!\operatorname{P}(1)
),
for some
h\in\operatorname{PSL}(2,\mathbb{R})
.

Definition 3.1
.

[
30
]

A
developing map
is an
orientation preserving local diffeomorphism
\gamma\colon\widetilde{\mathsf{C}}\to\mathbb{R}\!\operatorname{P}(1)

which is
quasi-periodic
, in the sense that

\gamma(\kappa\cdot x)=h\cdot\gamma(x)

for some
monodromy

h\in\operatorname{PSL}(2,\mathbb{R})
. We denote by

\mathcal{D}(\mathsf{C})\subseteq C^{\infty}(\widetilde{\mathsf{C}},\mathbb{R}\!\operatorname{P}(1))

the space of developing maps.

Thus, every normalized fundamental system for a Hill operator defines a developing map; a different choice of
fundamental system changes
\gamma
by the natural
\operatorname{PSL}(2,\mathbb{R})
-action,

(21)

(g\cdot\gamma)(x)=g\cdot\gamma(x).

Conversely, every developing map
\gamma
arises in this way from a unique Hill operator
L
.

Definition 3.2
.

A
normalized lift
of
\gamma\in\mathcal{D}(\mathsf{C})
is a pair of
-{\frac{1}{2}}
-densities
u_{1},u_{2}\in|\Omega|^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{\widetilde{\mathsf{C}}}
with
W(u_{1},u_{2})=-1
, with
\gamma=(u_{1}:u_{2})
.

Every
\gamma
admits a normalized lift, unique up to an overall sign.
With
\partial
as in (
4
), there is a unique Hill operator
having the normalized lift as its fundamental system of solutions. Explicitly,

(22)

L(u)=-\det\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}\partial^{*}\partial u&\partial^{*}\partial u_{1}&\partial^{*}\partial u_{2}\\
\partial u&\partial u_{1}&\partial u_{2}\\
u&u_{1}&u_{2}\end{array}\right)

(The right hand side defines an operator over
\widetilde{\mathsf{C}}
; one checks that it descends to
\mathsf{C}
.) This defines a surjective map

p\colon\mathcal{D}(\mathsf{C})\to\operatorname{Hill}(\mathsf{C})=\mathfrak{vir}^{*}_{1}(\mathsf{C}),

which is the quotient map for the principal
\operatorname{PSL}(2,\mathbb{R})
-action (
21
).

Writing

\gamma=(\sin\phi:\cos\phi)

with
\phi\in C^{\infty}(\widetilde{\mathsf{C}},\mathbb{R}),\ \phi^{\prime}>0
(defined uniquely up to multiples of
\pi
), a normalized lift is given by

(23)

u_{1}=(\partial\phi)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\sin\phi,\ u_{2}=(\partial\phi)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\cos\phi

(with
\partial\phi
the positive 1-density given by exterior differential).
For
\mathsf{C}=S^{1}=\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}
, one finds that the associated Hill potential is given by

(24)

3.3.
Action of diffeomorphisms

For the following discussion, we shall use the
Poincaré translation number

[
12
]
. The translation number is a quasi-homomorphism
\tau\colon\widetilde{\operatorname{SL}}(2,\mathbb{R})\to\mathbb{R}
,
defined in terms of the action of
\widetilde{\operatorname{SL}}(2,\mathbb{R})
on
\mathbb{R}=\widetilde{\mathbb{R}\!\operatorname{P}}(1)
by

(30)

\tau(\widetilde{g})=\lim_{N\to\pm\infty}\frac{\widetilde{g}^{N}.\phi_{0}}{N\pi},

for arbitrary
\phi_{0}\in\mathbb{R}

\phi_{0}
; it is furthermore conjugation invariant and continuous, and it satisfies the quasi-homomorphism property
|\tau(\widetilde{g}_{1}\widetilde{g}_{2})-\tau(\widetilde{g}_{1})-\tau(\widetilde{g}_{2})|\leq 1
.
One may think of the
\tau(\widetilde{g})

as the ‘asymptotic slope’ of the action of
\tilde{g}
on
\mathbb{R}
. We have

\tau(r_{\alpha})=\frac{1}{\pi}\alpha,\ \ \ \tau(h_{\beta,n})=n,\ \ \ \tau(p^{\pm}_{n})=n.

In terms of the picture of conjugacy classes,
\tau
is simply the horizontal coordinate.

Recall that the action of

on
\mathbb{R}\!\operatorname{P}(1)
has two fixed points if
h
is hyperbolic, and a single fixed point if
h
is parabolic.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
\gamma\in\mathcal{D}(\mathsf{C})
be a developing map with lifted monodromy
\widetilde{h}=q(\gamma)
.

(a)

If
\tau(\widetilde{h})>0
, then
\gamma\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}\!\operatorname{P}(1)
is surjective (and hence is a covering).

(b)

If
\tau(\widetilde{h})=0
, and
h
is hyperbolic, then the range of
\gamma
is an open arc whose end points are the
two fixed points of
h
.

(c)

If
\tau(\widetilde{h})=0
, and
h
is parabolic, then the range of
\gamma
is the complement of the unique fixed point of
h
.

Proof.

Write
\gamma=(\sin\phi:\cos\phi)\in\mathcal{D}(\mathsf{C})
. We shall use the property

\phi(\kappa^{N}(x))=\widetilde{h}^{N}\cdot\phi(x)

for

,
N\in\mathbb{Z}
.
If

, this property shows that

is surjective, and hence
is a global diffeomorphism. Suppose

. In particular,

must be hyperbolic or parabolic, and so its action

has (one or two) fixed points. Their pre-images are fixed points for the action of
\widetilde{h}
(since

). Since

is increasing, equation (
31
) shows that the range of
\phi
cannot contain any such fixed points, but that for any given

the limits

are fixed points. Hence, the range of

must be the interval between two successive fixed points.
∎

3.5.
Local sections

To complete our discussion of the geometry of the space of developing maps, let us show that the

.

of

(b)

We obtain similar sections

over each component of the set of elliptic elements, using the fact that every elliptic element

(c)

(d)

Finally, a section on an open neighborhood of

.

4.3.

4.6.
The 2-form

We now have all the ingredients to describe the 2-form on the space of developing maps. The formula involves an integral of
the density valued 2-form

(D_{L}\Theta)\,\Theta\in\Omega^{2}(\mathcal{D}(\mathsf{C}),|\Omega|^{1}_{\widetilde{\mathsf{C}}})

over the segment from
x_{0}
to
\kappa(x_{0})

by adding a boundary term involving the

Proposition 4.13
.

The 2-form
\varpi_{\mathcal{D}}\in\Omega^{2}(\mathcal{D}(\mathsf{C}))
, given as

\varpi_{\mathcal{D}}=-\int_{x_{0}}^{\kappa(x_{0})}(D_{L}\Theta)\,\Theta-B_{L,x_{0}}(\Theta,\kappa^{*}\Theta)

does not depend on the choice of
x_{0}\in\widetilde{\mathsf{C}}
.

Proof.

Let
x_{1}\neq x_{0}
be another choice. Without loss of generality, assume
x_{1}>x_{0}
with respect to the orientation. We have

\displaystyle\int_{x_{0}}^{\kappa(x_{0})}(D_{L}\Theta)\Theta-\int_{x_{1}}^{\kappa(x_{1})}(D_{L}\Theta)\Theta

\displaystyle=\int_{x_{0}}^{x_{1}}(D_{L}\Theta)\Theta-\int_{\kappa(x_{0})}^{\kappa(x_{1})}(D_{L}\Theta)\Theta

\displaystyle=\int_{x_{0}}^{x_{1}}\big((D_{L}\Theta)\Theta-\kappa^{*}(D_{L}\Theta)\kappa^{*}\Theta\big)

\displaystyle=-\int_{x_{0}}^{x_{1}}(D_{L}\Theta)\,(\kappa^{*}\Theta-\Theta)

where we used that
D_{L}\Theta
is
\mathbb{Z}
-invariant (Proposition
4.11
). Using the integration by parts formula
(
10
) with

\int_{x_{0}}^{x_{1}}\Theta\,D_{L}(\kappa^{*}\Theta-\Theta)=0

(since
D_{L}\kappa^{*}\Theta=\kappa^{*}D_{L}\Theta=D_{L}\Theta
) the last expression becomes

-B_{L,x_{0}}(\Theta,\kappa^{*}\Theta-\Theta)+B_{L,x_{1}}(\Theta,\kappa^{*}\Theta-\Theta).

Finally, note that
B_{L,x_{i}}(\Theta,\Theta)=0
by symmetry. ∎

Using the explicit expression for the boundary terms, as given in (
19
), we can write down the formula
for the case
\mathsf{C}=S^{1}=\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}
, as follows:

(54)

\varpi_{\mathcal{D}_{+}(\mathcal{S}^{1})}=-\int_{0}^{1}(D_{L}\Theta)\,\Theta-\Big(2\mathcal{T}_{0}\Theta_{0}\Theta_{1}+{\frac{1}{2}}\Theta_{0}\Theta_{1}^{\prime\prime}-{\frac{1}{2}}\Theta_{0}^{\prime}\Theta_{1}^{\prime}+{\frac{1}{2}}\Theta_{0}^{\prime\prime}\Theta_{1}\Big).

Here
\mathcal{T}
is the Hill potential corresponding to

, and

\mathcal{T}_{x},\Theta_{x},\Theta_{x}^{\prime},\Theta_{x}^{\prime\prime}
are the evaluations at

.
Writing everything in terms of

\mathcal{T}=(\phi^{\prime})^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\phi^{\prime\prime\prime}/\phi^{\prime}-\frac{3}{4}(\phi^{\prime\prime}/\phi^{\prime})^{2}
and
\Theta=-{\mathsf{d}}\phi/\phi^{\prime}
, this becomes a highly complicated expression in terms of
\phi
and its first three derivatives.

The properties of

, as described in Theorem

. On the other hand, they will also be immediate consequences of the construction by Drinfeld-Sokolov reduction, described in the next Section.

5.
Construction via Drinfeld-Sokolov method

We shall now give a construction of the 2-form
\varpi_{\mathcal{D}}
, using the Drinfeld-Sokolov approach
[
10
]
. Throughout, we take
\mathsf{C}
to be a compact, connected, oriented 1-manifold, presented as
\mathsf{C}=\widetilde{\mathsf{C}}/\mathbb{Z}
for a universal cover
\widetilde{\mathsf{C}}
.
The quotient map will be denoted
\pi\colon\widetilde{\mathsf{C}}\to\mathsf{C}

5.1.
Principal bundles over
\mathsf{C}

Throughout this subsection,

G
will denote an arbitrary connected Lie group; later we will specialize to
G=\operatorname{PSL}(2,\mathbb{R})

Let
Q\to\mathsf{C}
be a principal
G
-bundle, and let
\operatorname{Gau}(Q)
be its group of gauge transformations, with Lie algebra

\mathfrak{gau}(Q)=\Omega^{0}(\mathsf{C},Q\times_{G}\mathfrak{g})

the space
of sections of the adjoint bundle. Let
\mathcal{A}(Q)
be the affine space of principal connections on
Q
; the underlying linear space is
\Omega^{1}(\mathsf{C},Q\times_{G}\mathfrak{g})
.
The group
\operatorname{Gau}(Q)
acts on
\mathcal{A}(Q)
by gauge transformations of connections, with generating vector fields

where

\partial_{A}\colon\Omega^{0}(\mathsf{C},Q\times_{G}\mathfrak{g})\to\Omega^{1}(\mathsf{C},Q\times_{G}\mathfrak{g})

The action groupoid for the gauge action is Morita equivalent to the transformation groupoid for the conjugation action of
G
on itself

(55)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Gau}(Q)\ltimes\mathcal{A}(Q)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{P}(Q)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces G\ltimes G}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{A}(Q)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces G}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces q}

Here the equivalence bimodule is the space

\mathcal{P}(Q)=\{\tau\in\Gamma(\pi^{*}Q)|\ \exists h\in G\colon\kappa^{*}\tau=h\cdot\tau\}

of quasi-periodic sections of the pullback bundle
\pi^{*}Q\to\widetilde{\mathsf{C}}
. Furthermore,
q(\tau)=h
is the
monodromy
, and
p(\tau)
is the unique principal connection such that
\tau
is horizontal (for the pullback connection).
Since we are dealing with action groupoids, the Morita equivalence just means that the two actions commute, and that they are both principal actions, with
p
and
q
as their respective quotient maps. In particular, (
55

gives a 1-1 correspondence between
\operatorname{Gau}(Q)
-orbits in
\mathcal{A}(Q)
and conjugacy classes in
G
.

Remark 5.1
.

(a)

A choice of parametrization

(b)

\widetilde{G}
is the universal cover of

\operatorname{Gau}_{0}(Q)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Gau}_{0}(Q)\ltimes\mathcal{A}(Q)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{P}(Q)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces G\ltimes\widetilde{G}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{A}(Q)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widetilde{G}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces q}

(c)

One may also allow for disconnected

. Here

is possibly non-trivial, and the image of the map

The gauge action of
\operatorname{Gau}(Q)

\operatorname{Aut}_{+}(Q)
be the subgroup of automorphisms

-equivariant automorphisms of

. Note that the action of this group

.

We shall need a certain ‘tautological’ 1-form

5.2.

\varpi_{\mathcal{P}}

Suppose now that

The metric, together with integration, defines a non-degenerate

\mathfrak{gau}(Q)

\operatorname{Gau}(Q)

\operatorname{Gau}(Q)
-action on the affine space
\mathcal{A}(Q)

By the same mechanism as explained at the beginning of section

this defines a central extension

(62)

As a vector space,
\widehat{\mathfrak{gau}}(Q)
consists of all affine-linear functionals
\widehat{\xi}\colon\mathcal{A}(Q)\to\mathbb{R}
for which the underlying linear functional on
\Omega^{1}(\mathsf{C},Q\times_{G}\mathfrak{g})
is given by an element
\xi\in\mathfrak{gau}(Q)
;
the bracket reads as

[\widehat{\xi}_{1},\widehat{\xi}_{2}](A)=\int_{\mathsf{C}}\langle\partial_{A}\xi_{1},\,\xi_{2}\rangle.

From now on, we consider the affine action of
\operatorname{Gau}(Q)
on
\mathcal{A}(Q)=\widehat{\mathfrak{gau}}(Q)^{*}_{1}

.

The following result is due to
[

]
for the case

see also
[

,
20
]

Theorem 5.3
.

There is a natural 2-form

with the following properties:

(a)

The exterior differential of
\varpi_{\mathcal{P}}

{\mathsf{d}}\varpi_{\mathcal{P}}=q^{*}\eta,

where

(b)

\varpi_{\mathcal{P}}

G

(c)

\varpi_{\mathcal{P}}

\operatorname{Gau}(Q)
-invariant, and its contractions with generating vector fields, for

\iota(\xi_{\mathcal{P}})\varpi_{\mathcal{P}}=-p^{*}\langle dA,\xi\rangle.

In fact,
\varpi_{\mathcal{P}}

\operatorname{Aut}_{+}(Q)
-invariant.

For a given choice of base point

(63)

\varpi_{\mathcal{P}}=-{\frac{1}{2}}\int_{{x}_{0}}^{\kappa({x}_{0})}\langle\Xi,\,\Xi^{\prime}\rangle+{\frac{1}{2}}\,\langle\Xi_{{x}_{0}},\,q^{*}\theta^{L}\rangle.

\varpi_{\mathcal{P}}
does not depend on any additional choices. In particular; it
does not depend on the choice of

The

is verified by an argument parallel to that for

\displaystyle={\frac{1}{2}}\int_{x_{0}}^{x_{1}}\langle\Xi,\Xi^{\prime}\rangle-{\frac{1}{2}}\int_{x_{0}}^{x_{1}}\langle\operatorname{Ad}_{q}\Xi-q^{*}\theta^{R},\operatorname{Ad}_{q}\Xi^{\prime}\rangle

\displaystyle={\frac{1}{2}}\langle q^{*}\theta^{L},\Xi\rangle\Big|_{x_{0}}^{x_{1}},

which exactly compensates the change of the boundary term. This also shows that

is natural: Given principal

5.4.
Drinfeld-Sokolov reduction

The result
\varpi_{\mathcal{D}}=\widehat{\iota}^{*}\varpi_{\mathcal{P}}
has a conceptual explanation. We shall follow
the coordinate-free description of Drinfeld-Sokolov reduction

[
10
]
given in Segal’s paper
[
28
]
; see also
[
11
]
.
Let

\mathsf{P}\to\mathsf{C}=\widetilde{\mathsf{C}}/\mathbb{Z}

be an oriented rank
1
projective bundle, with a distinguished section
\sigma\in\Gamma(\mathsf{P})
.
Let
Q\to\mathsf{C}
be the associated principal
G=\operatorname{PSL}(2,\mathbb{R})
-bundle. The gauge group

\mathcal{G}=\operatorname{Gau}(Q)
acts by projective transformations on
\mathsf{P}
. The section
\sigma
determines subgroups

(70)

\mathcal{N}\subseteq\mathcal{B}\subseteq\mathcal{G}=\operatorname{Gau}(Q),

where
\mathcal{B}
are the gauge transformations fixing
\sigma
, and
\mathcal{N}
are those which furthermore act trivially on

\sigma^{*}V\mathsf{P}

is the vertical bundle. The corresponding Lie algebras

(71)

\operatorname{Lie}(\mathcal{N})\subseteq\operatorname{Lie}(\mathcal{B})\subseteq\operatorname{Lie}(\mathcal{G})=\mathfrak{gau}(Q)

act as infinitesimal gauge transformations on
\mathsf{P}
, and so are realized as spaces of vertical vector fields on
\mathsf{P}
. The metric on

induces a bundle metric on

\mathfrak{gau}(Q)

\operatorname{Lie}(\mathcal{G})/\operatorname{Lie}(\mathcal{B})=\Gamma(\sigma^{*}V\mathsf{P})

\operatorname{Lie}(\mathcal{N})

\mathsf{C}

\Omega^{1}(\mathsf{C},\sigma^{*}V\mathsf{P})=\operatorname{Lie}(\mathcal{N})^{*}

(the smooth dual). The space
\mathcal{A}(Q)
of principal connections may be regarded as the space
of projective connections on
\mathsf{P}
. Given such a connection, the composition of
T\sigma\colon T\mathsf{C}\to T\mathsf{P}

followed by vertical projection defines a bundle map

(
72

\mathcal{B}
-equivariant map

By construction,

if and only if
\sigma
is an

Proposition 5.10

The restriction of the central extension

A.2.
Quasi-symplectic groupoids

A 2-form
\omega\in\Omega^{2}(\mathcal{G})
on a Lie groupoid
\mathcal{G}\rightrightarrows M
is called
multiplicative
if

(78)

\operatorname{Mult}_{\mathcal{G}}^{*}\omega=\operatorname{pr}_{1}^{*}\omega+\operatorname{pr}_{2}^{*}\omega

where
\operatorname{Mult}_{\mathcal{G}}\colon\mathcal{G}^{(2)}\to\mathcal{G}
is the groupoid multiplication and
\operatorname{pr}_{1},\operatorname{pr}_{2}\colon\mathcal{G}^{(2)}\to\mathcal{G}
are the two projections (here
\mathcal{G}^{(2)}
denotes the space of composable arrows). This property implies that
\omega

pulls back to
0
on
M
(see
[
5
,
32
]
).
It therefore defines a bundle map

(79)

\rho\colon\operatorname{Lie}(\mathcal{G})=\nu(\mathcal{G},M)\to T^{*}M

such that
\langle\rho([w]),\,v\rangle=\omega(w,v)
for
v\in TM
and
[w]\in\nu(\mathcal{G},M)

(represented by an element
w\in T\mathcal{G}|_{M}
). Letting
\sigma^{L},\sigma^{R}
denote the left-,right-invariant vector fields defined by

\sigma\in\Gamma(\operatorname{Lie}(G))
,

(80)

\iota(\sigma^{L})\omega=\mathsf{s}^{*}\rho(\sigma),\ \ \iota(\sigma^{R})\omega=\mathsf{t}^{*}\rho(\sigma).

The pair
(\mathcal{G},\omega)
is called a
symplectic groupoid

[
6
]
if
\omega
is a multiplicative symplectic 2-form. In this case, the manifold of units acquires a Poisson structure, and the symplectic groupoid is called its integration.

Example A.5
.

The main example of a symplectic groupoid is the cotangent groupoid
T^{*}G\rightrightarrows\mathfrak{g}^{*}
of a Lie group. Using left trivialization
T^{*}G=G\times\mathfrak{g}^{*}
, the groupoid structure is that as an action groupoid for the coadjoint action; in particular,
\mathsf{t}(k,\mu)=k\cdot\mu,\ \mathsf{s}(k,\mu)=\mu
.
The 2-form is given by the canonical symplectic form of the cotangent bundle:

(81)

\omega=-{\mathsf{d}}\langle\mu,\theta^{L}\rangle

with the left-invariant Maurer-Cartan form
\theta^{L}\in\Omega^{1}(G,\mathfrak{g})
.
The induced Poisson structure on
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
is the standard Kirillov Poisson structure.

Quasi-symplectic groupoids

[
5
,
18
,
32
]
(called
twisted pre-symplectic groupoids
in
[
5
]
) are a generalization of symplectic groupoids. Here
\omega
need not be closed, but instead should satisfy

(82)

{\mathsf{d}}\omega=\mathsf{s}^{*}\eta-\mathsf{t}^{*}\eta

for a given closed 3-form
\eta\in\Omega^{3}(M)
. As a non-degeneracy condition, one requires that the map

(\mathsf{a},\rho)\colon\operatorname{Lie}(\mathcal{G})\to TM\oplus T^{*}M

given by the anchor
\mathsf{a}
the map (
79
), is an inclusion as a Lagrangian (i.e., maximal isotropic) subbundle.
In this case, the range of
(\mathsf{a},\rho)
defines an
\eta
-twisted Dirac structure on
M
, with
(\mathcal{G},\omega)
its integration in the sense
of
[
5
]
.

Remark A.6
.

By
[
5
, Corollary 3.4]
, a multiplicative 2-form

on a source-connected

Remark A.7

For any quasi-symplectic groupoid

A (left)

(c)

Here

are the

\mathcal{G}\times_{M}P

and

the definition of the fiber product
\mathcal{G}\times_{M}P

For the case of symplectic groupoids, conditions (

of Hamiltonian actions of symplectic groupoids due to Mikami-Weinstein
[

[

]

[

One may similarly define a right Hamiltonian space

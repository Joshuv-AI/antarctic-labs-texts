Existence of global solutions to the nonlocal Schrödinger equation on the line
Yi ZHAO, Engui FAN

Abstract
In this paper, we address the existence of global solutions to the Cauchy problem for the integrable nonlocal nonlinear Schrödinger (nonlocal NLS) equation with the initial data q 0 ​ ( x ) ∈ H 1 , 1 ​ ( ℝ ) q_{0}(x)\in H^{1,1}(\mathbb{R}) with the L 1 ​ ( ℝ ) L^{1}(\mathbb{R}) small-norm assumption. We rigorously show that the spectral problem for the nonlocal NLS equation admits no eigenvalues or resonances, as well as Zhou vanishing lemma is effective under the L 1 ​ ( ℝ ) L^{1}(\mathbb{R}) small-norm assumption. With inverse scattering theory and the Riemann-Hilbert approach, we rigorously establish the bijectivity and Lipschitz continuous of the direct and inverse scattering map from the initial data to reflection coefficients. By using reconstruction formula and the Plemelj projection estimates of reflection coefficients, we further obtain the existence of the local solution and the priori estimates, which assure the existence of the global solution to the Cauchy problem for the nonlocal NLS equation. Keywords: Nonlocal nonlinear Schrödinger equation; Riemann-Hilbert problem; global solution; Plemelj projection operator; Lipschitz continuous. MSC 2020: 35Q51; 35Q55; 35Q15; 37K15; 35A01; 35G25.

Introduction

In this paper, we show the existence of global solutions to the Cauchy problem for the integrable nonlocal nonlinear Schrödinger (nonlocal NLS) equation

\displaystyle iq_{t}(x,t)+q_{xx}(x,t)+2\sigma q^{2}(x,t)\bar{q}(-x,t)=0,\ \sigma=\pm 1,\ x\in\mathbb{R},\ t>0

\displaystyle q(x,0)=q_{0}(x),

(1.2)

[
1

they further obtain its Lax pair, an infinite number of conservation laws and is PT symmetry.
For rapidly decaying initial data and initial value with nonzero boundary conditions, the soliton solutions to the nonlocal NLS equation (

In fact, the nonlocal NLS equation ever was derived in a physical application of magnetics

The nonlocal NLS equation (

iq_{t}(x;t)=q_{xx}(x,t)+V[q,x,t]q(x,t)

,
5
]

Since PT symmetric systems allow for loseless-like propagation due to their balance of gain and loss
[
6
]

[

[

Besides, the nonlocal NLS equation is gauge-equivalent to the unconventional system of coupled Landau-Lifchitz equations and therefore can be useful in the physics of nanomagnetic

Related to the nonlocal NLS equation, Fokas analyzed a (2+1) dimensional integrable nonlocal NLS equation

Ablowitz introduced new reverse space-time and reverse time nonlocal nonlinear integrable
equations and their discrete version
[

Besides, an integrable discrete PT symmetric "discretization" of the nonlocal NLS equation was obtained from a new nonlocal PT symmetric reduction of the Ablowitz-Ladik scattering problem

The long-time behaviour of the solution to the nonlocal NLS equation (

Recently, the long-time behaviour for nonlocal NLS equation (

It is well-known that the existence of global solution or well-posedness of initial value problem of a partial

2.1
Some notations

We first fix some notations used in this paper:

•

If
I
is an interval on the real line
\mathbb{R}
and
X
is a Banach space, then
C(I,X)
denotes the space of continuous functions on
I

X

\|f\|_{C(I,X)}=\sup_{x\in I}\|f(x)\|_{X}.

•

H^{m}(\mathbb{R})
denotes the Sobolev space of distributions with square integrable derivatives up to the order m.

•

L^{2,m}(\mathbb{R})
denotes the weighted
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
space with the norm

\|q\|_{L^{2,m}(\mathbb{R})}=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\langle x\rangle^{2m}|q(x)|^{2}\rm dx\right)^{1/2}

where
\langle x\rangle=(1+x^{2})^{1/2}
.

•

H^{1,1}(\mathbb{R})
denotes the weighted Sobolev space

H^{1,1}(\mathbb{R})=\left\{q|q\in L^{2,1}(\mathbb{R}),\partial_{x}q\in L^{2,1}(\mathbb{R})\right\}.

•

\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{R})
denotes the function space

\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{R})=\left\{f(x)|f(x)\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R})\cap L^{2,1}(\mathbb{R}),xf(x)\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})\right\}

with the norm

\|f(x)\|_{\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{R})}=\|f(x)\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R})\cap L^{2,1}(\mathbb{R})}+\|xf(x)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}.

2.2
Jost functions and Lipschitz continuous

The nonlocal NLS equation is integrable and admits the Lax pair
[
1
]

\displaystyle\Phi_{x}+ik\sigma_{3}\Phi=U\Phi,

(2.1)

\displaystyle\Phi_{t}+2ik^{2}\sigma_{3}\Phi=V\Phi,

(2.2)

where the function
\Phi(x,t;k)
is a matrix-valued function,
k
is a spectral parameter, and the matrices U and V are given by

U=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&q(x,t)\\
-\sigma\bar{q}(-x,t)&0\end{array}\right),\quad V=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}A&B\\
C&-A\end{array}\right),

with

\begin{split}&A=i\sigma q(x,t)\bar{q}(-x,t),\\
&B=2kq(x,t)+iq_{x}(x,t),\\
&C=-2k\sigma\bar{q}(-x,t)+i\sigma(\bar{q}(-x,t))_{x}.\end{split}

The matrix
\sigma_{3}
is the standard Pauli matrix

\sigma_{3}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&0\\
0&-1\end{array}\right).

By a normalized transformation

\varphi_{\pm}(x;k)=[\Phi]_{1}(x;k)e^{ikx},\quad\phi_{\pm}(x;k)=[\Phi]_{2}(x;k)e^{-ikx},

where
[\Phi]_{1}
and
[\Phi]_{2}
denote the first and second column of the matrix
\Phi
, we have

\begin{split}&\varphi_{\pm}(x;k)\rightarrow e_{1}\quad as\quad x\rightarrow\pm\infty,\\
&\phi_{\pm}(x;k)\rightarrow e_{2}\quad as\quad x\rightarrow\pm\infty,\\
\end{split}

(2.3)

and the Jost functions
\varphi_{\pm}(x;k)
and
\phi_{\pm}(x;k)
satisfy the Volttera’s integral equation

\displaystyle\varphi_{\pm}(x;k)=e_{1}+\int_{\pm\infty}^{x}{\rm diag}(1,e^{2ik(x-y)})U(q(y))\varphi_{\pm}(y;k)dy,

(2.4)

\displaystyle\phi_{\pm}(x;k)=e_{2}+\int_{\pm\infty}^{x}{\rm diag}(e^{-2ik(x-y)},1)U(q(y))\phi_{\pm}(y;k)dy.

(2.5)

Lemma 2.1
.

If
q\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
and
\|q\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}<1
, then or every
k\in\mathbb{R}
, there exist unique solutions
\varphi_{\pm}(\cdot;k)\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
and
\phi_{\pm}(\cdot;k)\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
of the integral equations (
2.4
) and (
2.5
), respectively. Moreover, for every
x\in\mathbb{R}
,
\varphi_{-}(x;\cdot)
and
\phi_{+}(x;\cdot)
are continued analytically in
\mathbb{C}^{+}
, whereas
\varphi_{+}(x;\cdot)
and
\phi_{-}(x;\cdot)
are continued analytically in
\mathbb{C}^{-}
.

Proof.

We only give the proof for the Jost function
\varphi_{-}
, others can be given by similar procedure.

For a vector function
f(x;k)=(f_{1}(x;k),f_{2}(x;k))^{T}
, define a operator
K
by

(Kf)(x;k)=\int_{-\infty}^{x}{\rm diag}(1,e^{2ik(x-y)})U(q(y))f(y;k)dy,

(2.6)

then the integral equation can be rewritten as

\varphi_{-}=e_{1}+K\varphi_{-},

(2.7)

its deformation is

(I-K)\varphi_{-}=e_{1}.

According to the Fredholm’s alternative theorem, it is sufficient to prove that there exists a unique zero solution to the homogeneous equation
Kf=f
.

Since
q\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
, for every
k\in\mathbb{C}^{+}
, we have

\|Kf(\cdot;k)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}\leq\|q\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}\|f(\cdot;k)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}.

(2.8)

where the norm is defined by

\|f(\cdot;k)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}=\|f_{1}(\cdot;k)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}+f_{2}(\cdot;k)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}.

Due to the small-norm assumption
\|q\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}<1
, the operator
K
is a contraction from
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
to
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
. By the Banach fixed point theorem, there exists a unique solution
\varphi_{-}(\cdot;k)\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
of the integral equation (
2.6
) for every
k\in\mathbb{C}^{+}
.

To prove the analyticity of
\varphi(x;\cdot)
in
\mathbb{C}^{+}
, we define Neumann series

w(x;k)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}w_{n}(x;k)

with

\displaystyle w_{0}=e_{1},\ \ w_{n+1}(x;k)=\int_{-\infty}^{x}{\rm diag}(1,e^{2ik(x-y)})U(q(y))w_{n}(y;k)dy.

We see that

\|w_{n+1}(x;k)\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\leq\frac{1}{n!}\|U(q)\|^{n}_{L^{1}},

consequently, the Neumann series
w(x;k)
converges absolutely and uniformly for every
x\in\mathbb{R}
and
k\in\mathbb{C}^{+}
. As a result,
\varphi_{-}(x;\cdot)
is analytic in
\mathbb{C}^{+}
for every
x\in\mathbb{R}
.
∎

Since both the Jost functions

And given the linear dependence, we have the following properties of the scattering coefficients for

•

The scattering coefficients admit the integral expression:

Define the reflection coefficients

3
Inverse scattering transform

In this section, we will set up a RH problem
and show the existence and uniqueness of the solution to the RH problem for the given data
r_{1,2}(k)\in\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{R})
satisfying
|r_{1,2}(k)|<1
.

3.1
Setup of a RH problem

Define the matrix-valued functions

M(x;k)=\left\{\begin{split}&\left[\frac{\varphi_{-}(x;k)}{a(k)},\phi_{+}(x;k)\right],\quad&k\in\mathbb{C}^{+}\\
&\left[\varphi_{+}(x;k),\frac{\phi_{-}(x;k)}{d(k)}\right],\quad&k\in\mathbb{C}^{-}.\end{split}\right.

(3.1)

It follows from the linear dependence (
2.50
) and (
2.52
) that for
k\in\mathbb{R}
,

\displaystyle\varphi_{-}=a(k)\varphi_{+}+b(k)e^{+2ikx}\phi_{+},

(3.2)

\displaystyle\phi_{-}=-\sigma\overline{b(-k)}e^{-2ikx}\varphi_{+}+d(k)\phi_{+}.

(3.3)

Rewriting (
3.3
) as

\phi_{+}=\frac{\sigma\overline{b(-k)}e^{-2ikx}}{d(k)}\varphi_{+}+\frac{\phi_{-}}{d(k)}.

(3.4)

Utilizing (
3.2
) and (
3.4
), we have

\begin{split}\frac{\varphi_{-}}{a(k)}=&\varphi_{+}+\frac{b(k)e^{2ikx}}{a(k)}\phi_{+}\\
=&(1+\frac{b(k)}{a(k)}\frac{\sigma\overline{b(-k)}}{d(k)})\varphi_{+}+\frac{b(k)e^{2ikx}}{a(k)}\frac{\phi_{-}}{d(k)}.\end{split}

(3.5)

Therefore, for
k\in\mathbb{R}
, we obtain

M_{+}(x;k)-M_{-}(x;k)=M_{-}(x;k)S(x;k),\quad k\in\mathbb{R},

(3.6)

where

S(x;k)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\sigma r_{1}(k)r_{2}(k)&\sigma r_{2}(k)e^{-2ikx}\\
r_{1}(k)e^{2ikx}&0\end{array}\right).

And the RH problem can be described as

RH problem
Find a
2\times 2
matrix-valued function M(x;k) with the following properties:

•

M(x;\cdot):\ \mathbb{C}\backslash\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{2\times 2}
is analytic.

•

The limits of
M(x;k)
as k approaches
\mathbb{R}
from the upper and lower half-plane exists and are continuous on
\mathbb{R}
, and satisfy

M_{+}(x;k)-M_{-}(x;k)=M_{-}(x;k)S(x;k),\quad k\in\mathbb{R},

(3.7)

•

M(x;k)
satisfies the asymptotic

M(x;k)\rightarrow I,\quad|k|\rightarrow\infty,k\in\mathbb{C}\backslash\mathbb{R}.

It is convenient to deal with the RH problem to introduce a transformation

\Psi_{\pm}(x;k)=M_{\pm}(x;k)-{I},

then the jump condition (
3.6
) can be written as

\displaystyle\Psi_{+}(x;k)-\Psi_{-}(x;k)=\Psi_{-}(x;k)S(x;k)+S(x;k),\quad k\in\mathbb{R},

(3.8)

\displaystyle\Psi_{\pm}(x;k)\rightarrow 0,\quad|k|\rightarrow\infty,k\in\mathbb{C}\backslash\mathbb{R}.

(3.9)

3.2
Solvability of the RH problem

Before looking for the solution to the RH problem, we show some preliminary knowledge that will be used in the subsequent section. And this results have been given in the previous work.
[
27
]

For any function
h\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R})
with
1\leq p<\infty
, the Cauchy operator is defined as

\mathcal{C}(h)(k):=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{h(s)}{s-k}\rm ds,\quad k\in\mathbb{C}\backslash\mathbb{R},

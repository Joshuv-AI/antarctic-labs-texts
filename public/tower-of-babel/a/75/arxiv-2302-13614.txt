2D Smagorinsky type large eddy models as limits of stochastic PDEs
Franco Flandoli, Dejun Luo, Eliseo Luongo

Abstract
We prove that a version of Smagorinsky Large Eddy model for a 2D fluid in vorticity form is the scaling limit of suitable stochastic models for large scales, where the influence of small turbulent eddies is modeled by a transport type noise.



Keywords:
Smagoringsky model, eddy viscosity model, turbulence, transport noise, scaling limit

MSC (2020):
60H15, 76D05

1
Introduction

Recently, a new stochastic approach has been developed in
[
13
,
14
,
18
,
12
,
16
,
10
,
6
]
to
explain Boussinesq hypothesis that “turbulent fluctuations are dissipative on
large scales”
[
5
]
. The idea, better explained below in
Section
2
, is that the large scales satisfy a
Navier-Stokes type equation with a stochastic transport term corresponding to
the action of small scales. In a suitable scaling limit, we get a
deterministic Navier-Stokes equation with an additional dissipative term. The
turbulent viscosity is directly related to the noise (namely small-scale)
covariance. All the quoted works are related to dimension 2, with the
exception of
[
16
]
that deals with a 2D-3C model with some three
dimensional feature, including a stretching term of small scales over large
ones and the possibility of an AKA (anisotropic kinetic alpha) effect in the limit equation. For other
approaches to justify Boussinesq hypothesis and turbulent viscosity based on Eulerian formulations of fluid dynamical systems see for
instance
[
3
,
23
,
30
]
. There are also different models based on filtering the systems at the Lagrangian level rather then Eulerian one, we refer to
[
20
,
21
,
7
,
8
]
for rigorous analysis and some discussions on the topic.

The previous works on the stochastic approach are, however, limited to the
case of
linear
limit dissipation term, namely turbulent viscosity
independent of the solution. Smagorinsky type models are excluded from the
previous analysis and it was not clear for some time how to incorporate them
into this new theory. In this paper we solve this problem. This provides new
insight into these models and their motivations.

Since our techniques are, at present, well developed for the vorticity
equation, while they suffer certain difficulties for the velocity equation,
we present the results for vorticity type equations (however, as stated in
[
29
, Section 5]
, the performances of vorticity-velocity models are sometimes superior to those of velocity-pressure ones). We choose the following
form, discussed for instance in
[
9
]
:

\partial_{t}\omega_{L}+u_{L}\cdot\nabla\omega_{L}=\nu\Delta\omega_{L}+\operatorname{div}\left(g^{\prime}\left(\omega_{L}\right)\nabla\omega_{L}\right)

(1)

(written in this way so that
\operatorname{div}\left(g^{\prime}\left(\omega_{L}\right)\nabla\omega_{L}\right)=\Delta g\left(\omega_{L}\right)
) with the additional conditions
\omega_{L}=\nabla^{\perp}\cdot u_{L}
,
\operatorname{div}u_{L}=0
and the initial condition
\omega_{L}|_{t=0}=\omega_{0}^{L}
. Here,
L
stands for the large scale components of fluid vorticity and velocity,
see the next section for more discussions; the fields are assumed to be periodic, on a torus.
The function
g\left(r\right)
is subject to quite general assumptions
which include it is non-decreasing, so that
g^{\prime}
is not negative. The particular case treated in
[
9
]
(see also
[
25
,
29
,
11
]
) is

\displaystyle g^{\prime}(r)=(C_{s}\mathbf{\Delta})^{2}\lvert r\rvert,

(2)

where
\mathbf{\Delta}
is a subgrid characteristic length-scale and
C_{s}
is a non-dimensional constant which has to be calibrated and its value may vary with the type of the flow and the Reynolds number. However, similarly to the Smagorinsky model in velocity form, it may be useful to cover more general nonlinearities, see for instance
[
3
, Section 3.3.2]
. We
prove that this Smagorinsky type model is the limit of the large-scale
stochastic model

d\omega_{L}+\left(u_{L}\cdot\nabla\omega_{L}-\nu\Delta\omega_{L}\right)dt=-f^{\prime}\left(\omega_{L}\right)\sum_{k}\sigma_{k}\cdot\nabla\omega_{L}\circ dW_{t}^{k}

(3)

(where again
f^{\prime}\left(\omega_{L}\right)\sigma_{k}\cdot\nabla\omega_{L}=\sigma_{k}\cdot\nabla f\left(\omega_{L}\right)
)
with
f
such that
\frac{1}{4}f^{\prime}\left(r\right)^{2}=g^{\prime}\left(r\right)
. The limit is taken along a suitable sequence of small-scale
noise, namely we assume (roughly speaking) that
\sigma_{k}
are smaller and
smaller scale (an assumption of scale separation). The notations and
assumptions (like the fact that
\{W^{k}\}_{k}
are independent Brownian motions
and
\circ
is the Stratonovich multiplication operation) will be explained in
the technical sections.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
we
describe the heuristic ideas behind the stochastic model. In Section

3
we state our results and introduce some mathematical
tools. In Section
4
we show the existence of martingale
solutions of the problem (
3
) above.
Lastly, in Section
5
we will show our main result about
the convergence of martingale solutions of our stochastic models to a measure concentrated on
the unique weak solution of the Smagorinsky model
(
1
), see Theorem
5

below for the rigorous statement.

2
The heuristic idea

The idea described in this section is similar to the one given in

[
12
,
16
]
, but we repeat it and particularise
the models studied here, for completeness and to help the intuition behind the
model. Consider a 2D Newtonian viscous fluid in a torus, described in
vorticity form by the equations

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\omega+u\cdot\nabla\omega

\displaystyle=\nu\Delta\omega,

\displaystyle\omega=\nabla^{\perp}\cdot u,\quad{\rm div}\,u

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle\omega|_{t=0}

\displaystyle=\omega_{0},

where
\omega
is the vorticity
field and
u
the velocity field. Assume that the initial vorticity

\omega_{0}
is the sum of a large scale component
\omega_{0}^{L}
plus a
small-scale component
\omega_{0}^{S}
. Then, at least on a short time
interval
[0,\tau]
, it is reasonable to expect that the system

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\omega_{L}+u\cdot\nabla\omega_{L}

\displaystyle=\nu\Delta\omega_{L},

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\omega_{S}+u\cdot\nabla\omega_{S}

\displaystyle=\nu\Delta\omega_{S},

\displaystyle\omega_{L}|_{t=0}

\displaystyle=\omega_{0}^{L},\quad\omega_{S}|_{t=0}=\omega_{0}^{S}

represents quite well the evolution of the different vortex structures, as for
instance in the small vortex-blob limit to point vortices treated by

[
26
]
. The system above is equivalent to the
original one, by addition.

The next step is considering only the equation for the large scales, isolating
the term which is not closed, namely depends on the small scales:

\partial_{t}\omega_{L}+u_{L}\cdot\nabla\omega_{L}-\nu\Delta\omega_{L}=-u_{S}\cdot\nabla\omega_{L}.

Here
u_{L}
, with
\operatorname{div}u_{L}=0
, has the property

\nabla^{\perp}\cdot u_{L}=\omega_{L}
(namely
u_{L}
is reconstructed from

\omega_{L}
by Biot-Savart law). The field
u_{S}
should correspond to

\omega_{S}
by Biot-Savart law but we now introduce a stochastic closure
assumption. We replace
u_{S}\left(t,x\right)
by a white-in-time noise,
with suitable space dependence

u_{S}\left(t,x\right)\mapsto\chi\left(t,x\right)\sum_{k}\sigma_{k}\left(x\right)\frac{dW_{t}^{k}}{dt},

where
\{\sigma_{k}\}_{k}
are suitable divergence free vector fields, and
\chi(t,x)
is a scalar stochastic process which will be linked to the large scales, in order to model the idea that the turbulent small scales are more active where the large scales have more intense variations (e.g. larger shear);
\{W^{k}\}_{k}
are
independent scalar Brownian motions. In the replacement, Stratonovich
integrals are used, in accordance with the Wong-Zakai principle (see rigorous
results in
[
10
]
). Therefore the equation for large
scales, now closed and stochastic, takes the form

d\omega_{L}+\left(u_{L}\cdot\nabla\omega_{L}-\nu\Delta\omega_{L}\right)dt=-\chi\left(t,x\right)\sum_{k}\sigma_{k}\cdot\nabla\omega_{L}\circ dW_{t}^{k}.

Previous works developed this idea in the case when
\chi=1
, see e.g.
[
18
,
19
,
10
]
. Here we assume
that
\chi
is a function of
\omega_{L}
, that for notational convenience
will be written as

\chi\left(t,x\right)=f^{\prime}\left(\omega_{L}\left(t,x\right)\right)

for a suitable function
f
. As said above, the heuristic idea is that turbulence is more
developed in regions of high large-scale vorticity, hence the small-scale
noise should be modulated by an increasing function
f^{\prime}
.

This is the motivation for the stochastic model
(
3
) presented in the Introduction.
Our main purpose is showing that it leads to the Smagorinsky type
deterministic equation (
1
) in a suitable scaling
limit of the noise.

3
Functional Setting and Main Results

Let us set some notation before stating the main contributions of this work. Let
\mathbb{T}^{2}=\mathbb{R}^{2}/\mathbb{Z}^{2}
be the two dimensional torus and
\mathbb{Z}_{0}^{2}=\mathbb{Z}^{2}\setminus\{0\}
the nonzero lattice points. Let
(H^{s,p}(\mathbb{T}^{2}),\lVert\cdot\rVert_{H^{s,p}}),\ s\in\mathbb{R},\ p\in(1,+\infty)
be the Bessel spaces of zero mean periodic functions. In case of
p=2
, we simply write
H^{s}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
in place of
H^{s,2}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
and we denote by
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{H^{s}}
the corresponding scalar products. In case also
s>0
we denote by
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{H^{-s},H^{s}}
the dual pairing between
H^{s}
and
H^{-s}
. Lastly we denote by
H^{s-}(\mathbb{T}^{2})=\cap_{r<s}H^{r}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
.
In case of
s=0
we will write
L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
instead of
H^{0}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
and we will neglect the subscript in the notation for the norm and the inner product.
Similarly, we introduce the Bessel spaces of zero mean vector fields

\displaystyle\mathbf{H}^{s,p}

\displaystyle=\{(u_{1},u_{2})^{t}:\ u_{1},u_{2}\in H^{s,p}(\mathbb{T}^{2})\},

\displaystyle\langle u,v\rangle_{\mathbf{H}^{s}}

\displaystyle=\langle u_{1},v_{1}\rangle_{H^{s}}+\langle u_{2},v_{2}\rangle_{H^{s}},\quad\text{for }s\in\mathbb{R}.

Again, in case of
s=0
we will write
\mathbf{L}^{2}
instead of
\mathbf{H}^{0}
and we will neglect the subscript in the notation for the norm and the scalar product.

Let
Z
be a separable Hilbert space, with associated norm
\|\cdot\|_{Z}
. We denote by
C^{w}_{\mathcal{F}}\left(\left[0,T\right];Z\right)
the space of weakly continuous adapted processes
\left(X_{t}\right)_{t\in\left[0,T\right]}
with values in
Z
such that

\mathbb{E}\bigg[\sup_{t\in\left[0,T\right]}\left\|X_{t}\right\|_{Z}^{2}\bigg]<\infty

and by
L_{\mathcal{F}}^{p}\left(0,T;Z\right),\ p\in[1,\infty),
the space of progressively
measurable processes
\left(X_{t}\right)_{t\in\left[0,T\right]}
with
values in
Z
such that

\mathbb{E}\bigg[\int_{0}^{T}\left\|X_{t}\right\|_{Z}^{p}dt\bigg]<\infty.

Following the ideas introduced in Section
2
, we are interested in the following stochastic model with a more precise noise (cf.
[
22
,
13
]
)

\begin{cases}d\omega_{L}=(\nu\Delta\omega_{L}-u_{L}\cdot\nabla\omega_{L})\,dt-\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}_{0}}\theta_{k}\sigma_{k}\cdot\nabla f(\omega_{L})\circ dW^{k},\\
u_{L}=-\nabla^{\perp}(-\Delta)^{-1}\omega_{L},\\
\omega_{L}(0)=\omega_{0},\end{cases}

(4)

where
f\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{R})
,
\theta=(\theta_{k})_{k}\in\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{2}_{0})
satisfies

\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}_{0}}\theta_{k}^{2}=1,\quad\theta_{k}=\theta_{l}\quad\mbox{if }|k|=|l|;

(5)

\{\sigma_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}_{0}}
is the standard orthonormal basis of divergence free vector fields in
\mathbf{L}^{2}
made by the eigenfunctions of the Stokes operator, i.e.

\displaystyle\sigma_{k}(x)=\frac{k^{\perp}}{\lvert k\rvert}e_{k}(x),\quad e_{k}(x)=\sqrt{2}\begin{cases}\cos(2\pi k\cdot x)\quad\text{if }k\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}_{+},\\
\sin(2\pi k\cdot x)\quad\text{if }k\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}_{-},\end{cases}

where
k^{\perp}=(k_{2},-k_{1})
,
\mathbb{Z}^{2}_{+}:=\{k\in\mathbb{Z}_{0}^{2}:(k_{1}>0)\text{ or }(k_{1}=0,\ k_{2}>0)\}
and
\mathbb{Z}^{2}_{-}:=-\mathbb{Z}^{2}_{+}
;
\{W^{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}_{0}}
is a family of real independent Brownian motions. Moreover we assume that

\displaystyle\lvert f^{\prime}(x)\rvert\lesssim 1+\lvert x\rvert^{\alpha}

(6)

for some
\alpha\in[0,1]
. This implies in particular that

\displaystyle\lvert f(x)\rvert

\displaystyle\lesssim 1+\lvert x\rvert^{\alpha+1}.

(7)

In the sequel, we shall omit the subscript
L
to save notation. System (
4
) can be formulated easily in Itô form. Indeed, it holds

\displaystyle\sigma_{k}\cdot\nabla f(\omega)\circ dW^{k}

\displaystyle=\sigma_{k}\cdot\nabla f(\omega)\,dW^{k}+\frac{1}{2}d\left[\sigma_{k}\cdot\nabla f(\omega),W^{k}\right];

since

\displaystyle d\big(\sigma_{k}\cdot\nabla f(\omega)\big)

\displaystyle=\sigma_{k}\cdot\nabla\big(df(\omega)\big)

\displaystyle=\sigma_{k}\cdot\nabla(f^{\prime}(\omega)V)\,dt-\sum_{l}\theta_{l}\sigma_{k}\cdot\nabla\big(f^{\prime}(\omega)^{2}\sigma_{l}\cdot\nabla\omega\big)\circ dW^{l},

where
V:=\nu\Delta\omega-u\cdot\nabla\omega
, one has

\displaystyle d\left[\sigma_{k}\cdot\nabla f(\omega),W^{k}\right]

\displaystyle=-\theta_{k}\sigma_{k}\cdot\nabla\big(f^{\prime}(\omega)^{2}\sigma_{k}\cdot\nabla\omega\big)\,dt

\displaystyle=-\theta_{k}\,{\rm div}\big(f^{\prime}(\omega)^{2}(\sigma_{k}\otimes\sigma_{k})\nabla\omega\big)\,dt

which is due to the divergence free property of
\sigma_{k}
; hence,

\displaystyle-\sum_{k}\theta_{k}\sigma_{k}\cdot\nabla f(\omega)\circ dW^{k}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{k}\theta_{k}\sigma_{k}\cdot\nabla f(\omega)\,dW^{k}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k}\theta_{k}^{2}\,{\rm div}\big(f^{\prime}(\omega)^{2}(\sigma_{k}\otimes\sigma_{k})\nabla\omega\big)\,dt

\displaystyle=-\sum_{k}\theta_{k}\sigma_{k}\cdot\nabla f(\omega)\,dW^{k}+\frac{1}{4}{\rm div}\big(f^{\prime}(\omega)^{2}\nabla\omega\big)\,dt,

where the last step is due to the fact (cf.
[
15
, Lemma 2.6]
for a proof)

\sum_{k}\theta_{k}^{2}\,(\sigma_{k}\otimes\sigma_{k})=\frac{1}{2}I_{2},

(8)

the latter being the
2\times 2
unit matrix. Thanks to the computations on the Itô-Stratonovich corrector above, equation (
4
) can be rewritten as

\begin{cases}d\omega=\big(\nu\Delta\omega-u\cdot\nabla\omega+\frac{1}{4}\operatorname{div}(f^{\prime}(\omega)^{2}\nabla\omega)\big)\,dt-\sum_{k}\theta_{k}\sigma_{k}\cdot\nabla f(\omega)\,dW^{k},\\
u=-\nabla^{\perp}(-\Delta)^{-1}\omega,\\
\omega(0)=\omega_{0}.\end{cases}

(9)

We introduce the real function
g:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
defined as

g(x)=\frac{1}{4}\int_{0}^{x}f^{\prime}(t)^{2}\,dt,\quad x\in\mathbb{R},

which satisfies
g(0)=0
and

\displaystyle\lvert g(y)-g(x)\rvert

\displaystyle\lesssim\lvert y-x\rvert+\left\lvert y\lvert y\rvert^{2\alpha}-x\lvert x\rvert^{2\alpha}\right\rvert

(10)

\displaystyle\lesssim\lvert y-x\rvert\left(1+\lvert x\rvert^{2\alpha}\right)+\lvert y\rvert\left\lvert\lvert y\rvert^{2\alpha}-\lvert x\rvert^{2\alpha}\right\rvert.

From the definition of
g
it follows that system (
9
) can be rewritten as

\begin{cases}d\omega=\big(\nu\Delta\omega-u\cdot\nabla\omega+\Delta g(\omega)\big)\,dt-\sum_{k}\theta_{k}\sigma_{k}\cdot\nabla f^{\prime}(\omega)\,dW^{k},\\
u=-\nabla^{\perp}(-\Delta)^{-1}\omega,\\
\omega(0)=\omega_{0}.\end{cases}

(11)

The relation between
u
and
\omega
can be described in terms of the so-called Biot-Savart operator

\displaystyle K\in\mathcal{L}(H^{s,p},\mathbf{H}^{s+1,p}):\quad K[\omega]=-\nabla^{\perp}(-\Delta)^{-1}\omega\quad\text{for }p\in(1,+\infty),\ s\in\mathbb{R}.

We are now ready to define our notion of solution for system (
11
).

Definition 1

We say that system (
11
) has a weak solution if there exists a filtered probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathcal{F}_{t},\mathbb{P})
, a sequence of independent
\mathcal{F}_{t}
Brownian motions
W^{k}
and
\omega\in C^{w}_{\mathcal{F}}(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2}))\cap L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(0,T;H^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{2}))
such that for any
\phi\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
,
\mathbb{P}
-a.s.
\forall\,t\in[0,T]
, it holds

\displaystyle\langle\omega_{t},\phi\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\omega_{0},\phi\rangle+\nu\int_{0}^{t}\langle\omega_{s},\Delta\phi\rangle\,ds+\int_{0}^{t}\langle g(\omega_{s}),\Delta\phi\rangle\,ds

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{0}^{t}\langle\omega_{s},K[\omega_{s}]\cdot\nabla\phi\rangle\,ds+\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}_{0}}\int_{0}^{t}\theta_{k}\langle f(\omega_{s}),\sigma_{k}\cdot\nabla\phi\rangle\,dW^{k}_{s}.

Due to the nonlinearities appearing in equation (
11
) the existence of weak solutions is a nontrivial fact which will be proved in Section
4
. Indeed we will prove the following result.

Theorem 2

For each
\omega_{0}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
there exists at least one weak solution of system (
11
) in the sense of Definition
1
. Moreover

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\lVert\omega_{t}\rVert^{2}+2\nu\int_{0}^{T}\lVert\nabla\omega_{s}\rVert^{2}\,ds\leq 2\lVert\omega_{0}\rVert^{2}\quad\mathbb{P}-a.s.

Next, following the idea introduced for the first time in
[
22
]
, we consider a family
\{\theta^{N}\}_{N\in\mathbb{N}}\subseteq\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{2}_{0})
, satisfying relation (
5
) such that

\lim_{N\rightarrow+\infty}\lVert\theta^{N}\rVert_{\ell^{\infty}}=0,

(12)

and we call
\omega^{N}
the corresponding weak solution of equation (
11
) with
\{\theta^{N}_{k}\}_{k}
in place of
\{\theta_{k}\}_{k}
. In order to complete our plan, we want to show that the law of
\omega^{N}
converges weakly to a measure supported on the unique weak solution of the Navier-Stokes equation in vorticity form with Smagorinky correction, namely

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\overline{\omega}=\nu\Delta\overline{\omega}+\operatorname{\Delta}g(\overline{\omega})-\overline{u}\cdot\nabla\overline{\omega},\\
\overline{u}=-\nabla^{\perp}(-\Delta)^{-1}\overline{\omega},\\
\overline{\omega}(0)=\omega_{0}.\end{cases}

(13)

Remark 3

Taking
f(r)=\frac{4}{3}C_{s}\mathbf{\Delta}\lvert r\rvert^{1/2}r
,
C_{s}
and
\mathbf{\Delta}
being the same as in (
2
), we have
g(r)=\frac{1}{2}(C_{s}\mathbf{\Delta})^{2}r^{2}{\rm sign}(r)
, and thus
\operatorname{\Delta}g(\overline{\omega})=(C_{s}\mathbf{\Delta})^{2}{\rm div}(|\overline{\omega}|\nabla\overline{\omega})
. In this way, we recover the Smagorinsky model of
[
9
]
.

By a weak solution of (
13
) we mean the following:

Definition 4

We say that
\overline{\omega}
is a weak solution of equation (
13
) if

\displaystyle\overline{\omega}\in C_{w}(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2}))\cap L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{2}))

and for each
\phi\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
, for all
t\in[0,T]
, one has

\displaystyle\langle\overline{\omega}_{t},\phi\rangle-\langle\omega_{0},\phi\rangle

\displaystyle=\nu\int_{0}^{t}\langle\overline{\omega}_{s},\Delta\phi\rangle\,ds+\int_{0}^{t}\langle g(\overline{\omega}_{s}),\Delta\phi\rangle\,ds

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{0}^{t}\langle\overline{\omega}_{s},K[\overline{\omega}_{s}]\cdot\nabla\phi\rangle\,ds.

In Section
5
indeed we will first show the uniqueness of the weak solutions of (
13
), then we will show our main result which reads in the following way.

Theorem 5

Assume that
\{\theta^{N}\}_{N}\subset\ell^{2}
satisfies (
5
) and (
12
). Let
\omega^{N}
be a weak solution of (
11
) corresponding to
\theta^{N}
, and
Q^{N}
its law on
C([0,T];H^{-}(\mathbb{T}^{2}))\cap L^{2}(0,T;H^{1-}(\mathbb{T}^{2}))
. Then the family
\{Q^{N}\}_{N}
is tight on
C([0,T];H^{-}(\mathbb{T}^{2}))\cap L^{2}(0,T;H^{1-}(\mathbb{T}^{2}))
and it converges weakly to the Dirac measure
\delta_{\overline{\omega}}
, where
\overline{\omega}
is the unique weak solution of equation (
13
).

\chi_{n},\chi\in L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{-}(\mathbb{T}^{2}))\cap L^{2}(0,T;H^{1-}(\mathbb{T}^{2}))
such that

\displaystyle\chi_{n}\rightarrow\chi\quad\text{in }L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{-}(\mathbb{T}^{2}))\cap L^{2}(0,T;H^{1-}(\mathbb{T}^{2})).

(16)

Then
\forall\,\beta>2,\ \gamma\in[0,1)
such that
\beta\gamma<2

\displaystyle\chi_{n}\rightarrow\chi\in L^{\beta}(0,T;H^{\gamma}(\mathbb{T}^{2})).

Proof.
Let
\delta,\ \delta^{\prime}>0
such that

\displaystyle 1-\delta>\gamma,\quad 2-2\delta-\beta\gamma>0,\quad\delta^{\prime}<\frac{2-2\delta-\beta\gamma}{\beta-2}.

(17)

From our assumptions
\chi_{n}\rightarrow\chi\in L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{-\delta^{\prime}}(\mathbb{T}^{2}))\cap L^{2}(0,T;H^{1-\delta}(\mathbb{T}^{2}))
. Then the thesis follows by interpolation inequalities and Hölder inequality. Indeed, it holds

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\!\lVert\chi_{n}(t)-\chi(t)\rVert_{H^{\gamma}}^{\beta}dt

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{T}\!\lVert\chi_{n}(t)-\chi(t)\rVert_{H^{1-\delta}}^{\beta\frac{\gamma+\delta^{\prime}}{1-\delta+\delta^{\prime}}}\lVert\chi_{n}(t)-\chi(t)\rVert_{H^{-\delta^{\prime}}}^{\beta\frac{1-\gamma-\delta}{1-\delta+\delta^{\prime}}}dt

\displaystyle\leq\lVert\chi_{n}-\chi\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{t}H^{-\delta^{\prime}}_{x}}^{\beta\frac{1-\gamma-\delta}{1-\delta+\delta^{\prime}}}\int_{0}^{T}\!\lVert\chi_{n}(t)-\chi(t)\rVert_{H^{1-\delta}}^{\beta\frac{\gamma+\delta^{\prime}}{1-\delta+\delta^{\prime}}}dt

\displaystyle\lesssim\lVert\chi_{n}-\chi\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{t}H^{-\delta^{\prime}}_{x}}^{\beta\frac{1-\gamma-\delta}{1-\delta+\delta^{\prime}}}\int_{0}^{T}\!\lVert\chi_{n}(t)\rVert_{H^{1-}}^{\beta\frac{\gamma+\delta^{\prime}}{1-\delta+\delta^{\prime}}}\!+\!\lVert\chi(t)\rVert_{H^{1-}}^{\beta\frac{\gamma+\delta^{\prime}}{1-\delta+\delta^{\prime}}}dt,

where
\|\cdot\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}H^{-\delta^{\prime}}_{x}}
is the norm in
L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{-\delta^{\prime}})
. Under our assumptions on
\delta,\ \delta^{\prime}
it follows that
\beta\frac{\gamma+\delta^{\prime}}{1-\delta+\delta^{\prime}}\leq 2
. Therefore we have the thesis thanks to relation (
16
).

4
Existence of solutions

Our approach for showing the existence of martingale solutions of system (
11
) follows by a standard compactness argument. See for example
[
18
, Section 2.4]
and the references therein for some discussions on this method and further examples of application.

4.1
Galerkin Approximation

We introduce a sequence of Galerkin approximations
\omega^{n}
. Given the orthogonal projector
\Pi^{n}:L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2})\rightarrow{\rm span}\{e_{l},\ \lvert l\rvert\leq n\}
, we look for

\displaystyle\omega^{n}(t)=\sum_{\lvert l\rvert\leq n}c_{l}(t)\,e_{l}

such that
\forall\,\phi\in\Pi^{n}(L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2}))
,
\mathbb{P}
-a.s.
\forall\,t\in[0,T]
, it holds

\displaystyle\langle\omega^{n}_{t},\phi\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\omega^{n}_{0},\phi\rangle+\nu\int_{0}^{t}\langle\omega^{n}_{s},\Delta\phi\rangle\,ds+\int_{0}^{t}\langle g(\omega^{n}_{s}),\Delta\phi\rangle\,ds

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{0}^{t}\langle K[\omega^{n}_{s}]\cdot\nabla\phi,\omega^{n}_{s}\rangle\,ds+\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}_{0}}\int_{0}^{t}\theta_{k}\langle\sigma_{k}\cdot\nabla\phi,f(\omega^{n}_{s})\rangle\,dW^{k}_{s},

where
\omega_{0}^{n}=\Pi^{n}\omega_{0}
. Local existence of the solution
\omega^{n}
is a classical fact due to the regularity of the coefficients appearing in the equation, see for example
[
24
,
28
]
. Global existence follows from the following a priori estimates.

Lemma 10

\mathbb{P}
-a.s.,
\omega^{n}
satisfies

\displaystyle\lVert\omega^{n}_{t}\rVert^{2}+2\nu\int_{0}^{t}\lVert\nabla\omega^{n}_{s}\rVert^{2}\,ds\leq\lVert\omega^{n}_{0}\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\lVert\omega_{0}\rVert^{2}.

(18)

Proof.
By Itô formula and recalling the definition of
g
we have

\displaystyle d\lVert\omega^{n}\rVert^{2}+2\nu\lVert\nabla\omega^{n}\rVert^{2}\,dt

\displaystyle=-2\langle K[\omega^{n}]\cdot\nabla\omega^{n},\omega^{n}\rangle\,dt-\frac{1}{2}\langle f^{\prime}(\omega^{n})^{2}\nabla\omega^{n},\nabla\omega^{n}\rangle\,dt

\displaystyle\quad-2\sum_{k}\theta_{k}\langle\sigma_{k}\cdot\nabla\omega^{n},f(\omega^{n})\rangle\,dW^{k}

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{k}\theta_{k}^{2}\lVert\Pi^{n}(\sigma_{k}\cdot\nabla f(\omega^{n}))\rVert^{2}\,dt.

The first and the third terms are identically equal to
0
due to the classical properties of the trilinear form of Navier-Stokes equations and the following relation:

\langle\sigma_{k}\cdot\nabla\omega^{n},f(\omega^{n})\rangle=\langle\sigma_{k},\nabla F(\omega^{n})\rangle=-\langle\operatorname{div}\sigma_{k},F(\omega^{n})\rangle=0,

where the function
F
above is a primitive of
f.

Therefore we are left to show that

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\langle f^{\prime}(\omega^{n})^{2}\nabla\omega^{n},\nabla\omega^{n}\rangle+\sum_{k}\theta_{k}^{2}\lVert\Pi^{n}(\sigma_{k}\cdot\nabla f(\omega^{n}))\rVert^{2}\leq 0.

The last inequality is due to

\displaystyle\sum_{k}\theta_{k}^{2}\lVert\Pi^{n}(\sigma_{k}\cdot\nabla f(\omega^{n}))\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k}\theta_{k}^{2}\lVert\sigma_{k}\cdot\nabla f(\omega^{n})\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k}\theta_{k}^{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}(\nabla f(\omega^{n}))^{\ast}(\sigma_{k}\otimes\sigma_{k})\nabla f(\omega^{n})\,dx

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\lVert\nabla f(\omega^{n})\rVert^{2}=\frac{1}{2}\langle f^{\prime}(\omega^{n})^{2}\nabla\omega^{n},\nabla\omega^{n}\rangle,

where in the third step we have used (
8
).

Lemma
10
shows in particular that
\{\omega^{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
is bounded in
L^{p}(\Omega;L^{p}(0,T;L^{2}))\cap L^{2}(\Omega;L^{2}(0,T;H^{1})).
In order to apply Theorem
6
and Theorem
7
we need some energy estimates in
W^{s,r}(0,T;H^{-\beta})
,
s\geq 0,r\geq 2,\beta>0
satisfying suitable conditions. To this end we first prove the following Lemma.

Lemma 11

For each
M\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists a constant
C
independent of
n
such that for all
0\leq s\leq t\leq T
it holds

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\langle\omega^{n}_{t}-\omega^{n}_{s},e_{l}\rangle^{M}\right]

\displaystyle\leq C\left(1+\lVert\omega_{0}\rVert^{M(2\vee(2\alpha+1))}\right)\lvert l\rvert^{2M}\lvert t-s\rvert^{M/2},

where
\alpha\in[0,1]
is the parameter in (
6
).

Proof.
It is enough to consider
\lvert l\rvert\leq n
. From the weak formulation satisfied by
\omega^{n}
it follows that

\displaystyle\langle\omega^{n}_{t}-\omega^{n}_{s},e_{l}\rangle

\displaystyle=\nu\int_{s}^{t}\langle\omega^{n}_{r},\Delta e_{l}\rangle\,dr+\int_{s}^{t}\langle g(\omega^{n}_{r}),\Delta e_{l}\rangle\,dr

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{s}^{t}\langle K[\omega^{n}_{r}]\cdot\nabla e_{l},\omega^{n}_{r}\rangle\,dr+\sum_{k}\theta_{k}\langle\sigma_{k}\cdot\nabla e_{l},f(\omega^{n}_{r})\rangle\,dW^{k}_{r}

\displaystyle=I^{1}_{s,t}+I^{2}_{s,t}+I^{3}_{s,t}+I^{4}_{s,t}.

The analysis of
I^{1}_{s,t}
and
I^{3}_{s,t}
follows arguing exactly as in
[
13
, Lemma 3.4]
and leads us to

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[(I^{1}_{s,t})^{M}\right]+\mathbb{E}\left[(I^{3}_{s,t})^{M}\right]\lesssim\lVert\omega_{0}\rVert^{M}\lvert l\rvert^{2M}\lvert t-s\rvert^{M}+\lVert\omega_{0}\rVert^{2M}\lvert l\rvert^{M}\lvert t-s\rvert^{M}.

For what concerns
I_{s,t}^{2}
with
\alpha\in[1/2,1]
(the case
\alpha\in[0,1/2]
being easier), we have by Hölder’s inequality and relation (
10
) that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[(I^{2}_{s,t})^{M}\right]

\displaystyle\lesssim\mathbb{E}\left[\left\lvert\int_{s}^{t}\langle g(\omega^{n}_{r}),\Delta e_{l}\rangle\,dr\right\rvert^{M}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\left[\left\lvert\int_{s}^{t}\lVert g(\omega^{n}_{r})\rVert_{L^{1}}\lVert\Delta e_{l}\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\,dr\right\rvert^{M}\right]

\displaystyle\lesssim\lvert l\rvert^{2M}\,\mathbb{E}\left[\left\lvert\int_{s}^{t}\lVert 1+\lvert\omega^{n}_{r}\rvert^{2\alpha+1}\rVert_{L^{1}}\,dr\right\rvert^{M}\right]

\displaystyle\lesssim\lvert l\rvert^{2M}\left(\lvert t-s\rvert^{M}+\mathbb{E}\left[\left\lvert\int_{s}^{t}\lVert\omega^{n}_{r}\rVert_{L^{2\alpha+1}}^{2\alpha+1}\,dr\right\rvert^{M}\right]\right).

Next, by Sobolev embedding theorem and interpolation inequalities,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[(I^{2}_{s,t})^{M}\right]

\displaystyle\lesssim\lvert l\rvert^{2M}\left(\lvert t-s\rvert^{M}+\mathbb{E}\left[\left\lvert\int_{s}^{t}\lVert\omega^{n}_{r}\rVert_{H^{\frac{2\alpha-1}{2\alpha+1}}}^{2\alpha+1}dr\right\rvert^{M}\right]\right)

\displaystyle\leq\lvert l\rvert^{2M}\left(\lvert t-s\rvert^{M}+\mathbb{E}\left[\left\lvert\int_{s}^{t}\lVert\nabla\omega^{n}_{r}\rVert^{2\alpha-1}\lVert\omega_{r}^{n}\rVert^{2}dr\right\rvert^{M}\right]\right),

which, combined the estimates in Lemma
10
, yields

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[(I^{2}_{s,t})^{M}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\lvert l\rvert^{2M}\left(\lvert t-s\rvert^{M}+\lvert t-s\rvert^{M(\frac{3}{2}-\alpha)}\lVert\omega_{0}\rVert^{2M}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\lvert\int_{s}^{t}\lVert\nabla\omega^{n}_{r}\rVert^{2}dr\right\rvert^{M(\alpha-\frac{1}{2})}\right]\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim\lvert l\rvert^{2M}\left(\lvert t-s\rvert^{M}+\lvert t-s\rvert^{M(\frac{3}{2}-\alpha)}\lVert\omega_{0}\rVert^{M(2\alpha+1)}\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim\lvert l\rvert^{2M}(1+\lVert\omega_{0}\rVert^{M(2\alpha+1)})\lvert t-s\rvert^{M\left(1\wedge(\frac{3}{2}-\alpha)\right)}.

Lastly we need to deal with
I_{s,t}^{4}
. Recall that
\theta\in\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{2}_{0})
fulfills
\|\theta\|_{\ell^{2}}=1
, and
\|\sigma_{k}\|_{L^{\infty}}=\sqrt{2}
; by Burkholder-Davis-Gundy inequality and estimate (
7
),

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[(I^{4}_{s,t})^{M}\right]

\displaystyle\lesssim\mathbb{E}\left[\left\lvert\sum_{k}\theta_{k}^{2}\int_{s}^{t}\langle\sigma_{k}\cdot\nabla e_{l},f(\omega^{n}_{r})\rangle^{2}\,dr\right\rvert^{M/2}\right]

\displaystyle\lesssim\mathbb{E}\left[\left\lvert\int_{s}^{t}\lVert f(\omega^{n}_{r})\nabla e_{l}\rVert_{L^{1}}^{2}\,dr\right\rvert^{M/2}\right]

\displaystyle\lesssim\lvert l\rvert^{M}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\lvert\int_{s}^{t}\lVert 1+\lvert\omega^{n}_{r}\rvert^{\alpha+1}\rVert_{L^{1}}^{2}\,dr\right\rvert^{M/2}\right].

Then, similarly as for the treatment of
I^{2}_{s,t}
, by Sobolev embedding theorem, interpolation inequalities and Lemma
10
we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[(I^{4}_{s,t})^{M}\right]

\displaystyle\lesssim\lvert l\rvert^{M}\left(\lvert t-s\rvert^{M/2}+\mathbb{E}\left[\left\lvert\int_{s}^{t}\lVert\omega^{n}_{r}\rVert_{L^{\alpha+1}}^{2(\alpha+1)}\,dr\right\rvert^{M/2}\right]\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim\lvert l\rvert^{M}\left(\lvert t-s\rvert^{M/2}+\mathbb{E}\left[\left\lvert\int_{s}^{t}\lVert\omega^{n}_{r}\rVert^{2(\alpha+1)}\,dr\right\rvert^{M/2}\right]\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim\lvert l\rvert^{M}\lvert t-s\rvert^{M/2}\left(1+\lVert\omega_{0}\rVert^{M(\alpha+1)}\right).

Combining the estimates the thesis follows.

By Theorem
6
, a set bounded in
L^{2}(0,T;H^{1})\cap W^{s,r}(0,T;H^{-\gamma})
is relatively compact in
L^{2}(0,T;H^{1-\delta})
for each
\delta>0
if
s>0,\gamma>0,r\geq 2.
On the other side, given
\delta>0
, if
p>\frac{r_{1}}{\delta(s_{1}r_{1}-1)(\beta-\delta)}
, a set bounded in
L^{p}(0,T;L^{2})\cap W^{s_{1},r_{1}}(0,T;H^{-\beta})
with
s_{1}r_{1}>1
is relatively compact in
C(0,T;H^{-\delta})
. Since by Lemma
10
we can take
p
arbitrarily large, it is enough to show the boundedness of
\{\omega^{n}\}_{n}
in
W^{s_{1},r_{1}}(0,T;H^{-\beta})
for some
\beta.
This is guaranteed by the lemma below.

Lemma 12

If
\beta>3+\frac{2}{r_{1}},\ s_{1}<\frac{1}{2},\ s_{1}r_{1}>1
there exists a constant
C
independent of
n
such that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}\lVert\nabla\omega^{n}_{s}\rVert^{2}ds\right]+\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}\lVert\omega^{n}_{s}\rVert^{p}ds\right]+\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}dt\int_{0}^{T}ds\frac{\lVert\omega^{n}_{t}-\omega^{n}_{s}\rVert_{H^{-\beta}}^{r_{1}}}{|t-s|^{1+r_{1}s_{1}}}\right]\leq C.

Proof.
Thanks to Lemma
10
we need just to consider

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}dt\int_{0}^{T}ds\frac{\lVert\omega^{n}_{t}-\omega^{n}_{s}\rVert_{H^{-\beta}}^{r_{1}}}{|t-s|^{1+r_{1}s_{1}}}\right].

By Fubini theorem it follows that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}dt\int_{0}^{T}ds\frac{\lVert\omega^{n}_{t}-\omega^{n}_{s}\rVert_{H^{-\beta}}^{r_{1}}}{|t-s|^{1+r_{1}s_{1}}}\right]

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}dt\int_{0}^{T}ds\frac{\mathbb{E}\left[\lVert\omega^{n}_{t}-\omega^{n}_{s}\rVert_{H^{-\beta}}^{r_{1}}\right]}{|t-s|^{1+r_{1}s_{1}}}.

Let us understand better
\mathbb{E}\left[\lVert\omega^{n}_{t}-\omega^{n}_{s}\rVert_{H^{-\beta}}^{r_{1}}\right]
: by the definition of Sobolev norms and Hölder’s inequality,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\lVert\omega^{n}_{t}-\omega^{n}_{s}\rVert_{H^{-\beta}}^{r_{1}}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}_{0}}\frac{\langle\omega^{n}_{t}-\omega^{n}_{s},e_{l}\rangle^{2}}{\lvert l\rvert^{2\beta}}\right)^{r_{1}/2}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}_{0}}\frac{\langle\omega^{n}_{t}-\omega^{n}_{s},e_{l}\rangle^{2}}{\lvert l\rvert^{2(\beta-\frac{(1+\epsilon)(r_{1}-2)}{r_{1}})}\lvert l\rvert^{2(\frac{(1+\epsilon)(r_{1}-2)}{r_{1}})}}\right)^{r_{1}/2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}_{0}}\frac{1}{\lvert l\rvert^{2(1+\epsilon)}}\right)^{(r_{1}-2)/2}\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}_{0}}\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{\langle\omega^{n}_{t}-\omega^{n}_{s},e_{l}\rangle^{r_{1}}}{\lvert l\rvert^{\beta r_{1}-(1+\epsilon)(r_{1}-2)}}\right].

Thanks to Lemma
11
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\lVert\omega^{n}_{t}-\omega^{n}_{s}\rVert_{H^{-\beta}}^{r_{1}}\right]

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}_{0}}\frac{\left(1+\lVert\omega_{0}\rVert^{r_{1}(2\vee(2\alpha+1))}\right)\lvert l\rvert^{2r_{1}}\lvert t-s\rvert^{r_{1}/2}}{\lvert l\rvert^{\beta r_{1}-(1+\epsilon)(r_{1}-2)}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lvert t-s\rvert^{r_{1}/2}\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}_{0}}\frac{1}{\lvert l\rvert^{r_{1}(\beta-3)}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lvert t-s\rvert^{r_{1}/2}.

Therefore

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}dt\int_{0}^{T}ds\frac{\lVert\omega^{n}_{t}-\omega^{n}_{s}\rVert_{H^{-\beta}}^{r_{1}}}{|t-s|^{1+r_{1}s_{1}}}\right]

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{T}dt\int_{0}^{T}ds\frac{1}{\lvert t-s\rvert^{1+r_{1}(s_{1}-1/2)}}\lesssim 1.

The proof is complete.

Combining Lemma
12
with Theorems
6
,
7
we have the following tightness result by Markov’s inequality:

Corollary 13

The family of laws of
\omega^{n}
is tight on
C([0,T];H^{-})\cap L^{2}(0,T;H^{1-}).

4.2
Passage to the limit

Arguing as in
[
13
]
, by Skorohod’s representation Theorem, we can find, up to passing to subsequences, an auxiliary probability space, that for simplicity we continue to call
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
, and processes
(\tilde{\omega}^{n},W^{n}:=\{W^{n,k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}_{0}}),\ ({\omega},W:=\{W^{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}_{0}}),
such that

\displaystyle\tilde{\omega}^{n}\rightarrow\omega\quad\text{in }C([0,T];H^{-})\cap L^{2}(0,T;H^{1-})\quad\mathbb{P}-a.s.

\displaystyle W^{n}\rightarrow W\quad\text{in }C([0,T];\mathbb{Z}^{2}_{0})\quad\mathbb{P}-a.s.

Of course the convergence above between
W^{n}
and
W
can be seen as the uniform convergence of cylindrical Wiener processes
W^{n}=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}_{0}}e_{k}W^{n,k},\ W=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}_{0}}e_{k}W^{k}
on a suitable Hilbert space
U_{0}
. Before going on, in order to identify
\omega
as a weak solution of equation (
11
) we need further integrability properties of
\omega
. The proof of the proposition below is analogous to Lemma 3.5 in
[
13
]
, therefore we will omit the details in these notes.

Proposition 14

The process
\omega
has weakly continuous trajectories on
L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
and satisfies

\displaystyle\operatorname{sup}_{t\in[0,T]}\lVert\omega_{t}\rVert^{2}\leq\lVert\omega_{0}\rVert^{2}\quad\mathbb{P}-a.s.

\displaystyle 2\nu\int_{0}^{T}\lVert\nabla\omega_{s}\rVert^{2}ds\leq\lVert\omega_{0}\rVert^{2}\quad\mathbb{P}-a.s.

Now we are ready to prove Theorem
2
.

Proof of Theorem
2
.

Let
\phi\in\Pi^{M}(L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2}))
, by classical arguments for each
n\geq M
,
\tilde{\omega}^{n}
satisfies the following weak formulation:
\mathbb{P}
-a.s. for all
t\in[0,T]
,

\displaystyle\langle\tilde{\omega}^{n}_{t}-\omega^{n}_{0},\phi\rangle

\displaystyle=\nu\int_{0}^{t}\langle\tilde{\omega}^{n}_{s},\Delta\phi\rangle\,ds+\int_{0}^{t}\langle g(\tilde{\omega}^{n}_{s}),\Delta\phi\rangle\,ds

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{0}^{t}\langle\tilde{\omega}^{n}_{s},K[\tilde{\omega}^{n}_{s}]\cdot\nabla\phi\rangle\,ds+\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}_{0}}\int_{0}^{t}\theta_{k}\langle f(\tilde{\omega}^{n}_{s}),\sigma_{k}\cdot\nabla\phi\rangle\,dW^{n,k}_{s}.

Therefore we will show, up to passing to a further subsequence,
\mathbb{P}
-a.s. convergence of all the terms appearing above, uniformly in time. Indeed,

\displaystyle\operatorname{sup}_{t\in[0,T]}\lvert\langle\tilde{\omega}^{n}_{t}-\omega_{t},\phi\rangle\rvert\leq\lVert\tilde{\omega}^{n}-\omega\rVert_{C([0,T];H^{-})}\lVert\phi\rVert_{H^{1}}\rightarrow 0\quad\mathbb{P}\mbox{-a.s.}

(19)

and similarly for the initial conditions. Next,

\displaystyle\operatorname{sup}_{t\in[0,T]}\left\lvert\int_{0}^{t}\langle\tilde{\omega}^{n}_{s}-\omega_{s},\Delta\phi\rangle\,ds\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\lVert\phi\rVert_{H^{2}}\int_{0}^{T}\lVert\omega_{s}-\tilde{\omega}^{n}_{s}\rVert\,ds\rightarrow 0\quad\mathbb{P}\mbox{-a.s.}

(20)

due to the almost surely convergence in
L^{2}(0,T;H^{1-})
. Moreover,

\displaystyle\operatorname{sup}_{t\in[0,T]}\left\lvert\int_{0}^{t}\langle\tilde{\omega}^{n}_{s},K[\tilde{\omega}^{n}_{s}]\cdot\nabla\phi\rangle-\langle\omega_{s},K[\omega_{s}]\cdot\nabla\phi\rangle\,ds\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{T}\lvert\langle\tilde{\omega}^{n}_{s},(K[\tilde{\omega}^{n}_{s}]-K[\omega_{s}])\cdot\nabla\phi\rangle\rvert\,ds+\int_{0}^{T}\lvert\langle\tilde{\omega}^{n}_{s}-\omega_{s},K[\omega_{s}]\cdot\nabla\phi\rangle\rvert\,ds

\displaystyle\lesssim\lVert\phi\rVert_{W^{1,\infty}}\lVert\omega_{0}\rVert\int_{0}^{T}\lVert\tilde{\omega}^{n}_{s}-\omega_{s}\rVert ds\rightarrow 0\quad\mathbb{P}\mbox{-a.s.}

(21)

due to the almost surely convergence in
L^{2}(0,T;H^{1-})
. Thanks to relation (
10
) it follows that

\displaystyle\operatorname{sup}_{t\in[0,T]}\left\lvert\int_{0}^{t}\langle g(\tilde{\omega}^{n}_{s})-g(\omega_{s}),\Delta\phi\rangle\,ds\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\lVert\Delta\phi\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\int_{0}^{T}\left(\left\lVert\left\lvert\tilde{\omega}^{n}_{s}-\omega_{s}\right\rvert\big(1+|\omega_{s}|^{2\alpha}\big)\right\rVert_{L^{1}}+\left\lVert\left\lvert\tilde{\omega}^{n}_{s}\right\rvert\big||\tilde{\omega}^{n}_{s}|^{2\alpha}-|\omega_{s}|^{2\alpha}\big|\right\rVert_{L^{1}}\right)ds

\displaystyle=\lVert\phi\rVert_{W^{2,\infty}}(I_{1}+I_{2}),

(22)

where

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}\left\lVert\left\lvert\tilde{\omega}^{n}_{s}-\omega_{s}\right\rvert\big(1+|\omega_{s}|^{2\alpha}\big)\right\rVert_{L^{1}}\,ds,

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}\big\lVert\left\lvert\tilde{\omega}^{n}_{s}\right\rvert\left(\left\lvert\tilde{\omega}^{n}_{s}\right\rvert^{\alpha}-\left\lvert\omega_{s}\right\rvert^{\alpha}\right)\left(\left\lvert\tilde{\omega}^{n}_{s}\right\rvert^{\alpha}+\left\lvert\omega_{s}\right\rvert^{\alpha}\right)\big\rVert_{L^{1}}\,ds.

Let us show that,
\mathbb{P}
-a.s., both
I_{1}
and
I_{2}
tend to
0
.
We can control
I_{1}
thanks to Hölder inequality, Sobolev embedding theorem, interpolation inequalities,

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{T}\lVert\tilde{\omega}^{n}_{s}-\omega_{s}\rVert\left(1+\lVert\omega_{s}\rVert_{L^{4\alpha}}^{2\alpha}\right)ds

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{T}\lVert\tilde{\omega}^{n}_{s}-\omega_{s}\rVert\left(1+\lVert\omega_{s}\rVert_{H^{\frac{2\alpha-1}{2\alpha}}}^{2\alpha}\right)ds

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{T}\lVert\tilde{\omega}^{n}_{s}-\omega_{s}\rVert\left(1+\lVert\omega_{s}\rVert_{H^{1}}^{2\alpha-1}\lVert\omega_{s}\rVert\right)ds.

By Lemma
9
, we have for
\alpha\in(1/2,1]
(the other case being easier) that

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lVert\tilde{\omega}^{n}-\omega\rVert_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2})}+\lVert\omega_{0}\rVert\,\lVert\omega\rVert_{L^{2}(0,T;H^{1})}^{2\alpha-1}\lVert\tilde{\omega}^{n}-\omega\rVert_{L^{\frac{2}{3-2\alpha}}(0,T;L^{2})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lVert\tilde{\omega}^{n}-\omega\rVert_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2})}+\lVert\omega_{0}\rVert^{2\alpha}\lVert\tilde{\omega}^{n}-\omega\rVert_{L^{\frac{2}{3-2\alpha}}(0,T;L^{2})}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathbb{P}\mbox{\footnotesize-a.s.}}}{{\longrightarrow}}0.

(23)

For what concerns
I_{2}
similar arguments and the Hölderianity of
x^{\alpha}
lead to

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{T}\lVert\tilde{\omega}^{n}_{s}\rVert\left\lVert\left\lvert\tilde{\omega}^{n}_{s}-\omega_{s}\right\rvert^{\alpha}\left(\left\lvert\tilde{\omega}^{n}_{s}\right\rvert^{\alpha}+\left\lvert\omega_{s}\right\rvert^{\alpha}\right)\right\rVert

5.1
Tightness

The way of showing the tightness is completely analogous to Section
4
thanks to Proposition

Lemma 15

For each
M\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists a constant
C
independent of
N
such that for any
s,t
with
0\leq s\leq t\leq T
, it holds

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\langle\omega^{N}_{t}-\omega^{N}_{s},e_{l}\rangle^{M}\right]

\displaystyle\leq C\left(1+\lVert\omega_{0}\rVert^{M(2\vee(2\alpha+1))}\right)\lvert l\rvert^{2M}\lvert t-s\rvert^{M/2}.

Proof.
From the weak formulation satisfied by
\omega^{N}
it follows that

\displaystyle\langle\omega^{N}_{t}-\omega^{N}_{s},e_{l}\rangle

\displaystyle=\nu\int_{s}^{t}\langle\omega^{N}_{r},\Delta e_{l}\rangle\,dr+\int_{s}^{t}\langle g(\omega^{N}_{r}),\Delta e_{l}\rangle\,dr

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{s}^{t}\langle K[\omega^{N}_{r}]\cdot\nabla e_{l},\omega^{N}_{r}\rangle dr+\sum_{k}\theta^{N}_{k}\langle\sigma_{k}\cdot\nabla e_{l},f(\omega^{N}_{r})\rangle\,dW^{k}_{r}

\displaystyle=I^{1}_{s,t}+I^{2}_{s,t}+I^{3}_{s,t}+I^{4}_{s,t}.

All the terms above can be treated analogously to Lemma
11
, leading us to the following estimates:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[(I^{1}_{s,t})^{M}\right]+\mathbb{E}\left[(I^{3}_{s,t})^{M}\right]

\displaystyle\lesssim\lVert\omega_{0}\rVert^{M}\lvert l\rvert^{2M}\lvert t-s\rvert^{M}+\lVert\omega_{0}\rVert^{2M}\lvert l\rvert^{M}\lvert t-s\rvert^{M},

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[(I^{2}_{s,t})^{M}\right]

\displaystyle\lesssim\lvert l\rvert^{2M}(1+\lVert\omega_{0}\rVert^{M(2\alpha+1)})\lvert t-s\rvert^{M\left(1\wedge(\frac{3}{2}-\alpha)\right)},

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[(I^{4}_{s,t})^{M}\right]

\displaystyle\lesssim\lvert l\rvert^{M}\lvert t-s\rvert^{M/2}\big(1+\lVert\omega_{0}\rVert^{M(\alpha+1)}\big).

Combining them the thesis follows immediately.

Thanks to the discussion before Lemma

in order to obtain the required tightness in

We omit its proof since it is just a computation based on the definition of the Sobolev norms and the estimate guaranteed by Lemma

Proposition 18

Before exploiting the convergence properties of

Lemma 19

There exists at most one solution of (

Proof.
First note that, arguing for example as in

to time-dependent test functions in

our weak formulation becomes: for any

and

\displaystyle\langle\overline{\omega}_{t},\phi_{t}\rangle-\langle\omega_{0},\phi_{0}\rangle

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\langle\overline{\omega}_{s},\partial_{s}\phi_{s}\rangle\,ds+\nu\int_{0}^{t}\langle\overline{\omega}_{s},\Delta\phi_{s}\rangle\,ds

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{0}^{t}\langle g(\overline{\omega}_{s}),\Delta\phi_{s}\rangle\,ds+\int_{0}^{t}\langle\overline{\omega}_{s},K[\overline{\omega}_{s}]\cdot\nabla\phi_{s}\rangle\,ds.

Consider now two weak solutions
\omega,\tilde{\omega}\in C_{w}([0,T];L^{2})\cap L^{2}(0,T;H^{1})
.
Looking at the equation and exploiting the regularity of the weak solutions, it follows that actually
\omega,\tilde{\omega}\in C_{w}([0,T];L^{2})\cap W^{1,2}(0,T;H^{-2})
. Let
w_{t}=\omega_{t}-\tilde{\omega}_{t}
, then

\displaystyle\psi=(-\Delta)^{-1}w\in C_{w}([0,T];H^{2})\cap L^{2}(0,T;H^{3})\cap W^{1,2}(0,T;L^{2})

is a proper test function and we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\lVert w_{t}\rVert_{H^{-1}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\lVert\psi_{t}\rVert_{H^{1}}^{2}

\displaystyle=-\nu\int_{0}^{t}\lVert w_{s}\rVert^{2}ds-\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}\left(g(\omega_{s})-g(\tilde{\omega}_{s})\right)\left(\omega_{s}-\tilde{\omega}_{s}\right)dxds

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}w_{s}K[\omega_{s}]\cdot\nabla\psi_{s}\,dxds+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}\tilde{\omega}_{s}K[w_{s}]\cdot\nabla\psi_{s}\,dxds.

Thanks to the fact that the function

is monotone increasing, we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}\left(g(\omega_{s})-g(\tilde{\omega}_{s})\right)\left(\omega_{s}-\tilde{\omega}_{s}\right)dxds\geq 0.

Next, by the definition of
\psi
and integrating by parts,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}w_{s}K[\omega_{s}]\cdot\nabla\psi_{s}\,dx

\displaystyle=-\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}(\Delta\psi_{s})\,K[\omega_{s}]\cdot\nabla\psi_{s}\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}(\nabla\psi_{s})\cdot\nabla(K[\omega_{s}]\cdot\nabla\psi_{s})\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}(\nabla\psi_{s})\cdot\big((\nabla K[\omega_{s}])\cdot\nabla\psi_{s}\big)\,dx

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}(\nabla\psi_{s})\cdot\big(K[\omega_{s}]\cdot\nabla(\nabla\psi_{s})\big)\,dx;

the last integral vanishes since
K[\omega_{s}]
is divergence free. Therefore,
we can proceed as in the proof of
[
1

and obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\lVert\psi_{t}\rVert_{H^{1}}^{2}+\nu\int_{0}^{t}\lVert w_{s}\rVert^{2}\,ds

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}\lvert\nabla K[\omega_{s}]\rvert\,\lvert\nabla\psi_{s}\rvert^{2}\,dxds+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}|\tilde{\omega}_{s}|\,|K[w_{s}]|\,|\nabla\psi_{s}|\,dxds

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{t}\big(\lVert\nabla K[\omega_{s}]\rVert\,\lVert\nabla\psi_{s}\rVert_{L^{4}}^{2}+\lVert\tilde{\omega}_{s}\rVert\,\lVert\nabla\psi_{s}\rVert_{L^{4}}\lVert K[\operatorname{div}\nabla\psi_{s}]\rVert_{L^{4}}\big)\,ds.

We remark that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\lVert\psi_{t}\rVert_{H^{1}}^{2}+\nu\int_{0}^{t}\lVert w_{s}\rVert^{2}\,ds

\displaystyle\lesssim\lVert\nabla K\rVert_{L^{2}\rightarrow L^{2}}\int_{0}^{t}\lVert\omega_{s}\rVert\,\lVert\nabla\psi_{s}\rVert_{L^{4}}^{2}\,ds

\displaystyle\quad+\lVert K\operatorname{div}\rVert_{L^{4}\rightarrow L^{4}}\int_{0}^{t}\lVert\tilde{\omega}_{s}\rVert\,\lVert\nabla\psi_{s}\rVert_{L^{4}}^{2}\,ds

\displaystyle\leq C_{\nu}\!\int_{0}^{t}\!\!\left(\lVert\omega_{s}\rVert^{2}+\lVert\tilde{\omega}_{s}\rVert^{2}\right)\lVert\psi_{s}\rVert_{H^{1}}^{2}ds+\frac{\nu}{2}\!\int_{0}^{t}\lVert w_{s}\rVert^{2}\,ds.

\displaystyle J

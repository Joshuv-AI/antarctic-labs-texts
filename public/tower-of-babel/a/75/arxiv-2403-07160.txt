1.
Introduction

Self-adjointness for operators in a Hilbert space is a fundamental property as, in conjunction with the spectral theorem, it permits the construction of particular functions of a self-adjoint operator such as unitary groups, semigroups, cosine and sine functions, etc., which are basic in solving standard initial value problems which are first-order (such as heat or Schrödinger equation) and second-order (such as the wave equation) with respect to the time variable.

Before describing the principal topic of this paper, we briefly pause to introduce the basic underlying notions: Let
{\mathcal{H}}
denote a complex, separable Hilbert space. Then a linear operator
T
in
{\mathcal{H}}
with domain
\operatorname{dom}(T)\subseteq{\mathcal{H}}
dense in
{\mathcal{H}}
, that is,
\overline{\operatorname{dom}(T)}={\mathcal{H}}
, is called
symmetric
, if
T
is a restriction of its
adjoint

T^{*}
(equivalently,
T^{*}
is an extension of
T
), in symbols,
T\subseteq T^{*}
. Explicitly, this entails

(1.1)

\operatorname{dom}(T)\subseteq\operatorname{dom}(T^{*})\,\text{ and }\,Tf=T^{*}f,\quad f\in\operatorname{dom}(T).

T
is called
self-adjoint
if
T=T^{*}
. Moreover,
T
is called
essentially self-adjoint
, if the
closure
of
T
, denoted by
\overline{T}
, and given by the double adjoint
(T^{*})^{*}
(i.e.,
\overline{T}=(T^{*})^{*}
), is self-adjoint. Since taking the closure of a (closable) operator basically follows a standard procedure involving Cauchy sequences, proving that an operator is essentially self-adjoint is typically the major step toward proving self-adjointness after an operator is recognized to be symmetric.

In the following, the underlying Hilbert space
{\mathcal{H}}
will be the concrete Lebesgue space
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};d^{n}x)
,
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
n\geq 2
, with
d^{n}x
denoting the Lebesgue measure on
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, and the operator
T
in question will be the strongly singular, homogeneous differential operator of the type

(1.2)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&T_{2}(c)f=\big(\left(\Delta^{2}+c|x|^{-4}\right)f\big)(x),\quad x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\backslash\{0\},\quad c\in{\mathbb{R}},\\
&f\in\operatorname{dom}(T_{2}(c))=C_{0}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}\backslash\{0\}).\end{split}

in
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};d^{n}x)
. In one of our principal results we will prove that

(1.3)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\text{$T_{2}(c)=\left(\Delta^{2}+c|x|^{-4}\right)\big|_{C_{0}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}\backslash\{0\})}$ is essentially self-adjoint in $L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};d^{n}x)$}\\
&\quad\text{if and only if }\,c\geq\begin{cases}3(n+2)(6-n)&\mbox{for $2\leq n\leq 5$},\\[5.0pt]
{\displaystyle-\frac{n(n+4)(n-4)(n-8)}{16}}&\mbox{for $n\geq 6$}.\end{cases}\end{split}

In particular, in the special case
c=0
,

(1.4)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\text{$\Delta^{2}\big|_{C_{0}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}\backslash\{0\})}$ is essentially self-adjoint in
$L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};d^{n}x)$}\\
&\quad\text{if and only if $n\geq 8$}.\end{split}

Put differently, (
1.3
) and (
1.4
) assert that
C_{0}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}\backslash\{0\})
is an operator core for the closure,
\overline{T_{2}(c)}
, of

\overline{T_{2}(c)}
represents the natural self-adjoint operator associated with

in

Via separation of variables, our proof of (

We consider essential self-adjointness on the space

of differential operators associated with differential expressions of the type

(2.1)

\tau_{2}(c_{1},c_{2}):=\frac{d^{4}}{dr^{4}}+c_{1}\left(\frac{1}{r^{2}}\frac{d^{2}}{dr^{2}}+\frac{d^{2}}{dr^{2}}\frac{1}{r^{2}}\right)+\frac{c_{2}}{r^{4}},\quad r\in(0,\infty),\quad(c_{1},c_{2})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2},

in
L^{2}((0,\infty);dr)
. We note that for

,
(c_{1},c_{2})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
, and

(2.2)

\tau_{2}(c_{1},c_{2})r^{z}=D_{2}(c_{1},c_{1};z)r^{z-4},

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
(c_{1},c_{2})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}

has a root with real part equal to

(45+12c_{1}+c_{1}^{2}-c_{2})\bigg(\frac{105}{16}+\frac{19}{2}c_{1}+c_{2}\bigg)=0.

Proof.

Consider the polynomial
\widetilde{D}_{2}(c_{1},c_{2};\,\cdot\,)
given by

(2.5)

\widetilde{D}_{2}(c_{1},c_{2};z):=D_{2}(c_{1},c_{1};z-1/2),\quad z\in{\mathbb{C}};

one notes that
\widetilde{D}_{2}(c_{1},c_{2};\,\cdot\,)
has real coefficients. Explicitly, for

, one has

(2.6)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\widetilde{D}_{2}(c_{1},c_{2};z)\\
&\quad=z^{4}-8z^{3}+\left(\frac{43}{2}+2c_{1}\right)z^{2}+\left(-22-8c_{1}\right)z+\left(\frac{105}{16}+\frac{19}{2}c_{1}+c_{2}\right),\end{split}

and hence the Hurwitz matrix (see,
[
4
, § XV.6]
) associated with
\widetilde{D}_{2}(c_{1},c_{2};\,\cdot\,)
is of the form

(2.7)

H_{2}(c_{1},c_{2}):=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}-8&-22-8c_{1}&0&0\\[5.0pt]
1&\frac{43}{2}+2c_{1}&\frac{105}{16}+\frac{19}{2}c_{1}+c_{2}&0\\[5.0pt]

0&1&\frac{43}{2}+2c_{1}&\frac{105}{16}+\frac{19}{2}c_{1}+c_{2}\\[5.0pt]
\end{array}\right).

The determinant of

can be found by expansion along the last column:

(2.8)

\det(H_{2}(c_{1},c_{2}))=64(45+12c_{1}+c_{1}^{2}-c_{2})\bigg(\frac{105}{16}+\frac{19}{2}c_{1}+c_{2}\bigg).

Suppose

has a root with with real part equal to
-1/2
. Then

has a root

By (

2.4
) is satisfied.

3.
The Fourth-Order Strongly Singular Homogeneous

Differential Operator

\big(\Delta^{2}+c|x|^{-4}\big)\big|_{C_{0}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}\backslash\{0\})}
,
n\geq 2

In the following,

(3.1)

\Delta:=\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x_{1}^{2}}+\cdots+\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x_{n}^{2}},\quad x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n},\;n\in{\mathbb{N}},

represents the Laplacian on
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
.
We consider essential self-adjointness on the space
C_{0}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}\backslash\{0\})

of differential operators associated with differential expressions of the type

(3.2)

\Delta^{2}+c|x|^{-4},\quad x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\backslash\{0\},\quad n\in{\mathbb{N}},\;n\geq 2,\quad c\in{\mathbb{R}},

in
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};d^{n}x)
.

In addition, we introduce

(3.3)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\tau_{2,n,\ell}(c):=\left[\frac{d^{2}}{dr^{2}}-\frac{(n+2\ell-1)(n+2\ell-3)}{4r^{2}}\right]^{2}+\frac{c}{r^{4}},&\\
r\in(0,\infty),\quad n\in{\mathbb{N}},\;n\geq 2,\quad\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0},\quad c\in{\mathbb{R}}.&\end{split}

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
n\geq 2
. Then

\displaystyle\left(\Delta^{2}+c|x|^{-4}\right)\big|_{C_{0}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}\backslash\{0\})}\ \mbox{is essentially self-adjoint in $L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};d^{n}x)$}

(3.4)

if and only if

\displaystyle\,\tau_{2,n,\ell}(c)\big|_{C_{0}^{\infty}((0,\infty))}\ \mbox{is essentially self-adjoint in $L^{2}((0,\infty);dr)$ for all $\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}$.}

Proof.

We have a smooth diffeomorphism
\Phi:(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\backslash\{0\}

given by
\Phi(r,\theta)=r\theta
. This separation of variables then leads to a canonical decomposition

(3.5)

\displaystyle\begin{split}L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};d^{n}x)&=L^{2}((0,\infty);r^{n-1}dr)\otimes L^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{n-1};d^{n-1}\omega)\\
&=L^{2}((0,\infty);r^{n-1}dr)\otimes{\bigoplus}_{\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}\mathcal{H}_{\ell}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1}).\end{split}

Here
\mathcal{H}_{\ell}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})
is the space of spherical harmonics of degree
\ell
.
Explictely,
\mathcal{H}_{\ell}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})
consists of the linear span of all functions
Y_{\ell}(\,\cdot\,)\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})

such that

(3.6)

\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}Y_{\ell}(\theta)=-\ell(\ell+n-2)Y_{\ell}(\theta),\quad\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0},

where
\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}
denotes the Laplace–Beltrami operator in
L^{2}\big(\mathbb{S}^{n-1};d^{n-1}\omega\big)
, and
d^{n-1}\omega
represents the usual surface measure on
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
.
By the well-known formula for the radial part of the Laplacian, for
f\in C_{0}^{\infty}((0,\infty))

and
Y_{\ell}\in\mathcal{H}_{\ell}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})
, one gets

(3.7)

\Delta\bigg(f(r)Y_{\ell}(\theta)\bigg)=\left(\left[\frac{d^{2}}{dr^{2}}+\frac{n-1}{r}\frac{d}{dr}-\frac{\ell(\ell+n-2)}{r^{2}}\right]f(r)\right)Y_{\ell}(\theta).

Recalling that the volume element in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
in spherical coordinates is given by

(3.8)

d^{n}x=r^{n-1}dr\,d^{n-1}\omega(\theta),

it then follows that the differential operator
\left(\Delta^{2}+c|x|^{-4}\right)\big|_{C_{0}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}\backslash\{0\})}
is essentially self-adjoint
in
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};d^{n}x)
if and only if

(3.9)

\left(\left[\frac{d^{2}}{dr^{2}}+\frac{n-1}{r}\frac{d}{dr}-\frac{\ell(\ell+n-2)}{r^{2}}\right]^{2}+\frac{c}{r^{4}}\right)\Bigg|_{C_{0}^{\infty}((0,\infty))}

is essentially self-adjoint in
L^{2}((0,\infty);r^{n-1}dr)
for all
\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
.
Next, consider the unitary map

(3.10)

U:\begin{cases}L^{2}((0,\infty);r^{n-1}dr)\to L^{2}((0,\infty);dr),\\
f\mapsto r^{(n-1)/2}f.\end{cases}

A straightforward calculation shows that

(3.11)

U\left(\left[\frac{d^{2}}{dr^{2}}+\frac{n-1}{r}\frac{d}{dr}-\frac{\ell(\ell+n-2)}{r^{2}}\right]^{2}+\frac{c}{r^{4}}\right)U^{-1}=\tau_{2,n,\ell}(c),

where
\tau_{2,n,\ell}(c)
is the differential expression given by (
3.3
).
Thus, it follows that
\big(\Delta^{2}+c|x|^{-4}\big)\big|_{C_{0}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}\backslash\{0\})}
is essentially self-adjoint
in
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};d^{n}x)
if and only if
\tau_{2,n,\ell}(c)\big|_{C_{0}^{\infty}((0,\infty))}
is essentially self-adjoint in
L^{2}((0,\infty);dr)

for all
\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
.
∎

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
n\geq 2
. Then

\displaystyle\left(\Delta^{2}+c|x|^{-4}\right)\big|_{C_{0}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}\backslash\{0\})}\ \mbox{is essentially self-adjoint in $L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};d^{n}x)$}

(3.12)

if and only if

\displaystyle\;\;c\geq\begin{cases}3(n+2)(6-n)&\mbox{for $2\leq n\leq 5$},\\[10.0pt]
{\displaystyle-\frac{n(n+4)(n-4)(n-8)}{16}}&\mbox{for $n\geq 6$}.\end{cases}

In particular, in the special case
c=0
,
\Delta^{2}\big|_{C_{0}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}\backslash\{0\})}
is essentially self-adjoint in
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};d^{n}x)
if and only if
n\geq 8
.

Proof.

Let
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
n\geq 2
.
By Lemma
3.1
, it suffices to determine for which
c\in{\mathbb{R}}
the differential operator

\tau_{2,n,\ell}(c)\big|_{C_{0}^{\infty}((0,\infty))}
is essentially self-adjoint in
L^{2}((0,\infty);dr)
for all
\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
.
A straightforward calculation shows that

(3.13)

\tau_{2,n,\ell}(c)=\tau_{2}(c_{1},c_{2}),

with

(3.14)

c_{1}=-\frac{(n+2\ell-1)(n+2\ell-3)}{4}\quad\mbox{and}\quad c_{2}=c_{1}^{2}+c.

By Theorem
2.2
, one then finds that

(3.15)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\text{\it$\tau_{2,n,\ell}(c)\big|_{C_{0}^{\infty}((0,\infty))}$ is essentially self-adjoint in $L^{2}((0,\infty);dr)$}\\
&\quad\text{if and only if $c\geq\gamma_{2,n,\ell}$},\end{split}

where

(3.16)

\gamma_{2,n,\ell}:=\begin{cases}-3(n+2\ell+2)(n+2\ell-6)\quad\mbox{for}\ (n+2\ell-1)(n+2\ell-3)\leq 11,\\[5.0pt]
\displaystyle-\frac{(n+2\ell+4)(n+2\ell)(n+2\ell-4)(n+2\ell-8)}{16}\\
\hskip 65.44142pt\mbox{for}\ (n+2\ell-1)(n+2\ell-3)\geq 11.\\
\end{cases}

One notes that when
\ell=0
, one has
(n+2\ell-1)(n+2\ell-3)=(n-1)(n-3)\leq 11
if and only if
n\leq 5

and it follows that
\gamma_{2,n,0}
is equal to the right-hand side of the inequality in (
3.12

(3.17)

\gamma_{2,n,0}=\begin{cases}3(n+2)(6-n)&\mbox{for $2\leq n\leq 5$},\\[10.0pt]
{\displaystyle-\frac{n(n+4)(n-4)(n-8)}{16}}&\mbox{for $n\geq 6$}.\end{cases}

Next, we will prove that

(3.18)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\text{\it if $\tau_{2,n,\ell}(c)\big|_{C_{0}^{\infty}((0,\infty))}$ is essentially self-adjoint for $\ell=0$,}\\
&\quad\text{\it then it is essentially self-adjoint for all $\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}$},\\
\end{split}

by showing that
\gamma_{2,n,\ell}\leq\gamma_{2,n,0}
for all
\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
.
We consider five cases; the key in each case is to rewrite
\gamma_{2,n,\ell}
for
(n+2\ell-1)(n+2\ell-3)>11
in such a way that

\gamma_{2,n,\ell}\leq\gamma_{2,n,0}
becomes obvious.

(

n=2
.
Then
\gamma_{2,n,0}=48
,
\gamma_{2,n,1}=36<48
, and

(3.19)

\gamma_{2,n,\ell}=15-(\ell+2)(\ell-2)(\ell^{2}-6)\leq 15<48\ \mbox{for $\ell\geq 2$.}

(

) Suppose

. Then

,

, and

(3.20)

\gamma_{2,n,\ell}=\frac{231}{16}-\frac{(\ell+3)(\ell-2)[2\ell(\ell+1)-7]}{2}\leq\frac{231}{16}<45\ \mbox{for $\ell\geq 2$.}

(

and

(3.21)

(

(

&\quad-\frac{\ell(\ell+n-2)[2\ell(\ell+n-2)+n(n-4)-16]}{2}.\end{split}

The second summand of the right-hand side is zero for

and negative for

. Thus, once again,

.
∎

4.
Higher-Order Powers of the Laplacian

For any
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
, it is natural to consider essential self-adjointness on the space
C_{0}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}\backslash\{0\})

of differential operators associated with strongly singular, homogeneous differential expressions of the type
1
1

1

We consider powers of
-\Delta
to ensure nonnegativity of
(-\Delta)^{m}\big|_{H^{2m}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}
.

(4.1)

(-\Delta)^{m}+c|x|^{-2m},\quad x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\backslash\{0\},\quad n\in{\mathbb{N}},\;n\geq 2,\quad m\in{\mathbb{N}},\quad c\in{\mathbb{R}},

in
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};d^{n}x)
.
By the same separation of variables argument given in Section
3
,

\displaystyle\left((-\Delta)^{m}+c|x|^{-2m}\right)\big|_{C_{0}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}\backslash\{0\})}\ \mbox{\it is essentially self-adjoint in $L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};d^{n}x)$}

(4.2)

if and only if

\displaystyle\,\tau_{m,n,\ell}(c)\big|_{C_{0}^{\infty}((0,\infty))}\ \mbox{\it is essentially self-adjoint in $L^{2}((0,\infty);dr)$ for all $\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}$.}

Here,

(4.3)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\tau_{m,n,\ell}(c):=\left[-\frac{d^{2}}{dr^{2}}+\frac{(n+2\ell-1)(n+2\ell-3)}{4r^{2}}\right]^{m}+\frac{c}{r^{2m}},&\\
m,n\in{\mathbb{N}},\;n\geq 2,\quad\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0},\quad c\in{\mathbb{R}}.&\end{split}

By induction on
m
, one obtains that for
r>0
,
c\in{\mathbb{R}}
, and
z\in{\mathbb{C}}
,

(4.4)

\tau_{m,n,\ell}(c)r^{z}=D_{m,n,\ell}(c;z)r^{z-2m},

where
D_{m,n,\ell}(c;\,\cdot\,)
is the polynomial of degree
2m
given by

(4.5)

D_{m,n,\ell}(c;z):=(-1)^{m}\prod_{j=1}^{m}\left(z-\frac{n+2\ell+4j-5}{2}\right)\left(z+\frac{n+2\ell-4j+1}{2}\right)+c.

Then, as before (see again
[
6
]
),

(4.6)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\text{\it$\tau_{m,n,\ell}(c)\big|_{C_{0}^{\infty}((0,\infty))}$ is essentially self-adjoint if and only if }\\
&\quad\text{\it exactly $m$ roots of $D_{m,n,\ell}(c;\,\cdot\,)$ have real part $\leq-1/2$}\\
&\quad\text{\it and the remaining $m$ roots have real part $>-1/2$.}\end{split}

For
c\in{\mathbb{R}}
, let the roots of
D_{m,n,\ell}(c;\,\cdot\,)
be denoted
\alpha_{m,n,\ell;j}(c)
,
j=1,\ldots,2n
.
By the continuous dependence of the roots of a polynomial on the coefficients (see

[
12
, Theorem (1.4)]
), we may choose our labelling such that each function
\alpha_{m,n,\ell;j}(\,\cdot\,)
is continuous and

(4.7)

\operatorname{Re}[\alpha_{m,n,\ell;1}(c)]\leq\operatorname{Re}[\alpha_{m,n,\ell;2}(c)]\leq\cdots\leq\operatorname{Re}[\alpha_{m,n,\ell;2m}(c)],\quad c\in{\mathbb{R}}.

It is easy to see from (
4.4
) that if
c=0
, then the roots of
D_{m,n,\ell}(c;\,\cdot\,)
are real and symmetric about
m-1/2
.
It follows that for all
c\in{\mathbb{R}}
,

(4.8)

\frac{1}{2}\left(\operatorname{Re}[\alpha_{m,n,\ell;j}(c)]+\operatorname{Re}[\alpha_{m,n,\ell;2m-j+1}(c)]\right)=m-\frac{1}{2},\quad 1\leq j\leq m.

Furthermore, using Rouché’s theorem, one shows (see
[
6
, Lemma 4.3]
)

(4.9)

\lim_{c\to-\infty}\operatorname{Re}[\alpha_{m,n,\ell;m}(c)]=m-\frac{1}{2}

and

(4.10)

\lim_{c\to+\infty}\operatorname{Re}[\alpha_{m,n,\ell;m}(c)]=-\infty.

In particular, by continuity there exist some
c\in{\mathbb{R}}
such that
\operatorname{Re}[\alpha_{m,n,\ell;m}(c)]=-1/2
,
and it becomes natural to define

(4.11)

\gamma_{m,n,\ell}:=\max\{c\in{\mathbb{R}}\mid\operatorname{Re}[\alpha_{m,n,\ell;m}(c)]=-1/2\}<\infty.

We note that if
c\geq\gamma_{m,n,\ell}
, then
\operatorname{Re}[\alpha_{m,n,\ell;m}(c)]\leq-1/2
and

\operatorname{Re}[\alpha_{m,n,\ell;m+1}(c)]>-1/2
by (
4.8
).
Thus, by (
4.6
) and (
4.7
),

(4.12)

if
c\geq\gamma_{m,n,\ell}
, then
\tau_{m,n,\ell}(c)\big|_{C_{0}^{\infty}((0,\infty))}
is essentially self-adjoint.

To say more about the constants
\gamma_{m,n,\ell}
, we can again employ the Routh–Hurwitz theory (see
[
4
, Ch. XV]
). Let
\widetilde{D}_{m,n,\ell}(c;\,\cdot\,)
be the polynomial given by

(4.13)

\widetilde{D}_{m,n,\ell}(c;z):=D_{m,n,\ell}(c;z-1/2),\quad z\in{\mathbb{C}}.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
c\in{\mathbb{R}}
. If the polynomial
D_{m,n,\ell}(c;\,\cdot\,)
has a root with real part equal to
-1/2
, then

(4.14)

\det(H_{m,n,\ell}(c))=0,

where
H_{m,n,\ell}(c)
is the
2m\times 2m
Hurwitz matrix associated to
\widetilde{D}_{m,n,\ell}(c;\,\cdot\,)
in (
4.13
).
In particular,
\gamma_{m,n,\ell}
is less or equal to the largest real root of
\det(H_{m,n,\ell}(\,\cdot\,))
.

Proof.

If
D_{m,n,\ell}(c;\,\cdot\,)
has a root with real part equal to
-1/2
, then
\widetilde{D}_{m,n,\ell}(c;\,\cdot\,)
has a root

\alpha\in{\mathbb{C}}
with
\operatorname{Re}(\alpha)=(\alpha+\overline{\alpha})/2=0
. Note that since
\widetilde{D}_{m,n,\ell}(c;\,\cdot\,)
has real coefficents,

\overline{\alpha}
is also a root of
\widetilde{D}_{m,n,\ell}(c;\,\cdot\,)
. It now follows from Orlandos formula (see
[
4
, § XV.7]
) that
\det(H_{m,n,\ell}(c))=0
.
∎

We note that
\alpha=D_{m,n,\ell}(0;-1/2)
is a (real) root of
\det(H_{m,n,\ell}(\,\cdot\,))
. Therefore, the polynomial
\det(H_{m,n,\ell}(\,\cdot\,))
can be factored as the product

(4.15)

\det(H_{m,n,\ell}(z))=\left(z-D_{m,n,\ell}(0;-1/2)\right)\cdot Q_{m,n,\ell}(z),\ z\in{\mathbb{C}},

where
Q_{m,n,\ell}(\,\cdot\,)
is a polynomial of degree
m-1
with rational coefficients.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
n\geq 2
. Then

\displaystyle\left((-\Delta)^{3}+c|x|^{-6}\right)\big|_{C_{0}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}\backslash\{0\})}\ \mbox{is essentially self-adjoint in $L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};d^{n}x)$}

(4.16)

if and only if

\displaystyle\;\;c\geq\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{64}{27}\left(7112+504n-126n^{2}+(236+12n-3n^{2})\sqrt{964+60n-15n^{2}}\right)\\[5.0pt]
\hskip 254.65207pt\mbox{for $2\leq n\leq 9$},\\[5.0pt]
\displaystyle-\frac{(n+8)(n+4)n(n-4)(n-8)(n-12)}{64}\hskip 61.17325pt\mbox{for $n\geq 10$}.\\
\end{cases}

In particular, in the special case
c=0
,
(-\Delta)^{3}\big|_{C_{0}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}\backslash\{0\})}
is essentially self-adjoint in
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};d^{n}x)
if and only if
n\geq 12
.

Proof.

One recalls that the discriminant of a quadratic polynomial
az^{2}+bz+c
in
z\in{\mathbb{C}}
with coefficients
a,b,c\in{\mathbb{R}},a\not=0
, is the quantity

(4.17)

\operatorname{Disc}_{z}(az^{2}+bz+c):=b^{2}-4ac.

We claim that if
n+2\ell\geq 11
, then
\operatorname{Disc}_{z}(Q_{3,n,\ell}(z))<0
.
To see this, suppose
n+2\ell\geq k+11
for some
k\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
. Then, with the help of a computer algebra system (CAS) such as Mathematica, one shows that

(4.18)

\operatorname{Disc}_{z}(Q_{3,n,\ell}(z))=-764411904\left(3k^{2}+60k+52\right)^{2}\left(15k^{2}+300k+476\right)<0.

It follows that if
n+2\ell\geq 12
, then
\det(H_{3,n,\ell}(\,\cdot\,))
has exactly one real root, namely
D_{3,n,\ell}(0;-1/2)
. By Lemma
4.1
, we then must have

(4.19)

\gamma_{3,n,\ell}=D_{3,n,\ell}(0;-1/2)\quad\mbox{for $n+2\ell\geq 12$}.

Again with the help of a CAS one also shows that

(4.20)

D_{3,n,\ell}(0;-1/2)-D^{3}_{n,0}(0;-1/2)=-\frac{1}{16}\ell\left(\sum_{j=0}^{5}\sum_{k=0}^{5}a_{j,k}(n-11)^{j}\ell^{k}\right),

where
a_{j,k}\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
with
a_{0,0}>0
. Therefore, by (
4.19
), we have
\gamma_{3,n,\ell}<\gamma_{3,n,0}
for
n\geq 12
and
\ell\geq 1
.
This completes the proof of the theorem for
n\geq 12
.

For
2\leq n\leq 11
, a tedious case-by-case analysis (which we omit) shows that

(4.21)

\gamma_{3,n,\ell}=\begin{cases}\mbox{largest real root of $Q_{3,n,\ell}(\,\cdot\,)$}&\mbox{for $n+2\ell\leq 9$,}\\

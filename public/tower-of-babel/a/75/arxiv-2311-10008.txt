Cubic surfaces failing the integral Hasse principle Thanks: 2020 Mathematics Subject Classification 14G12 (primary), 11D25, 14G05, 14F22 (secondary).
Julian Lyczak, Vladimir Mitankin, H. Uppal

Abstract
. We study the integral Brauer–Manin obstruction for affine diagonal cubic surfaces, which we employ to construct the first counterexamples to the integral Hasse principle in this setting. We then count in three natural ways how such counterexamples are distributed across the family of affine diagonal cubic surfaces and how often such surfaces satisfy integral strong approximation off ∞ \infty .



1.
Introduction

This article is devoted to the study of integers represented by diagonal ternary cubic forms. A special case of this corresponds to the famous unsolved problem of which integers are the sum of three cubes, studied by Jacobi
[
Dic66
]
, Mordell
[
Mor42
]
, Heath-Brown
[
HB92
]
, Colliot-Thélène and Wittenberg
[
CTW12
]
, Booker
[
Boo19
]
, Booker and Sutherland
[
BS21
]
, Wang
[
Wan17
]
among many others. Our main results show for the first time that local representations do not suffice for the existence of an integral representation, i.e. the integral Hasse principle does not hold in this setting. We construct the first examples of diagonal ternary cubic forms representing an integer over
\mathbb{Q}
but for which the integral Hasse principle fails. In fact, we give two infinite families of such examples in §
6
and §
8
.

To set up the framework for our investigation, let
a_{0},a_{1},a_{2},a_{3}
be non-zero integers such that
\gcd(a_{1},a_{2},a_{3})=1
and denote by
U
the smooth affine surface over
\mathbb{Q}
given by

(1.1)

U:\quad a_{1}u_{1}^{3}+a_{2}u_{2}^{3}+a_{3}u_{3}^{3}=a_{0}.

We fix an integral model
\mathcal{U}
of
U
over
\mathbb{Z}
, defined by the same equation. Integral representations of
a_{0}
correspond to integral points on
\mathcal{U}
, whose set will be denoted by
\mathcal{U}(\mathbb{Z})
. A collection of local representations of
a_{0}
at all places of
\mathbb{Q}
corresponds to a point in the adelic space
\mathcal{U}(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Z}})=U(\mathbb{R})\times\prod_{p\neq\infty}\mathcal{U}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
. A necessary condition for
\mathcal{U}(\mathbb{Z})\neq\emptyset
is
\mathcal{U}(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Z}})\neq\emptyset
, if this condition is also sufficient then the
integral Hasse principle
holds. If
p^{k}\mid\gcd(a_{1},a_{2},a_{3})
for some
k>0
but
p^{k}\nmid a_{0}
, then clearly
\mathcal{U}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})=\emptyset
and questions about local-global principles are trivial. Moreover,
\gcd(a_{1},a_{2},a_{3})=1
implies
\gcd(a_{0},a_{1},a_{2},a_{3})=1
and thus ensures the existence of a
\mathbb{Z}
-model defined as above. On the other hand,
\mathcal{U}
is said to be an
(integral) Hasse failure
if
\mathcal{U}(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Z}})\neq\emptyset
but
\mathcal{U}(\mathbb{Z})=\emptyset
. If the closure of
\mathcal{U}(\mathbb{Z})
in the
finite adeles

\prod_{p\neq\infty}\mathcal{U}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
coincides with this set, then
integral strong approximation (ISA) off
\infty
holds for
\mathcal{U}
.

The Brauer–Manin obstruction
[
Man71
]
is a powerful tool to study local-to-global principles. Its wide applications and the conjecture of Colliot-Thélène
[
CT03
, p. 174]
claiming that it is capable of explaining all failures of the Hasse principle and weak approximation for a wide class of algebraic varieties testify for that. An integral version of this tool was developed by Colliot-Thélène and Xu in
[
CTX09
]
. They defined an integral Brauer–Manin set
\mathcal{U}(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Z}})^{\Br}
by taking those integral adeles that pair to zero with the Brauer group
\Br U
of
U
and thus obtained a chain of inclusions
\mathcal{U}(\mathbb{Z})\subseteq\mathcal{U}(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Z}})^{\Br}\subseteq\mathcal{U}(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Z}})
. A Brauer–Manin obstruction to the integral Hasse principle is present if
\mathcal{U}(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Z}})\neq\emptyset
but
\mathcal{U}(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Z}})^{\Br}=\emptyset
forcing
\mathcal{U}(\mathbb{Z})=\emptyset
. It obstructs strong approximation off
\infty
if the image of
\mathcal{U}(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Z}})^{\Br}
under the projection to
\prod_{p\neq\infty}\mathcal{U}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
is a strict subset.

Main results

To study the Brauer–Manin obstruction on
U
, as given as in (
1.1
), we first need to examine
\Br U
, which we calculate in full generality for the first time. We relate
\Br U
to the Brauer group
\Br X
of its compactification
X
, which was computed in
[
CTKS87
, §1, Prop. 1]
. As
\mathbb{Q}
contains no primitive third root of unity,
\Br X
modulo constants is either trivial or
\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Assume that
a_{0},a_{1},a_{2},a_{3}\in\mathbb{Q}^{\ast}
are cube-free. Then the algebraic part of
\Br U
is isomorphic to
\Br X
. Moreover,

\Br U=\begin{cases}\Br X\oplus\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}\ &\text{if}\ a_{1}a_{2}a_{3}\equiv 2\bmod\mathbb{Q}^{\ast 3},\\
\Br X&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Building on the work of Colliot-Thélène and Wittenberg
[
CTW12
]
, our approach in the proof of Theorem
1.1
yields a comprehensive understanding of the Brauer group of all smooth diagonal affine cubic surfaces. The method presented here is, in fact, capable of tacking more general surfaces. Specifically, it can be adapted to study Brauer groups of affine surfaces that are the complement of a smooth irreducible anticanonical curve on a geometrically rational surface.

We use the information obtained in Theorem
1.1
to facilitate a detailed examination of the arithmetic of
U
by employing the integral Brauer–Manin obstruction. As a result, for the first time failures of the integral Hasse principle on these surfaces are exhibited. We construct two rich infinite families of such examples in §
6
and §
8
. This was not previously possible due to the Brauer groups of these surfaces not being known in such a generality.

To explore the frequency of integral Hasse failures and to measure how often integral strong approximation off
\infty
holds in the family (
1.1
), we vary
a_{0}
, the coefficients of the cubic form
a_{1}
,
a_{2}
,
a_{3}
, and all
a_{0}
,
a_{1}
,
a_{2}
,
a_{3}
in a equal-sided box. In view of Remark
2.5
, the number of everywhere locally soluble
\mathcal{U}
up to height
B
in the three counting problems is of magnitude
B
,
B^{3}
,
B^{4}
, respectively. We give in Propositions
4.2
and
4.3
a sufficient criterion for the lack of a Brauer–Manin obstruction to the integral Hasse principle and for the presence of an Brauer–Manin obstruction to integral strong approximation off
\infty
. Counting surfaces failing this criterion allows us to obtain upper bounds for the amount of Hasse failures and simultaneously to estimate the number of surfaces satisfying strong approximation off
\infty
in all three natural counting problems.

We begin with the analogue of the sum of three cubes question, i.e. we fix
a_{1},a_{2},a_{3}
and we vary
a_{0}
. Set
\mathbb{Z}_{\prim}^{n}
for the the set of
n
-tuples in
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
with non-zero coprime coordinates. If
\mathbb{Z}_{\neq 0}
stands for the non-zero integers, for any real
B\geq 1
and
(a_{1},a_{2},a_{3})\in\mathbb{Z}_{\prim}^{3}
define

\begin{split}N_{a_{1},a_{2},a_{3}}(B)&=\#\left\{a_{0}\in[-B,B]\cap\mathbb{Z}_{\neq 0}\ :\ \mathcal{U}(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Z}})\neq\emptyset\text{ but }\mathcal{U}(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Z}})^{\Br}=\emptyset\right\},\\
N_{a_{1},a_{2},a_{3}}^{{}^{\prime}}(B)&=\#\left\{a_{0}\in[-B,B]\cap\mathbb{Z}_{\neq 0}\ :\ \mathcal{U}(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Z}})\neq\emptyset\text{ and }\mathcal{U}\text{ satisfies ISA off $\infty$}\right\}.\end{split}

Our next result provides upper bounds for these quantities.

Theorem 1.2
.

Assume that
a_{1}a_{2}a_{3}\not\equiv 2\bmod\mathbb{Q}^{\ast 3}
. We then have

N_{a_{1},a_{2},a_{3}}(B),\ N_{a_{1},a_{2},a_{3}}^{{}^{\prime}}(B)\ll_{a_{1}a_{2}a_{3}}B^{1/3},

as
B
goes to infinity.

Theorem
1.2
shows how rare Hasse failures are if the cubic form is fixed, thus revealing the difficulty of finding explicit examples of them. In fact,
N_{a_{1},a_{2},a_{3}}(B)
is zero for the two specific families
(a_{1},a_{2},a_{3})=(1,1,1)
and
(1,1,2)
studied by Colliot-Thélène and Wittenberg
[
CTW12
]
. Hence, finding the magnitude of
N_{a_{1},a_{2},a_{3}}(B)
, or even a lower bound for it, amounts to selecting specific choices of
(a_{1},a_{2},a_{3})
for which Hasse failures exist. Such choices are extremely rare, as we shall see in the next results.

We continue by varying the cubic form while
a_{0}\neq 0
stays fixed. Let

\begin{split}N_{a_{0}}(B)&=\#\left\{(a_{1},a_{2},a_{3})\in[-B,B]^{3}\cap\mathbb{Z}_{\prim}^{3}\ :\ \mathcal{U}(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Z}})\neq\emptyset\text{ but }\mathcal{U}(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Z}})^{\Br}=\emptyset\right\},\\
N_{a_{0}}^{{}^{\prime}}(B)&=\#\left\{(a_{1},a_{2},a_{3})\in[-B,B]^{3}\cap\mathbb{Z}_{\prim}^{3}\ :\ \mathcal{U}(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Z}})\neq\emptyset\text{ and }\mathcal{U}\text{ satisfies ISA off $\infty$}\right\}.\end{split}

Our methods establish upper bounds for these quantities, given in the next theorem.

Theorem 1.3
.

We have

N_{a_{0}}(B),\ N_{a_{0}}^{{}^{\prime}}(B)\ll B^{3/2},

as
B
goes to infinity.

Lastly, we vary all four coefficients of
\mathcal{U}
. For this purpose let

\begin{split}N(B)&=\#\left\{(a_{0},a_{1},a_{2},a_{3})\in[-B,B]^{4}\cap\mathbb{Z}_{\prim}^{4}\ :\ \mathcal{U}(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Z}})\neq\emptyset\text{ but }\mathcal{U}(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Z}})^{\Br}=\emptyset\right\},\\
N^{{}^{\prime}}(B)&=\#\left\{(a_{0},a_{1},a_{2},a_{3})\in[-B,B]^{4}\cap\mathbb{Z}_{\prim}^{4}\ :\ \mathcal{U}(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Z}})\neq\emptyset\text{ and }\mathcal{U}\text{ satisfies ISA off $\infty$}\right\}.\end{split}

Our final result delivers upper and lower bounds for
N(B)
, of the same magnitude modulo a small power of
\log B
, and an upper bound for
N^{{}^{\prime}}(B)
.

Theorem 1.4
.

We have

\begin{split}\frac{B^{2}}{\log B}\ll N(B)&\ll B^{2}(\log B)^{6}\quad\text{and}\quad N^{{}^{\prime}}(B)\ll B^{2}(\log B)^{2},\end{split}

as
B
goes to infinity.

We construct in §
6
and in §
8
the first examples of diagonal affine cubic surfaces with a Brauer–Manin obstruction to the integral Hasse principle. Moreover, all of the surfaces in §
6
and §
8
have a non-empty set of rational points (see Remarks
6.1
and
8.4
) and thus our examples of integral Hasse failures do not follow trivially from Hasse failures for rational points. Colliot-Thélène, Kanevsky and Sansuc constructed an infinite family of cubic surfaces, failing the Hasse principle for rational points in
[
CTKS87
, §7, Prop. 5]
. Each of their surfaces produces integral Hasse failures by taking away exactly one of the hyperplanes corresponding to the zero locus of a coordinate. It is relatively easy to see that the number of failures coming from Colliot-Thélène, Kanevsky and Sansuc’s family counted by
N(B)
is at most
B/(\log B)^{2}
. Hence, our lower bound does not follow from the results in
[
CTKS87
]
.

The lower bound of Theorem
1.4
is obtained by counting surfaces of the family featured in §
6
. At the same time, the counter-examples to the integral Hasse principle appearing in §
8
are interesting on their own right. Our approach in §
8
builds on the work of Colliot-Thélène, Kanevsky and Sansuc
[
CTKS87
]
and does not require any knowledge of explicit representatives of Brauer elements, unlike in §
6
. This is particularly handy, as explicit representatives of Brauer elements are genuinely very hard to get a hold of.

Finally, we note that the number of surfaces considered in Theorem
1.3
, whose transcendental Brauer group is non-trivial, is negligible compared to the upper bounds established there. This is shown in Proposition
7.1
. At the same time, all possible failures of the integral Hasse principle and integral strong approximation off
\infty
counted in
N_{a_{1},a_{2},a_{3}}(B)
,
N_{a_{1},a_{2},a_{3}}^{{}^{\prime}}(B)
and in
N(B)
,
N^{{}^{\prime}}(B)
may come from transcendental Brauer elements.

Integral points in families

In recent years the quantitative study of arithmetic properties in families has rapidly expanded its scope of investigation. Several papers focus on the relevance of the Brauer–Manin obstruction for rational points in families;
[
BB14b
]
,
[
Rom19
]
for Châtelet surfaces,
[
GLN22
]
,
[
San23
]
for certain classes of K3 surfaces,
[
MS22
]
for quartic del Pezzo surfaces,
[
BB14a
]
for coflasque tori, etc. It was shown in the proof of
[
BBL16
, Thm. 1.6]
that under certain geometrical assumptions weak approximation fails and at the same time the Brauer–Manin obstruction does not obstruct the Hasse principle for rational points 100% of the time, even if single members of the family might have a Brauer–Manin obstruction to the Hasse principle. No such framework exists for the study of integral points in families yet. There are very few examples studied in the literature, owing to the significance of this paper;
[
Mit17
]
,
[
San22
]
,
[
MNS22
]
studied affine quadrics surfaces,
[
Ber17
]
,
[
Mit20
]
,
[
BST22
]
considered affine generalized Châtelet surfaces and Markoff surfaces were treated in
[
GS22
]
,
[
LM21
]
,
[
CTWX20
]
.

The surface
U
is the complement of an anticanonical curve on a del Pezzo surface of degree 3. In particular, it is a log K3 surface in the sense of
[
Har17
, Def. 2.4]
. The integral Brauer–Manin obstruction was successfully employed to study integral points for such types of log K3s in
[
CTW12
]
,
[
Upp23
]
for affine cubic surfaces,
[
JS17
]
,
[
Lyc23
]
for affine del Pezzo surfaces of degree
4
and
5
and
[
LM21
]
,
[
CTWX20
]
for Markoff type log K3. The investigation on the frequency of Hasse failures for Markoff type log K3s was pioneered in
[
GS22
]
resulting in a wide open conjecture for that quantity. This conjecture was then explored in in
[
LM21
]
,
[
CTWX20
]
by measuring the amount of Hasse failures with or without a Brauer–Manin obstruction. No integral Brauer–Manin obstruction had been exhibited in the diagonal cubic case before the present work.

2.
Integral Brauer–Manin obstruction

In this section we recall the integral Brauer–Manin obstruction as introduced by Colliot-Thélène and Xu
[
CTX09
, §1]
.

Definition 2.1

(Brauer–Grothendieck group)
.

Let
V
be a scheme over a field
F
. The cohomological Brauer group of
V
is the second étale cohomology group
\Br V:=\HH^{2}_{\et}(V,\mathbb{G}_{m})
. We define the filtration

0\subseteq\Br_{0}V\subseteq\Br_{1}V\subseteq\Br V,

where
\Br_{0}V:=\text{Im}\left(\Br F\rightarrow\Br V\right)
and
\Br_{1}V:=\ker(\Br V\rightarrow\Br\bar{V})
. We call
\Br_{1}V
the
algebraic Brauer group of
V
and
\Br V/\Br_{1}V
the
transcendental Brauer group of
V
.

For all places
v\in\Omega_{K}
there exists a canonical injective map

\inv_{v}:\Br K_{v}\rightarrow\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}

[
CTS21
, Def. 13.1.7]
, whose image is
\{0,1/2\}
if
v
is a real place,
0
if it is a complex place and is an isomorphism if
v
is finite
[
CTS21
, Thm. 13.1.8]
.

Let
\mathcal{V}
be a separated scheme of finite type over
\mathcal{O}_{K}
and let
V
be its base change to
K
. For every
\alpha\in\Br V
there exists a finite set of places
S_{\alpha}\subseteq\Omega_{K}
such that the invariant map composed with evaluation
\inv_{v}\alpha:\mathcal{V}(\mathcal{O}_{v})\rightarrow\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}
is zero for all
v\not\in S_{\alpha}
,
[
CTX09
, §1]
. This shows that the Brauer–Manin pairing, as given below, is well defined:

\begin{split}\mathcal{V}(\mathbb{A}_{\mathcal{O}_{K}})\times\Br V&\longrightarrow\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z},\\
\left((x_{v})_{v},\alpha\right)&\longmapsto\sum\limits_{v\in\Omega_{K}}\inv_{v}(\alpha(x_{v})).\end{split}

Define the
integral Brauer–Manin set

\mathcal{V}(\mathbb{A}_{\mathcal{O}_{K}})^{\Br}
as the left kernel of this paring. Since the following diagram commutes:
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{V}\left(\mathcal{O}_{K}\right)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{V}\left(\mathbb{A}_{\mathcal{O}_{K}}\right)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Br K}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\bigoplus\limits_{v\in\Omega_{K}}\Br K_{v}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0,}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\alpha}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\alpha}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sum_{v}\inv_{v}}

where exactness of the bottom row is implied by the Albert–Brauer–Hasse–Noether theorem and class field theory, we get a chain of inclusions

\mathcal{V}(\mathcal{O}_{K})\subseteq\mathcal{V}(\mathbb{A}_{\mathcal{O}_{K}})^{\Br}\subseteq\mathcal{V}(\mathbb{A}_{\mathcal{O}_{K}}).

Definition 2.2
.

There is a
Brauer–Manin obstruction to the integral Hasse principle for
\mathcal{V}
, or
\mathcal{V}
is a
(integral) Hasse failure
, if
\mathcal{V}(\mathbb{A}_{\mathcal{O}_{K}})\neq\emptyset
but
\mathcal{V}(\mathbb{A}_{\mathcal{O}_{K}})^{\Br}=\emptyset
.

Even if there is no Brauer–Manin obstruction to the integral Hasse principle, the following two notions can be obstructed by the Brauer–Manin construction. This is seen in Sections
7
and
8
.

Definition 2.3
.

We say
V
satisfies
strong approximation
(resp.
weak approximation
) if the diagonal image
V(K)\hookrightarrow V(\mathbb{A}_{K})
(resp.
V(K)\hookrightarrow\prod_{v\in\Omega_{K}}V(K_{v})
) is dense. We say
\mathcal{V}
satisfies
integral strong approximation off
\infty
if the diagonal image
\mathcal{V}(\mathcal{O}_{K})\hookrightarrow\proj_{\infty}\mathcal{V}(\mathbb{A}_{\mathcal{O}_{K}})
is dense, where
\proj_{\infty}:\mathcal{V}(\mathbb{A}_{\mathcal{O}_{K}})\rightarrow\prod_{v\nmid\infty}\mathcal{V}(\mathcal{O}_{v})
means the projection to the finite adeles
\prod_{v\nmid\infty}\mathcal{V}(\mathcal{O}_{v})
.

Definition 2.4
.

There is a
Brauer–Manin obstruction to strong approximation
(resp.
integral strong approximation off
\infty
) if
V(\mathbb{A}_{K})^{\Br}
(resp.
\proj_{\infty}\mathcal{V}(\mathbb{A}_{\mathcal{O}_{K}})^{\Br}
) is a strict subset of the adeles
V(\mathbb{A}_{K})
(resp. finite integral adeles
\prod_{v\nmid\infty}\mathcal{V}(\mathcal{O}_{v})
).

Questions about local-global principles are non-trivial only for varieties with a non-empty adelic space. We discuss the amount of everywhere locally soluble surfaces in the family (
1.1
) in the next remark. For this purpose, with notation as in the introduction, for any real
B\geq 1
let

\begin{split}N_{a_{1},a_{2},a_{3}}^{\text{ELS}}(B)&=\#\left\{a_{0}\in[-B,B]\cap\mathbb{Z}_{\neq 0}\ :\ \mathcal{U}(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Z}})\neq\emptyset\right\}\text{ for }(a_{1},a_{2},a_{3})\in\mathbb{Z}_{\prim}^{3},\\
N_{a_{0}}^{\text{ELS}}(B)&=\#\left\{(a_{1},a_{2},a_{3})\in[-B,B]^{3}\cap\mathbb{Z}_{\prim}^{3}\ :\ \mathcal{U}(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Z}})\neq\emptyset\right\}\text{ for }a_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\neq 0},\\
N^{\text{ELS}}(B)&=\#\left\{(a_{0},a_{1},a_{2},a_{3})\in[-B,B]^{4}\cap\mathbb{Z}_{\prim}^{4}\ :\ \mathcal{U}(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Z}})\neq\emptyset\right\}.\end{split}

Remark 2.5
.

The limits

\lim_{B\to\infty}\frac{N_{a_{1},a_{2},a_{3}}^{\text{ELS}}(B)}{B},\quad\lim_{B\to\infty}\frac{N_{a_{0}}^{\text{ELS}}(B)}{B^{3}},\quad\lim_{B\to\infty}\frac{N^{\text{ELS}}(B)}{B^{4}}

exist and each of them equals a positive constant. This may be verified, for example, with the help of
[

3.5.
Explicit generators

For future computations it will useful to write down explicit elements for the Brauer group of diagonal affine cubic surfaces. As we have seen in the generic case this Brauer group is isomorphic to the Brauer group of its compactification which was studied by Colliot-Thélène, Sansuc and Kanevsky
[
CTKS87
]
. Unfortunately, there is no uniform generator for the family of all diagonal cubic surfaces
[
Uem14
, Thm. 1.2]
. However, in specific subfamilies one is able to write down generators. In particular, this is possible if
a_{1}=a_{2}=1
, which we will assume throughout this subsection. Colliot-Thélène and Wittenberg have made an extensive study in
[
CTW12
]
of the case where
a_{3}=1
or
a_{3}=2
. Note that the compactification
X
of
U
always has a non-empty set of rational points, namely
[0:1:-1:0]\in X(\mathbb{Q})
.

Lemma 3.15

(
[
CTKS87
, §1, Prop. 1]
)
.

If
a_{1}=a_{2}=1
and
a_{0},a_{3}
are cube-free, then

\Br X/\Br\mathbb{Q}=\begin{cases}0\ &\text{if}\ a_{0}a_{3}\ \text{or}\ a_{0}/a_{3}\ \text{are in}\ \mathbb{Q}^{\ast 3},\\
\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}\ &\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

In particular, if
\Br X/\Br\mathbb{Q}\cong\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}
, then
\Br X/\Br\mathbb{Q}
is generated by the cyclic algebra

\mathcal{A}^{\prime}=\Cores_{\mathbb{Q}(\omega)/\mathbb{Q}}\mathcal{A}\in\Br\mathbb{Q}(X),\text{ where}\quad\mathcal{A}^{\prime}=\left(\frac{a_{0}}{a_{3}},\frac{x_{1}+\omega x_{2}}{x_{1}+x_{2}}\right)_{\omega}.

Lemma 3.16
.

Assume that
a_{1}=a_{2}=1
and
a_{0},a_{3}
are cube-free. Then

\Br U=\begin{cases}\Br X\oplus\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}\ &\text{if}\ a_{3}\equiv 2\ \text{mod}\ \mathbb{Q}^{\ast 3},\\
\Br X&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

In particular, if
a_{3}\equiv 2\ \text{mod}\ \mathbb{Q}^{\ast 3}
, then the
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
factor is generated by the transcendental element

\left(a_{0}(x_{1}+x_{2}+2x_{3}),-3(x_{1}+x_{2}+2x_{3})(x_{1}+x_{2})\right)\in\Br\mathbb{Q}(U).

Proof.

The main statement is proved in Theorem
1.1
and the explicit generator for the
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
factor was determined in
[
CTW12
, Prop. 3.4]
.
∎

Remark 3.17
.

Consider the case
a_{1}=1
and
a_{2}a_{3}\equiv 2
mod
\mathbb{Q}^{*3}
. Over the field extension

the surface

By Lemma

we can deduce

. Hence, the two torsion element in

can be written as the corestriction from

of the two torsion element in Lemma
3.16
.

4.
Hilbert symbols

This section describes how to compute the local invariant map. We begin by defining Hilbert symbols and describe their relation to the invariant map. From there we give an algorithm from
[
CTKS87
]
which will enable us to compute the Brauer–Manin set for generic diagonal affine cubic surfaces. Here
U
is as defined in (
1.1
) and we keep the notational convention set up earlier.

4.1.
Construction of Hilbert symbol

Let
K
be a number field and
v
a place of
K
. Assume
K_{v}
contains a primitive
n
th root of unity
\omega_{n}
. There exists a pairing
[
Ser79
, Chap XIV, §2]

(\cdot,\cdot)_{\omega_{n}}:K_{v}^{\ast}/K_{v}^{\ast n}\times K_{v}^{\ast}/K_{v}^{\ast n}\rightarrow\Br K_{v},\ (a,b)\mapsto(a,b)_{\omega_{n}}.

In
\Br K_{v}
we have the relations

(4.1)

\begin{split}(aa^{\prime},b)_{\omega_{n}}=(a,b)_{\omega_{n}}+(a^{\prime},b)_{\omega_{n}},\quad(a,b)_{\omega_{n}}=-(b,a)_{\omega_{n}},\quad(a^{n},b)_{\omega_{n}}=0.\end{split}

We define the
Hilbert symbol

(a,b)_{\omega_{n},v}=\inv_{v}(a,b)_{\omega_{n}}\in\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}.

For any non-zero prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}
of
K
, which does not lie over any of the prime divisors of
n
, with associated place
v

(4.2)

(u,u^{\prime})_{\omega_{n},v}=0\quad\text{and}\quad(u,\pi)_{\omega_{n},v}=0\Leftrightarrow u\in K_{v}^{n},

for
u,u^{\prime}\in O_{K_{v}}^{\ast}
and
\pi\in O_{K_{v}}
a uniformiser. For
n=3
we recall some formulae from
[
CTKS87
, §4]
who identify
\tfrac{1}{3}\mathbb{Z}/\mathbb{Z}
with
\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}
, which agrees with
[
Ser79
, Chap XIV, §2]
. Throughout the rest of the paper we make the same identification. Let
\omega
be a fixed root of
x^{2}+x+1
and we fix an isomorphism
k:=\mathbb{Q}(\omega)\cong\mathbb{Q}[x]/(x^{2}+x+1)
by sending
x
to
\omega
.

(1)

Let
p
be a prime where
p\equiv 1
mod 3. As ideals in
\mathcal{O}_{k}
we have
(p)=\mathfrak{p}_{1}\mathfrak{p}_{2}
. Denote by
v
a place corresponding to the prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}_{1}
or
\mathfrak{p}_{2}
extending from the
p
-adic valuation on
\mathbb{Q}
. If
u
is a unit in the ring of integers of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}\cong k_{v}
, then we have the formula

(4.3)

(u,p)_{\omega,v}=-i\text{ mod }3

where
u^{\frac{p-1}{3}}\equiv\omega^{i}
mod
p
. In particular
(\omega,p)_{v}\equiv-\frac{p-1}{3}
mod
3
.

(2)

Let
p
be a prime where
p\equiv 2
mod 3. As ideals in
\mathcal{O}_{k}
we have
(p)=\mathfrak{p}
. Denote by
v
a place corresponding to the prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}
extending from the
p
-adic valuation on
\mathbb{Q}
. If
u
is a unit in the ring of integers of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}(\omega)\cong k_{v}
we have the formula

(4.4)

(u,p)_{\omega,v}=-i\text{ mod }3

where
u^{\frac{p^{2}-1}{3}}\equiv\omega
mod
p
. In particular we have
(\omega,p)_{\omega,v}\equiv-\frac{p^{2}-1}{3}
mod

.

(3)

Let
p=3
, then as ideals in
\mathcal{O}_{k}
we have
(3)=\mathfrak{p}^{2}
. Note
\mathcal{O}_{k}=\mathbb{Z}[\omega]
and the prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}
is generated by
\lambda^{\prime}=2\omega+1
which satisfies
\lambda^{\prime 2}=-3
. We choose the uniformizer
\lambda=\lambda^{\prime 2}\omega+\lambda^{\prime}=-3\omega+(2\omega+1)=1-\omega
, with minimal polynomial
\lambda^{2}-3\lambda+3=0
and the following relations

(4.5)

\displaystyle\omega

\displaystyle=1-\lambda,

\displaystyle 3\equiv-\lambda^{2}-\lambda\text{ mod }\lambda^{4},

\displaystyle 3

\displaystyle=-\omega^{2}\lambda^{2},

\displaystyle 2\equiv-1-\lambda^{2}-\lambda^{3}\text{ mod }\lambda^{4}.

The relations for
(-,-)_{\mathfrak{p}}
are given in
[
CTKS87
, p. 34]
, however we give a summary of the most important facts. Any element in

can be written as
\pm\lambda^{e}u
where

is a
1
-unit. We will write a
1
-unit

as
1+b_{1}\lambda+b_{2}\lambda^{2}+\ldots
with

. To compute the symbol at

with associated place

, we will only need the following information

(4.6)

\displaystyle(u_{b},u_{c})_{\omega,v}

\displaystyle=b_{1}c_{1}(b_{1}-c_{1})-b_{1}c_{2}+b_{2}c_{1},

(4.7)

\displaystyle(\lambda,u_{b})_{\omega,v}

\displaystyle=\frac{b_{1}-b_{1}^{2}}{3}+b_{1}b_{2}-b_{3}.

.

Note the relation (

and not on any

4.2.
Conditions for a Brauer–Manin obstruction

We are now in position to establish sufficient conditions for the lack of a Brauer–Manin obstruction to the integral Hasse principle on
\mathcal{U}
. This is done in Propositions
4.2
and
4.3
. We write
\mathcal{B}
for both a generator of
\Br X/\Br_{0}X
and its restriction to
U
.

Proposition 4.2
.

Assume that there exits a prime
p\neq 3
such that
p\mid a_{0}
but
p^{3}\nmid a_{0}
and
p\nmid a_{1}a_{2}a_{3}
. If
\mathcal{U}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})\neq\emptyset
, then
\inv_{p}\mathcal{B}:\mathcal{U}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})\rightarrow\frac{1}{3}\mathbb{Z}/\mathbb{Z}
is surjective.

Proof.

Under the conditions of the proposition we have
\Br X/\Br_{0}X\cong\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}
. Consider the composition

\mathcal{U}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})\xrightarrow{}{}\mathcal{X}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})\xrightarrow{\text{red}}{}E(\mathbb{F}_{p})

where
E
is the elliptic curve

a_{1}x_{1}^{3}+a_{2}x_{2}^{3}+a_{3}x_{3}^{3}=0

Clearly, this composition is surjective. The invariant map
\inv_{p}\mathcal{B}
factors as the surjective map
red
and a surjective homomorphism
E(\mathbb{F}_{p})\rightarrow\frac{1}{3}\mathbb{Z}/\mathbb{Z}
,
[
Jah14
, Chap 4, Thm 6.4 c)i)]
. By functionality we can deduce that
\inv_{p}\mathcal{B}:\mathcal{U}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})\rightarrow\frac{1}{3}\mathbb{Z}/\mathbb{Z}
is surjective.
∎

Proposition 4.3
.

Assume that there exits a prime
p\geq 17
such that
p\mid a_{i}
for some
i\in\{1,2,3\}
but
p^{3}\nmid a_{i}
and
p\nmid a_{j}
for
i\neq j
where
j\in[0,3]
. Then
\inv_{p}\mathcal{B}:\mathcal{U}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})\rightarrow\frac{1}{3}\mathbb{Z}/\mathbb{Z}
is surjective.

Proof.

The proof is very similar to the one of Proposition
4.2
with the only difference that the local invariant map now factors through the composition

\mathcal{U}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})\xrightarrow{}{}\mathcal{X}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})\xrightarrow{\text{red}}{}(E\setminus D)(\mathbb{F}_{p}),

where if
i=1
the elliptic curve is
E:a_{2}x_{2}^{3}+a_{3}x_{3}^{3}-a_{0}x_{0}^{3}=0
and
D
is the divisor on
E
given by the vanishing locus of
x_{0}
. As before this composition is surjective and hence the proof boils down to establishing that the homomorphism
(E\setminus D)(\mathbb{F}_{p})\rightarrow\frac{1}{3}\mathbb{Z}/\mathbb{Z}
is surjective. This follows from the Hasse–Weil bound and
[
Jah14
, Chap 4, Thm 6.4 c)i)]
as there are at least
(p+1-2\sqrt{p})/3-3
points with a given value of the local invariant map. This number is clearly positive provided that
p\geq 17
, which confirms our claim.
∎

Remark 6.1

and

and thus any unit mod

Here
11^{-1},17^{-1}
are the inverses of
11,17
mod
18
, respectively.

We split the sum over
d
in two sums, corresponding to the ranges
1\leq d\leq B^{1/2}
and
B^{1/2}<d\leq B
. If
B^{1/2}<d\leq B
, we may apply the trivial bound
B/d+O(1)
to the sums over
a
and over
b
. Bounding trivially the remaining sums over
d
then gives

(6.2)

\begin{split}S(B)=&\sum_{\chi,\psi\bmod 18}\frac{\bar{\chi}(17)\bar{\psi}(11)}{36}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\leq B^{1/2}\\
p\mid d\implies p\equiv 2\bmod 3\end{subarray}}\mu(d)\chi(d)\psi(d)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a,b\leq B/d\\
p\mid ab\implies p\equiv 2\bmod 3\end{subarray}}\chi(a)\psi(b)\\
&+O\left(B^{3/2}\right).\end{split}

It is clear that the two inside sums over
a
and over
b
are identical. For a real
x\geq 1
, let

T_{\chi}(x)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a\leq x\\
p\mid a\implies p\equiv 2\bmod 3\end{subarray}}\chi(a).

Consider the Dirichlet’s series
F(s,\chi)
of this sum. We employ the standard notation
s=\sigma+it
. If
\sigma>1
, the function
F(s,\chi)
can be written as an Euler product

F(s,\chi)=\sum_{p\mid a\implies p\equiv 2\bmod 3}\frac{\chi(a)}{a^{s}}=\prod_{p\equiv 2\bmod 3}\left(1-\frac{\chi(p)}{p^{s}}\right)^{-1}.

The quadratic character mod 3 may be used to detect congruences of that modulus. Let
\chi_{1}(\cdot)=\chi(\cdot)\left(\frac{\cdot}{3}\right)
. As
\chi(3)=\chi_{1}(3)=0
, the binomial series expansion shows that

(6.3)

\begin{split}F(s,\chi)&=\prod_{p}\left(1-\frac{1}{2}\left(1-\left(\frac{p}{3}\right)\right)\frac{\chi(p)}{p^{s}}\right)^{-1}=\prod_{p}\left(1-\frac{\chi(p)}{2p^{s}}+\frac{\chi_{1}(p)}{2p^{s}}\right)^{-1}\\
&=\prod_{p}\left(1-\frac{\chi(p)}{p^{s}}\right)^{-1/2}\left(1-\frac{\chi_{1}(p)}{p^{s}}\right)^{1/2}E_{p}(s)=\frac{L(s,\chi)^{1/2}}{L(s,\chi_{1})^{1/2}}E(s).\end{split}

Here
E_{p}(s)=1+O(1/p^{2s})
and
E(s)=\prod_{p}E_{p}(s)
. We conclude that
F(s,\chi)
admits an analytic continuation to
\sigma>1/2
. Moreover, since we are working with characters of fixed modulus, if
\chi
is non-trivial and if
L(s,\chi_{1})
has a Siegel zero, then for
T
sufficiently big this zero must lie to the left of
1-1/\log T
. Thus, if
\chi
is a non-trivial character mod 18, the function
F(s,\chi)
is holomorphic in the region
\sigma\geq 1-1/\log T
for big
T
.

Select
T=(\log(B/d))^{2}
,
\eta_{1}=1/T
and
\eta_{2}=1/(\log T)^{2}
. Perron’s formula
[
Tit86
, p.70, Lem 3.19]
now yields

\begin{split}T_{\chi}(B/d)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{1+\eta_{1}-iT}^{1+\eta_{1}+iT}F(s,\chi)\frac{(B/d)^{s}}{s}ds+O\left(\frac{B}{d\log(B/d)}\right).\end{split}

We move the integration to the contour from
1+\eta_{1}+iT
to
1-\eta_{2}+iT
on the left, followed by
1-\eta_{2}+iT
downwards to
1-\eta_{2}-iT
and finally from
1-\eta_{2}-iT
to
1+\eta_{1}-iT
on the right. If
\chi
is non-trivial, we can apply Cauchy’s theorem to see that

T_{\chi}(B/d)=-\frac{1}{2\pi i}\left(\int_{1+\eta_{1}+iT}^{1-\eta_{2}+iT}+\int_{1-\eta_{2}+iT}^{1-\eta_{2}-iT}+\int_{1-\eta_{2}-iT}^{1+\eta_{1}-iT}\right)+O\left(\frac{B}{d\log(B/d)}\right)

with
F(s,\chi)\frac{(B/d)^{s}}{s}
the underlying function in the three integrals above.

It follows from the triangle inequality that
|F(s,\chi)|\leq|\zeta(s)|
. On the other hand, there exists an absolute positive constant
c
such that
|\zeta(s)|\ll(\log T)^{c_{1}}
for some
c_{1}>0
in the region
\sigma\geq 1-c/2\log T

[
MV07
, Thm. 6.7, p.174]
, in which we are provided that
T
is sufficiently big. Bounding the two integrals over the real segments trivially now gives

\int_{1+\eta_{1}+iT}^{1-\eta_{2}+iT}+\int_{1-\eta_{2}-iT}^{1+\eta_{1}-iT}\ll\frac{(B/d)^{1+\eta_{1}}(\log T)^{c_{1}}}{\log(B/d)T}\ll\frac{B}{d\log(B/d)}.

Finally, bounding the integral over the imaginary line trivially shows that

\int_{1-\eta_{2}+iT}^{1-\eta_{2}-iT}\ll\frac{B^{1-\eta_{2}}(\log T)^{c_{1}+1}}{d^{1-\eta_{2}}}\ll\frac{B}{d\log(B/d)}.

If
\chi
is non-trivial, we have then verified that

T_{\chi}(B/d)\ll\frac{B}{d\log(B/d)}.

On the other hand, if
\chi=\chi_{0}
is the trivial character mod 18, we can use a Tauberian theorem
[
Ten15
, Thm. II.7.28]
to see that

T_{\chi_{0}}(B/d)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a\leq B/d\\
p\mid a\implies p\equiv 5\bmod 6\end{subarray}}1\asymp\frac{B}{d(\log B/d)^{1/2}}.

Substituting the above estimates for
T_{\chi}(B/d)
and
T_{\psi}(B/d)
in (
6.2
) shows that

S(B)\asymp B^{2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\leq B^{1/2}\\
p\mid d\implies p\equiv 5\bmod 6\end{subarray}}\frac{\mu(d)}{d^{2}}\frac{1}{\log(B/d)}.

It remains to observe that if
1\leq d\leq B^{1/2}
, we have
1/\log(B/d)\gg 1/\log B
and therefore

S(B)\gg\frac{B^{2}}{\log B}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\leq B^{1/2}\\
p\mid d\implies p\equiv 5\bmod 6\end{subarray}}\frac{\mu(d)}{d^{2}}.

The remaining sum over
d
is clearly convergent. Completing that sum gives
S(B)\gg B^{2}/\log B
, which verifies the claimed lower bound in Theorem
1.4
. ∎

7.
Upper bounds

We begin with an estimate of the number of
U
defined in (
1.1
) with a non-trivial transcendental Brauer group. Let

\begin{split}M_{a_{0}}^{\tr}(B)&=\#\left\{(a_{1},a_{2},a_{3})\in[-B,B]^{3}\cap\mathbb{Z}_{\prim}^{3}\ :\ \Br U/\Br_{1}U\text{ non-trivial}\right\},\\
M^{\tr}(B)&=\#\left\{(a_{0},a_{1},a_{2},a_{3})\in[-B,B]^{4}\cap\mathbb{Z}_{\prim}^{4}\ :\ \begin{aligned} &(a_{1},a_{2},a_{3})=1,\\
&\Br U/\Br_{1}U\text{ non-trivial}\end{aligned}\right\}.\end{split}

Proposition 7.1
.

We have

M^{\tr}_{a_{0}}(B)\asymp B(\log B)^{6}\quad\text{and}\quad M^{\tr}(B)\asymp B^{2}(\log B)^{6}.

Proof.

It follows from Theorem
1.1
that
U
has a non-trivial transcendental Brauer group if and only if
a_{1}a_{2}a_{3}\equiv 2\bmod\mathbb{Q}^{*3}
. We will prove the result in the statement for
M^{\tr}_{a_{0}}(B)
, as the one for
M^{\tr}(B)
immediately follows from it. Indeed,

M^{\tr}(B)=M^{\tr}_{a_{0}}(B)\sum_{a_{0}\leq B}1=BM^{\tr}_{a_{0}}(B)+O\left(M^{\tr}_{a_{0}}(B)\right).

Note that the number of
a_{1},a_{2},a_{3}\leq B
whose product
a_{1}a_{2}a_{3}\in 2\mathbb{Q}^{*3}
is of the same magnitude as the number of those
a_{1},a_{2},a_{3}\leq B
whose product is a cube. This follows, for example, from the fact that in the former problem at least one of
a_{1},a_{2},a_{3}
has to be even. Thus any solution to
a_{1}a_{2}a_{3}=2n^{3}
must come from a solution of
a_{1}a_{2}a_{3}=n^{3}
by multiplying one of the latter
a_{i}
by 2. But each solution of of
a_{1}a_{2}a_{3}=n^{3}
produces only finitely many solutions to
a_{1}a_{2}a_{3}=2n^{3}
via doubling a coordinate and a permutation.

The signs of the
a_{i}
are immaterial for the order of magnitude of
M^{\tr}_{a_{0}}(B)
, as they only change the leading constant in the asymptotic. It thus suffices to count
0<a_{1},a_{2},a_{3}\leq B
with
\gcd(a_{1},a_{2},a_{3})=1
and
a_{1}a_{2}a_{3}\in\mathbb{Q}^{*3}
. We thus have

M^{\tr}_{a_{0}}(B)\asymp\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a_{1},a_{2},a_{3}\leq B\\
(a_{1},a_{2},a_{3})=1,\ a_{1}a_{2}a_{3}\in\mathbb{Q}^{*3}\end{subarray}}1=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0<a_{1},a_{2},a_{3}\leq B\\
(a_{1},a_{2},a_{3})=1,\ a_{1}a_{2}a_{3}=n^{3}\end{subarray}}1.

As explained in
[
HBM99
]
, a condition
n\leq B
is redundant in the above problem. Then

M^{\tr}_{a_{0}}(B)\asymp\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0<a_{1},a_{2},a_{3},n\leq B\\
(a_{1},a_{2},a_{3})=1,\ a_{1}a_{2}a_{3}=n^{3}\end{subarray}}1.

The last sum has been investigated in the main result of
[
HBM99
]
, and it is asymptotically a constant times
B(\log B)^{6}
. This proves the claim in the statement of the proposition.
∎

We will also need the following two simple lemmas that will be applied at several instances in the study of the upper bounds considered here.

Lemma 7.2
.

Let
X,Y,Z\geq 1
be real numbers. Then

\sum_{k\ell\leq X,\ km\leq Y,\ \ell m\leq Z}1\ll(XYZ)^{1/2}.

Proof.

Without loss of generality we may assume that
X\geq Y\geq Z
. Let
S
denote the triple sum in the statement. Treating the sum over
m
first shows that

(7.1)

S=\sum_{k\ell\leq X,\ k\leq Y,\ \ell\leq Z}\left(\min\left\{\frac{Y}{k},\frac{Z}{\ell}\right\}+O(1)\right).

Let
R
be the sum corresponding to the error term above. Summing over one of
k,\ell
first and then over the other gives

R=\begin{cases}X\log(YZ/X)+O(X)&\text{if }X<YZ,\\
YZ+O(Y)&\text{if }X\geq YZ.\end{cases}

As
\log(x)\leq x^{1/2}
whenever
x>0
, we see that in both cases
R\ll(XYZ)^{1/2}
.

We then consider separately the two contributions coming from the minimum, depending on if
Y/k<Z/\ell
or vice versa. By doing so we get two identical sums
S_{1}
and
S_{2}
with arguments
1/k
and
1/\ell
, respectively. We analyse
S_{1}
, the argument for
S_{2}
being the same.

\begin{split}S_{1}&=Y\sum_{Y/Z<k\leq Y}\frac{1}{k}\sum_{\ell\leq\min\{kZ/Y,X/k\}}1=Y\sum_{Y/Z<k\leq Y}\frac{1}{k}\left(\min\left\{\frac{kZ}{Y},\frac{X}{k}\right\}+O(1)\right)\\
&=Z\sum_{Y/Z<k\leq\sqrt{XY/Z}}1+XY\sum_{\sqrt{XY/Z}<k\leq Y}\frac{1}{k^{2}}+O(Y\log Z),\end{split}

as
\sum_{k\leq x}1/k=\log x+O(1)
. The asymptotic formula for the first sum on the last line is
(XYZ)^{1/2}+O(Y)
, while the second sum is non-empty only when
X<YZ
and it is convergent in that case. By completing it we produce an error term of size
O(X)
, which in view of
X<YZ
is
O((XYZ)^{1/2})
. At the same time the completed sum equals
(XYZ)^{1/2}+O(Z)
. This altogether gives

S_{1}\ll(XYZ)^{1/2}+Y\log Z.

All error terms so far are
O((XYZ)^{1/2})
as
\log Z\ll Z^{1/2}\ll(XZ/Y)^{1/2}
. The same argument with
Z
and
Y
swapped applies to
S_{2}
, yielding

S\ll(XYZ)^{1/2},

since
S=S_{1}+S_{2}+O(R)
by (
7.1
). This completes the proof.
∎

A careful reading of the proof by chasing the error terms shows that, in fact, the upper bound presented here is attained by
S
. This is particularly visible if
X=YZ
, where the sum obtained from the summands with
m=1
is asymptotically
YZ=(XYZ)^{1/2}
.

Lemma 7.3
.

Let
X,Y,Z,W\geq 1
be real numbers. The following holds.

\begin{split}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\ell m\leq X,\ kns\leq Y\\
\ell nt\leq Z,\ mst\leq W\end{subarray}}1&\ll(XYZW)^{1/2}(\log(XYZW))^{2}.\end{split}

Proof.

Write the sextuple sum in the statement as

\sum_{k\ell m\leq X}\sum_{ns\leq Y/k,\ nt\leq Z/\ell,\ st\leq W/m}1.

Lemma
7.3
is now a straightforward application of Lemma
7.2
and
\sum_{r\leq X}\tau_{3}(r)/r^{1/2}\ll X^{1/2}(\log X)^{2}
, which follows from example from
[
SMC95
, II.12]
combined with partial summation, where
\tau_{3}(n)
denotes the 3-divisor function.
∎

7.1.
Upper bounds

We are now in position to prove the upper bounds in this article. Propositions
4.2
and
4.3
show that, under the arithmetic conditions given there, the Brauer–Manin set obtained from algebraic Brauer elements is non-empty but at the same time it is strictly smaller that the integral adelic set
\mathcal{U}(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Z}})
. In view of Remark
4.4
, the local invariant map at
\infty
of any Brauer element is constant, as every real number is a cube. Thus the projection to the finite adeles
\prod_{p\neq\infty}\mathcal{U}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
preserves strict inclusions. Any surface satisfying the arithmetic conditions of Propositions
4.2
and
4.3
then fails integral strong approximation off
\infty
and at the same time has no algebraic Brauer–Manin obstruction to the integral Hasse principle.

Therefore, in view of Proposition
7.1
it suffices to count surfaces failing the arithmetic conditions on
a_{0},a_{1},a_{2},a_{3}
given in Propositions
4.2
and
4.3
. This will give upper bounds for the number of surfaces in the family which have a Brauer–Manin obstruction to the integral Hasse principle or which satisfy strong approximation off
\infty
. We proceed with establishing the upper bounds in Theorems
1.4
,
1.3
and
1.2
in this order.

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

Propositions
4.2
and
4.3
imply that if the local invariant map does not surject at a given prime, then the coefficients of
\mathcal{U}
must factorise as

(7.2)

\begin{split}a_{0}&=\pm r_{0}b_{0}^{3}u_{01}u_{02}u_{03}v_{01}v_{10}^{2}v_{02}v_{20}^{2}v_{03}v_{30}^{2},\\
a_{1}&=\pm r_{1}b_{1}^{3}u_{01}u_{12}u_{13}v_{10}v_{01}^{2}v_{12}v_{21}^{2}v_{13}v_{31}^{2},\\
a_{2}&=\pm r_{2}b_{2}^{3}u_{02}u_{12}u_{23}v_{20}v_{02}^{2}v_{21}v_{12}^{2}v_{23}v_{32}^{2},\\
a_{3}&=\pm r_{3}b_{3}^{3}u_{03}u_{13}u_{23}v_{30}v_{03}^{2}v_{31}v_{13}^{2}v_{32}v_{23}^{2}.\end{split}

Here all variables on the right hand side are positive integers such that if
p\mid r_{i}
, then
p=3
for
i=0
and
p<17
for
i=1,2,3
while if
p\mid a_{i}/r_{i}
then
p\neq 3
for
i=0
and
p\geq 17
for
i=1,2,3
. Moreover,
a_{i}/(r_{i}b_{i}^{3})
are cube-free and
u_{ij},v_{ij},v_{ji}
are squarefree for any
i,j\in\{0,1,2,3\}
.

The definition of
r_{i}
implies that for any
\alpha>0
, we have

(7.3)

\begin{split}\sum_{r_{i}\leq X}\frac{1}{r_{i}^{\alpha}}\ll 1,\quad i=0,1,2,3.\end{split}

Indeed, let
\varepsilon(n)
be the indicator function for the set
\{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\ :\ p\mid n\implies p<17\}
. This function is non-negative and multiplicative and hence

\sum_{n\leq X}\frac{\varepsilon(n)}{n^{\alpha}}\ll\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\varepsilon(n)}{n^{\alpha}}=\prod_{p<17}\left(1-\frac{1}{p^{\alpha}}\right)^{-1}\ll 1.

Since the signs of
a_{i}
are immaterial to the count, assuming that all of them are positive only changes
N(B)
or
N^{{}^{\prime}}(B)
by a constant. Forgetting
(a_{1},a_{2},a_{3})=1
now gives

N(B)\ll M^{\tr}(B)+\sideset{}{{}^{\star}}{\sum}_{a_{0},a_{1},a_{2},a_{3}\leq B}1\quad\text{and}\quad N^{{}^{\prime}}(B)\ll\sideset{}{{}^{\star}}{\sum}_{a_{0},a_{1},a_{2},a_{3}\leq B}1,

where the
*
in the sum above means that the
a_{i}
counted satisfy (
7.2
). We separate the sum over
u_{ij}
, which allows us to apply Lemma
7.3
in the summation range

\begin{split}u_{01}u_{02}u_{03}&\leq\frac{B}{r_{0}b_{0}^{3}v_{01}v_{10}^{2}v_{02}v_{20}^{2}v_{03}v_{30}^{2}},\quad u_{01}u_{12}u_{13}\leq\frac{B}{r_{1}b_{1}^{3}v_{10}v_{01}^{2}v_{12}v_{21}^{2}v_{13}v_{31}^{2}},\\
u_{02}u_{12}u_{23}&\leq\frac{B}{r_{2}b_{2}^{3}v_{20}v_{02}^{2}v_{21}v_{12}^{2}v_{23}v_{32}^{2}},\quad u_{03}u_{13}u_{23}\leq\frac{B}{r_{3}b_{3}^{3}v_{30}v_{03}^{2}v_{31}v_{13}^{2}v_{32}v_{23}^{2}}.\end{split}

By doing so and by bounding all logarithms trivially, we get

\sideset{}{{}^{\star}}{\sum}_{a_{0},a_{1},a_{2},a_{3}\leq B}1\ll B^{2}(\log B)^{2}\sum\frac{1}{\prod_{i=0}^{3}r_{i}^{1/2}b_{i}^{3/2}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\neq k\end{subarray}}v_{jk}^{3/2}}.

The sums over
b_{i}
and
v_{jk}
are convergent and we may ignore them. We then deduce from (
7.3
) that the above sum is
O(1)
and by Proposition
7.1

N(B)\ll B^{2}(\log B)^{6}\quad\text{and}\quad N^{{}^{\prime}}(B)\ll B^{2}(\log B)^{2}.

This completes the proof of Theorem
1.4
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

The proof is very similar to the one of the upper bound in Theorem

. If
\mathcal{U}
is counted in

, then Propositions

and

a_{2}&=\pm r_{2}b_{2}^{3}u_{12}u_{23}v_{21}v_{12}^{2}v_{23}v_{32}^{2},\\

where
r_{i},b_{i}>0
and if

, then either
p\mid a_{0}
or

, the

and

. Recalling Proposition

, ignoring signs and the coprimality condition and then summing over

gives

N_{a_{0}}(B),\ N_{a_{0}}^{{}^{\prime}}(B)\ll B(\log B)^{6}+B^{3/2}\sum_{r_{i},b_{i},v_{jk}}\frac{1}{(r_{1}r_{2}r_{3})^{1/2}(b_{1}b_{2}b_{3})^{3/2}\prod_{j,k}v_{j,k}^{3/2}}.

) to see that

.
∎

Proof of Theorem

.

As

, the transcendental part of

is trivial by Theorem

. Similarly to the other upper bounds,

N_{a_{1},a_{2},a_{3}}(B),\ N_{a_{1},a_{2},a_{3}}^{{}^{\prime}}(B)\ll B^{1/3}\sum_{u\leq B}\frac{\mathbf{1}_{p\mid u\implies p\mid 3a_{1}a_{2}a_{3}}}{u^{1/3}}\ll_{a_{1}a_{2}a_{3}}B^{1/3}.

.
∎

8.
Examples of Brauer–Manin obstructions

The family of all affine diagonal cubic surfaces does not have a uniform generator of the Brauer group
[
Uem14
, Thm. 2]
. Getting a sharp lower bound on how many surfaces in this family have a Brauer–Manin obstruction thus requires a different approach than the one in
4
. To get around the issue of lacking a uniform generator, we showcase the results from
4
by giving instances of Brauer–Manin obstructions to the integral Hasse principle and to strong approximation without the need of having explicit representatives of Brauer elements. However, applying the method outlined here to the counting results is challenging as it requires controlling uniformly the parameters
\varepsilon
and
\eta
, introduced in
4
.

8.1.
An interesting family

Definition 8.1
.

We define
\mathcal{X}_{\ell,p,q}\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{3}_{\mathbb{Z}}
by

x_{0}^{3}+\ell x_{1}^{3}+pqx_{2}^{3}+q\ell^{2}x_{3}^{3}=0,

where

•

\ell
,
p
and
q
are distinct primes,

•

q\equiv 8\mod 9
,

•

\{p+9\mathbb{Z},\ell+9\mathbb{Z}\}=\{2+9\mathbb{Z},5+9\mathbb{Z}\}
.

We define
\mathcal{U}_{\ell,p,q}\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{3}_{\mathbb{Z}}
as the complement of the curve
x_{1}=0
. We will write
S=\{3,\ell,p,q\}
for the primes of bad reduction on
\mathcal{X}_{\ell,p,q}
.

In accordance with the notation of §
4.3
, we will consistently write
\lambda=\ell
,
\mu=pq
and
\nu=\ell/p
. Furthermore, recall that
\beta^{3}=\lambda\nu=\ell^{2}/p
.

Note that
\mathcal{X}_{\ell,p,q}
is
k_{v}
-rational for
v\in\{3,\ell,p\}
, or equivalently, there is a cross ratio of the four coefficients which is a cube in
k_{v}
. Indeed,
\ell\cdot q\ell^{2}/pq\in\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}
and
\ell\cdot pq/q\ell^{2}\in\mathbb{Z}_{q}
are cubes, since their valuations are multiples of
3
and
\ell\equiv q\equiv 2\mod 3
. Rationality at
3
follows from
(q\ell^{2}\cdot pq)/\ell\equiv p\ell\equiv 1\mod 9
.

The following two results show that the surfaces
\mathcal{U}_{\ell,p,q}
are everywhere locally soluble and always have an obstruction to strong/weak approximation. Moreover,
\mathcal{U}_{\ell,p,q}
should have a rational point (see Remark
8.4
), but there may be an integral Brauer–Manin obstruction to the integral Hasse principle on a different choice of integral model.

Lemma 8.2
.

The affine cubic
\mathcal{U}_{\ell,p,q}
is everywhere integrally locally soluble.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{X}:=\mathcal{X}_{\ell,p,q}
and
\mathcal{U}:=\mathcal{X}\setminus\mathcal{C}
where
\mathcal{C}
is the curve given by
x_{1}=0
on
\mathcal{X}
. Local solubility at the archimedean place is immediate. For
v\not\in\{3,\ell,p,q\}

\mathcal{X}
,
\mathcal{U}
and
\mathcal{C}
have good reduction. The Hasse–Weil bound for genus
1
curves and Weil’s Theorem
[
Man86
, Thm. 27.1]
, for smooth cubic surfaces gives

\#\mathcal{X}(\mathbb{F}_{v})\geq q_{v}^{2}-2q_{v}+1\quad\text{ and }\quad\#\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{F}_{v})\leq q_{v}+1+2\sqrt{q_{v}}.

We find
\mathcal{U}(\mathbb{F}_{v})>0
for
q\geq 5
and by Hensel’s lemma we can lift such an
\mathbb{F}_{v}
-point to an
\mathbb{Z}_{v}
-point. If
q_{v}\equiv 2\mod 3
we have that any unit is a cube. This implies for
v\neq\ell
we have
(-\sqrt[3]{\ell},1,0,0)\in\mathcal{U}(\mathbb{Z}_{v})
. For
v=\ell
it shows we can lift
(\sqrt[3]{pq},-1,0)\in\mathcal{U}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell})
to a
\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}
-point.

We are left with
v=3
. We have either
p\equiv 2\mod 9
and
\ell\equiv 5\mod 9
, or
p\equiv 5\mod 9
and
\ell\equiv 2\mod 9
, in both cases we can lift the point
(1,-1,1)\in\mathcal{U}(\mathbb{Z}/9\mathbb{Z})
to a
\mathbb{Z}_{3}
-point.
∎

Proposition 8.3
.

We have
\Br U_{\ell,p,q}/\Br\mathbb{Q}\cong\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}
. Moreover, for a generator
\mathcal{A}
the following holds

\inv_{v}\mathcal{A}^{\prime}\colon\begin{cases}U_{\ell,p,q}(\mathbb{Q}_{v})\to\frac{1}{3}\mathbb{Z}/\mathbb{Z}\text{ is constant}&\text{if }v\neq p,\\
\mathcal{U}_{\ell,p,q}(\mathbb{Z}_{v})\to\frac{1}{3}\mathbb{Z}/\mathbb{Z}\text{ is surjective}&\text{if }v=p.\end{cases}

Proof.

The first statement follows from Proposition
3.1
(2).

Using Proposition
4.2
we see that

\inv_{v}\mathcal{A}^{\prime}\colon\mathcal{U}_{\ell,p,q}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})\to\frac{1}{3}\mathbb{Z}/\mathbb{Z}

is surjective. This proves the claim for
v=p
.

For the remaining primes
v
we have that
v\not\in S
is a prime of good reduction or
\mathcal{X}_{\ell,p,q}
is
k_{v}
-rational. In those cases the invariant map is constant, see for example
[
CTKS87
, Lem. 5]
.
∎

Remark 8.4
.

For
X_{\ell,p,q}:=\mathcal{X}_{\ell,p,q}\times\mathbb{Q}
we have
X_{\ell,p,q}(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Q}})^{\textup{Br}}\neq\emptyset

[
CTKS87
, §5, Prop. 2]
and under a conjecture by Colliot-Thélène
[
CT03
, p. 174]
we have
X_{\ell,p,q}(\mathbb{Q})\neq\emptyset
. As
X_{\ell,p,q}
is a smooth cubic surface
X_{\ell,p,q}(\mathbb{Q})
is dense in
X_{\ell,p,q}
, hence the affine surface
U_{\ell,p,q}:=\mathcal{U}_{\ell,p,q}\times\mathbb{Q}
always has
U_{\ell,p,q}(\mathbb{Q})\neq\emptyset
. For example, specialising
(\ell,p,q)=(2,5,17)
we can find the rational point
(-1/2,1/2,-1/2)\in U_{2,5,17}(\mathbb{Q})
. Moreover, we see in Proposition
8.12
that a different choice of integral model for
U_{2,5,17}
has an integral Brauer–Manin obstruction. Note from Proposition
8.3
, we see that the invariant map is surjective at
v=p
, hence there is a Brauer–Manin obstruction to strong/weak approximation for all
U_{\ell,p,q}
.

The exact value of
\inv_{v}\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
for
v\neq p
depends on the choice of normalisation of
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
. To simplify the computation of the Brauer–Manin obstruction we make the following convenient choice by taking an
\varepsilon\in\mathbb{Q}(\beta)
satisfying (
4.8
) where
\varepsilon
a priori only lies in
\mathbb{Q}(\omega,\alpha,\gamma)
.

Lemma 8.5
.

There exists an
\varepsilon\in\mathbb{Q}(\beta)
such that
\Norm_{\mathbb{Q}(\beta)/\mathbb{Q}}\varepsilon=\mu
.

Proof.

By Remark
4.5
we need to show that
\mu\in k
is a local norm at all place
w\in\Omega_{k}
. Let
w_{3}
,
w_{\ell}
,
w_{p}
,
w_{q}
be the unique places of
k
above the indicated rational primes. Case (i) of Remark
4.5
deals with the places
w\not\in\{w_{3},w_{\ell},w_{p},w_{q}\}
. For the places
w\in\{w_{\ell},w_{q}\}
we use Case (ii) of the same remark. For the place
w=w_{p}
we note that
\ell,q\in k_{w}
are cubes, and
\ell^{2}/p=\beta^{3}
is a norm. Hence
pq
is also a norm. By the reciprocity law it follows that
\mu
is also a local norm at
w=w_{3}
.
∎

To obtain a counterexample to the Hasse principle, we would want
\inv_{p}\colon\mathcal{U}_{\ell,p,q}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})\rightarrow\tfrac{1}{3}\mathbb{Z}/\mathbb{Z}
to no longer be surjective. We will show that it at least assumes a single value on large subset of these points.

Proposition 8.6
.

For a point
P_{p}=[x_{0}:x_{1}:x_{2}:x_{3}]\in\mathcal{X}_{\ell,p,q}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
such that
x_{0}=0\mod p
and
x_{1}\not\equiv 0\mod p
, we have

\inv_{p}\mathcal{A}^{\prime}(P_{p})=\begin{cases}2/3&\text{if }p\equiv 2\mod 9;\\
1/3&\text{if }p\equiv 5\mod 9.\end{cases}

Proof.

Let
w_{p}
be the unique prime of
k
above
p
. As
\nu=\ell/p
and
\lambda/\nu=p
are not cubes in
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
but
\lambda=\ell\in\mathbb{Q}^{\ast 3}
, we fall into the case
G^{\langle p\rangle}=\langle s\rangle
from Table
2
. Hence, we can compute
\inv_{p}\mathcal{A}^{\prime}(P_{p})=\inv_{w_{p}}\mathcal{A}(P_{p})
as

\left(\frac{x_{0}+\alpha\omega x_{1}}{x_{0}+\alpha\omega^{2}x_{1}},\ell/p\right)_{\omega,w_{p}}=\left(\frac{\alpha\omega}{\alpha\omega^{2}},1/p\right)_{\omega,w_{p}}=(\omega,p)_{\omega,w_{p}}=-\frac{p^{2}-1}{9}\in\frac{1}{3}\mathbb{Z}/\mathbb{Z}.\qed

This shows that
\sum_{v}\inv_{v}\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
is constant on
\mathcal{U}^{\prime}(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Z}})
where
\mathcal{U}^{\prime}
is given by

\mathcal{U}^{\prime}\colon\quad p^{3}u_{1}^{3}+pqu_{2}^{3}+q\ell^{2}u_{3}^{3}=\ell.

To determine whether there is a Brauer–Manin obstruction, that is
\sum_{v}\inv_{v}\mathcal{A}^{\prime}\neq 0
, we will need to determine the constant value of
\inv_{v}\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
for all
v
in the set of bad primes for
X
and
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
,

S_{\mathcal{A}^{\prime}}:=\{v\colon v\mid w\text{ and }w(\varepsilon)\neq 0\}\cup S.

By Remark
4.5
we have
\inv_{v}\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
is identically
0
for all other primes.

In the next section we will construct a family where we have
S_{\mathcal{A}^{\prime}}=S
, and hence
\inv_{v}\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
is identically zero for
v\not\in\{3,\ell,p,q\}
.

8.2.
A family of counterexamples to the Hasse principle

The places where
\varepsilon
is not integral might still show up in the Brauer–Manin obstruction. However, in some cases we can ensure that there are no such primes.

Lemma 8.7
.

Restrict to
\ell=2
and
p=5
and consider the surface
\mathcal{U}_{2,5,q}
. There exists an
\varepsilon\in\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{Q}(\beta)}
for which
\Norm_{\mathbb{Q}(\beta)/\mathbb{Q}}(\varepsilon)=-\mu
. In particular, such an
\varepsilon
is a unit away from
5
and
q
.

Proof.

Let us write
M_{2}=\mathbb{Q}(\beta)
where
\beta^{3}=4/5
. In
\mathcal{O}_{M_{2}}
we can see that both
(5)
and
(q)
are divisible by prime ideals
\mathfrak{p}_{5}
and
\mathfrak{p}_{q}
of inertia degree
1
. Since
\text{Cl}(M_{2})=\{0\}
we see that the ideal
\mathfrak{p}_{5}\mathfrak{p}_{q}
is generated by an element
\varepsilon^{\prime}\in\mathcal{O}_{M_{2}}
. By definition of
\mathfrak{p}_{5}
and
\mathfrak{p}_{q}
we see that
\text{Norm}_{M_{2}/\mathbb{Q}}\left((\varepsilon^{\prime})\right)=(5q)
. Hence
\text{Norm}(\varepsilon^{\prime})=5qu
for some unit
u\in\mathbb{Z}^{\ast}
. So hence either
\varepsilon^{\prime}
or
-\varepsilon^{\prime}
is the
\varepsilon
we are looking for.
∎

Remark 8.8
.

Note that
\beta
itself is not integral; a basis for the free
\mathbb{Z}
-module
\mathcal{O}_{M_{2}}
is for example given by
1,5\beta,\tfrac{5\beta^{2}+10\beta+2}{6}
. So we can always write
\varepsilon=\tfrac{a_{2}\beta^{2}+a_{1}\beta+a_{0}}{6}
with
a_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}
.

For this reason we restrict to
\ell=2
and
p=5
, but many other families could be considered. Hence, we now consider the cubic surface

\mathcal{X}=\mathcal{X}_{2,5,q}\colon x_{0}^{3}+2x_{1}^{3}+5qx_{2}^{3}+4qx_{3}^{3}=0,

where
q
is a prime that is
q\equiv 8\mod 9
. Using the notation from Subsection
4.3
we have
\lambda=2,\mu=5q,\nu=2/5\text{ and }\beta^{3}=4/5
. It follows from Lemma
8.2
that
\mathcal{X}(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Z}})\neq\emptyset
.

Proposition 8.9
.

We have that the invariant map for
\mathcal{U}_{2,5,q}
at the places
v\neq p
are constant and satisfy

\inv_{v}\mathcal{A}^{\prime}=\begin{cases}0&\text{ if }v\neq 3,p;\\
1/3&\text{ if }v=3.\end{cases}

Proof.

We already saw in Proposition
8.3
that all invariant maps apart from
v=p
are constant.

For the primes of good reduction
v\not\in S
we refer to
[
CTKS87
, p. 31]
. The three remaining primes we compute the invariant map at a single point using Table
2
.

v=3

Note that
\lambda,\nu,\lambda/\nu
are not cubes in
\mathbb{Q}_{3}
but
\lambda\nu=4/5
is a cube in
\mathbb{Q}_{3}
, thus we fall into the case
G^{\langle 3\rangle}=\langle q\rangle
. Since
\varepsilon\in\mathbb{Q}(\beta)
is a
2
-unit we have for all
P_{3}\in X(\mathbb{Q}_{3})

\inv_{3}\mathcal{A}^{\prime}(P_{3})=\inv_{w_{3}}\mathcal{A}(P_{3})=(r(\varepsilon)/\varepsilon,2)_{\omega,w_{3}}-(h(P_{3}),2)_{\omega,w_{3}}=-(h(P_{3}),2)_{\omega,w_{3}},

where
w_{3}
is the unique place of
k
dividing
3
. As
\inv_{3}
is constant on
X(\mathbb{Q}_{3})
, it is sufficient to compute the invariant map at one point
P_{3}:=[-\sqrt[3]{q}:0:1:-1]\in X(\mathbb{Q}_{3})
. This gives

(h(P_{3}),2)_{\omega,w_{3}}=\left(\frac{1-\omega\beta}{1-\beta},2\right)_{\omega,w_{3}}=2/3.

v=\ell=2

Let
w_{2}
be the one place of
k
above
2
. Note that
\lambda
and
\nu
are not cubes in
\mathbb{Q}_{2}
but
\lambda/\nu=5
is a cube in
\mathbb{Q}_{2}
, thus we fall into the case
G^{\langle 2\rangle}=\langle r\rangle
. By Lemma
8.7
,
\varepsilon\in\mathbb{Q}(\beta)
, hence
q(\varepsilon)=\varepsilon
and for all
P_{2}\in X(\mathbb{Q}_{2})

\inv_{2}\mathcal{A}^{\prime}(P_{2})=(q(\varepsilon)/\varepsilon,2/5)_{\omega,w_{2}}=(1,2/5)_{\omega,w_{2}}=0.

v=q

As
\nu
is a cube in
\mathbb{Q}_{q}
we fall into the case
G^{\langle q\rangle}=\langle t\rangle
, hence
\inv_{q}=0
.
∎

Proposition 8.10
.

Let
P_{5}=[x_{0}:x_{1}:x_{2}:x_{3}]\in\mathcal{X}(\mathbb{Z}_{5})
such that
x_{0}=0\mod 5
and
x_{1}\not\equiv 0\mod 5
, then

\inv_{5}\mathcal{A}^{\prime}(P_{5})=1/3.

Proof.

This is precisely Proposition
8.6
for the case
\ell=2
and
p=5
.
∎

Corollary 8.11
.

Let
(P_{v})_{v\in\Omega_{\mathbb{Q}}}\in\mathcal{X}(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Z}})
such that
P_{5}=[x_{0}:x_{1}:x_{2}:x_{3}]
where
x_{0}\equiv 0
mod 5 and
x_{1}\not\equiv 0
mod 5. Then

\sum\limits_{v\in\Omega_{\mathbb{Q}}}\inv_{v}\mathcal{A}^{\prime}(P_{v})=2/3.

Proof.

As
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
is of order 3 in
\Br X
we have
\inv_{\infty}\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
is constant as
\Br\mathbb{R}\cong\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
. By our choice of normalisation we have
\inv_{\infty}\mathcal{A}^{\prime}(P_{\infty})=0
for all
P_{\infty}\in X(\mathbb{R})
. Then by Propositions
8.9
and
8.10
we have

\sum\limits_{v\in\Omega_{\mathbb{Q}}}\inv_{v}\mathcal{A}^{\prime}(P_{v})=2/3.\qed

The above result shows that strong approximation on
\mathcal{U}_{2,5,q}
fails. We can use this result to produce affine diagonal cubics for which the Brauer–Manin obstruction obstructs the integral Hasse principle.

Proposition 8.12
.

Let
q\equiv 8\mod 9
be a prime number. Consider the surface
\mathcal{U}^{\prime}
given by

5^{3}u_{1}^{3}+5qu_{2}^{3}+4qu_{3}^{3}=2\subset\mathbb{A}^{3}_{\mathbb{Z}}.

We have
\mathcal{U}^{\prime}(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Z}})\neq\emptyset
, but
\mathcal{U}^{\prime}(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Z}})^{\text{Br}}=\emptyset
. In particular,
\mathcal{U}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z})=\emptyset
.

Proof.

Let

i:\mathcal{U}^{\prime}\rightarrow\mathcal{X},(u_{1},u_{2},u_{3})\mapsto[5u_{1}:u_{2}:u_{3}:1]

and
U^{\prime}:=\mathcal{U}^{\prime}\times_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Q}
. Since
\mathcal{U}_{2,5,q}
and
\mathcal{U}^{\prime}
are isomorphic over
\mathbb{Z}[1/5]
the local solubility is immediate away from
5
. At the place
5
we can take the point
(0,0,1/\sqrt[3]{2q})\in\mathcal{U}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Z}_{5})
.

Since the surfaces are isomorphic over
\mathbb{Q}
we have that
i^{*}\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
generates
\Br U^{\prime}/\Br_{0}U^{\prime}
. By functoriality and Corollary
8.11
we see that for all
(P_{v})_{v\in\Omega_{\mathbb{Q}}}\in\mathcal{U}^{\prime}(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Z}})

\sum\limits_{v\in\Omega_{\mathbb{Q}}}\inv_{v}i^{*}\mathcal{A}^{\prime}(P_{v})=2/3.

Hence,
\mathcal{U}^{\prime}(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Z}})^{\Br}=\emptyset
.
∎

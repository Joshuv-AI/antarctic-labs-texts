Infinite families of linear codes supporting more t t -designs The research was supported by NSFC under Grant No. 11971053.
Qianqian Yan, Junling Zhou

Abstract
Tang and Ding [IEEE IT 67 (2021) 244-254] studied the class of narrow-sense BCH codes 𝒞 ( q , q + 1 , 4 , 1 ) \mathcal{C}_{(q,q+1,4,1)} and their dual codes with q = 2 m q=2^{m} and established that the codewords of the minimum (or the second minimum) weight in these codes support infinite families of 4-designs or 3-designs. Motivated by this, we further investigate the codewords of the next adjacent weight in such codes and discover more infinite classes of t t -designs with t = 3 , 4 t=3,4 . In particular, we prove that the codewords of weight 7 7 in 𝒞 ( q , q + 1 , 4 , 1 ) \mathcal{C}_{(q,q+1,4,1)} support 4 4 -designs when m ⩾ 5 m\geqslant 5 is odd and 3 3 -designs when m ⩾ 4 m\geqslant 4 is even, which provide infinite classes of simple t t -designs with new parameters. Another significant class of t t -designs we produce in this paper has supplementary designs with parameters 4- ( 2 2 ​ s + 1 + 1 , 5 , 5 ) (2^{2s+1}+1,5,5) ; these designs have the smallest index among all the known simple 4- ( q + 1 , 5 , λ ) (q+1,5,\lambda) designs derived from codes for prime powers q q ; and they are further proved to be isomorphic to the 4-designs admitting the projective general linear group PGL ( 2 , 2 2 ​ s + 1 ) (2,2^{2s+1}) as the automorphism group constructed by Alltop in 1969. 2000 MSC : 05B05, 51E10, 94B15 Keywords : BCH code, trace code, t t -design, elementary symmetric polynomial, isomorphic.

-design

-designs and hence the expression “

-design” will

t
-design”, unless otherwise stated. A

-
(v,k,\lambda)

(v,k,\lambda)

\lambda_{s}=\lambda\binom{v-s}{t-s}/\binom{k-s}{t-s}

(1)

for any

.

Let
\mathbb{D}=(\mathcal{P},\mathcal{B})
be a
t
-
(v,k,\lambda)

18

(2)

(3)

In what follows, the block set

Let

\mathcal{C}
by the elements in an
n

\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{C})
. For each
k

A_{k}\neq 0

\mathcal{B}_{k}(\mathcal{C})
denote the set of the supports of all codewords in
\mathcal{C}
with Hamming weight
k
. If the pair
(\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{C}),\mathcal{B}_{k}(\mathcal{C}))

t
-
(n,k,\lambda)

\mathcal{C}

holds
or

a
t
-design. This is our main approach of coding-theoretic construction for combinatorial designs in this paper.
There are other different means of generating
t
-designs.
The Assmus-Mattson Theorem
[
2
]
is very useful in producing
t
-designs from linear codes (see, for example,
[
8
,
21
]
). Tang et al.
[
20
]
generalized Assmus-Mattson Theorem and their new criterion outperforms the original Assmus-Mattson Theorem in some special cases. Additionally, we have another fundamental method to produce

-designs by considering codes admitting

-homogeneous or
t
-transitive automorphism groups (see, for example,
[
4
,
12
,
16

An
[n,k,d]
linear code
\mathcal{C}
over
{\operatorname{GF}}(q)
is
cyclic
if any cyclic shift of a
codeword is again a codeword, i.e.,
(c_{0},c_{1},\ldots,c_{n-1})\in\mathcal{C}
implies
(c_{n-1},c_{0},\ldots,

c_{n-2})\in\mathcal{C}
. We usually treat codewords in a cyclic code as polynomials
in
{\operatorname{GF}}(q)[x]
. This means if
(c_{0},c_{1},\ldots,c_{n-1})\in\mathcal{C}
then we associate the polynomial
c_{0}+c_{1}x+\cdots+c_{n-1}x^{n-1}\in{\operatorname{GF}}(q)[x]/(x^{n}-1).
In such a way any cyclic code of length
n
over
{\operatorname{GF}}(q)
corresponds to a subset of the residue class ring
{\operatorname{GF}}(q)[x]/(x^{n}-1)
. It is obvious that a linear code
\mathcal{C}
is cyclic if and only if the
corresponding subset in
{\operatorname{GF}}(q)[x]/(x^{n}-1)
is an ideal of the
{\operatorname{GF}}(q)[x]/(x^{n}-1)
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a cyclic code of length
n
over
{\operatorname{GF}}(q)
. Because
{\operatorname{GF}}(q)[x]/(x^{n}-1)
is a principal ideal ring, there is a unique monic polynomial
g(x)\in{\operatorname{GF}}(q)[x]
of lowest degree such that
\mathcal{C}=\langle g(x)\rangle
. This polynomial
g(x)
(as a divisor of
x^{n}-1
) is called the
generator polynomial
of
\mathcal{C}
.

Let
m={\operatorname{ord}}_{n}(q)
and let
\alpha
be a generator of
{\operatorname{GF}}(q^{m})^{*}:={\operatorname{GF}}(q^{m})\setminus\{0\}
. Let
\beta=\alpha^{(q^{m}-1)/n}
. Then clearly
\beta
is a primitive
n
-th root of unity in
{\operatorname{GF}}(q^{m})
. The
minimal polynomial

\mathbb{M}_{\beta^{s}}(x)
of
\beta^{s}
over
{\operatorname{GF}}(q)
is the monic polynomial of lowest degree over
{\operatorname{GF}}(q)
with
\beta^{s}
as a zero.
Let
\delta,h
be positive integers with
2\leqslant\delta\leqslant n
. A
BCH code
over GF
(q)
with length
n
and
designed distance

whose generator polynomial is

g(x)=\text{lcm}(\mathbb{M}_{\beta^{h}}(x),\mathbb{M}_{\beta^{h+1}}(x),\dots,\mathbb{M}_{\beta^{h+\delta-2}}(x))

with the least common multiple calculated over
{\operatorname{GF}}(q)
.
When
h=1
, the code
\mathcal{C}_{(q,n,\delta,h)}
is called a
narrow-sense
BCH code. As is known to us all, BCH codes are an important subclass of cyclic codes with many attractive properties.

A code
\mathcal{C}
is called a
maximum distance separable code
(or MDS code), if the minimum distance
d
meets
d=n-k+1
. The
Singleton defect
of an
[n,k,d]
code
\mathcal{C}
is defined to be def
(\mathcal{C})=n-k+1-d
and we say that
\mathcal{C}
is an
A
s
MDS code
if
s=
def
(\mathcal{C})
. Thus, A
0

1
MDS code is also called an

(AMDS code). Hence, AMDS codes have parameters
[n,k,n-k]
.
A code
\mathcal{C}
is said to be a

(NMDS code) if both
\mathcal{C}
and
\mathcal{C}^{\bot}
are AMDS codes.

MDS codes do hold
t
-designs with large

, but these designs are all trivial unfortunately. Ding and Tang
[
9
]
presented an infinite family of NMDS codes over

-designs and an infinite family of NMDS codes over

-designs. The first NMDS code supporting a

-design was the
[11,6,5]

(4,5,11)

[

]
. Recently Tang and Ding
[

and the dual codes

where

. It was shown that the codewords of the

-designs when
m\geqslant 5
is odd and they support
3
-designs when
m\geqslant 4
is even. In this paper, we will further investigate the codewords of the next adjacent weight (of weight 7) and prove that
{\cal B}_{7}(\mathcal{C}_{(q,q+1,4,1)})
also supports a
4
-design when
m\geqslant 5
is odd and it supports a
3
-design when
m\geqslant 4
is even. For

with odd
m\geqslant 5
, the minimum weight codewords in
\mathcal{C}^{\bot}_{(q,q+1,4,1)}

-
(q+1,q-5,\lambda)
designs in
[
19
]
and in this paper we will show the second minimum weight codewords (of weight

) support again 4-designs. It is very interesting and significant that the supplementary designs of these 4-designs have parameters
4
-
(q+1,5,5)
, because on one hand they achieve the smallest index among all the known simple

-
(q+1,5,\lambda)
designs (with

(2,q)
as the automorphism group constructed by Alltop

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we introduce the definition of elementary symmetric polynomials (ESPs) and develop block sets produced from three types of variants of ESPs. We also document several useful results produced in

Preliminaries

A polynomial
f
is
symmetric
if the polynomial is invariant under all permutations of its variables. The
elementary symmetric polynomial
(briefly by ESP) of degree
l
in
k
variables
u_{1},u_{2},\ldots,u_{k}
, is defined by

\sigma_{k,l}(u_{1},u_{2},\ldots,u_{k})=\sum\limits_{I\subseteq[k]\atop{|I|=l}}\prod\limits_{j\in I}u_{j}.

For simplicity,
\sigma_{k,l}(u_{1},u_{2},\ldots,u_{k})
is also denoted by
\sigma_{k,l}(B)
where
B=\{u_{1},u_{2},\ldots,u_{k}\}
or briefly denoted
\sigma_{k,l}
if the context is clear.

Throughout the paper we always let
q
be a prime power and let
U_{q+1}
be the set of all
(q+1)
-th roots of unity in
{\operatorname{GF}}(q^{2})
, that is,

U_{q+1}=\{u\in\text{GF}(q^{2}):u^{q+1}=1\}.

Let
f
be a symmetric polynomial in
k
variables whose coefficients are taken in GF
(q^{2})
. We define

\mathcal{B}_{f,q+1}=\bigg\{\{u_{1},u_{2},\ldots,u_{k}\}\in\binom{U_{q+1}}{k}:f(u_{1},u_{2},\ldots,u_{k})=0\bigg\}.

(4)

This produces an incidence structure
\mathbb{D}=(U_{q+1},\mathcal{B}_{f,q+1})
. If
\mathbb{D}
forms a
t
-
(q+1,k,\lambda)
design, then we say that
f
supports a
t
-
(q+1,k,\lambda)
design. In particular, for any positive integer
k\leqslant q+1
, the block set
\mathcal{B}_{\sigma_{k,l},q+1}
produced from the ESP
\sigma_{k,l}
is defined by

\mathcal{B}_{\sigma_{k,l},q+1}=\bigg\{B\in\binom{U_{q+1}}{k}:\sigma_{k,l}(B)=0\bigg\}.

(5)

In
[
19
]
, Tang and Ding presented several infinite families of linear codes supporting
t
-designs, whose block sets are isomorphic to
\mathcal{B}_{\sigma_{k,l},q+1}
where
(k,l,q)\in\{(4,2,2^{2s}),(4,2,3^{m}),(5,2,2^{2s}),

(6,3,2^{m})\}
. In this paper we also concentrate on the topic of linear codes supporting
t
-designs by handling the incidence structures produced primarily from three types of variants of ESPs.

We define a block set
\mathcal{B}^{u}_{\sigma_{k,l},q+1}
by

\mathcal{B}^{u}_{\sigma_{k,l},q+1}=\bigg\{B\in\binom{U_{q+1}}{k}:\sigma_{k,l}(B-a)=0\ \text{for some}\ a\in U_{q+1}\bigg\},

(6)

where
B-a:=\{b-a:b\in B\}
.
If
(U_{q+1},\mathcal{B}^{u}_{\sigma_{k,l},q+1})
forms a
t
-
(q+1,k,\lambda)
design, we say that the
u
-variant
of the ESP
\sigma_{k,l}
supports a
t
-
(q+1,k,\lambda)
design.

More restrictively, we define the block sets
\mathcal{B}_{\sigma_{k,l},q+1}^{b}
and
\mathcal{B}_{\sigma_{k,l},q+1}^{\overline{b}}
generated from the
b
-variant
and
\overline{b}
-variant
of ESPs
\sigma_{k,l}
respectively by

\mathcal{B}^{b}_{\sigma_{k,l},q+1}=\bigg\{B\in\binom{U_{q+1}}{k}:\sigma_{k,l}(B-a)=0\ \text{for some}\ a\in B\bigg\},

(7)

and

\mathcal{B}^{\overline{b}}_{\sigma_{k,l},q+1}=\mathcal{B}^{u}_{\sigma_{k,l},q+1}\setminus\mathcal{B}^{b}_{\sigma_{k,l},q+1}.

(8)

Let
B=\{u_{1},u_{2},\dots,u_{k}\}\in\binom{U_{q+1}}{k}
. One gets

\sigma_{k,l}(B-a)=\sum\limits_{I\subseteq[k]\atop{|I|=l}}\prod\limits_{j\in I}(u_{j}-a)=\sum\limits^{l}_{i=0}(-a)^{l-i}\binom{k-i}{l-i}\sigma_{k,i}(B).

(9)

Then
B\in\mathcal{B}^{u}_{\sigma_{k,l},q+1}
if and only if
\sum\limits^{l}_{i=0}(-a)^{l-i}\binom{k-i}{l-i}\sigma_{k,i}(B)=0
for some
a\in U_{q+1}
.

In this paper we will present new infinite families of
t
-designs with
t=3,4
by exploring the block sets generated from the
u
-variant,
b
-variant, or
\overline{b}
-variant of ESPs. The effect is twofold. From the perspective of coding theory, the three types of variants usually provide succinct descriptions for the designs held by the codewords of a fixed weight in a linear code. On the other hand, from the aspect of design theory, we produce several infinite families of
t
-designs. Comparing with
[
18
]
, we find that most of these parameters are new and an attractive feature of these designs lies in the simpleness of the designs although the indices

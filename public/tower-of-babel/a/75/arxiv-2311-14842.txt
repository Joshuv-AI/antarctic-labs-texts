A Characterization of Optimal-Rate Linear Homomorphic Secret Sharing Schemes, and Applications
Keller Blackwell, Mary Wootters

Abstract
A Homomorphic Secret Sharing (HSS) scheme is a secret-sharing scheme that shares a secret x x among s s servers, and additionally allows an output client to reconstruct some function f ⁡ ( x ) f(x) , using information that can be locally computed by each server. A key parameter in HSS schemes is download rate , which quantifies how much information the output client needs to download from each server. Recent work (Fosli, Ishai, Kolobov, and Wootters, ITCS 2022) established a fundamental limitation on the download rate of linear HSS schemes for computing low-degree polynomials, and gave an example of HSS schemes that meet this limit. In this paper, we further explore optimal-rate linear HSS schemes for polynomials. Our main result is a complete characterization of such schemes, in terms of a coding-theoretic notion that we introduce, termed optimal labelweight codes . We use this characterization to answer open questions about the amortization required by HSS schemes that achieve optimal download rate. In more detail, the construction of Fosli et al. required amortization over ℓ \ell instances of the problem, and only worked for particular values of ℓ \ell . We show that—perhaps surprisingly—the set of ℓ \ell ’s for which their construction works is in fact nearly optimal, possibly leaving out only one additional value of ℓ \ell . We show this by using our coding-theoretic characterization to prove a necessary condition on the ℓ \ell ’s admitting optimal-rate linear HSS schemes. We then provide a slightly improved construction of optimal-rate linear HSS schemes, where the set of allowable ℓ \ell ’s is optimal in even more parameter settings. Moreover, based on a connection to the MDS conjecture, we conjecture that our construction is optimal for all parameter regimes.

(1) A characterization of optimal-rate linear HSS schemes.

Our main result is a characterization of linear HSS schemes for
\mathrm{POLY}_{d,m}({\mathbb{F}})
with optimal download rate
(s-dt)/s

Definition 1

(Labelweight)
.

Let
\mathcal{C}\subseteq{\mathbb{F}}^{n}
be a linear code of dimension
\ell

\mathcal{L}:[n]\to[s]
be any function, which we refer to as a

of

is the number of distinct labels that the support of

touches:

\Delta_{\mathcal{L}}(\mathbf{c})=|\{\mathcal{L}(i)\,:\,i\in[n],c_{i}\neq 0\}|.

The
labelweight
of
\mathcal{C}
is the minimum labelweight of any nonzero codeword:

\Delta_{\mathcal{L}}(\mathcal{C})=\min_{c\in\mathcal{C}\setminus\{0\}}\Delta_{\mathcal{L}}(\mathbf{c}).

In particular, if
s=n
and
\mathcal{L}(j)=j
for all
j\in[n]
, then
\Delta_{\mathcal{L}}(\mathcal{C})
is just the minimum Hamming distance of
\mathcal{C}
. Thus, the labelweight of a code generalizes the standard notion of distance.

Our main characterization theorem is the following.

(Optimal linear HSS schemes are equivalent to optimal labelweight codes. (Informal, see Lemma

Conversely, suppose that there is a labeling function

1.2
Technical Overview

In this section, we give a high-level overview of our techniques.

Relationship between download-optimal HSS schemes and optimal labelweight codes.

Theorem
3
states that download-optimal linear HSS schemes are equivalent to optimal-labelweight codes. To give some intuition for the connection, we explain the forward direction, which is simpler. For simplicity, suppose that the function
f
is identity, amortized over
\ell
secrets
x^{(1)},\ldots,x^{(\ell)}\in{\mathbb{F}}
.
This problem is called
HSS for concatenation
in
[
FIKW22
]
; the goal is to share the
\ell

t+1
shares of each secret.

Consider a linear HSS scheme for this problem. By definition, the reconstruction algorithm
\mathsf{Rec}
is linear, and so can be represented by a matrix
G\in{\mathbb{F}}^{\ell\times n}
, where
n
is the total number of symbols of
{\mathbb{F}}
sent by all of the servers together. In more detail, we view each output share

as a vector over
{\mathbb{F}}
, and concatenate all of them to obtain a vector
\mathbf{z}\in{\mathbb{F}}^{n}
. Then we can linearly recover the
\ell
concatenated secrets from

:

\begin{pmatrix}x^{(1)}\\
\vdots\\
x^{(\ell)}\end{pmatrix}=\mathsf{Rec}(z_{1},\ldots,z_{s})=G\mathbf{z}.

Now we can define a labeling function
\mathcal{L}:[n]\to[s]
so that
\mathcal{L}(r)\in[s]
is the identity of the server sending the
r
’th symbol in
\mathbf{z}
. We claim that
G
is the generator matrix
4
4

4

When we say that
G
is the
generator matrix
of
\mathcal{C}\subseteq{\mathbb{F}}^{n}
, we mean that
\mathcal{C}
is the rowspan of
G
.
of a code
\mathcal{C}
with
\Delta_{\mathcal{L}}(\mathcal{C})\geq t+1
. (Recall that in HSS for concatenation,
d=1
, so
t+1=dt+1
is the bound on
\Delta_{\mathcal{L}}(\mathcal{C})
that we want in Theorem
3
). To see this, suppose that there were some codeword
\mathbf{m}^{T}G
for some
\mathbf{m}\in{\mathbb{F}}^{\ell}
with labelweight at most
t
. But consider the quantity

X:=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}m_{i}x^{(i)}=\mathbf{m}^{T}G\mathbf{z}.

If
\mathbf{m}^{T}G
had labelweight at most
t
(with this labeling
\mathcal{L}
), that means that the quantity
X
can be computed using messages coming from only

But this contradicts the
t

. Then if
x^{(2)}=\cdots=x^{(\ell)}=0
, some set of

For the full proof of this direction, we need to generalize to larger

The converse, showing that any optimal-labelweight code
\mathcal{C}
implies an optimal HSS scheme, is a bit trickier. The main challenge is that the connection above tells us what
\mathsf{Rec}
should be—it should be given by the generator matrix of
\mathcal{C}
—but it does not tell us what
\mathsf{Eval}
should be, or that an appropriate
\mathsf{Eval}

\mathsf{Eval}

\mathsf{Eval}

\mathsf{Eval}

Characterization of Optimal Labelweight Codes.

In order to understand when optimal labelweight codes exist, we give a characterization of them in terms of their generator matrices. We show that the generator matrices of optimal labelweight codes can be taken to be
block-totally-nonsingular
. We defer the formal definition to Definition
10
, but informally, a block-totally-nonsingular matrix is made up of
j\times j
invertible blocks
A\in GL({\mathbb{F}},j)
, with the property that any square sub-array of blocks (not necessarily contiguous) is full-rank.

Lemma 6

(Characterization of optimal label-weight codes. (Informal, see Lemma
22
))
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{C}\subseteq{\mathbb{F}}^{n}
is a code of rate
(s-dt)/s
, and suppose that there is a labeling function
\mathcal{L}:[n]\to[s]
so that
\Delta_{\mathcal{L}}(\mathcal{C})\geq dt+1
. Then, up to a permutation of the coordinates, there is a generator matrix
G
for
\mathcal{C}
that looks like

G=[I|A]

where
A
is block-totally-nonsingular. Conversely, any such matrix is the generator matrix for a code
\mathcal{C}
with
\Delta_{\mathcal{L}}(\mathcal{C})\geq dt+1
, where
\mathcal{L}:[n]\to[s]
is the labeling function
\mathcal{L}(x)=\lceil x/j\rceil
.

To give some intuition for the lemma, we first explain where the “block” structure comes from. We show (Lemma
13
) that in fact any labeling function
\mathcal{L}
so that
\Delta_{\mathcal{L}}(\mathcal{C})=dt+1
must be
balanced
, meaning that each set
\mathcal{L}^{-1}(i)
for
i\in[s]
has the same cardinality,
|\mathcal{L}^{-1}(i)|=j
, for some integer
j
. The blocks then correspond to the sets
\mathcal{L}^{-1}(i)
for
i\in[s]
.

Next, we give some intuition for the “totally non-singular” part. The basic idea is that if there were a square sub-array of blocks that were singular, then there would be a vector
\mathbf{m}\in{\mathbb{F}}^{\ell}
with support on only the relevant blocks, so that
\mathbf{m}^{T}A=0
. If
G=[I|A]
as in Lemma
6
, then this implies that
\mathbf{m}^{T}G
has support only on the labels that appear on the first
\ell
columns of
G
, which it turns out is small enough to contradict the optimal labelweight property. We refer to the proof of Lemma
22
for details on both points.

Applications to Amortization.

We apply the machinery described above to Question (2), about what amortization parameters
\ell
are possible, as follows. As described above, we show that optimal-download-rate HSS schemes are equivalent to optimal labelweight codes, which in turn are equivalent to to block-totally-nonsingular matrices. At this point, we already know that the amortization parameter
\ell
must be equal to
j(s-dt)
for some integer
j
; this follows from the
j\times j
block structure of block-totally-nonsingular matrices. It remains only to understand for which
j
’s such matrices exist. A limitation on such matrices follows from a standard counting argument, and this implies our infeasibility result, given formally as Theorem
20
.

As noted above, there is already a near-optimal construction of HSS schemes in
[
FIKW22
]
(Theorem
2
). In our language, that construction is based on systematic generator matrices of
Reed-Solomon Codes.
After an appropriate conversion to block form, such matrices are of the form
[I|A]
, where
A
is a block-totally-nonsingular matrix. Up to the conversion to block-form, the famous
MDS Conjecture
(Conjecture
27
) implies that Reed-Solomon codes are
nearly
optimal in this context, but it is known that a slight improvement is possible: instead of requiring
j\geq\log_{q}(s)
as in Theorem
2
, one can get
j\geq\log_{q}(s-2)
or
\log_{q}(s-1)
, as in Theorem
5
.
The change is simple: one essentially adds one or two more (block) columns to the generator matrix (see Appendix
A
and the proof of Theorem
19
).
Thus, we can slightly improve on the construction of
[
FIKW22
]
(Theorem
2
) by making this slight improvement to the underlying code.

is equivalent to the existence of linear codes with a particular rate and distance. However, this characterization only works when
d=1
. In contrast, our characterization, in terms of codes with good
labelweight
, a generalization of distance, applies for general
d
.

Finally, we mention the
MDS conjecture
, which is related to our results. The MDS conjecture (Conjecture
27
) was stated by Segre in 1955
[
Seg55
]
, and roughly says that Reed-Solomon codes have the best alphabet size possible for any
Maximum-Distance Separable
(MDS) code. After being open for over 60 years, the conjecture has been proved for
prime
order fields, and particular extension fields
[
BB12
]
. Our characterization of optimal-rate HSS schemes leads us to consider the related question of the best alphabet size possible for any
Totally Nonsingular
matrix, which we observe in Section
4.2.2
is equivalent to the MDS conjecture over extension fields. This connection leads us to conjecture that our construction is in fact optimal, and we show that the MDS conjecture implies that it is, within a natural class of constructions. See the discussion in Section
4.2.2
for more on this connection.

1.4
Open Questions and Future Directions

Before we get into the details, we take a moment to highlight some open questions.

•

The most obvious question that our work leaves open is about the edge cases in the characterization of amortization parameters
\ell
that are admissible for optimal-download linear HSS schemes. Given the relationship to the MDS conjecture over extension fields, resolving this may be quite a hard problem. However, there is some hope. In particular, the MDS conjecture would imply that our construction is optimal only within a natural class of constructions; it may be possible to get improved results by leaving that class.

•

Another open question is to find further applications of our characterization of download optimal HSS schemes. We use this characterization to nearly resolve the question of what amortization parameters are admissible for such schemes, but we hope that it will be useful for other questions about linear information-theoretic HSS.

•

Finally, it would be interesting to extend our results to linear HSS schemes that do not have optimal download rate. For example, perhaps it is possible to get a drastic improvement in the amortization parameter
\ell
by backing off from the optimal download rate by only a small amount.

Organization

In Section
2

2.3
CNF Sharing

The main

Definition 7

(
t
-private CNF sharing)
.

Let
{\mathbb{F}}
be a finite field.
The
t
-private,
s
-server CNF secret-sharing scheme over
{\mathbb{F}}
is a function
\mathsf{Share}:{\mathbb{F}}\times{\mathbb{F}}^{\binom{s}{t}-1}\to\left({\mathbb{F}}^{\binom{s-1}{t}}\right)^{s}
that shares a secret
x\in{\mathbb{F}}
as
s

, using
\binom{s}{t}-1
random field elements, as follows.

Let
x\in{\mathbb{F}}
, and let
\mathbf{r}\in{\mathbb{F}}^{\binom{s}{t}-1}
be a uniformly random vector. Using
\mathbf{r}
, choose
y_{T}\in{\mathbb{F}}
for each set

of size
t
, as follows: The
y_{T}
are uniformly random subject to the equation

x=\sum_{T\subseteq[s]:|T|=t}y_{T}.

Then for all

, define

\mathsf{Share}(x,\mathbf{r})_{j}=(y_{T}\,:\,j\not\in T)\in{\mathbb{F}}^{\binom{s-1}{t}}.

We observe that CNF-sharing is indeed
t

t+1

, and thus can reconstruct

. In contrast, any
t

) are missing the share

, and thus cannot learn anything about

.

The main reason we focus on CNF sharing is that it is

Theorem 8

(
[

]
)
.

Suppose that

are locally convertible into shares of

. That, is there are functions

so that

has the same distribution as

\mathsf{Share}^{\prime}(x,\mathbf{r})

for a uniformly random vector
\mathbf{r}
.

In particular, we prove several results of the form “no linear HSS with CNF sharing can do better than
____.” Because of Theorem

Finally, we record a useful lemma about HSS with

Lemma 9
.

[

Fix

so that
s\geq dt+1
, and suppose

.
Let
\pi=(\mathsf{Share},\mathsf{Eval},\mathsf{Rec})
be any linear HSS that

among

.
Then
\mathsf{Rec}
must depend on output shares

from at least

.

Proof.

From the definition of a nontrivial function class (Definition
6
, with
\ell=1
), there is some
\hat{f}\in\mathcal{F}
so that
\hat{f}
contains a monomial with at least
d
distinct variables.
Up to relabeling the secrets, we may assume that
\hat{f}
contains the monomial

. As the monomials of
\hat{f}
are linearly independent over
{\mathbb{F}}[X_{1},\ldots,X_{m}]
and
\mathsf{Rec}
is linear, if
\mathsf{Rec}
could recover
\hat{f}
with probability
1
, then
\mathsf{Rec}
could recover
f
with probability
1
as well. Thus, it suffices to show that no linear
\mathsf{Rec}
with output shares from at most
dt

with probability
1
.

Let
J\subseteq[s]
with
\left|J\right|<dt+1
be any collection of at most
dt
servers, and suppose towards a contradiction that the function
\mathsf{Rec}
depends only on the output shares
(z_{j}:j\in J)
.

For
j\in J
, let

\mathbf{y}_{j}=\left(y_{h,T}\;:\;h\in[d],T\subseteq[s],|T|=t,j\not\in T\right)

denote the set of
t
-CNF shares of
x_{1},\ldots,x_{d}
held by server
j
. Let

g_{j}
be the polynomial over
{\mathbb{F}}
in the variables
\mathbf{y}_{j}
so that

g_{j}(\mathbf{y}_{j})=\mathsf{Eval}(f,j,\mathbf{y}_{j}).

(Note that over a finite field
{\mathbb{F}}
, any function can be represented as a polynomial, whose degree in each variable is at most
|{\mathbb{F}}|-1
).

Let
T_{1}^{*},\ldots,T_{d}^{*}\subseteq[s]
be any collection of
t
-sized subsets satisfying
J\subseteq\bigcup_{i\in[d]}T_{i}^{*}
.
This exists because
\left|J\right|\leq dt
, so we can choose
d

subsets that cover
J
.
Next we observe that the monomial

W=\prod_{i=1}^{d}y_{i,T_{i}^{*}}

cannot appear in the polynomial
g_{j}
for any

, as none of the servers
j\in J
can compute

.

Because the HSS scheme is linear and we are assuming that
\mathsf{Rec}
depends only on
(z_{j}:j\in J)
, the output of
\mathsf{Rec}
can be written as a linear combination of the polynomials

for some

.
In particular,

However, as a formal polynomial, the function that we would like to reconstruct is

In particular, there are values of the shares

so that

is not the same as

, which means that the probability that they are the same is less than 1, contradicting the correctness requirement of an HSS scheme.

2.4
Linear Codes and Labelweights

Throughout, we will be working with
linear codes

\mathcal{C}\subset{\mathbb{F}}^{n}
. Such a code is just a subspace of
{\mathbb{F}}^{n}
. For a linear code
\mathcal{C}\subseteq{\mathbb{F}}^{n}
of dimension
\ell
, we say that a matrix
G\in{\mathbb{F}}^{\ell\times n}
is a
generator matrix
for
\mathcal{C}
if
\mathcal{C}=\mathrm{rowSpan}(G)
. Note that generator matrices are not unique. If
G
has the form

G=[I|A]

where
I={\mathbb{F}}^{\ell\times\ell}
is the identity matrix and
A\in{\mathbb{F}}^{\ell\times(n-\ell)}
, we say that
G
is in
systematic form
, and we refer to the first
\ell
coordinates as
systematic coordinates
.
The other coordinates we refer to as
non-systematic coordinates
.
The
rate
of a linear code
\mathcal{C}\subset{\mathbb{F}}^{n}
of dimension
\ell
is defined as

\textsf{Rate}(\mathcal{C}):=\frac{\ell}{n}.

As discussed in the Introduction, our characterization of download-optimal linear HSS schemes is in terms of linear codes with good
labelweight
.

Definition 8

(Labeling Function)
.

Let
U,V
be finite domains satisfying
|U|\geq|V|
. We say that a mapping
\mathcal{L}:U\to V
is a labeling function (or simply labeling) of
U
by
V
if
\mathcal{L}
is a surjection.

We will usually take
U=[n]
and
V=[s]
for
n,s\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
with
n\geq s
.
For a code
\mathcal{C}\subseteq{\mathbb{F}}^{n}
, and a labeling function
\mathcal{L}:[n]\to[s]
, the labelweight of a codeword is the number of distinct labels that its support touches, and the labelweight of a code is the minimum labelweight of any nonzero codeword. More precisely, we have the following definition.

Definition 9

(Labelweight)
.

Let
\mathcal{L}
be a labeling of
[n]
by
[s]
where
n,s\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
with
n\geq s
. Let
\mathbb{F}
denote a field. Given
\mathbf{c}\in\mathbb{F}^{n}
, we define the labelweight of
\mathbf{c}
by

\Delta_{\mathcal{L}}(\mathbf{c}):=\left|\left\{\mathcal{L}(i)\;:\;\mathbf{c}_{i}\neq 0\right\}\right|.

For a code
\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathbb{F}^{n}
, we define labelweight of
\mathcal{C}
by

\Delta_{\mathcal{L}}(\mathcal{C}):=\min_{\mathbf{0}\neq\mathbf{c}\in\mathcal{C}}\Delta_{\mathcal{L}}(\mathbf{c}).

We note that labelweight is a generalization of Hamming weight; indeed, let
\iota:[n]\to[n]
denote the identity function on
[n]
, which may be viewed as a labeling of
[n]
by
[n]
. Given a linear code
\mathcal{C}
of length
n
and
\mathbf{c}\in\mathcal{C}
, we see that
\Delta_{\iota}(\mathbf{c})
and
\Delta_{\iota}(\mathcal{C})
are equivalent to the Hamming weight of
\mathbf{c}
and the minimum Hamming distance of
\mathcal{C}
, respectively.

Linear HSS Schemes and Good Labelweight Codes

In this section we show that finding optimal-download linear HSS schemes for low-degree multivariate polynomials is equivalent to finding linear codes with high labelweight.
Before we show the equivalence, we make a few observations about linear reconstruction algorithms. The first is just the observation that any linear reconstruction scheme can be regarded as matrix:

Observation 10

Let
\ell,t,s,d,m,n
be integers. Let
\pi=(\mathsf{Share},\mathsf{Eval},\mathsf{Rec})
be a
t
-private,
s
-server HSS for some function class
\mathcal{F}\subseteq\mathrm{POLY}_{d,m}({\mathbb{F}})^{\ell}
with linear reconstruction
\mathsf{Rec}:{\mathbb{F}}^{n}\to{\mathbb{F}}^{\ell}
, where
n=\sum_{j\in[s]}n_{j}
, and the output share
z_{j}
of server
j
is an element of
{\mathbb{F}}^{n_{j}}
.
Let
\mathbf{z}\in{\mathbb{F}}^{n}
be the vector of all output shares. That is,

\mathbf{z}=z_{1}\circ z_{2}\circ\cdots\circ z_{s},

where
\circ
denotes concatenation.

Then there exists a matrix
G_{\pi}\in\mathbb{F}^{\ell\times n}
so that, for all
f\in\mathcal{F}
and for all secrets
\mathbf{x}\in({\mathbb{F}}^{m})^{\ell}
,

\mathsf{Rec}(\mathbf{z})=G_{\pi}\mathbf{z}=f(\mathbf{x})=\left[\begin{array}[]{c}f_{1}(\mathbf{x}^{(1)})\\
f_{2}(\mathbf{x}^{(2)})\\
\vdots\\
f_{\ell}(\mathbf{x}^{(\ell)})\end{array}\right]

For a linear HSS
\pi
, we call
G_{\pi}
as in the observation above the
reconstruction matrix
corresponding to
\mathsf{Rec}
.

We next observe that
G_{\pi}
has full rank.

Lemma 11
.

Let
t,s,d,m,\ell
be positive integers so that
m\geq d
and
n\geq\ell
, and let
\pi
be a
t
-private
s
-server linear HSS for some
\mathcal{F}\subseteq\mathrm{POLY}_{d,m}({\mathbb{F}})
, so that
\mathcal{F}
contains an element
(f_{1},\ldots,f_{\ell})
where for each
i\in[\ell]
,
f_{i}
is non-constant. Then
G_{\pi}\in{\mathbb{F}}^{\ell\times n}
has rank
\ell
.

Proof.

Suppose that
G_{\pi}
does not have rank
\ell
. Then there is a left kernel vector
\mathbf{v}
, so that
\mathbf{v}^{T}G_{\pi}=0
. Let
(f_{1},\ldots,f_{\ell})\in\mathcal{F}
be as in the lemma statement. By Observation
10
, we then have

0=\mathbf{v}^{T}G_{\pi}\mathbf{z}=\mathbf{v}^{T}\begin{bmatrix}f_{1}(\mathbf{x}^{(1)})\\
\vdots\\
f_{\ell}(\mathbf{x}^{(\ell)})\end{bmatrix}

for all values of secrets
\mathbf{x}^{(1)},\ldots,\mathbf{x}^{(\ell)}
. In particular,

\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}v_{i}f_{i}(\mathbf{x}^{(i)})
is identically zero as a polynomial in the variables
x_{k}^{(i)}
for
k\in[m],i\in[\ell]
.
However, this is a contradiction because the variables that show up in
\mathbf{x}^{(i)}
are different for different values of
i\in[\ell]
, and thus cannot cancel with each other.
∎

3.1
Optimal-Download HSS Implies Good Labelweight Codes

We now show the forward direction of the equivalence.

Lemma 12
.

Let
\ell,t,s,d,m
be integers, with
m\geq d
. Suppose there exists a
t
-private,
s
-server HSS
\pi=\left(\mathsf{Share},\mathsf{Eval},\mathsf{Rec}\right)
for some non-trivial (see Definition
6
)
\mathcal{F}\subseteq\text{POLY}_{d,m}(\mathbb{F})^{\ell}
, with download rate
\textsf{DownloadRate}(\pi)=(s-dt)/s
. Let
n=\ell/\textsf{DownloadRate}(\pi)
. Suppose also that
\pi
is linear over
{\mathbb{F}}
, and the
\mathsf{Share}
is
t
-CNF sharing. Then there exists a linear code
\mathcal{C}\subseteq{\mathbb{F}}^{n}
with rate
\textsf{DownloadRate}(\pi)
and a labeling
\mathcal{L}:[n]\to[s]
so that
\Delta_{\mathcal{L}}(\mathcal{C})\geq dt+1
.

Proof.

Let
G_{\pi}\in\mathbb{F}^{\ell\times n}
be the matrix representation of the linear reconstruction algorithm
\mathsf{Rec}
, as in Observation
10
. Let
\mathcal{C}
denote the linear code spanned by the rows of
G_{\pi}
.
By Lemma
11
,
G_{\pi}
has rank
\ell
, so the rate of
\mathcal{C}
is

\frac{\ell}{n}=\textsf{DownloadRate}(\pi).

It remains only to show
\Delta_{\mathcal{L}}(\mathcal{C})\geq dt+1
for some labeling function
\mathcal{L}
. Recall from Observation
10
that
n=\sum_{j\in[s]}n_{j}
is the total number of symbols of
{\mathbb{F}}
that appear in output shares across all of the servers, and that each column of
G
is associated with one such symbol. Let
\mathcal{L}:[n]\to[s]
be the labeling function so that the
j
’th column of
G
is associated with a symbol sent by the
\mathcal{L}(j)
’th server.

Let
\mathbf{x}=\left(\mathbf{x}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{x}_{\ell}\right)\in\left(\mathbb{F}^{m}\right)^{\ell}
be the vector of
m\ell
secrets, each of which is independently
t
-CNF shared to the
s
servers. Let
\mathbf{f}=(f_{1},\ldots,f_{\ell})\in\mathcal{F}
be a function guaranteed by the fact that
\mathcal{F}
is nontrivial (Definition
6
).

As in Observation
10
, let
\mathbf{z}\in\mathbb{F}^{n}
be the vector of
n
output shares. Notice that with the labeling function defined above, this means that the
j
’th coordinate of
\mathbf{z}
is sent by server
\mathcal{L}(j)
.

Let
G_{\pi,1},\ldots,G_{\pi,\ell}
denote the rows of
G_{\pi}
and observe that

G_{\pi}\mathbf{z}=\left[\begin{array}[]{c}G_{\pi,1}^{T}\mathbf{z}\\
G_{\pi,2}^{T}\mathbf{z}\\
\vdots\\
G_{\pi,\ell}^{T}\mathbf{z}\end{array}\right]=\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{x})=\left[\begin{array}[]{c}f_{1}(\mathbf{x}^{(1)})\\
f_{2}(\mathbf{x}^{(2)})\\
\vdots\\
f_{\ell}(\mathbf{x}^{(\ell)})\end{array}\right]

Let
\mathbf{v}\in{\mathbb{F}}^{\ell}\setminus\{\mathbf{0}\}
, and consider the codeword
\mathbf{c}\in\mathcal{C}
given by
\mathbf{c}=\mathbf{v}^{T}G_{\pi}
. Then from the above,

\mathbf{c}^{T}\mathbf{z}=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}v_{i}f_{i}(\mathbf{x}^{(i)})=:f(\mathbf{x}).

In particular, for
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}=\left\{f\right\}
,
\mathbf{c}
gives a linear reconstruction algorithm
\mathsf{Rec}^{\prime}
for a linear HSS
\pi^{\prime}=(\mathsf{Share},\mathsf{Eval},\mathsf{Rec}^{\prime})
for
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
. Notice that
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
is also non-trivial; indeed, each
f_{i}(\mathbf{x}^{(i)})
contains distinct variables (so they cannot cancel), and each
f_{i}
has a monomial with at least
d
distinct variables; thus
f
contains a monomial with at least
d
distinct variables.

Then by Lemma
9
,
\mathsf{Rec}^{\prime}
must depend on output shares from at least
dt+1
distinct servers
j\in[s]
. It follows from the definition of
\mathcal{L}
that
\Delta_{\mathcal{L}}(\mathbf{c})\geq dt+1
. Since
\mathbf{v}
, and hence
\mathbf{c}
, was arbitrary (non-zero), this implies that
\Delta_{\mathcal{L}}(\mathcal{C})\geq dt+1
, as desired.
∎

Lemma
12
shows that the existence of an optimal-rate linear HSS scheme for polynomials implies the existence of a linear code with with optimum labelweight. The converse is also true, but before we prove that result, we need to dive a bit deeper into the structure of codes with good labelweight, which we do next.

3.2
Structural Properties of Labeling Functions for Optimal Labelweight Codes

We establish a few structural properties of the labeling functions
\mathcal{L}
that appear in codes with optimal labelweight.

Our main structural result states that for any code
\mathcal{C}
of rate
(s-dt)/s
, if
\mathcal{L}
is such that
\Delta_{\mathcal{L}}(\mathcal{C})\geq dt+1
, then in fact the labeling function
\mathcal{L}
must be “balanced,” in the sense that each label shows up the same number of times. Below and throughout the paper, for
\mathcal{L}:[n]\to[s]
, we use
\mathcal{L}^{-1}(\lambda)=\left\{j\in[n]:\mathcal{L}(j)=\lambda\right\}
to denote the preimage of
\lambda\in[s]
under
\mathcal{L}
.

Lemma 13
.

Let
\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathbb{F}^{n}
be a rate
(s-dt)/s
linear code and
\mathcal{L}:[n]\to[s]
a labeling of its coordinates by
[s]
so that
\Delta_{\mathcal{L}}(\mathcal{C})\geq dt+1
. Then for all
\lambda\neq\lambda^{\prime}\in[s]
,
\left|\mathcal{L}^{-1}(\lambda)\right|=\left|\mathcal{L}^{-1}(\lambda^{\prime})\right|
.

Proof.

Since
\mathcal{C}
has rate
(s-dt)/s
, there exists some
j\in\mathbb{Q}^{+}
such that
\mathcal{C}
has block length
n=js
and dimension
\ell=j(s-dt)
. Let
G\in\mathbb{F}^{j(s-dt)\times js}
be an arbitrary generator matrix of
\mathcal{C}
. The lemma holds if and only if for each
\lambda\in[s]
, we have
|\mathcal{L}^{-1}(\lambda)|=js/s=j
. Thus, we assume towards a contradiction that this is not the case and break the proof into three cases, depending on the relationship between
s
and
2dt
.

Case 1:
s=2dt
.
In this case, consider
\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{dt}
chosen so that
\sum_{i=1}^{dt}|\mathcal{L}^{-1}(\lambda_{i})|
is minimized. As we assume towards a contradiction that there exists
\lambda\in[s]
such that
\left|\mathcal{L}^{-1}(\lambda)\right|\neq j
, there exists a choice of
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\ldots,\lambda_{dt}\in[s]
so that

\left|\bigcup_{i=1}^{dt}\mathcal{L}^{-1}(\lambda_{i})\right|=jdt-\sigma<jdt

for some
\sigma>0
. Without loss of generality we may assume that
\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{dt}
label the final
jdt-\sigma
columns of
G
. Since
s=2dt
,
G
has
j(s-dt)=jdt
rows; hence there exists some nonzero
\mathbf{m}\in\mathbb{F}^{jdt}
such that
\mathbf{m}^{T}G
has no support in its final
jdt-\sigma
coordinates. But then
\Delta_{\mathcal{L}}(\mathbf{m}^{T}G)\leq dt
, contradicting
\Delta_{\mathcal{L}}(\mathcal{C})\geq dt+1
.

Case 2:
s\geq 2dt+1
.
In this case, consider
\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{dt}
so that
\sum_{i=1}^{dt}|\mathcal{L}^{-1}(\lambda_{i})|
is maximized. As we assume towards a contradiction that there exists
\lambda\in[s]
such that
\left|\mathcal{L}^{-1}(\lambda)\right|\neq j
, there exists a choice of
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\ldots,\lambda_{dt}\in[s]
so that

\left|\bigcup_{i=1}^{dt}\mathcal{L}^{-1}(\lambda_{i})\right|=jdt+\sigma>jdt

for some
\sigma>0
. Without loss of generality we may assume that
\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{dt}
label the final
jdt+\sigma
columns of
G
, leaving the first
js-(jdt+\sigma)=j(s-dt)-\sigma
columns labeled with
[s]\setminus\{\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{dt}\}
. Since
G
has precisely
j(s-dt)
rows, there exists some
\mathbf{m}\in\mathbb{F}^{jdt}
such that
\mathbf{m}^{T}G
has no support in its first
j(s-dt)-\sigma
coordinates. It follows that
\Delta_{\mathcal{L}}(\mathbf{m}^{T}G)\leq dt
, contradicting
\Delta_{\mathcal{L}}(\mathcal{C})\geq dt+1
.

Case 3:
s\leq 2dt-1
.
Note that
s\geq dt+1
, as we assume that
\Delta_{\mathcal{L}}(\mathcal{C})\geq dt+1
. Thus, we may write
s=dt+\hat{s}
, where
\hat{s}\in[dt-1]
. In this case, consider
\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{\hat{s}}
so that
\sum_{i=1}^{\hat{s}}|\mathcal{L}^{-1}(\lambda_{i})|
is minimized. As we assume towards a contradiction that there exists
\lambda\in[s]
such that
\left|\mathcal{L}^{-1}(\lambda)\right|\neq j
, there exists a choice of
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\ldots,\lambda_{\hat{s}}\in[s]
so that

\left|\bigcup_{i=1}^{\hat{s}}\mathcal{L}^{-1}(\lambda_{i})\right|=j\hat{s}-\sigma<j\hat{s}

for some
\sigma>0
. Without loss of generality we may assume that
\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{\hat{s}}
label the first
j\hat{s}-\sigma
columns of
G
. Since there are
j\hat{s}
rows of
G
, there exists some
\mathbf{m}\in\mathbb{F}^{j\hat{s}}
such that
\mathbf{m}^{T}G
has no support in its first
j\hat{s}-\sigma
coordinates. It follows that
\Delta_{\mathcal{L}}(\mathbf{m}^{T}G)\leq dt
, contradicting
\Delta_{\mathcal{L}}(\mathcal{C})\geq dt+1
.
∎

Corollary 14
.

Let
s,d,t\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
satisfying
s-dt>0
. Let
\mathcal{C}
be a rate
(s-dt)/s
linear code and
\mathcal{L}
a labeling of its coordinates by
[s]
so that
\Delta_{\mathcal{L}}(\mathcal{C})\geq dt+1
. Then there exists some
j\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
such that:

(i)

\mathcal{C}
has length
n=js
and dimension
\ell=j(s-dt)
;

(ii)

|\mathcal{L}^{-1}(\lambda)|=j
for all
\lambda\in[s]
;

(iii)

there is a re-ordering of the coordinates of
\mathcal{C}
so that
\mathcal{L}:[js]\to[s]
is given by
\mathcal{L}:x\mapsto\lceil x/j\rceil
.

Proof.

Define
j=n/s
so that
js
is equal to the block length of
\mathcal{C}
. Then Lemma
13
implies that
|\mathcal{L}^{-1}(\lambda)|=j
for all
\lambda\in[s]
, and in particular that
j
is an integer. This establishes all three points.
∎

Corollary 15
.

Let
j
be a positive integer, and
let
\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{js}
be a linear code of length
js
and dimension
j(s-dt)
. Let
\mathcal{L}:[js]\to[s]
be a labeling such that
\Delta_{\mathcal{L}}(\mathcal{C})\geq dt+1
. Let
G\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{j(s-dt)\times js}
be an arbitrary generator matrix for
\mathcal{C}
. Given
\Lambda\subseteq[s]
, let
G(\Lambda)
be the submatrix of
G
consisting of the columns
G_{i}
of
G
for all
i\in[js]
so that
\mathcal{L}(i)\in\Lambda
.
Then for any
\Lambda\subseteq[s]
with
|\Lambda|=s-dt
,
G(\Lambda)\in\mathbb{F}^{j(s-dt)\times j(s-dt)}
and
\det(G(\Lambda))\neq 0
.

Proof.

The dimensions of
G
follow immediately from Corollary
14
; it remains only to show that
G(\Lambda)
is non-singular. Assume towards a contradiction there exists
\mathbf{m}\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{j(s-dt)}
such that
\mathbf{m}^{T}G(\Lambda)=\mathbf{0}
.
But then the support of
\mathbf{m}^{T}G\in\mathcal{C}
does not include any coordinates labeled with any
\lambda\in\Lambda
. In particular,
\Delta_{\mathcal{L}}(\mathbf{m}^{T}G)\leq dt
, which contradicts
\Delta_{\mathcal{L}}(\mathcal{C})\geq dt+1
.
∎

3.3
Good Labelweight Codes Imply Optimal-Download HSS

Now, we can prove a converse to Lemma
12
.

Theorem 16
.

Let
\ell,t,s,d,m,n
be integers, with
m\geq d
and
\ell s=n(s-dt)
.
Suppose that there exists a linear code
\mathcal{C}\subseteq{\mathbb{F}}^{n}
and a labeling
\mathcal{L}:[n]\to[s]
so that
\Delta_{\mathcal{L}}(\mathcal{C})\geq dt+1
.
Then there exists a
t
-private,
s
-server linear HSS
\pi=(\mathsf{Share},\mathsf{Eval},\mathsf{Rec})
for
\mathrm{POLY}_{d,m}({\mathbb{F}})^{\ell}
with download rate
\textsf{DownloadRate}(\pi)\geq(s-dt)/s
.

Proof.

The proof is by construction; we give an example of this construction in Appendix
B
, so the reader may wish to flip to that as they read the proof.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be as in the theorem statement, and let
G\in{\mathbb{F}}^{\ell\times n}
be any generator matrix for
\mathcal{C}
.
In order to define
\pi
, we need to define the functions
\mathsf{Share}
,
\mathsf{Eval}
, and
\mathsf{Rec}
. For
\mathsf{Share}
, we will use
t
-CNF sharing (Definition
7
). We define
\mathsf{Rec}
using the generator matrix
G
. In particular, we will (soon) define
\mathsf{Eval}
so that server
j\in[s]
returns
n_{j}:=|\mathcal{L}^{-1}(j)|
elements of
{\mathbb{F}}
as output shares. We will gather these output shares into a vector
\mathbf{z}\in{\mathbb{F}}^{n}
, where
n=\sum_{j\in[s]}n_{j}
. Then
\mathsf{Rec}
will be given by

\mathsf{Rec}(\mathbf{z})=G\mathbf{z}.

Finally, it remains to define
\mathsf{Eval}
. To do so, we will set up a linear system that essentially says “the
\mathsf{Rec}
function we just defined is correct.” Then we will show that this linear system has a solution, and that will yield our
\mathsf{Eval}
function.

Below, we assume without loss of generality that the function
\mathbf{f}=(f_{1},\ldots,f_{\ell})
that the HSS scheme is trying to compute has
f_{j}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{m})=\prod_{i=1}^{d}x_{i}
for all
j\in[\ell]
, and we will define
\mathsf{Eval}(\mathbf{f},j,\mathbf{y}_{j})
on only this
\mathbf{f}
. To obtain the general result for any polynomials
(p_{1},p_{2},\ldots,p_{\ell})\in\mathrm{POLY}_{d,m}({\mathbb{F}})^{\ell}
, we first observe that the argument goes through if the
f_{j}
’s are
any
monomials of degree at most
d
, possibly with a leading coefficient; this follows by re-ordering the secrets, and possibly including some dummy secrets that are identically equal to a constant (that is, increasing the parameter
m
, which does not appear in any of the results) to obtain monomials of degree less than
d
and/or with a leading coefficient.
Then to pass to general polynomials, and not just monomials, we observe that since
\mathsf{Rec}
is linear, we may define
\mathsf{Eval}
additively; that is, if
p_{i}(\mathbf{x}^{(i)})=\sum_{r}f_{i,r}(\mathbf{x}^{(i)})
for some monomials
f_{i,r}
, server
j
will compute
\mathsf{Eval}
on each
\mathbf{f}_{r}=(f_{1,r},\ldots,f_{\ell,r})
, and then return their sum.

Now we return to the task of setting up a linear system to define
\mathsf{Eval}
for the particular function
\mathbf{f}
defined above.
In order to set up this linear system, we introduce some notation. Let

\mathcal{T}=\{T\subseteq[s]:|T|=t\}
be the set of size-
t
subsets of
[s]
. Let
\mathbf{x}=(\mathbf{x}^{(1)},\ldots,\mathbf{x}^{(\ell)})\in({\mathbb{F}}^{m})^{\ell}
denote the secrets to be shared. For
T\in\mathcal{T}
,
r\in[\ell]
, and
j\in[m]
, let
y^{(r)}_{j,T}
denote the CNF shares of
x^{(r)}_{j}
, so

x^{(i)}_{j}=\sum_{T}y^{(i)}_{j,T}.

Thus, for each
i\in[\ell]
the function we would like to recover is

f_{i}(\mathbf{x}^{(i)})=\sum_{\mathbf{T}\in\mathcal{T}^{d}}\prod_{k=1}^{d}y^{(i)}_{k,T_{k}}.

(1)

Let
\mathbf{y}_{j}
denote the set of CNF shares that server
j
holds:
\mathbf{y}_{j}=(y_{k,T}^{(i)}:k\in[d],T\in\mathcal{T},i\in[\ell],j\not\in T)
. We will treat
\mathbf{y}_{j}
as tuples of formal variables.

Next, we define the following classes of monomials, in the variables
y_{j,T}^{(i)}
. Let

\mathcal{M}=\left\{y_{1,T_{1}}^{(i)}y_{2,T_{2}}^{(i)}\cdots y_{d,T_{d}}^{(i)}\;:\;\mathbf{T}\in\mathcal{T}^{d},i\in[\ell]\right\}.

Given a server
j\in[s]
, let

\mathcal{M}_{j}=\left\{y_{1,T_{1}}^{(i)}y_{2,T_{2}}^{(i)}\cdots y_{d,T_{d}}^{(i)}\in\mathcal{M}\;:\;\mathbf{T}\in\mathcal{T}^{d},i\in[\ell],j\not\in\bigcup_{k\in[d]}T_{k}\right\}.

That is,
\mathcal{M}_{j}
is
the subset of
\mathcal{M}
locally computable by server
j
.

The function
\mathsf{Eval}(\mathbf{f},j,\mathbf{y}_{j})
that determines server
j
’s output shares will be defined by a sequence of
n_{j}=|\mathcal{L}^{-1}(j)|
polynomials of degree
d
, constructed from the monomials in
\mathcal{M}_{j}
. To that end, we will define a vector of variables
\mathbf{e}\in{\mathbb{F}}^{\sum_{r\in[n]}|\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{L}(r)}|}
, indexed by pairs
(r,\chi)
for
\chi\in\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{L}(r)}
. The vector
\mathbf{e}
will encode the function
\mathsf{Eval}
as follows. For each
r\in[n]
, we define
z_{r}=z_{r}(\mathbf{y}_{\mathcal{L}(r)})
to be the polynomial in the variables
\mathbf{y}_{\mathcal{L}(r)}
given by

z_{r}(\mathbf{y}_{\mathcal{L}(r)}):=\sum_{\chi\in\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{L}(r)}}\mathbf{e}_{r,\chi}\cdot\chi(\mathbf{y}_{\mathcal{L}(r)}).

(2)

Then we define
\mathsf{Eval}
by

\mathsf{Eval}(\mathbf{f},j,\mathbf{y}_{j})=(z_{r}(\mathbf{y}_{j}):r\in\mathcal{L}^{-1}(j))\in{\mathbb{F}}^{n_{j}}

for each server
j\in[s]
.

Now we will set up our system to solve for the coefficients in
\mathbf{e}
, which will define
\mathsf{Eval}
as above.

Define the matrix
S\in{\mathbb{F}}^{\ell|\mathcal{M}|\times\sum_{r\in[n]}|\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{L}(r)}|}
as follows.

•

The rows of
S
are indexed by pairs
(i,{\mathfrak{m}})\in[\ell]\times\mathcal{M}
.

•

The columns of
S
are indexed by pairs
(r,\chi)
for
r\in[s]
and
\chi\in\mathcal{M}_{r}
.

•

The entry of
S
indexed by
(i,{\mathfrak{m}})
and
(r,\chi)
is given by:

S[(i,{\mathfrak{m}}),(r,\chi)]=\begin{cases}G[i,r]&{\mathfrak{m}}=\chi\\
0&\text{else}\end{cases}.

Define a vector
\mathbf{g}\in{\mathbb{F}}^{\ell|\mathcal{M}|}
so that the coordinates of
\mathbf{g}
are indexed by pairs
(i,{\mathfrak{m}})\in[\ell]\times\mathcal{M}
, so that

\mathbf{g}[(i,{\mathfrak{m}})]=\begin{cases}1&\psi_{i}({\mathfrak{m}})\\
0&\text{else}\end{cases}

where

\psi_{i}({\mathfrak{m}})=\begin{cases}1&\text{ ${\mathfrak{m}}$ is of the form $\prod_{k=1}^{d}y_{k,T_{k}}^{(i)}$ for some $\mathbf{T}\in\mathcal{T}^{d}$ }\\
0&\text{else}\end{cases}

Notice that (
1
) implies that for
i\in[\ell]
,

f_{i}(\mathbf{x}^{(i)})=\sum_{\mathbf{T}\in\mathcal{T}^{d}}\prod_{k=1}^{d}y_{k,T_{k}}^{(i)}=\sum_{{\mathfrak{m}}\in\mathcal{M}}\psi_{i}({\mathfrak{m}})\cdot{\mathfrak{m}}(\mathbf{y}).

(3)

Claim 17
.

Suppose that
S\cdot\mathbf{e}=\mathbf{g}
. Then the HSS scheme
\pi=(\mathsf{Share},\mathsf{Eval},\mathsf{Rec})
, where
\mathsf{Share}
and
\mathsf{Rec}
are as above, and
\mathsf{Eval}
is defined by
\mathbf{e}
as above, is correct. (That is, it satisfies the
Correctness
property in Definition
2
).

Proof.

Recall that we will set
\mathsf{Rec}(\mathbf{z})=G\mathbf{z}
, where the
r
’th coordinate of
\mathbf{z}
is given by
z_{r}
as in (
2
). In particular, for
i\in[\ell]
, the
i
’th output of
\mathsf{Rec}(\mathbf{z})
is

\displaystyle(G\mathbf{z})_{i}

\displaystyle=\sum_{r\in[n]}G[i,r]\cdot z_{r}=\sum_{r\in[n]}G[i,r]\left(\sum_{\chi\in\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{L}(r)}}\mathbf{e}_{(r,\chi)}\chi(\mathbf{y}_{\mathcal{L}(r)})\right).

(4)

On the other hand, consider the quantity
f_{i}(\mathbf{x}^{(i)})
that we want to recover, from (
3
). This is:

\displaystyle f_{i}(\mathbf{x}^{(i)})

\displaystyle=\ \sum_{{\mathfrak{m}}\in\mathcal{M}}\psi_{i}({\mathfrak{m}})\cdot{\mathfrak{m}}(\mathbf{y})

\displaystyle=\sum_{{\mathfrak{m}}\in\mathcal{M}}(S\cdot\mathbf{e})_{(i,{\mathfrak{m}})}\cdot{\mathfrak{m}}(\mathbf{y})\qquad\text{ as $S\cdot\mathbf{e}=\mathbf{g}$}

\displaystyle=\sum_{{\mathfrak{m}}\in\mathcal{M}}\left(\sum_{r\in[n]}\sum_{\chi\in\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{L}(r)}}S[(i,{\mathfrak{m}}),(r,\chi)]\cdot\mathbf{e}_{(r,\chi)}\right)\cdot{\mathfrak{m}}(\mathbf{y})

\displaystyle=\sum_{{\mathfrak{m}}\in\mathcal{M}}\left(\sum_{r\in[n]}\sum_{\chi\in\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{L}(r)}}\mathbf{1}[{\mathfrak{m}}=\chi]G[i,r]\cdot\mathbf{e}_{(r,\chi)}\right)\cdot{\mathfrak{m}}(\mathbf{y})

\displaystyle=\sum_{{\mathfrak{m}}\in\mathcal{M}}\left(\sum_{r\in[n]}\mathbf{1}[{\mathfrak{m}}\in\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{L}(r)}]G[i,r]\cdot\mathbf{e}_{(r,{\mathfrak{m}})}\right)\cdot{\mathfrak{m}}(\mathbf{y})

\displaystyle=\sum_{r\in[n]}G[i,r]\left(\sum_{\chi\in\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{L}(r)}}\mathbf{e}_{(r,\chi)}\chi(\mathbf{y}_{\mathcal{L}(r)})\right)

\displaystyle=(G\mathbf{z})_{i}\qquad\text{ by \eqref{eq:recis} }

\displaystyle=\mathsf{Rec}(\mathbf{z})_{i}.

Thus,
\mathsf{Rec}
returns what it is supposed to with probability
1
, and the HSS scheme is correct.
∎

Given Claim
17
, we now only need to show that we can find a vector
\mathbf{e}
so that
S\cdot\mathbf{e}=\mathbf{g}
. To do this, we will show that the matrix
S
has full row rank, and in particular we can solve the above affine system.

Claim 18
.

Let
S
be as above. Then
S
has full row rank.

Proof.

For
{\mathfrak{m}}\in\mathcal{M}
, consider the block
S^{({\mathfrak{m}})}
of

restricted to the rows
\{(i,{\mathfrak{m}}):i\in[\ell]\}
and the columns
\{(r,{\mathfrak{m}}):r\in[n]\}
. Thus,

, and we may index the rows of
S^{({\mathfrak{m}})}
by
i\in[\ell]
and the columns by
r\in[n]
. By the definition of
S
, we have

S^{({\mathfrak{m}})}[i,r]=G[i,r]\cdot\mathbf{1}[{\mathfrak{m}}\in\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{L}(r)}].

For
\Lambda\subseteq[s]
, let
G(\Lambda)
denote the restriction of
G
to the columns

so that
\mathcal{L}(r)\in\Lambda
. For
{\mathfrak{m}}\in\mathcal{M}
, let
\Lambda_{\mathfrak{m}}=\left\{\lambda\in[s]:{\mathfrak{m}}\in\mathcal{M}_{\lambda}\right\}.
Then

\mathrm{rank}(S^{({\mathfrak{m}})})=\mathrm{rank}(G(\Lambda_{{\mathfrak{m}}})),

(5)

as we can make
G(\Lambda_{{\mathfrak{m}}})
out of

by dropping all-zero columns.
We claim that the rank in (
5
) is exactly

. To see this, first
note that

. Indeed, if

{\mathfrak{m}}=\prod_{k=1}^{d}y^{(i)}_{k,T_{k}}
for some

, then the only

so that

are those so that

such

.

Next, observe that, after rearranging rows and columns appropriately,

∎

Claim

says that any

4
Linear Codes with Good Labelweight

Now that we know that download-optimal linear HSS schemes are equivalent to linear codes with good labelweight, we focus on constructions and limitations of such codes. In this section we give a construction, and a nearly-matching infeasibility result. We begin by stating the constructive result, which as noted in the Introduction is a very slight improvement over the construction in
[

]
.

Theorem 19

(Construction of linear codes with good labelweight)
.

Let
{\mathbb{F}}
be a finite field of size
q
.
Let
s,d,t\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
such that
s-dt>0
. For all integers

j\geq\begin{cases}\log_{q}(s-1)&q^{j}\text{ odd, or }(s-dt)\not\in\{3,q^{j}-1\}\\
\log_{q}(s-2)&q^{j}\text{ even, and }(s-dt)\in\{3,q^{j}-1\}\end{cases},

there is an explicit construction of a linear code
\mathcal{C}\subset{\mathbb{F}}^{js}
of dimension
j(s-dt)
, and a labeling
\mathcal{L}:[js]\to[s]
such that
\Delta_{\mathcal{L}}(\mathcal{C})\geq dt+1
.

The following theorem says that Theorem
19
is basically optimal, in that we cannot take
j
to be substantially smaller:

Theorem 20

(Limitations on linear codes with good labelweight)
.

Let
{\mathbb{F}}
be a finite field of size
q
. Let
s,d,t\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
such that
s-dt>0
.
Suppose that there is a code
\mathcal{C}\subseteq{\mathbb{F}}^{n}
with dimension
\ell
and rate
(s-dt)/s
; and a labeling function
\mathcal{L}:[n]\to[s]
so that
\Delta_{\mathcal{L}}(\mathcal{C})\geq dt+1
. Suppose that
j
is such that
\ell=j(s-dt)
and
n=js
. Then
j
is an integer, and

j\geq\lceil\max\left\{\log_{q}(s-dt+1),\log_{q}(dt+1)\right\}\rceil.

Remark 3

(Gap between upper and lower bounds on
j
.)
.

As discussed in the Introduction, Theorem
20
and Theorem
19
do not quite match. However, because
j
must be an integer, in fact the bounds are exactly the same for many parameter settings, especially when
q
is large.

We also remark that, when Theorems
20
and
19
disagree, we conjecture that the
construction
(Theorem
19
) is the correct one. Indeed, our construction in Theorem
19
follows from “puffing up” a totally nonsingular (TN) matrix; the value of the parameter

has to do with the field size over which this TN matrix is defined. We show in the Section
4.2.2
that, assuming the MDS conjecture for extension fields, the field size that we use in our construction—and hence the value of

—is the best possible. Thus, if Theorem
19
is not optimal, either the MDS conjecture is false over extension fields, or else there is an alternate way to construct Block TN matrices without going through TN matrices over larger fields.

Given Lemma
12
and Theorem
16
, Theorems
19
and
20
immediately imply the following corollary about linear HSS schemes.

Corollary 21

(Classification of Amortization Parameter for Linear HSS Schemes)
.

Let
s,d,t,m,\ell
be positive integers, so that
m\geq d
.

Let
\pi=(\mathsf{Share},\mathsf{Eval},\mathsf{Rec})
be a linear HSS scheme for some nontrivial (Definition
6
) function class
\mathcal{F}\subseteq\mathrm{POLY}_{d,m}({\mathbb{F}})
, with download rate
(s-dt)/s
. Then
\ell=j(s-dt)
for some integer

that satisfies

j\geq\max\{\lceil\log_{q}(s-dt+1)\rceil,\lceil\log_{q}(dt+1)\rceil\}.

Conversely, let
j
be any integer so that

is as in Theorem

. Then there are explicit constructions of linear HSS schemes for any

with download rate

and amortization parameter

.

Remark 4
.

As in Remark
3
, we observe that the bounds are quite close. Indeed, for any setting of parameters, there is at most one value of
\ell
(namely,
\ell=j(s-dt)
for one particular integer

) for which we do not know whether or not there exists a download-optimal linear HSS scheme for
\mathrm{POLY}_{d,m}({\mathbb{F}})^{\ell}
. Moreover, for many parameter settings (especially when
q
is large), the bounds exactly match.

We prove Theorems
19
and
20
later in this section.
The technical meat of the proofs is Lemma

(stated later), which characterizes codes of high labelweight in terms of

, which we informally defined in the Introduction and which we formally define in Definition

below. Theorems

and

then follow from an analysis of block totally nonsingular matrices.

4.1
Block Totally-Nonsingular Matrices

Now that we understand the structure of optimal labeling functions, we move on to the connection to block totally-nonsingular matrices, which is the main technical result in this section. We begin with some definitions.

We say that a matrix
A
is
totally nonsingular
if any square sub-matrix of
A
(not necessarily contiguous) is nonsingular
[
Gan80
]
. We extend the definition of total nonsingularity to block matrices as follows.

Definition 10

(Block Totally-Nonsingular Matrices)
.

Let
{\mathbb{F}}
be a finite field, and fix integers
j,r,u
.
Let
\mathbf{A}=\left[A_{i,k}\;:\;i\in[r],k\in[u]\right]\in\left(\text{GL}(\mathbb{F},j)\right)^{r\times u}
be a
r\times u
block array of invertible
j\times j
matrices
A_{i,k}
. We say that
\mathbf{A}
is
Block Totally Nonsingular
(Block TN) if every square sub-array of block matrices is full-rank. More precisely, for all
S\subseteq[r]
and
S^{\prime}\subseteq[u]
of the same size, the matrix
\mathbf{A}^{\prime}=[A_{i,i^{\prime}}\in\mathbf{A}\;:\;i\in S,i^{\prime}\in S^{\prime}]\in{\mathbb{F}}^{j|S|\times j|S^{\prime}|}
is nonsingular.

With Definition
10
out of the way, we can state and prove the main technical lemma of this section, which says that constructing codes with good labelweight is equivalent to constructing Block TN matrices:

Lemma 22
.

Let
j,s,d,t
be positive integers so that
s>dt
, and let
q
be a prime power. Then the following are equivalent.

(i)

There is a linear code
\mathcal{C}\subseteq{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{js}
with block length
js
and dimension
j(s-dt)
and a labeling
\mathcal{L}:[js]\to[s]
so that
\Delta_{\mathcal{L}}(\mathcal{C})\geq dt+1
.

(ii)

There exists a Block TN matrix
\mathbf{A}\in GL({\mathbb{F}}_{q},j)^{(s-dt)\times dt}
.

Moreover, the equivalence is constructive: if
\mathbf{A}
is a Block TN matrix as in (ii), then the code
\mathcal{C}
in (i) is generated by the matrix
[I|\mathbf{A}]
. If
\mathcal{C}
is a code as in (i), then it has a generator matrix of the form
[I|\mathbf{A}]
, where
\mathbf{A}
is the Block TN matrix as in (ii).

Proof.

We begin by showing that (i) implies (ii).
Let
\mathcal{C}
be a code as in (i), and
let
G\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{j(s-dt)\times js}
be a generator matrix of
\mathcal{C}
. Corollary
14
implies that the labeling function
\mathcal{L}
may be given by
\mathcal{L}:x\mapsto\lceil x/j\rceil
after a re-ordering of the codeword coordinates; and Corollary
15
implies that after this re-ordering, the first
s-dt
columns of any generator matrix
G
for
\mathcal{C}
form a square invertible matrix, so we may put
G
in systematic form by a sequence of row operations. Hence without loss of generality, we may write

G=[I|\mathbf{A}]

for some matrix
\mathbf{A}\in{\mathbb{F}}^{j(s-dt)\times jdt}
.
We will view
G
as a matrix of
j\times j
blocks, as follows:

G=\left[\begin{array}[]{c|c}\begin{matrix}I^{j\times j}&&&\\
&I^{j\times j}&&\\
&&\ddots&\\
&&&I^{j\times j}\end{matrix}&\left[\begin{matrix}A_{1,1}&A_{1,2}&\cdots&A_{1,dt}\\
A_{2,1}&A_{2,2}&\cdots&A_{2,dt}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
A_{s-dt,1}&A_{s-dt,2}&\cdots&A_{s-dt,dt}\end{matrix}\right]\end{array}\right]=:\left[\begin{array}[]{l}G_{1}\\
G_{2}\\
\vdots\\
G_{s-dt}\end{array}\right].

where
G_{1},G_{2},\ldots,G_{s-dt}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{j\times js}
.

We will show that
\mathbf{A}
is a block TN matrix as in (ii). The dimensions follow by construction, so we must show that every square sub-array of
\mathbf{A}
is nonsingular.
Let
\mathbf{A}^{\prime}\in(GL({\mathbb{F}}_{q},j))^{u\times u}
denote an arbitrary
u\times u
block sub-array of
\mathbf{A}
; suppose that the
u
block-rows in
\mathbf{A}^{\prime}
are indexed by
U\subseteq[s-dt]
and the
u
block-columns of
\mathbf{A}^{\prime}
are indexed by
V\subseteq[dt]
.

Let
\mathbf{A}^{\prime\prime}\in(GL({\mathbb{F}}_{q},j))^{u\times dt-u}
denote the block sub-array that contains block-rows indexed by
U
and block-columns indexed by
[dt]\setminus V
. That is,
\mathbf{A}^{\prime\prime}
is the “rest” of
\mathbf{A}
, restricted to the rows that
\mathbf{A}^{\prime}
touches.

To show that
\mathbf{A}^{\prime}
is full rank, we will show that for every vector
\mathbf{m}\in({\mathbb{F}}^{j})^{u}
,
\mathbf{m}^{T}\mathbf{A}^{\prime}\neq\mathbf{0}
.

Suppose towards a contradiction that
\mathbf{m}^{T}\mathbf{A}^{\prime}=\mathbf{0}
for some
\mathbf{m}
.
Consider the vector
\tilde{\mathbf{m}}\in({\mathbb{F}}^{j})^{(s-dt)}
that is identified with
\mathbf{m}
on
U
, and is
0
elsewhere.
Then
\mathbf{c}:=\tilde{\mathbf{m}}^{T}G
is a codeword in
\mathcal{C}
. Consider which blocks intersect the support of
\mathbf{c}
:

•

On the systematic coordinates (the first
s-dt
blocks), the support of
\mathbf{c}
is exactly the blocks indexed by
U
; there are
u
of these.

•

On the blocks indexed by
V
, the support of
\mathbf{c}
is the support of
\mathbf{m}^{T}\mathbf{A}^{\prime}
. As we are assuming that this is zero,
\mathbf{c}
has no support on these blocks.

•

On the blocks indexed by
[dt]\setminus V
, there is at most
|[dt]\setminus V|=dt-u
blocks of support.

But together, this implies that the number of blocks that the support of
\mathbf{c}
touches is at most
u+(dt-u)=dt<dt+1
. Since by construction, each block corresponds to a set
\mathcal{L}^{-1}(\lambda)
for some
\lambda\in[s]
, this implies that
\Delta_{\mathcal{L}}(\mathbf{c})\leq dt
, which contradicts the assumption that
\Delta_{\mathcal{L}}(\mathcal{C})\geq dt+1.
Thus
\mathbf{A}^{\prime}
is non-singular. Since
\mathbf{A}^{\prime}
was arbitrary, we conclude that all such matrices are non-singular, and hence
\mathbf{A}
is block TN, proving (ii).

Now we show that (ii) implies (i). Let
\mathbf{A}
be as in (ii), and consider the matrix

G=[I|\mathbf{A}].

Define
\mathcal{L}:[js]\to[s]
by
\mathcal{L}(x)=\lceil x/j\rceil
, and let
\mathcal{C}\subseteq{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{js}
be the code of dimension
j(s-dt)
whose generator matrix is
G
. Then
\mathcal{C}
has the block length and dimension in (i), so it remains to show that
\Delta_{\mathcal{L}}(\mathcal{C})\geq dt+1
. Let
\mathbf{c}=\mathbf{m}^{T}G
be an arbitrary codeword of
\mathcal{C}
, where we view
\mathbf{m}
as an element of
({\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{j})^{s-dt}
. Let
U\subseteq[s-dt]
be the support of
\mathbf{m}
(that is, the set of
blocks
on which it is nonzero); say that
|U|=u
. Consider
\mathbf{m}^{T}\mathbf{A}
, viewed as an element of
({\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{j})^{dt}
.
Let
V\subseteq[dt]
be the support of
\mathbf{m}^{T}\mathbf{A}
(again, note that
V
represents a set of
blocks
). We claim that
|V|\geq dt+1-u
. Indeed, otherwise the sub-array
\mathbf{A}^{\prime}
of
\mathbf{A}
whose block-rows are indexed by
U
and whose block-columns are indexed
by
V^{c}
would be a
u\times u
sub-array so that
\mathbf{m}_{U}^{T}\mathbf{A}^{\prime}=\mathbf{0}
(where
\mathbf{m}_{U}
represents the restriction of
\mathbf{m}
to
U
). Thus,
\mathbf{A}^{\prime}
would be singular, but this would contradict the block total nonsingularity of
\mathbf{A}
. We conclude that
|V|\geq dt+1-u
, as claimed.

Thus, the support of
\mathbf{c}
includes
u
blocks from the systematic coordinates, and at least
dt+1-u
blocks from the non-systematic coordinates, for a total of at least
dt+1
. With our definition of
\mathcal{L}
, this implies that
\Delta_{\mathcal{L}}(\mathbf{c})\geq dt+1
. Since
\mathbf{c}
was arbitrary, this implies that
\Delta_{\mathcal{L}}(\mathcal{C})\geq dt+1
as well. This proves (i).

∎

4.2
Understanding Block-TN Matrices: Proofs of Theorems
19
and
20

Lemma
22
tells us that constructing linear codes with optimal labelweight is equivalent to constructing block-TN matrices. Thus, in this section, we investigate how well we can do this, which will lead to the Theorems
19
and
20
stated earlier.

4.2.1
Limitations on block-TN matrices

We begin with infeasibility results.
In this section, we will prove the following lemma.

Lemma 23
.

Let
r,u\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
such that
\min\{r,u\}>1
. Let
q
be a prime power. Suppose that there exists a block-TN matrix
\mathbf{A}\in(GL({\mathbb{F}}_{q},j))^{r\times u}
. Then

j\geq\lceil\max\left\{\log_{q}(r+1),\log_{q}(u+1)\right\}\rceil.

We note that, along with Lemma
22
, Lemma
23
immediately implies Theorem
20
.

Before we prove Lemma
23
, we make an observation about the maximum size of sets of invertible matrices whose differences are also invertible:

Lemma 24
.

Let
{\mathbb{F}}
be a finite field of size
q
, and
let
\mathcal{W}\subseteq\text{GL}(\mathbb{F},j)
be a set so that for all distinct
W,W^{\prime}\in\mathcal{W}
,
\det(W-W^{\prime})\neq 0
. Then
|W|\leq q^{j}-1
.

Proof.

First, we observe that no element
W\in\mathcal{W}
can have a first row that is all zero, as each must be non-singular. Similarly, any two elements
W,W^{\prime}\in\mathcal{W}
cannot have the same first row, otherwise
W-W^{\prime}
would have a zero first row and be singular. The bound on
|W|
then follows immediately from the observation that there are only
q^{j}-1
non-zero elements of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{j}
.
∎

We remark that the bound above is tight. Indeed, consider the following example, which we will also use in our construction later. (See, e.g.,
[
War94
]
for more details).

Example 1
.

Fix a prime power
q
and any integer
e
.
Let
\mathbb{E}
be a finite field of size
q^{e}
, and let
\mathbb{F}\leq\mathbb{E}
be a subfield of size
q
.
Then
\mathbb{E}
is a vector space over
{\mathbb{F}}
of dimension
e
. Let
\mathcal{B}=\{b_{1},\ldots,b_{e}\}
be any basis for
\mathbb{E}
over
{\mathbb{F}}
. For any
\alpha\in\mathbb{E}
, multiplication by
\alpha
is an
{\mathbb{F}}
-linear map, and in particular it can be represented in the basis
\mathcal{B}
by a matrix
M_{\alpha}\in{\mathbb{F}}^{e\times e}
. Let
\varphi:\mathbb{E}\to{\mathbb{F}}^{e\times e}
be the embedding so that
\varphi:\alpha\mapsto M_{\alpha}
.

Then let
\mathcal{W}=\varphi(\mathbb{E}^{*})
.
We have
|\mathcal{W}|=q^{e}-1
. Further, every element of
\mathcal{W}
is invertible, as
\varphi(\alpha)^{-1}=\varphi(\alpha^{-1})
. Further, since
\varphi(\alpha)-\varphi(\beta)=\varphi(\alpha-\beta)
,
\mathcal{W}
is closed under differences and in particular
W-W^{\prime}
is also invertible for any distinct
W,W^{\prime}\in\mathcal{W}
.

Now we can prove Lemma
23
.

Proof of Lemma
23
.

Let
\mathbf{A}
be a block-TN matrix with entries
A_{i,k}\in\text{GL}(\mathbb{F}_{q},j)
:

\mathbf{A}=\left[\begin{matrix}A_{1,1}&A_{1,2}&\cdots&A_{1,u}\\
A_{2,1}&A_{2,2}&\cdots&A_{2,u}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
A_{r,1}&A_{r,2}&\cdots&A_{r,u}\end{matrix}\right].

Notice that the property of being Block-TN is maintained under elementary row operations within a given block-row
\mathbf{A}[i,:]
or elementary column operations within a given block-column
\mathbf{A}[:,k]
. Hence

\mathbf{A}^{\prime}=\left[\begin{array}[]{cccc}I&A_{1,1}^{-1}A_{1,2}&\cdots&A_{1,1}^{-1}A_{1,u}\\
I&A_{2,1}^{-1}A_{2,2}&\cdots&A_{2,1}^{-1}A_{2,u}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
I&A_{r,1}^{-1}A_{r,2}&\cdots&A_{r,1}^{-1}A_{r,u}\end{array}\right]

is also block TN. In particular, for any distinct
\rho,\sigma\in[r]
and
\mu\in[u]\setminus\{1\}
, we must have

0\neq\det\left(\left[\begin{matrix}I&A_{\rho,1}^{-1}A_{\rho,u}\\
I&A_{\sigma,1}^{-1}A_{\sigma,u}\end{matrix}\right]\right)=\det\left(A_{\sigma,1}^{-1}A_{\sigma,u}-A_{\rho,1}^{-1}A_{\rho,u}\right),

where the non-equality is due to the assumption of block total nonsingularity, and the equality is due to the fact that

\det\begin{bmatrix}I&W\\
I&W^{\prime}\end{bmatrix}=\det(W^{\prime}-W)

for any matrices
W,W^{\prime}
.
It follows that for all
\mu\in[u]
,
\mathbf{A}^{\prime}[:,\mu]
is a
r
-tuple of distinct elements of
\text{GL}(\mathbb{F}_{q},j)
, satisfying the condition that the sum of any two coordinates is again an element of
\text{GL}(\mathbb{F}_{q},j)
. Then by Lemma
24
, we have
r\leq q^{j}-1
so that
j\geq\log_{q}(r+1)
. By an identical argument, we also have that
j\geq\log_{q}(u+1)
, and this yields the result.
∎

4.2.2
Connection to MDS Conjecture

Before we give our construction (proving Theorem
19
), we take a quick detour to expain the connection between such constructions and the
MDS conjecture
(Conjecture
27
below) over extension fields, which is a long-standing open problem. This connection will inform our construction in Theorem
19
, and also suggest that we may not be able to do better.

As we will see formally below in Lemma
31
, one way to construct Block TN matrices with entries in
\text{GL}(\mathbb{F}_{q},j)
is to construct (non–block) TN matrices with entries in
\mathbb{F}_{q^{j}}
, and then “puff up” each entry using the embedding
\varphi
from Example
1
. We show below in Theorem
28
that constructing TN matrices is equivalent to constructing
MDS matrices
(that is, matrices that are generator matrices of MDS codes) over extension fields, which are well-studied.

As a corollary of that result, we get the main result for this section:
assuming
the MDS conjecture, we can characterize the best possible TN matrices we can get, and thus the best possible block-TN matrices that we can get via the method “start with a TN matrix and apply
\varphi
.” This is formally stated as the following corollary.

Corollary 25
.

Assume the MDS conjecture. Then there exists an (efficiently computable) totally nonsingular matrix
A\in\left(\mathbb{F}_{q^{j}}\setminus\{0\}\right)^{(s-dt)\times s}
if and only if
s-dt\leq q^{j}
and

j\geq\begin{cases}\log_{q}(s-1)&q^{j}\text{ odd, or }(s-dt)\not\in\{3,q^{j}-1\}\\
\log_{q}(s-2)&q^{j}\text{ even, and }(s-dt)\in\{3,q^{j}-1\}\end{cases}.

Furthermore, the assumption of the MDS conjecture is necessary only for the forward direction.

Before we state Theorem
28
and prove it and Corollary
25
, we briefly recall the MDS conjecture. First, we formally define MDS codes/matrices.

Definition 11
.

Let
\mathcal{C}\subseteq{\mathbb{F}}^{u}
be a linear code of dimension
r
and block length
u
. We say that
\mathcal{C}
is
Maximum Distance Separable
(MDS) if the Hamming distance of
\mathcal{C}
is
u-r+1
; equivalently, any
r
symbols of a codeword
\mathbf{c}\in\mathcal{C}
determine the rest of
\mathbf{c}
.

We say that a matrix
G\in{\mathbb{F}}^{r\times u}
is an
MDS matrix
if it is the generator matrix for an MDS code. Equivalently,
G
is an MDS matrix if and only if any
r\times r
submatrix of
G
has full rank.

The MDS conjecture concerns the minimum possible field size over which MDS matrices can exist. It is known that a slight variant on
Reed-Solomon
codes can yield a construction over reasonably small fields. For completeness, we provide the construction that proves Theorem
26
in Appendix
A
.

Theorem 26

(See, e.g.,
[
Wol69
]
,
[
BB12
]
)
.

Let
\mathbb{F}
be a field of order
q^{j}
. For any
r,u\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
satisfying
r\leq q^{j}
and

u\leq\begin{cases}q^{j}+1&q^{j}\text{ odd, or }r\not\in\{3,q^{j}-1\}\\
q^{j}+2&q^{j}\text{ even, and }r\in\{3,q^{j}-1\}\end{cases}

there exists an explicit MDS matrix
A\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{j}}^{r\times u}
,
r\leq u
.

The
MDS conjecture
says that the above bounds are optimal.

Conjecture 27

(MDS Conjecture
[
Seg55
]
)
.

The bounds of Theorem
26
are tight.

The MDS conjecture has been proven in prime order fields, and it is known to hold in subcases of extension fields
[
BB12
]
.

Now we can state and prove Theorem
28
, which says that an MDS matrix is equivalent to a TN matrix.

Theorem 28
.

Let
r,u\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
with
r\leq u
. Then there exists a totally nonsingular matrix
A\in\left(\mathbb{F}_{q^{j}}\setminus\{0\}\right)^{r\times u}
if and only if there exists a MDS matrix
A^{\prime}\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{j}}^{r\times(r+u)}
.
Furthermore, this biconditional is constructive:

(i)

if
A\in\left(\mathbb{F}_{q^{j}}\setminus\{0\}\right)^{r\times u}
is a totally nonsingular matrix, then
[I^{r\times r}\mid A]
is a
r\times(r+u)
MDS matrix; and

(ii)

if
A^{\prime}\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{j}}^{r\times(r+u)}
is an MDS matrix, then
A^{\prime}=[I^{r\times r}\mid A]
without loss of generality, where
A
is a totally nonsingular matrix.

To prove Theorem
28
, we prove each direction separately in Lemmas
29
and
30
, respectively. We then provide the proof of Corollary
25
.

Lemma 29
.

Let
A\in\left(\mathbb{F}_{q^{j}}\setminus\{0\}\right)^{r\times u}
be a totally non-singular matrix with
r\leq u
. Then there exists a
r\times(r+u)
MDS matrix
A^{\prime}\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{j}}^{r\times(r+u)}
.

Proof.

Let
A^{\prime}=[A|I^{r\times r}]\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{j}}^{r\times(r+u)}
. We claim that
A^{\prime}
is an MDS matrix. Since any
r\times r
submatrix of
A
is nonsingular, and since
I^{r\times r}
is also non-singular, we need only consider
r\times r
submatrices
B
of
A^{\prime}
that contain some
\rho\in\{0,\ldots,r-1\}
of the last
r
columns of
A^{\prime}
. Let
\hat{A}
be any
r\times(r-\rho)
submatrix of
A
, partitioned into an upper
(r-\rho)\times(r-\rho)
square matrix and a lower
\rho\times(r-\rho)
matrix as follows:

\hat{A}=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}\\
\hat{A}_{\text{up}}\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{j}}^{(r-\rho)\times(r-\rho)}\\
\\
\hat{A}_{\text{down}}\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{j}}^{\rho\times(r-\rho)}\\
\\
\end{array}\right]

Then up to a permutation of rows, we may write any
r\times r
submatrix
B
of
A^{\prime}
containing
\rho
of the last
r
columns as

B:=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}\\
\hat{A}_{\text{up}}&0^{(r-\rho)\times\rho}\\
\\
\hat{A}_{\text{down}}&I^{\rho\times\rho}\\
\\
\end{array}\right].

However, such a
B
is non-singular, as
\det(B)=\det(\hat{A}_{\text{up}})\neq 0
. Thus,
A^{\prime}
is MDS.
∎

Next, we prove the other direction of Theorem
28
.

Lemma 30
.

Let
A^{\prime}\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{j}}^{r\times(r+u)}
be an MDS matrix with
r\leq u
. Then there exists a totally non-singular matrix
A\in\left(\mathbb{F}_{q^{j}}\setminus\{0\}\right)^{r\times u}
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, assume that
A^{\prime}=[I|A]
is in reduced row-echelon form, with
A\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{j}}^{r\times u}
. We will show that
A
is totally non-singular. Let
R\subseteq[r],U\subseteq[u]
be so that
|R|=|U|=\rho
, for some
0\leq\rho\leq r
. Thus,
R
and
U
represent row and column indices of some (not necessarily contiguous) square submatrix of
A
; denote this matrix by
V\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{j}}^{\rho\times\rho}
. Thus, we want to show that
V
is full rank.

Denote by
W\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{j}}^{\rho\times(u-\rho)}
the submatrix of
A
with rows indexed by
R
and columns indexed by
[u]\setminus U
.
Let
S^{\prime}
be a submatrix of consisting of
A^{\prime}
consisting of rows indexed by
U
. Thus, up to a permutation of rows and columns, we may write
S^{\prime}
as

S^{\prime}=\left[\begin{array}[]{cccc}I^{\rho\times\rho}&0^{\rho\times(r-\rho)}&V&W\end{array}\right].

Now assume towards a contradiction that there exists some
\mathbf{m}\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{j}}^{\rho}
such that
\mathbf{m}^{T}S^{\prime}=\mathbf{0}
. Then, in particular, we have

\mathbf{m}^{T}S^{\prime\prime}:=\mathbf{m}^{T}\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}0^{\rho\times(r-\rho)}&V\end{array}\right]=\mathbf{0}.

But
S^{\prime\prime}
is a
\rho\times r
submatrix of
A^{\prime}
, which is contained in an
r\times r
submatrix of
A^{\prime}
; if
S^{\prime\prime}
is not full-rank, then there exists a singular
r\times r
submatrix of
A^{\prime}
, contradicting the premise that
A^{\prime}
is an MDS matrix.
∎

Thus, we have established both directions of Theorem
28
. This proves the theorem.

We are now equipped to prove Corollary
25
.

Proof of Corollary
25
.

In the forward direction, note that the total non-singularity of
A
implies
s-dt\leq q^{j}
. By Theorem
28
, there exists a MDS matrix
A^{\prime}\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{j}}^{(s-dt)\times s}
. Considering the pair of bounds
s\leq q^{j}+1,q^{j}+2
of the MDS conjecture yields the desired result.

In the reverse direction, Theorem
26
guarantees the existence of a MDS matrix
A^{\prime}\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{j}}^{(s-dt)\times s}
meeting the bounds of the MDS conjecture. We may assume
A^{\prime}=\left[I^{(s-dt)\times(s-dt)}\mid A\right]
where
A\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{j}}^{(s-dt)\times dt}
; by Theorem
28
,
A
must be a totally non-singular matrix.
∎

4.2.3
Near-optimal construction of block-TN matrices

Next, we move onto constructions of block-TN matrices, which will establish Theorem
19
. We begin with Lemma
31
below, which states that we can construct a block-TN matrix out of a TN matrix.

Lemma 31
.

Let
q
be a prime power and let
e
be any integer; let
{\mathbb{F}}\leq\mathbb{E}
be finite fields so that
|{\mathbb{F}}|=q
and
|\mathbb{E}|=q^{e}
.
Let
r,u
be positive integers.

Let
\mathbf{B}\in\left(\mathbb{E}\right)^{r\times u}
be a totally nonsingular matrix. Let
\varphi:\mathbb{F}\to GL({\mathbb{F}},e)
be as in Example
1
. Let
\mathbf{A}=\varphi(\mathbf{B})\in\text{GL}(\mathbb{F},e)^{r\times u}
be the
r\times u
array of invertible matrices obtained by applying
\varphi
element-wise to
\mathbf{B}
. Then
\mathbf{A}
is block totally nonsingular.

Proof.

We show the contrapositive. Suppose that
\mathbf{A}
is not block-TN. Then there exists some square subarray
\mathbf{A}^{\prime}
of
\mathbf{A}
that is singular; say
\mathbf{A}^{\prime}\in(GL({\mathbb{F}},e))^{a\times a}
. Since
\varphi
is invertible, we may consider
\varphi^{-1}
applied to each element of
\mathbf{A}
, which yields a square submatrix
\mathbf{B}^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{F}}^{a\times a}
of
\mathbf{B}
. We claim that
\mathbf{B}^{\prime}
is singular. Indeed, let
\mathbf{v}=\mathbf{v}^{(1)}\circ\cdots\circ\mathbf{v}^{(a)}\in({\mathbb{F}}^{e})^{a}
be a kernel vector for
\mathbf{A}
. Recall that
\varphi
was formed by picking a basis
\mathcal{B}
for
\mathbb{E}
over
{\mathbb{F}}
. For each
\mathbf{v}^{(i)}\in{\mathbb{F}}^{e}
, let
\gamma^{(i)}\in\mathbb{E}
be the field element represented by
\mathbf{v}^{(i)}
in the basis
\mathcal{B}
.
Then it follows from the definition of
\varphi
that the vector
(\gamma^{(1)},\ldots,\gamma^{(a)})\in\mathbb{E}^{a}
is a kernel vector for
\mathbf{B}^{\prime}
, so
\mathbf{B}^{\prime}
is singular, as claimed. Thus,
\mathbf{B}
is not totally nonsingular.
∎

Finally, we instantiate Lemma
31
with the following Corollary of Theorem
28
:

Corollary 32

(Existence of Block TN Matrices)
.

Let
{\mathbb{F}}
be a finite field of size
q
. Let
s,d,t\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
so that
s-dt>0
. For all integers

j\geq\begin{cases}\log_{q}(s-1)&q^{j}\text{ odd, or }(s-dt)\not\in\{3,q^{j}-1\}\\
\log_{q}(s-2)&q^{j}\text{ even, and }(s-dt)\in\{3,q^{j}-1\}\end{cases}

there is an explicit construction of a block totally nonsingular matrix
A\in(GL({\mathbb{F}},j))^{(s-dt)\times dt}
.

Proof.

By Theorem
26
(with
r\leftarrow s-dt
and
u\leftarrow s
), there is an MDS matrix
A^{\prime}\in({\mathbb{F}}_{q^{j}})^{(s-dt)\times s}
as long as
j
is as in the statement of the Corollary. By Theorem
28
(ii), if we write
A^{\prime}=[I|B]
, then
B
is totally non-singular. By Lemma
31
, if we apply the embedding
\varphi:{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{j}}\to{\mathbb{F}}^{j\times j}
from Example
1
coordinatewise to
B
, we obtain a matrix
A\in(GL({\mathbb{F}},j))^{(s-dt)\times dt}
that is block TN.
∎

Finally, we observe that Corollary
32
, along with Lemma
22
, implies Theorem
19
.

This completes the proofs of both Theorems
19
and
20
, providing nearly matching bounds on good labelweight codes, and finally establishing Corollary
21
, which nearly pins down the allowable amortization parameters
\ell
for download-optimal linear HSS schemes for polynomials.

On the automorphism groups of Lunardon-Polverino scattered linear sets
Wei Tang 1, Yue Zhou 1, † \dagger, Ferdinando Zullo 2

Abstract
. Lunardon and Polverino introduced in 2001 a new family of maximum scattered linear sets in PG ⁡ ( 1 , q n ) \mathrm{PG}(1,q^{n}) to construct linear minimal Rédei blocking sets. This family has been extended first by Lavrauw, Marino, Trombetti and Polverino in 2015 and then by Sheekey in 2016 in two different contexts (semifields and rank metric codes). These linear sets are called Lunardon-Polverino linear sets and this paper aims to determine their automorphism groups, to solve the equivalence issue among Lunardon-Polverino linear sets and to establish the number of inequivalent linear sets of this family. We then elaborate on this number, providing explicit bounds and determining its asymptotics.



1.
Introduction

The determination of the automorphism group of combinatorial objects has been a central topic in combinatorics for many years, especially because it gives information on the
symmetry
of the considered structure. However, very little is known about the automorphism groups of linear sets.

Let
q=p^{r}
, for some prime
p
and a positive integer
r
.
Let
n
be a positive integer and let
V
be a
2
-dimensional vector space over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
(up to coordinatize, we can suppose that
V=\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}^{2}
). A point set
L
of
\Lambda=\mathrm{PG}(V,\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}})=\mathrm{PG}(1,q^{n})
is said to be an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linear set
of
\Lambda
of rank
k
if it is defined by the non-zero vectors of a
k
-dimensional
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-vector subspace
U
of
V
, i.e.

L=L_{U}:=\{\langle{\bf u}\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}}:{\bf u}\in U\setminus\{{\bf 0}\}\}.

For any point
P=\mathrm{PG}(Z,\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}})
of
\Lambda
, the
weight
of
P
in
L_{U}
is defined as
w_{L_{U}}(P)=\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(U\cap Z)
.
For an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linear set
L
of rank
k
the following holds

(1)

|L|\leq\frac{q^{k}-1}{q-1}.

We also recall that two linear sets
L_{U}
and
L_{W}
in the projective line
\Lambda
are said to be
\mathrm{P\Gamma L}
-equivalent
(or simply
equivalent
) if there is an element
\varphi
in
\mathrm{P\Gamma L}(2,q^{n})
such that
L_{U}^{\varphi}=L_{W}
.
In general, it is very challenging to determine whether or not two linear sets are equivalent; see e.g.
[
7
]
.

An important family of linear sets are the
scattered
ones, that is those linear sets satisfying the equality in (
1
) (or, equivalently, the linear sets whose points have weight one); see e.g.
[
20
]
.
Scattered linear sets were originally introduced by Blokhuis and Lavrauw in
[
6
]
in a more general setting. Since the seminal paper by Blokhuis and Lavrauw, linear sets attracted a lot of attention especially because of their connections with blocking sets, semifields, rank metric codes, etc (see e.g.
[
13
,
20
,
21
]
).
In
[
6
]
, it has also been proved that a scattered linear set in
\Lambda
can have rank at most
n
and a scattered linear set of rank
n
is said to be
maximum scattered
.
The first example of maximum scattered linear set is the linear set
of pseudoregulus type
, that is

\{\langle(x,x^{q^{s}})\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}}\colon x\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}^{*}\},

with
\gcd(s,n)=1
.
In particular,

\left\{\langle(x,x^{q})\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}}\colon x\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}^{*}\right\}=\left\{\langle(x,x^{q^{s}})\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}}\colon x\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}^{*}\right\},

for any
s
such that
\gcd(s,n)=1
.
This family has been studied in details, indeed its geometric characterization has been provided by Csajbók and Zanella in
[
8
]
.

The next example of maximum scattered linear set in
\Lambda
which has been given by Lunardon and Polverino in
[
16
]
and generalized in
[
12
,
21
]
is

L=\{\langle(x,x^{q^{s}}+\delta x^{q^{n-s}})\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}}\colon x\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}^{*}\},

with
n\geq 4
,
\gcd(s,n)=1
,
q\neq 2
, and nonzero
\delta
such that
N_{q^{n}/q}(\delta)=\delta^{\frac{q^{n}-1}{q-1}}\neq 1
. This example is known as
Lunardon-Polverino
linear set. If we set
\delta=0
, then the Lunardon-Polverino linear set reduces to be of pseudoregulus type.
In
[
22
, Theorem 3.4]
, Zanella proved that the condition
N_{q^{n}/q}(\delta)\neq 1
is necessary for
L
to be scattered (see also
[
2
]
).
Also, a more geometric description for such linear sets has been given in
[
23
]
and an upper bound on the number of inequivalent Lunardon-Polverino linear sets has been provided in
[
15
, Proposition 2.3]
, but the exact number is unknown.

Let
L_{U}
be an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linear set of rank
n
in
\Lambda
. It is well-known that
{\rm P\Gamma L}(2,q^{n})
is
3
-transitive on
\Lambda
, and so we may assume that
L_{U}
does not contain the point
\langle(0,1)\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}}
, so that
L_{U}
can be written as

L_{f}=\{\langle(x,f(x))\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}}\colon x\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}^{*}\},

where
f
is a
q
-polynomial
over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
, that is
f=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}a_{i}X^{q^{i}}\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}[X]
.
Sheekey in
[
21
]
called
f

scattered
if
L_{f}
turns out to be a scattered
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linear set.
When choosing
f=X^{q^{s}}
then
L_{f}
turns out to be the linear set of pseudoregulus type, whereas if
f=X^{q^{s}}+\delta X^{q^{n-s}}
then
L_{f}
is the Lunardon-Polverino linear set.
These two families of polynomials play an important role in the classifications of scattered polynomials which are
exceptional
, that is, roughly speaking, polynomials which are scattered for infinitely many
n
. Indeed, the known classification results suggest that these two families are the only examples of exceptional scattered polynomials; see
[
4
,
3
,
5
,
9
]
.

In this paper we study Lunardon-Polverino linear sets.
We first completely solve the equivalence issue among two Lunardon-Polverino linear sets and we also determine the automorphism group of a Lunardon-Polverino linear set. This has been done by exploiting the problem of determining the relations between the coefficients of two
q
-polynomials
f
and
g
for which the value sets of
f(x)/x
and
g(x)/x
coincide.
As a byproduct and making use of careful computations, we completely determine
\Lambda(n,q)
the number of inequivalent Lunardon-Polverino linear sets.
Finally we compute explicit upper and lower bounds on
\Lambda(n,q)
and we determine its asymptotic behavior as

r\to+\infty
, by making use of a result by Gronwall (1913) involving the Euler-Mascheroni constant.

2.
Auxiliary results

We need the following well known result on the greatest common divisors. A proof can be found in
[
19
]
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
v(b):=\max\{i:2^{i}\mid b\}
. For a prime
p
,

(2)

\gcd(p^{i}+1,p^{j}-1)=\begin{cases}p^{\gcd(i,j)}+1,&v(i)<v(j);\\
\frac{(-1)^{p+1}+3}{2},&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Let
s
be a positive integer such that
\gcd(s,n)=1
and let
\sigma\colon x\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}\mapsto x^{q^{s}}\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
.
A
\sigma
-
polynomial
(or a
linearized polynomial
) over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
is a polynomial of the following form

f=\sum_{i=0}^{\ell}a_{i}X^{\sigma^{i}},

where
a_{i}\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
and
\ell
is a positive integer.
We will also call
f
a
q^{s}
-
polynomial
.
If
a_{\ell}\neq 0
we say that
\ell
is the
\sigma
-
degree
of
f
.
We will denote by
\mathcal{L}_{n,\sigma}
the set of all
\sigma
-polynomials over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
with
\sigma
-degree less than
n
(when
s=1
, we will denote it by
\mathcal{L}_{n,q}
). The set
\mathcal{L}_{n,\sigma}
equipped with the classical sum of polynomials, the composition modulo
X^{\sigma^{n}}-X
and the scalar multiplication by an element in
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, is an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-algebra isomorphic to the algebra of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linear endomorphism of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
.
This isomorphism allows us to identify a
\sigma
-polynomial with the
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linear map it defines.
We also want to underline that different choices of
\sigma
give a different representation of an element, but leave unchanged the algebra
\mathcal{L}_{n,\sigma}
. Hence,
\mathcal{L}_{n,\sigma}
is the same for any choice of
\sigma
, and one can just consider
\mathcal{L}_{n,q}
.
For more details on linearized polynomials we refer to
[
14
, Chapter 3, Section 4]
.

Consider the non-degenerate symmetric bilinear form of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
defined by

\langle x,y\rangle=\mathrm{Tr}_{q^{n}/q}(xy),

for every
x,y\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
where
\mathrm{Tr}_{q^{n}/q}(x)=x+\ldots+x^{q^{n-1}}
.
The
adjoint

\hat{f}
of the
\sigma
-polynomial
\displaystyle f=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}a_{i}X^{\sigma^{i}}\in\mathcal{L}_{n,\sigma}
with respect to the bilinear form
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
, i.e. the unique function over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
satisfying

\mathrm{Tr}_{q^{n}/q}(yf(z))=\mathrm{Tr}_{q^{n}/q}(z\hat{f}(y))

for every
y,z\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
, is given by

(3)

\hat{f}=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}a_{i}^{\sigma^{n-i}}X^{\sigma^{n-i}}.

In
[
1
, Lemma 2.6]
it has been proved that a
\sigma
-polynomial
f
and its adjoint always define the same linear set, and following the proof of
[
1
, Lemma 2.6]
one also obtain that the weight distribution of
L_{f}
coincides with the weight distribution of
L_{\hat{f}}
; see also
[
18
, Theorem 1]
.
More precisely, the following holds (which we prove for sake of completeness in a different way).

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
f
be a
\sigma
-polynomial in
\mathcal{L}_{n,\sigma}
. Then

(4)

\#\left\{x\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}^{*}:\frac{f(x)}{x}=b\right\}=\#\left\{y\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}^{*}:\frac{\hat{f}(y)}{y}=b\right\}.

In particular,
L_{f}=L_{\hat{f}}
and
w_{L_{f}}(P)=w_{L_{\hat{f}}}(P)
for every point
P\in\mathrm{PG}(1,q^{n})
.

Proof.

Let
\chi(x)=\zeta_{p}^{\mathrm{Tr}_{q^{n}/p}(x)}
, where
p
is the characteristic of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
and
\zeta_{p}
is a
p
-th root of unity in
\mathbb{C}
. Define
\chi_{a}(x)=\chi(ax)
for
a\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
. Then the character group of the additive group of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
is
\{\chi_{a}:a\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}\}
. For characters of groups, we refer to
[
11
, Chapter 8]
.

By the definition of adjoints, for any
b\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
,

(5)

\sum_{y\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}}\chi\left((f(x)-bx)y\right)=\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}}\sum_{y\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}}\chi\left((\hat{f}(y)-by)x\right).

By the orthogonality of characters, the left-hand-side of (
5
) equals

\#\{x\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}:f(x)=bx\}\cdot q^{n}=\left(\#\left\{x\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}^{*}:\frac{f(x)}{x}=b\right\}+1\right)\cdot q^{n}.

The same result holds for the right-hand-side of (
5
). Therefore (
4
) is proved and the last part of the assertion is a direct consequence.
∎

Given two arbitrary linearized polynomials
f
and
g
, it is usually quite difficult to determine whether
L_{f}=L_{g}
. However, we can obtain some conditions involving the coefficients of both
f
and
g
. Indeed, in
[
7
, Lemma 3.4]
the following condition arises

\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}}\left(\frac{f(x)}{x}\right)^{e}=\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}}\left(\frac{g(x)}{x}\right)^{e},

for any positive integer
e
.
For some special values of
e
, Csajbók, Marino and Polverino
[
7
]
have provided some more explicit conditions.

Lemma 2.3
.

[
7
, Lemma 3.6]

Let
f=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\alpha_{i}X^{q^{i}}
and
g=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\beta_{i}X^{q^{i}}
be two
q
-polynomials over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
such that
L_{f}=L_{g}
. Then

(6)

\alpha_{0}=\beta_{0}

and

(7)

\alpha_{k}\alpha_{n-k}^{q^{k}}=\beta_{k}\beta_{n-k}^{q^{k}}

for
k\in\{1,2,\cdots,n-1\}
, and

\alpha_{1}\alpha_{k-1}^{q}\alpha_{n-k}^{q^{k}}+\alpha_{k}\alpha_{n-1}^{q}\alpha_{n-k+1}^{q^{k}}=\beta_{1}\beta_{k-1}^{q}\beta_{n-k}^{q^{k}}+\beta_{k}\beta_{n-1}^{q}\beta_{n-k+1}^{q^{k}},

for
k\in\{2,3,\cdots,n-1\}
.

For some linearized polynomials with two terms, we can obtain a further necessary condition.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
s
and
n
be two relatively prime positive integers. Let
\sigma\colon x\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}\mapsto x^{q^{s}}\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
. For any nonzero elements
\theta,\delta,d\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
, let
f=X^{\sigma}+\theta X^{\sigma^{n-1}}
and
g=d(X^{\sigma}+\delta X^{\sigma^{n-1}})
be two
q
-polynomials over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
such that
L_{f}=L_{g}
. Then

(8)

1+N_{q^{n}/q}(\theta)=N_{q^{n}/q}(d)(1+N_{q^{n}/q}(\delta)),

when
n
is odd, and

(9)

1+N_{q^{n}/q}(\theta)+N_{q^{n}/q^{2}}(\theta)+N_{q^{n}/q^{2}}(\theta)^{\sigma}=N_{q^{n}/q}(d)(1+N_{q^{n}/q}(\delta)+N_{q^{n}/q^{2}}(\delta)+N_{q^{n}/q^{2}}(\delta)^{\sigma}),

when
n
is even.

Proof.

The
(1+q+q^{2}+\cdots+q^{n-1})
-th power of
f(X)/X
modulo
X^{\sigma^{n}}-X
equals,

(10)

(X^{\sigma-1}+\theta X^{\sigma^{n-1}-1})(X^{\sigma^{2}-\sigma}+\theta^{\sigma}X^{\sigma^{n}-\sigma})\cdots(X^{\sigma^{n}-\sigma^{n-1}}+\theta^{\sigma^{n-1}}X^{\sigma^{n-2}-\sigma^{n-1}}).

As
\gcd(s,n)=1
, the coefficient of
X^{q^{n}-1}
in (
10
) modulo
X^{q^{n}}-X
is the same as the coefficient of
X^{\sigma^{n}-1}
modulo
X^{\sigma^{n}}-X
.

To get
X^{\sigma^{n}-1}
in (
10
), we only have to find all the possible ways to choose exactly one term
Z_{i}
from each set
\{X^{\sigma^{i+1}-\sigma^{i}},X^{\sigma^{i-1}-\sigma^{i}}\}
such that
\prod_{i=0}^{n-1}Z_{i}\equiv X^{\sigma^{n}-1}\pmod{X^{\sigma^{n}}-X}
. To determine it, we may consider the two terms
X^{\sigma^{i+1}-\sigma^{i}}
and
X^{\sigma^{i-1}-\sigma^{i}}
in each
(X^{\sigma^{i+1}-\sigma^{i}}+\theta^{\sigma}X^{\sigma^{i-1}-\sigma^{i}})
as two arrays of length
n
defined recursively:

\displaystyle\mathbf{a}_{0}

\displaystyle=(-1,1,0,\cdots,0)\in\mathbb{Z}^{n},

\displaystyle\mathbf{b}_{0}

\displaystyle=(-1,0,\cdots,0,1)\in\mathbb{Z}^{n},

and for
i\in\{1,2,\cdots,n-1\}
,
\mathbf{a}_{i}
(resp.
\mathbf{b}_{i}
) is obtained from
\mathbf{a}_{i-1}
(resp.
\mathbf{b}_{i-1}
) by applying a shift on the right on its entries, that is

\displaystyle\mathbf{a}_{i}

\displaystyle=(\overbrace{0,\cdots,0}^{i},-1,1,0,\cdots,0)\in\mathbb{Z}^{n},

\displaystyle\mathbf{b}_{i}

\displaystyle=(\overbrace{0,\cdots,0}^{i-1},1,-1,0,\cdots,0)\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}.

In this way,
\mathbf{a}_{i}
represents the exponents of
\sigma
in the term
X^{\sigma^{i+1}-\sigma^{i}}
and, similarly,
\mathbf{b}_{i}
represents the exponents of
\sigma
in the term
X^{\sigma^{i-1}-\sigma^{i}}
, for any
i\in\{0,\ldots,n-1\}
.
Since our aim is to find the coefficient of
X^{\sigma^{n}-1}
, we have to find all the possible sum in which every addend is exactly one element from
\mathbf{a}_{i}
and
\mathbf{b}_{i}
for each
i\in\{0,\cdots,n-1\}
and such that the sum equals to the zero vector
\mathbf{0}
, which represents
X^{\sigma^{n}-1}
.
For any
n
, it is easy to see that

(11)

\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\mathbf{a}_{i}=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\mathbf{b}_{i}=\mathbf{0}.

When
n
is even, we also have

(12)

\sum_{2\mid i}\mathbf{a}_{i}+\sum_{2\nmid j}\mathbf{b}_{j}=\sum_{2\nmid i}\mathbf{a}_{i}+\sum_{2\mid j}\mathbf{b}_{j}=\mathbf{0}.

Claim
: All the possible ways to get
\mathbf{0}
are given in (
11
) and (
12
).

To prove the claim, we first have to notice that the two nonzero elements in
\mathbf{a}_{i}
or
\mathbf{b}_{i}
are adjacent to each other. Consequently, if
\mathbf{a}_{i}
appears in the sum, to make the
(i+2)
-th position in the array
0
, we have to add exactly one element from
\{\mathbf{a}_{i+1},\mathbf{b}_{i+1}\}
. Moreover, if

\mathbf{a}_{i}+\mathbf{a}_{i+1}=(\overbrace{0,\cdots,0}^{i},-1,0,1,0,\cdots,)

is contained in the sum, then it is easy to see that the
(i-1)
-th term of the sum has to be
\mathbf{a}_{i-1}
. The same result also holds for
\mathbf{b}_{i}+\mathbf{b}_{i+1}
.
Whereas, if
\mathbf{a}_{i}+\mathbf{b}_{i+1}
is contained in the sum, since
\mathbf{a}_{i}+\mathbf{b}_{i+1}=\mathbf{0}
, then we necessarily obtain the case (
12
).
As a consequence, we complete the proof of the claim.

By
Claim
, the coefficient of
X^{q^{n}-1}
in (
10
) is

c_{q^{n}-1}=\begin{cases}1+N_{q^{n}/q}(\theta),&\text{ if }2\nmid n;\\
1+N_{q^{n}/q}(\theta)+N_{q^{n}/q^{2}}(\theta)+N_{q^{n}/q^{2}}(\theta)^{\sigma},&\text{ if }2\mid n.\end{cases}

By the well known equation

\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}}x^{e}=\begin{cases}-1,&q^{n}-1\mid e;\\
0,&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

we have

\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}}(x^{q^{s}-1}+\theta x^{q^{n-s}-1})^{1+q+\cdots+q^{n-1}}=c_{q^{n}-1}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}}x^{q^{n}-1}=-c_{q^{n}-1}.

As
L_{g}=L_{f}
, by comparing the coefficients of
X^{q^{n}-1}
of
(f(X)/X)^{1+q+\cdots+q^{n-1}}
and
(g(X)/X)^{1+q+\cdots+q^{n-1}}
modulo
X^{q^{n}}-X
, we get (
8
) and (
9
).
∎

3.
Equivalence of the Lunardon-Polverino linear sets and their automorphism group

In this section, we completely determine the equivalence between different members of the Lunardon-Polverino maximum scattered linear sets in a projective line as well as their automorphism groups.
The main results of this section are the following.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
n,s,t\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
\gcd(s,n)=\gcd(t,n)=1
,
n\geq 3
and
1\leq s,t<n/2
. For any nonzero elements
\theta,\delta\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
satisfying
N_{q^{n}/q}(\theta),N_{q^{n}/q}(\delta)\neq 1
, let
f=X^{q^{s}}+\theta X^{q^{s(n-1)}}
and
g=X^{q^{t}}+\delta X^{q^{t(n-1)}}
. The linear sets
L_{f}
and
L_{g}
are equivalent if and only if
s=t
and one of the following collections of conditions is satisfied:

(a)

2\nmid n
,
N_{q^{n}/q}(\theta)\in\{N_{q^{n}/q}(\delta^{\tau}),N_{q^{n}/q}(1/\delta^{\tau})\}
for some
\tau\in\mathrm{Aut}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}})

(b)

2\mid n
,
N_{q^{n}/q^{2}}(\theta)\in\{N_{q^{n}/q^{2}}(\delta^{\tau}),N_{q^{n}/q^{2}}(1/\delta^{\tau})\}
for some
\tau\in\mathrm{Aut}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}})
.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
n,s\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
\gcd(s,n)=1
,
n\geq 3
and
1\leq s<n/2
. For any nonzero element
\theta\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
satisfying
N_{q^{n}/q}(\theta)\neq 1
, let
f=X^{q^{s}}+\theta X^{q^{s(n-1)}}
. Then the
\mathrm{P\Gamma L}
-automorphism group of
L_{f}
is

\mathrm{Aut}(L_{f})=\begin{cases}\mathcal{D},&n>4;\\
\mathcal{D}\cup\mathcal{C},&n=4,\end{cases}

where

(13)

\mathcal{D}:=\left\{\left(\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&d\end{pmatrix},\tau\right)\in\mathrm{P\Gamma L}(2,q^{n}):d^{\sigma+1}=\left(\frac{\theta}{\theta^{\tau}}\right)^{\sigma}\right\}

and

(14)

\mathcal{C}:=\left\{\left(\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
c&0\end{pmatrix},\tau\right)\in\mathrm{P\Gamma L}(2,q^{4}):\left(\frac{c}{\theta^{\tau q}-\theta^{-\tau q^{3}}}\right)^{q+1}=\left(-\theta^{\tau q^{3}+1}\right)^{q}\right\}.

In particular, its size is

(15)

\#\mathrm{Aut}(L_{f})=\begin{cases}N_{\tau}(\theta),&2\nmid n,2\mid q;\\
2N_{\tau}(\theta),&2\nmid n,2\nmid q;\\
(q+1)N_{\tau}(\theta),&2\mid n,n>4;\\
2(q+1)N_{\tau}(\theta),&n=4,\end{cases}

where

N_{\tau}(\theta):=\#\left\{\tau\in\mathrm{Aut}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}):N_{q^{n}/q}(\theta)=N_{q^{n}/q}(\theta^{\tau})\text{ or }1/N_{q^{n}/q}(\theta^{\tau})\right\}

for odd
n
and

N_{\tau}(\theta):=\#\left\{\tau\in\mathrm{Aut}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}):N_{q^{n}/q^{2}}(\theta)=\{N_{q^{n}/q^{2}}(\theta^{\tau})\text{ or }1/N_{q^{n}/q^{2}}(\theta^{\tau})\right\}

for even
n
.

Remark 3.3
.

Let
n,s\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
\gcd(s,n)=1
,
s>n/2
. It is easy to see that the linear set
L_{f}
defined by
f=X^{q^{s}}+\theta X^{q^{s(n-1)}}
is equivalent to
L_{g}
where

, with

. So, the assumptions in Theorem
3.1
on

and

are not restrictive.

In order to get Theorems
3.1
and
3.2
, we need to prove a series of lemmas.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
s
and
n
be two relatively prime positive integers, and
1\leq s<n/2
. Let
\sigma\colon x\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}\mapsto x^{q^{s}}\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
. For any nonzero elements
\theta,\delta\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
satisfying
N_{q^{n}/q}(\theta),N_{q^{n}/q}(\delta)\neq 1
, let
f=X^{\sigma}+\theta X^{\sigma^{n-1}}
and
g=X^{\sigma}+\delta X^{\sigma^{n-1}}
. There exists
d\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
such that the linear sets
L_{f}=L_{dg}
if there exists
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
such that

(16)

\alpha^{\sigma^{2}-1}=\left(\frac{\theta}{\delta}\right)^{\sigma}.

Proof.

Replace
X
by
\alpha X
in
f(X)/X
. Let
d=\alpha^{\sigma-1}
. Then we obtain

\frac{f(\alpha X)}{\alpha X}=\alpha^{\sigma-1}X^{\sigma-1}+\theta\alpha^{\sigma^{n-1}-1}X^{\sigma^{n-1}-1}=d\frac{g(X)}{X},

since
(d\delta)^{\sigma}=\alpha^{\sigma^{2}-\sigma}\delta^{\sigma}=\theta^{\sigma}\alpha^{1-\sigma}
, that is
d\delta=\theta\alpha^{\sigma^{n-1}-1}
. Therefore,
L_{f}=L_{dg}
.
∎

Remark 3.5
.

The sufficient condition given in Lemma
3.4
is actually the necessary and sufficient condition for the existences of permutation
q
-polynomials
L_{1}
and
L_{2}
such that
\mathcal{C}_{g}=\{L_{1}\circ h\circ L_{2}(X):h\in\mathcal{C}_{f}\}
, where

\mathcal{C}_{f}:=\{aX+bf(X):a,b\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}\}.

See
[
17
, Definition 4.1 and Theorem 4.4]
for more details. By Hilbert’s theorem 90, the condition given in (
16
) is equivalent to

(17)

\begin{cases}N_{q^{n}/q}(\theta)=N_{q^{n}/q}(\delta),&2\nmid n;\\
N_{q^{n}/q^{2}}(\theta)=N_{q^{n}/q^{2}}(\delta),&2\mid n.\end{cases}

On the other hand, we can get some necessary condition for
L_{f}=L_{dg}
for some
d\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
which is slightly different from (
17
).

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
s
and
n
be two relatively prime positive integers, and
1\leq s<n/2
. Let
\sigma\colon x\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}\mapsto x^{q^{s}}\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
. For any nonzero elements
\theta,\delta\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
satisfying
N_{q^{n}/q}(\theta),N_{q^{n}/q}(\delta)\neq 1
, let
f=X^{\sigma}+\theta X^{\sigma^{n-1}}
and
g=X^{\sigma}+\delta X^{\sigma^{n-1}}
. There exists
d\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
such that the linear sets
L_{f}=L_{dg}
if and only if

(18)

N_{q^{n}/q}(\theta)\in\{N_{q^{n}/q}(\delta),N_{q^{n}/q}(1/\delta)\},

when

is odd, and

(19)

is even.

Proof.

First we show that (
18
) and (

) are sufficient. As we have proved in Remark
3.5
that (

) is equivalent to (

N_{q^{n}/q}(\theta)=N_{q^{n}/q}(\delta)
for

and
N_{q^{n}/q^{2}}(\theta)=N_{q^{n}/q^{2}}(\delta)
for

, then there exists
d\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
such that
L_{f}=L_{dg}
.

By (
3
), the adjoint of
f
is
\hat{f}=\theta^{\sigma}X^{\sigma}+X^{\sigma^{n-1}}
. By Lemma
2.2
,
L_{f}=L_{\hat{f}}
and again we may apply Remark
3.5
and Lemma
3.4
, obtaining the existence of
d^{\prime}\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
such that

\left\{x^{\sigma-1}+\frac{1}{\theta^{\sigma}}x^{\sigma^{n-1}-1}:x\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}^{*}\right\}=\left\{d^{\prime}\left(x^{\sigma-1}+{\delta}x^{\sigma^{n-1}-1}\right):x\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}^{*}\right\},

provided that
N_{q^{n}/q}(\theta)=N_{q^{n}/q}(1/\delta)
for
n
odd and
N_{q^{n}/q^{2}}(\theta)=N_{q^{n}/q^{2}}(1/\delta)^{\sigma}
for
n
even. Therefore, we finish the proof of the sufficiency.

Next we prove the necessity of (
18
) and (
19
).
By (
7
) for
k=s
, we get

\theta^{\sigma}=d^{\sigma+1}\delta^{\sigma},

i.e.

(20)

d^{\sigma+1}=\left(\frac{\theta}{\delta}\right)^{\sigma}.

When

, (

) implies

N_{q^{n}/q}(d)^{2}=N_{q^{n}/q}\left(\frac{\theta}{\delta}\right).

Plugging it into (

), we have

1+N_{q^{n}/q}(\delta)N_{q^{n}/q}(d)^{2}=N_{q^{n}/q}(d)\left(1+N_{q^{n}/q}(\delta)\right),

which is

(21)

N_{q^{n}/q}(d)^{2}-\left(1+\frac{1}{N_{q^{n}/q}(\delta)}\right)N_{q^{n}/q}(d)+\frac{1}{N_{q^{n}/q}(\delta)}=0,

that is
N_{q^{n}/q}(d)
is a root of

X^{2}-\left(1+\frac{1}{N_{q^{n}/q}(\delta)}\right)X+\frac{1}{N_{q^{n}/q}(\delta)}.

It follows that
N_{q^{n}/q}(d)=1
or
N_{q^{n}/q}(d)=1/N_{q^{n}/q}(\delta)
which implies (
18
).

When

, (

) implies

(22)

N_{q^{n}/q}(d)=N_{q^{n}/q}(d)^{\sigma}=N_{q^{n}/q^{2}}\left(\frac{\theta}{\delta}\right).

Hence
N_{q^{n}/q^{2}}(\theta)=N_{q^{n}/q}(d)N_{q^{n}/q^{2}}(\delta)
and
N_{q^{n}/q}(\theta)=N_{q^{n}/q}(d)^{2}N_{q^{n}/q}(\delta)
. Plugging it into (

\displaystyle 1+N_{q^{n}/q}(d)^{2}N_{q^{n}/q}(\delta)+N_{q^{n}/q}(d)N_{q^{n}/q^{2}}(\delta)+N_{q^{n}/q}(d)N_{q^{n}/q^{2}}(\delta)^{\sigma}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle N_{q^{n}/q}(d)(1+N_{q^{n}/q}(\delta)+N_{q^{n}/q^{2}}(\delta)+N_{q^{n}/q^{2}}(\delta)^{\sigma}),

which is (

) again. It follows that
N_{q^{n}/q}(d)=1
or
N_{q^{n}/q}(d)=1/N_{q^{n}/q}(\delta)
. Together with (

), we get
N_{q^{n}/q^{2}}(\theta)=N_{q^{n}/q^{2}}(\delta)
or
N_{q^{n}/q^{2}}(\theta)=N_{q^{n}/q^{2}}(1/\delta)^{\sigma}
.
∎

Remark 3.7

In the proof of Lemma

Lemma 3.8

Let

, and

Suppose that the inverse map of

\left\{\frac{cx}{f(x)}:x\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}^{*}\right\}=\left\{\frac{cf^{-1}(x)}{x}:x\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}^{*}\right\},

For

Remark 3.10
.

Comparing the conditions in Lemma

4.
The number of inequivalent Lunardon-Polverino scattered linear sets

In this section, we determine the total number of inequivalent scattered polynomials over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
contained in the Lunardon-Polverino construction for given
q
. We always assume that
q=p^{r}
where
p
is a prime and
r
has the following prime factorization

r=\prod_{i=1}^{\ell}r_{i}^{s_{i}}

where
r_{1}<r_{2}<\cdots<r_{\ell}
and
s_{i}\geq 1
for
i=1,\cdots,\ell
.

Define

F(r)=\left\{x\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{r}}:x\notin\mathbb{F}_{p^{r^{\prime}}}\text{ with }r^{\prime}<r\text{ and }r^{\prime}|r\right\},

i.e.,
F(r)
consists of the elements in
\mathbb{F}_{q}
which do not belong to any proper subfield of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Then by the inclusion-exclusion principle, we get

|F(r)|=p^{r}-\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}p^{\frac{r}{r_{i}}}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j=1\\
i<j\end{subarray}}^{\ell}p^{\frac{r}{r_{i}r_{j}}}+\cdots+(-1)^{\ell}p^{\frac{r}{r_{1}r_{2}\cdots r_{\ell}}}.

Lemma 4.1
.

For
0\leq k<r
, if there exists
x\in F(r)
such that
x^{p^{k}+1}=1
, then
r=1
or
r
is even, and

(51)

k=\begin{cases}0,&r=1;\\
\frac{r}{2},&2\mid r.\end{cases}

Moreover, let
K(r)=\left\{x\in F(r):x^{p^{k}+1}=1,\text{ for some $k$ with }0\leq k<r\right\}
. Then

|K(r)|=\begin{cases}\frac{(-1)^{p+1}+3}{2},&r=1;\\
p^{\frac{r}{2}}-\frac{(-1)^{p+1}+1}{2},&r=2^{s_{1}};\\
p^{\frac{r}{2}}-\sum_{i=2}^{\ell}p^{\frac{r}{2r_{i}}}+\sum_{2\leq i<j\leq\ell}p^{\frac{r}{2r_{i}r_{j}}}+\cdots+(-1)^{\ell-1}p^{\frac{r}{2r_{2}\cdots r_{\ell}}},&2\mid r\text{ and }\ell>1;\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Proof.

As
x^{p^{k}+1}=1
,
x^{p^{2k}-1}=1
. Note that
x\in F(r)
, we have
\gcd(2k,r)=r
. If
2\mid r
, then
k
must be
\frac{r}{2}
because
0\leq k<r
. If
2\nmid r
, then we must have
k=0
and
r=1
.

Next we determine the size of
K(r)
. If
r=1
, then
k=0
and
x^{p^{k}+1}=x^{2}=1
. Thus
|K(r)|=\frac{(-1)^{p+1}+3}{2}
. If
r
is odd and
r>1
, then by the first part of the lemma, there is no
x\in F(r)
such that
x^{p^{k}+1}=1
. Hence
K(r)
is empty.

Note that
|\{x\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{m}}:x^{p^{k}+1}=1\}|=\gcd(p^{k}+1,p^{m}-1)
which can be determined by (
2
). Suppose that
r=2^{s_{1}}\prod_{i=2}^{\ell}r_{i}^{s_{i}}
with
s_{1}\geq 1
which means
r
is even. By (
51
), to determine the size of
K(r)
, we only have to consider the number of solutions to
x^{p^{k}+1}=1
for
k=\frac{r}{2}
. If
\ell=1
(that is
r
is a power of
2
), then

\displaystyle|K(r)|=

\displaystyle\left|\left\{x\in F(r):x^{p^{r/2}+1}=1\right\}\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\gcd(p^{\frac{r}{2}}+1,p^{r}-1)-\gcd(p^{\frac{r}{2}}+1,p^{\frac{r}{2}}-1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle p^{\frac{r}{2}}-\frac{(-1)^{p+1}+1}{2}.

For
\ell>1
, by the inclusion-exclusion principle,

\displaystyle|K(r)|=

\displaystyle\left|\left\{x\in F(r):x^{p^{r/2}+1}=1\right\}\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\gcd(p^{\frac{r}{2}}+1,p^{r}-1)-\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\gcd(p^{\frac{r}{2}}+1,p^{\frac{r}{r_{i}}}-1)

\displaystyle+\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq\ell}\gcd(p^{\frac{r}{2}}+1,p^{\frac{r}{r_{i}r_{j}}}-1)+\cdots+(-1)^{\ell}\gcd(p^{\frac{r}{2}}+1,p^{\frac{r}{r_{1}\cdots r_{\ell}}}-1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle p^{\frac{r}{2}}+1-\left(\frac{(-1)^{p+1}+3}{2}+\sum_{i=2}^{\ell}\left(p^{\frac{r}{2r_{i}}}+1\right)\right)+\left(\frac{(-1)^{p+1}+3}{2}(\ell-1)+\sum_{2\leq i<j\leq\ell}\left(p^{\frac{r}{2r_{i}r_{j}}}+1\right)\right)+\cdots

\displaystyle+(-1)^{m}\left(\frac{(-1)^{p+1}+3}{2}\binom{\ell-1}{m-1}+\sum_{2\leq i_{1}<i_{2}<\cdots<i_{m}\leq\ell}\left(p^{\frac{r}{2r_{i_{1}}r_{i_{2}}\cdots r_{i_{m}}}}+1\right)\right)+\cdots

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle p^{\frac{r}{2}}-\sum_{i=2}^{\ell}p^{\frac{r}{2r_{i}}}+\sum_{2\leq i<j\leq\ell}p^{\frac{r}{2r_{i}r_{j}}}+\cdots+(-1)^{\ell-1}p^{\frac{r}{2r_{2}\cdots r_{\ell}}}.\qed

Denote by
\varphi
the Euler totient function.

Theorem 4.2
.

For any integer
n\geq 3
and any prime power
q\neq 2
with
q=p^{r}
, let
\Lambda(n,q)
denote the total number of the
\mathrm{P\Gamma L}
-inequivalent Lunardon-Polverino scattered linear sets over

\Lambda(n,q)=\left(\sum_{r^{\prime}\mid r,r^{\prime}>1}\frac{|F(r^{\prime})|+|K(r^{\prime})|}{2r^{\prime}}+\epsilon\right)\frac{\varphi(n)}{2}

if

is odd, and

\Lambda(n,q)=\left(\sum_{r^{\prime}\mid r,r^{\prime}>1}\frac{|F(r^{\prime})|+|K(r^{\prime})|}{2r^{\prime}}+\sum_{r^{\prime}\mid 2r,r^{\prime}\nmid r}\frac{|F(r^{\prime})|-|K(r^{\prime})|}{2r^{\prime}}+\epsilon\right)\frac{\varphi(n)}{2}

if
n
is even, where

\epsilon=\begin{cases}\frac{p-1}{2},&p\neq 2,2\nmid n;\\
\frac{p-3}{2},&p\neq 2,2\mid n;\\
0,&p=2.\end{cases}

Proof.

Recall that a Lunardon-Polverino scattered polynomial is defined as
f=X^{q^{s}}+\theta X^{q^{n-s}}
.

By Theorem
3.1
, for different value of
s
, the scattered linear sets are inequivalent. Thus we only have to determine the number of polynomials, for which the associated scattered linear sets are inequivalent, in
\left\{X^{q^{s}}+\theta X^{q^{n-s}}:N_{q^{n}/q}(\theta)\neq 0,1\right\}
for given
1\leq s<n/2
.

Let

N(\theta):=\begin{cases}N_{q^{n}/q}(\theta),&2\nmid n;\\
N_{q^{n}/q^{2}}(\theta),&2\mid n.\end{cases}

By Theorem
3.1
,
X^{q^{s}}+\theta X^{q^{n-s}}
and
X^{q^{s}}+\delta X^{q^{n-s}}
are equivalent if and only if

N(\theta)=N(\delta)^{p^{k}}\text{ or }1/N(\delta)^{p^{k}}

for some
k\in\{0,\cdots,2r-1\}
when
n
is even, and for some
k\in\{0,\cdots,r-1\}
when
n
is odd.

Therefore, we have only to count the number of the orbits of
\mathbb{F}^{*}_{q^{w}}\setminus\{x\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{w}}:N_{q^{w}/q}(x)=1\}
under the action of the group
G
generated by
\mathrm{Aut}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{w}})
and the map
x\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{w}}^{*}\mapsto\frac{1}{x}\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{w}}^{*}
, where
w=1
for odd
n
and
w=2
for even
n
. Note that

G=\{x\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{w}}^{*}\mapsto x^{\pm p^{k}}\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{w}}^{*}:k\in\{0,\cdots,wr-1\}\}.

Depending on the parity of
n
, we separate the proof into two cases.

Case (I)
.
2\nmid n
.
Note that
\mathbb{F}_{q}\setminus\{0,1\}=\left(\bigcup_{r^{\prime}\mid r,r^{\prime}\neq 1}F(r^{\prime})\right)\cup\left(F(1)\setminus\{0,1\}\right)
. When
r^{\prime}
is odd, by Lemma
4.1
,
|K(r^{\prime})|=0
which means there is no
x\in F(r^{\prime})
such that

for any
k
. Consequently, there are exactly
\frac{|F(r^{\prime})|}{2r^{\prime}}
orbits of
F(r^{\prime})
under
G
for
r^{\prime}>1
. For
r^{\prime}=1
, it is easy to see that the elements in
F(1)\setminus\{0,1\}
is partitioned into
\epsilon=\frac{p-1}{2}
orbits for
p
odd, and for
p=2
there is no element in
F(1)\setminus\{0,1\}
.

When
r^{\prime}
is even, suppose that there are
N_{1}
orbits of length
2r^{\prime}
and
N_{2}
orbits of length
r^{\prime}
of
F(r^{\prime})
under
G
. Then

2r^{\prime}N_{1}+r^{\prime}N_{2}=|F(r^{\prime})|.

By the definition of
K(r^{\prime})
,
N_{2}=\frac{|K(r^{\prime})|}{r^{\prime}}
. By simple computation,

N_{1}=\frac{|F(r^{\prime})|-|K(r^{\prime})|}{2r^{\prime}}~~\text{ and }~~N_{1}+N_{2}=\frac{|F(r^{\prime})|+|K(r^{\prime})|}{2r^{\prime}}.

are

\sum_{r^{\prime}\mid r,r^{\prime}\neq 1}\frac{|F(r^{\prime})|+|K(r^{\prime})|}{2r^{\prime}}+\epsilon.

Case (II)
.

. Note that

\mathbb{F}^{*}_{q^{2}}\setminus\{x\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}:x^{q+1}=1\}=\bigcup_{r^{\prime}\mid 2r}\hat{F}(r^{\prime})=\left(\bigcup_{r^{\prime}\mid 2r,r^{\prime}\neq 1}\hat{F}(r^{\prime})\right)\cup\left(F(1)\setminus\{0,\pm 1\}\right),

.
To summarize, for

(52)

By the above analysis and the counting argument in Case (I), when

under

When

is even, we have to take care of two subcases. If

\frac{|F(r^{\prime})|+|K(r^{\prime})|}{2r^{\prime}}

Therefore, the total number of orbits is

\sum_{r^{\prime}\mid r,r^{\prime}>1}\frac{|F(r^{\prime})|+|K(r^{\prime})|}{2r^{\prime}}+\sum_{r^{\prime}\mid 2r,r^{\prime}\nmid r}\frac{|F(r^{\prime})|-|K(r^{\prime})|}{2r^{\prime}}+\epsilon,

where

equals the number of the orbits in

under the action of

.
∎

When

\epsilon\frac{\varphi(n)}{2}
by Theorem

(where

Theorem 4.3

and any prime power

and

denote the total number of the

-inequivalent Lunardon-Polverino scattered linear sets over

\sigma(r)=\sum_{d\mid r}d

(53)

\frac{p^{r}-p}{2r}<\frac{\Lambda(n,q)}{\varphi(n)/2}-\epsilon<\begin{cases}\frac{p^{r}}{2r}\left(1+\frac{\sigma(r)-r-1}{p^{r(1-\frac{1}{r_{1}})}}\right),&2\nmid r;\\
\frac{p^{r}}{2r}\left(1+\frac{\sigma(r)-r-1}{p^{\frac{r}{2}}}\right)+\sum_{i=1}^{s_{1}}p^{2^{i-1}},&r=2^{s_{1}};\\
\frac{p^{r}}{2r}\left(1+\frac{\sigma(r)-r}{p^{\frac{r}{2}}}+\frac{\sigma(r)-r-1}{p^{\frac{3r}{4}}}\right),&2\mid r\text{ and }\ell>1.\end{cases}

When

(54)

\frac{\Lambda(n,q)}{\varphi(n)/2}-\epsilon<\begin{cases}\frac{p^{2r}}{4r}\left(1+\frac{\sigma(2r)-2r-1}{p^{r}}\right),&2\nmid r;\\

\frac{p^{2r}}{4r}\left(1+\frac{\sigma(2r)-2r-1}{p^{r}}+\frac{2}{p^{\frac{3r}{2}}}+\frac{2(\sigma(r)-r-1)}{p^{\frac{7r}{4}}}\right),&2\mid r\text{ and }\ell>1.\end{cases}

and

(55)

\frac{\Lambda(n,q)}{\varphi(n)/2}-\epsilon>\begin{cases}\frac{p^{2r}-p}{4r}-\sum_{i=1}^{s_{1}+1}p^{2^{i-1}},&r=2^{s_{1}};\\
\frac{p^{2r}}{4r}\left(1-\frac{1}{p^{r}}-\frac{\sigma(2r)-2r-1}{p^{\frac{3r}{2}}}-\frac{1}{p^{2r-1}}\right),&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

According to a classical result by Gronwall
[
10
]
, the value of

\limsup_{r\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\sigma(r)}{r\log\log r}=e^{\gamma}

where
\gamma=0.5772156\dots
denotes the Euler-Mascheroni constant.
Therefore, by Theorem
4.2
, for given
n
, the value of
\Lambda(n,q)
is approximately
\frac{p^{r}\varphi(n)}{4r}
for odd
n
and
\frac{p^{2r}\varphi(n)}{8r}
for even
n
provided that
p^{r}
is large enough.

Proof of Theorem
4.3
.

We start by determining the upper bounds. To this aim we first give upper bounds on the quantities appearing in the expression of
\Lambda(n,q)
as in Theorem
4.2
.

As in the very beginning of Section
4
, we set
r_{1}
to be the smallest prime divisor of
r
.
As
F(m)\subsetneq\mathbb{F}_{p^{m}}
for any
m>1
,

\displaystyle\sum_{r^{\prime}\mid r,r^{\prime}>1}\frac{|F(r^{\prime})|}{2r^{\prime}}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2r}\sum_{r^{\prime}\mid r,r^{\prime}<r}r^{\prime}\left|F\left(\frac{r}{r^{\prime}}\right)\right|

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2r}\left(p^{r}+\sum_{r^{\prime}\mid r,1<r^{\prime}<r}r^{\prime}p^{\frac{r}{r^{\prime}}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{p^{r}}{2r}\left(1+\sum_{r^{\prime}\mid r,1<r^{\prime}<r}\frac{r^{\prime}}{p^{r-\frac{r}{r^{\prime}}}}\right)

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle\frac{p^{r}}{2r}\left(1+\sum_{r^{\prime}\mid r,1<r^{\prime}<r}\frac{r^{\prime}}{p^{r(1-1/r_{1})}}\right).

Thus

(56)

\sum_{r^{\prime}\mid r,r^{\prime}>1}\frac{|F(r^{\prime})|}{2r^{\prime}}<\frac{p^{r}}{2r}\left(1+\frac{\sigma(r)-r-1}{p^{r(1-1/r_{1})}}\right).

Next we derive an upper bound on the value of
\sum_{r^{\prime}\mid r,r^{\prime}>1}\frac{|K(r^{\prime})|}{2r^{\prime}}
.
Following the notation and results given in Lemma
4.1
, if
2\nmid r
and
r>1
, then

(57)

\sum_{r^{\prime}\mid r,r^{\prime}>1}\frac{|K(r^{\prime})|}{2r^{\prime}}=0.

If
r=2^{s_{1}}
and
r>1
, then

(58)

\sum_{r^{\prime}\mid r,r^{\prime}>1}\frac{|K(r^{\prime})|}{2r^{\prime}}=\sum_{i=1}^{s_{1}}\left(p^{2^{i-1}}-\frac{(-1)^{p+1}+1}{2}\right)\leq\sum_{i=1}^{s_{1}}p^{2^{i-1}},

since
|K(r^{\prime})|\leq p^{\frac{r^{\prime}}{2}}
. If
2\mid r
and
\ell>1
, then

\displaystyle\sum_{r^{\prime}\mid r,r^{\prime}>1}\frac{|K(r^{\prime})|}{2r^{\prime}}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2r}\sum_{r^{\prime}\mid r,r^{\prime}<r}r^{\prime}\left|K\left(\frac{r}{r^{\prime}}\right)\right|

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle\frac{p^{\frac{r}{2}}}{2r}\left(1+\sum_{r^{\prime}\mid r,1<r^{\prime}<r}\frac{r^{\prime}}{p^{\frac{r}{2}-\frac{r}{2r^{\prime}}}}\right)

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle\frac{p^{\frac{r}{2}}}{2r}\left(1+\sum_{r^{\prime}\mid r,1<r^{\prime}<r}\frac{r^{\prime}}{p^{\frac{r}{4}}}\right),

where the first inequality is obtained using that

\sum_{r^{\prime}\mid r,r^{\prime}<r}r^{\prime}\left|K\left(\frac{r}{r^{\prime}}\right)\right|=\left|K\left(r\right)\right|+\sum_{r^{\prime}\mid r,1<r^{\prime}<r}r^{\prime}\left|K\left(\frac{r}{r^{\prime}}\right)\right|<p^{\frac{r}{2}}+\sum_{r^{\prime}\mid r,1<r^{\prime}<r}r^{\prime}p^{\frac{r}{2r^{\prime}}}.

Hence,

(59)

\sum_{r^{\prime}\mid r,r^{\prime}>1}\frac{|K(r^{\prime})|}{2r^{\prime}}<\frac{p^{\frac{r}{2}}}{2r}\left(1+\frac{\sigma(r)-r-1}{p^{\frac{r}{4}}}\right).

Now, we are ready to prove the upper bound of
\Lambda(n,q)
.

When
n
is odd, plugging (
56
), (
57
), (
58
) and (
59
) into

\frac{\Lambda(n,q)}{\varphi(n)/2}-\epsilon=\sum_{r^{\prime}\mid r,r^{\prime}>1}\frac{|F(r^{\prime})|+|K(r^{\prime})|}{2r^{\prime}},

we get the upper bound given in (
53
).

When
n
is even,

\displaystyle\frac{\Lambda(n,q)}{\varphi(n)/2}-\epsilon=

\displaystyle\sum_{r^{\prime}\mid r,r^{\prime}>1}\frac{|F(r^{\prime})|+|K(r^{\prime})|}{2r^{\prime}}+\sum_{r^{\prime}\mid 2r,r^{\prime}\nmid r}\frac{|F(r^{\prime})|-|K(r^{\prime})|}{2r^{\prime}}

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle\sum_{r^{\prime}\mid 2r,r^{\prime}>1}\frac{|F(r^{\prime})|}{2r^{\prime}}+\sum_{r^{\prime}\mid r,r^{\prime}>1}\frac{|K(r^{\prime})|}{2r^{\prime}}.

Plugging (
56
), (
57
), (
58
) and (
59
) into it, we get (
54
).

Finally, we compute the lower bound. First, it is easy to see that

\sum_{r^{\prime}\mid r,r^{\prime}>1}|F(r^{\prime})|=|\mathbb{F}_{p^{r}}\setminus\mathbb{F}_{p}|=p^{r}-p.

When
n
is odd,

\displaystyle\frac{\Lambda(n,q)}{\varphi(n)/2}-\epsilon>

\displaystyle\sum_{r^{\prime}\mid r,r^{\prime}>1}\frac{|F(r^{\prime})|+|K(r^{\prime})|}{2r}

\displaystyle>

\displaystyle\frac{p^{r}-p}{2r}.

Therefore, (
53
) is proved.

When
n
is even,

\displaystyle\frac{\Lambda(n,q)}{\varphi(n)/2}-\epsilon>

\displaystyle\sum_{r^{\prime}\mid 2r,r^{\prime}>1}\frac{|F(r^{\prime})|}{2r^{\prime}}-\sum_{r^{\prime}\mid 2r,r^{\prime}>1}\frac{|K(r^{\prime})|}{2r^{\prime}}

\displaystyle>

\displaystyle\frac{p^{2r}-p}{4r}-\sum_{r^{\prime}\mid 2r,r^{\prime}>1}\frac{|K(r^{\prime})|}{2r^{\prime}}.

Then

\frac{\Lambda(n,q)}{\varphi(n)/2}-\epsilon>\begin{cases}\frac{p^{2r}-p}{4r}-\sum_{i=1}^{s_{1}+1}p^{2^{i-1}},&r=2^{s_{1}};\\
\frac{p^{2r}-p}{4r}-\frac{p^{r}}{4r}\left(1+\frac{\sigma(2r)-2r-1}{p^{\frac{r}{2}}}\right),&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Consequently, we obtain (
55
).
∎

Elementary Analysis of Policy Gradient Methods
Jiacai Liu, Wenye Li, Ke Wei

Abstract
Projected policy gradient under the simplex parameterization, policy gradient and natural policy gradient under the softmax parameterization, are fundamental algorithms in reinforcement learning. There have been a flurry of recent activities in studying these algorithms from the theoretical aspect. Despite this, their convergence behavior is still not fully understood, even given the access to exact policy evaluations. In this paper, we focus on the discounted MDP setting and conduct a systematic study of the aforementioned policy optimization methods. Several novel results are presented, including 1) global linear convergence of projected policy gradient for any constant step size, 2) sublinear convergence of softmax policy gradient for any constant step size, 3) global linear convergence of softmax natural policy gradient for any constant step size, 4) global linear convergence of entropy regularized softmax policy gradient for a wider range of constant step sizes than existing result, 5) tight local linear convergence rate of entropy regularized natural policy gradient, and 6) a new and concise local quadratic convergence rate of soft policy iteration without the assumption on the stationary distribution under the optimal policy. New and elementary analysis techniques have been developed to establish these results. Keywords. Reinforcement learning, policy gradient methods, entropy regularization, sublinear convergence, linear convergence, quadratic convergence

Introduction

Reinforcement Learning (RL) has achieved great success in the fields of both science and engineering, see for example
[
6
,
8
,
19
,
25
]
. As the fundamental model for RL, Markov Decision Process (MDP) can be represented as a tuple
{\cal M}({\cal S},{\cal A},P,r,\gamma)
, where
{\cal S}
is the state space and
{\cal A}
is the action space,

P(s^{\prime}|s,a)
is the transition probability from state
s
to state
s^{\prime}
under action
a
,
r:{\cal S}\times{\cal A}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is the reward function, and
\gamma\in[0,1)
is the discounted factor.
In this paper, we focus on the finite MDP setting, i.e.,
|{\cal S}|<\infty
and
|{\cal A}|<\infty
, and also assume
r\in[0,1]
for simplicity. Letting

\displaystyle\Delta({\cal A})=\Big\{\theta\in\mathbb{R}^{|{\cal A}|}|~\theta_{i}\geq 0,\;\sum_{i=1}^{|{\cal A}|}\theta_{i}=1\Big\},

we can define the set of all admissible policies for MDP as,

\displaystyle\Pi:=\left\{\pi=(\pi(\cdot|s))_{s\in{\cal S}}\;\big|\;\pi(\cdot|s)\in\Delta({\cal A})\;\mbox{for all }s\in{\cal S}\right\}.

Given policy
\pi\in\Pi
, the value function
V^{\pi}(s)
is defined as the average discounted cumulative reward over the random trajectory starting from
s
and induced by
\pi
,

\displaystyle V^{\pi}\left(s\right):=\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}{\gamma^{t}}r\left(s_{t},a_{t}\right)|s_{0}=s,\,\pi\right].

(1.1)

The overall goal of RL is to seek an optimal policy to maximize the state values, which can be expressed as

\displaystyle\max_{\pi\in\Pi}\;V^{\pi}(\mu)=\mathbb{E}_{s\sim\mu}\left[V^{\pi}(s)\right],

where
\mu\in\Delta(\mathcal{S})
denotes the initial state distribution.

Policy optimization refers to a family of effective methods for solving challenging RL problems. In contrast to value-based methods such as value iteration and policy iteration, policy optimization methods conduct direct search in the policy space through the parameterization of policies. More precisely, given a parameterized policy class
\{\pi_{\theta}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\Pi\}
, the state value in (
1.1
) becomes a function of the parameters, denoted
V^{\pi_{\theta}}(\mu)
. Thus, the problem of seeking an optimal policy over the parameterized policy class turns to be a finite dimensional optimization problem, and different kinds of optimization methods can be applied to solve this problem. Maybe the simplest one among them is the policy gradient method
[
28
,
26
]
,

\displaystyle\theta^{+}\leftarrow\theta+\eta\,\nabla_{\theta}V^{\pi_{\theta}}\left(\mu\right),

(1.2)

where
\nabla_{\theta}V^{\pi_{\theta}}\left(\mu\right)
is the policy gradient of
V^{\pi_{\theta}}(\mu)
. Different parameterization methods lead to different policy gradient methods, for instance projected policy gradient (PPG) under the simplex or direct parameterization and softmax policy gradient (PG) under the softmax parameterization. More details will be given in later sections. Natural policy gradient (NPG,
[
9
]
) is an important variant of policy gradient methods, which searches along a preconditioned policy gradient direction based on the Fisher information matrix.
Other important variants of policy optimization methods include trust region policy optimization (TRPO)
[
22
]
and proximal policy optimization
(PPO)
[
23
]
, which are closely related to NPG.

Exploration versus exploitation is an important paradigm in RL. Entropy regularization can be introduced to enhance the exploration ability of policies in policy optimization.
In this case, the entropy regularized state value is given by

\displaystyle V^{\pi}_{\tau}(s)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{\tau}\left[\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\gamma^{t}\left({r(s_{t},a_{t})-\tau\log\pi(a_{t}|s_{t})}\right)|s_{0}=s,\pi\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{\tau}\left[\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\gamma^{t}\left(r(s_{t},a_{t})+\tau\mathcal{H}(\pi(\cdot|s_{t})\right)|s_{0}=s,\pi\right],

where
\tau>0
is the regularization parameter and
\mathcal{H}(p)=-\sum_{a}p_{a}\log p_{a}
is the entropy of a probability distribution. Intuitively, larger entropy represents more possibilities. The policy gradient method for the entropy regularized policy optimization problem

\displaystyle\max_{\pi\in\Pi}\;V^{\pi}_{\tau}(\mu)=\mathbb{E}_{s\sim\mu}\left[V^{\pi}_{\tau}(s)\right],

is overall similar to that in (
1.2
), but with
\nabla_{\theta}V^{\pi_{\theta}}\left(\mu\right)
being replaced by
\nabla_{\theta}V^{\pi_{\theta}}_{\tau}\left(\mu\right)
.
Moreover, NPG can be similarly developed for the entropy regularized scenario.

1.1
Related Work

The convergence analyses of policy optimization methods have received a lot of attention recently under various settings, for example from the tabular MDP setting to the setting that incorporates function approximations, from the setting where exact gradient information is assumed to the setting that involves sample-based estimators. An exhaustive review about this is beyond the scope of this paper. Thus, we mainly focus on the convergence results of different exact policy gradients in the tabular setting, which are mostly related to our work.

Under the simplex or direct policy parameterization, the
O(1/\sqrt{k})
sublinear convergence has been established in
[
1
,
4
]
for projected policy gradient (PPG) with constant step size, which has been improved to
O(1/k)
subsequently in
[
29
,
32
]
. Note that the results in
[
1
,
4
,
29
,
32
]
all require the step size to be sufficiently small so that the smooth property of the value function can be used in the optimization analysis framework. In contrast, an approach by bounding the improvement of PPG at each state through the maximum improvement (or the improvement of PI at each state) is developed in
[
15
]
and the
O(1/k)
sublinear convergence of PPG is established for any constant step size. Moreover, it is further shown that in
[
15
]
that PPG can find an optimal policy in a finite number of iterations.

For exact policy gradient methods under softmax parameterization, the global convergence of softmax PG is established in
[
1
]
provided the step size
\eta
satisfies
\eta\leq 1/L
, where
L
is the smoothness constant of the value function. By utilizing the gradient dominant property of the value function, the
O(1/k)
sublinear convergence of softmax PG is established in
[
18
]
. In addition, it is shown in
[
16
]
that softmax PG can also achieve linear convergence using the adaptive step sizes based on the
geometric information.
In the presence of regularization, it is shown in
[
1
]
that softmax PG with log barrier regularization attain an
O(1/\sqrt{k})
convergence rate, while the linear convergence is established for softmax PG under entropy regularization (simply referred to as entropy softmax PG later) in
[
18
]
.

The
O(1/\sqrt{k})
convergence rate of NPG under softmax parameterization is obtained in
[
24
]
, and this rate has been improved to
O(1/k)
in
[
1
]
. The fast local linear convergence rate of softmax NPG is established in
[
11
]
based on the contraction of the non-optimal probability measure at each state. For softmax NPG with entropy regularization (simply referred to as entropy softmax NPG later), the linear convergence is established in
[
5
]
by expressing the update into a form for which the
\gamma
-contraction property of the Bellman operator can be used. This result has been extended to more general regularizers in
[
31
]
. Note that softmax NPG can be cast as a special policy mirror descent (PMD) method with KL divergence, and the convergence of PMD with and without regularizers has also been studied. The
O(1/k)
sublinear convergence of PMD is established in
[
29
]
for any constant step size which generalizes the analysis in
[
1
]
for softmax NPG based on the three-point lemma.
In
[
29
]
, the linear convergence is also provided for the non-adaptive geometrically increasing step sizes. The
\gamma
-rate convergence of PMD under the adaptive step sizes is established in
[
7
]
. The analyses in
[
29
]
have been extended to the log-linear policy and the more general function approximation scenario in
[
30
,
2
]
.
The convergence of PMD with general convex and strongly convex regularizers is studied in
[
12
]
and it is shown that PMD with strongly convex regularizer can achieve a linear convergence. By adopting a homotopic technique using diminishing regularization combined with increasing step sizes, the linear convergence of PMD with entropy regularization is established in
[
14
]
.

1.2
Main Contributions

Despite the recent intensive investigations, there are still a few important issues to be addressed on the convergence of the very basic policy optimization methods, including projected policy gradient (PPG), softmax PG and NPG in the both the non-entropy and entropy cases.
A thorough treatment will be given towards this line of research in this paper. The main contributions are summarized as follows:

•

Global linear convergence of PPG.
Further to the sublinear convergence of PPG in
[
15
]
, we extend the analysis therein to establish the linear convergence of PPG for any constant step size despite the highly non-convexity of the RL problem, see Theorem
3.3
. Moreover, a simple non-adaptive increasing step size is proposed to further improve the linear convergence rate of PPG, see Theorem
3.4
.

•

Sublinear convergence of softmax PG for a full range of step sizes.

As stated earlier, the sublinear convergence of softmax PG has been established in
[
18
]
for small constant step size. The analysis therein follows an optimization analysis framework and thus requires the step size to be overall smaller than the reciprocal of the smoothness constant of
V^{\pi_{\theta}}(\mu)
. In contrast, a new elementary analysis technique has been developed to break the step size restriction so that the sublinear convergence can be established for any constant step size
\eta>0
, see Theorem
4.5
.
A new sublinear lower bound is also established which demonstrates that softmax PG converges at most sublinearly for any
\eta>0
, see Theorem
4.6
. Moreover, a simple adaptive step size is proposed in Theorem
4.7
such that softmax PG can achieve linear convergence with better parameter dependency than the one in
[
16
]
.

•

Global linear convergence of softmax NPG for constant step size.

In addition to the existing sublinear convergence
[
1
]
and local linear convergence
[
29
]
of softmax NPG with constant step size, we show that a global linear convergence can also be established for it, see Theorem
5.4
. In addition, a local linear convergence lower bound is also established for general MDP which matches the fast local linear convergence upper bound established in
[
11
]
.

•

Global linear convergence of entropy softmax PG for a wider range of step sizes.
Utilizing an analysis technique that is similar to that for softmax PG, the global linear convergence of entropy softmax PG is established for a wider range of step sizes than that in
[
18
]
, also with improved convergence result, see Theorem
6.9
.

•

New local quadratic convergence rate of soft PI and tight local linear convergence rate of entropy softmax NPG.

Soft PI can be viewed as the limit of entropy softmax NPG as the step size approaches infinity. A new local quadratic convergence rate in the form of
\gamma^{\displaystyle 2^{k-k_{0}}}
has been established, see Theorem
6.13
. Compared with the result in
[
5
]
, our result only essentially relies on the discount factor
\gamma
and does not require an additional assumption on the stationary distribution under the optimal policy. As mentioned earlier, the global linear convergence of entropy softmax NPG has also been established in
[
5
]
. However, as
\eta\rightarrow\infty
, the limit of the rate is a fixed constant, which cannot reflect the fact that entropy softmax NPG should converge faster (locally) as
\eta
becomes larger since the algorithm tends to be soft PI and the latter one enjoys a local quadratic convergence rate. Motivated by this observation, a local linear convergence rate in the form of
\left(\frac{1}{(\eta\tau+1)^{2}}\right)^{k-k_{0}}
is established. It is meanwhile shown that this rate is indeed tight.

Overall, our analyses are elementary and a key ingredient is essentially about bounding the state-wise improvement, expressed in terms of the Bellman operator as
\mathcal{T}^{k+1}V^{k}(s)-V^{k}(s)
for the non-entropy case and as
\mathcal{T}_{\tau}^{k+1}V_{\tau}^{k}(s)-V_{\tau}^{k}(s)
for the entropy case, directly based on the update rules of the corresponding policy gradient methods. Roughly speaking,

•

for PPG, we use the existing bound of
\mathcal{T}^{k+1}V^{k}(s)-V^{k}(s)
based on the maximum improvement
\max_{a}A^{k}(s,a)
(established in
[
15
]
) to further establish the global linear convergence result, where
A^{k}(s,a)
is the advantage function at the
k
-th iteration;

•

for softmax PG, a bound of
\mathcal{T}^{k+1}V^{k}(s)-V^{k}(s)
is established through
\max_{a}|\pi^{k}(a|s)A^{k}(s,a)|
;

•

for softmax NPG, two different bounds of
\mathcal{T}^{k+1}V^{k}(s)-V^{k}(s)
are established through
\max_{a}A^{k}(s,a)
and
\sum_{a}\pi^{k,*}A^{k}(s,a)
, respectively, where
\pi^{k,*}
is a particular optimal policy chosen at the
k
-th iteration;

•

for entropy softmax PG, a bound of
\mathcal{T}_{\tau}^{k+1}V_{\tau}^{k}(s)-V_{\tau}^{k}(s)
is established through
\max_{a}|\pi^{k}(a|s)A^{k}_{\tau}(s,a)|
, where
A^{k}_{\tau}(s,a)
is the advantage function defined for the entropy case;

•

for entropy softmax NPG, a bound of
\mathcal{T}_{\tau}^{k+1}V_{\tau}^{k}(s)-V_{\tau}^{k}(s)
is established through
\mathrm{KL}(\pi^{k+1}(\cdot|s)\,\|\,\pi^{k}(\cdot|s))
.

2
General Analysis Framework

2.1
Preliminaries

Compared to the state value defined in (
1.1
) which fixes the initial state, the action value is defined by fixing the initial state-action pair,

\displaystyle Q^{\pi}\left(s,a\right)

\displaystyle:=\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}{\gamma^{t}r\left(s_{t},a_{t}\right)}|s_{0}=s,a_{0}=a,\pi\right].

(2.1)

In addition, the advantage function is defined as
A^{\pi}(s,a)=Q^{\pi}(s,a)-V^{\pi}(s)
.
It can be verified that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{a\sim\pi(\cdot|s)}\left[A^{\pi}(s,a)\right]=0.

For an arbitrary vector
V\in\mathbb{R}^{|{\cal S}|}
, the Bellman operator induced by a policy
\pi
is defined as

\displaystyle{\cal T}^{\pi}V(s):=\mathbb{E}_{a\sim\pi(\cdot|s)}\mathbb{E}_{s^{\prime}\sim P(\cdot|s,a)}\left[r(s,a)+\gamma V(s^{\prime})\right].

It is easy to see that
V^{\pi}
is the fixed point of
\mathcal{T}^{\pi}
, i.e.

{\cal T}^{\pi}V^{\pi}=V^{\pi}.

Given two policies
\pi_{1},\pi_{2}\in\Pi
, one has

\displaystyle{\cal T}^{\pi_{1}}V^{\pi_{2}}(s)-V^{\pi_{2}}(s)=\sum_{a\in{\cal A}}\pi_{1}(a|s)A^{\pi_{2}}(s,a).

Moreover, the value difference between two policies can be given based on
{\cal T}^{\pi_{1}}V^{\pi_{2}}(s)-V^{\pi_{2}}(s)
.

Lemma 2.1

(Performance difference lemma
[
10
]
)
.

Given two policies
\pi_{1}
and
\pi_{2}
, there holds

\displaystyle V^{\pi_{1}}(\rho)-V^{\pi_{2}}(\rho)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-\gamma}\mathbb{E}_{s\sim d^{\pi_{1}}_{\rho}}\left[{\cal T}^{\pi_{1}}V^{\pi_{2}}(s)-V^{\pi_{2}}(s)\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-\gamma}\mathbb{E}_{s\sim d^{\pi_{1}}_{\rho}}\left[\sum_{a\in{\cal A}}\pi_{1}(a|s)A^{\pi_{2}}(s,a)\right].

The optimal Bellman operator is defined as

\displaystyle{\cal T}V(s):=\max_{\pi\in\Pi}{\cal T}^{\pi}V(s)=\max_{a\in{\cal A}}\left\{\mathbb{E}_{s^{\prime}\sim P(\cdot|s,a)}\left[r(s,a)+\gamma V(s^{\prime})\right]\right\}.

Let
V^{*}
and
Q^{*}
be the optimal state and action values, with the corresponding optimal policy denoted by
\pi^{*}
(may be non-unique), and define
A^{*}(s,a)=Q^{*}(s,a)-V^{*}(s)
. It is well-known that
V^{*}
satisfies the Bellman optimality equation:
{\cal T}V^{*}=V^{*}
. It is also noted that both
{\cal T}^{\pi}
and
{\cal T}
are
\gamma
-contraction under the infinity norm:

\displaystyle\left\|{\cal T}^{\pi}V_{1}-{\cal T}^{\pi}V_{2}\right\|_{\infty}\leq\gamma\left\|V_{1}-V_{2}\right\|_{\infty}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\left\|{\cal T}V_{1}-{\cal T}V_{2}\right\|_{\infty}\leq\gamma\left\|V_{1}-V_{2}\right\|_{\infty}.

Given a state value
V^{k}
(short for
V^{\pi^{k}}
) associated with policy
\pi^{k}
, policy iteration (PI) attempts to obtain a new policy
\pi^{k+1}
through the greedy improvement:

\displaystyle\mathrm{supp}(\pi^{k+1}(\cdot|s))\subset\argmax_{a}Q^{k}(s,a),

(2.2)

where
Q^{k}(s,a)
is short for
Q^{\pi^{k}}(s,a)
.
It is easy to see that the improvement of
\pi^{k+1}
over
\pi^{k}
is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}^{k+1}V^{k}(s)-V^{k}(s)={\cal T}V^{k}(s)-V^{k}(s)=\max_{a\in{\cal A}}A^{k}(s,a),

where
\mathcal{T}^{k+1}
and
A^{k}(s,a)
are short for
\mathcal{T}^{\pi^{k+1}}
and
A^{\pi^{k}}(s,a)
, respectively.

Next we present a few useful lemmas that will be used frequently later. The first two lemmas can be verified directly.

Lemma 2.2
.

Assume
r(s,a)\in[0,1]
. For any policy
\pi
, one has

\displaystyle V^{\pi}(s)\in\left[0,\frac{1}{1-\gamma}\right],\;Q^{\pi}(s,a)\in\left[0,\frac{1}{1-\gamma}\right],\;A^{\pi}(s,a)\in\left[-\frac{1}{1-\gamma},\frac{1}{1-\gamma}\right].

Lemma 2.3
.

For any policy
\pi
,

\displaystyle\left\|Q^{*}-Q^{\pi}\right\|_{\infty}\leq\gamma\left\|V^{*}-V^{\pi}\right\|_{\infty},\quad\left\|A^{*}-A^{\pi}\right\|_{\infty}\leq\left\|V^{*}-V^{\pi}\right\|_{\infty}.

Before presenting the next lemma, further define
{\cal A}^{*}_{s}
as the optimal action set at state
s
,

\displaystyle{\cal A}^{*}_{s}:=\argmax_{a\in{\cal A}}\;A^{*}(s,a),

and define
b^{\pi}_{s}
as the probability on non-optimal actions

\displaystyle b^{\pi}_{s}:=\sum_{a\not\in{\cal A}^{*}_{s}}\pi(a|s).

The optimal advantage gap, denoted
\Delta
, is defined as

\displaystyle\Delta:=\min_{s\in\tilde{S},a\not\in{\cal A}^{*}_{s}}\left|A^{*}(s,a)\right|

where
\tilde{S}=\left\{s\in{\cal S}:{\cal A}^{*}_{s}\neq{\cal A}\right\}
. Intuitively,
b^{\pi}_{s}
measures the non-optimality of
\pi
, and
\Delta
in some extent reflects the difficulty of the RL problem in the MDP setting. The following lemma shows that the state value error is of the same order as the non-optimal probability measure, which can be proved directly by applying the performance difference lemma to
-(V^{\pi}(\rho)-V^{*}(\rho))
.

Lemma 2.4

(
[
11
,
15
]
)
.

For any
\rho\in\Delta({\cal S})
,

\displaystyle\Delta\cdot\mathbb{E}_{s\sim\rho}[b^{\pi}_{s}]\leq V^{*}(\rho)-V^{\pi}(\rho)\leq\frac{1}{(1-\gamma)^{2}}\cdot\mathbb{E}_{s\sim d^{\pi}_{\rho}}[b^{\pi}_{s}].

The last two lemmas are concerned with the properties of the covariance of random variables.
They can be easily verified and we include the proofs in Appendix
A
for completeness.

Lemma 2.5
.

For any random variable
X
and two real-valued functions
f
,
g
, there holds

\displaystyle\mathrm{Cov}(f(X),g(X))=\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}_{X,Y}\left[(f(X)-f(Y))(g(X)-g(Y))\right],

where
Y
is an i.i.d. copy of
X
.

Lemma 2.6
.

For any random variable
X
and two monotonically increasing functions
f
and
g
, there holds

\displaystyle\mathrm{Cov}(f(X),g(X))\geq 0.

In this paper, we consider minimizing
V^{\pi_{\theta}}(\mu)
for fixed
\mu\in\Delta(\mathcal{S})
with
\tilde{\mu}=\min_{s}\mu(s)>0
. The state value error
V^{*}(\rho)-V^{k}(\rho)
will be evacuated for an arbitrary
\rho\in\Delta(\mathcal{S})
. Whenever it is necessary, we will assume
\tilde{\rho}=\min_{s}\rho(s)>0
. Otherwise,
V^{*}(\rho)-V^{k}(\rho)
can be evaluated through
V^{*}(\mu)-V^{k}(\mu)
since

\displaystyle V^{*}(\rho)-V^{k}(\rho)=

\displaystyle\sum_{s}\rho(s)\left(V^{*}(s)-V^{k}(s)\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{s}\frac{\rho(s)}{\mu(s)}\mu(s)\left(V^{*}(s)-V^{k}(s)\right)

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\frac{\rho}{\mu}\right\|_{\infty}\left(V^{*}(\mu)-V^{k}(\mu)\right).

For any policy
\pi
, define the visitation measure
d_{\rho}^{\pi}:\mathcal{S}\rightarrow\Delta(\mathcal{S})
as

\displaystyle d_{\rho}^{\pi}\left(s\right):=\left(1-\gamma\right)\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}{\gamma^{t}\mathbf{1}\left\{s_{t}=s\right\}}|s_{0}\sim\rho,\,\pi\right].

The discuss in this section mainly centers around the following two quantities:

\displaystyle{\cal L}_{k}^{k+1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-\gamma}\mathbb{E}_{s\sim d_{\rho}^{\pi^{k,*}}}\left[{\cal T}^{k+1}V^{k}(s)-V^{k}(s)\right]=\frac{1}{1-\gamma}\mathbb{E}_{s\sim d_{\rho}^{\pi^{k,*}}}\left[\sum_{a\in{\cal A}}\pi^{k+1}(a|s)A^{k}(s,a)\right],

(2.3)

\displaystyle{\cal L}_{k}^{*}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-\gamma}\mathbb{E}_{s\sim d_{\rho}^{\pi^{k,*}}}\left[{\cal T}^{*}V^{k}(s)-V^{k}(s)\right]=\frac{1}{1-\gamma}\mathbb{E}_{s\sim d_{\rho}^{\pi^{k,*}}}\left[\sum_{a\in{\cal A}}\pi^{*}(a|s)A^{k}(s,a)\right],

(2.4)

where
d_{\rho}^{\pi^{k,*}}
is the visitation measure corresponding to certain optimal polity
\pi^{k,*}
chosen at the
k
-th iteration, and
\mathcal{T}^{*}
is the Bellman operator associated with
\pi^{k,*}
. Unless stated otherwise, we will assume the same optimal policy is used across all the
k
most of the times, and in this case
d_{\rho}^{\pi^{k,*}}
is simplified to
d_{\rho}^{*}
.
It follows from the performance difference lemma that

\displaystyle{\cal L}_{k}^{*}=V^{*}(\rho)-V^{k}(\rho),

and thus
{\cal L}_{k}^{*}
is irrelevant to the choice of the optimal policy. In addition, the following bounds hold for
\mathcal{L}^{*}_{k}
, whose proof can be found in Appendix
A
.

Lemma 2.7
.

For any optimal policy
\pi^{*}
, there holds

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{k}^{*}\leq\frac{1}{1-\gamma}\mathbb{E}_{s\sim d_{\rho}^{*}}\left[\max_{a}A^{k}(s,a)\right]\leq\frac{1}{(1-\gamma)\tilde{\rho}}\mathcal{L}^{*}_{k}.

In addition to
\mathcal{A}_{s}^{*}
, the notation
{\cal A}^{\pi}_{s}
will also be used, defined as

\displaystyle{\cal A}^{\pi}_{s}:=\argmax_{a\in{\cal A}}\;A^{\pi}(s,a).

Moreover, we may use for example
\pi^{k}(a|s)
and
\pi^{k}_{s,a}
,
\pi^{k}_{s}
and
\pi^{k}(\cdot|s)
,
A^{k}(s,a)
and
A^{k}_{s,a}
exchangeably.

2.2
Sublinear Convergence Analysis

In this subsection, we present two general conditions for the sublinear convergence of a RL algorithm, as well as a general condition to establish the sublinear linear convergence lower bound.

Theorem 2.8

(Sublinear Convergence)
.

Let
\{\pi^{k}\}_{k\geq 0}
be a policy sequence which satisfies
\mathcal{T}^{k+1}V^{k}\geq V^{k}
for all
k

(
or equivalently,
\sum_{a}\pi^{k+1}(a|s)A^{k}(s,a)\geq 0,\,\,\forall\,s
)
. Suppose there exists a sequence of optimal policies
\{\pi^{k,*}\}_{k\geq 0}
and a positive constant sequence
\{C_{k}\}_{k\geq 0}
such that

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{k}^{k+1}\geq C_{k}\left(\mathcal{L}_{k}^{*}\right)^{2},

where both
\mathcal{L}_{k}^{k+1}
and
\mathcal{L}_{k}^{*}
are defined with respect to the visitation measure
d_{\rho}^{\pi^{k,*}}
. Then

\displaystyle V^{*}(\rho)-V^{k}(\rho)\leq\frac{1}{k}\frac{1}{C\,(1-\gamma)\,\vartheta},

where
C=\inf_{k}C_{k}
and
\vartheta=\inf_{k}\left\|\frac{d_{\rho}^{\pi^{k,*}}}{\rho}\right\|_{\infty}^{-1}\geq\tilde{\rho}
.

Proof.

Note that
\mathcal{L}_{k}^{*}=V^{*}(\rho)-V^{k}(\rho)
is independent of
d_{\rho}^{\pi^{k,*}}
. In addition,

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{k}^{*}-\mathcal{L}_{k+1}^{*}=V^{k+1}(\rho)-V^{k}(\rho)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-\gamma}\sum_{s}d^{k+1}_{\rho}(s)\sum_{a}\pi^{k+1}(a|s)A^{k}(s,a)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-\gamma}\sum_{s}\frac{d^{k+1}_{\rho}(s)}{d^{\pi^{k,*}}_{\rho}(s)}d^{\pi^{k,*}}_{\rho}(s)\sum_{a}\pi^{k+1}(a|s)A^{k}(s,a)

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{s}\frac{\rho(s)}{d^{\pi^{k,*}}_{\rho}(s)}d^{\pi^{k,*}}_{\rho}(s)\sum_{a}\pi^{k+1}(a|s)A^{k}(s,a)

\displaystyle\geq(1-\gamma)\left\|\frac{d_{\rho}^{\pi^{k,*}}}{\rho}\right\|_{\infty}^{-1}\mathcal{L}_{k}^{k+1}

\displaystyle\geq(1-\gamma)\left\|\frac{d_{\rho}^{\pi^{k,*}}}{\rho}\right\|_{\infty}^{-1}C_{k}\,\left(\mathcal{L}_{k}^{*}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\geq C(1-\gamma)\,\vartheta\left(\mathcal{L}_{k}^{*}\right)^{2},

where the first inequality leverages that
\sum_{a}\pi^{k+1}(a|s)A^{k}(s,a)\geq 0
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\mathcal{L}_{k+1}^{*}}-\frac{1}{\mathcal{L}_{k}^{*}}=\frac{\mathcal{L}_{k}^{*}-\mathcal{L}_{k+1}^{*}}{\mathcal{L}_{k}^{*}\mathcal{L}_{k+1}^{*}}\geq\frac{\mathcal{L}_{k}^{*}-\mathcal{L}_{k+1}^{*}}{\left(\mathcal{L}_{k}^{*}\right)^{2}}\geq C\,(1-\gamma)\,\vartheta,

where the first inequality leverages that
{\cal L}_{k}^{*}\geq{\cal L}_{k+1}^{*}
. It follows that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\mathcal{L}_{k}^{*}}\geq\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\left(\frac{1}{\mathcal{L}_{j+1}^{*}}-\frac{1}{\mathcal{L}_{j}^{*}}\right)\geq C\,(1-\gamma)\,\vartheta\,k,

which completes the proof.
∎

Theorem 2.9

(Sublinear Convergence by Controlling Error Terms)
.

Let
\{\pi^{k}\}_{k\geq 0}
be a policy sequence which satisfies
\mathcal{T}^{k+1}V^{k}\geq V^{k}
for all
k

(
or equivalently,
\sum_{a}\pi^{k+1}(a|s)A^{k}(s,a)\geq 0,\,\,\forall\,s
)
. Assume that

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{k}^{k+1}\geq C\mathcal{L}_{k}^{*}-\varepsilon_{k}\quad\mbox{where }C>0\mbox{ and }\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\varepsilon_{k}<\infty.

(2.5)

Then

\displaystyle V^{*}(\rho)-V^{k}(\rho)\leq\frac{1}{k\,C}\left(\frac{1}{(1-\gamma)^{2}}+\sum_{t=0}^{k-1}\varepsilon_{t}\right).

Proof.

Noticing that
d_{d_{\rho}^{*}}^{k+1}(s)\geq(1-\gamma)d_{\rho}^{*}(s)
, one has

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{k}^{k+1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-\gamma}\sum_{s}d_{\rho}^{*}(s)\left(\mathcal{T}^{k+1}V^{k}(s)-V^{k}(s)\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{(1-\gamma)^{2}}\sum_{s}d_{d_{\rho}^{*}}^{k+1}(s)\left(\mathcal{T}^{k+1}V^{k}(s)-V^{k}(s)\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(1-\gamma)}\left(V^{k+1}(d_{\rho}^{*})-V^{k}(d_{\rho}^{*})\right).

It follows that

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{k}^{*}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{k}\sum_{t=0}^{k-1}\mathcal{L}_{t}^{*}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{k\,C}\sum_{t=0}^{k-1}\left(\mathcal{L}_{t}^{t+1}+\varepsilon_{t}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{k\,C}\left(\frac{1}{1-\gamma}\left(V^{k}(d_{\rho}^{*})-V^{0}(d_{\rho}^{*})\right)+\sum_{t=0}^{k-1}\varepsilon_{t}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{k\,C}\left(\frac{1}{(1-\gamma)^{2}}+\sum_{t=0}^{k-1}\varepsilon_{t}\right),

which completes the proof.
∎

Theorem 2.10

(Sublinear Lower Bound)
.

Let
\{\pi^{k}\}_{k\geq 0}
be a policy sequence which satisfies
\mathcal{T}^{k+1}V^{k}\geq V^{k}
for all
k

(
or equivalently,
\sum_{a}\pi^{k+1}(a|s)A^{k}(a|s)\geq 0,\,\,\forall\,s
)
.
Assume that

\displaystyle 0\leq\mathcal{L}_{k}^{k+1}\leq C\,\left(\mathcal{L}_{k}^{*}\right)^{2}.

Then for any fixed
\sigma\in(0,1)
, there exists a time
T(\sigma)
such that

\displaystyle\forall\,k\geq T(\sigma):\quad V^{*}(\rho)-V^{k}(\rho)\geq\frac{1}{k}\frac{1-\sigma}{2\,C}\left\|\frac{1}{d_{\rho}^{*}}\right\|_{\infty}^{-1}.

Proof.

First note that

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{k}^{*}-\mathcal{L}_{k+1}^{*}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-\gamma}\sum_{s}d_{\rho}^{k+1}(s)\sum_{a}\pi^{k+1}(a|s)A^{k}(s,a)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-\gamma}\sum_{s}\frac{d_{\rho}^{k+1}(s)}{d_{\rho}^{*}(s)}d_{\rho}^{*}(s)\sum_{a}\pi^{k+1}(a|s)A^{k}(s,a)

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\frac{1}{d_{\rho}^{*}}\right\|_{\infty}\mathcal{L}_{k}^{k+1}

\displaystyle\leq C\left\|\frac{1}{d_{\rho}^{*}}\right\|_{\infty}\left(\mathcal{L}_{k}^{*}\right)^{2},

where the first inequality utilizes the condition
\sum_{a}\pi^{k+1}(a|s)A^{k}(s,a)\geq 0
. It follows that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\mathcal{L}_{k+1}^{*}}-\frac{1}{\mathcal{L}_{k}^{*}}=\frac{\mathcal{L}_{k}^{*}-\mathcal{L}_{k+1}^{*}}{\mathcal{L}_{k}^{*}\mathcal{L}_{k+1}^{*}}\leq\frac{C\left\|\frac{1}{d_{\rho}^{*}}\right\|_{\infty}\left(\mathcal{L}_{k}^{*}\right)^{2}}{\mathcal{L}_{k}^{*}\mathcal{L}_{k+1}^{*}}=C\left\|\frac{1}{d_{\rho}^{*}}\right\|_{\infty}\frac{\mathcal{L}_{k}^{*}}{\mathcal{L}_{k+1}^{*}}.

Since
\mathcal{L}_{k}^{k+1}\geq 0
, the sequence
\{V^{k}(\rho)\}
is non-deceasing, and thus
\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}V^{k}(\rho)
exists. If
\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}V^{k}(\rho)<V^{*}(\rho)
, then the result holds automatically. Thus, it suffices to consider the case
\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}V^{k}(\rho)=V^{*}(\rho)
, in which case
\mathcal{L}_{k}^{*}\rightarrow 0
. Thus, there exists
T_{1}(\sigma)
such that
\mathcal{L}_{k}^{*}\leq\frac{\sigma}{C}\left\|\frac{1}{d_{\rho}^{*}}\right\|_{\infty}^{-1}
for
k\geq T_{1}(\sigma)
and

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{k+1}^{*}\geq\mathcal{L}_{k}^{*}-C\left\|\frac{1}{d_{\rho}^{*}}\right\|_{\infty}\left(\mathcal{L}_{k}^{*}\right)^{2}\geq(1-\sigma)\mathcal{L}_{k}^{*}.

Substituting this result into the above inequality gives

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\mathcal{L}_{k+1}^{*}}-\frac{1}{\mathcal{L}_{k}^{*}}\leq C\left\|\frac{1}{d_{\rho}^{*}}\right\|_{\infty}(1-\sigma)^{-1}.

Consequently, for
k\geq T_{1}(\sigma)
,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\mathcal{L}_{k}^{*}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{t=T_{1}(\sigma)}^{k-1}\left(\frac{1}{\mathcal{L}^{*}_{t+1}}-\frac{1}{\mathcal{L}_{t}^{*}}\right)+\frac{1}{\mathcal{L}_{T_{1}(\sigma)}^{*}}

\displaystyle\leq(k-T_{1}(\sigma))C\left\|\frac{1}{d_{\rho}^{*}}\right\|_{\infty}(1-\sigma)^{-1}+\frac{1}{\mathcal{L}_{T_{1}(\sigma)}^{*}}

\displaystyle\leq k\,C\left\|\frac{1}{d_{\rho}^{*}}\right\|_{\infty}(1-\sigma)^{-1}+\frac{1}{\mathcal{L}_{T_{1}(\sigma)}^{*}}

As
\frac{1}{\mathcal{L}_{T_{1}(\sigma)}^{*}}
is fixed, there exists a
T(\sigma)\geq T_{1}(\sigma)
such that
\frac{1}{\mathcal{L}_{T_{1}(\sigma)}^{*}}\leq k\,C\left\|\frac{1}{d_{\rho}^{*}}\right\|_{\infty}(1-\sigma)^{-1}
for
k\geq T(\sigma)
. Therefore, in this range,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\mathcal{L}_{k}^{*}}\leq 2\,k\,C\left\|\frac{1}{d_{\rho}^{*}}\right\|_{\infty}(1-\sigma)^{-1},

as claimed.
∎

2.3
Global Linear Convergence Analysis

In this subsection, we present the general conditions for the global linear convergence of a RL algorithm in terms of the weighted state value error as well as in terms of infinite norm.

Theorem 2.11

(Linear Convergence under Weighted State Value Error)
.

Let
\{\pi^{k}\}_{k\geq 0}
be a policy sequence which satisfies
\mathcal{T}^{k+1}V^{k}\geq V^{k}
for all
k

(
or equivalently,
\sum_{a}\pi^{k+1}(a|s)A^{k}(s,a)\geq 0,\,\,\forall\,s
)
.
Assume that

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{k}^{k+1}\geq C_{k}\,\mathcal{L}_{k}^{*},

where
\mathcal{L}_{k}^{k+1}
and
\mathcal{L}_{k}^{*}
are defined under certain optimal policy
\pi^{k,*}
.
One has

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{k+1}^{*}\leq\left(1-(1-\gamma)\left\|\frac{d_{\rho}^{\pi^{k,*}}}{\rho}\right\|_{\infty}^{-1}C_{k}\right)\mathcal{L}_{k}^{*}.

Proof.

First note that
\mathcal{L}_{k}^{*}-\mathcal{L}_{k+1}^{*}=V^{k+1}(\rho)-V^{k}(\rho)
. By Lemma
2.1
, one has

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{k}^{*}-\mathcal{L}_{k+1}^{*}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-\gamma}\sum_{s}d_{\rho}^{k+1}(s)\sum_{a}\pi^{k+1}(a|s)A^{k}(s,a)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-\gamma}\sum_{s}\frac{d_{\rho}^{k+1}(s)}{d_{\rho}^{\pi^{k,*}}(s)}d_{\rho}^{\pi^{k,*}}(s)\sum_{a}\pi^{k+1}(a|s)A^{k}(s,a)

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{s}\frac{\rho(s)}{d_{\rho}^{\pi^{k,*}}(s)}d_{\rho}^{\pi^{k,*}}(s)\sum_{a}\pi^{k+1}(a|s)A^{k}(s,a)

\displaystyle\geq(1-\gamma)\left\|\frac{d_{\rho}^{\pi^{k,*}}}{\rho}\right\|_{\infty}^{-1}\mathcal{L}_{k}^{k+1}

\displaystyle\geq(1-\gamma)\left\|\frac{d_{\rho}^{\pi^{k,*}}}{\rho}\right\|_{\infty}^{-1}C_{k}\mathcal{L}_{k}^{*},

which completes the proof after rearrangement. Note that the first inequality requires
\sum_{a}\pi^{k+1}(a|s)A^{k}(s,a)\geq 0,\,\forall s
.
∎

Theorem 2.12

(Linear Convergence under Infinite Norm: I)
.

Assume for some
C_{k}\in(0,1)
,

\displaystyle\forall\,s:\quad\sum_{a}\pi^{k+1}_{s,a}A^{k}_{s,a}\geq C_{k}\,\max_{a}A^{k}_{s,a}.

Then,

\displaystyle\|V^{*}-V^{k+1}\|_{\infty}\leq\left(1-(1-\gamma)\,C_{k}\right)\|V^{*}-V^{k}\|_{\infty}.

Proof.

First the assumption can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}^{k+1}V^{k}(s)-V^{k}(s)\geq C_{k}\left(\mathcal{T}V^{k}(s)-V^{k}(s)\right).

It follows that
\forall s
,

\displaystyle V^{*}(s)-V^{k+1}(s)

\displaystyle=V^{*}(s)-\mathcal{T}^{k+1}V^{k+1}(s)

\displaystyle\leq V^{*}(s)-\mathcal{T}^{k+1}V^{k}(s)

\displaystyle\leq V^{*}(s)-V^{k}(s)-C_{k}\left(\mathcal{T}V^{k}(s)-V^{k}(s)\right)

\displaystyle=C_{k}\left(\mathcal{T}V^{*}(s)-\mathcal{T}V^{k}(s)\right)+(1-C_{k})\left(V^{*}(s)-V^{k}(s)\right),

where the second line follows from the fact
V^{k+1}\geq V^{k}
since
\forall\,s

\displaystyle V^{k+1}(s)-V^{k}(s)=\sum_{a}\pi^{k+1}_{s,a}A^{k}_{s,a}\geq 0.

Noticing that
V^{*}(s)-V^{k}(s)\geq 0,\,\forall s
, the application of the contraction property of Bellman optimality operator yields that

\displaystyle\|V^{*}-V^{k+1}\|_{\infty}\leq\left(1-(1-\gamma)\,C_{k}\right)\|V^{*}-V^{k}\|_{\infty},

which completes the proof.
∎

A slightly different form of Theorem
2.12
is given below, which can be proved in a similar manner. The proof details are omitted.

Theorem 2.13

(Linear Convergence under Infinite Norm: II)
.

Let
\{\pi^{k}\}_{k\geq 0}
be a policy sequence which satisfies
\mathcal{T}^{k+1}V^{k}\geq V^{k}
for all
k
(or equivalently,
\sum_{a}\pi^{k+1}(a|s)A^{k}(s,a)\geq 0,\,\,\forall\,s
. Assume for some
C_{k}\in(0,1)
,

\displaystyle\forall\,s:\quad\sum_{a}\pi^{k+1}_{s,a}A^{k}_{s,a}\geq C_{k}\sum_{a}\pi^{k,*}_{s,a}A^{k}_{s,a},

where
\pi^{k,*}
is an optimal policy which may vary from iteration to iteration. Then,

\displaystyle\|V^{*}-V^{k+1}\|_{\infty}\leq\left(1-(1-\gamma)\,C_{k}\right)\|V^{*}-V^{k}\|_{\infty}.

Theorem 2.14

(Linear Convergence under Infinite Norm by Controlling Error Terms)
.

Let
\{\pi^{k}\}_{k\geq 0}
be a policy sequence which satisfies
\mathcal{T}^{k+1}V^{k}\geq V^{k}
for all
k

(
or equivalently,
\sum_{a}\pi^{k+1}(a|s)A^{k}(s,a)\geq 0,\,\,\forall\,s
)
.
Assume there exists a constant
c_{0}>0
such that

\displaystyle\forall\,s:\quad\sum_{a}\pi^{k+1}_{s,a}A^{k}_{s,a}\geq C\,\max_{a}A^{k}_{s,a}-\varepsilon_{k}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\sum_{t=0}^{k-1}\frac{\varepsilon_{t}}{(1-(1-\gamma)C)^{t+1}}\leq c_{0}.

(2.7)

Then

\displaystyle\|V^{*}-V^{k}\|_{\infty}\leq(1-(1-\gamma)C)^{k}\left(\|V^{*}-V^{0}\|_{\infty}+c_{0}\right).

Repeating the proof of Theorem

\displaystyle\|V^{*}-V^{k+1}\|_{\infty}\leq\left(1-(1-\gamma)C\right)\|V^{*}-V^{k}\|_{\infty}+\varepsilon_{k}.

\displaystyle=\left(1-(1-\gamma)C\right)^{k}\left(\|V^{*}-V^{0}\|_{\infty}+\sum_{t=0}^{k-1}\frac{\varepsilon_{t}}{(1-(1-\gamma)C)^{t+1}}\right),

which completes the proof.
∎

3
Projected Policy Gradient

Under the simplex (or direct) parameterization,

\pi=\left\{\left(\pi_{s}\right)_{s\in\mathcal{S}}\,\,|\,\,\pi_{s}\in\Delta\left(\mathcal{A}\right)\right\},

the policy gradient is given by
[
27
]

\displaystyle\nabla_{\pi_{s}}V^{\pi}\left(\mu\right)=\frac{d_{\mu}^{\pi}\left(s\right)}{1-\gamma}Q^{\pi}(s,\cdot).

Thus, the update of the projection policy gradient (PPG) under the simplex parameterization is given by

\displaystyle\pi^{k+1}_{s}

\displaystyle=\argmax_{p\in\Delta}\left\{\eta_{k}\left\langle\frac{d_{\mu}^{k}(s)}{1-\gamma}Q^{\pi}\left(s,\cdot\right),p-\pi_{s}^{k}\right\rangle-\frac{1}{2}\|p-\pi_{s}^{k}\|_{2}^{2}\right\}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Proj}_{\Delta(\mathcal{A})}\left(\pi_{s}^{k}+\eta_{s}^{k}\,Q^{k}(s,\cdot)\right),

where
\eta_{s}^{k}=\frac{\eta_{k}}{1-\gamma}d_{\mu}^{k}(s)
, and
\mathrm{Proj}_{\Delta(\mathcal{A})}\left(\cdot\right)
denotes the projection onto the probability simplex.

As already mentioned in the introduction, the
O(1/\sqrt{k})
sublinear convergence of PPG with constant step size has been
established in
[
1
,
4
]
while this rate has been improved to
O(1/k)
in
[
29
,
32
]
. The analyses in those works are overall conducted under the optimization framework and relies particularly on the smoothness constant of the state value function. Thus, the sublinear convergence can only be established for small step sizes therein. However, as
\eta\rightarrow\infty
, it is easy to see that PPG tends to be the PI method. Since PI always converges, it is natural to anticipate PPG also converges for large step sizes. Motivated by this observation, the
O(1/k)
sublinear convergence of PPG under any constant step size has been investigated and established in
[
15
]
. To overcome the step size barrier hidden the classical optimization analysis framework, an elementary analysis route has been adopted. The overall idea in
[
15
]
is to measure how large the policy improvement of PPG in each state is compared with the policy improved of PI, and establish the bound of the following form:

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}^{k+1}V^{k}(s)-V^{k}(s)\geq f\left(\mathcal{T}V^{k}(s)-V^{k}(s)\right),

where we note that the policy improvement of PI is given by
\mathcal{T}V^{k}(s)-V^{k}(s)
. More precisely, the following result (which expresses
\mathcal{T}^{k+1}V^{k}(s)-V^{k}(s)
and
\mathcal{T}V^{k}(s)-V^{k}(s)
explicitly) has been established.

Lemma 3.1

(Improvement Lower Bound
[
15
, Theorem 3.2]
)
.

Let
\eta_{k}>0
be step size in the
k
-th iteration of PPG. Then,

\displaystyle\sum_{a}\pi^{k+1}_{s,a}A^{k}_{s,a}\geq\frac{\left(\max_{a}A^{k}_{s,a}\right)^{2}}{\max_{a}A^{k}_{s,a}+\frac{2+5|\mathcal{A}|}{\eta_{s}^{k}}}.

The establishment of this lemma relies essentially on the explicit formula for the projection:

\displaystyle\mathrm{Proj}_{\Delta(\mathcal{A})}(y)=(y+\lambda\cdot\bm{1})_{+},

where
\lambda
is a constant such that
\sum_{a}(y_{a}+\lambda)_{+}=1.

Given the above lemma, recalling the definitions of
\mathcal{L}_{k}^{k+1}
and
\mathcal{L}_{k}^{*}
in (
2.3
) and (
2.4
), it is not hard to obtain that

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{k}^{k+1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-\gamma}\mathbb{E}_{s\sim d_{\rho}^{*}}\left[\sum_{a}\pi^{k+1}_{s,a}A^{k}_{s,a}\right]

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{1-\gamma}\mathbb{E}_{s\sim d_{\rho}^{*}}\left[\frac{\left(\max_{a}A^{k}_{s,a}\right)^{2}}{\max_{a}A^{k}_{s,a}+\frac{2+5|\mathcal{A}|}{\eta^{k}_{s}}}\right]

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{1-\gamma}\frac{\left(\mathbb{E}_{s\sim d_{\rho}^{*}}\left[\max_{a}A^{k}_{s,a}\right]\right)^{2}}{\mathbb{E}_{s\sim d_{\rho}^{*}}\left[\max_{a}A^{k}_{s,a}\right]+\frac{2+5|\mathcal{A}|}{\eta_{k}\,\tilde{\mu}}}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{(1-\gamma)(\mathcal{L}_{k}^{*})^{2}}{(1-\gamma)\mathcal{L}_{k}^{*}+C_{1}(\eta_{k})}

(3.1)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{(1-\gamma)(\mathcal{L}_{k}^{*})^{2}}{1+C_{1}(\eta_{k})},

where
C_{1}(\eta_{k}):=\frac{2+5|\mathcal{A}|}{\eta_{k}\,\tilde{\mu}}
. In the above derivation, the fact
\eta_{s}^{k}\geq\eta_{k}\,\tilde{\mu}
and the Jensen inequality are used in the third line, the monotonicity of the function and the fact
\mathbb{E}_{s\sim d_{\rho}^{*}}\left[\max_{a}A^{k}_{s,a}\right]\geq(1-\gamma)\mathcal{L}_{k}^{*}
are used in the fourth line, and the last line follows from
0\leq\mathcal{L}_{k}^{*}=V^{*}(\rho)-V^{k}(\rho)\leq\frac{1}{1-\gamma}
. As already mentioned in Remark
2.1
, letting
\eta_{k}=\eta
and invoking Theorem
2.8
yields the
O(1/k)
sublinear convergence of PPG.

In addition to the sublinear convergence, it is also shown in
[
15
]
that PPG terminates in a finite number of iterations based on the following property.

Lemma 3.2

(
[
15
, Lemma 4.2]
)
.

Consider PPG with constant step size
\eta_{k}=\eta

(
in this case
\eta_{s}^{k}
is simplified to
\eta_{s}
)
.
If the state value of
\pi^{k}
satisfies

\displaystyle\|V^{*}-V^{k}\|_{\infty}\leq\frac{\Delta}{2}\frac{\eta_{s}\Delta}{1+\eta_{s}\Delta},

then
\pi^{k+1}
is an optimal policy.

In this section we first extend the analysis in
[
15
]
to further show that PPG with a constant step size indeed displays a linear convergence before termination.

Theorem 3.3

(Linear Convergence for any Constant Step Size)
.

Consider PPG with any constant step size
\eta>0
. One has

\displaystyle V^{*}(\rho)-V^{k}(\rho)\leq\left(1-(1-\gamma)\left\|\frac{d_{\rho}^{*}}{\rho}\right\|_{\infty}^{-1}\cdot\frac{(1-\gamma)C_{2}\left(\eta\right)}{(1-\gamma)C_{2}\left(\eta\right)+C_{1}(\eta)}\right)^{k}\left(V^{*}(\rho)-V^{0}(\rho)\right),

where
C_{1}\left(\eta\right):=\frac{2+5\left|\mathcal{A}\right|}{\eta\tilde{\mu}}
is defined as above and
C_{2}\left(\eta\right):=\frac{\tilde{\rho}\,\Delta}{2}\frac{\eta\,\tilde{\mu}\,\Delta}{1+\eta\,\tilde{\mu}\,\Delta}
.

Proof.

Let
T
be the iteration at which PPG terminates with the optimal policy (i.e.,
\pi^{T}
is an output optimal policy). Then by Lemma
3.2
,

\displaystyle\|V^{*}-V^{k}\|_{\infty}>\frac{\Delta}{2}\frac{\eta_{s}\Delta}{1+\eta_{s}\Delta},\quad\mbox{if }k\leq T-2.

It follows that
\forall\,k\leq T-2
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{k}^{*}

\displaystyle=V^{*}(\rho)-V^{k}(\rho)\geq\tilde{\rho}\,\|V^{*}-V^{k}\|_{\infty}>\frac{\Delta}{2}\frac{\eta_{s}\Delta}{1+\eta_{s}\Delta}\tilde{\rho}\geq\frac{\Delta}{2}\frac{\eta\,\tilde{\mu}\,\Delta}{1+\eta\,\tilde{\mu}\,\Delta}\tilde{\rho}:=C_{2}(\eta),

where the last inequality follows from
\eta_{s}\geq\eta\,\tilde{\mu}
. Combining it with (
3.1
) gives

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{k}^{k+1}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{(1-\gamma)(\mathcal{L}_{k}^{*})^{2}}{(1-\gamma)\mathcal{L}_{k}^{*}+C_{1}(\eta)}

\displaystyle=\left(1-\frac{C_{1}(\eta)}{(1-\gamma)\mathcal{L}_{k}^{*}+C_{1}(\eta)}\right)\cdot\mathcal{L}_{k}^{*}

\displaystyle\geq\left(1-\frac{C_{1}(\eta)}{(1-\gamma)C_{2}\left(\eta\right)+C_{1}(\eta)}\right)\cdot\mathcal{L}_{k}^{*}

\displaystyle=\frac{(1-\gamma)C_{2}\left(\eta\right)}{(1-\gamma)C_{2}\left(\eta\right)+C_{1}(\eta)}\cdot\mathcal{L}_{k}^{*}.

Then the claim follows immediately from Theorem
2.11
.
∎

Remark 3.1
.

Considering the case
\eta\rightarrow\infty
, one has

C_{1}\left(\eta\right)\rightarrow 0\qquad\mathrm{and}\qquad C_{2}\left(\eta\right)\rightarrow\frac{\tilde{\rho}\,\Delta}{2}.

Then the linear convergence rate of PPG becomes

1-(1-\gamma)\left\|\frac{d_{\rho}^{*}}{\rho}\right\|_{\infty}^{-1}
,
which matches the convergence rate of PI provided
\left\|\frac{d_{\rho}^{*}}{\rho}\right\|_{\infty}^{-1}=1

(
for example when
\rho
is the stationary distribution under the optimal policy
)
. Indeed,
1-(1-\gamma)\left\|\frac{d_{\rho}^{*}}{\rho}\right\|_{\infty}^{-1}
is the rate that can be obtained if we analyse the convergence of PI based on the performance difference lemma instead of the
\gamma
-contraction of the Bellman optimality operator.

Next, we present a non-adaptive increasing step size selection rule for PPG, with improved linear convergence rate.

Theorem 3.4

(Linear Convergence for Non-Adaptive Increasing Step Size)
.

Letting
C_{3}>0
be any constant, assume the step size of PPG satisfies

\displaystyle\eta_{k}\geq\frac{2+5|\mathcal{A}|}{\tilde{\mu}}\frac{1-\gamma}{C_{3}}\left(1+\frac{1-\gamma}{C_{3}}\right)^{k+1}.

Then one has

\displaystyle V^{*}(\rho)-V^{k}(\rho)\leq\frac{1}{1-\gamma}\left(1-(1-\gamma)\left\|\frac{d_{\rho}^{*}}{\rho}\right\|_{\infty}^{-1}\frac{(1-\gamma)}{1-\gamma+C_{3}}\right)^{k}.

Proof.

The proof is by induction. It is obvious that the result holds for
k=0
. Assume it is true for
k\leq t
. First, the bound on
\eta_{t}
implies that

\displaystyle C_{1}(\eta_{t})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C_{3}}{1-\gamma}\left(1-\frac{(1-\gamma)}{1-\gamma+C_{3}}\right)^{t+1}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C_{3}}{1-\gamma}\left(1-(1-\gamma)\left\|\frac{d_{\rho}^{*}}{\rho}\right\|_{\infty}^{-1}\frac{(1-\gamma)}{1-\gamma+C_{3}}\right)^{t+1}.

Assume

\displaystyle V^{*}(\rho)-V^{t}(\rho)\geq\frac{1}{1-\gamma}\left(1-(1-\gamma)\left\|\frac{d_{\rho}^{*}}{\rho}\right\|_{\infty}^{-1}\frac{(1-\gamma)}{1-\gamma+C_{3}}\right)^{t+1}.

Otherwise, the result holds automatically by monotonicity. It follows that

\displaystyle C_{1}(\eta_{t})\leq C_{3}\,\mathcal{L}_{t}^{*}.

Inserting this inequality into
(
3.1
)
yields

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{t}^{t+1}\geq\frac{1-\gamma}{1-\gamma+C_{3}}\mathcal{L}_{t}^{*}.

Then the application of Theorem
2.11
implies that

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{t+1}^{*}\leq\left(1-(1-\gamma)\left\|\frac{d_{\rho}^{*}}{\rho}\right\|_{\infty}^{-1}\frac{1-\gamma}{1-\gamma+C_{3}}\right)\mathcal{L}_{t}^{*},

which completes the proof by the induction hypothesis.
∎

Remark 3.2
.

It is shown in
[
3
]
that PPG with exact line search can also achieve the linear convergence. The analysis therein simply uses the fact that the new value function obtained by exact linear search is at least as large as that for the PI policy. Note that assumption on the exact line search is not realistic.

4
Softmax Policy Gradient

The softmax parameterization is in the form of

\displaystyle\pi_{\theta}(a|s)=\frac{\exp(\theta_{s,a})}{\sum_{a^{\prime}\in\mathcal{A}}\exp(\theta_{s,a^{\prime}})},\quad\mbox{where }\theta=(\theta_{s,a})\in\mathbb{R}^{|\mathcal{S}|\times|\mathcal{A}|}.

Under this parameterization, by the chain rule, it is easy to see that the policy gradient with respect to
\theta
is given by

\displaystyle\frac{\partial V^{\pi_{\theta}}(\mu)}{\partial\theta_{s,a}}=\frac{1}{1-\gamma}d^{\pi_{\theta}}_{\mu}(s)\,\pi_{\theta}(a|s)A^{\pi_{\theta}}(s,a).

(4.1)

Thus, the update of softmax PG in the parameter space is given by

\displaystyle\theta_{s,a}^{k+1}=\theta^{k}_{s,a}+\eta_{s}^{k}\,\pi^{k}_{s,a}A^{k}_{s,a},

where
\eta_{s}^{k}=\frac{\eta_{k}}{1-\gamma}d_{\mu}^{k}(s)
.
In the policy domain, the update can be written as

\displaystyle\pi^{k+1}_{s,a}=\frac{\pi^{k}_{s,a}\exp\left(\eta_{s}^{k}\pi^{k}_{s,a}A^{k}_{s,a}\right)}{Z_{s}^{k}},

(4.2)

where
Z_{s}^{k}=\sum_{a^{\prime}}\pi_{s,a^{\prime}}^{k}\exp\left(\eta_{s}^{k}\,\pi^{k}_{s,a^{\prime}}A^{k}_{s,a^{\prime}}\right)
is the normalization factor.
For ease of notation, we will let
\hat{A}^{k}_{s,a}=\pi^{k}_{s,a}A^{k}_{s,a}
in this section. It is evident that

\displaystyle\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\hat{A}^{k}_{s,a}=0.

The
O(1/k)
sublinear convergence has been established for softmax PG with constant step size in
[
18
]
. The analysis therein is essentially based on the smoothness and the gradient domination property of the value function, and thus requires the step size to be sufficiently small. In addition, an adaptive step size based on the norm of the gradient has been proposed in
[
16
]
, with which softmax PG can achieve linear convergence.
In this section, we will establish the
O(1/k)
sublinear convergence of softmax PG to any constant step size. Additionally, a new adaptive step size selection rule will also be introduced.

In contrast to the optimization analysis framework adopted in
[
18
]
, the idea of analysis here is more elementary and similar to the one used in Section
3
. That being said, the extension is by no means trivial since the update of softmax PG is substantially different with that of PPG. In particular, we will establish the improvement lower bound of
\mathcal{T}^{k+1}V^{k}(s)-V^{k}(s)
for softmax PG in terms of a function of
\max_{a}|\hat{A}^{k}_{s,a}|
in contrast to
\max_{a}A^{k}_{s,a}
due to the existence of
\pi_{s,a}^{k}
in the exponent in (
4.2
). Moreover, as noted after Lemma
3.1
, the establishment of the improvement lower bound in terms of
\max_{a}A^{k}_{s,a}
for PPG relies heavily on the explicit formula for the projection which is not applicable for softmax PG. Instead, a complete different route is adopted to establish the improvement lower bound for softmax PG. Moreover, the improvement upper bound is also established which enables us to show that the
O(1/k)
sublinear convergence rate is tight for softmax PG with any constant step size.

4.1
Improvement Lower and Upper Bounds

We begin with a novel identity that plays an important role in the establishment of the improvement lower and upper bounds.

Lemma 4.1

(Improvement Identity)
.

For any state
s
, the improvement of softmax PG with step size
\eta_{k}
in the
k
-th iteration is equal to

\displaystyle\sum_{a}\pi^{k+1}_{s,a}A^{k}_{s,a}=\frac{1}{2|\mathcal{A}|Z_{s}^{k}}\sum_{a,a^{\prime}}\left(\hat{A}^{k}_{s,a}-\hat{A}^{k}_{s,a^{\prime}}\right)\left(\exp\left(\eta_{s}^{k}\,\hat{A}^{k}_{s,a}\right)-\exp\left(\eta_{s}^{k}\,\hat{A}^{k}_{s,a^{\prime}}\right)\right).

(4.3)

Proof.

The identity follows from a direct computation:

\displaystyle\sum_{a}\pi^{k+1}_{s,a}A^{k}_{s,a}

\displaystyle=\sum_{a}\frac{\pi^{k+1}_{s,a}}{\pi^{k}_{s,a}}\hat{A}^{k}_{s,a}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{Z_{s}^{k}}\sum_{a}\hat{A}^{k}_{s,a}\exp\left(\eta_{s}^{k}\,\hat{A}^{k}_{s,a}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{|\mathcal{A}|}{Z_{s}^{k}}\sum_{a}\frac{1}{|\mathcal{A}|}\hat{A}^{k}_{s,a}\exp\left(\eta_{s}^{k}\,\hat{A}^{k}_{s,a}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{|\mathcal{A}|}{Z_{s}^{k}}\mathbb{E}_{a\sim U}\left[\hat{A}^{k}_{s,a}\exp\left(\eta_{s}^{k}\,\hat{A}^{k}_{s,a}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{|\mathcal{A}|}{Z_{s}^{k}}\mathrm{Cov}_{a\sim U}\left(\hat{A}^{k}_{s,a},\;\exp\left(\eta_{s}^{k}\,\hat{A}^{k}_{s,a}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{|\mathcal{A}|}{2\,Z_{s}^{k}}\mathbb{E}_{a\sim U,a^{\prime}\sim U}\left[\left(\hat{A}^{k}_{s,a}-\hat{A}^{k}_{s,a^{\prime}}\right)\left(\exp\left(\eta_{s}^{k}\,\hat{A}^{k}_{s,a}\right)-\exp\left(\eta_{s}^{k}\,\hat{A}^{k}_{s,a^{\prime}}\right)\right)\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2|\mathcal{A}|Z_{s}^{k}}\sum_{a,a^{\prime}}\left(\hat{A}^{k}_{s,a}-\hat{A}^{k}_{s,a^{\prime}}\right)\left(\exp\left(\eta_{s}^{k}\,\hat{A}^{k}_{s,a}\right)-\exp\left(\eta_{s}^{k}\,\hat{A}^{k}_{s,a^{\prime}}\right)\right),

where
U
represents the uniform distribution on
{\cal A}
, the fifth equality follows from
\sum_{a}\hat{A}^{k}_{s,a}=0
, and the sixth equality follows from Lemma
2.5
.
∎

Lemma 4.2

(Improvement Lower Bound)
.

For any state
s\in\mathcal{S}
, the improvement of softmax PG with step size
\eta_{k}
in the
k
-th iteration has the following lower bound:

\displaystyle\sum_{a}\pi^{k+1}_{s,a}A^{k}_{s,a}\geq\frac{1}{|\mathcal{A}|}\left(\max_{a}|\hat{A}^{k}_{s,a}|\right)\left(1-\exp\left(-\eta_{k}\,\tilde{\mu}\max_{a}|\hat{A}^{k}_{s,a}|\right)\right).

Proof.

Noting that the term

\displaystyle\left(\hat{A}^{k}_{s,a}-\hat{A}^{k}_{s,a^{\prime}}\right)\left(\exp\left(\eta_{s}^{k}\,\hat{A}^{k}_{s,a}\right)-\exp\left(\eta_{s}^{k}\,\hat{A}^{k}_{s,a^{\prime}}\right)\right)

in the identity (
4.3
) is always non-negative, one has

\displaystyle\sum_{a}\pi^{k+1}_{s,a}A^{k}_{s,a}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2|\mathcal{A}|Z_{s}^{k}}\sum_{a,a^{\prime}}\left(\hat{A}^{k}_{s,a}-\hat{A}^{k}_{s,a^{\prime}}\right)\left(\exp\left(\eta_{s}^{k}\,\hat{A}^{k}_{s,a}\right)-\exp\left(\eta_{s}^{k}\,\hat{A}^{k}_{s,a^{\prime}}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{|\mathcal{A}|Z_{s}^{k}}\left(\max_{a}\hat{A}^{k}_{s,a}-\min_{a}\hat{A}^{k}_{s,a}\right)\left(\exp\left(\eta_{s}^{k}\,\max_{a}\hat{A}^{k}_{s,a}\right)-\exp\left(\eta_{s}^{k}\,\min_{a}\hat{A}^{k}_{s,a}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{|\mathcal{A}|}\left(\max_{a}\hat{A}^{k}_{s,a}-\min_{a}\hat{A}^{k}_{s,a}\right)\left(1-\exp\left(-\eta_{s}^{k}\left(\max_{a}\hat{A}^{k}_{s,a}-\min_{a}\hat{A}^{k}_{s,a}\right)\right)\right)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{|\mathcal{A}|}\left(\max_{a}\hat{A}^{k}_{s,a}-\min_{a}\hat{A}^{k}_{s,a}\right)\left(1-\exp\left(-\eta_{k}\,\tilde{\mu}\left(\max_{a}\hat{A}^{k}_{s,a}-\min_{a}\hat{A}^{k}_{s,a}\right)\right)\right)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{|\mathcal{A}|}\max_{a}|\hat{A}^{k}_{s,a}|\left(1-\exp\left(-\eta_{k}\,\tilde{\mu}\max_{a}|\hat{A}^{k}_{s,a}|\right)\right).

Here, the third line follows from

\displaystyle Z_{s}^{k}=\sum_{a}\pi_{s,a}^{k}\exp\left(\eta_{s}^{k}\,\hat{A}^{k}_{s,a}\right)\leq\exp\left(\eta_{s}^{k}\max_{a}\hat{A}^{k}_{s,a}\right),

the fourth line follows from
\eta_{s}^{k}\geq\eta_{k}\,\tilde{\mu}
, the fifth line follows from
f(x)=x(1-\exp(-cx))
(
c>0
) is non-negative, monotonically increasing on
[0,\infty)
and

\displaystyle\max_{a}\hat{A}^{k}_{s,a}-\min_{a}\hat{A}^{k}_{s,a}\geq\max_{a}|\hat{A}^{k}_{s,a}|

since
\sum_{a}\hat{A}^{k}_{s,a}=0
.
∎

Lemma 4.3

(Improvement Upper Bound)
.

For any state
s\in\mathcal{S}
, the improvement of softmax PG with step size
\eta_{k}
in the
k
-th iteration has the following upper bound:

\displaystyle\sum_{a}\pi^{k+1}_{s,a}A^{k}_{s,a}\leq\left(\exp\left(\frac{2\eta_{k}}{(1-\gamma)^{2}}\right)-1\right)|\mathcal{A}|(1-\gamma)\left(\max_{a}|\hat{A}^{k}_{s,a}|\right)^{2}.

Proof.

The proof also begins with the identity (
4.3
) as follows:

\displaystyle\sum_{a}\pi^{k+1}_{s,a}A^{k}_{s,a}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2|\mathcal{A}|Z_{s}^{k}}\sum_{a,a^{\prime}}\left(\hat{A}^{k}_{s,a}-\hat{A}^{k}_{s,a^{\prime}}\right)\left(\exp\left(\eta_{s}^{k}\,\hat{A}^{k}_{s,a}\right)-\exp\left(\eta_{s}^{k}\,\hat{A}^{k}_{s,a^{\prime}}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{|\mathcal{A}|}{2Z_{s}^{k}}\left(\max_{a}\hat{A}^{k}_{s,a}-\min_{a}\hat{A}^{k}_{s,a}\right)\left(\exp\left(\eta_{s}^{k}\,\max_{a}\hat{A}^{k}_{s,a}\right)-\exp\left(\eta_{s}^{k}\,\min_{a}\hat{A}^{k}_{s,a}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{|\mathcal{A}|}{2}\left(\max_{a}\hat{A}^{k}_{s,a}-\min_{a}\hat{A}^{k}_{s,a}\right)\left(\exp\left(\eta_{s}^{k}\left(\max_{a}\hat{A}^{k}_{s,a}-\min_{a}\hat{A}^{k}_{s,a}\right)\right)-1\right)

\displaystyle\leq|\mathcal{A}|\max_{a}|\hat{A}^{k}_{s,a}|\left(\exp\left(2\,\eta_{s}^{k}\max_{a}|\hat{A}^{k}_{s,a}|\right)-1\right)

\displaystyle\leq|\mathcal{A}|\max_{a}|\hat{A}^{k}_{s,a}|\left(\exp\left(\frac{2\,\eta_{k}}{1-\gamma}\max_{a}|\hat{A}^{k}_{s,a}|\right)-1\right),

where the third line follows from

\displaystyle Z_{s}^{k}=\sum_{a}\pi_{s,a}^{k}\exp\left(\eta_{s}^{k}\,\hat{A}^{k}_{s,a}\right)\geq\exp\left(\eta_{s}^{k}\min_{a}\hat{A}^{k}_{s,a}\right).

Noticing that
\max_{a}|\hat{A}^{k}_{s,a}|\leq\frac{1}{1-\gamma}
, together with the fact

\displaystyle\exp\left(\frac{2\eta_{k}}{1-\gamma}x\right)-1\leq\left(\exp\left(\frac{2\eta_{k}}{(1-\gamma)^{2}}\right)-1\right)(1-\gamma)\,x,\quad\mbox{for }x\in\left[0,\frac{1}{1-\gamma}\right],

one further has

\displaystyle\sum_{a}\pi^{k+1}_{s,a}A^{k}_{s,a}\leq\left(\exp\left(\frac{2\,\eta_{k}}{(1-\gamma)^{2}}\right)-1\right)(1-\gamma)|\mathcal{A}|\left(\max_{a}|\hat{A}^{k}_{s,a}|\right)^{2}.

The proof is now complete.
∎

4.2
Convergence Results

We first present the global convergence of softmax PG for any step size sequence
\eta_{k}
as long as
\inf_{k}\eta_{k}\geq\alpha
for some
\alpha>0
. The proof overall follows the route in
[
1
]
and is presented in Appendix
B
where we only point out how to generalize the proof in
[
1
]
to any step size sequence.

Theorem 4.4

(Global Convergence)
.

Consider softmax PG with step size
\eta_{k}
in the
k
-th iteration. Assume
\inf_{k}\eta_{k}\geq\alpha
for some
\alpha>0
. Then the produced state values converge to the optimal one,

\displaystyle\forall\,s\in{\cal S}:\quad\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}V^{k}(s)=V^{*}(s).

Recall that
b_{s}^{k}=\sum_{a\not\in\mathcal{A}_{s}^{*}}\pi^{k}(a|s)
is the probability of
\pi^{k}
on the non-optimal actions. We define

\displaystyle\kappa=\mathop{\mathrm{inf}}\limits_{k\geq 0,s\in\mathcal{S}}\min\left(1-b_{s}^{k}\right).

(4.4)

It follows immediately from the global convergence of softmax PG and the left inequality of Lemma
2.4

that
\kappa>0
. It is worth mentioning that
\kappa
can be very small for softmax PG on certain MDP problems, as demonstrated in
[
13
]
.
In the rest of this section, we present the sublinear convergence rate and the corresponding lower bound for softmax PG with any constant step size, as well as a new adaptive step size that enables softmax PG to converge linearly.

Theorem 4.5

(Sublinear Convergence for any Constant Step Size)
.

For any constant step size
\eta>0
, softmax PG converges sublinearly,

\displaystyle V^{*}(\rho)-V^{k}(\rho)\leq\frac{1}{k}\frac{1}{\tilde{\rho}\,(1-\gamma)^{3}}\frac{|\mathcal{A}|}{\kappa^{2}}\left(\max_{s}|\mathcal{A}_{s}^{*}|\right)^{2}\left(1+\frac{1-\gamma}{\eta\,\tilde{\mu}}\right).

In particular, if the optimal policy is unique, i.e.,
|\mathcal{A}_{s}^{*}|=1
, one has

\displaystyle V^{*}(\rho)-V^{k}(\rho)\leq\frac{1}{k}\frac{1}{(1-\gamma)^{3}}\frac{|\mathcal{A}|}{\kappa^{2}}\left\|\frac{d_{\rho}^{*}}{\rho}\right\|_{\infty}\left(1+\frac{1-\gamma}{\eta\,\tilde{\mu}}\right).

Proof.

By the improvement lower bound, one has

\displaystyle\sum_{a}\pi^{k+1}_{s,a}A^{k}_{s,a}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{|\mathcal{A}|}\left(\max_{a}|\hat{A}^{k}_{s,a}|\right)\left(1-\exp\left(-\eta\,\tilde{\mu}\max_{a}|\hat{A}^{k}_{s,a}|\right)\right)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{|\mathcal{A}|}(1-\gamma)\left(1-\exp\left(-\frac{\eta\,\tilde{\mu}}{1-\gamma}\right)\right)\left(\max_{a}|\hat{A}^{k}_{s,a}|\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{|\mathcal{A}|}(1-\gamma)\left(1-\exp\left(-\frac{\eta\,\tilde{\mu}}{1-\gamma}\right)\right)\left(\frac{1}{|\mathcal{A}_{s}^{*}|}\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}_{s}^{*}}\hat{A}^{k}_{s,a}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{A}|}(1-\gamma)\left(1-\exp\left(-\frac{\eta\,\tilde{\mu}}{1-\gamma}\right)\right)\left(\frac{1-b_{s}^{k}}{|\mathcal{A}_{s}^{*}|}\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}_{s}^{*}}\frac{\pi^{k}_{s,a}}{1-b_{s}^{k}}{A}^{k}_{s,a}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\kappa^{2}}{|\mathcal{A}|}(1-\gamma)\left(1-\exp\left(-\frac{\eta\,\tilde{\mu}}{1-\gamma}\right)\right)\left(\max_{s}|\mathcal{A}_{s}^{*}|\right)^{-2}\left(\sum_{a}\xi^{k}_{s,a}{A}^{k}_{s,a}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle:=C\left(\sum_{a}\xi^{k}_{s,a}{A}^{k}_{s,a}\right)^{2}.

where the second line follows from

\displaystyle 1-\exp\left(-\eta\,\tilde{\mu}\,x\right)\geq(1-\gamma)\left(1-\exp\left(-\frac{\eta\,\tilde{\mu}}{1-\gamma}\right)\right)x,\quad\mbox{for }x\in\left[0,\frac{1}{1-\gamma}\right],

and
\xi^{k}
is defined as

\displaystyle\xi^{k}(a|s)=\begin{cases}\frac{\pi^{k}(a|s)}{1-b_{s}^{k}}&\mbox{if }a\in\mathcal{A}_{s}^{*},\\
0&\mbox{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Noticing that
\xi^{k}
is indeed an optimal policy, under this policy, one has

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{k}^{k+1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-\gamma}\mathbb{E}_{s\sim d_{\rho}^{\xi^{k}}}\left[\sum_{a}\pi^{k+1}_{s,a}A^{k}_{s,a}\right]

\displaystyle\geq\frac{C}{1-\gamma}\mathbb{E}_{s\sim d_{\rho}^{\xi^{k}}}\left[\left(\sum_{a}\xi^{k}_{s,a}{A}^{k}_{s,a}\right)^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\geq\frac{C}{1-\gamma}\left(\mathbb{E}_{s\sim d_{\rho}^{\xi^{k}}}\left[\sum_{a}\xi^{k}_{s,a}{A}^{k}_{s,a}\right]\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=C(1-\gamma)\left(\mathcal{L}_{k}^{*}\right)^{2}.

The proof of the first result is completed by noting that
\exp(-x)\leq 1/(1+x)
for
x\geq 0
and then utilizing Theorem
2.8
. The second result follows immediately by noting that
|\mathcal{A}_{s}^{*}|=1
and
1/\vartheta=\|\frac{d_{\rho}^{*}}{\rho}\|_{\infty}
in Theorem
2.8
.
∎

Remark 4.1
.

By utilizing the smoothness and gradient dominance property of
V^{\pi_{\theta}}(\mu)
,
the following sublinear convergence rate has been established for
\eta<2/L
with
L
being the smoothness constant of
V^{\pi_{\theta}}(\mu)
. Consider the case where the optimal policy is unique. For
\eta=1/L=(1-\gamma)^{3}/8
, the best rate obtained in
[
18
, Theorem 4]
is

\displaystyle V^{*}\left(\rho\right)-V^{k}\left(\rho\right)\leq\frac{1}{k}\frac{1}{\left(1-\gamma\right)^{5}}\frac{16\left|S\right|}{\kappa^{2}}\left\|\frac{d_{\mu}^{*}}{\mu}\right\|_{\infty}^{2}\left\|\frac{\rho}{\mu}\right\|_{\infty}.

In contrast, our result for this particular step size is

\displaystyle V^{*}(\rho)-V^{k}(\rho)\leq\frac{1}{k}\frac{1}{(1-\gamma)^{5}}\frac{16|\mathcal{A}|}{\kappa^{2}}\left\|\frac{d_{\rho}^{*}}{\rho}\right\|_{\infty}\left\|\frac{1}{\mu}\right\|_{\infty},

which does not rely on
|\mathcal{S}|
but on
|\mathcal{A}|
. In addition, if
\eta\geq\frac{1-\gamma}{\tilde{\mu}}
, our result reduces to

\displaystyle V^{*}(\rho)-V^{k}(\rho)\leq\frac{1}{k}\frac{1}{(1-\gamma)^{3}}\frac{2|\mathcal{A}|}{\kappa^{2}}\left\|\frac{d_{\rho}^{*}}{\rho}\right\|_{\infty}\left\|\frac{1}{\mu}\right\|_{\infty},

which has better dependency on
1-\gamma
.

Theorem 4.6

(Sublinear Lower Bound for any Constant Step Size)
.

For any constant step size
\eta>0
and a given constant
\sigma\in(0,1)
, there exists a time
T(\sigma)
such that the state value sequence generated by softmax PG satisfies

\displaystyle\forall\,k\geq T(\sigma):\quad V^{*}(\rho)-V^{k}(\rho)\geq\frac{1}{k}\frac{\tilde{\rho}^{3}(1-\sigma)(1-\gamma)}{2|\mathcal{A}|^{3}}\left\|\frac{1}{d_{\rho}^{*}}\right\|_{\infty}^{-1}\left(\exp\left(\frac{2\eta}{(1-\gamma)^{2}}\right)-1\right)^{-1}.

Proof.

By the improvement upper bound one has

\displaystyle\sum_{a}\pi^{k+1}_{s,a}A^{k}_{s,a}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\exp\left(\frac{2\eta}{(1-\gamma)^{2}}\right)-1\right)|\mathcal{A}|(1-\gamma)\left(\max_{a}|\hat{A}^{k}_{s,a}|\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\exp\left(\frac{2\eta}{(1-\gamma)^{2}}\right)-1\right)|\mathcal{A}|^{3}(1-\gamma)\left(\max_{a}\hat{A}^{k}_{s,a}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\exp\left(\frac{2\eta}{(1-\gamma)^{2}}\right)-1\right)|\mathcal{A}|^{3}(1-\gamma)\left(\max_{a}{A}^{k}_{s,a}\right)^{2},

where the second line follows from
\max_{a}|\hat{A}^{k}_{s,a}|\leq|\mathcal{A}|\max_{a}\hat{A}^{k}_{s,a}
since
\sum_{a}\hat{A}^{k}_{s,a}=0
. It follows that

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{k}^{k+1}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\exp\left(\frac{2\eta}{(1-\gamma)^{2}}\right)-1\right)|\mathcal{A}|^{3}\mathbb{E}_{s\sim d_{\rho}^{*}}\left[\left(\max_{a}{A}^{k}_{s,a}\right)^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{|\mathcal{A}|^{3}}{(1-\gamma)\tilde{\rho}}\left(\exp\left(\frac{2\eta}{(1-\gamma)^{2}}\right)-1\right)\left(\mathbb{E}_{s\sim d_{\rho}^{*}}\left[\max_{a}{A}^{k}_{s,a}\right]\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{|\mathcal{A}|^{3}}{(1-\gamma)\,\tilde{\rho}^{3}}\left(\exp\left(\frac{2\eta}{(1-\gamma)^{2}}\right)-1\right)\left(\mathcal{L}_{k}^{*}\right)^{2},

where the second line follows from

\displaystyle\left(\mathbb{E}_{s\sim d_{\rho}^{*}}\left[\max_{a}A^{k}_{s,a}\right]\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{s}d_{\rho}^{*}(s)\max_{a}A^{k}_{s,a}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{s}(d_{\rho}^{*}(s))^{2}\left(\max_{a}A^{k}_{s,a}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\geq(1-\gamma)\tilde{\rho}\sum_{s}d_{\rho}^{*}(s)\left(\max_{a}A^{k}_{s,a}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=(1-\gamma)\tilde{\rho}\,\mathbb{E}_{s\sim d_{\rho}^{*}}\left[\left(\max_{a}A^{k}_{s,a}\right)^{2}\right].

and the third line follows from Lemma
2.7
.
The proof is then completed by utilizing Theorem
2.10
.
∎

Theorem 4.7

(Linear Convergence for Adaptive Step Size)
.

Let

\displaystyle{\mathcal{S}^{k}}=\left\{s:\,\max_{a}|\hat{A}^{k}_{s,a}|>0\right\}=\left\{s:\,\max_{a}\hat{A}^{k}_{s,a}>0\right\}.

Without loss of generality, assume
\mathcal{S}^{k}\neq\emptyset
. For any constant
C>0
, if the step size
\eta_{k}
satisfies

\displaystyle\eta_{k}\geq\frac{C}{\min_{s\in\mathcal{S}^{k}}\max_{a}|\hat{A}^{k}_{s,a}|},

then softmax PG achieves a linear convergence,

\displaystyle V^{*}(\rho)-V^{k}(\rho)\leq\left(1-(1-\gamma)\tilde{\rho}\frac{\kappa}{|\mathcal{A}|\,\max_{a}|\mathcal{A}_{s}^{*}|}\left(1-\frac{1}{C\,\tilde{\mu}+1}\right)\right)^{k}\left(V^{*}(\rho)-V^{0}(\rho)\right).

In particular, if the policy is unique, then

\displaystyle V^{*}(\rho)-V^{k}(\rho)\leq\left(1-(1-\gamma)\left\|\frac{d_{\rho}^{*}}{\rho}\right\|_{\infty}^{-1}\frac{\kappa}{|\mathcal{A}|}\left(1-\frac{1}{C\,\tilde{\mu}+1}\right)\right)^{k}\left(V^{*}(\rho)-V^{0}(\rho)\right).

Proof.

Recall the improvement lower bound as follows:

\displaystyle\sum_{a}\pi^{k+1}_{s,a}A^{k}_{s,a}\geq\frac{1}{|\mathcal{A}|}\left(\max_{a}|\hat{A}^{k}_{s,a}|\right)\left(1-\exp\left(-\eta_{k}\,\tilde{\mu}\max_{a}|\hat{A}^{k}_{s,a}|\right)\right).

For
s\in\mathcal{S}^{k}
, since
\eta_{k}\geq\frac{C}{\max_{a}|\hat{A}^{k}_{s,a}|}
, one has

\displaystyle\sum_{a}\pi^{k+1}_{s,a}A^{k}_{s,a}\geq\frac{1}{|\mathcal{A}|}\left(\max_{a}|\hat{A}^{k}_{s,a}|\right)\left(1-\exp\left(-C\,\tilde{\mu}\right)\right).

(4.5)

For
s\notin\mathcal{S}^{k}
, one has
\max_{a}|\hat{A}^{k}_{s,a}|=0
. Noting that
\pi^{k}_{s,a}>0
, it follows that
A^{k}_{s,a}=0,\,\forall a
and the above inequality holds automatically. Therefore, for any
s
,

\displaystyle\sum_{a}\pi^{k+1}_{s,a}A^{k}_{s,a}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{|\mathcal{A}|}\left(1-\exp\left(-C\,\tilde{\mu}\right)\right)\left(\max_{a}|\hat{A}^{k}_{s,a}|\right)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{|\mathcal{A}|}\left(1-\exp\left(-C\,\tilde{\mu}\right)\right)\left(\frac{1}{|\mathcal{A}_{s}^{*}|}\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}_{s}^{*}}|\hat{A}^{k}_{s,a}|\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{A}|}\left(1-\exp\left(-C\,\tilde{\mu}\right)\right)\left(\frac{1-b_{s}^{k}}{|\mathcal{A}_{s}^{*}|}\sum_{a}\xi^{k}_{s,a}|{A}^{k}_{s,a}|\right)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\kappa}{|\mathcal{A}|}\left(\max_{s}|\mathcal{A}_{s}^{*}|\right)^{-1}\left(1-\exp\left(-C\,\tilde{\mu}\right)\right)\left(\sum_{a}\xi^{k}_{s,a}|{A}^{k}_{s,a}|\right)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\kappa}{|\mathcal{A}|}\left(\max_{s}|\mathcal{A}_{s}^{*}|\right)^{-1}\left(1-\frac{1}{C\,\tilde{\mu}+1}\right)\left(\sum_{a}\xi^{k}_{s,a}|{A}^{k}_{s,a}|\right)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\kappa}{|\mathcal{A}|}\left(\max_{s}|\mathcal{A}_{s}^{*}|\right)^{-1}\left(1-\frac{1}{C\,\tilde{\mu}+1}\right)\left(\sum_{a}\xi^{k}_{s,a}{A}^{k}_{s,a}\right).

Taking expectation with respect to
d_{\rho}^{\xi^{k}}
yields that

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{k}^{k+1}\geq\frac{\kappa}{|\mathcal{A}|}\left(\max_{s}|\mathcal{A}_{s}^{*}|\right)^{-1}\left(1-\frac{1}{C\,\tilde{\mu}+1}\right)\,\mathcal{L}_{k}^{*}.

The proof is completed by using Theorem
2.11
and noting that
\left\|\frac{d^{\xi^{k}}_{\rho}}{\rho}\right\|^{-1}\geq\tilde{\rho}
.
∎

5
Softmax Natural Policy Gradient

Softmax NPG can be viewed as a preconditioned version of softmax PG in the parameter domain, see for example
[
9
,
1
]
. Here we only present the update of softmax NPG in the policy domain,

\displaystyle\pi^{k+1}_{s,a}

\displaystyle\propto{\pi^{k}_{s,a}\exp(\eta\,A^{k}_{s,a})}

\displaystyle\propto{\pi^{k}_{s,a}\exp(\eta\,Q^{k}_{s,a})}.

It is worth noting that NPG can be viewed as the policy mirror ascent method under the KL-divergence:

\displaystyle\pi_{s}^{k+1}=\argmax_{p\in\Delta(\mathcal{A})}\left\{{\eta}\left(\langle Q^{k}(s,\cdot),p\rangle\right)-\mathrm{KL}(p\|\pi^{k}_{s})\right\}.

Thus, it is easy to see that as
\eta\rightarrow\infty
, NPG is also close to PI.

Let
Z_{s}^{k}=\sum_{a^{\prime}}\pi^{k}_{s,a^{\prime}}\exp(\eta\,A^{k}_{s,a^{\prime}})
be the normalization factor such that

\displaystyle\pi^{k+1}_{s,a}=\frac{\pi^{k}_{s,a}\exp(\eta\,A^{k}_{s,a})}{Z_{s}^{k}},

(5.1)

one has

\displaystyle\log\frac{\pi^{k+1}_{s,a}}{\pi^{k}_{s,a}}=\eta\,A^{k}_{s,a}-\log Z_{s}^{k}.

(5.2)

Taking expectation with respect to
\pi^{k}_{s}
on both sides yields

\displaystyle\log Z_{s}^{k}=\mathrm{KL}(\pi^{k}_{s}\|\pi^{k+1}_{s}),

while taking expectation with respect to
\pi^{k+1}_{s}
yields

\displaystyle\mathrm{KL}(\pi^{k+1}_{s}\|\pi^{k}_{s})=\eta\,\mathbb{E}_{a\sim\pi^{k+1}(\cdot|s)}\left[A^{k}_{s,a}\right]-\mathrm{KL}(\pi^{k}_{s}\|\pi^{k+1}_{s}).

It follows that

\displaystyle\sum_{a}\pi^{k+1}_{s,a}A^{k}_{s,a}=\frac{1}{\eta}\mathrm{KL}(\pi^{k+1}_{s}\|\pi^{k}_{s})+\frac{1}{\eta}\mathrm{KL}(\pi^{k}_{s}\|\pi^{k+1}_{s}).

(5.3)

Moreover, taking expectation with respect to
\pi^{*}_{s}
on both sides of (
5.2
) yields

\displaystyle\mathrm{KL}(\pi_{s}^{*}\|\pi_{s}^{k})-\mathrm{KL}(\pi^{*}_{s}\|\pi_{s}^{k+1})

\displaystyle=\eta\sum_{a}\pi^{*}_{s,a}A^{k}_{s,a}-\log Z_{s}^{k}

\displaystyle=\eta\sum_{a}\pi^{*}_{s,a}A^{k}_{s,a}-\mathrm{KL}(\pi^{k}_{s}\|\pi^{k+1}_{s}).

Combining the above two inequalities together yields

\displaystyle\sum_{a}\pi^{k+1}_{s,a}A^{k}_{s,a}=\sum_{a}\pi^{*}_{s,a}A^{k}_{s,a}+\frac{1}{\eta}\mathrm{KL}(\pi^{*}_{s}\|\pi_{s}^{k+1})-\frac{1}{\eta}\mathrm{KL}(\pi_{s}^{*}\|\pi_{s}^{k})+\frac{1}{\eta}\mathrm{KL}(\pi^{k+1}_{s}\|\pi^{k}_{s}).

(5.4)

Indeed, recalling the definitions of
\mathcal{L}_{k}^{k+1}
and
\mathcal{L}_{k}^{*}
in (
2.3
) and (
2.4
), the above equality immediately implies that

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{k}^{k+1}\geq\mathcal{L}_{k}^{*}-\frac{1}{\eta\,(1-\gamma)}\left(\mathbb{E}_{s\sim d_{\rho}^{*}}\left[\mathrm{KL}(\pi_{s}^{*}\|\pi_{s}^{k})\right]-\mathbb{E}_{s\sim d_{\rho}^{*}}\left[\mathrm{KL}(\pi_{s}^{*}\|\pi_{s}^{k+1})\right]\right).

Together with Theorem
2.9
, the sublinear convergence of softmax NPG can be established, as shown in
[
1
]
.

Theorem 5.1

(
[
1
, Theorem 16]
)
.

For any constant step size
\eta>0
, softmax NPG converges sublinearly,

\displaystyle V^{*}(\rho)-V^{k}(\rho)\leq\frac{1}{k}\left[\frac{1}{(1-\gamma)^{2}}+\frac{\mathrm{KL}(\pi_{s}^{*}\|\pi_{s}^{0})}{\eta\,(1-\gamma)}\right].

In addition to the global sublinear convergence, the local linear convergence has been established in
[
11
]
based on the contraction of the sub-optimal probability
b_{s}^{k}
at each state and Lemma
2.4
. In this section, we are going to show the global linear convergence of softmax NPG. To this end, we need to consider the convergence in two phases, which requires us to establish two improvement lower bounds.

Lemma 5.2

(Improvement Lower Bound I)
.

For softmax NPG with any constant step size
\eta>0
, one has

\displaystyle\sum_{a}\pi^{k+1}_{s,a}A^{k}_{s,a}\geq\left[1-\frac{1}{1+\pi_{s}^{k}(\mathcal{A}_{s}^{k})\left(\exp\left(\eta\,\Delta_{s}^{k}\right)-1\right)}\right]\max_{a}A^{k}_{s,a},

where
\Delta_{s}^{k}=\max_{a}A^{k}_{s,a}-\max_{a^{\prime}\not\in\mathcal{A}_{s}^{k}}A^{k}_{s,a^{\prime}}
,
{\cal A}^{k}_{s}:=\argmax_{a\in{\cal A}}\;A^{k}(s,a)
and
\pi^{k}_{s}(\mathcal{A}^{k}_{s})=\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}_{s}^{k}}\pi^{k}(a|s)
.

Proof.

Define the policy
\xi^{k}_{-}(\cdot|s)
as

\displaystyle\xi^{k}_{-}(a|s)=\begin{cases}0&\mbox{if }a\in\mathcal{A}_{s}^{k},\\
{\pi^{k}(a|s)}/{(1-\pi_{s}^{k}(\mathcal{A}_{s}^{k}))}&\mbox{if }a\not\in\mathcal{A}_{s}^{k}.\end{cases}

Noting that
\sum_{a}\pi^{k}_{s,a}A^{k}_{s,a}=0
, one has

\displaystyle\pi^{k}_{s}(\mathcal{A}^{k}_{s})\max_{a}A^{k}_{s,a}+(1-\pi^{k}_{s}(\mathcal{A}_{s}^{k}))\,\mathbb{E}_{a^{\prime}\sim\xi^{k}_{-}(\cdot|s)}\left[A^{k}_{s,a^{\prime}}\right]=0.

(5.5)

By the definition of
\pi^{k+1}_{s,a}
in the NPG update, one has

\displaystyle\sum_{a}\pi^{k+1}_{s,a}A^{k}_{s,a}

\displaystyle=\sum_{a}\frac{\pi^{k}_{s,a}\exp(\eta\,A^{k}_{s,a})}{Z_{s}^{k}}A^{k}_{s,a}

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathbb{E}_{a\sim\pi^{k}(\cdot|s)}\left[\exp\left(\eta\,A^{k}_{s,a}\right)A^{k}_{s,a}\right]}{\mathbb{E}_{a\sim\pi^{k}(\cdot|s)}\left[\exp\left(\eta\,A^{k}_{s,a}\right)\right]}.

The numerator can be bounded as follows:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{a\sim\pi^{k}(\cdot|s)}\left[\exp\left(\eta\,A^{k}_{s,a}\right)A^{k}_{s,a}\right]

\displaystyle=\pi_{s}^{k}(\mathcal{A}_{s}^{k})\exp\left(\eta\,\max_{a}A^{k}_{s,a}\right)\max_{a}A^{k}_{s,a}+(1-\pi_{s}^{k}(\mathcal{A}_{s}^{k}))\mathbb{E}_{a^{\prime}\sim\xi^{k}_{-}(\cdot|s)}\left[\exp\left(\eta\,A^{k}_{s,a^{\prime}}\right)A^{k}_{s,a^{\prime}}\right]

\displaystyle\geq\pi_{s}^{k}(\mathcal{A}_{s}^{k})\exp\left(\eta\,\max_{a}A^{k}_{s,a}\right)\max_{a}A^{k}_{s,a}

\displaystyle+(1-\pi_{s}^{k}(\mathcal{A}_{s}^{k}))\mathbb{E}_{a^{\prime}\sim\xi^{k}_{-}(\cdot|s)}\left[\exp\left(\eta\,A^{k}_{s,a^{\prime}}\right)\right]\,\mathbb{E}_{a^{\prime}\sim\xi^{k}_{-}(\cdot|s)}\left[A^{k}_{s,a^{\prime}}\right]

\displaystyle=\pi_{s}^{k}(\mathcal{A}_{s}^{k})\left[\exp\left(\eta\,\max_{a}A^{k}_{s,a}\right)-\mathbb{E}_{a^{\prime}\sim\xi^{k}_{-}(\cdot|s)}\left[\exp\left(\eta\,A^{k}_{s,a^{\prime}}\right)\right]\right]\max_{a}A^{k}_{s,a},

where the first inequality is due to Lemma
2.6
and the last equality comes from (
5.5
). The denominator can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{a\sim\pi^{k}(\cdot|s)}\left[\exp\left(\eta\,A^{k}_{s,a}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=\pi_{s}^{k}(\mathcal{A}_{s}^{k})\exp\left(\eta\,\max_{a}A^{k}_{s,a}\right)+(1-\pi_{s}^{k}(\mathcal{A}_{s}^{k}))\mathbb{E}_{a^{\prime}\sim\xi^{k}_{-}(\cdot|s)}\left[\exp\left(\eta\,A^{k}_{s,a^{\prime}}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=\pi_{s}^{k}(\mathcal{A}_{s}^{k})\left[\exp\left(\eta\,\max_{a}A^{k}_{s,a}\right)-\mathbb{E}_{a^{\prime}\sim\xi^{k}_{-}(\cdot|s)}\left[\exp\left(\eta\,A^{k}_{s,a^{\prime}}\right)\right]\right]+\mathbb{E}_{a^{\prime}\sim\xi^{k}_{-}(\cdot|s)}\left[\exp\left(\eta\,A^{k}_{s,a^{\prime}}\right)\right].

Therefore,

\displaystyle\sum_{a}\pi^{k+1}_{s,a}A^{k}_{s,a}

\displaystyle\geq\left[1-\frac{1}{1+\pi_{s}^{k}(\mathcal{A}_{s}^{k})\left(\frac{\exp\left(\eta\,\max_{a}A^{k}_{s,a}\right)}{\mathbb{E}_{a^{\prime}\sim\xi^{k}_{-}(\cdot|s)}\left(\exp\left(\eta\,A^{k}_{s,a^{\prime}}\right)\right)}-1\right)}\right]\max_{a}A^{k}_{s,a}

\displaystyle\geq\left[1-\frac{1}{1+\pi_{s}^{k}(\mathcal{A}_{s}^{k})\left(\exp\left(\eta\,\Delta_{s}^{k}\right)-1\right)}\right]\max_{a}A^{k}_{s,a},

which completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 5.3

(Improvement Lower Bound II)
.

Let
\varepsilon\in(0,\Delta/2)
. If
\|V^{*}-V^{k}\|_{\infty}\leq\varepsilon
, then for softmax NPG with any constant step size
\eta>0
, one has

\displaystyle\sum_{a}\pi^{k+1}_{s,a}A^{k}_{s,a}\geq\left[1-\frac{1}{1+(1-b_{s}^{k})\left(\exp(\eta\,(\Delta-\varepsilon))-1\right)}\right]\sum_{a}\pi^{k,*}_{s,a}A^{k}_{s,a},

where
\pi^{k,*}
is an optimal policy.

Proof.

First define the following two polices based on
\mathcal{A}_{s}^{*}
:

\displaystyle\xi^{k}(a|s)=\begin{cases}\pi^{k}(a|s)/(1-b_{s}^{k})&\mbox{if }a\in\mathcal{A}_{s}^{*},\\
0&\mbox{if }a\not\in\mathcal{A}_{s}^{*},\end{cases}

and

\displaystyle\xi^{k}_{-}(a|s)=\begin{cases}0&\mbox{if }a\in\mathcal{A}_{s}^{*},\\
\pi^{k}(a|s)/b_{s}^{k}&\mbox{if }a\not\in\mathcal{A}_{s}^{*}.\end{cases}

Again, it follows from
\sum_{a}\pi^{k}_{s,a}A^{k}_{s,a}=0
that

\displaystyle(1-b_{s}^{k})\,\mathbb{E}_{a\sim\xi^{k}(\cdot|s)}\left[A^{k}_{s,a}\right]+b_{s}^{k}\,\mathbb{E}_{a^{\prime}\sim\xi^{k}_{-}(\cdot|s)}\left[A^{k}_{s,a^{\prime}}\right]=0.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{a\sim\pi^{k}(\cdot|s)}\left[\exp\left(\eta\,A^{k}_{s,a}\right)A^{k}_{s,a}\right]

\displaystyle=(1-b_{s}^{k})\,\mathbb{E}_{s\sim\xi^{k}(\cdot|s)}\left[\exp\left(\eta\,A^{k}_{s,a}\right)A^{k}_{s,a}\right]+b_{s}^{k}\,\mathbb{E}_{a^{\prime}\sim\xi^{k}_{-}(\cdot|s)}\left[\exp\left(\eta\,A^{k}_{s,a^{\prime}}\right)A^{k}_{s,a^{\prime}}\right]

\displaystyle\geq(1-b_{s}^{k})\,\mathbb{E}_{s\sim\xi^{k}(\cdot|s)}\left[\exp\left(\eta\,A^{k}_{s,a}\right)\right]\mathbb{E}_{s\sim\xi^{k}(\cdot|s)}\left[A^{k}_{s,a}\right]+b_{s}^{k}\,\mathbb{E}_{a^{\prime}\sim\xi^{k}_{-}(\cdot|s)}\left[\exp\left(\eta\,A^{k}_{s,a^{\prime}}\right)\right]\mathbb{E}_{a^{\prime}\sim\xi^{k}_{-}(\cdot|s)}\left[A^{k}_{s,a^{\prime}}\right]

\displaystyle=(1-b_{s}^{k})\,\mathbb{E}_{a\sim\xi^{k}(\cdot|s)}\left[A^{k}_{s,a}\right]\left(\mathbb{E}_{s\sim\xi^{k}(\cdot|s)}\left[\exp\left(\eta\,A^{k}_{s,a}\right)\right]-\mathbb{E}_{a^{\prime}\sim\xi^{k}_{-}(\cdot|s)}\left[\exp\left(\eta\,A^{k}_{s,a^{\prime}}\right)\right]\right).

In addition,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{a\sim\pi^{k}(\cdot|s)}\left[\exp\left(\eta\,A^{k}_{s,a}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=(1-b_{s}^{k})\,\mathbb{E}_{a\sim\xi^{k}(\cdot|s)}\left[\exp\left(\eta\,A^{k}_{s,a}\right)\right]+b_{s}^{k}\,\mathbb{E}_{a^{\prime}\sim\xi^{k}_{-}(\cdot|s)}\left[\exp\left(\eta\,A^{k}_{s,a^{\prime}}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=(1-b_{s}^{k})\left(\mathbb{E}_{a\sim\xi^{k}(\cdot|s)}\left[\exp\left(\eta\,A^{k}_{s,a}\right)\right]-\mathbb{E}_{a^{\prime}\sim\xi^{k}_{-}(\cdot|s)}\left[\exp\left(\eta\,A^{k}_{s,a^{\prime}}\right)\right]\right)+\mathbb{E}_{a^{\prime}\sim\xi^{k}_{-}(\cdot|s)}\left[\exp\left(\eta\,A^{k}_{s,a^{\prime}}\right)\right].

By the assumption
\|V^{*}-V^{k}\|_{\infty}\leq\varepsilon
, it is easy to see that
\|A^{*}-A^{k}\|_{\infty}\leq\varepsilon
, implying that

\displaystyle\forall a\in\mathcal{A}_{s}^{*}:

\displaystyle\quad-\varepsilon\leq A^{k}(s,a)\leq\varepsilon,\quad

\displaystyle\forall a^{\prime}\not\in\mathcal{A}_{s}^{*}:

\displaystyle\quad A^{k}(s,a^{\prime})\leq-\Delta+\varepsilon.

Since
A^{k}_{s,a^{\prime}}\leq 0,\,\forall a^{\prime}\not\in\mathcal{A}_{s}^{*}
, there must hold
\mathbb{E}_{a\sim\xi^{k}(\cdot|s)}\left[A^{k}_{s,a}\right]\geq 0
.
Together with the expressions in the above two inequalities, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{a}\pi^{k+1}_{s,a}A^{k}_{s,a}

\displaystyle\geq\left[1-\frac{1}{1+(1-b_{s}^{k})\left(\frac{\mathbb{E}_{a\sim\xi^{k}(\cdot|s)}\left[\exp\left(\eta\,A^{k}_{s,a}\right)\right]}{\mathbb{E}_{a^{\prime}\sim\xi^{k}_{-}(\cdot|s)}\left[\exp\left(\eta\,A^{k}_{s,a^{\prime}}\right)\right]}-1\right)}\right]\mathbb{E}_{a\sim\xi^{k}(\cdot|s)}\left[A^{k}_{s,a}\right]

\displaystyle\geq\left[1-\frac{1}{1+(1-b_{s}^{k})\left(\exp(\eta\,(\Delta-\varepsilon))-1\right)}\right]\mathbb{E}_{a\sim\xi^{k}(\cdot|s)}\left[A^{k}_{s,a}\right],

where the last inequality follows from the above property and

\mathbb{E}_{a\sim\xi^{k}(\cdot|s)}\left[\exp\left(\eta\,A^{k}_{s,a}\right)\right]\geq\exp\left(\eta\,\mathbb{E}_{a\sim\xi^{k}(\cdot|s)}\left[A^{k}_{s,a}\right]\right)\geq 1.

The proof is completed since
\xi^{k}
is an optimal policy.
∎

Theorem 5.4

(Global Linear Convergence)
.

For softmax NPG with any constant step size
\eta>0
, one has

\displaystyle\left\|V^{*}-V^{k}\right\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|V^{*}-V^{0}\right\|_{\infty}\prod_{t=0}^{k-1}\left(1-(1-\gamma)\left[1-\frac{1}{1+C^{t}}\right]\right)

\displaystyle\leq\left\|V^{*}-V^{0}\right\|_{\infty}\left(1-(1-\gamma)\left[1-\frac{1}{1+\inf_{t}C^{t}}\right]\right)^{k},

where
C^{t}=\min_{s}C_{s}^{t}
and

\displaystyle C_{s}^{t}=\begin{cases}\pi_{s}^{t}(\mathcal{A}_{s}^{t})\left(\exp\left(\eta\,\Delta_{s}^{k}\right)-1\right)&\mbox{ if }\|V^{*}-V^{t}\|_{\infty}>\varepsilon,\\
(1-b_{s}^{t})\left(\exp(\eta\,(\Delta-\varepsilon))-1\right)&\mbox{ if }\|V^{*}-V^{t}\|_{\infty}\leq\varepsilon.\end{cases}

Proof.

Together with Theorem
2.13
, the improvement lower bounds I and II imply the global linear convergence of softmax NPG. Note that
\inf_{t}C^{t}
exists since
b_{s}^{t}\rightarrow 0
as softmax NPG converges.
∎

It is not hard to see that the following dynamic convergence result solely based on the improvement bound I holds for all the
k
,

\displaystyle\left\|V^{*}-V^{k}\right\|_{\infty}\leq\left\|V^{*}-V^{0}\right\|_{\infty}\prod_{t=0}^{k-1}\left(1-(1-\gamma)\left[1-\frac{1}{1+\min_{s}\{\pi_{s}^{t}(\mathcal{A}_{s}^{t})\left(\exp\left(\eta\,\Delta_{s}^{k}\right)-1\right)\}}\right]\right)

(5.6)

Next we consider a bandit example which shows that the above dynamic is indeed tight, i.e., the equality can be achieved.

Example 5.1
.

For simplicity, consider a bandit problem with one state
s
and two actions
\{a_{1},a_{2}\}
. Assume

\displaystyle r(s,a_{1})=1\quad\mbox{and}\quad r(s,a_{2})=0.

Thus,
a_{1}
is the optimal action. For any
\pi
, we have

\displaystyle Q^{\pi}(s,a_{1})=1,\quad Q^{\pi}(s,a_{2})=0,\quad V^{\pi}(s)=\pi_{a_{1}},

\displaystyle A^{\pi}(s,a_{1})=1-\pi_{a_{1}},\quad A^{\pi}(s,a_{2})=-\pi_{a_{1}},\quad\Delta_{s}^{\pi}=1.

It is also clear that
V^{*}(s)=1
.

The update of NPG at
a_{1}
is given by

\displaystyle\pi^{k+1}_{a_{1}}=\frac{\pi^{k}_{a_{1}}\exp\left(\eta\,(1-\pi^{k}_{a_{1}})\right)}{Z^{k}},

where

\displaystyle Z^{k}=\pi^{k}_{a_{1}}\exp\left(\eta\,(1-\pi^{k}_{a_{1}})\right)+(1-\pi^{k}_{a_{1}})\exp\left(-\eta\,\pi^{k}_{a_{1}}\right).

It can be verified directly that

\displaystyle\left(V^{*}(s)-V^{k+1}(s)\right)\left(1+\pi_{a_{1}}^{k}(e^{\eta}-1)\right)=1-\pi_{a_{1}}^{k}=V^{*}(s)-V^{k}(s).

Consequently,

\displaystyle V^{*}(s)-V^{k}(s)

\displaystyle=\left(V^{*}(s)-V^{0}(s)\right)\prod_{t=0}^{k-1}\frac{1}{1+\pi_{a_{1}}^{t}(e^{\eta}-1)}.

Noting that
\gamma=0
,
\pi^{t}_{s}(\mathcal{A}_{s}^{t})=\pi_{a_{1}}^{t}
and
\Delta_{s}^{t}=1
, it shows that (
5.6
) holds with equality for this example.

Note that the best global linear rate that can be expected from Theorem
5.4
is
\gamma
no matter how
\eta
varies. As is already already mentioned, as
\eta\rightarrow\infty
, softmax NPG approaches PI. Since PI has a local quadratic linear convergence
[
21
]
, one might expect softmax NPG to have a faster linear convergence rate as
\eta\to\infty
. In
[
11
]
, such a local linear convergence has been established.

Theorem 5.5

(
[
11
, Theorem 3.1]
)
.

For any constant step size
\eta
,
\rho\in\Delta(\mathcal{S})
and arbitrary constant
\lambda>1
, there exists a time
T(\lambda)\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\forall\,k\geq T\left(\lambda\right):\qquad V^{*}\left(\rho\right)-V^{k}\left(\rho\right)\leq\left(\exp\left\{-\eta\,\Delta\left(1-\frac{1}{\lambda}\right)\right\}\right)^{k-T\left(\lambda\right)}\left\|\frac{1}{d_{\rho}^{T\left(\lambda\right)}}\right\|_{\infty}\left(V^{*}\left(\rho\right)-V^{T\left(\lambda\right)}\left(\rho\right)\right).

Note that Theorem
5.5
is presented in a slightly different form from the one presented in
[
11
]
which only requires a little adjustment in the proof.
In
[
11
]
, the lower bound on the local linear convergence rate for the bandit problem is also established. At the end of this section, we will show a lower bound for the general MDP problem which overall matches the upper bound.

Theorem 5.6

(Lower Bound of Local Linear Convergence for Any MDP)
.

For any constant step size
\eta>0
and an arbitrary constant
\sigma>0
, there exists a time
T(\sigma)
such that

\displaystyle\forall\,k\geq T(\sigma):\quad V^{*}(\rho)-V^{k}(\rho)\geq\tilde{\rho}\,\Delta\min_{s,a}\pi^{T(\sigma)}(a|s)\exp\left(-\eta(\Delta+\sigma)(k-T(\sigma))\right).

Proof.

Let
s_{0}=\arg\min_{s}\{\max_{a}Q^{*}(s,a)-\max_{a\not\in\mathcal{A}_{s}^{*}}Q^{*}(s,a)\}
and
a_{0}\in\arg\max_{a}Q^{*}(s_{0},a)
. According to the definition of
\Delta
, one has

\displaystyle A^{*}(s_{0},a_{0})=-\Delta.

Now we consider the ratio
\pi^{k+1}(s_{0},a_{0})/\pi^{k}(s_{0},a_{0})
. By the update of softmax NPG,

\displaystyle\frac{\pi^{k+1}(a_{0}|s_{0})}{\pi^{k}(a_{0}|s_{0})}

\displaystyle=\frac{\exp(\eta\,A^{k}(s_{0},a_{0}))}{\mathbb{E}_{a\sim\pi^{k}(\cdot|s_{0})}\left[\exp(\eta\,A^{k}(s_{0},a))\right]}\geq\exp\left(\eta\left(A^{k}(s_{0},a_{0})-\max_{a}A^{k}(s_{0},a)\right)\right).

Since
A^{k}\rightarrow A^{*}
, for any
\sigma>0
, there exists
T(\sigma)
such that

\displaystyle\forall\,k\geq T(\sigma):\quad\|A^{k}-A^{*}\|_{\infty}\leq\sigma/2,

which implies that

\displaystyle A^{k}(s_{0},a_{0})

\displaystyle\geq A^{*}(s_{0},a_{0})-\sigma/2\geq-\Delta-\sigma/2,

\displaystyle\max_{a}A^{k}(s_{0},a)

\displaystyle\leq\max_{a}A^{*}(s_{0},a)+\sigma/2\leq\sigma/2.

It follows that

\displaystyle\forall\,k\geq T(\sigma):\quad\frac{\pi^{k+1}(a_{0}|s_{0})}{\pi^{k}(a_{0}|s_{0})}\geq\exp\left(-\eta\left(\Delta+\sigma\right)\right).

By Lemma
2.4
,

\displaystyle V^{*}(\rho)-V^{k}(\rho)\geq\Delta\,\mathbb{E}_{s\sim\rho}\left[b_{s}^{k}\right]\geq\tilde{\rho}\,\Delta\,\pi^{k}(a_{0}|s_{0}),

and the claim follows immediately.
∎

6
Entropy Regularized Policy Gradient Methods

6.1
Preliminaries

Recall that the entropy regularized state value is given by

\displaystyle V^{\pi}_{\tau}(s)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{\tau}\left[\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\gamma^{t}\left({r(s_{t},a_{t})-\tau\log\pi(a_{t}|s_{t})}\right)|s_{0}=s,\pi\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{\tau}\left[\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\gamma^{t}\left(r(s_{t},a_{t})+\tau\mathcal{H}(\pi(\cdot|s_{t})\right)|s_{0}=s,\pi\right],

where
r(s,a)\in[0,1]
and
\mathcal{H}(p)=-\sum_{a}p_{a}\log p_{a}
defines the entropy of a distribution. Moreover, the action value and the advantage function are defined as

\displaystyle Q_{\tau}^{\pi}(s,a)=\mathbb{E}_{s^{\prime}\sim P(\cdot|s,a)}\left[r(s,a)+\gamma V^{\pi}_{\tau}(s^{\prime})\right]\quad\mbox{and}\quad A_{\tau}^{\pi}(s,a)=Q^{\pi}_{\tau}(s,a)-\tau\log\pi(a|s)-V_{\tau}^{\pi}(s).

It can be easily seen that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{a\sim\pi(\cdot|s)}[A^{\pi}_{\tau}(s,a)]=0.

Given a policy
\pi
, the Bellman operator with entropy is defined as

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}^{\pi}_{\tau}V(s)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{a\sim\pi(\cdot|s)}\mathbb{E}_{s^{\prime}\sim P(\cdot|s,a)}\left[r(s,a)+\gamma V(s^{\prime})-\tau\log\pi(a|s)\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{a\sim\pi(\cdot|s)}\left[Q^{V}(s,a)\right]+\tau\mathcal{H}(\pi(\cdot|s)),

where
Q^{V}(s,a)=\mathbb{E}_{s^{\prime}\sim P(\cdot|s,a)}[r(s,a)+\gamma V(s^{\prime})]
. It can be verified that
\mathcal{T}_{\tau}^{\pi}V^{\pi}_{\tau}=V_{\tau}^{\pi}
, which is the Bellman equation for the entropy regularization case. Moreover, given two policies
\pi_{1}
and
\pi_{2}
, one has

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{\tau}^{\pi_{1}}V_{\tau}^{\pi_{2}}(s)-V_{\tau}^{\pi_{2}}(s)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{a\sim\pi_{1}(|s)}[Q^{\pi_{2}}_{\tau}(s,a)-\tau\log\pi_{1}(a|s)-V_{\tau}^{\pi_{2}}(s)]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{a\sim\pi_{1}(|s)}[Q^{\pi_{2}}_{\tau}(s,a)-\tau\log\pi_{2}(a|s)-V_{\tau}^{\pi_{2}}(s)]

\displaystyle\quad-\tau\mathbb{E}_{a\sim\pi_{1}(\cdot|s)}[\log\pi_{1}(a|s)-\log\pi_{2}(a|s)]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{a\sim\pi_{1}(\cdot|s)}[A^{\pi_{2}}_{\tau}(s,a)]-\tau\mathrm{KL}(\pi_{1}(\cdot|s)\|\pi_{2}(\cdot|s)).

Similar to the non-entropy case, the performance difference of two policies can also be given in terms of
\mathcal{T}_{\tau}^{\pi_{1}}V_{\tau}^{\pi_{2}}(s)-V_{\tau}^{\pi_{2}}(s)
, which can be verified directly.

Lemma 6.1

(Performance difference lemma)
.

Given two policies
\pi_{1}
and
\pi_{2}
, there holds

\displaystyle V^{\pi_{1}}_{\tau}(\rho)-V^{\pi_{2}}_{\tau}(\rho)=\frac{1}{1-\gamma}\mathbb{E}_{s\sim d_{\rho}^{\pi_{1}}}\left[\mathcal{T}_{\tau}^{\pi_{1}}V_{\tau}^{\pi_{2}}(s)-V_{\tau}^{\pi_{2}}(s)\right].

In contrast to the case without entropy, the Bellman optimality operator with entropy is defined as

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{\tau}V(s)=\max_{\pi}\mathbb{E}_{a\sim\pi(\cdot|s)}\mathbb{E}_{s^{\prime}\sim P(\cdot|s,a)}[r(s,a)-\tau\log\pi(a|s)+\gamma V(s^{\prime})]=\tau\log\|\exp(Q^{V}(s,\cdot)/\tau)\|_{1},

where
Q^{V}(s,a)
is defined as above. Moreover, the optimal soft PI policy (or softmax policy), at which the maximum is achieved, is given by

\displaystyle\pi^{\mathrm{spi}}(\cdot|s)=\frac{\exp(Q^{V}(s,\cdot)/\tau)}{\|\exp(Q^{V}(s,\cdot)/\tau)\|_{1}}.

Note that the Bellman operator and the optimal Bellman operator also satisfy the
\gamma
-contraction property in the entropy case:

\displaystyle\left\|{\cal T}^{\pi}_{\tau}V_{1}-{\cal T}^{\pi}_{\tau}V_{2}\right\|_{\infty}\leq\gamma\cdot\left\|V_{1}-V_{2}\right\|_{\infty}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\left\|{\cal T}_{\tau}V_{1}-{\cal T}_{\tau}V_{2}\right\|_{\infty}\leq\gamma\cdot\left\|V_{1}-V_{2}\right\|_{\infty}.

In this section, we will let
V_{\tau}^{*}
and
Q_{\tau}^{*}
be the optimal entropy regularized state and action values, with the optimal policy denoted by
\pi^{*}
(unique). One can immediately see that the first inequality in Lemma
2.3
still holds:

\displaystyle\|Q_{\tau}^{\pi}-Q^{*}_{\tau}\|_{\infty}\leq\gamma\cdot\|V_{\tau}^{*}-V_{\tau}^{\pi}\|_{\infty}.

Moreover,
V_{\tau}^{*}
satisfies the Bellman optimality equation
\mathcal{T}_{\tau}V_{\tau}^{*}=V_{\tau}^{*}
. It can also be shown that
[
20
]

\displaystyle\forall s\in{\cal S},\;a\in{\cal A}:\quad V^{*}_{\tau}(s)=Q^{*}_{\tau}(s,a)-\tau\log(\pi^{*}(a|s)),

which is equivalent to

\displaystyle\forall s\in{\cal S},\;a\in{\cal A}:\quad A^{*}_{\tau}(s,a)=Q^{*}_{\tau}(s,a)-V^{*}_{\tau}(s)-\tau\log(\pi^{*}(a|s))=0.

(6.1)

In addition, given a policy
\pi
, one has

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{\tau}V^{\pi}_{\tau}(s)-V^{\pi}_{\tau}(s)

\displaystyle=\tau\log\|\exp(Q^{\pi}_{\tau}(s,\cdot)/\tau)\|_{1}-V^{\pi}_{\tau}(s)

\displaystyle=Q^{\pi}_{\tau}(s,a)-\tau\log\pi^{\mathrm{spi}}(a|s)-V^{\pi}_{\tau}(s),\quad\forall a,

\displaystyle=\sum_{a}\pi(a|s)\left(Q^{\pi}_{\tau}(s,a)-\tau\log\pi^{\mathrm{spi}}(a|s)-V^{\pi}_{\tau}(s)\right)

\displaystyle=\tau\sum_{a}\pi(a|s)(\log\pi(a|s)-\log\pi^{\mathrm{spi}}(a|s))

\displaystyle=\tau\mathrm{KL}\left(\pi(\cdot|s)\|\pi^{\mathrm{spi}}(\cdot|s)\right),

where
\pi^{\mathrm{spi}}(\cdot|s)
is obtained through
Q_{\tau}^{\pi}(s,a)
, and the fourth equality follows from
\mathbb{E}_{a\sim\pi(\cdot|s)}[Q^{\pi}_{\tau}(s,a)-\tau\log\pi(a|s)-V^{\pi}_{\tau}(s)]=0
.

Apart from Lemma
6.1
, there are several other lemmas that will be used throughout this section. The first one can be verified directly.

Lemma 6.2
.

For any policy
\pi
, one has

\displaystyle V_{\tau}^{\pi}(s)\in\left[0,\frac{1+\tau\log|\mathcal{A}|}{1-\gamma}\right]\quad\mbox{and}\quad Q_{\tau}^{\pi}(s,a)\in\left[0,\frac{1+\gamma\,\tau\log|\mathcal{A}|}{1-\gamma}\right].

The following lemma can be viewed as the entropy analogue of Lemma
2.4
, which characterizes the optimality of an policy in the value space based on the KL-divergence in the policy space. This lemma can be proved easily by applying Lemma
6.1
to
-\left(V_{\tau}^{\pi}(\rho)-V_{\tau}^{*}(\rho)\right)
and then using the fact
A_{\tau}^{*}(s,a)=0
in (
6.1
).

Lemma 6.3

(
[
18
, Lemma 26]
)
.

For any policy
\pi
and
\rho\in\Delta(\mathcal{S})
, there holds

\displaystyle V_{\tau}^{*}(\rho)-V_{\tau}^{\pi}(\rho)=\frac{\tau}{1-\gamma}\mathbb{E}_{s\sim d_{\rho}^{\pi}}\left[\mathrm{KL}(\pi(\cdot|s)\|\pi^{*}(\cdot|s))\right].

Letting
\{\pi^{k}\}
be a sequence of policies, we will use the shorthand notation

V_{\tau}^{k}
and
\mathcal{T}_{\tau}^{k+1}
for
V_{\tau}^{\pi^{k}}
and
\mathcal{\mathcal{}}{T}_{\tau}^{\pi^{k+1}}
, respectively. The next lemma is an entropy regularization version of Theorem
2.12
, and the proof details are thus omitted.

Lemma 6.4
.

If
\mathcal{T}_{\tau}^{k+1}V_{\tau}^{k}(s)-V_{\tau}^{k}(s)\geq C_{k}\left(\mathcal{T}_{\tau}V_{\tau}^{k}(s)-V_{\tau}^{k}(s)\right)
for some
C\in(0,1)
, then

\displaystyle\|V_{\tau}^{*}-V_{\tau}^{k+1}\|_{\infty}\leq\left(1-(1-\gamma)\,C_{k}\right)\|V^{*}_{\tau}-V^{k}_{\tau}\|_{\infty}.

In this section, we will define
\mathcal{L}_{k}^{k+1}
as

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{k}^{k+1}=\frac{1}{1-\gamma}\sum_{s}d_{\rho}^{*}(s)\left(\mathcal{T}_{\tau}^{k+1}V_{\tau}^{k}(s)-V_{\tau}^{k}(s)\right),

(6.2)

and define
\mathcal{L}_{k}^{*}
as

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{k}^{*}=\frac{1}{1-\gamma}\sum_{s}d_{\rho}^{*}(s)\left(\mathcal{T}_{\tau}^{*}V_{\tau}^{k}(s)-V_{\tau}^{k}(s)\right),

(6.3)

where

d_{\rho}^{*}
is the visitation measure with respect to the optimal policy
\pi^{*}
, and
\mathcal{T}^{*}_{\tau}
is the Bellman operator associated with
\pi^{*}
.
It is clear that the following result still holds:

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{k}^{*}=V_{\tau}^{*}(\rho)-V_{\tau}^{k}(\rho).

As in the non-entropy case, we consider minimizing
V_{\tau}^{\pi_{\theta}}(\mu)
for fixed
\mu
with
\tilde{\mu}=\min_{s}\mu(s)>0
and the state value error
V_{\tau}^{*}(\rho)-V_{\tau}^{k}(\rho)
will be evaluated for an arbitrary
\rho
with
\tilde{\rho}=\min_{s}\rho(s)>0
.

6.2
Entropy Softmax PG

Under softmax parameterization, the policy gradient of
V^{\pi_{\theta}}(\mu)
with respect to
\theta
is overall similar to that of the non-entropy case in (
4.1
), but with
A^{\pi_{\theta}}(s,a)
being replace by
A_{\tau}^{\pi_{\theta}}(s,a)
:

\displaystyle\frac{\partial V_{\tau}^{\pi_{\theta}}(\mu)}{\partial\theta_{s,a}}=\frac{1}{1-\gamma}d^{\pi_{\theta}}_{\mu}(s)\,\pi_{\theta}(a|s)A_{\tau}^{\pi_{\theta}}(s,a).

(6.4)

Then the update of entropy softmax PG with constant step size in the parameter space is given by

\displaystyle\theta_{s,a}^{k+1}=\theta^{k}_{s,a}+\eta_{s}\,\pi^{k}_{s,a}A^{k}_{\tau}(s,a),

while in the policy space is given by

\displaystyle\pi^{k+1}_{s,a}\propto\pi^{k}_{s,a}\exp\left(\eta_{s}\,\pi^{k}_{s,a}A^{k}_{\tau}(s,a)\right),

where
\eta_{s}=\frac{\eta}{1-\gamma}d_{\mu}^{k}(s)
and
d_{\mu}^{k}
is the visitation measure under the policy
\pi^{k}
. Again, we will use the shorthand notation
\pi^{k}_{s,a}
for
\pi^{k}(a|s)
and use
\pi^{k}_{s}
for
\pi^{k}(\cdot|s)
whenever it is convenient. As in the case for softmax PG, we let

\displaystyle\hat{A}^{k}_{\tau}(s,a)=\pi^{k}(a|s)A^{k}_{\tau}(s,a).

It is evident that
\sum_{a}\hat{A}^{k}_{\tau}(s,a)=0
. Moreover, we have the following result, whose proof can be found in Appendix
C
.

Lemma 6.5
.

For any policy
\pi
, one has

\displaystyle\|\hat{A}_{\tau}^{\pi}\|_{\infty}\leq\frac{1+\tau\log|\mathcal{A}|}{1-\gamma}.

In this subsection, we establish the linear convergence of entropy softmax PG for a wider range of constant step sizes than that can be allowed in
[
18
]
based on the similar analysis technique used for softmax PG in Section
4
. The following inequalities regarding the distances of two softmax policies will be used.

Lemma 6.6

(
[
18
, Lemma 27]
and
[
5
, Section A.2]
)
.

Let
\pi_{\theta}=\mathrm{softmax}(\theta)
and
\pi_{\theta^{\prime}}=\mathrm{softmax}(\theta^{\prime})
be two softmax policies. Then

\displaystyle\mathrm{KL}(\pi_{\theta}\|\pi_{\theta^{\prime}})\leq\frac{1}{2}\|\theta-\theta^{\prime}-c\cdot\bm{1}\|_{\infty}^{2}

for any constant
c\in\mathbb{R}
. In particular, taking
c=\log\|\exp(\theta)\|_{1}-\log\|\exp(\theta^{\prime})\|_{1}
yields

\displaystyle\mathrm{KL}(\pi_{\theta}\|\pi_{\theta^{\prime}})\leq\frac{1}{2}\|\log\pi_{\theta}-\log\pi_{\theta^{\prime}}\|_{\infty}^{2}.

Moreover, one has

\displaystyle\|\log\pi_{\theta}-\log\pi_{\theta^{\prime}}\|_{\infty}\leq 2\|\theta-\theta^{\prime}\|_{\infty}.

As in the non-entropy case, the analysis begins with a state-wise improvement lower bound in terms of
\max_{a}|\hat{A}_{\tau}^{k}(s,a)|
.

Lemma 6.7

(Improvement Lower Bound)
.

For entropy softmax PG with step size
\eta>0
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{\tau}^{k+1}V_{\tau}^{k}(s)-V_{\tau}^{k}(s)\geq\eta\,\tilde{\mu}\left[\exp\left(-\frac{2\,\eta\,(1+\tau\log|\mathcal{A}|)}{(1-\gamma)^{2}}\right)-\frac{\tau\,\eta}{2(1-\gamma)}\right]\,\max_{a}|\hat{A}_{\tau}^{k}(s,a)|^{2}.

Moreover, there exists a unique solution to

\displaystyle\exp\left(-\frac{2\,x\,(1+\tau\log|\mathcal{A}|)}{(1-\gamma)^{2}}\right)-\frac{\tau\,x}{2(1-\gamma)}=0,

denoted
\beta
, such that
\mathcal{T}_{\tau}^{k+1}V_{\tau}^{k}(s)-V_{\tau}^{k}(s)\geq 0
for
\eta\in(0,\beta)
.

Proof.

Recall that

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{\tau}^{k+1}V_{\tau}^{k}(s)-V_{\tau}^{k}(s)=\sum_{a}\pi^{k+1}(a|s)A^{k}_{\tau}(s,a)-\tau\,\mathrm{KL}(\pi^{k+1}(\cdot|s)\|\pi^{k}(\cdot|s)).

For the KL part, setting
c=0
in Lemma
6.6
gives

\displaystyle\mathrm{KL}(\pi^{k+1}(\cdot|s)\|\pi^{k}(\cdot|s))

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\|\theta^{k+1}_{s}-\theta^{k}_{s}\|_{\infty}^{2}=\frac{1}{2}\|\eta_{s}\,\hat{A}^{k}_{\tau}(s,\cdot)\|_{\infty}^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}(\eta_{s})^{2}\max_{a}|\hat{A}_{\tau}^{k}(s,a)|^{2}.

For the first term, letting
Z_{s}^{k}=\mathbb{E}_{a\sim\pi^{k}(\cdot|s)}\left[\exp\left(\eta_{s}\,\hat{A}^{k}_{\tau}(s,a)\right)\right]
and
\alpha=(1+\tau\log|\mathcal{A}|)/(1-\gamma)
, one has

\displaystyle\sum_{a}\pi^{k+1}(a|s)A^{k}_{\tau}(s,a)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{Z_{s}^{k}}\sum_{a}\hat{A}_{\tau}^{k}(s,a)\exp\left(\eta_{s}\,\hat{A}_{\tau}^{k}(s,a)\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{|\mathcal{A}|}{Z_{s}^{k}}\mathrm{Cov}_{a\sim U}\left(\hat{A}_{\tau}^{k}(s,a),\exp\left(\eta_{s}\,\hat{A}_{\tau}^{k}(s,a)\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\,Z_{s}^{k}|\mathcal{A}|}\sum_{a,a^{\prime}}\left[\hat{A}_{\tau}^{k}(s,a)-\hat{A}_{\tau}^{k}(s,a^{\prime})\right]\left[\exp\left(\eta_{s}\,\hat{A}_{\tau}^{k}(s,a)\right)-\exp\left(\eta_{s}\,\hat{A}_{\tau}^{k}(s,a^{\prime})\right)\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{Z_{s}^{k}|\mathcal{A}|}\sum_{\hat{A}_{\tau}^{k}(s,a)>\hat{A}_{\tau}^{k}(s,a^{\prime})}\left[\hat{A}_{\tau}^{k}(s,a)-\hat{A}_{\tau}^{k}(s,a^{\prime})\right]\left[\exp\left(\eta_{s}\,\hat{A}_{\tau}^{k}(s,a)\right)-\exp\left(\eta_{s}\,\hat{A}_{\tau}^{k}(s,a^{\prime})\right)\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{Z_{s}^{k}|\mathcal{A}|}\sum_{\hat{A}_{\tau}^{k}(s,a)>\hat{A}_{\tau}^{k}(s,a^{\prime})}\exp\left(\eta_{s}\,\hat{A}_{\tau}^{k}(s,a^{\prime})\right)\left[\hat{A}_{\tau}^{k}(s,a)-\hat{A}_{\tau}^{k}(s,a^{\prime})\right]\left[\exp\left(\eta_{s}\left(\hat{A}_{\tau}^{k}(s,a)-\hat{A}_{\tau}^{k}(s,a^{\prime})\right)\right)-1\right]

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\exp(-\eta_{s}\,\alpha)}{Z_{s}^{k}|\mathcal{A}|}\sum_{\hat{A}_{\tau}^{k}(s,a)>\hat{A}_{\tau}^{k}(s,a^{\prime})}\left[\hat{A}_{\tau}^{k}(s,a)-\hat{A}_{\tau}^{k}(s,a^{\prime})\right]\left[\exp\left(\eta_{s}\left(\hat{A}_{\tau}^{k}(s,a)-\hat{A}_{\tau}^{k}(s,a^{\prime})\right)\right)-1\right]

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\eta_{s}\exp(-\eta_{s}\,\alpha)}{Z_{s}^{k}|\mathcal{A}|}\sum_{\hat{A}_{\tau}^{k}(s,a)>\hat{A}_{\tau}^{k}(s,a^{\prime})}\left[\hat{A}_{\tau}^{k}(s,a)-\hat{A}_{\tau}^{k}(s,a^{\prime})\right]^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\eta_{s}\exp(-2\eta_{s}\,\alpha)}{|\mathcal{A}|}\sum_{\hat{A}_{\tau}^{k}(s,a)>\hat{A}_{\tau}^{k}(s,a^{\prime})}\left[\hat{A}_{\tau}^{k}(s,a)-\hat{A}_{\tau}^{k}(s,a^{\prime})\right]^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\eta_{s}\exp(-2\eta_{s}\,\alpha)}{2\,|\mathcal{A}|}\sum_{a,a^{\prime}}\left[\hat{A}_{\tau}^{k}(s,a)-\hat{A}_{\tau}^{k}(s,a^{\prime})\right]^{2}

\displaystyle=\eta_{s}|\mathcal{A}|\exp(-2\eta_{s}\,\alpha)\mathrm{Var}_{a\sim u}\left(\hat{A}_{\tau}^{k}(s,a)\right)

\displaystyle=\eta_{s}\exp(-2\eta_{s}\,\alpha)\sum_{a}\hat{A}_{\tau}^{k}(s,a)^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\eta_{s}\exp(-2\eta_{s}\,\alpha)\max_{a}\hat{A}_{\tau}^{k}(s,a)^{2},

where the third inequality follows from
Z_{s}^{k}\leq\exp(\eta_{s}\max_{a}\hat{A}_{\tau}^{k}(s,a))\leq\exp(\eta_{s}\,\alpha)
.

Combining the above two bounds together yields

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{\tau}^{k+1}V_{\tau}^{k}(s)-V_{\tau}^{k}(s)

\displaystyle\geq\eta_{s}\left(\exp(-2\eta_{s}\,\alpha)-\frac{\tau}{2}\eta_{s}\right)\max_{a}|\hat{A}_{\tau}^{k}(s,a)|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\eta\,\tilde{\mu}\left[\exp\left(-\frac{2\eta\,\alpha}{1-\gamma}\right)-\frac{\tau\,\eta}{2(1-\gamma)}\right]\max_{a}|\hat{A}_{\tau}^{k}(s,a)|^{2}.

where the second line follows from
\eta\,\tilde{\mu}\leq\eta_{s}\leq\eta/(1-\gamma)
. The proof of the first claim is now complete, and the second claim can be verified directly.
∎

Based on the improvement lower bound we can first establish the global convergence of entropy softmax PG for
\eta\in(0,\beta)
. The key is to show that the limit of
\pi^{k}_{s,a}
for any
(s,a)
exists. Once it is done, the proof follows closely the one for Lemma 16 in
[
18
]
. The proof of this result is deferred to Appendix
D
.

Lemma 6.8

(Global Convergence)
.

For any
\eta\in(0,\beta)
, the value sequence
\{V^{k}_{\tau}(\rho)\}
produced by entropy softmax PG converges to the optimal value,

\displaystyle\forall\,s\in{\cal S}:\quad\lim_{k\to\infty}V^{k}_{\tau}(s)=V^{*}_{\tau}(s).

Note that the following result can be obtained as a byproduct of the proof of Lemma
6.8
:

\displaystyle\kappa:=\inf_{k\geq 0}\min_{s,a}\pi^{k}_{s,a}>0.

Now we are in the position to show the linear convergence of entropy softmax PG.

Theorem 6.9

(Linear Convergence)
.

For any constant step size
\eta\in(0,\beta)
, entropy softmax PG converges linearly,

\displaystyle\|V_{\tau}^{*}-V_{\tau}^{k}\|_{\infty}\leq\left(1-(1-\gamma)\eta\,\tilde{\mu}\,\tau\,\kappa^{2}\left[2\exp\left(-\frac{2\,\eta\,(1+\tau\log|\mathcal{A}|)}{(1-\gamma)^{2}}\right)-\frac{\tau\,\eta}{(1-\gamma)}\right]\right)^{k}\|V_{\tau}^{*}-V_{\tau}^{k}\|_{\infty}.

Proof.

Define

\displaystyle\pi^{k,\mathrm{spi}}(\cdot|s)=\frac{\exp\left(Q^{k}_{\tau}(s,\cdot)/\tau\right)}{\|\exp\left(Q^{k}_{\tau}(s,\cdot)/\tau\right\|_{1}}.

By Lemma
6.6
, one has

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{\tau}V^{k}(s)-V^{k}(s)

(6.20)

Under the condition

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\mathrm{KL}(\pi_{s}^{k}\|\pi_{s}^{k+1})}{\mathrm{KL}(\pi_{s}^{k+1}\|\pi_{s}^{k})}-1\right|\leq\varepsilon,

it follows from (
6.18
) that

\displaystyle\frac{(\eta\tau+1)(1-\varepsilon)+1}{\eta}\mathrm{KL}(\pi_{s}^{k+1}\|\pi_{s}^{k})\leq\mathcal{T}_{\tau}^{k+1}V_{\tau}^{k}(s)-V_{\tau}^{k}(s)\leq\frac{(\eta\tau+1)(1+\varepsilon)+1}{\eta}\mathrm{KL}(\pi_{s}^{k+1}\|\pi_{s}^{k}),

which is equivalent to

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(\eta\tau+1)(1+\varepsilon)+1}\left(\mathcal{T}_{\tau}^{k+1}V_{\tau}^{k}(s)-V_{\tau}^{k}(s)\right)\leq\frac{\mathrm{KL}(\pi_{s}^{k+1}\|\pi_{s}^{k})}{\eta}\leq\frac{1}{(\eta\tau+1)(1-\varepsilon)+1}\left(\mathcal{T}_{\tau}^{k+1}V_{\tau}^{k}(s)-V_{\tau}^{k}(s)\right).

It follows that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(\eta\tau+1)(1+\varepsilon)+1}\mathcal{L}_{k}^{k+1}\leq\mathbb{E}_{s\sim d_{\rho}^{*}}\left[\frac{\mathrm{KL}(\pi_{s}^{k+1}\|\pi_{s}^{k})}{\eta\,(1-\gamma)}\right]\leq\frac{1}{(\eta\tau+1)(1-\varepsilon)+1}\mathcal{L}_{k}^{k+1}.

Inserting this result into (
6.20
) gives

\displaystyle\left(1-\frac{1}{(\eta\tau+1)(1+\varepsilon)+1}\right)\mathcal{L}_{k}^{k+1}\geq\mathcal{L}_{k}^{*}+\frac{\eta\tau+1}{\eta(1-\gamma)}\mathbb{E}_{s\sim d_{\rho}^{*}}\left[\mathrm{KL}(\pi^{*}_{s}\|\pi^{k+1}_{s})\right]-\frac{1}{\eta(1-\gamma)}\mathbb{E}_{s\sim d_{\rho}^{*}}\left[\mathrm{KL}(\pi^{*}_{s}\|\pi_{s}^{k})\right].

(6.21)

and

\displaystyle\left(1-\frac{1}{(\eta\tau+1)(1-\varepsilon)+1}\right)\mathcal{L}_{k}^{k+1}\leq\mathcal{L}_{k}^{*}+\frac{\eta\tau+1}{\eta(1-\gamma)}\mathbb{E}_{s\sim d_{\rho}^{*}}\left[\mathrm{KL}(\pi^{*}_{s}\|\pi^{k+1}_{s})\right]-\frac{1}{\eta(1-\gamma)}\mathbb{E}_{s\sim d_{\rho}^{*}}\left[\mathrm{KL}(\pi^{*}_{s}\|\pi_{s}^{k})\right]

(6.22)

Under the conditions

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\mathrm{KL}(\pi^{*}_{s}\|\pi_{s}^{k})}{\mathrm{KL}(\pi^{k}_{s}\|\pi_{s}^{*})}-1\right|\leq\varepsilon\quad\mbox{and}\quad\left|\frac{d_{\rho}^{*}(s)}{d_{\rho}^{k}(s)}-1\right|\leq\delta,

one has

\displaystyle\frac{1}{1-\gamma}\mathbb{E}_{s\sim d_{\rho}^{*}}\left[\mathrm{KL}(\pi^{*}_{s}\|\pi_{s}^{k})\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-\gamma}\sum_{s}d_{\rho}^{*}(s)\mathrm{KL}(\pi^{*}_{s}\|\pi_{s}^{k})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-\gamma}\sum_{s}\frac{d_{\rho}^{*}(s)}{d_{\rho}^{k}(s)}d_{\rho}^{k}(s)\mathrm{KL}(\pi^{*}_{s}\|\pi_{s}^{k})

\displaystyle\leq(1+\varepsilon)(1+\delta)\frac{1}{1-\gamma}\mathbb{E}_{s\sim d_{\rho}^{k}}\left[\mathrm{KL}(\pi_{s}^{k}\|\pi^{*}_{s})\right]

\displaystyle=(1+\varepsilon)(1+\delta)\frac{1}{\tau}(V_{\tau}^{*}(\rho)-V^{k}_{\tau}(\rho))

\displaystyle=(1+\varepsilon)(1+\delta)\frac{1}{\tau}\mathcal{L}_{k}^{*}

(6.23)

where the inequality holds since all the involved terms are all non-negative, and the last line follows from Lemma
6.3
and the fact
V_{\tau}^{*}(\rho)-V^{k}_{\tau}(\rho)=\mathcal{L}_{k}^{*}
. Moreover, the reverse direction

\displaystyle\frac{1}{1-\gamma}\mathbb{E}_{s\sim d_{\rho}^{*}}\left[\mathrm{KL}(\pi^{*}_{s}\|\pi_{s}^{k})\right]\geq(1-\varepsilon)(1-\delta)\frac{1}{\tau}\mathcal{L}_{k}^{*}

(6.24)

can also be obtained. Similarly, under the same conditions but for
k+1
, one can show that

\displaystyle(1-\varepsilon)(1-\delta)\frac{1}{\tau}\mathcal{L}_{k+1}^{*}\leq\frac{1}{1-\gamma}\mathbb{E}_{s\sim d_{\rho}^{*}}\left[\mathrm{KL}(\pi^{*}_{s}\|\pi_{s}^{k+1})\right]\leq(1+\varepsilon)(1+\delta)\frac{1}{\tau}\mathcal{L}_{k+1}^{*}.

(6.25)

Inserting (
6.23
), (
6.24
), and (
6.25
) into (
6.21
) and (
6.22
) completes the proof.
∎

Theorem 6.17

(Tight local linear convergence of entropy softmax NPG)
.

Consider entropy softmax NPG with constant step size
\eta>0
.
Under the conditions in (
6.15
) and (
6.16
), one has

\displaystyle\forall\,k\geq T(\varepsilon,\delta):\quad\frac{1-\sigma_{1}(\varepsilon,\delta)}{(\eta\,\tau+1)^{2}}\left(V_{\tau}^{*}(\rho)-V^{k}_{\tau}(\rho)\right)\leq V_{\tau}^{*}(\rho)-V^{k+1}_{\tau}(\rho)\leq\frac{1+\sigma_{2}(\varepsilon,\delta)}{(\eta\,\tau+1)^{2}}\left(V_{\tau}^{*}(\rho)-V^{k}_{\tau}(\rho)\right),

where
\sigma_{1}(\varepsilon,\delta)\geq 0
and
\sigma_{2}(\varepsilon,\delta)\geq 0
are two values that approach
0
as

\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
,
\delta\rightarrow 0
.

Proof.

By the performance difference lemma (Lemma
6.1
),

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{k}^{*}-\mathcal{L}_{k+1}^{*}

\displaystyle=V_{\tau}^{k+1}(\rho)-V_{\tau}^{k}(\rho)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-\gamma}\sum_{s}d_{\rho}^{k+1}(s)\left(\mathcal{T}_{\tau}^{k+1}V_{\tau}^{k}(s)-V_{\tau}^{k}(s)\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-\gamma}\sum_{s}\frac{d_{\rho}^{k+1}}{d_{\rho}^{*}}d_{\rho}^{*}(s)\left(\mathcal{T}_{\tau}^{k+1}V_{\tau}^{k}(s)-V_{\tau}^{k}(s)\right).

Noting that
\mathcal{T}_{\tau}^{k+1}V_{\tau}^{k}(s)-V_{\tau}^{k}(s)\geq 0
from (
6.18
), under the condition

\displaystyle\left|\frac{d_{\rho}^{k+1}(s)}{d_{\rho}^{*}(s)}-1\right|\leq\delta,

one has

\displaystyle(1-\delta)\mathcal{L}_{k}^{k+1}\leq\mathcal{L}_{k}^{*}-\mathcal{L}_{k+1}^{*}\leq(1+\delta)\mathcal{L}_{k}^{k+1}.

For ease of notation, let
\kappa_{1}=\frac{1}{(1+\varepsilon)(\eta\tau+1)}
. Together with the first inequality in Lemma
6.16
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{k}^{*}-\mathcal{L}_{k+1}^{*}\geq(1-\delta)(1+\kappa_{1})\left(\left(1-\frac{1}{\eta}(1+\varepsilon)(1+\delta\tau)\right)\mathcal{L}_{k}^{*}+\left(1+\frac{1}{\eta\tau}\right)(1-\varepsilon)(1-\delta)\mathcal{L}_{k+1}^{*}\right).

After rearrangement, one has

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{k+1}^{*}\leq\frac{1-(1-\delta)(1+\kappa_{1})\left(1-\frac{1}{\eta\tau}(1+\varepsilon)(1+\delta)\right)}{1+(1-\delta)^{2}(1+\kappa_{1})\left(1+\frac{1}{\eta\tau}\right)(1-\varepsilon)}\mathcal{L}_{k}^{*}:=\rho_{u}\,\mathcal{L}_{k}^{*}.

Similarly, letting
\kappa_{2}=\frac{1}{(1-\varepsilon)(\eta\tau+1)}
, together with the second inequality in Lemma
6.16
, one can obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{k+1}^{*}\geq\frac{1-(1+\delta)(1+\kappa_{2})\left(1-\frac{1}{\eta\tau}(1-\varepsilon)(1-\delta)\right)}{1+(1+\delta)^{2}(1+\kappa_{2})\left(1+\frac{1}{\eta\tau}\right)(1+\varepsilon)}\mathcal{L}_{k}^{*}:=\rho_{l}\,\mathcal{L}_{k}^{*}.

Since

\displaystyle\rho_{u}\rightarrow\frac{1}{(\eta\,\tau+1)^{2}},\quad\rho_{l}\rightarrow\frac{1}{(\eta\,\tau+1)^{2}}\quad\mbox{as }\varepsilon\rightarrow 0,\,\delta\rightarrow 0,

the proof is completed.
∎

Conclusion

In this paper, we comprehensively study the convergence of a family of basic policy gradient methods for RL, including PPG, softmax PG, softmax NPG, entropy softmax PG, entropy softmax NPG, and soft PI. New convergence results have been obtained for them based on elementary analysis techniques.
There are several lines of direction for future work. For instance, it is interesting to see whether the analysis techniques can be used to handle the stochastic case where the policy gradient is evaluated through samples or the case in which policy parameterization involves function approximation. It is also likely to extend the analysis study to other scenarios in addition to the discounted MDP setting, such as the average reward MPD setting or the risk sensitive RL problem.

Proof of Lemma

We are going to establish the global convergence of entropy softmax PG by proving the following four claims in order.

1.

The sequence of
\{V^{k}_{\tau}(s)\}
and
\{Q^{k}_{\tau}(s,a)\}
converge for all
s,a
, with the limits denoted by
V^{\infty}_{\tau}(s)
and

2.

For all
s,a
, the policy sequence
\{\pi^{k}(a|s)\}
has at most two limit points.

3.

For all
s,a
, the limit of the policy sequence
\{\pi^{k}(a|s)\}
indeed exists, namely there exists only one limit point.

4.

The limit of the value function is optimal, i.e.
V^{\infty}=V^{*}
.

Note that once Claim 3 have been justified, the proof of Claim 4 is overall the same as the proof of
[
18
, Lemma 16]

Claim 1

As
\eta\in(0,\beta)
, Lemma

implies that

\displaystyle\forall\,s\in{\cal S}:\quad{\cal T}_{\tau}^{k+1}V^{k}_{\tau}(s)-V^{k}_{\tau}(s)\geq 0.

6.1
),
\left\{V^{k}_{\tau}(s)\right\}
is a monotonically increasing sequence. Noting that
|V^{k}_{\tau}(s)|\leq(1+\tau\log|{\cal A}|)/(1-\gamma)

\displaystyle\forall\,s\in{\cal S}:\quad V^{\infty}_{\tau}(s):=\lim_{k\to\infty}V^{k}_{\tau}(s).

By the relation of

also exists, and

\displaystyle Q^{\infty}_{\tau}(s,a)=r(s,a)+\gamma\mathbb{E}_{s^{\prime}\sim P(\cdot|s,a)}\left[V^{\infty}_{\tau}(s^{\prime})\right].

Claim 2

As the limit of
V^{k}_{\tau}(s)
exists for any
s
, we have
\lim_{k\to\infty}\left(V^{k+1}_{\tau}(s)-V^{k}_{\tau}(s)\right)=0

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}\left(V^{k+1}_{\tau}(\mu)-V^{k}_{\tau}(\mu)\right)=0.

By Lemma

, one has

\displaystyle V^{k+1}_{\tau}(\mu)-V^{k}_{\tau}(\mu)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-\gamma}\sum_{s}d^{k+1}_{\mu}(s)\left({\cal T}_{\tau}^{k+1}V^{k}_{\tau}(s)-V^{k}_{\tau}(s)\right)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{1-\gamma}\sum_{s}d^{k+1}_{\mu}(s)\cdot C(\eta)\cdot\max_{a}\left|\hat{A}^{k}_{\tau}(s,a)\right|^{2},

where

is the constant given in Lemma

\displaystyle\forall\,s\in{\cal S}:\quad\lim_{k\to\infty}\max_{a\in{\cal A}}\left|\hat{A}^{k}_{\tau}(s,a)\right|^{2}=0

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow\quad

\displaystyle\forall\,s\in{\cal S},\;a\in{\cal A}:\quad\lim_{k\to\infty}\hat{A}^{k}_{\tau}(s,a)=0

(D.1)

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow\quad

\displaystyle\forall\,s\in{\cal S},\;a\in{\cal A}:\quad\lim_{k\to\infty}\pi^{k}(a|s)\cdot{A}^{k}_{\tau}(s,a)=0

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow\quad

\displaystyle\forall\,s\in{\cal S},\;a\in{\cal A}:\quad\lim_{k\to\infty}\pi^{k}(a|s)\cdot\left[Q^{k}_{\tau}(s,a)-V^{k}_{\tau}(s)-\tau\log\pi^{k}(a|s)\right]=0.

(D.2)

For any
a
, define
p(a)
as

\displaystyle p(a):=\exp\left(\frac{1}{\tau}\cdot\left[Q^{\infty}_{\tau}(s,a)-V^{\infty}_{\tau}(s)\right]\right).

Note that
p(a)

Q^{\infty}_{\tau}(s,a)-V^{\infty}_{\tau}(s)-\tau\log p(a)=0
.

For the case that
p(a)>1

Q^{\infty}_{\tau}(s,a)>V^{\infty}_{\tau}(s)

c_{0}>0
and a finite time
T_{0}

Q^{k}_{\tau}(s,a)-V^{k}_{\tau}(s)\geq c_{0}

For the case that
p(a)\in(0,1]

which follows from the expression for the gradient in (

\hat{A}^{k}_{\tau}(s,a)\to 0
for all
(s,a)
. Thus there exists a finite time
T_{1}>0
such that

\displaystyle\forall\,k\geq T_{1}:\quad\left\|\frac{\partial V^{k}_{\tau}(\mu)}{\partial\theta_{s,\cdot}}\right\|_{\infty}<\frac{1}{2\eta}\log\left(\frac{p(a)}{\epsilon}-1\right).

In addition, since
\pi^{k}(a|s)\to\{0,\;p(a)\}
, for
\epsilon<p(a)/2
, there exists a finite time
T_{0}>0
such that

\displaystyle\forall\,k\geq T_{0}:\quad\pi^{k}(a|s)\leq\epsilon\quad\mathrm{or}\quad p(a)-\epsilon\leq\pi^{k}(a|s)\leq p(a)+\epsilon.

Note that these two sets are disjointed.

The proof is then proceeded by contradiction.
Suppose
\lim_{k\to\infty}\pi^{k}(a|s)
does not exist.
Then
\pi^{k}(a|s)
will move between the
\epsilon
-neighborhood of
0
and
p(a)
infinite times since
\pi^{k}(a|s)\to\{0,p(a)\}
. Therefore there must exist a time
t\geq\max(T_{0},T_{1})
such that
\pi^{t}(a|s)\leq\epsilon
and
\pi^{t+1}(a|s)\geq p(a)-\epsilon
. According to the update rule of entropy softmax PG (let
l_{t}:=\left\|\frac{\partial V^{t}_{\tau}(\mu)}{\partial\theta_{s,\cdot}}\right\|_{\infty}
),

\displaystyle\pi^{t+1}(a|s)

\displaystyle=\frac{\exp\left(\theta_{s,a}^{t}+\eta_{t}\frac{\partial V^{t}_{\tau}(\mu)}{\partial\theta_{s,a}}\right)}{\sum_{a^{\prime}}\exp\left(\theta_{s,a^{\prime}}^{t}+\eta_{t}\frac{\partial V^{t}_{\tau}(\mu)}{\partial\theta_{s,a^{\prime}}}\right)}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\exp\left(\theta_{s,a}^{t}+\eta_{t}\,l_{t}\right)}{\sum_{a^{\prime}}\exp\left(\theta_{s,a^{\prime}}^{t}-\eta_{t}\,l_{t}\right)}

\displaystyle=\exp(2\eta_{t}\,l_{t})\cdot\pi^{t}(a|s)

\displaystyle\leq\exp(2\eta\,l_{t})\cdot\epsilon

\displaystyle<p(a)-\epsilon,

which contradicts the claim
\pi^{t+1}(a|s)\geq p(a)-\epsilon
.

Claim 4

Denote by
\pi^{\infty}(a|s)
the limit of
\pi^{k}(a|s)
, i.e.

\displaystyle\forall\,s\in{\cal S},\;a\in{\cal A}:\quad\lim_{k\to\infty}\pi^{k}(a|s)=\pi^{\infty}(a|s).

As in
[
18
]
, we divide the action set into the following two subsets:

\displaystyle{\cal A}_{0}(s)

\displaystyle:=\left\{a:\pi^{\infty}(a|s)=0\right\}

\displaystyle{\cal A}_{+}(s)

\displaystyle:=\left\{a:\pi^{\infty}(a|s)=p(a)\right\}.

Next we show
{\cal A}_{0}(s)=\varnothing
. Otherwise, for any
a_{0}\in{\cal A}_{0}(s)
, there exists a time
T\geq 0
such that,

\displaystyle\forall\,k\geq T:\quad-\log\pi^{k}(a_{0}|s)\geq\frac{1+\tau\log|{\cal A}|}{\tau(1-\gamma)}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\forall\,k\geq T:\quad\frac{\partial V^{k}_{\tau}(\mu)}{\partial\theta_{s,a_{0}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-\gamma}d^{k}_{\mu}(s)\pi^{k}(a|s)\left[Q^{k}_{\tau}(s,a)-V^{k}_{\tau}(s)-\tau\log\pi^{k}(a|s)\right]

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{1-\gamma}d^{k}_{\mu}(s)\pi^{k}(a|s)\left[0-\frac{1+\tau\log|{\cal A}|}{1-\gamma}+\tau\frac{1+\tau\log|{\cal A}|}{\tau(1-\gamma)}\right]\geq 0,

(D.4)

which implies that
\theta^{k}_{s,a_{0}}
is increasing for any
k\geq T
. Thus
\theta^{k}_{s,a_{0}}
is lower bounded. Then according to

\displaystyle\pi^{k}(a_{0}|s)=\frac{\exp(\theta^{k}_{s,a_{0}})}{\sum_{a}\exp(\theta^{k}_{s,a})}\to 0,

we know that

\sum_{a}\exp(\theta^{k}_{s,a})\to\infty
.
On the other hand, for any
a_{+}\in{\cal A}_{+}(s)
, by

\displaystyle\pi^{k}(a_{+}|s)=\frac{\exp(\theta^{k}_{s,a_{+}})}{\sum_{a}\exp(\theta^{k}_{s,a})}\to p(a)>0

we get
\exp(\theta^{k}_{s,a_{+}})\to\infty
, so
\theta^{k}_{s,a_{+}}\to\infty
, leading to
\sum_{a_{+}\in{\cal A}_{+}(s)}\theta^{k}_{s,a_{+}}\to\infty
.
Note that since
\sum_{a}\hat{A}^{k}_{\tau}(s,a)=0
, one has

\displaystyle\sum_{a}\frac{\partial V^{k}_{\tau}(\mu)}{\partial\theta_{s,a}}=\sum_{a_{0}\in{\cal A}_{0}(s)}\frac{\partial V^{k}_{\tau}(\mu)}{\partial\theta_{s,a_{0}}}+\sum_{a_{+}\in{\cal A}_{+}(s)}\frac{\partial V^{k}_{\tau}(\mu)}{\partial\theta_{s,a_{+}}}=0.

Thus, the result in (
D.4
) implies that
\sum_{a_{+}\in{\cal A}_{+}(s)}\frac{\partial V^{k}_{\tau}(\mu)}{\partial\theta_{s,a_{+}}}\leq 0,\,\forall\,k\geq T
, which means

\sum_{a_{+}\in{\cal A}_{+}(s)}\theta^{k}_{s,a_{+}}
eventually decreases, contradicting
\sum_{a_{+}\in{\cal A}_{+}(s)}\theta^{k}_{s,a_{+}}\to\infty
.

Since
\pi^{\infty}(a|s)>0
for all
s,\,a
, together with (
D.2
), one has

\displaystyle\forall\,s,a:\quad\lim_{k\to\infty}A^{k}_{\tau}(s,a)=\lim_{k\to\infty}\left[Q^{k}_{\tau}(s,a)-V^{k}_{\tau}(s)-\tau\log\pi^{k}(a|s)\right]=0.

It follows that

\displaystyle\forall\,s,a:\quad Q^{\infty}_{\tau}(s,a)-V^{\infty}_{\tau}(s)-\tau\log\pi^{\infty}(a|s)=r(s,a)+\gamma\mathbb{E}_{s^{\prime}\sim P(\cdot|s,a)}\left[V^{\infty}_{\tau}(s^{\prime})\right]-V^{\infty}_{\tau}(s)-\tau\log\pi^{\infty}(a|s)=0.

Thus, the application of
[
20
, Corollary 21]
implies that
\pi^{\infty}=\pi^{*}
and
V^{\infty}_{\tau}=V^{*}_{\tau}
, which concludes the proof.

Appendix E
Proof of Lemma
6.14

In order to prove Lemma
6.14
, we first introduce the following two lemmas regarding the gradient and Hessian of the KL divergence. The first lemma can be verified directly.

Lemma E.1
.

Let
\pi_{\theta}
and
\pi_{\theta^{\prime}}
be two smooth parameterized policies.

1)

For the gradient, we have

\displaystyle\left.\nabla_{\theta}\mathrm{KL}(\pi_{\theta}\|\pi_{\theta^{\prime}})\right|_{\theta=\theta^{\prime}}=\left.\nabla_{\theta}\mathrm{KL}(\pi_{\theta^{\prime}}\|\pi_{\theta})\right|_{\theta=\theta^{\prime}}=0.

2)

For the Hessian, we have

\displaystyle\nabla_{\theta}^{2}\mathrm{KL}(\pi_{\theta}\|\pi_{\theta^{\prime}})

\displaystyle=\sum_{a}\nabla_{\theta}^{2}\pi_{\theta}(a)(\log\pi_{\theta}(a)-\log\pi_{{\theta^{\prime}}}(a))+F(\theta)

and

\displaystyle\nabla_{\theta}^{2}\mathrm{KL}(\pi_{{\theta^{\prime}}}\|\pi_{{\theta}})=-\sum_{a}\left(\pi_{{\theta^{\prime}}}(a)-\pi_{\theta}(a)\right)\nabla_{\theta}^{2}\log\pi_{\theta}(a)+F(\theta),

where
F(\theta)=\sum_{a}\pi_{\theta}(a)\nabla_{\theta}\log\pi_{\theta}(a)\nabla_{\theta}\log\pi_{\theta}(a)^{T}
is the Fisher information matrix.

Lemma E.2
.

Let
\pi_{\theta}=\mathrm{softmax}(\theta)
and
\pi_{\theta^{\prime}}=\mathrm{softmax}(\theta^{\prime})
be two softmax policies.

1)

The Fisher information matrix
F(\theta)
has the following expression:

\displaystyle F(\theta)=\mathrm{diag}(\pi_{\theta})-\pi_{\theta}\pi_{\theta}^{T},

and there holds

\displaystyle\|F(\theta_{1})-F(\theta_{2})\|_{2}\leq 3\|\pi_{\theta_{1}}-\pi_{\theta_{2}}\|_{2}.

Moreover, one has
1^{T}F(\theta)\,1=0
and for any
x
such that
1^{T}x=0
,

\displaystyle x^{T}F(\theta)\,x\geq\min_{a}\pi_{\theta}(a)\cdot\|x\|_{2}^{2}.

2)

The Hessian of
\mathrm{KL}(\pi_{\theta^{\prime}}\|\pi_{\theta})
is equal to
F(\theta)
, i.e.,

\displaystyle\nabla_{\theta}^{2}\mathrm{KL}(\pi_{{\theta^{\prime}}}\|\pi_{{\theta}})=F(\theta).

3)

For any
x
, the Hessian of
\mathrm{KL}(\pi_{\theta}\|\pi_{\theta^{\prime}})
satisfies

\displaystyle\left(1-c(\theta,\theta^{\prime})\right)\left(x^{T}F(\theta)\,x\right)\leq x^{T}\nabla_{\theta}^{2}\mathrm{KL}(\pi_{\theta}\|\pi_{\theta^{\prime}})\,x\leq\left(1+c(\theta,\theta^{\prime})\right)\left(x^{T}F(\theta)\,x\right),

where
c(\theta,\theta^{\prime})=\|\log\pi_{\theta}-\log\pi_{\theta^{\prime}}\|_{\infty}+\mathrm{KL}(\pi_{\theta}\|\pi_{\theta^{\prime}})
is assumed to be sufficiently small.

Proof.

1) The first one follows from
\nabla_{\theta}\log\pi_{\theta}(a)=e_{a}-\pi_{\theta}
when
\pi_{\theta}
is a softmax policy.
Noting that

\displaystyle x^{T}(F(\theta_{1})-F(\theta_{2}))\,x

\displaystyle=\sum_{a}\left(\pi_{\theta_{1}}(a)-\pi_{\theta_{2}}(a)\right)x_{a}^{2}-\left((\pi_{\theta_{1}}^{T}x)^{2}-(\pi_{\theta_{2}}^{T}x)^{2}\right),

one has

\displaystyle\left|x^{T}(F(\theta_{1})-F(\theta_{2}))\,x\right|

\displaystyle\leq\|\pi_{\theta_{1}}-\pi_{\theta_{2}}\|_{\infty}\|x\|_{2}^{2}+\|\pi_{\theta_{1}}+\pi_{\theta_{2}}\|_{2}\|\pi_{\theta_{1}}-\pi_{\theta_{2}}\|_{2}\|x\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 3\|\pi_{\theta_{1}}-\pi_{\theta_{2}}\|_{2}\,\|x\|_{2}^{2}.

The fact
1^{T}F(\theta)\,1=0
can be seen easily. For the other one, assume
a^{\prime}=\arg\min_{a}\pi_{\theta}(a)
. One has

\displaystyle x^{T}F_{\theta}x-\pi_{\theta}(a^{\prime})\sum_{a}x_{a}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{a}\pi_{\theta}(a)x_{a}^{2}-\left(\sum_{a}\pi_{\theta}(a)x_{a}\right)^{2}-\pi_{\theta}(a^{\prime})\sum_{a}x_{a}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{a\neq a^{\prime}}(\pi_{\theta}(a)-\pi_{\theta}(a^{\prime}))x_{a}^{2}-\left(\sum_{a\neq a^{\prime}}(\pi_{\theta}(a)-\pi_{\theta}(a^{\prime}))x_{a}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\geq 0,

where the second equality uses the fact
x_{a^{\prime}}=-\sum_{a\neq a^{\prime}}x_{a}
.

2) This result follows immediately from

\displaystyle\nabla_{\theta}^{2}\log\pi_{\theta}(a)=-\mathrm{diag}(\pi_{\theta})+\pi_{\theta}\pi_{\theta}^{T}=-F(\theta),

which is independent of
a
.

3) Note that

\displaystyle\frac{\nabla_{\theta}^{2}\pi_{\theta}(a)}{\pi_{\theta}(a)}=(e_{a}-\pi_{\theta})(e_{a}-\pi_{\theta})^{T}-\left(\mathrm{diag}(\pi_{\theta})-\pi_{\theta}\pi_{\theta}^{T}\right).

Therefore,

\displaystyle x^{T}\left(\nabla_{\theta}^{2}\mathrm{KL}(\pi_{\theta}\|\pi_{\theta^{\prime}})-F(\theta)\right)\,x

\displaystyle=x^{T}\mathbb{E}_{a\sim\pi_{\theta}}\left[\left[(e_{a}-\pi_{\theta})(e_{a}-\pi_{\theta})^{T}-\left(\mathrm{diag}(\pi_{\theta})-\pi_{\theta}\pi_{\theta}^{T}\right)\right]\log\frac{\pi_{\theta}(a)}{\pi_{\theta^{\prime}}(a)}\right]x

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{a\sim\pi_{\theta}}\left[\left((e_{a}-\pi_{\theta})^{T}x\right)^{2}\log\frac{\pi_{\theta}(a)}{\pi_{\theta^{\prime}}(a)}\right]-\left[x^{T}\mathrm{diag}(\pi_{\theta})\,x-\left(\pi_{\theta}^{T}x\right)^{2}\right]\mathrm{KL}(\pi_{\theta}\|\pi_{\theta^{\prime}}).

It follows that

\displaystyle\left|x^{T}\left(\nabla_{\theta}^{2}\mathrm{KL}(\pi_{\theta}\|\pi_{\theta^{\prime}})-F(\theta)\right)\,x\right|

\displaystyle\leq\|\log\pi_{\theta}-\log\pi_{\theta^{\prime}}\|_{\infty}\,\mathbb{E}_{a\sim\pi_{\theta}}\left[\left((e_{a}-\pi_{\theta})^{T}x\right)^{2}\right]

\displaystyle+\mathrm{KL}(\pi_{\theta}\|\pi_{\theta^{\prime}})\left[x^{T}\mathrm{diag}(\pi_{\theta})\,x-\left(\pi_{\theta}^{T}x\right)^{2}\right].

The proof is completed since
\left[x^{T}\mathrm{diag}(\pi_{\theta})\,x-\left(\pi_{\theta}^{T}x\right)^{2}\right]=\mathbb{E}_{a\sim\pi_{\theta}}\left[\left((e_{a}-\pi_{\theta})^{T}x\right)^{2}\right]=x^{T}F(\theta)\,x
.
∎

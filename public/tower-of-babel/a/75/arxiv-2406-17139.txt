Algebras associated to inverse systems of projective schemes


Abstract
. Artin, Tate and Van den Bergh initiated the field of noncommutative projective algebraic geometry by fruitfully studying geometric data associated to noncommutative graded algebras. More specifically, given a field 𝕂 \mathbb{K} and a graded 𝕂 \mathbb{K} -algebra A A , they defined an inverse system of projective schemes Υ A = { Υ d ​ ( A ) } \Upsilon_{A}=\{{\Upsilon_{d}(A)}\} . This system affords an algebra, 𝐁 ⁡ ( Υ A ) {\mathbf{B}}(\Upsilon_{A}) , built out of global sections, and a 𝕂 \mathbb{K} -algebra morphism τ : A → 𝐁 ⁡ ( Υ A ) \tau:A\to{\mathbf{B}}(\Upsilon_{A}) . We study and extend this construction. We define, for any natural number n n , a category 𝙿𝚂𝚢𝚜 n {\tt PSys}^{n} of projective systems of schemes and a contravariant functor 𝐁 {\mathbf{B}} from 𝙿𝚂𝚢𝚜 n {\tt PSys}^{n} to the category of associative 𝕂 \mathbb{K} -algebras. We realize the schemes Υ d ​ ( A ) {\Upsilon_{d}(A)} as Proj ​ 𝐔 d ​ ( A ) \hbox{Proj}\ {\mathbf{U}}_{d}(A) , where 𝐔 d {\mathbf{U}}_{d} is a functor from associative algebras to commutative algebras. We characterize when the morphism τ : A → 𝐁 ⁡ ( Υ A ) \tau:A\to{\mathbf{B}}(\Upsilon_{A}) is injective or surjective in terms of local cohomology modules of the 𝐔 d ​ ( A ) {\mathbf{U}}_{d}(A) . Motivated by work of Walton, when Υ A \Upsilon_{A} consists of well-behaved schemes, we prove a geometric result that computes the Hilbert series of 𝐁 ⁡ ( Υ A ) {\mathbf{B}}(\Upsilon_{A}) . We provide many detailed examples that illustrate our results. For example, we prove that for some non-AS-regular algebras constructed as twisted tensor products of polynomial rings, τ \tau is surjective or an isomorphism.

Introduction

In their seminal paper
[
1
]
the authors introduced a new way to study graded algebras using algebraic geometry. The central focus of that work is the study of what are now called Artin-Schelter (AS) regular algebras of dimension 3. To each such algebra
A
, one can associate a geometric triple
(E,\sigma,\mathcal{L})
, consisting of a scheme
E
, an automorphism
\sigma:E\to E
and a base-point-free line bundle
\mathcal{L}
on
E
. Likewise, given any such triple, one can define the
twisted homogeneous coordinate ring

B(E,\sigma,\mathcal{L})
, which the authors show is either isomorphic to
A
, or is a quotient of
A
by a normal, regular homogeneous element whose degree is larger than those of the defining relations of
A
. The ability to recover
A
from
B(E,\sigma,\mathcal{L})
enabled the authors to classify, and to infer ring-theoretic properties of, three-dimensional AS-regular algebras. It is therefore natural to hope that geometric information can also be used to study AS-regular algebras of higher dimensions, and even graded algebras that are not AS-regular.

To that end, in this paper we investigate a different geometric construction from
[
1
]
- one that can be applied to any connected,
{\mathbb{N}}
-graded
\mathbb{K}
-algebra, finitely generated in degree 1. Given a graded algebra
A
we define projective schemes
\Upsilon_{d}(A)\subseteq({\mathbb{P}}^{n})^{\times d}
that parametrize isomorphism classes of
truncated point modules of length
d+1
for

. We then define the structure of a graded ring on the space
{\mathbf{B}}(A)=\bigoplus_{d}H^{0}(\Upsilon_{d}(A),i^{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{({\mathbb{P}}^{n})^{\times d}}(1))
, and a canonical graded algebra morphism
\tau_{A}:A\to{\mathbf{B}}(A)
, which is bijective in degree 1. In the case when
A
is a quadratic, AS-regular algebra of dimension 3, it can be shown that
{\mathbf{B}}(A)\cong B(E,\sigma,\mathcal{L})
, and that
\tau_{A}
is surjective with
\hbox{ker}\ \ \tau_{A}
generated in degree 3. However, in general,
\tau_{A}
need not be injective nor surjective; see Example 3 in Section
6
.

The primary goal of this paper is to provide some partial answers to two related, natural questions:

(1)

What conditions guarantee that
A
can be recovered from
{\mathbf{B}}(A)
via
\tau_{A}
?

(2)

When is
{\mathbf{B}}(A)
amenable to study via geometry?

In
[
10
]
(see also
[
11
]
) the author showed that if

is isomorphic to the quadratic closure of

To our knowledge, in the more than thirty years since

Preliminaries

In this paper we work with vector spaces, algebras, and schemes over a fixed algebraically closed field
\mathbb{K}
.
Let
{\tt Sch}
denote the category of
\mathbb{K}
-schemes. When referring to a
scheme
, we always mean an object of
{\tt Sch}
. Furthermore, all fiber products of schemes are taken with respect to

.

By

{\tt Vect}

\mathbb{K}
-vector spaces. We denote the tensor product of vector spaces

-graded,

We write

, and require morphisms of graded vector spaces to preserve degrees.

Whenever we refer to a

, we always mean a connected,
{\mathbb{N}}
-graded, locally finite-dimensional

-algebra. Let
{\tt Alg}
denote the category of graded algebras with degree-preserving algebra morphisms.

Objects of

in

there is a map

. Moreover, it is clear that
{\mathbf{T}}(f_{1})(\hbox{ker}\ \,\pi_{A})\subset\hbox{ker}\ \,\pi_{B}
.

We refer to a graded algebra

If

is a graded algebra, we denote by

the category of
{\mathbb{Z}}
-graded right

2.1.
The scheme of zeros and local cohomology.

Let
X
be a scheme,
\mathcal{F}
a locally free
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
-module, and
s_{1},\ldots,s_{n}\in H^{0}(X,\mathcal{F})
global sections, viewed as morphisms
s_{i}:{\mathcal{O}}_{X}\to\mathcal{F}
. Define
\mathcal{I}_{s_{i}}=\hbox{im}s_{i}^{\vee}
to be the image of the dual morphism
s_{i}^{\vee}:\mathcal{F}^{\vee}\to{\mathcal{O}}_{X}^{\vee}\cong{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
. Since
\mathcal{F}
is locally free, each
\mathcal{I}_{s_{i}}
is a quasicoherent sheaf of ideals, hence so is
\mathcal{I}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{I}_{s_{i}}
. The
scheme of zeros
of
s_{1},\ldots,s_{n}
is the closed subscheme
Z\subset X
determined by
\mathcal{I}
, or equivalently, the scheme-theoretic intersection of the closed subschemes determined by the
\mathcal{I}_{s_{i}}
. As a subspace of
X
,
Z={\rm Supp}({\mathcal{O}}_{X}/\mathcal{I})
, and the structure sheaf on
Z
is the unique (up to isomorphism) sheaf
{\mathcal{O}}_{Z}
such that
i_{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{Z}={\mathcal{O}}_{X}/\mathcal{I}
, where
i:Z\to X
is the inclusion map.

Here we collect a few general facts concerning schemes of zeros that will be needed later.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
f:X\to Y
be a morphism of ringed spaces with associated morphism
f^{\sharp}:f^{-1}\mathcal{O}_{Y}\to\mathcal{O}_{X}
. Let
\varphi:\mathcal{G}\to\mathcal{H}
be a morphism of
\mathcal{O}_{Y}
-modules. Let
\mathcal{I}=\hbox{im}(\varphi)
and let
\eta:\mathcal{I}\to\mathcal{H}
be the canonical inclusion. Then
f^{*}\eta:f^{*}\mathcal{I}\to f^{*}\mathcal{H}
is injective.

Proof.

Let
x\in X
. It suffices to check that the map
(f^{*}\eta)_{x}:(f^{*}\mathcal{I})_{x}\to(f^{*}\mathcal{H})_{x}
is injective. Making the usual natural identifications of stalks at
x
, it suffices to prove that the map

\eta_{f(x)}\otimes{\rm id}_{\mathcal{O}_{X,x}}:\mathcal{I}_{f(x)}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{Y,f(x)}}\mathcal{O}_{X,x}\to\mathcal{H}_{f(x)}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{Y,f(x)}}\mathcal{O}_{X,x}

is injective.

It follows from the restriction/induction of scalars adjunction that the map

\eta_{f(x)}\otimes{\rm id}_{\mathcal{O}_{X,x}}:\mathcal{I}_{f(x)}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{Y,f(x)}}\mathcal{O}_{X,x}\to\hbox{im}(\varphi_{f(x)}\otimes{\rm id}_{\mathcal{O}_{X,x}})

is an isomorphism. Since
\hbox{im}(\varphi_{f(x)}\otimes{\rm id}_{\mathcal{O}_{X,x}})
includes into
\mathcal{H}_{f(x)}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{Y,f(x)}}\mathcal{O}_{X,x}
, we conclude that
(f^{*}\eta)_{x}
is injective.

∎

Lemma 2.2
.

Let

Adopting the notation of the paragraph preceding Lemma

\displaystyle f^{\ast}\mathcal{I}

\displaystyle\cong\hbox{im}(f^{\ast}(\oplus_{i}s_{i}^{\vee}):f^{\ast}(\oplus_{i}\mathcal{F}^{\vee})\to{\mathcal{O}}_{X}),\quad\text{$f^{\ast}$ commutes with images}

\mathcal{I}_{Z}
on an affine open cover of
X
.

Let
f_{1},\ldots,f_{g}\in S_{1}
be a minimal generating set for
S
. Fix
1\leq i\leq n
and
1\leq j\leq g
and let
U=D_{+}(f_{j})
.
Since

is affine, we have
\mathcal{I}_{s_{i}}(U)=\hbox{im}s_{i}^{\vee}(U)
as
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(U)
-modules. The morphism

is given by
\phi\mapsto\phi\circ s_{i}|_{U}
, and its image corresponds to an ideal of
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(U)
via the identification

{\mathcal{O}}_{X}^{\vee}(U)\cong\hbox{Hom}_{S_{(f_{j})}}(S_{(f_{j})},S_{(f_{j})})\cong S_{(f_{j})}={\mathcal{O}}_{X}(U).

Since
f_{j}

S_{(f_{j})}
-module

, an element

\varphi\in\hbox{Hom}_{S_{(f_{j})}}(S(1)_{(f_{j})},S_{(f_{j})})\cong\mathcal{F}^{\vee}(U)

\varphi(f_{j})
. Also recall that

corresponds to the map

S_{(f_{j})}\to S(1)_{(f_{j})},\qquad 1\mapsto(\tilde{s}_{i}/f_{j})\cdot f_{j}.

Under these identifications,

\varphi(f_{j})
is arbitrary, the image of

corresponds to the ideal

(2)

\mathcal{I}_{Z}(U)=\langle\tilde{s}_{1}/f_{j},\ldots,\tilde{s}_{n}/f_{j}\rangle\subset{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(U).

The unique saturated ideal

of
S

Using (

\langle\tilde{s}_{1},\ldots,\tilde{s}_{n}\rangle
.

∎

2.3.
The categories
{\tt Sys}
and
{\tt PSys}^{n}

We denote by
{\tt Sys}
the category whose objects are collections

{\mathbf{Z}}=\{(Z_{d},\pi^{(d)}_{ij})\}_{d\geq 1,\,1\leq i\leq j\leq d},

where the
Z_{d}
are schemes and the
\pi^{(d)}_{ij}:Z_{d}\to Z_{j-i+1}
are scheme morphisms, compatible in the sense that

\pi^{(j-i+1)}_{i^{\prime}j^{\prime}}\circ\pi^{(d)}_{ij}=\pi^{(d)}_{i+i^{\prime}-1\ i+j^{\prime}-1}
for all
1\leq i\leq j\leq d
and all

We further require the map
\pi_{1d}^{(d)}:Z_{d}\to Z_{d}
to be the identity map for all
d\geq 1
.
A morphism
\varphi:{\mathbf{Z}}\to{\mathbf{Z}}^{\prime}

is a collection of scheme morphisms
\{\varphi_{d}:Z_{d}\to Z_{d}^{\prime}\}_{d\geq 1}

(\pi^{(d)}_{ij})^{\prime}\varphi_{d}=\varphi_{j-i+1}\pi^{(d)}_{ij}

,

{\tt Sys}

Lemma 2.6
.

The functor
{\mathbf{F}}:{\tt Sys}\to{\tt Sch}
given by
{\mathbf{F}}({\mathbf{Z}})=Z_{1}
and
\mathbf{F}(\varphi)=\varphi_{1}
has a right adjoint
{\mathbf{G}}:{\tt Sch}\to{\tt Sys}
, given on objects by

{\mathbf{G}}(X)=\{(X^{\times d},{\rm pr}_{ij}^{(d)})\}_{d\geq 1,1\leq i\leq j\leq d}

where
{\rm pr}_{ij}^{(d)}
are the canonical projections onto the factors in positions
i
through
j
.

Proof.

Let
X\in{\tt Sch}
. Define
{\mathbf{G}}(X)=\{(X_{d},\pi_{ij}^{(d)})\}_{d\geq 1,1\leq i\leq j\leq d}
as follows. Set
X_{1}=X
and
\pi_{11}^{(1)}={\rm id}_{X}
. Fix some
d\geq 2
and assume inductively that all
X_{d^{\prime}}
and
\pi_{ij}^{(d^{\prime})}
are constructed satisfying the desired compatibilities for all
d^{\prime}<d
. Let
X_{d}=X_{d-1}\times X_{1}
. Let
\pi_{1d}^{(d)}={\rm id}_{X_{d}}
. Let
\pi_{1,d-1}^{(d)}:X_{d}\to X_{d-1}
,
\pi_{dd}^{(d)}:X_{d}\to X_{1}
be the canonical maps from the fiber product. If
j\leq d-1
, let
\pi_{ij}^{(d)}=\pi_{ij}^{(d-1)}\circ\pi_{1,d-1}^{(d)}
. If
i<d
, let
\pi_{id}^{(d)}=(\pi_{i,d-1}^{(d)},\pi_{dd}^{(d)})
be the unique map
X_{d}\to X_{d-i+1}=X_{d-i}\times X_{1}
satisfying
\pi_{1,d-i}^{(d-i+1)}\circ\pi_{id}^{(d)}=\pi_{i,d-1}^{(d)}
and
\pi_{d-i+1,d-i+1}^{(d-i+1)}\circ\pi_{id}^{(d)}=\pi_{dd}^{(d)}
. Then one checks that the
\pi_{ij}^{(d)}
satisfy the required relations. Hence
{\mathbf{G}}(X)\in{\tt Sys}
.

Let
f:X\to X^{\prime}
be a morphism in
{\tt Sch}
. Define
{\mathbf{G}}(f):{\mathbf{G}}(X)\to{\mathbf{G}}(X^{\prime})
as follows. Set
{\mathbf{G}}(f)_{1}=f
. Fix some

and assume that all
{\mathbf{G}}(f)_{d^{\prime}}:X_{d^{\prime}}\to X^{\prime}_{d^{\prime}}
have been defined and commute with the

. Then one checks that

commutes with the
\pi_{ij}^{(k)}
,
\pi_{ij}^{{}^{\prime}(k)}
. Hence
{\mathbf{G}}(f)
is a morphism in
{\tt Sys}
. It is then straightforward to check that

is a functor.

Let
{\mathbf{F}}:{\tt Sys}\to{\tt Sch}
be the “forgetful” functor,
{\mathbf{F}}({\mathbf{Z}})=Z_{1}
for
{\mathbf{Z}}\in{\tt Sys}
and
{\mathbf{F}}(\varphi:{\mathbf{Z}}\to{\mathbf{Z}}^{\prime})=\varphi_{1}
. For any

and

∎

2.4.
The functor
{\mathbf{B}}:{\tt PSys}^{n}\to{\tt Alg}

Motivated by the construction of a graded algebra
B
from the collection of truncated point schemes associated to a graded algebra
A
in
[
1
, Section 3.17]
, we now define a contravariant functor
{\mathbf{B}}:{\tt PSys}^{n}\to{\tt Alg}
.

Fix an object
({\mathbf{Z}},\eta:{\mathbf{Z}}\to\Phi^{n})\in{\tt PSys}^{n}
. For ease of notation, in this subsection we write
{\rm pr}_{ij}
and
\pi_{ij}
instead of
{\rm pr}^{(d)}_{ij}
and
\pi^{(d)}_{ij}
when the domain is clear from context.
Let
\mathcal{L}_{d}=\eta_{d}^{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{\times d}}(1)
be the restriction of
{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{\times d}}(1)
to
Z_{d}
. Then there is a canonical isomorphism

\mathcal{L}_{i+j}\cong\pi_{1,i}^{*}\mathcal{L}_{i}\otimes_{{\mathcal{O}}_{Z_{i+j}}}\pi_{i+1,i+j}^{*}\mathcal{L}_{j}

for all
i,j\geq 1
.

For
d\geq 1
, let
B_{d}=H^{0}(Z_{d},\mathcal{L}_{d})
. Then for any
i,j\geq 1
we have
\mathbb{K}
-linear maps
\mu_{i,j}:B_{i}\otimes B_{j}\to B_{i+j}
given by

\displaystyle B_{i}\otimes B_{j}

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{\widehat{\pi}_{1,i}\otimes\widehat{\pi}_{i+1,i+j}}H^{0}(Z_{i+j},\pi_{1,i}^{*}\mathcal{L}_{i})\otimes H^{0}(Z_{i+j},\pi_{i+1,i+j}^{*}\mathcal{L}_{j})

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{\hskip 16.38895pt\cup\hskip 16.38895pt}H^{0}(Z_{i+j},\pi_{1,i}^{*}\mathcal{L}_{i}\otimes_{{\mathcal{O}}_{Z_{i+j}}}\pi_{i+1,i+j}^{*}\mathcal{L}_{j})

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{\hskip 16.38895pt\cong\hskip 16.38895pt}H^{0}(Z_{i+j},\mathcal{L}_{i+j})=B_{i+j},

where
\widehat{\pi}_{1,i}
and
\widehat{\pi}_{i+1,i+j}
are the canonical induced maps.
The second map is the cup product, and the third is obtained by applying
H^{0}
to the isomorphism above.
It is straightforward to check that, setting
B_{0}=\mathbb{K}
, the maps
\mu_{i,j}
endow the space
{\mathbf{B}}({\mathbf{Z}})=\bigoplus_{d\geq 0}B_{d}
with the structure of a unital, associative, graded algebra.

If
\varphi:{\mathbf{Z}}\to{\mathbf{Z}}^{\prime}
is any morphism in
{\tt PSys}^{n}
, it follows that

(\pi_{1i})^{\prime}\varphi_{i+j}=\varphi_{i}\pi_{1i}
and
(\pi_{i+1,i+j})^{\prime}\varphi_{i+j}=\varphi_{j}\pi_{i+1,i+j}
for all
1\leq i\leq j
. This, together with the fact that cup product is compatible with pullbacks, implies that the direct sum of the canonical maps
\widehat{\varphi}_{d}:H^{0}(Z^{\prime}_{d},\mathcal{L}^{\prime}_{d})\to H^{0}(Z_{d},\mathcal{L}_{d})
induces a morphism of graded algebras

{\mathbf{B}}(\varphi):{\mathbf{B}}({\mathbf{Z}}^{\prime})\to{\mathbf{B}}({\mathbf{Z}}).
One easily checks that
{\mathbf{B}}:{\tt PSys}^{n}\to{\tt Alg}
is a contravariant functor.

Example 2.9
.

Consider the projective system
(\Phi^{n},{\rm id})\in{\tt PSys}^{n}
. Let

be a vector space of dimension

. If

-linear map

.

Definition 3.3
.

Let

If

is the multilinearization data associated to

Lemma 3.4
.

Let

and

with multilinearization data
(V,I,S,R,\iota)

\displaystyle(K\cap R)_{(i,\ldots,i)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=2}^{d}\sum_{m=0}^{d-n}\sigma^{m}(\iota_{n}(I_{n}))\cdot_{S}S_{(i,\ldots,i)-e_{m,n}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=2}^{d}\sum_{m=0}^{d-n}\sigma^{m}(\iota_{n}(I_{n}))\cdot_{S}\left(V^{\otimes m}\otimes\mathbb{K}^{\otimes n}\otimes V^{\otimes d-(m+n)}\right)\cdot_{S}R_{i-1}

∎

be the map adjoint to
\rho_{i}:M_{i-1}\otimes V\to M_{i}
.
Since
M
is cyclic, generated in degree 0, the maps
\widetilde{\rho}_{i}(M)
are nonzero. Choosing bases for the graded components
M_{i}
, we may view the
\widetilde{\rho}_{i}(M)
as equivalence classes in
{\mathbb{P}}(V^{*})
, and the collection
\widetilde{\rho}(M)=\{\widetilde{\rho}_{i}(M)\}_{1\leq i\leq d}
as an element of the cartesian product
{\mathbb{P}}(V^{*})^{\times d}
.

Conversely, given a collection
\widetilde{\rho}=\{\widetilde{\rho}_{i}\in{\mathbb{P}}(V^{*})\}_{1\leq i\leq d}
, for
0\leq i\leq d
, let
M^{\prime}_{i}
be a 1-dimensional
\mathbb{K}
-vector space with distinguished basis element
x_{i}
. For
1\leq i\leq d
, define
\mathbb{K}
-linear maps
\rho^{\prime}_{i}:M^{\prime}_{i-1}\otimes V\to M^{\prime}_{i}
by
x_{i-1}\otimes v\mapsto[\widetilde{\rho}_{i}](v)x_{i}
, where
[\widetilde{\rho}_{i}]
is any representative of the equivalence class
\widetilde{\rho}_{i}
. Since
\mathbf{T}(V)
is free, these maps uniquely determine the structure of a graded right
\mathbf{T}(V)
-module on
M(\widetilde{\rho})=\bigoplus_{i=0}^{d}M^{\prime}_{i}.
Since the maps
[\widetilde{\rho}_{i}]
are nonzero,
M(\widetilde{\rho})
is cyclic, generated in degree 0, hence it is a truncated right point module of length
d+1
. One readily checks that
M(\widetilde{\rho}(M))\cong M
and
\widetilde{\rho}(M(\widetilde{\rho}))
differs from
\widetilde{\rho}
at most by rescalings of the
\widetilde{\rho}_{i}.
This proves the following.

Lemma 4.3
.

The maps
M\mapsto\widetilde{\rho}(M)
and
\widetilde{\rho}\mapsto M(\widetilde{\rho})
are inverse bijective correspondences between:

(1)

the set of isomorphism classes of truncated right point modules for
{\mathbf{T}}(V)
of length
d+1
, and

(2)

the
d
-fold cartesian product (of sets)
{\mathbb{P}}(V^{*})^{\times d}

The correspondence of Lemma
4.3
can be helpfully extended to include the more easily generalizable
set of
\mathbb{K}
-valued points of the fibered product
{\mathbb{P}}(V^{*})^{\times d}
,
which we now describe.

Notation 4.4
.

For the remainder of this section, let
V
be a finite-dimensional vector space, let

S={\rm Sym}(V)
, and let
R={\mathbf{U}}_{d}({\mathbf{T}}(V))
. Let
Y=\hbox{Proj}\ \mathbb{K}[t]
be the scheme over
\mathbb{K}
with one point.

If
M
is a truncated point module for
{\mathbf{T}}(V)
of length
d+1
, then for
1\leq i\leq d
, let
\mathcal{L}_{i}(M)=\widetilde{L_{i}(M)}
be the quasicoherent
{\mathcal{O}}_{Y}
-module associated to the trivial
\mathbb{K}[t]
-module
L_{i}(M)
. Composition induces an isomorphism of invertible sheaves
\mathcal{L}_{1}(M)\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathcal{L}_{d}(M)\cong\widetilde{L(M)}
where
L(M)=\hbox{Hom}_{\mathbb{K}}(M_{0},M_{d})
, hence
there is a canonical graded ring isomorphism

\Gamma_{*}(Y,\mathcal{L}_{1}(M))\circ\cdots\circ\Gamma_{*}(Y,\mathcal{L}_{d}(M))\to\Gamma_{*}(Y,\widetilde{L(M)}),

where
\circ
denotes the Segre product of graded rings. Henceforth, we suppress the scheme
Y
and write
\Gamma_{*}(\mathcal{L}_{i}(M))
for
\Gamma_{*}(Y,\mathcal{L}_{i}(M))
.
Note that
\Gamma_{*}(\mathcal{L}_{i}(M))\cong\mathbb{K}[t]
and hence
\Gamma_{*}(\mathcal{L}_{i}(M))_{(0)}=\mathbb{K}
.

Construction 4.5
.

(
\Delta,\nabla
)
Given
M
, a truncated point module for
{\mathbf{T}}(V)
of length
d+1
, let
L_{i}=L_{i}(M)
,
L=L(M)
,
\mathcal{L}_{i}=\mathcal{L}_{i}(M)
, and
\widetilde{\rho}=\widetilde{\rho}(M)
. There are surjective homomorphisms of graded rings
\widetilde{\psi}_{i}:S\to\Gamma_{*}(\mathcal{L}_{i})
given on generators by
\widetilde{\psi}_{i}(v)=\widetilde{\rho}_{i}(v)
.
Since
R=S\circ\cdots\circ S
, the
\widetilde{\psi_{i}}
induce a canonical ring map

\widetilde{\psi}:R\to\Gamma_{*}(\mathcal{L}_{1})\circ\cdots\circ\Gamma_{*}(\mathcal{L}_{d})\cong\Gamma_{*}(\widetilde{L})

given on generators by
v_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes v_{d}\mapsto\widetilde{\rho}_{d}(v_{d})\circ\cdots\circ\widetilde{\rho}_{1}(v_{1})
. This map is not zero since the
\widetilde{\rho}_{i}
are nonzero, so
\mathfrak{p}=\hbox{ker}\ \widetilde{\psi}\in\hbox{Proj}\ R
is a closed point, and
\widetilde{\psi}
localizes to a nonzero local homomorphism of local rings
\psi:R_{(\mathfrak{p})}\to\Gamma_{*}(\widetilde{L})_{(0)}=\mathbb{K}
. We define
\Delta(M)=(\mathfrak{p},\psi)
.

Observe that if
v\in R_{1}
such that
v\notin\mathfrak{p}
, then for any
w\in R_{1}
, we have

\psi(w/v)=\widetilde{\psi}(w)/\widetilde{\psi}(v)
. This implies that any nonzero rescaling of the
\widetilde{\rho}_{i}
yields the same pair
(\mathfrak{p},\psi)
. In particular,
\Delta(M)=\Delta(M(\widetilde{\rho}(M)))
.

Conversely, given
(\mathfrak{q},\varphi)
, where
\mathfrak{q}
is a closed point in
\hbox{Proj}\ R
and
\varphi:R_{(\mathfrak{q})}\to\mathbb{K}
is a nonzero local homomorphism of local rings, let
v=v_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes v_{d}\in R_{1}
such that
v\notin\mathfrak{q}
. Define

\varphi^{\prime}_{i}:S_{(v_{i})}\xrightarrow{j_{i}}S_{(v_{1})}\otimes\cdots\otimes S_{(v_{d})}\xrightarrow{\gamma}R_{(v)}\to R_{(\mathfrak{q})}\xrightarrow{\varphi}\mathbb{K}

where
j_{i}
includes the
i
-th tensor factor and
\gamma
is the canonical ring isomorphism.
For
0\leq i\leq d
, define
\widetilde{\rho}^{\prime}_{i}:V\to\mathbb{K}
by
\widetilde{\rho}^{\prime}_{i}(w)=\varphi^{\prime}_{i}(w/v_{i})
.
Define
\nabla(\mathfrak{q},\varphi)=M(\widetilde{\rho}^{\prime})
where
\widetilde{\rho}^{\prime}=\{\widetilde{\rho}^{\prime}_{i}\}_{1\leq i\leq d}
.

Recall that the data
(\mathfrak{q},\varphi)
in Construction
4.5
uniquely determines a scheme morphism
Y\to\hbox{Proj}\ T
, hence they may be viewed as the data of a
\mathbb{K}
-valued point of
\hbox{Proj}\ T
. Such pairs are important to the discussion below, so we make the following definition.

Definition 4.6
.

Let
T
be a standardly graded commutative
\mathbb{K}
-algebra. If
\mathfrak{p}
is a closed point of
\hbox{Proj}\ T
and
\psi:T_{(\mathfrak{p})}\to\mathbb{K}
is a nonzero local homomorphism of local rings, we call
(\mathfrak{p},\psi)
a
point pair
for
\hbox{Proj}\ T
.

If
M
is a truncated right point module for
{\mathbf{T}}(V)
, the pair
(\mathfrak{p},\psi)=\Delta(M)
described in Construction
4.5
will be referred to as
the point pair for
M
.

We show that point pairs for
R
determine point modules for
{\mathbf{T}}(V)
, up to isomorphism, extending Lemma
4.3
.

Proposition 4.7
.

There are bijective correspondences between the following:

(1)

the set of isomorphism classes of truncated right point modules for
{\mathbf{T}}(V)
of length
d+1
,

(2)

the
d
-fold cartesian product (of sets)
{\mathbb{P}}(V^{*})^{\times d}
, and

(3)

the set of point pairs
(\mathfrak{p},\psi)
for
\hbox{Proj}\ R
.

By the remark above, (3) is equivalent to (3’)
the set of scheme morphisms
Y\to\hbox{Proj}\ R
.

Proof.

By Lemma
4.3
, it suffices to show that
\Delta
and
\nabla
are inverse bijections.

First we consider
\nabla\circ\Delta
. Let
M
be a truncated point module of length
d+1
for
\mathbf{T}(V)
and let
\widetilde{\rho}=\{\widetilde{\rho}_{i}(M)\}_{1\leq i\leq d}
. Set
(\mathfrak{p},\psi)=\Delta(\widetilde{\rho})
.
We claim that
\nabla(\mathfrak{p},\psi)\cong M
. To see this, refer to Construction
4.5
and observe that

\psi^{\prime}_{i}(w/v_{i})=\psi(v_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes v_{i-1}\otimes w\otimes v_{i+1}\otimes\cdots\otimes v_{d}/v).

Furthermore,
\widetilde{\rho}^{\prime}_{i}(w)=\psi^{\prime}_{i}(w/v_{i})=\widetilde{\rho}_{i}(M)(w)/\widetilde{\rho}_{i}(M)(v_{i})

for all
w
, hence the
\widetilde{\rho}^{\prime}_{i}
recover the
\widetilde{\rho}_{i}(M)
up to rescaling. It follows that
M(\widetilde{\rho}^{\prime})\cong M
.

We now show that
\Delta\circ\nabla
is the identity. Let
(\mathfrak{q},\varphi)
be an arbitrary point pair and set
M(\widetilde{\rho})=\nabla(\mathfrak{q},\varphi)
. Recall that the construction of
M(\widetilde{\rho})
includes a choice of basis for each graded component. Let
L=\hbox{Hom}_{\mathbb{K}}(M(\widetilde{\rho})_{0},M(\widetilde{\rho})_{d})
and consider
\widetilde{\psi}:R\to\Gamma_{*}(\widetilde{L})
, the ring map associated to
M(\widetilde{\rho})
, described in the construction of
\Delta(M(\widetilde{\rho}))
. We claim that
\mathfrak{q}=\hbox{ker}\ \widetilde{\psi}
. Since
\mathfrak{q}
is a closed point of
\hbox{Proj}\ R
, and
\widetilde{\psi}
is surjective, it suffices to show
\mathfrak{q}\subseteq\hbox{ker}\ \widetilde{\psi}
.

Let
f\in R_{d}
be homogeneous and write
f=\sum w_{i_{1}}\cdots w_{i_{d}}
for
w_{i_{j}}\in R_{1}
. We may assume the
w_{i_{j}}
are pure tensors and denote the
k
-th tensor component
(w_{i_{j}})_{k}
. Then for the basis elements
x_{0}\in M(\widetilde{\rho})_{0}
and
x_{d}\in M(\widetilde{\rho})_{d}
, unravelling definitions yields

\displaystyle\widetilde{\psi}(w_{i_{j}})(x_{0})

\displaystyle=\widetilde{\rho}_{d}((w_{i_{j}})_{d})\circ\cdots\circ\widetilde{\rho}_{1}((w_{i_{j}})_{1})(x_{0})

\displaystyle=\varphi^{\prime}_{1}((w_{i_{j}})_{1}/v_{1})\cdots\varphi^{\prime}_{d}((w_{i_{j}})_{d}/v_{d})x_{d}

\displaystyle=\varphi(\gamma(j_{1}((w_{i_{j}})_{1}/v_{1})))\cdots\varphi(\gamma(j_{d}((w_{i_{j}})_{d}/v_{d})))x_{d}

\displaystyle=\varphi(\gamma((w_{i_{j}})_{1}/v_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes(w_{i_{j}})_{d}/v_{d}))x_{d}

\displaystyle=\varphi(w_{i_{j}}/v)x_{d}

It follows that
\widetilde{\psi}(f)(x_{0})=\varphi(f/v^{d})x_{d}
. In particular, if
w\in R_{1}
,
\psi(w/v)=\varphi(w/v)
, so
\psi=\varphi
. And if
f\in\mathfrak{q}
, then
f/v^{d}\in\hbox{ker}\ \varphi
hence
f\in\hbox{ker}\ \widetilde{\psi}
. We conclude that
\mathfrak{q}=\hbox{ker}\ \widetilde{\psi}
.

We have established that
\Delta
and
\nabla
are inverse bijections.

∎

Proposition 4.8
.

Let
M
be a truncated right point module for
{\mathbf{T}}(V)
of length
d+1
.
Let
(\mathfrak{p},\psi)
be the point pair for
M
.
An element
f\in{\mathbf{T}}(V)_{d}
annihilates
M
if and only if
\iota(f)\in\mathfrak{p}
.

Proof.

Let
\widetilde{\rho}(M)=\{\widetilde{\rho}_{i}\}_{1\leq i\leq d}
as in the discussion preceding Lemma
4.3
. For
f\in{\mathbf{T}}(V)_{d}
, write
f=\sum v_{i_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes v_{i_{d}}
, where
v_{i_{j}}\in V
. Then for any
m\in M_{0}
,

\rho(m\otimes f)=\sum\left(\widetilde{\rho}_{d}(v_{i_{d}})\circ\cdots\circ\widetilde{\rho}_{1}(v_{i_{1}})\right)(m)=\widetilde{\psi}(\iota(f))(m),

where
\widetilde{\psi}:R\to\Gamma_{*}(\widetilde{L})
is the map from Construction
4.5
that localizes to
\psi
.
By construction,
\mathfrak{p}=\hbox{ker}\ \,\widetilde{\psi}
, and the result follows.

∎

We can now record a version of Proposition
4.7
for
\hbox{Proj}\ {\mathbf{U}}_{d}(A)

via Proposition

Proposition 4.9
.

Let
A\in{\tt Alg}^{1}
. Write
A={\mathbf{T}}(V)/I
for

, where

(1)

isomorphism classes of truncated right point modules for

of length
d+1

(2)

point pairs

for
\hbox{Proj}\ R
such that
\iota(I_{d})\subseteq\mathfrak{p}
,

(3)

point pairs
(\mathfrak{q},\varphi)
for
\hbox{Proj}\ U_{d}(A)

Proof.

The bijection between (1) and (2) is a consequence of Propositions
4.7
and
4.8

Let
(\mathfrak{p},\psi)

\widetilde{\psi}_{A}:{\mathbf{U}}_{d}(A)\to\Gamma_{*}(\widetilde{L})

∎

Theorem 4.11

Let

. The following are equivalent:

(1)

f\in A_{d}
annihilates every truncated right point module for

of length

.

(2)

\widetilde{\iota}(f)\in\mathfrak{q}
for every closed point
\mathfrak{q}\in\hbox{Proj}\ {\mathbf{U}}_{d}(A)

\widetilde{\iota}(f)

and

, then
{\mathbf{U}}_{d}(A)_{(f)}
is nonzero, hence

Example 4.13
.

The inclusion of Corollary

may be strict when
\Upsilon_{d}(A)
is not reduced.
The algebra
A=\mathbb{K}\langle x,y,z\rangle/\langle xy-yx,zy,zx-yz\rangle
is
H(0)
in the classification of
[

. Let
K
be the multilinearization of
\langle xy-yx,zy,zx-yz\rangle
(see Definition

consists of

y_{i}x_{i+1}-x_{i}y_{i+1},\ z_{i}y_{i+1},\ z_{i}x_{i+1}-y_{i}z_{i+1},\quad 0\leq i\leq 1

y_{0}^{2}y_{1}z_{2},\ y_{i}^{2}z_{i+1},\ y_{i}y_{i+1}z_{i+1},\ x_{i}y_{i+1}^{2}z_{i+1},\qquad 0\leq i\leq 1

Let
m=\widetilde{\iota}(xyz)=x_{0}y_{1}z_{2}\in{\mathbf{U}}_{3}(A)
. Then the Gröbner basis implies

,
so

by Theorem

for all

5.
Computing Hilbert series of
{\mathbf{B}}(A)
using geometry

The four-term sequence used in the proof of Proposition
4.2
is of limited use in computing the Hilbert series of
{\mathbf{B}}(A)
. It is often the case that
\hbox{ker}\ \ \tau_{A}
is nonzero, and in such cases
0pt\ {\mathbf{U}}_{d}(A)=0
for
d
sufficiently large.
Furthermore, the condition
H^{1}_{\mathfrak{m}}({\mathbf{U}}_{d}(A))_{1}=0
often becomes impractical to check directly for large
d
. However, in some cases, it is possible to exploit the geometry of the schemes
\Upsilon_{d}(A)
to compute the Hilbert series of
{\mathbf{B}}(A)
. An approach to this calculation via normalization was outlined for a particular family of schemes in
[
10
]
, also see
[
11
]
. In this section, we show the normalization approach applies when the
\Upsilon_{d}(A)
are reduced schemes whose normalization map
\nu
is an isomorphism off a discrete set of singular points, assuming these singular points satisfy a certain separability condition.

Assumptions 5.1
.

Throughout this section, we assume
\mathbb{K}
is an algebraically closed field, and
X
is a reduced, Noetherian scheme whose singular locus
{\rm Sing}(X)
is discrete (hence finite). We furthermore assume that each irreducible component
W_{i}\subset X
is the scheme-theoretic image of a closed immersion
f_{i}:W^{\prime}_{i}\to X
with
W^{\prime}_{i}
normal.

Let
\nu:X^{\prime}\to X
be the normalization. By Assumptions
5.1
, we may take
(X^{\prime},\nu)=\left(\coprod W^{\prime}_{i},\coprod f_{i}\right)
where the structure maps
\psi_{i}:W^{\prime}_{i}\to X^{\prime}
satisfy
\nu\circ\psi=f_{i}
. Since
\nu=\coprod f_{i}
, we have
\nu_{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{X^{\prime}}=\bigoplus(f_{i})_{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{W^{\prime}_{i}}
. Since
X
is reduced, the induced morphism
\nu^{\#}:{\mathcal{O}}_{X}\to\nu_{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{X^{\prime}}
is injective. Since
\nu
is an isomorphism over the open, dense regular locus, we have a short exact sequence

0\to{\mathcal{O}}_{X}\xrightarrow{\nu^{\#}}\nu_{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{X^{\prime}}\xrightarrow{\rho}\mathcal{Q}\to 0,

where
\mathcal{Q}=\nu_{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{X^{\prime}}/{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
is supported on
{\rm Sing}(X)
. Since
{\rm Sing}(X)
is discrete,
\mathcal{Q}
is a (finite) direct sum of skyscraper sheaves supported on
{\rm Sing}(X)
.
Tensoring the sequence above with
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(1)
, and adopting the usual notation
\mathcal{F}(1):=\mathcal{F}\otimes_{{\mathcal{O}}_{X}}{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(1)
for an
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
-module
\mathcal{F}
, yields

0\to{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(1)\xrightarrow{\nu^{\#}\otimes 1}\nu_{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{X^{\prime}}(1)\xrightarrow{\rho\otimes 1}\mathcal{Q}(1)\to 0.

We note that, by the projection formula, there is an isomorphism of
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
-modules

\alpha:\bigoplus(f_{i})_{*}f_{i}^{*}({\mathcal{O}}_{X}(1))\xrightarrow{\cong}\nu_{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{X^{\prime}}(1).

While the notation
\mathcal{F}(1)
is unambiguous when
\mathcal{F}={\mathcal{O}}_{X}
, the same cannot be said of the expression
f_{i}^{*}\mathcal{F}(1)
. As
f_{i}^{*}({\mathcal{O}}_{X}(1))
makes later expressions somewhat tedious to parse, we henceforth abbreviate
\mathcal{F}_{i}=f_{i}^{*}({\mathcal{O}}_{X}(1))
.

For a scheme
Y
and an
{\mathcal{O}}_{Y}
-module
\mathcal{F}
, we write
h^{0}_{Y}(\mathcal{F})=\dim_{\mathbb{K}}H^{0}(Y,\mathcal{F}).

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
X
be a scheme satisfying Assumptions
5.1
. If
H^{0}(\rho\otimes 1)
is surjective, then

h^{0}_{X}({\mathcal{O}}_{X}(1))=\sum h^{0}_{W^{\prime}_{i}}(\mathcal{F}_{i})-\sum_{p\in{\rm Sing}(X)}\dim_{\mathbb{K}}\mathcal{Q}_{p}

Proof.

Since
H^{0}(\rho\otimes 1)
is surjective, the sequence

0\to H^{0}(X,{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(1))\xrightarrow{H^{0}(\nu^{\#}\otimes 1)}H^{0}(X,\nu_{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{X^{\prime}}(1))\xrightarrow{H^{0}(\rho\otimes 1)}H^{0}(X,\mathcal{Q}(1))\to 0

is exact. Since
H^{0}(\alpha)
is an isomorphism, we have

H^{0}(X,\nu_{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{X^{\prime}}(1))\cong H^{0}(X,\bigoplus(f_{i})_{*}\mathcal{F}_{i})\cong\bigoplus H^{0}(W^{\prime}_{i},\mathcal{F}_{i}).

Finally, since
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(1)
is an invertible
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
-module and since
\mathcal{Q}
is a sum of skyscraper sheaves,
\mathcal{Q}(1)\cong\mathcal{Q}
as
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
-modules. The result follows from a dimension count of the short exact sequence.

∎

Notation 5.3
.

Let
x\in X
.
Since
f_{i}
is a closed immersion, there is at most one
w\in W^{\prime}_{i}
such that
f_{i}(w)=x
. If such an element exists, we denote it
x^{\prime}_{i}
, or just
x^{\prime}
when there is no confusion about the component in question.

Let
I_{x}=\{i:f_{i}^{-1}(x)\text{ is nonempty}\}
.
For each
i\in I_{x}
, since
f_{i}
is a closed immersion, the canonical
{\mathcal{O}}_{X,x}
-module map
\gamma^{i}_{x,x^{\prime}}:((f_{i})_{*}\mathcal{F}_{i})_{x}\to(\mathcal{F}_{i})_{x^{\prime}_{i}}
is an isomorphism. (Note that
{\mathcal{O}}_{X,x}
acts on
(\mathcal{F}_{i})_{x^{\prime}_{i}}
via
(f_{i}^{\#})_{x^{\prime}_{i}}
.) Let
\gamma_{x,x^{\prime}}=\bigoplus_{I_{x}}\gamma^{i}_{x,x_{i}^{\prime}}
. Then
\gamma_{x,x^{\prime}}
is also an isomorphism.

Definition 5.4
.

Assume
X
satisfies Assumptions
5.1
. We say the normalization map
\nu:X^{\prime}\to X

separates singularities
if there
exists a function
\tau:{\rm Sing}(X)\to\bigcup H^{0}(W^{\prime}_{j},\mathcal{F}_{j})
with the property that for all
p\in{\rm Sing}(X)
, if
s=\tau(p)\in H^{0}(W^{\prime}_{i},\mathcal{F}_{i}),
then
s_{q^{\prime}}\in\mathfrak{m}_{{\mathcal{O}}_{W^{\prime}_{i},q^{\prime}}}(\mathcal{F}_{i})_{q^{\prime}}
if and only if
q^{\prime}\neq p^{\prime}
.

We note that if
|W_{i}\cap{\rm Sing}(X)|\leq 2
for every component
W_{i}
, then
\nu:X^{\prime}\to X
separates singularities, since
{\mathcal{O}}_{W^{\prime}_{i}}(1)
separates points.

Proposition 5.5
.

Assume
X
satisfies Assumptions
5.1
and that
\nu
separates singularities. If
p\in{\rm Sing}(X)
satisfies
\dim_{\mathbb{K}}\mathcal{Q}_{p}=1
, then there exists a section
t\in\hbox{im}\ H^{0}(\rho\otimes 1)
such that
{\rm Supp}(t)=\{p\}
and
t_{p}
generates
\mathcal{Q}(1)_{p}
as an
{\mathcal{O}}_{X,p}
-module.

Proof.

Let
i\in I_{p}
and
s\in H^{0}(W^{\prime}_{i},\mathcal{F}_{i})
satisfy the condition of Definition
5.4
. Extend
s
to a section

\overline{s}\in\bigoplus H^{0}(W^{\prime}_{i},\mathcal{F}_{i})=H^{0}\left(X,\bigoplus(f_{i})_{*}\mathcal{F}_{i}\right)

by setting
\overline{s}=(0,\ldots,s,\ldots,0)
, where
s
is in the
i
-th position. Let
s^{\prime}=H^{0}(\alpha)(\overline{s})\in H^{0}(X,\nu_{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{X^{\prime}}(1))
and define
t=H^{0}(\rho\otimes 1)(s^{\prime})
.

It is clear by construction that
t_{q}=0
for
q\notin W_{i}
. For any
q\in W_{i}\cap{\rm Sing}(X)
and
q^{\prime}=f_{i}^{-1}(q)
we have the following sequence of
{\mathcal{O}}_{X,q}
-module maps

(\mathcal{F}_{i})_{q^{\prime}}\xrightarrow{\iota_{i}}\bigoplus_{I_{q}}(\mathcal{F}_{i})_{q^{\prime}}\xrightarrow{\gamma^{-1}_{q,q^{\prime}}}\bigoplus_{I_{q}}((f_{i})_{*}\mathcal{F}_{i})_{q}\xrightarrow{\alpha_{q}}(\nu_{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{X^{\prime}}(1))_{q}\xrightarrow{\rho_{q}\otimes 1}\mathcal{Q}(1)_{q}

where
\iota_{i}
is the canonical inclusion. Recall that
\rho_{q}\otimes 1
is surjective. Since the
{\mathcal{O}}_{X,q}
-module structure on each
(\mathcal{F}_{j})_{q^{\prime}}
is induced by the local homomorphism
(f_{j}^{\#})_{q^{\prime}}:{\mathcal{O}}_{X,q}\to{\mathcal{O}}_{W^{\prime}_{j},q^{\prime}}
, it follows that
s_{q^{\prime}}\in\mathfrak{m}_{{\mathcal{O}}_{W^{\prime}_{j},q^{\prime}}}(\mathcal{F}_{j})_{q^{\prime}}
if and only if
t_{q}\in\mathfrak{m}_{{\mathcal{O}}_{X,q}}\mathcal{Q}(1)_{q}
.
By assumption,
s_{q^{\prime}}\in\mathfrak{m}_{{\mathcal{O}}_{W^{\prime}_{i},q^{\prime}}}(\mathcal{F}_{j})_{q^{\prime}}
if and only if
q\neq p
. Since
s_{p^{\prime}}\notin\mathfrak{m}_{{\mathcal{O}}_{W^{\prime}_{i},p^{\prime}}}(\mathcal{F}_{j})_{p^{\prime}}
, it follows that
t_{p}\neq 0
and
p\in{\rm Supp}(t)
. Finally, since
\mathcal{Q}(1)_{q}\cong\mathcal{Q}_{q}
and since we assume
\dim_{\mathbb{K}}\mathcal{Q}_{q}=1
, the Nakayama lemma implies
\mathfrak{m}_{{\mathcal{O}}_{X,q}}\mathcal{Q}(1)_{q}=0
. Consequently,
t_{q}=0
if
q\neq p
, and
t_{p}
generates
\mathcal{Q}(1)_{p}
. The result follows.

∎

Theorem 5.6
.

Let
X
be a scheme satisfying Assumptions
5.1
. If
\nu
separates singularities and
\dim_{\mathbb{K}}\mathcal{Q}_{p}=1
for all
p\in{\rm Sing}(X)
, then
H^{0}(\rho\otimes 1)
is surjective and

h^{0}_{X}({\mathcal{O}}_{X}(1))=\sum_{i}h^{0}_{W^{\prime}_{i}}(\mathcal{F}_{i})-|{\rm Sing}(X)|.

Proof.

If
{\rm Sing}(X)
is empty, then
X
is regular, hence normal, and
\nu
is an isomorphism. The conclusions are trivial in this case.

If
{\rm Sing}(X)
is nonempty, enumerate the singular points
{\rm Sing}(X)=\{p_{1},\ldots,p_{\ell}\}
. By Proposition
5.5
, for each
1\leq j\leq\ell
, there exists a nonzero section
t_{j}\in H^{0}(X,\mathcal{Q}(1))
such that
{\rm Supp}(t_{j})=\{p_{j}\}
. The
t_{j}
are linearly independent, since they are nonzero and their supports are disjoint. By assumption,
\dim_{\mathbb{K}}\mathcal{Q}_{p_{j}}=1
for all
j
, so

\dim_{\mathbb{K}}H^{0}(X,\mathcal{Q}(1))=\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}\dim_{\mathbb{K}}\mathcal{Q}(1)_{p_{j}}=\ell,

and the sections
t_{j}
span
H^{0}(X,\mathcal{Q}(1))
. Hence
H^{0}(\rho\otimes 1)
is surjective, and the result follows from Lemma
5.2
.

∎

Corollary 5.7
.

Assume that
X=\hbox{Proj}\ S
satisfies Assumptions
5.1
. Suppose that for each irreducible component
W_{i}\subset X
, there exists a surjective graded ring homomorphism
\varphi_{i}:S\to S_{i}
such that
W^{\prime}_{i}=\hbox{Proj}\ S_{i}
is normal, and the closed immersion
f_{i}:W^{\prime}_{i}\to X
induced by
\varphi_{i}
has image equal to
W_{i}
. If
\nu=\coprod f_{i}
separates singularities and
\dim_{\mathbb{K}}\mathcal{Q}_{p}=1
for all
p\in{\rm Sing}(X)
, then

h^{0}_{X}({\mathcal{O}}_{X}(1))=\sum_{i}h^{0}_{W^{\prime}_{i}}({\mathcal{O}}_{W^{\prime}_{i}}(1))-|{\rm Sing}(X)|.

6.
Examples

We now present three examples that illustrate the results of the preceding sections. Example 1 is a comprehensive calculation using all of the main theorems of the paper to prove that
\tau_{A}:A\to\mathbf{B}(A)
is surjective and to compute its kernel. In Example 2,
\tau_{A}
is shown to be an isomorphism by establishing regular sequences in each
\mathbf{U}_{d}(A)
and appealing to Proposition
4.2
. And in Example 3, we define an algebra
A
for which
\tau_{A}
is neither injective nor surjective.

Throughout this section, we use
\hbox{Proj}\ \mathbf{U}_{d}(A)
as a model for
\Upsilon_{d}(A)
; see Proposition
3.2
. Also recall from Section
4
the
\mathbb{K}
-linear isomorphism
\widetilde{\iota}:A_{d}\to{\mathbf{U}}_{d}(A)_{1}
. Via the isomorphism of Proposition
3.5
, we henceforth view
\widetilde{\iota}
as a map
A_{d}\to S^{\prime}_{1}\subset S/K
in the notation of Section
3
.

In each example, the algebra
A
is presented by generators and relations as a quotient of the free algebra
\mathbb{K}\langle x,y,z\rangle
. If
w\in A_{d}
is a monomial, meaning it can be represented by a word
w_{0}w_{1}\cdots w_{d-1}
where
w_{i}\in\{x,y,z\}
, we suppress tensors and write
\widetilde{\iota}(w)=w_{0}w_{1}\cdots w_{d-1}\in S^{\prime}_{1}.
In Examples 1 and 2,
A
has a monomial
\mathbb{K}
-basis of the form
\{x^{i}y^{j}z^{k}\ :\ i,j,k\in{\mathbb{N}}\}.
In these cases, it is convenient to set
m_{ijk}=\widetilde{\iota}(x^{i}y^{j}z^{k}).

6.1.
Example 1.

Let

A=\mathbb{K}\langle x,y,z\rangle/\langle xy-yx,yz-zy,zx\rangle.

This is the algebra
S^{\prime}(0,0)
in the classification of
[
4
, Theorem 5.2]
; it is a twisted tensor product of
\mathbb{K}[x,y]
and
\mathbb{K}[z]
. In particular,
\{x^{i}y^{j}z^{k}\}
is a
\mathbb{K}
-linear basis of
A
and the Hilbert series of
A
is
(1-t)^{-3}
. Our goal is to show that the algebra
{\mathbf{B}}(A)
is
1
-generated, and to prove that
{\mathbf{B}}(A)\cong A/\langle xyz\rangle
.

As discussed above, for any
(i,j,k)\in{\mathbb{N}}^{3}
,
i+j+k=d
, let
m_{i,j,k}=\widetilde{\iota}(x^{i}y^{j}z^{k})
. We need the following technical lemma.

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
d\geq 2
. Let
\mathfrak{m}={\mathbf{U}}_{d}(A)_{+}
. The following hold:

(1)

if
ijk\geq 1
, then
\mathfrak{m}
annihilates
m_{i,j,k}
;

(2)

m_{d,0,0}
annihilates
m_{0,i,d-i}
for
i<d
, and
m_{i,0,d-i}
for
i\leq d-2
;

(3)

m_{0,d,0}
annihilates
m_{i,0,d-i}
for
1\leq i\leq d-1
;

(4)

m_{0,0,d}
annihilates
m_{i,d-i,0}
for
1\leq i\leq d-1
, and
m_{i,0,d-i}
for
i\geq 2
;

(5)

for all
1\leq i\leq d-1
,
m_{i,0,d-i}
annihilates
m_{j,d-j,0}
for
j<d
,
m_{0,j,d-j}
for
j>0
, and
m_{j,0,d-j}
for
j\notin\{i-1,i,i+1\}
;

(6)

if
i\geq 1
and
j<d
, then
m_{i,d-i,0}m_{0,j,d-j}=0
.

Proof.

Let
S={\mathbf{Sym}}(A_{1})^{\otimes d}
and let
K
be the multilinearization of the ideal
\langle xy-yx,yz-zy,zx\rangle
, as in Definition
3.3
. Recall that
{\mathbf{U}}_{d}(A)
is isomorphic to the subalgebra of
S/K
generated by the
m_{i,j,k}
by Proposition
3.5
. In the ring
S/K
, for any index
i
,
0\leq i\leq d-1
we have

\displaystyle x_{i}y_{i}z_{i+1}=x_{i}y_{i+1}z_{i}=x_{i+1}y_{i}z_{i}=0,

\displaystyle x_{i}y_{i+1}z_{i+1}=x_{i+1}y_{i}z_{i+1}=x_{i+1}y_{i+1}z_{i}=0.

For (1), let
(i,j,k)\in{\mathbb{N}}^{3}
and
ijk\geq 1
. Then note that
m_{i,j,k}
contains
x_{i-1}y_{i}z_{i+1}
. Let
w^{\prime}=m_{i^{\prime},j^{\prime},k^{\prime}}
for some arbitrary
(i^{\prime},j^{\prime},k^{\prime})\in{\mathbb{N}}^{3}
, and let
w=w^{\prime}m_{i,j,k}
. We consider three cases. First, if
i^{\prime}-1\geq i
, then
x_{i}
occurs in
w^{\prime}
. So
x_{i}y_{i}z_{i+1}
is in
w
, hence
w=0
. Second, if
i^{\prime}\leq i
and
i^{\prime}+j^{\prime}\leq i
, then
z_{i}
occurs in
w^{\prime}
. So
x_{i-1}y_{i}z_{i}
is in
w
, hence
w=0
. Third, if
i^{\prime}\leq i
and
i^{\prime}+j^{\prime}\geq i+1
, then
y_{i}
is in
w^{\prime}
. So
x_{i-1}y_{i}z_{i+1}y_{i}
is in
w
. We have

x_{i-1}y_{i}z_{i+1}y_{i}=x_{i-1}y_{i}z_{i}y_{i+1}=0,

hence
w=0
.

Proofs of statements (2)-(6) are similar and are left to the reader.

∎

Next we determine the closed points of the scheme
\Upsilon_{d}(A)
.

Proposition 6.2
.

Let
d\geq 2
. Let
e_{1}=[1:0:0]
,
e_{3}=[0:0:1]\in{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
. The set of closed points of the scheme
\Upsilon_{d}(A)\subset({\mathbb{P}}^{2})^{\times d}
consists of the following:

\displaystyle[x:y:0]^{d}\text{ for all }[x:y]\in{\mathbb{P}}^{1};

\displaystyle[0:y:z]^{d}\text{ for all }[y:z]\in{\mathbb{P}}^{1};

\displaystyle e_{1}^{i}\times[x:0:z]\times e_{3}^{d-i-1}\text{ for all }[x:z]\in{\mathbb{P}}^{1}\text{ and }0\leq i\leq d-1.

Proof.

The closed points of
\Upsilon_{d}(A)
are the closed points of
({\mathbb{P}}^{2})^{\times d}
that satisfy the equations:

x_{i}y_{i+1}-y_{i}x_{i+1}=0,\quad y_{i}z_{i+1}-z_{i}y_{i+1}=0,\quad z_{i}x_{i+1}=0,

for all
0\leq i\leq d-1.
The result follows by a straightforward induction.

∎

Define the following basic affine open subsets of
\Upsilon_{d}(A)
:

U_{i}=D_{+}(m_{i,0,d-i})\text{ for }0\leq i\leq d,\text{ and }U_{d+1}=D_{+}(m_{0,d,0}).

Lemma 6.3
.

The collection
\{U_{i}\}_{0\leq i\leq d+1}
is an open cover of
\Upsilon_{d}(A)
.
Moreover,
U_{i}\cap U_{j}
is nonempty if and only if
j\equiv i-1\text{ or }j\equiv i+1\pmod{d+2}
.

Proof.

The scheme
\Upsilon_{d}(A)
is quasi-compact, so, for the first statement, it suffices to check that every closed point is in one of the
U_{i}
. This is easy to see based on Proposition
6.2
. For the second statement recall that
D_{+}(f)\cap D_{+}(g)=D_{+}(fg)
and apply Lemma
6.1
.

∎

We now prove that
\Upsilon_{d}(A)
is reduced and determine its singular points.

Theorem 6.4
.

Let
X=\Upsilon_{d}(A)
for some
d\geq 2
. Then the following hold.

(1)

For all
0\leq i\leq d+1
,
\mathcal{O}_{X}(U_{i})\cong\mathbb{K}[s,t]/(st)
.

(2)

X
is a reduced scheme.

(3)

Let
p_{i}=e_{1}^{i}\times e_{3}^{d-i}
for
0\leq i\leq d
, and let
p_{d+1}=e_{2}^{d}
in
({\mathbb{P}}^{2})^{\times d}
. The set of singular points of
X
is
{\rm Sing}(X)=\{p_{i}:0\leq i\leq d+1\},
and each singular point is a nodal singularity.

Proof.

Statement (1) follows from Lemma
6.1
and a Gröbner basis for the defining ideal of
{\mathbf{U}}_{d}(A)
. For example, for
i=0
, using Lemma
6.1
we have

\mathcal{O}_{X}(U_{0})=\mathbb{K}\Big[\frac{m_{0,d,0}}{m_{0,0,d}},\frac{m_{1,0,d-1}}{m_{0,0,d}}\Big].

Moreover,
m_{0,d,0}m_{1,0,d-1}=0
. Then one uses the Gröbner basis to conclude that

\mathcal{O}_{X}(U_{0})\cong\mathbb{K}[s,t]/(st).

For statement (2), the ring
\mathbb{K}[s,t]/(st)
is reduced since it is a quotient of a polynomial ring by a square-free monomial ideal. Then
X
is a reduced scheme since the
U_{i}
form an open affine cover of
X
.

Let
p\in X
be any point. It suffices to consider regularity of
p
on the affine open cover
\{U_{i}\}_{0\leq i\leq d+1}
. Suppose that
p\in U_{i}
. From statement (1),
U_{i}\cong\hbox{Spec }\mathbb{K}[s,t]/(st)
. It is then clear that
p
is regular unless
p=p_{i}
. The statement that each
p_{i}
is nodal is also obvious.

∎

Now we determine the irreducible components of the scheme
\Upsilon_{d}(A)
. We will use the notation established in Section
5
. The following result can easily be checked using a Gröbner basis. We omit the proof.

Lemma 6.5
.

For any
d\geq 2
, there is an isomorphism of graded algebras

{\mathbf{U}}_{d}(A/\langle y\rangle)\cong\frac{\mathbb{K}[m_{i,d-i}:0\leq i\leq d]}{(m_{i,d-i}m_{j,d-j}:j>i+1)}.

Using this result, for each
0\leq i\leq d-1
, define a algebra map
\psi_{i}:{\mathbf{U}}_{d}(A/\langle y\rangle)\to\mathbb{K}[s,t]
by:

m_{i,d-i}\mapsto s,\quad m_{i+1,d-i-1}\mapsto t,\quad m_{i^{\prime},j^{\prime}}\mapsto 0\text{ otherwise.}

Let
\pi_{y}:A\to A/\langle y\rangle
be the quotient map and define
\varphi_{i}:{\mathbf{U}}_{d}(A)\to\mathbb{K}[s,t]
as the composite
{\mathbf{U}}_{d}(\pi_{y})\circ\psi_{i}
. Let
\pi_{z}:A\to A/\langle z\rangle
and
\pi_{x}:A\to A/\langle x\rangle
be quotient maps and define
\varphi_{d-1}={\mathbf{U}}_{d}(\pi_{z})
and
\varphi_{d}={\mathbf{U}}_{d}(\pi_{x})
. Observe that
A/\langle z\rangle\cong\mathbb{K}[x,y]
and
A/\langle x\rangle\cong\mathbb{K}[y,z]
, hence, by Theorem
3.6
we have isomorphisms

{\mathbf{U}}_{d}(A/\langle z\rangle)\cong\mathbb{K}[x,y]_{(d)}\quad\text{ and }\quad{\mathbf{U}}_{d}(A/\langle x\rangle)\cong\mathbb{K}[y,z]_{(d)}.

Noting that the
\varphi_{i}
are surjective, we define the corresponding closed immersions
f_{i}:W_{i}^{\prime}\to\Upsilon_{d}(A)
, where

W_{i}^{\prime}=\begin{cases}\hbox{Proj}\ \mathbb{K}[s,t]\quad&0\leq i\leq d-1,\\
\hbox{Proj}\ \mathbb{K}[x,y]_{(d)}\quad&i=d-1,\\
\hbox{Proj}\ \mathbb{K}[y,z]_{(d)}\quad&i=d.\end{cases}

For
0\leq i\leq d+1
, let
W_{i}
be the scheme-theoretic image of
f_{i}
. Note that, as schemes,
W^{\prime}_{i}\cong{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{\mathbb{K}}
for all
i
. In particular,
W^{\prime}_{i}
is normal and irreducible. It’s clear from the description of the closed points of
\Upsilon_{d}(A)
in Lemma
6.2
and the definition of the
f_{i}
that the set of all closed points of
\Upsilon_{d}(A)
is contained in
\cup_{i}W_{i}
. Since
\Upsilon_{d}(A)
is of finite type over
\mathbb{K}
, the set of closed points is dense, so
\Upsilon_{d}(A)=\cup_{i}W_{i}
. Obviously there are no (nontrivial) containments between these irreducible closed subspaces, so we have proved the following.

Lemma 6.6
.

The irreducible components of
\Upsilon_{d}(A)
are the
W_{i}
,
0\leq i\leq d+1
.

Observe that we have proved that the scheme
X=\Upsilon_{d}(A)
satisfies Assumptions
5.1
. Recall that
\nu:X^{\prime}\to X
is the normalization of
X
, where
(X^{\prime},\nu)=\left(\coprod W^{\prime}_{i},\coprod f_{i}\right)
and the structure maps
\psi_{i}:W^{\prime}_{i}\to X^{\prime}
satisfy
\nu\circ\psi=f_{i}
. Since
\nu=\coprod f_{i}
, we have
\nu_{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{X^{\prime}}=\bigoplus(f_{i})_{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{W^{\prime}_{i}}
. Also recall the short exact sequence

0\to{\mathcal{O}}_{X}\xrightarrow{\nu^{\#}}\nu_{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{X^{\prime}}\xrightarrow{\rho}\mathcal{Q}\to 0,

where
\mathcal{Q}=\nu_{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{X^{\prime}}/{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
. The sheaf
\mathcal{Q}
is a direct sum of skyscraper sheaves supported on
{\rm Sing}(X)
.

Lemma 6.7
.

Let
X=\Upsilon_{d}(A)
. Then:

(1)

\dim X=1
;

(2)

\nu=\coprod f_{i}
separates singularities;

(3)

\dim_{\mathbb{K}}\mathcal{Q}_{p}=1
for all
p\in{\rm Sing}(X)
.

Proof.

Statement (1) is clear since
\dim W_{i}=\dim{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{\mathbb{K}}=1
for all
i
.

Recall that the set of singular points was determined in Theorem
6.4
. One then checks that
|W_{i}\cap{\rm Sing}(X)|=2
for all
i
. Statements (2) and (3) follow from this fact and statement (1).

∎

Now we can compute the Hilbert series of the ring
{\mathbf{B}}(A)
.

Theorem 6.8
.

Let
A=\mathbb{K}\langle x,y,z\rangle/\langle xy-yx,yz-zy,zx\rangle
. Then the Hilbert series of
{\mathbf{B}}(A)
is given by

H_{{\mathbf{B}}(A)}(t)=1+\sum_{d\geq 1}3d\,t^{d}.

Proof.

We’ve checked that all of the hypotheses of Corollary
5.7
hold for the scheme
\Upsilon_{d}(A)
,
d\geq 2
. It is well known that the dimension of the space of global sections of
\mathcal{O}_{W^{\prime}_{i}}(1)
is given by

h^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{W^{\prime}_{i}}(1))=\begin{cases}2\quad&0\leq i\leq d-1\\
d+1\quad&i=d-1,\,d.\end{cases}

By Theorem
6.4
, there are
d+2
singular points. Hence by Corollary
5.7
, we have
\dim_{\mathbb{K}}{\mathbf{B}}(A)_{d}=2d+2(d+1)-(d+2)=3d.

∎

Recall that in Section
4
we defined

J_{d}=\{f\in A_{d}:Pf=0\text{ for every truncated right point module $P$ of length $d+1$}\}.

Since the scheme
\Upsilon_{d}(A)
is reduced, Corollary
4.12
and its proof shows that
H^{0}_{\mathfrak{m}}({\mathbf{U}}_{d}(A))_{1}=\widetilde{\iota}(J_{d}),
where
{\mathfrak{m}}={\mathbf{U}}_{d}(A)_{+}
.

Proposition 6.9
.

We have

H^{0}_{\mathfrak{m}}({\mathbf{U}}_{d}(A))_{1}=\begin{cases}0\quad&d=2\\
\bigoplus_{i,j,k\geq 1}\mathbb{K}\,m_{ijk}\quad&d\geq 3.\end{cases}

Proof.

Let
P
be a truncated point module of length
d+1
. Recall, by Proposition
6.2
, that
P
corresponds to a unique closed point of
({\mathbb{P}}^{2})^{\times d}
of the form:

\displaystyle(1)\quad[x:y:0]^{d}\text{ for all }[x:y]\in{\mathbb{P}}^{1};

\displaystyle(2)\quad[0:y:z]^{d}\text{ for all }[y:z]\in{\mathbb{P}}^{1};

Examining

\displaystyle\deg_{U}\left(q_{i}m_{0,d,0}m_{d-1,0,1}-q_{i}m_{d,0,0}m_{0,d-1,1}\right)

\displaystyle\geq i+2,\quad\text{and, if $i<t$,}

\displaystyle\deg_{U}\left(a_{i+1}u_{0}+b_{i+1}v_{0}\right)

\displaystyle\geq i+2,

but the

are

for all

∎

The Claim implies that for
\displaystyle q=\sum_{i=0}^{t-1}q_{i}
, we have

and
b=-qu_{1}-q_{t}u_{0},

so it suffices to prove

, which implies

by Lemma
6.13
.

To see this, observe, as in the proof of the claim above, that the component of
au_{1}+bv_{1}=0
in
z_{d-1}
-degree

is

q_{t}(m_{0,d,0}m_{d-1,0,1}-m_{d,0,0}m_{0,d-1,1})=0.

The fact that
m_{0,d,0}m_{d-1,0,1}
and
m_{d,0,0}m_{0,d-1,1}
have different multidegrees implies
q_{t}m_{d-1,0,1}=q_{t}m_{0,d-1,1}=0
, as desired.

∎

Finally we are ready to prove our desired result.

Theorem 6.16

Proof.

The result holds for all

\displaystyle=Q_{1}^{nz}v_{k-2}

\displaystyle=Q_{1}^{nz}m_{0,d-k+1,k-1}+(Q_{1}^{>}+Q_{1}^{=})v_{k-1}

\displaystyle=Q_{2}^{nz}m_{0,d-k+1,k-1}

{\mathbf{U}}_{2}(A)

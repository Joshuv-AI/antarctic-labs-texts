On the role of the point at infinity in Deny’s principle of positivity of mass for Riesz potentials
Natalia Zorii

Abstract




Abstract.
First introduced by J. Deny, the classical principle of positivity of mass states that if
\kappa_{\alpha}\mu\leqslant\kappa_{\alpha}\nu
everywhere on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, then
\mu(\mathbb{R}^{n})\leqslant\nu(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Here
\mu,\nu
are positive Radon measures on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
n\geqslant 2
, and
\kappa_{\alpha}\mu
is the potential of
\mu
with respect to the Riesz kernel
|x-y|^{\alpha-n}
of order
\alpha\in(0,2]
,
\alpha<n
. We strengthen Deny’s principle by showing that
\mu(\mathbb{R}^{n})\leqslant\nu(\mathbb{R}^{n})
still holds even if
\kappa_{\alpha}\mu\leqslant\kappa_{\alpha}\nu
is fulfilled only on a proper subset
A
of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
that is not inner
\alpha
-thin at infinity; and moreover, this condition on
A
cannot in general be improved. Hence, if
\xi
is a signed measure on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
\int 1\,d\xi>0
, then
\kappa_{\alpha}\xi>0
everywhere on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, except for a subset which is inner
\alpha
-thin at infinity. The analysis performed is based on the author’s recent theories of inner Riesz balayage and inner Riesz equilibrium measures (Potential Anal., 2022), the inner equilibrium measure being understood in an extended sense where both the energy and the total mass may be infinite.

1.
A strengthened version of Deny’s principle of positivity of mass

In this paper we shall deal with the theory of potentials on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
n\geqslant 2
, with respect to the
\alpha
-Riesz kernel
\kappa_{\alpha}(x,y):=|x-y|^{\alpha-n}
of order
\alpha\in(0,2]
,
\alpha<n
, where
|x-y|
is the Euclidean distance between
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Denote by
\mathfrak{M}^{+}
the cone of all positive Radon measures
\mu
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that the
\alpha
-Riesz
potential

\kappa_{\alpha}\mu(x):=\int\kappa_{\alpha}(x,y)\,d\mu(y)

is not identically infinite, which according to
[
19
, Section I.3.7]
occurs if and only if

\int_{|y|>1}\frac{d\mu(y)}{|y|^{n-\alpha}}<\infty.

(1.1)

Note that the measures in question may be
unbounded
, namely with
\mu(\mathbb{R}^{n})=+\infty
.

The principle of positivity of mass was first introduced by J. Deny (see e.g.
[
12
]
), and for
\alpha
-Riesz potentials it reads as follows
[
16
, Theorem 3.11]
.

Theorem 1.1
.

For any
\mu,\nu\in\mathfrak{M}^{+}
such that

\kappa_{\alpha}\mu\leqslant\kappa_{\alpha}\nu\text{ \ everywhere on $\mathbb{R}^{n}$},

(1.2)

we have
\mu(\mathbb{R}^{n})\leqslant\nu(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

It is easy to verify that (
1.2
) can be slightly weakened by replacing ‘everywhere on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
’ by ‘nearly everywhere on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
’ (see e.g. the author’s recent result
[
27
, Theorem 2.1]
, establishing the principle of positivity of mass for potentials with respect to rather general function kernels on locally compact spaces). Recall that a proposition involving a variable point
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
is said to hold
nearly everywhere
(
n.e.
) on
A\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
if
c_{\alpha}(E)=0
, where
E
is the set of

for which the proposition fails to hold, while
c_{\alpha}(E)
is the
inner
\alpha
-Riesz capacity
of
E

[
19
, Section II.2.6]
(cf. Sect.
2
below).

In the present study we shall show that Theorem
1.1
still holds even if the assumption
\kappa_{\alpha}\mu\leqslant\kappa_{\alpha}\nu
is fulfilled only on a proper subset
A
of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, which however must be ‘large enough’ in an arbitrarily small neighborhood of
\infty_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
, the Alexandroff point
of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. This discovery illustrates a special role of the point at infinity in Riesz potential theory, in particular in the principle of positivity of mass.

To be exact, the following theorem holds true.

5.1.
Proof of Theorem
1.2

Given
\mu,\nu\in\mathfrak{M}^{+}
, assume that

\kappa_{\alpha}\mu\leqslant\kappa_{\alpha}\nu\text{ \ n.e.\ on $A$},

(5.1)

where
A\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is
not
inner
\alpha
-thin at infinity (Definition
4.3
). By Theorem
4.2
(iii),

\mu^{A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})=\mu(\mathbb{R}^{n}),

(5.2)

\mu^{A}
being the inner balayage of
\mu
to
A
. Noting from (
5.1
) that
\nu\in\Gamma_{A,\mu}
, where
\Gamma_{A,\mu}
was introduced by (

), we conclude from Definition

that

\kappa_{\alpha}\mu^{A}\leqslant\kappa_{\alpha}\nu\text{ \ everywhere on $\mathbb{R}^{n}$},

and hence, by applying Theorem
1.1
, that

\mu^{A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\leqslant\nu(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

Combining this with (
5.2
) proves
\mu(\mathbb{R}^{n})\leqslant\nu(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, which was the claim.

5.2.
Proof of Theorem
1.4

Let
A\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be inner
\alpha
-thin at infinity. Then, by virtue of Definition
4.3
and Theorem
4.2
(iii), one can choose
\mu_{0}\in\mathfrak{M}^{+}
so that

\mu_{0}^{A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})<\mu_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

(5.3)

But according to (

\kappa_{\alpha}\mu_{0}^{A}=\kappa_{\alpha}\mu_{0}\text{ \ n.e.\ on $A$},

which together with (

) validates the theorem with

, chosen above, and

.

5.3.
Proof of Theorem
1.5

Given
A\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
c_{\alpha}
-absolutely continuous measures

having property (
5.1
), we aim to show that then necessarily

The set
A^{c}
being
\xi

\int 1_{A}\,d\mu\leqslant\int 1_{A}\,d\nu,

A

We can certainly assume that the set
A

\alpha
-thin at infinity, for if not, the claim holds by virtue of Theorem
1.2
. According to Theorem
4.2
, then there exists the inner
\alpha
-Riesz equilibrium measure
\gamma_{A}
, uniquely determined by Definition
4.1
.

For any compact

\int\kappa_{\alpha}\gamma_{K}\,d\mu=\int\kappa_{\alpha}\mu\,d\gamma_{K}\leqslant\int\kappa_{\alpha}\nu\,d\gamma_{K}=\int\kappa_{\alpha}\gamma_{K}\,d\nu,

(5.5)

where
\gamma_{K}\in\mathcal{E}^{+}_{K}

K

\kappa_{\alpha}\mu\leqslant\kappa_{\alpha}\nu
holds n.e. on

\gamma_{K}
-a.e., every Borel
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
c_{\alpha}(E)=0
being
\xi
-negligible for any
\xi\in\mathcal{E}^{+}_{\alpha}
, see Sect.
2
.)

According to (
4.8
), the net
(\kappa_{\alpha}\gamma_{K})_{K\in\mathfrak{C}_{A}}
of positive, lower semicontinuous functions increases pointwise on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
to
\kappa_{\alpha}\gamma_{A}
. Applying
[
5

to the first and the last integrals in (

), we therefore get

\int\kappa_{\alpha}\gamma_{A}\,d\mu\leqslant\int\kappa_{\alpha}\gamma_{A}\,d\nu.

This implies (
5.4
), because
\kappa_{\alpha}\gamma_{A}=1
holds true n.e. on
A
(see (

(\mu+\nu)
-a.e. To verify the latter, observe that

-absolutely continuous, the set

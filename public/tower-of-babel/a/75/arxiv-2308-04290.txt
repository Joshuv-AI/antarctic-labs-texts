Navier-Stokes Equations with Navier Boundary Conditions and Stochastic Lie Transport: Well-Posedness and Inviscid Limit
Daniel Goodair

Abstract
We prove the existence and uniqueness of global, probabilistically strong, analytically strong solutions of the 2D Stochastic Navier-Stokes Equation under Navier boundary conditions. The choice of noise includes a large class of additive, multiplicative and transport models. We emphasise that with a transport type noise, the Navier boundary conditions enable direct energy estimates which appear to be prohibited for the usual no-slip condition. The importance of the Stochastic Advection by Lie Transport (SALT) structure, in comparison to a purely transport Stratonovich noise, is also highlighted in these estimates. In the particular cases of SALT noise, the free boundary condition and a domain of non-negative curvature, the inviscid limit exists and is a global, probabilistically weak, analytically weak solution of the corresponding Stochastic Euler Equation.

The first contribution enters a significant gap in the literature regarding the existence of analytically strong solutions of fluid PDEs perturbed by a transport type noise in a bounded domain. Prior to the author’s works of [

Such an assumption requires little to no extensions of the proof in the case of a general non-differential multiplicative noise, as the additional derivative can be directly controlled with the viscosity in energy estimates (see [

For a Stratonovich transport noise such an assumption may not be necessary, as conversion to Itô form yields a corrector term which may provide cancellation of the top order derivative arising from the noise in energy estimates. This type of estimate has been well understood since the works [

50

The Leray Projector fails to preserve the zero trace property, and in general destroys our boundary estimates in the tangent direction. The author’s successful result in [

We note that this was a local existence result in 3D. 1.2
Functional Framework

We now recap the classical functional framework for the study of the deterministic Navier-Stokes Equation. We formally define the operator
\mathcal{L}
as well as the divergence-free and Navier boundary conditions. The nonlinear operator
\mathcal{L}
is defined for sufficiently regular functions

by
\mathcal{L}_{f}g:=\sum_{j=1}^{2}f^{j}\partial_{j}g.
Here and throughout the text we make no notational distinction between differential operators acting on a vector valued function or a scalar valued one; that is, we understand
\partial_{j}g
by its component mappings
(\partial_{l}g)^{l}:=\partial_{j}g^{l}

is continuous (see e.g. [
44
] Lemma 1.2). Some more technical properties of the operator are given at the end of this subsection. For the divergence-free condition we mean a function
f
such that the property

\textnormal{div}f:=\sum_{j=1}^{2}\partial_{j}f^{j}=0

holds. We require this property and the boundary condition to hold for our solution
u
at all times, though there is some ambiguity as to how we understand these conditions for a solution
u
which need not be defined pointwise everywhere on
\bar{\mathscr{O}}
. We shall understand these conditions in their traditional weak sense, that is for weak derivatives
\partial_{j}
so
\sum_{j=1}^{2}\partial_{j}f^{j}=0
holds as an identity in
L^{2}(\mathscr{O};\mathbb{R})
whilst the boundary condition is understood in terms of trace. To be precise we first define the restriction mapping on functions
f\in W^{1,2}(\mathscr{O};\mathbb{R})\cap C(\bar{\mathscr{O}};\mathbb{R})
by the restriction of

to the boundary
\partial\mathscr{O}
, which is then shown to be a bounded operator into

1.5

then the trace operator is well defined on the whole of

as a continuous linear extension of the restriction mapping, and furthermore on

by the trace of the components. The rate of strain tensor
D
appearing in (
4

f\mapsto\begin{bmatrix}\partial_{1}f^{1}&\frac{1}{2}\left(\partial_{1}f^{1}+\partial_{2}f^{2}\right)\\
\frac{1}{2}\left(\partial_{1}f^{1}+\partial_{2}f^{2}\right)&\partial_{2}f^{2}\end{bmatrix}

or in component form,
(Df)^{k,l}:=\frac{1}{2}\left(\partial_{k}f^{l}+\partial_{l}f^{k}\right)

\displaystyle\lVert u^{n}_{(\gamma+r)\wedge\tau^{M,T}_{n}}\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle-\lVert u^{n}_{\gamma\wedge\tau^{M,T}_{n}}\rVert^{2}+2\nu\int_{\gamma\wedge\tau^{M,T}_{n}}^{(\gamma+r)\wedge\tau^{M,T}_{n}}\lVert u^{n}_{s}\rVert_{H}^{2}ds

\displaystyle=\int_{\gamma\wedge\tau^{M,T}_{n}}^{(\gamma+r)\wedge\tau^{M,T}_{n}}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\left(\left\langle\mathcal{Q}_{i}^{2}u^{n}_{s},u^{n}_{s}\right\rangle+\lVert\mathcal{P}_{n}\mathcal{P}\mathcal{G}_{i}u^{n}_{s}\rVert^{2}\right)ds-\int_{\gamma\wedge\tau^{M,T}_{n}}^{(\gamma+r)\wedge\tau^{M,T}_{n}}\langle\mathcal{G}u^{n}_{s},u^{n}_{s}\rangle d\mathcal{W}_{s}.

Passing to an inequality, applying (
16
), taking the supremum over
r\in[0,\delta_{j}]
and then using the Burkholder-Davis-Gundy Inequality,

\displaystyle\mathbbm{E}\left[\sup_{r\in[0,\delta_{j}]}\lVert u^{n}_{(\gamma+r)\wedge\tau^{M,T}_{n}}\rVert^{2}-\lVert u^{n}_{\gamma\wedge\tau^{M,T}_{n}}\rVert^{2}+2\nu\int_{\gamma\wedge\tau^{M,T}_{n}}^{(\gamma+\delta_{j})\wedge\tau^{M,T}_{n}}\lVert u^{n}_{s}\rVert_{H}^{2}ds\right]

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\leq\mathbbm{E}\int_{\gamma\wedge\tau^{M,T}_{n}}^{(\gamma+\delta_{j})\wedge\tau^{M,T}_{n}}c+c\lVert u^{n}_{s}\rVert^{2}+\nu\lVert u^{n}_{s}\rVert^{2}_{1}ds+c\mathbbm{E}\left(\int_{\gamma\wedge\tau^{M,T}_{n}}^{(\gamma+\delta_{j})\wedge\tau^{M,T}_{n}}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\langle\mathcal{G}_{i}u^{n}_{s},u^{n}_{s}\rangle^{2}ds\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Using (
18
) we pass to the inequality

\displaystyle\mathbbm{E}\left[\sup_{r\in[0,\delta_{j}]}\lVert u^{n}_{(\gamma+r)\wedge\tau^{M,T}_{n}}\rVert^{2}-\lVert u^{n}_{\gamma\wedge\tau^{M,T}_{n}}\rVert^{2}+2\nu\int_{\gamma\wedge\tau^{M,T}_{n}}^{(\gamma+\delta_{j})\wedge\tau^{M,T}_{n}}\lVert u^{n}_{s}\rVert_{H}^{2}ds\right]

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\leq\mathbbm{E}\int_{\gamma\wedge\tau^{M,T}_{n}}^{(\gamma+\delta_{j})\wedge\tau^{M,T}_{n}}c+c\lVert u^{n}_{s}\rVert^{2}+\nu\lVert u^{n}_{s}\rVert^{2}_{1}ds+\mathbbm{E}\left(\int_{\gamma\wedge\tau^{M,T}_{n}}^{(\gamma+\delta_{j})\wedge\tau^{M,T}_{n}}c+c\lVert u^{n}_{s}\rVert^{4}ds\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Here we remind ourselves that, as shown in the proof of Lemma
2.4
,
\lVert u^{n}_{s}\rVert_{1}^{2}\leq\lVert u^{n}_{s}\rVert_{H}^{2}
. In addition we use the control on
u^{n}
in
L^{2}_{\sigma}
up until the stopping time
\tau^{M,T}_{n}
, deducing that

\displaystyle\mathbbm{E}\left[\sup_{r\in[0,\delta_{j}]}\lVert u^{n}_{(\gamma+r)\wedge\tau^{M,T}_{n}}\rVert^{2}-\lVert u^{n}_{\gamma\wedge\tau^{M,T}_{n}}\rVert^{2}+\nu\int_{\gamma\wedge\tau^{M,T}_{n}}^{(\gamma+\delta_{j})\wedge\tau^{M,T}_{n}}\lVert u^{n}_{s}\rVert_{H}^{2}ds\right]

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\leq\mathbbm{E}\int_{\gamma\wedge\tau^{M,T}_{n}}^{(\gamma+\delta_{j})\wedge\tau^{M,T}_{n}}C_{M}ds+\mathbbm{E}\left(\int_{\gamma\wedge\tau^{M,T}_{n}}^{(\gamma+\delta_{j})\wedge\tau^{M,T}_{n}}C_{M}ds\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\leq\mathbbm{E}\left(C_{M}\delta_{j}+C_{M}\delta_{j}^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)

and further

\displaystyle\mathbbm{E}\left[\sup_{r\in[0,\delta_{j}]}\lVert u^{n}_{(\gamma+r)\wedge\tau^{M,T}_{n}}\rVert^{2}-\lVert u^{n}_{\gamma\wedge\tau^{M,T}_{n}}\rVert^{2}+\int_{\gamma\wedge\tau^{M,T}_{n}}^{(\gamma+\delta_{j})\wedge\tau^{M,T}_{n}}\lVert u^{n}_{s}\rVert_{1}^{2}ds\right]\leq\mathbbm{E}\left(C_{M}\delta_{j}+C_{M}\delta_{j}^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)

(42)

via the equivalence of the norms and a simple scaling with
\nu
. We now need to relate the expression on the left hand side with what we are interested in, that is
\lVert u^{n}\rVert_{UH,(\gamma+\delta_{j})\wedge\tau^{M,T}_{n}}^{2}-\lVert u^{n}\rVert_{UH,\gamma\wedge\tau^{M,T}_{n}}^{2}
. We have that

\displaystyle\lVert u^{n}\rVert_{UH,(\gamma+\delta_{j})\wedge\tau^{M,T}_{n}}^{2}

\displaystyle-\lVert u^{n}\rVert_{UH,\gamma\wedge\tau^{M,T}_{n}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sup_{s\in[0,(\gamma+\delta_{j})\wedge\tau^{M,T}_{n}]}\lVert u^{n}_{s}\rVert^{2}-\sup_{s\in[0,\gamma\wedge\tau^{M,T}_{n}]}\lVert u^{n}_{s}\rVert^{2}+\int_{\gamma\wedge\tau^{M,T}_{n}}^{(\gamma+\delta_{j})\wedge\tau^{M,T}_{n}}\lVert u^{n}_{s}\rVert_{1}^{2}ds.

and claim that

\sup_{s\in[0,(\gamma+\delta_{j})\wedge\tau^{M,T}_{n}]}\lVert u^{n}_{s}\rVert^{2}-\sup_{s\in[0,\gamma\wedge\tau^{M,T}_{n}]}\lVert u^{n}_{s}\rVert^{2}\leq\sup_{r\in[0,\delta_{j}]}\lVert u^{n}_{(\gamma+r)\wedge\tau^{M,T}_{n}}\rVert^{2}-\lVert u^{n}_{\gamma\wedge\tau^{M,T}_{n}}\rVert^{2}.

(43)

Indeed, we have that

\sup_{s\in[0,(\gamma+\delta_{j})\wedge\tau^{M,T}_{n}]}\lVert u^{n}_{s}\rVert^{2}\leq\sup_{s\in[0,\gamma\wedge\tau^{M,T}_{n}]}\lVert u^{n}_{s}\rVert^{2}+\sup_{s\in[\gamma\wedge\tau^{M,T}_{n},(\gamma+\delta_{j})\wedge\tau^{M,T}_{n}]}\lVert u^{n}_{s}\rVert^{2}-\lVert u^{n}_{\gamma\wedge\tau^{M,T}_{n}}\rVert^{2}

as the left hand side must equal either
\sup_{s\in[0,\gamma\wedge\tau^{M,T}_{n}]}\lVert u^{n}_{s}\rVert^{2}
or
\sup_{s\in[\gamma\wedge\tau^{M,T}_{n},(\gamma+\delta_{j})\wedge\tau^{M,T}_{n}]}\lVert u^{n}_{s}\rVert^{2}
, both of which are greater than the subtracted term
\lVert u^{n}_{\gamma\wedge\tau^{M,T}_{n}}\rVert^{2}
. Appreciating that

\sup_{s\in[\gamma\wedge\tau^{M,T}_{n},(\gamma+\delta_{j})\wedge\tau^{M,T}_{n}]}\lVert u^{n}_{s}\rVert^{2}=\sup_{r\in[0,\delta_{j}]}\lVert u^{n}_{(\gamma+r)\wedge\tau^{M,T}_{n}}\rVert^{2}

then yields the claim (
43
), which in combination with (
42
) grants that

\displaystyle\mathbbm{E}\left[\lVert u^{n}\rVert_{UH,(\gamma+\delta_{j})\wedge\tau^{M,T}_{n}}^{2}-\lVert u^{n}\rVert_{UH,\gamma\wedge\tau^{M,T}_{n}}^{2}\right]\leq\mathbbm{E}\left(C_{M}\delta_{j}+C_{M}\delta_{j}^{\frac{1}{2}}\right).

To conclude the proof we simply note that
C_{M}\delta_{j}+C_{M}\delta_{j}^{\frac{1}{2}}
is
\mathbbm{P}-a.s.
monotone decreasing (as
j\rightarrow\infty
) and convergent to
0
. The Monotone Convergence Theorem thus justifies that the limit as
j\rightarrow\infty
of the right hand side is zero, so the result is shown.

∎

Proposition 2.8
.

For any

(m_{j})

for all

\mathbb{P}-a.s.
.

Proof.

This comes from a direct application of Lemma
5.1
, for the Banach Space
X_{t}:=C\left([0,t];L^{2}_{\sigma}\right)\cap L^{2}\left([0,t];\bar{W}^{1,2}_{\sigma}\right)
equipped with norm
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{UH,t}
. The conditions (
52
) and (
53
) are given in Proposition
2.6
and Lemma
2.7
. The only component in need of proof is that the limiting process
\bm{\Psi}
we achieve from Lemma

is in fact
u
. We are given that
(u^{m_{j}})
converges to
\bm{\Psi}
in

which is a stronger convergence (up to

) than what we had in Theorem
2.1
, which was used to show that the limit process was a weak solution of the equation (

). Hence in the same manner as Proposition

, we see that

must be a

, so from the uniqueness of weak solutions we must have that

is in fact equal to

on

∎

This allows us to deduce the additional regularity required for

to be a strong solution.

Proposition 2.9
.

The process
u
is progressively measurable in
\bar{W}^{2,2}_{\alpha}
and such that for
\mathbb{P}-a.e.

\omega
,
u_{\cdot}(\omega)\in C\left([0,T];\bar{W}^{1,2}_{\sigma}\right)\cap L^{2}\left([0,T];\bar{W}^{2,2}_{\alpha}\right)
.

Proof.

Propositions
2.5
and
2.8
together imply that

\mathbbm{E}\left[\sup_{r\in[0,\tau^{R,T}]}\lVert u^{m_{j}}_{r}\rVert_{1}^{2}+\int_{0}^{\tau^{R,T}}\lVert u^{m_{j}}_{r}\rVert_{2}^{2}dr\right]\leq C_{R,\nu}

where
C_{R,\nu}
now incorporates
\lVert u_{0}\rVert^{2}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega;\bar{W}^{1,2}_{\sigma})}
and shows the dependency on
M
in terms of

. In consequence the sequence of processes
(u^{m_{j}}_{\cdot}\mathbbm{1}_{\cdot\leq\tau^{R,T}})
is uniformly bounded in both

and
L^{2}\left(\Omega;L^{2}\left([0,T];\bar{W}^{2,2}_{\alpha}\right)\right)
. We can deduce the existence of a subsequence which is weakly convergent in the Hilbert Space

, but we may also identify

and as such from the Banach-Alaoglu Theorem we can extract a further subsequence which is convergent to some
\tilde{\bm{\Phi}}
in the weak* topology. These limits imply convergence to both
\tilde{\bm{\Psi}}
and
\tilde{\bm{\Phi}}
in the weak topology of

. However, we know that
(u^{m_{j}}_{\cdot}\mathbbm{1}_{\cdot\leq\tau^{R,T}})

\mathbb{P}-a.s.

\mathbbm{P}\times\lambda-a.s.

\omega

(dependent on

. Therefore for
\mathbb{P}-a.e.

\omega

,

as the

.

It thus only remains to show that

\mathbb{P}-a.s.
. From Lemma
1.12
we have that

satisfies the identity (

\mathbb{P}-a.s.
, from which the continuity follows as an immediate application of Proposition

This concludes the proof that

, but the extension of a solution to a general

Verifying the Assumptions for SALT Noise

As mentioned in Subsection

, the majority of the assumptions were verified in [

(

We want to use the same approach as in [
44
] Lemma 2.7, where this bound was demonstrated on the torus. This requires a Green’s Identity type result in the
\lVert\mathcal{P}B_{i}\phi\rVert_{1}^{2}
term, for example Lemma
1.4
, though it is not clear how we can do this as
\mathcal{P}B_{i}\phi
does not necessarily satisfy the Navier boundary conditions (
4

1.7.1
, given that
\mathcal{P}B_{i}\phi\cdot\mathbf{n}
is zero on
\partial\mathscr{O}
, and Lemma
1.6
establishes that
\textnormal{curl}\left(\mathcal{P}B_{i}\phi\right)=\textnormal{curl}\left(B_{i}\phi\right)
. Inspecting this further,

\displaystyle\textnormal{curl}\left(B_{i}\phi\right)

\displaystyle=\partial_{1}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{2}\left[\xi_{i}^{j}\partial_{j}\phi^{2}+\phi^{j}\partial_{2}\xi_{i}^{j}\right]\right)-\partial_{2}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{2}\left[\xi_{i}^{j}\partial_{j}\phi^{1}+\phi^{j}\partial_{1}\xi_{i}^{j}\right]\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{2}\left(\partial_{1}\xi_{i}^{j}\partial_{j}\phi^{2}+\xi_{i}^{j}\partial_{1}\partial_{j}\phi^{2}+\partial_{1}\phi^{j}\partial_{2}\xi_{i}^{j}+\phi^{j}\partial_{1}\partial_{2}\xi_{i}^{j}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad-\sum_{j=1}^{2}\left(\partial_{2}\xi_{i}^{j}\partial_{j}\phi^{1}+\xi_{i}^{j}\partial_{2}\partial_{j}\phi^{1}+\partial_{2}\phi^{j}\partial_{1}\xi_{i}^{j}+\phi^{j}\partial_{2}\partial_{1}\xi_{i}^{j}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{2}\left(\partial_{1}\xi_{i}^{j}\partial_{j}\phi^{2}+\xi_{i}^{j}\partial_{1}\partial_{j}\phi^{2}+\partial_{1}\phi^{j}\partial_{2}\xi_{i}^{j}-\partial_{2}\xi_{i}^{j}\partial_{j}\phi^{1}-\xi_{i}^{j}\partial_{2}\partial_{j}\phi^{1}-\partial_{2}\phi^{j}\partial_{1}\xi_{i}^{j}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{2}\xi_{i}^{j}\partial_{j}\left(\partial_{1}\phi^{2}-\partial_{2}\phi^{1}\right)+\sum_{j=1}^{2}\left(\partial_{1}\xi_{i}^{j}\partial_{j}\phi^{2}+\partial_{1}\phi^{j}\partial_{2}\xi_{i}^{j}-\partial_{2}\xi_{i}^{j}\partial_{j}\phi^{1}-\partial_{2}\phi^{j}\partial_{1}\xi_{i}^{j}\right).

Observe that the first sum is simply
\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{i}}(\textnormal{curl}\phi)
, and to consider the second we expand out the summation:

\displaystyle\left(\partial_{1}\xi_{i}^{1}\partial_{1}\phi^{2}+\partial_{1}\phi^{1}\partial_{2}\xi_{i}^{1}-\partial_{2}\xi_{i}^{1}\partial_{1}\phi^{1}-\partial_{2}\phi^{1}\partial_{1}\xi_{i}^{1}\right)+\left(\partial_{1}\xi_{i}^{2}\partial_{2}\phi^{2}+\partial_{1}\phi^{2}\partial_{2}\xi_{i}^{2}-\partial_{2}\xi_{i}^{2}\partial_{2}\phi^{1}-\partial_{2}\phi^{2}\partial_{1}\xi_{i}^{2}\right)

for which the only remaining terms are

\partial_{1}\xi_{i}^{1}\partial_{1}\phi^{2}-\partial_{2}\phi^{1}\partial_{1}\xi_{i}^{1}+\partial_{1}\phi^{2}\partial_{2}\xi_{i}^{2}-\partial_{2}\xi_{i}^{2}\partial_{2}\phi^{1}

or simply

\left(\textnormal{curl}\phi\right)\sum_{j=1}^{2}\partial_{j}\xi_{i}^{j}

which is zero given that
\xi_{i}
is divergence-free. In summary then we have that
\textnormal{curl}\left(\mathcal{P}B_{i}\phi\right)=\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{i}}(\textnormal{curl}\phi)
. The fact that this term belongs to
W^{1,2}_{0}(\mathscr{O};\mathbb{R}^{2})
for

was shown in [
44
] pp.32. In particular the tangential component at the boundary is zero, so by Corollary
1.7.1
then

satisfies the boundary condition

In particular
\mathcal{P}B_{i}\phi
belongs to
\bar{W}^{2,2}_{2\kappa}
, so from Lemma
1.4

\lVert\mathcal{P}B_{i}\phi\rVert_{1}^{2}=-\langle\mathcal{P}B_{i}\phi,\Delta\mathcal{P}B_{i}\phi\rangle-\langle\kappa\mathcal{P}B_{i}\phi,\mathcal{P}B_{i}\phi\rangle.

Returning to what we have to show, (
17

\langle B_{i}^{2}\phi,A\phi\rangle-\langle\mathcal{P}B_{i}\phi,\Delta\mathcal{P}B_{i}\phi\rangle-\langle\kappa\mathcal{P}B_{i}\phi,\mathcal{P}B_{i}\phi\rangle_{L^{2}(\partial\mathscr{O};\mathbb{R}^{2})}

or equivalently

(44)

for any

\displaystyle\left|\langle\kappa\mathcal{P}B_{i}\phi,\mathcal{P}B_{i}\phi\rangle_{L^{2}(\partial\mathscr{O};\mathbb{R}^{2})}\right|

\displaystyle\leq c\lVert\mathcal{P}B_{i}\phi\rVert\lVert\mathcal{P}B_{i}\phi\rVert_{1}

\displaystyle\leq c\lVert\xi_{i}\rVert^{2}_{W^{2,\infty}}\lVert\phi\rVert_{1}\lVert\phi\rVert_{2}

where the bound on

1.4

\mathcal{P}
, then in particular

\nu

\nu\rightarrow 0
. To achieve this we shall consider the vorticity of
u
, and denote again
w:=\textnormal{curl}u
. Recall that in Subsection
2.3
we showed that
\textnormal{curl}(B_{i}f)=\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{i}}(\textnormal{curl}f)
. We denote
\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{i}}
by the simple
\mathcal{L}_{i}.

Lemma 3.1
.

The process
w
is progressively measurable in
W^{1,2}_{0}(\mathscr{O};\mathbb{R})
and such that for
\mathbb{P}-a.e.

\omega
,
w_{\cdot}(\omega)\in C\left([0,T];L^{2}(\mathscr{O};\mathbb{R})\right)\cap L^{2}\left([0,T];W^{1,2}_{0}(\mathscr{O};\mathbb{R})\right)
. Moreover
w
satisfies the identity

\displaystyle\langle w_{t},\phi\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathscr{O};\mathbb{R})}

\displaystyle=\langle w_{0},\phi\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathscr{O};\mathbb{R})}-\int_{0}^{t}\langle\mathcal{L}_{u_{s}}w_{s},\phi\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathscr{O};\mathbb{R})}ds-\nu\int_{0}^{t}\langle w_{s},\phi\rangle_{W^{1,2}_{0}(\mathscr{O};\mathbb{R})}ds

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\langle\mathcal{L}_{i}w_{s},\mathcal{L}_{i}\phi\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathscr{O};\mathbb{R})}ds-\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{t}\langle\mathcal{L}_{i}w_{s},\phi\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathscr{O};\mathbb{R})}dW^{i}_{s}

(46)

for every
\phi\in W^{1,2}_{0}(\mathscr{O};\mathbb{R})
,
\mathbb{P}-a.s.
in
\mathbb{R}
for all
t\in[0,T]
.

Proof.

We first comment on the regularity of
w
, noting that the curl is a continuous linear operator from
W^{2,2}(\mathscr{O};\mathbb{R}^{2})\rightarrow W^{1,2}(\mathscr{O};\mathbb{R})
and
W^{1,2}(\mathscr{O};\mathbb{R}^{2})\rightarrow L^{2}(\mathscr{O};\mathbb{R})
. This justifies the progressive measurability and pathwise regularity for the general
W^{1,2}(\mathscr{O};\mathbb{R})
space, whilst the fact that
w
belongs to
W^{1,2}_{0}(\mathscr{O};\mathbb{R})
is a direct consequence of Corollary
1.7.1
in the choice of
\alpha=2\kappa
. It only remains to show the identity (
46
), which we look to do by first considering
\phi\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathscr{O};\mathbb{R})
and defining
\psi\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathscr{O};\mathbb{R}^{2})
by
\psi^{1}:=\partial_{2}\phi
,
\psi^{2}:=-\partial_{1}\phi
. The similarity with the curl is swiftly noted, and the idea is to take the inner product of (
28
) with
\psi
and pass over the ‘curl’ through integration by parts. Proceeding with this,
u
satisfies the identity

\displaystyle\langle u_{t},\psi\rangle=\langle u_{0},\psi\rangle-\int_{0}^{t}\langle\mathcal{P}\mathcal{L}_{u_{s}}u_{s},\psi\rangle ds

\displaystyle-\nu\int_{0}^{t}\langle Au_{s},\psi\rangle ds

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\langle\mathcal{P}B_{i}^{2}u_{s},\psi\rangle ds-\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{t}\langle\mathcal{P}B_{i}u_{s},\psi\rangle dW^{i}_{s}

and we look to simplify each term down to its respective one in (
46
). Expanding the first inner product into its components,

\langle u_{t},\psi\rangle=\langle u^{1}_{t},\partial_{2}\phi\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathscr{O};\mathbb{R})}-\langle u^{2}_{t},\partial_{1}\phi\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathscr{O};\mathbb{R})}=-\langle\partial_{2}u^{1}_{t},\phi\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathscr{O};\mathbb{R})}+\langle\partial_{1}u^{2}_{t},\phi\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathscr{O};\mathbb{R})}=\langle w_{t},\phi\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathscr{O};\mathbb{R})}

having used that
\phi
is compactly supported. The same integration by parts process will be used for the remaining terms. There is nothing to note for the initial condition, so moving on to the nonlinear term, we recall the continuity of
\mathcal{L}:W^{2,2}(\mathscr{O};\mathbb{R}^{2})\rightarrow W^{1,2}(\mathscr{O};\mathbb{R}^{2})
, so that
\mathcal{P}\mathcal{L}_{u_{s}}u_{s}\in W^{1,2}(\mathscr{O};\mathbb{R}^{2})
hence
\textnormal{curl}\left(\mathcal{P}\mathcal{L}_{u_{s}}u_{s}\right)\in L^{2}(\mathscr{O};\mathbb{R})
. We use Lemma
1.6
to remove
\mathcal{P}
from consideration, from which the well established result that
\textnormal{curl}\left(\mathcal{L}_{u_{s}}u_{s}\right)=\mathcal{L}_{u_{s}}w_{s}
(see e.g. [
91
] Subsection 12.4) provides the required expression. We have thus far achieved that

\displaystyle\langle w_{t},\phi\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathscr{O};\mathbb{R})}=\langle w_{0},\phi\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathscr{O};\mathbb{R})}

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{t}\langle\mathcal{L}_{u_{s}}w_{s},\phi\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathscr{O};\mathbb{R})}ds-\nu\int_{0}^{t}\langle Au_{s},\psi\rangle ds

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\langle\mathcal{P}B_{i}^{2}u_{s},\psi\rangle ds-\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{t}\langle\mathcal{P}B_{i}u_{s},\psi\rangle dW^{i}_{s}.

For the stochastic integral there is nothing to prove given that
\textnormal{curl}(\mathcal{P}B_{i}u_{s})=\mathcal{L}_{i}w_{s}
was established in Subsection
2.3
. For the remaining terms we do not have sufficient regularity to conduct the integration by parts. To account for this we take an approximation of
u_{s}
by functions
(f^{n})
,
f^{n}\in W^{3,2}(\mathscr{O};\mathbb{R}^{2})
and
f^{n}\longrightarrow u_{s}
in
W^{2,2}(\mathscr{O};\mathbb{R}^{2})
. Firstly for the Stokes Operator, we have that

\displaystyle\langle Af^{n},\psi\rangle=\langle\textnormal{curl}\left(Af^{n}\right),\phi\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathscr{O};\mathbb{R})}=-\langle\textnormal{curl}\left(\Delta f^{n}\right),\phi\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathscr{O};\mathbb{R})}

\displaystyle=-\langle\Delta\textnormal{curl}\left(f^{n}\right),\phi\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathscr{O};\mathbb{R})}

\displaystyle=\langle\textnormal{curl}\left(f^{n}\right),\phi\rangle_{W^{1,2}_{0}(\mathscr{O};\mathbb{R})}

hence

\langle Au_{s},\psi\rangle=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\langle Af^{n},\psi\rangle=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\langle\textnormal{curl}\left(f^{n}\right),\phi\rangle_{W^{1,2}_{0}(\mathscr{O};\mathbb{R})}=\langle w_{s},\phi\rangle_{W^{1,2}_{0}(\mathscr{O};\mathbb{R})}.

Similarly we have that
\textnormal{curl}\left(\mathcal{P}B_{i}^{2}f^{n}\right)=\mathcal{L}_{i}\left[\textnormal{curl}\left(B_{i}f^{n}\right)\right]=\mathcal{L}_{i}^{2}\left[\textnormal{curl}(f^{n})\right]
. Thus

\langle\mathcal{P}B_{i}^{2}f^{n},\psi\rangle=\langle\mathcal{L}_{i}^{2}[\textnormal{curl}(f^{n})],\phi\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathscr{O};\mathbb{R})}=-\langle\mathcal{L}_{i}[\textnormal{curl}(f^{n})],\mathcal{L}_{i}\phi\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathscr{O};\mathbb{R})}

having used that the
L^{2}(\mathscr{O};\mathbb{R})
adjoint of the densely defined
\mathcal{L}_{i}
is
-\mathcal{L}_{i}
(which follows from Lemma
1.9
in the one dimensional case too). Via the same approximation just used, we conclude that the identity (
46
) holds for
\phi\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathscr{O};\mathbb{R})
. We use the density of this space in
W^{1,2}_{0}(\mathscr{O};\mathbb{R})
to finish the proof.
∎

We shall use this identity to consider estimates on the vorticity, which then translate to estimates on the velocity.

Proposition 3.2
.

For any
t\in[0,T]
,
w
satisfies the identity

\lVert w_{t}\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathscr{O};\mathbb{R})}^{2}+2\nu\int_{0}^{t}\lVert w_{s}\rVert_{W^{1,2}_{0}(\mathscr{O};\mathbb{R})}^{2}ds=\lVert w_{0}\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathscr{O};\mathbb{R})}^{2}

\mathbb{P}-a.s.
.

Proof.

Just as we required in Proposition
2.3
, energy methods demand that the weak solution satisfies an identity in
\left(W^{1,2}_{0}(\mathscr{O};\mathbb{R})\right)^{*}
to then apply Proposition
5.9
. The nonlinear term enjoys better regularity here as opposed to the weak solution considered in Proposition
2.3
, given that
u
has additional regularity over
w
. In the same manner we deduce that
w
satisfies, for every
t\in[0,T]
, the identity

w_{t}=w_{0}-\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{L}_{u_{s}}w_{s}\ ds+\nu\int_{0}^{t}\Delta w_{s}\,ds+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\mathcal{L}_{i}^{2}w_{s}ds-\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{L}_{i}w_{s}dW^{i}_{s}

\mathbb{P}-a.s.
in
\left(W^{1,2}_{0}(\mathscr{O};\mathbb{R})\right)^{*}
, with sufficient regularity to apply Proposition
5.9
for the spaces
\mathcal{H}_{1}:=W^{1,2}_{0}(\mathscr{O};\mathbb{R})
,
\mathcal{H}_{2}:=L^{2}(\mathscr{O};\mathbb{R})
and
\mathcal{H}_{3}:=\left(W^{1,2}_{0}(\mathscr{O};\mathbb{R})\right)^{*}
. Therefore
w
satisfies

\displaystyle\lVert w_{t}\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathscr{O};\mathbb{R})}^{2}=\lVert w_{0}\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathscr{O};\mathbb{R})}^{2}

\displaystyle-2\int_{0}^{t}\langle\mathcal{L}_{u_{s}}w_{s},w_{s}\rangle_{\left(W^{1,2}_{0}(\mathscr{O};\mathbb{R})\right)^{*}\times W^{1,2}_{0}(\mathscr{O};\mathbb{R})}ds

\displaystyle+2\nu\int_{0}^{t}\langle\Delta w_{s},w_{s}\rangle_{\left(W^{1,2}_{0}(\mathscr{O};\mathbb{R})\right)^{*}\times W^{1,2}_{0}(\mathscr{O};\mathbb{R})}ds

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\left(\langle\mathcal{L}_{i}^{2}w_{s},w_{s}\rangle_{\left(W^{1,2}_{0}(\mathscr{O};\mathbb{R})\right)^{*}\times W^{1,2}_{0}(\mathscr{O};\mathbb{R})}+\lVert\mathcal{L}_{i}w_{s}\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathscr{O};\mathbb{R})}\right)ds

\displaystyle-2\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{t}\langle\mathcal{L}_{i}w_{s},w_{s}\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathscr{O};\mathbb{R})}dW^{i}_{s}.

(47)

By definition

\langle\mathcal{L}_{u_{s}}w_{s},w_{s}\rangle_{\left(W^{1,2}_{0}(\mathscr{O};\mathbb{R})\right)^{*}\times W^{1,2}_{0}(\mathscr{O};\mathbb{R})}=\langle\mathcal{L}_{u_{s}}w_{s},w_{s}\rangle_{L^{4/3}(\mathscr{O};\mathbb{R})\times L^{4}(\mathscr{O};\mathbb{R})}

which is simply zero again from Lemma
1.9
, whilst

\langle\Delta w_{s},w_{s}\rangle_{\left(W^{1,2}_{0}(\mathscr{O};\mathbb{R})\right)^{*}\times W^{1,2}_{0}(\mathscr{O};\mathbb{R})}=-\lVert w_{s}\rVert_{W^{1,2}_{0}(\mathscr{O};\mathbb{R})}^{2}

is also directly from the definition. Similarly

\langle\mathcal{L}_{i}^{2}w_{s},w_{s}\rangle_{\left(W^{1,2}_{0}(\mathscr{O};\mathbb{R})\right)^{*}\times W^{1,2}_{0}(\mathscr{O};\mathbb{R})}=-\langle\mathcal{L}_{i}w_{s},\mathcal{L}_{i}w_{s}\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathscr{O};\mathbb{R})}=-\lVert\mathcal{L}_{i}w_{s}\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathscr{O};\mathbb{R})}

hence

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{L}_{i}^{2}w_{s},w_{s}\rangle_{\left(W^{1,2}_{0}(\mathscr{O};\mathbb{R})\right)^{*}\times W^{1,2}_{0}(\mathscr{O};\mathbb{R})}+\lVert\mathcal{L}_{i}w_{s}\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathscr{O};\mathbb{R})}=0

whilst we also have that

\langle\mathcal{L}_{i}w_{s},w_{s}\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathscr{O};\mathbb{R})}=0

again from Lemma
1.9
. Putting this all back into (
47
) gives the result.
∎

3.2
Tightness

We now consider a sequence of viscosities
(\nu^{k})
monotonically decreasing to zero as in Theorem
1.17
, and corresponding strong solutions of (
25
) for
\alpha:=2\kappa
given by
u^{k}
. Without loss of generality we assume
\nu^{k}<1
for all
k
.

Proposition 3.3
.

For any sequence of stopping times
(\gamma_{n})
with
\gamma_{n}:\Omega\rightarrow[0,T]
, and any
\phi\in\bar{W}^{1,2}_{\sigma}
,

\lim_{\delta\rightarrow 0^{+}}\sup_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\mathbbm{E}\left(\left|\langle u^{k}_{(\gamma_{k}+\delta)\wedge T}-u^{k}_{\gamma_{k}},\phi\rangle\right|\right)=0.

Proof.

We use the identity satisfied by the inner product, which is

\displaystyle\langle u^{k}_{(\gamma_{k}+\delta)\wedge T}-u^{k}_{\gamma_{k}},\phi\rangle

\displaystyle=-\int_{\gamma_{k}}^{(\gamma_{k}+\delta)\wedge T}\langle\mathcal{L}_{u^{k}_{s}}u^{k}_{s},\phi\rangle ds-\nu^{k}\int_{\gamma_{k}}^{(\gamma_{k}+\delta)\wedge T}\langle Au^{k}_{s},\phi\rangle ds

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\gamma_{k}}^{(\gamma_{k}+\delta)\wedge T}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\langle B_{i}^{2}u^{k}_{s},\phi\rangle ds-\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\int_{\gamma_{k}}^{(\gamma_{k}+\delta)\wedge T}\langle B_{i}u^{k}_{s},\phi\rangle dW^{i}_{s}.

having immediately passed
\mathcal{P}
over to
\phi
. Inspecting each term, from Lemma
1.8
we have that

\left|\langle\mathcal{L}_{u^{k}_{s}}u^{k}_{s},\phi\rangle\right|\leq c\lVert u^{k}_{s}\rVert^{\frac{1}{2}}\lVert u^{k}_{s}\rVert_{1}^{\frac{3}{2}}\lVert\phi\rVert_{1}\leq c\lVert w_{0}\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathscr{O};\mathbb{R})}^{2}

where we have used Corollary
3.2.1
and will continue to absorb norms of
\phi
into our constant. Similarly we have that
\langle Au^{k}_{s},\phi\rangle=\langle u^{k}_{s},\phi\rangle_{H}
and as
\nu^{k}<1
,

\nu^{k}\left|\langle Au^{k}_{s},\phi\rangle\right|\leq c\lVert u^{k}_{s}\rVert_{1}\lVert\phi\rVert_{1}\leq c\lVert w_{0}\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathscr{O};\mathbb{R})}

and using that
\langle B_{i}^{2}u^{k}_{s},\phi\rangle=\langle B_{i}u^{k}_{s},B_{i}^{*}\phi\rangle
we achieve the same bound there. Using that
\lVert w_{0}\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathscr{O};\mathbb{R})}\leq 1+\lVert w_{0}\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathscr{O};\mathbb{R})}^{2}
, we have so far shown that

\displaystyle\left|\langle u^{k}_{(\gamma_{k}+\delta)\wedge T}-u^{k}_{\gamma_{k}},\phi\rangle\right|\leq\int_{\gamma_{k}}^{(\gamma_{k}+\delta)\wedge T}c(1+\lVert w_{0}\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathscr{O};\mathbb{R})}^{2})ds+\left|\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\int_{\gamma_{k}}^{(\gamma_{k}+\delta)\wedge T}\langle B_{i}u^{k}_{s},\phi\rangle dW^{i}_{s}\right|.

Taking expectation and applying the Burkholder-Davis-Gundy Inequality, observe firstly that

\displaystyle\mathbbm{E}\left(\left|\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\int_{\gamma_{k}}^{(\gamma_{k}+\delta)\wedge T}\langle B_{i}u^{k}_{s},\phi\rangle dW^{i}_{s}\right|\right)

\displaystyle\leq c\mathbbm{E}\left(\int_{\gamma_{k}}^{(\gamma_{k}+\delta)\wedge T}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\langle B_{i}u^{k}_{s},\phi\rangle^{2}ds\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq c\mathbbm{E}\left(\int_{\gamma_{k}}^{(\gamma_{k}+\delta)\wedge T}\lVert w_{0}\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathscr{O};\mathbb{R})}^{2}ds\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq c\left[\mathbbm{E}\int_{\gamma_{k}}^{(\gamma_{k}+\delta)\wedge T}\lVert w_{0}\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathscr{O};\mathbb{R})}^{2}ds\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}

Using
\delta
as an upper bound for the length of the integrals, we have that

\mathbbm{E}\left(\left|\langle u^{k}_{(\gamma_{k}+\delta)\wedge T}-u^{k}_{\gamma_{k}},\phi\rangle\right|\right)\leq c\delta\mathbbm{E}\left(\lVert w_{0}\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathscr{O};\mathbb{R})}^{2}\right)+c\left[\delta\mathbbm{E}\left(\lVert w_{0}\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathscr{O};\mathbb{R})}^{2}\right)\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}

where the result now follows from the fact that
u_{0}\in L^{2}\left(\Omega;\bar{W}^{1,2}_{\sigma}\right)
implies
w_{0}\in L^{2}\left(\Omega;L^{2}(\mathscr{O};\mathbb{R})\right)
.
∎

Corollary 3.3.1
.

The sequence of the laws of
(u^{k})
is tight in the space of probability measures over
\mathcal{D}\left([0,T];L^{2}_{\sigma}\right)
.

Proof.

We apply Lemma
5.10
in the case of
V:=\mathcal{H}_{1}:=\bar{W}^{1,2}_{\sigma}
and
\mathcal{H}_{2}:=L^{2}_{\sigma}
. Condition (
85
) is satisfied from Corollary
3.2.1
. As for (
86
), we use Chebyshev’s Inequality to see that

\mathbbm{P}\left(\left\{\omega\in\Omega:\left|\left\langle u^{k}_{(\gamma_{k}+\delta)\wedge T}-u^{k}_{\gamma_{k}},\phi\right\rangle\right|>\varepsilon\right\}\right)\leq\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\mathbbm{E}\left(\left|\langle u^{k}_{(\gamma_{k}+\delta)\wedge T}-u^{k}_{\gamma_{k}},\phi\rangle\right|\right)

from which the condition follows courtesy of Proposition
3.3
.
∎

3.3
Passage to the Stochastic Euler Equation

Using the uniform boundedness from Corollary
3.2.1
and the tightness due to Corollary
3.3.1
, as well as the progressive measurability of the processes
(u^{k})
in
\bar{W}^{2,2}_{\alpha}

Theorem 3.4
.

There exists a filtered probability space
\left(\tilde{\Omega},\tilde{\mathcal{F}},(\tilde{\mathcal{F}}_{t}),\tilde{\mathbb{P}}\right)
, a cylindrical Brownian Motion
\tilde{\mathcal{W}}
over
\mathfrak{U}
with respect to
\left(\tilde{\Omega},\tilde{\mathcal{F}},(\tilde{\mathcal{F}}_{t}),\tilde{\mathbb{P}}\right)

\tilde{u}_{0}:\tilde{\Omega}\rightarrow\bar{W}^{1,2}_{\sigma}
, a sequence of processes
(\tilde{u}^{k})
,
\tilde{u}^{k}:\tilde{\Omega}\times[0,T]\rightarrow\bar{W}^{2,2}_{\alpha}
is progressively measurable and a progressively measurable process
\tilde{u}:\tilde{\Omega}\times[0,T]\rightarrow\bar{W}^{1,2}_{\sigma}
such that:

1.

\tilde{u}_{0}
has the same law as
u_{0}
;

2.

For each
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
t\in[0,T]
,
\tilde{u}^{k}
satisfies the identity

\tilde{u}^{k}_{t}=\tilde{u}_{0}-\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{P}\mathcal{L}_{\tilde{u}^{k}_{s}}\tilde{u}^{k}_{s}\ ds-\nu^{k}\int_{0}^{t}A\tilde{u}^{k}_{s}\,ds+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\mathcal{P}B_{i}^{2}\tilde{u}^{k}_{s}ds-\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{P}B\tilde{u}^{k}_{s}d\tilde{\mathcal{W}}_{s}

\tilde{\mathbb{P}}-a.s.
in
L^{2}_{\sigma}
;

3.

For
\tilde{\mathbb{P}}-a.e

\omega
,
\tilde{u}^{k}(\omega)\rightarrow\tilde{u}(\omega)
in
\mathcal{D}\left([0,T];L^{2}_{\sigma}\right)
;

4.

For
\tilde{\mathbb{P}}-a.e

\omega
,
\tilde{u}(\omega)\in L^{\infty}\left([0,T];\bar{W}^{1,2}_{\sigma}\right)
.

In fact we can immediately upgrade the convergence in item
3
to that in

\mathcal{D}\left([0,T];L^{2}_{\sigma}\right)

56

\tilde{\mathbb{P}}-a.e

\omega
, the convergent sequence

in
\mathcal{D}\left([0,T];L^{2}_{\sigma}\right)
is Cauchy in this space, hence too in

which is of course equal to that in

\tilde{u}\in C\left([0,T];L^{2}_{\sigma}\right)

\mathbbm{P}-a.s.
and has the necessary regularity to be a martingale weak solution of the equation (
26

\tilde{u}^{k}
is a strong solution of the equation (

satisfies the identity (

Proposition 3.5
.

The process
\tilde{u}
satisfies the identity (
29
): that is

\displaystyle\langle\tilde{u}_{t},\phi\rangle=\langle\tilde{u}_{0},\phi\rangle-\int_{0}^{t}\langle\mathcal{L}_{\tilde{u}_{s}}\tilde{u}_{s},\phi\rangle ds+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\langle B_{i}\tilde{u}_{s},B_{i}^{*}\phi\rangle ds-\int_{0}^{t}\langle B\tilde{u}_{s},\phi\rangle d\tilde{\mathcal{W}}_{s}

holds for every
\phi\in\bar{W}^{1,2}_{\sigma}

\tilde{\mathbb{P}}-a.s.
in
\mathbb{R}
for all
t\in[0,T]
.

Proof.

We fix arbitrary
\psi\in\bar{W}^{2,2}_{\alpha}
and
t\in[0,T]
, with the aim to show the result for
\psi
and pass to the limit for any
\phi\in\bar{W}^{1,2}_{\sigma}
. Taking the inner product with
\psi
in item
2
of Theorem
3.4
, we have that

\displaystyle\langle\tilde{u}^{k}_{t},\psi\rangle=\langle\tilde{u}_{0},\psi\rangle-\int_{0}^{t}\langle\mathcal{L}_{\tilde{u}^{k}_{s}}\tilde{u}^{k}_{s},\psi\rangle\ ds

\displaystyle-\nu^{k}\int_{0}^{t}\langle A\tilde{u}^{k}_{s},\psi\rangle\,ds

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\langle B_{i}^{2}\tilde{u}^{k}_{s},\psi\rangle ds-\int_{0}^{t}\langle B\tilde{u}^{k}_{s},\psi\rangle d\tilde{\mathcal{W}}_{s}

holds
\tilde{\mathbbm{P}}-a.s.
in
\mathbb{R}
, so we look to show the convergence of each term in turn
\tilde{\mathbb{P}}-a.s.
. It is immediate that
\langle\tilde{u}^{k}_{t},\psi\rangle
converges to
\langle\tilde{u}_{t},\psi\rangle
from the convergence in
C\left([0,T];L^{2}_{\sigma}\right)
. For the nonlinear term, we have that

\displaystyle\left|\langle\mathcal{L}_{\tilde{u}^{k}_{s}}\tilde{u}^{k}_{s},\psi\rangle-\langle\mathcal{L}_{\tilde{u}_{s}}\tilde{u}_{s},\psi\rangle\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\langle\mathcal{L}_{\tilde{u}^{k}_{s}-\tilde{u}_{s}}\tilde{u}^{k}_{s}+\mathcal{L}_{\tilde{u}_{s}}(\tilde{u}^{k}_{s}-\tilde{u}_{s}),\psi\rangle\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\langle\mathcal{L}_{\tilde{u}^{k}_{s}-\tilde{u}_{s}}\tilde{u}^{k}_{s},\psi\rangle\right|+\left|\langle\mathcal{L}_{\tilde{u}_{s}}(\tilde{u}^{k}_{s}-\tilde{u}_{s}),\psi\rangle\right|.

We treat both cases with Lemma
1.9
and Hölder’s Inequality:

\displaystyle\left|\langle\mathcal{L}_{\tilde{u}^{k}_{s}-\tilde{u}_{s}}\tilde{u}^{k}_{s},\psi\rangle\right|=\left|\langle\tilde{u}^{k}_{s},\mathcal{L}_{\tilde{u}^{k}_{s}-\tilde{u}_{s}}\psi\rangle\right|\leq\lVert\tilde{u}^{k}_{s}\rVert_{L^{6}}\lVert\mathcal{L}_{\tilde{u}^{k}_{s}-\tilde{u}_{s}}\psi\rVert_{L^{6/5}}

\displaystyle\leq c\sum_{l=1}^{2}\lVert\tilde{u}^{k}_{s}\rVert_{L^{6}}\lVert\tilde{u}^{k}_{s}-\tilde{u}_{s}\rVert\lVert\partial_{l}\psi\rVert_{L^{3}}

\displaystyle\leq c\lVert\tilde{u}^{k}_{s}\rVert_{1}\lVert\tilde{u}^{k}_{s}-\tilde{u}_{s}\rVert\lVert\psi\rVert_{W^{2,2}}

whilst

\displaystyle\left|\langle\mathcal{L}_{\tilde{u}_{s}}(\tilde{u}^{k}_{s}-\tilde{u}_{s}),\psi\rangle\right|=\left|\langle\tilde{u}^{k}_{s}-\tilde{u}_{s},\mathcal{L}_{\tilde{u}_{s}}\psi\rangle\right|

\displaystyle\leq c\sum_{l=1}^{2}\lVert\tilde{u}^{k}_{s}-\tilde{u}_{s}\rVert\lVert\tilde{u}_{s}\rVert_{L^{4}}\lVert\partial_{l}\psi\rVert_{L^{4}}

\displaystyle\leq c\lVert\tilde{u}^{k}_{s}-\tilde{u}_{s}\rVert\lVert\tilde{u}_{s}\rVert_{1}\lVert\psi\rVert_{W^{2,2}}

We now comment on the regularity of
\tilde{u}^{k}
; the estimates shown in Subsection
3.1
were done so for the unique strong solution of the equation (
25
) on the original probability space, though are identically obtained for the unique strong solution on this new probability space. Furthermore from Corollary
3.2.1
we have that there exists a constant
C
such that for every
k
and
\tilde{\mathbb{P}}-a.e.

\omega\in\tilde{\Omega}
,

\sup_{r\in[0,T]}\lVert\tilde{u}^{k}_{r}(\omega)\rVert_{1}^{2}\leq C\lVert\tilde{w}_{0}(\omega)\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathscr{O};\mathbb{R})}^{2}

where
\tilde{w}_{0}
is
\textnormal{curl}\tilde{u}_{0}
. With the same type of argument that we saw in Proposition
2.9
, taking a weak* convergent subsequence in
L^{\infty}\left([0,T];\bar{W}^{1,2}_{\sigma}\right)
and using Banach-Alaoglu, one also has that

\sup_{r\in[0,T]}\lVert\tilde{u}_{r}(\omega)\rVert_{1}^{2}\leq C\lVert\tilde{w}_{0}(\omega)\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathscr{O};\mathbb{R})}^{2}.

As
\tilde{u}_{0}
has the same law as
u_{0}
in
\bar{W}^{1,2}_{\sigma}
then
\tilde{u}_{0}\in L^{2}\left(\tilde{\Omega};\bar{W}^{1,2}_{\sigma}\right)
so
\tilde{w}_{0}\in L^{2}\left(\tilde{\Omega};L^{2}(\mathscr{O};\mathbb{R})\right)
. One therefore obtains that

\displaystyle\left|\int_{0}^{t}\langle\mathcal{L}_{\tilde{u}^{k}_{s}}\tilde{u}^{k}_{s},\psi\rangle ds-\int_{0}^{t}\langle\mathcal{L}_{\tilde{u}_{s}}\tilde{u}_{s},\psi\rangle ds\right|

\displaystyle\leq c\int_{0}^{t}\left(\lVert\tilde{u}^{k}_{s}\rVert_{1}+\lVert\tilde{u}_{s}\rVert_{1}\right)\lVert\tilde{u}^{k}_{s}-\tilde{u}_{s}\rVert\lVert\psi\rVert_{W^{2,2}}

\displaystyle\leq c\lVert\tilde{w}_{0}\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathscr{O};\mathbb{R})}\lVert\psi\rVert_{W^{2,2}}\int_{0}^{t}\lVert\tilde{u}^{k}_{s}-\tilde{u}_{s}\rVert ds

which we know to converges to zero
\tilde{\mathbb{P}}-a.s.
, again using the convergence in
C\left([0,T];L^{2}_{\sigma}\right)
. For the Stokes Operator term we have that

\left|\langle A\tilde{u}^{k}_{s},\psi\rangle\right|=\left|\langle\tilde{u}^{k}_{s},\psi\rangle_{H}\right|\leq c\lVert\tilde{u}^{k}_{s}\rVert_{1}\lVert\psi\rVert_{1}\leq c\lVert\tilde{w}_{0}\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathscr{O};\mathbb{R})}\lVert\psi\rVert_{1}

thus

\left|\nu^{k}\int_{0}^{t}\langle A\tilde{u}^{k}_{s},\psi\rangle\,ds\right|\leq ct\lVert\tilde{w}_{0}\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathscr{O};\mathbb{R})}\lVert\psi\rVert_{1}\nu^{k}

which is convergent to zero
\tilde{\mathbb{P}}-a.s.
. Next observe that

\displaystyle\left|\langle B_{i}^{2}\tilde{u}^{k}_{s},\psi\rangle-\langle B_{i}^{2}\tilde{u}_{s},\psi\rangle\right|=\left|\langle B_{i}^{2}(\tilde{u}^{k}_{s}-\tilde{u}_{s}),\psi\rangle\right|=\left|\langle\tilde{u}^{k}_{s}-\tilde{u}_{s},(B_{i}^{*})^{2}\psi\rangle\right|\leq c_{i}\lVert\tilde{u}^{k}_{s}-\tilde{u}_{s}\rVert\lVert\psi\rVert_{W^{2,2}}

from which we again see the desired convergence in this term. In the stochastic integral we have to pass to a subsequence. Indeed we can only show the convergence in expectation; to this end we have that

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathbbm{E}}\left(\left|\int_{0}^{t}\langle B\tilde{u}^{k}_{s},\psi\rangle d\tilde{\mathcal{W}}_{s}-\int_{0}^{t}\langle B\tilde{u}_{s},\psi\rangle d\tilde{\mathcal{W}}_{s}\right|\right)

\displaystyle\leq c\tilde{\mathbbm{E}}\left(\int_{0}^{t}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\langle B_{i}(\tilde{u}^{k}_{s}-\tilde{u}_{s}),\psi\rangle^{2}ds\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq c\tilde{\mathbbm{E}}\left(\int_{0}^{t}\lVert\tilde{u}^{k}_{s}-\tilde{u}_{s}\rVert^{2}\lVert\psi\rVert_{1}^{2}ds\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq c\lVert\psi\rVert_{1}\left[\tilde{\mathbbm{E}}\left(\int_{0}^{t}\lVert\tilde{u}^{k}_{s}-\tilde{u}_{s}\rVert^{2}ds\right)\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}.

The fact that this quantity approaches zero as
k\rightarrow\infty
now follows from the Dominated Convergence Theorem. Thus we can take a subsequence, indexed by
(k_{j})
, such that

\int_{0}^{t}\langle B\tilde{u}^{k_{j}}_{s},\psi\rangle d\tilde{\mathcal{W}}_{s}\longrightarrow\int_{0}^{t}\langle B\tilde{u}_{s},\psi\rangle d\tilde{\mathcal{W}}_{s}

\tilde{\mathbb{P}}-a.s.
. Moreover, taking the
\tilde{\mathbb{P}}-a.s.
convergence along this subsequence for all terms, we realise that
\tilde{u}
satisfies

\displaystyle\langle\tilde{u}_{t},\psi\rangle=\langle\tilde{u}_{0},\psi\rangle-\int_{0}^{t}\langle\mathcal{L}_{\tilde{u}_{s}}\tilde{u}_{s},\psi\rangle ds+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\langle B_{i}\tilde{u}_{s},B_{i}^{*}\psi\rangle ds-\int_{0}^{t}\langle B\tilde{u}_{s},\psi\rangle d\tilde{\mathcal{W}}_{s}

\tilde{\mathbb{P}}-a.s.
in
\mathbb{R}
. It only remains to show this identity for
\phi\in\bar{W}^{1,2}_{\sigma}
, for which we choose a sequence of such
\psi
approximating
\phi
in
\bar{W}^{1,2}_{\sigma}
. The convergence in all terms other than the stochastic integral is clear, whilst for the stochastic integral we know it only for a subsequence using a Stochastic Dominated Convergence Theorem (e.g. [
43
] Lemma 1.6.15), much like what we have just demonstrated. This completes the proof.
∎

4
Conclusion

We now address some key questions surrounding this paper and its context in the literature, as mentioned in the introduction.

•

How do the Navier boundary conditions solve the problems faced by the no-slip condition for the existence of strong solutions?

The details are of course implicit in the proof of Theorem
1.15
, which we highlight now. The author’s paper [
44
] brought attention to the difficulty in controlling the term

\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\left(\langle\mathcal{P}_{n}\mathcal{P}B_{i}^{2}u^{n}_{s},u^{n}_{s}\rangle_{1}+\lVert\mathcal{P}_{n}\mathcal{P}B_{i}u^{n}_{s}\rVert_{1}^{2}\right)

(48)

in the no-slip case, where
(\mathcal{P}_{n})
here are projections onto eigenfunctions of the Stokes Operator belonging to
W^{1,2}_{\sigma}
, which is the intersection of
\bar{W}^{1,2}_{\sigma}
with
W^{1,2}_{0}(\mathscr{O};\mathbb{R}^{2})
. This is the counterpart of (
39
) needed in showing uniform estimates of the Galerkin Approximation in the energy norm of the strong solution. The difficulty is the fact that
\mathcal{P}B_{i}^{2}u^{n}_{s}
and
\mathcal{P}B_{i}u^{n}_{s}
do not themselves belong to
W^{1,2}_{\sigma}
, and
\mathcal{P}_{n}
is
only self-adjoint on
W^{1,2}_{\sigma}
and not
\bar{W}^{1,2}_{\sigma}
. This owes to the fact that the system of eigenfunctions in
W^{1,2}_{\sigma}
is not a basis for
\bar{W}^{1,2}_{\sigma}
, whilst the system used in the present paper in any
\bar{W}^{2,2}_{\alpha}
is a basis for
\bar{W}^{1,2}_{\sigma}
. Therefore we cannot bound (
48
) by an expression

\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\left(\langle\mathcal{P}B_{i}^{2}u^{n}_{s},u^{n}_{s}\rangle_{1}+\lVert\mathcal{P}B_{i}u^{n}_{s}\rVert_{1}^{2}\right)

(49)

as we do for (
39
), formally in the
H
inner product but the approach works in either. The direct presence of the
\mathcal{P}_{n}
disables our commutator arguments used in the verification of (
17
), as good commutator properties between
\mathcal{P}_{n}
and
B_{i}
are not apparent. We note the key property that
\mathcal{P}B_{i}=\mathcal{P}B_{i}\mathcal{P}
used in [
44
], so that we can cope with the presence of
\mathcal{P}
and still achieve some cancellation of the top order derivative. A sufficient estimate on (
48
) thus remains open.

•

Why is the condition
\alpha\geq\kappa
necessary in Theorem
1.15
?

This property facilitated the use of the abstract framework of [
46
] which allowed us to lighten up the technical details given in this paper, though it may be unclear as to whether or not this condition was actually necessary. The alternative route to achieve Proposition
2.5
would be to work directly with the
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{1}
inner product. Using SALT noise as motivation, the issue is the same term discussed in the previous point, (
49
). Given the presence of
\mathcal{P}
our only option is to introduce the Stokes operator into these terms with Lemma
1.4
and then use the commutator estimates of [
44
] (see Proposition 1.29). Doing this we obtain that

\langle\mathcal{P}B_{i}^{2}u^{n}_{s},u^{n}_{s}\rangle_{1}=\langle\mathcal{P}B_{i}^{2}u^{n}_{s},Au^{n}_{s}\rangle+\langle(\kappa-\alpha)\mathcal{P}B_{i}^{2}u^{n}_{s},u^{n}_{s}\rangle_{L^{2}(\partial\mathscr{O};\mathbb{R})}

where we see that the issue is in the control of the
\mathcal{P}B_{i}^{2}u^{n}_{s}
term on the boundary, as with the usual employment of (
8
) we would require the
W^{3,2}
norm of
u^{n}_{s}
which is too much. The assumption that
\alpha\geq\kappa
allows us use of the
H
inner product in the Itô Formula (Proposition
5.9
), removing the need to manage this problematic boundary condition but of course this isn’t without a cost. Given that
\mathcal{P}B_{i}u^{n}_{s}\notin\bar{W}^{2,2}_{\alpha}
then we cannot say that

\lVert\mathcal{P}B_{i}u^{n}_{s}\rVert_{H}^{2}=\langle\mathcal{P}B_{i}u^{n}_{s},A\mathcal{P}B_{i}u^{n}_{s}\rangle

where the right hand side is the term that we need to reach to apply our commutator estimates. Thus we have to treat the boundary integral
-\langle(\kappa-\alpha)\mathcal{P}B_{i}u^{n}_{s},\mathcal{P}B_{i}u^{n}_{s}\rangle_{L^{2}(\partial\mathscr{O};\mathbb{R})}
directly, as we saw in the estimate of (
39
), which would not appear if we applied the Itô Formula for
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{1}
. The significance is that this boundary integral is controllable, as the derivatives are shared so the use of (
8
) now only demands the
W^{2,2}
norm of
u^{n}_{s}
in an acceptable way. There is the more hidden additional cost of the boundary integrals in
\lVert u^{n}_{t}\rVert_{H}^{2}
,
\lVert u^{n}_{0}\rVert_{H}^{2}
which are immediately dealt with once we have established the equivalence of
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{1}
and
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{H}
(and
only
because of this equivalence). Thus the assumption that
\alpha\geq\kappa
is necessary.

•

What types of noise satisfy the assumptions in Subsection
1.3
?

It is clear that the assumptions hold for any additive noise in
W^{1,2}(\mathscr{O};\mathbb{R}^{2})
or any linear
\mathcal{G}_{i}
,
\mathcal{Q}_{i}=\mathcal{G}_{i}
or
0
which is bounded as an operator
L^{2}(\mathscr{O};\mathbb{R}^{2})\rightarrow L^{2}(\mathscr{O};\mathbb{R}^{2})
and
W^{1,2}(\mathscr{O};\mathbb{R}^{2})\rightarrow W^{1,2}(\mathscr{O};\mathbb{R}^{2})
. Such a Nemystkii type noise has proven popular in the study of fluid SPDEs, see for instance ([
41
,
42
]. In addition an Itô type transport noise with sufficiently small gradient dependency relative to viscosity is also valid. A very interesting question is whether or not the assumptions hold for a purely transport Stratonovich noise. In [
45
] the majority of the assumptions are verified in the case of
\mathcal{G}_{i}=\mathcal{Q}_{i}:=\mathcal{P}\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{i}}
for
\xi_{i}
satisfying the same assumptions as in the SALT Noise, though (
17
) and (
19
) remain to be verified. Most critically is (
17
) pertaining to the key term that we have been considering, (
39
). For the SALT noise the property that
\mathcal{P}B_{i}=\mathcal{P}B_{i}\mathcal{P}
is vital; our estimate, as in (
44
), boils down to controlling

\langle\mathcal{P}B_{i}^{2}\phi,A\phi\rangle+\langle\mathcal{P}B_{i}\phi,A\mathcal{P}B_{i}\phi\rangle

which was done in [
44
] Lemma 2.7, relying heavily on this property that
\mathcal{P}B_{i}=\mathcal{P}B_{i}\mathcal{P}
. The question is whether or not we could achieve the necessary bound for

\langle(\mathcal{P}\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{i}})^{2}\phi,A\phi\rangle+\langle\mathcal{P}\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{i}}\phi,A\mathcal{P}\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{i}}\phi\rangle.

By taking the
\mathcal{P}
over, using that
A=A\mathcal{P}
and
\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{i}}^{*}=-\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{i}}
there is no issue in reducing this to

-\langle\mathcal{P}\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{i}}\phi,\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{i}}A\phi\rangle+\langle\mathcal{P}\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{i}}\phi,A\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{i}}\phi\rangle

(50)

or simply

\langle\mathcal{P}\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{i}}\phi,[A,\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{i}}]\phi\rangle

where
[A,\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{i}}]
represents the commutator
A\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{i}}-\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{i}}A
. It is sufficient to show that
[A,\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{i}}]
is of second order, but although that would be possible for
[\Delta,\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{i}}]
the presence of
\mathcal{P}
means this is not clear. We hope to make a compelling point regarding the importance of SALT noise here. To simply illustrate why the property that
\mathcal{P}B_{i}=\mathcal{P}B_{i}\mathcal{P}
is so important,
assume that
\mathcal{P}\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{i}}=\mathcal{P}\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{i}}\mathcal{P}
(which is untrue!)
. Then we could write (
50
) as

\displaystyle-\langle\mathcal{P}\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{i}}\phi,\mathcal{P}\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{i}}A\phi\rangle+\langle\mathcal{P}\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{i}}\phi,A\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{i}}\phi\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\mathcal{P}\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{i}}\phi,\mathcal{P}\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{i}}\Delta\phi\rangle-\langle\mathcal{P}\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{i}}\phi,\Delta\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{i}}\phi\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\mathcal{P}\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{i}}\phi,\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{i}}\Delta\phi\rangle-\langle\mathcal{P}\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{i}}\phi,\Delta\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{i}}\phi\rangle

or simply

-\langle\mathcal{P}\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{i}}\phi,[\Delta,\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{i}}]\phi\rangle

which as suggested is controllable. The proof for
B_{i}
is more complicated, but this is the essential idea. It seems, therefore, that the best method to show the necessary bound for
\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{i}}
is to actually write

\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{i}}=B_{i}-\mathcal{T}_{\xi_{i}}

then use the fact that
\mathcal{T}_{\xi_{i}}
is of zeroth order (so unproblematic) and then treat the
B_{i}
as described. More precisely, for the first term in (
50
), we would write

\displaystyle-\langle\mathcal{P}\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{i}}\phi,\mathcal{P}\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{i}}A\phi\rangle

\displaystyle=-\langle\mathcal{P}\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{i}}\phi,\mathcal{P}B_{i}A\phi\rangle+\langle\mathcal{P}\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{i}}\phi,\mathcal{P}\mathcal{T}_{\xi_{i}}A\phi\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\mathcal{P}\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{i}}\phi,\mathcal{P}B_{i}\Delta\phi\rangle+\langle\mathcal{P}\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{i}}\phi,\mathcal{P}\mathcal{T}_{\xi_{i}}A\phi\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\mathcal{P}\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{i}}\phi,\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{i}}\Delta\phi\rangle+\langle\mathcal{P}\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{i}}\phi,\mathcal{T}_{\xi_{i}}\Delta\phi\rangle+\langle\mathcal{P}\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{i}}\phi,\mathcal{T}_{\xi_{i}}A\phi\rangle.

Therefore the entirety of (
50
) is

-\langle\mathcal{P}\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{i}}\phi,[\Delta,\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{i}}]\phi\rangle+\langle\mathcal{P}\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{i}}\phi,\mathcal{T}_{\xi_{i}}\Delta\phi\rangle+\langle\mathcal{P}\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{i}}\phi,\mathcal{T}_{\xi_{i}}A\phi\rangle

or more compactly

\langle\mathcal{P}\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{i}}\phi,\left(\mathcal{T}_{\xi_{i}}\Delta+\mathcal{T}_{\xi_{i}}A-[\Delta,\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{i}}]\right)\phi\rangle.

This is now easy to control as the operator in the brackets is of second order, so the assumption (
17
) is indeed verified (we note that one can apply Lemma
1.4
in the same way using the
W^{3,2}_{0}(\mathscr{O};\mathbb{R}^{2})
regularity of the
\xi_{i}
directly). The proof for (
19
) sees no change to the one for
B_{i}
. In summary, a purely transport Stratonovich noise does satisfy the assumptions, but to prove it one has to revert to the SALT noise hence we emphasise how significant the structure of the SALT noise is for the analysis.

•

For which noise does the result of Theorem
1.17
still apply?

The choice to state and prove Theorem
1.17
for SALT noise specifically, instead of a general noise as in Theorem
1.15
, comes down to a few reasons. Firstly it is apparent that the case of an Itô transport noise where the gradient dependency is small relative to viscosity will not work (as viscosity is approaching zero), so if we wanted to keep this interesting case in our solution theory for Theorem
1.15
then a distinct set of assumptions would be required. On this note, it is also clear from our approach with the vorticity form that precise assumptions would be needed on
\textnormal{curl}\mathcal{Q}_{i}
which would be somewhat cumbersome. Moreover, as discussed in the previous point, we want to highlight the power of the SALT noise; we see this in Proposition
3.2
, as we can achieve pathwise bounds given the complete cancellation in the stochastic integral. This does not exclude the result for additive and Nemystkii type noise though: we instead can only demonstrate Proposition
3.2
as an inequality in expectation, however the tightness and hence passage to the Stochastic Euler Equation is still valid. Additional technicalities are needed to introduce a pathwise bound into the system, which is done through the familiar first hitting times. Proposition
3.3
is then shown up until these first hitting times, which still gives the tightness result exactly as seen in [
45
] Proposition 3.3. One would also have to take expectations in the proof of Proposition
3.5
, but the result follows with only these minor technical adjustments. The scenario of a transport noise is much more delicate, as the correct Itô-Stratonovich corrector in the weak formulation of the Euler Equation is
-\langle\mathcal{P}\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{i}}u_{s},\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{i}}u_{s}\rangle
where this different structure retaining the Leray Projector (as opposed to for
B_{i}
) requires some different considerations which we do not detail here. The idea, however, is to again introduce
\mathcal{T}_{\xi_{i}}
and use the good commutative properties of
\mathcal{P}
and
B_{i}
.

•

Could we have uniqueness for the SALT Euler Equation?

The question of uniqueness of weak solutions of the Euler Equation is certainly of interest, where both uniqueness and non-uniqueness have been established dependent on the regularity imposed. The classical (positive) uniqueness result is for initial vorticities in
L^{\infty}\left(\mathscr{O};\mathbb{R}\right)
proven by Yudovich in [
111
], which he then extends to a wider class in [
112
]. Simply talking about the
L^{\infty}\left(\mathscr{O};\mathbb{R}\right)
case here, we note that on the torus this result was proven in [
60
]. Success hinges on showing an energy estimate of the vorticity in
L^{\infty}\left([0,T];L^{\infty}(\mathscr{O};\mathbb{R})\right)
, and in fact it was proven in [
60
] (22) that the
L^{\infty}(\mathscr{O};\mathbb{R})
norm of the vorticity is constant in time via use of the Itô-Wentzel formula on the flow map. This was done for a sequence of strong solutions with more regular initial conditions approximating the desired
w_{0}\in L^{\infty}(\mathscr{O};\mathbb{R})
, which is an approach we couldn’t take here given that only weak solutions have been established. Any ideas for us would rely on showing additional regularity at the level of the Galerkin Approximations.

•

Do the strong solutions of (
11
) converge to the corresponding no-slip weak solutions shown to exist in [
45
]?

As discussed in the introduction, the idea is that as
\alpha
approaches infinity in (
4
) then the second term dominates the identity giving that
u\cdot\iota=0
on
\partial\mathscr{O}
, hence
u=0
on
\partial\mathscr{O}
. Existence and uniqueness of weak solutions of (
11
) under this boundary condition was proven in [
45
]. In the deterministic case this result is proven in [
59
] Theorem 9.2, though we appreciate that it is in fact done for the strong solution with no-slip condition and this a priori additional regularity is necessary in the approach. In lieu of this regularity, tightness arguments on the sequence of strong solutions of (
11
) with
\alpha
tending to infinity have proven fruitless.

In fact this method would be successful if we could show that the solutions have
L^{\infty}\left([0,T];\bar{W}^{1,2}_{\sigma}\right)\cap L^{2}\left([0,T];W^{2,2}(\mathscr{O};\mathbb{R}^{2})\right)
regularity uniformly in
\alpha
. Such a result is exceptionally appealing as with the limit inheriting this regularity, we would prove that
strong solutions
of the equation exist for the no-slip condition. The difficulty in this problem has already been explicated, so naturally this approach is of great interest. This regularity would enter at the level of the Galerkin Approximation, in particular Proposition
2.5
. Scanning through where the dependency on
\alpha
lies in this result, the first instance is in the
\lVert u^{n}_{r}\rVert_{H}^{2}
term, though as
\lVert u^{n}_{r}\rVert_{1}^{2}\leq\lVert u^{n}_{r}\rVert_{H}^{2}
then this is irrelevant. Importantly is the initial condition
\lVert u^{n}_{0}\rVert_{H}^{2}
, where the boundary integral
\langle(\kappa-\alpha)u^{n}_{0},u^{n}_{0}\rangle_{L^{2}(\partial\mathscr{O};\mathbb{R}^{2})}
is problematic. This is surely to be expected as strong solutions for the no-slip condition will only exist for
u_{0}\in W^{1,2}_{0}(\mathscr{O};\mathbb{R}^{2})
. Indeed if we impose this condition, then we can use that
\lVert u^{n}_{0}\rVert_{H}^{2}\leq\lVert u_{0}\rVert_{H}^{2}
and then the boundary integral
\langle(\kappa-\alpha)u_{0},u_{0}\rangle_{L^{2}(\partial\mathscr{O};\mathbb{R}^{2})}
is null. We see that there is no
\alpha
dependency in treating the nonlinear term, and in the Stokes Operator it is only in the equivalence of the
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{2}
and
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{W^{2,2}}
norms which does require some nuance but can be dealt with. Inevitably it is the transport type noise where this unravels, and we see the dependency on
\alpha
in the term

-\langle(\kappa-\alpha)\mathcal{P}\mathcal{G}_{i}\phi^{n},\mathcal{P}\mathcal{G}_{i}\phi^{n}\rangle_{L^{2}(\partial\mathscr{O};\mathbb{R}^{2})}

which appears to be impossible to handle. We bring attention to this as it is again the issue of the Leray Projector not preserving the zero trace property which prevents progress.

•

What are the overarching conclusions of the paper?

We hope, therefore, to have made two important points regarding the analysis of transport type stochastic fluid PDEs on a bounded domain. The first is that the choice of SALT noise satisfies some natural compatibility with the boundary which purely transport Stratonovich noise does not. The involved geometric derivation of the SALT noise does suggest this, and our results give analytical validation to the introduction of
\mathcal{T}_{\xi_{i}}
into the transport noise. The second point is that the less typical Navier boundary conditions appear to be the right choice for transport noise, given the unavoidable difficulty in the solution theory for the no-slip case. This is highlighted in both the direct Galerkin approach, and the
\alpha\rightarrow\infty
limit for solutions with Navier boundary conditions. The no-slip condition inherently demands that a transport type noise be both divergence-free and zero on the boundary, which is out of reach. It appears that these fine properties of transport noise in a bounded domain have not been appreciated, and we hope that this work can promote future studies in the area.

(\bm{\Psi}^{n})

\bm{\Psi}^{n}:\Omega\mapsto X_{T}
,
\lVert\bm{\Psi}^{n}\rVert_{X,\cdot}
is adapted and
\mathbb{P}-a.s.
continuous,
\bm{\Psi}^{n}\in L^{2}\left(\Omega;X_{T}\right)
, and such that
\sup_{n}\lVert\bm{\Psi}^{n}\rVert_{X,0}\in L^{\infty}\left(\Omega;\mathbb{R}\right)
. For any given
M>1
define the stopping times

\tau^{M,T}_{n}:=T\wedge\inf\left\{s\geq 0:\lVert\bm{\Psi}^{n}\rVert_{X,s}^{2}\geq M+\lVert\bm{\Psi}^{n}\rVert_{X,0}^{2}\right\}.

(51)

Furthermore suppose

\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{n\geq m}\mathbbm{E}\left[\lVert\bm{\Psi}^{n}-\bm{\Psi}^{m}\rVert^{2}_{X,\tau_{m}^{M,t}\wedge\tau_{n}^{M,t}}\right]=0

(52)

and that for any stopping time
\gamma
and sequence of stopping times
(\delta_{j})
which converge to
0

\mathbb{P}-a.s.
,

\lim_{j\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\mathbbm{E}\left(\lVert\bm{\Psi}^{n}\rVert_{X,(\gamma+\delta_{j})\wedge\tau^{M,T}_{n}}^{2}-\lVert\bm{\Psi}^{n}\rVert_{X,\gamma\wedge\tau^{M,T}_{n}}^{2}\right)=0.

(53)

Then there exists a stopping time
\tau^{M,T}_{\infty}
, a process
\bm{\Psi}:\Omega\mapsto X_{\tau^{M,T}_{\infty}}
whereby
\lVert\bm{\Psi}\rVert_{X,\cdot\wedge\tau^{M,T}_{\infty}}
is adapted and
\mathbb{P}-a.s.
continuous, and a subsequence indexed by
(m_{j})
such that

•

\tau^{M,T}_{\infty}\leq\tau^{M,T}_{m_{j}}

\mathbb{P}-a.s.
,

•

\lim_{j\rightarrow\infty}\lVert\bm{\Psi}-\bm{\Psi}^{m_{j}}\rVert_{X,\tau^{M,T}_{\infty}}=0

\mathbb{P}-a.s.
.

Moreover for any
R>0
we can choose
M
to be such that the stopping time

\tau^{R,T}:=T\wedge\inf\left\{s\geq 0:\lVert\bm{\Psi}\rVert_{X,s\wedge\tau^{M,T}_{\infty}}^{2}\geq R\right\}

(54)

satisfies
\tau^{R,T}\leq\tau^{M,T}_{\infty}

\mathbb{P}-a.s.
. Thus
\tau^{R,T}
is simply
T\wedge\inf\left\{s\geq 0:\lVert\bm{\Psi}\rVert_{X,s}^{2}\geq R\right\}
.

Proof.

Property (
52
) implies that for any given
j\in\mathbb{N}
we can choose an
n_{j}\in\mathbb{N}
such that for all
k\geq n_{j}
,

\mathbbm{E}\left(\lVert\bm{\Psi}^{k}-\bm{\Psi}^{n_{j}}\rVert^{2}_{X,\tau_{n_{j}}^{M,t}\wedge\tau_{k}^{M,t}}\right)\leq 2^{-4j}.

(55)

We shall make use of highly sensitive manipulations of the subsequence indexed by
(n_{j})
, and for this we introduce a new sequence of stopping times. We now impose that

M>2+\left\|\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\lVert\bm{\Psi}^{n}\rVert_{X,0}^{2}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{R})}

and define

\tilde{M}^{2}:=\frac{M-\left\|\sup_{n}\lVert\bm{\Psi}^{n}\rVert_{X,0}^{2}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{R})}}{2}.

The purpose of this is to define

\sigma^{M}_{j}:=T\wedge\inf\left\{s>0:\lVert\bm{\Psi}^{n_{j}}\rVert_{X,s}\geq(\tilde{M}-1+2^{-j})+\lVert\bm{\Psi}^{n_{j}}\rVert_{X,0}\right\}

and ensure that
\sigma^{M}_{j}\leq\tau^{M,T}_{n_{j}}
at every
\omega
. Note the key difference in not squaring the norm, and also that
\tilde{M}>1
so each
\sigma^{M}_{j}
is necessarily positive. To demonstrate the inequality, it is sufficient to show that,
\mathbb{P}-a.s.
,

\left((\tilde{M}-1+2^{-j})+\lVert\bm{\Psi}^{n_{j}}\rVert_{X,0}\right)^{2}\leq M+\lVert\bm{\Psi}^{n_{j}}\rVert_{X,0}^{2}

(56)

or more easily

\left(\tilde{M}+\lVert\bm{\Psi}^{n_{j}}\rVert_{X,0}\right)^{2}\leq M+\lVert\bm{\Psi}^{n_{j}}\rVert_{X,0}^{2}.

This is possible as

\displaystyle\left(\tilde{M}+\lVert\bm{\Psi}^{n_{j}}\rVert_{X,0}\right)^{2}\leq 2\tilde{M}^{2}+2\lVert\bm{\Psi}^{n_{j}}\rVert_{X,0}^{2}

\displaystyle=M-\left\|\sup_{n}\lVert\bm{\Psi}^{n}\rVert_{X,0}^{2}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{R})}+2\lVert\bm{\Psi}^{n_{j}}\rVert_{X,0}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq M+\lVert\bm{\Psi}^{n_{j}}\rVert_{X,0}^{2}.

The property (
56
) is thus verified, so
\sigma^{M}_{j}\leq\tau^{M,T}_{n_{j}}
and hence the subsequence
(\bm{\Psi}^{n_{j}})
enjoys the same properties up until the corresponding
\sigma^{M}_{j}
. In particular from (
55
),

\mathbbm{E}\left(\lVert\bm{\Psi}^{n_{j+1}}-\bm{\Psi}^{n_{j}}\rVert_{X,\sigma^{M}_{j}\wedge\sigma_{j+1}^{M}}\right)\leq\left[\mathbbm{E}\left(\lVert\bm{\Psi}^{n_{j+1}}-\bm{\Psi}^{n_{j}}\rVert_{X,\sigma^{M}_{j}\wedge\sigma_{j+1}^{M}}^{2}\right)\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq 2^{-2j}

(57)

hence in defining the sets

\Omega_{j}:=\left\{\omega\in\Omega:\lVert\bm{\Psi}^{n_{j+1}}(\omega)-\bm{\Psi}^{n_{j}}(\omega)\rVert_{X,\sigma^{M}_{j}(\omega)\wedge\sigma^{M}_{j+1}(\omega)}<2^{-(j+2)}\right\}

(58)

we have, by Chebyshev’s Inequality and (
57
),

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\Omega_{j}^{C}\right)\leq 2^{j+2}\mathbbm{E}\left(\lVert\bm{\Psi}^{n_{j+1}}-\bm{\Psi}^{n_{j}}\rVert_{X,\sigma^{M}_{j}\wedge\sigma^{M}_{j+1}}\right)\leq 2^{-j+2}.

We have, therefore, that

\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}\left(\Omega_{j}^{C}\right)<\infty

from which we see

\mathbb{P}\left(\bigcap_{K=1}^{\infty}\bigcup_{j=K}^{\infty}\Omega_{j}^{C}\right)=0

courtesy of Borel-Cantelli. It then follows that the set

\hat{\Omega}:=\bigcup_{K=1}^{\infty}\bigcap_{j=K}^{\infty}\Omega_{j}

is such that
\mathbb{P}(\hat{\Omega})=1
so that in verifying
\mathbb{P}-a.e.
properties, we can in fact simply show that they hold everywhere on
\hat{\Omega}
. More precisely, we also take
\hat{\Omega}
to be such that every
\lVert\bm{\Psi}^{n_{j}}\rVert_{X,\cdot}
is continuous on
\hat{\Omega}
, which is only a further countable intersection of full measure sets. We proceed by considering the sets

\hat{\Omega}_{K}:=\bigcap_{j=K}^{\infty}\Omega_{j}

with the idea to just show such properties on
\hat{\Omega}_{K}
for all
K
(as their union makes up
\hat{\Omega}
). We look to construct a new stopping time
\sigma^{M}_{\infty}
(which will prove to be the desired
\tau^{M,T}_{\infty}
) given as the
\mathbb{P}-a.e.
limit of
(\sigma^{M}_{j})
, built from demonstrating that
(\sigma^{M}_{j})
is monotone decreasing everywhere on
\hat{\Omega}_{K}
for all
K
. In other words we show that for sufficiently large
j
(in fact, just
j\geq K
) that the set

\{\sigma^{M}_{j}<\sigma^{M}_{j+1}\}\cap\hat{\Omega}_{K}

(59)

is empty. Firstly we observe from the strict inequality
\sigma^{M}_{j}<\sigma^{M}_{j+1}
on this set that
\sigma^{M}_{j}<T
, implying that

\sigma^{M}_{j}=\inf\left\{s>0:\lVert\bm{\Psi}^{n_{j}}\rVert_{X,s}\geq(\tilde{M}-1+2^{-j})+\lVert\bm{\Psi}^{n_{j}}\rVert_{X,0}\right\}

so by the continuity of
\lVert\bm{\Psi}^{n_{j}}\rVert_{X,\cdot}
,

\lVert\bm{\Psi}^{n_{j}}\rVert_{X,\sigma^{M}_{j}}=(\tilde{M}-1+2^{-j})+\lVert\bm{\Psi}^{n_{j}}\rVert_{X,0}.

(60)

Using the definition of
\Omega_{j}
, (
58
), for
j\geq K
, we have that

\lVert\bm{\Psi}^{n_{j}}\rVert_{X,\sigma^{M}_{j}\wedge\sigma^{M}_{j+1}}-\lVert\bm{\Psi}^{n_{j+1}}\rVert_{X,\sigma^{M}_{j}\wedge\sigma^{M}_{j+1}}\leq\lVert\bm{\Psi}^{n_{j+1}}-\bm{\Psi}^{n_{j}}\rVert_{X,\sigma^{M}_{j}\wedge\sigma^{M}_{j+1}}<2^{-\left(j+2\right)}

(61)

and also

\lVert\bm{\Psi}^{n_{j+1}}\rVert_{X,0}-\lVert\bm{\Psi}^{n_{j}}\rVert_{X,0}\leq\lVert\bm{\Psi}^{n_{j+1}}-\bm{\Psi}^{n_{j}}\rVert_{X,0}<2^{-\left(j+2\right)}.

(62)

Combining (
60
), (
61
) and (
62
), whilst using that
\sigma^{M}_{j}<\sigma^{M}_{j+1}
, we see that

\displaystyle\lVert\bm{\Psi}^{n_{j+1}}\rVert_{X,\sigma^{M}_{j}\wedge\sigma^{M}_{j+1}}

\displaystyle>\lVert\bm{\Psi}^{n_{j}}\rVert_{X,\sigma^{M}_{j}\wedge\sigma^{M}_{j+1}}-2^{-\left(j+2\right)}

\displaystyle=\lVert\bm{\Psi}^{n_{j}}\rVert_{X,\sigma^{M}_{j}}-2^{-\left(j+2\right)}

\displaystyle=\lVert\bm{\Psi}^{n_{j}}\rVert_{X,0}+(\tilde{M}-1+2^{-j})-2^{-\left(j+2\right)}

\displaystyle>\lVert\bm{\Psi}^{n_{j+1}}\rVert_{X,0}-2^{-(j+2)}+(\tilde{M}-1+2^{-j})-2^{-\left(j+2\right)}

\displaystyle=\lVert\bm{\Psi}^{n_{j+1}}\rVert_{X,0}+(\tilde{M}-1+2^{-(j+1)})

(63)

where in the last line we have used the manipulation

-2^{-(j+2)}-2^{-(j+2)}+2^{-j}=-2^{-j-1}+2^{-j}=2^{-j}\left(-2^{-1}+1\right)=2^{-j}\left(2^{-1}\right)=2^{-(j+1)}.

The hard work is done in showing that the set (
59
) is empty, as on this set note that

\lVert\bm{\Psi}^{n_{j+1}}\rVert_{X,\sigma^{M}_{j}\wedge\sigma^{M}_{j+1}}\leq\lVert\bm{\Psi}^{n_{j+1}}\rVert_{X,\sigma^{M}_{j+1}}\leq\lVert\bm{\Psi}^{n_{j+1}}\rVert_{X,0}+(\tilde{M}-1+2^{-(j+1)})

which contradicts (
63
), hence (
59
) must be empty. Thus on every
\hat{\Omega}_{K}
, and furthermore the whole of
\hat{\Omega}
, the sequence
(\sigma^{M}_{j})
is eventually monotone decreasing (and bounded below by
0
). Furthermore we define
\sigma^{M}_{\infty}
as the pointwise limit
\lim_{j\rightarrow\infty}\sigma^{M}_{j}
on
\hat{\Omega}
, which must itself be a stopping time as the
\mathbb{P}-a.s.
limit of stopping times. As mentioned this shall prove to be our
\tau^{M,T}_{\infty}
, and for the existence of
\bm{\Psi}
we show that on
\hat{\Omega}
the subsequence
(\bm{\Psi}^{n_{j}})
is Cauchy in
X_{\sigma^{M}_{\infty}}
. Every
\omega\in\hat{\Omega}
belongs to
\hat{\Omega}_{K}
for some
K
, and furthermore to
\hat{\Omega}_{L}
for all
L>K
. We fix arbitrary
\omega
and select an associated
K
. At this
\omega
, for any

, observe that

\displaystyle\lVert\bm{\Psi}^{n_{j}}-\bm{\Psi}^{n_{k}}\rVert_{X,\sigma^{M}_{\infty}}

\displaystyle=\lVert\bm{\Psi}^{n_{j}}-\bm{\Psi}^{n_{k+1}}+\bm{\Psi}^{n_{k+1}}-\bm{\Psi}^{n_{k}}\rVert_{X,\sigma^{M}_{\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq\lVert\bm{\Psi}^{n_{j}}-\bm{\Psi}^{n_{k+1}}\rVert_{X,\sigma^{M}_{\infty}}+\lVert\bm{\Psi}^{n_{k+1}}-\bm{\Psi}^{n_{k}}\rVert_{X,\sigma^{M}_{\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq\lVert\bm{\Psi}^{n_{j}}-\bm{\Psi}^{n_{k+1}}\rVert_{X,\sigma^{M}_{\infty}}+2^{-(k+2)}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{l=k}^{j}2^{-(l+2)}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{-(k+1)}

having carried out an inductive argument in the penultimate step. We are thus free to take
K
large enough so that this difference is arbitrarily small; therefore there exists a limit in the Banach Space
X_{\sigma^{M}_{\infty}}
, which we call
\bm{\Psi}
. The process
\lVert\bm{\Psi}\rVert_{X,\cdot\wedge\sigma^{M}_{\infty}}
is adapted and
\mathbb{P}-a.s.
continuous, as

\sup_{r\in[0,T]}\left|\lVert\bm{\Psi}\rVert_{X,r\wedge\sigma^{M}_{\infty}}-\lVert\bm{\Psi}^{n_{j}}\rVert_{X,r\wedge\sigma^{M}_{\infty}}\right|\leq\sup_{r\in[0,T]}\left|\lVert\bm{\Psi}-\bm{\Psi}^{n_{j}}\rVert_{X,r\wedge\sigma^{M}_{\infty}}\right|=\lVert\bm{\Psi}-\bm{\Psi}^{n_{j}}\rVert_{X,\sigma^{M}_{\infty}}

which has
\mathbb{P}-a.s.
limit as

equal to zero. Thus
\lVert\bm{\Psi}\rVert_{X,\cdot\wedge\sigma^{M}_{\infty}}
is given,
\mathbb{P}-a.s.
, as the uniform in time limit of adapted and continuous processes, verifying the result. Moving on, it is now that we make use of (
53
) much in the same way as we did for (
52
). This will be done in the context of
\gamma:=\sigma^{M}_{\infty}
and
\delta_{j}:=\sigma^{M}_{j}-\sigma^{M}_{\infty}
. Indeed for any
j\in\mathbb{N}
we can choose an
m_{j}\in\mathbb{N}
(where
m_{j}=n_{l}
some
l
) such that for all
k\geq m_{j}
,

\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\mathbbm{E}\left(\lVert\bm{\Psi}^{n}\rVert_{X,\sigma^{M}_{k}\wedge\tau^{M,T}_{n}}^{2}-\lVert\bm{\Psi}^{n}\rVert_{X,\sigma^{M}_{\infty}\wedge\tau^{M,T}_{n}}^{2}\right)\leq 2^{-2j}.

In particular, through a relabelling of
\sigma^{M}_{m_{j}}=\sigma^{M}_{l}
,

\mathbbm{E}\left(\lVert\bm{\Psi}^{m_{j}}\rVert_{X,\sigma^{M}_{m_{j}}}^{2}-\lVert\bm{\Psi}^{m_{j}}\rVert_{X,\sigma^{M}_{\infty}}^{2}\right)\leq 2^{-2j}

by choosing
n
as
m_{j}
and using that
\sigma^{M}_{\infty}\leq\sigma^{M}_{k}\leq\sigma^{M}_{m_{j}}\leq\tau^{M,T}_{m_{j}}
. In a familiar way we define

\Omega^{\prime}_{j}:=\left\{\lVert\bm{\Psi}^{m_{j}}\rVert_{X,\sigma^{M}_{m_{j}}}^{2}-\lVert\bm{\Psi}^{m_{j}}\rVert_{X,\sigma^{M}_{\infty}}^{2}<2^{-(j+2)}\right\}

so that, just as we showed for (
58
),

\check{\Omega}_{K}:=\bigcap_{j=K}^{\infty}\Omega^{\prime}_{j},\qquad\check{\Omega}:=\bigcup_{K=1}^{\infty}\check{\Omega}_{K},\qquad\mathbb{P}\left(\check{\Omega}\right)=1.

For arbitrary given
R>0
, the plan now is to find a constant
M
such that at every
\omega\in\hat{\Omega}\cap\check{\Omega}
, either
\sigma^{M}_{\infty}=T
or
\lVert\bm{\Psi}\rVert^{2}_{X,\sigma^{M}_{\infty}}\geq R
. In both instances it is clear that
\tau^{R,T}\leq\sigma^{M}_{\infty}
, thus proving the proposition. To this end we fix an
\omega\in\hat{\Omega}\cap\check{\Omega}
such that
\sigma^{M}_{\infty}<T
. As
\sigma^{M}_{\infty}
is the decreasing limit of
(\sigma^{M}_{m_{j}})
then for sufficiently large

we must also have that
\sigma^{M}_{m_{j}}<T
. Exactly as in (

),

\lVert\bm{\Psi}^{m_{j}}\rVert_{X,\sigma^{M}_{m_{j}}}=(\tilde{M}-1+2^{-j})+\lVert\bm{\Psi}^{m_{j}}\rVert_{X,0}.

(64)

From the proven convergence we also have that for sufficiently large
m_{j}
,

\lVert\bm{\Psi}-\bm{\Psi}^{m_{j}}\rVert_{X,\sigma^{M}_{\infty}}<1

(65)

which implies that
\lVert\bm{\Psi}\rVert_{X,\sigma^{M}_{\infty}}>\lVert\bm{\Psi}^{m_{j}}\rVert_{X,\sigma^{M}_{\infty}}-1
, and likewise as
\omega\in\check{\Omega}_{K}
for some
K
,

\lVert\bm{\Psi}^{m_{j}}\rVert_{X,\sigma^{M}_{m_{j}}}^{2}-\lVert\bm{\Psi}^{m_{j}}\rVert_{X,\sigma^{M}_{\infty}}^{2}<1.

(66)

We fix an
m_{j}
large enough so that (
64
), (
65
) and (
66
) all hold. Substituting (
64
) into (
66
) gives that

\lVert\bm{\Psi}^{m_{j}}\rVert_{X,\sigma^{M}_{\infty}}^{2}>\left((\tilde{M}-1+2^{-j})+\lVert\bm{\Psi}^{m_{j}}\rVert_{X,0}\right)^{2}-1>(\tilde{M}-1)^{2}-1.

If
\tilde{M}>2
then the expression on the right is positive and

\lVert\bm{\Psi}^{m_{j}}\rVert_{X,\sigma^{M}_{\infty}}>\left((\tilde{M}-1)^{2}-1\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Furthermore

\lVert\bm{\Psi}\rVert_{X,\sigma^{M}_{\infty}}>\left((\tilde{M}-1)^{2}-1\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}-1

where the right hand side is of course monotone increasing and unbounded in
\tilde{M}
and hence
M
. By choosing
M
large enough such that

\left[\left((\tilde{M}-1)^{2}-1\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}-1\right]^{2}>R

we complete the proof.

5.2
Abstract Framework for Solutions

We provide the framework of [
46
] Section 3 used in Subsection
2.2
, considering an Itô SPDE

\bm{\Psi}_{t}=\bm{\Psi}_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{A}(s,\bm{\Psi}_{s})ds+\int_{0}^{t}\tilde{\mathcal{G}}(s,\bm{\Psi}_{s})d\mathcal{W}_{s}.

(67)

We state the assumptions for a triplet of embedded Hilbert Spaces

V\hookrightarrow H\hookrightarrow U

and ask that there is a continuous bilinear form
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{U\times V}:U\times V\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
such that for
\phi\in H
and
\psi\in V
,

\langle\phi,\psi\rangle_{U\times V}=\langle\phi,\psi\rangle_{H}.

(68)

The mappings
\mathcal{A},\tilde{\mathcal{G}}
are such that

\mathcal{A}:[0,T]\times V\rightarrow U,\tilde{\mathcal{G}}:[0,T]\times V\rightarrow\mathscr{L}^{2}(\mathfrak{U};H)
are measurable. We assume that
V
is dense in
H
which is dense in
U
.

Assumption 5.2
.

There exists a system
(a_{n})
of elements of
V
such that, defining the spaces

V_{n}

1.

There exists some constant
c
independent of

such that for all
\phi\in H

\lVert\mathcal{P}_{n}\phi\rVert_{H}^{2}\leq c\lVert\phi\rVert_{H}^{2}.

(69)

2.

There exists a real valued sequence
(\mu_{n})
with
\mu_{n}\rightarrow\infty
such that for any
\phi\in H

represents the identity operator in
U
.

These conditions are supplemented by a series of assumptions on the mappings. We shall use general notation
c_{t}
to represent a function
c_{\cdot}:[0,\infty)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
bounded on
[0,T]
, evaluated at the time
t
. Moreover we define functions
K
,
\tilde{K}
relative to some non-negative constants
p,\tilde{p},q,\tilde{q}

\tilde{K}:H\rightarrow\mathbb{R}

\displaystyle K(\phi)

\displaystyle:=1+\lVert\phi\rVert_{U}^{p},\qquad K(\phi,\psi):=1+\lVert\phi\rVert_{U}^{p}+\lVert\psi\rVert_{U}^{q},

\displaystyle\tilde{K}(\phi)

\displaystyle:=K(\phi)+\lVert\phi\rVert_{H}^{\tilde{p}},\qquad\tilde{K}(\phi,\psi):=K(\phi,\psi)+\lVert\phi\rVert_{H}^{\tilde{p}}+\lVert\psi\rVert_{H}^{\tilde{q}}

Distinct use of the function
K

\tilde{K}
, when

then we shall denote the general
\tilde{K}
by

. In this case no further assumptions are made on the

. That is,
\tilde{K}_{2}
has the general representation

\tilde{K}_{2}(\phi,\psi)=K(\phi,\psi)+\lVert\phi\rVert_{H}^{2}+\lVert\psi\rVert_{H}^{2}

(71)

and similarly as a function of one variable.

We state the subsequent assumptions for arbitrary elements
\phi,\psi\in V
,

,

and

. Understanding

\tilde{\mathcal{G}}:[0,\infty)\times V\times\mathfrak{U}\rightarrow H
, we introduce the notation
\tilde{\mathcal{G}}_{i}(\cdot,\cdot):=\tilde{\mathcal{G}}(\cdot,\cdot,e_{i})

Assumption 5.3
.

\displaystyle\leq c_{t}K(\phi,\psi)\lVert\phi-\psi\rVert_{H}^{2}.

(74)

Assumption 5.4
.

\displaystyle 2\langle\mathcal{P}_{n}\mathcal{A}(t,\bm{\phi}^{n}),\bm{\phi}^{n}\rangle_{H}+\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\lVert\mathcal{P}_{n}\tilde{\mathcal{G}}_{i}(t,\bm{\phi}^{n})\rVert_{H}^{2}

Assumption 5.5
.

\displaystyle+\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\lVert\tilde{\mathcal{G}}_{i}(t,\bm{\phi})-\tilde{\mathcal{G}}_{i}(t,\bm{\psi})\rVert_{U}^{2}

(77)

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\langle\tilde{\mathcal{G}}_{i}(t,\bm{\phi})-\tilde{\mathcal{G}}_{i}(t,\bm{\psi}),\bm{\phi}-\bm{\psi}\rangle^{2}_{U}

\displaystyle\leq c_{t}\tilde{K}_{2}(\bm{\phi},\bm{\psi})\lVert\bm{\phi}-\bm{\psi}\rVert_{U}^{4}.

(78)

Assumption 5.6
.

\displaystyle 2\langle\mathcal{A}(t,\bm{\phi}),\bm{\phi}\rangle_{U}+\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\lVert\tilde{\mathcal{G}}_{i}(t,\bm{\phi})\rVert_{U}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq c_{t}K(\bm{\phi})\left[1+\lVert\bm{\phi}\rVert_{H}^{2}\right],

(79)

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\langle\tilde{\mathcal{G}}_{i}(t,\bm{\phi}),\bm{\phi}\rangle^{2}_{U}

\displaystyle\leq c_{t}K(\bm{\phi})\left[1+\lVert\bm{\phi}\rVert_{H}^{4}\right].

(80)

Assumption 5.7
.

\langle\mathcal{A}(t,\phi)-\mathcal{A}(t,\psi),\eta\rangle_{U}\leq c_{t}(1+\lVert\eta\rVert_{H})\left[K(\phi,\psi)+\lVert\phi\rVert_{V}+\lVert\psi\rVert_{V}\right]\lVert\phi-\psi\rVert_{H}.

(81)

Useful Results

Theorem 5.8

(Gagliardo-Nirenberg Inequality)
.

Let
p,q,\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
,
m\in\mathbb{N}
,
\mathscr{O}\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
be such that
p>q\geq 1
,
m>N(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p})
and
\frac{1}{p}=\frac{\alpha}{q}+(1-\alpha)(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{m}{N})
. Then there exists a constant

Lemma 5.10
.

Let
\mathcal{H}_{1},\mathcal{H}_{2}
be separable Hilbert Spaces with
\mathcal{H}_{1}
compactly embedded into
\mathcal{H}_{2}
, and
V
any dense set in
\mathcal{H}_{2}
. For some fixed
T>0
let
\bm{\Psi}^{n}:\Omega\rightarrow C\left([0,T];\mathcal{H}_{1}\right)
be a sequence of measurable processes such that

\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\mathbbm{E}\left(\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\lVert\bm{\Psi}^{n}_{t}\rVert_{\mathcal{H}_{1}}\right)<\infty

(85)

and for any sequence of stopping times
(\gamma_{n})
with
\gamma_{n}:\Omega\rightarrow[0,T]
, and any
\varepsilon>0
,
v\in V
,

\lim_{\delta\rightarrow 0^{+}}\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\mathbbm{P}\left(\left\{\omega\in\Omega:\left|\left\langle\bm{\Psi}^{n}_{(\gamma_{n}+\delta)\wedge T}-\bm{\Psi}^{n}_{\gamma_{n}},v\right\rangle_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}\right|>\varepsilon\right\}\right)=0.

(86)

Then the sequence of the laws of
(\bm{\Psi}^{n})
is tight in the space of probability measures over
\mathcal{D}\left([0,T];\mathcal{H}_{2}\right)
.

Proof.

We essentially combine the tightness criteria of [
56
] Theorem 3.1 and [
2
] Theorem 1, in the specific case outlined here. Note that this is only a tweak on the result [
45
] Lemma 4.2. Firstly in reference to [
56
] Theorem 3.1 we may take
E
to be
\mathcal{H}_{2}
and
\mathbbm{F}
to be the collection of functions defined for each
v\in V
by
\langle\cdot,v\rangle_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}
, which separates points in
\mathcal{H}_{2}
from the density of
V
. We also note that condition
(3.3)
in [
56
] is satisfied for
(\mu_{n})
taken to be the sequence of laws of
(\bm{\Psi}^{n})
over
\mathcal{D}\left([0,T];\mathcal{H}_{2}\right)
, owing to the property (
85
). Indeed as
\mathcal{H}_{1}
is compactly embedded into
\mathcal{H}_{2}
one only needs to take a bounded subset of
\mathcal{H}_{1}
, hence considering the closed ball of radius
M
in
\mathcal{H}_{1}
,
\tilde{B}_{M}
, we have that

\displaystyle\mathbbm{P}\left(\left\{\omega\in\Omega:\bm{\Psi}^{n}(\omega)\notin D\left([0,T];\tilde{B}_{M}\right)\right\}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbbm{P}\left(\left\{\omega\in\Omega:\bm{\Psi}^{n}(\omega)\notin C\left([0,T];\tilde{B}_{M}\right)\right\}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbbm{P}\left(\left\{\omega\in\Omega:\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\lVert\bm{\Psi}^{n}_{t}(\omega)\rVert_{\mathcal{H}_{1}}>M\right\}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{M}\mathbbm{E}(\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\lVert\bm{\Psi}^{n}_{t}\rVert_{\mathcal{H}_{1}})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{M}\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\mathbbm{E}(\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\lVert\bm{\Psi}^{n}_{t}\rVert_{\mathcal{H}_{1}})

from which we see an arbitrarily large choice of
M
will justify (3.3). Therefore by Theorem 3.1 it only remains to show that for every
v\in V
the sequence of the laws of
\langle\bm{\Psi}^{n},v\rangle_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}
is tight in the space of probability measures over
\mathcal{D}\left([0,T];\mathbb{R}\right)
. By Theorem 1 of [
2
] this is satisfied if we can show that for for any sequence of stopping times
(\gamma_{n})
,
\gamma_{n}:\Omega\rightarrow[0,T]
, and constants
(\delta_{n})
,
\delta_{n}\geq 0
and
\delta_{n}\rightarrow 0
as
n\rightarrow\infty
:

1.

For every
t\in[0,T]
, the sequence of the laws of
\langle\bm{\Psi}^{n}_{t},v\rangle_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}
is tight in the space of probability measures over
\mathbb{R}
,

2.

For every
\varepsilon>0
,
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathbbm{P}\left(\left\{\omega\in\Omega:\left|\left\langle\bm{\Psi}^{n}_{(\gamma_{n}+\delta_{n})\wedge T}-\bm{\Psi}^{n}_{\gamma_{n}},v\right\rangle_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}\right|>\varepsilon\right\}\right)=0.

We address each item in turn: as for
1
, we are required to show that for every
\varepsilon>0
and
t\in[0,T]
, there exists a compact
K_{\varepsilon}\subset\mathbb{R}
such that for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

\mathbbm{P}\left(\left\{\omega\in\Omega:\langle\bm{\Psi}^{n}_{t}(\omega),v\rangle_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}\notin K_{\varepsilon}\right\}\right)<\varepsilon.

To this end define
B_{M}
as the closed ball of radius
M
in
\mathbb{R}
, then

\displaystyle\mathbbm{P}\left(\left\{\omega\in\Omega:\langle\bm{\Psi}^{n}_{t}(\omega),v\rangle_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}\notin B_{M}\right\}\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbbm{P}\left(\left\{\omega\in\Omega:\left|\langle\bm{\Psi}^{n}_{t}(\omega),v\rangle_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}\right|>M\right\}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{M}\mathbbm{E}(\left|\langle\bm{\Psi}^{n}_{t},v\rangle_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}\right|)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\lVert v\rVert_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}}{M}\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\mathbbm{E}\left(\lVert\bm{\Psi}^{n}_{t}\rVert_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}\right)

so setting

M:=\frac{\varepsilon}{2\lVert v\rVert_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\mathbbm{E}\left(\lVert\bm{\Psi}^{n}_{t}\rVert_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}\right)}

justifies item
1
. As for
2
, note that for each fixed
j\in\mathbb{N}
we have that

\left|\left\langle\bm{\Psi}^{j}_{(\gamma_{j}+\delta_{j})\wedge T}-\bm{\Psi}^{j}_{\gamma_{j}},v\right\rangle_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}\right|\leq\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\left|\left\langle\bm{\Psi}^{n}_{(\gamma_{n}+\delta_{j})\wedge T}-\bm{\Psi}^{n}_{\gamma_{n}},v\right\rangle_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}\right|

so in particular

\lim_{j\rightarrow\infty}\mathbbm{P}\left(\left\{\left|\left\langle\bm{\Psi}^{j}_{(\gamma_{j}+\delta_{j})\wedge T}-\bm{\Psi}^{j}_{\gamma_{j}},v\right\rangle_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}\right|>\varepsilon\right\}\right)\leq\lim_{j\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\mathbbm{P}\left(\left\{\left|\left\langle\bm{\Psi}^{n}_{(\gamma_{n}+\delta_{j})\wedge T}-\bm{\Psi}^{n}_{\gamma_{n}},v\right\rangle_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}\right|>\varepsilon\right\}\right).

As
(\delta_{j})
was an arbitrary sequence of non-negative constants approaching zero, we can generically take
\delta\rightarrow 0^{+}
and
2
is implied by (
86
). The proof is complete.

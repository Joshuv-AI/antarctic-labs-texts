Abstract
. We study the joint limit behavior of sums, maxima and ℓ p \ell^{p} -type moduli for samples taken from an ℝ d {\mathbb{R}}^{d} -valued regularly varying stationary sequence with infinite variance. As a consequence, we can determine the distributional limits for ratios of sums and maxima, studentized sums, and other self-normalized quantities in terms of hybrid characteristic functions and Laplace transforms. These transforms enable one to calculate moments of the limits and to characterize the differences between the iid and stationary cases in terms of indices which describe effects of extremal clustering on functionals acting on the dependent sequence.

1.
Introduction

Consider an
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
-valued (strictly) stationary sequence
({\bf X}_{t})

with generic element
{\bf X}
. We call this time series heavy-tailed if
it does not have finite moments of a certain order. To be more
specific, we are interested in
regularly varying time series
.
This notion was introduced by Davis and Hsing
[
13
]

via the vague convergence of certain tail measures. We will stick to an equivalent
formulation via weak convergence conditional on a large value of
|{\bf X}_{0}|
; see
Basrak and Segers
[
8
]
: the stationary sequence
({\bf X}_{t})

is
regularly varying with (tail) index
\alpha>0
if there exist a
Pareto
(\alpha)
-distributed random variable
Y
, i.e.,
{\mathbb{P}}(Y>y)=y^{-\alpha}
,
y>1
,
and an
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
-valued sequence
(\mbox{\boldmath$\Theta$}_{t})
which is independent of
Y
such that for every
h\geq 0
,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}\big(x^{-1}({\bf X}_{-h},\ldots,{\bf X}_{h})\in\cdot\,\big|\,|{\bf X}_{0}|>x\big)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{\rightarrow}}{\mathbb{P}}\big(Y\,(\mbox{\boldmath$\Theta$}_{-h},\ldots,\mbox{\boldmath$\Theta$}_{h})\in\cdot\big)\,,\qquad x\to\infty\,.

We call
(\mbox{\boldmath$\Theta$}_{t})
the
spectral tail process of
({\bf X}_{t})
.
Under suitable
mixing
and
anti-clustering conditions

one has convergence of the
normalized maxima

\displaystyle a_{n}^{-1}M^{|{\bf X}|}_{n}=a_{n}^{-1}\max_{i=1,\ldots,n}|{\bf X}_{i}|\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{\rightarrow}}\eta_{\alpha}\,,\qquad n\to\infty\,,

where
\eta_{\alpha}
has a scaled Fréchet distribution, i.e.,

{\mathbb{P}}(\eta_{\alpha}\leq x)=\Phi_{\alpha}^{\theta_{|{\bf X}|}}(x)={\rm e}\,^{-\theta_{|{\bf X}|}\,x^{-\alpha}}
,
x>0
,
and the scaling factor
\theta_{|{\bf X}|}\in(0,1]
is the
extremal index
of the sequence
(|{\bf X}_{t}|)
. The sequence
(a_{n})
is chosen
such that
n\,{\mathbb{P}}(|{\bf X}|>a_{n})\to 1
as
n\to\infty
.
Moreover, for
\alpha\in(0,2)

the (centered) partial sums of the time series converge to an
\alpha
-stable limit
{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{\alpha}
:

\displaystyle a_{n}^{-1}{\bf S}_{n}=a_{n}^{-1}\big({\bf X}_{1}+\cdots+{\bf X}_{n}\big)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{\rightarrow}}{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{\alpha}\,,\qquad n\to\infty.

We refer to Section
2
for precise definitions and conditions.

The normalizing sequence
(a_{n})

is in general unknown and has to be estimated from data.
A way of replacing
(a_{n})
by a known quantity is
the principle of
self-normalization
, including the well-known

studentization
; see de la Peña et al.
[
23
]

for a general treatment of self-normalizing techniques.
A natural choice of self-normalization for the partial
sums
({\bf S}_{n})
is given by the sequence of the partial
maxima
(M_{n}^{|{\bf X}|})
. Indeed, for
\alpha\in(0,2)
both sequences require
the same normalization
(a_{n})
. Thus, if one can show the joint
convergence
a_{n}^{-1}(M_{n}^{|{\bf X}|},{\bf S}_{n})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{\rightarrow}}(\eta_{\alpha},{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{\alpha})
it follows that

\displaystyle\dfrac{{\bf S}_{n}}{M_{n}^{|{\bf X}|}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{\rightarrow}}\dfrac{{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{\alpha}}{\eta_{\alpha}}\,,\qquad n\to\infty\,,

where
\eta_{\alpha}
and
{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{\alpha}
are dependent even if
({\bf X}_{t})
is an iid sequence.
Moreover, we will extend this result to more general self-normalizing sequences.
In particular, we will show the convergence of
studentized
sums

\displaystyle\dfrac{{\bf S}_{n}}{\gamma_{n,p}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{\rightarrow}}\dfrac{{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{\alpha}}{\zeta_{\alpha,p}}\,,\qquad n\to\infty\,,

where

(1.1)

\displaystyle\gamma_{n,p}=\big(\sum_{t=1}^{n}|{\bf X}_{t}|^{p}\big)^{1/p}\,,\qquad p>\alpha\,,

is an

\ell^{p}
-norm-type modulus of the sample

{\bf X}_{1},\ldots,{\bf X}_{n}
, and
\zeta_{\alpha,p}^{p}
is a positive
\alpha/p
-stable random variable.
In contrast to the
\alpha
-stable limit
{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{\alpha}
of
(a_{n}^{-1}{\bf S}_{n})
the limit ratios

{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{\alpha}/\eta_{\alpha}
and
{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{\alpha}/\zeta_{\alpha,p}
have moments of order
\alpha
.

For
\alpha>2
,
{\bf X}
has finite second moment. Then, under general
weak dependence conditions the (centered) partial sums
({\bf S}_{n}/\sqrt{n})
satisfy a central limit theorem with Gaussian limit while
(a_{n}^{-1}M_{n}^{|{\bf X}|})
still has a Fréchet limit
\eta_{\alpha}
, and
(a_{n}/\sqrt{n})({\bf S}_{n}/M_{n}^{|{\bf X}|})
converges to a ratio
of a Gaussian vector and
\eta_{\alpha}
which, typically, are independent; see
Anderson and Turkman
[
3
,
4
,
5
]
, Hsing
[
16
]
. In this case, normalization of

{\bf S}_{n}
by
M_{n}^{|{\bf X}|}
is less meaningful because one needs to know
(a_{n})
.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2

we introduce the basic conditions used throughout the paper: mixing and anti-clustering.
We discuss these and also properties of a regularly varying stationary sequence. In Section
3
we continue with the main
results of this paper, dealing with the joint convergence of sums and maxima (Theorem
3.1
),
and sums, maxima and
\ell^{p}
-type moduli (Theorem
3.2
). In Section
4

we apply these results to ratios of sums and maxima, studentized sums, Greenwood statistics, and
ratios of norms. In Section
5
we indicate how the conditions of the results
in the previous sections can be verified. We focus on the class of regularly varying iterated random Lipschitz functions, including autoregressive processes and solutions to affine stochastic recurrence equations.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Mixing condition

Mixing conditions are standard for proving asymptotic theory for
sums and maxima of a stationary sequence. Since we are interested in the joint convergence of these objects
we introduce a mixing condition which is tailored for this situation.
Following Chow and Teugels
[
12
]
, we will express the mixing
condition through a combination of a characteristic function (for sums) and a distribution function (for maxima)
(so-called
hybrid characteristic function)
:

\displaystyle\Psi_{n}({\bf u},x)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\big[\exp\big(i\,a_{n}^{-1}{\bf u}^{\top}{\bf S}_{n}\big)\,{\mathbf{1}}\big(a_{n}^{-1}M_{n}^{|{\bf X}|}\leq x\big)\big]\,,\qquad{\bf u}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\,,\qquad x>0\,.

The hybrid characteristic function of a pair
({\bf Y},U)

of a random vector
{\bf Y}
and a non-negative random variable
U
determines the distribution of
({\bf Y},U)
. Moreover,

({\bf Y}_{n},U_{n})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{\rightarrow}}({\bf Y},U)
if and only if the hybrid characteristic functions of
({\bf Y}_{n},U_{n})
converge
point-wise to the corresponding hybrid characteristic function of
({\bf Y},U)
.

We will use the following
mixing condition
:
for some integer sequences
r_{n}\to\infty
,
k_{n}=[n/r_{n}]\to\infty
,

(2.1)

\displaystyle\Psi_{n}({\bf u},x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big({\mathbb{E}}\big[\exp\big(i\,a_{n}^{-1}{\bf u}^{\top}{\bf S}_{r_{n}}\big)\,{\mathbf{1}}\big(a_{n}^{-1}M_{r_{n}}^{|{\bf X}|}\leq x\big)\big]\Big)^{k_{n}}+o(1)\,,\qquad n\to\infty\,,

\displaystyle\qquad x>0\,,\qquad{\bf u}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\,.

Condition (
2.1
) ensures the asymptotic independence of
k_{n}
maxima
and sums over disjoint blocks of length
r_{n}
. It
follows from strong mixing properties of
({\bf X}_{n})
or by coupling arguments; see for example Rio
[
27
]
as a general reference and Section
5
below.

2.2.
The anti-clustering condition

Anti-clustering conditions are also standard for proving limit theory for
sums and maxima of stationary sequences; see for example Basrak and Segers

[
8
]
. They ensure that clusters of extreme events
cannot last forever.
The following condition was introduced
in Bartkiewicz et al.
[
6
]
;
it is particularly tailored for partial sums.

An
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
-valued stationary regularly varying sequence
({\bf X}_{t})

satisfies the
anti-clustering condition
if for some
r_{n}\to\infty
such that
k_{n}=[n/r_{n}]\to\infty
,

(2.2)

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}\limsup_{n\to\infty}n\,\sum_{j=k}^{r_{n}}{\mathbb{E}}\big[(|a_{n}^{-1}{\bf X}_{j}|\wedge x)\,(|a_{n}^{-1}{\bf X}_{0}|\wedge x)\big]=0\,,\qquad x=1\,.

Here and throughout the paper

(a_{n})
satisfies
n\,{\mathbb{P}}(|{\bf X}|>a_{n})\to 1
as
n\to\infty
.
Condition (
2.2
) can be checked by coupling arguments; see for example Kulik et al.
[
20
]
and Section
5
below.
For
a\geq 0
we have

a\wedge x\leq x\,(a\wedge 1)\,{\mathbf{1}}(x\geq 1)+(a\wedge 1)\,{\mathbf{1}}(x<1)
.
Therefore (
2.2
) holds for every
x>0
if it does for
x=1
.

Condition (
2.2
) is symmetric in time: since
({\bf X}_{t})

is stationary it is equivalent to

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}\limsup_{n\to\infty}n\,\sum_{j=k}^{r_{n}}{\mathbb{E}}\big[(|a_{n}^{-1}{\bf X}_{-j}|\wedge 1)\,(|a_{n}^{-1}{\bf X}_{0}|\wedge 1)\big]=0\,.

Since

a\wedge b=a\,{\mathbf{1}}(a\leq b)+b\,{\mathbf{1}}(a>b)
,
a,b>0
,
(
2.2
) implies

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}\limsup_{n\to\infty}n\,\sum_{j=k}^{r_{n}}{\mathbb{P}}\big(|{\bf X}_{j}|>x\,a_{n}\,,\,|{\bf X}_{0}|>x\,a_{n}\big)=0\,,\qquad x>0\,,

hence the anti-clustering
condition

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}\limsup_{n\to\infty}\,{\mathbb{P}}\big(\max_{j=k,\ldots,r_{n}}|{\bf X}_{j}|>x\,a_{n}\,\big|\,|{\bf X}_{0}|>x\,a_{n}\big)=0\,,\qquad x>0\,,

which is standard for proving limit theory for the extremes of a stationary
sequence; see for example Davis and Hsing
[
13
]
and Basrak and Segers
[
8
]
.

If
\alpha\in(1,2)
we have
{\mathbb{E}}[a_{n}^{-1}|{\bf X}|\wedge 1]\leq c/a_{n}
(here and in what follows,
c
denotes any positive constant whose value is not of interest)
while for
\alpha\in(0,1)
by Karamata’s theorem for regularly varying functions (see Bingham
et al.
[
9
]
)

{\mathbb{E}}[a_{n}^{-1}|{\bf X}|\wedge 1]\leq c/n
.
Therefore

\displaystyle n\,r_{n}({\mathbb{E}}[a_{n}^{-1}|{\bf X}|\wedge 1])^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}O(r_{n}/n)=o(1)\,,&\mbox{if $\alpha\in(0,1)$}\,,\\[5.69054pt]
O(r_{n}\,n/a_{n}^{2})=o(1)\,,&\mbox{if $\alpha\in(1,2)$ and also $r_{n}=o(a_{n}^{2}/n)$.}\end{array}\right.

Under these conditions on
(r_{n})
, (
2.2
) holds for an
iid sequence
({\bf X}_{t})
. Moreover, (
2.2
) turns into

(2.4)

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}\limsup_{n\to\infty}n\,\sum_{j=k}^{r_{n}}{\rm cov}\big(|a_{n}^{-1}{\bf X}_{j}|\wedge 1\,,|a_{n}^{-1}{\bf X}_{0}|\wedge 1\big)=0\,.

2.3.
Properties of regularly varying stationary sequences

Write
\mbox{\boldmath$\Theta$}=(\mbox{\boldmath$\Theta$}_{t})_{t\in{\mathbb{Z}}}
for the spectral tail process of
({\bf X}_{t})
.
Under (
2.1
) and (
2.2
) we have the property that

\|\mbox{\boldmath$\Theta$}\|_{\alpha}^{\alpha}=\sum_{t\in{\mathbb{Z}}}|\mbox{\boldmath$\Theta$}_{t}|^{\alpha}<\infty
a.s.; see
Janssen
[
17
]
and Buriticá et al.
[
11
]
. Then one can define
the
spectral cluster process

{\bf Q}=\mbox{\boldmath$\Theta$}/\|\mbox{\boldmath$\Theta$}\|_{\alpha}
. We will also make use of a change of measure of
{\bf Q}
in

\ell^{\alpha}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
given by

(2.5)

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}(\widetilde{\bf Q}\in\cdot)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}\Big(\dfrac{{\bf Q}}{\max_{t\in{\mathbb{Z}}}|{\bf Q}_{t}|}\in\cdot\Big|\max_{t\in{\mathbb{Z}}}|Y{\bf Q}_{t}|>1\Big)\,,

and the extremal index of
(|{\bf X}_{t}|)

can be expressed as

(2.6)

\displaystyle\theta_{|{\bf X}|}={\mathbb{P}}(Y\,\max_{t\in{\mathbb{Z}}}|{\bf Q}_{t}|>1)={\mathbb{E}}\big[\max_{t\in{\mathbb{Z}}}|{\bf Q}_{t}|^{\alpha}\big]\,.

An alternative way of defining regular variation of the stationary process

({\bf X}_{t})
is via the vague convergence relations on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d(h+1)}_{\bf 0}={\mathbb{R}}^{d(h+1)}\backslash\{\bf 0\}
, for
h\geq 0
,

(2.7)

\displaystyle n\,{\mathbb{P}}\big(a_{n}^{-1}({\bf X}_{0},\ldots,{\bf X}_{h})\in\cdot\big)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle v}}{{\rightarrow}}\mu_{h}(\cdot)\,,\qquad n\to\infty\,,

where the
tail measures

(\mu_{h})
are non-null and have the homogeneity
property
\mu_{h}(t\,\cdot)=t^{-\alpha}\,\mu_{h}(\cdot)
,
t>0
. We observe that, due to
stationarity of
({\bf X}_{t})
,
these measures have the consistency property
\mu_{h+1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times\cdot)=\mu_{h}(\cdot)=\mu_{h+1}(\cdot\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d})
; see Kulik and Soulier
[
19
]
.

An important tool for dealing with regularly varying stationary sequences is the
time-change
formula
(see Basrak and Segers
[
8
]
), for
h\geq 0
,
t\in{\mathbb{Z}}
,

(2.8)

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}((\mbox{\boldmath$\Theta$}_{-h},\ldots,\mbox{\boldmath$\Theta$}_{h})\in\cdot\mid\mbox{\boldmath$\Theta$}_{-t}\neq{\bf 0})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\Big[\dfrac{|\mbox{\boldmath$\Theta$}_{t}|^{\alpha}}{{\mathbb{E}}\big[|\mbox{\boldmath$\Theta$}_{t}|^{\alpha}\big]}{\mathbf{1}}\Big(\dfrac{(\mbox{\boldmath$\Theta$}_{t-h},\ldots,\mbox{\boldmath$\Theta$}_{t+h})}{|\mbox{\boldmath$\Theta$}_{t}|}\in\cdot\Big)\Big]\,.

3.
Joint convergence of sums, maxima and norms

3.1.
Joint convergence of sums and maxima

In this section we prove our main result about the joint convergence of normalized
maxima and sums based on a regularly varying stationary sequence. We restrict ourselves to
indices
\alpha\in(0,2)\backslash\{1\}
. The main reason for this is that
centering of sums for
\alpha=1
is complicated and stretches the proofs due to much
technical detail. Here and in the subsequent sections we use transform
techniques (hybrid characteristic functions, Laplace transforms). This is in contrast to
some of the more recent developments in limit theory for regularly varying stationary sequences;
various authors prefer to use point process techniques (first proving the weak convergence of the processes of the points
(a_{n}^{-1}{\bf X}_{t})
, then applying a.s. continuous
mappings to these points); see for example Davis and Hsing
[
13
]
,
Resnick
[
26
]
, Kulik and Soulier
[
19
]
,
Krizmanić
[
18
]
. They obtain the series representation (
0<\alpha<1
) of Lepage et al.
[
21
]

(3.1)

\displaystyle a_{n}^{-1}(M_{n}^{|{\bf X}|},{\bf S}_{n})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{\rightarrow}}\Big(\sup_{i\geq 1}\Gamma_{i}^{-1/\alpha}\sup_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}|{\bf Q}_{ij}|,\sum_{i\geq 1}\Gamma_{i}^{-1/\alpha}\sum_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}{\bf Q}_{ij}\Big)\,,\qquad n\to\infty\,.

When dealing with partial sums for
\alpha\in(1,2)
, a difficulty
is the
vanishing-small-values condition
, i.e., the sums of the truncated quantities

({\bf X}_{t}/a_{n})
have to be negligible. We avoid these conditions. A further advantage
of transform techniques is that it is easier to identify the (joint) limit distribution of
sums, maxima, self-normalized sums, and their distributional characteristics such as moments.

Theorem 3.1
.

Consider an
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
-valued regularly varying stationary process
({\bf X}_{t})

with index
\alpha\in(0,2)\backslash\{1\}
. If
\alpha\in(1,2)
we also assume
that
{\mathbb{E}}[{\bf X}]=0
.
Choose the normalizing constants

(a_{n})
such that
n\,{\mathbb{P}}(|{\bf X}|>a_{n})\to 1
as
n\to\infty
.
Assume the mixing condition (
2.1
) and
the anti-clustering condition (
2.2
)
for the same integer sequences

r_{n}\to\infty
,
k_{n}=[n/r_{n}]\to\infty
as
n\to\infty
.
Then

\displaystyle a_{n}^{-1}(M_{n}^{|{\bf X}|},{\bf S}_{n})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{\rightarrow}}(\eta_{\alpha},{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{\alpha})\,,\qquad n\to\infty\,,

where
\eta_{\alpha}
is Fréchet-distributed with a positive
extremal index
\theta_{|{\bf X}|}
and
{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{\alpha}
is
\alpha
-stable
with characteristic function

(3.2)

\displaystyle\varphi_{{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{\alpha}}({\bf u})=\exp\big(-c_{\alpha}\,\sigma^{\alpha}({\bf u})\big(1-i\,\beta({\bf u})\tan(\alpha\,\pi/2)\big)\big)\,,\qquad{\bf u}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\,,

constant
c_{\alpha}=\Gamma(2-\alpha)\,\cos(\alpha\,\pi/2)\,/(1-\alpha)
,
and parameter functions

(3.3)

\displaystyle\beta({\bf u})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{{\mathbb{E}}\big[({\bf u}^{\top}\sum_{t\in{\mathbb{Z}}}{\bf Q}_{t})_{+}^{\alpha}-({\bf u}^{\top}\sum_{t\in{\mathbb{Z}}}{\bf Q}_{t})_{-}^{\alpha}\big]}{{\mathbb{E}}\big[|{\bf u}^{\top}\sum_{t\in{\mathbb{Z}}}{\bf Q}_{t}|^{\alpha}\big]}\,,\qquad\sigma^{\alpha}({\bf u})={\mathbb{E}}\Big[\Big|{\bf u}^{\top}\sum_{t\in{\mathbb{Z}}}{\bf Q}_{t}\Big|^{\alpha}\Big]\,.

Moreover,
the joint distribution of
(\eta_{\alpha},{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{\alpha})
is characterized by the hybrid characteristic function

(3.4)

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\big[{\rm e}\,^{i\,{\bf u}^{\top}{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{\alpha}}{\mathbf{1}}(\eta_{\alpha}\leq x)\big]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varphi_{{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{\alpha}}({\bf u})\,\exp\Big(-\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}{\mathbb{E}}\Big[{\rm e}\,^{i\,y\,{\bf u}^{\top}\sum_{t=-\infty}^{\infty}{\bf Q}_{t}}\,{\mathbf{1}}\Big(y\,\max_{t\in{\mathbb{Z}}}|{\bf Q}_{t}|>x\Big)\Big]\,d(-y^{-\alpha})\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varphi_{{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{\alpha}}({\bf u})\,\Phi_{\alpha}^{\theta_{|{\bf X}|}}(x)\,\exp\Big(-\theta_{|{\bf X}|}\displaystyle\int_{x}^{\infty}{\mathbb{E}}\big[{\rm e}\,^{i\,y\,{\bf u}^{\top}\sum_{t=-\infty}^{\infty}\widetilde{\bf Q}_{t}}-1\big]\,d(-y^{-\alpha})\Big)\,,\;{\bf u}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\,,x>0\,,

where
{\bf Q}=\mbox{\boldmath$\Theta$}/\|\mbox{\boldmath$\Theta$}\|_{\alpha}
and
\widetilde{\bf Q}
is defined in (
2.5
) and the extremal index
\theta_{|{\bf X}|}
is given in (
2.6
).

In the iid univariate case and for general maximum domains of attraction,
Chow and Teugels
[
12
]
showed a
result of the type (
3.4
).

Setting
x=\infty
in (
3.4
), we conclude that

a_{n}^{-1}{\bf S}_{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{\rightarrow}}{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{\alpha}
. Similarly, setting
{\bf u}=\bf 0
, we obtain
for
x>0
,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}(a_{n}^{-1}M_{n}^{|{\bf X}|}\leq x)

\displaystyle\to

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}\big(\eta_{\alpha}\leq x\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\exp\Big(-\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}{\mathbb{P}}\Big(y\,\max_{t\in{\mathbb{Z}}}|{\bf Q}_{t}|>x\Big)\,d(-y^{-\alpha})\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\exp\Big(-x^{-\alpha}\,{\mathbb{E}}\Big[\max_{t\in{\mathbb{Z}}}|{\bf Q}_{t}|^{\alpha}\Big]\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Phi_{\alpha}^{\theta_{|{\bf X}|}}(x)\,,\qquad n\to\infty\,.

In the last step we used the identity (
2.6
). Therefore the factor

\displaystyle\exp\Big(-\theta_{|{\bf X}|}\displaystyle\int_{x}^{\infty}{\mathbb{E}}\big[{\rm e}\,^{i\,y\,{\bf u}^{\top}\sum_{t=-\infty}^{\infty}\widetilde{\bf Q}_{t}}-1\big]\,d(-y^{-\alpha})\Big)

in the limit hybrid characteristic function (
3.4
) describes the dependence
between
\eta_{\alpha}
and
{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{\alpha}
.

In the iid and asymptotically independent cases, i.e., when
\mbox{\boldmath$\Theta$}_{t}={\bf Q}_{t}=\bf 0
,
t\neq 0
,
{\bf Q}_{0}=\mbox{\boldmath$\Theta$}_{0}
, (
3.4
) turns into

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\big[{\rm e}\,^{i{\bf u}\,{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{\alpha}}\,{\mathbf{1}}(\eta_{\alpha}\leq x)\big]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varphi_{{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{\alpha}}({\bf u})\,\exp\Big(-\displaystyle\int_{x}^{\infty}{\mathbb{E}}\big[{\rm e}\,^{i\,y\,{\bf u}^{\top}\mbox{\boldmath$\Theta$}_{0}}\big]\,d(-y^{-\alpha})\Big)\,,

\displaystyle{\bf u}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\,,\qquad x>0\,.

The right-hand side does not factorize into
\varphi_{{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{\alpha}}({\bf u}){\mathbb{P}}(\eta_{\alpha}\leq x)
for all
{\bf u}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
and
x>0
. Therefore
\eta_{\alpha}

and
{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{\alpha}
are dependent. The hybrid characteristic function
\Psi
of
(\eta_{\alpha},{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{\alpha})
factorizes, i.e., for all
{\bf u}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,
x>0
,

\displaystyle\Psi({\bf u},x)={\mathbb{E}}\big[{\rm e}\,^{i{\bf u}\,{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{\alpha}}\,{\mathbf{1}}(\eta_{\alpha}\leq x)\big]=\varphi_{{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{\alpha}}({\bf u})\,{\mathbb{P}}(\eta_{\alpha}\leq x)\,,

if and only if
\eta_{\alpha}
,
{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{\alpha}
are independent since the quantity

\Psi
determines the distribution of
(\eta_{\alpha},{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{\alpha})
. In
view of (
3.4
),

\eta_{\alpha}
and
{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{\alpha}
are independent if and only if

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{x}^{\infty}{\mathbb{E}}\big[{\rm e}\,^{i\,y\,{\bf u}^{\top}\sum_{t=-\infty}^{\infty}\widetilde{\bf Q}_{t}}-1\big]\,d(-y^{-\alpha})=0\,,\qquad{\bf u}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\,,\qquad x>0\,.

If
Y
is Pareto
(\alpha)
and independent of
(\widetilde{\bf Q}_{t})
the latter condition
for
x=1
implies that the characteristic function of
Y\sum_{t=-\infty}^{\infty}\widetilde{\bf Q}_{t}
is
1
which in turn implies that

\sum_{t\in{\mathbb{Z}}}{\bf Q}_{t}=\sum_{t\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\mbox{\boldmath$\Theta$}_{t}=\bf 0
a.s. We conclude from the form of

\varphi_{{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{a}}
that
{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{\alpha}=\bf 0
a.s. Thus,
\eta_{\alpha}
and
{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{\alpha}

are independent if and only if
{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{\alpha}
degenerates.

Proof.

It suffices
to show that

(3.5)

\displaystyle\Psi_{n}({\bf u},x)\to\Psi({\bf u},x)\,,\qquad n\to\infty\,,\qquad{\bf u}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\,,\;x>0\,.

The mixing condition (
2.1
) and a Taylor expansion yield for
fixed
{\bf u}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,
x>0
,

(3.6)

\displaystyle\log\Psi_{n}({\bf u},x)

\displaystyle=

({\mathbb{E}}[{\bf S}_{n}/M_{n}^{|{\bf X}|}])
for dependent
({\bf X}_{t})
is an open question.

Proof.

By virtue of Theorem
3.1
and the continuous mapping theorem the limit relation
{\bf S}_{n}/{\bf M}_{n}^{|{\bf X}|}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{\rightarrow}}{\bf R}_{\alpha}
follows. It remains
to identify the characteristic function of
{\bf R}_{\alpha}
.

We re-write (
3.4
) as follows: for
x>0
and
{\bf u}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,

\displaystyle\Psi({\bf u},x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\big[{\rm e}\,^{i{\bf u}^{\top}{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{\alpha}}{\mathbf{1}}(\eta_{\alpha}\leq x)\big]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varphi_{{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{\alpha}}({\bf u})\exp\Big(-\theta_{|{\bf X}|}\displaystyle\int_{x}^{\infty}{\mathbb{E}}\big[{\rm e}\,^{i\,y\,{\bf u}^{\top}\sum_{t=-\infty}^{\infty}\widetilde{\bf Q}_{t}}\big]\,d(-y^{-\alpha})\Big)\,.

Denote the density of
\eta_{\alpha}
by
f_{\eta_{\alpha}}
. We differentiate both
sides of the latter identity with respect to
x
:

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\big[{\rm e}\,^{i\,{\bf u}^{\top}{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{\alpha}}\;\big|\;\eta_{\alpha}=x\big]\,f_{\eta_{\alpha}}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Psi({\bf u},x)\,\,\alpha\,x^{-\alpha-1}\,\theta_{|{\bf X}|}\,\underbrace{{\mathbb{E}}\big[{\rm e}\,^{i\,x\,{\bf u}^{\top}\sum_{t=-\infty}^{\infty}\widetilde{\bf Q}_{t}}\big]}_{=:g(x\,{\bf u})}\,.

In particular, we obtain

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\big[{\rm e}\,^{i{\bf u}^{\top}{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{\alpha}/x}\;\big|\;\eta_{\alpha}=x\big]\,f_{\eta_{\alpha}}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Psi({\bf u}/x,x)\,\,\alpha\,x^{-\alpha-1}\theta_{|{\bf X}|}\,\,g({\bf u})\,.

Integration yields the following expression for
\varphi_{{\bf R}_{\alpha}}({\bf u})
:

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}{\mathbb{E}}\big[{\rm e}\,^{i{\bf u}^{\top}{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{\alpha}/\eta_{\alpha}}\;\big|\;\eta_{\alpha}=x\big]\,f_{\eta_{\alpha}}(x)\,dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g({\bf u})\,\theta_{|{\bf X}|}\,\int_{0}^{\infty}\Psi({\bf u}/x,x)\,d(-x^{-\alpha})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g({\bf u})\,\theta_{|{\bf X}|}\,\int_{0}^{\infty}\varphi_{{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{\alpha}}({\bf u}/x)\,\exp\Big(-\theta_{|{\bf X}|}\displaystyle\int_{x}^{\infty}{\mathbb{E}}\big[{\rm e}\,^{i\,(y/x)\,{\bf u}^{\top}\sum_{t=-\infty}^{\infty}\widetilde{\bf Q}_{t}}\big]\,d(-y^{-\alpha})\Big)\,d(-x^{-\alpha})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g({\bf u})\,\theta_{|{\bf X}|}\,\int_{0}^{\infty}\exp\Big(x^{-\alpha}\Big(\log\varphi_{{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{\alpha}}({\bf u})-\theta_{|{\bf X}|}\displaystyle\int_{1}^{\infty}{\mathbb{E}}\big[{\rm e}\,^{i\,y\,{\bf u}^{\top}\sum_{t=-\infty}^{\infty}\widetilde{\bf Q}_{t}}\big]\,d(-y^{-\alpha})\Big)\Big)\,d(-x^{-\alpha})\,.

Plugging in the characteristic function
\varphi_{{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{\alpha}}
and
changing variables, we obtain

\displaystyle g({\bf u})\,\theta_{|{\bf X}|}\,\int_{0}^{\infty}\exp\Big(z\theta_{|{\bf X}|}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}{\mathbb{E}}\Big[{\rm e}\,^{iy{\bf u}^{\top}\sum_{t=-\infty}^{\infty}\widetilde{\bf Q}_{t}}{\mathbf{1}}(y\leq 1)-1-i\,y\,{\bf u}^{\top}\,\sum_{t\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\widetilde{\bf Q}_{t}\,{\mathbf{1}}_{(1,2)}(\alpha)\Big]

\displaystyle\hskip 256.0748ptd(-y^{-\alpha})\Big)\,dz

\displaystyle=\dfrac{{\mathbb{E}}\big[{\rm e}\,^{i\,{\bf u}^{\top}\sum_{t=-\infty}^{\infty}\widetilde{\bf Q}_{t}}\big]}{\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}{\mathbb{E}}\Big[1+i\,y\,{\bf u}^{\top}\,\sum_{t\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\widetilde{\bf Q}_{t}\,{\mathbf{1}}_{(1,2)}(\alpha)-{\rm e}\,^{iy{\bf u}^{\top}\sum_{t=-\infty}^{\infty}\widetilde{\bf Q}_{t}}{\mathbf{1}}(y\leq 1)\Big]\,d(-y^{-\alpha})}\,.

This is the desired formula for the characteristic function of
{\bf R}_{\alpha}
.
∎

Remark 4.3
.

By the continuous mapping theorem and a similar argument as in the proof above we have

\displaystyle\lim_{m\to\infty}\lim_{n\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}\big[{\rm e}\,^{i{\bf u}^{T}{\bf S}_{n}/M_{n}^{|{\bf X}|}}\mid M_{n}^{|{\bf X}|}>m\,a_{n}\big]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{m\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}\big[{\rm e}\,^{i{\bf u}^{T}{\bf R}_{\alpha}}\mid\eta_{\alpha}>m\big]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{m\to\infty}\dfrac{g({\bf u})\int_{m}^{\infty}\Psi({\bf u}/x,x)\,d(-x^{-\alpha})}{g({\bf 0})\int_{m}^{\infty}\Psi({\bf 0},x)d(-x^{-\alpha})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g({\bf u})\lim_{m\to\infty}\dfrac{\int_{0}^{m^{-\alpha}}\Psi({\bf u}\,y^{1/\alpha},y^{-1/\alpha})\,dy}{\int_{0}^{m^{-\alpha}}\Psi({\bf 0},y^{-1/\alpha})dy}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g({\bf u})\lim_{m\to\infty}\dfrac{\Psi({\bf u}/m,m)}{\Psi({\bf 0},m)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g({\bf u})\,.

Here we also used l’Hospital’s rule and the explicit form of the
hybrid characteristic function
\Psi
, satisfying
\Psi({\bf u}/m,m)\to 1
as
m\to\infty
.
Because
g({\bf u})={\mathbb{E}}\big[{\rm e}\,^{i\,{\bf u}^{\top}\sum_{t=-\infty}^{\infty}\widetilde{\bf Q}_{t}}\big]
,
{\bf u}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, this implies that as
m\to\infty
,

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}{\mathbb{P}}\Big(\dfrac{{\bf S}_{n}}{M_{n}^{|{\bf X}|}}\in\cdot\Big|M_{n}^{|{\bf X}|}>m\,a_{n}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}\Big(\dfrac{{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{\alpha}}{\eta_{\alpha}}\in\cdot\Big|\eta_{\alpha}>m\Big)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle w}}{{\rightarrow}}{\mathbb{P}}\Big(\sum_{t=-\infty}^{\infty}\widetilde{\bf Q}_{t}\in\cdot\Big)\,.

The case
{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{\alpha}=\bf 0
corresponds to
\sum_{t=-\infty}^{\infty}\widetilde{\bf Q}_{t}=\bf 0
a.s. In what follows, we assume that this condition is not satisfied.
Then
|{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{\alpha}|
and
\eta_{\alpha}
are tail-equivalent.
Using the regular variation property of
\eta_{\alpha}
, we get the convergence of

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}\big(m^{-1}({\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{\alpha},\eta_{\alpha})\in\{({\bf u},x)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times(0,\infty):({\bf u}/x,x)\in A\times(y,\infty)\}\big)/{\mathbb{P}}(\eta_{\alpha}>m)\,,\qquad m\to\infty\,,

for every continuity set
A
of the
distribution of
\sum_{t=-\infty}^{\infty}\widetilde{\bf Q}_{t}
and
y>0
. The sets
A\times(y,\infty)
generate the vague convergence in
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times(0,\infty)
of these measures, implying
that
({\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{\alpha},\eta_{\alpha})
is an
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}
-valued
regularly varying vector with index
\alpha>0
.
The regular variation properties of
({\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{\alpha},\eta_{\alpha})
then follows from the vague convergence on
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times(0,\infty)
extended to
(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}_{+})\setminus\{{\bf 0}\}
, using the regular variation properties of the
\alpha
-stable distribution of
{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{\alpha}
.

4.2.
Studentized sums

Corollary
4.1
deals with a special case of self-normalization
of the (possibly centered) sum processes
({\bf S}_{n})
constructed from an

{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
-valued regularly varying sequence
({\bf X}_{t})
with index
\alpha\in(0,2)\backslash\{1\}
. Indeed,
by virtue of the joint convergence of the partial sums and maxima
in Theorem
3.1

with the same
normalization
(a_{n})
the latter sequence can be replaced by
(M_{n}^{|{\bf X}|})
,
leading to a ratio limit theorem for sums and maxima. An advantage of
this approach is that the
unknown
sequence
(a_{n})
is replaced by
the observed maxima
(M_{n}^{|{\bf X}|})
. The price one has to pay for this is that
the limit distribution of the ratio is an unfamiliar distribution which does not belong to the
\alpha
-stable class and, for its evaluation, one has to employ Monte-Carlo simulations or numerical techniques.

The classical example of self-normalization is studentization:
the standard deviation in the normalization of the classical central limit theorem is replaced by an empirical estimator. Our goal is to derive limit theory
for the partial sums
({\bf S}_{n})
with the
\ell^{p}
-norm-type moduli

\gamma_{n,p}
for
p>0
defined in (
1.1
).
We assume
\alpha<2
and
p>\alpha
. Then
(|{\bf X}_{t}|^{p})
is a regularly varying sequence with index
\alpha/p
. In particular, we can always choose
p=2
, corresponding
to the classical studentization.

Following the ideas of the proof of Corollary
4.1
,
we obtain a limit for the ratios
(\gamma_{n,p}/M_{n}^{|{\bf X}|})
.

Corollary 4.4
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
3.2
we have

\displaystyle\dfrac{\gamma_{n,p}}{M_{n}^{|{\bf X}|}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{\rightarrow}}\dfrac{\zeta_{\alpha,p}}{\eta_{\alpha}}\,,\qquad n\to\infty.

For
p>\alpha
,
(\zeta_{\alpha,p}/\eta_{\alpha})^{p}
has Laplace transform

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}[{\rm e}\,^{-\lambda\,(\zeta_{\alpha,p}/\eta_{\alpha})^{p}}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{{\mathbb{E}}\big[{\rm e}\,^{-\lambda\,\sum_{t=-\infty}^{\infty}|\widetilde{\bf Q}_{t}|^{p}}\big]}{\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}{\mathbb{E}}\big[1-{\rm e}\,^{-y^{p}\,\lambda\,\sum_{t=-\infty}^{\infty}|\widetilde{\bf Q}_{t}|^{p}}{\mathbf{1}}(y\leq 1)\big]\,d(-y^{-\alpha})}\,,\qquad\lambda>0\,.

The proof follows the lines of the one for Corollary
4.1
; it
is omitted. Similarly to Remark
4.3
we can also derive that

(\zeta_{\alpha,p},\eta_{\alpha})
is an
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}
-value regularly varying vector with index
\alpha
, satisfying

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}\Big(\dfrac{\zeta_{\alpha,p}}{\eta_{\alpha}}\in\cdot\Big|\eta_{\alpha}>m\Big)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle w}}{{\rightarrow}}{\mathbb{P}}\Big(\sum_{t=-\infty}^{\infty}|\widetilde{\bf Q}_{t}|^{p}\in\cdot\Big),\qquad m\to\infty\,.

The studentized sums also converge

(4.2)

\displaystyle\dfrac{{\bf S}_{n}}{\gamma_{n,p}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{\rightarrow}}\dfrac{{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{\alpha}}{\zeta_{\alpha,p}}=:{{\bf R}}_{\alpha,p}\,,\qquad n\to\infty\,.

However, it is even more difficult to describe
the limit distribution. We can still derive the first moment of the ratio

{{\bf R}}_{\alpha,p}
from the joint characteristic function– Laplace transform

\Phi_{{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{\alpha},\zeta_{\alpha,p}^{p}}({\bf u},\lambda):={\mathbb{E}}\big[{\rm e}\,^{i\,{\bf u}^{\top}\,{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{\alpha}-\lambda\,\zeta_{\alpha,p}^{p}}\big]
,

({\bf u},\lambda)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}
, given in Theorem
3.2
.

Proposition 4.5
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
3.2
, (
4.2
) holds
and

(4.3)

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}[{\bf R}_{\alpha,p}]=\dfrac{\Gamma((1-\alpha)/p)}{\Gamma(1/p)\Gamma(1-\alpha/p)}{\mathbb{E}}\Big[\frac{\|{\bf Q}\|_{p}^{\alpha}}{{\mathbb{E}}[\|{\bf Q}\|_{p}^{\alpha}]}\dfrac{\sum_{t=-\infty}^{\infty}{\bf Q}_{t}}{\|{\bf Q}\|_{p}}\Big]\,.

Proof.

We start by verifying the finiteness of
{\mathbb{E}}[\|{\bf Q}\|_{p}^{\alpha-1}\|{\bf Q}\|_{1}]
,
\alpha<p
, ensuring
the integrability properties needed throughout this proof.
For
\alpha\geq 1
we use the monotonicity of the
\ell^{p}
-norms

\|{\bf Q}\|_{p}\leq\|{\bf Q}\|_{1}
for
p\geq 1
.
We obtain
{\mathbb{E}}[\|{\bf Q}\|_{p}^{\alpha-1}\|{\bf Q}\|_{1}]\leq{\mathbb{E}}[\|{\bf Q}\|_{1}^{\alpha}]
which is finite; see Remark
3.3
.
For
\alpha<1
we have
\|{\bf Q}\|_{p}\geq\|{\bf Q}\|_{\infty}
hence

\displaystyle\|{\bf Q}\|_{p}^{\alpha-1}\|{\bf Q}\|_{1}\leq\|{\bf Q}\|_{\infty}^{\alpha-1}\|{\bf Q}\|_{1}\leq\|{\bf Q}\|_{\alpha}^{\alpha}=1\,,

by definition of the spectral cluster process
({\bf Q}_{t})
.

The following formula is immediate from the definition of the
\Gamma
-function:

(4.4)

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{x^{1/p}}=\dfrac{1}{\Gamma(1/p)}\int_{0}^{\infty}\lambda^{1/p-1}{\rm e}\,^{-\lambda x}d\lambda=\dfrac{p}{\Gamma(1/p)}\int_{0}^{\infty}{\rm e}\,^{-\lambda^{p}x}d\lambda\,,\qquad x>0\,.

We need the following lemma whose proof is postponed to Appendix
B
.

Lemma 4.6
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
3.2
we have
{\mathbb{E}}\big[|{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{\alpha}|{\rm e}\,^{-\lambda^{p}\zeta_{\alpha,p}^{p}}\big]<\infty
.

Then an application of Fubini’s theorem and (
4.4
) yields

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}[{\bf R}_{\alpha,p}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\Big[\dfrac{{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{\alpha}}{(\zeta_{\alpha,p}^{p})^{1/p}}\Big]=\dfrac{p}{\Gamma(1/p)}\int_{0}^{\infty}{\mathbb{E}}\big[{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{\alpha}{\rm e}\,^{-\lambda^{p}\zeta_{\alpha,p}^{p}}\big]d\lambda\,.

By another application of Lemma
4.6
the integrand coincides with

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{i}\dfrac{\partial\Phi_{{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{\alpha},\zeta_{\alpha,p}^{p}}({\bf 0},\lambda^{p})}{\partial{\bf u}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}{\mathbb{E}}\Big[y\,\sum_{t=-\infty}^{\infty}{\bf Q}_{t}\,\big({\rm e}\,^{-(\lambda y)^{p}\|{\bf Q}\|_{p}^{p}}-{\mathbf{1}}_{(1,2)}(\alpha)\big)\Big]\,d(-y^{-\alpha})\;{\mathbb{E}}\big[{\rm e}\,^{-\lambda^{p}\zeta_{\alpha,p}^{p}}\big]\,.

Exploiting (
4.4
) for
0<\alpha<1
, the formula

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}z\,\big({\rm e}\,^{-z^{p}\,x}-1\big)\,d(-z^{-\alpha})=(\alpha/p)\Gamma((1-\alpha)/p)\,x^{(\alpha-1)/p}\,,

for
\alpha>1
,
and changing the variable to
z=\lambda\,y\,\|{\bf Q}\|_{p}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}{\mathbb{E}}\Big[y\sum_{t=-\infty}^{\infty}{\bf Q}_{t}{\rm e}\,^{-(\lambda y)^{p}\|{\bf Q}\|_{p}^{p}}\big({\rm e}\,^{-(\lambda y)^{p}\|{\bf Q}\|_{p}^{p}}-{\mathbf{1}}_{(1,2)}(\alpha)\big)\Big]d(-y^{-\alpha})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha}{p}\Gamma((1-\alpha)/p)\lambda^{\alpha-1}{\mathbb{E}}\Big[\|{\bf Q}\|_{p}^{\alpha}\dfrac{\sum_{t=-\infty}^{\infty}{\bf Q}_{t}}{\|{\bf Q}\|_{p}}\Big]\,.

Combining these results, one obtains

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}[{\bf R}_{\alpha,p}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{\Gamma((1-\alpha)/p)}{\Gamma(1/p)}{\mathbb{E}}\Big[\|{\bf Q}\|_{p}^{\alpha}\dfrac{\sum_{t=-\infty}^{\infty}{\bf Q}_{t}}{\|{\bf Q}\|_{p}}\Big]\int_{0}^{\infty}\alpha\lambda^{\alpha-1}{\mathbb{E}}\big[{\rm e}\,^{-\lambda^{p}\zeta_{\alpha,p}^{p}}\big]d\lambda\,.

We recall the Laplace transform of
\zeta_{\alpha,p}^{p}
derived in Remark
3.3
, i.e.,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\big[{\rm e}\,^{-\lambda^{p}\zeta_{\alpha,p}^{p}}\big]=\exp\big(-\Gamma(1-\alpha/p)\,{\mathbb{E}}[\|{\bf Q}\|_{p}^{\alpha}]\,\lambda^{\alpha}\big)\,,\qquad\lambda>0\,.

Plugging in the previous expression, we arrive at

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}[{\bf R}_{\alpha,p}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{\Gamma((1-\alpha)/p)}{\Gamma(1/p)}{\mathbb{E}}\Big[\|{\bf Q}\|_{p}^{\alpha}\dfrac{\sum_{t=-\infty}^{\infty}{\bf Q}_{t}}{\|{\bf Q}\|_{p}}\Big]\int_{0}^{\infty}{\rm e}\,^{-\Gamma(1-\alpha/p){\mathbb{E}}[\|{\bf Q}\|_{p}^{\alpha}]\lambda^{\alpha}}d(\lambda^{\alpha})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{\Gamma((1-\alpha)/p)}{\Gamma(1/p)}{\mathbb{E}}\Big[\|{\bf Q}\|_{p}^{\alpha}\dfrac{\sum_{t=-\infty}^{\infty}{\bf Q}_{t}}{\|{\bf Q}\|_{p}}\Big]\dfrac{1}{\Gamma(1-\alpha/p){\mathbb{E}}[\|{\bf Q}\|_{p}^{\alpha}]}\,.

The desired result follows.
∎

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}[T_{n,p}]\to{\mathbb{E}}\Big[\dfrac{\zeta_{\alpha,p}^{p}}{\xi_{\alpha}^{p}}\Big]

Consider a regularly varying stationary process
({\bf X}_{t})

with index
\alpha>0

{\|{\bf X}\|_{q}}/{\|{\bf X}\|_{r}}

(|\mbox{\boldmath$\Theta$}_{t}|^{q})

(Z_{t})
satisfies the conditions of Theorem

,

as in (

)
hold for every
({\bf u},x,\lambda)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times{\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{2}
and Theorem

applies.

Proof.

We start by checking (

it is enough to show that (

) holds. Using the basic inequality

\displaystyle|{\rm cov}(V,W)|\leq{\mathbb{E}}[|W-W^{\ast}||V|]\,,

where

independent of

for

a.s., we easily obtain the sufficient condition

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}\limsup_{n\to\infty}n\,\sum_{j=k}^{r_{n}}{\mathbb{E}}\big[\big(\big|a_{n}^{-1}\big({\bf X}_{j}-{\bf X}_{j}^{*}\big)\big|\wedge 1\big)(|a_{n}^{-1}{\bf X}_{0}|\wedge 1)\big]=0\,.

The desired result follows by observing that

for

Next we verify (

). We start by showing that

\displaystyle k_{n}\,a_{n}^{-q}\sum_{t=\ell_{n}}^{r_{n}}{\mathbb{E}}\big[|{\bf X}_{t}-{\bf X}_{t}^{\ast}|^{q}+\big||{\bf X}_{t}|^{p}-|{\bf X}_{t}^{\ast}|^{p}\big|^{q/p}\big]\to 0\,,\qquad n\to\infty\,.

For
\alpha<p\leq 1
this follows by the triangular inequality

\displaystyle\big||{\bf X}_{t}|^{p}-|{\bf X}_{t}^{\ast}|^{p}\big|^{q/p}\leq|{\bf X}_{t}-{\bf X}_{t}^{\ast}|^{q}\,,\qquad t\geq 1\,.

For

we apply the mean value theorem: there exists some

such that

\displaystyle\big||{\bf X}_{t}|^{p}-|{\bf X}_{t}^{\ast}|^{p}\big|^{q/p}=\big(p||{\bf X}_{t}|+\xi(|{\bf X}_{t}^{\ast}|-|{\bf X}_{t}|)|^{(p-1)}||{\bf X}_{t}|-|{\bf X}_{t}^{\ast}||\big)^{q/p}\,.

Then the Hölder inequality yields

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\big[\big||{\bf X}_{t}|^{p}-|{\bf X}_{t}^{\ast}|^{p}\big|^{q/p}\big]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c_{1}\,\big({\mathbb{E}}[||{\bf X}_{t}|+\xi(|{\bf X}_{t}^{\ast}|-|{\bf X}_{t}|)|^{q}\big]\big)^{(p-1)/p}\big({\mathbb{E}}\big[|{\bf X}_{t}-{\bf X}_{t}^{\ast}|^{q}\big]\big)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c_{2}\big({\mathbb{E}}\big[|{\bf X}_{t}-{\bf X}_{t}^{\ast}|^{q}\big]\big)^{1/p}\,,\qquad t\geq 1\,,

where

c_{2}>0
do not depend on

p\geq 1

Next we introduce the intermediate sequence
(\ell_{n})
in the mixing condition (

) by an application of an asymptotic negligibility argument on functionals on small blocks

,

Because the anti-clustering condition (

we deduce similarly as in the proof of Theorem

, see equation (

\displaystyle\Big(\dfrac{n}{\ell_{n}}\log\big({\mathbb{E}}\big[\exp\big(i\,a_{n}^{-1}{\bf u}^{\top}{\bf S}_{\ell_{n}}-a_{n}^{-p}\lambda\gamma_{\ell_{n},p}^{p}\big)\,{\mathbf{1}}\big(a_{n}^{-1}M_{\ell_{n}}^{|{\bf X}|}\leq x\big)\big]\big)\Big)

converge for every
({\bf u},x,\lambda)

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\log\Big({\mathbb{E}}\big[\exp\big(i\,a_{n}^{-1}{\bf u}^{\top}{\bf S}_{\ell_{n}}-a_{n}^{-p}\lambda\gamma_{\ell_{n},p}^{p}\big)\,{\mathbf{1}}\big(a_{n}^{-1}M_{\ell_{n}}^{|{\bf X}|}\leq x\big)\big]^{k_{n}}\Big)

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\dfrac{\ell_{n}}{r_{n}}\dfrac{n}{\ell_{n}}\log\big({\mathbb{E}}\big[\exp\big(i\,a_{n}^{-1}{\bf u}^{\top}{\bf S}_{\ell_{n}}-a_{n}^{-p}\lambda\gamma_{\ell_{n},p}^{p}\big)\,{\mathbf{1}}\big(a_{n}^{-1}M_{\ell_{n}}^{|{\bf X}|}\leq x\big)\big]\big)=0\,.

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\Big[\prod_{j=1}^{k_{n}}\exp\big(i\,a_{n}^{-1}{\bf u}^{\top}{\bf S}_{jr_{n}-\ell_{n},jr_{n}}-a_{n}^{-p}\lambda\gamma_{jr_{n}-\ell_{n},jr_{n},p}^{p}\big){\mathbf{1}}\big(a_{n}^{-1}M_{jr_{n}-\ell_{n},jr_{n}}^{|{\bf X}|}\leq x\big)\Big]

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\Big({\mathbb{E}}\big[\exp\big(i\,a_{n}^{-1}{\bf u}^{\top}{\bf S}_{\ell_{n}}-a_{n}^{-p}\lambda\gamma_{\ell_{n},p}^{p}\big)\,{\mathbf{1}}\big(a_{n}^{-1}M_{\ell_{n}}^{|{\bf X}|}\leq x\big)\big]\Big)^{k_{n}}=o(1)\,.

We conclude the asymptotic negligibility of the functionals on small blocks
({\bf X}_{r_{n}(j-1)+t})_{1\leq t\leq\ell_{n}}
,
1\leq j\leq k_{n}
under the modified condition (
5.1
) such that
(\ell_{n})
is replaced by
(r_{n}-\ell_{n})
.

Then the mixing condition (
3.16
) turns into one based on large blocks only

(5.4)

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\Big[\prod_{j=1}^{k_{n}}\exp\big(i\,a_{n}^{-1}{\bf u}^{\top}{\bf S}_{j(r_{n}-1)+\ell_{n},jr_{n}}-a_{n}^{-p}\lambda\gamma_{j(r_{n}-1)+\ell_{n},jr_{n},p}^{p}\big){\mathbf{1}}\big(a_{n}^{-1}M_{j(r_{n}-1)+\ell_{n},jr_{n}}^{|{\bf X}|}\leq x\big)\Big]

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\Big({\mathbb{E}}\big[\exp\big(i\,a_{n}^{-1}{\bf u}^{\top}{\bf S}_{\ell_{n},r_{n}}-a_{n}^{-p}\lambda\gamma_{\ell_{n},r_{n},p}^{p}\big)\,{\mathbf{1}}\big(a_{n}^{-1}M_{\ell_{n},r_{n}}^{|{\bf X}|}\leq x\big)\big]\Big)^{k_{n}}=o(1)\,.

We use a telescoping sum argument over

on the difference
and show that it converges to zero as

Using the properties of the coupled version, this difference can be expressed as a sum of

summands plus negligible terms which we ignore. Up to a constant multiplier the absolute value of a typical summand
is bounded by

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\big[\big|{\rm e}\,^{i\,a_{n}^{-1}\,{\bf u}^{\top}{\bf S}_{\ell_{n},r_{n}}-a_{n}^{-p}\,\lambda\,\gamma_{\ell_{n},r_{n},p}^{p}}{\mathbf{1}}\big(a_{n}^{-1}M_{\ell_{n},r_{n}}^{|{\bf X}|}\leq x\big)-{\rm e}\,^{i\,a_{n}^{-1}\,{\bf u}^{\top}{\bf S}_{\ell_{n},r_{n}}^{\ast}-a_{n}^{-p}\,\lambda\,\gamma_{\ell_{n},r_{n},p}^{\ast\,p}}{\mathbf{1}}\big(a_{n}^{-1}M_{\ell_{n},r_{n}}^{|{\bf X}^{\ast}|}\leq x\big)\big|\big]\,,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\big[\big|{\rm e}\,^{i\,a_{n}^{-1}{\bf u}^{\top}{\bf S}_{\ell_{n},r_{n}}}-{\rm e}\,^{i\,a_{n}^{-1}{\bf u}^{\top}{\bf S}_{\ell_{n},r_{n}}^{\ast}}\big|\big]+{\mathbb{E}}\big[\big|{\rm e}\,^{-a_{n}^{-p}\,\lambda\,\gamma_{\ell_{n},r_{n},p}^{p}}-{\rm e}\,^{-a_{n}^{-p}\,\lambda\,\gamma_{\ell_{n},r_{n},p}^{\ast\,p}}\big|\big]

\displaystyle+{\mathbb{E}}\big[\big|{\mathbf{1}}\big(a_{n}^{-1}M_{\ell_{n},r_{n}}^{|{\bf X}^{|}}\leq x\big)-{\mathbf{1}}\big(a_{n}^{-1}M_{\ell_{n},r_{n}}^{|{\bf X}^{\ast}|}\leq x\big)\big|\big]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\big[\big|\big(a_{n}^{-1}{\bf u}^{\top}({\bf S}_{\ell_{n},r_{n}}-{\bf S}_{\ell_{n},r_{n}}^{\ast})\big)\wedge 1\big|^{q}\big]+{\mathbb{E}}\big[\big|\big(a_{n}^{-p}\,\lambda\,(\gamma_{\ell_{n},r_{n},p}^{p}-\gamma_{\ell_{n},r_{n},p}^{\ast\,p})\big)\wedge 1\big|^{q^{\prime}}\big]

\displaystyle+\Big[{\mathbb{P}}\big(a_{n}^{-1}M_{\ell_{n},r_{n}}^{|{\bf X}|}>x\,,a_{n}^{-1}M_{\ell_{n},r_{n}}^{|{\bf X}^{\ast}|}\leq x\big)+{\mathbb{P}}\big(a_{n}^{-1}M_{\ell_{n},r_{n}}^{|{\bf X}^{\ast}|}>x\,,a_{n}^{-1}M_{\ell_{n},r_{n}}^{|{\bf X}|}\leq x\big)\Big]

\displaystyle=:

\displaystyle I_{1}+I_{2}+I_{3}\,.

Here we choose
q^{\prime}=q/p<1
. Then
we have for some constant
c=c({\bf u},\lambda)>0
,

\displaystyle k_{n}(I_{1}+I_{2})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c\,k_{n}\,a_{n}^{-q}\,\sum_{t=\ell_{n}}^{r_{n}}{\mathbb{E}}\big[|{\bf X}_{t}-{\bf X}_{t}^{\ast}|^{q}\big]+\big||{\bf X}_{t}|^{p}-|{\bf X}_{t}^{\ast}|^{p}\big|^{q/p}\big]\,,

and the right-hand side converges to zero in view of (
5.3
).

By a symmetry argument it suffices to bound the first term in
I_{3}
; the second one can be treated analogously. Since the anti-clustering condition is satisfied
by
(|{\bf X}_{t}|)
there exists
\theta>0
such that
for every
x>0
, we apply Theorem 3 of Segers
[
29
]
to obtain

\displaystyle\theta_{n}=\dfrac{{\mathbb{P}}(M_{r_{n}}^{|{\bf X}|}>x\,a_{n})}{r_{n}\,{\mathbb{P}}(|{\bf X}|>x\,a_{n})}\to\theta\,,\qquad n\to\infty\,.

Thus, for every
\varepsilon>0
,

\displaystyle k_{n}{\mathbb{P}}\Big(a_{n}^{-1}M_{r_{n}}^{|{\bf X}|}>x\,,a_{n}^{-1}M_{r_{n}}^{|{\bf X}^{\ast}|}\leq x\,,\max_{1\leq t\leq r_{n}}|{\bf X}_{t}-{\bf X}_{t}^{\ast}|\leq x\,\varepsilon\,a_{n}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle k_{n}\,\big({\mathbb{P}}\big(M_{r_{n}}^{|{\bf X}|}>x\,a_{n}\big)-{\mathbb{P}}\big(M_{r_{n}}^{|{\bf X}|}>x\,(1+\varepsilon)\,a_{n}\big)\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\theta\,n\,\big({\mathbb{P}}(|{\bf X}|>x\,a_{n})-{\mathbb{P}}(|{\bf X}|>x(1+\varepsilon)\,a_{n})\big)+o(1)

\displaystyle\to

\displaystyle\theta\,x^{-\alpha}\,(1-(1+\varepsilon)^{-\alpha})\,,\qquad n\to\infty\,,

and the right-hand side converges to
0
as
\varepsilon\downarrow 0
.
Moreover, for every
x,\varepsilon>0
,

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}k_{n}\,{\mathbb{P}}\big(\max_{1\leq t\leq r_{n}}|{\bf X}_{t}-{\bf X}_{t}^{\ast}|>x\,\varepsilon\,a_{n}\big)=0\,.

This follows from (
5.1
) and an application of Markov’s inequality of the order

q
. Thus we proved that
\lim_{n\to\infty}k_{n}\,I_{3}=0
. The proof is finished.
∎

5.2.
Iterated random Lipschitz functions

Assume
({\bf X}_{t})
is the solution of a system of iterated random functions: there exists a function
g
and an iid sequence
(\varepsilon_{t})
such that

(5.6)

\displaystyle{\bf X}_{t}=g({\bf X}_{t-1},\varepsilon_{t}),\qquad t\in{\mathbb{Z}}\,.

We assume that this system is
contractive
, i.e., there exist

0<\rho<1
and
q>0
such that

(5.7)

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}[|g({\bf x}_{0},\varepsilon_{0})-g({\bf x}_{1},\varepsilon_{0})|^{q}]\leq\rho\,|{\bf x}_{0}-{\bf x}_{1}|^{q}\,,\qquad{\bf x}_{0},{\bf x}_{1}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\,.

If there exists
{\bf x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that

(5.8)

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}[|g({\bf x},\varepsilon_{0})|^{q}]<\infty\,,

the fixed point theorem in the complete space
L^{q}
ensures
the existence of a unique stationary solution
({\bf X}_{t})

which admits finite moments of order
q>0
.

A coupled version
({\bf X}_{t}^{\ast})
is easily obtained as follows:

\displaystyle{\bf X}_{t}^{\ast}=\begin{cases}g({\bf X}_{t-1}^{\ast},\epsilon_{t}),&t\geq 1\,,\\
g({\bf X}_{t-1}^{\ast},\epsilon_{t}^{\prime}),&t\leq 0\,,\end{cases}

where
(\epsilon_{t}^{\prime})
is an independent copy of
(\epsilon_{t})
. We have

Proposition 5.2
.

Assume that the unique stationary solution
({\bf X}_{t})
of the
iterated random function system (
5.6
) is regularly varying with index
\alpha>0
and satisfies (
5.7
),
(
5.8
) for some
\rho\in(0,1)
and
q<\alpha\wedge 1
. Then the conditions of Proposition
5.1

are satisfied.

Proof.

A recursive argument yields for some constant
c>0
,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\big[|{\bf X}_{t}-{\bf X}_{t}^{\ast}|^{q}]\leq c\,\rho^{t}\,,\qquad t\geq 1\,.

Then (2) in Proposition
5.1
follows choosing
C>0
sufficiently large in the intermediate sequence
\ell_{n}=[C\log n]
such that

\displaystyle k_{n}\,a_{n}^{-q}\sum_{t=\ell_{n}}^{r_{n}}\big({\mathbb{E}}\big[|{\bf X}_{t}-{\bf X}_{t}^{\ast}|^{q}\big]\big)^{(1/p)\vee 1}\leq c\rho^{\ell_{n}}\dfrac{n}{r_{n}a_{n}^{q}}\leq c\dfrac{n^{C\log\rho+1-\delta}}{a_{n}^{q}}\to 0\,,\qquad n\to\infty\,.

It remains to verify (3) in Proposition
5.1
.We also have

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\big[\big(\big|a_{n}^{-1}\big({\bf X}_{t}-{\bf X}_{t}^{*}\big)\big|^{q}\wedge 1\big)\mid{\bf X}_{t-1},{\bf X}_{t-1}^{\ast}\big]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\big[|a_{n}^{-1}({\bf X}_{t}-{\bf X}_{t}^{*})|^{q}\mid{\bf X}_{t-1},{\bf X}_{t-1}^{\ast}\big]\wedge 1

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\big(\rho\,\big|a_{n}^{-1}\big({\bf X}_{t-1}-{\bf X}_{t-1}^{*}\big)\big|^{q})\wedge 1\,.

Thus, using the filtration
\mathcal{F}_{t}=\sigma(\epsilon_{t},\ldots,\epsilon_{1},{\bf X}_{0},{\bf X}_{0}^{\prime})
,
t\geq 1
, and the Markov property we obtain

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\big[\big(\big|a_{n}^{-1}\big({\bf X}_{t}-{\bf X}_{t}^{*}\big)\big|^{q}\wedge 1\big)\big(\big|a_{n}^{-1}{\bf X}_{0}\big|^{q}\wedge 1\big)\big]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\big[{\mathbb{E}}\big[\big(\big|a_{n}^{-1}\big({\bf X}_{t}-{\bf X}_{t}^{*}\big)\big|^{q}\wedge 1\big)\mid\mathcal{F}_{t-1}\big]\big(\big|a_{n}^{-1}{\bf X}_{0}\big|^{q}\wedge 1\big)\big]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\big[\big(\big(\rho\,\big|a_{n}^{-1}\big({\bf X}_{t-1}-{\bf X}_{t-1}^{*}\big)\big|^{q})\wedge 1\big)\big(\big|a_{n}^{-1}{\bf X}_{0}\big|^{q}\wedge 1\big)\big]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\big[\big(\big(\rho^{t}\,\big|a_{n}^{-1}\big({\bf X}_{0}-{\bf X}_{0}^{*}\big)\big|^{q})\wedge 1\big)\big(\big|a_{n}^{-1}{\bf X}_{0}\big|^{q}\wedge 1\big)\big]\,,

again using a recursive argument in the last step. Observing that

{\bf X}_{0}
and
{\bf X}_{0}^{\ast}
are independent, we obtain

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\big[\big(\big|a_{n}^{-1}\big({\bf X}_{t}-{\bf X}_{t}^{*}\big)\big|^{q}\wedge 1\big)\big(\big|a_{n}^{-1}{\bf X}_{0}\big|^{q}\wedge 1\big)\big]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\big[\big(\,|\rho^{t/q}a_{n}^{-1}{\bf X}_{0}|^{q}\wedge 1\big)\big(\big|a_{n}^{-1}{\bf X}_{0}\big|^{q}\wedge 1\big)\big]+\rho^{t}\,a_{n}^{-2q}{\mathbb{E}}\big[\big|{\bf X}_{0}\big|^{q}\big]^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Big({\mathbb{E}}\big[\big(\,|\rho^{t/q}a_{n}^{-1}{\bf X}_{0}|^{q}\wedge 1\big)^{2}\big]\,{\mathbb{E}}\big[\big(\big|a_{n}^{-1}{\bf X}_{0}\big|^{q}\wedge 1\big)^{2}\big]\Big)^{1/2}+\rho^{t}\,a_{n}^{-2q}\big({\mathbb{E}}\big[\big|{\bf X}_{0}\big|^{q}\big]\big)^{2}\,.

By Karamata’s theorem there exists a positive constant
c>0
such that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\big[\big(\,|\rho^{t/q}a_{n}^{-1}{\bf X}_{0}|^{q}\wedge 1\big)^{2}\big]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c\rho^{\alpha t/q}{\mathbb{P}}(|X_{0}|>a_{n})

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\big[\big(\,|a_{n}^{-1}{\bf X}_{0}|^{q}\wedge 1\big)^{2}\big]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c\,{\mathbb{P}}(|X_{0}|>a_{n})\,.

For
q>\alpha/2
we achieve

\displaystyle n{\mathbb{E}}\big[\big(\big|a_{n}^{-1}\big({\bf X}_{t}-{\bf X}_{t}^{*}\big)\big|^{q}\wedge 1\big)\big(\big|a_{n}^{-1}{\bf X}_{0}\big|^{q}\wedge 1\big)\big]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c\rho^{\alpha t/(2q)}+o(1)\,.

Then (3) in Proposition
5.1
and the desired result follow.
∎

5.3.
Examples

In this section we consider two examples of regularly varying stationary time series:
an autoregressive process or order 1 (AR(1)) and the solution to
an affine stochastic recurrence equation (SRE).

Example 5.3
.

A regularly varying AR(1) process.

We consider the causal stationary solution of the AR(1) equations

X_{t}=\varphi X_{t-1}+Z_{t}
,
t\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, for some
\varphi\in(-1,1)\backslash\{0\}

and an iid regularly varying noise sequence
(Z_{t})
. This means that a generic element

Z
satisfies the tail balance condition, for
q_{\pm}\geq 0
such that
q_{+}+q_{-}=1
,

\displaystyle\dfrac{{\mathbb{P}}(\pm Z>x)}{{\mathbb{P}}(|Z|>x)}\to q_{\pm}\,,\qquad x\to\infty\,.

Then
(Z_{t})
has spectral tail process
{\mathbb{P}}(\Theta_{0}^{Z}=\pm 1)=q_{\pm}
,
\Theta_{t}^{Z}=0
,
t\neq 0
.
It is well known (e.g. Kulik and Soulier
[
19
]
)
that
(X_{t})
is regularly varying with index
\alpha
and spectral tail process

\displaystyle\Theta_{t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Theta_{Z}\,{\rm sign}(\varphi^{J+t})|\varphi|^{t}\,{\mathbf{1}}(J+t\geq 0)=\Theta_{Z}\,{\rm sign}(\varphi^{J})\varphi^{t}\,{\mathbf{1}}(t\geq-J)=\Theta_{0}\,\varphi^{t}\,{\mathbf{1}}(t\geq-J)\,,\qquad t\in{\mathbb{Z}}\,,

where
{\mathbb{P}}(\Theta_{0}=\pm 1)=p_{\pm}
,
J
and
(\Theta_{Z})
are independent, and

\displaystyle p_{\pm}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle q_{\pm}\,{\mathbf{1}}(\varphi>0)+\dfrac{q_{\pm}+q_{\mp}|\varphi|^{\alpha}}{1+|\varphi|^{\alpha}}\,{\mathbf{1}}(\varphi<0)\,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}(J=j)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|\varphi|^{\alpha\,j}\,(1-|\varphi|^{\alpha})\,,\qquad j=0,1,\ldots\,.

The
forward spectral tail process
is given by

\Theta_{t}=\Theta_{0}\,\varphi^{t}
,
t\geq 0
, and the spectral cluster process
(Q_{t})

by
Q_{t}=\Theta_{t}/\|\Theta\|_{\alpha}=\Theta_{t}\,(1-|\varphi|^{\alpha})^{1/\alpha}
,
t\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, and
the extremal index by
\theta_{|X|}=1-|\varphi|^{\alpha}
.

We observe that
X_{t}=g(X_{t-1},Z_{t})=\varphi\,X_{t-1}+Z_{t}
,
t\in{\mathbb{Z}}
,
constitute a
contractive iterative function system: for
q<\alpha
,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\big[|g(x_{0},Z_{0})-g(x_{1},Z_{0})|^{q}\big]=|\varphi|^{q}\,|x_{0}-x_{1}|^{q}\,.

Hence the conditions of Proposition
5.2
are satisfied and all
mixing and anti-clustering conditions needed for the results in this paper
hold.

Example 5.4
.

A regularly varying solution to a SRE.

We consider the causal solution to an affine SRE
X_{t}=A_{t}X_{t-1}+B_{t}
,
t\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, where
(A_{t},B_{t})
,
t\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, is an iid
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
-valued sequence.
We further assume that a generic element
(A,B)
of this sequence satisfies
the following conditions: (i) there exists
\alpha>0
such that
{\mathbb{E}}[|A|^{\alpha}]=1
,
{\mathbb{E}}[|A|^{\alpha}\log^{+}|A|]<\infty
,
{\mathbb{E}}[|B|^{\alpha}]<\infty
, (ii) the conditional law of

\log|A|
given
\{A\neq 0\}
is non-arithmetic, (iii)
{\mathbb{P}}(A\,x+B=x)<1
for every

x\in{\mathbb{R}}
. Then there exists an a.s. unique causal solution to the SRE
with the property
{\mathbb{P}}(\pm X_{0}>x)\sim c_{\pm}\,x^{-\alpha}
as
x\to\infty

for constants
c_{\pm}
such that
c_{+}+c_{-}>0
. This follows from classical
Kesten-Goldie theory; cf. Theorem 2.4.7 in Buraczewski et al.
[
10
]
. The sequence
(X_{t})
is regularly varying with index
\alpha
,
forward spectral tail process
\Theta_{t}=\Theta_{0}\,A_{1}\cdots A_{t}
,
t\geq 0
,
where
{\mathbb{P}}(\Theta=\pm 1)=c_{\pm}/(c_{+}+c_{-})
and extremal index

\theta_{|X|}={\mathbb{P}}\big[\big(1-\sup_{t\geq 1}|A_{1}\cdots A_{t}|^{\alpha}\big)_{+}\big]
;
see Basrak and Segers
[
8
]
.

We observe that
X_{t}=g(X_{t-1},(A_{t},B_{t}))=A_{t}X_{t-1}+B_{t}
,
t\in{\mathbb{Z}}
,
constitute a contractive iterative function system: for
0<q<\alpha
, with
\rho={\mathbb{E}}[|A_{0}|^{q}]
,

{\mathbb{E}}[|g(x_{0},(A_{0},B_{0}))-g(x_{1},(A_{0},B_{0}))|^{q}]=\rho\,|x_{0}-x_{1}|^{q}\,.

The value
\rho<1
by convexity of the function
f(q)={\mathbb{E}}[|A_{0}|^{q}]
and since

f(\alpha)=1
. Hence the conditions of Proposition
5.2
are satisfied and all
mixing and anti-clustering conditions needed for the results in this paper
hold.

Appendix A
Some auxiliary results

Lemma A.1
.

Consider an
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
-valued stationary regularly varying sequence
({\bf X}_{t})

with index
\alpha\in(1,2)
. If the anti-clustering condition (
2.2
)
is satisfied then

(A.1)

\displaystyle J:={\mathbb{E}}\Big[\Big(\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}|\mbox{\boldmath$\Theta$}_{j}|\Big)^{\alpha-1}\Big]<\infty\,.

Proof.

By sub-additivity we have

\displaystyle J

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\Big[\Big(\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}|\mbox{\boldmath$\Theta$}_{j}|\,{\mathbf{1}}(|\mbox{\boldmath$\Theta$}_{j}|\leq 1)\Big)^{\alpha-1}\Big]+{\mathbb{E}}\Big[\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}|\mbox{\boldmath$\Theta$}_{j}|^{\alpha-1}\,{\mathbf{1}}(|\mbox{\boldmath$\Theta$}_{j}|>1)\Big]=:I_{1}+I_{2}\,.

By Jensen’s inequality,

\displaystyle I_{1}\leq\Big({\mathbb{E}}\Big[\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}|\mbox{\boldmath$\Theta$}_{j}|\,{\mathbf{1}}(|\mbox{\boldmath$\Theta$}_{j}|\leq 1)\Big]\Big)^{\alpha-1}\,.

We prove that the right-hand side is finite by showing

(A.2)

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}{\mathbb{E}}\big[|\mbox{\boldmath$\Theta$}_{j}|\wedge 1\big]<\infty\,.

Using the decomposition

\displaystyle|a_{n}^{-1}{\bf X}_{j}|\wedge 1

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|a_{n}^{-1}{\bf X}_{j}|\,{\mathbf{1}}(|{\bf X}_{j}|\leq a_{n})+{\mathbf{1}}(|{\bf X}_{j}|>a_{n})\,,

condition (
2.2
) implies

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}\limsup_{n\to\infty}\sum_{j=k}^{r_{n}}\,n\,\,{\mathbb{E}}\big[(|a_{n}^{-1}{\bf X}_{j}|\wedge 1)\,{\mathbf{1}}(|{\bf X}_{0}|>a_{n})\big]=0\,.

Hence for every

\varepsilon>0
there exists an integer
k_{0}
sufficiently large such that

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}\sum_{j=k}^{k+h}\,n\,\,{\mathbb{E}}\big[(|a_{n}^{-1}{\bf X}_{j}|\wedge 1)\,{\mathbf{1}}(|{\bf X}_{0}|>a_{n})\big]\leq\varepsilon\,,\qquad k\geq k_{0}\,,\qquad h\geq 0\,.

Using the regular variation of
({\bf X}_{t})
with tail process
(Y\,\mbox{\boldmath$\Theta$}_{t})
,
we can determine the limit of each summand

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}n\,\,{\mathbb{E}}\big[(|a_{n}^{-1}{\bf X}_{j}|\wedge 1)\,{\mathbf{1}}(|{\bf X}_{0}|>a_{n})\big]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}\big[(|a_{n}^{-1}{\bf X}_{j}|\wedge 1)\;\big|\;|{\bf X}_{0}|>a_{n}\big]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\big[|Y\,\mbox{\boldmath$\Theta$}_{j}|\wedge 1\big]\,.

Thus the Cauchy criterion for the sequence
(\sum_{j=0}^{k}{\mathbb{E}}\big[|Y\mbox{\boldmath$\Theta$}_{j}|\wedge 1\big])_{k\geq 0}
holds.
Since
Y>1
a.s. (
A.2
) follows.

It remains to show that
I_{2}<\infty
.
By stationarity we can show similarly to (
A.2
) that

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}{\mathbb{E}}[|\mbox{\boldmath$\Theta$}_{-j}|\land 1]<\infty.

By the time-change formula (
2.8
) on p.
2.8
we obtain that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}[|\mbox{\boldmath$\Theta$}_{-j}|\land 1]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}[|\mbox{\boldmath$\Theta$}_{-j}|\land 1\mid\mbox{\boldmath$\Theta$}_{-j}\neq{\bf 0}]{\mathbb{P}}(\mbox{\boldmath$\Theta$}_{-j}\neq{\bf 0})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}[|\mbox{\boldmath$\Theta$}_{-j}|\land|\mbox{\boldmath$\Theta$}_{0}|\mid\mbox{\boldmath$\Theta$}_{-j}\neq{\bf 0}]{\mathbb{E}}\big[|\mbox{\boldmath$\Theta$}_{j}|^{\alpha}\big]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}[|\mbox{\boldmath$\Theta$}_{j}|^{\alpha}\,(|\mbox{\boldmath$\Theta$}_{j}|^{-1}\land 1)]\,.

We conclude that

\displaystyle\infty

\displaystyle>

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}{\mathbb{E}}[|\mbox{\boldmath$\Theta$}_{-j}|\land 1]=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}{\mathbb{E}}[|\mbox{\boldmath$\Theta$}_{j}|^{\alpha}\,(|\mbox{\boldmath$\Theta$}_{j}|^{-1}\land 1)]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}{\mathbb{E}}[|\mbox{\boldmath$\Theta$}_{j}|^{\alpha-1}\land|\mbox{\boldmath$\Theta$}_{j}|^{\alpha}]>\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}{\mathbb{E}}[|\mbox{\boldmath$\Theta$}_{j}|^{\alpha-1}{\mathbf{1}}(|\mbox{\boldmath$\Theta$}_{j}|>1)],

implying
I_{2}<\infty
.

∎

Appendix B
Proof of Lemma
4.6

Proof.

For
1<\alpha<2
,
{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{\alpha}
is
\alpha
-stable and
{\mathbb{E}}[|{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{\alpha}|]<\infty
which implies the statement of Lemma
4.6
.

Next we consider the case
0<\alpha<1
. We use the series representation (
3.1
):

\displaystyle|{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{\alpha}|=\Big|\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\Gamma_{i}^{-1/\alpha}\sum_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}{\bf Q}_{ij}\Big|\leq\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\Gamma_{i}^{-1/\alpha}\sum_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}|{\bf Q}_{ij}|=:\xi_{\alpha}^{\prime}\,.

The right-hand side represents a positive
\alpha
-stable random variable
\xi_{\alpha}^{\prime}
.
Indeed,
(\sum_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}|{\bf Q}_{ij}|)_{i\in{\mathbb{Z}}}
is a sequence of iid random variables satisfying
{\mathbb{E}}[(\sum_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}|{\bf Q}_{j}|)^{\alpha}]<\infty
since
\|{\bf Q}\|_{\alpha}=1
by definition.
We apply Fubini’s theorem for positive random variables and obtain

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\Big[\dfrac{\xi_{\alpha}^{\prime}}{(\zeta_{\alpha,p}^{p})^{1/p}}\Big]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{p}{\Gamma(1/p)}\int_{0}^{\infty}{\mathbb{E}}\big[\xi_{\alpha}^{\prime}{\rm e}\,^{-\lambda^{p}\zeta_{\alpha,p}^{p}}\big]d\lambda\,.

We will show that the integrand coincides with

\displaystyle\lim_{x\to 0^{+}}-\dfrac{\partial\Psi_{\xi_{\alpha}^{\prime},\zeta_{\alpha,p}^{p}}(x,\lambda^{p})}{\partial x}\,,\qquad\Psi_{\xi_{\alpha}^{\prime},\zeta_{\alpha,p}^{p}}(x,\lambda^{p}):={\mathbb{E}}\big[{\rm e}\,^{-x\,\xi_{\alpha}^{\prime}-\lambda^{p}\,\zeta_{\alpha,p}^{p}}\big]\,.

From the expression of the characteristic function - Laplace transform of Theorem
3.2
we have

\displaystyle\Psi_{\xi_{\alpha}^{\prime},\zeta_{\alpha,p}^{p}}(x,\lambda^{p})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\exp\Big(\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}{\mathbb{E}}\Big[{\rm e}\,^{-y\,x\,\|{\bf Q}\|_{1}-y^{p}\lambda\|{\bf Q}\|_{p}^{p}}-1\Big]d(-y^{-\alpha})\Big)

\displaystyle-\dfrac{\partial\Psi_{\xi_{\alpha}^{\prime},\zeta_{\alpha,p}^{p}}(x,\lambda^{p})}{\partial x}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}{\mathbb{E}}\Big[y\,\|{\bf Q}\|_{1}{\rm e}\,^{-xy\|{\bf Q}\|_{1}-(\lambda y)^{p}\|{\bf Q}\|_{p}^{p}}\Big]d(-y^{-\alpha})\;{\mathbb{E}}\big[{\rm e}\,^{-x\xi_{\alpha}^{\prime}-\lambda^{p}\zeta_{\alpha,p}^{p}}\big]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\Big[\|{\bf Q}\|_{p}^{\alpha}\dfrac{\|{\bf Q}\|_{1}}{\|{\bf Q}\|_{p}}\int_{0}^{\infty}y\,{\rm e}\,^{-xy\|{\bf Q}\|_{1}/\|{\bf Q}\|_{p}-(\lambda y)^{p}}d(-y^{-\alpha})\Big]\;{\mathbb{E}}\big[{\rm e}\,^{-x\xi_{\alpha}^{\prime}-\lambda^{p}\zeta_{\alpha,p}^{p}}\big]

\displaystyle\to

\displaystyle\dfrac{\alpha}{p}\Gamma((1-\alpha)/p)\lambda^{\alpha-1}{\mathbb{E}}\Big[\|{\bf Q}\|_{p}^{\alpha}\dfrac{\|{\bf Q}\|_{1}}{\|{\bf Q}\|_{p}}\Big]\;{\mathbb{E}}\big[{\rm e}\,^{-\lambda^{p}\zeta_{\alpha,p}^{p}}\big]\,,\qquad x\to 0^{+}\,,

where we exploit (
4.4
) in the last step. By monotone convergence we also have

\displaystyle\lim_{x\to 0^{+}}-\dfrac{\partial\Psi_{\xi_{\alpha}^{\prime},\zeta_{\alpha,p}^{p}}(x,\lambda^{p})}{\partial x}=\lim_{x\to 0^{+}}{\mathbb{E}}\big[\xi_{\alpha}^{\prime}{\rm e}\,^{-x\,\xi_{\alpha}^{\prime}-\lambda^{p}\,\zeta_{\alpha,p}^{p}}\big]={\mathbb{E}}\big[\xi_{\alpha}^{\prime}{\rm e}\,^{-\lambda^{p}\,\zeta_{\alpha,p}^{p}}\big]

since the limit exists. Thus
{\mathbb{E}}\big[\xi_{\alpha}^{\prime}{\rm e}\,^{-\lambda^{p}\,\zeta_{\alpha,p}^{p}}\big]<\infty
and we conclude the proof of Lemma
4.6
using the domination
|{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{\alpha}|\leq\xi_{\alpha}^{\prime}
.
∎

Appendix C
Proof of the integrability of
{\bf R}_{\alpha}

We proceed as in Appendix
B
dominating

|{\bf R}_{\alpha}|\leq\xi_{\alpha}^{\prime}/\eta_{\alpha}
for
\alpha\in(0,1)
. The integrability of
{\bf R}_{\alpha}
,
\alpha\in(1,2)
, follows easily from
{\mathbb{E}}[|{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{\alpha}|]<\infty
. We introduce the hybrid Laplace transform

\displaystyle\Psi_{\xi_{\alpha}^{\prime},\eta_{\alpha}}(u,x):={\mathbb{E}}\big[{\rm e}\,^{-u\,\xi_{\alpha}^{\prime}}{\mathbf{1}}(\eta_{\alpha}\leq x)\big]\,,\qquad u,x>0.

From the expression of the hybrid characteristic function of Theorem
3.1
we have

\displaystyle\Psi_{\xi_{\alpha}^{\prime},\eta_{\alpha}}(u,x)=\exp\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}{\mathbb{E}}\big[{\rm e}\,^{-yu\|{\bf Q}\|_{1}}{\mathbf{1}}(y\|{\bf Q}\|_{\infty}\leq x)-1\big]d(-y^{-\alpha})\Big)\,,\qquad u,x>0\,.

Then by monotone convergence we obtain

\displaystyle\lim_{u\to 0^{+}}-\dfrac{\partial\Psi_{\xi_{\alpha}^{\prime},\eta_{\alpha}}(u,x)}{\partial u}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{u\to 0^{+}}\int_{0}^{\infty}{\mathbb{E}}\big[y\|{\bf Q}\|_{1}{\rm e}\,^{-yu\|{\bf Q}\|_{1}}{\mathbf{1}}(y\|{\bf Q}\|_{\infty}\leq x)]d(-y^{-\alpha})\Psi_{\xi_{\alpha}^{\prime},\eta_{\alpha}}(u,x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\alpha{\mathbb{E}}\Big[\|{\bf Q}\|_{1}\int_{0}^{x/\|{\bf Q}\|_{\infty}}y^{-\alpha}dy\Big]\Psi_{\xi_{\alpha}^{\prime},\eta_{\alpha}}(0,x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{\alpha}{1-\alpha}{\mathbb{E}}\Big[\dfrac{\|{\bf Q}\|_{1}}{\|{\bf Q}\|_{\infty}^{1-\alpha}}\Big]x^{1-\alpha}\exp(-\theta_{|{\bf X}|}x^{-\alpha})\,.

The limit is finite since we show the integrability of
\sum_{t\in{\mathbb{Z}}}|\widetilde{\bf Q}_{t}|
in Remark
4.2
and

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\Big[\dfrac{\|{\bf Q}\|_{1}}{\|{\bf Q}\|_{\infty}^{1-\alpha}}\Big]={\mathbb{E}}\Big[\|{\bf Q}\|_{\infty}^{\alpha}\dfrac{\|{\bf Q}\|_{1}}{\|{\bf Q}\|_{\infty}}\Big]=\theta_{|{\bf X}|}{\mathbb{E}}[\|\widetilde{\bf Q}\|_{1}]<\infty\,.

Then we proved the finiteness of

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}[\xi_{\alpha}^{\prime}{\mathbf{1}}(\eta_{\alpha}\leq x)]=\lim_{u\to 0^{+}}-\dfrac{\partial\Psi_{\xi_{\alpha}^{\prime},\eta_{\alpha}}(u,x)}{\partial u}=\dfrac{\alpha}{1-\alpha}\theta_{|{\bf X}|}{\mathbb{E}}[\|\widetilde{\bf Q}\|_{1}]x^{1-\alpha}\exp(-\theta_{|{\bf X}|}x^{-\alpha})\,,\qquad x>0\,.

Applying Fubini’s theorem we achieve

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\Big[\dfrac{\xi_{\alpha}^{\prime}}{\eta_{\alpha}}\Big]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\Big[\xi_{\alpha}^{\prime}\int_{0}^{\infty}{\mathbf{1}}(\eta_{\alpha}\leq x)\dfrac{dx}{x^{2}}\Big]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}{\mathbb{E}}[\xi_{\alpha}^{\prime}{\mathbf{1}}(\eta_{\alpha}\leq x)]\dfrac{dx}{x^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{{\mathbb{E}}[\|\widetilde{\bf Q}\|_{1}]}{1-\alpha}\int_{0}^{\infty}\alpha\theta_{|{\bf X}|}x^{1-\alpha}\exp(-\theta_{|{\bf X}|}x^{-\alpha})\dfrac{dx}{x^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{{\mathbb{E}}[\|\widetilde{\bf Q}\|_{1}]}{1-\alpha}\,.

The left-hand side term is finite which proves that
{\bf R}_{\alpha}
is integrable for
0<\alpha<1
.

Spectral conditions for graphs in which every edge belongs to a factor
Jin Cai, Bo Zhou

Abstract
A factor of a graph is a spanning subgraph. Spectral sufficient conditions are provided via spectral radius and signless Laplacian spectral radius for graphs with (i) a matching of given size (particularly, 1 1 -factor) containing any given edge, and (ii) a star factor with a component isomorphic to stars of order two or three containing any given edge, respectively. Mathematics Subject Classifications: 05C50 Keywords: 1 1 -factor, star factor, spectral radius, signless Laplacian spectral radius

The following lemma, following from the Perron-Frobenius theorem, is well known.

Lemma 2.1

Let

The matrix

.

3.1
Spectral radius

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
n,s,k
be positive integers with
n\geq\max\{5k+6,2s+k\}
,
s\geq 2
and
0\leq k\leq n
. For fixed
n
and
k

\rho(K_{s}\vee((s+k-1)K_{1}\cup K_{n-2s-k+1}))
is uniquely maximized when

Proof.

Let
H_{s}=K_{s}\vee((s+k-1)K_{1}\cup K_{n-2s-k+1})
.
Partition
V(H_{s})
into
S\cup V_{1}\cup V_{2}
, where
V_{1}=V((s+k-1)K_{1})
and
V_{2}=V(K_{n-2s-k+1})
.
It is easy to see that this partition is an equitable partition. The corresponding quotient matrix is

\mathbf{B}=\begin{pmatrix}s-1&s+k-1&n-2s-k+1\\
s&0&0\\
s&0&n-2s-k\end{pmatrix}.

By a simple calculation, the characteristic polynomial of
\mathbf{B}
is

f_{s}(x)=x^{3}-(n-k-s-1)x^{2}-(n+s^{2}+(k-2)s-k)x-2s^{3}+(n-3k+2)s^{2}+((k-1)n-k^{2}+k)s.

By Lemma
2.2

\rho(H_{s})
is equal to the largest root of the equation

.
In particular, for
H_{2}=K_{2}\vee((k+1)K_{1}\cup K_{n-k-3})

\rho(H_{2})
is equal to the largest root of
f_{2}(x)=0
.

Suppose that
s>2
. We only need to show that
\rho(H_{s})<\rho(H_{2})
.
Note that

\frac{f_{s}(x)-f_{2}(x)}{s-2}=h(x):=x^{2}-(s+k)x-2s^{2}+(n-3k-2)s+(k+1)n-k^{2}-5k-4.

As a quadratic function of

is strictly increasing for

. As
n\geq frac{5}{3}k+\frac{8}{3}
and

, one gets
x_{0}\leq n-k-2
.
Then, for

is strictly increasing, so

\displaystyle h(x)

\displaystyle\geq h(n-k-2)

\displaystyle=-2s^{2}-2ks+n^{2}-(2k+3)n+k^{2}+k

\displaystyle\geq-\frac{(n-k)^{2}}{2}-k(n-k)+n^{2}-(2k+3)n+k^{2}+k

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}n^{2}-(2k+3)n+\frac{3}{2}k^{2}+k

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}(5k+6)^{2}-(2k+3)(5k+6)+\frac{3}{2}k^{2}+k

\displaystyle=4k^{2}+4k

\displaystyle\geq 0,

where the second inequality follows because as a quadratic function of
s
,
h(n-k-2)
is strictly decreasing for
2\leq s\leq\frac{n-k}{2}
, and the third inequality follows because
as a quadratic function of
n
,
\frac{1}{2}n^{2}-(2k+3)n-\frac{1}{2}k^{2}+k
is strictly increasing for

n\geq 5k+6>2k+3
. Thus,
h(x)>0
for
x\in[n-k-2,+\infty)
, as
h(x)=0
implies
k=0
, but then
h(x)\geq h(n-2)>0
, a contradiction.
That is,
f_{s}(x)>f_{2}(x)
for
x\in[n-k-2,+\infty)
. By Lemma
2.1
, we have

\rho(H_{2})>\rho(K_{n-k-1})=n-k-2
,
so
f_{s}(x)>f_{2}(x)\geq 0
for
x\in[\rho(H_{2}),+\infty)
.
implying that
\rho(H_{s})<\rho(H_{2})
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Suppose by contradiction that
G
does not have a matching of size
\frac{n-k}{2}
containing any given edge. By Lemma

, there exists a vertex subset
S\subset V(G)
with
|S|=s
such that

or

, one gets

.
Let
t=o(G-S)
,
n_{1}\leq\dots\leq n_{t-1}
be the orders
the odd components of
G-S
with the first
t-1

n_{t}=n-s-n_{1}-\dots-n_{t-1}
.
Then
G
is a spanning subgraph of the graph
G^{\prime}:=G[S]\vee(K_{n_{1}}\cup\dots\cup K_{n_{t}})
.
By Lemma
2.1
,

\rho(G)\leq\rho(G^{\prime})

with equality when
S\neq\emptyset
if and only if
G\cong G^{\prime}
.
Let
H_{s}=K_{s}\vee((s+k-1)K_{1}\cup K_{n-2s-k+1})
.

Case 1.
G[S]
is not empty.

In this case,
s\geq 2
and
n\geq s+o(G-S)\geq 2s+k
. So
2\leq s\leq\frac{n-k}{2}
. By Lemma
2.1

\rho(G)\leq\rho(G^{*})

with equality if and only if
G\cong G^{*}
, where

.
By Lemmas
2.3
and
2.4
, we have

\rho(G^{*})\leq\rho(H_{s})

with equality if and only if

. Thus

\rho(G)\leq\rho(H_{s})

with equality if and only if
G\cong H_{s}
.

. Thus

\rho(H_{2})\leq\rho(G)\leq\rho(H_{s})

By Lemma
3.2
,
\rho(G)=\rho(H_{2})
, so

, which is a contradiction.

Case 2.
G[S]
is empty.

Suppose first that
s=0
. Then
t\geq k+2

and

3.2
Signless Laplacian spectral radius

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
n,s,k
be positive integers with
n\geq\max\{5k+7,2s+k\}
,
s\geq 2
and
0\leq k\leq n
. For fixed
n
and
k
,

q(K_{s}\vee((s+k-1)K_{1}\cup K_{n-2s-k+1}))
is uniquely maximized when
s=2
.

Proof.

Let
H_{s}=K_{s}\vee((s+k-1)K_{1}\cup K_{n-2s-k+1})
.
Partition
V(H_{s})
into
S\cup V_{1}\cup V_{2}
, where
V_{1}=V((s+k-1)K_{1})
and
V_{2}=V(K_{n-2s-k+1})
.
It is easy to see that this partition is an equitable partition. The corresponding quotient matrix is

\mathbf{B}=\begin{pmatrix}n+s-2&s+k-1&n-2s-k+1\\
s&s&0\\
s&0&2n-3s-2k\end{pmatrix}.

By a simple calculation, the characteristic polynomial of
\mathbf{B}
is

\displaystyle f_{s}(x)

\displaystyle=x^{3}-(3n-2k-s-2)x^{2}-(4s^{2}-(n-4k+4)s-2n^{2}+2(k+2)n-4k)x

\displaystyle\quad-2s^{3}+(4n-4k-2)s^{2}-(2n^{2}-2(2k+1)n+2k^{2}+2k)s.

By Lemma
2.2
,
q(H_{s})
is equal to the largest root of the equation
f_{s}(x)=0
.
Similarly, let
H_{2}=K_{2}\vee((k+1)K_{1}\cup K_{n-k-3})
.

q(H_{2})
is equal to the largest root of
f_{2}(x)=0
.

Suppose that
s>2
.
It is easy to see that

\displaystyle\frac{f_{s}(x)-f_{2}(x)}{s-2}

\displaystyle=h(x):=x^{2}+(n-4s-4k-4)x-2s^{2}+(4n-4k-6)s-2n^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+(4k+10)n-2k^{2}-10k-12.

Evidently,
h(x)
is strictly increasing for
x\geq x_{0}:=\frac{4s-n+4k+4}{2}
. As
n\geq\max\{2k+4,2s+k\}
, one gets

x_{0}\leq 2n-2k-4
.
It then follows that
h(x)
is strictly increasing for
x\in[2n-2k-4,+\infty)
.
Thus, for
x\in[2n-2k-4,+\infty)
, one gets

\displaystyle h(x)

\displaystyle\geq h(2n-2k-4)

\displaystyle=-2s^{2}-(4n-4k-10)s+4n^{2}-(14k+18)n+10k^{2}+30k+20

\displaystyle\geq-\frac{(n-k)^{2}}{2}-\frac{(4n-4k-10)(n-k)}{2}+4n^{2}-(14k+18)n+10k^{2}+30k+20

\displaystyle=\frac{3}{2}n^{2}-(9k+13)n+\frac{15}{2}k^{2}+25k+20

\displaystyle\geq\frac{3}{2}(5k+7)^{2}-(9k+13)(5k+7)+\frac{15}{2}k^{2}+25k+20

\displaystyle=2k+\frac{5}{2}

\displaystyle>0,

where the second inequality follows because
h(2n-2k-4)
is strictly decreasing for

q(G)\geq q(G_{2})
. Thus
q(G)=q(G_{2})
, implying that
G\cong G_{2}
,
a contradiction.

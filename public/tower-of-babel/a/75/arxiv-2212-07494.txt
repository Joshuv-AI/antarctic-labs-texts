Second-order force scheme for lattice Boltzmann method
1 Xuhui Li, 1 Wenyang Duan, 2 Xiaowen Shan

Abstract
We present an a priori derivation of the force scheme for lattice Boltzmann method based on kinetic theoretical formulation. We show that the discrete lattice effect, previously eliminated a posteriori in BGK collision model, is due to first-order space-time discretization and can be eliminated generically for a wide range of collision models with second-order space-time discretization. Particularly, the force scheme for the recently developed spectral multiple-relaxation-time (SMRT) collision model is obtained and numerically verified.



I
Introduction

Since the early development of lattice Boltzmann method (LBM), implementation of
the body force term has been generating continued interests
Bawazeer et al. 2021
.
This is especially true in recent years as more complex collision models are
adopted in place of the Bhatnagar-Gross-Krook (BGK)
Bhatnagar et al. 1954
model,
and LBM applied to multiphase flows where the interaction is modeled by a Vlasov
body force
Shan and Chen 1993
;
Shan and Chen 1994
. Both trends call for more accurate
treatment of the body force term. Literally a dozen force schemes have been
proposed and extensive numerical evaluations
conducted
Huang et al. 2011
;
Sun et al. 2012
. Nevertheless, conclusion has not been
reached on how the force term should be implemented independent of the details
of the collision model and underlying lattice.

One of the earliest force schemes is the intuitive velocity-shift method used in
the modeling of inter-molecular interactions
Shan and Chen 1993
. This method shifts
the velocity in the equilibrium distribution by
\bm{g}\Delta t
, where
\bm{g}
is
the acceleration and
\Delta t
the time step. A more refined
scheme
Guo et al. 2002
eliminated the discrete lattice effect by introducing
unknown coefficients in the force term and determining them by matching the
recovered macroscopic equation with Navier-Stokes equations. Benchmarks in the
context of non-ideal gas showed that with the velocity-shift scheme, the
equilibrium densities has an un-physical dependence on the relaxation time
whereas with the latter scheme this abnormality is completely
eliminated
Yu and Fan 2009
. Li
et al.

Li et al. 2012
analyzed the exact
difference method (EDM)
Kupershtokh 2004
, and found that its great
stability in Shan-Chen (SC) model
Shan and Chen 1993
attributes to the extra error
introduced to the pressure tensor. Furthermore, Li
et
al.

Li et al. 2012
proposed an improved force scheme to deal with the
high-density ratio in the SC model. Several force schemes for the central-moment
multiple-relaxation-time collision models with standard lattice have also been
developed
Lycett-Brown and Luo 2016
;
De Rosis 2017
;
Fei and Luo 2017
.

It is worth noting that in terms of the hydrodynamic moments, the leading effect
of all the models are unanimously the same, namely, when considered as an
addition to the normal collision term, the zeroth moment of the body force term
vanishes to ensure mass conservation and the first moment equals to
\bm{g}\Delta t
. The subtle differences are only in the second and higher moments
representing the additional momentum and energy fluxes caused by the body force.
This effect manifests in cases such as multiphase flow modeling where the
additional stress plays a significant role
Yu and Fan 2009
.

The complexity and controversy are partially due to the fact that the
development of the force schemes mostly followed the same
a
posteriori
approach of the LB development. The LB equation is constructed as a
kinetic model fully discretized in both the velocity and configuration spaces
with a discretized time. The errors caused by all discretizations are then
optimized to achieve the correct macroscopic Navier-Stokes equation. Besides
being disconnected from the Boltzmann equation which includes the effect of the
body force, the multiple expansion approach quickly becomes unmanageable for
more complex collision models.

In the kinetic theoretical formulation of LB
Shan and He 1998
;
Shan et al. 2006
, the LB
equation is obtained by first discretizing the continuum Boltzmann-BGK equation
in the velocity space, resulting in a set of partial differential equations in
the normal space and time for a set of discrete-velocity distribution function.
Without any ambiguity, the body force is given by the finite Hermite expansion
of the body force term
Martys et al. 1998
;
Shan et al. 2006
. However, to further
discretize the space and time with a finite difference scheme, multiple schemes
exist.

In the present work we first show that the scheme of Guo
et
al

Guo et al. 2002
can be obtained
a priori
by further discretizing the
discrete-velocity Boltzmann equation with the second-order finite difference in
space and time. The same finite difference scheme can be applied to the
recently suggested SMRT collision model
Shan 2019
;
Li et al. 2019
;
Shan et al. 2021
.

The LBE is obtained by further discretizing Eq. (
19
) in space and time
using first-order forward-Euler finite difference scheme. It was shown by
multiple expansions that after absorbing the leading order error into the
dissipation term, the scheme is effectively second-order with a viscosity
proportional to
\tau+1/2
instead of
\tau

Chen and Doolen 1998
. In presence of a
body force, the discrete lattice effect can be eliminated
Guo et al. 2002

a posteriori
. We show in this section that the same correction can be
obtained
a priori
for the BGK model by integrating Eq. (
19
)
using second-order quadrature rules
He et al. 1998
. In the next section, the same
technique is used to obtain the force scheme for the Hermite-space MRT model.

With the BGK collision model, the description of the collision as a

uniform
relaxation process of the distribution function towards its
equilibrium is in many cases simplistic. In a previous series of
papers
Li and Shan 2021
;
Shan et al. 2021
;
Shi and Shan 2021
, the SMRT collision model was developed
where the
irreducible components
of the Hermite coefficients are relaxed
separately in the reference frame moving with the fluid. These components are
the minimum tensor components that can be separately relaxed without violating
rotation symmetry.

is presented in Section
II
with the discrete-velocity force

II.1
, the derivation of the force scheme
for BGK model in Section
II.2
, and force scheme for the SMRT
collision model in Section
II.3
. Some numerical verification is given
in Section
III
and the conclusion and discussions are given in
Sec.
IV
.

II
Boltzmann-BGK equation

II.1
Background

In kinetic theory
Chapman and Cowling 1970
, the evolution of the single-particle
distribution,
f(\bm{x},\bm{\xi},t)
, under an external or
self-generated body force with acceleration
\bm{g}
is described by the Boltzmann
equation:

\frac{\partial f}{\partial t}+\bm{\xi}\cdot\nabla f+\bm{g}\cdot\nabla_{\bm{\xi}}f=\Omega(f),

(1)

where,
\bm{x}
and
\bm{\xi}
are coordinates in physical and velocity spaces
respectively,
t
the time,
\Omega(f)
the collision term describing the effect
of inter-particle collision. Due to its extreme complexity, the collision term
is often simplified by models, of which the most widely used is the BGK model:

\Omega_{BGK}(f)=-\frac{1}{\tau}\left[f-f^{(eq)}\right],

(2)

where
\tau
is a relaxation time and
f^{(eq)}
the Maxwell-Boltzmann
distribution. Choosing the characteristic speed
\sqrt{k_{B}T_{0}/m_{0}}
with
k_{B}

the Boltzmann constant and
T_{0}
and
m_{0}
the reference temperature and
molecule mass,
f^{(eq)}
has the dimensionless form

f^{(eq)}(\bm{x},\bm{\xi},t)=\frac{\rho}{(2\pi\theta)^{D/2}}\exp\left[-\frac{(\bm{\xi}-\bm{u})^{2}}{2\theta}\right],

(3)

where
\rho
is the density,
\bm{u}
the fluid velocity and
\theta\equiv T/T_{0}

the temperature, all dimensionless.

The lattice Boltzmann equation was formulated as a special velocity-space
discretization of the Boltzmann equation based on two
observations
Shan et al. 2006
. First, in Chapman-Enskog
calculation
Huang 1987
, the macroscopic hydrodynamics only depends on the
leading moments of the distribution function rather than its entirety. The
distribution function can therefore be approximated by its low-order Hermite
expansion without altering the hydrodynamics
Grad 1949a
;
Grad 1949b
. This
truncation is equivalent to projecting Eq. (
1
) into a
low-order Hilbert space spanned by the Hermite polynomials. Denoting the
N
-th
order Hermite series by:

f_{N}(\bm{x},\bm{\xi},t)=\omega(\bm{\xi})\sum_{n=0}^{N}\frac{1}{n!}\bm{a}^{(n)}:{\cal H}^{(n)}(\bm{\xi}),

(4)

where
{\cal H}^{(n)}(\bm{\xi})
is the
n
-th Hermite polynomial, and

\omega(\bm{\xi})\equiv(2\pi)^{-D/2}\exp\left(-\xi^{2}/2\right),

(5)

is the weight function with respect to which the Hermite polynomials are
orthogonal. The expansion coefficients:

\bm{a}^{(n)}(\bm{x},t)=\int f_{N}(\bm{x},\bm{\xi},t){\cal H}^{(n)}(\bm{\xi})d\bm{\xi},

(6)

are
velocity moments
of the distribution function with
the leading few being the familiar hydrodynamic variables,
\rho
,
\bm{u}
and

\theta
.

Second, any finite Hermite series is completely determined by its values on a
finite set of
\bm{\xi}_{i}
. Let
\{(w_{i},\bm{\xi}_{i}),i=1,\cdots,d\}
be the weights
and abscissas of a
Q
-th degree Hermite quadrature such that for any
Q
-th
degree polynomial,
p(\bm{\xi})
, we have:

\int\omega(\bm{\xi})p(\bm{\xi})d\bm{\xi}=\sum_{i=0}^{d}w_{i}p(\bm{\xi}_{i}).

(7)

The
M
-th moment of
f_{N}
is then

\displaystyle\int f_{N}(\bm{\xi})\bm{\xi}^{M}d\bm{\xi}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\omega(\bm{\xi})\left[\frac{f_{N}(\bm{\xi})\bm{\xi}^{M}}{\omega(\bm{\xi})}\right]d\bm{\xi}

(8)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{d}\frac{w_{i}f_{N}(\bm{\xi}_{i})\bm{\xi}_{i}^{M}}{\omega(\bm{\xi}_{i})},

provided that
Q\geq M+N
, as the integrand in the brackets is a polynomial of
degree
M+N
. Hence, all expansion coefficients are completely determined by

f_{N}(\bm{\xi}_{i})
as long as
\{\bm{\xi}_{i}\}
forms the abscissas of a quadrature rule
of a degree
Q\geq 2N
. If we further define the convenience variable:

f_{i}\equiv\frac{w_{i}f_{N}(\bm{x},\bm{\xi}_{i},t)}{\omega(\bm{\xi}_{i})},

(9)

the integral velocity moments has the discrete form:

\int f_{N}(\bm{\xi})\bm{\xi}^{M}d\bm{\xi}=\sum_{i=0}^{d}f_{i}\bm{\xi}_{i}^{M},

(10)

provided that the quadrature conditions are met. Noting by Eq. (
6
)
that the expansion coefficients are also velocity moments,
\bm{a}^{(n)}
and
f_{i}

can be transformed through the following general discrete Fourier transform:

\displaystyle\bm{a}^{(n)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{d}f_{i}{\cal H}^{(n)}(\bm{\xi}_{i}),

(11a)

\displaystyle f_{i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle w_{i}\sum_{n=0}^{N}\frac{1}{n!}\bm{a}^{(n)}:{\cal H}^{(n)}(\bm{\xi}_{i}).

(11b)

The dynamic equations of
f_{i}
are taken as the direct evaluation of the

projected
Eq. (
1
) at
\bm{\xi}_{i}
. This amounts to
expanding all terms in terms of Hermite polynomials and truncating to a finite
order. The expansion of
\nabla_{\bm{\xi}}f_{N}
can be obtained by taking the
velocity-space derivative of Eq. (
4
) and using the
following Rodrigues formula:

{\cal H}^{(n)}(\bm{\xi})=\frac{(-1)^{n}}{\omega(\bm{\xi})}\nabla^{n}_{\bm{\xi}}\omega(\bm{\xi}),

(12)

to write:

\displaystyle\nabla_{\bm{\xi}}f_{N}=\sum_{n=0}^{N}\frac{1}{n!}\bm{a}^{(n)}:\nabla_{\bm{\xi}}\left[\omega(\bm{\xi}){\cal H}^{(n)}(\bm{\xi})\right]

(13)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\omega(\bm{\xi})\sum_{n=0}^{N}\frac{1}{n!}\bm{a}^{(n)}:{\cal H}^{(n+1)}(\bm{\xi})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\omega(\bm{\xi})\sum_{n=1}^{N+1}\frac{1}{n!}\left[n\bm{a}^{(n-1)}\right]:{\cal H}^{(n)}(\bm{\xi}).

The body-force term, denoted by
F(\bm{\xi})\equiv-\bm{g}\cdot\nabla_{\bm{\xi}}f_{N}
, has
thus the following expansion:

F(\bm{\xi})=\omega(\bm{\xi})\sum_{n=1}^{N}\frac{1}{n!}\left[n\bm{g}\bm{a}^{(n-1)}\right]:{\cal H}^{(n)}(\bm{\xi}),

(14)

Denoting the Hermite coefficients of
F(\bm{\xi})
by
\bm{a}^{(n)}_{F}
, we
have

\bm{a}^{(0)}_{F}=0,\quad\mbox{and}\quad\bm{a}^{(n)}_{F}=n\bm{g}\bm{a}^{(n-1)},\quad n\geq 1.

(15)

where
\bm{g}\bm{a}^{(n-1)}
is to be understood as the
symmetric product

between
\bm{g}
and
\bm{a}^{(n-1)}
,
e.g.
, in component form,
\bm{g}\bm{a}^{(2)}=(g_{\alpha}a_{\beta\gamma}+g_{\beta}a_{\gamma\alpha}+g_{\gamma}a_{\alpha\beta})/3
.

The explicit expressions for the first several orders of
\bm{a}^{(n)}_{0}
can be found
in the literature
Shan et al. 2006
. Thus we have the expanded force term up to
the fourth-order:

\displaystyle F\thickapprox\omega(\bm{\xi})\rho\{\underbrace{\bm{g}\cdot\bm{\xi}}_{1st}+\underbrace{(\bm{g}\cdot\bm{\xi})(\bm{\xi}\cdot\bm{u})-\bm{g}\cdot\bm{u}}_{2nd}

\displaystyle+\underbrace{\frac{1}{6\rho}3\bm{g}\left[\rho(\bm{u}^{2}+(\theta-1)\bm{\delta})+\bm{a}^{(2)}_{1}\right]:{\cal H}^{(3)}(\bm{\xi})}_{3rd}

\displaystyle+\underbrace{\frac{1}{24\rho}4\bm{g}(\bm{a}^{(3)}_{0}+\bm{a}^{(3)}_{1}):{\cal H}^{(4)}(\bm{\xi})}_{4th}\}\qquad

(16)

If the expansion of the force term is truncated to second order, the familiar
force term can be obtained:

F(\bm{\xi})=\rho\omega(\bm{\xi})\left[\bm{g}\cdot(\bm{\xi}-\bm{u})+(\bm{g}\cdot\bm{\xi})(\bm{u}\cdot\bm{\xi})\right].

(17)

Similar to Eq. (
9
), defining:

F_{i}\equiv\frac{w_{i}F(\bm{\xi}_{i})}{\omega(\bm{\xi}_{i})},

(18)

the discrete-velocity Boltzmann equation with body force is:

\frac{\partial f_{i}}{\partial t}+\bm{\xi}_{i}\cdot\nabla f_{i}=\Omega_{i}+F_{i},\quad i=1,\cdots,d.

(19)

The above equation was previously given by Martys
et
al

Martys et al. 1998
and its relations to the previous models were also
analyzed. Naturally the body force term is independent of the collision term,
and other than the truncation order, there is no ambiguity.

II.2
force in BGK collision model

The LBE is obtained by further discretizing Eq. (
19
) in space and time
using first-order forward-Euler finite difference scheme. It was shown by
multiple expansions that after absorbing the leading order error into the
dissipation term, the scheme is effectively second-order with a viscosity
proportional to
\tau+1/2
instead of
\tau

Chen and Doolen 1998
. In presence of a
body force, the discrete lattice effect can be eliminated
Guo et al. 2002

a posteriori
. We show in this section that the same correction can be
obtained
a priori
for the BGK model by integrating Eq. (
19
)
using second-order quadrature rules
He et al. 1998
;
Malaspinas 2009
;
Li et al. 2022
. In the
next section, the same technique is used to obtain the force scheme for the
Hermite-space MRT model.

The discrete-velocity BGK model can be written as:

\Omega_{i}=-\frac{1}{\tau}\left[f_{i}-f_{i}^{(eq)}\right],

(20)

where:

f^{(eq)}_{i}=\rho w_{i}\left\{1+\bm{u}\cdot\bm{\xi}+\frac{1}{2}\left[(\bm{u}\cdot\bm{\xi})^{2}-u^{2}\right]\cdots\right\}

(21)

is the truncated Hermite expansion of the Maxwellian evaluated at

\bm{\xi}_{i}

Shan et al. 2006
.
Integrating Eq. (
19
) along the characteristic line and deal with the integral on the right-hand side by the trapezoidal rule,
we have

\displaystyle f_{i}(\bm{x}+\bm{\xi},t+1)-

\displaystyle f_{i}(\bm{x},t)=\frac{1}{2}[\Omega_{i}(\bm{x}+\bm{\xi},t+1)+\Omega_{i}(\bm{x},t)]

(22)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}[F_{i}(\bm{x}+\bm{\xi},t+1)+F_{i}(\bm{x},t)]

in which the time step
\Delta t=1
is applied for brevity.

Define a new distribution function

\bar{f}_{i}=f_{i}-\frac{1}{2}\Omega_{i}-\frac{1}{2}F_{i}

(23)

Apply the new defined distribution function, the implicit evolution equation Eq.(
22
) can be reconstructed as an explicit evolution equation

\bar{f}_{i}(\bm{x}+\bm{\xi},t+1)=\bar{f}_{i}(\bm{x},t)+\Omega_{i}(\bm{x},t)+F_{i}(\bm{x},t)

(24)

Substitute the BGK collision term, Eq. (
20
), into the above
equation and replace
f
by
\bar{f},f^{(eq)}
and
F_{i}
with
Eq. (
23
), the above evolution equation can be written as the
following complete explicit form

\displaystyle\bar{f}_{i}(\bm{x}+\bm{\xi}_{i},t+1)=

\displaystyle\bar{f}_{i}(\bm{x},t)-\frac{1}{\hat{\tau}}\left[\bar{f}_{i}(\bm{x},t)-f_{i}^{(eq)}(\bm{x},t)\right]

(25)

\displaystyle+(1-\frac{1}{2\hat{\tau}})F_{i}(\bm{x},t)

with
\hat{\tau}=\tau+\frac{1}{2}
. It should be noted that the equilibrium
and force term in the above equation are the original forms and
\bar{f}_{i}
is
the actual distribution function in the numerical implementation.

The zero-order, first-order and second-order moments of the new defined
distribution function can be evaluated according to the original
one
Li et al. 2022
. The zero-order moment is as followings

\sum\bar{f}_{i}=\sum(f_{i}+\frac{1}{2\tau}f_{i}^{(1)}-\frac{1}{2}F_{i})=\sum f_{i}

(26)

in which the zero-order moments of the nonequilibrium and force term are null.
Thus we have

\bar{\rho}=\rho

(27)

The first-order moment of the new distribution function is as followings

\displaystyle\sum\bar{f}_{i}\xi_{i,\alpha}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum{(f_{i}+\frac{1}{2\tau}f_{i}^{(1)}-\frac{1}{2}F_{i})\xi_{i,\alpha}}

(28)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum{(f_{i}-\frac{1}{2}F_{i})\xi_{i,\alpha}}

in which the first-order moment of the nonequilibrium is null.
Thus we have

\rho\bar{\bm{u}}=\rho\bm{u}-\frac{\bm{F}}{2}

(29)

Then the physical velocity can be written as

\bm{u}=\bar{\bm{u}}+\frac{\bm{F}}{2\rho}

(30)

The second-order central-moment of the new defined distribution function is as followings

\displaystyle\sum\bar{f}_{i}c_{i,\alpha}c_{i,\beta}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum{(f_{i}+\frac{1}{2\tau}f_{i}^{(1)}-\frac{1}{2}F_{i})c_{i,\alpha}}c_{i,\beta}

(31)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum{(f_{i}+\frac{1}{2\tau}f_{i}^{(1)})c_{i,\alpha}}c_{i,\beta}

The trace of the second-order tensors in the above equation can be obtained by
contraction of the subscript index

D\rho\bar{\theta}=D\rho\theta

(32)

in which the trace of the shear stress is null. This indicates that the physical
temperature is equal to the contraction of the second-order central-moment of
the new distribution function

\theta=\bar{\theta}

(33)

It is worth noting that the macroscopic variables used in the equilibrium and
force term in Eq. (
25
) are the physical variables

\rho,\bm{u},\theta
, instead of
\bar{\rho},\bar{\bm{u}},\bar{\theta}
. In general,
the force scheme in the evolution equation of the distribution function is
derived via a rigorous and priori approach here. If thermohydrodynamic level is
not considered and the force term is only expanded to second-order, it is
reduced to the force scheme derived by Guo
et al.

Guo et al. 2002
.

II.3
force in SMRT collision model

The force scheme in the raw-moment Hermite MRT collision model is organized in

;

;

Note that this is precisely the Hermite polynomials used by

\bm{d}^{(n)}_{0}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\rho\theta^{-D/2},&n=0\\
0,&n>0\end{array}\right..

condition which is a non-trival issue for the high-order lattie LB model.

Womersley flow

IV
Conclusions and discussions

In this work, a generic
a priori
derivation of the force scheme for
lattice Boltzmann method based on kinetic theoretical formulation is proposed
for the SMRT collision model. A second-order force scheme for the SMRT collision
model is obtained and numerically verified at isothermal level. This generic
approach of incorporating the force scheme actually can be applied to a wide
range of collision models. At the isothermal level, we found that the force term
is only not null at the first-order in the RCM space if the high-order
non-equilibrium terms are ignored. The new force scheme can account for body
force effects on high-order hydrodynamic moments which may be significant in
flows with high density and temperature gradients, which will be reported in the
following work.

Appendix: MRT force scheme in Raw-Moment Space

In the raw-moment MRT model,the Galilean invariance and rotational invariance are not considered. For the convenient reading of this manuscript,
the derivation of the force scheme of the raw-moment MRT model is stated here.
Similarly, starting from Eq. (
19

\displaystyle\bm{F},

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\bm{F}\bm{u}

(59c)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 4\bm{F}\left[\bm{u}\bm{u}\bm{u}+3(\theta-1)\bm{u}\bm{\delta}\right]

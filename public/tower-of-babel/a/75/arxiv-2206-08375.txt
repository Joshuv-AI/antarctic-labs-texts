A counterexample to a conjecture of M. Ismail
K. Castillo, D. Mbouna

Abstract
. In an earlier work [K. Castillo et al., J. Math. Anal. Appl. 514 (2022) 126358], we give positive answer to the first, and apparently more easy, part of a conjecture of M. Ismail concerning the characterization of the continuous q q -Jacobi polynomials, Al-Salam-Chihara polynomials or special or limiting cases of them. In this note we present an example that disproves the second part of such a conjecture, and so this issue is definitively closed.



2010 Mathematics Subject Classification
33D45

1.
Introduction

The Askey-Wilson divided difference operator is defined by

(1)

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{q}\,f(x)=\frac{\breve{f}\big(q^{1/2}e^{i\theta}\big)-\breve{f}\big(q^{-1/2}e^{i\theta}\big)}{\breve{e}\big(q^{1/2}e^{i\theta}\big)-\breve{e}\big(q^{-1/2}e^{i\theta}\big)},

where, for each polynomial
f
,
\breve{f}(e^{i\theta})=f(\cos\theta)
and
e(x)=x
(see
[
3
, Section 12.1]
). In
[
1
]
, we give positive answer to the first part of the following Ismail’s conjecture (see
[
3
, Conjecture 24.7.8]
):

Conjecture
.

Let
(p_{n})_{n\geq 0}
be a sequence of orthogonal polynomials and let
\pi
be a polynomial which does not depend on
n
. If

\displaystyle\pi(x)\mathcal{D}_{q}\,p_{n}(x)=\sum_{k=-1}^{1}c_{n,k}p_{n+k}(x),

then
(p_{n})_{n\geq 0}
are continuous
q
-Jacobi polynomials or Al-Salam-Chihara polynomials, or special or limiting cases of them. The same conclusion follows if

(2)

\displaystyle\pi(x)\mathcal{D}_{q}\,p_{n}(x)=\sum_{k=-r}^{s}c_{n,k}p_{n+k}(x),

for positive integers
r
and
s
.

The second part of this conjecture is certainly a much more complex problem than the first one. However, after much manipulation of similar structural relations in a number of recent works, we found the second part of the conjecture less and less convincing. Now in the next section we propose a counterexample.

2.
Counterexample

Throughout this section we assume that
0<q<1
. Set
x(s)=(q^{s}+q^{-s})/2
. Taking
e^{i\theta}=q^{s}
in (
1
),
\mathcal{D}_{q}
reads

\mathcal{D}_{q}f(x(s))=\frac{f\big(x(s+\frac{1}{2})\big)-f\big(x(s-\frac{1}{2})\big)}{x(s+\frac{1}{2})-x(s-\frac{1}{2})}.

Define

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{q}f(x(s))=\frac{f\big(x(s+\frac{1}{2})\big)+f\big(x(s-\frac{1}{2})\big)}{2},

and

\displaystyle\alpha_{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{q^{n/2}+q^{-n/2}}{2},\quad\gamma_{n}=\frac{q^{n/2}-q^{-n/2}}{q^{1/2}-q^{-1/2}}\quad(n=0,1,\dots),

and
\alpha=\alpha_{1}
.
Recall that

(3)

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{q}\big(fg\big)

\displaystyle=\big(\mathcal{D}_{q}f\big)\big(\mathcal{S}_{q}g\big)+\big(\mathcal{S}_{q}f\big)\big(\mathcal{D}_{q}g\big),

(4)

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{q}\big(fg\big)

\displaystyle=\big(\mathcal{D}_{q}f\big)\big(\mathcal{D}_{q}g\big)\texttt{U}_{2}+\big(\mathcal{S}_{q}f\big)\big(\mathcal{S}_{q}g\big),

where
\texttt{U}_{2}(x)=(\alpha^{2}-1)(x^{2}-1)
. All these properties and definitions, even the notation, can be found, for instance, in
[
2
]
. The Askey-Wilson polynomials are defined by

p_{n}(x;a,b,c,d\,|\,q)=a^{-n}\,(ab,ac,ad;q)_{n}\,{}_{4}\phi_{3}\left(\left.\genfrac{.}{.}{0.0pt}{}{q^{-n}, a b c b d q^{n-1}, a e^{i \theta}, a e^{-i \theta}}{ab, ac, ad}\right|\,q\mathchar 59\relax\mkern 6.0muq\right),

where
x=\cos\theta
. If we take
a=q^{1/2\alpha+1/4}
,
b=q^{1/2\alpha+3/4}
,
c=-a
, and
d=-b
, we get the continuous
q
-Jacobi polynomials. If we take
d=0
, we get the continuous dual
q
-Hahn polynomials. If we take
c=d=0
, we get the Al-Salam-Chihara polynomials. The sequence of monic continuous dual
q
-Hahn polynomials,
(H_{n}(x;a,b|q))_{n\geq 0}
, satisfies

(5)

\displaystyle xH_{n}(x;a,b,c|q)=H_{n+1}(x;a,b,c|q)+a_{n}H_{n}(x;a,b,c|q)+b_{n}H_{n-1}(x;a,b,c|q)\;,

where
H_{-n-1}(\cdot;a,b,c|q)=0
and

\displaystyle a_{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}(a+a^{-1}-a(1-q^{n})(1-bcq^{n-1})-a^{-1}(1-abq^{n})(1-acq^{n})),

\displaystyle b_{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}(1-abq^{n-1})(1-acq^{n-1})(1-bcq^{n-1})(1-q^{n}).

Define
P_{n}=H_{n}(\cdot;1,-1,q^{1/4}|q^{1/2})
. Clearly,
P_{n}
is not a continuous
q
-Jacobi polynomial or Al-Salam-Chihara polynomial or, much less, special or limiting cases of them. These polynomials satisfy, among other relations, a relation of type (
2
) with
r=2
and
s=1
.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
P_{n}(x)=H_{n}(x;1,-1,q^{1/4}|q^{1/2})
. The sequence
(P_{n})_{n\geq 0}
satisfies the following relations:

(6)

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{q}P_{n}(x)

\displaystyle=\alpha_{n}P_{n}(x)+c_{n}P_{n-1}(x),

(7)

\displaystyle\texttt{U}_{2}(x)\mathcal{D}_{q}P_{n}(x)

\displaystyle=(\alpha^{2}-1)\gamma_{n}P_{n+1}(x)+\big(c_{n+1}-\alpha c_{n}+(1-\alpha)\alpha_{n}B_{n}\big)P_{n}(x)

\displaystyle\quad+\big((B_{n}-\alpha B_{n-1})c_{n}+(1-\alpha^{2})\gamma_{n}C_{n}\big)P_{n-1}(x)

\displaystyle\quad+(c_{n-1}C_{n}-\alpha c_{n}C_{n-1})P_{n-2}(x),

where

\displaystyle B_{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\Big((1+q^{-1/2})q^{n/2}+1-q^{-1/2}\Big)q^{(2n+1)/4},

\displaystyle C_{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}(1+q^{(n-1)/2})(1-q^{n/2})(1-q^{n-1/2}),

\displaystyle c_{n}

\displaystyle=C_{n}\,q^{-(2n-1)/4}.

Proof.

(
5
) makes it obvious that

(8)

\displaystyle xP_{n}(x)=P_{n+1}(x)+B_{n}P_{n}(x)+C_{n}P_{n-1}(x)\quad(n=0,1,\dots).

The proof is by complete mathematical induction on
n
. Note that
c_{0}=0
and
\alpha_{0}=1
, and so

c_{1}-\alpha c_{0}+(1-\alpha)\alpha_{0}B_{0}=0.

Hence, for
n=0
we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{q}P_{0}

\displaystyle=1=\alpha_{0}P_{0}+c_{0}P_{-1}=1,

\displaystyle\texttt{U}_{2}(x)\mathcal{D}_{q}P_{0}(x)

\displaystyle=0=(\alpha^{2}-1)\gamma_{0}P_{1}+0P_{0}=0.

Assuming (
6
) and (
7
) hold for all
n\leq k
, we will prove it for
n=k+1
. Set

\displaystyle c_{n,1}

\displaystyle=(\alpha^{2}-1)\gamma_{n},

\displaystyle c_{n,2}

\displaystyle=c_{n+1}-\alpha c_{n}+(1-\alpha)\alpha_{n}B_{n},

\displaystyle c_{n,3}

\displaystyle=(B_{n}-\alpha B_{n-1})c_{n}+(1-\alpha^{2})\gamma_{n}C_{n},

\displaystyle c_{n,4}

\displaystyle=c_{n-1}C_{n}-\alpha c_{n}C_{n-1}.

Now (
7
) reads as

(9)

\displaystyle\texttt{U}_{2}(x)\mathcal{D}_{q}P_{n}(x)=c_{n,1}P_{n+1}(x)+c_{n,2}P_{n}(x)+c_{n,3}P_{n-1}(x)+c_{n,4}P_{n-2}(x).

Applying
\mathcal{S}_{q}
to (
8
), and using (
4
), we get

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{q}P_{n+1}(x)

\displaystyle=\texttt{U}_{2}(x)\mathcal{D}_{q}\,x\mathcal{D}_{q}P_{n}(x)+\mathcal{S}_{q}\,x\mathcal{S}_{q}P_{n}(x)-B_{n}\mathcal{S}_{q}P_{n}(x)+C_{n}\mathcal{S}_{q}P_{n-1}(x)

(10)

\displaystyle=\texttt{U}_{2}(x)\mathcal{D}_{q}P_{n}(x)-B_{n}\mathcal{S}_{q}P_{n}(x)-C_{n}\mathcal{S}_{q}P_{n-1}(x)+\alpha x\mathcal{S}_{q}P_{n}(x).

From (
10
), and using (
9
) for
n=k
and (
6
) for
n=k-1
and
n=k
, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{q}P_{k+1}(x)

\displaystyle=c_{k,1}P_{k+1}(x)+c_{k,2}P_{k}(x)+c_{k,3}P_{k-1}(x)+c_{k,4}P_{k-2}(x)

\displaystyle\quad-B_{k}(\alpha_{k}P_{k}(x)+c_{k}P_{k-1}(x))-C_{k}(\alpha_{k-1}P_{k-1}(x)+c_{k-1}P_{k-2}(x))

\displaystyle\quad+\alpha x(\alpha_{k}P_{k}(x)+c_{k}P_{k-1}(x)).

From (
8
) we have

\displaystyle\alpha x(\alpha_{k}P_{k}(x)+c_{k}P_{k-1}(x))

\displaystyle=\alpha\alpha_{k}(P_{k+1}(x)+B_{k}P_{k}(x)+C_{k}P_{k-1}(x))

\displaystyle\quad+\alpha c_{k}(P_{k}(x)+B_{k-1}P_{k-1}(x)+C_{k-1}P_{k-2}(x)).

We leave to the reader the verification that

c_{k,3}+(\alpha\alpha_{k}-\alpha_{k-1})C_{k}+(\alpha B_{k-1}-B_{k})c_{k}=c_{k,4}+\alpha c_{k}C_{k-1}-c_{k-1}C_{k}=0,

and

\alpha_{k+1}=c_{k,1}+\alpha\,\alpha_{k},\quad c_{k+1}=c_{k,2}+\alpha c_{k}+(\alpha-1)\alpha_{k}B_{k}.

We thus get

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{q}P_{k+1}(x)

\displaystyle=(c_{k,1}+\alpha\,\alpha_{k})P_{k+1}(x)+(c_{k,2}+\alpha c_{k}+(\alpha-1)\alpha_{k}B_{k})P_{k}(x)

\displaystyle\quad+(c_{k,3}+(\alpha\alpha_{k}-\alpha_{k-1})C_{k}+(\alpha B_{k-1}-B_{k})c_{k})P_{k-1}(x)

\displaystyle\quad+(c_{k,4}+\alpha c_{k}C_{k-1}-c_{k-1}C_{k})P_{k-2}(x)

\displaystyle=\alpha_{k+1}P_{k+1}(x)+c_{k+1}P_{k}(x),

and (
6
) holds for
n=k+1
. Applying now
\mathcal{D}_{q}
to (
8
), and using (
3
), we get

(11)

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{q}P_{k+1}(x)=\mathcal{S}_{q}P_{k}(x)+(\alpha x-B_{k})\mathcal{D}_{q}P_{k}(x)-C_{k}\mathcal{D}_{q}P_{k-1}(x).

From (
8
) we have

\displaystyle\texttt{U}_{2}(x)P_{k}(x)

\displaystyle=(\alpha^{2}-1)P_{k+2}(x)+(\alpha^{2}-1)(B_{k}+B_{k+1})P_{k+1}(x)

\displaystyle\quad+(\alpha^{2}-1)(B_{k}^{2}+C_{k+1}+C_{k}-1)P_{k}(x)

\displaystyle\quad+(\alpha^{2}-1)C_{k}(B_{k}+B_{k-1})P_{k-1}(x)+(\alpha^{2}-1)C_{k}C_{k-1}P_{k-2}(x).

Hence, multiplying (
11
) by
\texttt{U}_{2}
and using (
6
) for
n=k
and (
9
) for
n=k-1
and
n=k
, we get

\displaystyle\texttt{U}_{2}(x)\mathcal{D}_{q}P_{k+1}(x)

\displaystyle=d_{k,1}P_{k+2}(x)+d_{k,2}P_{k+1}(x)+d_{k,3}P_{k}(x)+d_{k,4}P_{k-1}(x)

\displaystyle\quad+d_{k,5}P_{k-2}(x)+d_{k,6}P_{k-3}(x),

where

\displaystyle d_{k,1}

\displaystyle=(\alpha^{2}-1)\alpha_{k}+\alpha c_{k,1},

\displaystyle d_{k,2}

\displaystyle=(\alpha^{2}-1)(c_{k}+\alpha_{k}(B_{k}+B_{k+1}))+\alpha c_{k,2}-(B_{k}-\alpha B_{k+1})c_{k,1},

\displaystyle d_{k,3}

\displaystyle=(\alpha^{2}-1)((B_{k}+B_{k-1})c_{k}+\alpha_{k}(B_{k}^{2}+C_{k}+C_{k+1}-1))+\alpha c_{k,1}C_{k+1}

\displaystyle\quad-c_{k-1,1}C_{k}+(\alpha-1)c_{k,2}B_{n}+\alpha c_{k,3},

\displaystyle d_{k,4}

\displaystyle=(\alpha^{2}-1)((B_{k}+B_{k-1})\alpha_{k}C_{k}+(C_{k}+B_{k-1}^{2}+C_{k-1}-1)c_{k})

\displaystyle\quad-(c_{k-1,2}-\alpha c_{k,2})C_{k}-(B_{k}-\alpha B_{k-1})c_{k,3}+\alpha c_{k,4},

\displaystyle d_{k,5}

\displaystyle=(\alpha^{2}-1)C_{k-1}(\alpha_{k}C_{k}+c_{k}(B_{k-1}+B_{k-2}))+\alpha c_{k,3}C_{k-1}-c_{k-1,3}C_{k}

\displaystyle\quad-(B_{k}-\alpha B_{k-2})c_{k,4},

\displaystyle d_{k,6}

\displaystyle=(\alpha^{2}-1)c_{k}C_{k-1}C_{k-2}+\alpha c_{k,4}C_{k-2}-c_{k-1,4}C_{k}.

Finally, the reader should satisfy himself that
d_{k,1}=c_{k+1,1}
,
d_{k,2}=c_{k+1,2}
,
d_{k,3}=c_{k+1,3}
,
d_{k,4}=c_{k+1,4}
,
d_{k,5}=0
, and
d_{k,6}=0
, and (
7
) holds for
n=k+1
, but this is easily verified using any mathematical software. That completes the inductive step, and hence the proof.
∎

The previous proof by induction was elaborated after having found the sequence
(P_{n})_{n\geq 0}
by a method similar to the one used in
[
1
]
, which involves a high degree of technical complexity. However, as Galileo reputedly said: “All truths are easy to understand once they are discovered. The point is to discover them”. In this sense, and view of Proposition
2.1
, an interesting open problem is to characterize the sequence of orthogonal polynomials
(p_{n})_{n\geq 0}
such that
\mathcal{S}_{q}p_{n}
can be written as a linear combination of
p_{n}
and
p_{n-1}
.

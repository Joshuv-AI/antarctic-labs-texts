1.
Introduction

Let
\mathbb{D}:=\{z:\,|z|<1\}
be the unit disk in the
complex plane
\mathbb{C}
and
\mathcal{H}
denote the space of
all analytic functions on
{\mathbb{D}}
. Here we think of
\mathcal{H}
as
a topological vector space endowed with the topology of uniform
convergence over compact subsets of
\mathbb{D}
. A function
f
is said to be univalent if it is one-to-one. Further, let

\mathcal{A}:=\{f\in\mathcal{H}:\,f(0)=f^{\prime}(0)-1=0\}

and
\mathcal{S}
denote the class of
univalent
functions in

\mathcal{A}
. A domain
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{C}
is called a starlike domain with respect to a point
z_{0}\in\Omega
if the line segment
tw+(1-t)z_{0}
joining
z_{0}
to any other point
w
in
\Omega
lies entirely in
\Omega
. If
z_{0}=0
then the domain
\Omega
is called a starlike domain. A function
f\in\mathcal{S}
is said to be starlike with respect to
w_{0}\in f(\mathbb{D})
if the image domain
f(\mathbb{D})
is starlike with respect to
w_{0}
. If
w_{0}=0
,
f
is simply called a starlike function.
The class of univalent starlike functions is denoted by
{\mathcal{S}}^{*}
.
A domain
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{C}
is said to be convex if the line segment joining any two points of the domain
\Omega
is contained in
\Omega
. A function
f
is said to be convex if the image of the domain
\Omega
under
f
is a convex domain. The class of univalent convex functions is denoted by
{\mathcal{C}}
.

A function
f\in\mathcal{A}
is called
close-to-convex
if there exists a convex (univalent) function
g
and a number
\phi\in{\mathbb{R}}
such that
{\rm Re\,}(e^{i\phi}f^{\prime}(z)/g^{\prime}(z))>0

for
z\in{\mathbb{D}}
. Each univalent starlike function
f
is characterized by the
analytic condition
{\rm Re\,}(zf^{\prime}(z)/f(z))>0
in
{\mathbb{D}}
. Due to Alexender’s theorem
it is known that
zf^{\prime}
is starlike if, and only if,
f
is convex (see
[
5
]
). In
1952
, Kaplan
[
6
]
proved that
every close-to-convex function is univalent in
{\mathbb{D}}
. A function
f\in\mathcal{S}
is said to be starlike function of order
\alpha,-1/2\leq\alpha<1
if it satisfies the condition

{\rm Re\,}\left(\frac{zf^{\prime}(z)}{f(z)}\right)>\alpha\,\,\,\,\,\mbox{for}\quad z\in\mathbb{D}.

Let
\mathcal{S}^{*}(\alpha)
be the class of all starlike functions of order
\alpha
in
\mathbb{D}
. A function
f\in\mathcal{S}
is said to be convex function of order
\alpha,-1/2\leq\alpha<1
if it satisfies the condition

{\rm Re\,}\left(1+\frac{zf^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\right)>\alpha\,\,\,\,\mbox{for}\quad z\in\mathbb{D}.

Let
\mathcal{C}(\alpha)
be the class of all convex functions of order
\alpha
in
\mathbb{D}
.

Let
\mathcal{F}\subset\mathcal{A}
and
z_{0}\in\mathbb{D}
. Then upper and lower estimates of the form

K_{1}\leq f(z_{0})\leq K_{2},\,\,\,M_{1}\leq|f^{\prime}(z_{0})|\leq M_{2},\,\,\,m_{1}\leq\mbox{Arg}f^{\prime}(z_{0})\leq m_{2}\,\,\mbox{for all}\,\,f\in\mathcal{F},

are respectively called a growth theorem, a distortion theorem and a rotation theorem at
z_{0}
for
\mathcal{F}
, where
K_{i},M_{i}
and
m_{i}

(i=1,2)
are some non-negative constants. These estimates deal only with the absolute values of
f(z_{0})
and
f^{\prime}(z_{0})
or with the argument of
f^{\prime}(z_{0})
. If one wants to study the exact value of
f(z_{0})
and
f^{\prime}(z_{0})
, then it is necessary to consider the region of variability of
f(z_{0})
and
f^{\prime}(z_{0})
when
f
ranges over the class
\mathcal{F}
.

Region of variability for several classes of univalent functions has become an interesting area of research in geometric function theory. Several authors have studied the region of variability for several classes of functions. In
2005
, Yanagihara
[
15
]
studied region of variability for functions of bounded derivatives. In
2006
, Yanagihara
[
16
]
proved the region of variability for convex functions. In
2007
, Ponnusamy and Vasudevarao
[
7
]
studied the region of variability of two subclases of univalent functions. In
2009
, Ponnusamy
et al.

[
10
]
have studied the region of variability for the class
\mathcal{B}(\alpha,\beta,M)
of analytic and univalent funtions in the unit disk
\mathbb{D}
with
f(0)=0
,
f^{\prime}(0)=\alpha
, and
f^{\prime\prime}(0)=M\beta
satisfying
|zf^{\prime\prime}(z)|\leq M
for
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{C}
and
M\in\mathbb{R}
with
0<M\leq|\alpha|
and
|\beta|\leq 1
. Furthermore, Ponnusamy
et al.

[
10
]
have also studied the region of variability for the class
\mathcal{P}(\alpha,M)
of analytic and univalent funtions in
\mathbb{D}
with
f(0)=0
,
f^{\prime}(0)=\alpha
, and
f^{\prime\prime}(0)=M\beta
satisfying
{\operatorname{Re}\,}zf^{\prime\prime}(z)>-M
in
\mathbb{D}
, where
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}
and
0<M\leq 1/\log\,4
. In
2009
, Ponnusamy
et al.

[
11
]
studied the region of variability for spiral-like functions with respect to a boundary point. In
2010
, Ponnusamy and Vasudevarao
[
8
]
extensively studied the region of variability for functions with positive real part. In
2011
, Ponnusamy
et al.

[
12
]
investigated the region of variability for exponentially convex univalent functions. For other interesting results on region of variability, we refer to
[
7
,
9
,
13
]
.

If
f
is locally univalent function, then pre-Schwarzian norm is defined by

(1.1)

\|f\|=\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}\,(1-|z|^{2})\bigg|\dfrac{f^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\bigg|.

The class of univalent functions is preserved under a number of elementary transformations. The preservation of
\mathcal{S}
under the disk automorphism leads to the study of the behaviour of the pre-Schwarzian norm of
f
. The pre-Schwarzian norm has the close connection with functions in
\mathcal{S}
. Hence the study of region of variability problems together with pre-Schwarzian norm is very classic and worthy. It is well known that
||f||\leq 6
if f is univalent in
\mathbb{D}
, and conversely if
||f||\leq 1
then
f
is univalent in
\mathbb{D}
, and these bounds are sharp (see
[
3
]
).

The following lemma is useful to prove our main result.

Lemma 1.2
.

[
7
]

For
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
and
0<\lambda<1
, the function

\displaystyle G(z)=\int_{0}^{z}\frac{e^{i\theta}z}{\left(1+\left(e^{i\theta}-1\right)\lambda z-e^{i\theta}z\right)^{2}}\,dz,\;z\in\mathbb{D}

has a double zero at the origin and no zeros elsewhere in
\mathbb{D}
. Furthermore, these exists a starlike univalent function
G_{0}
in
\mathbb{D}
such that
G=2^{-1}e^{i\theta}G^{2}_{0}
and
G_{0}(0)=G^{\prime}_{0}(0)-1
.

For a positive integer
p
, let

\left(\mathcal{S}^{*}\right)^{p}=\{f=f_{0}^{p}:f_{0}\in\mathcal{S}^{*}\}.

Lemma 1.3
.

[
15
]

Let
f
be an analytic function in
\mathbb{D}
with
f(z)=z^{p}+\cdots.
If

{\operatorname{Re}\,}\left(1+z\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\right)>0,\,\,\,z\in\mathbb{D},

then
f\in\left(\mathcal{S}^{*}\right)^{p}
.

2.
Preliminaries

For
0<\alpha<1
and
0<\beta<1
, let

(2.1)

f_{\alpha}(z)=\displaystyle\frac{z}{1-z^{\alpha}},\,\,z\in\mathbb{D}

and

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}=\bigg\{f_{\alpha}\in\mathcal{A}:{\rm Re}\left(1+\frac{zf^{\prime\prime}_{\alpha}(z)}{f^{\prime}_{\alpha}(z)}\right)>\beta\bigg\}.

Let
{\mathcal{F}}(\lambda)=\{f_{\alpha}\in\mathcal{F}:\,f_{\alpha}^{\prime\prime}(0)=2\lambda(1-\beta)\,\,\,\mbox{for}\,\,\,0\leq\lambda\leq 1\}
.
For
f_{\alpha}\in\mathcal{F}
, let

(2.2)

P_{f}(z)=1+\displaystyle\frac{zf^{\prime\prime}_{\alpha}(z)}{f^{\prime}_{\alpha}(z)}-\beta.

Clearly,
P_{f}(0)=1-\beta
. Let
\mathcal{B}_{0}
be the class of analytic functions
\omega
in the unit disk
\mathbb{D}
such that
|\omega(z)|\leq 1
in
\mathbb{D}
and
\omega(0)=0
. Then for each
f\in\mathcal{F}(\lambda)
, there exists an
\omega_{f}\in\mathcal{B}_{0}
of the form

(2.3)

\displaystyle\omega_{f}(z):=\frac{P_{f}(z)-(1-\beta)}{P_{f}(z)+(1-\beta)}.

A simple computation shows that

\displaystyle\omega^{\prime}_{f}(0)=\lim_{z\rightarrow 0}\frac{\omega_{f}(z)-\omega_{f}(0)}{z-0}=\lim_{z\rightarrow 0}\frac{\omega_{f}(z)}{z}.

Let
\omega_{f}^{\prime}(0)=\lambda
, where
|\lambda|\leq 1
. From (
2.3
), we have

\omega_{f}^{\prime}(z)=\dfrac{2(1-\beta)P^{\prime}_{f}(z)}{\left(P_{f}(z)+(1-\beta)\right)^{2}}.

Since
P_{f}(0)=1-\beta
, we have

(2.4)

\omega^{\prime}_{f}(0)=\frac{P^{\prime}_{f}(0)}{2(1-\beta)}.

Therefore

(2.5)

\lambda=\frac{P^{\prime}_{f}(0)}{2(1-\beta)}.

From (
2.2
), we have

(2.6)

P_{f}(z)f^{\prime}_{\alpha}(z)=(1-\beta)f^{\prime}_{\alpha}(z)+zf^{\prime\prime}_{\alpha}(z).

Differentiating (
2.6
) gives

(2.7)

P^{\prime}_{f}(z)f^{\prime}_{\alpha}(z)+\left(P_{f}(z)-(1-\beta)-1\right)f^{\prime\prime}_{\alpha}(z)+zf^{\prime\prime}_{\alpha}(z)=0.

A simple computation of (
2.7
) at
z=0
shows that

(2.8)

P^{\prime}_{f}(0)=f^{\prime\prime}_{\alpha}(0).

From (
2.5
) and (
2.8
), we obtain

(2.9)

f^{\prime\prime}_{\alpha}(0)=2\lambda(1-\beta).

Define
g:\mathbb{D}\rightarrow\mathbb{D}
by

g(z)=\displaystyle\frac{\frac{\omega_{f}(z)}{z}-\lambda}{1-\frac{\overline{\lambda}\omega_{f}(z)}{z}},

equivalently

g(z)=\dfrac{\omega_{f}(z)-\lambda z}{z-\overline{\lambda}\omega_{f}(z)}.

Then, clearly
g(0)=0
and
g
is an analytic function in
\mathbb{D}
. Therefore
g\in\mathcal{B}_{0}
.

First we prove that the class
\mathcal{F}(\lambda)
is rotationally invariant.
The class
\mathcal{F}(\lambda)
is rotationally invariant if, and only if,
f_{\theta}\in\mathcal{F}(\lambda)
, where
f_{\theta}:=e^{-i\theta}f_{\alpha}(e^{i\theta}z)
, where
f_{\alpha}
is defined by (
2.1
).
A simple computation shows that

\displaystyle f^{\prime}_{\theta}(z)=f_{\alpha}^{\prime}\left(e^{i\theta}z\right)\;\;\mbox{and}\,\,f^{\prime\prime}_{\theta}(z)=e^{i\theta}f_{\alpha}^{\prime\prime}\left(e^{i\theta}z\right).

Then, it is easy to see that

\displaystyle{\rm Re}\left(1+\frac{zf^{\prime\prime}_{\theta}(z)}{f^{\prime}_{\theta}(z)}\right)={\rm Re}\left(1+\frac{ze^{i\theta}f^{\prime\prime}\left(e^{i\theta}z\right)}{f^{\prime}\left(e^{i\theta}z\right)}\right)={\rm Re}\left(1+\frac{\xi f^{\prime\prime}(\xi)}{f^{\prime}(\xi)}\right)>\beta,

where
\xi=ze^{i\theta}
. Since
f_{\alpha}\in\mathcal{F}(\lambda)
, it is easy to see that
f_{\theta}(0)=0
,
f^{\prime}_{\theta}(0)=1
, and
f^{\prime\prime}_{\theta}(0)=2\lambda(1-\beta)e^{i\theta}
. Therefore,
f_{\theta}\in\mathcal{F}(\lambda)
. Hence the class
\mathcal{F}(\lambda)
is rotational invariant. Since the class
\mathcal{F}(\lambda)
is rotationally invariant, without loss of generality we may assume that
0\leq\lambda\leq 1
instead of
\lambda\in\mathbb{D}
.

We wish to construct an extremal function
F_{a,\lambda}(z)
where
a\in\overline{\mathbb{D}}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|\leq 1\}
and it belongs to the class
\mathcal{F}(\lambda)
.
From the second condition of the Schwarz lemma, we have
g(z)=az
, where
|a|=1
. Therefore, we see that

\displaystyle g(z)=\dfrac{\omega_{f}(z)-\lambda z}{z-\overline{\lambda}\omega_{f}(z)}=\frac{P_{f}(z)-(1-\beta)\left(\dfrac{1+\lambda z}{1-\lambda z}\right)}{P_{f}(z)+(1-\beta)\left(\dfrac{z+\lambda}{z-\lambda}\right)}=az

which implies that

(2.10)

\displaystyle\dfrac{\displaystyle\frac{f_{\alpha}^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f_{\alpha}^{\prime}(z)}-\frac{2(1-\beta)\lambda}{1-\lambda z}}{\displaystyle\frac{f_{\alpha}^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f_{\alpha}^{\prime}(z)}+\frac{2(1-\beta)}{z-\lambda}}=az\left(\frac{z-\lambda}{1-\lambda z}\right):=az\,\delta(z,\lambda),\,\,\mbox{where}\,\,\delta(z,\lambda)=\frac{z-\lambda}{1-\lambda z}.

Further simplification of (
2.10
) gives

(2.11)

\displaystyle\dfrac{f_{\alpha}^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f_{\alpha}^{\prime}(z)}=2(1-\beta)\dfrac{\dfrac{\lambda}{1-\lambda}+\dfrac{az\delta(z,\lambda)}{z-\lambda}}{1-az\delta(z,\lambda)}=\dfrac{2(1-\beta)(az+\lambda)}{1-\lambda z-az^{2}+\lambda az}.

From (
2.11
), we obtain

(2.12)

\displaystyle\frac{f_{\alpha}^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f_{\alpha}^{\prime}(z)}=2(1-\beta)\frac{\delta(az,\lambda)}{1-\delta(az,\lambda)z}.

Integrating both sides of (
2.12
), we obtain

(2.13)

\displaystyle F_{a,\lambda}(z):=f_{\alpha}(z)=\int_{0}^{z}\exp\left(\int_{0}^{\xi}\frac{2(1-\beta)\delta(a\xi,\lambda)}{1-\delta(a\xi,\lambda)}\,\,d\xi\right)d\xi.

Clearly,
F_{a,\lambda}(0)=0,F_{a,\lambda}^{\prime}(0)=1
and
F_{a,\lambda}^{\prime\prime}(0)=2\lambda(1-\beta)
.
Hence
F_{a,\lambda}\in\mathcal{F}(\lambda)
.

If
f\in\mathcal{F}(\lambda)
, we define

(2.14)

h(z)=\frac{P_{f}(z)}{1-\beta}.

where

P_{f}(z)=1+zf_{\alpha}^{\prime\prime}/{f_{\alpha}^{\prime}}-\beta.

So that
{\rm Re\,}h(z)>0
and
h(0)=1
.

Therefore by Herglotz representation for functions with positive real part there exists unit probability measure
\mu
on
(-\pi,\pi]
,
i.e.,

\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\,d\mu(t)=1,

such that

(2.15)

h(z)=\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\frac{1+ze^{-it}}{1-ze^{-it}}\,\,d\mu(t).

In view of (
2.14
), we have

P_{f}(z)=(1-\beta)\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\frac{1+ze^{-it}}{1-ze^{-it}}\,\,d\mu(t).

From (
), we obtain

(2.16)

1+\frac{zf_{\alpha}^{\prime\prime}}{f_{\alpha}^{\prime}}-\beta=(1-\beta)\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\frac{1+ze^{-it}}{1-ze^{-it}}\,\,d\mu(t),

which implies that

\frac{zf_{\alpha}^{\prime\prime}}{f_{\alpha}^{\prime}}=(1-\beta)\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\bigg(\frac{1+ze^{-it}}{1-ze^{-it}}-1\bigg)\,\,d\mu(t).

Therefore,

\frac{f_{\alpha}^{\prime\prime}}{f_{\alpha}^{\prime}}=2(1-\beta)\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\frac{e^{-it}}{1-ze^{-it}}\,\,d\mu(t).

Integrating on both sides with respect to
z
, we obtain

\log f_{\alpha}^{\prime}(z)=2(1-\beta)\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}-\log(1-ze^{-it})\,\,d\mu(t),

and hence

(2.17)

f_{\alpha}(z)=\int_{0}^{t}\exp\bigg(2(1-\beta)\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}-\log(1-ze^{-it})\,d\mu(t)\bigg).

The main aim of this paper is to obtain the region of variability of
\log f^{\prime}(z_{0})
where
f
runs over the class
\mathcal{F}(\lambda)
. Further, we demonstrate the region of variability of
\log f^{\prime}(z_{0})
for the class
\mathcal{F}(\lambda)
by using Mathematica.

3.
Main Results

Consider the following set

V(z_{0},\lambda,\beta)=\{\log f^{\prime}(z_{0}):f\in\mathcal{F}(\lambda)\}.

Now we mention some basic properties of the set
V(z_{0},\lambda,\beta)
.

Proposition 3.1
.

We have

(1)

V(z_{0},\lambda,\beta)
is compact.

(2)

V(z_{0},\lambda,\beta)
is convex.

(3)

For
|\lambda|=1
or
z_{0}=0
, we have

(3.2)

V(z_{0},\lambda,\beta)=\{-2(1-\beta)\log(1-\lambda z)\}

(4)

For
|\lambda|<1
and
z_{0}\in\mathbb{D}\setminus\{0\}
,
V(z_{0},\lambda,\beta)
has
-2(1-\beta)\log(1-\lambda z)
as an interior point.

Proposition 3.3
.

For
f\in\mathcal{F}(\lambda)
where
0\leq\lambda<1
, we have

\displaystyle\left|\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}-c(z,\lambda,\beta)\right|\leq r(z,\lambda,\beta),\;z\in\mathbb{D}.

where

c(z,\lambda,\beta)=\frac{2(1-\beta)\left(\lambda(1-|z|^{2})+(|z|^{2}-\lambda^{2})\right)\bar{z}}{(1-|z|^{2})(1-2\lambda({\rm Re}z)+|z|^{2})}

and

r(z,\lambda,\beta)=\frac{2(1-\beta)(1-\lambda^{2})|z|}{(1-|z|^{2})(1-2\lambda({\rm Re}z)+|z|^{2})}

For each
z\in\mathbb{D}\setminus\{0\}
,equality hold if, and only if,
f=F_{e^{i\theta},\lambda}
for some
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
.

Corollary 3.4
.

Let
\gamma:=z(t)
, where
0\leq t\leq 1
be a
C^{1}
curve in
\mathbb{D}
with
z(0)=0
and
z(1)=z_{0}
. Then, we have

\displaystyle V(z_{0},\lambda,\beta)\subset\bigg\{w\in\mathbb{C}:|\omega-C(\lambda,\gamma,\beta)|\bigg\}\leq R(\lambda,\gamma,\beta),

where
C(\lambda,\gamma,\beta)=\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}c(z(t),\lambda,\beta)z^{\prime}(t)dt
and

R(\lambda,\gamma,\beta)=\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}r(z(t),\lambda,\beta)|z^{\prime}(t)|dt
.

Now we state the following result which is vital to prove our main result.

Proposition 3.5
.

(Uniqueness of the curve)
Let
z_{0}\in\mathbb{D}\setminus\{0\}
. Then, for
\theta\in(-\pi,\pi]
, we have

\displaystyle\log F^{\prime}_{e^{i\theta},\lambda}(z_{0})\in\partial V(z_{0},\lambda,\beta).

Furthermore, if
\log f^{\prime}(z_{0})=\log F^{\prime}_{e^{i\theta},\lambda}(z_{0})
for some
f\in\mathcal{F}(\lambda)
and
\theta\in(-\pi,\pi]
, then
f=F_{e^{i\theta},\lambda}
.

Finally, we state our main result to obtain the region of variability of
\log f^{\prime}(z_{0})
where
f
runs over the class
\mathcal{F}(\lambda)
.

Theorem 3.6
.

For
0\leq\lambda<1
and
z_{0}\in\mathbb{D}\setminus\{0\}
,
the boundary
\partial{V(z_{0},\lambda,\beta)}
is the Jordan curve given
by

(3.7)

(-\pi,\pi]\ni\theta\mapsto\log F^{\prime}_{e^{i\theta},\lambda}(z_{0})=\int_{0}^{z_{0}}\frac{\delta(e^{i\theta}z,\lambda)}{z\delta(e^{i\theta}z,\lambda)-1}\,dz,\quad z\in\mathbb{D}.

If
\log f^{\prime}(z_{0})=\log F^{\prime}_{e^{i\theta},\lambda}(z_{0})
for some

f\in\mathcal{F}(\lambda)
and
\theta\in(-\pi,\pi]
, then
f(z)=F_{e^{i\theta},\lambda}(z)
. Here
F_{e^{i\theta},\lambda}(z)
is
given by

(3.8)

F_{e^{i\theta},\lambda}(z)=f_{\alpha}(z)=\int_{0}^{z}\exp\left(\int_{0}^{\xi}\frac{2(1-\beta)\delta(e^{i\theta}\xi,\lambda)}{1-\delta(e^{i\theta}\xi,\lambda)}d\xi\right)d\xi.

4.
Proof of the main results

Proof of Proposition
3.1
.

(1) To show that
V(z_{0},\lambda,\beta)
we need to show that the set
\mathcal{F}(\lambda)
is compact. For this, we need to show that
\mathcal{F}(\lambda)
is closed and locally bounded. Let
f_{n}
be a sequence of functions in
F(\lambda)
such that
f_{n}\rightarrow f
uniformly on every compact subsets of the unit disk. Then to show that
F(\lambda)
is compact, we show that
f\in F(\lambda)
. Since
f_{n}\rightarrow f
uniformly on every compact subset of
\mathbb{D}
, we have
f^{\prime}_{n}\rightarrow f^{\prime}
uniformly on every compact subsets of
\mathbb{D}
. Therefore, if
f_{n}\rightarrow f
uniformly, then
f_{n}(0)\rightarrow f(0)
pointwise which gives that
f(0)=0
. Since,
f^{\prime}_{n}\rightarrow f^{\prime}
uniformly on every compact subsets of
\mathbb{D}
, we get

(4.1)

f^{\prime}_{n}(0)\rightarrow f^{\prime}(0),\,\,f^{\prime\prime}_{n}(0)\rightarrow f^{\prime\prime}(0)

pointwise. Since
f_{n}\in\mathcal{F}(\lambda)
,
f^{\prime}_{n}(0)=1
and
f^{\prime\prime}_{n}(0)=2\lambda(1-\beta)
. In view of (
4.1
), we have
f^{\prime}(0)=1
and
f^{\prime\prime}(0)=2\lambda(1-\beta)
. In view of the above discussion, it is easy to see that

1+\dfrac{zf^{\prime\prime}_{n}(z)}{f^{\prime}_{n}(z)}\longrightarrow 1+\dfrac{zf^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}

uniformly on every compact subsets of
\mathbb{D}
. Since
f_{n}\in\mathcal{F}(\lambda)
, we have

0<{\operatorname{Re}\,}\left(1+\dfrac{zf^{\prime\prime}_{n}(z)}{f^{\prime}_{n}(z)}\right)\longrightarrow{\operatorname{Re}\,}\left(1+\dfrac{zf^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\right)\geq 0

in
\mathbb{D}
. Therefore, by the maximum modulus principle, it follows that

{\operatorname{Re}\,}\left(1+\dfrac{zf^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\right)>0

in
\mathbb{D}
. Locally boundedness follows from the definition itself, thus

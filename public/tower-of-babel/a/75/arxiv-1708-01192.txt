The rational points on certain Abelian varieties over function fields
Sajad Salami

Abstract
In this paper, we consider Abelian varieties over function fields that arise as twists of Abelian varieties by cyclic covers of irreducible quasi-projective varieties. Then, in terms of Prym varieties associated to the cyclic covers, we prove a structure theorem on their Mordell-Weil group. Our results give an explicit method for construction of elliptic curves, hyper- and super-elliptic Jacobians that have large ranks over function fields of certain varieties.

Introduction and main results

Let
{\mathcal{A}}
be an Abelian variety defined over an arbitrary global field
k
. By the Mordell-Weil Theorem, the set
{\mathcal{A}}(k)

of
k
-rational points on
A
is a finitely generated abelain group
[
6
]
. In other words, one has
{\mathcal{A}}(k)\cong{\mathcal{A}}(k)_{tors}\oplus{\mathbb{Z}}^{r}
where
{\mathcal{A}}(k)_{tors}
is
a finite subgroup of
{\mathcal{A}}(k)
that is called the torsion subgroup; and
r
is a non-negative number

r
which is called the (Mordell-Weil) rank of
{\mathcal{A}}
over
k
and is denoted by
{\text{rk}}({\mathcal{A}}(k))
.
It is a mysterious quantity associated to an Abelian variety.
Finding Abelian varieties with large ranks is one of the most challenging problems in Arithmetic and Diophantine geometry.

For example, when
{\mathcal{A}}
is an elliptic curve defined over
k={\mathbb{Q}}
a folklore conjecture asserts that the
rank of an elliptic curve over
{\mathbb{Q}}
can be arbitrary large
[
12
]
. This conjecture should now be regarded as being in serious doubt by the results of J. Park and et. al., in
[
10
]
. In short, they predict that the
number of elliptic curves over
{\mathbb{Q}}
with rank
\geq 21
is finite. However, in 2006, Elkies showed the
existence of an elliptic curve over
{\mathbb{Q}}
with
28
independent generators.
In the case of elliptic curves over quadratic number fields
k={\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt{d})

the largest known rank is
30
with
d=-3
, which has been found by F. Najman.
To see the equation of these curves and more information on the high rank elliptic
curves over rational numbers and quadratic number fields, we refer the reader to
[
3
]
.

In contrast, for each prime
p
, there are known explicit elliptic curves over
k={\mathbb{F}}_{p}(t)
with arbitrary large rank

[
13
,
14
]
. In the case
k={\mathbb{C}}(t)
, it has been proved that for a very general elliptic curve
E
over
k

with height
d\geq 3
and every finite rational extension
k^{\prime}
of
k
the Mordell-Weil group
E(k^{\prime})

is a trivial group, see
[
15
]
.

In this paper, we generalize the main result of Hazama in
[
5
]

to an arbitrary cyclic
s
-covers of irreducible quasi-projective varieties for any integer

.
We fix a global field

of characteristic

, so that

s

.
Let us denote by

the subgroup of
k
-rational
s
-division points of

.
We assume that there exists an order

Given the irreducible quasi-projective varieties

and

with function fields

See Section

.
Let

Denote by

Theorem 1.1

Notation being as above, we assume that there exists a

Then we have an isomorphism of Abelian groups:

{\mathcal{A}}_{a}({\mathcal{K}})\cong{\text{Hom}}_{k}({\text{Prym}}_{{\mathcal{V}}^{\prime}/{\mathcal{V}}},{\mathcal{A}})\oplus{\mathcal{A}}[s](k).

Moreover, if

is
k
-isogenous to

2
The Prym variety associated to the cyclic covers

The notion of Prym variety was introduced by Mumford in
[
9
]
and has been extensively studied
for double covers of curves in
[
1
]
. It has been generalized for double covers of irreducible quasi-projective
varieties in
[
5
]
. Here, we generalize this notion to the case of cyclic
s
-covers of
varieties.

Definition 2.1

For an integer
s\geq 2
, the Prym variety of the cyclic
s
-cover
\pi:{\mathcal{V}}^{\prime}\rightarrow{\mathcal{V}}
of irreducible
quasi-projective varieties over
k
is defined by the quotient Abelian variety

{\text{Prym}}_{{\mathcal{V}}^{\prime}/{\mathcal{V}}}:=\frac{{\text{Alb}}({\mathcal{V}}^{\prime})}{\text{Im}(id+\tilde{\gamma}+\cdots+\tilde{\gamma}^{s-1})},

where
{\text{Alb}}({\mathcal{V}}^{\prime})
is the Albanese variety and
\tilde{\gamma}
is the automorphism of
{\text{Alb}}({\mathcal{V}}^{\prime})

induced by an order
s
automorphism
\gamma\in{\text{Aut}}({\mathcal{V}}^{\prime})
defined over
k
.

We note that if both of the varieties
{\mathcal{V}}
and
{\mathcal{V}}^{\prime}
are curves, then this definition is
compatible with the one given in
[
8
]
, by the following lemma.

Lemma 2.2

Given an integer
s\geq 2
, let
\pi:{\mathcal{V}}^{\prime}\rightarrow{\mathcal{V}}
be a cyclic
s
-cover of irreducible quasi-projective varieties,
both as well as
\pi
defined over
k
.
Suppose that
\gamma\in{\text{Aut}}({\mathcal{V}}^{\prime})
is an automorphism of order
s
defined over
k
.
Denote by
\tilde{\gamma}
the automorphism of the Albanese variety
{\text{Alb}}({\mathcal{V}}^{\prime})
induced by
\gamma.

Then there is a
k
-isogeny of Abelian varieties,

{\text{Prym}}_{{\mathcal{V}}^{\prime}/{\mathcal{V}}}\sim_{k}\ker(id+\tilde{\gamma}+\cdots+\tilde{\gamma}^{s-1}:{\text{Alb}}({\mathcal{V}}^{\prime})\rightarrow{\text{Alb}}({\mathcal{V}}^{\prime}))^{\circ},

where
(*)^{\circ}
means the connected component of its origin.

Proof.
Let
{\mathcal{A}}={\text{Alb}}({\mathcal{V}}^{\prime})
and denote its dimension by
m
. Given

\gamma\in{\text{Aut}}({\mathcal{A}})
of order
s
. Define

m_{1}:=\dim\ker(id-\gamma)^{\circ},
and
m_{2}:=\dim\ker(id+\gamma+\cdots+\gamma^{s-1})^{\circ}.

Then,
m=m_{1}+m_{2}
by considering the induced action on the tangent space of
{\mathcal{A}}
at the origin.
We have
\gamma(P)=P
for each point
P
belonging to the intersection of

\ker(id-\gamma)^{\circ}
and
\ker(id+\gamma+\cdots+\gamma^{s-1})^{\circ}
, so

0=(id+\gamma+\cdots+\gamma^{s-1})(P)=sP,
which implies that

\ker(id-\gamma)^{\circ}\cap\ker(id+\gamma+\cdots+\gamma^{s-1})^{\circ}\subseteq{\mathcal{A}}[s].

Thus
{\mathcal{A}}
is
k
-isogenous to their product, i.e.,

{\mathcal{A}}\sim_{k}\ker(id-\gamma)^{\circ}\times\ker(id+\gamma+\cdots+\gamma^{s-1})^{\circ}.

Moreover, we note that
\text{Im}(id+\gamma+\cdots+\gamma^{s-1})\subseteq\ker(id-\gamma)^{\circ}
and

m-m_{2}=\dim\text{Im}(id+\gamma+\cdots+\gamma^{s-1})=\dim\ker(id-\gamma)^{\circ}=m_{1}.

Therefore,
\text{Im}(id+\gamma+\cdots+\gamma^{s-1})=\ker(id-\gamma)^{\circ}
that gives the desired result.

Here, we describe a general method of construction of new
s
-cover using the
given ones, which we will use in the proof of Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
.

Let
\pi_{i}:{\mathcal{V}}^{\prime}_{i}\rightarrow{\mathcal{V}}_{i}
for
i=1,2
be
s
-covers of irreducible quasi-projective varieties,
all defined over
k
.
Assume there exist
k
-rational simple points
v^{\prime}_{i}\in{\mathcal{V}}^{\prime}_{i}
. Denote by

G_{i}
the cyclic Galois group of the corresponding function field extensions.
Then, the covering
\pi_{1}\times\pi_{2}:{\mathcal{V}}^{\prime}_{1}\times{\mathcal{V}}^{\prime}_{2}\rightarrow{\mathcal{V}}_{1}\times{\mathcal{V}}_{2}
has Galois group

G_{1}\times G_{2}\cong{\mathbb{Z}}/s{\mathbb{Z}}\times{\mathbb{Z}}/s{\mathbb{Z}}.

Suppose that
{\mathcal{W}}
is its intermediate cover
{\mathcal{V}}^{\prime}_{1}\times{\mathcal{V}}^{\prime}_{2}/G
, where

G
is the group generated by
\gamma=(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})\in{\text{Aut}}({\mathcal{V}}^{\prime}_{1}\times{\mathcal{V}}^{\prime}_{2})
.
Let
\tilde{\gamma}=(\tilde{\gamma}_{1},\tilde{\gamma}_{2})
be the order
s

automorphism in
{\text{Aut}}({\text{Alb}}({\mathcal{V}}^{\prime}_{1})\times{\text{Alb}}({\mathcal{V}}^{\prime}_{2}))
corresponding to
\gamma
, where

\tilde{\gamma}_{i}
is an automorphism of
{\text{Alb}}({\mathcal{V}}^{\prime}_{i})

induced by
\gamma_{i}\in{\text{Aut}}({\mathcal{V}}^{\prime}_{i})
of order
s\geq 2
for
i=1,2
.
Then there exists a
k
-rational isomorphism

\phi:={\text{Alb}}({\mathcal{V}}^{\prime}_{1})\times{\text{Alb}}({\mathcal{V}}^{\prime}_{2})\rightarrow{\text{Alb}}({\mathcal{V}}^{\prime}_{1}\times{\mathcal{V}}^{\prime}_{2}),

given by
\phi=\tilde{\phi}_{1}+\tilde{\phi}_{2}
, where
\tilde{\phi}_{i}:{\text{Alb}}({\mathcal{V}}^{\prime}_{i})\rightarrow{\text{Alb}}({\mathcal{V}}^{\prime}_{1})\times{\text{Alb}}({\mathcal{V}}^{\prime}_{2})
is induced by the inclusion map
\phi_{i}:{\mathcal{V}}^{\prime}_{i}\rightarrow{\mathcal{V}}^{\prime}_{1}\times{\mathcal{V}}^{\prime}_{2}
defined by

\phi_{1}(v)=(v,v^{\prime}_{2})
and
\phi_{2}(v)=(v^{\prime}_{1},v)
.
By this isomorphism, we have

\ker(\mu)\sim_{k}\ker(\mu_{1})\times\ker(\mu_{2}),

where
\mu:=id+\tilde{\gamma}+\cdots+\tilde{\gamma}^{s-1}
and

\mu_{i}:=id+\tilde{\gamma}_{i}+\cdots+\tilde{\gamma}_{i}^{s-1}
for
i=1,2
.
This implies that

\ker(\mu)^{\circ}\sim_{k}\ker(\mu_{1})^{\circ}\times\ker(\mu_{2})^{\circ}.

Therefore, applying Lemma (
2.2
) and putting everything together, we conclude the following result.

Proposition 2.3

As a
k
-rational isogeny of Abelian varieties, we have

{\text{Prym}}_{{\mathcal{V}}^{\prime}_{1}\times{\mathcal{V}}^{\prime}_{2}/{\mathcal{W}}}\sim_{k}{\text{Prym}}_{{\mathcal{V}}^{\prime}_{1}/{\mathcal{V}}_{1}}\times{\text{Prym}}_{{\mathcal{V}}^{\prime}_{2}/{\mathcal{V}}_{2}}.

3
The result of Hazama

In
[
4
,
5
]
, Hazama gave an explicit method of construction of Abelian varieties
that have large rank over function fields using the twist theory
[
2
,
4
]
.
In
[
16
]
, Wang extended the result of
[
4
]
to cyclic covers of the projective
line with prime degrees.
Inspired by Hazama’s result, in
[
17
]
, Yamagishi reduces the problem of
constructing elliptic curves of rank
n\geq 1
with generators to the problem of finding rational points on a certain varieties.
By providing a parametrization for the rational points on those varieties, she gets all of the
elliptic curves of rank
1\leq n\leq 7
defined over a field of characteristic different from two.

Here, we briefly recall the main result of Hazama from
[
5
]
.
Let
{\mathcal{A}}
be an Abelian variety over
k
with characteristic different from two.
Suppose that
\pi:{\mathcal{V}}^{\prime}\rightarrow{\mathcal{V}}
is a double cover with Prym variety

{\text{Prym}}({\mathcal{V}}^{\prime}/{\mathcal{V}})
, of irreducible quasi-projective varieties
{\mathcal{V}}
and
{\mathcal{V}}^{\prime}
defined over
k
.
Let
{\mathcal{K}}
and
{\mathcal{K}}^{\prime}
be the function field of
{\mathcal{V}}
and
{\mathcal{V}}^{\prime}
, respectively, and

G
the Galois group of the extension
{\mathcal{K}}^{\prime}|{\mathcal{K}}
. Denote by
{\mathcal{A}}_{a}
the twist of
{\mathcal{A}}
by
the
1
-cocycle
a=(a_{u})\in Z^{1}(G,Aut({\mathcal{A}}))
defined by
a_{id}=id
and
a_{\iota}=-id
.

Theorem 3.1

With the above notations, assume that there exist a
k
-rational simple point
v^{\prime}_{0}\in{\mathcal{V}}^{\prime}
.
Then we have an isomorphism of Abelian groups:

{\mathcal{A}}_{a}({\mathcal{K}})\cong{\text{Hom}}_{k}({\text{Prym}}({\mathcal{V}}^{\prime}/{\mathcal{V}}),{\mathcal{A}})\oplus{\mathcal{A}}[2](k).

Moreover, if
{\text{Prym}}_{{\mathcal{V}}^{\prime}/{\mathcal{V}}}
is
k
-isogenous with
{\mathcal{E}}^{n}\times{\mathcal{B}}
for some positive integer
n
,
where
{\mathcal{E}}
is an elliptic curve over
k
and
{\mathcal{B}}
is an Abelian variety with no simple component

k
-isogenous to
{\mathcal{E}}
, then

{\text{rk}}({\mathcal{E}}_{b}({\mathcal{K}}))=n\cdot{\text{rk}}({\text{End}}_{k}({\mathcal{E}})).

We refer the reader to 2.2 and 2.3 in
[
5
]
, for the proof of the above theorem.

Proof of Theorem
1.1

Suppose that the natural map
i_{{\mathcal{V}}^{\prime}}:{\mathcal{V}}^{\prime}\rightarrow{\text{Alb}}({\mathcal{V}}^{\prime})
sends
v^{\prime}_{0}
to the origin
of
{\text{Alb}}({\mathcal{V}}^{\prime})
so that
i_{{\mathcal{V}}^{\prime}}
is defined over
k
. Then, using Theorem 4 of chapter II in
[
7
]
, we have

{\mathcal{A}}({\mathcal{K}}^{\prime})=\{\text{$k$-rational maps ${\mathcal{V}}^{\prime}\rightarrow{\mathcal{A}}$ }\}\cong{\text{Hom}}_{k}({\text{Alb}}({\mathcal{V}}^{\prime}),{\mathcal{A}})\oplus{\mathcal{A}}(k),

where
P\in{\mathcal{A}}({\mathcal{K}}^{\prime})
corresponds to the pair
(\lambda,Q)\in{\text{Hom}}_{k}({\text{Alb}}({\mathcal{V}}^{\prime}),{\mathcal{A}})\oplus{\mathcal{A}}(k)
such that

P(v^{\prime})=\lambda(i_{{\mathcal{V}}^{\prime}}(v^{\prime}))+Q
for each
v^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{V}}^{\prime}
.
This implies that the action of
\gamma^{j}\in G
is given by
\gamma^{j}(\lambda,Q)=(\lambda\circ\tilde{\gamma}^{j},Q)

for
j=0,\cdots,s-1,
where
\tilde{\gamma}
is the automorphism of the Albanese variety
{\text{Alb}}({\mathcal{V}}^{\prime})
induced by
\gamma\in{\text{Aut}}({\mathcal{V}}^{\prime}).

Since
\gamma^{s}=id
and hence
\tilde{\gamma}^{s}=id
, so

{\mathcal{A}}_{a}({\mathcal{K}})\cong\{P\in{\mathcal{A}}({\mathcal{K}}^{\prime}):b_{\gamma^{j}}\cdot^{\gamma^{j}}(P)=P,\ \forall{\gamma^{j}}\in G\},

by applying the proposition 1.1 in
[
4
]
.
This implies that
(\lambda,Q)\in{\mathcal{A}}_{a}({\mathcal{K}})
if and only if

\gamma^{j}(\lambda,Q)=(\lambda\circ\tilde{\gamma}^{j},Q)=(\lambda\circ\tilde{\gamma}^{s-j},Q)=\gamma^{s-j}(\lambda,Q).

Thus,
(\lambda,Q)\in{\mathcal{A}}_{a}({\mathcal{K}})
if and only if

\lambda
annihilates
Im(id+\tilde{\gamma}+\cdots+\tilde{\gamma}^{s-1})
and
Q\in{\mathcal{A}}[s](k)
.
Therefore,

{\mathcal{A}}_{a}({\mathcal{K}})\cong{\text{Hom}}_{k}({\text{Prym}}_{{\mathcal{V}}^{\prime}/{\mathcal{V}}},{\mathcal{A}})\oplus{\mathcal{A}}[s](k).

Furthermore, if we assume that
{\text{Prym}}_{{\mathcal{V}}^{\prime}/{\mathcal{V}}}
is
k
-isogenous with
{\mathcal{A}}^{n}\times{\mathcal{B}}
for some positive integer
n
,
where
{\mathcal{A}}
and
{\mathcal{B}}
are Abelian varieties defined over
k
such that
\dim({\mathcal{B}})=0
or
\dim({\mathcal{B}})>\dim({\mathcal{A}})

and none of irreducible components of
{\mathcal{B}}
is
k
-isogenous to
{\mathcal{A}}
, then

\displaystyle{\mathcal{A}}_{a}({\mathcal{K}})

\displaystyle\cong{\text{Hom}}_{k}({\text{Prym}}_{{\mathcal{V}}^{\prime}/{\mathcal{V}}},{\mathcal{A}})\oplus{\mathcal{A}}[s](k)

\displaystyle\cong{\text{Hom}}_{k}({\mathcal{A}}^{n}\times{\mathcal{B}},{\mathcal{A}})\oplus{\mathcal{A}}[s](k)

\displaystyle\cong{\text{Hom}}_{k}({\mathcal{A}}^{n},{\mathcal{A}})\oplus{\text{Hom}}_{k}({\mathcal{B}},{\mathcal{A}})\oplus{\mathcal{A}}[s](k)

\displaystyle\cong(End_{k}({\mathcal{A}}))^{n}\oplus{\text{Hom}}_{k}({\mathcal{B}},{\mathcal{A}})\oplus{\mathcal{A}}[s](k).

Therefore, as
{\mathbb{Z}}
-modules, we have

{\text{rk}}({\mathcal{A}}_{a}({\mathcal{K}}))\geq n\cdot{\text{rk}}({\text{End}}_{k}({\mathcal{A}})).

The proof of the theorem

Given the integers
2\leq s\leq r\leq n
, fix a polynomial
f(x)\in k[x]
of degree
r
.
Consider the curve
{\mathcal{C}}_{s,f}:y^{s}=f(x)
with a rational point

.
It admits an order
s
automorphism
\iota:(x,y)\mapsto(x,\zeta\cdot y)
. For each
1\leq i\leq n
, let
{\mathcal{C}}_{s,f}^{(i)}

a copy of
{\mathcal{C}}_{s,f}

and
denote by
\iota_{i}
the corresponding automorphism for each of these curves.
Define
{\bf C}_{n}:=\prod_{i=1}^{n}{\mathcal{C}}_{s,f}^{(i)}
which can be expressed by the simultaneous equations

y_{i}^{s}=f(x_{i})
for

. Let

to be the order

If we assume that

Define

It is also easy to check that

contains the following

P_{1}:=(x_{1},1)\ \text{and}\ P_{i}:=(x_{i+1},z_{i}/f(x_{1}))\ \text{ for}\ (1\leq i\leq n-1).

Remark 5.1

The construction of the varieties

and
{\bf V}_{n}
generalizes that given by Yamagishi in

]
,
which is used to find elliptic curves of high rank that have a given set of algebraic numbers as

By the fact that the Albanese and Jacobian varieties of curves coincide and

{\text{Prym}}_{{\mathcal{C}}_{s,f}^{(i)}/{\mathbb{P}}^{1}}=\frac{J({\mathcal{C}}_{s,f}^{(i)})}{\text{Im}(id+\tilde{\iota}+\cdots+\tilde{\iota}^{s-1})}\sim_{k}\ker\big(id+\tilde{\iota}+\cdots+\tilde{\iota}^{s-1})^{\circ}.

By applying Proposition
2.3

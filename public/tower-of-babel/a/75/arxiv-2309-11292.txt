Multivariate Dirichlet Moments and a Polychromatic Ewens Sampling Formula
Lorenzo Dello Schiavo, Filippo Quattrocchi

Abstract
We present an elementary non-recursive formula for the multivariate moments of the Dirichlet distribution on the standard simplex, in terms of the pattern inventory of the moments’ exponents. We obtain analog formulas for the multivariate moments of the Dirichlet–Ferguson and Gamma measures. We further introduce a polychromatic analogue of Ewens sampling formula on colored integer partitions, discuss its relation with suitable extensions of Hoppe’s urn model and of the Chinese restaurant process, and prove that it satisfies an adapted notion of consistency in the sense of Kingman.

Introduction

We present extensions of some celebrated models of random integer partitions, to the case when such partitions are decorated by a subordinate specification, for simplicity described as a categorically distributed coloring.
The
fil rouge
of our presentation is an algebraic approach to the count of integer partitions, which we draw from well-known connections among the
Dirichlet distribution
,
Ewens sampling formula
(ESF),

Our starting point is the observation that univariate moments of the Dirichlet distribution are the generating functions of the (standard, ‘monocromatic’) ESF (cf. (

Here, our goal is to describe the relation between

variate moments of the Dirichlet distribution and a ‘
poly
chromatic’ ESF on colored partitions.
A systematic treatment of the arising ‘colored partition structure’, including a representation theorem in the sense of Kingman
[
17

Denote by

For

Moments of Dirichlet measures

To find useful representations for the moments of

As a first main result, we provide a simple, elementary, closed formula for all multivariate moments of

(1.1)

By ‘simple’ we mean that our formula is not further simplifiable in terms of actions of the symmetric groups

Ewens Sampling Formula

For a permutation
\pi

\mathfrak{S}_{n}

Let

on the set of integer partitions
\boldsymbol{\lambda}
of

\sum_{i}i\lambda_{i}=n

It is the probability that a
\theta
-biased permutation has given cycle structure

\lambda_{2}
transpositions,
\lambda_{3}

3
-cycles, etc.
In particular, the distribution

We refer the reader to the recent surveys

28

The proof of Theorem

The ESF in population genetics

In the seminal work
[
9
]
, W.J. Ewens introduced the formula later named after him, and showed that
E_{\theta}
is the joint probability distribution of the number of selectively neutral alleles
A_{i}^{\scriptscriptstyle{(n)}}
represented
i
times in a sample of
n
genes taken from a large
(\gg n)
population, viz.

\mathbf{P}[A^{\scriptscriptstyle{(n)}}_{1}=\lambda_{1},\dotsc,A^{\scriptscriptstyle{(n)}}_{n}=\lambda_{n}]=E_{\theta}(\boldsymbol{\lambda})\,\mathrm{,}\;\,

where the parameter
\theta>0
defines the rate
\tfrac{\theta}{\theta+n}
at which novel alleles appear.

The polychromatic analogue

to the ESF is the distribution of the very same model, when alleles are additionally marked by a ‘color’ in
[q]
.
Such a marking describes any of

(hereditary or non-hereditary) features specific to a given allele and which are not reflected by the sequence of its base pairs.
This includes, for instance,
in situ
epigenetic modifications such as DNA-methylation.

2.2.1
Some bijections of the symmetric group

Let
G
be any finite group.
For
h\in G
we denote by
\tau_{h}\colon G\to G
the conjugation map
\tau_{h}\colon g\mapsto hgh^{-1}
.
For each
\pi
in
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
and
i,j\in[n]
we write

i\mathrel{\underset{\pi}{\scalebox{1.5}[1]{$\sim$}}}j\quad\text{if}\quad j=\pi^{p}(i)\quad\text{for some }p\in\Z\,\mathrm{,}\;\,

i.e., if
i,j\in[n]
belong to the same orbit (cycle) of
\pi
.
We note that
\mathrel{\underset{\pi}{\scalebox{1.5}[1]{$\sim$}}}
is an equivalence relation on
[n]
, and that

i\mathrel{\underset{\pi}{\scalebox{1.5}[1]{$\sim$}}}j\iff\sigma(i)\mathrel{\underset{\tau_{\sigma}(\pi)}{\scalebox{2}[1]{$\sim$}}}\sigma(j)\,\mathrm{,}\;\,\qquad i,j\in[n]\,\mathrm{,}\;\,\quad\pi,\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}\,\mathrm{.}

(2.10)

Let
(B_{n},\circ)
be the group of bijections of
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
leaving conjugacy classes invariant.
That is,
g\in B_{n}
if and only if
g(\pi)
has the same cycle structure as
\pi
for every
\pi\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}
.
We have
B_{n}\cong\bigtimes_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}\vdash n}\mathfrak{S}_{M_{2}(\boldsymbol{\lambda})}
in a natural way.

We denote by
H_{n}
the subset of all
h\in B_{n}
such that

\displaystyle i\mathrel{\underset{\pi}{\scalebox{1.5}[1]{$\sim$}}}j\implies i\mathrel{\underset{h(\pi)}{\scalebox{2}[1]{$\sim$}}}j\,\mathrm{,}\;\,\qquad i,j\in[n]\,\mathrm{,}\;\,\quad\pi\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}\,\mathrm{.}

(2.11)

Let
h_{1},h_{2}\in H_{n}
.
Consecutive applications of (
2.11
) show that

i\mathrel{\underset{\pi}{\scalebox{1.5}[1]{$\sim$}}}j\implies i\mathrel{\underset{h_{1}(\pi)}{\scalebox{1.5}[1]{$\sim$}}}j\implies i\mathrel{\underset{(h_{2}\circ h_{1})(\pi)}{\scalebox{2.5}[1]{$\sim$}}}j\,\mathrm{,}\;\,\qquad i,j\in[n]\,\mathrm{,}\;\,\pi\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}\,\mathrm{.}

Thus,
H_{n}
is closed under
\circ
and therefore it is a subgroup of (the
finite
group)
B_{n}
.

In order to exemplify some elements of
H_{n}
, for
\pi,\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}
, set

\displaystyle f_{\sigma,\pi}(i)\eqdef\sigma^{p_{\sigma,\pi}(i)}(i)\,\mathrm{,}\;\,\quad p_{\sigma,\pi}(i)\eqdef\min\left\{p\geq 1:\sigma^{p}(i)\mathrel{\underset{\pi}{\scalebox{1.5}[1]{$\sim$}}}i\right\}\,\mathrm{,}\;\,\quad i\in[n]\,\mathrm{.}

(2.12)

For each
\pi\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}
it is readily verified that
f_{\sigma,\pi}\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}
is a bijection and
f_{\sigma,\pi}^{-1}=f_{\sigma^{-1},\pi}
.

Example 2.7
.

For
\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}
define
h_{\sigma}
by

h_{\sigma}\colon\pi\mapsto\tau_{f_{\sigma,\pi}}(\pi)
. Then,
h_{\sigma}\in H_{n}
and
h_{\sigma}^{-1}=h_{\sigma^{-1}}
.

Proof.

We verify that
h_{\sigma}\in H_{n}
.
Firstly, since
f_{\sigma,\pi}^{-1}=f_{\sigma^{-1},\pi}
, we have that
h_{\sigma}
is a bijection with inverse
h_{\sigma^{-1}}
.
By definition,
h_{\sigma}(\pi)
is conjugate to
\pi
for every
\pi\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}
.
Thus,
h_{\sigma}\in B_{n}
and it suffices to show (
2.11
).
Furthermore,
f_{\sigma,\pi}(i)\mathrel{\underset{\pi}{\scalebox{1.5}[1]{$\sim$}}}i
for every
i
by the definition (
2.12
) of
f_{\sigma,\pi}
.
Thus,
\pi
and
h_{\sigma}(\pi)
define the same equivalence relation, viz.

i\mathrel{\underset{\pi}{\scalebox{1.5}[1]{$\sim$}}}j\iff i\mathrel{\underset{h_{\sigma}(\pi)}{\scalebox{2.5}[1]{$\sim$}}}j\,\mathrm{,}\;\,\quad i,j\in[n]\,\mathrm{,}\;\,\qquad\pi\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}\,\mathrm{,}\;\,

(2.13)

which implies (
2.11
).
∎

Proposition 2.13
.

The orbit space
\mathfrak{S}_{{{}\mathbf{n}}_{\bullet}}/G
is (parametrized by) the set

of all

-colored partitions, and
\left\lvert G.\pi\right\rvert=M_{\mathbf{n}}(\Pi(\pi))
for every
\pi\in\mathfrak{S}_{{{}\mathbf{n}}_{\bullet}}

Proof.

For every
\pi,\pi^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{S}_{{{}\mathbf{n}}_{\bullet}}

\Pi(\pi)=\Pi(\pi^{\prime})
if and only if
\pi\in G.\pi^{\prime}
.
Let
\pi=\kappa_{1}\cdots\kappa_{r}
and
\pi^{\prime}=\kappa_{1}^{\prime}\cdots\kappa_{r^{\prime}}^{\prime}
be cycle decompositions.
If
\Pi(\pi)=\Pi(\pi^{\prime})

and, up to reordering the cycles, we may assume without loss of generality that
\Pi(\kappa_{i})=\Pi(\kappa_{i}^{\prime})
for every

Therefore, there exists

such that for every

\kappa_{i}
and

transitively permute the same set of numbers.
Equivalently,

2.2.3
Necklaces

Theorem
2.14
provides an algebraic interpretation for (
2.9
).
Let us now give a combinatorial interpretation of the same formula, i.e. of the multinomial coefficient
M_{\mathbf{n}}
, in terms of necklaces, which will in turn provide a connection to ESF via the extension of the CRP discussed in §
1
.

On the one hand, waiters in our busy restaurant take care to remember, for every table, which clients order from each menu.
The arrangement of the customers around the table is important in serving them efficiently.
All the information the waiters need about the customers’ arrangement is thus contained in a
q
-colored necklace.
On the other hand, chefs in the restaurant only care about how many customers at each table order from each menu, so that customers at the same table may be served at the same time.
All the information the chefs need about the customers’ arrangement is thus contained in a
q
-colored partition.
Let us now count
q
-colored partitions by collecting together
q
-colored necklaces with the same occurrences of each color.

For integer
q\in{\mathbb{N}}_{1}
denote by
[q]^{*}
the free monoid generated by
[q]
.
Elements of

3.1
Overview on Dirichlet measures

Moment and Laplace/Fourier-transform methods for
D_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}
and its infinite-dimensional counterpart, the Dirichlet–Ferguson measure
\mathcal{D}_{\alpha}

[
12
]
over a measure space
(X,\alpha)
are notoriously difficult, as we briefly summarize below.

Transforms

It is well-known that the Fourier transform
\widehat{D_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}}
of the Dirichlet distribution
D_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}
may be expressed in terms of
{}_{k}\Phi_{2}
, the
k
-variate
confluent hypergeometric Lauricella function of type
D

[
20
,
10
]

The power-series representation of
{}_{k}\Phi_{2}
is «
inconvenient for numerical calculations when

»
[
22
, p. 4]
.
Instead, the complex-contour-integral representation
[
7
, Eqn. (7)]
is preferred, but its treatment remains quite involved, see e.g.
[
25
]
.
In particular, differentiating
\widehat{D_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}}
in this form does not provide any useful representation for the moments of the measure.

For decades the Fourier transform
\widehat{\mathcal{D}_{\alpha}}
of
\mathcal{D}_{\alpha}
was widely considered intractable
[
14
]
, which led to the introduction of other characterizing transforms, such as the
Markov–Krein transform

[
15
]
and the

-transform

[
14
]
.
These methods too are unsatisfactory, since there is no counterpart for such transforms of foundational results available for the Fourier transform, such as, for instance, Bochner–Minlos–Sazonov’s (BMS) or Lévy’s Continuity Theorem.
The Fourier transform
\widehat{\mathcal{D}_{\alpha}}
was eventually computed in
[
4
]
by methods in combinatorics and representation theory.

Moments

Multivariate moments of
D_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}
are virtually well-known in the form (

) below, which may be regarded as an extension of the ESF.
Whereas easily computable for small

and thus provides no insight on multivariate moments of

Partially in order to overcome this issue, other expressions have been considered:

1.

the univariate moment

in terms of incomplete Bell polynomials, solely in the case

and

2.

more general univariate moments for

have implicitly appeared in
[

in iterative form;

3.

univariate moments for both

and

have appeared in full generality in
[
4
]
in terms of the cycle index polynomials

As for multivariate moments, they have appeared:

4.

in
[

in
[

[

[

The measure

Among them are: the law

\tilde{\nu}_{\mathbf{n}}[\mathbf{S};\boldsymbol{\alpha}]\eqdef\frac{\left\langle\boldsymbol{\alpha}\right\rangle_{{{}\mathbf{n}}_{\bullet}}}{\mathbf{n}!}\nu_{\mathbf{n}}[\mathbf{S};\boldsymbol{\alpha}]\qquad\text{and}\qquad\tilde{\zeta}_{\mathbf{n}}[\mathbf{S};\boldsymbol{\alpha}]\eqdef\frac{\left\langle\boldsymbol{\alpha}\right\rangle_{{{}\mathbf{n}}_{\bullet}}}{\mathbf{n}!}\zeta_{\mathbf{n}}[\mathbf{S};\boldsymbol{\alpha}]\,\mathrm{.}

We show that
\tilde{\nu}_{\mathbf{n}}[\mathbf{S};\boldsymbol{\alpha}]=\tilde{\zeta}_{\mathbf{n}}[\mathbf{S};\boldsymbol{\alpha}]
and conclude the assertion by Lemma
3.8
.

Step 1

We claim that

\displaystyle\tilde{\nu}_{\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{e}_{j}}[\mathbf{S};\boldsymbol{\alpha}+\mathbf{e}_{\ell}]=\sum_{\mathbf{e}_{j}\leq_{\diamond}\mathbf{h}\leq_{\diamond}\mathbf{n}}\mathbf{S}_{\ell}^{\mathbf{h}-\mathbf{e}_{j}}\frac{({{}\mathbf{h}}_{\bullet}-1)!}{(\mathbf{h}-\mathbf{e}_{j})!}\,\tilde{\nu}_{\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{h}}[\mathbf{S};\boldsymbol{\alpha}]\,\mathrm{,}\;\,

(3.7)

where, conventionally,

\tilde{\nu}_{\mathbf{n}}[\mathbf{S};\boldsymbol{\alpha}]=0\quad\text{whenever}\quad\mathbf{n}\not\geq^{\diamond}\zero_{q}\,\mathrm{.}

(3.8)

We argue by induction on
{{}\mathbf{n}}_{\bullet}
with trivial (i.e.
1=1
) base step for
{{}\mathbf{n}}_{\bullet}=1
.

Inductive step.
Let
\partial_{a}^{b}\eqdef\partial_{s_{a}^{b}}
, set
\mathbf{E}_{a}^{b}\eqdef[\delta_{ai}\delta_{bj}]_{i}^{j}\in\left\{0,1\right\}^{k\times q}
, and note that

\displaystyle\partial_{a}^{b}\tilde{\nu}_{\mathbf{n}}[\mathbf{S};\boldsymbol{\alpha}]=

\displaystyle\ \sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{M}\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}^{k\times q}\\
\mathsf{col}(\mathbf{M})=\mathbf{n}\end{subarray}}\left\langle\boldsymbol{\alpha}\right\rangle_{\mathsf{row}(\mathbf{M})}\partial_{a}^{b}\frac{\mathbf{S}^{\mathbf{M}}}{\mathbf{M}!}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{E}_{a}^{b}\leq_{\diamond}\mathbf{M}\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}^{k\times q}\\
\mathsf{col}(\mathbf{M})=\mathbf{n}\end{subarray}}\alpha_{a}\left\langle\boldsymbol{\alpha}+\mathbf{e}_{a}\right\rangle_{\mathsf{row}(\mathbf{M}-\mathbf{E}_{a}^{b})}\frac{\mathbf{S}^{\mathbf{M}-\mathbf{E}_{a}^{b}}}{(\mathbf{M}-\mathbf{E}_{a}^{b})!}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{M}\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}^{k\times q}\\
\mathsf{col}(\mathbf{M})=\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{e}_{b}\end{subarray}}\alpha_{a}\left\langle\boldsymbol{\alpha}+\mathbf{e}_{a}\right\rangle_{\mathsf{row}(\mathbf{M})}\frac{\mathbf{S}^{\mathbf{M}}}{\mathbf{M}!}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \alpha_{a}\tilde{\nu}_{\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{e}_{b}}[\mathbf{S};\boldsymbol{\alpha}+\mathbf{e}_{a}]\,\mathrm{.}

(3.9)

Applying the inductive hypothesis to
\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{e}_{b}
with
\boldsymbol{\alpha}+\mathbf{e}_{a}
in place of
\boldsymbol{\alpha}
, we have

\displaystyle\alpha_{a}\tilde{\nu}_{\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{e}_{j}-\mathbf{e}_{b}}[\mathbf{S};\boldsymbol{\alpha}+\mathbf{e}_{\ell}+\mathbf{e}_{a}]=

\displaystyle\ \alpha_{a}\sum_{\mathbf{e}_{j}\leq_{\diamond}\mathbf{h}\leq_{\diamond}\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{e}_{b}}\mathbf{S}_{\ell}^{\mathbf{h}-\mathbf{e}_{j}}\frac{({{}\mathbf{h}}_{\bullet}-1)!}{(\mathbf{h}-\mathbf{e}_{j})!}\,\tilde{\nu}_{\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{h}-\mathbf{e}_{b}}[\mathbf{S};\boldsymbol{\alpha}+\mathbf{e}_{a}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \alpha_{a}\sum_{\mathbf{e}_{j}\leq_{\diamond}\mathbf{h}\leq_{\diamond}\mathbf{n}}\mathbf{S}_{\ell}^{\mathbf{h}-\mathbf{e}_{j}}\frac{({{}\mathbf{h}}_{\bullet}-1)!}{(\mathbf{h}-\mathbf{e}_{j})!}\,\tilde{\nu}_{\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{h}-\mathbf{e}_{b}}[\mathbf{S};\boldsymbol{\alpha}+\mathbf{e}_{a}]\,\mathrm{,}\;\,

(3.10)

where the last equality holds by (
3.8
).

Now, let
\ell\neq a
.
Applying (
3.9
) with
\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{e}_{j}
in place of
\mathbf{n}
and
\boldsymbol{\alpha}+\mathbf{e}_{\ell}
in place of
\boldsymbol{\alpha}
,

\displaystyle\partial_{a}^{b}\tilde{\nu}_{\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{e}_{j}}[\mathbf{S};\boldsymbol{\alpha}+\mathbf{e}_{\ell}]=\alpha_{a}\tilde{\nu}_{\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{e}_{j}-\mathbf{e}_{b}}[\mathbf{S};\boldsymbol{\alpha}+\mathbf{e}_{\ell}+\mathbf{e}_{a}]\,\mathrm{.}

(3.11)

Since
\ell\neq a
, applying (
3.9
) with
\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{h}
in place of
\mathbf{n}
for every
\mathbf{h}\leq_{\diamond}\mathbf{n}
yields

\displaystyle\partial_{a}^{b}

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{\mathbf{e}_{j}\leq_{\diamond}\mathbf{h}\leq_{\diamond}\mathbf{n}}\mathbf{S}_{\ell}^{\mathbf{h}-\mathbf{e}_{j}}\frac{({{}\mathbf{h}}_{\bullet}-1)!}{(\mathbf{h}-\mathbf{e}_{j})!}\tilde{\nu}_{\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{h}}[\mathbf{S};\boldsymbol{\alpha}]\right)=

(3.12)

\displaystyle=\alpha_{a}\sum_{\mathbf{e}_{j}\leq_{\diamond}\mathbf{h}\leq_{\diamond}\mathbf{n}}\mathbf{S}_{\ell}^{\mathbf{h}-\mathbf{e}_{j}}\frac{({{}\mathbf{h}}_{\bullet}-1)!}{(\mathbf{h}-\mathbf{e}_{j})!}\,\tilde{\nu}_{\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{h}-\mathbf{e}_{b}}[\mathbf{S};\boldsymbol{\alpha}+\mathbf{e}_{a}]\,\mathrm{.}

Combining (
3.11
), (
3.12
), and (
3.10
) yields

\displaystyle\partial_{a}^{b}\left(\tilde{\nu}_{\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{e}_{j}}[\mathbf{S};\boldsymbol{\alpha}+\mathbf{e}_{\ell}]-\sum_{\mathbf{e}_{j}\leq_{\diamond}\mathbf{h}\leq_{\diamond}\mathbf{n}}\mathbf{S}_{\ell}^{\mathbf{h}-\mathbf{e}_{j}}\frac{({{}\mathbf{h}}_{\bullet}-1)!}{(\mathbf{h}-\mathbf{e}_{j})!}\tilde{\nu}_{\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{h}}[\mathbf{S};\boldsymbol{\alpha}]\right)=0\,\mathrm{.}

By arbitrariness of
a,b
we conclude that the bracketed quantity is a polynomial in the sole variables
s_{\ell}^{1},\dotsc,s_{\ell}^{q}
.
As a consequence, every monomial not in the sole variables
s_{\ell}^{1},\dotsc,s_{\ell}^{q}
cancels out by arbitrariness of the variables
\mathbf{S}
.
Thus,

\displaystyle\tilde{\nu}_{\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{e}_{j}}

\displaystyle[\mathbf{S};\boldsymbol{\alpha}+\mathbf{e}_{\ell}]-\sum_{\mathbf{e}_{j}\leq_{\diamond}\mathbf{h}\leq_{\diamond}\mathbf{n}}\mathbf{S}_{\ell}^{\mathbf{h}-\mathbf{e}_{j}}\frac{({{}\mathbf{h}}_{\bullet}-1)!}{(\mathbf{h}-\mathbf{e}_{j})!}\tilde{\nu}_{\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{h}}[\mathbf{S};\boldsymbol{\alpha}]

\displaystyle=\left\langle\alpha_{\ell}+1\right\rangle_{{{}\mathbf{n}}_{\bullet}-1}\frac{\mathbf{S}_{\ell}^{\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{e}_{j}}}{(\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{e}_{j})!}-\sum_{\mathbf{e}_{j}\leq_{\diamond}\mathbf{h}\leq_{\diamond}\mathbf{n}}\mathbf{S}_{\ell}^{\mathbf{h}-\mathbf{e}_{j}}\frac{({{}\mathbf{h}}_{\bullet}-1)!}{(\mathbf{h}-\mathbf{e}_{j})!}\left\langle\alpha_{\ell}\right\rangle_{{{}\mathbf{n}}_{\bullet}-{{}\mathbf{h}}_{\bullet}}\frac{\mathbf{S}_{\ell}^{\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{h}}}{(\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{h})!}\,\mathrm{.}

The latter quantity is proved to vanish as soon as

\displaystyle\frac{\left\langle\alpha_{\ell}+1\right\rangle_{{{}\mathbf{n}}_{\bullet}-1}}{(\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{e}_{j})!}=\sum_{\mathbf{e}_{j}\leq_{\diamond}\mathbf{h}\leq_{\diamond}\mathbf{n}}\frac{({{}\mathbf{h}}_{\bullet}-1)!}{(\mathbf{h}-\mathbf{e}_{j})!}\frac{\left\langle\alpha_{\ell}\right\rangle_{{{}\mathbf{n}}_{\bullet}-{{}\mathbf{h}}_{\bullet}}}{(\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{h})!}\,\mathrm{,}\;\,

or, equivalently, by the Chu–Vandermonde identity,

\displaystyle\frac{({{}\mathbf{n}}_{\bullet}-1)!}{(\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{e}_{j})!}\sum_{i=1}^{{{}\mathbf{n}}_{\bullet}}\frac{\left\langle\alpha_{\ell}\right\rangle_{{{}\mathbf{n}}_{\bullet}-i}}{({{}\mathbf{n}}_{\bullet}-i)!}=\sum_{i=1}^{{{}\mathbf{n}}_{\bullet}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{e}_{j}\leq_{\diamond}\mathbf{h}\leq_{\diamond}\mathbf{n}\\
{{}\mathbf{h}}_{\bullet}=i\end{subarray}}\frac{({{}\mathbf{h}}_{\bullet}-1)!}{(\mathbf{h}-\mathbf{e}_{j})!}\frac{\left\langle\alpha_{\ell}\right\rangle_{{{}\mathbf{n}}_{\bullet}-i}}{(\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{h})!}\,\mathrm{.}

The latter is implied by the equality of each of the summands, viz.

\displaystyle\frac{({{}\mathbf{n}}_{\bullet}-1)!}{(\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{e}_{j})!}\frac{1}{({{}\mathbf{n}}_{\bullet}-i)!}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}{\mathbf{e}_{j}\leq_{\diamond}\mathbf{h}\leq_{\diamond}\mathbf{n}}\\
{{}\mathbf{h}}_{\bullet}=i\end{subarray}}\frac{({{}\mathbf{h}}_{\bullet}-1)!}{(\mathbf{h}-\mathbf{e}_{j})!}\frac{1}{(\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{h})!}\,\mathrm{,}\;\,

which is in turn a consequence of Lemma
3.9
, after relabeling
\mathbf{n}
as
\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{e}_{j}
.

Step 2

We now verify that
\tilde{\nu}_{\mathbf{n}}=\tilde{\zeta}_{\mathbf{n}}
.
We argue by strong induction on
{{}\mathbf{n}}_{\bullet}
with trivial (i.e.
1=1
) base step
{{}\mathbf{n}}_{\bullet}=0
.

Inductive step.
Assume for every
\boldsymbol{\alpha}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{k}_{+}
that
\tilde{\nu}_{\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{h}}[\mathbf{S};\boldsymbol{\alpha}]=\tilde{\zeta}_{\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{h}}[\mathbf{S};\boldsymbol{\alpha}]
for every
\mathbf{h}\leq_{\diamond}\mathbf{n}
with
\mathbf{h}\neq\zero
.
Now,

\displaystyle\mathbf{n}!\,\partial_{p}^{b}\tilde{\zeta}_{\mathbf{n}}[\mathbf{S};\boldsymbol{\alpha}]=

\displaystyle\ \sum_{A\vDash\mathbf{n}}M_{\mathbf{n}}(A)\,\partial_{p}^{b}\underbrace{\prod_{\mathbf{a}\in\mathsf{supp}A}\left(\big({\mathbf{s}_{1}^{\diamond a_{1}}\diamond\cdots\diamond\mathbf{s}_{q}^{\diamond a_{q}}}\big)\cdot\boldsymbol{\alpha}\right)^{A(\mathbf{a})}}_{\eqqcolon J(\mathbf{S};\boldsymbol{\alpha};\mathbf{A})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \sum_{A\vDash\mathbf{n}}M_{\mathbf{n}}(A)\,\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{a}\in\mathsf{supp}A\\
\mathbf{a}\geq_{\diamond}\mathbf{e}_{b}\end{subarray}}\frac{A(\mathbf{a})\,a_{b}\,\alpha_{p}\,\mathbf{S}_{p}^{\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{e}_{b}}}{\big({\mathbf{s}_{1}^{\diamond a_{1}}\diamond\cdots\diamond\mathbf{s}_{q}^{\diamond a_{q}}}\big)\cdot\boldsymbol{\alpha}}\,J(\mathbf{S};\boldsymbol{\alpha};A)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \alpha_{p}\sum_{\mathbf{e}_{b}\leq_{\diamond}\mathbf{h}\leq_{\diamond}\mathbf{n}}\sum_{A\vDash\mathbf{n}}M_{\mathbf{n}}(A)\frac{A(\mathbf{h})\,h_{b}\,\mathbf{S}_{p}^{\mathbf{h}-\mathbf{e}_{b}}}{\big({\mathbf{s}_{1}^{\diamond h_{1}}\diamond\cdots\diamond\mathbf{s}_{q}^{\diamond h_{q}}}\big)\cdot\boldsymbol{\alpha}}\,J(\mathbf{S};\boldsymbol{\alpha};A)\,\mathrm{.}

(3.13)

For each
\mathbf{h}\leq_{\diamond}\mathbf{n}
and each
A\vDash\mathbf{n}
with
A\geq\car_{\mathbf{h}}
, set
C\eqdef A-\car_{\mathbf{h}}
.
Note that

\displaystyle M_{\mathbf{n}}(A)=

\displaystyle\ \mathbf{n}!\prod_{\mathbf{a}\in\mathsf{supp}A}\frac{\binom{{{}\mathbf{a}}_{\bullet}}{\mathbf{a}}^{A(\mathbf{a})}}{{{}\mathbf{a}}_{\bullet}^{A(\mathbf{a})}A(\mathbf{a})!}=\frac{\mathbf{n}!\,{{}\mathbf{h}}_{\bullet}!}{{{}\mathbf{h}}_{\bullet}\,\mathbf{h}!\,A(\mathbf{h})}\prod_{\mathbf{a}\in\mathsf{supp}C}\frac{\binom{{{}\mathbf{a}}_{\bullet}}{\mathbf{a}}^{C(\mathbf{a})}}{{{}\mathbf{a}}_{\bullet}^{C(\mathbf{a})}C(\mathbf{a})!}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \frac{M_{\mathbf{n}}(C)}{A(\mathbf{h})}\frac{({{}\mathbf{h}}_{\bullet}-1)!}{\mathbf{h}!}\frac{\mathbf{n}!}{(\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{h})!}

(3.14)

and

\displaystyle J(\mathbf{S};\boldsymbol{\alpha};A)=

\displaystyle\ J(\mathbf{S};\boldsymbol{\alpha};C)\,\big({\mathbf{s}_{1}^{\diamond h_{1}}\diamond\cdots\diamond\mathbf{s}_{q}^{\diamond h_{q}}}\big)\cdot\boldsymbol{\alpha}\,\mathrm{.}

(3.15)

Substituting (
3.14
) and (
3.15
) in (
3.13
) above, and simplifying
\mathbf{n}!
,

\displaystyle\partial_{p}^{b}\,\tilde{\zeta}_{\mathbf{n}}[\mathbf{S};\boldsymbol{\alpha}]=

\displaystyle\ \alpha_{p}\sum_{\mathbf{e}_{b}\leq_{\diamond}\mathbf{h}\leq_{\diamond}\mathbf{n}}\sum_{C\vDash\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{h}}\frac{M_{\mathbf{n}}(C)}{(\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{h})!}\frac{({{}\mathbf{h}}_{\bullet}-1)!}{(\mathbf{h}-\mathbf{e}_{b})!}\ \mathbf{S}_{p}^{\mathbf{h}-\mathbf{e}_{b}}\,J(\mathbf{S};\boldsymbol{\alpha};C)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \alpha_{p}\sum_{\mathbf{e}_{b}\leq_{\diamond}\mathbf{h}\leq_{\diamond}\mathbf{n}}\frac{({{}\mathbf{h}}_{\bullet}-1)!}{(\mathbf{h}-\mathbf{e}_{b})!}\ \mathbf{S}_{p}^{\mathbf{h}-\mathbf{e}_{b}}\,\tilde{\zeta}_{\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{h}}[\mathbf{S};\boldsymbol{\alpha}]\,\mathrm{.}

Combining the inductive hypothesis with (
3.7
) and (
3.9
) with
a=\ell
and
b=j
,

\displaystyle\partial_{p}^{b}\,\tilde{\zeta}_{\mathbf{n}}[\mathbf{S};\boldsymbol{\alpha}]=

\displaystyle\ \alpha_{p}\sum_{\mathbf{e}_{b}\leq_{\diamond}\mathbf{h}\leq_{\diamond}\mathbf{n}}\frac{({{}\mathbf{h}}_{\bullet}-1)!}{(\mathbf{h}-\mathbf{e}_{b})!}\ \mathbf{S}_{p}^{\mathbf{h}-\mathbf{e}_{b}}\,\tilde{\nu}_{\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{h}}[\mathbf{S};\boldsymbol{\alpha}]=\partial_{p}^{b}\,\tilde{\nu}_{\mathbf{n}}[\mathbf{S};\boldsymbol{\alpha}]\,\mathrm{.}

By arbitrariness of
p
and
b
we conclude that
\tilde{\zeta}_{\mathbf{n}}[\mathbf{S};\boldsymbol{\alpha}]-\tilde{\nu}_{\mathbf{n}}[\mathbf{S};\boldsymbol{\alpha}]
is constant as a function of
\mathbf{S}
, hence vanishing by choosing
\mathbf{S}=\zero
.
∎

4
A Polychromatic ESF

Let
r
be the number of cycles of a random permutation
\pi\in\mathfrak{S}_{{{}\mathbf{n}}_{\bullet}}
.
Assume that
\pi
is chosen with a probability proportional to
\theta^{r}
for some
\theta>0
.
Then, the probability that
\pi
has cycle structure
\boldsymbol{\lambda}\vdash n
is precisely the Ewens distribution
E_{\theta}(\boldsymbol{\lambda})
.
We provide a generalization of this statement to the case of colored permutations, with coloring and cycle structure indexed by a
q
-colored partition.

Let

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{n}\eqdef\bigcup_{\mathbf{n}\in{\mathbb{N}}^{q}_{*}:{{}\mathbf{n}}_{\bullet}=n}\mathcal{A}_{\mathbf{n}}

(4.1)

be the family of all multisets
A
on
{\mathbb{N}}^{q}_{*}
with
\mathsf{shape}(A)\vdash n
.

Definition 4.1

(Polychromatic ESF)
.

Fix
n,q\in{\mathbb{N}}_{1}
,
\theta>0
, and
\mathbf{p}\in\Delta^{q-1}
.
The
polychromatic ESF

E_{\theta,\mathbf{p}}^{n}
is the probability distribution on
\mathcal{A}_{n}
given by

\begin{aligned} E_{\theta,\mathbf{p}}^{n}(A)\eqdef\frac{n!}{\left\langle\theta\right\rangle_{n}}\,\theta^{{\mathsf{card}(A)}}\,\frac{\mathbf{p}^{\mathsf{col}(A)}}{\mathsf{col}(A)!}M_{\mathsf{col}(A)}(A)\end{aligned}\,\mathrm{,}\;\,\qquad A\in\mathcal{A}_{n}\,\mathrm{.}

(4.2)

Proof.

Let us verify that
E_{\theta,\mathbf{p}}^{n}
is indeed a probability distribution on
\mathcal{A}_{n}
.
For fixed
k>n
set
\mathbf{s}_{j}\eqdef p_{j}{\mathbf{1}}^{\scriptscriptstyle{(k)}}
,
j\in[q]
, and
\boldsymbol{\alpha}\eqdef(\theta/k){\mathbf{1}}^{\scriptscriptstyle{(k)}}
.
Respectively by: the Multinomial Theorem, Theorem
3.1
, and the definition (
2.7
) of
Z_{\mathbf{n}}
,

\displaystyle 1=

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathbf{n}\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}^{q}:{{}\mathbf{n}}_{\bullet}=n}\binom{n}{\mathbf{n}}\mathbf{p}^{\mathbf{n}}=\sum_{\mathbf{n}\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}^{q}:{{}\mathbf{n}}_{\bullet}=n}\binom{n}{\mathbf{n}}\int_{\Delta^{k-1}}\prod_{j}^{q}(\mathbf{s}_{j}\cdot\mathbf{x})^{n_{j}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}D_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}(\mathbf{x})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathbf{n}\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}^{q}:{{}\mathbf{n}}_{\bullet}=n}\binom{n}{\mathbf{n}}\frac{\mathbf{n}!}{\left\langle\theta\right\rangle_{n}}\frac{1}{\mathbf{n}!}\sum_{A\vDash\mathbf{n}}M_{\mathbf{n}}(A)\prod_{\mathbf{a}\in\mathsf{supp}A}(\theta\,\mathbf{p}^{\mathbf{a}})^{A(\mathbf{a})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{A\in\mathcal{A}_{n}}\frac{\theta^{{\mathsf{card}(A)}}}{\left\langle\theta\right\rangle_{n}}\binom{n}{\mathsf{col}(A)}\mathbf{p}^{\mathsf{col}(A)}M_{\mathsf{col}(A)}(A)\,\mathrm{.}\qed

Remark 4.2

(
q=1
)
.

When
q=1
, we have
\mathbf{p}=p=1
and
\mathsf{col}(A)=n
for every
A\in\mathcal{A}_{n}
, thus (
4.2
) reduces to the standard ESF by Remark
2.4
.

Lemma 4.3

(Conditioning)
.

Fix
\mathbf{n}\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}^{q}
with
{{}\mathbf{n}}_{\bullet}=n
. Then, the conditional probability
E_{\theta,\mathbf{p}}^{n}\left[{\,\cdot\,}|\mathsf{col}({\,\cdot\,})=\mathbf{n}\right]
satisfies

E_{\theta,\mathbf{p}}^{n}\left[{\,\cdot\,}|\mathsf{col}({\,\cdot\,})=\mathbf{n}\right]=\frac{\theta^{{\mathsf{card}(A)}}}{\left\langle\theta\right\rangle_{n}}M_{\mathbf{n}}(A)\,\mathrm{,}\;\,\qquad A\in\mathcal{A}_{\mathbf{n}}\,\mathrm{.}

(4.3)

Proof.

For fixed
k>n
set
\mathbf{s}_{1}=\cdots=\mathbf{s}_{q}\eqdef{\mathbf{1}}^{\scriptscriptstyle{(k)}}
, and
\boldsymbol{\alpha}\eqdef(\theta/k){\mathbf{1}}^{\scriptscriptstyle{(k)}}
.
By Theorem
3.1
and by the definition (
2.7
) of
Z_{\mathbf{n}}
,

\displaystyle 1=\int_{\Delta^{k-1}}\prod_{j}^{q}(\mathbf{s}_{j}\cdot\mathbf{x})^{n_{j}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}D_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}=\frac{\mathbf{n}!}{\left\langle\theta\right\rangle_{n}}\frac{1}{\mathbf{n}!}\sum_{A\vDash\mathbf{n}}M_{\mathbf{n}}(A)\prod_{\mathbf{a}\in\mathsf{supp}A}\theta^{A(\mathbf{a})}\,\mathrm{,}\;\,

hence

\sum_{A\vDash\mathbf{n}}\theta^{{\mathsf{card}(A)}}M_{\mathbf{n}}(A)=\left\langle\theta\right\rangle_{n}\,\mathrm{.}

(4.4)

Now,

E_{\theta,\mathbf{p}}^{n}\left[A|\mathsf{col}(A)=\mathbf{n}\right]=\frac{E_{\theta,\mathbf{p}}^{n}(A)}{E_{\theta,\mathbf{p}}^{\mathbf{n}}\left[\mathsf{col}({\,\cdot\,})=\mathbf{n}\right]}\quad\text{if}\quad\mathsf{col}(A)=\mathbf{n}

(4.5)

and
0
otherwise.
Furthermore,

\displaystyle E_{\theta,\mathbf{p}}^{n}\left[\mathsf{col}({\,\cdot\,})=\mathbf{n}\right]=

\displaystyle\sum_{A\vDash\mathbf{n}}E_{\theta,\mathbf{p}}^{\mathbf{n}}(A)=\sum_{A\vDash\mathbf{n}}\frac{n!}{\left\langle\theta\right\rangle_{n}}\,\theta^{{\mathsf{card}(A)}}\,\frac{\mathbf{p}^{\mathsf{col}(A)}}{\mathsf{col}(A)!}M_{\mathsf{col}(A)}(A)

(4.6)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{n!}{\left\langle\theta\right\rangle_{n}}\frac{\mathbf{p}^{\mathbf{n}}}{\mathbf{n}!}\sum_{A\vDash\mathbf{n}}\theta^{{\mathsf{card}(A)}}M_{\mathsf{col}(A)}(A)=n!\frac{\mathbf{p}^{\mathbf{n}}}{\mathbf{n}!}

by (
4.4
).
Combining (
4.5
), (
4.6
), and (
4.2
) thus yields

E_{\theta,\mathbf{p}}^{n}\left[A|\mathsf{col}(A)=\mathbf{n}\right]=\frac{\theta^{{\mathsf{card}(A)}}}{\left\langle\theta\right\rangle_{n}}M_{\mathbf{n}}(A)\quad\text{if}\quad\mathsf{col}(A)=\mathbf{n}

and
0
otherwise.
∎

Since
E_{\theta,\mathbf{p}}^{n}\left[{\,\cdot\,}|\mathsf{col}({\,\cdot\,})=\mathbf{n}\right]
does not depend on
\mathbf{p}
, let us set

E_{\theta}^{\mathbf{n}}\eqdef E_{\theta,\mathbf{p}}^{n}\left[{\,\cdot\,}|\mathsf{col}({\,\cdot\,})=\mathbf{n}\right]\quad\text{on}\quad\mathcal{A}_{\mathbf{n}}\,\mathrm{.}

In analogy with the standard ESF, the conditional probability
E_{\theta}^{\mathbf{n}}
counts
\theta
-biased
q
-colored permutations, as we now show.

Proposition 4.4
.

Fix
\theta>0
and let
\pi\in\mathfrak{S}_{{{}\mathbf{n}}_{\bullet}}
be a
\theta
-biased random permutation. Then,

\mathbf{P}\big[\Pi(\pi)=A\big]=E_{\theta}^{\mathbf{n}}(A)\,\mathrm{,}\;\,\qquad A\in\mathcal{A}_{\mathbf{n}}\,\mathrm{.}

(4.7)

Proof.

Let
r
be the number of cycles of
\pi
including fixed points. Since
\pi
is
\theta
-biased and applying Proposition
2.13
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\big[\Pi(\pi)=A\big]=C_{\theta}\,\theta^{r}\left\lvert\Pi^{-1}(A)\right\rvert=C_{\theta}\,\theta^{r}M_{\mathsf{col}(A)}(A)\,\mathrm{.}

The conclusion follows since
E_{\theta}^{\mathbf{n}}
is a probability measure by Lemma
4.3
.
∎

Remark 4.5
.

We can rephrase Proposition
4.4
by saying that
E_{\theta}^{\mathbf{n}}
is the push-forward via
\Pi
of the law
\mathbf{P}
of a
\theta
-biased random permutation in
\mathfrak{S}_{{{}\mathbf{n}}_{\bullet}}
.
Furthermore, as a consequence of Lemma
4.3
and Corollary
3.3
, we see that

E_{\theta}(\boldsymbol{\lambda})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}A\vDash\mathbf{n}\\
\mathsf{shape}{A}=\boldsymbol{\lambda}\end{subarray}}E_{\theta}^{\mathbf{n}}(A)\,\mathrm{,}\;\,\qquad\boldsymbol{\lambda}\vdash n\,\mathrm{.}

(4.8)

That is,
E_{\theta}
is the push-forward of
E_{\theta}^{\mathbf{n}}
via the function
\mathsf{shape}
.
In this sense, the newly defined measure
E_{\theta}^{\mathbf{n}}
can be seen as ‘intermediate’ between
\mathbf{P}
and
E_{\theta}
.

Finally, let us collect here the main properties of
E_{\theta,\mathbf{p}}^{n}
with respect to manipulations of
\mathbf{p}
.
For each set partition
\mathbf{L}\eqdef\left\{L_{1},\dotsc,L_{r}\right\}\vdash[q]
denote by
s_{\mathbf{L}}\colon[q]\to[r]
the
\mathbf{L}
-
degeneracy map
defined by
s_{\mathbf{L}}^{-1}(k)=L_{k}
for
k\in[r]
.
Further let
\mathbf{S}_{\mathbf{L}}\in\left\{0,1\right\}^{r\times q}
be the matrix
[\mathbf{S}_{\mathbf{L}}]_{i}^{j}\eqdef\car_{j\in s_{\mathbf{L}}^{-1}(i)}
and note that
\mathbf{S}_{\mathbf{L}}\colon{\mathbb{N}}^{q}_{*}\to{\mathbb{N}}^{r}_{*}
and
\mathbf{S}_{\mathbf{L}}\colon\Delta^{q-1}\to\Delta^{r-1}
.

Arguing similarly as in the proof of Definition
4.1
, choosing
\mathbf{s}_{j}=\mathbf{s}_{j^{\prime}}
in (
3.2
) whenever
j,j^{\prime}\in L_{i}
for some
i
, we have the following.

Proposition 4.6

(Aggregation)
.

Let
n,q\in{\mathbb{N}}_{1}
,
\theta>0
, and
\mathbf{p}\in\Delta^{q-1}
. Then, cf. (
2.1
),

{(\mathbf{S}_{\mathbf{L}})_{*}}_{\sharp}E_{\theta,\mathbf{p}}^{n}=E_{\theta,\mathbf{S}_{\mathbf{L}}\mathbf{p}}^{n}\,\mathrm{,}\;\,\qquad\mathbf{L}\vdash[q]\,\mathrm{.}

4.1
A Hoppe-type urn model

In
[
13
]
, F. M. Hoppe showed that the ESF
E_{\theta}
is the marginal distribution of a discrete-time Markov process
\left(\Pi_{t}\right)_{t}
of integer partitions
\Pi_{t}\vdash t
obtained from the sampling process
\left(X_{t}\right)_{t}
of what is now known as
Hoppe’s urn model
.
We adapt his construction to a similar urn model, resulting in a Markov process with values in the space of colored integer partitions and with marginal distribution
E_{\theta,\mathbf{p}}^{t}
at time
t
.

Denote by
\mathrm{Cat}_{\mathbf{p}}
the categorical distribution on
[q]
with parameters
\mathbf{p}\in\Delta^{q-1}
.

Consider a process
Y_{\circ}\eqdef\left(Y_{t}\right)_{t}
generated by sampling from an urn containing one cube and various numbers of labelled colored balls.
At time
0
, the urn contains only the cube.
At every (integer) time
t
, the labels are consecutive and ranging in
{\mathbb{N}}_{1}
, while the colors range in
[q]
.
The cube has mass
\theta
and every ball has mass
1
.
At time
t
, an object in the urn is selected at random with a probability proportional to its mass.
If it is a ball, it is returned together with one additional ball of the same label and of a color chosen according to
\mathrm{Cat}_{\mathbf{p}}
independently of the label.
If it is the cube, it is returned together with a ball with the smallest label previously not present in the urn and of a color chosen according to
\mathrm{Cat}_{\mathbf{p}}
.
We define random variables
r_{t}\in{\mathbb{N}}_{1}
and
Y_{t}\in{\mathbb{N}}_{1}\times[q]
as the number of distinct labels (i.e. the maximal label) present in the urn, and the label and color of the additional ball returned after the
t^{\text{th}}
drawing.
Observe that, for every
T\in{\mathbb{N}}_{1}
, the process
Y_{\circ}
defines a random
q
-colored partition
\mathscr{A}_{T}
by letting

\displaystyle\mathbf{a}_{T}(i)\eqdef\left(a_{T,1}(i),\dotsc,a_{T,q}(i)\right)\,\mathrm{,}\;\,a_{T,j}(i)\eqdef\left\lvert\left\{t\in[T]:Y_{t}=(i,j)\right\}\right\rvert\,\mathrm{,}\;\,\qquad\mathscr{A}_{T}\eqdef\sum_{i}^{r_{T}}\car_{\mathbf{a}_{T}(i)}\,\mathrm{.}

(4.9)

As a consequence, in the notation of
[
13
]
, the first component
Y_{t,1}
of
Y_{t}
satisfies
Y_{t,1}=X_{t}
, while
\mathsf{shape}(\mathscr{A}_{T})
coincides with
\Pi_{T}
.
We call the Markov process
Y_{\circ}
the
polychromatic Hoppe urn
(PHU), and the process
\mathscr{A}_{\circ}\eqdef\left(\mathscr{A}_{T}\right)_{T}
the
PHU-partition process
.

Proposition 4.7
.

\mathscr{A}_{\circ}
is a Markov process with marginal distribution

\mathbf{P}[\mathscr{A}_{T}=A]=E_{\theta,\mathbf{p}}^{T}(A)\,\mathrm{,}\;\,\qquad A\in\mathcal{A}_{T}\,\mathrm{.}

(4.10)

Proof.

The Markov property is trivially satisfied.
With the notation of (
4.9
), the random variables
\left({{}\mathbf{a}_{T}(i)}_{\bullet}\right)_{i}
are
\left(Y_{t,1}\right)_{t\leq T}
-measurable.
In order to compute the marginal distribution at time
T
, fix
A\in\mathcal{A}_{T}
, and set
\boldsymbol{\lambda}\eqdef\mathsf{shape}(A)
and
r\eqdef{{}\boldsymbol{\lambda}}_{\bullet}
.

We introduce two families of functions:

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}\coloneqq\big\{\mathbf{f}:[r]\to\mathsf{supp}(A)\,\colon\,\left\lvert\mathbf{f}^{-1}(\mathbf{a})\right\rvert=A(\mathbf{a})\,\mathrm{,}\;\,\quad\mathbf{a}\in\mathsf{supp}(A)\big\}\,\mathrm{,}\;\,

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}\coloneqq\big\{g={{}({\,\cdot\,})}_{\bullet}\circ\mathbf{f}={{}\mathbf{f}({\,\cdot\,})}_{\bullet}\,\colon\,\mathbf{f}\in\mathcal{F}\big\}\,\mathrm{.}

Since the colors
Y_{t,2}
are chosen independently of one another and of the labels
Y_{t,1}
,

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}

\displaystyle\big[\mathscr{A}_{T}=A\big|\left(Y_{t,1}\right)_{t\leq T}\big]=

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mathbf{f}\in\mathcal{F}}\mathbf{P}\big[\mathbf{f}({\,\cdot\,})=\mathbf{a}_{T}({\,\cdot\,})\big|\left(Y_{t,1}\right)_{t\leq T}\big]=\sum_{\mathbf{f}\in\mathcal{F}}\prod_{i=1}^{r}\mathbf{P}\big[\mathbf{f}(i)=\mathbf{a}_{T}(i)\big|\left(Y_{t,1}\right)_{t\leq T}\big]

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mathbf{f}\in\mathcal{F}}\prod_{i=1}^{r}\binom{{{}\mathbf{f}(i)}_{\bullet}}{\mathbf{f}(i)}\mathbf{p}^{\mathbf{f}(i)}\car_{\left\{{{}\mathbf{f}(i)}_{\bullet}={{}\mathbf{a}_{T}(i)}_{\bullet}\right\}}=\sum_{\mathbf{f}\in\mathcal{F}}\mathbf{p}^{\mathsf{col}(A)}\car_{\left\{{{}\mathbf{f}({\,\cdot\,})}_{\bullet}={{}\mathbf{a}_{T}({\,\cdot\,})}_{\bullet}\right\}}\prod_{\mathbf{a}\in\mathsf{supp}(A)}\binom{{{}\mathbf{a}}_{\bullet}}{\mathbf{a}}^{A(\mathbf{a})}

\displaystyle=\left\lvert\left\{\mathbf{f}\in\mathcal{F}\,\colon\,{{}\mathbf{f}({\,\cdot\,})}_{\bullet}={{}\mathbf{a}_{T}({\,\cdot\,})}_{\bullet}\right\}\right\rvert\mathbf{p}^{\mathsf{col}(A)}\prod_{\mathbf{a}\in\mathsf{supp}(A)}\binom{{{}\mathbf{a}}_{\bullet}}{\mathbf{a}}^{A(\mathbf{a})}\,\mathrm{.}

It can be easily checked that for every
g\in\mathcal{G}
the following identities hold:

\displaystyle\left\lvert\left\{\mathbf{f}\in\mathcal{F}:g={{}({\,\cdot\,})}_{\bullet}\circ\mathbf{f}\right\}\right\rvert=\prod_{i}\binom{\lambda_{i}}{\left(A(\mathbf{a})\right)_{\mathbf{a}\in\mathsf{supp}(A)\,:\,{{}\mathbf{a}}_{\bullet}=i}}=\frac{\boldsymbol{\lambda}!}{\displaystyle\prod_{\mathbf{a}\in\mathsf{supp}(A)}A(\mathbf{a})!}\,\mathrm{.}

Thus,

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\big[\mathscr{A}_{T}=A\big|\left(Y_{t,1}\right)_{t\leq T}\big]

\displaystyle=\left\lvert\left\{g\in\mathcal{G}:g({\,\cdot\,})={{}\mathbf{a}_{T}({\,\cdot\,})}_{\bullet}\right\}\right\rvert\boldsymbol{\lambda}!\,\mathbf{p}^{\mathsf{col}(A)}\prod_{\mathbf{a}\in\mathsf{supp}(A)}\frac{\binom{{{}\mathbf{a}}_{\bullet}}{\mathbf{a}}^{A(\mathbf{a})}}{A(\mathbf{a})!}

\displaystyle=\car_{\left\{\mathsf{shape}(\mathscr{A}_{T})=\boldsymbol{\lambda}\right\}}\boldsymbol{\lambda}!\,\mathbf{p}^{\mathsf{col}(A)}\prod_{\mathbf{a}\in\mathsf{supp}(A)}\frac{\binom{{{}\mathbf{a}}_{\bullet}}{\mathbf{a}}^{A(\mathbf{a})}}{A(\mathbf{a})!}\,\mathrm{.}

(4.11)

Taking the expectation over
\left(Y_{t}\right)_{t\leq T}
on both sides of (
4.11
), we infer that

\mathbf{P}[\mathscr{A}_{T}=A]=\mathbf{P}[\mathsf{shape}(\mathscr{A}_{T})=\boldsymbol{\lambda}]\,\boldsymbol{\lambda}!\,\mathbf{p}^{\mathsf{col}(A)}\prod_{\mathbf{a}\in\mathsf{supp}(A)}\frac{\binom{{{}\mathbf{a}}_{\bullet}}{\mathbf{a}}^{A(\mathbf{a})}}{A(\mathbf{a})!}\,\mathrm{.}

(4.12)

By the formula for the marginal distribution of Hoppe’s urn model,
[
13
, Eqn. (1)]
,

\mathbf{P}[\mathsf{shape}(\mathscr{A}_{T})=\boldsymbol{\lambda}]=\frac{T!}{\left\langle\theta\right\rangle_{T}}\prod_{i=1}^{T}\frac{\theta^{\lambda_{i}}}{i^{\lambda_{i}}\lambda_{i}!}=\frac{T!}{\boldsymbol{\lambda}!}\frac{\theta^{r}}{\left\langle\theta\right\rangle_{T}}\prod_{\mathbf{a}\in\mathsf{supp}A}\frac{1}{{{}\mathbf{a}}_{\bullet}^{A(\mathbf{a})}}\,\mathrm{.}

(4.13)

Combining (
4.12
) and (
4.13
), the identity (
4.10
) follows.
∎

4.2
Consistency

In
[
16
,
17
]
, J.F.C. Kingman introduced a celebrated notion of
consistency
for stochastic processes on partitions, and showed that a sequence of random partitions
\left(\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{n}\right)_{n}
with
\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{n}\vdash n
distributed according to
E_{\theta}
, satisfies this notion.
Precisely, if
n
objects are partitioned into classes with sizes given by
\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{n}
, and one object is deleted uniformly at random, independently of
\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{n}
, the partition of the
n-1
remaining objects has class sizes distributed as
\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{n-1}
, cf. e.g.
[
23
, p. 146]
.

In this section, we show that the polychromatic ESF satisfies a similar consistency property.
Denote by
\mathcal{A}\eqdef\bigcup_{n}\mathcal{A}_{n}
the family of all finite multisets on
{\mathbb{N}}^{q}_{*}
, and set

A_{\setminus\mathbf{a},j}\eqdef\begin{cases}A-\car_{\mathbf{a}}&\text{if }\mathbf{a}=\mathbf{e}_{j}\,\mathrm{,}\\
A-\car_{\mathbf{a}}+\car_{\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{e}_{j}}&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}\,\mathrm{,}\;\,\qquad\mathbf{a}\in\mathsf{supp}A\,\mathrm{,}\;\,j\in[q]\,\mathrm{.}

Following
[
17
]
, we define a system
S=S_{nm}
,
n\in{\mathbb{N}}_{1}
,
m\leq n
, of probability kernels on
\mathcal{A}
.
Firstly, set

\displaystyle S(A,B)\eqdef

\displaystyle\ \car_{A=B}\,\mathrm{,}\;\,

\displaystyle A,B

\displaystyle\in\mathcal{A}_{n}\,\mathrm{,}\;\,

(4.14a)

\displaystyle S(A,B)\eqdef

\displaystyle\ \begin{cases}\frac{a_{j}A(\mathbf{a})}{n}&\text{if }B=A_{\setminus\mathbf{a},j}\,\mathrm{,}\\
0&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}\,\mathrm{,}\;\,

\displaystyle A\in\mathcal{A}_{n}\,\mathrm{,}\;\,

\displaystyle B\in\mathcal{A}_{n-1}\,\mathrm{,}\;\,

(4.14b)

and note that
S(A,{\,\cdot\,})
is a probability on
\mathcal{A}_{n-1}
for every
A\in\mathcal{A}_{n}
.
Secondly, let
S
be the unique system of kernels extending (
4.14
) and satisfying the cocycle relation

S(A,C)=\sum_{B\in\mathcal{A}_{m}}S(A,B)\,S(B,C)\,\mathrm{,}\;\,\qquad A\in\mathcal{A}_{n}\,\mathrm{,}\;\,C\in\mathcal{A}_{\ell}\,\mathrm{,}\;\,\quad\ell<m<n\,\mathrm{.}

(4.15)

Note that
S_{nm}(A,{\,\cdot\,})
is a probability on
\mathcal{A}_{m}
for every
m
and every
A\in\mathcal{A}_{n}
, since it is so for
m=n-1
as noted above, and in light of (
4.15
).

Remark 4.8
.

Analogously to the case of usual integer partitions, the system
S
may be interpreted as the selection of a random sampling (uniform, without replacement) of
m
elements from a given
q
-colored partition
A\in\mathcal{A}_{n}
, resulting in the
q
-colored partition
B\in\mathcal{A}_{m}
.
The cocycle relation (
4.15
) is then a consequence of the consistency of random sub-sampling.

Let us now turn to probability measures on
\mathcal{A}
.
For
n\in{\mathbb{N}}_{1}
let
\mathscr{P}(\mathcal{A}_{n})
be the set of all probability measures on
\mathcal{A}_{n}
.
Define a system
\sigma
of maps
\sigma_{nm}\colon\mathscr{P}(\mathcal{A}_{n})\to\mathscr{P}(\mathcal{A}_{m})
by

\big({\sigma_{nm}\mathbf{P}}\big)(B)\longmapsto\mathbf{P}[S({\,\cdot\,},B)]\,\mathrm{,}\;\,

and note that
\sigma
satisfies the cocycle relation

\sigma_{n\ell}=\sigma_{m\ell}\circ\sigma_{nm}\,\mathrm{,}\;\,\qquad\ell<m<n\,\mathrm{.}

(4.16)

Definition 4.9

(Consistency)
.

We say that a family
\left(\mathbf{P}_{n}\right)_{n}
of probability measures
\mathbf{P}_{n}
on
\mathcal{A}_{n}
is
consistent
(w.r.t. the system
\sigma
) if
\mathbf{P}_{m}=\sigma_{nm}\mathbf{P}_{n}
for every
m\leq n
.

Theorem 4.10
.

For every
\theta>0
and
\mathbf{p}\in\Delta^{q-1}
the family
{\big(E_{\theta,\mathbf{p}}^{n}\big)}_{n}
is consistent.

Proof.

In light of (
4.16
), it suffices to verify that
\sigma_{nm}E_{\theta,\mathbf{p}}^{n}=E_{\theta,\mathbf{p}}^{m}
for
m=n-1
and for every
n
.
To this end, let
\mathbf{Q}
be the law of the PHU partition
\mathscr{A}_{\circ}
on its path space.
By Bayes formula, and Proposition
4.7
,

\displaystyle\mathbf{Q}[\mathscr{A}_{n-1}=B\mid\mathscr{A}_{n}=A]=

\displaystyle\ \frac{\mathbf{Q}[\mathscr{A}_{n}=A\mid\mathscr{A}_{n-1}=B]\,\mathbf{Q}[\mathscr{A}_{n-1}=B]}{\mathbf{Q}[\mathscr{A}_{n}=A]}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \frac{\mathbf{Q}[\mathscr{A}_{n}=A\mid\mathscr{A}_{n-1}=B]\,E_{\theta,\mathbf{p}}^{n-1}(B)}{E_{\theta,\mathbf{p}}^{n}(A)}\,\mathrm{.}

(4.17)

Furthermore, it follows from the definition of
\mathscr{A}_{\bullet}
that

\displaystyle\mathbf{Q}[\mathscr{A}_{n}=A\mid\mathscr{A}_{n-1}=B]=

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathbf{a}\in\mathsf{supp}A}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\in[q]:\\
\mathbf{e}_{j}\leq_{\diamond}\mathbf{a},\mathbf{e}_{j}\neq\mathbf{a}\end{subarray}}\car_{A=B+\car_{\mathbf{a}}-\car_{\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{e}_{j}}}\frac{{{}\mathbf{a}}_{\bullet}-1}{\theta+n-1}B(\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{e}_{j})\,p_{j}

(4.18)

\displaystyle+\sum_{j=1}^{q}\car_{A=B+\car_{\mathbf{e}_{j}}}\frac{\theta}{\theta+n-1}\,p_{j}\,\mathrm{.}

On the other hand, by definition (
4.2
) of
E_{\theta,\mathbf{p}}^{n}
,

\displaystyle\frac{E_{\theta,\mathbf{p}}^{n-1}(B)}{E_{\theta,\mathbf{p}}^{n}(A)}=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{\theta+n-1}{np_{j}}\frac{a_{j}}{{{}\mathbf{a}}_{\bullet}-1}\frac{A(\mathbf{a})}{A(\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{e}_{j})+1}&\text{if }A=B+\car_{\mathbf{a}}-\car_{\mathbf{e}_{j}}\\
\displaystyle\frac{\theta+n-1}{\theta np_{j}}A(\mathbf{e}_{j})&\text{if }A=B+\car_{\mathbf{e}_{j}}\end{cases}

(4.19)

Combining (
4.17
)–(
4.19
), we thus have

\displaystyle\mathbf{Q}[\mathscr{A}_{n-1}=B\mid\mathscr{A}_{n}=A]=

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathbf{a}\in\mathsf{supp}A}\sum_{j\in[q]:\mathbf{e}_{j}<\mathbf{a}}\car_{A=B+\car_{\mathbf{a}}-\car_{\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{e}_{j}}}\frac{a_{j}A(\mathbf{a})}{n}+\sum_{j=1}^{q}\car_{A=B+\car_{\mathbf{e}_{j}}}\frac{A(\mathbf{e}_{j})}{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathbf{a}\in\mathsf{supp}A}\sum_{j}^{q}\car_{A_{\setminus\mathbf{a},j}=B}\frac{a_{j}A(\mathbf{a})}{n}=S_{n\ n-1}(A,B)\,\mathrm{.}

Finally, respectively by: the definition of
\sigma
, the previous equality and Proposition
4.7
, the law of total probability, and again Proposition
4.7
,

\displaystyle\big({\sigma_{n\ n-1}E_{\theta,\mathbf{p}}^{n}}\big)(B)=

\displaystyle\sum_{A\in\mathcal{A}_{n}}S_{n\ n-1}(A,B)\,E_{\theta,\mathbf{p}}^{n}(A)=\sum_{A\in\mathcal{A}_{n}}\mathbf{Q}[\mathscr{A}_{n-1}=B\mid\mathscr{A}_{n}=A]\,\mathbf{Q}[\mathscr{A}_{n}=A]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \mathbf{Q}[\mathscr{A}_{n-1}=B]=E_{\theta,\mathbf{p}}^{n-1}(B)\,\mathrm{.}\qed

Countably Colorful Hyperplane Transversal
Sutanoya Chakraborty, Arijit Ghosh, Soumi Nandi

Abstract
Let { ℱ n } n ∈ ℕ \left\{\mathcal{F}_{n}\right\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}} be an infinite sequence of families of compact connected sets in ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d} . An infinite sequence of compact connected sets { B n } n ∈ ℕ \left\{B_{n}\right\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}} is called heterochromatic sequence from { ℱ n } n ∈ ℕ \left\{\mathcal{F}_{n}\right\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}} if there exists an infinite sequence { i n } n ∈ ℕ \left\{i_{n}\right\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}} of natural numbers satisfying the following two properties • Property A: { i n } n ∈ ℕ \{i_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}} is a monotonically increasing sequence, and • Property B: for all n ∈ ℕ n\in\mathbb{N} , we have B n ∈ ℱ i n B_{n}\in\mathcal{F}_{i_{n}} We show that if every heterochromatic sequence from { ℱ n } n ∈ ℕ \left\{\mathcal{F}_{n}\right\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}} contains d + 1 d+1 sets that can be pierced by a single hyperplane then there exists a finite collection ℋ \mathcal{H} of hyperplanes from ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d} that pierces all but finitely many families from { ℱ n } n ∈ ℕ \left\{\mathcal{F}_{n}\right\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}} . As a direct consequence of our result, we get that if every countable subcollection from an infinite family ℱ \mathcal{F} of compact connected sets in ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d} contains d + 1 d+1 sets that can be pierced by a single hyperplane then ℱ \mathcal{F} can be pierced by finitely many hyperplanes. This is an infinite and colorful generalization of the ( p , q ) (p,q) -Theorem for hyperplanes by Alon and Kalai (Discrete & Computational Geometry 1995). To establish the optimality of our result we show, for all d ∈ ℕ d\in\mathbb{N} , there exists an infinite sequence { ℱ n } n ∈ ℕ \left\{\mathcal{F}_{n}\right\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}} of families of compact connected sets satisfying the following two conditions • Condition 1: for all n ∈ ℕ n\in\mathbb{N} , ℱ n \mathcal{F}_{n} is not pierceable by finitely many hyperplanes, and • Condition 2: for any m ∈ ℕ m\in\mathbb{N} and every sequence { B n } n = m ∞ \left\{B_{n}\right\}_{n=m}^{\infty} of compact connected sets in ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d} , where B i ∈ ℱ i B_{i}\in\mathcal{F}_{i} for all i ≥ m i\geq m , there exists a hyperplane in ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d} that pierces at least d + 1 d+1 sets in the sequence.

Introduction

Helly’s Theorem, proved more than a century ago has been one of the most fundamental results in Convex and Discrete Geometry
[
ADLS17
,
BK22
]
.
In its original form, Helly’s Theorem, asserts that for a family
\mathcal{F}
of compact convex sets in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
if any
d+1
members from
\mathcal{F}
have a nonempty intersection then the intersection of all the members of
\mathcal{F}
is also nonempty.
Note that the compactness condition can be removed if
\mathcal{F}
is finite, however, if
\mathcal{F}
is not finite then the compactness condition becomes necessary.

Geometric transversal theory
, an area that studies extensions of the Helly Theorem and its different relatives, has been an active area of research in Convex and Discrete Geometry.
In this paper, we will study an infinite variant of geometric transversal.

We will first give some required definitions from geometric transversal theory.
A
k
-flat
H
is said to
hit
or
pierce
a set
F\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
if intersection of
F
and
H
is nonempty. A set
\mathcal{T}
of
k
-flats is defined as a
k
-transversal
for a family
\mathcal{F}
of subsets in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
if, for every
F\in\mathcal{F}
, there exists a
k
-flat in
\mathcal{T}
that pierces
F
. Moreover, when
k=0
,
k=1
, or
k=d-1
,
\mathcal{T}
is denoted as
point transversal
,
line transversal
, and
hyperplane transversal
of
\mathcal{F}
, respectively. We also state that a family
\mathcal{F}
has a
k
-transversal of size
m
(or at most
m
) if there exists a
k
-transversal
\mathcal{T}
of
\mathcal{F}
with
|\mathcal{T}|=m
(or
|\mathcal{T}|\leq m
). For any two natural numbers
p
and
q
, a family (finite or infinite) of subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
is said to satisfy the
(p,q)
-property with respect to
k
-flats
if, for any
p
members of
\mathcal{F}
, there exists a
k
-flat that intersects at least
q
members out of the
p

Hadwiger and Debrunner
[
HD57
]
were the first to introduce the
(p,q)
-property, and they also proved the following important generalization of Helly’s Theorem.

Theorem 1.1

(Hadwiger and Debrunner
[
HD57
]
)
.

For all
p\geq q\geq d+1
satisfying
q>p\left(1-\frac{1}{d}\right)+1
, if
\mathcal{F}
is a family of compact convex sets in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
satisfying the
(p,q)
-property with respect to points, then
\mathcal{F}
has a point transversal of size at most
p-q+1
.

Hadwiger and Debrunner
[
HD57
]
asked the following fundamental question.

Question 1.2

(Hadwiger and Debrunner
(p,q)
-problem
[
HD57
]
)
.

Given
p\geq q\geq d+1
, does there exist a constant

such that if a family
\mathcal{F}
of compact convex sets in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
satisfies the
(p,q)
-property for point transversals, then
\mathcal{F}
has a point transversal of size at most

Notably, the above problem remained open for
35

Alon and Kleitman
[

]
proved the following fundamental theorem answering the above question.

Theorem 1.3

(Alon-Kleitman
[
AK92
]
:
(p,q)
-theorem)
.

For any three natural numbers
p\geq q\geq d+1
,
\exists c=c(p,q,d)
such that if
\mathcal{F}
is a finite collection of compact convex sets satisfying the
(p,q)
-property in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
then there exists a point transversal for
\mathcal{F}
of size at most

.

Alon and Kleitman’s
(p,q)
-theorem has been extremely influential and since then, this area has seen a lot of interest.
Alon and Kalai
[
AK95
]
proved the hyperplane variant of the above theorem.

Theorem 1.4

(Alon-Kalai
[
AK95
]
:
(p,q)
-theorem for hyperplane transversal)

For any three natural numbers

\exists c^{\prime}=c^{\prime}(p,q,d)

-property with respect to hyperplanes then there exists a hyperplane transversal for

Unlike point and hyperplane transversal,
Alon, Kalai, Matoušek, and Meshulam
[

]
established the impossibility of getting a

-theorem for

-transversal when

Bárány, Fodor, Montejano, Oliveros, and Pór
[

gave a heterochromatic generalization of Theorem

(Bárány et al.
[

]
: Heterochromatic

.

Let

Keller and Perles
[
KP22
]
studied infinite variants of
(p,q)
-type properties with a primary focus on establishing conditions that determine the existence of a finite-sized
k
-transversal for infinite families of convex sets. As part of this investigation, Keller and Perles

]
modified the classical

-property, referred to as

-property with respect to

-flats
. This modification involves considering an infinite sequence: within every infinite sequence of convex sets from a family

, there must exist

sets that can be pierced by a

-flat, where

is an appropriate function of

. Using this new property, Keller and Perles proved the following important theorem.

Theorem 1.6

(Keller-Perles
[
KP22
]
:
(\aleph_{0},k+2)
-theorem for
k
-transversal)
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a family of compact convex sets in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
0<r<R
, and
0\leq k\leq d-1
.

(i)

If each convex set

in
\mathcal{F}
contains a ball of radius

and is contained in a ball of radius

, and
\mathcal{F}
satisfies
(\aleph_{0},k+2)
-property with respect to

has a

-transversal of finite size.

(ii)

If
\mathcal{F}
is a family of closed balls and
\mathcal{F}
satisfies
(\aleph_{0},2)
-property with respect to points then the whole family
\mathcal{F}
has a point transversal of finite size.

In an updated version
[
KP23
]
Keller and Perles have extended Theorem
1.6
to families of

. Note that near balls are a generalization of closed balls, and also the convex sets considered in Theorem
1.6
. Refer to
[

]
for the exact definition of near-balls.

For all
k

,
Keller and Perles observed the impossibility of getting a

-theorem for
k
-transversal similar to the above theorem for infinite collections of arbitrary compact convex sets. They also gave an explicit example showing that the compactness assumption in Theorem

can not be weakened.

Chakraborty, Ghosh, and Nandi
[

]
gave heterochromatic generalizations of Theorem

,
(\aleph_{0},2)
-Theorem does not hold.

Our results

In this paper, we give the
(\aleph_{0},d+1)
-Theorem for hyperplane transversal in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

Theorem 1.7

(
(\aleph_{0},d+1)
-Theorem for hyperplane transversal)
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a family of compact connected sets in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that any infinite subcollection contains
d+1
members that can be pierced by a single hyperplane. Then the family
\mathcal{F}
has a finite hyperplane transversal.

We get the following corollary directly from the above theorem.

Corollary 1.8

(
(\aleph_{0},d+1)
-Theorem for hyperplane transversal of convex sets)
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a family of compact convex sets in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that any infinite subcollection contains
d+1
members that can be pierced by a single hyperplane. Then the family
\mathcal{F}
has a finite hyperplane transversal.

Observe that
d+1
in Theorem
1.7
is optimal since any nonempty
d
sets in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
can be pierced by a single hyperplane in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
Also, unlike for
k<d-1
, observe that the above theorem gives a
(\aleph_{0},d+1)
-Theorem for hyperplane trasversal for arbitrary compact connected sets.

Let
\{\mathcal{F}_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of families of compact connected sets in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
A
heterochromatic sequence
with respect to
\{\mathcal{F}_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is defined as a sequence
\{B_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
where we have a strictly increasing sequence
\{m_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
in
\mathbb{N}
such that
\forall n\in\mathbb{N}
, we have
B_{n}\in\mathcal{F}_{m_{n}}
.
A
strongly heterochromatic
sequence with respect to
\{\mathcal{F}_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is defined as a sequence
\{B_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
where
\forall n\in\mathbb{N}
, we have
B_{n}\in\mathcal{F}_{n}
. We will also prove the following heterochromatic generalization of Theorem
1.7
.

Theorem 1.9

(Heterochromatic
(\aleph_{0},d+1)
-Theorem for hyperplane transversal)
.

Let
\{\mathcal{F}_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of families of compact connected sets such that every heterochromatic sequence with respect to
\{\mathcal{F}_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
has
d+1
sets that are pierceable by a single hyperplane. Then all but finitely many
\mathcal{F}_{n}
’s are pierceable by finitely many hyperplanes.

Additionally, from Theorem
1.7
and Theorem
1.9
, we can also show the existence of a finite-size collection of hyperplanes for the whole sequence
\left\{\mathcal{F}_{n}\right\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
that is a transversal for all but finitely many families from the sequence
\left\{\mathcal{F}_{n}\right\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
.

Theorem 1.10

(Stronger variant of Theorem
1.9
)
.

Let
\left\{\mathcal{F}_{n}\right\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of families of compact connected sets such that every heterochromatic sequence with respect to
\{\mathcal{F}_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
has
d+1
sets that are pierceable by a single hyperplane.
Then there exists a finite-sized collection
\mathcal{H}
of hyperplanes in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that
\mathcal{H}
pierces all but finitely many families from
\left\{\mathcal{F}_{n}\right\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
.

We complement the above result by showing the following no-go theorem which establishes the optimality of our generalization of Theorem
1.7
.

Theorem 1.11

(
No-go
theorem for strongly heterochromatic generalization)
.

There exists a sequence
\{\mathcal{F}_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of families of compact convex sets in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that

•

for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
\mathcal{F}_{n}
is not pierceable by finitely many hyperplanes, and

•

every strongly heterochromatic sequence with respect to
\left\{\mathcal{F}_{n}\right\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
contains
d+1
balls which can be pierced by a single hyperplane.

The proof of the above no-go theorem shows the impossibility of getting a strongly heterochromatic
(\aleph_{0},d+1)
-Theorem for hyperplanes even when the compact connected sets in question have a nice shape when
d>1
as closed unit balls do.
Additionally, we can prove the following stronger impossibility result.

Theorem 1.12

(Stronger variant of Theorem
1.11
)
.

There exists a
sequence
\{\mathcal{F}_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of compact convex sets in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
satisfying the following two conditions:

•

for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
\mathcal{F}_{n}
is not pierceable by finitely many hyperplanes, and

•

for any

and every sequence
\left\{B_{n}\right\}_{n=m}^{\infty}
of compact connected sets in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, where

Abstract
. In this article we study convex non-autonomous variational problems with differential forms and corresponding function spaces. We introduce a general framework for constructing counterexamples to the Lavrentiev gap, which we apply to several models, including the double phase, borderline case of double phase potential, and variable exponent. The results for the borderline case of double phase potential provide new insights even for the scalar case, i.e., variational problems with 0 0 -forms.

Introduction

In this article we study variational problems and corresponding function spaces associated with the integral functionals of the form

(1.1)

\mathcal{F}_{\varPhi,b}(\omega):=\int\limits_{\Omega}\varPhi(x,|d\omega|)\,dV+\int\limits_{\Omega}b\wedge d\omega

We study calculus of variations for the non-autonomous models with general growth and differential forms. For our knowledge no regularity results are know for such classes. The focus of the present paper is on the conditions, separating the case with the energy gap from the regular case (density of smooth functions) for the integrands with nonstandard growth, in particular, for the variable exponent and double phase models. An essential feature of the nonautonomous models with nonstandard growth is the presence of the Lavrentiev gap phenomenon. The energy
\mathcal{F}_{\varPhi,b}
defines the corresponding generalized partial Sobolev-Orlicz spaces of differential forms
W^{d,\varPhi(\cdot)}(\Omega,\Lambda^{k})
(the natural energy space for
\mathcal{F}_{\varPhi,0}
, which consists of forms with
W^{1,1}(\Omega)
coefficients which fall in the domain of
\mathcal{F}_{\varPhi,0}
) described in Section
2.5
. The Lavrentiev gap in this case is the inequality

(1.7)

\inf\mathcal{F}_{\varPhi,b}(W^{d,\varPhi(\cdot)}_{c}(\Omega,\Lambda^{k}))<\inf\mathcal{F}_{\varPhi,b}(C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega,\Lambda^{k})).

where
W^{d,\varPhi(\cdot)}_{c}(\Omega,\Lambda^{k})
is the set of
W^{d,\varPhi(\cdot)}(\Omega,\Lambda^{k})
forms compactly supported in
\Omega
.

A closely related problem is density of smooth functions in the natural energy space of the functional. Denote the closure of smooth forms from
W^{d,\varPhi(\cdot)}(\Omega,\Lambda^{k})
in this space by
H^{d,\varPhi(\cdot)}(\Omega,\Lambda^{k})
. If any function from the domain of
\mathcal{F}_{\varPhi,b}
can be approximated by smooth functions with energy convergence (equivalently, if
H^{d,\varPhi(\cdot)}(\Omega,\Lambda^{k})=W^{d,\varPhi(\cdot)}(\Omega,\Lambda^{k})
, which is abbreviated to
H=W
) then the Lavrentiev gap is obviously absent. In the autonomous case, when the integrand
\varPhi=\varPhi(t)
is an Orlicz function independent of
x
, the Lavrentiev phenomenon is absent (
H=W
).

Now we state the main results of this paper. We work with three models: classical double phase potential, borderline double phase potential, and variable exponent. For each of these cases we construct examples for the Lavrentiev gap. However, the construction presented in this paper is not limited to these models. For instance, it can be also used to treat the weighted energy. Let
\Omega
be a ball in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
and
k\in\{0,\ldots,N-2\}
.

Theorem A
.

Let
p>1
,
\alpha\in[0,1]
, and
q>p+\alpha\max((k+1)^{-1},(p-1)(N-k-1)^{-1})
Then there exists an integrand
\varPhi(x,t)=t^{p}+a(x)t^{q}
where nonnegative weight
a=a(x)
is bounded,
a\in C^{\alpha}(\overline{\Omega})
if
\alpha\in(0,1)
and
a\in\mathrm{Lip}(\overline{\Omega})
if
\alpha=1
, such that
H^{d,\varPhi(\cdot)}(\Omega,\Lambda^{k})\neq W^{d,\varPhi(\cdot)}(\Omega,\Lambda^{k})
and (
1.7
) holds.

Theorem B
.

Let
p_{0}>1
,
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{R}
,
\varkappa\geq 0
such that
\alpha+\beta>p_{0}+\varkappa
. Let
\varphi
and
\psi
be two Orlicz functions such that
\varphi(t)\sim t^{p_{0}}\ln^{-\beta}t
and
\psi(t)\sim t^{p_{0}}\ln^{\alpha}t

for large

. Then there exists an integrand
\varPhi(x,t)=\varphi(t)+a(x)\psi(t)
where
a=a(x)
is a nonnegative function with the modulus of continuity
C\ln^{-\varkappa}(1/t)
such that
H^{d,\varPhi(\cdot)}(\Omega,\Lambda^{k})\neq W^{d,\varPhi(\cdot)}(\Omega,\Lambda^{k})
and (
1.7
) holds.

Theorem C
.

Let
1<p_{-}<p_{+}
. There exists a variable exponent
p:\Omega\to[p_{-},p_{+}]
with the modulus of continuity
\varkappa_{0}(\ln t^{-1})^{-1}\ln\ln t^{-1}
,
\varkappa_{0}=\varkappa_{0}(p_{-},p_{+},N,k)>0
, such that for
\varPhi(x,t)=t^{p(x)}
there holds
H^{d,\varPhi(\cdot)}(\Omega,\Lambda^{k})\neq W^{d,\varPhi(\cdot)}(\Omega,\Lambda^{k})
and (
1.7
).

are proved in Section

. The weight

in Theorems

Our setting can be called “semivectorial”, or generalized Uhlenbeck structure, since the integrand is isotropic. In this respect it has substantially more rigid structure than the fully vectorial problems (say, of elasticity theory) with quasi-convex integrands. Note that in the “fully” vectorial setting the situation is more delicate, and the Lavrentiev phenomenon is possible even for “standard” growth conditions in the autonomous (but anisotropic!) case, see Ball, Mizel
[
BM85

and Foss, Hruza, Mizel

The models with Lavrentiev phenomenon are also challenging to study numerically since the standard numerical schemes fail to converge to the

[

2.
Differential forms and Sobolev-Orlicz spaces

Here we recall some basic facts and definitions from the theory of differential forms. In general we follow definitions and notations from
[
CDK12
]
[Chapters 1.2;2.1;3.1-3.3], but the Hodge codifferential is the formal adjoint of the exterior derivative
d
(as in
[
IL93
,
GT06
]
).

2.1.
Exterior algebra.

The Grassman algebra of exterior
k
-forms (i.e. skew-symmetric
k
-linear functions) over
\mathbb{R}^{N}
is denoted by
\Lambda^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, or for brevity just by
\Lambda^{k}
. The exterior product of
f\in\Lambda^{k}
and
g\in\Lambda^{l}
is defined by

(f\wedge g)(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{k+l})=\sum\mathrm{sign}\,(i_{1},\ldots,i_{k},j_{1},\ldots,j_{l})f(\xi_{i_{1}},\ldots,\xi_{i_{k}})g(\xi_{j_{1}},\ldots,\xi_{j_{l}})

where the summation is over permutations
(i_{1},\ldots,i_{k},j_{1},\ldots,j_{l})
of
(1,2,\ldots,k+l)
such that
i_{1}<\ldots<i_{k}
,
j_{1}<\ldots<j_{l}
. This operation is linear in both arguments, associative, and for
f\in\Lambda^{k}
and
g\in\Lambda^{l}
there holds
f\wedge g=(-1)^{kl}g\wedge f
.

Let
e_{j}
be an orthonormal basis
\{e_{j}\}_{j=1}^{N}
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
and
\{e^{j}\}_{j=1}^{N}
be its dual system in
\Lambda^{1}
,
e^{j}(e_{l})=\delta_{jl}
. The monomials
e^{i_{1}}\wedge\ldots\wedge e^{i_{k}}
,
i_{1}<i_{2}<\ldots<i_{k}
form a basis in
\Lambda^{k}
. Denote
f_{i_{1}\ldots i_{k}}=f(e_{i_{1}},\ldots,e_{i_{k}})
. Then the set of
f_{i_{1}\ldots i_{k}}
with
i_{1}<i_{2}<\ldots<i_{k}
gives the coordinates of
f
:

f=\sum_{1\leq i_{1}<\ldots<i_{k}\leq N}f_{i_{1}\ldots i_{k}}e^{i_{1}}\wedge\ldots\wedge e^{i_{k}}.

The scalar product of
f,g\in\Lambda^{k}
with coordinates
f_{i_{1}\ldots i_{k}}
and
g_{i_{1}\ldots i_{k}}
is given by

\langle f,g\rangle=\sum_{1\leq i_{1}<\ldots<i_{k}\leq N}f_{i_{1}\ldots i_{k}}g_{i_{1},\ldots i_{k}}.

The scalar product does not depend on the particular choice of the orthonormal basis
\{e_{j}\}_{j=1}^{N}
. We denote
|f|=\langle f,f\rangle^{1/2}
.

The Hodge star operator
\ast:\Lambda^{k}\to\Lambda^{N-k}
is defined by
f\wedge g=\langle\ast f,g\rangle e^{1}\wedge\ldots\wedge e^{N}
for any
g\in\Lambda^{N-k}
, or equivalently by
f\wedge\ast g=\langle f,g\rangle e^{1}\wedge\ldots\wedge e^{N}
for all
f,g\in\Lambda^{k}
. The Hodge star operator
\ast
is an isometry between
\Lambda^{k}
and
\Lambda^{N-k}
and for
f\in\Lambda^{k}
there holds

\ast(\ast f)=(-1)^{k(N-k)}f,\quad\ast^{-1}f=(-1)^{k(N-k)}\ast f.

For any
f\in\Lambda^{k}
and shuffle
j_{1},\ldots,j_{N}
there holds
(\ast f)_{j_{k+1}\ldots j_{N}}=\mathrm{sign}\,(j_{1},\ldots,j_{N})f_{j_{1}\ldots j_{k}}
.

The interior product (contraction) of
f\in\Lambda^{k}
and
g\in\Lambda^{l}
defined by

g\lrcorner f=(-1)^{(N-k)(k-l)}\ast(g\wedge(\ast f))

is the adjoint of the wedge product:

\langle g\wedge\alpha,f\rangle=\langle\alpha,g\lrcorner f\rangle\quad\text{for any}\quad\alpha\in\Lambda^{k-l},\quad f\in\Lambda^{k},\quad g\in\Lambda^{l}.

There holds
\ast(g\lrcorner f)=(-1)^{(k-l)l}g\wedge\ast f
and
\ast(g\wedge f)=(-1)^{kl}g\lrcorner\ast f
.

If
l=0
then
g\lrcorner f=gf
. If
l>k
then
g\lrcorner f=0
, if
l=k
then
g\lrcorner f=f\lrcorner g=(f,g)
. If
l\leq k
then

(g\lrcorner f)_{j_{1}\ldots j_{k-l}}=\sum_{1\leq i_{1}<\ldots<i_{l}\leq N}g_{i_{1}\ldots i_{l}}f_{i_{1}\ldots i_{l}j_{1}\ldots j_{k-l}}.

For
w,v\in\Lambda^{1}
there holds

(2.1)

w\lrcorner(v\wedge f)+v\wedge(w\lrcorner f)=\langle w,v\rangle f.

For a vector
X
the operator
\imath_{X}:\Lambda^{k}\to\Lambda^{k-1}
is defined by
(\imath_{X}f)(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{k-1})=f(X,\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{k-1})
. There holds
\imath_{v}(f\wedge g)=(\imath_{v}f)\wedge g+(-1)^{\mathrm{deg}\,f}f\wedge(\imath_{v}g)
, and
\imath_{v}\imath_{w}=-\imath_{w}\imath_{v}
,
\imath_{v}\imath_{v}=0
. For a vector
v\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
and the 1-form
v^{\flat}\in\Lambda^{1}
with the same coordinates there holds
v^{\flat}\lrcorner f=\imath_{v}f
.

2.2.
Differential forms.

A differential form is a mapping from
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
to
\Lambda^{k}
. Further
\Omega
will be a bounded contractible domain with sufficiently regular boundary. Using the canonical basis
dx^{i_{1}}\wedge\ldots\wedge dx^{i_{k}}
a
k
-form can be represented as

(2.2)

f=\sum_{1\leq i_{1}<\ldots<i_{k}\leq N}f_{i_{1}\ldots i_{k}}dx^{i_{1}}\wedge\ldots\wedge dx^{i_{k}}.

Then
|f|^{2}(x):=\sum_{1\leq i_{1}<\ldots<i_{k}\leq N}|f_{i_{1}\ldots i_{k}}|^{2}(x)
.

For two differential forms
f
and
g
of order
k
their scalar product in the sense of
L^{2}(\Omega,\Lambda^{k})
is

(f,g)=\int\limits_{\Omega}f\wedge\ast g=\int\limits_{\Omega}\langle f,g\rangle\,dV,\quad dV=dx^{1}\wedge\ldots\wedge dx^{N}.

The operation of exterior differentiation
d
is a unique mapping from
k
-forms to
(k+1)
-forms such that
df
coincides with the differential of
f
for
0
-forms (functions),
d\circ d=0
,
d(\alpha\wedge\beta)=d\alpha\wedge\beta+(-1)^{k}\alpha\wedge d\beta
for any
\alpha\in C^{1}(\Omega,\Lambda^{k})
and
\beta\in C^{1}(\Omega,\Lambda^{l})
. For a
k
-form
f
,

df(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{k+1})=\sum_{j=1}^{k+1}(-1)^{j-1}[f^{\prime}(x)\xi_{j}](\xi_{1},\ldots,\widehat{\xi_{j}},\ldots,\xi_{k+1}),\quad\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{k+1}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}.

The interior derivative (Hodge codifferential) of a
k
-form
f
is

\delta f=(-1)^{N(k-1)+1}\ast d\ast f=(-1)^{k}\ast^{-1}d\ast f.

There holds
d^{2}=0
,
\delta^{2}=0
. On
k
-forms

(2.3)

\ast\delta=(-1)^{k}d\ast\quad\text{and}\quad\ast d=(-1)^{k+1}\delta\ast.

For a
k
-form
f
and
l
-form
g
there holds

d(f\wedge g)=df\wedge g+(-1)^{k}f\wedge dg,\quad\delta(f\lrcorner g)=(-1)^{k+l+1}df\lrcorner g-f\lrcorner\delta g.

Formally one can write
df=\nabla\wedge f
,
\delta f=-\nabla\lrcorner f
, and in coordinates, for the form (
2.2
), using the Einstein convention of summation over repeated indices we have

(2.4)

(df)_{i_{1}\ldots i_{k+1}}=(-1)^{l-1}\partial_{x_{i_{l}}}f_{i_{1}\ldots\hat{\imath}_{l}\ldots i_{k+1}},\quad(\delta f)_{i_{1}\ldots i_{k-1}}=-\partial_{x_{j}}f_{ji_{1}\ldots i_{k-1}}.

Let
\nu=(\nu_{1},\ldots,\nu_{N})
be the unit outer normal to
\Omega
and
\nu^{\flat}=\nu_{1}dx^{1}+\ldots+\nu_{N}dx^{N}
. For a differential
k
-form
f
the standard Gauss theorem reads as (see (
2.4
))

\int\limits_{\Omega}(df)_{i_{1}\ldots i_{k+1}}dV=\int\limits_{\partial\Omega}(\nu^{\flat}\wedge f)_{i_{1}\ldots i_{k+1}}d\sigma,\quad\int\limits_{\Omega}(\delta f)_{i_{1}\ldots i_{k-1}}dV=-\int\limits_{\partial\Omega}(\nu^{\flat}\lrcorner f)_{i_{1}\ldots i_{k-1}}d\sigma

for each
1\leq i_{1}<\ldots<i_{k}\leq N
, where
dV=dx^{1}\ldots dx^{N}
is the standard volume form and
d\sigma
is the surface area element. The same reasoning also gives the integration-by-parts formula

(2.5)

\int\limits_{\Omega}\langle df,g\rangle\,dV-\int\limits_{\Omega}\langle f,\delta g\rangle\,dV=\int\limits_{\partial\Omega}{\langle{\nu^{\flat}\wedge f},{g}\rangle}\,d\sigma=\int\limits_{\partial\Omega}{\langle{f},{\nu^{\flat}\lrcorner g}\rangle}\,d\sigma.

In the sense of forms, the surface element
d\sigma
is connected to the volume form
dV
by
d\sigma=\imath_{\nu}dV
. The orientation of
\partial\Omega
is chosen such that the integral of
d\sigma
over any “substantial” boundary part is positive.

The operators
d
and
\delta
are adjoint on compactly supported forms. By direct computation (use (
2.1
) with
v=\nabla
,
w=-\nabla
),
d\delta+\delta d=-\triangle
, where the Laplace operator is applied componentwise.

A form
f
satisfying
df=0
is closed. A form
f
satisfying
\delta f=0
is coclosed. If
f=dg
then
f
is exact, and if
f=\delta g
then
f
is coexact. If both
df=0
and
\delta f=0
the form is called harmonic (or harmonic field).

The pullback of the form
f
under the mapping
\varphi
is defined by
\varphi^{*}f
,

(\varphi^{*}f)(x;\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{k})=f(\varphi(x);\varphi^{\prime}(x)\xi_{1},\ldots,\varphi^{\prime}(x)\xi_{k}).

This operation satisfies
\varphi^{*}(\alpha\wedge\beta)=\varphi^{*}\alpha\wedge\varphi^{*}\beta
and
\varphi^{*}d=d\varphi^{*}
.

2.3.
Tangential and normal part of a form.

Let
\nu=(\nu_{1},\ldots,\nu_{N})
be the unit outer normal to
\partial\Omega
and for a differential form
\omega
define its tangential part

t\omega(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{k})=\omega(\xi_{1}-(\nu,\xi_{1})\nu,\ldots,\xi_{k}-(\nu,\xi_{k})\nu)

and its normal part
n\omega=\omega-t\omega
. Define the
1
-form
\nu^{\flat}=\nu_{1}dx^{1}+\ldots+\nu_{N}dx^{N}
, then

\displaystyle tf=\nu^{\flat}\lrcorner(\nu^{\flat}\wedge f),\quad nf=\nu^{\flat}\wedge(\nu^{\flat}\lrcorner f),\quad f=tf+nf,

\displaystyle\nu^{\flat}\wedge tf=\nu^{\flat}\wedge f,\quad\nu^{\flat}\lrcorner\,tf=0,\quad tf=0\Leftrightarrow\nu^{\flat}\wedge f=0,

\displaystyle\nu^{\flat}\wedge nf=0,\quad\nu^{\flat}\lrcorner\,nf=\nu^{\flat}\lrcorner f,\quad nf=0\Leftrightarrow\nu^{\flat}\lrcorner f=0.

That is, setting
tf
is equivalent to setting
\nu^{\flat}\wedge f
and setting
nf
is equivalent to setting
\nu^{\flat}\lrcorner f
.

While integrating over
\partial\Omega
, the tangential part of a form coincides with its pullback under the inclusion
\jmath:\partial\Omega\to\overline{\Omega}
, that is
t\omega=\jmath^{*}\omega
, and the normal part of the form vanishes.

The decomposition of a form into the tangential and normal parts can be also done using coordinates in a “collar” neighbourhood of
\partial\Omega
, by choosing (locally) an “admissible” coordinate system (map)
\varphi:U\to V
,
U,V\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
, such that
\partial\Omega\cap V\subset\{\varphi(y^{\prime},0)\,:\,(y^{\prime},0)\subset U\}
and
(\varphi_{y_{i}}(y^{\prime},0),\varphi_{y_{N}}(y^{\prime},0))=\delta_{iN}
,
1\leq i\leq N
. In this coordinate system for

\omega=\sum_{1\leq i_{1}<\ldots<i_{k}\leq N}\omega_{i_{1}\ldots i_{k}}dy^{i_{1}}\wedge\ldots\wedge dy^{i_{k}}

we have
\omega=t\omega+n\omega
, where

\displaystyle t\omega

\displaystyle=\sum_{1\leq i_{1}<\ldots<i_{k}<N}\omega_{i_{1}\ldots i_{k}}dy^{i_{1}}\wedge\ldots\wedge dy^{i_{k}},

\displaystyle n\omega

\displaystyle=\sum_{1\leq i_{1}<\ldots<i_{k-1}<N}\omega_{i_{1}\ldots i_{k}}dy^{i_{1}}\wedge\ldots\wedge dy^{i_{k-1}}\wedge dy^{N}.

If
\omega
is a function (
0
-form) we set
t\omega=\omega
and
n\omega=0
. If
\omega
is an
N
-form we set
t\omega=0
,
n\omega=\omega
. The decomposition
\omega=t\omega+n\omega
is invariant on
\partial\Omega
and we have

\displaystyle t\ast=\ast n,\quad n\ast=\ast t,\quad td=dt,\quad n\delta=\delta n.

In particular,
t\omega=0
on
\partial\Omega
implies
td\omega=0
on
\partial\Omega
and
n\omega=0
on
\partial\Omega
implies
n\delta\omega=0
on
\partial\Omega
.

In terms of the Stokes theorem, integration-by-parts formula (
2.5
) reads as follows: by (
2.3
) for a
k
-form
f
and a
(k+1)
-form
g
there holds
d(f\wedge\ast g)=df\wedge\ast g-f\wedge\ast\delta g
, therefore

(2.6)

(df,g)-(f,\delta g)=\int\limits_{\Omega}\bigl(df\wedge\ast g-f\wedge\ast\delta g\bigr)=\int\limits_{\Omega}d(f\wedge\ast g)=\int\limits_{\partial\Omega}f\wedge\ast g=\int\limits_{\partial\Omega}tf\wedge\ast ng.

2.4.
Orlicz functions setup

We say that
\phi\,:\,[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty]
is an Orlicz
function if
\phi
is convex, left-continuous,
\phi(0)=0
,

\lim\limits_{t\to 0}t^{-1}\phi(t)=0
and

\lim\limits_{t\to\infty}t^{-1}\phi(t)=\infty
. The conjugate Orlicz
function
\phi^{*}
is defined by

\displaystyle\phi^{*}(s)

\displaystyle:=\sup_{t\geq 0}\big(st-\phi(t)\big).

In particular,
st\leq\phi(t)+\phi^{*}(s)
.

In the following we assume that

\varPhi\,:\,\Omega\times[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty]
is a generalized
Orlicz function, i.e.
\varPhi(x,\cdot)
is an Orlicz function for
every
x\in\Omega
and
\varPhi(\cdot,t)
is measurable for
every
t\geq 0
. We define the conjugate function
\varPhi^{*}
point-wise,
i.e.
\varPhi^{*}(x,\cdot):=(\varPhi(x,\cdot))^{*}
.

We assume that
\varPhi
satisfies the “nonstandard” growth condition

(2.7)

\displaystyle-c_{0}+c_{1}{\lvert{t}\rvert}^{p_{-}}\leq\varPhi(x,t)\leq c_{2}{\lvert{t}\rvert}^{p_{+}}+c_{0},

where
1<p_{-}\leq p_{+}<\infty
,
c_{0}\geq 0
,
c_{1},c_{2}>0
, and the following properties:

(a)

\varPhi
satisfies the
\Delta_{2}
-condition,
i.e. there exists
c\geq 2
such that for
all
x\in\Omega
and all
t\geq 0

(2.8)

\displaystyle\varPhi(x,2t)

\displaystyle\leq c\,\varPhi(x,t).

(b)

\varPhi
satisfies the
\nabla_{2}
-condition,
i.e.
\varPhi^{*}
satisfies the
\Delta_{2}
-condition. As a consequence,
there exist
s>1
and
c>0
such that for all
x\in\Omega
,

t\geq 0
and
\gamma\in[0,1]
there holds

(2.9)

\displaystyle\varPhi(x,\gamma t)\leq c\,\gamma^{s}\,\varPhi(x,t).

(c)

\varPhi
and
\varPhi^{*}
are proper, i.e. for every
t\geq 0
there holds

\int\limits_{\Omega}\varPhi(x,t)\,dV<\infty\quad\text{and}\quad\int\limits_{\Omega}\varPhi^{*}(x,t)\,dV<\infty.

2.5.
Sobolev-Orlicz spaces of differential forms

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
be a bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
. In our applications this will always be a ball or a cube.

Different functional spaces like Lebesgue spaces
L^{p}(\Omega)
and Lebesgue-Orlicz spaces
L^{\varphi(\cdot)}(\Omega)
, Sobolev spaces
W^{1,p}(\Omega)
and Sobolev-Orlicz spaces
W^{1,\varPhi(\cdot)}(\Omega)
, spaces of
k
times continuosly differentiable functions
C^{k}(\Omega)
are defined in the usual way (see, for example,
[
HH19
]
).

The Lebesgue-Orlicz space
L^{\varPhi(\cdot)}(\Omega)
is the set of all measurable functions in
\Omega
with finite Luxemburg norm

\|f\|_{L^{\varPhi(\cdot)}(\Omega)}=\inf\biggl\{\lambda>0\,:\,\int\limits_{\Omega}\varPhi(x,|f|\lambda^{-1})\,dV\leq 1\biggr\}.

The Sobolev-Orlicz space
W^{1,\varPhi(\cdot)}(\Omega)
is the set of functions
f\in W^{1,1}(\Omega)
such that
|\nabla f|\in L^{\varPhi(\cdot)}(\Omega)
, endowed with the norm
\|f\|_{W^{1,\varPhi(\cdot)}(\Omega)}=\|f\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}+\|\nabla f\|_{L^{\varPhi(\cdot)}(\Omega)}
.

For a generalized Orlicz function
\varPhi(x,t)
we define the Lebesgue-Orlicz space
L^{\varPhi(\cdot)}(\Omega,\Lambda^{k})
as the space of measurable differential
k
-form such that
|f|\in L^{\varPhi(\cdot)}(\Omega)
. The norm in this space is the norm of
|f|
in
L^{\varPhi(\cdot)}(\Omega)
. For constant
\varPhi\equiv p\geq 1
we get the standard Lebesgue space
L^{p}(\Omega,\Lambda^{k})
.

Let
r\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
. For
E=\Omega
or
E=\overline{\Omega}
the space
C^{r}(E,\Lambda^{k})
is the space of all differential
k
-forms for which all partial derivatives
D^{\alpha}f^{I}
up to the order
r
are continuous in
E
. By
C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega,\Lambda^{k})
we denote the space of smooth
k
-forms with compact support in
\Omega
.

Definition 1

(Full Sobolev-Orlicz Space)
.

We say that a
k
-form
f\in W^{1,\varPhi(\cdot)}(\Omega,\Lambda^{k})
if
f_{i_{1}\cdots i_{k}}\in W^{1,\varPhi(\cdot)}(\Omega)
for every
1\leq i_{1}<\cdots<i_{k}\leq N
. The norm is defined componentwise:

\|f\|_{W^{1,\varPhi(\cdot)}(\Omega,\Lambda^{k})}=\sum_{1\leq i_{1}<\ldots<i_{k}\leq N}\|f_{i_{1}\ldots i_{k}}\|_{W^{1,\varPhi}(\Omega)}.

If
\Omega
is of class
C^{2}
then for
f\in W^{1,1}(\Omega,\Lambda^{k})
the boundary trace of
f
exists, belongs to
L^{1}(\partial\Omega)
, and the Stokes theorem holds (see
[
Sch95
]
,
[
Ren13
, Theorem 6.4]
):

\int\limits_{\partial\Omega}f=\int\limits_{\Omega}df.

We say that
u\in L^{1}_{loc}(\Omega,\Lambda^{k})
has a
weak differential

du\in L^{1}_{loc}(\Omega,\Lambda^{k+1})
if for any
\xi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega,\Lambda^{k+1})
there holds
(u,\delta\xi)=(du,\xi)
in
L^{2}(\Omega,\Lambda^{k})
, or equivalently

\int\limits_{\Omega}u\wedge d\xi=(-1)^{k+1}\int\limits_{\Omega}du\wedge\xi

for any
\xi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega,\Lambda^{N-k-1})
.

We say that
u\in L^{1}_{loc}(\Omega,\Lambda^{k})
has a
weak codifferential

\delta u\in L^{1}_{loc}(\Omega,\Lambda^{k-1})
if for any
\xi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega,\Lambda^{k-1})
there holds
(u,d\xi)=(\delta u,\xi)
in
L^{2}(\Omega,\Lambda^{k})
, or equivalently

\int\limits_{\Omega}u\wedge\delta\xi=(-1)^{k}\int\limits_{\Omega}\delta u\wedge\xi,

for any
\xi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega,\Lambda^{N-k+1})
.

Both weak differential and codifferential are unique.

Definition 2

(Partial Sobolev-Orlicz Space)
.

For
0\leq k\leq N-1
we define the partial Sobolev-Orlicz space
W^{d,\varPhi(\cdot)}(\Omega,\Lambda^{k})
as the set of forms
\omega\in L^{1}(\Omega,\Lambda^{k})
with weak differential
d\omega\in L^{\varPhi(\cdot)}(\Omega,\Lambda^{k})
, endowed with the norm

\displaystyle{\lVert{\omega}\rVert}_{W^{d,\varPhi(\cdot)}(\Omega,\Lambda^{k})}:={\lVert{\omega}\rVert}_{L^{1}(\Omega,\Lambda^{k})}+{\lVert{d\omega}\rVert}_{L^{\varPhi(\cdot)}(\Omega,\Lambda^{k+1})}.

The space
H^{d,\varPhi(\cdot)}(\Omega,\Lambda^{k})
is the closure of smooth forms from
W^{d,\varPhi(\cdot)}(\Omega,\Lambda^{k})
in this space.

For
1\leq k\leq N
we define the space
W^{\delta,\varPhi(\cdot)}(\Omega,\Lambda^{k})
as the set of forms
\omega\in L^{1}(\Omega,\Lambda^{k})
with weak codifferential
\delta\omega\in L^{\varPhi(\cdot)}(\Omega,\Lambda^{k})
endowed with the norm

\displaystyle{\lVert{\omega}\rVert}_{W^{\delta,\varPhi(\cdot)}(\Omega,\Lambda^{k})}:={\lVert{\omega}\rVert}_{L^{1}(\Omega,\Lambda^{k})}+{\lVert{\delta\omega}\rVert}_{L^{\varPhi(\cdot)}(\Omega,\Lambda^{k-1})}.

The space
H^{\delta,\varPhi(\cdot)}(\Omega,\Lambda^{k})
is the closure of smooth forms from
W^{\delta,\varPhi(\cdot)}(\Omega,\Lambda^{k})
in this space.

If
\Omega
is a bounded
C^{2}
domain (or a polyhedral domain), then the following Green’s formulas hold
[
CDK12
, Theorem 3.28]
. Let
0\leq k\leq N-1
and let
p>1
. If
f\in W^{d,p}(\Omega,\Lambda^{k})
,
g\in W^{1,p^{\prime}}(\Omega,\Lambda^{k+1})
, then

\displaystyle\int\limits_{\Omega}{\langle{df},{g}\rangle}\,dV-\int\limits_{\Omega}{\langle{\delta g},{f}\rangle}\,dV=\int\limits_{\partial\Omega}{\langle{\nu\wedge f},{g}\rangle}\,d\sigma.

If
f\in W^{1,p}(\Omega,\Lambda^{k})
,
g\in W^{\delta,p^{\prime}}(\Omega,\Lambda^{k+1})
, then

\displaystyle\int\limits_{\Omega}{\langle{df},{g}\rangle}\,dV-\int\limits_{\Omega}{\langle{\delta g},{f}\rangle}\,dV=\int\limits_{\partial\Omega}{\langle{f},{\nu\lrcorner g}\rangle}\,d\sigma.

The boundary traces
\nu\wedge f
and
\nu\lrcorner g
in these formulas are given by bounded linear mappings from
W^{d,p}(\Omega,\Lambda^{k})
to
W^{-1/p,p}(\partial\Omega,\Lambda^{k+1})
and from
W^{\delta,p^{\prime}}(\Omega,\Lambda^{k+1})
to
W^{-1/p^{\prime},p^{\prime}}(\partial\Omega,\Lambda^{k})
, respectively. These mappings are generated by these very integration-by-parts formulas. If
f
belongs to the full Sobolev space
W^{1,p}(\Omega,\Lambda^{k})
, then both tangential and normal components of its boundary trace
tf
and
nf
are from
W^{1-1/p,p}(\partial\Omega,\Lambda^{k})
.

Let
W^{d,\varPhi(\cdot)}_{c}(\Omega,\Lambda^{k})
be the set of forms from
W^{d,\varPhi(\cdot)}(\Omega,\Lambda^{k})
with compact support in
\Omega
.

Definition 3

(Spaces with zero tangential component)
.

For
0\leq k\leq N-1
we define the space
W^{d,\varPhi(\cdot)}_{T}(\Omega,\Lambda^{k})
as the set of
\omega\in W^{d,\varPhi(\cdot)}(\Omega,\Lambda^{k})
such that
(d\omega,\beta)_{L^{2}(\Omega,\Lambda^{k+1})}=(\omega,\delta\beta)_{L^{2}(\Omega,\Lambda^{k})}
for all
\beta\in C^{1}(\overline{\Omega},\Lambda^{k+1})
, endowed with the norm of
W^{d,\varPhi(\cdot)}(\Omega,\Lambda^{k})
.

The space
\widetilde{W}^{d,\varPhi(\cdot)}_{T}(\Omega,\Lambda^{k})
is the closure of
W^{d,\varPhi(\cdot)}_{c}(\Omega,\Lambda^{k})
in
W^{d,\varPhi(\cdot)}(\Omega,\Lambda^{k})
.

The space
H^{d,\varPhi(\cdot)}_{T}(\Omega,\Lambda^{k})
is the closure of
C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega,\Lambda^{k})
in
W^{d,\varPhi(\cdot)}(\Omega,\Lambda^{k})
.

Clearly,
H^{d,\varPhi(\cdot)}_{T}(\Omega,\Lambda^{k})\subset\widetilde{W}^{d,\varPhi(\cdot)}_{T}(\Omega,\Lambda^{k})\subset W^{d,\varPhi(\cdot)}_{T}(\Omega,\Lambda^{k})
. A smooth
k
-form
\omega
belongs to
H^{d,\varPhi(\cdot)}_{T}(\Omega,\Lambda^{k})
if and only if its tangential component
t\omega
is zero on
\partial\Omega
.

Let
W^{\delta,\varPhi(\cdot)}_{c}(\Omega,\Lambda^{k})
be the set of forms from
W^{\delta,\varPhi(\cdot)}(\Omega,\Lambda^{k})
with compact support in
\Omega
.

Definition 4

(Spaces with zero normal component)
.

For
1\leq k\leq N
we define the space

W^{\delta,\varPhi(\cdot)}_{N}(\Omega,\Lambda^{k})
as the set of
\omega\in W^{\delta,\varPhi(\cdot)}(\Omega,\Lambda^{k})
such that
(\delta\omega,\beta)_{L^{2}(\Omega,\Lambda^{k-1})}=(\omega,d\beta)_{L^{2}(\Omega,\Lambda^{k})}
for all
\beta\in C^{1}(\overline{\Omega},\Lambda^{k-1})
, endowed with the norm of
W^{\delta,\varPhi(\cdot)}(\Omega,\Lambda^{k})
.

The space
\widetilde{W}^{\delta,\varPhi(\cdot)}_{N}(\Omega,\Lambda^{k})
is the closure of
W^{\delta,\varPhi(\cdot)}_{c}(\Omega,\Lambda^{k})
in
W^{\delta,\varPhi(\cdot)}(\Omega,\Lambda^{k})
.

The space
H^{\delta,\varPhi(\cdot)}_{N}(\Omega,\Lambda^{k})
is the closure of
C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega,\Lambda^{k})
in
W^{\delta,\varPhi(\cdot)}(\Omega,\Lambda^{k})
.

Clearly,
H^{\delta,\varPhi(\cdot)}_{N}(\Omega,\Lambda^{k})\subset\widetilde{W}^{\delta,\varPhi(\cdot)}_{N}(\Omega,\Lambda^{k})\subset W^{\delta,\varPhi(\cdot)}_{N}(\Omega,\Lambda^{k})
. A smooth
k
-form
\omega
belongs to
H^{\delta,\varPhi(\cdot)}_{N}(\Omega,\Lambda^{k})
if and only if its normal component component
n\omega
is zero on
\partial\Omega
.

The following proposition is straightforward.

Proposition 5
.

All the spaces introduced in Definitions
2
,
3
,
4
are Banach spaces.

For
\varPhi(\cdot)\equiv p\in[1,\infty]
we get the classical partial Sobolev spaces
W^{d,p}_{T}(\Omega,\Lambda^{k})
and
W^{\delta,p}_{N}(\Omega,\Lambda^{k})
with vanishing tangential (correspondingly normal) component on the boundary. The spaces
W^{d,p}_{T}(\Omega,\Lambda^{k})
and
W^{\delta,p}_{N}(\Omega,\Lambda^{k})
coincide with the closures of
C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega,\Lambda^{k})
in
W^{d,p}(\Omega,\Lambda^{k})
and
W^{\delta,p}(\Omega,\Lambda^{k})
, respectively (see
[
IL93
]
). In this case there is no difference between between
H
and
W
spaces.

If
\overline{\Omega}\Subset\Omega^{\prime}
, a form from
W^{d,\varPhi(\cdot)}(\Omega,\Lambda^{k})
or
W^{\delta,\varPhi(\cdot)}(\Omega,\Lambda^{k})
belongs to
W^{d,\varPhi(\cdot)}_{T}(\Omega,\Lambda^{k})
or
W^{\delta,\varPhi(\cdot)}_{N}(\Omega,\Lambda^{k})
, respectively, iff its extension by zero to
\Omega^{\prime}\setminus\Omega
produces an element from
W^{d,\varPhi(\cdot)}(\Omega^{\prime},\Lambda^{k})
or
W^{\delta,\varPhi(\cdot)}(\Omega^{\prime},\Lambda^{k})
, respectively.

3.
Minimization problem for non-autonomous functionals with differential forms

3.1.
Gauge fixing

Recall (for instance,
[
CDK12
, Theorem 6.5]
) the following facts regarding the harmonic forms with vanishing tangential or normal components at the boundary. Let
\mathcal{H}_{T}(\Omega,\Lambda^{k})
be the set of harmonic forms from
W^{1,2}_{T}(\Omega,\Lambda^{k})
and
\mathcal{H}_{N}(\Omega,\Lambda^{k})
be the set of harmonic forms from
W^{1,2}_{N}(\Omega,\Lambda^{k})
. The spaces
\mathcal{H}_{T}(\Omega,\Lambda^{k})
and
\mathcal{H}_{N}(\Omega,\Lambda^{k})
are finite dimensional, closed in
L^{2}(\Omega,\Lambda^{k})
, for contractible domains
\mathcal{H}_{T}(\Omega,\Lambda^{k})=\{0\}
for
0\leq k\leq N-1
and
\mathcal{H}_{N}(\Omega,\Lambda^{k})=\{0\}
for
1\leq k\leq N
. The space
\mathcal{H}_{T}(\Omega,\Lambda^{N})
is the span of
dx^{1}\wedge\ldots\wedge dx^{N}
and the space
\mathcal{H}_{N}(\Omega,\Lambda^{0})
is the span of
1
.

We need the following result on the solvability of the Cauchy-Riemann type systems for differential forms. This result is a particular case of theorems
[
CDK12
, Theorem 7.2]
for
p\geq 2
and
[
Sil16
, Theorems 2.43]
for any
p>1
, and triviality of the set of harmonic forms with zero tangential component at the boundary.

Corollary 6
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded contractible
C^{3}
domain in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
0\leq k\leq N-1
,
p>1
,
\omega_{0}\in W^{1,p}(\Omega,\Lambda^{k})
, and
\beta\in\omega_{0}+W^{d,p}_{T}(\Omega,\Lambda^{k})
. The problem

\displaystyle d\omega=d\beta,\quad\delta\omega=0\quad\text{in}\quad\Omega,

\displaystyle\nu^{\flat}\wedge\omega=\nu^{\flat}\wedge\omega_{0}\quad\text{on}\quad\partial\Omega

has a unique solution
\omega\in W^{1,p}(\Omega,\Lambda^{k})
with

\|\omega\|_{W^{1,p}(\Omega,\Lambda^{k})}\leq C\left(\|\omega_{0}\|_{W^{1,p}(\Omega,\Lambda^{k})}+\|d\beta\|_{L^{p}(\Omega,\Lambda^{k+1})}\right).

with
C=C(N,p,\Omega)
.

3.2.
Existence of minimizers

In this section
0\leq k\leq N-1
,
\Omega
is a bounded contractible
C^{3}
domain in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
\varPhi:\Omega\times[0,+\infty)\to[0,+\infty)
is a generalized Orlicz function satisfying (
2.7
),
b\in L^{\varPhi^{*}(\cdot)}(\Omega,\Lambda^{N-k-1})
. We study the existence of solutions to the following two variational problems.

(W-minimization). Let
\omega_{0}\in W^{1,\varPhi(\cdot)}(\Omega,\Lambda^{k})
and minimize
\mathcal{F}_{\varPhi,b}
over the set
\omega_{0}+W^{1,\varPhi(\cdot)}_{T}(\Omega,\Lambda^{k})
:

(3.1)

\mathcal{F}_{\varPhi,b}(\omega)=\int\limits_{\Omega}\varPhi(x,|d\omega|)\,dV+\int\limits_{\Omega}b\wedge d\omega\ \rightarrow\min,\quad\omega\in\omega_{0}+W^{1,\varPhi(\cdot)}_{T}(\Omega,\Lambda^{k}).

(H-minimization) Let
\omega_{0}\in H^{1,\varPhi(\cdot)}(\Omega,\Lambda^{k})
and minimize
\mathcal{F}_{\varPhi,b}
over the set
\omega_{0}+H^{1,\varPhi(\cdot)}_{T}(\Omega,\Lambda^{k})
:

(3.2)

\mathcal{F}_{\varPhi,b}(\omega)\rightarrow\min,\quad\omega\in\omega_{0}+H^{1,\varPhi(\cdot)}_{T}(\Omega,\Lambda^{k}).

Theorem 7
.

The variational problem (
3.1
) has a minimizer
\omega\in\omega_{0}+W^{d,\varPhi(\cdot)}_{T}(\Omega,\Lambda^{k})
with
\delta\omega=0
.

Proof.

Let
\omega_{s}
be a minimizing sequence, clearly

\displaystyle{\lVert{d\omega_{s}}\rVert}_{L^{\varPhi(\cdot)}(\Omega,\Lambda^{k+1})}\leq c.

Due to the coercitivity condition (
1.2
) we have

\displaystyle{\lVert{d\omega_{s}}\rVert}_{L^{p_{-}}(\Omega,\Lambda^{k+1})}\leq c.

By Corollary
6
there exists
\alpha_{s}\in\omega_{0}+W^{1,p_{-}}_{T}(\Omega,\Lambda^{k})
satisfying
d\alpha_{s}=d\omega_{s}
and
\delta\alpha_{s}=0
in
\Omega

such that

\displaystyle{\lVert{\alpha_{s}}\rVert}_{W^{1,p_{-}}(\Omega,\Lambda^{k})}\leq c.

Clearly
\alpha_{s}\in W^{d,\varPhi(\cdot)}(\Omega,\Lambda^{k})
and

\displaystyle{\lVert{\alpha_{s}}\rVert}_{W^{d,\varPhi(\cdot)}(\Omega,\Lambda^{k})}\leq c.

The sequence
\alpha_{s}
is bounded in the space
W^{d,\varPhi(\cdot)}(\Omega,\Lambda^{k})\cap W^{1,p_{-}}(\Omega,\Lambda^{k})
endowed with the norm which is the sum of norms in
W^{d,\varPhi(\cdot)}(\Omega,\Lambda^{k})
and
W^{1,p_{-}}(\Omega,\Lambda^{k})
. Its dual space is separable, therefore there exists

\alpha\in\omega_{0}+W^{d,\varPhi(\cdot)}_{T}(\Omega,\Lambda^{k})\cap W^{1,p_{-}}(\Omega,\Lambda^{k})

such that
\delta\alpha=0
and up to the subsequence,

\displaystyle\alpha_{s}\to\alpha\quad\text{in }\quad L^{p_{-}}(\Omega,\Lambda^{k}),

\displaystyle d\alpha_{s}\rightharpoonup d\alpha\quad\text{in }\quad L^{\varPhi(\cdot)}(\Omega,\Lambda^{k+1}).

Due to the lower-semicontinuity of
\mathcal{F}
, which follows from the convexity of
\varPhi(x,\cdot)
and Mazur’s lemma, we have

\displaystyle\liminf_{s\to\infty}\int\limits_{\Omega}\varPhi(x,|d\alpha_{s}|)\,dV\geq\int\limits_{\Omega}\varPhi(x,|d\alpha|)\,dV.

Since in the linear part we have convergence, the proof is complete.

∎

Theorem 8
.

Let
\omega_{0}
in
H^{1,\varPhi(\cdot)}(\Omega,\Lambda^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{N}))
. Then the problem (
3.2
) has a minimizer
\omega\in\omega_{0}+H^{d,\varPhi(\cdot)}_{T}(\Omega,\Lambda^{k})
with
\delta\omega=0
.

Proof.

We keep the notation from the proof of Theorem
7
. Let
\omega_{s}=\omega_{0}+\gamma_{s}
,
\gamma_{s}\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega,\Lambda^{k})
, be a minimizing sequence, clearly

{\lVert{d\omega_{s}}\rVert}_{L^{\varPhi(\cdot)}(\Omega,\Lambda^{k+1})},\ {\lVert{d\gamma_{s}}\rVert}_{L^{\varPhi(\cdot)}(\Omega,\Lambda^{k+1})}\leq c.

Due to the coercitivity condition (
1.2
) we have

{\lVert{d\omega_{s}}\rVert}_{L^{p_{-}}(\Omega,\Lambda^{k+1})},\ {\lVert{d\gamma_{s}}\rVert}_{L^{p_{-}}(\Omega,\Lambda^{k+1})}\leq c.

By Corollary
6
there exists
\alpha_{s}\in\omega_{0}+W^{1,p_{-}}_{T}(\Omega,\Lambda^{k})
satisfying
d\alpha_{s}=d\omega_{s}
and
\delta\alpha_{s}=0
in
\Omega

such that

{\lVert{\alpha_{s}}\rVert}_{W^{1,p_{-}}(\Omega,\Lambda^{k})}\leq c.

Writing
\alpha_{s}=\omega_{0}+\beta_{s}
, one gets
\beta_{s}\in W^{1,p_{-}}_{T}(\Omega,\Lambda^{k})
satisfying
d\beta_{s}=d\gamma_{s}
,
\delta\beta_{s}=-\delta\omega_{0}
. Extend
\beta_{s}
to
\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega
by zero.

Clearly
\alpha_{s}\in W^{d,\varPhi(\cdot)}(\Omega,\Lambda^{k})
and

{\lVert{\alpha_{s}}\rVert}_{W^{d,\varPhi(\cdot)}(\Omega,\Lambda^{k})},\ {\lVert{\beta_{s}}\rVert}_{W^{d,\varPhi(\cdot)}(\Omega,\Lambda^{k})}\leq c.

Let
\varphi_{t}:\Omega\times[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
t\in(0,1)
, be
C^{2}
mapping such that
\varphi_{1}=\mathrm{id}
, and
\varphi_{t}^{-1}\Omega\Subset\Omega
. If
\Omega
is a ball centered at the origin, one takes
\varphi_{t}(x)=x/t
. Consider the pullbacks
\varphi_{t}^{*}\beta_{s}
. These forms have compact support in
\Omega
, with
d\varphi_{t}^{*}\beta_{s}
uniformly converging to
d\beta_{s}=d\gamma_{s}\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega,\Lambda^{k+1})
and
\delta\varphi_{t}^{*}\beta_{s}

-\delta\omega_{0}
in

as

\varphi_{t}^{*}\beta_{s}
converges to
\beta_{s}
in

as

.

, where

,

with

, converge to
\varphi_{t}^{*}\beta_{s}
in

,

,

Therefore, keeping the same notation for

while replacing it by

for appropriate

Therefore there exists

such that

and up to the subsequence,

\displaystyle\beta_{s}\to\beta\quad\text{in }\quad W^{1,p_{-}}(\Omega,\Lambda^{k}),

\displaystyle d\beta_{s}\rightharpoonup d\beta\quad\text{in }\quad L^{\varPhi(\cdot)}(\Omega,\Lambda^{k+1}).

Due to the convexity of

and for

\displaystyle\liminf_{s\to\infty}\int\limits_{\Omega}\varPhi(x,|d\alpha_{s}|)\,dV\geq\int\limits_{\Omega}\varPhi(x,|d\alpha|)\,dV.

Since in the linear part we have convergence, the proof is complete.

∎

4.
Lavrentiev gap and non-density

In this section we design the general framework for the construction of the examples on Lavrentiev gap. We introduce the set of assumptions for the examples in the Section
4.1
and show how to obtain non-density of smooth functions and the special type of the non-uniqueness of the minimisers under these assumptions.
In Section
4.2
we introduce basic forms which will be building blocks of our examples. These building blocks correspond to the one saddle-point geometry of the classical checkerboard Zhikov example and are then used in Sections
4.3
and
4.4
to construct more advanced examples using fractal Cantor barriers. The results are summarised in the subsection
4.5
.

4.1.
Separating pairs of forms and separating functionals

Here we present some “conditional” statements. We shall use two assumptions. Let
\Omega
be a domain in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
with sufficiently regular boundary,
k\in\{1,\ldots,N-1\}
, and
{\mathfrak{S}}\subset\Omega
be a closed set of zero Lebesgue
N
-measure. Our argument will be based upon defining a suitable set
{\mathfrak{S}}
and
(k-1)
-form
u
and
(N-k-1)
-form
A
, which are smooth in
\Omega\setminus{\mathfrak{S}}
and give a “counterexample” to the Stokes theorem. The regularity of
\partial\Omega
is assumed to be such that the classical Stokes theorem holds. Further
\Omega
will be either cube of ball in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
.

Let
\varPhi:\Omega\times[0,+\infty)\to[0,+\infty)
be a generalized Orlicz function.

Definition 9
.

We say that a pair of
(k-1)
-form and
(N-k-1)
-form
(u,A)
defined in
\Omega
is
(\varPhi,k)
-separating if there exists a closed set
{\mathfrak{S}}\subset\Omega
of zero Lebesgue
N
-measure such that

(i)

u
and
A
are regular outside
{\mathfrak{S}}
;

(ii)

u\in W^{d,1}(\Omega,\Lambda^{k-1})
and
A\in W^{d,1}(\Omega,\Lambda^{N-k-1})
;

(iii)

\int\limits_{\partial\Omega}A\wedge du=1
;

(iv)

|du|\cdot|dA|=0\quad\text{in }\quad\Omega\setminus{\mathfrak{S}}
;

(v)

\int\limits_{\Omega}\varPhi(x,|du|)\,dV<\infty
and
\int\limits_{\Omega}\varPhi^{*}(x,|dA|)\,dV<\infty
.

When invoking a pair of
(\varPhi,k)
-separating forms we assume that the set
{\mathfrak{S}}
comes from this definition and when necessary denote it by
{\mathfrak{S}}(u,A)
.

The essential property of
(\varPhi,k)
-separating forms is that
A\wedge du
“contradicts” the Stokes theorem. Indeed, disregarding the singular set
\Sigma
we would arrive at

0=\int\limits_{\Omega}dA\wedge du=\int\limits_{\Omega}d(A\wedge du)=\int\limits_{\partial\Omega}A\wedge du=1.

Definition 10
.

Let
u
and
A
be a pair of
(\varPhi,k)
-separating forms and
\eta\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega)
with
\eta=1
in a neighbourhood of
{\mathfrak{S}}
.
Set

\displaystyle u^{\circ}

\displaystyle=\eta u,

\displaystyle u^{\partial}

\displaystyle=(1-\eta)u,

\displaystyle A^{\circ}

\displaystyle=\eta A,

\displaystyle A^{\partial}

\displaystyle=(1-\eta)A.

On
W^{d,\varPhi(\cdot)}(\Omega,\Lambda^{k-1})
we define the functionals
\mathcal{S}
,
\mathcal{S}^{\circ}
, and
\mathcal{S}^{\partial}
by

\mathcal{S}(w):=\int\limits_{\Omega}dA\wedge dw,\quad\mathcal{S}^{\circ}(w):=\int\limits_{\Omega}dA^{\circ}\wedge dw,\quad\mathcal{S}^{\partial}(w):=\int\limits_{\Omega}dA^{\partial}\wedge dw.

Proposition 11

(Separating functional)
.

The following holds

(a)

\mathcal{S},\mathcal{S}^{\circ},\mathcal{S}^{\partial}
define linear functionals on
W^{d,\varPhi(\cdot)}(\Omega,\Lambda^{k-1})
.

(b)

For all
w\in H^{d,\varPhi(\cdot)}(\Omega,\Lambda^{k-1})
we have
S^{\circ}(w)=0
.

(c)

For the functions
u,u^{\partial},u^{\circ}
it holds:

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}(u)

\displaystyle=0,\quad\mathcal{S}(u^{\partial})=1,\quad\mathcal{S}(u^{\circ})=-1,

\displaystyle\mathcal{S^{\partial}}(u)

\displaystyle=1,\quad\mathcal{S^{\partial}}(u^{\partial})=1,\quad\mathcal{S^{\partial}}(u^{\circ})=0,

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}^{\circ}(u)

\displaystyle=-1,\quad\mathcal{S}^{\circ}(u^{\partial})=0,\quad\mathcal{S}^{\circ}(u^{\circ})=-1.

Proof.

The first claim follows from
dA\in L^{\varPhi^{*}}(\Omega,\Lambda^{N-k})
. Due to the Stokes theorem and by approximation for all
\omega\in H^{d,\varPhi(\cdot)}(\Omega,\Lambda^{k-1})
it holds

\displaystyle\int\limits_{\Omega}dA^{\circ}\wedge d\omega=\int\limits_{\partial\Omega}A^{\circ}\wedge d\omega=0.

Now
du\wedge dA=0
almost everywhere, therefore
\mathcal{S}(u)=0
.
Since

u^{\partial}\in C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega},\Lambda^{k-1})
and
A^{\partial}\in C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega},\Lambda^{N-k-1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}))
, we can use the Stokes theorem and the third property of
(\varPhi,k)
-separating pair to obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}^{\partial}(u^{\partial})

\displaystyle=\int\limits_{\Omega}dA^{\partial}\wedge du^{\partial}=\int\limits_{\partial\Omega}A^{\partial}\wedge du^{\partial}=\int\limits_{\partial\Omega}A\wedge du=1.

Since
A^{\circ}\wedge du^{\partial}
belongs to
C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega,\Lambda^{N-1})
, and
d(A^{\circ}\wedge du^{\partial})=dA^{\circ}\wedge du^{\partial}
, again by the Stokes theorem we get

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}^{\circ}(u^{\partial})=\int_{\Omega}dA^{\circ}\wedge du^{\partial}=0.

Analogously, we obtain
\mathcal{S}^{\partial}(u^{\circ})=0
. Now,

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}^{\circ}(u^{\circ})

\displaystyle=\mathcal{S}(u)-\mathcal{S}^{\partial}(u^{\partial})-\mathcal{S}^{\circ}(u^{\partial})-\mathcal{S}^{\partial}(u^{\circ})=0-1-0-0=-1.

This proves the claim.
∎

Corollary 12
.

If there exists a pair of
(\varPhi,k)
-separating forms then

H^{d,\varPhi(\cdot)}(\Omega,\Lambda^{k-1})\neq W^{d,\varPhi(\cdot)}(\Omega,\Lambda^{k-1}).

Proof.

By Proposition
11
,
\mathcal{S}^{\circ}=0
on
H^{d,\varPhi(\cdot)}(\Omega,\Lambda^{k-1})
. On the other hand,
u\in W^{d,\varPhi(\cdot)}(\Omega,\Lambda^{k-1})
and
\mathcal{S}^{\circ}(u)=-1
.
∎

Corollary 13
.

If there exists a pair of
(\varPhi,k)
-separating forms then

H^{d,\varPhi(\cdot)}_{T}(\Omega,\Lambda^{k-1})\neq\widetilde{W}^{d,\varPhi(\cdot)}_{T}(\Omega,\Lambda^{k-1}).

Proof.

For any
\varphi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega,\Lambda^{k-1})
by the Stokes theorem we have

\mathcal{S}(\varphi)=\int\limits_{\Omega}dA\wedge d\varphi=\int\limits_{\partial\Omega}A\wedge d\varphi=0.

On the other hand,
u^{\circ}\in\widetilde{W}^{d,\varPhi(\cdot)}_{T}(\Omega,\Lambda^{k-1})
and by Proposition
11
we have
\mathcal{S}(u^{\circ})=-1
.
∎

Theorem 14

(Lavrentiev gap)
.

If there exists a pair
(u,A)
of
(\varPhi,k)
-separating forms then for
b=dA^{\circ}
the functional

\mathcal{F}_{\varPhi,b}(w)=\int\limits_{\Omega}\varPhi(x,|dw|)\,dV+\mathcal{S}^{\circ}(w)=\int\limits_{\Omega}\varPhi(x,|dw|)\,dV+\int\limits_{\Omega}b\wedge dw

satisfies

\inf\mathcal{F}(W^{d,\varPhi(\cdot)}_{c}(\Omega,\Lambda^{k-1}))<\inf\mathcal{F}(C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega,\Lambda^{k-1}))

and as a corollary

\inf\mathcal{F}(W^{d,\varPhi(\cdot)}_{T}(\Omega,\Lambda^{k-1}))<\inf\mathcal{F}(H^{d,\varPhi(\cdot)}_{T}(\Omega,\Lambda^{k-1})).

Proof.

By Proposition
11
and nonnegativity of
\varPhi
,
\mathcal{F}_{\varPhi,b}(w)\geq 0
for all
w\in H^{d,\varPhi(\cdot)}_{T}(\Omega,\Lambda^{k-1})
. On the other hand, for
t>0
, using Proposition
11
and (
2.9
), we have

\mathcal{F}_{\varPhi,b}(tu^{\circ})=\int\limits_{\Omega}\varPhi(x,t|du^{\circ}|)\,dV-t\leq ct^{s}-t

with some
s>1
. This implies
\mathcal{F}_{\varPhi,b}(tu^{\circ})<0
for sufficiently small
t
. ∎

Now we discuss the Dirichlet problem. First, we repeat certain result from
[
BDS20
]
. Let

\mathcal{F}(\omega)=\int\limits_{\Omega}\varPhi(x,|d\omega|)\,dV,\quad\mathcal{F}^{*}(g):=\int_{\Omega}\varPhi^{*}(x,{\lvert{g(x)}\rvert})\,dV.

Let
(u,A)
be a
(\varPhi,k)
-separating pair. Denote
b=dA
.

Assumption 15
.

There exist
s,t>0
such that
\mathcal{F}(tu)+\mathcal{F}^{*}(sb)<ts
.

Theorem 16

(H-harmonic
\neq
W-harmonic)
.

Under Assumption
15
, for

\displaystyle w_{t}

\displaystyle=\mathop{\mathrm{arg\,min}}\mathcal{F}\big(tu^{\partial}+W_{0}^{1,\varPhi(\cdot)}(\Omega,\Lambda^{k-1})\big)\qquad

\displaystyle h_{t}

\displaystyle=\mathop{\mathrm{arg\,min}}\mathcal{F}\big(tu^{\partial}+C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega,\Lambda^{k-1})\big)

we have
w_{t}\neq h_{t}
and
\mathcal{F}(w_{t})<\mathcal{F}(h_{t})
.

Proof.

Set
b=dA
. We have
tu=tu^{\partial}+tu^{\circ}\in tu^{\partial}+W_{0}^{1,\varPhi(\cdot)}(\Omega,\Lambda^{k-1})
. Thus,

(4.1)

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}(w_{t})

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{F}(tu).

By the properties of the Hodge dual and the Young inequality,

sb\wedge dh_{t}=s(*b,dh_{t})\leq s|*b|\cdot|dh_{t}|=s|b|\cdot|dh_{t}|\leq\varPhi(x,{\lvert{dh_{t}}\rvert})+\varPhi^{*}(x,s{\lvert{b}\rvert}).

Hence

\mathcal{F}(h_{t})=\int_{\Omega}\varPhi(x,{\lvert{dh_{t}}\rvert})\,dx\geq s\int_{\Omega}b\wedge dh_{t}-\int_{\Omega}\varPhi^{*}(x,s{\lvert{b}\rvert})\,dx=s\,\mathcal{S}(h_{t})-\mathcal{F}^{*}(sb).

See
[
IKKS04
]
for estimates of exterior product submultiplication constant.

Since
h_{t}-tu^{\partial}\in H_{0}^{d,\varPhi(\cdot)}(\Omega)
, we have

\mathcal{S}(h_{t}-tu^{\partial})=0
by
Proposition
11
. This and

\mathcal{S}(u^{\partial})=1
by the same Proposition imply

(4.2)

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}(h_{t})

\displaystyle=s\,\mathcal{S}(tu^{\partial})-\mathcal{F}^{*}(sb)=ts-\mathcal{F}^{*}(sb).

Combining (
4.1
) and (
4.2
) we get

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}(h_{t})-\mathcal{F}(w_{t})\geq ts-\mathcal{F}(tu)-\mathcal{F}^{*}(sb)

for all
t,s>0
. By Assumption
15
the right hand-side of last inequality is positive, and thus
\mathcal{F}(h_{t})>\mathcal{F}(w_{t})
. This proves the claim.
∎

4.2.
Basic forms

In this section we introduce differential forms which will be building blocks of our examples. We do necessary calculations in the cubic setting, where the boundary orientation is straightforward.

Let
k\in\{1,\ldots,N-1\}
. Define two groups of variables
\bar{x}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k})
and
\hat{x}=(x_{k+1},\ldots,x_{N})
. Let
\Gamma_{l}(x)
,
x\in\mathbb{R}^{l}
, denote the fundamental solution of the Laplace equation in
\mathbb{R}^{l}
with pole at the origin:

\Gamma_{l}(x)=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{2}|x|,\quad&l=1,\\
-\frac{1}{2\pi}\ln\frac{1}{|x|},\quad&l=2,\\
-\frac{1}{(l-2)\sigma_{l}}|x|^{2-l},\quad&l>2.\end{cases}

Here and below
\sigma_{l}
denotes the surface area (
(l-1)
-volume) of the unit sphere in
\mathbb{R}^{l}
, and
|x|
denotes the standard Euclidian norm of
x
.

Let
\theta:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
be a smooth increasing function such that

\theta(t)=1\quad\text{for}\quad t\geq\frac{1}{2},\quad\theta(t)=0\quad\text{for}\quad t\leq\frac{1}{4},\quad|\theta^{\prime}|\leq 4.

Let
\eta:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
be a smooth increasing function such that

\eta(t)=t\quad\text{for}\quad t\leq\frac{1}{4},\quad\eta(t)=\frac{1}{2}\quad\text{for}\quad t\geq\frac{3}{4},\quad\eta^{\prime\prime}(t)\leq 0.

Our basic forms are

(4.3)

\displaystyle u=\theta\left(\sqrt{N}\frac{|\hat{x}|}{\eta(|\bar{x}|)}\right)\ast_{\hat{x}}d\Gamma_{N-k}(\hat{x}),

(4.4)

\displaystyle A=\theta\left(\sqrt{N}\frac{|\bar{x}|}{\eta(|\hat{x}|)}\right)\ast_{\bar{x}}d\Gamma_{k}(\bar{x}),

Here
\ast_{\hat{x}}
and
\ast_{\bar{x}}
are applied only within respective variables, that is

\displaystyle\ast_{\hat{x}}d\Gamma_{N-k}(\hat{x})=\frac{1}{\sigma_{N-k}}\sum_{j=k+1}^{N}(-1)^{j-k-1}\frac{x_{j}}{|\hat{x}|^{N-k}}dx_{k+1}\wedge\ldots\wedge\widehat{dx_{j}}\wedge\ldots\wedge dx_{N},

\displaystyle\ast_{\bar{x}}d\Gamma_{k}(\bar{x})=\frac{1}{\sigma_{k}}\sum_{j=1}^{k}(-1)^{j-1}\frac{x_{j}}{|\bar{x}|^{k}}dx_{1}\wedge\ldots\wedge\widehat{dx_{j}}\wedge\ldots\wedge dx_{k}.

Further for
(N-k-1)
-form

from (

) and
(k-1)
-form

from (
4.4
)we use the notation

and

. Also, in this case we denote
{\mathfrak{C}}=\{0\}^{k}\subset\mathbb{R}^{k}
,
{\mathfrak{S}}=\{0\}^{k}\times\{0\}^{N-k}\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
, and this pair of forms is denoted by

,

The following facts are straightforward.

Proposition 17
.

Both
\ast_{\hat{x}}d\Gamma_{N-k}(\hat{x})
and
\ast_{\bar{x}}d\Gamma_{k}(\bar{x})
are harmonic

\displaystyle d(\ast_{\hat{x}}d\Gamma_{N-k}(\hat{x}))=0,\quad\delta(\ast_{\hat{x}}d\Gamma_{N-k}(\hat{x}))=0,

\displaystyle d(\ast_{\bar{x}}d\Gamma_{k}(\bar{x}))=0,\quad\delta(\ast_{\bar{x}}d\Gamma_{k}(\bar{x}))=0

outside

and
\bar{x}=0
correspondingly. For cubes
(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)^{N-k}\subset\mathbb{R}^{N-k}
and

where the natural induced orientations of the boundary are assumed.

Proposition 18
.

For the forms
u
and
A
given by (
4.3
) and (
4.4
)

(a)

There holds

\{u\neq 0\}\subset\{|\hat{x}|>\eta(|\bar{x}|)/(4\sqrt{N})\},\quad\{A\neq 0\}\subset\{|\bar{x}|>\eta(|\hat{x}|)/(4\sqrt{N})\}.

(b)

The forms

and

For any bounded domain

(4.6)

\begin{gathered}|du|\lesssim|\hat{x}|^{k-N},\quad|dA|\lesssim|\bar{x}|^{-k},\\

(c)

(d)

For a nonnegative function

\int\limits_{[-1,1]^{N}}F(|\hat{x}|,|du|)\,dV\lesssim\int\limits_{0}^{\sqrt{N}}F\left(t,t^{k-N}\right)t^{N-1}\,dt.

For a nonnegative function

with nonnegative arguments, satisfying

(4.8)

\int\limits_{[-1,1]^{N}}G(|\hat{x}|,|dA|)\,dV\lesssim\int\limits_{0}^{\sqrt{N}}G\bigl(t,t^{-k}\bigr)\,t^{N-1}\,dt.

Proof.

The first two statements follow from the definition of

4.3.
Generalized Cantor sets and their properties.

In this section we construct (generalized) Cantor sets.

Let
l_{j}
,
j=0,1,2,\ldots
be a decreasing sequence of positive numbers starting from
l_{0}=1
:

1=l_{0}>l_{1}>l_{2}>\ldots

such that
l_{j-1}>2l_{j}
for all
j\in\mathbb{N}
. We start from
I_{0,1}=[-1/2,1/2]
. On each
m
-th step we we remove the open middle third of length
l_{j}-2l_{j+1}
from the interval
I_{m,j}
,
j=1,\ldots,2^{m}
to obtain the next generation set of closed intervals
I_{m+1,j}
,
j=1,\ldots,2^{m+1}
. The union of the closed intervals
I_{m,j}=[a_{m,j},b_{m,j}]
,
j=1,\ldots,2^{m}
of length
l_{m}
from the same generation forms the pre-Cantor set
C_{m}=\bigcup_{j=1}^{2^{m}}I_{m,j}
. The Cantor set
{\mathfrak{C}}=\cap_{m=0}^{\infty}C_{m}
is the intersection of all pre-Cantor sets
C_{m}
.

On each
m
-th step we define the pre-Cantor measure as
\mu_{m}=|C_{m}|^{-1}\mathbbm{1}_{C_{m}}
, where
|C_{m}|=2^{m}l_{m}
is the standard Lebesgue measure of
C_{m}
, and the weak limit of the measures
\mu_{m}
is the Cantor measure corresponding to
{\mathfrak{C}}
.

We require further that

l_{m-1}-2l_{m}>l_{m}-2l_{m+1}\Leftrightarrow l_{m+1}>\frac{3l_{m}-l_{m-1}}{2},

at least for all sufficiently large
m
.

If the sequence
l_{j}
satisfies the conditions above only for sufficiently large
j\geq j_{0}
, then we modify it by taking the sequence
\widetilde{l}_{j}=l_{j+j_{0}}(l_{j_{0}})^{-1}
,
j=0,1,2,\ldots

For
k\in\mathbb{N}
by
{\mathfrak{C}}^{k}
and
\mu^{k}
we denote the Cartesian powers of
k
copies of
{\mathfrak{C}}
and its corresponding Cantor measure, respectively.

Definition
. (
Generalized Cantor sets
). Let
l_{j}=\lambda^{j}j^{\gamma}
,
\lambda\in(0,1/2)
,
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
. We denote the corresponding Cantor set by
{\mathfrak{C}}_{\lambda,\gamma}
, the Cartesian product of its
k
copies is
{\mathfrak{C}}^{k}_{\lambda,\gamma}
. For
{\mathfrak{C}}^{k}_{\lambda,\gamma}
we denote
{\mathfrak{D}}=-k\ln 2/\ln\lambda
, so that
\lambda^{{\mathfrak{D}}}=2^{-k}
. We denote the Cantor measure corresponding to
{\mathfrak{C}}_{\lambda,\gamma}
by
\mu_{\lambda,\gamma}
and its
k
-th Cartesian power by
\mu^{k}_{\lambda,\gamma}
.

Definition
. (
Meager Cantor sets
). Let
l_{j}=\exp(-2^{j/\gamma})
,
\gamma>0
. Denote the corresponding Cantor set by
{\mathfrak{C}}_{0,\gamma}
, and its Cartesian products by
{\mathfrak{C}}^{k}_{0,\gamma}
. For these sets we denote
{\mathfrak{D}}=0
. We denote the corresponding Cantor measures by
\mu^{k}_{0,\gamma}
.

Denote
{\mathfrak{C}}_{t}=\{\mathrm{dist}(\bar{x},{\mathfrak{C}})<t\}
, where
{\mathfrak{C}}
is one of
{\mathfrak{C}}_{\lambda,\gamma}^{k}
or
{\mathfrak{C}}^{k}_{0,\gamma}
defined above. Denote by
d_{\infty}(\bar{x},{\mathfrak{C}})
the distance from
\bar{x}
to
{\mathfrak{C}}
in the maximum norm and let
{\mathfrak{C}}_{*,t}=\{d_{\infty}(\bar{x},{\mathfrak{C}})<t\}
. It is clear that
{\mathfrak{C}}_{t}\subset{\mathfrak{C}}_{*,t}
. Let
|F|_{k}
denote the standard Lebesgue
k
-measure of
F\subset\mathbb{R}^{k}
. In the following lemma
B^{\bar{x}}_{t}
is the open ball in
\mathbb{R}^{k}
with center at
\bar{x}
and radius
t
.

Lemma 21
.

We have

(4.11)

|({\mathfrak{C}}^{k}_{\lambda,\gamma})_{t}|_{k}\lesssim t^{k-{\mathfrak{D}}}(\ln t^{-1})^{\gamma{\mathfrak{D}}},\quad\mu_{\lambda,\gamma}(B^{\bar{x}}_{t})\lesssim t^{\mathfrak{D}}(\ln t^{-1})^{-\gamma{\mathfrak{D}}},

and

(4.12)

|({\mathfrak{C}}^{k}_{0,\gamma})_{t}|_{k}\lesssim t^{k}(\ln t^{-1})^{\gamma{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}k}},\quad\mu_{0,\gamma}(B^{\bar{x}}_{t})\lesssim(\ln t^{-1})^{-\gamma{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}k}}.

Proof.

Let
l_{j}
be the sequence of interval lengthes defining the corresponding Cantor set. Let
t\in(l_{j}/2-l_{j+1},l_{j-1}/2-l_{j})
. The set
{\mathfrak{C}}_{*,t}
consists of
2^{kj}
identical cubes of the form

|\bar{x}-\bar{x}_{j,s}|_{\infty}<\frac{l_{j}}{2}+t.

So

|{\mathfrak{C}}_{*,t}|_{k}\leq 2^{k(j-1)}(l_{j}+2t)^{k}.

First consider the case
{\mathfrak{C}}={\mathfrak{C}}^{k}_{\lambda,\gamma}
with
\lambda>0
. Then
l_{j}+2t\leq l_{j-1}-l_{j}\leq ct
with some constant
c
independent of
j
, so

|{\mathfrak{C}}_{t}|_{k}\leq|{\mathfrak{C}}_{*,t}|_{k}\lesssim 2^{kj}t^{k}.

Recalling that
{\mathfrak{D}}=-k\ln 2/\ln\lambda
and
\lambda^{{\mathfrak{D}}}=2^{-k}
, we get

2^{kj}=\lambda^{-j{\mathfrak{D}}}=l_{j}^{-{\mathfrak{D}}}j^{\gamma{\mathfrak{D}}}\eqsim l_{j}^{-{\mathfrak{D}}}(\ln(1/l_{j}))^{\gamma{\mathfrak{D}}}\eqsim t^{-{\mathfrak{D}}}(\ln t^{-1})^{\gamma{\mathfrak{D}}}.

Thus we arrive at the first inequality in (
4.11
).

Now consider the case
{\mathfrak{C}}={\mathfrak{C}}^{k}_{0,\gamma}
(ultrathin Cantor sets). Then we get

2^{kj}=\left(\ln\frac{1}{l_{j}}\right)^{\gamma k}\approx\left(\ln\frac{1}{t}\right)^{\gamma k}

and this yields the second inequality from (
4.11
).

Now let us estimate
\mu(B^{\bar{x}}_{r})
. Any interval of length
2t
with
t\in(l_{j}/2-l_{j+1},l_{j-1}/2-l_{j})
can intersect at most one interval forming the
j
-th iteration of the pre-Cantor set. Since
B^{\bar{x}}_{t}
lies within a cube with edge
2t
, then
\mu_{\lambda,\gamma}^{k}(B^{\bar{x}}_{t})\leq 2^{-jk}
. Using the above estimates for
2^{jk}
we arrive at (
4.12
). The proof of Lemma
21
is complete.

∎

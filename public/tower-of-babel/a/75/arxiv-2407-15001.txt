Integral and hypergeometric representations for multiple orthogonal polynomials
Amílcar Branquinho 1, Juan EF Díaz 2, Ana Foulquié-Moreno 3, Manuel Mañas 4, Thomas Wolfs 5

Abstract
. This paper addresses two primary objectives in the realm of classical multiple orthogonal polynomials with an arbitrary number of weights. Firstly, it establishes new and explicit hypergeometric expressions for type I Hahn multiple orthogonal polynomials. Secondly, applying the residue theorem and the Mellin transform, the paper derives contour integral representations for several families of orthogonal polynomials. Specifically, it presents contour integral formulas for both type I and type II multiple orthogonal polynomials in the Laguerre of the first kind, Jacobi–Piñeiro, and Hahn families. The evaluation of these integrals leads to explicit hypergeometric representations.

1.
Introduction

The first four coauthors embarked on a program to find explicit hypergeometric expressions for both the continuous and discrete versions of classical multiple orthogonal polynomials. Initially, in
[
5
]
, they identified all families under the Hahn polynomials in the multiple Askey scheme, except for the Hermite family, for the more simple case of two weights. This included the Jacobi–Piñeiro, Laguerre of the first and second kinds, Meixner of the first and second kinds, Kravchuk, and Charlier polynomials. The focus then shifted to an arbitrary number of weights. Formulas for Jacobi–Piñeiro and Laguerre of the first kind were presented in
[
6
]
, while those for Laguerre of the second kind and Hermite multiple orthogonal polynomials of type I were given in
[
4
]
. Notably, explicit expressions for type I Hermite multiple orthogonal polynomials were previously unknown even for two weights. Additionally, the recursion coefficients for all these cases were determined explicitly.

However, finding the Hahn multiple orthogonal polynomials of type I for an arbitrary number of weights proved elusive for the initial group of coauthors. A recent shift in perspective led to the breakthrough presented in this paper: for the first time, explicit hypergeometric expressions for the type I Hahn polynomials are provided.

Simultaneously, the last coauthor, as part of his PhD thesis supervised by Walter Van Assche, developed a contour integral technique based on the residue theorem and the Mellin transform to derive families of multiple orthogonal polynomials of types I and II
[
13
,
14
]
. Using this technique, he independently found contour integral representations for the Hahn multiple orthogonal polynomials that led to the same hypergeometric expressions for the type I Hahn polynomials. It also enabled him to obtain hypergeometric expressions for the type II Hahn polynomials different than those in
[
3
]
.

At the Arno Kuijlaars 60th anniversary meeting in Leuven, discussions among several coauthors revealed that had independently arrived at the same novel result using different perspectives and methods. Encouraged by Professor Van Assche, we decided to combine these developments and present both techniques side by side.

The paper is structured as follows.
We conclude this introduction by presenting some preliminary facts essential for the development of this study. Specifically, we discuss fundamentals of multiple orthogonal polynomials, hypergeometric-type series, the Mellin transform, and certain aspects related to the Askey scheme in the context of multiple orthogonal polynomials.
Section
2
focuses on the motivation and proof of one of the main results of this paper, Theorem
2.1
. This result provides for the first time explicit hypergeometric expressions for the type I Hahn multiple orthogonal polynomials.
In Section
3
, we
apply
the residue theorem and the Mellin transform to derive, in Theorems
3.14
and
3.19
, contour integral expressions for the Laguerre of the first kind, Jacobi–Piñeiro, and Hahn multiple orthogonal polynomials of both types I and II. These integral representations constitute another key result of the paper and lead to the hypergeometric explicit representations discussed earlier.

1.1.
Multiple Orthogonal Polynomials

For the upcoming discussion, we require some basics about discrete multiple orthogonal polynomials
[
7
, Chapter 23.6]
, see also
[
9
]
. We begin by examining a system characterized by an arbitrary number, say
p
, of weight functions, denoted as

\displaystyle w_{1},\ldots,w_{p}:\Delta\subseteq\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}.

In certain cases, a sequence of type II polynomials
B_{\vec{n}}
may exist, which are monic with
\deg{B_{\vec{n}}}\leqslant|\vec{n}|
, satisfying the orthogonality relations

(1)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \int_{\Delta}x^{j}B_{\vec{n}}(x)w_{i}(x)\,\operatorname{d}x&=0,&j&\in\{0,\ldots,n_{i}-1\},&i&\in\{1,\ldots,p\}.\end{aligned}

Additionally, there are
p
sequences of type I polynomials, denoted as
A^{(1)}_{\vec{n}},\ldots,A^{(p)}_{\vec{n}}
, with
\deg A^{(i)}_{\vec{n}}\leqslant n_{i}-1
, meeting the orthogonality conditions

(2)

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{p}\int_{\Delta}x^{j}A^{(i)}_{\vec{n}}(x)w_{i}(x)\,\operatorname{d}x=\begin{cases}0,&\text{if }j\in\{0,\ldots,|\vec{n}|-2\},\\
1,&\text{if }j=|\vec{n}|-1.\end{cases}

In this context,
\vec{n}=(n_{1},\ldots,n_{p})\in\mathbb{N}^{p}_{0}
and
|\vec{n}|\coloneqq n_{1}+\cdots+n_{p}
. For this paper, we will use the notation
\mathbb{N}\coloneqq\{1,2,3,\ldots\}
and
\mathbb{N}_{0}\coloneqq\{0\}\cup\mathbb{N}
.

These conditions imply that the polynomials are uniquely defined and satisfy the biorthogonality conditions:

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{p}\int_{\Delta}B_{\vec{n}}(x)A^{(i)}_{\vec{m}}(x)w_{i}(x)\,\operatorname{d}x=\begin{cases}0,&\text{if }m_{i}\leqslant n_{i},\;i\in\{1,\ldots,p\},\\
1,&\text{if }|\vec{m}|=|\vec{n}|+1,\\
0,&\text{if }|\vec{n}|+1<|\vec{m}|.\end{cases}

Analogously, we can consider a system of discrete weight functions
w_{1},\ldots,w_{p}:\Delta\subseteq\mathbb{Z}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{\geq}
, leading to the discrete orthogonality relations

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\Delta}k^{j}B_{\vec{n}}(k)w_{i}(k)

\displaystyle=0,\quad j\in\{0,\ldots,n_{i}-1\},\quad i\in\{1,\ldots,p\},

(3)

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{p}\sum_{k\in\Delta}k^{j}A^{(i)}_{\vec{n}}(k)w_{i}(k)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}0,&\text{if }j\in\{0,\ldots,|\vec{n}|-2\},\\
1,&\text{if }j=|\vec{n}|-1,\end{cases}

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{p}\sum_{k\in\Delta}B_{\vec{n}}(k)A^{(i)}_{\vec{m}}(k)w_{i}(k)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}0,&\text{if }m_{i}\leqslant n_{i},\;i\in\{1,\ldots,p\},\\
1,&\text{if }|\vec{m}|=|\vec{n}|+1,\\
0,&\text{if }|\vec{n}|+1<|\vec{m}|.\end{cases}

1.2.
Hypergeometric Functions

It’s important to recall that, in many cases, these polynomials can be represented using generalized hypergeometric series
[
1
]
:

\displaystyle{}_{p}F_{q}\biggl[\genfrac{.}{.}{0.0pt}{}{a_1, \ldots, a_p}{\alpha_1, \ldots, \alpha_q};x\biggr]\coloneqq\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\dfrac{(a_{1})_{l}\cdots(a_{p})_{l}}{(\alpha_{1})_{l}\cdots(\alpha_{q})_{l}}\dfrac{x^{l}}{l!}.

In other cases, it is necessary to go a step further and use the Kampé de Fériet series
[
1
,
11
,
12
]
:

(4)

F^{p:r;s}_{q:n;k}\left[{(a_{1},\ldots,a_{p}):(b_{1},\ldots,b_{r});(c_{1},\ldots,c_{s})\atop(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{q}):(\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{n});(\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{k})}\Bigg|x,y\right]\\
\coloneqq\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\dfrac{(a_{1})_{l+m}\cdots(a_{p})_{l+m}}{(\alpha_{1})_{l+m}\cdots(\alpha_{q})_{l+m}}\dfrac{(b_{1})_{l}\cdots(b_{r})_{l}}{(\beta_{1})_{l}\cdots(\beta_{n})_{l}}\dfrac{(c_{1})_{m}\cdots(c_{s})_{m}}{(\gamma_{1})_{m}\cdots(\gamma_{k})_{m}}\dfrac{x^{l}}{l!}\dfrac{y^{m}}{m!}.

Here,
(x)_{n}
,
x\in\mathbb{C}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, known as the Pochhammer symbol, is defined as:

\displaystyle(x)_{n}=\dfrac{\Gamma(x+n)}{\Gamma(x)}=\begin{cases}x(x+1)\cdots(x+n-1),&\text{if }n\in\mathbb{N},\\
1,&\text{if }n=0.\end{cases}

Recall that for
\operatorname{Re}(z)>0
, the Eulerian gamma function has the integral representation:

\displaystyle\Gamma(z)=\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{z-1}\operatorname{e}^{-t}\,\operatorname{d}t.

The gamma function is subsequently defined as the analytic continuation of this integral to a meromorphic function that is analytic across the entire complex plane, except at zero and the negative integers, where it exhibits simple poles. The asymptotic Stirling formula for the gamma function

(5)

\Gamma(z)=\sqrt{\frac{2\pi}{z}}\left(\frac{z}{\operatorname{e}}\right)^{z}\left(1+\operatorname{O}\left({\frac{1}{z}}\right)\right).

is valid for
|z|\to\infty
in the sector
|\arg(z)|<\pi-\epsilon
,
\epsilon>0
.

The Chu–Vandermode formula

(6)

\displaystyle(a+b)_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}(a)_{k}(b)_{n-k},

will be relevant later.

To establish the results related to the type I polynomials, we will rely on several results involving these functions. In order to deduce the guess for the main result, we will need the Kummer transformation for the
{}_{3}F_{2}
function:

(7)

\displaystyle{}_{3}F_{2}\biggl[\genfrac{.}{.}{0.0pt}{}{a_1, a_2, a_3}{b_1, b_2};1\biggr]=\dfrac{\Gamma(b_{2})\Gamma(b_{1}+b_{2}-a_{1}-a_{2}-a_{3})}{\Gamma(b_{2}-a_{1})\Gamma(b_{1}+b_{2}-a_{2}-a_{3})}\,{}_{3}F_{2}\biggl[\genfrac{.}{.}{0.0pt}{}{a_1, b_1 - a_2, b_1 - a_3}{b_1, b_1 + b_2 - a_2 - a_3};1\biggr],

with
\operatorname{Re}(b_{2}),\operatorname{Re}(b_{1}+b_{2}-a_{1}-a_{2}-a_{3})>0
,
as well as the following reduction formula due to Rakha and Rathie, cf.
[
10
, equation 3.20]
:

(8)

\displaystyle F^{2:1;2}_{2:0;1}\left[{\alpha,\lambda:\epsilon;\beta-\epsilon,\gamma\atop\beta,\mu:--;\delta}\Bigg|1,1\right]=\dfrac{\Gamma(\mu)\Gamma(\mu-\alpha-\lambda)}{\Gamma(\mu-\alpha)\Gamma(\mu-\lambda)}\,{}_{4}F_{3}\biggl[\genfrac{.}{.}{0.0pt}{}{\alpha, \lambda, \beta- \epsilon, \delta- \gamma}{\beta, \delta, 1 - \mu+ \alpha+ \lambda};1\biggr],

which was already used to prove the main result in
[
5
]
.

Finally, the proof of the main result of this paper, which generalizes the one in
[
5
]
, will rely on the following reformulated version (cf.
[
6
, Lemma 1]
) of a theorem by Karp and Prilepkina
[
8
, Theorem 2.2]
.

Lemma 1.1
.

Let
a\in\mathbb{C}
,
\vec{f}=(f_{1},\ldots,f_{r})\in\mathbb{C}^{r}
,
\vec{b}=(b_{1},\ldots,b_{l})\in\mathbb{C}^{l}
with all components distinct,
\vec{m}=(m_{1},\ldots,m_{r})\in\mathbb{N}^{r}
, and
\vec{k}=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{l})\in\mathbb{N}^{l}
. If
\operatorname{Re}(k_{1}+\cdots+k_{l}-a-m_{1}-\cdots-m_{r})>0
, then

(9)

{}_{r+l+1}F_{r+l}\biggl[\genfrac{.}{.}{0.0pt}{}{a, f_1 + m_1, \ldots, f_r + m_r, b_1, \ldots, b_l}{f_1, \ldots, f_r, b_1 + k_1, \ldots, b_l + k_l};1\biggr]\\
=\Gamma(1-a)\dfrac{(b_{1})_{k_{1}}\cdots(b_{l})_{k_{l}}}{(f_{1})_{m_{1}}\cdots(f_{r})_{m_{r}}}\sum_{q=1}^{l}\dfrac{(-1)^{k_{q}-1}\prod_{j=1}^{r}(\tilde{f}_{j}-m_{j})_{m_{j}}\Gamma(b_{q}+k_{q}-1)}{(k_{q}-1)!\prod_{j=1,j\neq q}^{l}(b_{j}-b_{q}-k_{q}+1)_{k_{j}}\Gamma(b_{q}+k_{q}-a)}\\
\times{}_{r+l+1}F_{r+l}\biggl[\genfrac{.}{.}{0.0pt}{}{-k_q + 1, -b_q - k_q + 1 + a, \tilde{f}_1, \ldots, \tilde{f}_r, \vec{b}^{\ast q} - (b_q + k_q - 1) \vec{e}_{l-1}}{-b_q - k_q + 2, \tilde{f}_1 - m_1, \ldots, \tilde{f}_r - m_r, \vec{b}^{\ast q} + \vec{k}^{\ast q} - (b_q + k_q - 1) \vec{e}_{l-1}};1\biggr],

with
\tilde{f}_{j}\coloneq f_{j}-b_{q}+1-k_{q}+m_{j}
,
j=1,\ldots,r
.

Remark 1.2
.

Notice that here it has been introduced the following notation. With the unit vector
represented as

\displaystyle\vec{e}_{p}\coloneq(1,\ldots,1)\in\mathbb{R}^{p},

given a vector
\vec{a}\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
, then

\vec{a}^{\ast q}\in\mathbb{R}^{p-1}
means that the
q
-th component has been removed. This notation will often appear along the rest of the text.

1.3.
Mellin Transform

As observed in
[
13
,
14
]
, the Mellin transform serves as a useful tool in the study of multiple orthogonal polynomials. The Mellin transform of a function
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{\geqslant 0})
is given by

\displaystyle(\mathcal{M}f)(s)=\int_{0}^{\infty}f(x)x^{s-1}\,\operatorname{d}x.

Typically, it is analytic in a strip
\{s\in\mathbb{C}\mid a<\operatorname{Re}(s)<b\}
. In that case, for any
c\in(a,b)
, there exists an inverse transform, namely

\displaystyle f(x)=\int_{c-\operatorname{i}\infty}^{c+\operatorname{i}\infty}(\mathcal{M}f)(s)x^{-s}\frac{\operatorname{d}s}{2\pi\operatorname{i}},\quad x\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}.

Some standard examples are the Mellin transform of a gamma density on
(0,\infty)
,

(10)

\displaystyle(\mathcal{M}\operatorname{e}^{-x})(s)=\Gamma(s),

and the Mellin transform of a beta density on
(0,1)
,

(11)

(\mathcal{M}(1-x)^{\beta}\chi_{(0,1)}(x))(s)=\frac{\Gamma(s)\Gamma(\beta+1)}{\Gamma(s+\beta+1)}.

In the discrete setting, where the underlying measure
\nu_{N}=\sum_{k=0}^{N}\delta_{k}
has mass points on a lattice

\mathcal{L}_{N}=\{0,\ldots,N\}
, we may view the transform

\displaystyle(\mathcal{M}_{N}f)(s)=\int_{0}^{\infty}f(x)\frac{\Gamma(x+s)}{\Gamma(x+1)}\operatorname{d}\nu_{N}(x),

as the analogue of the Mellin transform. Its inverse is described in the result below.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
\mathcal{C}\subset\mathbb{C}
be an anticlockwise contour enclosing once
[-N,0]
. Then,

\displaystyle f(x)=\int_{\mathcal{C}}(\mathcal{M}_{N}f)(s)\frac{\Gamma(x+1)}{\Gamma(x+s+1)}\frac{\operatorname{d}s}{2\pi\operatorname{i}},\quad x\in\mathcal{L}_{N}.

Proof.

By definition, we have

\displaystyle(\mathcal{M}_{N}f)(s)=\sum_{k=0}^{N}\frac{f(k)}{k!}\Gamma(s+k).

Observe that the ratio

\frac{\Gamma(s+k)}{\Gamma(x+s+1)}=\frac{(s)_{k}}{(s)_{x+1}}

has no poles if
k\geq x+1
and has simple poles at
s\in\{k,\ldots,x\}
if
k\leq x
. The residue theorem therefore implies that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{C}}(\mathcal{M}_{N}f)(s)\frac{\Gamma(x+1)}{\Gamma(x+s+1)}\frac{\operatorname{d}s}{2\pi\operatorname{i}}=\sum_{k=0}^{x}\sum_{l=k}^{x}\frac{f(k)}{k!}\frac{(-1)^{l-k}}{(l-k)!}\frac{x!}{(x-l)!}.

It then remains to note that

\displaystyle\sum_{l=k}^{x}\frac{(-1)^{l-k}}{(l-k)!}\frac{x!}{(x-l)!}=\sum_{l=0}^{x-k}\frac{(-1)^{l}}{l!}\frac{x!}{(x-k-l)!}=\frac{x!}{(x-k)!}(1-1)^{x-k}=k!\delta_{k,x},

in order to obtain the desired result.
∎

1.4.
Askey Scheme

Here is a summary of the previously known results that we aim to expand upon in this paper.

1.4.1.
Laguerre of the First Kind Family

The Laguerre weights are given by:

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} w_{i}(x;\alpha_{i})&=\operatorname{e}^{-x}x^{\alpha_{i}},&i&\in\{1,\ldots,p\},&\Delta&=[0,\infty).\end{aligned}

Here,
\vec{\alpha}=(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{p})
with
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{p}>-1
. To establish an AT system, we require that
\alpha_{i}-\alpha_{j}\notin\mathbb{Z}
for
i\neq j
.

The monic type II polynomials admit two equivalent expressions, as shown in
[
3
, §4]
:

\displaystyle\hskip 11.38092ptL_{\vec{n}}(x)=(-1)^{|\vec{n}|}\prod_{q=1}^{p}(\alpha_{q}+1)_{n_{q}}\sum_{l_{1}=0}^{n_{1}}\cdots\sum_{l_{p}=0}^{n_{p}}\prod_{q=1}^{p}\dfrac{(-n_{q})_{l_{q}}}{l_{q}!}\dfrac{(\alpha_{1}+n_{1}+1)_{l_{2}+\cdots+l_{p}}\cdots(\alpha_{p-1}+n_{p-1}+1)_{l_{p}}}{(\alpha_{1}+1)_{l_{1}+\cdots+l_{p}}\cdots(\alpha_{p}+1)_{l_{p}}}x^{l_{1}+\cdots+l_{p}},

(12)

\displaystyle\hskip 15.6491ptL_{\vec{n}}(x)=(-1)^{|\vec{n}|}\prod_{q=1}^{p}(\alpha_{q}+1)_{n_{q}}\operatorname{e}^{x}\,{}_{p}F_{p}\biggl[\genfrac{.}{.}{0.0pt}{}{\vec{\alpha}+\vec{n}+\vec{e}_p}{\vec{\alpha}+\vec{e}_p};-x\biggr].

The type I polynomials were recently found in
[
6
]
:

(13)

\displaystyle L^{(i)}_{\vec{n}}(x)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{|\vec{n}|-1}\dfrac{1}{(n_{i}-1)!\prod_{k=1,k\neq i}^{p}(\alpha_{k}-\alpha_{i})_{n_{k}}\Gamma(\alpha_{i}+1)}\,{}_{p}F_{p}\biggl[\genfrac{.}{.}{0.0pt}{}{-n_i+1, (\alpha_i+1)\vec{e}_{p-1} - \vec{\alpha}^{\ast i} - \vec{n}^{\ast i}}{\alpha_i+1, (\alpha_i+1)\vec{e}_{p-1} - \vec{\alpha}^{\ast i}};x\biggr].

1.4.2.
Jacobi–Piñeiro Family

Consider a system of Jacobi weights:

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} w_{i}(x;\alpha_{i},\beta)&=x^{\alpha_{i}}(1-x)^{\beta},&i&\in\{1,\ldots,p\},&\Delta&=[0,1].\end{aligned}

Here
\vec{\alpha}=(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{p})
with
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{p},\beta>-1
. To establish an AT system, we require that
\alpha_{i}-\alpha_{j}\not\in\mathbb{Z}
for
i\neq j
.

The monic Jacobi–Piñeiro multiple orthogonal polynomials of type II admit both equivalent expressions, as shown in
[
3
, §3]
:

\displaystyle P_{\vec{n}}(x)=

\displaystyle\begin{multlined}(-1)^{|\vec{n}|}\prod_{q=1}^{p}\dfrac{(\alpha_{q}+1)_{n_{q}}}{(\alpha_{q}+\beta+|\vec{n}|+1)_{n_{q}}}\sum_{l_{1}=0}^{n_{1}}\cdots\sum_{l_{p}=0}^{n_{p}}\,\prod_{q=1}^{p}\dfrac{(-n_{q})_{l_{q}}}{l_{q}!}\\
\times\dfrac{(\alpha_{1}+n_{1}+1)_{l_{2}+\cdots+l_{p}}\cdots(\alpha_{p-1}+n_{p-1}+1)_{l_{p}}}{(\alpha_{1}+\beta+n_{1}+1)_{l_{2}+\cdots+l_{p}}\cdots(\alpha_{p-1}+\beta+n_{1}+\cdots+n_{p-1}+1)_{l_{p}}}\\
\times\dfrac{(\alpha_{1}+\beta+n_{1}+1)_{l_{1}+\cdots+l_{p}}\cdots(\alpha_{p}+\beta+|\vec{n}|+1)_{l_{p}}}{(\alpha_{1}+1)_{l_{1}+\cdots+l_{p}}\cdots(\alpha_{p}+1)_{l_{p}}}\,x^{l_{1}+\cdots+l_{p}},\end{multlined}

(17)

\displaystyle P_{\vec{n}}(x)=

\displaystyle(-1)^{|\vec{n}|}\prod_{q=1}^{p}\dfrac{(\alpha_{q}+1)_{n_{q}}}{(\alpha_{q}+\beta+|\vec{n}|+1)_{n_{q}}}(1-x)^{-\beta}\,{}_{p+1}F_{p}\biggl[\genfrac{.}{.}{0.0pt}{}{-|\vec{n}|-\beta,\vec{\alpha}+\vec{n}+\vec{e}_p}{\vec{\alpha}+\vec{e}_p};x\biggr].

In
[
6
]
, it was proved that the type I Jacobi–Piñeiro multiple orthogonal polynomials are, for
i\in\{1,\ldots,p\}
given by:

(18)

P^{(i)}_{\vec{n}}(x)=(-1)^{|\vec{n}|-1}\dfrac{\prod_{k=1}^{p}(\alpha_{k}+\beta+|\vec{n}|)_{n_{k}}}{(n_{i}-1)!\prod_{k=1,k\neq i}^{p}(\alpha_{k}-\alpha_{i})_{n_{k}}}\dfrac{\Gamma(\alpha_{i}+\beta+|\vec{n}|)}{\Gamma(\beta+|\vec{n}|)\Gamma(\alpha_{i}+1)}\\
\times{}_{p+1}F_{p}\biggl[\genfrac{.}{.}{0.0pt}{}{-n_i+1,\alpha_i+\beta+|\vec{n}|, (\alpha_i+1)\vec{e}_{p-1}-\vec{\alpha}^{\ast i}-\vec{n}^{\ast i}}{\alpha_i+1,(\alpha_i+1)\vec{e}_{p-1}-\vec{\alpha}^{\ast i}};x\biggr].

1.4.3.
Hahn Family

This family arises from considering a system of discrete weights of the form:

(19)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} w_{i}(x;\alpha_{i},\beta,N)&=\frac{\Gamma(\alpha_{i}+x+1)}{\Gamma(\alpha_{i}+1)\Gamma(x+1)}\frac{\Gamma(\beta+N-x+1)}{\Gamma(\beta+1)\Gamma(N-x+1)},&i&\in\{1,\ldots,p\},&\Delta&=\{0,\ldots,N\}.\end{aligned}

Here,
\vec{\alpha}=(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{p})
with
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{p},\beta>-1
,
|\vec{n}|\leq N\in\mathbb{N}
, and to establish an AT system, we require that
\alpha_{i}-\alpha_{j}\notin\mathbb{Z}
for
i\neq j
.
The corresponding monic type II polynomials were first found for
p=2
in
[
2
]
and then generalized for
p\geq 2
in
[
3
]
.
These are given by:

(20)

Q_{\vec{n}}(x)=\prod_{q=1}^{p}\dfrac{(\alpha_{q}+1)_{n_{q}}}{(\alpha_{q}+\beta+|\vec{n}|+1)_{n_{q}}}\\
\times\sum_{l_{1}=0}^{n_{1}}\cdots\sum_{l_{p}=0}^{n_{p}}\prod_{q=1}^{p}\dfrac{(-n_{q})_{l_{q}}}{l_{q}!}\frac{(-N)_{|\vec{n}|}}{(-N)_{l_{1}+\cdots+l_{p}}}\dfrac{(\alpha_{1}+\beta+n_{1}+1)_{l_{1}+\cdots+l_{p}}\cdots(\alpha_{p}+\beta+|\vec{n}|+1)_{l_{p}}}{(\alpha_{1}+1)_{l_{1}+\cdots+l_{p}}\cdots(\alpha_{p}+1)_{l_{p}}}\\
\times\dfrac{(\alpha_{1}+n_{1}+1)_{l_{2}+\cdots+l_{p}}\cdots(\alpha_{p-1}+n_{p-1}+1)_{l_{p}}}{(\alpha_{1}+\beta+n_{1}+1)_{l_{2}+\cdots+l_{p}}\cdots(\alpha_{p-1}+\beta+n_{1}+\cdots+n_{p-1}+1)_{l_{p}}}(-x)_{l_{1}+\cdots+l_{p}}.

In
[
5
, §2]
, it was proven that for
p=2
,
i\in\{1,2\}
, the type I polynomials can be expressed through a Kampé de Fériet function as:

(21)

Q_{(n_{1},n_{2})}^{(i)}(x)=\frac{(-1)^{n_{i}-1}(N+1-n_{1}-n_{2})!(n_{1}+n_{2}-2)!}{(n_{1}-1)!(n_{2}-1)!(\beta+1)_{n_{1}+n_{2}-1}(\alpha_{i}+\beta+n_{1}+n_{2}+n_{i})_{N+1-n_{1}-n_{2}}}\frac{(\hat{\alpha}_{i}+\beta+\hat{n}_{i}+1)_{n_{1}+n_{2}-1}}{(\alpha_{i}-\hat{\alpha}_{i}-\hat{n}_{i}+1)_{n_{1}+n_{2}-1}}\\
\times F^{2:1;3}_{2:0;2}\left[{(-n_{i}+1,-N):(\hat{\alpha}_{i}-\alpha_{i}-n_{i}+1);(\alpha_{i}+\beta+n_{1}+n_{2},\alpha_{i}-\hat{\alpha}_{i}-\hat{n}_{i}+1,-x)\atop(-n_{1}-n_{2}+2,\hat{\alpha}_{i}+\beta+\hat{n}_{i}+1):--;(\alpha_{i}+1,-N)}\Bigg|1,1\right],

where
\hat{\alpha}_{i}\coloneq\delta_{i,2}\alpha_{1}+\delta_{i,1}\alpha_{2}
and
\hat{n}_{i}\coloneq\delta_{i,2}n_{1}+\delta_{i,1}n_{2}
.

2.
Hypergeometric Representations: Hahn Type I Polynomials

The main aim of this section is to generalize the previous result of the Hahn type I polynomials for an arbitrary number of weight functions. To achieve this, we will reformulate the previous expression in a more convenient way. From the definition of the Kampé de Fériet series (
4
), expression (
21
) can be written as

Q_{(n_{1},n_{2})}^{(i)}(x)=\frac{(-1)^{n_{i}-1}(N+1-n_{1}-n_{2})!(n_{1}+n_{2}-2)!}{(n_{1}-1)!(n_{2}-1)!(\beta+1)_{n_{1}+n_{2}-1}(\alpha_{i}+\beta+n_{1}+n_{2}+n_{i})_{N+1-n_{1}-n_{2}}}\frac{(\hat{\alpha}_{i}+\beta+\hat{n}_{i}+1)_{n_{1}+n_{2}-1}}{(\alpha_{i}-\hat{\alpha}_{i}-\hat{n}_{i}+1)_{n_{1}+n_{2}-1}}\\
\times\sum_{l=0}^{n_{i}-1}\frac{(-n_{i}+1)_{l}(\alpha_{i}+\beta+n_{1}+n_{2})_{l}(\alpha_{i}-\hat{\alpha}_{i}-\hat{n}_{i}+1)_{l}(-x)_{l}}{l!(-n_{1}-n_{2}+2)_{l}(\hat{\alpha}_{i}+\beta+\hat{n}_{i}+1)_{l}(\alpha_{i}+1)_{l}}\,{}_{3}F_{2}\biggl[\genfrac{.}{.}{0.0pt}{}{-n_i + 1 + l, -N + l, \hat{\alpha}_i - \alpha_i - n_i + 1}{-n_1 - n_2 + 2 + l, \hat{\alpha}_i + \beta+ \hat{n}_i + 1 + l};1\biggr].

Now, the previous

{}_{3}F_{2}\biggl[\genfrac{.}{.}{0.0pt}{}{-n_i + 1 + l, -N + l, \hat{\alpha}_i - \alpha_i - n_i + 1}{-n_1 - n_2 + 2 + l, \hat{\alpha}_i + \beta+ \hat{n}_i + 1 + l};1\biggr]\\

By replacing this new

Q_{(n_{1},n_{2})}^{(i)}(x)=\dfrac{(-1)^{n_{i}-1}(N+1-n_{1}-n_{2})!(n_{1}+n_{2}-2)!(\alpha_{1}+\beta+n_{1}+n_{2})_{n_{1}}(\alpha_{2}+\beta+n_{1}+n_{2})_{n_{2}}}{(n_{1}-1)!(n_{2}-1)!(\beta+1)_{n_{1}+n_{2}-1}(\alpha_{i}+\beta+n_{1}+n_{2})_{N+2-n_{1}-n_{2}}(\alpha_{i}-\hat{\alpha}_{i}-\hat{n}_{i}+1)_{n_{1}+n_{2}-1}}\\
\times\underbrace{F^{2:1;2}_{2:0;1}\left[{(-n_{i}+1,\alpha_{i}-\hat{\alpha}_{i}-\hat{n}_{i}+1):(-n_{1}-n_{2}+2+N);(\alpha_{i}+\beta+n_{1}+n_{2},-x)\atop(-n_{1}-n_{2}+2,\alpha_{i}+\beta+N+2):--;(\alpha_{i}+1)}\Bigg|1,1\right]}_{\dfrac{(\alpha_{i}-\hat{\alpha}_{i}+1)_{n_{i}-1}}{(\hat{n}_{i})_{n_{i}-1}}\,{}_{4}F_{3}\biggl[\genfrac{.}{.}{0.0pt}{}{-n_i + 1, \alpha_i + \beta+ n_1 + n_2, \alpha_i - \hat{\alpha}_i - \hat{n}_i + 1, x + \alpha_i + 1}{\alpha_i + 1, \alpha_i - \hat{\alpha}_i + 1, \alpha_i + \beta+ N + 2};1\biggr]\text{ by }\eqref{RR}}.

Simplifying, we arrive at the following formula:

Q_{(n_{1},n_{2})}^{(i)}(x)=\dfrac{(-1)^{n_{1}+n_{2}-1}(N+1-n_{1}-n_{2})!}{(n_{i}-1)!(\beta+1)_{n_{1}+n_{2}-1}(\alpha_{i}+\beta+n_{1}+n_{2})_{N+2-n_{1}-n_{2}}}\dfrac{(\alpha_{1}+\beta+n_{1}+n_{2})_{n_{1}}(\alpha_{2}+\beta+n_{1}+n_{2})_{n_{2}}}{(\hat{\alpha}_{i}-\alpha_{i})_{\hat{n}_{i}}}\\

This simplified formula is easier to generalize than (

Theorem 2.1
.

The Hahn multiple orthogonal polynomials of type I for an arbitrary number

p
of weights are expressed as:

\displaystyle Q_{\vec{n}}^{(i)}(x)

\displaystyle=\begin{multlined}\dfrac{(-1)^{|\vec{n}|-1}(N+1-|\vec{n}|)!}{(n_{i}-1)!(\beta+1)_{|\vec{n}|-1}(\alpha_{i}+\beta+|\vec{n}|)_{N+2-|\vec{n}|}}\dfrac{\prod_{k=1}^{p}({\alpha}_{k}+\beta+|\vec{n}|)_{n_{k}}}{\prod_{k=1,k\neq i}^{p}({\alpha}_{k}-{\alpha}_{i})_{{n}_{k}}}\\

(24)

with

(25)

Proof.

In this scenario, the orthogonality conditions (

3.
Integral Representations

In this section, we will derive some contour integral representations for the Hahn multiple orthogonal polynomials of types I and II. Rather than starting from the hypergeometric representations in Theorem
2.1
and in equation (
20
), we aim to provide a constructive proof for these integral representations based on the residue theorem and the Mellin transform. This approach will also lead to a constructive proof for the associated hypergeometric representations. In order to get a better understanding of the ideas behind the techniques presented here, we will first investigate the settings of the multiple Laguerre polynomials of the first kind and the Jacobi–Piñeiro polynomials.

3.1.
Laguerre of the First Kind Multiple Orthogonal Polynomials

Here, we rely on §
1.4.1
.

3.1.1.
Multiple Orthogonal Polynomials of Type I

Our approach relies on encoding the type I linear forms
L_{\vec{n}}^{(I)}(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{p}L_{\vec{n}}^{(i)}(x)x^{\alpha_{i}}
as a contour integral in the following manner.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let us consider:

i)

a linear form

F_{\vec{n}}(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{p}A_{\vec{n},i}(x)x^{\alpha_{i}}
with
\deg A_{\vec{n},i}\leqslant n_{i}-1
,

ii)

an anticlockwise contour
\Sigma\subset\mathbb{C}
enclosing once
\mathcal{I}=\cup_{i=1}^{p}[\alpha_{i},n_{i}+\alpha_{i}-1]
,

iii)

and a function
\varphi
analytic inside and on
\Sigma
that doesn’t vanish on
\mathcal{I}
.

Then there exists a unique polynomial
p_{\left|\vec{n}\right|-1}(t)
of degree at most
\left|\vec{n}\right|-1
such that

(27)

\displaystyle F_{\vec{n}}(x)=\int_{\Sigma}\frac{p_{\left|\vec{n}\right|-1}(t)}{\prod_{i=1}^{p}(\alpha_{i}-t)_{n_{i}}}\varphi(t)x^{t}\frac{\operatorname{d}t}{2\pi\operatorname{i}}.

Proof.

Suppose that
p_{|\vec{n}|-1}(t)
is a polynomial of degree at most
|\vec{n}|-1
. Then, it follows from the residue theorem that

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma}\frac{p_{|\vec{n}|-1}(t)}{\prod_{i=1}^{p}(\alpha_{i}-t)_{n_{i}}}\varphi(t)x^{t}\frac{\operatorname{d}t}{2\pi\operatorname{i}}=\sum_{i=1}^{p}\sum_{k=0}^{n_{i}-1}\frac{(-1)^{k}p_{|\vec{n}|-1}(k+\alpha_{i})\varphi(k+\alpha_{i})}{(n_{i}-1-k)!k!\prod_{j=1,j\neq i}^{p}(\alpha_{j}-\alpha_{i}-k)_{n_{j}}i}x^{k+\alpha_{i}}.

If we require the Equation
(
27
)
to be satisfied, and we write
A_{\vec{n},i}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n_{i}-1}A_{\vec{n},i}[k]x^{k}
, we conclude that

\displaystyle A_{\vec{n},i}[k]=\frac{(-1)^{k}p_{|\vec{n}|-1}(k+\alpha_{i})\varphi(k+\alpha_{i})}{(n_{i}-1-k)!k!\prod_{j=1,j\neq i}^{p}(\alpha_{j}-\alpha_{i}-k)_{n_{j}}}.

These
|\vec{n}|
interpolation conditions then uniquely determine the polynomial
p_{|\vec{n}|-1}(t)
of degree
|\vec{n}|-1
.
∎

The orthogonality conditions, along with the normalization condition, will enable us to explicitly determine the underlying polynomials for the type I linear forms
L_{\vec{n}}^{(I)}(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{p}L_{\vec{n}}^{(i)}(x)x^{\alpha_{i}}
. A crucial element in the proof will be the following lemma.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\Sigma\subset\mathbb{C}
be an anticlockwise contour enclosing once
\cup_{i=1}^{p}[\alpha_{i},n_{i}+\alpha_{i}-1]
. Suppose that
p_{\left|\vec{n}\right|-1}(t)
is polynomial of degree at most
\left|\vec{n}\right|-1
for which

(28)

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma}\frac{p_{\left|\vec{n}\right|-1}(t)}{\prod_{i=1}^{p}(\alpha_{i}-t)_{n_{i}}}q_{\left|\vec{n}\right|-2}(t)\frac{\operatorname{d}t}{2\pi\operatorname{i}}=0,

for every polynomial
q_{\left|\vec{n}\right|-2}(t)
of degree at most
\left|\vec{n}\right|-2
. Then
p_{\left|\vec{n}\right|-1}(t)
is a constant.

Proof.

Note that the assumption holds in particular for every polynomial of the form

(29)

\displaystyle q_{\left|\vec{n}\right|-2}(t)=\frac{\prod_{i=1}^{p}(\alpha_{i}-t)_{n_{i}}}{(\alpha_{1}-t)(\alpha_{j}+k-t)},

where
k\in\{0,\ldots,n_{j}-1\}
and
j\in\{1,\ldots,p\}
with
\alpha_{j}+k\neq\alpha_{1}
. After an application of the residue theorem, the identity

(30)

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma}\frac{p_{\left|\vec{n}\right|-1}(t)}{(\alpha_{1}-t)(\alpha_{j}+k-t)}\frac{\operatorname{d}t}{2\pi\operatorname{i}}=0,

gives

(31)

\displaystyle p_{\left|\vec{n}\right|-1}(\alpha_{j}+k)=p_{\left|\vec{n}\right|-1}(\alpha_{1}),

indicating that
p_{\left|\vec{n}\right|-1}(t)
remains constant across the
\left|\vec{n}\right|
zeros of
\prod_{i=1}^{p}(\alpha_{i}-t)_{n_{i}}
. Since
p_{\left|\vec{n}\right|-1}(t)
has degree at most
\left|\vec{n}\right|-1
, it follows that
p_{\left|\vec{n}\right|-1}(t)
is a constant.
∎

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
\Sigma\subset\mathbb{C}
be as in Proposition
3.1
and take
\operatorname{Re}(t)>-1
. Then the linear form

\displaystyle L_{\vec{n}}^{(I)}(x)\coloneq\sum_{i=1}^{p}L_{\vec{n}}^{(i)}(x)x^{\alpha_{i}},

is given by

(32)

\displaystyle L_{\vec{n}}^{(I)}(x)=(-1)^{\left|\vec{n}\right|-1}\int_{\Sigma}\frac{1}{\Gamma(t+1)}\frac{x^{t}}{\prod_{i=1}^{p}(\alpha_{i}-t)_{n_{i}}}\frac{\operatorname{d}t}{2\pi\operatorname{i}}.

Proof.

According to Proposition
3.1
, the linear form
L_{\vec{n}}^{(I)}(x)
is given by

\displaystyle L_{\vec{n}}^{(I)}(x)=\int_{\Sigma}\frac{p_{\left|\vec{n}\right|-1}(t)}{\prod_{i=1}^{p}(\alpha_{i}-t)_{n_{i}}}\varphi(t)x^{t}\frac{\operatorname{d}t}{2\pi\operatorname{i}},

where
p_{\left|\vec{n}\right|-1}(t)
is a polynomial of degree at most
\left|\vec{n}\right|-1
, and
\varphi
is chosen below. Recalling (
10
) and applying the weighted Mellin transform, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}L_{\vec{n}}^{(I)}(x)\operatorname{e}^{-x}x^{s}\operatorname{d}x=\int_{\Sigma}\frac{p_{\left|\vec{n}\right|-1}(t)}{\prod_{i=1}^{p}(\alpha_{i}-t)_{n_{i}}}\varphi(t)\Gamma(s+t+1)\frac{\operatorname{d}t}{2\pi\operatorname{i}}.

By choosing
\varphi(t)=1/\Gamma(t+1)
, for
s\in\mathbb{N}
, a polynomial of degree
s
appears in the integrand. The orthogonality conditions (
2
) then require

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma}\frac{p_{\left|\vec{n}\right|-1}(t)}{\prod_{i=1}^{p}(\alpha_{i}-t)_{n_{i}}}(t+1)_{s}\frac{\operatorname{d}t}{2\pi\operatorname{i}}=0,\quad s\in\{0,\ldots,\left|\vec{n}\right|-2\},

or equivalently,

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma}\frac{p_{\left|\vec{n}\right|-1}(t)}{\prod_{i=1}^{p}(\alpha_{i}-t)_{n_{i}}}q_{\left|\vec{n}\right|-2}(t)\frac{\operatorname{d}t}{2\pi\operatorname{i}}=0,

for every polynomial
q_{\left|\vec{n}\right|-2}(t)
of degree at most
\left|\vec{n}\right|-2
. Hence, it follows from Lemma
3.2
that
p_{\left|\vec{n}\right|-1}(t)
is a constant, say
p_{\left|\vec{n}\right|-1}(t)=p_{\left|\vec{n}\right|-1}[0]
. The specific value is determined by the normalization condition

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma}\frac{p_{\left|\vec{n}\right|-1}[0]}{\prod_{j=1}^{p}(\alpha_{i}-t)_{n_{i}}}(t+1)_{\left|\vec{n}\right|-1}\frac{\operatorname{d}t}{2\pi\operatorname{i}}=1.

Since the integrand decays as
\operatorname{O}(t^{-1})
as
|t|\to\infty
, we evaluate the integral using the residue at infinity:

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma}\frac{p_{\left|\vec{n}\right|-1}[0]}{\prod_{i=1}^{p}(\alpha_{i}-t)_{n_{i}}}(t+1)_{\left|\vec{n}\right|-1}\frac{\operatorname{d}t}{2\pi\operatorname{i}}=-\lim_{|t|\to\infty}t\frac{p_{\left|\vec{n}\right|-1}[0]}{\prod_{j=1}^{p}(\alpha_{i}-t)_{n_{i}}}(t+1)_{\left|\vec{n}\right|-1}=(-1)^{\left|\vec{n}\right|-1}p_{\left|\vec{n}\right|-1}[0].

Thus,
p_{\left|\vec{n}\right|-1}[0]=(-1)^{\left|\vec{n}\right|-1}
.
∎

Corollary 3.4
.

The computation of the contour integral (
32
) leads to the identities given in (
13
).

Proof.

The integrand only has simple poles at
t=\alpha_{i}+k
for
k\in\{0,\ldots,n_{i}-1\}
and
i\in\{1,\ldots,p\}
. The residue at
t=\alpha_{i}+k
is given by

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\Gamma(k+\alpha_{i}+1)}

\displaystyle\frac{(-1)^{k}}{k!(n_{i}-k-1)!}\frac{1}{\prod_{j=1,j\neq i}^{p}(\alpha_{j}-\alpha_{i}-k)_{n_{j}}}x^{k+\alpha_{i}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(\alpha_{i}+1)_{k}\Gamma(\alpha_{i}+1)}\frac{(-n_{i}+1)_{k}}{k!(n_{i}-1)!}\prod_{j=1,j\neq i}^{p}\frac{(\alpha_{i}-\alpha_{j}-n_{j}+1)_{k}}{(\alpha_{i}-\alpha_{j}+1)_{k}(\alpha_{j}-\alpha_{i})_{n_{j}}}x^{k+\alpha_{i}}.

Consequently,

\displaystyle L_{\vec{n}}^{(i)}[k]=\frac{(-1)^{\left|\vec{n}\right|-1}}{(n_{i}-1)!\prod_{j=1,j\neq i}^{p}(\alpha_{j}-\alpha_{i})_{n_{j}}\Gamma(\alpha_{i}+1)}\frac{(-n_{i}+1)_{k}}{(\alpha_{i}+1)_{k}}\prod_{j=1,j\neq i}^{p}\frac{(\alpha_{i}-\alpha_{j}-n_{j}+1)_{k}}{(\alpha_{i}-\alpha_{j}+1)_{k}}\frac{1}{k!},

which leads to (
13
).
∎

3.1.2.
Multiple Orthogonal Polynomials of Type II

Our approach for the type II polynomials in §
1.4.1
is based on our ability to explicitly compute their weighted Mellin transform. The result below for
p=2
was previously found in
[
13

, using a different approach.

Proposition 3.5
.

The Mellin transform of the functions
L_{\vec{n}}(x)\operatorname{e}^{-x}
is given by

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}L_{\vec{n}}(x)\operatorname{e}^{-x}x^{s-1}\operatorname{d}x=(-1)^{\left|\vec{n}\right|}\Gamma(s)\prod_{i=1}^{p}(\alpha_{i}+1-s)_{n_{i}}.

Proof.

For the LHS, Equation (
10
) and the expression
L_{\vec{n}}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{\left|\vec{n}\right|}L_{\vec{n}}[k]x^{k}
leads to

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\left|\vec{n}\right|}L_{\vec{n}}[k]\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{s+k-1}\operatorname{e}^{-x}\operatorname{d}x=\sum_{k=0}^{\left|\vec{n}\right|}L_{\vec{n}}[k]\Gamma(s+k).

Let us take out the common factor
\Gamma(s)
to obtain

\displaystyle\Gamma(s)\sum_{k=0}^{\left|\vec{n}\right|}L_{\vec{n}}[k](s)_{k}=\Gamma(s)p_{\left|\vec{n}\right|}(s),

in terms of some polynomial
p_{\left|\vec{n}\right|}(s)
of degree at most
\left|\vec{n}\right|
. It then follows from the orthogonality conditions (
1
) that
p_{\left|\vec{n}\right|}(s)
needs to vanish at the
\left|\vec{n}\right|
values
k+\alpha_{i}
for
k\in\{1,\ldots,n_{i}\}
and
i\in\{1,\ldots,p\}
. Hence,
p_{\left|\vec{n}\right|}(s)
must be a scalar multiple of
\prod_{i=1}^{p}(\alpha_{i}+1-s)_{n_{i}}
. The fact that
L_{\vec{n}}(x)
is monic, i.e.
L_{\vec{n}}[\left|\vec{n}\right|]=1
, then implies that
\displaystyle p_{\left|\vec{n}\right|}(s)=(-1)^{\left|\vec{n}\right|}\prod_{i=1}^{p}(\alpha_{i}+1-s)_{n_{i}}
.
∎

We employ the inverse Mellin transform to derive the following integral representation for the weighted type II polynomials. Notice the similarities with the contour integral used for the type I linear form in Theorem
3.3
.

Theorem 3.6
.

For
x\in(0,\infty)
, the function
L_{\vec{n}}(x)\operatorname{e}^{-x}
admits the following integral representation:

(33)

\displaystyle L_{\vec{n}}(x)\operatorname{e}^{-x}=(-1)^{\left|\vec{n}\right|}\int_{\mathcal{C}}\Gamma(s)\prod_{i=1}^{p}(\alpha_{i}+1-s)_{n_{i}}x^{-s}\frac{\operatorname{d}s}{2\pi\operatorname{i}},

where
\mathcal{C}
is an anticlockwise contour enclosing
(-\infty,0]
once.

Corollary 3.7
.

The explicit hypergeometric expression in (
12
) follows from the evaluation of the contour integral (
33
).

Proof.

The integrand only has simple poles at
s=-k
for
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. The residue at
t=-k
is given by

\displaystyle\frac{(-1)^{k}}{k!}\prod_{i=1}^{p}(\alpha_{i}+1+k)_{n_{i}}x^{k}=\prod_{i=1}^{p}\frac{(\alpha_{i}+1)_{n_{i}}(n_{i}+\alpha_{i}+1)_{k}}{(\alpha_{i}+1)_{k}}\frac{(-x)^{k}}{k!}.

To establish (

), it then remains to note that we may replace the contour integral
\int_{\mathcal{C}}
by
\lim_{M\to\infty}\int_{\mathcal{C}_{M}}
in terms of anticlockwise oriented rectangles

K>0
such that

and thus

\displaystyle\lim_{M\to\infty}\int_{-M-\frac{\operatorname{i}}{2}}^{-M+\frac{\operatorname{i}}{2}}\Gamma(s)\prod_{i=1}^{p}(\alpha_{i}+1-s)_{n_{i}}x^{-s}\frac{\operatorname{d}s}{2\pi\operatorname{i}}=0.

∎

3.2.1.

The counterpart of Theorem

Theorem 3.8
.

Let

Using Proposition

where

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}P_{\vec{n}}^{(I)}(x)(1-x)^{\beta}x^{s}\operatorname{d}x=\int_{\Sigma}\frac{p_{\left|\vec{n}\right|-1}(t)}{\prod_{i=1}^{p}(\alpha_{i}-t)_{n_{i}}}\varphi(t)\frac{\Gamma(s+t+1)\Gamma(\beta+1)}{\Gamma(s+t+\beta+2)}\frac{\operatorname{d}t}{2\pi\operatorname{i}},

were (

.
∎

Corollary 3.9
.

The explicit expressions (
18

Proof.

The integrand only has simple poles inside the contour
\Sigma
at

for

and

. Since

\quad=\frac{(\left|\vec{n}\right|+\alpha_{i}+\beta)_{k}\Gamma(\left|\vec{n}\right|+\alpha_{i}+\beta)}{(\alpha_{i}+1)_{k}\Gamma(\alpha_{i}+1)}\frac{(-n_{i}+1)_{k}}{k!(n_{i}-1)!}\frac{1}{(\alpha_{i}+1)_{k}\Gamma(\alpha_{i}+1)}\frac{(-n_{i}+1)_{k}}{k!(n_{i}-1)!}\\

The Mellin transform of

\left|\vec{n}\right|

, where

Here, we rely on §

3.3.1.
Multiple Orthogonal Polynomials of Type I

Recall that the type I multiple Hahn polynomials are vectors of polynomials
(Q_{\vec{n}}^{(1)},\ldots,Q_{\vec{n}}^{(p)})
, where
\deg Q_{\vec{n}}^{(i)}\leq n_{i}-1
. The linear form

\displaystyle Q_{\vec{n}}^{(I)}(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{p}Q_{\vec{n}}^{(i)}(x)\frac{\Gamma(x+\alpha_{i}+1)}{\Gamma(\alpha_{i}+1)},

satisfies the conditions:

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}Q_{\vec{n}}^{(I)}(x)\frac{\Gamma(\beta+N-x+1)}{\Gamma(x-k+1)\Gamma(N-x+1)}\operatorname{d}\nu_{N}(x)=\begin{cases}0,&\text{if }k\in\{0,\ldots,|\vec{n}|-2\},\\
\Gamma(\beta+1),&\text{if }k=|\vec{n}|-1.\end{cases}

Recall that
\nu_{N}=\sum_{k=0}^{N}\delta_{k}
has mass points on a lattice
\mathcal{L}_{N}=\{0,\ldots,N\}
.
Observe that we consider orthogonality with respect to
(x-k+1)_{k}=\Gamma(x+1)/\Gamma(x-k+1)
instead of the standard
x^{k}
. This is more suitable for our approach.

To proceed further, we need the following analogue of Theorem
3.3
.

Proposition 3.13
.

Let us consider:

i)

a linear form

F_{\vec{n}}(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{p}A_{\vec{n},i}(x)\Gamma(x+\alpha_{i}+1)
with
\deg A_{\vec{n},i}\leqslant n_{i}-1
,

ii)

an anticlockwise contour
\Sigma\subset\mathbb{C}
enclosing once
\mathcal{I}=\cup_{i=1}^{p}[\alpha_{i},n_{i}+\alpha_{i}-1]
,

iii)

and a function
\varphi
analytic inside and on
\Sigma
that doesn’t vanish on
\mathcal{I}
.

Then there exists a unique polynomial
p_{\left|\vec{n}\right|-1}(t)
of degree at most
\left|\vec{n}\right|-1
such that

\displaystyle F_{\vec{n}}(x)=\int_{\Sigma}\frac{p_{\left|\vec{n}\right|-1}(t)}{\prod_{i=1}^{p}(\alpha_{i}-t)_{n_{i}}}\varphi(t)\Gamma(x+t+1)\frac{\operatorname{d}t}{2\pi\operatorname{i}}.

Proof.

Suppose that
p_{\left|\vec{n}\right|-1}(t)
is a polynomial of degree at most
\left|\vec{n}\right|-1
. By applying the residue theorem, we derive that

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma}\frac{p_{\left|\vec{n}\right|-1}(t)}{\prod_{i=1}^{p}(\alpha_{i}-t)_{n_{i}}}\varphi(t)\Gamma(x+t+1)\frac{\operatorname{d}t}{2\pi\operatorname{i}}=\sum_{i=1}^{p}\sum_{k=0}^{n_{i}-1}\frac{(-1)^{k}p_{\left|\vec{n}\right|-1}(k+\alpha_{i})\varphi(k+\alpha_{i})}{(n_{i}-1-k)!k!\prod_{j=1,j\neq i}^{p}(\alpha_{j}-\alpha_{i}-k)_{n_{j}}}\Gamma(x+k+\alpha_{i}+1).

Thus, expanding
A_{\vec{n},i}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n_{i}-1}A_{\vec{n},i}[k](x+\alpha_{i}+1)_{k}
in the basis
\{(x+\alpha_{i}+1)_{k}\}_{k=0}^{n_{i}-1}
, we find that

A_{\vec{n},i}[k]=\frac{(-1)^{k}p_{\left|\vec{n}\right|-1}(k+\alpha_{i})\varphi(k+\alpha_{i})}{(n_{i}-1-k)!k!\prod_{j=1,j\neq i}^{p}(\alpha_{j}-\alpha_{i}-k)_{n_{j}}}.

These
\left|\vec{n}\right|
interpolation conditions uniquely determine the degree
\left|\vec{n}\right|-1
polynomial
p_{\left|\vec{n}\right|-1}(t)
.
∎

The orthogonality conditions, together with the normalization condition, allow us to explicitly determine the polynomials we have to use in order to recover the type I linear forms

Q_{\vec{n}}^{(I)}(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{p}Q_{\vec{n}}^{(i)}(x)\frac{\Gamma(x+\alpha_{i}+1)}{\Gamma(\alpha_{i}+1)}.

Theorem 3.14
.

Let
\Sigma\subset\mathbb{C}
be as in Proposition
3.13
and take
\operatorname{Re}(t)>-1
. Then the linear form

\displaystyle Q_{\vec{n}}^{(I)}(x)\coloneq\sum_{i=1}^{p}Q_{\vec{n}}^{(i)}(x)\frac{\Gamma(x+\alpha_{i}+1)}{\Gamma(\alpha_{i}+1)},

is given by

(37)

\displaystyle Q_{\vec{n}}^{(I)}(x)=(-1)^{\left|\vec{n}\right|-1}(N-\left|\vec{n}\right|+1)!\frac{\prod_{i=1}^{p}(\alpha_{i}+\beta+\left|\vec{n}\right|)_{n_{i}}}{(\beta+1)_{\left|\vec{n}\right|-1}}\int_{\Sigma}\frac{\Gamma(t+\beta+\left|\vec{n}\right|)}{\Gamma(t+1)\Gamma(t+\beta+N+2)}\frac{\Gamma(x+t+1)}{\prod_{i=1}^{p}(\alpha_{i}-t)_{n_{i}}}\frac{\operatorname{d}t}{2\pi\operatorname{i}}.

Proof.

Using Proposition
3.13
, we can express

\displaystyle Q_{\vec{n}}^{(I)}(x)=\int_{\Sigma}\frac{p_{\left|\vec{n}\right|-1}(t)}{\prod_{i=1}^{p}(\alpha_{i}-t)_{n_{i}}}\varphi(t)\Gamma(x+t+1)\frac{\operatorname{d}t}{2\pi\operatorname{i}},

where
p_{\left|\vec{n}\right|-1}(t)
is a polynomial of degree at most
\left|\vec{n}\right|-1
and
\varphi(t)
(and the contour
\Sigma
) will be chosen appropriately later. The orthogonality conditions can be written as

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}Q_{\vec{n}}^{(I)}(x)\frac{\Gamma(\beta+N-x+1)}{\Gamma(x-s+1)\Gamma(N-x+1)}\operatorname{d}\nu_{N}(x)=0,\quad s\in\{0,\ldots,\left|\vec{n}\right|-2\}.

Bringing the integral inside the contour integral, we get

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma}\frac{p_{\left|\vec{n}\right|-1}(t)}{\prod_{i=1}^{p}(\alpha_{i}-t)_{n_{i}}}\varphi(t)\left(\sum_{k=s}^{N}\frac{\Gamma(k+t+1)}{(k-s)!}\frac{\Gamma(N-k+\beta+1)}{(N-k)!}\right)\frac{\operatorname{d}t}{2\pi\operatorname{i}}=0.

The inner sum can be simplified using the Chu–Vandermonde identity (
6
). Specifically,

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \sum_{k=s}^{N}\frac{\Gamma(k+t+1)}{(k-s)!}\frac{\Gamma(N-k+\beta+1)}{(N-k)!}&=\sum_{k=0}^{N-s}\frac{\Gamma(k+s+t+1)}{k!}\frac{\Gamma(N-s-k+\beta+1)}{(N-s-k)!}\\
&=\Gamma(s+t+1)\Gamma(\beta+1)\frac{(s+t+\beta+2)_{N-s}}{(N-s)!}.\end{aligned}

Substituting this back into the contour integral, we have

\displaystyle\frac{\Gamma(\beta+1)}{(N-s)!}\int_{\Sigma}\frac{p_{\left|\vec{n}\right|-1}(t)}{\prod_{i=1}^{p}(\alpha_{i}-t)_{n_{i}}}\varphi(t)\Gamma(t+\beta+N+2)\frac{\Gamma(s+t+1)}{\Gamma(s+t+\beta+2)}\frac{\operatorname{d}t}{2\pi\operatorname{i}}=0.

It is convenient to choose
\varphi(t)=\Gamma(t+\beta+\left|\vec{n}\right|)/(\Gamma(t+\beta+N+2)\Gamma(t+1))
, transforming the above to

\displaystyle\frac{\Gamma(\beta+1)}{(N-s)!}\int_{\Sigma}\frac{p_{\left|\vec{n}\right|-1}(t)}{\prod_{i=1}^{p}(\alpha_{i}-t)_{n_{i}}}(t+1)_{s}(s+t+\beta+2)_{\left|\vec{n}\right|-2-s}\frac{\operatorname{d}t}{2\pi\operatorname{i}}=0,

which matches the contour integral derived in the proof of Theorem
3.8
. We can then conclude from Lemma
3.2
that
p_{\left|\vec{n}\right|-1}(t)
must be a constant, say
p_{\left|\vec{n}\right|-1}(t)=p_{\left|\vec{n}\right|-1}[0]
. The precise value of this constant is determined by the normalization condition:

\displaystyle\frac{\Gamma(\beta+1)}{(N-\left|\vec{n}\right|+1)!}\int_{\Sigma}\frac{p_{\left|\vec{n}\right|-1}[0]}{\prod_{i=1}^{p}(\alpha_{i}-t)_{n_{i}}}\frac{(t+1)_{\left|\vec{n}\right|-1}}{t+\left|\vec{n}\right|+\beta}\frac{\operatorname{d}t}{2\pi\operatorname{i}}=\Gamma(\beta+1).

Since the integrand decays as

\operatorname{O}(|t|^{-2})
as
|t|\to\infty
, we can evaluate the integral using the residue at
t=-\left|\vec{n}\right|-\beta
(note that
-\left|\vec{n}\right|-\beta<-1
for
\left|\vec{n}\right|\geq 2
):

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma}\frac{p_{\left|\vec{n}\right|-1}[0]}{\prod_{i=1}^{p}(\alpha_{i}-t)_{n_{i}}}\frac{(t+1)_{\left|\vec{n}\right|-1}}{t+\left|\vec{n}\right|+\beta}\frac{\operatorname{d}t}{2\pi\operatorname{i}}=\frac{p_{\left|\vec{n}\right|-1}[0]}{\prod_{i=1}^{p}(\alpha_{i}+\beta+\left|\vec{n}\right|)_{n_{i}}}(-\left|\vec{n}\right|-\beta+1)_{\left|\vec{n}\right|-1}.

We can therefore conclude that

\displaystyle p_{\left|\vec{n}\right|-1}[0]=(-1)^{\left|\vec{n}\right|-1}(N-\left|\vec{n}\right|+1)!\frac{\prod_{i=1}^{p}(\alpha_{i}+\beta+\left|\vec{n}\right|)_{n_{i}}}{(\beta+1)_{\left|\vec{n}\right|-1}}
.
∎

As a byproduct we find the following constructive proof of the previously given hypergeometric representation of type I Hahn multiple orthogonal polynomials.

Corollary 3.15
.

The explicit hypergeometric formula (
2.1
)
follows from the evaluation of the contour integral (
37
).

Proof.

The integrand only has simple poles inside the contour
\Sigma
at
t=\alpha_{i}+k
for
k\in\{0,\ldots,n_{i}-1\}
and
i\in\{1,\ldots,p\}
. Since
\text{Re}(t)>-1
, the poles of

\frac{\Gamma(t+\beta+\left|\vec{n}\right|)}{\Gamma(t+\beta+N+2)}=\frac{1}{(t+\beta+\left|\vec{n}\right|)_{N+2-\left|\vec{n}\right|}}

are outside of
\Sigma
(assuming that
\left|\vec{n}\right|\geq 2
). The residue at
t=\alpha_{i}+k
is given by

\frac{\Gamma(k+\alpha_{i}+\beta+\left|\vec{n}\right|)}{\Gamma(k+\alpha_{i}+1)\Gamma(k+\alpha_{i}+\beta+N+2)}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{k!(n_{i}-k-1)!}\frac{1}{\prod_{j=1,j\neq i}^{p}(\alpha_{j}-\alpha_{i}-k)_{n_{j}}}\Gamma(x+k+\alpha_{i}+1)\\
=\frac{(\left|\vec{n}\right|+\alpha_{i}+\beta)_{k}\Gamma(\left|\vec{n}\right|+\alpha_{i}+\beta)}{(\alpha_{i}+1)_{k}\Gamma(\alpha_{i}+1)(\alpha_{i}+\beta+N+2)_{k}\Gamma(\alpha_{i}+\beta+N+2)}\frac{(-n_{i}+1)_{k}}{k!(n_{i}-1)!}\\
\times\prod_{j=1,j\neq i}^{p}\frac{(\alpha_{i}-\alpha_{j}-n_{j}+1)_{k}}{(\alpha_{i}-\alpha_{j}+1)_{k}(\alpha_{j}-\alpha_{i})_{n_{j}}}\Gamma(x+k+\alpha_{i}+1).

Consequently,

Q_{\vec{n}}^{(i)}(x)=\frac{(-1)^{\left|\vec{n}\right|-1}(N-\left|\vec{n}\right|+1)!\Gamma(\left|\vec{n}\right|+\alpha_{i}+\beta)\prod_{j=1}^{p}(\alpha_{j}+\beta+\left|\vec{n}\right|)_{n_{j}}}{(\beta+1)_{\left|\vec{n}\right|-1}(n_{i}-1)!\Gamma(\alpha_{i}+\beta+N+2)\prod_{j=1,j\neq i}^{p}(\alpha_{j}-\alpha_{i})_{n_{j}}}\\
\times\sum_{k=0}^{n_{i}-1}\frac{(-n_{i}+1)_{k}(\left|\vec{n}\right|+\alpha_{i}+\beta)_{k}}{(\alpha_{i}+1)_{k}(\alpha_{i}+\beta+N+2)_{k}}\prod_{j=1,j\neq i}^{p}\frac{(\alpha_{i}-\alpha_{j}-n_{j}+1)_{k}}{(\alpha_{i}-\alpha_{j}+1)_{k}}\frac{(x+\alpha_{i}+1)_{k}}{k!},

which leads to the result in Theorem
2.1
.

∎

3.3.2.
Multiple Orthogonal Polynomials of Type II

Recall that the type II multiple Hahn polynomials are monic polynomials
Q_{\vec{n}}(x)
that satisfy the orthogonality conditions

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}Q_{\vec{n}}(x)\frac{\Gamma(x+k+\alpha_{i}+1)}{\Gamma(x+1)}\frac{\Gamma(\beta+N-x+1)}{\Gamma(N-x+1)}\operatorname{d}\nu_{N}(x)=0,

for
k\in\{0,\ldots,n_{i}-1\}
and
i\in\{1,\ldots,p\}
. Note that we consider orthogonality with respect to
(x+\alpha_{i}+1)_{k}
instead of the standard
x^{k}
, as this will be more compatible with our approach.

The analogue of Proposition
3.10
is stated below. Indeed, it follows from Stirling’s formula (
5
) that

\displaystyle\frac{\Gamma(N-Nx+\beta+1)}{\Gamma(N-Nx+1)}

\displaystyle\sim(N(1-x))^{\beta},

\displaystyle\frac{\Gamma(Nx+s)}{\Gamma(Nx+1)}

\displaystyle\sim(Nx)^{s-1},

\displaystyle N

\displaystyle\to\infty.

Proposition 3.16
.

Let us use the notation

\displaystyle\tilde{Q}_{\vec{n}}(x)=Q_{\vec{n}}(x)\frac{\Gamma(N-x+\beta+1)}{\Gamma(N-x+1)}.

Then

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \int_{0}^{\infty}\tilde{Q}_{\vec{n}}(x)\frac{\Gamma(x+s)}{\Gamma(x+1)}\operatorname{d}\nu_{N}(x)=\frac{(-1)^{\left|\vec{n}\right|}\Gamma(\left|\vec{n}\right|+\beta+1)}{(N-\left|\vec{n}\right|)!\prod_{i=1}^{p}(\alpha_{i}+\beta+\left|\vec{n}\right|+1)_{n_{i}}}\frac{\Gamma(s)\Gamma(s+N+\beta+1)}{\Gamma(s+\left|\vec{n}\right|+\beta+1)}\prod_{i=1}^{p}(\alpha_{i}+1-s)_{\left|\vec{n}\right|-1}.\end{aligned}

Proof.

To proceed, we will expand
Q_{\vec{n}}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{\left|\vec{n}\right|}Q_{\vec{n}}[k](-x)_{k}
in terms of
(-x)_{k}=(-1)^{k}\Gamma(x+1)/\Gamma(x-k+1)
. This allows us to express the integrand as follows:

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\left|\vec{n}\right|}(-1)^{k}Q_{\vec{n}}[k]\frac{\Gamma(x+s)}{\Gamma(x-k+1)}\frac{\Gamma(N-x+\beta+1)}{\Gamma(N-x+1)}.

After evaluating the integral, we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\left|\vec{n}\right|}(-1)^{k}Q_{\vec{n}}[k]\sum_{l=k}^{N}\frac{\Gamma(l+s)}{(l-k)!}\frac{\Gamma(N-l+\beta+1)}{(N-l)!}.

We can simplify the inner sum using the Chu–Vandermonde identity (
6
), which gives:

\displaystyle\Gamma(s+k)\Gamma(\beta+1)\sum_{l=0}^{N-k}\frac{(s+k)_{l}}{l!}\frac{(\beta+1)_{N-k-l}}{(N-k-l)!}=\Gamma(s+k)\Gamma(\beta+1)\frac{(s+k+\beta+1)_{N-k}}{(N-k)!}.

Thus, the integral can be written as

(38)

\displaystyle\Gamma(\beta+1)\frac{\Gamma(s)\Gamma(s+N+\beta+1)}{\Gamma(s+\left|\vec{n}\right|+\beta+1)}\sum_{k=0}^{\left|\vec{n}\right|}\frac{(-1)^{k}Q_{\vec{n}}[k]}{(N-k)!}(s)_{k}(s+k+\beta+1)_{\left|\vec{n}\right|-k}=\frac{\Gamma(s)\Gamma(s+N+\beta+1)}{\Gamma(s+\left|\vec{n}\right|+\beta+1)}p_{\left|\vec{n}\right|}(s),

where
p_{\left|\vec{n}\right|}(s)
is a polynomial of degree at most
\left|\vec{n}\right|
.

From the orthogonality conditions, we infer that
p_{\left|\vec{n}\right|}(s)
must vanish at the
\left|\vec{n}\right|
values
\alpha_{i}+k
for
k\in\{0,\ldots,n_{i}-1\}
and
i\in\{1,\ldots,p\}
. Thus, we have:

\displaystyle p_{\left|\vec{n}\right|}(s)=\mathcal{Q}_{\vec{n}}\prod_{i=1}^{p}(\alpha_{i}+1-s)_{n_{i}},

for some constant
\mathcal{Q}_{\vec{n}}\in\mathbb{R}
.

We can determine the constant
\mathcal{Q}_{\vec{n}}
by setting
s=-\left|\vec{n}\right|-\beta
in (
38
). Since
Q_{\vec{n}}(x)
is monic, and thus
Q_{\vec{n}}[\left|\vec{n}\right|]=(-1)^{\left|\vec{n}\right|}
, it follows that:

\displaystyle\Gamma(\beta+1)\frac{(-\left|\vec{n}\right|-\beta)_{\left|\vec{n}\right|}}{(N-\left|\vec{n}\right|)!}=\mathcal{Q}_{\vec{n}}\prod_{i=1}^{p}(\alpha_{i}+\beta+\left|\vec{n}\right|+1)_{n_{i}}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{\vec{n}}=\frac{(-1)^{\left|\vec{n}\right|}\Gamma(\left|\vec{n}\right|+\beta+1)}{(N-\left|\vec{n}\right|)!\prod_{i=1}^{p}(\alpha_{i}+\beta+\left|\vec{n}\right|+1)_{n_{i}}}
.
∎

Remark 3.17
.

If we make use of formula (
1.4.2
), we can now provide a constructive proof for the expression of the type II Hahn polynomials in (
20
) through the following result.

Proposition 3.18
.

Let us write
Q_{\vec{n}}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{\left|\vec{n}\right|}Q_{\vec{n}}[k](-x)_{k}
and
P_{\vec{n}}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{\left|\vec{n}\right|}P_{\vec{n}}[k]x^{k}
. Then the relationship between the coefficients
Q_{\vec{n}}[k]
and
P_{\vec{n}}[k]
can be given by:

\displaystyle Q_{\vec{n}}[k]=(-1)^{k}\frac{(N-k)!}{(N-\left|\vec{n}\right|)!}P_{\vec{n}}[k].

Proof.

It follows from the proofs of Propositions
3.10
and
3.16
, specifically from equations (
35
) and (
38
), that we have the following identities:

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\left|\vec{n}\right|}\frac{(-1)^{k}Q_{\vec{n}}[k]}{(N-k)!}(s)_{k}(s+k+\beta+1)_{\left|\vec{n}\right|-k}

\displaystyle=\frac{(-1)^{\left|\vec{n}\right|}(\beta+1)_{\left|\vec{n}\right|}}{(N-\left|\vec{n}\right|)!\prod_{i=1}^{p}(\alpha_{i}+\beta+\left|\vec{n}\right|+1)_{n_{i}}}\prod_{i=1}^{p}(\alpha_{i}+1-s)_{n_{i}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(N-\left|\vec{n}\right|)!}\sum_{k=0}^{\left|\vec{n}\right|}P_{\vec{n}}[k](s)_{k}(s+k+\beta+1)_{\left|\vec{n}\right|-k}.

We therefore have the stated result.
∎

An application of the inversion theorem for the Mellin transform in the discrete setting, as stated in Theorem
1.3
, leads to the following result. Note the duality with the integral representation for the type I linear form as shown in Theorem
3.14
.

Theorem 3.19
.

For
x\in\mathcal{L}_{N}
, the function

\displaystyle\tilde{Q}_{\vec{n}}(x)=Q_{\vec{n}}(x)\frac{\Gamma(N-x+\beta+1)}{\Gamma(N-x+1)}

is given by

\displaystyle\tilde{Q}_{\vec{n}}(x)=\frac{(-1)^{\left|\vec{n}\right|}\Gamma(\left|\vec{n}\right|+\beta+1)}{(N-\left|\vec{n}\right|)!\prod_{i=1}^{p}(\alpha_{i}+\beta+\left|\vec{n}\right|+1)_{n_{i}}}\int_{\mathcal{C}}\frac{\Gamma(s)\Gamma(s+N+\beta+1)}{\Gamma(s+\left|\vec{n}\right|+\beta+1)}\prod_{i=1}^{p}(\alpha_{i}+1-s)_{n_{i}}\frac{\Gamma(x+1)}{\Gamma(x+s+1)}\frac{\operatorname{d}s}{2\pi\operatorname{i}},

where
\mathcal{C}
is an anticlockwise contour enclosing
[-N,0]
once.

This result, along with the residue theorem, leads to the conclusion below.

Theorem 3.20
.

For
x\in\mathcal{L}_{N}
, the function

\tilde{Q}_{\vec{n}}(x)

is given by

(39)

\displaystyle\tilde{Q}_{\vec{n}}(x)=(-1)^{\left|\vec{n}\right|}\frac{\Gamma(N+\beta+1)}{(N-\left|\vec{n}\right|)!}\frac{\prod_{i=1}^{p}(\alpha_{i}+1)_{n_{i}}}{\prod_{i=1}^{p}(\alpha_{i}+\beta+\left|\vec{n}\right|+1)_{n_{i}}}{}_{p+2}F_{p+1}\biggl[\genfrac{.}{.}{0.0pt}{}{-x,-\beta-|\vec n|,\vec\alpha+\vec n+\vec e_p}{-\beta-N,\vec\alpha+\vec e_p};1\biggr].

Proof.

It follows from Theorem
3.19
and the residue theorem that

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \tilde{Q}_{\vec{n}}(x)&=\frac{(-1)^{\left|\vec{n}\right|}\Gamma(\left|\vec{n}\right|+\beta+1)}{(N-\left|\vec{n}\right|)!\prod_{i=1}^{p}(\alpha_{i}+\beta+\left|\vec{n}\right|+1)_{n_{i}}}\sum_{l=0}^{N}\frac{(-1)^{l}}{l!}\frac{\Gamma(N+\beta+1-l)}{\Gamma(\left|\vec{n}\right|+\beta+1-l)}\prod_{i=1}^{p}(\alpha_{i}+1+l)_{n_{i}}(x-l+1)_{l},\end{aligned}

which we may write as

(40)

\displaystyle\tilde{Q}_{\vec{n}}(x)=(-1)^{\left|\vec{n}\right|}\frac{\Gamma(N+\beta+1)}{(N-\left|\vec{n}\right|)!}\frac{\prod_{i=1}^{p}(\alpha_{i}+1)_{n_{i}}}{\prod_{i=1}^{p}(\alpha_{i}+\beta+\left|\vec{n}\right|+1)_{n_{i}}}\sum_{l=0}^{N}\prod_{i=1}^{p}\frac{(n_{i}+\alpha_{i}+1)_{l}}{(\alpha_{i}+1)_{l}}\frac{(-\left|\vec{n}\right|-\beta)_{l}}{(-N-\beta)_{l}}\frac{(-x)_{l}}{l!}.

This coincides with the expression in (
39
), because both series get truncated after
l=x\in\mathcal{L}_{N}
due to the Pochhammer symbol
(-x)_{l}
.
∎

Conclusions and Outlook

The paper focuses on two main objectives. Firstly, it establishes explicit hypergeometric expressions for the type I Hahn multiple orthogonal polynomials, as demonstrated in Theorem
2.1
. This result is pivotal for understanding the properties and applications of these polynomials.
Secondly,
applying
the residue theorem and the Mellin transform, the paper derives contour integral representations for various families of muliple orthogonal polynomials within the classical families. Specifically, it provides integral representations for the Laguerre of the first kind, Jacobi–Piñeiro, and both types I and II Hahn multiple orthogonal polynomials. These integral formulas constitute significant contributions to the field, leading to explicit hypergeometric representations that enhance the understanding and computational aspects of these polynomial families.

We are currently working on providing explicit hypergeometric expressions and integral representations for the discrete descendants of the Hahn polynomials in the Askey scheme. These include the multiple Meixner polynomials of the first and second kinds, as well as the Kravchuk and Charlier polynomials.

An unexplored frontier lies in mixed multiple orthogonal polynomials, which extend the classical families of multiple orthogonal polynomials discussed in this paper to the mixed multiple case. Currently, explicit hypergeometric expressions for mixed Hahn multiple orthogonal polynomials remain elusive, as do complex contour integral representations for these polynomials. This area presents an open avenue for future research, promising new insights into the interplay between different orthogonal polynomial families and their applications.

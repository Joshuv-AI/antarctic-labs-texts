Gluing variations
Piotr T. Chruściel, and Wan Cong

Abstract
We establish several results on gluing/embedding/extending geometric structures in vacuum spacetimes with a cosmological constant in any spacetime dimensions d ≥ 4 d\geq 4 , with emphasis on characteristic data. A useful tool is provided by the notion of submanifold-data of order k k . As an application of our methods we prove that vacuum Cauchy data on a spacelike Cauchy surface with boundary can always be extended to vacuum data defined beyond the boundary.

As such, the general relativistic gluing problem can be viewed as the following question: given two spacetimes, solutions of vacuum Einstein equations, can one find a third one where non-trivial subsets of each of the original spacetimes are isometrically included?

6
The “hand-crank construction”

As already pointed out, characteristic initial data on a single null hypersurface do not lead to a well posed Cauchy problem, i.e., a setup which guarantees both existence and uniqueness of associated solutions of the field equations. In this section we present a construction which provides existence of solutions of the vacuum Einstein equations realising the data.
A similar idea can be found in
Kehle:2022uvc
.

We have:

Proposition
6.1
.

Given a smooth vacuum characteristic initial data set
{{\Psi[{\mycal N},k]}}{}
,
k\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}
, on a

(n+1)
-dimensional manifold,
n\geq 3
,

of the form

{\mycal N}=[r_{1},r_{2}]\times{\mathbf{S}}\,,\qquad r_{1}<r_{2}\,,

there exist (many) smooth solutions of the vacuum Einstein equations
({\mycal M},{\mathbf{g}})
so that
{{\Psi[{\mycal N},k]}}{}
is obtained by restriction to a characteristic hypersurface within
{\mycal M}
.

Proof.

If
k<\infty
we can extend
{{\Psi[{\mycal N},k]}}{}
to
{{\Psi[{\mycal N},\infty]}}{}
in any way. Therefore it suffices to assume that
k=\infty
.

Let us write
{\mathbf{S}}_{c}
for
{\mycal N}\cap\{r=c\}
. Let
{{\Psi[{\mathbf{S}}_{r},k]}}{}
be the cross-section data induced by
{{\Psi[{\mycal N},k]}}{}
on
{\mathbf{S}}_{r}
. Let
{{{\underline{{\mycal N}}}}_{\,r_{1}}}=[0,1]\times{\mathbf{S}}
be a hypersurface on which we prescribe any smooth Isenberg-Moncrief data
({g}_{AB},\alpha)

compatible with
{{\Psi[{\mathbf{S}}_{r_{1}},k]}}{}
;
thus
({{{\underline{{\mycal N}}}}_{\,r_{1}}},{g}_{AB},\alpha)
are characteristic initial data on the hypersurface

{{{\underline{{\mycal N}}}}_{\,r_{1}}}
meeting
{\mycal N}
transversally at
r=r_{1}
towards the
future
of
{\mathbf{S}}_{r_{1}}
, see Figure
6.1
. By definition of compatibility, all derivatives of
({g}_{\mu\nu},\alpha)
in directions tangential to
{{{\underline{{\mycal N}}}}_{\,r_{1}}}
and transverse to
{\mycal N}
have to match at
{\mathbf{S}}_{r_{1}}
with those in
{{\Psi[{\mathbf{S}}_{r_{1}},k]}}{}
,
which can be achieved by Borel summation.

Similarly
let
{{{\underline{{\mycal N}}}}_{\,r_{2}}}=[0,1]\times{\mathbf{S}}
be a hypersurface, meeting
{\mycal N}
transversally at
{\mathbf{S}}_{r_{2}}
towards the
past
, on which we give smooth characteristic data compatible with

:
The “hand-crank construction”.

We can solve the characteristic Cauchy problem to the future with data on the transversally intersecting hypersurfaces

{\mycal N}

and
{{{\underline{{\mycal N}}}}_{\,r_{1}}}
, resulting in a smooth vacuum metric, say
{\mathbf{g}}_{+}
, defined on

{{\Psi[{\mycal N},k]}}{}

One can likewise solve the characteristic Cauchy problem to the past with data on the transversally intersecting hypersurfaces
{\mycal N}
and
{{{\underline{{\mycal N}}}}_{\,r_{2}}}

{\mathbf{g}}_{-}

{{\Psi[{\mycal N},k]}}{}

{\mycal N}{}

It is well known

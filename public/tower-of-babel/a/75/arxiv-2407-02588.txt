Parabolic-equivariant modules over polynomial rings in infinitely many variables Thanks: TY was supported by NSF grant DGE-2241144.
Teresa Yu

Abstract
. We study the category of 𝐏 \mathbf{P} -equivariant modules over the infinite variable polynomial ring, where 𝐏 \mathbf{P} denotes the subgroup of the infinite general linear group 𝐆𝐋 ⁡ ( 𝐂 ∞ ) \mathbf{GL}(\mathbf{C}^{\infty}) consisting of elements fixing a flag in 𝐂 ∞ \mathbf{C}^{\infty} with each graded piece infinite-dimensional. We decompose the category into simpler pieces that can be described combinatorially, and prove a number of finiteness results, such as finite generation of local cohomology and rationality of Hilbert series. Furthermore, we show that this category is equivalent to the category of representations of a particular combinatorial category generalizing 𝐅𝐈 \mathbf{FI} .



1.
Introduction

Equivariant infinite-dimensional commutative algebra has arisen in close connection to the theory of twisted commutative algebras (tca’s) and representation stability. Despite being recent developments, the results and ideas from these fields have found far-reaching impacts, such as in algebraic topology
[
1
,
12
]
, commutative algebra
[
5
,
6
]
, number theory
[
11
]
, and algebraic statistics
[
8
,
4
]
.

There are still relatively few well-understood examples in the field of equivariant infinite-dimensional commutative algebra, the most notable being the
\mathbf{GL}
-algebras
\text{Sym}(\mathbf{C}^{\infty}\otimes\mathbf{C}^{d})
and
\text{Sym}(\text{Sym}^{2}(\mathbf{C}^{\infty}))

[
18
,
19
,
14
]
. Our main contribution is a thorough study of modules over infinite variable polynomial rings that have compatible actions of an infinite parabolic subgroup of the infinite general linear group. These parabolic subgroups have not previously been considered in this context, and the techniques used in this paper highlight how to extend ideas from the study of classical tca’s to that of “multivariate” tca’s with additional symmetries. Our primary motivation for studying these equivariant modules comes from the study of
\mathfrak{S}_{\infty}
-equivariant modules over the infinite variable polynomial ring; we discuss this further in §
1.2.2
.

1.1.
Main results

Let
\mathbf{V}=\mathbf{C}^{\infty}
be an infinite-dimensional vector space equipped with a flag of length
n\geq 1
where each graded piece is infinite-dimensional. Let
\mathbf{P}\subset\mathbf{GL}(\mathbf{V})
be the parabolic subgroup of elements stabilizing this flag. Concretely,
\mathbf{P}
consists of invertible
(n\times n)
-block upper triangular matrices, so matrices along the diagonal are in
\mathbf{GL}(\mathbf{C}^{\infty})
. Let

\textbf{A}=\text{Sym}(\mathbf{V})=\mathbf{C}[x_{ij}:i\in[n],j\geq 1]

be the infinite variable polynomial ring. An
A
-module
M
is a module over the ring
A
equipped with a compatible structure of a polynomial representation of
\mathbf{P}
(see §
2.2
for a precise definition). Our goal is to study the commutative algebra of
A
by understanding the structure of the category
\text{Mod}_{\textbf{A}}
of
A
-modules.

In finite-dimensional commutative algebra, the prime spectrum of a ring provides a good starting point for the study of modules over the ring. The analogous space to study for
A
-modules is the spectrum of
\mathbf{P}
-prime ideals of
A
(defined in §
2.3
). We find that the equivariant spectrum is quite simple, as there are exactly
n+1
such
\mathbf{P}
-prime ideals that are of the form
\mathfrak{p}_{i}=(x_{jk}:1\leq j\leq n-i,k\geq 1)
, for
i=0,\ldots,n
.

Theorem 1.1
.

The
\mathbf{P}
-prime ideals of
A
form a single chain of length
n
in
A
:

0=\mathfrak{p}_{n}\subsetneq\mathfrak{p}_{n-1}\subsetneq\cdots\subsetneq\mathfrak{p}_{0}\subsetneq\textbf{A}.

This result follows from the polynomial representation theory of
\mathbf{P}
, which we studied extensively in
[
22
]
. In particular, representations of
\mathbf{P}
are not generally semisimple, and so there are much fewer indecomposable
\mathbf{P}
-submodules of
A
compared to indecomposable submodules with respect to the Levi subgroup.

The equivariant spectrum provides a way to decompose
\text{Mod}_{\textbf{A}}
. Let
\text{Mod}_{\textbf{A},\leq d}
denote the full subcategory of modules supported on
\mathfrak{p}_{d}
, i.e., locally annihilated by a power of
\mathfrak{p}_{d}
. Then we have a filtration of
\text{Mod}_{\textbf{A}}
:

\text{Mod}_{\textbf{A},\leq 0}\subset\text{Mod}_{\textbf{A},\leq 1}\subset\cdots\subset\text{Mod}_{\textbf{A},\leq n}=\text{Mod}_{\textbf{A}}.

Our goal is to understand each graded piece of the filtration, given by the Serre quotient

\text{Mod}_{\textbf{A},d}=\text{Mod}_{\textbf{A},\leq d}/\text{Mod}_{\textbf{A},\leq d-1}.

The use of this filtration to understand
\text{Mod}_{\textbf{A}}
closely follows the rank stratification used by Sam–Snowden to study
\mathbf{GL}
-equivariant modules over infinite variable polynomial rings
[
19
]
. However, we are able to more directly describe a good approximation of the graded pieces of the filtration by studying the Serre quotient
\text{Mod}_{d}^{\mathrm{gen}}
of the category of
\textbf{A}/\mathfrak{p}_{d}
-modules by the subcategory of torsion modules.

To describe these quotient categories, we show that they are equivalent to the categories
\text{Rep}(H_{d})
of polynomial representations of subgroups
H_{d}\subset\mathbf{P}
that stabilize certain points in the classical spectrum of
A
. We provide a combinatorial description for
\text{Rep}(H_{d})
which allows us to deduce finiteness results for
\text{Mod}_{d}^{\mathrm{gen}}
.

Theorem 1.2
.

For each
d\in[n]
, the category
\text{Mod}_{d}^{\mathrm{gen}}
is locally of finite length, and finite length objects have finite injective dimension.

Although
\text{Mod}^{\mathrm{gen}}_{d}
is smaller than
\text{Mod}_{\textbf{A},d}
, the categories are sufficiently close for us to understand
\text{Mod}_{\textbf{A},d}
from this description. We are then able to study
\text{Mod}_{\textbf{A}}
more broadly from these graded pieces.
To accomplish this, we study local cohomology
\mathrm{R}^{i}\Gamma_{d}
and derived saturation
\mathrm{R}^{i}\Sigma_{d}
with respect to each prime
\mathfrak{p}_{d}
(see §
5
for definitions), and show that they preserve finite generation.

Theorem 1.3
.

For each
d=0,\ldots,n
and any finitely generated
A
-module
M
, the local cohomology modules
\mathrm{R}^{i}\Gamma_{>d}(M)
and derived saturation modules
\mathrm{R}^{i}\Sigma_{>d}(M)
are finitely generated for all
i
, and they vanish for
i\gg 0
.

This theorem is instrumental in proving our main structural results on the category
\text{Mod}_{\textbf{A}}
and the bounded derived category
\mathrm{D}^{b}_{\mathrm{fg}}(\textbf{A})
of finitely generated
A
-modules:

•

We show that the Krull–Gabriel dimension of
\text{Mod}_{\textbf{A}}
is
n
, and so any positive integer can be realized as the dimension of a category studied in this paper
(Proposition
4.9
).

•

We describe a semi-orthogonal decomposition of
\mathrm{D}^{b}_{\mathrm{fg}}(\textbf{A})
, where each piece can be described combinatorially via the categories
\text{Rep}(H_{d})
(Corollary
5.10
).

•

We find a set of generators for
\mathrm{D}^{b}_{\mathrm{fg}}(\textbf{A})
as a triangulated category (Corollary
5.11
).

•

We show that the Grothendieck group
\mathrm{K}(\textbf{A})
of
\text{Mod}_{\textbf{A}}
is a free module of rank
(n+1)
over
\Lambda^{\otimes n}
, where
\Lambda
denotes the ring of symmetric polynomials (Theorem
5.13
).

•

We provide a natural definition for the Hilbert series of an
A
-module, and prove a corresponding rationality result for the Hilbert series that moreover detects the support of the module (Theorem
5.14
).

Finally, we define in §
6
a combinatorial category
\mathbf{FI}(n)
that generalizes the category
\mathbf{FI}
of finite sets and injections to a “weighted” setting. Thus,
\mathbf{FI}(n)
-modules generalize
\mathbf{FI}
-modules, which were introduced by Church–Ellenberg–Farb and have found numerous applications
[
1
]
. We show that the category of
\mathbf{FI}(n)
-modules is equivalent to the category
\text{Mod}_{\textbf{A}}
of
A
-modules as tensor categories (Theorem
6.5
). When
n=1
, this recovers the equivalence between
\mathbf{FI}
-modules and
\mathbf{GL}(\mathbf{C}^{\infty})
-equivariant modules over
\text{Sym}(\mathbf{C}^{\infty})
.

1.2.
Relation to other work

1.2.1.
Equivariant modules over infinite-dimensional algebras

Motivated by the ubiquity of
\mathbf{FI}
-modules and their equivalence with
\mathbf{GL}(\mathbf{C}^{\infty})
-equivariant modules over the infinite variable polynomial ring
\text{Sym}(\mathbf{C}^{\infty})
, Sam–Snowden studied the category of such modules from an algebraic and homological perspective in
[
18
]
. This has sparked the systematic study of equivariant modules over infinite-dimensional algebras from the perspective of commutative and homological algebra; see
[
7
, Table 1]
for a summary of the literature. The arguments and ideas of this paper are most closely related to those appearing in
[
14
,
19
,
20
]
.

1.2.2.
The infinite symmetric group

One of the original examples of an infinite-dimensional algebra that is noetherian up to symmetry comes from the infinite variable polynomial ring
R=\mathbf{C}[\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\ldots]
with the action of the infinite symmetric group
\mathfrak{S}_{\infty}

[
3
]
. However, the structure of
\mathfrak{S}_{\infty}
-equivariant modules over
R
is still quite mysterious. Only recently were the
\mathfrak{S}_{\infty}
-stable ideals of
R
classified by Nagpal–Snowden
[
15
]
, and there are still a number of open problems and conjectures on the asymptotic behavior of invariants related to these ideals
[
9
,
10
,
13
]
.

Let
\mathfrak{h}_{n}=(\xi_{i}^{n}:i\geq 1)
denote the ideal of
R
generated by the
n^{\text{th}}
powers of all the variables. There is a natural way to consider an
\mathbf{FI}(n)
-module as a module over
R/\mathfrak{h}_{n+1}
with a compatabile
\mathfrak{S}_{\infty}
-action, and so we have a functor
\text{Mod}_{\textbf{A}}\to\text{Mod}_{R/\mathfrak{h}_{n+1}}
. We hope the results in this paper, especially those on the structure of
\text{Mod}^{\mathrm{gen}}_{n}
, will see use in studying the category of symmetric
R/\mathfrak{h}_{n+1}
-modules and
R
-modules more generally.

1.2.3.
Representation stability and applications

Finiteness properties of equivariant infinite-dimensional algebras are intimately linked to the notion of representation stability as introduced by Church–Farb
[
2
]
. One of the most notable examples of representation stability comes from the cohomology of configuration spaces of distinct points: fixing the cohomological degree while varying the number of points, the cohomology groups form a finitely generated
\mathbf{FI}
-module and therefore exhibit representation stability
[
1
]
. We hope that the weighted generalizations of
\mathbf{FI}
-modules introduced in this paper will have similar applications, perhaps to configuration spaces of multicolored points.

Preliminaries

2.1.
Representation theory of
\mathbf{P}

Let
\mathbf{GL}=\bigcup_{i=1}^{\infty}\mathbf{GL}_{i}(\mathbf{C})
denote the infinite general linear group with defining representation
\mathbf{V}=\mathbf{C}^{\infty}
. Equip
\mathbf{V}
with a flag
0=\mathbf{V}_{0}\subset\mathbf{V}_{1}\subset\cdots\subset\mathbf{V}_{n}=\mathbf{V}
of length
n
, where each associated graded piece
\mathbf{V}_{(i)}=\mathbf{V}_{i}/\mathbf{V}_{i-1}=\mathbf{C}^{\infty}
is infinite-dimensional, with basis
\{e_{ij}:j\geq 1\}
. Define
\mathbf{P}\subset\mathbf{GL}
to be the subgroup preserving this flag. We call
\mathbf{V}
equipped with the choice of flag the
standard representation
of
\mathbf{P}
, and say a representation of
\mathbf{P}
is
polynomial
if it occurs as a subquotient of a (possibly infinite) direct sum of tensor powers of the standard representation. We let
\text{Rep}(\mathbf{P})
denote the category of polynomial representations of
\mathbf{P}
. We review some of the important aspects of polynomial representation theory of the group
\mathbf{P}
, which was studied in
[
22
]
.

(1)

Let
\mathbf{FB}(n)
denote the following combinatorial category: objects are
[n]
-weighted finite sets, and morphisms are bijections that do not decrease weights. An
\mathbf{FB}(n)
-module
is a functor
\mathbf{FB}(n)\to\text{Vec}
, and the tensor product of
\mathbf{FB}(n)
-modules is defined by Day convolution. The categories of finite length
\mathbf{FB}(n)
-modules and finite length polynomial
\mathbf{P}
-representations are equivalent as tensor categories
[
22
, Theorem 1.1]
.

(2)

Let
\textbf{L}\subset\mathbf{P}
denote the Levi subgroup, so
\textbf{L}\cong\prod_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{GL}
. Polynomial representations of
L
are semisimple, and every polynomial
\mathbf{P}
-representation can be considered as a polynomial
L
-representation via restriction. Let
\mathbf{FB}
denote the combinatorial category of finite sets and bijections, and let
\mathbf{FB}^{\otimes n}
denote its
n
-fold product. One can consider
\mathbf{FB}^{\otimes n}
as a subcategory of
\mathbf{FB}(n)
using the
[n]
-weighting, and so
\mathbf{FB}(n)
-modules can also be regarded as
\mathbf{FB}^{\otimes n}
-modules. The categories of polynomial
L
-representations and
\mathbf{FB}^{\otimes n}
-modules are equivalent using classical Schur–Weyl duality, i.e., the equivalence between
\mathbf{FB}
-modules and polynomial representations of
\mathbf{GL}

[
16
, §5.4]
.

Given a polynomial
L
-representation
V
, there is a decomposition of
V
with respect to the weight spaces of the torus action. We identify weights with
n
-tuples of nonnegative integer sequences
\alpha=((\alpha_{1j}),\ldots,(\alpha_{nj}))
such that each sequence
(\alpha_{ij})_{j\geq 1}
is eventually zero. If two weights
\alpha,\beta
have disjoint supports, i.e., for any given
i\in[n]
and
j\geq 1
,
\alpha_{ij}
and
\beta_{ij}
are not both nonzero, then we say that the weights are
disjoint
.

(3)

The indecomposable injective objects
T_{\underline{\lambda}}
of
\text{Rep}(\mathbf{P})
are indexed by
n
-tuples of partitions
\underline{\lambda}=(\lambda^{1},\ldots,\lambda^{n})

[
22
, Proposition 4.8]
. The object
T_{\underline{\lambda}}
is given by

T_{\underline{\lambda}}=\bigotimes_{i=1}^{n}\textbf{S}_{\lambda^{i}}(\mathbf{V}/\mathbf{V}_{n-i}),

where
\textbf{S}_{\lambda^{i}}(-)
denotes the Schur functor corresponding to the partition
\lambda^{i}
. Furthermore, it is the injective envelope of the simple
\mathbf{P}
-representation

S_{\underline{\lambda}}=\bigotimes_{i=1}^{n}\textbf{S}_{\lambda^{i}}(\mathbf{V}_{n-i+1}/\mathbf{V}_{n-i}).

The projective cover of the simple
S_{\underline{\lambda}}
is the representation
\bigotimes_{i=1}^{n}\textbf{S}_{\lambda^{i}}(\mathbf{V}_{n-i+1}).

Finite length objects have finite injective and projective resolutions.

Note that the simple
\mathbf{P}
-representations are given by simple
L
-representations, and so the Grothendieck ring
\mathrm{K}(\text{Rep}(\mathbf{P}))
can be identified with the ring
\Lambda^{\otimes n}
, where
\Lambda
denotes the ring of symmetric polynomials.

(4)

Let
a\in\mathbf{N}
be a nonnegative integer. Let
G_{a}
denote the subgroup of
\mathbf{P}
consisting of block diagonal matrices such that each nonzero block is of the form

\begin{pmatrix}\text{id}_{a}&0\\
0&*\end{pmatrix},

where
\text{id}_{a}
denotes the
a\times a
identity matrix. Let
P_{a}
denote the subgroup of
\mathbf{P}
consisting of matrices where the
(i,j)
-blocks are each block matrices of the form

\begin{pmatrix}*&0\\
0&\text{id}\end{pmatrix}\quad\text{if }i=j,\quad\begin{pmatrix}*&0\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}\quad\text{if }i<j,

where the top left blocks are of size
a\times a
and the bottom right blocks are of size
(\infty-a)\times(\infty-a)
in both cases. We have that
G_{a}
and
P_{a}
commute with each other, and so we have a left-exact specialization functor
\mathscr{S}_{a}:\text{Rep}(\mathbf{P})\to\text{Rep}(P_{a})
defined by taking
G_{a}
invariants,
V\mapsto V^{G_{a}}
. By
[
22
, Lemma 3.1]
, this is a tensor functor. Furthermore, for any element
v\in V
, we have that
v\in\mathscr{S}_{a}(V)
for any
a\gg 0
, and the
L
-weight of any element in
\mathscr{S}_{a}(V)
is given by an
n
-tuple
\alpha=((\alpha_{ij}))
of sequences such that for each
i
, the sequence
(\alpha_{ij})_{j\geq 1}
is
0
for
j>a
.

(5)

Let
\text{End}(\mathbf{V})
be the monoid of endomorphisms of
\mathbf{V}
that preserve the flag structure on
\mathbf{V}
, i.e., linear maps
f:\mathbf{V}\to\mathbf{V}
such that
f(\mathbf{V}_{i})\subset\mathbf{V}_{i}
for all
i
. For any polynomial representation
V
of
\mathbf{P}
, there is an induced action of
\text{End}(\mathbf{V})
. In particular, let
a\geq 1
, and let
W\subset V
be the subspace spanned by elements with
L
-weight disjoint from the weight
((1^{a}),\ldots,(1^{a}))
. Then if
g,h\in\mathbf{P}
, there exists an endomorphism
f
whose action on
\mathscr{S}_{a}(V)
agrees with that of
g
on
\mathscr{S}_{a}(V)
, while the action of
f
on elements in
W
agrees with that of
h
.

2.2.
\text{Mod}_{\textbf{A}}
and noetherianity

Let
A
denote the commutative algebra object
\text{Sym}(\mathbf{V})
in
\text{Rep}(\mathbf{P})
. We consider
A
as an infinite variable polynomial ring over
\mathbf{C}
with variables
\{x_{ij}:i\in[n],j\geq 1\}
, where the corresponding basis for the associated graded piece
\mathbf{V}_{i}/\mathbf{V}_{i-1}
is given by the variables
\{x_{ij}:j\geq 1\}
. We let
\lvert\textbf{A}\rvert
denote the same infinite variable polynomial ring but without the
\mathbf{P}
-representation structure.

An
A
-module

M
is a module object in
\text{Rep}(\mathbf{P})
, i.e.,
M
is a polynomial
\mathbf{P}
-representation equipped with a multiplication map
\textbf{A}\otimes M\to M
that is equivariant with respect to
\mathbf{P}
. Let
\text{Mod}_{\textbf{A}}
denote the category of all
A
-modules. An
A
-module
M
is said to be
finitely generated
if there exist finitely many elements such that their
\mathbf{P}
-orbits generate
M
as an
\lvert\textbf{A}\rvert
-module.
If
M
is a finitely generated
A
-module, then there exists a surjection
\textbf{A}\otimes V\to M
, where
V
is a polynomial
\mathbf{P}
-representation of finite length. We say an
A
-module
M
is
noetherian
if any submodule is finitely generated.

Another finiteness condition for
A
-modules is defined using the structure of being a polynomial representation of
L
. Polynomial representations of
L
are semisimple, and since the simple
\mathbf{P}
-representations are also the simple
L
-representations, such representations are also indexed by
n
-tuples
\underline{\lambda}=(\lambda^{1},\ldots,\lambda^{n})
of partitions. We say a polynomial
L
-representation
V
is
bounded
if there exists an integer
s
such that for any simple representation
S_{\underline{\lambda}}
appearing as a factor in
V
, we have that
\lambda^{i}_{t}=0
for all
i\in[n]
and
t>s
, i.e., the length of each partition
\lambda^{i}
is at most
s
. If
V
is bounded, we let
\ell(V)
denote the minimum such
s

Example 2.1
.

(1)

Any finite length
L
-representation is bounded.

(2)

As a polynomial
L
-representation, the algebra
A
is bounded: every simple object appearing is indexed by a tuple of partitions of the form
((a_{1}),\ldots,(a_{n}))
with
a_{i}\in\mathbf{N}
, and so
\ell(\textbf{A})=1
.

(3)

If
V,W
are bounded representations, then one sees that their tensor product
V\otimes W
is also bounded using the Littlewood–Richardson rule. Therefore, any finitely generated
A
-module
M
is bounded, since it is a quotient of a bounded representation
\textbf{A}\otimes V
with
V
of finite length.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
M
be a finitely generated (
\mathbf{P}
-equivariant)
A
-module. Then it is finitely generated as an
L
-equivariant module of
A
.

Proof.

By
[
22
, Theorem 1.1]
, the categories of finite length polynomial
\mathbf{P}
-representations and finite length
\mathbf{FB}(n)
-modules are equivalent. A finite length
\mathbf{FB}(n)
-module is also finite length as a module over the subcategory
\mathbf{FB}^{\otimes n}\subset\mathbf{FB}(n)
. Thus, a finite length polynomial
\mathbf{P}
-representation also has finite length as a representation of
\textbf{L}\subset\mathbf{P}
.

Now, suppose
M
is a finitely generated
A
-module. Then
M
is the quotient of an
A
-module of the form
\textbf{A}\otimes V
, where
V
is a finite length polynomial
\mathbf{P}
-representation. By the previous paragraph,
V
is also a finite length polynomial
L
-representation. Thus,
M
is also finitely generated as an
L
-equivariant module of
A
.
∎

Proposition 2.3
.

A
is noetherian as an algebra, i.e., every finitely generated
A
-module is noetherian.

Proof.

The ring
A
is finitely generated as an algebra object in the category of polynomial
L
-representations, and it is essentially bounded. Then by the multivariate version of
[
14
, Proposition 2.4]
, every finitely generated
L
-equivariant module of
A
is noetherian. If
M
is a finitely generated
\mathbf{P}
-equivariant module of
A
, then it is finitely generated as an
L
-equivariant module by Lemma
2.2
. Furthermore, any chain of
\mathbf{P}
-equivariant submodules of
M
is also a chain of
L
-equivariant submodules. Thus,
M
as a
\mathbf{P}
-equivariant module satisfies the ascending chain condition.
∎

2.3.
Equivariant ideals and
\text{Spec}\,_{\mathbf{P}}(\textbf{A})

A
\mathbf{P}
-ideal
of
A
is an
A
-submodule of
A
. Equivalently, it is an ideal of the ring
\lvert\textbf{A}\rvert
in the usual sense that is stable under the action of
\mathbf{P}
. A
\mathbf{P}
-ideal
\mathfrak{p}
is said to be a
\mathbf{P}
-prime ideal
if for any
\mathbf{P}
-ideals
\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{b}
such that
\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{b}\subset\mathfrak{p}
, we have that either
\mathfrak{a}\subset\mathfrak{p}
or
\mathfrak{b}\subset\mathfrak{p}
.

For
i=0,\ldots,n
, let
\mathfrak{p}_{i}\subset\textbf{A}
be the ideal

\mathfrak{p}_{i}=(x_{jk}:1\leq j\leq n-i,k\geq 1),

so
\mathfrak{p}_{n}
is the zero ideal and
\mathfrak{p}_{0}
is the homogeneous maximal ideal of
A
.
In particular, we have that

0=\mathfrak{p}_{n}\subset\mathfrak{p}_{n-1}\subset\cdots\subset\mathfrak{p}_{0}\subset\textbf{A}.

It is clear that sums of powers of these ideals are
\mathbf{P}
-ideals. The following result shows that all
\mathbf{P}
-ideals are of this form, and that the
\mathfrak{p}_{i}
’s constitute all of the
\mathbf{P}
-prime ideals of
A
.

Theorem 2.4
.

Every
\mathbf{P}
-ideal is of the form
\mathfrak{p}_{i_{1}}^{a_{1}}+\cdots+\mathfrak{p}_{i_{m}}^{a_{m}}
, with
n\geq i_{1}>\cdots>i_{m}\geq 0
and
1\leq a_{1}<\cdots<a_{m}
. Every
\mathbf{P}
-prime ideal is of the form
\mathfrak{p}_{i}
.

Proof.

Every indecomposable
\mathbf{P}
-submodule of
\textbf{A}=\text{Sym}(\mathbf{V})
is of the form
\text{Sym}^{a}(\mathbf{V}_{i})
, and so all
\mathbf{P}
-ideals are given by sums of powers of the
\mathfrak{p}_{i}
’s. If
n\geq i>j\geq 0
and
a\geq b\geq 1
, then
\mathfrak{p}_{i}^{a}+\mathfrak{p}_{j}^{b}=\mathfrak{p}_{j}^{b}
. Thus, all
\mathbf{P}
-ideals can be written in the desired form.

Using this characterization of
\mathbf{P}
-ideals, one sees that the
\mathfrak{p}_{i}
’s are indeed
\mathbf{P}
-prime. Furthermore, these are all of the
\mathbf{P}
-primes, since
\mathbf{P}
-prime ideals are prime in the usual sense.
∎

We define the
\mathbf{P}
-equivariant spectrum

\text{Spec}\,_{\mathbf{P}}(\textbf{A})
to be the set of
\mathbf{P}
-prime ideals of
A
equipped with the Zariski topology. The following corollary will be used to compute the Krull–Gabriel dimension of the category
\text{Mod}_{\textbf{A}}
in §
4
.

Corollary 2.5
.

The Krull dimension of
\text{Spec}\,_{\mathbf{P}}(\textbf{A})
is
n
.

Remark 2.6
.

For a ring
A
that may or may not be
\mathbf{P}
-equivariant, we use
\text{Spec}\,(A)
to denote the usual spectrum of
A
.

2.4.
Torsion modules

For
d=0,\ldots,n
, let
A_{d}=\textbf{A}/\mathfrak{p}_{d}
. We say an
A_{d}
-module
M
is
torsion
if every element of
M
is annihilated by a nonzero element of
A_{d}
. To understand the structure of
\text{Mod}_{\textbf{A}}
, we will study the category
\text{Mod}_{A_{d}}
of
A_{d}
-modules and its Serre quotient by the subcategory of torsion modules. The following result gives another characterization of torsion modules.

Proposition 2.7
.

Let
M
be a finitely generated
A_{d}
-module. Then
M
is torsion if and only if it is annihilated by a nonzero
\mathbf{P}
-ideal.

Proof.

It is clear that if
M
is annihilated by a
\mathbf{P}
-ideal, then it is torsion. Now assume
M
is nonzero and torsion. Given
x\in M
nonzero, let
a\in A_{d}
nonzero be such that
ax=0
. We claim there exists a
k
such that
a^{k}(gx)=0
for all
g\in\mathbf{P}
. If this holds, then the
\mathbf{P}
-orbit of
a^{k}
kills
x
, and so the nonzero
\mathbf{P}
-ideal generated by
a^{k}
kills
x
. Then, if
M
is finitely generated by elements
x_{1},\ldots,x_{r}
, one can find a single nonzero element
a\in A_{d}
such that
ax_{i}=0
for each
i
; here,
a\neq 0
since
\lvert A_{d}\rvert
is a domain. By the claim, there exists a single
k\gg 0
for which the nonzero
\mathbf{P}
-ideal generated by
a^{k}
kills each
x_{i}
, and so this
\mathbf{P}
-ideal also annihilates
M
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
denote the Lie algebra of
\mathbf{P}
, and let
\mathscr{U}(\mathfrak{g})
be the universal enveloping algebra. We first show by induction on
k
that if
X_{1},\ldots,X_{k}\in\mathfrak{g}
, then
a^{k+1}X_{k}\cdots X_{1}x=0
. The
k=0
case holds by assumption that
ax=0
, so suppose
a^{k}X_{k-1}\cdots X_{1}x=0
, and apply
X_{k}
to this product:

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=(X_{k}(a^{k}))(X_{k-1}\cdots X_{1}x)+a^{k}(X_{k}X_{k-1}\cdots X_{1}x)

\displaystyle=ka^{k-1}(X_{k}a)(X_{k-1}\cdots X_{1}x)+a^{k}X_{k}\cdots X_{1}x.

Then, multiplying by
a
, we see that the first term is
0
by induction (note that
a^{k},X_{k}a\in A_{d}
and therefore commute), and so
a^{k+1}X_{k}\cdots X_{1}x=0
as desired.

Let
V\subset M
be the
\mathbf{P}
-representation generated by
x
, and let
b\geq 1
be such that
a\in\mathscr{S}_{b}(A_{d})
. Using the action of the Levi subgroup
\textbf{L}\subset\mathbf{P}
, one sees that there exists
y\in V
such that the weight of
y
with respect to
L
is disjoint from that of
a
, and that
y
also generates
V
as a
\mathbf{P}
-representation.
Let
X\in\mathscr{U}(\mathfrak{g})
be such that
Xx=y
, and so by the above, there exists
k
such that
a^{k}y=0
. We now show that this same
k
is such that
a^{k}(gx)=0
for all
g\in\mathbf{P}
.

Fix
g\in\mathbf{P}
. Since
a,y
have disjoint
L
-weights, there exists an endomorphism
F_{g}:\mathbf{V}\to\mathbf{V}
preserving the flag structure on
\mathbf{V}
, and such that the action of
F_{g}
on
y
agrees with that of
g
,
and the action of
F_{g}
on
\mathscr{S}_{b}(\mathbf{V})
agrees with that of the identity. In particular,
F_{g}(a)=a
, while
F_{g}(y)=gy
. Then,

0=F_{g}(a^{k}y)=F_{g}(a^{k})F_{g}(y)=a^{k}(gy).

Every element in the
\mathbf{P}
-orbit of
x
can be realized as an element in the
\mathbf{P}
-orbit of
y
, and so the proof is complete.
∎

3.
Representations of stabilizer subgroups

For each
d\in[n]
, let
\xi_{d}:\mathbf{V}\to\mathbf{C}
be the linear form defined by

\xi_{d}(e_{ij})=\begin{cases}1\quad&(i,j)=(n-d+1,1),\\
0\quad&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Let
H_{d}\subset\mathbf{P}
be the subgroup that stabilizes
\xi_{d}
. Concretely,
H_{d}
consists of matrices of
\mathbf{P}
such that the
(n-d+1,n-d+1)
-block is an element of the infinite general affine group
\textbf{GA}(\mathbf{V}_{(n-d+1)})
whose top row is zero except for a one in the upper left corner, and whose top rows of the
(n-d+1,i)
-blocks for
i>n-d+1
are all zero.

A representation
V
of
H_{d}
is
polynomial
if it occurs as a subquotient of a (possibly infinite) direct sum of tensor powers of
\mathbf{V}
. In this section, we describe the category
\text{Rep}(H_{d})
of polynomial
H_{d}
-representations.

3.1.
Combinatorial description

For each
d\in[n]
, we define a combinatorial category
\mathscr{C}_{d}
as follows. Objects are
[n]
-weighted finite sets
S=\bigsqcup_{i=1}^{n}S_{i}
, where the elements of the subset
S_{i}
have weight
i
. A morphism is an injection of sets
\varphi:S\to T
such that weights do not decrease, i.e.,
\varphi(S_{i})\subset\bigsqcup_{j=i}^{n}T_{j}
, and
\varphi
is surjective on
\bigsqcup_{i=1}^{d-1}T_{i}
. A
\mathscr{C}_{d}
-module
is a functor
\mathscr{C}_{d}\to\text{Vec}
. Let
\text{Mod}_{\mathscr{C}_{d}}
denote the category of
\mathscr{C}_{d}
-modules, and let
\text{Mod}_{\mathscr{C}_{d}}^{\mathrm{lf}}
denote the full subcategory spanned by locally finite length objects. We will show that
\text{Mod}^{\mathrm{lf}}_{\mathscr{C}_{d}}
is equivalent to
\text{Rep}(H_{d})
. Before showing this equivalence, we describe some important aspects of this category.

Let
\underline{a}\in\mathbf{N}^{n}
be an
n
-tuple of nonnegative integers. There is a weighted set with elements of weight
i
given by
[a_{i}]=\{1,\ldots,a_{i}\}
; we also use
\underline{a}
to denote this object. Every object of
\mathscr{C}_{d}
is isomorphic to a unique
\underline{a}
, and the group of automorphisms of
\underline{a}
is the group
\mathfrak{S}_{a_{1}}\times\cdots\times\mathfrak{S}_{a_{n}}
, which we denote by
\mathfrak{S}_{\underline{a}}
.

For a tuple of partitions
\underline{\lambda}
with
\underline{a}=(\lvert\lambda^{i}\rvert)
, we have the simple
\mathscr{C}_{d}
-module
\textbf{M}_{\underline{\lambda}}
given by

\underline{b}\mapsto\begin{cases}M_{\lambda^{1}}\boxtimes\cdots\boxtimes M_{\lambda^{n}}&\quad\text{if $\underline{b}=\underline{a}$,}\\
0&\quad\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

where
M_{\lambda^{i}}
denotes the simple
\mathfrak{S}_{a_{i}}
-module corresponding to the partition
\lambda^{i}
. All simple
\mathscr{C}_{d}
-modules are of this form. We also use
\textbf{M}_{\underline{\lambda}}
to denote the corresponding simple
\mathfrak{S}_{\underline{a}}
-module.

The category
\mathscr{C}_{d}
is
inwards finite
, meaning for every object
\underline{a}
, there are only finitely many objects
\underline{b}
up to isomorphism such that there exists a morphism
\underline{b}\to\underline{a}
in
\mathscr{C}_{d}
. This implies that the injective envelope of a simple object has finite length, and every
\mathscr{C}_{d}
-module of finite length has finite injective dimension
[
17
, §2.1.5]
.

3.2.
Simple representations

Let
W_{d}\subset\mathbf{V}
denote the
H_{d}
-submodule
W_{d}=\ker\xi_{d}\cap\mathbf{V}_{n-d+1}
. For an
n
-tuple
\underline{\lambda}
, consider the following polynomial
H_{d}
-representation:

\displaystyle S_{d,\underline{\lambda}}

\displaystyle=\left(\bigotimes_{i\neq d}\textbf{S}_{\lambda^{i}}(\mathbf{V}_{(n-i+1)})\right)\otimes\textbf{S}_{\lambda^{d}}(W_{d}/\mathbf{V}_{n-d}).

It is irreducible, as it is irreducible as a representation of the Levi subgroup
L_{d}\subset H_{d}
.
The goal of this subsection is to show that these are all of the simple
H_{d}
-modules.

Consider the opposite category
\mathscr{C}^{\mathrm{op}}_{d}
of the combinatorial category described above. We define a functor
\mathscr{T}_{d}:\mathscr{C}_{d}^{\mathrm{op}}\to\text{Rep}(H_{d})
as follows. For an object
\underline{a}
, define the corresponding polynomial
H_{d}
-representation, denoted
T_{d,\underline{a}}
, by

T_{d,\underline{a}}=\bigotimes_{i=1}^{n}(\mathbf{V}/\mathbf{V}_{n-i})^{\otimes a_{i}}.

Given a morphism
\varphi:\underline{a}\to\underline{b}
in
\mathscr{C}_{d}^{\mathrm{op}}
, we define
T_{d,\underline{a}}\to T_{d,\underline{b}}
as follows. If
x\in\underline{a}
is an element of weight
i
for which
\varphi(x)
is defined and is of weight
j\leq i
, then map the tensor factor
\mathbf{V}/\mathbf{V}_{n-i}
of
T_{d,\underline{a}}
corresponding to
x
to the tensor factor
\mathbf{V}/\mathbf{V}_{n-j}
of
T_{d,\underline{b}}
corresponding to
\varphi(x)
via the natural quotient. If
\varphi(x)
is not defined, then
x
is of weight
i\geq d
and so
n-i\leq n-d
. The linear functional
\xi_{d}
is therefore well-defined on
\mathbf{V}/\mathbf{V}_{n-i}
and we apply it to the corresponding tensor factor.

Example 3.1
.

Let
n=3
and
d=3
. Consider the objects
\underline{b}=(1,2,0)
and
\underline{a}=(0,2,2)
in
\mathscr{C}_{3}
, and define a morphism
\underline{b}\to\underline{a}
by mapping an element of weight
2
in
\underline{b}
to an element of weight
3
in
\underline{a}
, and mapping the other two elements of
\underline{b}
to the elements of weight
2
in
\underline{a}
. Let
\varphi:\underline{a}\to\underline{b}
be the induced morphism in
\mathscr{C}_{3}^{\mathrm{op}}
. Then the corresponding map of polynomial
H_{3}
-representations is given by

(\mathbf{V}/\mathbf{V}_{1})\otimes(\mathbf{V}/\mathbf{V}_{1})\otimes\mathbf{V}\otimes\mathbf{V}\to(\mathbf{V}/\mathbf{V}_{2})\otimes(\mathbf{V}/\mathbf{V}_{1})\otimes(\mathbf{V}/\mathbf{V}_{1}),

where the first three tensor factors of the left side map onto the right side via the maps
\mathbf{V}/\mathbf{V}_{1}\to\mathbf{V}/\mathbf{V}_{2}
,
\mathbf{V}/\mathbf{V}_{1}\to\mathbf{V}/\mathbf{V}_{1}
, and
\mathbf{V}\to\mathbf{V}/\mathbf{V}_{1}
, while the linear functional
\xi_{3}:\mathbf{V}\to\mathbf{C}
is applied to the last tensor factor
\mathbf{V}
.

For a tuple
\underline{a}
, define the polynomial
H_{d}
-representation
K_{d,\underline{a}}
by

K_{d,\underline{a}}=\bigcap\ker(T_{d,\underline{a}}\to T_{d,\underline{b}}),

where the intersection is over all non-isomorphisms
\underline{a}\to\underline{b}
in
\mathscr{C}_{d}^{\mathrm{op}}
; note that this intersection is finite since
\mathscr{C}_{d}
is inwards finite.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\underline{a}
be a tuple. Then

K_{d,\underline{a}}=\left(\bigotimes_{i\neq d}\mathbf{V}_{(n-i+1)}^{\otimes a_{i}}\right)\otimes(W_{d}/\mathbf{V}_{n-d})^{\otimes a_{d}}.

Proof.

First, let
j\in[n-1]
and
\underline{b}=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{j-1},0,a_{j}+a_{j+1},a_{j+2},\ldots,a_{n})
. Consider the following morphism
\varphi_{j}:\underline{a}\to\underline{b}
in
\mathscr{C}^{\mathrm{op}}_{d}
: it is a bijection as a map of sets, and it is the identity on elements of weight not equal to
j
. Then the kernel of the surjection
\varphi_{j}:T_{d,\underline{a}}\to T_{d,\underline{b}}
is

\left(\bigotimes_{i\neq j}(\mathbf{V}/\mathbf{V}_{n-i})^{\otimes a_{i}}\right)\otimes\mathbf{V}_{(n-j+1)}^{\otimes a_{j}}.

Now let
\underline{c}=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{d-1},0,a_{d+1},\ldots,a_{n})
, and consider the morphism
\psi:\underline{a}\to\underline{c}
in
\mathscr{C}^{\mathrm{op}}_{d}
given by the identity on elements of weight not equal to
d
. Then the surjection
\psi:T_{d,\underline{a}}\to T_{d,\underline{c}}
has kernel

\left(\bigotimes_{i\neq d}(\mathbf{V}/\mathbf{V}_{n-i})^{\otimes a_{i}}\right)\otimes(W_{d}/\mathbf{V}_{n-d})^{\otimes a_{d}}.

Thus,

K_{d,\underline{a}}\subset\ker\psi\cap\bigcap_{j}\ker\varphi_{j}=\left(\bigotimes_{i\neq d}(\mathbf{V}_{n-i+1}/\mathbf{V}_{n-i})^{\otimes a_{i}}\right)\otimes(W_{d}/\mathbf{V}_{n-d})^{\otimes a_{d}}.

The vector

v=\left(\bigotimes_{i\neq d}e_{n-i+1,1}\otimes\cdots\otimes e_{n-i+1,a_{i}}\right)\otimes e_{n-d+1,2}\otimes\cdots\otimes e_{n-d+1,a_{d}+1}

generates the right-hand side as an
H_{d}
-module, and this vector has weight

(1^{a_{n}},\ldots,1^{a_{d+1}},0,1^{a_{d}},\ldots,1^{a_{1}})

under the action of the Levi subgroup
L_{d}
. Thus, if
T_{d,\underline{b}}
does not have a weight space of this weight, then the right-hand side is in the kernel of any map
T_{d,\underline{a}}\to T_{d,\underline{b}}
.

We now show that for any
\underline{b}
with
\varphi:\underline{a}\to\underline{b}
a non-isomorphism in
\mathscr{C}^{\mathrm{op}}_{d}
, there is indeed no weight space of this weight in
T_{d,\underline{b}}
. If
\lvert\underline{b}\rvert<\lvert\underline{a}\rvert
, then this is clearly true. Otherwise, we must have that
\lvert\underline{a}\rvert=\lvert\underline{b}\rvert
with
(a_{n},\ldots,a_{1})>(b_{n},\ldots,b_{1})
under the dominance order, and so there is no element of this weight in this case as well.
∎

Remark 3.3
.

Every morphism in
\mathscr{C}^{\mathrm{op}}_{d}
can be written as a composition of morphisms of the following two forms:

(1)

a bijection
\underline{a}\to\underline{b}
where
\lvert\underline{a}\rvert=\lvert\underline{b}\rvert
and
(a_{n},\ldots,a_{1})>(b_{n},\ldots,b_{1})
is a cover relation;

(2)

a morphism
\underline{a}\to\underline{b}
, where

b_{i}=\begin{cases}a_{i}&\quad i\neq d,\\
a_{d}-1&\quad i=d,\end{cases}

and the morphism corresponds to the natural injection
\underline{b}\to\underline{a}
in
\mathscr{C}_{d}
.

Thus,
K_{d,\underline{a}}
can also be given as the intersection of kernels of maps
T_{d,\underline{a}}\to T_{d,\underline{b}}
, where
\underline{a}\to\underline{b}
is of one of the above two forms.

Proposition 3.4
.

All irreducible constituents of
T_{d,\underline{a}}
are of the form
S_{d,\underline{\lambda}}
.
In particular, the
S_{d,\underline{\lambda}}
’s constitute all irreducible objects in
\text{Rep}(H_{d})
.

Proof.

We have an exact sequence

0\to K_{d,\underline{a}}\to T_{d,\underline{a}}\to\bigoplus_{\underline{a}\to\underline{b}}T_{d,\underline{b}},

where the direct sum ranges over all morphisms
\underline{a}\to\underline{b}
described in Remark
3.3
. The simple constituents of
K_{d,\underline{a}}
consist of the
S_{d,\underline{\lambda}}
’s with
\underline{a}=(\lvert\lambda^{i}\rvert)
by Lemma
3.2
. We induct on the objects
\underline{b}
, with base case given by
\underline{b}=(b,0,\ldots,0)
for
b=a_{1}+\cdots+a_{d-1}
. Then
T_{d,\underline{b}}=\mathbf{V}_{(n)}^{\otimes b}
, which has simple constituents of the form
\textbf{S}_{\lambda}(\mathbf{V}_{(n)})
. Therefore, all simple constituents of
T_{d,\underline{a}}
have the desired form.
∎

Lemma 3.5
.

The socle of
T_{d,\underline{a}}
is
K_{d,\underline{a}}
.

Proof.

Let
U_{d}\subset H_{d}
denote the unipotent radical. Since every simple
H_{d}
-representation is of the form
S_{d,\underline{\lambda}}
, the action of
U_{d}
on any semisimple
H_{d}
-representation is trivial.

We have that
K_{d,\underline{a}}
is semisimple by Lemma
3.2
. If
V\subset T_{d,\underline{a}}
is a submodule that properly contains
K_{d,\underline{a}}
, then
V
must have a nontrivial action of
U_{d}
, so
V
cannot be semisimple.
∎

3.3.
Indecomposable injectives

We now show that
\text{Rep}(H_{d})
and
\text{Mod}_{\mathscr{C}_{d}}^{\mathrm{lf}}
are equivalent, and use this to identify the indecomposable injectives of
\text{Rep}(H_{d})
. The functor giving the equivalence is described by the structured tensor product
\odot=\odot_{\mathscr{C}_{d}}
, which is defined in
[
17
, §2.1.9]
.

Proposition 3.6
.

We have an equivalence of categories
\text{Rep}(H_{d})\cong\text{Mod}^{\mathrm{lf}}_{\mathscr{C}_{d}}
.

Proof.

We have a functor
\text{Mod}_{\mathscr{C}_{d}}^{\mathrm{lf}}\to\text{Rep}(H_{d})
given by the structured tensor product
M\mapsto M\odot\mathscr{T}_{d}
. This functor is cocontinuous, and both categories are locally noetherian and artinian. It therefore suffices to show an equivalence among the subcategories of finite length objects. By
[
17
, Corollary 2.1.12]
, this functor is an equivalence if the following conditions hold:

(1)

For any simple
\mathfrak{S}_{\underline{a}}
-module
\textbf{M}_{\underline{\lambda}}
with
\underline{a}=(\lvert\lambda^{i}\rvert)
, the polynomial
H_{d}
-representation
\text{Hom}_{\mathfrak{S}_{\underline{a}}}(\textbf{M}_{\underline{\lambda}},K_{d,\underline{a}})
is simple.

(2)

For every simple
H_{d}
-representation
S_{d,\underline{\lambda}}
, there is a unique tuple
\underline{a}
such that the space
\text{Hom}_{H_{d}}(S_{d,\underline{\lambda}},T_{d,\underline{a}})
is nonzero, and it is an irreducible representation of
\mathfrak{S}_{\underline{a}}
.

By Lemma
3.2
and Schur–Weyl duality,
\text{Hom}_{\mathfrak{S}_{\underline{a}}}(\textbf{M}_{\underline{\lambda}},K_{d,\underline{a}})=S_{d,\underline{\lambda}}
,
so (1) holds. Condition (2) holds by Lemma
3.5
, as
S_{d,\underline{\lambda}}
is a submodule of
T_{d,\underline{a}}
if and only if
\underline{a}=(\lvert\lambda^{i}\rvert)
, and the Hom space in this case is the simple
\mathfrak{S}_{\underline{a}}
-representation
\textbf{M}_{\underline{\lambda}}
.
∎

For a tuple of partitions
\underline{\lambda}
, define the
H_{d}
-representation
T_{d,\underline{\lambda}}
to be

T_{d,\underline{\lambda}}=\bigotimes_{i=1}^{n}\textbf{S}_{\lambda^{i}}(\mathbf{V}/\mathbf{V}_{n-i}).

Corollary 3.7
.

The injective envelope of
S_{d,\underline{\lambda}}
is
T_{d,\underline{\lambda}}
, and the
T_{d,\underline{\lambda}}
’s constitute all indecomposable injective objects of
\text{Rep}(H_{d})
.

Proof.

Let
B\mathfrak{S}_{\underline{a}}
denote the category with a single object that has automorphism group
\mathfrak{S}_{\underline{a}}
. There is a natural fully faithful functor
i:B\mathfrak{S}_{\underline{a}}\to\mathscr{C}^{\mathrm{op}}_{d}
, which induces the pullback functor
i^{*}:\text{Mod}_{\mathscr{C}^{\mathrm{op}}_{d}}\to\text{Rep}(\mathfrak{S}_{\underline{a}})
and its left Kan extension
i_{\#}:\text{Rep}(\mathfrak{S}_{\underline{a}})\to\text{Mod}_{\mathscr{C}^{\mathrm{op}}_{d}}
, which is left adjoint to
i^{*}
. We have that
\textbf{M}_{\underline{\lambda}}
is an indecomposable projective object in
\text{Rep}(\mathfrak{S}_{\underline{a}})
. Then by
[
17
, §2.1.5]
, the object
i_{\#}(\textbf{M}_{\underline{\lambda}})
is the projective cover of the simple
\mathscr{C}^{\mathrm{op}}_{d}
-module
\textbf{M}_{\underline{\lambda}}
, and every indecomposable projective object in
\text{Mod}_{\mathscr{C}_{d}^{\mathrm{op}}}^{\mathrm{lf}}
is of this form.

Recall the
\mathscr{C}^{\mathrm{op}}_{d}
-module
\mathscr{T}_{d}
defined by
\underline{a}\mapsto T_{d,\underline{a}}
. This defines a contravariant functor
\Phi:\text{Mod}_{\mathscr{C}^{\mathrm{op}}_{d}}^{\mathrm{lf}}\to\text{Rep}(H_{d})
given by
M\mapsto\text{Hom}_{\mathscr{C}^{\mathrm{op}}_{d}}(M,\mathscr{T}_{d})
, and by
[
17
, Theorem 2.1.11]
and Proposition
3.6
, this is an equivalence of categories. Therefore,
\Phi(i_{\#}(\textbf{M}_{\underline{\lambda}}))
is the injective envelope of the simple
H_{d}
-representation
\Phi(\textbf{M}_{\underline{\lambda}})=S_{d,\underline{\lambda}}
. Using the adjunction, we see that

\displaystyle\Phi(i_{\#}(\textbf{M}_{\underline{\lambda}}))

\displaystyle=\text{Hom}_{\mathscr{C}^{\mathrm{op}}_{d}}(i_{\#}(\textbf{M}_{\underline{\lambda}}),\mathscr{T}_{d})=\text{Hom}_{\mathfrak{S}_{\underline{a}}}(\textbf{M}_{\underline{\lambda}},i^{*}(\mathscr{T}_{d}))=\text{Hom}_{\mathfrak{S}_{\underline{a}}}(\textbf{M}_{\underline{\lambda}},T_{d,\underline{a}})=T_{d,\underline{\lambda}}.

The result then follows.
∎

Remark 3.8
.

In
[
22
]
, we showed that all indecomposable injectives in the category
\text{Rep}(\mathbf{P})
of finite length polynomial
\mathbf{P}
-representations are also of the form
\bigotimes_{i=1}^{n}\textbf{S}_{\lambda^{i}}(\mathbf{V}/\mathbf{V}_{n-i})
, for a tuple
\underline{\lambda}
of partitions. Thus, all injective
H_{d}
-representations are restrictions of injective
\mathbf{P}
-representations.

The following key finiteness result follows from the properties of
\text{Mod}_{\mathscr{C}_{d}}
.

Corollary 3.9
.

Let
V
be a finitely generated object of
\text{Rep}(H_{d})
. Then
V
has finite length and finite injective dimension.

4.
Generic categories

4.1.
Statement of the main theorem

For
d=1,\ldots,n
, let
A_{d}=\textbf{A}/\mathfrak{p}_{d}
; note that
A_{n}=\textbf{A}
. Let
\text{Mod}_{d}^{\mathrm{tors}}
be the category of torsion
A_{d}
-modules, and let
\text{Mod}_{d}^{\mathrm{gen}}
denote the Serre quotient of
\text{Mod}_{A_{d}}
by
\text{Mod}_{d}^{\mathrm{tors}}
. We call
\text{Mod}_{d}^{\mathrm{gen}}
the
generic category
of
A_{d}
-modules. Our goal for this section is to understand the structure of
\text{Mod}_{d}^{\mathrm{gen}}
via the category
\text{Rep}(H_{d})
studied in the previous section. In particular, we show that
\text{Mod}_{d}^{\mathrm{gen}}\cong\text{Rep}(H_{d})
as tensor categories.

The intuition for the equivalence is as follows. Consider the scheme
\text{Spec}\,(A_{d})
. Then,
A_{d}
-modules correspond to
\mathbf{P}
-equivariant quasi-coherent sheaves on
\text{Spec}\,(A_{d})
, torsion modules correspond to such sheaves that restrict to zero on a dense open subset
U
, and generic
A_{d}
-modules correspond to
\mathbf{P}
-equivariant quasi-coherent sheaves on
U
. Recall the linear functional
\xi_{d}
defined in §
3
; its
\mathbf{P}
-orbit is a dense open subset of
\text{Spec}\,(A_{d})
, and its stabilizer is
H_{d}\subset\mathbf{P}
. Thus,
\mathbf{P}
-equivariant quasi-coherent sheaves on
U
should correspond to representations of
H_{d}
.

This intuition comes from standard theory on representations of algebraic groups. However, since we are working with infinite-dimensional spaces, the details in our setting are a bit more technical. To prove the equivalence of categories, we define the functor
\Phi_{d}:\text{Mod}_{A_{d}}\to\text{Rep}(H_{d})
as follows.
The form
\xi_{d}
induces a ring homomorphism
\lvert A_{d}\rvert\to\mathbf{C}
, which has kernel
\mathfrak{m}_{d}\subset\lvert A_{d}\rvert
, a maximal (not
\mathbf{P}
-equivariant) ideal. For an
A_{d}
-module
M
, define
\Phi_{d}(M)=M/\mathfrak{m}_{d}M
. This is indeed a polynomial
H_{d}
-representation:
\mathfrak{m}_{d}
is
H_{d}
-equivariant, and since
M
is a quotient of
A_{d}\otimes V
for some polynomial
\mathbf{P}
-representation
V
, then
\Phi(M)
is a quotient of the polynomial
H_{d}
-representation
\Phi_{d}(A_{d}\otimes V)=V
. Furthermore,
\Phi_{d}
is a tensor functor.

The main result of this section is the following.

Theorem 4.1
.

The functor
\Phi_{d}
induces an equivalence of tensor categories
\text{Mod}_{d}^{\mathrm{gen}}\cong\text{Rep}(H_{d})
.

Let
\overline{T}_{d}:\text{Mod}_{A_{d}}\to\text{Mod}_{d}^{\mathrm{gen}}
denote the localization functor. The following result is an immediate consequence of Theorem
4.1
and the results in §
3
.

Corollary 4.2
.

For any
d=1,\ldots,n
, we have the following.

(1)

If
M
is a finitely generated
A_{d}
-module, then
\overline{T}_{d}(M)
has finite length in
\text{Mod}_{d}^{\mathrm{gen}}
. In particular,
\text{Mod}_{d}^{\mathrm{gen}}
is locally of finite length.

(2)

Objects of finite length in
\text{Mod}_{d}^{\mathrm{gen}}
have finite injective dimension.

(3)

The injective objects of
\text{Mod}_{d}^{\mathrm{gen}}
are the objects of the form
\overline{T}_{d}(A_{d}\otimes V)
, for
V
an injective polynomial
\mathbf{P}
-representation.

(4)

The simple objects of
\text{Mod}_{d}^{\mathrm{gen}}
are the objects of the form
\overline{T}_{d}(A_{d}\otimes S_{d,\underline{\lambda}})
, where
S_{d,\underline{\lambda}}
is a simple
H_{d}
-representation.

Remark 4.3
.

Consider
A_{0}=\textbf{A}/\mathfrak{p}_{0}
, which is isomorphic to
\mathbf{C}
as a vector space. Then
\text{Mod}_{A_{0}}\cong\text{Rep}(\mathbf{P})
are equivalent via the functor
\text{Rep}(\mathbf{P})\to\text{Mod}_{A_{0}}:V\mapsto A_{0}\otimes V
. Thus,
\text{Mod}_{A_{0}}
is already locally of finite length, and objects of finite length also have finite injective dimension (recall the background on
\text{Rep}(\mathbf{P})
in §
2.1
).

4.2.
Proof of the theorem

We now prove Theorem
4.1
by showing that
\Phi_{d}
induces an exact tensor functor
\overline{\Phi}_{d}:\text{Mod}^{\mathrm{gen}}_{d}\to\text{Rep}(H_{d})
that is fully faithful and essentially surjective.

For
a\geq 1
, recall from §
2.1
the subgroups
G_{a},P_{a}\subset\mathbf{P}
, and the specialization functor
\mathscr{S}_{a}:\text{Rep}(\mathbf{P})\to\text{Rep}(P_{a})
taking
G_{a}
-invariants.

Lemma 4.4
.

\Phi_{d}
is exact.

Proof.

It suffices to prove that
\Phi_{d}
is exact on finitely generated modules, since
\Phi_{d}
is cocontinuous. Suppose
a\gg 0
. Then by generic flatness and since the
P_{a}
-orbit of
\mathfrak{m}_{d}\cap\mathscr{S}_{a}(A_{d})
is an open subset of
\text{Spec}\,(\mathscr{S}_{a}(A_{d}))
, finitely generated
P_{a}
-equivariant modules over
\mathscr{S}_{a}(A_{d})
are flat over any point in that open subset, including at
\mathfrak{m}_{d}\cap\mathscr{S}_{a}(A_{d})
.

We have that
A_{d}/\mathfrak{m}_{d}
is the colimit
\varinjlim\mathscr{S}_{a}(A_{d})/(\mathfrak{m}_{d}\cap\mathscr{S}_{a}(A_{d}))
over
a
. The lemma then follows from the fact that flatness is preserved under colimits.
∎

Lemma 4.5
.

The kernel of
\Phi_{d}
is
\text{Mod}_{d}^{\mathrm{tors}}
.

Proof.

Suppose
M
is a finitely generated torsion
A_{d}
-module. Then by Theorem
2.4
and Proposition
2.7
, there exists some
k>0
such that
x_{n-d+1,1}^{k}
annihilates
M
. Since
x_{n-d+1,1}^{k}-1\in\mathfrak{m}_{d}
, we see that
\mathfrak{m}_{d}M=M
, and so
\Phi_{d}(M)=0
. The argument for when
M
is not finitely generated also follows.

Conversely, suppose
M
is a nonzero
A_{d}
-module such that
\Phi_{d}(M)=0
. Since
\Phi_{d}
is cocontinuous, we may assume that
M
is finitely generated. For
a\gg 0
, we see that the point
\mathfrak{m}_{d}\cap\mathscr{S}_{a}(A_{d})\in\text{Spec}\,(\mathscr{S}_{a}(A_{d}))
is not in the support of
\mathscr{S}_{a}(M)
. Furthermore, since
\mathscr{S}_{a}(M)
is
P_{a}
-equivariant, the
P_{a}
-orbit of this point is also not in the support. In particular,
\mathscr{S}_{a}(M)
is only supported at the homogeneous maximal ideal
\mathscr{S}_{a}(\mathfrak{p}_{0})\in\text{Spec}\,(\mathscr{S}_{a}(A_{d}))
, and so
M
is also only supported at
\mathfrak{p}_{0}
. This implies that
M
is torsion.
∎

Thus,
\Phi_{d}
induces an exact tensor functor
\overline{\Phi}_{d}:\text{Mod}^{\mathrm{gen}}_{d}\to\text{Rep}(H_{d})
. We show that
\overline{\Phi}_{d}
is fully faithful and essentially surjective to prove Theorem
4.1
.

Lemma 4.6
.

\overline{\Phi}_{d}
is full.

Proof.

Suppose
M,N
are
A_{d}
-modules, and
f:\Phi_{d}(M)\to\Phi_{d}(N)
is a morphism of polynomial representations of
H_{d}
. We may further assume without loss of generality that
M,N
are finitely generated. For
a\gg 0
, let
U\subset\text{Spec}\,(\mathscr{S}_{a}(A_{d}))
denote the open
P_{a}
-orbit of
\mathfrak{m}_{d}\cap\mathscr{S}_{a}(A_{d})
, and let
\iota:U\hookrightarrow\text{Spec}\,(\mathscr{S}_{a}(A_{d}))
denote the open immersion.

Let
M_{a}=\mathscr{S}_{a}(M)
and
N_{a}=\mathscr{S}_{a}(N)
. By taking
(G_{a}\cap H_{d})
-invariants, we have a map of
(P_{a}\cap H_{d}
)-representations corresponding to a morphism on pullbacks
\iota^{*}(M_{a})\to\iota^{*}(N_{a})
. This induces a map of
\mathscr{S}_{a}(A_{d})
-modules

f_{a}:M_{a}\to\iota_{*}(\iota^{*}(M_{a}))\to\iota_{*}(\iota^{*}(N_{a})).

Note that this map is
P_{a}
-equivariant by functoriality, but
\iota_{*}(\iota^{*}(N_{a}))
may not be necessarily a polynomial
P_{a}
-representation. Let
N^{\prime}_{a}\subset\iota_{*}(\iota^{*}(N_{a}))
be the maximal polynomial subrepresentation with respect to the action of the Levi subgroup of
P_{a}
; in particular,
N^{\prime}_{a}
is a
\mathscr{S}_{a}(A_{d})
-module that contains
N_{a}
, and the image of the map
M_{a}
under the map
M_{a}\to\iota_{*}(\iota^{*}(N_{a}))
is contained in
N^{\prime}_{a}
, since
M_{a}
is a polynomial
P_{a}
-representation.

Let
N^{\prime}
be the canonical
A_{d}
-module such that
\ell(N^{\prime})\leq a
and such that
\mathscr{S}_{a}(N^{\prime})=N^{\prime}_{a}
. Then
f_{a}
determines a map of
A_{d}
-modules
g:M\to N^{\prime}
. We now claim that
N^{\prime}\cong N
in
\text{Mod}_{d}^{\mathrm{gen}}
. Indeed, pulling back
\iota_{*}(\iota^{*}(N_{a}))/N_{a}
along
\iota^{*}
results in
N_{a}/N_{a}=0
and so

\mathscr{S}_{a}(N^{\prime})/N_{a}\subset\iota_{*}(\iota^{*}(N_{a}))/N_{a}

is a torsion module. Since
a\gg 0
, this implies that
N^{\prime}/N
is torsion as an
A_{d}
-module, and so
N^{\prime}\cong N
in
\text{Mod}^{\mathrm{gen}}_{d}
. Thus,
\Phi_{d}(g)=f
, and
\overline{\Phi}_{d}
is indeed full.
∎

Lemma 4.7
.

\overline{\Phi}_{d}
is faithful.

Proof.

Suppose
f:M\to N
is a map of
A_{d}
-modules such that the map of
H_{d}
-representations
\Phi_{d}(f):\Phi_{d}(M)\to\Phi_{d}(N)
is zero. Then the image of
f
is torsion by Lemma
4.5
, and so
f=0
as a map in
\text{Mod}^{\mathrm{gen}}_{d}
.
∎

Lemma 4.8
.

\overline{\Phi}_{d}
is essentially surjective.

Proof.

First note that all
H_{d}
-representations of the form
T_{d,\underline{a}}
are in the essential image of
\Phi_{d}
, as

\Phi_{d}(A_{d}\otimes T_{d,\underline{a}})=A_{d}/\mathfrak{m}_{d}\otimes T_{d,\underline{a}}\cong T_{d,\underline{a}}.

By Corollary
3.7
, every finitely generated polynomial
H_{d}
-representation
M
can be realized as the kernel of a map
f:S\to T
between direct sums of representations of the form
T_{d,\underline{a}}
. By Lemma
4.6
,
f=\overline{\Phi}_{d}(g)
for some morphism
g:S^{\prime}\to T^{\prime}
in
\text{Mod}_{d}^{\mathrm{gen}}
. By Lemma
4.4
,
\overline{\Phi}_{d}
is exact, and so
M=\overline{\Phi}_{d}(\ker g)
. Thus,
M
is also in the essential image of
\overline{\Phi}_{d}
.
∎

4.3.
Krull–Gabriel dimension of
\text{Mod}_{\textbf{A}}

We can use Theorem
4.1
to calculate the Krull–Gabriel dimension of
\text{Mod}_{\textbf{A}}
, which is an important invariant of abelian categories. For a locally noetherian abelian category
\mathscr{A}
, we define it as follows. Let
\mathscr{A}^{\mathrm{fg}}
be the category of finitely generated objects, and let
\mathscr{A}_{0}\subset\mathscr{A}^{\mathrm{fg}}
be the Serre subcategory on finite length objects. For
i>0
, let
\mathscr{A}_{i}\subset\mathscr{A}^{\mathrm{fg}}
be the subcategory on objects whose images in the Serre quotient category
\mathscr{A}^{\mathrm{fg}}/\mathscr{A}_{i-1}
are of finite length. Then the
Krull–Gabriel dimension
of
\mathscr{A}
is defined to be the minimal
k
for which
\mathscr{A}_{k}=\mathscr{A}^{\mathrm{fg}}
. In particular, if all finitely generated objects of
\mathscr{A}
are of finite length, then its Krull–Gabriel dimension is
0
.

Proposition 4.9
.

The Krull–Gabriel dimension of
\text{Mod}_{\textbf{A}}
is
n
.

Proof.

By Corollary
4.2
, the Krull–Gabriel dimension of
\text{Mod}^{\mathrm{gen}}_{d}
is
0
for
d=1,\ldots,n
. Every quotient
A/\mathfrak{p}_{d}
for
\mathfrak{p}_{d}
a non-maximal
\mathbf{P}
-prime ideal is given by the
A_{d}
’s. Then, by
[
19
, Proposition 3.8]
, the Krull–Gabriel dimension of
\text{Mod}_{\textbf{A}}
is given by the Krull dimension of the space
\text{Spec}\,_{\mathbf{P}}(\textbf{A})
. The result then follows from Corollary
2.5
.
∎

4.4.
The section functors

We now give an alternative description of the generic categories
\text{Mod}_{d}^{\mathrm{gen}}
, and use this to describe the right adjoints to the localization functors
\overline{T}_{d}
.

Let
K_{d}=\text{Frac}(\lvert A_{d}\rvert)
be the fraction field of the domain
\lvert A_{d}\rvert
; this field comes with an induced action of
\mathbf{P}
, and we let
\lvert K_{d}\rvert
denote the field itself without the
\mathbf{P}
-action. Suppose
V
is a
\lvert K_{d}\rvert
-vector space equipped with a compatible
\mathbf{P}
-representation structure. There is a
\lvert K_{d}\rvert
-subspace
V^{\mathrm{pol}}
consisting of
polynomial elements
, which are elements of
V
that generate a polynomial
\mathbf{P}
-representation over
\mathbf{C}
. We say
V
is a
K_{d}
-module
if it is spanned by these polynomial elements over
\lvert K_{d}\rvert
, and we let
\text{Mod}_{K_{d}}
denote the category of
K_{d}
-modules.

We have a functor
\overline{S}_{d}:\text{Mod}_{K_{d}}\to\text{Mod}_{A_{d}}
mapping a
K_{d}
-module
M
to the set
M^{\mathrm{pol}}
of polynomial elements, which is naturally an
A_{d}
-module. Our goal is to show that
\overline{S}_{d}
is right adjoint to
\overline{T}_{d}
. To do this, we first identify
\text{Mod}_{K_{d}}
with
\text{Mod}_{d}^{\mathrm{gen}}
.

Lemma 4.10
.

We have an equivalence of categories
\text{Mod}^{\mathrm{gen}}_{d}\cong\text{Mod}_{K_{d}}
.

Proof.

The functor
\text{Mod}_{A_{d}}\to\text{Mod}_{K_{d}}
given by
M\mapsto K_{d}\otimes_{A_{d}}M
is exact and kills torsion
A_{d}
-modules. Thus, we have an induced exact functor
F:\text{Mod}_{d}^{\mathrm{gen}}\to\text{Mod}_{K_{d}}
. Notice that
\overline{S}_{d}
is right adjoint to
F\overline{T}_{d}
. Then, the proof that
F
is an equivalence of categories, with right quasi-inverse
\overline{T}_{d}\overline{S}_{d}
, follows the proofs of
[
14
, Propositions 2.7 and 2.8]
.
∎

For the remainder of the paper, we identify
\text{Mod}_{d}^{\mathrm{gen}}\cong\text{Mod}_{K_{d}}
, and call
\overline{S}_{d}
the
section functor
; it is right adjoint to the localization functor
\overline{T}_{d}:\text{Mod}_{A_{d}}\to\text{Mod}_{d}^{\mathrm{gen}}
. The following result will be important in §
5
to study the bounded derived category of
A
-modules.

Proposition 4.11
.

For any finite length polynomial
\mathbf{P}
-representation
M
, the natural map
A_{d}\otimes M\to\overline{S}_{d}(\overline{T}_{d}(A_{d}\otimes M))
is an isomorphism.

Proof.

We show that if
m\in K_{d}\otimes M
is a polynomial element, then
m\in A_{d}\otimes M
. We can write
m
as

m=\sum_{i=1}^{p}\frac{f_{i}}{g}\otimes m_{i},

where
f_{i},g\in A_{d}
with
\gcd(g,f_{1},\ldots,f_{p})=1
, and
m_{i}\in M
linear independent over
\mathbf{C}
. Let
a\gg 0
be such that
g,f_{1},\ldots,f_{p}\in\mathscr{S}_{a}(A_{d})
. Let
L_{a+1}=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{GL}_{a+1}
denote the Levi subgroup of
P_{a+1}
. We have that
m
generates a finite-dimensional polynomial representation of
L_{a+1}
, so let
\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{r}\}
be a basis, where

e_{j}=\sum_{k=1}^{n_{j}}\frac{f_{jk}}{g_{j}}\otimes m_{jk},\qquad f_{jk},g_{j}\in A_{d},m_{jk}\in M.

Thus, every element of this
L_{a+1}
-representation can be written with common denominator
g^{\prime}=g_{1}\cdots g_{r}
. The hypersurface defined by
g^{\prime}
contains the
L_{a+1}
-orbit of the hypersurface defined by
g
, and so this orbit is finite; otherwise, the orbit would be a dense open subset of
\text{Spec}\,(\mathscr{S}_{a}(A_{d}))
and could not be contained in the hypersurface defined by
g^{\prime}
. Furthermore,
L_{a+1}
fixes each irreducible component of the hypersurface
g=0
as
L_{a+1}
is connected and
1\in L_{a+1}
.
The irreducible components are therefore fixed under the
L_{a+1}
-action, and so
L_{a+1}
acts by a scalar in
\mathbf{C}
on
g
. In particular, the polynomial
L_{a+1}
-representation generated by
g
is one-dimensional. Then, the action of
L_{a+1}
on
g
is given by products of powers of determinants: if
A\in L_{a+1}
is a block diagonal matrix given by
(A_{1},\ldots,A_{n})
where each
A_{i}
is of size
(a+1)\times(a+1)
, then

A\cdot g=\prod_{i=1}^{n}(\det A_{i})^{d_{i}}g,\qquad d_{i}\geq 0.

Recall that
g\in\mathscr{S}_{a}(A_{d})
, and is thus invariant under the action of matrices of
L_{a+1}
where each block along the diagonal is of the form

\begin{pmatrix}\text{id}_{a}&0\\
0&\alpha\end{pmatrix},\qquad\alpha\in\mathbf{C}.

Since
g
is nonzero, this implies that
L_{a+1}
must actually act trivially on
g
, and so
L
also acts trivially on
g
. Thus,
g\in\mathbf{C}
, as
(A_{d})^{\textbf{L}}=\mathbf{C}
, and so
m\in A_{d}\otimes M
, as desired.
∎

5.
Structure of
A
-modules

5.1.
Set-up and notation

For
d=0,\ldots,n
let
\text{Mod}_{\textbf{A},\leq d}
denote the full subcategory of
\text{Mod}_{\textbf{A}}
spanned by
A
-modules locally annihilated by a power of
\mathfrak{p}_{d}
, i.e., supported on
V(\mathfrak{p}_{d})
. This gives a chain of Serre subcategories

\text{Mod}_{\textbf{A},\leq 0}\subset\text{Mod}_{\textbf{A},\leq 1}\subset\cdots\subset\text{Mod}_{\textbf{A},\leq n}=\text{Mod}_{\textbf{A}}.

We have Serre quotient categories

\text{Mod}_{\textbf{A},>d}=\text{Mod}_{\textbf{A}}/\text{Mod}_{\textbf{A},\leq d},\qquad\text{Mod}_{\textbf{A},d}=\text{Mod}_{\textbf{A},\leq d}/\text{Mod}_{\textbf{A},\leq d-1}.

We let
\text{Mod}_{\textbf{A},d}^{\mathrm{fg}}
denote the subcategory of
\text{Mod}_{\textbf{A},d}
on finitely generated objects.

For each
d=0,\ldots,n
, we have the following functors associated to these categories:

•

The localization functor
T_{>d}:\text{Mod}_{\textbf{A}}\to\text{Mod}_{\textbf{A},>d}
.

•

The localization functor
T_{d}:\text{Mod}_{\textbf{A},\leq d}\to\text{Mod}_{\textbf{A},d}
; this is the restriction of
T_{>d-1}
.

•

The section functor
S_{>d}:\text{Mod}_{\textbf{A},>d}\to\text{Mod}_{\textbf{A}}
, which is right adjoint to
T_{>d}
.

•

The section functor
S_{d}:\text{Mod}_{\textbf{A},d}\to\text{Mod}_{\textbf{A},\leq d}
, which is right adjoint to
T_{d}
; by uniqueness of right adjoint, it agrees with the restriction of
S_{>d-1}
.

•

The left-exact saturation functor
\Sigma_{>d}=S_{>d}\circ T_{>d}
.

•

The left-exact torsion functor
\Gamma_{\leq d}:\text{Mod}_{\textbf{A}}\to\text{Mod}_{\textbf{A},\leq d}
, defined by taking the maximal submodule supported on
V(\mathfrak{p}_{d})
.

For each
d=0,\ldots,n
, recall the following notation from the previous section:

•

The quotient ring
A_{d}=\textbf{A}/\mathfrak{p}_{d}
and the category
\text{Mod}_{A_{d}}
of
A_{d}
-modules.

•

The quotient category
\text{Mod}_{d}^{\mathrm{gen}}
of
\text{Mod}_{A_{d}}
by the subcategory of torsion
A_{d}
-modules, with subcategory
(\text{Mod}_{d}^{\mathrm{gen}})^{\mathrm{fg}}
on finitely generated objects (let
\text{Mod}_{0}^{\mathrm{gen}}=\text{Mod}_{A_{0}}
).

•

The localization functor
\overline{T}_{d}:\text{Mod}_{A_{d}}\to\text{Mod}^{\mathrm{gen}}_{d}
(for
d=0
, this is the identity functor).

•

The section functor
\overline{S}_{d}:\text{Mod}_{d}^{\mathrm{gen}}\to\text{Mod}_{A_{d}}
(for
d=0
, this is the identity functor).

Let
\mathrm{D}(\textbf{A})
denote the derived category of
A
-modules. We have the following associated triangulated categories:

•

The bounded derived category
\mathrm{D}^{b}_{\mathrm{fg}}(\textbf{A})
of objects with finitely generated cohomology.

•

The full subcategory
\mathrm{D}(\textbf{A})_{\leq d}
on objects
M
for which
\mathrm{R}\Sigma_{>d}(M)=0
.

•

The full subcategory
\mathrm{D}(\textbf{A})_{>d}
on objects
M
for which
\mathrm{R}\Gamma_{\leq d}(M)=0
.

•

The category
\mathrm{D}(\textbf{A})_{d}=\mathrm{D}(\textbf{A})_{\leq d}\cap\mathrm{D}(\textbf{A})_{>d}
.

5.2.
Decomposition of
\text{Mod}_{\textbf{A}}

The filtration of
\text{Mod}_{\textbf{A}}
by the subcategories
\text{Mod}_{\textbf{A},\leq d}
provides a way to decompose
\text{Mod}_{\textbf{A}}
via the results in
[
19
, §4.2]
. The key hypothesis needed for these results is the
(Inj) hypothesis
, which is that injective objects in
\text{Mod}_{\textbf{A},\leq d}
remain injective in
\text{Mod}_{\textbf{A}}
. We now verify that this hypothesis holds.

Lemma 5.1
.

Injective objects in
\text{Mod}_{\textbf{A},\leq d}
remain injective in
\text{Mod}_{\textbf{A}}
.

Proof.

By
[
19
, Corollary 4.19]
, it suffices to show that the Artin–Rees lemma holds for each
\mathbf{P}
-prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}_{d}
ideal, i.e., that if
M
is a finitely generated
A
-module and
N\subset M
is a submodule, then there exists
r
such that
\mathfrak{p}_{d}^{m}M\cap N=\mathfrak{p}_{d}^{m-r}(\mathfrak{p}_{d}^{r}M\cap N)
for all
m\geq r
.

Let
a>\ell(M)
. By the classical Artin–Rees lemma, there exists an
r
such that for all
m\geq r
, we have the desired equality after applying the specialization functor
\mathscr{S}_{a}(-)
:

\mathscr{S}_{a}(\mathfrak{p}_{d}^{m}M\cap N)=\mathscr{S}_{a}(\mathfrak{p}_{d}^{m-r}(\mathfrak{p}_{d}^{r}M\cap N)),\qquad m\geq r.

Then, Artin–Rees holds before specialization as well using the same
r
. This is because
a>\ell(M)\geq\ell(\mathfrak{p}_{d}^{m}M\cap N)
, and so if there were a strict containment
\mathfrak{p}_{d}^{m}M\cap N\supsetneq\mathfrak{p}_{d}^{m-r}(\mathfrak{p}_{d}^{r}M\cap N)
for some
m\geq r
, then one would have strict containment after specialization as well.
∎

This lemma implies the results in
[
19
, §4.1-2]
. In particular, we have the following.

Lemma 5.2
.

An
A
-module
M
is derived saturated, i.e., the natural map
M\to\mathrm{R}\Sigma_{>d}(M)
is an isomorphism, if and only if
\mathrm{R}\text{Hom}_{\textbf{A}}(N,M)=0
for all
N\in\text{Mod}_{\textbf{A},\leq d}
.

Lemma 5.3
.

The functors
S_{>d}
and
T_{>d}
induce bijections among injectives of
\text{Mod}_{\textbf{A},>d}
and injectives
I
of
\text{Mod}_{\textbf{A}}
for which
\Gamma_{\leq d}(I)=0
.

Remark 5.4
.

The analogous results for
\text{Mod}_{A_{d}}
and the subcategory
\text{Mod}_{d}^{\mathrm{tors}}
hold, i.e.,
\text{Mod}_{d}^{\mathrm{tors}}\subset\text{Mod}_{A_{d}}
satisfy the (Inj) hypothesis, and so the results in
[
19
]
also apply.

5.3.
Comparison with generic
\textbf{A}/\mathfrak{p}_{d}
-modules

Recall the categories
\text{Mod}_{\textbf{A}/\mathfrak{p}_{d}}=\text{Mod}_{A_{d}}
of
A
-modules annihilated by
\mathfrak{p}_{d}
, and the generic categories
\text{Mod}^{\mathrm{gen}}_{\textbf{A}/\mathfrak{p}_{d}}=\text{Mod}^{\mathrm{gen}}_{d}
studied in §
4
. The following results are essentially the same as
[
19
, Propositions 6.1 and 6.2]
, but we provide some added detail and use our notation.

Lemma 5.5
.

\text{Mod}_{d}^{\mathrm{gen}}
is equivalent to the subcategory
\text{Mod}_{A_{d},d}\subset\text{Mod}_{\textbf{A},d}
on objects of the form
T_{d}(M)
, where
M
is an
A
-module such that
\mathfrak{p}_{d}M
is supported on
V(\mathfrak{p}_{d-1})
.

Proof.

The functor
T_{d}:\text{Mod}_{\textbf{A},\leq d}\to\text{Mod}_{\textbf{A},\leq d-1}
is exact. If
M\in\text{Mod}_{A_{d}}\subset\text{Mod}_{\textbf{A},\leq d}
, then
\mathfrak{p}_{d}M=0
and so it is supported on
V(\mathfrak{p}_{d-1})
; thus,
T_{d}(M)\in\text{Mod}_{A_{d},d}
. Furthermore,
T_{d}
kills objects of
\text{Mod}_{A_{d}}
supported on
V(\mathfrak{p}_{d-1})
, i.e.,
\text{Mod}_{d}^{\mathrm{tors}}
. Thus,
T_{d}
induces a functor
\mathscr{F}:\text{Mod}^{\mathrm{gen}}_{d}\to\text{Mod}_{A_{d},d}
, which we will show is an equivalence of categories.

We first show that
\mathscr{F}
is essentially surjective by showing that the localization functor
T_{d}
restricted to
\text{Mod}_{A_{d}}
is essentially surjective. Consider an object of the form
T_{d}(M)\in\text{Mod}_{A_{d},d}
, where
M\in\text{Mod}_{\textbf{A}}
such that
\mathfrak{p}_{d}M
is supported on
V(\mathfrak{p}_{d-1})
. Then we have the
A_{d}
-module
\overline{M}=M/\mathfrak{p}_{d}M
, and
M\to\overline{M}
is a surjection with kernel
\mathfrak{p}_{d}M
supported on
V(\mathfrak{p}_{d-1})
. Thus,
T_{d}(\overline{M})\cong T_{d}(M)
as objects in
\text{Mod}_{\textbf{A},d}
, and so
\mathscr{F}
is essentially surjective.

Suppose
M,N\in\text{Mod}_{A_{d}}
, and recall that
\overline{T}_{d}:\text{Mod}_{A_{d}}\to\text{Mod}^{\mathrm{gen}}_{d}
denotes the localization functor. We have that

\text{Hom}_{\text{Mod}_{d}^{\mathrm{gen}}}(\overline{T}_{d}(M),\overline{T}_{d}(N))\cong\text{Hom}_{\text{Mod}_{A_{d},d}}(T_{d}(M),T_{d}(N)),

as both are given by
\varinjlim\text{Hom}_{A_{d}}(M^{\prime},N^{\prime})
, where the colimit is over
A_{d}
-modules
M^{\prime},N^{\prime}
such that
M^{\prime}\subset M
is an inclusion,
N\to N^{\prime}
is a surjection, and both
M/M^{\prime}
and
\ker(N\to N^{\prime})
are in
\text{Mod}_{d}^{\mathrm{tors}}
. Since
\mathscr{F}(\overline{T}_{d}(M))=T_{d}(M)
, this shows that
\mathscr{F}
is fully faithful.
∎

Recall the section functors
\overline{S}_{d}:\text{Mod}_{d}^{\mathrm{gen}}\to\text{Mod}_{A_{d}}
studied in §
4.4
, and let
\mathrm{R}\overline{S}_{d}
denote its right derived functor. We now compare
\mathrm{R}\overline{S}_{d}
and
\mathrm{R}S_{d}
as functors from the derived category of
\text{Mod}_{d}^{\mathrm{gen}}\cong\text{Mod}_{A_{d},d}
. The former is computed by considering injective resolutions in
\text{Mod}_{d}^{\mathrm{gen}}
, while the latter is computed by considering injective resolutions in
\text{Mod}_{\textbf{A},d}
. Although injective objects in these categories are different, the following result says that the derived functors still agree; this is key for describing the derived category
\mathrm{D}^{b}_{\mathrm{fg}}(\textbf{A})
.

Proposition 5.6
.

Let
M\in\text{Mod}_{d}^{\mathrm{gen}}
. Then
\mathrm{R}\overline{S}_{d}(M)\cong\mathrm{R}S_{d}(M)
are isomorphic.

Proof.

First, by Lemma
5.5
, we may identify the functor
\overline{T}_{d}:\text{Mod}_{A_{d}}\to\text{Mod}_{d}^{\mathrm{gen}}
with the restriction of the functor
T_{d}:\text{Mod}_{\textbf{A},\leq d}\to\text{Mod}_{\textbf{A},d}
. Then by uniqueness of right adjoint, we have that
\overline{S}_{d}
agrees with
S_{d}
on
\text{Mod}_{d}^{\mathrm{gen}}
.

Now, to prove the proposition, we explain why it suffices to show the following claim: if
I\in\text{Mod}_{d}^{\mathrm{gen}}
is injective, then the map
\overline{S}_{d}(I)\to\mathrm{R}\Sigma_{>d-1}(\overline{S}_{d}(I))
is an isomorphism. If so, then since

\mathrm{R}\Sigma_{>d-1}(\overline{S}_{d}(I))=\mathrm{R}S_{>d-1}(T_{>d-1}\overline{S}_{d}(I))=\mathrm{R}S_{>d-1}(I)=\mathrm{R}S_{d}(I),

we have that
\overline{S}_{d}(I)\cong\mathrm{R}S_{d}(I)
and so
I
is
S_{d}
-acyclic. Furthermore,
S_{d}(I)=\overline{S}_{d}(I)
by the previous paragraph.
Then, suppose
M\to I^{\bullet}
is an injective resolution of
M
in
\text{Mod}_{d}^{\mathrm{gen}}
. Since each injective object is
S_{d}
-acyclic, we see that
S_{d}(I^{\bullet})
gives
\mathrm{R}S_{d}(M)
. We also have that
\overline{S}_{d}(I^{\bullet})=S_{d}(I^{\bullet})
, and the former computes
\mathrm{R}\overline{S}_{d}(M)
. Thus,
\mathrm{R}S_{d}(M)=\mathrm{R}\overline{S}_{d}(M)
agree.

Let
J=\overline{S}_{d}(I)\in\text{Mod}_{A_{d}}
.
To prove the claim, it suffices to show by Lemma
5.2
that
\text{Ext}^{i}_{\textbf{A}}(N,J)=0
for all
N\in\text{Mod}_{\textbf{A},\leq d-1}
and all
i\geq 0
. First let
i=0
. We may assume that
N
is finitely generated, and so since
N\in\text{Mod}_{\textbf{A},\leq d-1}
, it is annihilated by
\mathfrak{p}_{d-1}^{k}
for some
k
. Then by dévissage, we may also assume
N
itself is annihilated by
\mathfrak{p}_{d-1}
: we have a filtration

0=\mathfrak{p}_{d-1}^{k}N\subset\mathfrak{p}_{d-1}^{k-1}N\subset\cdots\subset\mathfrak{p}_{d-1}N\subset N

where each associated graded piece is annihilated by
\mathfrak{p}_{d-1}
, and if there are no maps from any given piece of the filtration to
J
, then
\text{Hom}_{\textbf{A}}(N,J)=0
as well. If
\mathfrak{p}_{d-1}N=0
, then
\mathfrak{p}_{d}N=0
as well, and so
N
is a torsion
A_{d}
-module. But
J=\overline{S}_{d}(I)
is saturated with respect to this category, and so
\text{Hom}_{\textbf{A}}(N,J)=\text{Hom}_{A_{d}}(N,J)=0
by Lemma
5.2
.

Now suppose
j\geq 1
. By derived adjunction, we have

\mathrm{R}\text{Hom}_{A_{d}}(N\overset{\mathrm{L}}{\otimes}_{\textbf{A}}A_{d},J)=\mathrm{R}\text{Hom}_{\textbf{A}}(N,\mathrm{R}\text{Hom}_{\textbf{A}}(A_{d},J))=\mathrm{R}\text{Hom}_{\textbf{A}}(N,J),

where the last equality is because
J=\overline{S}_{d}(I)
is an
A_{d}
-module. Since
I
is injective, we have that
J
is injective as an
A_{d}
-module by Lemma
5.3
, and so we have that

\text{Ext}^{i}_{\textbf{A}}(N,J)=\text{Hom}_{A_{d}}(\text{Tor}^{\textbf{A}}_{i}(N,A_{d}),J).

Note that
\text{Tor}_{i}^{\textbf{A}}(N,A_{d})
is supported on
V(\mathfrak{p}_{d-1})
since
N
is. We thus reduce to the
i=0
case above and see that
\text{Ext}^{i}_{\textbf{A}}(N,J)=0
, as desired.
∎

Corollary 5.7
.

Suppose
M
is an
A_{d}
-module. Then
\mathrm{R}^{i}\Sigma_{>d-1}(M)
is annihilated by
\mathfrak{p}_{d}
for all
i\geq 0
.

Proof.

We have that
\mathrm{R}^{i}\Sigma_{>d-1}(M)=\mathrm{R}^{i}S_{d}(T_{d}(M))
. Since
T_{d}(M)\in\text{Mod}_{d}^{\mathrm{gen}}
, Proposition
5.6
shows that
\mathrm{R}^{i}S_{d}(T_{d}(M))=\mathrm{R}^{i}\overline{S}_{d}(T_{d}(M))
, which is an object in
\text{Mod}_{A_{d}}
.
∎

5.4.
Generators for
\mathrm{D}^{b}_{\mathrm{fg}}(\textbf{A})

In this subsection, we prove the main finiteness results on local cohomology and derived saturation. We first prove the following lemma on finite generation of the derived section functors.

Lemma 5.8
.

If
M\in\text{Mod}^{\mathrm{fg}}_{\textbf{A},d}
, then
\mathrm{R}^{i}S_{d}(M)
is a finitely generated
A
-module for all
i\geq 0
, and is zero for
i\gg 0
.

Proof.

By dévissage, we may assume that
M
is annihilated by
\mathfrak{p}_{d}
, and so
M
is a finitely generated object of
\text{Mod}^{\mathrm{gen}}_{d}
. By Corollary
4.2
, there is a finite injective resolution
M\to\overline{T}_{d}(A_{d}\otimes V^{\bullet})
, with
V^{i}
each finite length injective
\mathbf{P}
-representations. Each
\overline{T}_{d}(A_{d}\otimes V^{i})
is
\overline{S}_{d}
-acyclic. Thus,
\mathrm{R}\overline{S}_{d}(M)
is given by

\overline{S}_{d}\overline{T}_{d}(A_{d}\otimes V^{\bullet})=A_{d}\otimes V^{\bullet},

where the equality follows from Proposition
4.11
. Since
M\in\text{Mod}^{\mathrm{gen}}_{d}
by assumption, this is also equal to
\mathrm{R}S_{d}(M)
by Proposition
5.6
. The resolution
A_{d}\otimes V^{\bullet}
is finite and each object is finitely generated, and so the result follows.
∎

Theorem 5.9
.

For each
d=0,\ldots,n
, if
M\in\mathrm{D}^{b}_{\mathrm{fg}}(\textbf{A})
, then
\mathrm{R}\Sigma_{>d}(M),\mathrm{R}\Gamma_{\leq d}(M)
are also in
\mathrm{D}^{b}_{\mathrm{fg}}(\textbf{A})
.

Proof.

The proof of
[
19
, Theorem 6.10]
applies here.
∎

The results of
[
19
, §4.3]
therefore apply to our filtration of
\text{Mod}_{\textbf{A}}
. In particular, we have the following semi-orthogonal decomposition of
\mathrm{D}^{b}_{\mathrm{fg}}(\textbf{A})
.

Corollary 5.10
.

We have a semi-orthogonal decomposition

\mathrm{D}^{b}_{\mathrm{fg}}(\textbf{A})=\langle\mathrm{D}^{b}_{\mathrm{fg}}(\textbf{A})_{0},\ldots,\mathrm{D}^{b}_{\mathrm{fg}}(\textbf{A})_{n}\rangle.

Finally, we describe a set of generators of
\mathrm{D}^{b}_{\mathrm{fg}}(\textbf{A})
. Recall that
S_{\underline{\lambda}}
denotes the simple
\mathbf{P}
-representation corresponding to the
n
-tuple of partitions
\underline{\lambda}
.

Corollary 5.11
.

For
d=0,\ldots,n
, the category
\mathrm{D}^{b}_{\mathrm{fg}}(\textbf{A})_{d}
is the triangulated subcategory of
\mathrm{D}^{b}_{\mathrm{fg}}(\textbf{A})
generated by the objects of the form
A_{d}\otimes S_{\underline{\lambda}}
. The category
\mathrm{D}^{b}_{\mathrm{fg}}(\textbf{A})
is generated by all such objects, allowing
d
to vary.

Proof.

By
[
19
, Proposition 4.14]
, we have an equivalence of categories
\mathrm{R}S_{d}:\mathrm{D}^{b}_{\mathrm{fg}}(\text{Mod}_{\textbf{A},d})\to\mathrm{D}^{b}_{\mathrm{fg}}(\textbf{A})_{d}
. Finitely generated objects of
\text{Mod}_{\textbf{A},d}
have finite length filtrations with associated graded pieces given by objects in
(\text{Mod}^{\mathrm{gen}}_{d})^{\mathrm{fg}}
, and
\text{Mod}_{\textbf{A},d}
generates
\mathrm{D}^{b}_{\mathrm{fg}}(\text{Mod}_{\textbf{A},d})
. Thus, the image of
(\text{Mod}^{\mathrm{gen}}_{d})^{\mathrm{fg}}
under
\overline{S}_{d}
generates
\mathrm{D}^{b}_{\mathrm{fg}}(\textbf{A})_{d}
.

By Corollary
4.2
, each object of
(\text{Mod}^{\mathrm{gen}}_{d})^{\mathrm{fg}}
has a finite resolution by objects of the form
\overline{T}_{d}(A_{d}\otimes V)
, where
V
is a finite length injective object of
\text{Rep}(\mathbf{P})
. Then, such objects have finite length filtrations with associated graded pieces of the form
\overline{T}_{d}(A_{d}\otimes S_{\underline{\lambda}})
with
S_{\underline{\lambda}}
a simple
\mathbf{P}
-representation. The result then follows from
\overline{S}_{d}(\overline{T}_{d}(A_{d}\otimes S_{\underline{\lambda}}))=A_{d}\otimes S_{\underline{\lambda}}
by Proposition
4.11
.
∎

5.5.
Grothendieck groups

The Grothendieck ring
\mathrm{K}(\text{Rep}(\mathbf{P}))
is given by
\Lambda^{\otimes n}
, where
\Lambda
is the ring of symmetric functions. One can consider the category
\text{Mod}_{\textbf{A}}
as a module over
\text{Rep}(\mathbf{P})
, and so
\mathrm{K}(\text{Mod}_{\textbf{A}})
is a module over
\Lambda^{\otimes n}
. In this subsection, we describe this module structure.

Lemma 5.12
.

For each
d=0,\ldots,n
, the Grothendieck group
\mathrm{K}(\mathrm{D}^{b}_{\mathrm{fg}}(\textbf{A})_{d})
is a free
\Lambda^{\otimes n}
-module of rank
1
with generator
[A_{d}]
.

Proof.

By Corollary
5.11
, the
\Lambda^{\otimes n}
-linear map

\mathrm{K}(\text{Rep}(\mathbf{P}))\to\mathrm{K}(\mathrm{D}^{b}_{\mathrm{fg}}(\textbf{A})_{d}):[V]\mapsto[A_{d}\otimes V]

is an isomorphism.
∎

Theorem 5.13
.

We have an isomorphism of
\Lambda^{\otimes n}
-modules

\mathrm{K}(\text{Mod}_{\textbf{A}})\cong\bigoplus_{d=0}^{n}\mathrm{K}(\mathrm{D}^{b}_{\mathrm{fg}}(\textbf{A})_{d}).

In particular,
\mathrm{K}(\text{Mod}_{\textbf{A}})
is a free
\Lambda^{\otimes n}
-module of rank
n+1
.

Proof.

By
[
19
, Proposition 4.17]
, the above isomorphisms holds as groups. The projection maps
\mathrm{K}(\text{Mod}_{\textbf{A}})\to\mathrm{K}(\mathrm{D}^{b}_{\mathrm{fg}}(\textbf{A})_{d})
giving the isomorphism is defined by
\mathrm{R}\Sigma_{>d-1}\circ\mathrm{R}\Gamma_{\leq d}
, and so the projection maps are
\Lambda^{\otimes n}
-linear. Thus, we have the desired isomorphism of
\Lambda^{\otimes n}
-modules. The second statement now follows from Lemma
5.12
.
∎

5.6.
Hilbert series

In this subsection, we define (enhanced) Hilbert series and show that they are rational for finitely generated
A
-modules. This is a generalization of the definitions and results for
\mathbf{FI}
-modules given in
[
18
, §5]
.

Suppose
M
is a polynomial representation of
\textbf{L}\cong\prod_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{GL}
. Equivalently,
M
can be regarded as an
\mathbf{FB}^{\otimes n}
-module, i.e., a sequence
M=(M_{\underline{a}})_{\underline{a}\in\mathbf{N}^{n}}
of representations of products of symmetric groups
\mathfrak{S}_{\underline{a}}
. Under this identification, we have that the first factor of
\mathbf{FB}
corresponds to
\mathbf{GL}(\mathbf{V}_{(n)})
, while the
n^{\text{th}}
factor corresponds to
\mathbf{GL}(\mathbf{V}_{(1)})
; see
[
22
, §4.1]
for details.

Let
\underline{\lambda}
be an
n
-tuple of partitions, with
\underline{a}=(\lvert\lambda^{i}\rvert)
. To define the enhanced Hilbert series, we introduce the following notation.

•

Let
c_{\underline{\lambda}}
denote the conjugacy class of elements of
\mathfrak{S}_{\underline{a}}
with cycle type
\underline{\lambda}
.

•

Let
\mathrm{Tr}(c_{\underline{\lambda}}\mid M)
denote the trace of the action of
c_{\underline{\lambda}}
on
M_{\underline{a}}
.

•

Let
t^{\underline{\lambda}}
denote the monomial

t^{\underline{\lambda}}=\prod_{i\in[n],\,j\geq 1}t_{ij}^{m_{j}(\lambda^{i})},

where
m_{j}(\lambda^{i})
denotes the number of times the positive integer
j
appears in the partition
\lambda^{i}
.

•

Let
\underline{\lambda}!
denote the positive integer

\underline{\lambda}!=\prod_{i\in[n],\,j\geq 1}m_{j}(\lambda^{i})!.

Suppose
M
is an
\mathbf{FB}^{\otimes n}
-module with
M_{\underline{a}}
finite-dimensional for each
\underline{a}
. We define the
(enhanced) Hilbert series

\widetilde{H}_{M}(t)
of
M
to be the formal series in variables
\{t_{ij}:i\in[n],j\geq 1\}
given by

\widetilde{H}_{M}(t)=\sum_{\underline{\lambda}}\mathrm{Tr}(c_{\underline{\lambda}}\mid M)\frac{t^{\underline{\lambda}}}{\underline{\lambda}!}.

Thus, the isomorphism class of
M
as an
\mathbf{FB}^{\otimes n}
-module can be completely determined by
\widetilde{H}_{M}(t)
. Under the equivalence of tensor categories between polynomial representations of
L
and
\mathbf{FB}^{\otimes n}
, the tensor product of
\mathbf{FB}^{\otimes n}
-modules is defined by Day convolution, which is given as follows. Suppose
M,N
are
\mathbf{FB}^{\otimes n}
-modules. For an
n
-tuple
\underline{a}=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})
, the
\mathfrak{S}_{\underline{a}}
-representation corresponding to
M\otimes N
is given by

(M\otimes N)_{\underline{a}}=\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}\underline{b},\underline{c}\in\mathbf{N}^{n},\\
a_{i}=b_{i}+c_{i}\end{subarray}}\text{Ind}_{\mathfrak{S}_{\underline{b}}\times\mathfrak{S}_{\underline{c}}}^{\mathfrak{S}_{\underline{a}}}(M_{\underline{b}}\otimes N_{\underline{c}}).

Then, the fact that the Hilbert series is multiplicative follows from the
n=1
case.

For
d\in[n]
, define
T_{d}\in\mathbf{Q}\llbracket t\rrbracket
by

T_{d}=\sum_{j\geq 1}(t_{1j}+t_{2j}+\cdots+t_{dj}).

Theorem 5.14
.

Let
M
be a finitely generated
A
-module. Then there exist
a\in\mathbf{N}
and polynomials
p_{0}(t),p_{1}(t),\ldots,p_{n}(t)\in\mathbf{Q}[t_{ij}:i\in[n],j\geq 1]
so that

\widetilde{H}_{M}(t)=p_{0}(t)+\sum_{d=1}^{n}p_{k}(t)\exp(T_{d}).

Proof.

We first show that
\widetilde{H}_{A_{d}}(t)=\exp(T_{d})
for
d\in[n]
. As an
\mathbf{FB}^{\otimes n}
-module, one may observe that the
\mathfrak{S}_{\underline{a}}
-representation corresponding to
A_{d}
is given by

(A_{d})_{\underline{a}}=\begin{cases}\text{triv}_{a_{1}}\boxtimes\cdots\boxtimes\text{triv}_{a_{d}}\quad&\text{$a_{i}=0$ for $i>d$,}\\
0\quad&\text{otherwise,}\end{cases}

where
\text{triv}_{a}
denotes the trivial representation of
\mathfrak{S}_{a}
. Thus,

\mathrm{Tr}(c_{\underline{\lambda}}\mid A_{d})=\begin{cases}1\quad&\text{$\lambda^{i}=0$ for $i>d$},\\
0\quad&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

The expression for
\widetilde{H}_{A_{d}}(t)
then follows.

Now, suppose
M=S_{\underline{\lambda}}
is a simple
\mathbf{P}
-representation. Let
\underline{a}=(\lvert\lambda^{i}\rvert)
. Then as an
\mathbf{FB}^{\otimes n}
-module,

M_{\underline{b}}=\begin{cases}M_{\lambda^{1}}\boxtimes\cdots\boxtimes M_{\lambda^{n}}\quad&\underline{b}=\underline{a},\\
0\quad&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

where
M_{\lambda^{i}}
denotes the simple representation of
\mathfrak{S}_{a_{i}}
corresponding to the partition
\lambda^{i}
. It is clear in this case that
\widetilde{H}_{M}(t)
is a polynomial.

The result now follows from Theorem
5.13
and the multiplicativity of the Hilbert series.
∎

6.
Equivalence with
\mathbf{FI}(n)
-modules

Definition 6.1
.

The category
\mathbf{FI}(n)
of
[n]
-weighted finite sets and injections that do not decrease weights is the following category:

•

The objects are
[n]
-weighted finite sets, i.e., finite sets
S=\bigsqcup_{i=1}^{n}S_{i}
, where
S_{i}
consists of the elements of weight
i
. We also use the notation
S=(S_{1},\ldots,S_{n})
.

•

A morphism
S\to T
is an injection
\varphi:S\to T
of sets such that weights do not decrease, i.e., for each
i
, we have
\varphi(S_{i})\subset\bigsqcup_{j=1}^{n}T_{j}
.

When
n=1
, this category is exactly the classical
\mathbf{FI}
category of finite sets and injections.

An
\mathbf{FI}(n)
-module

M
is a functor
\mathbf{FI}(n)\to\text{Vec}
, and a morphism of
\mathbf{FI}(n)
-modules is a natural transformation of functors. Let
\text{Mod}_{\mathbf{FI}(n)}
denote the category of
\mathbf{FI}(n)
-modules.
The goal of this section is to show that
\mathbf{FI}(n)
-modules are equivalent to
A
-modules.

6.1.
Preliminaries on
\mathbf{FI}(n)
-modules

6.1.1.
Morphisms in
\mathbf{FI}(n)

Let
\underline{a}\in\mathbf{N}^{n}
be a tuple of nonnegative integers. Similar to the combinatorial categories
\mathscr{C}_{d}
studied in §
3
, objects in
\mathbf{FI}(n)
can be identified with such tuples; we use
\underline{a}=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})
to denote the weighted set
([a_{1}],\ldots,[a_{n}])
, where the set in the
i^{\text{th}}
coordinate denotes the elements of weight
i
.

There is a partial order on
\mathbf{N}^{n}
known as the
dominance order
: we have that
(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})\geq(b_{1},\ldots,b_{n})
if and only if

a_{1}+\cdots+a_{i}\geq b_{1}+\cdots+b_{i},\quad\text{for each $i=1,\ldots,n$}.

This partial order describes exactly when a map
\underline{b}\to\underline{a}
exists in
\mathbf{FI}(n)
. In particular, for a tuple
\underline{a}=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})
, let
\tau(\underline{a})=(a_{n},\ldots,a_{1})
denote the reverse tuple. Then there is a map
\underline{b}\to\underline{a}
if and only if
\tau(\underline{b})\leq\tau(\underline{a})
.

6.1.2.
Simple and projective modules

The simple
\mathbf{FI}(n)
-objects
\textbf{M}_{\underline{\lambda}}
are indexed by
n
-tuples of partitions
\underline{\lambda}=(\lambda^{1},\ldots,\lambda^{n})
and given by simple representations of products of symmetric groups:

\textbf{M}_{\underline{\lambda}}(\underline{b})\mapsto\begin{cases}M_{\lambda^{1}}\boxtimes\cdots\boxtimes M_{\lambda^{n}}\quad&\text{if $b_{i}=\lvert\lambda^{i}\rvert$,}\\
0\quad&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

The main family of
\mathbf{FI}(n)
-modules of interest for us is defined as follows. For
\underline{a}\in\mathbf{N}^{n}
, let
\mathscr{P}_{\underline{a}}
be the
\mathbf{FI}(n)
-module defined by

\mathscr{P}_{\underline{a}}(S)=\mathbf{C}[\text{Hom}_{\mathbf{FI}(n)}(\underline{a},S)].

Then, if
M
is any
\mathbf{FI}(n)
-module, we have by
[
21
, Proposition 3.2]
that

\text{Hom}_{\mathbf{FI}(n)}(\mathscr{P}_{\underline{a}},M)=M(\underline{a}).

Thus,
\mathscr{P}_{\underline{a}}
is a projective
\mathbf{FI}(n)
-module, and we call it the
principal projective
at
\underline{a}
.

The group
\mathfrak{S}_{\underline{a}}
acts on
\mathscr{P}_{\underline{a}}
by
\mathbf{FI}(n)
-module automorphisms. If
\underline{\lambda}
is such that
\lvert\lambda^{i}\rvert=a_{i}
for each
i
, we have a projective
\mathbf{FI}(n)
-module
\mathscr{P}_{\underline{\lambda}}
given by the
\textbf{M}_{\underline{\lambda}}
-isotypic piece of
\mathscr{P}_{\underline{a}}
:

\mathscr{P}_{\underline{\lambda}}=\text{Hom}_{\mathfrak{S}_{\underline{a}}}(\textbf{M}_{\underline{\lambda}},\mathscr{P}_{\underline{a}}).

We have that
\mathscr{P}_{\underline{\lambda}}
is the projective cover of
\textbf{M}_{\underline{\lambda}}
. Then, since the
\mathscr{P}_{\underline{\lambda}}
’s are direct summands of the principal projectives, all
\mathbf{FI}(n)
-modules have a presentation by principal projective
\mathbf{FI}(n)
-modules.

6.1.3.
Tensor product

There is a natural symmetric monoidal structure
\amalg
on
\mathbf{FI}(n)
given by disjoint union. For two weighted sets
S,T
, their product
S\amalg T
is
(S_{1}\sqcup T_{1},\ldots,S_{n}\sqcup T_{n})
. In particular, we have that
\underline{a}\amalg\underline{b}=(a_{1}+b_{1},\ldots,a_{n}+b_{n})
.

The monoidal structure on
\mathbf{FI}(n)
defines a symmetric tensor product
\otimes
of
\mathbf{FI}(n)
-modules, known as
Day convolution
or
convolution tensor product
(see e.g.,
[
17
, §2.1.14]
and
[
21
, §3.10]
). This tensor product among principal projectives has an especially nice description: by
[
21
, Proposition 3.25(d)]
, we have that

\mathscr{P}_{\underline{a}}\otimes\mathscr{P}_{\underline{b}}=\mathscr{P}_{\underline{a}\amalg\underline{b}}.

6.2.
A mapping property

We describe a mapping property for certain projective objects of
\text{Rep}(\mathbf{P})
that will be used to show the equivalence. For a tuple
\underline{a}
, let
W_{\underline{a}}
denote the principal projective
\mathbf{P}
-representation

W_{\underline{a}}=\bigotimes_{i=1}^{n}(\mathbf{V}_{n-i+1})^{\otimes a_{i}}.

It is generated as a
\mathbf{P}
-representation by the vector

\epsilon_{\underline{a}}=(e_{n1}\otimes\cdots e_{na_{1}})\otimes\cdots\otimes(e_{11}\otimes\cdots\otimes e_{1a_{n}}).

Let
\lambda(\underline{a})=((1^{a_{n}}),\ldots,(1^{a_{1}}))
denote the weight of this vector, and for any
\mathbf{P}
-representation
V
, let
V^{\lambda(\underline{a})}
denote the
\lambda(\underline{a})
-weight space of
V
as a representation of
\textbf{L}\subset\mathbf{P}
.

Proposition 6.2
.

Let
V
be a polynomial representation of
\mathbf{P}
. Then the map

\text{Hom}_{\mathbf{P}}(W_{\underline{a}},V)\to V^{\lambda(\underline{a})}:f\mapsto f(\epsilon_{\underline{a}})

is an isomorphism.

Proof.

If
f:W_{\underline{a}}\to V
is a map of
\mathbf{P}
-representations, then it must preserve
L
-weight spaces. In addition,
\epsilon_{\underline{a}}
generates
W_{\underline{a}}
, and so its image under
f
determines
f
as a map. Therefore, it suffices to show that the above assignment is surjective.

We first consider the case when
V
is also a principal projective object
V=W_{\underline{b}}
. Recall from §
2.1
that the categories of finite length
\mathbf{FB}(n)
-modules and finite length polynomial
\mathbf{P}
-representations are equivalent.
Applying this equivalence and the mapping property for principal projectives for
\mathbf{FB}(n)
-modules
[
22
, §2.3]
, the dimension of
\text{Hom}_{\mathbf{P}}(W_{\underline{a}},W_{\underline{b}})
as a vector space is equal to that of
\text{Hom}_{\mathbf{FB}(n)}(\underline{b},\underline{a}).

We now consider the dimension of
(W_{\underline{b}})^{\lambda(\underline{a})}
. If
a_{1}+\cdots+a_{n}\neq b_{1}+\cdots+b_{n}
, then this weight space is clearly
0
. Otherwise, the dimension agrees with that of
\text{Hom}_{\mathbf{FB}(n)}(\underline{b},\underline{a})
: the tensor factor
(\mathbf{V}_{n-i+1})^{\otimes b_{i}}
can only contribute to the first
n-i+1
coordinates of
n
-tuple
\lambda(\underline{a})
, which are
((1^{a_{n}}),\ldots,(1^{a_{i}}),0,\ldots,0)
, and so
(W_{\underline{b}})^{\lambda(\underline{a})}
has a basis indexed by
\text{Hom}_{\mathbf{FB}(n)}(\underline{b},\underline{a})
.
This shows that
\text{Hom}_{\mathbf{P}}(W_{\underline{a}},W_{\underline{b}})\cong(W_{\underline{b}})^{\lambda(\underline{a})}
.

Now suppose
V
is an arbitrary representation of
\mathbf{P}
; without loss of generality, we may also assume
V
is of finite length. By
[
22
, Proposition 4.8]
, there is a surjection
\varphi:\bigoplus W_{\underline{b}}\to V
from a finite direct sum of principal projective objects. Let
v\in V^{\lambda(\underline{a})}
, and let
\hat{v}\in\varphi^{-1}(v)
be a lift of
v
. By the previous paragraph, there exists a
\mathbf{P}
-equivariant map
f:W_{\underline{a}}\to\bigoplus W_{\underline{b}}
such that

f(\epsilon_{\underline{a}})=\hat{v}\in\bigoplus(W_{\underline{b}})^{\lambda(\underline{a})}.

Then, the composition
\varphi\circ f:W_{\underline{a}}\to V
gives the desired map of
\mathbf{P}
-representations that shows the above assignment is an isomorphism.
∎

6.3.
Proof of the equivalence

We now prove that
\text{Mod}_{\mathbf{FI}(n)}\cong\text{Mod}_{\textbf{A}}
as tensor categories. Since both
\text{Mod}_{\textbf{A}}
and
\text{Mod}_{\mathbf{FI}(n)}
are abelian categories with enough projectives, it suffices to show that their respective categories of projective objects are equivalent as tensor categories.

The projective objects of
\text{Mod}_{\textbf{A}}
are exactly those of the form
\textbf{A}\otimes V
, where
V
is a projective polynomial representation of
\mathbf{P}
. Recall from §
2.1
that the projective covers of the simple
\mathbf{P}
-representations are of the form
\bigotimes_{i=1}^{n}\textbf{S}_{\lambda^{i}}(\mathbf{V}_{n-i+1})
. These representations can all be realized as direct summands of
W_{\underline{a}}
’s.

For a tuple
\underline{a}\in\mathbf{N}^{n}
, let
Q_{\underline{a}}
denote the projective
A
-module
\textbf{A}\otimes W_{\underline{a}}
. It follows that every
A
-module has a presentation by
Q_{\underline{a}}
’s, and so we call these the
principal projective

A
-modules. To show that the categories of projective objects are equivalent, it is enough to show that the abelian tensor categories generated by the
Q_{\underline{a}}
’s and the principal projective
\mathbf{FI}(n)
-modules
\mathscr{P}_{\underline{a}}
are equivalent.

Note that
Q_{\underline{a}}
is generated as an
A
-module by the element

v_{\underline{a}}=1\otimes\epsilon_{\underline{a}}=1\otimes(e_{n1}\otimes\cdots\otimes e_{na_{1}})\otimes\cdots\otimes(e_{11}\otimes\cdots\otimes e_{1a_{n}}),

which has weight
\lambda(\underline{a})=((1^{a_{n}}),\ldots,(1^{a_{1}}))
under the
L
-action. Furthermore, the tensor product
Q_{\underline{a}}\otimes Q_{\underline{b}}
is also a principal projective. Let
\underline{a+b}
denote the tuple
(a_{1}+b_{1},\ldots,a_{n}+b_{n})
. Then, we have

Q_{\underline{a}}\otimes Q_{\underline{b}}=\textbf{A}\otimes\bigotimes_{i=1}^{n}(\mathbf{V}_{n-i+1})^{\otimes(a_{i}+b_{i})}=Q_{\underline{a+b}}.

We show that the assignment
\mathscr{P}_{\underline{a}}\mapsto Q_{\underline{a}}
induces a tensor functor that gives the equivalence. We now define the functor on morphisms. A map
\mathscr{P}_{\underline{a}}\to\mathscr{P}_{\underline{b}}
is the same as giving a morphism
\sigma:\underline{b}\to\underline{a}
in
\mathbf{FI}(n)
. The corresponding map of
A
-modules
f_{\sigma}:Q_{\underline{a}}\to Q_{\underline{b}}
is given as follows.

Since
v_{\underline{a}}
generates
Q_{\underline{b}}
, it suffices to describe its image under
f_{\sigma}
so that the restriction of
f_{\sigma}
to
W_{\underline{a}}\subset\textbf{A}\otimes W_{\underline{a}}
is
\mathbf{P}
-equivariant. The image
f_{\sigma}(v_{\underline{a}})
is a pure tensor: in the tensor factor of
Q_{\underline{b}}
corresponding to an element
i\in\underline{b}
of weight
d
, we have the element that is in the tensor factor of
Q_{\underline{a}}
corresponding to the element
\sigma(i)\in\underline{b}
; in the tensor factor corresponding to
A
, we have the monomial consisting of the product of
x_{ij}
’s, where the indices are coming from those in
v_{\underline{a}}
that are not given by a tensor factor in the image of
\sigma
. One sees that the image
f_{\sigma}(v_{\underline{a}})
is in the
\lambda(\underline{a})
-weight space of
Q_{\underline{b}}
. By Proposition
6.2
, this gives a well-defined map of
\mathbf{P}
-modules, and therefore also of
A
-modules.

Example 6.3
.

Let
n=3
. Let
\underline{a}=(0,1,2)
and
\underline{b}=(1,1,0)
, and consider the morphism
\sigma:\underline{b}\to\underline{a}
in
\mathbf{FI}(n)
given by mapping the element of weight
1
in
\underline{b}
to the element of weight
2
in
\underline{a}
, and mapping the element of weight
2
in
\underline{b}
to the second element of weight
3
in
\underline{a}
. Then the corresponding map
f_{\sigma}:Q_{\underline{a}}\to Q_{\underline{b}}
is given by

1\otimes e_{21}\otimes e_{11}\otimes e_{12}\mapsto x_{11}\otimes e_{21}\otimes e_{12}\in\textbf{A}\otimes\mathbf{V}_{3}\otimes\mathbf{V}_{2}.

Proposition 6.4
.

The assignment
\sigma\mapsto f_{\sigma}
satisfies the following properties:

(1)

It induces an isomorphism of vector spaces
\text{Hom}_{\mathbf{FI}(n)}(\mathscr{P}_{\underline{a}},\mathscr{P}_{\underline{b}})\cong\text{Hom}_{\textbf{A}}(Q_{\underline{a}},Q_{\underline{b}})
.

(2)

For two morphisms
\sigma:\underline{c}\to\underline{b},\pi:\underline{b}\to\underline{a}
in
\mathbf{FI}(n)
, we have that
f_{\pi\circ\sigma}=f_{\sigma}\circ f_{\pi}
.

(3)

For two morphisms
\sigma:\underline{c}\to\underline{a},\pi:\underline{d}\to\underline{b}
in
\mathbf{FI}(n)
, we have that
f_{\sigma\amalg\pi}=f_{\sigma}\otimes f_{\pi}
as maps
Q_{\underline{a+b}}\to Q_{\underline{c+d}}
.

Proof.

(1): We have that
\text{Hom}_{\mathbf{FI}(n)}(\underline{b},\underline{a})=0
if and only if
\tau(\underline{b})\not\leq\tau(\underline{a})
. Furthermore, we have that there is no weight space of weight
\lambda(\underline{a})=((1^{a_{n}}),\ldots,(1^{a_{1}}))
in
Q_{\underline{b}}
when
\tau(\underline{b})\not\leq\tau(\underline{a})
. Thus,
\text{Hom}_{\mathbf{FI}(n)}(\mathscr{P}_{\underline{a}},\mathscr{P}_{\underline{b}})=0
implies
\text{Hom}_{\textbf{A}}(Q_{\underline{a}},Q_{\underline{b}})=0
.

Now suppose
\tau(\underline{b})\leq\tau(\underline{a})
. Then the weight space of weight
\lambda(\underline{a})
in
Q_{\underline{b}}
is exactly the vector space spanned by the
f_{\sigma}(v_{\underline{a}})
’s as we range over all morphisms
\sigma:\underline{b}\to\underline{a}
in
\mathbf{FI}(n)
. Since the
f_{\sigma}
’s are indeed maps of
A
-modules, we have the desired isomorphism of vector spaces.

(2) and (3): These follow from the naturality of the construction of the
f_{\sigma}
’s, along with the fact for (3) that
Q_{\underline{a}}\otimes Q_{\underline{b}}\cong Q_{\underline{a+b}}
is given by the monoidal structure in
\mathbf{FI}(n)
.
∎

Along with the discussion at the beginning of this subsection, the equivalence of categories now follows.

Theorem 6.5
.

We have an equivalence of tensor categories
\text{Mod}_{\textbf{A}}\cong\text{Mod}_{\mathbf{FI}(n)}
.

1.
Introduction

Let
L
be a positive self-adjoint differential operator densely defined in a Hilbert space
L_{2}(\Omega,d\mu)
.
Fractional powers
L^{\sigma}
, for
\sigma>0
, can be defined in various
(abstract) equivalent ways,
one of the most standard being via spectral theory:

(1.1)

\langle L^{\sigma}f,g\rangle\,=\,\int_{\sigma(L)}{\lambda}^{\sigma}\,dE_{f,g},\quad f\in\mathop{\rm Dom}(L^{\sigma}),\;\;g\in L_{2}(\Omega,d\mu),

where
E
denotes a resolution of the identity associated with
L
; see e.g.
[
13
, Ch 13]
. When the spectrum is discrete

\sigma(L)=\{{\lambda}_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
, and
\{{\varphi}_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
is an orthonormal basis of eigenfunctions, then (
1.1
) takes the form

L^{\sigma}f\,=\,\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\,{\lambda}_{n}^{\sigma}\,\langle f,{\varphi}_{n}\rangle\,{\varphi}_{n},

say for
f\in\mbox{\rm span}\,\{{\varphi}_{n}\}
. Alternatively, it is also possible to express
L^{\sigma}
in terms of the contraction
semigroup
\{e^{-tL}\}_{t>0}
. For instance, when
\sigma\in(0,1)
, via the Bochner formula

(1.2)

L^{\sigma}f=\tfrac{1}{{\Gamma}(-\sigma)}\int_{0}^{\infty}(e^{-tL}f-f)\,\frac{dt}{t^{1+\sigma}};

see e.g.
[
17
, Ch IX.11]
and references therein.

When
L
equals the classical Laplacian
-{\Delta}
and
f\in\mathcal{S}({{\mathbb{R}}^{d}})
, both (
1.1
) and (
1.2
) lead to the explicit expression

(1.3)

(-{\Delta})^{\sigma}f(x)\,=\,c_{d,\sigma}\,\mbox{{\small PV}}\int_{{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}}\frac{f(x)-f(y)}{|x-y|^{d+2\sigma}}\,dy,\quad x\in{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}},\;\;\sigma\in(0,1),

with a suitable constant
c_{d,\sigma}>0
; see e.g.
[
9
]
. This pointwise formula does actually make sense for a larger class of functions,
e.g. when
f\in L_{1}(dy/(1+|y|)^{d+2\sigma})
and
f
is Hölder continuous of order
2\sigma+{\varepsilon}
near the point
x
.
For general operators
L
, however, explicit expressions such as (
1.3
) are not common, and
the definition of
L^{\sigma}f
as in (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) must necessarily be restricted to a suitable
dense class of functions
f
.

Based on work of Caffarelli and Silvestre
[
2
]
, Stinga and Torrea proposed in
[
14
]
to define
L^{\sigma}f
as the
Neumann boundary value associated with the elliptic PDE

(1.4)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}u_{tt}+\tfrac{1-2\sigma}{t}\,u_{t}\,=\,Lu,&&t>0\\
u(0,x)=f(x).&&\\
\end{array}\right.

More precisely, if
\sigma\in(0,1)
and
\mathfrak{c}_{\sigma}=2^{2\sigma-1}{\Gamma}(\sigma)/{\Gamma}(1-\sigma)
, they set

(1.5)

L^{\sigma}f(x)\,=\,-\mathfrak{c}_{\sigma}\,\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}\,t^{1-2\sigma}\,\partial_{t}u(t,x),

where
u(t,x)
is the solution of (
1.4
) given by the Poisson-like integral

(1.6)

u(t,x)=P^{\sigma}_{t}f(x):=\frac{(t/2)^{2\sigma}}{{\Gamma}(\sigma)}\,\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\frac{t^{2}}{4s}}\,e^{-sL}f(x)\,\,\frac{ds}{s^{1+\sigma}};

see
[
14
, Theorem 1.1]
. Then the authors specialize to the Hermite operator

L=-{\Delta}+|x|^{2}\quad\mbox{in $L_{2}({{\mathbb{R}}^{d}})$},

and prove that the
pointwise limit
in (
1.5
) exists at every
x\in{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}
,
and coincides with (
1.2
),
whenever
f\in C^{2}({{\mathbb{R}}^{d}})\cap L_{1}(dy/(1+|y|)^{N})
, for some
N>0
; see
[
14
, Theorem 4.2]
,
and
[
15
,
12
]
for slightly less restrictive smoothness assumptions.

The purpose of this paper is to show the validity of (
1.5
) for a wider class of functions
f
, with optimal integrability assumptions and very mild smoothness at a given point
x_{0}\in{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}
.
We also consider the slightly more general family of operators

(1.7)

L=-{\Delta}+|x|^{2}+m,\quad\mbox{in $L_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})$},

with a constant parameter
m\geq-d
(so that
L
is positive). For
m=0
this is the usual Hermite operator,
while for
m=-d
it can be transformed (after a change of variables) into the
Ornstein-Uhlenbeck operator

\mathcal{O}=-{\Delta}+2x\cdot\nabla,\quad\mbox{in }\;L_{2}({{\mathbb{R}}^{d}},e^{-|x|^{2}}\,dx);

see
\lx@sectionsign\ref{S_OU}
below.

To state our results, we now describe the integrability
and smoothness conditions we shall use below. Given a weight
\Phi(y)\geq 0
,
we say that
f\in L_{1}(\Phi)
when

(1.8)

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}|f(y)|\,\Phi(y)\,dy<\infty.

Associated with
L=-{\Delta}+|x|^{2}+m
in (
1.7
), and
\sigma\in(0,1)
we define the weight

(1.9)

\Phi_{\sigma}(y)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\frac{e^{-|y|^{2}/2}}{(1+|y|)^{\frac{d+m}{2}}[\ln(e+|y|)]^{1+\sigma}}&\mbox{ if }m>-d\\
&\\
\displaystyle\frac{e^{-|y|^{2}/2}}{[\ln(e+|y|)]^{\sigma}}&\mbox{ if }m=-d.\\
\end{array}\right.

Then,
f\in L_{1}(\Phi_{\sigma})

ensures that
the Poisson-like integral
P^{\sigma}_{t}f(x)
in (
1.6
) is a well-defined (smooth) function when
(t,x)\in(0,\infty)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,
and this condition is actually best possible for that property;
see
[
6
, Theorem 1.1]
(or
[
8
,
5
]
).

Next, when
{\alpha}\in(0,2)
, we shall say that a (locally integrable) function
f
is
{\alpha}
-smooth at
x_{0}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, denoted
f\in\mathcal{D}^{\alpha}(x_{0})
, if it
satisfies the Dini-type condition

\int_{|h|\leq{\delta}}\frac{|f(x_{0}+h)+f(x_{0}-h)-2f(x_{0})|}{|h|^{d+{\alpha}}}\,dh\,<\,\infty,\quad\mbox{for some ${\delta}>0$}.

We say that
f
is
strictly
{\alpha}
-smooth at
x_{0}
, denoted
f\in\mathcal{D}_{\rm st}^{\alpha}(x_{0})
, if

f\in\mathcal{D}^{\alpha}(x_{0}){\quad\mbox{and}\quad}\int_{|h|\leq{\delta}}\frac{|f(x_{0}+h)-f(x_{0}-h)|}{|h|^{d+{\alpha}-3}}\,dh\,<\,\infty.

Observe that this last condition is redundant if
d+{\alpha}-3\leq 0
;
in particular, if
d=1
and
{\alpha}\in(0,2)
, or
d=2
and
{\alpha}\in(0,1]
.
In other cases however, the classes are different and we shall need this distinction to obtain our results.
We refer to
\lx@sectionsign\ref{S_smooth}
below for several examples of these notions,
and their relation with local Lipschitz conditions at
x_{0}
.

Our first main result can now be stated as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
L
be the Hermite operator in (
1.7
),
\sigma\in(0,1)
and
\Phi_{\sigma}(y)
as in (
1.9
). Suppose that

f\in L_{1}(\Phi_{\sigma}),{\quad\mbox{and}\quad}f\in\mathcal{D}_{\rm st}^{2\sigma}(x_{0})\quad\mbox{for some $x_{0}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}$}.

Then, the number
L^{\sigma}f(x_{0})
exists both,
in the limiting sense of (
1.5
) and as the absolutely convergent integral in (
1.2
), and both definitions agree.

The optimality of the smoothness condition is discussed in §6 below. The corresponding version for the Ornstein-Uhlenbeck operator
takes the following form.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
L=\mathcal{O}=-{\Delta}+2x\cdot\nabla
and
\sigma\in(0,1)
. Suppose that

f\in L_{1}(e^{-|y|^{2}}/[\log(e+y)]^{\sigma}),{\quad\mbox{and}\quad}f\in\mathcal{D}_{\rm st}^{2\sigma}(x_{0})\quad\mbox{for some $x_{0}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}$}.

Then, the number
\mathcal{O}^{\sigma}f(x_{0})
exists both,
in the limiting sense of (
1.5
) and as the absolutely convergent integral in (
1.2
), and both definitions agree.

Below, we have attempted to present our results in sufficient generality so that many of the arguments can be applied to
general operators
L
, while only a few steps require explicit estimates on the kernels.
We illustrate this fact in §
5
, where with a minimal effort we obtain a version of the above theorems for the operator
{\mathbb{L}}=-{\Delta}+R
, with
R>0
;
see Theorem
5.2
below.

2.
Preliminary results for general operators
L

In this section we shall assume that
L
is the infinitesimal generator of a semigroup of operators

\{e^{-tL}\}_{t>0}
in
L_{2}({{\mathbb{R}}^{d}})
, and that these are described by the integrals

(2.1)

e^{-tL}f(x)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}h_{t}(x,y)f(y)\,dy,

for a suitable positive kernel
h_{t}(x,y)
.
For each
\sigma\in(0,1)
we then consider the family of subordinated operators
\{P^{\sigma}_{t}=P^{\sigma,L}_{t}\}_{t>0}
,
formally given by

P^{\sigma}_{t}f:=\tfrac{(t/2)^{2\sigma}}{{\Gamma}(\sigma)}\,\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\frac{t^{2}}{4s}}\,e^{-sL}(f)\,\,\frac{ds}{s^{1+\sigma}}.

More precisely, we let

(2.2)

P^{\sigma}_{t}f(x)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}p^{\sigma}_{t}(x,y)f(y)\,dy,

where the corresponding kernels
p^{\sigma}_{t}(x,y)=p^{\sigma,L}_{t}(x,y)
are defined by

(2.3)

p^{\sigma}_{t}(x,y)=\tfrac{(t/2)^{2\sigma}}{\Gamma(\sigma)}\,\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\frac{t^{2}}{4s}}\,h_{s}(x,y)\,\frac{ds}{s^{1+\sigma}}.

Observe that a crude estimate such as
0<h_{s}(x,y)\lesssim s^{-d/2}
(which will be satisfied by all the operators
L
we shall use)
guarantees that the integral in (
2.3
) is absolutely convergent, and moreover

(2.4)

\partial_{t}\big[p^{\sigma}_{t}(x,y)\big]\,=\,\mathfrak{a}_{\sigma}\,t^{2\sigma-1}\int_{0}^{\infty}\Big(2\sigma-\frac{t^{2}}{2s}\Big)e^{-\frac{t^{2}}{4s}}h_{s}(x,y)\frac{ds}{s^{1+\sigma}},

with
\mathfrak{a}_{\sigma}=1/(4^{\sigma}{\Gamma}(\sigma))
. It is also not hard to show that

(2.5)

t\,\Big|\partial_{t}\big[p^{\sigma}_{t}(x,y)\big]\Big|\,\lesssim\,p_{t}^{\sigma}(x,y)+p_{t/\sqrt{2}}^{\sigma}(x,y),

using the fact that
\sup_{v>0}ve^{-v}<\infty
. However, in order to handle derivatives of the expression
P^{\sigma}_{t}f(x)
in (
2.2
) we shall need more information
on the decay of the kernel
p^{\sigma}_{t}(x,y)
.

In the case that
L
is a Hermite operator the decay is given by the following result from
[
6
, Lemma 3.1]
,
which also clarifies the optimal role of the functions
\Phi_{\sigma}(y)
in (
1.9
).

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
L
be the Hermite operator in (
1.7
),
\sigma\in(0,1)
and
\Phi_{\sigma}(y)
as in (
1.9
).
Then, for every
t>0
and
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, there exist finite numbers
c_{1}(t,x)>0
and
c_{2}(t,x)>0
such
that

(2.6)

c_{1}(t,x)\,\Phi_{\sigma}(y)\,\leq\,p^{\sigma}_{t}(x,y)\,\leq\,c_{2}(t,x)\,\Phi_{\sigma}(y)\;,\quad\forall\;y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}.

In this section we wish to keep the general setting for the operator
L
described above, but we shall
additionally
assume that,
for each given
\sigma\in(0,1)
, the kernel
p^{\sigma}_{t}(x,y)
satisfies

(2.7)

p^{\sigma}_{t}(x,y)\,\leq\,c_{2}(t,x)\,\Phi(y)\;,\quad\forall\;y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}.

for some function
\Phi(y)
and some
c_{2}(t,x)>0
.
In all the results in this section we shall not need the explicit expression of
\Phi(y)
.

Our first result will establish a relation between the two
pointwise
definitions of
L^{\sigma}f(x)
presented in (
1.2
) and (
1.5
) above.
We first consider the following general definition.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
\sigma\in(0,1)
and
L
be an operator such that (
2.7
) holds for some
\Phi(y)
. Given
f\in L_{1}(\Phi)
, we say that a point
x_{0}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
is

L^{\sigma}
-admissible for
f
, denoted
x_{0}\in\mathcal{A}_{f}(L^{\sigma})
, if

(2.8)

\int_{0}^{\infty}\big|e^{-sL}f(x_{0})-f(x_{0})\big|\frac{ds}{s^{1+\sigma}}<\infty.

In that case we let

(2.9)

L^{\sigma}f(x_{0}):=\tfrac{1}{{\Gamma}(-\sigma)}\int_{0}^{\infty}\Big(e^{-sL}f(x_{0})-f(x_{0})\Big)\,\frac{ds}{s^{1+\sigma}},

where
{\Gamma}(-\sigma)={\Gamma}(1-\sigma)/(-\sigma)
.

The following result is partially based on the proof of
[
14
, (4.6)]
.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
\sigma\in(0,1)
and
L
be an operator such that (
2.7
) holds for some
\Phi(y)
.
If
f\in L_{1}(\Phi)
and
x\in\mathcal{A}_{f}(L^{\sigma})
then

(2.10)

\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}\,-\mathfrak{c}_{\sigma}\,t^{1-2\sigma}\,\partial_{t}\Big[P^{\sigma}_{t}f(x)\Big]=L^{\sigma}f(x)\,,

with
\mathfrak{c}_{\sigma}=2^{2\sigma-1}{\Gamma}(\sigma)/{\Gamma}(1-\sigma)
.

Proof.

Using (
2.2
), (
2.4
), (
2.5
) and
f\in L_{1}(\Phi)
one can justify that

t^{1-2\sigma}\,\partial_{t}\Big[P^{\sigma}_{t}f(x)\Big]=\mathfrak{a}_{\sigma}\,\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\Big(2\sigma-\frac{t^{2}}{2s}\Big)e^{-\frac{t^{2}}{4s}}h_{s}(x,y)\,\frac{ds}{s^{1+\sigma}}\,f(y)\,dy,

with
\mathfrak{a}_{\sigma}=1/(4^{\sigma}{\Gamma}(\sigma))
.
We claim that

I=\int_{0}^{\infty}\Big(2\sigma-\frac{t^{2}}{2s}\Big)e^{-\frac{t^{2}}{4s}}\frac{ds}{s^{1+\sigma}}=0.

In fact, using the
change
z=t^{2}/4s
we see that

I=\frac{2\cdot 4^{\sigma}}{t^{2\sigma}}\int_{0}^{\infty}(\sigma-z)e^{-z}z^{\sigma-1}\,dz=\frac{2\cdot 4^{\sigma}}{t^{2\sigma}}\big(\sigma\Gamma(\sigma)-\Gamma(\sigma+1)\big)=0.

Then

\displaystyle t^{1-2\sigma}\,\partial_{t}\Big[P^{\sigma}_{t}f(x)\Big]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathfrak{a}_{\sigma}\,\int_{0}^{\infty}\Big(2\sigma-\frac{t^{2}}{2s}\Big)e^{-\frac{t^{2}}{4s}}\,\Big[\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}h_{s}(x,y)f(y)\,dy-f(x)\Big]\frac{ds}{s^{1+\sigma}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathfrak{a}_{\sigma}\,\int_{0}^{\infty}\Big(2\sigma-\frac{t^{2}}{2s}\Big)e^{-\frac{t^{2}}{4s}}\,\Big[e^{-sL}f(x)-f(x)\Big]\frac{ds}{s^{1+\sigma}}.

Since we assume that
x\in\mathcal{A}_{f}(L^{\sigma})
, by the Lebesgue dominated convergence theorem we can take limits as
t\to 0^{+}
,
and after adjusting the constants one easily obtains the result.
∎

In view of Proposition
2.3
, we are interested in finding conditions on a function
f
which guarantee that a given point
x\in\mathcal{A}_{f}(L^{\sigma})
. Our next observation shows that only one part of the integral in (
2.8
) must be checked.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
\sigma\in(0,1)
and
L
be an operator such that (
2.7
) holds for some
\Phi(y)
. Then, for every
A>0
and every
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
there exists
c(x,A)>0
such that

(2.11)

\int_{A}^{\infty}h_{s}(x,y)\,\frac{ds}{s^{1+\sigma}}\leq c(x,A)\,\Phi(y),\quad y\in{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}.

Moreover, if
f\in L_{1}(\Phi)
and
|f(x)|<\infty
then

\int_{A}^{\infty}|e^{-sL}f(x)-f(x)|\,\frac{ds}{s^{1+\sigma}}<\infty.

Proof.

To prove (
2.11
), note that

\int_{A}^{\infty}h_{s}(x,y)\,\frac{ds}{s^{1+\sigma}}\leq e^{\frac{1}{4A}}\,\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\frac{1}{4s}}\,h_{s}(x,y)\,\frac{ds}{s^{1+\sigma}}=c\,p_{1}^{\sigma}(x,y)\leq c(x,A)\,\Phi(y).

For the last statement,

\displaystyle\int_{A}^{\infty}\!|e^{-sL}f(x)-f(x)|\frac{ds}{s^{1+\sigma}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}}|f(y)|\int_{A}^{\infty}\!h_{s}(x,y)\,\frac{ds}{s^{1+\sigma}}\,dy+|f(x)|\,\int_{A}^{\infty}\!\frac{ds}{s^{1+\sigma}}

by (
2.11
)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle c(x,A)\int_{{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}}|f(y)|\,\Phi(y)\,dy+|f(x)|<\infty.

∎

In order to show that
x_{0}\in\mathcal{A}_{f}(L^{\sigma})
we expect that some smoothness of
f
at the point
x_{0}
must be required. Actually, the smoothness of
f
will only play a

role in the integrals defining the property
\mathcal{A}_{f}(L^{\sigma})
. This motivates to consider a local notion of

Definition 2.5
.

Let
\sigma\in(0,1)
, and
L
an operator as above.
Given a locally integrable function
f
, we say that a point
x_{0}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
is

locally
L^{\sigma}
-admissible for
f
, denoted
x_{0}\in\mathcal{A}^{\rm loc}_{f}(L^{\sigma})

{\delta}>0
and
A>0
such that the integrals

(2.12)

\mathcal{I}_{\delta}f(x_{0},s)=\int_{|x_{0}-y|<\delta}h_{s}(x_{0},y)\,\big[f(y)-f(x_{0})\big]dy,\quad s\in(0,A)

satisfy the property

(2.13)

\int_{0}^{A}\big|\mathcal{I}_{\delta}f(x_{0},s)\big|\,\frac{ds}{s^{1+\sigma}}\,<\,\infty.

The next lemma gives decay conditions on the kernel and smoothness of
f
at
x_{0}
that guarantee the validity of the previous property.

Proposition 2.6
.

Let
\sigma\in(0,1)
, and
L
an operator as above.
Let
x_{0}\in{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}
be fixed, and assume that the kernel

{\delta}>0
and

(2.14)

\int_{0}^{A}h_{s}(x_{0},x_{0}+y)\frac{ds}{s^{1+\sigma}}\,\leq\frac{c(x_{0})}{|y|^{d+2\sigma}},\quad\mbox{when }\;\;|y|\leq{\delta}

and

(2.15)

\int_{0}^{A}\Big|h_{s}(x_{0},x_{0}+y)-h_{s}(x_{0},x_{0}-y)\Big|\frac{ds}{s^{1+\sigma}}\,\leq\,\frac{c(x_{0})}{|y|^{(d+2\sigma-3)_{+}}},\quad\mbox{when }\;\;|y|\leq{\delta}.

Then, for every locally integrable

it holds

(2.16)

f\in\mathcal{D}_{\rm st}^{2\sigma}(x_{0})\quad\implies\quad x_{0}\in\mathcal{A}^{\rm loc}_{f}(L^{\sigma}).

Moreover, (
2.13

Before proving the result, recall the standard notation

f_{\rm even}(x)=\frac{f(x)+f(-x)}{2}{\quad\mbox{and}\quad}f_{\rm odd}(x)=\frac{f(x)-f(-x)}{2}.

We shall use the following elementary lemma.

Lemma 2.7
.

Let

(2.17)

\int_{B}F(x)G(x)\,dx=\int_{B}F_{\rm even}(x)G_{\rm even}(x)\,dx+\int_{B}F_{\rm odd}(x)G_{\rm odd}(x)\,dx.

\triangle^{1}_{z}f(x)=f(x+z)-f(x-z),{\quad\mbox{and}\quad}\triangle^{2}_{z}f(x)=f(x+z)+f(x-z)-2f(x).

Observe that, after dividing by 2, these expressions are respectively

Remark 2.8
.

When the heat kernel at a given
x_{0}
satisfies

h_{s}(x_{0},y)=\rho_{s,x_{0}}(|x_{0}-y|),

for some function
\rho_{s,x_{0}}
(for instance, if
h_{s}
is of convolution type and radial), then the condition (
2.15

2.18
) vanishes, so no bound is needed involving
\triangle_{z}^{1}f(x_{0})
. Thus, in that setting, the conclusion (
2.16
) of the proposition holds with the weaker smoothness assumption

3.
The Hermite operator
L=-{\Delta}+|x|^{2}+m
, with
m\geq-d

In this section we specialize to the case when

L=-{\Delta}+|x|^{2}+m,\quad\mbox{with $m\geq-d$.}

We recall the kernel expressions in this setting. For the heat kernel
h_{t}(x,y)
, associated with
e^{-tL}
, we have the Mehler formula

h_{t}(x,y)=\,e^{-tm}\,\frac{e^{-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{2{\operatorname{th}\,}{2t}}-{\operatorname{th}\,}{t}\,x\cdot y}}{[2\pi{\operatorname{sh}\,}{2t}]^{\frac{d}{2}}}\,,\quad t>0,\;x,y\in{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}};

see e.g.
[
16
, (4.3.14)]
. Changing variables to
t=t(s)=\frac{1}{2}\ln(\frac{1+s}{1-s})
(or equivalently,
s={\operatorname{th}\,}(t)
), the kernel takes the form

(3.1)

h_{t(s)}(x,y)=\,\frac{(1-s)^{\frac{m+d}{2}}}{(1+s)^{\frac{m-d}{2}}}\,\frac{e^{-\frac{1}{4}(\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{s}+s|x+y|^{2})}}{(4\pi s)^{\frac{d}{2}}}\,.

In the next subsection we shall collect the decay and smoothness estimates of this kernel that will be needed in the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

3.1.
Kernel estimates

Throughout this section we denote

(3.2)

\mathcal{K}(x,y):=\int_{0}^{A}h_{t}(x,y)\,\frac{dt}{t^{1+\sigma}},\quad x,y\in{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}},

where we select
A>0
so that
{\operatorname{th}\,}A=1/2
(any other
A>0
would also be fine).
Performing the change of variables in (
3.1
)
(so that
dt=\frac{ds}{1-s^{2}}
) we obtain

(3.3)

\mathcal{K}(x,y)=\int_{0}^{1/2}\,\frac{(1-s)^{\frac{m+d}{2}-1}\,e^{-\frac{1}{4}(\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{s}+s|x+y|^{2})}}{(1+s)^{\frac{m-d}{2}+1}\,(4\pi s)^{\frac{d}{2}}\,\big(\frac{1}{2}\ln\frac{1+s}{1-s}\big)^{1+\sigma}}\,ds.

Observe that in this range of integration we have
\ln\frac{1+s}{1-s}\approx s
, and
1\pm s\approx 1
,
so
\mathcal{K}(x,y)
becomes comparable to

(3.4)

\mathcal{K}_{1}(x,y)=\int_{0}^{1/2}\,\frac{e^{-\frac{1}{4}(\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{s}+s|x+y|^{2})}}{s^{1+\sigma+\frac{d}{2}}}\,ds.

The first lemma shows the decay condition in (
2.14
).
The argument in the proof is similar to the one used in
[
8
, (4.13)]

(where a better estimate is obtained).

Lemma 3.1
.

With the notation in (
3.3
), for every
\sigma>0
there exists
c=c(\sigma)>0
such that

\mathcal{K}(x,y)\leq\frac{c}{|x-y|^{d+2\sigma}},\quad\forall\;x,y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}.

Proof.

Changing variables
u=\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{4s}
in (
3.4
) we see that

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}(x,y)

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{1}(x,y)=\Big(\frac{4}{|x-y|^{2}}\Big)^{\sigma+\frac{d}{2}}\,\int_{\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{2}}^{\infty}e^{-u}\,e^{-\frac{|x+y|^{2}\,|x-y|^{2}}{16u}}\,u^{\sigma+\frac{d}{2}}\,\frac{du}{u}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{4^{\sigma+\frac{d}{2}}}{|x-y|^{d+2\sigma}}\,\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-u}\,u^{\sigma+\frac{d}{2}}\,\frac{du}{u}=\frac{c}{|x-y|^{d+2\sigma}}.

∎

Remark 3.2
.

This lemma may also be proved directly from (

) using the property

h_{t}(x,y)\lesssim t^{-d/2}e^{-c\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{t}},\quad 0<t\lesssim 1.

This property is known to hold for many other operators

We now show the smoothness condition in (

Lemma 3.3
.

For every
\sigma>0
, there exists

such that

(3.5)

\int_{0}^{A}|h_{t}(x,x+y)-h_{t}(x,x-y)|\,\frac{dt}{t^{1+\sigma}}\leq\,\frac{c\,|x|}{|y|^{(d+2\sigma-3)_{+}}},\quad\forall\;x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d},\;|y|\leq 1.

In the proof of (
3.5

Lemma 3.4
.

If

then

\Big|e^{-|x+y|^{2}}-e^{-|x-y|^{2}}\Big|\leq 4|x|\,|y|\;e^{-\min|x\pm y|^{2}}.

Proof.

Using
|x\pm y|^{2}=|x|^{2}+|y|^{2}\pm 2x\cdot y
we can write

\Big|e^{-|x+y|^{2}}-e^{-|x-y|^{2}}\Big|=e^{-|x|^{2}-|y|^{2}}\,\Big|e^{2x\cdot y}-e^{-2x\cdot y}\Big|.

Now, letting

, and using the inequality

e^{t}-e^{-t}=\int^{t}_{-t}e^{s}\,ds\leq 2te^{t},

we obtain

\Big|e^{-|x+y|^{2}}-e^{-|x-y|^{2}}\Big|\leq 4|x|\,|y|\,e^{2|x\cdot y|}\,e^{-|x|^{2}-|y|^{2}}=4|x|\,|y|\,e^{-\min|x\pm y|^{2}}.

∎

P
ROOF
of Lemma

Denote by

Then, performing the change of variables in (

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}^{\prime}(x,y)

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
\sigma>0

and

\mathcal{K}(x,y)\leq c\,e^{-(\frac{1}{2}+{\gamma})|y|^{2}},\quad\mbox{when $|y|\geq 10\,\max\{|x|,1\}$. }

Proof.

For simplicity denote

Given a small

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{-\frac{\eta b^{2}}{4s}}\,e^{-\frac{1}{4}(sa^{2}+(1-\eta)\frac{b^{2}}{s})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle e^{-\frac{\eta b^{2}}{4s}}\,e^{-\frac{1}{4}\,\big(\frac{9}{10}\big)^{2}\,|y|^{2}\,\big(s+(1-\eta)\frac{1}{s}\big)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle e^{-\frac{\eta b^{2}}{4s}}\,e^{-\frac{1}{4}\,\big(\frac{9}{10}\big)^{2}\,|y|^{2}\,(1-\eta)\,\frac{5}{2}},

using that

3.2.
Regular positive eigenvectors

Definition 3.6
.

We say that
\psi(x)\in\mathop{\rm Dom}(L)
is a
regular positive eigenvector
of
L
if

(a)

\psi\in C^{\infty}({{\mathbb{R}}^{d}})

(b)

\psi(x)>0
,
\forall\,x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}

(c)

L(\psi)={\lambda}\psi
, for some
{\lambda}\geq 0
.

When
L=-{\Delta}+|x|^{2}+m
, it is elementary to find an explicit regular positive eigenvector, namely

\psi(x)=e^{-|x|^{2}/2}.

Indeed, it is easily verified that
L(\psi)={\lambda}\psi
with
{\lambda}=m+d\geq 0
.

We have the following simple lemma, which is valid for general operators
L
.

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
\psi
be a regular positive eigenvector of
L
. Then, for all
\sigma\in(0,1)

and all
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
it holds

\int_{0}^{\infty}\Big|e^{-tL}\psi(x)-\psi(x)\Big|\,\frac{dt}{t^{1+\sigma}}\,<\,\infty.

That is,
x\in\mathcal{A}_{\psi}(L^{\sigma})
, for all
x\in{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}
.

Proof.

Since
e^{-tL}\psi=e^{-t{\lambda}}\psi
,
the result is clear if
{\lambda}=0
. If
{\lambda}>0
, then
we have

(3.6)

\int_{0}^{\infty}\Big|e^{-tL}\psi(x)-\psi(x)\Big|\,\frac{dt}{t^{1+\sigma}}=\psi(x)\,\int_{0}^{\infty}|e^{-t{\lambda}}-1|\,\frac{dt}{t^{1+\sigma}}.

Now, from the elementary estimate

|e^{-t{\lambda}}-1|=\Big|\int_{0}^{t}\,{\lambda}\,e^{-s{\lambda}}\,ds\Big|\leq\min\{{\lambda}\,t,2\}.

one deduces that (
3.6
) is a finite expression when
\sigma\in(0,1)
.
∎

Remark 3.8
.

In this paper we shall not pursue this notion with other operators
L
, but it is well-known
that such eigenvectors exist when
L=-{\Delta}+V(x)
, under very general conditions on

; see e.g.
[

5.5
) we obtain that

\mathcal{K}(x,y)=\,c\,\frac{F_{\sigma+d/2}\big(\sqrt{R}\,|x-y|\big)}{|x-y|^{2\sigma+d}}\,\lesssim\,\frac{\big(1+\sqrt{R}\,|x-y|\big)^{\sigma+\frac{d-1}{2}}}{|x-y|^{2\sigma+d}}\,e^{-\sqrt{R}\,|x-y|}.

If we now assume that
|y|\geq 2\max\{|x|,1\}
, the right side is easily seen to be controlled by

c(x)\,\Phi^{\mathbb{L}}_{\sigma}(y)
; see e.g.
[
8
, (5.6)]
and subsequent lines for a detailed argument.
∎

We can now state the corresponding theorem for the operator
{\mathbb{L}}=-{\Delta}+R
.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
{\mathbb{L}}=-{\Delta}+R
with
R>0
fixed. Let
\sigma\in(0,1)
and
\Phi^{\mathbb{L}}_{\sigma}(y)
be as in (
5.3
).
Suppose that

f\in L_{1}(\Phi^{\mathbb{L}}_{\sigma}){\quad\mbox{and}\quad}f\in\mathcal{D}^{2\sigma}(x_{0})\quad\mbox{at some $x_{0}\in{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}$}.

Then
(-{\Delta}+R)^{\sigma}(x_{0})
is well defined in the limiting sense of (
2.10
), and as the absolutely convergent integral in (
2.9
), and both definitions agree.

Proof.

By Proposition
2.3
we must show that
x=x_{0}\in\mathcal{A}_{f}({\mathbb{L}}^{\sigma})
.
Observe that
\psi(y)\equiv 1
is a regular positive eigenvector for
{\mathbb{L}}
, according to Definition
3.6
.
So, by Lemma
3.7
and the inequalities following (
3.8
) (applied with
A=\infty
), if suffices to show that

\displaystyle J_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\Big|\big(e^{-t{\mathbb{L}}}f\big)(x)-f(x)\big(e^{-t{\mathbb{L}}}\psi\big)(x)\Big|\,\frac{dt}{t^{1+\sigma}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\Big|\int_{{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}}h_{t}(x,y)\big[f(y)-f(x)\big]\,dy\Big|\,\frac{dt}{t^{1+\sigma}}<\infty.

We let
{\delta}=3\max\{|x|,1\}
, and as before, split the inner integral into the regions

\{|y-x|<{\delta}\}
and
\{|y-x|\geq{\delta}\}
. So, using the notation for
\mathcal{I}_{\delta}f(x,t)
in (
2.12
) we have

J_{1}\,\leq\,\int_{0}^{\infty}\big|\mathcal{I}_{\delta}f(x,t)\big|\,\frac{dt}{t^{1+\sigma}}\,+\,J_{1}^{*}

where

\displaystyle J_{1}^{*}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{|y-x|\geq{\delta}}h_{t}(x,y)\big|f(y)-f(x)\big|\,dy\,\tfrac{dt}{t^{1+\sigma}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{|y|\geq\max\{|x|,1\}}\,\big|f(y)-f(x)\big|\,\mathcal{K}(x,y)\,dy,

with
\mathcal{K}(x,y)
as in (
5.4
). By Lemma
5.1
,

J^{*}_{1}\,\leq\,c(x)\,\int_{{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}}\big(|f(x)|+|f(y)|\big)\,\Phi^{\mathbb{L}}_{\sigma}(y)\,dy,

which is a finite expression. So we have reduced matters to show that

\int_{0}^{\infty}\big|\mathcal{I}_{\delta}f(x,t)\big|\,\frac{dt}{t^{1+\sigma}}<\infty.

But under the smoothness assumption that
f\in\mathcal{D}^{2\sigma}(x)
, this is a consequence of Proposition
2.6
(setting
A=\infty
), since the kernel

h_{t}(x,y)
trivially satisfies (
2.14
) (by Remark
3.2
) and (
2.15
) (whose left hand side is identically 0; see Remark
2.8
). Finally observe, also by Remark
2.8
, that only the weaker smoothness condition
f\in\mathcal{D}^{2\sigma}(x)
is used, due to the convolution structure of the kernel
h_{t}(x,y)
.
∎

6.
Smoothness conditions

In this section we give some examples to illustrate the smoothness conditions
from §1.
Recall that, for
{\alpha}\in(0,2)
, a locally integrable function
f\in\mathcal{D}^{{\alpha}}(x_{0})
if

\int_{|h|\leq{\delta}}\frac{|f(x_{0}+h)+f(x_{0}-h)-2f(x_{0})|}{|h|^{d+{\alpha}}}\,dh\,<\,\infty,

for some
{\delta}>0
(hence for all
{\delta}>0
).
Also,
f\in\mathcal{D}_{\rm st}^{{\alpha}}(x_{0})
if

f\in\mathcal{D}^{{\alpha}}(x_{0}){\quad\mbox{and}\quad}\int_{|h|\leq{\delta}}\frac{|f(x_{0}+h)-f(x_{0}-h)|}{|h|^{d+{\alpha}-3}}\,dh\,<\,\infty.

Observe that if
f
is bounded near
x_{0}
, this last condition is redundant, so
{\alpha}
-smooth and strictly
{\alpha}
-smooth agree in this case.
The two classes also coincide if
d+{\alpha}-3\leq 0
, that is

(6.1)

\mathcal{D}^{{\alpha}}(x_{0})=\mathcal{D}_{\rm st}^{{\alpha}}(x_{0}),\quad\mbox{if $d=1$, or $d=2$ and ${\alpha}\in(0,1]$.}

In other cases, the classes are different, as shown by the example in (
6.4
) below.
Finally, note that strict
{\alpha}
-smoothness can also be characterized as follows.

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
{\alpha}\in(0,2)
. Then
f\in\mathcal{D}_{\rm st}^{{\alpha}}(x_{0})
if and only if

(6.2)

f\in\mathcal{D}^{{\alpha}}(x_{0}){\quad\mbox{and}\quad}\int_{|h|\leq{\delta}}\frac{|f(x_{0}+h)-f(x_{0})|}{|h|^{d+{\alpha}-3}}\,dh\,<\,\infty.

Proof.

The implication “
\Leftarrow
” is obvious since

|f(x_{0}+h)-f(x_{0}-h)|\leq|f(x_{0}+h)-f(x_{0})|+|f(x_{0}-h)-f(x_{0})|.

For the converse implication “
\Rightarrow
” note that

2\big(f(x_{0}+h)-f(x_{0})\big)=\Big[f(x_{0}+h)-f(x_{0}-h)\Big]+\Big[f(x_{0}+h)+f(x_{0}-h)-2f(x_{0})\Big].

∎

We next collect a few further elementary observations.

(1)

If
f
is odd about
x_{0}
and
f(x_{0})=0
, then
f\in\mathcal{D}^{{\alpha}}(x_{0})
, for all
{\alpha}\in(0,2)
. For instance, if
{\gamma}\in[0,d)
then

(6.3)

f(x)=\mathop{\rm sign\,}(x\cdot\mathbf{e}_{1})/|x|^{\gamma}\quad\mbox{if $x\not=0$},\quad f(0)=0,

belongs to
\mathcal{D}^{{\alpha}}(0)
for all
0<{\alpha}<2
, even though it is discontinuous there. However, by (
6.2
),
f\in\mathcal{D}_{\rm st}^{{\alpha}}(0)
only if
additionally
{\alpha}\in(0,3-{\gamma})
. In particular, the function

(6.4)

g(x)=\frac{\mathop{\rm sign\,}((x-x_{0})\cdot\mathbf{e}_{1})}{|x-x_{0}|^{3-{\alpha}}}\quad\mbox{if $x\not=x_{0}$},\quad g(x_{0})=0,

which is locally integrable if
d+{\alpha}-3>0
, shows that
\mathcal{D}_{\rm st}^{{\alpha}}(x_{0})\subsetneq\mathcal{D}^{{\alpha}}(x_{0})
in the complementary range of (
6.1
).

(2)

There exists a function
f\in\mathcal{D}_{\rm st}^{{\alpha}}(x_{0})
, for all
{\alpha}\in(0,2)
, but which is discontinuous and unbounded at
x_{0}
. Indeed,
if
d\geq 2
(and
x_{0}=0
), the function
f
defined in (
6.3
) with parameter
{\gamma}=1
has this property.
If
d=1
, one may take any (locally integrable) odd function unbounded near
x_{0}
.

(3)

If
f\in{\rm Lip}_{\beta}(x_{0})
for some
\beta\in(0,2]
then
f\in\mathcal{D}_{\rm st}^{{\alpha}}(x_{0})
for all
{\alpha}<\beta
. Here,
f\in{\rm Lip}_{\beta}(x_{0})
, if
\beta\in(0,1]
, means that

|f(x_{0}+h)-f(x_{0})|\leq c\,|h|^{\beta},\quad\forall\,|h|\leq{\delta},

for some
c,{\delta}>0
.
If
\beta\in(1,2]
, it means that
f
is differentiable at
x_{0}
and

\big|f(x_{0}+h)-f(x_{0})-\nabla f(x_{0})\cdot h\big|\leq c\,|h|^{\beta},\quad\forall\,|h|\leq{\delta}.

Indeed, in either case it is clear that
f\in{\rm Lip}_{\beta}(x_{0})
implies

|\triangle_{h}^{2}f(x_{0})|=\big|f(x_{0}+h)-2f(x_{0})+f(x_{0}-h)\big|\,\leq\,2c\,|h|^{\beta},

and

|\triangle_{h}^{1}f(x_{0})|=\big|f(x_{0}+h)-f(x_{0}-h)\big|\,\leq\,c^{\prime}\,|h|^{\min\{\beta,1\}},

which in turn implies
f\in\mathcal{D}_{\rm st}^{{\alpha}}(x_{0})
, for all
{\alpha}<\beta
.

(4)

The following examples relate
{\rm Lip}_{\beta}(x_{0})
and
\mathcal{D}^{{\alpha}}(x_{0})
when
\beta={\alpha}
:

\displaystyle f(x)=|x-x_{0}|^{\alpha}\in{\rm Lip}_{\alpha}(x_{0})\setminus\mathcal{D}^{{\alpha}}(x_{0}),\quad\forall\,{\alpha}\in(0,2)

\displaystyle g(x)=\mathop{\rm sign\,}\big((x-x_{0})\cdot\mathbf{e}_{1}\big)\,|x-x_{0}|^{\alpha}\in{\rm Lip}_{\alpha}(x_{0})\cap\mathcal{D}_{\rm st}^{{\alpha}}(x_{0}),\quad\forall\;{\alpha}\in(0,2).

(5)

We mention an example relating the above smoothness conditions at a point
x_{0}
and the existence
of
(-{\Delta})^{\frac{{\alpha}}{2}}f(x_{0})
, as defined in (
1.3
). Consider the two functions
f,g
, defined as in point (
4
) above but
additionally multiplied by a smooth cut-off
{\varphi}(|x-x_{0}|)
, where
{\varphi}\in C^{\infty}_{c}({\mathbb{R}})
with
{\varphi}\equiv 1
in
[-1,1]
.
Then, it is easily seen that

(-{\Delta})^{\frac{{\alpha}}{2}}f(x_{0})=-\infty\quad\mbox{but}\quad(-{\Delta})^{\frac{{\alpha}}{2}}g(x_{0})=0.

So in general,
f\in{\rm Lip}_{{\alpha}}(x_{0})
is not enough to define pointwise fractional powers,
L^{\frac{{\alpha}}{2}}f(x_{0})
,
justifying the search for a different condition such as
f\in\mathcal{D}^{{\alpha}}(x_{0})
or
f\in\mathcal{D}_{\rm st}^{{\alpha}}(x_{0})
.

(6)

When
L
is the Hermite operator in (
1.7
), one can show that the function
g(x)
defined in (
6.4
),
when additionally multiplied by a smooth cut-off
{\varphi}(|x-x_{0}|)
supported near the point
x_{0}=\mathbf{e}_{1}
, has the property

L^{\frac{{\alpha}}{2}}g(x_{0})=\infty.

Thus, when
x_{0}\not=0
, in Theorem
1.1
one cannot replace the assumption
f\in\mathcal{D}_{\rm st}^{2\sigma}(x_{0})
by the weaker condition
f\in\mathcal{D}^{2\sigma}(x_{0})

(except of course when the two classes coincide; see (
6.1
)). This example also shows that there are compactly supported
g
such that

(-{\Delta})^{\frac{{\alpha}}{2}}g(x_{0})=0\quad\mbox{but}\quad L^{\frac{{\alpha}}{2}}g(x_{0})=\infty

(the latter in the sense of Definition
2.2
).

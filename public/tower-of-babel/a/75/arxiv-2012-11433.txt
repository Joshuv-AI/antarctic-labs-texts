On the MMP for rank one foliations on threefolds
Paolo Cascini and Calum Spicer

Abstract
. We prove existence of flips and the base point free theorem for log canonical foliated pairs of rank one on a ℚ \mathbb{Q} -factorial projective klt threefold. This, in particular, provides a proof of the existence of a minimal model for a rank one foliation on a threefold for a wider range of singularities, after McQuillan. Moreover, we show abundance in the case of numerically trivial log canonical foliated pairs of rank one.

14E30, 37F75

1.
Introduction

As in the classical Minimal Model Program, it is expected that every foliation on a complex projective manifold is either uniruled or
it admits a minimal model, i.e. a good representative in its birational class. For rank one foliations on a complex surface, this is known to
be true thanks to the work of Brunella, McQuillan and Mendes (e.g. see
[
Bru00
,
McQ08
,
Men00
]
).
For foliations of rank two on a threefold, the program was carried out in
[
Spi20
,
CS18
,
SS19
]
.

In
[
McQ04
]
, McQuillan proved the
existence of minimal models
for foliations by curves. More specifically, he showed that if

X
is a projective variety with quotient singularities
and
\cal F
is a rank one foliation on
X
with log canonical singularities, then
\cal F
admits a minimal model.

The goal of this paper is to explore this result in the case of rank one foliations
on threefolds. In particular, we are interested in proving a generalisation
of McQuillan’s theorem, exploring some applications of the theorem, such as the base point free theorem, the study
of foliations with trivial canonical class, and understanding the
relationship between the birational geometry of foliations and
classical birational geometry.

1.1.
Statement of main results

Our first main result is to show that flips exist for log canonical foliated pairs of rank one on a
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial threefold with klt singularities:

Theorem 1.1

(= Theorem
8.7
)
.

Let
X
be a
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial klt threefold and let
(\cal F,\Delta)
be a rank one foliated pair on
X
with log canonical singularities.
Let
R
be a
(K_{\cal F}+\Delta)
-negative extremal ray such that

\operatorname{loc}\,R
has dimension one (cf. Section
2.13
).

Then the flipping contraction
\phi\colon X\to Z
associated to
R
and the
(K_{\cal F}+\Delta)
-flip exist.

The theorem in particular implies that the foliated MMP can be run with
natural assumptions on the singularities of the underlying variety,
as well as allowing for the presence of a boundary divisor:

Theorem 1.2

(= Theorem
8.8
)
.

Let
X
be a
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial
projective threefold with klt singularities and let
(\cal F,\Delta)
be a log canonical foliated pair of rank one on
X
.
Assume that
K_{\cal F}+\Delta
is pseudo-effective.

Then
(\cal F,\Delta)
admits a minimal model.

Our ideas and proofs are greatly indebted to McQuillan’s strategies and insights, however ultimately our approach
to the existence of minimal models of foliations is independent from the proof given in

[
McQ04
]
and is based on techniques from the existence of minimal models
in the case of varieties.

Similarly to the case of foliation of rank two on a threefold
[
CS18
, Theorem 1.3]
, we show that existence of flips and divisorial contractions yields a base point free theorem:

Theorem 1.3

(=Theorem
9.2
)
.

Let
X
be a normal projective threefold with
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial klt singularities
and let
(\cal F,\Delta)
be a rank one foliated pair on
X
with log canonical singularities. Assume that
\Delta=A+B
where
A
is an ample
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor and
B\geq 0
is a
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor.
Suppose that
K_{\cal F}+\Delta
is nef.

Then
K_{\cal F}+\Delta
is semi-ample.

Our next main result is a special case of foliation abundance:

Theorem 1.4

(= Theorem
10.1
)
.

Let
X
be a normal projective threefold and let
(\cal F,\Delta)

be a log canonical foliated pair.
Suppose that
K_{\cal F}+\Delta\equiv 0
.

Then
K_{\cal F}+\Delta\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}0
.

In particular, it follows that after a quasi-étale cover that such a foliation
can be defined by a global vector field.

A very satisfying structure theorem is known for foliations of co-rank one with
trivial canonical class (see
[
Tou08
,
LPT18
,
Dru18
,
Dru19
,
DO19
]
).
We hope that this theorem provides a first step to understanding such a structure
theorem for foliations of larger co-rank.

Finally, we prove several results which relate the birational
geometry of foliations to classical birational geometry.
For instance, it is a striking feature of the canonical model
theorem for foliation by curves on surfaces that
the singularities on the underlying surface of the canonical
model are never worse than log canonical
(see

[
McQ08
, Fact I.2.4 and Theorem III.3.2]
).

We were interested if such a bound could be proven on threefolds
without making recourse to a canonical model theorem for foliations
on threefolds, which to our knowledge is unknown.
In this direction we prove the following:

Theorem 1.5

(=Theorem
4.5
)
.

Let
X
be a normal threefold and let
\cal F
be a rank one foliation on
X
with canonical singularities. Let
0\in X
be an isolated
singularity.

Then
X
has log canonical singularities.

Simple examples show that this result is close to optimal in the sense
that if
0\in X
is not an isolated singularity then there is in general
no such bound on the singularities of
X
(see Example
4.6
).

1.2.
Sketch of the proof

We briefly explain our approach to the proof of existence of flips.
Let
X
be a
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial projective threefold with klt singularities and let
\cal F
be a foliation with canonical singularities on
X
.
Let
f\colon X\rightarrow Z
be a
K_{\cal F}
-negative flipping contraction which contracts
a single curve
C
. We first note that
C
is necessarily
\cal F
-invariant.

Our basic approach is to reduce the
K_{\cal F}
-flip to a
(K_{X}+D)
-flip
for some well chosen divisor
D
. If
D
is an arbitrary divisor
then there is no reason to expect any relation between
\cal F

and the pair
(X,D)
. However, if every component of
D

is invariant then much of the geometry
of
(X,D)
is controlled by
\cal F
.

In particular, in Section
4

we show that if
(X,D)
is log canonical and
C
is a log canonical centre
of
(X,D)
then
(K_{X}+D)\cdot C<0
.
Thus, the challenge in producing the
K_{\cal F}
-flip becomes to produce
a large number of
\cal F
-invariant divisors containing
C
. These divisors
give us the flexibility to produce a divisor
D
with the desired properties.
This is achieved in Section
5
.
The idea is to perform a careful analysis of
the singularities of the induced foliation
f_{*}\cal F
on
Z
at
f(C)
. Unfortunately,
as in the classical MMP, the divisor
K_{f_{*}\cal F}
is not

\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier and so working directly with
f_{*}\cal F
is very difficult. Rather,
we demonstrate the existence of an auxiliary divisor
E
on
Z
, which is a foliated version of a
complement in the classical MMP and such that
K_{f_{*}\cal F}+E
is
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier
and the pair
(f_{*}\cal F,E)
has mild singularities. An analysis
of the pair is much more feasible and in fact we are able to
show that
(f_{*}\cal F,E)
admits a particularly simple normal form which, roughly,
can be given by a vector field of the form
\sum n_{i}x_{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}

where the
n_{i}
are non-negative integers. Examining this normal form, we are able
to produce a large number of invariant divisors containing
C
.

It is worth spending a moment to compare this with McQuillan’s approach to the existence of a flip.
In dimension three, it is possible to show
that
C\cap\operatorname{Sing}\,\cal F
consists of a single point
P

and that if
\partial
is a vector field defining
\cal F
near
P

then
\partial=-t\frac{\partial}{\partial t}+ax\frac{\partial}{\partial x}+by\frac{\partial}{\partial y}

where
C=\{x=y=0\}
and
a,b
are positive integers. From this, it is possible to deduce
that the normal bundle of
C
splits as
\cal O(-a)\oplus\cal O(-b)
. By an inductive
analysis
of
\cal F
along
C
, we can lift this splitting of the normal bundle to a splitting
on a formal neighbourhood of
C
in
X
, i.e.,
C
is a complete intersection of two formal divisors.
With this description of the formal neighbourhood of
C
in hand, it is easy to construct the flip
by an explicit procedure consisting of a single weighted blow up followed by a single weighted blow down.

2.
Preliminary Results

2.1.
Notations

We work over the field of complex numbers
\mathbb{C}
.

Given a normal variety
X
, we denote by
\Omega^{1}_{X}
its sheaf of Kähler differentials and,
by
T_{X}:=(\Omega^{1}_{X})^{*}
its tangent sheaf.
For any positive integer
p
, we denote
\Omega_{X}^{[p]}\coloneqq(\Omega_{X}^{p})^{**}
.
Let
A
be a
\mathbb{R}
-Weil divisor on
X
and let
D
be a prime divisor.
We denote by
\mu_{D}A
the coefficient of
D
in
A
.
A log pair
(X,\Delta)
is a pair of a normal variety and a
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor
\Delta
such that
K_{X}+\Delta
is
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier.
We refer to
[
KM98
]
for the classical definitions of singularities (e.g., klt, log
canonical) appearing in the minimal model program, except for the fact that in our
definitions we require the pairs to have effective boundaries. In addition, we say that a log pair
(X,\Delta)
is
sub log canonical
, or sub lc, if
a(E,X,\Delta)\geq-1
for any geometric valuation
E

over
X
.
A
fibration

f\colon X\to Y
is a surjective morphism between normal varieties with connected fibres.
We refer to
[
CS18
, Section 2.5]
for some of the basic notions, commonly used in the MMP.

A
foliation of rank
r
on a normal variety
X
is a rank
r
coherent subsheaf
\cal F\subset T_{X}
such that

(1)

{\cal F}
is saturated in
T_{X}
, and

(2)

\cal F
is closed under Lie bracket.

Note that if
r=1
then (2) is automatically satisfied. By (1), it follows that
T_{X}/{\mathcal{F}}
is torsion free.
We denote by
\mathcal{N}^{*}_{\cal F}=(T_{X}/{\mathcal{F}})^{*}
the
conormal sheaf
of
\cal F
. The
normal sheaf

\cal N_{F}
of
\cal F
is the dual of the conormal sheaf.
The
canonical divisor
of
\cal F
is a divisor
K_{\cal F}
such that
\mathcal{O}_{X}(-K_{\cal F})\simeq\det T_{\mathcal{F}}
. The foliation
\mathcal{F}
is said to be
Gorenstein
(resp.
\mathbb{Q}
-Gorenstein) if
K_{\mathcal{F}}
is a Cartier (resp.
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier) divisor.
More generally, a
rank
r
foliated pair

(\cal F,\Delta)
is a pair of a foliation
\cal F
of rank
r
and a
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor
\Delta\geq 0

such that
K_{\cal F}+\Delta
is
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier.

Let
X
be a normal variety and let
\cal F
be a rank
r
foliation
on
X
. We can associate to
\cal F
a morphism

\phi\colon\Omega^{[r]}_{X}\rightarrow\cal O_{X}(K_{\cal F})

defined by taking the double dual of the
r
-wedge product of the map
\Omega^{[1]}_{X}\to\cal F^{*}
, induced by the inclusion

\cal F\subset T_{X}
. We will call
\phi
the
Pfaff field
associated to
\cal F
.
Following
[
Dru18
, Definition 5.4]
, we define the twisted Pfaff field
as the induced map

\phi^{\prime}\colon(\Omega^{[r]}_{X}\otimes\cal O_{X}(-K_{\cal F}))^{**}\rightarrow\cal O_{X}

and we define the
singular locus
of
\cal F
, denoted by
\operatorname{Sing}\,\cal F
, to be the cosupport of the image of
\phi^{\prime}
.

Let
\sigma\colon Y\dashrightarrow X
be a dominant map between normal varieties and let
\cal F
be a foliation of rank
r
on
X
. We denote by
\sigma^{-1}\cal F
the
induced foliation
on
Y
(e.g. see
[
Dru18
, Section 3.2]
).
If
\sigma\colon Y\to X
is a birational morphism then the induced foliation
\sigma^{-1}\cal F
is called the
pulled back foliation
. If
f\colon X\dashrightarrow X^{\prime}
is a birational map, then the induced foliation on
X^{\prime}
by
f^{-1}
is called the
transformed foliation
of
\cal F
by
f
and we will denote it by
f_{*}\cal F
.
Moreover, if
q\colon X^{\prime}\rightarrow X
is a quasi-étale cover
and
\cal F^{\prime}=q^{-1}\cal F
then
K_{\cal F^{\prime}}=q^{*}K_{\cal F}
and

[
Dru18
, Proposition 5.13]
implies that
\cal F^{\prime}
is non-singular if and only if

\cal F
is.

2.2.
Invariant subvarieties

Let
X
be a normal variety and let
\cal F
be a rank
r
foliation on
X
.
Let
S\subset X
be a subvariety. Then
S
is said to be
\cal F
-invariant
, or
invariant by
\cal F
, if for any open subset
U\subset X
and any section
\partial\in H^{0}(U,\cal F)
, we have that

\partial(\mathcal{I}_{S\cap U})\subset\mathcal{I}_{S\cap U},

where
\mathcal{I}_{S\cap U}
denotes the ideal sheaf of
S\cap U
.
If
D\subset X
is a prime divisor then we define
\epsilon(D)=1
if
D
is not
\cal F
-invariant and
\epsilon(D)=0
if it is
\cal F
-invariant.

Assume now that
\mathcal{F}
is a foliation of rank one on an affine variety
X=\text{Spec}\,A
such that
K_{\cal F}
is Cartier. In this case,
\mathcal{F}
is defined by a vector field
\partial
. Let
S
be a subvariety defined by the ideal
I\subset A
.
Then
S
is
\cal F
-invariant if and only if
I
is
\partial
-invariant.

We will occasionally need to consider a logarithmic version
of invariance.
Let
X
be a normal variety and let

(\cal F,D)
be a rank one foliated pair on
X
, where
D
is an integral divisor.
We say that a subvariety
S\subset X
is
(\cal F,D)
-invariant
provided
the following holds.
Let
q\colon V\to X
be a quasi-étale cover where
U:=q(V)
is an open set intersecting
S
and such that

\cal L:=q^{[*]}\mathcal{O}_{X}(K_{\cal F}+D)
is locally free. Let
T=q^{-1}(S)
,
\cal F_{V}=q^{-1}\cal F
and let
D^{\prime}\geq 0
be the integral divisor
on
V
such that
K_{\cal F_{V}}+D^{\prime}=q^{*}(K_{\cal F}+D)
.
Then there exists a vector field

\partial\in H^{0}(V,{\cal F_{V}}(-D^{\prime}))
which generates
{\cal F_{V}}(-D^{\prime})
and
T
is
\partial
-invariant.

For smooth Gorenstein foliations, we have the following generalisation of Frobenius theorem:

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
X
be an
n
-dimensional normal
analytic variety and let
\cal F
be a rank one foliation on
X
. Let
P\in X
be a closed point around which,
\cal F
is Gorenstein and smooth.

Then there exists an analytic neigbourhood
U
of
P
,
a normal analytic variety
B
of dimension
n-1
and a holomorphic submersion
f\colon U\rightarrow B

such that the
\cal F
is the foliation induced by
f
.

Proof.

Since
\cal F
is Gorenstein, there exists an open neighbourhood
U
of
P
over which
\cal F
is generated by a vector field
\partial
.
Consider an embedding
U\hookrightarrow M
where
M
is a manifold and let
\partial_{M}
be a lift
of
\partial
to
M
. Denote by
\cal F_{M}
the lifted foliation.
After possibly shrinking
M
, we may assume that
\cal F_{M}
is also smooth.
Thus, the Frobenius theorem implies that
there exists a submersion
F\colon M\rightarrow N
such that
\cal F_{M}
is the foliation induced by
F
.
This gives us a submersion
f:=F|_{U}\rightarrow B
where
B
is the normalisation of
f(U)

and the claim follows.
∎

We will also need the following version of Riemann-Hurwitz formula for foliations (e.g. see
[
Dru18
, Lemma 3.4]
):

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
\sigma\colon Y\rightarrow X
be a finite surjective morphism between normal varieties, let
\cal F
be a foliation
on
X
and let
\cal G=\sigma^{-1}\cal F
.

Then we may write

K_{\cal G}=\sigma^{*}K_{\cal F}+\sum\epsilon(\sigma(D))(r_{D}-1)D

where the sum runs over all the prime divisors on
Y
and
r_{D}
is the ramification index of
\sigma
along
D
.
In particular, if every ramified divisor is
\cal G
-invariant then
K_{\cal G}=\sigma^{*}K_{\cal F}
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
X=\text{Spec}\,A
be an affine variety and let
\partial\in H^{0}(X,T_{X})
.
Let
I\subset A
be a
\partial
-invariant ideal such that its radical
\text{rad}\,I

is prime.

Then
\text{rad}\,I
is
\partial
-invariant.

Proof.

First, note that we may replace
A
by its localisation at
\text{rad}\,I
.
Suppose for sake
of contradiction that
\text{rad}\,I
is not
\partial
-invariant. Choose

f\in\text{rad}\,I

such that
\partial f\notin\text{rad}\,I
, i.e.,
\partial f
is a unit.
Let
n
be minimal positive integer so that
f^{n}\in I
.
Then
nf^{n-1}\partial f=\partial(f^{n})\in I
. However,
since
\partial f
is a unit,
it follows that

f^{n-1}\in I
, a contradiction.
∎

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
\sigma\colon Y\rightarrow X
be a finite surjective morphism between normal varieties, let
\cal F
be
a foliation of rank one on
X
and let
\cal G\coloneqq\sigma^{-1}\cal F
.
Let
D\geq 0
be an integral divisor on
X
such that
K_{\cal F}+D
is
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier
and

K_{\cal G}+E=\sigma^{*}(K_{\cal F}+D)

where
E\geq 0
is integral.
Let
W\subset X
be a subvariety and let
Z=\sigma^{-1}(W)
.

Then
W
is
(\cal F,D)
-invariant
if and only if
Z
is
(\cal G,E)
-invariant.

Proof.

Without loss of generality we may assume that
X=\text{Spec}\,A
and
Y=\text{Spec}\,B
are affine and
W
and
Z
are irreducible.
Note that we have an inclusion
A\subset B
.

Let
q\colon Y^{\prime}\rightarrow Y
be a quasi-étale cover such that
q^{*}(K_{\cal G}+E)
is Cartier and let
\cal G^{\prime}\coloneqq q^{-1}\cal G
and
E^{\prime}\coloneqq q^{-1}(E)
.
By definition, a subvariety is
(\cal G,E)
-invariant
if and only if its preimage in
Y^{\prime}
is
(\cal G^{\prime},E^{\prime})
-invariant. Thus, we may freely replace
Y
,
\cal G
and
E
by

Y^{\prime}
,
\cal G^{\prime}
and
E^{\prime}
respectively and so we may assume that
K_{\cal G}+E
is Cartier.

We first handle the case where
K_{\cal F}+D
is Cartier, in which case
we have vector fields
\partial_{X}\in{\cal F}(-D)
and
\partial_{Y}\in{\cal G}(-E)

such that
\sigma^{*}\partial_{X}=\partial_{Y}
.

We first show that
Z
being
\partial_{Y}
-invariant implies that
W
is
\partial_{X}
-invariant.
Let
I\subset B
be the ideal of
Z
and let
J\subset A
be the ideal
of

.
We have

\partial_{X}(J)=\partial_{X}(I\cap A)\subset\partial_{Y}(I)\cap A\subset I\cap A=J,

as required.
For the opposite implication, note that
J\otimes B\subset I
is
\partial_{X}
-invariant and that

\text{rad}\,(J\otimes B)=I
.
We may apply Lemma
2.3
to conclude.

Now we handle the general case. Let
p\colon X^{\prime}\rightarrow X
be a quasi-étale morphism such that

is Cartier
and let

Y^{\prime}
be the normalization of the main component of

, let

and

be the induced morphisms.
Applying our previous arguments to the maps

and

, we may conclude.
∎

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
X
be a normal variety and let
(\cal F,D)
be a rank one foliated pair on
X
where
D
is an integral divisor. Let
p\colon Y\rightarrow X
be a proper
morphism
and assume that

K_{\cal G}+E=p^{*}(K_{\cal F}+D)

where
\cal G=p^{-1}\cal F
and
E\geq 0
is integral.

Then the following hold:

(1)

If
W\subset Y
is a
(\cal G,E)
-invariant subvariety then
p(W)
is
(\cal F,D)
-invariant.

(2)

If
Z\subset X
is a
(\cal F,D)
-invariant subvariety then
p^{-1}(Z)

is
(\cal G,E)
-invariant.

Proof.

Observe that this is a local problem on
X
, so without loss of generality we may
restrict to a neighborhood of
P\in X
which admits a quasi-étale cover

q\colon X^{\prime}\rightarrow X
such that
q^{*}(K_{\cal F}+D)
is Cartier
and write
K_{\cal F^{\prime}}+D^{\prime}=q^{*}(K_{\cal F}+D)
.
Let
Y^{\prime}
be the normalization of the main component of
Y\times_{X}X^{\prime}
, let
r\colon Y^{\prime}\rightarrow Y
be the
induced map and observe that we may write

K_{\cal G^{\prime}}+E^{\prime}=r^{*}(K_{\cal G}+E)
where
E^{\prime}\geq 0
is integral.

By applying
Lemma
2.4

to
q
and
r
we see that we may assume without loss of generality that

K_{\cal F}+D
and
K_{\cal G}+E
are Cartier.

Let
Y\xrightarrow{\alpha}W\xrightarrow{\beta}X
be the Stein factorisation
of
p
. Since
\beta
is finite we may again apply Lemma
2.4
to
replace
X
by
W
. Thus, without loss of generality, we may assume that

p_{*}\cal O_{Y}=\cal O_{X}
.

We have that
T_{\cal F}(-D)
is generated by a vector field
\partial

which lifts to a vector field
\tilde{\partial}
on
Y
which generates
T_{\cal G}(-E)
.

We first prove (1). Let
J
denote the ideal of
p(W)
and let
I
denote the ideal sheaf of
W
. By abusing notation slightly, we have that
p_{*}I=J
.
Let
f\in J
and notice that
p^{*}\partial f=\tilde{\partial}(p^{*}f)
.
Since
W
is
(\cal G,E)
-invariant and
p
is proper, we have

p^{*}\partial f=\tilde{\partial}(p^{*}f)\in H^{0}(Y,I)=J.

Thus,
J
is
\partial
-invariant and (1) follows.

We now prove (2). Let
I
denote the ideal sheaf of
Z
and let

f_{1},...,f_{k}
be generators of
I
. Then
p^{*}f_{1},\dots,p^{*}f_{k}
are generators of
p^{-1}I\cal O_{Y}
, the ideal sheaf
of
p^{-1}(Z)
.
Since
\partial(f_{i})\in I
we get that

\tilde{\partial}(p^{*}f_{i})=p^{*}\partial f_{i}\in p^{-1}I\cal O_{Y}

We now explain some generalities comparing foliation adjunction and classical adjunction on a threefold:

Let

\cal F

For divisors which are transverse to the foliation, we have the following version of adjunction:

Proposition 2.19
.

Let
X
be a normal threefold and let

\cal F
be a rank one
\mathbb{Q}
-Gorenstein foliation on

. Let

be a
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier prime divisor which is not
\cal F
-invariant and let

be its normalisation.

Then there exists a
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor

such that

(K_{\cal F}+S)|_{S^{\nu}}\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}\Delta.

Proof.

Consider the composition of the natural maps

\mathcal{O}_{S}(-S)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\phi}}{{\to}}\Omega^{[1]}_{X}|_{S}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\psi}}{{\to}}\mathcal{O}_{S}(K_{\cal F})

Assume that the composition
\psi\circ\phi
is zero. Then, on the smooth locus of

, we have that
\psi
factors through
\Omega^{1}_{S}
, which implies that

is
\cal F
-invariant, a contradiction.

Thus, the existence of the non-zero map

\mathcal{O}_{S^{\nu}}(-S)\to\mathcal{O}_{S^{\nu}}(K_{\cal F})

and the claim follows.

2.6.
Characterising log canonical vector fields

Let
X
be a normal variety and let
\partial
be a vector field which defines a foliated pair
(\cal F,D)
such that
K_{\cal F}+D
is Cartier. Then we say that
\partial
is
terminal
(resp.
canonical, log canonical
) if the foliated pair
(\cal F,D)
is such.

Let
P\in Z
be a germ of a normal variety and let
\partial\in H^{0}(Z,T_{Z})
be a vector
field which leaves
P
invariant. By Lemma
2.12
,
\partial
is singular at
P
.
Let
V\coloneqq\mathfrak{m}/\mathfrak{m}^{2}
where
\mathfrak{m}
is the maximal ideal at
P

and observe that
\partial
induces a linear map
\partial_{0}\colon V\rightarrow V
.
Let
\cal F
be the foliation defined by
\partial
so that
\partial

is a section of
{\cal F}(-D)
for some divisor
D\geq 0
.

We recall the following result, see
[
MP13
, Fact I.ii.4]
.

Proposition 2.20
.

Set up as above.

Then the vector field
\partial
is log canonical at
P
if and only if
\partial_{0}

is non-nilpotent.

For convenience, we will explain a proof of this proposition.
We remark that this proposition holds with no assumptions on the singularities
of
Z
other than normality and holds even when
\partial
has a codimension
one singular locus.

Lemma 2.21
.

Let
U\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
be a neighbourhood of the point
0\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
and let
\mathfrak{m}
be its maximal ideal.
Let
\partial
be a vector field on
U
such that
\partial

is singular at
0
and the induced linear map
\partial_{0}\colon\mathfrak{m}/\mathfrak{m}^{2}\to\mathfrak{m}/\mathfrak{m}^{2}
is non-zero and nilpotent.
Let
\pi\colon X\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{n}
be the blow up at
0
.
Let
\partial^{\prime}=\pi^{*}\partial
and let
P\in\operatorname{Sing}\,\partial^{\prime}

and let
\partial^{\prime}_{0}
be the induced linear map at
P
.

Then
\partial^{\prime}_{0}
is nilpotent, and possibly equal to zero.

Proof.

This follows from a direct calculation. For simplicity we will perform this calculation
in the case where
n=2
(the higher dimensional case is completely analogous).
We may choose coordinates
x,y
on
\mathbb{C}^{2}
so that

\partial=y\frac{\partial}{\partial x}+\delta
where
\delta\in\mathfrak{m}^{2}T_{\mathbb{C}^{2}}
.

First, consider the chart in which the blow up is given by
(s,t)\mapsto(st,t)
.
We can compute that
\pi^{*}\partial=\frac{\partial}{\partial s}+\pi^{*}\delta

where
\pi^{*}\delta
vanishes along
\{t=0\}
. In particular, we see that
\pi^{*}\partial

is non-singular in this chart.

Now consider the chart given by
(s,t)\mapsto(s,st)
. In this chart
we have
\pi^{*}\partial=(st\frac{\partial}{\partial s}-t^{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial t})+\pi^{*}\delta
.
Notice moreover that if we write
\delta=ax^{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial y}+\delta^{\prime}

where
a\in\mathbb{C}
then
\pi^{*}\delta=as\frac{\partial}{\partial t}+\pi^{*}\delta^{\prime}

where
\pi^{*}\delta^{\prime}
vanishes to order two at
\{s=t=0\}
.
It follows that

\pi^{*}\partial
is singular only at
\{s=t=0\}
and
(\pi^{*}\partial)_{0}

is nilpotent at that point.
∎

.

Proof.

Again, for simplicity we will consider the case

(the general case can

where

Consider the blow up at

\partial^{\prime}_{0}

We now show that

only extracts divisors of discrepancy
\geq-\epsilon
.
Indeed, if

is a

-exceptional divisor, then either
\partial^{\prime}

is smooth at the generic point of

, in which case the discrepancy is zero,
or

is contained in the singular locus of
\partial^{\prime}
, in which case, since

is non-nilpotent along

\partial^{\prime}=ut\frac{\partial}{\partial t}
where

is a unit
and

is a uniformizing parameter. In this case, it follows that
\epsilon(E)=1
and that the discrepancy is also equal to
-1
.

We now show that

is log canonical. Suppose for sake of contradiction that
there exists a divisor

of discrepancy
\leq-\epsilon-1
. Observe that we may
extract

by performing a sequence of blow ups in
\partial
-invariant centres.
However, by our above observations we know that such an

must necessarily have discrepancy

\geq-\epsilon(E)
, a contradiction.
∎

(Inversion of adjunction for vector fields)
.

2.7.
Jordan decomposition of a vector field

We follow the notation of
[
Mar81
]
. Let
X\coloneqq\widehat{\mathbb{C}^{m}}
be the completion of
\mathbb{C}^{m}
at the origin
0\in X
and let
\partial
be a
vector field on
X
which leaves
W\coloneqq\{0\}
invariant. Let
\mathfrak{m}
be the maximal ideal defining
W

and note that, by the Leibniz rule, the ideal
\mathfrak{m}^{n}
is
\partial
-invariant for all positive integer
n
. Thus, we get a linear map

\partial_{n}\colon\mathfrak{m}/\mathfrak{m}^{n+1}\rightarrow\mathfrak{m}/\mathfrak{m}^{n+1}.

We may write
\partial_{n}=\partial_{S,n}+\partial_{N,n}
as the Jordan decomposition of
\partial_{n}

into its semi-simple and nilpotent parts.
This decomposition respects the exact sequences

0\rightarrow\mathfrak{m}^{n}/\mathfrak{m}^{n+1}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}[[X]]/\mathfrak{m}^{n+1}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}[[X]]/\mathfrak{m}^{n}\rightarrow 0

for each positive integer
n

and it yields a decomposition
\partial=\partial_{S}+\partial_{N}
.

We summarise briefly some of the key properties of this decomposition:

(1)

[\partial_{S},\partial_{N}]=0
;

(2)

we may find coordinates
y_{1},...,y_{m}
on
\widehat{\mathbb{C}^{m}}
and
\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{m}\in\mathbb{C}
so that

\partial_{S}=\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}y_{i}\partial_{y_{i}}
; and

(3)

if
Z\subset\widehat{\mathbb{C}^{m}}

is
\partial
-invariant then
Z
is both
\partial_{S}
and
\partial_{N}
-invariant.

We briefly explain (3). Let
I_{Z}\subset\mathbb{C}[[X]]
be the ideal of
Z
and let
I_{Z,n}
denote
its restriction to
\mathfrak{m}/\mathfrak{m}^{n+1}
, for each positive integer
n
. Then
I_{Z,n}\subset\mathfrak{m}/\mathfrak{m}^{n+1}

is a
\partial_{n}
-invariant subspace and, in particular, it is both
\partial_{S,n}
and
\partial_{N,n}
-invariant. Thus, (3) follows.

More generally, we can define the Jordan decomposition for any vector field
\partial
on the completion of a variety
X
at a point
P\in X
. Indeed, consider an embedding
\iota\colon Z\hookrightarrow\mathbb{C}^{m}
and a lift
\widetilde{\partial}

of
\partial
to a vector field on
\mathbb{C}^{m}
. We can define
\widetilde{\partial}_{S}
and
\widetilde{\partial}_{N}

as above. Then
\widetilde{\partial}_{S}
and
\widetilde{\partial}_{N}
leave
Z
invariant
and, therefore, they restrict to vector fields
\partial_{S}
and
\partial_{N}
on
Z
.
Thus,
\partial=\partial_{S}+\partial_{N}
and this decomposition has all the properties of the Jordan
decomposition, as described above.

2.8.
Eigenvalues of vector fields

In this section we provide some results on the eigenvalues of a vector field with singularities in
codimension one. Let
Z
be a normal variety
and let
\partial\in H^{0}(Z,T_{Z})
be a vector field such that the zero locus of
\partial
admits an irreducible component
E\subset Z
of codimension one.
By normality,
\cal O_{Z,E}
is a DVR and we denote by
t
a local parameter. Let
P\in E
and
let
\mathfrak{m}
denote the maximal ideal of
P
in
Z
. Note that both
E
and
P
are invariant.
Let
V=\mathfrak{m}/\mathfrak{m}^{2}
and let
\partial_{0}\colon V\rightarrow V
be the induced linear map.
Assume that
\partial
is log canonical and so, by Proposition
2.20
,
\partial_{0}
is non-nilpotent.

The following proposition is related to
[
MP13
, Fact III.i.3]
.

Proposition 2.24
.

Set up as above.

Then, after possibly rescaling and taking a change of coordinates, we have that
\partial
is semi-simple and
its eigenvalues are all non-negative integers.

Proof.

For any positive integer
p
,
let
V_{p}\coloneqq\mathfrak{m}/\mathfrak{m}^{p}
.
Let
\partial_{p}
be the map induced by
\partial
on
V_{p}
. It is enough to show that, for all sufficiently large
p
, the linear map
\partial_{p}
is semi-simple and that its eigenvalues are all non-negative integers.
Note that
V_{p}
is filtered by subspaces

V_{p}^{k}\coloneqq\{f\mod\mathfrak{m}^{p}\mid\ord_{E}(f)\geq k\}\subset V_{p}\qquad\text{for }k=0,1,\dots.

By a local computation at the general point of
E
, it follows that for all
f\in\mathfrak{m}
, we have

\ord_{E}(f)\leq\ord_{E}(\partial f).

Thus,

\partial_{p}
preserves the subspace
V_{p}^{k}
for any
k
. Let
D_{p}^{k}
be the induced linear map on
V_{p}^{k}/V_{p}^{k+1}
. It is enough to show that,
for all
p
sufficiently large, the linear map
D^{k}_{p}
coincides with
kI
where
I
is the
identity map on
V^{k}_{p}/V^{k+1}_{p}
.

Since
\partial
is log canonical, Proposition
2.20
implies that
\partial_{p}
is non-nilpotent. Since
E
is contained in the zero locus of
\partial
, we have that

\partial_{p}(V_{p})\subset V^{1}_{p}
. It follows that the restriction map

\partial_{p}|_{V^{1}_{p}}
is non-nilpotent for each
p
.
Thus, after possibly rescaling
\partial
by a constant, if
p
is sufficiently large, then there exists a non-zero element
v_{p}\in V^{1}_{p}/V^{2}_{p}
so that
D^{1}_{p}(v_{p})=v_{p}
.
It follows that there exists
f_{p}\in\mathfrak{m}
such that
\ord_{E}(f_{p})=1
and

\partial f_{p}=f_{p}+R\mod\mathfrak{m}^{p}

where
\text{ord}_{E}R\geq 2
.

Let
0\neq g\in\mathfrak{m}
and let
k=\ord_{E}(g)
. At the generic point of
E
, we may write

g=uf_{p}^{k}\mod\mathfrak{m}^{p}
where
u
is a unit. We have

\displaystyle\partial g

\displaystyle=\partial(uf_{p}^{k})\mod\mathfrak{m}^{p}

\displaystyle=u\partial(f_{p}^{k})+f_{p}^{k}\partial u\mod\mathfrak{m}^{p}

\displaystyle=kuf_{p}^{k-1}\partial(f_{p})+f_{p}^{k}\partial u\mod\mathfrak{m}^{p}

\displaystyle=kg+kuf_{p}^{k-1}R+f_{p}^{k}\partial u\mod\mathfrak{m}^{p}.

Since
\ord_{E}R\geq 2
and
\ord_{E}\partial u\geq 1
, it follows that

\ord_{E}(kuf_{p}^{k-1}R+f_{p}^{k}\partial u)\geq k+1.

Thus,
D_{p}^{k}=kI
, as claimed.
∎

Remark 2.25
.

Let

be a normal

-dimensional variety and let

2.9.
Canonical bundle formula

We recall some results on the canonical bundle formula which will be used later (see
[
Amb04
]
for more details).

Let
(X,\Delta)
be a sub log canonical pair and let
f\colon X\to Y
be a fibration. Assume that
the horizontal part
\Delta^{h}
of
\Delta
is effective and that
there exists a
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor
D
on
Y
such that

K_{X}+\Delta\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}f^{*}D.

If
P
is a prime divisor on
Y
, we denote by
\eta_{P}
its generic point and we define the
log canonical threshold
of
f^{*}P
with respect to
(X,\Delta)
to be

\lct(X,\Delta;f^{*}P)\coloneqq\sup\{t\in\mathbb{R}\mid(X,\Delta+tf^{*}P)\text{ is sub log canonical over $\eta_{P}$}\}.

Let
b_{P}\coloneqq 1-\lct(X,\Delta;f^{*}P)
. Then we define the
discriminant
of
f
with respect to
\Delta
as

B_{Y}\coloneqq\sum_{P}b_{P}P
, where the sum runs over all the prime divisors
P
in
Y
.
Let
r
be the smallest positive integer such that there exists a rational function
\phi
on
X
satisfying

K_{X}+\Delta+\frac{1}{r}(\phi)=f^{*}D.

Then there exists a
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor
M_{Y}
such that

K_{X}+\Delta+\frac{1}{r}(\phi)=f^{*}(K_{Y}+B_{Y}+M_{Y}).

M_{Y}
is called the
moduli part
of
f
with respect to
\Delta
.

We will need the following results:

Lemma 2.26
.

Set up as above. Assume that
X
is a threefold,
Y
is a surface and
M_{Y}
is
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier.

Then
M_{Y}
is semi-ample.

Proof.

By
[
PS09
, Theorem 8.1]
,
there exists a birational morphism
\alpha\colon Y^{\prime}\to Y
such that if
X^{\prime}
is the normalisation of the main component of
X\times_{Y}Y^{\prime}
with induced morphisms
f^{\prime}\colon X^{\prime}\to Y^{\prime}
and
\beta\colon X^{\prime}\to X
, and we write
\beta^{*}(K_{X}+\Delta)=K_{X^{\prime}}+\Delta^{\prime}
, then the moduli part
M_{Y^{\prime}}
of
f^{\prime}
with respect to
\Delta^{\prime}
is semi-ample.
Since
M_{Y}\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}\alpha_{*}M_{Y^{\prime}}
, it follows that the stable base locus of
M_{Y}
has codimension at least two. Since
Y
is a surface and
M_{Y}
is
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier, Fujita Theorem
[
Fuj83
, Theorem 1.10]
implies that
M_{Y}
is semi-ample, as claimed.
∎

Lemma 2.27
.

Let
(X,\Delta)
be a two dimensional log canonical pair, let
f\colon X\to Y
be a fibration onto a curve
Y
and let
D
be a
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor on
Y
such that
K_{X}+\Delta\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}f^{*}D
.
Let
y\in Y
be a closed point and assume that there exists an open neighbourhood

of

such that, if we denote

X_{U}\coloneqq f^{-1}(U)\qquad\text{and}\qquad X_{u}\coloneqq f^{-1}(u)\quad\text{for }u\in U

then
(X_{U},\Delta|_{X_{U}})
is log smooth and there exists an isomorphism

\phi_{u}\colon X_{u}\to X_{y}\qquad\text{such that}\qquad\phi^{*}_{u}(\Delta|_{X_{y}})=\Delta|_{X_{u}}\qquad\text{for all }u\in U.

Then the moduli part of
f
is trivial, i.e.
M_{Y}\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}0
.

Proof.

By
[
Kol07
, Proposition 8.4.9]
,
we may freely
perform a base change.
Thus, without loss of generality, we may freely
assume that
X\rightarrow Y
is semi-stable and
\Delta+f^{*}P
is a divisor with simple normal crossing for any prime divisor
P
on
Y
.

Let
G
be the support of
\Delta
. By our hypotheses, after possibly replacing
Y
by a higher cover, we may find an open subset
V\subset Y

so that
X=X_{0}\times V
and
G=G_{0}\times V
, where
X_{0}
is a smooth curve and

is a finite set. Since

only depends on the generic fibre we are
therefore free to assume that

and

the result is immediate.
∎

.

Let

2.13.
Nakamaye’s theorem and the structure of extremal rays

Let
X
be a normal projective variety and let
M
be a
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier divisor on
X
.
We define the exceptional locus of
M
to be

\operatorname{Null}\,M\coloneqq\bigcup_{M|_{V}\text{ is not big }}V

where the union runs over all the subvarieties
V\subset X
of positive dimension such that
M|_{V}
is not big. We denote by
\mathbb{B}(M)
the
stable base locus
of
M
,

\mathbb{B}(M)\coloneqq\bigcap\operatorname{Bs}\,|mM|

where the intersection runs over all the sufficiently divisible positive integers
m
.
Finally, given a ray
R
in the cone of curves
\overline{\rm NE}(X)
, we define the
locus of
R
to be the subset

\operatorname{loc}\,R:=\bigcup_{[C]\in R}C.

We recall the following result originally due to Nakamaye, in the case of smooth varieties.

Lemma 2.42
.

Let
X
be a normal projective variety. Let
A
be an ample
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor and let

M
be a big and nef Cartier divisor on
X
.

Then
\operatorname{Null}\,M=\mathbb{B}(M-\epsilon A)
for any sufficiently small rational number
\epsilon>0
.

Proof.

See
[
Bir17
, Theorem 1.4]
.
∎

Proposition 2.43
.

Let
X
be a
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial normal projective variety. Let

M
be a big and nef Cartier divisor on
X
. Let
W=\operatorname{Null}\,M
and suppose that
M|_{W}\equiv 0
.

Then there exists
a birational contraction
\phi\colon X\to Z
to an algebraic spaces
Z
, such that
\phi
contracts
W
to a point and which is an isomorphism outside
W
.

Proof.

Consider the rational map
\phi\colon X\dashrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{N}
defined by the linear system
|m(M-\epsilon A)|
where
\epsilon>0
is a sufficiently small rational number
and
m
is a sufficiently divisible and large positive integer and note that
\phi
is birational onto the closure of its image
Y\subset\mathbb{P}^{N}
.
Let
p\colon\overline{X}\to X
and
q\colon\overline{X}\to Y
be birational morphisms which resolve the indeterminancy locus of
\phi
.

By Lemma
2.42
, it follows that
p(\operatorname{Exc}\,q)=W
.
We may write

p^{*}(m(M-\epsilon A))=H+F

where
F\geq 0
is
q
-exceptional
and
H=q^{*}L
for some very ample Cartier divisor
L
on
Y
.
Since
X
is
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial we may choose
G\geq 0
to be
p
-exceptional so that
-G
is
p
-ample.
Choose
\delta>0
sufficiently small so that
A^{\prime}\coloneqq p^{*}(m\epsilon A)-\delta G
is ample.

We therefore have
F+\delta G=p^{*}(mM)-A^{\prime}-H
and
F+\delta G
is a
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier
q
-exceptional divisor.
Since
p(\operatorname{Exc}\,q)=W
, it follows that
p^{*}M
restricted to
\operatorname{Exc}\,q
is numerically trivial. Thus, if
k
is a sufficiently divisible positive integer so that
k(F+\delta G)
is a Cartier divisor, then

-k(F+\delta G)|_{k(F+\delta G)}\equiv k(A^{\prime}+H)|_{k(F+\delta G)}.

Since ampleness of a line bundle on a scheme is equivalent to ampleness of the line bundle
restricted to the reduction and normalisation, and since
A^{\prime}+H
restricted to the reduction and normalisation
of each component of
\operatorname{Exc}\,q
is ample,
we see that
-k(F+\delta G)|_{k(F+\delta G)}
is ample.

We may therefore apply Artin’s Theorem
[
Art70
, Theorem 6.2]
to produce a morphism of algebraic spaces
\overline{X}\rightarrow Z

which contracts
F+\delta G
to a point. By the rigidity lemma this contraction factors through
\overline{X}\rightarrow X

giving our desired birational contraction
\phi\colon X\rightarrow Z
.
∎

Proposition 2.44
.

Let
X
be a normal projective threefold and let
R
be an extremal ray of the cone of curves
\overline{\rm NE}(X)
.

Then
\operatorname{loc}\,R
is Zariski closed.

Proof.

Let
H_{R}
be the supporting hyperplane to
R
. In particular,
H_{R}
is big and nef. By Lemma
2.42
, it follows that
\operatorname{Null}\,H_{R}
is a proper Zariski closed subset of
X
.
We have
\operatorname{loc}\,R\subset\operatorname{Null}\,H_{R}
. If
\operatorname{loc}\,R
is not Zariski closed, then there exists a surface
S\subset\operatorname{Null}\,H_{R}
which contains infinitely many curves in
R
but such that
S

is not contained in
\operatorname{loc}\,R
.
Thus,
H_{R}|_{S}
is a nef divisor on

which intersects infinitely many curves trivially and
therefore it must intersect some moving curve trivially. It follows that

, a contradiction.
∎

The following simple observation is a crucial technical ingredient:

.

.

,

is not

of

is Cartier.

, after possibly shrinking

, we may assume that there exists a submersion

is induced by
f_{i,j}
. Indeed,
f_{i,j}
is the Stein factorisation of the morphism
V_{i,j}\to T_{i}
.
Let
\sigma_{i,j}\colon T_{i,j}\to T_{i}
be the induced morphism.
Note that the preimage of
C
in
V_{i,j}
is mapped to a point
z_{i,j}\in T_{i,j}
such that
\sigma_{i,j}(z_{i,j})=z_{i}
.
After possibly shrinking
U_{i}
and
U_{j}
, we may assume that
\sigma_{i,j}
is surjective.
It follows that
\sigma_{i,j}
is étale. Thus, after replacing
V_{i}
by

V_{i}\times_{T_{i}}T_{i,j}

we may assume that
T_{i}=T_{j}
.
After repeating this process, finitely many times, we may assume that
T\coloneqq T_{i}
and
z\coloneqq z_{i}\in T
do not depend on
i=1,\dots,k
. Note that, by the construction above, the choice of the germ
(T,z)
is uniquely determined by
\cal F
and
C
.

Pick
c\in C^{\circ}
. Let
\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{N}
be loops based at
c
around
c_{1},\dots,c_{N}
respectively. The orbifold fundamental group of
C^{\circ}
with weight
n_{k}
at
c_{k}
is defined as the quotient

\pi(C^{\circ},c;n_{1},\dots,n_{N})\coloneqq\pi(C^{\circ},c)/\langle\gamma_{1}^{n_{1}},\dots,\gamma_{N}^{n_{N}}\rangle.

We now want to define the
holonomy map

\rho\colon\pi(C^{\circ},c;n_{1},\dots,n_{N})\to\Aut(T,z),

where
\Aut(T,z)
denotes the group of biholomorhpic automorphisms on the germ
(T,z)
.
Let
\gamma\colon[0,1]\to C^{\circ}
be a continuous path which is contained in
U_{i}
for some
i=1,\dots,\ell
. Then, since
q_{i}\colon V_{i}\to U_{i}
is unramified outside
\Sigma
, there exists a lifting
\tilde{\gamma}\colon[0,1]\to V_{i}
of
\gamma
in
V_{i}
. Note that
f_{i}
maps the image of
\tilde{\gamma}
to the point
z\in T
. Proceeding as in the construction of the classic holonomy map (e.g. see
[
CN85
]
), we can define a homomorphism

\rho^{\prime}\colon\pi(C^{0},c)\to\Aut(T,z).

Note that if
c_{k}\in U_{i}
for some
i
and
k
, then the ramification index of
q_{i}
at any point in
q_{i}^{-1}(c_{k})
is equal to
n_{k}
. Thus, it follows that
\rho^{\prime}(\gamma_{k}^{n_{k}})
is the identity automorphism of
T
for any
k=1,\dots,N
and, in particular, the holonomy map

\rho\colon\pi(C^{\circ},c;n_{1},\dots,n_{N})\to\Aut(T,z)

is well defined.

We are now ready to state our singular version of Reeb stability theorem:

Theorem 3.2
.

Set up as above. Assume that the image of the holonomy map
\rho
is finite.

Then there exists an analytic open subset
W
of
X
containing
C
such that the leaf
C_{t}
of
\cal F
passing through
t\in W
is a compact analytic subvariety of
W
.

Proof.

The proof of the Theorem is an easy generalisation of the classical Reeb stability theorem (e.g. see
[
CN85
, Theorem IV.5]
).
∎

As a direct application of Reeb stability theorem, we get the following result (see also
[
McQ04
, II.d.5]
):

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
X
be a normal variety and let
\cal F
be a rank one foliation on
X
. Let
C\subset X

be an
\cal F
-invariant curve and suppose that
\cal F

is terminal at every closed point
P\in C
. Suppose moreover that
K_{\cal F}\cdot C<0
.

Then

C
moves in a family of
\cal F
-invariant curves covering
X
.

Proof.

Lemma
2.13
implies
that
\cal F
is Gorenstein at the generic point of
C
.
Let
c_{1},...,c_{N}\in C
be the non-Gorenstein points of
\cal F
and let
n_{k}

denote the Cartier index of
K_{\cal F}
at
c_{k}
, for
k=1,\dots,N
. Let
C^{\circ}=C\setminus\{c_{1},\dots,c_{N}\}
.

It follows from foliation adjunction (cf. Proposition
2.16
),
that

K_{\cal F}\cdot C=-2+\sum_{k=1}^{N}\frac{n_{k}-1}{n_{k}}.

In particular, since
K_{\cal F}\cdot C<0

it follows that the orbifold fundamental group
\pi_{1}(C^{\circ},c;n_{1},\dots,n_{N})

is finite. Thus, Theorem
3.2
implies the claim.
∎

4.
Subadjunction result in the presence of a foliation

Given a log pair
(X,S)
, a minimal log canonical centre
W
of
(X,S)
and an ample divisor
A
on
X
, we may write

(K_{X}+S+A)|_{W}=K_{W}+\Theta
for an effective divisor
\Theta\geq 0
. We are interested in this situation in the presence of a foliation
which leaves the components of
S
invariant. In this case we are able to get some control on
\Theta
in terms of the singularities
of the foliation.

4.1.
Dlt modification

Let
X
be a normal threefold singularity and let
\cal F
be a rank one foliation
on
X
with canonical singularities.
Let
S_{1},\dots,S_{k}
be prime
\cal F
-invariant divisors. Our goal here is to control the singularities
of the pair
(X,S\coloneqq\sum a_{i}S_{i})
, where
a_{1},\dots,a_{k}\in(0,1]\cap\mathbb{Q}
, in terms of the singularities of
\cal F
.
As the following example shows a canonical foliation singularity
will in general have worse than quotient singularities on the ambient variety (in contrast to the surface case):

Example 4.1
.

Let
X=\{xy-zw=0\}\subset\mathbb{C}^{4}

and consider the vector field
\partial=x\partial_{x}-y\partial_{y}+z\partial_{z}-w\partial_{w}

on
\mathbb{C}^{4}
. Note that
X
is
\partial
-invariant and so
\partial
induces a rank one foliation

\cal F
on
X
.
We claim that
\cal F
has canonical singularities.
Indeed,
\operatorname{Sing}\,\cal F=\{0\}

and if
\mathfrak{m}
is the maximal ideal at
0
then the induced linear map
\mathfrak{m}/\mathfrak{m}^{2}\rightarrow\mathfrak{m}/\mathfrak{m}^{2}
is non-nilpotent,
and Proposition
2.20
implies that it is log canonical.
The eigenvalues of
\partial
are not all positive rational numbers and
[
MP13
, Fact III.i.3]
implies that

\cal F
has a
canonical singularity at
(0,0,0,0)
.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
X
be a normal variety and let
\cal F
be a rank one foliation on
X
with canonical singularities.
Let
(X,\Gamma=\sum a_{i}S_{i})
be a log pair whose irreducible components
S_{1},\dots,S_{k}
are
\cal F
-invariant
and
a_{1},\dots,a_{k}\in(0,1]
.

Then there exists a birational morphism
\mu\colon\overline{X}\rightarrow X
of

(X,\Gamma)

such that

(1)

K_{\overline{\cal F}}=\mu^{*}K_{\cal F}+F

where
\overline{\cal F}
is the foliation induced on
\overline{X}
and
F\geq 0
is a
\mu
-exceptional divisor whose centre in
X
is contained in the locus where

\mathcal{F}
is not Gorenstein; and

(2)

(\overline{X},\overline{\Gamma}+E)
is dlt and
K_{\overline{X}}+\overline{\Gamma}+E

is nef over
X
, where
E
is the sum of all the
\mu
-exceptional divisors
and
\overline{\Gamma}
is the strict transform of
\Gamma
in
\overline{X}
.

We call the morphism
\mu
a
dlt modification
of
(X,\Gamma)
with respect to
\cal F
.

Proof.

Let
U\subset X
be the Gorenstein locus of
\cal F
, i.e., the open subset of all points
P\in X
such that
K_{\cal F}

is Cartier in a neighbuorhood of
P
. In particular,
X\setminus U
, being contained in
\operatorname{Sing}\,X
, has codimension at least two.
By
[
BM97
]
, there exists
a log resolution
p\colon V\to U
of
(U,\sum S_{i}|_{U})
obtained by only blowing up foliation invariant centres.
Let
\cal F_{V}
be the foliation induced on
V
. By Lemma
2.12
and since
\cal F
admits canonical singularities, we have that
K_{\cal F_{V}}=p^{*}(K_{\cal F}|_{U})
.
Let
Y
be a normal variety which is a compactification of
V
such that there exists a projective morphism
\pi\colon Y\to X
which extends
p
. Let
\Gamma_{Y}=\pi^{-1}_{*}\Gamma
and let
G
be the sum of all the
\pi
-exceptional divisors. Let
Z\to Y
be a log resolution of
(Y,\Gamma_{Y}+G)
, which is an isomorphism over
V
, and let
\rho\colon Z\to X
be the induced morphism. In particular, if
\mathcal{F}_{Z}
is the induced foliation on
Z
,
W=\rho^{-1}(U)
and
q=\rho|_{W}\colon W\to U
is the restriction morphism, then
K_{\mathcal{F}_{Z}}|_{W}=q^{*}(K_{\mathcal{F}}|_{U})
.

We may construct a morphism
\mu\colon\overline{X}\to X
satisfying (2) as the output of an MMP over
X
starting from
Z
(e.g. see
[
Kol13
, Theorem 1.34]
). Let
\overline{\mathcal{F}}
be the foliation induced on
\overline{X}
. It follows that, if
\overline{U}=\mu^{-1}U
, then we have that
K_{\overline{\mathcal{F}}}|_{\overline{U}}=r^{*}(K_{\mathcal{F}}|_{U})
, where
r=\mu|_{\overline{U}}\colon\overline{U}\to U
is the restriction morphism. Thus, since
\cal F
has canonical singularities, (1) follows.
∎

Bounding singularities

We consider the following set up.
Let

be a normal threefold and let
\cal F

with canonical singularities

.
Let

\overline{\Gamma}

in

By classical adjunction, there exists a

Since

\overline{\cal F}
-invariant.
Thus, the claim follows.

Lemma 4.4
.

Set up as above.
Let
C\subset E_{0}
be a curve which is not
\cal G
-invariant.

Then
\mu_{C}\Delta\geq\mu_{C}\Theta

Proof.

We may assume that
C
is contained in the support of
\Theta

Since
(E_{0},\Theta)

Note that

\operatorname{Sing}\,\overline{X}\cap E_{0}

on

\operatorname{Supp}\,\overline{\Gamma}\cap E_{0}

Let

\overline{\cal F}\coloneqq\mu^{-1}\cal F

\overline{\cal F}

By classical adjunction and by Proposition

\mathbb{Q}

\Delta_{i},\Theta_{i}\geq 0

For any

Note that

Now suppose that

Let

4.3.
Subadjuntion

We work in the following set up. Let
X
be a

\mathbb{Q}
-factorial threefold with klt singularities, let

be a rank one foliation on

Let

and suppose that there are no one-dimensional non-log canonical centres.
Suppose moreover that
\cal F
has canonical singularities and that
\cal F
is terminal and Gorenstein
at a general point of

.
Theorem
4.5
implies that

is log canonical.
By Lemma
4.10
,
it follows that

Thus, by Proposition

.

Set up as above. Then

(1)

(C^{\nu},\Theta)

(2)

\lfloor\Theta\rfloor
is supported on the pre-image of centres of canonical singularities of

(3)

if

In particular, we have

(K_{X}+\Gamma)\cdot C\leq K_{\cal F}\cdot C.

Proof.

Let
p\colon\overline{X}\rightarrow X
be a dlt modification of

with respect to
\cal F

as in Lemma
4.2

and let
\overline{\Gamma}
be the strict transform of

in
\overline{X}
.
Since

K_{\overline{X}}+\overline{\Gamma}+E=p^{*}(K_{X}+\Gamma)

.
Lemma
2.8
implies that

is
\overline{\cal F}
-invariant.
Let
E_{0}
be an irreducible component of

dominating
C

.
By adjunction we may write

(K_{\overline{X}}+E+\overline{\Gamma})|_{E_{0}}=K_{E_{0}}+\Theta_{0}

\Theta_{0}\geq 0
. Let
\overline{\cal F}
be the induced foliation on
\overline{X}

\cal G
be the induced foliation on

2.16
.

Let
f\coloneqq p|_{E_{0}}\colon E_{0}\rightarrow C^{\nu}
be the restriction morphism. Then

is
f
-trivial

K_{E_{0}}+\Theta_{0}=f^{*}(K_{C^{\nu}}+M+B)

.
In particular, we have

.

We first show that property (1),…,(4) above hold for

.
Since
(E_{0},\Theta_{0})
is dlt, it follows that
(C^{\nu},B)
is log canonical.
If

4.10
implies that
\nu(Q)

\cal F
. It follows that
\lfloor B\rfloor
is supported
on the pre-image of the isolated canonical centres of

contained in

.

Now, let
Q\in C^{\nu}
be a point such that
\cal F
is terminal and Gorenstein at
\nu(Q)
. We want to show that

is not contained
in the support of
B
. Lemma
2.12
implies that
Q
is not
\cal F
-invariant and it is a smooth point
of
\cal F
and, therefore, of

. Lemma
2.5
implies that

does not contain any
\overline{\cal F}
-invariant curve.
By (1) of Lemma
4.2
, we may assume that
\overline{\cal F}
is Gorenstein around

does not contain any canonical centre.
In particular, Lemma
2.12

\operatorname{Sing}\,\overline{X}
is
\overline{\cal F}
-invariant.
Let
\Sigma
be an irreducible component of
\Theta_{0}
. Note
that

is supported on the union of

and

. Then

\Sigma
is an irreducible component of the intersection of

\overline{\cal F}
-invariant subvarieties and, thus, it is

\overline{\cal F}
-invariant.
Thus,
it follows that no component of
\Theta_{0}
is contained in
f^{-1}(Q)
.

We now want to prove that each component
\Sigma
of
\Theta_{0}

which dominates
C^{\nu}
is

\cal G
-invariant. Suppose for sake of contradiction
that
\Sigma
is not
\cal G
-invariant.
Then, since
\Sigma
is
\overline{\cal F}
-invariant, it is contained in
\operatorname{Sing}\,\overline{\cal F}
,
and so for a generic

\overline{\cal F}
.
Since

near

, a contradiction.

is
\cal G
-invariant.

A similar argument allows us to conclude that
f^{-1}(Q)
is smooth and
E_{0}
is smooth along

.
We claim that

f^{-1}(Q)
is everywhere transverse to
\cal G
. Indeed, let

and observe that the number of tangencies
of

and
\cal G
is exactly

(e.g. see
[
Bru00
, Proposition 2.2]
).
On the other hand, we know that, since
\cal F
is Gorenstein near
Q
, we have that

in a neighbourhood of

and so restriting to

and

is a divisor on

\cal G
-invariant and

is not contained in the support of

Finally, let

be a terminal non-Gorenstein point of
\cal F
and let

be the Cartier index of

at

be a neighborhood of

in

is Cartier and,

\cal F^{\prime}
is terminal at

. Let

and note that

is
\cal F^{\prime}

is smooth at

.

Let
\overline{V}\coloneqq\overline{X}\times_{X}V

and let
\tau\colon\overline{V}\rightarrow\overline{X}
be the induced morphism.
Set

and, since
\cal F
is Gorenstein at the general point of

, we have that
\tau
is unramified at the generic point of

.
Write
K_{F_{0}}+\Xi_{0}=\eta^{*}(K_{E_{0}}+\Theta_{0})
where
\eta\colon F_{0}\rightarrow E_{0}
is the induced morphism.
Let

be the induced morphism.
Note that, for all

, we have

\eta^{*}(K_{E_{0}}+\Theta_{0}+tf^{*}P)=K_{F_{0}}+\Xi_{0}+ntg^{*}P^{\prime}.

Thus,

\lct(E_{0},\Theta_{0};f^{*}P)=\frac{1}{n}\lct(F_{0},\Xi_{0};g^{*}P^{\prime})

and so to prove our claim about

it suffices to show that

\lct(F_{0},\Xi_{0};g^{*}P^{\prime})=1.

This can be shown by arguing exactly as in the case where we assumed that
Q
was a terminal Gorenstein
point of the foliation, proving our claim. Thus the log pair
(C^{\nu},B)
satisfies (1),…,(4).

We now handle the moduli part in the canonical bundle formula.
We claim the following

Claim
.

Let
Q\in C
be a general point. There exists an open neighbourhood
U\subset C
of
Q
such that, if
E_{U}=f^{-1}(U)
then
(E_{U},\Theta_{0}|_{E_{U}})
is log smooth and for all

, there exists an isomorphism between
(f^{-1}(Q),\Theta_{0}|_{f^{-1}(Q)})

and
(f^{-1}(t),\Theta_{0}|_{f^{-1}(t)})

2.27

to conclude that

. Thus,
\Theta=B
and (1),
\dots

By assumption,
\cal F
is terminal and Gorenstein at
Q
.
Let
\partial
be a local generator of
\cal F
near
Q
and observe that
\partial

lifts to
\overline{X}
and, by restricting it to
E_{0}
, we produce a vector field
\tilde{\partial}

generating
\cal G
in a neighbourhood of
f^{-1}(Q)
.
As observed earlier we know that

is smooth and everywhere transverse to
\cal G

\cal G

We claim that there exists an analytic open

and a holomorphic submersion which induces
\cal G

Indeed, locally on
X
, we may find a holomorphic first
integral of
\cal F
, call it
F\colon V\rightarrow S

is a surface and

is an analytic neighbourhood of

, with

5.
The formal neighborhood of a flipping curve

5.1.
Producing invariant surfaces around flipping curves on threefolds

Let
X
be a normal threefold, let
\cal F
be a rank one foliation on
X

and let
f\colon X\rightarrow Z
be a
K_{\cal F}
-flipping contraction. Let
C={\rm Exc}(f)
.
In the case where
C
is smooth and irreducible McQuillan has produced a rather complete picture of a the structure
of a formal neighborhood of
C
by examining the formal holonomy around the curve,
in particular, he shows the existence of a formal
\cal F
-invariant divisor
containing
C
.

In this section we provide a different approach to producing such an invariant divisor.
Our two main ingredients are a foliated analogue of the existence of complements and an analysis
of the structure of log canonical foliation singularities. We then provide a bound on the singularities
of these surfaces when the foliation has canonical singularities.

5.2.
Preliminary results

We begin with the following results:

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
X
be a normal threefold with only quotient singularities and let
C\subset X
be a curve such that
\operatorname{Sing}\,X\cap C=\{x_{1},...,x_{k}\}
is finite. Let
H
be an ample divisor and assume that for each
i=1,\dots,k
, there exists a prime divisor
D_{i}
such that
D_{i}
is Kawamata log terminal at
x_{i}
,
C
is contained in
D_{i}
, and the log pair
(D_{i},C)
is log canonical at
the point
x_{i}
.

Then, after possibly replacing
X
by an étale neighbourhood of
C
, there exists a divisor
L
such that for any sufficiently large positive integer
m

the general element
D
of the linear system

\{\Sigma\in|L+mH|\mid C\subset\Sigma\}

is such that
D
is Kawamata log terminal at each point
x_{1},\dots,x_{k}
and
(D,C)
is log canonical.

Proof.

After possibly replacing
X
by an étale neighbourhood of
C
, for each
i=1,\dots,k
we may find an effective divisor
M_{i}
on
X
, such that

\sum_{j\neq i}D_{j}+M_{i}\text{ is Cartier at $x_{i}$}\qquad\text{ and }\qquad x_{j}\notin M_{i}\text{ for any }j\neq i.

Let
L:=\sum_{i=1}^{k}(D_{i}+M_{i})
. Then
L-D_{i}
is Cartier at
x_{i}
for each
i=1,\dots,k
. Thus, if
m
is a sufficiently large positive integer, we have that
x_{i}
is not contained in the base locus of
|L-D_{i}+mH|
. In particular, there exists
\Sigma_{i}\in|L+mH|
such that
C\subset\Sigma_{i}
,
\Sigma_{i}
has Kawamata log terminal singularities at
x_{i}
and
(\Sigma_{i},C)
is log canonical at
x_{i}
. Thus, the general element in the linear system

\{\Sigma\in|L+mH|\mid C\subset\Sigma\}

satisfies the required properties.
∎

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
X
be a normal variety of dimension at least two and with only quotient singularities and let
C
be a Cartier divisor on
X
.
Let
H
be an ample divisor on
X
, let
L
be a divisor on
X

and let
D\in\lvert mH+L\rvert
for a sufficiently large positive integer
m
. Suppose that
C|_{D}\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}0
.

Then
C\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}0
.

Proof.

After replacing
C
by a multiple, we may assume that
C|_{D}\sim 0
and that there exists
a compactification
\overline{X}
of
X
which is normal and it admits a Cartier divisor
\overline{C}
such that
\overline{C}|_{X}=C
.

Let
\pi\colon Y\rightarrow\overline{X}
be a finite cover which is unramified along the general point of
D
and such
that
L_{Y}\coloneqq\pi^{*}L
is Cartier.
Let
C_{Y}\coloneqq\pi^{*}\overline{C}

and
D_{Y}\coloneqq\pi^{-1}(D)
. Notice that
C_{Y}|_{D_{Y}}\sim 0
. Let
\overline{D_{Y}}
be the
closure of
D_{Y}
in
Y
.
It follows that
C_{Y}|_{\overline{D_{Y}}}\sim\sum a_{i}C_{i}|_{\overline{D_{Y}}}
where
a_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}
and
C_{i}
is a divisor contained in
Y\setminus U
, where
U\coloneqq\pi^{-1}(X)\subset Y
.

By choosing
m\gg 0
we may assume by Serre duality and Serre vanishing that

H^{1}(Y,\cal O_{Y}(-\overline{H_{Y}}))=0
. By the exact sequence

1\rightarrow\cal O_{Y}(-\overline{D_{Y}})\rightarrow\cal O^{*}_{Y}\rightarrow\cal O^{*}_{\overline{D_{Y}}}\rightarrow 1

it follows that

\text{Pic}\,Y\rightarrow\text{Pic}\,\overline{D_{Y}}
is injective.
Thus,
C_{Y}\sim\sum a_{i}C_{i}
and, in particular,
C_{Y}|_{U}\sim 0
.

Perhaps passing to the Galois closure of
U\rightarrow X
with Galois
group
G
we see that if
s\in H^{0}(U,C_{Y}|_{U})
is a non-vanishing section then
\prod_{g\in G}g\cdot s

is a nonvanishing
G
-invariant section of

, where
q=\#G
, and so descends
to a nonvanishing section of
qC
.
Thus, the claim follows.
∎

5.3.
Flipping contractions

Let
X
be a
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial normal threefold and let
\cal F
be a rank one foliation on
X
with log canonical singularities. Let
R
be a
K_{\cal F}
-negative extremal ray and assume that
\operatorname{loc}\,R
is a curve

is
\cal F
-invariant. Let

be a supporting hyperplane to

\overline{NE}(X)

Lemma 5.3
.

Set up as above.
Let
S
be a surface.

Then
H_{R}^{2}\cdot S>0
and, in particular,
\operatorname{Null}\,H_{R}=\operatorname{loc}\,R
.

Suppose for sake of contradiction that

.

Let
\nu\colon S^{\nu}\rightarrow S
be the normalisation of

.
Since

is big and nef we may write

is ample,

and does not contain
S

in its support and

. In particular,
-\nu^{*}S
is big, and since
\operatorname{loc}\,R
is one dimensional it follows
that
\nu^{*}H_{R}\cdot\nu^{*}S<0

We may also write
H_{R}\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}K_{\cal F}+A^{\prime}
where
A^{\prime}
is ample. Since

\nu^{*}H_{R}\cdot\nu^{*}K_{\cal F}=-\nu^{*}H_{R}\cdot\nu^{*}A^{\prime}<0

Suppose first that
S
is not
\cal F
-invariant. Then Proposition
2.19
implies that there exists a
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor

\cal F
-invariant. Let

Lemma 5.4
.

Set up as above.

Then the contraction of the flipping locus exists in the category of algebraic spaces.

Proof.

By Lemma
5.3

\operatorname{Null}\,H_{R}=\operatorname{loc}\,R

2.43

Lemma 5.7
.

Set up as above. Let

Then, after possibly replacing

We now prove that the induced morphism

Lemma 5.10

Let

Fix

Thus, (2) implies that

for all

.

5.6.

We now show that if

We begin with the following:

Lemma 5.12
.

Let
\partial

We may write

(a,-b,-c)

Proposition 5.13

6.
Threefold contractions and flips

6.1.
Divisorial contractions

Let
X
be a
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial normal threefold and let
\cal F
be a rank one foliation on
X
with log canonical singularities. Let
R
be a
K_{\cal F}
-negative extremal ray.
Assume first that
D\coloneqq\operatorname{loc}\,R
is of dimension two. Note that in this case, since

is
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial,

is an irreducible divisor and
D\cdot\Sigma<0
for any curve
\Sigma
such that
[\Sigma]\in R
. By Corollary
2.40
, it follows that

is
\cal F
-invariant.

Let
\nu\colon D^{\nu}\rightarrow D
be the normalisation of

. Then
Proposition
2.16
implies that there exists a foliated pair
(\cal G,\Delta)
on

such that

Let
\Theta\geq 0

\mathbb{Q}
-divisor on

(K_{X}+D)|_{D^{\nu}}=K_{D^{\nu}}+\Theta.

Lemma 6.1
.

Set up as above.
Suppose that
(X,D)
is log canonical and that
\cal F
admits canonical singularities.

Then the divisorial contraction associated to
R
exists.

Proof.

We want to prove that
R
is
(K_{X}+D)
-negative.
Proposition
2.17
implies that for any curve
C\subset D^{\nu}
which is not
\cal G
-invariant, we have that
\mu_{C}\Delta\geq\mu_{C}\Theta
.
Since
(D^{\nu},\Theta)
is log canonical and since
\cal G
is algebraically integrable, Lemma
2.15
implies
that all the
(K_{\cal G}+\Delta)
-negative curves in

which are
\cal G
-invariant
are in fact
(K_{D^{\nu}}+\Theta)
-negative. Thus,
R
is
(K_{X}+D)
-negative and, therefore, the divisorial contraction associated to

exists
[
Amb03
, Theorem 5.6]
.∎

Theorem 6.2
.

Let
X
be a
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial klt threefold and let
\cal F
be a rank one foliation on

with canonical singularities. Let

be a

-negative extremal ray such that

Then the divisorial contraction associated to

exists. In particular, there exists a projective birational morphism

, whose exceptional divisor coincides with

and such that, if
\cal F^{\prime}
is the foliation induced on

(1)

Y
is projective;

(2)

\rho(X/Y)=1
;

(3)

\cal F^{\prime}
has canonical singularities and it is terminal along
c(\operatorname{Exc}\,c)
; and

(4)

if
\Gamma\geq 0
if a
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor on
X
with
\cal F
-invariant support and such that
(X,\Gamma)
is log canonical, then

(Y,(c_{R})_{*}\Gamma)
is log canonical.

Proof.

If
(X,D)
is log canonical we may apply Lemma
6.1
to produce our desired contraction.

So assume that
(X,D)
is not log canonical.
Let
\lambda
denote the log canonical threshold of
X
with respect to
D
. Then
\lambda<1
and Theorem
4.5
implies
that
(X,\lambda D)
admits a one-dimensional log canonical centre

.
Proposition
4.7
implies that
C
is not contained in
\operatorname{Sing}\,\cal F
.
Let
\nu\colon D^{\nu}\to D
be the normalisation of

.
By Proposition
2.16
, there exists a foliated pair
(\cal G,\Delta)
on
D^{\nu}
such that

K_{\cal F}|_{D^{\nu}}=K_{\cal G}+\Delta.

By Lemma
2.12
, we have that
C
is
\cal F
-invariant and since

is not contained in
\operatorname{Sing}\,\cal F
and it is not contained in the singular locus of

is
\cal G
-invariant.
Since

, it follows that

and, in particular,

.

implies that

is

-negative. Thus, we can realise the

-contractions as a

-negative contraction. In particular, (1) and (2) hold.

implies (3).

We now prove (4). Note that, using the same argument as above, we may take the contraction to be associated to the log pair

6.2.
Flips

Let
X
be a
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial projective threefold and let
\cal F
be a rank one foliation on
X
with log canonical singularities. Let
R
be a
K_{\cal F}
-negative extremal ray.
Assume now that
\operatorname{loc}\,R
is of dimension one. By Lemma
5.4
, the flipping contraction associated to
R
exists in the category of algebraic spaces.

We now prove the following:

Theorem 6.3
.

Let
X
be a normal threefold and let
\cal F
be a rank one foliation on
X
with simple singularities. Let
R
be a
K_{\cal F}
-negative extremal ray such that

D\coloneqq\operatorname{loc}\,R
has dimension one.

Then the flipping contraction
c_{R}\colon X\to Y
associated to
R
exists in the category of projective varieties. Moreover, the flip
\phi\colon X\dashrightarrow X^{+}
associated to
R
exists and if
\cal F^{+}
is the foliation induced on
X^{+}
then

(1)

X^{+}
is projective and has quotient singularities;

(2)

\rho(X/Y)=1
;

(3)

\cal F^{+}
has simple singularities and

\cal F^{+}
is terminal along
\operatorname{Exc}\,\phi^{-1}
; and

(4)

if
\Gamma\geq 0
is a
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor on
X
with
\cal F
-invariant support such that
(X,\Gamma)
is log canonical, then

(X^{+},\phi_{*}\Gamma)
is log canonical.

Proof.

Lemma
2.34
implies that
X
has quotient singularities. In particular,
X
is klt and
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial.
By Proposition
5.13
and Lemma
5.14
, we may assume that
R=\mathbb{R}_{+}[C]
, where
C
is a smooth irreducible curve
such that

.

Let
c_{R}\colon X\to Y
be the flipping contraction associated to
R
in the category of algebraic spaces and whose existence is guaranteed by Lemma
5.4
.
Let
H_{R}
be a supporting hyperplane to

for
\overline{NE}(X)
.
To prove the projectivity of

it suffices to show that

descends to a
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier divisor

on
Y
. Indeed,
in this case
M^{\dim Z}\cdot Z>0
for all positive dimensional

and so
M
is ample
by the Nakai-Moishezon criterion and, in particular,
Y
is projective.

The problem of descending
H_{R}
and of constructing the flip is étale local on the base. Thus, we may replace

Y
by an étale neighbourhood of a point in
c_{R}(\operatorname{Exc}\,c_{R})
. Moreover, throughout of the proof, we may need to replace

Y
by a smaller étale neighbourhood and this does not cause problems.

By Lemma
5.15
, we may find
\cal F
-invariant divisors
S_{1},\dots,S_{\ell}

so that, if
S\coloneqq\sum S_{i}
then
C
is a non-klt centre of
(X,S)
.
By Lemma
5.15
, it also follows that, near

, if
\widehat{X}
denotes the
formal completion of
X
at
P
, then the only possible non-log canonical centre
of
(\widehat{X},S)
is
C
itself.

For any closed point

any non-lc centre meeting

is
\cal F
-invariant, however,

is the only

Q
. Thus, if we let
\lambda
be the log canonical threshold of
(\widehat{X},S)

along
C
then
(\widehat{X},\lambda S)
is log canonical.
By applying Theorem
4.11
we see that
(K_{\widehat{X}}+\lambda S)\cdot C<0
.

Fix

and let
X_{n}
denote the
n
-th infinitessimal neighborhood of
C
in
X
.
By our approximation results (cf. Section

), after possibly replacing

by a smaller étale neighborhood, we may
find a divisor
\tilde{S}

such that
\tilde{S}|_{X_{n}}=S|_{X_{n}}
and
\cal O_{X}(\tilde{S})|_{\widehat{X}}\cong\cal O_{\widehat{X}}(S)
.
By Lemma
2.30
, it follows that taking

to be sufficiently large, the pair
(X,\lambda\tilde{S})
is log canonical
and
(K_{X}+\lambda\tilde{S})\cdot C<0
.
In particular, the
K_{\cal F}
-flipping contraction (resp. flip) can be realised as a
(K_{X}+\lambda\tilde{S})
-flipping contraction (resp. flip),
and so the flip exists.

In particular,
Y
and
X^{+}
are projective and
\rho(X/Y)=1
. By Proposition
3.1
, it follows that
\cal F^{+}
has simple singularities, and
Lemma
2.34
implies that
X^{+}
has quotient singularities. Thus, (1) and (2) follow.
Lemma
2.10
implies (3).

We now prove (4). Let
\Gamma
be an
\cal F
-invariant divisor such that
(X,\Gamma)
is log canonical. As in the proof of
5.15
(see also Remark
5.16
), we may find a divisor

such that
(X,\Gamma+S^{\prime})
is log canonical
and such that, if

denotes the formal completion of

at
P
then

is the only non log canonical centre of
(U,\Gamma+\sum S_{i})
. Thus, as above, if
\lambda^{\prime}
is the log canonical threshold of
(\widehat{X},\Gamma)
with respect to
S^{\prime}
along

then
(\widehat{X},\Gamma+\lambda^{\prime}S^{\prime})
is log canonical and

-(K_{\widehat{X}^{\prime}}+\Gamma+\lambda^{\prime}S^{\prime})
is ample. It follows by the negativity lemma (cf.
[
KM98
, Lemma 3.38]
) that
(X^{+},\phi_{*}(\Gamma+\lambda^{\prime}S^{\prime}))
is log canonical
and, therefore,
(X^{+},\phi_{*}\Gamma)
is log canonical. Thus, (4) follows.
∎

7.
Termination of flips

The goal of this section is to prove the following:

Theorem 7.1

(Termination of flips)
.

Let
X
be a normal variety and let
\cal F

be a rank one foliation on
X
with canonical singularities.

Then any sequence of
K_{\cal F}
-flips terminates.

We begin with the following

Lemma 7.2
.

Let
X
be a normal variety and let
\cal F

be a rank one foliation on
X
with canonical singularities.
Let
\phi\colon X\dashrightarrow X^{+}
be a
K_{\cal F}
-flip and let
Z^{+}\subset X^{+}
be the flipped locus.

Then
Z^{+}\cap\operatorname{Sing}\,\cal F^{+}=\emptyset
.

Note that the corresponding statement for higher rank foliations, including the absolute case, is easily shown to be false.

Proof.

Suppose not and let
P\in Z^{+}\cap\operatorname{Sing}\,\cal F^{+}
be a closed point. Then Lemma
2.12
implies that
\cal F^{+}
is not terminal near
P
.
Thus, there exists an exceptional divisor
E
over
X
centred at
P
and such that
a(E,\cal F^{+})=0
. The negativity Lemma (cf. Lemma
2.10
) implies that
a(E,\cal F)<0
, a contradiction.
∎

Proposition 7.3

(Special termination)
.

Let
X
be a normal variety and let
\cal F

be a rank one foliation on
X
with canonical singularities.
Let
\phi\colon X\dashrightarrow X^{+}
be a
K_{\cal F}
-flip and let
\cal F^{+}
be the induced foliation on
X^{+}
.
Let

X=X_{0}\dashrightarrow X_{1}\dashrightarrow X_{2}\dashrightarrow\dots

be a sequence of
K_{\cal F}
-flips and let
\cal F_{i}
be the induced foliation on
X_{i}
.

Then, after finitely many flips,
the flipping and flipped locus are disjoint from all the log canonical
centres of
\cal F_{i}
.

Proof.

Let
S_{i}=\operatorname{Sing}\,\cal F_{i}
.
Observe that the sequence of flips induces a sequence
of rational maps

...\dashrightarrow S_{i}\dashrightarrow S_{i+1}\dashrightarrow...

and denote this map
\psi_{i}
. By Lemma
7.2
, it follows that
\psi_{i}

is either an isomorphism
or it contracts some irreducible component of
S_{i}
. Since the latter can
happen only finitely many times, we may assume
that, after finitely many steps, each
\psi_{i}
is an isomorphism.

It remains, therefore, to show that the flipping locus is disjoint
from
S_{i}
. Let
T_{i}\subset S_{i}
denote the intersection
of the flipping locus with
S_{i}
. Observe
that
\psi_{i}(T_{i})\subset S_{i+1}
is contained in the intersection
of the flipped locus with
S_{i+1}
.
But this implies that the flipped locus meets
S_{i+1}
, a contradiction.
∎

Remark 7.4
.

In fact, this argument shows that each flip contracts an entire
component of the singular locus of the foliation, i.e.,
if
Z\subset\operatorname{Sing}\,\cal F
meets the flipping locus
then in fact it is contained in the flipping locus.
This follows from the explicit description
of the flip given in
[
McQ04
]
,
but it is interesting to note that this can also be proven
by a simple discrepancy calculation.

Proof of Theorem
7.1
.

By Lemma
2.12
and Proposition
3.3
,
it follows that if
C\subset X
is a flipping
curve then
C
must meet
\operatorname{Sing}\,\cal F
at some point and, in particular,
it meets a log canonical centre of
\cal F
. Thus,
Proposition
7.3
implies the claim. ∎

8.
Running the MMP

8.1.
Running the MMP with simple singularities

Proposition 8.1
.

Let
X
be a normal projective threefold and let
(\cal F,\Delta)
be a rank one foliated pair on
X
with log canonical singularities and such that
\cal F
admits simple singularities. Assume that
K_{\cal F}+\Delta
is pseudo-effective.

Then
(\cal F,\Delta)
admits a minimal model
\psi\colon X\dashrightarrow Y
. Moreover, if
\cal G\coloneqq\psi_{*}\cal F
and
\Gamma\coloneqq\psi_{*}\Delta
, then the following hold:

(1)

\cal G
admits simple singularities;

(2)

(\cal G,\Gamma)
is log canonical;

(3)

if
\Theta\geq 0
is a
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor on
X
with
\cal F
-invariant support such that
(X,\Theta)
is log canonical, then

(Y,\psi_{*}\Theta)
is log canonical.

Proof.

If
K_{\cal F}+\Delta
is nef then there is nothing to prove, so we may assume that
K_{\cal F}+\Delta
is not nef.
Let
R
be a
(K_{\cal F}+\Delta)
-negative extremal ray. By Theorem
2.36
, we may find
an
\cal F
-invariant curve
C
spanning
R
.

Since
(\cal F,\Delta)
is log canonical, it follows
that no component of
\Delta
is invariant and so
\Delta\cdot C\geq 0
and, in particular,

K_{\cal F}\cdot C<0
.

We may therefore apply Theorem
6.2
and Theorem
6.3
to conclude that the contraction associated to
R
exists and,
if the contraction is small, that the flip exists. Call this step of the MMP
\phi\colon X\dashrightarrow X^{\prime}
and let
\cal F^{\prime}
be the induced foliation on
X^{\prime}
. Theorem
6.2
and Theorem
6.3
imply that
\cal F^{\prime}
has simple singularities and that if
\Theta\geq 0
is a
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor on
X
with
\cal F
-invariant support such that
(X,\Theta)
is log canonical, then

(X^{\prime},\phi_{*}\Theta)
is log canonical.
Moreover, Lemma
2.10
implies that
(\cal F^{\prime},\Delta^{\prime})
is log canonical and
X^{\prime}
is projective. Thus, replacing

X,\Delta
and
\Theta
by
X^{\prime},\phi_{*}\Delta
and
\phi_{*}\Theta
, we may continue this process.

Each divisorial contraction drops the Picard number by one, and so we can only contract a divisor
finitely many times. By Theorem
7.1
we can only have finitely many flips and so
this process must eventually terminate in our desired minimal model.
∎

8.2.
Foliated plt blow ups

In this section, we explain how to perform a foliated analogue of the classical plt blow up. We begin with the following:

Lemma 8.2
.

Let
X
be a normal threefold and let

(\cal F,\Delta)
be a foliated pair on
X
with log canonical singularities.
Let
E
be a valuation which is exceptional over
X
and such that
a(E,\cal F,\Delta)<0
.

Then
a(E,\cal F,\Delta)=-1
.
In particular, if
a(E,\cal F,\Delta)>-1
for any exceptional divisor
E
over
X
then

(\cal F,\Delta)
is canonical.

Proof.

Let
p\colon Y\rightarrow X
be the birational morphism whose existence is guaranteed by Theorem
2.33

and such that
E
is a divisor on
Y
.
Let

\cal F_{Y}\coloneqq p^{-1}\cal F
and let
\Delta_{Y}\coloneqq p^{-1}_{*}\Delta
.
We may write

K_{{\cal F}_{Y}}+\Delta_{Y}+F^{\prime}=p^{*}(K_{\cal F}+\Delta)+F^{\prime\prime}

where
F^{\prime},F^{\prime\prime}\geq 0
are
p
-exceptional
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor with no common components.
After possibly passing to a higher resolution,
we may assume that

(\cal F_{Y},\Delta_{Y}+F)
is log canonical, where
F\coloneqq\sum\epsilon(F_{i})F_{i}
and the sum runs over all the prime
p
-exceptional divisors.

Assume by contradiction that
a(E,\cal F,\Delta)\in(-1,0)
. Since
(\cal F,\Delta)
is log canonical, it follows that
E
is not
\cal F^{\prime}

We begin with the following:

Lemma 10.2

Let

\cal F

Proposition 10.15
.

Set up as above.
Suppose that
\dim Z=0
or
1
.

Then
K_{\cal F}\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}0
.

Proof.

If
\dim X=0

X
is Fano.

If
\dim Z=1
and the general fibre of
f
is
\cal F
-invariant then after performing a base change

(as in the proof

, Lemma 12.15]

10.14
.

If
\cal F

it is easy to see that

. If

10.8
.

\mathbb{Q}
-divisor on

K_{\cal F}
is torsion.
∎

Well-posedness of generalized KdV and one-dimensional fourth-order derivative nonlinear Schrödinger equations for data with an infinite L 2 L^{2} norm
Yufeng Lu

Abstract
. We study the Cauchy problem for the generalized KdV and one-dimensional fourth-order derivative nonlinear Schrödinger equations, for which the global well-posedness of solutions with the small rough data in certain scaling limit of modulation spaces ℳ 2 , 1 μ \mathscr{M}_{2,1}^{\mu} is shown, which contain some data with infinite L 2 L^{2} norm.



2010 Mathematics Subject Classification
35Q55,35G25,42B35,42B37.

1.
Introduction

In this paper, We consider the generalized KdV and fourth-order derivative nonlinear Schrödinger equations on real line as follows.

(1.1)

\displaystyle i\partial_{t}u+\partial_{x}^{4}u=\lambda\partial_{x}\left(u^{m+1}\right),\ u(0,x)=u_{0}(x),

(1.2)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u+\partial_{x}^{3}u=\lambda\partial_{x}(u^{m+1}),\ \ \ u(0,x)=u_{0}(x).

Here
u(t,x)
is a complex-value function of
(t,x)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{1+1},i=\sqrt{-1},\lambda\in{\mathbb{C}},\partial_{t}=\partial/\partial t,\partial_{x}=\partial/\partial x,\partial_{x}^{n}=\partial^{n}/\partial x^{n}
for
n=3,4
.
m
is an integer. We will study the global well-posedness of (
1.1
) and (
1.2
) with small rough data in scaling limit of modulation spaces
\mathscr{M}_{2,1}^{\mu}
.

The modulation space
M_{p,q}^{s}
is one of the function spaces, introduced by Feichtinger
[
6
]
in the 1980s using the short-time Fourier transform to measure the decay and the regularity of the function differently from the usual
L^{p}
Sobolev spaces or Besov-Triebel spaces. Roughly speaking, Besov-Triebel spaces mostly use the dyadic decompositions of the frequency space, while the modulation spaces use the uniform decompositions. Modulation spaces also have many applications in the analysis of partial differential equations. For example, the Schrödinger and wave semigroups, which are not bounded on neither
L^{p}
nor
B_{p,q}^{s}
for
p\neq 2
, are bounded on
M_{p,q}^{s}
(see
[
1
]
). So, the modulation space is a good space for the initial data of the Cauchy problem for nonlinear dispersive equations (see
[
38
,
36
,
34
,
25
,
18
,
26
]
). However, Sugimoto and Tomita in
[
32
]
got the sharp scaling property of
M_{p,q}
. Their result showed that the modulation spaces did not have good scaling properties like
L^{p}
spaces. Therefore, in order to obtain some kinds of modulation spaces with good scaling property, B
\acute{e}
nyi and Oh in
[
2
]
, also Sugimoto and Wang in
[
33
]
introduced the scaling limit of modulation spaces like
\mathfrak{M}_{p,q,\infty}^{\rho}
and
\mathscr{M}_{p,q}^{\mu}
. They studied the basic properties of these spaces such as the scaling property, the dual space, and the algebraic property. And they got some applications in NLS. In this paper, we obtain some other applications of the scaling limit of modulation spaces in nonlinear dispersive equations.

Our main results are as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
m\geqslant 8,p=m/2,A=\frac{3(p-4)}{2(3p+2)},0\leqslant\mu\leqslant\frac{3(m-8)}{2(3m+4)}
. Then there exists
\varepsilon>0
, such that for any
u_{0}\in\mathscr{M}_{2,1}^{\mu}
, with
\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{\mathscr{M}_{2,1}^{\mu}}\leqslant\varepsilon
, then (
1.1
) has a unique global solution
u\in C({\mathbb{R}},\mathscr{M}_{2,1}^{\mu})\cap X^{\mu-A}(L_{x}^{p+2/3}L_{t}^{3p+2})
, where
X^{\mu-A}(L_{x}^{p+2/3}L_{t}^{3p+2})
will be defined in Definition
2.1
. Moreover, the data-to-solution map above is Lipschitz continuous.

Remark 1.2
.

By the inclusion relation between modulation spaces and the scaling limit of modulation spaces (Proposition 3.2 in
[
4
]
), we know that when
0\leqslant\mu\leqslant 3(m-8)/(6m+8)
, we have
M_{2,1}\hookrightarrow\mathscr{M}_{2,1}^{\mu}\hookrightarrow M_{p_{0},1}
, where
p_{0}=(3m+4)/14
. Also, by Proposition
3.1
, when
\mu>0
,
\mathscr{M}_{2,1}^{\mu}\hookrightarrow L^{2}
is not true, which means that there exists some
u_{0}\in\mathscr{M}_{2,1}^{\mu}
with arbitrarily large
L^{2}
norm, such that (
1.1
) is global well-posedness with initial date
u_{0}
. Moreover, by Example
3.2
, the
L^{2}
norm of
u_{0}
could even be infinite, which means that
\mathscr{M}_{2,1}^{\mu}
is
L^{2}
supercritical.

The fourth-order nonlinear Schrödinger equations (4NLS) have been studied by many authors. For equations without derivative on nonlinear term, the well-posedness theory in
H^{s}
has been studied in
[
30
,
31
,
27
,
40
]
. Hayashi et al.
[
12
,
11
,
10
]
studied the well-posedness and scattering theory in weighted Sobolev spaces. For equations with first order derivatives on nonlinear terms (d-4NLS) such as (
1.1
), the scaling invariant homogeneous Sobolev space is
\dot{H}^{s_{c}}
, where
s_{c}=1/2-3/m
. That is to say, for any solution
u(t,x)
of (
1.1
) with initial data
u_{0}
, the scaling function
u_{\lambda}(t,x)=\lambda^{3/m}u(\lambda^{4}t,\lambda x)
is also a solution of (
1.1
) with initial data
u_{0,\lambda}(x)=\lambda^{3/m}u_{0}(\lambda x)
, and satisfies

\displaystyle\left\|u_{0,\lambda}\right\|_{\dot{H}^{s_{c}}}=\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{\dot{H}^{s_{c}}}.

Wang in
[
39
]
proved the global well-posedness of (
1.1
) with small initial data in
H^{s_{c}}
when
m\geqslant 4
. The cases of
m=2,3
and high dimensional cases of (d-4NLS) have been studied by Hirayama and Okamoto in
[
15
]
. The well-posedness theory in weighted Sobolev spaces was studied by Hayashi et al. in
[
14
,
13
]
. For equations with higher order derivatives on nonlinear terms, local well-posedness in
H^{s},s\geqslant 1/2
was obtained by Segata in
[
29
]
. One can also refer to
[
16
,
17
]
. For the high-dimensional case, Hao et al. in
[
8
]
proved local well-posedness in
H^{s},s>d+2+1/2
. Ruzhansky et al. in
[
28
]
obtained the global well-posedness in
M_{2,1}^{s}
for
s\geqslant 3+1/m
.

For the generalized KdV equation (
1.2
), our main result is as follows.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
m\geqslant 4,A=(m-4)/(2m+2),0\leqslant\mu\leqslant A
. Then there exists
\varepsilon>0

u_{0}\in\mathscr{M}_{2,1}^{\mu}

\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{\mathscr{M}_{2,1}^{\mu}}\leqslant\varepsilon
, then (

) has a unique global solution
u\in C({\mathbb{R}},\mathscr{M}_{2,1}^{\mu})\cap X^{\mu-A}(L_{x}^{m+1}L_{t}^{2(m+1)})
. Moreover, the data-to-solution map above is Lipschitz continuous.

.

By the inclusion between modulation spaces and the scaling limit of modulation spaces, we have
M_{2,1}\hookrightarrow\mathscr{M}_{2,1}^{\mu}\hookrightarrow M_{2(m+1)/5,1}
. Therefore, Theorem
1.3
is a generalization of the corresponding result of Theorem 1.2 in
[
35
]
. Meanwhile, the proof of Theorem
1.3
is similar to the proof of Theorem
1.1
. we omit it for simplicity.

By the same scaling argument as for (

), we know that the critical Sobolev spaces of (
1.2
) is
\dot{H}^{s_{c}},s_{c}=1/2-2/m
. Recall that Kenig et al. in
[
20

showed the global well-posedness of (
1.2
) with small initial data in
\dot{H}^{s_{c}}
when
m\geqslant 4
. Later, they proved the ill-posedness of focusing KdV in
H^{s}
with
s<s_{c}
in
[
21
]
. Molinet and Ribaud in
[

]
, extended the global well-posedness result in
\dot{B}_{2,\infty}^{s_{c}}
. Wang and Huang in
[
35
]
proved the global well-posedness of (
1.2
) in
M_{2,1}
, which was the case of
\mu=0
in Theorem

[

2.
Preliminary

2.1.
Notation

We write
\mathscr{S}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
to denote the Schwartz space of all complex-valued rapidly decreasing infinity differentiable functions on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, and
\mathscr{S}^{\prime}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
to denote the dual space of
\mathscr{S}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
, all called the space of all tempered distributions. For simplification, we omit
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
without causing ambiguity. The Fourier transform is defined by
{\mathscr{F}}f(\xi)=\hat{f}(\xi)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}f(x)e^{-ix\xi}d\xi
, and the inverse Fourier transform by
{\mathscr{F}^{-1}}f(x)=(2\pi)^{-d}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}f(\xi)e^{ix\xi}d\xi
. For
1\leqslant p<\infty
, we define the
L^{p}
norm:

\displaystyle\left\|f\right\|_{p}=\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\left|f(x)\right|^{p}dx\right)^{1/p}

and
\left\|f\right\|_{\infty}=\esssup_{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\left|f(x)\right|
.

We use the notation
I\lesssim J
if there is an independent constant C such that
I\leqslant CJ
. Also, we denote
I\approx J
if
I\lesssim J
and
J\lesssim I
. For
1\leqslant p\leqslant\infty
, we denote the dual index
p^{\prime}
with
1/p+1/p^{\prime}=1
. Let
W(t)=e^{it\partial_{x}^{4}}={\mathscr{F}^{-1}}e^{it\xi^{4}}{\mathscr{F}}
, the fourth-order Schrödinger semigroup;
\mathscr{A}f=\int_{0}^{t}W(t-s)f(s)ds.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
(A,\left\|\cdot\right\|_{A})
be a Banach space. For
\mu\in{\mathbb{R}}
, denote

\displaystyle X^{\mu}(A):=\left\{u\in\mathscr{S}^{\prime}({\mathbb{R}}^{2}):u=\sum_{j\leqslant 0}u_{j},\mbox{ with }\sum_{j\leqslant 0}2^{j\mu}\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\left\|\Box_{j,k}u_{j}\right\|_{A}<\infty\right\},

where the norm of this space is as follows.

\displaystyle\left\|u\right\|_{X^{\mu}(A)}:=\inf\sum_{j\leqslant 0}2^{j\mu}\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\left\|\Box_{j,k}u_{j}\right\|_{A},

where the infimum is taken over all the decompositions of
u=\sum_{j\leqslant 0}u_{j}\in X^{\mu}(A)
.

Remark 2.2
.

By the definition above, we could easily obtain
X^{\mu}(A)\hookrightarrow X^{\mu+\varepsilon}(A)
for any
\varepsilon>0
.

2.2.
Modulation spaces and scaling limit of modulation spaces

Recall that the short time Fourier transform of
f
respect to a window function
g\in\mathscr{S}
is defined as (see
[
6
]
):

\displaystyle V_{g}f(x,\xi)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}f(t)\overline{g(t-x)}e^{-it\xi}dt.

And then, for
1\leqslant p,q\leqslant\infty
, we denote

\displaystyle\left\|f\right\|_{M_{p,q}}=\left\|\left\|V_{g}f(x,\xi)\right\|_{L_{x}^{p}}\right\|_{L_{\xi}^{q}}:=\left\|V_{g}f(x,\xi)\right\|_{L_{\xi}^{q}L_{x}^{p}}.

The modulation space
M_{p,q}
is defined as the space of all tempered distributions
f\in\mathscr{S}^{\prime}
for which
\left\|f\right\|_{M_{p,q}}
is finite.

Also, we know another equivalent definition of modulation spaces by uniform decomposition of the frequency space (see
[
7
,
3
]
).

Let
\sigma
be a smooth cut-off function adapted to the unit cube
[-1/2,1/2]^{d}
and
\sigma=0
outside the cube
[-3/4,3/4]^{d}
, we write
\sigma_{k}=\sigma(\cdot-k)
, and assume that

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}\sigma_{k}(\xi)\equiv 1,\ \forall\xi\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}.

Denote
\sigma_{k}(\xi)=\sigma(\xi-k)
, and
\Box_{k}={\mathscr{F}^{-1}}\sigma_{k}{\mathscr{F}}
, then we have the following equivalent norm of modulation space:

\displaystyle\left\|f\right\|_{M_{p,q}}=\left\|\left\|\Box_{k}f\right\|_{L_{x}^{p}}\right\|_{\ell_{k}^{q}({\mathbb{Z}}^{d})}.

Then let us recall the definition of the scaling limit of modulation spaces (see
[
33
]
). Denote
\sigma_{j,k}(\xi)=\sigma(2^{-j}\xi-k)
, and
\Box_{j,k}={\mathscr{F}^{-1}}\sigma_{j,k}{\mathscr{F}}
, then we denote

(2.1)

\left\|f\right\|_{M_{p,q}^{[j]}}:=\left\|\left\|\Box_{j,k}f\right\|_{L_{x}^{p}}\right\|_{\ell_{k}^{q}({\mathbb{Z}}^{d})}.

The scaling limit of modulation spaces
\mathscr{M}_{p,q}^{\mu}
are defined as follows.

\displaystyle\mathscr{M}_{p,q}^{\mu}=\left\{f\in\mathscr{S}^{\prime}:\exists f_{j}\in M_{p,q}^{[j]}\mbox{ such that }f=\sum_{j\leqslant 0}f_{j},\sum_{j\leqslant 0}2^{j\mu}\left\|f_{j}\right\|_{M_{p,q}^{[j]}}<\infty\right\}

and the norm on
\mathscr{M}_{p,q}^{\mu}
is defined as

\displaystyle\left\|f\right\|_{\mathscr{M}_{p,q}^{\mu}}=\inf\sum_{j\leqslant 0}2^{j\mu}\left\|f_{j}\right\|_{M_{p,q}^{[j]}},

where the infimum is taken over all the decompositions of
f=\sum_{j\leqslant 0}f_{j}\in\mathscr{M}_{p,q}^{\mu}.

Remark 2.3
.

By the trivial decomposition
f=f+0+\cdots
, we know
M_{p,q}\hookrightarrow\mathscr{M}_{p,q}^{\mu}
. For this reason, we could regard
\mathscr{M}_{p,q}^{\mu}
as an extension of
M_{p,q}
.

2.3.
Useful lemmas

In this subsection, we gather some useful results.

Lemma 2.4

(Bernstein’s inequality, Lemma 6.1 in
[
37
]
)
.

Let
1\leqslant p\leqslant q\leqslant\infty,R>0,\xi_{0}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
. Denote

L_{B(\xi_{0},R)}^{p}=\left\{f\in L^{p}:\mbox{ supp }\hat{f}\subseteq B(\xi_{0},R)\right\}.

Then there exists
C>0
, such that

\displaystyle\left\|f\right\|_{q}\leqslant CR^{d(1/p-1/q)}\left\|f\right\|_{p}

hold for all
f\in L_{B(\xi_{0},R)}^{p}
and
C
is independent of
R>0
and
\xi_{0}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
.

Lemma 2.5

(Christ–Kiselev, Lemma 2 in
[
24
]
)
.

Let
T
be a linear operator defined in space-time functions
f(t,x)
by

\displaystyle Tf(t)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}K(t,s)f(s)ds,

such that

\displaystyle\left\|Tf\right\|_{L_{x}^{p_{1}}L_{t}^{q_{1}}}\lesssim\left\|f\right\|_{L_{x}^{p_{2}}L_{t}^{q_{2}}},

where
p_{2}\vee q_{2}<p_{1}\wedge q_{1}
. Then

\displaystyle\left\|\int_{0}^{t}K(t,s)f(s)ds\right\|_{L_{x}^{p_{1}}L_{t}^{q_{1}}}\lesssim\left\|f\right\|_{L_{x}^{p_{2}}L_{t}^{q_{2}}}.

Lemma 2.6

(Lemma 4.1 in
[
4
]
)
.

Let
1\leqslant p,q,r\leqslant\infty
. Then
M_{p,q}\hookrightarrow L^{r}
if and only if
p\vee q\leqslant r\leqslant q^{\prime}
.

Lemma 2.7

(Lemma 5.2 in
[
4
]
)
.

Let
1\leqslant p,q,r\leqslant\infty
. Then
M_{p,q}\hookrightarrow{\mathscr{F}}L^{r}
if and only if
p\leqslant 2,q\leqslant r\leqslant p^{\prime}
.

3.
Inclusion between
\mathscr{M}_{p,q}^{\mu}
and
L^{r}

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
1\leqslant p,q,r\leqslant\infty
, denote

\displaystyle a(p,q)

\displaystyle=d(1/p+1/q-1),

\displaystyle\sigma(p,q)

\displaystyle=0\wedge d(1/q-1/p)\wedge a(p,q).

When
\sigma(p,q)\leqslant\mu\leqslant a(p,q),\sigma(p,q)<a(p,q)
, then
\mathscr{M}_{p,q}^{\mu}\hookrightarrow L^{r}
if and only if
p\vee q\leqslant r\leqslant q^{\prime},\mu-d/p\leqslant-d/r.

Sufficiency: for any
f=\sum_{j\leqslant 0}f_{j},f_{j}\in M_{p,q}^{[j]}
. When
p\vee q\leqslant r\leqslant q^{\prime}
, by Lemma
2.6
, we have
M_{p,q}\hookrightarrow L^{r}
. Therefore, when
\mu-d/p+d/r\leqslant 0
, by the triangular inequality, we have

\displaystyle\left\|f\right\|_{r}

\displaystyle\leqslant\sum_{j\leqslant 0}\left\|f_{j}\right\|_{r}\leqslant\sum_{j\leqslant 0}2^{j(\mu-d/p+d/r)}\left\|f_{j}\right\|_{r}

\displaystyle\leqslant\sum_{j\leqslant 0}2^{j(\mu-d/p)}\left\|(f_{j})_{2^{-j}}\right\|_{r}

\displaystyle\leqslant\sum_{j\leqslant 0}2^{j(\mu-d/p)}\left\|(f_{j})_{2^{-j}}\right\|_{M_{p,q}}=\sum_{j\leqslant 0}2^{j\mu}\left\|f_{j}\right\|_{M_{p,q}^{[j]}}.

To take the infimum of the decomposition of
f
, we have
\left\|f\right\|_{r}\lesssim\left\|\mathscr{M}_{p,q}^{\mu}\right\|.

Necessity: if we know
\mathscr{M}_{p,q}^{\mu}\hookrightarrow L^{r}
, then by Remark
2.3
, we have
M_{p,q}\hookrightarrow\mathscr{M}_{p,q}^{\mu}\hookrightarrow L^{r}
. By Lemma
2.6
, we have
p\vee q\leqslant r\leqslant q^{\prime}
. Meanwhile, when
\sigma(p,q)<a(p,q)
, we know that
p,q<\infty
. Then we could use the scaling property of
\mathscr{M}_{p,q}^{\mu}
(See Proposition 5.5 in
[
33
]
).

If we have
\left\|f\right\|_{r}\lesssim\left\|f\right\|_{\mathscr{M}_{p,q}^{\mu}}
, take
f=\varphi_{\lambda}
for some
\varphi\in\mathscr{S},0<\lambda<1
. Then we have

\displaystyle\lambda^{-d/r}\left\|f\right\|_{r}=\left\|f_{\lambda}\right\|_{r}\lesssim\left\|f_{\lambda}\right\|_{\mathscr{M}_{p,q}^{\mu}}\lesssim\lambda^{\mu-d/p}\left\|f\right\|_{\mathscr{M}_{p,q}^{\mu}}.

So, we have
\mu-d/p\leqslant-d/r.

∎

Example 3.2
.

For
\mu>0
, there exists
u\in\mathscr{M}_{2,1}^{\mu}\setminus L^{2}
.

For any
j\leqslant 0,j\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, denote
k_{j}=(j,\cdots,0)\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
. Choose
\varphi\in\mathscr{S}
with
{\mbox{supp}\ }\widehat{\varphi}\subseteq[-1/8,1/8]^{d}
. Take

\displaystyle u=\sum_{j\leqslant 0}\frac{2^{j(d/2-\mu)}}{j^{2}}e^{ik_{j}x}\varphi(2^{j}x).

Then we have

\displaystyle\left\|u\right\|_{2}\geqslant\left\|\Box_{j,k_{j}}u\right\|_{2}\approx\frac{2^{-j\mu}}{j^{2}}.

Let
j\rightarrow-\infty
, we have
\left\|u\right\|_{2}=\infty
.

On the other hand, by the definition of
\mathscr{M}_{2,1}^{\mu}
in Section
2
, we have

\displaystyle\left\|u\right\|_{\mathscr{M}_{2,1}^{\mu}}\leqslant\sum_{j\leqslant 0}2^{j\mu}\left\|\frac{2^{j(d/2-\mu)}}{j^{2}}e^{ik_{j}x}\varphi(2^{j}x)\right\|_{M_{2,1}^{[j]}}\lesssim\sum_{j\leqslant 0}\frac{1}{j^{2}}\lesssim 1.

By a similar method to Proposition
3.1
with Lemma
2.7
, we could also characterize the inclusion between
\mathscr{M}_{p,q}^{\mu}
and
{\mathscr{F}}L^{r}
.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
1\leqslant p,q,r\leqslant\infty,\sigma(p,q)\leqslant\mu\leqslant a(p,q),\sigma(p,q)<a(p,q)
. Then
\mathscr{M}_{p,q}^{\mu}\hookrightarrow{\mathscr{F}}L^{r}
if and only if
q\leqslant r\leqslant p^{\prime},p\leqslant 2,\mu-d/p\leqslant-d/r^{\prime}.

4.
Linear estimates

Lemma 4.1

(
[
19
]
)
.

Denote
D^{s}f={\mathscr{F}^{-1}}\left|\xi\right|^{s}\widehat{f}(\xi)
. Then we have

\displaystyle\left\|D^{3/2}W(t)u\right\|_{L_{x}^{\infty}L_{t}^{2}}\lesssim\left\|u\right\|_{2};

\displaystyle\left\|W(t)u\right\|_{L_{x}^{4}L_{t}^{\infty}}\lesssim\left\|u\right\|_{\dot{H}^{1/4}}.

We will use Lemma
4.1
to derive some smoothing effect estimates and maximal estimates in local frequency spaces, as follows.

Lemma 4.2
.

For any
p\geqslant 4,k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
. We have

\displaystyle\left\|D^{3/2}\Box_{k}W(t)u\right\|_{L_{x}^{\infty}L_{t}^{2}}\lesssim\left\|\Box_{k}u\right\|_{2};

\displaystyle\left\|\Box_{k}W(t)u\right\|_{L_{x}^{p}L_{t}^{\infty}}\lesssim\left\|\Box_{k}u\right\|_{\dot{H}^{1/p}}.

Replace
u
by
\Box_{k}u
in the above estimates, we could get the first estimate and the second estimate with
p=4
.

By Bernstein’s inequality (Lemma
2.4
) and
L^{2}
isometry of
W(t)
, we have

\displaystyle\left\|\Box_{k}W(t)u\right\|_{L_{t,x}^{\infty}}\lesssim\left\|\Box_{k}W(t)u\right\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}}=\left\|\Box_{k}u\right\|_{2}.

Take the interpolation between
p=4
and
p=\infty
, we could get the result as desired.
∎

Proposition 4.3
.

For any
p\geqslant 4,k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, we have

\displaystyle\left\|\Box_{k}W(t)u\right\|_{L_{x}^{p+2/3}L_{t}^{3p+2}}\lesssim\left\|\Box_{k}u\right\|_{2};

\displaystyle\left\|\Box_{k}W(t)\partial_{x}u\right\|_{L_{x}^{3p+2}L_{t}^{(3p+2)/(p+1)}}\lesssim\left\|\Box_{k}u\right\|_{2}.

Take the interpolation of the two estimates in Lemma
4.2
, we have

\displaystyle\left\|D^{\alpha}\Box_{k}W(t)u\right\|_{L_{x}^{a}L_{t}^{b}}\lesssim\left\|\Box_{k}u\right\|_{\dot{H}^{s}},

when

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} \alpha&=\frac{3(1-\theta)}{2}+0\cdot\theta,\\
s&=0(1-\theta)+\theta/p,\\
\frac{1}{a}&=\frac{1-\theta}{\infty}+\frac{\theta}{p},\\
\frac{1}{b}&=\frac{1-\theta}{2}+\frac{\theta}{\infty},\end{aligned}\right.

which is equivalent to

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} \theta&=p/a,\\
\alpha&=3/b,\\
s&=1/a,\\
1&=\frac{p}{a}+\frac{2}{b}.\end{aligned}\right.

Take
\alpha=s
into the equations above, we have
a=p+2/3,b=3p+2
, which is the first estimate in the proposition. Also, taking
\alpha-s=1
, we can obtain the second estimate as desired.
∎

By the duality argument, we could get the following estimate of
\mathscr{A}\partial_{x}f
.

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
p\geqslant 4,k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
. Then we have

\displaystyle\left\|\Box_{k}\mathscr{A}\partial_{x}f\right\|_{L_{x}^{p+2/3}L_{t}^{3p+2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|\Box_{k}f\right\|_{L_{x}^{\left(3p+2\right)^{\prime}}L_{t}^{\left((3p+2)/(p+1)\right)^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle=\left\|\Box_{k}f\right\|_{L_{x}^{\frac{3p+2}{3p+1}}L_{t}^{(3p+2)/(2p+1)}}.

By the Christ-Kiselev lemma (Lemma
2.5
), we only need to prove the following estimate.

\displaystyle\left\|\Box_{k}\int W(t-s)\partial_{x}f(s)ds\right\|_{L_{x}^{p+2/3}L_{t}^{3p+2}}\lesssim\left\|\Box_{k}f\right\|_{L_{x}^{\left(3p+2\right)^{\prime}}L_{t}^{\left((3p+2)/(p+1)\right)^{\prime}}}.

\displaystyle\left\|\Box_{k}\int W(t-s)\partial_{x}f(s)ds\right\|_{L_{x}^{p+2/3}L_{t}^{3p+2}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\sup_{\left\|g\right\|_{L_{x}^{\left(p+2/3\right)^{\prime}}L_{t}^{\left(3p+2\right)^{\prime}}}\leqslant 1}\left\|\Box_{k}\int W(-s)\partial_{x}f(s)ds\right\|_{2}\sum_{\left|\ell\right|\leqslant 1}\left\|\Box_{k+\ell}\int W(-t)g(t)dt\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|\Box_{k}f\right\|_{L_{x}^{\left(3p+2\right)^{\prime}}L_{t}^{\left((3p+2)/(p+1)\right)^{\prime}}},

where the last inequality is the dual version of the estimate in Proposition

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
0<p,r,p_{1},r_{1},q\leqslant\infty,0<\alpha<\infty,k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
. If we have

\displaystyle\left\|D^{\alpha}\Box_{k}W(t)u\right\|_{L_{x}^{p}L_{t}^{r}}\lesssim\left\|\Box_{k}u\right\|_{q},

\displaystyle\left\|D^{\alpha}\Box_{k}\mathscr{A}f\right\|_{L_{x}^{p}L_{t}^{r}}\lesssim\left\|\Box_{k}f\right\|_{L_{x}^{p_{1}}L_{t}^{r_{1}}}.

Then for any
j\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, we have

\displaystyle\left\|D^{\alpha}\Box_{j,k}W(t)u\right\|_{L_{x}^{p}L_{t}^{r}}\lesssim 2^{j\delta(p,r,q,\alpha)}\left\|\Box_{j,k}u\right\|_{q},

\displaystyle\left\|D^{\alpha}\Box_{j,k}\mathscr{A}f\right\|_{L_{x}^{p}L_{t}^{r}}\lesssim 2^{j\tau(p,r,p_{1},r_{1},\alpha)}\left\|\Box_{j,k}f\right\|_{L_{x}^{p_{1}}L_{t}^{r_{1}}},

where

\displaystyle\delta(p,r,q,\alpha)

\displaystyle=\alpha-4/r-1/p+1/q,

\displaystyle\tau(p,r,p_{1},r_{1},\alpha)

\displaystyle=\alpha-4-4/r-1/p+4/r_{1}+1/p_{1}.

The proof is based on scaling. For simplicity, we only give the proof of
\mathscr{A}f
.

By definition of
D^{\alpha},\Box_{j,k},W(t)
and change of variable, we have

\displaystyle D^{\alpha}\Box_{j,k}\mathscr{A}f(x,t)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}{\mathscr{F}^{-1}}\left|\xi\right|^{\alpha}\sigma(2^{-j}\xi-k)e^{i(t-s)\left|\xi\right|^{4}}\widehat{f}(\xi,s)(x)ds

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}{\mathscr{F}^{-1}}2^{j\alpha}\left|\xi\right|^{\alpha}\sigma(\xi-k)e^{i4^{j}(t-s)\left|\xi\right|^{4}}2^{jd}\widehat{f}(2^{j}\xi,s)(2^{j}x)ds

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{2^{4j}t}{\mathscr{F}^{-1}}2^{j\alpha}\left|\xi\right|^{\alpha}\sigma(\xi-k)e^{i(2^{4j}t-s)\left|\xi\right|^{4}}2^{jd}2^{-4j}\widehat{f}(2^{j}\xi,2^{-4j}s)(2^{j}x)ds

\displaystyle=2^{j\left(\alpha-4\right)}D^{\alpha}\Box_{k}\mathscr{A}f_{2^{-j}}(2^{j}x,2^{4j}t),

where
f_{2^{-j}}(x,t)=f(2^{-j}x,2^{-4j}t)
. Similarly, by change of variable, we have
\Box_{k}f_{2^{-j}}(x,t)=\Box_{j,k}f(2^{-j}x,2^{-4j}t)
.
Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\left\|D^{\alpha}\Box_{j,k}\mathscr{A}f\right\|_{L_{x}^{p}L_{t}^{r}}

\displaystyle=\left\|2^{j\left(\alpha-4\right)}D^{\alpha}\Box_{k}\mathscr{A}f_{2^{-j}}(2^{j}x,2^{4j}t)\right\|_{L_{x}^{p}L_{t}^{r}}

\displaystyle=2^{j\left(\alpha-4\right)}2^{-4j/r}2^{-j/p}\left\|D^{\alpha}\Box_{k}\mathscr{A}f_{2^{-j}}\right\|_{L_{x}^{p}L_{t}^{r}}

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{j(\left(\alpha-4-1/p-4/r\right))}\left\|\Box_{k}f_{2^{-j}}\right\|_{L_{x}^{p_{1}}L_{t}^{r_{1}}}

\displaystyle=2^{j\tau(p,r,p_{1},r_{1},\alpha)}\left\|\Box_{j,k}f\right\|_{L_{x}^{p_{1}}L_{t}^{r_{1}}}.

∎

5.
Nonlinear estimates

In this section, we give the nonlinear estimate of the work space
X^{\mu}(L_{x}^{p}L_{t}^{\gamma})
. First, we need two lemmas.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
\ell\leqslant j\leqslant 0,1\leqslant p,\gamma\leqslant\infty
. Then we have

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\left\|\Box_{j,k}u\right\|_{L_{x}^{p}L_{t}^{\gamma}}\lesssim\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\left\|\Box_{\ell,k}u\right\|_{L_{x}^{p}L_{t}^{\gamma}}.

For any
k_{1}\in{\mathbb{Z}},
denote
\wedge_{k_{1},j}=\left\{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}:\Box_{\ell,k}\Box_{j,k_{1}}\neq 0\right\}
. Then we know that

\displaystyle\Box_{j,k_{1}}u=\sum_{k\in\wedge_{k_{1},j}}\Box_{\ell,k}\Box_{j,k_{1}}u.

Meanwhile,
\left\{\wedge_{k_{1},j}\right\}_{k_{1}\in{\mathbb{Z}}}
are finitely overlapped. In fact, if there exists
k\in\wedge_{k_{1},j}\cap\wedge_{k_{2},j}
, then we have
\left|2^{j}k_{1}-2^{j}k_{2}\right|\leqslant 2^{j}+2^{\ell}
, which means that
\left|k_{1}-k_{2}\right|\lesssim 1
.

Therefore, by the triangular inequality, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{k_{1}\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\left\|\Box_{j,k_{1}}u\right\|_{L_{x}^{p}L_{t}^{\gamma}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\sum_{k_{1}\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\sum_{k\in\wedge_{k_{1},j}}\left\|\Box_{\ell,k}\Box_{j,k_{1}}u\right\|_{L_{x}^{p}L_{t}^{\gamma}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{k_{1}\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\sum_{k\in\wedge_{k_{1},j}}\left\|\Box_{\ell,k}u\right\|_{L_{x}^{p}L_{t}^{\gamma}}\lesssim\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\left\|\Box_{\ell,k}\right\|_{L_{x}^{p}L_{t}^{\gamma}}.

∎

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
1\leqslant p_{1}\leqslant\gamma\leqslant p\leqslant\infty,\mu\in{\mathbb{R}}
. Then we have

X^{\mu+1/p_{1}}(L_{x}^{p_{1}}L_{t}^{\gamma})\hookrightarrow X^{\mu+1/p}(L_{x}^{p}L_{t}^{\gamma}).

For any
u=\sum_{j\leqslant 0}u_{j}
, by Definition
2.1
, Minkowski’s inequality and Bernstein’s inequality (Lemma
2.4
), we have

\displaystyle\left\|u\right\|_{X^{\mu+1/p}(L_{x}^{p}L_{t}^{\gamma})}

\displaystyle\leqslant\sum_{j\leqslant 0}2^{j(\mu+1/p)}\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\left\|\Box_{j,k}u\right\|_{L_{x}^{p}L_{t}^{\gamma}}\leqslant\sum_{j\leqslant 0}2^{j(\mu+1/p)}\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\left\|\Box_{j,k}u\right\|_{L_{t}^{\gamma}L_{x}^{p}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\sum_{j\leqslant 0}2^{j(\mu+1/p_{1})}\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\left\|\Box_{j,k}u\right\|_{L_{t}^{\gamma}L_{x}^{p_{1}}}\leqslant\sum_{j\leqslant 0}2^{j(\mu+1/p_{1})}\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\left\|\Box_{j,k}u\right\|_{L_{x}^{p_{1}}L_{t}^{\gamma}}.

So, we have

\displaystyle\left\|u\right\|_{X^{\mu+1/p}(L_{x}^{p}L_{t}^{\gamma})}\leqslant\left\|u\right\|_{X^{\mu+1/p_{1}}(L_{x}^{p_{1}}L_{t}^{\gamma})},

which is equivalent to
X^{\mu+1/p_{1}}(L_{x}^{p_{1}}L_{t}^{\gamma})\hookrightarrow X^{\mu+1/p}(L_{x}^{p}L_{t}^{\gamma})
.
∎

Proposition 5.3
.

Let
1\leqslant p,p_{1},p_{2},\gamma,\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}\leqslant\infty,\mu\in{\mathbb{R}}
, satisfying

\displaystyle\frac{1}{p}=\frac{1}{p_{1}}+\frac{1}{p_{2}},\quad\frac{1}{\gamma}=\frac{1}{\gamma_{1}}+\frac{1}{\gamma_{2}}.

Then we have

\displaystyle\left\|uv\right\|_{X^{\mu}(L_{x}^{p}L_{t}^{\gamma})}\lesssim\left\|u\right\|_{X^{\mu}(L_{x}^{p_{1}}L_{t}^{\gamma_{1}})}\left\|v\right\|_{X^{0}(L_{x}^{p_{2}}L_{t}^{\gamma_{2}})}+\left\|u\right\|_{X^{0}(L_{x}^{p_{1}}L_{t}^{\gamma_{1}})}\left\|v\right\|_{X^{\mu}(L_{x}^{p_{2}}L_{t}^{\gamma_{2}})}.

The proof is based on the proof of Lemma 8.8 in
[
33
]
.

For any
u=\sum_{j\leqslant 0}u_{j},v=\sum_{\ell\leqslant 0}v_{\ell}
, we have

\displaystyle uv=\sum_{j,\ell\leqslant 0}u_{j}v_{\ell}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\leqslant 0}\sum_{\ell\leqslant j}u_{j}v_{\ell}+\sum_{\ell\leqslant 0}\sum_{j<\ell}u_{j}v_{\ell}

\displaystyle:=I+II.

By the triangular inequality, we have

(5.1)

\displaystyle\left\|uv\right\|_{X^{\mu}(L_{x}^{p}L_{t}^{\gamma})}\leqslant\left\|I\right\|_{X^{\mu}(L_{x}^{p}L_{t}^{\gamma})}+\left\|II\right\|_{X^{\mu}(L_{x}^{p}L_{t}^{\gamma})}.

By Definition
2.1
and the triangular inequality, we have

\displaystyle\left\|I\right\|_{X^{\mu}(L_{x}^{p}L_{t}^{\gamma})}

\displaystyle\leqslant\sum_{j\leqslant 0}2^{j\mu}\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\left\|\Box_{j,k}\left(\sum_{\ell\leqslant j}u_{j}v_{\ell}\right)\right\|_{L_{x}^{p}L_{t}^{\gamma}}

(5.2)

\displaystyle\leqslant\sum_{j\leqslant 0}2^{j\mu}\sum_{\ell\leqslant j}\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\left\|\Box_{j,k}(u_{j}v_{\ell})\right\|_{L_{x}^{p}L_{t}^{\gamma}}

Take decomposition of
u_{j}=\sum_{k_{1}\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\Box_{j,k_{1}}u_{j},v_{\ell}=\sum_{k_{2}\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\Box_{j,k_{2}}v\ell
, by the orthogonality of
\Box_{j,k}\left(\Box_{j,k_{1}}u_{j}\Box_{j,k_{2}}v_{\ell}\right)
, we have

(
5
)

\displaystyle\leqslant\sum_{j\leqslant 0}2^{j\mu}\sum_{\ell\leqslant j}\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\sum_{k_{1},k_{2}\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\left\|\Box_{j,k}(\Box_{j,k_{1}}u_{j}\Box_{j,k_{2}}v_{\ell})\right\|_{L_{x}^{p}L_{t}^{\gamma}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\sum_{j\leqslant 0}2^{j\mu}\sum_{\ell\leqslant j}\sum_{k_{1},k_{2}\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\sum_{k:\left|k-k_{1}-k_{2}\right|\lesssim 1}\left\|\Box_{j,k}(\Box_{j,k_{1}}u_{j}\Box_{j,k_{2}}v_{\ell})\right\|_{L_{x}^{p}L_{t}^{\gamma}}

(5.3)

\displaystyle\leqslant\sum_{j\leqslant 0}2^{j\mu}\sum_{\ell\leqslant j}\sum_{k_{1},k_{2}\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\left\|\Box_{j,k_{1}}u_{j}\Box_{j,k_{2}}v_{\ell}\right\|_{L_{x}^{p}L_{t}^{\gamma}}

By Hölder’s inequality and Lemma
5.1
, we have

(
5
)

\displaystyle\leqslant\sum_{j\leqslant 0}2^{j\mu}\sum_{\ell\leqslant j}\sum_{k_{1},k_{2}\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\left\|\Box_{j,k_{1}}u_{j}\right\|_{L_{x}^{p_{1}}L_{t}^{\gamma_{1}}}\left\|\Box_{j,k_{2}}v_{\ell}\right\|_{L_{x}^{p_{2}}L_{t}^{\gamma_{2}}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\leqslant 0}2^{j\mu}\sum_{k_{1}\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\left\|\Box_{j,k_{1}}u_{j}\right\|_{L_{x}^{p_{1}}L_{t}^{\gamma_{1}}}\sum_{\ell\leqslant j}\sum_{k_{2}\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\left\|\Box_{j,k_{2}}v_{\ell}\right\|_{L_{x}^{p_{2}}L_{t}^{\gamma_{2}}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\sum_{j\leqslant 0}2^{j\mu}\sum_{k_{1}\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\left\|\Box_{j,k_{1}}u_{j}\right\|_{L_{x}^{p_{1}}L_{t}^{\gamma_{1}}}\sum_{\ell\leqslant j}\sum_{k_{2}\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\left\|\Box_{\ell,k_{2}}v_{\ell}\right\|_{L_{x}^{p_{2}}L_{t}^{\gamma_{2}}}.

Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\left\|I\right\|_{X^{\mu}(L_{x}^{p}L_{t}^{\gamma})}\lesssim\left\|u\right\|_{X^{\mu}(L_{x}^{p_{1}}L_{t}^{\gamma_{1}})}\left\|v\right\|_{X^{0}(L_{x}^{p_{2}}L_{t}^{\gamma_{2}})}.

By the same method, we also have

\displaystyle\left\|II\right\|_{X^{\mu}(L_{x}^{p}L_{t}^{\gamma})}\lesssim\left\|u\right\|_{X^{0}(L_{x}^{p_{1}}L_{t}^{\gamma_{1}})}\left\|v\right\|_{X^{\mu}(L_{x}^{p_{2}}L_{t}^{\gamma_{2}})}.

Combine these two estimates, we could get the estimate as desired in the proposition.

Corollary 5.4
.

Let
1\leqslant m\in{\mathbb{N}},1\leqslant p,p_{i},\gamma,\gamma_{i}\leqslant\infty

. If

\displaystyle\frac{1}{p}=\sum_{i=1}^{m+1}\frac{1}{p_{i}},\quad\frac{1}{\gamma}=\sum_{i=1}^{m+1}\frac{1}{\gamma_{i}}.

Then we have

\displaystyle\left\|u^{m+1}\right\|_{X^{0}(L_{x}^{p}L_{t}^{\gamma})}\lesssim\prod_{i=1}^{m+1}\left\|u\right\|_{X^{0}(L_{x}^{p_{i}}L_{t}^{\gamma_{i}})}.

Applying this corollary to the work space

we could get the estimate as follows.

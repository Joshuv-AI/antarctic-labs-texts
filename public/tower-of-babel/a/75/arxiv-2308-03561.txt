Bidiagonal matrix factorisations associated with symmetric multiple orthogonal polynomials and lattice paths
Hélder Lima

Abstract
The central object of study in this paper are infinite banded Hessenberg matrices admitting factorisations as products of bidiagonal matrices. In the two main novel results of this paper, we show that these Hessenberg matrices are associated with the decomposition of ( r + 1 ) (r+1) -fold symmetric r r -orthogonal polynomials and are the production matrices of the generating polynomials of r r -Dyck paths. We combine the aforementioned bidiagonal matrix factorisations and the recently found connection of multiple orthogonal polynomials with lattice paths and branched continued fractions to study ( r + 1 ) (r+1) -fold symmetric r r -orthogonal polynomials on a star-like set of the complex plane and their decomposition via multiple orthogonal polynomials on the positive real line. As an explicit example, we give formulas as terminating hypergeometric series for the Appell sequences of ( r + 1 ) (r+1) -fold symmetric r r -orthogonal polynomials on a star-like set and show that the densities of their orthogonality measures can be expressed via Meijer G-functions on the positive real line.



Keywords:

Hessenberg matrices, bidiagonal matrices, multiple orthogonal polynomials,
m
-fold symmetry, production matrices, lattice paths.

1
Introduction

The central object of study in this paper are infinite matrices
\mathrm{H}^{(r;j)}
, with
r\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
and
0\leq j\leq r
, admitting factorisations

\mathrm{H}^{(r;j)}=\mathrm{L}_{j+1}\cdots\mathrm{L}_{r}\,\mathrm{U}\,\mathrm{L}_{1}\cdots\mathrm{L}_{j},

(1.1)

as products of
r
lower-bidiagonal matrices
\mathrm{L}_{1},\cdots,\mathrm{L}_{r}
and an upper-bidiagonal matrix
\mathrm{U}
of the form

\mathrm{L}_{k}=\begin{bmatrix}1\\
\alpha_{k}&1\\
&\alpha_{r+1+k}&1\\
&&\ddots&\ddots\end{bmatrix}\text{ for }1\leq k\leq r,\quad\text{and}\quad\mathrm{U}=\begin{bmatrix}\alpha_{0}&1\\
&\alpha_{r+1}&1\\
&&\alpha_{2(r+1)}&1\\
&&&\ddots&\ddots\end{bmatrix},

(1.2)

whose nontrivial entries are given by a sequence
\left(\alpha_{n}\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
. The matrices
\mathrm{H}^{(r;j)}
are
(r+2)
-banded unit-lower-Hessenberg matrices, i.e., all their nonzero entries are located on the supradiagonal, the main diagonal, and the first
r
subdiagonals, with all entries on the supradiagonal equal to
1
.

(Positive) bidiagonal factorisations of Hessenberg matrices (or, more generally, banded matrices) associated with (mixed) multiple orthogonal polynomials is a timely topic of research, as it can be seen in
[
8
,
6
,
7
]
.

In the two main novel results of this paper, both presented in Section
3
, we show that the banded Hessenberg matrices satisfying the bidiagonal factorisations (
1.1
) are the recurrence matrices for the components of the decomposition of
(r+1)
-fold symmetric
r
-orthogonal polynomials and are the production matrices of the generating polynomials of partial
r
-Dyck paths.
Using these results, we can apply the connection of multiple orthogonal polynomials with lattice paths and branched continued fractions, introduced in
[
34
]
and further explored in
[
24
]
, to study
(r+1)
-fold symmetric
r
-orthogonal polynomials.
In Section
4
, we revisit known results about the location of the zeros of these polynomials and their multiple orthogonality with respect to measures supported on a star-like set of the complex plane, give combinatorial interpretations for the moments of their dual sequence, find orthogonality measures on the positive real line for the components of their decomposition, and present an explicit example of
(r+1)
-fold symmetric
r
-orthogonal polynomials. The latter form Appell polynomial sequences, can be represented as terminating hypergeometric series, and satisfy orthogonality conditions with respect to measures that can be expressed via Meijer G-functions.

2
Background

2.1
Multiple orthogonality and
m
-fold symmetry

Multiple orthogonal polynomials are a generalisation of conventional orthogonal polynomials, satisfying orthogonality relations with respect to several measures instead of just one.
We give a brief introduction to this topic, presenting some basic definitions and notation that we use throughout the paper.
See
[
22
, Ch. 23]
,
[
28
, Ch. 4]
, and
[
36
, §3]
for more information about multiple orthogonal polynomials.

There are two types of multiple orthogonal polynomials: type I and type II.
Both type I and type II polynomials satisfy orthogonality conditions with respect to a system of
r
measures (or linear functionals), for a positive integer
r
, and depend on a multi-index
\vec{n}=\left(n_{1},\cdots,n_{r}\right)\in\mathbb{N}^{r}
of norm
|\vec{n}|=n_{1}+\cdots+n_{r}
.
Here and always throughout this paper, we consider
0\in\mathbb{N}
.
Both type I and type II polynomials reduce to conventional orthogonal polynomials when the number of orthogonality measures (or functionals) is equal to
1
.

In this paper we focus only on type II multiple orthogonal polynomials for multi-indices on the so-called step-line.
A multi-index
\left(n_{1},\cdots,n_{r}\right)\in\mathbb{N}^{r}
is on the
step-line
if
n_{1}\geq n_{2}\geq\cdots\geq n_{r}\geq n_{1}-1
.
For a fixed
r\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
, there is a unique multi-index of norm
n
on the step-line of
\mathbb{N}^{r}
for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
:
if
n=rk+j
with
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
0\leq j\leq r-1
, then the multi-index of norm
n
on the step-line is of the form

\vec{n}=\left(k+1,\cdots,k+1,k,\cdots,k\right)
, with
j
entries equal to
k+1
and
r-j
entries equal to
k
.

The degree of a type II multiple orthogonal polynomial is equal to the norm of its multi-index.
Therefore, the type II polynomials on the step-line form a sequence with exactly one polynomial of degree
n
, for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, and we can replace the multi-index by the degree of the polynomial without any ambiguity.

The type II multiple orthogonal polynomials on the step-line of
\mathbb{N}^{r}
are often referred to as
r
-orthogonal polynomials
[
27
]
, where
r
is the number of orthogonality measures (or functionals).
A polynomial sequence
\left(P_{n}(x)\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is
r
-orthogonal
, i.e.,
\left(P_{n}(x)\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is the sequence of type II multiple orthogonal polynomials on the step-line, with respect to a system of measures
\left(\mu_{1},\cdots,\mu_{r}\right)
if it satisfies

\int x^{k}P_{n}(x)\mathrm{d}\mu_{j}(x)=\begin{cases}N_{n}\neq 0&\text{ if }n=rk+j-1,\\
\hfil 0&\text{ if }n\geq rk+j,\end{cases}\quad\text{for all }1\leq j\leq r.

(2.1)

Multiple orthogonality conditions can also be written using linear functionals in a generic field
\mathbb{K}
instead of measures on
\mathbb{R}
or
\mathbb{C}
.
A linear functional on
\mathbb{K}[x]
is a linear map
u:\mathbb{K}[x]\to\mathbb{K}
.
The action of a linear functional
u
on a polynomial
f\in\mathbb{K}[x]
is denoted by
u\left[f\right]
.
For
n\in\mathbb{N}
, the moment of order
n
of a linear functional
u
is
u\left[x^{n}\right]
.
By linearity, every linear functional
u
is uniquely determined by its moments. A polynomial sequence
\left(P_{n}(x)\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is
r
-orthogonal with respect to a system of linear functionals
\left(v_{1},\cdots,v_{r}\right)
if (
2.1
) holds with
\displaystyle\int{x^{k}P_{n}(x)\mathrm{d}\mu_{j}(x)}
replaced by
v_{j}\left[x^{k}\,P_{n}(x)\right]
for all values of
k,n,j
.

A sequence of linear functionals
\left(u_{k}\right)_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
is the
dual sequence
of a polynomial sequence
\left(P_{n}(x)\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
if

u_{k}\left[P_{n}\right]=\delta_{k,n}=\begin{cases}1&\text{if }k=n,\\
0&\text{if }k\neq n.\end{cases}

(2.2)

If
\left(P_{n}(x)\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a
r
-orthogonal polynomial sequence with dual sequence
\left(u_{k}\right)_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
, then
\left(P_{n}(x)\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is
r
-orthogonal with respect to
\left(u_{0},\cdots,u_{r-1}\right)
.
Based on
[
27
, Thm. 2.1]
, a polynomial sequence
\left(P_{n}(x)\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is
r
-orthogonal if and only if it satisfies a
(r+1)
-order recurrence relation of the form

P_{n+1}(x)=x\,P_{n}(x)-\sum_{k=0}^{\min(n,r)}\gamma_{n-k}^{\,[k]}\,P_{n-k}(x).

(2.3)

The recurrence coefficients in (
2.3
) are collected in the infinite
(r+2)
-banded unit-lower-Hessenberg matrix
\mathrm{H}=\left(h_{n,k}\right)_{n,k\in\mathbb{N}}
, whose nonzero entries are

h_{n,n+1}=1
and
h_{n+k,n}=\gamma^{[k]}_{n}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
0\leq k\leq r
.
The
r
-orthogonal polynomials
\left(P_{n}(x)\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
are the characteristic polynomials of the
(n\times n)
-matrices
\mathrm{H}_{n}
formed by the first
n
rows and columns of
\mathrm{H}
(see
[
11
, §2.2]
), i.e.,
P_{n}(x)=\det\left(x\,\mathrm{I}_{n}-\mathrm{H}_{n}\right)

for any
n\geq 1
,
where
\mathrm{I}_{n}
denotes the
(n\times n)
-identity matrix.

For
m\geq 2
, a polynomial sequence
\left(P_{n}(x)\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is
m
-fold symmetric
if

P_{n}\left(\mathrm{e}^{\frac{2\pi i}{m}}x\right)=\mathrm{e}^{\frac{2n\pi i}{m}}P_{n}(x)\quad\text{for all }n\in\mathbb{N}.

(2.4)

Observe that, for
m=2
, (
2.4
) corresponds to the definition of a symmetric polynomial sequence.

The study of multiple orthogonal polynomial sequences satisfying (
2.4
) goes back to works of Douak and Maroni (see, e.g.,
[
17
]
), where a
d
-symmetric sequence corresponds to what we call here a
(d+1)
-fold symmetric sequence.
The terminology of
m
-fold symmetry in the context of multiple orthogonality was introduced in
[
25
]
.

The definition (
2.4
) is equivalent to the existence of
m
polynomial sequences
\displaystyle\left(P_{n}^{[j]}(x)\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
,
0\leq j\leq m-1
, such that

P_{mn+j}(x)=x^{j}\,P^{[j]}_{n}\left(x^{m}\right)\quad\text{for all }n\in\mathbb{N}\text{ and }0\leq j\leq m-1.

(2.5)

Moreover, for
r\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
,
\left(P_{n}(x)\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a
(r+1)
-fold symmetric
r
-orthogonal polynomial sequence if and only if it satisfies a three-term recurrence relation of order
r+1
of the form

P_{n+r+1}(x)=x\,P_{n+r}(x)-\alpha_{n}\,P_{n}(x)\quad\text{for all }n\in\mathbb{N},

(2.6)

with
\alpha_{n}\neq 0
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
and initial conditions
P_{j}(x)=x^{j}
for
0\leq j\leq r
(see
[
17
, Thm. 5.1]
).

The corresponding Hessenberg matrix, which we denote by
\mathrm{H}^{(r)}=\left(h^{(r)}_{m,n}\right)_{n,k\in\mathbb{N}}
, only has nonzero entries in the supradiagonal and in the
r^{\text{th}}
-subdiagonal, respectively equal to
1
and to the elements of
\left(\alpha_{n}\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
, i.e.,

h_{n,n+1}=1\quad\text{and}\quad h_{n+r,n}=\alpha_{n}\quad\text{for all }n\in\mathbb{N},\quad\text{and}\quad h_{m,n}=0\text{ whenever }m-n\not\in\{-1,r\}.

(2.7)

It is known that a sequence
\left(P_{n}(x)\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
satisfying (
2.6
) with
\alpha_{n}>0
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, is
r
-orthogonal with respect to a vector of positive measures
\left(\mu_{1},\cdots,\mu_{r}\right)
supported on the star-like set

\mathrm{St}^{(r+1)}=\bigcup\limits_{k=0}^{r}\left\{\left.x\,\mathrm{e}^{\frac{2\pi ik}{r+1}}\right|x\geq 0\right\},

(2.8)

and uniquely determined by their densities on the positive real line.
This result was implicitly obtained in
[
3
]
, although the set
\mathrm{St}^{(r+1)}
is not mentioned therein (see
[
35
, Eq. 2.15]
and
[
2
, Thm. 1.1]
for explicit statements of this result).
Furthermore, it was shown in
[
5
, Thm. 2.2]
that the zeros of a sequence
\left(P_{n}(x)\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
satisfying (
2.6
) with
\alpha_{n}>0
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
are all located on
\mathrm{St}^{(r+1)}
, are invariant under rotations of angle
\frac{2k\pi}{r+1}
for
1\leq k\leq r
, and satisfy interlacing properties that we revisit in Subsection
4.1
.

We refer to
\mathrm{St}^{(r+1)}
as the
(r+1)
-star.
Note that the “

2.2
Lattice paths and production matrices

We are interested in two types of lattice paths,
r
-Dyck paths and
r
-Lukasiewick paths, where
r
is a positive integer, and on generating polynomials of sets of these lattice paths.
We follow the definitions in
[
30
,
29
]
.

For
r\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
, a
r
-Dyck path
is a path in the lattice
\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{N}
, starting and ending at height
0
, only using steps
(1,1)
, called
rises
, and
(1,-r)
, called
r
-falls
.
When
r=1
, the
1
-Dyck paths are simply known as Dyck paths.
More generally, a
partial
r
-Dyck path
is a path in
\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{N}
allowed to start and end anywhere, again only using steps
(1,1)
and
(1,-r)
.

For
r\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}\cup\{\infty\}
, a
r
-Lukasiewick path
is a path in
\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{N}
, starting and ending at height
0
, only using steps
(1,j)
with
-r\leq j\leq 1
.
A
1
-Lukasiewick path is called a
Motzkin path
and a
\infty
-Lukasiewick path is simply called a
Lukasiewick path
.
Likewise for
r
-Dyck paths, we can consider
partial
r
-Lukasiewick paths
, which are paths in
\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{N}
, only using steps
(1,j)
with
-r\leq j\leq 1
and allowed to start and end anywhere.
Observe that a (partial)
r
-Lukasiewick path without any steps
(1,j)
with
1-r\leq j\leq 0
is a (partial)
r
-Dyck path.

In Fig.
2
, we give examples of a
r
-Dyck path and a
r
-Lukasiewick path, with
r=2
and
r=3
, respectively.

Figure 2:
A
2
-Dyck path of length
12
(left) and a
3
-Lukasiewick path of length
13
(right).

Throughout this paper and as usual, the weight of a lattice path is the product of the weights of its steps and the generating polynomial of a set of lattice paths is the sum of the weights of the paths in the set.

Observe that the length of a
r
-Dyck path is always a multiple of
r+1
.
For any
r\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
and a sequence
\left(\alpha_{i}\right)_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
, the
r
-Stieltjes-Rogers polynomials

S_{n}^{\,(r)}\left(\mathbf{\alpha}\right)
, with
n\in\mathbb{N}
, are the generating polynomials of the sets of
r
-Dyck paths of length
(r+1)n
, where each rise has weight
1
and each
r
-fall to height
i
has weight
\alpha_{i}
.
Analogously, for
r\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}\cup\{\infty\}
and sequences
\left(\gamma_{i}^{[j]}\right)_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
, for
0\leq j\leq r
, the
r
-Jacobi-Rogers polynomials

J_{n}^{\,(r)}\left(\gamma\right)
, with
n\in\mathbb{N}
, are the generating polynomials of the sets of
r
-Lukasiewicz paths from
(0,0)
to
(n,0)
, where each step
(1,1)
gets weight
1
and each step
(1,-j)
, with
0\leq j\leq r
, to height
i
gets weight
\gamma_{i}^{[j]}
.

The
r
-Stieltjes-Rogers and
r
-Jacobi-Rogers polynomials were introduced in
[
30
]
as extensions of the Stieltjes-Rogers and Jacobi-Rogers polynomials, which correspond to the case
r=1
, introduced by Rogers in
[
32
]
and also studied by Flajolet in
[
20
]
.
We are interested in further generalisations of these polynomials.

Observe that every vertex
(x,y)
of a partial
r
-Dyck path starting at
(0,0)
, and in particular its final vertex, satisfies
x=(r+1)m+y
for some
m\in\mathbb{N}
, where
x
is the total number of steps and
m
is the number of
r
-falls.
For a sequence
\displaystyle\left(\alpha_{i}\right)_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
, the
generalised
r
-Stieltjes-Rogers polynomials of type
j
,
S_{n,k}^{\,(r;\,j)}\left(\mathbf{\alpha}\right)
, with
n,k,j\in\mathbb{N}
, are the generating polynomials of the sets of partial
r
-Dyck paths from
(0,0)
to
\big((r+1)n+j,(r+1)k+j\big)
, where each rise has weight
1
and each
r
-fall to height
i
has weight
\alpha_{i}
.
These polynomials were introduced in
[
12
, § A.3]
.
When
k=0
, the generalised
r
-Stieltjes-Rogers polynomials of type
j
reduce to the
modified
r
-Stieltjes-Rogers polynomials of type
j
,
S_{n}^{\,(r;\,j)}\left(\mathbf{\alpha}\right)
, the generating polynomials of the set of partial
r
-Dyck paths from
(0,0)
to
((r+1)n+j,j)
.
The
generalised
r
-Jacobi-Rogers polynomials
,
J_{n,k}^{\,(r)}\left(\gamma\right)
, with
n,k\in\mathbb{N}
, are the generating polynomials of partial
r
-Lukasiewicz paths from
(0,0)
to
(n,k)
, where each step
(1,1)
has weight
1
and each step
(1,-j)
, with
0\leq j\leq r
, to height
i
has weight
\gamma_{i}^{[j]}
.

The method of production matrices was originally introduced in
[
14
]
.
Let
\mathrm{H}=\left(h_{i,j}\right)_{i,j\in\mathbb{N}}
be an infinite matrix with entries in a commutative ring
R
.
If all the powers of
\mathrm{H}
are well-defined (for example, if all rows or columns of
\mathrm{H}
have finite nonzero entries), we can define an infinite matrix
A=\left(a_{n,k}\right)_{n,k\in\mathbb{N}}
such that
a_{n,k}=\left(\mathrm{H}^{n}\right)_{0,k}
.
We call
\mathrm{H}
the
production matrix
and
A
the
output matrix
.

Writing out the matrix multiplications explicitly, we get

a_{n,k}=\sum_{i_{1},\cdots,i_{n-1}\in\mathbb{N}}\;h_{0,i_{1}}h_{i_{1},i_{2}}\cdots h_{i_{n-2},i_{n-1}}h_{i_{n-1},k}.

(2.9)

Therefore,
a_{n,k}
is the sum of the weights of all
n
-step walks in
\mathbb{N}
from
i_{0}=0
to
i_{n}=k
, where the weight of a walk is the product of the weights of its steps and a step from
i
to
j
has weight
h_{i,j}
.

Based on
[
30
, Prop. 8.2]
, the production matrices of
\mathrm{J}^{(r)}=\left(J_{n,k}^{\,(r)}\left(\gamma\right)\right)_{n,k\in\mathbb{N}}
and
\mathrm{S}^{(r;0)}=\left(S_{n,k}^{\,(r;\,0)}\left(\mathbf{\alpha}\right)\right)_{n,k\in\mathbb{N}}
, the matrices of generalised
r
-Jacobi-Rogers polynomials and generalised
r
-Stieltjes-Rogers polynomials of type
0
, respectively, are
(r+2)
-banded unit-lower-Hessenberg matrices:

•

the production matrix of
\mathrm{J}^{(r)}=\left(j^{(r)}_{n,k}\right)_{n,k\in\mathbb{N}}
has nonzero entries

j^{(r)}_{n,n+1}=1
and
j^{(r)}_{n+k,n}=\gamma^{[k]}_{n}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
0\leq k\leq r
,
so it is the recurrence matrix of the polynomial sequence
\left(P_{n}(x)\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
satisfying (
2.3
),

•

the production matrix of
\mathrm{S}^{(r;0)}
is
\mathrm{H}^{(r;0)}=\mathrm{L}_{1}\cdots\mathrm{L}_{r}\,\mathrm{U}
,
where
\mathrm{L}_{1},\cdots,\mathrm{L}_{r}
and
\mathrm{U}
are the infinite bidiagonal matrices in (
1.2
).

3
Bidiagonal matrix factorisations for banded Hessenberg matrices

In this section, we present the two main novel results of this paper:
we show that the banded Hessenberg matrices admitting the bidiagonal matrix factorisation given by (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) are the recurrence matrices for the components of the decomposition of
(r+1)
-fold symmetric
r
-orthogonal polynomials (Theorem
3.1
)
and that the same Hessenberg matrices are the production matrices of the generating polynomials of partial
r
-Dyck paths (Theorem
3.4
).
Moreover, we give explicit expressions for the nontrivial entries of those Hessenberg matrices, which are the recurrence coefficients of the corresponding
r
-orthogonal polynomial sequences, and present an alternative proof of a total-positivity result for generalised
r
-Stieltjes-Rogers polynomials, firstly obtained in
[
12
]
.

3.1
Hessenberg matrices associated with symmetric multiple orthogonal polynomials

The components of the decomposition of a
(r+1)
-fold symmetric
r
-orthogonal polynomial sequence
\left(P_{n}(x)\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
are also
r
-orthogonal (see
[
17
, Thm. 5.2]
).
In Theorem
3.1
, we show that the recurrence matrices of those components are the banded Hessenberg matrices determined by (
1.1
)-(
1.2
).

Theorem 3.1
.

For
r\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
and a sequence
\left(\alpha_{n}\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of nonzero elements in a field
\mathbb{K}
, let:

•

\left(P_{n}(x)\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be the
(r+1)

Theorem 3.4
.

For

,

, and a sequence

Proof.

Following the terminology used in
[

,

,

]
, we say that a matrix with real entries is

if all its minors are nonnegative (different terminology is used on some other references on total positivity, e.g.
[

]
).
The definition of a totally positive matrix can be naturally extended for matrices with entries in any partially ordered commutative ring (see
[
30
, §9.1]
for more details).
A bidiagonal matrix is totally positive if and only if all its entries are nonnegative, because all its nonzero minors are products of entries (see
[

, Lemma 9.1]
).
Moreover, using the Cauchy-Binet formula (see
[

, Prop. 1.4]
), the product of matrices preserves total positivity.
Therefore, we have the following result as a direct consequence of Theorem

.

Corollary 3.5
.

For

and
0\leq j\leq r
, the matrix
\mathrm{H}^{(r;j)}
defined by (

) is totally positive in the polynomial ring
\mathbb{Z}[\alpha]
equipped with the coefficient-wise partial order, i.e., all minors of
\mathrm{H}^{(r;j)}
are elements of
\mathbb{Z}[\alpha]
whose coefficients are all nonnegative.

Furthermore, the output matrix of a totally positive matrix is also totally positive (see
[
30
, Thm. 9.4]
).
As a result, Theorem
3.4
implies that the matrix of generalised
r
-Stieltjes-Rogers polynomials of type

and

.
Moreover, we can reduce the generalised
r
-Stieltjes-Rogers polynomials of type higher than

to the ones of type

via the relation

which is a direct consequence of the definition of the generalised

-Stieltjes-Rogers polynomials.
As a consequence,

is a submatrix of

obtained by removing its first

Therefore, we obtain the following result.

Corollary 3.6
.

For any
r\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
and

, the matrix
\mathrm{S}^{(r;j)}=\left(S_{n,k}^{\,(r;\,j)}\left(\bm{\alpha}\right)\right)_{n,k\in\mathbb{N}}
, is totally positive in the polynomial ring

\mathrm{S}^{(r;j)}

Oscillation matrices are a class of finite square matrices intermediary between totally positive and strictly totally positive matrices.
A finite square matrix

This result is known as the Gantmacher-Krein theorem and can be found in
[

\left(P_{n}(x)\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
to find that

v_{j+i}\left[x^{k}\,P_{n}^{[j]}(x)\right]=u_{j+i-1}\left[x^{(r+1)k+j+i-1}\,P_{n}^{[j]}\left(x^{r+1}\right)\right]=u_{j+i-1}\left[x^{(r+1)k+i-1}\,P_{(r+1)n+j}(x)\right].

(4.18)

If
n=rk+i-1
, then
(r+1)n+j=r\left((r+1)k+i-1\right)+j+i-1
, so, using (
4.17
), we have

v_{j+i}\left[x^{k}\,P_{n}^{[j]}(x)\right]=u_{j+i-1}\left[x^{(r+1)k+i-1}\,P_{(r+1)n+j}(x)\right]=\begin{cases}N_{n}\neq 0&\text{if }n=rk+i-1,\\
\hfil 0&\text{if }n\geq rk+i.\end{cases}

(4.19)

For
1\leq i\leq j
, we can again use (
4.16
) and the
(r+1)
-fold decomposition (
3.2
) of
\left(P_{n}(x)\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
to find that

x\,v_{i}\left[x^{k}\,P_{n}^{[j]}(x)\right]=u_{i-1}\left[x^{(r+1)(k+1)+i-1}\,P_{n}^{[j]}\left(x^{r+1}\right)\right]=u_{i-1}\left[x^{(r+1)k+r-j+i}\,P_{(r+1)n+j}(x)\right].

(4.20)

If
n=rk+r-j+i-1
, then
(r+1)n+j=r\big((r+1)k+r-j+i\big)+i-1
, so, using (
4.17
), we have

x\,v_{i}\left[x^{k}\,P_{n}^{[j]}(x)\right]=u_{i-1}\left[x^{(r+1)k+r-i-j}\,P_{(r+1)n+j}(x)\right]=\begin{cases}N_{n}\neq 0&\text{if }n=rk+r-j+i-1,\\
\hfil 0&\text{if }n\geq rk+r-j+i.\end{cases}

(4.21)

Combining (
4.19
) and (
4.21
), we conclude that
\left(P_{n}^{[j]}(x)\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is
r
-orthogonal with respect to the system of linear functionals
\left(v_{j+1},\cdots,v_{r},x\,v_{1},\cdots,x\,v_{j}\right)
.

If
\alpha_{n}>0
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, there exist positive measures
\mu_{1},\cdots,\mu_{r}
on
\mathbb{R}^{+}
satisfying (
4.8
), i.e., with the same moments as
v_{1},\cdots,v_{r}
.
Therefore,
\left(P_{n}^{[j]}(x)\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is
r
-orthogonal with respect to the system
\left(\mu_{1}^{[j]},\cdots,\mu_{r}^{[j]}\right)
determined by (
4.15
).
∎

4.3
An explicit example involving hypergeometric Appell polynomials

We finish this paper giving an explicit example of a
(r+1)
-fold symmetric
r
-orthogonal polynomial sequence
\left(P_{n}(x)\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
satisfying the recurrence relation (
3.1
) for a specific sequence of coefficients
\left(\alpha_{n}\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
.
These polynomials can be expressed via terminating hypergeometric series and form Appell sequences, i.e.,

P_{n+1}^{\,\prime}(x)=(n+1)P_{n}(x)\quad\text{for all }n\in\mathbb{N}.

(4.22)

The Hermite polynomials form the only Appell orthogonal polynomial sequence, up to a linear transformation (see
[
33
, Thm. IV]
).
An investigation of the Appell
r
-orthogonal polynomials is presented in
[
16
]
.
The polynomial sequences described in Theorem
4.8
are the only Appell
(r+1)
-fold symmetric
r
-orthogonal polynomial sequences, up to a scaling change of variable.

The components of the
(r+1)
-fold decomposition of the polynomials in Theorem
4.8
are particular cases of the multiple orthogonal polynomials with respect to Meijer G-functions
G^{\,r,0}_{0,r}
on the positive real line introduced in
[
23
]
to investigate singular values of products of Ginibre random matrices.
Therefore, we can show that the corresponding
(r+1)
-fold symmetric polynomials are multiple orthogonal with respect to measures on the star-like set
\mathrm{St}^{(r+1)}
, whose densities can be expressed via the same Meijer G-functions.

Before stating Theorem
4.8
, we recall the definitions of the hypergeometric series and the Meijer G-function.
For
r,s\in\mathbb{N}
, the (generalised) hypergeometric series (see
[
1
,
26
,
15
]
) is defined by

{}_{s}F_{r}\left(\left.\begin{matrix}b_{1},\cdots,b_{s}\\
a_{1},\cdots,a_{r}\end{matrix}\,\right|\,z\right)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{\left(b_{1}\right)_{n}\cdots\left(b_{s}\right)_{n}}{\left(a_{1}\right)_{n}\cdots\left(a_{r}\right)_{n}}\,\frac{z^{n}}{n!},

(4.23)

where the Pochhammer symbol
\left(c\right)_{n}
is defined by
\left(c\right)_{0}=1
and
\left(c\right)_{n}:=c(c+1)\cdots(c+n-1)
for
n\geq 1
.
The hypergeometric series appearing here are terminating, thus they converge and define polynomials.

For
r\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
, the Meijer G-function
G^{\,r,0}_{0,r}
(see
[
26
,
15
]
for more details and for information on the general Meijer G-function
G^{\,m,n}_{s,r}
) is defined by the Mellin-Barnes type integral

G_{0,r}^{\,r,0}\left(\left.\begin{matrix}-\\
a_{1},\cdots,a_{r}\end{matrix}\,\right|\,x\right)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{c-i\infty}^{c+i\infty}\Gamma\left(a_{1}+u\right)\cdots\Gamma\left(a_{r}+u\right)x^{-u}\mathrm{d}u,\quad c>-\min\limits_{1\leq i\leq r}\{\operatorname{Re}(a_{i})\}.

(4.24)

As usual,
\Gamma(z)
denotes Euler’s gamma function.
A fundamental property of the Meijer G-function
G^{\,r,0}_{0,r}
is that its Mellin transform, when it exists, is a product of gamma function values
(see
[
9
, Eq. 2.24.2.1]
):

\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}G_{0,r}^{\,r,0}\left(\left.\begin{matrix}-\\
a_{1},\cdots,a_{r}\end{matrix}\,\right|\,x\right)x^{z-1}\,\mathrm{d}x=\Gamma\left(a_{1}+z\right)\cdots\Gamma\left(a_{r}+z\right),\quad\operatorname{Re}(z)>-\min\limits_{1\leq i\leq r}\{\operatorname{Re}\left(a_{i}\right)\}.

(4.25)

Another useful property of the Meijer G-function
G^{\,r,0}_{0,r}
, which can be easily deduced from (
4.24
) and can be found in
[
18
, Eq. 5.3.8]
, is

x\,G_{0,r}^{\,r,0}\left(\left.\begin{matrix}-\\
a_{1},\cdots,a_{r}\end{matrix}\,\right|\,x\right)=G_{0,r}^{\,r,0}\left(\left.\begin{matrix}-\\
a_{1}+1,\cdots,a_{r}+1\end{matrix}\,\right|\,x\right).

(4.26)

Theorem 4.8
.

For
r\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
, let
\left(P_{n}(x)\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be the
(r+1)
-fold symmetric polynomial sequence satisfying the recurrence relation

P_{n+r+1}(x)=x\,P_{n+r}(x)-\frac{\left(n+1\right)_{r}}{(r+1)^{r}}\,P_{n}(x)\quad\text{for all }n\in\mathbb{N},

(4.27)

with initial conditions
P_{j}(x)=x^{j}
for all
0\leq j\leq r
.
Then:

(a)

\left(P_{n}(x)\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is
r
-orthogonal with respect to the system of measures
\left(\hat{\mu}_{1},\cdots,\hat{\mu}_{r}\right)
on the star-like set
\mathrm{St}^{(r+1)}
with densities

\mathrm{d}\hat{\mu}_{j}(z)=G_{0,r}^{\,r,0}\left(\left.\begin{matrix}-\\
a_{1}^{[j-1]},\cdots,a_{r}^{[j-1]}\end{matrix}\,\right|\,z^{r+1}\right)\frac{\mathrm{d}z}{z^{r+j}}\quad\text{for all }1\leq j\leq r\text{ and }z\in\mathrm{St}^{(r+1)},

(4.28)

with

a_{i}^{[j]}=\frac{i+j}{r+1}\text{ if }1\leq i\leq r-j\quad\text{and}\quad a_{i}^{[j]}=\frac{i+j+1}{r+1}\text{ if }r-j+1\leq i\leq r.

(4.29)

(b)

\left(P_{n}(x)\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
can be represented via terminating hypergeometric series by

P_{(r+1)n+j}(x)=\frac{(-1)^{n}\left(j+1\right)_{(r+1)n}}{n!\,(r+1)^{(r+1)n}}\,x^{j}\,{}_{1}F_{r}\left(\left.\begin{matrix}-n\\
a_{1}^{[j]},\cdots,a_{r}^{[j]}\end{matrix}\,\right|\,x^{r+1}\right)\quad\text{for all }n\in\mathbb{N}\text{ and }0\leq j\leq r.

(4.30)

(c)

\left(P_{n}(x)\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is an Appell sequence, i.e.,
\left(P_{n}(x)\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
satisfies (
4.22
), and any Appell
(r+1)
-fold symmetric
r
-orthogonal polynomial sequence is of the form
\displaystyle\left(c^{-n}P_{n}(cx)\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
for some
c\neq 0
.

For
r=1
,
\left(P_{n}(x)\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
are the classical Hermite orthogonal polynomials,
(
4.28
) reduces to
\mathrm{d}\hat{\mu}_{1}(z)=\mathrm{e}^{-z^{2}}\mathrm{d}z
for all
z\in\mathbb{R}
,
and (
4.30
) gives the quadratic decomposition of the Hermite polynomials in Laguerre polynomials.
For
r=2
, the sequence
\left(P_{n}(x)\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
and the components of its cubic decomposition were introduced in
[
17
]
and further characterised in
[
4
, §5]
and
[
25
, §3.1]
.

Proof.

For
0\leq j\leq r
, let
a_{1}^{[j]},\cdots,a_{r}^{[j]}
be given by (
4.29
) and
a_{r+1}^{[j]}=1
. Moreover, let:

•

a_{i}^{[(r+1)n+j]}=a_{i}^{[j]}+n
for any
1\leq i\leq r+1
,
0\leq j\leq r
, and
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
(4.31)

•

\hat{a}_{i}^{[k]}=\dfrac{i}{r+1}+\left\lceil\dfrac{k+1-i}{r+1}\right\rceil
for any
1\leq i\leq r+1
and
k\in\mathbb{N}
.
(4.32)

Then, for any
1\leq i\leq r+1
and
0\leq j\leq r
,

\hat{a}_{i}^{[j]}=\frac{i}{r+1}+\left\lceil\dfrac{k+1-i}{r+1}\right\rceil=\begin{cases}\dfrac{i+r+1}{r+1}=a_{i+r-j}^{[j]}&\text{for }1\leq i\leq j,\\
\hfil\dfrac{i}{r+1}=a_{i-j}^{[j]}&\text{for }j+1\leq i\leq r+1.\end{cases}

(4.33)

Moreover, it is clear that

\hat{a}_{i}^{[(r+1)n+j]}=\hat{a}_{i}^{[j]}+n
for any
1\leq i\leq r+1
,
0\leq j\leq r
, and
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
Hence,

\left\{a_{1}^{[j]},\cdots,a_{r}^{[j]}\right\}=\left\{\hat{a}_{1}^{[j]},\cdots,\hat{a}_{r}^{[j]}\right\}\quad\text{and}\quad a_{r+1}^{[j]}=\hat{a}_{r+1}^{[j]}\quad\text{for any }j\in\mathbb{N}.

(4.34)

Therefore, using
[
24
, Cor. 5.7]
,

\prod_{i=1}^{r}\left(a_{i}^{[j]}\right)_{n}=\prod_{i=1}^{r}\left(\hat{a}_{i}^{[j]}\right)_{n}=S_{n}^{\,(r;\,j)}\left(\alpha\right)\quad\text{for all }n\in\mathbb{N}\text{ and }0\leq j\leq r,

(4.35)

with

\alpha_{k}=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1\\
i\not\equiv_{r+1}k\end{subarray}}^{r+1}\hat{a}_{i}^{[k]}=\prod_{i=1}^{r}\frac{k+i}{r+1}=\frac{\left(k+1\right)_{r}}{(r+1)^{r}}>0\quad\text{for all }k\in\mathbb{N}.

(4.36)

Observe that these are the coefficients of the recurrence relation (
4.27
) satisfied by
\left(P_{n}(x)\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
.

Let
\left(\mu_{1},\cdots,\mu_{r}\right)
be the system of measures on the positive real line with densities

\mathrm{d}\mu_{j}(x)=\frac{1}{\prod\limits_{i=1}^{r}\Gamma\left(a_{i}^{[j-1]}\right)}\,G_{0,r}^{\,r,0}\left(\left.\begin{matrix}-\\
a_{1}^{[j-1]},\cdots,a_{r}^{[j-1]}\end{matrix}\,\right|\,x\right)\,\frac{\mathrm{d}x}{x}\quad\text{for all }1\leq j\leq r\text{ and }x\in\mathbb{R}^{+}.

(4.37)

Using (
4.25
), we have, for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
0\leq j\leq r
,

\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{n}\mathrm{d}\mu_{j}(x)=\frac{\prod\limits_{i=1}^{r}\Gamma\left(a_{i}^{[j-1]}+n\right)}{\prod\limits_{i=1}^{r}\Gamma\left(a_{i}^{[j-1]}\right)}=\prod_{i=1}^{r}\left(a_{i}^{[j-1]}\right)_{n}=S_{n}^{\,(r;\,j-1)}\left(\alpha\right)\text{ for all }n\in\mathbb{N}\text{ and }1\leq j\leq r.

(4.38)

As a result, using Theorem
4.6
, we find that
\left(P_{n}(x)\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is
r
-orthogonal with respect to the system of measures
\left(\hat{\mu}_{1},\cdots,\hat{\mu}_{r}\right)
on
\mathrm{St}^{(r+1)}
, whose densities are given by

\mathrm{d}\hat{\mu}_{j}(z)=z^{1-j}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{j}\left(z^{r+1}\right)\quad\text{for all }1\leq j\leq r\text{ and }z\in\mathrm{St}^{(r+1)},

(4.39)

with
\left(\mu_{1},\cdots,\mu_{r}\right)
determined by (
4.37
).
This is equivalent to (
4.28
), so we proved part (a) of the theorem.

Furthermore, using Theorem
4.7
, the components
\left(P_{n}^{[j]}(x)\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
, with
0\leq j\leq r
, of the decomposition (
3.2
) of
\left(P_{n}(x)\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
are
r
-orthogonal with respect to the systems of measures
\left(\mu_{1}^{[j]},\cdots,\mu_{r}^{[j]}\right)
on
\mathbb{R}^{+}
such that

\mathrm{d}\mu_{i}^{[j]}(x)=\mathrm{d}\mu_{j+i}(x)=G_{0,r}^{\,r,0}\left(\left.\begin{matrix}-\\
a_{1}^{[j+i-1]},\cdots,a_{r}^{[j+i-1]}\end{matrix}\,\right|\,x\right)\,\frac{\mathrm{d}x}{x}\quad\text{for}\quad 1\leq i\leq r-j,

(4.40)

and, using (
4.26
) and
\hat{a}_{k}^{[i-1]}+1=\hat{a}_{k}^{[r+i]}
for any
1\leq i,k\leq r
,

\mathrm{d}\mu_{r-j+i}^{[j]}(x)=x\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{i}(x)=G_{0,r}^{\,r,0}\left(\left.\begin{matrix}-\\
a_{1}^{[r+i]},\cdots,a_{r}^{[r+i]}\end{matrix}\,\right|\,x\right)\,\frac{\mathrm{d}x}{x}\quad\text{for}\quad 1\leq i\leq j.

(4.41)

Therefore, using
[
24
, Thm. 8.1]
, we find that

P_{n}^{[j]}(x)=(-1)^{n}\prod_{i=1}^{r}\left(a_{i}^{[j]}\right)_{n}\,{}_{1}F_{r}\left(\left.\begin{matrix}-n\\
a_{1}^{[j]},\cdots,a_{r}^{[j]}\end{matrix}\,\right|\,x\right)\quad\text{for all }n\in\mathbb{N}\text{ and }0\leq j\leq r.

(4.42)

Moreover, for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
0\leq j\leq r
,

\prod_{i=1}^{r}\left(a_{i}^{[j]}\right)_{n}=\frac{1}{n!}\prod_{i=1}^{r+1}\left(\frac{i+j}{r+1}\right)_{n}=\frac{1}{n!}\prod_{i=1}^{r+1}\prod_{k=0}^{n-1}\frac{(r+1)k+i+j}{r+1}=\frac{\left(j+1\right)_{(r+1)n}}{n!(r+1)^{(r+1)n}}.

(4.43)

As a result, using (
3.2
), we derive (
4.30
).
Hence, we proved part (b) of the theorem.

Applying the definition of the hypergeometric series (
4.23
) to (
4.30
), we obtain

P_{(r+1)n+j}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}c_{n,k}^{[j]}\,x^{(r+1)k+j}\quad\text{for all }n\in\mathbb{N}\text{ and }0\leq j\leq r,

(4.44)

with

c_{n,k}^{[j]}=\frac{(-1)^{n}\left(j+1\right)_{(r+1)n}}{n!\,(r+1)^{(r+1)n}}\,\frac{\left(-n\right)_{k}\,(r+1)^{(r+1)k}}{\left(j+1\right)_{(r+1)k}}=\frac{(-1)^{n-k}\,\left((r+1)k+j+1\right)_{(r+1)(n-k)}}{(r+1)^{(r+1)(n-k)}\,(n-k)!}.

(4.45)

Observe that, for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
0\leq k\leq n
:

•

\big((r+1)k+j\big)c_{n,k}^{[j]}=\big((r+1)n+j\big)c_{n,k}^{[j-1]}

for
1\leq j\leq r
,

•

(r+1)(k+1)c_{n+1,k+1}^{[0]}=(r+1)(n+1)c_{n,k}^{[r]}
.

As a result, it follows immediately from differentiating (
4.44
) that
P_{n+1}^{\,\prime}(x)=(n+1)P_{n}(x)
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
i.e.,
\left(P_{n}(x)\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is an Appell sequence.
Based on
[
16
, Eq. 2.11]
, a
(r+1)
-fold symmetric
r
-orthogonal polynomial sequence
\left(Q_{n}(x)\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is Appell if and only if there exists
\alpha\neq 0
such that
\left(Q_{n}(x)\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
satisfies the recurrence relation

Q_{n+r+1}(x)=x\,Q_{n+r}(x)-\alpha\binom{n+r}{r}\,Q_{n}(x)\quad\text{for all }n\in\mathbb{N}.

(4.46)

Observe that if
\alpha=r!(r+1)^{-r}
, then
\left(Q_{n}(x)\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is equal to
\left(P_{n}(x)\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
satisfying (
4.27
).
Hence,
\left(Q_{n}(x)\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
satisfies the recurrence relation (
4.46
) for some
\alpha\neq 0
if and only if

Q_{n}(x)=c^{-n}P_{n}(cx)\quad\text{for all }n\in\mathbb{N},\quad\text{with }c=\left(\frac{r!}{\alpha(r+1)^{r}}\right)^{\frac{1}{r+1}}.

(4.47)

Therefore, part (c) of the theorem also holds, concluding our proof.
∎

VECTOR-SPREAD MONOMIAL IDEALS AND ELIAHOU–KERVAIRE TYPE RESOLUTIONS
Antonino Ficarra

Abstract
We introduce the class of vector-spread monomial ideals. This notion generalizes that of t t -spread ideals introduced by Ene, Herzog and Qureshi. In particular, we focus on vector-spread strongly stable ideals, we compute their Koszul cycles and describe their minimal free resolution. As a consequence the graded Betti numbers and the Poincaré series are determined. Finally, we consider a generalization of algebraic shifting theory for such a class of ideals. † † Keywords: monomial ideals, minimal graded resolution, vector-spread ideals, t t -spread ideals, Koszul homology, Eliahou–Kervaire resolution. 2020 Mathematics Subject Classification: 05E40, 13B25, 13D02, 16W50, 68W30.

Algebraic shifting

[

[

. Lately, algebraic shifting theory and monomial ideals arising from
shifting operators

[
23
]
have seen a resurgence. The
t
-spread monomial ideals
have been introduced in 2019 by Ene, Herzog and Qureshi
[
18
]
. The homological and combinatorial properties of these and some related classes of ideals are the subject of a large body of research
[

,

,

In this paper, our aim is to investigate the more general possible class of such ideals. Let
S=K[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
be a polynomial ring, with
K
a field. Given a vector
{\bf t}=(t_{1},t_{2},\dots,t_{d-1})\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}^{d-1}
,
d\geq 2
, of non negative integers, we say that a monomial
u=x_{j_{1}}x_{j_{2}}\cdots x_{j_{\ell}}\in S
, with
j_{1}\leq j_{2}\leq\dots\leq j_{\ell}
and
\ell\leq d
, is a
vector-spread monomial of type
{\bf t}
or simply a
{\bf t}
-spread monomial
if

j_{i+1}-j_{i}\geq t_{i},\ \ \text{for all}\ i=1,\ldots,\ell-1.

For instance,
u=x_{1}^{3}x_{2}x_{4}
is
(0,0,1,2)
-spread, but not
(1,0,1,2)
-spread. A monomial ideal
I\subseteq S
is a
{\bf t}
-spread monomial ideal
if it is generated by
{\bf t}
-spread monomials. If
t_{i}=t
, for all
i
, a
{\bf t}
-spread monomial is called an
ordinary
or
uniform

t
-spread monomial, see
[
18
]
. A
{\bf 1}=(1,1,\dots,1)
-spread monomial ideal is in particular
squarefree
.

Let
{\bf t}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}^{d-1}
,
d\geq 2
. We say that a
{\bf t}
-spread monomial ideal
I\subset S=K[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
is a
{\bf t}
-spread strongly stable ideal
if for any
{\bf t}
-spread monomial
u\in I
, and all
j<i
such that
x_{i}
divides
u
and
x_{j}(u/x_{i})
is
{\bf t}
-spread, then
x_{j}(u/x_{i})\in I
. For
{\bf t}={\bf 0}=(0,0,\dots,0)
(
{\bf t}={\bf 1}=(1,1,\dots,1)
) one obtains the classical notion of strongly stable (squarefree strongly stable) ideals
[
23
]
. On the other hand, strongly stable ideals have a central role in Commutative Algebra. Indeed, for a field
K
of characteristic zero, they appear as
generic initial ideals

[
23
]
. Eliahou and Kervaire constructed their minimal free resolutions
[
17
]
. Bigatti and Hulett showed that among all homogeneous ideals with the same Hilbert function, the lexicographic ideals (which are also strongly stable) have the biggest Betti numbers
[
12
,
26
]
. Using shifting theory
[
18
,
23
,
27
]
, Aramova, Herzog and Hibi extended these results to squarefree ideals
[
10
,
11
]
.

In
[
18
]
, it was shown that ordinary
t
-spread strongly stable ideals have
linear quotients

[
23
,
25
]
. For ideals with linear quotients, the graded Betti numbers can be easily computed. Moreover, if the so–called
decomposition function
of
I
is
regular

[
25
]
, then the minimal free resolution can be explicitly described. Unfortunately, even ordinary 2-spread strongly stable ideals do not have
regular decomposition functions
, as noted in
[
18
]
. Hence, the minimal free resolution of ordinary
t
-spread strongly stable ideals could not be determined and has remained elusive ever since.

The purpose of this paper is twofold, we construct the minimal free resolution of vector-spread strongly stable ideals generalizing the
Eliahou–Kervaire resolution

[
17
]
, and extend
algebraic shifting theory
to vector-spread strongly stable ideals
[
27
]
.

The paper is organized as follows. After some preliminary materials in Section
1
, we introduce in Section

the concept of vector-spread monomials and ideals.

In Sections
3
and
4
we construct the minimal free resolution of
I
a
{\bf t}
-spread strongly stable ideal of

. As pointed out before, the method of linear quotients is unavailable to us, as the decomposition functions of vector-spread strongly stable ideals are, in general, non regular. Thus we use

as developed by Aramova and Herzog in
[
9
]
. Let
H_{i}({\bf x};S/I)
the
i
th homology module of
{\bf x}=x_{1},\dots,x_{n}
with respect to
S/I
. Due to the isomorphism
\operatorname{Tor}_{i}^{S}(K,S/I)_{j}\cong H_{i}({\bf x};S/I)_{j}
, one can calculate the graded Betti numbers of

as
\beta_{i,j}(S/I)=\dim_{K}H_{i}({\bf x};S/I)_{j}
. Thus one has to determine a basis of this
K
-vector space. To do so we compute the
Koszul cycles
of

. As many examples indicate, Koszul cycles of arbitrary
{\bf t}
-spread strongly stable ideals do not have a nice expression as in the cases
{\bf t}={\bf 0},{\bf 1}

[

10

(Remark

is a
{\bf t}
-spread monomial, the
{\bf t}
-spread support
of

is the set

{\operatorname{supp}}_{\bf t}(u):=\textstyle\bigcup\limits_{i=1}^{\ell-1}\ \big[j_{i},j_{i}+(t_{i}-1)\big],

where
[a,b]=\{c:a\leq c\leq b\}
, for
a,b\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 1}
. Furthermore, we set
[n]=\{1,2,\dots,n\}
, for
n\geq 1
. Let

be the unique minimal set of monomial generators of

. For a monomial

\max(u)=\max\{j:x_{j}\ \textup{divides}\ u\}
. The main result of Section

Theorem

Let

be a
{\bf t}
-spread strongly stable ideal. Then, for all

, the

-vector space

has as a basis the homology classes of the Koszul cycles

e(u;\sigma)\ \ \ \textit{such that}\ \ \ u\in G(I),\ \ \ \sigma\subseteq[\max(u)-1]\setminus{\operatorname{supp}}_{\bf t}(u),\ \ \ |\sigma|=i-1.

Firstly, we show that the elements

(Definition

). Then, we inductively determine the basis for the Koszul homologies of

on partial sequences of

. Note that for
{\bf t}={\bf 0}
(
{\bf t}={\bf 1}
) the conditions that

are the same as in
[

, Proposition 2.1]
(
[

). So, we get a formula for the graded Betti numbers (Corollary

) independent from the characteristic of the underlying field

,

In Section

, we introduce the
{\bf t}
-spread decomposition function
(Definition

are explicitly described (Theorem

Finally, Section
5
is devoted to a generalization of the algebraic shifting theory. Classically, a
simplicial complex

\Delta
on the vertex set
[n]
is called
shifted
if for all
F\in\Delta
, all
i\in F
,
j\in[n]
,
j>i
, then
(F\setminus\{i\})\cup\{j\}\in\Delta

[
23
,
27
]
. Note that
\Delta
is shifted if and only if the Stanley–Reisner ideal of
\Delta
,
I_{\Delta}
, is an ordinary squarefree (1-spread) strongly stable ideal
[
23
]
. The usefulness of
Combinatorial shifting
comes from the fact that a simplicial complex shares the same
f
-vector
of its shifted simplicial complex
[
23
]
, and moreover the
f
-vector of the shifted complex is easier to compute.

From the algebraic point of view,
Algebraic shifting
is defined as follows. Let
K
be a field of characteristic zero. Let
\operatorname{Gin}(I)
the
generic initial ideal
of
I\subset S
with respect to the reverse lexicographic order
[
23
]
, in particular
\operatorname{Gin}(I)
is (
{\bf 0}
-spread) strongly stable. One defines
I^{s}=(\operatorname{Gin}(I))^{\sigma}
, where
\sigma
is the
squarefree operator
that assign to each monomial
u=x_{i_{1}}x_{i_{2}}\cdots x_{i_{d}}
the monomial
\sigma(u):=x_{i_{1}}x_{i_{2}+1}\cdots x_{i_{d}+(d-1)}
and to each monomial ideal
I
, the monomial ideal
I^{\sigma}
with minimal generating set
G(I^{\sigma})=\{\sigma(u):u\in G(I)\}

[
23
,
27
]
. Then, the following properties hold

(\textup{Shift}_{1})

I^{s}
is a squarefree strongly stable monomial ideal;

(\textup{Shift}_{2})

I^{s}=I
if
I
is a squarefree strongly stable ideal;

(\textup{Shift}_{3})

I
and
I^{s}
have the same Hilbert function;

(\textup{Shift}_{4})

If
I\subseteq J
, then
I^{s}\subseteq J^{s}
.

We are mainly interested in the algebraic side of this construction. We introduce an analogous “
{\bf t}
-spread” algebraic shifting by the assignment
I^{s,{\bf t}}=(\operatorname{Gin}(I))^{\sigma_{{\bf 0},{\bf t}}}
, where
\sigma_{{\bf 0},{\bf t}}
will be a suitable shifting operator. The
{\bf t}
-spread versions of
(\textup{Shift}_{1})
-
(\textup{Shift}_{4})
will be established. For
{\bf t}={\bf 1}=(1,1,\dots,1)
, our construction returns the classical one. In particular,
(\operatorname{Gin}(I))^{\sigma_{{\bf 0},{\bf t}}}=I
, if
I
is a
{\bf t}
-spread strongly stable ideal (Theorem
5.5
).

1. Preliminaries

1.1. Monomial Ideals.

For the rest of the paper,
S=K[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
is the standard graded polynomial ring in
n
indeterminates, with
K
a field. Let
u=x_{1}^{a_{1}}x_{2}^{a_{2}}\cdots x_{n}^{a_{n}}\in S
be a monomial, the integer
\deg(u)=a_{1}+a_{2}+\dots+a_{n}
is called the
degree
of
u
.

By
{\operatorname{Mon}}(S)
we denote the set of all monomials of
S
. Whereas,
{\operatorname{Mon}}_{\ell}(S)
denotes the set of all monomial of
S
having degree
\ell
. For a monomial
u\in S
, the
support
of
u
is the set
{\operatorname{supp}}(u):=\{i:x_{i}\ \text{divides}\ u\}
. Furthermore, we set
\max(u):=\max{\operatorname{supp}}(u)
and
\min(u):=\min{\operatorname{supp}}(u)
. For convenience we let
\min(1)=\max(1)=n
, for the monomial
1\in S
. For
I
a monomial ideal of
S
,
G(I)
denotes the unique minimal set of monomial generators of
I
, whereas
G(I)_{\ell}:=\big\{u\in G(I):\deg(u)=\ell\big\}
.

Any monomial ideal
I
of
S
has a unique minimal graded free resolution

{\mathbb{F}}:0\rightarrow\bigoplus_{j\geq 0}S(-j)^{\beta_{r,j}}\xrightarrow{\ d_{r}\ }\cdots\xrightarrow{\ d_{1}\ }\bigoplus_{j\geq 0}S(-j)^{\beta_{0,j}}\xrightarrow{\ d_{0}\ }I\rightarrow 0,

where
S(-j)
is the free
S
-module obtained by shifting the degrees of
S
by
j
.
For all
i,j\geq 0
, the numbers
\beta_{i,j}=\beta_{i,j}(I)=\dim_{K}\operatorname{Tor}^{S}_{i}(K,I)_{j}
are called the
graded Betti numbers
of
I
, and
\beta_{i}(I)=\sum_{j}\beta_{i,j}(I)
is the
i
th
total Betti number
of
I
. Furthermore,
{\operatorname{pd}}(I)=\max\{i:\beta_{i}(I)\neq 0\}
is the
projective dimension
of
I
. Whereas, the (
Castelnuovo–Mumford
)
regularity
of
I
is
{\operatorname{reg}}(I)=\max\{j:\beta_{i,i+j}(I)\neq 0,\ \textup{for some}\ i\}
.

1.2. The Koszul complex.

To compute the graded Betti numbers one can use the
Koszul complex

[
23
]
. Let
{\bf f}=f_{1},\dots,f_{m}
be a sequence of elements of
S
. The Koszul complex
K_{{}_{\text{\large$\bm{\cdot}$}}}({\bf f};S)
attached to the sequence
{\bf f}
is defined as follows: let
F
be the free
S
-module with basis
e_{1},\dots,e_{m}
.

-

We let
K_{i}({\bf f};S)={\bigwedge}^{i}F
, for all
i=0,\dots,m
. A basis of the free
S
-module
K_{i}({\bf f};S)
is given by the wedge products
e_{\tau}=e_{k_{1}}\wedge e_{k_{2}}\wedge\dots\wedge e_{k_{i}}
, where
\tau=\{k_{1}<k_{2}<\dots<k_{i}\}\subseteq[m]=\{1,\dots,m\}
, with
\deg(u_{\tau})=|\tau|=i
.

-

We define the differential
\partial_{i}:K_{i}({\bf f};S)\rightarrow K_{i-1}({\bf f};S)
,
i=1,\dots,m-1
by

\partial_{i}(e_{\tau})\ =\ \sum_{\ell=1}^{i}(-1)^{\ell+1}f_{k_{\ell}}e_{\tau\setminus\{k_{\ell}\}}.

We order the wedge products
lexicographically
, as follows:

Let
\sigma=\{k_{1}<k_{2}<\dots<k_{p}\},\tau=\{\ell_{1}<\ell_{2}<\dots<\ell_{q}\}\subseteq[m]
, we define
\sigma>\tau
if
p=q
and for some
j\in[p]=\{1,\dots,p\}
one has

k_{1}=\ell_{1},\ \ k_{2}=\ell_{2},\ \ \ldots,\ \ k_{j-1}=\ell_{j-1}\ \ \text{and}\ \ k_{j}<\ell_{j}.

If
\sigma>\tau
, then we set
e_{\sigma}>e_{\tau}
. For example,

e_{1}\wedge e_{2}\ >\ e_{1}\wedge e_{3}\ >\ e_{1}\wedge e_{4}\ >\ e_{2}\wedge e_{3}\ >\ e_{2}\wedge e_{4}\ >\ e_{3}\wedge e_{4}.

2. Basic concepts

From now on,
{\bf t}=(t_{1},\dots,t_{d-1})
is a vector of non negative integers and
d\geq 2
.

Definition 2.1

Let
u=x_{j_{1}}x_{j_{2}}\cdots x_{j_{\ell}}\in S
be a monomial of degree
\ell\leq d
, with
1\leq j_{1}\leq j_{2}\leq\dots\leq j_{\ell}\leq n
. We say that
u
is a
vector-spread monomial of type

{\bf t}
, or simply a
{\bf t}
-spread monomial
, if

j_{i+1}-j_{i}\geq t_{i},\ \ \text{for all}\ i=1,\ldots,\ell-1.

In particular, any variable
x_{j}
is
{\bf t}
-spread. We assume that
u=1
is
{\bf t}
-spread too.
Whereas, we say that a monomial ideal
I\subseteq S
is a
vector-spread monomial ideal of type

{\bf t}
, or simply a
{\bf t}
-spread monomial ideal
, if all monomials
u\in G(I)
are
{\bf t}
-spread.

For instance, the monomial
u=x_{1}^{3}x_{2}x_{4}x_{5}
is
(0,0,1,2,1)
-spread, but it is not
(1,0,1,2,1)
-spread.
For a
{\bf t}
-spread monomial ideal
I\subset S
, it is
G(I)_{k}=\varnothing
for
k>d
. Let
{\bf 0}=(0,0,\dots,0)
be the null vector with
d-1
components. All monomials of degree
\ell\leq d
are
{\bf 0}
-spread. If
t_{i}\geq 1
for all
i
, a
{\bf t}
-spread monomial is
squarefree

[
23
]
.

Definition 2.2

In our context, if
t_{i}=t\geq 0
, for all
i=1,\dots,d-1
, we say that a
{\bf t}
-spread monomial (ideal)
u\in{\operatorname{Mon}}(S)
, (
I\subseteq S
), is an
uniform
or
ordinary
{\bf t}
-spread
monomial (ideal). Such definition agrees with that given in
[
18
]
. In this case, we drop the bold character “
{\bf t}
” and we simply speak of
t
-spread monomial ideals.

Let
T=K[x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{n},\dots]
be the polynomial ring in infinitely many variables. We let
{\operatorname{Mon}}(T;{\bf t})
to be the set of all
{\bf t}
-spread monomials of
T
. Analogously,
{\operatorname{Mon}}(S;{\bf t})
denotes the set of all
{\bf t}
-spread monomials of
S
. Furthermore, for all
0\leq\ell\leq d
, we define the following sets

\displaystyle{\operatorname{Mon}}_{\ell}(T;{\bf t})

\displaystyle:=\big\{u\in{\operatorname{Mon}}(T;{\bf t}):\deg(u)=\ell\big\},

\displaystyle{\operatorname{Mon}}_{\ell}(S;{\bf t})

\displaystyle:=\big\{u\in{\operatorname{Mon}}(S;{\bf t}):\deg(u)=\ell\big\}.

Note that
{\operatorname{Mon}}_{\ell}(S;{\bf t})={\operatorname{Mon}}_{\ell}(T;{\bf t})\cap S
,
{\operatorname{Mon}}_{\ell}(S;{\bf t})=\varnothing
for
\ell>d
, and
{\operatorname{Mon}}(S;{\bf t})
is the disjoint union of the sets
{\operatorname{Mon}}_{\ell}(S;{\bf t})
,
\ell=0,\dots,d
.

Sometimes, we may use the abbreviation
M_{n,\ell,{\bf t}}
for
{\operatorname{Mon}}_{\ell}(S;{\bf t})
. For instance,

M_{5,4,(1,0,2)}=\big\{x_{1}x_{2}^{2}x_{4},x_{1}x_{2}^{2}x_{5},x_{1}x_{2}x_{3}x_{5},x_{1}x_{3}^{2}x_{5},x_{2}x_{3}^{2}x_{5}\big\}.

In order to compute the cardinality of the sets
M_{n,\ell,{\bf t}}
, we introduce a new
shifting operator
, see
[
11
]
. Let
{\bf 0}=(0,0,,\dots,0)
be the null vector with
d-1
components, we define the map
\sigma_{{\bf 0},{\bf t}}:{\operatorname{Mon}}(T;{\bf 0})\rightarrow{\operatorname{Mon}}(T;{\bf t})
, by setting
\sigma_{{\bf 0},{\bf t}}(1)=1
,
\sigma_{{\bf 0},{\bf t}}(x_{i})=x_{i}
and for all monomials
u=x_{j_{1}}x_{j_{2}}\cdots x_{j_{\ell}}\in{\operatorname{Mon}}(T;{\bf 0})
with
j_{1}\leq j_{2}\leq\dots\leq j_{\ell}
,
2\leq\ell\leq d

\displaystyle\sigma_{{\bf 0},{\bf t}}(x_{j_{1}}x_{j_{2}}\cdots x_{j_{\ell}}):=\prod_{k=1}^{\ell}x_{j_{k}+\sum_{s=1}^{k-1}t_{s}}.

Whereas,
\sigma_{{\bf t},{\bf t}}:{\operatorname{Mon}}(T;{\bf t})\rightarrow{\operatorname{Mon}}(T;{\bf t})
denotes the identity function of
{\operatorname{Mon}}(T;{\bf t})
.

Lemma 2.3

The map
\sigma_{{\bf 0},{\bf t}}
is a bijection.

Proof.
We define the map
\sigma_{{\bf t},{\bf 0}}:{\operatorname{Mon}}(T;{\bf t})\rightarrow{\operatorname{Mon}}(T;{\bf 0})
, by setting
\sigma_{{\bf t},{\bf 0}}(1)=1
,
\sigma_{{\bf t},{\bf 0}}(x_{i})=x_{i},\ \text{for all}\ i\in{\mathbb{N}}
, and for all monomials
u=x_{j_{1}}x_{j_{2}}\cdots x_{j_{\ell}}\in{\operatorname{Mon}}(T;{\bf t})
with
1\leq j_{1}\leq j_{2}\leq\dots\leq j_{\ell}
, and
2\leq\ell\leq d
,

\displaystyle\sigma_{{\bf t},{\bf 0}}(x_{j_{1}}x_{j_{2}}\cdots x_{j_{\ell}})

\displaystyle:=\textstyle\prod\limits_{k=1}^{\ell}x_{j_{k}-\sum_{s=1}^{k-1}t_{s}}.

One immediately verifies that
\sigma_{{\bf 0},{\bf t}}\circ\sigma_{{\bf t},{\bf 0}}=\sigma_{{\bf t},{\bf t}}
and
\sigma_{{\bf t},{\bf 0}}\circ\sigma_{{\bf 0},{\bf t}}=\sigma_{{\bf 0},{\bf 0}}
.

\Box

In particular, the restriction
\sigma_{{\bf t},{\bf 0}}|_{M_{n,\ell,{\bf t}}}
is a injective map whose image is the set
M_{n-(t_{1}+t_{2}+\ldots+t_{\ell-1}),\ell,{\bf 0}}={\operatorname{Mon}}_{\ell}(K[x_{1},\dots,x_{n-(t_{1}+t_{2}+\ldots+t_{\ell-1})}])
. Thus,

Corollary 2.4

For all

|M_{n,\ell,{\bf t}}|=\binom{n+(\ell-1)-\sum_{j=1}^{\ell-1}t_{j}}{\ell}.

Now, we introduce three fundamental classes of
{\bf t}
-spread ideals.

Definition 2.5

Let

be a non empty subset of
M_{n,\ell,{\bf t}}
,
\ell\leq d
. We say that

-

U
is a
{\bf t}
-spread stable set
, if for all
u\in U
, and all

such that

is

-spread, then
x_{j}(u/x_{\max(u)})\in U
;

-

U
is a
{\bf t}
-

, if for all
u\in U
, and all
j<i
such that

divides

and
x_{j}(u/x_{i})
is
{\bf t}
-spread, then
x_{j}(u/x_{i})\in U
;

-

U
is a
{\bf t}
-
spread lexicographic set
, if for all

,

such that

, then

, where

is the lexicographic order with

[
23
]
.

We assume the empty set
\varnothing
to be a
{\bf t}
-spread stable, strongly stable and lexicographic set. Whereas, for
I
a
{\bf t}
-spread ideal of

, we say that

is a

{\bf t}
-spread stable, strongly stable, lexicographic set, respectively, for all

For

. For

. Finally, if

{\bf t}
-spread monomial ideals of

{\bf t}
-spread lexicographic ideals
\implies

-spread strongly stable ideals

-spread stable ideals.

The next lemma provides the existence of a
standard decomposition
for all
{\bf t}
-spread monomials belonging to a
{\bf t}
-spread strongly stable ideal.

Lemma 2.6

Let
I
be a
{\bf t}
-spread strongly stable ideal of
S
, and
w\in I
a
{\bf t}
-spread monomial. Then, there exist unique monomials
u\in G(I)
and
v\in{\operatorname{Mon}}(S)
such that
w=uv
and
\max(u)\leq\min(v)
.

Proof.
The statement holds when
w\in G(I)
. In such a case,
w=w\cdot 1
, with
w\in G(I)
,
1\in{\operatorname{Mon}}(S)
and
\max(w)\leq n=\min(1)
. Otherwise, there exists a
{\bf t}
-spread monomial
u\in G(I)
such that
u
divides
w
and
\deg(u)<\deg(w)
. We choose
u
to be of minimal degree. Then
w=uv
, for a suitable monomial
v\in{\operatorname{Mon}}(S)
. Write
w=x_{j_{1}}x_{j_{2}}\cdots x_{j_{\ell}}
, then
u=x_{j_{k_{1}}}x_{j_{k_{2}}}\cdots x_{j_{k_{s}}}
for
1\leq k_{1}<k_{2}<\dots<k_{s}<\ell
. Now,
u_{1}=x_{j_{1}}x_{j_{2}}\cdots x_{j_{s}}\in I
, as
u_{1}
is
{\bf t}
-spread and
I
is
{\bf t}
-spread strongly stable. Moreover,
u_{1}
divides
w
and it is a minimal generator. Otherwise, if there exists
u_{2}\in G(I)
such that
u_{2}
divides
u_{1}
and
\deg(u_{2})<\deg(u_{1})
, then
\deg(u_{2})<\deg(u)
and
u_{2}
divides
w
, an absurd for the choice of
u
. Hence
w=u_{1}v_{1}
with
u_{1}=x_{j_{1}}\cdots x_{j_{s}}\in G(I)
and
v_{1}=x_{j_{s+1}}\cdots x_{j_{\ell}}\in{\operatorname{Mon}}(S)
. Clearly, the monomials
u_{1}
and
v_{1}
satisfying the statement are unique.

\Box

As a consequence we have the following

Corollary 2.7

Let
I
be a
{\bf t}
-spread monomial ideal of
S
. Then, the following conditions are equivalent:

(i)

is a
{\bf t}
-spread strongly stable ideal;

(ii)

for all
u\in G(I)
,

,

such that

is a
{\bf t}
-spread monomial, then
x_{j}(u/x_{i})\in I
.

Proof.
(i)
\implies
(ii) is obvious. For the converse, let
w\in I
be a
{\bf t}
-spread monomial,
i\in{\operatorname{supp}}(w)
and
j<i
such that
w_{1}=x_{j}(w/x_{i})
is
{\bf t}
-spread, we need to prove that
w_{1}\in I
. Write
w=uv
, with
u
and
v
as in Lemma
2.6
. If
i\notin{\operatorname{supp}}(u)
, then
u
divides
w_{1}
, and so
w_{1}\in I
. Otherwise, if
i\in{\operatorname{supp}}(u)
, then
j<i\leq\max(u)
and so
j\notin{\operatorname{supp}}(v)
. Thus,
w_{1}=x_{j}(w/x_{i})=x_{j}(u/x_{i})v=u_{1}v
with
u_{1}=x_{j}(u/x_{i})
, and
u_{1}
is
{\bf t}
-spread, as
w_{1}
is. By (ii),
u_{1}\in I
. Hence
u_{1}
divides
w_{1}
and so
w_{1}\in I
.

\Box

3. Koszul cycles of vector-spread strongly stable ideals

The main computational tool in this paper is Theorem
3.8

K_{{}_{\text{\large$\bm{\cdot}$}}}({\bf x};S/I)
, where
{\bf x}=x_{1},\dots,x_{n}
and
I
is a
{\bf t}
-spread strongly stable ideal of
S
.

The symbol
[n]
denotes the set
\{1,2,\dots,n\}
, where
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 1}
. If
j,k\geq 1
are integers, we set
[j,k]:=\{\ell\in\mathbb{N}:j\leq\ell\leq k\}
, and
[j,k]\neq\varnothing
if and only if
j\leq k
. If
j=k=0
, we set
[0,0]=[0]:=\varnothing
. For a monomial
u\in S
,
u\neq 1
, we set
u^{\prime}=u/x_{\max(u)}
.

The next combinatorial tool will be fundamental for our aim.

Definition 3.1

Let
u=x_{j_{1}}x_{j_{2}}\cdots x_{j_{\ell}}\in{\operatorname{Mon}}(S;{\bf t})
a
{\bf t}
-spread monomial of
S
, with

{\bf t}
-spread support
of

Note that
{\operatorname{supp}}_{\bf 0}(u)=\varnothing
, and if

is squarefree,
{\operatorname{supp}}_{\bf 1}(u)={\operatorname{supp}}(u/x_{\max(u)})=\big\{j_{1},j_{2},\dots,j_{\ell-1}\big\}
, where
{\bf 1}=(1,1,\dots,1)\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}^{d-1}
.

Let us explain now the

Remark 3.7

Let

5. Generalized Algebraic Shifting theory

In this final Section, we extend algebraic shifting theory to vector-spread strongly stable ideals. From now on,
K
is a field of characteristic zero. We recall that by the symbol
\operatorname{Gin}(I)
we mean the
generic initial ideal
of a monomial ideal
I\subset S
, with respect to the reverse lexicographic order, with
x_{1}>x_{2}>\cdots>x_{n}

[
23
]
. It is known that
\operatorname{Gin}(I)
is a (
{\bf 0}
-spread) strongly stable ideal.

Firstly, we need some notions.

Let
{\bf t},{\bf s}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}^{d-1}

{\bf t}=(t_{1},\dots,t_{d-1})

-spread monomial ideal into a

\sigma_{{\bf t},{\bf s}}
, where

. Note that

\sigma_{{\bf t},{\bf s}}(1)=1
,
\sigma_{{\bf t},{\bf s}}(x_{i})=x_{i}
, and for all monomials

,

\displaystyle\sigma_{{\bf t},{\bf s}}(x_{j_{1}}x_{j_{2}}\cdots x_{j_{\ell}})=\textstyle\prod\limits_{k=1}^{\ell}x_{j_{k}-\sum_{r=1}^{k-1}t_{r}+\sum_{r=1}^{k-1}s_{r}}.

Finally, for
I

{\bf t}
-spread monomial ideal, we let

the monomial ideal whose minimal generating set is

. Note that

As mentioned in the Introduction, we define the

-spread algebraic shifting

(\textup{Shift}_{1})

-spread strongly stable monomial ideal;

is a
{\bf t}
-spread strongly stable ideal;

Corollary 5.2

Let

{\bf t}
-spread strongly stable ideal of

Proof.
Let

. By equation (

\beta_{i,i+j}(I)=\beta_{i+1,i+j}(S/I)=\dim_{K}H_{i+1}({\bf x};S/I)_{i+j}

\big[e(u;\sigma)\big]
of

. For a fixed

\sigma\subseteq[\max(u)-1]\setminus{\operatorname{supp}}_{\bf t}(u)

\binom{|[\max(u)-1]\setminus{\operatorname{supp}}_{\bf t}(u)|}{i}=\binom{\max(u)-1-\sum_{\ell=1}^{j-1}t_{\ell}}{i}

. Summing all these binomials over

yields

\displaystyle\beta_{i,i+j}(I^{\sigma_{{\bf t},{\bf s}}})\

\displaystyle=\ \sum_{\sigma_{{\bf t},{\bf s}}(u)\in G(I^{\sigma_{{\bf t},{\bf s}}})_{j}}\binom{\max({\sigma_{{\bf t},{\bf s}}}(u))-1-\sum_{\ell=1}^{j-1}s_{\ell}}{i}

\displaystyle=\ \sum_{u\in G(I)_{j}}\binom{\max(u)-\sum_{\ell=1}^{j-1}t_{\ell}+\sum_{\ell=1}^{j-1}s_{\ell}-1-\sum_{\ell=1}^{j-1}s_{\ell}}{i}

\displaystyle=\ \sum_{u\in G(I)_{j}}\binom{\max(u)-1-\sum_{\ell=1}^{j-1}t_{\ell}}{i}\ =\ \beta_{i,i+j}(I).

\Box

As a consequence, the property
(\textup{Shift}_{3})
is verified too. Indeed, it is known that
I
and
\operatorname{Gin}(I)
have the same Hilbert function. Moreover, by Lemma
5.4
,
\operatorname{Gin}(I)
and
(\operatorname{Gin}(I))^{\sigma_{{\bf 0},{\bf t}}}
have the same graded Betti numbers and thus the same Hilbert function.

Note that condition
(\textup{Shift}_{4})
is trivially verified. Finally it remains to establish condition
(\textup{Shift}_{2})
. This is accomplished in the next theorem.

Theorem 5.5

Let
K
be a field of characteristic zero. Let
I\subset S
be a
{\bf t}
-spread strongly stable ideal. Then

I=(\operatorname{Gin}(I))^{\sigma_{{\bf 0},{\bf t}}}.

Proof.
We proceed by induction on the integer
\ell=\max\{\max(u):u\in G(I)\}\geq 1
. If
\ell=1
, then
G(I)=\{x_{1}^{a}\}
,
I=(x_{1}^{a})
, and
\operatorname{Gin}(I)=I=(x_{1}^{a})
, moreover
\sigma_{{\bf t},{\bf 0}}(x_{1}^{a})=x_{1}^{a}
, for some
a\geq 1
. So, the thesis holds for
\ell=1
.

Let
\ell>1
. By
[
23
, Lemma 11.2.8]
we can assume
\ell=n
. So, there exists a monomial
u\in G(I)
with
\max(u)=n
. Let
p=\max\{p:x_{n}^{p}\ \textup{divides}\ w\ \textup{for some}\ w\in G(I)\}
, our hypothesis implies that
p\geq 1
. We consider the following ideals:

\displaystyle I^{\prime}

\displaystyle=I:(x_{n}^{p}),\ \ \ \ I^{\prime\prime}=\big(u\in G(I):\max(u)<n\big).

Both are again
{\bf t}
-spread strongly stable ideals, and
I^{\prime\prime}\subseteq I\subseteq I^{\prime}
. By inductive hypothesis,
\operatorname{Gin}(I^{\prime})=(I^{\prime})^{\sigma_{{\bf t},{\bf 0}}}
and
\operatorname{Gin}(I^{\prime\prime})=(I^{\prime\prime})^{\sigma_{{\bf t},{\bf 0}}}
. Equivalently,

I^{\prime}=\operatorname{Gin}(I^{\prime})^{\sigma_{{\bf 0},{\bf t}}}\ \ \textup{and}\ \ I^{\prime\prime}=\operatorname{Gin}(I^{\prime\prime})^{\sigma_{{\bf 0},{\bf t}}}.

Therefore,
I^{\prime\prime}\subseteq\operatorname{Gin}(I)^{\sigma_{{\bf 0},{\bf t}}}\subseteq I^{\prime}
.

Claim 2.
It is

I\subseteq\operatorname{Gin}(I)^{\sigma_{{\bf 0},{\bf t}}}.

(24)

To prove
Claim 2
, it is enough to show that each
u\in G(I)
with
\max(u)=n
belongs to
\operatorname{Gin}(I)^{\sigma_{{\bf 0},{\bf t}}}
. Indeed, since
I^{\prime\prime}\subseteq\operatorname{Gin}(I)^{\sigma_{{\bf 0},{\bf t}}}
, all monomials
u\in G(I)
with
\max(u)<n
are in
\operatorname{Gin}(I)^{\sigma_{{\bf 0},{\bf t}}}
.

Let
u\in G(I)
with
\max(u)=n
. We set
a=n-1-\sum_{j=1}^{\deg(u)-1}t_{j}
and
b=a+\deg(u)
. By Corollary
5.2
we have

\displaystyle\beta_{a,b}(I)\

\displaystyle=\ \sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v\in G(I)\\
\deg(v)=\deg(u)\end{subarray}}\binom{\max(v)-1-\sum_{j=1}^{\deg(u)-1}t_{j}}{n-1-\sum_{j=1}^{\deg(u)-1}t_{j}}

\displaystyle=\ \big|\big\{v\in G(I):\max(v)=n,\ \deg(v)=\deg(u)\big\}\big|.

Similarly, as
\operatorname{Gin}(I)^{\sigma_{{\bf 0},{\bf t}}}
is
{\bf t}
-spread strongly stable,

\displaystyle\beta_{a,b}(\operatorname{Gin}(I)^{\sigma_{{\bf 0},{\bf t}}})\

\displaystyle=\ \sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}w\in G(\operatorname{Gin}(I)^{\sigma_{{\bf 0},{\bf t}}})\\
\deg(w)=\deg(u)\end{subarray}}\binom{\max(w)-1-\sum_{j=1}^{\deg(u)-1}t_{j}}{n-1-\sum_{j=1}^{\deg(u)-1}t_{j}}

\displaystyle=\ \big|\big\{w\in G(\operatorname{Gin}(I)^{\sigma_{{\bf 0},{\bf t}}}):\max(w)=n,\ \deg(w)=\deg(u)\big\}\big|.

Moreover, by
[
23
, Corollary 3.3.3]
and by Lemma
5.4
, we have

\beta_{a,b}(I)\ \leq\ \beta_{a,b}(\operatorname{Gin}(I))\ =\ \beta_{a,b}(\operatorname{Gin}(I)^{\sigma_{{\bf 0},{\bf t}}}).

Hence

\displaystyle\big|\big\{w\in G(\operatorname{Gin}(I)^{\sigma_{{\bf 0},{\bf t}}}):\max(w)=n,\deg(w)=\deg(u)\big\}\big|\geq

(25)

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \ \ \big|\big\{v\in G(I):\max(v)=n,\deg(v)=\deg(u)\big\}\big|.

Our aim is to prove that
u\in G(I)
with
\max(u)=n
belongs to
\operatorname{Gin}(I)^{\sigma_{{\bf 0},{\bf t}}}
.

Let
w_{1},\dots,w_{s}
be the monomial generators in
G(\operatorname{Gin}(I)^{\sigma_{{\bf 0},{\bf t}}})
such that
\max(w_{i})=n
and
\deg(w_{1})\leq\deg(w_{2})\leq\dots\leq\deg(w_{s})
. Since
\operatorname{Gin}(I)^{\sigma_{{\bf 0},{\bf t}}}\subseteq I^{\prime}
, we have
w_{i}x_{n}^{p}\in I
, for all
i=1,\dots,s
. We prove that
w_{i}\in I
for all
i
. Since
w_{i}x_{n}^{p}\in I
, there is a monomial
v_{i}\in G(I)
such that
v_{i}
divides
w_{i}x_{n}^{p}
. We have
\deg(v_{i})\leq\deg(w_{i})+p
, for all
i=1,\dots,s
.

If
\deg(v_{1})<\deg(w_{1})
, setting
u=v_{1}
in (
25
), we would have an absurd. Hence
\deg(v_{1})\geq\deg(w_{1})
. By finite induction,
\deg(v_{i})\geq\deg(w_{i})
, for all
i=1,\dots,s
.

Now, if
\deg(v_{s})\geq\deg(w_{s})+1
, setting
u=v_{s}
in (
25
), we would obtain an absurd. Hence,
\deg(v_{s})\leq\deg(w_{s})
, and since we have proved that
\deg(v_{s})\geq\deg(w_{s})
, we obtain
\deg(v_{s})=\deg(w_{s})
. Iterating this argument,
\deg(v_{i})=\deg(w_{i})
, for all
i=1,\dots,s
.

If
v_{i}=(w_{i}x_{n}^{p})/x_{n}^{p}=w_{i}
we set
u_{i}=v_{i}
and note that
u_{i}=w_{i}
divides
w_{i}
. Otherwise,
v_{i}=(w_{i}x_{n}^{p})/z_{i}
for some monomial
z_{i}\neq x_{n}^{p}
, we note that
v_{i}
has bigger sorted indexes than
w_{i}
, thus since
I
is
{\bf t}
-spread strongly stable
w_{i}\in I
. Hence, there is a monomial
u_{i}\in G(I)
that divides
w_{i}
. Finally, we have constructed monomials
u_{1},\dots,u_{s}\in G(I)
such that
u_{i}
divides
w_{i}
, for all
i=1,\dots,s
. Repeating the same argument as before, using (
25
), we see that
\deg(u_{i})\geq\deg(w_{i})
, for all
i
, hence
u_{i}=w_{i}
, since
u_{i}
divides
w_{i}
, for all
i=1,\dots,s
.

Thus,
w_{i}=u_{i}\in G(I)
, for all
i=1,\dots,s
, and we get the inclusion

\big\{w\in G(\operatorname{Gin}(I)^{\sigma_{{\bf 0},{\bf t}}}):\max(w)=n\big\}\subseteq\big\{u\in G(I):\max(u)=n\big\}.

This equation together with (
25
) yield

\big\{w\in G(\operatorname{Gin}(I)^{\sigma_{{\bf 0},{\bf t}}}):\max(w)=n\big\}=\big\{u\in G(I):\max(u)=n\big\}.

Hence,
Claim 2
is true.

Finally,
I
and
\operatorname{Gin}(I)
have the same Hilbert function. Moreover, by Lemma
5.4
,
\operatorname{Gin}(I)
and
\operatorname{Gin}(I)^{\sigma_{{\bf 0},{\bf t}}}
have the same Hilbert function. Hence
I
and
\operatorname{Gin}(I)^{\sigma_{{\bf 0},{\bf t}}}
have the same Hilbert function. Formula (
24
) and this observation imply that
I=\operatorname{Gin}(I)^{\sigma_{{\bf 0},{\bf t}}}
, or equivalently
\operatorname{Gin}(I)=I^{\sigma_{{\bf t},{\bf 0}}}
, proving the theorem.

\Box

We end the paper by remarking that the operator
\sigma_{{\bf t},{\bf s}}
establishes a bijection between
{\bf t}
-spread strongly stable ideals and
{\bf s}
-spread strongly stable ideals.

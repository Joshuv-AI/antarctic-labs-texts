Tensor product of C C -injective modules
Mohammad Rahmani, Abdoljavad Taherizadeh

Abstract
. Let R R be a Noetherian ring and let C C be a semidualizing R R -module. In this paper, we are concerned with the tensor and torsion product of C C -injective modules. Firstly, it is shown that the tensor product of any two C C -injective R R -modules is C C -injective if and only if the injective hull of C C is C C -flat. Secondly, it is proved that C C is a pointwise dualizing R R -module if and only if Tor i R ​ ( M , N ) \mbox{Tor}\,^{R}_{i}(M,N) is C C -injective for all C C -injective R R -modules M M and N N , and all i ≥ 0 i\geq 0 . These results recover the celebrated theorems of Enochs and Jenda [ 5 ] .

1.
introduction

Throughout this paper,
R
is a commutative Noetherian ring with non-zero identity. A classical question of Yoneda asks when the tensor
product of two injective modules is injective. The first partial result related
to this question was given by Hattori
[
9
]
. He showed that the tensor product
of two injective
R
-modules is injective when
R
is an integral domain. It is easy to see that
the Hattori’s result is also true when
R
is the product of a
finite number of integral domains. Next, Ishikawa
[
11
]
showed that if
R
is
a Noetherian ring such that the injective hull
E(R)
of
R
is a flat
R
-module, then the
tensor product of any two injective
R
-modules is injective. It is well-known
that if
R
is Gorenstein, then the injective hull of
R
is flat. More precisely, if
R
is Gorenstein, then
E(R)=S^{-1}R
, where
S
is the set of non-zero divisors of
R
. As a generalization, Cheatham and Enochs in
[
2
]
, considered a ring
R
, with the property that
E(R)
is flat, and showed that
R
has this property if and only if
R_{\mathfrak{p}}
is Gorenstein for all
\mathfrak{p}\in\mbox{Ass}\,(R)
. Finally, a complete answer
to the question of Yoneda was given by Enochs and Jenda in
[
5
]
. They proved that
E(R)
is flat if and only if the tensor
product of any two injective
R
-modules is injective. Also, they showed that
R
is Gorenstein if and only if the torsion product of any two injective
R
-modules is injective.

Grothendieck
[
8
]
introduced dualizing modules as tools for investigating cohomology
theories in algebraic geometry. An
R
-module is called a pointwise dualizing, if its localization at any maximal ideal of
R
is dualizing. Note that
if
\mbox{dim}\,(R)<\infty
, then a pointwise dualizing module is dualizing. Over
a Noetherian ring
R
, a finitely generated
R
-module
C
is semidualizing if the natural
homothety map
R\longrightarrow\mbox{Hom}\,_{R}(C,C)
is an isomorphism and
\mbox{Ext}\,^{i}_{R}(C,C)=0
for all
i>0
. Foxby
[
6
]
, Vasconcelos
[
17
]

and Golod
[
7
]
independently initiated the study of semidualizing modules.

In this paper, we generalize the main results of
[
5
]
, and characterize dualizing modules in terms of the torsion product of
C
-injective modules (See Definition 2.4). Note that if
C
is dualizing, then
E(C)=S^{-1}C\cong C\otimes_{R}S^{-1}R
is
C
-flat. Hence we consider those semidualizing
R
-modules
C
, whose
injective hulls are
C
-flat. We prove that
E(C)
is
C
-flat if and only if the tensor product of any two
C
-injective
R
-modules is
C
-injective. Next we characterize dualizing modules as follows:
C
is pointwise dualizing if and only if the torsion product of any two
C
-injective
R
-modules is
C
-injective.

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
C
be a semidualizing
R
-module. Then we have the following:

(i)

\emph{\mbox{Supp}\,}(C)=\emph{\mbox{Spec}\,}(R)
,
\emph{\mbox{dim}\,}C=\emph{\mbox{dim}\,}R

\emph{\mbox{Ass}\,}(C)=\emph{\mbox{Ass}\,}(R)

(ii)

If

(iii)

If

is a semidualizing

-module .

(iv)

\emph{0pt}_{R}(C)=\emph{0pt}(R)
.

(v)

If

is a flat ring homomorphism, then

is a semidualizing

-module.

(vi)

Let

-module. Then

if and only if
\emph{\mbox{Hom}\,}_{R}(C,M)\neq 0

The parts (i), (iii) and (v) follow from the definition of semidualizing modules. For (ii), note that if

, and this is the case if and only if

Remark 2.11

Let

-module. There are isomorphisms

\begin{array}[]{rl}\mbox{Hom}\,_{R}(M,E(R/\mathfrak{p}))&\cong\mbox{Hom}\,_{R}(M,E(R/\mathfrak{p})\otimes_{R}R_{\mathfrak{p}})\\

3.
main results

Throughout this section,
C
is a semidualizing
R
-module. We begin with two lemmas.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\mathfrak{p}
be a prime ideal of
R
and let
M
be a
(C,\mathfrak{p})
-injective
R
-module. Then
M\otimes_{R}M\neq 0
if and only if
\emph{0pt}_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}(C_{\mathfrak{p}})=0
.

Proof.

First assume that
0pt_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}(C_{\mathfrak{p}})=0
. Then
0pt(R_{\mathfrak{p}})=0
and so by
[
5
, Lemma 2.2]
, we have
E(R/\mathfrak{p})\otimes_{R}E(R/\mathfrak{p})\neq 0
. Now using the Hom-evaluation isomorphism and Proposition 2.5(vi), one can see that
M\otimes_{R}M\neq 0
. Conversely, if
M\otimes_{R}M\neq 0
then another use of Hom-evaluation isomorphism and Proposition 2.5(vi), show that
E(R/\mathfrak{p})\otimes_{R}E(R/\mathfrak{p})\neq 0
. Therefore in view of
[
5
, Lemma 2.2]
, we obtain
0pt_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}(C_{\mathfrak{p}})=0ptR_{\mathfrak{p}}=0
, as wanted.
∎

In
[
12
]
, Kubik introduced the dual notion of semidualizing modules. Over a Noetherian local ring
(R,\mathfrak{m})
, an Artinian
R
-module
T
is called a quasidualizing
R
-module, if the natural homothety map
\widehat{R}\rightarrow\mbox{Hom}\,_{R}(T,T)
is an isomorphism and
\mbox{Ext}\,^{i}_{R}(T,T)=0
for all
i>0
. She proved that a quasidualizing module is a cogenerator, that is, if
\mbox{Hom}\,_{R}(L,T)=0
, for an
R
-module
L
, then
L=0
. She then asked
[
12
, Question 3.12]
, does
T\otimes_{R}L=0
imply
L=0
? Lemma 3.1 provides a counterexample. Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be complete local with
0pt(R)>0
. Then one checks easily that
\mbox{Hom}\,_{R}(C,E(R/\mathfrak{m}))
is quasidualizing. Now by lemma 3.1, we have
\mbox{Hom}\,_{R}(C,E(R/\mathfrak{m}))\otimes_{R}\mbox{Hom}\,_{R}(C,E(R/\mathfrak{m}))=0
.

Let

\mbox{Tor}\,^{R}_{i}(M,M)\cong\mbox{Tor}\,^{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}_{i}(M_{\mathfrak{p}},M_{\mathfrak{p}})
for each
i\geq 0
. Therefore, in order to establish the desired isomorphism, it is enough to show that, if
(R,\mathfrak{m},k)
is a
d
-dimensional local ring and

, then

\mbox{Tor}\,^{R}_{d}(M,M)\cong M
and
\mbox{Tor}\,^{R}_{i}(M,M)=0
for all
i\neq d
. Set
(-)^{\vee}=\mbox{Hom}\,_{R}(-,E(k))
. Then we have the isomorphisms

\mbox{Tor}\,^{R}_{i}(M,M)^{\vee}\cong\mbox{Ext}\,^{i}_{R}(M,M^{\vee})\cong\mbox{Ext}\,^{i}_{R}(M,C\otimes_{R}\widehat{R}).

Observe that
\mu^{i}(\mathfrak{m},C)=0
for all
i\neq d
, since
C
is dualizing. Note also that
C\otimes_{R}\widehat{R}
is dualizing for
\widehat{R}
by Proposition 2.8(i). Now using lemma 3.5(i), we have

\mbox{Ext}\,^{i}_{R}(M,C\otimes_{R}\widehat{R})\cong\mbox{Ext}\,^{i}_{\widehat{R}}(M,C\otimes_{R}\widehat{R})=0
,

for all
i\neq d
, and that

\begin{array}[]{rl}\mbox{Ext}\,^{d}_{R}(M,C\otimes_{R}\widehat{R})&\cong\mbox{Ext}\,^{d}_{\widehat{R}}(M,C\otimes_{R}\widehat{R})\\
&\cong\mbox{Hom}\,_{\widehat{R}}(M,E(k)^{\mu^{d}(\mathfrak{m}\widehat{R},C\otimes_{R}\widehat{R})})\\
&\cong\mbox{Hom}\,_{R}(M,E(k)^{\mu^{d}(\mathfrak{m},C)})\\
&\cong\mbox{Hom}\,_{R}(M,E(k)),\\
\end{array}

where the third and the fourth isomorphisms hold because both
M
and
E(k)
are Artinian and that
\mu^{i}(\mathfrak{m},C)=1=\mu^{i}(\mathfrak{m}\widehat{R},C\otimes_{R}\widehat{R})
for all

i\geq 0
. It follows that
\mbox{Tor}\,^{R}_{i}(M,M)=0
for all
i\neq d
, and
\mbox{Tor}\,^{R}_{d}(M,M)\cong M
.

(ii)
\Longrightarrow
(iii). Any

M
, is a direct sum of

\mbox{Tor}\,^{R}_{i}(\mbox{Hom}\,_{R}(C,E(R/\mathfrak{p})),\mbox{Hom}\,_{R}(C,E(R/\mathfrak{q})))=0

, and that any direct sum of

Remark 3.7
.

After preparing this paper and while we wanted to submit it to ”arxiv”, we observed the paper: a criterion for dualizing modules
[
3
]
. The proof of Theorem 3.6 (resp. Lemma
3.5(ii)) is essentially the same as the proof of Theorem 2.7 (resp. Lemma 2.6) in

[
3
]
. It is just dual arguments.

The following corollary is an application of Theorem 3.6 in local cohomology. For an
R
-module
M
, the
i
-th local cohomology module of
M
with respect to an ideal
\mathfrak{a}
of
R
, denoted by
\mbox{H}^{i}_{\mathfrak{a}}(M)
, is defined to be

\mbox{H}^{i}_{\mathfrak{a}}(M)=\underset{\underset{n\geq 1}{\longrightarrow}}{\lim}\mbox{Ext}\,^{i}_{R}(R/\mathfrak{a}^{n},M)
.

For the basic properties of local cohomology modules, please see the textbook
[
1
]
.

Corollary 3.8
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a
d
-dimensional Cohen-Macaulay local ring. Then

\emph{\mbox{Tor}\,}^{R}_{i}(\emph{\mbox{H}}^{d}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R),\emph{\mbox{H}}^{d}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R))\cong\left\{\begin{array}[]{c l}\emph{\mbox{H}}^{d}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R)&\text{ \ \ \ \ \ $i=d$,}\\
0&\text{ \ \ $\text{ \ \ $i\neq d$}$.}\end{array}\right.

Proof.

Note that
\widehat{R}
is a
d
-dimensional complete Cohen-Macaulay ring, and hence admits a canonical module
\omega_{\widehat{R}}
. The
R
-module
\mbox{H}^{d}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R)
is Artinian by
[
1
, Theorem 7.1.6]
. Hence
\mbox{Tor}\,^{R}_{i}(\mbox{H}^{d}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R),\mbox{H}^{d}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R))
is Artinian for all
i\geq 0
by
[
13
, Corollary 3.2]
. Hence there are isomorphisms

\begin{array}[]{rl}\mbox{Tor}\,^{R}_{i}(\mbox{H}^{d}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R),\mbox{H}^{d}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R))&\cong\mbox{Tor}\,^{R}_{i}(\mbox{H}^{d}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R),\mbox{H}^{d}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R))\otimes_{R}\widehat{R}\\
&\cong\mbox{Tor}\,^{\widehat{R}}_{i}(\mbox{H}^{d}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R)\otimes_{R}\widehat{R},\mbox{H}^{d}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R)\otimes_{R}\widehat{R})\\
&\cong\mbox{Tor}\,^{\widehat{R}}_{i}\big(\mbox{H}^{d}_{\mathfrak{m}\widehat{R}}(\widehat{R}),\mbox{H}^{d}_{\mathfrak{m}\widehat{R}}(\widehat{R})\big),\\
\end{array}

in which the second isomorphism is from
[
4
, Theorem 2.1.11]
, and the last one is the flat base change
[
1
, Theorem 4.3.2]
. On the other hand, there are isomorphisms

\begin{array}[]{rl}\mbox{H}^{d}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R)&\cong\mbox{H}^{d}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R)\otimes_{R}\widehat{R}\\
&\cong\mbox{H}^{d}_{\mathfrak{m}\widehat{R}}(\widehat{R})\\
&\cong\mbox{Hom}\,_{\widehat{R}}\big(\omega_{\widehat{R}},E_{\widehat{R}}(\widehat{R}/\mathfrak{m}\widehat{R})\big),\\
\end{array}

in which the first isomorphism holds because
\mbox{H}^{d}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R)
is Artinian, the second isomorphism is the flat base change, and the last one is the local duality
[
1
, Theorem 11.2.8]
. Hence
\mbox{H}^{d}_{\mathfrak{m}\widehat{R}}(\widehat{R})

is
\omega_{\widehat{R}}
-injective
\widehat{R}
-module and then by Theorem 3.5, we have

\mbox{Tor}\,^{\widehat{R}}_{i}(\mbox{H}^{d}_{\mathfrak{m}\widehat{R}}(\widehat{R}),\mbox{H}^{d}_{\mathfrak{m}\widehat{R}}(\widehat{R}))\cong\left\{\begin{array}[]{c l}\mbox{H}^{d}_{\mathfrak{m}\widehat{R}}(\widehat{R})&\text{ \ \ \ \ \ $i=d$,}\\
0&\text{ \ \ $\text{ \ \ $i\neq d$}$.}\end{array}\right.\\

Therefore, in view of
[
1
, Remark 10.2.9]
, we have

\mbox{Tor}\,^{R}_{i}(\mbox{H}^{d}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R),\mbox{H}^{d}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R))\cong\left\{\begin{array}[]{c l}\mbox{H}^{d}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R)&\text{ \ \ \ \ \ $i=d$,}\\
0&\text{ \ \ $\text{ \ \ $i\neq d$}$.}\end{array}\right.

∎

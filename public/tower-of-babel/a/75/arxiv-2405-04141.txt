Element orders in extraspecial groups
Mihai-Silviu Lazorec

Abstract
By using the structure and some properties of extraspecial and generalized/almost extraspecial p p -groups, we explicitly determine the number of elements of specific orders in such groups. As a consequence, one may find the number of cyclic subgroups of any (generalized/almost) extraspecial group. For a finite group G G , the ratio of the number of cyclic subgroups to the number of subgroups is called the cyclicity degree of G G and is denoted by cdeg ⁡ ( G ) \cdeg(G) . We show that the set containing the cyclicity degrees of all finite groups is dense in [ 0 , 1 ] [0,1] . This is equivalent to giving an affirmative answer to the following question posed by Tóth and Tărnăuceanu: “For every a ∈ [ 0 , 1 ] a\in[0,1] , does there exist a sequence ( G n ) n ≥ 1 (G_{n})_{n\geq 1} of finite groups such that lim n → ∞ cdeg ⁡ ( G n ) = a \displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\cdeg(G_{n})=a ?” . We show that such sequences are formed of finite direct products of extraspecial groups of a specific type.

Central products play an important role in understanding the structure of a (generalized/almost) extraspecial

Lemma 2.1.

Let

be a finite

-group.

i)

If

is extraspecial, then there is an integer

such that
|G|=p^{2n+1}
and

\bullet

G\cong D_{8}^{\circ n}
or

\bullet

G\cong He_{p}^{\circ n}

ii)

If

such that

and

\bullet

G\cong D_{8}^{\circ n}\circ C_{4}
if

;

\bullet

G\cong He_{p}^{\circ n}\circ C_{p^{2}}

or

\bullet

G\cong D_{8}^{\circ n}\times C_{2}^{k}

In what concerns item

If one is interested in studying the element orders of a finite
p
-group

, then it is helpful to know preliminary information about

. Let

be a (generalized/almost) extraspecial
p
-group. Since

, it follows that

. By Theorem 5.2 (ii) of

, then

. It follows that

if

, as well. If

is isomorphic to any other group outlined in Lemma 2.1, then by the construction of the central product, it is known that

has a subgroup

such that

or
H\cong Q_{8}
if

, and

or

and, since

and

, it follows that

. To summarize, we outline another preliminary result.

Lemma 2.2.

Let
G
be a (generalized/almost) extraspecial
p
-group and
n\geq 1,k\geq 0
be integers.

i)

If
p=2
, then
\exp(G)=4
;

ii)

If
p

\exp(G)=\begin{cases}p&\mbox{, if }G\cong He_{p}^{\circ n}\text{ or }G\cong He_{p}^{\circ n}\times C_{p}^{k}\\
p^{2}&\mbox{, in any other cases}\end{cases}.

Since a generalized extraspecial
p
-group

is a direct product of finite
p
-groups, it is useful to count the number of elements of order
p
of a direct product with respect to the numbers of elements of order
p
of its components.

Proposition 2.3.

Let
G\cong G_{1}\times G_{2}\times\ldots\times G_{l}
where
l\geq 2
is an integer and

is a finite group for all
i\in\{1,2,\ldots,l\}

n_{p}(G)=\prod\limits_{i=1}^{l}(n_{p}(G_{i})+1)-1.

Proof.
Under the above hypotheses, let
x=(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{l})\in G
. Then

o(x)=p\Longleftrightarrow\begin{cases}x^{p}=1\\
x\not=1\end{cases}\Longleftrightarrow\begin{cases}x_{i}^{p}=1_{G_{i}},\ \forall\ i\in\{1,2,\ldots,l\}\\
\exists\ i\in\{1,2,\ldots,l\}\text{ such that }x_{i}\not=1_{G_{i}}\end{cases},

where

denotes the identity of
G_{i}
for all

. Hence, the general form of a solution of the last system is

where at least one component

is an element of order

in

. Therefore, the number of solutions (which coincides with

) is

n_{p}(G)=\prod\limits_{i=1}^{l}(n_{p}(G_{i})+1)-1,

as desired.

Lemma 2.6.

Let
G
be a
p
-group of order

where

is an integer. Let

\Omega_{k}(G)=\Omega_{\{k\}}(G)

. Since
\Omega_{k}(G)=\Omega_{\{k\}}(G)

We recall that a finite

is called powerful if either

or

and

.
For a regular

-group

\Omega_{k}(G)=\Omega_{\{k\}}(G)
, for all

(see section 4.3 of

(or, equivalently,

; see Theorem 7.2

\displaystyle n_{p^{k}}(G)=|\Omega_{\{k\}}(G)|-|\Omega_{\{k-1\}}(G)|,\ \forall\ k\geq 1.

The same thing can be said about powerful

n_{p}(G)=p^{2n+1}-1,n_{p^{2}}(G)=(p-1)p^{2n+1}

\psi(G)=(p-1)p^{2n+3}+p(p^{2n+1}-1)+1;

iv)

If
G\cong He_{p}^{\circ n}\times C_{p}^{k}
, then
n_{p}(G)=p^{2n+k+1}-1
and
\psi(G)=p^{2n+k+2}-p+1
;

v)

If
G\cong M_{p^{3}}^{\circ n}\times C_{p}^{k}
, then
n_{p}(G)=p^{2n+k}-1,n_{p^{2}}(G)=(p-1)p^{2n+k}
and

\psi(G)=(p-1)p^{2n+k+2}+p(p^{2n+k}-1)+1;

vi)

If
G\cong(He_{p}^{\circ n}\circ C_{p^{2}})\times C_{p}^{k}
, then
n_{p}(G)=p^{2n+k+1}-1,n_{p^{2}}(G)=(p-1)p^{2n+k+1}
and

\psi(G)=(p-1)p^{2n+k+3}+p(p^{2n+k+1}-1)+1.

Proof.
Let
G
be a (generalized/almost) extraspecial
p
-group where
p
is odd. For items
i)
and
iv)
, by Lemma 2.2
ii)
we have
\exp(G)=p
and, by using the information on
|G|
given by Lemma 2.1, we easily determine
n_{p}(G)
and
\psi(G)
.

For all the other 4 cases, we know that
\exp(G)=p^{2}
by Lemma 2.2
ii)
. Since
G
is regular, we have
\Omega_{1}(G)=\Omega_{\{1\}}(G)
and
[G:\Omega_{1}(G)]=|\mho^{1}(G)|
. As we previously noticed,
\mho^{1}(G)=G^{\prime}
and, since
|G^{\prime}|=p
, by applying Lemma 2.6 for
k=j=1
, we get
|\Omega_{\{1\}}(G)|=\frac{|G|}{p}
. Hence
n_{p}(G)=\frac{|G|}{p}-1
. By replacing
|G|
with
p^{2n+1}
,
p^{2n+2}
,
p^{2n+k+1}
and
p^{2n+k+2}
, we obtain the values of
n_{p}(G)
given by items
ii)
,
iii)
,
v)
and
vi)
, respectively. To conclude, for the same 4 cases, we can easily deduce the number of elements of order
p^{2}
and the sum of element orders by applying the formulas

n_{p^{2}}(G)=|G|-1-n_{p}(G)\text{ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ and \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ }\psi(G)=1+pn_{p}(G)+p^{2}n_{p^{2}}(G),

respectively.

3
A density result on the cyclicity degree of a finite group

Our following main aim is to give a positive answer to Question 1.1 which was recalled in the first section of this paper. Along with the notations already established, for a positive integer
k\geq 1
, we denote by
p_{k}
the
k
th odd prime number. Also, for a topological space
(X,\tau_{X})
and
A\subseteq X
, the closure of
A
with respect to
\tau_{X}
is denoted by
\overline{A}_{\tau_{X}}
. We start by highlighting some preliminary results related to calculus and to the cyclicity degree of a finite group.

Lemma 3.1.

Let
(x_{k})_{k\geq 1},(y_{k})_{k\geq 1}
be sequences of positive real numbers. Let
(G_{k})_{k\in I}
be a family of finite groups where
I
is a finite non-empty set. Let
(X,\tau_{X})
and
(Y,\tau_{Y})
be topological spaces,
f\colon(X,\tau_{X})\longrightarrow(Y,\tau_{Y})
be a continuous function and
A,B\subseteq X
.

i)

If
\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{x_{k}}{y_{k}}\in(0,\infty),

then the series
\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}x_{k}
and
\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}y_{k}
have the same nature (see Theorem 10.9 of
[
2
]
);

ii)

\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{p_{k}}=\infty
(consequence of the main result of
[
25
]
);

iii)

If
\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}x_{k}=0
and
\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}x_{k}=\infty
,
then the set containing the sums of all finite subsequences of
(x_{k})_{k\geq 1}
is dense in
[0,\infty)
(consequence of the Proposition outlined on p. 863 of
[
26
]
);

iv)

If
\overline{A}_{\tau_{X}}=\overline{B}_{\tau_{X}}
, then
\overline{f(A)}_{\tau_{Y}}=\overline{f(B)}_{\tau_{Y}}
(see Proposition 6.12 of
[
31
]
);

v)

\cdeg(M_{p^{n}})=\frac{(n-1)p+2}{(n-1)p+n+1}
(see Theorem 3.3.2 of
[
34
]
);

vi)

\cdeg\bigg({{\mathrel{\mathop{{\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\mbox{\Large$\times$}}\limits}}}\limits_{{k\in I}}}{}\!}{}\!}G_{k}\bigg)=\prod\limits_{k\in I}\cdeg(G_{k})
(see Proposition 2.2 of
[
34
]
).

For a class
\mathscr{C}
of finite groups, we denote by

CD_{\mathscr{C}}=\{\cdeg(G)\ |\ G\in\mathscr{C}\}.

The following theorem is the main result of this section and it guarantees that Question 1.1 can be answered positively.

Theorem 3.2.

Let
\mathscr{M}
be a class of finite groups containing all finite direct products of distinct extraspecial
p
-groups of the form
M_{p^{3}}
where
p
is an odd prime. Then
CD_{\mathscr{M}}
is dense in
[0,1].

Proof.
Let
(y_{k})_{k\geq 1}
be the sequence of the reciprocals of the odd primes, i.e.

y_{k}=\frac{1}{p_{k}},\ \forall\ k\geq 1.

We define a sequence of positive real numbers
(x_{k})_{k\geq 1}
where

x_{k}=\ln\frac{1}{\cdeg(M_{p_{k}^{3}})},\ \forall\ k\geq 1.

By Lemma 3.1
v)
applied for
n=3
, we deduce that

\displaystyle\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}x_{k}=\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}\ln\bigg(\frac{p_{k}+2}{p_{k}+1}\bigg)=0.

(4)

Also, since

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{x_{k}}{y_{k}}=\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{\ln\big(\frac{p_{k}+2}{p_{k}+1}\big)}{\frac{1}{p_{k}}}=1,

by Lemma 3.1
i), ii)
, we have

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}x_{k}=\infty.

(5)

Hence, by (
4
) and (
5
), we conclude that the sequence
(x_{k})_{k\geq 1}
satisfies the hypotheses of Lemma 3.1
iii)
. This implies that

\overline{\bigg\{\sum\limits_{k\in I}x_{k}\ \bigg|\ I\subseteq\mathbb{N}^{*},|I|<\infty\bigg\}}_{\tau_{\mathbb{R}}}=[0,\infty),

so, by the standard properties of the natural logarithm and Lemma 3.1
vi)
, we have

\displaystyle\overline{\Bigg\{\ln\frac{1}{\cdeg\bigg({{\mathrel{\mathop{{\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\mbox{\Large$\times$}}\limits}}}\limits_{{k\in I}}}{}\!}{}\!}M_{p_{k}^{3}}\bigg)}\ \Bigg|\ I\subseteq\mathbb{N}^{*},|I|<\infty\Bigg\}}_{\tau_{\mathbb{R}}}=[0,\infty),

(6)

where
\tau_{\mathbb{R}}
is the usual topology of
\mathbb{R}
. Since (
6
) outlines an equality between the closures of two subsets of
\mathbb{R}
and the exponential function

\exp:(\mathbb{R},\tau_{\mathbb{R}})\longrightarrow(\mathbb{R},\tau_{\mathbb{R}}),\text{ given by }\exp(x)=e^{x},\ \forall\ x\in\mathbb{R},

is continuous, by Lemma 3.1
iv)
, it follows that

\displaystyle\overline{\Bigg\{\frac{1}{\cdeg\bigg({{\mathrel{\mathop{{\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\mbox{\Large$\times$}}\limits}}}\limits_{{k\in I}}}{}\!}{}\!}M_{p_{k}^{3}}\bigg)}\ \Bigg|\ I\subseteq\mathbb{N}^{*},|I|<\infty\Bigg\}}_{\tau_{\mathbb{R}}}=[1,\infty).

(7)

Let
Y=(0,\infty)
and
\tau_{Y}
be the subspace topology on
Y
. It is known that for a subset
X
of
Y
, we have
\overline{X}_{\tau_{Y}}=\overline{X}_{\tau_{\mathbb{R}}}\cap Y
. Hence, by (
7
), we deduce that

\displaystyle\overline{\Bigg\{\frac{1}{\cdeg\bigg({{\mathrel{\mathop{{\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\mbox{\Large$\times$}}\limits}}}\limits_{{k\in I}}}{}\!}{}\!}M_{p_{k}^{3}}\bigg)}\ \Bigg|\ I\subseteq\mathbb{N}^{*},|I|<\infty\Bigg\}}_{\tau_{Y}}=[1,\infty).

(8)

Finally, by applying the continuous function

f:(Y,\tau_{Y})\longrightarrow(\mathbb{R},\tau_{\mathbb{R}}),\text{ given by }f(y)=\frac{1}{y},\ \forall\ y\in Y,

to both sides of (
8
), we get

\displaystyle\overline{\bigg\{\cdeg\bigg({{\mathrel{\mathop{{\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\mbox{\Large$\times$}}\limits}}}\limits_{{k\in I}}}{}\!}{}\!}M_{p_{k}^{3}}\bigg)\bigg|\ I\subseteq\mathbb{N}^{*},|I|<\infty\bigg\}}_{\tau_{\mathbb{R}}}=[0,1].

(9)

Since

\bigg\{\cdeg\bigg({{\mathrel{\mathop{{\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\mbox{\Large$\times$}}\limits}}}\limits_{{k\in I}}}{}\!}{}\!}M_{p_{k}^{3}}\bigg)\bigg|\ I\subseteq\mathbb{N}^{*},|I|<\infty\bigg\}\subseteq CD_{\mathscr{M}}\subseteq[0,1],

and taking closures preserve inclusion, we get

\overline{\bigg\{\cdeg\bigg({{\mathrel{\mathop{{\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\mbox{\Large$\times$}}\limits}}}\limits_{{k\in I}}}{}\!}{}\!}M_{p_{k}^{3}}\bigg)\bigg|\ I\subseteq\mathbb{N}^{*},|I|<\infty\bigg\}}_{\tau_{\mathbb{R}}}\subseteq\overline{CD_{\mathscr{M}}}_{\tau_{\mathbb{R}}}\subseteq[0,1].

Therefore, as a consequence of (
9
), we obtain

\overline{CD_{\mathscr{M}}}_{\tau_{\mathbb{R}}}=[0,1],

so,
CD_{\mathscr{M}}
is dense in
[0,1]
.

Theorem 3.2 implies the following immediate result which end our paper. Its second item constitutes an affirmative answer to Question 1.1.

Corollary 3.3.

i)

Let
\mathscr{M}
be a class of finite groups containing all finite direct products of distinct extraspecial
p
-groups of the form
M_{p^{3}}
where
p
is an odd prime, and let
\mathscr{C}
be a class of finite groups such that
\mathscr{M}\subseteq\mathscr{C}
. Then
CD_{\mathscr{C}}
is dense in
[0,1].

ii)

Let
a\in[0,1]
. Then there exists a sequence
(G_{n})_{n\geq 1}
of finite groups such that

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\cdeg(G_{n})=a.

Based on the proof of Theorem 3.2, each term of the sequence
(G_{n})_{n\geq 1}
is a finite direct product of distinct extraspecial
p
-groups of the form
M_{p^{3}}
where
p
is an odd prime.

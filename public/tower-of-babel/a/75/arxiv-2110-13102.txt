On the Birch–Swinnerton-Dyer conjecture for modular abelian surfaces Thanks: Supported by the following grants: ERC Consolidator Grant #101001051 “Shimura varieties and the BSD conjecture” (Loeffler); EPSRC New Horizons Grant (Zerbes).
David Loeffler, Sarah Livia Zerbes

Abstract
. Let A A be a modular abelian surface over 𝐐 \mathbf{Q} which either has trivial geometric endomorphism ring, or arises as the restriction of scalars of an elliptic curve over an imaginary quadratic field which is modular and is not a 𝐐 \mathbf{Q} -curve. In the former case, assume that there exists an odd Dirichlet character χ \chi such that L ⁡ ( A , χ , 1 ) ≠ 0 L(A,\chi,1)\neq 0 . We prove the following implication: if L ⁡ ( A , 1 ) ≠ 0 L(A,1)\neq 0 , and the p p -adic eigenvariety for GSp 4 \GSp_{4} is smooth at the point corresponding to A A (and some auxiliary technical hypotheses hold), then A ⁡ ( 𝐐 ) A(\mathbf{Q}) is finite, as predicted by the Birch–Swinnerton-Dyer conjecture, and the p p -part of the Tate–Shafarevich group is also finite. We also prove one inclusion of the cyclotomic Iwasawa Main Conjecture for A A . Moreover, we also prove analogous results for cohomological automorphic representations of GSp 4 \GSp_{4} , removing many of the restrictive hypotheses in our earlier work [ LZ20a ] ; for cohomological representations we do not need to assume smoothness of the eigenvariety, since it is automatic in this case. The main ingredient in the proof is the Euler system attached to the spin representations of genus 2 2 Siegel modular forms constructed in our earlier work with Skinner [ LSZ17 ] .



1.
Introduction

1.1.
Abelian surfaces

One of the most famous open problems in number theory is the Birch–Swinnerton-Dyer conjecture, which relates the arithmetic of a rational elliptic curve
E
to the properties of the Hasse-Weil
L
-function
L(E,s)
: it predicts that
\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbf{Z}}E(\mathbf{Q})=\ord_{s=1}L(E,s)
. The conjecture has a straightforward generalisation to elliptic curves defined over a number field
K
(and more generally abelian varieties defined over
K
), assuming analytic continuation of the
L
-function to the point
s=1
.

One of the strongest results on the BSD conjecture for elliptic curves over
\mathbf{Q}
is due to Kolyvagin
[
Kol90
]
: using the first example of what is now called an Euler system, he proved that if
L(E,1)\neq 0
, then
\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbf{Z}}E(\mathbf{Q})=0
. In this paper, we prove a similar result for abelian surfaces
A
over
\mathbf{Q}
, although our result is conditional on several other conjectures (which we hope should be easier to attack than BSD itself).

Theorem A
.

Let
A/\mathbf{Q}
be an abelian surface, and suppose that
A
satisfies the following hypotheses:

(1)

L(A,1)\neq 0
.

(2)

Either
A
is generic (that is,
\End_{\overline{\mathbf{Q}}}(A)=\mathbf{Z}
); or
A=\Res_{K/\mathbf{Q}}(E)
where
K
is an imaginary quadratic field, and
E
is an elliptic curve over
K
which is not a
\mathbf{Q}
-curve (i.e. is not CM and is not
\overline{\mathbf{Q}}
-isogenous to its Galois conjugate).

(3)

A
is modular, associated to a cuspidal automorphic representation of
\GSp_{4}/\mathbf{Q}
.

(4)

there exists an odd Dirichlet character
\chi_{-}
such that
L(A,\chi_{-},1)\neq 0
.

Let
p
be a prime such that the following conditions hold:

(5)

A
has good ordinary reduction at
p
;

(6)

For any Dirichlet character
\chi
, the 4-dimensional
p
-adic Galois representation
V_{p}(A)(\chi)
satisfies the “big image” conditions of
[
MR04
, §3.5]
;

(7)

the automorphic representation
\pi
associated to
A
is
deformable
at
p
, in the sense of
Section

6
.

Then
A(\mathbf{Q})
and
\Sha_{p^{\infty}}(A/\mathbf{Q})
are finite. If
A=\Res_{K/\mathbf{Q}}(E)
, then
E(K)
and
\Sha_{p^{\infty}}(E/K)
are finite.

We also obtain one inclusion of the cyclotomic Iwasawa Main Conjecture for
A
, assuming hypotheses (2)–(7); see
Theorem

8.2.4
.

Remark 1.1.1
.

The Birch–Swinnerton-Dyer conjecture predicts, of course, that hypothesis (1) alone should be sufficient for the conclusion of Theorem A. Let us discuss the remaining hypotheses:

•

Hypothesis (
2
) is not a major restriction, since the excluded cases correspond to automorphic forms for smaller groups, and thus should be treatable by existing methods (e.g. see
[
KL89
]
for the case of
\GL_{2}
-type abelian surfaces). For the case of inductions from
real
quadratic fields, see
[
LZ20c
]
.

•

The Paramodular Conjecture
[
BK14
]
predicts that (
2
) should imply (
3
), and many partial results are known; for instance, it is shown in
[
BCGP18
]
that (
3
) holds for infinitely many
\overline{\mathbf{Q}}
-isomorphism classes of generic abelian surfaces over
\mathbf{Q}
. For explicit examples, see
[
CCG20
]
and the references therein. For lifts from imaginary quadratic fields, much stronger results are available, since the modularity of
E
is known in many cases, including a positive proportion of elliptic curves
E
over any given
K

[
AKT19
]
; and this implies modularity of the Weil restriction using functorial lifts from
\GL_{2}/K
to
\GSp_{4}/\mathbf{Q}
.

•

Hypothesis (
4
) is a special case of more general non-vanishing conjectures for twists of automorphic
L
-functions (see the introduction of
[
DJR20
]
for a survey). These conjectures are wide open in general; however, in the case of inductions from
\GL_{2}/K
with
K
quadratic imaginary, hypothesis (
4
) follows from hypothesis (1), since we can take
\chi_{-}
to be the quadratic character associated to
K
.

•

Under our assumptions on
A
, hypothesis (
5
) is known to hold for a density 1 set of primes
p
, by
[
Saw16
]
. Similarly, condition (
6
) holds for almost all
p
: in the generic case the image of Galois is
\GSp_{4}(\mathbf{Z}_{p})
for all but finitely many
p
(by results of of Serre quoted in
[
CG20
, §4]
), from which the big-image condition is immediate, and the case of inductions from imaginary quadratic fields follows from Serre’s results for
E/K
, exactly as in
[
LZ20c
, §2.5]
in the real-quadratic case.

•

This leaves hypothesis (
7
), which is a little more awkward. We conjecture that this should hold for all
p
, and we explain in an appendix to this paper how this is related to existing conjectures on the geometry of eigenvarieties. In particular, we sketch an argument showing that if
A
is induced from an imaginary quadratic field
K
, then a conjecture of Calegari–Mazur
[
CM09
]
implies that
A
should be deformable at all primes split in
K
.

1.2.
The Bloch–Kato conjecture

A
will be deduced as a special case of the following, more general theorem, regarding the Bloch–Kato conjecture for automorphic representations of
\GSp_{4}
.

Theorem B
.

Let
\pi
be a globally generic, cuspidal automorphic representation of
\GSp_{4}
, with
\pi_{\infty}
a discrete-series or limit of discrete-series representation of infinitesimal character
(r_{1}+2,r_{2}+1;-r_{1}-r_{2})
for some integers
r_{1}\geqslant r_{2}\geqslant-1
. Let
\Pi
be the unitary twist of
\pi
, and suppose the following conditions hold, for some prime
p\geqslant 3
:

(1)

\pi_{p}
is unramified and Borel-ordinary, with respect to some choice of embedding
\iota:\mathbf{Q}(\pi)\hookrightarrow\overline{\mathbf{Q}}_{p}
.

(2)

\pi
is deformable in the sense of
Section

6
(which is automatic if
r_{2}\geqslant 0
).

(3)

The 4-dimensional
p
-adic Galois representation
V_{p}(\pi)^{*}(\chi)
satisfies the “big image” condition for any
\chi
;

(4)

If
r_{1}=r_{2}
, then there exists an odd Dirichlet character
\chi
such that
L(\Pi\times\chi,\tfrac{1}{2})\neq 0
.

Let
j
be an integer with
0\leqslant j\leqslant r_{1}-r_{2}
. If
L(\Pi,\tfrac{1-r_{1}+r_{2}}{2}+j)\neq 0
, then the Bloch–Kato Selmer group
H^{1}_{\mathrm{f}}(\mathbf{Q},V_{p}(\pi)^{*}(-1-r_{2}-j))
vanishes.

This is a considerable strengthening of the main theorem of
[
LZ20a
]
, in which
\pi
was assumed to be unramified at all finite places, and to have highly regular weight (in which case conditions (2) and (4) are automatically satisfied). Our new argument dispenses with these auxiliary hypotheses to give a considerably more general result. As in the abelian-surface case, our methods also give one inclusion in the Iwasawa main conjecture for
V_{p}(\pi)^{*}
(Theorem
8.1.2
).

1.3.
Outline of the argument

The main tool for proving Theorems
A
and
B
is an Euler system attached to the Galois representations
V_{p}(\pi)^{*}
for cuspidal automorphic representations of
\GSp_{4}
. In
[
LSZ17
]
, such an Euler system is constructed for the representations
V_{p}(\pi)^{*}
associated to representations
\pi
of
regular weight
, i.e. with
r_{1}\geqslant r_{2}\geqslant 0
. To incorporate the non-regular weight case
r_{2}=-1
, we use our subsequent work with Rockwood
[
LRZ21
]
, showing that the construction of
[
LSZ17
]
extends to give
p
-adic families of Euler systems associated to Hida families of automorphic representations. If we can put our representation
\pi
into such a family, then we can obtain an Euler system for
V_{p}(\pi)^{*}
by specialising this family of Euler systems at the point corresponding to
\pi
.

In order to use the Euler system to bound Selmer groups, we need to prove an explicit reciprocity law, relating the bottom class of the Euler system to the values of
L(\Pi,s)
. In turn, this explicit reciprocity law requires the existence of a three-variable
p
-adic
L
-function for the Hida family through
\pi
(with
r_{1},r_{2}
, and the cyclotomic twist all varying independently). In
[
LZ20a
]
, this was deduced from the results of
[
BDW21
]
(and its forthcoming sequel) applied to the functorial lift of
\pi
to
\GL_{4}
. However, this cannot be applied in our setting, since when
r_{2}=-1
(as in the abelian-surface case), the lift of
\pi
does not contribute to Betti cohomology, and hence the critical values of its
L
-function are invisible to the constructions of
[
BDW21
]
.

In this paper, we rely instead on a different and much more flexible construction of
p
-adic
L
-functions in families, relying on the functoriality results for higher Coleman theory established in
[
LZ21
]
. This allows us to permit arbitrary ramification at the primes away from
p
, and – much more importantly – to access the critical
L
-values of non-regular-weight automorphic representations. This requires a very delicate study of the spectral sequences of higher Coleman theory, in order to understand the local behaviour of families around a singular-weight classical point; this is where the “deformability” condition arises.

(Note that the proof of the explicit reciprocity law relies on the vanishing statements for Hecke eigenspaces in the rigid cohomology of certain strata in Siegel modular threefolds of Klingen level at
p
, formulated in
[
LZ20a
]
, whose proofs will appear in forthcoming work of Lan–Skinner. It also relies on the endoscopic classification of
\GSp_{4}
automorphic representations due to Arthur and Gee–Taïbi, which uses certain cases of the fundamental lemma whose proofs have not yet appeared; see
[
BCGP18
, §1.4]
for further details.)

One we have the explicit reciprocity law in hand, we apply the Euler system machine and a leading term argument to obtain a bound on the
p
-Selmer group of
\pi
. This gives Theorem
B
; and Theorem
8.2.2
follows directly from this using the standard exact sequence relating the Selmer group to the Mordell–Weil and Tate–Shafarevich groups.

Remark 1
.

The proofs of
A
and
8.2.4
can easily be generalised to the case of modular abelian surfaces twisted by
2
-dimensional odd Artin representations. In this case, the underlying Euler system is the one constructed in
[
HJS20
]
, which is related to critical values of the degree
8

L
-function of automorphic representations of
\GSp_{4}\times\GL_{2}
(see
[
LZ21
]
). In order to prove cases of the equivariant BSD conjecture in analytic rank
0
, one needs to show that both the Euler system and the explicit reciprocity law deform to non-regular weights. The deformation of the Euler system follows from
[
LRZ21
]
, and the deformation of the explicit reciprocity law can be proved using the methods introduced in this paper.

Remark 2
.

This work was inspired by the beautiful article
[
BDR15
]
of Bertolini, Darmon and Rotger, where they use
p
-adic deformation of the Beilinson–Flach Euler system to prove many cases of the equivariant BSD conjecture for elliptic curves over
\mathbf{Q}
.

B_{H}=H\cap B_{G}
. We let

2.2.
Dirichlet characters

If
\chi
is a Dirichlet character, we let

G(\chi)=\sum_{a\in(\mathbf{Z}/N\mathbf{Z})^{\times}}\chi(a)\exp(2\pi ia/N)

be the Gauss sum, where
N
is the conductor of
\chi
. We formally define
G(\chi)=1
if
\chi

For a prime
\ell\mid N
, we let
\chi=\chi_{\ell}\cdot\chi^{(\ell)}
be the unique factorisation of
\chi
as a product of Dirichlet characters of
\ell

G(\chi)=\prod_{\ell\mid N}\left(G(\chi_{\ell})\cdot\chi^{(\ell)}(\ell^{v_{\ell}(N)})\right).

We write
\widehat{\chi}

\mathbf{Q}^{\times}\backslash\mathbf{A}^{\times}

\widehat{\chi}(\varpi_{\ell})=\chi(\ell)

\varpi_{\ell}
is a uniformizer at
\ell

\chi
with a character of
\widehat{\mathbf{Z}}^{\times}

G(\chi_{\ell})\cdot\chi^{(\ell)}(\ell^{v_{\ell}(N)})=\int_{\ell^{-c}\mathbf{Z}_{\ell}^{\times}}\widehat{\chi}^{-1}(x)\psi_{\ell}(x)\mathrm{d}x=\varepsilon(\widehat{\chi}_{\ell},\psi_{\ell},\mathrm{d}x),

where

, and the local

-factor is as defined in
[

]
. Thus
G(\chi)=\prod_{\ell}\varepsilon(\widehat{\chi}_{\ell},\psi_{\ell},\mathrm{d}x)
. Here
\psi
is the unique additive character of
\mathbf{A}/\mathbf{Q}
with
\psi_{\infty}(x_{\infty})=\exp(-2\pi ix)
, and

3.1.
Automorphic representations of
\GSp_{4}

Let
\pi
be an automorphic representation of
G
which is globally generic, and such that
\pi_{\infty}
is a discrete-series or limit of discrete-series representation with non-vanishing
(\mathfrak{p},K)
-cohomology. Then there are integers
r_{1}\geqslant r_{2}\geqslant-1
such that
\pi_{\infty}
has infinitesimal character
(r_{1}+2,r_{2}+1;c)
for some
c
, and via twisting by a power of the norm character we may suppose
c=-(r_{1}+r_{2})
. We write
\Pi
for the unitary twist of
\pi
; and we write
\chi_{\pi}
for the nebentype of
\pi
, i.e. the Dirichlet character such that
\pi
has central character
|\cdot|^{-(r_{1}+r_{2})}\widehat{\chi}_{\pi}
. Note that
\chi_{\pi}(-1)=(-1)^{r_{1}+r_{2}}
.

Remark 3.1.1
.

To compare our parameters with the more classical language: if
\pi
is not a Yoshida lift, then there exists an automorphic representation which is locally isomorphic to
\pi
at all finite primes, and is generated by a holomorphic Siegel modular form of weight
(r_{1}+3,r_{2}+3)
(thus scalar-valued if
r_{1}=r_{2}
, and vector-valued if
r_{1}>r_{2}
).

The Archimedean factor
\pi_{\infty}
is discrete-series if
r_{2}\geqslant 0
, in which case
\pi
has non-vanishing
(\mathfrak{g},K)
-cohomology with coefficients in the algebraic representation of weight
\nu=(r_{1},r_{2};r_{1}+r_{2})
. In the
r_{2}=-1
case, it is a non-regular limit of discrete series (and
\nu
is a non-dominant weight), so it does not contribute to
(\mathfrak{g},K)
-cohomology, but it does contribute to
(\mathfrak{p},K)
cohomology, and thus to coherent cohomology of toroidal compactifications of the Shimura variety for
G
.

Notation 3.1.2
.

For
\ell
a prime such that
\pi_{\ell}
is unramified, let
P_{\ell}(\pi,X)
be the polynomial such that

L(\Pi_{\ell},s-\tfrac{r_{1}+r_{2}+3}{2})=L(\pi_{\ell},s-\tfrac{3}{2})=P_{\ell}(\pi,\ell^{-s})^{-1}.

Theorem 3.1.3
.

Let
S
be any finite set of places containing
\infty
and all primes such that
\pi_{\ell}
is ramified. Then the coefficients of the
P_{\ell}(\pi,\ell^{-s})
for
\ell\notin S
generate a finite extension
\mathbf{Q}(\pi)/\mathbf{Q}
(independent of the choice of
S
), and the finite part
\pi_{\mathrm{f}}
is definable as a
\mathbf{Q}(\pi)
-linear representation.

Proof.

This follows from Arthur’s classification of discrete automorphic representations of
\GSp_{4}
(see
[
Art04
,
GT19
]
), together with the strong-multiplicity-one theorems for
\GL_{n}
for
n=1,2,4
.
∎

As a by-product of the construction of
p
-adic
L
-functions in
[
LPSZ19
]
, one can obtain the following rationality result for
L
-values. To include the case
r_{1}=r_{2}
we need to impose the following hypothesis (compare Hypothesis 10.5 of
[
LPSZ19
]
):

Hypothesis 3.1.4
.

For
\epsilon\in\{\pm 1\}
, we let “Hypothesis
NV_{\epsilon}(\pi)
” be the following assumption:

•

There exists a Dirichlet character
\chi_{\epsilon}
such that
\chi_{\epsilon}(-1)=\epsilon
and
L(\Pi\times\chi_{\epsilon},\tfrac{1}{2})\neq 0
.

We let
NV^{\sharp}_{\epsilon}(\pi)
be the slightly stronger hypothesis that we may choose such a character with
\chi_{\epsilon}\notin\{1,\chi_{\pi}^{-1}\}
(this will not be used until

Remark 3.1.5
.

We expect that all (limit of) discrete-series automorphic representations of
\GSp_{4}
with

should satisfy Hypothesis
NV_{\epsilon}(\pi)
for either sign

, analogously to the results of Rohrlich
[
Roh89
]
for
\GL_{2}

As noted in the introduction, if
\pi
is a

\GL_{2}

, where
\chi_{K}

Theorem 3.1.6

(Rationality for
\GSp_{4}
)
.

Let
\epsilon\in\{\pm 1\}
, and assume that either
r_{1}>r_{2}
, or Hypothesis

holds. Then we may find a period
\Omega_{\pi}^{\epsilon}\in\mathbf{C}^{\times}
, well-defined up to multiplication by

, and every Dirichlet character
\chi

\frac{\Lambda(\Pi\times\chi,\tfrac{1-r_{1}+r_{2}}{2}+j)}{G(\chi)^{2}\cdot\Omega_{\pi}^{\epsilon}}\in\mathbf{Q}(\pi,\chi).

Remark 3.1.7
.

In the regular-weight (

) case, this result follows from a more general theorem due to Grobner–Raghuram
[
GR14
]
, whose proof relies on Betti cohomology of

However, these methods cannot be applied when

(and, more relevantly, its lifting to

3.2.
Hecke parameters at
p

We choose a prime
p
and an embedding
\iota:\mathbf{Q}(\pi)\hookrightarrow L
, where
L/\mathbf{Q}_{p}
is a finite extension. We suppose that
\pi
is unramified at
p
, so we may write

P_{p}(\pi,X)=(1-\alpha X)(1-\beta X)(1-\gamma X)(1-\delta X),\qquad\alpha\delta=\beta\gamma=p^{(r_{1}+r_{2}+3)}\chi_{\pi}(p)

We also suppose that
\pi
is Klingen-ordinary (with respect to
\iota
), so we can (and do) order the parameters
(\alpha,\dots,\delta)
so that
v_{p}(\alpha\beta)=r_{2}+1
.

Proposition 3.2.1
.

None of
(\alpha,\dots,\delta)
have the form
p^{r}\zeta
, for
r\in\mathbf{Z}
and
\zeta
a root of unity.

Proof.

This follows by comparing the complex and
p
-adic absolute values of the Hecke parameters. More precisely, the analytically-normalised parameters
p^{-(r_{1}+r_{2}+3)/2}\alpha
etc, must have complex absolute value strictly between
p^{-1/2}
and
p^{1/2}
, since otherwise
\Pi_{p}
would be non-unitary. (Of course we expect that their absolute value is exactly 1, i.e. that
\Pi_{p}
is tempered, and this is known if
r_{2}\geqslant 0
; but the weaker bound is known for
r_{2}=-1
as well, and this suffices for our purposes.) Hence none of
\alpha,\dots,\delta
can be of the form
p^{r}\zeta
, except possibly if
r_{1}+r_{2}
is odd and the “bad” Hecke parameter is a root of unity times
p^{(r_{1}+r_{2}+3)/2}
. However, Klingen-ordinarity implies that the
p
-adic valuation of any Hecke parameter must be either
\leqslant r_{2}+1
or
\geqslant r_{1}+2
, so it cannot be equal to
(r_{1}+r_{2}+3)/2
.
∎

Cyclotomic

-adic

Definition 3.3.1
.

For

4.
Tame test data

4.1.
The idea

We would now like to study the variation of
p
-adic
L
-functions (and, subsequently, also Euler systems) in
p
-adic families. However, what we shall achieve is something a little weaker. Our
p
-adic
L
-function is defined as a value of a zeta-integral, which depends on certain auxiliary test data at the primes in
S
. If these data are “optimal” for
\pi

p
-adic
L
-function interpolates the complex
L
-function (with the correct local factors at all places, including the ones in
S

p
-adic family.

So we shall, instead, interpolate the zeta-integrals for

choices of test data, thus sidestepping the question of whether the test data can be chosen so they are “optimal” for all specialisations at once.

5.2.
One-parameter families

In order to simplify the geometry of the situation (in particular for non-regular weights), we pass from the full eigenvariety

This is an instance of “Bellaïche’s freeness lemma” (Lemma 4.1 of

5.3.
Specialisation at singular weights

The scheme-theoretic fibre of

Proposition 5.3.1

There is an exact sequence

∎

Definition 5.3.2

We let

Remark 5.3.3

All of the above discussion simplifies considerably if

(1)

Proof.

This is exactly
Theorem

8.1.3
applied to the automorphic representation
\pi
associated to
A
.
∎

Remark 8.2.3
.

In order to obtain one divisibility in the exact BSD leading-term formula for
A

. It does not appear to be obvious how to do this.

We also obtain one inclusion of the Iwasawa main conjecture: let
\widetilde{R\Gamma}_{\Iw}(\mathbf{Q}_{\infty},V_{p}(A))
denote the Nekovar Selmer complex, with the unramified local conditions at
\ell\neq p
, and at
p

\Fil^{2}V_{\pi}^{*}
.

Theorem 8.2.4
.

Assume that Conditions (1)–(4) above are satisfied, and that
L(A,1)\neq 0
. Then
\widetilde{H}^{1}_{\Iw}(\mathbf{Q}_{\infty},V_{p}A)=0
, and
\widetilde{H}^{2}_{\Iw}(\mathbf{Q}_{\infty},V_{p}A)
is a finitely-generated torsion module over

Proof.

An application of the Euler system machine (c.f.

).

Appendix A
Remarks on the deformability hypothesis

The aim of this section is to discuss evidence for the “deformability” condition (
7
) in our main theorem, and relate it to existing conjectures on the geometry of eigenvarieties.

A.1.
The generic case

Let
A
be a semistable, modular abelian surface with
\End_{\overline{\mathbf{Q}}}(A)=\mathbf{Z}
.

A.1.1.
Selmer vanishing

We recall the following result of Calegari–Geraghty–Harris (from the Appendix to
[
CG20
]
):

Theorem A.1.1
.

There exists a density one set of primes
p
such that the following hold:

•

A
is good ordinary at
p
;

•

the Galois representation
V_{p}(A)
is residually irreducible;

•

the unit eigenvalues
\alpha,\beta
of Frobenius on the Dieudonné module of
A
are distinct mod
p
;

•

we have
H^{1}_{\mathrm{f}}(\mathbf{Q},\mathrm{ad}^{0}(V_{p}A))=0
, where
\mathrm{ad}^{0}(V_{p}A)
denotes the 10-dimensional
\operatorname{PGSp}_{4}
adjoint representation.

We let
p
be a prime for which the conclusions of the theorem hold. It is important to note that the adjoint motive of
A
is
not
critical, so we do not also obtain vanishing of the Tate dual
\mathrm{ad}^{0}(V_{p}A)(1)
(in contrast to the case of elliptic curves over totally-real fields); instead,
H^{1}_{\mathrm{f}}(\mathbf{Q},\mathrm{ad}^{0}\left(V_{p}A\right)(1))
is one-dimensional.

A.1.2.
Deformation spaces

Let
\bar{\rho}
be the dual of the residual representation of
V_{p}(A)
, and
R
a complete Noetherian local
\mathbf{Z}_{p}
-algebra. We consider deformations of
\bar{\rho}
as a
\Gamma_{\mathbf{Q},S}
-representation, where
S
is the set of primes where
\rho
is ramified. All our deformations will be symplectic, with multiplier equal to the inverse cyclotomic character. Let us write
\operatorname{ur}(x)
for the unramified character mapping geometric Frobenius to
x
.

Definition A.1.2
.

A deformation
\rho
of
\bar{\rho}
to
R
is
Klingen-ordinary
if at
p
it has the form

\begin{pmatrix}\operatorname{ur}(u)&0&\star&\star\\
0&\operatorname{ur}(v)&\star&\star\\
0&0&\ddots\\
0&0&\end{pmatrix}

for some
u,v\in R^{\times}
lifting
\bar{\alpha},\bar{\beta}
respectively. It is
Borel-ordinary
if it has the shape

\begin{pmatrix}\operatorname{ur}(u)&\star&\star&\star\\
0&\operatorname{ur}(v)&\star&\star\\
0&0&\ddots\\
0&0&\end{pmatrix}

for some
u,v
(note the extra star). It is
weakly Borel-ordinary
if it has the form

\begin{pmatrix}\tau_{1}&\star&\star&\star\\
0&\tau_{2}&\star&\star\\
0&0&\ddots\\
0&0&\end{pmatrix}

for characters
\tau_{1},\tau_{2}
lifting
\operatorname{ur}(\bar{\alpha})
,
\operatorname{ur}(\bar{\beta})
respectively (but we do not assume the
\tau_{i}
to be unramified). Finally, it is
weakly Klingen-ordinary
if it has the form

\begin{pmatrix}\tau_{1}&0&\star&\star\\
0&\tau_{2}&\star&\star\\
0&0&\ddots\\
0&0&\end{pmatrix}

with
\tau_{1},\tau_{2}
lifting
\operatorname{ur}(\bar{\alpha})
,
\operatorname{ur}(\bar{\beta})
, as before, and the
ratio
of the
\tau_{i}
is unramified.

For each of these deformation problems, we can define a global deformation space parametrising deformations of
\bar{\rho}
which satisfy the given local condition at
p
, and the “minimal” local condition at primes away from
p
. We denote the rigid-analytic generic fibres of the corresponding deformation spaces by
\mathcal{X}_{\bar{\rho}}^{?}
, with
?=\Kl,\mathrm{B},\mathrm{wKl},\mathrm{wB}
. The logical implications among our deformation conditions give maps

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{X}_{\bar{\rho}}^{\mathrm{B}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{X}_{\bar{\rho}}^{\Kl}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{X}_{\bar{\rho}}^{\mathrm{wB}}.}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{X}_{\bar{\rho}}^{\mathrm{wKl}}}

By construction,
\mathcal{X}_{\bar{\rho}}^{\mathrm{wB}}
maps naturally to the product of two copies of the unit disc (parametrising characters of the inertia subgroup of
\Gamma_{\mathbf{Q}_{p}}^{\mathrm{ab}}
) and the fibre over the identity is
\mathcal{X}_{\bar{\rho}}^{\mathrm{B}}
. Similarly,
\mathcal{X}_{\bar{\rho}}^{\mathrm{wKl}}
maps to one copy of the unit disc and the fibre over the identity is
\mathcal{X}_{\bar{\rho}}^{\Kl}
.

Remark A.1.3
.

Note that
\mathcal{X}_{\bar{\rho}}^{\mathrm{wKl}}
is contained in the preimage in
\mathcal{X}_{\bar{\rho}}^{\mathrm{wB}}
of the “parallel weight” locus (where
\tau_{1}/\tau_{2}
is unramified). However, the containment may be strict, because of the extra
\star
in the
(1,2)
position for
?=\mathrm{wB}
.

A.1.3.
Tangent spaces

The deformation
\rho=(V_{p}A)^{*}
(and a choice of labelling of the unit-root Frobenius eigenvalues) determines a point of
\mathcal{X}_{\bar{\rho}}^{\Kl}
, and hence of the other spaces as well. By standard methods, we can compute the tangent space at this point in terms of Galois cohomology:

Proposition A.1.4
.

The tangent space of
\mathcal{X}_{\bar{\rho}}^{?}
at
\rho
is given by a Selmer group

H^{1}_{?}(\mathbf{Q},\mathrm{ad}^{0}(\rho))\coloneqq\operatorname{ker}\left(H^{1}(\Gamma_{\mathbf{Q},S},\mathrm{ad}^{0}(\rho))\longrightarrow\bigoplus_{\ell\in S}H^{1}_{?}(\mathbf{Q}_{\ell},\mathrm{ad}^{0}(\rho))\right),

where
H^{1}_{?}(\mathbf{Q}_{\ell},\mathrm{ad}^{0}(\rho)_{L})\subset H^{1}(\mathbf{Q}_{\ell},\mathrm{ad}^{0}(\rho))
is a subspace determined by the local condition
?
at
\ell
. For
\ell\neq p
, we have
H^{1}_{?}(\mathbf{Q}_{\ell},\mathrm{ad}^{0}(\rho))=H^{1}_{\mathrm{f}}(\mathbf{Q}_{\ell},\mathrm{ad}^{0}(\rho))
(whatever the value of
?
).

So our tangent-space Selmer groups differ only in the local condition at
p
. The following is shown in §4 of
[
CG20
]
:

Proposition A.1.5
.

We have
H^{1}_{\Kl}(\mathbf{Q}_{p},\mathrm{ad}^{0}\rho)=H^{1}_{\mathrm{f}}(\mathbf{Q}_{p},\mathrm{ad}^{0}\rho)
, so (by our assumptions on
A
and
p
) the tangent space of
\mathcal{X}_{\bar{\rho}}^{\Kl}
at
\rho
is zero.

Let us write
H^{1}_{?}(\mathbf{Q}_{p},\mathrm{ad}^{0}(\rho)(1))
for the orthogonal complement of
H^{1}_{?}(\mathbf{Q}_{p},\mathrm{ad}^{0}\rho)
under Tate duality. If
?\in\{\mathrm{wKl},\mathrm{B},\mathrm{wB}\}
, then there are localisation maps

({\dagger})\qquad H^{1}_{?}(\mathbf{Q},\mathrm{ad}^{0}\rho)\to\frac{H^{1}_{?}(\mathbf{Q}_{p},\mathrm{ad}^{0}\rho)}{H^{1}_{\mathrm{f}}(\mathbf{Q}_{p},\mathrm{ad}^{0}\rho)}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\frac{H^{1}_{\mathrm{f}}(\mathbf{Q}_{p},\mathrm{ad}^{0}\rho(1))}{H^{1}_{?}(\mathbf{Q}_{p},\mathrm{ad}^{0}\rho(1))}\leftarrow H^{1}_{\mathrm{f}}(\mathbf{Q},\mathrm{ad}^{0}\rho(1))\qquad({\ddagger})

and the Poitou–Tate global duality theorem shows that the images of these maps are orthogonal complements (with respect to the perfect pairing between the middle two spaces induced by local Tate duality).

Definition A.1.6
.

We say the deformation problem
?
is
unobstructed
if the right-hand map
({\ddagger})
is nonzero (and hence injective), so
H^{1}_{?}(\mathbf{Q},\mathrm{ad}^{0}\rho(1))=0
.

We can compute the dimensions of the local-condition spaces in much the same way as Lemma 4.8 of
[
CG20
]
, and we obtain the following formula:

\dim H^{1}_{?}(\mathbf{Q},\mathrm{ad}^{0}(\rho)_{L})=\delta_{?}+\begin{cases}-1&\text{if $?=\Kl$},\\
0&\text{if $?=\mathrm{B}$ or $\mathrm{wKl}$},\\
2&\text{if $?=\mathrm{wB}$}.\end{cases}

where
\delta_{?}
is 1 if the deformation problem is obstructed and 0 if it is unobstructed. (Clearly,
?=\Kl
is always obstructed.) Of course, if either of the deformation problems
?=\mathrm{B}
or
?=\mathrm{wKl}
is unobstructed, then
?=\mathrm{wB}
is
a fortiori
unobstructed.

Remark A.1.7
.

There seems to be “no particular reason” for the image of
H^{1}_{\mathrm{f}}(\mathbf{Q},\mathrm{ad}^{0}\rho(1))
in the local cohomology
H^{1}_{\mathrm{f}}(\mathbf{Q}_{p},\mathrm{ad}^{0}\rho(1))
to land in the much smaller subspace
H^{1}_{\mathrm{wB}}(\mathbf{Q}_{p},\mathrm{ad}^{0}\rho(1))
. So the conjecture that the
\mathrm{wB}
Selmer group should always be unobstructed seems very plausible.

A.2.
Application to deformability

Proposition A.2.1
.

If the deformation problem
?=\mathrm{wB}
is unobstructed, then
\rho
is deformable in the sense of
Section

6
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{E}
denote the
\GSp_{4}
eigenvariety of the appropriate tame level, as above. Then the automorphic representation
\pi
associated to
A
defines a point of
\mathcal{E}
. Moreover, we can find a deformation of the Galois representation
\rho
to a neighbourhood of
\pi
: that is, there exists a neighbourhood
\mathcal{N}
of
\rho
, and a homomorphism
\mathcal{N}\to\mathcal{X}^{\mathrm{wB}}_{\bar{\rho}}
, mapping classical points to their associated Galois representations (and, in particular, sending
\pi
to
\rho
). By construction, this map intertwines the weight map
\mathcal{N}\subset\mathcal{E}\to\mathcal{W}^{2}
with the natural map from
\mathcal{X}^{\mathrm{wB}}_{\bar{\rho}}
to two copies of the unit disc.

The map
\mathcal{N}\to\mathcal{X}_{\bar{\rho}}^{\mathrm{wB}}
must be injective, since the Hecke eigensystems associated to automorphic representations are determined by their Galois representations. So, if the deformation problem
?=\mathrm{wB}
is unobstructed, then the tangent space of
\mathcal{E}
at
\bar{\rho}
has dimension
\leqslant 2
(the dimension of the tangent space of
\mathcal{X}_{\bar{\rho}}^{\mathrm{wB}}
); and the relative tangent space over weight space has dimension
\leqslant 1
(the dimension of the tangent space of
\mathcal{X}_{\bar{\rho}}^{\mathrm{B}}
, which is the fibre of
\mathcal{X}_{\bar{\rho}}^{\mathrm{wB}}
over weight space).

Since
\rho
is irreducible,
\mathcal{E}
must be flat over weight space around
\rho
and thus its tangent space cannot have dimension
<2
. So it is smooth at
\rho
and the differential of the weight map is non-zero, as required.
∎

Remark A.2.2
.

We expect, of course, that the map from
\mathcal{E}
to the deformation space should be an isomorphism (an “R = T” conjecture for Borel-ordinary families). If this holds (at least locally around
\rho
), then the converse of the above proposition is also true – i.e. deformability is
equivalent
to the
\mathrm{wB}
deformation problem being unobstructed.

A.3.
Relation to the Klingen partial eigenvariety

Let
\mathbf{T}^{\Kl}_{\bar{\rho}}
denote the Hecke algebra acting on the
\bar{\rho}
-localisation of the Klingen-ordinary cohomology complex of
[
Pil20
]
; and let
\mathcal{E}^{\Kl}_{\bar{\rho}}
denote the generic fibre of its formal spectrum. As in Theorem 7.9.4 of
[
BCGP18
]
, one can construct a deformation of
\bar{\rho}
valued in
\mathbf{T}^{\Kl}_{\bar{\rho}}
, compatible with the Galois representations associated to classical specialisations of
\mathbf{T}^{\Kl}_{\bar{\rho}}
; and this deformation satisfies the “weak Klingen-ordinary” deformation condition, so we obtain a map
\mathcal{E}_{\bar{\rho}}^{\Kl}\to\mathcal{X}_{\bar{\rho}}^{\mathrm{wKl}}
.

Conjecture A.3.1

(“R = T” for Klingen-ordinary deformations)
.

This deformation
is universal
, i.e. it induces an isomorphism between the universal weakly-Klingen-ordinary deformation ring and the Hecke algebra
\mathbf{T}^{\mathrm{wKl}}_{\bar{\rho}}
.

This conjecture ought to be accessible using the methods of
[
BCGP18
]
(although it is a little stronger than the
R=T
results actually proved in
op.cit.
). If this holds, then we can identify
\mathcal{X}_{\bar{\rho}}^{\mathrm{wKl}}
with the tangent space of
\mathcal{E}_{\bar{\rho}}^{\mathrm{wKl}}
at
\rho
.

Corollary A.3.2
.

If the above conjecture holds, then the deformation problem
?=\mathrm{wKl}
is unobstructed if and only if
\rho
is an isolated and reduced point of
\mathcal{E}_{\bar{\rho}}^{\mathrm{wKl}}
.

In particular, if
\mathbf{T}^{\mathrm{wKl}}_{\bar{\rho}}[1/p]
is finite and étale over
\mathbf{Q}_{p}
, then
?=\mathrm{wKl}
is unobstructed. It is conjectured in the introduction of
[
BP20
]
that
\mathbf{T}^{\mathrm{wKl}}_{\bar{\rho}}
should be torsion over the Iwasawa algebra, and hence its generic fibre finite over
\mathbf{Q}_{p}
, whenever
\bar{\rho}
is not induced from a 2-dimensional representation over a real quadratic field. So it seems natural to expect that
\mathbf{T}^{\mathrm{wKl}}_{\bar{\rho}}[1/p]
should also be étale for “most” primes
p
; and, as we have seen, this implies deformability in the sense of the main text.

A.4.
Lifts from quadratic fields

We now consider the case when
A=\Res_{K/\mathbf{Q}}(E)
where
K
is an imaginary quadratic field. This case is not immediately covered by the results of
[
CG20
]
, but it should be within reach via the same methods to show that
H^{1}_{\mathrm{f}}(\mathbf{Q},\mathrm{ad}^{0}(V_{p}A))=0
for many primes
p
; in any case this is predicted by the Bloch–Kato conjecture for the adjoint. We shall assume this for the remainder of this section.

Proposition A.4.1
.

Let
\tau
be the 2-dimensional
G_{K}
-representation from which
\rho
is induced. Then we have

\mathrm{ad}^{0}(\rho)=\Ind_{K}^{\mathbf{Q}}(\mathrm{ad}^{0}\tau)\oplus\As(\tau)(1),

where
\As(\tau)
denotes the 4-dimensional Asai (tensor-induction) representation.

This gives decompositions of the source and target of the map
({\ddagger})
, for each deformation problem
?
, into a “
\GL_{2}/K
adjoint” part and an “Asai” part; and the map
({\ddagger})
itself preserves this decomposition.

Corollary A.4.2
.

If
K
is imaginary quadratic, then the
?=\mathrm{B}
deformation problem is
always
obstructed.

Proof.

By Poitou–Tate duality, the Selmer group of
\Ind_{K/\mathbf{Q}}\mathrm{ad}^{0}(\tau)(2)
has to have dimension
\geqslant 1
. So the source of the map
({\ddagger})
lies entirely in the “adjoint” summand. However, for
?=\mathrm{B}
the target of the map lies entirely in the “Asai” summand. Hence the map
({\ddagger})
is the zero map.
∎

So, if the Borel-ordinary
R=T
conjecture outlined in Remark
A.3.1
holds, then the eigenvariety is
always
ramified over weight space at points lifted from elliptic curves over imaginary quadratic fields.

However, it is
?=\mathrm{wB}
which is important for our applications; and the difference between the
?=\mathrm{wB}
and
?=\mathrm{B}
local conditions lies entirely in the “
\GL_{2}/K
adjoint” summand. Hence it is related to nearly-ordinary infinitesimal deformations of
\tau
. The assumption that the Bloch–Kato Selmer group vanishes means that these deformations are classified by their
infinitesimal Hodge–Tate weights
in the sense of
[
CM09
]
.

Proposition A.4.3
.

In the imaginary-quadratic case, the
?=\mathrm{wB}
deformation problem is unobstructed if and only if the infinitesimal Hodge–Tate weights of all nearly-ordinary infinitesimal deformations of
\tau
span a proper subspace of
K\otimes\mathbf{Q}_{p}
.

Assuming
p
splits in
K
, Calegari and Mazur have conjectured
[
CM09
, Conjecture 1.3]
that
\tau
cannot admit any infinitesimal deformation whose infinitesimal Hodge–Tate weight lies in
K\subset K\otimes\mathbf{Q}_{p}
. So the Calegari–Mazur conjecture certainly implies that
?=\mathrm{wB}
is always unobstructed for split primes
p
, and hence that deformability holds for all split
p
.

Remark A.4.4
.

It seems natural to conjecture that the
\GL_{2}/K
eigenvariety should lift to a subspace of the
\GSp_{4}/K
eigenvariety, and the
\GSp_{4}/\mathbf{Q}
eigenvariety should ramify over weight space along the image of this map. However, even verifying the existence of the lifting is difficult, since classical points are not dense in the
\GL_{2}/K
eigenvariety and hence we cannot deduce this from the analogous lifting result for classical automorphic forms.

Remark A.4.5
.

For lifts from real quadratic fields, the numerology is different: the “obstruction class” in the cohomology of
\mathrm{ad}^{0}(\rho)(1)
is forced to live in the Asai summand, not the adjoint summand. This means that the
\mathrm{wKl}
deformation problem is automatically obstructed, which accounts for the observation in
[
Pil20
]
that real-quadratic lifts give components of the Klingen partial eigenvariety with larger-than-expected dimension.

Since the deformation theory of the
\GL_{2}/K
adjoint is unobstructed, we obtain an entire component of the
\GSp_{4}
eigenvariety passing through
\pi
consisting of forms lifted from
\GL_{2}/K
. In this case,
?=\mathrm{wB}
is obstructed if and only if
?=\mathrm{B}
is, and (assuming the expected
R=T
conjecture) this holds precisely when there exist additional, non-lift components pasing through
\pi
.

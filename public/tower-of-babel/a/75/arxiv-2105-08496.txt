Combinatorics of minimal absent words for a sliding window
Tooru Akagi 1, Yuki Kuhara 1, Takuya Mieno 1,2, Yuto Nakashima 1, Shunsuke Inenaga 1,3, Hideo Bannai 4, Masayuki Takeda 1

Abstract
A string w w is called a minimal absent word (MAW) for another string T T if w w does not occur in T T but the proper substrings of w w occur in T T . For example, let Σ = { 𝚊 , 𝚋 , 𝚌 } \Sigma=\{\mathtt{a,b,c}\} be the alphabet. Then, the set of MAWs for string w = 𝚊𝚋𝚊𝚊𝚋 w=\mathtt{abaab} is { 𝚊𝚊𝚊 , 𝚊𝚊𝚋𝚊 , 𝚋𝚊𝚋 , 𝚋𝚋 , 𝚌 } \{\mathtt{aaa,aaba,bab,bb,c}\} . In this paper, we study combinatorial properties of MAWs in the sliding window model, namely, how the set of MAWs changes when a sliding window of fixed length d d is shifted over the input string T T of length n n , where 1 ≤ d < n 1\leq d<n . We present tight upper and lower bounds on the maximum number of changes in the set of MAWs for a sliding window over T T , both in the cases of general alphabets and binary alphabets. Our bounds improve on the previously known best bounds [Crochemore et al., 2020].

1.2
MAWs for sliding window

This paper follows the recent line of research on

MAWs for the sliding window model
,
which was initiated by Crochemore et al.
[
10
]
.
In this model, the goal is to compute or analyze
\mathsf{MAW}(T[i..i+d-1])
,
as
i
is incremented, each time by 1, from 1 to
n-d+1
. For intuition, consider sliding a length-
d
window on
T
from left to right.

Crochemore et al.
[
10
]
presented a
suffix-tree based algorithm that maintains the set of all MAWs
for a sliding window in
O(\sigma n)
time using
O(\sigma d)
working space.
Crochemore et al.
[
10
]
also showed
how their algorithm can be applied to
approximate pattern matching under the
length weighted index

(
LWI
) metric
[
6
]
.

The (in)efficiency of their algorithms is heavily dependent on
combinatorial properties of MAWs for the sliding window.
In particular, Crochemore et al.
[
10
]

studied the number of MAWs to be added/deleted when the current window is shifted to the right by one character.
As was done in
[
10
]
,
for ease of discussion let us separately consider

•

adding a new character
T[i+d]
to the current window
T[i..i+d-1]
of length
d
which forms
T[i..i+d]
, and

•

deleting the leftmost character
T[i-1]

from the current window
T[i-1..i+d-1]
which forms
T[i..i+d-1]
of length
d
.

We remark that these two operations are symmetric.

Crochemore et al.
[
10
]

considered how many MAWs can change before and after
the window has been shifted by one position,
and showed that

\displaystyle|\mathsf{MAW}(T[i..i+d])\bigtriangleup\mathsf{MAW}(T[i..i+d-1])|\leq(s_{i}-s_{\alpha})(\sigma-1)+\sigma+1,

\displaystyle|\mathsf{MAW}(T[i-1..i+d-1])\bigtriangleup\mathsf{MAW}(T[i..i+d-1])|\leq(p_{i}-p_{\beta})(\sigma-1)+\sigma+1,

where

denotes the symmetric difference and

•

s_{i}
is the length of the longest repeating suffix of
T[i..i+d-1]

•

is the length of the longest suffix of
T[i..i+d-1]

\alpha=T[i+d]

•

p_{i}
is that of the longest repeating prefix of
T[i..i+d-1]
, and

•

p_{\beta}
is the length of the longest prefix of
T[i..i+d-1]

Since both

are:

\begin{array}[]{rcl}|\mathsf{MAW}(T[i..i+d])\bigtriangleup\mathsf{MAW}(T[i..i+d-1])|&\in&O(\sigma d),\\
|\mathsf{MAW}(T[i-1..i+d-1])\bigtriangleup\mathsf{MAW}(T[i..i+d-1])|&\in&O(\sigma d).\end{array}

(1)

Crochemore et al.
[
10
]
also considered

in the set of MAWs for every sliding window
over the string

, and showed that

\sum_{i=1}^{n-d}\big(|\mathsf{MAW}(T[i..i+d-1])\bigtriangleup\mathsf{MAW}(T[i+1..i+d])|\big)\in O(\sigma n).

(2)

1.3
Our contribution

The goal of this paper is to give more rigorous analyses
on the number of MAWs for the sliding window model.
This study is well motivated since
revealing more combinatorial insights to the sets of MAWs for the sliding windows
can lead to more efficient algorithms for computing them.

In this paper, we first give the following upper bounds:

\begin{array}[]{rcl}|\mathsf{MAW}(T[i..i+d])\bigtriangleup\mathsf{MAW}(T[i..i+d-1])|&\leq&d+\sigma_{i,i+d-1}+1,\\
|\mathsf{MAW}(T[i-1..i+d-1])\bigtriangleup\mathsf{MAW}(T[i..i+d-1])|&\leq&d+\sigma_{i,i+d-1}+1,\end{array}

(3)

where
\sigma_{x,y}
is the number of distinct characters in
T[x,y]
.
We then show that our new upper bounds in (
3
) are
tight

by showing a family of strings achieving these bounds.

Since
\sigma_{i,i+d-1}\leq d
always holds,
we immediately obtain new asymptotic upper bounds

\begin{array}[]{rcl}|\mathsf{MAW}(T[i..i+d])\bigtriangleup\mathsf{MAW}(T[i..i+d-1])|&\in&O(d),\\
|\mathsf{MAW}(T[i-1..i+d-1])\bigtriangleup\mathsf{MAW}(T[i..i+d-1])|&\in&O(d).\end{array}

(4)

Our new upper bounds in (
4
) improve Crochemore et al.’s upper
bounds in (
1
)
for any alphabet of size
\sigma\in\omega(1)
.
Our upper bounds in (
4
)
are also
tight
as there exists a family of strings achieving the matching lower bounds
\Omega(d)
.

In this paper, we also present
a new upper bound for the total changes of MAWs:

\sum_{i=1}^{n-d}\big(|\mathsf{MAW}(T[i..i+d-1])\bigtriangleup\mathsf{MAW}(T[i+1..i+d])|\big)\in O(\min\{\sigma,d\}n)

(5)

which improves the previous bound
O(\sigma n)
in (
2
).
We then show that this new upper bound
in (
5
) is also
tight
.

All of our new bounds aforementioned
are tight for any alphabet of size
\sigma_{i,i+d-1}\geq 3
.
We further explore the case of binary alphabets with
\sigma_{i,i+d-1}=2
,
and show that there exist even tighter bounds in the binary case.
Namely, for
\sigma_{i,i+d-1}=2
, we prove that

\begin{array}[]{rcl}|\mathsf{MAW}(T[i..i+d])\bigtriangleup\mathsf{MAW}(T[i..i+d-1])|&\leq&\max\{3,d\},\\
|\mathsf{MAW}(T[i-1..i+d-1])\bigtriangleup\mathsf{MAW}(T[i..i+d-1])|&\leq&\max\{3,d\}.\end{array}

(6)

We remark that plugging
\sigma_{i,i+d-1}=2
into
(
3
) for the general case
only gives
d+\sigma_{i,i+d-1}+1=d+3
,
which is larger than
\max\{3,d\}
in (
6
).
We consider the case
\sigma_{i,i+d-1}\geq d
in Lemmas
10
and
11
.
We also show that the upper bounds
\max\{3,d\}
in (
6
)
are
tight
by giving matching lower bounds with a family of binary strings.

A part of the results reported in this article appeared in
a preliminary version of this paper
[
18
]
.
In addition, this present article considers the case of binary alphabets
and presents tight upper and lower bounds for this case.

2.2
Minimal absent words (MAWs)

A string

Let

2.3
MAWs for a sliding window

Given a string
T

n

a sliding window

our goal is to analyze how many MAWs for the sliding window
can change when the window shifts over the string

We will consider both the maximum change per one shift,
and the maximum total number of changes when sliding the window from the beginning to the end.

As was done in
[
10
]

for simplicity, we separately consider two symmetric operations
of appending a new character to the right of the window and
of deleting the leftmost character from the window.

Example 2
.

Let
\Sigma=\{\mathtt{a,b,c,d}\}
. Consider appending character
\mathtt{c}
to the right of string
\mathtt{cbaaaa}
.
Then,

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\mathsf{MAW}(\mathtt{cbaaaa})&=&\{\mathtt{cc,bb,aaaaa,bc,ab,ca,ac,d}\},\\
\mathsf{MAW}(\mathtt{cbaaaac})&=&\{\mathtt{cc,bb,aaaaa,bc,ab,ca,acb,bac,baac,baaac,d}\}.\end{array}

Thus

\mathsf{MAW}(\mathtt{cbaaaa})\bigtriangleup\mathsf{MAW}(\mathtt{cbaaaac})=\{\mathtt{\underline{ac},acb,bac,baac,baaac}\}

where the underlined string is deleted from
and the strings without underlines are added to
the set of MAWs by appending
\mathtt{c}
to
\mathtt{cbaaaa}

Let

3.1.1
Changes to MAWs when a character already occurring in the window is added to the right

In this subsection, we consider the case where a new character

that is appended to the right of the current window

already occurs in

. This means that
\sigma_{i,j}=\sigma_{i,j+1}

The next lemma shows that a conflict occurs between
\mathcal{M}_{1}
and
\mathcal{M}_{2}
when

Lemma 6
.

For any

such that

and
\sigma_{i,j}=\sigma_{i,j+1}
,

|\mathcal{M}_{1}|+|\mathcal{M}_{2}|\leq\sigma_{i,j}

Proof.

Let
\mathtt{c}=T[j+1]
and let

be the length of the maximum run of
\mathtt{c}
’s that is a suffix of
T[i..j]
.
If
T[j]\neq\mathtt{c}
then let

.
By the definition of
\mathcal{M}_{1}
,
\mathtt{c}^{k+2}
is the only candidate
for the Type-1 MAW for

, in which case

occurs only once
in

as a suffix.
This means that
\mathtt{c}^{k+2}

only if
\mathtt{c}^{k}
is the longest run of
\mathtt{c}
’s in
T[i..j]
.

Now suppose that
\mathtt{c}^{k+2}
is a Type-1 MAW for
T[i..j+1]
,
and let
a^{\prime}u^{\prime}\mathtt{c}
denote a Type-2 MAW for
T[i..j+1]
.
Then, by definition,
a^{\prime}u^{\prime}
occurs only once in
T[i..j+1]
as a suffix
(see also the middle of Figure
1
).

•

If
|u^{\prime}|\geq k
, then

\mathtt{c}^{k+1}
is a suffix of
u^{\prime}
as shown in Figure
3
.
However, by the definition of Type-2 MAWs,

must occur in

(see also the middle of Figure

),
which implies that
\mathtt{c}^{k+1}
occurs in
T[i..j]
.
This contradicts that
\mathtt{c}^{k}
is the longest run of
\mathtt{c}
’s in
T[i..j]
.

•

If
|u^{\prime}|<k
,
then

with

as a suffix,
and this contradicts that

is a MAW for

.

Hence

cannot be in

, which leads to

by Lemma

.
Thus,
|\mathcal{M}_{1}|+|\mathcal{M}_{2}|\leq\sigma_{i,j}

for any string

such that

contains at least one character that is equal to

.
∎

Recall that Lemma
2
and Lemma

in the case where

.
Compared to this, Lemma

shaves the total size of

by one

Coupled with Lemma

leads us to the following corollary:

Corollary 1
.

For any

,

|\mathsf{MAW}(T[i..j+1])\bigtriangleup\mathsf{MAW}(T[i..j])|\leq\sigma_{i,j+1}+d

.

3.2
Changes to MAWs when the leftmost character is deleted

Next, we analyze the number of changes of MAWs when deleting the leftmost character from a string.
By a symmetric argument to Theorem
1
, we obtain:

Corollary 2
.

For any
1<i\leq j\leq n
,

|\mathsf{MAW}(T[i..j])\bigtriangleup\mathsf{MAW}(T[i-1..j])|\leq\sigma_{i,j}+d+1
where

and

\sigma_{i,j}
is the number of distinct characters that occur in

.
Also, the upper bound is tight
when
\sigma\geq 3
and
\sigma_{i,j}+1\leq\sigma
.

Finally, by combining Theorem
1
and Corollary

,
we obtain the next theorem:

Theorem 2
.

Let
d
be the window length.
For any string
T
of length
n>d
and each position

in
T
with

,

|\mathsf{MAW}(T[i..i+d-1])\bigtriangleup\mathsf{MAW}(T[i+1..i+d])|\in O(d)
.
Also, there exists a string

with

which satisfies

|\mathsf{MAW}(T^{\prime}[j..j+d-1])\bigtriangleup\mathsf{MAW}(T^{\prime}[j+1..j+d])|\in\Omega(d)

for some

.

This theorem improves Crochemore et al.’s upper bound
for
|\mathsf{MAW}(T[i..i+d-1])\bigtriangleup\mathsf{MAW}(T[i+1..i+d])|\in O(\sigma d)

for any alphabet of size

.

3.3
Total changes of MAWs when sliding a window on a string

In this subsection, we consider the total number of changes of MAWs
when sliding the window of length
d
from the beginning of
T
to the end of

We denote the total number of changes of MAWs by

\mathcal{S}(T,d)=\sum_{i=1}^{n-d}|\mathsf{MAW}(T[i..i+d-1])\bigtriangleup\mathsf{MAW}(T[i+1..i+d])|

The following lemma is known:

Lemma 7

(
[
10
]
)
.

For a string
T
of length
n>d
over an alphabet
\Sigma
of size
\sigma

\mathcal{S}(T,d)\in O(\sigma n)

The aim of this subsection is to give a more rigorous bound for

We first show that the above bound is tight under some conditions.

Lemma 8
.

The upper bound of Lemma
7

is tight when
\sigma\leq d
and

Proof.

If
\sigma=2
, the lower bound

\mathcal{S}(T,d)\in\Omega(n-d)=\Omega(\sigma(n-d))

by string

\mathsf{MAW}((\mathtt{ab})^{d/2})\bigtriangleup\mathsf{MAW}((\mathtt{ba})^{d/2})=\{(\mathtt{ab})^{d/2},(\mathtt{ba})^{d/2}\}

In the sequel, we consider the case where
\sigma\geq 3
.
Let
k

(k-1)(\sigma-1)\leq d<k(\sigma-1)

Note that

\sigma\leq d

Let

We consider a string

For any two distinct occurrences

.
Otherwise,

Additionally, the number

.

Next, we consider the case where

Lemma 9
.

For a string

Next, we show that there is a string

Theorem 3
.

.

.

We remark that

covers most interesting cases

to

.

4
Tighter bounds for binary alphabets

In this section we consider the case where
\sigma^{\prime}=2
,
i.e. when both the current sliding window
S=T[i..i+d-1]
and
the next window
S\alpha=T[i..i+d]

with a new character
\alpha=T[i+d]

consist of two distinct characters.
The goal of this section is to show that

.

In what follows, let us denote by

As a warm up, we begin with the two following lemmas which show that

and

Lemma 10
.

For any string
S
over
\Sigma_{2}
with

,

|\mathsf{MAW}(S)\bigtriangleup\mathsf{MAW}(S\mathtt{0})|\leq 3
.

For each

of length

,

\displaystyle\mathsf{MAW}(\mathtt{0})\bigtriangleup\mathsf{MAW}(\mathtt{00})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{\underline{\mathtt{00}},\mathtt{000}\},

\displaystyle\mathsf{MAW}(\mathtt{1})\bigtriangleup\mathsf{MAW}(\mathtt{10})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{\underline{\mathtt{0}},\mathtt{00},\mathtt{01}\},

where the underlined strings are those in
\mathsf{MAW}(S)\setminus\mathsf{MAW}(S\mathtt{0})

and the strings without underlines are those in
\mathsf{MAW}(S\mathtt{0})\setminus\mathsf{MAW}(S)
.
Thus the lemma holds.
∎

Lemma 11
.

For any string
S
over
\Sigma_{2}

|\mathsf{MAW}(S)\bigtriangleup\mathsf{MAW}(S\mathtt{0})|\leq 3

For each

\displaystyle\mathsf{MAW}(\mathtt{00})\bigtriangleup\mathsf{MAW}(\mathtt{000})

\displaystyle\{\underline{\mathtt{000}},\mathtt{0000}\},

\displaystyle\{\underline{\mathtt{10}},\mathtt{101}\},

\displaystyle\{\underline{\mathtt{00}},\mathtt{000}\},

\displaystyle\mathsf{MAW}(\mathtt{11})\bigtriangleup\mathsf{MAW}(\mathtt{110})

\displaystyle\{\underline{\mathtt{0}},\mathtt{00},\mathtt{01}\},

Thus the lemma holds.
∎

We move onto the case where

.
Our first observation is that it is sufficient to consider the case that

is not unary.
For any

, it is clear that

Now let us consider
\mathtt{1}^{d}
in the next lemma.

Lemma 12
.

For any

.
Then, there exists another string

over
\Sigma_{2}

Since

\mathsf{MAW}(V)\setminus\mathsf{MAW}(V\mathtt{0})=\{\mathtt{0}\}
.

As in the case of non-binary alphabets,

/

/

Since the appended character is

, then
S[1]=\mathtt{1}
.
However, since the appended character is
\mathtt{0}
,

\mathtt{10}
must occur somewhere in
S\mathtt{0}

(see also the bottom picture of Figure
5
).
Thus
\mathtt{10}
is not a MAW for
S\mathtt{0}
.

Hence, the first position of
S
cannot be
assigned to any MAW of Type 3 for
S\mathtt{0}
,
leading to
|\mathcal{M}_{3}|\leq d-2
for any binary string
S
of length
d\geq 3
.
∎

In other words, Lemma
13
shows that in the binary case
with
\sigma^{\prime}=2
,
the maximum number of added Type-3 MAWs is 1 less than in the case
with
\sigma^{\prime}\geq 3
.

,

Next, we consider the total number of added Type-1/Type-2 MAWs.

From Lemma
6
, the next corollary holds.

Corollary 3
.

|\mathcal{M}_{1}|+|\mathcal{M}_{2}|\leq 2
on any binary string
S
when the character to be appended already occurs in
S
.

A direct consequence of Lemma
13
and Lemma
6

is an upper bound for the added MAWs

|\mathcal{M}_{1}|+|\mathcal{M}_{2}|+|\mathcal{M}_{3}|\leq d
for any binary string
S0

with
|S|=d\geq 3
.
In what follows, we further reduce this upper bound to
|\mathcal{M}_{1}|+|\mathcal{M}_{2}|+|\mathcal{M}_{3}|\leq d-1
. For this purpose, we introduce the next lemma:

Lemma 14
.

For any binary string
S
over
\Sigma_{2}

such that
|S|=d\geq 3
,

|\mathcal{M}_{2}|
is at most the number of occurrences of
\mathtt{0}
in
S[1..d-1]
,
and
|\mathcal{M}_{3}|
is at most the number of occurrences of
\mathtt{1}
in
S[3..d]
.

Proof.

First we consider Type-2 MAWs for
S\mathtt{0}
.
Since
S
is a binary string, by Lemma
3
,
there are at most two MAWs in
\mathcal{M}_{2}
.
We assume that there are two MAWs in
\mathcal{M}_{2}
and let
au\mathtt{0}
and
a^{\prime}\mathtt{1}
be the two MAWs where
a,a^{\prime}\in\Sigma_{2}
and
u,u^{\prime}\in\Sigma_{2}^{*}
.
By the definition of Type-2 MAWs,
u\mathtt{0}
and
u^{\prime}\mathtt{1}
occur in
S
and
u[|u|]=u^{\prime}[u^{\prime}]=\mathtt{0}
since
au
and
a^{\prime}u^{\prime}
occur in
S\mathtt{0}
as suffixes.
For any two positions
t_{0}
and
t_{1}
such that
S[t_{0}..t_{0}+|u\mathtt{0}|-1]=u\mathtt{0}
,
S[t_{1}..t_{1}+|u^{\prime}\mathtt{1}|-1]=u^{\prime}\mathtt{1}
,
t_{0}+|u|\neq t_{1}+|u^{\prime}|
. Consequently, there are at least two occurrences of
\mathtt{0}
in
S
since
S[t_{0}+|u|-1]=\mathtt{0}
and
S[t_{1}+|u^{\prime}|-1]=\mathtt{0}
.
Since
t_{0}+|u\mathtt{0}|-1\leq d
,
|\mathcal{M}_{2}|
is at most the number of occurrences of
\mathtt{0}
in
S[1..d-1]
.

Second we consider Type-3 MAWs for
S\mathtt{0}
.
let
au\mathtt{0}
be the Type-3 MAW where
a\in\Sigma_{2}
,
u,\in\Sigma_{2}^{*}
since
u\mathtt{0}
must be a suffix of
S\mathtt{0}
.
By the definition of Type-3 MAW,
there has to be an occurrence of
au
in
S
.
Note that this occurrence has to be immediately followed by a
\mathtt{1}

since
au\mathtt{0}
does not occur in
S\mathtt{0}
.
Thus, for each
au\mathtt{0}\in\mathcal{M}_{3}
,
we need an occurrence of
au\mathtt{1}
in
S
.
Since
|au|\geq 1
, we clearly cannot use
the first position of
S
as the ending position of
au1
.
Also, it follows from Lemma
13
(and its proof) that
the second position of
S
cannot be the ending position of
au

for any Type-3 MAW
aub
for
S\mathtt{0}
.
This implies that there is no Type-3 MAW that corresponds to
the
\mathtt{1}
in the second position of
S
.
Thus, the total number of Type-3 MAWs for
S\mathtt{0}

is upper bounded by the number of occurrences of
\mathtt{1}
in
S[3..d]
.
∎

Intuitively, Lemma
14
implies that
flipping substrings
\mathtt{1}
in
S[3..d-1]

does not increase the total number of Type-2 and Type-3 MAWs for
S\mathtt{0}
.

Lemma 15
.

For any binary string
S
over
\Sigma_{2}
with
|S|=d\geq 3
,

|\mathcal{M}_{1}|+|\mathcal{M}_{2}|+|\mathcal{M}_{3}|\leq d-1
.

Proof.

S
is not unary due to Lemma
12
.
It immediately follows from Lemma
13
and Corollary
3
that
|\mathcal{M}_{1}|+|\mathcal{M}_{2}|+|\mathcal{M}_{3}|\leq d
,
and assume on the contrary that there exists a binary string
S^{\prime}
over
\Sigma_{2}
such that
|\mathcal{M}_{1}|+|\mathcal{M}_{2}|+|\mathcal{M}_{3}|=d
.
Then, it has to be
|\mathcal{M}_{1}|+|\mathcal{M}_{2}|=2
and
|\mathcal{M}_{3}|=d-2
, again by Lemma
13
and Corollary
3
. Therefore,
S^{\prime}[3..d]=\mathtt{1}^{d-2}
by Lemma
14
, and
|\mathcal{M}_{1}|=0
. Hence we must have
|\mathcal{M}_{2}|=2
, which leads to
S^{\prime}=\mathtt{00}\mathtt{1}^{d-2}
.

Now the sets of all the added MAWs for
S^{\prime}\mathtt{0}=\mathtt{00}\mathtt{1}^{d-2}\mathtt{0}
are

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\emptyset,

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{\mathtt{100},\mathtt{101}\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{3}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{\mathtt{0}\mathtt{1}^{k}\mathtt{0}\mid 1\leq k\leq d-3\},

which leads to
|\mathcal{M}_{1}|+|\mathcal{M}_{2}|+|\mathcal{M}_{3}|=d-1
, a contradiction. Thus the lemma holds.
∎

We obtain our main theorem:

Theorem 4
.

For any binary string
S
over
\Sigma_{2}
with
|S|=d\geq 3
,

|\mathsf{MAW}(S)\bigtriangleup\mathsf{MAW}(S\mathtt{0})|\leq d
,
and this upper bound is tight.

Proof.

The upper bound follows from Lemmas
1
and
15
,
and its tightness follows from
and our construction of the string
S^{\prime}\mathtt{0}
in the proof for Lemma
15
.
∎

The next corollary summarizes the results of this section.

Corollary 4
.

For any binary string
S
over
\Sigma_{2}
with
|S|=d
,

|\mathsf{MAW}(S)\bigtriangleup\mathsf{MAW}(S\mathtt{0})|\leq\max\{3,d\}
,
and this upper bound is tight for any
d\geq 1
.

Proof.

The upper bound follows from Lemmas
10
and
11
, Theorem
4
,
and its tightness follows from all possible binary cases shown in the proof for Lemmas
10
and
11
for
d=\{1,2\}
,
and our construction of the string

in the proof for Lemma
15
for

.
∎

5
Conclusions and future work

In this paper, we revisited the problem of
computing the minimal absent words (MAWs) for the sliding window model,
which was first considered by Crochemore et al.
[
10
]
.

We investigated combinatorial properties of
MAWs for a sliding window of fixed length
d
over a string of length
n
.
Our contributions are
matching upper and lower bounds

for the number of changes in the set of MAWs
for a sliding window when the window is shifted to the right by one character.
For the general case where the window
S
and the extended window
S\alpha

contain three or more distinct characters (i.e.
\sigma^{\prime}\geq 3
),
the number of changes in the set of MAWs for
S
and
S\alpha
is
at most
d+\sigma^{\prime}+1
and this bound is tight.
For the case of binary alphabets (i.e.
\sigma^{\prime}=2
),
it is upper bounded by
\max\{3,d\}
and this bound is also tight.

We also gave an asymptotically tight bound
O(\min\{d,\sigma\}n)

for the number
\mathcal{S}(T,d)
of total changes in the set of MAWs
for every sliding window of length
d
over any string
T
of length
n
,
where
\sigma
is the alphabet size for the whole input string
T
.

The following open questions are intriguing:

•

We showed that a matching lower bound
\mathcal{S}(T,d)\in\Omega(\min\{d,\sigma\}n)
when
n-d\in\Omega(n)
. Is there a similar lower bound when
n-d\in o(n)
?

•

Crochemore et al.
[
10
]

gave an online algorithm that maintains the set of MAWs
for a sliding window of length
d
in
O(\sigma n)
time.
Can one improve the running time to optimal
O(\min\{d,\sigma\}n)
?

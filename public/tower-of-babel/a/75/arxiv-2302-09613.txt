1.
Preliminaries

We denote by
\mathbb{D}

the unit disk and
\mathbb{U}:=\partial\mathbb{D}
the
unit circle.
For
z=x+iy\in\mathbb{C}
, the two complex
differential operators are defined by

u_{z}=\partial_{z}(u)=\frac{1}{2}\left(u_{x}-iu_{y}\right)~\mbox{and}~u_{\bar{z}}=\overline{\partial}_{z}(u)=\frac{1}{2}\left(u_{x}+iu_{y}\right),

where
u
is a complex valued function on
\mathbb{D}
.

1.1.
\alpha
-harmonic functions

For
\alpha>-1
, we denote by
\Delta_{\alpha}
the weighted Laplace operator corresponding to the so-called standard weight
w_{\alpha}(z)=(1-|z|^{2})^{\alpha}
, that is, for
z\in\mathbb{D}
,

(1.1)

\displaystyle\Delta_{\alpha,z}=\partial_{z}w_{\alpha}(z)^{-1}\overline{\partial}_{z}.

The weighted Laplacians of the form (
1.1
) were first systematically studied by Garabedian in
[
4
]
.

Let
g\in\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{D})

and
f\in\mathcal{C}^{2}(\mathbb{D})
. Of particular interest to us is the following
inhomogeneous
\alpha
-harmonic equation
in
\mathbb{D}
:

(1.2)

\displaystyle\Delta_{\alpha}(f)=g.

We also consider the associated
Dirichlet boundary value problem
of functions
f
, satisfying the equation (
1.2
),

(1.3)

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} \Delta_{\alpha}(f)&=g\;\;\;\;\text{in}\;\mathbb{D},\\
f&=f^{\ast}\;\;\text{on}\;\mathbb{T}.\end{aligned}\right.

Here the boundary data
f^{\ast}
is a distribution on
\mathbb{T}
, i.e.
f^{\ast}\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{T})
, and the boundary condition in (
1.3
) is understood as
f_{r}\to f^{\ast}\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{T})
as
r\to 1^{-}
, where

f_{r}(e^{i\theta}):=f(re^{i\theta}),\quad r\in[0,1).

If
g\equiv 0
in (
1.2
), the solutions to (
1.2
) are said to be
\alpha
-harmonic functions
. Obviously,
0
-harmonic functions are simply harmonic functions.

In
[
14
]
, Olofsson and Wittsten showed that if an
\alpha
-harmonic function
f
satisfies

\lim_{r\to 1^{-}}f_{r}=f^{\ast}\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{T})\;\;(\alpha>-1),

then it has the form of a
Poisson type integral

(1.4)

\displaystyle f(z)=\mathcal{P}_{\alpha}[f^{\ast}](z)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int^{2\pi}_{0}P_{\alpha}(ze^{-i\theta})f^{\ast}(e^{i\theta})d\theta

in
\mathbb{D}
,
where

P_{\alpha}(z)=\frac{(1-|z|^{2})^{\alpha+1}}{(1-z)(1-\overline{z})^{\alpha+1}}

is the
\alpha
-harmonic Poisson kernel
in
{\mathbb{D}}
.

Moreover,

P_{\alpha}(z)=\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}e_{\alpha,k}(z),\quad z\in\mathbb{D},

where

e_{\alpha,k}(z)=z^{k},\quad k\geq 0,

and

e_{\alpha,-k}(z)=\frac{1}{B(k,\alpha+1)}\left(\int_{0}^{1}t^{k-1}(1-t|z|^{2})^{\alpha}dt\right)\overline{z}^{k},\quad k\geq 1,

see
[
14
]
.

In particular, we obtain

e_{\alpha,k}(z)=\mathcal{P}_{\alpha}[e^{ik\theta}](z),\quad k\in\mathbb{Z}.

In addition, we have

(1.5)

\overline{\partial}e_{\alpha,-k}(z)=\frac{1}{B(k,\alpha+1)}(1-|z|^{2})^{\alpha}\,{\overline{z}}^{k-1},\quad k\geq 1,

where
B
is the beta function, see
[
14
, Lemma 1.1]
.

Recall that

M_{\alpha}(r)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{U}}|P_{\alpha}(ze^{-it})|dt.

The mapping
M_{\alpha}
is increasing on
[0,1)
and

(1.6)

\lim_{r\to 1}M_{\alpha}(r)=c_{\alpha}=\frac{\Gamma(\alpha+1)}{\Gamma^{2}(\alpha/2+1)}.

See
[
10
,
13
]
for related discussions.

1.2.
Generalized Hardy spaces

For
p\in(0,\infty]
, the
generalized Hardy space

\mathcal{H}^{p}_{\mathcal{G}}(\mathbb{D})
consists of all
measurable functions from
\mathbb{D}
to
\mathbb{C}
such that

M_{p}(r,f)
exists for all
r\in(0,1)
, and
\|f\|_{p}<\infty
,
where

M_{p}(r,f)=\left(\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}|f(re^{i\theta})|^{p}\,d\theta\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

and

\|f\|_{p}=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\sup\{M_{p}(r,f):\;0<r<1\}&\mbox{if }p\in(0,\infty),\\
\displaystyle\sup\{|f(z)|:\;z\in\mathbb{D}\}&\mbox{if }p=\infty.\end{cases}

The classical
Hardy space
\mathcal{H}^{p}(\mathbb{D})
(resp.
h^{p}(\mathbb{D})
) is the set of all elements of
\mathcal{H}^{p}_{\mathcal{G}}(\mathbb{D})
which are analytic (resp. harmonic) on
\mathbb{D}
. (cf.
[
2
,
3
]
).
Obviously,
\mathcal{H}^{p}_{\mathcal{G}}(\mathbb{D})\subset L^{p}(\mathbb{D})
for each
p\in(0,\infty]
.
We denote by
h^{p}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{D})
the corresponding Hardy space for
\alpha
-harmonic functions.

Denote by
L^{p}(\mathbb{T})~(p\in[1,\infty])
the space of all measurable functions
F

of
\mathbb{T}
into
\mathbb{C}
with

\|F\|_{p}=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}|F(e^{i\theta})|^{p}d\theta\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}&\mbox{if }p\in[1,\infty),\\
\displaystyle\sup\{|F(e^{i\theta})|:\;\theta\in[0,2\pi)\}&\mbox{if }p=\infty.\\
\end{cases}

It is well-known that if
F
is absolutely continuous, then it is of bounded variation. This implies that for
almost all
e^{i\theta}\in\mathbb{T}
, the derivative
\dot{F}(e^{i\theta})
exists, where

\dot{F}(e^{i\theta}):=\frac{dF(e^{i\theta})}{d\theta}.

In this paper, we consider the following problem :

For
\alpha>-1
, under what conditions on the boundary function
F
ensure that the
partial derivatives of its
\alpha
-harmonic extension
f=\mathcal{P}_{\alpha}[F]
, i.e.,
f_{z}
and
f_{\overline{z}}
, are in the space
\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{G}}^{p}(\mathbb{D})
(or
L^{p}(\mathbb{D})
), where
p\in[1,\infty]
?

It is worth noting that a similar problem for harmonic functions was treated in
[
15
]
and improved in
[
1
,
9
]
.

Theorem A.
(
[
15
, Theorem 1.2]
),
[
1
, Theorem 1.1]
)

Suppose that
f=P[F]
is a harmonic mapping in
\mathbb{D}
and

\dot{F}\in L^{p}(\mathbb{T})
, where
F
is an absolutely continuous function.

(1)

If
p\in[1,\infty)
, then both
f_{z}
and
\overline{f_{\overline{z}}}
are in
L^{p}(\mathbb{D}).

(2)

If
p=\infty
, then there exists a harmonic
mapping
f=P[F]
, with
\dot{F}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})
, such that
neither
f_{z}
nor
\overline{f_{\overline{z}}}
is in
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{D}).

Furthermore, under some additional conditions of
f
, they proved that the partial derivatives are in
H^{p}(\mathbb{D})
, for
p\in[1,\infty]
.

Theorem B.
(
[
15
, Theorem 1.3]
,
[
1
, Theorem 1.2]
)

Suppose that
p\in[1,\infty]
and
f=P[F]
is a
(K,K^{\prime})
-elliptic mapping in
\mathbb{D}
with

\dot{F}\in L^{p}(\mathbb{T})
, where
F
is an absolute continuous function,

and

. Then both

f_{z}
and
\overline{f_{\overline{z}}}
are in

In
[
1
]
, the authors showed that Theorem B also holds true for harmonic elliptic mappings, which are more general than harmonic quasiregular mapping.

In
[
9
]
, we provide a refinement of the two previous theorems, we prove that for
1<p<\infty
, both

f_{z}
and
\overline{f_{\overline{z}}}
are in
H^{p}(\mathbb{D})
without any extra conditions on

f
.

Theorem C.

[
9
]

Suppose that
F
is an absolute continuous function on
\mathbb{U}
and
f=P[F]
is a harmonic mapping in
\mathbb{D}
and

\dot{F}\in L^{p}(\mathbb{T})
.

(1)

If
p\in(1,\infty)
, then both
f_{z}
and
\overline{f_{\overline{z}}}
are in
\mathcal{H}^{p}(\mathbb{D}).
Moreover, there exists a constant

such that

\max(\|f_{z}\|_{p},\|\overline{f_{\overline{z}}}\|_{p})\leq A_{p}\|\dot{F}\|_{p}.

(2)

If

or

, then both

f_{z}
and
\overline{f_{\overline{z}}}
are in
\mathcal{H}^{p}(\mathbb{D})
if and only if
H(\dot{F})\in L^{p}(\mathbb{U})
. Moreover,

2izf_{z}(z)=P[\dot{F}+iH(\dot{F})](z),

where

denotes the Hilbert transformation of

.

Main results

Assume

2.1.
Estimate of the angular derivative
\partial_{\theta}f
,
f
is
\alpha

Theorem 2.1
.

Suppose
\alpha>-1
and
1\leq p\leq\infty
. Let
f=P_{\alpha}[F]
be an
\alpha
-harmonic mapping on
\mathbb{D}
with the boundary
F
being absolute continuous with
\dot{F}\in L^{p}(0,2\pi)
. Then

\partial_{\theta}f=\mathcal{P}_{\alpha}[{\dot{F}}]\in h_{\alpha}^{p}(\mathbb{D})

and

\|\partial_{\theta}f\|_{p}\leq c_{\alpha}\|\dot{F}\|_{p},

where
c_{\alpha}
is defined in (
1.6
).

Proof.
Assume that
f
is
\alpha
-harmonic function and

f=\mathcal{P}_{\alpha}[F],

with
F
is absolute continuous and
\dot{F}\in L^{p}(0,2\pi)
, with
p\geq 1
.

f(z)=\mathcal{P}_{\alpha}[F](z)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{U}}P_{\alpha}(ze^{-it})F(e^{it})\,dt.

For
z=re^{i\theta}
, we have

\partial_{\theta}P_{\alpha}(ze^{-it})=-\partial_{t}P_{\alpha}(ze^{-it}).

By integration by parts, we deduce

(2.2)

\partial_{\theta}f=\mathcal{P}_{\alpha}[{\dot{F}}].

Using Jensen’s inequality, we have

|\partial_{\theta}f(re^{i\theta})|^{p}\leq M^{p-1}_{\alpha}(r)\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}|P_{\alpha}(ze^{-it})||\dot{F}(e^{it})|^{p}\,dt.

Using Fubini theorem, we obtain

\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}|\partial_{\theta}f(re^{i\theta})|^{p}d\theta\leq M^{p}_{\alpha}(r)\|\dot{F}\|_{L^{p}}^{p}.

Thus,

\sup_{0<r<1}\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}|\partial_{\theta}f(re^{i\theta})|^{p}d\theta\leq c^{p}_{\alpha}\|\dot{F}\|_{L^{p}}^{p}.

For
p=\infty
, it is clear that

|\partial_{\theta}f(re^{i\theta})|\leq M_{\alpha}(r)\|\dot{F}\|_{\infty}\leq c_{\alpha}\|\dot{F}\|_{\infty}.

Therefore, we obtain the desired result.
∎

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\alpha>-1
and
f=\mathcal{P}_{\alpha}[F]
be an
\alpha
-harmonic mapping on
\mathbb{D}
with the boundary function
F
being absolute continuous. Then

(2.3)

{\overline{z}}\overline{\partial}f(z)=-\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{(1-|z|^{2})^{\alpha}}{(1-{\overline{z}}e^{it})^{\alpha+1}}\,\dot{F}(e^{it})\,dt.

Proof.
Let
\alpha>-1
, an easy computation shows that

\overline{\partial}P_{\alpha}(z)=\frac{\alpha+1}{(1-{\overline{z}})^{2}}\left(\frac{1-|z|^{2}}{1-{\overline{z}}}\right)^{\alpha}=(\alpha+1)\frac{(1-|z|^{2})^{\alpha}}{(1-{\overline{z}})^{\alpha+2}}.

As

f(z)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}P_{\alpha}(ze^{-it})F(e^{it})\,dt,
we obtain

\overline{\partial}f(z)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\overline{\partial}[P_{\alpha}(ze^{-it})]F(e^{it})\,dt=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\overline{\partial}P_{\alpha}(ze^{-it})e^{it}F(e^{it})\,dt.

Hence,

\overline{\partial}f(z)=(\alpha+1)(1-|z|^{2})^{\alpha}\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{e^{it}}{(1-{\overline{z}}e^{it})^{\alpha+2}}\,F(e^{it})\,dt.

Using integration by parts, we obtain

(2.4)

\overline{\partial}f(z)=-\frac{1}{{\overline{z}}}\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{(1-|z|^{2})^{\alpha}}{(1-{\overline{z}}e^{it})^{\alpha+1}}\dot{F}(e^{it})\,dt.

∎

Denote

I_{\alpha}(r)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{(1-|z|^{2})^{\alpha}}{|1-{\overline{z}}e^{it}|^{\alpha+1}}dt,

where
r=|z|.

Lemma 2.2
.

[
8

Let

.

(1)

If
\displaystyle\alpha>0
, then

I_{\alpha}(r)\leq\frac{\Gamma(\alpha)}{\Gamma^{2}(\alpha/2+1/2)}.

(2)

If
\displaystyle-1<\alpha<0
, then

I_{\alpha}(r)\leq\frac{\Gamma(-\alpha)}{\Gamma^{2}(1/2-\alpha/2)}(1-r^{2})^{\alpha}.

Proposition 2.1
.

Suppose
\alpha>-1
and
1\leq p\leq\infty
. Let
f=\mathcal{P}_{\alpha}[F]
be an
\alpha
-harmonic mapping on
\mathbb{D}
with
F
is absolute continuous and satisfies

. Then

We use the equation (
2.4
) and apply Jensen’s inequality to estimate the mean of the function
{\overline{z}}\overline{\partial}f(z)
.

Proof.

Using (
2.4
), we have

|{\overline{z}}\overline{\partial}f(z)|\leq\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{(1-|z|^{2})^{\alpha}}{|1-{\overline{z}}e^{it}|^{\alpha+1}}\,|\dot{F}(e^{it})|\,dt.

3.
Schwartz Lemma for
\alpha
-harmonic functions

The Schwarz lemma for analytic functions plays a vital role in complex analysis and has been generalized to various spaces of functions.

Heinz
[
6
]
generalized it to the class of complex-valued harmonic functions, that
is, if
f
is a complex-valued harmonic function from
\mathbb{D}
into itself with
f(0)=0
, then for
z\in\mathbb{D}
,

\ f(z)|\leq\frac{4}{\pi}\arctan|z|.

Hethcote
[
7
]
improved Heinz’s result, by removing the assumption
f(0)=0
, i .e., let
f
be a harmonic function from
\mathbb{D}
to
\mathbb{D}
, then

\left|f(z)-\frac{1-|z|^{2}}{1+|z|^{2}}f(0)\right|\leq\frac{4}{\pi}\arctan|z|.

In
[
11
]
, Li et al. stated a Schwarz type lemma for solutions of the
\alpha
-harmonic equation
\Delta_{\alpha}(f)=g
, with the condition
\alpha\geq 0
. For
g=0
, they obtain

Theorem D.

[
11
, Theorem 2.1]

Let
\alpha\geq 0
and
f^{*}\in\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{U})
and
f=P_{\alpha}[f^{*}]
an
\alpha
-harmonic function on
\mathbb{D}
such that
f(0)=0
. Then

|f(z)|\leq 2^{\alpha}\frac{4}{\pi}|f^{*}|_{\infty}\arctan|z|.

In the proof, the authors used the assumption
P_{\alpha}[|f^{*}|](0)=0
instead of
P_{\alpha}[f^{*}](0)=0
. Clearly the assumption
P_{\alpha}[|f^{*}|](0)=0
implies that
f^{*}=0
and thus
f=0
. In order to fill this gap, in
[
12
]
, the authors proved the following

Theorem E.

[
12
, Theorem 1.1]
Suppose that
f^{*}\in\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{U})
and
f=P_{\alpha}[f^{*}]
.

(a)

If
\alpha\geq 0
, then

|f(z)|\leq 2^{\alpha+1}\frac{|f|_{\infty}}{\pi}\arctan\left(\frac{1+|z|}{1-|z|}\tan\frac{c\pi}{2}\right).

(b)

If
\alpha<0
, then

|f(z)|\leq 2^{1-\alpha}\frac{|f|_{\infty}}{\pi}(1-|z|^{2})^{\alpha}\arctan\left(\frac{1+|z|}{1-|z|}\tan\frac{c\pi}{2}\right),

where
\displaystyle c=\frac{P_{\alpha}[|f^{*}|](0)}{|f^{*}|_{\infty}}
.

In our opinion, it is more natural to provide Schwarz type lemmas for
\alpha
-harmonic functions involving
f(0)
instead of
P_{\alpha}[|f^{*}|](0)
.

The aim of this section is to prove a Schwarz type lemma for
\alpha
-harmonic functions from the unit disc to itself in the spirit of Hethcote
[
7
]
.

Let

g_{\alpha}(z):=\left(\frac{1-|z|^{2}}{1-\overline{z}}\right)^{\alpha},

so

(3.1)

P_{\alpha}(z)=\left(\frac{1-|z|^{2}}{1-\overline{z}}\right)^{\alpha}\frac{1-|z|^{2}}{|1-z|^{2}}=g_{\alpha}(z)P(z),

where

P(z)=\frac{1-|z|^{2}}{|1-z|^{2}},

is the classical Poisson kernel.

Let

|g_{\alpha}(r)|_{\infty}=\sup_{0\leq\theta\leq 2\pi}|g_{\alpha}(re^{i\theta})|.

One can check that

(3.2)

|g_{\alpha}(r)|_{\infty}\leq 2^{\alpha},\quad\mbox{for}\quad\alpha\geq 0,

and

(3.3)

|g_{\alpha}(r)|_{\infty}\leq(1-r)^{\alpha},\quad\mbox{for}\quad\alpha<0.

Let
f:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{D}
be an
\alpha
-harmonic mapping from the unit disc to itself. Then, we can write

f(z)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{U}}P_{\alpha}(ze^{-it})f^{*}(e^{it})\,dt,

where
f^{*}
is the boundary function of
f
, with
|f^{*}|_{\infty}\leq 1.

Let

G_{\alpha}(z):=g_{\alpha}[f](z)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{U}}g_{\alpha}(ze^{-it})f^{*}(e^{it})\,dt=\frac{(1-|z|^{2})^{\alpha}}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{U}}\frac{f^{*}(e^{it})}{(1-{\overline{z}}e^{it})^{\alpha}}\,dt.

Remark, in the case
\alpha=0
,
G_{0}(z)=f(0)
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\alpha>-1
and
f
be an
\alpha
-harmonic function from the unit disc to itself. Then

\left|f(z)-\frac{1-|z|^{2}}{1+|z|^{2}}G_{\alpha}(z)\right|\leq\frac{4}{\pi}|g_{\alpha}(|z|)|_{\infty}\arctan|z|.

In particular

(1)

If
\alpha\geq 0
, then

\left|f(z)-\frac{1-|z|^{2}}{1+|z|^{2}}G_{\alpha}(z)\right|\leq\frac{2^{\alpha+2}}{\pi}\arctan|z|.

(2)

If
\alpha<0
, then

\left|f(z)-\frac{1-|z|^{2}}{1+|z|^{2}}G_{\alpha}(z)\right|\leq\frac{4}{\pi}(1-|z|)^{\alpha}\arctan|z|.

Proof.

We have

\left|f(z)-\frac{1-|z|^{2}}{1+|z|^{2}}G_{\alpha}(z)\right|\leq\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{U}}\left|P(ze^{-it})-\frac{1-|z|^{2}}{1+|z|^{2}}\right|\left|g_{\alpha}(ze^{-it})f^{*}(e^{it})\ \right|\,dt

\leq\frac{4}{\pi}|f^{*}|_{\infty}|g_{\alpha}(|z|)_{\infty}\arctan|z|.

Indeed, by Hethcote
[
7
]
, it yields
\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{U}}\left|P(ze^{-it})-\frac{1-|z|^{2}}{1+|z|^{2}}\right|dt\leq\frac{4}{\pi}\arctan|z|.

The conclusion follows from the inequalities (
3.2
) and (
3.3
).
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\alpha>-1
and
r\in[0,1)
. Then

\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{U}}\left|(1-re^{it})^{-\alpha}-1\right|\,dt\leq\left|(1-r)^{-\alpha}-1\right|.

Proof.
Set
u(r,t)=(1-re^{it})^{-\alpha}.

Since
u_{r}(r,t)=\alpha e^{it}(1-re^{it})^{-\alpha-1}
, and
-\alpha-1<0
, we have

|u_{r}(r,t)|\leq|\alpha|(1-r)^{-\alpha-1},\quad r\in[0,1]\mbox{ and }t\in\mathbb{R}.

Next, using
u(r,t)-u(0,t)=\int_{0}^{r}u_{x}(x,t)dx
, we obtain

|u(r,t)-u(0,t)|\leq\int_{0}^{r}|u^{\prime}_{x}(x,t)|dx\leq\int_{0}^{r}|\alpha|(1-x)^{-\alpha-1}dx=\frac{|\alpha|}{\alpha}((1-r)^{-\alpha}-1)
and therefore

\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{U}}\left|(1-re^{it})^{-\alpha}-1\right|\,dt\leq\left|(1-r)^{-\alpha}-1\right|.

∎

Combining the previous two lemmas, we obtain the main result of this section.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
\alpha>-1
and
f:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{D}
be an
\alpha
-harmonic function. Then

(1)

If
\alpha\geq 0
, then

\left|f(z)-\frac{(1-|z|^{2})^{\alpha+1}}{1+|z|^{2}}f(0)\right|\leq\frac{2^{\alpha+2}}{\pi}\arctan|z|+2^{\alpha+1}(1-|z|)\left(1-(1-|z|)^{\alpha}\right).

(2)

If
\alpha<0
, then

\left|f(z)-\frac{(1-|z|^{2})^{\alpha+1}}{1+|z|^{2}}f(0)\right|\leq\frac{4}{\pi}(1-|z|)^{\alpha}\arctan|z|+\left((1-|z|)^{\alpha}-1\right).

Proof.

Let

H_{\alpha}(z):=\frac{G_{\alpha}(z)}{(1-|z|^{2})^{\alpha}}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{U}}\frac{f^{*}(e^{it})}{(1-{\overline{z}}e^{it})^{\alpha}}\,dt.

and

K_{\alpha}(z):=H_{\alpha}(z)-f(0).

Notice that

f(z)-\frac{(1-|z|^{2})^{\alpha+1}}{1+|z|^{2}}f(0)=\left(f(z)-\frac{1-|z|^{2}}{1+|z|^{2}}G_{\alpha}(z)\right)+\frac{(1-|z|^{2})^{\alpha+1}}{1+|z|^{2}}K_{\alpha}(z).

Using the previous lemmas, it yields:

(1) if
\alpha\geq 0
, then

\left|f(z)-\frac{(1-|z|^{2})^{\alpha+1}}{1+|z|^{2}}f(0)\right|\leq\frac{2^{\alpha+2}}{\pi}|f^{*}|_{\infty}\arctan|z|+\frac{(1-|z|^{2})^{\alpha+1}}{1+|z|^{2}}|K_{\alpha}(z)|

K_{\alpha}(z)=H_{\alpha}(z)-f(0)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{U}}\left[(1-{\overline{z}}e^{it})^{-\alpha}-1\right]f^{*}(e^{it})\,dt.

Thus

|K_{\alpha}(z)|\leq\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{U}}\left|(1-|z|e^{it})^{-\alpha}-1\right|\,dt\leq(1-r)^{-\alpha}-1.

(2) First, we remark that

\frac{(1-|z|^{2})^{\alpha+1}}{1+|z|^{2}}\leq(1-|z|^{2})^{\alpha}\leq(1-|z|)^{\alpha}.

The case
\alpha<0
is treated similarly as the previous case.
∎

Using the identity for
p\in(0,1)
and
x,y\geq 0

(x+y)^{p}\leq x^{p}+y^{p}

and the convexity of the function
(1-x)^{\alpha}
, we obtain
(1-x)^{\alpha}\geq 1-\alpha x
for
\alpha\geq 1
and
x\in[0,1]
.

Corollary 2
.

Let
f:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{D}
be an
\alpha
-harmonic function.

(1)

If
0<\alpha\leq 1
, then

\left|f(z)-\frac{(1-|z|^{2})^{\alpha+1}}{1+|z|^{2}}f(0)\right|\leq 2^{\alpha+1}\left(\frac{2}{\pi}\arctan|z|+|z|^{\alpha}\right).

(2)

If
\alpha\geq 1
, then

\left|f(z)-\frac{(1-|z|^{2})^{\alpha+1}}{1+|z|^{2}}f(0)\right|\leq 2^{\alpha+1}\left(\frac{2}{\pi}\arctan|z|+\alpha|z|\right).

We close the paper by providing the power series expansion of
H_{\alpha}
, as it is anti-analytic function.

Since

\frac{1}{(1-{\overline{z}}e^{it})^{\alpha}}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(\alpha)_{n}}{n!}\,{\overline{z}}^{n}e^{int},

we deduce that

H_{\alpha}(z)=\frac{G_{\alpha}(z)}{(1-|z|^{2})^{\alpha}}=f(0)+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(\alpha)_{n}}{n!}\widehat{f^{*}}(-n)\,{\overline{z}}^{n},

where

\widehat{f^{*}}(-n)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{U}}f^{*}(e^{it})e^{int}\,dt.

Here we are using that
f
has the following series expansion

f(z)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\widehat{f^{*}}(-n)e_{\alpha,-n}(z)+\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\widehat{f^{*}}(n)z^{n},

see
[
14
, Sectiuon 5]
.

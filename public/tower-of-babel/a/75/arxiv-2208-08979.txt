Skew Howe duality for U q ​ ( 𝔤 ​ 𝔩 n ) U_{q}(\mathfrak{gl}_{n}) via quantized Clifford algebras
Willie Aboumrad

Abstract
. We develop an operator commutant version of the First Fundamental Theorem of invariant theory for the general linear quantum group U q ​ ( 𝔤 ​ 𝔩 n ) U_{q}(\mathfrak{gl}_{n}) by using a double centralizer property inside a quantized Clifford algebra. In particular, we show that U q ​ ( 𝔤 ​ 𝔩 m ) U_{q}(\mathfrak{gl}_{m}) generates the centralizer of the U q ​ ( 𝔤 ​ 𝔩 n ) U_{q}(\mathfrak{gl}_{n}) -action on the tensor product of braided exterior algebras ⋀ q ( ℂ n ) ⊗ m \bigwedge_{q}(\mathbb{C}^{n})^{\otimes m} . We obtain a multiplicity-free decomposition of the U q ​ ( 𝔤 ​ 𝔩 n ) ⊗ U q ​ ( 𝔤 ​ 𝔩 m ) U_{q}(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})\otimes U_{q}(\mathfrak{gl}_{m}) -module ⋀ q ( ℂ n ) ⊗ m ≅ ⋀ q ( ℂ n ​ m ) \bigwedge_{q}(\mathbb{C}^{n})^{\otimes m}\cong\bigwedge_{q}(\mathbb{C}^{nm}) by computing explicit joint highest weight vectors. We find that the irreducible modules in this decomposition are parametrized by the same dominant weights as in the classical case of the well-known skew G ​ L n × G ​ L m GL_{n}\times GL_{m} -duality. Clifford algebras are an essential feature of our work: they provide a unifying framework for classical and quantized skew Howe duality results that can be extended to include orthogonal algebras of types 𝐁𝐃 \mathbf{BD} .



1.
Introduction

In this article we develop a classical and a quantum
skew
Howe duality result for Type
\mathbf{A}
algebras. The classical result lays the foundations for our quantized theorem, and the quantized result suggests the generalization to orthogonal types obtained in
[
Abo22
]

\mathbf{ABD}

Our duality results extend a long and rich tradition in invariant theory tracing back to at least Weyl’s prominent book,
The Classical Groups

[
Wey39
]
. The fundamental theorems of classical invariant theory may be formulated as a solution to the following problem: given a module
V
for a reductive group
G

\End_{k[G]}(V^{\otimes m})
. The First Fundamental Theorem (FFT) gives generators for the centralizer algebra and the Second Fundamental Theorem (SFT) describes all relations amongst them
[

,
Wey39
]
. A typical case addressed by the fundamental theorems is when
G
is one of the classical complex Lie groups and
V
is the natural
G
-module. In these cases, the fundamental theorems are known as Schur-Weyl-Brauer duality. For details, see Section 5.6 in
[
GW09
]
.

Various results are known in the quantized setting. For instance, when
\mathfrak{g}
is a classical complex Lie algebra and
V
is the natural module of the quantized enveloping algebra
U_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
,
\End_{U_{q}(\mathfrak{g})}(V^{\otimes m})
is a quotient of a Hecke algebra if

and it is a quotient of a Birman-Murakami-Wenzl (BMW) algebra in the remaining cases
[
Jim86
,
LR97
,
LZ06
]
. Lehrer and Zhang describe
\End_{U_{q}(\mathfrak{g})}(V^{\otimes m})
for any irreducible
U_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2})
-module
V
and when
V
is the
7
-dimensional irreducible
U_{q}(\mathfrak{g}_{2})
-module in
[
LZ06
]
. Wenzl covered some cases where
\mathfrak{g}
is of Lie types
\mathbf{D}
and
\mathbf{E}
in
[
Wen12
,
Wen03
]

There are various equivalent formulations of the fundamental theorems; in this article and the next we consider

versions
[

-skew-symmetric variables.

3.3.
Multiplicity-free decomposition of
\bigwedge(\mathbb{C}^{nm})

We now recall the classical duality result for Type
\mathbf{A}
. Recall that every irreducible finite dimensional representation of a semisimple Lie group
G
contains a
highest weight vector
that is essentially unique (all highest weight vectors are scalar multiples of each other), and the highest weight vector identifies the module
[
Bum04
, Theorem 22.3]
. The highest weight vector in an irreducible
G
-module is fixed by the unipotent subgroup of any Borel subgroup of
G

[
Bum04
, Theorem 26.5]
. At the Lie algebra level, this means that the highest weight vector of an irreducible module is the unique weight vector that is annihilated by all the simple positive root vectors. In particular, a vector
v
in some
\mathfrak{g}
-module is a highest weight vector if and only if
v
is a joint eigenvector of the
H_{i}
and
E_{i}v=0
for each
i=1,\ldots,r
. There is a one-to-one correspondence between highest weight vectors and
dominant weights
, as specified by
Equation

3
. When
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
, the dominant weights
\mu\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
satisfy

\mu_{1}\geq\mu_{2}\geq\cdots\geq\mu_{n}.

We refer the reader to Proposition 3.1.20 in
[
GW09
]
for more details. In what follows, we let
\pi_{\mu}^{G}
denote the irreducible
G
-module with highest weight
\mu
.

Theorem 3.10

(Skew
GL_{n}\times GL_{m}
-duality)
.

Consider the natural action of
GL(U)\times GL(W)
on
V=U\otimes W
and its extension to
\bigwedge(U\otimes W)
, as described by the commutative diagram (
3
). As a
GL(U)\times GL(W)
-module, the exterior algebra
\bigwedge(U\otimes W)
decomposes as

\bigwedge(U\otimes W)\cong\sum_{\mu}\pi_{\mu}^{GL(U)}\otimes\pi_{\mu^{\prime}}^{GL(W)}.

Here, the sum ranges over all partitions
\mu
with at most
n=\dim U
rows of length at most
m=\dim W
: all partitions fitting inside an
n\times m
rectangle.

Remark 3.11
.

At the character level, skew
GL_{n}\times GL_{m}
-duality is the well-known
dual Cauchy identity
, which states that if
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n}
and
\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{m}
are complex numbers of absolute value
<1
then

\prod_{i=1}^{n}\prod_{j=1}^{m}(1+\alpha_{i}\beta_{j})=\sum_{\lambda}s_{\lambda}(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n})s_{\lambda^{\prime}}(\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{m}).

The sum ranges over all partitions
\lambda
fitting in an
n\times m
box and each
s_{\lambda}
is a Schur polynomial. For details, see e.g.
[
Bum04
, Theorem 38.2]
.

There is a short proof of
3.10
based on Schur Duality, due to Howe
[

Theorem 3.12

.

[

(a)

Let

Proof.

The proof follows from direct calculation. Considering the explanatory remarks preceding the proposition, it remains to show that the action induced by the root vectors of

U_{q}(\mathfrak{gl}_{m})

\kappa_{<b,j}

\psi_{i+(a-1)n}^{\dagger}\psi_{i+1+(a-1)n}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad-\kappa_{<b,j}\,\psi_{b+(j-1)n}^{\dagger}\psi_{b+jn}^{\phantom{\dagger}}\kappa_{i,>a}\,\psi_{i+(a-1)n}^{\dagger}\psi_{i+1+(a-1)n}^{\phantom{\dagger}}\big)

or

because we can appropriately rearrange the creation and annihilation operators. Thus we see that the commutator

\displaystyle[\lambda_{q}(E_{i}^{(n)}),\rho_{q}(E_{j}^{(m)})]

\displaystyle=\sum_{a,b=i,i+1}\kappa_{i,>a}\,\psi_{i+(a-1)n}^{\dagger}\psi_{i+1+(a-1)n}^{\phantom{\dagger}}\kappa_{<b,j}\,\psi_{b+(j-1)n}^{\dagger}\psi_{b+jn}^{\phantom{\dagger}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a=j,j+1\\
b=i,i+1\end{subarray}}[\kappa_{i,>a}\psi_{i+(a-1)n}^{\dagger}\psi_{i+1+(a-1)n}^{\phantom{\dagger}},\kappa_{<b,j}\psi_{b+(j-1)n}^{\dagger}\psi_{b+jn}^{\phantom{\dagger}}].

\displaystyle=q^{-1}\kappa_{i,>j}\kappa_{<(i+1),j}[\psi_{i+(j-1)n}^{\dagger}\psi_{i+1+(j-1)n}^{\phantom{\dagger}},\psi_{i+1+(j-1)n}^{\dagger}\psi_{i+1+jn}^{\phantom{\dagger}}]

\displaystyle\quad-\kappa_{i,>(j+1)}\kappa_{<i,j}\psi_{i+(j-1)n}^{\dagger}\big\{\psi_{i+jn}^{\dagger},\psi_{i+jn}^{\phantom{\dagger}}\big\}\psi_{i+1+jn}^{\phantom{\dagger}}

\displaystyle=\kappa_{i,>(j+1)}\kappa_{<i,j}\big(q^{-1}\omega_{i+(j-1)n}^{-1}\omega_{i+1+jn}\big\{\psi_{i+1+(j-1)n}^{\phantom{\dagger}},\psi_{i+1+(j-1)n}^{\dagger}\big\}

In the second-to-last equality we used
to slide the anticommutators to the left of
\psi_{i+(j-1)n}^{\dagger}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{\sum_{p=i+1+(j-1)n}^{i+jn}p}\kappa_{i,>(j+1)}\,\kappa_{<i,j}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\cdot\big(q^{-1}\omega_{i+(j-1)n}^{-1}\omega_{i+1+jn}\big\{\psi_{i+1+(j-1)n}^{\phantom{\dagger}},\psi_{i+1+(j-1)n}^{\dagger}\big\}

\displaystyle\quad\quad-\big\{\psi_{i+jn}^{\dagger},\psi_{i+jn}^{\phantom{\dagger}}\big\}\big)v(\ell+e_{i+(j-1)n}-e_{i+1+jn})

\displaystyle=(-1)^{\sum_{p=i+1+(j-1)n}^{i+jn}p}\,\kappa_{i,>(j+1)}\kappa_{<i,j}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\cdot\big(v(\ell+e_{i+(j-1)n}-e_{i+1+jn})-v(\ell+e_{i+(j-1)n}-e_{i+1+jn})\big)

4.3.
Multiplicity-free decomposition of
\bigwedge_{q}(V^{(nm)})

In the last section we found homomorphic images of
U_{q}(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})
and
U_{q}(\mathfrak{gl}_{m})
in the quantum Clifford algebra
Cl_{q}(nm)
that generate commuting actions on the
Cl_{q}(nm)
-module
\bigwedge_{q}(V^{(nm)})
. In this subsection we use those actions to compute a multiplicity-free decomposition of
\bigwedge_{q}(V^{(nm)})
as a
U_{q}(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})\otimes U_{q}(\mathfrak{gl}_{m})
-module. We rely on the classical skew duality
3.10
and on the Double Commutant
3.12
(b) to prove our main quantized duality result,
4.17
.

Theorem 4.17
.

As a
U_{q}(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})\otimes U_{q}(\mathfrak{gl}_{m})
-module,

(16)

\displaystyle\textstyle\bigwedge_{q}(V^{(nm)})=\displaystyle\bigoplus_{\mu}V_{\mu}^{(n)}\otimes V^{(m)}_{\mu^{\prime}}.

The sum ranges over all partitions
\mu
fitting in an
n\times m
rectangle,
V_{\mu}^{(p)}
denotes the irreducible
U_{q}(\mathfrak{gl}_{p})
-module parametrized by
\mu
, and
\mu^{\prime}
denotes the conjugate of
\mu
. In particular,
\bigwedge_{q}(V^{(nm)})
is multiplicity-free as a
U_{q}(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})\otimes U_{q}(\mathfrak{gl}_{m})
-module.

Consequently, the maps
\lambda_{q}\colon U_{q}(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})\to Cl_{q}(nm)
and
\rho_{q}\colon U_{q}(\mathfrak{gl}_{m})\to Cl_{q}(nm)
generate mutual commutants in
\End\big(\bigwedge_{q}(V^{(nm)})\big)
.

Remark 4.18
.

Recall that in the classical case, we first establish that

and
\mathfrak{gl}_{m}
generate each others commutants in

as a

In the quantum case, the argument flows the opposite way. First we construct a multiplicity-free decomposition of

as a

then

and

indeed generate each others full commutants in

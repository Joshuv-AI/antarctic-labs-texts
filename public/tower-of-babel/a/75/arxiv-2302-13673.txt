Three-particle distribution in B meson and charm-quark loops in FCNC B decays
Dmitri Melikhov

Abstract
We discuss a nonfactorizable (NF) contribution of a charm loop to the FCNC B B -decay amplitude given through the three-particle Bethe-Salpeter amplitude (3BS) of the B B -meson. This 3BS contains one heavy-quark field and two light fields (a light quark and a gluon). Our discussion is aimed at clarifying properties of the B B -meson 3BS necessary to describe properly charm-loop contributions to the amplitudes of FCNC B B -decays. We demonstrate that the dominant contribution of nonfactorizable charm to FCNC B B -decay amplitude is given in the heavy-quark limit by a convolution of some hard kernel and the B B -meson 3BS in a “double-collinear” light cone (LC) configuration: one of the light degrees of freedom ϕ ⁡ ( x ) \phi(x) , x 2 = 0 x^{2}=0 , lies on the ( + ) (+) -direction of the LC, whereas another light degree of freedom ϕ ′ ​ ( x ′ ) \phi^{\prime}(x^{\prime}) , x ′ 2 = 0 x^{\prime 2}=0 lies along the ( − ) (-) -direction. We show the emergence of new constraints on the distribution amplitudes which parametrize the 3BS in this double-collinear configuration.

3.1.3
The amplitude of the FCNC

Making use of the leading contributions of the

the upper and the lower parts of the diagram are aligned along the different light-cone directions.

3.1.4

3.1.6
Collinear vs Double-collinear 3DAs

Here we would like to emphasize the differences between the collinear and the double-collinear 3DAs.
Let us get back to Eq. (

Again introduce LC variables in the

\bullet
In a collinear LC configuration,

Then two Lorentz structures in (

\displaystyle\langle 0|G_{\mu\nu}(n\tau)\phi_{b}(0)\phi(n\tau^{\prime})|B(p)\rangle=\int D(\omega,\omega^{\prime})e^{-iM_{B}(\omega\tau+\omega^{\prime}\tau^{\prime})}\frac{1}{2}\left(n_{\mu}n^{\prime}_{\nu}-n_{\nu}n^{\prime}_{\mu}\right)\left(X^{(1)}_{A}+X^{(2)}_{A}\right),

\bullet

\mu\leftrightarrow\nu

Another possibility to handle the

Taking into account that the quadratic form in the denominator is the quark propagator, we reconstruct the imaginary

\displaystyle\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\frac{da}{(a-a_{+})(a-a_{-})-i0}=\frac{1}{a_{+}-a-}\log\left({-\frac{a_{-}}{a_{+}}}\right)+i\pi\frac{1}{a_{+}-a-},\quad a_{-}<0,\quad a_{+}>0\quad\mbox{for }0<\omega<1.

By virtue of the

4
Conclusions

We studied a generic
B
-decay amplitude of the FCNC-type — i.e., an amplitude given by diagrams in which the heavy
field hits the middle point of the line along which light degrees of freedom propagate — and obtained the following results:

(i) As already demonstrated in the literature
mk2018
;
m2019
;
m2022
, the leading contribution to the amplitude of a

B
-decay of FCNC-type,
B\to j(q)j^{\prime}(q^{\prime})
, is given by the convolution formula of the hard kernel composed of propagators
of the light degrees of freedom and the 3BS of the
B
-meson

\displaystyle\langle 0|\phi(x)\phi_{b}(0)\phi^{\prime}(x^{\prime})|B(p)\rangle

(4.48)

in the following configuration:

\displaystyle x^{2}=0;\qquad x^{\prime 2}=0,\qquad xx^{\prime}\neq 0.

(4.49)

We have now formulated this result as a factorization theorem by a direct analysis of Feynman diagrams:
Considering the FCNC-type
B
-decay into two real photons (
q^{2}=q^{\prime 2}=0
) in the rest frame of
the
B
-meson and choosing the momenta
q
and
q^{\prime}
along the
(+)
and
(-)
axes of the light cone,
respectively, we have shown that the dominant contribution to the amplitude in the heavy-quark limit
comes from the “double-collinear” configuration

\displaystyle\langle 0|\phi(x_{+})\phi_{b}(0)\phi^{\prime}(x^{\prime}_{-})|B(p)\rangle.

(4.50)

Eq. (
3.22
) represents the factorization formula for the amplitudes of FCNC-type; corrections to the contribution
given by Eq. (
3.22
) are suppressed by powers of the heavy-quark mass.

(ii) The
B
-meson 3BS in a collinear LC configuration

\displaystyle\langle 0|\phi(x)\phi_{b}(0)\phi^{\prime}(\lambda x)|B(p)\rangle,\qquad x^{2}=0,\qquad 0<\lambda<1,

(4.51)

does not appear
in the convolution formula (
3.22
) for the leading
3BS contribution to the FCNC
B
-decay amplitude. This means that the calculation of the FCNC
B
-decay amplitude
as the convolution of the hard kernel and the collinear 3BS as is done e.g. in
hidr
does not provide
a well-defined starting point: corrections to this approximation are not suppressed by any large parameter.

This makes an essential difference between the 3DA contributions to charming loops in FCNC
B
-decays
and to SL
B
-decays:
The 3DA contribution to the SL form factor factorizes into the convolution of a hard kernel and the collinear LC
3BS
offen2007
, corrections to this configuration being suppressed by
1/m_{b}
.

(iii) The generic 3BS involves more Lorentz structures and 3DAs than the collinear 3BS.
In particular, it involves structures of the type

\displaystyle\langle 0|G_{\mu\nu}(x)\phi_{b}(0)\phi^{\prime}(x^{\prime})|B(p)\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int D(\omega,\omega^{\prime})\;e^{-ipx\omega-ipx^{\prime}\omega^{\prime}}

(4.52)

\displaystyle\times\left\{\left(\frac{p_{\mu}x_{\nu}-p_{\nu}x_{\mu}}{xp}\right)X^{(1)}_{A}(\omega,\omega^{\prime})+\left(\frac{p_{\mu}x^{\prime}_{\nu}-p_{\nu}x^{\prime}_{\mu}}{x^{\prime}p}\right)X^{(2)}_{A}(\omega,\omega^{\prime})\right\}

with
D(\omega,\omega^{\prime})=d\omega d\omega^{\prime}\theta(\omega)\theta(\omega)\theta(1-\omega-\omega^{\prime})
.
These Lorentz structures contain kinematical singularities in
1/xp
or
1/x^{\prime}p
. Such singularities are
unphysical. Requiring that the 3DA is a continuous and finite function of its variables
at
x^{2}=0
,
x^{\prime 2}=0
,
xp=0
and
x^{\prime}p=0
, we conclude that the kinematical singularities
should vanish due to the properties of the 3DAs
X^{(1)}_{A}
and
X^{(2)}_{A}
, leading to the following constraints:

\displaystyle\int\limits_{0}^{1-\omega}d\omega X^{(1)}_{A}(\omega,\omega^{\prime})=0\quad\forall\omega^{\prime};\qquad\int\limits_{0}^{1-\omega^{\prime}}d\omega^{\prime}X^{(2)}_{A}(\omega,\omega^{\prime})=0\quad\forall\omega.

(4.53)

In other words, the primitives

\overline{X}^{(1)}_{A}(\omega,\omega^{\prime})
and
\overline{X}^{(2)}_{A}(\omega,\omega^{\prime})
[Eqs. (
2.10
) and (
2.11
)]
should vanish on the boundaies of the
X_{A}
-suppport area:

\displaystyle\overline{X}^{(1)}_{A}(\omega=0,\omega^{\prime})=\overline{X}^{(1)}_{A}(1-\omega^{\prime},\omega^{\prime})=0\quad\forall\omega^{\prime},

\displaystyle\overline{X}^{(2)}_{A}(\omega,\omega^{\prime}=0)=\overline{X}^{(2)}_{A}(\omega,1-\omega)=0\quad\forall\omega.

(4.54)

Constraints of this type emerge for all 3DAs which parametrize the Lorents structures

x_{\mu}/xp
,
x^{\prime}_{\mu}/x^{\prime}p
,
x_{\mu}x_{\nu}/(xp)^{2}
,
x_{\mu}x^{\prime}_{\nu}/(xp)(x^{\prime}p)
etc in the 3BS of the
B
-meson.
These constraints should be taken into account when building models for the 3DAs.

(iv) The
B
-meson 3BS of the
X_{A}
-type of Eq. (
4.52
) in the collinear LC configuration
x_{\mu}=\tau n_{\mu}
,

x^{\prime}_{\mu}=\tau^{\prime}n_{\mu}
,
n^{2}=0
, Eq. (
3.26
),
and in the double-collinear LC configuration,
x_{\mu}=\tau n_{\mu}
,

x^{\prime}_{\mu}=\tau^{\prime}n^{\prime}_{\mu}
,
n^{2}=n^{\prime 2}=0
,
n^{\prime}n=2
, Eq. (
3.27
),
may be parametrized via one and the same Lorentz structure

Exact and approximate computation of the scatter halfspace depth
Xiaohui Liu , Yuzi Liu, Petra Laketa, Stanislav Nagy, Yuting Chen, [1ex] School of Statistics, Jiangxi University of Finance and Economics, Nanchang, Jiangxi 330013, China

Abstract




Abstract
. The scatter halfspace depth (
sHD

sHD
for data of dimension
d\geq 2
has, however, not been addressed in the literature. We develop an exact algorithm for the computation of

in any dimension
d

C++

R

Introduction: Scatter halfspace depth and its computation

The location halfspace (or Tukey) depth is a powerful method of nonparametric analysis that is applicable to multivariate data
(
Tukey, 1975
;
Donoho and Gasko, 1992
;
Chen et al., 2018
)
. For a dataset
\mathcal{X}
composed of observations
\textbf{{x}}_{1},\dots,\textbf{{x}}_{n}\in\mathcal{R}^{d}
, the location halfspace depth of a point
\textbf{{x}}\in\mathcal{R}^{d}
with respect to (w.r.t.) the dataset
\mathcal{X}
can be defined as the smallest number of data points lying in a closed halfspace whose boundary hyperplane passes through
x

\mathrm{HD}(\textbf{{x}},\mathcal{X})=\inf_{\textbf{{u}}\in\mathcal{S}^{d-1}}\#\left\{i\in\left\{1,\dots,n\right\}\colon\textbf{{u}}^{\top}\textbf{{x}}_{i}\leq\textbf{{u}}^{\top}\textbf{{x}}\right\}.

(1)

Here,
\mathcal{S}^{d-1}=\left\{\textbf{{u}}\in\mathcal{R}^{d}\colon\left\|\textbf{{u}}\right\|=1\right\}
is the unit sphere in
\mathcal{R}^{d}
, and
\#A
stands for the number of elements of a finite set
A
. The unit vector
\textbf{{u}}\in\mathcal{S}^{d-1}
plays in (
1
) the role of the outer normal vector to the halfspace whose mass we evaluate.

The location halfspace depth (
1
) provides a natural data-dependent ordering in
\mathcal{R}^{d}
— points
\textbf{{x}}\in\mathcal{R}^{d}
positioned “centrally” w.r.t. the data cloud
\mathcal{X}
obtain higher values of the depth (
1
), while peripheral points are distinguished by their low depth values. This ordering allows us to devise a theory of nonparametric statistics based on ranks in
\mathcal{R}^{d}
. A prime example is (the barycenter of) the set of maximizers of the depth function (
1
) over
\textbf{{x}}\in\mathcal{R}^{d}
. That collection of deepest points is frequently called the set of halfspace medians of
\mathcal{X}
, and presents a plausible analogue of the univariate median suitable for
\mathcal{R}^{d}
-valued observations
(
Donoho and Gasko, 1992
;
Chen et al., 2018
)
. Another prominent example of a depth-based method is the bagplot, which is a version of the boxplot applicable to multivariate data
(
Rousseeuw et al., 1999
)
. All these concepts are already firmly embedded in the nonparametrics of multivariate data, and a substantial progress has been made into their theory, practice, and computational aspects.

The location halfspace depth introduces elements of the first-order inference to
\mathcal{R}^{d}
; in (
1

\textbf{{x}}\in\mathcal{R}^{d}
for their suitability as location estimators of
\mathcal{X}
. A next step is the analysis of the dispersion of
\mathcal{X}
. Expanding the scope of the location halfspace depth toward the scatter of multivariate data clouds, the scatter halfspace depth (

\mathcal{X}
of
\mathcal{R}^{d}

\textbf{{x}}_{1},\dots,\textbf{{x}}_{n}
and a given centering vector

2
Methodology and main results

Our algorithm is based on considerations from spherical geometry, coupled with elements of convex geometry, and matrix theory. It is described in several steps. We begin in Section
2.1
, where we interpret
sHD
in geometric terms. Our algorithm is guaranteed to give an exact result under a mild assumption of sufficiently general position of the data points, introduced in Section
2.2
. To simplify the exposition, in Section
2.3
we use the affine invariance of
sHD
to reduce the problem of the computation of
sHD
. Instead of computing the depth for any positive definite matrix
\Sigma\in\mathcal{R}^{d\times d}
centered at an arbitrary location
\bm{\mu}\in\mathcal{R}^{d}
, we use the equivalent formulation of finding
sHD
of the unit
d\times d
matrix
\mathbb{I}_{d}
centered at the origin
\bm{0}_{d}
, w.r.t. a transformed sample of data points. Having covered the preliminaries, we state our main result as Theorem
1
in Section
2.4
, and outline the way how to use that result to develop our algorithm in Section
2.5
.

➤C6:
Steps 5(c) and 6(d): Intersecting (anti-)circles and affine hulls

We need to be able to find intersections of (anti-)circles analytically. To compute those points efficiently, we state an equivalence of

(i)

(ii)

To simplify our argument, consider only circles; the case of anti-circles is dealt with completely analogously. The proof of the following lemma can be found in the Appendix, Section

A set of

This result is not applied directly in

3.3
Approximate algorithms

We will see in Section
4
that especially in higher dimensions
d>4
and sample sizes starting at hundreds of points, the exact computation of
sHD
depth becomes slow. For that reason, we propose two approximate computational algorithms:

•

An algorithm based on randomly sampled directions
\textbf{{u}}_{j}\in\mathcal{S}^{d-1}
,
j=1,\dots,N
, distributed uniformly in the unit sphere
\mathcal{S}^{d-1}
. The approximation of
\mathrm{sHD}(\Sigma,\bm{\mu},\mathcal{X})
is

\min_{j=1,\dots,N}\min\left\{\#\left\{i\colon|\textbf{{u}}_{j}^{\top}\Sigma^{-1/2}(\textbf{{x}}_{i}-\bm{\mu})|\leq 1\right\},\#\left\{i\colon|\textbf{{u}}_{j}^{\top}\Sigma^{-1/2}(\textbf{{x}}_{i}-\bm{\mu})|\geq 1\right\}\right\}.

In this formula we replace the whole sphere
\mathcal{S}^{d-1}
in (
2
) by the finite set of randomly chosen points
\left\{\textbf{{u}}_{1},\dots,\textbf{{u}}_{N}\right\}\subset\mathcal{S}^{d-1}
and use affine invariance of
sHD
as discussed in Section
2.3
.

•

A random extension of Algorithm
2
, where instead of running through all
\binom{n}{d-1}
tuples of sample points in the outer loop of
Step 5
, only a random subset of
N
of the
(d-1)
-tuples of distinct points is selected in
Step 5
. The search for maximal directions in less than
d-1
(anti-)circles in
Step 6
is not executed in this approximation.

In the
R
package
scatterdepth
the first approximation method is called
rdirections
(standing for
random directions
) and the second
rpoints
(standing for
random points
). The method
rdirections
can be seen as an extension of the idea of approximate computation applicable to any depth satisfying the projection property
(
Dyckerhoff, 2004
, Section 6)
. Especially in higher dimensions, the method
rdirections
is not expected to perform well; that was observed already for the location halfspace depth in
Nagy et al., 2020
and
Dyckerhoff et al., 2021
. Both approximation methods are compared in a numerical study in Section
4
.

Cluster Random Fields and Random-Shift Representations
Enkelejd Hashorva

Abstract




Abstract:
Cluster random fields (CRFs) play a crucial role in the study of extremes of stationary regularly varying random fields (RFs). In particular, they appear in the
Rosiński representation
of max-stable and
\alpha
-stable RFs. In this contribution we introduce CRFs
in an abstract setting proving that they are crucial for the construction of
shift-generated
classes of
\alpha
-homogeneous RFs.
Further, we investigate the relations between CRFs,
tail RFs
and
spectral tail RFs
. Applications discussed in this contribution include new representations of extremal functional indices and purely dissipative max-stable RFs.

Key words
: Cluster random fields; random-shift representation

\alpha
-homogeneous classes of random fields;
max-stable random fields; extremal functionals;
Rosiński representation; lattices; shift-generated classes; tail random fields; spectral tail random fields.

To set up our mathematical framework, let us fix two positive integers
d
and
l
, and consider below the parameter set
\mathcal{T}=\mathbb{R}^{l}
or
\mathcal{T}=\mathbb{Z}^{l}
. Fix next
\alpha>0
and
write
\mathfrak{D}
for the space of functions
f:\mathcal{T}\mapsto\mathbb{R}^{d}
equipped with the product (cylindrical)
\sigma
-field
\mathscr{D}
. Taking
\mathcal{E}=\mathbb{R}
or
\mathcal{E}=[0,\infty]
, let
\mathcal{H}
consist of all
\mathscr{D}/\mathscr{B}((-\infty,\infty])
-measurable maps
F:\mathfrak{D}\mapsto\mathcal{E}
, which are bounded if
\mathcal{E}=\mathbb{R}
. Let
\mathcal{H}_{\beta},\beta\geq 0
consists of all

F\in\mathcal{H}
satisfying
F(cf)=c^{\beta}F(f)
for all
f\in\mathfrak{D}
and
c>0
and
set

\mathcal{H}_{\beta}^{+}=\{F\in\mathcal{H}_{\beta},F\geq 0\}.

In our notation
\mathscr{B}(S)
stands for the Borel
\sigma
-field of a topological space
S
. Throughout the paper
\kappa\in\mathcal{H}_{\alpha}^{+}
is fixed and for a given RF
V(t),t\in\mathcal{T}
we write

(1.1)

\displaystyle V_{\kappa}(t)=\kappa(B^{-t}V),\quad B^{-t}f=f({\cdot+t}),\quad f\in\mathfrak{D},\quad t\in\mathcal{T}.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
\mathfrak{W}_{\kappa}
be the class of all
\mathbb{R}^{d}
-valued RFs
V(t),t\in\mathcal{T}
defined on some complete non-atomic probability space such that
V_{\kappa}
is stochastically continuous.

Necessary and sufficient conditions for
V_{\kappa}
to have a separable and jointly measurable version are given in
[
19
, Prop 9.4.2]
, which can be formulated with respect to 2-dimensional marginal distributions of
V_{\kappa}
. In particular, if
V_{\kappa}
is stochastically continuous, then in view of
[
20
, Thm 5, p. 169, Thm 1, p. 171]
it has a separable version with separant
{\mathbb{T}_{0}}
being further jointly measurable.

Hereafter, elements of
\mathfrak{W}_{\kappa}
are assumed to be separable with separant
{\mathbb{T}_{0}}
and jointly measurable.
Further
\lambda(\cdot)
stands for the Lebesgue measure if
\mathcal{T}=\mathbb{R}^{l}
or the counting measure on
\mathcal{T}
when the latter is discrete.

Below
{\mathbb{T}_{0}}
consists
of all
t\in\mathbb{R}^{l}\cap\mathcal{T}
, which have rational coordinates and set

\sup_{t\in K}f(t)=\sup_{t\in K\cap{\mathbb{T}_{0}}}f(t),\quad f\in\mathfrak{D},\quad K\subset\mathcal{T}.

Definition 1.2
.

Given
\kappa\in\mathcal{H}_{\alpha}^{+}
we call
Q\in\mathfrak{W}_{\kappa}
a CRF if

(1.2)

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left\{\sup_{t\in\mathcal{T}}Q_{\kappa}(t)>0\right\}=1,\quad\quad\int_{\mathcal{T}}\mathbb{E}\left\{\sup_{t\in[-c,c]^{l}}Q_{\kappa}(t-v)\right\}\lambda(dv)<\infty,\quad\forall c>0.

The second condition in (
1.2
) means that we are interested in locally bounded CRFs.
It turns out that
\sup_{t\in\mathcal{T}}Q_{\kappa}(t)
has a finite expectation, see (
3.2
) below.

Example 1.3
.

Hereafter
\lVert\cdot\rVert
denotes a norm on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Three natural choices for
\kappa
are:

(1)

\kappa(f)=\lVert f(0)\rVert^{\alpha},f\in\mathfrak{D}
;

(2)

\kappa(f)=(\sum_{i=1}^{d}\left\lvert f_{i}(0)\right\rvert^{\alpha}/d)^{1/\alpha},f=(f_{1},\ldots,f_{d})\in\mathfrak{D}
;

(3)

\kappa(f)=\sup_{t\in K\cap{\mathbb{T}_{0}}}\lVert f(t)\rVert^{\alpha},f\in\mathfrak{D}
for some compact set
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{l}
.

Given
\kappa\in\mathcal{H}_{\alpha}^{+}
as above, if

L:\mathbb{R}^{l}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a deterministic function such that
\kappa(B^{t}L),t\in\mathbb{R}^{l}
is a càdlàg pdf and
\int_{\mathcal{T}}\sup_{t\in[-c,c]^{l}}\kappa(B^{v}L)\lambda(dv)<\infty
for all
c>0
, then a non-random CRF
Q

is simply

Q(t)=L(t),t\in\mathcal{T}.

Consider
Z\in\mathfrak{W}_{\kappa}

defined on a complete non-atomic probability space
(\Omega,\mathscr{F},\mathbb{P})

satisfying (recall our notation (
1.1
))

(1.3)

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left\{\sup_{t\in\mathcal{T}}Z_{\kappa}(t)>0\right\}=1,\quad\mathbb{E}\left\{\sup_{t\in[-c,c]^{l}}Z_{\kappa}(t)\right\}\in(0,\infty),\quad\forall c\in[0,\infty).

Similarly,
\widetilde{Z}\in\mathfrak{W}_{\kappa}
is defined on a complete non-atomic probability space
(\widetilde{\Omega},\widetilde{\mathscr{F}},\widetilde{\mathbb{P}})
.
We introduce next important classes of
\alpha
-homogeneous RFs, which in the case of
shift-generated
dissipative classes (defined below) are directly constructed by CRFs.

Definition 1.4
.

As in
[
21
]
, we call
\mathscr{C}_{\kappa}[Z]
with
\kappa\in\mathcal{H}_{\alpha}^{+}
an
\alpha
-homogeneous class of RFs with representer
Z
, if it contains
Z
and all
\widetilde{Z}\in\mathfrak{W}_{\kappa}

that satisfy (
1.3
) and moreover

(1.4)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\{F(Z)\}=\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}\{F(\widetilde{Z})\},\quad\forall F\in\mathcal{H}_{\alpha}.

\mathscr{C}_{\kappa}[Z]
is called shift-generated (and then denoted by
\mathcal{C}_{\kappa}[Z]
) if further

B^{h}\widetilde{Z}\in\mathscr{C}_{\kappa}[Z],\quad\forall h\in\mathcal{T}

for some (and then for all)
\widetilde{Z}\in\mathscr{C}_{\kappa}[Z]
.

If the random variable (rv)
C
is almost surely (a.s.) positive, with
\mathbb{E}\{C\}=1
being further independent of
Z
, then clearly
\widetilde{Z}=C^{1/\alpha}Z\in\mathscr{C}_{\kappa}[Z]
and

(1.5)

\displaystyle\mathscr{C}_{\kappa}[Z]=\mathscr{C}_{\kappa}[C^{1/\alpha}Z].

Note that in
1.4
we require that

(1.6)

\displaystyle p^{>}_{Z_{\kappa}}=\mathbb{P}\left\{\sup_{t\in\mathcal{T}}Z_{\kappa}(t)>0\right\}=1.

When
C=0
with non-zero probability, then
\widetilde{Z}=C^{1/\alpha}Z
does not satisfy the first condition of (
1.3
). Hence by
3.1
,
Item

2
below we conclude that
\widetilde{Z}\not\in\mathscr{C}_{\kappa}[Z]
.

In view of
[
22
]
, if
\alpha=1
and
Z
is symmetric (non-negative),
\mathscr{C}_{\kappa}[Z]
contains all symmetric (non-negative) RFs
\widetilde{Z}\in\mathfrak{W}_{\kappa}
that are zonoid-equivalent to
Z
.
Note that therein
(\widetilde{\Omega},\widetilde{\mathscr{F}},\widetilde{\mathbb{P}})
is assumed to be a Borel probability space. The next example explains that
\mathscr{C}_{\kappa}[Z]
’s naturally arise in connection with max-stable and
\alpha
-stable RFs, a fact which is known from
[
22
]
.

Example 1.5
.

Given
\widetilde{Z}\in\mathscr{C}_{\kappa}[Z]
let

\widetilde{Z}^{(i)},i\in\mathbb{N}
be independent copies of
\widetilde{Z}
and define the max-stable stationary RF
\widetilde{X}(t),t\in\mathcal{T}
via its de Haan representation (see e.g.,
[
23
,
24
]
) by

(1.7)

\displaystyle\widetilde{X}(t)=\max_{i\geq 1}\Bigl(\sum_{k=1}^{i}\mathcal{V}_{k}\Bigr)^{-1/\alpha}\widetilde{Z}^{(i)}(t),\quad t\in\mathcal{T}.

Here the maximum is applied component-wise,
with
\mathcal{V}_{k},k\geq 1
unit iid exponential rvs being independent of any other random element. The RF
\widetilde{Z}
is referred to as the representer of
\widetilde{X}
.

For
\alpha\in(0,2)
assuming further that the elements of
\mathscr{C}_{\kappa}[Z]
are symmetric if
\alpha\in[1,2)
,
in view of
[
9
, Lem C1]
we can define an
\alpha
-stable RF
\widetilde{X}_{\Sigma}
by the following LePage representation

\widetilde{X}_{\Sigma}(t)=\sum_{i\geq 1}\Bigl(\sum_{k=1}^{i}\mathcal{V}_{k}\Bigr)^{-1/\alpha}\widetilde{Z}^{(i)}(t),\quad t\in\mathcal{T}.

By
[
22
, Thm 9]
, when
\alpha=1
the laws of
\widetilde{X}
and
\widetilde{X}_{\Sigma}
do not depend on the choice of
\widetilde{Z}
. See also
[
19
, Thm 1.4.2]
covering the case
\alpha\not=1
.

Conversely, if
\kappa
is as in
Example

1.3
,
given a stochastically continuous max-stable RF
\widetilde{X}
with de Haan representation (
1.7
) and a representer
Z
with non-negative components satisfying
(
1.3
), then
\mathscr{C}_{\kappa}[Z]
contains all non-negative RFs
\widetilde{Z}\in\mathfrak{W}_{\kappa}
that are valid representers
for
\widetilde{X}
and satisfy
\mathbb{P}\{\sup_{t\in\mathcal{T}}\kappa(B^{t}\widetilde{Z})>0\}=1
.

Surprisingly,

\mathcal{C}_{\kappa}[Z]

Hereafter, the

It turns out that the construction of

Namely, if

is a given CRF, then letting

(1.8)

\displaystyle Z_{N}(t)=\frac{B^{N}Q(t)}{[p_{N}(N)]^{1/\alpha}},\quad t\in\mathcal{T},

which in view of

\mathscr{C}_{\kappa}[Z_{N}]
denoted simply below by
\mathcal{C}_{\kappa,N}[Q]
is a
shift-generated

\alpha
-homogeneous class of RFs.

By our assumption the RF
Z_{\kappa}
is jointly measurable and non-negative. Hence the completeness of

, the non-negativity of the map
\kappa
and the Tonelli Theorem yield

\mathcal{S}({Z})=\int_{\mathcal{T}}\kappa(B^{-t}Z)\lambda(dt)=\int_{\mathcal{T}}Z_{\kappa}(t)\lambda(dt)

is a well-defined non-negative rv.

Definition 1.6
.

If
\mathbb{P}\{\mathcal{S}({Z})=\infty\}=1/0
, then we call
\mathcal{C}_{\kappa}[Z]
purely conservative/dissipative.

Lemma 1.7
.

Let
\mathcal{C}_{\kappa}[Z]
be shift invariant. It is purely conservative/dissipative if and only if (iff) the max-stable RF
X_{\kappa}

with representer
Z^{1/\alpha}_{\kappa}
and de Haan representation as in (
1.7
)
is purely conservative/dissipative.

Lemma 1.8

If

Example 1.9
.

(Brown-Resnick
\mathcal{C}_{\kappa}[Z]
)
Let
\kappa
be as in

1.3
and define

(1.9)

\displaystyle Z(t)=(e^{W_{1}(t)-\mathbb{E}\{W_{1}^{2}(t)\}/2},\ldots,e^{W_{d}(t)-\mathbb{E}\{W_{d}^{2}(t)\}/2}),\quad t\in\mathcal{T},

where
W(t),t\in\mathcal{T}
is a centered
\mathbb{R}^{d}
-valued Gaussian RF with stochastically continuous sample paths. Define the matrix-valued pseudo-cross variogram

\gamma
by

\gamma_{ij}(s,t)=\mathbb{E}\{(W_{i}(t)-W_{j}(s))^{2}\},\quad 1\leq i,j\leq d,s,t\in\mathcal{T}

If we suppose further that
\gamma_{ij}(s,t)
’s depend only on
t-s
for all

and all
positive integers

, as shown in
[

see also
[

, Lem 4.2]

, it follows that

is shift-generated.
In view of
[

]
, see also
[

, Rem 15]
, setting below

\lVert h\rVert_{*}=\sum_{i=1}^{l}\left\lvert t_{i}\right\rvert,\quad h=(h_{1},\ldots,h_{l})\in\mathbb{R}^{l}

and using

, we conclude that
\mathcal{C}_{\kappa}[Z]
is purely dissipative, provided that

(1.10)

\displaystyle\liminf_{h\to\infty}\max_{1\leq i\leq d}\frac{\gamma_{ii}(0,h)}{\ln\lVert h\rVert_{*}}>c

for some

sufficiently large.

If
\mathscr{C}_{\kappa}[Z]
is shift-generated,
the law of the corresponding max-stable RF

depends only on the cross variogram. Moreover, if a corresponding CRF

can be constructed, then also the law of

depends only on
\gamma
.

Example 1.10

(Brown-Lévy-Resnick
\mathcal{C}_{\kappa}[Z]
)
Let

\psi_{i}(\theta)=\ln\mathbb{E}\{e^{\theta W_{i}(1)}\}

Z(t)=\prod_{i=1}^{l}e^{\mathbb{I}(t_{i}\geq 0)W_{i}(t_{i})-\mathbb{I}(t_{i}<0)W_{i}^{(\alpha)}((-t_{i})-)},\quad t=(t_{1},\ldots,t_{l}).

\mathcal{C}_{\kappa}[Z]

Definition 1.11
.

We say that
\mathscr{C}_{\kappa}[Z]

Proof of

We show first that

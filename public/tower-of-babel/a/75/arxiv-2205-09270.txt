A simple improved low temperature correction for the hierarchical equations of motion
Thomas P. Fay

Abstract
The study of open system quantum dynamics has been transformed by the hierarchical equations of motion (HEOM) method, which gives the exact dynamics for a system coupled to a harmonic bath at arbitrary temperature and system-bath coupling strength. However in its standard form the method is only consistent with the weak-coupling quantum master equation at all temperatures when many auxiliary density operators are included in the hierarchy, even when low temperature corrections are included. Here we propose a new low temperature correction scheme for the termination of the hierarchy based on Zwanzig projection which alleviates this problem, and restores consistency with the weak-coupling master equation with a minimal hierarchy. The utility of the new correction scheme is demonstrated on a range of model systems, including the Fenna-Metthews-Olson complex. The new closure is found to improve convergence of the HEOM even beyond the weak-coupling limit and is very straightforward to implement in existing HEOM codes.



I
Introduction

The hierarchical equations of motion (HEOM) method, originally derived by Tanimura and Kubo,
Tanimura and Kubo 1989
;
Tanimura 2020
is a powerful tool for studying the dynamics of open quantum systems, where a sub-system of interest is coupled to a harmonic oscillator bath. The method has found applications in a wide range of problems, including the study of photosynthetic light-harvesting,
Ishizaki and Fleming 2009
;
Kreisbeck, Kramer, and Aspuru-Guzik 2014
;
Sarovar et al. 2010
;
Tong et al. 2020
electron transport,
Firmino et al. 2016
;
Ke, Borrelli, and Thoss 2022
spin relaxation,
Takahashi and Tanimura 2020
non-equilibrium heat transport,
Kato and Tanimura 2016
;
Hsieh et al. 2019
non-linear spectroscopy,
Tanimura and Kubo 1989
;
Chen et al. 2010
;
Dijkstra and Prokhorenko 2017
and molecular spin transport,
Fay, Lindoy, and Manolopoulos 2018
;
Fay and Limmer 2021
to name but a few examples. The method can treat problems at arbitrary temperatures and system-bath interaction strengths, and fully accounts for quantum mechanical and non-Markovian effects, and therefore it has also been an essential tool for testing approximate quantum dynamics methods.
Berkelbach, Markland, and Reichman 2012
;
Fetherolf and Berkelbach 2017
;
Lawrence et al. 2019
;
Pfalzgraff et al. 2019
;
Mannouch and Richardson 2022

The HEOM method gives the exact system dynamics in terms of a formally infinite hierarchy of coupled auxiliary density operators, which has to be terminated at some finite number of terms for practical calculations. However for systems at low temperatures, the terminated HEOM solution can become unstable, leading to incorrect and sometimes highly unphysical dynamics.
Dunn, Tempelaar, and Reichman 2019
The “brute force” solution to this problem is increasing the number of modes included in the hierarchy, but for systems with many baths this can dramatically increase the computational cost of HEOM calculations. One widely employed solution to correct for modes excluded from the hierarchy is the Ishizaki-Tanimura low-temperature correction.
Ishizaki and Tanimura 2005
However, even with this correction, costly calculations with large hierarchies can be required in systems with very weak system-bath coupling, as will be demonstrated below.

Several methods have been proposed to resolve issues of instabilities and slow convergence of the HEOM in a computationally efficient manner. The simplest approaches involve using different truncation schemes for the hierarchy,
Ishizaki and Tanimura 2005
;
Härtle et al. 2013
;
Dijkstra and Prokhorenko 2017
wherein the hierarchy is pruned according to some criterion which selects only the elements of the hierarchy which make significant contributions to the dynamics. A more sophisticated method involves removing small auxiliary density operators “on the fly” during an HEOM calculation to generate an optimally truncated hierarchy.
Shi et al. 2009a
Convergence of the HEOM can also be improved using various alternative decompositions of the bath correlation function, such as the Padé decomposition,
Hu et al. 2011
and the more recently proposed Fano spectrum
Cui et al. 2019
and Prony fitting schemes,
Chen et al. 2022
as well as the generalized HEOM approach.
Ikeda and Scholes 2020

Other methods to improve convergence of the HEOM include modified termination schemes for the hierarchy such as the partial-ordering prescription (POP),
Xu et al. 2005
Caldeira-Legget (CL) master equation truncation,
Kong et al. 2015
and the time derivative truncation (TD).
Hou et al. 2015
These schemes have all been demonstrated to improve convergence of the HEOM, but they come at the cost of adding complexity to the simple structure of the HEOM. For example the POP scheme adds time-dependent terms to the generator for the HEOM, and the CL and TD schemes add many couplings between auxiliary density operators in the deepest layer of the hierarchy.
Kong et al. 2015
;
Hou et al. 2015
These methods reduce the depth of hierarchy needed to achieve a desired accuracy, but they do not directly reduce the number of modes (e.g. Matsubara frequencies) needed for convergence. A Prony filtration method has also recently been proposed to remove instabilities from the HEOM,
Dunn, Tempelaar, and Reichman 2019
but this requires storage of the hierarchy at previous times, which could become prohibitive for large systems coupled to many baths. Collective bath coordinate mapping approaches have also been used to obtain converged HEOM dynamics in systems with strong system-bath coupling where the conventional HEOM breaks down,
Ikeda and Nakayama 2022
;
Li, Yan, and Shi 2022
but these involve an explicit treatment of a collective coordinate for each bath, which may limit these methods to systems with a small number of baths.

Another successful solution to converge the HEOM for difficult problems is to use the matrix product state (MPS)/tensor train (TT)
Shi et al. 2018
;
Borrelli 2019
;
Yan et al. 2021
;
Ke, Borrelli, and Thoss 2022
as an
ansatz
for the full HEOM, which enables a “brute force” solution to the convergence problem by facilitating calculations with much larger hierarchies. Using time-dependent variational principles these
ansätze
can be used to find very accurate solutions to the HEOM with very large hierarchies, thereby resolving convergence issues. These methods can however be very complicated to implement efficiently and their use is likely excessive in systems with weak system bath coupling but large system energy scales, where weak-coupling quantum master equations already fairly accurate.

Many of the aforementioned methods still employ the Ishizaki-Tanimura low temperature correction in some form (if any low temperature correction is used in calculations). Here we argue that the origin of slow convergence of the HEOM at low temperatures in some cases, even when system-bath coupling is weak, is in the inconsistency between the Ishizaki-Tanimura low temperature correction and the correct weak-coupling quantum master equation. In this paper a very simple solution to this problem is found by deriving a new HEOM low temperature correction, which restores consistency between the HEOM and the correct weak-coupling quantum master equation at all temperatures, using Zwanzig projection operator techniques. This new correction can easily be used with many of the advanced HEOM methods outlined above. We also demonstrate how this idea can be applied to derive an approximate terminator for the HEOM, which is closely related to the previously mentioned time-derivative truncation scheme.
Hou et al. 2015
This terminator can also help to remove instabilities from the HEOM, at no additional cost in propagating the hierarchy of ADOs. We also test the new low temperature correction scheme and terminator on a set of model problems, including a model of
\mathrm{S}_{1}\to\mathrm{S}_{0}
internal conversion and a model of exciton dynamics in the Fenna-Metthews-Olson (FMO) protein-pigment complex.

The hierarchical equations of motion

(1)

Shi et al. 2009a

II.2
HEOM termination

The hierarchy in Eq. (
2
) is formally infinite, so the set of auxiliary density operators has to be truncated to some finite set for practical calculations. One part of this truncation is done by only explicitly including some set of modes up to
k=M=k_{\mathrm{c}}-1
, in the calculation and by truncating the remaining infinite set of ADOs to some finite set. The truncation of the hierarchy for
k\leq M
is typically performed using various truncation criteria, such as by truncating ADOs above some maximum excitation level
L=\sum_{k=0}^{M}n_{k}
,
Ishizaki and Tanimura 2005
or by truncating ADOs above some maximum frequency
|\gamma_{\mathbf{n}}|=|\sum_{k=0}^{M}n_{k}\nu_{k}|
.
Dijkstra and Prokhorenko 2017
The HEOM dynamics can then be converged to arbitrary precision by increasing the number of modes,
k
, included and the number of ADOs used for these explicitly treated modes.

A standard approach to correct for the truncated terms in the series expansion of
C(t)
is to treat the terms with
k\geq k_{\mathrm{c}}
with a one-sided delta function,
e^{-\nu_{k}t}\approx(1/\nu_{k})\delta_{+}(t)
, in the path integral expression for the reduced density operator.
Ishizaki and Tanimura 2005
;
Tanimura 2020
This gives the following correction, which is added to the right-hand side of Eq. (
2
) for the explicitly treated ADOs,

\displaystyle\Xi_{0}\hat{\rho}_{\mathbf{n}}(t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=k_{\mathrm{c}}}^{\infty}\nu_{k}^{-1}\pazocal{L}_{k-}\pazocal{L}_{k+}\hat{\rho}_{\mathbf{n}}(t).

(3)

This type of correction is commonly referred to as a “low temperature” correction because it was first used to correct for truncated Matsubara frequencies terms in the Matsubara expansion of
C(t)
, which only become important at low temperatures.
Ishizaki and Tanimura 2005
;
Tanimura 2020

For this approximation to be valid, we require that the approximated terms decay much faster than the characteristic system frequencies. In other words
\nu_{k}\gg\omega_{\mathrm{s}}
should be satisfied for
k\geq k_{\mathrm{c}}
.
For large system energy scales this approximation can break down, leading to slow convergence of the HEOM dynamics with respect to
k_{\mathrm{c}}
, even though such systems can be well approximated with simple perturbative theories. This is because the
k_{\mathrm{c}}=0
limit of
\Xi_{0}
is not consistent with weak-coupling perturbative theories, as explained in Appendix
C
. Examples of this slow convergence of the HEOM are shown in the left hand panels of Figs.
1
and
2
for a spin boson model (the details of which are given below).

II.3
The Nakajima-Zwanzig Low Temperature Correction

An alternative modified low temperature correction term can be derived by taking a Zwanzig-projected quantum master equation perspective on the hierarchy truncation. First we note that we can write down the complete hierarchy of ADOs as

\displaystyle|\rho(t)\rangle\!\rangle=\sum_{\mathrm{all}\ \mathbf{n}}|\rho_{\mathbf{n}}(t)\rangle\!\rangle\otimes|\mathbf{n}\rangle\!\rangle

(4)

where
|\mathbf{n}\rangle\!\rangle
is a basis vector corresponding to auxiliary density operator
\mathbf{n}
, and
|\rho_{\mathbf{n}}(t)\rangle\!\rangle
is the Liouville space vector of this ADO. We can write down the equation of motion for the ADOs more compactly as
Shi et al. 2009b
;
Ikeda and Nakayama 2022
;
Ke, Borrelli, and Thoss 2022

\displaystyle\derivative{t}|\rho(t)\rangle\!\rangle

\displaystyle=\pazocal{L}|\rho(t)\rangle\!\rangle=(\pazocal{L}_{\mathrm{s}}\otimes\pazocal{I}_{\mathrm{ado}}-\pazocal{I}_{\mathrm{s}}\otimes\Gamma+\pazocal{V})|\rho(t)\rangle\!\rangle

(5)

where
\pazocal{L}_{\mathrm{s}}=-(i/\hbar)[\hat{H}_{\mathrm{s}},\ \cdot\ ]
is the system Liouvillian,
\pazocal{I}_{\mathrm{s}}
and
\pazocal{I}_{\mathrm{ado}}
are identity operators on the system Liouville space and the set of ADOs respectively,
\Gamma
is diagonal matrix of decay rates for each ADO,

\displaystyle\Gamma=\sum_{\mathrm{all}\ \mathbf{n}}\gamma_{\mathbf{n}}|\mathbf{n}\rangle\!\rangle\langle\!\langle\mathbf{n}|

(6)

in which
\gamma_{\mathbf{n}}=\sum_{k=0}^{M}n_{k}\nu_{k}
, and
\pazocal{V}
is the term that couples different ADOs within the hierarchy

\displaystyle\begin{split}\pazocal{V}&=\sum_{\mathrm{all}\ \mathbf{n}}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\bigg(\sqrt{n_{k}+1}\pazocal{L}_{k-}\otimes|\mathbf{n}\rangle\!\rangle\langle\!\langle\mathbf{n}_{k}^{+}|\\
&+\sqrt{n_{k}}\pazocal{L}_{k+}\otimes|\mathbf{n}\rangle\!\rangle\langle\!\langle\mathbf{n}_{k}^{-}|\bigg).\end{split}

(7)

We can now obtain an equation for the hierarchy of ADOs where the number of modes is truncated at
k=M=k_{\mathrm{c}}-1
, by projecting this equation with the projection operator

\pazocal{P}=\sum_{\mathbf{n}\in\pazocal{N}_{M}}\pazocal{P}_{\mathbf{n}}=\sum_{\mathbf{n}\in\pazocal{N}_{M}}\pazocal{I}_{\mathrm{s}}\otimes|\mathbf{n}\rangle\!\rangle\!\langle\!\langle\mathbf{n}|

where
\pazocal{N}_{M}
is the set of all ADO indices where
n_{k}=0
for
k>M
. The projected hierarchy of ADOs
\pazocal{P}|\rho(t)\rangle\!\rangle
contains all ADOs which are treated explicitly, and its dynamics are given exactly by the Nakajima-Zwanzig equation,
Nakajima 1958
;
Zwanzig 1960
;
Mori 1965
(a brief derivation of which is given in Appendix
A
)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\derivative{t}\pazocal{P}|\rho(t)\rangle\!\rangle&=\pazocal{P}\pazocal{L}\pazocal{P}|\rho(t)\rangle\!\rangle\\
+\int_{0}^{t}&\differential{\tau}\pazocal{P}\pazocal{V}e^{(1-\pazocal{P})\pazocal{L}\tau}(1-\pazocal{P})\pazocal{V}\pazocal{P}|\rho(t-\tau)\rangle\!\rangle.\end{split}

(8)

We can approximate the last term in this equation to second order in the coupling term
\pazocal{V}
, and apply the Markovian approximation to arrive at a perturbative form of the above equation

\displaystyle\derivative{t}\pazocal{P}|\rho(t)\rangle\!\rangle

\displaystyle=\pazocal{P}\pazocal{L}\pazocal{P}|\rho(t)\rangle\!\rangle+\pazocal{K}\pazocal{P}|\rho(t)\rangle\!\rangle

(9)

\displaystyle\pazocal{K}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\differential{t}\pazocal{P}\pazocal{V}(1-\pazocal{P})e^{\pazocal{L}_{0}t}(1-\pazocal{P})\pazocal{V}\pazocal{P}

(10)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mathbf{n}\in\pazocal{N}_{M}}\Xi_{\mathbf{n}}\pazocal{P}_{\mathbf{n}},

(11)

where
\pazocal{L}_{0}=\pazocal{L}_{\mathrm{s}}\otimes\pazocal{I}_{\mathrm{ado}}-\pazocal{I}_{\mathrm{s}}\otimes\Gamma
. In this equation we have eliminated the time-convolution and replaced the upper limit of the integral with
\infty
to make the equation time-homogeneous, an approximation which is valid when the terms in the kernel decay on a shorter time-scale than the dynamics of interest
Sparpaglione and Mukamel 1988
and for long-time dynamics.
Fay, Lindoy, and Manolopoulos 2018
;
Fay and Manolopoulos 2019
Noting that
(1-\pazocal{P})\pazocal{V}\pazocal{P}_{\mathbf{n}}
only couples ADO
\mathbf{n}
to
\mathbf{n}_{k}^{+}
for
k\geq k_{\mathrm{c}}
, and that
\pazocal{P}\pazocal{V}(1-\pazocal{P})
only couples this back to
\mathbf{n}
, the new low temperature correction term
\Xi_{\mathbf{n}}
for each ADO is found to be

\displaystyle\Xi_{\mathbf{n}}=\sum_{k=k_{\mathrm{c}}}^{\infty}\pazocal{L}_{k-}\Pi_{\mathrm{s}}(\nu_{k}+\gamma_{\mathbf{n}}-\Lambda_{\mathrm{s}})^{-1}\Pi_{\mathrm{s}}^{-1}\pazocal{L}_{k+}

(12)

where
\Pi_{\mathrm{s}}
and
\Lambda_{\mathrm{s}}
are the matrix of eigenvectors, and eigenvalues of
\pazocal{L}_{\mathrm{s}}
, i.e.
\pazocal{L}_{\mathrm{s}}=\Pi_{\mathrm{s}}\Lambda_{\mathrm{s}}\Pi_{\mathrm{s}}^{-1}
. This gives an alternative low-temperature correction which accounts for the finite system frequencies. Clearly it is very closely related to the original form in Eq. (
3
), to which it reduces if
\|\gamma_{\mathbf{n}}-\Lambda_{\mathrm{s}}\|\ll\nu_{k}
. The generalization of this correction for an arbitrary exponential approximation, for example the Padé decomposition,
Hu et al. 2011
is given in Appendix
B
.

This new low temperature correction reduces the HEOM to the perturbative Markovian Nakajima-Zwanzig equation in the limit where only
\hat{\rho}_{\mathbf{0}}(t)
is included in the hierarchy, which in the weak-coupling limit is exact and obeys detailed balance. In this limit the original low-temperature corrected HEOM reduces to a high temperature master equation
Takahashi and Tanimura 2020
which does not satisfy detailed balance at all temperatures. This is expanded upon in the appendix. For these reasons the new low temperature corrected HEOM should converge faster in the low temperature limit.
It is also possible to use the time-homogeneous limit of the time-convolutionless quantum master equation to close the HEOM,
Breuer, Kappler, and Petruccione 2001
as is outlined in the supplemental material. However we find that this approach is inferior to the Nakajima-Zwanzig approach described here, which interestingly is in agreement with previous findings that the Nakajima-Zwanzig approach gives more accurate results for systems with non-unitary reference dynamics.
Fay, Lindoy, and Manolopoulos 2019
We also remark in passing that the Zwanzig projection approach described here could also be used to derive higher order low temperature corrections to the HEOM,
Berkelbach, Markland, and Reichman 2012
but for now we will restrict ourselves to the lowest order correction.

II.4
The Diagonal Nakajima-Zwanzig Terminator

We can also extend this projection operator treatment of the HEOM truncation to obtain a correction term for the termination of the hierarchy to some truncated set of
\mathbf{n}\in\pazocal{N}_{\mathrm{trunc}}
. If we only include the diagonal contributions in the hierarchy, i.e. terms that only couple
|\rho_{\mathbf{n}}(t)\rangle\!\rangle
to itself, so
\pazocal{K}
is approximated as

\displaystyle\pazocal{K}\approx\sum_{\mathbf{n}\in\pazocal{N}_{\mathrm{trunc}}}\pazocal{P}_{\mathbf{n}}\pazocal{K}\pazocal{P}_{\mathbf{n}}.

(13)

The correction term for ADO
\mathbf{n}
arising from hierarchy truncation is

\displaystyle\Xi_{\mathbf{n}}^{\mathrm{trunc}}\!=\!\sum_{k\in{\pazocal{M}}_{\mathbf{n}}^{\mathrm{trunc}}}(n_{k}+1)\pazocal{L}_{k-}\Pi_{\mathrm{s}}(\nu_{k}+\gamma_{\mathbf{n}}-\Lambda_{\mathrm{s}})^{-1}\Pi_{\mathrm{s}}^{-1}\pazocal{L}_{k+}

(14)

where
\pazocal{M}_{\mathbf{n}}^{\mathrm{trunc}}
is the set of mode indices at which ADO
\mathbf{n}
terminates. Including this diagonal correction term adds no additional dense blocks to the generator (
\pazocal{P}\pazocal{L}\pazocal{P}+\pazocal{K}
) for the truncated hierarchical equations of motion with the low-temperature correction above. Furthermore
\Xi_{\mathbf{n}}^{\mathrm{trunc}}
can be pre-computed along with
\Xi_{\mathbf{n}}
and used with no extra computational cost in propagating the HEOM.

This truncation term can be viewed as modification of Tanimura
et al.
’s fast modulation scheme,
Tanimura and Wolynes 1991
;
Tanimura and Mukamel 1994
and unlike the POP scheme proposed in Ref.
Xu et al. 2005
, Eq. (
14
) does not produce a time-dependent generator in the equations of motion, which adds complexity to the time propagation. It is interesting to note that if we applied the TCL2 approach to derive a terminator, we arrive at the POP scheme, which was originally derived from a path integral approach. Eq. (
14
) is closely related to the Caldeira-Leggett termination scheme
Kong et al. 2015
and the time-derivative truncation scheme
Hou et al. 2015
; indeed it is simply an approximation to the time-derivative scheme where cross couplings between
\mathbf{n}
and
\mathbf{n}_{k,k^{\prime}}^{+-}=(n_{0},...,n_{k}+1,...,n_{k^{\prime}}-1,...)
are excluded. The full perturbative correction term
\pazocal{K}
for the hierarchy truncation, which is equivalent to the time-derivative truncation scheme, includes these cross-terms which couple different ADOs, which adds considerable computational effort to integrating the equations of motion. We have found however that these terms can be neglected and convergence of the HEOM can still be improved with just the diagonal contributions in Eq. (
14
). It should be noted that this termination correction, and the termination schemes mentioned above, are separate from the low temperature corrections in Eqs. (
3
) and (
12
), although clearly they are closely related, and here we have clarified how they can both be derived in a consistent Zwanzig projection framework.

IV
Concluding remarks

In conclusion, we have shown that using perturbative Nakajima-Zwanzig theory we can obtain corrections for truncation of the HEOM which restore consistency with the weak-coupling quantum master equation
at all temperatures
. In the high temperature limit this closure reduces to the widely used Ishizaki-Tanimura closure,
Ishizaki and Tanimura 2005
but at lower temperatures it accounts for finite system frequencies unlike the Ishizaki-Tanimura correction. The new correction scheme is found to improve convergence of the HEOM in several cases, including in a model of energy transport in a photosynthetic protein-pigment complex, with negligible additional computational effort.

The new low temperature correction scheme can be used in place of the Ishizaki-Tanimura correction along with many of the methods for improving HEOM convergence and efficiency, because many of these involve some truncation of a mode expansion of
C(t)
at some
k=M
.
Shi et al. 2009a
;
Yan 2014
;
Dunn, Tempelaar, and Reichman 2019
;
Hu et al. 2011
;
Cui et al. 2019
;
Chen et al. 2022
;
Ikeda and Nakayama 2022
;
Ikeda and Scholes 2020
;
Li, Yan, and Shi 2022
We have presented one such generalization in Appendix
B
where we have demonstrated how to include this low temperature scheme for arbitrary exponential approximations to
C(t)
, for example the Padé scheme. For example this low temperature can easily be applied along with Prony filtration,
Dunn, Tempelaar, and Reichman 2019
on-the-fly truncation of the hierarchy,
Shi et al. 2009a
or approaches based on collective bath coordinate mapping.
Ikeda and Nakayama 2022
;
Li, Yan, and Shi 2022
It should also be noted that the low temperature correction and terminator presented here can be applied with any cut-off criterion for the HEOM.
Dijkstra and Prokhorenko 2017
;
Ishizaki and Tanimura 2005
;
Härtle et al. 2013

The Zwanzig projection approach described here could also be used to derive more accurate closures for the HEOM. For example higher order corrections can be derived, which can be approximately resummed to infinite order,
Chen, Berkelbach, and Reichman 2016
or it may be possible to derive self-consistent approximations to improve these closures.
Sowa et al. 2020
These possibilities certainly warrant further investigation. Another possible use of the Zwanzig approach is that it yields an estimate for the truncated part of the hierarchy
(1-\pazocal{P})|\rho(t)\rangle\!\rangle\approx-\pazocal{L}_{0}^{-1}(1-\pazocal{P})\pazocal{V}\pazocal{P}|\rho(t)\rangle\!\rangle
. This could potentially be used with the Shi
et al.
’s “on the fly” truncation scheme
Shi et al. 2009a
to reintroduce truncated ADOs in a consistent and adaptive manner.

Finally, it should be noted that the low temperature correction scheme presented here is remarkably simple. This means it can be very easily implemented in existing HEOM codes,
Kreisbeck, Kramer, and Aspuru-Guzik 2014
;
Johansson, Nation, and Nori 2013
;
Ikeda and Scholes 2020
;
Pyr
in addition to the publicly available Matlab code “HEOM-lab”,
heo
which was used to perform the calculations presented in this paper. Given this, we anticipate this new low temperature correction could be used routinely in future HEOM calculations.

Appendix A
The Nakajima-Zwanzig equation

In this appendix we briefly outline the derivation of the Nakajima-Zwanzig equation and its Markovian limit. First we note that we can split the equaiton of motion for
|\rho(t)\rangle\!\rangle
into equations for
\pazocal{P}|\rho(t)\rangle\!\rangle
and
(1-\pazocal{P})|\rho(t)\rangle\!\rangle
as

\displaystyle\derivative{t}\pazocal{P}|\rho(t)\rangle\!\rangle

\displaystyle=\pazocal{P}\pazocal{L}\pazocal{P}|\rho(t)\rangle\!\rangle+\pazocal{P}\pazocal{L}(1-\pazocal{P})|\rho(t)\rangle\!\rangle

(18)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\derivative{t}(1-\pP)|\rho(t)\rangle\!\rangle&=(1-\pazocal{P})\pazocal{L}\pazocal{P}|\rho(t)\rangle\!\rangle\\
&+(1-\pazocal{P})\pazocal{L}(1-\pazocal{P})|\rho(t)\rangle\!\rangle.\end{split}

(19)

The equation for
(1-\pazocal{P})|\rho(t)\rangle\!\rangle
can be integrated formally to give

\displaystyle(1-\pazocal{P})|\rho(t)\rangle\!\rangle=\int_{0}^{t}\differential{\tau}e^{(1-\pazocal{P})\pazocal{L}(t-\tau)}(1-\pazocal{P})\pazocal{L}\pazocal{P}|\rho(\tau)\rangle\!\rangle,

(20)

where we have assumed
\pazocal{P}|\rho(t=0)\rangle\!\rangle=|\rho(t=0)\rangle\!\rangle
. Inserting this into the above equation for
\pazocal{P}|\rho(t)\rangle\!\rangle
, noting that
\pazocal{P}\pazocal{L}_{0}(1-\pazocal{P})=(1-\pazocal{P})\pazocal{L}_{0}\pazocal{P}=0
, and using the idempotency of
\pazocal{P}
, together with a change of variables in the integral
\tau\to t-\tau
, straightforwardly gives the Nakajima-Zwanzig equation, Eq.
8
.

The Markovian limit of this equation can be obtained by Laplace transforming the Nakajima-Zwanzig equation to turn the time-convolution into a simple product term,
Sparpaglione and Mukamel 1988

\displaystyle s\pazocal{P}|\tilde{\rho}(s)\rangle\!\rangle-|\rho(0)\rangle\!\rangle

\displaystyle=\pazocal{P}\pazocal{L}\pazocal{P}|\tilde{\rho}(s)\rangle\!\rangle+\tilde{\kappa}(s)\pazocal{P}|\tilde{\rho}(s)\rangle\!\rangle

(21)

where
\tilde{f}(s)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\differential{t}f(t)e^{-st}
, and
\kappa(t)=\pazocal{P}\pazocal{L}e^{(1-\pazocal{P})\pazocal{L}t}\pazocal{P}\pazocal{L}\pazocal{P}
. To obtain the time-homogeneous Markovian approximation to the Nakajima-Zwanzig equation we replace
\tilde{\kappa}(s)\to\tilde{\kappa}(s=0)
, and then transform the equation back to the time-domain.
Sparpaglione and Mukamel 1988
This approximation is valid when the kernel
\kappa(t)
decays faster than the dynamics of
\pazocal{P}|\rho(t)\rangle\!\rangle
, and it exactly preserves the long-time limit and time-integrated properties of the
\pazocal{P}|\rho(t)\rangle\!\rangle
dynamics.
Fay and Manolopoulos 2019
Making a perturbative approximation to the projected propagator in
\kappa(t)
, together with this approximation, yields Eq. (
10
).

Appendix B
Generalized low-temperature correction

In this appendix we derive a generalized version of the low temperature correction for the HEOM based on an arbitrary exponential approximation to the exact
C(t)
. Such approximations include the Padé scheme
Hu et al. 2011
and the Prony fitting scheme.
Chen et al. 2022
We denote such an approximation by

\displaystyle\tilde{C}(t)=\sum_{k=0}^{k_{\mathrm{c}}-1}\tilde{c}_{k}e^{-\tilde{\gamma}_{k}t}\text{ and }\tilde{C}(t)^{*}=\sum_{k=0}^{k_{\mathrm{c}}-1}\tilde{\bar{c}}_{k}^{*}e^{-\tilde{\gamma}_{k}t}

(22)

and we assume we know an exact (possibly infinite) expansion
C(t)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}c_{k}e^{-\gamma_{k}t}
and
C(t)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\bar{c}_{k}e^{-\gamma_{k}t}
(for example the full Matsubara expansion). With the generalized HEOM formalism, we can write an HEOM in terms of the exact expansion of
C(t)
as

\displaystyle C(t)

\displaystyle=\tilde{C}(t)+\delta C(t).

(23)

The hierarchy is expanded as a sum of exponentials
C(t)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}a_{k}e^{-\nu_{k}t}\ \text{and}\ C(t)^{*}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\bar{a}_{k}^{*}e^{-\nu_{k}t}
with decay rates
{\bf\it\nu}=(\tilde{{\bf\it\gamma}},\tilde{{\bf\it\gamma}},{\bf\it\gamma})
, and coefficients
{\bf\it a}=(\tilde{{\bf\it c}},-\tilde{{\bf\it c}},{\bf\it c})
,
\bar{{\bf\it a}}=(\tilde{\bar{{\bf\it c}}},-\tilde{\bar{{\bf\it c}}},\bar{{\bf\it c}})
,
[{\bf\it\nu}]_{k}=\nu_{k}
and likewise for the other row vectors. Now the terms from
k=0
to
k=k_{\mathrm{c}}-1
are treated explicitly, and the remaining terms, corresponding to
\delta C(t)
, are projected out as described in Sec.
II.3
. This gives the following generalized low temperature correction term

\displaystyle\begin{split}\Xi_{\mathbf{n}}&=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}{\pazocal{L}}_{k-}\Pi_{\mathrm{s}}(\gamma_{k}+\gamma_{\mathbf{n}}-\Lambda_{\mathrm{s}})^{-1}\Pi_{\mathrm{s}}^{-1}{\pazocal{L}}_{k+}\\
&-\sum_{k=0}^{k_{\mathrm{c}}-1}\tilde{\pazocal{L}}_{k-}\Pi_{\mathrm{s}}(\tilde{\gamma}_{k}+\gamma_{\mathbf{n}}-\Lambda_{\mathrm{s}})^{-1}\Pi_{\mathrm{s}}^{-1}\tilde{\pazocal{L}}_{k+}\end{split}

(24)

where
\pazocal{L}_{k-}=-i\sqrt{|c_{k}|}[\hat{V},\ \cdot\ ]
and
\pazocal{L}_{k+}=(c_{k}\hat{V}\ \cdot\ -\bar{c}_{k}^{*}\ \cdot\ \hat{V})/\sqrt{|c_{k}|}
and
\tilde{\pazocal{L}}_{k-}=-i\sqrt{|\tilde{c}_{k}|}[\hat{V},\ \cdot\ ]
and
\tilde{\pazocal{L}}_{k+}=(\tilde{c}_{k}\hat{V}\ \cdot\ -\tilde{\bar{c}}_{k}^{*}\ \cdot\ \hat{V})/\sqrt{|\tilde{c}_{k}|}
. This can be extended to the Ikeda & Scholes’ generalized HEOM
Ikeda and Scholes 2020
in the same way; all that is needed is a decomposition of
\delta C(t)
as a sum of exponentials.

Appendix C
Relationship to the weak-coupling master equation

In order to obtain a weak-coupling quantum master equation, we can directly apply Markovian second order Nakajima-Zwanzig theory to the full sub-system plus bath Hamiltonian, Eq. (
1
), with the projection operator
\pazocal{P}=\hat{\rho}_{\mathrm{b}}\Tr_{\mathrm{b}}[\ \cdot\ ]
to obtain a weak-coupling quantum master equation which is valid at all temperatures.
Nakajima 1958
;
Zwanzig 1960
;
Mori 1965
This equation is given by

\displaystyle\derivative{t}\hat{\rho}(t)=\pazocal{L}_{\mathrm{s}}\hat{\rho}(t)+\pazocal{R}\hat{\rho}(t)

(25)

where the relaxation superoperator
\pazocal{R}
is given by

\displaystyle\pazocal{R}\hat{\rho}

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{\infty}\differential{t}\Tr_{\mathrm{b}}[[\hat{V}\hat{B},e^{(\pazocal{L}_{\mathrm{s}}+\pazocal{L}_{\mathrm{b}})t}[\hat{V}\hat{B},\hat{\rho}\hat{\rho}_{\mathrm{b}}]]]

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{\infty}\differential{t}\hat{V}^{\times}\Tr_{\mathrm{b}}[\hat{B}e^{(\pazocal{L}_{\mathrm{s}}+\pazocal{L}_{\mathrm{b}})t}[\hat{V}\hat{B},\hat{\rho}\hat{\rho}_{\mathrm{b}}]]]

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{\infty}\differential{t}\hat{V}^{\times}e^{\pazocal{L}_{\mathrm{s}}t}\left(C(t)\hat{V}\hat{\rho}-C(t)^{*}\hat{\rho}\hat{V}\right)

(26)

where
\hat{V}^{\times}=[\hat{V},\ \cdot\ ]
, and
\pazocal{L}_{\mathrm{b}}=-\frac{i}{\hbar}[\hat{H}_{\mathrm{b}},\ \cdot\ ]
. Inserting the decomposition of
C(t)
and
C(t)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}a_{k}e^{-\nu_{k}t}
, and
C(t)^{*}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\bar{a}_{k}^{*}e^{-\nu_{k}t}
, and inserting the eigen-decomposition of
\pazocal{L}_{\mathrm{s}}=\Pi_{\mathrm{s}}\Lambda_{\mathrm{s}}\Pi_{\mathrm{s}}^{-1}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\pazocal{R}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\pazocal{L}_{k-}\Pi_{\mathrm{s}}(\nu_{k}-\Lambda_{\mathrm{s}})^{-1}\Pi_{\mathrm{s}}^{-1}\pazocal{L}_{k+}

(27)

which is exactly
\Xi_{\mathbf{n}=\mathbf{0}}
with
k_{\mathrm{c}}=0
. Within the secular approximation, when the system bath coupling is weak and coherences and populations decouple in the system energy eigenbasis
\ket{E_n}
(i.e. when the secular approximation becomes valid), the energy eigenstate population transfer rates,
\pazocal{R}_{E_{n}E_{n}\leftarrow E_{m}E_{m}}=\Tr_{\mathrm{s}}[\outerproduct{E_n}{E_n}(\pazocal{R}\outerproduct{E_m}{E_m})]
, satisfy detailed balance,
Zwanzig 2001

\displaystyle\frac{\pazocal{R}_{E_{n}E_{n}\leftarrow E_{m}E_{m}}}{\pazocal{R}_{E_{m}E_{m}\leftarrow E_{n}E_{n}}}=e^{-\beta(E_{n}-E_{m})}.

(28)

For the standard low-temperature correction, when
k_{\mathrm{c}}=0
we have
Takahashi and Tanimura 2020

\displaystyle\Xi_{0}\hat{\rho}=-\int_{0}^{\infty}\differential{t}\hat{V}^{\times}\left(C(t)\hat{V}\hat{\rho}-C(t)^{*}\hat{\rho}\hat{V}\right),

(29)

which is not consistent with the weak-coupling master equation, Eqs. (
25
) and (
C
), except in the high temperature limit. This means the standard low-temperature correction to the HEOM breaks down in the low temperature limit when
k_{\mathrm{c}}=0
, even for arbitrarily weak system-bath coupling. In this limit the detailed balance condition is not satisfied, which will lead to errors in long time populations.

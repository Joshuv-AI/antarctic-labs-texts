Independent sets in hypergraphs
Jacques Verstraete, Chase Wilson

Abstract
A theorem of Shearer states that every n n -vertex triangle-free graph of maximum degree d ≥ 2 d\geq 2 contains an independent set of size at least ( d ​ log ⁡ d − d + 1 ) / ( d − 1 ) 2 ⋅ n (d\log d-d+1)/(d-1)^{2}\cdot n . Ajtai, Komlós, Pintz, Spencer and Szemerédi proved that every ( r + 1 ) (r+1) -uniform n n -vertex “uncrowded” hypergraph of maximum degree d ≥ 1 d\geq 1 has an independent set of size at least c r ​ ( log ⁡ d ) 1 / r / d 1 / r ⋅ n c_{r}(\log d)^{1/r}/d^{1/r}\cdot n for some c r > 0 c_{r}>0 depending only on r r . Shearer asked whether his method for triangle-free graphs could be extended to uniform hypergraphs. In this paper, we answer this in the affirmative, thereby giving a short proof of the theorem of Ajtai, Komlós, Pintz, Spencer and Szemerédi for a wider class of “locally sparse” hypergraphs.

Introduction

The
independence number

\alpha(H)
of a hypergraph
H

is the maximum size of an
independent set
of vertices of
H
– a set of vertices containing no edges of
H
. For
k\geq 2
, a
k
-cycle
is a hypergraph consisting of edges
e_{0},e_{1},\dots,e_{k-1}
for which there exist distinct vertices
v_{0},v_{1},\dots,v_{k-1}
such that
v_{i}\in e_{i}\cap e_{i+1}
for
0\leq i<k
with subscripts mod
k
. A hypergraph
H
is
linear
if it contains no 2-cycle – equivalently, for any edges
e,f
in
H
,
|e\cap f|\leq 1
.
A hypergraph is
locally sparse
if it contains no 2-cycle or 3-cycle. Following
[
1
]
, a hypergraph is
uncrowded
if it has no
k
-cycles for
k\leq 4
.
Independent sets in linear, locally sparse and uncrowded hypergraphs have been studied at length and have numerous connections and applications, for instance to independent sets in Steiner systems
[
13
,
14
,
28
,
30
,
24
]
, Heilbronn’s triangle problem
[
11
,
26
,
29
]
, line arrangements
[
19
,
21
]
, Ramsey theory
[
3
,
2
,
8
,
9
,
23
,
25
]
,
coloring hypergraphs
[
4
,
7
,
12
,
17
,
18
]
and the method of containers
[
6
,
32
,
33
]
.

A theorem of Shearer
[
34
]
states that every
n
-vertex triangle-free (locally sparse) graph of average degree
d\geq 2
contains an independent set
of size at least
(d\log d-d+1)/(d-1)^{2}\cdot n
, improving an earlier result of Ajtai, Komlós and Szemerédi
[
2
]
.
For uniform hypergraphs, a fundamental theorem due to Ajtai, Komlós, Pintz, Spencer and Szemerédi
[
1
]
states
that for any
n
-vertex
(r+1)
-uniform uncrowded hypergraph

, as

\alpha(H)\geq\frac{0.98}{e}\cdot 10^{-\frac{5}{r}}\cdot\Bigl(\frac{\log d}{d}\Bigr)^{\frac{1}{r}}\cdot n.

(1)

The proof of (

) is via a randomized greedy algorithm akin to the

]
, although the analysis is substantially more complicated. This result improves the bound
[
10
]
obtained by random sampling

-uniform hypergraph of maximum degree

\alpha(H)\geq\Bigl(1-\frac{1}{r}\Bigr)\frac{n}{d^{1/r}}.

(2)

Shearer asked whether the method in
[
34
]
for triangle-free (locally sparse) graphs could apply to the uniform hypergraph setting. We answer this in the affirmative,

]
for the slightly wider class of locally sparse hypergraphs:

Theorem 1
.

For
r\geq 1
, there exists

such that for any
(r+1)
-uniform
n
-vertex locally sparse hypergraph
H
with maximum degree

.

\alpha(H)\geq c_{r}\cdot\Bigl(\frac{\log d}{d}\Bigr)^{\frac{1}{r}}\cdot n.

(3)

where

for some function

.

The proof of Theorem

is inspired by the approach of Kostochka, Mubayi and the first author
[
24
]
and Shearer
[
34
]
for triangle-free graphs, and gives an improvement of the constant

[
1
]
for small
r

1

for large enough

, which improves on the constant value
0.98/10^{5/2}e\approx 0.00114007
in (

). The value of

from the proof of Theorem

is

c_{r}=(1-o_{d}(1))\frac{(r-1)^{1-\frac{1}{r}}(2^{r}-1)}{r^{2+\frac{2}{r}}2^{r}}

(4)

and, in particular,
c_{r}=(1-o_{r}(1))(1-o_{d}(1))/r
as

. Via random sampling, as in Duke, Lefmann and Rödl
[

, one may obtain

(1-o_{d}(1))(1-2/(3r-1))^{1/r}
times the lower bound (
3
) in Theorem
1
when
H
is a linear
(r+1)
-uniform hypergraph of maximum degree

. In the case of

-vertex
(r+1)
-uniform

-regular hypergraphs

with no

(1-o_{d}(1))\cdot\Bigl(\frac{\log d}{rd}\Bigr)^{\frac{1}{r}}\cdot n-\frac{d^{g}}{rg!}\cdot n\leq\mathrm{E}(|I|)\leq(1+o_{d}(1))\cdot\Bigl(\frac{\log d}{rd}\Bigr)^{\frac{1}{r}}\cdot n+\frac{d^{g}}{rg!}\cdot n.

In particular, if

(1+o_{d}(1))(\log d)^{1/r}/(rd)^{1/r}\cdot n
.

Notation and terminology.
Let
H
be a
(r+1)
-uniform hypergraph with vertex set
V=V(H)
and edge set
E(H)
. We write

\mathcal{I}(H)
for the set of independent sets in
H
, and let
\alpha(H)=\max\{|I|:I\in\mathcal{I}(H)\}
denote the
independence number
of
H
.
For
v\in V
, and
1\leq k\leq r
, define

N_{k}(v)=\{f\in{V\setminus\{v\}\choose k}:\exists e\in E(H),f\cup\{v\}\subseteq e\}.

In particular, we let
N(v)=N_{1}(v)
denote the set of vertices in a common edge with
v
– sometimes called the
neighborhood
of
v
. Define for
I\subseteq V
the
k
-shadow of
H
on
I
as

\partial_{k}I=\{e\in{I\choose k}:\exists f\in E(H),e\subseteq f\}=\displaystyle{\bigcup_{v\in V}}N_{k}(v)\cap{I\choose k}.

We use the following asymptotic notation. If for each
r\in\mathbb{N}
,
f_{r},g_{r}:\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{+}
are positive real-valued functions, then we write
f_{r}=O_{r}(g_{r})
and
g_{r}=\Omega_{r}(f_{r})
if there exists for each
r\in\mathbb{N}

a constant
C_{r}
such that
f_{r}(x)\leq C_{r}g_{r}(x)
for all
x\in\mathbb{N}
, and

f_{r}=o_{r}(g_{r})
if
\lim_{r\rightarrow\infty}C_{r}=0
.

2
A Non-Uniform Distribution on Independent Sets

This section serves as intuition and motivation for the proof of Theorem
1
. If
H
is a locally sparse
n
-vertex
(r+1)
-uniform hypergraph of maximum degree
d
,
and we sample an independent set
I\in\mathcal{I}(H)
uniformly, then following Shearer
[
34
]
(see page 272 in Alon and Spencer
[
5
]
), it is natural to consider for
v\in V(H)
the random variable
X_{v}
which depends on the
r
-shadow
\partial_{r}I
of
H
on
I
:

X_{v}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}d^{1/r}&\mbox{ if }v\in I\\
|N_{r}(v)\cap\partial_{r}I|&\mbox{ if }v\not\in I.\end{array}\right.

(6)

Let
X=\sum_{v\in V(G)}X_{v}
. By definition,
X=d^{1/r}\cdot|I|+|\partial_{r}I|
. To derive a lower bound on
\mathrm{E}(X_{v})
, let

I_{v}=I\backslash(N(v)\cup\{v\})\quad\mbox{ and }\quad Y_{v}=|\{e\in N_{r}(v):I_{v}\cup e\in\mathcal{I}(H)\}|.

Since
H
is linear,
N_{r}(v)
consists of disjoint
r
-element sets, and since
H
is locally sparse, if
I_{v}\cup e
and
I_{v}\cup f
are independent sets in

H
for
e,f\in N_{r}(v)
, then so is
I_{v}\cup e\cup f
. We conclude

\mathrm{E}(X_{v}|I_{v})=\frac{Y_{v}2^{r(Y_{v}-1)}+d^{1/r}(2^{r}-1)^{Y_{v}}}{2^{rY_{v}}+(2^{r}-1)^{Y_{v}}}.

A simple optimization shows this is always at least
b_{r}\log d
for some positive constant
b_{r}=(1-o_{r}(1))/r
depending only on
r
. By the tower property of conditional expectation,
\mathrm{E}(X_{v})=\mathrm{E}(\mathrm{E}(X_{v}|I_{v}))
. Therefore, if
X=\sum_{v\in V(H)}X_{v}
,

b_{r}\cdot n\log d\leq\mathrm{E}(X)\leq d^{1/r}\cdot\mathrm{E}(|I|)+\mathrm{E}(|\partial_{r}I|).

Now
|\partial_{r}I|
may be as large as
d|I|
, and if we use this upper bound, we only obtain the very weak bound

\mathrm{E}(|I|)\geq b_{r}(\log d)/(d+d^{1/r})\cdot n
. The value of
\mathrm{E}(|\partial_{r}I|)
seems to be difficult
to determine when
I
is a uniformly chosen independent set in a linear or locally sparse hypergraph.

To remedy the lack of control of
\mathrm{E}(|\partial I|)
, we shall sample independent sets in a locally sparse
(r+1)
-uniform hypergraph with a carefully chosen
non-uniform probability measure
\mathrm{Pr}(I)
, which weights our sampling of
I\in\mathcal{I}(H)
by
\exp(-\delta|\partial_{r}I|)
, where
\delta^{r}\approx(\log d)/d
.
This has the effect that the probability that
\partial_{r}I
is large is vanishingly small. But this introduces a new problem: we may have that
N_{r}(v)\cap\partial_{r}I
is empty but
N_{r-1}\cap\partial_{r-1}I
is large with high probability. In this case, we do not get anything from the
|N_{r}(v)\cap\partial_{r}I|
term in
X_{v}
, but also adding
v
to
I
decreases its weight by enough so that
\Pr(v\in I)
is small. To fix this, we add a term counting
|N_{r-1}(v)\cap\partial_{r-1}I|
to
X_{v}
. This forces us to weight our sampling of
I\in\mathcal{I}(H)
by
\exp(-2\delta^{2}|\partial_{r-1}I|)
in order to control the upper bound on
X_{v}
. This effect cascades and leads us to the above definitions for
\Pr(I)
and
X_{v}
which depend on all of
|\partial_{k}I|
where
1\leq k\leq r
– see definitions (
8
) and (
9
) in the next section. The analysis for the lower bound then becomes, either there is some
k
for which
|N_{k}(v)\cap\partial_{k}I|
is large in which case we achieve the desired lower bound from the corresponding term, or
|N_{k}(v)\cap\partial_{k}I|
is small for all
k
in which case
v
can be added to
I
with good probability. This gives our intuition for the proof of Theorem
1
, which we now present.

3
Proof of Theorem
1

3.1
Definition of parameters

Let
H
be a
(r+1)
-uniform locally sparse hypergraph of maximum degree
d\geq 1
with
\alpha(H)=m
. For
2\leq k\leq r
, set the following parameters:

\alpha=d^{-\frac{1}{r}}(\log d)^{\frac{1}{r}},\quad\qquad\delta^{r}=\frac{\alpha^{r}}{r^{2}(r-1)},\quad\qquad\beta_{k}=\exp(-(r-k+1)\delta^{r-k+1})

(7)

and let
\beta_{0}=\beta_{1}=1
and
\beta_{r+1}=0
.
We sample each
I\in\mathcal{I}(H)
with probability

\mathrm{Pr}(I)=\frac{\prod_{k=2}^{r}\beta_{k}^{|\partial_{k}I|}}{\sum_{J\in\mathcal{I}(H)}\prod_{k=2}^{r}\beta_{k}^{|\partial_{k}J|}}.

(8)

For
I\in\mathcal{I}(H)
, let
X=X(I)=\sum_{v\in V(H)}X_{v}(I)
where
X_{v}=X_{v}(I)
is defined by

X_{v}=\begin{cases}d^{\frac{1}{r}}&v\in I\\
\sum_{k=1}^{r}\delta^{r-k}|N_{k}(v)\cap\partial_{k}I|&v\not\in I.\end{cases}

(9)

To prove Theorem
1
, we find an upper and lower bound for
\mathrm{E}(X)
. We use the careful definition of the probability measure

\mathrm{Pr}(I)
to show
\mathrm{E}(X)\leq(1+o_{d}(1))\alpha(H)(\log d)/\delta(r-1)
– this is Claim 2. On the other hand, we show for each
v\in V(H)
,

\mathrm{E}(X_{v})\geq(1-o_{d}(1))(2^{r}-1)\log d/r^{2}2^{r}
– this is Claim 5. Combining these bounds gives Theorem
1
.

3.2
The upper bound on
\mathrm{E}(X)

In this section, we prove
\mathrm{E}(X)\leq(1+o_{d}(1))(m\log d)/\delta(r-1)
as
d\rightarrow\infty
.

Claim 1.
Let
Z=\log\prod_{k=2}^{r}\beta_{k}^{-|\partial_{k}I|}
. Then

X\leq\Bigl(d^{\frac{1}{r}}+\frac{\log d}{\delta r(r-1)}\Bigr)|I|+\frac{Z}{\delta}.

(10)

Proof.
Since
|N_{1}(w)|\leq dr
for
w\in V(H)
,
\delta^{r-1}|N_{1}(w)|\leq\delta^{r-1}rd
.
As
H
is linear, for
k\geq 2
each element of
\partial_{k}I
is in
N_{k}(w)
for at most
r-k+1
vertices
w\in V(H)\backslash I
.
By definition (
9
),

\displaystyle X

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle d^{\frac{1}{r}}|I|+\sum_{k=1}^{r}\sum_{v\in V}\delta^{r-k}|N_{k}(v)\cap\partial_{k}I|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle d^{\frac{1}{r}}|I|+r\delta^{r-1}d|I|+\frac{1}{\delta}\sum_{k=2}^{r}(r-k+1)\delta^{r-k+1}|\partial_{k}I|.

By definition (
7
) of
\beta_{k}
, the last term is exactly
Z/\delta
. Since
|I|\leq\alpha(H)
, using
the definition (
7
) of
\delta
, we have
r\delta^{r-1}d=(\log d)/\delta r(r-1)
,
which proves Claim 1.
{\scriptstyle\blacksquare}

Claim 2.
As
d\rightarrow\infty
,

\mathrm{E}(X)\leq(1+o_{d}(1))\cdot\frac{m\log d}{\delta(r-1)}.

(11)

Proof.
By definition of
\beta_{k}
and (
8
), if
Z(I)\geq m/r\log d+4m
then

\Pr(I)=\frac{\exp({-Z(I)})}{\sum_{J\in\mathcal{I}(H)}\prod_{k=2}^{r}\beta_{k}^{|\partial_{k}J|}}\leq\frac{\exp(-m/r\log d-4m)}{\prod_{k=2}^{r}\beta_{k}^{|\partial_{k}\emptyset|}}=\exp(-m/r\log d-4m)

Since
|\mathcal{I}(H)|\leq 2^{m}{n\choose m}
,

\displaystyle\Pr\Bigl(Z(I)\geq\frac{m}{r}\log d+4m\Bigr)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\exp\Bigl(-\frac{m}{r}\log d-4m\Bigr)\cdot 2^{m}{n\choose m}.

Using
{n\choose m}\leq(en/m)^{m}
and the basic lower bound
m\geq(1-1/r)n/d^{1/r}
from (
2
),

\displaystyle 2^{m}{n\choose m}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\exp\Bigl(m\log 2+m\log\frac{en}{m}\Bigr)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\exp\Bigl(\frac{m}{r}\log d+m\log 2+m+m\log\frac{r}{r-1}\Bigr)\;\;\leq\;\;\exp\Bigl(\frac{m}{r}\log d+3m\Bigr).

Therefore the above probability is at most
\exp(-m)=o(m^{-r})
as
d\rightarrow\infty
.
Since
Z=O(m^{r})
, we conclude
\mathrm{E}(Z)\leq(1+o_{d}(1))m(\log d)/r
. Using Claim 1 and
d^{1/r}=o(\log d/(r\delta))
,

\mathrm{E}(X)\leq d^{\frac{1}{r}}m+\frac{m\log d}{\delta r(r-1)}+(1+o_{d}(1))\frac{m\log d}{\delta r}=(1+o_{d}(1))\cdot\frac{m\log d}{\delta(r-1)}.

This proves Claim 2.
{\scriptstyle\blacksquare}

3.3
The lower bound on
\mathrm{E}(X)

In this section we give a lower bound on
\mathrm{E}(X_{v})
for each
v\in V(H)
. Fixing a vertex
v\in V(H)
, let

I_{v}=I\backslash(N(v)\cup\{v\}).

For an edge
e\in E(H)
with
v\in e
, let

\omega_{e}=\{x\in e\backslash\{v\}:I_{v}\cup\{x\}\in\mathcal{I}(H)\}\quad\mbox{ and }\quad Y_{v}=\{\omega_{e}:v\in e\in E(H)\}.

For
\omega_{e}\in Y_{v}
and
S\subseteq\omega_{e}
, let

\displaystyle\beta_{S}=\prod_{x\in S}\prod_{k=1}^{r-1}\beta_{k+1}^{|N_{k}(x)\cap I_{v}|}\qquad\qquad f_{S}=\prod_{j=0}^{|S|}\beta_{j}^{{|S|\choose j}}\qquad\qquad f_{S}^{+}=\prod_{j=0}^{|S|}\beta_{j+1}^{{|S|\choose j}}.

The quantity
f_{S}
corresponds to the weight in (
8
) coming from the edge
e
after adding
S
to
I_{v}
and
f_{S}^{+}
corresponds to the weight after adding
S\cup\{v\}
to
I_{v}
.
\beta_{S}
corresponds to the weight in (
8
) coming from edges of the form
x\cup T
where
x\in S
and
T\subseteq I_{v}
after adding
S
to
I_{v}
. Since
H
is locally sparse, this is the only additional weight in (
9
) we will incur from adding
S
or
S\cup\{v\}
to
I_{v}
. In light of this, for
\omega\in Y_{v}
, define:

\displaystyle W_{\omega}=\sum_{S\subseteq\omega}\beta_{S}f_{S}\qquad\qquad W^{+}_{\omega}=\sum_{S\subseteq\omega}f_{S}^{+}\beta_{S}

W_{\omega}
is the total weight incurred when we do not add
v
to
I
and
W_{\omega}^{+}
is the total weight incurred when we do add
v
to
I
.

Claim 3.
For
v\in V(H)
and
I\in\mathcal{I}(H)
, let
I_{v}=I\backslash\bigcup N_{1}(v)\cup\{v\}

and let
A_{v}
be the event
v\in I
conditional on
I_{v}
and let
A_{v}^{c}
be the complement of
A_{v}
. Then

\displaystyle\frac{\Pr(A_{v}^{c})}{\Pr(A_{v})}=\prod_{\omega\in Y_{v}}\left[1+\displaystyle{\frac{\sum_{S\subseteq\omega}(1-f_{S}^{+}/f_{S})\cdot\beta_{S}f_{S}}{W^{+}_{\omega}}}\right].

Proof.
Since
H
is linear, the sets in
Y_{v}
are disjoint, and since
H
is locally sparse, each edge of
H
contains at most one vertex from at most one set in
Y_{v}
.
Therefore, conditioning on
I_{v}
, we may add any subset of vertices in
\bigcup_{\omega\in Y_{v}}\omega
to
I
and we may add
v
to
I
iff none of the
\omega\in Y_{v}
with
|\omega|=r
is completly added to
I
. Therefore by summing over all subsets of
\bigcup_{\omega\in Y_{v}}
to add to
I
and by definition (
8
),

\displaystyle\Pr(A_{v})

\displaystyle=\frac{\prod_{\omega\in Y_{v}}\sum_{S\subseteq\omega,|S|<|r|}\beta_{S}f_{S}^{+}}{\prod_{\omega\in Y_{v}}\sum_{S\subseteq\omega,|S|<r}\beta_{S}f_{S}^{+}+\sum_{S\subseteq\omega}\beta_{S}f_{S}}

(12)

\displaystyle=\frac{\prod_{\omega\in Y_{v}}\sum_{S\subseteq\omega}\beta_{S}f_{S}^{+}}{\prod_{\omega\in Y_{v}}\sum_{S\subseteq\omega}\beta_{S}f_{S}^{+}+\sum_{S\subseteq\omega}\beta_{S}f_{S}}

(13)

\displaystyle=\frac{\prod_{\omega\in Y_{v}}W^{+}_{\omega}}{\prod_{\omega\in Y_{v}}W^{+}_{\omega}\;\;+\;\;\prod_{\omega\in Y_{v}}W_{\omega}}.

(14)

The equality of the first and second line follows from the fact that
\beta_{r+1}=0
and so
f_{S}^{+}=0
when
|S|=r
. From the left side in (
3.3
), we obtain

\displaystyle\prod_{\omega\in Y_{v}}\left[1+\displaystyle{\frac{\sum_{S\subseteq\omega}(1-f_{S}^{+}/f_{S})\cdot\beta_{S}f_{S}}{W^{+}_{\omega}}}\right]=\prod_{\omega\in Y_{v}}\left[\displaystyle{\frac{W_{\omega}^{+}+\sum_{S\subseteq\omega}(\beta_{S}f_{S}-\beta_{S}f_{S}^{+})}{W^{+}_{\omega}}}\right]=\prod_{\omega\in Y_{v}}\frac{\displaystyle{W_{\omega}}}{{W_{\omega}^{+}}}.

By (
12
), this is precisely
\Pr(A_{v}^{c})/\Pr(A_{v})
, as required.
{\scriptstyle\blacksquare}

Claim 4.
For
v\in V(H)
and
\omega\in Y_{v}
,

\frac{W_{\omega}}{W^{+}_{\omega}}\leq 1+\frac{1}{2^{r}-1}+o_{d}(1).

Proof.
Recall from (
7
) that
\beta_{r+1}=0
and
\beta_{i}\rightarrow 1
as
d\rightarrow\infty
for
0\leq i\leq r
. Therefore if
|S|<r
,
f_{S}\rightarrow 1=f_{S}^{+}
as
d\rightarrow\infty
, and if
|S|=r
,
f^{+}_{S}=0
and
f_{S}\rightarrow 1
as
d\rightarrow\infty
. So, if
|\omega|<r
, then

W_{\omega}/W_{\omega}^{+}\rightarrow 1
as
d\rightarrow\infty
. On the other hand, if
|\omega|=r
, then

\displaystyle\limsup_{d\rightarrow\infty}\frac{W_{\omega}}{W^{+}_{\omega}}

\displaystyle\leq\limsup_{d\rightarrow\infty}\frac{f_{\omega}\beta_{\omega}+\sum_{S\subsetneq\omega}f_{S}\beta_{S}}{\sum_{S\subsetneq\omega}f^{+}_{S}\beta_{S}}

\displaystyle=\limsup_{d\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\beta_{\omega}+\sum_{S\subsetneq\omega}\beta_{S}}{\sum_{S\subsetneq\omega}\beta_{S}}

\displaystyle=1+\limsup_{d\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{\sum_{S\subsetneq\omega}\beta_{S}/\beta_{\omega}}\;\;\leq\;\;1+\frac{1}{2^{r}-1}.

Here we used the fact that
\beta_{\omega}\leq\beta_{S}
for
S\subseteq\omega
.
{\scriptstyle\blacksquare}

Claim 5.
For
v\in V(H)
and
I\in\mathcal{I}(H)
,

\mathrm{E}(X_{v}|I_{v})\geq\Pr(A_{v})\cdot d^{\frac{1}{r}}+(1-o_{d}(1))\frac{(2^{r}-1)}{r2^{r}}\cdot\Pr(A_{v}^{c})\log\frac{\Pr(A_{v}^{c})}{\Pr(A_{v})}.

(15)

In particular, for all
v\in V(H)
,

\mathrm{E}(X_{v})\geq(1-o_{d}(1))\frac{2^{r}-1}{r^{2}2^{r}}\cdot\log d.

(16)

Proof.
Since
-\log(\beta_{j+1}/\beta_{j})\leq-\log\beta_{j+1}
and
1-x\leq-\log x
for
x>0
, for
0<|S|<r
,

\displaystyle 1-\frac{f_{S}^{+}}{f_{S}}=1-\prod_{j=0}^{|S|}\Bigl(\frac{\beta_{j+1}}{\beta_{j}}\Bigr)^{{|S|\choose j}}\leq\sum_{j=0}^{|S|}{|S|\choose j}(r-j)\delta^{r-j}\leq r\delta^{r-|S|}\sum_{j=0}^{|S|}{|S|\choose j}\delta^{|S|-j}

By the binomial theorem, this is exactly

\displaystyle r(1+\delta)^{|S|}\delta^{r-|S|}\leq r(1+\delta)^{r}\cdot\delta^{r-|S|}.

For
|S|=r
, we have
1-f_{S}^{+}/f_{S}\leq 1\leq r(1+\delta)^{r}\delta^{r-|S|}
, and for
|S|=0
,
1-f_{S}^{+}/f_{S}=0
. Using
\log(1+t)\leq t
for
t>-1
, Claim 3 gives

\displaystyle\log\frac{\Pr(A_{v}^{c})}{\Pr(A_{v})}\leq r(1+\delta)^{r}\cdot\sum_{\omega\in Y_{v}}\frac{1}{W_{\omega}^{+}}\sum_{\emptyset\subsetneq S\subseteq\omega}\delta^{r-|S|}\beta_{S}f_{S}.

(17)

By definition (
9
) of
X_{v}

\displaystyle\mathrm{E}(X_{v}|I_{v})

\displaystyle=\Pr(A_{v})\cdot d^{\frac{1}{r}}+\Pr(A_{v}^{c})\cdot\sum_{\omega\in Y_{v}}\sum_{0\subseteq S\subseteq\omega}P(I\cap\omega=S)\sum_{0\subsetneq T\subseteq S}\delta^{r-|T|}

\displaystyle=\Pr(A_{v})\cdot d^{\frac{1}{r}}+\Pr(A_{v}^{c})\cdot\sum_{\omega\in Y_{v}}\frac{1}{W_{\omega}}\sum_{0\subsetneq S\subseteq\omega}\beta_{S}f_{S}\sum_{0\subsetneq T\subseteq S}\delta^{r-|T|}

We now apply (
17
) and Claim 4 to this expression to obtain

\displaystyle\mathrm{E}(X_{v}|I_{v})

\displaystyle\geq\Pr(A_{v})\cdot d^{\frac{1}{r}}+(1-o_{d}(1))\cdot\frac{\Pr(A_{v}^{c})}{1+1/(2^{r}-1)}\cdot\sum_{\omega\in Y_{v}}\frac{1}{W_{\omega}^{+}}\sum_{0\subsetneq S\subseteq\omega}\beta_{S}f_{S}\delta^{r-|S|}

\displaystyle\geq\Pr(A_{v})\cdot d^{\frac{1}{r}}+(1-o_{d}(1))\cdot\frac{\Pr(A_{v}^{c})}{(1+1/(2^{r}-1))r(1+\delta)^{r}}\cdot\log\frac{\Pr(A_{v}^{c})}{\Pr(A_{v})}

\displaystyle=\Pr(A_{v})\cdot d^{\frac{1}{r}}+(1-o_{d}(1))\cdot\frac{2^{r}-1}{r2^{r}(1+\delta)^{r}}\cdot\Pr(A_{v}^{c})\log\frac{\Pr(A_{v}^{c})}{\Pr(A_{v})}.

The fact that
(1+\delta)^{r}=1+o_{d}(1)
finishes the proof of (
15
). To obtain (
16
), note that the lower bound in (
15
)
is minimized as a function of
\Pr(A_{v})
when

\Pr(A_{v})=d^{-\frac{1}{r}+o_{d}(1)}

in which case the minimum for each
v\in V
is at least

(1-o_{d}(1))\frac{2^{r}-1}{r^{2}2^{r}}\cdot\log d.

We conclude
\mathrm{E}(X_{v}|I_{v})
is at least this value for all
v\in V(H)
, which proves (
16
).
{\scriptstyle\blacksquare}

3.4
Proof of Theorem
1

To prove Theorem
1
, it is enough to show

\alpha(H)\geq(1-o_{d}(1))\cdot\frac{(r-1)(2^{r}-1)}{r^{2}2^{r}}\cdot\delta n

due to the definition of
\delta
. The upper bound in Claim 2 and the lower bound (
15
) in Claim 5 give

(1-o_{d}(1))\cdot\frac{2^{r}-1}{r^{2}2^{r}}\cdot n\log d\;\;\leq\;\;\mathrm{E}(X)\;\;\leq\;\;(1+o_{d}(1))\frac{\log d}{\delta(r-1)}\cdot\alpha(H).

Rearranging terms, we obtain

\displaystyle\alpha(H)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle(1-o_{d}(1))\cdot\frac{(r-1)(2^{r}-1)}{r^{2}2^{r}}\cdot\delta n,

as required. ∎

4
Concluding remarks

\bullet
By a suitable change of constants, Theorem
1
can be applied to the case of
(r+1)
-uniform hypergraphs of
average
degree
d
, by simply deleting all high degree vertices.

\bullet
If
H
is a random
d
-regular
(r+1)
-uniform
n
-vertex hypergraph, where
r\geq 1
and
d\geq 1
, then it may be shown that with positive probability,
H
is locally sparse if
d
is not too large relative to
n
and, with high probability,

\alpha(H)=(1+o_{d}(1))(1+1/r)^{1/r}\cdot\Bigl(\frac{\log d}{d}\Bigr)^{\frac{1}{r}}\cdot n.

(18)

It is a major open question as to whether this is tight in the case of graphs, namely, does
every locally sparse (i.e. triangle-free)
n
-vertex graph of maximum degree
d
have an independent set of size at least
(2+o_{d}(1))n(\log d)/d
as
d\rightarrow\infty
? Shearer
[
34
]
proved
that every triangle-free
n
-vertex graph
G
of maximum degree
d
has
\alpha(G)\geq n(d\log d-d+1)/(d-1)^{2}
for
d\geq 2
, which is a factor two
away from (
18
). We make the following more modest conjecture:

Conjecture A
.

For
r\geq 2
, there exists a constant
a_{r}>0
such that
a_{r}=1-o_{r}(1)
as
r\rightarrow\infty
and every

(r+1)
-uniform
n
-vertex linear hypergraph
H
with maximum degree
d\geq 1
has

\alpha(H)\geq(a_{r}-o_{d}(1))\cdot\Bigl(\frac{\log d}{d}\Bigr)^{\frac{1}{r}}\cdot n\quad\quad\mbox{ as }d\rightarrow\infty.

(19)

It appears to be difficult to determine whether this is true with
a_{r}=(1+1/r)^{1/r}
for any
r\geq 1
, or whether one may take
a_{r}>1
for any
r\geq 1
.
For
d
-regular hypergraphs of “large girth”, one obtains
a_{r}=r^{-1/r}
, as shown in
[
27
]
, which shows Conjecture
18
is true for this class of hypergraphs.

\bullet
The following strengthening of Conjecture
A
was proposed for linear triple systems (see Conjecture 1.2 in
[
16
]
and see
[
24
]
):

Conjecture B
.

If
H
is an
n
-vertex linear triple system, then

\alpha(H)\geq(1-o_{n}(1))\cdot\sqrt{3n\log n}\quad\mbox{ as }n\rightarrow\infty.

(20)

In particular, this states that every Steiner triple system has independence number asymptotically at least as large as the expected independence number of random triple system of the same density on the same set of vertices – see the third concluding remark above. Note that any linear
n
-vertex triple system has maximum degree at most
d=(n-1)/2
and, as in (
18
), a random
d
-regular triple system has expected independence number
(1+o_{n}(1))\sqrt{3n\log n}
as
n\rightarrow\infty
.

\bullet
As remarked in Section
2
, if
I
is a uniformly selected independent set in a locally sparse or linear
(r+1)
-uniform
n
-vertex
hypergraph
H
of maximum degree
d
, then we lack a good upper bound for
\mathrm{E}(|\partial_{r}I|)
. We make the following conjecture, which may be
independently interesting:

Conjecture C
.

For
r\geq 2
there exists
C_{r}>0
such that if
H
is any
n
-vertex linear
(r+1)
-uniform hypergraph of maximum degree
d\geq 1
and
I\in\mathcal{I}(H)
is sampled uniformly, then

\mathrm{E}(|\partial_{r}I|)\leq C_{r}\cdot d\alpha(H)^{r}n^{1-r}.

(21)

Note that if
I
is a uniformly sampled subset of
V(H)
of size
\alpha(H)
, then
\mathrm{E}(|\partial I|)\leq d\alpha(H)^{r}n^{1-r}
, so
the conjecture is proposing that the expected value of
|\partial_{r}I|
is not much larger than if
I
were any subset of vertices of
H

of size
\alpha(H)
chosen uniformly at random. In that case, following Section
2
, we would have for some
b_{r}>0
:

b_{r}\cdot n\log d\leq\mathrm{E}(X)\leq d^{1/r}\cdot\mathrm{E}(|I|)+C_{r}\cdot d\alpha(H)^{r}n^{1-r}

and therefore for some
B_{r}>0
where
B_{r}\approx(b_{r}/C_{r})^{1/r}
, we have

\alpha(H)\geq B_{r}\cdot\frac{n(\log d)^{1/r}}{d^{1/r}}

which gives a bound of the same order of magnitude as in Theorem
1
. If
C_{r}
is sub-exponential in
r
– and perhaps
C_{r}=1+o_{d}(1)
– then
this shows Conjecture
C
implies Conjecture
A
.
If
H
is an
n
-vertex Steiner triple system, then
d=(n-1)/2
and for every set
I\subseteq V(H)
,
\partial_{2}I={I\choose 2}
and so
|\partial_{2}I|\leq d\alpha(H)^{2}/n
, consistent with (
21
). This was used in
[
24
]
to give good lower bounds on the independence number
of Steiner systems.

\bullet
Duke, Lefmann and Rödl
[
14
]
showed that by randomly sampling vertices, one may easily obtain a lower bound on
\alpha(H)
of the same order of magnitude
as in Theorem
1
for all linear
n
-vertex
(r+1)
-uniform hypergraphs
H
of maximum degree
d\geq 1
when
r\geq 2
. Specifically,
if we sample vertices with probability
p
, then the expected number of
k
-cycles whose vertices are all sampled
is at most
p^{rk}((r+1)d)^{k-1}n
, whereas the expected number of edges of sampled vertices on a sampled vertex is at most
p^{r}d
.
Choosing
p=(2(r+1)d)^{-2/(3r-1)}
and using probabilistic methods, we obtain with positive probability an induced
subgraph
H^{\prime}
with
(1-o_{d}(1))pn
vertices and in which all vertices have degree at most roughly
D=(1+o_{d}(1))p^{r}d
. Applying Theorem
1

to
H^{\prime}
, we obtain

\alpha(H)\geq\alpha(H^{\prime})\geq c_{r}\cdot\Bigl(\frac{\log D}{D}\Bigr)^{\frac{1}{r}}\cdot(1-o_{d}(1))pn\geq(1-o_{d}(1))\cdot\Bigl(1-\frac{2r}{3r-1}\Bigr)^{\frac{1}{r}}\cdot c_{r}\cdot\Bigl(\frac{\log d}{d}\Bigr)^{\frac{1}{r}}\cdot n.

In particular, asymptotically as
d,r\rightarrow\infty
, this gives the same lower bound as in Theorem
1
for linear hypergraphs.

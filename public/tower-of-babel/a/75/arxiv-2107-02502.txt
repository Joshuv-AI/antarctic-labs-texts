1
Introduction and setting of the problem

We are here concerned with an Ornstein–Uhlenbeck process in
\mathbb{R}^{d}=H,\;d\in\mathbb{N}
,

X(t,x)=e^{tA}x+\int_{0}^{t}e^{(t-s)A}\sqrt{C}\;dW(s),\hskip 10.00002ptx\in H,\;t\geq 0,

(1)

where
A
and
C
are
d\times d
matrices,
C
being symmetric and semi-definite positive. Moreover
W(t),\;t\geq 0
, represents an
H
–valued standard Wiener process defined on a probability space
(\Omega,\mathscr{F},\mathbb{P})
. We denote by

W_{A}
the
stochastic convolution

W_{A}(t):=\int_{0}^{t}e^{(t-s)A}\sqrt{C}\;dW(s),\hskip 10.00002ptt\geq 0.

(2)

Our basic assumption is the following.

Hypothesis 1
.

The matrix
Q_{t}:=\int_{0}^{t}e^{sA}Ce^{sA^{*}}ds
is non singular for all
t>0.

Remark 1
.

Hypothesis 1 arises in controllability problems
for the deterministic system
D_{t}\xi=A\xi+\sqrt{C}\,u
, where

u
is a control. See e.g.
[
Za92
]
.

As well known, the transition semigroup
R_{t},\,t\geq 0,
corresponding to the process
X(t,x)
is given by

R_{t}\varphi(x)=\int_{H}\varphi(y)N_{e^{tA}x,Q_{t}}(dy),\hskip 10.00002ptt\geq 0,\;x\in H,\;\varphi\in B_{b}(H),

(3)

where

N_{e^{tA}x,Q_{t}}
is the gaussian probability measure on
H
of mean
e^{tA}x
and covariance
Q_{t}
and
B_{b}(H)
denotes the space of all mappings
H\to H
which are bounded and Borel.
By Hypothesis 1 the matrix
\Lambda_{t}:=Q_{t}^{-1/2}e^{tA}
is non singular for all
t>0
; consequently, by the Cameron–Martin Theorem it follows that

N_{e^{tA}x,Q_{t}}<<N_{Q_{t}}
and

\frac{dN_{e^{tA}x,Q_{t}}}{dN_{Q_{t}}}(y)=e^{-\frac{1}{2}|\Lambda_{t}x|_{H}^{2}+\langle\Lambda_{t}x,Q_{t}^{-1/2}y\rangle_{H}},\hskip 10.00002ptt>0,\,y\in H.

(4)

Therefore a well known representation formula for
R_{t}
follows by (
3
),

R_{t}\varphi(x)=\int_{H}\varphi(y)e^{-\frac{1}{2}|\Lambda_{t}x|_{H}^{2}+\langle\Lambda_{t}x,Q_{t}^{-1/2}y\rangle_{H}}N_{Q_{t}}(dy),\hskip 10.00002ptt\geq 0,\;x\in H,\;\varphi\in B_{b}(H).

(5)

By (
5
) we can deduce that
R_{t}\varphi
is differentiable infinitely many times, in particular it is strongly Feller.

The goal of this paper is to generalise the above regularity results
to the stopped semigroup

R^{\mathscr{O}_{r}}_{T},\;T\geq 0,
defined by

\displaystyle R^{\mathscr{O}_{r}}_{T}\varphi(x)=\mathbb{E}\left[\varphi(X(T,x))1\!\!\!\;\mathrm{l}_{T\leq\tau_{x}}\right],\hskip 10.00002ptT\geq 0,\;\varphi\in B_{b}(\overline{\mathscr{O}_{r}}),

(6)

where
\mathscr{O}_{r}
is an open convex bounded subset of
H

and
\tau_{x}
is the exit time from
\overline{\mathscr{O}_{r}}
.

More precisely, we shall assume

Hypothesis 2
.

(i)
g:H\to\mathbb{R}
is a convex function of class
C^{1}
such that
g(0)=0
,
g(x)>0
and
g^{\prime}(x)\neq 0
for all
x\neq 0
. For any
r>0
we set

\mathscr{O}_{r}=\{g<r\}
,
\overline{\mathscr{O}_{r}}=\{g\leq r\}
and
\partial\mathscr{O}_{r}=\{g^{-1}(r)\}
. Moreover,
\mathscr{O}_{r}
is bounded.

(ii) There exist
a,\,b>0
such that
|g(x)|+|g^{\prime}(x)|_{H}\leq a+e^{b|x|_{H}}
for all
x\in H
.

The semigroup
R^{\mathscr{O}_{r}}_{T},\;T\geq 0,
is related, as well known, to the Dirichlet problem in
\overline{\mathscr{O}_{r}}
for the Kolmogorov operator,

\mathscr{K}\varphi:=\frac{1}{2}\,\mbox{\rm Tr}\,[CD^{2}\varphi]+\langle Ax,D\varphi\rangle.

(7)

This problem is elliptic when the matrix
C
is non singular, otherwise is hypoelliptic. In the last case the existence of the gradient of
R^{\mathscr{O}_{r}}_{T}\varphi,\;T\geq 0,
when
\varphi
is namely bounded and Borel, is more challenging.

Here is a simple example where Hypothesis
1
is fulfilled.

Example 2
.

Let
d=2
and

A=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&0\\
1&0\end{array}\right),\;C=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&0\\
0&0\end{array}\right).

Then we have

Q_{t}=\int_{0}^{t}e^{sA}Ce^{sA^{*}}ds=\int_{0}^{t}\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&s\\
s&s^{2}\end{array}\right)ds=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}t&t^{2}/2\\
t^{2}/2&t^{3}/3\end{array}\right).

Therefore det
Q_{t}>0
for any
t>0
. If
f\in B_{b}(H)
and
t>0
, we conclude that
R_{t}f
is of class
C^{\infty}
. Note that
u(t,\xi)=R_{t}f(\xi),
is the solution of the well known Kolmogorov equation

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle D_{t}u(t,\xi_{1},\xi_{2})=\tfrac{1}{2}\,D^{2}_{\xi_{1}}u(t,\xi_{1},\xi_{2})+\xi_{1}D_{\xi_{2}}u(t,\xi_{1},\xi_{2})\\
\\
u(0,\xi)=f(\xi),\quad\xi=(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2},\end{array}\right.

(8)

which is hypoelliptic.

Let us explain our result. We start from an obvious consequence of (
6
),

R^{\mathscr{O}_{r}}_{T}\varphi(x)=\int_{\{g(e^{sA}x+h(s))\leq r,\,\forall\;s\in[0,T]\}}\varphi(h(T)+e^{TA}x)N_{\mathbb{Q}_{T}}(dh),\hskip 10.00002pt\varphi\in B_{b}(\overline{\mathscr{O}_{r}}),

(9)

where
N_{\mathbb{Q}_{T}}
is the law of
W_{A}(\cdot)
in
X:=L^{2}(0,T;H)
or in
E:=C([0,T];H)
, see Lemma
3
below.

Let
x\in H
; then we cannot eliminate
x
in identity (
9
) making the translation
h\to h-e^{\cdot A}x

and using the Cameron–Martin formula
because the measures
N_{e^{\cdot A}x,\mathbb{Q}}
and
N_{\mathbb{Q}}

are singular.
For this reason we look for another translation
h\to h-a(x,\cdot)
such that
a(x,\cdot)
belongs to
\mathbb{Q}_{T}(X)
for all
x\in H
(and a–fortiori to

\mathbb{Q}^{1/2}_{T}(X)
, the Cameron–Martin space of
N_{\mathbb{Q}_{T}}
) and
such that
:

a(x,T)=e^{TA}x,\hskip 10.00002pt\forall\,x\in H

(10)

(see Proposition
6
below).
Then the measures
N_{a(x,\cdot),\mathbb{Q}_{T}}
and
N_{\mathbb{Q}_{T}}
are equivalent, so that by the Cameron–Martin Theorem we have

\frac{dN_{a(x,\cdot),\mathbb{Q}_{T}}}{dN_{\mathbb{Q}_{T}}}(h)=\exp\left\{-\tfrac{1}{2}|\mathbb{Q}_{T}^{-1/2}a(x,\cdot)|_{X}^{2}+W_{\mathbb{Q}_{T}^{-1/2}a(x,\cdot)}(h)\right\},\hskip 10.00002ptx\in H,\;h\in X,

where
\mathbb{Q}_{T}^{-1/2}
is the pseudo inverse of
\mathbb{Q}_{T}^{1/2}
, see e.g.
[
DaZa14
, Theorem 2.23]
.
Now we take advantage of the special form of
a(x,\cdot)
for simplifying identity (
9
). We write

\displaystyle|\mathbb{Q}_{T}^{-1/2}a(x,\cdot)|_{X}^{2}=\langle\mathbb{Q}_{T}^{-1}a(x,\cdot),a(x,\cdot)\rangle_{X}=:F(x),

(11)

and,

W_{\mathbb{Q}_{T}^{-1/2}a(x,\cdot)}(h)=\langle\mathbb{Q}_{T}^{-1/2}a(x,\cdot),\mathbb{Q}_{T}^{-1/2}h\rangle_{X}=\langle\mathbb{Q}_{T}^{-1}a(x,\cdot),h\rangle_{X}=:G(x,h).

(12)

Note that both
F
and
G
are regular.
Now (
1
) becomes

\frac{dN_{a(x,\cdot),\mathbb{Q}_{T}}}{dN_{\mathbb{Q}_{T}}}(h)=\exp\left\{-\tfrac{1}{2}\,F(x)+G(x,h)\right\},\hskip 10.00002ptx\in H,\;h\in X,

(13)

and (
9
)

R^{\mathscr{O}_{r}}_{T}\varphi(x)=\int_{\{\Gamma(h+d(x,\cdot)\leq r\}}\varphi(h(T))\exp\left\{-\tfrac{1}{2}\,F(x)+G(x,h)\right\}N_{\mathbb{Q}_{T}}(dh),\hskip 10.00002pt\varphi\in B(\overline{\mathscr{O}_{r}}),

(14)

where

\Gamma(h+d(x,\cdot))=\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\,g(h(t)+d(x,t)),\hskip 10.00002ptk\in E,\hskip 10.00002ptd(x,t)=e^{tA}x-a(x,t),\hskip 10.00002ptt\in[0,T].

(15)

In the integral (
14
) the variable
x
does not appear under the argument of
\varphi
. Since the mapping
x\to\Gamma(h+d(x,\cdot))
is continuous this fact implies that the semigroup
R^{\mathscr{O}_{r}}_{T},\,T>0
, is
strong Feller
that is
\varphi\in B_{b}(H)\Rightarrow R^{\mathscr{O}_{r}}_{T}\varphi\in C_{b}(H)
for all
T>0
, see Proposition
7
below.

More difficult is to show that
R^{\mathscr{O}_{r}}_{T}\varphi
is differentiable for all
T>0
. As it is expected, this will produce a surface integral which, unfortunately, is not covered by the classical assumptions from Airault–Malliavin,
[
AiMa88
]
see also
[
DaLuTu14
]
. To overcome this difficulty we introduce in Section 3 an approximating semigroup
R^{\mathscr{O}_{r}}_{T,n},\,T>0
, for all decomposition
\{t_{j}=\tfrac{jT}{2^{n}},\,j=0,1,\ldots,2^{n}\}
of
[0,T]
, namely by approximating any function
h
from
E
by step functions.
Then we arrive to an identity for
R^{\mathscr{O}_{r}}_{T,n}\varphi(x)
(see (
43
)) that can be easily differentiated with respect to
x
, see identity (
46
).
It remains to let
n\to\infty
; this is not easy due to the factor

\langle\mathbb{Q}_{T,n}^{-1/2}(d_{x}(x,\cdot)y),\mathbb{Q}_{T,n}^{-1/2}\,h\rangle_{H^{2^{n}}}

which appears in the identity (
46
) because
d_{x}(x,\cdot)y
does not belong
to the Cameron–Martin space of
N_{\mathbb{Q}_{T}}
.
Some additional work is required, based on the Ehrhard inequality for the gaussian measure
N_{\mathbb{Q}_{T}}
and the selection principle of Helly. After some manipulations, we
arrive at the representation formula (
70
) which is the main result of the paper.
Our procedure was partially inspired by a paper by Linde
[
Li86
]
, which was dealing, however, with a completely different situation.

We believe that our method could be extended to more general Kolmogorov operators of the form

\mathscr{K}_{1}\varphi=\frac{1}{2}\,\mbox{\rm Tr}\,[CD^{2}\varphi]+\langle Ax+b(x),D\varphi\rangle,\hskip 10.00002pt\varphi\in C^{2}(\overline{\mathscr{O}_{r}}).

(16)

where
b:H\to H
is suitable nonlinear mapping. This will be the object of a future work.

T>0
we consider the law of
X(\cdot,x)
both in the Banach space

and in the Hilbert space

(in the second case it is concentrated on

which is a Borel subset of
X
).
We shall denote by
|\cdot|_{X}
(resp.
|\cdot|_{E}
) the norm of
X
(resp. of

). The scalar product from two elements
x,y\in H
(resp.
X
) will be denoted
either by
\langle x,y\rangle_{H}
(resp.
\langle x,y\rangle_{X}
) or by
x\cdot y
. If
\varphi\in C^{1}_{b}(E)
and
\eta\in E
we denote by
D\varphi(h)\cdot\eta
the derivative of
\varphi
at
h
in the direction
\eta
.

In what follows several integrals with respect to

will be considered, according to the convenience, both in
X
and in
E
.

2
Strong Feller property of
R^{\mathscr{O}_{r}}_{T},\,T>0

We first recall some properties of the gaussian measure
\mathbb{Q}_{T}
.
The following lemma is well known, see e.g
[
DaZa14
, Theorem 5.2]
.

Lemma 3
.

The law of
W_{A}(\cdot)
is gaussian
N_{\mathbb{Q}_{T}}
both in
E
and in
X
, where
\mathbb{Q}_{T}
is given by

(\mathbb{Q}_{T}\,h)(t)=\int_{0}^{T}K(t,s)h(s)\,ds,\hskip 10.00002ptt\in[0,T],\;h\in X

(17)

where

K(t,s)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{s}e^{(t-r)A}Ce^{(s-r)A^{*}}dr\quad\mbox{\rm if}\;0\leq s\leq t\leq T\\
\\
\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}e^{(t-r)A}Ce^{(s-r)A^{*}}dr\quad\mbox{\rm if}\;0\leq t\leq s\leq T.\end{array}\right.

(18)

We note that there exists an orthonormal basis
(e_{j})
on
X
and a sequence
(\lambda_{j})
of nonnegative numbers such that

\mathbb{Q}_{T}e_{j}=\lambda_{j}e_{j},\hskip 10.00002pt\,j\in\mathbb{N},

and an integer
k_{0}\geq 0
such that

\lambda_{1}=\lambda_{2}=\cdots=\lambda_{k_{0}}=0,\hskip 10.00002pt\lambda_{j}>0,\hskip 10.00002pt\forall\,j>k_{0}.

If
k_{0}=0
then
\mathbb{Q}_{T}
is non degenerate.

We shall denote by
\mathbb{L}_{T}
the linear operator from
X
into itself defined by

\mathbb{L}_{T}h(t)=\int_{0}^{t}e^{(t-s)A}\sqrt{C}\,h(s)ds,\hskip 10.00002pth\in X,\;t\in[0,T].

(19)

Its adjoint
\mathbb{L}_{T}^{*}
is given by

\mathbb{L}_{T}^{*}g(t)=\int_{t}^{T}\sqrt{C}e^{(s-t)A^{*}}g(s)ds,\hskip 10.00002ptg\in X,\;t\in[0,T].

(20)

It is easily checked that

\mathbb{Q}_{T}=\mathbb{L}_{T}\,\mathbb{L}_{T}^{*}
. Moreover,
by
[
DaZa14
, Corollary B5]
, the Cameron–Martin space of the Gaussian measure
N_{\mathbb{Q}_{T}}

is given by

\mathbb{Q}_{T}^{1/2}(X)=\mathbb{L}_{T}(X),

(21)

both in
E
and in
X
.

Remark 4
.

If det
C=0
one checks easily that the gaussian measure
\mathbb{Q}_{T}
is degenerate and

\mbox{\rm Ker}\,Q=\{h\in X:\,\mathbb{L}_{T}^{*}h=0\}.

For instance, coming back to Example
2
we see that in that case

\mbox{\rm Ker}\;Q=\{h=(h_{1},\,h_{2})\in E:\,h^{\prime}_{1}(t)=h_{2}(t)\}.

We shall denote
in what follows by
\mathbb{Q}_{T}^{-1}
(resp.
\mathbb{Q}_{T}^{-1/2}
) the pseudo–inverse of
\mathbb{Q}_{T}
(resp. the pseudo–inverse of
\mathbb{Q}_{T}^{1/2}
)
(1)
(1)

(1)

Let
S:X\to Y
be a linear, bounded and compact operator; the pseudo–inverse
S^{-1}
of
S
is defined as follows. For any
y\in S(X)
we denote by
S^{-1}y
the element of minimal norm from the convex set
\{x\in X:\,S(x)=y\}
.
.
Clearly, the domain of
\mathbb{Q}_{T}^{-1}
is equal to
\mathbb{Q}_{T}(X)
and

h\in\mathbb{Q}_{T}^{-1}(X)
if and only if the following series is convergent in
X

\mathbb{Q}_{T}^{-1}h=\sum_{j=k_{0}+1}^{\infty}\lambda^{-1}_{j}\langle h,e_{j}\rangle_{X}\,e_{j}.

Similar assertion holds for
\mathbb{Q}_{T}^{-1/2}
.

Now to introduce the required translation;
first we need a lemma.

Lemma 5
.

Define
U:=\int_{0}^{T}re^{rA}Ce^{rA^{*}}dr.

Then
\det U>0
.

Proof.

We have in fact

U\geq\frac{T}{2}\int_{T/2}^{T}e^{rA}Ce^{rA^{*}}dr=\frac{T}{2}\int_{0}^{T/2}e^{(T/2+z)A}Ce^{(T/2+z)A^{*}}dz=\frac{T}{2}\,e^{AT/2}\,Q_{T/2}\,e^{A^{*}T/2}.

It follows that
\det U\geq\frac{T}{2}\,e^{T\,{\rm Tr}\,A}\,\det Q_{T/2}>0,
as claimed.
∎

Proposition 6
.

For all
x\in H
set

u(x,t):=e^{(T-t)A^{*}}U^{-1}e^{TA}x,\hskip 10.00002ptt\in[0,T]

(22)

and define
a(x,\cdot):=\mathbb{Q}_{T}u(x,\cdot)
.
Then it results
a(x,T)=e^{TA}x.

Moreover, there is
c_{T},\,c_{1,T}>0
such that

|u(t,x)|_{H}\leq c_{T}|x|_{H},\hskip 10.00002pt\forall\,t\in[0,T],\,x\in H,

(23)

and

|a(x,t)|_{H}\leq c_{1,T}|x|_{H},\hskip 10.00002pt\forall\,t\in[0,T],\,x\in H.

(24)

Proof.

Write

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle a(x,T)=\int_{0}^{T}K(T,s)u(x,s)\,ds=\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\left(\int_{0}^{s}e^{(T-r)A}Ce^{(s-r)A^{*}}dr\right)e^{(T-s)A^{*}}U^{-1}e^{TA}xds\\
\\
=\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\left(\int_{0}^{s}e^{(T-r)A}Ce^{(T-r)A^{*}}dr\right)U^{-1}e^{TA}xds=\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}(T-r)e^{(T-r)A}Ce^{(T-r)A^{*}}dr\,U^{-1}e^{TA}x=e^{TA}x,\end{array}

as required.
Finally,

|u(x,t)|_{H}\leq\sup_{s\in[0,T]}\,\|e^{sA}\|^{2}_{\mathscr{L}(H)}\|U^{-1}\|_{\mathscr{L}(H)}\,|x|_{H},\hskip 10.00002ptt\in[0,T],

so that
(
23
) and (
24
) follow easily.
∎

Now we prove the first new result of the paper.

Proposition 7
.

Under Hypotheses
1
and
2
the semigroup
R^{\mathscr{O}_{r}}_{T},\;T>0
,
is strong Feller.

Proof.

Let
\varphi\in B(\overline{\mathscr{O}_{r}})
and
x_{0},x\in\overline{\mathscr{O}_{r}}
. Then by (
14
) we find

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle|R^{\mathscr{O}}_{T}\varphi(x)-R^{\mathscr{O}}_{T}\varphi(x_{0})|\leq\displaystyle\|\varphi\|_{\infty}\int_{X}\,\left|\exp\left\{-\tfrac{1}{2}\,F(x)+G(x,h)\right\}-\exp\left\{-\tfrac{1}{2}\,F(x_{0})+G(x_{0},h)\right\}\right|N_{\mathbb{Q}_{T}}(dh)\\
\\
\displaystyle+\|\varphi\|_{\infty}\int_{X}{\mathds{1}}_{{\{\Gamma(h+d(x_{0},\cdot)\leq r\}\setminus\{\Gamma(h+d(x,\cdot)\leq r\}}}\,\exp\left\{-\tfrac{1}{2}\,F(x_{0})+G(x_{0},h)\right\}N_{\mathbb{Q}_{T}}(dh)=:A_{1}+A_{2}.\end{array}

Taking into account (
23
) we have

F(x)=\langle\mathbb{Q}_{T}^{-1}a(x,\cdot),a(x,\cdot)\rangle_{X}=\langle u(x,\cdot),\mathbb{Q}_{T}u(x,\cdot)\rangle_{H}\leq\|\mathbb{Q}_{T}\|_{\mathscr{L}(H)}c^{2}_{T}\,|x|^{2}_{H}

and

|G(x,h)|=|\langle\mathbb{Q}_{T}^{-1}a(x,\cdot),h\rangle_{X}|\leq c_{T}T|x|_{H}|h|_{X}

Therefore

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle\exp\left\{-\tfrac{1}{2}\,F(x)+G(x,h)\right\}-\exp\left\{-\tfrac{1}{2}\,F(x_{0})+G(x_{0},h)\right\}\\
\\
\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\exp\left\{-\tfrac{1}{2}\,F((1-\alpha)x_{0}+\alpha x)+G((1-\alpha)x_{0}+\alpha x,h))\right\}(x-x_{0})\,d\alpha\\
\\
\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{1}\exp\left\{G((1-\xi)x_{0}+\xi x,h))\right\}|x-x_{0}|\,d\alpha\leq\exp\{c_{T}T|x|_{H}|h|_{X}\}|x-x_{0}|.\end{array}

It follows that

A_{1}\leq\|\varphi\|_{\infty}\int_{X}\exp\{c_{T}T|x|_{H}|h|_{X}\}\,dN_{\mathbb{Q}_{T}}\,|x-x_{0}|.

Since the integral above is finite we have
\lim_{x\to x_{0}}A_{1}=0.

Concerning
A_{2}
we have
\lim_{x\to x_{0}}A_{2}=0

by the continuity of
d(x,\cdot)
and the dominated convergence theorem.
The proof is complete.
∎

Approximating semigroup

,

on

),

Lemma 8
.

(i) It results

|\Gamma_{n}(h+d(x,\cdot))-\Gamma_{n}(h_{1}+d(x,\cdot))|\leq a+be^{|h|_{E}+|h_{1}|_{E}},\hskip 10.00002pth_{1},\,h_{2}\in E.

Non-monotonic constitutive curves and shear banding in dry and wet granular flows
Christopher Ness, Suzanne M. Fielding

Abstract
We use particle simulations to map comprehensively the shear rheology of dry and wet granular matter, in both fixed pressure and fixed volume protocols. At fixed pressure we find non-monotonic constitutive curves that are shear thinning, whereas at fixed volume we find non-monotonic constitutive curves that are shear thickening. We show that the presence of one non-monotonicity does not imply the other. Instead, there exists a signature in the volume fraction measured under fixed pressure that, when present, ensures non-monotonic constitutive curves at fixed volume. In the context of dry granular flow we show that gradient and vorticity bands arise under fixed pressure and volume respectively, as implied by the constitutive curves. For wet systems our results are consistent with a recent experimental observation of shear thinning at fixed pressure. We furthermore predict discontinuous shear thickening in the absence of critical load friction.

In this Letter, we map comprehensively the rheology of dry and wet granular matter,
at both fixed
V
and fixed
\sigma_{yy}
.
Our simulation results reveal constitutive curves that are indeed non-monotonic and shear thinning (thickening) at fixed
\sigma_{yy}
(fixed
V

We demonstrate a mapping whereby constitutive curves measured at fixed

Performing this on our data,
we investigate whether non-monotonicity at fixed

We show that this is not so,
uncovering instead a signature in the fixed-

In the context of dry systems, we give the first simulation evidence for shear bands with layer-normals in the gradient direction at fixed

We furthermore demonstrate a non-monotonic

We show separately that discontinuous shear thickening (DST) can arise at fixed

Conclusion.—

We have shown that the constitutive curves of dry and wet granular matter sheared at both fixed
\Sigma_{yy}
and fixed
V
are non-monotonic, leading to gradient (vorticity) banding at fixed
\Sigma_{yy}
(fixed
V
).
Mapping between protocols we find
that non-monotonicity in
\mu(I)
(or
\mu(J)
) at fixed
\Sigma_{yy}
does not imply an S-shaped non-monotonicity in
\Sigma_{xy}(\dot{\gamma}_{d})
(or
\dot{\gamma}_{w}
) at fixed
V
,
or vice-versa.
Instead, a non-monotonicity in
\phi(I)
(or
\phi(J)
) associated with dilatancy under increasing
\dot{\gamma}
and
\Sigma_{yy}
maps to non-monotonic
\Sigma_{xy}
at fixed
V

On q q -series and the moment problem associated to local factors
Alain Connes, Caterina Consani, Henri Moscovici

Abstract
Résumé. We investigate the moment problem and Jacobi matrix associated– by the operator theoretic framework of the semilocal trace formula– to each finite set S S of places of ℚ {\mathbb{Q}} containing the archimedean place. The measure is given by the absolute value squared of the product over S S of local factors restricted to the critical line. We treat the case S = { p , ∞ } S=\{p,\infty\} , where a single prime p p is adjoined to the archimedean place. We find that all the key ingredients such as the moments, the orthogonal polynomials and the Jacobi matrices can be expressed as power series in terms of the parameter q := 1 / p q:=1/p . We show that the series which appear for the moments themselves are Lambert series. The study of the q q -series for the coefficients of the Jacobi matrix, and for the associated orthogonal polynomials reveals an intriguing integrality result : all those coefficients belong to the ring ℤ ⁡ [ 1 2 ] {\mathbb{Z}}[\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}] obtained by adjoining 1 / 2 1/\sqrt{2} to the ring of integers. The main result of this paper is the conceptual explanation of this integrality property using Catalan numbers.

2.1.
Spectral meaning of orthogonal polynomials

Let
d\mu
be a probability measure on
{\mathbb{R}}
. Orthogonal polynomials are defined by orthogonalization process from the powers
x^{n}
of the variable and they form an orthonormal basis of the Hilbert space
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}},d\mu)
. There is however another understanding of their role, showing that their zeros compute the spectrum of the compression of the operator of multiplication by
x
on the subspace of
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}},d\mu)
given by polynomials of degree
<n

We work in the general framework of orthogonal polynomials. We start from the formula (up to normalization) which gives the orthogonal polynomial

as a determinant (see
[
8
]
, (2.2.6) page 27). We let

be the moments of the measure

be the determinant of the Hankel matrix

with entries

for

that the orthogonal polynomial

is given by

where

is the Hankel matrix with entries

for
i,j\in\{0,\ldots,n\}
. More specifically, and with
e.g.

, one starts from (2.2.6) page 27, of
op.cit.

P_{4}(x)=(D_{4}D_{3})^{-1/2}\det\left(\begin{array}[]{ccccc}c(0)&c(1)&c(2)&c(3)&c(4)\\
c(1)&c(2)&c(3)&c(4)&c(5)\\
c(2)&c(3)&c(4)&c(5)&c(6)\\
c(3)&c(4)&c(5)&c(6)&c(7)\\
1&x&x^{2}&x^{3}&x^{4}\\
\end{array}\right)

One then reduces the last line to

by replacing the

by

to get

P_{4}(x)=(D_{4}D_{3})^{-1/2}\det\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}c(1)-c(0)x&c(2)-c(1)x&c(3)-c(2)x&c(4)-c(3)x\\

c(3)-c(2)x&c(4)-c(3)x&c(5)-c(4)x&c(6)-c(5)x\\

The next Proposition holds in the general case of orthogonal polynomials.

\propname

\the\smf@thm
.

The orthogonal polynomial

is, up to a multiplicative constant, the characteristic polynomial of the compression
\Pi_{n-1}X\ \Pi_{n-1}
of the operator
X
of multiplication by
x
on the subspace

of

.

Let

be the orthonormal polynomials.
By the result of A.C. Aitken (see
[
1
]
), the inverse of the Hankel matrix

is equal to
{\mathcal{B}}_{n}\,{\mathcal{B}}_{n}^{*}
where
{\mathcal{B}}_{n}
is the upper triangular matrix with matrix entries

and
{\mathcal{B}}_{n}^{*}
is its adjoint

{\mathcal{B}}=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccccc}b_{0,0}&b_{0,1}&b_{0,2}&b_{0,3}&b_{0,4}\\
0&b_{1,1}&b_{1,2}&b_{1,3}&b_{1,4}\\

In fact one has the identity

(2)

which gives the required result, since the

. ∎

2.2.
The even case

We assume that the measure

3.2.
Determinacy of moment problem

Passing to the general semilocal case, we let now
S
be a finite set of places with

and consider the measure
\ d\mu_{S}(s):=\Big|\prod_{v\in S}L_{p}(\frac{1}{2}-is)\Big|^{2}\,ds

\propname

\the\smf@thm
.

The moment problem for the sequence

\displaystyle\big\{c_{n}^{S}:=\int s^{n}d\mu_{S}(s)\big\}_{n\geq 0}

is determinate.

Démonstration.

By Carleman’s Criterion (see
[
7

), a Hamburger problem
with moments
\gamma_{n}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}}t^{n}d\mu(t)
is determinate if
\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 1}\gamma_{2n}^{-\frac{1}{2n}}=\infty
.
We shall actually show that

(13)

\displaystyle\exists\,M>0\quad\text{such that}\quad\gamma_{2n}\leq M^{n}(2n)!\,,\quad\forall n\in{\mathbb{N}},

which implies Carleman’s condition (see
[
7
, Corollary 4.10]
).

Since excluding the Archimedean place the product

\,\Big|\prod_{v\in S\setminus\{\infty\}}L_{v}(\frac{1}{2}-is)\Big|^{2}
is bounded, it suffices to
verify (
13
) for the sequence of Archimedean moments
\{c_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
.
Furthermore, the ratio

\displaystyle|\Gamma(\frac{1}{4}+is)|^{2}/|\Gamma(\frac{1}{2}+is)|^{2}
is
bounded too, since

|\Gamma(\frac{1}{4}+is)|/|\Gamma(\frac{1}{2}+is)|\sim|s|^{-\frac{1}{4}}\quad\text{as}\quad|s|\to\infty

(see https ://dlmf.nist.gov/5.11 [(iii) Ratios)]. Thus, it remains to check that

(14)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}s^{n}|\Gamma(\frac{1}{2}+i\frac{s}{2})|^{2}ds\leq M^{n}n!\,,\quad\forall n\in{\mathbb{N}}

for some
M>0
.
Using the formula
\displaystyle|\Gamma(\frac{1}{2}+it)|^{2}=\frac{\pi}{\cosh\pi t}
,   one has

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}s^{n}|\Gamma(\frac{1}{2}+i\frac{s}{2})|^{2}ds

\displaystyle=2^{n+1}\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{n}|\Gamma(\frac{1}{2}+it)|^{2}dt=2^{n+1}\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{n}\frac{2\pi}{e^{\pi t}+e^{-\pi t}}dt

\displaystyle\leq 2^{n+2}\pi\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{n}e^{-\pi t}dt=2^{n+2}\pi^{-n}n!,

which achieves the proof.

5.3.
Integrality and Catalan numbers

In fact the computations show that there is a refined form of Fact
4.1
, namely that it seems to hold for each

. In order to prove these facts one investigates the entries of the matrices
{\mathcal{A}}_{n}^{\pm}(q)
using Proposition
5.2
, and one finds that they belong to the ring
{\mathbb{Z}}^{\rm cyc}[\frac{1}{2}]
where
{\mathbb{Z}}^{\rm cyc}
is the ring of cyclotomic integers. In order to prove this, one needs the following integrality result :

Lemma 5.5
.

(i)
For each
k\leq a
the central binomial coefficient
\binom{2k}{k}
divides the product
\binom{2a}{a}\binom{a}{k}
.

(ii)
Let
p_{\ell}(m)
be given by (
49
), then for any
\ell,m
the following number is an integer

\binom{2\ell}{\ell}\,p_{\ell}(m)\in{\mathbb{Z}}

(iii)
For any
\ell,m
the following number is an integer

(51)

\sigma(\ell,m):=\binom{2\ell}{\ell}\binom{2m}{m}p_{\ell}(m)^{2}

Démonstration.

(i)
Let
k=a-u
. One has

\binom{2a}{a}/\binom{2k}{k}=\frac{(2a)!}{(2(a-u))!}((a-u)!)^{2}(a!)^{-2}

\frac{(2a)!}{(2(a-u))!}=\prod_{0\leq j\leq 2u-1}(2a-j),\ \ \frac{a!}{(a-u)!}=\prod_{0\leq j\leq u-1}(a-j)

Thus one gets

\frac{(2a)!}{(2(a-u))!}/\frac{a!}{(a-u)!}=2^{u}\prod_{0\leq j\leq u-1}(2a-2j-1)

so that

\binom{2a}{a}/\binom{2k}{k}=2^{u}\prod_{0\leq j\leq u-1}(2a-2j-1)/\prod_{0\leq j\leq u-1}(a-j)

Moreover

\binom{a}{k}=\binom{a}{u}=\left(\prod_{0\leq j\leq u-1}(a-j)\right)/u!

Thus one obtains the following equality

(52)

\binom{a}{k}\binom{2a}{a}/\binom{2k}{k}=\frac{2^{u}}{u!}\prod_{0\leq j\leq u-1}(2a-2j-1)=2^{2u}\binom{a-\frac{1}{2}}{u}=2^{2(a-k)}\binom{a-\frac{1}{2}}{a-k}

To obtain that this number is an integer one reduces to the computation of

2^{2k-1}\binom{\frac{1}{2}}{k}=(-4)^{k-1}(k!)^{-1}\prod_{0\leq j\leq k-2}\left(\frac{1}{2}+j\right)

These numbers are equal to
(-1)^{k-1}C_{k-1}
with the Catalan numbers

C_{n}:=\frac{\binom{2n}{n}}{n+1}

which are integers. The reduction to
a=\frac{1}{2}
is done as for the Pascal triangle using the relation

\binom{x}{k}=\binom{x-1}{k}+\binom{x-1}{k-1}

It is important to note, in order to perform the induction, that all the binomial coefficients
\binom{x}{k}
with
x
a half integer and
k<0
are equal to zero.

(ii)
One has

\binom{2\ell}{\ell}\,p_{\ell}(m)=\sum_{k=0}^{\ell}2^{3k}\binom{2\ell}{\ell}\,\binom{\ell}{k}\binom{2k}{k}^{-1}\binom{m}{k}

By
(i)
each term

is an integer thus since the other terms of the product are integers so are each term of the sum.

(iii)
One has by (
49
) that
p_{\ell}(m)=p_{m}(\ell)
, thus one gets, using
(ii)
,

\sigma(\ell,m)=\binom{2\ell}{\ell}\,p_{\ell}(m)\binom{2m}{m}\,p_{m}(\ell)\in{\mathbb{Z}}

which gives the required integrality. ∎

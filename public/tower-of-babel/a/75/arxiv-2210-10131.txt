Symmetric homology is representation homology
Yuri Berest, Ajay C. Ramadoss

Abstract
. Symmetric homology is a natural generalization of cyclic homology, in which symmetric groups play the role of cyclic groups. In the case of associative algebras, the symmetric homology theory was introduced by Z. Fiedorowicz [ 19 ] and was further developed in the work of S. Ault [ 1 , 2 ] . In this paper, we show that, for algebras defined over a field of characteristic 0 0 , the symmetric homology theory is naturally equivalent to the (one-dimensional) representation homology theory introduced by the authors (jointly with G. Khachatryan) in [ 7 ] . Using known results on representation homology, we compute symmetric homology explicitly for basic algebras, such as polynomial algebras and universal enveloping algebras of (DG) Lie algebras. As an application, we prove two conjectures of Ault and Fiedorowicz, including the main conjecture of [ 3 ] on topological interpretation of symmetric homology of polynomial algebras.

4

2.1.
Functor tensor products

We begin by recalling the classical construction of functor tensor products (see
[
25
, Appendix C.10]
). For a small category
\mathcal{C}
, we let
{\rm Mod}_{k}(\mathcal{C})
and
{\rm Mod}_{k}(\mathcal{C}^{\rm op})
denote the categories of all covariant and contravariant functors from
\mathcal{C}
to
{\rm Mod}_{k}
, respectively. It is well known that these are abelian categories with sufficiently many projective and injective objects. Moreover, there is a natural bi-additive functor

\mbox{--}\otimes_{\mathcal{C}}\mbox{--}\,:\,{\rm Mod}_{k}(\mathcal{C}^{\rm op})\times{\rm Mod}_{k}(\mathcal{C})\to{\rm Mod}_{k}

called the
functor tensor product
.
Explicitly, for
\mathcal{M}:\mathcal{C}\to\mathrm{Mod}_{k}
and
\mathcal{N}:\mathcal{C}^{\rm op}\to\mathrm{Mod}_{k}
, it is defined by

(2.1)

\mathcal{N}\otimes_{\mathcal{C}}\mathcal{M}\,:=\,\big[\Moplus_{c\in\mathcal{C}}\mathcal{N}(c)\otimes\mathcal{M}(c)\big]/R

where
R
is the
k
-submodule generated by elements of the form
\mathcal{N}(\varphi)(x)\otimes y-x\otimes\mathcal{M}(\varphi)(y)
for all
x\in\mathcal{N}(c^{\prime})
,
y\in\mathcal{M}(c)
and
\varphi\in{\rm Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(c,c^{\prime})
. We extend the bifunctor (
2.1
) to the categories of
chain complexes of
\mathcal{C}
-modules (which we denote by
{\rm Ch}({\rm Mod}_{k}(\mathcal{C}^{\rm op}))
and
{\rm Ch}({\rm Mod}_{k}(\mathcal{C}))
) and define

(2.2)

{\rm Tor}^{\mathcal{C}}_{\ast}(\mathcal{N},\mathcal{M})\,:=\,{\rm H}_{\ast}(\mathcal{N}\otimes^{\bm{L}}_{\mathcal{C}}\mathcal{M})

for
\mathcal{N}\in{\rm Ch}({\rm Mod}_{k}(\mathcal{C}^{\rm op}))
and
\mathcal{M}\in{\rm Ch}({\rm Mod}_{k}(\mathcal{C}))
. Note that
\mathcal{N}\otimes^{\bm{L}}_{\mathcal{C}}\mathcal{M}
is an object in the (unbounded) derived category
\mathscr{D}(k):=\mathscr{D}({\rm Mod}_{k})
of
k
-modules, and (
2.2
) is just the usual hyper-Tor functor on chain complexes. Next, we observe that there is a natural functor
transforming the
\mathcal{C}^{\rm op}
-diagrams in
\mathrm{sSet}
(simplicial presheaves on
\mathcal{C}
) to
chain complexes over
{\rm Mod}_{k}(\mathcal{C}^{\rm op})
:

(2.3)

\mathrm{sSet}^{\mathcal{C}^{\rm op}}\,\xrightarrow{\,k[\,\mbox{--}\,]\,}\,{\rm sMod}_{k}({\mathcal{C}}^{\rm op})\xrightarrow{N}\,{\rm Ch}({\rm Mod}_{k}(\mathcal{C}^{\rm op}))\,,

where
k[\,\mbox{--}\,]
is the (
\mathcal{C}
-objectwise extension of the) free module functor on
simplicial sets and
N
is the classical Dold-Kan normalization functor. Abusing notation, we will write (
2.3
) simply as
\,k[\,\mbox{--}\,]\,
.

Finally, we recall one well-known result on derived functor tensor products that we will use repeatedly in this paper. Let
p:\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{D}
be a map of small categories. The restriction functor
p^{*}:\mathrm{Mod}_{k}(\mathcal{D})\to\mathrm{Mod}_{k}(\mathcal{C})
associated to

is exact and has a left adjoint
\,p_{!}:\mathrm{Mod}_{k}(C)\to\mathrm{Mod}_{k}(\mathcal{D})\,
, which is a right exact functor on chain complexes. Replacing

with its
(abelian) left derived functor
\bm{L}p_{!}
, we get a natural adjunction between derived categories:

\bm{L}p_{!}\,:\,\mathscr{D}({\rm Mod}_{k}(\mathcal{C}))\rightleftarrows\mathscr{D}({\rm Mod}_{k}(\mathcal{D}))\,:\,p^{\ast}\ .

Lemma 2.1
.

For
\mathcal{N}\in{\rm Mod}_{k}(\mathcal{D}^{\rm op})
,
\mathcal{M}\in{\rm Mod}_{k}(\mathcal{C})
there is a natural isomorphism in
\mathscr{D}(k)
:

p^{\ast}\mathcal{N}\otimes^{\bm{L}}_{\mathcal{C}}\mathcal{M}\,\cong\,\mathcal{N}\otimes^{\bm{L}}_{\mathcal{D}}\bm{L}p_{!}\mathcal{M}\ .

The proof of Lemma
2.1
and some of its consequences can be found, for example, in
[
17
, Prop. 1.1]

2.2.

We review the definition of representation homology of (simplicial) groups and monoids, following the approach

Let

More generally, for any simplicial (or DG) cocommutative Hopf algebra

(2.5)

\underline{{\mathcal{A}}}:\mathfrak{G}^{\rm op}\to{\rm Ch}({\rm Mod}_{k})\,,\qquad\langle n\rangle\mapsto{\mathcal{A}}^{\otimes n}\ .

Dually (see, e.g.,

Definition 2.1
.

The

of a group

{\rm HR}_{\ast}(\Gamma,{\mathcal{H}})\,:=\,{\rm Tor}^{\mathfrak{G}}_{\ast}(\underline{k[\Gamma]},\,\underline{{\mathcal{H}}})\ .

In the special case when

Next, recall that there are classical adjoint functors (originally constructed in

Proposition 2.1

Let

Proof.

Let

m
-simplices is flat, since the module

\mathrm{HR}_{\ast}^{\rm Mon}(N,M_{n}(k))\,\cong\,\pi_{\ast}[k[R]\otimes_{\mathfrak{M}}\underline{\mathcal{O}}(M_{n})]\ .

Using the same argument as in

Next, we compare the representation homology of (simplicial) monoids to that of (simplicial) groups.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
k
be a field of characteristic

\mathrm{HR}_{\ast}(\Gamma,{\rm{GL}}_{n}(k))\,\cong\,\mathrm{HR}_{\ast}^{\rm Mon}(\Gamma,M_{n}(k))\ .

Proof.

We have

\mathrm{HR}_{\ast}^{\rm Mon}(\Gamma,M_{n}(k)):={\rm{Tor}}^{\mathfrak{M}}_{\ast}(k[\Gamma],\underline{\mathcal{O}}(M_{n}))={\rm{Tor}}^{\mathfrak{M}}_{\ast}(p^{\ast}\underline{k[\Gamma]},\underline{\mathcal{O}}(M_{n}))\cong{{\rm{Tor}}}^{\mathfrak{G}}_{\ast}(\underline{k[\Gamma]},\,p_{!}\underline{\mathcal{O}}(M_{n}))\,,

where the last isomorphism is due to Lemma

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
k
be a field of characteristic
0

\bm{L}p_{!}\underline{\mathcal{O}}(M_{n})\cong\underline{\mathcal{O}}({\rm{GL}}_{n})\ .

The first isomorphism above follows from the fact that

Since the algebra

Let

2.3.
Symmetric bar construction

We recall (see
[
25
, 6.1.4]
)
that the
symmetric category

\Delta S
is defined to be the extension of
\Delta
having the same objects as
\Delta
(and
\Delta C
), with morphisms satisfying the two properties:

(1)

For each
n\geqslant 0
,
{\rm Aut}_{\Delta S}([n])\cong\Sigma_{n+1}^{\rm op}\,
, where
\Sigma_{n+1}
is the
(n+1)
-th symmetric group.

(2)

Any morphism
f:[n]\to[m]
in
\Delta S
can be factored uniquely as the composite
f=g\circ\sigma
with
g\in{\rm Hom}_{\Delta}([n],[m])
and
\sigma\in{\rm Aut}_{\Delta S}([n])\cong\Sigma_{n+1}^{\rm op}
.

It is convenient use the following notation for the morphisms in
\Delta S
(see
[
1
, Sect. 1.1]
). Any
\,f:\,[n]\xrightarrow{\sigma}[n]\xrightarrow{g}[m]\,
in
\Delta S
can be written uniquely as the ‘tensor product’ of
m+1
noncommutative monomials
\,X_{0},\,X_{1},\,\ldots,X_{m}\,
in
n+1
formal variables
\{x_{0},x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\}
:

(2.12)

f=X_{0}\otimes X_{1}\otimes\ldots\otimes X_{m}

in such a way that each
X_{i}
is the product
\,x_{i_{1}}x_{i_{2}}\ldots x_{i_{r}}\,
of
r=|f^{-1}(i)|
variables whose indices
i_{k}
occur in the fibre
f^{-1}(i)
and
that are ordered in the same way as numbers in
\{\sigma(0),\ldots,\sigma(n)\}
, i.e.,
\sigma(i_{1})<\sigma(i_{2})<\ldots<\sigma(i_{r})
.

Using the above notation, the
symmetric bar construction
of an algebra
A\in\mathrm{Alg}_{k}
is defined as the functor
\,B_{\rm sym}A:\Delta S\to\mathrm{Mod}_{k}\,
that is given on objects by

(2.13)

B_{\rm sym}A([n])=A^{\otimes(n+1)}\ ,\quad\forall\,n\geq 0\,,

and on morphisms by the following ‘substitution formula’:

(2.14)

B_{\rm sym}A(f)(a_{0}\otimes\ldots\otimes a_{n})\,:=\,(a_{i_{1}}\ldots a_{i_{r}})\otimes\ldots\otimes(a_{k_{1}}\ldots a_{k_{s}})\ .

where
f\in{\rm{Hom}}_{\Delta S}([n],[m])
is represented by

\,f=(x_{i_{1}}\ldots x_{i_{r}})\otimes\cdots\otimes(x_{k_{1}}\ldots x_{k_{s}})\,

(
cf.

[
25
, 6.1.12]
).

The symmetric bar construction extends naturally to differential graded and simplicial
k
-algebras: for
A\in\mathrm{dgAlg}_{k}
, we have
B_{\rm sym}A\in{\rm dgMod}_{k}(\Delta S)\cong{\rm Ch}({\rm Mod}_{k}(\Delta S))
and for
A\in\mathrm{sAlg}_{k}
,
\,B_{\rm sym}A\in{\rm sMod}_{k}(\Delta S)\cong{\rm Ch}({\rm Mod}_{k}(\Delta S))
, where in the last case the identification is given via the Dold-Kan normalization functor
N
. In this way, using
the same formula (
1.3
), we can extend the definition of symmetric homology

\mathrm{HS}_{*}(A)
to both DG and simplicial algebras.

For cocommutative Hopf algebras, we can give the following interpretation
of the symmetric bar construction. By
[
9
, Lemma 4.2]
, there is a natural functor

(2.15)

\Psi_{\rm sym}:\ \Delta S\to\mathfrak{G}^{\rm op}

defined on objects by
\Psi_{\rm sym}([n])=\langle n+1\rangle
and on morphisms by the following formula: if
f\in{\rm{Hom}}_{\Delta S}([n],[m])
is represented by
f=(x_{i_{1}}\ldots x_{i_{r}})\otimes\cdots\otimes(x_{k_{1}}\ldots x_{k_{s}})\ ,
, then

(2.16)

\Psi_{\rm sym}(f):\langle m+1\rangle\to\langle n+1\rangle\,,\qquad X_{0}\mapsto x_{i_{1}}\ldots x_{i_{r}}\,,\ \ldots\ ,\,X_{m}\mapsto x_{k_{1}}\ldots x_{k_{s}}\ ,

where
\langle m+1\rangle=\mathbb{F}\langle X_{0},\ldots,X_{m}\rangle
and
\langle n+1\rangle=\mathbb{F}\langle x_{0},\ldots,x_{n}\rangle
. Now, every (simplicial/DG) cocommutative Hopf algebra
{\mathcal{A}}
defines DG right
\mathfrak{G}
-module
\underline{{\mathcal{A}}}
by formula (
2.5
). As in the case of group algebras (see
[
9
, Lemma 3.3]
), it is straightforward to verify that

(2.17)

B_{\rm sym}{\mathcal{A}}\,=\,\Psi_{\rm sym}^{\ast}(\underline{{\mathcal{A}}})\ ,

where
\Psi_{\rm sym}^{\ast}
denotes the restriction functor via (
2.15
).

{\tt diag}(CR)\to R_{\rm ab}
is the same as that induced on homologies by (

), which is
{\tt{SR}}_{\ast}(A)
. Since the map
{\tt diag}(CR)\to R_{\rm ab}
is a homomorphism of simplicial

-algebras,

is a morphism of graded commutative algebras. This finishes the proof of the proposition.
∎

Remark 3.1
.

The result of Proposition

holds true in greater generality: for any algebra

that is flat over a commutative ring

. This last condition is automatic when

is a field.

Remark 3.2
.

In general, for

, the derived character map

does not seem to factor through

From now on, we will assume that

Theorem

Theorem 3.1

For any simplicial algebra

The proof of Theorem

is based on the following key proposition.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
V
be a
k

denote the tensor algebra of

{\rm Tor}^{\Delta S}_{i}(k,B_{\rm sym}TV)\,\cong\,\begin{cases}S_{k}(V)&i=0\\
0&i\neq 0\end{cases}

We will give two different proofs of Proposition
3.2
, both relying on topological results. Our first proof is parallel to that of
[
11
, Proposition 2.1]
:
it is based on
[

, Theorem 3.2]
, its Corollary
[

, Corollary 3.2]
as well as

[

, Proposition 5.3]
and
[

First proof of Proposition
3.2
.

By
[
1
, Theorem 86]
, we know that

\,\mathrm{HS}_{0}(A)\cong A_{\rm ab}\,
for any
A\in\mathrm{Alg}_{k}
. Hence
\,k\otimes_{\Delta S}B_{\rm sym}TV\cong S_{k}(V)\,
. We need only to prove the vanishing of higher homology:

{\rm Tor}^{\Delta S}_{i}(k,B_{\rm sym}TV)=0\quad\text{for all}\quad i>0\,

To this end we assign
V
homological degree
2
, and let
\mathcal{L}V
denote the free graded Lie algebra generated by
V
. Then
TV\cong U(\mathcal{L}V)
, where
U(\mbox{--})
stands for universal enveloping algebra. This gives

the structure of a (graded) cocommutative Hopf algebra. Let
\underline{TV}
denote the corresponding graded right
\mathfrak{G}
-module, whose component in homological degree
2q
is
\underline{TV}_{q}
, the component of
\underline{TV}
of weight
q
. More explicitly,
\underline{TV}_{q}(\langle m\rangle)
is the span (over
k

w_{d_{1}}\otimes\ldots\otimes w_{d_{m}}\in(TV)^{\otimes m}
such that

and

. By (
2.17

acquires the structure of a graded
\Delta S

(B_{\rm sym}TV)_{q}\,:=\,\Psi_{\rm sym}^{\ast}\underline{TV}_{q}\ .

Thus,

\mathrm{H}_{n}[k\otimes^{\bm{L}}_{\Delta S}B_{\rm sym}TV]\,\cong\,\Moplus_{2q+i=n}{\rm Tor}^{\Delta S}_{i}(k,(B_{\rm sym}TV)_{q})\ .

The desired proposition will follow once we show that

(3.8)

\mathrm{H}_{\ast}[k\otimes^{\bm{L}}_{\Delta S}B_{\rm sym}TV]\,\cong\,\Lambda_{k}(V)\ .

By
[
11
, Theorem A.1]
, there are Quillen equivalences refining the Dold-Kan equivalence
N:\mathtt{Com}_{k}\rightleftarrows\mathtt{sVect}_{k}:N^{-1}

N^{\ast}\,:\,\mathrm{dgLie}_{k}^{+}\rightleftarrows\mathtt{sLie}_{k}\,:\,N\,,\qquad N^{\ast}\,:\,\mathrm{dgAlg}_{k}^{+}\rightleftarrows\mathrm{sAlg}_{k}\,:\,N

where
\mathtt{s}\mathcal{C}
denotes the category of simplicial objects in a category
\mathcal{C}
. Equip
N^{\ast}(TV)\cong T(N^{-1}V)
(see formula A.4 in the Appendix of

) with simplicial cocommutative Hopf algebra structure given by its identification with the universal enveloping algebra

of the free Lie algebra generated by

\mathfrak{G}

\underline{N^{\ast}TV}
. Let
B_{\rm sym}N^{\ast}(TV)=\Psi_{\rm sym}^{\ast}(\underline{N^{\ast}TV})
. Since

(see formula A.4 in the appendix of
[

) is a semi-free simplicial model of the space

given by the wedge of

the right

The second isomorphism above is by

. Finally, as observed in (the proof of)

, there is a quasi-isomorphism of right

Our second proof of Proposition

Second proof of Proposition
3.2

Choose a basis in

, so that

, where

as the classical James construction of the discrete space

[

{\rm HS}_{\ast}(TV)\,\cong\,{\rm HS}_{\ast}(k[J(X_{+})])\,\cong\,\mathrm{H}_{\ast}[\mathcal{N}X;k]\ ,

Here,

, and

in the formula above stands for the nerve. By (the proof of)

is the free

\mathrm{H}_{\ast}[\mathcal{D}X;k]\,\cong\,\Moplus_{n=0}^{\infty}k[E_{\ast}\Sigma_{n}^{\rm{op}}]\otimes_{k[\Sigma_{n}]}k[X]^{\otimes n}\,\cong\,\Moplus_{n=0}^{\infty}k[E_{\ast}\Sigma_{n}^{\rm{op}}]\otimes_{k[\Sigma_{n}]}V^{\otimes n}\ .

The last isomorphism because

\mathrm{H}_{\ast}\bigg(\prod_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{C}_{\infty}({\mathbb{S}}^{0})\times\prod_{i=2}^{n}\,\Omega^{\infty}\Sigma^{\infty}({\mathbb{S}}^{i-1})^{\binom{n}{i}}\,,\,k\bigg)\,\cong\,\mathrm{HS}_{\ast}(k[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}])\ ,\quad\forall\,n\geq 1\,.

Proof.

Since
\mathcal{C}_{\infty}
is an
E_{\infty}
-operad, and the monads associated to different
E_{\infty}
-operads are equivalent (see
[
27
]
), we can use the monad (
3.11
) to compute the homology of
\mathcal{C}_{\infty}({\mathbb{S}}^{0})
: specifically, writing
{\mathbb{S}}^{0}=\{\ast,x\}
as a discrete two-point space, we can identify

\mathrm{H}_{\ast}(\mathcal{C}_{\infty}({\mathbb{S}}^{0}),\,k)\,\cong\,\mathrm{H}_{\ast}[\Moplus_{n=0}^{\infty}k[E_{\ast}\Sigma_{n}^{\rm op}]\otimes_{k[\Sigma_{n}]}(k\cdot x)^{\otimes n}]\,\cong\,k[x]\,,

where the last isomorphism holds thanks to the fact that
k
is a field of characteristic
0
. Then, by Künneth’s Theorem,

(3.20)

\mathrm{H}_{\ast}(\mathcal{C}_{\infty}({\mathbb{S}}^{0})\,\times\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle n}}{{\ldots}}\,\times\,\mathcal{C}_{\infty}({\mathbb{S}}^{0}),\ k)\,\cong\,S_{k}(V)\,=\,\Lambda_{k}(V)\,.

where
V
is the
k
-vector space spanned by the set
\{x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n}\}

of zero-dimensional cycles corresponding to
n
different copies of
{\mathbb{S}}^{0}
.
On the other hand,

(3.21)

\mathrm{H}_{\ast}(\Omega^{\infty}\Sigma^{\infty}({\mathbb{S}}^{i-1}),\,k)\,\cong\,\mathrm{H}_{\ast}(\mathrm{SP}^{\infty}({\mathbb{S}}^{i-1}),\,k)\,\cong\,\Lambda_{k}(k[i-1])\,,

where again the first isomorphism holds thanks
to the fact that
k
is a field of characteristic
0
(see, e.g.,
[
13
, Sect. 7.3, Remark, p. 565]
and the second
because
{\rm SP}^{\infty}({\mathbb{S}}^{i-1})
is a
K(i-1,\mathbb{Z})
-space. It follows from (
3.21
) that there is a (non-canonical) isomorphism

(3.22)

\mathrm{H}_{\ast}[\Omega^{\infty}\Sigma^{\infty}({\mathbb{S}}^{i-1})\,\times\stackrel{{\scriptstyle{\binom{n}{i}}}}{{\ldots}}\,\times\,\Omega^{\infty}\Sigma^{\infty}({\mathbb{S}}^{i-1}),\,k]\,\cong\,\Lambda_{k}(\wedge^{i}V[i-1])

Now, combining (
3.20
) and (
3.22
), we conclude by Künneth’s Theorem:

\mathrm{H}_{\ast}\bigg(\prod_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{C}_{\infty}({\mathbb{S}}^{0})\times\prod_{i=2}^{n}\,\Omega^{\infty}\Sigma^{\infty}({\mathbb{S}}^{i-1})^{\binom{n}{i}}\,,\,k\bigg)\ \cong\ \Lambda_{k}(V)\otimes\,\Motimes_{i=2}^{n}\Lambda_{k}(\wedge^{i}V[i-1])\ \cong\ \Motimes_{i=1}^{n}\Lambda_{k}(\wedge^{i}V[i-1])\,,

Comparing with formula (
3.18
) shows that this last homology is indeed isomorphic
to the symmetric homology of the polynomial algebra in
n
variables.
This completes the proof of the corollary.

Prospects for thermalization of microwave-shielded ultracold molecules Preprint: APS/123-QED
Reuben R. W. Wang, John L. Bohn

Abstract
We study anisotropic thermalization in dilute gases of microwave shielded polar molecular fermions. For collision energies above the threshold regime, we find that thermalization is suppressed due to a strong preference for forward scattering and a reduction in total cross section with energy, significantly reducing the efficiency of evaporative cooling. We perform close-coupling calculations on the effective potential energy surface derived by Deng et al. [Phys. Rev. Lett. 130, 183001 (2023)], to obtain accurate 2-body elastic differential cross sections across a range of collision energies. We use Gaussian process regression to obtain a global representation of the differential cross section, over a wide range of collision angles and energies. The route to equilibrium is then analyzed with cross-dimensional rethermalization experiments, quantified by a measure of collisional efficiency toward achieving thermalization.

Shielded collisions

Outlook and conclusions
—By constructing a GP model of the elastic differential cross section between microwave shielded polar molecular fermions, we have found that non-threshold collisions can greatly diminish the efficacy of collisions toward thermalization of a nondegenerate gas.
It is thus prudent to perform evaporation in the threshold regime, with the caveat that Pauli blocking in fermions would also lower the collisional efficiency below the Fermi temperature
Aikawa et al. 2014
.
If deployed in direct simulation Monte Carlo solvers
Bird 1970
;
Sykes and Bohn 2015
;
Wang and Bohn 2021
, this GP model could also permit accurate dynamical studies in the Fermi degenerate or hydrodynamic regimes.
The latter is motivated by restrictions of
\varepsilon_{ij}
, only being able to describe thermalization in dilute samples. With larger molecular dipoles at densities required to achieve quantum degeneracy, the collision rate is far exceeded by the mean trapping frequency, demanding equilibration of trapped dipolar gases be treated within a hydrodynamic framework
Wang and Bohn 2022a
;
Wang and Bohn 2022b
;
Wang and Bohn 2023a
;
Wang and Bohn 2023b
.
The method of GP interpolation proposed here could similarly be applied to DC field shielded molecules
Lassablière and Quéméner 2022
and bosonic species.

\displaystyle\left(\frac{d^{2}}{dr^{2}}-\frac{\ell(\ell+1)}{r^{2}}+k^{2}\right)u_{E,\ell,m_{\ell}}(r)\approx 0.

(8)

This asymptotic radial equation is solved by the 2 independent solutions (up to arbitrary normalization):

\displaystyle f_{E,\ell}(r)

\displaystyle=krj_{\ell}(kr),

(9b)

(10a)

\displaystyle G_{\ell,m_{\ell}}^{\ell^{\prime},m^{\prime}_{\ell}}(r;E)

(10b)

\bm{N}

The reactance matrix can be written in terms of the logarithmic derivative via

\displaystyle\bm{K}

\displaystyle=\left.\frac{\bm{F}(r)\bm{Y}(r)-\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\bm{F}(r)}{\bm{G}(r)\bm{Y}(r)-\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\bm{G}(r)}\right|_{r=r_{\rm match}},

from which, we can then compute the other scattering matrices via the relations

\displaystyle\bm{S}

\displaystyle=\frac{\bm{I}+i\bm{K}}{\bm{I}-i\bm{K}},

(13a)

\displaystyle\bm{T}

\displaystyle=i(\bm{S}-\bm{I}).

(13b)

\displaystyle f_{\rm sc}(\bm{k},\hat{\bm{k}}^{\prime})

\displaystyle=-\frac{2\pi}{k}\sum_{m_{\ell}}\sum_{\ell,\ell^{\prime}}i^{\ell}Y_{\ell,m_{\ell}}^{*}(\hat{\bm{k}})T_{\ell,m_{\ell}}^{\ell^{\prime},m_{\ell}}(k)Y_{\ell^{\prime},m_{\ell}}(\hat{\bm{k}}^{\prime})i^{-\ell^{\prime}},

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\absolutevalue{ f_{\rm sc}( \boldsymbol{k}, \hat{\boldsymbol{k}}' )
-

(15)

total cross section

\displaystyle\sigma(\bm{k})

\displaystyle=\int d^{2}\hat{\bm{k}}^{\prime}{\cal D}_{\rm el}(\bm{k},\hat{\bm{k}}^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\frac{4\pi^{2}}{k^{2}}\sum_{m_{\ell}}\sum_{\tilde{\ell},\ell,\ell^{\prime}}i^{\ell-\tilde{\ell}}Y_{\tilde{\ell},m_{\ell}}(\hat{\bm{k}})Y_{\ell,m_{\ell}}^{*}(\hat{\bm{k}})\left[T_{\tilde{\ell},m_{\ell}}^{\ell^{\prime},m_{\ell}}(k)\right]^{*}T_{\ell,m_{\ell}}^{\ell^{\prime},m_{\ell}}(k),

(16)

and integral total cross section

\displaystyle\overline{\sigma}

II
Frame transformations for Gaussian process fitting

For efficient GP fitting of the elastic differential cross section, it is optimal to choose a coordinate frame whereby the symmetries are most conveniently handled.
Naively, the differential cross section during a two-body collision involves 3 unit vectors: 1) the dipole alignment axis
\hat{\bm{{\cal E}}}
, 2) the incident relative momentum
\bm{k}
and 3) the outgoing relative momentum
\bm{k}^{\prime}
, therefore requiring 6 angular coordinates. This description is the case in a lab-frame (LF), where without loss of generality, we define it such that the dipole axis lies in its
x,z
-plane
\hat{\bm{{\cal E}}}_{\rm LF}=(\sin\Theta,0,\cos\Theta)^{T}
, and the other 2 vectors are given in terms of spherical coordinates as

\displaystyle\hat{\bm{k}}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}\sin\theta\cos\phi\\
\sin\theta\sin\phi\\
\cos\theta\end{pmatrix},

(20a)

\displaystyle\hat{\bm{k}}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}\sin\theta^{\prime}\cos\phi^{\prime}\\
\sin\theta^{\prime}\sin\phi^{\prime}\\
\cos\theta^{\prime}\end{pmatrix}.

(20b)

However, we can also define a dipole-frame (DF) which utilizes the dipole alignment direction as its
z
-axis,
\hat{\bm{{\cal E}}}=\hat{\bm{z}}_{\rm DF}
, while its
x
axis is aligned to the plane in which both
\hat{\bm{{\cal E}}}
and
\hat{\bm{k}}
lie, so that

\displaystyle\hat{\bm{y}}_{\rm DF}

\displaystyle=\frac{\hat{\bm{{\cal E}}}\crossproduct\hat{\bm{k}}}{|\hat{\bm{{\cal E}}}\crossproduct\hat{\bm{k}}|}\frac{1}{|\hat{\bm{{\cal E}}}\crossproduct\hat{\bm{k}}|}\begin{pmatrix}-\cos\Theta\sin\theta\sin\phi\\
\cos\Theta\sin\theta\cos\phi-\sin\Theta\cos\theta\\
\sin\Theta\sin\theta\sin\phi\end{pmatrix}.

(21)

The remaining
\hat{\bm{x}}_{\rm DF}
axis is then obtained with the cross product
\hat{\bm{x}}_{\rm DF}=\hat{\bm{y}}_{\rm DF}\crossproduct\hat{\bm{z}}_{\rm DF}
. In the event where
\hat{\bm{{\cal E}}}
and
\hat{\bm{k}}
coincide, we simply choose

\displaystyle\hat{\bm{x}}_{\rm DF}=\begin{pmatrix}\sin(\Theta+ \pi/2)\\
0\\
\cos(\Theta+ \pi/2)\end{pmatrix},

(22)

and also
\hat{\bm{y}}_{\rm DF}=\hat{\bm{{\cal E}}}\crossproduct\hat{\bm{x}}_{\rm DF}
. The differential cross section only cares about the relative angle between
\hat{\bm{k}}
and
\hat{\bm{{\cal E}}}
, but not the vectors themselves. The dipole frame allows a convenient handling of this fact, we can simply write
\hat{\bm{k}}=(\sin\eta,0,\cos\eta)^{T}
where
\eta=\cos^{-1}\hat{\bm{k}}\cdot\hat{\bm{{\cal E}}}
. So to obtain the post-collision relative momentum in the dipole-frame, we can construct the required rotation matrix
\bm{R}({\rm DF}\leftarrow{\rm LF})
, by the method of direction cosines

\displaystyle\bm{R}({\rm DF}\leftarrow{\rm LF})

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}\hat{\bm{x}}_{\rm DF}\cdot\hat{\bm{x}}_{\rm LF}&\hat{\bm{x}}_{\rm DF}\cdot\hat{\bm{y}}_{\rm LF}&\hat{\bm{x}}_{\rm DF}\cdot\hat{\bm{z}}_{\rm LF}\\
\hat{\bm{y}}_{\rm DF}\cdot\hat{\bm{x}}_{\rm LF}&\hat{\bm{y}}_{\rm DF}\cdot\hat{\bm{y}}_{\rm LF}&\hat{\bm{y}}_{\rm DF}\cdot\hat{\bm{z}}_{\rm LF}\\
\hat{\bm{z}}_{\rm DF}\cdot\hat{\bm{x}}_{\rm LF}&\hat{\bm{z}}_{\rm DF}\cdot\hat{\bm{y}}_{\rm LF}&\hat{\bm{z}}_{\rm DF}\cdot\hat{\bm{z}}_{\rm LF}\end{pmatrix}.

(23)

The outbound relative collision vector is then given in dipole-frame as
\hat{\bm{k}}^{\prime}({\rm DF})=\bm{R}({\rm DF}\leftarrow{\rm LF})\hat{\bm{k}}^{\prime}({\rm LF})
.
We then denote inclination and azimuthal scattering angles in the dipole frame as
\theta_{s}
and
\phi_{s}
respectively.

Furthermore, the dipole frame as defined makes the differential cross section symmetric about the
x,z
-plane, only requiring us to specify
\phi_{s}
within the range
[0,\pi]
. The entire differential cross section can then be obtained by specifying its value in the appropriate energy interval, and for
\eta,\theta_{s},\phi_{s}\in[0,\pi]
.

III
The Eikonal approximation

At collision energies much larger than
E_{\rm dd}
, the scattering becomes semiclassical with the total cross section well approximated by the Eikonal approximation
Bohn et al. 2009
. Within this approximation, the scattering amplitude, considering on the
1/r^{3}
long-range tail of
V_{\rm eff}
, is given by

\displaystyle f_{\rm sc}^{\rm Ei}(\bm{k},\hat{\bm{k}}^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\frac{a_{d}\tilde{k}}{2\pi i}\int_{0}^{2\pi}d\phi\int_{0}^{\infty}d\tilde{b}\>\tilde{b}e^{i\tilde{q}\tilde{b}\cos\phi}\left[\exp\left(-\frac{1}{\tilde{k}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\tilde{V}_{\rm eff}(\tilde{\bm{r}}^{\prime})d\tilde{z}^{\prime}\right)-1\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{a_{d}\tilde{k}}{2\pi i}\int\tilde{b}d\tilde{b}d\phi e^{i\tilde{q}\tilde{b}\cos\phi}\left[\exp\left(-\frac{2i}{\tilde{k}\tilde{b}^{2}}\sin^{2}\alpha\cos( 2 \phi- 2 \beta)\right)-1\right],

where
\alpha=\cos^{-1}(\hat{\bm{k}}\cdot\hat{\bm{{\cal E}}})
,
\beta=\tan^{-1}(\hat{\bm{y}}\cdot\hat{\bm{{\cal E}}}/\hat{\bm{x}}\cdot\hat{\bm{{\cal E}}})
,
\bm{q}=\bm{k}-\bm{k}^{\prime}
is the momentum transfer vector,
b
is the impact parameter and tildes denote adimensional quantities normalized by the relevant dipole units (see the main text).

From the scattering amplitude, we can compute the total cross section using the optical theorem

\displaystyle\sigma^{\rm Ei}_{\rm total}(\bm{k})

\displaystyle=\frac{4\pi}{k}{\rm Im}\left\{f_{\rm sc}^{\rm Ei}(\tilde{q}=0)\right\},

(24)

which requires evaluation of the scattering amplitude at forward scattering
\tilde{q}=0
:

\displaystyle f_{\rm sc}^{\rm Ei}(\bm{k},\hat{\bm{k}}^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\frac{a_{d}}{2\pi i\tilde{k}}\int\tilde{\ell}d\tilde{\ell}d\phi\left[e^{-\frac{2i\tilde{k}}{\tilde{\ell}^{2}}\sin^{2}\alpha\cos( 2 \phi- 2 \beta)}-1\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{a_{d}}{2\pi i\tilde{k}}\int\tilde{\ell}d\tilde{\ell}d\phi\Bigg[\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\epsilon_{m}(-i)^{m}J_{m}\left(\frac{2\tilde{k}}{\tilde{\ell}^{2}}\sin^{2}\alpha\right)\cos[2m(\phi-\beta)]-1\Bigg]

\displaystyle=\frac{a_{d}}{i\tilde{k}}\int\tilde{\ell}d\tilde{\ell}\Bigg[J_{0}\left(\frac{2\tilde{k}}{\tilde{\ell}^{2}}\sin^{2}\alpha\right)-1\Bigg]

\displaystyle=ia_{d}\sin^{2}\alpha,

(25)

where
\epsilon_{m}=1
if
m=0
, and
\epsilon_{m}=2
if
m>0
, and we utilized the substitution
\ell=kb
. Then plugging the forward scattering amplitude into the optical theorem gives

\displaystyle\sigma^{\rm Ei}_{\rm total}(\bm{k})=\frac{4\pi a_{d}}{k}\left[1-\big(\hat{\bm{k}}\cdot\hat{\bm{{\cal E}}}\big)^{2}\right],

(26)

which averaged over incident directions, gives

\displaystyle\overline{\sigma}^{\rm Ei}_{\rm total}=\frac{8\pi a_{d}}{3k},

(27)

identical to the formula obtained for point-dipole scattering in Ref.
Bohn et al. 2009
. The result above is applicable to distinguishable dipoles, and so may not be quantitatively accurate in describing our study of scattering between identical fermions. Nevertheless, it serves to provide a useful visual aid to the expectation energy scaling, and seems to actually give rather favorable quantitative agreement.

IV
Deriving the collisional efficiency toward thermalization

Obtaining the form of
{\cal N}_{ij}
in the main text requires formulation of the Enskog equations. To do so, we define the phase space averaged quantity
\langle\chi_{i}\rangle=k_{B}({\cal T}_{i}-T_{\rm eq})
, which quantifies the system’s deviation from its equilibration temperature,
T_{\rm eq}
. Then multiplying the Boltzmann equation
Reif 2009
by
\chi_{i}
and integrating it over phase space variables
Wang and Bohn 2021
, we derived Enskog equations that govern the relaxation of
\langle\chi_{j}\rangle
:

\displaystyle\frac{d\langle\chi_{i}\rangle}{dt}

\displaystyle={\cal C}[\chi_{i}],

(28a)

\displaystyle{\cal C}[\chi_{i}]

\displaystyle=\frac{\langle n\rangle}{2}\int\frac{d^{3}\bm{p}_{r}}{m}p_{r}c_{r}(\bm{p}_{r},t)\int d^{2}\Omega^{\prime}{\cal D}_{\rm el}\Delta\chi_{i},

(28b)

where
\langle n\rangle
is the average number density,
c_{r}(\bm{p}_{r},t)
is the distribution of relative momentum
\bm{p}_{r}
, and
\Delta\chi\equiv\chi^{\prime}+\chi_{1}^{\prime}-\chi-\chi_{1}
denotes the amount by which
\chi
changes during a collision event.

Taken only perturbatively from equilibrium along axis
i
, Eq.
28a
is approximated by the decay law
{\cal C}[\chi_{j}]\approx-\gamma_{ij}\langle\chi_{j}\rangle
, which results in the short-time relation

\displaystyle\gamma_{ij}=-\left.\frac{1}{\left(\mathcal{T}_{j}(t)-T_{\text{eq}}\right)}\frac{d\mathcal{T}_{j}(t)}{dt}\right|_{t=0},

(29)

identifying
\gamma_{ij}
as the thermalization rate.
Now considering the collision integral

\displaystyle{\cal C}[\chi_{j}]

\displaystyle=\frac{\langle n\rangle}{2}\int\frac{d^{3}\bm{p}_{r}}{m}p_{r}c_{r}(\bm{p}_{r},t)\int d^{2}\Omega^{\prime}{\cal D}_{\rm el}\Delta\chi_{j},

\displaystyle=\frac{k_{B}\langle n\rangle}{2}\int\frac{d^{3}\bm{p}_{r}}{m}p_{r}c_{r}(\bm{p}_{r},t)\int d^{2}\Omega^{\prime}{\cal D}_{\rm el}\Delta{\cal T}_{j},

(30)

we move to center of mass and relative momentum coordinates,
\bm{P}=(\bm{p}+\bm{p}_{1})/2
and
\bm{p}_{r}=\bm{p}-\bm{p}_{1}
respectively, so that the change in
\chi_{i}
is given as

\displaystyle\Delta\chi_{i}

\displaystyle=\frac{\Delta p_{i}^{2}}{2m}

\displaystyle=\frac{p^{\prime 2}_{j}+p^{\prime 2}_{1,j}-p_{j}^{2}-p_{1,j}^{2}}{2m}

\displaystyle=\frac{p^{\prime 2}_{r,j}+P^{\prime 2}_{j}-p^{2}_{r,j}-P^{2}_{j}}{4m}

\displaystyle=\frac{p^{\prime 2}_{r,i}-p^{2}_{r,i}}{4m}.

(31)

As for the relative momentum distribution, we Taylor expand it at
t=0
with respect to
\delta_{i}/k_{B}T_{0}
to get

\displaystyle c_{r}(\bm{p}_{r},0)

\displaystyle=\prod_{i}\left(\frac{1}{4\pi mk_{B}T_{i}}\right)^{1/2}\exp\left(-\frac{p_{r,i}^{2}}{4mk_{B}T_{i}}\right)

\displaystyle\approx c_{r}^{(0)}(\bm{p}_{r})\left[1+\left(\frac{p_{r,i}^{2}}{4mk_{B}T_{0}}-\frac{1}{2}\right)\frac{\delta_{i}}{k_{B}T_{0}}\right],

(32a)

\displaystyle c_{r}^{(0)}(p_{r})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(4\pi mk_{B}T_{0})^{3/2}}\exp\left(-\frac{p_{r}^{2}}{4mk_{B}T_{0}}\right).

(32b)

The expressions above render the collision integral

\displaystyle{\cal C}[\chi_{j}]

\displaystyle\approx\frac{\langle n\rangle}{2}\int\frac{d^{3}\bm{p}_{r}}{m}p_{r}c_{r}^{(0)}(\bm{p}_{r})\left[1+\left(\frac{p_{r,i}^{2}}{4mk_{B}T_{0}}-\frac{1}{2}\right)\frac{\delta_{i}}{k_{B}T_{0}}\right]\int d^{2}\Omega^{\prime}{\cal D}_{\rm el}\left(\frac{p^{\prime 2}_{r,j}-p^{2}_{r,j}}{4m}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\delta_{i}}{16(mk_{B}T_{0})^{2}}\frac{\langle n\rangle}{2}\int\frac{d^{3}\bm{p}_{r}}{m}c_{r}^{(0)}(\bm{p}_{r})p_{r}\int d^{2}\Omega^{\prime}{\cal D}^{\prime}_{\rm el}p_{r,i}^{2}\left(p^{\prime 2}_{r,j}-p^{2}_{r,j}\right),

(33)

which upon utilizing the time-reversal symmetry of elastic collisions

\displaystyle{\cal C}[\chi_{j}]

\displaystyle\approx\frac{\delta_{i}}{16(mk_{B}T_{0})^{2}}\frac{\langle n\rangle}{2}\int p_{r}^{2}dp_{r}c_{r}^{(0)}(p_{r})\frac{p_{r}}{m}\int d^{2}\Omega d^{2}\Omega^{\prime}{\cal D}^{\prime}_{\rm el}p_{r,i}^{2}\left(p^{\prime 2}_{r,j}-p^{2}_{r,j}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\delta_{i}}{16(mk_{B}T_{0})^{2}}\frac{\langle n\rangle}{2}\int p_{r}^{2}dp_{r}c_{r}^{(0)}(p_{r})\frac{p_{r}}{m}\int d^{2}\Omega^{\prime}d^{2}\Omega{\cal D}^{\prime}_{\rm el}p_{r,i}^{\prime 2}\left(p^{2}_{r,j}-p^{\prime 2}_{r,j}\right),

(34)

the expression above can also be written in a form that is explicit in the symmetry under exchange of indices
i
and
j
:

\displaystyle{\cal C}[\chi_{j}]

\displaystyle=-\frac{\delta_{i}}{32(mk_{B}T_{0})^{2}}\frac{\langle n\rangle}{2}\int\frac{d^{3}\bm{p}_{r}}{m}c_{r}^{(0)}(p_{r})p_{r}\int d^{2}\Omega^{\prime}{\cal D}^{\prime}_{\rm el}\left(p^{\prime 2}_{r,i}-p^{2}_{r,i}\right)\left(p^{\prime 2}_{r,j}-p^{2}_{r,j}\right).

(35)

We have used the suggestive notation
{\cal D}^{\prime}_{\rm el}={\cal D}_{\rm el}(p_{r},\Omega^{\prime})
.
Plugging
{\cal C}[\chi_{j}]
as written into Eq. (
29
) and taking
\mathcal{T}_{j}(t)-T_{\rm eq}=\epsilon_{j}/k_{B}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\gamma_{ij}

\displaystyle=-\frac{{\cal C}[\mathcal{T}_{j}]}{\left(\mathcal{T}_{j}(t)-T_{\rm eq}\right)}

\displaystyle=-\frac{k_{B}}{\epsilon_{j}}{\cal C}[\mathcal{T}_{j}]

\displaystyle=\frac{\delta_{i}}{\epsilon_{j}}\frac{\langle n\rangle}{512}\int\frac{p_{r}^{2}dp_{r}}{(\pi mk_{B}T_{0})^{3/2}}\exp\left(-\frac{p_{r}^{2}}{4mk_{B}T_{0}}\right)\frac{p_{r}}{m}\int d^{2}\Omega d^{2}\Omega^{\prime}{\cal D}^{\prime}_{\rm el}\left(\frac{p^{\prime 2}_{r,i}-p^{2}_{r,i}}{mk_{B}T_{0}}\right)\left(\frac{p^{\prime 2}_{r,j}-p^{2}_{r,j}}{mk_{B}T_{0}}\right).

(36)

Finally, taking the limit of
\delta_{i}/(k_{B}T_{0})\rightarrow 0
,
we obtain
\varepsilon_{ij}
in the main text,
having defined
\alpha_{ij}={\delta_{i}/\epsilon_{j}}
and using the equipartition theorem (
T_{\rm eq}=T_{0}+\delta_{i}/3k_{B}
) to get

\displaystyle\frac{\delta_{i}}{\epsilon_{j}}=\begin{cases}{3/2},&\quad i=j,\\
-3,&\quad i\neq j.\end{cases}

(37)

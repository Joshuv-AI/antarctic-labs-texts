1
Introduction

The notion of a Poisson algebra was probably introduced in the first time by A.M. Vinogradov and J. S. Krasil’shchik in 1975 under the name ”canonical algebra” and
by J. Braconnier in his short note ”Algèbres de Poisson” (Comptes rendus
Ac.Sci) in 1977.

It was a natural ”algebraic interpretation” of the notion of Poisson structure and Poisson brackets appeared in XIX century in the framework of Classical Mechanics.

Nowadays, Poisson algebras have proved to have a very rich mathematical structure
(see the beautiful short article of Y. Kosmann-Schwarzbach in ”Encyclopedia of Mathematics” (Poisson algebra, Encyclopedia of Mathematics,
http://encyclopediaofmath.org/index.php?title=Poisson_algebra&oldid=51639
).

There are many good books and lecture notes devoted to Poisson structures and
much more less sources concerning the algebraic side of the story. We should mention the extensive book of C. Laurent-Gengoux, A. Pichereau and P. Vanhaecke ”Poisson structures” which covers also many algebraic aspects and structures associated with the notion of a Poisson variety
[
8
]
.

These notes are slightly extended reproduction of the mini-course of the same title (5 lectures) given by the first author during the virtual (online) Winter School and Workshop ”Wisla 20-21” and handled and written by the second.

The virtual character of lectures has imposed few constraints and has defined a specific choice of material and chosen subjects. The authors had to pass between
Scylla of unknown audience level and tantalizing Charybdis of their will to exit out of the standard set of content in numerous existed lecture notes about Poisson structures in algebra and in geometry.

This contradiction can probably explain some strange ”jumps” of a difficulty level between various chapters of our notes. We hope to come back to these notes and to extend, to improve, or even, to write a new comprehensive book covering the subject. Right now we were on a serious time (and the Covid pandemic) limits pressure and it was also a reason of some non-homogeneous choice of the lecture notes matters and its details.

There are (almost) no ”new results” in the lectures. The only exception is a content of the last brief chapter where we provide our classification results (joint with A. Odesskii and V. Sokolov) on the ”low rank” double quadratic Poisson brackets. We had kept this chapter almost in it’s specific form of a computer presentation.

The lectures are based on many various well- and less known sources, which we had tried to carefully quot in the main body of the text. But we need to specify most influential: the beautiful paper of A.M. Vinogradov and J.S. Krasil’shchik

Motivation

2.1
Lagrangian and Hamiltonian mechanics

A fundamental discovery of Lagrange: a
Lagrangian
function
L\colon TM\to\Rbb

\displaystyle L=T-V\ ,

describes the motion of a particle. The equation of motion is described by the Euler-Lagrange equation

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}(\frac{\del L}{\del\dot{q^{i}}})=\frac{\del L}{\del q^{i}}\ .

Here
(\bar{q})=(q^{1},\ldots,q^{n})
are coordinates on
M
,
(\bar{q},\bar{\dot{q}})=(q^{1},\ldots,q^{n},\dot{q}^{1},\ldots,\dot{q}^{n})
are the induced coordinates on the tangent bundle
TM
. The above Euler-Lagrange equation follows from the variational principle

\displaystyle\delta\int L(\bar{q},\bar{\dot{q}})dt=0\ ,

where
\delta
(in this context) refers to the variational derivative. Using the Legendre transformation, one can pass from the tangent bundle to the cotangent bundle
T^{*}M
with the coordinates
(\bar{q},\bar{p})
, and reformulate the Euler-Lagrange equations equivalently by the Hamiltons equations

\displaystyle\dot{\bar{q}}=\frac{\del H}{\del\bar{p}},\ \ \ \dot{\bar{p}}=-\frac{\del H}{\del\bar{q}}

(1)

The link between Lagrangian and Hamiltonian is given by

\displaystyle H(\bar{q},\bar{p})=<\bar{p},\bar{\dot{q}}>-L(\bar{q},\bar{\dot{q}})\ .

2.2
Hamiltonian mechanics and Poisson brackets

Instead of starting with the Lagrangian
L
function, one can start with the Hamiltonian function
H\colon T^{*}M\to\Rbb
and build the mechanics independently of the Lagrangian. We now go to the simpler setup of flat spaces, i.e. consider
M\cong\Rbb^{N}
. We will get back to the setup of smooth manifolds later on.

Let
F,G
be two differentiable functions on
\Rbb^{2n}
with coordinates
(\bar{q},\bar{p})
, where
\bar{p}=(p_{1},\dots,p_{n}),\bar{q}=(q_{1},\dots,q_{n})
. Introduce a new functions as the result of the following skew-symmetric operation on
F
and
G
.

\displaystyle\{F,G\}:=\sum_{k=1}^{n}(\frac{\del F}{\del q_{k}}\frac{\del G}{\del p_{k}}-\frac{\del G}{\del q_{k}}\frac{\del F}{\del p_{k}})=\frac{\del F}{\del\bar{q}}\frac{\del G}{\del\bar{p}}-\frac{\del G}{\del\bar{q}}\frac{\del F}{\del\bar{p}}\in C^{\infty}(\Rbb^{2n})\ .

(2)

This operations allows to write the Hamilton’s equations (
1
) associated with a Hamiltonian function
H=H(\bar{q},\bar{p})
in the following manner (putting
\bar{q}
and
\bar{p}
on an equal footing):

\displaystyle\dot{\bar{q}}=\{\bar{q},H\}\ ,\ \ \ \dot{\bar{p}}=\{\bar{p},H\}\ .

(3)

Indeed we have

\displaystyle\{q_{i},H\}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}(\frac{\del q_{i}}{\del q_{k}}\frac{\del H}{\del p_{k}}-\frac{\del H}{\del q_{k}}\frac{\del q_{i}}{\del p_{k}})=\frac{\del H}{\del p_{i}}\ ,

\displaystyle\{p_{i},H\}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}(\frac{\del p_{i}}{\del q_{k}}\frac{\del H}{\del p_{k}}-\frac{\del H}{\del q_{k}}\frac{\del p_{i}}{\del p_{k}})=-\frac{\del H}{\del q_{i}}\ ,

since
\frac{\del q_{i}}{\del q_{k}}=\frac{\del p_{i}}{\del p_{k}}=\delta^{i}_{k}
and
\frac{\del q_{i}}{\del p_{k}}=\frac{\del p_{i}}{\del q_{k}}=0
.

Note that for each solution
\{(q(t),p(t)\}
of the Hamilton system (
3
) and for any
F\in C^{\infty}(\Rbb^{2n})

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}F(\bar{q}(t),\bar{p}(t))

\displaystyle:=\sum_{k=1}^{n}(\frac{\del F}{\del q_{i}}\dot{q_{i}}+\frac{\del F}{\del p_{i}}\dot{p_{i}})

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{n}(\frac{\del F}{\del q_{i}}\frac{\del H}{\del p_{i}}-\frac{\del F}{\del p_{i}}\frac{\del H}{\del q_{i}})

\displaystyle=\{F,H\}(\bar{q}(t),\bar{p}(t))\ .

In particular, for the Hamiltonian function
H
,

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}H(\bar{q}(t),\bar{p}(t))=\{H,H\}(\bar{q}(t),\bar{p}(t))=0\ ,

which is the
conservation law
for
H
(i.e.
H(\bar{q}(t),\bar{p}(t))
is constant along the trajectories).
D. Poisson had observed that if
\{F,H\}
and
\{G,H\}
vanish, then
\{\{F,G\},H\}
vanishes (Poisson bracket of two constants of motion is again a constant of motion). This statement can be explained abstractly with the help of the
Jacobi identity

\displaystyle\{\{F,G\},H\}+\{\{G,H\},F\}+\{\{H,F\},G\}=0\ ,

(4)

since if
\{F,H\}=\{G,H\}=0
, then the above equality gives
\{\{F,G\},H\}=0
. Now let
\pi_{ij}
be arbitrary smooth functions on
\Rbb^{d}
,
x_{1},\dots,x_{d}
cooridnates on
\Rbb^{d}
. Define

\displaystyle\{F,G\}:=\sum_{k=1}^{d}\pi_{ij}\frac{\del F}{\del x_{i}}\frac{\del G}{\del x_{j}}\ .

(5)

Then (
5
) satisfies the Jacobi identity (
4
) if and only if

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{d}(\pi_{lk}\frac{\del\pi_{ij}}{\del x_{l}}+\pi_{li}\frac{\del\pi_{jk}}{\del x_{l}}+\pi_{lj}\frac{\del\pi_{ki}}{\del x_{l}})=0\ .

(6)

Skew-symmetry and the Jacobi identity imply that the operation
\{\cdot,\cdot\}
defines a
Lie algebra
structure on
C^{\infty}(\Rbb^{d})
. Moreover this structure is compatible with the product operation
\cdot
on
C^{\infty}(\Rbb^{d}),U\subset\Rbb^{d}
, meaning that it satisfies the
Leibniz rule

\displaystyle\{FG,H\}=F\{G,H\}+\{F,H\}G\ .

3
Poisson algebras

There are two ways to think about a Poisson structure (given by a Poisson bracket) on a manifold
M
(algebraic, smooth, analytic, etc.).

A geometric viewpoint
. To each function
H
(smooth, holomorphic, etc.) on
M
, one can associate a vector field
X_{H}
, where
H
is the Hamiltonian in the mechanical interpretation. The vector field is given by

\displaystyle X_{H}:=\{-,H\}\ .

(7)

An algebraic viewpoint
. Consider a vector space (typically infinite dimensional)
\A
, with two algebra structures:

1.

Structure of an associative and commutative algebra with a unit.

2.

A Lie algebra structure.

The Poisson structure:
commutative structure + Lie algebra structure + compatibility conditions.

Extracting the algebraic aspects of the above ideas, one is lead to the following definition.

Definition 3.1

(Poisson algebra)
.

Let
(\A,\cdot)
be an associative, unital and commutative
\Kbb
-algebra (
\operatorname{char}\Kbb=0
), with the unit denoted
1
. Let

\displaystyle\{-,-\}\colon\A\times\A\to\A

be a Lie bracket defining a
\Kbb
-Lie algebra structure on
\A
. Then
\A
is called
a Poisson algebra
if the operations
\cdot
and
\{-,-\}
are compatible in the following sense

\displaystyle\{a\cdot b,c\}=a\cdot\{b,c\}+\{a,c\}\cdot b\ .

(8)

The Lie bracket is then called
a Poisson bracket
.

When the context is clear, we will usually omit the
\cdot
symbol of the associative operation in
\A
.

Remark 3.1
.

Note that the Lie bracket
\{-,-\}
in the above definition is not necessarily given by the commutator. In fact, if it is given by the commutator, i.e.
\{a,b\}=a\cdot b-b\cdot a
, then
\{a,b\}=0
for all
a,b\in\A
, since the
\cdot
is assumed to be a commutative operation on
\A
.

3.1
Subalgebras and ideals

Definition 3.2

(Subalgebras)
.

A vector subspaces
\B\subset\A
is
a Poisson subalgebra
in a Poisson algebra
(\A,\{-,-\},\cdot)
if
\B\cdot\B subset\B
and
\{\B,\B\}\subset\B
.

Definition 3.3

(Ideals)
.

A vector subspaces
\J\subset\A
is
a Poisson ideal
of a Poisson algebra
(\A,\{-,-\},\cdot)
if
\J\cdot\A\subset\J
and
\{\J,\A\}\subset\J
.

Observation 3.1
.

In the first definition,
\B
is itself a Poisson algebra with operations given by restriction of operations in
\A
. The inclusion map
\iota\colon\B\subset\A
is a Poisson algebra morphism. In the second definition, the quotient algebra
\A/\J
inherits a Poisson bracket from
\A
by the requirement that the canonical projection on the quotient
p\colon\A\to\A/\J
is a Poisson morphism.

Remark 3.2
.

For a fixed field
\Kbb
(
\operatorname{char}\Kbb=0)
,
\Poiss_{\Kbb}
denotes a category, whose objects are the Poisson
\Kbb
-algebras, and whose morphisms are
\Kbb
-morphisms of Poisson algebras. Group object in
\Poiss_{\Kbb}
is the Poisson-Lie group
(G,\cdot,\{-,-\})
s.t. the bracket on
G
is
multiplicative
, i.e. the multiplication
\cdot G\times G\to G
is a Poisson morphism, where
G\times G
is considered with a Poisson bracket given by
\{-,-\}_{G\times G}:=\{-,-\}_{G}\oplus\{-,-\}_{G}
.

Definition 3.4

(Prime)
.

A Poisson ideal
\mathcal{P}\subset\A
is
Poisson prime
, if for all Poisson ideals
\I,\J\subset\A

\displaystyle\I\J\subset\mathcal{P}\Rightarrow\I\subset\mathcal{P}\text{ or }\J\subset\mathcal{P}

Remark 3.3
.

If
\A
is
Noetherian

Given an increasing sequence of ideals in
\A
,

, there always exists

for all
n\in\Nbb
.
, then the above definition is equivalent to

being both prime ideal and Poisson ideal.

Definition 3.5

(Spectrum)
.

The
Poisson spectrum of

\A
, denoted
\PSpec(\A)
, is the set of Poisson prime ideals of
\A
.

Definition 3.6

(Maximal ideal)
.

A maximal ideal
\mathcal{M}\subset\A
is a
Poisson maximal ideal
if it is a Poisson ideal.

Remark 3.4
.

A Poisson maximal ideal is not the same thing as a maximal Poisson ideal.

Definition 3.7

(Core)
.

The
Poisson core
of an ideal
\I\subset\A
, denoted
\PC(\I)
, is the largest Poisson ideal of
\A
contained in
\I
.

Remark 3.5
.

If
\I
is a prime ideal of
\A
, then
\PC(\I)
is Poisson prime.

Definition 3.8

(Primitive ideal)
.

A Poisson ideal
\mathcal{P}
of
\A
is a
Poisson primitive
if
\mathcal{P}
is the Poisson core of some maximal ideal
\mathcal{M}
, i.e.
\PC(\mathcal{M})=\mathcal{P}

Remark 3.6
.

Each Poisson primitive ideal is Poisson prime.

(Localization)
.

Let

3.2
Morphisms and derivations

Definition 3.10

(Morphisms and isomorphisms)
.

Let
(\A_{i},\{-,-\}_{i},\cdot_{i})_{i=1,2}
be two Poisson algebras over
\Kbb
and let
\varphi\colon\A_{1}\to\A_{2}
be a
\Kbb
-linear map satisfying for all
a,b\in\A_{1}

1.

\varphi(a\cdot_{1}b)=\varphi(a)\cdot_{2}\varphi(b)
,

2.

\varphi(\{a,b\}_{1})=\{\varphi(a),\varphi(b)\}_{2}

Then
\varphi
is called
a morphism of Poisson algebras
. If
\varphi
is a bijective morphism of Poisson algebras s.t. the inverse map
\varphi^{-1}\colon\A_{2}\to\A_{1}
is also a Poisson algebra morphism, then
\varphi
is called
an isomorphism of Poisson algebras
.

Definition 3.11

(Derivation)
.

A
\Kbb
-linear map
\varphi\colon\A\to\A
is called
a
\Kbb
-derivation
on
\A
if

\displaystyle\varphi(a\cdot b)=a\varphi(b)+\varphi(a)\cdot b\ .

(9)

The set of all derivations of
\A
is denoted by
\mathcal{D}(\A)
,
\mathcal{D}^{1}(\A)
or
\mathcal{D}(\A,\A)
. A bilinear map
\varphi\colon\A\times\A\to\A
is called
a bi-derivation
if

\begin{gathered}\varphi(ab,c)=a\varphi(b,c)+b\varphi(a,c),\\
\varphi(a,bc)=\varphi(a,b)c+b\varphi(a,c)\end{gathered}

(10)

Observation 3.2
.

Directly from the definition,
\mathcal{D}(\A)\subset\End_{\Kbb}(\A)=\Hom_{\Kbb}(\A,\A)
. The
\Kbb
-linear map, given for every
b\in\A
,
a\mapsto\{a,b\}
is a derivation on
\A
.

Observation 3.3
.

The biderivation associated with derivation on
\A
given by a Poisson bracket is always skew-symmetric.

From now on, we will often abbreviate the notation and refer to a Poisson algebra
(\A,\{-,-\},\cdot)
simply by
\A
, if the context is clear. Also, all the vector spaces, algebras (Poisson, Lie), and the corresponding linear maps (morphisms, derivations), will be considered over a general field
\Kbb
, with
\operatorname{char}\Kbb=0
, unless stated otherwise.

Hamiltonian derivations and Casimirs

Definition 4.1

(Hamiltonian derivations and Casimirs)
.

Let
\A
be a Poisson algebra and
a\in\A
. The derivation
X_{a}:=\{-,a\}\in\mathcal{D}(\A)
is called a Hamiltonian derivation with a Hamiltonian (or a Hamilton function)

X_{a}
. We denote by

\displaystyle\Ham(\A):=\{X_{a}\ |\ a\in\A\}

\A\to\Ham(\A)

Casimir element
. We denote by

\displaystyle\Cas(\A):=\{a\in\A|X_{a}=0\}

(12)

the set of all Casimir elements.

Definition 4.2
.

The (Poisson)

\operatorname{Z}_{P}(\A):=\{a\in\A\ |\ \{a,b\}=0\text{ for all }b\in\A\}

\Cas(\A)=\operatorname{Z}_{P}(\A)

For arbitrary monic polynomial

Graded Poisson algebras

Let
\A
be a Poisson algebra. We shall suppose that
\A
is an associative
gradded algebra
, that is,
\A
contains a set of vector subspaces
(\A^{k})_{k\in\Nbb_{0}}
s.t.
A=\bigoplus_{k\in\Nbb_{0}}\A^{k}
and
\A^{k}\cdot\A^{l}\subset\A^{k+l}
for all
k,l\in\Nbb_{0}
. Moreover, we assume
\A_{0}:=\Kbb
.

Definition 7.1
.

Let
d\in\Nbb_{0}
be arbitrary.
\A
is called a
graded Poisson algebra of degree
d
if
\forall a\in\A^{k},b\in\A^{l}:\{a,b\}\in\A^{k+l-d}
(for
n<0
define
A^{n}=0
).

The graded Poisson algebras can be constructed from non-commutative, associative, unital algebras
\U
, which are
filtered
:

\U=\bigcup_{k\in\Nbb_{0}}\U_{k},\text{ where }1\in\Kbb=\U_{0}\subset\U_{1}\subset\dots\subset\U_{k}\subset\dots.

To every such algebra
\U
, we can define the associated graded algebra
\S=\gr(\U)
,
\S=\bigoplus_{k\in\Nbb_{0}}\S_{k}
, where
\S_{k}:=\U_{k}/U_{k-1}
,
k\geq 1
, and
\S_{0}:=\U_{0}=\Kbb
. We denote by
\gr_{k}\colon\U_{k}\to\S_{k}
the canonical projections. Then
\U
is a graded Poisson algebra of degree
d\geq 1
if
uv-vu\in\U_{k+l-d}
, for all
u\in\U_{k},v\in\U_{l}
(define
\U_{k}=0
for
k<0
).

Proposition 7.1
.

The associated graded algebra
\S
given by the graded algebra
\U
of degree
d
is a graded Poisson algebra of degree
d
.

Proof.

Since the canonical projection
\gr_{k}\colon\U_{k}\to S_{k}
is a surjection, to each
a\in\S
exists
k\in\Nbb_{0}
so that
a\in\S_{k}
, thus there exists
u\in\U_{k}
s.t.
a=\gr_{k}(u)
. Let
b\in\S_{l}
and
v\in\U_{l}
s.t.
b=gr_{l}(v)
. The product in
\S
is defined by

ab=gr_{k+l}(uv)\in\S_{k+l}\ .

The product is well-defined, because if we pick different representatives, say
\tilde{a}=a+x,x\in\U_{k-1}
and
\tilde{b}=b+y,y\in\U_{l-1}
, then

\tilde{a}\tilde{b}=(a+x)(b+y)=ab+\underbrace{ay+xb+xy}_{\in\U_{k+l-1}}=ab\ .

The unit in
\S
is the same as in
\U
, and the associativity of
\S
is also inherited from the associativity of
\U
. So we see that
\S
is a graded algebra.
The Lie bracket is

\{a,b\}=\{\gr_{k}(u),\gr_{l}(v)\}:=gr_{k+l-d}(uv-vu)

and is of degree
d
. By a similar argument as for the product,
\{-,-\}
is well-defined on
\S
. Since
ab\in\S_{k+l}
and
ba\in\S_{l+k}=\S_{k+l}
, there exist
u,u^{\prime}\in\U_{k+l}
s.t.
ab=\gr_{k+l}(u),ba=\gr_{k+l}(u^{\prime})
and thus
ab-ba=\gr_{k+l}(u-u^{\prime})=0
. This shows that the product in
\S
is commutative, hence
\S
is Poisson graded of degree
d
.
∎

Example 7.1

(Lie algebra structure on
\Diff_{*}^{(+)}(\A)
)
.

Consider

Using the derivation property from lemma

\displaystyle\delta_{a}[\varphi,\psi]=[\delta_{a}\varphi,\psi]+[\varphi,\delta_{a}\psi]

acts as a derivation on the commutator. Suppose that

which does not have to vanish. Hence

is not a Lie algebra. Applying

\displaystyle\delta_{c}\circ\delta_{b}\circ\delta_{a}[\varphi,\psi]=0\ .

Thus

is of order
\leq k+l
, that is

Remark 7.2
.

The

Example 7.2

Let

\Kbb

•

\displaystyle\tau\colon V^{*}\times\ldots\times V^{*}\to\Kbb\ ,

\displaystyle T^{k}(V)\otimes T^{l}(V)\to T^{k+l}(V)\ .

The tensor algebra satisfies the following universal property: for every
\Kbb

\A
and arbitrary linear map
\psi\colon V\to\A

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces V}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces T(V)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\A}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\iota}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\psi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\exists!\tilde{\psi}}

Symmetric algebra of a vector space

Let

\Kbb

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces V}

Remark 8.1
.

Let us mention some useful properties about the above defined algebras.

•

[\tilde{s}]=[s],[\tilde{t}]=[t]

where the last equality follows from the fact that

•

•

•

•

Exterior algebra of a vector space

Poisson manifolds

10.1
Poisson structure on the cotangent bundle

Let
F,G\in C^{\infty}(T^{*}M)
be smooth functions on the cotangent bundle, and let
(q^{i},p_{i})
be local coordinates on
T^{*}M
. Define the
Poisson brackets
by

\displaystyle\{F,G\}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}(\frac{\del F}{\del p_{i}}\frac{\del G}{\del q^{i}}-\frac{\del F}{\del q^{i}}\frac{\del G}{\del p_{i}})\ .

(43)

This is a bilinear mapping (linear in both arguments
F
and
G
) which is
skew-symmetric

\displaystyle\{F,G\}=-\{G,F\}\,

(44)

satisfies the
Leibniz rule

\displaystyle\{F,GH\}=\{F,G\}H+G\{F,H\}\ ,

(45)

and the
Jacobi identity

\displaystyle\{F,\{G,H\}\}+\{G,\{H,F\}\}+\{H,\{F,G\}\}=0\ .

(46)

10.2
Poisson manifolds

Generalizing the above idea of Poisson brackets on the cotangent bundle, we can give the following definition.

Definition 10.1
.

A Poisson bracket on a manifold
M
is a bilinear mapping

\displaystyle\{-,-\}\colon C^{\infty}(M)\times C^{\infty}(M)\to C^{\infty}(M)\ ,

such that (
44
), (
45
), and (
46
) are satisfied. A manifold
M
equipped with a Poisson bracket is called a Poisson manifold.

Remark 10.1
.

Every
F\in C^{\infty}(M)
, the mapping
X_{F}\colon C^{\infty}(M)\to C^{\infty}(M)
, defined via the Poisson bracket as

\displaystyle X_{F}:=\{F,-\}\ ,

is a
derivation
on the algebra

. More precisely,

is a
1

. This field is called

with

F

10.3
Hamiltonian mapping

For every

, The Leibniz rule (

\displaystyle X_{FG}=FX_{G}+GX_{F}

and one can define a mapping

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}\colon T^{*}M\to TM

by

, or

\displaystyle<dG,\mathcal{H}(dF)>:=\{F,G\}\ ,

where the bracket on the left-hand side denotes the evaluation of the

\displaystyle H^{kl}:=\{x^{k},x^{l}\}\ .

Denoting

\displaystyle\{F,G\}=\sum_{k,l}H^{kl}\del_{k}F\del_{l}G\ ,

and the components of the vector field

\displaystyle X_{F}^{k}=\sum_{l}H^{lk}\del_{k}F\ .

Suppose the manifold is the cotangent bundle

and the local coordinates

are
(q^{i},p_{i})

\displaystyle H=\begin{pmatrix}0&-I_{n}\\

where

\displaystyle H^{kl}=-H^{lk}\ ,

and the Jacobi identity is

\displaystyle\sum_{i}(H^{ki}\del_{i}H^{lm}+H^{li}\del_{i}H^{mk}+H^{mi}\del_{i}H^{kl})=0\ .

If the matrix

\displaystyle\underline{\Omega}=-\frac{1}{6}\sum_{k,l,m}\Omega_{klm}dx^{k}\wedge dx^{l}\wedge dx^{m}\ ,

\displaystyle\Omega_{klm}:=\del_{k}\Omega_{lm}+\del_{l}\Omega_{mk}+\del_{m}\Omega_{kl}\ .

The above formula for

\displaystyle\Omega_{klm}:=\sum_{p,q,r}\Omega_{kp}\Omega_{lq}\Omega_{mr}\left(\sum_{i}\mathcal{H}^{pi}\del_{i}\mathcal{H}^{qr}+\mathcal{H}^{qi}\del_{i}\mathcal{H}^{rp}+\mathcal{H}^{ri}\del_{i}\mathcal{H}^{pq}\right)\ .

10.4
Poisson bracket on a symplectic manifold

Definition 10.2
.

A smooth manifold
M

2
-form
\Omega

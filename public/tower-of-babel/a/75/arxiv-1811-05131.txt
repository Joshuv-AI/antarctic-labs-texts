Sensitivity Analysis of a Stationary Point Set Map under Total Perturbations. Part 2: Robinson Stability
D. T. K. Huyen, J.-C. Yao, N. D. Yen

Abstract
In Part 1 of this paper, we have estimated the Fréchet coderivative and the Mordukhovich coderivative of the stationary point set map of a smooth parametric optimization problem with one smooth functional constraint under total perturbations. From these estimates, necessary and sufficient conditions for the local Lipschitz-like property of the map have been obtained. In this part, we establish sufficient conditions for the Robinson stability of the stationary point set map. This allows us to revisit and extend several stability theorems in indefinite quadratic programming. A comparison of our results with the ones which can be obtained via another approach is also given.

Introduction

Appeared at the early stage of optimization theory, smooth programming problems continue to attract common attention of the optimization community due to their importance and beauty. Polynomial optimization problems, including nonconvex quadratic programs, are typical examples of such problems.

The present paper investigates the Lipschitz-like property and the Robinson stability of the stationary point set map of a smooth parametric optimization problem with one smooth functional constraint under total perturbations.

In Part 1 of the paper
Huyen_Yao_Yen_2018a
, we have computed and estimated the Fréchet coderivative and the Mordukhovich coderivative of the stationary point set map by applying some theorems of Levy and Mordukhovich
LeMo04
and other related results. From the obtained formulas we derive necessary and sufficient conditions for the local Lipschitz-like property of the stationary point set map. This leads us to new insights into the preceding deep investigations of Levy and Mordukhovich in the just-cited paper and of Qui
Qui_JOTA2014
;
Qui_JoGO_2016
.

The reader is referred to Part 1 of this paper
Huyen_Yao_Yen_2018a
for a survey on the local Lipschitz-like property of multifunctions, the Robinson stability of an implicit multifunction, the Mordukhovich criterion for the local Lipschitz-like property of locally closed multifunctions, and some relevant material.

This part of the paper is organized as follows. Section 2 recalls some basic concepts from variational analysis, formulates the problem studied herein, and presents a series of auxiliary results in a unified form. In Section 3, we obtain sufficient conditions for the Robinson stability of the stationary point set map. Section 4 is devoted to several stability theorems in indefinite quadratic programming. A comparison of our results with the ones which can be obtained via Robinson’s theory of strongly regular generalized equations
Robinson_1980
is given in Section 5. The final section contains some concluding remarks.

Preliminaries

The scalar product and the norm in a finite-dimensional Euclidean space are denoted respectively by
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
and
\|\cdot\|
. The symbols
B(x,\rho)
and
\bar{B}(x,\rho)
stand for the open (resp., closed) ball centered at
x\in X
with radius
\rho>0
. The distance
\displaystyle\inf_{u\in A}\|x-u\|
from
x\in X
to a subset
A\subset X
is denoted by
d(x,A)
.

We now recall several basic concepts from variational analysis
B-M06
;
Rock_Wets_1998
which will be used intensively later on.

The
Fréchet normal cone
(also called the
prenormal
cone
, or the
regular normal cone
) to a set

\Omega\subset I\!\!R^{s}
at
\bar{v}\in\Omega
is given by

\displaystyle\widehat{N}_{\Omega}(\bar{v})=\left\{v^{\prime}\in I\!\!R^{s}\,\mid\,\displaystyle\limsup_{v\xrightarrow{\Omega}\bar{v}}\,\displaystyle\frac{\langle v^{\prime},v-\bar{v}\rangle}{\|v-\bar{v}\|}\leq 0\right\},

where

v\xrightarrow{\Omega}\bar{v}
means
v\to\bar{v}
with
v\in\Omega
. By
convention,
\widehat{N}_{\Omega}(\bar{v}):=\emptyset
when
\bar{v}\notin\Omega
.
Provided that
\Omega
is locally closed around
\bar{v}\in\Omega
, one calls

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{rl}N_{\Omega}(\bar{v})&=\displaystyle\mathop{{\rm Lim}\,{\rm sup}}_{v\to\bar{v}}\widehat{N}_{\Omega}(v)\\
&:=\big\{v^{\prime}\in I\!\!R^{s}\,\mid\,\exists\mbox{ sequences }v_{k}\to\bar{v},\ v_{k}^{\prime}\rightarrow v^{\prime},\\
&\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad\ \mbox{with }v_{k}^{\prime}\in\widehat{N}_{\Omega}(v_{k})\,\mbox{ for all }\,k=1,2,\dots\big\}\end{array}

the
Mordukhovich
(or
limiting/basic
)
normal cone
to
\Omega
at
\bar{v}
.
If
\bar{v}\notin\Omega
, then one puts
N_{\Omega}(\bar{v})=\emptyset
.

A multifunction
\Phi:I\!\!R^{n}\rightrightarrows I\!\!R^{m}
is
said to be
locally closed
around a point
\bar{z}=(\bar{x},\bar{y})
from
\mbox{\rm gph}\,\Phi:=\{(x,y)\in I\!\!R^{n}\times I\!\!R^{m}\,\mid\,y\in\Phi(x)\}
if
\mbox{\rm gph}\,\Phi
is locally closed around
\bar{z}
. Here, the product space

I\!\!R^{n+m}=I\!\!R^{n}\times I\!\!R^{m}
is equipped with the topology generated by the sum norm
\|(x,y)\|=\|x\|+\|y\|
.

For any
\bar{z}=(\bar{x},\bar{y})\in\mbox{gph}\,\Phi
,

\displaystyle\widehat{D}^{*}\Phi(\bar{z})(y^{\prime}):=\big\{x^{\prime}\in I\!\!R^{n}\,\mid\,(x^{\prime},-y^{\prime})\in\widehat{N}_{\mbox{gph}\,\Phi}(\bar{z})\big\}\quad(y^{\prime}\in I\!\!R^{m})

are called the
Fréchet coderivative
values of
\Phi
at
\bar{z}
. Similarly, the
Mordukhovich coderivative
(limiting coderivative) values of
\Phi
at
\bar{z}
are defined by

\displaystyle D^{*}\Phi(\bar{z})(y^{\prime}):=\big\{x^{\prime}\in I\!\!R^{n}\,\mid\,(x^{\prime},-y^{\prime})\in N_{\mbox{gph}\,\Phi}(\bar{z})\big\}\quad(y^{\prime}\in I\!\!R^{m}).

Thus,
\widehat{D}^{*}\Phi(\bar{z})
and
D^{*}\Phi(\bar{z})
are multifuntions from
I\!\!R^{m}
to
I\!\!R^{n}
. By
(
B-M06
, Theorem 1.38)
, if
\Phi
is strictly Fréchet differentiable at
\bar{x}
, then

\widehat{D}^{*}\Phi(\bar{x})(y^{\prime})=D^{*}\Phi(\bar{x})(y^{\prime})=\{\nabla\Phi(\bar{x})^{*}(y^{\prime})\}

for any
y^{\prime}\in I\!\!R^{m}
.

Suppose that
X
,
Y
, and
Z
are finite-dimensional Euclidean spaces. Consider a function
\psi:X\rightarrow\bar{I\!\!R}
with
|\psi(\bar{x})|<\infty
. The set

\partial\psi(\bar{x}):=\{x^{\prime}\in X^{*}\mid(x^{\prime},-1)\in N_{{\rm epi}\,\psi}(\bar{x},\psi(\bar{x}))\}

is the
Mordukhovich subdifferential
of
\psi
at
\bar{x}
. We put
\partial\psi(\bar{x})=\emptyset
if
|\psi(\bar{x})|=\infty
. The set

\partial^{\infty}\psi(\bar{x}):=\{x^{*}\in X^{*}\mid(x^{*},0)\in N_{{\rm epi}\,\psi}(\bar{x},\psi(\bar{x}))\}

is the
singular subdifferential
of
\psi
at
\bar{x}
. For a set
\Omega\subset X
and a point
\bar{x}\in\Omega
, we have

N_{\Omega}(\bar{x})=\partial\delta_{\Omega}(\bar{x})=\partial^{\infty}\delta_{\Omega}(\bar{x}),

where
\delta_{\Omega}(\bar{x})
is the indicator function of
\Omega
; see
(
B-M06
, Proposition 1.79)
. If
\psi
depends on two variables
x
and
y
, and
|\psi(\bar{x},\bar{y})|<\infty
, then
\partial_{x}\psi(\bar{x},\bar{y})
denotes the Mordukhovich subdifferential of
\psi(.,\bar{y})
at
\bar{x}
. For any
\bar{v}\in\partial\psi(\bar{x})
,

\partial^{2}\psi(\bar{x}|\bar{v})(u):=D^{*}(\partial\psi)(\bar{x}|\bar{v})(u)\quad(u\in X^{**}=X)

is the
limiting second-order subdifferential
(or the generalized Hessian).

A multifunction
G:Y\rightrightarrows X
is said to be
locally Lipschitz-like
around
(\bar{y},\bar{x})\in{\rm gph}\,G
if there exists a constant
\ell>0
and neighborhoods
U
of
\bar{x}
,
V
of
\bar{y}
such that

G(y^{\prime})\cap U\subset G(y)+\ell\|y^{\prime}-y\|\bar{B}_{X}\quad\forall y,y^{\prime}\in V,

where
\bar{B}_{X}
denotes the closed unit ball in
X
. When
G
is locally closed around
(\bar{y},\bar{x})
, the
Mordukhovich criterion
(see
B-M_TAMS_1993
,
(
Rock_Wets_1998
, Theorem 9.40)
, and
(
B-M06
, Theorem 4.10)
) says that
G
is locally Lipschitz-like around
(\bar{y},\bar{x})
if and only if

D^{*}G(\bar{y},\bar{x})(0)=\{0\}.

For a multifunction
F:X\times Y\rightrightarrows Z
and a pair
(\bar{x},\bar{y})\in X\times Y
satisfying
0\in F(\bar{x},\bar{y})
, we say that the
implicit multifunction

G:Y\rightrightarrows X
given by

G(y)=\{x\in X\mid 0\in F(x,y)\}
has the
Robinson stability
at
\omega_{0}:=(\bar{x},\bar{y},0)
if there exist constants
r>0
,
\gamma>0
, and neighborhoods
U
of
\bar{x}
,
V
of
\bar{y}
such that

d(x,G(y))\leq rd(0,F(x,y))

for any
(x,y)\in U\times V
with
d(0,F(x,y))<\gamma
.
Note that the condition
d(0,F(x,y))<\gamma
can be omitted if
F
is
inner semicontinuous
at
(\bar{x},\bar{y},0)
; see
Huyen_Yen_2016
. Note that, in some cases, the Robinson stability of
G
at
(\bar{x},\bar{y},0)
implies its local Lipschitz-likeness around
(\bar{y},\bar{x})
; see, e.g.,
Gfrerer_Mor_SIOPT2016
. For the generalized linear constraint system studied in
Huyen_Yen_2016
, these properties are equivalent. In the sequel, we will see that the regularity conditions in use guarantee for our stationary point set map to have both properties.

Now, let
f_{0}
and
F
be twice continuously differentiable real-valued functions (
C^{2}
-functions for brevity) defined on the product
I\!\!R^{n}\times I\!\!R^{d}
of two Euclidean spaces. For every
w\in I\!\!R^{d}
, we consider the parametric optimization problem

(P_{w})\quad\ \,{\rm Minimize}\ \,f_{0}(x,w)\ \;{\rm subject\ to}\ x\in I\!\!R^{n}\ \,{\rm and}\ \,F(x,w)\leq 0.

The constraint set of
(P_{w})
is
C(w):=\{x\in I\!\!R^{n}\mid F(x,w)\leq 0\}
. The stationary point set of
(P_{w})
is defined by

S(w)=\{x\in I\!\!R^{n}\mid 0\in\nabla_{x}f_{0}(x,w)+N_{C(w)}(x)\}.

(2)

When
w
varies on
I\!\!R^{d}
, one has a multifunction
S:I\!\!R^{d}\rightrightarrows I\!\!R^{n}
with
S(w)
being calculated by (
2
). Setting
f(x,w)=g(F(x,w))=(g\circ F)(x,w)
, where
g(y)=\delta_{I\!\!R_{-}}(y)
, i.e.,
g(y)=0
for
y\in(-\infty,0]
and
g(y)=+\infty
for
y>0
, we can rewrite (
2
) as

S(w)=\{x\in I\!\!R^{n}\mid 0\in\nabla_{x}f_{0}(x,w)+\partial_{x}f(x,w)\}.

(3)

Fix a vector
w=\bar{w}\in I\!\!R^{d}
and suppose that
\bar{x}\in S(\bar{w})
. Since
(P_{\bar{w}})
has a single smooth inequality constraint, the Mangasarian-Fromovitz Constraint Qualification is fulfilled at
\bar{x}\in C(\bar{w})
if and only if

\text{If}\ F(\bar{x},\bar{w})=0,\ \text{then}\ \nabla_{x}F(\bar{x},\bar{w})\neq 0.\quad\quad

(
MFCQ
)

In what follows, we assume that
(MFCQ)
is valid.
To study the stability of the stationary point set map
S
around the
(\bar{w},\bar{x})
in
{\rm gph}\,S
, we compute the Mordukhovich and the Fréchet coderivatives of the partial subdifferential map
\partial_{x}f:I\!\!R^{n}\times I\!\!R^{d}\rightrightarrows I\!\!R^{n}
. In general, there is no explicit formula for the coderivatives of such maps. However, the results of
LeMo04
provide us with some tools which allow us to estimate the coderivative value
D^{*}S(\bar{w}|\bar{x})(x^{\prime})
for every
x^{\prime}\in I\!\!R^{n}
.

The fulfillment of MFCQ at
(\bar{x},\bar{w})
implies that
g(x,w)=g(F(x,w))
is a strongly amenable in
x
at
\bar{x}
with compatible parameterization in
w
at
\bar{w}
. Then, by
(
Rock_Wets_1998
, Theorem 10.49)
, for
(x,w)
near
(\bar{x},\bar{w})
, we have

\partial f(x,w)=\nabla F(x,w)^{*}(\partial g(F(x,w)))

(4)

and

\partial_{x}f(x,w)=\nabla_{x}F(x,w)^{*}(\partial g(F(x,w)));

(5)

see
(
LeMo04
, formulas (14) and (15))
.

In order to estimate the limiting second-order subdifferential of
f
, we need the following result.

Lemma 2.1

(see
(
LeMo04
, Theorem 3.1)
)

Suppose that
\bar{v}\in\partial f(\bar{x},\bar{w})
. Then, for
any

\begin{array}[]{rl}&\partial^{2}f((\bar{x},\bar{w})|\bar{v})(v^{\prime})\\
&\subset\ \displaystyle\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\bar{y}\in\partial g(F(\bar{x},\bar{w}))\;\text{with}\\
\nabla F(\bar{x},\bar{w})^{*}\bar{y}=\bar{v}\end{subarray}}\bigg(\nabla^{2}(\bar{y}\cdot F)(\bar{x},\bar{w})v^{\prime}+D^{*}(\partial g\circ F)(\bar{x},\bar{w})|\bar{y})(\nabla F(\bar{x},\bar{w})v^{\prime})\bigg),\end{array}

\partial^{2}g(F(\bar{x},\bar{w})|\bar{y})(0)\cap{\rm ker}\nabla F(\bar{x},\bar{w})^{*}=\{0\},

(6)

&=\{u^{\prime}\in I\!\!R\mid(u^{\prime},0)\in N_{{\rm gph}\,\partial g}(F(\bar{x},\bar{w}),\bar{y})\}=\{0\}.\end{array}

For any

\partial^{2}f((\bar{x},\bar{w})|\bar{v})(v^{\prime})\subset\displaystyle\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\bar{y}\in\partial g(F(\bar{x},\bar{w}))\;\text{with}\\

\widehat{D}^{*}\widetilde{M}(\omega_{0})(v^{\prime}_{1})=\nabla G(\bar{x},\bar{w})^{*}v^{\prime}_{1}+\widehat{D}^{*}M(\bar{\tau})(v^{\prime}_{1})

for any
v^{\prime}_{1}\in I\!\!R^{n}
, where
\omega_{0}:=(\bar{x},\bar{w},0)\in{\rm gph}\,\widetilde{M}
. Therefore, we can write

\widehat{\Gamma}(x^{\prime})=\displaystyle\bigcup_{v^{\prime}_{1}\in I\!\!R^{n}}\left\{w^{\prime}\in I\!\!R^{d}\mid(-x^{\prime},w^{\prime})\in\widehat{D}^{*}\widetilde{M}(\omega_{0})(v^{\prime}_{1})\right\}.

Note that
0\in\widehat{\Gamma}(0)
. According to the Mordukhovich criterion, if
S
is locally Lipschitz-like around
(\bar{w},\bar{x})
, then
D^{*}S(\bar{w}|\bar{x})(0)=\{0\}
and
\widehat{\Gamma}(0)=\{0\}
as a result. In addition, if the constraint qualification (
C1
) is fulfilled, then Lemma
2.3
yields
D^{*}S(\bar{w}|\bar{x})(x^{\prime})\subset\Gamma(x^{\prime})
for any
x^{\prime}\in I\!\!R^{n}
.
In particular,
D^{*}S(\bar{w}|\bar{x})(0)\subset\Gamma(0)
. Hence, if (
C1
) is valid and
\Gamma(0)=\{0\}
, then

D^{*}S(\bar{w}|\bar{x})(0)=\{0\}.

So, due to the Mordukhovich criterion,
S
is locally Lipschitz-like around
(\bar{w},\bar{x})
. This idea has been presented in
LeeYen11AA
and we will follow it throughout this paper.

Put
\mathcal{D}=\{(x,w)\in I\!\!R^{n}\times I\!\!R^{d}\mid F(x,w)\leq 0\}
. If
F(\bar{x},\bar{w})<0
, then
(\bar{x},\bar{w})
is an
interior point
of
\mathcal{D}
. If
F(\bar{x},\bar{w})=0
, then
(\bar{x},\bar{w})
is a
boundary point
of
\mathcal{D}
. In the next two sections, we will consider separately these two possibilities of the reference point
(\bar{x},\bar{w})
. Remind that
\bar{w}\in I\!\!R^{d}
and
\bar{x}\in S(\bar{w})
are fixed and all the notations of this section are kept unchanged.

3
The Robinson Stability of the Stationary Point Set Map

Now we turn attention to the Robinson stability of the stationary point set map
S
of the problem
(P_{w})
. As in the preceding sections, we assume the fulfillment of the condition
(MFCQ)
, which requires that
\nabla_{x}F(\bar{x},\bar{w})\neq 0
whenever
F(\bar{x},\bar{w})=0
.

From
(
B-M06
, Theorem 1.62)
, we have a formula similar to (
2
):

D^{*}\widetilde{M}(\omega_{0})(v^{\prime}_{1})=\nabla G(\bar{x},\bar{w})^{*}v^{\prime}_{1}+D^{*}M(\bar{\tau})(v^{\prime}_{1})

(13)

for any
v^{\prime}_{1}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. So, condition (
C1
) can be rewritten as

{\rm ker}\,D^{*}\widetilde{M}(\omega_{0})=\{0\}.

By
(
Yen_Yao_NA_2009
, Theorem 3.1)
,
S
has the Robinson stability at
\omega_{0}=(\bar{x},\bar{w},0)\in{\rm gph}\,\widetilde{M}
if (
C1
) and the condition

\left\{w^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\mid\exists v^{\prime}_{1}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\ {\rm with}\ (0,w^{\prime})\in D^{*}\widetilde{M}(\omega_{0})(v^{\prime}_{1})\right\}=\{0\},\quad\quad

(
C2
)

is fulfilled. By (
13
) we can rewrite (
C2
) equivalently as

\left\{w^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\mid\exists v^{\prime}_{1}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\ {\rm with}\ (0,w^{\prime})\in\nabla G(\bar{x},\bar{w})^{*}v^{\prime}_{1}+D^{*}M(\bar{\tau})(v^{\prime}_{1})\right\}=\{0\}.

With
\Gamma(x^{\prime})
defined by (
2.3
), we can assert that (
C2
) is equivalent to the requirement
\Gamma(0)=\{0\}
. In the proof of
(
LeMo04
, Corollary 2.2)
, the authors have commented that the constraint qualification (
9
), which is equivalent to (
C0
), is stronger than (
C1
). Now we go back to three cases considered in Sects.
3
and
4
of Part 1.

First, for the case
(\bar{x},\bar{w})\in{\rm int}\,\mathcal{D}
, we have shown in Sect.
3
of Part 1 that
D^{*}M(\bar{\tau})(v^{\prime}_{1})=\{0\}
for any
v^{\prime}_{1}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. So, condition (
C2
) becomes

\left\{w^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\mid\exists v^{\prime}_{1}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\ {\rm with}\ (0,w^{\prime})\in\nabla G(\bar{x},\bar{w})^{*}v^{\prime}_{1}\right\}=\{0\}.

Since
\nabla G(\bar{x},\bar{w})^{*}v^{\prime}_{1}=(\nabla^{2}_{xx}f_{0}(\bar{x},\bar{w})v^{\prime}_{1},\nabla^{2}_{wx}f_{0}(\bar{x},\bar{w})v^{\prime}_{1})
, this is equivalent to

\left\{w^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\mid\exists v^{\prime}_{1}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\ {\rm with}\ \nabla^{2}_{xx}f_{0}(\bar{x},\bar{w})v^{\prime}_{1}=0,\;w^{\prime}=\nabla^{2}_{wx}f_{0}(\bar{x},\bar{w})v^{\prime}_{1}\right\}=\{0\}.

The latter can be rewritten as

{\rm ker}\,\nabla^{2}_{xx}f_{0}(\bar{x},\bar{w})\subset{\rm ker}\,\nabla^{2}_{wx}f_{0}(\bar{x},\bar{w}).

(14)

Besides, if the condition

{\rm ker}\,\nabla^{2}_{xx}f_{0}(\bar{x},\bar{w})\cap{\rm ker}\,\nabla^{2}_{wx}f_{0}(\bar{x},\bar{w})=\{0\}

(15)

is fulfilled, then (
C0
) is valid and (
C1
) is also valid as a result. Thus,
if (
15
) and (
14
) are simultaneously satisfied, then
S
has the Robinson stability at
\omega_{0}
.

Let us move to the next case where
F(\bar{x},\bar{w})=0
and the Lagrange multiplier
\lambda
corresponding to the stationary point
\bar{x}\in S(\bar{w})
is positive. First, it is worth to stress that for
(P_{w})
, the assumptions (i), (ii), and (10) in
(
LeMo04
, Proposition 2.1)
are fulfilled. So, from
(
LeMo04
, Corollary 2.1)
, for any
v^{\prime}_{1}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

D^{*}M(\bar{\omega})(v^{\prime}_{1})\subset\displaystyle\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\bar{v}\in\partial f(\bar{x},\bar{w})\;\text{with}\\
{\rm proj}_{1}\bar{v}=-\nabla_{x}f_{0}(\bar{x},\bar{w})\end{subarray}}\partial^{2}f((\bar{x},\bar{w})|\bar{v})(v^{\prime}_{1},0).

With
\Omega_{2}(v^{\prime}_{1})
defined by (
11
), using (
10
) we have

\Omega_{2}(v^{\prime}_{1})=\displaystyle\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\bar{v}\in\partial f(\bar{x},\bar{w})\;\text{with}\\
{\rm proj}_{1}\bar{v}=-\nabla_{x}f_{0}(\bar{x},\bar{w})\end{subarray}}\partial^{2}f((\bar{x},\bar{w})|\bar{v})(v^{\prime}_{1},0).

Hence,

D^{*}M(\bar{\omega})(v^{\prime}_{1})\subset\Omega_{2}(v^{\prime}_{1})
for any
v^{\prime}_{1}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Therefore, from formula (
2.3
) and the presentation
\nabla G(\bar{x},\bar{w})^{*}(v^{\prime}_{1})=\nabla^{2}f_{0}(\bar{x},\bar{w})^{*}(v^{\prime}_{1},0)
, we have

\Gamma(x^{\prime})\subset\left\{w^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\mid\exists v^{\prime}_{1}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\ {\rm with}\ (-x^{\prime},w^{\prime})-\nabla^{2}f_{0}(\bar{x},\bar{w})^{*}(v^{\prime}_{1},0)\in\Omega_{2}(v^{\prime}_{1})\right\}

(16)

for any
x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. This implies that
\Gamma(x^{\prime})
is contained in
\Gamma_{2}(x^{\prime})
which is defined in Subsect.
4.1
of Part 1. In particular,
\Gamma(0)\subset\Gamma_{2}(0)
. So, if
\Gamma_{2}(0)=\{0\}
, then
\Gamma(0)=\{0\}
. We have shown that
\Gamma_{2}(0)=\{0\}
if and only if the inclusion

{\rm ker}\,A_{1}\cap\left({\rm ker}\,\nabla_{x}F(\bar{x},\bar{w})\times I\!\!R\right)\subset{\rm ker}\,A_{2}.

(17)

is valid. Therefore, if (
17
) is satisfied, then
\Gamma(0)=\{0\}
which implies the fulfillment of (
C2
).
Let

A_{1}=\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}\nabla^{2}_{xx}f_{0}(\bar{x},\bar{w})+\lambda\nabla^{2}_{xx}F(\bar{x},\bar{w})&\ \nabla_{x}F(\bar{x},\bar{w})\end{array}\right]\in I\!\!R^{n\times(n+1)}

(18)

and

A_{2}=\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}\nabla^{2}_{wx}f_{0}(\bar{x},\bar{w})+\lambda\nabla^{2}_{wx}F(\bar{x},\bar{w})&\ \nabla_{w}F(\bar{x},\bar{w})\end{array}\right]\in I\!\!R^{d\times(n+1)},

(19)

where
\nabla_{x}F(\bar{x},\bar{w})
and
\nabla_{w}F(\bar{x},\bar{w})
are interpreted as column vectors. If the equality

{\rm ker}\,A_{1}\cap{\rm ker}\,A_{2}\cap\left({\rm ker}\,\nabla_{x}F(\bar{x},\bar{w})\times I\!\!R\right)=\{(0,0)\}.

(20)

is satisfied then, as shown in Subsect.
4.1
of Part 1, (
C0
) is fulfilled; consequently, (
C1
) is valid. Thus,
in the case under our consideration, once (
20
) and (
17
) are simultaneously satisfied,
S
has the Robinson stability at
\omega_{0}
.

Finally, we consider the case where
F(\bar{x},\bar{w})=0
and the Lagrange multiplier
\lambda
corresponding to the stationary point
\bar{x}\in S(\bar{w})
equals to zero. In this case, if

{\rm ker}\,A^{\prime}_{1}\cap{\rm ker}\,A^{\prime}_{2}=\{0\},

(21)

where

A^{\prime}_{1}:=\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}\nabla^{2}_{xx}f_{0}(\bar{x},\bar{w})&\ \nabla_{x}F(\bar{x},\bar{w})\end{array}\right]\in I\!\!R^{n\times(n+1)},

and

A^{\prime}_{2}:=\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}\nabla^{2}_{wx}f_{0}(\bar{x},\bar{w})&\ \nabla_{w}F(\bar{x},\bar{w})\end{array}\right]\in I\!\!R^{d\times(n+1)},

is valid, then (
C0
) holds (see Subsect.
4.2
of Part 1). So, (
21
) guarantees the validity of (
C1
). Concerning condition (
C2
), we will show that if the conditions

{\rm ker}\,A^{\prime}_{1}\cap\left({\rm ker}\,\nabla_{x}F(\bar{x},\bar{w})\times I\!\!R\right)\subset{\rm ker}\,A^{\prime}_{2},

(22)

{\rm ker}\,A^{\prime}_{1}\cap\Delta_{1}\subset{\rm ker}\,A^{\prime}_{2},

(23)

and

{\rm ker}\,\nabla^{2}_{xx}f_{0}(\bar{x},\bar{w})\cap\Delta_{2}\subset{\rm ker}\,\nabla^{2}_{wx}f_{0}(\bar{x},\bar{w}).

(24)

are satisfied, then (
C2
) is fulfilled.

Let
\Gamma_{3}(x^{\prime})
be the set of vectors
w^{\prime}\in I\!\!R^{d}
for which there exists
v^{\prime}_{1}\in I\!\!R^{n}
with

\begin{cases}\left(-x^{\prime}-\nabla^{2}_{xx}f_{0}(\bar{x},\bar{w})v^{\prime}_{1},w^{\prime}-\nabla^{2}_{wx}f_{0}(\bar{x},\bar{w})v^{\prime}_{1}\right)\in\{\gamma\nabla F(\bar{x},\bar{w})\mid\gamma\in I\!\!R\},\\
\nabla_{x}F(\bar{x},\bar{w})v^{\prime}_{1}=0,\end{cases}

or

\begin{cases}\left(-x^{\prime}-\nabla^{2}_{xx}f_{0}(\bar{x},\bar{w})v^{\prime}_{1},w^{\prime}-\nabla^{2}_{wx}f_{0}(\bar{x},\bar{w})v^{\prime}_{1}\right)\in\{\gamma\nabla F(\bar{x},\bar{w})\mid\gamma\in I\!\!R_{+}\},\\
\nabla_{x}F(\bar{x},\bar{w})v^{\prime}_{1}>0,\end{cases}

or

\begin{cases}-x^{\prime}-\nabla^{2}_{xx}f_{0}(\bar{x},\bar{w})v^{\prime}_{1}=0,\ \,w^{\prime}-\nabla^{2}_{wx}f_{0}(\bar{x},\bar{w})v^{\prime}_{1}=0,\\
\nabla_{x}F(\bar{x},\bar{w})v^{\prime}_{1}<0.\end{cases}

As it has been proved in Subsect.
4.2
of Part 1,

\Omega_{2}(v^{\prime}_{1})\subset\begin{cases}\{\gamma\nabla F(\bar{x},\bar{w})\mid\gamma\in I\!\!R\}&\mbox{ if }\nabla_{x}F(\bar{x},\bar{w})v^{\prime}_{1}=0,\\
\{\gamma\nabla F(\bar{x},\bar{w})\mid\gamma\in\mathbb{R_{+}}\}&\mbox{ if }\nabla_{x}F(\bar{x},\bar{w})v^{\prime}_{1}>0,\\
\{0\}&\mbox{ if }\nabla_{x}F(\bar{x},\bar{w})v^{\prime}_{1}<0.\end{cases}

(25)

From (
16
) and the inclusion (
25
) we have
\Gamma(x^{\prime})\subset\Gamma_{3}(x^{\prime})
for any
x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. In particular,
\Gamma(0)\subset\Gamma_{3}(0)
. Hence, if
\Gamma_{3}(0)=\{0\}
, then
\Gamma(0)=\{0\}
. We have shown that
\Gamma_{3}(0)=\{0\}
holds if and only if the system (
22
)–(
24
) is satisfied. Thus, the validity of (
22
)–(
24
) implies
\Gamma(0)=\{0\}
which yields the fulfillment of (
C2
). Therefore,
if (
21
) and the system (
22
)–(
24
) are simultaneously satisfied, then
S
has the Robinson stability at
\omega_{0}
.

We have thus shown that the sufficient conditions for
S
being locally Lipschitz-like around
(\bar{w},\bar{x})
in each case also guarantee for
S
having the Robinson stability at
\omega_{0}
.

Our results on the Robinson stability of
S
are summarized as follows.

Theorem 3.1

The stationary point set map
S
of
(P_{w})
has the Robinson stability at
\omega_{0}=(\bar{x},\bar{w},0)
if one of the following is valid:

(a)

F(\bar{x},\bar{w})<0
and the condition

{\rm ker}\,\nabla^{2}_{xx}f_{0}(\bar{x},\bar{w})=\{0\}

(26)

holds;

(b)

F(\bar{x},\bar{w})=0
, the Lagrange multiplier
\lambda
corresponding to the stationary point
\bar{x}\in S(\bar{w})
is positive, and

{\rm ker}\,A_{1}\cap\left({\rm ker}\,\nabla_{x}F(\bar{x},\bar{w})\times I\!\!R\right)=\{0\};

(27)

(c)

F(\bar{x},\bar{w})=0
, the Lagrange multiplier
\lambda
corresponding to the stationary point
\bar{x}\in S(\bar{w})
equals to zero, and

\begin{cases}{\rm ker}\,A^{\prime}_{1}\cap{\rm ker}\,A^{\prime}_{2}=\{0\},\\
{\rm ker}\,A^{\prime}_{1}\cap\left({\rm ker}\,\nabla_{x}F(\bar{x},\bar{w})\times I\!\!R\right)\subset{\rm ker}\,A^{\prime}_{2},\\
{\rm ker}\,A^{\prime}_{1}\cap\Delta_{1}\subset{\rm ker}\,A^{\prime}_{2},\\
{\rm ker}\,\nabla^{2}_{xx}f_{0}(\bar{x},\bar{w})\cap\Delta_{2}\subset{\rm ker}\,\nabla^{2}_{wx}f_{0}(\bar{x},\bar{w}).\end{cases}

(28)

It is worthy to stress that the Robinson stability of
S
at
\omega_{0}
is available for the examples of the previous section where our sufficient conditions for the local Lipschitz-likeness of
S
around
(\bar{w},\bar{x})
are fulfilled.

4
Applications to Quadratic Programming

In this section, the above general results are applied to a class of nonconvex quadratic programming problems. Namely, we will consider the problems of minimizing a linear-quadratic function under one linear-quadratic functional constraint. Special cases of such problems have been considered, e.g., in
LeeTamYen_SIOPT2012
,
LeeYen14NA
, and
QuiYen_SIOPT2014
.

Denote by
\mathcal{S}_{n}
the space of
n\times n
symmetric matrices. Let
D,A\in\mathcal{S}_{n}
,
c
and
b
be vectors in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and
\alpha
a real number. Put
w=(w_{1},w_{2})
with
w_{1}:=(D,c)
and
w_{2}:=(A,b,\alpha)
. Denote the problem
(P_{w})
with

f_{0}(x,w)=\frac{1}{2}x^{T}Dx+c^{T}x
and
F(x,w)=\frac{1}{2}x^{T}Ax+b^{T}x+\alpha
by
(QP_{w})
. For convenience, we put
W_{1}=\mathcal{S}_{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
W_{2}=\mathcal{S}_{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}
, and
W=W_{1}\times W_{2}
.
Fix a vector
\bar{w}=(\bar{w}_{1},\bar{w}_{2})\in W
with
\bar{w}_{1}=(\bar{D},\bar{c})
,
\bar{w}_{2}=(\bar{A},\bar{b},\bar{\alpha})
, and suppose that a stationary point
\bar{x}\in S(\bar{w})
is given.

To ease the description of certain second order differential operators, sometimes we will present the matrices
D
and
A
in the following column forms

D=\left(\begin{matrix}d^{T}_{1}\\
d^{T}_{2}\\
\vdots\\
d^{T}_{n}\end{matrix}\right),\quad A=\left(\begin{matrix}a^{T}_{1}\\
a^{T}_{2}\\
\vdots\\
a^{T}_{n}\end{matrix}\right),

where
d_{i}=(d_{i1}\dots d_{in})
and
a_{i}=(a_{i1}\dots a_{in})
are, respectively, the
i
-th row of
D
and the
i
-th row of
A
. We have
\nabla_{x}f_{0}(\bar{x},\bar{w})=\bar{D}\bar{x}+\bar{c}
,

\nabla_{w_{1}}f_{0}(\bar{x},\bar{w})=\left(\begin{matrix}\frac{1}{2}\bar{x}_{1}\bar{x}_{1}\ \dots\ \frac{1}{2}\bar{x}_{1}\bar{x}_{n}\ \dots\ \frac{1}{2}\bar{x}_{n}\bar{x}_{1}\ \dots\ \frac{1}{2}\bar{x}_{n}\bar{x}_{n}\ \bar{x}_{1}\ \dots\ \bar{x}_{n}\end{matrix}\right)^{T},

\nabla^{2}_{xx}f_{0}(\bar{x},\bar{w})=\bar{D}
,
\nabla^{2}_{w_{2}x}f_{0}(\bar{x},\bar{w})=0_{W_{2}}
, and

\nabla^{2}_{w_{1}x}f_{0}(\bar{x},\bar{w})=\begin{pmatrix}\bar{X}&\dots&0\\
&\ddots\\
0&\dots&\bar{X}\\
1&\dots&0\\
&\ddots\\
0&\dots&1\end{pmatrix}.

Here,
\bar{X}:=\begin{pmatrix}\bar{x}_{1}\\
\vdots\\
\bar{x}_{n}\end{pmatrix}
is an
n\times 1
matrix.
Similarly,
\nabla_{x}F(\bar{x},\bar{w})=\bar{A}\bar{x}+\bar{b}
,
\nabla^{2}_{xx}F(\bar{x},\bar{w})=\bar{A}
,

\nabla_{w_{2}}F(\bar{x},\bar{w})=\left(\begin{matrix}\frac{1}{2}\bar{x}_{1}\bar{x}_{1}\ \dots\ \frac{1}{2}\bar{x}_{1}\bar{x}_{n}\ \dots\ \frac{1}{2}\bar{x}_{n}\bar{x}_{1}\ \dots\ \frac{1}{2}\bar{x}_{n}\bar{x}_{n}\ \bar{x}_{1}\ \dots\ \bar{x}_{n}\ 1\end{matrix}\right)^{T},

\nabla^{2}_{w_{1}x}F(\bar{x},\bar{w})=0_{W_{1}}
, and

\nabla^{2}_{w_{2}x}F(\bar{x},\bar{w})=\begin{pmatrix}\bar{X}&\dots&0\\
&\ddots\\
0&\dots&\bar{X}\\
1&\dots&0\\
&\ddots\\
0&\dots&1\\
0&\dots&0\end{pmatrix}.

We have

\nabla^{2}_{wx}f_{0}(\bar{x},\bar{w})=\left(\begin{matrix}\nabla^{2}_{w_{1}x}f_{0}(\bar{x},\bar{w})\ \ \nabla^{2}_{w_{2}x}f_{0}(\bar{x},\bar{w})\end{matrix}\right).

Since
\nabla^{2}_{w_{2}x}f_{0}(\bar{x},\bar{w})=0
,

{\rm ker}\,\nabla^{2}_{wx}f_{0}(\bar{x},\bar{w})=\left\{v^{\prime}_{1}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mid\nabla^{2}_{w_{1}x}f_{0}(\bar{x},\bar{w})v^{\prime}_{1}=0\right\}=\{0\}.

First, we consider the case of interior points
(\bar{x},\bar{w})
, i.e.,
F(\bar{x},\bar{w})<0
. The conditions (
15
), (
14
), and (
26
) are equivalent due to
{\rm ker}\,\nabla^{2}_{wx}f_{0}(\bar{x},\bar{w})=\{0\}
. Thus, by Theorem 3.1 of Part 1, the stationary point set map
S
of
(P_{w})
is locally Lipschitz-like around
(\bar{w},\bar{x})
if and only if
{\rm ker}\,\nabla^{2}_{xx}f_{0}(\bar{x},\bar{w})=\{0\}
, or
{\rm ker}\,\bar{D}=\{0\}
. In other words,
S
is locally Lipschitz-like around
(\bar{w},\bar{x})
if and only if matrix
\bar{D}
is nonsingular. By Theorem
3.1
, this condition is sufficient for
S
having the Robinson stability at
\omega_{0}
.

Next, consider the second case where
(\bar{x},\bar{w})
is a boundary point of
\mathcal{D}
and the Lagrange multiplier
\lambda
corresponding to
\bar{x}\in S(\bar{w})
is positive. As in Part 1,
\lambda
is defined by

\nabla_{x}f_{0}(\bar{x},\bar{w})+\lambda\nabla_{x}F(\bar{x},\bar{w})=0,

which is rewritten as

\lambda(\bar{A}\bar{x}+\bar{b})=-(\bar{D}\bar{x}+\bar{c}).

(29)

We have

\nabla^{2}_{xx}f_{0}(\bar{x},\bar{w})+\lambda\nabla^{2}_{xx}F(\bar{x},\bar{w})=\bar{D}+\lambda\bar{A}
and

\nabla^{2}_{wx}f_{0}(\bar{x},\bar{w})+\lambda\nabla^{2}_{wx}F(\bar{x},\bar{w})\\
=\begin{pmatrix}\bar{X}&\dots&0\\
&\ddots\\
0&\dots&\bar{X}\\
1&\dots&0\\
&\ddots\\
0&\dots&1\\
\lambda\bar{X}&\dots&0\\
&\ddots\\
0&\dots&\lambda\bar{X}\\
\lambda&\dots&0\\
&\ddots\\
0&\dots&\lambda\\
0&\dots&0\end{pmatrix}.

Now, the matrices
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
defined in Sect.
3
are described as follows

A_{1}=\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}\bar{D}+\lambda\bar{A}&\bar{A}\bar{x}+\bar{b}\end{array}\right]

and

A_{2}=\begin{pmatrix}\bar{X}&\dots&0&0\\
&\ddots&&\vdots\\
0&\dots&\bar{X}&0\\
1&\dots&0&0\\
&\ddots&&\vdots\\
0&\dots&1&0\\
\lambda\bar{X}&\dots&0&\frac{1}{2}\bar{x}_{1}\bar{x}_{1}\\
&\ddots&&\vdots\\
0&\dots&\lambda\bar{X}&\frac{1}{2}\bar{x}_{1}\bar{x}_{n}\\
\lambda&\dots&0&\bar{x}_{1}\\
&\ddots&&\vdots\\
0&\dots&\lambda&\bar{x}_{n}\\
0&\dots&0&1\end{pmatrix}.

Hence,
{\rm ker}\,A_{2}=\{0\}
. This implies that
(
20
) is automatically satisfied. So, according to Theorem 4.1 of Part 1,
S
is locally Lipschitz-like around
(\bar{w},\bar{x})
if and only if (
17
) is fulfilled. Note that

{\rm ker}\,A_{1}=\{(v^{\prime}_{1},\gamma)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}\mid(\bar{D}+\lambda\bar{A})v^{\prime}_{1}+\gamma(\bar{A}\bar{x}+\bar{b})=0\}

and

{\rm ker\,}\nabla_{x}F(\bar{x},\bar{w})=\{v^{\prime}_{1}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mid(\bar{A}\bar{x}+\bar{b})^{T}v^{\prime}_{1}=0\}.

Hence, (
17
) holds if and only if

\begin{cases}(\bar{D}+\lambda\bar{A})v^{\prime}_{1}+\gamma(\bar{A}\bar{x}+\bar{b})=0\\
(\bar{A}\bar{x}+\bar{b})^{T}v^{\prime}_{1}=0\\
\end{cases}\qquad\ \Longrightarrow\ \,\begin{cases}v^{\prime}_{1}=0\\
\gamma=0\end{cases}

or, equivalently,

\det\begin{pmatrix}\bar{D}+\lambda\bar{A}&\bar{A}\bar{x}+\bar{b}\\
(\bar{A}\bar{x}+\bar{b})^{T}&0\end{pmatrix}\neq 0.

(30)

Thus,
S
is locally Lipschitz-like around
(\bar{w},\bar{x})
if and only if (
30
) is satisfied. Moreover, by Theorem
3.1
, (
30
) guarantees for
S
having the Robinson stability at
\omega_{0}
.

\gamma=0,\end{cases}

(31)

[\bar{D}v^{\prime}_{1}+\gamma(\bar{A}\bar{x}+\bar{b})=0,\ \gamma\geq 0]\ \;\Longrightarrow\ \;(\bar{A}\bar{x}+\bar{b})^{T}v^{\prime}_{1}\leq 0,

Theorem 4.1

The following assertions are true:

If

(\bar{w},\bar{x})

\bar{x}\in S(\bar{w})

)–(

Following the detailed hints of one referee of this paper, we will compare our results with those which can be obtained by using the theory of strongly regular generalized equations of Robinson
Robinson_1980

Suppose that
\bar{x}\in S(\bar{w})
and the condition
(MFCQ)
is satisfied. It is not difficult to show that, thanks to

, there exist a neighborhood

of

such that

-F(x,w)\end{matrix}\right)+N_{I\!\!R^{n}\times I\!\!R_{+}}(x,\alpha),

and denote its solution set by

defined by (

). (The writing of the necessary optimality condition of a constrained smooth mathematical programming problem in a form similar to (
37

(
Robinson_1980
, p. 54)
.) From what has been said we have

, p. 45)

\bar{x}\in S(\bar{w})
is zero. If (
4a
)–(
4c
) are satisfied, then
S
has a Lipschitz continuous single-valued localization around
\bar{w}
for
\bar{x}
.

The result stated in Proposition
5.2
is better than assertion (b) of Theorem 4.2 in Part 1, which says that if (

Proof of Lemma
5.1
By the definition of Robinson
(
Robinson_1980
, p. 45)
, the strong regularity of (
37
) at
(\bar{x},\lambda)
is identical to the strong regularity of the affine variational inequality

0\in A\left(\begin{matrix}x\\
\alpha\end{matrix}\right)+\bar{q}+N_{I\!\!R^{n}\times I\!\!R_{+}}(x,\alpha),

(42)

where

A:=\nabla_{(x,\alpha)}g(\bar{x},\lambda,\bar{w})=\begin{pmatrix}\nabla_{xx}^{2}{\mathcal{L}}(\bar{x},\lambda,\bar{w})&\ \;\nabla_{x}F(\bar{x},\bar{w})\\
-\nabla_{x}F(\bar{x},\bar{w})^{T}&\ \;0\end{pmatrix}

(43)

and

\bar{q}:=g(\bar{x},\lambda,\bar{w})-A\left(\begin{matrix}\bar{x}\\
\lambda\end{matrix}\right),

at
(\bar{x},\lambda)
.

According to
(
DR_1996
, Theorem 1)
, the affine variational inequality (
42
) is strongly regular at
(\bar{x},\lambda)
if and only if
the multifunction
L:I\!\!R^{n}\times I\!\!R\rightrightarrows I\!\!R^{n}\times I\!\!R
with

L(q):=\left\{\left(\begin{matrix}x\\
\alpha\end{matrix}\right)\,\mid\,0\in A\left(\begin{matrix}x\\
\alpha\end{matrix}\right)+q+N_{I\!\!R^{n}\times I\!\!R_{+}}(x,\alpha)\right\}

is locally Lipschitz-like around
(\bar{q},(\bar{x},\lambda))
. Furthermore, applying
(
DR_1996
, Theorem 2)
, we can assert that the latter is valid iff the
critical face condition
holds at
(\bar{q},(\bar{x},\lambda))
, i.e.,
for any choice of closed faces
F_{1}
and
F_{2}
of the critical cone
K_{0}
with
F_{1}\supset F_{2}
,

\big[u\in F_{1}-F_{2},\ \,A^{T}u\in(F_{1}-F_{2})^{*}\big]\ \;\Longrightarrow\ \;u=0.

(44)

Here,

K_{0}=K((\bar{x},\lambda),v_{0}):=\big\{(x^{\prime},\alpha^{\prime})\in T_{I\!\!R^{n}\times I\!\!R_{+}}(\bar{x},\lambda)\mid(x^{\prime},\alpha^{\prime})\perp v_{0}\big\},

with

v_{0}:=-A\left(\begin{matrix}\bar{x}\\
\lambda\end{matrix}\right)-\bar{q}\in N_{I\!\!R^{n}\times I\!\!R_{+}}(\bar{x},\lambda).

Recall that a convex subset
F
of a convex set
C\subset I\!\!R^{p}
is a
face
of
C
if every closed line segment in
C
with a relative interior point in
F
has both endpoints in
F
. When
K_{0}
is a linear subspace of
I\!\!R^{n}\times I\!\!R
, it has a unique closed face, namely itself. Then, the critical face condition is reduced to

\big[u\in K_{0},\ \,A^{T}u\perp K_{0}\big]\ \;\Longrightarrow\ \;u=0.

(45)

In the case
\lambda>0
, the critical face is equivalent to the nonsingularity of the matrix in (
38
). Indeed,
the condition
\lambda>0
implies
N_{I\!\!R^{n}\times I\!\!R_{+}}(\bar{x},\lambda)=\{(0,0)\}
,

T_{I\!\!R^{n}\times I\!\!R_{+}}(\bar{x},\lambda)=I\!\!R^{n}\times I\!\!R
, and
v_{0}=(0,0)
. It follows that
K_{0}=I\!\!R^{n}\times I\!\!R
. So, the critical face is reduced to (
45
), which is

A^{T}u=0\ \;\Longrightarrow\ \;u=0.

The latter means that
A
is nonsingular; or, equivalently, the matrix (
38
) is nonsingular.

Thus, we have proved that the generalized equation (
37
) is strongly regular at
(\bar{x},\lambda)
iff the matrix (
38
) is nonsingular.
\hfill\Box

Proof of Lemma
5.2
.
The arguments described in the beginning of the proof of Lemma
5.1
show that the generalized equation (
37
) is strongly regular at
(\bar{x},\lambda)
iff the critical face condition holds at
(\bar{q},(\bar{x},\lambda))
, i.e., for any choice of closed faces
F_{1}
and
F_{2}
of the critical cone
K_{0}
with
F_{1}\supset F_{2}
the condition (
44
) is fulfilled.

Since
\lambda=0
,
N_{I\!\!R^{n}\times I\!\!R_{+}}(\bar{x},\lambda)=\{0\}\times I\!\!R_{-}
, and

T_{I\!\!R^{n}\times I\!\!R_{+}}(\bar{x},\lambda)=I\!\!R^{n}\times I\!\!R_{+}.

As
v_{0}\in N_{I\!\!R^{n}\times I\!\!R_{+}}(\bar{x},\lambda),
there are two situations: (a)

v_{0}=(0,\beta)
with
\beta<0
; (b)
v_{0}=(0,0)
. If (a) occurs, then
K_{0}=I\!\!R^{n}\times\{0\}
. Since
K_{0}
is a linear subspace, the critical face condition is reduced to (
45
). Using the formula for
A
in (
43
), one can easily show that (
45
) is equivalent to the requirement that the matrix
\nabla_{xx}^{2}{\mathcal{L}}(\bar{x},\lambda,\bar{w})
is nonsingular. As
\lambda=0
, one has
\nabla_{xx}^{2}{\mathcal{L}}(\bar{x},\lambda,\bar{w})=\nabla_{xx}^{2}f_{0}(\bar{x},\bar{w})
. So, (
45
) is also equivalent to the condition saying that the matrix
\nabla_{xx}^{2}f_{0}(\bar{x},\bar{w})
is nonsingular. Now, suppose that the situation (b) occurs. Then,

K_{0}=K((\bar{x},\lambda),v_{0})=I\!\!R^{n}\times I\!\!R_{+}.

Obviously,
K_{0}
has only two nonempty faces:
I\!\!R^{n}\times\{0\}
and
I\!\!R^{n}\times I\!\!R_{+}
.

For
F_{1}=F_{2}=I\!\!R^{n}\times\{0\},
one has
F_{1}-F_{2}=I\!\!R^{n}\times\{0\}
. Then,

(F_{1}-F_{2})^{*}=\{0\}\times I\!\!R

and (
44
) is satisfied iff, for any
u^{\prime}\in I\!\!R^{n}
,

\nabla_{xx}^{2}{\mathcal{L}}(\bar{x},\lambda,\bar{w})u^{\prime}=0\ \;\Longrightarrow\ \;u^{\prime}=0.

As
\lambda=0
, it holds that
\nabla_{xx}^{2}{\mathcal{L}}(\bar{x},\lambda,\bar{w})=\nabla_{xx}^{2}f_{0}(\bar{x},\bar{w}).

Therefore, (
44
) is valid iff, for any
u^{\prime}\in I\!\!R^{n}
,

\nabla_{xx}^{2}f_{0}(\bar{x},\bar{w})u^{\prime}=0\ \;\Longrightarrow\ \;u^{\prime}=0.

This is equivalent to saying that
\nabla_{xx}^{2}f_{0}(\bar{x},\bar{w})
is nonsingular.

For
F_{1}=F_{2}=I\!\!R^{n}\times I\!\!R_{+}
,
F_{1}-F_{2}=I\!\!R^{n}\times I\!\!R
. Then,
(F_{1}-F_{2})^{*}=\{0\}\times\{0\}
and (
44
) is satisfied iff the matrix

A^{T}=\begin{pmatrix}\nabla_{xx}^{2}f_{0}(\bar{x},\bar{w})&\ \;-\nabla_{x}F(\bar{x},\bar{w})\\
\nabla_{x}F(\bar{x},\bar{w})^{T}&\ \;0\end{pmatrix}

is nonsingular, or,
A
is nonsingular.

For
F_{1}=I\!\!R^{n}\times I\!\!R_{+}
and
F_{2}=I\!\!R^{n}\times\{0\}
,
F_{1}-F_{2}=I\!\!R^{n}\times I\!\!R_{+}
and
(F_{1}-F_{2})^{*}=\{0\}\times I\!\!R_{-}
. Then, (
44
) is fulfilled iff

\begin{cases}\nabla_{xx}^{2}f_{0}(\bar{x},\bar{w})u^{\prime}-\gamma\nabla_{x}F(\bar{x},\bar{w})=0\\
\nabla_{x}F(\bar{x},\bar{w})^{T}u^{\prime}\leq 0\\
u^{\prime}\in I\!\!R^{n},\ \gamma\geq 0\end{cases}\ \;\Longrightarrow\ \;\begin{cases}u^{\prime}=0\\
\gamma=0.\end{cases}

(46)

The proof of the “necessity part” of Lemma
5.2
will be completed if we can show that (
39
) is valid. If (
39
) does not hold, then by putting

u^{\prime}=\nabla_{xx}^{2}f_{0}(\bar{x},\bar{w})^{-1}\nabla_{x}F(\bar{x},\bar{w}),

we have

\nabla_{x}F(\bar{x},\bar{w})^{T}u^{\prime}=\nabla_{x}F(\bar{x},\bar{w})^{T}\nabla_{xx}^{2}f_{0}(\bar{x},\bar{w})^{-1}\nabla_{x}F(\bar{x},\bar{w})\leq 0.

So, for
\gamma=1
, one has

\begin{cases}\nabla_{xx}^{2}f_{0}(\bar{x},\bar{w})u^{\prime}-\gamma\nabla_{x}F(\bar{x},\bar{w})=0\\
\nabla_{x}F(\bar{x},\bar{w})^{T}u^{\prime}\leq 0\\
u^{\prime}\in I\!\!R^{n},\ \gamma\geq 0.\end{cases}

This contradicts (
46
). We have thus proved (
39
) is valid.

To prove the “sufficiency part” of Lemma
5.2
, we suppose that the matrix
\nabla_{xx}^{2}{\mathcal{L}}(\bar{x},\lambda,\bar{w})=\nabla_{xx}^{2}f_{0}(\bar{x},\bar{w})

is nonsingular and (
39
) is fulfilled. To verify the fulfillment of the critical face condition at
(\bar{q},(\bar{x},\lambda))
, we need only to show that the matrix
A
is nonsingular and the implication (
46
) holds.

To obtain the nonsingularity of
A
, suppose to the contrary that there exists a pair
(u^{\prime},\gamma)\neq(0,0)
satisfying

\begin{cases}\nabla_{xx}^{2}f_{0}(\bar{x},\bar{w})u^{\prime}-\gamma\nabla_{x}F(\bar{x},\bar{w})=0\\
\nabla_{x}F(\bar{x},\bar{w})^{T}u^{\prime}=0.\end{cases}

(47)

If
\gamma=0
, then the first equation of (
47
) forces
u^{\prime}=0
, because the matrix
\nabla_{xx}^{2}f_{0}(\bar{x},\bar{w})
is nonsingular. So, we must have
\gamma\neq 0
. From the first equation of (
47
) we deduce that

u^{\prime}=\gamma\nabla_{xx}^{2}f_{0}(\bar{x},\bar{w})^{-1}\nabla_{x}F(\bar{x},\bar{w}).

Hence, by the second equation of (
47
), we obtain

\gamma\nabla_{x}F(\bar{x},\bar{w})^{T}\nabla_{xx}^{2}f_{0}(\bar{x},\bar{w})^{-1}\nabla_{x}F(\bar{x},\bar{w})=0.

This obviously contradicts (
39
). Thus,
A
is nonsingular.

Finally, to obtain the implication (
46
), let
u^{\prime}\in I\!\!R^{n}
and
\gamma\geq 0
be such that

\begin{cases}\nabla_{xx}^{2}f_{0}(\bar{x},\bar{w})u^{\prime}-\gamma\nabla_{x}F(\bar{x},\bar{w})=0\\
\nabla_{x}F(\bar{x},\bar{w})^{T}u^{\prime}\leq 0\end{cases}

(48)

Multiplying both sides of the equation in (

1\times n
matrix
\nabla_{x}F(\bar{x},\bar{w})^{T}\nabla_{xx}^{2}f_{0}(\bar{x},\bar{w})^{-1}
, one obtains

\nabla_{x}F(\bar{x},\bar{w})^{T}u^{\prime}-\gamma\nabla_{x}F(\bar{x},\bar{w})^{T}\nabla_{xx}^{2}f_{0}(\bar{x},\bar{w})^{-1}\nabla_{x}F(\bar{x},\bar{w})=0.

(49)

Due to (
39
) and the condition
\gamma\geq 0
,

-\gamma\nabla_{x}F(\bar{x},\bar{w})^{T}\nabla_{xx}^{2}f_{0}(\bar{x},\bar{w})^{-1}\nabla_{x}F(\bar{x},\bar{w})\leq 0.

Combining this with the inequality
\nabla_{x}F(\bar{x},\bar{w})^{T}u^{\prime}\leq 0
from (
48
), by (
49
) one has

-\gamma\nabla_{x}F(\bar{x},\bar{w})^{T}\nabla_{xx}^{2}f_{0}(\bar{x},\bar{w})^{-1}\nabla_{x}F(\bar{x},\bar{w})=0.

Due to (
39
),
\gamma=0
. Then, the first equation in (

\nabla_{xx}^{2}f_{0}(\bar{x},\bar{w})u^{\prime}=0
. As
\nabla_{xx}^{2}f_{0}(\bar{x},\bar{w})
is nonsingular, one has
u^{\prime}=0
. Thus, (
46
) is valid.

The proof is complete.
\hfill\Box

7
Conclusions

In this paper, we have analyzed the stability of the stationary point set map of a smooth parametric optimization problem with one smooth functional constraint under total perturbations. Not only sufficient conditions for the local Lipschitz-like property of the stationary point set map were given, but also necessary conditions for the latter property have been obtained. Sufficient conditions for the Robinson stability of the stationary point set map were given. In addition, we have revisited several stability theorems in indefinite quadratic programming.

It is still unclear to us whether the Lipschitz continuous single-valued localization mentioned in Propositions
5.1
and
5.2
implies the Robinson stability of the stationary point set map, or not.

Extensions of the obtained results to the case of smooth parametric optimization problem with more than one smooth functional constraint under total perturbations are worthy further investigations.

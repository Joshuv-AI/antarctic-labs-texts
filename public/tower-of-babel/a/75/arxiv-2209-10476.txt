Random graphs from structured classes
Colin McDiarmid

Abstract
Given a class 𝒢 {\mathcal{G}} of graphs, let 𝒢 n {\mathcal{G}}_{n} denote the set of graphs in 𝒢 {\mathcal{G}} on vertex set [ n ] [n] . For certain classes 𝒢 {\mathcal{G}} , we are interested in the asymptotic behaviour of a random graph R n R_{n} sampled uniformly from 𝒢 n {\mathcal{G}}_{n} . Call 𝒢 {\mathcal{G}} smooth if n ​ | 𝒢 n − 1 | / | 𝒢 n | n|{\mathcal{G}}_{n-1}|/|{\mathcal{G}}_{n}| tends to a limit as n → ∞ n\to\infty . Showing that a graph class is smooth is a key step in an approach to investigating properties of R n R_{n} , in particular the asymptotic probability that R n R_{n} is connected, and more generally the asymptotic behaviour of the fragment of R n R_{n} outside the largest component. The composition method of Bender, Canfield and Richmond [ 1 ] shows that the class of graphs embeddable in a given surface is smooth; and similarly we have smoothness for any minor-closed class of graphs with 2-connected excluded minors [ 15 ] . Here we develop the approach further, and give results encompassing both these cases and much more. We see that, under quite general conditions, our graph classes are smooth and we can describe for example the limiting distribution of the fragment of R n R_{n} and the size of the core; and we obtain similar results for the graphs in the class with minimum degree at least 2.



1
Introduction and statement of results

Given a class
{\mathcal{G}}
of graphs (closed under isomorphism), let
{\mathcal{G}}_{n}
denote the set of graphs in
{\mathcal{G}}
on vertex set
[n]
for non-negative integers
n
. The notation
R_{n}\in_{u}{\mathcal{G}}
indicates that the random graph
R_{n}
is uniformly distributed over
{\mathcal{G}}_{n}
(with the implicit assumption that
{\mathcal{G}}_{n}\neq\emptyset
).
In order to deduce results about
R_{n}
, we often need to know that the numbers
|{\mathcal{G}}_{n}|
do not behave too erratically as
n
varies. Let
G(x)
denote the corresponding (exponential) generating function
\sum_{n\geq 0}|{\mathcal{G}}_{n}|x^{n}/n!
, and let
\rho_{{\mathcal{G}}}
(or sometimes
\rho({\mathcal{G}})
) denote the radius of convergence of
G(x)
,
0\leq\rho_{{\mathcal{G}}}\leq\infty
.
If
0<\gamma<\infty
and

(|{{\mathcal{G}}}_{n}|/n!)^{1/n}\to\gamma\;\;\mbox{ as }n\to\infty,

we say that
{\mathcal{G}}
has
growth constant

\gamma
. In this case
\gamma
must be
\rho_{{\mathcal{G}}}^{-1}
.

Showing that
{\mathcal{G}}
has a growth constant is a first step in proving results about
R_{n}\in_{u}{\mathcal{G}}
: a next step is to show smoothness. Suppose that
0<\rho_{{\mathcal{G}}}<\infty
. Let
r_{n}=n|{\mathcal{G}}_{n-1}|/|{\mathcal{G}}_{n}|
. Observe that, if
{\mathcal{G}}
is closed under adding and removing isolated vertices, then
r_{n}
is the expected number of isolated vertices in
R_{n}
. It is easy to see that

\liminf_{n\to\infty}r_{n}\leq\rho_{{\mathcal{G}}}\leq\limsup_{n\to\infty}r_{n}.

(1)

Thus if
r_{n}
tends to a limit as
n\to\infty
it must be
\rho_{{\mathcal{G}}}
. We call
{\mathcal{G}}

smooth
if
r_{n}\to\rho_{{\mathcal{G}}}
as
n\to\infty
. It is easy to see that in this case
{\mathcal{G}}
must have a growth constant.

The classes for which we know an asymptotic counting formula are typically smooth, for example the class
{\mathcal{F}}
of forests
[
26
]
, the classes of outerplanar graphs and of series-parallel graphs
[
3
,
4
]
,
the class
{\mathcal{P}}
of planar graphs
[
10
,
11
]
, and indeed the class
{\mathcal{E}}^{S}

of graphs embeddable in a given surface
S

[
2
,
7
]
. In particular, for forests we have
|{\mathcal{F}}_{n}|\sim e^{\frac{1}{2}}n^{n-2}
(see
[
26
]
)
and so

\frac{n|{\mathcal{F}}_{n-1}|}{|{\mathcal{F}}_{n}|}\sim\frac{n(n-1)^{n-3}}{n^{n-2}}\sim e^{-1}=\rho_{{\mathcal{F}}}.

(Of course, we would need to make adjustments for example if
{\mathcal{G}}
is the class of cubic planar graphs, see
[
5
]
, where
|{\mathcal{G}}_{n}|=0
for odd integers
n
.)

If we do not know an asymptotic counting formula, in order to prove smoothness we may be able to use the composition method of Bender, Canfield and Richmond
[
1
]
, see section
3
below.
In this way we can show that the class of graphs embeddable in a given surface is smooth
[
1
]
, and that any minor-closed graph class
which has 2-connected excluded minors is smooth
[
15
]
.
The proofs of our main results here, Theorems
2
and
3
,
are based on this approach with some further development.
We focus on structured classes of graphs satisfying some natural conditions, so that our results apply to surface classes
{\mathcal{E}}^{S}
and addable minor-closed classes and much more.

Related results are obtained in
[
18
]
by a different proof approach, which requires the graph class to have a growth constant but not necessarily to be smooth.

1.1
Preliminaries

We now introduce some properties which we shall need for a graph class
{\mathcal{G}}
.
The
null graph
(or empty graph) has no vertices or edges. By convention, a class
{\mathcal{G}}
of graphs contains the null graph unless it is explicitly a class of connected graphs, when it does not contain the null graph. The class
{\mathcal{G}}
is
proper
if it contains a graph other than the null graph and it does not contain all graphs. We may sometimes assume implicitly that this holds. We call
{\mathcal{G}}

decomposable
when a graph is in
{\mathcal{G}}
if and only if each component is. Observe that the class of planar graphs is decomposable but the class of graphs embeddable in the torus is not. (For example, the complete graph
K_{5}
embeds in the torus but two disjoint copies of
K_{5}
do not.) Indeed the surface class
{\mathcal{E}}^{S}
is decomposable only if
S
is the sphere. If
{\mathcal{G}}
is decomposable, and
{\mathcal{C}}
is the class of connected graphs in
{\mathcal{G}}
with generating function
C(x)
, then the
exponential formula
says that
G(x)=e^{C(x)}
.

A graph
H
is a
minor
of a graph
G
if we may obtain a copy of
H
by starting with a subgraph of
G
and contracting edges (we replace any multiple edges by a single edge, and delete loops). A class of graphs is
minor-closed
if whenever
G\in{\mathcal{G}}
and
H
is a minor of
G
we have
H\in{\mathcal{G}}
. If
{\mathcal{G}}
is minor-closed the minor-minimal graphs not in
{\mathcal{G}}
are the
excluded minors
for
{\mathcal{G}}
. The Robertson-Seymour Theorem says that for every minor-closed class the set of excluded minors is finite, see for example
[
8
]
. It is easy to see that a minor-closed class is decomposable if and only if each excluded minor is connected.

We call a graph class
{\mathcal{G}}

bridge-addable
if whenever
G\in{\mathcal{G}}
and
u
and
v
are in different components of
G
then
G+uv\in{\mathcal{G}}
. Here
G+uv
denotes the graph obtained by adding the edge
uv
to
G
.
(This property was called ‘weakly addable’ in
[
22
]
.)
By Theorem 2.2 of
[
21
]
or
[
22
]
, if
{\mathcal{G}}
is bridge-addable and
R_{n}\in_{u}{\mathcal{G}}
then

\mathbb{P}(R_{n}\mbox{ is connected})\geq 1/e\,.

(2)

Following
[
22
]
, we say that the class
{\mathcal{G}}
is
addable

if it is both decomposable and bridge-addable. For example, the class
{\mathcal{P}}
of planar graphs is addable. Recall that
{\mathcal{P}}
is minor-closed, with excluded minors
K_{5}
and
K_{3,3}
. In general, a minor-closed class
{\mathcal{G}}
is addable if and only if each excluded minor is 2-connected, as noted in
[
15
]
.
The surface class
{\mathcal{E}}^{S}
is always bridge-addable, but as we already noted it is not decomposable (and so not addable) except in the planar case.

Given a graph
G
, the
core
(or 2-
core
)
{\rm Core}(G)
of
G
is the null graph if
G
is a forest, and otherwise it is the unique maximal subgraph of
G
with minimum degree at least 2. Thus
{\rm Core}(G)
is the graph obtained by repeatedly trimming off leaves (in any order) until none remain, and then deleting any isolated vertices. The number of vertices in
{\rm Core}(G)
is denoted by
{\rm core}(G)
.
Given a class
{\mathcal{G}}
of graphs we let
{\mathcal{G}}^{\delta\geq 2}
denote the class of graphs in
{\mathcal{G}}
with minimum degree at least two.

We say that a class
{\mathcal{G}}
of graphs
is
trimmable
if for any graph
G

G\in{\mathcal{G}}\;\mbox{ if and only if }\>{\rm Core}(G)\in{\mathcal{G}}.

(3)

Observe that a trimmable graph class which contains the null graph must contain all forests.
We may see that
{\mathcal{G}}
is trimmable
precisely when, for every graph
G
with a leaf
v
,
G\in{\mathcal{G}}
if and only if
G\!-\!v\in{\mathcal{G}}
. (Here
G\!-\!v
denotes
G
with
v
and the incident edge removed.)
Also, if
{\mathcal{G}}
is hereditary (closed under forming induced subgraphs), closed under adding an isolated vertex and bridge-addable, then
{\mathcal{G}}
is trimmable.

Clearly each surface class
{\mathcal{E}}^{S}
is trimmable.
Consider a minor-closed class
{\mathcal{G}}
of graphs.
It is not hard to see that
{\mathcal{G}}
is trimmable if and only if each excluded minor has minimum degree at least two.
Also, if each excluded minor has minimum degree at least one (so
{\mathcal{G}}
is closed under adding an isolated vertex) and
{\mathcal{G}}
is bridge-addable then
{\mathcal{G}}
is trimmable.
[The class of graphs with no minor the ‘bowtie graph’ (obtained from two disjoint triangles by identifying a vertex in one triangle with a vertex from the other triangle) is an example of a graph class which is trimmable but not bridge-addable.] See also the paragraph below where we define when a graph is ‘free for
{\mathcal{G}}
’ (before Theorem 2).

The
fragment

\mbox{{\rm Frag}}(G)
of a graph
G
is the graph remaining (perhaps the null graph) when we discard the component
\rm Big(G)
with the most vertices (breaking ties lexicographically or in some other way).
If
G
has vertex set
[n]
then
\mbox{{\rm Frag}}(G)
has vertex set some subset of
[n]
. it is natural to consider the fragment as an unlabelled graph, and we shall do so.

Finally in this subsection let us recall the Boltzmann Poisson random graph, see
[
15
]
.
Let the class
{\mathcal{G}}
of graphs be decomposable, and let
\tilde{{\mathcal{G}}}
denote the set of unlabelled graphs in
{\mathcal{G}}
. (Recall that by convention the null graph
\emptyset
is in
{\mathcal{G}}
and in
\tilde{{\mathcal{G}}}
.) Fix
\rho>0
such that
G(\rho)
is finite; and let

\mu(H)=\frac{\rho^{v(H)}}{\mbox{\small aut}(H)}\;\mbox{ for each }H\in\tilde{{\mathcal{G}}}

with
\mu(\emptyset)=1
.
Here
v(H)
is the number of vertices in
H
, and
\mbox{\small aut}(H)
is the size of the automorphism group.
Routine manipulations (see for example
[
15
]
) show that

G(\rho)=\sum_{H\in\tilde{{\mathcal{G}}}}\mu(H).

The
Boltzmann Poisson random graph

R=BP({\mathcal{G}},\rho)
takes values in
\tilde{{\mathcal{G}}}
, with

\mathbb{P}(R=H)=\frac{\mu(H)}{G(\rho)}\;\;\mbox{ for each }H\in\tilde{{\mathcal{G}}}.

(4)

Let
{\mathcal{C}}
denote the class of connected graphs in
{\mathcal{G}}
. (Recall that by convention
\emptyset
is not in
{\mathcal{C}}
.) For each
H\in\tilde{{\mathcal{C}}}
let
\kappa(G,H)
denote the number of components of
G
isomorphic to
H
. Then (see
[
15
]
) the random variables
\kappa(R,H)
for
H\in\tilde{{\mathcal{C}}}
are independent, with
\kappa(R,H)\sim\mbox{\rm Po}(\mu(H))
. In particular,

\mathbb{P}(R=\emptyset)=e^{-C(\rho)}=1/G(\rho)
.

1.2
Statement of main results

We first present Theorem
1
, in order to bring together previously published results in this area. It is taken from Theorems 1.2 and 1.5 of
[
15
]
, and Theorems 2.2 and 2.4 of
[
17
]
. (The latter paper concerns more general weighted distributions: we stick to the uniform distribution here.) Recall that a proper addable minor-closed class of graphs is trimmable, and contains the class
{\mathcal{F}}
of forests.

Theorem 1
.

Let
{\mathcal{G}}
and
{\mathcal{A}}
be graph classes with
{\mathcal{G}}\supseteq{\mathcal{A}}
; and suppose that either (i)
{\mathcal{G}}
is a proper addable minor-closed class and
{\mathcal{A}}
is
{\mathcal{G}}
, or (ii)
{\mathcal{G}}
is the class
{\mathcal{E}}^{S}
of graphs embeddable in a given surface
S
and
{\mathcal{A}}
is the class
{\mathcal{P}}
of planar graphs. Let
{\mathcal{C}}
be the class of connected graphs in
{\mathcal{G}}
, and let
\rho_{0}=\rho_{\mathcal{A}}
. Then the following statements (a), (b) hold.

(a) Both
{\mathcal{G}}
and
{\mathcal{C}}
are smooth, with radius of convergence
\rho_{0}
,
\,0<\rho_{0}\leq 1/e
.

(b)

\,0<A(\rho_{0})<\infty
, so
BP({\mathcal{A}},\rho_{0})
is well defined; and for
R_{n}\in_{u}{\mathcal{G}}
, the fragment of
R_{n}
converges in distribution to
BP({\mathcal{A}},\rho_{0})
. In particular, the probability that
R_{n}
is connected tends to
e^{-C(\rho_{0})}
as
n\to\infty
.

Now add the condition in case (i) that
{\mathcal{A}}
contains a graph with a cycle.
Then the following statements (c), (d) hold.

(c)
0<\rho_{0}<1/e
, and

\rho_{2}:=\rho({\mathcal{A}}^{\delta\geq 2})
is the unique root
x
with
0<x<1
to
xe^{-x}=\rho_{0}\,
(so
\rho_{2}>\rho_{0}
); and both
{\mathcal{G}}^{\delta\geq 2}
and
{\mathcal{C}}^{\delta\geq 2}
have growth constant
\rho_{2}^{-1}
.

(d)
Given
\epsilon>0
, for
R_{n}\in_{u}{\mathcal{G}}
or
R_{n}\in_{u}{\mathcal{C}}
we have

\mathbb{P}(|\,{\rm core}(R_{n})-(1-\rho_{2})n\,|>\epsilon n)=e^{-\Omega(n)}.

(5)

We shall give two new theorems, which between them extend Theorem
1
in two directions.
They provide a generalisation of the relationship between the class
{\mathcal{E}}^{S}

of graphs embeddable in a given surface
S
and the subclass
{\mathcal{P}}
of planar graphs.
Note that the only proper addable minor-closed class
{\mathcal{A}}
of graphs with
\rho_{\mathcal{A}}\geq 1/e
is the class
{\mathcal{F}}
of forests, with
\rho_{\mathcal{F}}=1/e
.
(For
{\mathcal{A}}
must contain
{\mathcal{F}}
, and if
{\mathcal{A}}\neq{\mathcal{F}}
then it contains a cycle
H
, and by the Pendant Appearances Theorem (see Theorem 4.1 in
[
21
]
) we have
\rho_{\mathcal{A}}<\rho_{\mathcal{F}}=1/e
.)
When we extend Theorem
1
we shall exclude this case.

Observe that
{\mathcal{E}}^{S}
is bridge-addable and trimmable,
{\mathcal{E}}^{S}\supseteq{\mathcal{P}}
and
\rho({\mathcal{E}}^{S})=\rho_{{\mathcal{P}}}
(
[
14
]
, see also
[
2
,
7
]
). The original application of the composition method was to show that both
{\mathcal{E}}^{S}
and the subclass of connected graphs in
{\mathcal{E}}^{S}
are smooth classes
[
1
]
.
Given a class
{\mathcal{G}}
of graphs, call a connected graph
H

free
for
{\mathcal{G}}
when, for each graph
G
disjoint from
H
, the following statements (a), (b) and (c) are equivalent: (a)
G\in{\mathcal{G}}
; (b)
G\cup H\in{\mathcal{G}}
; (c)
G^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{G}}
, where
G^{\prime}
is formed from
G\cup H
by adding an edge (bridge) between a vertex in
H
and a vertex in
G
.
Call a general graph
free
for
{\mathcal{G}}
when each component is free for
{\mathcal{G}}
.
(We also say that the null graph is free for any class
{\mathcal{G}}
.)
Observe that
{\mathcal{G}}
is trimmable if and only if the single vertex graph
K_{1}
is free for
{\mathcal{G}}
. Also, given a trimmable class
{\mathcal{G}}
, a connected graph is free for
{\mathcal{G}}
if and only if its core is free for
{\mathcal{G}}
.

In our first new theorem, Theorem
2
, the first three parts correspond to parts (a), (c) and (d) of Theorem
1
. We defer consideration of the fragment of
R_{n}
(corresponding to part (b) of Theorem
1
) until Theorem
3
.

Theorem 2
.

Let
{\mathcal{G}}
be a trimmable class of graphs
with
0<\rho_{\mathcal{G}}<\infty
, and let
{\mathcal{C}}
be the class of connected graphs in
{\mathcal{G}}
.
Suppose that there is an addable subclass
{\mathcal{A}}
of
{\mathcal{G}}
such that
{\mathcal{A}}
contains a graph which has a cycle, and
\rho_{{\mathcal{A}}}=\rho_{{\mathcal{G}}}:=\rho_{0}
.
Then the following statements hold.

(a)
Both
{\mathcal{G}}
and
{\mathcal{C}}
are smooth, with growth constant
\rho_{0}^{-1}
.

(b)

0<\rho_{0}<1/e
;

\rho_{2}:=\rho({\mathcal{A}}^{\delta\geq 2})
is the unique root
x
with
0<x<1
to
xe^{-x}=\rho_{0}
; and both
{\mathcal{G}}^{\delta\geq 2}
and
{\mathcal{C}}^{\delta\geq 2}
have growth constant
\rho_{2}^{-1}
.

(c)
Given
\epsilon>0
, for
R_{n}\in_{u}{\mathcal{G}}
or for
R_{n}\in_{u}{\mathcal{C}}
we have

\mathbb{P}(|\,{\rm core}(R_{n})-(1-\rho_{2})n\,|>\epsilon n)=e^{-\Omega(n)}.

(d) If some cycle
is free for
{\mathcal{G}}

then both
{\mathcal{G}}^{\delta\geq 2}
and
{\mathcal{C}}^{\delta\geq 2}
are smooth.

Before we give our second new theorem we give some examples for Theorem
2
.
Two natural examples come from Theorem
1
: they
are (i) when
{\mathcal{G}}
is minor-closed and addable, and
{\mathcal{A}}={\mathcal{G}}
, and (ii) when
{\mathcal{G}}
is a surface class
{\mathcal{E}}^{S}
and
{\mathcal{A}}={\mathcal{P}}
.
In both cases every cycle in
{\mathcal{A}}
is free for
{\mathcal{G}}
. From these examples we may essentially read off parts (a), (c) and (d) of Theorem
1
. Also,
by part (d) of Theorem
2
, both
{\mathcal{G}}^{\delta\geq 2}
and
{\mathcal{C}}^{\delta\geq 2}
are smooth: for the case when
{\mathcal{G}}
is an addable minor-closed class, this result was first presented by the author in an invited lecture at RANDOM 2014 in Barcelona.

In the above examples the classes
{\mathcal{G}}
and
{\mathcal{A}}
are minor-closed, but that is not necessarily the case in our further examples.
We could for example let
{\mathcal{A}}
be the class of bipartite planar graphs,
or planar graphs of girth
t
(for some integer
t\geq 3
); and let
{\mathcal{G}}
be the corresponding class of graphs embeddable in some given surface
S
.
Also, we do not need to consider only a fixed surface.
For example, let
g=g(n)\geq 0
be non-decreasing,
and let
{\mathcal{H}}{\mathcal{E}}^{g}
be the class of graphs
G
such that every induced subgraph
G^{\prime}
of
G
can be embedded in a surface of Euler genus at most
g(n^{\prime})
where
n^{\prime}=v(G^{\prime})
. (The
{\mathcal{H}}{\mathcal{E}}
here is for ‘hereditarily embeddable’.) Observe that
{\mathcal{H}}{\mathcal{E}}^{g}
is trimmable. It is shown in
[
19
]
that if
g(n)=o(n/\log^{3}n)
then
\rho({\mathcal{H}}{\mathcal{E}}^{g})=\rho_{{\mathcal{P}}}
. Thus for such a function
g
, in Theorem
2
we could take
{\mathcal{G}}={\mathcal{H}}{\mathcal{E}}^{g}
and
{\mathcal{A}}={\mathcal{P}}
.

Finally we give one rather different example for
Theorem
2
.
Fix a positive integer
k
, and let
{\mathcal{G}}
be the class of graphs with at most
k
edge-disjoint subgraphs contractible to a diamond (
K_{4}
less an edge).
Clearly
{\mathcal{G}}
is trimmable (though it is not minor-closed).
Let
{\mathcal{A}}
be the addable class of graphs with no minor a diamond (so
0<\rho_{\mathcal{A}}<\infty
). Then
the conditions of Theorem
2
hold.
For, by an edge-version of the Erdős-Pósa theorem
[
25
]
, there is an integer
f(k)
such that by removing at most
f(k)
edges we may obtain a graph in
{\mathcal{A}}\,
: it follows that
|{\mathcal{G}}_{n}|=O(n^{2f(k)})\,|{\mathcal{A}}_{n}|
,
and so
\rho_{{\mathcal{G}}}=\rho_{{\mathcal{A}}}
.

For our second new theorem here, Theorem
3
concerning fragments, we slightly strengthen the conditions in Theorem
2
, but the above examples for Theorem
2
are also examples to which Theorem
3
applies.

Theorem 3
.

Let the class
{\mathcal{G}}
of graphs be bridge-addable and closed under taking subgraphs,
with
0<\rho_{\mathcal{G}}<\infty
. Let
{\mathcal{A}}
be the class of graphs which are
free for
{\mathcal{G}}
; and suppose that
{\mathcal{A}}
contains a graph which has a cycle, and that
\rho_{{\mathcal{A}}}=\rho_{{\mathcal{G}}}:=\rho_{0}
.
Then the conditions in Theorem
2
hold, so we have the conclusions (a) - (d) of that theorem (including the specification of
\rho_{2}
).

Let
R_{n}\in_{u}{\mathcal{G}}
. Then the fragment of
R_{n}
converges in distribution to
BP({\mathcal{A}},\rho_{0})
;
and whp the fragment of the core of
R_{n}
is the same as the core of the fragment of
R_{n}
, and they and the fragment of

each converge in distribution to

.

In this theorem it is straightforward to check
that the conditions in Theorem
2
hold, see the start of Section
6
.
Also, note that a graph

is free for
{\mathcal{G}}
if and only if the disjoint union
G\cup H\in{\mathcal{G}}
whenever

.

The first part of Theorem
3
yields part (b) of Theorem
1
assuming that some graph with a cycle is free for
{\mathcal{G}}

(and we have already seen that Theorem

extends the other parts of Theorem
1
, with this same assumption).
To see this, note that, letting
{\mathcal{A}}
be the class of graphs which are
free for
{\mathcal{G}}
, we have (i) if
{\mathcal{G}}
is

is
{\mathcal{G}}
, and (ii) if
{\mathcal{G}}
is

then
{\mathcal{A}}
is

.
The special case of the last part of Theorem

when

is an addable minor-closed class was first presented at the same time as the corresponding case of the last part of Theorem
2
, at RANDOM 2014.

We commented that for Theorem
3

we strengthened the conditions in Theorem

. The following example shows that we needed to do this. Let
{\mathcal{G}}
be the trimmable class

and let

Plan of the rest of the paper

The rest of the paper is essentially devoted to proving Theorems

Weak growth constant

In this section, we introduce the concept of a weak growth constant, which is implicit in
[
1
]
;
and we see in particular that if
\rho({\mathcal{G}})<\rho({\mathcal{H}})
then the labelled product
{\mathcal{G}}\otimes{\mathcal{H}}
has a weak growth constant if and only if
{\mathcal{G}}
does.

Given an infinite set
S\subseteq\mathbb{N}
, for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
let
n^{+}=\min\{m\in S:m\geq n\}
and let
{\rm gap}_{S}(n)=n^{+}-n
. We say that a set
S\subseteq\mathbb{N}
has
sublinear gaps
if
S
is infinite and
{\rm gap}_{S}(n)=o(n)
as
n\to\infty
.
Thus
S
has sublinear gaps if and only if for each
\epsilon>0
there is an
n_{0}
such that
for each
n\geq n_{0}
there is an
n^{\prime}\in S
with
n\leq n^{\prime}\leq n+\epsilon n
.

|n-n^{\prime}|<\epsilon n
.)

We may rephrase the above definition in terms of ‘slowly increasing’ functions. Call a function or sequence
f:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}

slowly increasing
if it is increasing and
(f(n+1)-f(n))/f(n)\to 0
as
n\to\infty
.
For example
f(n)=n^{k}
is slowly increasing for any fixed
k>0
, but
f(n)=2^{n}
is not.
Given an infinite set
S
of positive integers, let
f_{S}:\mathbb{N}\to S
be the function or sequence enumerating
S
; that is

f_{S}(n)=k\mbox{ if }|S\cap\{1,\ldots,k-1\}|=n-1\mbox{ and }k\in S.

Then
S
has sublinear gaps if and only if
f_{S}
is slowly increasing.

Let the graph class
{\mathcal{G}}
satisfy
0<\rho_{{\mathcal{G}}}<\infty
.
Given
0<\eta<1
, we say that
n\in\mathbb{N}
is
(1\!-\!\eta)
-
rich
for
{\mathcal{G}}
if

(|{\mathcal{G}}_{n}|/n!)^{1/n}\geq(1-\eta)\rho_{{\mathcal{G}}}^{-1}.

Note that
{\mathcal{G}}
has a growth constant if and only if, for each
\eta>0
, all sufficiently large
n\in\mathbb{N}
are
(1-\eta)
-rich for
{\mathcal{G}}
.
We say that
{\mathcal{G}}
has a
weak growth constant
if for each
\eta>0
the set of
n\in\mathbb{N}
which are
(1-\eta)
-rich for
{\mathcal{G}}

has sublinear gaps.
If a class has a growth constant then clearly it has a weak growth constant. The converse does not hold in general: for example, cubic planar graphs do not have a growth constant (since they must have an even number of vertices) but they do have a weak growth constant
(see
[
5
]
).
However, for a trimmable class
{\mathcal{G}}
the converse does hold, see Lemma
6
below; and indeed when
\rho_{{\mathcal{G}}}<e^{-1}
the class
{\mathcal{G}}
must be smooth, see Lemmas
11
and
12
.

Next we discuss how having a weak growth constant, or growth constant, or being smooth, behaves under unions and products of graph classes.

Growth constants and unions of graph classes

First let us record some easily checked observations concerning a union of two graph classes
{\mathcal{G}}
and
{\mathcal{H}}
. It is easy to see that

\rho({\mathcal{G}}\cup{\mathcal{H}})=\min\{\rho_{{\mathcal{G}}},\rho_{{\mathcal{H}}}\}
.
If
\rho_{{\mathcal{G}}}\leq\rho_{{\mathcal{H}}}
and
{\mathcal{G}}
has a weak growth constant or growth constant then so does
{\mathcal{G}}\cup{\mathcal{H}}
. If
\rho_{{\mathcal{G}}}<\rho_{{\mathcal{H}}}
then
{\mathcal{G}}\cup{\mathcal{H}}
has a weak growth constant, growth constant, or is smooth if and only if
{\mathcal{G}}
has the same property.

Growth constants and products of graph classes

Now consider the (labelled) product

The exponential generating function of

are the exponential generating functions of

respectively. Thus

\rho({\mathcal{G}}\otimes{\mathcal{H}})=\min\{\rho_{{\mathcal{G}}},\rho_{{\mathcal{H}}}\}.

Lemma 4
.

Let
{\mathcal{G}}
and

If
\rho_{{\mathcal{G}}}<\rho_{{\mathcal{H}}}

is smooth then

When are there converse results?
Consider the example where

Lemma 5
.

Let
{\mathcal{G}}
and
{\mathcal{H}}
be graph classes with
0<\rho_{{\mathcal{G}}}<\rho_{{\mathcal{H}}}
, and suppose that
{\mathcal{G}}\otimes{\mathcal{H}}
has a weak growth constant. Then
{\mathcal{G}}
has a weak growth constant.

Proof.

Given
0<\epsilon<1
,
0<\eta<1
and
n
, let
P(\epsilon,\eta\,;n)
be the proposition that there is no
k
with
(1-\epsilon)n\leq k\leq n
which is
(1-\eta)
-rich for
{\mathcal{G}}
. The main step in the proof of the lemma is to establish the following claim.

Claim
Let
0<\epsilon<1
and
0<\eta<1
. Define
\delta

\delta=\tfrac{1}{2}\min\{{1-(\rho_{{\mathcal{G}}}/\rho_{{\mathcal{H}}})^{\epsilon}},{1-(1-\eta)^{1-\epsilon}}\},

and note that
0<\delta<\frac{1}{2}
.
Then there exists
n_{0}=n_{0}(\epsilon,\eta)
such that for each
n\geq n_{0}
, if
P(\epsilon,\eta\,;n)
holds then
n
is not
(1-\delta)
-rich for
{\mathcal{G}}\otimes{\mathcal{H}}
.

Proof of Claim.

Suppose that
P(\epsilon,\eta\,;n)
holds. Then
|({\mathcal{G}}\otimes{\mathcal{H}})_{n}|
is at most

\sum_{k<(1\!-\!\epsilon)n}\!\binom{n}{k}|{\mathcal{G}}_{k}|\,|{\mathcal{H}}_{n-k}|\;+\sum_{(1\!-\!\epsilon)n\leq k\leq n}\!\binom{n}{k}(1\!-\!\eta)^{k}\rho_{{\mathcal{G}}}^{-k}k!\,|{\mathcal{H}}_{n-k}|.

The first sum equals

\displaystyle n!\sum_{k<(1\!-\!\epsilon)n}\frac{|{\mathcal{G}}_{k}|}{k!}\frac{|{\mathcal{H}}_{n-k}|}{(n-k)!}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle n!\>n\,(\rho_{{\mathcal{G}}}+o(1))^{-(1-\epsilon)n}(\rho_{{\mathcal{H}}}+o(1))^{-\epsilon n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1+o(1))^{n}n!\,\rho_{{\mathcal{G}}}^{-n}(\rho_{{\mathcal{G}}}/\rho_{{\mathcal{H}}})^{\epsilon n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle o((1-\delta)^{n})\cdot n!\,\rho_{{\mathcal{G}}}^{-n}.

The second sum is at most

(1+o(1))^{n}(1-\eta)^{(1-\epsilon)n}\cdot n!\,\rho_{{\mathcal{G}}}^{-n}=o((1-\delta)^{n})\cdot n!\,\rho_{{\mathcal{G}}}^{-n}.

Hence (recalling that
\rho({\mathcal{G}}\otimes{\mathcal{H}})=\rho_{{\mathcal{G}}}
), if

is sufficiently large and

holds then

|({\mathcal{G}}\otimes{\mathcal{H}})_{n}|<(1-\delta)^{n}\cdot n!\,\rho({\mathcal{G}}\otimes{\mathcal{H}})^{-n},

and so

is not
(1\!-\!\delta)
-rich for
{\mathcal{G}}\otimes{\mathcal{H}}
, which completes the proof of the claim.
∎

Now we use the claim to complete the proof of the lemma.
Suppose that
{\mathcal{G}}
does not have
a weak growth constant.
Then there exist

and
0<\eta<1
such that for every

for which

holds. Observe that if

Fix such
\epsilon
and
\eta
;
and let
\delta
and

be as in the Claim. Let
n_{1}\geq(1-\epsilon)^{-1}n_{0}
.

Now consider
n\geq n_{1}
such that

holds. Let
(1-\epsilon)n\leq m\leq n
. Then
m\geq n_{0}
and

holds; and so, by the Claim,

-rich for

Growth constants and trimmable graph classes

The final lemma in this subsection shows that,
if a trimmable graph class
{\mathcal{G}}
has a weak growth constant, then it has a growth constant. Note that if a class is trimmable then it is closed under adding a leaf. We shall not need this lemma here: it is included for completeness.

Lemma 6
.

Let the class
{\mathcal{G}}
of graphs be closed under adding a leaf.
If
{\mathcal{G}}
has a weak growth constant, then it has a growth constant. Similarly, if the class
{\mathcal{C}}
of connected graphs in
{\mathcal{G}}
has a weak growth constant, then it has a growth constant, and so also
{\mathcal{G}}
has a growth constant.

Proof.

Let
{\mathcal{A}}
be either
{\mathcal{G}}
or
{\mathcal{C}}
.
Write
\gamma
for
\rho_{{\mathcal{A}}}^{-1}
, and suppose that
{\mathcal{A}}
has weak growth constant
\gamma
.
Let
\hat{{\mathcal{A}}}
be the class of graphs in
{\mathcal{A}}
which have a leaf.
It is easy to see that

|\hat{{\mathcal{A}}}_{n+1}|\geq n|{\mathcal{A}}_{n}|
, and it follows that
\hat{{\mathcal{A}}}
has weak growth constant
\gamma
.

Let
0<\epsilon<\gamma
. Let
n_{1},n_{2},\ldots
be a slowly increasing sequence such that

(|\hat{{\mathcal{A}}}_{n_{k}}|/n_{k}!)^{1/n_{k}}\geq\gamma-\epsilon
for each
k
.
Given
n\geq n_{1}
let
k=k(n)
be such that
n_{k}\leq n<n_{k+1}
.
Let
n\geq n_{1}
. Pick an
n_{k}
-subset
W
of
[n]
, and a
graph
G\in\hat{{\mathcal{A}}}
on
W
. Pick a leaf
v
of
G
, with adjacent vertex
u
. Pick an ordering
v_{1},\ldots,v_{n-n_{k}}
of
[n]\setminus W
, and subdivide the edge
vu
so that it becomes the path
v,v_{1},\ldots,v_{n-n_{k}},u
.
This gives at least

\binom{n}{n_{k}}\,|\hat{{\mathcal{A}}}_{n_{k}}|\,(n-n_{k})!=\frac{n!}{n_{k}!}\,|\hat{{\mathcal{A}}}_{n_{k}}|

constructions of graphs in
\hat{{\mathcal{A}}}_{n}
. Each graph can be constructed at most
n
times (since we know
k
and so once we guess the leaf
v
we know the whole construction).
Thus

|\hat{{\mathcal{A}}}_{n}|\geq\frac{n!}{n_{k}!}\,|\hat{{\mathcal{A}}}_{n_{k}}|\cdot\frac{1}{n}

and so

\displaystyle\left(\frac{|\hat{{\mathcal{A}}}_{n}|}{n!}\right)^{\frac{1}{n}}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\left(\frac{|\hat{{\mathcal{A}}}_{n_{k}}|}{n_{k}!}\right)^{\frac{1}{n}}\cdot n^{-\frac{1}{n}}\;\geq\;(\gamma-\epsilon)^{\frac{n_{k}}{n}}\,n^{-\frac{1}{n}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1+o(1))\,(\gamma-\epsilon)^{\frac{n_{k}}{n}}\;=\;\gamma-\epsilon+o(1)

as
n\to\infty
. It follows that
\hat{{\mathcal{A}}}
has growth constant
\gamma
, and thus so does
{\mathcal{A}}
.
∎

Composition of graph classes and smoothness

A key idea in our proofs here is that the graphs in a trimmable class
{\mathcal{G}}
may be constructed by first choosing the core and then rooting trees at the vertices of the core, so if the numbers of graphs in
{\mathcal{G}}^{\delta\geq 2}_{n}
behave reasonably well as
n
varies (for example if
{\mathcal{G}}^{\delta\geq 2}
has a weak growth constant), then we may deduce smoothness for
{\mathcal{G}}
. (We may handle tree components separately.)
We now describe the composition approach of Bender, Canfield and Richmond

We call the graph

-

of

,

-core of

4
Pendant Appearances

We shall need a slight extension of the ‘Pendant Appearances Theorem’ (from
[
21
,
22
]
) in the proofs of Lemmas
14
and
15

below. Given a vertex-rooted connected graph
H^{\bullet}
, we say that a graph
G
has a
pendant appearance
of
H^{\bullet}
if

G
has a bridge
e
such that one component of
G-e
is a copy of
H
and
e
is incident to the root vertex of
H^{\bullet}
. (In
[
21
]
we considered graphs on subsets of
\mathbb{N}
, and insisted that the increasing bijection was an isomorphism between the component of
G-e
and
H
: we no longer do this.)

Call a connected rooted graph
H^{\bullet}

attachable
to
{\mathcal{G}}
if whenever we have a graph
G
in
{\mathcal{G}}
and a disjoint copy of
H
, and we add an edge between a vertex in
G
and the root of
H^{\bullet}
, then the resulting graph (which has a pendant appearance of
H^{\bullet}
) must be in
{\mathcal{G}}
.
For an addable minor-closed class
{\mathcal{G}}
, every connected rooted graph in
{\mathcal{G}}
is attachable in
{\mathcal{G}}
; and
for a surface class
{\mathcal{E}}^{S}
, the attachable graphs are the connected rooted planar graphs.

In the original version of the Pendant Appearances Theorem
[
21
,
22
]
, we assume that
{\mathcal{G}}
has a growth constant.
We need a slight generalisation of this result, Lemma
10
, in which we assume only that
0<\rho_{\mathcal{G}}<\infty
. For recent stronger results see
[
18
]
.

Lemma 10
.

Let
{\mathcal{G}}
be a class of graphs with
0<\rho_{{\mathcal{G}}}<\infty
, and let the connected rooted graph
H
be attachable to
{\mathcal{G}}
. Then there exists
\alpha>0
such that the following holds. Let
{\mathcal{H}}
denote the class of graphs in
{\mathcal{G}}
with at most
\alpha n
pendant appearances of
H
. Then
\rho_{{\mathcal{H}}}>\rho_{{\mathcal{G}}}
.

Lemma
10
can be proved in a very similar way to the original version of the Pendant Appearances Theorem
[
21
,
22
]
.
Of course
\rho_{{\mathcal{H}}}\geq\rho_{{\mathcal{G}}}
, so we suppose for a contradiction that
\rho_{{\mathcal{H}}}=\rho_{{\mathcal{G}}}
.
The idea of the proof is to show that,
when
n
is large and
|{\mathcal{H}}_{n}|
is close to
\rho_{{\mathcal{H}}}^{-n}n!
, we can construct “too many” graphs in
{\mathcal{G}}
on
(1+\delta)n
vertices. Essentially this is done by putting an
n

on a subset of the vertices, and attaching linearly many copies of

using the other vertices:
the fact that
G
has ‘few’ pendant appearances of

limits the amount of double-counting involved.

Further, let
{\mathcal{H}}
be the subclass of
{\mathcal{G}}^{\delta\geq 2}
consisting of the (not necessarily connected) graphs with minimum degree at least 2 such that the trimming process ends up with no components in
{\mathcal{D}}
(so each component is in case (a) or case (b)). Observe that each graph in
{\mathcal{H}}
is free for
{\mathcal{G}}
.

Let us establish the uniqueness mentioned above for case (c), and the equivalent definition of the icore as the unique maximal induced subgraph in
{\mathcal{D}}
.
Let
G
be a connected graph. Call a non-empty set
W
of vertices in
G

safe
if the induced subgraph

has minimum degree at least 2, is not the
k
-cycle
C_{k}
,
and has no pendant appearance of
H^{\bullet}_{1}
; that is, if the induced subgraph
G[W]
is in
{\mathcal{D}}
.

Claim
A union of safe sets is safe.

Let
W_{1}
and
W_{2}
be safe sets. We want to show that
W:=W_{1}\cup W_{2}
is safe. Clearly each vertex degree in
G[W]
is at least 2. If
G[W]
is
C_{k}
and
v\in W_{1}
then both neighbours
x
and
y
of
v
in the cycle must be in
W_{1}
(since
G[W_{1}]
has minimum degree at least 2), and similarly for
x
and
y
; and it follows easily that
W=W_{1}
and
G[W_{1}]
is a
k
-cycle, contradicting our choice of
W_{1}
as safe. Thus
G[W]
cannot be
C_{k}
.
To prove the claim, it remains only to check that
G[W]
has no pendant appearance of
H^{\bullet}_{1}
. But suppose that
G[W]
has a pendant appearance of
H^{\bullet}_{1}
, on vertex set
Y\subset W
, with the root vertex
r_{1}
of
H^{\bullet}_{1}
adjacent to vertex
v\in W\backslash Y
.
Let
r_{1}
be in
W_{1}
say. Then both
v
and
r_{0}
must be in
W_{1}
(since
r_{1}
has degree 2 in
G[W]
and degree at least 2 in
G[W_{1}]
). Similarly, at least one of the neighbours of
r_{0}
on the
k
-cycle

(in the copy of

) must be in

, and we may carry on round the
k
-cycle to see that all of it is in
W_{1}
; and thus
G[W_{1}]
has a pendant appearance of
H^{\bullet}_{1}
- contradicting our choice of
W_{1}
as safe. We have now established the above claim.

If there is no safe set in
G
, then the trimming process clearly must end up in case (a) or (b).
Suppose that there is a safe set in
G
. Let
W^{*}
be the union of all the safe sets, and note that
W^{*}
is safe by the claim above.
Suppose that the trimming process ends up with a connected graph
H
. We shall see that
V(H)=W^{*}
.
We claim first that
V(H)\supseteq W^{*}
. For if not, then there must be a vertex set
U\supseteq W^{*}
such that in
G[U]
there is a
pendant appearance of
H^{\bullet}_{1}
containing a vertex
v\in W^{*}
. Now, using the fact that in

each vertex has degree at least 2, we can argue as before that we have a pendant appearance of
H^{\bullet}_{1}
in

, which contradicts
W^{*}
being safe.
This establishes the claim that
V(H)\supseteq W^{*}
.
But also we cannot have

, since then
V(H)
would be a safe set larger than

. Thus
V(H)=W^{*}
, and
H
is uniquely specified as the graph

.

The above discussion yields the following lemma. Given a class
{\mathcal{A}}
of graphs we write
{\mathcal{A}}^{conn}
for the class of connected graphs in
{\mathcal{A}}
. (Thus
{\mathcal{C}}
could also be written as
{\mathcal{G}}^{conn}

Lemma 13
.

Let
{\mathcal{G}}
be a trimmable class of graphs, let
{\mathcal{C}}
be the class of connected graphs in
{\mathcal{G}}
, and suppose that for some

{\mathcal{G}}

(16)

(17)

We can now show that

Lemma 14
.

Let the class
{\mathcal{G}}
of graphs be trimmable and have a weak growth constant, and assume that some cycle

Next we show that

By equation (

Lemma 15

6.2
Fragment and core

We shall want to know when the fragment of the core of a graph is the same as the core of the fragment.

Lemma 17
.

Let the graph
G
satisfy
\,{\rm core}(G)>2\,{\mbox{\rm frag}}(G)
. Then

\mbox{{\rm Frag}}({\rm Core}(G))={\rm Core}(\mbox{{\rm Frag}}(G))\,.

6.3
The core of a Boltzmann Poisson random graph

Theorem
3
refers to the core of the fragment of a random graph
R_{n}\in_{u}{\mathcal{G}}
. We have often seen that the fragment of
R_{n}
converges in distribution to a Boltzmann Poisson random graph.
We show that, under rather general conditions, the core of a Boltzmann Poisson random graph
BP({\mathcal{G}},\rho)
has distribution
BP({\mathcal{G}}^{\delta\geq 2},\rho_{2})
(for the natural value of
\rho_{2}
).
It will follow then that the core of the fragment of
R_{n}
must converge in distribution to a Boltzmann Poisson random graph.

Lemma 18
.

Let the graph class
{\mathcal{G}}
be decomposable and trimmable, and suppose that
{\mathcal{G}}^{\delta\geq 2}\neq\emptyset
. Let
\rho_{0}>0
satisfy
G(\rho_{0})<\infty
(so
\rho_{0}\leq 1/e
), and let
R\sim BP({\mathcal{G}},\rho_{0})
.
Let
\rho_{2}=T^{\bullet}(\rho_{0})
, so
\rho_{2}
is the unique root
x
with
0<x<1
to
xe^{-x}=\rho_{0}
.
Then
G^{\delta\geq 2}(\rho_{2})<\infty
, and

{\rm Core}(R)\sim BP({\mathcal{G}}^{\delta\geq 2},\rho_{2}).

Proof.

Let
H
be a (fixed) connected graph in
{\mathcal{G}}^{\delta\geq 2}
. Let
{\mathcal{H}}
be the set of connected graphs
G\in{\mathcal{G}}
such that
{\rm Core}(G)
is isomorphic to
H
, and let
\tilde{{\mathcal{H}}}
be the corresponding set of unlabelled graphs. Let

\mu_{G}=\frac{\rho_{0}^{v(G)}}{\mbox{\small aut}(G)}\;\;\mbox{ for }G\in\tilde{{\mathcal{G}}}.

Denote
{\rm Core}(R)
by
R^{\prime}
. Then
\kappa(R^{\prime},H)=\kappa(R,{\mathcal{H}})
, which has distribution
\mbox{\rm Po}(\lambda)
where
\lambda=\sum_{G\in\tilde{{\mathcal{H}}}}\mu_{G}
. Also, for distinct unlabelled connected graphs
H_{1},H_{2},\cdots
in
\tilde{{\mathcal{G}}}^{\delta\geq 2}
, the corresponding sets
\tilde{{\mathcal{H}}}_{1},\tilde{{\mathcal{H}}}_{2},\ldots
are disjoint, so
\kappa(R^{\prime},H_{1}),\kappa(R^{\prime},H_{2}),\ldots
are independent. Thus it remains to show that

\sum_{G\in\tilde{{\mathcal{H}}}}\mu_{G}=\frac{\rho_{2}^{v(H)}}{\mbox{\small aut}(H)}.

(19)

(It suffices to consider only the connected graph
H
, see Theorem 1.3 of
[
15
]
.)

Let
F_{H}(x)=\frac{x^{v(H)}}{\mbox{\small aut}(H)}
. Then the generating function
H(x)
for the (labelled) graphs
G
with
{\rm Core}(G)
isomorphic to
H
is given by the composition formula
H(x)=F_{H}(T^{\bullet}(x))
. Now recall that
T^{\bullet}(x)=xe^{T^{\bullet}(x)}
, so
x=T^{\bullet}(x)e^{-T^{\bullet}(x)}
,
and thus
\rho_{2}=T^{\bullet}(\rho_{0})
satisfies
xe^{-x}=\rho_{0}
, as claimed. Also,

G(x)=G^{\delta\geq 2}(T^{\bullet}(x))\cdot F(x)

(20)

(where
F(x)
is the generating function for the forests).
Thus
G(\rho_{0})=G^{\delta\geq 2}(\rho_{2})\cdot F(\rho_{0})
, and so
G^{\delta\geq 2}(\rho_{2})<\infty
.

Finally let us check (
19
): we find

\displaystyle\sum_{G\in\tilde{{\mathcal{H}}}}\mu_{G}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n}\sum_{G\in\tilde{{\mathcal{H}}}_{n}}\mu_{G}\;\;=\sum_{n}\sum_{G\in\tilde{{\mathcal{H}}}_{n}}\frac{\rho_{0}^{n}}{\mbox{\small aut}(G)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n}\rho_{0}^{n}\sum_{G\in\tilde{{\mathcal{H}}}_{n}}\frac{1}{\mbox{\small aut}(G)}\;\;=\;\;\sum_{n}\rho_{0}^{n}\frac{|{\mathcal{H}}_{n}|}{n!}\;=H(\rho_{0})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle F_{H}(T^{\bullet}(\rho_{0}))\;=\;\ F_{H}(\rho_{2})\;\ =\;\frac{\rho_{2}^{v(H)}}{\mbox{\small aut}(H)},

as required.
∎

6.4
Sublinearly limited graphs

The next two lemmas concern induced subgraphs that cannot often appear disjointly in a graph from a given class
{\mathcal{G}}
of graphs. Given a connected graph
H
, for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
we let
k_{n}({\mathcal{G}},H)
be the maximum number of vertex disjoint copies of
H
in a graph
G\in{\mathcal{G}}_{n}
. For example, if
{\mathcal{G}}
is the class of graphs embeddable in a surface
S
of Euler genus
g
, and
H
is non-planar, then
k_{n}({\mathcal{G}},H)\leq g
. If
k_{n}({\mathcal{G}},H)=o(n)
we say that
H
is
sublinearly limited
in
{\mathcal{G}}
. Observe that if
{\mathcal{G}}
is closed under taking subgraphs and
H\not\in{\mathcal{G}}
then trivially
k_{n}({\mathcal{G}},H)=0
.

Lemma 19
.

Let the class
{\mathcal{G}}
of graphs be bridge-addable and closed under taking subgraphs.
Let
{\mathcal{A}}
be the subclass of graphs in
{\mathcal{G}}
which are addable to
{\mathcal{G}}
, and suppose that
0<\rho_{\mathcal{A}}=\rho_{\mathcal{G}}<\infty
. Let
H
be a connected graph in
{\mathcal{G}}\setminus{\mathcal{A}}
. Then
H
is sublinearly limited in
{\mathcal{G}}
.

Note that in this lemma the graphs which are addable to
{\mathcal{G}}
are in fact free for
{\mathcal{G}}
.

Proof.

Write
k_{n}
for
k_{n}({\mathcal{G}},H)
, and suppose for a contradiction that

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\,k_{n}/n=\nu>0.

Let
\rho:=\rho_{\mathcal{A}}=\rho_{\mathcal{G}}
, and let
h=v(H)
.
Let
0<\eta\leq\nu/2
be sufficiently small that
2\eta\,h<1
and

\left(\frac{\rho^{h}h}{2\eta\,(\mbox{\small aut}H)}\right)^{\eta}=1+\delta>1

for some
0<\delta<\frac{1}{2}
.
The class
{\mathcal{A}}
is bridge-addable and decomposable, so it is addable; and
0<\rho_{\mathcal{A}}<\infty
. Hence

{\mathcal{A}}
has growth constant
\rho^{-1}
, by Theorem 3.3 of
[
21
]
.
Let
j=j(n)=\lceil\eta n\rceil
for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
Let
n_{0}
be sufficiently large that for each
n\geq n_{0}/2

|{\mathcal{A}}_{n}|\geq n!\,\rho^{-n}(1-\delta/2)^{n}\,,

(21)

and for each
n\geq n_{0}
we have
n\geq 2hj
and

|{\mathcal{G}}_{n}|<n!\,\rho^{-n}(1+\delta/4)^{n}.

(22)

Let
n\geq n_{0}
be such that
k_{n}\geq j
.
We construct many graphs in
{\mathcal{G}}_{n}
as follows. Start with any graph on
[n]
consisting of
j
disjoint copies of
H
together with isolated vertices (this graph must be in
{\mathcal{G}}_{n}
);
add a graph in
{\mathcal{A}}
on the set
W
of
n-jh\geq n/2\geq n_{0}/2
vertices not in the copies of
H
;
and finally form
G\in{\mathcal{G}}_{n}
by adding, for each copy of
H
, a link edge between a vertex in
H
and a vertex in
W
. The number of constructions is at least

\frac{(n)_{jh}}{j!\,(\mbox{\small aut}\,H)^{j}}\,|{\mathcal{A}}_{n-jh}|\,(h\,n/2)^{j}.

But
{\mathcal{A}}
is closed under taking subgraphs, so graphs in
{\mathcal{A}}
cannot have a component
H
or a pendant appearance of
H
; and thus each graph
G
is constructed just once. Hence, using the three inequalities (
21
),
j!\leq(\eta n)^{j}
(for
n
sufficiently large), and
(1+\delta)(1-\delta/2)>1+\delta/4
, we have

\displaystyle|{\mathcal{G}}_{n}|

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{(n)_{jh}}{j!\,(\mbox{\small aut}\,H)^{j}}(n-jh)!\,\rho^{-n+jh}(1-\delta/2)^{n}\,(h\,n/2)^{j}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle n!\,\rho^{-n}\,\left(\frac{\rho^{h}hn}{2\,\eta n\,(\mbox{\small aut}\,H)}\right)^{j}\,(1-\delta/2)^{n}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle n!\,\rho^{-n}\,(1+\delta)^{n}(1-\delta/2)^{n}

\displaystyle>

\displaystyle n!\,\rho^{-n}\,(1+\delta/4)^{n}.

This contradicts (
22
) and completes the proof.
∎

The following lemma is related to Lemma 4.6 in
[
14
]
and Lemmas 3.3 and 3.4 in
[
17
]
.

Lemma 20
.

Let the class
{\mathcal{G}}
of graphs be bridge-addable, and let
{\mathcal{A}}
be a decomposable subclass of
{\mathcal{G}}
.
Suppose that each connected graph
H
not in
{\mathcal{A}}
is sublinearly limited in
{\mathcal{G}}
. Then for
R_{n}\in_{u}{\mathcal{G}}
, whp
\mbox{{\rm Frag}}(R_{n})\in{\mathcal{A}}
.

Proof.

Let
H
be a connected graph not in
{\mathcal{A}}
, and
write
k_{n}
for
k_{n}({\mathcal{G}},H)
. Let
{\mathcal{B}}
be the set of graphs
G\in{\mathcal{G}}
such that
\mbox{{\rm Frag}}(G)
has a component
H
and
{\mbox{\rm frag}}(G)\leq n/3
, where
n=v(G)
.
We shall show first that

\mathbb{P}(R_{n}\in{\mathcal{B}})\leq\frac{3\,k_{n}}{2\,v(H)\,n}.

(23)

Given a graph
G\in{\mathcal{B}}_{n}
, add any edge between a component
H
and a vertex in
\rm Big(G)
, to form
G^{\prime}
. Since
\rm big(G)\geq 2n/3
this gives at least
v(H)\cdot 2n/3
constructions of graphs
G^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{G}}_{n}
.
But each graph
G^{\prime}
constructed can have at most
k_{n}
pendant copies of
H
; and thus
G^{\prime}
can be constructed at most
k_{n}
times. Hence

v(H)\frac{2n}{3}|{\mathcal{B}}_{n}|\leq k_{n}|{\mathcal{G}}_{n}|

and (
23
) follows.

Now let
\epsilon>0
. Since
{\mathcal{G}}
is bridge-addable,
\mathbb{E}[{\mbox{\rm frag}}(R_{n})]<2
(see
[
17
]
), and so
\mathbb{P}({\mbox{\rm frag}}(R_{n})\geq 4/\epsilon)<\epsilon/2
by Markov’s inequality.
List the connected graphs not in

{\mathcal{A}}
in order of non-decreasing number of vertices as
H_{1},H_{2},\ldots
. Let
j_{0}
be sufficiently large that
v(H_{j})>4/\epsilon
for any

. Assume that

. Then, using (

) for the third inequality below (and noting that

),

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(\mbox{{\rm Frag}}(R_{n})\not\in{\mathcal{A}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(\mbox{{\rm Frag}}(R_{n})\mbox{ has a component not in }{\mathcal{A}})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(\mbox{{\rm Frag}}(R_{n})\mbox{ has a component in }\{H_{1},\ldots,H_{j_{0}}\})+\mathbb{P}({\mbox{\rm frag}}(R_{n})>4/\epsilon)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{j_{0}}\mathbb{P}(\mbox{{\rm Frag}}(R_{n})\mbox{ has a component }H_{j})+\epsilon/2

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{j_{0}}\frac{3\,k_{n}({\mathcal{G}},H_{j})}{2\,v(H_{j})\,n}+\epsilon/2\;\;=\;\;o(1)+\epsilon/2\;\;<\epsilon

if
n
is sufficiently large.
∎

6.5
Graphs which are addable and removable in
{\mathcal{G}}^{\delta\geq 2}

We need one last easy deterministic lemma.

Lemma 21
.

Let the class
{\mathcal{G}}
of graphs be trimmable with
{\mathcal{G}}^{\delta\geq 2}
non-empty, let
{\mathcal{A}}
be the class of graphs which are addable and removable in

{\mathcal{G}}
, and let
{\mathcal{B}}
be the class of graphs which are addable and removable in

{\mathcal{G}}^{\delta\geq 2}
. Then
{\mathcal{B}}={\mathcal{A}}^{\delta\geq 2}
.

Proof.

Let
H\in{\mathcal{A}}^{\delta\geq 2}
. Consider any graph
G
. If
G\cup H\in{\mathcal{G}}^{\delta\geq 2}
then
G\in{\mathcal{G}}
(since
G\cup H\in{\mathcal{G}}
and
H\in{\mathcal{A}}
) so
G\in{\mathcal{G}}^{\delta\geq 2}
.
If
G\in{\mathcal{G}}^{\delta\geq 2}
then
G\cup H\in{\mathcal{G}}
(since
H\in{\mathcal{A}}
) so
G\cup H\in{\mathcal{G}}^{\delta\geq 2}
. Hence
H\in{\mathcal{B}}
, and it follows that
{\mathcal{A}}^{\delta\geq 2}\subseteq{\mathcal{B}}
.

Now let
H\in{\mathcal{B}}
. Consider any graph
G
.
If
G\cup H\in{\mathcal{G}}
then
{\rm Core}(G)\cup H\in{\mathcal{G}}^{\delta\geq 2}
, so
{\rm Core}(G)
is in
{\mathcal{G}}^{\delta\geq 2}
or is the null graph, and so
G\in{\mathcal{G}}
.
If
G\in{\mathcal{G}}
then
{\rm Core}(G)
is in
{\mathcal{G}}^{\delta\geq 2}
or is the null graph; so
{\rm Core}(G)\cup H\in{\mathcal{G}}^{\delta\geq 2}
, and thus
G\cup H\in{\mathcal{G}}
.
Hence
H\in{\mathcal{A}}
, and it follows that
{\mathcal{B}}\subseteq{\mathcal{A}}^{\delta\geq 2}
.
∎

6.6
Completing the proof of Theorem
3

We may now use the assembled lemmas above, Lemma
16
to Lemma
21
, to complete the proof of Theorem
3
.

Proof of Theorem
3
.

Let
{\mathcal{G}}
and
{\mathcal{A}}
satisfy the conditions in Theorem
3
.
Since
{\mathcal{G}}
is closed under taking subgraphs,
{\mathcal{A}}
must also have this property. Thus
K_{1}
is in
{\mathcal{A}}
and so
K_{1}
is free for
{\mathcal{G}}
; that is,
{\mathcal{G}}
is trimmable. Also
{\mathcal{A}}
is addable, and
{\mathcal{A}}\supset{\mathcal{F}}
. Hence
{\mathcal{G}}
and
{\mathcal{A}}
satisfy the conditions in Theorem
2
(as stated in the theorem). Let
\rho_{2}
be as in Theorem
2
.

By Lemma
19
, each connected graph not in
{\mathcal{A}}
is sublinearly limited in
{\mathcal{G}}
.
Let
R_{n}\in_{u}{\mathcal{G}}
. Then, by Lemma
20
, whp
\mbox{{\rm Frag}}(R_{n})\in{\mathcal{A}}
. Hence, by Lemma
16
,
A(\rho)
is finite, and the fragment of
R_{n}
converges in distribution to
BP({\mathcal{A}},\rho)
.

Now consider cores as well as fragments.
By part (c) of Theorem
2
, there exists
\alpha>0
such that whp
{\rm core}(R_{n})\geq\alpha n
. But
\mathbb{E}[{\mbox{\rm frag}}(R_{n})]<2
, and it follows easily (using Markov’s inequality) that whp
{\rm core}(R_{n})\gg{\mbox{\rm frag}}(R_{n})
. Hence by Lemma
17
whp

\mbox{{\rm Frag}}({\rm Core}(R_{n}))={\rm Core}(\mbox{{\rm Frag}}(R_{n}))
.
Also, since
\mbox{{\rm Frag}}(R_{n})
converges in distribution to
BP({\mathcal{G}},\rho_{0})
it follows that
{\rm Core}(\mbox{{\rm Frag}}(R_{n}))
converges in distribution to
{\rm Core}(BP({\mathcal{G}},\rho_{0}))
and thus by Lemma
18
to
BP({\mathcal{G}}^{\delta\geq 2},\rho_{2})
.

Finally consider
\mbox{{\rm Frag}}(R^{\prime}_{n})
where
R^{\prime}_{n}\in_{u}{\mathcal{G}}^{\delta\geq 2}
.
By Theorem
2
part (d),
{\mathcal{G}}^{\delta\geq 2}
is smooth.
Observe that
{\mathcal{G}}^{\delta\geq 2}
is bridge-addable, and by Lemma
21

{\mathcal{A}}^{\delta\geq 2}
is the class of graphs which are addable and removable in
{\mathcal{G}}^{\delta\geq 2}
.
By Lemma
19
each connected graph not in
{\mathcal{A}}
is sublinearly limited in
{\mathcal{G}}
.
Thus by Lemma
20
applied to
{\mathcal{G}}^{\delta\geq 2}
and
{\mathcal{A}}^{\delta\geq 2}
, whp
\mbox{{\rm Frag}}(R^{\prime}_{n})
is in
{\mathcal{A}}^{\delta\geq 2}
.
Hence, by Lemma
16
applied to
{\mathcal{G}}^{\delta\geq 2}
and
{\mathcal{A}}^{\delta\geq 2}
, we see that
the fragment of
R^{\prime}_{n}
converges in distribution to
BP({\mathcal{A}}^{\delta\geq 2},\rho_{2})
,
as required.

We have now completed the proof of Theorem
3
. But note that, given that the core of
R_{n}
has vertex set
W
, the core is uniformly distributed on the graphs in
{\mathcal{G}}^{\delta\geq 2}
on
W
.
Thus the last part of the theorem we proved, that the fragment of
R^{\prime}_{n}\in_{u}{\mathcal{G}}^{\delta\geq 2}
converges in distribution to
BP({\mathcal{A}}^{\delta\geq 2},\rho_{2})
, easily implies the earlier result that for
R_{n}\in_{u}{\mathcal{G}}
, the fragment of
{\rm Core}(R_{n})
has the same limiting distribution.
∎

7
Concluding Remarks

We have seen how certain results concerning the fragment and the 2-core of random graphs sampled from an addable minor-closed class or a surface class
{\mathcal{E}}^{S}
(as in Theorem
1
) can be extended to random graphs sampled from more general graph classes with some similar structure. The main new theorems, Theorems
2
and
3
, apply to addable minor-closed classes and surface classes but also to many other graph classes.
Theorem
2
concerns a trimmable graph class
{\mathcal{G}}
with
0<\rho_{\mathcal{G}}<\infty
which has a suitably large addable subclass, and Theorem
3
has slightly stronger assumptions.
We have focussed on showing smoothness of
{\mathcal{G}}
and related graph classes, showing that the limiting distribution of the fragment of a corresponding random graph
R_{n}
is a Boltzmann Poisson distribution, determining the order of the core of
R_{n}
, and considering the fragment of the core.

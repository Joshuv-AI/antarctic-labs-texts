Several series expansions for real powers and several formulas for partial Bell polynomials of sinc and sinhc functions in terms of central factorial and Stirling numbers of second kind Thanks: This paper was typeset usingAmS- L a T e X
Feng Qi, Peter Taylor

Abstract
. In the paper, with the aid of the Faà di Bruno formula, in terms of central factorial numbers of the second kind, and with the terminology of the Stirling numbers of the second kind, the authors derive several series expansions for any positive integer powers of the sinc and sinhc functions, discover several closed-form formulas for partial Bell polynomials of all derivatives of the sinc function, establish several series expansions for any real powers of the sinc and sinhc functions, and present several identities for central factorial numbers of the second kind and for the Stirling numbers of the second kind.

1.
Motivations

According to common knowledge in complex analysis, the principal value of the number
\alpha^{\beta}
for
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{C}
with
\alpha\neq 0
is defined by
\alpha^{\beta}=\te^{\beta\ln\alpha}
, where
\ln\alpha=\ln|\alpha|+\ti\arg\alpha
and
\arg\alpha
are principal values of the logarithm and argument of
\alpha\neq 0
respectively. In what follows, we always consider principal values of real or complex functions discussed in this paper.

In mathematical sciences, one commonly considers elementary functions

\displaystyle\te^{z},\quad\ln(1+z),\quad\sin z,\quad\csc z,\quad\cos z,\quad\sec z,\quad\tan z,\quad\cot z,

\displaystyle\arcsin z,\quad\arccos z,\quad\arctan z,\quad\sinh z,\quad\csch z,\quad\cosh z,\quad\sech z,

\displaystyle\tanh z,\quad\coth z,\quad\arcsinh z,\quad\arccosh z,\quad\arctanh z

and their series expansions at the point
z=0
. Their series expansions can be found in mathematical handbooks such as
[
1
,
15
,
27
]
.

What are series expansions at
x=0
of positive integer powers or real powers of these functions?

It is combinatorial knowledge
[
10
,
13
]
that coefficients of the series expansion of the power function
(\te^{z}-1)^{k}
for
k\in\mathbb{N}=\{1,2,\dotsc\}
are the Stirling numbers of the second kind, while coefficients of the series expansion of the power function
[\ln(1+z)]^{k}
for
k\in\mathbb{N}
are the Stirling numbers of the first kind. In other words, the power functions
(\te^{z}-1)^{k}
and
[\ln(1+z)]^{k}
for
k\in\mathbb{N}
are generating functions of the Stirling numbers of the first and second kinds.

In the paper
[
9
]
, among other things, Carlitz introduced the notion of weighted Stirling numbers of the second kind
R(n,k,r)
. Carlitz also proved in
[
9
]
that the numbers
R(n,k,r)
can be generated by

\frac{(\te^{z}-1)^{k}}{k!}\te^{rz}=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}R(n,k,r)\frac{z^{n}}{n!}

(1.1)

and can be explicitly expressed by

R(n,k,r)=\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{j=0}^{k}(-1)^{k-j}\binom{k}{j}(r+j)^{n}

(1.2)

for
r\in\mathbb{R}
and
n\geq k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}=\{0,1,2,\dotsc\}
.
Specially, when
r=0
, the quantities
R(n,k,0)
become the Stirling numbers of the second kind
S(n,k)
. By the way, the notion

{n\brace k}_{r}=R(n-r,k-r,r)

is called the
r
-Stirling numbers of the second kind in
[
6
]
by Broder.

The central factorial numbers of the second kind
T(n,\ell)
for
n\geq\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
can be generated
[
8
,
22
]
by

\frac{1}{\ell!}\biggl(2\sinh\frac{z}{2}\biggr)^{\ell}=\sum_{n=\ell}^{\infty}T(n,\ell)\frac{z^{n}}{n!}.

(1.3)

In
[
39
, Chapter 6, Eq. (26)]
, it was established that

T(n,\ell)=\frac{1}{\ell!}\sum_{j=0}^{\ell}(-1)^{j}\binom{\ell}{j}\biggl(\frac{\ell}{2}-j\biggr)^{n}.

(1.4)

Note that
T(0,0)=1
and
T(n,0)=0
for
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
See also
[
8
, Proposition 2.4, (xii)]
and
[
33
,
37
]
. Comparing (
1.3
) with (
1.1
) or comparing (
1.4
) with (
1.2
) gives the relation

R\biggl(n,\ell,-\frac{\ell}{2}\biggr)=T(n,\ell)

(1.5)

between weighted Stirling numbers of the second kind and central factorial numbers of the second kind.
See also
[
37
, Theorem 3.1]
.

In the handbook
[
15
]
, series expansions at
z=0
of the functions
\arcsin^{2}z
,
\arcsin^{3}z
,
\sin^{2}z
,

For
z\in\mathbb{C}
, the functions

\sinc z=\begin{cases}\frac{\sin z}{z},&z\neq 0\\
1,&z=0\end{cases}

and

\sinhc z=\begin{cases}\frac{\sinh z}{z},&z\neq 0\\
1,&z=0\end{cases}

are called the sinc function and hyperbolic sinc function respectively. The function
\sinc z
is also called the sine cardinal or sampling function, as well as the function
\sinhc z
is also called hyperbolic sine cardinal, see
[
40
]
. The sinc function
\sinc z
arises frequently in signal processing, the theory of the Fourier transforms, and other areas in mathematics, physics, and engineering. It is easy to see that these two functions
\sinc z
and
\sinhc z
are analytic on
\mathbb{C}
, that is, they are entire functions.

In
[
10
, Theorem 11.4]
and
[
13
, p. 139, Theorem C]
, the Faà di Bruno formula is given for
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
z\in\mathbb{C}
by

\frac{\td^{n}}{\td z^{n}}f\circ h(z)=\sum_{k=1}^{n}f^{(k)}(h(z))B_{n,k}\bigl(h^{\prime}(z),h^{\prime\prime}(z),\dotsc,h^{(n-k+1)}(z)\bigr),

(1.6)

where partial Bell polynomials
B_{n,k}
are defined for
n\geq k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
by

B_{n,k}(z_{1},z_{2},\dotsc,z_{n-k+1})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i\leq n-k+1\\
\ell_{i}\in\{0\}\cup\mathbb{N}\\
\sum_{i=1}^{n-k+1}i\ell_{i}=n\\
\sum_{i=1}^{n-k+1}\ell_{i}=k\end{subarray}}\frac{n!}{\prod_{i=1}^{n-k+1}\ell_{i}!}\prod_{i=1}^{n-k+1}\biggl(\frac{z_{i}}{i!}\biggr)^{\ell_{i}}

in
[
10
, Definition 11.2]
and
[
13
, p. 134, Theorem A]
.

In this paper, with the help of the Faà di Bruno formula (
1.6
), in terms of central factorial numbers of the second kind
T(n,k)
, and with the terminology of the Stirling numbers of the second kind
S(n,k)
, we will derive several series expansions at
z=0
of the positive integer power functions
\sinc^{\ell}z
and
\sinhc^{\ell}z
for
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
and
z\in\mathbb{C}
, we will deduce several closed-form formulas for central factorial numbers of the second kind
T(j+\ell,\ell)
with
j,\ell\in\mathbb{N}
in terms of the Stirling numbers of the second kind
S(n,k)
, we will discover several closed-form formulas of specific partial Bell polynomials

B_{n,k}\biggl(0,-\frac{1}{3},0,\frac{1}{5},\dotsc,\frac{(-1)^{n-k}}{n-k+2}\sin\frac{(n-k)\pi}{2}\biggr)

for
n\geq k\in\mathbb{N}
, we will establish series expansions at
z=0
of the real power functions
\sinc^{r}z
and
\sinhc^{r}z
for

and
r\in\mathbb{R}
, and we will present several identities for central factorial numbers of the second kind
T(n,k)
and for the Stirling numbers of the second kind
S(n,k)
.

2.
Several series expansions of positive integer powers

In this section, we derive several series expansions at
z=0
of the positive integer power functions
\sinc^{\ell}z
and
\sinhc^{\ell}z
for
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
and
z\in\mathbb{C}
in terms of central factorial numbers of the second kind
T(n,k)
and the Stirling numbers of the second kind
S(n,k)
, we deduce several closed-form formulas of
T(j+\ell,\ell)
for
j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
in terms of the Stirling numbers of the second kind
S(n,k)
, and we present several identities for central factorial numbers of the second kind
T(n,k)
.

Theorem 2.1
.

For
\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
z\in\mathbb{C}
, we have

\sinc^{\ell}z=1+\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{j}\frac{T(\ell+2j,\ell)}{\binom{\ell+2j}{\ell}}\frac{(2z)^{2j}}{(2j)!}.

(2.1)

Proof.

For
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
, the formula

\sin^{\ell}z=\frac{(-1)^{\ell}}{2^{\ell}}\sum_{q=0}^{\ell}(-1)^{q}\binom{\ell}{q}\cos\biggl[(2q-\ell)z-\frac{\ell\pi}{2}\biggr]

(2.2)

is given in
[
18
, Corollary 2.1]
.
Applying the identity

\cos(z-y)=\cos z\cos y+\sin z\sin y

to the formula (
2.2
) leads to

\displaystyle\sin^{\ell}z

\displaystyle=\frac{(-1)^{\ell}}{2^{\ell}}\sum_{q=0}^{\ell}(-1)^{q}\binom{\ell}{q}\biggl(\cos[(2q-\ell)z]\cos\frac{\ell\pi}{2}+\sin[(2q-\ell)z]\sin\frac{\ell\pi}{2}\biggr)

\displaystyle=\frac{(-1)^{\ell}}{2^{\ell}}\cos\frac{\ell\pi}{2}\sum_{q=0}^{\ell}(-1)^{q}\binom{\ell}{q}\Biggl[1+\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{j}(2q-\ell)^{2j}\frac{z^{2j}}{(2j)!}\Biggr]

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{(-1)^{\ell}}{2^{\ell}}\sin\frac{\ell\pi}{2}\sum_{q=0}^{\ell}(-1)^{q}\binom{\ell}{q}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{j}(2q-\ell)^{2j+1}\frac{z^{2j+1}}{(2j+1)!}

\displaystyle=\frac{(-1)^{\ell}}{2^{\ell}}\cos\frac{\ell\pi}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{j}\Biggl[\sum_{q=0}^{\ell}(-1)^{q}\binom{\ell}{q}(2q-\ell)^{2j}\Biggr]\frac{z^{2j}}{(2j)!}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{(-1)^{\ell}}{2^{\ell}}\sin\frac{\ell\pi}{2}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{j}\Biggl[\sum_{q=0}^{\ell}(-1)^{q}\binom{\ell}{q}(2q-\ell)^{2j+1}\Biggr]\frac{z^{2j+1}}{(2j+1)!}.

Replacing
\ell
by
2\ell-1
and by
2\ell
and simplifying result in the series expansions

\sin^{2\ell-1}z=\frac{(-1)^{\ell}}{2^{2\ell-1}}\sum_{j=\ell}^{\infty}(-1)^{j-1}\Biggl[\sum_{q=0}^{2\ell-1}(-1)^{q}\binom{{2\ell-1}}{q}(2q-2\ell+1)^{2j-1}\Biggr]\frac{z^{2j-1}}{(2j-1)!}

(2.3)

and

\sin^{2\ell}z=\frac{(-1)^{\ell}}{2^{2\ell}}\sum_{j=\ell}^{\infty}(-1)^{j}2^{2j}\Biggl[\sum_{q=0}^{2\ell}(-1)^{q}\binom{{2\ell}}{q}(q-\ell)^{2j}\Biggr]\frac{z^{2j}}{(2j)!}.

(2.4)

The series expansions (
2.3
) and (
2.4
) can be reformulated as

\sinc^{2\ell-1}z=\frac{1}{2^{2\ell-1}}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{j-1}}{(2\ell+2j-1)!}\Biggl[\sum_{q=0}^{2\ell-1}(-1)^{q}\binom{{2\ell-1}}{q}(2q-2\ell+1)^{2\ell+2j-1}\Biggr]z^{2j}

and

\sinc^{2\ell}z=\frac{1}{2^{2\ell}}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{j}}{(2\ell+2j)!}\Biggl[\sum_{q=0}^{2\ell}(-1)^{q}\binom{{2\ell}}{q}(2q-2\ell)^{2\ell+2j}\Biggr]z^{2j}.

for
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
and
z\in\mathbb{C}
. These two series expansions can be unified and rearranged as the series expansion (
2.1
). Theorem
2.1
is thus proved.
∎

Corollary 2.1
.

For
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

T(2j-1,2\ell-1)=\begin{cases}0,&1\leq j\leq\ell-1\\
1,&j=\ell\end{cases}

and

T(2j,2\ell)=\begin{cases}0,&1\leq j\leq\ell-1\\
1,&j=\ell\end{cases}

Proof.

This follows from Theorem
2.1
and its proof.
∎

Theorem 2.2
.

For
j,\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, we have

T(2j+\ell+1,\ell)=0.

(2.5)

For
\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
z\in\mathbb{C}
, the series expansions

\sinhc^{\ell}z=1+\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{T(2j+\ell,\ell)}{\binom{2j+\ell}{\ell}}\frac{(2z)^{2j}}{(2j)!}

(2.6)

and (
2.1
) are valid.

Proof.

Replacing
z
by
2z
in (
1.3
) and rearranging yield

\biggl(\frac{\sinh z}{z}\biggr)^{\ell}=1+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{T(n+\ell,\ell)}{\binom{n+\ell}{\ell}}\frac{(2z)^{n}}{n!}.

Considering that the function
\frac{\sinh x}{x}
is even on
\mathbb{R}
, we conclude that the identity (
2.5
) and the series (
2.6
) are valid.

Substituting
z\operatorname{i}
for
z
in (
2.6
) and employing the relation
\sinh(z\operatorname{i})=\operatorname{i}\sin z
give the series expansion (
2.1
) in Theorem
2.1
.
∎

Theorem 2.3
.

For
j,\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
z\in\mathbb{C}
, we have

\sum_{k=0}^{2j+1}(-1)^{k}\binom{2j+1}{k}\biggl(\frac{2}{\ell}\biggr)^{k}\frac{S(k+\ell,\ell)}{\binom{k+\ell}{\ell}}=0

(2.7)

and

\sinc^{\ell}z=1+\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{j}\Biggl[\sum_{k=0}^{2j}(-1)^{k}\binom{2j}{k}\biggl(\frac{2}{\ell}\biggr)^{k}\frac{S(k+\ell,\ell)}{\binom{k+\ell}{\ell}}\Biggr]\frac{(\ell z)^{2j}}{(2j)!}.

(2.8)

Proof.

Taking
r=0
in (
1.1
) and reformulating give

\biggl(\frac{\te^{z}-1}{z}\biggr)^{k}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{S(n+k,k)}{\binom{n+k}{k}}\frac{z^{n}}{n!}.

(2.9)

Since

\sin z=\frac{\te^{z\ti}-\te^{-z\ti}}{2\ti}=\frac{\te^{2z\ti}-1}{2\ti}\te^{-z\ti},

by (
2.9
) and the Cauchy product of two series, we obtain

\displaystyle\sinc^{\ell}z

\displaystyle=\biggl(\frac{\sin z}{z}\biggr)^{\ell}=\biggl(\frac{\te^{2z\ti}-1}{2z\ti}\biggr)^{\ell}\te^{-\ell z\ti}

\displaystyle=\Biggl[\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{S(n+\ell,\ell)}{\binom{n+\ell}{\ell}}\frac{(2z\ti)^{n}}{n!}\Biggr]\Biggl[\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-\ell z\ti)^{n}}{n!}\Biggr]

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\Biggl[\sum_{n=0}^{j}\frac{S(n+\ell,\ell)}{\binom{n+\ell}{\ell}}\frac{(2\ti)^{n}}{n!}\frac{(-\ell\ti)^{j-n}}{(j-n)!}\Biggr]z^{j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{j}\frac{\ell^{j}}{j!}\Biggl[\sum_{k=0}^{j}(-1)^{k}\binom{j}{k}\biggl(\frac{2}{\ell}\biggr)^{k}\frac{S(k+\ell,\ell)}{\binom{k+\ell}{\ell}}\Biggr]\biggl(\cos\frac{j\pi}{2}+\ti\sin\frac{j\pi}{2}\biggr)z^{j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{j}\frac{\ell^{2j}}{(2j)!}\Biggl[\sum_{k=0}^{2j}(-1)^{k}\binom{2j}{k}\biggl(\frac{2}{\ell}\biggr)^{k}\frac{S(k+\ell,\ell)}{\binom{k+\ell}{\ell}}\Biggr]z^{2j}

\displaystyle\quad+\ti\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{j}\frac{\ell^{2j-1}}{(2j-1)!}\Biggl[\sum_{k=0}^{2j-1}(-1)^{k}\binom{2j-1}{k}\biggl(\frac{2}{\ell}\biggr)^{k}\frac{S(k+\ell,\ell)}{\binom{k+\ell}{\ell}}\Biggr]z^{2j-1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{j}\frac{\ell^{2j}}{(2j)!}\Biggl[\sum_{k=0}^{2j}(-1)^{k}\binom{2j}{k}\biggl(\frac{2}{\ell}\biggr)^{k}\frac{S(k+\ell,\ell)}{\binom{k+\ell}{\ell}}\Biggr]z^{2j}.

The proof of Theorem
2.3
is complete.
∎

Corollary 2.2
.

For
j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

\frac{T(2j+\ell,\ell)}{\binom{2j+\ell}{\ell}}=\sum_{m=0}^{2j}(-1)^{m}\binom{2j}{m}\biggl(\frac{\ell}{2}\biggr)^{m}\frac{S(2j+\ell-m,\ell)}{\binom{2j+\ell-m}{\ell}}.

(2.10)

Proof.

This follows from comparing the series expansion (
2.1
) in Theorem
2.1
with the series expansion (
2.8
) in Theorem
2.3
and simplifying.
∎

Corollary 2.3
.

For
j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

\frac{T(j+\ell,\ell)}{\binom{j+\ell}{\ell}}=\sum_{m=0}^{j}(-1)^{m}\binom{j}{m}\biggl(\frac{\ell}{2}\biggr)^{m}\frac{S(j+\ell-m,\ell)}{\binom{j+\ell-m}{\ell}}.

(2.11)

Proof.

This follows from combining the identities (
2.5
), (
2.7
), and (
2.10
).
∎

3.
Closed-form formulas for specific partial Bell polynomials

In this section, with the help of Theorem
2.1
and other results in the above section, we establish several closed-form formulas for specific partial Bell polynomials
B_{n,k}
of all derivatives at
z=0
of the sinc function
\sinc z
.

Theorem 3.1
.

For
n\geq k\geq 1
and
m\in\mathbb{N}
, partial Bell polynomials
B_{n,k}
satisfy

B_{2m-1,k}\biggl(0,-\frac{1}{3},0,\frac{1}{5},\dotsc,\frac{(-1)^{m}}{2m-k+1}\cos\frac{k\pi}{2}\biggr)=0

and

B_{2m,k}\biggl(0,-\frac{1}{3},0,\frac{1}{5},\dotsc,\frac{(-1)^{m}}{2m-k+2}\sin\frac{k\pi}{2}\biggr)\\
=(-1)^{m+k}\frac{2^{2m}}{k!}\sum_{j=1}^{k}(-1)^{j}\binom{k}{j}\frac{T(2m+j,j)}{\binom{2m+j}{j}}.

Proof.

From

\sinc z=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{j}}{2j+1}\frac{z^{2j}}{(2j)!},\quad z\in\mathbb{C},

it follows that

(\sinc z)^{(2j)}\big|_{z=0}=\frac{(-1)^{j}}{2j+1}\quad\text{and}\quad(\sinc z)^{(2j-1)}\big|_{z=0}=0

(3.1)

for
j\in\mathbb{N}
.

On
[
13
, p. 133]
, the identity

\frac{1}{m!}\Biggl(\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}z_{\ell}\frac{t^{\ell}}{\ell!}\Biggr)^{m}=\sum_{n=m}^{\infty}B_{n,m}(z_{1},z_{2},\dotsc,z_{n-m+1})\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

(3.2)

is given for
m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. The formula (
3.2
) implies that

B_{n+k,k}(z_{1},z_{2},\dotsc,z_{n+1})=\binom{n+k}{k}\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{\td^{n}}{\td t^{n}}\Biggl[\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\frac{z_{\ell+1}}{(\ell+1)!}t^{\ell}\Biggr]^{k}

(3.3)

for
n\geq k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. Substituting
z_{2j}=\frac{(-1)^{j}}{2j+1}
and
z_{2j-1}=0
, that is,
z_{j}=\frac{1}{j+1}\cos\bigl(\frac{j}{2}\pi\bigr)
, for
j\in\mathbb{N}
into (
3.3
) results in

\displaystyle\quad B_{n+k,k}\biggl(0,-\frac{1}{3},0,\frac{1}{5},\dotsc,\frac{1}{n+2}\cos\biggl(\frac{n+1}{2}\pi\biggr)\biggr)

\displaystyle=\binom{n+k}{k}\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{\td^{n}}{\td t^{n}}\Biggl[\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(\ell+2)!}\cos\biggl(\frac{\ell+1}{2}\pi\biggr)t^{\ell}\Biggr]^{k}

\displaystyle=\binom{n+k}{k}\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{\td^{n}}{\td t^{n}}\biggl(\frac{\sinc t-1}{t}\biggr)^{k}

\displaystyle=\binom{n+k}{k}\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{\td^{n}}{\td t^{n}}\Biggl[\frac{(-1)^{k}}{t^{k}}+\frac{(-1)^{k}}{t^{k}}\sum_{j=1}^{k}(-1)^{j}\binom{k}{j}(\sinc t)^{j}\Biggr]

\displaystyle=\binom{n+k}{k}\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{\td^{n}}{\td t^{n}}\Biggl(\frac{(-1)^{k}}{t^{k}}\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{\ell}\Biggl[\sum_{j=1}^{k}\binom{k}{j}\frac{1}{2^{j}}\frac{1}{(j+2\ell)!}

\displaystyle\quad\times\sum_{q=0}^{j}(-1)^{q}\binom{{j}}{q}(2q-j)^{j+2\ell}\Biggr]t^{2\ell}\Biggr)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{k}\binom{n+k}{k}\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{\td^{n}}{\td t^{n}}\sum_{\ell=k}^{\infty}(-1)^{\ell}\Biggl[\sum_{j=1}^{k}\binom{k}{j}\frac{1}{2^{j}}\frac{1}{(j+2\ell)!}

\displaystyle\quad\times\sum_{q=0}^{j}(-1)^{q}\binom{{j}}{q}(2q-j)^{j+2\ell}\Biggr]t^{2\ell-k}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{k}\binom{n+k}{k}\lim_{t\to 0}\sum_{\ell=k}^{\infty}(-1)^{\ell}\Biggl[\sum_{j=1}^{k}\binom{k}{j}\frac{1}{2^{j}}\frac{1}{(j+2\ell)!}

\displaystyle\quad\times\sum_{q=0}^{j}(-1)^{q}\binom{{j}}{q}(2q-j)^{j+2\ell}\Biggr]\langle 2\ell-k\rangle_{n}t^{2\ell-k-n}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}0,\quad n+k=2m+1\\
(-1)^{k+m}\frac{(2m)!}{k!}\sum_{j=1}^{k}\binom{k}{j}\frac{1}{2^{j}(j+2m)!}\sum_{q=0}^{j}(-1)^{q}\binom{{j}}{q}(2q-j)^{j+2m},\quad n+k=2m\end{cases}

for
m\in\mathbb{N}
and
n\geq k\geq 1
, where we used the series expansion (
2.1
) in Theorem
2.1
. The proof of Theorem
3.1
is complete.
∎

Corollary 3.1
.

For
k\geq 2
and
1\leq\ell\leq k-1
, we have

\sum_{j=1}^{k}(-1)^{j}\binom{k}{j}\frac{T(2\ell+j,j)}{\binom{2\ell+j}{j}}=0

(3.4)

and

\sum_{j=1}^{k}(-1)^{j}\binom{k}{j}\sum_{m=0}^{2\ell}(-1)^{m}\binom{2\ell}{m}\biggl(\frac{j}{2}\biggr)^{m}\frac{S(2\ell+j-m,j)}{\binom{2\ell+j-m}{j}}=0.

Proof.

This follows from the proof of Theorem
3.1
and further making use of the formula (
2.10
).
∎

Corollary 3.2
.

For
n\geq k\geq 1
, partial Bell polynomials
B_{n,k}
satisfy

B_{n,k}\biggl(0,-\frac{1}{3},0,\frac{1}{5},\dotsc,\frac{1}{n-k+2}\cos\biggl(\frac{n-k+1}{2}\pi\biggr)\biggr)\\
=(-1)^{k}\cos\biggl(\frac{n\pi}{2}\biggr)\frac{2^{n}}{k!}\sum_{j=1}^{k}(-1)^{j}\binom{k}{j}\frac{T(n+j,j)}{\binom{n+j}{j}}

and

B_{n,k}\biggl(0,-\frac{1}{3},0,\frac{1}{5},\dotsc,\frac{1}{n-k+2}\cos\biggl(\frac{n-k+1}{2}\pi\biggr)\biggr)\\
=(-1)^{k}\cos\biggl(\frac{n\pi}{2}\biggr)\frac{2^{n}}{k!}\sum_{j=1}^{k}(-1)^{j}\binom{k}{j}\sum_{m=0}^{n}(-1)^{m}\binom{n}{m}\biggl(\frac{j}{2}\biggr)^{m}\frac{S(n+j-m,j)}{\binom{n+j-m}{j}}.

Proof.

This follows from combining the identity (
2.5
) with Theorem
3.1
and the formula (
2.11
).
∎

Applying Theorem
3.1
and Corollary
3.2
, we deduce the following corollary.

Corollary 3.3
.

For
z\in\mathbb{C}
, we have

\displaystyle\te^{\sinc z-1}

\displaystyle=1+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{k}\Biggl[\sum_{j=1}^{2k}\frac{(-1)^{j}}{j!}\sum_{\ell=1}^{j}(-1)^{\ell}\binom{j}{\ell}\frac{T(2k+\ell,\ell)}{\binom{2k+\ell}{\ell}}\Biggr]\frac{(2z)^{2k}}{(2k)!}

\displaystyle=1-\frac{z^{2}}{6}+\frac{z^{4}}{45}-\frac{107z^{6}}{45360}+\frac{1189z^{8}}{5443200}-\frac{1633z^{10}}{89812800}+\dotsm

and

\te^{\sinc z-1}=1+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{k}\Biggl[\sum_{j=1}^{2k}\frac{(-1)^{j}}{j!}\sum_{\ell=1}^{j}(-1)^{\ell}\binom{j}{\ell}\\
\times\sum_{m=0}^{2k}(-1)^{m}\binom{2k}{m}\biggl(\frac{\ell}{2}\biggr)^{m}\frac{S(2k+\ell-m,\ell)}{\binom{2k+\ell-m}{\ell}}\Biggr]\frac{(2z)^{2k}}{(2k)!}.

Proof.

Making use of the Faà di Bruno formula (
1.6
), the derivatives in (
3.1
), and Theorem
3.1
, we obtain

\displaystyle\te^{\sinc z}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\biggl(\lim_{z\to 0}\frac{\td^{k}\te^{\sinc z}}{\td z^{k}}\biggr)\frac{z^{k}}{k!}

\displaystyle=\te+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\Biggl[\lim_{z\to 0}\sum_{j=1}^{k}\te^{\sinc z}B_{k,j}\bigl((\sinc z)^{\prime},(\sinc z)^{\prime\prime},\dotsc,(\sinc z)^{(k-j+1)}\bigr)\Biggr]\frac{z^{k}}{k!}

\displaystyle=\te+\te\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\Biggl[\sum_{j=1}^{k}B_{k,j}\bigl((\sinc z)^{\prime}\big|_{z=0},(\sinc z)^{\prime\prime}\big|_{z=0},\dotsc,(\sinc z)^{(k-j+1)}\big|_{z=0}\bigr)\Biggr]\frac{z^{k}}{k!}

\displaystyle=\te+\te\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\Biggl[\sum_{j=1}^{k}B_{k,j}\biggl(0,-\frac{1}{3},\dotsc,\frac{1}{k-j+2}\cos\biggl(\frac{k-j+1}{2}\pi\biggr)\biggr)\Biggr]\frac{z^{k}}{k!}

\displaystyle=\te+\te\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\Biggl[\sum_{j=1}^{2k}B_{2k,j}\biggl(0,-\frac{1}{3},\dotsc,\frac{1}{2k-j+2}\cos\biggl(\frac{2k-j+1}{2}\pi\biggr)\biggr)\Biggr]\frac{z^{2k}}{(2k)!}

\displaystyle=\te+\te\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\Biggl[\sum_{j=1}^{2k}(-1)^{k+j}\frac{2^{2k}}{j!}\sum_{\ell=1}^{j}(-1)^{\ell}\binom{j}{\ell}\frac{T(2k+\ell,\ell)}{\binom{2k+\ell}{\ell}}\Biggr]\frac{z^{2k}}{(2k)!}.

Further considering (
2.10
), we prove Corollary
3.3
.
∎

4.
Series expansions of real powers of sinc and sinhc functions

In this section, with the aid of Theorem
3.1
and other results in the above sections, we establish series expansions at the point
z=0
of the power functions
\sinc^{r}z
and
\sinhc^{r}z
for real
r\in\mathbb{R}
.

Theorem 4.1
.

When
r\geq 0
, the series expansions

\sinc^{r}z=1+\sum_{q=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{q}\Biggl[\sum_{k=1}^{2q}\frac{(-r)_{k}}{k!}\sum_{j=1}^{k}(-1)^{j}\binom{k}{j}\frac{T(2q+j,j)}{\binom{2q+j}{j}}\Biggr]\frac{(2z)^{2q}}{(2q)!}

(4.1)

and

\sinc^{r}z=1+\sum_{q=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{q}\Biggl[\sum_{k=1}^{2q}\frac{(-r)_{k}}{k!}\sum_{j=1}^{k}(-1)^{j}\binom{k}{j}\\
\times\sum_{m=0}^{2q}(-1)^{m}\binom{2q}{m}\biggl(\frac{j}{2}\biggr)^{m}\frac{S(2q+j-m,j)}{\binom{2q+j-m}{j}}\Biggr]\frac{(2z)^{2q}}{(2q)!}

(4.2)

are convergent in
z\in\mathbb{C}
, where the rising factorial
(r)_{k}
is defined by

(r)_{k}=\prod_{\ell=0}^{k-1}(r+\ell)=\begin{cases}r(r+1)\dotsm(r+k-1),&k\geq 1;\\
1,&k=0.\end{cases}

When
r<0
, the series expansions (
4.1
) and (
4.2
) are convergent in
|z|<\pi
.

Proof.

By virtue of the Faà di Bruno formula (
1.6
), we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\td^{j}(\sinc^{r}z)}{\td z^{j}}=\sum_{k=1}^{j}\frac{\td^{k}u^{r}}{\td u^{k}}B_{j,k}\bigl((\sinc z)^{\prime},(\sinc z)^{\prime\prime},\dotsc,(\sinc z)^{(j-k+1)}\bigr)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{j}\langle r\rangle_{k}\sinc^{r-k}zB_{j,k}\bigl((\sinc z)^{\prime},(\sinc z)^{\prime\prime},\dotsc,(\sinc z)^{(j-k+1)}\bigr)

\displaystyle\to\sum_{k=1}^{j}\langle r\rangle_{k}B_{j,k}\biggl(0,-\frac{1}{3},0,\frac{1}{5},\dotsc,\frac{1}{j-k+2}\sin\frac{(j-k)\pi}{2}\biggr),\quad z\to 0

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}0,&j=2m-1\\
\sum_{k=1}^{2m}\langle r\rangle_{k}B_{2m,k}\biggl(0,-\frac{1}{3},0,\frac{1}{5},\dotsc,\frac{1}{j-k+2}\sin\frac{(2m-k)\pi}{2}\biggr),&j=2m\\
\end{cases}

for
m\in\mathbb{N}
, where
u=u(z)=\sinc z
, the notation

\langle r\rangle_{k}=\prod_{k=0}^{k-1}(r-k)=\begin{cases}r(r-1)\dotsm(r-k+1),&k\geq 1\\
1,&k=0\end{cases}

for
r\in\mathbb{R}
is called the falling factorial, and we used derivatives in (
3.1
). Therefore, with the help of Theorem
3.1
, we arrive at

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \sinc^{r}z&=1+\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\biggl[\lim_{z\to 0}\frac{\td^{j}(\sinc^{r}z)}{\td z^{j}}\biggr]\frac{z^{j}}{j!}\\
&=1+\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\biggl[\lim_{z\to 0}\frac{\td^{2m}(\sinc^{r}z)}{\td z^{2m}}\biggr]\frac{z^{2m}}{(2m)!}\end{aligned}

\displaystyle=1+\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\Biggl[\sum_{k=1}^{2m}\langle r\rangle_{k}B_{2m,k}\biggl(0,-\frac{1}{3},0,\frac{1}{5},\dotsc,\frac{1}{j-k+2}\sin\frac{(2m-k)\pi}{2}\biggr)\Biggr]\frac{z^{2m}}{(2m)!}

\displaystyle=1+\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\Biggl[\sum_{k=1}^{2m}(-1)^{m+k}\frac{\langle r\rangle_{k}}{k!}\sum_{j=1}^{k}\binom{k}{j}\frac{1}{2^{j}}\frac{1}{(2m+j)!}\sum_{q=0}^{j}(-1)^{q}\binom{{j}}{q}(2q-j)^{2m+j}\Biggr]z^{2m}

\displaystyle=1+\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{m}\Biggl[\sum_{k=1}^{2m}\frac{(-r)_{k}}{k!}\sum_{j=1}^{k}(-1)^{j}\binom{k}{j}\frac{T(2m+j,j)}{\binom{2m+j}{j}}\Biggr]\frac{(2z)^{2m}}{(2m)!}.

By virtue of (
2.10
), the proof of Theorem
4.1
is thus complete.
∎

Corollary 4.1
.

For
r\in\mathbb{R}
and
z\in\mathbb{C}
, we have

\sinhc^{r}z=1+\sum_{q=1}^{\infty}\Biggl[\sum_{k=1}^{2q}\frac{(-r)_{k}}{k!}\sum_{j=1}^{k}(-1)^{j}\binom{k}{j}\frac{T(2q+j,j)}{\binom{2q+j}{j}}\Biggr]\frac{(2z)^{2q}}{(2q)!}

(4.3)

and

\sinhc^{r}z=1+\sum_{q=1}^{\infty}\Biggl[\sum_{k=1}^{2q}\frac{(-r)_{k}}{k!}\sum_{j=1}^{k}(-1)^{j}\binom{k}{j}\\
\times\sum_{m=0}^{2q}(-1)^{m}\binom{2q}{m}\biggl(\frac{j}{2}\biggr)^{m}\frac{S(2q+j-m,j)}{\binom{2q+j-m}{j}}\Biggr]\frac{(2z)^{2q}}{(2q)!}.

(4.4)

Proof.

The series expansions (
4.3
) and (
4.4
) follow from replacing
\sinc z
by
\sinhc(z\ti)
in (
4.1
) and (
4.2
) and then substituting
z\ti
for
z
.
∎

5.
Combinatorial proofs of two identities

We now modify combinatorial proofs at
https://mathoverflow.net/a/420309/
for the identities (
2.5
) and (
3.4
) as follows.

First combinatorial proof of the identity (
2.5
).

Theorem 8 in
[
6
, p. 247]
states that weighted Stirling numbers of the second kind

{n+k\brace k}_{r}=R(n+k-r,n-r,r)

are the monomial symmetric functions of degree
k
of the integers
r,\dotsc,n
. This means

R(n,k,r)=h_{n-k}(r,r+1,r+2,\dotsc,r+k),

where
h_{m}
is the complete homogenous symmetric function. So it follows that

R\biggl(2m+j-1,j,-\frac{j}{2}\biggr)=h_{2m-1}\biggl(-\frac{j}{2},1-\frac{j}{2},2-\frac{j}{2},\dotsc,\frac{j}{2}\biggr)

and we can pair up each monomial
x_{a_{1}}x_{a_{2}}x_{a_{3}}\dotsm
with

x_{2m-a_{1}}x_{2m-a_{2}}x_{2m-a_{3}}\dotsm=(-1)^{2m-1}x_{a_{1}}x_{a_{2}}x_{a_{3}}\dotsm,

which gives cancellation. The only terms which don’t pair up like this with a different term are those which include
x_{m}=0
and pair with themselves, but by virtue of including a zero multiplicand they don’t contribute anything to the sum. Consequently, by the relation (
1.5
), the identity (
2.5
) is proved.
∎

Second combinatorial proof of the identity (
2.5
).

Let

\mathcal{T}(n,k)=2^{n-k}T(n,k),\quad n\geq k\geq 0

(5.1)

with
\mathcal{T}(0,0)=1
.
This scaled central triangle number
\mathcal{T}(n,k)
counts set partitions of
n
elements into
k
odd-sized blocks. See the references
[
14
,
26
]
. This immediately gives the identity (
2.5
), since an even-sized set cannot be partitioned into an odd number of odd-sized blocks, nor an odd-sized set partitioned into an even number of odd-sized blocks.
∎

A combinatorial proof of the identity (
3.4
).

Since

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{k}(-1)^{j}\binom{k}{j}\frac{T(2\ell+j,j)}{\binom{2\ell+j}{j}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{k}(-1)^{j}\frac{k!(2\ell)!}{(k-j)!(2\ell+j)!}T(2\ell+j,j)

\displaystyle=\frac{(-1)^{k}}{2^{2\ell}\binom{2\ell+k}{k}}\sum_{j=1}^{k}(-1)^{k-j}\binom{2\ell+k}{2\ell+j}\mathcal{T}(2\ell+j,j),

the identity (
3.4
) is equivalent to

\sum_{j=1}^{k}(-1)^{k-j}\binom{2\ell+k}{2\ell+j}\mathcal{T}(2\ell+j,j)=0,\quad 1\leq\ell<k,

(5.2)

where
\mathcal{T}(2\ell+j,j)
is defined by (
5.1
).
The equality (
5.2
) has the following combinatorial proof.

Consider set partitions of
2\ell+k
elements into
k
odd-sized blocks where blocks of size
3
or greater are coloured red and singleton blocks can be coloured red or blue. Then the sum counts such set partitions weighted by
(-1)^{\text{number of blue partitions}}
. Note that
j
is the number of red partitions. Observe that partitions containing at least one singleton can be paired with the partition which differs only in the colour assigned to the singleton with the smallest element, so that the sum counts the number of partitions of
2\ell+k
elements into
k
odd-sized blocks of at least
3
elements each. But, if
k>\ell
, there are no such partitions. The required proof is complete.
∎

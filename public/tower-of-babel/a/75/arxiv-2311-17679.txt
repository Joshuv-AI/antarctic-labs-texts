Density functions for Epsilon multiplicity and families of ideals
Suprajo Das, Sudeshna Roy, Vijaylaxmi Trivedi

Abstract
. A density function for an algebraic invariant is a measurable function on ℝ \mathbb{R} which measures the invariant on an ℝ \mathbb{R} -scale. This function carries a lot more information related to the invariant without seeking extra data. It has turned out to be a useful tool, which was introduced by the third author in [ Tri18 ] , to study the characteristic p p invariant, namely Hilbert-Kunz multiplicity of a homogeneous 𝐦 {\bf m} -primary ideal. Here we construct density functions f A , { I n } f_{A,\{I_{n}\}} for a Noetherian filtration { I n } n ∈ ℕ \{I_{n}\}_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}} of homogeneous ideals and f A , { I n ~ } f_{A,\{\widetilde{I^{n}}\}} for a filtration given by the saturated powers of a homogeneous ideal I I in a standard graded domain A A . As a consequence, we get a density function f ε ⁡ ( I ) f_{\varepsilon(I)} for the epsilon multiplicity ε ⁡ ( I ) \varepsilon(I) of a homogeneous ideal I I in A A . We further show that the function f A , { I n } f_{A,\{I_{n}\}} is continuous everywhere except possibly at one point, and f A , { I n ~ } f_{A,\{\widetilde{I^{n}}\}} is a continuous function everywhere and is continuously differentiable except possibly at one point. As a corollary the epsilon density function f ε ⁡ ( I ) f_{\varepsilon(I)} is a compactly supported continuous function on ℝ \mathbb{R} except at one point, such that ∫ ℝ ≥ 0 f ε ⁡ ( I ) = ε ⁡ ( I ) \int_{\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}}f_{\varepsilon(I)}=\varepsilon(I) . All the three functions f A , { I n } f_{A,\{I^{n}\}} , f A , { I n ~ } f_{A,\{\widetilde{I^{n}}\}} and f ε ⁡ ( I ) f_{\varepsilon(I)} remain invariant under passage to the integral closure of I I . As a corollary of this theory, we observe that the ‘rescaled’ Hilbert-Samuel multiplicities of the diagonal subalgebras form a continuous family.

Introduction

Several well-known algebraic invariants or properties of an ideal are studied in terms of the powers of the ideal and often in an asymptotic way. One such invariant is the epsilon multiplicity
\varepsilon(I)
of an ideal
I
. It is a well-known fact, which was established by Rees, that two
{\bf m}
primary deals
I\subseteq J
in an equidimensional universally catenary Noetherian local ring
(A,{\bf m})
have the same integral closure if and only if they have the same (Hilbert-Samuel) multiplicity. Due to its immense
applications in commutative algebra and algebraic geometry (especially, in the singularity theory), researchers started searching for an analogue of the multiplicity for non
{\bf m}
-primary ideals to examine integral dependence. For this the notion of epsilon multiplicity was introduced by Kleiman
[
KUV
]
and Ulrich-Validashti
[
UV08
]
, which was initially defined as follows:

If
(A,{\bf m})
is a
d
-dimensional local ring and
I
is an ideal in
A
, then the epsilon multiplicity
\varepsilon(I)
of
I
is

\varepsilon(I)=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\dfrac{\ell_{A}(H^{0}_{\bf m}(A/I^{n}))}{n^{d}/d!}=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{\ell_{A}({\widetilde{I^{n}}}/I^{n})}{n^{d}/d!},

because by definition
H^{0}_{\bf m}(A/I^{n})={\widetilde{I^{n}}}/I^{n}
, where
{\widetilde{I^{n}}}=(I^{n}:_{A}{\bf m}^{\infty})
denotes the saturation of the ideal
I^{n}
. In particular, if
I
is
{\bf m}
-primary then it coincides with the usual multiplicity of
I
. It was shown by Cutkosky
[
Cut14
]
that the ‘limsup’ can be replaced by ‘lim’ under very mild conditions on
A
. In
[
CHST05
]
, the authors gave an example of an ideal in a regular local ring having irrational epsilon multiplicity, which suggests that it might be a difficult invariant to compute in general.

In this paper we work in a graded setup and construct a
density function
for
\varepsilon(I)
.
Roughly speaking, a density function for a given invariant, say
\epsilon
, is an integrable function
f_{\epsilon}:{\mathbb{R}}\longrightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
which gives a
new measure

\mu_{f_{\epsilon}}
on
{\mathbb{R}}
such that the integration
\int_{E}f_{\epsilon}
on a subset
E\subset{\mathbb{R}}
is the measure of the invariant
\epsilon
on
E
.
The notion of density function, though seemingly complicated, keeps track of the behaviour of the ‘original’ invariant in more than one way, and also carries information about other related invariants. Such properties were used while studying Hilbert-Kunz multiplicity, which seems hard to compute, and one was able to answer two long-standing questions in
[
Tri23
]
and
[
Tri20
]
. In
[
Tri20
]
one of the crucial steps is the fact (
[
TW21
, Theorem 6.4]
) that the support of the Hilbert-Kunz density function determines the
F
-threshold
c^{I}({\bf m})
of
I
at
{\bf m}
. See
[
MT20
]
,
[
TW22
]
,
[
MM24
]
, and
[
CSTZ24
]
for some recent works on these kind of density functions.

Following is a quick summary of the main results in this paper. Let
A=\mbox{$\oplus$}_{n\geq 0}A_{n}
be a standard graded finitely generated algebra over a field
A_{0}=k
, with homogeneous maximal ideal
{\bf m}=\mbox{$\oplus$}_{n\geq 1}A_{n}
. Further, assume that
A
is a domain of Krull dimension
d\geq 2
. Let
I\subsetneq A
be a nonzero homogeneous ideal. Suppose that
I
is minimally generated in degrees
d_{1}<d_{2}<\cdots<d_{l}

(which is well-defined by Nakayama’s lemma).
We define a function
f_{A,\{I^{n}\}}:{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}\longrightarrow{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}

as

f_{A,\{I^{n}\}}(x)=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{\ell_{k}\big((I^{n})_{\lfloor xn\rfloor}\big)}{n^{d-1}/d!}.

The following result is proved in Theorem
4.4

in a more general form. A crucial input comes from a result of Sturmfels
[
Stu95
]
on vector partition functions.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let the notations be as above. Then the following statements are true:

(1)

f_{A,\{I^{n}\}}(x)
exists as a limit for all
x\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
and it is continuous everywhere except possibly at
x=d_{1}
.

(2)

Further

f_{A,\{I^{n}\}}(x)=\begin{cases}0&\forall x\in[0,d_{1}),\\
{\bf p}_{1}(x)&\forall x\in(d_{1},d_{2}],\\
{\bf p}_{j}(x)&\forall x\in[d_{j},d_{j+1}]~~\mbox{and}~~j=2,\ldots,l-1,\\
{\bf p}_{l}(x)&\forall x\in[d_{l},\infty),\end{cases}

where for each
j=1,\ldots,l
,
{\bf p}_{j}(x)
is
a nonzero rational polynomial of degree
\leq d-1
. Moreover,
{\bf p}_{l}(x)
has degree
d-1
.

(3)

For all integers
c>0
, we have

\int_{0}^{c}f_{A,\{I^{n}\}}(x)dx=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\sum_{m=0}^{cn}\ell_{k}\big((I^{n})_{m}\big)}{n^{d}/d!}.

Next we formulate and study a density function for the family of ideals
\{\widetilde{I^{n}}\}_{n}
.
If the ideal
I
is
\mathfrak{m}
-primary then
\widetilde{I^{n}}=A
for all
n\geq 0
.
Therefore we assume that
I
is non
\mathfrak{m}
-primary. Let
{\mathcal{I}}
be the ideal sheaf associated to
I
on
V=\mbox{Proj}~A
. Let

\pi:X={\bf Proj}\left(\oplus_{n\geq 0}\mathcal{I}^{n}\right)\longrightarrow V

be the blow up of
V
along
{\mathcal{I}}
with the exceptional divisor
E
. Let
H
be the pull back of a hyperplane
section on
V
. Define the constants

\alpha_{I}=\min\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}\mid xH-E~~~~\mbox{is pseudoeffective}\},\quad\beta_{I}=\min\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}\mid xH-E~~~~\mbox{is nef}\}

and define the density function
f_{A,\{\widetilde{I^{n}}\}}:{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}\longrightarrow{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}
as

f_{A,\{\widetilde{I^{n}}\}}(x)=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{\ell_{k}\big((\widetilde{I^{n}})_{\lfloor xn\rfloor}\big)}{n^{d-1}/d!}.

When the saturated powers and symbolic powers coincide (e.g. if
\dim A/I=1
) then
\alpha_{I}
is precisely the ‘Waldschmidt constant’. The definition of
\beta_{I}
is taken from
[
CEL01
]
, where it is called the ‘
s
-invariant’ of the ideal sheaf
\mathcal{I}
.

We prove the following result in Section
5
under the additional assumption that the underlying field
k
is algebraically closed. However, Remark
5.22
justifies the validity of these results over an arbitrary field. For the proof, we use several results about the volume function of
{\mathbb{R}}
-divisors, as given in
[
Laz04a
]
,
[
Laz04b
]
,
[
LM09
]
,
[
Cut14
]
, and
[
Cut15
]
. A slightly weaker result in positive characteristic is obtained simply by using Frobenius morphism and some basic results in algebraic geometry.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let the notations be as before. Then the following statements are true:

(1)

f_{A,\{\widetilde{I^{n}}\}}(x)
exists as a limit for all
x\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
,
f_{A,\{\widetilde{I^{n}}\}}:{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}\longrightarrow{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}
is continuous and

f_{A,\{\widetilde{I^{n}}\}}(x)=d\cdot\mathrm{vol}_{X}(xH-E).

Further, it is a continuously differentiable function everywhere except possibly at
x=\alpha_{I}
.

(2)

If
x\leq\alpha_{I}
, then

f_{A,\{\widetilde{I^{n}}\}}(x)=0
.

(3)

If
x\geq\beta_{I}
, then

f_{A,\{\widetilde{I^{n}}\}}(x)=d\cdot(xH-E)^{d-1}=\sum_{i=0}^{d-1}(-1)^{i}\frac{d!}{(d-1-i)!i!}(H^{d-1-i}\cdot E^{i})x^{d-1-i},

where
H^{d-1-i}\cdot E^{i}
denotes the intersection number of the Cartier divisors
H^{d-1-i}
and
E^{i}
.

(4)

For all real numbers
y\geq x\geq\alpha_{I}
, there is an
inequality

\left(f_{A,\{\widetilde{I^{n}}\}}(y)\right)^{\frac{1}{d-1}}\geq\left(f_{A,\{\widetilde{I^{n}}\}}(x)\right)^{\frac{1}{d-1}}+(y-x)(d\cdot e_{0}(A))^{\frac{1}{d-1}},

where
e_{0}(A)
denotes the multiplicity of
A
.

(5)

For all integers
c>0
, we have

\int_{0}^{c}f_{A,\{\widetilde{I^{n}}\}}(x)dx=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\sum_{m=0}^{cn}\ell_{k}\big((\widetilde{I^{n}})_{m}\big)}{n^{d}/d!}.

Now we are ready to construct a density function associated to the
\varepsilon
-multiplicity.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let the notations be as before. Then the following statements are true:

(1)

The limit

f_{\varepsilon(I)}(x)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\ell_{k}\left(H^{0}_{\bf m}(A/I^{n})\right)_{\lfloor xn\rfloor}}{n^{d-1}/d!}

exists for all
x\in{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}
. Moreover,

f_{\varepsilon(I)}(x)=f_{A,\{\widetilde{I^{n}}\}}(x)-f_{A,\{I^{n}\}}(x)
.

(2)

The function
f_{\varepsilon(I)}
is continuous everywhere except possibly at
x=d_{1}
. Further, it is continuously differentiable outside the finite set
\{\alpha_{I},d_{1},\ldots,d_{l}\}
.

(3)

The support of
f_{\varepsilon(I)}

is contained in the closed interval
[\alpha_{I},d_{l}]
.

(4)

\int_{0}^{\infty}f_{\varepsilon(I)}(x)dx=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\ell_{A}\left(H^{0}_{\bf m}(A/I^{n})\right)}{n^{d}/d!}=:\varepsilon(I).

The function
f_{\varepsilon(I)}
will be called the
\varepsilon
-density function. Here we list some applications of density functions (see Sections
5
,
6
and
7
for more details).

Corollary 1.4
.

Let the notations be as before. Then the following statements are true:

(1)

We have
0<\alpha_{I}\leq d_{1}
, (it is known that
\alpha_{I}\leq\beta_{I}\leq d_{l}
).

(2)

If
\alpha_{I}=\beta_{I}
, then
\varepsilon(I)
is an algebraic number.

(3)

If
\dim A=2
, then
f_{\varepsilon(I)}
is a piecewise (with finitely many pieces) rational polynomial of degree at most one and

\varepsilon(I)\in{\mathbb{Q}}
.

(4)

If
\dim A=3
then
f_{\varepsilon(I)}
is a piecewise (with countably many pieces) real polynomial of degree at most two.

(5)

There exist constants
c\geq 0
and
\beta\geq 0
such that for all
m\geq nd_{l}+c
and
n\geq\beta
,

\ell_{k}\left((I^{n})_{m}\right)=\sum_{i=0}^{d-1}\dfrac{(-1)^{i}}{(d-1-i)!i!}(H^{d-1-i}\cdot E^{i})m^{d-1-i}n^{i}+Q_{l}(m,n),

where
Q_{l}(X,Y)\in{\mathbb{Q}}[X,Y]
is a polynomial of degree
<d-1
(see Remark
5.21
). In particular, the mixed multiplicity
e_{i}(A[It])
of the Rees algebra of the ideal
I
is given in terms of the intersection number (upto sign) of the Cartier divisors
H^{i}
and
E^{d-1-i}
on

X
.

(6)

The multiplicities of the diagonal subalgebras (see Definition
4.7
)
R_{\Delta_{I(p,q)}}
of
R:=A[It]
are determined by the multiplicity
e\big(R_{\Delta_{I(d_{1},1)}}\big)
and polynomials
{\bf p}_{1}(x),\ldots,{\bf p}_{l}(x)
as in Theorem
1.1
: Given
(p,q)\in\mathbb{N}^{2}
,

\displaystyle p/q=d_{1}

\displaystyle\implies e(R_{\Delta_{I(d_{1},1)}})=q^{d-1}e(R_{\Delta_{I(d_{1},1)}}),

\displaystyle p/q\in I(d_{j},d_{j+1})

\displaystyle\implies e(R_{\Delta_{I(p,q)}})=\tfrac{q^{d-1}}{d}\cdot\mathbf{p}_{j}(p/q).

In particular, the rescaled multiplicities of the diagonal subalgebras
\{R_{\Delta_{I(p,q)}}\mid p/q\neq d_{1}\}

form a continuous family.

It should be pointed out that the notion of mixed multiplicities of the Rees algebra
[
HT03
, Section 4]
differs from the classical notion of mixed multiplicities due to Teissier, see
[
KV89
]
. However, in Subsection
5.3
, we show how these two notions are related. We also obtain their interpretation in terms of intersection numbers, which does seem to appear in the literature.

We also observe (Theorem
6.2
) that all the above three density functions remain invariant under passage to the integral closure of
I
. In particular, if
I
and
J
are two homogeneous ideals with the same integral closure then

f_{\varepsilon(I)}(x)=f_{\varepsilon(J)}(x),\quad\mbox{for all}\;\;x\in{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}.

Remark 1.5
.

The converse question about equality of these density functions has been recently answered by the authors in
[
DRT24
]
. By using the above theory of density functions, we gave a set of numerical characterizations of the integral dependence of ideals in a graded setup, where the invariants involved are computatable and well-studied.

We briefly describe the techniques which go in the proofs. We first study the length function

\ell_{k}:\cup_{j}{\mathfrak{R}}C_{j}\longrightarrow{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}\quad\mbox{given by}\quad(m,n)\mapsto\ell_{k}(I^{n})_{m},

where

\{{\mathfrak{R}}C_{j}\}_{1\leq j\leq l}
is a set of cones in
{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}^{2}
. In fact we do it for more general family, namely any Noetherian filtration
\{I_{n}\}_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
of ideals, but to avoid technical notations here we assume
\{I_{n}\}_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}=\{I^{n}\}_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
, where
I
is generated by homogeneous elements of degrees
d_{1}<d_{2}<\cdots<d_{l}
. So far, other than on diagonal subalgebras,
the behaviour of length function has been studied (see
[
Rob98
]
,
[
HT03
]
),
on the set

\{(m,n)\mid m\geq d_{l}n+c,~~~n\geq\beta\}
, where
c
and
\beta
are nonnegative constants,
i.e.
, for
(m,n)
lying in the last cone
{\mathfrak{R}}C_{l}
. We recall that for
(m,n)
belonging to this cone has the nice property that
(m,n)
-diagonal subalgebra is a standard graded ring generated by
(I^{n})_{m}
.

To elaborate further we need to recall some notations used in the paper. Here
A=\mbox{$\oplus$}_{n\geq 0}A_{n}
denotes a standard graded Noetherian domain over a field
k
and
I
be a homogeneous ideal in
A
. The bigraded algebra
A[It]=\mbox{$\oplus$}_{n\geq 0}I^{n}t^{n}
is generated by finitely many homogeneous elements of bidegrees
\{(d_{i},1)\mid 1\leq i\leq l\}
over
A
.
The bidegrees of
A[It]
and the Betti numbers of the resolution of
A[It]
give the following data in the Euclidean plane.

(1)

For
1\leq j<l
let
{\mathfrak{C_{j}}}
denote the cone in
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
generated by the vectors

(d_{j},1)
and
(d_{j+1},1)
and
{\mathfrak{C_{l}}}
be the cone generated by
(d_{l},1)
and
(1,0)
.

(2)

(\lambda_{1},\beta_{1}),\ldots,(\lambda_{l},\beta_{l})\in{\mathbb{Q}}^{2}_{\geq 0}
are associated to the Betti numbers (see Remark
3.2
).

(3)

{\mathfrak{R}}C_{j}={\mathfrak{C}_{j}}+(\lambda_{j},\beta_{j})
are the
restricted cones
which are nothing but the translates of
{\mathfrak{C}_{j}}
by the vector
(\lambda_{j},\beta_{j})
(see Figure
2
).

We prove the following (for more general statement on Noetherian family
\{I_{n}\}_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
see Theorem
3.10
and Theorem
4.4
).

Theorem 1.6
.

There exist a set of homogeneous polynomials
\{P_{1}(X,Y)
,
\ldots,P_{l}(X,Y)\}
and a set of quasi-polynomials
\{Q_{1}(X,Y),\ldots,Q_{l}(X,Y)\}
in
{\mathbb{Q}}[X,Y]
such that for all

,
\deg Q_{j}(X,Y)<\deg P_{j}(X,Y)=d-1
and

(m,n)\in{\mathfrak{R}}C_{j}\cap{\mathbb{N}}^{2}\implies\ell_{k}(I^{n})_{m}=P_{j}(m,n)+Q_{j}(m,n),

and
Q_{l}(X,Y)
is a polynomial.

In Example
3.6
we list a few interesting families of homogeneous ideals which do not arise as a powers of an ideal but do form Noetherian families. So even for the last restricted cone the result is more general.

The proof of Theorem
1.6
makes crucial use of vector partition theorem (Theorem
2.3
) due to Sturmfels
[
Stu95
]
. The vector partition theorem gives that the length function on the restricted cones
{\mathfrak{R}}C_{j}
is a quasi-polynomial function with period given in terms of
(d_{j},1)
. Further (see Corollary
3.4
) the length function restricted to the last chamber

has no periodicity and therefore is a polynomial function. This recovers of
[

, Theorem 1.1]
.

However, if
{\mathfrak{R}}C_{j}
is not the last chamber then Example
3.13
shows that the length function may not be a polynomial function on
{\mathfrak{R}}C_{j}
. The main point of Theorem
1.6
is to show, nevertheless, that the top degree terms, namely,
P_{j}(X,Y)
of the length function is a homogeneous polynomial function of degree
d-1
.

Now if
x\in I(d_{j},d_{j+1})
then (see Lemma
2.9
and Figure
1
) for

the vector
(\lfloor xn\rfloor,n)
belongs to the restricted cone
{\mathfrak{R}}C_{j}
.
This along with the above theorem establishes that the density function
f_{A,\{I^{n}\}}
is a polynomial function on each of the open intervals

.

However, if
x=d_{j}
, where

, then the vector
(d_{j},1)
belongs to the boundaries of the cones
\mathfrak{C}_{j}
and
\mathfrak{C}_{j+1}
and therefore for any

, the vector
(xn,n)
does not belong to any of the restricted cones.

But in the case when
A
is a domain, we have a nonzerodivisor of degree
(d_{1},1)
in

. We use this nonzerodivisor along with elementary Lemmas

and

in Euclidean plane geometry to approximate the elements of degrees
(d_{j}n,n)
, for

and thus prove the continuity of

on

.

Next we construct the density function
f_{A,\{\widetilde{I^{n}}\}}
for the family
\{\widetilde{I^{n}}\}_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
. Note that in general
\{\widetilde{I^{n}}\}_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
(see Example

) need not form a Noetherian filtration of ideals. In particular, the above approach for constructing

does not work here.

Here we consider a sequence
\{g_{n}:{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}\longrightarrow{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}\}_{n}
, where
g_{n}(x)=\frac{\ell_{k}\left((\widetilde{I^{n}})_{\lfloor xn\rfloor}\right)}{n^{d-1}/d!}
. To construct density function

) uses basic results from algebraic geometry. Here we restrict to a subsequence
\{g_{q}\mid q=p^{n}\}_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
and prove that it is uniformly convergent sequence on any given compact subset of
{\mathbb{R}}
and converges to a continous function denoted as
f_{A,\{\widetilde{I^{q}}\}}
. Further the function
f_{A,\{\widetilde{I^{q}}/I^{q}\}}
, which is defined as

f_{A,\{\widetilde{I^{q}}/I^{q}\}}(x)=f_{A,\{\widetilde{I^{q}}\}}(x)-f_{A,\{I^{q}\}}(x)\quad\mbox{gives}\quad\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}f_{A,\{\widetilde{I^{q}}/I^{q}\}}(x)dx=\lim_{q\to\infty}\frac{\ell_{k}(\widetilde{I^{q}}/I^{q})}{q^{d-1}/d!}.

Now the result
[
CHST05
, Theorem
2.3
]
or
[

, Corollary

]
about the existence of the epsilon multiplicity

as a limit implies that

\varepsilon(I)
.

In the case of arbitrary characteristic, we prove in Theorem
5.16
the convergence of
\{g_{n}\}_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
using several results about volume functions of
{\mathbb{R}}
-divisors (following
[

]
,
[
LM09
]
and
[

]

equals

for all

.
This approach gives another proof of the existence of the limit

\varepsilon(I)=\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\frac{\ell_{A}(\widetilde{I^{n}}/I^{n})}{n^{d-1}/d!}
(in the graded case), and also implies that
f_{A,\{\widetilde{I^{q}}\}}=f_{A,\{\widetilde{I^{n}}\}}
when

has characteristic

.

Question 1.8
.

Let
(A,{\bf m})

I

2.
Vector partition function

Definition 2.1
.

Let
M
be an
m_{1}\times n_{1}
matrix of rank
m_{1}
with entries in
{\mathbb{N}}
. Then the corresponding
vector partition function

\phi_{M}\colon\mathbb{N}^{m_{1}}\to\mathbb{N}
is defined as

\phi_{M}({\bf u})=\#\left\{[\lambda]=\scalebox{.8}{$\begin{bmatrix}\lambda_{1}\\
\vdots\\
\lambda_{n_{1}}\end{bmatrix}$}\in\mathbb{N}^{n_{1}}~\big|~M\cdot[{\bf\lambda}]={\bf u}\right\},\quad\mbox{where}\;{\bf u}=\scalebox{.8}{$\begin{bmatrix}u_{1}\\
\vdots\\
u_{m_{1}}\end{bmatrix}$}\in{\mathbb{N}}^{m_{1}}

We recall the notations and a result due to Sturmfels
[
Stu95
]
which gives a polynomial expression of the function
\phi_{M}({\bf u})
in terms of the vector
{\bf u}
. All vectors will be treated as column vectors.

Notations 2.2
.

Let
{\bf v_{i}}
denote the
i^{\rm th}
column vector of the matrix
M
. Define the polyhedral cone

\mathrm{pos}(M)=\Big\{\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n_{1}}\lambda_{i}{\bf v_{i}}\in\mathbb{R}^{m_{1}}~\big|~\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n_{1}}\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\Big\}.

For
\sigma\subset\{1,\ldots,n_{1}\}
,

(1)

M_{\sigma}=[{\bf v_{i}}\mid i\in\sigma]
is the submatrix of
M
.

(2)

\mathrm{pos}(M_{\sigma})
denotes the polyhedral cone generated by the vectors
\{{\bf v_{i}}\mid i\in\sigma\}
.

(3)

M_{\sigma}\mathbb{Z}\subset{\mathbb{Z}}^{m_{1}}
is the abelian group spanned by the columns of
M_{\sigma}
.

A subset
\sigma
of
\{1,\ldots,n_{1}\}
is a
basis
if
\#\sigma=\mathrm{rank}(M_{\sigma})=m_{1}
. The
chamber complex
is the polyhedral subdivision of the cone
\mathrm{pos}(M)
which is defined as the common refinement of cones
\mathrm{pos}(M_{\sigma})
, where
\sigma
runs over all bases. By a
chamber
, we mean the polyhedral cone corresponding to a basis
\sigma
. For a chamber
{\mathfrak{C}}
, let

\Delta({\mathfrak{C}})=\left\{\sigma\subset\{1,\ldots,n_{1}\}\mid{\mathfrak{C}}\subseteq\mathrm{pos}(M_{\sigma})\right\}.

Since the group
M_{\sigma}\mathbb{Z}
has finite index in
\mathbb{Z}^{m_{1}}
for each
\sigma\in\Delta({\mathfrak{C}})
, the group of residue classes

G_{\sigma}:=\mathbb{Z}^{m_{1}}/M_{\sigma}\mathbb{Z}
is a finite group. The image of the element
{\bf u}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{m_{1}}
under the quotient map
{\mathbb{Z}}^{\sigma}\rightarrow G_{\sigma}
is denoted by
[{\bf u}]_{\sigma}
. We say
\sigma\in\Delta({\mathfrak{C}})
is
non-trivial
if
G_{\sigma}\neq\{0\}
.

Theorem 2.3
.

[
Stu95
, Theorem
1
]

If
M
is an
m_{1}\times n_{1}
matrix of nonnegative integers with
m_{1}\leq n_{1}
and

is the corresponding vector partition function given by

{\bf u}\longmapsto\#\bigg\{[\lambda]=\scalebox{.8}{$\begin{bmatrix}\lambda_{1}\\

(m_{1},n_{1})=(\lfloor xn\rfloor+\alpha_{0}+i_{1}h,n+\beta_{0})
. Since
(m_{1},n_{1})\in{\mathfrak{R}}C_{j_{0}}
and
(m_{1}h^{i},n_{1}h^{i})\in\mathfrak{C}_{j_{0}}
, by Lemma
2.8

\{(m_{1}h^{i}+m_{1},n_{1}h^{i}+n_{1})\mid i\geq 1\}\subset{\mathfrak{R}}C_{j_{0}}\cap{\mathbb{N}}^{2}.

For the similar reason
(m_{1}+j,n_{1})
and
(m_{1}+h,n_{1})\in\mathfrak{R}C_{j_{0}}
implies

(3.8)

\{(m_{1}h^{i}+m_{1}+j,n_{1}h^{i}+n_{1})\mid i\geq 1\}\cup\{(m_{1}h^{i}+m_{1}+h,n_{1}h^{i}+n_{1})\mid i\geq 1\}\subseteq{\mathfrak{R}}C_{j_{0}}\cap{\mathbb{N}}^{2}.

Since
\ell_{k}\big((I_{n_{1}})_{m_{1}}\big)=P_{0,0}(m_{1},n_{1})
, we have

\begin{array}[]{lll}\ell_{k}\big((I_{n_{1}h^{i}+n_{1}})_{m_{1}h^{i}+m_{1}}\big)&=&P_{0,0}(m_{1}h^{i}+m_{1},n_{1}h^{i}+n_{1}),\\
\ \ell_{k}\big((I_{n_{1}h^{i}+n_{1}})_{m_{1}h^{i}+m_{1}+j}\big)&=&P_{j,0}(m_{1}h^{i}+m_{1}+j,n_{1}h^{i}+n_{1}),\\
\ \ell_{k}\big((I_{n_{1}h^{i}+n_{1}})_{m_{1}h^{i}+m_{1}+h}\big)&=&P_{0,0}(m_{1}h^{i}+m_{1}+h,n_{1}h^{i}+n_{1}).\end{array}

On the other hand, since
\mbox{depth}~A\geq 1
, the bigraded ring
R
has a nonzerodivisor of bidegree
(1,0)
. This gives the inequalities

\ell_{k}\big((I_{n_{1}h^{i}+n_{1}})_{m_{1}h^{i}+m_{1}}\big)\leq\ell_{k}\big((I_{n_{1}h^{i}+n_{1}})_{m_{1}h^{i}+m_{1}+j}\big)\leq\ell_{k}\big((I_{n_{1}h^{i}+n_{1}})_{m_{1}h^{i}+m_{1}+h}\big)

for all

. Therefore,

\displaystyle\lim_{i\to\infty}\dfrac{P_{0,0}(m_{1}h^{i}+m_{1},n_{1}h^{i}+n_{1})}{(n_{1}h^{i}+n_{1})^{\mathchoice{\widetilde{r}}{\widetilde{r}}{\lower 2.0pt\hbox{$\textstyle{\tilde{\raise 2.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle{r}$}}}$}}{\tilde{r}}}}

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{i\to\infty}\dfrac{P_{j,0}(m_{1}h^{i}+m_{1}+j,n_{1}h^{i}+n_{1})}{(n_{1}h^{i}+n_{1})^{\mathchoice{\widetilde{r}}{\widetilde{r}}{\lower 2.0pt\hbox{$\textstyle{\tilde{\raise 2.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle{r}$}}}$}}{\tilde{r}}}}

\displaystyle\leq\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\dfrac{P_{0,0}(m_{1}h^{i}+m_{1}+h,n_{1}h^{i}+n_{1})}{(n_{1}h^{i}+n_{1})^{\mathchoice{\widetilde{r}}{\widetilde{r}}{\lower 2.0pt\hbox{$\textstyle{\tilde{\raise 2.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle{r}$}}}$}}{\tilde{r}}}},

which implies

\sum_{l=0}^{{\mathchoice{\widetilde{r}}{\widetilde{r}}{\lower 2.0pt\hbox{$\textstyle{\tilde{\raise 2.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle{r}$}}}$}}{\tilde{r}}}}\lambda^{0}_{l}\left(\tfrac{m_{1}}{n_{1}}\right)^{l}\leq\sum_{l=0}^{{\mathchoice{\widetilde{r}}{\widetilde{r}}{\lower 2.0pt\hbox{$\textstyle{\tilde{\raise 2.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle{r}$}}}$}}{\tilde{r}}}}\lambda^{j}_{l}\left(\tfrac{m_{1}}{n_{1}}\right)^{l}\leq\sum_{l=0}^{{\mathchoice{\widetilde{r}}{\widetilde{r}}{\lower 2.0pt\hbox{$\textstyle{\tilde{\raise 2.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle{r}$}}}$}}{\tilde{r}}}}\lambda^{0}_{l}\left(\tfrac{m_{1}}{n_{1}}\right)^{l}.

This contradicts the choice of
(m_{1},n_{1})
and thus proves Claim
(i)
.

Proof of the Claim
(ii)
. Let
{\bar{r}}=\max\{r_{0,0},r_{0,j}\}
. Then we can express

\tfrac{P_{0,0}(X,Y)}{Y^{\bar{r}}}=\sum_{l=0}^{\bar{r}}\nu^{0}_{l}(\tfrac{X}{Y})^{l}+\sum_{i=1}^{\bar{r}}\tfrac{S^{0}_{{\bar{r}}-i}(\tfrac{X}{Y})}{Y^{i}},\quad\quad\frac{P_{0,j}(X,Y)}{Y^{\bar{r}}}=\sum_{l=0}^{\bar{r}}\nu^{j}_{l}(\tfrac{X}{Y})^{l}+\sum_{i=1}^{\bar{r}}\tfrac{S^{j}_{{\bar{r}}-i}(\tfrac{X}{Y})}{Y^{i}},

where
S^{0}_{i}(\tfrac{X}{Y})
and
S^{j}_{i}(\tfrac{X}{Y})
are polynomials in
{\mathbb{Q}}[\tfrac{X}{Y}]
of degree
\leq i
.
Note that if
r_{0,j}<{\bar{r}}
then all
\nu^{j}_{i}=0
. Same goes for
r_{0,0}
.

If the claim does not hold then
\sum_{l=0}^{\bar{r}}(\nu^{0}_{l}-\nu^{j}_{l})(\tfrac{X}{Y})^{l}
is a nonzero polynomial. Thus there is
0\leq i_{2}\leq{\bar{r}}
such that

\sum_{l=0}^{\bar{r}}(\nu^{0}_{l}-\nu^{j}_{l})\left(\tfrac{n_{2}}{m_{2}}\right)^{l}\neq 0\quad\mbox{for}\quad(m_{2},n_{2})=(\lfloor xn\rfloor+\alpha_{0}+i_{2}h,n+\beta_{0}).

Since
(m_{2},n_{2})
,
(m_{2},n_{2}+j)
and
(m_{2},n_{2}+h)\in\mathfrak{R}C_{j_{0}}
we have from Lemma
2.8
that

(3.9)

\{(m_{2}h^{i}+m_{2},n_{2}h^{i}+n_{2})\mid i\geq 1\}\cup\{(m_{2}h^{j}+m_{2},n_{2}h^{i}+n_{2}+j)\mid i\geq 1\}\\
\ \cup\{(m_{2}h^{i}+m_{2},n_{2}h^{i}+n_{2}+h)\mid i\geq 1\}\subseteq{\mathfrak{R}}C_{j_{0}}\cap{\mathbb{N}}^{2}.

By (
3.7
),
\ell_{k}\big((I_{n_{2}})_{m_{2}}\big)=P_{0,0}(m_{2},n_{2})
. Therefore

\ell_{k}\big((I_{n_{2}h^{i}+n_{2}+j})_{m_{2}h^{i}+m_{2}}\big)=P_{0,j}(m_{2}h^{i}+m_{2},n_{2}h^{i}+n_{2}+j).

As
I_{n_{2}h^{i}+n_{2}+h}\subset I_{n_{2}h^{i}+n_{2}+j}\subset I_{n_{2}h^{i}+n_{2}}
so we have

(I_{n_{2}h^{i}+n_{2}+h})_{m_{2}h^{i}+m_{2}}\subset(I_{n_{2}h^{i}+n_{2}+j})_{m_{2}h^{i}+m_{2}}\subset(I_{n_{2}h^{i}+n_{2}})_{m_{2}h^{i}+m_{2}},

which gives

\begin{array}[]{lcl}P_{0,0}(m_{2}h^{i}+m_{2},n_{2}h^{i}+n_{2}+h)&\leq&P_{0,j}(m_{2}h^{i}+m_{2},n_{2}h^{i}+n_{2}+j)\\
&\leq&P_{0,0}(m_{2}h^{i}+m_{2},n_{2}h^{i}+n_{2}).\end{array}

In particular,

\displaystyle\sum_{l=0}^{\bar{r}}\nu^{0}_{l}\left(\tfrac{n_{2}}{m_{2}}\right)^{l}

\displaystyle=\lim_{i\to\infty}\frac{P_{0,0}(m_{2}h^{i}+m_{2},n_{2}h^{i}+n_{2}+h)}{(m_{2}h^{i})^{\bar{r}}}

\displaystyle=\lim_{i\to\infty}\frac{P_{0,j}(m_{2}h^{i}+m_{2},n_{2}h^{i}+n_{2}+j)}{(m_{2}h^{i})^{\bar{r}}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=0}^{\bar{r}}\nu^{j}_{l}\left(\tfrac{n_{2}}{m_{2}}\right)^{l},

which contradicts the choice of
(m_{2},n_{2})
. This proves Claim
(ii)
and hence the assertion of Step
1
.

Step

2
. It now remains to show that the degree of the polynomial

is bounded above by

. Let

. Then

Lemma 4.3
.

(2)

If

,

by

Throughout this subsection we assume that the underlying field

Lemma 5.3

There exists an integer

and an exact of

-modules

(5.2)

Since

, any nonzero homogeneous element

of

Then we have the isomophism of varieties
\pi\mid_{X_{f}}\colon X_{f}=\pi^{-1}(V_{f})\longrightarrow V_{f}

If

where

Now we have an affine open set

of

Proof.

Assertion

(1)

Remark 5.6

5.2.
Density function in arbitrary characteristic

Here we construct a density function for
(A,\{\widetilde{I^{n}}\})
in arbitrary characteristic based on several results about the volume function of
{\mathbb{R}}
-divisors. For this we recall some relevant terminology and results from
[
Laz04a
]
and
[
LM09
]
.

5.2.1.
Preliminaries

Let
X
be a
(d-1)
-dimensional projective variety over an algebraically closed field
k
. Let
{\rm Div}(X)
denote the group of integral (Cartier) divisors on
X
.
Henceforth, an integral divisor will be referred as a divisor.

Let
N^{1}(X)={\rm Div}(X)/\equiv_{\mathrm{num}}
be the numerical equivalence classes of divisors on
X
, where two divisors
D_{1}
and
D_{2}
are
numerically equivalent
, written
D_{1}\equiv_{\mathrm{num}}D_{2}
, if
\left(D_{1}\cdot C\right)=\left(D_{2}\cdot C\right)
for every closed integral curve
C\subset X
. The first basic fact about
N^{1}(X)
is the
theorem of base
which states that
N^{1}(X)
, the so-called Néron-Severi group, is a free abelian group of finite rank.

A
{\mathbb{Q}}
-Cartier (resp.
{\mathbb{R}}
-Cartier) divisor is, by definition, an element of
\mbox{Div}(X)\otimes_{{\mathbb{Z}}}{\mathbb{Q}}
(resp.
\mbox{Div}(X)\otimes_{{\mathbb{Z}}}{\mathbb{R}}
). The resulting vector space of numerically equivalence classes is denoted by
N^{1}(X)_{{\mathbb{Q}}}
(resp.
N^{1}(X)_{{\mathbb{R}}}
) and we have the equalities
N^{1}(X)_{{\mathbb{Q}}}=N^{1}(X)\otimes_{{\mathbb{Z}}}{\mathbb{Q}}
and
N^{1}(X)_{{\mathbb{R}}}=N^{1}(X)\otimes_{{\mathbb{Z}}}{\mathbb{R}}
.

Definition 5.7
.

The
volume
of a divisor
D\in\mbox{Div}(X)
is the nonnegative real number given by

\mbox{vol}_{X}(D)=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\dfrac{h^{0}\left(X,{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(nD)\right)}{n^{d-1}/(d-1)!}.

For a fixed
a\in{\mathbb{N}}
we have
\mbox{vol}_{X}(aD)=a^{d-1}\mbox{vol}_{X}(D)
. When
D
is a
{\mathbb{Q}}
-divisor, the volume
\mbox{vol}_{X}(D)
of
D
can be defined by choosing some integer
a>0
such that
aD
is integral and then set

\mbox{vol}_{X}(D)=\frac{1}{a^{d-1}}\mbox{vol}_{X}(aD).

This definition is independent of the choice of
a
. Also numerically equivalent divisors have the same volume. Therefore we have a well-defined function
\mathrm{vol}_{X}\colon N^{1}(X)_{{\mathbb{Q}}}\longrightarrow{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}
. Now this and the following theorem about the continuity property of the volume function implies that the volume function extends uniquely to a continuous function

\mbox{vol}_{X}\colon N^{1}(X)_{{\mathbb{R}}}\longrightarrow{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}.

Theorem 5.8

(Continuity of volume)
.

[
Laz04a
, Theorem
2.2.44
]

Let
X
be as in Preliminaries
5.2.1
. Fix any norm
\|\;\|
on
N^{1}(X)_{{\mathbb{R}}}
inducing the usual topology on that finite dimensional vector space. Then there is a constant
C>0
such that

\left|\mathrm{vol}_{X}(\psi)-\mathrm{vol}_{X}(\psi^{\prime})\right|\leq C\cdot\left(\max\left(\|\psi\|,\|\psi^{\prime}\|\right)\right)^{d-2}\cdot\|\psi-\psi^{\prime}\|,

for any two classes of
\psi
,
\psi^{\prime}\in N^{1}(X)_{{\mathbb{Q}}}
.

Definition 5.9
.

We know that
N^{1}(X)_{{\mathbb{R}}}
can be considered as a finite dimensional
{\mathbb{R}}
-vector space with the Euclidean topology. By a
cone
in
N^{1}(X)_{{\mathbb{R}}}
we mean a set which is stable under multiplication by positive scalars.

An
{\mathbb{R}}
-divisor
D
is
big
if
\mbox{vol}_{X}(D)>0
. The
big cone

\mbox{Big}(X)\subseteq N^{1}(X)_{{\mathbb{R}}}
of
X
is the (open) convex cone of all big
{\mathbb{R}}
-divisor classes on
X
. Similarly, the
ample cone

\mbox{Amp}(X)\subseteq N^{1}(X)_{{\mathbb{R}}}
of
X
is the (open) convex cone of all ample
{\mathbb{R}}
-divisor classes on
X
. Also the
nef cone

\mbox{Nef}(X)\subseteq N^{1}(X)_{{\mathbb{R}}}
of
X
is the (closed) convex cone of all nef
{\mathbb{R}}
-divisor classes on
X
, where a divisor
D\in\mbox{Div}(X)
is
nef
(numerically effective) if
\left(D\cdot C\right)\geq 0
for all closed integral curves
C\subset X
. The pseudoeffective cone
\overline{\mbox{Eff}}(X)\subseteq N^{1}(X)_{{\mathbb{R}}}
is the closure of the convex cone spanned by the classes of effective
{\mathbb{R}}
-divisors on
X
. One has
N^{1}(X)_{{\mathbb{R}}}\supseteq\overline{\mbox{Eff}}(X)\supseteq\mbox{Nef}(X)
and a basic fact is

\mbox{interior}\big(\overline{\mbox{Eff}}(X)\big)=\mbox{Big}(X)\quad\mbox{and}\quad\mbox{interior}\big(\mbox{Nef}(X)\big)=\mbox{Amp}(X).

Theorem 5.10

(Asymptotic Riemann-Roch)
.

Let
X
be as in Preliminaries
5.2.1
and

be a nef divisor on

. Then

h^{0}(X,{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(nD))=\dfrac{(D^{d-1})}{(d-1)!}\cdot n^{d-1}+O(n^{d-2}).

In particular, if
D

\mbox{vol}_{X}(D)=(D)^{d-1}>0

The next theorem is a fundamental result which shows that the

Theorem 5.11

\theta\in\big(\widetilde{I^{tq_{t}N_{t}}}\big)_{tp_{t}N_{t}}
. Then

\lim_{n\to\infty}\dfrac{\ell_{k}\big((\widetilde{I^{tq_{t}N_{t}n}})_{(tp_{t}+q_{t})N_{t}n}\big)}{(tq_{t}N_{t}n)^{d-1}/d!}\geq\lim_{n\to\infty}\dfrac{\ell_{k}\Big(\Big({(\widetilde{I^{tq_{t}N_{t}}})}^{n}\Big)_{(tp_{t}+q_{t})N_{t}n}\Big)}{(tq_{t}N_{t}n)^{d-1}/d!}\geq\lim_{n\to\infty}\dfrac{\ell_{k}\left((\theta^{n})_{(tp_{t}+q_{t})N_{t}n}\right)}{(tq_{t}N_{t}n)^{d-1}/d!}>0.

Therefore,
\mathrm{vol}_{X}(y_{t}H-E)>0
and from the continuity of the volume function we get
\alpha_{I}\leq\gamma_{I}
.
∎

Remark 5.15
.

In contrast with Lemma
5.14

(1)
we have
\beta_{I}=\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\frac{d(\widetilde{I^{n}})}{n}
(see
[
CEL01
, Theorem
3.2
]
),
where
d(\widetilde{I^{n}})
denotes the maximum among the minimal generating degrees of
\widetilde{I^{n}}
.

Theorem 5.16
.

Let the hypotheses be as in Notations
5.1
,
5.2
and
5.13
. The following are true.

(1)

The sequence
\{g_{n}\}_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
converges uniformly to the density function
f_{A,\{\widetilde{I^{n}}\}}
on any given compact interval in
{\mathbb{R}}
. Moreover, the function
f_{A,\{\widetilde{I^{n}}\}}\colon{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}\longrightarrow{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}
given by

f_{A,\{\widetilde{I^{n}}\}}(x)=\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\frac{\ell_{k}\big((\widetilde{I^{n}})_{\lfloor xn\rfloor}\big)}{n^{d-1}/d!}

is continuous on the interval
I[0,\infty)
.

(2)

The function
f_{A,\{\widetilde{I^{n}}\}}
is continuously differentiable on the interval
I(\alpha_{I},\infty)
and also supported on
I(\alpha_{I},\infty)
.

(3)

Further, for all
x\in I[\beta_{I},\infty)
,

f_{A,\{\widetilde{I^{n}}\}}(x)=\sum_{i=0}^{d-1}(-1)^{i}\frac{d!}{(d-1-i)!i!}(H^{d-1-i}\cdot E^{i})x^{d-1-i}.

Proof.

Assertion
(1)
. Let
n_{0}
be an integer as in (
5.1
). Then for
x\in{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}
, we have

g_{n}(x)=\frac{\ell_{k}\big((\widetilde{I^{n}})_{\lfloor xn\rfloor}\big)}{n^{d-1}/d!}=\frac{h^{0}(X,{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(\lfloor xn\rfloor H-nE))}{n^{d-1}/d!}\quad\mbox{if}\quad n\geq n_{0}.

Therefore by Lemma
5.12
, the function
f_{A,\{\widetilde{I^{n}}\}}
given by

f_{A,\{\widetilde{I^{n}}\}}(x)=\lim_{n\to\infty}g_{n}(x)=d\cdot\mbox{vol}_{X}(xH-E)

is well-defined. Now the uniform convergence of
\{g_{n}\}_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
on any compact interval and the continuity property of the density function
f_{A,\{\widetilde{I^{n}}\}}
follows from the continuity property of the volume function (see Theorem
5.8
).

Assertion
(2)
. By
[
LM09
, Remark
4.29
]
, the volume function on
\mathrm{Big}(X)
and therefore its restriction to the closed subset

\{xH-E\mid x>\alpha_{I}\}
is
{\mathcal{C}}^{1}
-differentiable.

Assertion
(3)
. Note that
xH-E
is nef for all
x\geq\beta_{I}
. By the asymptotic Riemann-Roch (see Theorem
5.10
), for all
x\geq\beta_{I}
,

f_{A,\{\widetilde{I^{n}}\}}(x)=d\cdot\mbox{vol}_{X}(xH-E)=d(xH-E)^{d-1}=\sum_{i=0}^{d-1}(-1)^{i}\frac{d!}{(d-1-i)!i!}(H^{d-1-i}\cdot E^{i})x^{d-1-i}.

∎

Remark 5.17
.

Let the hypotheses be as above.

(1)

The density function
f_{A,\{\widetilde{I^{n}}\}}
is strictly increasing on the interval
I(\alpha_{I},\infty)
such that for all
y>x>\alpha_{I}
,

{f_{A,\{\widetilde{I^{n}}\}}(y)}^{\frac{1}{d-1}}\geq{f_{A,\{\widetilde{I^{n}}\}}(x)}^{\frac{1}{d-1}}+(y-x){e_{0}(A)}^{\frac{1}{d-1}},

where
e_{0}(A)
is the multiplicity of
A
with respect to its homogeneous maximal ideal
\mathbf{m}
.

To prove this we fix such an
x
and
y
. Then by the log-concavity relation for volume functions (see
[
Laz04a
, Remark
2.2.50
]
or
[
LM09
, Corollary
4.12
]
), we get

\displaystyle{f_{A,\{\widetilde{I^{n}}\}}(y)}^{\frac{1}{d-1}}={(d\cdot\mbox{vol}_{X}(yH-E))}^{\frac{1}{d-1}}

\displaystyle\geq{(d\cdot\mbox{vol}_{X}(xH-E))}^{\frac{1}{d-1}}+{(d\cdot\mbox{vol}_{X}((y-x)H))}^{\frac{1}{d-1}}

\displaystyle={f_{A,\{\widetilde{I^{n}}\}}(x)}^{\frac{1}{d-1}}+(y-x){(d\cdot\mbox{vol}_{X}(H))}^{\frac{1}{d-1}},

where it is known that
(H)^{d-1}=({\mathcal{O}}_{V}(1))^{d-1}=e_{0}(A)
.

(2)

Assume that the ideal
I
has a set of homogeneous generators of degrees
d_{1}<d_{2}<\ldots<d_{l}
. Then

(a)

\alpha_{I}\leq d_{1}
. This is obvious as
(I^{n})_{\lfloor xn\rfloor}\subset(\widetilde{I^{n}})_{\lfloor xn\rfloor}
, for all
x\in{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}
and
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
. But we can have
\alpha_{I}<d_{1}
(see the Example
6.6
, where
\alpha_{I}=4
and
d_{1}=5
).

(b)

\beta_{I}\leq d_{l}
.
To see the inequality
\beta_{I}\leq d_{l}
, we recall the arguement used in
[
CEL01
, Lemma
1.4
]
. For the associated ideal sheaf
{\mathcal{I}}
on
V=\mbox{Proj}~A
, we have a natural surjective map of
{\mathcal{O}}_{V}
-modules (obtained by mapping it to the generators of
I
)

{\mathcal{O}}_{V}(-d_{1})^{\oplus a_{1}}\oplus\cdots\oplus{\mathcal{O}}_{V}(-d_{l})^{\oplus a_{l}}\longrightarrow{\mathcal{I}}.

Hence
{\mathcal{I}}(d_{l})
is generated by global sections. So for
\pi:X=\mathbf{Proj}\left(\oplus_{n\geq 0}\mathcal{I}^{n}\right)\longrightarrow V
,

\pi^{*}({\mathcal{I}}(d_{l}))=\pi^{*}({\mathcal{I}}\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{V}(d_{l}))={\mathcal{I}}{\mathcal{O}}_{X}\otimes\pi^{*}({\mathcal{O}}(d_{l}))={\mathcal{O}}_{X}(-E+d_{l}H)

is globally generated and therefore nef and hence
\beta_{I}\leq d_{l}
.

Now we are ready to give a well-defined notion of
\varepsilon
-density function.

Definition 5.18
.

Let
A=\mbox{$\oplus$}_{n\geq 0}A_{n}
be a standard graded domain over an algebraically closed field
A_{0}=k
of dimension
d\geq 2
. Let
I\subset A
be a homogeneous ideal, which is generated in degrees
\{d_{1}<d_{2}<\cdots<d_{l}\}
. Then the
\varepsilon
-density function of
A
with respect to
I
is the function
f_{\varepsilon(I)}\colon{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}\longrightarrow{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}
given by

x\longmapsto\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\ell_{k}\big({(\widetilde{I^{n}}/I^{n})_{\lfloor xn\rfloor}\big)}}{n^{d-1}/d!}.

Theorem 5.19
.

Let
A
and
I
be as in Definition
5.18
. Also assume that
0<\mathrm{height}\,(I)<d
.

(1)

The function
f_{\varepsilon(I)}
is continuous everywhere possibly except at
x=d_{1}
. Further, it is continuously differentiable outside the finite set
\{\alpha_{I},d_{1},\ldots,d_{l}\}
.

(2)

The support of
f_{\varepsilon(I)}
is contained in the interval
I(\alpha_{I},d_{l}]
, and
\alpha_{I}>0
.

(3)

Further,

\int_{0}^{\infty}f_{\varepsilon(I)}(x)dx=\varepsilon(I).

Proof.

Assertion
(1)
. Consider the sequence

h_{n}\colon R_{\geq 0}\longrightarrow{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}\quad\mbox{given by}\quad x\mapsto\frac{\ell_{k}\big((\widetilde{I^{n}}/I^{n})_{\lfloor xn\rfloor}\big)}{n^{d-1}/d!}.

Then by Theorem
4.4
and Theorem
5.16
,

\lim_{n\to\infty}h_{n}(x)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\ell_{k}\big((\widetilde{I^{n}})_{\lfloor xn\rfloor}\big)}{n^{d-1}/d!}-\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\ell_{k}\big(({I^{n}})_{\lfloor xn\rfloor}}{n^{d-1}/d!}=f_{A,\{\widetilde{I^{n}}\}}(x)-f_{A,\{I^{n}\}}(x)

is a well-defined continuous function on
{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}\setminus\{d_{1}\}
and is continuously differentiable outside the finite set
\{\alpha_{I},d_{1},\ldots,d_{l}\}
.

Assertion
(2)
. By Remark
5.17
(2)(b) and Theorem
4.4
, there exist polynomials
{\mathchoice{\widetilde{\bf p}}{\widetilde{\bf p}}{\lower 2.0pt\hbox{$\textstyle{\tilde{\raise 2.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\bf p}$}}}$}}{\tilde{\bf p}}}(x)
and
{\bf p}_{l}(x)
in
{\mathbb{Q}}[x]

such that

f_{A,\{\widetilde{I^{n}}\}}(x)={\mathchoice{\widetilde{\bf p}}{\widetilde{\bf p}}{\lower 2.0pt\hbox{$\textstyle{\tilde{\raise 2.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\bf p}$}}}$}}{\tilde{\bf p}}(x)}\quad\text{and}\quad f_{A,\{I^{n}\}}(x)={\bf p}_{l}(x)\quad\forall x\in I(d_{l},\infty).

Therefore
{\mathchoice{\widetilde{\bf p}}{\widetilde{\bf p}}{\lower 2.0pt\hbox{$\textstyle{\tilde{\raise 2.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\bf p}$}}}$}}{\tilde{\bf p}}}(x)-{\bf p}_{l}(x)\geq 0
for all
x\in I(d_{l},\infty)
. Now as argued in the proof of Theorem
5.5
(replacing
q
by
n
everywhere) there is a constant
C_{0}
such that for every
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
,

\int_{0}^{m}f_{\varepsilon(I)}(x)dx=\int_{0}^{m}(f_{A,\{\widetilde{I^{n}}\}}(x)-f_{A,\{I^{n}\}}(x))dx\leq C_{0}

which implies that

\int_{d_{l}}^{m}({\mathchoice{\widetilde{\bf p}}{\widetilde{\bf p}}{\lower 2.0pt\hbox{$\textstyle{\tilde{\raise 2.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\bf p}$}}}$}}{\tilde{\bf p}}}(x)-{\bf p}_{l}(x))dx\leq C_{0}
, for all integers
m\geq d_{l}
. This forces

{\mathchoice{\widetilde{\bf p}}{\widetilde{\bf p}}{\lower 2.0pt\hbox{$\textstyle{\tilde{\raise 2.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\bf p}$}}}$}}{\tilde{\bf p}}}(x)={\bf p}_{l}(x)
and therefore
f_{\varepsilon(I)}(x)=0
for all
x\in I(d_{l},\infty)
.

Now we need to prove that
\alpha_{I}>0
. Recall Hartshorne’s definition
[
Har77
, Chapter II, Exercise 6.2]
of the degree of a divisor. We know that
\deg H=e_{0}
, where
e_{0}=e(A,{\bf m})
is the multiplicity of
A
with respect to the homogeneous maximal ideal
{\bf m}
. Since
E
is an effective divisor, so
\deg E>0
. Therefore if we choose
\alpha=\frac{\deg E}{e_{0}}
then
H^{0}(X,{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(\lfloor xn\rfloor H-nE))=0
for all
n\in{\mathbb{N}}_{>0}
and
x\in I[0,\alpha)
. In particular,
\mbox{vol}_{X}(xH-E)=0
for all
x\in I[0,\alpha]
. Note that the volume function is monotonically increasing. Since
\overline{\mathrm{Big}(X)}=\overline{\mathrm{Eff}}(X)
, hence the
{\mathbb{R}}
-divisor divisor
xH-E
is big for all
x\in I(\alpha_{I},\infty)
and has positive volume. Thus,
\alpha_{I}\geq\alpha=\frac{\deg E}{e_{0}}>0
.

Assertion
(3)
From the Lebesgue’s dominated convergence theorem, one gets that

\int_{0}^{\infty}f_{\varepsilon(I)}(x)dx=\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{0}^{d_{l}}h_{n}(x)dx=\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{m=0}^{d_{l}n}\frac{\ell_{k}\big((\widetilde{I^{n}}/I^{n})_{m}\big)}{n^{d}/d!}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\ell_{A}({\mathchoice{\widetilde{I}}{\widetilde{I}}{\lower 2.0pt\hbox{$\textstyle{\tilde{\raise 2.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle{I}$}}}$}}{\tilde{I}}^{n}}/{I^{n}})}{n^{d}/d!}=\varepsilon(I).

∎

Remark 5.20
.

Let
A
and
I
be as in Definition
5.18
. Thus, by the continuity of volume function and the existence of density function
f_{\varepsilon(I)}
, we have shown that

\varepsilon(I)=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{\ell_{A}\left(H^{0}_{\bf m}(A/{I^{n}})\right)}{n^{d}/d!}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\ell_{A}\left(H^{0}_{\bf m}(A/{I^{n}})\right)}{n^{d}/d!}.

Remark 5.21
.

From the proof of Theorem
5.19

(2)
, one has
f_{A,\{\widetilde{I^{n}}\}}(x)=f_{A,\{I^{n}\}}(x)
for all
x\in I(d_{l},\infty)
, where it is same as the polynomial given in Theorem
5.16

(3)
. Hence the assertion follows by Theorem
3.10
.

Remark 5.22
.

Equation (
5.1
) continues to hold over an arbitrary field. The saturation density function

was then studied by interpreting it as a volume function. This was done following
[

]
and
[

5.3.
Mixed multiplicities

We first recall the classical notion of mixed multiplicities of a pair of ideals. Let
(A,{\bf m})
be a Noetherian local ring of dimension
d>0
. Let
I
be an
{\bf m}
-primary ideal and
J
be an arbitrary ideal with positive height. Bhattacharya
[
Bha57
]
showed that for all
u\gg 0
and
v\gg 0
, the bivariate Hilbert function
\ell_{A}\left(I^{u}J^{v}/I^{u+1}J^{v}\right)
agrees with a bivariate numerical polynomial
Q(u,v)
of total degree
d-1
. Moreover, we can write

Q(u,v)=\sum_{i=0}^{d-1}\dfrac{e_{i}\left(I|J\right)}{i!(d-1-i)!}u^{d-1-i}v^{i}+\text{lower degree terms},

where the coefficients
e_{i}\left(I|J\right)
are integers. Risler and Tessier
[
Tei73
]
studied these coefficients when
J
is
{\bf m}
-primary and called them the
mixed multiplicities of
I
and
J
. Later in
[
KV89
]
, Katz and Verma analyzed the coefficients when
J
is an arbitrary ideal.

Now let
A
and
I
be as in Definition
5.18
. It follows from Corollary
3.4
or
[
HT03
, Theorem 4.2]
that there exist constants
m_{0},n_{0}>0
such that for all
m\geq d_{l}m+m_{0}
and
n\geq n_{0}
, the Hilbert function
\ell_{k}\left({(I^{n})}_{m}\right)
is given by a bivariate numerical polynomial
P(m,n)
of total degree
d-1
. Further, we can write

P(m,n)=\sum_{i=0}^{d-1}\dfrac{e_{i}\left(A[It]\right)}{i!(d-1-i)!}m^{i}n^{d-1-i}+\text{lower degree terms},

where the coefficients
e_{i}\left(A[It]\right)
are all integers.

The connection between intersection numbers and mixed multiplicities of
\mathbf{m}
-primary ideals in a local ring is well-known, for example see
[
Laz04a
, Chapter 1.6]
. The following result can be seen as a generalization in the graded framework.

Proposition 5.23
.

Let
A
and
I
be as in Definition
5.18
. Let
\bf m
denote the unique homogeneous maximal ideal of
A
. Then the following statements are true.

(1)

For all
i=0,\ldots,d-1
, we have
e_{i}\left(A[It]\right)=(-1)^{d-1-i}\left(H^{i}\cdot E^{d-1-i}\right)
.

(2)

Suppose that
I
is generated in equal degrees
d_{l}
. Then for all
i=0,\ldots,d-1
, we have

e_{i}\left({\bf m}|I\right)=\sum_{j=0}^{i}\binom{i}{j}d_{l}^{j}e_{d-1-i+j}\left(A[It]\right).

In particular,
e_{i}\left({\bf m}|I\right)=\left((d_{l}H-E)^{i}\cdot H^{d-1-i}\right)
for all such
i
.

Proof.

Part
(1)
of this proposition follows from part
(3)
of Theorem
5.16
and Remark
5.21
. Henceforth, let
I
be generated in equal degrees
d_{l}
. Then the required expression for
e_{i}\left({\bf m}|I\right)
is given in
[
DDRV24
, Lemma 5.3]
. Using part
(1)
, we have
e_{d-1-i+j}\left(A[It]\right)=(-1)^{i-j}\left(H^{d-1-i+j}\cdot E^{i-j}\right)
. Now substituting this into the expression for
e_{i}\left({\bf m}|I\right)
, we get

e_{i}\left({\bf m}|I\right)=\sum_{j=0}^{i}(-1)^{i-j}\binom{i}{j}d_{l}^{j}\left(H^{d-1-i+j}\cdot E^{i-j}\right)=\left((d_{l}H-E)^{i}\cdot H^{d-1-i}\right),

where the last equality is a consequence of the binomial exapansion.
∎

6.
Some properties of density functions

6.1.
Density function
f_{A,\{I^{n}\}}
and diagonal subalgebras of
A[It]

We recall that the homogenity property of the volume function on the integral divisors
\mathrm{Div}(X)
allowed the function to extend to
{\mathbb{Q}}
-Cartier divisors of
X
. Further the continuity property of the volume function on
{\mathbb{Q}}
-divisors allowed it to extend to
{\mathbb{R}}
-Cartier divisors.

Here the theory of
f_{A,\{I^{n}\}}
asserts a similar phenomenon for the ‘rescaled’ diagonal multiplicity function, denoted by
{\mathchoice{\widetilde{e}}{\widetilde{e}}{\lower 2.0pt\hbox{$\textstyle{\tilde{\raise 2.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle{e}$}}}$}}{\tilde{e}}_{\mathrm{rHSm}}}
.

For each
(p,q)\in{\mathbb{N}}^{2}
, we have a
(p,q)
-diagonal subalgebra
R_{\Delta_{I(p,q)}}
as in Definition
4.7
, where

A
is a domain in addition. If
p/q<d_{1}
then
R_{\Delta_{I(p,q)}}=k
. Let
p/q>d_{1}
then it is known that
R_{\Delta_{I(p,q)}}
is of dimension
d
. This can also be seen here easily because
f_{A,\{I^{n}\}}(p/q)\neq 0
, which implies that the multiplicity of the
(p,q)
-diagonal subalgebra
e(R_{\Delta_{I(p,q)}})=\tfrac{q^{d-1}}{d}\cdot f_{A,\{I^{n}\}}(p/q)\neq 0
.

Moreover, the homogenity property holds: If
a\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
(p,q)\in{\mathbb{N}}^{2}
then
e(R_{\Delta_{I(ap,aq)}})=a^{d-1}e(R_{\Delta_{I(p,q)}})
. This gives a well-defined function

{\mathchoice{\widetilde{e}}{\widetilde{e}}{\lower 2.0pt\hbox{$\textstyle{\tilde{\raise 2.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle{e}$}}}$}}{\tilde{e}}_{\mathrm{rHSm}}}:{\mathbb{Q}}_{\geq 0}\longrightarrow{\mathbb{Q}}_{\geq 0}\quad\mbox{where}\quad\tfrac{p}{q}\longmapsto\tfrac{e(R_{\Delta_{I(p,q)}})}{q^{d-1}}=\tfrac{1}{d}\cdot f_{A,\{I^{n}\}}(p/q).

Now the continuity property of the density function
f_{A,\{I^{n}\}}
on
{\mathbb{R}}\setminus\{d_{1}\}
implies that this function extends uniquely to
{\mathchoice{\widetilde{e}}{\widetilde{e}}{\lower 2.0pt\hbox{$\textstyle{\tilde{\raise 2.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle{e}$}}}$}}{\tilde{e}}_{\mathrm{rHSm}}}:{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}\longrightarrow{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}
. In particular, we have the following.

Corollary 6.1
.

Let
I
and
J
be homogeneous ideals in a standard graded domain
A
of dimension
d\geq 1
over a field
k
.
Then

f_{A,\{I^{n}\}}(x)=f_{A,\{J^{n}\}}(x)\;\;\mbox{for all}\;\;x\in{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}\;\;\mbox{ $\Longleftrightarrow$ }\;\;e(R_{\Delta_{I(p,q)}})=e(R_{\Delta_{J(p,q)}})\;\;\mbox{for all}\;\;(p,q)\in{\mathbb{N}}^{2}.

6.2.
Density functions and integral closure

Theorem 6.2
.

Let
I
be a homogeneous ideal in a standard graded algebra
A
over a field
k
. Let
J=\overline{I}
be the integral closure of the ideal
I
in
A
. Then

(1)

f_{A,\{I^{n}\}}(x)=f_{A,\{J^{n}\}}(x)
for all
x\in{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}\setminus\{d_{1},\ldots,d_{l}\}
, where
\{d_{1}<\cdots<d_{l}\}
denote the distinct degrees of a set of homogeneous generators of
I
.

(2)

If in addition
A
is an integral domain then

(a)

f_{A,\{I^{n}\}}(x)=f_{A,\{J^{n}\}}(x)
for all
x\in{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}
and

(b)

f_{A,\{\widetilde{I^{n}}\}}(x)=f_{A,\{\widetilde{J^{n}}\}}(x)
for all
x\in{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}
.

Proof.

Assertion
(1)
. First we note that since
J
has no term of degree
<d_{1}
, the function
f_{A,\{J^{n}\}}\equiv 0
on
I[0,d_{1})
. There exists integer an
c\geq 0
such that
J^{c}I^{n-c}=J^{n}
for all
n\geq c
. Now for
x\in I(d_{i},d_{i+1})
we can choose
n\gg 0
such that both
(\lfloor xn\rfloor,n)
and
(\lfloor xn\rfloor,n-c)
lie in
{\mathfrak{R}}C_{i}
.
Then by Theorem
3.10

\ell_{k}\big((I^{n})_{\lfloor xn\rfloor}\big)=P_{i}(\lfloor xn\rfloor,n)+Q_{i}(\lfloor xn\rfloor,n)\quad\mbox{and}\quad\ell_{k}\big((I^{n-c})_{\lfloor xn\rfloor}\big)=P_{i}(\lfloor xn\rfloor,n-c)+Q_{i}(\lfloor xn\rfloor,n-c),

where
\deg Q_{i}(X,Y)<\deg P_{i}(X,Y)
. Since
(I^{n})_{\lfloor xn\rfloor}\subset(J^{n})_{\lfloor xn\rfloor}\subset(I^{n-c})_{\lfloor xn\rfloor}
we get that

f_{A,\{I^{n}\}}(x)=f_{A,\{J^{n}\}}(x)
for
x\in I(d_{i},d_{i+1})
.

Assertion
(2)(a)
. Now if
A
is a domain then the functions
f_{A,\{I^{n}\}}
and
f_{A,\{J^{n}\}}
are continuous on
I(d_{1},\infty)
. Hence equality on the dense set
{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}\setminus\{d_{1},\ldots,d_{l}\}
implies equality on
I(d_{1},\infty)
. Further
J\subset{\bar{I}}
implies that the least degree of generator of
J
is
d_{1}
.
Hence the equality of the density functions holds for
x<d_{1}
too.

Now for
x=d_{1}
we note that both
(d_{1},1)
-diagonal subalgebra of

A[It]
and
A[Jt]
are standard graded as generated by
I_{d_{1}}
and
J_{d_{1}}
respectively.
Therefore

R_{\Delta_{I(d_{1},1)}}\longrightarrow R_{\Delta_{J(d_{1},1)}}
is a finite extension of integral domains. Hence

f_{R,\{I^{n}\}}(d_{1})=d\cdot e(R_{\Delta_{I(d_{1},1)}})=d\cdot e(R_{\Delta_{J(d_{1},1)}}=f_{R,\{J^{n}\}}(d_{1}).

Assertion
(2)(b)
. Note that we have
\widetilde{I^{n}}\subseteq\widetilde{J^{n}}\subseteq\widetilde{I^{n-c}}
for all
n\geq c
. This gives, for
x\in{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}

\lim_{n\to\infty}\dfrac{\ell_{k}\big((\widetilde{I^{n}})_{\lfloor xn\rfloor}\big)}{n^{d-1}/d!}\leq\lim_{n\to\infty}\dfrac{\ell_{k}\big((\widetilde{I^{n}})_{\lfloor xn\rfloor}\big)}{n^{d-1}/d!}\leq\lim_{n\to\infty}\dfrac{\ell_{k}\big((\widetilde{I^{n-c}})_{\lfloor xn\rfloor}\big)}{n^{d-1}/d!}.

Now

\lim_{n\to\infty}\dfrac{\ell_{k}\big((\widetilde{I^{n-c}})_{\lfloor xn\rfloor}\big)}{n^{d-1}/d!}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{h^{0}\left(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}\left(\lfloor xn\rfloor H-(n-c)E\right)\right)}{n^{d-1}/d!}=d\cdot\mbox{vol}_{X}(xH-E),

where the last equality follows by the arguments as in Lemma
5.12
. In particular,
f_{A,\{\widetilde{J^{n}}\}}(x)=d\cdot\mbox{vol}_{X}(xH-E)
and therefore
f_{A,\{\widetilde{I^{n}}\}}\equiv f_{A,\{\widetilde{J^{n}}\}}
.
∎

Lemma 6.3
.

Let
A=k[X,Y]
be a standard graded polynomial ring in two variables over a field
k
. Let
I_{1}\subseteq I_{2}
be two homogeneous ideals in
A
such that
f_{A,\{I^{n}_{1}\}}(x)=f_{A,\{I^{n}_{2}\}}(x)
for all real numbers
x\geq 0
. Then
I_{1}\subseteq I_{2}
is a reduction.

Proof.

Let
\mathbf{m}=(X,Y)
denote the homogeneous maximal ideal of
A
. Since
A
is a two-dimensional UFD, we may write

I_{j}=(g_{j})\cdot Q_{j}\quad\mbox{ for $j=1,2$,}

where
g_{j}
is a homogeneous element and
Q_{j}
is an
\mathbf{m}
-primary homogeneous ideal. Notice that
\widetilde{I_{j}^{n}}=(g_{j}^{n})
for all
n\geq 1
. So

\ell_{A}\left(H^{0}_{\mathfrak{m}}\left(A/I_{j}^{n}\right)\right)=\ell_{A}\big(\widetilde{I_{j}^{n}}/I_{j}^{n}\big)=\ell_{A}(A/Q_{j}^{n}),

as
g_{j}
is a nonzerodivisor on
A
. It thus follows that
\varepsilon(I_{j})=e(Q_{j})
. There exists an integer
c>\max\{\deg g_{1},\deg g_{2}\}
such that
(I_{j}^{n})_{m}={(\mathchoice{\widetilde{I}}{\widetilde{I}}{\lower 2.0pt\hbox{$\textstyle{\tilde{\raise 2.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle{I}$}}}$}}{\tilde{I}}_{j}^{n})}_{m}
for every
m\geq cn
. So for all real numbers
x\geq c
,

f_{A,\{I^{n}_{j}\}}(x)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\dfrac{\ell_{k}\big((I_{j}^{n})_{\lfloor xn\rfloor}\big)}{n/2}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\dfrac{\ell_{k}\big((\widetilde{I_{j}^{n}})_{\lfloor xn\rfloor}\big)}{n/2}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\dfrac{\ell_{k}\big((g_{j}^{n})_{\lfloor xn\rfloor}\big)}{n/2}=2(x-\deg g_{j}).

The hypothesis then implies that
\deg g_{1}=\deg g_{2}
. From the inclusion
(g_{1})=\widetilde{I_{1}}\subseteq\widetilde{I_{2}}=(g_{2})
, we conclude that
(g_{1})=(g_{2})
. Therefore,
Q_{1}\subseteq Q_{2}
and
f_{A,\{\widetilde{I^{n}_{1}}\}}(x)=f_{A,\{\widetilde{I^{n}_{2}}\}}(x)
. Hence
f_{\varepsilon(I_{1})}(x)=f_{\varepsilon(I_{2})}(x)
. Thus
e(Q_{1})=e(Q_{2})
. The statement now follows from Rees’s theorem.
∎

6.3.
When the filtration
\{\widetilde{I^{n}}\}_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
is Noetherian

Corollary 6.4
.

Let
A
be a standard graded domain over a field
k
and let
I\subseteq A
be a homogeneous ideal such that
\{\widetilde{I^{n}}\}_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
is a Noetherian filtration.
Then
f_{\varepsilon(I)}
is a piecewise polynomial function in the following sense: There exists a set of nonnegative rational numbers
a_{1}<\cdots<a_{r}
and polynomials
{\bf q}_{1}(x),\ldots,{\bf q}_{r}(x)
in
{\mathbb{Q}}[x]
of degrees at most
\dim A-1
such that

f_{\varepsilon(I)}(x)=\begin{cases}0&\text{if}\;\;x<a_{1}\;\text{or}\;x>a_{r}\\
{\bf{q}}_{i}(x)&\text{if}\;\;a_{i}<x<a_{i+1},\;\text{where}\;i=1,\ldots,r-1\end{cases}.

In particular,
\varepsilon(I)
is a rational number.

Proof.

Let
h_{1}<\cdots<h_{r_{1}}
be a distinct set of degrees of a homogeneous generating set of
I
. Hence the Rees algebra
A[It]
is generated in bidegrees
(h_{1},1),\ldots,(h_{r_{1}},1)
. Set
h_{r_{1}+1}=\infty
. Let
\mbox{$\oplus$}_{n\geq 0}\widetilde{I^{n}}t^{n}
be generated in bidegrees
\{(d_{1},e_{1}),\ldots,(d_{s},e_{s})\}
as a bigraded
k
-algebra. Without any loss of generality, we may assume that
d_{1}/e_{1}<\cdots<d_{r_{2}}/e_{r_{2}}
are the possible distinct rationals from the set
\{d_{i}/e_{i}\}_{i=1}^{s}
. Further, let
d_{r_{2}+1}/e_{r_{2}+1}=\infty
.

Then the function
f_{A,\{I^{n}\}}
agrees with a polynomial with rational coefficients on each open interval
(h_{i},h_{i+1})
. Similarly,
f_{A,\{\widetilde{I^{n}}\}}
is given by a polynomial with rational coefficients on every interval
(d_{j}/e_{j},d_{j+1}/e_{j+1})
.
If we present the set

\{h_{1},\ldots,h_{r_{1}},d_{1}/e_{1},\ldots,d_{r_{2}}/e_{r_{2}}\}=\{a_{1}<\cdots<a_{r}\}

then the corollary follows as
f_{\varepsilon(I)}=f_{A,\{\widetilde{I^{n}}\}}-f_{A,\{I^{n}\}}
.
∎

Remark 6.5
.

The rationality of
\varepsilon(I)
is known in a more general context: If
I
is an ideal in a local ring
(A,\mathbf{m})
such that
\{\widetilde{I^{n}}\}_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
is a Noetherian filtration, then by
[
HPV08
, Theorems 2.4 and 3.3]
the function
\ell_{A}(\widetilde{I^{n}}/I^{n})
is of quasi-polynomial type. Moreover, all the associated polynomials have the same degree and the same leading coefficient. Consequently,
\varepsilon(I)
is a rational number.

Example 6.6
.

Let
I
be a monomial ideal in a standard graded polynomial ring
A
over a field
k
. Then by
[
HHT07
, Theorem 3.2]
the filtration
\{\widetilde{I^{n}}\}_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
is Noetherian. In particular, the density function
f_{A,\{\widetilde{I^{n}}\}}
and therefore the
\varepsilon
-density function
f_{\varepsilon(I)}
is a piecewise polynomial function and
\varepsilon(I)
is a rational number. This fact also follows from
[
JM13
, Theorem 5.1]
.

Here we give a concrete example. Let
A=k[X,Y,Z]
and let

I=(X^{2}Y^{3},X^{3}Y^{2},XY^{2}Z^{4},XY^{3}Z^{3})=(X)\cap(Y^{2})\cap(X^{2},Z^{3})\cap(X^{3},Y^{3},Z^{4}).

Then by
[
HHT07
, Lemma
3.1
]

\widetilde{I^{n}}={\big(\widetilde{I}\big)}^{n}={(X^{2}Y^{2},XY^{2}Z^{3})}^{n}\quad\mbox{for all}\quad n\geq 1

and by
Macaulay2
the bigraded Hilbert series for
\mbox{$\oplus$}_{(m,n)\in{\mathbb{N}}^{2}}\big(\widetilde{I^{n}}\big)_{m}t^{n}
is

\sum_{(m,n)\in{\mathbb{N}}^{2}}\ell_{k}\big((\widetilde{I^{n}})_{m}\big)x^{m}y^{n}=\dfrac{1-x^{7}y}{(1-x)^{3}(1-x^{6}y)(1-x^{4}y)}.

Then

f_{A,\{\widetilde{I^{n}}\}}(x)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\dfrac{\ell_{k}\big((\widetilde{I^{n}})_{m}\big)}{n^{2}/3!}=\begin{cases}0&\mbox{if}\;\;x\leq 4\\
\frac{9}{2}(x-4)^{2}&\mbox{if}\;\;4\leq x\leq 6\\
3x^{2}-18x+18&\mbox{if}\;\;x\geq 6\end{cases}.

6.4.
When
\varepsilon(I)=0

Then
f_{A,\{\widetilde{I^{n}}\}}=f_{A,\{I^{n}\}}
. In particular,
\alpha_{I}=d_{1}
and the volume function
\mbox{vol}_{X}
is a piecewise polynomial function with rational coefficients when restricted to the ray
\{xH-E\mid x\in{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}\}
of
N^{1}(X)_{{\mathbb{R}}}
.

6.5.
When
I
is generated in equal degrees
d_{1}=\cdots=d_{l}

Then there are two possibilities:

(1)

\varepsilon(I)=0
, and in this case
d_{l}=\alpha_{I}
,

(2)

\varepsilon(I)\neq 0
, and in this case
d_{l}>\alpha_{I}
and
\varepsilon(I)\geq(d_{l}-\alpha_{I})^{d}\cdot e_{0}(A)
.

Note that
d_{1}=\cdots=d_{l}
implies that

f_{\varepsilon(I)}(x)=\begin{cases}f_{A,\{\widetilde{I^{n}}\}}(x)&\text{if}\;\;\alpha_{I}\leq x<d_{l}\\
0&\text{if}\;\;x\leq\alpha_{I}\;\;\text{or}\;\;x>d_{l}\end{cases}.

On the other hand, the log-concavity relation for volume functions implies

f_{A,\{\widetilde{I^{n}}\}}(x)\geq d\cdot(x-\alpha_{I})^{d-1}e_{0}(A)

for all real numbers
x>\alpha_{I}
. Hence,
\varepsilon(I)=0
if and only if
d_{l}=\alpha_{I}
. Further

\varepsilon(I)=\int_{0}^{\infty}f_{\varepsilon(I)}(x)dx=\int_{\alpha_{I}}^{d_{l}}f_{A,\{\widetilde{I^{n}}\}}(x)dx\geq de_{0}(A)\cdot\int_{\alpha_{I}}^{d_{l}}(x-\alpha_{I})^{d-1}dx=(d_{l}-\alpha_{I})^{d}\cdot e_{0}(A).

6.6.
When
\alpha_{I}=\beta_{I}

In this case
\varepsilon(I)
is an algebraic number. This is because

f_{A,\{\widetilde{I^{n}}\}}(x)=\begin{cases}0&\mbox{ if }x\leq\alpha_{I}\\
d\cdot(xH-E)^{d-1}&\mbox{ if }x\geq\alpha_{I}\end{cases}.

Since
f_{A,\{\widetilde{I^{n}}\}}
is a continuous function so
\alpha_{I}
must be a root of the polynomial
(xH-E)^{d-1}
and therefore is an algebraic number. Let
I
be generated in degrees
d_{1}\leq\cdots\leq d_{l}
. Then

\varepsilon(I)=\int_{0}^{d_{l}}\big(f_{A,\{\widetilde{I^{n}}\}}(x)-f_{A,\{I^{n}\}}(x)\big)dx=\int_{\alpha_{I}}^{d_{l}}f_{A,\{\widetilde{I^{n}}\}}(x)dx-\int_{d_{1}}^{d_{l}}f_{A,\{I^{n}\}}(x)dx,

where the first term is an algebraic number and the second term is a rational number. In particular, if
\alpha_{I}=\beta_{I}
and
\varepsilon(I)\neq 0
then
\beta_{I}<d_{l}
.

7.
Density functions in dimensions two and three

Proposition 7.1
.

Let
A
be a two dimensional standard graded domain over an algebraically closed field
k
. Let
I\subseteq A
be a nonzero homogeneous ideal. Then
\alpha_{I}=\beta_{I}
and
\varepsilon(I)
is a rational number.

Proof.

As
X=\mathbf{Proj}\left(\oplus_{n\geq 0}\mathcal{I}^{n}\right)
is a projective curve, it follows from
[
Laz04a
, Example 1.2.3]
that
\overline{\mathrm{Eff}}(X)=\overline{\mathrm{Amp}(X)}=\mathrm{Nef}(X)
. Consequently,
\alpha_{I}=\beta_{I}
. Now as seen above
\alpha_{I}
is a root of the polynomial
(\deg H)x-(\deg E)
and hence it is a rational number. Thus
\varepsilon(I)
is also rational.
∎

Proposition 7.2
.

Let
A
be a three dimensional standard graded domain over an algebraically closed field
k
. Let
I\subseteq A
be a nonzero homogeneous ideal. Then
f_{A,\{\widetilde{I^{n}}\}}
and
f_{\varepsilon(I)}
are given by piecewise (possibly countably many pieces) real polynomial functions of degrees at most two.

Proof.

If
I
is of finite colength then
\widetilde{I^{n}}=A
for all
n
, hence the assertion is obvious. So we assume that
I
is not of finite colength. In view of Theorems
4.4
and
5.19
, it suffices to show that
f_{A,\{\widetilde{I^{n}}\}}(x)
is given by a piecewise real polynomials having countably many pieces. From the proof of Theorem
5.16
, it follows that there exist Cartier divisors
H
and
E
on the projective surface
X=\mathbf{Proj}\left(\oplus_{n\geq 0}\mathcal{I}^{n}\right)
such that

3\cdot\mathrm{vol}_{X}(xH-E)=3\cdot\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\dfrac{h^{0}\left(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}\left(\lfloor xn\rfloor H-nE\right)\right)}{n^{2}/2}=\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\dfrac{\ell_{k}\big({(\widetilde{I^{n}})}_{\lfloor xn\rfloor}\big)}{n^{2}/3!}=:f_{A,\{\widetilde{I^{n}}\}}(x)

for all real numbers
x\geq 0
. Let
\varphi\colon X^{\prime}\to X
be a resolution of singularities and consider the pullbacks
H^{\prime}=\varphi^{*}H
and
E^{\prime}=\varphi^{*}E
to
X^{\prime}
.
Since the volume function is a birational invariant,

\mathrm{vol}_{X^{\prime}}(xH^{\prime}-E^{\prime})=\mathrm{vol}_{X}(xH-E)=\frac{1}{3}\cdot f_{A,\{\widetilde{I^{n}}\}}(x).

Note that

\alpha_{I}=\min\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\mid xH^{\prime}-E^{\prime}\in\overline{\mathrm{Eff}}(X^{\prime})\right\}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\beta_{I}=\min\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\mid xH^{\prime}-E^{\prime}\in\mathrm{Nef}(X^{\prime})\right\}.

Now if
\alpha_{I}=\beta_{I}
, then
f_{A,\{\widetilde{I^{n}}\}}(x)
is given by a single quadratic polynomial. So we may further assume that
\alpha_{I}<\beta_{I}
.

Since
X^{\prime}
is a nonsingular projective surface, by
[
BKS04
]
there is a locally ﬁnite decomposition of
\mathrm{Big}(X^{\prime})
into rational locally polyhedral (convex) subcones such that on each subcone, the support of the negative part of the Zariski decomposition of the divisors in the subcone is constant, and the volume function is given by a single polynomial of degree
2
. This means that given
x>\alpha_{I}
, there is an open interval
(x_{0},x_{1})
with
\alpha_{I}<x_{0}<x<x_{1}
, such that if

D^{\prime}_{x_{0}}:=x_{0}H^{\prime}-E^{\prime}=P_{D^{\prime}_{x_{0}}}+N_{D^{\prime}_{x_{0}}},\quad\text{and}\quad D^{\prime}_{x_{1}}:=x_{1}H^{\prime}-E^{\prime}=P_{D^{\prime}_{x_{1}}}+N_{D^{\prime}_{x_{1}}}

are their respective Zariski decompositions then for all
\lambda\in(0,1)
,

\left(\lambda P_{D^{\prime}_{x_{0}}}+(1-\lambda)P_{D^{\prime}_{x_{1}}}\right)+\left(\lambda N_{D^{\prime}_{x_{0}}}+(1-\lambda)N_{D^{\prime}_{x_{1}}}\right)

is the Zariski decomposition of
\lambda D^{\prime}_{x_{0}}+(1-\lambda)D^{\prime}_{x_{1}}=\left(\lambda x_{0}+(1-\lambda)x_{1}\right)H^{\prime}-E^{\prime}
. In other words,

\displaystyle\mathrm{vol}_{X^{\prime}}\left(\left(\lambda x_{0}+(1-\lambda)x_{1}\right)H^{\prime}-E^{\prime}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\lambda P_{D^{\prime}_{x_{0}}}+(1-\lambda)P_{D^{\prime}_{x_{1}}}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=\lambda^{2}\big(P_{D^{\prime}_{x_{0}}}^{2}\big)+2\lambda(1-\lambda)\big(P_{D^{\prime}_{x_{0}}}\cdot P_{D^{\prime}_{x_{1}}}\big)+(1-\lambda)^{2}\big(P_{D^{\prime}_{x_{1}}}^{2}\big)

for all
\lambda\in(0,1)
. Thus if
x=\lambda x_{0}+(1-\lambda)x_{1}
, where
\lambda\in(0,1)
, then

\displaystyle f_{A,\{\widetilde{I^{n}}\}}(x)

\displaystyle=3\cdot\mathrm{vol}_{X^{\prime}}(xH^{\prime}-E^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\dfrac{3}{(x_{1}-x_{0})^{2}}\left[(x_{1}-x)^{2}\big(P_{D^{\prime}_{x_{0}}}^{2}\big)+2(x_{1}-x)(x-x_{0})\big(P_{D^{\prime}_{x_{0}}}\cdot P_{D^{\prime}_{x_{1}}}\big)+(x-x_{0})^{2}\big(P_{D^{\prime}_{x_{1}}}^{2}\big)\right]

is a polynomial in
x
of degree at most
2
.
∎

Example 7.3

(Nagata)
.

Suppose that
A=\mathbb{C}[X,Y,Z]
is the homogeneous coordinate ring of
\mathbb{P}^{2}_{\mathbb{C}}
, where
\mathbb{C}
denotes the field of complex numbers. Let

be an integer and let

general points in

3\cdot(x^{2}-s^{2})&\text{if}\;\;x\geq s\end{cases}.

Let
\pi\colon X\to\mathbb{P}^{2}_{\mathbb{C}}

\displaystyle H^{0}\left(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}\left(mH-nE\right)\right)

\displaystyle=H^{0}\left(\mathbb{P}^{2}_{\mathbb{C}},\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{2}_{\mathbb{C}}}(mH^{\prime})\otimes\mathcal{I}_{p_{1}}^{n}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathcal{I}_{p_{s^{2}}}^{n}\right)=\bigcap\limits_{i=1}^{s^{2}}\left(I(p_{i})^{n}\right)_{m}=\big(\widetilde{I^{n}}\big)_{m}.

∎

On Preimages Question
Yohsuke Matsuzawa, Kaoru Sano

Abstract
. For a surjective self-morphism on a projective variety defined over a number field, we study the preimages question, which asks if the set of rational points on the iterated preimages of an invariant closed subscheme eventually stabilize. We prove the answer is positive for self-morphisms on ℙ 1 × ℙ 1 {\mathbb{P}}^{1}\times{\mathbb{P}}^{1} . We also prove that the answer is positive even if we allow finite extension of the number field by a bounded degree if the morphism is étale or the invariant subscheme is 0 0 -dimensional. We prove certain uniformity of the stabilization for self-morphisms on abelian varieties. When the subscheme is not invariant, we provide counterexamples and a result in a positive direction for products of polynomial maps on ℙ 1 × ℙ 1 {\mathbb{P}}^{1}\times{\mathbb{P}}^{1} .

1.
Introduction

Let
f\colon X\longrightarrow X
be a surjective morphism on a projective
variety
X
defined over a number field
K
.
For a closed subscheme
Y\subset X
, the iterated preimages
f^{-n}(Y)

tend to become complicated as
n
approaches
\infty
, and so
it is reasonable to expect that the
K
-rational points on
f^{-n}(Y)

might be sparse in a certain sense.
Such a kind of question is investigated in
[
2
]
,
motivated by a question proposed in
[
10
]
.
The central problem is the following (
[
10
, Question 8.4(1)]
,
[
2
, Question 1.1]
).

Question 1.1

(Preimages Question (PIQ))
.

Let
K
be a number field.
Let
X
be a projective variety over
K

(i.e. integral projective scheme over
K
) and

f\colon X\longrightarrow X
a surjective morphism over
K
.
Let
Y\subset X
be an
f
-invariant closed subscheme
(i.e.
f|_{Y}
factors through
Y
).
Then is there
s_{0}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}
such that for all
s\geq s_{0}
,
we have

(1.1)

\displaystyle\left(f^{-s-1}(Y)\smallsetminus f^{-s}(Y)\right)(K)=\emptyset\ ?

Let us give some comments on the assumptions.
We cannot remove the projectivity of
X
as there is a counterexample

[
2
, section 6.1]
.
The
f
-invariance of
Y
is more mysterious.
We can pose a similar question for
Y
that is not necessarily
f
-invariant (see
4.1
). While there are counterexamples (cf.
section

4
), it seems plausible to assume that certain additional conditions on
f
or
Y
might yield a positive answer. The
f
-invariance of
Y
can be seen as one such condition, but we anticipate that other types of conditions exist. Currently, we do not comprehend what causes the set of rational points on
f^{-n}(Y)
to be sparse.

Let us also give a comment on other ground fields.
Obviously, the question is nonsense if
K
is algebraically closed.
A slightly non-trivial fact is that it has a counterexample
over
p
-adic fields in general (see
[
2
, section 6.2]
).
However, for a specific type of morphisms, PIQ is true
over
p
-adic fields or finitely generated fields over
{\mathbb{Q}}

(cf.
[
2
, Proof of Theorem 3.1 Step 2]
,
theorem

9.1
).

Remark 1.2
.

The statement of
1.1
makes sense for arbitrary self-map
f\colon X\longrightarrow X

on a set
X
and its
f
-invariant subset
Y\subset X
.
We use the terminology Preimages Question or PIQ for such situations flexibly.
For example, PIQ is true for
(X,f,Y)
means there is
s_{0}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}
such that for all
s\geq s
, we have

(1.2)

\displaystyle f^{-s-1}(Y)\smallsetminus f^{-s}(Y)=\emptyset.

In this terminology,
1.1
is equivalent to PIQ for
f\colon X(K)\longrightarrow X(K)
and subset
Y(K)\subset X(K)
.

Remark 1.3
.

For a map
f\colon X\longrightarrow X
on a set
X
and
an
f
-invariant subset
Y\subset X
, the following are equivalent.

(1)

PIQ is true for
(X,f,Y)
.

(2)

PIQ is true for
(X,f^{n},Y)
for some
n\geq 1
.

(3)

There is
s_{0}\geq 0
with the following property: for every

, if

for some

, then
f^{s_{0}}(x)\in Y
.

Back to

, the original
[

Let

Let

.
Let

Theorem B

(

.

The answer to

, or

Note that

Theorem C

(
theorem

9.1
)
.

Let
K
be a finitely generated field over
{\mathbb{Q}}
.
Let
\varphi\colon{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{K}\times_{K}{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{K}\longrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{K}\times_{K}{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{K}

We have mentioned that the invariance of

Theorem D

(
theorem

4.5
)
.

Let
K

Let

\displaystyle\biggl((f\times g)^{-s-1}(\Delta)\smallsetminus\bigcup_{0\leq i\leq s}(f\times g)^{-i}(\Delta)\biggr)(K)=\emptyset.

We have seen that the answer to

It is then natural to ask how much extent the (minimum of the) number

in

When

Theorem E

(

)
.

Let

be an abelian variety over a number field

Then there exists

depending only on

with the

For any surjective morphism

and
any reduced closed subscheme

(1.6)

\displaystyle(f^{-s_{0}-1}(Y)\smallsetminus f^{-s_{0}}(Y))(K)=\emptyset.

Remark 1.5
.

In the course of the proof of

,
we prove a uniform bound of the periods of periodic abelian subvarieties
under isogenies

.
While our proof heavily relies on several special properties of abelian varieties,
it is worth noting the similarity of this and
the problem raised in
[

Remark 1.6
.

Under the assumption of general uniform boundedness conjecture on the torsion points

Refer to

4.
Preimages Question when the subvariety is not invariant

Let
f\colon X\longrightarrow X
be a surjective morphism on a projective variety.
Consider an arbitrary subvariety
Y\subset X
, not necessarily
f
-invariant.
One might expect that the preimages
f^{-s}(Y)
are typically geometrically
complicated and admit few rational points.
Stemming from this intuition, the following question
(PIQ without the constraint of
Y
being invariant) naturally arises.

Question 4.1
.

Let
K
be a number field.
Let
X
be a projective variety over
K
and

f\colon X\longrightarrow X
a surjective morphism over
K
.
Let
Y\subset X
be a closed subscheme.
Then is there
s_{0}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}
such that for all
s\geq s_{0}
,
we have

(4.1)

\displaystyle\biggl(f^{-s-1}(Y)\smallsetminus\bigcup_{0\leq i\leq s}f^{-i}(Y)\biggr)(K)=\emptyset\ ?

There are situations where the answer is negative by trivial reason, e.g.,
f
is an automorphism and
Y
is a single rational point with infinite
f
-orbit.
It was somewhat surprising that
the question has a negative answer even if
f
is polarized.
The following is a recipe for constructing counterexamples.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
K
be a number field and

X
a projective variety over
K
.
Let
f,g\colon X\longrightarrow X
be surjective morphisms over
K

and
Y\subset X
be a closed subvariety.
Suppose

(1)

f\circ g=g\circ f
;

(2)

f(g(Y))=Y
;

(3)

Y\cap g^{n}(Y)\subsetneq Y
for all
n\geq 1
;

(4)

Y(K)
is Zariski dense in
Y
.

Then the answer to
4.1
for
X,f,Y

is negative.

Proof.

For all integers
n\geq m\geq 0
, we have

(4.2)

\displaystyle f^{m}(g^{n}(Y))=g^{n-m}\left((f\circ g)^{m}(Y)\right)=g^{n-m}(Y).

Thus for all
s\geq 0
, we have

(4.3)

\displaystyle\biggl(f^{-s-1}(Y)\smallsetminus\bigcup_{0\leq i\leq s}f^{-i}(Y)\biggr)(K)

(4.4)

\displaystyle\supset\biggl(g^{s+1}(Y)\smallsetminus\bigcup_{0\leq i\leq s}f^{-i}(Y\cap g^{s+1-i}(Y))\biggr)(K).

By assumption,
Y\cap g^{s+1-i}(Y)
is a proper closed subset of
Y
.
Since
Y
is irreducible and
f^{i},g^{s+1}
are finite,
f^{-i}(Y\cap g^{s+1-i}(Y))
does not contain
g^{s+1}(Y)
.
Since
g^{s+1}(Y)(K)
is Zariski dense in
g^{s+1}(Y)
,
the right hand side of
eq.

4.3
is non-empty.
∎

In this section, we prove

At its core, the uniformity arises from the finiteness of torsion points

Let us start with a linear algebra lemma.

Lemma 6.1
.

Let

be a finite-dimensional
{\mathbb{Q}}
-vector space.
Then there exists

\dim V

with the following property.
For any
{\mathbb{Q}}
-linear automorphism

f

-subspace

\displaystyle f^{n_{0}}(W)=W.

Here a subspace

is said to be

-periodic if

for some

.

Proof.

Let

be an
f
-periodic
{\mathbb{Q}}
-subspace of

and let

.
If

, then

.
Suppose

Consider the
{\mathbb{Q}}
-linear automorphism

(6.2)

\displaystyle\scalebox{1}[1.5]{$\wedge$}^{d}\,f\colon\scalebox{1}[1.5]{$\wedge$}^{d}\,V\longrightarrow\scalebox{1}[1.5]{$\wedge$}^{d}\,V.

Then, it is easy to see that

(6.3)

\displaystyle f^{n}(W)=W\iff(\scalebox{1}[1.5]{$\wedge$}^{d}\,f)^{n}(\scalebox{1}[1.5]{$\wedge$}^{d}\,W)=\scalebox{1}[1.5]{$\wedge$}^{d}\,W

for all
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 1}
.
Therefore, it is enough to show the statement for one-dimensional subspaces

.

Now, suppose
W\subset V
is an
f
-periodic one-dimensional subspace.
We will show that there is
n_{0}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 1}
depending only on
\dim V

such that
f^{n_{0}}(W)=W
.
Take
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 1}
such that
f^{n}(W)=W
.
Let

and set

(6.4)

\displaystyle U=\langle w,f(w),\dots,f^{n-1}(w)\rangle_{{\mathbb{Q}}},

the subspace of

generated by

.
We have

and there is

such that

is multiplication by

.
In particular,

is diagonalizable over

.
Replacing

with

if necessary, we may assume

Take

such that

.
Let

be all the roots of the characteristic polynomial
of

(possibly repeated).
Note

.
Then we have

, and hence we can write

(6.5)

\displaystyle\lambda_{i}=\zeta_{i}b

Then

(6.6)

\displaystyle{\mathbb{Q}}\ni\lambda_{1}\cdots\lambda_{r}=\zeta_{1}\cdots\zeta_{r}b^{r}

Thus

(6.7)

\displaystyle[{\mathbb{Q}}(\zeta_{i}):{\mathbb{Q}}]=[{\mathbb{Q}}(b^{-1}\lambda_{i}):{\mathbb{Q}}]\leq r^{2}\leq(\dim V)^{2}.

Therefore, there is

(6.8)

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}^{n_{0}}=\cdots=\lambda_{r}^{n_{0}}\in{\mathbb{Q}}.

Remembering

-vector space.

\varphi
and

Then

induces an
{\mathbb{Q}}
-linear automorphism

We summarize some facts on minimal polynomials of endomorphisms on abelian varieties

\varphi
.
Since
\pi(Y_{1})(K)
is Zariski dense in
\pi(Y_{1})
,
\pi(Y_{1})

is geometrically irreducible (cf.
[
14
, Proposition 2.3.26]
).

We also have
\Stab(\pi(Y_{1})_{\overline{K}})^{\circ}=\Stab(\pi(Y_{1}))^{\circ}_{\overline{K}}=0
.
Thus by
[

, (3.7) Theorem]
,
\pi(Y_{1})_{\overline{K}}

is a variety of general type (possibly a single point).
As
\pi(Y_{1})
is
\overline{f}
-periodic, there is
m\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 1}

such that
\overline{f}^{m}|_{\pi(Y_{1})}=\id_{\pi(Y_{1})}
.
Applying this to

, we get

(6.23)

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}\overline{\varphi}^{i}(\pi(a))=0.

In particular, we get
\overline{\varphi}^{m}|_{\pi(Y_{1})}=\id_{\pi(Y_{1})}
.
Let
H\subset X/A
be the abelian subvariety generated by
the
\overline{\varphi}
-orbit of
\pi(Y_{1})
.
Then
H
is
\overline{\varphi}
-invariant and

\overline{\varphi}|_{H}^{m}=\id_{H}
.
Thus
P_{\overline{\varphi}|_{H}}(t)\mid t^{m}-1
and we also have

P_{\overline{\varphi}|_{H}}\mid P_{\overline{\varphi}}\mid P_{\varphi}
.
Since the cyclotomic factor of
P_{\varphi}
is
(t-1)^{d}
, we get

P_{\overline{\varphi}|_{H}}(t)=t-1
, in other words,

\overline{\varphi}|_{H}=\id_{H}
.

Now let
B=(X/A)/(\overline{\varphi}-\id)(X/A)
.

Case 1
. Suppose
\dim B\geq 1
.
Let
\nu\colon X/A\longrightarrow B
be the quotient morphism.
The morphism
\overline{\varphi}
descends to
\id
on
B
and thus
by
eq.

6.23
,
\nu(\pi(a))\in B(K)_{\rm tors}
.
Note that we have
B=X/C
where
C=A+(\varphi-\id)(X)

is an abelian subvariety.
By
[
9
]
, the isomorphic classes of the quotient of
X
by an abelian subvariety are finite.
Thus there is
m_{0}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 1}
depending only on
X
such that

m_{0}\nu(\pi(a))=0
.
Thus, we have the commutative diagram

(6.24)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces B}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f^{m_{0}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mu}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\nu\circ\pi=:\mu}

where
\mu

by

.
Now let

for some

.
Then
(f^{m_{0}})^{s}(x)\in Y
for some

Note that
f^{m_{0}}
induces

(6.25)

\displaystyle f^{m_{0}}\colon\mu^{-1}(\mu(x))=x+C\longrightarrow x+C.

Note also that

As
\dim B\geq 1

\dim C<\dim X

.
Since

[

we can take
s_{0}
depending only on

.

Case 2
.
Suppose

.
Then
\overline{\varphi}-\id
is an isogeny and hence
H=0
.
In particular, we have
\pi(Y_{1})=0
.
Moreover,
P_{\overline{\varphi}}
has no cyclotomic factor because
if it has, it must be the power of
t-1
.

By
eq.

6.23
,
\overline{f}^{m}=\overline{\varphi}^{m}
.
Noting
\pi(Y_{2})=\overline{f}(\pi(Y_{1}))=\pi(a)
, we have

\overline{\varphi}^{m}(\pi(a))=\pi(a)
.
Hence
\pi(a)\in(X/A)(K)_{\rm tors}
.

Now let

be such that

for some

.
Then
f^{s}(x)\in Y_{1}
for some

Then
\overline{f}^{s}(\pi(x))=0
and this implies

\displaystyle\overline{\varphi}^{s}(\pi(x))=-\sum_{i=0}^{s-1}\overline{\varphi}^{i}(\pi(a)).

The right-hand side is a torsion point, and thus

.
Since

we have

Again by

Then we have

∎

As for the first one, there is a following strong uniformity conjecture.

If we assume this conjecture, we have the following.

.

.

.

Let

Indeed,

(6.29)

\displaystyle Y+\varphi(A)=(a+\varphi(Y))+\varphi(A)=a+\varphi(Y+A)=a+\varphi(Y)=Y

Consider the following commutative diagram:

.

.

7.
Preliminaries on polydisc algebras

We prepare some preliminary results on polydisc algebras,
especially on prime factorization in the polydisc algebras.
The contents of this section might be well-known to experts, but we include them here for completeness.
For basic materials on Tate algebras or strictly affinoid algebras,
see for example
[
3
,
11
]
.

Let
(K,|\ |)
be a complete Non-archimedean valued field.
For
r=(r_{1},\dots,r_{n})\in{\mathbb{R}}_{>0}^{n}
, the polydisc algebra with radii
r
is

(7.1)

\displaystyle K\{r^{-1}T\}

\displaystyle=K\{r_{1}^{-1}T_{1},\dots,r_{n}^{-1}T_{n}\}

(7.2)

\displaystyle=\biggl\{\sum_{I\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}^{n}}a_{I}T^{I}\in K\llbracket T\rrbracket\mathrel{\bigg|}\lim_{I\to\infty}|a_{I}|r^{I}=0\biggr\}.

Here we use the standard multi index notation: for
I=(i_{1},\dots,i_{n})
,

T^{I}:=T_{1}^{i_{1}}\cdots T_{n}^{i_{n}}
and
r^{I}=r_{1}^{i_{1}}\cdots r_{n}^{i_{n}}
.
The Gauss norm on
K\{r^{-1}T\}
is

(7.3)

\displaystyle\biggl\|\sum_{I\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}^{n}}a_{I}T^{I}\biggr\|_{K\{r^{-1}T\}}=\max\left\{|a_{I}|r^{I}\ \middle|\ I\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}^{n}\right\}.

We drop the subscript and simply write
\|\ \|
when there should be no confusion.
For
I=(i_{1},\dots,i_{n})\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}^{n}
, we set
|I|=i_{1}+\cdots+i_{n}
.

Definition 7.1
.

For
f\in K\{r^{-1}T\}\smallsetminus\{0\}
, we define

(7.4)

\displaystyle\ord(f)=\ord_{K\{r^{-1}T\}}(f):=\max\left\{|I|\ \middle|\ |a_{I}|r^{I}=\|f\|\right\}.

When
f=0
, we set
\ord_{K\{r^{-1}T\}}(0)=\infty
.

Lemma 7.2
.

Let
K
be a complete Non-archimedean valued field and
r=(r_{1},\dots,r_{n})\in{\mathbb{R}}_{>0}^{n}
.

(1)

For
f,g\in K\{r^{-1}T\}
, we have
\ord(fg)=\ord(f)+\ord(g)
.

(2)

For
f\in K\{r^{-1}T\}
,
f
is unit if and only if
\ord(f)=0
.

(3)

For any finite extension
L
of
K
equipped with the uniquely extended absolute value
and
f\in K\{r^{-1}T\}
, we have

(7.5)

\displaystyle\ord_{L\{r^{-1}T\}}(f)=\ord_{K\{r^{-1}T\}}(f).

(4)

For
f\in K\{r^{-1}T\}\smallsetminus\{0\}
, the following set is finite:

(7.6)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{I}}(f):=\left\{I\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}^{n}\ \middle|\ \exists s\in\prod_{i=1}^{n}(0,r_{i}],|a_{I}|s^{I}=\|f\|_{K\{s^{-1}T\}}\right\}.

In particular, the set of orders

(7.7)

\displaystyle\left\{\ord_{K\{s^{-1}T\}}(f)\ \middle|\ s\in\prod_{i=1}^{n}(0,r_{i}]\right\}

is finite.

Proof.

(1)
Let us write
f=\sum_{k\geq 0}f_{k}
,
g=\sum_{l\geq 0}g_{l}

where
f_{k},g_{l}
are homogeneous part of degree
k,l
respectively.
Let
d=\ord(f),e=\ord(g)
.
Then, by the standard argument, we have

(7.8)

\displaystyle\left\|\sum_{i=0}^{d+e}f_{i}g_{d+e-i}\right\|

\displaystyle=\left\|f_{d}g_{e}\right\|=\left\|fg\right\|,

(7.9)

\displaystyle\left\|\sum_{i=0}^{m}f_{i}g_{m-i}\right\|

\displaystyle<\left\|f_{d}g_{e}\right\|\quad(\text{for }m>d+e)

and thus
\ord(fg)=d+e
.

(2)
If
f
is a unit, then
0=\ord(1)=\ord(ff^{-1})=\ord(f)+\ord(f^{-1})
.
This implies
\ord(f)=0
.
Suppose
\ord(f)=0
.
Then
f(0)\neq 0
and we can write

(7.10)

\displaystyle f=f(0)\left(1+g\right)

with
g
such that
\|g\|<1
.
Thus,
f
is a unit.

(3) This is obvious from the definition.

(4)
For
I=(i_{1},\dots,i_{n})\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}^{n}
, let

(7.11)

\displaystyle C(I)=\{(j_{1},\dots,j_{n})\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}^{n}\mid\text{$j_{k}\geq i_{k}$ for $k=1,\dots,n$}\}.

Note that for any subset
{\mathcal{I}}\subset{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}^{n}
, there are finitely many elements
I_{1},\dots,I_{t}
such that

(7.12)

\displaystyle\bigcup_{I\in{\mathcal{I}}}C(I)=\bigcup_{k=1}^{t}C(I_{k}).

To see this, note that
C(I)
is exactly the set of exponents of monomials
contained in the ideal generated by
x_{1}^{i_{1}}\cdots x_{n}^{i_{n}}

in the polynomial ring
{\mathbb{Q}}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
.
The left-hand side is the set of exponents of monomials contained in
the ideal generated by
(x^{I})_{I\in{\mathcal{I}}}
, but the Hilbert basis theorem says
this ideal is generated by finitely many
x^{I}
’s.

Now, we go back to our situation.
We prove (4) by induction on
n
.
The statement is clear when
n=1
.
Suppose
n\geq 2
and
\#{\mathcal{I}}(f)=\infty
.
By the above discussion, there are
I_{1},\dots,I_{t}\in{\mathcal{I}}(f)
such that

(7.13)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{I}}(f)\subset\bigcup_{I\in{\mathcal{I}}(f)}C(I)=\bigcup_{k=1}^{t}C(I_{k}).

Since
\#{\mathcal{I}}(f)=\infty
, there is
I_{k}
such that

(7.14)

\displaystyle\#{\mathcal{I}}(f)\cap C(I_{k})=\infty.

Define
J_{1}=I_{k}
.
Next,

(7.15)

\displaystyle\bigcup_{I\in{\mathcal{I}}(f)\cap C(J_{1})\smallsetminus\{J_{1}\}}C(I)

is also a finite union, and thus there is

J_{2}\in{\mathcal{I}}(f)\cap C(J_{1})\smallsetminus\{J_{1}\}

such that
\#\left({\mathcal{I}}(f)\cap C(J_{1})\smallsetminus\{J_{1}\}\right)\cap C(J_{2})=\infty
.
Repeat this and we get a sequence
(J_{k})_{k=1}^{\infty}
such that

(7.16)

\displaystyle J_{k}\in{\mathcal{I}}(f)\ \text{for $k=1,2,\dots$}

(7.17)

\displaystyle C(J_{1})\supsetneq C(J_{2})\supsetneq\cdots.

Now let us write
f=\sum_{I}a_{I}T^{I}
and take
I_{0}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}^{n}
such that

(7.18)

\displaystyle|a_{I_{0}}|r^{I_{0}}=\|f\|_{K\{r^{-1}T\}}\ \text{and}\ |I_{0}|=\ord_{K\{r^{-1}T\}}(f).

Then we have
{\mathcal{I}}(f)\cap C(I_{0})=\{I_{0}\}
.
Indeed, if
J\in C(I_{0})\smallsetminus\{I_{0}\}
and
s\in\prod_{i=1}^{n}(0,r_{i}]
, then

(7.19)

\displaystyle|a_{J}|s^{J}=|a_{J}|r^{J}\frac{s^{J}}{r^{J}}<|a_{I_{0}}|r^{I_{0}}\frac{s^{J}}{r^{J}}=|a_{I_{0}}|s^{I_{0}}\frac{r^{I_{0}}}{s^{I_{0}}}\frac{s^{J}}{r^{J}}\leq|a_{I_{0}}|s^{I_{0}}.

By
eq.

7.16
, we have
J_{k}\not\in C(I_{0})
for all
k
.
By replacing it with a subsequence, we may assume that one of the coordinates of

J_{k}
are constant. By changing coordinates, we may assume the last coordinates are
constant. Let us write
J_{k}=(J_{k}^{\prime},j)
where
J_{k}^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}^{n-1}
and

j\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}
.
Consider

(7.20)

\displaystyle g=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}a_{J_{k}}T^{J_{k}}\frac{1}{T_{n}^{j}}\in K\{r_{1}^{-1}T_{1},\dots,r_{n-1}^{-1}T_{n-1}\}.

Since
J_{k}\in{\mathcal{I}}(f)
, we have
J_{k}^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{I}}(g)
.
This implies that
{\mathcal{I}}(g)
is infinite, which contradicts the induction hypothesis.
∎

Corollary 7.3
.

Let
K
be a complete Non-archimedean valued field
and
r=(r_{1},\dots,r_{n})\in{\mathbb{R}}_{>0}^{n}
.
Let
f\in K\{r^{-1}T\}\smallsetminus\{0\}
.
Then there is
C>0
with the following property:
for all finite extension
L
of
K
with the uniquely extended absolute value and
for all
s=(s_{1},\dots,s_{n})\in\prod_{i=1}^{n}(0,r_{i}]
with
s_{i}\in|L|
for
i=1,\dots n
, we have

\displaystyle\left(\hbox{\hbox{\kern 0.0pt\raise-7.26447pt\vbox{\halign{\relax\hfil\txtline@@{#}\hfil\cr\hbox{{\ignorespaces the number of prime factors counted with multiplicity }\\\hbox{{\ignorespaces of $f$ as an element of $L\{s^{-1}T\}$}\crcr}}}}\right)\leq C.}}

Here note that
L\{s^{-1}T\}
is UFD, because
s_{i}\in|L|

and hence
L\{s^{-1}T\}\simeq L\{T\}
.
(cf.
[
3
, (5.2.6) Theorem 1]
.)

Proof.

Let
L
and
s
as in the statement.
Let
f=P_{1}\dots P_{l}
be the prime factorization in
L\{s^{-1}T\}
.
Then, by
lemma

7.2
(1), (2), and (3), we obtain

(7.23)

\displaystyle\ord_{K\{s^{-1}T\}}(f)=\ord_{L\{s^{-1}T\}}(f)=\sum_{i=1}^{l}\ord_{L\{s^{-1}T\}}(P_{i})\geq l.

By
lemma

7.2
(4),
\ord_{K\{s^{-1}T\}}(f)
is bounded
when
s
varies, we are done.
∎

Corollary 7.4
.

Let
K
be a complete Non-archimedean valued field.
Let
r=(r_{1},\dots,r_{n})\in{\mathbb{R}}_{>0}^{n}
with

r_{i}\in|K|
for
i=1,\dots,n
.
Let
f\in K\{r^{-1}T\}\smallsetminus\{0\}
.
Then there is a finite extension
L
of
K
such that
for all finite extensions
L^{\prime}
of
L
, the prime factorization
of
f
in
L\{r^{-1}T\}
is the prime factorization of

f
in
L^{\prime}\{r^{-1}T\}
as well.

Proof.

By
corollary

7.3
, the number of prime factors of
f

as an element of
L\{r^{-1}T\}
are bounded when
L
varies finite extensions of
K
.

Hence

9.117

Thus we proved every

.
Thus there is a finite product
\widetilde{\Psi}
of some of

.
Finally, since
\Psi

∎

Now note that

.
Thus, replacing
\alpha
with iterates, we may assume
every minimal prime ideal of

containing

is either fixed or

(cf. Case 2-2).
Let

where

are distinct primes

(9.119)

\displaystyle\left(\frac{a^{k}}{b^{l}}\right)^{d^{n}}=1.

Since

\displaystyle\left(\frac{a^{k}}{b^{l}}\right)^{d^{n_{1}}}=1

Lemma 9.5
.

Let
p
be a prime and
P(X)\in{\mathbb{C}}_{p}[X]
be a non-zero polynomial.
Let
d\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 1}
be such that
p\not\mid d
.
If a
\xi\in{\mathbb{C}}_{p}
with
|\xi|=1
satisfies

(9.126)

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}P(\xi^{d^{n}})=0,

then
\xi
is a root of
1
.

Proof.

Let
\zeta_{1},\dots,\zeta_{r}\in{\mathbb{C}}_{p}
be all the distinct roots of
P

to which the sequence
\{\xi^{d^{n}}\}_{n}
accumulates.
We may replace
P
with
(X-\zeta_{1})\cdots(X-\zeta_{r})
.
Note that for each
\zeta_{i}
, there is
\zeta_{j}
such that

\zeta_{i}^{d}=\zeta_{j}
.
Indeed, take a subsequence
\{n_{k}\}_{k}
such that

\xi^{d^{n_{k}}}\to\zeta_{i}
.
Then
\xi^{d^{n_{k}+1}}\to\zeta_{i}^{d}
.
By
eq.

9.126
,
\zeta_{i}^{d}
must be equal to some
\zeta_{j}
.

Now take
m\geq 1
so that
\zeta_{i}^{d^{m}}=\zeta_{i}

or
\zeta_{i}\neq\zeta_{i}^{d^{m}}=\zeta_{i}^{d^{2m}}
for all
i
.
Then take small mutually disjoint open balls
B_{i}

around
\zeta_{i}
. Take small open balls
\zeta_{i}\in B_{i}^{\prime}\subset B_{i}

so that the power map
z\mapsto z^{d^{m}}
maps
B_{i}^{\prime}
into

B_{j}
if
\zeta_{i}^{d^{m}}=\zeta_{j}
.
Then there is
n_{0}\geq 1
such that for all
n\geq n_{0}
, we have

\xi^{d^{n}}\in B_{i}^{\prime}
for a unique
i
.
Take
i
such that
\zeta_{i}^{d^{m}}=\zeta_{i}
and

n_{1}\geq n_{0}
such that
\xi^{d^{n_{1}}}\in B_{i}^{\prime}
.
Then we have
\xi^{d^{n_{1}+m}}\in B_{i}
and hence

\xi^{d^{n_{1}+m}}\in B_{i}^{\prime}
.
Repeat this and we get
\xi^{d^{n_{1}+nm}}\in B_{i}^{\prime}
for all
n\geq 0
.
By
eq.

9.126
, we have

\lim_{n\to\infty}\xi^{d^{n_{1}+nm}}=\zeta_{i}
.
Set
\eta=\xi^{d^{n_{1}}}
and
\zeta=\zeta_{i}
.
Then we have
\lim_{n\to\infty}\eta^{(d^{m})^{n}}=\zeta

and
\zeta^{d^{m}}=\zeta
.
Note that
|\zeta|=1
since
|\eta|=1
and hence

\zeta
is a root of
1
.

To end the proof, it is enough to show that
\eta
is a root of
1
.
Let us take an
n\geq 0
such that

|\eta^{(d^{m})^{n}}-\zeta|<1
.
Then we have

(9.127)

\displaystyle\eta^{(d^{m})^{n+1}}

\displaystyle=\left(\zeta+\eta^{(d^{m})^{n}}-\zeta\right)^{d^{m}}

(9.128)

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=0}^{d^{m}}\binom{d^{m}}{l}\zeta^{d^{m}-l}\left(\eta^{(d^{m})^{n}}-\zeta\right)^{l}

(9.129)

\displaystyle=\zeta^{d^{m}}+d^{m}\zeta^{d^{m}-1}\left(\eta^{(d^{m})^{n}}-\zeta\right)+\sum_{l=2}^{d^{m}}\binom{d^{m}}{l}\zeta^{d^{m}-l}\left(\eta^{(d^{m})^{n}}-\zeta\right)^{l}

(9.130)

\displaystyle=\zeta+d^{m}\zeta^{d^{m}-1}\left(\eta^{(d^{m})^{n}}-\zeta\right)+\sum_{l=2}^{d^{m}}\binom{d^{m}}{l}\zeta^{d^{m}-l}\left(\eta^{(d^{m})^{n}}-\zeta\right)^{l}.

Now since
p\not\mid d
, we have
|d|=1
.
Thus, combined with
|\zeta|=1
, we have

(9.131)

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{l=2}^{d^{m}}\binom{d^{m}}{l}\zeta^{d^{m}-l}\left(\eta^{(d^{m})^{n}}-\zeta\right)^{l}\right|<\left|d^{m}\zeta^{d^{m}-1}\left(\eta^{(d^{m})^{n}}-\zeta\right)\right|

for
2\leq l\leq d^{m}
and hence

(9.132)

\displaystyle\left|\eta^{(d^{m})^{n+1}}-\zeta\right|=\left|d^{m}\zeta^{d^{m}-1}\left(\eta^{(d^{m})^{n}}-\zeta\right)\right|=\left|\eta^{(d^{m})^{n}}-\zeta\right|.

This implies
\left|\eta^{(d^{m})^{n}}-\zeta\right|
is constant as

n
goes to infinity. Since
\eta^{(d^{m})^{n}}
converges to
\zeta
, we have
\eta^{(d^{m})^{n}}=\zeta
for a large
n

and we are done.
∎

Lemma 9.6
.

Let
K\subset L
be a field extension.
Let
f\in K\llbracket T_{1},\dots,T_{n}\rrbracket\smallsetminus\{0\}
and

g\in L\llbracket T_{1},\dots,T_{n}\rrbracket
.
If
fg\in K\llbracket T_{1},\dots,T_{n}\rrbracket
, then we have
g\in K\llbracket T_{1},\dots,T_{n}\rrbracket
.

Proof.

We prove by induction on
n
.
If
n=0
, then the statement is trivial.
Suppose
n\geq 1
. Write

(9.133)

\displaystyle f=\sum_{i\geq 0}a_{i}T_{n}^{i},\ g=\sum_{j\geq 0}b_{j}T_{n}^{j}

where
a_{i}\in K\llbracket T_{1},\dots,T_{n-1}\rrbracket,b_{j}\in L\llbracket T_{1},\dots,T_{n-1}\rrbracket
.
As
fg\in K\llbracket T_{1},\dots,T_{n}\rrbracket
,

(9.134)

\displaystyle a_{0}b_{0},\ a_{0}b_{1}+a_{1}b_{0},\dots,\sum_{i=0}^{k}a_{i}b_{k-i},\dots

are contained in
K\llbracket T_{1},\dots,T_{n-1}\rrbracket
.
Using the induction hypothesis, we inductively see that

b_{j}\in K\llbracket T_{1},\dots,T_{n-1}\rrbracket
for all
j
.
∎

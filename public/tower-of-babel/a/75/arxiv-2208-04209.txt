Abstract
Corkite, PbFe 3 ​ ( PO 4 ​ ) ( SO 4 ​ ) ( OH ) 6 \text{PbFe}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}}\text{(}\text{PO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{4}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{4}}\text{}}\text{)}\text{(}\text{SO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{4}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{4}}\text{}}\text{)}\text{(}\text{OH}\text{)}\text{}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{6}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{6}}\text{}} , an understudied relative of the jarosite family of Heisenberg antiferromagnets, has been synthesized and its magnetic properties characterized for the first time. Relative to natural samples, synthetic corkite displays signatures in both infrared and Raman spectra of a more ordered arrangement of polyanion groups about the kagomé sublattice that retains inversion symmetry. Magnetic susceptibility measurements reveal that dried corkite undergoes a transition to a long-range, antiferromagnetically-ordered state below T N T_{N} = 48 K, lower than that observed in the majority of jarosite phases, and indicative of further spin frustration. Curie-Weiss fitting of the measured magnetic susceptibility yields an effective magnetic moment of p eff = 6.29(1) μ B \mu_{B} /Fe 3+ and θ C ​ W \theta_{CW} = -526.0(1.1) K, analogous to that observed in similar high-spin Fe 3+ systems, and indicative of strong antiferromagnetic coupling. Estimation of the change in magnetic entropy as a function of temperature from T = 0 K to T = 195 K, Δ ​ S m ​ a ​ g \Delta S_{mag} = 14.86 J ⋅ m ​ o ​ l F ​ e 3 + − 1 ​ K − 1 J\cdot mol_{Fe^{3+}}^{-1}K^{-1} , is also in good agreement with the Δ ​ S m ​ a ​ g \Delta S_{mag} = Rln(2S+1) = 14.9 J ⋅ m ​ o ​ l − 1 ​ K − 1 J\cdot mol^{-1}K^{-1} expected for a S = 5/2 system. In comparison to the pure jarosites, where both structure and magnetism remain largely invariant upon a variety of chemical substitutions, the replacement of one sulfate group per formula unit with a higher-valent phosphate group applies additional steric and electronic pressure on the kagomé lattice in corkite, further frustrating the magnetic ground state of the material. Corkite thus represents both an outlier in the known body of jarosite-type materials, and an illustration of how existing structures may be further strained in the development of highly frustrated magnetic systems.

Introduction

Materials containing the kagomé lattice, consisting of a two-dimensional network of corner-sharing triangles, have long been studied for the novel phenomena they are often host to, including strongly correlated topological states
Zhang et al. 2021
, superconductivity,
Neupert et al. 2022
and most notably high degrees of magnetic frustration.
Shores et al. 2005
;
Hiroi et al. 2001
;
Meschke et al. 2021
One such family of materials, the jarosites, (
\text{AB}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}}\text{(}\text{XO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{4}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{4}}\text{}}\text{)}\text{}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}\text{(}\text{OH}\text{)}\text{}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{6}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{6}}\text{}}
, where A = a monovalent or divalent cation, B = a trivalent cation, and
\text{XO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{4}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{4}}\text{}}
= a divalent polyanion group) have long been studied for the impact of kagomé-sublattice vacancies on their magnetic properties
Grohol et al. 2003
;
Basciano and Peterson 2007
. The majority of the jarosite phases possess metal-ligand-metal (M-L-M) bridging angles on the order of 130-140°, are known to order antiferromagnetically between
T
= 55-65 K, and are considered near-ideal Heisenberg antiferromagnets, with weakly ferromagnetic intralayer coupling and antiferromagnetic interplanar coupling
Inami et al. 2000
;
Wills et al. 2000
. The strongly anisotropic magnetism in these materials arises from the Dzyaloshinskii-Moriya (DM) interaction, whereby the tilting of the Fe octahedra which comprise the kagomé lattice produces a canting of the spins about each triangular hole into a spin umbrella configuration
Yildirim and Harris 2006
. Despite the high degree of variability in both the cation and capping groups which have been successfully substituted into the jarosite structure, the tilt angle and degree of distortion of the kagomé-layer
\text{FeO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{6}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{6}}\text{}}
octahedra remains relatively consistent across the family.

However, several jarosite phases have been reported which contradict this observed trend. The hydronium and deuterated jarosite analogues, owing to the favorability of interactions between
\text{H}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}}\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{}}\text{+}}
and
\text{OH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{}}\text{\hskip 0.90417pt--\hskip 0.90417pt}}
ions in the structure, instead exhibit a spin glass transition around
T_{g}
= 15 K
Grohol and Nocera 2007
.
In order to maintain charge neutrality, jarosite phases containing divalent cations have only a 1/2 A-site occupancy, but otherwise possess quite similar structural and magnetic properties to those possessing full A-site occupancy. The Pb-analogue plumbojarosite,
\text{Pb}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{0.5}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{0.5}}\text{}}\text{Fe}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}}\text{(}\text{SO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{4}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{4}}\text{}}\text{)}\text{(}\text{OH}\text{)}\text{}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{6}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{6}}\text{}}
, is observed to order antiferromagnetically below
T_{N}
= 56.4 K, which is slightly lower than other members of the family, and likely due to increased steric effects from the larger
\text{Pb}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{}}\text{2+}}
cation.
Bartlett and Nocera 2005
. Beudantite,
\text{PbFe}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}}\text{(}\text{SO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{4}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{4}}\text{}}\text{)}\text{(}\text{AsO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{4}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{4}}\text{}}\text{)}\text{(}\text{OH}\text{)}\text{}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{6}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{6}}\text{}}
, containing alternating sulfate and larger arsenate groups, has only been characterized from a heavily
\text{H}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}}\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{}}\text{+}}
-substituted natural sample, and was observed to order at around
T_{N}
= 45 K, indicating that the additional steric pressure on the kagomé plane further frustrates the spins, thereby suppressing long-range order
Wills et al. 2004
. A recent computational study suggested that corkite,
\text{PbFe}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}}\text{(}\text{PO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{4}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{4}}\text{}}\text{)}\text{(}\text{SO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{4}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{4}}\text{}}\text{)}\text{(}\text{OH}\text{)}\text{}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{6}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{6}}\text{}}
, a related mineral with a similar calculated ground state Ising spin configuration to the jarosites, would benefit from magnetic characterization to determine whether it behaves as a Heisenberg antiferromagnet, or if it exhibits more interesting magnetism at low temperatures
Meschke et al. 2021
. The structure of corkite has been previously probed in both natural and synthetic samples (Figure
1
a,
1
b), however its magnetic properties have not yet been reported.
Giuseppetti and Tadini 1987
;
Frost and Palmer 2011
While pure phase specimens of corkite would not contain the rather reactive
\text{H}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}}\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{}}\text{+}}
species, it is not known whether the substitution of one sulfate group per formula unit with a higher-valent, larger phosphate group could disrupt the interlayer couplings observed in the pure jarosites, producing a more ideal Heisenberg antiferromagnet.

Here we report the hydrothermal synthesis of synthetic corkite, as well as the characterization of its structural and magnetic properties. A combination of powder x-ray diffraction and IR and Raman spectroscopy measurements are used to confirm prior structural assignments of both natural and synthetic corkite samples in the
R
\bar{3}
m
space group (166). Magnetic susceptibility and heat capacity measurements are also performed and show synthetic corkite to order antiferromagnetically below T
N
= 48 K, with an effective magnetic moment on the order expected for an Fe
3+
, S = 5/2 system. Our results show corkite to be a frustrated Heisenberg antiferromagnet, and provide a deeper understanding of how the split polyanion site occupancy, and subsequently larger steric and electronic strain, impacts the magnetic behavior of corkite, relative to the broader family of jarosite materials.

3
Conclusion

In conclusion, we report the hydrothermal synthesis and magnetic characterization of corkite,
\text{PbFe}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}}\text{(}\text{PO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{4}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{4}}\text{}}\text{)}\text{(}\text{SO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{4}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{4}}\text{}}\text{)}\text{(}\text{OH}\text{)}\text{}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{6}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{6}}\text{}}
, an understudied relative of the jarosite family of materials. Relative to previous studies on natural samples of this phase, the structure appears to have a more even and ordered distribution of phosphate and sulfate groups bounding the kagomé sublattice, as supported by the presence of additional features in and overall higher resolution of the measured infrared and Raman spectra. Magnetic susceptibility measurements of synthetic corkite reveal a transition to a long-range AFM state below
T_{N}
= 48 K, lower than that observed in the majority of jarosite phases, indicating that the alternating bounding polyanion groups do indeed further suppress magnetic ordering in the system. The calculated effective magnetic moment and observed saturation of magnetic entropy are both on the order of what is expected for a S = 5/2 system, indicating that Fe
3+
is the main species present on the kagomé sublattice, and that corkite’s Fe-site occupancy is analogous to jarosite phases synthesized by similar methods. TGA/DTA measurements also show that the structure incorporates a small amount of water under atmospheric conditions, producing a small suppression of the calculated effective magnetic moment. Our results highlight the importance of steric strain in the development and design of novel frustrated magnetic systems, and contribute to a more complete understanding of the jarosite family of Heisenberg antiferromagnets.

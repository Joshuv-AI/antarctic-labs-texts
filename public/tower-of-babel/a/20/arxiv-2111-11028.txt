Charged Rényi negativity of massless free bosons
Hui-Huang Chen

Abstract
In this paper, we consider the computation of charged moments of the reduced density matrix of two disjoint intervals in the 1 + 1 1+1 dimensional free compactified boson conformal field theory (CFT) by studying the four-point function of the fluxed twist fields. We obtained the exact scaling function of this four-point function and discussed its decompactification limit. This scaling function was used to obtain the charged moments of the partial transpose which we refer as charged Rényi negativity. These charged moments and the charged moments of the partial transpose are essential for the problem of symmetry decomposition of the corresponding entanglement measures. We test our analytic formula against exact numerical computation in the complex harmonic chain, finding perfect agreements.

In this manuscript, we will first consider the charged moments of the reduced density matrix of two disjoint intervals in 1+1 dimensional free boson conformal field theory (CFT) by the method of fluxed twist fields. The charge imbalance resolved negativity can be obtained by Fourier transforming the charged Rényi negativity and taking the replica limit which has not worked out in this paper due to technical complications.

Entanglement negativity in CFT

2.1
Moments of reduced density matrix

Figure 1:
In 1+1 dimensional spacetime, the time slice is one dimensional, we will consider the density matrix for the field theory on the subsystem
A=A_{1}\cup A_{2}
upon tracing out the local degree of freedom in environment
B
. The endpoints of sub subsystem
A_{1},A_{2}
is denoted by
u_{1},v_{1}

Let us briefly review the calculation of Rényi entanglement negativity via path integrals in two-dimensional CFTs. We denote
\phi
as the basic field in the theory. The matrix elements of the density matrix
\rho
of vacuum are
\rho(\phi,\phi^{\prime})=\braket{\phi|0}\braket{0|\phi^{\prime}}
.
Now we consider the subsystem
A
consists
N
disjoint interval on the real axis,
A=\cup_{i=1}^{N}[u_{i},v_{i}]
. The matrix elements of RDM
\rho_{A}
are given by tracing out the degree of freedom not in
A

\rho_{A}(\phi,\phi^{\prime})=\frac{1}{Z}\int\mathcal{D}\phi_{B}\braket{\phi_{A}=\phi,\phi_{B}|0}\braket{0|\phi_{A}=\phi^{\prime},\phi_{B}}.

(2.1)

For integer
n
, the moments of RDM
\rho_{A}
are

{\textrm{Tr}}\rho_{A}^{n}=\frac{1}{Z_{1}^{n}}\int\prod_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{D}\phi_{i}\rho_{A}(\phi_{i},\phi_{i+1}),\quad\phi_{n+1}\equiv\phi_{1},

(2.2)

where
Z_{1}
is the partition function on the sphere and from the path integral above we should glue the cuts cyclicly along with
A
, which turns out to be the partition function on an
n
-sheeted Riemann surface
\mathcal{R}_{n,N}
with genus
(n-1)(N-1)
. The simplest and most important example is the entanglement entropy of an interval
A
of length
l
in an infinite line. Since the genus of the corresponding Riemann surface for
N=1
is zero, the entanglement entropy
S_{A}
has the universal form

S_{A}=\frac{c}{3}\log\frac{l}{\epsilon}+\mathrm{const},

(2.3)

where
c
is the central charge of the CFT, and
\epsilon
is the UV cutoff.

For general
N>1
, it’s convenient to introduce branch point twist fields in the complex plane to avoid from studying complicated Riemann surfaces. As a result, the partition function on Riemann surface
\mathcal{R}_{n,N}
can be written as
2N
point function of branch point twist fields in the complex plane
[
8
]

{\textrm{Tr}}\rho_{A}^{n}=\langle\prod_{i=1}^{N}\mathcal{T}_{n}(u_{i})\tilde{\mathcal{T}}_{n}(v_{i})\rangle

(2.4)

The twist fields
\mathcal{T}_{n}
and anti-twist field
\tilde{\mathcal{T}_{n}}
are primary operators and they have the same dimension

\Delta_{n}=\frac{c}{12}\left(n-\frac{1}{n}\right).

(2.5)

By conformal map
w(z)=\frac{(u_{1}-z)(u_{N}-v_{N})}{(u_{1}-u_{N})(z-v_{N})}
, the above
2N
point function only depends on
2N-3
cross-ratios
x_{1}=w(v_{1}),x_{2}=w(u_{2}),\cdots,x_{2N-3}=w(v_{N-1})
, then we can write

{\textrm{Tr}}\rho_{A}^{n}=c_{n}^{N}\Big|\frac{\prod_{i<j}(u_{j}-u_{i})(v_{j}-v_{i})}{\prod_{i,j}(v_{j}-u_{i})}\Big|^{2\Delta_{n}}\mathcal{F}_{N,n}(x_{1},\cdots,x_{2N-3}),

(2.6)

where
\mathcal{F}_{N,n}(x_{1},\cdots,x_{2N-3})
is sensitive to the full operator content of the theory and usually is very complicated. Only very few results are known
[
42
,
43
,
44
,
45
,
46
,
47
]
. For the free real boson compactified on a circle of radius
\mathrm{R}
, analytic expressions are available for
N=2
and for integer
n>1
. In this case, there is only one independent cross ration
x=\frac{(u_{1}-v_{1})(u_{2}-v_{2})}{(u_{1}-u_{2})(v_{1}-v_{2})}
. Then eq. (
2.6
) specialised to the case
N=2
reads

{\textrm{Tr}}\rho_{A}^{n}=c_{n}^{2}\left(\frac{(u_{2}-u_{1})(v_{2}-v_{1})}{(v_{1}-u_{1})(v_{2}-u_{2})(v_{2}-u_{1})(u_{2}-v_{1})}\right)^{2\Delta_{n}}\mathcal{F}_{n}(x),

(2.7)

where
\mathcal{F}_{n}(x)\equiv\mathcal{F}_{2,n}(x)
. The function
\mathcal{F}_{n}(x)
is
[
43
]
(parametrized in terms of
\eta=\mathrm{R}^{2}/2
)

\mathcal{F}_{n}(x)=\frac{\Theta(\mathbb{0}|\eta\tau)\Theta(\mathbb{0}|\tau/\eta)}{\Theta(\mathbb{0}|\tau)^{2}},

(2.8)

where
\tau
is
(n-1)\times(n-1)
matrix with the following entries:

\tau_{rs}=\frac{2\mathrm{i}}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\sin(\frac{\pi k}{n})\frac{{}_{2}F_{1}(k/n,1-k/n;1;1-x)}{{}_{2}F_{1}(k/n,1-k/n;1;x)}\cos[\frac{2\pi k}{n}(r-s)].

(2.9)

While
\Theta
is the Siegel theta function defined by

\Theta(\mathbf{z}|M)=\sum_{\mathbf{m}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n-1}}e^{\mathrm{i}\pi\mathbf{m}^{t}\cdot M\cdot\mathbf{m}+2\pi\mathrm{i}\mathbf{m}^{t}\cdot\mathbf{z}},

(2.10)

which is a function of the
(n-1)
dimensional vector
\mathbf{z}
and of the
(n-1)\times(n-1)
matrix
M
which must be symmetric and with positive imaginary part.
In the remaining part of this paper, we will mainly focus on the case
N=2
.

2.2
The repica trick for negativity

In this section, we briefly review the computation of entanglement negativity in 1+1 dimensional CFT using twist fields. Let
\ket{e_{i}^{(1)}}
and
\ket{e_{j}^{(2)}}
be two arbitrary bases of the Hilbert spaces associated to the degree of freedom on
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
respectively. The partial transpose (with respect to the second space) of
\rho_{A}
is defined as

\bra{e_{i}^{(1)}e_{j}^{(2)}}\rho_{A}^{T_{2}}\ket{e_{k}^{(1)}e_{l}^{(2)}}=\bra{e_{i}^{(1)}e_{l}^{(2)}}\rho_{A}\ket{e_{k}^{(1)}e_{j}^{(2)}}.

(2.11)

The Rényi negativity is defined as

R_{n}={\textrm{Tr}}\{(\rho_{A}^{T_{2}})^{n}\}=\sum_{\lambda_{i}}\lambda_{i}^{n},

(2.12)

and entanglement negativity is obtained by taking the limit

\mathcal{N}=\lim_{n\rightarrow\frac{1}{2}}\frac{1}{2}(R_{2n}-1).

(2.13)

As mentioned in the last subsection,
{\textrm{Tr}}\rho_{A}^{n}
for the union of two disjoint interval
A=[u_{1},v_{1}]\cup[u_{2},v_{2}]
is given by the correlator
\langle\mathcal{T}_{n}(u_{1})\tilde{\mathcal{T}}_{n}(v_{1})\mathcal{T}_{n}(u_{2})\tilde{\mathcal{T}}_{n}(v_{2})\rangle
.Taking partial transpose with respect to the interval
A_{2}
means the endpoints of
A_{2}
are exchanged while
A_{1}
stay untouched. According to standard procedure, the path integral representation of
{\textrm{Tr}}\{(\rho_{A}^{T_{2}})^{n}\}
is proportional to the partition function on the Riemann surface
\tilde{\mathcal{R}}_{n,2}
.
\tilde{\mathcal{R}}_{n,2}
differs from
\mathcal{R}_{n,2}
only by the exchange
u_{2}\leftrightarrow v_{2}
, and this immediately translates in terms of correlation function of twist fields as
{\textrm{Tr}}(\rho_{A}^{T_{2}})^{n}=\langle\mathcal{T}_{n}(u_{1})\tilde{\mathcal{T}}_{n}(v_{1})\tilde{\mathcal{T}}_{n}(u_{2})\mathcal{T}_{n}(v_{2})\rangle
. Since the exchange
u_{2}\leftrightarrow v_{2}
sends
x\rightarrow x/(x-1)
, the period matrix
\tilde{\tau}
of
\tilde{\mathcal{R}}_{n,2}
for
x\in(0,1)
is given by

\tilde{\tau}(x)=\tau(x/(x-1))=\mathcal{R}+i\mathcal{I},

(2.14)

where
\tau
is the period matrix of
\mathcal{R}_{n,2}
whose elements have been given in eq. (
2.9
) and we denote the real and imaginary part of
\tilde{\tau}(x)
by
\mathcal{R}
and
\mathcal{I}
respectively.

Since the moments of the partial transposed RDM can be written as four-point functions of twist fields. This four-point function still have the same form with equation eq. (
2.7
)

{\textrm{Tr}}(\rho_{A}^{T_{2}})^{n}=c_{n}^{2}\left(\frac{(u_{2}-u_{1})(v_{2}-v_{1})}{(v_{1}-u_{1})(v_{2}-u_{2})(v_{2}-u_{1})(u_{2}-v_{1})}\right)^{2\Delta_{n}}\mathcal{G}_{n}(x),

(2.15)

and we have the following relation

\mathcal{G}_{n}(x)=(1-x)^{4\Delta_{n}}\mathcal{F}_{n}\left(\frac{x}{x-1}\right).

(2.16)

The result eq. (
2.8
) is valid only for
0<x<1
. For generic
x\in\mathbb{C}
, it can be written as

\mathcal{F}_{n}(x,\bar{x})=\frac{\Theta(\mathbf{0}|T)}{|\Theta(\mathbf{0}|\tau)|^{2}},

(2.17)

where the
2(n-1)\times 2(n-1)
symmetric matrix
T
is

T=\begin{pmatrix}i\eta\mathcal{I}&\mathcal{R}\\
\mathcal{R}&i\mathcal{I}/\eta\end{pmatrix},

(2.18)

When
x\in(0,1)
, the period matrix
\tau(x)
is purely imaginary. Then
\mathcal{R}=0
and the Siegel theta function in eq. (
2.17
) factorizes, giving back the result eq. (
2.8
).

3
Symmetry decomposition of negativity

Let us first review some basic facts about the symmetry resolution of the entanglement entropy.
In this section and the remaining part of this paper, we will assume our system presents a global
U(1)
symmetry
Q
and assuming that
[\rho,Q]=0
, which implies
[\rho_{A},Q_{A}]=0
. We take the subsystem
A
consist of two disjoint region
A=A_{1}\cup A_{2}
. Then
\rho_{A}
admits charge decomposition according to eigenvalues
q
of local charge
Q_{A}

\rho_{A}=\oplus_{q}\Pi_{q}\rho_{A}=\oplus_{q}p(q)\rho_{A}(q),\quad p(q)={\textrm{Tr}}(\Pi_{q}\rho_{A}).

(3.1)

The symmetry resolved Rényi entropies are then defined as

S_{n}(q)=\frac{1}{1-n}\log{\textrm{Tr}}[\rho_{A}(q)]^{n}.

(3.2)

It’s very difficult to compute the symmetry resolved entropy using the above definition due to the non-local feature of the projector
\Pi_{q}
. However, we can bypass this difficulty by using the Fourier representation of the projector and focusing on the charged moments of
\rho_{A}
,

Z_{n}(\mu)={\textrm{Tr}}(e^{i\mu Q_{A}}\rho_{A}^{n}).

(3.3)

Then it’s sufficient to compute the Fourier transform

Z_{n}(q)=\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{d\mu}{2\pi}e^{-iq\mu}Z_{n}(\mu)

(3.4)

to obtain the entropies of the sector of charge
q
as

S_{n}(q)=\frac{1}{1-n}\log\left[\frac{Z_{n}(q)}{Z_{1}(q)^{n}}\right].

(3.5)

Now we turn to the symmetry decomposition of entanglement negativity under
U(1)
charge
Q
. Since the charge
Q
is local, we can write
Q_{A}=Q_{1}+Q_{2}
. From the relation
[\rho_{A},Q_{A}]=0
, performing a partial transposition with respect to the second region
A_{2}
of subsystem
A
, we obtain

[\rho_{A}^{T_{2}},\mathcal{Q}]=0,\quad\mathcal{Q}\equiv Q_{1}-Q_{2}^{T_{2}},

(3.6)

where we have introduced the charge imbalance operator
\mathcal{Q}
and we will denote it’s eigenvalues as
\mathrm{q}
to make a distinction with the eigenvalues of
Q_{A}
.
Then
\rho_{A}^{T_{2}}
has a block matrix form, each block was characterized by different eigenvalues
\mathrm{q}
of the imbalance operator
\mathcal{Q}

\rho_{A}^{T_{2}}=\oplus_{\mathrm{q}}\Pi_{\mathrm{q}}\rho_{A}^{T_{2}}.

(3.7)

we define the normalized charge imbalance partially transposed density matrix

\rho_{A}^{T_{2}}({\mathrm{q}})=\frac{\Pi_{\mathrm{q}}\rho_{A}^{T_{2}}}{{\textrm{Tr}}(\Pi_{\mathrm{q}}\rho_{A}^{T_{2}})}.

(3.8)

Then we can write

\rho_{A}^{T_{2}}({\mathrm{q}})=\oplus_{\mathrm{q}}p({\mathrm{q}})\rho_{A}^{T_{2}}({\mathrm{q}}),

(3.9)

where
p({\mathrm{q}})={\textrm{Tr}}(\Pi_{\mathrm{q}}\rho_{A}^{T_{2}})
is the probability of finding
{\mathrm{q}}
as the outcome of measurement of
\mathcal{Q}
.

The charge imbalance resolved negativity is defined as

\mathcal{N}({\mathrm{q}})=\frac{1}{2}({\textrm{Tr}}|\rho_{A}^{T_{2}}({\mathrm{q}})|-1).

(3.10)

The total negativity is given by the sum of charge imbalance resolved negativity weighted by the corresponding probability

\mathcal{N}=\sum_{\mathrm{q}}p({\mathrm{q}})\mathcal{N}({\mathrm{q}}).

(3.11)

The charge imbalance resolved Rényi negativity is defined as

R_{n}({\mathrm{q}})={\textrm{Tr}}[(\rho_{A}^{T_{2}}({\mathrm{q}}))^{n}]=\frac{1}{p({\mathrm{q}})^{n}}{\textrm{Tr}}[\Pi_{\mathrm{q}}(\rho_{A}^{T_{2}})^{n}].

(3.12)

Then the charge imbalance entanglement negativity can be obtained by taking the limit

\mathcal{N}({\mathrm{q}})=\lim_{n\rightarrow\frac{1}{2}}\frac{1}{2}(R_{2n}({\mathrm{q}})-1).

(3.13)

The projection operator
\Pi_{\mathrm{q}}
has the following integral representation

\Pi_{\mathrm{q}}=\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{d\mu}{2\pi}e^{-i\mu{\mathrm{q}}}e^{i\mu(Q_{1}-Q_{2}^{T_{2}})}.

(3.14)

We can first compute the charged Rényi negativity

R_{n}(\mu)={\textrm{Tr}}[(\rho_{A}^{T_{2}})^{n}e^{i\mu(Q_{1}-Q_{2}^{T_{2}})}],

(3.15)

then the charge imbalance resolved Rényi negativity are related by Fourier transform

R_{n}({\mathrm{q}})=\frac{\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{d\mu}{2\pi}e^{-i\mu{\mathrm{q}}}R_{n}(\mu)}{[\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{d\mu}{2\pi}e^{-i\mu{\mathrm{q}}}R_{1}(\mu)]^{n}}.

(3.16)

4
Charged moments of two disjoint interval in free boson CFT

4.1
Fluxed twist fields and charged moments

In a generic two-dimensional QFT, according to the path integral representation of the charged moments
Z_{n}(\mu)
defined in eq. (
3.3
), we can treat it as the partition function on the Riemann surface
\mathcal{R}_{n,N}
pierced by an Aharonov-Bohm flux, such that the total phase accumulated by the field upon going through the entire surface is
\mu
. We denote this fluxed Riemann surface by
\mathcal{R}_{n,N}^{(\mu)}
. The presence of the flux corresponds to impose twist boundary condition.
This boundary condition fuses with twist field at the endpoints of subsystem
A
can be implemented by two local fields
\mathcal{T}_{n,\mu}
and
\tilde{\mathcal{T}}_{n,\mu}
termed as fluxed twist fields and fluxed anti-twist fields. These fluxed twist fields take into account not only the internal permutational symmetry among the replicas but also the presence of the flux. The partition function on
\mathcal{R}_{n,N}^{(\mu)}
is related to the
2N
-point function of the fluxed twist operators.

Studying field theory on the complicated fluxed Riemann surface usually turns out to be very hard. It’s more convenient to move the topology of the world-sheet to the target space where the fields lie. In other words, we can work on a theory defined on a single complex plane formed by
n
independent copies of the original theory. In this multi-copy theory, we have to deal with fields with
n
-component and we denote
\phi_{j}
as the field on the
j
-th copy.
For subsystem
A
made of two disjoint intervals, we have

Z_{n}(\mu)=\langle\mathcal{T}_{n,\mu}(u_{1})\tilde{\mathcal{T}}_{n,\mu}(v_{1})\mathcal{T}_{n,\mu}(u_{2})\tilde{\mathcal{T}}_{n,\mu}(v_{2})\rangle.

(4.1)

Upon crossing the cut
A
, the field
\phi_{i}
transforms as
\phi_{i}\rightarrow(T_{\mu})_{ij}\phi_{j}
, where the matrix elements of the transformation matrix
T_{\mu}
are given by
(T_{\mu})_{ij}=e^{\mathrm{i}\mu/n}\delta_{i+1,j}
with boundary condition
n+1\equiv 1
. The eigenvectors of the matrix
T_{\mu}
are

\varphi_{k}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}e^{2\pi i\frac{k}{n}j}\phi_{j},\qquad k=0,1,2,\cdots,n-1,

(4.2)

with corresponding eigenvalues given by
\lambda_{k}=e^{\mathrm{i}\frac{\mu}{n}+2\pi\mathrm{i}\frac{k}{n}}
. In other words, in the space span by the fields
\varphi_{k}
, the action of the fluxed twist field decoupled, i.e. they act on each
k
independently. Thus we can write

\mathcal{T}_{n,\mu}=\prod_{k=0}^{n-1}\mathcal{T}_{n,\mu,k},\qquad\tilde{\mathcal{T}}_{n,\mu}=\prod_{k=0}^{n-1}\tilde{\mathcal{T}}_{n,\mu,k}.

(4.3)

Thus, the partition function
Z_{n}(\mu)
also factorize as

Z_{n}(\mu)=\prod_{k=0}^{n-1}Z_{n,k}(\mu)=\prod_{k=0}^{n-1}\langle\mathcal{T}_{n,\mu,k}(u_{1})\tilde{\mathcal{T}}_{n,\mu,k}(v_{1})\mathcal{T}_{n,\mu,k}(u_{2})\tilde{\mathcal{T}}_{n,\mu,k}(v_{2})\rangle.

(4.4)

Now let us consider a concrete model. Namely, we will consider the complex bosonic free compactified field with Euclidean action

\mathcal{S}[\phi]=\frac{g}{4\pi}\int dzd\bar{z}\partial_{z}\phi\partial_{\bar{z}}\phi.

(4.5)

In the multi-copy theory, we have the
n
-component field
\Phi=(\phi_{1},\phi_{2},\cdots,\phi_{n})^{T}
, each component
\phi_{j}
are free but compactified on a circle. Since the field is complex, the target space is a torus with radius
\mathrm{R}_{1}
and
\mathrm{R}_{2}
. Taking the non-trivial winding into account, encircling the endpoints of
A
will lead to

\begin{split}\phi_{j}(e^{2\pi\mathrm{i}}z_{i},e^{-2\pi\mathrm{i}}\bar{z}_{i})=e^{\mathrm{i}\mu/n}\phi_{j-1}(z_{i},\bar{z}_{i})+m_{j,1}\mathrm{R}_{1}+\mathrm{i}m_{j,2}\mathrm{R}_{2},\qquad m_{j,1},m_{j,2}\in\mathbb{Z}\\
\phi_{j}(e^{2\pi\mathrm{i}}\tilde{z}_{i},e^{-2\pi\mathrm{i}}\bar{\tilde{z}}_{i})=e^{-\mathrm{i}\mu/n}\phi_{j+1}(\tilde{z}_{i},\bar{\tilde{z}}_{i})+\tilde{m}_{j,1}\mathrm{R}_{1}+\mathrm{i}\tilde{m}_{j,2}\mathrm{R}_{2},\qquad\tilde{m}_{j,1},\tilde{m}_{j,2}\in\mathbb{Z},\end{split}

(4.6)

where
z_{i}\equiv z-u_{i},\tilde{z}_{i}\equiv z-v_{i}
for
i=1,2
. In this theory,
\mathcal{T}_{n,\mu,k}
and
\tilde{\mathcal{T}}_{n,\mu,k}
are bosonic
U(1)
twist field with dimension (see appendix A for details)

\Delta_{n,k,\mu}=\left(\frac{k}{n}+\frac{\mu}{2\pi n}\right)\left(1-\frac{k}{n}-\frac{\mu}{2\pi n}\right).

(4.7)

Then, the dimension of the fluxed twist fields
\mathcal{T}_{n,\mu}
can be obtained by

\Delta_{n,\mu}=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\Delta_{n,k,\mu}=\frac{1}{6}\left(n-\frac{1}{n}\right)-\frac{\mu^{2}}{4\pi^{2}n}+\frac{\mu}{2\pi n}.

(4.8)

We can simplify this non-trivial monodromy a bit by introducing
\varphi_{k}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}e^{2\pi\mathrm{i}jk/n}\phi_{j}
. Then eq. (
4.6
) becomes

\begin{split}\varphi_{k}(e^{2\pi\mathrm{i}}z_{i},e^{-2\pi\mathrm{i}}\bar{z}_{i})=e^{\mathrm{i}\mu/n+2\pi\mathrm{i}k/n}\varphi_{k}(z_{i},\bar{z}_{i})+\mathrm{R}\sum_{j=1}^{n}e^{2\pi\mathrm{i}jk/n}m_{j},\\
\varphi_{k}(e^{2\pi\mathrm{i}}\tilde{z}_{i},e^{-2\pi\mathrm{i}}\bar{\tilde{z}}_{i})=e^{-\mathrm{i}\mu/n-2\pi\mathrm{i}k/n}\varphi_{k}(\tilde{z}_{i},\bar{\tilde{z}}_{i})+\mathrm{R}\sum_{j=1}^{n}e^{2\pi\mathrm{i}jk/n}\tilde{m}_{j}.\end{split}

(4.9)

Here, for simplicity we set
\mathrm{R}_{1}=\mathrm{R}_{2}=\mathrm{R}
and
m_{j},\tilde{m}_{j}\in\mathbb{Z}+\mathrm{i}\mathbb{Z}
. Consequently, the target space of the field
\varphi_{k}
is a complicated two dimensional lattice
\Lambda

\Lambda=\left\{\mathrm{R}\sum_{j=1}^{n}e^{2\pi\mathrm{i}jk/n}m_{j}\Big|m_{j}\in\mathbb{Z}+\mathrm{i}\mathbb{Z}\right\}.

(4.10)

In order to calculate the partition function, we can split
\varphi_{k}
into a classical and a quantum part
\varphi_{k}=\varphi^{\mathrm{cl}}_{k}+\varphi^{\mathrm{qu}}_{k}
. The classical part takes into account the nontrivial structure of the target space

\varphi^{\mathrm{cl}}_{k}(e^{2\pi\mathrm{i}}z_{i},e^{-2\pi\mathrm{i}}\bar{z}_{i})=e^{2\pi\mathrm{i}a}\varphi^{\mathrm{cl}}_{k}(z_{i},\bar{z}_{i})+\lambda,

(4.11)

where
\lambda\in\Lambda
, while the quantum part is transparent to it

\varphi^{\mathrm{qu}}_{k}(e^{2\pi\mathrm{i}}z_{i},e^{-2\pi\mathrm{i}}\bar{z}_{i})=e^{2\pi\mathrm{i}a}\varphi^{\mathrm{qu}}_{k}(z_{i},\bar{z}_{i}),

(4.12)

where we have defined
a\equiv\frac{k}{n}+\frac{\mu}{2\pi n}
and similar relation holds when encircling around
\tilde{z}_{i}
.
The computation of quantum part is already done in
[
48
]
and we report its derivation in the appendix
A

Z^{\mathrm{qu}}_{n,k}(\mu)=\mathrm{const}\left(\frac{(u_{2}-u_{1})(v_{2}-v_{1})}{(v_{1}-u_{1})(v_{2}-u_{2})(v_{2}-u_{1})(u_{2}-v_{1})}\right)^{2\Delta_{n,k,\mu}}\frac{1}{I_{n,k,\mu}(x)},

(4.13)

where

I_{n,k,\mu}(x)=2F_{a}(x)F_{a}(1-x)=2\beta_{a}[F_{a}(x)]^{2},

(4.14)

and

F_{y}(x)\equiv{{}_{2}F_{1}(y,1-y;1;x)},\quad\beta_{y}(x)\equiv\frac{F_{y}(1-x)}{F_{y}(x)}.

(4.15)

The action for a given classical configuration is given by (eq. (
A.27
) with
\alpha_{a}=0
)

\mathcal{S}^{\mathrm{cl}}_{a}=\frac{2g\pi\sin\pi a}{n\beta_{a}}[|\xi_{1}|^{2}+\beta_{a}^{2}|\xi_{2}|^{2}],

(4.16)

where
\xi_{1},\xi_{2}\in\Lambda
.
Then the partition function can be written as

Z_{n}(\mu)=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{2n}}\prod_{k=0}^{n-1}Z_{a}^{\mathrm{qu}}Z_{a}^{\mathrm{cl}}.

(4.17)

The classical part of the partition function reads

Z^{\mathrm{cl}}_{n}(\mu)=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{2n}}\prod_{k=0}^{n-1}Z_{a}^{\mathrm{cl}}=\sum_{\mathbf{m}\in\mathbb{Z}^{2n}}\prod_{k=0}^{n-1}\exp\Big\{-\frac{2g\pi\sin\pi a}{n}[|\xi_{1}|^{2}\beta_{a}+\beta_{a}^{-1}|\xi_{2}|^{2}]\Big\}.

(4.18)

Since the quantum part does not depend on
\mathbf{m}
, the function
\mathcal{F}_{n}(\mu,x)
can be written as

\mathcal{F}_{n}(\mu,x)=\prod_{k=0}^{n-1}\frac{\mathrm{const}}{\beta_{a}[F_{a}(x)]^{2}}\sum_{\mathbf{m}\in\mathbb{Z}^{2n}}\prod_{k=0}^{n-1}\exp\Big\{-\frac{2g\pi\sin\pi a}{n}[|\xi_{1}|^{2}\beta_{a}+\beta_{a}^{-1}|\xi_{2}|^{2}]\Big\}.

(4.19)

Given
\xi_{i}=\mathrm{R}\sum_{r=1}^{n}e^{2\pi\mathrm{i}kr/n}(m_{r,1}^{(i)}+\mathrm{i}m_{r,2}^{(i)}),i=1,2
,
we have

|\xi_{i}|^{2}=\mathrm{R}^{2}\sum_{r,s=1}^{n}\Big[\sum_{j=1}^{2}m^{(i)}_{r,j}m^{(i)}_{s,j}\Big[\cos\frac{2\pi(r-s)k}{n}\Big]+(m^{(i)}_{r,1}m^{(i)}_{s,2}-m^{(i)}_{s,1}m^{(i)}_{r,2})\sin\Big[\frac{2\pi(r-s)k}{n}\Big]\Big].

(4.20)

Then substitute eq. (
4.20
) into eq. (
4.18
) and after some simplification, one can rewrite the result in terms of Siegel theta functions. Since the computation is almost the same as
[
43
]
, we just report the final result here

Z^{\mathrm{cl}}_{n}(\mu)=[\Theta(\mathbf{0}|\eta\Omega)\Theta(\mathbf{0}|\eta\tilde{\Omega})]^{2},

(4.21)

where
\Omega
and
\tilde{\Omega}
are
n\times n
matrix with elements

\Omega_{rs}=\frac{2\mathrm{i}}{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\sin(\pi a)\beta_{a}\cos[\frac{2\pi k}{n}(r-s)],

(4.22)

\tilde{\Omega}_{rs}=\frac{2\mathrm{i}}{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\sin(\pi a)\frac{1}{\beta_{a}}\cos[\frac{2\pi k}{n}(r-s)],

(4.23)

and
\eta\equiv g\mathrm{R}^{2}
.
The spectrum of the matrices
\Omega
and
\tilde{\Omega}
are given by

\{\omega_{p}|p=1,2,\cdots,n-1\}\cup\Big\{\omega_{n}\equiv\mathrm{i}\sin\left(\frac{\mu}{2n}\right)\beta_{\frac{\mu}{2\pi n}}\Big\}

(4.24)

and

\{\tilde{\omega}_{p}|p=1,2,\cdots,n-1\}\cup\Big\{\tilde{\omega}_{n}\equiv\mathrm{i}\sin\left(\frac{\mu}{2n}\right)\Big/\beta_{\frac{\mu}{2\pi n}}\Big\}

(4.25)

respectively, where for
p=1,2,\cdots,n-1
, we have

\omega_{p}=\mathrm{i}\sin\left[\pi\left(\frac{p}{n}-\frac{\mu}{2\pi n}\right)\right]\beta_{\frac{p}{n}-\frac{\mu}{2\pi n}}+\mathrm{i}\sin\left[\pi\left(\frac{p}{n}+\frac{\mu}{2\pi n}\right)\right]\beta_{\frac{p}{n}+\frac{\mu}{2\pi n}},

(4.26)

\tilde{\omega}_{p}=\mathrm{i}\sin\left[\pi\left(\frac{p}{n}-\frac{\mu}{2\pi n}\right)\right]\Big/\beta_{\frac{p}{n}-\frac{\mu}{2\pi n}}+\mathrm{i}\sin\left[\pi\left(\frac{p}{n}+\frac{\mu}{2\pi n}\right)\right]\Big/\beta_{\frac{p}{n}+\frac{\mu}{2\pi n}}.

(4.27)

The matrices
\Omega
and
\tilde{\Omega}
have a common eigenbasis whose normalized eigenvectors can be written as

(e_{p})_{q}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}e^{2\pi\mathrm{i}pq/n},\quad p,q=1,2,\cdots,n.

(4.28)

Then
\Omega
and
\tilde{\Omega}
can be diagonalized simultaneously by matrix
U
whose elements are given by
U_{pq}=(e_{p})_{q}
.

We finally have

\mathcal{F}_{n}(\mu,x)=s_{n,\mu}\frac{[\Theta(\mathbf{0}|\eta\Omega)\Theta(\mathbf{0}|\eta\tilde{\Omega})]^{2}}{\prod_{k=0}^{n-1}\beta_{a}[F_{a}(x)]^{2}}.

(4.29)

Here
s_{n,\mu}
can be fixed by requiring
\mathcal{F}_{n}(\mu,0)=1
as (See appendix
B
for details)

s_{n,\mu}=2n\eta^{n}\cos^{n-1}\left(\frac{\mu}{2n}\right)\sin\left(\frac{\mu}{2n}\right).

(4.30)

Using identities of the
\Theta
function in the appendix
B
, we find another expression of
\mathcal{F}_{n}(\mu,x)

\mathcal{F}_{n}(\mu,x)=s_{n,\mu}\frac{[\Theta(\mathbf{0}|\eta\Omega)\Theta(\mathbf{0}|\hat{\Omega}/\eta)]^{2}}{\prod_{p=1}^{n}(-\mathrm{i}\eta\tilde{\omega}_{p})\prod_{k=0}^{n-1}\beta_{a}[F_{a}(x)]^{2}},

(4.31)

where

\hat{\Omega}_{rs}=-\tilde{\Omega}^{-1}_{rs}=\frac{-1}{n}\sum_{p=1}^{n}\tilde{\omega}_{p}^{-1}\cos[\frac{2\pi p}{n}(r-s)].

(4.32)

4.2
Small
x
regime

For
x\rightarrow 0
, the function
\beta_{a}(x)
behave as

\beta_{a}(x)=-\frac{\sin\pi a}{\pi}(\log x+f_{a})+\mathcal{O}(x),\quad f_{a}\equiv 2\gamma_{E}+\psi(a)+\psi(1-a),

(4.33)

where
\gamma_{E}
is the Euler constant and
\psi(z)
is the diagamma function. Then

\Theta(0|\eta\Omega)=1+\sum_{\mathbf{m}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}/{\mathbf{0}}}x^{\frac{2\eta}{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\sin^{2}(\pi a)\mathbf{m}^{\mathrm{t}}\cdot C_{k/n}\cdot\mathbf{m}}e^{\frac{2\eta}{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\sin^{2}(\pi a)f_{a}\mathbf{m}^{\mathrm{t}}\cdot C_{k/n}\cdot\mathbf{m}}+\cdots,

(4.34)

where
(C_{k/n})_{rs}\equiv\cos[\frac{2\pi k}{n}(r-s)]

and the following relation holds

\frac{2}{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\sin^{2}(\pi a)\mathbf{m}^{\mathrm{t}}\cdot C_{k/n}\cdot\mathbf{m}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}m_{j}^{2}-\cos\left(\frac{\mu}{n\pi}\right)\sum_{j=1}^{n}m_{j}m_{j+1},

(4.35)

where
m_{n+1}\equiv m_{1}
. This expression is obviously minimized when the non-zero vector
\mathbf{m}
has only one entry equals to
\pm 1
and all other elements are zero. There are
2n
such vectors for which eq. (
4.35
) equals to
1
. All these
2n
vectors gives the same value for
\mathbf{m}^{\mathrm{t}}\cdot C_{k/n}\cdot\mathbf{m}
, and the result is 1.
Using the following integral representation of diagamma function

\psi(z)+\gamma_{E}=\int_{0}^{\infty}dt\frac{e^{-t}-e^{-zt}}{1-e^{-t}},

(4.36)

we find the formula

\begin{split}\frac{2}{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\sin^{2}(\pi a)f_{a}=f_{\frac{\mu}{2\pi}}-2\log n+{\textrm{Re}}~B_{\theta}\left(\frac{\mu}{2\pi},0\right)+{\textrm{Re}}~B_{\theta}\left(1-\frac{\mu}{2\pi},0\right),\end{split}

(4.37)

where
B_{z}(a,b)
is the incomplete Beta function

B_{z}(a,b)=\int_{0}^{z}t^{a-1}(1-t)^{b-1}dt,

(4.38)

and
\theta\equiv e^{2\pi\mathrm{i}/n}
.

Then the coefficient of
x^{\eta}
is
2n^{1-2\eta}\exp\{\eta[f_{\mu/2\pi}+{\textrm{Re}}~B_{\theta}(\mu/2\pi,0)+{\textrm{Re}}~B_{\theta}(1-\mu/2\pi,0)]\}
.

To find the small
x
expansion of
\Theta(\mathbf{0}|\hat{\Omega}/\eta)
, we first compute

\tilde{\omega}_{p}^{-1}=\begin{cases}\frac{\mathrm{i}}{2\pi}\left(\log x+\frac{1}{2}\left(f_{a^{+}}+f_{a^{-}}\right)\right)+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{\log x}\right),&p=1,2,\cdots,n-1\\
\frac{\mathrm{i}}{2\pi}\left(\log x+f_{\frac{\mu}{2\pi n}}\right)+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{\log x}\right),&p=n\end{cases}

(4.39)

where
a^{\pm}\equiv\frac{k}{n}\pm\frac{\mu}{2\pi n}
.
Then

\Theta(0|\hat{\Omega}/\eta)=1+\sum_{\mathbf{m}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}/{\mathbf{0}}}x^{\frac{1}{2n\eta}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\mathbf{m}^{\mathrm{t}}\cdot C_{k/n}\cdot\mathbf{m}}e^{\frac{1}{4n\eta}\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}(f_{a^{+}}+f_{a^{-}})\mathbf{m}^{\mathrm{t}}\cdot C_{k/n}\cdot\mathbf{m}}e^{\frac{1}{2n\eta}f_{\frac{\mu}{2\pi n}}\mathbf{m}^{\mathrm{t}}\cdot C_{n/n}\cdot\mathbf{m}}+\cdots

(4.40)

From the relation

\frac{1}{2n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\mathbf{m}^{\mathrm{t}}\cdot C_{k/n}\cdot\mathbf{m}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{n}m_{j}^{2},

(4.41)

it’s easy to see that this expression is minimized when the non-zero vector
\mathbf{m}
has only one entry equals to
\pm 1
and all other elements are zero. There are
2n
such vectors for which eq. (
4.41
) equals to
\frac{1}{2}
.
From the relation

\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}(f_{a^{+}}+f_{a^{-}})+f_{\frac{\mu}{2\pi n}}=n(f_{\frac{\mu}{2\pi}}-2\log n),

(4.42)

we see that the coefficient of
x^{\frac{1}{2\eta}}
is
2n\exp[\frac{1}{2\eta}(f_{\frac{\mu}{2\pi}}-2\log n)]
.
Therefore we get the small
x
behavior of the scaling function

\mathcal{F}_{n}(\mu,x)=1+\xi_{n,\mu}x^{\alpha}+\cdots

(4.43)

where

\xi_{n,\mu}=\begin{cases}4n\exp[\frac{1}{2\eta}(f_{\frac{\mu}{2\pi}}-2\log n)],&\text{if }\frac{1}{2\eta}\leq\eta\\
4n^{1-\eta}\exp\{\eta[f_{\frac{\mu}{2\pi}}+{\textrm{Re}}~B_{\theta}\left(\frac{\mu}{2\pi},0\right)+{\textrm{Re}}~B_{\theta}\left(1-\frac{\mu}{2\pi},0\right)]\},&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

(4.44)

and
\alpha=\min(\eta,\frac{1}{2\eta})
.

4.3
Decompactification regime

For fixed
x
, in the limit of large
\eta
, we have
\Theta(\mathbf{0}|\eta\Omega)=1+\cdots
and
\Theta(\mathbf{0}|\eta\tilde{\Omega})=1+\cdots
, where
\cdots
denotes terms which vanish when taking the limit
\eta\rightarrow\infty
. Therefore, in the large
\eta
limit, we find

\mathcal{F}_{n}(\mu,x)=\frac{s_{n,\mu}}{\prod_{k=0}^{n-1}F_{a}(1-x)F_{a}(x)},

(4.45)

which recovering the correct quantum result with the proper
\eta
dependent normalisation.

5
Charged Rényi negativity

As explained in section
2
, for the charged Rényi negativity defined in eq. (
3.15
), we have the following relation

\begin{split}&R_{n}(\mu)=\langle\mathcal{T}_{a}(u_{1})\tilde{\mathcal{T}}_{a}(v_{1})\tilde{\mathcal{T}}_{a}(u_{2})\mathcal{T}_{a}(v_{2})\rangle\\
&=c_{n,\mu}^{2}\left(\frac{(u_{2}-u_{1})(v_{2}-v_{1})}{(v_{1}-u_{1})(v_{2}-u_{2})(v_{2}-u_{1})(u_{2}-v_{1})}\right)^{2\Delta_{n,\mu}}\mathcal{G}_{n}(\mu;y),\end{split}

(5.1)

where we have introduced the ordered four point ratio

y=\frac{(v_{1}-u_{1})(v_{2}-u_{2})}{(u_{2}-u_{1})(v_{2}-v_{1})}=\frac{x}{x-1}

(5.2)

with
0<y<1
. The two scaling function
\mathcal{F}_{n}(\mu;x)
and
\mathcal{G}_{n}(\mu;y)
are related as

\mathcal{G}_{n}(\mu;x)=(1-y)^{4\Delta_{n,\mu}}\mathcal{F}_{n}\left(\mu;\frac{y}{y-1}\right).

(5.3)

Since the scaling function
\mathcal{F}_{n}(\mu,x)
given in eq. (
4.29
) is only valid in the region
0<x<1
, while in our case
-\infty<\frac{y}{y-1}<0
. So we must find the expression of the scaling function
\mathcal{F}_{n}(\mu;x,\bar{x})
for generic four-point ratio
x,\bar{x}\in\mathbb{C}
. As explained in appendix
A
, for generic
x\in\mathbb{C}
,
\mathcal{F}_{n}(\mu;x,\bar{x})
reads

\mathcal{F}_{n}(\mu;x,\bar{x})=\prod_{k=0}^{n-1}\frac{\mathrm{const}}{F_{a}(x)\bar{F}_{a}(1-\bar{x})+\bar{F}_{a}(\bar{x})F_{a}(1-x)}\sum_{\mathbf{m}\in\mathbb{Z}^{2n}}\prod_{k=0}^{n-1}e^{-S_{a}^{\mathrm{cl}}}.

(5.4)

In this case, the classical action is given by eq. (
A.27
)

S_{a}^{\mathrm{cl}}=\frac{2g\pi\sin(\pi a)}{n}\left[\frac{|\tau_{a}|^{2}}{\beta_{a}}|\xi_{1}|^{2}+\frac{\alpha_{a}}{\beta_{a}}(\xi_{1}\bar{\xi}_{2}\bar{\gamma}+\bar{\xi}_{1}\xi_{2}\gamma)+\frac{|\xi_{2}|^{2}}{\beta_{a}}\right].

(5.5)

For generic
x\in\mathbb{C}
, the real part
\alpha_{a}(x)
of the modulus
\tau_{a}(x)
of a “fake torus” is nonzero

\tau_{a}(x)\equiv\mathrm{i}\frac{F_{a}(1-x)}{F_{a}(x)}=\alpha_{a}(x)+\mathrm{i}\beta_{a}(x).

(5.6)

Substitute the explicit expression of
\xi_{i}
into eq. (
5.5
) and summing over
k
and
\mathbf{m}
, the classical part of the partition function can be written as some Siegel theta function. This calculation is almost the same with
[
12
]
, and the final result can be obtained by tiny adjustment

\mathcal{F}_{n}(\mu;x,\bar{x})=s_{n,\mu}\frac{\Theta(\mathbf{0}|\eta G)^{2}}{\prod_{k=0}^{n-1}{\textrm{Re}}[F_{a}(x)\bar{F}_{a}(1-\bar{x})]}.

(5.7)

The
2n\times 2n
symmetric matrix
G
introduced in the
\mathrm{r.h.s}
of eq. (
5.7
) is purely imaginary and can be written in terms of
n\times n
block matrices as

G=2\mathrm{i}\begin{pmatrix}A&W\\
W^{\mathrm{t}}&B\end{pmatrix},

(5.8)

with

\begin{split}&A_{rs}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\frac{|\tau_{a}|^{2}}{\beta_{a}}\sin(\pi a)\cos\Big[\frac{2\pi k}{n}(r-s)\Big],\\
&B_{rs}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\frac{1}{\beta_{a}}\sin(\pi a)\cos\Big[\frac{2\pi k}{n}(r-s)\Big],\\
&W_{rs}=-\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\frac{\alpha_{a}}{\beta_{a}}\sin(\pi a)\sin\Big[\frac{2\pi k}{n}(r-s+\frac{1}{2})\Big].\end{split}

(5.9)

For
x\in(0,1)
, we have
\alpha_{a}(x)=0
for every
k
and therefore the off diagonal blocks
W
of
G
are zero, then the Siegel theta function
\Theta(\mathbf{0}|\eta G)
factorizes into a product of two Siegel theta functions
\Theta(\mathbf{0}|\eta A)\Theta(\mathbf{0}|\eta B)=\Theta(\mathbf{0}|\eta\Omega)\Theta(\mathbf{0}|\eta\tilde{\Omega})
and eq. (
5.7
) gives back eq. (
4.29
).

Then we can write
\mathcal{G}(\mu;y)
for
0<y<1
as

\mathcal{G}(\mu;y)=s_{n,\mu}(1-y)^{4\Delta_{n,\mu}}\frac{\Theta(\mathbf{0}|\eta G(\frac{y}{y-1}))^{2}}{\prod_{k=0}^{n-1}{\textrm{Re}}[F_{a}(\frac{y}{y-1})\bar{F}_{a}(\frac{1}{1-y})]}.

(5.10)

In the decompactification limit
\eta\rightarrow\infty
, we have

\mathcal{G}(\mu;y)=\frac{s_{n,\mu}(1-y)^{4\Delta_{n,\mu}}}{\prod_{k=0}^{n-1}{\textrm{Re}}[F_{a}(\frac{y}{y-1})\bar{F}_{a}(\frac{1}{1-y})]}.

(5.11)

6
Numerical results

In this section, we consider the charged moments and the charged Rényi negativity for the complex harmonic chain with periodic boundary conditions. We will use this lattice model to check the CFT formula obtained in section
4
and section
5
in the decompactification regime.

The Hamiltonian of the real harmonic chain made by
L
sites reads

H_{HC}=\sum_{j=0}^{L-1}\left(\frac{1}{2M}p_{j}^{2}+\frac{M\omega^{2}}{2}q_{j}^{2}+\frac{K}{2}(q_{j+1}-q_{j})^{2}\right),

(6.1)

where periodic boundary conditions
q_{L}\equiv q_{0},p_{L}\equiv p_{0}
are imposed and variables
p_{j}
and
q_{j}
satisfy standard bosonic commutation relations
[q_{i},q_{j}]=[p_{i},p_{j}]=0
and
[q_{i},p_{j}]=\mathrm{i}\delta_{ij}
. We can work with
M=K=1
without loss of generality. The lattice version of the complex non-compact bosonic field theory is the sum of two of the above harmonic chain. In terms of the variables
q^{(1)},p^{(1)}
and
q^{(2)},p^{(2)}
, the Hamiltonian is

H_{CHC}(p^{(1)}+\mathrm{i}p^{(2)},q^{(1)}+\mathrm{i}q^{(2)})=H_{HC}(p^{(1)},q^{(1)})+H_{HC}(p^{(2)},q^{(2)}).

(6.2)

Since the bosonic field is not compactified and massless, we must compare the continuum limit of eq. (
6.2
) with the regime
\eta\rightarrow\infty
of the CFT results computed in section
4
and section
5
.
The Hamiltonian eq. (
6.2
) can be diagonalized by introducing the creation and annihilation operators
a_{k},a_{k}^{\dagger}
and
b_{k},b_{k}^{\dagger}
, satisfying
[a_{k},a_{k^{\prime}}^{\dagger}]=\delta_{kk^{\prime}}
and
[b_{k},b_{k^{\prime}}^{\dagger}]=\delta_{kk^{\prime}}
. In terms of these operators, the Hamiltonian eq. (
6.2
) is diagonal

H_{CHC}=\sum_{k=0}^{L-1}\epsilon_{k}(a_{k}^{\dagger}a_{k}+b_{k}^{\dagger}b_{k}),\quad\epsilon_{k}=\sqrt{\mathbf{\omega}^{2}+\frac{4K}{M}\sin^{2}\left(\frac{\pi k}{L}\right)}.

(6.3)

While the
U(1)
charge is

Q=\sum_{k=0}^{L-1}\epsilon_{k}(a_{k}^{\dagger}a_{k}-b_{k}^{\dagger}b_{k}).

(6.4)

The conserved charge is local and can also be written in the position space and for a given subsystem
A
reads

Q_{A}=\sum_{j\in A}\epsilon_{k}(a_{j}^{\dagger}a_{j}-b_{j}^{\dagger}b_{j}).

(6.5)

The charged Rényi entropies of any subsystem
A

l
lattice sites can be computed from these correlators. Firstly, we have to consider the correlation matrices
\mathbf{Q}_{A}
and
\mathbf{P}_{A}
obtained by restricting the the indices of the correlation matrices
\mathbf{Q}
and
\mathbf{P}
to the sites belonging to

. Then find the eigenvalues of the

\mathbf{Q}_{A}\mathbf{P}_{A}
which are denoted by
\{\sigma_{1}^{2},\cdots,\sigma_{l}^{2}\}
. We also introduce the basis
\ket{\mathbf{n}}\equiv\otimes_{k=1}^{l}\ket{n_{k}}
, defined by products of Fock states of the number operator in the subsystem
A

\rho_{A}=\sum_{\mathbf{n}}\prod_{k=1}^{l}\frac{1}{\sigma_{k}+1/2}\left(\frac{\sigma_{k}-1/2}{\sigma_{k}+1/2}\right)^{n_{k}}\ket{\mathbf{n}}\bra{\mathbf{n}}.

(6.8)

Finally, the charged moments for real harmonic chain are given by

\begin{split}{\textrm{Tr}}\rho_{A}^{n}e^{\mathrm{i}\mu N_{A}}&=\sum_{\mathbf{n}}\prod_{k=1}^{l}\left[\frac{1}{\sigma_{k}+1/2}\left(\frac{\sigma_{k}-1/2}{\sigma_{k}+1/2}\right)^{n_{k}}\right]^{n}e^{\mathrm{i}n_{k}\mu}\\
&=\prod_{k=1}^{l}\left[\left(\sigma_{k}+\frac{1}{2}\right)^{n}-e^{\mathrm{i}\mu}\left(\sigma_{k}-\frac{1}{2}\right)^{n}\right]^{-1}.\end{split}

(6.9)

Therefore the charged moments of RDM of an arbitrary subsystem

\begin{split}&Z_{n}(\mu)=|{\textrm{Tr}}\rho_{A}^{n}e^{\mathrm{i}\mu N_{A}}|^{2}\\
&=\prod_{k=1}^{l}\left[\left(\sigma_{k}+\frac{1}{2}\right)^{n}-e^{\mathrm{i}\mu}\left(\sigma_{k}-\frac{1}{2}\right)^{n}\right]^{-1}\left[\left(\sigma_{k}+\frac{1}{2}\right)^{n}-e^{-\mathrm{i}\mu}\left(\sigma_{k}-\frac{1}{2}\right)^{n}\right]^{-1}.\end{split}

(6.10)

7
Conclusion and discussion

In this paper, we consider the charged moments of reduced density matrices of two disjoint regions in 1+1 dimensional compact free boson CFT. We discuss two important regimes of the scaling function, one is the small
x
regime, which will be useful to study the conformal block of the fluxed twist fields. The other region is the decompactification regime, in this regime, the bosonic fields become non-compact, and will be convenient for numerical checks. We also compute the charged Rényi negativity.
We test our analytic results against exact numerical computations in the complex harmonic chain, finding perfect agreement.

We must mention we just obtained the charged moments and the charged Rényi negativity, it would be very interesting to apply Fourier transformation and analytic continue our results to obtain the corresponding symmetry resolved entanglement entropy and charge imbalance resolved entanglement negativity.

It would be also interesting the further check our analytic formula by considering the conformal block expansion of the four-point correlation function of fluxed twist fields and applying the Zamolodchikov recursion formula
[
49
,
50
]
for each conformal block. In this method, each term in the conformal block expansion can be analytically continued and this approach can provide a good approximation for the symmetry resolved entanglement entropy and charge imbalance resolved entanglement negativity.

In this paper, we focus on the charged moments and charged Rényi negativity of complex boson with
U(1)
symmetry. In 1+1 dimensional, for fermions with
U(1)
symmetry
\psi_{k}\rightarrow e^{\mathrm{i}\mu}\psi_{k}
, the corresponding fluxed twist field admits a bosonisation formula. We can write the complex fermionic field as
\psi_{k}\sim e^{\mathrm{i}\varphi_{k}}
, then the fermion twist field is given by the vertex operator
\mathcal{T}_{n,k,\mu}=e^{\mathrm{i}(\frac{k}{n}+\frac{\mu}{2\pi n})\varphi_{k}}
, which have a different scaling dimension
\Delta_{n,k,\mu}=\left(\frac{k}{n}+\frac{\mu}{2\pi n}\right)^{2}
. This can be understood, since via bosonisation of
U(1)

complex fermions, the corresponding bosons transform by translation, and should instead satisfy boundary condition
\varphi_{k}(e^{2\pi\mathrm{i}}z)=\varphi_{k}(z)+\mu
.

Appendix A
Correlation function of fluxed twist fields

In this section, we give some detail of the calculation of four point correlation functions of the
U(1)
twist fields
\mathcal{T}_{n,k,\mu}
. We will call it generally
\mathcal{T}_{a}
, where
a=\frac{k}{n}+\frac{\mu}{2\pi n}
with
0<a<1
. This problem is already encountered in the theory of orbifold CFT
[
48
]
. Here we can just borrow the results with slightly change. However, for the reader’s convenience we briefly review this method here.

Let us consider a complex scalar field
X(z,\bar{z})
defined on a Riemann sphere. The action is given by

\mathcal{S}[X,\bar{X}]=\frac{1}{4\pi}\int d^{2}z(\partial X\bar{\partial}\bar{X}+\bar{\partial}X\partial\bar{X})

(A.1)

If the field
\mathcal{T}_{n,\mu,k}
inserted at the origin, then we have

X(e^{2\pi\mathrm{i}}z,e^{-2\pi\mathrm{i}}\bar{z})=e^{2\pi\mathrm{i}a}X(z,\bar{z})+\lambda,

(A.2)

where
\lambda\in\Lambda
. Let us now split the field
X
into a classical piece
X_{\mathrm{cl}}
and a quantum fluctuation
X_{\mathrm{qu}}
as described in section
4
. The classical field should behave the same as the full field

X_{\mathrm{cl}}(e^{2\pi\mathrm{i}}z,e^{-2\pi\mathrm{i}}\bar{z})=e^{2\pi\mathrm{i}a}X_{\mathrm{cl}}(z,\bar{z})+\lambda,

(A.3)

which implies

X_{\mathrm{qu}}(e^{2\pi\mathrm{i}}z,e^{-2\pi\mathrm{i}}\bar{z})=e^{2\pi\mathrm{i}a}X_{\mathrm{qu}}(z,\bar{z}),

(A.4)

In order to determine the quantum Green functions in the presence of the twist fields and classical solutions, one needs some global information that can be obtained from the monodromy conditions for transporting
X_{\mathrm{qu}}
and
X_{\mathrm{cl}}
around collections of twist fields that have net twist zero. Such paths
\mathcal{C}_{i}
are called closed loops. For any closed loop
\mathcal{C}_{i}
, we must have

\Delta_{\mathcal{C}_{i}}X_{\mathrm{qu}}=\oint_{\mathcal{C}_{i}}dz~\partial X_{\mathrm{qu}}+\oint_{\mathcal{C}_{i}}d\bar{z}~\bar{\partial}X_{\mathrm{qu}}=0

(A.5)

and

\Delta_{\mathcal{C}_{i}}X_{\mathrm{cl}}=\oint_{\mathcal{C}_{i}}dz~\partial X_{\mathrm{cl}}+\oint_{\mathcal{C}_{i}}d\bar{z}~\bar{\partial}X_{\mathrm{cl}}=\lambda,

(A.6)

where now for our case
\lambda\in(1-e^{2\pi\mathrm{i}a})\Lambda
.

Now let us consider the following four-point function of the twist field
\mathcal{T}_{a}

Z=\langle\mathcal{T}_{a}(z_{1},\bar{z}_{1})\tilde{\mathcal{T}}_{a}(z_{2},\bar{z}_{2})\mathcal{T}_{a}(z_{3},\bar{z}_{3})\tilde{\mathcal{T}}_{a}(z_{4},\bar{z}_{4})\rangle=\int[dX][d\bar{X}]e^{-\mathcal{S}[X,\bar{X}]}=Z_{\mathrm{qu}}\sum_{X_{\mathrm{cl}}}e^{-\mathcal{S}[X_{\mathrm{cl}},\bar{X}_{\mathrm{cl}}]}.

(A.7)

We can first compute the Green function in the presence of four twist fields

g(z,w;z_{i})=\frac{\langle-\frac{1}{2}\partial_{z}X\partial_{w}\bar{X}\mathcal{T}_{a}(z_{1})\tilde{\mathcal{T}}_{a}(z_{2})\mathcal{T}_{a}(z_{3})\tilde{\mathcal{T}}_{a}(z_{4})\rangle}{\langle\mathcal{T}_{a}(z_{1})\tilde{\mathcal{T}}_{a}(z_{2})\mathcal{T}_{a}(z_{3})\tilde{\mathcal{T}}_{a}(z_{4})\rangle}.

(A.8)

This Green function should obey the asymptotic conditions:
g(z,w;z_{i})\sim(z-w)^{-2}
as
z\rightarrow w
and for
z\rightarrow z_{i}
we have

g(z,w;z_{i})\sim(z-z_{j})^{-a}
when
j=1,3
and
g(z,w;z_{i})\sim(z-z_{j})^{-(1-a)}
when
j=2,4
.
The holomorphic fields for the cut
z
-plane in this case are:

\begin{split}\partial X(z)\equiv\omega_{a}(z)=[(z-z_{1})(z-z_{3})]^{-a}[(z-z_{2})(z-z_{4})]^{-(1-a)},\\
\partial\bar{X}(z)\equiv\omega_{1-a}(z)=[(z-z_{1})(z-z_{3})]^{-(1-a)}[(z-z_{2})(z-z_{4})]^{-a}.\end{split}

(A.9)

Then the unique form of the Green function with the desired properties reads

\begin{split}g(z,w;z_{i})=\omega_{a}(z)\omega_{1-a}&(w)\Big[a\frac{(z-z_{1})(z-z_{3})(w-z_{2})(w-z_{4})}{(z-w)^{2}}\\
&+(1-a)\frac{(z-z_{2})(z-z_{4})(w-z_{1})(w-z_{3})}{(z-w)^{2}}+A(z_{i},\bar{z}_{i})\Big].\end{split}

(A.10)

We can find the expectation value of the stress tensor
T=-\frac{1}{2}:\partial X\partial\bar{X}:
in the presence of twist fields by taking
w\rightarrow z
of
g(z,w;z_{i})
and subtracting the diverging pieces. We have

\begin{split}&\frac{\langle T(z)\mathcal{T}_{a}(z_{1})\tilde{\mathcal{T}}_{a}(z_{2})\mathcal{T}_{a}(z_{3})\tilde{\mathcal{T}}_{a}(z_{4})\rangle}{\langle\mathcal{T}_{a}(z_{1})\tilde{\mathcal{T}}_{a}(z_{2})\mathcal{T}_{a}(z_{3})\tilde{\mathcal{T}}_{a}(z_{4})\rangle}=\lim_{w\rightarrow z}(g(z,w;z_{i})-(z-w)^{-2})\\
&=\frac{1}{2}a(1-a)\left(\frac{1}{z-z_{1}}+\frac{1}{z-z_{3}}-\frac{1}{z-z_{2}}-\frac{1}{z-z_{4}}\right)^{2}+\frac{A}{(z-z_{1})(z-z_{2})(z-z_{3})(z-z_{4})}.\end{split}

(A.11)

From the operator product expansion

T(z)\tilde{\mathcal{T}}_{a}(z_{2})\sim\frac{h_{a}\tilde{\mathcal{T}}_{a}(z_{2})}{(z-z_{2})^{2}}+\frac{\partial_{z_{2}}\tilde{\mathcal{T}}_{a}(z_{2})}{z-z_{2}},

(A.12)

it’s easy to know that the conformal dimensions of the twist fields
\tilde{\mathcal{T}}_{a}
(and also of
\mathcal{T}_{a}
) are

h_{a}=\bar{h}_{a}=\frac{1}{2}a(1-a)=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{k}{n}+\frac{\mu}{2\pi n}\right)\left(1-\frac{k}{n}-\frac{\mu}{2\pi n}\right)

(A.13)

and the scaling dimension is

\Delta_{a}=h_{a}+\bar{h}_{a}=\left(\frac{k}{n}+\frac{\mu}{2\pi n}\right)\left(1-\frac{k}{n}-\frac{\mu}{2\pi n}\right).

(A.14)

The partition function satisfies the following differential equation
(z_{ij}\equiv z_{i}-z_{j})

\partial_{z_{2}}\ln Z_{\mathrm{qu}}(z_{i},\bar{z}_{i})=-2h_{a}\left(\frac{1}{z_{21}}+\frac{1}{z_{23}}-\frac{1}{z_{24}}\right)+\frac{A(z_{i},\bar{z}_{i})}{z_{21}z_{23}z_{24}}.

(A.15)

It’s convenient to use
SL(2,\mathbb{C})
symmetry
z\rightarrow\frac{(z_{1}-z)(z_{3}-z_{4})}{(z_{1}-z_{3})(z-z_{4})}
to fix the locations of three of the four twist operators to
z_{1}=0,z_{2}=x\equiv\frac{z_{12}z_{34}}{z_{13}z_{24}},z_{3}=1,z_{4}\rightarrow\infty
.

\partial_{x}\ln Z_{\mathrm{qu}}(x,\bar{x})=-2h_{a}\left(\frac{1}{x}-\frac{1}{1-x}\right)-\frac{A(x,\bar{x})}{x(1-x)}.

(A.16)

We can use the global monodromy conditions
A.5
to determine
A(x,\bar{x})
. Before doing this, we must introduce the auxiliary correlation function

h(\bar{z},w;z_{i})=\frac{\langle-\frac{1}{2}\partial_{\bar{z}}X\partial_{w}\bar{X}\mathcal{T}_{a}(z_{1})\tilde{\mathcal{T}}_{a}(z_{2})\mathcal{T}_{a}(z_{3})\tilde{\mathcal{T}}_{a}(z_{4})\rangle}{\langle\mathcal{T}_{a}(z_{1})\tilde{\mathcal{T}}_{a}(z_{2})\mathcal{T}_{a}(z_{3})\tilde{\mathcal{T}}_{a}(z_{4})\rangle}=B(z_{i},\bar{z}_{i})\bar{\omega}_{1-a}(\bar{z})\omega_{1-a}(w),

(A.17)

which is determined up to the constant factor
B(z_{i},\bar{z}_{i})
in the same way as
g
was. Then we have

\oint_{\mathcal{C}_{i}}dz~g(z,w)+\oint_{\mathcal{C}_{i}}d\bar{z}~h(\bar{z},w)=0.

(A.18)

Dividing by
\omega_{1-a}(w)
and letting
w\rightarrow\infty

A\oint_{\mathcal{C}_{i}}dz~\omega_{a}+B\oint_{\mathcal{C}_{i}}d\bar{z}~\bar{\omega}_{1-a}=-(1-a)\oint_{\mathcal{C}_{i}}dz~(z-x)\omega_{a}.

(A.19)

All the contour integrals appeared in the above equation can be expressed in terms of the hypergeometric function
F(x)\equiv{{}_{2}F_{1}}(a,1-a,1,x)
and its derivative

\begin{split}\oint_{\mathcal{C}_{1}}dz~\omega_{a}=2\pi\mathrm{i}e^{-\pi\mathrm{i}a}F(x),\qquad\oint_{\mathcal{C}_{2}}dz~\omega_{a}=2\pi\mathrm{i}F(x),\\
\oint_{\mathcal{C}_{1}}d\bar{z}~\bar{\omega}_{1-a}=2\pi\mathrm{i}e^{-\pi\mathrm{i}a}\bar{F}(\bar{x}),\qquad\oint_{\mathcal{C}_{2}}d\bar{z}~\bar{\omega}_{1-a}=-2\pi\mathrm{i}\bar{F}(1-\bar{x}),\\
-(1-a)\oint_{\mathcal{C}_{1}}dz~(z-x)\omega_{a}=2\pi\mathrm{i}e^{-\pi\mathrm{i}a}x(1-x)\frac{dF(x)}{dx},\\
-(1-a)\oint_{\mathcal{C}_{2}}dz~(z-x)\omega_{a}=2\pi\mathrm{i}x(1-x)\frac{dF(x)}{dx}.\end{split}

(A.20)

Solving eq. (
A.19
) for
A

A(x,\bar{x})=x(1-x)\partial_{x}\ln I(x,\bar{x}),\quad I(x,\bar{x})=F(x)\bar{F}(1-\bar{x})+\bar{F}(\bar{x})F(1-x).

(A.21)

Then

Z_{\mathrm{qu}}(x,\bar{x})=\frac{\mathrm{const}}{|x(1-x)|^{2\Delta_{a}}}\frac{1}{I(x,\bar{x})}.

(A.22)

Then to compute the classical action, we need to find properly normalized classical solutions
X_{\mathrm{cl}}(z,\bar{z})
and
X_{\mathrm{cl}}(z,\bar{z})
. From the equations of motion, it’s easily sees that
\partial_{z}X_{\mathrm{cl}}
and
\partial_{z}\bar{X}_{\mathrm{cl}}

are holomorphic while
\partial_{\bar{z}}X_{\mathrm{cl}}
and
\partial_{\bar{z}}\bar{X}_{\mathrm{cl}}
are antiholomorphic

\begin{split}&\partial X_{\mathrm{cl}}(z)=\mathfrak{a}~\omega_{a}(z),\quad\bar{\partial}X_{\mathrm{cl}}(\bar{z})=\mathfrak{b}~\bar{\omega}_{1-a}(\bar{z}),\\
&\partial\bar{X}_{\mathrm{cl}}(z)=\tilde{\mathfrak{a}}~\omega_{a}(z),\quad\bar{\partial}\bar{X}_{\mathrm{cl}}(\bar{z})=\tilde{\mathfrak{b}}~\bar{\omega}_{a}(\bar{z}).\end{split}

(A.23)

We can first construct two classical solutions which have simple global monodromy:

\Delta_{\mathcal{C}_{i}}X_{\mathrm{cl},j}=\Delta_{\mathcal{C}_{i}}\bar{X}_{\mathrm{cl},j}=2\pi\delta_{ij},\quad i,j=1,2

(A.24)

Let
\mathfrak{a}_{i},\mathfrak{b}_{i},\tilde{\mathfrak{a}}_{i}
and
\tilde{\mathfrak{b}}_{i}
be the coefficients for
X_{\mathrm{cl},i}
and

Appendix B
Normalisation of
\mathcal{F}_{n}(\mu,x)

The Siegel theta function
\Theta(\mathbf{0}|M)
for any symmetric
n\times n
matrix
M
with positive imaginary part can be rewritten as

\begin{split}\Theta(\mathbf{0}|M)=\sum_{\mathbf{m}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}e^{\mathrm{i}\pi\mathbf{m}^{\mathrm{t}}\cdot M\cdot\mathbf{m}}=\int d^{n}\mathbf{x}\sum_{\mathbf{m}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\delta^{n}(\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{m})e^{\mathrm{i}\pi\mathbf{x}^{\mathrm{t}}\cdot M\cdot\mathbf{x}}.\end{split}

(B.1)

Using Poisson resummation formula

\sum_{\mathbf{m}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\delta^{n}(\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{m})=\sum_{\mathbf{p}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}e^{2\pi\mathrm{i}\mathbf{p}\cdot\mathbf{x}},

(B.2)

we have

\begin{split}\Theta(\mathbf{0}|M)=\sum_{\mathbf{p}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\int d^{n}\mathbf{x}~e^{\mathrm{i}\pi\mathbf{x}^{\mathrm{t}}\cdot M\cdot\mathbf{x}+2\pi\mathrm{i}\mathbf{p}\cdot\mathbf{x}}=\sum_{\mathbf{p}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\det(-\mathrm{i}M)}}e^{-\mathrm{i}\pi\mathbf{p}^{\mathrm{t}}\cdot M^{-1}\cdot\mathbf{p}}=\frac{\Theta(\mathbf{0}|-M^{-1})}{\sqrt{\det(-\mathrm{i}M)}}.\end{split}

(B.3)

Now applying this formula to our problem. Firstly, we have

\eta\tilde{\Omega}=-A(\Omega/\eta)^{-1}A,

(B.4)

where
A=U^{\dagger}HU
with
H={\textrm{diag}}(\cdots,(-\omega_{p}\tilde{\omega}_{p})^{1/2},\cdots)
.
Then by setting
M=\eta\tilde{\Omega}
in eq. (
B.3
), we find

\Theta(\mathbf{0}|\eta\tilde{\Omega})=\frac{\sqrt{\det(\Omega/(\mathrm{i}\eta))}}{\det A}\Theta(\mathbf{0}|A^{-1}(\Omega/\eta)A^{-1}).

(B.5)

We also find

\frac{\det(\Omega/(\mathrm{i}\eta))}{(\det A)^{2}}=\prod_{k=1}^{n}\frac{-\mathrm{i}\omega_{k}}{(-\eta\omega_{k}\tilde{\omega}_{k})}=\prod_{k=1}^{n}(-\mathrm{i}\eta\tilde{\omega}_{k})^{-1}

(B.6)

and

[A(\Omega/\eta)^{-1}A]_{rs}=\frac{\eta}{n}\sum_{p=1}^{n}\tilde{\omega}_{p}^{-1}\cos[\frac{2\pi p}{n}(r-s)]

(B.7)

In the limit
x\rightarrow 0
, we have
F_{a}(x)\rightarrow 1
,
\beta_{a}(x)\rightarrow\infty
, thus
\Theta(\mathbf{0}|\eta\Omega)\rightarrow 1
. Then

\begin{split}&\lim_{x\rightarrow 0}\frac{[\Theta(\mathbf{0}|\eta\Omega)\Theta(\mathbf{0}|\eta\tilde{\Omega})]^{2}}{\prod_{k=0}^{n-1}\beta_{a}[F_{a}(x)]^{2}}=\lim_{x\rightarrow 0}\frac{\det(\Omega/(\mathrm{i}\eta))}{(\det A)^{2}\prod_{k=0}^{n-1}\beta_{\frac{k}{n}+\frac{\mu}{2\pi n}}}=\eta^{-n}\lim_{x\rightarrow 0}\prod_{p=1}^{n}(-\mathrm{i}\tilde{\omega}_{p}\beta_{\frac{p-1}{n}+\frac{\mu}{2\pi n}})^{-1}\\
&=\eta^{-n}\Big[2\sin\left(\frac{\mu}{2n}\right)\Big]^{-1}\prod_{p=1}^{n-1}\left[\sin\Big(\frac{\pi p}{n}+\frac{\mu}{2n}\Big)+\sin\Big(\frac{\pi p}{n}-\frac{\mu}{2n}\Big)\right]^{-1}\\
&=\frac{1}{2n\eta^{n}}\Big[\sin\left(\frac{\mu}{2n}\right)\Big]^{-1}\Big[\cos\Big(\frac{\mu}{2n}\Big)\Big]^{1-n},\end{split}

(B.8)

where we have used the identity

\prod_{p=1}^{n-1}\left[\sin\Big(\frac{\pi p}{n}+\frac{\mu}{2n}\Big)+\sin\Big(\frac{\pi p}{n}-\frac{\mu}{2n}\Big)\right]=n\Big[\cos\Big(\frac{\mu}{2n}\Big)\Big]^{n-1}.

(B.9)

Thus, the normalisation constant
s_{n,\mu}
of
\mathcal{F}_{n}(\mu,x)
is obtained.

A conjugacy class counting in Teichmüller space
Pouya Honaryar

Abstract
. Let γ \gamma be a pseudo-Anosov homeomorphism and X X an element of the Teichmüller space of a genus g g surface. In this paper, we find asymptotics for the number of pseudo-Anosov homeomorphisms that are conjugate to γ \gamma and the axis of their action on Teichmüller space intersects the ball of radius R R centered at X X , as R R tends to infinity.



1.
Introduction

1.1.
Statement of results

In
[
Mar04
]
, Margulis obtained asymptotics for
the volume growth and orbit counting for balls of large radius, in the setting of manifolds with negative curvature.
Similar asymptotics were obtained in
[
ABEM12
]
for the Teichmüller space. To state their results,
let
\mathcal{T}_{g}
be the Teichmüller space of
S
, a surface of genus
g
, and denote the mapping class group of
S
by
\Gamma
. Given
X,Y\in\mathcal{T}_{g}
, let
B(X,R)
be the ball of radius
R
centered at
X
, where the distance here is measured with respect to the Teichmüller metric. Denoting the orbit of
Y
under the action of
\Gamma
by
\Gamma\cdot Y
, Theorem 1.2 of
[
ABEM12
]
gives

|\Gamma\cdot Y\cap B(X,R)|\sim\frac{\Lambda^{2}}{h\Vol(\mathcal{M}_{g})}e^{hR}\,\,\,\,\,\,\text{ as }\,\,\,\,\,\,R\rightarrow\infty.

Here,
h=6g-6
is the entropy of the Teichmüller geodesic flow with respect to Masur-Veech measure and
\Lambda
is the Hubbard-Masur constant
[
ABEM12
,
Dum15
]
. The term
\Vol(\mathcal{M}_{g})
is the normalized volume of the moduli space
\mathcal{M}_{g}
as explained at the end of Section 2.2 of
[
ABEM12
]
.
The cardinality of a finite set
S
is denoted by
|S|
and
A(R)
is said to be asymptotic to
B(R)
, written
A(R)\sim B(R)
, if
A(R)/B(R)\rightarrow 1
as
R\rightarrow\infty
.
Theorem 1.3 of of the same paper gives the following asymptotics for the volume of
B(X,R)
:

\Vol(B(X,R))\sim\frac{\Lambda^{2}}{h}e^{hR}\,\,\,\,\,\,\text{ as }\,\,\,\,\,\,R\rightarrow\infty.

Now fix
\gamma\in\Gamma
to be a pseudo-Anosov homeomorphism and let
\mathcal{L}_{\gamma}
be the axis of its action on Teichmüller space, namely, the unique geodesic that is kept fixed by this action.
The cyclic group generated by
\gamma
, denoted by
\langle\gamma\rangle
, acts on
\mathcal{T}_{g}

properly discontinuously, hence we can form the quotient to be the cylinder

C_{\gamma}=\langle\gamma\rangle\backslash\mathcal{T}_{g}
.
The elements of
C_{\gamma}
are of the form
[Y]=\langle\gamma\rangle.Y
for
Y\in\mathcal{T}_{g}
.
Since the action of
\gamma
on
\mathcal{L}_{\gamma}
is by translation, the quotient
\bar{\mathcal{L}}_{\gamma}=\langle\gamma\rangle\backslash\mathcal{L}
is a closed geodesic in
\mathcal{C}_{\gamma}
. Define

B(\bar{\mathcal{L}}_{\gamma},R)=\{[Y]\in\mathcal{C}_{\gamma}:d([Y],\bar{\mathcal{L}}_{\gamma})\leq R\},

where the distance in
\mathcal{C}_{\gamma}
is the one induced by the Teichmüller distance on its cover
\mathcal{T}_{g}
. Define the
\Gamma
–orbit of
[X]\in\mathcal{C}_{g}
to be

\Gamma\cdot[X]=\{[gX]:g\in\Gamma\}.

The goal of this paper is to establish orbit counting and volume asymptotics, similar to the ones obtained in
[
ABEM12
]
, for
B(\bar{\mathcal{L}}_{\gamma},R)
instead of
B(X,R)
. To state these results, we need a few definitions.
Define

B_{\Ext}(\mathcal{L}_{\gamma})=\{\zeta\in\mathcal{MF}:\inf_{X\in\mathcal{L}_{\gamma}}\ext(\zeta,X)\leq 1\}.

The action of
\langle\gamma\rangle
on
B_{\ext}(\mathcal{L}_{\gamma})
is proper and discontinuous, given we remove the two endpoints of
{\mathcal{L}}_{\gamma}
from the set, hence we can form the quotient to be

\mathcal{C}_{\gamma,\ext}=\langle\gamma\rangle\backslash B_{\Ext}({\mathcal{L}}_{\gamma}).

The Thurston measure on
\mathcal{MF}
induces a measure on
\mathcal{C}_{\gamma,\ext}
, which we denote by
\nu
as well.
The orbit counting asymptotics is given by:

Theorem A
.

Let
\gamma
and
\bar{\mathcal{L}}_{\gamma}
be as above and
[X]\in\mathcal{C}_{\gamma}
. Then as
R\to\infty
,

(1)

\displaystyle|\Gamma\cdot[X]\cap B(\bar{\mathcal{L}}_{\gamma},R)|\sim\frac{\Lambda^{2}}{h\Vol(\mathcal{M}_{g})}\nu(\mathcal{C}_{\gamma,\ext})e^{hR}.

Note that
\Gamma\cdot[X]\cap B(\bar{\mathcal{L}}_{\gamma},R)
is in one-to-one correspondence with the mapping class group translations
g\mathcal{L}_{\gamma}
of
\mathcal{L}_{\gamma}
that intersects
B(X,R)
. This in turn is in one-to-one correspondence with the conjugates of
\gamma
whose axis intersects
B(X,R)
. Therefore, we have the following:

Corollary B
.

Let
\gamma
be a pseudo-Anosov homeomorphism and
X\in\mathcal{T}_{g}
. Then as
R\to\infty
,

|\{\gamma^{\prime}\in\Gamma:\gamma^{\prime}\text{ is conjugate to $\gamma$ and }\mathcal{L}_{\gamma^{\prime}}\cap B(X,R)\neq\varnothing\}|\sim\frac{\Lambda^{2}}{h\Vol(\mathcal{M}_{g})}\nu(\mathcal{C}_{\gamma,\ext})e^{hR}.

The volume asymptotics is given by:

Theorem C
.

Let
\gamma
and
\bar{\mathcal{L}}_{\gamma}
be as above. Then as
R\to\infty
,

\Vol(B(\bar{\mathcal{L}}_{\gamma},R))\sim\frac{\Lambda^{2}}{h}\nu(\mathcal{C}_{\gamma,\ext})e^{hR}.

1.2.
Remarks and the relation to other works

If
\Sigma
is a surface of constant negative curvature
-1
and
\Gamma
its fundamental group, then Theorem 2.5 of
[
EM93
]
gives

|B(\bar{\mathcal{L}}_{\gamma},R)\cap\Gamma\cdot[x]|\sim\frac{\operatorname{Length}(\bar{\mathcal{L}}_{\gamma})}{\operatorname{Area}(\Sigma)}e^{R}\,\,\,\,\,\,\text{ as }\,\,\,\,\,\,R\to\infty,

where the terms in the above expression are defined in the same way as before. A calculation in hyperbolic metric shows Theorem
C
in this setting, namely, as
R\rightarrow\infty
,

\operatorname{Area}(B(\bar{\mathcal{L}}_{\gamma},R))\sim\operatorname{Length}(\bar{\mathcal{L}}_{\gamma})e^{R}.

For
M
a compact manifold of (variable) negative curvature, we can define
\Gamma,\,\gamma,\,\mathcal{L}_{\gamma}
and
\bar{\mathcal{L}}_{\gamma}
similarly. The asymptotics for
|\Gamma\cdot[x]\cap B(\bar{\mathcal{L}}_{\gamma},R)|
can be obtained as a special case of
common perpendicular counting
.
To explain this, let
\mathcal{L}^{\prime}_{\gamma}
and
x^{\prime}
be the images of
\mathcal{L}_{\gamma}
and
x
under the covering map
\Pi\colon\thinspace\widetilde{M}\to M
.
Then
\Gamma\cdot[x]\cap B(\bar{\mathcal{L}}_{\gamma},R)
is in one-to-one correspondence with
\operatorname{Perp}(x^{\prime},\mathcal{L}^{\prime}_{\gamma},R)
, the perpendiculars from
x^{\prime}
to
\mathcal{L}^{\prime}_{\gamma}
of length less than
R
,
where such a perpendicular is defined as a locally geodesic path that starts from
x^{\prime}
and arrives perpendicularly at
\mathcal{L}^{\prime}_{\gamma}
.
It follwos from Theorem 1 of
[
PP17
]
that for some constant
c_{\gamma}>0
,

|\Gamma\cdot[x]\cap B(\bar{\mathcal{L}}_{\gamma},R)|\sim c_{\gamma}e^{\delta R}\,\,\,\,\,\,\text{ as }R\to\infty.

Here,
\delta
is the topological entropy of the geodesic flow on
T^{1}M
, the unit tangent bundle of
M
. Moreover,
under some additional conditions, an exponentially small error term is obtained for the above asymptotics. (See Theorem 3 of the same paper for the precise statement.)

As a final remark, let us mention that our methods for proving Theorem
A
are quite flexible.
In particular, Theorem
A
can be proved for an arbitrary compact set
\mathcal{K}_{\gamma}\subset\mathcal{C}_{\gamma}
replacing
\bar{\mathcal{L}}_{\gamma}
, and the proof is word for word the same, given we change an
\epsilon
–net of
\bar{\mathcal{L}}_{\gamma}
by an
\epsilon
–cover of
\mathcal{K}_{\gamma}
. (see Section
1.3
.)
Volume asymptotics in this case can be obtained in the same way as we obtained Theorem

from Theorem

.

The outline of the proof

Theorem

, proved at the end of Section

[

]
.
Hence the main task is to prove Theorem

. Let
\gamma

\mathcal{L}
be as in Section
1.1
and fix a point
P\in\mathcal{T}_{g}
. By definition,
\Gamma\cdot[P]\cap B(\bar{\mathcal{L}}_{\gamma},R)

(2)

\displaystyle\langle\gamma\rangle\backslash(\Gamma\cdot P\cap B(\mathcal{L}_{\gamma},R))=\{\langle\gamma\rangle.X:X\in\Gamma\cdot P\cap B(\mathcal{L}_{\gamma},R)\},

where

Note that taking the quotient by

–invariant.

Fix a point

be an

Translating this net by the powers

\gamma
–invariant
\epsilon
–net
(...,X_{-1},X_{0},X_{1},...)
of
\mathcal{L}_{\gamma}
. Define the closest point map

\mathcal{P}\colon\thinspace\mathcal{PMF}\to\mathbb{Z}\,\,\,\,\,\,by\,\,\,\,\,\,\mathcal{P}[\zeta]=i\,\text{ if }\,\ext(\zeta,X_{i})=\inf_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\ext(\zeta,X_{j}).

Setting
\mathcal{A}_{i}=\mathcal{P}^{-1}(i)
, we obtain a
\gamma
–invariant partition of
\mathcal{PMF}
.
Let
S(X_{i},\mathcal{A}_{i},R)
be the sector of radius

centered at

and observing

1.4.
A formula for the derivative of extremal length

We end this introduction by stating a formula that we obtained in Section
3
in the course of proving Proposition
3.1
.
For
X\in\mathcal{T}_{g}
and
\zeta\in\mathcal{MF}
, denote the extremal length of
\zeta
in
X
by
\ext(\zeta,X)
.
Fixing
\zeta
, we can consider
E_{\zeta}=\ext(\zeta,\centerdot)
as a function from
\mathcal{T}_{g}
to
\mathbb{R}
.
This function is differentiable and its derivative at
X\in\mathcal{T}_{g}
,
d_{X}E_{\zeta}\colon\thinspace T_{X}(\mathcal{T}_{g})\to\mathbb{R}
, is given by the Gardiner’s formula
[
Gar84
]

d_{X}E_{\zeta}(\mu)=2\Re\int_{X}\mu.\mathcal{V}_{X}^{-1}(\zeta),

where
T_{X}(\mathcal{T}_{g})
is the tangent space to
\mathcal{T}_{g}
at
X
,
\mu\in T_{X}(\mathcal{T}_{g})
is a Beltrami differential and the homeomorphism
\mathcal{V}_{X}\colon\thinspace Q(X)\to\mathcal{MF}
is defined by sending a quadratic differential to its vertical measured foliation.

If we fix
X\in\mathcal{T}_{g}
instead, we can define

E_{X}\colon\thinspace\mathcal{MF}\to\mathbb{R}\,\,\,\,\,\,\text{ by }\,\,\,\,\,\,E_{X}(\zeta)=\ext(\zeta,X).

In order to compute the derivative of
E_{X}
we need a differential structure on
\mathcal{MF}
. In general, the manifold
\mathcal{MF}
equipped with train-track charts is only piecewise linear.
However, if
\zeta
is generic, meaning that it does not have a leaf connecting any two singularities and all the singularities are simple, then
\mathcal{MF}
is smooth at
\zeta
(in a sense to be defined at the beginning of Section
3.2
), hence the tangent space at this point to
\mathcal{MF}
,
T_{\zeta}\mathcal{MF}
, is defined. The derivative of
E_{X}
at such a
\zeta
is given by the following theorem: (for the precise statement see Theorem
3.4
.)

Theorem D
.

Fix
X\in\mathcal{T}_{g}
and let
\zeta\in\mathcal{MF}
be a generic measured foliation. Then
E_{X}
is smooth at
\zeta
and there is an
\eta\in T_{\zeta}\mathcal{MF}
such that

d_{\zeta}E_{X}\colon\thinspace T_{\zeta}\mathcal{MF}\to\mathbb{R}\,\,\,\,\,\,\text{ is given by }\,\,\,\,\,\,d_{\zeta}E_{X}(\centerdot)=\omega_{\operatorname{Th}}(\eta,\centerdot),

where
\omega_{\operatorname{Th}}
stands for the Thurston symplectic form (see Section
3.1
for a definition). Moreover,
\eta
can be completely described in certain train track coordinates around
\zeta
.

Notation

For a set
A
and subsets
A_{\delta}
indexed by
\delta\in(0,s)
for some
s>0
, we say
A_{\delta}\uparrow A
as
\delta\downarrow 0
if the follwoing holds:
A_{\delta_{2}}\supseteq A_{\delta_{1}}
for
\delta_{2}<\delta_{1}
and
\bigcup A_{\delta}=A
. Similarly, we say
B_{\delta}\downarrow B
as
\delta\downarrow 0
if
B_{\delta_{2}}\subseteq B_{\delta_{1}}
for
\delta_{2}<\delta_{1}
and
\bigcap B_{\delta}=B
. Finally, for real numbers
a,b,c
we write
a\simeq_{c}b
if
|a-b|<c
.

2.
Background on Teichmüller space

Teichmüller space

Let
S
be a compact surface of genus
g\geq 2
. We denote the Teichmüller space of
S
by
\mathcal{T}_{g}
. This is the space of equivalent classes of orientation preserving homeomorphism
f\colon\thinspace S\to X
, where
X
is a Riemann surface and
f\colon\thinspace S\to X
is said to be equivalent to
g\colon\thinspace S\to Y
if there exists a biholomorphism
h\colon\thinspace X\to Y
such that
g
is isotopic to
h\circ f
. We denote an element
[f\colon\thinspace S\to X]
of
\mathcal{T}_{g}
by
X
and keep the marking in the back of our mind.
The mapping class group (or modular group) of
S
is denoted by
\Gamma
. This is the group of orientation preserving homeomorphisms of
S
up to isotopy.
An element of mapping class group
[\gamma\colon\thinspace S\to S]
acts on
[f\colon\thinspace S\to X]\in\mathcal{T}_{g}
by change of marking, namely
[\gamma].[f]=[f\circ\gamma^{-1}]
.
Taking the quotient of
\mathcal{T}_{g}
by
\Gamma
we obtain the moduli space
\mathcal{M}_{g}=\Gamma\backslash\mathcal{T}_{g}
.

Quadratic differentials

For a Riemann surface
X
, let
Q(X)
be the space of holomorphic quadratic differentials (or quadratic differentials for short) on
X
.
For a
\phi\in Q(X)
, define the norm of
\phi
to be

|\phi|=\int_{X}|\phi(z)||dz|^{2}.

The union of
Q(X)
for
X\in\mathcal{T}_{g}
forms the space of quadratic differentials, denoted by
\mathcal{QT}_{g}
.
More precisely,
\mathcal{QT}_{g}
is the space of equivalent classes

[f\colon\thinspace S\to(X,\phi)]
, where
f
and
X
are as before and
\phi\in Q(X)
.
We denote
[f]
by
(X,\phi)
or just
\phi
.
Sending
(X,\phi)
to
X
gives a projection map
\pi\colon\thinspace\mathcal{QT}_{g}\to\mathcal{T}_{g}
.
The principal domain
\mathcal{P}(1,..,1)\subset\mathcal{QT}_{g}
is defined to be quadratic differentials with only simple zeros.

A flat chart for
(X,\phi)
is a holomorphic chart
\varphi\colon\thinspace U\subset\mathbb{C}\to X
on which the pullback of
\phi
is
dz^{2}
.
The change of coordinates between two flat charts is of the form
z\to\pm z+c
.
For
\phi\in\mathcal{QT}_{g}
and
A\in\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
,
A.\phi
is defined as the unique element
\psi\in\mathcal{QT}_{g}
such that the change of marking
\phi\rightarrow A.\psi
is given by
multiplication by
A
on the corresponding flat charts. With this definition, Teichmüller geodesic flow is given by

g_{t}=\begin{pmatrix}e^{t}&0\\
0&e^{-t}\end{pmatrix}.

The cotangent space at
X\in\mathcal{T}_{g}
is naturally identified with
Q(X)
, hence the norm on
Q(X)
induces a Finsler norm on Teichmüller space. The resulting metric is called the Teichmüller metric, the distance between two points
X,Y\in\mathcal{T}_{g}
is denoted by
d(X,Y)
and the geodesic connecting
X
to
Y
is shown by
[X,Y]
. For
X\in\mathcal{T}_{g}
and
\zeta\in\mathcal{MF}
, there exists a unique
\phi\in Q(X)
such that
\phi
has
\zeta
as its vertical measured foliation (
[
HM79
]
).
For such
X
and
\zeta
, define

[X,\zeta)=\{\pi(g_{t}.\phi),\,0\leq t\leq\infty\}.

Extremal length

Let
\mathcal{V}\colon\thinspace\mathcal{QT}_{g}\to\mathcal{MF}
be the function that sends a quadratic differential to its vertical measured foliation. For
X\in\mathcal{T}_{g}
, the restriction of
\mathcal{V}
to
Q(X)
,
\mathcal{V}_{X}\colon\thinspace Q(X)\to\mathcal{MF}
, is a homeomorphism, and the extremal length of
\zeta\in\mathcal{MF}
at such an
X
can be defined by

\Ext(\zeta,X)=|\mathcal{V}_{X}^{-1}(\zeta)|.

The Busemann functions are defined by

\displaystyle\beta(\zeta,X)=\frac{1}{2}\log\ext(\zeta,X);

\displaystyle\beta(\zeta,X,Y)=\beta(\zeta,Y)-\beta(\zeta,X).

Note that
\beta(\zeta,X,Y)
only depends on
[\zeta]
, hence it can be denoted by
\beta([\zeta],X,Y)
.
Kerschoff inequality states that

\frac{\ext(\zeta,Y)}{\ext(\zeta,X)}\leq e^{2d(X,Y)},

hence taking logarithms we obtain

\beta(\zeta,Y)\leq\beta(\zeta,X)+d(X,Y).

If we think of
\beta(\zeta,X)
as the ”length at infinity” of
[X,\zeta)
, the above can be thought of as the triangle inequality in
\bigtriangleup(\zeta,X,Y)
.

3.
equidistant measured foliations are negligible

3.1.
Background on train tracks

We define a train track to be an embedded
3
–regular graph in
S
such that its vertices are locally modeled on Figure
1
. The vertices of this graph are called
switches
and the edges are called
branches
of the train track.
In the same figure,
a
is called an
incoming
branch and
b,c
are called
outgoing
branches.
A branch is said to be
large
if it is the outgoing branch for both of its endpoints. A
splitting
along the large branch
e
is shown in Figure
2
.
A train track
\tau
is said to be
complete
if all the components of
S-\tau
are cusped triangles.

Figure 1.
A switch

A function
w
from the set of branches of
\tau
to
\mathbb{R}
is called a
weight
if we have
w(a)=w(b)+w(c)
for every switch as in Figure
1
. Let
W(\tau)
be the set of all weights on
\tau
.
A weight
w\in W(\tau)
is said to be
positive
(or a
measure
on the train track
\tau
), denoted by
w>0
, if
w(a)>0
for all the branches
a
of
\tau
. Denote the set of all measures on
\tau
by
W^{+}(\tau)
.

Given
\mu\in W^{+}(\tau)
, we can foliate a rectangular neighborhood of
\tau
according to
\mu
. Shrinking the components of the complement of this neighborhood, we get a measured foliation, denoted by
\mathcal{F}(\tau,\mu)
.
A measured foliation
\zeta
is said to be
carried
by
\tau
if there exists a measure
\mu\in W^{+}(\tau)
such that
\zeta=\mathcal{F}(\tau,\mu)
.

A measured foliation is called
generic
if it has only simple singularities and does not have a leaf connecting any two of its singularities.
Let
\zeta\in\mathcal{MF}
be generic and assume
\tau
is a train track carrying
\zeta
, say,
\zeta=\mathcal{F}(\tau,\mu)
for some
\mu\in W^{+}(\tau)
. Since
\zeta
is generic,
\tau
should be complete, hence
W^{+}(\tau)
is of maximal dimension
6g-6
and

\varphi_{\tau}\colon\thinspace W^{+}(\tau)\to\mathcal{MF}\,\,\,\,\,\,\text{ defined by }\,\,\,\,\,\,\mu_{1}\mapsto\mathcal{F}(\tau,\mu_{1})

gives a chart around
\zeta
.
If the train track
\tau^{\prime}
carries
\zeta
as well, then the change of coordinates
\varphi_{\tau}^{-1}\circ\varphi_{\tau}
is linear in a neighbourhood of
\mu
. This gives
\mathcal{MF}
a linear structure at such a
\zeta
. (see the explanation after Proposition
3.1
.)

For a train track
\tau
, define the antisymmetric pairing

\omega_{\operatorname{Th}}\colon\thinspace W(\tau)\times W(\tau)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
by

(3)

\displaystyle\omega_{\operatorname{Th}}(w_{1},w_{2})=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{v}\det\begin{pmatrix}w_{1}(b_{v})&w_{1}(c_{v})\\
w_{2}(b_{v})&w_{2}(c_{v})\end{pmatrix},

where the sum is over all the switches
v
of
\tau
and at each switch
v
, the incoming branch and outgoing branches are labeled by
a_{v},b_{v},c_{v}
respectively, in such a way that
a_{v}b_{v}c_{v}
is clockwise.
Since
W(\tau)
is a vector space,
T_{\mu}W^{+}(\tau)
is naturally identified with
W(\tau)
for every
\mu\in W^{+}(\tau)
, hence (
3
) gives an antisymmetric form on
W^{+}(\tau)
, denoted by
\omega_{\operatorname{Th}}

4.1.

In this section, we state a few general facts about the geodesics that lie completely in the thick part, where by a geodesic we always mean a bi-infinite geodesic in the Teichmüller space, unless otherwise stated. Denoting the covering map from Teichmüller space to the moduli space by
\Pi\colon\thinspace\mathcal{T}_{g}\to\mathcal{M}_{g}
, for a subset
\mathcal{K}\subset\mathcal{M}_{g}
we define

to be

Definition 4.1
.

Let
\mathcal{K}\subset\mathcal{M}_{g}
be compact. A Teichmüller geodesic
\mathcal{G}
is said to be
\mathcal{K}
–

–thick for some compact

\displaystyle\operatorname{proj}_{\mathcal{G}}X

\displaystyle=\{Y\in\mathcal{G}:d(X,Y)=d(X,\mathcal{G})\};

\displaystyle\operatorname{proj}_{\mathcal{G}}[\zeta]

\displaystyle=\operatorname{proj}_{\mathcal{G}}\zeta=\{Y\in\mathcal{G}:\ext(\zeta,Y)=\ext(\zeta,\mathcal{G})\},

There have been many analogies between the Teichmüller space, equipped with the Teichmüller metric, and a hyperbolic space.

\mathsf{U}^{\delta}_{i}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{K};C,R)=\mathsf{U}^{\delta}_{i}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{K})\cap B(X_{i},R){\smallsetminus}B(X_{i},C).

We claim that

\Pi_{\gamma}\colon\thinspace\mathsf{U}^{\delta}_{i}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{K};C,R)\to\Gamma\cdot[P]\cap B(\bar{\mathcal{L}},R)

Is an injection. To prove this claim, assume
\Pi_{\gamma}(X)=\Pi_{\gamma}(Y)
for
X,Y\in\mathsf{U}^{\delta}_{i}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{K};C,R)
.
This imlies that there exists
k\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
\gamma^{k}X=Y
, so
Y
belongs to the intersection of
\mathsf{U}^{\delta}_{i}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{K};C,R)
with

\gamma^{k}\mathsf{U}^{\delta}_{i}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{K};C,R)=\mathsf{U}^{\delta}_{i+kN}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{K};C,R)
. By the definition of
C
, we have
k=0
, hence
X=Y
. This proves the claim.

A similar argument implies that
\Pi_{\gamma}\left(\mathsf{U}^{\delta}_{i}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{K};C,R)\right)
are disjoint for
0\leq i<N(\mathcal{X})
, so

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{N(\mathcal{X})-1}|\mathsf{U}^{\delta}_{i}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{K};C,R)|\leq|\Gamma\cdot[P]\cap B(\bar{\mathcal{L}},R)|.

Multiplying both sides by
e^{-hR}
and taking
\liminf
as
R\rightarrow\infty
, we get

(10)

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{N(\mathcal{X})-1}s^{\delta}_{i}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{K})

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{R\rightarrow\infty}e^{-hR}|\Gamma\cdot[P]\cap B(\bar{\mathcal{L}},R)|,

where
s^{\delta}_{i}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{K})
is defined by

\displaystyle s^{\delta}_{i}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{K})

\displaystyle=\liminf_{R\rightarrow\infty}e^{-hR}|\mathsf{U}^{\delta}_{i}(\mathcal{K})\cap B(X_{i},R)|

Note that (
10
) is valid for every
\epsilon
–net
\mathcal{X}
, compact set
\mathcal{K}\subset\mathcal{M}_{g}
and
\delta>0
.
Now, fixing
\mathcal{X}
and
\delta
, we let the compact sets
(\mathcal{K}_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathcal{M}_{g}
form an exhaustion of
\mathcal{M}_{g}
. This means that
\mathcal{K}_{n}\subset\mathring{\mathcal{K}}_{n+1}
and
\mathcal{M}_{g}=\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\mathcal{K}_{n}
. Then, by (
6
) and (
7
),

s^{\delta}_{i}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{K}_{n})\uparrow\frac{\Lambda^{2}}{h\Vol(\mathcal{M}_{g})}\nu(\mathcal{U}^{\delta}_{i}(\mathcal{X}))\,\,\,\,\,\,\text{ as }\,\,\,\,\,\,n\to\infty,

so we get

\frac{\Lambda^{2}}{h\Vol(\mathcal{M}_{g})}\sum_{i=0}^{N(\mathcal{X})-1}\nu(\mathcal{U}^{\delta}_{i}(\mathcal{X}))\leq\liminf_{R\rightarrow\infty}e^{-hR}|\Gamma\cdot[P]\cap B(\bar{\mathcal{L}},R)|.

Now, keeping the net
\mathcal{X}
fixed in the above expression and letting
\delta\downarrow 0
, we have

\mathcal{U}^{\delta}_{i}(\mathcal{X})\uparrow\mathring{\mathcal{A}}_{i}(\mathcal{X})
, hence

\nu(\mathcal{U}^{\delta}_{i}(\mathcal{X}))\uparrow\nu(\mathring{\mathcal{A}}_{i}(\mathcal{X}))=\nu(\mathcal{A}_{i}(\mathcal{X}))
. Thus,

\displaystyle\frac{\Lambda^{2}}{h\Vol(\mathcal{M}_{g})}\sum_{i=0}^{N(\mathcal{X})-1}\nu(\mathcal{A}_{i}(\mathcal{X}))\leq\liminf_{R\rightarrow\infty}e^{-hR}|\Gamma\cdot[P]\cap B(\bar{\mathcal{L}},R)|.

Finally, using Lemma
5.2
and making the
\epsilon
–net
\mathcal{X}
finer, i.e., letting
\epsilon\rightarrow 0
, proves the lower bound

\displaystyle\frac{\Lambda^{2}}{h\Vol(\mathcal{M}_{g})}\nu(\mathcal{C}_{\ext,\gamma})\leq\liminf_{R\rightarrow\infty}e^{-hR}|\Gamma\cdot[P]\cap B(\bar{\mathcal{L}},R)|.

∎

Lemma 5.5
.

Let the axis
\mathcal{L}
and the point
P\in\mathcal{T}_{g}
be as before, and assume
\mathcal{X}=(X_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}

\mathcal{K}\subset\mathcal{M}_{g}

\delta>0
, there exists

such that the following holds: if

is such that

Proof.

Let

\inf_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}d(Y,X_{i})

as a function of

, described in Corollary
4.4
.
Let the geodesic from
X_{i_{0}}
to

hit the boundary at
[\zeta]
, i.e.,

. By Proposition
4.11
, there exists

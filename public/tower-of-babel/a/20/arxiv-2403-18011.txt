On AdS 4 deformations of celestial symmetries
and David Skinner

Abstract
Celestial holography has led to the discovery of new symmetry algebras arising from the study of collinear limits of perturbative gravity amplitudes in flat space. We explain from the twistor perspective how a non-vanishing cosmological constant Λ \Lambda naturally modifies the celestial chiral algebra. The cosmological constant deforms the Poisson bracket on twistor space, so the corresponding deformed algebra of Hamiltonians under the new bracket is automatically consistent. This algebra is equivalent to that recently found by Taylor and Zhu. We find a number of variations of the deformed algebra. We give the Noether charges arising from the expression of this algebra as a symmetry of the twistor action for self-dual gravity with cosmological constant.

Twistors for AdS

The twistor space
\mathbb{PT}
of conformally compactified Minkowski space-time
\mathbb{M}
is
\mathbb{CP}^{3}
, often described by homogeneous coordinates
Z^{A}=(\mu^{\dot{\alpha}},\lambda_{\alpha})
defined modulo the
\mathbb{C}^{*}
action
Z^{A}\sim tZ^{A}
for
t\in\mathbb{C}^{*}

L_{x}\cong\mathbb{CP}^{1}\subset\mathbb{CP}^{3}
. Explicitly, a standard affine patch of the compactified space-time is given by

\mu^{\dot{\alpha}}=\mathrm{i}\,x^{\dot{\alpha}\alpha}\lambda_{\alpha}\,,

(2.1)

with points in real Minkowski space corresponding to choosing
x^{\dot{\alpha}\alpha}
Hermitian.

The construction of twistor space

is conformally invariant. Indeed,
\mathbb{CP}^{3}
naturally carries an action of
\mathrm{SL}(4,\mathbb{C})
, the spin group of the complexification of the conformal group
\mathrm{SO}(2,4)
in four dimensions, acting linearly on the homogeneous coordinates
Z^{A}
. We can describe the incidence relations more invariantly using embedding space coordinates: we first notice that we can pick a rational line by choosing any pair of distinct twistors
Z_{1},Z_{2}
. Thus we can coordinatize space-time points in terms of skew bitwistors of the form
X^{AB}=Z_{1}^{[A}Z_{2}^{B]}
. Conversely, any bitwistor
X^{AB}
is of this form for some

if it satisfies the simplicity condition

X\cdot X\coloneqq\varepsilon_{ABCD}X^{AB}X^{CD}=0\,.

(2.2)

Skew bitwistors are natural homogeneous coordinates on
\mathbb{CP}^{5}
, so this construction identifies complexified, conformally compactified Minkowski space as the quadric
\{X\cdot X=0\}\subset\mathbb{CP}^{5}
. In this sense, the skew bitwistors
X^{AB}
are embedding coordinates. In terms of these, the incidence relations simply read

\varepsilon_{ABCD}Z^{A}X^{BC}=0\,,

(2.3)

which ensures that
Z
lies on the line defined by the simple
X

As is standard, we will let
\mathcal{O}(n)\to\mathbb{CP}^{3}
denote the holomorphic line bundle whose sections are functions of homogeneity

in

. Provided

\mathbb{CP}^{1}
and we similarly denote the holomorphic line bundle whose sections are functions of homogeneity
n
in

\mathcal{O}(n)\to\mathbb{CP}^{1}
; the distinction between these bundles will be clear from the context.

2.1
The infinity twistor and Poisson structure

A conformal scale is encoded by a choice of skew bitwistor
I^{AB}_{\Lambda}
known as the
infinity twistor
. As our notation emphasises, this bitwistor depends on the cosmological constant
\Lambda
, and is normalized to obey

I^{AB}_{\Lambda}I^{\Lambda}_{CB}=\Lambda\,\delta^{A}_{C}\,,

(2.4)

where
I_{AB}^{\Lambda}=\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon_{ABCD}I_{\Lambda}^{CD}
is the dual of
I^{AB}_{\Lambda}
. In particular this implies (and in fact is implied by
I^{AB}_{\Lambda}I^{\Lambda}_{AB}=4\Lambda
) that only when the cosmological constant is zero does the infinity twistor define a line
L_{I}\subset\mathbb{PT}
, or a point in space-time. A standard representation is

I^{AB}_{\Lambda}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\varepsilon^{\dot{\alpha}\dot{\beta}}&0\\
0&\Lambda\,\varepsilon_{\alpha\beta}\end{array}\right)

(2.5a)

for the infinity twistor, or equivalently

I^{\Lambda}_{AB}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\Lambda\,\varepsilon_{\dot{\alpha}\dot{\beta}}&0\\
0&\varepsilon^{\alpha\beta}\end{array}\right)

(2.5b)

for its dual. Using the infinity twistor, we may define an
\mathcal{O}(-2)
-valued holomorphic Poisson structure

\{f,g\}_{\Lambda}:=I_{\Lambda}^{AB}\frac{\partial f}{\partial Z^{A}}\frac{\partial g}{\partial Z^{B}}=\varepsilon^{\dot{\alpha}\dot{\beta}}\frac{\partial f}{\partial\mu^{\dot{\alpha}}}\frac{\partial g}{\partial\mu^{\dot{\beta}}}+\Lambda\,\varepsilon_{\alpha\beta}\frac{\partial f}{\partial\lambda_{\alpha}}\frac{\partial g}{\partial\lambda_{\beta}}\,,

(2.6)

where the second equalities follow from using the representation
(
2.5b
). Dually, we can use
I^{\Lambda}_{AB}
to define the
\mathcal{O}(2)
-valued 1-form

\tau_{\Lambda}\coloneqq I^{\Lambda}_{AB}\,Z^{A}\mathrm{d}Z^{B}=\langle\lambda\,\mathrm{d}\lambda\rangle+\Lambda[\mu\,\mathrm{d}\mu]\,.

(2.7)

We can state the non-degeneracy condition (
2.4
) more geometrically as follows. Let
\Omega
be the
\mathcal{O}(4)
-valued holomorphic volume form

\ \Omega\coloneqq\frac{1}{4!}\varepsilon_{ABCD}Z^{A}\mathrm{d}Z^{B}\wedge\mathrm{d}Z^{C}\wedge\mathrm{d}Z^{D}\,.

(2.8)

Then (
2.4
) can also be written as

\tau_{\Lambda}\wedge\mathrm{d}\tau_{\Lambda}=2\Lambda\,\Omega\,.

(2.9)

The Poisson structure is then defined as the bivector obtained by contracting
\tau_{\Lambda}
with the inverse of
\Omega
.
In this context,
\tau_{\Lambda}
is often known as a
contact
form.

On
\mathbb{C}^{2}\times\mathbb{C}^{*}\subset\mathbb{PT}
, we can introduce the inhomogeneous coordinates
(v^{\dot{0}},v^{\dot{1}},z)=(\mu^{\dot{0}}/\lambda_{1},\mu^{\dot{1}}/\lambda_{1},\lambda_{0}/\lambda_{1})
in which (
2.6
) takes the form

\{f,g\}_{\Lambda}=\frac{\partial f}{\partial v^{\dot{\alpha}}}\frac{\partial g}{\partial v_{\dot{\alpha}}}+\Lambda\bigg(v^{\dot{\alpha}}\frac{\partial f}{\partial v^{\dot{\alpha}}}\partial_{z}g-2f\partial_{z}g-v^{\dot{\alpha}}\frac{\partial g}{\partial v^{\dot{\alpha}}}\partial_{z}f+2g\partial_{z}f\bigg)\,,

(2.10)

where
f,g
are sections of
\mathcal{O}(2)
restricted to
\mathbb{C}^{2}\times\mathbb{C}^{*}\subset\mathbb{PT}
, on which the bundle becomes trivial. The two terms
\Lambda(2g\partial_{z}f-2f\partial_{z}g)
resulting from this trivialisation make it manifest that (
2.10
) does not obey Leibniz’s rule and hence is strictly speaking not a Poisson bracket but rather a so-called
Jacobi bracket
.

Points at the conformal boundary
\mathscr{I}_{\Lambda}
of AdS
4
are characterised in embedding space coordinates by

\mathscr{I}_{\Lambda}=\{X|X^{AB}I_{AB}=0\}\,.

(2.11)

In twistor space this is the condition that the restriction of
\tau_{\Lambda}
to the corresponding twistor line vanishes. Using the incidence relations (
2.1
) in (

) gives the intersection of

with the Euclidean patch as

\mathscr{I}_{\Lambda}=\left\{x^{\alpha\dot{\alpha}}\in\mathbb{R}^{4}:x^{2}=-2/\Lambda\right\}\,,

(2.12)

as expected from the standard form of the AdS metric

g=\frac{\mathrm{d}x^{\alpha\dot{\alpha}}\mathrm{d}x_{\alpha\dot{\alpha}}}{\left(1+\frac{1}{2}\Lambda x^{2}\right)^{2}}\,.

(2.13)

This fixing of the conformal boundary justifies calling
I_{\Lambda}
the infinity twistor.

The form (
2.11
) represents Euclidean AdS
4
as the interior of a ball of radius
\sqrt{-2/\Lambda}
. We can instead present the infinity twistors so that Euclidean AdS
4
is represented as an upper half space in Poincaré coordinates. Introduce a constant vector
z^{\alpha\dot{\alpha}}\coloneqq o^{\alpha}\bar{o}^{\dot{\alpha}}-\iota^{\alpha}\bar{\iota}^{\dot{\alpha}}
of length

so that the vertical coordinate of the upper half space representation of

can be defined to be

on which the metric becomes

(2.14)

Eliminating dotted indices via

I^{\Lambda}_{AB}=I^{AB}_{\Lambda}=\sqrt{\Lambda}\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&\varepsilon^{\alpha\beta}\\

(2.15)

This gives
the Poisson bracket and contact form

\displaystyle\{\cdot,\,\cdot\}^{P}_{\Lambda}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\Lambda}\,\frac{\partial}{\partial\lambda_{\alpha}}\wedge\frac{\partial}{\partial\mu^{\alpha}}\,,

(2.16)

\displaystyle\tau_{\Lambda}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\Lambda}\,\langle\mu\,\mathrm{d}\lambda\rangle-\sqrt{\Lambda}\,\langle\mathrm{d}\mu\,\lambda\rangle\,.

For the most part we will use to the ball coordinates (

Symmetries and gravitons

The infinitesimal symmetries of AdS

are naturally defined by holomorphic functions of homogeneity degree 2 on twistor space, given as

3.1
Elements of
\mathfrak{ham}_{\Lambda}(\mathbb{C}^{2}\times\mathbb{C}^{*})

More general singular elements of the celestial chiral algebra correspond to allowing positive helicity gravitons as fluctuations on the background. In twistor space, the construction of Theorem

identifies self-dual gravitons with infinitesimal deformations that are Hamiltonian. In the original Čech presentation
[

is then simply represented as a homogeneity

The gauge modes with

together with a coordinate charts on

If

]

for
\Theta
the Heaviside step function. Substituting into (
3.4
) gives

\oint\frac{\langle\lambda\,\mathrm{d}\lambda\rangle}{\lambda_{0}^{p-a-2}\lambda_{1}^{p+a-2}}\,w^{p}_{m}(\lambda)=\oint\mathrm{d}z\,z^{a+2-p}w^{p}_{m}(z)\,.

(3.5)

In this way the Hamiltonians
w^{p}_{m,a}
are naturally identified with the modes of the operators
w^{p}_{m}(z)
.

Invariance of the coupling (
3.4
) under the local holomorphic diffeomorphism symmetry on twistor space necessitates the following operator products

\displaystyle

\displaystyle w^{p}_{m}(z_{1})w^{q}_{n}(z_{2})\sim\frac{m(q-1)-n(p-1)}{z_{12}}w^{p+q-2}_{m+n}(z_{2})

(3.6)

\displaystyle-\frac{\Lambda}{z_{12}^{2}}\big((p+q-4)w^{p+q-1}_{m+n}(z_{2})+z_{12}(p-2)\partial_{z}w^{p+q-1}_{m+n}(z_{2})\big)\,.

This is the (tree level) celestial chiral algebra of SD gravity on AdS
4
represented as a vertex algebra. We remark that the field
T(z)=w^{1}_{0}(z)/\Lambda
plays the role of a stress tensor, with OPEs

T(z_{1})w^{p}_{m}(z_{2})\sim\frac{1}{z_{12}^{2}}\big((3-p)w^{p}_{m}(z_{2})+z_{12}\partial_{z}w^{p}_{m}(z_{2})\big)\,.

(3.7)

The corresponding central charge vanishes. Furthermore,
T(z)
is the field of conformal spin 2 in a vertex subalgebra generated by
w^{p}_{0}(z)/\Lambda
for
p\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
. This resembles the
w_{\infty}
vertex algebra which has the same defining operator products, but for labels taking values in the range
p\in\mathbb{Z}_{\leq 1}
.
w_{\infty}
is generated by fields of all integer conformal spins
s\geq 2
rather than
s\leq 2
.

The fields
w^{p}_{m}(z)
can be conveniently organised into hard generating functions depending on an auxiliary right-handed spinor
\tilde{\lambda}_{\dot{\alpha}}
, defined by

w(\tilde{\lambda},z)=\sum_{p\pm m\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}}\frac{2\tilde{\lambda}_{\dot{0}}^{p+m-1}\tilde{\lambda}_{\dot{1}}^{p-m-1}}{(p+m-1)!(p-m-1)!}w^{p}_{m}(z)\,.

(3.8)

In terms of these hard generators the vertex algebra (
3.6
) reads

\displaystyle w(\tilde{\lambda}_{1},z_{1})w(\tilde{\lambda}_{2},z_{2})

(3.9)

\displaystyle\sim\frac{[12]}{z_{12}}w(\tilde{\lambda}_{1}+\tilde{\lambda}_{2},z_{2})-\frac{\Lambda}{z_{12}^{2}}\Big(\big(\tilde{\lambda}_{\dot{\alpha}}\partial_{\tilde{\lambda}_{\dot{\alpha}}}-4\big)w(\tilde{\lambda},z_{2})+z_{12}\big(\tilde{\lambda}_{1\dot{\alpha}}\partial_{\tilde{\lambda}_{\dot{\alpha}}}-2\big)\partial_{z}w(\tilde{\lambda},z_{2})\Big)\Big|_{\tilde{\lambda}=\tilde{\lambda}_{1}+\tilde{\lambda}_{2}}\,.

We recognise the coefficient
[12]/z_{12}
as the tree graviton splitting function on flat space
[
34
]
. But the
\Lambda
dependent coefficients are not simply functions of the spinor-helicity variables, instead taking the form of differential operators. This results from the loss of supertranslation invariance on AdS
4
.

3.3
Variations and extensions

Had we used the representation of the infinity twistor (
2.15
) appropriate to the Poincaré patch, we’d have obtained the algebra

\displaystyle

\displaystyle\{\hat{w}^{p}_{m,a},\hat{w}^{q}_{n,b}\}_{\Lambda}^{P}=\big((p+m-1)(q-b-2)-(q+n-1)(p-a-2)\big)\hat{w}^{p+q-3/2}_{m+n-1/2,a+b-1/2}

(3.10)

\displaystyle+\big((p-m-1)(q+b-2)-(q-n-1)(p+a-2)\big)\hat{w}^{p+q-3/2}_{m+n+1/2,a+b+1/2}\,,

where the generators of the algebra have been redefined to

\hat{w}^{p}_{m,a}\coloneqq\frac{2}{\sqrt{\Lambda}}w_{m,a}^{p},

(3.11)

Although these again provide an extension of the
\mathrm{AdS}_{4}
symmetries, they are not suitable for expansion around
\Lambda=0
. They are however well adapted to soft limits for momentum eigenstates based on translations of the Poincaré patch. We emphasise that the Lie algebra (
3.10
) is
not
isomorphic to (
3.3
). The difference is essentially the choice of the location of the line
\lambda_{\alpha}=0
: up to AdS
4
isometries there are two such choices, the first where the line is in the complement of the unit ball, and the second here where the line corresponds to a point of
\mathscr{I}
. These two choices provide the two algebras (
3.3
) & (
3.10
), respectively. On the other hand, the choices of the sets
U_{0}
and
U_{1}
used to define our basis
\{w^{p}_{m,n}\}
are more associated to the choice of cohomology representation. Indeed, these can be made canonically in split signature.

Following the steps outlined in section
3.2
, one may recover the vertex algebra associated to (
3.10
), which reads

\displaystyle w^{p}_{m}(z_{1})w^{q}_{n}(z_{2})\sim\frac{(p+q+m+n-2)}{z_{12}^{2}}\hat{w}^{p+q-3/2}_{m+n-1/2}(z_{2})+\frac{(p+m-1)}{z_{12}}\partial_{z}\hat{w}^{p+q-3/2}_{m+n-1/2}(z_{2})

(3.12)

\displaystyle-\frac{(p+q-m-n-2)}{z_{12}^{2}}z_{2}\hat{w}^{p+q-3/2}_{m+n+1/2}(z_{2})-\frac{(p-m-1)}{z_{12}}\partial_{z}(z_{2}\hat{w}^{p+q-3/2}_{m+n+1/2}(z_{2}))

\displaystyle+\frac{2\big((p-2)(n-1)-(q-2)(m-1)\big)}{z_{12}}\hat{w}^{p+q-3/2}_{m+n+1/2}(z_{2})\,.

It’s straightforward to recast the above in terms of hard generators, which we leave as an exercise for the interested reader.

Both the Lie algebra adapted to the ball (
3.3
) and the Poincaré patch (
3.10
) can easily be extended to incorporate free fields propagating on the gravitational background. By the linear Penrose transform, solutions to free field equations of spin
s
on AdS
4
are in bijection with cohomology classes
[\varphi]\in H^{1}(\mathbb{PT},\mathcal{O}(2s-2))
. Fluctuations of such fields can be represented in the Čech language by holomorphic functions on
U_{0}\cap U_{1}
, with homogeneity
2s-2
. Regularity near
\mu^{\dot{\alpha}}=0
allows us to decompose
\varphi
into modes

x^{p}_{m,a}=\frac{(\mu^{\dot{0}})^{p+m-1}(\mu^{\dot{1}})^{p-m-1}}{2\lambda_{0}^{p-a-s}\lambda_{1}^{p+a-s}}

(3.13)

where as above
p\pm m-1\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
. The Hamiltonians
w^{p}_{m,a}
naturally act on these modes via the Poisson bracket, furnishing us with modules for the Lie algebras
(
3.3
)
and (
3.10
). For example, in the case of the ball model this action reads

\{w^{p}_{m,a},x^{q}_{n,b}\}_{\Lambda}=(m(q-1)-n(p-1))x^{p+q-2}_{m+n,a+b}-\Lambda(a(q-s)-b(p-2))x^{p+q-1}_{m+n,a+b}\,.

(3.14)

Extending by these modules gives symmetry algebras for the coupled systems. As we can see in equation (
3.14
), for
\Lambda\neq 0
the structure constants of such extended algebras depend explicitly on
s
. This reflects the fact that for general fluctuations
h
the resulting curved twistor space does not holomorphically fibre over
\mathbb{CP}^{1}
. The complex structure of the line bundles
\mathcal{O}(2s-2)
is deformed to
\mathcal{K}^{(1-s)/2}
for
\mathcal{K}
the canonical bundle of the curved twistor space. The
s
-dependent term in (
3.14
) is generated by this shift.

To incorporate self-dual Yang-Mills is no more difficult. Conformal invariance ensures that the
S
-algebra is undeformed on AdS
4
, and the natural action of (
3.3
) and (
3.10
) outlined above distributes over it’s commutators. Therefore we can extend in the same way.

It is also easy to extend these considerations to include supersymmetry. We adjoin
\mathcal{N}
fermionic coordinates
\eta^{I}
to give the homogeneous coordinates
\mathcal{Z}^{\mathcal{I}}\coloneqq(Z^{A},\eta^{I})
on
\mathbb{CP}^{3|\mathcal{N}}
, acted on by the superconformal group
\mathrm{SL}(4|\mathcal{N})
. As shown in
[
35
,
16
]
, self-dual
\mathrm{SO}(\mathcal{N})
gauged supergravity on AdS
4
may be described by the non-linear graviton construction, where the infinity twistor is extended to a non-degenerate graded skew supertwistor
I^{\mathcal{I}\mathcal{J}}
defining graded Poisson structure and contact form. This preserves a
\mathrm{OSP}(2|\mathcal{N})
subgroup of the superconformal group. Ungauged supergravities can be obtained by considering infinity twistors that do not have maximal rank in the fermionic directions. In particular, the fully ungauged supergravity arises when we continue to use the non-supersymmetric Poisson structure. By augmenting the
w^{p}_{m,a}
to a basis of functions of the homogeneous supertwistor coordinates
\mathcal{Z}^{\mathcal{I}}
of homogeneity 2, we obtain supersymmetric extensions of the above algebras. These provide extensions of the relevant super-Lie algebras of symmetries for supersymmetric extensions of
\mathrm{AdS}_{4}
depending the choice of infinity twistor. They also includes twistor functions for all the supergravity modes.

4
Symmetries of the twistor action

These symmetries can be understood via Noether arguments applied to the twistor action. We give a brief sketch here. Self-dual gravity with
\Lambda\neq 0
may be described on twistor space by the Poisson-BF theory

S_{\text{PBF}}=\int_{\mathbb{PT}}\Omega\wedge G\wedge\left(\bar{\partial}H+\frac{1}{2}\{H,H\}_{\Lambda}\right)\,,

(4.1)

first introduced in
[
16
]
. Here,
\Omega
is the
\mathcal{O}(4)
-valued top holomorphic form on twistor space, while
H\in\Omega^{0,1}(\mathbb{PT},\mathcal{O}(2))
is a Hamiltonian governing the complex structure deformation on twistor space via the the deformed Dolbeault operator

\bar{\nabla}:=\bar{\partial}+\{H,\,\cdot\,\}_{\Lambda}.

(4.2)

Finally,
G\in\Omega^{0,1}(\mathbb{PT},\mathcal{O}(-6))
is a Lagrange multiplier that, on-shell, may be interpreted as the Penrose transform of a linearized ASD Weyl spinor propagating on the SD background.

The celestial chiral algebra above is related to gauge transformations of this Lagrangian. These are a combination of Poisson diffeomorphisms of twistor space, generated by the Hamiltonian vector field associated to a smooth function
\chi
of homogeneity degree 2, and a further transformation generated by the Hamiltonian vector field of a smooth function
\tilde{\chi}
of weight
-6
. These transformations act on the fields as

\delta H=\bar{\nabla}\chi\,,\qquad\delta G=\bar{\nabla}\tilde{\chi}+\{\chi,G\}_{\Lambda}\,.

(4.3)

To make contact with the celestial chiral algebra, we compute the Noether charges associated to these field transformations. The (pre-)symplectic form on the space of classical solutions of the twistor action (
4.1
) can easily be seen to be

\omega(\delta H,\delta G)=\int_{\Sigma}\delta H\wedge\delta G\wedge\Omega\,,

(4.4)

where
\Sigma
is real co-dimension 1 slice of
\mathbb{PT}
. The Noether charges corresponding to (
4.3
) are then given by the integrals

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{\chi}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Sigma}\bar{\nabla}\chi\wedge G\wedge\Omega

(4.5)

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{\tilde{\chi}}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Sigma}\bar{\nabla}\tilde{\chi}\wedge H\wedge\Omega\,,

On shell, after integrating by parts these reduce to boundary terms

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{\chi}

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial\Sigma}\chi\,G\wedge\Omega

(4.6)

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{\tilde{\chi}}

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial\Sigma}\tilde{\chi}\,H\wedge\Omega\,.

as expected for Noether charges of gauge transformations. The real four-manifold
\partial\Sigma
can for example be taken to be the 4-surface swept out by Riemann spheres
L_{x}
as
x
varies over some choice of 2-surface in space-time. In this case, the Penrose transform may be used to express the integrals (
4.6
) in terms of fields on space-time.

The Noether charge integrals (
4.6
) are not themselves gauge invariant unless
\chi,\tilde{\chi}
are holomorphic. Such global holomorphic
\chi
of homogeneity degree two are, as described above, the Hamiltonians that generate the global isometries of AdS
4
. Because
\tilde{\chi}
has weight
-6
, there are no such global
\tilde{\chi}
. If we wish to allow singularities so as to extend the
\chi
to be the generators (
3.1
) of our extended algebra, we must extend our on-shell phase space by imposing boundary conditions so that
H=G=0
near
\lambda_{0}=0
and
\lambda_{1}=0
. This is equivalent to choosing holomorphic Darboux coordinates on small neighbourhoods of
\lambda_{0}=0
and
\lambda_{1}=0
as discussed earlier. The Noether charges (
4.6
) make sense on this extended phase space and generate the celestial chiral algebra for
\Lambda\neq 0
described earlier.

5
Conclusion and discussion

We have seen that the first order deformation to the flat space celestial chiral algebra found in
[
4
]
naturally arises from local Poisson diffeomorphisms of twistor space, once the flat-space Poisson structure is replaced by the Poisson structure defined by a non-degenerate infinity twistor. In
[
4
]
, the algebra was constructed via the AdS amplitudes first found in
[
29
,
30
,
31
,
32
]
, although in a rather
ad hoc
manner: the algebra that arises naïvely from the AdS amplitudes fails to satisfy the Jacobi identity, so a suitable modification of the
\Lambda
-deformed graviton OPE is needed to restore associativity. In contrast, here we have constructed the algebra from first principles directly from the Poisson bracket on twistor space, so the Jacobi identity is automatically satisfied. We thus see that this algebra is the celestial chiral algebra of self-dual gravity of AdS
4
, at least at the classical level. We have seen further that the algebra can be understood as symmetries of an extension of the twistor action for the SD Einstein equations leading to Noether charges on an extended phase space.

Perhaps the most interesting question is the extent to which these symmetries can yield useful insights beyond the self-dual sector. However, many more modest directions deserve to be explored in further work:

Twistor sigma models.

The chiral sigma models of
[
36
,
8
]
naturally extend to ones with a non-degenerate infinity twistor. They represent the
\Lambda
-deformed chiral algebra directly in the OPE of their vertex operators. Correlation functions of these vertex operators naturally then give rise to the split signature correlators for Einstein gravity.

Perturbiner calculations.

In flat space, the celestial chiral algebra can be seen in the splitting function of gravity amplitudes. This is essentially a pertubiner: two on-shell positive helicity states joined to a propagator at a trivalent vertex, taken in the limit that the external momentum become (holomorphically) collinear. It would be interesting to perform these calculations in AdS
4
, with bulk-to-boundary propagators as external states. This would bring together the perspective of
[
4
]
with the current work, albeit in the context of self-dual gravity.

Space-time realizations and AdS/CFT.

Here we have focused on formulations in twistor space. There are by now a number of space-time formulations of many of these ideas at
\Lambda=0
such as
[
37
,
38
]
and it would be interesting to extend these to
\Lambda\neq 0
, perhaps following on from the frameworks developed by
[
39
,
40
,
41
]
. In a different direction, it will be interesting to identify the self-dual sectors of conventional AdS
4
/CFT
3
correspondences such as, for example, those described in
[
42
]
, so as to see how these structures arise there. There are also a number of other
\Lambda
-BMS proposals to be compared to from a space-time perspective such as
[
43
,
44
,
45
]
.

Further deformations with
\Lambda\neq 0
.

There is by now a fairly large number of works on classical deformations of celestial chiral algebras (or the absence thereof) in asymptotically (locally) flat space-times
[
28
,
6
,
46
,
47
]
. All of these are loop algebras of deformations of
\mathfrak{ham}(\mathbb{C}^{2})
or
\mathfrak{ham}(\mathbb{C}^{2}/\Gamma)
for some finite subgroup
\Gamma\subset\mathrm{SU}(2)
which are very restricted. The Lie algebra (
3.3
) in turn is not the loop algebra of a deformation of
\mathfrak{ham}(\mathbb{C}^{2})
. It is natural to ask whether there are further deformations of this form and what their geometric bulk-interpretations are. Gravitational instantons in the presence of a cosmological constant are currently being investigated
[
48
]
.

Quantum corrections.

For
\Lambda=0
Poisson-BF theory on twistor space is anomalous
[
49
]
, signalling a loss of integrability in SD gravity at the quantum level. Certainly we should expect a similar anomaly in Poisson-BF theory for
\Lambda\neq 0
. It would be interesting to compute this, particularly to see whether there exist alternative methods of anomaly cancellation. Successful cancellation of the anomaly would presumably lead to consistent quantum counterparts of the tree level celestial chiral algebra (
3.6
) along the lines of
[
50
,
5
]
.

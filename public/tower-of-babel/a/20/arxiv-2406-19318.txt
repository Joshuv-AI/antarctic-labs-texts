On p p -adic solutions to KZ equations, ordinary crystals, and p s p^{s} -hypergeometric solutions
Alexander Varchenko ⋆, Vadim Vologodsky ∘

Abstract
. In this paper we consider the KZ connection associated with a family of hyperelliptic curves of genus g g over the ring of p p -adic integers ℤ p \mathbb{Z}_{p} . Then the dual connection is the Gauss-Manin connection of that family. We observe that the Gauss-Manin connection has an ordinary F F -crystal structure and its unit root subcrystal is of rank g g . We prove that all local flat sections of the KZ connection annihilate the unite root subcrystal, and the space of all local flat sections of the KZ connection is a free ℤ p \mathbb{Z}_{p} -module of rank g g . We also consider the reduction modulo p s p^{s} of the unit root subcrystal for any s ≥ 1 s\geq 1 . We prove that its annihilator is generated by the so-called p s p^{s} -hypergeometric flat sections of the KZ connection. In particular, that means that the reduction modulo p s p^{s} of an arbitrary local flat section of the KZ connection over ℤ p \mathbb{Z}_{p} is a linear combination of the p s p^{s} -hypergeometric flat sections.

1.
Introduction

The Knizhnik-Zamolodchikov (KZ)
equations were discovered in conformal field theory, see
[
KZ
,
EFK
]
.
Versions of these equations have been observed
in representation theory, algebraic geometry, combinatorics, field theory, … In
[
CF
,
DF
,
DJMM
,
SV1
]
the KZ equations were identified with equations for flat sections of suitable Gauss-Manin connections, and solutions to the KZ equations were constructed in the form of multidimensional hypergeometric integrals.

In
[
SV2
,
V3
]
, the KZ equations were considered modulo
p^{s}
where
p
is a prime number,
and polynomial solutions to the KZ equations modulo
p^{s}
were constructed by an elementary
procedure as suitable
p^{s}
-approximations of the hypergeometric integrals.

In
[
VV
]
, the question was addressed of whether all solutions to the KZ equations in characteristic
p
are generated by the
p
-hypergeometric functions. The affirmative answer was demonstrated in a non-trivial example.

In
[
V3
,
VZ
]
, the KZ equations associated with a family of hyperelliptic curves of genus
g
were studied.
It was demonstrated that the module of
p^{s}
-hypergeometric solutions has a
p
-adic limit as
s
tends to infinity.
This limit is a KZ-invariant rank
g
subbundle
\mathcal{W}
of the associated KZ connection.

In this paper we describe how the
p^{s}
-hypergeometric solutions and subbundle
\mathcal{W}
are related to the ordinary
F
-crystal
of the Gauss-Manin connection of that family of hyperelliptic curves.
More precisely, we consider the KZ connection associated with the family
of hyperelliptic curves over the
ring of
p
-adic integers
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
. Then the dual connection
is the Gauss-Manin connection of that family. We observe that the Gauss-Manin connection
has an ordinary
F
-crystal structure and its unit root subcrystal is of rank
g
.
We prove that all local flat sections of the KZ connection annihilate the unite root subcrystal,
the space of all local flat sections of the KZ connection is a free
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-module of rank
g
,
the space of all local flat sections generate the subbundle
\mathcal{W}
.

We also consider the reduction modulo
p^{s}
of the unit root subcrystal for any
s\geq 1
.
We prove that its annihilator is generated by the

p^{s}
-hypergeometric flat sections of the KZ connection. In particular, that means that
the reduction
modulo
p^{s}
of an arbitrary local flat section of the KZ connection over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
is a linear combination
of the
p^{s}
-hypergeometric solutions.

The general project is to analyze solutions of arbitrary KZ equations over

\mathbb{Z}_{p}
and relate them to
p^{s}
-hypergeometric solutions and associated

F
-crystals. At this stage, we consider first non-trivial examples to establish a map
of the interrelations between these concepts.

1.1.
KZ equations

Let
g
be a positive integer. Denote
n=2g+1
.

In this paper we consider the following example of the KZ equations.
Let
\Omega_{ij}
be the
n\times n
-matrix
(c_{kl})
with

c_{ij}=c_{ji}=-c_{ii}=-c_{jj}=1
and all other entries equal to
0
.
Let
z=(z_{1},\dots,z_{n})
.
Define

\displaystyle H_{i}(z)=\sum_{j\neq i}\frac{\Omega_{ij}}{z_{i}-z_{j}},\quad i=1,\dots,n,

the Gaudin Hamiltonians.
Consider the system of differential and algebraic equations for
column vectors
I(z)=(I_{1}(z)
, …,
I_{n}(z))^{\intercal}
:

(1.1)

\displaystyle\phantom{aaaaaa}2\partial_{i}I\ -\ \,H_{i}(z)\,I\,=\,0,\quad i=1,\dots,n,\quad I_{1}(z)+\dots+I_{n}(z)=0,

where
\partial_{i}
denotes
\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{i}}
;
M^{\intercal}
denotes the transpose matrix of a matrix
M
.

Let
\mathbb{A}^{n}
be affine space with coordinates
z_{1},\dots,z_{n}
.
Let

S
be the complement to the union of all diagonal hyperplanes defined by equations

z_{i}=z_{j}
,
i\neq j
.
The KZ equations define a flat KZ connection
\nabla^{\operatorname{KZ}}

on the trivial bundle
S\times V\to S
whose fiber is
the vector space
V
of
n
-vectors with the zero sum of coordinates.
Denote this bundle by
\mathcal{V}
and call the pair
(\mathcal{V},\nabla^{\operatorname{KZ}})
the KZ bundle.

Remark 1.1
.

The system of equations (
1.1
) is the system of the original KZ equations
associated with the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
and the subspace of singular vectors of weight
n-2

of the tensor power
({\mathbb{C}}^{2})^{{\otimes}n}
of the two-dimensional irreducible
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
-module, up to a gauge
transformation, see this example in
[
V1
, Section 1.1]
. The original KZ equations depend on a parameter
\kappa
.
In our paper,
\kappa=2
, see the coefficient of
\partial_{i}
in (
1.1
).

1.2.
Gauss-Manin connection

Consider the family
X
of hyperelliptic curves
X(z)
defined by the affine equation

(1.2)

\displaystyle y^{2}=(x-z_{1})\dots(x-z_{n}).

For
z\in S
, the curve
X(z)
is a smooth projective curve of genus
g
.
The KZ connection
\nabla^{\operatorname{KZ}}

is dual to the Gauss-Manin connection
\nabla^{\operatorname{GM}}

over
S
with fiber
H^{1}_{dR}(X(z))
.

For example, the flat sections of the KZ equations over the field
{\mathbb{C}}
are labeled by flat sections

\gamma(z)
of the Gauss-Manin connection over
S
with fiber
H_{1}(X(z),{\mathbb{C}})

and given by the following construction.
Define the master function

(1.3)

\displaystyle\Phi(x,z)=\big((x-z_{1})\dots(x-z_{n})\big)^{-1/2}

and the
{n}
-vector of hyperelliptic integrals

(1.4)

\displaystyle I^{(\gamma)}(z)=(I_{1}(z),\dots,I_{n}(z))^{\intercal},\qquad I_{j}=\int_{\gamma(z)}\frac{dx}{(x-z_{j})y}=\int_{\gamma(z)}\frac{\Phi(x,z)dx}{x-z_{j}}\,.

Theorem 1.2
.

The vector
I^{(\gamma)}(z)
satisfies the KZ equations (
1.1
).
All complex solutions to the KZ equations have this form.
The complex vector space of solutions of the form (
1.4
) is
n-1
-dimensional.

Theorem
1.2
is a classical statement discussed as an example in
[
V1
, Section 1.1]
, also see

[
V1
, Formula (1.3)]
.

The solutions in (
1.4
) are called the hypergeometric solutions to the KZ equations.

1.3.
Solutions modulo
p^{s}

Let
p
be an odd prime number. For an integer
s\geq 1
define the
master polynomial

\displaystyle\Phi_{s}(x,z)=\big((x-z_{1})\dots(x-z_{n})\big)^{(p^{s}-1)/2}

-approximation of the master function

For

-vector

\displaystyle Q^{s,\ell}(z)=(Q^{s,\ell}_{1},\dots,Q^{s,\ell}_{n})^{\intercal}

as the coefficient of

in the column

-vector of polynomials

\big(\frac{\Phi_{s}(x,z)}{x-z_{1}},\dots,\frac{\Phi_{s}(x,z)}{x-z_{n}}\big)^{\intercal}
.
If
\ell>g
, then the polynomial

\frac{\Phi_{s}(x,z)}{x-z_{i}}
does not have the monomial

x^{\ell p^{s}-1}
.

Theorem 1.3

(
[
V3
, Theorems 4.4]
,
[
VZ
, Theorem 6.9]
)
.

The column
n
-vectors
Q^{s,\ell}(z),

\ell=1,\dots,g
, of polynomials in
z
are solutions of the KZ equations
(
1.1
) modulo
p^{s}
. They generate a free
(\mathbb{Z}/p^{s})[z]
-module of rank
g
.

The

n
-vectors
Q^{s,\ell}(z)
,
\ell=1,\dots,g
, are called
the
p^{s}
-hypergeometric solutions
to the KZ equations
(
1.1
).

1.4.
Formal
p
-adic schemes over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}

Recall some definitions.
A formal
p
-adic scheme
Y/\mathbb{Z}_{p}
is a collection of schemes
Y_{s}/(\mathbb{Z}/p^{s})
together with isomorphisms

\displaystyle Y_{s+1}\times_{\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb{Z}/p^{s+1}}\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb{Z}/p^{s}\simeq Y_{s}\,.

If
f_{1},\dots,f_{k}\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}[z_{1},\dots,z_{n}]
, then

\displaystyle\mathbb{Z}_{p}[z_{1},\dots,z_{n},f_{1}^{-1},\dots,f_{k}^{-1}]^{\wedge}_{p}\ :\,=\ \lim_{\leftarrow}(\mathbb{Z}/p^{s})[z_{1},\dots,z_{n},f_{1}^{-1},\dots,f_{k}^{-1}]\,

is the
p
-adic completion of

\mathbb{Z}_{p}[z_{1},\dots,z_{n},f_{1}^{-1},\dots,f_{k}^{-1}]
.
The formal
p
-adic scheme associated with
\mathbb{Z}_{p}[z_{1},\dots,z_{n},f_{1}^{-1},\dots,f_{k}^{-1}]^{\wedge}_{p}

is denoted by

\displaystyle\operatorname{Spf}\,\mathbb{Z}_{p}[z_{1},\dots,z_{n},f_{1}^{-1},\dots,f_{k}^{-1}]^{\wedge}_{p}.

Let

\mathfrak{S}={\operatorname{Spf}}\,{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}[z_{1},\dots,z_{n},(z_{i}-z_{j})^{-1},\;1\leq i\neq j\leq n]^{\wedge}_{p}

be the formal
p
-adic scheme of the complement to the union of diagonal hyperplanes
z_{i}-z_{j}=0
,

1\leq i,j\leq n
.

In what follows we consider the KZ bundle
\mathcal{V}
over the formal
p
-adic scheme

\mathfrak{S}
and its subschemes and reductions modulo
p^{s}
.

In
[
V3
, Section 5.1]
, a
g\times g
-matrix
A(z)
was defined with entries in
\mathbb{Z}[z]
and
determinant having a nonzero projection to
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}[z]
. The matrix
A(z)
projected to
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}[z]

is the Hasse–Witt matrix of the curve
X(z)
considered over
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
. The fact that
\det A(z)
is nonzero
over
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
means that the curve
X(z)
is ordinary in the terminology of
[
DI
]
.

Let

\mathfrak{D}={\operatorname{Spf}}\,{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}[z_{1},\dots,z_{n},(\det A)^{-1},(z_{i}-z_{j})^{-1},\;1\leq i\neq j\leq n]^{\wedge}_{p}

be the open formal
p
-adic subscheme of
\mathfrak{S}
defined by
\det A(z)\neq 0
.

Denote

(1.5)

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}(\mathfrak{S})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}[z_{1},\dots,z_{n},(z_{i}-z_{j})^{-1},\;1\leq i\neq j\leq n]^{\wedge}_{p}\,.

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}(\mathfrak{D})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}[z_{1},\dots,z_{n},(\det A)^{-1},(z_{i}-z_{j})^{-1},\;1\leq i\neq j\leq n]^{\wedge}_{p}\,.

According to
[
VZ
, Section 6.5]
, for any
s\geq 1
,
the column vectors

Q^{s,\ell}(z)
,
\ell=1,\dots,g
, generate a rank
g
flat subbundle of the KZ bundle

\mathcal{V}_{s}
over
\mathfrak{D}_{s}
. The subbundle is denoted by
\mathcal{W}_{s}
.
According to
[
VZ
, Theorem 6.9]
, we have
\mathcal{W}_{s+1}|_{\mathfrak{D}_{s}}=\mathcal{W}_{s}
as subbundles of
\mathcal{V}_{s}
.
Thus, the sequence of bundles
\mathcal{W}_{s}
has a
p
-adic limit as

, denoted by
\mathcal{W}
, where
\mathcal{W}\subset\mathcal{V}|_{\mathfrak{D}}
.

1.5.
F
-crystal

Consider our family
X
of hyperelliptic curves over
\mathfrak{S}
.
Let
H^{1}_{dR}(X/\mathfrak{S})
be the relative de Rham cohomology of the family.
This is a bundle of rank
2g
equipped with the Gauss-Manin connection
\nabla^{\operatorname{GM}}
.
We denote it by
\mathcal{E}
.

We identify
\mathcal{V}
with
\mathcal{E}^{*}
dual to
\mathcal{E}
,
cf. formula (
1.4
).
This identification allows us to identify the flat sections of the
KZ connection with the
\nabla^{\operatorname{GM}}
-flat linear functionals
I:\mathcal{E}\to\mathcal{O}(\mathfrak{S})
.

The map
z_{i}\mapsto z_{i}^{p}
,
i=1,\dots,n
, extends to a well-defined algebra endomorphism

F:\mathcal{O}(\mathfrak{S})\to\mathcal{O}(\mathfrak{S})
.
Denote

\displaystyle F^{*}\mathcal{E}=\mathcal{O}(\mathfrak{S}){\otimes}_{\mathcal{O}(\mathfrak{S}),F}\mathcal{E}.

Thus,
F^{*}\mathcal{E}
is the quotient of
\mathcal{O}(\mathfrak{S})\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathcal{E}

by the subgroup generated by
r\otimes r^{\prime}v-F(r^{\prime})r\otimes v
,
r,r^{\prime}\in\mathcal{O}(\mathfrak{S})
,
v\in\mathcal{E}
.
Let

\displaystyle\phi:F^{*}\mathcal{E}\to\mathcal{E}

be the crystalline Frobenius, see
[
BO
]
. The morphism
\phi
respects the connections.
The pair
(\mathcal{E},\phi)
is called an
F
-crystal.

The
F
-crystal
(\mathcal{E},\phi)
restricted to
\mathfrak{D}
is ordinary in the sense of
[
DI
]
since

\det A(z)\neq 0
on
\mathfrak{D}
. In particular, by

[
DI
]
, there is a unique exact sequence of bundles with connections over
\mathfrak{D}
,

(1.6)

\displaystyle 0\to\mathcal{U}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{E}\to\mathcal{E}/\mathcal{U}\to 0,

such that

(i)

\operatorname{rank}\,\mathcal{U}=g
,

(ii)

\phi\,|_{\mathcal{F}^{*}\mathcal{U}}:F^{*}\mathcal{U}\to\mathcal{U}
is an isomorphism,

(iii)

\phi\,|_{F^{*}(\mathcal{E}/\mathcal{U})}:F^{*}(\mathcal{E}/\mathcal{U})\to\mathcal{E}/\mathcal{U}

has the form
p\cdot\phi_{1}
, where
\phi_{1}:F^{*}(\mathcal{E}/\mathcal{U})\to\mathcal{E}/\mathcal{U}
is an isomorphism, and
p\,\cdot

is multiplication by
p
.

(iv)

\mathcal{U}
equals
\bigcap_{i}\phi^{i}((F^{i})^{*}\mathcal{E})\subset\mathcal{E}
, where
\phi^{i}
is viewed as
(F^{i})^{*}\mathcal{E}\to\mathcal{E}
.

Note that (iv) is implied by (ii) and (iii).

The pair
(\mathcal{U},\phi)
is called the unit root subcrystal of
(\mathcal{E},\phi)
. We refer to sequence (
1.6
) as the slope sequence.

The KZ bundle
\mathcal{V}=\mathcal{E}^{*}
has the dual
F
-crystal structure,

(1.7)

\displaystyle p\cdot(\phi^{*})^{-1}:F^{*}(\mathcal{E}^{*})\to\mathcal{E}^{*},

denoted by
(\mathcal{V},\psi)
.
Here
\phi^{*}
is the morphism dual to
\phi
. The restriction
of the dual
F
-crystal to
\mathfrak{D}
is ordinary.
Let

(1.8)

\displaystyle 0\to\mathcal{U}^{\prime}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{V}\to\mathcal{V}/\mathcal{U}^{\prime}\to 0

be the corresponding unique exact sequence of bundles with connections over
\mathfrak{D}

with properties (i)-(iv). Here
\mathcal{U}^{\prime}
is the annihilator of
\mathcal{U}
.

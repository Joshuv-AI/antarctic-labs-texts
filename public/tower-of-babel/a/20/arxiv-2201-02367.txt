Deligne-Beilinson cohomology of the universal K3 surface
Zhiyuan Li, Xun Zhang

Abstract
. O’Grady’s generalized Franchetta conjecture (GFC) is concerned with codimension 2 algebraic cycles on universal polarized K3 surfaces. In [ 3 ] , this conjecture has been studied in the Betti cohomology groups. Following a suggestion of Voisin, we investigate this problem in the Deligne-Beilinson (DB) cohomology groups. In this paper, we develop the theory of Deligne-Beilinson cohomology groups on separated (smooth) Deligne-Mumford stacks. Using the automorphic cohomology group and Noether-Lefschetz theory, we compute the 4-th DB-cohomology group of universal oriented polarized K3 surfaces with at worst an A 1 A_{1} -singularity and show that GFC for such family holds in DB-cohomology. In particular, this confirms O’Grady’s original conjecture in DB cohomology.

1.
Introduction

Zero-cycles on K3 surfaces have been studied by Beauville and Voisin in
[
2
]
. They have shown that if
S
is a K3 surface, then there is a canonical zero-cycle
c_{S}
on
S
that satisfies the following two properties:

(1)

The intersection of two divisor classes on
S
lies in
\mathbb{Z}c_{S}\subset{\rm CH}_{0}(S)
.

(2)

The second Chern class
c_{2}(T_{S})
equals
24c_{S}\in{\rm CH}_{0}(S)
.

This canonical cycle is called Beauville-Voisin class in
{\rm CH}_{0}(S)
. A natural question is to investigate the codimension 2 cycles on the universal K3 surfaces. More precisely, let
\mathscr{F}_{g}^{\circ}
be the moduli space of primitively polarized complex K3 surfaces of genus
g>2
with only trivial automorphism groups, which carries a universal family

\pi:\mathscr{S}_{g}^{\circ}\rightarrow\mathscr{F}_{g}^{\circ}.

O’Grady
[
38
]
has conjectured that the
0
-th Chow group with rational coefficients of the generic fiber of
\pi
is spanned by the Beauville-Voisin class. This is referred to as the generalized Franchetta conjecture which can be viewed as a higher dimensional analogue to the Franchetta conjecture on
\mathcal{M}_{g}
.

Conjecture 1.1

(Generalized Franchetta Conjecture for K3 surfaces)
.

Let
\mathcal{T}_{\pi}
be the relative tangent bundle of
\pi:\mathscr{S}_{g}^{\circ}\to\mathscr{F}_{g}^{\circ}
. For any
\alpha\in{\rm CH}^{2}(\mathscr{S}^{\circ}_{g})
, there exists
m\in\mathbb{Q}
such that
\alpha-mc_{2}(\mathcal{T}_{\pi})
is supported on a proper subset of
\mathscr{F}_{g}^{\circ}
.

This conjecture has been confirmed when
g\leq 12
and
g=15
(
cf
.
[
40
,
13
]
) by using the explicit geometric models. It remains open in general. Recently, Beauville has shown in
[
1
]
that there exists for every
g
a hypersurface in
\mathscr{F}_{g}^{\circ}
such that GFC holds for the corresponding family. We also refer the readers to
[
14
,
15
,
27
]
for the generalization to hyper-Kähler varieties. In
[
3
]
, the authors have verified GFC in cohomology groups. In this paper, we give more evidences to support this conjecture by considering the cycle classes of the universal K3 surface in the Deligne-Beilinson cohomology group. Recall that the Deligne-Beilinson cohomology
\mathrm{H}^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{DB}}(X_{\mathbb{C}},\mathbb{Z}(p))
of a quasi-projective complex algebraic variety
X_{\mathbb{C}}
is defined as the hypercohomology of the Deligne-Beilinson complex (cf.
[
12
,
42
]
). Denote by

(1.1)

{\rm cl}_{\mathrm{DB}}:{\rm CH}^{k}(X_{\mathbb{C}})\rightarrow\mathrm{H}^{2k}_{\mathrm{DB}}(X_{\mathbb{C}},\mathbb{Q}(k)).

the DB-cycle class map. Compared with the ordinary cohomology group, the DB cohomology group is closer to the Chow groups because the cycle class map
{\rm cl}:{\rm CH}^{k}(X_{\mathbb{C}})\to\mathrm{H}^{2k}(X_{\mathbb{C}},\mathbb{Q}(k))
factors through
{\rm cl}_{\mathrm{DB}}
. The main result of this article is

Theorem 1.1.1
.

For any
\alpha\in{\rm CH}^{2}(\mathscr{S}_{g}^{\circ})
, there exists a rational number
m\in\mathbb{Q}
such that the class

(1.2)

{\rm cl}_{\mathrm{DB}}(\alpha-mc_{2}(\mathcal{T}_{\pi}))\in\mathrm{H}^{4}_{\mathrm{DB}}(\mathscr{S}_{g}^{\circ},\mathbb{Q}(2))

is supported on a proper closed subset of
\mathscr{F}_{g}^{\circ}
, i.e. the restriction of
{\rm cl}_{\mathrm{DB}}(\alpha-mc_{2}(\mathcal{T}_{\pi}))
is zero on
\pi^{-1}(V)
for some open subset
V\subseteq\mathscr{F}_{g}^{\circ}
.

We shall mention that the generalized Franchetta conjecture over
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
can be deduced from the Bloch-Beilinson conjecture. When
X
is defined over a number field, Beilinson conjectured that the rational Chow ring
{\rm CH}^{\ast}(X_{\bar{\mathbb{Q}}})
injects into the DB cohomology
[
4
, Conj. 2.4.2.1]
via the cycle class map
{\rm cl}_{\mathrm{DB}}
.
Note that the universal family
\mathscr{S}^{\circ}_{g}
can be defined over a number field, so whenever Beilinson’s conjecture holds either for
\mathscr{S}^{\circ}_{g}
or for K3 surfaces, one can immediately obtain the generalized Franchetta conjecture over
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
.

Let us conclude this introduction by explaining the ideas of the proof. As cohomological generalized Franchetta conjecture is known, it suffices to calculate the kernel of the map

\mathrm{H}^{4}_{\mathrm{DB}}(\mathscr{S}_{g}^{\circ},\mathbb{Q}(2))\to\mathrm{H}^{4}(\mathscr{S}_{g}^{\circ},\mathbb{Q}(2)),

which lies in
\mathrm{H}^{3}(\mathscr{S}_{g}^{\circ},\mathbb{C})/F^{2}\mathrm{H}^{3}(\mathscr{S}_{g}^{\circ},\mathbb{C})
.
In
[
3
]
, the low-degree cohomology groups (with coefficients) of the moduli stack
\mathscr{F}_{g}
of quasi-polarized K3 surfaces and its special moduli substacks have been completely computed. But
the cohomology groups of
\mathscr{F}_{g}^{\circ}
seem rather difficult to compute due to the complexity of the boundary of
\mathscr{F}_{g}^{\circ}
in
\mathscr{F}_{g}
. To overcome this difficulty, we have to work with the separated substack of some covering of
\mathscr{S}
. In our situation, we consider the separated locus of the moduli stack of quasi-polarized oriented K3 surfaces (which is a double covering of
\mathscr{F}_{g}^{\circ}
) and our proof of Theorem
1.1.1
relies on a dedicated calculation on the cohomology groups of this open substack.

Organization of the paper

In Section
2
and Section
3
, we review the basic theory of Shimura varieties of orthogonal type and moduli stacks of quasi-polarized K3 surfaces. In Section
4
, we introduce the DB cohomology on separated Deligne-Mumford stacks and construct the DB-cycle class map for smooth ones. As expected, we show that it is parallel to the traditional DB cohomology theory for varieties. In Section
5
, we consider the moduli stack of quasi-polarized oriented K3 surfaces and study the separated locus of this stack.
Section
6
is devoted to proving Theorem
1.1.1
. We perform the NL-number computation on families of unigonal and hyperelliptic K3 surfaces. Combined with the results in
[
3
]
, this allows us to show that the fourth DB cohomology group of universal family injects into the Betti cohomology. The assertion of Theorem
1.1.1
follows immediately.

2.
Preliminary: Shimura variety and special cycle

In this section, we recall some basic results on Shimura varieties of orthogonal type which will be used in the proof.

2.1.
Shimura variety of orthogonal type

Let
V
be a non-degenerate quadratic even lattice over
\mathbb{Z}
of signature
(2,n)
and let
G={\rm SO}(V)
be the corresponding special orthogonal group. The group
G(\mathbb{R})
of real points of
G
is isomorphic to
{\rm SO}(2,n)
. Fix a maximal compact subgroup of
K_{\mathbb{R}}\subset G(\mathbb{R})
and let
\mathrm{D}_{V}
be the connected component of
G(\mathbb{R})/K_{\mathbb{R}}
. It is a type IV Hermitian symmetric domain of complex dimension
n
.

Let
\hat{\mathbb{Z}}
be the profinite completion of
\mathbb{Z}
and
\mathbb{A}_{f}=\hat{\mathbb{Z}}\otimes\mathbb{Q}
be the ring of finite adeles. Let
\mathbf{K}\subseteq G(\mathbb{A}_{f})
be the discriminant kernel, i.e. the largest subgroup of
G(\hat{\mathbb{Z}})
that acts trivially on the discriminant group
V^{\vee}/V
. To any compact open subgroup
K\subseteq\mathbf{K}
of
G(\mathbb{A}_{f})
it corresponds a quotient space

{\rm Sh}_{K}(V)=G(\mathbb{Q})\backslash\widehat{\mathrm{D}}_{V}\times G(\mathbb{A}_{f})/K.

We shall simply write
{\rm Sh}(V)
when
K=\mathbf{K}
.

If
K
is neat, e.g. when
K
is sufficiently small, then
{\rm Sh}_{K}(V)
is a smooth quasi-projective variety. In general,
{\rm Sh}_{K}(V)
is not connected. The connected component corresponding to the coset of
\mathrm{D}_{V}\times\{\mathrm{id}\}
is an arithmetic quotient

X_{\Gamma}:=\Gamma\backslash\mathrm{D}_{V}

where
\Gamma=G(\mathbb{Q})\cap K
.
If
V
contains a hyperbolic plane, then one has

{\rm Sh}(V)=\Gamma_{V}\backslash\mathrm{D}_{V},

where
\Gamma_{V}=\mathbf{K}\cap G(\mathbb{Z})
is the stable special orthogonal group.

2.2.
Special cycles on
X_{\Gamma}

Given a vector
{\bf v}\in(V^{\vee})^{r}\subseteq V^{r}(\mathbb{Q})
, we let
U=U({\bf v})
be the
\mathbb{Q}
-subspace of
V(\mathbb{Q})
spanned by the components of
{\bf v}
. Let
\mathrm{D}_{{\bf v}}\subset\mathrm{D}_{V}
be the subset consisting of
2
-planes which lie in
U^{\perp}
.
The codimension
r
natural cycle
Z({\bf v})
on
X_{\Gamma}
is defined to be the image of

(2.1)

\Gamma_{{\bf v}}\backslash\mathrm{D}_{\bf v}\rightarrow\Gamma\backslash\mathrm{D}_{V}.

where
\Gamma_{{\bf v}}
is the stabilizer of
U
in
\Gamma
. For any
\beta\in\mathrm{Sym}_{r\times r}(\mathbb{Q})
of rank
t(\beta)
, we set

\Omega_{\beta}=\{{\bf v}\in V^{r}\;|\;\frac{1}{2}({\bf v},{\bf v})=\beta,\dim U({\bf v})=t(\beta)\}.

which comes with a natural
\Gamma
-action.
For each function
\varphi\in{\rm Hom}((V^{\vee}/V)^{r},\mathbb{C})
, Kudla associates a
special cycle

Z(\beta,\varphi;\Gamma)\in{\rm CH}^{r}(X_{\Gamma})

as some linear combinations of
\lambda^{r-t(\beta)}\cdot Z({\bf v})
with
{\bf v}\in\Omega_{\beta}
(cf.
[
26
]
). Here
\lambda
is the Hodge line bundle on
X_{\Gamma}
. They are compatible with natural pullback maps
\pi:X_{\Gamma^{\prime}}\to X_{\Gamma}
for
\Gamma^{\prime}\subseteq\Gamma
, i.e.

\pi^{\ast}(Z(\beta,\varphi;\Gamma))=Z(\beta,\varphi;\Gamma^{\prime}).

Hence we may write
Z(\beta,\varphi)=Z(\beta,\varphi;\Gamma)
for simplicity. Let us denote by
Z(\beta)
the map

\varphi\mapsto Z(\beta,\varphi)\in{\rm Hom}((V^{\vee}/V)^{r},\mathbb{C})^{\vee}\otimes{\rm CH}^{r}(X_{\Gamma})_{\mathbb{C}}.

Let
{\rm Mp}_{2r}(\mathbb{Z})
be the metaplectic double covering of
{\rm Sp}_{2r}(\mathbb{Z})
and
\rho_{r}
be the Weil representation of
{\rm Mp}_{2r}(\mathbb{Z})
acting on
{\rm Hom}((V^{\vee}/V)^{r},\mathbb{C})
. A celebrated theorem for special cycles on
X_{\Gamma}
is

Theorem 2.2.1

(Kudla’s modularity conjecture)
.

The generating series

(2.2)

\Theta(\tau)=\sum\limits_{\beta\geq 0}Z(\beta)e^{2\pi i\mathrm{tr}(\beta\tau)},~\tau\in\mathbb{H}_{r}

with coefficients in
{\rm Hom}((V^{\vee}/V)^{r},\mathbb{C})^{\vee}\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}{\rm CH}^{r}(X_{\Gamma})_{\mathbb{C}}
is a Siegel modular form of type
\rho_{r}
, weight
\frac{n+2}{2}
and genus
r
in with values in
{\rm CH}^{r}(X_{\Gamma})_{\mathbb{C}}
, i.e.

\Theta(A\tau)=\det(c\tau+d)^{\frac{n+2}{2}}\rho_{r}(A,\pm\sqrt{\det(c\tau+d)})\Theta(\tau),

for all
A=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}a&b\\
c&d\end{array}\right)\in{\rm Sp}_{2r}(\mathbb{Z})
.

Example 2.3
.

If
V
is unimodular, then
V^{\vee}/V
is trivial and the special cycle
Z(\beta)
can be written as the quotient by
\Gamma
of the union of hyperplanes

(2.3)

Z(\beta)=\Gamma\backslash\sum\limits_{{\bf v}\in\Omega_{\beta}}U({\bf v})^{\perp}.

which is a linear combination of
Z({\bf v})
for
{\bf v}\in\Gamma\backslash\Omega_{\beta}
.
In this case, Theorem
2.2.1
reads as the generating series
\Theta(\tau)
is a classical Siegel modular form of weight
\frac{n+2}{2}
and genus
r
.

Another important result concerning the special cycles is the following

Theorem 2.3.1

(
[
3
]
)
.

Let
E
be a
\mathbb{C}
-representation of
\mathrm{O}(V_{\mathbb{R}})
and let
\underline{E}
be the associated local system on
X_{\Gamma}
.
Suppose
n\geq 8
, then we have

(1)

\mathrm{H}^{1}(X_{\Gamma},\underline{E})=\mathrm{H}^{3}(X_{\Gamma},\underline{E})=0
.

(2)

\mathrm{H}^{2i}(X_{\Gamma}),\underline{E})
is spanned by special cycles with coefficients in
\underline{E}
if
i=1
or
2
.

3.
Moduli spaces of quasi-polarized K3 surfaces

In this section, we give an overview of quasi-polarized K3 surfaces and discuss some Noether-Lefschetz cycles.

3.1.
Moduli space of (quasi-)polarized K3 surfaces

A
(quasi-)polarized K3 surface of genus
g\geq 2
is a pair
(S,L)
, where
S
is a K3 surface and
L\in{\rm Pic}(S)
is an ample (resp. nef) line bundle with
L^{2}=2g-2
. The middle cohomology
\mathrm{H}^{2}(S,\mathbb{Z})
equipped with the bilinear intersection form
q(-,-)
is an even unimodular lattice which is isomorphic to the
K3 lattice

\Lambda=U^{\oplus 3}\oplus E_{8}(-1)^{\oplus 2}.

where
U
is the hyperbolic lattice of rank two and
E_{8}
is the positive definite lattice associated to the Lie algebra of the same name. Let
\{e_{1},f_{1}\}
be a standard basis of the first hyperbolic lattice. The primitive part
\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\rm prim}(S,\mathbb{Z}):=c_{1}(L)^{\perp}
has signature
(2,19)
and it is isomorphic to the lattice

\Lambda_{g}:=\left<e_{1}-(g-1)f\right>\oplus U^{\oplus 2}\oplus E_{8}(-1)^{\oplus 2},

by identifying
c_{1}(L)
with the vector
e_{1}+(g-1)f_{1}
.
The Hodge structures on
\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\rm prim}(S,\mathbb{Z})
are parametrized by the period domain
\mathrm{D}_{\Lambda_{g}}
associated to
\Lambda_{g}
.

Let
\mathscr{F}_{g}
be the moduli stack of primitively quasi-polarized complex K3 surfaces of genus
g
and
\mathscr{F}_{g}^{p}\subseteq\mathscr{F}_{g}
the open substack of polarized K3 surfaces of genus
g.
The following result is well-known.

Theorem 3.1.1
.

(cf.
[
20
]
)
The moduli stack
\mathscr{F}_{g}
is a smooth Deligne-Mumford stack of finite type and it is coarsely represented by a quasi-projective variety
\mathcal{F}_{g}
and there is an isomorphism

\mathcal{F}_{g}\to\widetilde{\mathrm{O}}(\Lambda_{g})\backslash\mathrm{D}_{\Lambda_{g}}.

Moreover, the open substack
\mathscr{F}_{g}^{p}
is separated.

More generally, one can consider the moduli space of lattice polarized K3 surfaces, which behaves similarly as
\mathscr{F}_{g}
.

Definition 3.2

(Lattice polarized K3)
.

Let
S
be a projective K3 surface and
\Sigma
a non-degenerate lattice. We say that
S
is a
\Sigma
-(quasi-)polarized K3 surface if there exists a primitive embedding
\iota:\Sigma\to{\rm Pic}(S)
such that the image of
\Sigma
in
{\rm Pic}(S)
under
\iota
contains an ample (resp. nef and big) class.

Let
\mathcal{F}_{\Sigma}
be the coarse moduli space of
\Sigma
-quasi-polarized K3 surfaces. In
[
10
]
, Dolgachev proved that

Theorem 3.2.1
.

(cf.
[
10
, Remark 3.4]
)
Fix a primitive embedding of
\Sigma
into
\Lambda
and consider
\Sigma
as a sublattice of
\Lambda
via this embedding. Then there is an isomorphism

\mathcal{F}_{\Sigma}\cong\Gamma_{\Sigma}\backslash\mathrm{D}_{\Sigma^{\perp}}

where
\mathrm{D}_{\Sigma^{\perp}}:=\{[\sigma]\in\mathrm{D}_{\Lambda}|~q(\sigma,\Sigma)=0\}
is the period domain of
\Sigma
-(quasi-)polarized K3 surfaces and
\Gamma_{\Sigma}:=\{\gamma\in\mathrm{O}(\Lambda)|~\gamma|_{\Sigma}=id\}.

3.3.
Example: Moduli of elliptic K3 surfaces

Let us consider the
U
-quasi-polarized K3 surfaces. If
S
is
U
-quasi-polarized, we have
L_{1},L_{2}\in{\rm Pic}(S)
satisfying
L_{i}^{2}=0
and
L_{1}L_{2}=1
. We may assume that
L_{1}
is effective without fixed component. Then the map

f:S\to|L_{1}|\cong\mathbb{P}^{1}

is an elliptic fibration and the difference
L_{1}-L_{2}
represents a section class of
f
. Conversely, if
f:S\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
is an elliptic fibration with a section
O
, then the fiber class
F
and
F+O
gives a
U
-quasi-polarization. We may also call
S
a
unigonal
K3 surface. Let
\mathcal{F}_{U}
be the coarse moduli space of
U
-quasi-polarized K3 surfaces. According to Theorem
3.2.1
, we have

\mathcal{F}_{U}=\Gamma_{U}\backslash\mathrm{D}_{U^{\perp}}.

Note that the orthogonal complement
U^{\perp}\cong U^{\oplus 2}\oplus E_{8}^{\oplus 2}
is a unimodular lattice.

Let us follow
[
34
, III.2]
to describe the projective models of
U
-quasi-polarized K3 surfaces. Let
\pi:S\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
be the elliptic fibration and
C
a section of
\pi
. Then
S
has a unique Weierstrass model with at worst ADE singularities, i.e. we can think it as the closed subscheme of
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(-4)\oplus\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(-6))
defined by the equation

y^{2}z=x^{3}+axz^{2}+bz^{3}

where
a\in\mathrm{H}^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{1},\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(8))
and
b\in\mathrm{H}^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{1},\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(12))
satisfying certain conditions (cf.
[
33
, Theorem 2.1]
). As shown in
[
33
]
, such equations are naturally parametrized by an open subset of the weighted projective space
\mathbb{P}(2^{(9)},3^{(13)})
equipped with a
{\rm SL}_{2}
-action. Recently, Odaka-Oshima
[
37
]
have shown that there is actually an isomorphism

(3.1)

\mathbb{P}(2^{(9)},3^{(13)})/\!\!/{\rm SL}_{2}\cong\mathcal{F}_{U}^{\ast},

between the GIT quotient and the Baily-Borel compactification of
\mathcal{F}_{U}
.

There is another description of the projective model of
\pi:S\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
: as

\pi_{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{S}(2C)\cong\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(-4),

the natural surjection
\pi^{\ast}\pi_{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{S}(2C)\to\mathcal{O}_{S}(2C)
defines a double covering

S\to\mathbf{F}_{4}=:\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(-4)).

Its Weierstrass model is a double covering of
\mathbf{F}_{4}
branched over the trisection
x^{3}+axz^{2}+bz^{3}=0
and the section
z=0
. Conversely, Let
A=\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(-4))
and
F
a ruling of
\mathbf{F}_{4}
. Choose
B\in|3A+12F|
which is disjoint from
A
and smooth, then the double covering of
\mathbf{F}_{4}
branched over
(A+B)
is a unigonal K3 surface.

3.4.
Noether-Lefschetz loci on
\mathcal{F}_{g}

The Noether-Lefschetz (NL) locus
\mathcal{N}\mathcal{L}^{1}(\mathcal{F}_{g})\subset\mathcal{F}_{g}
is the locus parametrizing
K3
surfaces in
\mathcal{F}_{g}
with Picard number greater than
1
. It is a union of countably many divisors defined as follows: given
d,n\in\mathbb{Z}
, the
NL-divisor

\mathcal{H}_{d,n}^{g}\subset\mathcal{F}_{g}
is the locus of quasi-polarized K3 surfaces
(S,L)\in\mathcal{F}_{g}
whose Picard lattice
{\rm Pic}(S)
contains a rank two sublattice

(3.2)

\begin{array}[]{c|c|c}&L&\alpha\\
\hline\cr L&2g-2&d\\
\hline\cr\alpha&d&n\end{array}

where
\alpha\in{\rm Pic}(S)
. In general,
\mathcal{H}_{d,n}^{g}
is not irreducible and one can define the so called
irreducible NL-divisor

\mathcal{P}_{d,n}^{g}
by requiring the lattice (
3.2
) to be primitive.
As divisors,
\mathcal{H}_{d,n}^{g}
can be written as a linear combination of primitive NL divisors via the triangulated relation

(3.3)

\mathcal{H}^{g}_{d,n}=\sum\limits_{(d_{i},n_{i})}\mu_{d_{i},n_{i}}\mathcal{P}^{g}_{d_{i},n_{i}}

where
(d_{i},n_{i})
runs over
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
modulo the relation
(d,n)\sim(d^{\prime},n^{\prime})
if

(3.4)

d^{2}-2n(g-1)=(d^{\prime})^{2}-2n^{\prime}(g-1)\quad\hbox{and}\quad d\equiv d^{\prime}\mod 2g-2;

the coefficient
\mu_{d_{i},g_{i}}\in\{0,1,2\}
is the number of integer solutions
(x,y)
of the equations

(d^{2}_{i}-2n_{i}(g-1))x^{2}=d^{2}-2n(g-1);~(2g-2)y=d-xn_{i}.

Let
\Delta_{d,g}
be the determinant of the matrix (
3.2
) and let
\varpi
be the generator of the cyclic group
\Lambda_{g}^{\vee}/\Lambda_{g}
. According to
[
31
, Lemma 3]
, the projection
\overline{\alpha}
of
\alpha
to
\mathrm{H}^{2}_{prim}(S,\mathbb{Z})
is a vector with norm

\frac{\Delta_{d,g}}{4g-4}
and the image of
\overline{\alpha}
in
\Lambda^{\vee}_{g}/\Lambda_{g}
is
d\varpi
, thus
the NL divisor
\mathcal{H}^{g}_{d,n}
can be identified as the special cycle of codimension one

(3.5)

\mathcal{Z}(\frac{\Delta_{d,g}}{4g-4},d):=\tilde{\mathrm{O}}(\Lambda_{g})\backslash\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v^{2}=\frac{\Delta_{d,g}}{4g-4},\\
v\equiv d\varpi\mod\Lambda_{g}\end{subarray}}v^{\perp}

Note that
\mathcal{Z}(\frac{\Delta_{d,g}}{4g-4},d)
has multiplicity two if
2d\varpi=0
in
\Lambda_{g}^{\vee}/\Lambda_{g}
because
v
and
-v
will both occur in taking the sum.

Similarly, one can define the higher codimensional Noether-Lefschetz loci
\mathcal{N}\mathcal{L}^{m}(\mathcal{F}_{g})
parametrizing quasi-polarized K3 surfaces in
\mathcal{F}_{g}
with Picard number greater than
m
. To specify the irreducible components of
\mathcal{N}\mathcal{L}^{m}(\mathcal{F}_{g})
, we define the NL-cycles of codimension
m
on
\mathcal{F}_{g}
as below.

Definition 3.5
.

Let
M
be a symmetric matrix of signature
(1,m)
. We define

\mathcal{H}_{M}^{g}\subseteq\mathcal{N}\mathcal{L}^{m}(\mathcal{F}_{g})

to be the locus of quasi-polarized K3 surfaces
(S,L)\in\mathcal{F}_{g}
for which there exist classes

\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{m}\in{\rm Pic}(S)

such that the gram matrix of (
L,\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{m})
is
M
.

Using the period map, the NL-cycle
\mathcal{H}_{M}^{g}
can be identified as linear combinations of special cycles in the following way: assume
L\cdot\alpha_{i}=d_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}
. Let
\overline{\alpha}_{i}
be the projection of
\alpha_{i}
in
\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\rm prim}(S,\mathbb{Z})
and let
\overline{M}
be the gram matrix of
\overline{\alpha_{i}}
. Let
\Omega_{M}
be the collection of vectors
{\bf v}=(v_{1},\ldots,v_{m})\in\Lambda^{m}_{g}
such that

•

the gram matrix of
(v_{1},\ldots,v_{m})
is
\overline{M}
,

•

v_{i}\equiv d_{i}\varpi\mod\Lambda_{g}
.

Then
\mathcal{H}_{M}^{g}
can be written as the quotient by
\widetilde{\mathrm{O}}(\Lambda_{g})

\widetilde{\mathrm{O}}(\Lambda_{g})\backslash\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{m}\}\\

One can easily see the irreducible components of

3.6.
First example: locus of exceptional K3 surfaces

A typical example is the NL-locus of K3 surfaces in

Proof.

See
[
18

.
∎

Then we have

Proposition 3.8
.

(cf.
[
7

)The complement

The irreducible components of

3.9.

Proposition 3.10
.

\mathcal{H}_{A_{2}}^{g}
is irreducible and

is in one of the following situations:

(1)

if

(2)

if

\mathcal{H}_{A_{1,1}}^{g^{\prime\prime\prime}}

(3)

Proof.

Let us first consider the case

\widetilde{\mathrm{O}}(\Lambda_{g})
as the subgroup of
\mathrm{O}(\Lambda)
fixing the vector
e_{1}+(g-1)f_{1}
, then we just need to classify the

and

. One should note that
\mathcal{P}^{g}_{g-1,\frac{g-2}{2}}\cap\mathcal{P}^{g}_{g-1,\frac{g-2}{2}}=\emptyset

3.8
,
\mathcal{H}^{g}_{A_{1,1}}
is contained in the primitive NL-locus

. Hence up to a
\widetilde{\mathrm{O}}(\Lambda_{g})
-action, we can assume that

is spanned by the vectors

and

and

Denote by

\Lambda_{A_{1}}=\left<e_{1}-(g-1)f_{1}\right>\oplus U^{\oplus 2}\oplus E_{8}\oplus W_{7}

and
t_{1}

W_{7}=t_{1}^{\perp}\subset E_{8}

in

. We are reduced to consider the

-orbits of

, we know that the

-orbits of

. The discriminant group

ii)

For

Furthermore, one can consider the binodal locus and cuspidal locus on the moduli space of lattice polarized K3 surfaces. The following result is straightforward.

Proposition 3.11
.

\mathcal{H}_{U,A_{2}}

4.1.

4.4.
Cohomology on Deligne-Mumford stacks

Let
\mathscr{X}
be a Deligne-Mumford stack of finite type over
\mathbb{C}
. We denote by

the étale site of

to
(U\to\mathscr{X})

Remark 4.5
.

Consider the full subcategory
{\rm\acute{E}}{\rm t}^{\rm sep}(\mathscr{X})
of

(U\to\mathscr{X})
for which

Let
\mathcal{F}

\mathscr{X}_{{\rm\acute{e}t}}

is given by an étale morphism

is equivalent to
\mathscr{X}_{{\rm\acute{e}t}}/U

\mathscr{X}_{{\rm\acute{e}t}}/U

We now review the method to “compute” the cohomology using an atlas. Let

9

[
28

is then a separated simplicial scheme.

Since

\mathscr{X}
, there is a localized topos
\mathscr{X}_{{\rm\acute{e}t}}/X_{\bullet}
and a morphism of topoi

(

[

[

Since

Remark 4.6

C
. If the isomorphism of the pairs
(\mathcal{X}_{K},\mathcal{L}_{K})\cong(\mathcal{X}^{\prime}_{K},\mathcal{L}_{K}^{\prime})
can not be extended to
{\rm Spec~}R
, then the birational map
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{L})\dashrightarrow(\mathcal{X}^{\prime},\mathcal{L}^{\prime})
is an elementary transform associated to
C
(cf.
[
6
, Theorem 2]
). However, the monodromy group of a single elementary transform acts on
\mathrm{H}^{2}(\mathcal{X}_{\eta},\mathbb{Z})
as a reflection, which does not preserve the orientation of the generic fiber. This contradicts to our assumption.
∎

Let
\widetilde{\pi}:\widetilde{\mathscr{S}}_{g}^{s}\to\widetilde{\mathscr{F}}_{g}^{s}
be the universal family. Then
\widetilde{\mathscr{S}}_{g}^{s}
is also a separated Deligne-Mumford stack (non quasi-projective) and we denote by
{\rm CH}^{2}(\widetilde{\mathscr{S}}_{g}^{s})
the rational Chow group. We can state a stacky version of O’Grady’s generalized Franchetta conjecture (GFC) for universal oriented K3 surfaces.

Conjecture 5.6

(GFC for Oriented K3 surfaces)
.

For any cycle class
\alpha\in{\rm CH}^{2}(\widetilde{\mathscr{S}}_{g}^{s})
, there exists
m\in\mathbb{Q}
and an open substack
U\subseteq\widetilde{\mathscr{F}}_{g}^{s}
such that the restriction of
\alpha-mc_{2}(\mathcal{T}_{\widetilde{\pi}})
to
\widetilde{\pi}^{-1}(U)
is zero.

Certainly, Conjecture
5.6
will automatically imply Conjecture
1.1
via applying the push-forward map. The cohomological version of Conjecture
5.6
has also been confirmed in
[
3
]
.

6.
Deligne-Beilinson cohomology of
\widetilde{\mathscr{S}}_{g}^{s}

In this section, we construct some two-dimensional testing families of polarized K3 surfaces and calculate the NL-numbers. Together with the cohomological generalized Franchetta conjecture, this leads to a proof of Theorem
1.1.1
.

6.1.
Vanishing of odd cohomology groups

Based on the vanishing result in Theorem
2.3.1
, let us first give a sufficient condition for the vanishing of
\mathrm{H}^{3}(\widetilde{\mathscr{S}}_{g}^{s},\mathbb{C})
.

Lemma 6.2
.

Let
\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}_{A_{1,1}}
and
\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}_{A_{2}}
be the preimages of
\mathcal{H}_{A_{1,1}}
and
\mathcal{H}_{A_{2}}
in
\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{g}
respectively. Suppose the fundamental classes of irreducible components of
\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}_{A_{1,1}}
and
\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}_{A_{2}}
are linearly independent in
\mathrm{H}^{4}(\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{g},\mathbb{Q})
, then
\mathrm{H}^{3}(\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{g}^{s},\mathbb{C})=0
and
\mathrm{H}^{1}(\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{g}^{s},\underline{\Lambda_{g}}|_{\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{g}^{s}})=0
.

Proof.

Write
Y=\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{g}-\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}^{s}_{g}=\mathcal{H}^{g}_{A_{1,1}}\cup\mathcal{H}^{g}_{A_{2}}
. We first compute
\mathrm{H}^{3}(\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{g}^{s},\mathbb{C})
. Note that there is a topological commutative diagram of exact sequences

(6.1)

\textstyle{\cdots\to 0=\mathrm{H}^{3}(\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{g},\mathbb{C})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\cap~[\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{g}]}
\textstyle{\mathrm{H}^{3}(\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{g}^{s},\mathbb{C})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\cap~[\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{g}^{s}]}
\textstyle{\mathrm{H}^{4}_{Y}(\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{g},\mathbb{C})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\rho}
\scriptstyle{\cap~[Y]}
\textstyle{\mathrm{H}^{4}(\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{g},\mathbb{C})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\cdots\to\mathrm{H}_{35}(\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{g},\mathbb{C})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathrm{H}_{35}(\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}^{s}_{g},\mathbb{C})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathrm{H}_{34}(Y,\mathbb{C})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathrm{H}_{34}(\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{g},\mathbb{C})}

Here, the first row is the classical sequence of cohomology (and with supports in
Y
) associated to the pair
(\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{g},Y)
,
the second row is the standard exact sequence of Borel-Moore homology of the complement
\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{g}^{s}=\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{g}-Y
, the vertical arrows are given by cap products with the fundamental classes.

Note that
\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{g}
and
\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{g}^{s}
are finite quotient of smooth quasi-projective varieties, the cap products
\cap~[\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{g}]
and
\cap~[\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{g}^{s}]
are isomorphisms on all degrees. Hence the map

\cap~[Y]:\mathrm{H}^{4}_{Y}(\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{g},\mathbb{C})\to\mathrm{H}_{34}(Y,\mathbb{C})

is also an isomorphism. The space
\mathrm{H}^{4}_{Y}(\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{g},\mathbb{C})
is spanned by the fundamental classes of irreducible components of
Y
. It follows from Proposition
6.9
that
\rho
is injective. This implies
\mathrm{H}^{3}(\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{g}^{s},\mathbb{C})=0
.

A similar argument shows that
\mathrm{H}^{1}(\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{g},\underline{\Lambda_{g}}|_{\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{g}^{s}})=0
.
∎

6.3.
Families of K3 surfaces in
\mathcal{F}_{g}

Regard
\mathcal{F}_{g}
as the coarse moduli space of polarized K3 surfaces with at worst isolated ADE singularities. Let us construct two-dimensional proper families of polarized K3 surfaces in
\mathcal{F}_{g}
for all
g
.

Hyperelliptic family for
g>1
odd

Let
\Sigma_{1}=\mathbb{P}^{2}
. Let us take
\mathcal{C}_{1}\subseteq\Sigma_{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}
a general member in
|\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}(2)\boxtimes\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}}(-2K_{\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}})|
. Each fiber
\mathcal{C}_{1,t}
of the projection
\mathcal{C}_{1}\to\Sigma_{1}
over a point
t\in\Sigma_{1}
is a curve in
|\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}}(-2K_{\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}})|
with at worst a cusp or two nodes. Consider the double covering

\mathcal{X}_{1}\to\Sigma_{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}

branched over
\mathcal{C}_{1}
, equipped with natural projections to the three factors. The first projection gives a family
\phi_{1}:\mathcal{X}_{1}\to\Sigma_{1}
of surfaces over
\Sigma_{1}
. For any
t\in\Sigma_{1}
, the fiber
\mathcal{X}_{1,t}=\phi_{1}^{-1}(t)
is a double covering of
\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}
branched over the curve
\mathcal{C}_{1,t}
. It is a hyperelliptic K3 surface and it is smooth (resp. with
A_{1}
or
A_{2}
singularity) if and only if the branching curve
\mathcal{C}_{1,t}
is smooth (resp. nodal or cuspidal). Moreover, the family
\mathcal{X}_{1}\to\Sigma_{1}
has a relative polarization

\mathcal{L}_{1}=\phi_{1}^{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}(1)+\pi_{2}^{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(1)+\frac{g-1}{2}\pi_{3}^{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(1),

of odd genus
g
, which induces a map
\Sigma_{1}\to\mathcal{F}_{g}
factoring through the natural map
\Sigma_{1}\to|-2K_{\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}}|
. Conversely, a hyperelliptic K3 surface in
\mathcal{F}_{g}
(
g>1
is odd) with at worst ADE singularities arises as a double covering of
\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}
branching over an integral curve in
|-2K_{\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}}|
.

Hyperelliptic family for
g>2
even

Let
\Sigma_{2}=\mathbb{P}^{2}
. Consider the Hirzebruch surface
\mathbf{F}_{1}=\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(-1))
, we denote by
s
the section of
\mathbf{F}_{1}\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
and
h
the fiber class. Take

\mathcal{C}_{2}\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{2}\times\mathbf{F}_{1}
a general member
in
|\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}(2)\boxtimes\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{F}_{1}}(-2K_{\mathbf{F}_{1}})|
and let
\mathcal{X}_{2}\to\Sigma_{2}\times\mathbf{F}_{1}
be the double covering branched over
\mathcal{C}_{2}
. The projection

\phi_{2}:\mathcal{X}_{2}\to\Sigma_{2}

to the first factor gives a family of hyperelliptic K3 surfaces. The fiber
\mathcal{X}_{2,t}=\phi_{2}^{-1}(t)
is a double covering of
\mathbf{F}_{1}
branched over the curve
\mathcal{C}_{2,t}
. Similarly as before, this is a family of hyperelliptic K3 surfaces which carries a relative polarization

\mathcal{L}_{2}=(\pi_{1}^{\prime})^{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}(1)+(\pi_{2}^{\prime})^{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{F}_{1}}(s+\frac{g}{2}h)

of even genus
g
. Similarly, we have a moduli map
\Sigma_{2}\to\mathcal{F}_{g}
factoring through
\Sigma_{2}\to|-2K_{\mathbf{F}_{1}}|
for a generic choice of
\mathcal{C}_{2}
.

Unigonal family

The construction of the family of unigonal K3 surfaces needs more attentions.
To get a two-dimensional proper family of unigonal K3 surfaces, we follow the construction in
[
29
, §4.2.2]
to proceed as follows:

Let
\Sigma_{3}=(\mathbb{P}^{2})^{*}
be the dual projective plane,
\Phi=\{([L],p)\in(\mathbb{P}^{2})^{*}\times\mathbb{P}^{2}|~p\in L\}
the universal line, and
\pi_{1}:\Phi\to(\mathbb{P}^{2})^{*}
and
\pi_{2}:\Phi\to\mathbb{P}^{2}
the projections.

Consider the fourfold

\mathcal{Y}=\mathbb{P}_{\Phi}(\mathcal{O}_{\Phi}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{\Phi}(-4)),

where
\mathcal{O}_{\Phi}(1)
is the pull-back of
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}(1)
along
\pi_{2}:\Phi\to\mathbb{P}^{2}
. Let
\mathcal{A}=\mathbb{P}_{\Phi}(\mathcal{O}_{\Phi}(-4))
and
\mathcal{F}
the pull-back of a line in
\mathbb{P}^{2}
along
\mathcal{Y}\to\Phi\xrightarrow{\pi_{2}}\mathbb{P}^{2}
. Take a general member

\mathcal{B}\in|3\mathcal{A}+12\mathcal{F}|

such that it is smooth and disjoint from
\mathcal{A}
, and form the double covering
\mathcal{X}_{3}\to\mathcal{Y}
branched over
(\mathcal{A}+\mathcal{B})
. Then the composite

\mathcal{X}_{3}\to\mathcal{Y}\to\Phi\xrightarrow{\pi_{1}}(\mathbb{P}^{2})^{*}

gives a family of unigonal K3 surfaces, denoted by
\phi_{3}:\mathcal{X}_{3}\to\Sigma_{3}
.

6.4.
The NL-number computation

For the three families
\phi_{i}
(
i=1,2,3
) constructed above, we are going to compute the intersection numbers

(6.2)

\Sigma_{i}\cdot\mathcal{H}_{A_{1,1}}^{g}~\hbox{and}~\Sigma_{i}\cdot\mathcal{H}_{A_{2}}^{g}

in
\mathcal{F}_{g}
.

Hyperelliptic family

We first consider the hyperelliptic families. By taking
\mathcal{C}_{1}
and
\mathcal{C}_{2}
generically, we can further assume that all the fibers of
\mathcal{C}_{i}\to\Sigma_{i}
have only trivial automorphisms. Then we have embeddings

\psi_{1}:\Sigma_{1}\to|-2K_{\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}}|~\hbox{and}~\psi_{2}:\Sigma_{2}\to|-2K_{\mathbf{F}_{1}}|.

Let us first consider the case
i=1
, and similar arguments will apply to the case
i=2
. Denote by

\mathcal{A}_{1,1}\subseteq|-2K_{\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}}|
and
\mathcal{A}_{2}\subseteq|-2K_{\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}}|

the Zariski closure of the binodal locus and respectively the cuspidal locus in
|-2K_{\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}}|\cong\mathbb{P}^{24}
which parametrize curves in
|-2K_{\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}}|
with at worst two nodes (resp. a cusp). It is known that
\mathcal{A}_{1,1}
and
\mathcal{A}_{2}
are both of codimension two. Then we have

(6.3)

\displaystyle\Sigma_{1}\cdot\mathcal{H}_{A_{1,1}}^{g}

\displaystyle=\Sigma_{1}\cdot\mathcal{A}_{1,1}

\displaystyle\Sigma_{1}\cdot\mathcal{H}_{A_{2}}^{g}

\displaystyle=\Sigma_{1}\cdot\mathcal{A}_{2}.

where the right hand side is taking intersections in
|-2K_{\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}}|
, since by construction the intersection numbers are the numbers of binodal and cuspidal members in the two-dimensional family of the branching curves
\mathcal{C}_{1}\to\Sigma_{1}
respectively. It suffices to compute the degree of
\mathcal{A}_{1,1}
and
\mathcal{A}_{2}
in
|-2K_{\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}}|
. This becomes a classical enumeration problem for singular members in a family of curves, which has been studied extensively in
[
23
]
,
[
24
]
, etc.

By taking a general two-dimensional linear subspace
B=\mathbb{P}^{2}\subseteq|-2K_{\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}}|
, we obtain a family
f:\mathcal{C}\to B
of curves in
|-2K_{\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}}|
called a
net of curves
in
|-2K_{\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}}|
. Let
\Delta_{f}\subseteq B
be the discriminant curve of
f
, i.e. the locus in
B
over which the fibers are singular. Suppose that
f
has at worst cuspidal or binodal fibers, and let
a_{2}(f)
and
a_{1,1}(f)
be the numbers of cuspidal and binodal fibers of
f
respectively. In this case, we have

\deg(\mathcal{A}_{2})=a_{2}(f)
and
\deg(\mathcal{A}_{1,1})=a_{1,1}(f)
.

By our assumption, the discriminant curve
\Delta_{f}
has only nodes and cusps as its singularities and
a_{2}(f)
is equal to the number of cusps of
\Delta_{f}
while
a_{1,1}(f)
is equal to the number of nodes of
\Delta_{f}
. Then we have the following explicit formulae which compute
a_{1,1}(f)
and
a_{2}(f)
.

Proposition 6.5
.

(cf.
[
11
, §11.4.4]
)
Let
S
be a smooth projective surface and
L
a very ample line bundle on
S
. Let
f:\mathcal{C}\to\mathbb{P}^{2}
be a general net of curves in
|L|
whose fibers are at worst cuspidal or binodal. Then the numbers of cuspidal fibers and binodal fibers of
f
are given by

(6.4)

\displaystyle a_{2}(f)

\displaystyle=2g-d+2(e-1)

(6.5)

\displaystyle a_{1,1}(f)

\displaystyle=d-3g-e(e-1)+\frac{3}{2}(e-1)(e-2)

respectively, where
c_{i}=c_{i}(\Omega_{S}^{1})
,
\alpha=c_{1}(\mathcal{L})
,
g=\frac{1}{2}(9\alpha^{2}+9\alpha c_{1}+2c_{1}^{2})+1
,
d=3\alpha^{2}+\alpha c_{1}
and
e=3\alpha^{2}+2\alpha c_{1}+c_{2}
.

Note that for
(S,L)=(\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1},|-2K_{\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}}|)
or
(\mathbf{F}_{1},|-2K_{\mathbf{F}_{1}}|)
, we always have

\alpha^{2}=32,~c_{1}^{2}=8,~\alpha c_{1}=-16,~c_{2}=4.

By applying Proposition
6.5
to a general net of curves in
|-2K_{\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}}|
or
|-2K_{\mathbf{F}_{1}}|
, one can compute that in both cases the numbers of cuspidal and binodal members in such a net are

(6.6)

\displaystyle a_{2}(f)

\displaystyle=216

(6.7)

\displaystyle a_{1,1}(f)

\displaystyle=1914,

respectively. Since the surface
\Sigma_{i}
has degree
4
in the linear system, it follows that

Proposition 6.6
.

For
i=1,2
,
\Sigma_{i}\cdot\mathcal{H}^{g}_{A_{2}}=864
and
\Sigma_{i}\cdot\mathcal{H}^{g}_{A_{1,1}}=7656
.

Now we consider the unigonal family
\phi_{3}:\mathcal{X}_{3}\to\Sigma_{3}=(\mathbb{P}^{2})^{*}
. Let

\psi_{3}:\Sigma_{3}\to\mathcal{F}_{g}

be the induced moduli map. As above, the intersection numbers
\deg(\psi_{3}^{\ast}(\mathcal{H}^{g}_{A_{2}}))
and
\deg(\psi_{3}^{\ast}(\mathcal{H}^{g}_{A_{2}}))
are, by construction, the numbers of binodal and cuspidal members in the two-dimensional family of the branching curves
\mathcal{B}\to\Sigma_{3}
.

M

6.10.
NL-numbers via Kudla’s modularity conjecture

We will provide an alternative way to compute the binodal and cuspidal fibers on a family of unigonal K3 surfaces which uses less classical enumerative geometry. For any two-dimensional nontrivial family of unigonal K3 surfaces (with only ADE singularities)

f:\mathcal{X}\to\Sigma

over a smooth projective surface
\Sigma
, it induces a map
\psi:\Sigma\to\mathcal{F}_{U}\cong\Gamma_{U}\backslash\mathrm{D}_{U^{\perp}}
. To compute the Noether-Lefschetz numbers with respect to
f
, we can applying Theorem
2.2.1
(see also Example
2.3
and Example
3.3
) to
\mathcal{F}_{U}
and get that the generating series

(6.13)

\displaystyle\Theta_{\psi}(\tau):

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{\beta\geq 0}(\deg(\psi^{\ast}Z(\beta)))e^{2\pi i\mathrm{tr}(\beta\tau)}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{k,l,m\geq 0}N_{k,l,m}\tilde{q}^{k}p^{l}q^{m}

is a classical scalar-valued Siegel modular form of weight
10
and genus
2
, where we write
\tau=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}z_{1}&z_{2}\\
z_{2}&z_{3}\end{array}\right)\in\mathbb{H}_{2}
,
\tilde{q}=e^{2\pi iz_{1}}
,
p=e^{2\pi iz_{2}}
and
q=e^{2\pi iz_{3}}
for
z_{1},z_{3}\in\mathbb{H},z_{2}\in\mathbb{C}
. Then we see that all the Noether-Lefschetz numbers can be read off from the Fourier coefficients of some Siegel modular form. The geometric meaning of some coefficients

are given as below (see also Proposition
3.11
):

•

the constant term
N_{0,0,0}=(R^{2}f_{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}})^{2}
is the self-intersection of the Hodge line bundle on
\Sigma
.

•

is the degree of the Hodge line bundle on the discriminant curve
\Delta\subseteq\Sigma
.

•

N_{1,1,1}=\deg(\psi^{\ast}Z(\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}-2&1\\
1&-2\end{array}\right)))
is twice the number of cuspidal fibers.

•

N_{1,0,1}=\deg(\psi^{\ast}(Z(\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}-2&0\\
0&-2\end{array}\right))))
is twice the number of binodal fibers.

Next, due to Igusa’s work, a Siegel modular form of weight

\displaystyle\chi_{10}(\tau)

\displaystyle=\tilde{q}pq\prod\limits_{(r,s,t)>0}(1-\tilde{q}^{r}p^{s}q^{t})^{c(4rt-s^{2})}

\displaystyle=\tilde{q}pq-2\tilde{q}q-16\tilde{q}pq^{2}+\cdots

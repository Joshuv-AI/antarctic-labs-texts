Lower bounds for coefficients of certain cyclotomic polynomials
Ákos Borsányi

Abstract
In this paper I prove a conjecture which gives a lower bound for the largest absolute value of the coefficients of the n n -th cyclotomic polynomial for some n n . Moreover this estimate is essentially sharp.

1
Introduction

The
n
-th cyclotomic polynomial is defined by the next product.

\Phi_{n}(z)=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}l=1\\
(l,n)=1\end{subarray}}^{n}\left(z-\varepsilon^{l}\right),\qquad\text{where }\varepsilon=e^{\frac{2i\pi}{n}}.

It is well known that

\Phi_{n}(z)=\prod_{d\mid n}\left(z^{\frac{n}{d}}-1\right)^{\mu(d)}

(1)

(Möbius’s inversion formula).

We denote by
A(n)
the largest absolute value of the coefficients of
\Phi_{n}(z)
. From (
1
) it is easy to see that if
p
is a prime divisor of
n
then
\Phi_{np}(z)=\Phi_{n}\left(z^{p}\right)
, so
A(np)=A(n)
. Thus, if we investigate
A(n)
we may assume that
n
is square free.
In the sequel we assume that
2<p_{1}<p_{2}<\ldots<p_{k}
are primes and
n=\prod_{l=1}^{k}p_{l}
. In
[
1
]

it is proved that

A(n)\leq\prod_{l=1}^{k-2}p_{l}^{2^{k-1-l}-1}

(2)

moreover I have shown in my thesis
[
2
]
that

A(n)\leq c_{k}\prod_{l=1}^{k-2}p_{l}^{2^{k-1-l}-1}

(3)

where
c_{1}=c_{2}=1
,
c_{3}=c_{4}=\frac{3}{4}
and
c_{k}=\left(\frac{3}{8}\right)^{2^{k-5}}
if
k\geq 5
.

It is not obvious but not so difficult to see that

\prod_{l=1}^{k-2}p_{l}^{2^{k-1-l}-1}\leq n^{\frac{2^{k-1}}{k}-1}

hence

A(n)\leq c_{k}n^{\frac{2^{k-1}}{k}-1}.

(4)

If the primes
p_{l}
are closed to each other, that is
p_{k}-p_{1}
is small then (
4
) is not much weaker than (
3
). But note that by (
2
) the size of
A(n)
is independent of the size of
p_{k-1}
and
p_{k}
. Because of this if
p_{1},\ldots,p_{k-2}
are fixed and
p_{k-1}
and
p_{k}
tends to infinity we get
A(n)=O(1)
and hence there do not exist any constants
d_{k}
for which

A(n)\geq d_{k}n^{\frac{2^{k-1}}{k}-1}

(5)

for every
n
.

On the other hand it has been conjectured (see
[
1
]
) that (
5
) holds for infinitely many
n
, where
\omega(n)=k
and
d_{k}
is fixed for every
k
. The purpose of this paper is to give a proof of this conjecture using the next recent result due to Maynard
[
3
]
. For each
k
positive integer there exists a bound
L_{k}
for which there exist infinitely many
k
-tuples
p_{1}<p_{2}<\ldots<p_{k}\leq p_{1}+L_{k}
consisting of primes.

2
The proof of the conjecture

Let
k
be fixed,
p_{1}<p_{2}<\ldots<p_{k}
and
n=\prod_{l=1}^{k}p_{l}
. By (
1
) for any real
x
for which
x\neq\frac{a\pi}{n}
where
(a,n)\neq 1
we have

\left|\Phi_{n}\left(e^{2ix}\right)\right|=\prod_{d\mid n}\left|e^{2dix}-1\right|^{\mu\left(\frac{n}{d}\right)}

and for any real
x
for which
x\neq\frac{ap_{k}\pi}{n}
where
\left(a,\frac{n}{p_{k}}\right)\neq 1
we have

\begin{split}\left|\Phi_{n}\left(e^{2ix}\right)\right|&=\left|\sum_{l=0}^{p_{k-1}}e^{2p_{1}\cdots p_{k-1}lix}\right|\cdot\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\mid n\\
d\neq n\\
d\neq n/p_{k}\end{subarray}}\left|e^{dix}-e^{-dix}\right|^{\mu\left(\frac{n}{d}\right)}=\\
&=\left|\sum_{l=0}^{p_{k-1}}e^{2\frac{n}{p_{k}}lix}\right|\cdot\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\mid n\\
d\neq n,n/p_{k}\end{subarray}}\left|\sin dx\right|^{\mu\left(\frac{n}{d}\right)}\end{split}

(6)

Denote by
k_{1}
the integer part of
\frac{k}{2}
and set
x=\frac{a\pi}{p_{1}\cdots p_{k-1}}
where for the first time
a
is an arbitary integer relatively prime to
\prod_{l=1}^{k-1}p_{l}
. Let
\varepsilon=e^{\frac{2i\pi}{p_{1}\cdots p_{k-1}}}
. Now by (
6
)

\left|\Phi_{n}(\varepsilon^{a})\right|=p_{k}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\mid n\\
d\neq n,n/p_{k}\end{subarray}}\left|\sin\frac{ad\pi}{p_{1}\cdots p_{k-1}}\right|^{\mu\left(\frac{n}{d}\right)}=\\
=p_{k}\frac{\prod_{m=1}^{k_{1}}\prod_{1\leq i_{1}<\ldots<i_{2m-1}\leq k-1}\left|\sin\frac{a\pi}{\prod_{s=1}^{2m-1}p_{i_{s}}}\right|\cdot\prod_{m=1}^{k_{1}}\prod_{1\leq i_{1}<\ldots<i_{2m}\leq k-1}\left|\sin\frac{ap_{k}\pi}{\prod_{s=1}^{2m}p_{i_{s}}}\right|}{\prod_{m=1}^{k_{1}}\prod_{1\leq i_{1}<\ldots<i_{2m}\leq k-1}\left|\sin\frac{a\pi}{\prod_{s=1}^{2m}p_{i_{s}}}\right|\cdot\prod_{m=1}^{k_{1}}\prod_{1\leq i_{1}<\ldots<i_{2m-1}\leq k-1}\left|\sin\frac{ap_{k}\pi}{\prod_{s=1}^{2m-1}p_{i_{s}}}\right|}.

(7)

Note that in case when
k=2k_{1}
the
\prod_{1\leq i_{1}<\ldots<i_{k}\leq k-1}\left|\sin\frac{ap_{k}\pi}{\prod_{s=1}^{k}p_{i_{s}}}\right|
and the
\prod_{1\leq i_{1}<\ldots<i_{k}\leq k-1}\left|\sin\frac{a\pi}{\prod_{s=1}^{k}p_{i_{s}}}\right|
products are empty.

According to Maynard’s theorem mentioned in the introduction there exists
L_{k}=\colon L
such that

p_{1}<\ldots<p_{k}\leq p_{1}+L_{k}=p_{1}+L

(8)

for infinitely many (
p_{1},\ldots,p_{k}
)
k
-tuples where
p_{1},\ldots,p_{k}
are all primes.

In the sequel we suppose that the primes
p_{l}
(
1\leq l\leq k
) and
L
are satisfying (
8
). For
1\leq l\leq k
we define the integers
j_{l}
by the equality
p_{l}=p_{1}+2j_{l}
. Now we distinguish the next two cases.

Case 1.

j_{2}
is odd i.e.
4\mid p_{1}+p_{2}
.

Case 2.

j_{2}
is even i.e.
4\mid p_{1}+p_{2}-2
.

We carry out the proof in case 1.

Let
f_{l}(p_{1})=\frac{p_{1}^{2l-1}+p_{2}^{2l-1}}{4}=\frac{p_{1}^{2l-1}+(p_{1}+2j_{2})^{2l-1}}{4}
(
1\leq l\leq k_{1}
) and define
a
by
a=\sum_{l=1}^{k_{1}}f_{l}(p_{1})
. The essence of the proof is to investigate the factors of the fraction (
7
). The main observation is that if
a,b
and
M
are integers,
M>0
and
a\equiv b(\mod M)
then
\left|\sin\frac{a\pi}{M}\right|=\left|\sin\frac{b\pi}{M}\right|
.

First of all we shall show that if
m
and
i_{1},i_{2},\ldots\,i_{2m-1}
are fixed and
p_{1}
tends to infinity then
\left|\sin\frac{a\pi}{\prod_{s=1}^{2m-1}p_{i_{s}}}\right|\to 1
. It is easy to see that if
l<m
then
\frac{p_{1}^{2l-1}+p_{2}^{2l-1}}{4\prod_{s=1}^{2m-1}p_{i_{s}}}
tends to
0
and
\lim_{p_{1}\to\infty}\frac{p_{1}^{2m-1}+p_{2}^{2m-1}}{4\prod_{s=1}^{2m-1}p_{i_{s}}}=\frac{1}{2}
. Now let
l\geq m+1
. Then

\displaystyle f_{l}(p_{1})=\frac{p_{1}^{2l-1}+p_{2}^{2l-1}}{4}=\frac{p_{1}^{2l-2}+(p_{1}+2j_{2})^{2l-1}}{4}=

\displaystyle=\frac{p_{1}+(2l-1)j_{2}}{2}p_{1}^{2l-1}+\sum_{t=2}^{2l-1}\binom{2l-1}{t}2^{t-2}j_{2}^{t}p_{1}^{2l-1-t}

that is

f_{l}(p_{1})=\frac{p_{1}+(2l-1)j_{2}}{2}p_{1}^{2l-2}+\sum_{t=2}^{2l-3}\binom{2l-1}{t}2^{2l-3-t}j_{2}^{2l-1-t}p_{1}^{t}.

(9)

Now

\frac{p_{1}+(2l-1)j_{2}}{2}p_{1}^{2l-2}\equiv\frac{p_{1}+(2l-1)j_{2}}{2}\left(p_{1}^{2l-2}-p_{1}^{2l-2m-1}\prod_{s=1}^{2m-1}(p_{1}+2j_{i_{s}})\right)\mod\prod_{s=1}^{2m-1}p_{i_{s}}.

(10)

Observe that the right hand side of the congruence (
10
) is a polynomial of
p_{1}
with intger coefficients. So, because of (
9
) and (
10
) we get
f_{l}(p_{1})\equiv g_{l}(p_{1})\mod\prod_{s=1}^{2m-1}p_{i_{s}}
where
g_{l}
is a polynomial of
p_{1}
with integer coefficients and of degree at most
2l-2
.
Since
g_{l}(x)\in\mathbb{Z}[x]
and furthermore
\prod_{s=1}^{2m-1}p_{i_{s}}=\prod_{s=1}^{2m-1}(p_{1}+2j_{i_{s}})
is a monic polynomial of
p_{1}
with also integer coefficients
g_{l}(p_{1})\equiv h_{l}(p_{1})\mod\prod_{s=1}^{2m-1}p_{i_{s}}
where
\deg h_{l}\leq 2m-2
and the magnitude of the coefficients of
h_{l}
depends only on
k
. Therefore

\left|\sin\frac{a\pi}{\prod_{s=1}^{2m-1}p_{i_{s}}}\right|=\left|\sin\frac{\sum_{l=1}^{k_{1}}f_{l}(p_{1})\pi}{\prod_{s=1}^{2m-1}p_{i_{s}}}\right|=\left|\sin\frac{\sum_{l=1}^{m-1}f_{l}(p_{1})\pi+f_{m}(p_{1})\pi+\sum_{l=m+1}^{k_{1}}h_{l}(p_{1})\pi}{\prod_{s=1}^{2m-1}p_{i_{s}}}\right|

(11)

Since the degree of the polynomial
\sum_{l=1}^{m-1}f_{l}(p_{1})+\sum_{l=m+1}^{k_{1}}h_{l}(p_{1})
is at most
2m-2
,

\lim_{p_{1}\to\infty}\frac{\sum_{l=1}^{m-1}f_{l}(p_{1})+f_{m}(p_{1})+\sum_{l=m+1}^{k_{1}}h_{l}(p_{1})}{\prod_{s=1}^{2m-1}p_{i_{s}}}=\lim_{p_{1}\to\infty}\frac{f_{m}(p_{1})}{\prod_{s=1}^{2m-1}p_{i_{s}}}=\frac{1}{2},

so because of (
11
)
\lim_{p_{1}\to\infty}\left|\sin\frac{a\pi}{\prod_{s=1}^{2m-1}p_{i_{s}}}\right|=1
.

Now consider
\left|\sin\frac{ap_{k}\pi}{\prod_{s=1}^{2m}p_{i_{s}}}\right|
where
1\leq m\leq k_{1}-1
if
k=2k_{1}
and
1\leq m\leq k_{1}
if
k=2k_{1}+1
and
m
and
i_{1},\ldots,i_{2m}
are fixed. Similarly to the previous argument if
l\geq m+1
then
f_{l}(p_{1})\equiv g_{l}(p_{1})\mod\prod_{s=1}^{2m}p_{i_{s}}
where
g_{l}(x)\in\mathbb{Z}[x]
and
\deg g_{l}=2l-2
. So, because
\prod_{s=1}^{2m}p_{i_{s}}=\prod_{s=1}^{2m}(p_{1}+2j_{i_{s}})
is a monic polynomial of
p_{1}
having integer coefficients,
p_{k}f_{l}(p_{1})=(p_{1}+2j_{k})f_{l}(p_{1})\equiv(p_{1}+2j_{k})g_{l}(p_{1})\equiv h_{l}(p_{1})\mod\prod_{s=1}^{2m}p_{i_{s}}
where
h_{l}(x)\in\mathbb{Z}[x]
,
\deg h_{l}\leq 2m-1
and the magnitude of the coefficients of
h_{l}(x)
depends only on
k
. Hence as previously

\lim_{p_{1}\to\infty}\left|\sin\frac{ap_{k}\pi}{\prod_{s=1}^{2m}p_{i_{s}}}\right|=\lim_{p_{1}\to\infty}\left|\sin\frac{p_{k}\sum_{l=1}^{m-1}f_{l}(p_{1})+p_{k}f_{m}(p_{1})+\sum_{l=m+1}^{k_{1}}h_{l}(p_{1})}{\prod_{s=1}^{2m}p_{i_{s}}}\pi\right|=1.

Consider a factor of the denominator of the fraction (
7
) being of the form
\left|\sin\frac{a\pi}{\prod_{s=1}^{2m}p_{i_{s}}}\right|
. Since
a=\sum_{l=1}^{k_{1}}\frac{p_{1}^{2l-1}+(p_{1}+2j_{1})^{2l-1}}{4}=\sum_{l=1}^{k_{1}}f_{l}(p_{1})
can be written as

a=\sum_{l=1}^{m-1}f_{l}(p_{1})+f_{m}(p_{1})+\sum_{l=m+1}^{k_{1}}f_{l}(p_{1})
and as we have seen above that

\sum_{l=m+1}^{k_{1}}f_{l}(p_{1})\equiv g(p_{1})\mod\prod_{s=1}^{2m}p_{i_{s}}
where
g(x)\in\mathbb{Z}[x]
,
\deg g\leq 2m-1
, it can be seen that
a\equiv h(p_{1})\mod\prod_{s=1}^{2m}p_{i_{s}}
, where
h
is a polynomial having degree at most
2m-1
and the size of the coefficients of
h
depends only on
k
. But taking the term
f_{m}(p_{1})
into consideration the leading coefficient of
h
is of the form
b+\frac{1}{2}
where
b\in\mathbb{Z}
so the degree of
h
is in fact
2m-1
(as
b+\frac{1}{2}
cannot be equal to
0
).

Now because of this
\left|\sin\frac{a\pi}{\prod_{s=1}^{2m}p_{i_{s}}}\right|=\left|\sin\frac{h(p_{1})\pi}{\prod_{s=1}^{2m}p_{i_{s}}}\right|=O\left(\frac{1}{p_{1}}\right)
but not equal to
O\left(\frac{1}{p_{1}^{2}}\right)
. Here the constant in the symbol
O
depends only on
k
.

Finally, investigate the factor
\left|\sin\frac{ap_{k}\pi}{\prod_{s=1}^{2m-1}p_{i_{s}}}\right|
where
1\leq m\leq k_{1}
and
1\leq i_{1}<\ldots<i_{2m-1}\leq k-1
are fixed. We take apart
a
in the following way.

a=\sum_{l=1}^{m-2}f_{l}(p_{1})+f_{m-1}(p_{1})+\sum_{l=m}^{k_{1}}f_{l}(p_{1})

(12)

By (
9
) the degree of
p_{k}\sum_{l=1}^{m-2}f_{l}(p_{1})=(p_{1}+2j_{k})\sum_{l=1}^{m-2}f_{l}(p_{1})
is
2m-4
and
\deg(p_{1}+2j_{k})f_{m-1}(p_{1})=2m-2
. Furthermore the leading coefficient of
(p_{1}+2j_{k})f_{m-1}(p_{1})
is of the form
b+\frac{1}{2}
where
b\in\mathbb{Z}
. Now consider the term
\frac{p_{1}+(2l-1)j_{2}}{2}p_{1}^{2l-2}(p_{1}+2j_{k})
of
f_{l}(p_{1})(p_{1}+2j_{k})
where
m\leq l\leq k_{1}
.

\frac{p_{1}+(2l-1)j_{2}}{2}p_{1}^{2l-2}(p_{1}+2j_{k})=(j_{k}p_{1}+(2l-1)j_{2}j_{k})p_{1}^{2l-2}+\frac{p_{1}+(2l-1)j_{2}}{2}p_{1}^{2l-1}\equiv\\
\equiv j_{k}p_{1}^{2l-1}+(2l-1)j_{2}j_{k}p_{1}^{2l-2}-\frac{p_{1}+(2l-1)j_{2}}{2}\left(p_{1}^{2(l-m)}\prod_{s=1}^{2m-1}(p_{1}+2j_{i_{s}})-p_{1}^{2l-1}\right)\mod\prod_{s=1}^{2m-1}p_{i_{s}}

Denote this polynomial of
p_{1}
by
g_{l}(p_{1})
. It is easy to see that
g_{l}(x)\in\mathbb{Z}[x]
.

Since
\prod_{s=1}^{2m-1}(p_{1}+2j_{i_{s}})
is a monic polynomial of
p_{1}
, by (
9
)

(p_{1}+2j_{k})f_{l}(p_{1})\equiv g_{l}(p_{1})+(p_{1}+2j_{k})\sum_{t=0}^{2l-3}\binom{2l-1}{t}2^{2l-3-t}j_{2}^{2l-1-t}p_{1}^{t}\equiv h_{l}(p_{1})\mod\prod_{s=1}^{2m-1}p_{i_{s}}

where
h_{l}\in\mathbb{Z}[x]
and
\deg h_{l}\leq 2m-2
.

According to (
12
) and the previous arguments
ap_{k}=a(p_{1}+2j_{k})\equiv h(p_{1})\mod\prod_{s=1}^{2m-1}p_{i_{s}}
and
\deg h\leq 2m-2
but the leading coefficient of
h
is of the form
b+\frac{1}{2}
(
b\in\mathbb{Z}
) so
\deg h=2m-2
. So we get

\left|\sin\frac{ap_{k}\pi}{\prod_{s=1}^{2m-1}p_{i_{s}}}\right|=\left|\sin\frac{h(p_{1})\pi}{\prod_{s=1}^{2m-1}p_{i_{s}}}\right|=O\left(\frac{1}{p_{1}}\right)

where the constant in the symbol
O
depends only on
k
. Because of the previous remark
\left|\sin\frac{ap_{k}\pi}{\prod_{s=1}^{2m-1}p_{i_{s}}}\right|\neq O\left(\frac{1}{p_{1}^{2}}\right)
.

it is not so difficult to see that the number of the factors in the denominator of (
7
) is
2^{k-1}-1
, so by (
7
)
|\Phi_{n}(\varepsilon^{a})|>c_{k}p_{1}^{2^{k-1}}
for some
c_{k}
.

Finally because
nA(n)\geq(\varphi(n)+1)A(n)\geq|\Phi_{n}(\varepsilon^{a})|>c_{k}p_{1}^{2^{k-1}}
,

A(n)>d_{k}n^{\frac{2^{k-1}}{k}-1}

for some
d_{k}
and this completes the proof of the conjecture in case 1.

The beginning of the proof in case 2.
For
1\leq l\leq k_{1}
let

f_{l}(p_{1})=\frac{p_{1}^{2l-1}+(p_{2}-2)^{2l-1}}{4}=\frac{p_{1}^{2l-1}+(p_{1}+2(j_{2}-1))^{2l-1}}{4}

and
a=\sum_{l=1}^{k_{1}}f_{l}(p_{1})
. Now

f_{l}(p_{1})=\frac{p_{1}+(2l-1)(j_{2}-1)}{2}p_{1}^{2l-2}+\sum_{t=2}^{2l-1}\binom{2l-1}{t}2^{t-2}(j_{2}-1)^{t}p_{1}^{2l-1-t}=\\
=\frac{p_{1}+(2l-1)(j_{2}-1)}{2}p_{1}^{2l-2}+\sum_{t=0}^{2l-3}\binom{2l-1}{t}2^{2l-3-t}(j_{2}-1)^{2l-1-t}p_{1}^{t}

and from this the proof of the conjecture is the same as in case 1.

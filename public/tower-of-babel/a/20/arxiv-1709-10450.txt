Semiorthogonal decompositions of equivariant derived categories of invariant divisors
Bronson Lim, Alexander Polishchuk

Abstract
. Given a smooth variety X X with an action of a finite group G G , and a semiorthogonal decomposition of the derived category, 𝒟 ⁡ ( [ X / G ] ) \mathcal{D}([X/G]) , of G G -equivariant coherent sheaves on X X into subcategories equivalent to derived categories of smooth varieties, we construct a similar semiorthogonal decomposition for a smooth G G -invariant divisor in X X (under certain technical assumptions). Combining this procedure with the semiorthogonal decompositions constructed in [ PV15 ] , we construct semiorthogonal decompositions of some equivariant derived categories of smooth projective hypersurfaces.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Semiorthogonal decompositions for
{\mathcal{D}}([X/G])

Let
X
be a smooth quasiprojective variety over an algebraically closed field
k
of
characteristic zero. Suppose

is a finite group acting on

by
automorphisms. Then there is a decomposition of the Hochschild homology of the
quotient stack

,

(1.1)

HH_{\ast}([X/G])\cong\bigoplus\limits_{\lambda\in G/{\sim}}HH_{\ast}(X_{\lambda})^{C(\lambda)},

where

is the set of conjugacy classes of

is the centralizer of
\lambda

is the
invariant subvariety of

[
PV15

. In
[

, Theorem
1.1]
, the authors show that the decomposition (
1.1
)
has a motivic origin in an appropriate sense, and that a similar decomposition
exists for any additive invariant of dg-categories. In

[
BGLL17
]
a
related decomposition of the equivariant zeta function is given.

In the case when the geometric quotient

is smooth one can
identify

with

(see
[

Conjecture A

[
PV15

Assume a finite group

This conjecture was verified in

Because of the results mentioned above on the analogs of the decomposition (

In all known cases of Conjecture A, the semiorthogonal decompositions are

{\mathcal{D}}(X/G)

i.e., the Fourier-Mukai kernels giving the components of the semiorthogonal decomposition

Let us set for brevity
\overline{X}=X/G

For each conjugacy class
\lambda

the corresponding invariant locus. We set
\overline{X}_{\lambda}=X_{\lambda}/C(\lambda)
(the geometric quotient),

(1.2)

\overline{Z}_{\lambda}=\overline{X}_{\lambda}\times_{\overline{X}}X.

Note that
\overline{Z}_{\lambda}
is equipped with a natural

For example, for

Definition 1.1.1
.

Let us say that the action of a finite group

1

1.2.
Restricting (MSOD) to
G
-invariant divisors and application to

-invariant hypersurfaces

-invariant divisors. Namely, we assume that the action of

on

We will impose the following assumption on a divisor

(
\ast
) for every

.

Assume that the pair

\ast

To get applications of this theorem, one should start with some pairs

We mostly focus on the case of the

Note that in this case the conjugacy classes

\lambda

Theorem 1.2.2
.

Let

S_{n}
-invariant homogeneous polynomial on

2.
Semiorthogonal decompositions and base change for stacks

In this section, we will prove a version of Kuznetsov’s base change for semiorthogonal decompositions
of derived categories of stacks.

2.1.
Semiorthogonal decompositions

Recall that a
semiorthogonal decomposition
of a triangulated category

\mathcal{T}
is a pair
\mathcal{A,B}
of full triangulated subcategories
of
\mathcal{T}
such that
\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{B,A})=0
,
and every object
t\in\mathcal{T}
fits into an exact triangle

b\to t\to a\to b[1]

where
a\in\mathcal{A},b\in\mathcal{B}
. In this case, we write

\mathcal{T}=\langle\mathcal{A,B}\rangle
. We can iterate this definition to
get semiorthogonal decompositions with any finite number of components

\mathcal{A}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{A}_{n}
and we write

\mathcal{T}=\langle\mathcal{A}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{A}_{n}\rangle.

For an overview of semiorthogonal
decompositions in algebraic geometry, see

[
BO02
,
Bri06
]
.

2.2.
Fourier-Mukai functors

Recall that following
[
Kre09
]
, we call a DM-stack
{\mathcal{X}}

quasiprojective
if it has quasiprojective coarse moduli space and
is a global quotient of a quasiprojective scheme by a reductive algebraic group.
For example, the quotient stack
[X/G]
, where
G
is a finite group
acting on a quasiprojective scheme
X
, satisfies these conditions and its coarse moduli space is
X/G
.
By
[
Kre09
, Prop. 5.1]
, such a stack has the resolution property, i.e., every coherent sheaf
on it admits a surjective morphism from a vector bundle. Also, such a stack has an affine diagonal.
We denote by
{\mathcal{D}}({\mathcal{X}})
the bounded derived category of coherent sheaves on
{\mathcal{X}}
and by

\operatorname{Perf}({\mathcal{X}})\subset{\mathcal{D}}({\mathcal{X}})
the perfect derived category.

An object

\mathcal{K}\in\mathcal{D}({\mathcal{X}}\times{\mathcal{Y}})
, whose support is proper over
{\mathcal{Y}}
,
gives rise to an exact functor
\Phi_{\mathcal{K}}\colon\operatorname{Perf}({\mathcal{X}})\to\mathcal{D}({\mathcal{Y}})
defined by

\Phi_{\mathcal{K}}(F)=\pi_{{\mathcal{Y}}\ast}(\pi_{\mathcal{X}}^{\ast}F\otimes\mathcal{K}),

where
\pi_{\mathcal{X}}:{\mathcal{X}}\times{\mathcal{Y}}\to{\mathcal{X}}
and
\pi_{\mathcal{Y}}:{\mathcal{X}}\times{\mathcal{Y}}\to{\mathcal{Y}}
are the projections.
We will refer to
\mathcal{K}
as a
Fourier-Mukai kernel
and

\Phi_{\mathcal{K}}
a
Fourier-Mukai functor
.
Note that this functor also has a natural extension
{\mathcal{D}}_{qc}({\mathcal{X}})\to{\mathcal{D}}_{qc}({\mathcal{Y}})

to unbounded derived categories of quasicoherent sheaves, which has the right adjoint

(2.1)

\Phi_{\mathcal{K}}^{!}(G)=\pi_{{\mathcal{X}}\ast}\underline{\Hom}({\mathcal{K}},\pi_{\mathcal{Y}}^{!}G),

where
\pi_{\mathcal{Y}}^{!}
is the right adjoint to
\pi_{{\mathcal{Y}}\ast}
.

The formalism of Fourier-Mukai functors, e.g., as in
[
Huy06
]
,
extends routinely to the case of smooth DM-stacks (see
[
BZNP17
]
where Fourier-Mukai functors
are considered in a much more general context).

Note that in the case when
{\mathcal{X}}
and
{\mathcal{Y}}
have maps to some DM-stack
{\mathcal{S}}
,
then it is natural to consider relative Fourier-Mukai functors
\Phi_{{\mathcal{K}}}
associated with
kernels
{\mathcal{K}}
on
{\mathcal{X}}\times_{\mathcal{S}}{\mathcal{Y}}
, defined in the same way as above. (One gets the same functor
by considering the usual Fourier-Mukai functor associated with the push-forward of
{\mathcal{K}}
with
respect to the morphism
{\mathcal{X}}\times_{\mathcal{S}}{\mathcal{Y}}\to{\mathcal{X}}\times{\mathcal{Y}}
.)
We refer to such Fourier-Mukai functors as
{\mathcal{S}}
-linear
since they commute with tensoring by the pull-backs
of objects in
\operatorname{Perf}({\mathcal{S}})
(as one can easily see from the projection formula).
Note that the right adjoint functor
\Phi_{\mathcal{K}}^{!}
is also
{\mathcal{S}}
-linear (see
[
Kuz06
, Lemma 2.34]
).
Also, under appropriate assumptions, such relative Fourier-Mukai functors are compatible with pull-backs
under a base change (see Proposition
2.3.3
below).

Remark 2.3.4

The condition that

The following result is similar to (but more special than)

Lemma 2.3.5
.

Assume that
{\mathcal{X}}
,
{\mathcal{Y}}
and
{\mathcal{S}}
are smooth,
{\mathcal{S}}
is separated, the morphisms
{\mathcal{X}}\to{\mathcal{S}}
and
{\mathcal{Y}}\to{\mathcal{S}}
are proper, and
the morphism
{\mathcal{Y}}\to{\mathcal{S}}
is flat.
Let
\Phi_{\mathcal{K}}:{\mathcal{D}}({\mathcal{X}})\to{\mathcal{D}}({\mathcal{Y}})
be the
{\mathcal{S}}
-linear Fourier-Mukai functor associated with
a kernel
{\mathcal{K}}
in
{\mathcal{D}}({\mathcal{X}}\times_{\mathcal{S}}{\mathcal{Y}})
,
and let
\varphi:\mathcal{T}\to{\mathcal{S}}
be a faithful base change for both
{\mathcal{X}}
and
{\mathcal{Y}}
. Assume that
the support of
{\mathcal{K}}
is proper over
{\mathcal{X}}
and that
\Phi_{\mathcal{K}}
is
fully faithful. Then
\Phi_{{\mathcal{K}}_{\mathcal{T}}}:\operatorname{Perf}({\mathcal{X}}_{\mathcal{T}})\to{\mathcal{D}}({\mathcal{Y}}_{\mathcal{T}})
is also fully faithful.

Proof.

First, we claim that the functor
\Phi_{{\mathcal{K}}}^{!}
sends
{\mathcal{D}}({\mathcal{Y}})
to
{\mathcal{D}}({\mathcal{X}})
.
To this end we observe that
\Phi_{\mathcal{K}}
can be computed as the absolute Fourier-Mukai functor
\Phi_{{\mathcal{K}}^{\prime}}
,
where the kernel
{\mathcal{K}}^{\prime}
is given by the push-forward of
{\mathcal{K}}
with respect to the finite
morphism
{\mathcal{X}}\times_{\mathcal{S}}{\mathcal{Y}}\to{\mathcal{X}}\times{\mathcal{Y}}
(finiteness of this morphism follows from the
finiteness of the diagonal morphism for
{\mathcal{S}}
). Since
{\mathcal{X}}\times{\mathcal{Y}}
is smooth, the right adjoint functor

\Phi^{!}_{{\mathcal{K}}^{\prime}}
sends
{\mathcal{D}}({\mathcal{Y}})
to
{\mathcal{D}}({\mathcal{X}})
(as follows from formula (
2.1
)).

Thus, the fact that
\Phi_{\mathcal{K}}
is fully faithful on
{\mathcal{D}}({\mathcal{X}})
implies that the natural morphism

(2.3)

F\to\Phi_{\mathcal{K}}^{!}\Phi_{\mathcal{K}}(F)

is an isomorphism for
F\in{\mathcal{D}}({\mathcal{X}})
.

Now to check that
\Phi_{{\mathcal{K}}_{\mathcal{T}}}
is fully faithful on
\operatorname{Perf}({\mathcal{X}}_{\mathcal{T}})
,
by Lemma
2.3.2
, it is enough to check that the morphism

\widetilde{F}\to\Phi_{{\mathcal{K}}_{\mathcal{T}}}^{!}\Phi_{{\mathcal{K}}_{\mathcal{T}}}(\widetilde{F})

is an isomorphism for objects of the form

\widetilde{F}=\varphi_{\mathcal{X}}^{\ast}F\otimes f_{\mathcal{T}}^{\ast}G
, where
F\in\operatorname{Perf}({\mathcal{X}})
and
G\in\operatorname{Perf}(\mathcal{T})
.
But this easily follows from
\mathcal{T}
-linearity of our functors and from Proposition
2.3.3
(note that

\varphi
has finite Tor-dimension since
{\mathcal{S}}
is smooth):

\Phi_{{\mathcal{K}}_{\mathcal{T}}}^{!}\Phi_{{\mathcal{K}}_{\mathcal{T}}}(\varphi_{\mathcal{X}}^{\ast}F\otimes f_{\mathcal{T}}^{\ast}G)\simeq\varphi_{\mathcal{X}}^{\ast}\Phi_{{\mathcal{K}}}^{!}\Phi_{{\mathcal{K}}}(F)\otimes f_{\mathcal{T}}^{\ast}G,

so the needed assertion follows from the fact that (
2.3
) is an isomorphism.
∎

Suppose we have an
{\mathcal{S}}
-linear semiorthogonal decomposition

\operatorname{Perf}({\mathcal{X}})=\langle{\mathcal{A}}_{1},\ldots,{\mathcal{A}}_{m}\rangle.

Let us define subcategories
{\mathcal{A}}_{i\mathcal{T}}\subset\operatorname{Perf}({\mathcal{X}}_{\mathcal{T}})
by the formula

{\mathcal{A}}_{iT}=\langle\varphi_{\mathcal{X}}^{\ast}A\otimes f_{\mathcal{T}}^{\ast}G\rangle_{A\in{\mathcal{A}}_{i},G\in\operatorname{Perf}(\mathcal{T})}.

The following two theorems are analogs of
[

, Thm. 5.6]

Theorem 2.3.6
.

Suppose
\varphi\colon\mathcal{T}\to{\mathcal{S}}
is faithful
for
f\colon{\mathcal{X}}\to{\mathcal{S}}
. Assume that there is an

{\mathcal{S}}
-linear semiorthogonal decomposition

\operatorname{Perf}({\mathcal{X}})=\langle{\mathcal{A}}_{1},\ldots,{\mathcal{A}}_{m}\rangle.

Then the subcategories
{\mathcal{A}}_{iT}
form a
\mathcal{T}
-linear semiorthogonal decomposition of

\operatorname{Perf}({\mathcal{X}}_{\mathcal{T}})=\langle{\mathcal{A}}_{1\mathcal{T}},\ldots,{\mathcal{A}}_{m\mathcal{T}}\rangle.

As in

, the semiorthogonality

Theorem 2.3.7
.

Suppose
{\mathcal{X}}
and
{\mathcal{S}}
are smooth,
{\mathcal{S}}
is separated, the morphism
f\colon{\mathcal{X}}\to{\mathcal{S}}
is flat and proper, and there is an

{\mathcal{S}}
-linear semiorthogonal decomposition

{\mathcal{D}}({\mathcal{X}})=\langle{\mathcal{D}}({\mathcal{X}}_{1}),\ldots,{\mathcal{D}}({\mathcal{X}}_{m})\rangle,

where for

, the stacks

{\mathcal{X}}_{i}
are smooth, the maps
{\mathcal{X}}_{i}\rTo{f_{i}}{\mathcal{S}}
are proper, and
the embedding functors

\Phi_{i}:{\mathcal{D}}({\mathcal{X}}_{i})\to{\mathcal{D}}({\mathcal{X}})

are given by some kernels
{\mathcal{K}}_{i}
in
{\mathcal{D}}({\mathcal{X}}_{i}\times_{{\mathcal{S}}}{\mathcal{X}})
.
Assume now that

\varphi\colon\mathcal{T}\to{\mathcal{S}}
is a base change, faithful for
f
and for each

.
Set
{\mathcal{X}}_{i\mathcal{T}}={\mathcal{X}}_{i}\times_{\mathcal{S}}\mathcal{T}

{\mathcal{K}}_{i\mathcal{T}}
of
{\mathcal{K}}_{i}
to

and their images give a
\mathcal{T}
-linear semiorthogonal decompositon

Proof.

Let us set
{\mathcal{A}}_{i}=\Phi_{i}\operatorname{Perf}({\mathcal{X}}_{i})
. By Theorem

, we get
a semiorthogonal decomposition of

into the subcategories
{\mathcal{A}}_{i\mathcal{T}}

(note that

Now we observe that for

(see Proposition

and

(and

for

for

An easy example of the faithful base change is restricting to an open subset. In particular, we deduce
that condition (MSOD) is preserved when passing to a
G
-invariant open subset.

Corollary 2.3.8
.

Assume that the pair
(X,G)
satisfies (MSOD), and let
U\subset X
be a
G
-invariant open subset.
Then the pair
(U,G)
also satisfies (MSOD), and the corresponding kernels on

\overline{U}_{\lambda}\times_{\overline{U}}U
are obtained as pull-backs of the kernels on
\overline{X}_{\lambda}\times_{\overline{X}}X
.

Proof.

To deduce this from Theorem
2.3.7
, we observe that
U
is the preimage of the open subset

\overline{U}=U/G\subset\overline{X}
, and
\overline{U}_{\lambda}\subset\overline{X}_{\lambda}
is the preimage of
\overline{U}
under the map

\overline{X}_{\lambda}\to\overline{X}
. Note that the natural map of stacks over
[X/G]
,

[U/G]\to[X/G]\times_{X}U\simeq[X/G]\times_{\overline{X}}\overline{U},

is an equivalence, since it becomes an isomorphism after the base change
X\to[X/G]
.

Thus, we can apply Theorem
2.3.7
to the faithful base change
\overline{U}\to\overline{X}
.
Furthermore, we have

(\overline{X}_{\lambda}\times_{\overline{X}}X)\times_{\overline{X}}\overline{U}\simeq\overline{X}_{\lambda}\times_{\overline{X}}U\simeq\overline{X}_{\lambda}\times_{\overline{X}}\overline{U}\times_{\overline{U}}U\simeq\overline{U}_{\lambda}\times_{\overline{U}}U,

so the new kernels live on the correct spaces.
∎

2.4.
Products

We observe that condition (MSOD) is compatible with products
(see
[
Kuz11
, Corollary 5.10]
for a more general result).

Lemma 2.4.1
.

Let
G
(resp.,
G^{\prime}
) be a finite group acting on a smooth variety
X
(resp.,
X^{\prime}
),
and assume that condition (MSOD) is satisfied for
(X,G)
(resp.,
(X^{\prime},G^{\prime})
).
Then condition (MSOD) is also satisfied for the action of

G\times G^{\prime}
on
X\times X^{\prime}
.

Proof.

Let
(\lambda,\lambda^{\prime})
be a conjugacy class in
G\times G^{\prime}
. The corresponding scheme

\overline{Z}_{\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}}=(\overline{X}_{\lambda}\times\overline{X^{\prime}}_{\lambda^{\prime}})\times_{\overline{X}\times\overline{X^{\prime}}}(X\times X^{\prime})

is naturally identified with
\overline{Z}_{\lambda}\times\overline{Z}_{\lambda^{\prime}}
so we can define the kernel

{\mathcal{K}}_{\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}}
on
\overline{Z}_{\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}}
as the exterior tensor product

{\mathcal{K}}_{\lambda}\boxtimes{\mathcal{K}}_{\lambda^{\prime}}
.
The corresponding functor sends
F\boxtimes F^{\prime}
, where
F\in{\mathcal{D}}(\overline{X}_{\lambda})
,
F^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{D}}(\overline{X^{\prime}}_{\lambda})
,
to
\Phi_{{\mathcal{K}}_{\lambda}}(F)\boxtimes\Phi_{{\mathcal{K}}_{\lambda^{\prime}}}(F^{\prime})
. Thus, computing morphisms between such objects
using the Künneth formula, we deduce that the functor
\Phi_{{\mathcal{K}}_{\lambda}\boxtimes{\mathcal{K}}_{\lambda^{\prime}}}
is fully faithful on objects of the form
F\boxtimes F^{\prime}
,
and hence on all objects. Similarly, we check semiorthogonality
between the images and generation. Thus, we get a semiorthogonal decomposition of
{\mathcal{D}}([X\times X^{\prime}/(G\times G^{\prime})])

with respect to any total ordering of conjugacy classes in
G\times G^{\prime}
compatible with the partial
order
(\lambda_{1},\lambda^{\prime}_{1})\leq(\lambda_{2},\lambda^{\prime}_{2})
if
\lambda_{1}\leq\lambda_{2}
and
\lambda^{\prime}_{1}\leq\lambda^{\prime}_{2}
(where we use the total orders on
conjugacy classes in
G
and
G^{\prime}
corresponding to the original decompositions).
∎

3.1.
Smooth

-invariant divisors

Throughout this section we fix a smooth connected variety
X
with an effective action of a finite group
G
, such that

\overline{X}=X/G
is smooth. We denote by
X^{fr}\subset X
the open subset on which the action of
G
is free.
Recall that
X_{\lambda}\subset X
denotes the
\lambda
-invariant locus in
X
(where
\lambda
runs over a set of representatives
of conjugacy classes in
G
), and
\overline{X}_{\lambda}=X_{\lambda}/C(\lambda)
. Note that the ideal sheaf of
X_{\lambda}
is generated locally
by elements of the form
\lambda^{*}(f)-f

, and with this subscheme structure
X_{\lambda}
is smooth.

For each
\lambda

and every connected component
Y\subset X_{\lambda}
, let us denote by
W(Y)

the quotient of

that acts effectively on

denote the open subset on which

W(Y)
acts freely.

Lemma 3.1.1
.

The morphism
X\to\overline{X}=X/G
is finite flat of degree
|G|
.

Proof.

It is well known that the morphism
X\to X/G
is finite surjective of degree
|G|
(see
[
Mum08
, Ch. II.7]
).
Since
X
and
\overline{X}
are smooth, it is flat by the miracle flatness theorem.
∎

Now let
H\subset X
be a smooth
G
-invariant divisor.
We have the induced action of
G
on
H
, so we can consider varieties
\overline{H}=H/G
,

H_{\lambda}\subset H
and
\overline{H}_{\lambda}=H_{\lambda}/C(\lambda)
.
It is easy to see that

H_{\lambda}=H\cap X_{\lambda},

the scheme-theoretic intersection.

We start by observing that the smoothness of the geometric quotient is preserved
upon passing to a smooth
G
-invariant divisor.

Proposition 3.1.2
.

For a smooth
G
-invariant divisor
H\subset X
, the quotient
\overline{H}=H/G
is smooth.

Proof.

Assume first that
x\in H
is a
G
-invariant point. We can linearize the action in a formal neighborhood of
x
in
X
, so the
divisor
H
will be a
G
-invariant hyperplane. Since
X/G
is smooth at
x
,

G
is generated by pseudo-reflections. Hence, the same is true for the induced action of
G
on
H
, so the quotient
H/G
is
smooth at
x
.

Now let
x\in H
be arbitrary, and let
\mathrm{St}_{x}\subset G
denote the stabilizer subgroup of
x
.
By Luna’s étale slice theorem,

[
Lun73
]
, the map
X/\mathrm{St}_{x}\to X/G
is étale near the image of

x
in
X/\mathrm{St}_{x}
. Since
X/G
is smooth, this implies
X/\mathrm{St}_{x}

is also smooth at
x
. Thus,
H/\mathrm{St}_{x}
is smooth at
x
, by the previous
argument. Since the mapping
H/\mathrm{St}_{x}\to H/G
is étale at the image of
x
in
H/\mathrm{St}_{x}
,
we conclude that
H/G
is smooth at the image of
x
(using
[
Gro67
, Theorem 17.11.1]
).
∎

Corollary 3.1.3
.

For any
\lambda
, if
\overline{X}_{\lambda}
is smooth then
\overline{H}_{\lambda}
is smooth.

Proof.

The scheme
X_{\lambda}
is smooth as the fixed locus of a finite order
automorphism in
X
. Similarly,
H_{\lambda}
is smooth as
H
is smooth and

H_{\lambda}
is the fixed locus of
\lambda
. Thus,

is a smooth divisor in

, so we can apply Proposition
3.1.2
.
∎

3.3.
Subschemes
Z_{\lambda}
in

The content of this subsection is not used anywhere else in the paper. Here we discuss certain natural subschemes of
X_{\lambda}\times_{\overline{X}}X
.
In some situations considered in
[
PV15
]
they are related to the equivariant cohomology of the Springer fibers.

Assume that
G
is a finite group acting on a quasiprojective smooth variety
X
, such that all
\overline{X}_{\lambda}=X_{\lambda}/C(\lambda)

are smooth.

For each
\lambda\in G/{\sim}
, let us define the closed subscheme
Z_{\lambda}\subset X_{\lambda}\times_{\overline{X}}X
by

Z_{\lambda}:=X_{\lambda}\times_{\overline{X}}X,

and let
Z_{\lambda}^{\operatorname{red}}\subset Z_{\lambda}
be the corresponding reduced subscheme. Note that

Z_{\lambda}^{\operatorname{red}}

is the union of the graphs of the embeddings
g:X_{\lambda}\to X
, for
g
running over
G/C(\lambda)
.

2
2

2

Our notation is different from
[
PV15
]
where
Z_{\lambda}
denotes
the reduced subscheme.

It is easy to see that
\overline{Z}_{\lambda}=Z_{\lambda}/C(\lambda)
, where
\overline{Z}_{\lambda}
is defined by (
1.2
).
Also, we have
\overline{Z}_{\lambda}^{\operatorname{red}}=Z_{\lambda}^{\operatorname{red}}/C(\lambda)
since both are subschemes of
\overline{Z}_{\lambda}

defined by a nilpotent ideal and since the quotient of a reduced scheme by a finite group is reduced.

The schemes
\overline{Z}_{\lambda}^{\operatorname{red}}
play an important role in the work
[
PV15
]
:
in the examples considered in that paper (see also Sec.

below),
the kernels of the functors defining the semiorthogonal decompositions of
{\mathcal{D}}([X/G])
are given
by some vector bundles on
\overline{Z}_{\lambda}^{\operatorname{red}}
.

The simplest example below shows that
Z_{\lambda}
and
\overline{Z}_{\lambda}
are typically nonreduced.

Example 3.3.1
.

Let
X=\mathbb{A}^{1}
,
G={\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
acting on
\mathbb{A}^{1}
by
x\mapsto-x
. We can take
t=x^{2}
as a coordinate
on
X/G\simeq\mathbb{A}^{1}
. Then for
\lambda\neq 1
,
\overline{Z}_{\lambda}=Z_{\lambda}\subset\mathbb{A}^{1}
is the subscheme corresponding to the ideal
(x^{2})
.

Note that in the special case
\lambda=1
, we have
\overline{Z}_{1}=X
, which is reduced.
It turns out that the subscheme
Z_{1}=X\times_{\overline{X}}X\subset X\times X
is still reduced (and is equal to the union
of the graphs of all
g\in G
acting on
X
) provided the action of
G
is effective.

Lemma 3.3.2
.

Assume that the action of
G
on
X
is effective, and the schemes
X
and
X/G
are
smooth. Then
Z_{1}
is reduced.

Proof.

Since the projection
X\to\overline{X}
is finite flat, the same is true about the projection
p_{1}:Z_{1}\to X
.
Thus,
p_{1*}\mathcal{O}_{Z_{1}}
is locally free over
\mathcal{O}_{X}
, in particular, it is torsion free as an
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-module.
Furthermore, the fact that the action of
G
is effective implies that
Z_{1}
is reduced over a generic point of
X
.
Hence, the nilradical of
\mathcal{O}_{Z_{1}}
would give a torsion submodule
p_{1*}\mathcal{O}_{Z_{1}}
, so this nilradical has to be trivial.
∎

3.4.
Passing to the quotient stacks

Note that a general theory of inducing semiorthogonal decompositions on quotients of varieties by actions of reductive groups
is considered in
[
Ela12
]
. We need an analogous result where instead of varieties we
consider stacks of the form
[X/G]
.

Namely, assume that condition (MSOD) holds for a pair
(X,G)
.
Assume in addition that there is
a reductive algebraic group
{\mathbb{G}}
acting on
X
, such that the actions of
G

and
{\mathbb{G}}
commute and
[X/{\mathbb{G}}]
is a DM-stack. In particular, the subvarieties
X_{\lambda}
acquire the action of
C(\lambda)\times{\mathbb{G}}

and there is an induced action of
{\mathbb{G}}
on
\overline{X}_{\lambda}=X_{\lambda}/C(\lambda)
and on
\overline{Z}_{\lambda}
. This
action is compatible with the projections to
\overline{X}_{\lambda}
and to
X
.
Assume also that each kernel
{\mathcal{K}}_{\lambda}
in
{\mathcal{D}}([\overline{Z}_{\lambda}/G])

comes from an object
\widetilde{{\mathcal{K}}}_{\lambda}
in
{\mathcal{D}}([\overline{Z}_{\lambda}/(G\times{\mathbb{G}})])
.
In this case each
\widetilde{{\mathcal{K}}}_{\lambda}
defines the Fourier-Mukai functor

\Phi^{{\mathbb{G}}}_{{\mathcal{K}}_{\lambda}}:{\mathcal{D}}([\overline{X}_{\lambda}/{\mathbb{G}}])\to{\mathcal{D}}([X/(G\times{\mathbb{G}})])

that fits into a commutative square

{diagram}

where the vertical arrows are given by forgetting the
{\mathbb{G}}
-action.
In other words,
\Phi^{{\mathbb{G}}}_{{\mathcal{K}}_{\lambda}}
is defined by the same formula as
\Phi_{{\mathcal{K}}_{\lambda}}
, but we view
the result as an object of the
G\times{\mathbb{G}}
-equivariant derived category.

Lemma 3.4.1
.

The functors
\Phi^{\mathbb{G}}_{{\mathcal{K}}_{\lambda}}
are fully faithful and their images give a
semiorthogonal decomposition

{\mathcal{D}}([X/(G\times{\mathbb{G}})])=\langle{\mathcal{D}}([\overline{X}_{\lambda_{1}}/{\mathbb{G}}]),\ldots,{\mathcal{D}}([\overline{X}_{\lambda_{r}}/{\mathbb{G}}])\rangle.

Proof.

For a pair of objects
{\mathcal{F}},\mathcal{G}\in{\mathcal{D}}([\overline{X}_{\lambda}/{\mathbb{G}}])
, we have a commutative square

{diagram}

in which the vertical arrows are isomorphisms since
{\mathbb{G}}
is reductive.
Furthermore, since
\Phi_{{\mathcal{K}}_{\lambda}}
is fully faithful, the bottom horizontal arrow is
an isomorphism. Hence, the top horizontal arrow is also an isomorphism, i.e.,
\Phi^{\mathbb{G}}_{{\mathcal{K}}_{\lambda}}
is fully faithful.

Similarly, if
\Hom(\Phi_{{\mathcal{K}}_{\lambda}}(\cdot),\Phi_{{\mathcal{K}}_{\mu}}(\cdot))=0
then by passing to
{\mathbb{G}}
-invariants, we deduce that

\Hom(\Phi_{{\mathcal{K}}_{\lambda}}^{\mathbb{G}}(\cdot),\Phi_{{\mathcal{K}}_{\mu}}^{\mathbb{G}}(\cdot))=0
. Hence, the semiorthogonality still holds for the images of

\Phi_{{\mathcal{K}}_{\lambda}}^{\mathbb{G}}
.

Finally, to see that the images of
\Phi_{{\mathcal{K}}_{\lambda}}^{\mathbb{G}}
generate
{\mathcal{D}}([X/(G\times{\mathbb{G}})])
, we
observe that the right adjoint functors
(\Phi^{\mathbb{G}}_{{\mathcal{K}}_{\lambda}})^{!}
and
\Phi_{{\mathcal{K}}_{\lambda}}^{!}
to
\Phi^{\mathbb{G}}_{{\mathcal{K}}_{\lambda}}
and

\Phi_{{\mathcal{K}}_{\lambda}}
are still compatible with
the forgetful functors, i.e., we have a commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{D}}([X/G\times{\mathbb{G}}])}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{D}}([\overline{X}_{\lambda}/{\mathbb{G}}])}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{D}}([X/G])}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{D}}(\overline{X}_{\lambda})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\Phi_{{\mathcal{K}}_{\lambda}}^{\mathbb{G}})^{!}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{forg}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{forg}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Phi_{{\mathcal{K}}_{\lambda}}^{!}}

Indeed, for any
F\in{\mathcal{D}}([X/G\times{\mathbb{G}}])
, we can define
(\Phi_{{\mathcal{K}}_{\lambda}}^{\mathbb{G}})^{!}(F)
by
the same formula for
\Phi_{{\mathcal{K}}_{\lambda}}^{!}
(see (
2.1
)) understood in terms of
equivariant categories. Then the above commutative diagram holds and the adjunction follows
from the chain of isomorphisms

\displaystyle\Hom_{{\mathcal{D}}([X/G\times{\mathbb{G}}])}(\Phi_{{\mathcal{K}}_{\lambda}}^{\mathbb{G}}(F),F^{\prime})\simeq\Hom_{{\mathcal{D}}([X/G])}(\Phi_{{\mathcal{K}}_{\lambda}}(F),F^{\prime})^{{\mathbb{G}}}

\displaystyle\simeq\Hom_{{\mathcal{D}}(\overline{X}_{\lambda})}(F,\Phi_{{\mathcal{K}}_{\lambda}}^{!}(F^{\prime}))^{\mathbb{G}}\simeq\Hom_{{\mathcal{D}}([\overline{X}_{\lambda}/{\mathbb{G}}])}(F,(\Phi_{{\mathcal{K}}_{\lambda}}^{\mathbb{G}})^{!}(F^{\prime})).

Now suppose
{\mathcal{F}}\in{\mathcal{D}}([X/G\times{\mathbb{G}}])
is right
orthogonal to the images of
\Phi_{{\mathcal{K}}_{\lambda}}^{\mathbb{G}}
. Then by adjointness,

(\Phi_{{\mathcal{K}}_{\lambda}}^{\mathbb{G}})^{!}({\mathcal{F}})=0

for all
\lambda
. Thus, using the above commutative diagram, we obtain that
\operatorname{forg}({\mathcal{F}})

is right orthogonal to the images of all
\Phi_{{\mathcal{K}}_{\lambda}}
. Using the original semiorthogonal decomposition,
we conclude that
\operatorname{forg}({\mathcal{F}})=0
. But the forgetful functor is conservative, so

{\mathcal{F}}=0
.
∎

Remark 3.4.2
.

The above lemma can also be deduced from the conservative descent theorem of Bergh and Schnürer
[
BS17
]
.

4.1.
Motivic decomposition for

(1\ldots\lambda_{1})(\lambda_{1}+1\ldots\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2})\ldots

Under the identiication (

V_{\lambda}^{fr}=\{(y_{1},\ldots,y_{\ell})\ |\ y_{i}\neq y_{j}\text{ whenever }\lambda_{i}=\lambda_{j},i\neq j\}.

Recall that we have the reduced subscheme

,
invariant under the action of

(see Sec.

).
Explicitly, this is the union of graphs of all maps

The quotient

Let us consider the kernels

.

Theorem 4.1.1

(

.

For each
\lambda

, the functor

\mathcal{D}([\mathbb{A}^{n}/S_{n}])=\langle\mathcal{D}(\overline{V}_{\lambda_{1}}),\ldots,\mathcal{D}(\overline{V}_{\lambda_{r}})\rangle.

on
\mathbb{A}^{n}
and the kernels
(\mathcal{O}_{\overline{Z}^{\operatorname{red}}_{\lambda}})

For each
\lambda

-action on

-action on

.

There is a semiorthogonal decomposition

by first restricting the semiorthogonal decomposition

3.4.1
to the natural

-equivariant structures on the corresponding kernels

-invariant correspondences).
∎

4.2.
S_{n}
-invariant hypersurfaces

Let

-invariant closed subset containing the

\lambda

H(f|_{V_{\lambda}})\cap V_{\lambda}^{fr}\setminus T

{\mathcal{D}}([H(f)\setminus T/S_{n}])=\langle{\mathcal{D}}((H(f|_{V_{\lambda_{1}}})\setminus T)/W_{\lambda_{1}}),\ldots,{\mathcal{D}}((H(f|_{V_{\lambda_{r}}})\setminus T)/W_{\lambda_{r}})\rangle

Proof.

First, we use Corollary

to prove that condition (MSOD), that holds for the pair

. Next, we want to check condition (
\ast
)
for the divisor

to the smooth divisor

∎

The following simple observation will be useful for us.

Lemma 4.2.2
.

For a homogeneous
S_{n}
-invariant polynomial

\lambda

Indeed, consider the morphism

vanishes identically at the

-invariant point

.

Since

∎

Now we are ready to prove Theorem

. Recall that in this theorem we assume that

is a

-invariant polynomial such that

Proof of Theorem

.

We would like to apply Corollary

, the restriction

In the case when the degree of

Now assume that

The semiorthogonal decomposition given by Theorem

is usually
not motivic since its components are derived categories of some quotient stacks.
The biggest component of the semiorthogonal decomposition of

corresponds to the

\lambda=(1^{n})
and is the image of the pull-back functor with respect to the natural morphism of stacks

\pi:[\mathbb{P}H(f)/S_{n}]\to\mathbb{P}H(\overline{f})\subset\mathbb{P}(1,2,\ldots,n),

-torsors and the top horizontal arrow is the coarse moduli map for

on

Example 4.2.3

(

-invariant plane curves)
.

Let

.
Note that

{\rm p}_{2}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})|_{\iota_{\lambda}(1,-1;0,\ldots,0)}\neq 0.

Therefore, the same is true for any power of
{\rm p}_{2}
, and hence,
for a generic
S_{n}
-invariant polynomial of even degree.
∎

In the next proposition we show that some components of the semiorthogonal decomposition
of Theorem
1.2.2
are derived categories of weighted projective space stacks.

Proposition 4.2.5
.

Let
f(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
be an
S_{n}
-invariant homogeneous polynomial of degree
d\leq n

such that
\mathbb{P}H(f)
is smooth, and such that in the expression of
f
as a polynomial in
{\rm p}_{1},\ldots,{\rm p}_{n}

the coefficient of
{\rm p}_{d}
is nonzero.
Then for a partition
\lambda=(1^{r_{1}},2^{r_{2}},\ldots,p^{r_{p}})
, such that

r_{l}\geq d
for some
l
, the stack
[\mathbb{P}H(\overline{f}_{\lambda})]
is isomorphic to
the weighted projective space stack with the weights obtained by removing one weight
d
from the sequence

(1,\ldots,r_{1},1,\ldots,r_{2},\ldots,1,\ldots,r_{p}).

Proof.

Let
y_{1},\ldots,y_{r_{l}},z_{1},\ldots,z_{N}
be the coordinates on

V_{\lambda}
, where
y_{1},\ldots,y_{r_{l}}
are the coordinates corresponding to the parts of
\lambda
equal to
l
,
so that in the embedding
V_{\lambda}\hookrightarrow V
each of
coordinates
y_{1},\ldots,y_{r_{l}}
is repeated
l
times (see (
4.1
)).
Note that the coordinates on
\overline{V}_{\lambda}
are given by
the functions
({\rm p}_{i}(y_{1},\ldots,y_{r_{l}}))_{1\leq i\leq r_{l}}
, as well as some symmetric functions in other groups of variables.
Since
r_{l}\geq d
,
{\rm p}_{d}(y_{1},\ldots,y_{r_{l}})
is one of the coordinates on
\overline{V}_{\lambda}
.

It suffices to check that
{\rm p}_{d}(y_{1},\ldots,y_{r_{l}})
occurs with nonzero coefficient in
f_{\lambda}
. Indeed,
then we can use
f_{\lambda}
to express the coordinate
{\rm p}_{d}(y_{1},\ldots,y_{r_{l}})
in terms of other coordinates on
\overline{V}_{\lambda}
,
which gives our assertion. We have

f(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})=\alpha\cdot{\rm p}_{d}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})+g(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}),

where
g
is a polynomial in
({\rm p}_{i}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}))_{1\leq i<d}
. Hence, the restriction of
g
to
V_{\lambda}
is expressed in
terms of coordinates of weight
<d
on
\overline{V}_{\lambda}
, so it does not contribute to
the coefficient of
{\rm p}_{d}(y_{1},\ldots,y_{r_{l}})
. Furthermore, we have

{\rm p}_{d}(\underbrace{y_{1},\ldots,y_{1}}_{l},\ldots,\underbrace{y_{r_{l}},\ldots,y_{r_{l}}}_{l},z_{1},\ldots)=l\cdot{\rm p}_{d}(y_{1},\ldots,y_{r_{l}})\ \operatorname{mod}({\mathbb{C}}z_{1}^{d}+\ldots+{\mathbb{C}}z_{N}^{d}).

Hence, the coefficient of
{\rm p}_{d}(y_{1},\ldots,y_{r_{l}})
in
f_{\lambda}
is equal to
l\cdot\alpha
. In particular, this
coefficient is nonzero, as required.
∎

Corollary 4.2.6
.

The conclusion of Proposition
4.2.5
holds for any

S_{n}
-invariant homogeneous polynomial
f
of degree
d\leq 3
such that
\mathbb{P}H(f)
is smooth.

Proof.

The case
d=1
is trivial. In the case
d=2
, we have

f=\alpha{\rm p}_{2}+\beta{\rm p}_{1}^{2},

while in the case
d=3
, we have

f=\alpha{\rm p}_{3}+\beta{\rm p}_{1}{\rm p}_{2}+\gamma{\rm p}_{1}^{3},

In both cases
\alpha\neq 0
, since otherwise
f
would be reducible.
Hence, we can apply Proposition
4.2.5
.
∎

In the case of cubic forms in
\leq 6
variables, we obtain from Theorem

1.2.2
the following decompositions of
S_{n}
-equivariant derived
categories.

Proposition 4.2.7
.

Let
f(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
be a generic
S_{n}
-invariant homogeneous cubic
polynomial, where
n\leq 5
. Then
{\mathcal{D}}([\mathbb{P}H(f)/S_{n}])
has a full exceptional
collection. For
n=6
, there is an exceptional collection in
{\mathcal{D}}([\mathbb{P}H(f)/S_{6}])

such that its right orthogonal is equivalent to
{\mathcal{D}}(E)
, where
E

is the elliptic curve given by the cubic
f_{(3,2,1)}
in
\mathbb{P}V_{(3,2,1)}\simeq\mathbb{P}^{2}
.

Proof.

First of all, we observe that for
n\leq 5
, a partition
\lambda
of
n
can have at most two distinct parts, while for
n=6
the only
partition with
3
distinct parts is
(3,2,1)
.

By Corollary
4.2.6
, if
\lambda
has a part of multiplicity

\geq 3
then the corresponding piece in the semiorthogonal decomposition of
Theorem
1.2.2
is the derived category of the weighted
projective space stack, so it has a full exceptional collection (see
[
AKO08
, Sec. 2]
).

Now we claim that all partitions with at most two distinct parts, each of mulitplicity at most
2
, lead to subcategories
generated by exceptional collections. We prove this case by case. Note that the partition
\lambda=(n)
does not contribute to
the semiorthogonal decomposition since
f(1,\ldots,1)\neq 0
.

Case
\lambda=(l,l)
. Then
V_{\lambda}
has coordinates
x,y
and
\overline{V}_{\lambda}
has coordinates
{\rm p}_{1}=x+y
,
{\rm p}_{2}=x^{2}+y^{2}
.
The unique stacky point of the weighted projective line stack
\mathbb{P}\overline{V}_{\lambda}=\mathbb{P}(1,2)
is given by
{\rm p}_{1}=0
.
Note that
{\rm p}_{3}=x^{3}+y^{3}
is divisible by
{\rm p}_{1}
, so
\overline{f}_{\lambda}
vanishes at this point. It follows that

\mathbb{P}H(\overline{f}_{\lambda})
is the union of one point and of one stacky point with the automorphism group
{\mathbb{Z}}/2
.
The derived category of such stacky point splits as the direct sum of two derived categories of the usual point.

Case
\lambda=(l_{1},l_{2})
, where
l_{1}>l_{2}
. Then
f_{\lambda}
is a cubic on the
2
-dimensional space

V_{\lambda}
, with isolated singularity at the origin, so
\mathbb{P}H(f_{\lambda})
is the union of three distinct points.

Case
\lambda=(l,1,1)
with
l>1
or
\lambda=(2,2,1)
. Then
V_{\lambda}
has coordinates
x,y,z
, where
W_{\lambda}=S_{2}

swaps
x
and
y
, so that
\overline{V}_{\lambda}
has coordinates
{\rm p}_{1}=x+y
,
{\rm p}_{2}=x^{2}+y^{2}
and
z
, and
\mathbb{P}\overline{V}_{\lambda}=\mathbb{P}(1,1,2)
.
The cubic
\overline{f}_{\lambda}
should have form

\overline{f}_{\lambda}={\rm p}_{2}(\alpha z+\beta{\rm p}_{1})+C({\rm p}_{1},z),

where
C({\rm p}_{1},z)
is a binary cubic form.
It is easy to see that for generic
S_{n}
-invariant
f
,
one has
\alpha\neq 0
, so we can make the change of variables
z_{1}=\alpha z+\beta{\rm p}_{1}
. Furthermore,

C({\rm p}_{1},z)
is not divisible by
z_{1}
, since
f_{\lambda}
has an isolated singularity at
0
.
Thus, rescaling the variables, we can bring
f
to the form

\overline{f}_{\lambda}={\rm p}_{2}z_{1}+z_{1}Q({\rm p}_{1},z_{1})+{\rm p}_{1}^{3},

where
Q
is a binary quadratic form. Now taking

u={\rm p}_{2}+Q({\rm p}_{1},z_{1})
as a new variable of weight
2
, we get

\overline{f}_{\lambda}=uz_{1}+{\rm p}_{1}^{3}.

It is easy to see that
\mathbb{P}H(\overline{f}_{\lambda})
is isomorphic to the weighted projective line stack
\mathbb{P}(1,2)
.
Namely, there is an isomorphism given by

\mathbb{P}(1,2)\to\mathbb{P}H(\overline{f}_{\lambda}):(t:v)\mapsto(u=v^{3},z_{1}=-t^{3},{\rm p}_{1}=vt).

Case
\lambda=(2,2,1,1)
.
Then we have coordinates
x_{1},y_{1},x_{2},y_{2}
on
V_{\lambda}
, and
W_{\lambda}=S_{2}\times S_{2}

permutes
x_{1}
with
y_{1}
and
x_{2}
with
y_{2}
. Set
{\rm p}_{1}(i)=x_{i}+y_{i}
,
{\rm p}_{2}(i)=x_{i}^{2}+y_{i}^{2}
. Then
the cubic
\overline{f}_{\lambda}
has form

\overline{f}_{\lambda}={\rm p}_{2}(1)z_{1}+{\rm p}_{2}(2)z_{2}+C({\rm p}_{1}(1),{\rm p}_{1}(2)),

where
z_{1}
and
z_{2}
are some linear forms in
{\rm p}_{1}(1),{\rm p}_{1}(2)
. It is easy to see that for generic
f
, the linear forms
z_{1}
and
z_{2}
will be linearly independent, so we can view
{\rm p}_{2}(1),{\rm p}_{2}(2),z_{1},z_{2}
as independent variables. Now
adding to
{\rm p}_{2}(i)
appropriate quadratic expressions of
z_{1},z_{2}
, we can rewrite
\overline{f}_{\lambda}
as

\overline{f}_{\lambda}=u_{1}z_{1}+u_{2}z_{2},

where
u_{1},u_{2},z_{1},z_{2}
are independent variables (
\deg(u_{i})=2
,
\deg(z_{i})=1
).
Thus, we can identify
\mathbb{P}H(\overline{f}_{\lambda})
with
\mathbb{P}(1,2)\times\mathbb{P}^{1}
via the isomorphism

\mathbb{P}(1,2)\times\mathbb{P}^{1}\to\mathbb{P}H(\overline{f}_{\lambda})
,

(t:v),(s_{1}:s_{2})\mapsto(u_{1}=vs_{1},u_{2}=vs_{2},z_{1}=ts_{2},z_{2}=-ts_{1}).

Thus, for
n\leq 6
, all of the subcategories corresponding to
\lambda\neq(3,2,1)

admit full exceptional collections. The remaining subcategory corresponding to
\lambda=(3,2,1)
(for
n=6
)
is equivalent to
{\mathcal{D}}(E)
, where
E
is the elliptic curve given by
f_{(3,2,1)}
.
∎

Remark 4.2.8
.

Using Corollary
4.2.6
we see that for an
S_{n}
-invariant nondegenerate quadric
f
, the components of the semiorthogonal decomposition
of Theorem
1.2.2
are either smooth projective quadrics (for partitions with distinct parts)
or weighted projective space stacks (for the remaining partitions). In particular, in this case

{\mathcal{D}}([\mathbb{P}H(f)/S_{n}])
has a full exceptional collection.

4.3.
Products of curves

First, let us consider the case of an action of a finite group
G
on a smooth curve (we assume that the action is
effective).
Note that in this case the quotient
C/G
is smooth
(since passing to invariants of a finite group preserves normality)
and the stabilizer subgroup
\operatorname{St}_{x}
of every point
x\in C
is cyclic. Let

R=D_{1}\sqcup\ldots\sqcup D_{r}\subset C

be the decomposition into
G
-orbits of the ramification locus of the projection

\pi:C\to C/G
. Then each
D_{i}
is a fiber of
\pi
and the stabilizer of a point in
D_{i}
is isomorphic to
{\mathbb{Z}}/m_{i}{\mathbb{Z}}
.
Then the proof of
[
Pol06
, Thm. 1.2]

implies that for each
i
, there is an exceptional collection of
G
-equivariant sheaves on
C
,

(4.5)

(\omega_{C}|_{D_{i}},\omega^{\otimes 2}_{C}|_{D_{i}},\ldots,\omega_{C}^{\otimes m_{i}-1}|_{D_{i}}),

and if
{\mathcal{A}}_{i}\subset{\mathcal{D}}([C/G])
is the subcategory generated by this collection, then there is a semiorthogonal decomposition

(4.6)

{\mathcal{D}}([C/Y])=\langle{\mathcal{A}}_{1},\ldots,{\mathcal{A}}_{r},\pi^{*}{\mathcal{D}}(C/G)\rangle,

where
\pi^{*}:{\mathcal{D}}(C/G)\to{\mathcal{D}}([C/G])
is the pull-back functor.
More precisely, in
[
Pol06
]
a different decomposition was considered,

(4.7)

{\mathcal{D}}([C/Y])=\langle\pi^{*}{\mathcal{D}}(C/G),{\mathcal{B}}_{1},\ldots,{\mathcal{B}}_{r}\rangle,

with
{\mathcal{A}}_{i}=\omega_{C}\otimes{\mathcal{B}}_{i}
, from which (
4.6
) is obtained by mutation.
Also, in
[
Pol06
]
it was shown that each
{\mathcal{B}}_{i}

is generated by the exceptional collection

(\mathcal{O}_{(m_{i}-1)D_{i}},\ldots,\mathcal{O}_{2D_{i}},\mathcal{O}_{D_{i}}),

which can be mutated into
(\mathcal{O}_{D_{i}},\mathcal{O}(-D_{i})|_{D_{i}},\ldots,\mathcal{O}(-(m_{i}-2))|_{D_{i}})
.
The collection (
4.5
) in
{\mathcal{A}}_{i}
is obtained from the latter collection by tensoring with
\omega_{C}
.

This leads to the following result.

Proposition 4.3.1
.

Let
G
be a finite group acting effectively on a smooth curve
C
. Then condition (MSOD) is satisfied,
where the kernel corresponding to
\lambda=1
is the structure sheaf of the graph of
\pi
.

Proof.

We claim that the semiorthogonal decomposition (
4.6
)
(or (
4.7
)) can be restructured to get
the decomposition required by (MSOD). Namely, (
4.6
) consists of the image of the
pull-back functor
\pi^{*}:{\mathcal{D}}(C/G)\to{\mathcal{D}}([C/G])
, along with
m_{i}-1
exceptional objects supported on
D_{i}
, for

i=1,\ldots,r
. On the other hand, for (MSOD) to hold, for each conjugacy class representative
g\neq 1
,
and each
C(g)
-orbit in
C^{g}
, we need to have one exceptional object in
{\mathcal{D}}([C/G])

supported on the corresponding
G
-orbit in
C
. The fact that the numbers of exceptional objects supported
on each
G
-orbit match was proved in
[
PV15
, Rem. 4.3.2]
.
∎

Using Lemma
2.4.1
we deduce the following

Corollary 4.3.2
.

Let
C_{1},\ldots,C_{n}
be smooth curves, and for each
i
, let
G_{i}
be a finite
group acting effectively on
C_{i}
. Then condition (MSOD) holds for the action of

G_{1}\times\cdots\times G_{n}
on
C_{1}\times\cdots\times C_{n}
.

Example 4.3.3
.

For the standard action of the cyclic group
\mu_{d}
on
\mathbb{A}^{1}
,
the geometric
quotient is isomorphic to
\mathbb{A}^{1}_{d}
, where the subscript
d
indicates the

\mathbb{G}_{m}
-weight, so that the quotient map
\pi\colon\mathbb{A}^{1}\to\mathbb{A}^{1}_{d}
is given by
x\mapsto x^{d}
.
We have a semiorthogonal decomposition

\mathcal{D}([\mathbb{A}^{1}/\mu_{d}])=\langle\mathcal{O}_{p}\otimes\chi^{d-1},\ldots,\mathcal{O}_{p}\otimes\chi,\pi^{\ast}\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{A}^{1}_{d})\rangle,

where
\mathcal{O}_{p}
denotes the structure sheaf of the origin, and

\chi:\mu_{d}\to{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
is the character given by the natural embedding.

Now, for positive integers
d_{1},\ldots,d_{k}
, let us consider the natural
action of
G=\mu_{d_{1}}\times\cdots\times\mu_{d_{k}}
on
\mathbb{A}^{k}
(where the

i
th factor acts on the
i
th coordinate). By Corollary

4.3.2
, we have a motivic semiorthogonal decomposition of

\mathcal{D}([\mathbb{A}^{k}/G])
. We can describe explicitly the components of this
decomposition as follows. The fixed locus of an element of

g=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{k})\in G
is isomorphic to the affine space

\mathbb{A}^{n_{g}}
, where
n_{g}
is the number of trivial components of

g
. The geometric quotient by
C(g)=G
is
\pi_{g}\colon\mathbb{A}^{n_{g}}\to\mathbb{A}^{n_{g}}_{{\bf d}_{g}}
, where
{\bf d}_{g}
is a multi-index giving weights
for the
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-action (
{\bf d}_{g}
is the set of
d_{i}
for which

z_{i}=1
). Let
\iota_{g}\colon\mathbb{A}^{n_{g}}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{A}^{k}
denote the natural embedding. Then the composite functor

\iota_{g\ast}\circ\pi_{g}^{\ast}\colon\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{A}^{n_{g}}_{{\bf d}_{g}})\to\mathcal{D}([\mathbb{A}^{k}/G])

is fully faithful.

For each
i
, let
\zeta_{d_{i}}
be a
d_{i}
th primitive root of unity. For

g=(\zeta_{1}^{m_{1}},\ldots,\zeta_{k}^{m_{k}})\in G
, where
0\leq m_{i}<d_{i}
, we define
the character
\chi_{g}
of
G
by setting
\chi_{g}=\chi_{1}^{m_{1}}\cdots\chi_{k}^{m_{k}}
, where
\chi_{i}\colon G\to{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
is given by
the
i
th projection.

Then the functors giving the semiorhogonal decomposition of
{\mathcal{D}}([\mathbb{A}^{k}/G])

(numbered by
g\in G
) are

(\iota_{g\ast}\circ\pi_{g}^{\ast})\otimes\chi_{g}\colon\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{A}^{n_{g}}_{{\bf d}_{g}})\to\mathcal{D}([\mathbb{A}^{k}/G]),

ordered lexicographically with respect to the reverse order on each set

\{0,\ldots,d_{i}-1\}
. Thus, we have a semiorthogonal decomposition

{\mathcal{D}}([\mathbb{A}^{k}/G])=\langle{\mathcal{D}}(pt)\otimes\chi_{1}^{d_{1}-1}\cdots\chi_{k}^{d_{k}-1},\ldots,\pi^{\ast}{\mathcal{D}}(\mathbb{A}^{k}_{d_{1},\ldots,d_{k}})\rangle.

As before, we can delete the origin in all the affine spaces and pass to

\mathbb{G}_{m}
-equivariant categories. In this way we get a semiorthogonal
decomposition of
\mathcal{D}([\mathbb{P}^{k-1}/G])
indexed by the elements
of
G
. The components of this semiorthogonal decomposition will be the weighted
projective space stacks
\mathbb{P}({\bf d}_{g})
.

We can also apply Theorem
1.2.1
to get, as in Section

4.2
, a semiorthogonal decomposition of
{\mathcal{D}}([\mathbb{P}H(f)/G])
,
where
f
is a
G
-invariant homogeneous polynomial on
\mathbb{A}^{k}
. More
precisely, we have to assume that
\mathbb{P}H(f)
is smooth and that restrictions of

f
to certain coordinate subspaces are nonzero. Namely, in the case when
there exists an index
i
with
d_{i}=1
, we have to assume the
nonvanishing of the restriction of
f
to the subspace where all coordinates
with
d_{i}>1
are set to zero. In the case when all
d_{i}>1
, we have to assume
that the restriction of
f
to each coordinate line is nonzero.

For example, if
d_{1}>1
,
d_{2}=\ldots=d_{k}=1
, and

f=x_{1}^{d_{1}}-g(x_{2},\ldots,x_{k})
, then
\mathbb{P}H(f)
is a cyclic cover of

\mathbb{P}^{k-2}
and our decomposition of
{\mathcal{D}}([\mathbb{P}H(f)/\mu_{d_{1}}])
matches the one
given by Kuznetsov-Perry in
[
KP17
, Theorem 4.1]
.

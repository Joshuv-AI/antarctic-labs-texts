Homological stability for general linear groups over Dedekind domains
Oscar Randal-Williams

Abstract
. We prove a new kind of homological stability theorem for automorphism groups of finitely-generated projective modules over Dedekind domains, which takes into account all possible stabilisation maps between these, rather than only stabilisation by the free module of rank 1. We show the same kind of stability holds for Clausen and Jansen’s reductive Borel–Serre spaces.



1.
Introduction

The title of this note requires explanation, for Charney
[
Cha80
]
has already shown that general linear groups over Dedekind domains enjoy homological stability. Our goal is to show how these results may be obtained, and improved, by using the machinery of cellular
E_{k}
-algebras which we developed together with Galatius and Kupers
[
GKRW18a
]
. More precisely, in Section 18.2 of that paper it was explained how the case of Dedekind domains of class number 1 (i.e. PID’s) may be treated, and here we analyse the general case. Rather than completeness, our motivation is that a new
kind
of homological stability theorem is appropriate when the class number is
>1
: we will establish a generic homological stability theorem of this kind. We will also show that it applies to Clausen and Jansen’s reductive Borel–Serre spaces.

1.1.
Modules over a Dedekind domain

Let
\mathcal{O}
be a Dedekind domain, and
\mathrm{Pic}(\mathcal{O})
denote the abelian group of isomorphism classes of
\otimes
-invertible
\mathcal{O}
-modules (which are the rank 1 projective modules; this group may be identified with the ideal class group of
\mathcal{O}
). The classification of finitely-generated modules over a Dedekind domain has the following consequence: any finitely-generated projective
\mathcal{O}
-module has the form
M\cong\mathcal{O}^{n-1}\oplus L
for some
[L]\in\mathrm{Pic}(\mathcal{O})
. In other words finitely-generated projective
\mathcal{O}
-modules
M
are classified up to isomorphism by the data of

(i)

their rank
n=\mathrm{rk}(M)
, and

(ii)

the isomorphism class
[\Lambda^{n}_{\mathcal{O}}M]\in\mathrm{Pic}(\mathcal{O})
of their top exterior power.

1.2.
Earlier results

Unless otherwise mentioned, we consider integral homology. For an
\mathcal{O}
-module
M
, let
\mathrm{GL}(M)
denote the group of
\mathcal{O}
-module automorphisms of
M
. Charney’s theorem states that for finitely-generated projective
\mathcal{O}
-modules
M
and
N
, the homomorphism
-\oplus\mathrm{Id}_{N}:\mathrm{GL}(M)\to\mathrm{GL}(M\oplus N)

(-\oplus\mathrm{Id}_{N})_{*}:H_{d}(\mathrm{GL}(M))\longrightarrow H_{d}(\mathrm{GL}(M\oplus N))

and an epimorphism for

(with an improvement if

and an epimorphism for
d\leq\tfrac{n}{2}
. His results are for very general rings, but only treat free modules. These methods were extended by Friedrich

to treat

(not even projective!) modules over the same class of rings: in the case of Dedekind domains she shows

1.3.
The homological stability statement

We will prove a generic homological stability theorem in the context of
E_{2}
-algebras, having the following explicit consequence for general linear groups over Dedekind domains.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\mathcal{O}
be a Dedekind domain,
[L]\in\mathrm{Pic}(\mathcal{O})
, and
M
be a finitely-generated projective
\mathcal{O}
-module. Then

(-\oplus\mathrm{Id}_{L})_{*}:H_{d}(\mathrm{GL}(M))\longrightarrow H_{d}(\mathrm{GL}(M\oplus L))

is an isomorphism for
d<\tfrac{\mathrm{rk}(M)-2}{2}
and an epimorphism for
d<\tfrac{\mathrm{rk}(M)}{2}
.

Given the selection of stabilisation maps, one for each element of
\mathrm{Pic}(\mathcal{O})
, it is a little unnatural to formulate a result such as Theorem
1.1
for one stabilisation map at a time. Instead, the symmetric monoidal groupoid of all finitely-generated projective
\mathcal{O}
-modules has homology

\bigoplus_{[M]}H_{*}(\mathrm{GL}(M)),

where the sum is over representatives of each isomorphism class. Direct sum of modules endows this with the structure of a graded-commutative algebra, and recording the rank of a module endows it with an additional
\mathbb{N}
-grading. By the classification of finitely-generated projective
\mathcal{O}
-modules, in homological degree 0 we have an isomorphism of
\mathbb{N}
-graded algebras

\bigoplus_{[M]}H_{0}(\mathrm{GL}(M))\cong\mathbb{Z}[\mathrm{Pic}(\mathcal{O})]/(\rho\cdot\rho^{\prime}-\sigma\cdot(\rho\otimes\rho^{\prime}))=:A_{\mathcal{O}}

where we write
(\mathrm{Pic}(\mathcal{O}),\otimes,\sigma=[\mathcal{O}])
for the abelian group of
\otimes
-invertible
\mathcal{O}
-modules and
A_{\mathcal{O}}
is
\mathbb{N}
-graded by considering each element of
\mathrm{Pic}(\mathcal{O})
to have grading 1.

\mathbb{Z}[\mathrm{Pic}(\mathcal{O})]
denotes the polynomial algebra on the set
\mathrm{Pic}(\mathcal{O})

\bigoplus_{[M]}H_{d}(\mathrm{GL}(M))

has the structure of an
\mathbb{N}

For each degree

\leq 2d+1

1.4.
Reductive Borel–Serre spaces

The spaces
B\mathrm{GL}(M)
can be considered as an unstable approximation to the algebraic
K
-theory space
\Omega^{\infty}_{0}\mathrm{K}(\mathcal{O})
, from which point of view the homological stability results of the previous section can be viewed as estimating the quality of this approximation.

Another unstable approximation to
\Omega^{\infty}_{0}\mathrm{K}(\mathcal{O})
has been recently introduced by Clausen and Jansen
[
CJ24
]
, in the form of the reductive Borel–Serre spaces
|\mathrm{RBS}(M)|
; they are better approximations in the sense that there is a factorisation

B\mathrm{GL}(M)\longrightarrow|\mathrm{RBS}(M)|\longrightarrow\Omega^{\infty}_{0}\mathrm{K}(\mathcal{O}).

Using recent work of Jansen
[
Jan24
]
, we will explain how our general method applies equally well to these spaces, and the exact analogues of Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
hold with
|\mathrm{RBS}(M)|
in place of
B\mathrm{GL}(M)
.

Remark 1.4
.

We will show elsewhere that when
\mathcal{O}
has class number 1 then the
|\mathrm{RBS}(M)|
enjoy homological stability with slope 1, which is much better than the
B\mathrm{GL}(M)
. Perhaps this is also the case for general
\mathcal{O}
; we do not pursue that here.

1.5.
The generic homological stability statement

To formulate our generic homological stability theorem, we assume familiarity with the language and notation developed in
[
GKRW18a
]
. Fix a commutative ring
\mathbbm{k}
, and work in the category
\mathsf{sMod}_{\mathbbm{k}}^{\mathbb{N}}
of
\mathbb{N}
-graded simplicial
\mathbbm{k}
-modules. The statement will make use of the following construction, which axiomatises what we did in the previous section. Let
P=(P,\star,\sigma)
be an abelian group, and define a commutative
\mathbbm{k}
-algebra

A_{P}:=\mathbbm{k}[P]/(\rho\cdot\rho^{\prime}-\sigma\cdot(\rho\star\rho^{\prime})\,|\,\rho,\rho^{\prime}\in P).

As the relations imposed are homogeneous with respect to word-length in the free commutative
\mathbbm{k}
-algebra
\mathbbm{k}[P]
, we may consider
A_{P}
as being
\mathbb{N}
-graded. It has an augmentation
\epsilon:A_{P}\to\mathbbm{k}
by sending all
\rho\in P
to 0. Following
[
GKRW18a
, §12.2.1]
, for a non-unital
E_{2}
-algebra
\mathbb{R}
we write
\overline{\mathbb{R}}
for the unital associative algebra obtained by adding a unit and strictifying the
E_{1}
-structure.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
\mathbb{R}\in\mathsf{Alg}_{E_{2}}(\mathsf{sMod}_{\mathbbm{k}}^{\mathbb{N}})
satisfy
H_{0,0}(\mathbb{R})=0
as well as

(I)

H_{n,d}^{E_{1}}(\mathbb{R})=0
for
d<n-1
, and

(II)

there is an abelian group
(P,\star,\sigma)
such that
H_{*,0}(\overline{\mathbb{R}})\cong A_{P}
as
\mathbb{N}
-graded
\mathbbm{k}
-algebras.

Then for each degree
d
,
H_{*,d}(\overline{\mathbb{R}})
has the structure of an
\mathbb{N}
-graded
A_{P}
-module, and as such it is generated in gradings
\leq 3d
and presented in gradings
\leq 3d+1
. In particular, for any element
\rho\in P\subset H_{1,0}(\overline{\mathbb{R}})
the homotopy cofibre
\overline{\mathbb{R}}/\rho
of the map
\rho\cdot-:S^{1,0}\otimes\overline{\mathbb{R}}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
satisfies
H_{n,d}(\overline{\mathbb{R}}/\rho)=0
for
d<\tfrac{n-1}{3}
.

Suppose that in addition to the above we have

(III)

H_{3,2}^{\overline{\mathbb{R}}}(A_{P})=0
.

Then the
\mathbb{N}
-graded
A_{P}
-module
H_{*,d}(\overline{\mathbb{R}})
is generated in gradings
\leq 3d-1
, and is presented in gradings
\leq 3d+1
, but also
H_{n,d}(\overline{\mathbb{R}}/\rho)=0
for
d<\tfrac{n-1}{2}
.

Suppose that in addition to all the above we have

(IV)

H_{4,3}^{\overline{\mathbb{R}}}(A_{P})=0
.

Then the
\mathbb{N}
-graded
A_{P}
-module
H_{*,d}(\overline{\mathbb{R}})
is generated in gradings
\leq 2d
, and is presented in gradings
\leq 2d+1
.

Remark 1.6
.

The special case
P=\mathbb{Z}/2
has been analysed in much more detail by Sierra
[
Sie22b
, Section 2]
. See also
[
Sie22a
, Theorem 2.4]
.

We also record here the following theorem, which resulted from early attempts to prove a stability range of slope
\tfrac{1}{2}
only under assumptions (I) and (II). It is mainly notable because the stability range
d<\tfrac{n-\sqrt{n}}{2}
in the first part is best possible under assumptions (I) and (II) (see Remark
4.3
). We find this surprising, as we have never seen an honestly non-linear stability range “in nature”.

Theorem 1.7
.

Let
\mathbb{R}\in\mathsf{Alg}_{E_{\infty}}(\mathsf{sMod}_{\mathbbm{k}}^{\mathbb{N}})
satisfy (I) and (II) above, with
\mathbbm{k}
a field of characteristic zero. Then we have
H_{n,d}(\overline{\mathbb{R}}/\rho)=0
for
d<\tfrac{n-\sqrt{n}}{2}
.

If
P
is finite then we also have
H_{n,d}(\overline{\mathbb{R}}/\rho)=0
for
d<\tfrac{n-|P|+1}{2}
.

Proof of Theorem

2.1.
Regularity for
A_{P}

We begin with some homological algebra over
A:=A_{P}
, considered with its
\mathbb{N}
-grading, and with its unique augmentation
\epsilon:A\to\mathbbm{k}
. We write
I:=I_{P}
for its augmentation ideal. From the relations defining
A_{P}
we see that:
A(0)=\mathbbm{k}
,
A(1)=\mathbbm{k}\{P\}
, and more generally for any
n\geq 1
there is an isomorphism
\mathbbm{k}\{P\}\overset{\sim}{\to}A(n)
given by sending
\rho\in P
to
\sigma^{\cdot(n-1)}\cdot\rho
.

For an
\mathbb{N}
-graded
A
-module
M
, we will consider the (bigraded)
\mathrm{Tor}
-groups

H^{A}_{n,d}(M):=H_{n,d}(\mathbbm{k}\otimes^{\mathbb{L}}_{A}M)=\mathrm{Tor}^{A}_{n,d}(\mathbbm{k},M),

and write

h^{A}_{d}(M):=\max\{n\in\mathbb{N}\,|\,H^{A}_{n,d}(M)\neq 0\}.

The module
M
is therefore generated in gradings
\leq h_{0}^{A}(M)
, and is presented in gradings
\leq\max(h_{0}^{A}(M),h_{1}^{A}(M))
.

Lemma 2.1
.

We have
h^{A}_{d}(M)\leq d-1+h_{1}^{A}(M)
for
d\geq 2
.

Proof.

Let
B:=\mathbbm{k}[\sigma]
, a polynomial algebra in one variable, and let
B\to A
send
\sigma
to the symbol of the same name. Via this map we can consider
M
as a
B
-module. There is an equivalence
\mathbbm{k}\otimes^{\mathbb{L}}_{B}M\simeq(\mathbbm{k}\otimes_{B}^{\mathbb{L}}A)\otimes^{\mathbb{L}}_{A}M
.

We may compute
B
-module homology by using the free
B
-module resolution
0\to S^{1,0}\otimes B\overset{\sigma\cdot-}{\to}B\to\mathbbm{k}\to 0
. This shows that
H^{B}_{*,d}(-)=0
for
d\geq 2
, and with the explicit presentation of
A
gives

H^{B}_{n,d}(A)=\begin{cases}\mathbbm{k}&(n,d)=(0,0)\\
\mathbbm{k}\{P\}/\mathbbm{k}\{\sigma\}&(n,d)=(1,0)\\
0&\text{else}.\end{cases}

In particular there is a homotopy cofibre sequence of right
A
-modules of the form

S^{1,0}\otimes\mathbbm{k}\{P\}/\mathbbm{k}\{\sigma\}\longrightarrow\mathbbm{k}\otimes_{B}^{\mathbb{L}}A\longrightarrow S^{0,0}\otimes\mathbbm{k}.

Applying
-\otimes^{\mathbb{L}}_{A}M
and using the equivalence above leads to a long exact sequence

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cdots(\mathbbm{k}\{P\}/\mathbbm{k}\{\sigma\})\otimes H^{A}_{n-1,d}(M)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{B}_{n,d}(M)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{A}_{n,d}(M)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\mathbbm{k}\{P\}/\mathbbm{k}\{\sigma\})\otimes H^{A}_{n-1,d-1}(M)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{B}_{n,d-1}(M)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{A}_{n,d-1}(M)\cdots.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\partial}

For
d\geq 2
, as
H^{B}_{*,d}(M)=0
this gives an injection

\partial:H^{A}_{n,d}(M)\longrightarrow(\mathbbm{k}\{P\}/\mathbbm{k}\{\sigma\})\otimes H^{A}_{n-1,d-1}(M)

so that
h^{A}_{d}(M)\leq h^{A}_{d-1}(M)+1
as required.
∎

Corollary 2.2
.

The algebra
A
is Koszul, i.e.
\mathrm{Tor}^{A}_{n,d}(\mathbbm{k},\mathbbm{k})=0
for
n\neq d
.

Proof.

Applying Lemma
2.1
with

, which has

, shows that
\mathrm{Tor}^{A}_{n,d}(\mathbbm{k},\mathbbm{k})=0

I

,

\mathrm{Tor}^{A}_{n,d}(\mathbbm{k},\mathbbm{k})=0
for

.

Lemma 2.4
.

We have

\mathbbm{k}\otimes_{\overline{\mathbb{R}}}^{\mathbb{L}}\mathbb{M}\simeq(\mathbbm{k}\otimes_{\overline{\mathbb{R}}}^{\mathbb{L}}\mathbb{A})\otimes^{\mathbb{L}}_{\mathbb{A}}\mathbb{M}.

By the second part of Lemma

H^{E_{1}}_{n,d}(\mathbf{RBS})\cong\bigoplus_{\mathrm{rk}(M)=n}{H}_{d-n+1}(\mathrm{GL}(M);\mathrm{St}(M)\otimes\mathbbm{k}).

In particular these groups vanish for
d<n-1
. This verifies axiom (I).

There is a map of
E_{\infty}
-algebras
\mathbf{BGL}\to\mathbf{RBS}
(by consulting Jansen’s construction), which in each grading is induced by the natural maps
B\mathrm{GL}(M)\to|\mathrm{RBS}(M)|
. As these spaces are both connected, we have

A_{\mathrm{Pic}(\mathcal{O})}\cong H_{*,0}(\overline{\mathbf{BGL}})\cong H_{*,0}(\overline{\mathbf{RBS}}),

so
\mathbf{RBS}
also satisfies axiom (II).

Verifying axioms (III) and (IV)

We will verify these axioms for both
\mathbf{BGL}
and
\mathbf{RBS}

\overline{\mathbf{BGL}}\longrightarrow\overline{\mathbf{RBS}}\longrightarrow\mathbb{A}

All three groups vanish for

in these two examples, and the third in fact vanishes for

For

(3.1)

\bigoplus_{\mathclap{\mathrm{rk}(M)=n}}H_{0}(\mathrm{GL}(M);\widetilde{\mathrm{St}}(M)\otimes\mathbbm{k})\to\bigoplus_{\mathclap{\mathrm{rk}(M)=n}}H_{0}(\mathrm{GL}(M);{\mathrm{St}}(M)\otimes\mathbbm{k})\to\mathrm{Tor}^{A}_{n,n}(\mathbbm{k},\mathbbm{k}).

Theorem 3.5
.

The right-hand map in (
3.1
) is surjective.

Proof.

Church, Farb, and Putman
[
CFP19
, §5.2]
have constructed for each finitely-generated projective
\mathcal{O}
-module
M
of rank
n
a certain map

H_{0}(\mathrm{GL}(M);\mathrm{St}(M))\longrightarrow\widetilde{H}_{n-2}(|X_{n-1}(\mathrm{Pic}(\mathcal{O}))|;\mathbb{Z})

and have shown
[
CFP19
, Proposition 5.5]
that it is surjective. We will explain why the right-hand map above is simply the sum, over isomorphism classes of rank
n
projective
\mathcal{O}
-modules, of these maps (tensored with
\mathbbm{k}
): then the claim follows.

To do so it is simplest to put
\mathbb{A}
in the framework of “categories with filtrations” of
[
CJ24
, §7.1]
and rely on the functoriality of that framework. In fact it is awkward to make
\mathbb{A}
fit exactly into that setting, so we make the following observation. It is not actually necessary to have an underlying category to make many of the constructions involving “categories with filtrations”: rather, it suffices to have a collection of objects as well as a class of “short exact sequences”, satisfying certain properties. Among these properties one must be able to compose admissible monomorphisms, and also compose admissible epimorphisms, but for example one never needs to compose morphisms of the two kinds. Formally, one can view this as as a pair
\mathcal{C}=(\mathcal{C}^{mono},\mathcal{C}^{epi})
of categories with the same objects, and with certain pairs of arrows
(a\hookrightarrow b,b\twoheadrightarrow c)
distinguished as “short exact sequences”, again satisfying certain properties. This data suffices to define
\mathscr{M}_{RBS}(\mathcal{C})
as in
[
Jan24
, Definition 6.1]
.

Now define
\mathcal{A}=(\mathcal{A}^{mono},\mathcal{A}^{epi})
to have common set of objects the isomorphism classes of finitely-generated projective
\mathcal{O}
-modules. Let

\mathcal{A}^{mono}([P],[Q]):=\begin{cases}*&\mathrm{rk}(P)<\mathrm{rk}(Q)\text{ or }[P]=[Q]\\
\emptyset&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}=:\mathcal{A}^{epi}([Q],[P]),

so both categories are posets. The short exact sequences are those pairs
([P]\hookrightarrow[Q],[Q]\twoheadrightarrow[R])
such that
[Q]=[P\oplus R]
.

The category
\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{O})
of finitely-generated projective
\mathcal{O}
-modules is a category with filtrations as in
[
CJ24
, Example 7.3 (3)]
, and can be considered in the form
\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{O})=(\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{O})^{mono},\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{O})^{epi})
as above. Sending a module to its isomorphism class then gives a morphism

\phi:(\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{O})^{mono},\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{O})^{epi})\longrightarrow(\mathcal{A}^{mono},\mathcal{A}^{epi}).

This induces a map of
E_{1}
-algebras

(3.2)

\mathbf{RBS}:=\mathscr{M}_{RBS}(\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{O}))\longrightarrow\mathscr{M}_{RBS}(\mathcal{A}).

We can identify the latter as (see
[
Jan24
, Definition 6.1, Notation 6.4]
)

\mathrm{RBS}([P])\simeq\begin{cases}\text{Objects are lists }([Q_{1}],\ldots,[Q_{n}])\text{ of non-zero isomorphism classes}\\
\text{such that }[P]=[\bigoplus_{i}Q_{i}].\\
\text{A morphism }([Q_{1}],\ldots,[Q_{n}])\to([R_{1}],\ldots,[R_{m}])\text{ is an order preserving }\\
\theta:\{1,\ldots,n\}\twoheadrightarrow\{1,\ldots,m\}\text{ such that }[R_{j}]=[\bigoplus_{i\in\theta^{-1}(j)}Q_{i}].\end{cases}

Observe that this is a poset, and the one-element list
([P])
is terminal so
|\mathrm{RBS}([P])|\simeq*
. Thus
\mathscr{M}_{RBS}(\mathcal{A})
has one contractible path component for each isomorphism class of finitely-generated projective
\mathcal{O}
-module, i.e. it is
\mathbb{A}
. The map (
3.2
) is thus identified with the truncation map
\mathbf{RBS}\to\mathbb{A}
.

Now
\partial\mathrm{RBS}([P])
is the full subposet of
\mathrm{RBS}([P])
on all objects other than
[P]
. We will explain how to identify it with the poset of simplices of the the realisation of the poset
X_{rk(P)-1}(\mathrm{Pic}(\mathcal{O}))
defined in
[

sdX_{rk(P)-1}(\mathrm{Pic}(\mathcal{O}))
of this poset.

Recall that the poset
X_{rk(P)-1}(\mathrm{Pic}(\mathcal{O}))
is given by
\{1,2,\ldots,rk(P)-1\}\times\mathrm{Pic}(\mathcal{O})
, with order relation
(r,[L])<(r^{\prime},[L^{\prime}])
if and only if
r<r^{\prime}
. We assign to a list
([Q_{1}],\ldots,[Q_{n}])
in the poset
\mathrm{RBS}([P])
the chain

(\mathrm{rk}(Q_{1}),[\det(Q_{1})])<(\mathrm{rk}(Q_{1}\oplus Q_{2}),[\det(Q_{1}\oplus Q_{2})])<\cdots<(\mathrm{rk}(\oplus_{i}Q_{i}),[\det(\oplus_{i}Q_{i})])

(r_{1},[L_{1}])<(r_{2},[L_{2}])<\ldots<(r_{n},[L_{n}])

([\mathcal{O}^{r_{1}-1}\oplus L_{1}],[\mathcal{O}^{r_{2}-r_{1}-1}\oplus(L_{2}\otimes L_{1}^{-1})],\ldots,[\mathcal{O}^{r_{n}-r_{n-1}-1}\oplus(L_{n}\otimes L_{n-1}^{-1})]).

We can form the diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{|\mathcal{F}(P)\setminus\emptyset|/\!\!/\mathrm{GL}(P)}}

4.
Proof of Theorem
1.7

We require the following algebraic lemma, a consequence of a theorem of Józefiak and Weyman
[
JW88
]
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\mathbb{K}
be a field of characteristic zero,
V
be a
\mathbb{K}
-module, and consider the cdga

C_{\bullet,*}:=\left(\mathrm{Sym}^{*}(V[1,0])\otimes\Lambda^{*}(\mathrm{Sym}^{2}(V)[2,1]),\partial\right)

with additional
\mathbb{N}
-grading denoted
\bullet
, where
\partial
is determined by the identity map
C_{2,1}=\mathrm{Sym}^{2}(V)\to C_{2,0}=\mathrm{Sym}^{2}(V)
and the graded Leibniz rule. Then

H_{n,d}(C_{\bullet,*})=0\text{ for }d<\tfrac{n-\sqrt{n}}{2}.

If
V
is finite-dimensional then
H_{n,d}(C_{\bullet,*})=0
for
d<\tfrac{n-\dim(V)}{2}
. If
n
is a square,
d=\tfrac{n-\sqrt{n}}{2}
, and
\dim(V)\geq n-2d
, then
H_{n,d}(C_{\bullet,*})\neq 0
.

Proof.

Józefiak and Weyman
[
JW88
]
(see
[
KRW01
, Theorem 2.5]
for the following formulation) identify the homology of
C_{\bullet,*}
in terms of Schur functors of
V
, as

H_{n,d}(C_{\bullet,*})\cong\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}\lambda\\
|\lambda|=n,\lambda=\lambda^{t},d(\lambda)=n-2d\end{subarray}}S_{\lambda}(V)

where
d(\lambda)
denotes the length of the diagonal when
\lambda
is viewed as a Young diagram. In order for there to be any summands at all, we must have that
n\geq(n-2d)^{2}
(as if
\lambda
has diagonal of length
n-2d
then it contains the
(n-2d)\times(n-2d)
square), i.e. that
d\geq\tfrac{n-\sqrt{n}}{2}
. Thus it vanishes if
d<\tfrac{n-\sqrt{n}}{2}
.

If
V
is finite-dimensional then
S_{\lambda}(V)=0
if and only if the number of rows of
\lambda
is greater than
\dim(V)
. If
d(\lambda)=n-2d
then it must have at least
n-2d
rows, so
S_{\lambda}(V)=0
when
n-2d>\dim(V)
, i.e. when
d<\tfrac{n-\dim(V)}{2}
.

If
n
is a square and
d=\tfrac{n-\sqrt{n}}{2}
, so
n=(n-2d)^{2}
, then taking
\lambda
to be the
(n-2d)\times(n-2d)
square we see that
S_{\lambda}(V)\neq 0
when
\dim(V)\geq n-2d
, which contributes to
H_{n,d}(C_{\bullet,*})
.
∎

Remark 4.2
.

Over a field
\mathbb{K}
of positive characteristic, choosing a basis
\{b_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in I}
of
V
, a basis for
C_{\bullet,*}
may be given by symbols

b_{\alpha_{1}}\cdots b_{\alpha_{i}}\otimes(b_{\alpha_{i+1}}\cdot b_{\alpha_{i+2}})\wedge\cdots\wedge(b_{\alpha_{i+2k+1}}\cdot b_{\alpha_{i+2k+2}}).

The differential is given by an alternating sum of moving each wedge-summand across the tensor: in particular the multiset of subscripts
\alpha_{i}
is unchanged by the differential. Thus
C_{n,*}
may be described as a direct sum of chain complexes, one for each
n
-element multiset in
I
. If
n\leq|I|
then there is a summand given by an
n
-element multiset of
I
with no repeated elements, i.e. an injection
\{1,2,\ldots,n\}\to I
. The corresponding direct summand of
C_{n,*}
may be identified with the 1-fold suspension of the augmented simplicial chain complex of the
matching complex

M(\{1,2,\ldots,n\})
on the set
\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
, see e.g.
[
KRW01
]
. So as long as
\dim(V)\geq n
then

\widetilde{H}_{d-1}(M(\{1,2,\ldots,n\});\mathbb{K})\subset H_{n,d}(C_{\bullet,*}).

Now Bouc
[
Bou92
, Proposition 7]
has shown that

\widetilde{H}_{k}(M(\{1,2,\ldots,3k+4\});\mathbb{Z})\cong\mathbb{Z}/3\text{ for all }k\geq 1,

so
H_{3d+1,d}(C_{\bullet,*};\mathbb{K})\neq 0
if
\mathbb{K}
has characteristic 3. Thus Lemma
4.1
cannot hold in positive characteristic (or at least not in characteristic 3).

Proof of Theorem
1.7
.

Recall that
\mathbbm{k}
is a field of characteristic zero. We construct a CW-approximation to
\mathbb{R}
by

\mathbb{C}:=\mathbb{E}_{\infty}(S^{1,0}\otimes\mathbbm{k}\{P\})\cup^{E_{\infty}}_{\rho\cdot\rho^{\prime}-\sigma\cdot(\rho\star\rho^{\prime})}D^{2,1}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R},

where we attach a
(2,1)
-cell for each pair
(\rho,\rho^{\prime})\in P^{2}
: the map to
\mathbb{R}
extends over these cells as
[\rho]\cdot[\rho^{\prime}]=[\sigma]\cdot[\rho\star\rho^{\prime}]\in H_{2,1}(\mathbb{R})
by assumption (II). The map
H_{*,0}(\overline{\mathbb{C}})\to H_{*,0}(\overline{\mathbb{R}})=A_{P}
is an isomorphism, by construction. As both
\mathbb{R}
and
\mathbb{C}
satisfy assumption (I), it follows from
[
GKRW18a
, Theorem 14.4]
and the long exact sequence on
E_{\infty}
-homology for this map that
H_{n,d}^{E_{\infty}}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{C})=0
for
d<\tfrac{1}{2}n
. We may therefore attach
(n,d)
-cells with
d\geq\tfrac{1}{2}n
to
\mathbb{C}
to obtain a CW-approximation
\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{C}^{\prime}\overset{\sim}{\to}\mathbb{R}
, equipped with a relative skeletal filtration. We will treat stability with respect to
\sigma\in P
, but the argument can be easily adapted to any other
\rho\in P
: the only change is notation.

As, for example, in the proof of
[
GKRW18a
, Theorem 18.3]
, this leads to a spectral sequence

E^{1}_{n,p,q}=H_{n,p+q,q}(\overline{\mathbb{C}}/\sigma[0]\otimes E_{\infty}^{+}(\bigoplus_{\alpha\in I}S^{n_{\alpha},d_{\alpha},d_{\alpha}}))\Longrightarrow H_{n,p+q}(\overline{\mathbb{R}}/\sigma),

with
d_{\alpha}\geq\tfrac{1}{2}n_{\alpha}
. As the homology of
E_{\infty}^{+}(\bigoplus_{\alpha\in I}S^{n_{\alpha},d_{\alpha},d_{\alpha}})
is supported in slopes
\geq\tfrac{1}{2}
, whatever vanishing is enjoyed by
\overline{\mathbb{C}}/\sigma
will also hold for
\overline{\mathbb{R}}/\sigma
, up to slope
\tfrac{1}{2}
.

We now analyse
\overline{\mathbb{C}}/\sigma
, using Lemma
4.1
. Letting
W:=\mathbbm{k}\{P\}
, the homology of
\overline{\mathbb{C}}
is calculated by the bigraded cdga

D_{\bullet,*}:=(\mathrm{Sym}^{*}(W[1,0])\otimes\Lambda^{*}(W\otimes W[2,1]),\partial)

with differential determined by

\rho\otimes\rho^{\prime}\mapsto\rho\cdot\rho^{\prime}-\sigma\cdot(\rho\star\rho^{\prime}):D_{2,1}=W\otimes W\longrightarrow D_{2,0}=\mathrm{Sym}^{2}(W)

and the Leibniz rule. As in the first few lines of the proof of
[
GKRW20
, Theorem 9.5]
, as
\mathbbm{k}
has characteristic zero there is an equivalence
\overline{\mathbb{C}}/\sigma\simeq\overline{\mathbb{C}\cup^{E_{\infty}}_{\sigma}D^{1,1}}
. Letting
V:=\mathbbm{k}\{P\setminus\{\sigma\}\}
, the homology of
\overline{\mathbb{C}\cup^{E_{\infty}}_{\sigma}D^{1,1}}
may be calculated by the bigraded cdga

D^{\prime}_{\bullet,*}:=(\mathrm{Sym}^{*}(V[1,0])\otimes\Lambda^{*}(W\otimes W[2,1]),\partial)

with differential determined by

\rho\otimes\rho^{\prime}\mapsto\begin{cases}\rho\cdot\rho^{\prime}&\rho\neq\sigma\text{ and }\rho^{\prime}\neq\sigma\\
0&\rho=\sigma\text{ or }\rho^{\prime}=\sigma\end{cases}

and the Leibniz rule. Now in terms of the cdga
C_{\bullet,*}
introduced in Lemma

we have
D^{\prime}_{\bullet,*}=C_{\bullet,*}\otimes\{\begin{subarray}{c}\text{a free graded commutative algebra with}\\

, and so

H_{*,*}(\overline{\mathbb{C}}/\sigma)\cong H_{*,*}(C_{\bullet,*})\otimes\{\begin{subarray}{c}\text{a free graded commutative algebra}\\

It then follows from Lemma

that

(i)

H_{n,d}(\overline{\mathbb{C}}/\sigma)=0
for

, and

(ii)

if

H_{n,d}(\overline{\mathbb{C}}/\sigma)=0

5.
An example

Consider the ring of integers
\mathcal{O}=\mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{-5}]
of the number field
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-5})
, and write
\omega:=\sqrt{-5}
. This field has class number 2, and so
\mathrm{Pic}(\mathcal{O})=\{\sigma,\lambda\}
is the group of order 2: its nontrivial element
\lambda
may be represented by the (fractional) ideal
\mathfrak{l}=(2,1+\omega)
.

Rank 1.
Both
\mathrm{GL}(\mathcal{O})
and
\mathrm{GL}(\mathfrak{l})
are given by
\mathcal{O}^{\times}=\{\pm 1\}
.

Rank 2.
A presentation for the group
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathcal{O})
has been given by Swan
[
Swa71
, Theorem 11.1]
: it is generated by the matrices

J=\begin{pmatrix}-1&0\\
0&-1\end{pmatrix},T=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\
0&1\end{pmatrix},U=\begin{pmatrix}1&\omega\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}

A=\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\
1&0\end{pmatrix},B=\begin{pmatrix}-\omega&2\\
2&\omega\end{pmatrix},C=\begin{pmatrix}-\omega-4&-2\omega\\
2\omega&\omega-4\end{pmatrix}

subject to the relations

J^{2}=1,J\text{ central},TU=UT,A^{2}=J,B^{2}=J,(TA)^{3}=J,(AB)^{2}=J

(AUBU^{-1})^{2}=J,ACA=JTCT^{-1},UBU^{-1}CB=JTCT^{-1}.

This abelianises to
\mathbb{Z}/2\oplus\mathbb{Z}/6\oplus\mathbb{Z}\oplus\mathbb{Z}
, generated for example by
B,T,U,C
(
[
Swa71
, Corollary 11.2]
). Furthermore,
J\sim 0
in this group. The matrix
E:=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}-1&0\\
0&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)
acts on
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathcal{O})
by conjugation, as
B\mapsto JUBU^{-1}\sim B
,
T\mapsto T^{-1}\sim-T
,
U\mapsto U^{-1}\sim-U
, and
C\mapsto TC^{-1}T^{-1}\sim-C
. This shows that

\mathrm{GL}(\mathcal{O}\oplus\mathcal{O})^{ab}=(\mathbb{Z}/2)^{5}

generated by
E,U,T,B,C
(
[
Swa71
, Corollary 11.3]
).

It remains to describe the abelianisation of
\mathrm{GL}(\mathcal{O}\oplus\mathfrak{l})
. Using that
\mathfrak{l}^{-1}=\tfrac{1}{2}\mathfrak{l}
, we may describe
\mathrm{GL}(\mathcal{O}\oplus\mathfrak{l})
as the subgroup:

\left\{\begin{pmatrix}a&\tfrac{1}{2}b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}\in\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Q}(\omega))\,|\,a,d\in\mathcal{O},b,c\in\mathfrak{l}\right\}\leq\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Q}(\omega)).

In
[
FGT10
, Section 2.2]
we find a presentation for
\mathrm{PSL}(\mathcal{O}\oplus\mathfrak{l})
which is easily lifted to the following presentation of
\mathrm{SL}(\mathcal{O}\oplus\mathfrak{l})
: it is generated by the matrices

J=\begin{pmatrix}-1&0\\
0&-1\end{pmatrix},A=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\
0&1\end{pmatrix},V=\begin{pmatrix}1&\tfrac{1+\omega}{2}\\
0&1\end{pmatrix},C=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
2&1\end{pmatrix},D=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
1-\omega&1\end{pmatrix}

subject to the relations

J^{2}=1,J\text{ central},AV=VA,CD=DC,(AC^{-1})^{2}=J,(DV^{-1})^{3}=1,(CD^{-1}VA^{-1})^{3}=1.

This presentation abelianises to
\mathbb{Z}/3\oplus\mathbb{Z}\oplus\mathbb{Z}
, generated by
DV^{-1}
,
C
, and
D
. The matrix
E
above acts by conjugation, as
DV^{-1}\mapsto D^{-1}V\sim-(DV^{-1})
,
C\mapsto C^{-1}\sim-C
, and
D\mapsto D^{-1}\sim-D
. This gives

\mathrm{GL}(\mathcal{O}\oplus\mathfrak{l})^{ab}=(\mathbb{Z}/2)^{3}

generated by
E
,
C
, and
D
. Furthermore,
J\sim 0
in this group.

Rank 3.
By Bass–Milnor–Serre
[
BMS67
, Corollary 4.3 a)]
the group
\mathrm{SL}_{3}(\mathcal{O})
is perfect, so the map
\det:\mathrm{GL}_{3}(\mathcal{O})\to\mathcal{O}^{\times}=\{\pm 1\}
is the abelianisation. As
[\mathfrak{l}]\in\mathrm{Pic}(\mathcal{O})
has order 2, tensoring with it gives an isomorphism

\mathfrak{l}\otimes-:\mathrm{GL}(\mathcal{O}\oplus\mathcal{O}\oplus\mathfrak{l})\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}\mathrm{GL}(\mathfrak{l}\oplus\mathfrak{l}\oplus\mathfrak{l}^{\otimes 2})\cong\mathrm{GL}(\mathcal{O}\oplus\mathcal{O}\oplus\mathcal{O})=\mathrm{GL}_{3}(\mathcal{O}),

and so
\det:\mathrm{GL}(\mathcal{O}\oplus\mathcal{O}\oplus\mathfrak{l})\to\mathcal{O}^{\times}=\{\pm 1\}
is the abelianisation too.

Modules of finite injective dimension
Mohsen Asgharzadeh

Abstract
. Motivated by Bass’ conjecture, we study finitely generated modules of finite injective dimension and the additional constraints they impose on the ambient ring. Beyond ensuring the Cohen–Macaulay property, the presence of such modules enforces further conditions on the ring, including reducedness, normality, being domain, and various (non)-singularity conditions such as complete intersection, Gorenstein, and beyond. We also address the reflexivity (respectively, torsionlessness) of modules with finite injective dimension and show that this forces the ring to be quasi-normal.

Introduction

Bass
[
10

conjectured that the existence of a finitely generated module of finite injective dimension imposes strong restrictions on a ring, specifically requiring it to be Cohen–Macaulay. Peskine and Szpiro
[
26

[

In particular, they investigated additional constraints on the ring when attention is restricted to certain classes of modules with finite injective dimension. We also note that Matlis studied ideals of injective dimension one. These considerations motivate the present note.

Recall that

Proof.

Following Bass’ conjecture,

In both cases,

Proposition 2.7

\operatorname{id}_{R}(\mathfrak{p})<\infty

Proof.

As Proposition

Let

\operatorname{id}_{R}(M)<\infty

By Fact

The notation

\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{R}(Q/R,R)\cong R
. Then

R
is
complete in
R
-topology.

Proof.

The sequence
0\to R\to Q\to Q/R\to 0
implies

0=\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(Q,R)\to\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(R,R)\to\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{R}(Q/R,R)\to\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{R}(Q,R)\to\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{R}(R,R)=0.

Recall that
\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{R}(Q,R)=\oplus_{I}Q
is a
Q
-vector space. We localize the above sequence and use the assumption

\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{R}(Q/R,R)\cong R
to deduce that
\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{R}(Q,R)=\oplus_{I}Q=0
. The reduced assumption implies that
0\to R\to\operatornamewithlimits{\varprojlim}_{r\in R}R/rR\to\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{R}(Q,R)\to 0
is exact. Since
\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{R}(Q,R)=0
, we have
R=\operatornamewithlimits{\varprojlim}_{r\in R}R/rR
, as claimed.∎

7.
Reflexivity and injective dimension

We start by recalling the following result:

Fact 7.1
.

(Auslander, see
[
26
, I. Proposition 4.14]
).
Suppose
M
is of finite projective dimension and there is a
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Supp}(M)\cap\operatorname{Ass}(R)
.
Then
\operatorname{Supp}(M)=\operatorname{Spec}(R)
.

Lemma 7.2
.

Suppose
R
is complete and there is a torsion-less module
M
of finite injective dimension. Then
\operatorname{Supp}(M)=\operatorname{Spec}(R)
.

Proof.

Let
\tilde{M}:=\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(\omega_{R},M)
. Then

\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(\tilde{M})=\operatorname{Supp}(\omega_{R})\cap\operatorname{Ass}(M)=\operatorname{Spec}(R)\cap\operatorname{Ass}(M)=\operatorname{Ass}(M).

Since
M
is torsion-less, then
M\subseteq R^{n}
and so
\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(\tilde{M})=\operatorname{Ass}(M)\subseteq\operatorname{Ass}(R).
Recall that
\tilde{M}
is of finite projective dimension. By using
Fact
7.1
we know
\operatorname{Supp}(\tilde{M})=\operatorname{Spec}(R)
. Consequently,
\operatorname{Ass}(R)=\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(\tilde{M})=\operatorname{Ass}(M)\subseteq\operatorname{Ass}(R)
. The claim follows.
∎

The following extends Corollary
2.8
.

Lemma 7.3
.

Suppose there is a nonzero torsion-less module
M
of finite injective dimension. Then
\widehat{R}
is generically normal.

Proof.

We may assume that
R
is complete. The ring
R
is unmixed.
Let
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}(R)=\min{R}
. According to the previous lemma, we know
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Supp}(M)
. This shows that
M_{\mathfrak{p}}
is (of finite) injective (dimension) over artinian ring
R_{\mathfrak{p}}
. As
\operatorname{id}(-)
is bounded by depth, we deduce that
M_{\mathfrak{p}}=\oplus_{n>0}\omega_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}
. Localization of torsionless is again torsion less. It follows that the canonical module of
R_{\mathfrak{p}}
is torsion-less. This implies that
R_{\mathfrak{p}}
is Gorenstein (see
[
3
, Corollary 3.7]
).
∎

The following is the main result of this section.

Proposition 7.4
.

Suppose there is a nonzero reflexive module
L
of finite injective dimension. Then
\widehat{R}
is quasi-normal.

Proof.

We may assume that
R
is complete.
Let
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Spec}(R)
be of height one. By previous lemma,
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Supp}(L)
. Set
A:=R_{\mathfrak{p}}
and
M:=L_{\mathfrak{p}}
. Then
\operatorname{id}_{A}(M)=0pt_{A}(A)=1
. Consequently,
\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{2}(\operatorname{Tr}(M^{\ast}),(M^{\ast})^{\ast})=0,
where
\operatorname{Tr}(-)
denotes the Auslander’s transpose (see
[
7
]
for its definition). Then the natural map

f:((M^{\ast})^{\ast})\otimes_{R}(M^{\ast})\longrightarrow\operatorname{Hom}_{R}((M^{\ast})^{\ast},(M^{\ast})^{\ast})

is surjective, as the cokernel of
f
is
\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{2}(\operatorname{Tr}(M^{\ast}),(M^{\ast})^{\ast})=0,
see
[
7
, Proposition (2.6)(a)]
. It turns out that
M^{\ast}
is free as an
A
-module. Since
M
is reflexive,
M\cong M^{\ast\ast}

is free as well. Thus,
\operatorname{id}(A)

is finite. This means that
A=R_{\mathfrak{p}}
is Gorenstein.
So,
R
satisfies
(G_{1})
.
Following Bass’ conjecture,
R
is Cohen-Macaulay. It remains to note that if a ring satisfies
(S_{2})
and
(G_{1})
, then it is quasi-normal.
∎

Corollary 7.5
.

Let
M
be reflexive such that
\operatorname{id}_{R}(M)<\infty
,
and
\mu(M)\leq 2

Then

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we may
assume
(R,\mathfrak{m})
is complete in
\mathfrak{m}
-adic topology. In view of above result, the ring is quasi-normal. Now, apply Proposition

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be of type at most two, and let
M
be an indecomposable reflexive module such that

,

Corollary 7.7
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be of type two, and let
M
be a module such that
\operatorname{id}_{R}(M)<\infty

\mu(M)
is even.

An
R
-module
M
is called
k
-torsionless if
\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{i}(\operatorname{Tr}M,R)=0
for all
i\leq k
.
Recall that torsionless is 1-torstionless and reflexive is 2-torstionless.
To close the paper, we pose the following question and observation, inspired by the main result of this section, and present

Question 7.8
.

Let
k\geq 0
.
Suppose there is a nonzero
(k+1)
-torsionless module of finite injective dimension. When is
R
satisfy the property
(G_{k})
?

Observation 7.9
.

Let
A
be a homomorphic image of Gorenstein ring. Suppose a nonzero module
N
is
(k+2)
-torsionless
and of finite injective dimension. Then
A
satisfy the property
(G_{k})
.

Proof.

Let
\mathfrak{p}
be in
\operatorname{Spec}(A)
be of height
k
. Let
R:=A_{\mathfrak{p}}
and
M:=N_{\mathfrak{p}}
. Then things reduced to show that
R
is Gorenstein. The ring has canonical module. This helps to apply Lemma
7.2
, and thus,
M
is nonzero.
Then, without loss of generality we can assume in addition that
k=d:=\dim R
, since
(\ell)
-torsionless behaves well with respect to localization.
By definition, we know that

M
is reflexive and
\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{i}(M^{\ast},R)=0
for all
i\leq d
, as
M^{\ast}
is the second syzygy of
\operatorname{Tr}M
. Thanks to Bass,
d=\dim R=0pt(R)
. We proceed by induction on
d
to deduce that
M
is free. The initial case

7.3
. By using induction hypothesis, we may assume in addition that
M
is locally free over the punctured spectrum. Let

. Then

is locally free over the punctured spectrum and
\operatorname{id}_{R}(L^{\ast})\leq d

\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{d+1}(\operatorname{Tr}L,L^{\ast})=0

\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{i}(L,R)=0
for all

.
Under these assumptions, Kimura

[

that
L
is free. Then
M\cong M^{\ast\ast}\cong L^{\ast}

\operatorname{id}_{R}(R)\leq d
. Consequently,
R
is Gorenstein.
∎

A note on affine cones over Grassmannians and their stringy E E -functions Thanks: This work was supported by the Research Foundation - Flanders (FWO) Ph.D. Fellowship fundamental research 95249.
Timothy De Deyn

Abstract
. We compute the stringy E E -function of the affine cone over a Grassmannian. If the Grassmannian is not a projective space then its cone does not admit a crepant resolution. Nonetheless the stringy E E -function is sometimes a polynomial and in those cases the cone admits a noncommutative crepant resolution. This raises the question as to whether the existence of a noncommutative crepant resolution implies that the stringy E E -function is a polynomial.

1.
Introduction

In
[
Bat98
]
Batyrev defined the notion of a ‘stringy
E
-function’, which is an invariant defined for any normal irreducible algebraic variety with at worst log-terminal singularities.
An easy consequence of the definition is that a variety admitting a crepant resolution has a polynomial stringy
E
-function.
One can wonder whether the same holds for ‘noncommutative analogues’ of crepant resolutions.

In this note we show the following

More generally we compute the stringy
E
-function for certain affine cones over Fano varieties, see Proposition

.

Proposition

(Propositions
3.1
and
4.1
)
.

Let
X
denote the affine cone over the Grassmannian
\Gr(k,n)
.
Then its stringy
E
-function

E_{st}(X;u,v)=\binom{n}{k}_{uv}\frac{(uv-1){uv}^{n}}{(uv)^{n}-1}.

In particular, this is a polynomial if and only if
\gcd(k,n)=1
.

This gives an easier negative answer to a question asked by Batyrev
[
Bat98
, Question 5.5]
, namely whether a GIT quotient of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
modulo an action of a semisimple subgroup
G\subset\SL_{n}(\mathbb{C})
has a polynomial stringy
E
-function, than the one previously known
[
Kie03
]
.

Furthermore, this affine cone never admits a crepant resolution (if
k\neq 1
or
n-1
).
However, when
\gcd(k,n)=1
it does admit a noncommutative crepant resolution (NCCR) (and a strongly crepant categorical resolution), which raises the question:

Question

(Question
4.3
)
.

If a variety admits a NCCR (or a strongly crepant categorical resolution), is its stringy
E
-function a polynomial?

Moreover, we show that the ‘stringy Euler characteristic’ of the affine cone over the Grassmannian is equal to the rank of the Grothendieck group and to the Euler characteristic (computed via periodic cyclic homology) of its NCCR, see Proposition
4.9
.
The following question thus seems reasonable.

Question

(Question
4.11
)
.

Does the stringy Euler characteristic of a variety equal the Euler characteristic (computed via periodic cyclic homology) of a NCCR?

This indicates that NCCRs, if they exist, seem to encode interesting ‘stringy’ information of a variety.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
E
-function

Let
X
be a variety of pure dimension
d
.
The cohomology groups with compact supports
H^{i}_{c}(X,\mathbb{Q})
(
0\leq i\leq 2d
) carry a natural mixed Hodge structure.
One defines the
E
-polynomial (also called the Hodge-Deligne polynomial) by

E(X;u,v)=\sum_{i=0}^{2d}(-1)^{i}\sum_{p,q}h^{p,q}(H^{i}_{c}(X,\mathbb{C}))u^{p}v^{q},

where
h^{p,q}(H^{i}_{c}(X,\mathbb{C}))
denotes the dimension of the
(p,q)
-type Hodge component in
H^{i}_{c}(X,\mathbb{C})
.

We recall some well-known facts that will be used below.

Facts 2.1
.

(1)

If
Y\to X
is a Zariski locally trivial fibration with fibre
F
, then
E(Y;u,v)=E(F;u,v)E(X;u,v)
, see e.g.
[
DK86
, Corollary 1.9]
.

(2)

E(\mathbb{C}^{*};u,v)=uv-1
.

(3)

E(\Gr(k,n);u,v)=\binom{n}{k}_{uv}
is the Gaussian binomial coefficient (also called
q
-binomial coefficient), defined as

\binom{n}{k}_{q}:=\frac{(1-q^{n})(1-q^{n-1})\cdots(1-q^{n-k+1})}{(1-q)(1-q^{2})\cdots(1-q^{k})}.

2.2.
Stringy
E
-function

Now let
X
be a normal irreducible variety with at worst log-terminal singularities.
The stringy
E
-function of

is defined as a certain motivic integral over its formal arc space, see e.g.
[
CLNS18
,
VS21
]
.
However, to actually compute the integral, one usually performs a ‘change of variables’ along a log resolution of
X
.
We will only give an expression for the stringy

Thus, let
f:Y\to X
be a log resolution, i.e. proper birational morphism with
Y
smooth and such that the exceptional
locus is a simple normal crossing (snc) divisor

.
Denote the irreducible components of

by

and set

Define, for any subset

D_{J}:=\begin{cases}\bigcap_{j\in J}D_{j}&\text{if }J\neq\emptyset\\
Y&\text{if }J=\emptyset\end{cases}\quad\text{and}\quad D^{\circ}_{J}:=D_{J}-\bigcup_{i\in I-J}D_{i}.

Moreover, let

denote the discrepancy coefficient of

, i.e.
K_{Y}-f^{*}K_{X}=\sum_{i=1}^{r}a_{i}D_{i}

After performing the change of variables one arrives at the following expression for the stringy

E_{st}(X;u,v)=\sum_{J\subseteq I}E(D^{\circ}_{J};u,v)\prod_{j\in J}\frac{uv-1}{(uv)^{a_{j}+1}-1},

where
\prod_{j\in J}
is
1
when
J=\emptyset
.

Proposition 3.1

Let

As explained in §

The cone

Putting it together

Using Facts

\displaystyle E_{st}(X;u,v)

\displaystyle=E(Y-D;u,v)+E(D;u,v)\frac{uv-1}{(uv)^{n}-1}

4.
Cones over Grassmannians

4.1.
Set-up and stringy

Fix two integers
4

If

1<k<n-1

\mathbb{G}

Then

We are interested in when this function is a polynomial.
As the Gaussian binomial coefficients are always polynomials, this boils down to understanding their irreducible factors.

Proposition 4.1
.

The stringy

is a polynomial if and only if
\gcd(k,n)=1
.

Proof.

Let
\Phi_{j}(q)
denote the

It is well-known that

q^{n}-1=\prod_{j\mid n}\Phi_{j}(q).

The result now follows since for

, by
[
CH06
, Proposition 3.]
,
\Phi_{j}(q)
divides
\binom{n}{k}_{q}
if and only if
j\nmid k
.
∎

There is an alternative description of

as an

Let

and

be respectively

-dimensional vector spaces over
\mathbb{C}

Consider the standard action of

Thus Proposition

.

When

the affine cone over the Grassmannian

modulo an action of a semisimple subgroup

4.2.
(Noncommutative) crepant resolutions

As
X
is an
\SL_{k}(\mathbb{C})

is connected with no

In addition, by the previous section we see that

However, if

Moreover,

This together with Proposition

Question 4.3
.

If a variety admits a NCCR (or a strongly crepant categorical resolution), is its stringy
E
-function a polynomial?

At this point it is worthwhile to indicate the relationship between NCCRs and strongly crepant categorical resolutions. However, in order not to disturb the flow of the text, this is done in Appendix

Remark 4.4
.

As far as we are aware there are no general results showing the non-existence of NCCRs when

However, for

Of course, if Question

In fact, one of the main motivations behind Question

Remark 4.5
.

One can easily check that every example in

-function.

Remark 4.6
.

As the case

shows, admitting a weakly crepant categorical resolution is not enough to imply that the stringy

-function is a polynomial.

Remark 4.7
.

The negative answer to
[

, Question 5.5]
in
[
Kie03
]
comes from the GIT quotient of

acting diagonally on three copies of the adjoint representation.
It is interesting to note that this singularity has a twisted NCCR by
[

Hence admitting a twisted NCCR is not enough to imply that the stringy

-function is a polynomial.

Remark 4.8
.

It is known that Gorenstein toric varieties and Gorenstein quotient singularities have polynomial stringy

-functions
[
Bat98
, Proposition 4.4]
and
[

]

[

This is compatible with affine Gorenstein quotient singularities (of the form

4.3.
Stringy Euler characteristic

The stringy Euler characteristic of
X
is defined by

e_{st}(X):=\lim_{q\to 1}E_{st}(X;q)=\binom{n}{k}\frac{1}{n}.

When
\gcd(k,n)=1

.

We briefly give the explicit form of the NCCR

Recall that

Since

This leads to the following question.

Question 4.11
.

Does the stringy Euler characteristic of a variety equal the Euler characteristic (computed via periodic cyclic homology) of a NCCR?

Remark 4.12
.

It is shown in
[
Bat98
, Proposition 4.10]
that the stringy Euler characteristic of a normal

-Gorenstein toric variety defined by a fan

Now, for example, for the NCCRs of toric affine varieties constructed in
[

,

]
the Euler characteristic also equals this volume by
[

, Theorem A.1]
, the derived invariance of

and a similar reasoning as above equating the rank of

and the Euler characteristic.
In addition, for affine Gorenstein quotient singularities (of the form

with

finite) it follows from (the proof of)
[
Bat99
, Theorem 8.4]
that the stringy Euler characteristic equals the number of conjugacy classes in

G
.
This in turn equals the number of non-isomorphic indecomposable summands of the reflexive module defining a NCCR of the quotient singularity, see e.g.
[
ŠVdB17
, §1.2]
.
As above this number equals the Euler characteristic of the NCCR.

Remark 4.13
.

In
[
BW19
]
Borisov and Wang define ‘Clifford-stringy Euler characteristics’ as a stringy invariant of a Clifford noncommutative variety
(\mathbb{P}W,\mathcal{B}_{0})
(notation of loc.cit.).
It is worthwhile to note that this invariant is exactly the Euler characteristic of
\mathcal{B}_{0}
calculated via periodic cyclic homology.
This follows from their main theorem
[
BW19
, Theorem 7.2]
, the equivalence
D^{b}(Y_{W})\cong D^{b}(\mathbb{P}W,\mathcal{B}_{0})
(notation of loc.cit.) and the derived invariance of periodic cyclic homology.

Remark 4.14
.

The idea of using noncommutative algebras to resolve singular varieties and compute relevant data appeared early on in physics, see e.g.
[
BL01
]
.
From this point of view, it is natural to expect a relation between suitable ‘string theoretic invariants’ and noncommutative geometry.

Appendix A
Noncommutative crepant resolutions vs. strongly crepant categorical resolutions

In this appendix we briefly indicate the relationship between NCCRs and strongly crepant categorical resolutions (in the affine setting).
This is probably well-known to experts, but as far as the author is aware this is not written down anywhere explicitly, however see
[

, Lemma 1.2]

[

, Lemma 2.2.1.]

For the remainder of this appendix let

-algebra) Gorenstein normal ring.
By a

of

Proposition A.1
.

Let
\Lambda=\End_{R}(M)
be a noncommutative resolution of
R

(

Assume that

\Lambda

Then

gives a categorical resolution of singularities of
\Spec R
.
Furthermore, this categorical resolution is strongly crepant if and only if
\Lambda
is a noncommutative crepant resolution.

Proof.

The functor
\pi^{*}
has as right adjoint
\pi_{*}:=\RHom_{\Lambda}(M,-)
.
In order to show that
(\operatorname{\mathsf{Perf}}(\Lambda),\allowbreak\pi_{*},\pi^{*})
is a categorical resolution of singularities we have to show that
\operatorname{\mathsf{Perf}}(\Lambda)
is smooth and that the natural transformation

(1)

1_{\operatorname{\mathsf{Perf}}(R)}\to\pi_{*}\pi^{*}

is an isomorphism.

The smoothness of
\operatorname{\mathsf{Perf}}(\Lambda)
follows as
\operatorname{\mathsf{Perf}}(\Lambda)=D^{b}(\operatorname{\mathsf{mod}}\Lambda)

\Lambda
has finite global dimension, and the latter is smooth by
[
ELS20

.
(Alternatively one can show the smoothness of
\Lambda

[
SVdB08
, Lemma 4.2.]

To see that (
1
) is an isomorphism it suffices to note, since
R

\operatorname{\mathsf{Perf}}(R)

Finally, to show that the two notions of crepancy correspond to each other it suffices to observe that both are equivalent to the relative Serre functor being the identity.
For strongly crepant categorical resolutions this is by definition, whilst for NCCRs this follows from (the proof of)

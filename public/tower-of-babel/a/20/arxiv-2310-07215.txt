Affine Springer Fibers and Generalized Haiman Ideals
Joshua Turner

Abstract
. We compute the Borel-Moore homology of unramified affine Springer fibers for GL n \mathrm{GL}_{n} under the assumption that they are equivariantly formal and relate them to certain ideals discussed by Haiman. For n = 3 n=3 , we give an explicit description of these ideals, compute their Hilbert series, generators and relations, and compare them to generalized ( q , t ) (q,t) Catalan numbers. We also compare the homology to the Khovanov-Rozansky homology of the associated link, and prove a version of a conjecture of Oblomkov, Rasmussen, and Shende in this case.



1.
Introduction

In this paper, we study a broad class of affine Springer fibers and their equivariant Borel-Moore homology, using geometric and algebraic techniques. This homology is related to the Langlands program
[
23
]
, Hilbert schemes of singular curves
[
27
,
26
]
, and is conjectured to be related to knot homology
[
28
]
. Sometimes these relationships are discussed in terms of compactified Jacobians, which are equivalent to affine Springer fibers
[
24
]
.

Given a matrix
\gamma\in\mathfrak{gl}_{n}((t))
(or
\mathfrak{sl}_{n}((t))
), the affine Springer fiber
\mathrm{Sp}_{\gamma}
is a certain ind-subvariety of the affine Grassmanian, see Definition
2.3
.

The connection to knot homology is of particular interest. Given
\gamma
, its characteristic polynomial
p(\lambda,t)=\text{det}(\gamma-\lambda I)
defines a singular curve
C
in
\mathbb{C}^{2}
, called the spectral curve of
\gamma
. As long as the spectral curve
C
is reduced,
\mathrm{Sp}_{\gamma}
depends only on the curve
C_{\gamma}
, in particular on it’s completion at the origin
(C,0)
. In general we will be working with
\gamma
with distinct eigenvalues, so
C
will be reduced.

Fact 1.1

(
[
29
]
)
.

If
(C,0)
is irreducible, then
\mathrm{Sp}_{\gamma}
is a projective variety, but if
(C,0)
is not irreducible,
\mathrm{Sp}_{\gamma}
is an ind-variety with infinitely many irreducible components.

Intersecting the spectral curve with a small sphere around the singularity gives a link
L_{\gamma}
in
S^{3}
. The number of irreducible components of the curve is the same as the number of components of the link, and smooth components correspond to unknots. Oblomkov, Rasmussen, and Shende
[
28
]
have conjectured that the homology of
\mathrm{Sp}_{\gamma}
is closely related to the triply-graded Khovanov-Rozansky homology
[
21
,
20
]
(also called
\mathrm{HHH}
) of this link. This relationship has been shown for torus knots, and for
(n,nd)
-torus links by Kivinen in
[
22
]
.

Conjecture 1.2

(
[
28
]
)
.

If
L_{\gamma}
is the link associated to
\gamma
, we have

\mathrm{gr}_{P}H_{*}(\mathrm{Sp}_{\gamma})\otimes\mathbb{C}[x]\cong\mathrm{HHH}^{a=0}(L_{\gamma})

where
\mathrm{gr}_{P}
is a certain perverse filtration on
\mathrm{Sp}_{\gamma}
.

Example 1.3
.

For the matrix

\gamma=\begin{pmatrix}0&t^{2}\\
t&0\end{pmatrix},

the characteristic polynomial is given by
p(\lambda,t)=\lambda^{2}-t^{3}.
The associated link to this curve in
\mathbb{C}^{2}
is a trefoil. The affine Springer fiber
\mathrm{Sp}_{\gamma}
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{P}^{1}
, and the reduced HHH homology of the trefoil is isomorphic to
H_{*}(\mathbb{P}^{1})
.

In this paper, we will calculate the homology for a large class of affine Springer fibers with
\gamma
of the form

(1)

\gamma=\begin{pmatrix}z_{1}t^{d_{1}}&&0\\
&\ddots&\\
0&&z_{n}t^{d_{n}}\end{pmatrix},

with
z_{i}\in\mathbb{C}^{*}
pairwise distinct and
d_{i}\geq 0
.

The characteristic polynomial of this
\gamma
equals
\prod_{i}(\lambda-z_{i}t^{d_{i}})
. The corresponding curve
C
has
n
irreducible components which are all smooth, and the the pairwise intersection numbers of components are
d_{ij}=\min(d_{i},d_{j})
. So the corresponding link
L_{\gamma}
is a link of
n
unknots with pairwise linking numbers
d_{ij}
.

In order to calculate
H_{*}^{T}(\mathrm{Sp}_{\gamma})
, it helps to view it as a module over

R=H^{*}_{T}(pt)\otimes\mathbb{C}[\Lambda]\cong\mathbb{C}[t_{1},\dots,t_{n},x_{1}^{\pm},\dots,x_{n}^{\pm}].

Here, the
t_{i}
’s are our equivariant parameters, and the
x_{i}
’s parametrize the integer lattice
\Lambda
which acts on
\mathrm{Sp}_{\gamma}
by translations.

In
[
11
]
, Goresky, Kottwitz, and MacPherson conjectured that
\mathrm{Sp}_{\gamma}
is pure for all unramified (i.e. diagonal)
\gamma
. The following is a more narrow conjecture that is what we will rely on in this paper.

Conjecture 1.4

(
[
11
]
)
.

For
\gamma
as above,
\mathrm{Sp}_{\gamma}
is equivariantly formal, as defined in Definition
2.6
.

Assuming that
\mathrm{Sp}_{\gamma}
is equivariantly formal, we can calculate its equivariant Borel-Moore homology.

Theorem 1.5
.

Consider
\gamma
as in (
1
). Define the ideal

\mathcal{J}=\mathcal{J}_{\gamma}=\bigcap_{i\neq j}(t_{i}-t_{j},x_{i}-x_{j})^{d_{ij}}\subseteq R

with
d_{ij}=\text{min}(d_{i},d_{j})
. If
\mathrm{Sp}_{\gamma}
is equivariantly formal, then as
R
-modules,

\Delta H_{*}^{T}(\mathrm{Sp}_{\gamma})\cong\mathcal{J}\quad\mathrm{where}\quad\Delta=\prod_{i\neq j}(t_{i}-t_{j})^{d_{ij}}.

These
\mathcal{J}
ideals are similar to the ideals considered by Haiman in his work on Hilbert schemes of points
[
10
]
, so we can refer to them as generalized Haiman ideals.

If
n=3,4
, it is known that
\mathrm{Sp}_{\gamma}
is equivariantly formal, shown in
[
25
]
and
[
8
]
respectively.

Corollary 1.6
.

For
n\leq 4
,

\Delta H_{*}^{T}(\mathrm{Sp}_{\gamma})\cong\mathcal{J}=\bigcap_{i\neq j}(t_{i}-t_{j},x_{i}-x_{j})^{d_{ij}}\subseteq R.

In the second half of the paper, we study algebraic properties and combinatorics of the ideal
J
when
n=3
in order to better understand the homology of
\mathrm{Sp}_{\gamma}
. These results allow us to prove several conjectures for
n=3
.

For the rest of the introduction we assume that the
d_{i}
’s are ordered:
d_{1}\leq\ldots\leq d_{n}
. We will
consider a similar ideal

J^{\prime}(d_{1},\ldots,d_{n})=\bigcap_{i\neq j}(t_{i}-t_{j},x_{i}-x_{j})^{d_{ij}}\subseteq\mathbb{C}[t_{1},\ldots,t_{n},x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}].

The ideal
\mathcal{J}
is obtained from
J^{\prime}(d_{1},\ldots,d_{n})
by localization in
(x_{1}\cdots x_{n})
.

Conjecture 1.7
.

a) The Hilbert series of the ideal
J^{\prime}(d_{1},\ldots,d_{n})
equals
H(d_{1},\ldots,d_{n})
.

b) The Hilbert series of the generating set
J^{\prime}(d_{1},\ldots,d_{n})/\mathfrak{m}J^{\prime}(d_{1},\ldots,d_{n})
equals
F(d_{1},\ldots,d_{n})
. Here
\mathfrak{m}=(t_{1},\ldots,t_{n},x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
is the maximal ideal.

Here
H(d_{1},\ldots,d_{n})
and
F(d_{1},\ldots,d_{n})
are two rational functions defined in Section
4.1
. The function
F(d_{1},\dots,d_{n})
is also known as the generalized
q,t
-Catalan number, see
[
14
]
.

In particular, Conjecture
1.7
implies that
F(d_{1},\ldots,d_{n})
is a polynomial in
q
and
t
with nonnegative coefficients (see
[
14
, Conjecture 1.3]
) and provides an explicit algebraic interpretation of these coefficients.
Similarly, the conjecture implies that
H(d_{1},\ldots,d_{n})
is a power series in
q
and
t
with nonnnegative coefficients.

Theorem 1.8
.

Conjecture
1.7
holds for
n=3
.

In
[
16
]
Gorsky, Kivinen and Oblomkov defined a graded algebra
\mathcal{A}_{G}=\bigoplus_{d=0}^{\infty}{}_{0}\mathcal{A}_{d}
depending only on the group
G
(called the graded Coulomb branch algebra) with some specific properties. One of the key properties is that for any
\gamma\in\mathfrak{g},
the direct sum of homologies of affine Springer fibers

F_{\gamma}=\bigoplus_{k=0}^{\infty}H_{*}(\mathrm{Sp}_{t^{k}\gamma})

is a graded module over
\mathcal{A}_{G}
or, equivalently, that there is a corresponding quasi-coherent sheaf
\mathcal{F}_{\gamma}
on
\mathrm{Proj}\ \bigoplus_{d=0}^{\infty}{}_{0}\mathcal{A}_{d}
.

Conjecture 1.9

(
[
16
]
)
.

The module
F_{\gamma}
is finitely generated and that the sheaf
\mathcal{F}_{\gamma}
is coherent.

Theorem 1.10
.

Conjecture
1.9
holds for
G=\mathrm{GL}_{3}
and
\gamma
as in (
1
).

In
[
10
]
, Haiman shows the following concrete fact about the ideal
J(d\ldots d)
for any
n
.

Theorem 1.11

(Haiman)
.

The ideal
J^{\prime}(d,\ldots,d)\subseteq\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{t},\mathbf{x}]
is equal to a power of
J^{\prime}(1,\ldots,1)
,

J^{\prime}(d,\ldots,d)=J^{\prime}(1,\ldots,1)^{d}.

It is easy to see that in general

J^{\prime}(d_{1},\dots,d_{n})\cdot J^{\prime}(d_{1}^{\prime},\dots d_{n}^{\prime})\subseteq J^{\prime}(d_{1}+d_{1}^{\prime},\dots,d_{n}+d_{n}^{\prime}).

In Corollary
4.5
we show a partial generalization of Haiman’s calculation in the
n=3
case, namely that
J^{\prime}(d_{1},d_{2})=J^{\prime}(1,1)^{d_{1}}\cdot J^{\prime}(0,1)^{d_{2}-d_{1}}
. We conjecture that this generalization will work for all
n
.

Conjecture 1.12
.

For any
n
, assume
d_{1}\leq d_{2}\leq\cdots\leq d_{n}
as above. Then

J^{\prime}(d_{1},\ldots,d_{n})=J^{\prime}(1,\ldots,1)^{d_{1}}\cdot J^{\prime}(0,1,\ldots,1)^{d_{2}-d_{1}}\cdot\ldots\cdot J^{\prime}(0,\ldots,0,1)^{d_{n}-d_{n-1}}.

In Section
6
, we use a recursive process of Hogancamp and Elias
[
9
]
to compute
\mathrm{HHH}^{a=0}(L_{\gamma})
for the link that corresponds to
\gamma
for
n=3
and identify it with homology of
\mathrm{Sp}_{\gamma}
. We are able to show that a weaker version of Conjecture
1.2
that doesn’t have the perverse filtration holds in this case.

Theorem 1.13
.

For
n=3
and
\gamma
as in
1
,

\mathrm{HY}^{a=0}(L_{\gamma})\otimes_{\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{x}]}\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{x},\mathbf{x}^{\pm}]=\Delta H_{*}^{T}(\mathrm{Sp}_{\gamma})

and

\mathrm{HHH}^{a=0}(L_{\gamma})\otimes_{\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{x}]}\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{x},\mathbf{x}^{\pm}]=H_{*}(\mathrm{Sp}_{\gamma}).

We are optimistic that the proof of Theorem
1.13
can be generalized to arbitrary
n
, and one
may actually be able to find a recursion for
\mathrm{HHH}^{a=0}(L_{\gamma})
for any
n
, which could tell us more about what
H_{*}(\mathrm{Sp}_{\gamma})
looks like beyond
n=3
.

Finally, in the appendix we discuss the cells of the fundamental domain of
\mathrm{Sp}_{\gamma}
, as described by Chen in
[
7
]
, and relate this to the combinatorics of
J^{\prime}
for
n=3
. We show that there is a bijection between half of the cells in the fundamental domain and the generators of the ideal
J^{\prime}(d_{1},d_{2}).

2.
Background

First, some notation. Let
\mathcal{K}=\mathbb{C}((t))
be the field of Laurent power series in
t
, and
\mathcal{O}=\mathbb{C}[[t]]
be the ring of power series in
t
. For
f\in\mathcal{K}
, let
\nu(f)
denote the order of
f
, which is the degree of the smallest nonzero term. Throughout,
H_{*}^{T}(X)
will refer to the equivariant Borel-Moore homology of
X
, and
H_{*}(X)
refers to regular Borel-Moore homology.

Definition 2.1
.

A
lattice

\Lambda\subseteq\mathcal{K}^{n}
is a free
\mathcal{O}
-submodule of
\mathcal{K}^{n}
of rank
n
such that
\Lambda\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}\mathcal{K}=\mathcal{K}^{n}
. In other words, it is the
\mathcal{O}
-span of a basis of
\mathcal{K}^{n}
over
\mathcal{K}
.

Definition 2.2
.

The
affine Grassmanian

\text{Gr}_{\text{GL}_{n}}(\mathbb{C})
of
\text{GL}_{n}
over
\mathbb{C}
is an ind-scheme defined as the space of all lattices
\Lambda\subseteq\mathcal{K}^{n}
.

We can equivalently define the affine Grassmanian as

\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathcal{K})/\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathcal{O}),

as
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathcal{K})
acts transitively on the space of lattices, and the stabilizer of the standard lattice
\mathcal{O}^{n}
is precisely
\text{GL}_{n}(\mathcal{O})
. We will often conflate a matrix
g\in\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathcal{K})
with its coset representative in
\mathrm{Gr}(\mathrm{GL}_{n})
.

We define
\mathrm{Gr}(\mathrm{SL}_{n})
similarly, either as
\mathrm{SL}_{n}(\mathcal{K})/\mathrm{SL}_{n}(\mathcal{O})
, or as the space of lattices of
\mathrm{SL}_{n}
type. We say that a lattice
\Lambda
is of
\mathrm{SL}_{n}
type if it can be written as
\Lambda=g\mathcal{O}^{n}
for some
g\in\mathrm{SL}_{n}(\mathcal{K})
.

We will always be working over
\mathbb{C}
, so we will use
\mathrm{Gr}(\mathrm{GL}_{n})
or
\mathrm{Gr}(\mathrm{SL}_{n})
for the affine Grassmanian, or just
\mathrm{Gr}
when the group
G
is clear.

Definition 2.3

(
[
19
]
)
.

The
affine Springer fiber

\mathrm{Sp}_{\gamma}
of an element
\gamma\in\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(\mathcal{K})
is a sub ind-scheme of the affine Grassmanian, defined accordingly as the space of lattices
\Lambda\in\text{Gr}_{\text{GL}_{n}}
such that
\gamma\Lambda\subseteq\Lambda
, or as the space of
g\in\text{GL}_{n}(\mathcal{K})/\text{GL}_{n}(\mathcal{O})
such that
g^{-1}\gamma g\in\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(\mathcal{O})
.

Fact 2.4

(
[
29
]
)
.

If
\gamma
is regular, semi-simple, and topologically nilponent, then
\mathrm{Sp}_{\gamma}
is finite dimensional, although it can still have infinitely many irreducible components. In our cases, these conditions essentially are that
\gamma
is diagonalizable, has distinct eigenvalues
\lambda_{i}
(over
\bar{\mathcal{K}}
), and that
\nu(\lambda_{i})\geq 0
for all
i
.

Example 2.5

(
[
29
]
)
.

Consider the matrix

\gamma=\begin{pmatrix}t&0\\
0&-t\end{pmatrix}

in
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}(\mathcal{K})
. Then
\mathrm{Sp}_{\gamma}
looks like an infinite chain of
\mathbb{P}^{1}
’s connected at
0
and
\infty
. There is a
\mathbb{C}^{*}
action that scales each
\mathbb{P}^{1}
, and a
\mathbb{Z}
action that translates them.

There is a natural action of the centralizer
C(\gamma)
on
\mathrm{Sp}_{\gamma}
. In the case where
\gamma
is diagonal, this gives a torus action of
T=(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{n}
and lattice action of
\Lambda=\mathbb{Z}^{n}
on
\mathrm{Sp}_{\gamma}
over
\mathrm{GL}_{n}
(or
(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{n-1}
and
\mathbb{Z}^{n-1}
over
\mathrm{SL}_{n}
) that can be respectively seen as multiplication by the matrices in
C(\gamma)
:

\lambda=\begin{pmatrix}\lambda_{1}&&\\
&\ddots&\\
&&\lambda_{n}\end{pmatrix}

and

Z=\begin{pmatrix}t^{m_{1}}&&\\
&\ddots&\\
&&t^{m_{n}}\end{pmatrix}.

In general for a
T
-ind-scheme
X
,
H_{*}^{T}(X)
is naturally a module over
H_{T}^{*}(pt)
via the cap product.

Definition 2.6
.

We will say that
X
is
equivariantly formal
if
H_{*}^{T}(X)
is free as a module over
H_{T}^{*}(pt)
.

We will need the following localization lemma as stated by Brion.

Lemma 2.7

(Brion
[
6
]
)
.

Let
X
be a
T
-ind-scheme and
T^{\prime}\subseteq T
a subtorus. If
i:X^{T^{\prime}}\rightarrow X
is the inclusion of
T^{\prime}
-fixed points, then the induced map

i_{*}:H^{T}_{*}(X^{T^{\prime}})\rightarrow H^{T}_{*}(X)

is an isomorphism after inverting finitely many characters of
T
that restrict nontrivially to
T^{\prime}
. Further, if
X
is equivariantly formal, then the induced map
i_{*}
is injective, and we have that

\prod_{\chi,\chi_{T^{\prime}}\neq 0}\chi H_{*}^{T}(X)\subseteq H_{*}^{T}(X^{T^{\prime}})

where we take the product over all characters of
T
that restrict nontrivially to
T^{\prime}
.

We will also make frequent use of the Iwasawa decomposition for
\mathrm{Gr}(\mathrm{GL}_{n})
, which tells us that all
g\in\mathrm{Gr}(\mathrm{GL}_{n})
can be represented by a product
DU
of a diagonal matrix

(2)

D=\begin{pmatrix}t^{m_{1}}&&\\
&\ddots&\\
&&t^{m_{n}}\end{pmatrix}

with a unipotent matrix
U
with
1
’s on the diagonal and entries
\chi_{ij}
above the diagonal
[
29
]
.
Further, these
\chi_{ij}
’s are unique up to
\mathcal{O}
, so we can choose them to have all coefficients nonnegative powers of
t
be
0
, so that each matrix
DU
represents a unique element
g\in\mathrm{Gr}
.

Lemma 2.8
.

The
T
-fixed points of
\mathrm{Gr}(\mathrm{GL}_{n})
can be uniquely represented by diagonal matrices
D
as in the Iwasawa decomposition.

Proof.

Let
\lambda\in T
and
g=DU
be as in the Iwasawa decomposition. Since
\lambda^{-1}\in\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathcal{O})
, up to multiplication on the right by
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathcal{O}),
we get

\lambda g=\lambda g\lambda^{-1}=DU^{\prime},

where
D
is as above, and
U^{\prime}
is unipotent with
\frac{\lambda_{i}}{\lambda_{j}}\chi_{ij}
above the diagonal. If
g
is a fixed point under the action of
T
, we must have
\frac{\lambda_{i}}{\lambda_{j}}\chi_{ij}=\chi_{ij}
for all
i,j
and for all
\lambda\in T
. This can only happen if
\chi_{ij}=0
for all
i,j
, so
g=D
is diagonal as desired.
∎

Since the
T
-action on
\mathrm{Sp}_{\gamma}
comes from the action on
\mathrm{Gr}
, the
T
-fixed points of
\mathrm{Sp}_{\gamma}
are simply the
T
-fixed points of
\mathrm{Gr}
that are contained in
\mathrm{Sp}_{\gamma}
.

Lemma 2.9
.

If
\gamma
is diagonal and the orders of its eigenvalues are all nonnegative, then

\mathrm{Sp}_{\gamma}^{T}=\mathrm{Gr}^{T}.

Proof.

For any
\gamma
,
\mathrm{Sp}_{\gamma}^{T}\subseteq\mathrm{Gr}^{T}
as stated above. If
g\in\mathrm{Gr}^{T}
, then

g=\begin{pmatrix}t^{m_{1}}&&\\
&\ddots&\\
&&t^{m_{n}}\end{pmatrix}

by Lemma
2.8
. As
g
and
\gamma
are diagonal,
g^{-1}\gamma g=\gamma,
and
\gamma\in\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(\mathcal{O})
, since the eigenvalues of
\gamma
are all in
\mathcal{O}
. So
\mathrm{Gr}^{T}\subseteq\mathrm{Sp}_{\gamma}^{T}
.
∎

In particular, this means that the
T
-fixed points of
\mathrm{Sp}_{\gamma}
are discrete and isomorphic to the integer lattice
\Lambda=\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, so can view
H_{*}(\mathrm{Sp}_{\gamma})
as a module over

H^{*}_{T}(\mathrm{Sp}_{\gamma}^{T})\cong H^{*}_{T}(pt)\otimes\mathbb{C}[\Lambda]\cong\mathbb{C}[t_{1},\dots,t_{n},x_{1}^{\pm},\dots,x_{n}^{\pm}].

Here, the
t_{i}
’s are our equivariant parameters, and a monomial
x_{1}^{a_{1}}\cdots x_{n}^{a_{n}}
corresponds to the fixed point
\mathrm{diag}(t^{a_{1}},\ldots,t^{a_{n}})
.
The lattice
\Lambda
acts on
\mathrm{Sp}_{\gamma}^{T}
and on
\mathrm{Sp}_{\gamma}
by translation.

Lemma 2.10
.

Fix
i<j
. If
T^{\prime}\subseteq T
is a codimension 1 subtorus cut out by
t_{i}=t_{j}
, then the
T^{\prime}
-fixed points of
\mathrm{Gr}(\mathrm{GL}_{n})
are of the form
DU
, where
D
is as in (
2
) and

U=\begin{pmatrix}1&&\chi_{ij}\\
&\ddots&\\
&&1\end{pmatrix}

with all
\chi
’s zero except for
\chi_{ij}
.

Proof.

As before, up to equivalence,

\lambda g=\lambda g\lambda^{-1}=DU^{\prime},

where
U^{\prime}
has
\frac{\lambda_{k}}{\lambda_{l}}\chi_{kl}
above the diagonal. If
g
is a fixed point for
T
, since
\lambda_{i}=\lambda_{j},

\chi_{ij}
can be arbitrary, but
\chi_{kl}=0
for all
(k,l)\neq(i,j)
. So the fixed points are as described.
∎

Corollary 2.11
.

If
T^{\prime}
is a codimension 1 subtorus of
T
cut out by
t_{i}=t_{j}
, then

\mathrm{Gr}(\mathrm{GL}_{n})^{T^{\prime}}\cong\mathrm{Gr}(\mathrm{GL}_{2})\times\mathbb{Z}^{n-2}.

Proof.

Each of the
T^{\prime}
fixed points is represented by
DU
above. Looking at the
2\times 2
submatrix of
DU
in rows and columns
i,j
, we see a copy of
\mathrm{Gr}(\mathrm{GL}_{2})
. The rest of the
m_{i}
are free integers, and there are
n-2
of them.
∎

3.
Borel-Moore Homology of
\mathrm{Sp}_{\gamma}

We want to find the equivariant Borel-Moore homology
H_{*}^{T}(\mathrm{Sp}_{\gamma})
of the class of affine Springer fibers with

\gamma=\begin{pmatrix}\gamma_{1}&&0\\
&\ddots&\\
0&&\gamma_{n}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}z_{1}t^{d_{1}}&&0\\
&\ddots&\\
0&&z_{n}t^{d_{n}}\end{pmatrix}.

Here
z_{i}\in\mathbb{C}^{*}
pairwise distinct and
d_{i}\geq 0
. We can assume up to a change of basis that
d_{1}\leq\dots\leq d_{n}
.

It is known that for
\gamma
as above,
\mathrm{Sp}_{\gamma}
is equivariantly formal (see Definition
2.6
) over
\text{GL}_{n}
for
n\leq 4
(see
[
25
]
for
n=3
and
[
8
]
for
n=4
), and it is known to be equivariantly formal for all
n
if
d_{i}=d
for all
i
(
[
12
]
). But it is not known over
\text{GL}_{n}
in general. It would be sufficient to know that the homology of
\mathrm{Sp}_{\gamma}
is supported in even degrees, and we conjecture that this is the case for all
n
. We will need to assume that
\mathrm{Sp}_{\gamma}
is equivariantly formal in order to calculate its homology.

Theorem 3.1
.

Assume that
\mathrm{Sp}_{\gamma}
is equivariantly formal for
\gamma
as in Equation (
1
).
Consider the ideal

\mathcal{J}\subseteq H^{*}_{T}(\text{pt})\otimes\mathbb{C}[\mathbb{Z}^{n}]\cong\mathbb{C}[t_{1},\dots,t_{n},x_{1}^{\pm},\dots,x_{n}^{\pm}]

\mathcal{J}=\bigcap_{i\neq j}(t_{i}-t_{j},x_{i}-x_{j})^{d_{ij}}

with
d_{ij}=\text{min}(d_{i},d_{j})
. Then the equivariant Borel-Moore homology of
\mathrm{Sp}_{\gamma}
is
isomorphic to

\Delta H_{*}^{T}(\mathrm{Sp}_{\gamma})\cong\mathcal{J}

where
\Delta=\prod_{i\neq j}(t_{i}-t_{j})^{d_{ij}}.

The rough outline of the proof is as follows:

(1)

Take a codimension one subtorus
T^{\prime}\subseteq T

-fixed points of

are essentially isomorphic to an affine Springer fiber

with

(2)

Relate the homology of

to that of

(3)

Take enough subtori

Step 3 will require the assumption that

is equivariantly formal.

Lemma 3.2
.

If
T^{\prime}\subseteq T
is the subtorus cut out by

, then up to
\mathbb{Z}^{n-2}
, the

-fixed points of
\mathrm{Sp}_{\gamma}
are isomorphic to an affine Springer fiber over

,

\mathrm{Sp}_{\gamma}^{T^{\prime}}\cong\mathrm{Sp}_{\beta_{ij}}\times\mathbb{Z}^{n-2},

where

\beta_{ij}=\begin{pmatrix}z_{i}t^{d_{i}}&0\\
0&z_{j}t^{d_{j}}\end{pmatrix}.

Proof.

In Lemma
2.10
we’ve already characterized the
T^{\prime}
-fixed points of
\mathrm{Gr}
as
DU
, where
U
is unipotent with only a single nonzero
\chi_{ij}
. We just need to check which of those fixed points are in
\mathrm{Sp}_{\gamma}
. If
g\in\mathrm{Gr}(\mathrm{GL}_{n})^{T^{\prime}}
, then

g^{-1}\gamma g=\begin{pmatrix}z_{1}t^{d_{1}}&&\chi_{ij}(z_{i}t^{d_{i}}-z_{j}t^{d}_{j})\\
&\ddots&\\
0&&z_{n}t^{d_{n}}\end{pmatrix}.

Again looking at the
2\times 2

i,j
submatrix, we see a matrix identical to
g^{-1}\beta_{ij}g
. So a
T^{\prime}
-fixed point

is in
\mathrm{Sp}_{\gamma}
if and only if the
2\times 2
matrix

\begin{pmatrix}t^{m_{i}}&\chi_{ij}\\
0&t^{m_{j}}\end{pmatrix}

is in
\mathrm{Sp}_{\beta_{ij}}
, i.e.

\mathrm{Sp}_{\gamma}\cong\mathrm{Sp}_{\beta_{ij}}\times\mathbb{Z}^{n-2}.

∎

Lemma 3.3
.

Given
\beta_{ij}\in\mathfrak{gl}_{2}
as in Theorem 1, we have
\mathrm{Sp}_{\beta_{ij}}\cong\mathrm{Sp}_{\tilde{\beta}_{ij}},
where

\tilde{\beta}_{ij}=\begin{pmatrix}z_{i}t^{d_{ij}}&0\\
0&z_{j}t^{d_{ij}}\end{pmatrix}

and
d_{ij}=\text{min}(d_{i},d_{j})
.

Proof.

Again using the Iwasawa decomposition, write
g=DU
, where

U=\begin{pmatrix}1&\chi_{ij}\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}.

Then,

g^{-1}\gamma g=\begin{pmatrix}\gamma_{i}&\chi_{ij}(\gamma_{i}-\gamma_{j})\\
0&\gamma_{j}\end{pmatrix}.

By definition,
g\in\mathrm{Sp}_{\beta_{ij}}
if and only if
\chi_{ij}(\gamma_{i}-\gamma_{j})\in\mathcal{O}
. Note that
\nu(\gamma_{i}-\gamma_{j})=\min(\nu(\gamma_{i}),\nu(\gamma_{j}))=\min(d_{i},d_{j})=d_{ij}
. So
g\in\mathrm{Sp}_{\beta_{ij}}
if and only if
\chi_{ij}
has order at least
-d_{ij}
. This is the same as the condition for
g
to be in
\mathrm{Sp}_{\tilde{\beta}_{ij}}
, since

g^{-1}\tilde{\beta}_{ij}g=\begin{pmatrix}z_{i}t^{d_{ij}}&(z_{i}-z_{j})\chi_{ij}t^{d_{ij}}\\
0&z_{j}t^{d_{ij}}\end{pmatrix}.

∎

Remark 3.4

The one-dimensional quotient torus

naturally acts on
\mathrm{Sp}_{\gamma}^{T^{\prime}}

is isomorphic to the one-dimensional torus

which acts on
\mathrm{Sp}_{\beta_{ij}}
and on
\mathrm{Sp}_{\tilde{\beta}_{ij}}

The isomorphisms

-equivariant.

The homology for the affine Springer fiber of matrices like

with all powers the same is known. It is found using GKM theory by Kivinen in

Theorem 3.5

(Kivinen
[
22
]
)

If

&\ddots&\\

.

We have the following canonical isomorphism of

Here

acts on

Now in order to piece together these homologies of

Lemma 3.7

(Algebraic Hartogs’ lemma)
.

If
A
is an integrally closed Noetherian integral domain, then

A=\bigcap_{p\text{ codimension 1}}A_{p},

where we take the intersection over all codimension 1 prime ideals of

If

Since we assumed that

Proof of Theorem
3.1
.

By Lemma
2.7
, we have that, up to localization away from
(t_{i}-t_{j})

H^{T}_{*}(\mathrm{Sp}_{\gamma})\cong_{loc}H^{T}_{*}(\mathrm{Sp}_{\gamma}^{T^{\prime}})\cong H_{*}^{T/T^{\prime}}\left(\mathrm{Sp}_{\gamma}^{T^{\prime}}\right)\otimes H^{*}_{T^{\prime}}(pt)=H^{T/T^{\prime}}_{*}(\mathrm{Sp}_{\tilde{\beta}_{ij}}\times\mathbb{Z}^{n-2})\otimes H^{*}_{T^{\prime}}(pt).

Note that in localization
\Delta=(t_{i}-t_{j})^{d_{ij}}
up to an invertible factor, so by Lemma
3.3
we get

\Delta H^{T}_{*}(\mathrm{Sp}_{\gamma})\cong_{loc}(t_{i}-t_{j})^{d_{ij}}H_{*}^{T/T^{\prime}}\left(\mathrm{Sp}_{\widetilde{\beta}_{i,j}}\right)\otimes H^{*}_{T^{\prime}}(pt)\otimes\mathbb{C}[\mathbb{Z}^{n-2}]=(t_{i}-t_{j},x_{i}-x_{j})^{d_{ij}}\subseteq\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{t},\mathbf{x}^{\pm}].

We have inclusion map

\mathrm{Sp}_{\gamma}^{T^{\prime}}\xrightarrow{i}\mathrm{Sp}_{\gamma}

and by Lemma

\prod_{(k,\ell)\neq(i,j)}(t_{k}-t_{\ell})H_{*}^{T}(\mathrm{Sp}_{\gamma})\subseteq i_{*}H_{*}^{T}(\mathrm{Sp}_{\gamma}^{T^{\prime}}).

We also have that

\Delta H_{*}^{T}(\mathrm{Sp}_{\gamma})\subseteq\prod_{(k,\ell)\neq(i,j)}(t_{k}-t_{\ell})(t_{i}-t_{j})^{d_{ij}}H_{*}^{T}(\mathrm{Sp}_{\gamma}).

By the above, this is contained in

We conclude that

\Delta H^{T}_{*}(\mathrm{Sp}_{\gamma})\subseteq(t_{i}-t_{j},x_{i}-x_{j})^{d_{ij}}.

This holds for all codimension-1 subtori

In fact we have already seen that

is exactly the localization

where

So by Lemma

\Delta H^{T}_{*}(\mathrm{Sp}_{\gamma})\cong\bigcap_{i\neq j}(t_{i}-t_{j},x_{i}-x_{j})^{d_{ij}}.

∎

.

The ideal

.

For

, the generators

and

are not defined, while

for

In particular, it is easy to see that there is at most one generator in each

-bidegree, see also Proposition

such that the monomial factor in

Proposition 4.3
.

The ideal

has the following basis (over

Example 4.4
.

For

we get the following 5 generators of

We can change the variables back to see that the generators of

over

Corollary 4.5
.

We have that

J(d_{1},d_{2})=J(1,1)^{d_{1}}\cdot J(0,1)^{d_{2}-d_{1}},

J^{\prime}(d_{1},d_{2})=J^{\prime}(1,1)^{d_{1}}\cdot J^{\prime}(0,1)^{d_{2}-d_{1}}

\mathcal{J}(d_{1},d_{2})=\mathcal{J}(1,1)^{d_{1}}\cdot\mathcal{J}(0,1)^{d_{2}-d_{1}}.

We prove the first equation, and the other two equations follow immediately.

The containment

is clear, so it is sufficient to show that any generator of

can be written as a product of

generators of

(listed in Example

) and

A_{i,j}=a^{d_{1}-j}c^{d_{2}-j}(a-c)^{i}(b-d)^{d_{1}-j-i}(ad-bc)^{j}=(ac(a-c))^{i}\cdot(ac(b-d))^{d_{1}-j-i}\cdot(ad-bc)^{j}\cdot c^{d_{2}-d_{1}},

4.1.
Hilbert Series

Let us introduce two rational functions

H(d_{1},\ldots,d_{n})=\sum_{T}\frac{z_{1}^{d_{n}}\cdots z_{n}^{d_{1}}}{(1-q)^{n}(1-t)^{n}}\prod_{i=2}^{n}\frac{1}{(1-z_{i}^{-1})}\prod_{i<j}\omega(z_{i}/z_{j})

and

F(d_{1},\ldots,d_{n})=\sum_{T}z_{1}^{d_{n}}\cdots z_{n}^{d_{1}}\prod_{i=2}^{n}\frac{1}{(1-z_{i}^{-1})(1-qtz_{i-1}/z_{i})}\prod_{i<j}\omega(z_{i}/z_{j}).

Here the sums are over standard tableaux
T
with
n
boxes,
z_{i}
is the
(q,t)
-content
q^{c-1}t^{r-1}
of the box labeled by
i
in row
r
and column
c
in
T
, and
\omega(x)=\frac{(1-x)(1-qtx)}{(1-qx)(1-tx)}
. By convention, all the factors of the form
(1-1)
in the above products (either in the numerator or in denominator) should be ignored.

The function
F(d_{1},\ldots,d_{n})
is also known as generalized
q,t
-Catalan number, see
[
14
]
for more details and context. Note that the order of the
d_{i}
is reversed here compared to
[
14
]
.

Conjecture 4.8
.

a) The Hilbert series of the ideal
J^{\prime}(d_{1},\ldots,d_{n})
equals
H(d_{1},\ldots,d_{n})
.

b) The Hilbert series of the generating set
J^{\prime}(d_{1},\ldots,d_{n})/\mathfrak{m}J^{\prime}(d_{1},\ldots,d_{n})
equals
F(d_{1},\ldots,d_{n})
. Here
\mathfrak{m}=(t_{1},\ldots,t_{n},x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
is the maximal ideal.

In particular, this conjecture implies that
F(d_{1},\ldots,d_{n})
is a polynomial in
q
and
t
with nonnegative coefficients (see
[
14
, Conjecture 1.3]
) and provides an explicit algebraic interpretation of these coefficients.
Similarly, the conjecture implies that
H(d_{1},\ldots,d_{n})
is a power series in
q
and
t
with nonnnegative coefficients.

Example 4.9
.

For

we get

Similarly,

is free over

Theorem 4.10
.

Conjectures

Proposition 4.12

The generators of

2d_{1}+d_{2}\geq x+y\geq d_{1}+d_{2},\ x+2y\geq 2d_{1}+d_{2},\ 2x+y\geq 2d_{1}+d_{2}.

Proof.

It is easy to check that all of the generators of

We will count the lattice points going by diagonals, starting with the top diagonal

x+2y\geq 2d_{1}+d_{2}
and
2x+y\geq 2d_{1}+d_{2}
are trivially satisfied, since
x,y\geq 0
. So
x
and

can both range from
0
to
2d_{1}+d_{2}
, and there are
2d_{1}+d_{2}+1
points on this diagonal.

On the next diagonal,
x+y=2d_{1}+d_{2}-1
, we have that
x+2y=2d_{1}+d_{2}-1+y
and
2x+y=2d_{1}+d_{2}-1+x

(1,2d_{1}+d_{2}-2),\ (2,2d_{1}+d_{2}-1),\ \ldots(2d_{1}+d_{2}-2,1),

to

\sum_{j=0}^{d_{1}}[2d_{1}+d_{2}+1-3j].

If we count the generators of

as laid out in Theorem

A_{i,j}:\sum_{j=0}^{d_{1}}(d_{1}-j),\quad\,B_{i,j}:\sum_{j=0}^{d_{1}}(d_{1}-j),\quad C_{i,j}:\sum_{j=0}^{d_{1}}(d_{2}-j),\quad D_{j}:\sum_{j=0}^{d_{1}}1,

5.1.
Proof of Theorem
4.1

After doing the change of variables to

It is relatively easy to test when

if and only if all monomials

\deg_{2}(m)\geq d_{2}
.
Note that these degrees should not be confused with

and

defined above.

We will show that in general,

will just be products of the generators of

as described in Example
4.4
, along with some extra factors of
c
and

. First, we will show a key lemma.

Consider some

f=\varphi(b-d)+\psi(ad-bc)\in M

for some
\varphi,\psi\in R.

We can modify the coefficients

(3)

(\varphi,\psi)=(\varphi+\gamma(ad-bc),\psi-\gamma(b-d))

Lemma 5.1
.

If

f=\varphi(b-d)+\psi(ad-bc)\in M

for some
\varphi,\psi\in R,

then up to the relation (

\varphi\in M

Proof.

Since

\varphi(b-d)+\psi(ad-bc)\in M

When we expand, all monomials come in pairs from distributing

\frac{m}{cd}(ad-bc)+\frac{m}{d}(b-d)=m\left(\frac{b}{d}\right)\left(\frac{ad}{bc}\right)-m\left(\frac{b}{d}\right)+m\left(\frac{b}{d}\right)-m=m\left(\frac{b}{d}\right)\left(\frac{ad}{bc}\right)-m.

We can continue to follow a chain of cancellation until either we get two terminal monomials that do not cancel with anything, or we eventually reach a monomial that cancels with the starting monomial

Vertices represent monomials, and edges represent a difference of two monomials, i.e. a pair. The full path represents the sum of all the pairs of monomials represented by each edge, and the end vertices of the path are the terminal monomials. For example, the above cancellation can be represented by the path:

m
m\left(\frac{b}{d}\right)
m\left(\frac{ad}{bc}\right)\left(\frac{b}{d}\right)

Cycles in these paths correspond to the equivalence

(\varphi,\psi)=(\varphi+\gamma(ad-bc),\psi-\gamma(b-d)).

To see this, let
m
be a monomial. A cycle looks like this:

m
m\left(\frac{b}{d}\right)
m\left(\frac{ad}{bc}\right)\left(\frac{b}{d}\right)
m\left(\frac{ad}{bc}\right)

This corresponds to the identity

\frac{m}{d}(b-d)+\frac{m}{cd}(ad-bc)-\frac{ma}{bc}(b-d)-\frac{m}{bc}(ad-bc)=0.

For all of these terms to be monomials,
b,c,d
must all divide
m
. Multiplying through by
bcd
and grouping, we get

-m(ad-bc)(b-d)+m(b-d)(ad-bc)=0.

So adding this cycle corresponds to using the above equivalence with
\gamma=m
, and of course does not change the total sum.

We can add and subtract this square loop to any path in order to both eliminate any loops, and to reorder cancellation. For example:

m
m\left(\frac{b}{d}\right)
m\left(\frac{ad}{bc}\right)\left(\frac{b}{d}\right)
=
m
m\left(\frac{b}{d}\right)
m\left(\frac{ad}{bc}\right)\left(\frac{b}{d}\right)
m\left(\frac{ad}{bc}\right)

m
m\left(\frac{ad}{bc}\right)
m\left(\frac{ad}{bc}\right)\left(\frac{b}{d}\right)

So up to equivalence, we can make any path into one with vertical edges first and horizontal edges after, going from

m
m\left(\frac{b}{d}\right)^{k}
m\left(\frac{ad}{bc}\right)^{j}\left(\frac{b}{d}\right)^{k}

\varphi=\frac{m\left(\frac{b}{d}\right)^{k}-m}{b-d}\ \mathrm{and}\ \psi=\frac{m\left(\frac{b}{d}\right)^{k}\left(\frac{ad}{bc}\right)^{j}-m\left(\frac{b}{d}\right)^{k}}{ad-bc}.

Notice for any monomial

Given a reduced path as above, we know that the terminal monomials

For a general

5.2.
Hilbert Series Calculation

Let us compute the Hilbert series using the basis in Proposition

.

Lemma 5.6
.

We have

\sum_{\alpha+\beta\geq s}q^{\alpha}t^{\beta}=\frac{q^{s}}{(1-q)(1-t/q)}+\frac{t^{s}}{(1-t)(1-q/t)}.

Proof.

We have

\sum_{\alpha+\beta\geq s}q^{\alpha}t^{\beta}=\sum_{\beta=0}^{s-1}\frac{q^{s-\beta}t^{\beta}}{(1-q)}+\sum_{\beta=s}^{\infty}\frac{t^{\beta}}{1-q}=

\frac{q^{s}-t^{s}}{(1-q)(1-t/q)}+\frac{t^{s}}{(1-q)(1-t)}.

Now we can use the identity

\frac{1}{(1-q)(1-t)}-\frac{1}{(1-q)(1-t/q)}=\frac{1}{(1-t)(1-q/t)}.

∎

Theorem 5.7
.

The bigraded Hilbert series of
J(d_{1},d_{2})
is equal to

\frac{q^{2d_{1}+d_{2}}}{(1-q)^{2}(1-t/q)(1-t/q^{2})}+\frac{t^{2d_{1}+d_{2}}}{(1-t)^{2}(1-q/t)(1-q/t^{2})}+

\frac{q^{d_{1}}t^{d_{2}}(1+t)}{(1-q)(1-t)(1-q/t)(1-t^{2}/q)}+\frac{q^{d_{2}}t^{d_{1}}(1+q)}{(1-t)(1-q)(1-t/q)(1-q^{2}/t)}.

Proof.

We compute the contribution of various basis elements.

1.
The contribution of

In

Finally, we compare the above combinatorial results with the fundamental domain. Observe that for

the fundamental domain

Theorem A.12
.

The integer points in a half of
\mathcal{P}(d_{1},d_{2})
(including boundary) are in bijection with the generators of the ideal
\mathcal{J}(d_{1},d_{2})
.

Proof.

We construct the desired bijection in several steps:

1) The integer points in a half of
\mathcal{P}(d_{1},d_{2})
are in bijection with the subdiagrams of
\lambda_{d_{1},d_{2}}
.
Indeed, we can write such points as
(d_{1},0,d_{1}+d_{2})+a(1,0,-1)+b(0,1,-1)
. It is easy to see by comparing Figures

and
3

0\leq a\leq d_{1}
and

, and hence
(a,b)

\mu=(a+b,a)
.

2) By Theorem
A.11
, we have a bijection
\phi
between the subdiagrams of
\lambda_{d_{1},d_{2}}
and the points in the trapezoid (
10
).

3) By Proposition
4.12

\mathcal{J}(d_{1},d_{2})

10
).
∎

We expect that the bijection in Theorem
A.12
is far more than a combinatorial coincidence. In particular, by tracing through the bijections we see that
\mathrm{dinv}
defines a piecewise linear function on the fundamental domain
\mathcal{P}(d_{1},d_{2})
, and we expect this function to be closely related to the dimension of cells in an appropriately chosen cell decomposition of
\mathrm{Sp}_{\gamma}
. The corresponding (equivariant) homology classes of the cells would then correspond to some elements of
\mathcal{J}(d_{1},d_{2})
, and we expect that these would indeed generate the ideal. We plan to study these questions and generalize them to
n>3
in the future work.

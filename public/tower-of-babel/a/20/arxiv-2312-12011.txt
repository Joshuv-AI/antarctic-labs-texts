Arithmetic Properties Modulo Powers of 2 for Overpartition
k

Abstract
Recently, Drema and Saikia (2023) proved several congruences modulo powers of 2 2 and 3 3 for overpartition triples with odd parts. We extend their list substantially. We prove several congruences modulo powers of 2 2 for overpartition k k -tuples with odd parts, along with a few infinite families of congruences for overpartition triples with odd parts and overpartition k k -tuples with odd parts (for k = 4 k=4 and for odd k k ).



Keywords:
Partitions, Ramanujan-type congruences.

2020 Mathematics Subject Classification:
11P83, 05A17.

1
Introduction

A partition of a positive integer
n
is a finite non-increasing sequence of positive integers
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\ldots,\lambda_{k})
such that the parts
\lambda_{i}
sum up to
n
. For instance,
4,3+1,2+2,2+1+1
and
1+1+1+1
are the five partitions of
4
. The number of partitions of
n
is denoted by
p(n)
, and its generating function found by Euler is given by

\sum_{n\geq 0}p(n)q^{n}=\frac{1}{(q;q)_{\infty}},

where

(a;q)_{\infty}:=\prod_{i\geq 0}(1-aq^{i}),\quad|q|<1.

Throughout the paper, we will use the notation
f_{k}:=(q^{k};q^{k})_{\infty}
.

Among the many beautiful properties satisfied by the partition function, undoubtedly some of the most beautiful are the congruences modulo primes that the partition function satisfies, as discovered by Ramanujan. Several mathematicians have since studied the arithmetic properties of the partition function, as well as other generalized classes of partitions. Recently, Drema and Saikia
[
8
]
studied congruences modulo small powers of
2
and
3
satisfied by the function which counts overpartition triples with odd parts. The aim of the present paper is to extend their list and to also look at some more general cases.

An overpartition of a nonnegative integer
n
is a non-increasing sequence of natural numbers whose sum is
n
, where the first occurrence (or equivalently, the last occurrence) of a number may be overlined. The eight overpartitions of 3 are

3,\bar{3},2+1,\bar{2}+1,2+\bar{1},\bar{2}+\bar{1},1+1+1,~\text{and}~\bar{1}+1+1.

The number of overpartitions of
n
is denoted by
\overline{p}(n)
and its generating function is given by

\sum_{n\geq 0}\overline{p}(n)q^{n}=\frac{f_{2}}{f_{1}^{2}}.

An overpartition triple
\xi
of
n
is a triplet
(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\xi_{3})
of overpartitions such that the sum of all the parts is
n
. For instance
(\bar{3}+2,\bar{5},1+1+1+1+1)
is an overpartition triple of
15
. An overpartition triple into odd parts is a triplet of overpartitions
(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\xi_{3})
such that the parts of all the overpartitions
\xi_{1},\xi_{2}
and
\xi_{3}
are restricted to odd integers. For instance
(\bar{3}+\bar{1}+1,\bar{5},\bar{1}+1+1+1+1)
is an overpartition triple into odd parts of
15
. Let
\overline{OPT}(n)
denote the number of such overpartition triples of
n
with odd parts. Then the generating function for
\overline{OPT}(n)
is given by
[
8
, Eq. (1)]

\sum_{n\geq 0}\overline{OPT}(n)q^{n}=\frac{f_{2}^{9}}{f_{1}^{6}f_{4}^{3}}=\prod_{i\geq 0}\left(\frac{1+q^{2i+1}}{1-q^{2i+1}}\right)^{3}.

(1)

Before proceeding further, we mention (without commentary) the following easy consequence of the binomial theorem which will be used below.

Lemma 1.1
.

[
13
, Lemma 2.6]

For a prime
p
, and positive integers
k
and
l
, we have

\displaystyle f_{k}^{p^{l}}\equiv f_{pk}^{p^{l-1}}\pmod{p^{l}}.

It can be shown easily
[
8
, Eq. (43)]
that

\overline{OPT}(2n+1)\equiv 0\pmod{2},

for all
n\geq 0
.

Indeed, working modulo 2, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 0}\overline{OPT}(n)q^{n}\equiv\prod_{i\geq 0}\left(\frac{1+q^{2i+1}}{1-q^{2i+1}}\right)^{3}\equiv\prod_{i\geq 0}\left(\frac{1-q^{2i+1}}{1-q^{2i+1}}\right)^{3}\equiv 1\pmod{2}.

Hence, for
n\geq 1
, we have

\displaystyle\overline{OPT}(n)\equiv 0\pmod{2}.

The last congruence is reminiscent of the fact that

\overline{p}(n)\equiv 0\pmod{2},\quad\text{for all}~n>0.

In this paper we prove several infinite families of Ramanujan-type congruences modulo powers of
2
for the
\overline{OPT}(n)
function (Theorems
2.1
,
2.2
,
2.3
and
2.5
.)

As can be seen, there is nothing special about the definition of an overpartition triple, and it can be easily extended to an overpartition
k
-tuple, as was done by the third author with Keister and Vary
[
11
]
. An overpartition
k
-tuple of
n
is a
k
-tuple of overpartitions (
\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\ldots,\xi_{k})
such that the sum of the parts of
\xi_{i}
’s equals
n
. The generating function for the number of overpartition
k
-tuples of
n
, denoted by
\overline{p}_{k}(n)
is given by

\sum_{n\geq 0}\overline{p}_{k}(n)q^{n}=\frac{f_{2}^{k}}{f_{1}^{2k}}.

We can similarly define an overpartition
k
-tuple of
n
with odd parts to be an overpartition
k
-tuple
(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\ldots,\xi_{k})
of
n
where all parts of
\xi_{i}
’s are odd. The generating function for the number of overpartition
k
-tuples of
n
with odd parts, denoted by
\overline{OPT}_{k}(n)
is given by

\sum_{n\geq 0}\overline{OPT}_{k}(n)q^{n}=\frac{f_{2}^{3k}}{f_{1}^{2k}f_{4}^{k}}.

The case
k=3
here corresponds to overpartition tuples with odd parts. The case
k=2
has also been studied, the interested reader can look at the work of Lin
[
12
]
. The study of the arithmetic properties of the case
k=1
was initiated by the third author and Hirschhorn
[
9
]
, who used the notation
\overline{p}_{o}(n)
for
\overline{OPT}_{1}(n)
, which we will use from now on.

In this paper we prove several Ramanujan-type congruences modulo powers of
2
for the functions
\overline{OPT}_{2k+1}(n)
(for
k\geq 1
) (Theorems
2.6
,
2.8
,
2.9
and
2.10
). We also give two infinite families of congruences modulo powers of
2
for the
\overline{OPT}_{4}(n)
(Theorem
2.11
) and
\overline{OPT}_{2k+1}(n)
(Theorem
2.12
) functions.

The paper is organized as follows: in Section
2

and

We now state some results for overpartition
k
-tuples with odd parts. The first such result is reminiscent of a result proved by the third author with Keister and Vary
[
11
, Theorem 8]
.

Theorem 2.6
.

Let
k=(2^{m})r
with
m>0
and
r
odd. Then for all integers
n\geq 1
we have

\overline{OPT}_{k}(n)\equiv 0\pmod{2^{m+1}}.

The proof is very short and we can give it immediately using the following lemma from Keister, Sellers and Vary
[
11
, Lemma 7]
.

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
m
be a nonnegative integer, then for all integers
n
such that
1\leq n\leq 2^{m}
, we have

\binom{2^{m}}{n}2^{n}\equiv 0\pmod{2^{m+1}}.

Proof of Theorem
2.6
.

We have the following

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 0}\overline{OPT}_{k}(n)q^{n}

\displaystyle=\prod_{i=1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{1+q^{2i+1}}{1-q^{2i+1}}\right)^{k}

\displaystyle=\left[\prod_{i=1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{1+q^{2i+1}}{1-q^{2i+1}}\right)^{2^{m}}\right]^{r}

\displaystyle=\left[\prod_{i=1}^{\infty}\left(1+\frac{2q^{2i+1}}{1-q^{2i+1}}\right)^{2^{m}}\right]^{r}.

Now, using the binomial theorem and Lemma
2.7
we can conclude

\sum_{n\geq 0}\overline{OPT}_{k}(n)q^{n}\equiv 1\pmod{2^{m+1}}.

This completes the proof.
∎

We now state three results for the general cases of
\overline{OPT}_{2k+1}(n)
.

Theorem 2.8
.

For all
n\geq 0
and
k\geq 1
with
n,k\in\mathbb{Z}
we have

\overline{OPT}_{2k+1}(n)\equiv\overline{p}_{o}(n)\pmod{4}.

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 0}\overline{OPT}_{2k+1}(n)q^{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{f_{2}^{3(2k+1)}}{f_{1}^{2(2k+1)}f_{4}^{2k+1}}

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{f_{2}^{6k}f_{2}^{3}}{f_{2}^{2k}f_{2}^{4k}f_{1}^{2}f_{4}}\pmod{4}

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{f_{2}^{3}}{f_{1}^{2}f_{4}}\pmod{4}

(7)

\displaystyle\equiv\sum_{n\geq 0}\overline{p}_{o}(n)q^{n}\pmod{4}.

∎

We can use Theorem
2.8
to prove an infinite family of Ramanujan–like congruences modulo 4 for the functions
\overline{OPT}_{2k+1}.

Theorem 2.9
.

For all
n\geq 0
,
k\geq 0
with
n,k\in\mathbb{Z}
and
p\geq 5
prime, and all quadratic nonresidues
r
modulo
p
with
1\leq r\leq p-1
we have

\overline{OPT}_{2k+1}(2pn+R)\equiv 0\pmod{4},

where

R=\begin{cases}r,&\text{if $r$ is odd,}\\
p+r,&\text{if $r$ is even.}\end{cases}

Proof.

From the work of Hirschhorn and the third author
[
9
, Theorem 1.1]
, we know that, for all
n\geq 1,

\overline{p}_{o}(n)\equiv 0\pmod{4}
if and only if
n
is neither a square nor twice a square. So we simply need to show that
2pn+R
as defined above is never a square and never twice a square.
Note that
2pn+R
is always odd by definition, so we see immediately that
2pn+R
can never be twice a square.
Next, we see that
2pn+R\equiv r\pmod{p}
again from the definition of
R.
And since
r
is defined to be a quadratic nonresidue modulo
p
, we know that
r
cannot be congruent to a square modulo
p.
Thus,
2pn+R
cannot equal a square. This concludes the proof.
∎

3
Preliminaries

Ramanujan’s general theta function
f(a,b)
is defined by

\displaystyle f(a,b)=\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}a^{\frac{k(k+1)}{2}}b^{\frac{k(k-1)}{2}},\quad|ab|<1.

Two special cases of
f(a,b)
are given by

\displaystyle\phi(q)

\displaystyle:=f(q,q)=\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}q^{k^{2}}=1+2\sum_{n\geq 1}q^{n^{2}},

(9)

and

\displaystyle\psi(q):=f(q,q^{3})=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}q^{k(k+1)/2}.

We recall the following results.

Lemma 3.1
.

[
3
, p. 40, Entries 25(i) and 25(ii)]

We have

\displaystyle\phi(q)=\phi(q^{4})+2q\psi(q^{8}).

(10)

.

[
9
, Theorem 2.4]

We have

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 0}\overline{p}_{o}(n)q^{n}=\phi(q)\phi(q^{2})\phi(q^{4})^{2}\phi(q^{8})^{4}\cdots

Some known 2-, 3-dissections (see for example
[
2
, Lemmas 2 and 3]
) are stated in the following lemma, which will be used subsequently.

Lemma 3.3
.

We have

\displaystyle f_{1}^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{f_{2}f_{8}^{5}}{f_{4}^{2}f_{16}^{2}}-2q\frac{f_{2}f_{16}^{2}}{f_{8}},

(11)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{f_{1}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{f_{8}^{5}}{f_{2}^{5}f_{16}^{2}}+2q\frac{f_{4}^{2}f_{16}^{2}}{f_{2}^{5}f_{8}},

(12)

\displaystyle f_{1}^{4}

\displaystyle=\frac{f_{4}^{10}}{f_{2}^{2}f_{8}^{4}}-4q\frac{f_{2}^{2}f_{8}^{4}}{f_{4}^{2}},

\displaystyle=\frac{f_{6}f_{9}^{4}}{f_{3}f_{18}^{2}}-qf_{9}f_{18}-2q^{2}\frac{f_{3}f_{18}^{4}}{f_{6}f_{9}^{2}},

Proof of Theorem
2.10
.

From Theorem
3.2
and the generating function for
\overline{OPT}_{k}(n)
we have, for any odd

,

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 0}\overline{OPT}_{t}(n)q^{n}=\phi(q)^{t}\phi(q^{2})^{t}\phi(q^{4})^{2t}\phi(q^{8})^{4t}\cdots

Since
\left(\prod_{i\geq 3}\phi(q^{2^{i}})\right)^{2^{i-1}\cdot t}
is a function of
q^{8}
, it is enough to do the 8-dissection of the first three terms. Also, as the highest modulus involved in the theorem is 16 and the other moduli are divisors of 16, we will prove our result modulo 16.

Hence,

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 0}\overline{OPT}_{t}(n)q^{n}

\displaystyle=\phi(q)^{t}\phi(q^{2})^{t}\phi(q^{4})^{2t}\phi(q^{8})^{4t}\cdots

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{j=0}^{7}a_{t,j}q^{j}F_{t,j}(q^{8})\right)\left(\prod_{i\geq 3}\phi(q^{2^{i}})\right)^{2^{i-1}\cdot t}.

The result now follows easily from Lemma
6.1
.
∎

The Existence and Uniqueness of Solutions for Kernel-Based System Identification
Mohammad Khosravi, Roy S. Smith

Abstract
The notion of reproducing kernel Hilbert space (RKHS) has emerged in system identification during the past decade. In the resulting framework, the impulse response estimation problem is formulated as a regularized optimization defined on an infinite-dimensional RKHS consisting of stable impulse responses. The consequent estimation problem is well-defined under the central assumption that the convolution operators restricted to the RKHS are continuous linear functionals. Moreover, according to this assumption, the representer theorem hold, and therefore, the impulse response can be estimated by solving a finite-dimensional program. Thus, the continuity feature plays a significant role in kernel-based system identification. This paper shows that this central assumption is guaranteed to be satisfied in considerably general situations, namely when the kernel is an integrable function and the input signal is bounded. Furthermore, the strong convexity of the optimization problem and the continuity property of the convolution operators imply that the kernel-based system identification admits a unique solution. Consequently, it follows that kernel-based system identification is a well-defined approach.

III
Kernel-Based System Identification

Consider a stable LTI system
{\mathcal{S}}
characterized by an impulse response
{\mathrm{g}}^{{\scalebox{.5}{$({\mathcal{S}})$}}}:=({g}^{{\scalebox{.5}{$({\mathcal{S}})$}}}_{t})_{t\in{\mathbb{T}}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{{\mathbb{T}}}
,
where
{\mathbb{T}}
is
{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}
or
{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
respectively for the case that the system is discrete-time or continuous-time.
Suppose the system
{\mathcal{S}}
is actuated by a signal
{\mathrm{u}}\in{\mathscr{L}}^{\infty}
, and the resulting output signal is measured with measurement noise at

{n_{{\scalebox{.5}{$\!{\mathscr{D}}$}}}}
time instants
t_{1},\ldots,t_{{n_{{\scalebox{.5}{$\!{\mathscr{D}}$}}}}}
.
Let the measured output of the system at time instant
t_{i}
, and the corresponding measurement uncertainty, be denoted by
y_{t_{i}}
and
w_{t_{i}}
, respectively. Due to the definition of operators
\{{\mathrm{L}}^{\!{\mathrm{u}}}_{t}|t\in{\mathbb{T}}_{\pm}\}
, we know that

\vskip 0.0pty_{t_{i}}={\mathrm{L}}^{\!{\mathrm{u}}}_{t_{i}}({\mathrm{g}}^{{\scalebox{.5}{$({\mathcal{S}})$}}})+w_{t_{i}},\qquad i=1,\ldots,{n_{{\scalebox{.5}{$\!{\mathscr{D}}$}}}}.

(1)

Therefore, we are provided with a set of input-output measurement data denoted by
{\mathscr{D}}
.
Accordingly, the impulse response identification problem is formalized as estimating
{\mathrm{g}}^{{\scalebox{.5}{$({\mathcal{S}})$}}}
, the impulse response of stable system
{\mathcal{S}}
, based on the measurement data. In the kernel-based identification framework, this problem is formulated as an impulse response estimation in a reproducing kernel Hilbert space (RKHS) endowed with a stable kernel.
To introduce the main result of this paper, we need to discuss this paradigm briefly.

Definition 1

(
[
14
]
)
.

Consider symmetric function
\mathbbm{k}:{\mathbb{T}}\times{\mathbb{T}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
, that is assumed to be continuous if
{\mathbb{T}}={\mathbb{R}}_{+}
. We say
\mathbbm{k}
is a Mercer kernel when we have

\vskip 0.0pt\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{i}\mathbbm{k}(t_{i},t_{j})a_{j}\geq 0,

(2)

for all
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
t_{1},\ldots,t_{n}\in{\mathbb{T}}
, and
a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\in{\mathbb{R}}
.
Furthermore, with respect to each
t\in{\mathbb{T}}
, the section of kernel
\mathbbm{k}
at
t
is the function
\mathbbm{k}_{t}:{\mathbb{T}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
defined as
\mathbbm{k}_{t}(\cdot)=\mathbbm{k}(t,\cdot)
.

Theorem 1

(
[
14
]
)
.

With respect to each Mercer kernel
\mathbbm{k}:{\mathbb{T}}\times{\mathbb{T}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
, a unique Hilbert space
{\mathscr{H}}_{\mathbbm{k}}\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^{{\mathbb{T}}}
endowed with inner product
{\langle{\cdot,\cdot}\rangle}_{{\mathscr{H}}_{\mathbbm{k}}}
exists such that, for each
t\in{\mathbb{T}}
, one has

i)

\mathbbm{k}_{t}\in{\mathscr{H}}_{\mathbbm{k}}
, and

ii)

{\langle{{\mathrm{g}},\mathbbm{k}_{t}}\rangle}_{{\mathscr{H}}_{\mathbbm{k}}}=g_{t}
, for all
{\mathrm{g}}=(g_{s})_{s\in{\mathbb{T}}}\in{\mathscr{H}}_{\mathbbm{k}}
.

In this case, we say
{\mathscr{H}}_{\mathbbm{k}}
is the
RKHS with kernel

\mathbbm{k}
. Moreover, the second feature is called the reproducing property.

Due to Theorem
1
, one can see that each RKHS is uniquely characterized by the corresponding Mercer kernel.
Since the to-be-estimated impulse response is known to be stable in the bounded-input-bounded-output (BIBO) sense, the employed kernel
\mathbbm{k}
is required to guarantee that
{\mathscr{H}}_{\mathbbm{k}}\subseteq{\mathscr{L}}^{1}
.
The sufficient and necessary condition for this property is established by the following theorem.

Theorem 2

(
[
58
,
59
]
)
.

Consider the Mercer kernel
\mathbbm{k}:{\mathbb{T}}\times{\mathbb{T}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
and the corresponding RKHS
{\mathscr{H}}_{\mathbbm{k}}
. Then,
{\mathscr{H}}_{\mathbbm{k}}\subseteq{\mathscr{L}}^{1}
if and only if, for any
{\mathrm{u}}=(u_{s})_{s\in{\mathbb{T}}}\in{\mathscr{L}}^{\infty}
,
one has

\vskip 0.0pt\sum_{t\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}}\bigg|\sum_{s\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}}u_{s}\mathbbm{k}(t,s)\bigg|<\infty,

(3)

when
{\mathbb{T}}={\mathbb{Z}}_{+}
, and,

\vskip 0.0pt\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{+}}\bigg|\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{+}}u_{s}\mathbbm{k}(t,s)\mathrm{d}s\bigg|\mathrm{d}t<\infty,

(4)

when
{\mathbb{T}}={\mathbb{R}}_{+}
.
When this property holds, kernel
\mathbbm{k}
is called
stable
and
{\mathscr{H}}_{\mathbbm{k}}
is said to be a
stable RKHS
.

Given the stable kernel
\mathbbm{k}
and the measurement data, the
kernel-based impulse response estimation
problem is formulated as

\vskip 0.0pt\min_{{\mathrm{g}}\in{\mathscr{H}}_{\mathbbm{k}}}\ \sum_{i=1}^{{n_{{\scalebox{.5}{$\!{\mathscr{D}}$}}}}}({\mathrm{L}}^{\!{\mathrm{u}}}_{t_{i}}({\mathrm{g}})-y_{i})^{2}+\lambda\|{\mathrm{g}}\|_{{\mathscr{H}}_{\mathbbm{k}}}^{2},

(5)

where
\lambda>0
is the regularization weight.
Based on the same arguments as in
[
12
, Theorem 1.3.1]
, one can describe the solution of (
5
) in terms of the sections of the kernel at
t_{1},\ldots,t_{{n_{{\scalebox{.5}{$\!{\mathscr{D}}$}}}}}
.
To this end, we need vector
{\mathrm{y}}
defined as
{\mathrm{y}}=\big[y_{t_{1}},\ldots,y_{t_{{n_{{\scalebox{.5}{$\!{\mathscr{D}}$}}}}}}\big]^{{\scalebox{.63}{$\mathsf{T}$ }}}\!\in{\mathbb{R}}^{{n_{{\scalebox{.5}{$\!{\mathscr{D}}$}}}}}
, and the
output kernel matrix

{\mathrm{O}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{{n_{{\scalebox{.5}{$\!{\mathscr{D}}$}}}}\times{n_{{\scalebox{.5}{$\!{\mathscr{D}}$}}}}}
formed from the input signal and defined entry-wise as

\vskip 0.0pt[{\mathrm{O}}]_{(i,j)}\!=\!\left\{\!\begin{array}[]{ll}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{+}}\!\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{+}}\!\mathbbm{k}(s,t)u_{t_{i}-s}u_{t_{j}-t}\!\ \mathrm{d}s\!\ \mathrm{d}t,&\ \text{ if }{\mathbb{T}}_{+}\!=\!{\mathbb{R}}_{+},\\
\operatornamewithlimits{\text{\scalebox{1.25}{$\sum$}}}\limits_{t\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}}\operatornamewithlimits{\text{\scalebox{1.25}{$\sum$}}}\limits_{s\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}}\!\mathbbm{k}(s,t)u_{t_{i}-s}u_{t_{j}-t},&\ \text{ if }{\mathbb{T}}_{+}\!=\!{\mathbb{Z}}_{+},\\
\end{array}\right.

for each
i,j=1,\ldots,{n_{{\scalebox{.5}{$\!{\mathscr{D}}$}}}}
.

Theorem 3

(Representer Theorem for System Identification,
[
21
]
)
.

Let
{\mathrm{L}}^{\!{\mathrm{u}}}_{t_{i}}:{\mathscr{H}}_{\mathbbm{k}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
be a continuous linear operator, for each
i=1,\ldots,{n_{{\scalebox{.5}{$\!{\mathscr{D}}$}}}}
. Then, the minimizer of (
5
) is
{\mathrm{g}}^{\star}=(g_{t}^{\star})_{t\in{\mathbb{T}}}\in{\mathscr{H}}_{\mathbbm{k}}
defined as

\vskip 0.0ptg_{t}^{\star}=\sum_{i=1}^{{n_{{\scalebox{.5}{$\!{\mathscr{D}}$}}}}}c_{i}^{\star}{\mathrm{L}}^{\!{\mathrm{u}}}_{t_{i}}(\mathbbm{k}_{t}),\qquad\forall t\in{\mathbb{T}},

(6)

where the vector
{\mathrm{c}}^{\star}=\big[c_{1}^{\star},\ldots,c_{{n_{{\scalebox{.5}{$\!{\mathscr{D}}$}}}}}^{\star}\big]^{{\scalebox{.63}{$\mathsf{T}$ }}}\!\in{\mathbb{R}}^{{n_{{\scalebox{.5}{$\!{\mathscr{D}}$}}}}}

\vskip 0.0pt{\mathrm{c}}^{\star}=\big({\mathrm{O}}+\lambda\mathbb{I}_{{n_{{\scalebox{.5}{$\!{\mathscr{D}}$}}}}}\big)^{-1}{\mathrm{y}},

(7)

and,
\mathbb{I}_{{n_{{\scalebox{.5}{$\!{\mathscr{D}}$}}}}}
denotes identity matrix of dimension
{n_{{\scalebox{.5}{$\!{\mathscr{D}}$}}}}
.

The main assumption in Theorem
3
is the continuity of convolution operators
{\mathrm{L}}^{\!{\mathrm{u}}}_{t_{1}},\ldots,{\mathrm{L}}^{\!{\mathrm{u}}}_{t_{{n_{{\scalebox{.5}{$\!{\mathscr{D}}$}}}}}}
, which depends mainly on the input signal
{\mathrm{u}}
and kernel
\mathbbm{k}
.
Accordingly, a natural question one may ask is
under what conditions are the convolution operators continuous
.
Indeed, one should note that in Theorem
3
, the convolution operators are restricted to
{\mathscr{H}}_{\mathbbm{k}}\subset{\mathscr{L}}^{1}
, and consequently, the continuity of
{\mathrm{L}}^{\!{\mathrm{u}}}_{t}:{\mathscr{L}}^{1}\to{\mathbb{R}}
does not imply that the restricted operator
{\mathrm{L}}^{\!{\mathrm{u}}}_{t}:{\mathscr{H}}_{\mathbbm{k}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
is continuous as well.
We address this continuity concern in the next section.

&=\sum_{i=1}^{M_{n}}a^{(n)}_{i}\Big\langle\int_{\mathrm{J}_{i}^{(n)}}\mathbbm{k}(\cdot,s)\mathrm{d}s,{\mathrm{g}}\Big\rangle_{{\mathscr{H}}_{\mathbbm{k}}}\\
&=\Big\langle\sum_{i=1}^{M_{n}}a^{(n)}_{i}\int_{\mathrm{J}_{i}^{(n)}}\mathbbm{k}(\cdot,s)\mathrm{d}s,{\mathrm{g}}\Big\rangle_{{\mathscr{H}}_{\mathbbm{k}}}\\
&=\Big\langle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{+}}\sum_{i=1}^{M_{n}}a^{(n)}_{i}\mathbf{1}_{\mathrm{J}_{i}^{(n)}}(s)\mathbbm{k}(\cdot,s)\mathrm{d}s,{\mathrm{g}}\Big\rangle_{{\mathscr{H}}_{\mathbbm{k}}}\\
&=\Big\langle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{+}}v^{(n)}_{s}\mathbbm{k}(\cdot,s)\mathrm{d}s,{\mathrm{g}}\Big\rangle_{{\mathscr{H}}_{\mathbbm{k}}}\\
&=\langle{\mathrm{f}}_{n},{\mathrm{g}}\rangle_{{\mathscr{H}}_{\mathbbm{k}}},\end{split}

(19)

where the second equality is due to Lemma
4
.
Let
\varepsilon
be an arbitrary positive real scalar.
Define
l
as

l:=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\times{\mathbb{R}}_{+}}v_{t}\mathbbm{k}(t,s)v_{s}\mathrm{d}t\mathrm{d}s,

(20)

and, for any
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
l_{n}
as

l_{n}:=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\times{\mathbb{R}}_{+}}v^{(n)}_{t}\mathbbm{k}(t,s)v_{s}\mathrm{d}t\mathrm{d}s.

(21)

For almost all
s,t\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
, we have

\lim_{n\to\infty}v^{(n)}_{t}\mathbbm{k}(t,s)v_{s}=v_{t}\mathbbm{k}(t,s)v_{s}.

(22)

Moreover, for any
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, we know that

\big|v^{(n)}_{t}\mathbbm{k}(t,s)v_{s}\big|\leq\|{\mathrm{v}}\|_{\infty}|\mathbbm{k}(t,s)|.

(23)

Since
\mathbbm{k}
is integrable, from the dominated convergence theorem
[
60
]
, we have

\begin{split}\lim_{n\to\infty}l_{n}&=\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\times{\mathbb{R}}_{+}}v^{(n)}_{t}\mathbbm{k}(t,s)v_{s}\mathrm{d}s\mathrm{d}t\\
&=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\times{\mathbb{R}}_{+}}\lim_{n\to\infty}v^{(n)}_{t}\mathbbm{k}(t,s)v_{s}\mathrm{d}s\mathrm{d}t\\
&=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\times{\mathbb{R}}_{+}}v_{t}\mathbbm{k}(t,s)v_{s}\mathrm{d}s\mathrm{d}t=l.\end{split}

(24)

Therefore, there exist
N_{\varepsilon}\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that
|l_{n}-l|\leq\frac{1}{8}\varepsilon^{2}
, for each
n\geq N_{\varepsilon}
.
Define
l_{n,m}
as

l_{n,m}:={\langle{{\mathrm{f}}_{n},{\mathrm{f}}_{m}}\rangle}_{{\mathscr{H}}_{\mathbbm{k}}}
, for each
m,n\in{\mathbb{N}}
.
Accordingly, from (
18
), Corollary
5
and the linearity of integration and inner product, it follows that

\begin{split}l_{n,m}\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!&\ \ \ \ \ \ =\sum_{i=1}^{M_{n}}\sum_{j=1}^{M_{m}}a^{(n)}_{i}a^{(m)}_{j}\Big\langle\int_{\mathrm{J}_{i}^{(n)}}\!\!\mathbbm{k}(\cdot,s)\mathrm{d}s,\int_{\mathrm{J}_{j}^{(m)}}\!\!\mathbbm{k}(\cdot,t)\mathrm{d}t\Big\rangle_{{\mathscr{H}}_{\mathbbm{k}}}\\
&=\sum_{i=1}^{M_{n}}\sum_{j=1}^{M_{m}}a^{(n)}_{i}a^{(m)}_{j}\int_{\mathrm{J}_{i}^{(n)}}\int_{\mathrm{J}_{j}^{(m)}}\mathbbm{k}(s,t)\mathrm{d}s\mathrm{d}t\\
&=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\times{\mathbb{R}}_{+}}\sum_{i=1}^{M_{n}}\sum_{j=1}^{M_{m}}a^{(n)}_{i}a^{(m)}_{j}\mathbf{1}_{\mathrm{J}_{i}^{(n)}}(t)\mathbf{1}_{\mathrm{J}_{j}^{(m)}}(s)\mathbbm{k}(s,t)\mathrm{d}s\mathrm{d}t.\end{split}

Therefore, due to the definition of
{\mathrm{v}}_{n}
and
{\mathrm{v}}_{m}
, we have

l_{n,m}={\langle{{\mathrm{f}}_{n},{\mathrm{f}}_{m}}\rangle}_{{\mathscr{H}}_{\mathbbm{k}}}=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\times{\mathbb{R}}_{+}}v^{(n)}_{s}\mathbbm{k}(s,t)v^{(m)}_{t}\mathrm{d}s\mathrm{d}t.

(25)

Accordingly, since
\|{\mathrm{v}}_{n}\|_{\infty},\|{\mathrm{v}}_{m}\|_{\infty}\leq\|{\mathrm{v}}\|_{\infty}
, one can see that

\begin{split}\!\!\!\!\!\!|l_{n,m}-l_{n}|&\!=\!\bigg|\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\times{\mathbb{R}}_{+}}\!\!v^{(n)}_{t}\Big[\mathbbm{k}(t,s)(v^{(m)}_{s}\!-\!v_{s})\Big]\mathrm{d}t\mathrm{d}s\Big|\\
&\!\leq\!\|{\mathrm{v}}\|_{\infty}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\times{\mathbb{R}}_{+}}\!\!\Big|\mathbbm{k}(t,s)(v^{(m)}_{s}\!-\!v_{s})\Big|\mathrm{d}t\mathrm{d}s.\end{split}

(26)

From
\|{\mathrm{v}}_{n}\|_{\infty},\|{\mathrm{v}}_{m}\|_{\infty}\leq\|{\mathrm{v}}\|_{\infty}
,
we have

|\mathbbm{k}(t,s)(v^{(m)}_{s}-v_{s})|\leq 2\|{\mathrm{v}}\|_{\infty}|\mathbbm{k}(t,s)|.

(27)

Moreover, for almost all
s\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
, we know that

\lim_{m\to\infty}|\mathbbm{k}(t,s)(v^{(m)}_{s}-v_{s})|=0.

(28)

Since
\mathbbm{k}
is integrable, from the dominated convergence theorem
[
60
]
, it follows that

\lim_{m\to\infty}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\times{\mathbb{R}}_{+}}|\mathbbm{k}(t,s)(v^{(m)}_{s}-v_{s})|\mathrm{d}s\mathrm{d}t=0.

(29)

Therefore, due to (
26
), there exists
M_{\varepsilon}
such that, for any
m\geq M_{\varepsilon}
, we have

|l_{n,m}-l_{n}|\leq\frac{1}{8}\varepsilon^{2}
.
Accordingly, from triangle inequality, we have
|l_{m,n}-l|\leq\frac{1}{4}\varepsilon^{2}
, for any
m,n\geq K_{\varepsilon}:=\max\{M_{\varepsilon},N_{\varepsilon}\}
.
Subsequently, it follows that

\begin{split}\|{\mathrm{f}}_{n}&-{\mathrm{f}}_{m}\|^{2}={\langle{{\mathrm{f}}_{n},{\mathrm{f}}_{n}}\rangle}_{{\mathscr{H}}_{\mathbbm{k}}}-2{\langle{{\mathrm{f}}_{n},{\mathrm{f}}_{m}}\rangle}_{{\mathscr{H}}_{\mathbbm{k}}}+{\langle{{\mathrm{f}}_{m},{\mathrm{f}}_{m}}\rangle}_{{\mathscr{H}}_{\mathbbm{k}}}\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\\
&=l_{n,n}-2l_{n,m}+l_{m,m}\\
&\leq(l+\frac{1}{4}\varepsilon^{2})-2(l-\frac{1}{4}\varepsilon^{2})+(l+\frac{1}{4}\varepsilon^{2})=\varepsilon^{2}.\end{split}

(30)

Hence, for any
m,n\geq K_{\varepsilon}
, we have
\|{\mathrm{f}}_{n}-{\mathrm{f}}_{m}\|_{{\mathscr{H}}_{\mathbbm{k}}}\leq\varepsilon
.
Therefore,
\{{\mathrm{f}}_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
is a Cauchy sequence in
{\mathscr{H}}_{\mathbbm{k}}
, and there exists
{\mathrm{f}}=(f_{s})_{s\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}}\in{\mathscr{H}}_{\mathbbm{k}}
such that
\lim_{n\to\infty}{\mathrm{f}}_{n}={\mathrm{f}}
.
Accordingly, due to the reproducing property, for any
t\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
, we have

\lim_{n\to\infty}f_{n,t}=\lim_{n\to\infty}{\langle{\mathbbm{k}_{t},{\mathrm{f}}_{n}}\rangle}_{{\mathscr{H}}_{\mathbbm{k}}}={\langle{\mathbbm{k}_{t},{\mathrm{f}}}\rangle}_{{\mathscr{H}}_{\mathbbm{k}}}=f_{t}.

(31)

For any
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
and for almost all
s,t\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
, we have

\Big|\mathbbm{k}(t,s)v^{(n)}_{s}\Big|\leq\|{\mathrm{v}}\|_{\infty}|\mathbbm{k}(t,s)|,

(32)

and

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbbm{k}(t,s)v^{(n)}_{s}=\mathbbm{k}(t,s)v_{s}.

(33)

Accordingly, since
\mathbbm{k}
is integrable, from the dominated convergence theorem
[
60
]
, (
18
) and (
31
), it follows that

\begin{split}f_{t}&=\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{+}}\mathbbm{k}(t,s)v^{(n)}_{s}\mathrm{d}s\\
&=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{+}}\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbbm{k}(t,s)v^{(n)}_{s}\mathrm{d}s\\
&=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{+}}\mathbbm{k}(t,s)v_{s}\mathrm{d}s,\end{split}

(34)

i.e., we have

{\mathrm{f}}=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{+}}\mathbbm{k}(\cdot,s)v_{s}\mathrm{d}s.

(35)

For almost all
s\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
, we know that

\lim_{n\to\infty}g_{s}v^{(n)}_{s}=g_{s}v_{s}
.
Moreover, one has that

|g_{s}v^{(n)}_{s}|\leq\|{\mathrm{v}}\|_{\infty}|g_{s}|
, for each
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
.
Since
{\mathrm{g}}=(g_{s})_{s\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}}\in{\mathscr{H}}_{\mathbbm{k}}
and each element of
{\mathscr{H}}_{\mathbbm{k}}
is integrable,
due to the dominated convergence theorem
[
60
]
, (
19
) and
\lim_{n\to\infty}{\mathrm{f}}_{n}={\mathrm{f}}
,
we have

\begin{split}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{+}}g_{s}v_{s}\mathrm{d}s&=\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{+}}g_{s}v^{(n)}_{s}\mathrm{d}s\\
&=\lim_{n\to\infty}{\langle{{\mathrm{f}}_{n},{\mathrm{g}}}\rangle}_{{\mathscr{H}}_{\mathbbm{k}}}\\
&={\langle{{\mathrm{f}},{\mathrm{g}}}\rangle}_{{\mathscr{H}}_{\mathbbm{k}}}.\end{split}

(36)

Let
\varphi_{\tau}^{\text{\rm{(u)}}}=(\varphi_{\tau,t}^{\text{\rm{(u)}}})_{t\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}}

be defined such that for any
t\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
,
we have

\varphi_{\tau,t}^{\text{\rm{(u)}}}=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{+}}\mathbbm{k}(t,s)u_{\tau-s}\mathrm{d}s,

(37)

i.e.,
\varphi_{\tau}^{\text{\rm{(u)}}}=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{+}}\mathbbm{k}(\cdot,s)u_{\tau-s}\mathrm{d}s
.
Due to (
34
) and the fact that
v_{s}=u_{\tau-s}
, for

, we know that
\varphi_{\tau}^{\text{\rm{(u)}}}={\mathrm{f}}\in{\mathscr{H}}_{\mathbbm{k}}
.
Accordingly, from (
36
), we have

\begin{split}{\mathrm{L}}^{\!{\mathrm{u}}}_{\tau}({\mathrm{g}})&=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{+}}v_{s}u_{\tau-s}\mathrm{d}s\\
&=\Big\langle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{+}}\mathbbm{k}(\cdot,s)u_{\tau-s}\mathrm{d}s,{\mathrm{g}}\Big\rangle_{{\mathscr{H}}_{\mathbbm{k}}}\\
&={\langle{\varphi_{\tau}^{\text{\rm{(u)}}},{\mathrm{g}}}\rangle}_{{\mathscr{H}}_{\mathbbm{k}}},\end{split}

which implies that
{\mathrm{L}}^{\!{\mathrm{u}}}_{\tau}
is a continuous (bounded) operator on
{\mathscr{H}}_{\mathbbm{k}}
. This concludes the proof for the case of
{\mathbb{T}}={\mathbb{R}}_{+}
.

Case II:
Let
{\mathbb{T}}={\mathbb{Z}}_{+}
and, similarly to the previous case, define

as

, for any

For any

Let
\varepsilon
be an arbitrary positive real scalar.
Since
\mathbbm{k}
is summable, there exists

\sum_{s,t\geq N_{\varepsilon}+1}|\mathbbm{k}(t,s)|\leq\frac{1}{\|{\mathrm{v}}\|_{\infty}^{2}}\varepsilon^{2}.

For any
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, let
{\mathrm{f}}_{n}=(f_{n,t})_{t\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}}

. Let

.
Without loss of generality, assume

\begin{split}\|{\mathrm{f}}_{n}-{\mathrm{f}}_{m}\|_{{\mathscr{H}}_{\mathbbm{k}}}^{2}&=\Big\|\sum_{s=n+1}^{m}\mathbbm{k}(\cdot,s)v_{s}\Big\|_{{\mathscr{H}}_{\mathbbm{k}}}^{2}\\

i.e.,

, and there exists
{\mathrm{f}}=(f_{t})_{t\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}}

\lim_{n\to\infty}{\mathrm{f}}_{n}={\mathrm{f}}

f_{t}={\langle{\mathbbm{k}_{t},{\mathrm{f}}}\rangle}_{{\mathscr{H}}_{\mathbbm{k}}}=\lim_{n\to\infty}{\langle{\mathbbm{k}_{t},{\mathrm{f}}_{n}}\rangle}_{{\mathscr{H}}_{\mathbbm{k}}}=\lim_{n\to\infty}f_{n,t},

for any

.

\begin{split}\sum_{s\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}}g_{s}v_{s}&=\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{s=0}^{n}v_{s}{\langle{\mathbbm{k}_{s},{\mathrm{g}}}\rangle}_{{\mathscr{H}}_{\mathbbm{k}}}\\

&={\langle{{\mathrm{f}},{\mathrm{g}}}\rangle}_{{\mathscr{H}}_{\mathbbm{k}}}.\end{split}

(43)

Let
\varphi_{\tau}^{\text{\rm{(u)}}}=(\varphi_{\tau,t}^{\text{\rm{(u)}}})_{t\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}}
be defined as

\varphi_{\tau,t}^{\text{\rm{(u)}}}=f_{t}=\sum_{s\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}}\mathbbm{k}(t,s)v_{s},

(44)

for any
t\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}
.
Accordingly, we have

{\mathrm{L}}^{\!{\mathrm{u}}}_{\tau}({\mathrm{g}})\!=\!\sum_{s\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}}g_{s}v_{s}\!=\!\langle\varphi_{\tau}^{\text{\rm{(u)}}},{\mathrm{g}}\rangle_{{\mathscr{H}}_{\mathbbm{k}}}\!=\!\Big\langle\sum_{s\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}}\mathbbm{k}(\cdot,s)v_{s},{\mathrm{g}}\Big\rangle_{{\mathscr{H}}_{\mathbbm{k}}},

for any
{\mathrm{g}}\in{\mathscr{H}}_{\mathbbm{k}}
.
This concludes the proof.

Corollary 8
.

Let
\mathbbm{k}
be an integrable kernel and
{\mathrm{u}}\in{\mathscr{L}}^{\infty}
.
Then, the kernel-based impulse response estimation problem (
5
) admits a unique solution introduced in (
6
).

Proof.

From Theorem
7
, it follows that the objective in (
5
) is function
{\mathcal{J}}:{\mathscr{H}}_{\mathbbm{k}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
defined as

{\mathcal{J}}({\mathrm{g}})=\sum_{i=1}^{{n_{{\scalebox{.5}{$\!{\mathscr{D}}$}}}}}\big({\langle{\varphi_{t_{i}}^{\text{\rm{(u)}}},{\mathrm{g}}}\rangle}_{{\mathscr{H}}_{\mathbbm{k}}}-y_{t_{i}}\big)^{2}+\lambda\|{\mathrm{g}}\|_{{\mathscr{H}}_{\mathbbm{k}}}^{2},

(45)

for any
{\mathrm{g}}\in{\mathscr{H}}_{\mathbbm{k}}
.
This implies that

{\mathcal{J}}

Accordingly, from

Therefore, due to

The proof concludes from (

∎

V
Conclusion

The kernel-based system identification stands on the central assumption that the convolution operators restricted to the chosen RKHS are continuous linear functionals. Current research work in the literature assumes, implicitly or explicitly, that this continuity property holds without elaborating the required conditions. In this work, we have addressed this long-standing question by specifying these conditions: the boundedness of the input signal and the integrability of the kernel function. Furthermore, owing to the strong convexity of the optimization problem and the resulted continuity feature of the convolution operators, we have shown that the kernel-based approach is well-defined by guaranteeing the existence and uniqueness properties for the solution of the identification problem.

\begin{split}\!\!\!\!\!\!{\langle{{\mathrm{f}}_{n},{\mathrm{f}}_{m}}\rangle}_{{\mathscr{H}}_{\mathbbm{k}}}&\!=\!\Big\langle\Delta_{n}\sum_{i=1}^{N_{n}}\mathbbm{k}(t_{i}^{(n)},\cdot),\Delta_{m}\sum_{j=1}^{N_{m}}\mathbbm{k}(t_{j}^{(m)},\cdot)\Big\rangle_{{\mathscr{H}}_{\mathbbm{k}}}\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\\

\mathrm{I}_{i}^{(n)}
is defined as
[t_{i}^{(n)},t_{i}^{(n)}+\Delta_{n})
.
Accordingly, we have

f_{s}=\operatornamewithlimits{lim}\limits_{n\to\infty}f_{n,s}=\int_{[\underline{\tau},\overline{\tau}]}\mathbbm{k}(s,t)\mathrm{d}t,

(53)

which says that
{\mathrm{f}}=\int_{[\underline{\tau},\overline{\tau}]}\mathbbm{k}(\cdot,t)\mathrm{d}t\in{\mathscr{H}}_{\mathbbm{k}}
.
Moreover, from
\lim_{n\to\infty}{\mathrm{f}}_{n}={\mathrm{f}}
, we know that

\|{\mathrm{f}}\|_{{\mathscr{H}}_{\mathbbm{k}}}^{2}=\operatornamewithlimits{lim}\limits_{n\to\infty}\|{\mathrm{f}}_{n}\|_{{\mathscr{H}}_{\mathbbm{k}}}^{2}=\operatornamewithlimits{lim}\limits_{n\to\infty}{\langle{{\mathrm{f}}_{n},{\mathrm{f}}_{n}}\rangle}_{{\mathscr{H}}_{\mathbbm{k}}}.

(54)

Therefore, from (
51
) and the definition of
{\mathrm{f}}
and
I
, it follows that

\begin{split}\Big\|\int_{[\underline{\tau},\overline{\tau}]}\mathbbm{k}(\cdot,t)\mathrm{d}t\Big\|_{{\mathscr{H}}_{\mathbbm{k}}}^{2}&=\operatornamewithlimits{lim}\limits_{n\to\infty}{\langle{{\mathrm{f}}_{n},{\mathrm{f}}_{n}}\rangle}_{{\mathscr{H}}_{\mathbbm{k}}}\\
&=\int_{[\underline{\tau},\overline{\tau}]\times[\underline{\tau},\overline{\tau}]}\mathbbm{k}(s,t)\mathrm{d}s\mathrm{d}t.\end{split}

(55)

Note that, for any
s
such that
t+s\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
, due to the reproducing property of the kernel,
we have

\begin{split}\|\mathbbm{k}_{t+s}-\mathbbm{k}_{t}\|_{{\mathscr{H}}_{\mathbbm{k}}}^{2}=&\ \mathbbm{k}(t+s,t+s)\\
&-\mathbbm{k}(t,t+s)-\mathbbm{k}(t+s,t)+\mathbbm{k}(t,t),\end{split}

which implies that
\lim_{s\to 0}\|\mathbbm{k}_{t+s}-\mathbbm{k}_{t}\|_{{\mathscr{H}}_{\mathbbm{k}}}=0
due to continuity of the kernel.
Meanwhile, for each
{\mathrm{g}}=(g_{t})_{t\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}}\in{\mathscr{H}}_{\mathbbm{k}}
,
from the Cauchy-Schwartz inequality and the reproducing property,
one has

\begin{split}|g_{t+s}-g_{t}|&=|{\langle{\mathbbm{k}_{t+s}-\mathbbm{k}_{t},{\mathrm{g}}}\rangle}_{{\mathscr{H}}_{\mathbbm{k}}}|\\
&\leq\|\mathbbm{k}_{t+s}-\mathbbm{k}_{t}\|_{{\mathscr{H}}_{\mathbbm{k}}}\|{\mathrm{g}}\|_{{\mathscr{H}}_{\mathbbm{k}}}.\end{split}

Accordingly, we have
\lim_{s\to 0}|g_{t+s}-g_{t}|=0
, which says that
{\mathrm{g}}=(g_{t})_{t\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}}
is a continuous function of
t
.
Hence, the Riemann integral of
{\mathrm{g}}
exists, and we have

\begin{split}\int_{[\underline{\tau},\overline{\tau}]}g_{t}\mathrm{d}t&=\operatornamewithlimits{lim}\limits_{n\to\infty}\sum_{i=1}^{2^{n}}g\big(\underline{\tau}+(i-1)2^{-n}\Delta\tau\big)2^{-n}\Delta\tau\\
&=\operatornamewithlimits{lim}\limits_{n\to\infty}\sum_{i=1}^{N_{n}}g(t_{i}^{(n)})\Delta_{n}\\
&=\operatornamewithlimits{lim}\limits_{n\to\infty}{\langle{{\mathrm{f}}_{n},{\mathrm{g}}}\rangle}_{{\mathscr{H}}_{\mathbbm{k}}},\end{split}

where the last equality is due the definition of
{\mathrm{f}}_{n}
in (
46
) and the reproducing property.
Therefore, from
\lim_{n\to\infty}{\mathrm{f}}_{n}={\mathrm{f}}
, one can see that

\begin{split}\int_{[\underline{\tau},\overline{\tau}]}g_{t}\mathrm{d}t&=\operatornamewithlimits{lim}\limits_{n\to\infty}{\langle{{\mathrm{f}}_{n},{\mathrm{g}}}\rangle}_{{\mathscr{H}}_{\mathbbm{k}}}\\
&={\langle{{\mathrm{f}},{\mathrm{g}}}\rangle}_{{\mathscr{H}}_{\mathbbm{k}}}\\
&=\Big\langle\int_{[\underline{\tau},\overline{\tau}]}\mathbbm{k}(\cdot,t)\mathrm{d}t,{\mathrm{g}}\Big\rangle_{{\mathscr{H}}_{\mathbbm{k}}}.\end{split}

(56)

Now, we consider the case where
\overline{\tau}=\infty
.
For each integer
n\geq\tau
, let

{\mathrm{h}}_{n}=(h_{n,s})_{s\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}}
be function

{\mathrm{h}}_{n}=\int_{[\underline{\tau},n]}\mathbbm{k}(\cdot,t)\mathrm{d}t
which is well-defined and belongs to
{\mathscr{H}}_{\mathbbm{k}}
.
Let
\varepsilon
be an arbitrary positive real scalar.
Since
\mathbbm{k}
is absolutely integrable, we know that

\lim_{\tau\to\infty}\int_{\tau}^{\infty}\int_{\tau}^{\infty}|\mathbbm{k}(s,t)|\mathrm{d}s\mathrm{d}t=0.

(57)

Let
\tau_{\varepsilon}\geq\underline{\tau}
be the smallest positive real scalar such that

\int_{\tau_{\varepsilon}}^{\infty}\int_{\tau_{\varepsilon}}^{\infty}|\mathbbm{k}(s,t)|\mathrm{d}s\mathrm{d}t\leq\varepsilon^{2},

(58)

and
n,m\in{\mathbb{N}}
be arbitrary indices such that
n,m\geq\tau_{\varepsilon}
.
Without loss of generality, assume
n\geq m
.
Then, due to the discussion above and the triangle inequality, we have

\begin{split}\!\!\|{\mathrm{h}}_{n}&-{\mathrm{h}}_{m}\|_{{\mathscr{H}}_{\mathbbm{k}}}^{2}=\Big\|\int_{m}^{n}\mathbbm{k}(\cdot,t)\mathrm{d}t\Big\|_{{\mathscr{H}}_{\mathbbm{k}}}^{2}\\
&=\int_{m}^{n}\int_{m}^{n}\mathbbm{k}(s,t)\mathrm{d}s\mathrm{d}t\\
&\leq\int_{m}^{n}\int_{m}^{n}|\mathbbm{k}(s,t)|\mathrm{d}s\mathrm{d}t\\
&\leq\int_{\tau_{\varepsilon}}^{\infty}\int_{\tau_{\varepsilon}}^{\infty}|\mathbbm{k}(s,t)|\mathrm{d}s\mathrm{d}t.\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\end{split}

(59)

Accordingly, from (
58
), we know that
\|{\mathrm{h}}_{n}-{\mathrm{h}}_{m}\|_{{\mathscr{H}}_{\mathbbm{k}}}\leq\varepsilon
, which implies that
\{{\mathrm{h}}_{n}\}_{n\in{\mathbb{N}},n\geq\underline{\tau}}
is a Cauchy sequence in
{\mathscr{H}}_{\mathbbm{k}}
. Therefore, there exists
{\mathrm{h}}=(h_{s})_{s\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}}\in{\mathscr{H}}_{\mathbbm{k}}
such that
\lim_{n\to\infty}{\mathrm{h}}_{n}={\mathrm{h}}
.
Based on an argument similar to (
52
), one can show that
\lim_{n\to\infty}h_{n,s}=h_{s}
, for any
s\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
.
Since
\mathbbm{k}(s,\cdot)\in{\mathscr{H}}_{\mathbbm{k}}
and the elements of
{\mathscr{H}}_{\mathbbm{k}}
are integrable, due to the dominated convergence theorem
[
60
]
, we have

\begin{split}h_{s}&=\lim_{n\to\infty}h_{n}(s)\\
&=\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\underline{\tau}}^{n}\mathbbm{k}(s,t)\mathrm{d}t\\
&=\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\underline{\tau}}^{\infty}\mathbbm{k}(s,t)\mathbf{1}_{[{\underline{\tau}},n]}(t)\mathrm{d}t\\
&=\int_{\underline{\tau}}^{\infty}\mathbbm{k}(s,t)\mathrm{d}t.\!\!\!\!\!\!\end{split}

(60)

In other words, one has
{\mathrm{h}}=\int_{\underline{\tau}}^{\infty}\mathbbm{k}(\cdot,t)\mathrm{d}t
.
Hence, from
\lim_{n\to\infty}{\mathrm{h}}_{n}={\mathrm{h}}
and the above discussion, we have

\begin{split}\!\!\!\!\Big\|\!&\int_{\underline{\tau}}^{\infty}\!\mathbbm{k}(\cdot,t)\mathrm{d}t\Big\|_{{\mathscr{H}}_{\mathbbm{k}}}^{2}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\|{\mathrm{h}}_{n}\|_{{\mathscr{H}}_{\mathbbm{k}}}^{2}\\
&=\!\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\underline{\tau}}^{n}\!\!\!\int_{\underline{\tau}}^{n}\!\mathbbm{k}(s,t)\mathrm{d}s\mathrm{d}t\\
&=\!\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\underline{\tau}}^{\infty}\!\!\!\int_{\underline{\tau}}^{\infty}\!\!\mathbbm{k}(s,t)\mathbf{1}_{[{\underline{\tau}},n]^{2}}(s,t)\mathrm{d}s\mathrm{d}t\\
&=\!\int_{\underline{\tau}}^{\infty}\!\!\!\!\int_{\underline{\tau}}^{\infty}\!\!\!\!\mathbbm{k}(s,t)\mathrm{d}s\mathrm{d}t,\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\end{split}

(61)

where the last equality is according to the dominated convergence theorem.
Let
{\mathrm{g}}=(g_{t})_{t\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}}
be an arbitrary element of
{\mathscr{H}}_{\mathbbm{k}}
.
Based on same arguments as before, one can see that

\begin{split}\Big\langle\int_{\underline{\tau}}^{\infty}\mathbbm{k}(\cdot,t)\mathrm{d}t,&{\mathrm{g}}\Big\rangle_{{\mathscr{H}}_{\mathbbm{k}}}=\lim_{n\to\infty}{\langle{{\mathrm{h}}_{n},{\mathrm{g}}}\rangle}_{{\mathscr{H}}_{\mathbbm{k}}}\\
=&\lim_{n\to\infty}\Big\langle\int_{\underline{\tau}}^{n}\mathbbm{k}(\cdot,t)\mathrm{d}t,{\mathrm{g}}\Big\rangle_{{\mathscr{H}}_{\mathbbm{k}}}\\
=&\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\underline{\tau}}^{n}g_{t}\mathrm{d}t\\
=&\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\underline{\tau}}^{\infty}g_{t}\mathbf{1}_{[{\underline{\tau}},n]}(t)\mathrm{d}t\\
=&\int_{\underline{\tau}}^{\infty}g_{t}\mathrm{d}t,\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\end{split}

(62)

where the last equality is due to the dominated convergence theorem and the fact that
{\mathrm{g}}
is integrable.
∎

A-C

Proof of Lemma
6

We know that
\mathbbm{k}(\cdot,t)\in{\mathscr{H}}_{\mathbbm{k}}
, for each
t=\underline{\tau},\ldots,\overline{\tau}
.
If
\overline{\tau}
is finite, one can see that
\sum_{\underline{\tau}\leq t\leq\overline{\tau}}\mathbbm{k}(\cdot,t)
belongs to
{\mathscr{H}}_{\mathbbm{k}}
, hence, it is well-defined.
Moreover, using the definition of norm and the reproducing property, one can show (
13
). Similarly, (
14
) is concluded from the reproducing property.
Now, we consider the case
\overline{\tau}=\infty
.
For
n\geq\underline{\tau}
, we define
{\mathrm{f}}_{n}\in{\mathscr{H}}_{\mathbbm{k}}
as

{\mathrm{f}}_{n}=(f_{n,s})_{s\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}}:=\sum_{\underline{\tau}\leq t\leq n}\mathbbm{k}(\cdot,t).

Let
\varepsilon
be an arbitrary positive real scalar, and
N_{\varepsilon}
be the smallest non-negative integer such that

\sum_{s,t\geq N_{\varepsilon}}|\mathbbm{k}(s,t)|\leq\varepsilon.

Note that since
\mathbbm{k}
is integrable, there exist such
N_{\varepsilon}
for any positive
\varepsilon
.
Now, let
n,m\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that
n,m\geq N_{\varepsilon}
and without loss of generality, we assume
n\geq m
.
Based on the previous case, we know that

\begin{split}\|{\mathrm{f}}_{n}-{\mathrm{f}}_{m}\|_{{\mathscr{H}}_{\mathbbm{k}}}^{2}&=\Big\|\sum_{t=m+1}^{n}\mathbbm{k}(\cdot,t)\Big\|_{{\mathscr{H}}_{\mathbbm{k}}}^{2}\\
&=\sum_{m+1\leq s,t\leq n}\mathbbm{k}(s,t)\\
&\leq\sum_{N_{\varepsilon}\leq s,t}|\mathbbm{k}(s,t)|\\
&\leq\varepsilon^{2}.\end{split}

Accordingly, we have
\|{\mathrm{f}}_{n}-{\mathrm{f}}_{m}\|_{{\mathscr{H}}_{\mathbbm{k}}}\leq\varepsilon
, which implies that
\{{\mathrm{f}}_{n}\}_{n\geq\underline{\tau}}
is a Cauchy sequence and hence convergent.
Let
{\mathrm{f}}=(f_{s})_{s\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}}
denote the limit of this sequence.
For any
s\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}
, we know that

\begin{split}|f_{s}-f_{n,s}|&=|{\langle{{\mathrm{f}}-{\mathrm{f}}_{n},\mathbbm{k}_{s}}\rangle}_{{\mathscr{H}}_{\mathbbm{k}}}|\\
&\leq\mathbbm{k}(s,s)^{\frac{1}{2}}\|{\mathrm{f}}-{\mathrm{f}}_{n}\|_{{\mathscr{H}}_{\mathbbm{k}}},\end{split}

(63)

and consequently, we have
\lim_{n\to\infty}f_{n,s}=f_{s}
.
Subsequently, since
\mathbbm{k}(s,\cdot)
is absolutely integrable, it follows that

f_{s}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{\underline{\tau}\leq t\leq n}\mathbbm{k}(s,t)=\sum_{\underline{\tau}\leq t}\mathbbm{k}(s,t),

(64)

i.e.,
{\mathrm{f}}=\sum_{\underline{\tau}\leq t}\mathbbm{k}(\cdot,t)
.
Moreover, we have

\begin{split}\!\!\Big\|\sum_{\underline{\tau}\leq t}\mathbbm{k}(\cdot,t)\Big\|_{{\mathscr{H}}_{\mathbbm{k}}}^{2}&=\lim_{n\to\infty}\|{\mathrm{f}}_{n}\|_{{\mathscr{H}}_{\mathbbm{k}}}^{2}\\
&=\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{\underline{\tau}\leq s,t\leq n}\mathbbm{k}(s,t)\\
&=\sum_{\underline{\tau}\leq s,t}\mathbbm{k}(s,t),\end{split}

(65)

where the last equality is due to the dominated convergence theorem
[
60
]
and being
\mathbbm{k}
integrable.
For any
{\mathrm{g}}=(g_{t})_{t\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}}\in{\mathscr{H}}_{\mathbbm{k}}
, we know that
{\mathrm{g}}
is integrable, i.e.,
\sum_{t\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}}|g_{t}|<\infty
.
Therefore,
from
\lim_{n\to\infty}{\mathrm{f}}_{n}={\mathrm{f}}
,
we have

\begin{split}\sum_{\tau\leq t}g_{t}&=\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{\tau\leq t\leq n}g_{t}\\
&=\lim_{n\to\infty}\Big\langle\sum_{\tau\leq t\leq n}\mathbbm{k}(\cdot,t),{\mathrm{g}}\Big\rangle_{{\mathscr{H}}_{\mathbbm{k}}}\\
&=\lim_{n\to\infty}{\langle{{\mathrm{f}}_{n},{\mathrm{g}}}\rangle}_{{\mathscr{H}}_{\mathbbm{k}}}\\
&={\langle{{\mathrm{f}},{\mathrm{g}}}\rangle}_{{\mathscr{H}}_{\mathbbm{k}}}\\
&=\Big\langle\sum_{\underline{\tau}\leq t}\mathbbm{k}(\cdot,t),{\mathrm{g}}\Big\rangle_{{\mathscr{H}}_{\mathbbm{k}}}.\end{split}

(66)

This concludes the proof.
∎

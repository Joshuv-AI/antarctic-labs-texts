Characterization of Sasakian manifolds
Vladimir Rovenski

Abstract
Weak contact metric manifolds, i.e., the linear complex structure on the contact distribution is replaced by a nonsingular skew-symmetric tensor, defined by the author and R. Wolak, allowed us to take a new look at the theory of contact manifolds. In this paper, we continue our study [ 16 , 17 ] of a structure of this type, called a weak nearly Sasakian structure, and prove two theorems characterizing Sasakian manifolds. Our main result generalizes the theorem by A. Nicola–G. Dileo–I. Yudin (2018) and provides a new criterion for a weak almost contact metric manifold to be Sasakian. Keywords : Almost contact structure, nearly Sasakian manifold, Riemannian curvature. Mathematics Subject Classifications (2010) 53C15, 53C25, 53D15

Introduction

Contact Riemannian geometry plays an important role in both pure mathematics and theoretical physics.
It considers a
(2n+1)
-dimensional smooth manifold
M
equipped with an almost contact metric structure
(\varphi,\xi,\eta,g)
, i.e.,

g
is a Riemannian metric with the Levi-Civita connection
\nabla
,

\varphi
is a
(1,1)
-tensor,
\xi
is a unit vector field and
\eta
is its dual 1-form (
\eta(\xi)=1
) satisfying

g(\varphi X,\varphi Y)=g(X,Y)-\eta(X)\,\eta(Y)
for any
X,Y\in\mathfrak{X}_{M}
,
and
\mathfrak{X}_{M}
is the space of smooth vector fields on
M
.
An important class of almost contact metric manifolds
M^{\,2n+1}(\varphi,\xi,\eta,g)
consists of Sasakian manifolds characterized by the equality, see
[
1
, Theorem 6.3]
,

(\nabla_{X}\,\varphi)Y=g(X,Y)\,\xi-\eta(Y)X,\quad X,Y\in\mathfrak{X}_{M}.

(1)

D. Blair, D. Showers and Y. Komatu
[
2
]
defined nearly Sasakian structure
(\varphi,\xi,\eta,g)

using a condition similar to (
1
) for the symmetric part of
\nabla\varphi
:

(\nabla_{Y}\,\varphi)Y=g(Y,Y)\,\xi-\eta(Y)Y,\quad Y\in\mathfrak{X}_{M},

(2)

and showed that a normal nearly Sasakian structure is Sasakian and hence is contact.
Nearly Sasakian structure is a counterpart of the nearly Kähler structure
(J,g)
defined by A. Gray
[
6
]
by the condition that the symmetric part of
\nabla J
vanishes, in contrast to the Kähler case where
\nabla J=0
.
An example of a nearly Sasakian (non-Sasakian) manifold is the sphere
S^{\,5}
endowed with an almost contact metric structure induced by the almost Hermitian structure of
S^{\,6}
.

The curvature and topology of nearly Sasakian manifolds have been studied by several authors, e.g.,
Z. Olszak, see
[
11
,
12
]
, B. Cappelletti-Montano, G. Dileo, A.D. Nicola and I. Yudin, see
[
3
,
10
]
.
Any nearly Sasakian manifold of dimension greater than 5 is Sasakian, see
[
10
]
,
and any 5-dimensional nearly Sasakian manifold has Einstein metric of positive scalar curvature, see
[
3
]
;
and a 3-dimensional nearly Sasakian manifold is Sasakian, see
[
12
]
.

In
[
13
,
14
,
15
]
and
[
19
, Section 5.3.8]
, we introduced metric structures
that generalize the almost contact, Sasakian, etc. metric structures.
In
[
16
,
17
]
we investigated new structures of this type, called weak nearly Sasakian structure
and weak nearly Kähler structure, and asked the question:
under what conditions are weak nearly Sasakian manifolds Sasakian
?

These so-called “weak” structures (the linear complex structure on the contact distribution is replaced by a nonsingular skew-symmetric tensor)
made it possible to take a new look at the classical theory and find new applications,
e.g., in Sasakian twistor spinors theory, see E.C. Kim
[
8
]
.
For an oriented Riemannian manifold, its twistor space can be seen as the space of linear complex structures on the tangent space
that are compatible with the metric
g
and orientation.
Some authors, for example, A.C. Herrera in
[
7
]
, studied the problem of finding parallel skew-symmetric 2-tensors different from almost-complex structures on a Riemannian manifold and classified such tensors.
We believe that theories like twistor spinors can be naturally extended by considering non-singular skew-symmetric tensors instead of linear complex structures.

In this article, we continue our study of the geometry of weak nearly Sasakian manifolds and
find new criterions for a weak almost contact metric manifold to be Sasakian.
Section
2
, following the introductory Section
1
, reviews the basics of weak almost contact manifolds
and auxiliary results on the geometry of weak almost Sasakian structure with conditions (
5
)–(
6
).
Section
3
presents some properties of the curvature tensor and covariant derivatives of tensors
\varphi
and
h
.
In Theorem
2
, we consider weak almost contact metric manifolds with the condition (
5
)
and characterize Sasakian manifolds in this class using the property (
1
).
In Theorem
3
(generalizing Theorem 3.3 from
[
10
]
, see also Theorem 4.9 from
[
4
]
) we characterize Sasakian manifolds of dimension greater than five in the class of weak almost contact metric manifolds with conditions (
5
)–(
6
).
In the proof of Theorem
3
we also use our result from
[
17
]
(see Theorem
1
) that
under certain conditions the weak nearly Sasakian structure foliates into two types of totally geodesic foliations.
In Section
4

3

Our proofs use the properties of new tensors, as well as classical constructions.

2
Preliminaries

A weak almost contact metric structure on a smooth manifold
M^{2n+1}\ (n>0)
is a set
(\varphi,Q,\xi,\eta,g)
,
where
\varphi
is a
(1,1)
-tensor,
\xi
is a vector field,
\eta
is a dual 1-form (
\eta(\xi)=1
),

g
is a Riemannian metric and
Q
is a nonsingular
(1,1)
-tensor on
M
, satisfying, see
[
13
,
14
]
,

\displaystyle g(\varphi X,\varphi Y)=g(X,Q\,Y)-\eta(X)\,\eta(Y),\quad X,Y\in\mathfrak{X}_{M}.

(3)

Assume that a
2n
-dimensional distribution
\ker\eta
is
\varphi
-invariant, i.e.,
\varphi(\ker\eta)\subset\ker\eta
,
as in the classical theory
[
1
]
, where
Q={\rm id}_{\,TM}
.
By this and (
3
),
\ker\eta
is
Q
-invariant and the following hold:

\displaystyle\varphi\,\xi=0,\quad\eta\circ\varphi=0,\quad\eta\circ Q=\eta,\quad[Q,\,\varphi]:={Q}\circ\varphi-\varphi\circ{Q}=0.

For a weak almost contact metric structure,
\varphi
is skew-symmetric and
Q
is self-adjoint and positive definite.
Setting
Y=\xi
in (
3
), we obtain
\eta(X)=g(\xi,X)
.
We get a weak contact metric structure is the following is true:

d\eta(X,Y)=g(X,\varphi Y),\quad X,Y\in\mathfrak{X}_{M},

where the exterior derivative
d\eta
of
\eta
is given by

d\eta(X,Y)=\frac{1}{2}\,\{X(\eta(Y))-Y(\eta(X))-\eta([X,Y])\}
, for example,
[
1
]
.
A 1-form
\eta
on a manifold
M^{\,2n+1}
is said to be contact if
\eta\wedge(d\eta)^{n}\neq 0
, e.g.,
[
1
]
.
For a weak contact metric structure
(\varphi,Q,\xi,\eta,g)
, the 1-form
\eta
is contact, see
[
18
, Lemma 2.1]
.

Definition 1

(see
[
16
]
)
.

A weak almost contact metric manifold
M^{\,2n+1}(\varphi,Q,\xi,\eta,g)
is called
weak nearly Sasakian
if (
2
) is true, or, equivalently,

(\nabla_{Y}\,\varphi)Z+(\nabla_{Z}\,\varphi)Y=2\,g(Y,Z)\,\xi-\eta(Z)Y-\eta(Y)Z,\qquad Y,Z\in\mathfrak{X}_{M}.

(4)

In addition to (
4
), the following two conditions, which are automatically satisfied by almost contact metric manifolds,
play a significant role in this paper:

\displaystyle(\nabla_{X}\,\widetilde{Q})\,Y=0,\quad X\in\mathfrak{X}_{M},\ Y\in\ker\eta,

(5)

\displaystyle R_{\widetilde{Q}X,Y}Z\in\ker\eta,\quad X,Y,Z\in\ker\eta,

(6)

where

\widetilde{Q}=Q-{\rm id}_{\,TM}
is a “small” tensor, and

R_{{X},{Y}}=\nabla_{X}\nabla_{Y}-\nabla_{Y}\nabla_{X}-\nabla_{[X,Y]}
for all
X,Y\in\mathfrak{X}_{M}
,
is the curvature tensor, e.g.,
[
5
]
.
From (
6
) and the Bianchi identity we get

R_{\,X,Y}\,\widetilde{Q}Z\in\ker\eta,\quad X,Y,Z\in\ker\eta.

Using the equalities
(\nabla_{\xi}\,\varphi)\,\xi=0
and
\varphi\,\xi=0
for a weak nearly Sasakian manifold, we conclude that
\xi
is a geodesic field (
\nabla_{\xi}\,\xi=0
).
From (
5
) and
\nabla_{\xi}\,\xi=0
, we get

\nabla_{\xi}\,\widetilde{Q}=0.

The
\xi
-curves form a Riemannian geodesic foliation, and
-\nabla\xi
is its splitting operator (co-nullity tensor), see
[
19
, Section 1.3.1]
.
Taking derivative of the equality
g(\varphi V,Z)=-g(V,\varphi Z)
, we see that the tensor
\nabla_{Y}\,\varphi
of a weak nearly Sasakian manifold is skew-symmetric:

g((\nabla_{Y}\,\varphi)V,Z)=-g((\nabla_{Y}\,\varphi)Z,V).

(7)

The Ricci identity (commutation formula) for the (1,1)-tensor
\varphi
can be written as (e.g.,
[
5
]
)

g((\nabla^{2}_{X,Y}\,\varphi)V,Z)-g((\nabla^{2}_{Y,X}\,\varphi)V,Z)=g(R_{\,X,Y}\,\varphi V,Z)+g(R_{\,X,Y}V,\varphi Z).

(8)

where the second covariant derivative is given by
\nabla^{2}_{X,Y}=\nabla_{X}\nabla_{Y}-\nabla_{\nabla_{X}Y}
.
Taking covariant derivative of (
7
), we see that
\nabla^{2}_{X,Y}\,\varphi
of a weak nearly Sasakian manifold is skew-symmetric:

g((\nabla^{2}_{X,Y}\,\varphi)V,Z)=-g((\nabla^{2}_{X,Y}\,\varphi)Z,V).

In the rest of the section, we survey some properties of weak nearly Sasakian manifolds, see
[
17
]
.
Define a (1,1)-tensor field
h
on
M
(as in the classical case, e.g.,
[
10
]
),

h=\nabla\xi+\varphi.

(9)

We get
\eta\circ h=0
and
h(\ker\eta)\subset\ker\eta
. Since
\xi
is a geodesic vector field, we also get
h\,\xi=0
.
Since
\xi
is a Killing vector field, see
[
16
]
, and
\varphi
is skew-symmetric, the tensor
h
is skew-symmetric.
The distribution
\ker\eta
is integrable,
[X,Y]\in\ker\eta\ (X,Y\in\ker\eta)
, if and only if
h=\varphi
,
and in this case, our manifold splits along
\xi
and
\ker\eta
, i.e., is locally the metric product.

Lemma 1

(see
[
17
]
)
.

For a weak nearly Sasakian manifold
M^{\,2n+1}(\varphi,Q,\xi,\eta,g)
we obtain

\displaystyle(\nabla_{X}\,h)\,\xi=-h(h-\varphi)X,

(10)

\displaystyle(\nabla_{X}\,\varphi)\,\xi=-\varphi(h-\varphi)X,

(11)

\displaystyle h\,\varphi+\varphi\,h=-2\,\widetilde{Q},

(12)

\displaystyle h\,Q=Q\,h\quad(h\ {\rm commutes\ with}\ Q).

(13)

Moreover,

h^{2}\varphi=\varphi h^{2}
,
h\varphi^{2}=\varphi^{2}h
,
h^{2}\varphi^{2}=\varphi^{2}h^{2}
, etc.

If a weak nearly Sasakian manifold satisfies the condition (
6
), then its contact distribution
is curvature invariant, see
[
17
]
, i.e.,

R_{\,X,Y}Z\in\ker\eta,\quad X,Y,Z\in\ker\eta.

(14)

For example, the tangent bundle of a totally geodesic submanifold in a Riemannian manifold and the distribution
\ker\eta
of any 1-form
\eta
on a real space form satisfy (
14
).

Lemma 2

(see
[
17
]
)
.

For a weak nearly Sasakian manifold
M^{\,2n+1}(\varphi,Q,\xi,\eta,g)
with the condition
(
6
), we obtain

\displaystyle R_{\,X,\xi}\,Y=(\nabla_{X}\,(h-\varphi)\,)\,Y=(\nabla_{X}\,(h-\varphi))Y=g((h-\varphi)^{2}X,Y)\,\xi-\eta(Y)\,(h-\varphi)^{2}X.

(15)

In particular,
\nabla_{\xi}\,h=\nabla_{\xi}\,\varphi=\varphi h+\widetilde{Q}
and

\displaystyle g(R_{\,\xi,X}Y,Z)=\eta(Y)\,g((h-\varphi)^{2}X,Z)-\eta(Z)\,g((h-\varphi)^{2}X,Y).

(16)

A Sasakian manifold is defined as a contact metric manifold such that the tensor

[\varphi,\varphi]+d\eta\otimes\xi
vanishes identically,
where the Nijenhuis torsion
[\varphi,\varphi]
of
\varphi

is given by (for example,
[
1
]
)

\displaystyle[\varphi,\varphi](X,Y)=\varphi^{2}[X,Y]+[\varphi X,\varphi Y]-\varphi[\varphi X,Y]-\varphi[X,\varphi Y].

The following statement, generalizing
[
12
, Lemma 2.1]
, will be used in the proof of Theorem
2
.

Proposition 1

(see also
[
17
]
)
.

For a weak nearly Sasakian manifold with the property (
5
), the equality
h=0
holds if and only if the manifold is Sasakian.

For a weak nearly Sasakian manifold satisfying (
5
) and (
6
),
the eigenvalues (and their multiplicities) of the self-adjoint operator
h^{2}
are constant, see
[
17
]
.
Since
h
is skew-symmetric, the nonzero eigenvalues of
h^{2}
are negative, and the spectrum of
h^{2}
has the form

Spec(h^{2})=\{0,-\lambda_{1}^{2},\ldots-\lambda_{r}^{2}\},

(17)

where
\lambda_{i}
is a positive real number and
\lambda_{i}\neq\lambda_{j}
for
i\neq j
.
If
X
is a unit eigenvector of
h^{2}
with eigenvalue
-\lambda^{2}_{i}
, then by (
12
) and (
13
),

X,\varphi X,hX
and
h\,\varphi X
are nonzero eigenvectors of
h^{2}
with eigenvalue
-\lambda^{2}_{i}
.
Denote by
[\xi]
the 1-dimensional distribution generated by
\xi
,
and by
D_{0}
a distribution of the eigenvectors with eigenvalue 0 orthogonal to
\xi
.
Denote by
D_{i}
a distribution of the eigenvectors with eigenvalue
-\lambda^{2}_{i}
.
Note that

D_{0}
and
D_{i}\ (i=1,\ldots,r)
belong to
\ker\eta
and are
\varphi
- and
h
- invariant.
The tensors
h
and
\widetilde{Q}
of a weak nearly Sasakian manifold vanish on
D_{0}
,
[
17
]
.

The following statement generalizes
[
3
, Theorems 3.3 and 3.5]
.

Theorem 1

(see
[
17
]
)
.

Let
M^{\,2n+1}(\varphi,Q,\xi,\eta,g)
be a weak nearly Sasakian manifold with conditions (
5
) and (
6
),
and let the spectrum of the self-adjoint operator
h^{2}
have the form (

).
Then, the distribution
[\xi]\oplus D_{0}
and each distribution
[\xi]\oplus D_{i}\ (i=1,\ldots,r)
are integrable with totally geodesic leaves.
In particular, the eigenvalue
0
has multiplicity
2p+1
for some integer
p\geq 0
.
If

, then

(a)

the distribution
[\xi]\oplus D_{1}\oplus\ldots\oplus D_{r}
is integrable and defines a Riemannian foliation with totally geodesic leaves;

(b)

the leaves of
[\xi]\oplus D_{0}
are
(2p+1)
-dimensional Sasakian manifolds.

3
Main results

Here, we prove some properties of the curvature tensor and covariant derivatives of the tensors
\varphi
and
h
,
and prove two theorems characterizing Sasakian manifolds in the class of weak almost contact metric manifolds.

First, we consider weak almost contact metric manifolds with the condition (
5

characterize Sasakian manifolds in this class using the property (

Theorem 2
.

Let
M(\varphi,Q,\xi,\eta,g)
be a weak almost contact metric manifold with conditions (
1
) and (
5
).
Then
Q={\rm id}_{\,TM}
and the structure
(\varphi,\xi,\eta,g)
is Sasakian.

Proof.

Differentiating the equality
g(\varphi\,Y,\xi)=0
yields

0=Xg(\varphi\,Y,\xi)=g((\nabla_{X}\,\varphi)Y,\xi)+g(\varphi\,Y,(h-\varphi)X).

Applying to this (
1
) and then (
3
) gives
h\varphi=-\widetilde{Q}
. Since
h,\varphi
are skew-symmetric and
\widetilde{Q}
is self-adjoint, we also get
\varphi h=-\widetilde{Q}
. Therefore,

h\varphi=\varphi h=-\widetilde{Q}.

(18)

Differentiating (
3
) and using (
5
), (
18
) and the skew-symmetry of
\nabla_{X}\,\varphi
, we get

-\eta(Y)\,g(hX,Z)-\eta(Z)\,g(\varphi X+Q\,(h-\varphi)X,Y)=0.

Taking
Y=\xi
in the above equality, we get
g(hX,Z)=0
for all
X,Z\in\mathfrak{X}_{M}
.
Therefore,
h=\widetilde{Q}=0
and by Proposition
1
, the structure is Sasakian.
∎

Lemma 4
.

The curvature tensor of a weak nearly Sasakian manifold satisfies

\displaystyle g(R_{\,\varphi X,Y}Z,V)+g(R_{\,X,\varphi Y}Z,V)+g(R_{\,X,Y}\,\varphi Z,V)+g(R_{\,X,Y}Z,\varphi V)

\displaystyle=g(Y,V)\,g((h-\varphi)X,Z)-g(X,Y)\,g(Z,(h-\varphi)V)+g(Y,Z)\,g(X,(h-\varphi)V)

\displaystyle g(R_{\,\varphi X,\varphi Y}Z,V)=g(R_{\,X,Y}\,\varphi Z,\varphi V)-(1/2)\,\delta(X,Y,Z,V)-g(X,Y)\,g(Z,\widetilde{Q}V)

\displaystyle\ +(1/2)\,g(Y,V)\,g(\varphi(h-\varphi)X,Z)-(1/2)\,g(Y,\varphi Z)\,g(X,(h-\varphi)V)

\displaystyle\ -(1/2)\,g(Y,\varphi V)\,g((h-\varphi)X,Z)-(1/2)\,g(Y,Z)\,g(X,(h-\varphi)\varphi V)

\displaystyle\ -(1/4)\,g(X,\varphi Z)\,g(Y,(h-\varphi)V)-(1/4)\,g(X,Z)\,g(Y,(h-\varphi)\varphi V),

Remark 1
.

The tensor
\delta
of a weak nearly Sasakian manifold has the following symmetries:

\displaystyle\delta(Y,X,Z,V)=\delta(X,Y,V,Z)=\delta(Z,V,X,Y)=-\delta(X,Y,Z,V).

If (
6

\displaystyle\delta(X,Y,Z,\xi)=g(R_{\,X,Y}\,\widetilde{Q}Z,\xi)-g(R_{\widetilde{Q}X,Y}Z,\xi)-g(R_{\,X,\widetilde{Q}Y}Z,\xi)

\displaystyle\qquad=g(R_{\,\xi,\,\widetilde{Q}Z}Y,X)+g(R_{\,\xi,Z}\,\widetilde{Q}X,Y)+g(R_{\,\xi,Z}\,X,\widetilde{Q}Y)=0,

hence,
\delta(\xi,Y,Z,V)=\delta(X,\xi,Z,V)=\delta(X,Y,\xi,V)=\delta(X,Y,Z,\xi)=0
.

For a weak nearly Sasakian manifold with conditions (

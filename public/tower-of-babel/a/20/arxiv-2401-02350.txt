Locally dualizable modules abound
Jon F. Carlson, Srikanth B. Iyengar

Abstract
. It is proved that given any prime ideal 𝔭 \mathfrak{p} of height at least 2 in a countable commutative noetherian ring A A , there are uncountably many more dualizable objects in the 𝔭 \mathfrak{p} -local 𝔭 \mathfrak{p} -torsion stratum of the derived category of A A than those that are obtained as retracts of images of perfect A A -complexes. An analogous result is established dealing with the stable module category of the group algebra, over a countable field of positive characteristic p p , of an elementary abelian p p -group of rank at least 3.

13D09 (primary); 18G80, 14F08 (secondary)

1.
Introduction

This work is about dualizable objects in tensor triangulated
categories arising in commutative algebra and the modular
representation theory of finite groups. An object
D
in a
tensor triangulated category
\mathcal{C}
is
dualizable

if the natural map

\operatorname{\mathcal{H}\!\!\;\mathit{om}}(D,\mathds{1})\otimes X\longrightarrow\operatorname{\mathcal{H}\!\!\;\mathit{om}}(D,X)

is an isomorphism for all
X
in
\mathcal{C}
. Here
\operatorname{\mathcal{H}\!\!\;\mathit{om}}
and

\otimes
are the internal function object and product in

\mathcal{C}
, respectively, and
\mathds{1}
is the unit of the product.

Consider
\operatorname{D}(\operatorname{Mod}A)
, the derived category of a commutative noetherian
ring
A
, with tensor structure given by the derived tensor product

-\otimes^{\mathbf{L}}-
, the unit is
A
, and function object is
\operatorname{RHom}_{A}(-,-)
.
The dualizable objects in
\operatorname{D}(\operatorname{Mod}A)
are precisely the perfect

A
-complexes,
\operatorname{Perf}A
. These are also the compact objects, and hence

\operatorname{D}(\operatorname{Mod}A)
is rigid as a compactly generated tensor triangulated category.

We consider also
\operatorname{StMod}(kG)
the stable module category of
a finite group
G
, with
k
a field of positive characteristic
dividing
|G|
. In this case the tensor structure is given by

-\otimes_{k}-
with diagonal
G
action and the function object
is
\operatorname{Hom}_{k}(-,-)
, again with diagonal
G
-action; the unit is
k

with trivial
G
-action. The dualizable objects are those in

\operatorname{stmod}(kG)
, namely, the
kG
modules that are stably isomorphic
to finite dimensional ones, and hence coincide with the compact
objects, so
\operatorname{StMod}(kG)
is also rigid.

Both
\operatorname{D}(\operatorname{Mod}A)
and
\operatorname{StMod}(kG)
admit natural stratifications
into “local” triangulated subcategories that determine, to a
large extent, their global structure. In
\operatorname{D}(\operatorname{Mod}A)
the strata
are parameterized by points
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Spec}A
and the stratum
corresponding to
\mathfrak{p}
consists of the
\mathfrak{p}
-local and
\mathfrak{p}
-torsion
complexes in
\operatorname{D}(\operatorname{Mod}A)
, which is denoted
\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}\operatorname{D}(\operatorname{Mod}A)
;
see Section
2
for details. There is an
analogous stratification
\operatorname{StMod}(kG)
, with parameter space

\operatorname{Proj}H^{*}(G,k)
; see Section
3
. In both cases,
the strata are again tensor triangulated subcategories, and it is
of interest to understand the dualizable objects in these categories.
A noteworthy feature now is that, unless
\mathfrak{p}
is minimal, there are
many more dualizable objects than compact ones, so the strata are not rigid.

There is a natural functor
\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}\colon\operatorname{D}(\operatorname{Mod}A)\to\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}\operatorname{D}(\operatorname{Mod}A)
and the dualizable objects in
\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}\operatorname{D}(\operatorname{Mod}A)
are generated as a thick subcategory by
\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}A
; see
[
3
, Theorem]
. The question arose whether
\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}\operatorname{Perf}A
is dense in the subcategory of local dualizable objects; in other words, whether each dualizable object in
\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}\operatorname{D}(\operatorname{Mod}A)
is a retract of a complex
\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}P
, with
P
a perfect
A
-complex. The point of this paper is that when
A
is countable, there are uncountably many, mutually non-isomorphic, indecomposable dualizable objects in
\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}\operatorname{D}(\operatorname{Mod}A)
, but at most countably many that are retracts of images of perfect complexes in
A
; see Theorem
2.1
.

There is an analogous description of the dualizable objects in
\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}\operatorname{StMod}(kG)
, established in
[
4
]
, and once again it turns that there can be many more dualizable objects in this strata than direct summands of those induced from
\operatorname{stmod}(kG)
, the global dualizable objects; see Theorem
3.1
.

2.
Local algebra

Let
A
be a commutative noetherian ring and
\operatorname{D}(\operatorname{Mod}A)
the (full) derived category of
A
-modules, viewed as a tensor triangulated category, where the product of
A
-complexes
X,Y
is the derived tensor product
X\otimes^{\mathbf{L}}_{A}Y
. The derived category is rigidly compactly generated, with compact objects the perfect
A
-complexes, that is to say, those that are isomorphic, in
\operatorname{D}(\operatorname{Mod}A)
, to bounded complexes of finitely generated projective modules. We denote this category
\operatorname{Perf}A
. It is the thick subcategory of
\operatorname{D}(\operatorname{Mod}A)
generated by
A
.

Given a point
\mathfrak{p}\subseteq\operatorname{Spec}R
consider the exact functor

\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}\colon\operatorname{D}(\operatorname{Mod}A)\to\operatorname{D}(\operatorname{Mod}A)\quad\text{where $X\mapsto\mathbf{R}\varGamma_{V(\mathfrak{p})}(X_{\mathfrak{p}})$}

where
\mathbf{R}\varGamma_{V(\mathfrak{p})}(-)
is the functor representing local cohomology with support in the ideal
\mathfrak{p}
of
A
. The image
\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}\operatorname{D}(\operatorname{Mod}A)
consists of precisely the
\mathfrak{p}
-local and
\mathfrak{p}
-torsion complexes. It is a tensor triangulated category in its own right, with product induced from that on
\operatorname{D}(\operatorname{Mod}A)
, unit
\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}A
, and function object
\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}\operatorname{RHom}_{A}(-,-)
. It is not rigid, unless
\mathfrak{p}
is minimal, and there are many more rigid objects than compact ones. See
[
3
, Section 4]
for proofs of these assertions.

The main result of this section is as follows.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
A
be a countable commutative noetherian ring and
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Spec}A
such that
\mathrm{height}\,\mathfrak{p}\geq 2
. There exist uncountably many mutually non-isomorphic indecomposable dualizable objects in
\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}\operatorname{D}(\operatorname{Mod}A)
none of which is a retract of an object in
\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}\operatorname{Perf}A
.

In fact the argument, which is given towards the end of this section,
shows that
\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}\operatorname{Perf}A
contains only countably many isomorphism
classes of objects, even allowing for retracts, but that there are
uncountably many non-isomorphic indecomposable objects
\operatorname{Thick}(\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}A)
.
It is easy to check that the latter subcategory consists of dualizable
objects in
\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}\operatorname{D}(\operatorname{Mod}A)
. As it happens, these are all the dualizable
objects. This is the main result in
[
3
]
,
but we do not need this fact here.

We record a simple observation.

Lemma 2.2
.

When
A
is a countable noetherian ring, there are only countably many
isomorphism classes of objects in
\operatorname{mod}A
, and, more generally,
only countably many isomorphism classes in
\operatorname{D}^{b}(\operatorname{mod}A)
.

Proof.

Any finitely generated
A
-module occurs as a cokernel of a map
A^{m}\to A^{n}
, for each nonnegative integers
m,n
, and each such map is given by a matrix of size
m\times n
with coefficients in
A
. Since
A
is countable, there are only countably many such matrices, which justifies the claim about
\operatorname{mod}A
. The one about complexes follows because in
\operatorname{D}(\operatorname{Mod}A)
any complex with finitely generated homology is isomorphic to one of the form

0\longrightarrow M_{n}\longrightarrow F_{n-1}\longrightarrow\cdots\longrightarrow F_{1}\longrightarrow F_{0}\longrightarrow 0\,,

where
M_{n}
is in
\operatorname{mod}R
and each
F_{i}
is a finite free
A
-module.
∎

The result below is also well-known; see
[
9
, p. 500]
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
A
be a commutative noetherian local ring that is complete with
respect to
\mathfrak{m}
, its maximal ideal. If
\dim A\geq 2
, then there
exists an uncountable collection of distinct prime ideals in
A
,
each of height one. In the same vein, there is an uncountable
collection of elements
\{a_{u}\}_{u\in U}
with

\sqrt{a_{u}}\neq\sqrt{a_{v}}
for
u\neq v
.

Proof.

Assume to the contrary that there are only countable many prime
ideals
\{\mathfrak{p}_{i}\}_{i\geqslant 1}
of height one. Since each
non-invertible element in
A
is contained in a height one prime,
by Krull’s Principal Ideal theorem, it follows that

\mathfrak{m}\subseteq\cup_{i}\mathfrak{p}_{i}
. Since
A
is complete, it has the
countable prime avoidance property; see, for instance,

[
6
,
10
]
. We deduce that
\mathfrak{m}\subseteq\mathfrak{p}_{i}

for some
i
, contradicting the hypothesis that
\dim A\geq 2
.

Because every prime ideal of height one is minimal over a principal
ideal, and the radical of principal ideal is a finite intersection of
prime ideals, the second part of the assertion follows from the first.
∎

Proof of Theorem
2.1
.

One has that
\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}\operatorname{D}(\operatorname{Mod}A)\simeq\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}\operatorname{D}(\operatorname{Mod}A_{\mathfrak{p}})
, so replacing
A
by its localization at
\mathfrak{p}
we can suppose it is local, say with maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}
, with
\dim A\geq 2
. Let
\widehat{A}
be the
\mathfrak{m}
-adic completion of
A
. A key input in the arguments presented below is that the natural map of rings
A\to\operatorname{Ext}_{A}^{*}(\mathbf{R}\varGamma_{\mathfrak{m}}A,\mathbf{R}\varGamma_{\mathfrak{m}}A)
factors through the completion map
A\to\widehat{A}
and yields an isomorphism

\widehat{A}\xrightarrow{\ \cong\ }\operatorname{Ext}_{A}^{*}(\mathbf{R}\varGamma_{\mathfrak{m}}A,\mathbf{R}\varGamma_{\mathfrak{m}}A).

Hence, derived Morita theory yields the Greenlees-May adjoint equivalence

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Thick}_{\widehat{A}}(\widehat{A})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Thick}_{A}(\mathbf{R}\varGamma_{\mathfrak{m}}A)\,.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{RHom}_{A}(\mathbf{R}\varGamma_{\mathfrak{m}}A,-)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbf{R}\varGamma_{\mathfrak{m}}A\otimes^{\mathbf{L}}_{\widehat{A}}-}

It is also helpful that
\operatorname{RHom}_{A}(\mathbf{R}\varGamma_{\mathfrak{m}},-)\cong\mathbf{L}\varLambda^{\mathfrak{m}}(-)
, the left derived functor of
\mathfrak{m}
-adic completion. See the discussion around
[
3
, (4.2), (4.3)]
.

Let
\{\mathfrak{p}_{u}\}_{u}
be the uncountable collection of prime ideals in
\widehat{R}
supplied by Lemma
2.3
. For each
\mathfrak{p}_{u}
let
K_{u}
denote the Koszul complex on
\mathbf{R}\varGamma_{\mathfrak{m}}R
on a minimal generating set for the ideal
\mathfrak{p}_{u}
of
\widehat{R}
. The complex
K_{u}
has the following properties:

(1)

Each
K_{u}
is dualizable in
\varGamma_{\mathfrak{m}}\operatorname{D}(\operatorname{Mod}A)
;

(2)

One has
K_{u}\not\cong K_{v}
for
u\neq v
;

(3)

Each
K_{u}
is indecomposable.

Indeed (1) holds because
\mathbf{R}\varGamma_{\mathfrak{m}}A
, being the unit of the product on
\varGamma_{\mathfrak{m}}\operatorname{D}(\operatorname{Mod}A)
is dualizable, and
K_{u}
is finitely built from
\mathbf{R}\varGamma_{\mathfrak{m}}A
.

Under the adjoint equivalence above,
\mathbf{R}\varGamma_{\mathfrak{m}}A
is mapped to
\mathbf{L}\varLambda^{\mathfrak{m}}A
, so
K_{u}
is mapped to the Koszul complex on
\widehat{A}
on the chosen minimal generating set for
\mathfrak{p}_{u}
. It follows that the kernel
I_{u}
of the natural map

\widehat{A}\longrightarrow\operatorname{Ext}^{*}_{A}(K_{u},K_{u})\cong\operatorname{Ext}^{*}_{\widehat{A}}(\mathbf{L}\varLambda^{\mathfrak{m}}K_{u},\mathbf{L}\varLambda^{\mathfrak{m}}K_{u})

satisfies
\sqrt{I_{u}}=\mathfrak{p}_{u}
. Since the
\{\mathfrak{p}_{u}\}_{u}
are distinct, (2)
follows. Moreover, the Koszul complex over
\widehat{A}
on any minimal
generating set for the ideal
\mathfrak{p}
is indecomposable in
\operatorname{D}(\operatorname{Mod}\widehat{A})
,
by
[
1
, Proposition 4.7]
. Consequently,
K_{u}

is indecomposable in
\operatorname{D}(\operatorname{Mod}A)
. This justifies (3).

Since the collection
\{K_{u}\}_{u}
is uncountable, to complete the proof we have to verify that there are only finitely many isomorphism classes of indecomposable direct summands of
\varGamma_{\mathfrak{m}}P
, with
P
a perfect
A
-complex. To see this, note that since
\widehat{A}
is complete,
\operatorname{Thick}_{\widehat{A}}(\widehat{A})
is a Krull-Schmidt category, and hence so is
\operatorname{Thick}_{A}(\mathbf{R}\varGamma_{\mathfrak{m}}A)
, by the equivalence above. It remains to recall that there are only countably many isomorphism classes of perfect
A
-complexes, by Lemma
2.2
.
∎

3.
Finite groups

Let
G
be a finite group and
k
a field of positive characteristic
p
,
where
p
divides the order of
G
. The stable category
\operatorname{StMod}(kG)
of

kG
-modules modulo projective modules is a tensor triangulated category,
there the product is
-\otimes_{k}-
with the diagonal
G
-action, and
the unit is the trivial
kG
-module
k
. The compact objects are the
modules equivalent to finitely generated modules.
They form a thick subcategory denoted
\operatorname{stmod}(kG)
.

The cohomology ring
\operatorname{H}^{*}(G,k)
is a finitely generated graded
k
-algebra and
\operatorname{Ext}^{*}_{kG}(M,N)
is a finitely generated module over
\operatorname{H}^{*}(G,k)
, for all
M,N
in
\operatorname{mod}kG
. The projectivized spectrum
V_{G}(k)=\operatorname{Proj}\operatorname{H}^{*}(G,k)
is the collection of homogeneous prime ideals in
H^{*}(G,k)
, except the the maximal one,
H^{\geqslant 1}(G,k)
.

The support variety
V_{G}(M)
of a finitely generated module
M
is defined as the collection of those ideals that contain the annihilator of
\operatorname{Ext}^{*}_{kG}(M,M)
in
\operatorname{H}^{*}(G,k)
. Support varieties for infinite dimensional
kG
-modules are introduced in
[
5
]
; see also
[
2
]
. These are subsets of
V_{G}(k)
that are not necessarily closed.

As in the previous section, for each
\mathfrak{p}
in
V_{G}(k)
, there exists an exact functor

\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}\colon\operatorname{StMod}(kG)\longrightarrow\operatorname{StMod}(kG)

whose image is the subcategory consisting of all
kG
-modules whose support
is contained in
V
. Then,
\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}\operatorname{StMod}(kG)
is again tensor triangulated,
with tensor product inherited from
\operatorname{StMod}(kG)
. The unit is
\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}k
,
and the function object is
\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}\operatorname{Hom}_{k}(-,-)
.
There are numerous equivalent ways to characterize dualizable modules
in this category; see
[
4
]
.
Most importantly, the full subcategory of dualizable modules in

\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}\operatorname{StMod}(kG)
form a thick triangulated subcategory, and

\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}M
is dualizable for each
M
in
\operatorname{stmod}(kG)
. The theorem
below and its proof are similar to Theorem
2.1
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
k
be a countable field of characteristic
p
, and
G
an elementary abelian
p
-group of rank
\geq 3
. Let
\mathfrak{p}
be a closed point in
V_{G}(k)
. There exists an uncountable collection of mutually non-isomorphic, indecomposable dualizable modules in
\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}\operatorname{StMod}(kG)
, none of which is a direct summand of
\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}M
for
M
in
\operatorname{stmod}(kG)
.

Proof.

Since the dualizable objects form a thick subcategory, it suffices to prove that there is an uncountable collection of mutually non-isomorphic, indecomposable objects in the thick category generated by
\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}\operatorname{stmod}(kG)
that are not retracts of the images of the finite dimensional ones. This has nothing to do with tensor triangulated structure on
\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}\operatorname{StMod}(kG)
, and we are free to choose any coalgebra structure on
kG
that is convenient.

We may assume
G=H\times\langle z\rangle
where
H
is elementary abelian of rank
r-1
and
z^{p}=1
. That is, we can assume that the ideal
\mathfrak{p}
is the radical of the restriction to a subalgebra
k[z]/(z^{p})\in kG
, the inclusion into
kG
being a
\pi
-point associated to
\mathfrak{p}
in the language of
[
8
]
. The choice of the complementary subalgebra
kH
is somewhat arbitrary. That is, we can choose
kH
to be the subalgebra generated by any collection
x_{1},\dots,x_{r-1}
in
\operatorname{rad}(kG)
such that the images of
z,x_{1},\dots,x_{r-1}
in
\operatorname{rad}(kG)/\operatorname{rad}^{2}(kG)
form a
k
-basis. Then
kG\cong kC\otimes kH
where
C
is generated by the unit
1+z
and
H
is generated by
1+x_{1},\dots,1+x_{r-1}
. This is an isomorphism of
k
-algebras, but not generally as Hopf algebras.

Keeping in mind that
\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}=\varGamma_{V(\mathfrak{p})}
, from
[
7
, Proposition 5.2]
we see that

R\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\underline{\operatorname{End}}_{kG}(\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}k)=\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}_{kG}(\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}k,\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}k)\cong\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}_{kG}(\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}k,k)\cong\prod_{i=0}^{\infty}\operatorname{H}^{i}(H,k)\,,

as an additive group. The product is the obvious one, except that, if
p
is odd, then any two elements of odd degree multiply to zero.
Hence the endomorphism ring is a commutative local ring that, modulo a nilpotent ideal, is the completion of a polynomial ring of degree
r-1
. In particular its Krull dimension is
r-1\geq 2
.

For each
\zeta\in R
, we define the module
K_{\zeta}
to be the third object in the triangle

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces K_{\zeta}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}k}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}k}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\,.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\zeta}

Evidently,
K_{\zeta}
is in the thick subcategory generated by

\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}\operatorname{stmod}(kG)
. Let
\mathfrak{a}_{\mathfrak{p}}(K_{\zeta})
be the annihilator of
the
R
-module
\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}_{kG}(\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}k,K_{\zeta}))
. Then we recall

[
7
, Theorem 7.6]
that the radical of

\mathfrak{a}_{\mathfrak{p}}(K_{\zeta})
coincides with the radical of the ideal
generated by
\zeta
. Thus, we have that if
\zeta
and
\gamma

are elements in
R
that generate ideals with different radicals,
then
K_{\zeta}
is not isomorphic to
K_{\gamma}
.

When
M
is a finite dimensional module, the
R
-module
\underline{\operatorname{End}}_{kG}(\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}M)
is finitely generated. Consequently,
\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}M
has only a finite number of indecomposable summands, as otherwise,
\underline{\operatorname{End}}_{kG}(\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}M)
would have an infinite number of idempotents. It follows from the Lemma
2.2
that the collection of modules
K_{\zeta}
that can be direct summands of modules of the form
\varGamma_{V}M
for
M
finitely generated, is countable. Since
\dim R\geq 2
it remains to recall from Lemma
2.3
that it has an uncountable number of elements
\zeta
having mutually distinct radicals.
∎

Example 3.2
.

Suppose that
p=2
and that
G
is elementary abelian of order
8
.
We use the notation of the previous proof. We write
kG=kH\otimes kC

where
kH=k[x,y]/(x^{2},y^{2})
and
kC=k[z]/(z^{2})
. Here the variety

V
is the point corresponding to the inclusion
kC\hookrightarrow kG
.
Choose
\zeta\in\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}_{kG}(\varGamma_{V}k,\varGamma_{V}k)
to have the form

\zeta=(0,\zeta_{1},\zeta_{2},\dots)
where
\zeta_{i}\in\operatorname{H}^{i}(H,k)
.
Assume that
\zeta_{1}\neq 0
. Because
\zeta_{1}\neq 0
, we have that
the restriction to
kH
of
K_{\zeta}
is a direct sum

\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}U_{i}
where
U_{i}\cong kH
. Choose
u_{i}\in U_{i}

to be a
kH
-generator, for all
i
. With some calculation, it can be
shown that the action of
z
is give by a formula

zu_{i}=\sum_{j=0}^{i}(\alpha_{j}x+\beta_{j}y)u_{i-j}

where for each
j
, the elements
\alpha_{j},\beta_{j}\in k
, depend on
the choices of
\zeta_{\ell}
for
\ell\leq j
. With some slight
adjustment in the proof, Theorem
3.1
tells us that
if
k
is countable, then there is an uncountable collection of
such elements
\zeta
such that the resulting modules
M_{\zeta}

are mutually non-isomorphic and not isomorphic to a direct summand of
any
\varGamma_{V}M
for any
M\in\operatorname{stmod}(kG)
.

General finite groups

We end with the following result, extending Theorem
3.1
to any finite group.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
k
be a countable field and
G
a finite group. Suppose that
\mathfrak{p}
be a closed point in
V_{G}(k)

that is contained in
\operatorname{res}^{*}_{G,E}(V_{G}(k))
for some elementary abelian

p
-subgroup
E
having rank
\geq 3
. There exists an uncountable
collection of mutually non-isomorphic, indecomposable dualizable modules
in
\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}\operatorname{StMod}(kG)
, none of which is a direct summand of

\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}M
for
M
in
\operatorname{stmod}(kG)
.

Proof.

We use the induction functor
\operatorname{StMod}(kE)\to\operatorname{StMod}(kG)
that takes a

kE
-module
M
to
M^{\uparrow G}=kG\otimes_{kE}M
.
The restriction to a
kE
-module of the idempotent
module
\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}k
is still an idempotent
module, and a support variety argument establishes that, in the
stable category, it has the form

(\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}k)_{\downarrow E}\cong\sum\varGamma_{\mathfrak{q}}k

where the sum is over the finite finite collection of closed points

\mathfrak{q}\in V_{E}(k)
such that
\operatorname{res}_{G,E}^{*}(\mathfrak{q})=\mathfrak{p}
.
Then by Frobenius reciprocity, we have that

\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}k\otimes(k_{\downarrow E})^{\uparrow G}\cong((\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}k)_{\downarrow E})^{\uparrow G}\cong\sum(\varGamma_{\mathfrak{q}}k)^{\uparrow G}.

As a consequence, the modules
(\varGamma_{\mathfrak{q}}k)^{\uparrow G}
are dualizable.

Let
\mathfrak{q}
denote any one of the points with
\operatorname{res}_{G,E}^{*}(\mathfrak{q})=\mathfrak{p}
.
Because the induction functor is exact, for
\zeta
in

\operatorname{Hom}_{kE}(\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}k,\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}k)
, the module
K_{\zeta}^{\uparrow G}
is also
dualizable. Moreover, by the Mackey Theorem, any such module has
at most a finite number of indecomposable direct summands. Now the
theorem follows from the fact that, by Theorem
3.1
, there is an uncountable number
of such modules and they are in
\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}\operatorname{StMod}(kG)
. On the other hand,
the thick subcategory obtained by taking the idempotent completion
of
\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}\operatorname{stmod}(kG)
has only a countable number of
indecomposable objects.
∎

Dual diffeomorphisms and finite distance asymptotic symmetries in 3d gravity
Marc Geiller & Christophe Goeller

Abstract
We study the finite distance boundary symmetry current algebra of the most general first order theory of 3d gravity. We show that the space of quadratic generators contains diffeomorphisms but also a notion of dual diffeomorphisms, which together form either a double Witt or centreless BMS 3 algebra. The relationship with the usual asymptotic symmetry algebra relies on a duality between the null and angular directions, which is possible thanks to the existence of the dual diffeomorphisms.

Lagrangian and phase space

Our starting point is the most general first order Lorentz-invariant theory constructed with a triad and a connection variable. This is described by the so-called Mielke–Baekler Lagrangian
Mielke and Baekler 1991

\displaystyle L

\displaystyle=\frac{\sigma_{0}}{3}e\wedge[e\wedge e]+2\sigma_{1}e\wedge F

\displaystyle\phantom{\ =}+\sigma_{2}\omega\wedge\left(\mathrm{d}\omega+\frac{1}{3}[\omega\wedge\omega]\right)+\sigma_{3}e\wedge\mathrm{d}_{\omega}e.

(1)

Its equations of motion can be written in the form

\displaystyle 2F+p[e\wedge e]\approx 0,\qquad 2\mathrm{d}_{\omega}e+q[e\wedge e]\approx 0,

(2)

which reveals sources of Lorentzian curvature and torsion measured respectively by
p
and
q
. These parameters are determined by the couplings via
\sigma_{0}=p\sigma_{1}+q\sigma_{3}
and
\sigma_{3}=p\sigma_{2}+q\sigma_{1}
. The solution to the torsion equation of motion is
\omega=\Gamma-qe/2
, where
\Gamma(e)
is the torsionless Levi–Civita connection. This can then be used to find the second order form of the equations of motion, which is
R_{\mu\nu}+2\lambda g_{\mu\nu}=0
with
\lambda=(p+q^{2}/4)\in\mathbb{R}
.

Sticking to the first order formulation, we find that the pre-symplectic potential, which is the starting point for the covariant phase space analysis, takes the simple form
\theta=2\sigma_{1}\delta\omega\wedge e+\sigma_{2}\delta\omega\wedge\omega+\sigma_{3}\delta e\wedge e
.

The theory is invariant under the internal Lorentz transformations
\delta^{\text{j}}
and translations
\delta^{\text{t}}
, which act as

\displaystyle\begin{matrix}[l]\delta^{\text{j}}_{\alpha}e=[e,\alpha],&\qquad&\delta^{\text{j}}_{\alpha}\omega=\mathrm{d}_{\omega}\alpha,\cr\delta^{\text{t}}_{\phi}e=\mathrm{d}_{\omega}\phi+q[e,\phi],&\qquad&\delta^{\text{t}}_{\phi}\omega=p[e,\phi].\end{matrix}

(3)

The commutators of these internal gauge transformations can be represented by the six-dimensional algebra

\displaystyle\begin{matrix}[J^{i},T^{j}]={\varepsilon^{ij}}_{k}T^{k},\qquad[J^{i},J^{j}]={\varepsilon^{ij}}_{k}J^{k},\cr[T^{i},T^{j}]={\varepsilon^{ij}}_{k}(pJ^{k}+qT^{k}),\end{matrix}

(4)

whose Casimir operators are

\displaystyle C_{1}=T^{i}T_{i}+pJ^{i}J_{i},\qquad C_{2}=J^{i}T_{i}+T^{i}J_{i}-qJ^{i}J_{i}.

(5)

On-shell of (
2
), diffeomorphisms
\delta^{\text{d}}_{\xi}=\pounds_{\xi}
can be written as field-dependent gauge transformations in the form

\displaystyle\delta^{\text{d}}_{\xi}\approx\delta^{\text{j}}_{\xi\raisebox{0.85355pt}{\text{$\lrcorner\,$}}\omega}+\delta^{\text{t}}_{\xi\raisebox{0.85355pt}{\text{$\lrcorner\,$}}e}.

(6)

This follows from the topological nature of the theory.

Quasi-local algebra and dual diffeomorphisms

Using the covariant phase space formalism and considering field-independent gauge parameters, the charges of Lorentz transformations and translations are found to be

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}(\alpha)=2\mathbin{\!\int\hskip-8.5pt\text{\large{$\circ$}}}\alpha(\sigma_{1}e+\sigma_{2}\omega),\qquad\mathcal{T}(\phi)=2\mathbin{\!\int\hskip-8.5pt\text{\large{$\circ$}}}\phi(\sigma_{1}\omega+\sigma_{3}e),

where all the boundary integrals are on
\varphi\in S^{1}

\displaystyle\big\{\mathcal{J}(\alpha),\mathcal{T}(\phi)\big\}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{T}([\alpha,\phi])-2\sigma_{1}\mathbin{\!\int\hskip-8.5pt\text{\large{$\circ$}}}\alpha\mathrm{d}\phi,

\displaystyle\big\{\mathcal{J}(\alpha),\mathcal{J}(\beta)\big\}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{J}([\alpha,\beta])-2\sigma_{2}\mathbin{\!\int\hskip-8.5pt\text{\large{$\circ$}}}\alpha\mathrm{d}\beta,

(7)

\displaystyle\big\{\mathcal{T}(\phi),\mathcal{T}(\chi)\big\}

\displaystyle=p\mathcal{J}([\phi,\chi])+q\mathcal{T}([\phi,\chi])-2\sigma_{3}\mathbin{\!\int\hskip-8.5pt\text{\large{$\circ$}}}\phi\mathrm{d}\chi.

(8)

After defining the generator
\mathcal{P}\coloneqq\mathcal{T}-q\mathcal{J}/2

\displaystyle\big\{\mathcal{J}(\alpha),\mathcal{P}(\phi)\big\}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{P}([\alpha,\phi])-c_{1}\mathbin{\!\int\hskip-8.5pt\text{\large{$\circ$}}}\alpha\mathrm{d}\phi,

\displaystyle\big\{\mathcal{J}(\alpha),\mathcal{J}(\beta)\big\}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{J}([\alpha,\beta])-c_{2}\mathbin{\!\int\hskip-8.5pt\text{\large{$\circ$}}}\alpha\mathrm{d}\beta,

(9)

\displaystyle\big\{\mathcal{P}(\phi),\mathcal{P}(\chi)\big\}

\displaystyle=\lambda\left(\mathcal{J}([\phi,\chi])-c_{2}\mathbin{\!\int\hskip-8.5pt\text{\large{$\circ$}}}\phi\mathrm{d}\chi\right),

(10)

where the central charges are now
c_{1}=2\sigma_{1}-q\sigma_{2}
and
c_{2}=2\sigma_{2}
. We will however continue to work with
\mathcal{T}

6

(\mathcal{J},\mathcal{T})

6

Field-dependent gauge transformations are generically not integrable without imposing extra conditions on the gauge parameters and/or the dynamical fields. In particular, when viewing diffeomorphisms as field-dependent gauge transformations as in (
6

-\xi\raisebox{0.85355pt}{\text{$\lrcorner\,$}}\theta

\not{\delta}\mathcal{D}(\xi)

(e,\omega)

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}(\xi)=\mathcal{J}(\xi\raisebox{0.85355pt}{\text{$\lrcorner\,$}}\omega)+\mathcal{T}(\xi\raisebox{0.85355pt}{\text{$\lrcorner\,$}}e),

which mirrors (
6
). Let us momentarily focus on tangent vector fields, before relaxing this requirement later on.

It is now natural to ask if, aside from the diffeomorphisms, there is another combination of field-dependent gauge transformations which can be made integrable and consistently defined on phase space, and if so what the properties of the corresponding charge is. The answer is affirmative, and the object fulfilling these requirements is the dual diffeomorphism charge. For tangent vector fields, it is defined as

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}^{*}(\xi)\coloneqq p\mathcal{J}(\xi\raisebox{0.85355pt}{\text{$\lrcorner\,$}}e)+q\mathcal{T}(\xi\raisebox{0.85355pt}{\text{$\lrcorner\,$}}e)+\mathcal{T}(\xi\raisebox{0.85355pt}{\text{$\lrcorner\,$}}\omega).

The proof leading to this charge is given for completeness in the appendix. It boils down to showing that the space of well-defined field-dependent gauge transformations is two-dimensional and can be parametrized by the diffeomorphism charge and its dual. The explicit expression for these charges, which we will need later on, is

\displaystyle\begin{matrix}[l]\phantom{{}^{*}}\mathcal{D}(\xi)\displaystyle=\mathbin{\!\int\hskip-8.5pt\text{\large{$\circ$}}}~2\sigma_{1}(\xi\raisebox{0.85355pt}{\text{$\lrcorner\,$}}\omega)e+\sigma_{2}(\xi\raisebox{0.85355pt}{\text{$\lrcorner\,$}}\omega)\omega+\sigma_{3}(\xi\raisebox{0.85355pt}{\text{$\lrcorner\,$}}e)e,\cr\mathcal{D}^{*}(\xi)\displaystyle=\mathbin{\!\int\hskip-8.5pt\text{\large{$\circ$}}}~2\sigma_{3}(\xi\raisebox{0.85355pt}{\text{$\lrcorner\,$}}\omega)e+\sigma_{1}(\xi\raisebox{0.85355pt}{\text{$\lrcorner\,$}}\omega)\omega+\sigma_{0}(\xi\raisebox{0.85355pt}{\text{$\lrcorner\,$}}e)e,\end{matrix}

(11)

where we should recall that in order to write these charges we have assumed that
\xi
is tangent and field-independent. Notice that the dual diffeomorphisms cannot be obtained from Chern–Simons theory, since there the only field-dependent gauge transformation which can be written is
\mathcal{F}(\xi\raisebox{0.85355pt}{\text{$\lrcorner\,$}}A)
, and corresponds to the diffeomorphisms.

With the definition of
\mathcal{D}^{*}
in terms of field-dependent gauge transformations, we can compute the action of the corresponding bulk symmetry on the fields using (
3

2
) this gives

\displaystyle\delta^{\text{d*}}_{\xi}e\approx\pounds_{\xi}\omega+q\pounds_{\xi}e,\qquad\delta^{\text{d*}}_{\xi}\omega\approx p\pounds_{\xi}e.

(12)

This action is therefore geometrical, just as the combination (
6
) giving the action of the diffeomorphisms
\mathcal{D}
. As surprising as it may seem, one can explicitly check that the transformations
\delta^{\text{d*}}_{\xi}
are indeed symmetries of the theory. This is in fact to be expected since we have obtained
\mathcal{D}^{*}
in terms of field-dependent gauge transformations, and
all

3

\mathcal{D}

Seen as a universal enveloping algebra, the current algebra formed by
(\mathcal{J},\mathcal{T})
actually
contains
the diffeomorphisms, which can be reconstructed as quadratics in the currents. This is the essence of the Sugawara construction. This latter enables to obtain, starting from the current algebra, a Witt algebra from generators which are quadratic in the currents

For this, let us switch to the Fourier representation, where the currents are denoted
(\mathcal{J}^{i}_{n},\mathcal{T}^{i}_{n})

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4(\sigma_{2}\sigma_{3}-\sigma_{1}^{2})}\big(\sigma_{1}\mathcal{Q}^{1}_{n}-\sigma_{2}\mathcal{Q}^{2}_{n}-\sigma_{3}\mathcal{Q}^{3}_{n}\big),

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4(\sigma_{2}\sigma_{3}-\sigma_{1}^{2})}\big(p\sigma_{1}\mathcal{Q}^{3}_{n}-p\sigma_{2}\mathcal{Q}^{1}_{n}+(\sigma_{1}-q\sigma_{2})\mathcal{Q}^{2}_{n}\big),

\xi_{\text{\tiny{$\parallel$}}}=e^{-\mathrm{i}n\varphi}\partial_{\varphi}

The existence of the dual diffeomorphisms can also be traced back to the fact that the Sugawara construction relies on the quadratic Casimir operators of the global algebra underlying the current algebra of boundary symmetries
Di Francesco et al. 1997
. In three-dimensional gravity this algebra always admits two Casimirs, which in the most general formulation studied here are given by (
5

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}\propto\sigma_{2}\widetilde{C}_{1}-\sigma_{1}\widetilde{C}_{2},\qquad\mathcal{D}^{*}\propto(\sigma_{1}-q\sigma_{2})\widetilde{C}_{1}-p\sigma_{2}\widetilde{C}_{2},

where the tilde denotes the lift of the Casimirs to the universal enveloping algebra. The two-dimensionality of the space of well-defined quadratic operators matches the fact that the underlying algebra has two Casimirs. This is a non-trivial consistency check, as one can show that it is only the quadratics forming a stable algebra with the currents which can be written in terms of the Casimirs.

Sticking to tangent vectors, we can now compute the algebra of the diffeomorphisms and their dual, to find

\displaystyle\big\{\mathcal{D}^{*}(\xi),\mathcal{D}(\zeta)\big\}

\displaystyle=-\mathcal{D}^{*}([\xi,\zeta]),

\displaystyle\big\{\mathcal{D}(\xi),\mathcal{D}(\zeta)\big\}

\displaystyle=-\mathcal{D}([\xi,\zeta]),

(14)

\displaystyle\big\{\mathcal{D}^{*}(\xi),\mathcal{D}^{*}(\zeta)\big\}

\displaystyle=-p\mathcal{D}([\xi,\zeta])-q\mathcal{D}^{*}([\xi,\zeta]).

(15)

Remarkably, this reflects exactly the
(\mathcal{J},\mathcal{T})
current algebra from which we started, and can therefore be thought of as its representation in terms of vector fields instead of Lie algebra elements. However, since the vector fields are tangent we obtain no central extensions. This algebra can now be put in a more suggestive form by redefining the dual generator as
\mathcal{A}\coloneqq\mathcal{D}^{*}-q\mathcal{D}/2
, leading to

\displaystyle\big\{\mathcal{A}(\xi),\mathcal{D}(\zeta)\big\}

\displaystyle=-\mathcal{A}([\xi,\zeta]),

(16)

\displaystyle\big\{\mathcal{D}(\xi),\mathcal{D}(\zeta)\big\}

\displaystyle=-\mathcal{D}([\xi,\zeta]),

(17)

\displaystyle\big\{\mathcal{A}(\xi),\mathcal{A}(\zeta)\big\}

\displaystyle=-\lambda\mathcal{D}([\xi,\zeta]),

which reflects the
(\mathcal{J},\mathcal{P})
current algebra. In the case
\lambda=0
, which can be achieved for
q^{2}=-4p
, this is the centreless
\mathfrak{bms}_{3}
algebra. When
\lambda\neq 0
, the redefinition
\mathcal{D}^{\pm}\coloneqq(\mathcal{D}\pm\lambda^{-1/2}\mathcal{A})/2
reveals as usual the direct sum of two Witt (or centreless Virasoro) algebras. Note that this also works in the dS case
\lambda<0
.

This is the main result of the present letter, namely the construction, in the most general first order theory of three-dimensional gravity, of the quadratic generators in the universal enveloping algebra of boundary symmetries. These are the diffeomorphisms and their dual, which at any finite distance form a double Witt algebra. We note that the only related result where a
\mathfrak{bms}_{3}
algebra was built from the current algebra using two independent quadratics seems to be
Caroca et al. 2018
, which however did not provide a gravitational interpretation of this construction.

Relation with asymptotic charges and algebra

It is now enlightening to compare our construction with the derivation of the asymptotic double Virasoro algebra and its flat limit. For this, we consider the Bondi gauge line element
Barnich et al. 2012

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}s^{2}=(\mathscr{M}-\lambda r^{2})\mathrm{d}u^{2}-2\mathrm{d}u\,\mathrm{d}r+\mathscr{N}\,\mathrm{d}u\,\mathrm{d}\varphi+r^{2}\mathrm{d}\varphi^{2},

where the two free functions
(\mathscr{M},\mathscr{N})
are independent of

\partial_{u}\mathscr{M}=\lambda\mathscr{N}^{\prime}
and
\partial_{u}\mathscr{N}=\mathscr{M}^{\prime}
, where prime is the angular derivative. The on-shell asymptotic Killing vectors are
\xi=(\xi^{u},\xi^{r},\xi^{\varphi})

\displaystyle\xi^{u}=f,\qquad\xi^{r}=f^{\prime\prime}-rg^{\prime}-\frac{\mathscr{N}f^{\prime}}{2r},\qquad\xi^{\varphi}=g-\frac{f^{\prime}}{r},

\partial_{u}g=\lambda f^{\prime}

\displaystyle\delta^{\text{d}}_{\xi}\mathscr{M}

\displaystyle=g\mathscr{M}^{\prime}+2\mathscr{M}g^{\prime}-2g^{\prime\prime\prime}+\lambda(2\mathscr{N}f^{\prime}+f\mathscr{N}^{\prime}),

\displaystyle\delta^{\text{d}}_{\xi}\mathscr{N}

\displaystyle=f\mathscr{M}^{\prime}+2\mathscr{M}f^{\prime}-2f^{\prime\prime\prime}+2\mathscr{N}g^{\prime}+g\mathscr{N}^{\prime}.

The fact that we have recovered the asymptotic symmetry algebra at any arbitrary location in the bulk is reminiscent of the so-called symplectic nature of these symmetries

\mathcal{O}(r^{-1})
piece in the charge
\mathcal{D}_{\text{B}}
. This contribution can however be removed by replacing the Lie derivative with the Kosmann derivative
Jacobson and Mohd 2015
. This latter is defined as
\mathscr{K}_{\xi}\coloneqq\pounds_{\xi}+\delta^{\text{j}}_{k}
with a field-dependent parameter given by
2k^{i}\coloneqq-{\varepsilon^{i}}_{jk}g^{\mu\nu}e^{j}_{\mu}\pounds_{\xi}e^{k}_{\nu}
, and is such that
\mathscr{K}_{\xi}e=0
when
\xi
is Killing. The charge of the Lorentz transformation is integrable and cancels exactly the subleading term in
\mathcal{D}_{\text{B}}
, so that the charge associated with the Kosmann derivative is therefore
r
-independent.

Perspectives

In this letter we have studied the finite distance symmetry algebra of the most general first order theory of three-dimensional gravity, and explained how aspects of asymptotic symmetry algebras are embedded in it. This relies on the construction of the second order charges in the universal enveloping algebra of the
(\mathcal{J},\mathcal{T})
current algebra. We have shown that the existence of two (and only two) well-defined quadratic charges, namely the diffeomorphism
\mathcal{D}
and its dual
\mathcal{D}^{*}
, can be understood in terms of the Sugawara construction and its dependency on the Casimirs of the global part of the current algebra. Focusing on tangent vector fields, which enables to work without imposing boundary conditions, we have shown that these charges form a double Witt algebra which in the flat limit reduces to a centreless
\mathfrak{bms}_{3}
algebra. The absence of central extensions comes from the tangentiality of the vector fields, and can be lifted by considering a twisted Sugawara construction. This is outlined in
Geiller et al. 2020
and will be the focus of future work.

Our construction has raised a natural question: Is it possible to understand the asymptotic double Virasoro and
\mathfrak{bms}_{3}
algebras in terms of the diffeomorphisms and their dual? We have shown that the answer is positive but subtle. The angular part of the asymptotic Bondi gauge charge is captured by a tangent diffeomorphism at any finite distance in the bulk. More surprisingly, the null part of the asymptotic Bondi gauge charge is captured by the dual diffeomorphism evaluated on a tangent vector field. This means that the null direction of a usual diffeomorphism can be understood as a tangential direction from the viewpoint of the dual diffeomorphism. This is reminiscent of
Adami et al. 2020b
;
Ruzziconi and Zwikel 2020
, where a change of vector field basis is used to render any diffeomorphism integrable.

This opens up interesting directions. At finite distance, it is now necessary to study the diffeomorphism and its dual for arbitrary non-tangent vector fields. This requires the discussion of integrability and boundary conditions, whose relation with the twisted Sugawara construction must also be spelled out. This is a necessary generalization in order to obtain central extensions. Considering arbitrary vector fields will potentially extend the finite distance symmetry algebra, and a question is therefore whether this can also be realized asymptotically. Another crucial question is if and how these dual charges are related to those studied in four-dimensional gravity in
Godazgar et al. 2019a
;
Godazgar et al. 2019b
;
Godazgar et al. 2019c
;
Kol and Porrati 2019
;
Godazgar et al. 2020a
;
Godazgar et al. 2020b
;
Oliveri and Speziale 2020
. Other questions concern the role of the dual diffeomorphisms in the quantization of the theory and the construction of representations of its symmetry algebra.

Appendix 1: Well-definiteness of
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{D}^{*}}

To introduce the dual diffeomorphisms, we start with the most general linear combination of field-dependent gauge transformations obtained by contracting a vector field with a connection or a triad field. This is

\displaystyle\not{\delta}\mathcal{G}(\xi)=a\not{\delta}\mathcal{J}(\xi\raisebox{0.85355pt}{\text{$\lrcorner\,$}}e)+b\not{\delta}\mathcal{J}(\xi\raisebox{0.85355pt}{\text{$\lrcorner\,$}}\omega)+c\not{\delta}\mathcal{T}(\xi\raisebox{0.85355pt}{\text{$\lrcorner\,$}}e)+d\not{\delta}\mathcal{T}(\xi\raisebox{0.85355pt}{\text{$\lrcorner\,$}}\omega).

Writing out explicitly these field-dependent charges, it is not hard to see that they can be made integrable without restricting the dynamical fields if we impose the algebraic relation
b\sigma_{1}+d\sigma_{3}-a\sigma_{2}-c\sigma_{1}=0
and consider tangent vector fields. With integrability being achieved, we can then compute the brackets of these new charges with the diffeomorphisms, the Lorentz transformations, and the translations. We then find that all these brackets are well-defined, aside from

\displaystyle\big\{\mathcal{G}(\xi),\mathcal{T}(\phi)\big\}=

\displaystyle-a\mathcal{J}(\pounds_{\xi}\phi)-c\mathcal{T}(\pounds_{\xi}\phi)

\displaystyle-2(b\sigma_{3}+d\sigma_{0}-a\sigma_{1}-c\sigma_{3})\mathbin{\!\int\hskip-8.5pt\text{\large{$\circ$}}}[\xi\raisebox{0.85355pt}{\text{$\lrcorner\,$}}e,\phi]\omega,

(22)

which does not close because of the field-dependent piece. This latter is problematic because it is not the charge of an integrable gauge transformation. Moreover, when considering iterated Poisson brackets it leads to higher and higher order terms in the fields. Cancelling this piece by imposing the vanishing of its coefficient, and choosing for convenience the normalization
d=1
, we find
a=p
and
c=b+q
. We therefore obtain the well-defined charges
\mathcal{G}(\xi)=p\mathcal{J}(\xi\raisebox{0.85355pt}{\text{$\lrcorner\,$}}e)+q\mathcal{T}(\xi\raisebox{0.85355pt}{\text{$\lrcorner\,$}}e)+\mathcal{T}(\xi\raisebox{0.85355pt}{\text{$\lrcorner\,$}}\omega)+b\mathcal{D}(\xi)
. Finally, we can take
b=0
and consider
\mathcal{D}^{*}
and
\mathcal{D}
as the independent charges. In summary, starting from the four-dimensional space of field-dependent gauge transformations
\not{\delta}\mathcal{G}(\xi)
, we have imposed two conditions in order to achieve integrability and obtain a stable algebra. We are therefore left with a two-dimensional space, which can be parametrized by the independent generators

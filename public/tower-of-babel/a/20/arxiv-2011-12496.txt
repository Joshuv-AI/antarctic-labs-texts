The Paired Domination Number of Cubic Graphs
Bin Sheng, Changhong Lu

Abstract
Let G G be a simple undirected graph with no isolated vertex. A paired dominating set of G G is a dominating set which induces a subgraph that has a perfect matching. The paired domination number of G G , denoted by γ p ​ r ​ ( G ) \gamma_{pr}(G) , is the size of its smallest paired dominating set. Goddard and Henning conjectured that γ p ​ r ​ ( G ) ≤ 4 ​ n / 7 \gamma_{pr}(G)\leq 4n/7 holds for every graph G G with δ ⁡ ( G ) ≥ 3 \delta(G)\geq 3 , except the Petersen Graph. In this paper, we prove this conjecture for cubic graphs.

Introduction

Dominating set is one of the most classic problems in graph theory. Many variants of it have been studied due to its wide applications. There are excellent books for this topic, like
[
Hay17
,
HHS98
]
. In this paper, we study the paired domination problem.

Hayes and Slater raised the notion of paired domination in
[
HS98
]
, as a model for the problem of assigning security guards that can protect each other. It has been studied from many perspectives
[
CLZ09
,
CKN07
,
EFT13
,
FH04
,
HP05
]
.

Computing the paired-domination number of a graph has been shown to be NP-complete
[
HS98
]
.
Chen et al.
[
CLZ10
]
further proved that it is NP-complete for bipartite graphs, chordal graphs, and even for split graphs. Thus obtaining tight upper bounds on the paired-domination number of a graph is an interesting problem.

For general graphs, Haynes and Slater
[
HS98
]
bound the paired-domination number with respect to the number of vertices in the graph.

Theorem 1
.

(
[
HS98
]
)
If
G
is a connected graph with
n(\geq 3)
vertices, then
\gamma_{pr}\leq n-1
, the equality holds if and only if
G
is
C_{3},C_{5}
or a subdivided star.

If we require that
\delta(G)\geq 2
, then the following theorem improves the result in Theorem
1
.

Theorem 2
.

(
[
HS98
]
)
If
G
is a connected graph with
n(\geq 6)
vertices and
\delta(G)\geq 2
, then
\gamma_{pr}(G)\leq 2n/3
.

The result in Theorem
2
can be further strengthened if we increase the size bound of the graph.

Theorem 3
.

(
[
Hen07
]
)
If
G
is a connected graph with
n(\geq 10)
vertices and
\delta(G)\geq 2
, then
\gamma_{pr}\leq 2(n-1)/3
. The equality holds for infinitely many graphs.

Graph with at least 14 vertices and minimum degree at least 2 are also considered in
[
Hen07
]
.

There is also a series of results for graphs with
\delta(G)\geq 3
.

Theorem 4
.

(
[
CSC08
]
)
If
G
is a cubic graph with
n
vertices, then
\gamma_{pr}(G)\leq 3n/5
.

In
[
CSC08
]
, the authors made the following conjecture.

Conjecture 1
.

Let
G
be a connected graph with
n(\geq 11)
vertices and
\delta(G)\geq 3
, then
\gamma_{pr}(G)\leq 4n/7
.

Goddard and Henning improved the result in Theorem
4
, and made a strengthened conjecture in
[
GH09
]
.

Theorem 5
.

(
[
GH09
]
)
If
G
is a connected cubic graph, then
\gamma_{pr}(G)\leq 3n/5
. Moreover, the equality holds if and only if
G
is the Petersen Graph.

Conjecture 2
.

Let
G
be a connected graph with
n
vertices and
\delta(G)\geq 3
. If
G
is not the Petersen Graph, then
\gamma_{pr}(G)\leq 4n/7
.

Lu et al.
[
LWW16
]
proved Conjecture 2 for
k
-regular graphs with

, and later
[
LWWW19
]
proved it for claw-free graphs with minimum degree at least 3.

Theorem 6
.

(
[
LWWW19
]
)
If G is a connected claw-free graph of order n with

, then
\gamma_{pr}(G)\leq 4n/7
.

People also give bound for the paired domination number of special graphs, such as
P_{5}
-free graphs
[
DG08
]
, subdivided star-free graphs
[
DG10
]
, generalized claw-free graphs
[
DGH07
]
, claw-free graphs
[
HKS13
]
, and so on.

In this paper, we follow this line of research and prove Conjecture 2 for cubic graphs, which completes the picture for regular graphs. Our result does not rely on the graph to be claw-free. With the intuition that denser graph should have smaller power domination set, our result is a promising step towards proving Conjecture 1.

Notations and Terminology

Here we give a brief list of the graph theory concepts used in this paper, for other
notations and terminology, we refer readers to
[
Bol13
]
.

All the graph considered in this paper are simple and undirected. The
neighborhood
of a vertex
u\in V
is
N_{G}(u)=\{v\in V|uv\in E\}
. The
degree
of
v
in G, denoted by
d_{G}(v)
, is the number
|N_{G}(v)|
. A
cubic graph
is a graph in which every vertex has degree 3. We use
\delta(G)
to denote the minimum vertex degree of
G
. A vertex with degree one is a
leaf
. For a vertex set
S\subseteq V
, we use
G[S]
to denote the subgraph induced by
S
. The neighborhood of
v
in
S
is
N_{S}(v)=N_{G}(v)\cap S
. For two disjoint vertex sets
X
and
Y
, we use
[X,Y]
to denote the set of edges between
X
and
Y
.

Given a graph
G
, we say
S\subseteq V(G)
is a paired dominating set(abbreviated as PDS) of
G
, if
S
is a dominating set of
G
and
G[S]
has a perfect matching.
Let
S
be a PDS of
G
, we say
x\in V\setminus S
is a
private neighbor
of
u\in S
, if
N_{S}(x)=\{u\}
. Fix a perfect matching
M
of
G[S]
, we say
u
and
\overline{u}
form a pair if
u\overline{u}\in M
, and we use
S_{u}
to denote the pair of vertices
u
and
\overline{u}
.
A pair
S_{u}
is a
solo pair
if both
u
and
\overline{u}
are leaves in
G[S]
. Otherwise, we call it a
linked pair
.
For a subset
S\subseteq V
,
\lambda(S)
denotes the number of edges in
G[S]
. We use
u^{\prime}
to denote the private neighbor of
u\in S
if
u
has exactly one private neighbor. For any positive integer
n
, we use
[n]
to denote the set
\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
.

Let
E_{1}
be a subset of edges in
M
, the perfect matching of
G[S]
. Let
E_{2}
be a subset of
E(G)
.
Replacing
E_{1}
with

means to replace
S
with
S^{\prime}=S\setminus V(E_{1})\cup V(E_{2})
. Moreover, the edges in

belong to the perfect matching of

.

Let

(P1)

\lambda(S)
is minimized;

(P2)

|A\cup B|

P_{1}

The following lemma lists some properties of

]

Lemma 2
.

(
[
HKS13
]
)
Let
G
be a connected cubic graph and
S
a minimum PDS of
G
satisfying P1 and P2. Let

and

be the four vertex

1.

3.

We adopt the strategy used in
[
LWWW19
]
to prove Conjecture 2 for cubic graphs. We design a weight function for

, such that for every vertex in

, the weights of all edges incident with it sum to one. Thus the total weight of edges in
[V\setminus S,S]
are exactly
|V\setminus S|
.
At the same time, we show that averagely the edges incident with each pair have total weight at least 3/2. It follows that the total weight of edges in
[V\setminus S,S]
is at least
3|S|/4
. Thus we have
3|S|/4\leq|V\setminus S|
and
|S|\leq 4n/7
.

We now define the weight function
f:[V\setminus S,S]\rightarrow[0,1]
.

1.

If
x
is a private neighbor of some vertex

, then
f(xu)=1
;

2.

Otherwise, if
N_{S}(x)\cap(B\cup C\cup D)\neq\emptyset

3.

If

is a subset of

or

f(xu)=1/|N_{S}(x)|
, for each

4.

If

f(xu)=0
for every vertex
u\in A

f(xu)=1/6
for every vertex
u\in B\cup C
, and
f(xu)=\frac{1-|N_{B\cup C}(x)|/6}{|N_{D}(x)|}
for every vertex
u\in D
.

According to the definition of the weight function
f
, we have the following observations.

Lemma 3
.

For any edge
xu

,

f(xu)\in\{0,1/6,1/3,5/12,1/2,2/3,5/6,1\}

4
Conclusion

In this paper, we study the paired domination problem in cubic graphs. We show that, except the Petersen Graph,
\gamma_{pr}(G)\leq 4n/7
holds for all cubic graphs. Our result confirms the remaining case of Conjecture 2 for regular graphs.
As our result does not require the graph to be claw-free, it is a promising step towards proving Conjecture 2 completely.
